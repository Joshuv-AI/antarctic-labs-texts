One-cycles on Gushel-Mukai fourfolds and the Beauville-Voisin filtration
Ruxuan Zhang

Abstract
. We prove that the invariant locus of the involution associated with a general double EPW sextic is a constant cycle surface and introduce a filtration on CH 1 \operatorname{CH}_{1} of a Gushel-Mukai fourfold. We verify the sheaf/cycle correspondence for sheaves supported on low-degree rational curves, parallel to the cubic fourfolds case of Shen-Yin’s work.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14J42 , 14C15

1.
Introduction

A Gushel-Mukai (GM) fourfold is a smooth prime Fano fourfold
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{8}
of degree
10
and index
2
. Throughout the paper, a GM fourfold is always an ordinary GM fourfold,
which can be obtained as a smooth dimensionally transverse intersection

X=\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)\cap\mathbb{P}^{8}\cap Q\subset\mathbb{P}^{9},

where
\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)\subset\mathbb{P}^{9}
is the Plücker embedding,
\mathbb{P}^{8}
is a linear subspace and
Q
is a quadric hypersurface.

GM fourfolds share many properties with cubic fourfolds. One of the distinguished facts is that there is a parallel between GM and cubic fourfolds from a categorical viewpoint.
Similarly to the semiorthogonal decomposition of the derived category of a cubic fourfold, Kuznetsov and Perry showed in
[
15
]
that the derived category of a GM fourfold admits a semiorthogonal decomposition:

{\rm D}^{b}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{X}(1),\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\vee}(1)\rangle,

where
\mathcal{U}_{X}
is the tautological bundle of
\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)
restricted to
X
and
\mathcal{A}_{X}
is a K3 category.

Another fact is that a GM fourfold has an associated irreducible holomorphic symplectic (IHS) variety, which is called the dual double EPW sextic, compared with the Fano variety of lines associated with a cubic fourfold, see
[
3
]
[
19
]
[
14
]
for details.

The Fano variety of lines of a cubic fourfold and the dual double EPW sextic of a GM fourfold both admit constructions via moduli spaces of stable objects and Hilbert schemes of rational curves.
In
[
22
, Proposition 5.17]
, it has been shown that at least one of the double EPW sextic and the dual double EPW sextic can be realized as a moduli space of stable objects of
\mathcal{A}_{X}
and conjecturally both are.
In
[
14
]
, Iliev and Manivel gave a more geometrical description of the dual double EPW sextic. For a general GM fourfold
X
,
let
F(X)
be the Hilbert scheme of conics on
X
and
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
be the associated dual double EPW sextic of
X
, there exists a morphism

F(X)\rightarrow\tilde{Y}^{\vee}

such that a general fiber is
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Quite generally, for a
2n
-dimensional IHS variety
M
, Voisin predicted in
[
32
]
that there exists a filtration of the Chow ring
\operatorname{CH}^{*}(M)
(called Beauville–Voisin filtration), which can be viewed as an opposite to the conjectural Bloch-Beilinson filtration.
For 0-cycles, the filtration
{\rm S}_{\bullet}\operatorname{CH}_{0}(M)
is defined by

{\rm S}_{i}\operatorname{CH}_{0}(M):=\left<x\in M|~\dim O_{y}\geq n-i\right>,

where
O_{y}
is the orbit of
y
under the rational equivalence (here we follow the definition in
[
24
]
, which is opposite to Voisin’s original definition). Let
Z\subset M
be a subvariety. If any two points on
Z
have the same class in
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(M)
, we call that
Z
is a constant cycle variety on
M
.
In particular, for an IHS variety of dimension
4
, the filtration is determined by constant cycle surfaces and uniruled divisors.
Let
{\rm S}_{i}M\subset M
be the set of points with dim
O_{y}\geq n-i
. Voisin conjectured in
[
32
, Conjecture 0.4]
that

\mathrm{dim}~{\rm S}_{i}M=n+i.

(1.1)

It is closely related to the existence of algebraically coisotropic subvarieties on
M
.

Let
M
be a nonsingular projective moduli space of stable objects on a K3 category
\mathcal{A}
, then it is an IHS variety in many cases.
Shen and Yin predicted that all the Beauville–Voisin filtrations associated with different moduli spaces of stable objects on a fixed K3 category should be controlled by a universal filtration on the Grothendieck group of the K3 category, see
[
24
, Speculation 0.1]
.

When the K3 category is the derived category of a K3 surface
S
and
M
is a
2d
-dimensional nonsingular moduli space of stable objects, the O’Grady’s filtration on
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(S)

{\rm S}_{i}(S):=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\deg([z])=i\\
z~\text{effective}\end{subarray}}\{[z]+\mathbb{Z}\cdot[o_{S}]\}

serves as the universal filtration for all moduli space of stable objects. It has been shown in
[
25
]
that

c_{2}(\mathcal{E})\in{\rm S}_{d}(S)

for any
\mathcal{E}\in M
and that
c_{2}(\mathcal{E})\in{\rm S}_{i}(S)
implies
\mathcal{E}\in{\rm S}_{i}\operatorname{CH}_{0}(M)
.

Another example arises when the K3 category is the Kuznetsov component
\mathcal{A}_{X}
of a cubic fourfold
X
. Using the Beauville-Voisin filtration on the Fano variety of lines on
X
, Shen-Yin introduced in
[
25
]
a filtration
{\rm S}_{\bullet}(X)
on
\operatorname{CH}_{1}(X)
induced by the incidence correspondence. They conjectured that
the filtration
{\rm S}_{\bullet}(X)
may serve as a universal filtration on
K_{0}(\mathcal{A}_{X})
, i.e.,
for
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
, there should be

c_{3}(\mathcal{E})\in{\rm S}_{d}(X),

where
d=\frac{1}{2}\dim\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
. This implies (
1.1
) by a standard argument and they verified the prediction when
\mathcal{E}
is supported on low-degree rational curves.

It is natural to ask whether there exists a universal filtration on
\mathcal{A}_{X}
for a GM fourfold, similarly to the case of a cubic fourfold. We first study the Beauville–Voisin filtration on
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})
by finding a natural constant cycle surface on
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
. The invariant locus
Z
of the involution associated with the dual double EPW sextic is a surface of general type, whose
\operatorname{CH}_{0}
might be huge. However, it was asked whether
Z
is a constant cycle surface on
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
, see
[
17
, Remark 2.7]
.
Our first result is (Theorem
4.14
):

Theorem 1.1
.

The invariant locus
Z
of the involution associated with a general dual double EPW sextic
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
is a constant cycle surface. If
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
is very general, any other constant cycle surface
Z^{\prime}
meets
Z
.

The
0
-piece of the Beauville–Voisin filtration of
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
is represented by a point on
Z
and the
1
-piece is represented by a point on a uniruled divisor. Theorem
1.1
allows us to construct a filtration
{\rm S}_{\bullet}(X)
on
\operatorname{CH}_{1}(X)
by the incidence correspondence of Iliev and Manivel’s construction. For a very general GM fourfold, we predict that the filtration should serve as a universal filtration on
\mathcal{A}_{X}
in the following way:

The Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{A}_{X}
is shown to be generated by
\operatorname{pr}[\mathcal{O}_{c}(1)]
and
\operatorname{pr}[\mathcal{O}_{p}]
for a very general
X
, where
c
is a conic,
p
is a point on
X
and
\operatorname{pr}
is the projection

\operatorname{pr}:K_{0}(X)\rightarrow K_{0}(\mathcal{A}_{X}).

Unlike the cubic fourfold case, the dual double EPW sextic determines the GM fourfold only up to period partners and the Chern class
c_{3}(\operatorname{pr}[\mathcal{O}_{p}])
of a skyscraper sheaf might lie in different pieces of the filtration with respect to different GM fourfolds. We define a modification

p:K_{0}(\mathcal{A}_{X})\rightarrow\operatorname{CH}_{1}(X)

of
c_{3}
by dropping the effect of
\operatorname{pr}[\mathcal{O}_{p}]
, see Section
6
for details.

We propose the following conjecture relating the
K3
category
\mathcal{A}_{X}
to the filtration
{\rm S}_{\bullet}(X)
as a parallel to the case of a cubic fourfold.

Conjecture 1.2
.

For any object
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
, we have

p(\mathcal{E})\in{\rm S}_{d(\mathcal{E})}(X).

Let
i^{*}
be the left adjoint functor of the natural inclusion
\mathcal{A}_{X}\hookrightarrow D^{b}(X)
. We show that the classes of lines and conics on a very general GM fourfold
X
are in
{\rm S}_{2}(X)
, which implies
our second main result concerning sheaves supported on low-degree rational curves (Theorem
6.3
):

Theorem 1.3
.

We assume that
X
is very general.
If
\mathcal{F}
is supported on lines or conics on
X
, conjecture
1.2
holds for
\mathcal{E}=i^{*}\mathcal{F}
.

Our approach to deducing Theorem
1.1
has another application, concerning with the relation between
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})
and
\operatorname{CH}_{1}(X)
. When
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
is birational to
S^{[2]}
for some K3 surface
S
, it has been shown in
[
17
]
that
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})
with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients has a natural decomposition for the Chow group. For 0-cycles, the decomposition is

\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})=\mathbb{Q}\cdot o\oplus\operatorname{CH}_{0}(S)_{\rm hom}\oplus\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})^{+}_{\rm hom},

where
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})^{+}_{\rm hom}
is the
\iota
-invariant part of
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})_{\rm hom}
. We show that in the general case, the group
\operatorname{CH}_{1}(X)
would play the role of the group
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(S)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

(Theorem
5.5
)
When the dual double EPW sextic
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
is general, we have the following isomorphism of groups:

\mathbb{Z}\cdot o\oplus\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})^{-}_{\rm hom}\cong\operatorname{CH}_{1}(X),

where
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})^{-}_{\rm hom}
is the
\iota
-anti-invariant part of
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})_{\rm hom}
.

In particular, the group
\operatorname{CH}_{1}(X)
is torsion-free and we see that if
X^{\prime}
is another GM fourfold with the same dual double EPW sextic as
X
, then their groups of 1-cycles are isomorphic.

Our results rely on Iliev and Manivel’s construction and hence all dual double EPW sextics are assumed to be general in the rest of the paper.

3.
Preliminaries

Let
X=\operatorname{Gr}(2,V_{5})\cap H\cap Q
be a very general GM fourfold and
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}\rightarrow Y^{\vee}
be its associated double cover of the dual EPW sextic. Denote by
\iota:\tilde{Y}^{\vee}\rightarrow\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
the associated involution and by
Z
the invariant locus of
\iota
. We recall some facts about GM varieties and EPW sextics in this section. Throughout the paper,
V_{m}
is an
m
-dimensional vector space.

3.1.
EPW sextics

Debarre and Kuznetsov gave a way to associate a GM variety with an EPW sextic in
[
3
]
via linear algebra. We will follow
[
4
]
to recall some basic facts about EPW sextics. A GM variety
X_{n}=\operatorname{Gr}(2,V_{5})\cap\mathbb{P}(W)\cap Q
of dimension
n
is a smooth intersection of a linear subspace of dimension
n+4
, a quadratic hypersurface and
\operatorname{Gr}(2,V_{5})
inside
\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{2}V_{5})
.
It has an associated GM data
(W_{n+5},V_{6},V_{5},q)
, where

•

V_{6}
is a
6
-dimensional vector space, consisting of quadrics containing
X
.

•

V_{5}
is a hyperplane section of
V_{6}
, consisting of quadrics containing
\operatorname{Gr}(2,V_{5})
.

•

W_{n+5}\subset\bigwedge^{2}V_{5}
is a subspace of dimension
n+5
, corresponding to the linear subspace in the definition of GM variety.

•

q:V_{6}\rightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{2}W_{n+5}^{\vee}
, is a linear map such that (here we choose an isomorphism
\bigwedge^{5}V_{5}\cong\mathbb{C}
)

\forall v\in V_{5},q(v)(w,w)=v\wedge w\wedge w.

Then
X=\bigcap_{v\in V_{6}}Q(v)\subset\mathbb{P}(W_{n+5})
, where
Q(v)
is defined by
q(v)
. For a general GM data,
X
is a smooth GM variety.

On the other hand,
\bigwedge^{3}V_{6}
can be endowed with a symplectic form by wedge product.
Debarre and Kuznetsov defined the Lagrangian data set
(V_{6},V_{5},A)
with
A\subset\bigwedge^{3}V_{6}
a Lagrangian subspace and
V_{5}\subset V_{6}
a hyperplane. It gives an EPW data

Y_{A}^{\geq l}:=\{[v]\in\mathbb{P}(V_{6})~|~\mathrm{dim}(A\cap(v\wedge\bigwedge^{2}V_{6}))\geq l\}

and an extra
V_{5}
. Here,
Y_{A}^{\geq 1}
is a sextic in
\mathbb{P}(V_{6})
and
Y_{A}^{\geq 2}
is a surface.
In
[
20
]
, O’Grady showed that

Theorem 3.2
.

If
A
contains no decomposable vectors and
Y_{A}^{\geq 3}=\emptyset
, there is a double cover
\tilde{Y}_{A}\rightarrow Y_{A}^{\geq 1}
branched along the surface
Y_{A}^{\geq 2}
.
\tilde{Y}_{A}
is a smooth IHS variety, called the double EPW sextic.

Iliev and Manivel described in
[
14
]
another way to construct the EPW sextic associated with a GM variety
X
. Define
\widetilde{\mathrm{Disc}}(X)
to be the subscheme of
\mathbb{P}(V_{6})
of singular quadrics containing
X
. It is a hypersurface of degree
n+5
and the multiplicity of the hyperplane
\mathbb{P}(V_{5})
of Plücker quadrics is at least
n-1
. Then

\mathrm{Disc}(X):=\widetilde{\mathrm{Disc}}(X)-(n-1)\mathbb{P}(V_{5})

equals the EPW sextic
Y_{A}
.

One can use the same construction for the Lagrangian subspace
A^{\vee}\subset\bigwedge^{3}V_{6}^{\vee}
, the corresponding
\tilde{Y}_{A^{\vee}}
is called the dual double EPW sextic. We mainly deal with the dual double EPW sextic associated with a GM variety and abbreviate it as
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
in the following sections.

There is a bijection between the GM data sets and the Lagrangian data sets by
[
4
, Theorem 2.2]
.
However, the GM fourfolds associated with a fixed dual EPW sextic are not unique. Debarre and Kuznetsov proved the following
[
7
]
[
4
, Theorem 2.6]
:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
M^{GM}
and
M^{EPW}
be the coarse moduli spaces of ordinary GM
n
-folds and EPW sextics. Then there exists a surjective morphism:

\pi:M^{GM}\longrightarrow M^{EPW}

with fibre
\pi^{-1}(A^{\vee})=Y_{A}^{5-n}
.

If two GM fourfolds are in the same fiber of
\pi
, we say that they are
period partners
.

3.4.
Lines and Conics on
X

In this subsection, we gather some facts about lines and conics on a general GM fourfold
X
following
[
14
, Section 3]
and
[
6
]
.

Let
F(X)
be the Hilbert scheme of conics and
F_{1}(X)
the Hilbert scheme of lines on
X
.
F(X)
is a smooth fivefold.
For a conic
c\subset X
, we denote by
\langle c\rangle
the plane spanned by
c
. For
V_{4}\subset V_{5}
, we set

S_{V_{4}}=\operatorname{Gr}(2,V_{4})\cap H\cap Q\subset\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{2}V_{4})\cap H\cong\mathbb{P}^{4},

which is a degree
4
Del Pezzo surface.

The hyperplane
H
can be viewed as a two-form
\omega
on
V_{5}
with a one-dimensional kernel
W_{1}
. Let
V_{i}\subset V_{5}
be a subspace of dimension
i
. There are three types of conics on
X
:

(1)

a
\tau
-conic
: The plane
\langle c\rangle
spanned by the conic
c
is not contained in
\operatorname{Gr}(2,V_{5})
. The normal bundle of a smooth
\tau
-conic
c
is
N_{c/X}=\mathcal{O}_{c}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{c}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{c}(1)
. A general conic is a
\tau
-conic.

(2)

a
\rho
-conic
: The plane
\langle c\rangle
is of the form
\mathbb{P}(V_{3}^{\vee})
and contained in
H
, or equivalently,
W_{1}\subset V_{3}
and
V_{3}
is isotropic for
\omega
.
For a general
X
, the normal bundle of a smooth
\rho
-conic
c
is
N_{c/X}=\mathcal{O}_{c}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{c}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{c}(1)
. They form a
3
-dimensional subvariety in
F(X)
.

(3)

a
\sigma
-conic
: The plane
\langle c\rangle
is of the form
\mathbb{P}(V_{1}\bigwedge V_{4})
and contained in
H
, or equivalently,
V_{4}\subset V_{1}^{\perp_{\omega}}
. For
V_{1}\neq W_{1}
, dim
V_{1}^{\perp_{\omega}}=4
. They are parameterized by
Bl_{[W_{1}]}(\mathbb{P}(V_{5}))
and form a
4
-dimensional subvariety in
F(X)
.

We see that all the
\rho
-conics (resp.
\sigma
-conics) are represented by the same class in
\operatorname{CH}_{1}(X)
and denote it by
\rho
(resp.
\sigma
) for abuse of notations.

Lines on
X
are related to the double EPW sextic
\tilde{Y}_{A}
.
The general
\sigma
-conics which are unions of two lines are parameterized by

[V_{1}]\in Y_{A}^{\geq 1}\cap\mathbb{P}(V_{5})

and a general line is one of the components of a degenerate
\sigma
-conic by
[
6
, Theorem 4.7]
. More precisely, the Hilbert scheme of lines
F_{1}(X)
is the blow-up of
\tilde{Y}_{A}\times_{\mathbb{P}(V_{6})}\mathbb{P}(V_{5})
at a point.
There is a rational involution

\iota_{1}:F_{1}(X)\dashrightarrow F_{1}(X),

with
l\cup\iota_{1}(l)
a
\sigma
-conic.

The Fano variety of conics
F(X)
admits birationally a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibration to
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
in the following way. A conic
c\subset X
spans a degree
2
surface in
\mathbb{P}(V_{5})
, which is degenerate and hence contained in some
\mathbb{P}(V_{4})
. Therefore,
c\subset S_{V_{4}}
. One may check that if
c
is not of
\rho
-type, the corresponding
V_{4}
is unique.

The linear system
|c|
in
S_{V_{4}}
is one-dimensional, which defines the birational
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibration to the dual double EPW sextic, see
[
14
, Proposition 4.18]
:

\alpha:F(X)\rightarrow\tilde{Y}^{\vee}.

The fibration maps all
\sigma
and
\rho
conics to two points
y_{1}
and
y_{2}=\iota(y_{1})
on
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
and further to the Plücker hyperplane
[V_{5}]\in Y\subset\mathbb{P}(V_{6}^{\vee})
, by
[
14
, Lemma 4.12]
.

For the second part, assume that

. Let

be the intersection number of

\begin{split}0=&G\cdot n^{2}(I^{2}_{*}([c_{1}])-I^{2}_{*}([c_{2}]))\\
=&G\cdot n^{2}(aW+I\cdot A+B+C)([c_{1}]-[c_{2}])\\
=&G\cdot an^{2}W([c_{1}]-[c_{2}])\\
=&amn^{2}\cdot(\iota([c_{1}])-\iota([c_{2}])),\end{split}

here we view the cycles in

as morphisms from
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(F)
to
\operatorname{CH}_{1}(F)
. Then

\iota([c_{1}])\sim\iota([c_{2}])

again by the torsion-freeness of

and the fact that

Combining Lemma

Theorem 4.14
.

The invariant locus

of the involution is a constant cycle surface on

.

Thus by (

in

For a very general

is another constant cycle surface, then for any point

,

is rationally equivalent to

Proof.

It suffices to show that for a very general
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
, any two surfaces on
\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
intersect. We obtain this by showing that

\tilde{Y}^{\vee}
is strictly nef, i.e., having a positive intersection number with any non-zero effective

Let

be the space of

-coefficients, which is a

5.1.
Basics of the filtration

A uniruled divisor on a

-dimensional variety

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}

We define a filtration on

for a general GM fourfold induced by the Beauville–Voisin filtration on

. In the rest of the paper, we study some properties of the filtration.

Definition 5.2

The EPW sextics associated with period partners

and

are the same. We have an immediate corollary according to the definition:

For any

iff
\Psi^{\prime}_{*}(t)\in{\rm S}_{i}(X^{\prime})
, where

is similarly defined as

.

By the proof of Proposition

, there exists an integer

such that

is a linear sum of conics for every

that every conic is rationally equivalent to a sum of conics in

Let

be the

-anti-invariant part of

and
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})^{+}_{\rm hom}

\iota
-invariant part.

We show that the filtration on

can actually be defined on the

-anti-invariant part

Proposition 5.4
.

The morphism
\Psi:\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})\rightarrow\operatorname{CH}_{1}(X)
is zero on
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})^{+}_{\rm hom}
and an isomorphism onto

when restricting to

Proof.

Due to the divisibility of

, the elements in

are of the form
\sum(t_{i}+\iota(t_{i})-2o)
and elements in

are of the form

.
This yields

is zero on

by Lemma
4.3
.

For the second part, there exists an integer

, such that for any

,

is a linear combination of conics. Then
\operatorname{CH}_{1}(X)_{\rm hom}
is generated by conics by the divisibility of
\operatorname{CH}_{1}(X)_{\rm hom}
.
Therefore, it suffices to show that
\Psi
is injective on
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\tilde{Y}^{\vee})^{-}
.

Let
G\subset F
be an ample divisor and
G.L=m
, where

is a general fibre of

. Denote by

and

and

If we have the relation

it implies that:

\sum(D_{c_{i}}-D_{\iota(c_{i})})=\sum(D_{c_{i}^{\prime}}-D_{\iota(c_{i}^{\prime})}).

By Lemma

, we have:

G\cdot(D_{c_{i}}-D_{\iota(c_{i})})\cdot(D_{c_{i}}+D_{\iota(c_{i})})=G\cdot(D_{c_{i}}^{2}-D_{\iota(c_{i})}^{2})=4m(\iota(t_{i})-t_{i}).

via the isomorphism

.
By Lemma

, we know that

is constant in

we denote it by

. Therefore we obtain

∎

On the Rainbow Ramsey theorem, and the Canonical Ramsey Theorem for pairs without AC
Amitayu Banerjee, Alexa Gopaulsingh, Zalán Molnár

Abstract
. Fix m , n ∈ ω \ { 0 , 1 } m,n\in\omega\backslash\{0,1\} . In set theory without the Axiom of Choice ( 𝖠𝖢 \mathsf{AC} ), we study the deductive strength of a generalized version of the Rainbow Ramsey theorem (“If X X is an infinite set and χ : [ X ] m → C \chi:[X]^{m}\rightarrow C is an n n -bounded coloring for some infinite set C C , then there is an infinite Y ⊆ X Y\subseteq X which is polychromatic for χ \chi ”), we abbreviate by 𝖱𝖱𝖳 n m \mathsf{RRT}^{m}_{n} , and the Canonical Ramsey Theorem for pairs introduced by Erdős and Rado, concerning their interrelation with several weak choice forms. In this direction, we extend some work by Justin Palumbo from 2013. Moreover, we partially answer two open questions concerning the relation of Kurepa’s Theorem (“Every poset such that all of its antichains are finite and all of its chains are countable is countable”) with weak choice forms.

Definition 2.1
.

As usual,

A subset

is known as its

. A subset

is

if for every

there is an element
c\in C
such that
x\leq c
. A set

is called

if
\aleph_{0}\not\leq|X|
i.e., if there is no one-to-one function
f:\omega\rightarrow X
. Otherwise,

is called
Dedekind-infinite
. An infinite set

is

if
X
cannot be written as a
disjoint union of two infinite subsets.
A set

is called
uncountable
if
|X|\not\leq\aleph_{0}
. A family
\mathscr{A}
of sets is called a
\Delta
-system
if there is a set

such that for any two
distinct elements
x,y
of
\mathscr{A}
,
x\cap y=r
. The set
r
is called the
root
of the
\Delta
-system.
The coloring

is

for each

.
If
\chi:[X]^{m}\rightarrow C

if for all

\chi(y_{1})=\chi(y_{2})

if for all
z_{1},z_{2}\in[Z]^{m}

\chi(z_{1})\neq\chi(z_{2})

[Z]^{n}
receives a different color. A graph

consists of a set
V_{G}
of vertices and a set
E_{G}\subseteq[V_{G}]^{2}
of edges. An
independent set
of

is a set of vertices of
G
, no two of which are adjacent vertices. A
maximal independent set
is an independent set that is not a subset of any other independent set.

(b).

(a,b_{i})x\not=x
for any
b_{i}\in P_{2}\backslash\{b\}
.

(c).

(a,b_{i})x\not=(a,b_{j})x
for any
b_{i},b_{j}\in P_{2}
such that
b_{i}\neq b_{j}
.

Proof.

(a)
. Suppose
(a,b_{i})x=(a,b)x
for some
b_{i}\in P_{2}\backslash\{b\}
. Then,

(a,b)(b_{i},a)(a,b_{i})x=(a,b)(b_{i},a)(a,b)x\implies(a,b)x=(b,b_{i})x
.

However,
(b,b_{i})\in
fix
{}_{\mathscr{G}}(E_{2})
and so
(b,b_{i})x=x
, while
(a,b)x\not=x
which is a contradiction.

(b)
. Suppose
(a,b_{i})x=x
for some
b_{i}\in P_{2}\backslash\{b\}
. Thus
(a,b_{i})(b,b_{i})(a,b_{i})x=(a,b_{i})(b,b_{i})x
.
Since
(b,b_{i})\in
fix
{}_{\mathscr{G}}(E_{2})
and so
(b,b_{i})x=x
, we have
(a,b)x=(a,b_{i})x
, which contradicts
(a)
.

(c)
. Assume
(a,b_{i})x=(a,b_{j})x
for some
b_{i},b_{j}\in P_{2}
such that
b_{i}\neq b_{j}
.
Thus,
(a,b_{i})(a,b_{j})(a,b_{i})x=(a,b_{i})(a,b_{j})(a,b_{j})x
.
Since
(b_{i},b_{j})\in
fix
{}_{\mathscr{G}}(E_{2})
and so
(b_{i},b_{j})x=x
, we have

(b_{i},b_{j})x=(a,b_{i})x\implies x=(a,b_{i})x

which contradicts
(b)
.
∎

We note that for any
b_{i}\in P_{2}
,
\{a,b_{i}\}\subseteq P
. Thus
(a,b_{i})\in
fix
{}_{\mathscr{G}}(E_{1})
. Since
E_{1}
is a support of
S
, we have
(a,b_{i})S=S
, and so
(a,b_{i})y\in S
for any
y\in S
. Thus,

T=\{(a,b_{i})x:b_{i}\in P_{2}\}

is an infinite subset of
S
by claim
6.6
(c)
.
Since the set of atoms
A
has no infinite amorphous subset in
\mathscr{V}_{fp}
by Lemma
6.3
(2), and
P_{2}
is an infinite subset of
A
, we can pick a partition of
P_{2}
into two infinite sets, say
P_{2,1}
and
P_{2,2}
. Define,

U=\{(a,d_{i})x:d_{i}\in P_{2,1}\}
, and

V=\{(a,e_{i})x:e_{i}\in P_{2,2}\}
.

Let
E_{3}
be a partition whose blocks are
P_{1}\backslash\{a\}
,
P_{2,1}\cup\{a\}
,
P_{2,2}
, and all blocks of
E_{2}\backslash\{P_{1},P_{2}\}
.
Define
Y=\{\pi(x):\pi\in
fix
{}_{\mathscr{G}}(E_{3})\}
.
By claim
6.6
(c)
,
U
and
V
are infinite sets. Since
U\subseteq Y
and
V\subseteq S\backslash Y
, we have
Y
and
S\backslash Y
are infinite. Moreover,
E_{3}
is a support of
Y
and
S
, as
E_{1}
is a support of
S
and
E_{3}
is a refinement of
E_{1}
. Thus,
E_{3}
is a support of
S\backslash Y
.
Consequently,
Y
and
S\backslash Y
are infinite sets in
\mathscr{V}_{fp}
, and
Y
and
S\backslash Y
form a partition of
S
.

(4). In
\mathscr{V}_{fp}
, let
G=(V_{G},E_{G})
be a graph and
f:[V_{G}]^{2}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
be a coloring such that all sets monochromatic in color
0
are countable, and all sets monochromatic in color
1
are finite. If
V_{G}
is well-orderable then we are done by Fact
3.1
(12). Otherwise, by Lemma
6.2
, there exists a bijection from an infinite subset
A^{\prime}
of
A
onto some
H\subset V_{G}
.
Define the following partition of
[H]^{2}
:

X=\{\{a,b\}\in[H]^{2}:f(\{a,b\})=0\}
,
Y=\{\{a,b\}\in[H]^{2}:f(\{a,b\})=1\}
.
Since
(H,E_{G}\restriction H)
is an infinite graph where all sets monochromatic in color

are finite, there is no infinite subset
B^{\prime}\subseteq H
such that
[B^{\prime}]^{2}\subseteq Y
. Since
\mathsf{RT}^{2}_{2}
holds in
\mathscr{V}_{fp}
, there is an infinite subset

such that

. So
(H,E_{G}\restriction H)
has an infinite set monochromatic in color

.
By assumption,

is a countably infinite subset of

A^{\prime}
is Dedekind-infinite in
\mathscr{V}_{fp}
since

. Consequently, the set
A
of atoms is Dedekind-infinite in

, which contradicts the fact that

is a Dedekind-finite set in

(1)).
∎

Concluding Remarks

Remark 7.1
.

In
[
17
, a part of Question 6.3]
, it is asked whether
\mathsf{AC^{LO}}
implies
\mathsf{CAC}^{\aleph_{0}}_{1}
in
\mathsf{ZFA}
. We show that
\mathsf{AC^{LO}}
does not imply
\mathsf{CAC}^{\aleph_{0}}_{1}
in
\mathsf{ZFA}
.
Recently, Tachtsis
[
16
, Theorem 3]
constructed the following permutation model to prove that
\mathsf{AC^{LO}}
does not imply
\mathsf{EDM}
in
\mathsf{ZFA}
:
We start with a model
M
of
\mathsf{ZFA}+\mathsf{AC}
with an
\aleph_{1}
-sized set
A
of atoms which is a disjoint union of
\aleph_{1}
unordered pairs, so that
A=\bigcup\{A_{i}:i<\aleph_{1}\}
,
|A_{i}|=2
for all
i<\aleph_{1}
, and
A_{i}\cap A_{j}=\emptyset
for all
i,j<\aleph_{1}
with
i\neq j
. Let
\mathscr{G}
be the group of all permutations
\phi
of
A
such that
(\forall i<\aleph_{1})(\exists j<\aleph_{1})(\phi(A_{i})=A_{j})
. Let
\mathscr{F}
be the normal filter of subgroups of
\mathscr{G}
generated by the pointwise stabilizers fix
{}_{G}(E)
, where
E=\bigcup\{A_{i}:i\in I\}
for some
I\in[\aleph_{1}]^{\leq\aleph_{0}}
. Let
\mathscr{N}
be the permutation model determined by
M
,
\mathscr{G}
and
\mathscr{F}
. In
\mathscr{N}
, the following holds (cf.
[
16
, claims in the proof of Theorem 3]
):

(1)

\mathsf{AC^{LO}}
holds.

(2)

The power set of
\mathscr{A}=\{A_{i}:i<\aleph_{1}\}
consists exactly of the countable
and the co-countable subsets of
\mathscr{A}
.

(3)

No co-countable subset of
A
has a choice function in
\mathscr{N}
.

Modifying the proof of
[
16
, claim 3 of Theorem 3]
), one can see that
\mathsf{CAC}^{\aleph_{0}}_{1}
fails in
\mathscr{N}
. Define a binary relation
\leq
on the uncountable set
A=\bigcup\mathscr{A}
as follows: for all
a,b\in A
, let
a\leq b
if and only if
a=b
or
a\in A_{n}
,
b\in A_{m}
and
n<m
. It is easy to see that
\leq
is a partial order on
A
. If
C\subset A
is an antichain in
(A,\leq)
, then
C\subseteq A_{i}
for some
i\in\aleph_{1}
. Thus, all antichains in
(A,\leq)
are finite as
A_{i}
is finite for all
i\in\aleph_{1}
. Since any two elements of
A
are
\leq
-comparable if and only if they belong to distinct
A_{i}
’s, we can see that no chain in
(A,\leq)
is uncountable by (2) and (3).

Remark 7.2
.

In
[
17
, a part of Question 6.4]
, it is asked whether
\mathsf{WOAM}
implies Kurepa’s theorem (
\mathsf{CAC}^{\aleph_{0}}_{1}
) in
\mathsf{ZF}
. The
answer to the above question is in the affirmative
. Firstly,
we recall the following known facts:

(1)

The statement “Every infinite poset

such that

is well-orderable has either an infinite chain or an infinite anti-chain” holds in
\mathsf{ZF}
(see
[

(2)

\mathsf{CAC}^{\aleph_{0}}

(3)

\mathsf{CAC}^{\aleph_{0}}_{1}

\mathsf{ZF}
(see
[
17
, Theorem 8(1)]
).

We note that
\mathsf{CAC^{\aleph_{0}}}
implies
\mathsf{CAC}
in
\mathsf{ZF}
. Let

be an infinite poset. If all chains and all antichains in

Thus, by (2),
\mathsf{WOAM}
implies
\mathsf{CAC}

\mathsf{ZF}
and so
\mathsf{WOAM}

\mathsf{CAC}^{\aleph_{0}}_{1}

by applying (3).

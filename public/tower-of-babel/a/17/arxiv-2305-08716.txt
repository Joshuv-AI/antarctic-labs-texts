Abstract
. A stacked d d -sphere S S is the boundary complex of a stacked ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -ball, which is obtained by taking cone over a free d d -face repeatedly from a ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -simplex. A stacked sphere S S is called linear if every cone is taken over a face added in the previous step. In this paper, we study the transversal number of facets of stacked d d -spheres, denoted by τ ⁡ ( S ) \tau(S) , which is the minimum number of vertices intersecting with all facets. Briggs, Dobbins and Lee showed that the transversal ratio of a stacked d d -sphere is bounded above by 2 d + 2 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) \frac{2}{d+2}+o(1) and can be as large as 2 d + 3 \frac{2}{d+3} . We improve the lower bound by constructing linear stacked d d -spheres with transversal ratio 6 3 ​ d + 8 \frac{6}{3d+8} and general stacked d d -spheres with transversal ratio 2 ​ d + 3 ( d + 2 ) 2 \frac{2d+3}{(d+2)^{2}} . Finally, we show that 6 3 ​ d + 8 \frac{6}{3d+8} is optimal for linear stacked 2 2 -spheres, that is, the transversal ratio is at most 3 7 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) \frac{3}{7}+o(1) for linear stacked 2 2 -spheres.



1.
Introduction

A
hypergraph
on
V
is a collection of subsets of
V
.
For a hypergraph
H
on
V
, we say
V=V(H)
is the
vertex set
of
H
and each element
e\in H
is called an
edge
of
H
.
We say a vertex set
T\subset V
is a
transversal
of
H
if
e\cap T\neq\emptyset
for every edge
e\in H
and we define the
transversal number

\tau(H)
of
H
as the minimum size of a transversal of
H
.
In general, the transversal number of a hypergraph can be as large as the size of the vertex set up to a constant difference: for instance, if
H=\binom{V}{d}
, then
\tau(H)=|V|-d+1
.
When we have a given class of hypergraphs, it is natural to ask how large the “transversal ratio”
\tau(H)/|V(H)|\in(0,1)
can be over all hypergraphs
H
in the class.

Among various classes of hypergraphs, we are mainly interested in facet hypergraphs of polytopes and their transversal numbers.
(For more references related to transversals of hypergraphs from geometry, see
[
1
,
4
]
.)
Let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
d
-dimensional polytope with the vertex set
V=V(P)
.
A
face
of
P
is given as
h\cap P
for some hyperplane
h
such that one of the two closed half-spaces of
h
contains
P
.
A
facet
of
P
is a
(d-1)
-dimensional face of
P
.
We define the
facet hypergraph
of
P
as the hypergraph on
V
whose edges are the vertex sets of the facets of
P
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{d}
be the class of all facet hypergraphs of
d
-dimensional polytopes.
Then how large can the transversal ratio
\tau(H)/|V(H)|
be over all
H\in\mathcal{H}_{d}
?
It is known that
\tau(H)\leq|V(H)|/2
for all
H\in\mathcal{H}_{3}
, which can be easily verified using the
4
-color theorem.
[
2
]

However, if
d\geq 4
, then answering this question seems challenging.
It is even unknown whether the transversal ratio can be bounded away from
1
for any
d\geq 4
.

Question 1

(Question 1,
[
2
]
)
.

Let
d\geq 4
be an integer.
How large can the transversal ratio
{\tau(H)}/{n}
be for sufficiently large
n
and
H\in\mathcal{H}_{d}
on
n
vertices?

To answer Question
1
, we can ask whether there is a constant
c=c(d)<1
such that for sufficiently large
n
and every
H\in\mathcal{H}_{d}
on
n
vertices,
\tau(H)\leq cn
.
A quick reduction is that to find such a constant
c(d)
, it suffices to consider only
simplicial polytopes
whose facets are all simplices.
This is because for a given polytope
P
, by moving some vertices of
P
slightly towards its interior, we get a simplicial polytope whose facet hypergraph is isomorphic to a refinement of that of
P
.

In
[
2
]
, the authors studied the transversal ratio for classes of simplicial polytopes.
As a special class of simplicial polytopes, they also studied the class of “stacked balls”. (Some literature, for instance
[
6
]
, refers to these as stacked polytopes.)

A
simplicial complex

K
on
V
is a collection of subsets of
V
that is closed under taking subsets, and each element in
K
is called a
face
of
K
and a
facet
of
K
is a maximal face of
K
.
A face
\sigma\in K
is called

if there is an unique facet containing
\sigma
in
K
.
The
dimension
of a face
\sigma\in K
is
\dim(\sigma)=|\sigma|-1
and the
dimension
of
K
is the maximum dimension of its faces.
We say a simplicial complex
K
on
V
is a
simplex
if
K=2^{V}
and a
d
-dimensional simplex is just called a
d
-simplex
.
When
\sigma
is a set with
d+1
vertices, then we denote by
\overline{\sigma}
the
d
-simplex on
\sigma
.
The
facet hypergraph

H(K)
of a simplicial complex
K
on
V
is a hypergraph on
V
whose edges are the facets of
K
.

H(K):=\{\sigma\subset V:\sigma\text{ is a facet of }K\}.

The
transversal number

\tau(K)
of
K
is the transversal number of its facet hypergraph, i.e.
\tau(K)=\tau(H(K))
.

A
stacked
d
-ball
is a
d
-dimensional simplicial complex that can be obtained from a
d
-simplex by repeatedly gluing another
d
-simplex onto its
(d-1)
-dimensional free faces.
More precisely, it can be described as follows.

Definition 2
.

A simplicial complex
B
is called a stacked
d
-ball if there is a sequence of simplicial complexes
B_{1},\dots,B_{m}=B
and a sequence of
d
-simplices
(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m})
satisfying the following:

(i)

B_{1}=\sigma_{1}
.

(ii)

For each
i\in[m-1]
,
B_{i+1}=B_{i}\cup\sigma_{i+1}
and
B_{i}\cap\sigma_{i+1}=\eta_{i+1}
is a simplex on a
(d-1)
-dimensional free face of
B_{i}
.

We denote by
\Gamma(B)=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m})
the sequence of
d
-simplices constructing
B
.

Let
B
be a stacked
(d+1)
-ball where
\Gamma(B)=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m})
for some
(d+1)
-simplices
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m}
.
Then we have
|V(B)|=d+m+1
.
We can consider the
d
-dimensional simplicial complex

on

where the facets of
S
are the
d
-dimensional free faces of
B
, and we call

is a
stacked
d
-sphere
and denote

.
If

and

, then we also write

.
Note that a stacked
(d+1)
-ball can be considered as a
(d+1)
-dimensional polytope, and a stacked

-sphere is a special case of a
simplicial
d
-sphere
, which is a simplicial complex homeomorphic to the
d
-dimensional sphere.

Now, we define the
dual graph

of a stacked
(d+1)
-ball

\Gamma(B)=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m})
.
The dual graph

is a graph on the vertex set

and two vertices

are adjacent if and only if
\sigma_{i}\cap\sigma_{j}
is a

Equivalently, when we construct

\sigma_{j}
is attached to

\eta_{j}\subset\sigma_{i}
.
Note that by the definition of a stacked ball,

is a tree for every stacked ball

is connected and does not contain a cycle.
Now, as a special case, we say a stacked
d
-sphere

Our main concern is the following question.

Question 3

(Question 3,
[
2
]
)
.

What are the following values for a fixed
d\geq 2
?

(1)

\displaystyle T(d):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max\left\{\frac{\tau(S)}{n}:S\text{ is a stacked }d\text{-sphere on }n\text{ vertices}\right\}
.

(2)

\displaystyle T^{\text{path}}(d):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max\left\{\frac{\tau(S)}{n}:S\text{ is a linear stacked }d\text{-sphere on }n\text{ vertices}\right\}
.

Briggs et al.
[
2
]
showed that

for every
d\geq 2
.
They also constructed an infinite family of linear stacked
d
-spheres that shows

, and asked whether this is the optimal value.
We figured out that this is actually not true, by constructing an infinite family of linear stacked
d
-spheres showing

.

Theorem 4

(Lower bound)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
be integers.
There exists a linear stacked
d
-sphere
S_{d}
on
(3d+8)k
vertices such that
\tau(S_{d})\geq 6k
.

We also proved that the bound
\frac{6}{3d+8}
is actually optimal for linear stacked
2
-spheres, that is,
T^{\text{path}}(2)=\frac{3}{7}
.

Theorem 5

(Upper bound)
.

Let
n\geq 4
be an integer and let
S
be a linear stacked
2
-sphere on
n
vertices. Then there is a transversal of

with at most
\lceil\frac{3n}{7}\rceil
vertices.

If we consider general stacked
d
-spheres, then Theorem
4
also gives a lower bound
\frac{6}{3d+8}
of

since

.
We could find a better bound, by constructing an infinite family of stacked
d
-spheres showing

for

and
T(2)\geq\frac{6}{13}
.
Note that this implies that
T(2)
is strictly larger than
T^{\text{path}}(2)=\frac{3}{7}
.

Theorem 6

(Lower bound)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
be integers.

(1)

There exists a stacked
d
-sphere
S_{2}
on
13k
vertices such that
\tau(S_{2})\geq 6k
.

(2)

There exists a stacked
d
-sphere

on

\tau(S_{d})\geq(2d+3)k
.

We remark that there exists simplicial

-polytopes with transversal ratio
\frac{2}{d+2}
on arbitrary many vertices
[
2
]
.
Moreover, even-dimensional cyclic polytopes can have transversal ratio arbitrary close to
\frac{1}{2}

[
2
,
5
]
.
Some

require more vertices to transverse all their facets;

[

and simplicial

-spheres with transversal ratio

On the other hand, stacked spheres have relatively small transversal number since they are constructed in a more restrictive way.

2.
Constructions for lower bounds

In this section, we construct infinite families of stacked
d
-spheres showing Theorem
4
and Theorem
6
respectively.
The constructions are obtained by “gluing” a number of copies of certain stacked spheres of small size.
We first present lemmas that describe how to glue two stacked spheres.
A similar idea to the following lemma was used in

Lemma 7
.

Let
S,T
be stacked
d
-spheres with
|V(S)|=m
and
|V(T)|=n
.
Suppose there are facets
f\in S
and
g\in T
such that
\tau(S-\{f\})=k
and
\tau(T-\{g\})=\ell
.
Then there is a stacked
d
-sphere

on
m+n
vertices and a facet

such that
\tau(K-\{h\})\geq k+\ell
.

Proof.

We assume
V(S)
and
V(T)
are disjoint.
Let
S=\partial(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m-d-1})
and
T=\partial(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n-d-1})
for some
(d+1)
-simplices
\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}
.
We will first show that we may assume that
f\in\alpha_{m-d-1}
and
g\in\beta_{1}
by reordering and relabeling
\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}
’s.
Suppose
g\in\beta_{i}
for some
i\in[n-d-1]
.
Let
B
be the stacked
(d+1)
-ball where
\Gamma(B)=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n-d-1})
.
Consider the dual graph
G(B)
of
B
.
We can take an ordering

of

such that

and
(\beta_{j_{1}},\dots,\beta_{j_{n-d-1}})
defines the same stacked
(d+1)
-ball

.
(For instance, take a depth-first search ordering of

starting from

Therefore, by reordering and relabeling, we may assume
g\in\beta_{1}
.
Also assume
f\in\alpha_{m-d-1}
following the same logic.

Let
f=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{d+1}\}
and

For each

, let
\gamma_{i}

-simplex on

Let

Then

Since

∎

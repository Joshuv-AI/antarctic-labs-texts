The implication of J / ψ → ( γ + ) ​ invisible J/\psi\to(\gamma+){\rm invisible} for the effective field theories of neutrino and dark matter Preprint: CPPC-2021-05
Tong Li, Xiao-Dong Ma, Michael A. Schmidt, Rui-Jia Zhang

Abstract
We study the implication of J / ψ J/\psi decay into invisible particles for light sterile neutrino and sub-GeV dark matter (DM). The low-energy effective field theories (EFTs) are used for the description of general neutrino interactions and the Dirac fermion DM coupled to charm quark. For J / ψ → γ + invisible J/\psi\to\gamma+{\rm invisible} , we perform the likelihood fits for the individual neutrino and DM operators with distinct Lorentz structures and photon spectra. The limits on the decay branching fractions are obtained for different neutrino or DM scenarios and then converted to the lower bounds on the new energy scales. The most stringent bounds on the energy scale in neutrino and DM EFTs are 12.8 GeV and 11.6 GeV, respectively. The purely invisible decay J / ψ → invisible J/\psi\to{\rm invisible} provides complementary constraints on the effective operators. The relevant bound on the energy scale is above 100 GeV for the dipole operators. We also evaluate the limit on the DM-nucleon scattering cross section converted from J / ψ J/\psi data. The data of J / ψ J/\psi invisible decays are sensitive to the light DM mass range where the DM direct detection experiments cannot probe yet. The future Super Tau Charm Factory after one year run can push the limits down by two orders of magnitude.

VI
Conclusions

The heavy quarkonium experiments can help us to study the possible NP associated with heavy quarks and provide complementary constraints on the NP scale where the high-energy colliders lose sensitivity.
Inspired by the searches for
J/\psi
decays into invisible particles, we investigate the implication for light sterile neutrino and sub-GeV dark matter in effective field theories.

We make use of the low-energy EFTs for general neutrino operators up to dim-6 and the Dirac fermion DM operators up to dim-7.
For
J/\psi\to\gamma+{\rm invisible}
decay, we perform the likelihood fits for the individual LNEFT and DMEFT operators with distinct Lorentz structures and photon spectra.
The limits on the decay branching fractions are obtained for different neutrino or DM scenarios and then converted to the lower bounds on the new energy scales.
The most stringent bound on the energy scale in LNEFT comes from the lepton-number-conserving operators
\mathcal{O}_{c\nu 1,2}^{V}
and turns out to be 12.8 GeV.
For DMEFT, the most stringent bound on the energy scale is 11.6 GeV for the axialvector operators
\mathcal{O}_{c\chi 3,4}
.
The purely invisible decay
J/\psi\to{\rm invisible}
provides complementary constraints on the effective operators. The most stringent bound on the energy scale is above 100 GeV for the dipole operators.

Finally, we evaluate the limit on the DM-nucleon scattering cross section converted from
J/\psi
data. The invisible decay of
J/\psi
is sensitive to the light DM mass range where the DM direct detection experiments cannot probe yet.
For the case of only charm quark coupling and
m_{\chi}=10^{-2}
GeV for instance, the BESIII limit of cross section
\sigma^{\rm SI}_{\chi c5}
becomes
1.2\times 10^{-38}~{\rm cm}^{2}
and the STCF with
3.4\times 10^{12}
samples of
J/\psi

Zhou
can reach a sensitivity down to
6.3\times 10^{-41}~{\rm cm}^{2}
after one year of running. Under the assumption of universal quark coupling, the SI cross section limit for
\mathcal{O}_{\chi q1}
from
J/\psi\to{\rm invisible}
is stronger by one order of magnitude than that for
\mathcal{O}_{\chi q5}
from
J/\psi\to\gamma+{\rm invisible}
.
For the SD cross section under the assumption of universal quark coupling and
m_{\chi}=10^{-2}
GeV, the BESIII limit reaches
6.5\times 10^{-37}~{\rm cm}^{2}
and the STCF projection is
3.3\times 10^{-39}~{\rm cm}^{2}
.

Appendix A
The proof of redundant DM operators

We prove that the four DM operators
\hat{\cal O}_{\chi q15},\hat{\cal O}_{\chi q16},\hat{\cal O}_{\chi q17},\hat{\cal O}_{\chi q18}
in Eq. (
22
) are actually redundant operators which can be shifted into the other operators by
the Dirac gamma matrix identity (GI) and the equation of motion (EoM) of DM fields. They all have a derivative acting on the DM current such as

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}(\overline{\chi}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\chi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\overline{\chi}\overleftarrow{\partial_{\mu}}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\chi)+(\overline{\chi}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\chi)

(57)

\displaystyle\overset{\rm GI}{=}

\displaystyle(\overline{\chi}i\overleftrightarrow{\partial}^{\nu}\chi)+(\overline{\chi}i\overleftarrow{\not{\partial}}\gamma^{\nu}\chi)-(\overline{\chi}\gamma^{\nu}i\not{\partial}\chi)

\displaystyle\overset{\rm EoM}{=}

\displaystyle(\overline{\chi}i\overleftrightarrow{\partial}^{\nu}\chi)-2m_{\chi}(\overline{\chi}\gamma^{\nu}\chi)\;,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}(\overline{\chi}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}\chi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\overline{\chi}\overleftarrow{\partial_{\mu}}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}\chi)+(\overline{\chi}\gamma_{5}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\chi)

(58)

\displaystyle\overset{\rm GI}{=}

\displaystyle(\overline{\chi}i\overleftrightarrow{\partial}^{\nu}\gamma_{5}\chi)+(\overline{\chi}i\overleftarrow{\not{\partial}}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma_{5}\chi)-(\overline{\chi}\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\nu}i\not{\partial}\chi)

\displaystyle\overset{\rm EoM}{=}

\displaystyle(\overline{\chi}i\overleftrightarrow{\partial}^{\nu}\gamma_{5}\chi)\;.

Then, it is straightforward to obtain

\displaystyle\hat{\cal O}_{\chi q15}

\displaystyle={\cal O}_{\chi q11}-2m_{\chi}{\cal O}_{\chi q1}\;,

\displaystyle\hat{\cal O}_{\chi q16}

\displaystyle={\cal O}_{\chi q12}\;,

The missed four independent operators are

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{\chi q15}

\displaystyle=(\overline{\chi}\gamma^{[\mu}i\overleftrightarrow{\partial}^{\nu]}\chi)(\overline{q}\sigma_{\mu\nu}q)\;,

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{\chi q15}

We then define the kinematic functions

\displaystyle f(\mu_{+})

\displaystyle=1-x_{\gamma}-\mu_{+}\;,

\displaystyle g(\mu_{-})

\displaystyle={1\over 2x_{\gamma}^{2}}\left[2(1-x_{b}+\mu_{-})(1-x_{c}-\mu_{-})+x_{\gamma}\left((1-x_{b}+\mu_{-})x_{b}+(1-x_{c}-\mu_{-})x_{c}\right)\right]\;,

\mu_{\pm}\equiv\mu_{b}\pm\mu_{c}=\mu_{\alpha}\pm\mu_{\beta}

\displaystyle\overline{|\mathcal{M}_{\nu_{\alpha}\bar{\nu}_{\beta}}|}^{2}

The integration over the kinematic functions results in the distribution functions

\displaystyle F(\mu_{+},\mu_{-})=\int dx_{b}f(\mu_{+})

\displaystyle={x_{\gamma}\sqrt{(1-x_{\gamma})(1-x_{\gamma}-2\mu_{+})+\mu_{-}^{2}}\over 1-x_{\gamma}}(1-x_{\gamma}-\mu_{+})\;,

\displaystyle G(\mu_{+},\mu_{-})=\int dx_{b}g(\mu_{-})

\displaystyle={x_{\gamma}\sqrt{(1-x_{\gamma})(1-x_{\gamma}-2\mu_{+})+\mu_{-}^{2}}\over 6(1-x_{\gamma})^{3}}

\displaystyle\times\left(2(1-x_{\gamma})^{2}(2-x_{\gamma})+(1-x_{\gamma}^{2})\mu_{+}-(5-x_{\gamma})\mu_{-}^{2}\right)\;.

(77)

For massless neutrinos, they are simplified to be

\displaystyle F(0,0)=x_{\gamma}(1-x_{\gamma})={2\over m_{J}^{2}}E_{\gamma}(m_{J}-2E_{\gamma})\;,\;\;G(0,0)={1\over 3}x_{\gamma}(2-x_{\gamma})={4\over 3m_{J}^{2}}E_{\gamma}(m_{J}-E_{\gamma})\;.

(78)

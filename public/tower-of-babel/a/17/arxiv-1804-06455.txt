MultiMesh Finite Elements with Flexible Mesh Sizes
August Johansson, Mats G. Larson, Anders Logg

Abstract
We analyze a new framework for expressing finite element methods on arbitrarily many intersecting meshes: multimesh finite element methods. The multimesh finite element method, first presented in [ 40 ] , enables the use of separate meshes to discretize parts of a computational domain that are naturally separate; such as the components of an engine, the domains of a multiphysics problem, or solid bodies interacting under the influence of forces from surrounding fluids or other physical fields. Furthermore, each of these meshes may have its own mesh parameter. In the present paper we study the Poisson equation and show that the proposed formulation is stable without assumptions on the relative sizes of the mesh parameters. In particular, we prove optimal order a priori error estimates as well as optimal order estimates of the condition number. Throughout the analysis, we trace the dependence of the number of intersecting meshes. Numerical examples are included to illustrate the stability of the method.

The contributions of this paper is first a generalization of the formulation in
[
40
]
for the Poisson equation to allow for meshes of arbitrary mesh sizes. In the formulation, each mesh has its own mesh size and can be placed in a general position. The
properties of the mesh arrangement is encoded in terms of the maximum number of overlapping meshes at any point in the domain. Naturally, this number may be much lower than the total number of meshes. The second contribution is a detailed analysis of the method. We carefully trace the dependency of the number of intersecting meshes in the coercivity of the method, in the error estimates and in the analysis of the condition number. The analysis holds for two and three dimension as well as for higher order elements, and extends previous works on cut finite elements for overlapping meshes and interface problems to much more general mesh arrangements and mesh sizes. We restrict ourselves to two dimensions in the numerical examples. See also

2.5
Notation for function spaces

•

Let
W^{s}_{p}(\omega)
denote the standard Sobolev spaces on
\omega\subset\Omega
with norm denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{W^{s}_{p}(\omega)}
and semi-norm
|\cdot|_{W^{s}_{p}(\omega)}
. The special case

is denoted by

and the space with

and zero trace is denoted by

(see also e.g.
[
13
,
14
]
). The Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is denoted by
|\cdot|_{N}
. The corresponding inner products are labeled accordingly. The same notation is used for the Lebesgue measure and absolute value. It will be clear from the argument which is used.

•

In order to define function spaces on the different meshes in the multimesh configuration we recall the concept of external direct sums of vector spaces, see for instance
[
35
]
. Let the external direct sum
X
of the vector spaces
X_{i}
be denoted by
X=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}X_{i}
, Here
X
is the vector space with elements
x\in X
which are
N+1
tuples of the form
x=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{N})
, where
x_{i}\in X_{i}
for
i=0,\dots,N
, equipped with component wise addition

x+y=(x_{0}+y_{0},\dots,x_{N}+y_{N})\qquad x,y\in X,

(2.3)

and scalar multiplication

tx=(tx_{0},\dots tx_{N})\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in X.

(2.4)

If the vector spaces
X_{i}
are equipped with inner products
(\cdot,\cdot)_{X_{i}}
and associated norms
\|\cdot\|_{X_{i}}
we let

(x,y)_{X}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}(x_{i},y_{i})_{X_{i}},\qquad\|x\|^{2}_{X}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|x_{i}\|^{2}_{X_{i}}.

(2.5)

Note that the direct sum is a purely algebraic construction, which is not dependent on the fact that the component spaces
X_{i}
have specific further properties for instance being function spaces, and since we always have a finite number of component spaces the direct sum is identical to the direct or Cartesian product of spaces, see
[
35
]
for further details.

•

Let
H^{s}(\Omega_{h,i})
,
s\geq 0
, be the standard Sobolev spaces of order
s
on the domain
\Omega_{h,i}
, for
i=0,\dots,N
, and define the so called multimesh Sobolev space as the external direct sum

\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}H^{s}(\Omega_{h,i}).

(2.6)

•

Let
V_{h,i}
be a continuous piecewise polynomial finite element space on the partition

\mathcal{K}_{h,i}
of
\Omega_{h,i}
, and let the multimesh finite element space
V_{h}
be the
external direct sum

V_{h}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}V_{h,i}

(2.7)

of the finite element spaces
V_{h,i}
,
i=0,\dots,N
. To simplify the presentation we assume homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on
\partial\Omega
with strong implementation of the boundary conditions in the finite element space, i.e.,

on
\partial\Omega
for

. Other boundary conditions can be enforced using standard techniques.

•

To represent a function

,
s\geq 0
, as a multimesh function let
E^{\dagger}:H^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}H^{s}(\Omega_{h,i})

be the embedding defined by

\displaystyle(E^{\dagger}v)_{i}=v|_{\Omega_{h,i}}\qquad i=0,\dots,N.

(2.8)

•

To interpret the multimesh function

as a function in

we define an embedding

(2.9)

We note that it follows from the definitions of

that

(2.10)

since

(EE^{\dagger}v)|_{\Omega_{i}}=(E^{\dagger}v)_{i}|_{\Omega_{i}}=(v|_{\Omega_{h,i}})|_{\Omega_{i}}=v|_{\Omega_{i}},

(2.11)

where we used the fact

in the last step.
For implementation purposes the following equivalent definition is useful

(Ev)(x)=\max_{\{i\,:\,x\in\Omega_{h,i}\}}v_{i}(x),

(2.12)

which corresponds to picking the top-most mesh in the case when

belongs to several meshes.

•

For
s\geq 0
we define the sum of
V_{h}
and
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}H^{s}(\Omega_{h,i})
by

V_{h}+\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}H^{s}(\Omega_{h,i})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}\left(V_{h,i}+H^{s}(\Omega_{h,i})\right),

(2.13)

where each of the component spaces

consists of functions of the form

with

and

3
Finite element method

As a model problem we consider the Poisson problem

\displaystyle-\Delta u

\displaystyle=f\qquad\text{in }\Omega,

(3.1a)

\displaystyle\qquad u

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

(3.1b)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a polygonal domain. The multimesh finite element method for (
3.1
) is to find
u_{h}\in V_{h}
such that

\displaystyle A_{h}(u_{h},v)

\displaystyle=l_{h}(v)\qquad\forall v\in V_{h},

(3.2)

where for
v,w\in V_{h}
,

\displaystyle A_{h}(v,w)

\displaystyle=a_{h}(v,w)+s_{h}(v,w),

(3.3)

\displaystyle a_{h}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N}(\nabla v_{i},\nabla w_{i})_{\Omega_{i}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(\langle n_{i}\cdot\nabla v\rangle,[w])_{\Gamma_{ij}}+([v],\langle n_{i}\cdot\nabla w\rangle)_{\Gamma_{ij}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\beta_{0}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-1}([v],[w])_{\Gamma_{ij}},

(3.4)

\displaystyle s_{h}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\beta_{1}(\llbracket\nabla v\rrbracket,\llbracket\nabla w\rrbracket)_{\mathcal{O}_{ij}},

(3.5)

\displaystyle l_{h}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N}(f,v_{i})_{\Omega_{i}}.

(3.6)

and we recall that the Dirichlet boundary condition
u=0
on
\partial\Omega

is for simplicity enforced strongly in
V_{h,0}
. Here,
\beta_{0}>0
is the Nitsche (interior) penalty parameter and
\beta_{1}>0
is a stabilization parameter. Note the relation
s_{h}(v,v)=\beta_{1}\|v\|_{s_{h}}^{2}
between the stabilization term
s_{h}
and the norm
\|\cdot\|_{s_{h}}
(
2.20
).

4
Energy norm, consistency, Galerkin orthogonality and continuity

In the forthcoming analysis we will need to compare the exact solution
u\in H^{s}(\Omega)
and the finite element solution
u_{h}\in V_{h}
. To facilitate the analysis we will see that it is natural to represent the exact solution
u\in H^{s}(\Omega)
as a multimesh function in
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}H^{s}(\Omega_{h,i})
, using the
E^{\dagger}
operator (
2.8
), and define the error by

E^{\dagger}u-u_{h}\in V_{h}+E^{\dagger}H^{s}(\Omega).

(4.1)

For clarity, when there is no risk of miss understanding we use the simplified
notation
u=E^{\dagger}u
and write
u-u_{h}=E^{\dagger}u-u_{h}
.

We define the energy norm
|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|\cdot|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}
on
V_{h}
by

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|v|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|^{2}_{h}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}}_{I}+\underbrace{\|v\|^{2}_{s_{h}}}_{II}+\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(h_{i}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}+h_{j}\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}})}_{III}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-1}\|[v]\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}}_{IV}.

(4.2)

The numbering of the terms will be used to alleviate the analysis of the method.

In order for
A_{h}
and the norm
|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|\cdot|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}
to be well defined also on

E^{\dagger}u
we note that the traces of the normal flux appearing in the forms are
well defined if
u\in H^{3/2+\epsilon}(\Omega)
,
\epsilon>0
. We then have using

\Gamma_{ij}\subset\partial\widehat{\Omega}_{i}
and standard trace inequalities

\|\nabla v\|_{\Gamma_{ij}}\leq\|\nabla v\|_{\partial\widehat{\Omega}_{i}}\lesssim\|\nabla v\|_{H^{1/2+\epsilon}(\widehat{\Omega}_{i})}\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{3/2+\epsilon}(\Omega)},

(4.3)

noting that
\widehat{\Omega}_{i}
does not depend on
h
.
In view of these observations we define

V=E^{\dagger}H^{3/2+\epsilon}(\Omega)+V_{h}.

(4.4)

We note that
V_{h}\subset V
, the error
u-u_{h}=E^{\dagger}u-u_{h}\in V
,

A_{h}
is a bilinear form on
V
, and
|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|\cdot|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}
is a norm on
V
. We next
establish the consistency, Galerkin orthogonality and continuity of the form
A_{h}
.

Proposition 4.1

(Consistency)
.

The form
A_{h}
is consistent; that is,

\displaystyle A_{h}(u,v)=l_{h}(v)\qquad\forall v\in V_{h},

(4.5)

where
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{3/2+\epsilon}(\Omega)
is the solution to (
3.1
).

Proof.

This result follows by for
v=(v_{0},\dots,v_{N})\in V_{h}
, multiplying (
3.1a
) by
v_{i}
, integrating by parts on
\Omega_{i}\subset\Omega_{h,i}

and summing the contributions

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N}(f,v_{i})_{\Omega_{i}}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}-(\Delta u,v_{i})_{\Omega_{i}}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\left((\nabla u,\nabla v_{i})_{\Omega_{i}}-(n_{i}\cdot\nabla u,v_{i})_{\partial\Omega_{i}}\right).

(4.6)

For the boundary term we note the decomposition

\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{i}=\left(\cup_{j=0}^{i-1}\Gamma_{ij}\right)\cup\left(\cup_{j=i+1}^{N}\Gamma_{ji}\right)

(4.7)

to conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N}(n_{i}\cdot\nabla u,v_{i})_{\partial\Omega_{i}}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(n_{i}\cdot\nabla u,v_{i})_{\Gamma_{ij}}+\sum_{i=0}^{N}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}(n_{i}\cdot\nabla u,v_{i})_{\Gamma_{ji}}.

(4.8)

Using summation by parts and then swapping the indices

i
and
j
, the second term takes the form

\sum_{i=0}^{N}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}(n_{i}\cdot\nabla u,v_{i})_{\Gamma_{ji}}=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}(n_{i}\cdot\nabla u,v_{i})_{\Gamma_{ji}}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(n_{j}\cdot\nabla u,v_{j})_{\Gamma_{ij}}

(4.9)

Now on
\Gamma_{ij}
we obtain using the fact that
n_{j}=-n_{i}
and the definition
of the jump (
2.15
) and average (
2.16
) with weights summing to one (
2.18
),

\displaystyle(n_{i}\cdot\nabla u,v_{i})_{\Gamma_{ij}}+(n_{j}\cdot\nabla u,v_{j})_{\Gamma_{ij}}

\displaystyle\qquad=(n_{i}\cdot\nabla u,v_{i})_{\Gamma_{ij}}-(n_{i}\cdot\nabla u,v_{j})_{\Gamma_{ij}}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\qquad=(n_{i}\cdot\nabla u,v_{i}-v_{j})_{\Gamma_{ij}}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\qquad=(\langle n_{i}\cdot\nabla u\rangle,[v])_{\Gamma_{ij}}.

(4.12)

Here all traces of the gradient are well defined in view of (
4.3
). Now observing
that
u|_{\Omega_{i}}=(E^{\dagger}u)_{i}|_{\Omega_{i}}
we obtain using the notation

u_{i}=(E^{\dagger}u)_{i}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N}(f,v_{i})_{\Omega_{i}}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}(\nabla u_{i},\nabla v_{i})_{\Omega_{i}}-\sum_{i=0}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{j-1}(\langle n_{i}\cdot\nabla u_{i}\rangle,[v])_{\Gamma_{ij}},

(4.13)

which combined with the observation that

[u]=[E^{\dagger}u]=u_{i}-u_{j}=0\qquad\text{on $\Gamma_{ij}$},

(4.14)

and similarly

\llbracket u\rrbracket=\llbracket E^{\dagger}u\rrbracket=0,\qquad\text{on $\mathcal{O}_{ij}$}.

(4.15)

∎

Proposition 4.2

(Galerkin orthogonality)
.

The form
A_{h}
satisfies the Galerkin orthogonality; that is,

\displaystyle A_{h}(u-u_{h},v)=0\qquad\forall v\in V_{h},

(4.16)

where
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{3/2+\epsilon}(\Omega)
is the solution of (
3.1
) and
u_{h}\in V_{h}
is a solution of (
3.2
).

Proof.

The result follows directly from Proposition
4.1
and (
3.2
).
∎

Proposition 4.3

(Continuity)
.

The form
A_{h}
is continuous; that is,

\displaystyle A_{h}(v,w)\lesssim|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|v|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|w|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}\qquad\forall v,w\in V,

(4.17)

where

is defined in (
4.4
).

Proof.

The result follows by repeated use of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.

5
Coercivity

To prove that the form
A_{h}
is coercive, we will make use of the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

For all
v\in V_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{h}^{2}\lesssim N_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}+\|v\|_{s_{h}}^{2}.

(5.1)

Proof.

Take an element
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h,i}
and observe that
K=\cup_{j=i}^{N}K\cap\Omega_{j}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{K\cap\Omega_{i}}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{K\cap\Omega_{j}}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{K\cap\Omega_{i}}+2\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}(\|\nabla(v_{i}-v_{j})\|^{2}_{K\cap\Omega_{j}}+\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{K\cap\Omega_{j}})

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=i}^{N}\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{K\cap\Omega_{j}}+2\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\|\nabla(v_{i}-v_{j})\|^{2}_{K\cap\Omega_{j}}.

(5.4)

Here we have made use of the inequality
a^{2}\leq 2(a-b)^{2}+2b^{2}
, which follows by Young’s inequality
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
applied to
a^{2}=(a-b+b)^{2}=(a-b)^{2}+b^{2}+2(a-b)b
.

Summing over all elements
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h,i}
we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,i}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=i}^{N}\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,i}\cap\Omega_{j}}+2\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\|\nabla(v_{i}-v_{j})\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,i}\cap\Omega_{j}}

(5.5)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=i}^{N}\delta_{ij}\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,i}\cap\Omega_{j}}+2\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\|\nabla(v_{i}-v_{j})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{O}_{ij}}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=i}^{N}\delta_{ij}\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{j}}+2\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\|\nabla(v_{i}-v_{j})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{O}_{ij}},

(5.7)

where we have used
\Omega_{h,i}\cap\Omega_{j}=\mathcal{O}_{ij}\subseteq\Omega_{j}
for
i<j
. Note that the second sum is empty for
i=N
.

Summing over all domains, we obtain by (
2.2
)

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i=0}^{N}\sum_{j=i}^{N}\delta_{ij}\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{j}}+2\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\|\nabla(v_{i}-v_{j})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{O}_{ij}}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leq 2N_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{j}}+2\|v\|_{s_{h}}^{2},

(5.9)

which proves the estimate.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

There is a dependence on the maximum number of overlaps
N_{\mathcal{O}}
in Lemma
5.1
. In practice,
N_{\mathcal{O}}
is of moderate size and this dependence is not an issue. The interpolation error estimates and condition number estimates (shown below) have a different kind of dependence.

Remark 5.3
.

Using an inverse bound of the form (see e.g.
[
13
]
)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{H^{l}(K)}\lesssim h^{m-l}|v|_{H^{m}(K)}\qquad m,l\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},\ m\leq l,

(5.10)

one can show that the stabilization term
s_{h}(v,w)
may alternatively be formulated as

\displaystyle s_{h}(v,w)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\beta_{1}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-2}(\llbracket v\rrbracket,\llbracket w\rrbracket)_{\mathcal{O}_{ij}}.

(5.11)

Using Lemma
5.1
, we may now proceed to prove the coercivity of the bilinear form.

Proposition 5.4

(Coercivity)
.

The form
A_{h}
is coercive. More precisely, for
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
large enough, we have

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|v|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|^{2}_{h}\lesssim A_{h}(v,v)\qquad\forall v\in V_{h}.

(5.12)

Proof.

We first note that

\displaystyle A_{h}(v,v)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\beta_{0}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-1}\|[v]\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}+\beta_{1}s_{h}(v,v)

\displaystyle\qquad-\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}2|(\langle n_{i}\cdot\nabla v\rangle,[v])_{\Gamma_{ij}}|}_{\bigstar}.

(5.13)

Now, for
l=i
or
l=j
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{h,l}(\Gamma_{ij})=\{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h,l}:K\cap\Gamma_{ij}\neq\emptyset\}

(5.14)

denote the set of elements in
\mathcal{K}_{h,l}
which intersect
\Gamma_{ij}
. Using an inverse estimate (see
[
37
]
), we have

\displaystyle h_{l}\|\nabla v_{l}\|^{2}_{K\cap\Gamma_{ij}}\lesssim\|\nabla v_{l}\|^{2}_{K},

(5.15)

where the constant is independent of the position of
\Gamma_{ij}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}h_{l}\|\nabla v_{l}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\delta_{ji}h_{l}\|\nabla v_{l}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}

(5.16)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\delta_{ji}\|\nabla v_{l}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}_{h,l}(\Gamma_{ij})}

(5.17)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\delta_{ji}\|\nabla v_{l}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,l}},

(5.18)

where we have noted that
\Gamma_{ij}
(the part of
\Gamma_{i}
bordering to
\Omega_{j}
for
j<i
) is empty if the overlap
\mathcal{O}_{ji}
(the part of
\Omega_{h,j}
intersected by
\Gamma_{i}
for
j<i
) is empty, as indicated by
\delta_{ji}
. See also Remark
2.2
.

For
l=i
, we thus obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}h_{i}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\delta_{ji}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,i}}

(5.19)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N}\delta_{ji}\right)\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,i}}

(5.20)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}N_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,i}}

(5.21)

\displaystyle\leq N_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,i}}

(5.22)

\displaystyle=N_{\mathcal{O}}\|\nabla v\|_{h}^{2},

(5.23)

while for
l=j
, we obtain the similar estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}h_{j}\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\delta_{ji}\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,j}}

(5.24)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{N}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N}\delta_{ji}\right)\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,j}}

(5.25)

\displaystyle=N_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,j}}

(5.26)

\displaystyle=N_{\mathcal{O}}\|\nabla v\|_{h}^{2}.

(5.27)

Proceeding with
\bigstar
using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality with weight
\epsilon(h_{i}+h_{j})
, where
\epsilon
is a positive number, and Young’s inequality
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
we obtain

\displaystyle\bigstar=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}2|(\langle n_{i}\cdot\nabla v\rangle,[v])_{\Gamma_{ij}}|

(5.28)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}2\epsilon^{1/2}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{1/2}\|\langle n_{i}\cdot\nabla v\rangle\|_{\Gamma_{ij}}\epsilon^{-1/2}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-1/2}\|[v]\|_{\Gamma_{ij}}

(5.29)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\epsilon(h_{i}+h_{j})\|\langle n_{i}\cdot\nabla v\rangle\|_{\Gamma_{ij}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\epsilon^{-1}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-1}\|[v]\|_{\Gamma_{ij}}^{2}

(5.30)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\epsilon\big(h_{i}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}+h_{j}\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}\big)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\epsilon^{-1}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-1}\|[v]\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}

(5.31)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon N_{\mathcal{O}}\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{h}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\epsilon^{-1}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-1}\|[v]\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}.

(5.32)

In (
5.31
) we used that

\displaystyle(h_{i}+h_{j})\|\langle n_{i}\cdot\nabla v\rangle\|_{\Gamma_{ij}}^{2}

(5.33)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2(h_{i}+h_{j})\kappa^{2}_{i}\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{\Gamma_{ij}}^{2}+2(h_{i}+h_{j})\kappa^{2}_{j}\|\nabla v_{j}\|_{\Gamma_{ij}}^{2}

(5.34)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2h_{i}\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{\Gamma_{ij}}^{2}+2h_{j}\|\nabla v_{j}\|_{\Gamma_{ij}}^{2}.

(5.35)

where we used the definition (
2.17
) of the weights
\kappa_{l}
to obtain

\displaystyle(h_{i}+h_{j})\kappa_{l}^{2}=\frac{h_{l}^{2}}{h_{i}+h_{j}}=\frac{h_{l}}{h_{i}+h_{j}}h_{l}\leq h_{l}\qquad l=i,j.

(5.36)

In (
5.32
) we made use of (
5.23
) and (
5.27
).

By Lemma
5.1
, we may now estimate the
\|\cdot\|_{h}
norm in terms of the
\|\cdot\|_{\Omega}
norm and the
\|\cdot\|_{s_{h}}
norm to obtain

\displaystyle\bigstar

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon N_{\mathcal{O}}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{\Omega_{i}}^{2}+\epsilon N_{\mathcal{O}}\|v\|_{s_{h}}^{2}+\epsilon^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-1}\|[v]\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}.

(5.37)

Combining (
5
) and (
5.37
), we find that

\displaystyle A_{h}(v,v)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=0}^{N}(1-\epsilon CN_{\mathcal{O}}^{2})\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}+(\beta_{1}-\epsilon CN_{\mathcal{O}})\|v\|_{s_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(\beta_{0}-\epsilon^{-1}C)(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-1}\|[v]\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}},

(5.38)

and thus, by choosing
\epsilon
small enough and then
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
large enough,

\displaystyle A_{h}(v,v)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}+\|v\|_{s_{h}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-1}\|[v]\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}.

(5.39)

The coercivity now follows by noting that term
III
in (
4.2
) may be controlled by terms
I
and
II
as above
in the estimate of
\bigstar
.
∎

6
Interpolation error estimate

To construct an interpolation operator into
V_{h}
, we pick a standard interpolation operator into
V_{h,i}
,

\displaystyle\pi_{h,i}:H^{1}(\Omega_{h,i})\longrightarrow V_{h,i},\qquad i=0,1,\dots,N,

(6.1)

where
\pi_{h,i}
satisfies the standard interpolation error estimate (see e.g.
[
13
]
)

\displaystyle\|v-\pi_{h,i}v\|_{H^{m}(K)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{k+1-m}|v|_{H^{k+1}(\mathcal{N}_{h}(K))}.

(6.2)

Here,
\mathcal{N}_{h}(K)
denotes the set of elements that share a vertex with
K
. We then define
the interpolation operator

\displaystyle\Pi_{h}:\bigoplus_{i=0}^{N}H^{1}(\Omega_{h,i})\ni v\longmapsto\oplus_{i=0}^{N}\pi_{h,i}v_{i}\in V_{h},

(6.3)

which by composition with
E^{\dagger}
, see (
2.8
), provides the interpolation operator

\displaystyle\pi_{h}:H^{1}(\Omega)\ni v\longmapsto\Pi_{h}E^{\dagger}v=\oplus_{i=0}^{N}\pi_{h,i}(v|_{\Omega_{h,i}})\in V_{h}.

(6.4)

Figure 6:
Balls
B_{\delta}(x)
centered at
x\in\Gamma
.

To prove an interpolation error estimate for
\pi_{h}
, recall Remark
2.1
regarding the non-intersecting boundaries and let
U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})
denote the tubular neighborhood of
\Gamma_{ij}
defined by

\displaystyle U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})=\bigcup_{x\in\Gamma_{ij}}B_{\delta}(x),

(6.5)

where
B_{\delta}(x)
is the ball of radius
\delta
centered at
x
; see Figure
6
. In addition, let

\displaystyle U_{\delta}(\Gamma)=\bigcup_{i,j}U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}).

(6.6)

Proposition 6.1

(Interpolation error estimate)
.

The interpolation operator
\pi_{h}
satisfies the interpolation error estimate

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|v-\pi_{h}v|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|^{2}_{h}\lesssim C_{h,N}\sum_{i=0}^{N}h_{i}^{2k}|v_{i}|^{2}_{H^{k+1}(\Omega_{h,i})\cap W^{k+1}_{\infty}(U_{h}(\Gamma_{i}))},

(6.7)

where

\displaystyle C_{h,N}=1+\max_{0\leq i\leq N}h^{d}_{i}N_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}+\max_{0\leq i\leq N}h_{i}|\Gamma_{i}|,

(6.8)

and the norm is defined by

\displaystyle|v|^{2}_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)\cap W^{k+1}_{\infty}(U_{h}(\Gamma))}=|v|^{2}_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}+|v|^{2}_{W^{k+1}_{\infty}(U_{h}(\Gamma))}.

(6.9)

Proof.

We first let
\eta=v-\pi_{h}v
denote the interpolation error and recall
the numbering of the terms in the definition of the energy norm
|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|\cdot|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}
(
4.2
). Starting with term
I
, we have

\displaystyle I(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla\eta_{i}\|_{\Omega_{i}}^{2}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla\eta_{i}\|_{\Omega_{h,i}}^{2}.

(6.10)

For term
II
, we have, since
\cup_{j=i+1}^{N}\mathcal{O}_{ij}\subseteq\Omega_{h,i}
and
\mathcal{O}_{ij}\subseteq U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ji})\cap\Omega_{j}
(see Remark
2.2
) with
\delta\sim h_{i}
,

\displaystyle II(\eta)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}(\|\nabla\eta_{i}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{O}_{ij}}+\|\nabla\eta_{j}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{O}_{ij}})

(6.11)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\|\nabla\eta_{i}\|_{\Omega_{h,i}}^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\delta_{ij}\|\nabla\eta_{j}\|_{U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ji})\cap\Omega_{j}}^{2}.

(6.12)

For term
III
, recall the inverse estimate (
5.16
) and note that
\mathcal{K}_{h,j(\Gamma_{ij})}\subseteq U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})
with
\delta\sim h_{j}
. Thus,

\displaystyle III(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(h_{i}\|\nabla\eta_{i}\|_{\Gamma_{ij}}^{2}+h_{j}\|\nabla\eta_{j}\|_{\Gamma_{ij}}^{2})

(6.13)

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla\eta_{i}\|_{\Omega_{h,i}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\delta_{ji}\|\nabla\eta_{j}\|_{U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})}^{2}.

(6.14)

For term
IV
, we first handle the jump term as in
II
and then proceed as for
III
,

\displaystyle IV(\eta)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-1}(\|\eta_{i}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}+\|\eta_{j}\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}})

(6.15)

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}^{-2}\|\eta_{i}\|_{\Omega_{h,i}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\delta_{ji}h_{j}^{-2}\|\eta_{j}\|_{U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})}^{2}.

(6.16)

Now, note that since
\Omega_{h,i}\subseteq\Omega_{i}\cup_{j=i+1}^{N}U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|v_{i}\|_{\Omega_{h,i}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|v_{i}\|_{\Omega_{i}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\delta_{ij}\|v_{i}\|_{U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})}^{2}.

(6.17)

Therefore, there are only two terms in
I
–
IV
that need to be estimated. First

\displaystyle h_{i}^{2(m-1)}|\eta_{i}|_{H^{m}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}\lesssim h_{i}^{2k}|v_{i}|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}\qquad m=0,1,

(6.18)

which follows immediately by (
6.2
). Second, we make use of the disjoint partition of
\Gamma_{i}
and noting that

\displaystyle|U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})|\lesssim h_{i}\max(h_{i}^{d-1},|\Gamma_{i}|),

(6.19)

we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}h_{i}^{2(m-1)}|\eta_{i}|_{H^{m}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}h_{i}^{2k}|v_{i}|_{H^{k+1}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}))}^{2}

(6.20)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}h_{i}^{2k}h_{i}\max(h_{i}^{d-1},|\Gamma_{ij}|)|v_{i}|_{W^{k+1}_{\infty}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}))}^{2}

(6.21)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(h_{i}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\max(h_{i}^{d-1},|\Gamma_{ij}|)\right)h_{i}^{2k}|v_{i}|_{W^{k+1}_{\infty}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{i}))}^{2}

(6.22)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}(h^{d}_{i}N_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}+h_{i}|\Gamma_{i}|)h_{i}^{2k}|v_{i}|_{W^{k+1}_{\infty}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{i}))}^{2}.

(6.23)

Due to the maximum norm, this estimate also holds with
\eta_{i}
replaced by
\eta_{j}
, and the desired estimate holds.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

Note that the stronger control
v_{i}\in W^{k+1}_{\infty}(U_{h}(\Gamma))
enables
us to establish the estimate (
6.7
) with the constant given by (
6.8
) which only have weak dependence on the configuration of the overlapping mesh configuration encoded in terms of
N_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}
and
|\Gamma_{i}|
.

7
A priori
error estimates

We may now prove the following optimal order
a priori
error estimates.
The estimates are supported by the numerical results presented in Figure
7
. For details on these results, we refer to the accompanying paper
[

]
.

Theorem 7.1

(
A priori
error estimates)
.

The finite element solution
u_{h}
of (
3.2
) satisfies the following
a priori
error estimates:

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|u-u_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{h,N}\sum_{i=0}^{N}h_{i}^{2k}|u|^{2}_{H^{k+1}(\Omega_{h,i})\cap W^{k+1}_{\infty}(U_{h}(\Gamma_{i}))},

(7.1)

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(N_{\mathcal{O}}+1)^{1/2}C_{h,N}h\sum_{i=0}^{N}h_{i}^{2k}|u|^{2}_{H^{k+1}(\Omega_{h,i})\cap W^{k+1}_{\infty}(U_{h}(\Gamma_{i}))}.

(7.2)

Proof.

The proof of (
7.1
) follows the standard procedure of splitting the error and using the energy norm interpolation error estimate from Proposition
6.1
,

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|u-u_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|u-\pi_{h}u|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}+|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|\pi_{h}u-u_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}.

(7.3)

To estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
7.3
),
we use the coercivity (Proposition
5.4
), Galerkin orthogonality (Proposition
4.2
) and continuity (Proposition
4.3
) of
A_{h}
to obtain

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|\pi_{h}u-u_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim A_{h}(\pi_{h}u-u_{h},\pi_{h}u-u_{h})

(7.4)

\displaystyle=A_{h}(\pi_{h}u-u,\pi_{h}u-u_{h})

(7.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|\pi_{h}u-u|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|\pi_{h}u-u_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}.

(7.6)

It follows that

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|\pi_{h}u-u_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|u-\pi_{h}u|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}.

(7.7)

Combining (
7.3
) and (
7.7
) with the interpolation error estimate of Proposition
6.1
now yields (
7.1
).

To prove (
7.2
), we use a standard duality argument (see e.g.
[
13
]
). Let
\phi
be the solution to the dual problem

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi

\displaystyle=\psi\qquad\text{in }\Omega,

(7.8a)

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{on }\Omega,

(7.8b)

with
\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Using elliptic regularity we then have

\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{\Omega},

(7.9)

from which it follows that
\phi\in V
. Using the fact that
A_{h}
is symmetric it follows from consistency (Proposition
4.1
) that

\displaystyle A_{h}(v,\phi)=(v,\psi)_{\Omega}\qquad\forall v\in V.

(7.10)

We now take
v=e=u-u_{h}
and use the Galerkin orthogonality (Proposition
4.2
), continuity (Proposition
4.3
) and a standard
interpolation inequality on each set
\Omega_{h,i}
(note that we cannot use stronger
regularity than
\phi\in H^{2}(\Omega)
since
\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and thus the interpolation bound (
6.1
) is not applicable for
\phi
) to obtain

\displaystyle(e,\psi)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=A_{h}(e,\phi)

(7.11)

\displaystyle=A_{h}(e,\phi-\pi_{h}\phi)

(7.12)

\displaystyle\leq|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|e|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|\phi-\pi_{h}\phi|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}

(7.13)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|e|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N}h_{i}^{2}|\phi|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h,i})}\right)^{1/2}

(7.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|e|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N}h_{i}^{2}|\phi|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega_{h,i}\setminus\Omega_{i})}+\sum_{i=0}^{N}h_{i}^{2}|\phi|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega_{i})}\right)^{1/2}

(7.15)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|e|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}\left((N_{\mathcal{O}}+1)h^{2}|\phi|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\right)^{1/2}

(7.16)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|e|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}(N_{\mathcal{O}}+1)^{1/2}h\|\psi\|_{\Omega},

(7.17)

where we used the fact that the maximum number of overlapping meshes is
N_{\mathcal{O}}

and in the last step we have used the standard elliptic regularity estimate (see e.g.
[
13
]
). Note that we have continuity (
7.13
) also for functions in
H^{3/2+\epsilon}(\Omega)
,
\epsilon>0
, as noted in Proposition
4.3
. The desired estimate (
7.2
) now follows from (
7.17
) by (
7.1
) and taking
\psi=e
.
∎

8
Condition number estimate

To prove a bound on the condition number, we first introduce some notation and definitions. Let
\{\varphi_{i,j}\}_{j=1}^{M_{i}}
be the finite element basis of
V_{h,i}
. We then have the expansion

\displaystyle v_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{i}}\widehat{v}_{i,j}\varphi_{i,j},

(8.1)

for each part
v_{i}
of a multimesh function
v=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{N})
. Collecting all expansion coefficients for the
1+N
parts into a vector
\hat{v}
of dimension
M=\sum_{i=0}^{N}M_{i}
, the total stiffness matrix
\widehat{A}
for the multimesh system is defined by

\displaystyle(\widehat{A}\widehat{v},\widehat{w})_{M}=A_{h}(v,w)\qquad\forall v,w\in V_{h},

(8.2)

with condition number

\displaystyle\kappa(\widehat{A})=|\widehat{A}|_{M}|\widehat{A}^{-1}|_{M}.

(8.3)

To derive an estimate of
\kappa(\widehat{A})
we make use of the following Lemmas.

Lemma 8.1

(Inverse inequality)
.

It holds that

\displaystyle|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|v|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}^{2}\lesssim(1+N_{\mathcal{O}})\max_{0\leq i\leq N}h_{i}^{-2}\|v\|_{h}^{2}\qquad\forall v\in V_{h}.

(8.4)

Proof.

Recall the definition of the energy norm (
4.2
).
We first note that

\displaystyle I=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{\Omega_{i}}^{2}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{\Omega_{h,i}}^{2}.

(8.5)

For term
II
, we have by recalling (
6.12
)

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{\Omega_{h,i}}^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\delta_{ij}\|\nabla v_{j}\|_{U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})\cap\Omega_{j}}^{2}.

(8.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{\Omega_{h,i}}^{2}.

(8.7)

Term
III
may be estimated similarly to obtain

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{\Omega_{h,i}}^{2}.

(8.8)

For term
IV
, we have by recalling (
6.16
)

\displaystyle IV

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}^{-2}\|v_{i}\|_{\Omega_{h,i}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\delta_{ji}h_{j}^{-2}\|v_{j}\|_{U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})}^{2}

(8.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=0}^{N}h_{i}^{-2}\|v_{i}\|_{\Omega_{h,i}}^{2}.

(8.10)

The desired estimate now follows using the standard inverse inequality (
5.10
).
∎

Lemma 8.2

(Poincaré inequality)
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{h}^{2}\lesssim C_{P}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|v|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}^{2}\qquad\forall v\in V_{h}.

(8.11)

where

\displaystyle C_{P}=1+\max_{0\leq i\leq N}h_{i}^{2/d}N_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}+\max_{0\leq i\leq N}h_{i}^{2}N_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}.

(8.12)

Proof.

First note that by a Taylor expansion argument and Lemma
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{N}\big(\|v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}+h_{i}^{2}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,i}}\big)

(8.13)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}+N_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\left(h_{i}^{2}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}+h_{i}^{2}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\|\nabla(v_{i}-v_{j})\|_{\mathcal{O}_{ij}}^{2}\right).

(8.14)

To control the first term on the right-hand side in (
8.14
), let
\phi\in H^{2}(\Omega)
be the solution to the dual problem

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi

\displaystyle=\psi\qquad\text{in }\Omega,

(8.15a)

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

(8.15b)

where
\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Multiplying the dual problem with
v\in V_{h}
and integrating by parts, we obtain using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N}(v_{i},\psi)_{\Omega_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N}(v_{i},-\Delta\phi)_{\Omega_{i}}

(8.16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N}(\nabla v_{i},\nabla\phi)_{\Omega_{i}}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}([v],n_{i}\cdot\nabla\phi)_{\Gamma_{ij}}

(8.17)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{\Omega_{i}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{\Omega_{i}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(h_{i}+h_{i})^{-1/2}\|[v]\|_{\Gamma_{ij}}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{1/2}\|\nabla\phi\|_{\Gamma_{ij}}

(8.18)

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-1}\|[v]\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\bigg(\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{\Omega}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(h_{i}+h_{j})\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}\bigg)^{1/2}.

(8.19)

Now we continue with the second factor in (
8
). Using the trace inequality

\displaystyle\|v\|^{2}_{\gamma\cap K}\lesssim h^{-1}\|v\|^{2}_{K}+h\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{K},

(8.20)

with constant independent of the position of an interface
\gamma
(see
[
37
]
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}h_{l}\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\delta_{ji}\big(\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}_{h,i}(\Gamma_{ij})}+h_{l}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}_{h,i}(\Gamma_{ij})}\big)\qquad l=i,j.

(8.21)

By the construction of
U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})
, see (
6.5
), we have
\mathcal{K}_{h,i}(\Gamma_{ij})\subseteq U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})
with
\delta\sim h_{i}
. Furthermore, by the Hölder inequality
[
14
]
with coefficients
r,s
such that
1/r+1/s=1
we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}_{h,i}(\Gamma_{ij})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})}

(8.22)

\displaystyle=\|1\cdot|\nabla\phi|^{2}\|_{L^{1}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}))}

(8.23)

\displaystyle\leq\|1\|_{L^{s}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}))}\||\nabla\phi|^{2}\|_{L^{r}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}))}

(8.24)

\displaystyle=|U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})|^{1/s}\||\nabla\phi|^{2}\|_{L^{r}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}))}

(8.25)

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{i}^{1/s}|\Gamma_{ij}|^{1/s}\||\nabla\phi|^{2}\|_{L^{r}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}))}

(8.26)

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{i}^{1-2/p}\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{L^{p}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}))}

(8.27)

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{i}^{1-2/p}\|\phi\|^{2}_{W^{1}_{p}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}))}

(8.28)

with
p=2r
and

1/s=1-1/r=1-2/p
.

To determine
p
in (
8.28
), we use the Sobolev embedding
W^{l}_{q}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{k}_{p}(\Omega)

[
14
]
with
k=1
,
l=2
and
q=2
. This is motivated by the fact that due to elliptic regularity and
\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have
\phi\in H^{2}(\Omega)
. Since the embedding holds for

1/p-k/d=1/q-l/d

[
14
]
,
we obtain

p=2d/(d-2)
,
where
p=\infty
for
d=2
. Thus

\displaystyle h_{i}^{1-2/p}\|\phi\|^{2}_{W^{1}_{p}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{i}^{2/d}\|\phi\|^{2}_{W^{2}_{2d/(d-2)}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}))}

(8.29)

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{i}^{2/d}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{2}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}))}.

(8.30)

Cf.
[
14
]
regarding the last inequality for
d=2,3
. Returning to the second factor in (
8
) we thus have, using (
8
), (
8.28
) and (
8.30
) together with a standard duality argument (see e.g.
[
13
]
), elliptic regularity, a stability estimate, the Poincaré equality and ignoring higher order terms that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{\Omega}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(h_{i}+h_{j})\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}

(8.31)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{\Omega}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\delta_{ji}\left(h_{i}^{2/d}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{2}(U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij}))}+(h_{i}^{2}+h_{j}^{2})\|\nabla^{2}\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}_{h,i}(\Gamma_{ij})}\right)

(8.32)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|^{2}_{\Omega}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\delta_{ji}\left(h_{i}^{2/d}\|\psi\|^{2}_{U_{\delta}(\Gamma_{ij})}+(h_{i}^{2}+h_{j}^{2})\|\psi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}_{h,i}(\Gamma_{ij})}\right)

(8.33)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|^{2}_{\Omega}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(N_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}h_{i}^{2/d}\|\psi\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}+N_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}h_{i}^{2}\|\psi\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}\right)

(8.34)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{N}(1+h_{i}^{2/d}N_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}+h_{i}^{2}N_{\mathcal{O}_{i}})\|\psi\|_{\Omega_{i}}^{2}

(8.35)

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{P}\|\psi\|_{\Omega}^{2}.

(8.36)

The bound on
\sum_{i}(v_{i},\psi)_{\Omega_{i}}
in the left-hand side in (
8.16
) with
\psi=v
now reads

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|v_{i}\|_{\Omega_{i}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{P}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-1}\|[v]\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}\bigg)^{1/2}

(8.37)

since
\|v\|_{\Omega}
is bounded.

To conclude, recall (
8.14
) and insert (
8.37
) to obtain the desired estimate

\displaystyle\|v\|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{P}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{-1}\|[v]\|^{2}_{\Gamma_{ij}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad+N_{\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\left(h_{i}^{2}\|\nabla v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{i}}+h_{i}^{2}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\|\nabla(v_{i}-v_{j})\|_{\mathcal{O}_{ij}}^{2}\right)

(8.38)

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{P}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|v|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}^{2}.

(8.39)

∎

Theorem 8.3

(Condition number estimate)
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\kappa(\widehat{A})\lesssim C_{P}(1+N_{\mathcal{O}})^{2}h^{-2}.

(8.40)

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{K}_{h,i}
is conforming and quasi-uniform we have the equivalence

\displaystyle\|v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,i}}\sim h_{i}^{d}|\widehat{v}_{i}|^{2}_{M_{i}}\qquad\forall v_{i}\in V_{h,i};

(8.41)

see e.g.
[
13
]
. It follows that

\displaystyle\|v\|^{2}_{h}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|v_{i}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h,i}}\sim\sum_{i=0}^{N}h_{i}^{d}|\widehat{v}_{i}|^{2}_{M_{i}}\sim h^{d}|\widehat{v}|^{2}_{M}.

(8.42)

Recall the definition of the matrix norm

\displaystyle|\widehat{A}|_{M}=\sup_{\widehat{v}\neq 0}\frac{|\widehat{A}\widehat{v}|_{M}}{|\widehat{v}|_{M}}.

(8.43)

To estimate
|\widehat{A}|_{M}
, we use the definition of the stiffness matrix (
8.2
), the inverse inequality (
8.4
) and the equivalence (
8.42
) to obtain

\displaystyle|\widehat{A}\widehat{v}|_{M}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\widehat{w}\neq 0}\frac{(\widehat{A}\widehat{v},\widehat{w})_{M}}{|\widehat{w}|_{M}}

(8.44)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\widehat{w}\neq 0}\frac{A_{h}(v,w)}{|\widehat{w}|_{M}}

(8.45)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\widehat{w}\neq 0}\frac{|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|v|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|w|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}}{|\widehat{w}|_{M}}

(8.46)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\widehat{w}\neq 0}\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle(1+N_{\mathcal{O}})h^{-2}\|v\|_{h}\|w\|_{h}}{|\widehat{w}|_{M}}

(8.47)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+N_{\mathcal{O}})h^{d-2}|\widehat{v}|_{M}.

(8.48)

Dividing by
|\widehat{v}|
and using the definition of the matrix norm (
8.43
) yields

\displaystyle|\widehat{A}|_{M}\lesssim(1+N_{\mathcal{O}})h^{d-2}.

(8.49)

To estimate
|\widehat{A}^{-1}|_{M}
, we proceed similarly, and additionally use the Poincaré inequality Lemma
8.2
and the coercivity of the bilinear form (
5.12
) to obtain

\displaystyle h^{d}|\widehat{v}|_{M}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\|v\|_{h}^{2}

(8.50)

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{P}|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|v|\mspace{-1mu}|\mspace{-1mu}|_{h}^{2}

(8.51)

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{P}A_{h}(v,v)

(8.52)

\displaystyle=C_{P}(\widehat{A}\widehat{v},\widehat{v})_{M}

(8.53)

\displaystyle\leq C_{P}|\widehat{A}\widehat{v}|_{M}|\widehat{v}|_{M}.

(8.54)

The inequality thus reads

\displaystyle h^{d}|\widehat{v}|_{M}\lesssim C_{P}|\widehat{A}\widehat{v}|_{M}.

(8.55)

Setting
\widehat{v}=\widehat{A}^{-1}\widehat{w}
yields

\displaystyle h^{d}|\widehat{A}^{-1}\widehat{w}|_{M}\lesssim C_{P}|\widehat{w}|_{M}.

(8.56)

Dividing by
|\widehat{w}|_{M}
and using the definition of the matrix norm (
8.43
) now gives

\displaystyle|\widehat{A}^{-1}|_{M}\lesssim C_{P}(1+N_{\mathcal{O}})h^{-d}.

(8.57)

By using (
8.49
) and (
8.57
) in the definition of the condition number (
8.3
), we obtain the desired estimate (
8.40
).
∎

The estimate for the condition number is supported by the numerical results presented in Figure
8
. The slope is found to be
-1.76
. The details on this example is found in
[
40
]
.

9
Numerical results

To demonstrate the applicability and robustness of the multimesh finite element formulation, we present here a couple of numerical examples. For additional examples, we refer to the companion paper
[
40
]
.

9.1
Convergence under variable mesh size

For the first example, we construct two multimesh configurations
I
and
II
, each consisting of three parts (overlapping meshes) as show in Figure
9
. We consider a simple Poisson problem with analytical solution

\displaystyle u(x,y)=\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y).

(9.1)

The goal is to study the convergence under refinement of the three meshes for each of the two test cases. Starting from initial coarse meshes with equal mesh sizes, we refine each part separately, using
8
different mesh sizes, and compute the
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
error norms. A piecewise linear finite element basis is used for both configurations.

The refinement procedure is as follows. First we will refine part
0
in
8
steps, then part
1
in
8
steps, and finally part
2
in
8
steps. Then we swap the order and refine part
1
first, followed by parts
0
and
2
. We do this for all permutations of the order of the parts; in total there are
3!
combinations. This procedure is performed for both
I
and
II
.

Configuration
I
is a nested configuration (but not hierarchical). Configuration
II
is generated by placing the second and third parts in a “random“ position on top of the background mesh of
\Omega_{0}=[0,1]^{2}
. Specifically, we have

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}^{I}

\displaystyle=[0.2,0.8]^{2},

(9.2)

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}^{I}

\displaystyle=[0.4,0.6]^{2},

(9.3)

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}^{II}

\displaystyle=[0.2,0.8]\times[0.3,0.75],\text{ rotated }23^{\circ},

(9.4)

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}^{II}

\displaystyle=[0.3,0.5]\times[0.05,0.8],\text{ rotated }44^{\circ},

(9.5)

as illustrated in Figure
9
. The meshes are refined with mesh sizes
2^{-k}
,
k=3,\ldots,10
, i.e.,
8
steps. Thus, the mesh size ratio between two parts are in this example at most
2^{7}=128
.

Figure 9:
Configuration
I
(left) and and configuration
II
(right) exemplified by refined part 2 for
I
and refined part 1 for
II
. The coarse and fine meshes have mesh sizes
2^{-3}
and
2^{-6}
respectively.

In Figure
10
we show the
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
errors for the two configurations. As expected, the different curves start and end in the same point. Moreover, we see that during refinement of the first part, errors decrease but flatten. This is due to the fact that the errors from the other two, unrefined, parts dominate. When the second part starts being refined, the errors drop but will again flatten since the errors are dominated by the third and last unrefined part. Refining this part results in a sharp decrease in the error. Due to the effect of dominating errors from different parts, we observe two L-shaped drops for each refinement permutation, for both configurations and for both error quantities.

There is no significant L-shape decrease for the first refined part, but it would be possible to construct a multimesh configuration such that this would be the case. For the example with this analytical solution, the first part would have a dominating error if the area of the part would be dominating.

It is worth noting is that the errors decrease smoothly and the method is stable despite the large differences in mesh size.

9.2
Boundary layer resolution

To demonstrate the potential of the multimesh formulation for local adaptation, we consider the boundary value problem

\displaystyle-\Delta u+\epsilon^{-2}u

\displaystyle=f\qquad\text{in }\Omega,

(9.6a)

\displaystyle\qquad u

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{on }\Gamma_{0},

(9.6b)

\displaystyle\qquad u

\displaystyle=1\qquad\text{on }\Gamma_{1}.

(9.6c)

For
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, the PDE reduces to
u=0
which is compatible with the boundary condition on
\Gamma_{0}
. As a consequence, the solution for small
\epsilon
is
u\approx 0
away from the boundary
\Gamma_{1}
and then an exponential transition to
u=1
close to
\Gamma_{1}
. The width of the boundary layer is

Conclusions

We have analyzed a general framework for discretization of the Poisson equation posed on a domain defined by an arbitrary number of intersecting meshes with arbitrary mesh sizes. The analysis show that for sufficiently large Nitsche and stabilization parameters, the method is optimal and stable. As expected, there is a dependency on the maximum number of intersecting meshes in the coercivity, error analysis and in the condition number estimate. This was seen numerically in the accompanying paper
[
40
]
, and here we are able to quantify this dependence. In addition, the numerical results presented in this paper show that the method is indeed stable when the meshes involved have vastly different mesh sizes.

As mentioned in the introduction, the multimesh method may be advantageous in the case of dynamic domains, since remeshing may be avoided. This is due to the fact that the computational geometry routines automatically identify the elements constituting the active meshes, and this can easily be done every time the domains move. Although so far only studied for two-dimensional problems
[
26
]
reports a speed up. The same approach is also applied in
[
27
]
.

Future work involves extending the implementation to include three-dimensional meshes, which is a challenge due to requirements of efficient and accurate computational geometry routines in the case of arbitrary many intersecting meshes. That the multimesh formulation is valid in the case of two meshes in three dimensions is explored in
[
43
]
for the Stokes problem.

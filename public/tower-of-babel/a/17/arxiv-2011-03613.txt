1.
Introduction

Perfectoid spaces are certain infinitely ramified nonarchimedean analytic spaces introduced by Scholze
[
17
]
. They have played a crucial role in settling a number of conjectures in arithmetic geometry and led to advances in
p
-adic Hodge theory, the Langlands program, and the study of Shimura varieties
[
3
]
,
[
16
]
, not least because they facilitate a correspondence between characteristic zero objects and their positive characteristic analogues. Given the paramount importance of perfectoid spaces it is of interest to develop some algebro-geometric tools to work with these highly non-noetherian objects.

In positive characteristic, perfectoid spaces arise functorially from varieties over
\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)
by taking the
completed perfection
: first taking the perfect closure and then completing the underlying rings with respect to the
t
-adic topology. In mixed characteristic, the constructions are more subtle, but there are several known analogues including constructions for projective space, toric varieties
[
17
, Section 8]
, and abelian varieties
[
2
]
.

In
[
7
]
, the first author studies the first case, considering the perfectoid analogue of projective space (dubbed
projectivoid space
), and shows among other things that it has Picard group canonically isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}[p^{-1}]
. In this manuscript, we investigate whether the methods developed in
[
7
]
apply to other classes of naturally arising perfectoid spaces, noticing that the construction of projectivoid space is a special case of the more general construction of perfectoid covers of toric varieties
[
17
, Section 8]
. In positive characteristic, this construction coincides with the completed perfection. The following is a generalization of the first author’s result
[
7
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be a perfectoid field with residue characteristic
p>0
, and
\Sigma
be a complete smooth fan consisting of strongly convex rational cones. Let
X=X_{\Sigma,K}
be the toric variety over
K
associated to
\Sigma
and let
\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}\to X
be its perfectoid cover. Then the Picard group
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}})
is canonically isomorphic to
\operatorname{Pic}(X)[p^{-1}]
.

We prove this in a similar way to
[
7
]
, noticing first that
\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}
has a natural integral model whose special fiber is the scheme theoretic perfect closure of the toric variety associated to
\Sigma
over the residue field of
K
. The analogous result for scheme theoretic perfect closures is easily deduced, leaving two steps: first that every line bundle over the residue deforms uniquely to the integral model, and second that every line bundle on
\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}
extens uniquely to the integral model.

With Theorem
1.1
we may identify a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}
with a formal
p
-power root
\mathcal{M}^{1/p^{k}}
of a line bundle
\mathcal{M}
on
X
. We then compute the cohomology of
\mathcal{L}
in terms of the cohomology of powers of
\mathcal{M}
, generalizing
[
7
, Theorem 3.26]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
i\geq 0
, there are topologies for which the cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}},\mathcal{L})
is canonically isomorphic to the completion of
\operatorname{colim}\mathrm{H}^{i}(X,\mathcal{M}^{p^{n}})
.

This allows us to deduce cohomological results on
\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}
from known results about the cohomology of toric varieties, and allows us to promote to the perfectoid setting the Demazure vanishing theorem
[
6
]
and the Batyrev-Borisov vanishing theorem
[
1
]
.

The authors point out that Heuer generalizes our results in
[
10
, Theorem 1.5]
, using the
v
-topology to study the Picard group of perfectoid covers of varieties with good reduction.

2.
Toric Varieties and Toric Schemes

We begin by reviewing the construction of a toric scheme associated to a fan and recalling a few useful properties. One can find a complete reference about the theory over the complex numbers in
[
4
]
, and over general fields in
[
5
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
N
be a free abelian group of finite rank. Let
M:=\text{Hom}(N,\mathbb{Z})
be the dual lattice, and denote the canonical pairing
M_{\mathbb{R}}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
by
\langle m,n\rangle:=m(n)
.

(i)

A
strongly convex polyhedral rational cone
(henceforth,
cone
)
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
is a set of the form
\lambda_{1}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}+\cdots+\lambda_{t}\mathbb{R}_{\geq_{0}}
with
\lambda_{i}\in N
subject to the condition that no line through the origin is contained in
\sigma
. If the set
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{t}
can be completed to a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
N
then
\sigma
is called
smooth
.

(ii)

Given a cone
\sigma
the
dual cone
of
\sigma
is

\sigma^{\vee}:=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\langle m,u\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }u\in\sigma\}.

(iii)

A cone
\tau
is said to be a
face
of
\sigma
if it is of the form
\sigma\cap H_{m}
where to
m\in\sigma^{\vee}
, we associate the hyperplane
H_{m}=\{n\in N_{\mathbb{R}}:\langle m,n\rangle=0\}
.

(iv)

A nonempty and finite collection of strongly convex polyhedral cones
\Sigma
is called a
fan
if it is closed under taking faces and if the intersection of any two cones is a face of both of them. A fan
\Sigma
is
smooth
if all of its cones are.

(v)

The
support
of
\Sigma
is the union
|\Sigma|:=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma
. If
|\Sigma|
is all of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, then
\Sigma
is called
complete
.

The monoid
\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
is finitely generated by
[
4
, Proposition 1.2.7]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring and
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
a strongly convex polyhedral cone. The affine toric scheme over
A
associated to
\sigma
is

U_{\sigma,A}:=\operatorname{Spec}A[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M].

Each element
m\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
induces an element of the ring of regular functions of
U_{\sigma,A}
. We denote this function by
\chi^{m}\in A[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
and call it the
character
associated to
m
.
When
A
is understood we omit it from the notation.

When
\sigma
is smooth the structure of
U_{\sigma,A}
is rather simple.

Example 2.3
.

[
4
, Example 1.2.21]

Let
A
be a commutative ring and
\sigma
a smooth strongly convex polyhedral cone. Then there is a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
of
M
such that
\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
is generated by

\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{r},\pm e_{r+1},\cdots,\pm e_{n}\}.

In particular, there is an isomorphism

A[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]\cong A[x_{1},\cdots,x_{r},x_{r+1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]

so that
U_{\sigma,A}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{r}_{A}\times\mathbb{G}_{m,A}^{n-r}
.

If
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
then the inclusion
\sigma^{\vee}\subseteq\tau^{\vee}
induces a localization map
A[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]\to A[\tau^{\vee}\cap M]
. Passing to prime spectra induces an open immersion
U_{\tau}\hookrightarrow U_{\sigma}
. Gluing along these open immersions allows us to define the toric scheme over
A
associated to a fan
\Sigma
, which we denote by
X_{\Sigma,A}
. As above, when
A
is understood we omit it from the notation. Notice also that this construction is compatible with base change in
A
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\Sigma
is a fan and
A\to B
is a ring homomorphism, then there is a canonical isomorphism

X_{\Sigma,A}\times_{\operatorname{Spec}A}\operatorname{Spec}B\cong X_{\Sigma,B}.

Proof.

The affine case is clear, and since the isomorphisms are canonical over the affine cover, they glue.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Given a fan
\Sigma
and a base scheme
S
, one can define the relative toric scheme
X_{\Sigma,S}\to S
by building toric schemes as in Definition
2.2
affine locally on
S
, and then gluing canonically via Lemma
2.4
.

When
A=k
is a field, there is a well known structural result.

Definition 2.6
.

A toric variety over
k
is a normal separated scheme of finite type with an action by a split torus
T=\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
such that there exists a point
x\in X(k)
with trivial stabilizer and open dense orbit. By
[
13
, Chapter 1, Theorem 6]
every toric variety is isomorphic to
X_{\Sigma,k}
for a unique fan
\Sigma
, and conversely if
\Sigma
is a fan then
X_{\Sigma,k}
is a toric variety.

Remark 2.7
.

In the literature one encounters toric varieties which may not be normal. The toric varieties we study all correspond to fans, and are therefore normal by
[
13
, Chapter 1, Theorem 7]
. Therefore when we say toric variety we shall always assume normality.

We record a few more results that we will be using throughout the paper.

Proposition 2.8
.

A relative toric scheme
X_{\Sigma,S}\to S
is proper if and only if
\Sigma
is complete.

Proof.

The case where
S=\operatorname{Spec}A
is affine is
[
6
, Section 4 Proposition 4]
, and properness is local on the target.
∎

It is shown in
[
5
, Corollary 7.4]
that proper toric varieties have acyclic structure sheaves, and cohomology and base change theorems extend this property to the relative setting, implying in particular that a proper toric variety over any ring has an acyclic structure sheaf.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
S
be a scheme and
\Sigma
a complete fan, and
f:X_{\Sigma,S}\to S
the associated relative toric scheme. Then
R^{i}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{\Sigma,S}}=0
for all
i>0
. In particular, if
S=\operatorname{Spec}A
is affine,
\mathcal{O}_{X_{\Sigma,A}}
is acyclic.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
,
X
is the pullback to
S
of the proper
\mathbb{Z}
scheme
X_{\Sigma,\mathbb{Z}}\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}
. By
[
5
, Corollary 7.4]
, for each prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{Z}
, the module
\mathcal{O}_{X_{\Sigma,\mathbb{Z}}}\otimes k(\mathfrak{p})=\mathcal{O}_{X_{\Sigma,k(\mathfrak{p})}}
is acyclic. In particular, for
i>0
the function
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\dim_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\mathrm{H}^{i}(X_{k(\mathfrak{p})},\mathcal{O}_{X_{\Sigma,k(\mathfrak{p})}})
is the constant zero function on
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore by Grauert’s theorem of cohomology and base change
[
19

\mathcal{O}_{X_{\Sigma,\mathbb{Z}}}
is acyclic and its cohomology commutes with arbitrary base change, whence the result follows.
∎

A toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
comes equipped with an affine cover
\mathfrak{U}=\{U_{\sigma}\to X_{\Sigma}\}
as
\sigma
varies over the maximal cones of
\Sigma
. By
[

, every line bundle on

trivializes on

so that much of this study reduces to the combinatorics of

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan,
k
a field, and
X=X_{\Sigma,k}
the associated toric variety. The Picard group of
X
does not depend on the field
k
.

Proof.

Let
\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\cdots,\sigma_{r}\}\subset\Sigma
be the set of maximal cones. Set
U_{i}=U_{\sigma_{i},k}
, and denote by
\mathfrak{U}
the cover
\{U_{i}\to X\}
. The finite intersections of the
U_{i}
are affine toric varieties and therefore by
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
all have trivial Picard group. Therefore the Čech-to-derived functor spectral sequence degenerates to an isomorphism

\operatorname{Pic}(X)\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*})\cong\check{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(\mathfrak{U},\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*}).

Consider the first three terms of the Čech sequence,

C^{0}(\mathfrak{U},\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta^{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}C^{1}(\mathfrak{U},\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta^{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}C^{2}(\mathfrak{U},\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*})\longrightarrow\cdots

for some

and

. Let
D^{1}\subseteq\ker\delta^{1}

D^{0}=D^{1}\cap\operatorname{im}\delta^{0}

The proof of Proposition
2.10

Definition 2.11

(The
n
th power map)
.

Fix a fan
\Sigma
and an integer
n
. For each
\sigma\in\Sigma
the multiplication by
n
on
M
map induces a homomorphism
A[M\cap\sigma^{\vee}]\to A[M\cap\sigma^{\vee}]
. This map is compatible with restrictions to faces
\tau\leq\sigma
, and therefore glues to a morphism
\varphi_{n}:X_{\Sigma,A}\to X_{\Sigma,A}
, which we will call the
n
th power map on
X_{\Sigma,A}

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
k
be a field and
\Sigma
a fan. The
n
th power map on
X=X_{\Sigma,k}
induces a pullback homomorphism
\varphi_{n}^{*}:\operatorname{Pic}X\to\operatorname{Pic}X
which can be identified with multiplication by
n
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{U}
be the same cover from the proof of Proposition
2.10
, and recall that an arbitrary Čech cocycle in
\check{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(\mathfrak{U},\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*})
may be represented by monic monomials, thus be of the form

. Then pullback along

corresponds to multiplication by

on

so that it takes

3.
Perfectoid Covers of Toric Varieties

To begin this section we review Scholze’s construction of the perfectoid cover of a toric variety
X=X_{\Sigma,K}
. We use Huber’s theory of
adic spaces
, a non-archimedean analogue of complex analytic spaces, which are general enough to handle the infinite ramification required to define perfectoid spaces. We will not develop the entire theory of adic spaces here, but refer the reader to
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
where it was introduced, or to
[
17
, Section 2]
for an introduction with more of an emphasis on perfectoid spaces. We do introduce a few preliminary notions.

Definition 3.1
.

A
Tate ring
is a complete topological ring
A
such that there exists a topologically nilpotent unit called the
pseudouniformizer

\varpi\in A
and an open subring
A_{0}\subset A
containing
\varpi
such that
A_{0}
has the
\varpi
-adic topology. A ring of integral elements
A^{+}\subseteq A
is an open integrally closed subring of the power bounded elements
A^{\circ}
.

Associated to such a pair
(A,A^{+})
is the
adic spectrum

\text{Spa}(A,A^{+})
which is a topological space whose underlying set consists of equivalence classes of valuations on
A
which are bounded by
1
on
A^{+}
. If
x\in\text{Spa}(A,A^{+})
is a point, we suggestively denote the associated valuation by
f\mapsto|f(x)|
for
f\in A
. General adic spaces are locally of the form
\text{Spa}(A,A^{+})
, and all adic spaces come equipped with various sheaves related to their structure sheaf. We make heavy use of these sheaves so we define them carefully.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an adic space with structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
.
The sheaf of integral elements

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}^{+}
, a sheaf of (topological) rings defined by the rule

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}^{+}(U)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(U)\text{ }:\text{ }|f(x)|\leq 1\text{ for all }x\in U\right\}.

The sheaf of integral elements has an ideal
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}^{++}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}^{+}
called
the sheaf of topologically nilpotent elements
defined by the rule

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}^{++}(U)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{O}^{+}_{\mathcal{X}}(U)\text{ }:\text{ }|f(x)|<1\text{ for all }x\in U\right\}.

Perfectoid spaces are adic spaces built from special kinds of rings.

Definition 3.3
.

A topological ring
A
is a
perfectoid Tate ring
if it is a Tate ring containing a pseudouniformizer
\varpi\in A
such that

•

A
is
uniform
, i.e.
A^{\circ}
is bounded,

•

\varpi^{p}|p
in
A^{\circ}
,

•

the
p
-power Frobenius map is an isomorphism

\Phi:A^{\circ}/\varpi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}A^{\circ}/\varpi^{p}.

A (Tate) perfectoid space is an adic space which is locally the adic spectrum of perfectoid Tate rings.

Remark 3.4
.

There is a more general framework of so called Huber rings and perfectoid rings from which one can construct adic spaces and perfectoid spaces which are not Tate. The main difference here is that one does not assert the existence of a pseudouniformizer as in Definitions
3.1
and
3.3
. This allows one to apply the theory to rings which are more integral in nature. Nevertheless, we will not require this level of generality because we will always be working over a perfectoid base field and will therefore inherit a pseudouniformizer from this field.

Let
K
be a perfectoid field with valuation ring
K^{\circ}
and residue characteristic
p
. To any cone
\sigma
we can associate the Tate ring
K\langle\sigma^{\vee}\cap M\rangle
of convergent power series in the characters of
\sigma
. We form the associated adic space

\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}:=\text{Spa}\left(K\langle\sigma^{\vee}\cap M\rangle,K^{\circ}\langle\sigma^{\vee}\cap M\rangle\right).

As with toric spaces, if
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
we have an induced open immersion
\mathcal{U}_{\tau}\subset\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}
(we note that this is generally open in the analytic topology rather than the much coarser Zariski topology). Therefore, if
\Sigma
is a fan we can form an adic space
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
.

Remark 3.5
.

There is a fully faithful analytification functor
X\mapsto X^{ad}
from schemes over
K
to adic spaces over
K
, but if
X_{\Sigma}
is not proper then
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
is not in general isomorphic to
(X_{\Sigma})^{ad}
. For example, if
\Sigma
is the cone associated to
\mathbb{N}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
is the rigid analytic disk whereas
(X_{\Sigma})^{ad}
is affine space. Nevertheless, if
\Sigma
is a complete fan (so that
X_{\Sigma}
is proper), then we do have
(\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma})=(X_{\Sigma})^{ad}
by
[
17
, Section 8]
.

As in Definition
2.11
, multiplication by
p
on
M
induces a map
K\langle\sigma^{\vee}\cap M\rangle\to K\langle\sigma^{\vee}\cap M\rangle
inducing the
p
-power map
\varphi
on the adic spectra. Passing to the completed direct limit results in the perfectoid algebra
K\langle\sigma^{\vee}\cap M[p^{-1}]\rangle
and on adic spectra this translates to a so called
tilde inverse limit
(as in
[
17
]
):

\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}^{\textit{perf}}\sim\varprojlim_{\varphi}\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}.

This construction globalizes. If
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
, then
K\langle\sigma^{\vee}\cap M[p^{-1}]\rangle\to K\langle\tau^{\vee}\cap M[p^{-1}]\rangle
is a rational localization so that
\mathcal{U}_{\tau}^{\textit{perf}}\to\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}^{\textit{perf}}
is an open immersion. Therefore given a fan
\Sigma
, we can glue the affinoid perfectoid spaces
\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}^{\textit{perf}}
along their intersections and construct a perfectoid space
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}^{\textit{perf}}
. As in Definition
2.11
, the map
\varphi
commutes with this gluing, so that it induces a map of adic spaces
\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}_{\Sigma}\to\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
, and checking locally we see that in fact
\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}_{\Sigma}\sim\varprojlim_{\varphi}\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
.

Definition 3.6
.

Given a complete fan
\Sigma
the map
\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}_{\Sigma}\to\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
is called the
perfectoid cover
of the toric variety
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
.

If
K
has characteristic
p>0
then
\varphi
can be identified with relative Frobenius over
K
and
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}^{\textit{perf}}
is the completed perfection of the adic space
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
.

Remark 3.7
.

Inverse limits do not exist in general in the category of adic spaces. That being said, if affinoid locally the completed direct limit of the ring of functions is perfectoid, then the perfectoid space built from those rings satisfies the universal property of inverse limit among all perfectoid spaces. It is in this sense that the tilde inverse limit is well defined.

The tilde inverse limit agrees with a categorical inverse limit if we enlarge our category. More precisely, perfectoid spaces embed in the category of diamonds
[
15
]
which do have inverse limits. In this case the diamond in the limit is representable by a perfectoid space, and this perfectoid space is the tilde inverse limit. We will not use this formalism.

Example 3.8

•

Extension Step:

•

is a quasicompact quasiseparated scheme over a field of characteristic

\operatorname{Pic}(S^{\mathrm{perf}})\cong\operatorname{Pic}(S)[p^{-1}]

Proof.

\operatorname{Pic}(S)\to\operatorname{Pic}(S^{\mathrm{perf}})

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\operatorname{Pic}(S)\to\operatorname{Pic}(S)[p^{-1}]\to\operatorname{Pic}(S^{\mathrm{perf}}).}

We must show the righthand map is an isomorphism. We remind ourselves that

along Frobenius. As Frobenius is an affine morphism and

is quasicompact and quasiseparated, every line bundle on

is the pullback of a line bundle from one of the factors

To show injectivity it is equivalent to show that the kernel of

in the kernel, and represent it by a cover

such that each for each

5.3.
Vanishing Theorems

The cohomology of line bundles on toric varieties has been extensively studied, for example in
[
6
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
4
, 9.1-9.4]
. Therefore, given a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
{\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}}
, we may first use Theorem
4.1
to identify it with
\mathcal{M}^{1/p^{k}}
for a line bundle
\mathcal{M}
on
X
, and then use known results about the cohomology of
\mathcal{M}
together with Theorem
5.2
to explicitly compute the cohomology of
\mathcal{L}
. We will use this philosophy to promote Demazure and Batyrev-Borisov vanishing theorems for toric varieties to the perfectoid setting. These theorems concern globally generated line bundles, so to deduce them from the classical theorems about toric varieties using Theorem

, we need the correspondance between line bundles of Theorem
4.1
to preserve the property of being globally generated.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}
and identify it with
\mathcal{M}^{1/p^{k}}
for
\mathcal{M}\in\operatorname{Pic}\mathcal{X}
. Then
\mathcal{L}
is a globally generated
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}}
-module if and only if
\mathcal{M}^{p^{t}}
is a globally generated
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
-module for

.

Proof.

As in Remark
5.3
we may assume

. If
\mathcal{M}^{p^{t}}
is globally generated, then we can pick a surjection
\rho:\bigoplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{p^{t}}
. Letting
\varphi
be the
p
-power map then
\pi_{0}=\varphi^{t}\circ\pi_{t}
. Therefore (applying Lemma
2.12
)

\mathcal{L}\cong\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{M}=\pi_{t}^{*}(\varphi^{t})^{*}\mathcal{M}\cong\pi_{t}^{*}\mathcal{M}^{p^{t}}.

As pulling back by
\pi_{t}
is right exact,
\pi_{t}^{*}\rho:\bigoplus\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{L}
gives the desired surjection.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is globally generated. As
\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}
is compact,
\mathcal{L}
may be generated by a finite set of sections
s_{1},\cdots,s_{n}
. This proof will have 2 parts. First we will show that the
s_{j}
can without loss of generality be assumed to come from global sections of
\mathcal{M}^{p^{t}}
for some
t
(under the identification of Theorem
5.2
on zeroth cohomology). We will then show that (perhaps increasing
t
) these
s_{j}
generate
\mathcal{M}^{p^{t}}
.

Trivialize
\mathcal{L}
over the
\mathcal{U}^{\textit{perf}}_{i}
, and consider the
s_{j}
as elements of

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}}(\mathcal{U}^{\textit{perf}}_{i})=K\left\langle\sigma_{i}^{\vee}\cap M[p^{-1}]\right\rangle.

As
\mathcal{U}^{\textit{perf}}_{i}
is affinoid, the
s_{j}
generate the unit ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{U}^{\textit{perf}}_{i})
. Therefore there are
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}}(\mathcal{U}^{\textit{perf}}_{i})
such that
a_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}s_{n}=1
. Applying Theorem
5.2
on zeroth cohomology, there are global sections
\tilde{s}_{j}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{M}^{p^{t}})
which are arbitrarily close to the
s_{j}
(increasing
t
as necessary). Since there are finitely many
\mathcal{U}^{\textit{perf}}_{i}
, we may choose them so that on each
\mathcal{U}^{\textit{perf}}_{i}
:

||(a_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}s_{n})-(a_{1}\tilde{s}_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\tilde{s}_{n})||<1.

That is,
\xi:=1-(a_{1}\tilde{s}_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\tilde{s}_{n})
is topologically nilpotent. Therefore the geometric series for
(1-\xi)^{-1}
coverges so that
(1-\xi)
is a unit. As
1-\xi
is in the ideal generated by the
\tilde{s}_{j}
, they generate the unit ideal. In particular, we see that the
\tilde{s}_{j}
generate
\mathcal{L}
. This completes the first step.

The second step applies a similar argument, but now to the coefficients. We use that trivializations of
\mathcal{L}
over the
\mathcal{U}_{i}^{\textit{perf}}
and of
\mathcal{M}
over
\mathcal{U}_{i}
are compatible, so that we can identify the map
\mathcal{M}^{p^{t}}(\mathcal{U}_{i})\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{U}_{i}^{\textit{perf}})
with the inclusion
K\left\langle\sigma_{i}^{\vee}\cap p^{-t}M\right\rangle\hookrightarrow K\left\langle\sigma_{i}^{\vee}\cap M[p^{-1}]\right\rangle
, and the
\tilde{s}_{j}
can be considered as elements of the target. We know they generate the unit ideal in
K\left\langle\sigma_{i}^{\vee}\cap M[p^{-1}]\right\rangle
, that is there are coefficients
b_{j}
such that
b_{1}\tilde{s}_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}\tilde{s}_{n}=1
. Arguing as above and perhaps increasing
k
, there are
\tilde{b}_{j}\in K\left\langle\frac{1}{p^{t}}M\cap\sigma_{i}^{\vee}\right\rangle
so that:

||(b_{1}\tilde{s}_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}\tilde{s}_{n})-(\tilde{b}_{1}\tilde{s}_{1}+\cdots+\tilde{b}_{n}\tilde{s}_{n})||<1.

Therefore
\xi^{\prime}=1-(\tilde{b}_{1}\tilde{s}_{1}+\cdots+\tilde{b}_{n}\tilde{s}_{n})
is topologically nilpotent so that
1-\xi^{\prime}
is a unit in
K\left\langle\sigma_{i}^{\vee}\cap p^{-t}M\right\rangle
. But it is also in the ideal generated by the
\tilde{s}_{j}
, so they generate the unit ideal. Doing this over all the
\mathcal{U}_{i}

a finite amount of times, we see that the
\tilde{s}_{j}
generate
\mathcal{M}^{p^{t}}
.
∎

\chi^{m}
, and the map
\operatorname{div}
fits into the following exact sequence
[
4
, Theorem 4.1.3]

(5.8)

M\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\mathbb{Z}\cdot D_{\rho}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(X)\longrightarrow 0.

The term in the middle is the set of Weil divisors of
X_{\Sigma}
invariant under the torus action. Central to the statement of Batyrev-Borisov vanishing is a polyhedron associated to an torus invariant divisor.

Definition 5.13

(
[
4
, (4.3.2)]
)
.

Fix a torus invariant divisor
D=\sum a_{\rho}D_{\rho}
. Its associated polytope is

P_{D}=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}:\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle\geq-a_{\rho}\text{ for all }\rho\in\Sigma(1)\}.

Definition 5.14
.

Let
S\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}
. The
affine hull
of
S
is

\operatorname{Aff}(S):=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}x_{i}:x_{i}\in S\text{ and }\sum\alpha_{i}=1\right\}.

The relative interior
\operatorname{Relint}(S)
is the interior of the affine hull of
S
.

We can now state Batyrev-Borisov vanishing, first established in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 5.15

(Batyrev-Borisov Vanishing
[
4
, Theorem 9.3.5]
)
.

Let
X=X_{\Sigma}
be a complete toric variety over a field
k
, and
D
a basepoint free divisor. Then

•

\mathrm{H}^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}(-D))=0
for all
i\not=\dim P_{D}
.

•

If
i=\dim P_{D}
then

\mathrm{H}^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}(-D))=\bigoplus_{m\in\operatorname{Relint}(P_{D})\cap M}k\cdot\chi^{-m}.

On our perfectoid space we have not yet developed a good notion of divisors, so we need a dictionary between line bundles and divisors. This isn’t too difficult, because as
X
is a normal noetherian scheme, there is a natural injection
\operatorname{Pic}X\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}X
(see for example
[
19
, 14.2.7]
). Given a line bundle
\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}X
we can use this injection and the exact sequence
5.8
to build a divisor
D=\sum a_{\rho}D_{\rho}
such that
\mathcal{L}\cong\mathcal{O}(D)
, and this divisor is well defined up to an element of
M
. Notice that essentially by definition
\mathcal{L}
is globally generated if and only if
D
is basepoint free. Furthermore, we can associate
\mathcal{L}^{n}
to the divisor
nD
. The dimension of the polytope from Defintion
5.13
isolated the important cohomological degree in the Batyrev-Borisov vanishing theorem, and we can now access that integer in the perfectoid setting.

Definition 5.16
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete fan,
X=X_{\Sigma}
and
{\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}}\to X
the associated perfectoid cover. Assume that the Picard group of the rigid space
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
trivializes on the affinoid cover induced by
\Sigma
. Fix
\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}{\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}}
and use Theorem
4.1
to identify it with
\mathcal{M}^{1/p^{k}}
for
\mathcal{M}\in\operatorname{Pic}X
. Associate to
\mathcal{M}
a divisor
D
using sequence
5.8
, and consider the polytope
P_{D}
. We define

d_{\mathcal{L}}:=\dim P_{D}.

Lemma 5.17
.

The integer
d_{\mathcal{L}}
is well defined.

Proof.

Suppose
D
and
D^{\prime}
are two torus invariant divisors associated to
\mathcal{M}
. the sequence
5.8
asserts that
D
and
D^{\prime}
differ by the divisor of some
m\in M
, so that by
[
4
, Section 4.3]
(1)
P_{D^{\prime}}
is a translate of
P_{D}
by
m
, and hence share dimension. The choice of
\mathcal{M}
is well defined up to a power of
p
, which may replace
D
with
p^{t}D
. Again by
[
4
, Section 4.3]

P_{p^{t}D}=p^{t}P_{D}
is a scaling and hence the dimension is unchanged.
∎

We can now prove the result.

Theorem 5.18

(Batyrev-Borisov Vanishing in the Perfectoid Setting)
.

Consider the setup as in Definition
5.16
, and suppose
\mathcal{L}
is globally generated. For all
i\not=d_{\mathcal{L}}
:

\mathrm{H}^{i}({\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}},\mathcal{L}^{-1})=0

If
i=d_{\mathcal{L}}
we have an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{d_{\mathcal{L}}}({\mathcal{X}^{\textit{perf}}},\mathcal{L}^{-1})\cong\left(\operatorname{colim}_{n}\left(\bigoplus_{m\in\operatorname{Relint}(p^{n}P_{D})\cap M}K\cdot\chi^{-m}\right)\right)^{\wedge{}}

where the transition maps are induced by
\chi^{m}\mapsto\chi^{pm}
.

Proof.

For each
n
, we have
\mathcal{M}^{-p^{n}}=\mathcal{O}(-p^{n}D)
, and as in Lemma
5.17
the dimension of
P_{p^{n}D}
is
d_{\mathcal{L}}
. As
\mathcal{L}
is globally generated, Proposition
5.11
implies that
\mathcal{M}^{p^{t}}
is globally generated for
t
large enough, and therefore for large
t
the divisor
p^{t}D
is basepoint free. The results now follow, applying Theorem
5.15
to all large enough
p
-powers of
\mathcal{M}
, passing to the completed direct limit, and applying Theorem
5.2
.
∎

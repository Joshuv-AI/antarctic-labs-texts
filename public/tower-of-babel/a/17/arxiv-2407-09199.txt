Abstract
. Rudin’s version of the classical Julia-Wolff-Carathéodory theorem is a cornerstone of holomorphic function theory in the unit ball of ℂ d \mathbb{C}^{d} . In this paper we obtain a complete generalization of Rudin’s theorem for a holomorphic map f : D → D ′ f\colon D\to D^{\prime} between convex domains of finite type. In particular, given a point ξ ∈ ∂ D \xi\in\partial D with finite dilation we show that the K K -limit of f f at ξ \xi exists and is a point η ∈ ∂ D ′ \eta\in\partial D^{\prime} , and we obtain asymptotic estimates for all entries of the Jacobian matrix of the differential d ​ f z df_{z} in terms of the multitypes at the points ξ \xi and at η \eta . We introduce a generalization of Bracci-Patrizio-Trapani’s pluricomplex Poisson kernel which, together with the dilation at ξ \xi , gives a formula for the restricted K K -limit of the normal component of the normal derivative ⟨ d ​ f z ​ ( n ξ ) , n η ⟩ \langle df_{z}(n_{\xi}),n_{\eta}\rangle . Our principal tools are methods from Gromov hyperbolicity theory, a scaling in the normal direction, and the strong asymptoticity of complex geodesics. To obtain our main result we prove a conjecture by Abate on the Kobayashi type of a vector v v , proving that it is equal to the reciprocal of the line type of v v , and we give new extrinsic characterizations of both K K -convergence and restricted convergence to a point ξ ∈ ∂ D \xi\in\partial D in terms of the multitype at ξ \xi .

Introduction

The Julia–Wolff–Carathéodory theorem is a classical result in the theory of one complex variable collecting results obtained by the three authors in the decade 1920-1930
[

62

f\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}

\lambda_{\xi}:=\liminf_{z\to\xi}\frac{1-|f(z)|}{1-|z|}

is finite
1
1

1

Notice that the dilation is never 0.

\angle\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta,

The Julia–Wolff–Carathéodory theorem shows that this intuition is correct.

Theorem 1.1

(Julia–Wolff–Carathéodory)

Let

In his book on function theory in the unit ball
[
59
]

. There are two main issues which make this setting radically different from the case of the unit disc.
The first (obvious) one, is that instead of dealing with just a derivative, we have now a whole Jacobian matrix to consider, and as we will see the asymptotics of different entries will be different.

The second issue, more subtle, is that in
\mathbb{B}^{d}
there are two relevant generalizations of the concept of non-tangential convergence to a boundary point, and both are different from the concept one would at first expect, that is, convergence inside a cone with vertex

Indeed, as first remarked in 1969 by Korányi

Instead, Korányi introduced a natural concept of convergence to

Such regions have an anisotropic shape: they look like cones near

The second relevant notion of convergence is slightly more restrictive but still more general than convergence inside cones. It stems out of the Lindelöf principle, which is a main ingredient in the proof of the Julia–Wolff–Carathéodory theorem. It is natural to try to generalize this result to other bounded domains in several complex variables.
In 1990 Abate
[
2

A crucial point of his approach is the following: instead of working with objects defined in Euclidean terms, he redefines the dilation, horospheres,

As an example, the dilation
\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}

\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}=\liminf_{z\to\xi}k_{D}(p,z)-k_{D^{\prime}}(p^{\prime},f(z)),

(1.4)

where

Notice that, if

\xi\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{d}

\lambda_{\xi}:=\liminf_{z\to\xi}\frac{1-\|f(z)\|}{1-\|z\|}={\rm exp}\left(\liminf_{z\to\xi}{k_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}}(z,0)-k_{\mathbb{B}^{q}}(f(z),0)\right)=\lambda_{\xi,0,0}.

(1.5)

We are interested in the case of convex domains of finite type.
The D’Angelo finite type condition

For a convex domain with

If

The

The domain

Every vector in
\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus T^{\mathbb{C}}_{\xi}\partial D

\geq 2

In strongly convex domains every nonzero complex tangent vector has line type 2. In this paper we consider only the line type.

A partial Julia–Wolff–Carathéodory theorem in this setting was obtained by Abate-Tauraso
[
8

in 2002. They studied the case of a holomorphic function
f\colon D\to\mathbb{D}
defined on a bounded convex domain of finite type and with values in the unit disc. Their result needs technical assumptions:

is assumed to be a convex domain of finite type with

\varphi(1)=\xi

\lim_{t\to 1}\varphi^{\prime}(t)

Finally their result is expressed in terms of regions which are smaller than the

Their result highlights however a very interesting phenomenon: as

Another point to stress is that the choice of the bases

We use the following notations:

•

We denote by
(e_{j})_{j=0}^{d-1}
the canonical basis of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

•

For
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
we denote with
\langle u,v\rangle
the standard Hermitian product of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
\|u\|:=\sqrt{\langle u,u\rangle}
the standard Euclidean norm of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

•

Let
\mathbb{D}:=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:|\zeta|<1\}
and
\mathbb{B}^{d}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:\|z\|<1\}.
We denote by
\mathbb{H}:=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:{\operatorname{Re}\,}\zeta<0\}
the left half-plane. We will use the left half-plane instead of the more conventional right (or upper) half-plane because in this way the normal outer vector at the origin is 1, which makes the computations cleaner.

•

Denote

\mathscr{C}(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta-1}{\zeta+1},\quad\mathscr{C}^{-1}(\zeta)=\frac{1+\zeta}{1-\zeta}.

The biholomorphism
\mathscr{C}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{H}
is the Cayley transform which sends
1\in\partial\mathbb{D}
to
0\in\partial\mathbb{H}
.

•

If
D\subsetneq\mathbb{C}^{d}
is a domain and
p\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
let

\delta_{D}(p):=\min\{\|p-\xi\|:\xi\in\partial D\}.

•

If
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
is a Kobayashi hyperbolic domain, we denote by
k_{D}
its Kobayashi distance and by
\kappa_{D}
its Kobayashi–Royden metric. For all
z\in D
and
r>0
, we denote

B_{D}(z,r):=\{w\in D:k_{D}(z,w)<r\}.

•

By
curve
in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
we always mean a
continuous
function
\gamma\colon I\to\mathbb{C}^{d}
defined on an interval
I
.

We use the following normalizations (see e.g. Kobayashi
[
45
]
) for the
Poincaré metric and distance in the disc
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
:

\kappa_{\mathbb{D}}(z,v)=\frac{2|v|}{1-|z|^{2}},\quad k_{\mathbb{D}}(0,z)=\log\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}=2\,{\rm arctanh}(|z|).

As a consequence, if
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
is a domain, the Kobayashi–Royden
pseudometric is defined as follows: if
z\in D
,
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
,

\kappa_{D}(z,v)=\inf\left\{\frac{2}{R}\,:\,R>0,\exists\varphi\in\textrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D},D),\varphi(0)=z,\,\varphi^{\prime}(0)=Rv\right\}.

(2.1)

An important advantage of this choice of normalization is that, as a result, the definition of a central concept such as the dilation of a map at a boundary point is given by the same exact formula (
1.4
) in both the complex case and in the metric non-expanding case. In other words, the dilation of a holomorphic map
f
coincides with the dilation of
f
considered as a non-expanding map between metric spaces, see also
[
12
,
13
]
.
Some authors normalize differently the Poincaré metric in the disc, dividing it by 2. As a consequence they define the Kobayashi–Royden pseudometric
\kappa_{D}(z,v)
as the above formula (
2.1
) divided by 2. Similar considerations hold for the Kobayashi distance.

We now review some basic definitions for convex domains of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

Definition 2.1

(
\mathbb{C}
-proper)
.

A convex domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
is
\mathbb{C}
-proper
if
D
does not contain a complex affine line.

Remark 2.2
.

Harris
[
40
]
and Barth
[
15
]
(see also
[
24
]
) showed that for a convex domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
the following are equivalent:

(1)

D
is
\mathbb{C}
-proper,

(2)

D
is Kobayashi hyperbolic,

(3)

D
is complete Kobayashi hyperbolic.

Definition 2.3

(Line type)
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a convex domain. Let
L\geq 2
, and assume that
\partial D
is of class
C^{L}
in a neighborhood of a boundary point
\xi\in\partial D
. Let

r
be a defining function of class
C^{L}
for
\partial D
in a neighborhood of
\xi
.
We say that the point
\xi
has
finite (line) type

L
if:

(1)

for every
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
the function

\zeta\mapsto r(\xi+\zeta v)
, defined on
\mathbb{C}
, has order of vanishing
smaller than or equal to
L
at
0
(we denote such number as
m_{\xi}(v)
and we call it the
type
of
v
at
\xi
), and

(2)

there exists
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
with type
m_{\xi}(v)=L
.

Notice that the type
m_{\xi}(v)
of a vector is necessarily an even number.

We say that a point
\xi\in\partial D
has
locally finite (line) type
if there exist
L\geq 2
and a neighborhood
U
of
\xi
such that
\partial D\cap U
is of class
C^{L}
and every point in
\partial D\cap U
has line type at most
L
.
Finally, we say that the convex domain
D
has
finite (line) type
if there exists
L\geq 2
such that the boundary
\partial D
is of class
C^{L}
and every point has line type at most
L
.

Remark 2.4
.

At a strongly convex boundary point
\xi\in\partial D
the line type is
2
. The same is true at a strongly pseudoconvex boundary point of
D
.
If
\partial D
is
C^{\infty}
then
McNeal
[
52
]
proved that
\xi
has finite line type
L
if and only if it has finite D’Angelo type
L
.
It then follows from
[
33
, Theorem 2 p. 131]
that if
\partial D
is
C^{\infty}
then
\xi
has finite line type if and only if it has locally finite line type.

In this paper we consider only the line type, hence we will call it type
tout-court
.

3.
Gromov hyperbolicity methods

Zimmer
[
63
]
proved that if
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
is a bounded convex domain of finite type, then the metric space
(D,k_{D})
is Gromov hyperbolic and its Gromov compactification is canonically homeomorphic to
\overline{D}
. Gromov hyperbolicity is a coarse notion of negative curvature that has numerous applications in complex geometry (see for example
[
37
,
16
,
64
,
20
,
21
,
35
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
).
In this section we review the metric properties of bounded convex domains of finite type inherited from Gromov hyperbolicity, and then we show how to adapt them to our setting (that is, near a point of locally finite type of a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain) via a localization result.

Definition 3.1
.

(Geodesics, almost-geodesics, quasi-geodesics)
Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a Kobayashi hyperbolic domain.
A
geodesic
is a map
\gamma
from an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
to
D
which is an isometry with respect to the Euclidean distance on
I
and the Kobayashi distance on
D
, that is for all
s,t\in I
,

k_{D}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=|t-s|.

If the interval is
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
(resp. closed and bounded) we call
\gamma
a
geodesic ray
(resp.
geodesic segment
).

We say that a map
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to D
is an
almost-geodesic ray
if for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
T_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for all
s,t\geq T_{\varepsilon}
,

|t-s|-\varepsilon\leq k_{D}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq|t-s|.

Now fix
A\geq 1
,
B\geq 0
.
A (non-necessarily continuous) map
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to D
is a
(A,B)

quasi-geodesic ray
if for every
t,s\geq 0

A^{-1}|t-s|-B\leq k_{D}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq A|t-s|+B.

Clearly every almost-geodesic is a
(1,B)
quasi-geodesic for some
B\geq 0
.

Finally, a map
\gamma\colon[0,T)\to D
, where
T\in(0,+\infty]
, is said to have
endpoint

\gamma(T)=\xi\in\partial D
if
\lim_{t\to T}\gamma(t)=\xi
.

Remark 3.2
.

If
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and
\xi\in\partial D
is a point of locally finite type, then it follows by
the generalized Hopf-Rinow theorem that any two points of
D
can be connected by a geodesic segment, and it follows from Proposition
4.3
below that, given any point
p\in D
, there exists a geodesic ray
\gamma
with endpoint
\xi
and with
\gamma(0)=p
.

Definition 3.3
.

(Gromov product)
Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a Kobayashi hyperbolic domain. For all
z,w,p\in D
define the
Gromov product

(z|w)_{p}
as follows:

(z|w)_{p}:=\frac{1}{2}[k_{D}(z,p)+k_{D}(w,p)-k_{D}(z,w)].

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain of finite type.

(i)

(Boundary and Gromov product) If
\xi,\eta
are two distinct points in
\partial D
, then

\limsup_{(z,w)\to(\xi,\eta)}(z|w)_{p}<+\infty.

(ii)

(4-point condition) there exists
\delta\geq 0
such that for all
z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},p\in D
we have

(z_{1}|z_{2})_{p}\geq\min\{(z_{1}|z_{3})_{p},(z_{2}|z_{3})_{p}\}-\delta.

(iii)

(Asymptoticity of (1,B) quasi-geodesics) Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to D
be two
(1,B)
quasi-geodesic rays with the same endpoint
\xi\in\partial D
. Then

\sup_{t\geq 0}k_{D}(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t))<\infty.

Proof.

It is proved in
[
63
]
that
(D,k_{D})
is Gromov hyperbolic and that the identity map
\mathrm{id}_{D}\colon D\to D
extends to an homeomorphism between the Gromov compactification and
\overline{D}
.
Points (i) and (ii) are classical results for proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric spaces, see e.g.
[
18
,
31
]
. For a proof of (iii), see
[
12
, Lemma 5.8]
.
∎

In order to adapt these properties to our setting, we will use the following localization argument.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.
Let
L\geq 2
be given by Definition
2.3
. Let
W
be a neighborhood of
\xi
such that

(1)

\tilde{D}:=D\cap W
is a bounded convex domain;

(2)

the boundary
\partial\tilde{D}
is of class
C^{L}
;

(3)

every point of
\partial\tilde{D}
has line type at most
L
.

The domain
\tilde{D}
enjoys the visibility property (see for instance
[
21
]
for the definition of visibility), hence it follows from
[
21
, Theorem 1.4]
that for every neighborhood
V\subset\subset W
of
\xi
there exists
C\geq 0
such that

k_{D}(z,w)\leq k_{\tilde{D}}(z,w)\leq k_{D}(z,w)+C,\quad\forall\,z,w\in V.

(3.1)

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain. We denote by
\partial^{*}D
the boundary of
D
in the one-point compactification of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain, and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.

(i)

Let
\eta\in\partial^{*}D\setminus\{\xi\}
and
p\in D
. Then

\limsup_{(z,w)\to(\xi,\eta)}(z|w)_{p}<+\infty.

(ii)

There exist
\delta^{\prime}\geq 0
and a neighborhood
V
of
\xi
such that for all
z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},p\in D\cap V
we have

(z_{1}|z_{2})_{p}\geq\min\{(z_{1}|z_{3})_{p},(z_{2}|z_{3})_{p}\}-\delta^{\prime}.

(iii)

If
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to D
are two
(1,B)
quasi-geodesic rays with endpoint
\xi
, then

\sup_{t\geq 0}k_{D}(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t))<+\infty.

Proof.

Let
W
,
\tilde{D}
and
C
be given by Remark
3.5
. Let
V\subset\subset W
be a neighborhood of
\xi
.

(i)
Denote by
(\cdot|\cdot)^{D}_{p}
the Gromov product with respect to
k_{D}
.
Notice that we can suppose that
p\in V
, indeed for all
z,w,p,q\in D
, by the triangle inequality we have
|(z|w)^{D}_{p}-(z|w)^{D}_{q}|\leq k_{D}(p,q)
.

By contradiction, assume there exist
\eta\in\partial^{*}D\backslash\{\xi\}
and
z_{n}\to\xi
,
w_{n}\to\eta
such that

(z_{n}|w_{n})^{D}_{p}\to+\infty.

For all
n\geq 0
let
\gamma_{n}\colon[0,T_{n}]\to D
be a geodesic segment with
\gamma_{n}(0)=z_{n}
,
\gamma_{n}(T_{n})=w_{n}
. Let
B\subset\subset V
be a small Euclidean ball centered in
\xi
such that
\eta\notin B
.
Let
N\geq 0
such that
z_{n}\in B
for all
n\geq N
. Then for all
n\geq N

there exists
t_{n}\in(0,T_{n}]
such that
\gamma_{n}(t_{n})\in D\cap\partial B
. Set
w^{\prime}_{n}:=\gamma_{n}(t_{n})
. We have

\displaystyle 2(z_{n}|w_{n})^{D}_{p}

\displaystyle=k_{D}(z_{n},p)+k_{D}(w_{n},p)-k_{D}(z_{n},w_{n})

\displaystyle=k_{D}(z_{n},p)+k_{D}(w_{n},p)-k_{D}(z_{n},w^{\prime}_{n})-k_{D}(w^{\prime}_{n},w_{n})

\displaystyle\leq k_{D}(z_{n},p)+k_{D}(w^{\prime}_{n},p)-k_{D}(z_{n},w^{\prime}_{n})=2(z_{n}|w^{\prime}_{n})^{D}_{p}.

Since
p\in V
and
z_{n},w^{\prime}_{n}\in V
for all
n\geq N
, by (
3.1
) we have

(z_{n}|w_{n}^{\prime})^{\tilde{D}}_{p}\geq(z_{n}|w_{n}^{\prime})^{D}_{p}-\frac{C}{2},

and thus
(z_{n}|w_{n}^{\prime})^{\tilde{D}}_{p}\to+\infty.

It easily follows that the sequence
(w_{n}^{\prime})
is not relatively compact in
\tilde{D}
, and thus, up to a subsequence, we can assume that it converges to a point
\eta^{\prime}\in\partial\tilde{D}\cap\partial B
. This contradicts (i) of Theorem
3.4
because
\eta^{\prime}\neq\xi
.

(ii) By (
3.1
) and (ii) in Theorem
3.4
there exists
\delta\geq 0
such that for all
z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},p\in V

(z_{1}|z_{2})^{D}_{p}\geq\min\{(z_{1}|z_{3})^{D}_{p},(z_{2}|z_{3})^{D}_{p}\}-\frac{3C}{2}-\delta.

(iii) Let
T\geq 0
be such that
\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t)\in V
for all
t\geq T
.
By (
3.1
) the curves
\gamma_{1}|_{[T,+\infty)}
and
\gamma_{2}|_{[T,+\infty)}
are
(1,B+C)
quasi-geodesics with respect to
k_{\tilde{D}}
, so we conclude with (iii) of Theorem
3.4
.
∎

Point (i) of the previous proposition immediately implies the following.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain, and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type. If
(z_{n}),(w_{n})
are sequences in
D
such that
z_{n}\to\xi
and
(k_{D}(z_{n},w_{n}))_{n}
is bounded, then
w_{n}\to\xi
.

We end this section with an estimate of the Kobayashi distance near the boundary which will be useful later on.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain, and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.
There exists a neighborhood
V
of
\xi
such that for all
p\in D
there exists
c\geq 0
such that

-\log\delta_{D}(z)-c\leq k_{D}(z,p)\leq-\log\delta_{D}(z)+c,\quad\forall\,z\in D\cap V.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{D}
and
V
be given by Remark
3.5
.
By
[
8
, Lemma 1.1, Lemma 1.3]
given
z_{0}\in V\cap D
there exists a constant
c^{\prime}\geq 0
such that

-\log\delta_{\tilde{D}}(z)-c^{\prime}\leq k_{\tilde{D}}(z,z_{0})\leq-\log\delta_{\tilde{D}}(z)+c^{\prime},\quad\forall z\in\tilde{D}.

Up to taking a smaller neighborhood
V
of
\xi
we clearly have
\delta_{D}=\delta_{\tilde{D}}
on
V\cap D
.
We conclude using (
3.1
).
∎

4.
Complex geodesics

Definition 4.1

(Complex geodesics)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a Kobayashi hyperbolic domain. An
extremal map
is a holomorphic map
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D

such that there exist distinct points
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathbb{D}
satisfying

k_{D}(\varphi(\zeta_{1}),\varphi(\zeta_{2}))=k_{\mathbb{D}}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}).

A
complex geodesic
is a holomorphic map
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
such that
k_{D}(\varphi(\zeta_{1}),\varphi(\zeta_{2}))=k_{\mathbb{D}}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})
for all
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathbb{D}
.
If
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
is a complex geodesic, a holomorphic map
\tilde{\rho}\colon D\to\mathbb{D}
is called a
left inverse
of
\varphi
if
\tilde{\rho}\circ\varphi=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{D}}.

Analogous definitions hold for holomorphic maps defined in the left half-plane
\mathbb{H}
. In particular we say that a holomorphic map
\varphi\colon\mathbb{H}\to D
is a complex geodesic if
k_{D}(\varphi(\zeta_{1}),\varphi(\zeta_{2}))=k_{\mathbb{H}}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})
for all
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathbb{H}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain.
Then

(i)

every extremal map
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
is a complex geodesic;

(ii)

for any two distinct points
z,w\in D
there exists a complex geodesic
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
such that
\varphi(0)=z
and
\varphi(\tanh(k_{D}(z,w)/2))=w
;

(iii)

every complex geodesic admits a left inverse.

Proof.

Follows from
[
24
]
and Proposition 11.1.4, Proposition 11.1.7 and Theorem 11.2.1 in
[
42
]
.
∎

The following result was proved by Abate
[
6
]
in the case of bounded strongly convex domains with
C^{3}
boundary. Our proof is based on a Gromov hyperbolicity method.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.
Let
p\in D
. Then there exists a complex geodesic
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
with
\varphi(0)=p
and

\lim_{\mathbb{R}\ni t\to 1^{-}}\varphi(t)=\xi.

Proof.

Let
p\in D
. Let
(z_{n})
be a sequence in
D
converging to
\xi
.
For all
n\geq 0
let
\varphi_{n}
be a complex geodesic such that
\varphi_{n}(0)=p
and
\varphi_{n}(t_{n})=z_{n}
,
where
t_{n}:=\tanh(k_{D}(p,z_{n})/2)\to 1.

Since
D
is taut, up to a subsequence we can assume that
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
uniformly on compact sets. Clearly
\varphi
is a complex geodesic of
D
.
We show that
\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}\varphi(t)=\xi
. Assume by contradiction that this is false, that is, there exists a sequence
(s_{k})
in
[0,1)
converging to
1
such that
\varphi(s_{k})
converges to a point
\eta\in\partial^{*}D
different from
\xi
.
Since
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
, it follows that for all
k\geq 0
we can find
n_{k}
such that

\|\varphi(s_{k})-\varphi_{n_{k}}(s_{k})\|<\frac{1}{k},

in particular
\varphi_{n_{k}}(s_{k})\to\eta
.

Now

(\varphi_{n_{k}}(t_{n_{k}})|\varphi_{n_{k}}(s_{k}))_{p}=\min\{k_{\mathbb{D}}(0,t_{n_{k}}),k_{\mathbb{D}}(0,s_{k})\}\to+\infty,

which contradicts (i) of Proposition
3.6
.

∎

Definition 4.4
.

Denote by
\gamma_{\mathbb{D}}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{D}
the geodesic ray in
\mathbb{D}
with starting point
0
and endpoint
1
, that is

\gamma_{\mathbb{D}}(t)={\rm\tanh}(t/2).

Denote by
\gamma_{\mathbb{H}}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to D
the geodesic ray in
\mathbb{H}
with starting point
-1
and endpoint
0
,
that is
\gamma_{\mathbb{H}}(t)=\mathscr{C}\circ\gamma_{\mathbb{D}}=-e^{-t}.

Let now
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain.
If
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
(resp.
\varphi\colon\mathbb{H}\to D
) is a holomorphic map, we denote by
\tilde{\varphi}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to D
the curve
\tilde{\varphi}=\varphi\circ\gamma_{\mathbb{D}}
(resp.
\varphi\circ\gamma_{\mathbb{H}}
).
If the curve
\tilde{\varphi}
has an endpoint
\xi\in\partial D
, then we say that
\xi
is the
endpoint
of
\varphi
.
Notice that if
\varphi
is a complex geodesic, then
\tilde{\varphi}
is a geodesic ray.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain. Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
(resp.
\varphi\colon\mathbb{H}\to D
) be a holomorphic map. We say that the point
1\in\partial\mathbb{D}
(resp.

) is a
regular contact point
of
\varphi

if the curve
\tilde{\varphi}
is an almost-geodesic ray of

with endpoint of locally finite type

.
By Corollary
3.7
this implies
that
\varphi
has non-tangential limit
\xi
at

(resp. at 0).

Remark 4.6
.

In Section
10
we will show that regular contact points can be defined equivalently as
points of finite dilation (see Definition

and Corollary

). We will also extend the definition of regular contact points to the case of holomorphic maps between two
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains.

Remark 4.7
.

Clearly if
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
(resp.
\varphi\colon\mathbb{H}\to D
) is a complex geodesic in a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain
D
with endpoint of locally finite type

, then 1 (resp. 0) is a regular contact point.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a bounded strongly convex domain with
C^{3}
boundary, and let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
be a complex geodesic with endpoint
\xi\in\partial D
. Then
\varphi

5.
Multitype

We review the flag of complex subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
introduced by Yu
[
61
]
.
Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of finite type
L\geq 2
. Set
m_{\xi}(0):=+\infty
.
For all integers
m\geq 1
define

S_{m}:=\{v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:m_{\xi}(v)\geq m\}.

Yu shows, using the convexity of
D
, that the sets
S_{m}
are complex linear subspaces.
It immediately follows from the definition that
S_{1}=\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
S_{m_{2}}\subseteq S_{m_{1}}
for
m_{1}\leq m_{2}
and that
S_{m}=\{0\}
if
m>L
. Moreover
S_{2}
coincides with the complex tangent subspace
T_{\xi}^{\mathbb{C}}\partial D
, indeed

v\in S_{2}\iff\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}r(\xi+\zeta v)|_{\zeta=0}=0\iff\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{\partial r}{\partial z_{j}}(\xi)v_{j}=0.

Now set
l_{1}=1
. For all
j\geq 1
define recursively
l_{j}>l_{j-1}
as the smallest integer such that
S_{l_{j}}\subsetneq S_{l_{j-1}}
. This procedure stops when we find an integer
j
such that

l_{j}=L
. We denote such integer
k
.
We thus obtain the
multitype flag
of complex subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
:

\{0\}\subsetneq S_{L}\subsetneq S_{l_{k-1}}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq S_{1}=\mathbb{C}^{d}.

We say that an orthonormal basis
v_{0},\dots,v_{d-1}
of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
is a
multitype basis
at
\xi
if

v_{0}
is the outer normal versor
n_{\xi}
at
\xi
, and if the basis
v_{0},\dots,v_{d-1}

is adapted to the multitype flag (and presented in reverse order), that is,
for all
j=0,\dots,k-1
the last
{\rm dim}\,S_{l_{k-j}}
vectors are a basis of the subspace
S_{l_{k-j}}
.
For all
j=1,\dots,d-1
set
m_{j}:=m_{\xi}(v_{j})
.

Following Yu, we call the vector
(m_{0},m_{1},\cdots,m_{d-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
the
(linear) multitype
of
\partial D
at
\xi
. If
\partial D
is smoothly bounded, then it is proved in
[
61
]
that
(m_{0},m_{1},\cdots,m_{d-1})
coincides with the multitype in the sense Catlin (see
[
27
]
). Notice that
m_{0}=1
and
2\leq m_{j}\leq L
if
j=1,\dots,d-1
.

By an affine unitary change of coordinates we can assume that
\xi=0
and that
v_{j}=e_{j}
for
j=0,\dots,d-1
.
We call the new coordinates
multitype coordinates
. In multitype coordinates the outer normal versor at the origin is
n_{0}=e_{0}
and
the hypersurface
\partial D
has the following defining function in a neighborhood of the origin (see e.g.
[
36
]
)

r(z)={\operatorname{Re}\,}z_{0}+H(z_{1},\cdots,z_{d-1})+R(z),

(5.1)

where
H\colon\mathbb{C}^{d-1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a convex non-negative polynomial which is

(1)

non-degenerate
, that is the set
\{H=0\}
does not contain any complex line;

(2)

weighted homogeneous
with respect to
(m_{1},\dots,m_{d-1})
, i.e. for all
t>0
and
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{d-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{d-1}
we have

H\left(t^{1/\penalty m_{1}}z_{1},\dots,t^{1/\penalty m_{d-1}}z_{d-1}\right)=tH(z_{1},\cdots,z_{d-1}).

The remainder satisfies

R(z)=o\bigg(|z_{0}|+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}|z_{j}|^{m_{j}}\bigg).

We end this section introducing a dual version of the type of a vector at
\xi
. Denote by
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{*}
the dual vector space of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of finite type
L\geq 2
.
If
\theta\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, then we define its
cotype

M_{\xi}(\theta)
at
\xi
as the integer
1\leq l_{j_{0}}\leq L
, where

j_{0}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\{j:\theta\not\in{\rm Ann}(S_{j})\},

and
{\rm Ann}(S_{j})
denotes the annihilator of
S_{j}
in
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{*}
.
Notice that, since
S_{j}
is a decreasing sequence of subspaces, if
\theta\in{\rm Ann}(S_{j})
, then
\theta\in{\rm Ann}(S_{i})
for all
i\geq j
.
If
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
we
define the
cotype

M_{\xi}(v)
of
v
as the cotype of the linear functional
w\mapsto\langle w,v\rangle
.

Remark 5.2
.

If
v_{0},\dots,v_{d-1}
is a multitype basis at
\xi
and
v=\sum a_{j}v_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, then
it is easy to see that
M_{\xi}(v)=\max\{m_{j}:a_{j}\neq 0\}
.
Notice that
m_{\xi}(v)=\min\{m_{j}:a_{j}\neq 0\}
, so in particular we have
M_{\xi}(v_{j})=m_{\xi}(v_{j})
for all
j=0,\dots,d-1
.

6.
Scaling

In this section we recall Gaussier’s scaling in the normal direction and we use it to prove several properties of complex geodesics with endpoint of locally finite type.

Definition 6.1
.

If
H\colon\mathbb{C}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex non-negative non-degenerate weighted homogeneous polynomial
we call the domain

D_{H}:=\{(z_{0},w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{d-1}:{\operatorname{Re}\,}z_{0}+H(w)<0\},\quad

a
scaling model
.
If
D
is a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain with a point of locally finite type at the origin in multitype coordinates,
then we say that the domain
D_{H}
, where
H
is the polynomial given by (
5.1
), is the
scaling model
of
D
.

We recall that the
Hausdorff distance
between two compact sets
X,Y\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
is given by

d_{H}(X,Y):=\max\left\{\sup_{x\in X}\inf_{y\in Y}\|x-y\|,\sup_{y\in Y}\inf_{x\in X}\|x-y\|\right\}.

Moreover, a sequence of convex domains
(D_{n})
in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
converges in the
local Hausdorff topology
to a convex domain
D_{\infty}\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
if, for all
r>0
,

\lim_{n\to+\infty}d_{H}(\overline{D_{n}}\cap\overline{r\mathbb{B}^{d}},\overline{D_{\infty}}\cap\overline{r\mathbb{B}^{d}})=0.

The following theorem is proved in
[
36
]
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain with a point of locally finite type at the origin in multitype coordinates.
Let
(\lambda_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
converging to
+\infty
and for all
n\geq 0
define the linear map
A_{n}\colon\mathbb{C}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}^{d}
by

A_{n}(z)=(\lambda_{n}z_{0},\lambda_{n}^{1/m_{1}}z_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}^{1/m_{d-1}}z_{d-1}).

Then the domain
A_{n}D
converges in the local Hausdorff topology to the scaling model
D_{H}
of
D
.

Points (i) and (ii) of the following corollary are proved in
[
63
, Lemma 4.4]
and
[
38
, Theorem 9.1]
respectively. For lack of a reference of point (iii) we give a short proof.

Corollary 6.3
.

In the assumptions of the previous theorem,

(i)

every compact subset
K\subset D_{H}
is eventually contained in the domain
A_{n}D
;

(ii)

we have
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\kappa_{A_{n}D}=\kappa_{D_{H}}
and
\lim_{n\to+\infty}k_{A_{n}D}=k_{D_{H}}

uniformly on compact sets of
D_{H}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
D_{H}\times D_{H}
, respectively;

(iii)

if
0<r<R
and
z\in D_{H}
, then eventually
B_{A_{n}D}(z,r)\subseteq B_{D_{H}}(z,R).

Proof.

Let
0<r<R
and let
z\in D_{H}
. Notice that by (i) the point
z
is eventually contained in
A_{n}D
.
Fix
0<\delta<1
such that
k_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\delta)>r
. Then by
[
63
, Lemma 4.6]
there exists
N_{1}\geq 0
such that for all
n\geq N_{1}
, if
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to A_{n}D
is holomorphic with
\varphi(0)=z
, then
\varphi(\delta\mathbb{D})\subset D_{H}
.

We claim that there exists
C>0
such that for all
n\geq N_{1}
we have
\overline{B_{A_{n}D}(z,r)}\subseteq\overline{B_{D_{H}}(z,C)}
. Indeed, let
n\geq N_{1}
, let
w\in\overline{B_{A_{n}D}(z,r)}
and consider a complex geodesic
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to A_{n}D
such that
\varphi(0)=z
and
\varphi(t)=w
with
t:=\tanh(k_{D}(z,w)/2)
.
The map
\varphi_{\delta}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}^{d}
given by
\varphi_{\delta}(\zeta):=\varphi(\delta\zeta)
has image in
D_{H}
. Notice that
t\leq\tanh(r/2)<\delta
, so

k_{D_{H}}(z,w)=k_{D_{H}}(\varphi_{\delta}(0),\varphi_{\delta}(t/\delta))\leq k_{\mathbb{D}}(0,t/\delta)\leq k_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\tanh(r/2)/\penalty\delta)=:C,

which proves the claim.
The result now follows using (ii) on the compact subset
K:=\overline{B_{D_{H}}(z,C)}
.
∎

Notice that in multitype coordinates the inner normal segment
\sigma\colon[t_{0},1)\to\mathbb{C}^{d}
with endpoint

is given by

\sigma(t)=(t-1,0)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{d-1}.

Corollary 6.4
.

In the assumptions of the previous theorem,
if
(z_{n})
is a sequence in
D
converging to 0 and there exists
M\geq 0
such that
k_{D}(z_{n},\sigma)\leq M
for all

, then there exist
\lambda_{n}\to+\infty
and
N\geq 0

(A_{n}z_{n})_{n\geq N}
is relatively compact in

.

Proof.

Let
(a_{n})
be a sequence of points in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
k_{D}(z_{n},(-a_{n},0))\leq M
for all
n\geq 0
. Clearly
a_{n}\to 0
. Set
\lambda_{n}:=\frac{1}{a_{n}}.
Then for all
n\geq 0
we have
A_{n}(-a_{n},0)=(-1,0)
and

k_{A_{n}D}(A_{n}z_{n},(-1,0))\leq M
. By (iii) of Corollary

, given
M^{\prime}>M

N\geq 0
such that for all

A_{n}z_{n}\in B_{A_{n}D}((-1,0),M)\subset B_{D_{H}}((-1,0),M^{\prime})\subset\subset D_{H}.

∎

Definition 6.5
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi
be a point of locally finite type in
\partial D
.
Two holomorphic maps
\varphi,\psi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
with a regular contact point at 1 and with endpoint
\xi
are
strongly asymptotic

if there exists

T\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}k_{D}(\tilde{\varphi}(t),\tilde{\psi}(t+T))=0.

In the next proposition we prove that if we rescale any holomorphic map
\varphi:\mathbb{H}\to D
with a regular contact point at
0
and endpoint at the origin we obtain in the limit the “slice” complex geodesic
\zeta\mapsto(\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(0)\zeta,0)
of the model domain
D_{H}
.
This considerably generalizes
[
12
, Lemma 5.13]
, where
D
is assumed to be a bounded convex domain of finite type and where
\varphi
is assumed to be a complex geodesic.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain with a point of locally finite type at the origin in multitype coordinates.
Let
D_{H}
be the scaling model of
D
.
Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{H}\to D
be a holomorphic map with a regular contact point at
0
and endpoint the origin. Let
(\lambda_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
converging to
+\infty
. Then the sequence
(A_{n}\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{-1}\cdot))
converges uniformly on compact subsets to the complex geodesic
\hat{\varphi}\colon\mathbb{H}\to D_{H}
defined by

\hat{\varphi}(\zeta)=(\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(0)\zeta,0).

Proof.

By the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 5.13]
the sequence
(A_{n}\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{-1}\cdot))
converges (up to a subsequence) uniformly on compact subsets to a holomorphic
map
\hat{\varphi}\colon\mathbb{D}\to D_{H}
such that if we write

\hat{\varphi}(\zeta)=(\hat{\varphi}_{0}(\zeta),\hat{\varphi}_{1}(\zeta))\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{d-1},

we have

\hat{\varphi}_{0}(\zeta)=\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(0)\zeta.

Fix
s,t<0
. We claim that
k_{D_{H}}(\hat{\varphi}(s),\hat{\varphi}(t)))=k_{\mathbb{H}}(s,t).

Indeed

k_{D_{H}}(\hat{\varphi}(s),\hat{\varphi}(t))=\lim_{n\to+\infty}k_{A_{n}D}(A_{n}\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{-1}s),A_{n}\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{-1}t))\\
=\lim_{n\to+\infty}k_{D}(\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{-1}s),\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{-1}t)).

Clearly for all
n\geq 0
we have
k_{D}(\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{-1}s),\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{-1}t))\leq k_{\mathbb{H}}(\lambda_{n}^{-1}s,\lambda_{n}^{-1}t)=k_{\mathbb{H}}(s,t)
.
On the other hand, fix
\varepsilon>0
.
If
n
is large enough, then
\lambda_{n}^{-1}s
and
\lambda_{n}^{-1}t
are
>-1,
and thus

k_{D}(\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{-1}s),\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{-1}t))=k_{D}(\tilde{\varphi}(-\log(-\lambda_{n}^{-1}s)),\tilde{\varphi}(-\log(-\lambda_{n}^{-1}t))).

Since
\tilde{\varphi}
is an almost geodesic, if
n
is large enough we have

k_{D}(\tilde{\varphi}(-\log(-\lambda_{n}^{-1}s)),\tilde{\varphi}(-\log(-\lambda_{n}^{-1}t)))\geq\left|\log\frac{s}{t}\right|-\varepsilon=k_{\mathbb{H}}(s,t)-\varepsilon,

and thus
k_{D_{H}}(\hat{\varphi}(s),\hat{\varphi}(t)))\geq k_{\mathbb{H}}(s,t)-\varepsilon
, and the claim is proved.
It follows that
\hat{\varphi}\colon\mathbb{H}\to D_{H}
is an extremal map and thus by Proposition
4.2
the map
\hat{\varphi}
is a complex geodesic.
It now follows from the proof of
[
12

that
\hat{\varphi}_{1}\equiv 0
, and thus

\hat{\varphi}(\zeta)=(\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(0)\zeta,0).

∎

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.
Let
\varphi,\psi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
be two holomorphic maps with a regular contact point at 1 and endpoint
\xi
, and satisfying
\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(1)=\psi^{\prime}_{N}(1)
. Then

\angle\lim_{z\to 1}k_{D}(\varphi(z),\psi(z))=0.

Proof.

Consider multitype coordinates. We work with
\mathbb{H}
instead of
\mathbb{D}
, the result will follow by composing with the Cayley transform.
Let
\varphi,\psi\colon\mathbb{H}\to D
be two holomorphic maps with a regular contact point at 0 and endpoint
\xi
, and satisfying
\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(0)=\psi^{\prime}_{N}(0)=:c
.
By contradiction, suppose that there exists a sequence
(\zeta_{n})
in
\mathbb{H}
converging non-tangentially to 0 such that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}k_{D}(\varphi(\zeta_{n}),\psi(\zeta_{n}))=M>0.

For all
n\geq 0
set
\lambda_{n}:=|{\operatorname{Re}\,}\zeta_{n}|^{-1}
and define the maps
\varphi_{n}(z):=A_{n}\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{-1}z)
and
\psi_{n}(z):=A_{n}\psi(\lambda_{n}^{-1}z)
. By Proposition
6.6
the two sequences converge uniformly on compact subsets to

\hat{\varphi}(\zeta)=(c\zeta,0)=\hat{\psi}(\zeta).

Since
\zeta_{n}\to 0
non-tangentially, there exists
A>0
such that
|{\operatorname{Im}\,}\zeta_{n}|<A|{\operatorname{Re}\,}\zeta_{n}|
, and thus
\lambda_{n}\zeta_{n}
converges up to a subsequence to
\hat{\zeta}\in\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:{\operatorname{Re}\,}\zeta=-1,|{\operatorname{Im}\,}\zeta|\leq A\}\subset\subset\mathbb{H}
. It follows that

0<M=\lim_{n\to+\infty}k_{D}(\varphi(\zeta_{n}),\psi(\zeta_{n}))=\lim_{n\to+\infty}k_{A_{n}D}(A_{n}\varphi(\zeta_{n}),A_{n}\psi(\zeta_{n}))=k_{D_{H}}(\hat{\varphi}(\hat{\zeta}),\hat{\psi}(\hat{\zeta}))=0,

which gives a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.
Let
\varphi,\psi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
be two holomorphic maps with a regular contact point at 1 and with endpoint
\xi
.
Then
\varphi
and
\psi
are strongly asymptotic.

Proof.

Let
\lambda:=\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(0)/\psi^{\prime}_{N}(0)
, and define a holomorphic map
\theta\colon\mathbb{H}\to D
by
\theta(z)=\psi(\lambda z)
.
Then

\tilde{\theta}(t)=\psi(-e^{\log\lambda-t})=\tilde{\psi}(t-\log\lambda),

and
\theta^{\prime}_{N}(0)=\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(0)
, hence the result follow from Corollary
6.7
.
∎

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.
Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
be a holomorphic map with a regular contact point at 1 and with endpoint
\xi
, let
n_{\xi}
denote the outer normal versor in
\xi
. Then if we parametrize the inner normal segment at
\xi
as

\sigma(t)=\xi+(t-1)\varphi_{N}^{\prime}(1)n_{\xi},\quad t\in[t_{0},1),

we have

\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}k_{D}(\varphi(t),\sigma(t))=0.

Proof.

Consider multitype coordinates. Then
\sigma(t)=(\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(1)(t-1),0)
. Set
\psi:=\varphi\circ\mathscr{C}^{-1}
,
\theta(t):=\sigma\circ\mathscr{C}^{-1}(t)=\left(\frac{2t}{1-t}\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(1),0\right)
. We will show that

\lim_{t\to 0^{-}}k_{D}(\psi(t),\theta(t))=0.

Assume by contradiction that this is not the case. Then there exist a sequence
(t_{n})
in
\mathbb{R}_{<0}
converging to 0 and
M>0
such that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}k_{D}(\psi(t_{n}),\theta(t_{n}))=M>0.

Set
\lambda_{n}:=|t_{n}|^{-1}
. By Proposition
6.6
the rescaled sequence
(A_{n}\psi(\lambda^{-1}_{n}z))
converges uniformly on compact subsets to the map
\zeta\mapsto(2\varphi_{N}^{\prime}(1)\zeta,0)
(since by (
4.3
)
\psi^{\prime}_{N}(0)=2\varphi_{N}^{\prime}(1)
).
On the other hand, the rescaled sequence
(A_{n}\theta(\lambda^{-1}_{n}t))
converges uniformly on compact subsets to the map
t\mapsto(2\varphi_{N}^{\prime}(1)t,0)
, so with an argument similar to the one in Corollary
6.7
we obtain a contradiction.
∎

The strong asymptoticity of complex geodesics yields the existence of horospheres centered at points of locally finite type of
\partial D
. The proof is similar to the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 3.5]
, but due to its importance in this paper we include a shortened version for the convenience of the reader.

Theorem 6.10

(Existence of horospheres)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type. Fix a base-point
p\in D
. Then as
w\to\xi
the function
k_{D}(\cdot,w)-k_{D}(w,p)
converges uniformly on compact subsets of
D
to a function
h_{\xi,p}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
be a complex geodesic with endpoint
\xi
.
Then the family of functions

(k_{D}(\cdot,\varphi(t))-k_{D}(\varphi(t),\varphi(0)))_{t\in[0,1)}=(k_{D}(\cdot,\varphi(t))-t)_{t\in[0,1)}

is pointwise non-increasing, locally uniformly bounded family of 1-Lipschitz functions. Hence as
t\to 1^{-}
it converges to a function which can be easily seen to be
1
-Lipschitz, and thus by Dini’s theorem the convergence is uniform on compact subsets. It follows that
for every
p\in D
the family

(k_{D}(\cdot,\varphi(t))-k_{D}(\varphi(t),p))_{t\in[0,1)}

converges uniformly on compact subsets to a function
B_{\varphi}(z,p)
.
By Corollary
6.8
two complex geodesics of
D
with endpoint
\xi
are strongly asymptotic. It easily follows (see
[
12
, Proposition 3.3]
), that the function
B_{\varphi}(\,\cdot\,,p)
does not depend on the choice of
\varphi
.

We show that for any sequence
w_{n}\to\xi
in
D
we have

k_{D}(\,\cdot\,,w_{n})-k_{D}(w_{n},p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to+\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}B_{\varphi}(\,\cdot\,,p),

uniformly on compact subsets of
D
.
By the Ascoli–Arzelà Theorem it is enough to prove that for all
z\in D
, every convergent subsequence
\left(k_{D}(z,w_{n_{k}})-k_{D}(w_{n_{k}},p)\right)
of the sequence
(k_{D}(z,w_{n})-k_{D}(w_{n},p))
converges to
B_{\varphi}(z,p)
.
Let
(\varphi_{k}\colon\mathbb{D}\to D)
be a sequence of complex geodesics with
\varphi(0)=z
and
\varphi(r_{k})=w_{n_{k}}
with
r_{k}\in[0,1)
. Up to extracting another subsequence we can assume that
(\varphi_{k})
converges uniformly on compact subsets to a complex geodesic
\varphi
. Arguing as in Proposition
4.3
we see that
\varphi
has endpoint
\xi
.
Similarly we obtain a sequence
(\psi_{k}\colon\mathbb{D}\to D)
of complex geodesics with
\psi_{k}(0)=p
and
\psi_{k}(s_{k})=w_{n_{k}}
with
s_{k}\in[0,1)
, converging uniformly on compact subsets to a complex geodesic
\psi
with endpoint
\xi
.
For
a\in(-1,1)
define the automorphism of the disc
\tau_{a}(z)=\frac{z-a}{1-az}
. Since
\tau^{\prime}(1)=\frac{1+a}{1-a}
, there exists
a\in(-1,1)
such that

\tau_{a}^{\prime}(1)=\varphi_{N}^{\prime}(1)/\psi^{\prime}_{N}(1).

Define
\Psi=\psi\circ\tau_{a}
and
\Psi_{k}=\psi_{k}\circ\tau_{a}
for all
k
.
Then
\Psi^{\prime}_{N}(1)=\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(1)
and thus by Corollary
6.7
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}k_{D}(\varphi(t),\Psi(t))=0.

Fix
|a|\leq t<1
.
For
k
large enough we have

\displaystyle k_{D}(z,w_{n_{k}})

\displaystyle=k_{D}(z,\varphi_{k}(t))+k_{D}(\varphi_{k}(t),w_{n_{k}}),

\displaystyle k_{D}(w_{n_{k}},p)

\displaystyle\leq k_{D}(w_{n_{k}},\varphi_{k}(t))+k_{D}(\varphi_{k}(t),p),

\displaystyle k_{D}(w_{n_{k}},p)

\displaystyle=k_{D}(w_{n_{k}},\Psi_{k}(t))+k_{D}(\Psi_{k}(t),p)

\displaystyle\geq k_{D}(w_{n_{k}},\varphi_{k}(t))+k_{D}(\varphi_{k}(t),p)-2k_{D}(\varphi_{k}(t),\Psi_{k}(t)),

and thus

\displaystyle k_{D}(z,w_{n_{k}})-k_{D}(w_{n_{k}},p)

\displaystyle\geq k_{D}(z,\varphi_{k}(t))-k_{D}(\varphi_{k}(t),p),

\displaystyle k_{D}(z,w_{n_{k}})-k_{D}(w_{n_{k}},p)

\displaystyle\leq k_{D}(z,\varphi_{k}(t))-k_{D}(\varphi_{k}(t),p)+2k_{D}(\varphi_{k}(t),\Psi_{k}(t)).

Taking the limit as
k\to\infty
we obtain

k_{D}(z,\varphi(t))-k_{D}(\varphi(t),p)\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}k_{D}(z,w_{n_{k}})-k_{D}(w_{n_{k}},p)\leq k_{D}(z,\varphi(t))-k_{D}(\varphi(t),p)+2k_{D}(\varphi(t),\Psi(t)),

and by letting
t\to 1^{-}
it follows
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}k_{D}(z,w_{n_{k}})-k_{D}(w_{n_{k}},p)=B_{\varphi}(z,p)
, as desired.

∎

Definition 6.11
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type. The
horosphere
centered at
\xi
of radius
R>0
and with base-point
p\in D
is the level set

E_{p}(\xi,R)=\{z\in D\colon h_{\xi,p}(z)<\log R\}.

Remark 6.12
.

Notice that if
p,q\in D
, then

h_{\xi,q}=h_{\xi,p}+h_{\xi,q}(p),

hence changing the base-point leaves the family of horospheres centered in
\xi
invariant, and amounts to multiplying the radius by a fixed constant.

Proposition 6.13
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.
Then, for all
R>0
,

\overline{E_{p}(\xi,R)}^{*}\cap\partial^{*}D=\{\xi\},

(6.1)

where
\overline{E_{p}(\xi,R)}^{*}
denotes the closure of
E_{p}(\xi,R)
in the one-point compactification of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.
Moreover,

\bigcap_{R>0}\overline{E_{p}(\xi,R)}^{*}=\{\xi\}.

(6.2)

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
12
, Proposition 6.5]
, but we include it here for completeness.
Fix
R>0
and
p\in D
. Let
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to D
be a geodesic ray with
\gamma(0)=p
and endpoint
\xi
. Then for all
t\geq 0

h_{\xi,p}(\gamma(t))=-t,

which means that
\gamma(t)\in E_{p}(\xi,R)
if
t>-\log R
, and so
\xi\in\overline{E_{p}(\xi,R)}^{*}\cap\partial^{*}D
.
Conversely, given a sequence
(z_{n})
in
D
converging to
\eta\in\partial^{*}D\backslash\{\xi\}
, we want to show that
\lim_{n\to+\infty}h_{\xi,p}(z_{n})=+\infty
. Let
(w_{m})
be a sequence in
D
converging to
\xi
. By (i) of Proposition
3.6
there exist
M\geq 0
and
N\geq 0
such that for all
n,m\geq N

(z_{n}|w_{m})_{p}:=\frac{1}{2}[k_{D}(z_{n},p)+k_{D}(w_{m},p)-k_{D}(z_{n},w_{m})]\leq M.

Now for all
n\geq N

h_{\xi,p}(z_{n}):=\lim_{m\to\infty}k_{D}(z_{n},w_{m})-k_{D}(w_{m},p)\geq-2M+k_{D}(z_{n},p)

so
\lim_{n\to+\infty}h_{\xi,p}(z_{n})=+\infty
. This proves (
6.1
), and (
6.2
) easily follows.
∎

7.
K
-convergence and
K^{\prime}
-convergence

In this section we discuss
K
-convergence and
K^{\prime}
-convergence (or restricted convergence) to a boundary point of locally finite type of a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain. We first deal with
K
-convergence.
Given a boundary point
\xi
of a bounded strongly convex domain with
C^{3}
boundary
D
,
Abate (see e.g.
[
1
]
) introduced a generalization of the classical Korányi regions in the ball, namely the
K
-region
with vertex
\xi
, base-point
p\in D
and amplitude
M>1
as the set

K_{p}(\xi,M):=\{z\in D\colon h_{\xi,p}(z)+k_{D}(z,p)<2\log M\}.

Notice that
h_{\xi,p}(z)+k_{D}(p,z)\geq 0
.

K
-regions are comparable to Korányi–Stein admissible regions
[
60
,
48
]
and to Krantz admissible regions
[
50
]
.
Thanks to the existence of horospheres (Theorem
6.10
) this definition carries on verbatim to the case where
D
is a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and
\xi\in\partial D
is a point of locally finite type, and allows us to define
K
-convergence and
K
-limits in such domains.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type. Let
(z_{n})
be a sequence in
D
converging to
\xi
. We say that
(z_{n})

K
-converges
to
\xi
if it is contained in a
K
-region with vertex
\xi
.
Let
f\colon D\to\mathbb{C}
be a function.
We say that
f
has
K
-limit

L\in\mathbb{C}
at
\xi
if for every sequence
(z_{n})

K
-converging to
\xi
we have
f(z_{n})\to L
. In this case we write

K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=L.

Finally we say that
f
is
K
-bounded
at
\xi
if it is bounded in every
K
-region with vertex
\xi
.

Notice that the previous definitions do not depend on the chosen base-point. Indeed by Remark
6.12
for all
p,q\in D

K_{q}(\xi,M)\subseteq K_{p}\left(\xi,Me^{\frac{h_{\xi,p}(q)+k_{D}(q,p)}{2}}\right).

(7.1)

The notion of geodesic region was introduced in
[
9
]
(for bounded strongly convex domains with
C^{3}
boundary) as regions which are comparable to
K
-regions and thus define the same notions of convergence, limit and boundedness, but are often much easier to work with. See also
[
12
,
13
]
, where such notion is used to define a generalization of the
K
-limit to the context of proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic metric spaces.

Definition 7.2

(Geodesic region)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.
Let
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to D
be a geodesic ray with endpoint
\xi
, and let
R>0
.
We define the
geodesic region

A(\gamma,R)
as the open connected subset defined by

A(\gamma,R)=\left\{z\in D:\inf_{t\geq 0}k_{D}(z,\gamma(t))<R\right\}.

We say that the point
\xi
is the
vertex
of
A(\gamma,R)
.
If
\varphi
is a complex geodesic with endpoint
\xi
, by a slight abuse of notation we denote
by
A(\varphi,R)
the geodesic region
A(\tilde{\varphi},R),
where

\tilde{\varphi}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to D
is the geodesic ray
\tilde{\varphi}(t)=\varphi({\rm tanh}(t/2)).

Lemma 7.3

(Properties of geodesic regions)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type. Let
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to D
be a geodesic ray with endpoint
\xi
.

(i)

If
\theta\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to D
is another geodesic ray with endpoint
\xi
, then there exists
S>0
such that, for all
R>0
,

A(\gamma,R)\subseteq A(\theta,R+S),\quad A(\theta,R)\subseteq A(\psi,R+S).

(ii)

For all
R>0
we have that
\overline{A(\gamma,R)}^{*}\cap\partial^{*}D=\{\xi\}
,
where
\overline{A(\gamma,R)}^{*}
denotes the closure of
A(\gamma,R)
in the one-point compactification of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

(iii)

Let
R>0
, then for all
z\in A(\gamma,R)

h_{\xi,\gamma(0)}(z)<2R-k_{D}(z,\gamma(0));

in particular a sequence converging to
\xi
inside a geodesic region is eventually contained in every horosphere centered at
\xi
.

Proof.

By (iii) of Proposition
3.6
there exists
S>0
such that

k_{D}(\gamma(t),\theta(t))\leq S
for each
t\geq 0
, which implies (i). Point (ii) immediately follows from Corollary
3.7
.
We now prove (iii). Let
z\in A(\gamma,R)
and consider
t^{*}\geq 0
such that
k_{D}(z,\gamma(t^{*}))<R
, then

\displaystyle h_{\xi,\gamma(0)}(z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}k_{D}(z,\gamma(t))-k_{D}(\gamma(t),\gamma(0))

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{t\to 1^{-}}k_{D}(z,\gamma(t^{*}))+k_{D}(\gamma(t^{*}),\gamma(t))-k_{D}(\gamma(t),\gamma(0))

\displaystyle=k_{D}(z,\gamma(t^{*}))-k_{D}(\gamma(t^{*}),\gamma(0))

\displaystyle\leq 2k_{D}(z,\gamma(t^{*}))-k_{D}(z,\gamma(0))

\displaystyle<2R-k_{D}(z,\gamma(0)).

∎

We now show that geodesic regions and
K
-regions are comparable. The proof of
[
9
, Lemma 7.8]

does not work in this context since it is based on the Gromov hyperbolicity of the domain
D
.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type. Then every geodesic region with vertex
\xi
is contained in a
K
-region with vertex
\xi
, and vice versa.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to D
be a geodesic ray with endpoint
\xi
and set
p=\gamma(0)
. Let
z\in A(\gamma,R)
, then by (iii) in Lemma
7.3
we have

h_{\xi,p}(z)+k_{D}(z,p)<2R,

which implies
A(\gamma,R)\subseteq K_{p}(\xi,e^{R})
.

We now prove the converse.

Let
\delta^{\prime}>0
and
V
be given by (ii) of Proposition
3.6
.
Let
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to D
be a geodesic ray with endpoint
\xi
that is contained in
V
and set
q:=\gamma(0)
.
We will show that every
K
-region
K_{q}(\xi,M)
is contained in a geodesic region with vertex
\xi
. By
(
7.1
) this will prove the result.
Notice that
K_{q}(\xi,M)\cap V^{\complement}
is relatively compact in
D
, so we can find
R>0
such that
K_{q}(\xi,M)\cap V^{\complement}\subseteq A(\gamma,R)
. Now assume that
z\in K_{q}(\xi,M)\cap V
, and consider
z_{\gamma}:=\gamma(k_{D}(z,q))
. Let
t>k_{D}(z,q)
, then by (ii) in Proposition
3.6

(z|z_{\gamma})_{q}\geq\min\{(z|\gamma(t))_{q},(z_{\gamma}|\gamma(t))_{q}\}-\delta^{\prime}.

(7.2)

Now

(z|\gamma(t))_{q}=\frac{1}{2}[k_{D}(z,q)+k_{D}(\gamma(t),q)-k_{D}(z,\gamma(t))]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\to+\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\frac{1}{2}[k_{D}(z,q)-h_{\xi,q}(z)]

and

(z_{\gamma}|\gamma(t))_{q}=\frac{1}{2}[k_{D}(z_{\gamma},q)+k_{D}(\gamma(t),q)-k_{D}(z_{\gamma},\gamma(t))]=k_{D}(z_{\gamma},q)=k_{D}(z,q).

Expanding the Gromov product in left-hand side of (
7.2
) we have

2k_{D}(z,q)-k_{D}(z,z_{\gamma})\geq\min\{k_{D}(z,q)-h_{\xi,q}(z),2k_{D}(z,q)\}-2\delta^{\prime},

which implies

k_{D}(z,\gamma)\leq k_{D}(z,z_{\gamma})\leq\max\{h_{\xi,q}(z)+k_{D}(z,q),0\}+2\delta^{\prime}\leq 2\log M+2\delta^{\prime},

so
K_{q}(\xi,M)\subseteq A(\gamma,R^{\prime})
, where
R^{\prime}:=\max\{R,2\log M+2\delta^{\prime}\}.

∎

There are many instances in which
K
-convergence is the natural generalization to several complex variables of non-tangential convergence in the disc (see e.g. the Julia Lemma in Section
10
). There is however a notable exception, namely the Lindelöf principle. Indeed, one can find a bounded holomorphic function defined on the ball
\mathbb{B}^{d}
which admits radial limit at
e_{0}
but does not admit
K
-limit at
e_{0}
, see e.g. (
1.2
). This is the main reason to consider a second, more restrictive definition of convergence to a boundary point
\xi
, for which the Lindelöf principle holds:
K^{\prime}
-convergence (also called restricted convergence). For the classical definition in the unit ball we refer to
[
59
]
.
K^{\prime}
-convergence has been generalized by Abate (see e.g.
[
1
]
) to the case of strongly convex domains, and by Abate–Tauraso
[
8
, Section 3]
to the case of convex domains of finite type.
In our context we introduce the following definition.

Definition 7.5
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type. Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
be a complex geodesic with endpoint
\xi
, and let
(z_{n})
be a sequence in
D
converging to
\xi
.
We say that
(z_{n})

K^{\prime}
-converges
to
\xi
(or is
restricted
) if it
K
-converges to
\xi
and if

\lim_{n\to+\infty}k_{D}(z_{n},\varphi(\mathbb{D}))=0.

(7.3)

If
f\colon D\to\mathbb{C}
is a function, we say that
f
has
K^{\prime}
-limit
(or
restricted
K
-limit
)
L\in\mathbb{C}
at
\xi
if
f(z_{n})\to L
for any restricted sequence
(z_{n})
converging to
\xi
. In this case we write

K^{\prime}\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=L.

Remark 7.6
.

Let
(z_{n})
be a restricted sequence converging to
\xi
and let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
be a complex geodesic with endpoint
\xi
.
Let
(\zeta_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{D}
such that
k_{D}(z_{n},\varphi(\zeta_{n}))\to 0
. Then
\zeta_{n}\to 1
(non-tangentially), since for all
R>0
,

\varphi(A^{\mathbb{D}}({\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}},R))=A^{D}(\varphi,R)\cap\varphi(\mathbb{D}).

(7.4)

Remark 7.7
.

This notion of restricted convergence is a priori more general than the one given by Abate–Tauraso
[
8
, Section 3]
for a convex domain of finite type
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Given a complex geodesic
\varphi
with endpoint
\xi\in\partial D
they choose a left inverse
\tilde{\rho}\colon D\to\mathbb{D}
of
\varphi
. Their definition is (equivalent to) the following:
a sequence
(z_{n})
converging to
\xi
is restricted if it
K
-converges to
\xi
and if

k_{D}(\gamma(t),\varphi(\tilde{\rho}(z_{n})))\to 0.

Clearly a sequence with this property satisfies (
7.3
), but the converse is unclear. It is also unclear whether Abate–Tauraso’s definition depends on the choice of the complex geodesic
\varphi
. The next result show that Definition
7.5
does not depend on the choice of the complex geodesic
\varphi
.

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.
Let
\varphi,\psi
be two complex geodesics with the same endpoint
\xi
.
Then a sequence
(z_{n})
in
D
converging to
\xi
is restricted w.r.t.
\varphi
if and only if it is restricted w.r.t.
\psi
.

Proof.

Let
\zeta_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
such that
k_{D}(z_{n},\varphi(\zeta_{n}))\to 0
. By Remark
7.6

\zeta_{n}\to 1
non-tangentially. Up to a change of parametrization of
\psi
we can suppose
\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(1)=\psi^{\prime}_{N}(1)
, so by Proposition
6.7

k_{D}(z_{n},\psi(\zeta_{n}))\leq k_{D}(z_{n},\varphi(\zeta_{n}))+k_{D}(\varphi(\zeta_{n}),\psi(\zeta_{n}))\to 0.

∎

We now prove the following extrinsic characterization for
K
-convergence and for
K^{\prime}
-convergence. Notice the analogy with the classic definitions in the ball (see (2.2.33) and (2.2.34) in
[
1
]
).

Theorem 7.9
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type. Let
(v_{j})_{j=0}^{d-1}
be a multitype basis at
\xi
, and
let
(z_{n})
be a sequence in
D
. Then

(i)

(z_{n})

K
-converges to
\xi
if and only if

\langle z_{n}-\xi,v_{j}\rangle=O\left(\delta_{D}(z_{n})^{1/m_{j}}\right),\quad\forall\,0\leq j\leq d-1;

(7.5)

(ii)

(z_{n})

K^{\prime}
-converges to
\xi
if and only if
(
7.5
) holds for
j=0
and

\langle z_{n}-\xi,v_{j}\rangle=o\left(\delta_{D}(z_{n})^{1/m_{j}}\right),\quad\forall\,1\leq j\leq d-1.

(7.6)

Proof.

For the sake of clarity, in this proof we denote the sequence in
D
converging to the origin by
(z^{(n)})
instead of
(z_{n})
.
Consider multitype coordinates at
\xi
.
Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{H}\to D
be a complex geodesic with endpoint the origin. Up to a change of parametrization, we may assume
\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(0)=1
. Set
\lambda_{n}:=\delta_{D}(z^{(n)})^{-1}
and consider the scaling

A_{n}(z)=(\lambda_{n}z_{0},\lambda_{n}^{1/m_{1}}z_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}^{1/m_{d-1}}z_{d-1}).

Notice that in multitype coordinates the sequence
(A_{n}z^{(n)})
is bounded in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
if and only if (
7.5
) holds for all
j=0,\dots,d-1
.

We prove (i).
Assume that the sequence
(A_{n}z^{(n)})
is bounded in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Let
r
be the local defining function of
D
near the origin as in (
5.1
). We have that

\lambda_{n}r(A_{n}^{-1}z)\to r_{D_{H}}(z):={\operatorname{Re}\,}z_{0}+H(z_{1},\cdots,z_{d-1})

uniformly on compact sets of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. It is well known (see, for example,
[
57
, Chapter 2, Lemma 2.5]
) that
for
z\in D
close to the origin the defining function
r
is bi-Lipschitz to the distance function
\delta_{D}
, that is there exist
0<c<C
such that

-C\delta_{D}(z)\leq r(z)\leq-c\delta_{D}(z).

It follows that

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}r_{D_{H}}(A_{n}z^{(n)})=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\lambda_{n}r(A_{n}^{-1}A_{n}z^{(n)})=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{r(z^{(n)})}{\delta_{D}(z^{(n)})}\leq-c<0,

which implies that the limit set of the sequence
(A_{n}z^{(n)})
is contained in a compact subset
K
of
D_{H}
. By Proposition
6.6
the sequence
(A_{n}\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{-1}\cdot))
converges uniformly on compact subsets to
\hat{\varphi}(\zeta)=(\zeta,0)
, so by Corollary
6.3
we have

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}k_{D}(z^{(n)},\varphi(-\lambda_{n}^{-1}))=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}k_{A_{n}D}(A_{n}z^{(n)},A_{n}\varphi(-\lambda_{n}^{-1}))\leq\max_{z\in K}k_{D_{H}}(z,(-1,0))<+\infty,

which means that the sequence
(z^{(n)})
is contained in a geodesic region.

Conversely, assume that the sequence
(z^{(n)})

K
-converges to
\xi
. Let
(t_{n})
be a sequence in
(0,1]
converging to 0 such that

k_{D}(z^{(n)},\varphi(-t_{n}))\leq M
for all
n\geq 0
. We claim that there are two constants
0<c_{1}<C_{1}
such that

c_{1}\leq\lambda_{n}t_{n}\leq C_{1}.

(7.7)

Indeed, by Lemma
3.8
we can find
c_{2}\geq 0
such that

c_{2}\geq|k_{D}(\varphi(-t_{n}),\varphi(-1))+\log\delta_{D}(\varphi(-t_{n}))|=|k_{\mathbb{H}}(-t_{n},-1)+\log\delta_{D}(\varphi(-t_{n}))|=\left|\log\frac{\delta_{D}(\varphi(-t_{n}))}{t_{n}}\right|.

By Proposition 2.4 in
[
53
]
we have

k_{D}(p,q)\geq\left|\log\frac{\delta_{D}(p)}{\delta_{D}(q)}\right|
for all
p,q\in D
, so

\left|\log\frac{t_{n}}{\delta_{D}(z^{(n)})}\right|\leq\left|\log\frac{t_{n}}{\delta_{D}(\varphi(-t_{n}))}\right|+\left|\log\frac{\delta_{D}(\varphi(-t_{n}))}{\delta_{D}(z^{(n)})}\right|\leq c_{2}+k_{D}(\varphi(-t_{n}),z^{(n)})\leq c_{2}+M,

which proves the claim.
Thus the sequence
(-\lambda_{n}t_{n})
is contained in
[-C_{1},-c_{1}]\subset\subset\mathbb{H}
. Hence the limit set of the sequence

A_{n}\varphi(-t_{n})=A_{n}\varphi(\lambda_{n}^{-1}(-\lambda_{n}t_{n}))

is contained in
[-C_{1},-c_{1}]\times\{0\}\subset\subset D_{H}
. Recalling that
k_{D}(z^{(n)},\varphi(-t_{n}))\leq M
, by (iii) in Corollary
6.3

it follows that the limit set of the sequence
(A_{n}z^{(n)})
is relatively compact in
D_{H}
. Hence
(A_{n}z^{(n)})
is bounded in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

We now prove (ii).
Assume that the sequence
(z^{(n)})

K^{\prime}
-converges to
\xi
. By definition there exists
(\zeta_{n})
in
\mathbb{H}
such that
k_{D}(z^{(n)},\varphi(\zeta_{n}))\to 0
.
By Remark
7.6

\zeta_{n}\to 0
non-tangentially, so there exists
A>0
such that
|{\operatorname{Im}\,}\zeta_{n}|\leq A|{\operatorname{Re}\,}\zeta_{n}|
. It follows that

k_{D}(z^{(n)},\varphi({\operatorname{Re}\,}\zeta_{n}))\leq k_{D}(z^{(n)},\varphi(\zeta_{n}))+k_{\mathbb{H}}(\zeta_{n},{\operatorname{Re}\,}\zeta_{n})\leq k_{D}(z^{(n)},\varphi(\zeta_{n}))+2\arcsinh\frac{A}{2},

which is bounded. Arguing as above (setting
t_{n}:=-{\operatorname{Re}\,}\zeta_{n}
) we get that there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

c_{1}\leq-\lambda_{n}{\operatorname{Re}\,}\zeta_{n}\leq C_{1},

which implies that the sequence
(\lambda_{n}\zeta_{n})
is contained in a compact subset of
\mathbb{H}
.
Let
(n_{k})
be a subsequence such that
(A_{n_{k}}z^{(n_{k})})
converges to a point
\hat{z}\in D_{H}
and
(\lambda_{n_{k}}\zeta_{n_{k}})
converges to a point
\hat{\zeta}\in\mathbb{H}.

We have

0=\lim_{k\to+\infty}k_{D}(z^{(n_{k})},\varphi(\zeta_{n_{k}}))=\lim_{k\to+\infty}k_{A_{n_{k}}D}(A_{n_{k}}z^{(n_{k})},A_{n_{k}}\varphi(\zeta_{n_{k}}))=k_{D_{H}}(\hat{z},(\hat{\zeta},0)),

thus
A_{n_{k}}z^{(n_{k})}\to(\hat{\zeta},0)
, which means that

\lambda_{n_{k}}^{1/m_{j}}z^{(n_{k})}_{j}\to 0
for all
j=1,\cdots,d-1
,
which implies (
7.6
).

Conversely, assume (
7.6
), and we show that
k_{D}(z^{(n)},\varphi(z^{(n)}_{0}))\to 0
.
By (
7.5
) the sequence
(\lambda_{n}z_{0}^{(n)})
is bounded in
\mathbb{C}
. Moreover, from the convexity of
D
, we have
\lambda_{n}:=\delta_{D}(z^{(n)})\leq-{\operatorname{Re}\,}z_{0}^{(n)}
, so
(\lambda_{n}z^{(n)}_{0})
is contained in a compact subset of
\mathbb{H}
. Let
(n_{k})
be a subsequence such that
(\lambda_{n_{k}}z^{(n_{k})}_{0})
converges to a point
\hat{\zeta}\in\mathbb{H}.

By (
7.6
) we have
A_{n_{k}}z^{(n_{k})}\to(\hat{\zeta},0)
, and
by Proposition
6.6
we have

A_{n_{k}}\varphi(\lambda_{n_{k}}^{-1}\lambda_{n_{k}}z_{0}^{(n_{k})})\to(\hat{\zeta},0).

Thus

k_{D}(z^{(n_{k})},\varphi(z^{(n_{k})}_{0}))=k_{A_{n_{k}}D}(A_{n_{k}}z^{(n_{k})},A_{n_{k}}\varphi(z^{(n_{k})}_{0}))\to 0.

∎

Remark 7.10
.

Notice that the (i) and (ii) in previous theorem immediately imply the following:

(i)

\langle z-\xi,v_{j}\rangle=O_{K}\left(\delta_{D}(z)^{1/m_{j}}\right)
for all
0\leq j\leq d-1
;

(ii)

\langle z-\xi,v_{j}\rangle=o_{K^{\prime}}\left(\delta_{D}(z)^{1/m_{j}}\right)
for all
1\leq j\leq d-1
.

Remark 7.11
.

It is natural to wonder whether, if a sequence
(z^{(n)})

K
-converges to
\xi
, one has for all
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,

\langle z^{(n)}-\xi,v\rangle=O\left(\delta_{D}(z^{(n)})^{1/m_{\xi}(v)}\right).

This turns out to be false in general, as the following example shows:
let
m\geq 2
be an even integer and let
\mathbb{E}_{m}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
be the egg domain defined in Example
4.8
. Let
\xi:=(1,0)\in\partial\mathbb{E}_{m}
. Fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}^{*}
and consider the curve
\gamma\colon[0,1)\to\mathbb{E}_{m}

with endpoint
\xi
defined as
\gamma(t)=(t,\lambda\sqrt[m]{1-t^{2}}),
which satisfies

\delta_{\mathbb{E}_{m}}(\gamma(t))\approx 1-t.

Let
t_{n}\nearrow 1
and set
z^{(n)}:=\gamma(t_{n}).
By the previous theorem
z^{(n)}
converges to
\xi
inside a
K
-region.
Notice that the outer normal versor
n_{\xi}
at
\xi
is
(1,0)
and that the complex tangent at
\xi
is
T_{\xi}^{\mathbb{C}}\partial\mathbb{E}_{m}=\{z_{0}=0\}
.
Now let
v\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{z_{0}z_{1}=0\}
. The type of
v
is
m_{\xi}(v)=1
while its cotype is
M_{\xi}(v)=m
.
A simple computation shows that

\langle z^{(n)}-\xi,v\rangle
is
O((1-t_{n})^{1/m})
but is not
O(1-t_{n})
.
In general it follows by simple linear algebra that for all
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
one has

\langle z^{(n)}-\xi,v\rangle=O\left(\delta_{D}(z^{(n)})^{1/M_{\xi}(v)}\right),

where
M_{\xi}(v)
is the cotype of
v
.
Similar considerations hold for the restricted convergence.

Remark 7.12
.

In the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{d}
the conditions (
7.5
) for
j=1,\dots,d-1
are redundant, that is,
a sequence
(z_{n})
in
\mathbb{B}^{d}

K
-converges to
\xi\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{d}
if and only if

\langle z_{n}-\xi,n_{\xi}\rangle=O\left(\delta_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}(z_{n})\right).
Indeed one has, for all
M>1
,

K_{0}(\xi,M)=\left\{z\in\mathbb{B}^{d}:\frac{|1-\langle z,\xi\rangle|}{1-\|z\|}<M\right\}.

(7.8)

The next result shows that the same is true in
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains at strongly linearly convex boundary points. We recall that a
C^{2}
boundary point of a domain is called
strongly linearly convex
if the Hessian of some (equivalently, any) defining function is definite positive on the complex tangent space.
Notice that every strongly linearly convex point of a convex domain has line type 2.

Corollary 7.13
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a strongly linearly convex boundary point. Let
(z_{n})
be a sequence in
D
, then

(z_{n})

K
-converges to
\xi
if and only if

\langle z_{n}-\xi,n_{\xi}\rangle=O\left(\delta_{D}(z_{n})\right).

Proof.

By (i) in Theorem
7.9
is enough to prove that, if

\langle z_{n}-\xi,n_{\xi}\rangle=O\left(\delta_{D}(z_{n})\right),

then

\langle z_{n}-\xi,\tau\rangle=O\left(\delta_{D}(z_{n})^{1/2}\right)

for all
\tau\in T_{\xi}^{\mathbb{C}}\partial D
.
Since
\xi
is a strongly linearly convex boundary point, by
[
49
, Proposition 22]
there exists
M\geq 0
such that

\|z_{n}-\xi\|\leq M|\langle z_{n}-\xi,n_{\xi}\rangle|^{1/2},

hence for all
\tau\in T_{\xi}^{\mathbb{C}}\partial D
we have

|\langle z_{n}-\xi,\tau\rangle|\leq\|z_{n}-\xi\|\leq M|\langle z_{n}-\xi,n_{\xi}\rangle|^{1/2}=O\left(\delta_{D}(z_{n})^{1/2}\right).

∎

Remark 7.14
.

The conditions (
7.5
) for
j=1,\dots,d-1
are however not redundant in general.
Consider as an example the
tube
domain

D:=\{(z_{0},z_{1})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:{\operatorname{Re}\,}z_{0}+2({\operatorname{Re}\,}z_{1})^{2}<0\},

a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain which is biholomorphic to the Siegel Half-space

\mathbb{H}_{2}:=\{(z_{0},z_{1})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:{\rm Re}\,z_{0}+|z_{1}|^{2}<0\}

via the biholomorphism
\Psi\colon\mathbb{H}_{2}\to D
given by

\Psi(z_{0},z_{1})=\left(z_{0}-z_{1}^{2},z_{1}\right),\quad\Psi^{-1}(z_{0},z_{1})=\left(z_{0}+z_{1}^{2},z_{1}\right).

In particular
D
is biholomorphic to the ball
\mathbb{B}^{2}
.
Every point of
\partial D
is strongly pseudoconvex (and thus has line type 2)
and no point of
\partial D
is strongly linearly convex.
Let
\alpha>0
and
t_{n}\searrow 0
, and consider the sequence
z^{(n)}:=(-t_{n},it_{n}^{\alpha})
converging to the origin. The outer normal versor at the origin is
n_{0}=(1,0)
. Notice that
\tau=(0,i)\in T_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}\partial D
and that
\delta_{D}(z_{n})=t_{n}
and

\langle z^{(n)},n_{0}\rangle=-t_{n},\quad\langle z^{(n)},\tau\rangle=t_{n}^{\alpha}.

Hence we have
\langle z^{(n)},n_{0}\rangle=O(\delta_{D}(z^{(n)}))
for all
\alpha>0
, but
\langle z^{(n)},\tau\rangle=O(\delta_{D}(z^{(n)})^{1/2})
if and only if
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

We end this section with another corollary of Theorem
7.9
.

Corollary 7.15
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain, let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type, and let
f\colon D\to\mathbb{C}
be a function. Then

(i)

if
f
has
K
-limit
L
at
\xi
, then
f
has
K^{\prime}
-limit
L
at
\xi
;

(ii)

if
f
has
K^{\prime}
-limit
L
at
\xi
, then
f
has non-tangential limit
L
at
\xi
(in the sense of cones).

Proof.

(i) follows from the definition of restricted sequence. We prove (ii).
Let
(z_{n})
be a sequence in
D
converging to
\xi
inside a cone of aperture
<\pi
.
Let
B\subseteq D
be an Euclidean ball internally tangent at
\xi
, then a simple geometric argument shows that there exists
A>1
such that
\|z_{n}-\xi\|\leq A\delta_{B}(z_{n})
and obviously
\delta_{B}(z_{n})\leq\delta_{D}(z_{n})
,
thus by Theorem
7.9
the sequence
(z_{n})
is restricted in
D
.
∎

8.
Kobayashi type

The following definition was introduced by Abate in
[
5
]
, see also Abate–Tauraso
[
8
]
.

Definition 8.1

(Kobayashi type)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and
\xi\in\partial D
. Let
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
v\neq 0
, then the
Kobayashi type

s_{\xi}(v)
of
v
at
\xi
is the number

s_{\xi}(v):=\inf\{s>0:\kappa_{D}(z,v)=O_{K}(\delta_{D}(z)^{-s})\}.

Abate conjectures the following in
[
5
]
: “the Kobayashi type might be the inverse of the D’Angelo type of
\partial D
at
\xi
along the direction
v
(that is, the highest order of contact of
\partial D
with a complex curve tangent to
v
at
\xi
)”. He also leaves as an open question whether the infimum in the definition of
s_{\xi}(v)
is always attained.
In this section we prove the conjecture (the equality between D’Angelo type at
\xi
along the direction
v
and
m_{\xi}(v)
can be proved as in
[
17
]
), and we answer the question in the positive.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be point of locally finite type. Let
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.
Then the function

\delta_{D}(z)^{1/m_{\xi}(v)}\kappa_{D}(z,v)

and its reciprocal are
K
-bounded at
\xi
.
In particular, the infimum in the definition of
s_{\xi}(v)
is attained and

s_{\xi}(v)=\frac{1}{m_{\xi}(v)}.

Proof.

Let
(m_{j})_{j=0}^{d-1}
be the multitype at
\xi
and consider multitype coordinates at
\xi
.
Write
v=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}a_{j}e_{j}
.
Denote

j_{0}:=\min\{j:a_{j}\neq 0\},

and notice that

m_{\xi}(v)=m_{j_{0}}
.
We first show that
\delta_{D}(z)^{1/m_{\xi}(v)}\kappa_{D}(z,v)
is
K
-bounded.
Assume by contradiction that there exists a sequence
z^{(n)}\to 0
inside a geodesic region such that

\delta_{D}^{1/m_{\xi}(v)}(z^{(n)})\kappa_{D}(z^{(n)},v)\to+\infty.

Set
\lambda_{n}:=\delta_{D}(z^{(n)})^{-1}
and consider the scaling maps

A_{n}(z)=(\lambda_{n}z_{0},\lambda_{n}^{1/m_{1}}z_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}^{1/m_{d-1}}z_{d-1}).

Arguing as in (i) in Theorem
7.9
, we obtain that up to a subsequence

A_{n}z^{(n)}\to z^{(\infty)}\in D_{H}.

Now

+\infty=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\lambda_{n}^{-1/m_{\xi}(v)}\kappa_{D}(z^{(n)},v)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\kappa_{A_{n}D}(A_{n}z^{(n)},\lambda_{n}^{-1/m_{\xi}(v)}A_{n}v).

Notice that

\lambda_{n}^{-1/m_{\xi}(v)}A_{n}v=\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{d-1}\lambda_{n}^{1/m_{j}-1/m_{\xi}(v)}a_{j}e_{j}.

Since
m_{j}\geq m_{\xi}(v)
for
j=j_{0},\cdots,d-1
, it follows that

\lambda_{n}^{-1/m_{\xi}(v)}A_{n}v\to v_{\infty}:=\sum_{j\colon m_{j}=m_{\xi}(v)}a_{j}e_{j}.

Finally

+\infty=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\kappa_{A_{n}D}(A_{n}z^{(n)},\lambda_{n}^{-1/m_{\xi}(v)}A_{n}v)=\kappa_{D_{H}}(z^{(\infty)},v_{\infty})<\infty,

obtaining a contradiction.
Now we show that the reciprocal is
K
-bounded. Assume by contradiction that there exists a sequence
z^{(n)}\to 0
inside a geodesic region such that

\delta_{D}^{1/m_{\xi}(v)}(z^{(n)})\kappa_{D}(z^{(n)},v)\to 0.

Reasoning as before and noting that
v_{\infty}\neq 0
, we obtain

0=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\lambda_{n}^{-1/m_{\xi}(v)}\kappa_{D}(z^{(n)},v)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\kappa_{A_{n}D}(A_{n}z^{(n)},\lambda_{n}^{-1/m_{\xi}(v)}A_{n}v)=\kappa_{D_{H}}(z^{(\infty)},v_{\infty})>0,

obtaining a contradiction.
∎

Remark 8.3
.

The previous result can be rephrased as the following estimate for the Kobayashi metric: if
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and
\xi\in\partial D
is a point of locally finite type, then for all
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,
p\in D
,
M>1
there exist
c,C>0
such that for all
z\in K_{p}(\xi,M)
we have

\frac{c}{\delta_{D}(z)^{1/m_{\xi}(v)}}\leq\kappa_{D}(z,v)\leq\frac{C}{\delta_{D}(z)^{1/m_{\xi}(v)}}.

This estimate was obtained along the normal segment by Lee in
[
51
]
(see also
[
55
]
for the
\mathbb{C}
-convex case).

9.
Lindelöf principle

The purpose of this section is to prove the following version of the Lindelöf principle.

Definition 9.1
.

A
restricted curve

\gamma:[0,1)\to D
with endpoint
\xi
is a curve such that for all sequences
(t_{n})
in
[0,1)
converging to 1 we have that the sequence
(\gamma(t_{n}))
is restricted in the sense of Definition
7.5
.
Clearly this is equivalent to saying that
\gamma([0,1))
is contained in a
K
-region with vertex
\xi
and that
k_{D}(\gamma(t),\varphi(\mathbb{D}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\to 1^{-}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,
where
\varphi
is a complex geodesic with endpoint
\xi
.
We say that curve
\gamma:[0,1)\to D
with endpoint
\xi
is
strongly restricted
if
\gamma([0,1))
is contained in a
K
-region with vertex
\xi
and if there exists a (continuous) curve
\tilde{\gamma}\colon[0,1)\to\mathbb{D}
such that

\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}k_{D}(\gamma(t),\varphi(\tilde{\gamma}(t)))=0,

(9.1)

where
\varphi
is a complex geodesic with endpoint
\xi
.

Remark 9.2
.

If
\gamma
is strongly restricted, the curve
\varphi\circ\tilde{\gamma}
is contained in a geodesic region with vertex
\xi
, and thus by (
7.4
) it follows that the curve
\tilde{\gamma}(t)
has endpoint 1 and is non-tangential. Notice also that the definition of strongly restricted curve does not depend on the choice of the complex geodesic
\varphi
.

The definition of strongly restricted curve is only slightly more stringent than the definition of restricted curve, as the following lemma shows.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.
Then any rectifiable restricted curve with endpoint
\xi
is strongly restricted.

Proof.

Define a sequence
(t_{n})
as follows. Set
t_{0}=0
. Then define inductively
t_{n+1}
as the minimum
t\geq t_{n}
such that
k_{D}(\gamma(t),\gamma(t_{n}))\geq 1/n.

It easily follows from the fact that
\gamma
is rectifiable that
t_{n}\to 1
.
Set
z_{n}:=\gamma(t_{n})
. Since the sequence
(z_{n})
is restricted, there exists a sequence
(\zeta_{n})
in
\mathbb{D}
such that
k_{D}(z_{n},\varphi(\zeta_{n}))\to 0
. Define the curve
\tilde{\gamma}\colon[0,1)\to\mathbb{D}
as follows: for all
n\geq 0
set

\tilde{\gamma}(t_{n}):=\zeta_{n},
and then interpolate for all
t\in[0,1)
using (suitably reparametrized) geodesic segments.
It is easy to see that
\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}k_{D}(\gamma(t),\varphi(\tilde{\gamma}(t)))=0.

∎

Theorem 9.4

(Lindelöf Principle)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type. Let
f\colon D\to\mathbb{C}
be a holomorphic function which is
K
-bounded at
\xi
, and assume that there exists a strongly restricted curve
\gamma\colon[0,1)\to D
such that
f(\gamma(t))\to L
as
t\to 1
. Then
f
has restricted
K
-limit
L
at
\xi
.

Remark 9.5
.

This statement is only apparently similar to
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
, given our more general notion of restricted convergence, see Remark
7.7
(moreover, we assume
K
-boundedness instead of
T
-boundedness).
The proof is also different, as it is only based on the one variable Lindelöf Principle, the Royden localization lemma and metric arguments.

Lemma 9.6

(Royden Localization Lemma)
.

[
58
]

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a Kobayashi hyperbolic domain and let
U\subset D
be a domain. Then

\kappa_{U}(z,v)\leq\coth(k_{D}(z,D\backslash U)/2)\kappa_{D}(z,v)

for any
z\in U
and
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, where
k_{D}(z,D\backslash U):=\inf_{w\in D\backslash U}k_{D}(z,w)
.

Lemma 9.7
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.
Let
(z_{n})
be a restricted sequence converging to
\xi
, and let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
be a complex geodesic with endpoint
\xi
. Let
R>0
be such that
z_{n}\in A(\varphi,R)
for all
n\geq 0
.
Let
(\zeta_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{D}
such that

k_{D}(z_{n},\varphi(\zeta_{n}))\to 0.
Then for all
R^{\prime}>R
the sequence
(\varphi(\zeta_{n}))
is eventually contained in
A(\varphi,R^{\prime}
) and we have

\lim_{n\to+\infty}k_{A(\varphi,R^{\prime})}(z_{n},\varphi(\zeta_{n}))=0.

Proof.

For all
n\geq 0
, let
\gamma_{n}\colon[0,T_{n}]\to D
be a geodesic segment between
z_{n}
and
\varphi(\zeta_{n})
with respect to
k_{D}
.
Fix
R<S<R^{\prime}
. Clearly
\gamma_{n}([0,T_{n}])
is eventually contained in
A(\gamma,S)
.
By the Royden localization lemma, we have

\kappa_{A(\varphi,R^{\prime})}(z,v)\leq B\kappa_{D}(z,v),\ \ \ z\in A(\varphi,S),v\in\mathbb{C}^{d},

where
B:=\coth(R^{\prime}-S)>1
, which implies

\displaystyle k_{A(\varphi,R^{\prime})}(z_{n},\varphi(\zeta_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\kappa_{A(\varphi,R^{\prime})}(\gamma_{n}(t),\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(t))dt

\displaystyle\leq B\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\kappa_{D}(\gamma_{n}(t),\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(t))dt

\displaystyle=Bk_{D}(z_{n},\varphi(\zeta_{n}))\to 0.

∎

Remark 9.8
.

It follows from the previous lemma that, if
\gamma\colon[0,1)\to D
is a strongly restricted curve such that
\gamma(t)\in A(\varphi,R)
for all

and
\tilde{\gamma}\colon[0,1)\to\mathbb{D}
is a curve satisfying (

), then for all

we have that

\varphi(\tilde{\gamma}(t))
is eventually contained in

and

\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}k_{A(\varphi,R^{\prime})}(\gamma(t),\varphi(\tilde{\gamma}(t)))=0.

Proof of Theorem
9.4
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
be a complex geodesic with endpoint
\xi
and
let
\tilde{\gamma}\colon[0,1)\to\mathbb{D}
be a curve satisfying (
9.1
).
Let
R>0
such that
\gamma(t)\in A(\varphi,R)
for all

, and let

.
Since

is

there exists

such that

, and thus by Lemma

.

Let now

∎

10.
Julia lemma

The main result in this section is the generalization to our setting of the intrinsic Julia lemma proved by Abate for bounded strongly convex domains with
C^{3}
boundary
[
1
, Theorem 2.4.16, Proposition 2.7.15]
. For bounded convex domains of finite type this is already done in
[
12
, Theorem 6.28]
. In our local finite type assumptions the statement and the proof have to be adapted since the classical argument which gives the existence of the
K
-limit at
\xi
does not work. Lemma
10.4
below is the main tool to deal with this issue.

Definition 10.1

(Dilation)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains. Let
\xi\in\partial D
be a
point of locally finite type.
Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a holomorphic map, and fix base-points
p\in D,p^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
. The
dilation

\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}\in(0,+\infty]
of
f
at
\xi
is the number defined by

\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}=\liminf_{z\to\xi}k_{D}(z,p)-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(z),p^{\prime}).

If
\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}<+\infty,
we call a sequence
(z_{n})
in
D
converging to
\xi

dilation minimizing
if

\lim_{n\to+\infty}k_{D}(z_{n},p)-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(z_{n}),p^{\prime})=\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}},

while we say that
(z_{n})
has
bounded dilation
if the sequence
(k_{D}(z_{n},p)-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(z_{n}),p^{\prime}))
is bounded from above. Notice that the notion of bounded dilation is base-point independent, that is, if a sequence has bounded dilation w.r.t base-points
p,p^{\prime}
, then by the triangle inequality it has bounded dilation also w.r.t. any
q,q^{\prime}
.
In case
D^{\prime}=\mathbb{D}
we simply denote
\lambda_{\xi,p}:=\lambda_{\xi,p,0}
.

Remark 10.2
.

Notice that
\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}
cannot be
-\infty
. Indeed,

k_{D}(z,p)-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(z),p^{\prime})\geq k_{D^{\prime}}(f(z),f(p))-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(z),p^{\prime})\geq-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(p),p^{\prime}).

Remark 10.3
.

A sequence
(z_{n})
in
D
satisfies
h_{\xi,p}(z_{n})\to-\infty
if and only if
it is eventually contained in every horosphere centered at
\xi
.
This is the case for example if
(z_{n})

K
-converges to
\xi
(by (iii) in Lemma
7.3
).

Lemma 10.4
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains. Let
\xi\in\partial D,\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
be
points of locally finite type, and let
p\in D,p^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
.
Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a holomorphic map such that
\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}<+\infty
. Let
(z_{n})
be a sequence in
D
converging to
\xi
inside a horosphere centered at
\xi
such that
f(z_{n})\to\eta
.
If
(x_{n})
is a sequence in
D
converging to
\xi
with bounded dilation, then we have
f(x_{n})\to\eta
.

Proof.

Let
A\geq 0
be such that for all
n\geq 0
we have

k_{D}(x_{n},p)-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(x_{n}),p^{\prime})\leq A,

so
k_{D^{\prime}}(f(x_{n}),p^{\prime})\geq k_{D}(x_{n},p)-A\to+\infty
. Hence, up to subsequence,
(f(x_{n}))
converges to a point
\nu\in\partial^{*}D^{\prime}.

Now

\displaystyle 2(f(x_{n})|f(z_{m}))_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=k_{D^{\prime}}(f(x_{n}),p^{\prime})+k_{D^{\prime}}(f(z_{m}),p^{\prime})-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(x_{n}),f(z_{m}))

\displaystyle\geq k_{D}(x_{n},p)+k_{D^{\prime}}(f(z_{m}),p^{\prime})-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(x_{n}),f(z_{m}))-A

\displaystyle\geq k_{D}(x_{n},p)+k_{D^{\prime}}(f(z_{m}),p^{\prime})-k_{D}(x_{n},z_{m})-A.

Since
h_{\xi,p}(z_{m}):=\lim_{n\to+\infty}k_{D}(z_{m},x_{n})-k_{D}(x_{n},p)
is bounded from above there exists a constant
M\geq 0
and a subsequence
(n_{m})
such that for all
m\geq 0
we have

k_{D}(z_{m},x_{n_{m}})-k_{D}(x_{n_{m}},p)\leq M.

Hence

\lim_{m\to+\infty}(f(x_{n_{m}})|f(z_{m}))=+\infty,

which implies by (i) in Proposition
3.6
that
\nu=\eta
.
∎

Lemma 10.5
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains. Let
\xi\in\partial D,\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
be points of locally finite type, let
p,q\in D
and
p^{\prime},q^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
. Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a holomorphic map such that
\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}<+\infty
and assume that there exists a sequence
(z_{n})
in
D
converging to
\xi
inside a horosphere centered at
\xi
such that

f(z_{n})\to\eta
.
Then

(i)

\log\lambda_{\xi,q,q^{\prime}}=\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}+h^{D}_{\xi,p}(q)+h^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta,q^{\prime}}(p^{\prime});

(ii)

the notion of dilation minimizing is base-point independent, that is any dilation minimizing sequence w.r.t. base-points
p,p^{\prime}
is dilation minimizing also w.r.t. any
q,q^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
w_{n}\to\xi
be a dilation minimizing sequence w.r.t.
p,p^{\prime}
. We have

\displaystyle k_{D}(q,w_{n})-k_{D^{\prime}}(q^{\prime},f(w_{n}))

\displaystyle=k_{D}(p,w_{n})-k_{D^{\prime}}(p^{\prime},f(w_{n}))+k_{D}(q,w_{n})-k_{D}(p,w_{n})

\displaystyle+k_{D^{\prime}}(p^{\prime},f(w_{n}))-k_{D^{\prime}}(q^{\prime},f(w_{n})).

By Lemma
10.4

f(w_{n})\to\eta
, and by Theorem
6.10

it follows that

k_{D}(q,w_{n})-k_{D^{\prime}}(q^{\prime},f(w_{n}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to+\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}+h^{D}_{\xi,p}(q)+h^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta,q^{\prime}}(p^{\prime}).

(10.1)

Hence

\log\lambda_{\xi,q,q^{\prime}}\leq\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}+h^{D}_{\xi,p}(q)+h^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta,q^{\prime}}(p^{\prime}),

and considering a minimizing sequence w.r.t.
q,q^{\prime}
we obtain (i). Notice that (
10.1
) together with (i) implies (ii).
∎

Proposition 10.6

(Julia Lemma)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains. Let
\xi\in\partial D,\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
be points of locally finite type. Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a holomorphic map, and let
p\in D
and
p^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}<+\infty
and there exists a sequence
(z_{n})
in
D
converging to
\xi
inside a horosphere centered at
\xi
such that
f(z_{n})\to\eta
;

(2)

for all
z\in D

h^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta,p^{\prime}}(f(z))-h^{D}_{\xi,p}(z)\leq\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}},

or equivalently,

f(E^{D}_{p}(\xi,R))\subseteq E^{D^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}(\eta,\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}R),\quad\forall R>0;

(3)

the function
h^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta,p^{\prime}}(f(z))-h^{D}_{\xi,p}(z)
is bounded from above;

(4)

\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}<+\infty
and
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta
.

Proof.

We prove
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
.
Let
(w_{n})
be a sequence in
D
converging to
\xi
such that

k_{D}(w_{n},p)-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(w_{n}),p^{\prime})\to\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}.

By Lemma
10.4
we have
f(w_{n})\to\eta
. Then (2) follows from the metric Julia Lemma
[
12
, Lemma 6.14]
.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
is trivial.

We prove
(3)\Rightarrow(4)
.
If
(w_{n})
is a sequence
K
-converging to
\xi
, then by (iii) in Lemma
7.3

h^{D}_{\xi,p}(w_{n})\to-\infty
, and thus

h^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta,p^{\prime}}(f(w_{n}))\to-\infty.
By Remark
6.12
it follows that
f(w_{n})\to\eta
, and thus
\eta
is the
K
-limit of
f
at
\xi
.
Moreover, if
\gamma
is a geodesic ray with
\gamma(0)=p
and endpoint
\xi
, then for all
t\geq 0
we have

k_{D}(p,\gamma(t))-k_{D^{\prime}}(p^{\prime},f(\gamma(t)))\leq h^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta,p^{\prime}}(f(\gamma(t)))-h^{D}_{\xi,p}(\gamma(t)),

(10.2)

and thus
\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}<+\infty.

(4)\Rightarrow(1)
is trivial.

∎

Remark 10.7
.

If the codomain
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
is a bounded convex domain of finite type, then it is easy to see that if
\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}<+\infty,
then there exists
\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
such that
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta
.

Definition 10.8
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains. Let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type. Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
holomorphic, and fix base-points
p\in D,p^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
. We say that
\xi
is a
regular contact point
if
\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}<+\infty
and if
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta
, where
\eta
is a point of locally finite type in
\partial D^{\prime}
.

Remark 10.9
.

By Lemma
10.5
the previous definition does not depend on the chosen base-points.

The next result is an important consequence of the Julia Lemma.

Proposition 10.10
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains.
Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a holomorphic map, and let
p\in D
and
p^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
. Let
\xi\in\partial D
,
\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
be points of locally finite type and assume that
\xi
is a regular contact point with
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta
.
Then, if
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow D
is a geodesic ray with endpoint

, we have

\lim_{t\to+\infty}k_{D}(\gamma(t),p)-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(\gamma(t)),p^{\prime})=\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}.

(10.3)

As a consequence

\sup_{z\in D}\left(h^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta,p^{\prime}}(f(z))-h^{D}_{\xi,p}(z)\right)=\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}.

(10.4)

Proof.

Set
q:=\gamma(0)
. Arguing as in

[
13
, Lemma 3.12]
using (2) in Proposition
10.6

we have that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}k_{D}(\gamma(t),q)-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(\gamma(t)),p^{\prime})=\log\lambda_{\xi,q,p^{\prime}}.

Then (
10.3
) follows from (ii) in Lemma
10.5
.
Equation (
10.4
) now follows using (
10.2
).
∎

The definition of regular contact point given above is different from the one given in Definition
4.5
for holomorphic maps with domain of definition
\mathbb{D}
. The next result shows that the two definitions are in fact equivalent. The proof is similar to
[
13
, Proposition 3.8, Lemma 3.13]
so we omit it.

Proposition 10.11
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains. Let
\xi\in\partial D,\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
be points locally finite type, and let
p\in D,p^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
.
Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a holomorphic map. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}<+\infty
and
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta
;

(2)

there exists a geodesic ray
\gamma
with endpoint
\xi
such that

is a
(1,B)
quasi-geodesic with endpoint
\eta
;

(3)

for all geodesic rays
\gamma
with endpoint

is an almost-geodesic with endpoint
\eta
.

We now prove other consequences of the Julia lemma. The first is that
K
-regions are mapped into
K
-regions.

Corollary 10.12
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains. Let
\xi\in\partial D,\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
be

.
Let

be holomorphic, and assume that

is a regular contact point with

,

Proof.

Let

, then by the Julia Lemma

\displaystyle h_{\eta,f(p)}(f(z))+k_{D^{\prime}}(f(z),f(p))\leq h_{\xi,p}(z)+\log\lambda_{\xi,p,f(p)}+k_{D}(z,p)

\displaystyle<2\log M+\log\lambda_{\xi,p,f(p)}=2\log(\lambda_{\xi,p,f(p)}^{1/2}M).

∎

Another corollary of the Julia Lemma is a chain rule for the dilation.

Corollary 10.13
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{d^{\prime}}
, and
D^{\prime\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{d^{\prime\prime}}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains.
Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
,
g\colon D^{\prime}\to D^{\prime\prime}
be holomorphic maps and assume that

\xi\in\partial D
is a regular contact point of
f
with
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
, and that

\eta
is a regular contact point of

with

. Then
\xi
is a regular contact point of

with

and

\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime\prime}}(g\circ f)=\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}(f)\cdot\lambda_{\eta,p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}(g),

where

.

Proof.

By the Julia Lemma we obtain

h_{\zeta,p^{\prime\prime}}(g(f(z)))\leq h_{\xi,p}(z)+\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}(f)+\log\lambda_{\eta,p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}(g),\quad\forall z\in D.

Again by the Julia Lemma this implies that
\xi
is a regular contact point of the map

,
and that the boundary point
\zeta
is the

-limit of

at
\xi
. By (

) we obtain

\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime\prime}}(g\circ f)\leq\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}(f)+\log\lambda_{\eta,p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}(g).

On the other hand, if
\gamma
is a geodesic ray in
D
with endpoint
\xi
, by (

) we have

\displaystyle\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime\prime}}(g\circ f)=\lim_{t\to+\infty}k_{D}(\gamma(t),p)-k_{D^{\prime\prime}}(g(f(\gamma(t))),p^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{t\to+\infty}k_{D}(\gamma(t),p)-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(\gamma(t)),p^{\prime})+\liminf_{t\to+\infty}k_{D^{\prime}}(f(\gamma(t)),p^{\prime})-k_{D^{\prime\prime}}(g(f(\gamma(t))),p^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}(f)+\log\lambda_{\eta,p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime}}(g).

∎

We end this section with an extrinsic characterization of regular contact points.

Proposition 10.14
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains. Let
\xi\in\partial D,\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
be points of locally finite type. Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a holomorphic map and assume
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta
.
The following are equivalent.

(1)

\xi
is a regular contact point;

(2)

\delta_{D^{\prime}}(f(z))/\delta_{D}(z)
is
K
-bounded at
\xi
;

(3)

there exists a sequence
(z_{n})
in
D
,
K
-converging to
\xi
, such that
the sequence
(\delta_{D^{\prime}}(f(z_{n}))/\delta_{D}(z_{n}))
is bounded.

Proof.

We prove
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Let
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to D
be a geodesic ray with endpoint
\xi
. Since
\xi
is a regular contact point, by (
10.3
) there exists
A\geq 0
such that for all
t\geq 0

k_{D}(\gamma(t),p)-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(\gamma(t)),p^{\prime})\leq A.

Fix
R>0
and let
z\in A(\gamma,R)
be close to
\xi
, then by definition there exists
t^{*}\geq 0
such that
k_{D}(z,\gamma(t^{*}))<R
. Now using Lemma
3.8
for
D
near
\xi
and
D^{\prime}
near
\eta
we can find

such that

\displaystyle\log\frac{\delta_{D^{\prime}}(f(z))}{\delta_{D}(z)}

\displaystyle\leq k_{D}(z,p)-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(z),p^{\prime})+c

\displaystyle\leq k_{D}(z,\gamma(t^{*}))+k_{D}(\gamma(t^{*}),p)+k_{D^{\prime}}(f(z),f(\gamma(t^{*})))-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(\gamma(t^{*})),p^{\prime})+c

\displaystyle\leq 2R+A+c.

The implication
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
is trivial, and

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
follows from Lemma
3.8
.
∎

The previous result can be strengthened if the codomain
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
is a bounded convex domain of finite type.

Proposition 10.15
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain, and let
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be a bounded convex domain of finite type. Let
\xi\in\partial D
be a
point of locally finite type. Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a holomorphic map.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\xi
is a regular contact point;

(2)

\delta_{D^{\prime}}(f(z))/\delta_{D}(z)
is
K
-bounded at
\xi
;

(3)

\liminf_{z\to\xi}\delta_{D^{\prime}}(f(z))/\delta_{D}(z)<+\infty.

Proof.

If
\xi
is a regular contact point then (2) holds by the previous proposition. (2)
\Rightarrow
(3) is trivial.
If (3) holds, let
(w_{n})
be a sequence in
D
converging to
\xi
such that

\delta_{D^{\prime}}(f(w_{n}))/\delta_{D}(w_{n})
is bounded. Up to a subsequence we may assume that the sequence
(f(w_{n}))
converges to a point in
\eta\in\overline{D^{\prime}}
. Since
\delta_{D}(w_{n})\to 0
we have
\delta_{D^{\prime}}(f(w_{n}))\to 0
, and thus
\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
. By Lemma

and Remark

\xi
is a regular contact point.
∎

11.
Pluricomplex Poisson kernel and normalized dilation

In our generalization of the Julia-Wolff-Carathéodory theorem we want to establish a relationship between the dilation
\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}(f)
of a holomorphic map at a regular contact point
\xi\in\partial D
(with
K
-limit
\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
) and the restricted
K
-limit of the normal component of the normal directional derivative

\langle df_{z}(n_{\xi}),n_{\eta}\rangle
. The fact that the dilation
\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}(f)
depends on the base-points
p\in D,p^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
is, from this point of view, a disturbance. We now introduce, in the setting of
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains of finite type, a function which generalizes the pluricomplex Poisson kernel originally defined (with completely different methods) by Bracci–Patrizio–Trapani
[
22
,
23
]
on strongly convex domains with
C^{\infty}
boundary, and we use it to normalize the dilation and eliminate the dependence on the base-points.

Recall that by Corollary
6.8
two complex geodesics with the same endpoint
\xi\in\partial D
are strongly asymptotic.

Lemma 11.1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be of locally finite type.
Let
\varphi,\psi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
be two complex geodesics with the same endpoint
\xi
.
Let
T\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
k_{D}(\tilde{\varphi}(t),\tilde{\psi}(t+T))\to 0.
Then

T=h_{\xi,\varphi(0)}(\psi(0)).

In particular, if
\varphi(0)=\psi(0)
, then
T=0.

Proof.

We have

h_{\xi,\varphi(0)}(\psi(0))=\lim_{t\to+\infty}k_{D}(\tilde{\psi}(0),\tilde{\varphi}(t))-k_{D}(\tilde{\varphi}(t),\tilde{\varphi}(0))=\lim_{t\to+\infty}k_{D}(\tilde{\psi}(0),\tilde{\psi}(t+T))-k_{D}(\tilde{\varphi}(t),\tilde{\varphi}(0))=T.

∎

Proposition 11.2
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type. Let
\varphi,\psi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
be two complex geodesics with the same endpoint
\xi
.
Let
T\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
k_{D}(\tilde{\varphi}(t),\tilde{\psi}(t+T))\to 0.

Then

T=\log\frac{\psi^{\prime}_{N}(1)}{\varphi_{N}^{\prime}(1)},

(11.1)

and thus

h_{\xi,\varphi(0)}(\psi(0))=\log\frac{\psi^{\prime}_{N}(1)}{\varphi_{N}^{\prime}(1)}.

(11.2)

In particular if
\varphi(0)=\psi(0)
, then
\psi^{\prime}_{N}(1)=\varphi_{N}^{\prime}(1).

Proof.

Let
a\in(-1,1)
be such that
\frac{1-a}{1+a}=\frac{\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(1)}{\psi_{N}^{\prime}(1)}
and let
\tau_{a}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be the automorphism of the disc given by
\tau_{a}(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta+a}{1+a\zeta}
. Notice that
\tau^{\prime}_{a}(1)=\frac{1-a}{1+a}.

The complex geodesic
\psi_{a}:=\psi\circ\tau_{a}
satisfies
(\psi_{a})^{\prime}_{N}(1)=\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(1)
, so by Theorem
6.7
we have

\lim_{t\to+\infty}{k_{D}}(\tilde{\varphi}(t),\tilde{\psi}_{a}(t))=0.

A simple computation shows that
\tilde{\psi}_{a}(t)=\tilde{\psi}\left(t+\log\frac{\psi^{\prime}_{N}(1)}{\varphi_{N}^{\prime}(1)}\right)
.
Equation
11.2
follows combining (
11.1
) with Lemma
11.1
.
∎

Definition 11.3
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.
For all
z\in D
we define the
pluricomplex Poisson kernel

\Omega_{\xi}\colon D\to(-\infty,0)
as

\Omega_{\xi}(z)=-\frac{1}{\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(1)},

where
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
is a complex geodesic such that
\varphi(0)=z
and with endpoint
\xi
. Notice that it is not known whether there is a
unique
complex geodesic connecting the point
z
to
\xi
, however
\Omega_{\xi}
is well-defined thanks to Proposition
11.2
.

Remark 11.4
.

It follows from Proposition
11.2
that if
z,w\in D
, then

h_{\xi,z}(w)=\log|\Omega_{\xi}(z)|-\log|\Omega_{\xi}(w)|,

(11.3)

hence
\Omega_{\xi}
is continuous and its level sets are exactly the horospheres centered in
\xi
.

The function
\Omega^{\mathbb{D}}_{1}
coincides with the classical (negative) Poisson kernel in the disc:

\Omega^{\mathbb{D}}_{1}(\zeta)=-\frac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|1-\zeta|^{2}}.

Moreover, the function
\Omega_{\xi}
is a multiple of the classical Poisson kernel in the unit disc when restricted to a complex geodesic with endpoint
\xi
.

Corollary 11.5
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type. Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
be a complex geodesic with endpoint
\xi
.
Then, for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
,

\Omega_{\xi}^{D}(\varphi(\zeta))=|\Omega_{\xi}^{D}(\varphi(0))|\Omega_{1}^{\mathbb{D}}(\zeta)=\frac{\Omega_{1}^{\mathbb{D}}(\zeta)}{\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(1)}.

Proof.

By (
11.3
),

h^{\mathbb{D}}_{1,0}(\zeta)=-\log|\Omega^{\mathbb{D}}_{1}(\zeta)|,\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{D}.

Again by (
11.3
) we obtain, for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
,

-\log|\Omega^{\mathbb{D}}_{1}(\zeta)|=h^{\mathbb{D}}_{1,0}(\zeta)=h^{D}_{\xi,\varphi(0)}(\varphi(\zeta))=\log|\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(\varphi(0))|-\log|\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(\varphi(\zeta))|.

∎

Example 11.6
.

Using the previous corollary and formula (
4.2
) one easily obtains an explicit formula for the pluricomplex Poisson kernel of the egg domain
\mathbb{E}_{m}
at the point
\xi=(1,0)
, that is

\Omega^{\mathbb{E}_{m}}_{\xi}(z_{0},z_{1})=-\frac{1-|z_{0}|^{2}-|z_{1}|^{m}}{|1-z_{0}|^{2}}.

Corollary 11.7
.

If
D\subset\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
is strongly convex with
C^{\infty}
boundary, the function
\Omega_{\xi}
coincides with the pluricomplex Poisson kernel introduced by Bracci–Patrizio–Trapani
[
22
,
23
]
.

Proof.

See (1.2) in
[
23
]
.
∎

If
D\subset\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
is strongly convex with

boundary, the pluricomplex Poisson kernel
\Omega_{\xi}
solves the following homogeneous Monge-Ampère equation with a simple singularity at the boundary:

\begin{cases}\Omega_{\xi}\mbox{ is plurisubharmonic in }D\\
(\partial\overline{\partial}\Omega_{\xi})^{d}=0\ \mbox{ in }D\\
\Omega_{\xi}<0\ \mbox{ in }D\\
\Omega_{\xi}(\eta)=0\ \mbox{ if }\eta\in\partial D\backslash\{\xi\}\\
\Omega_{\xi}(z)\approx\|z-\xi\|^{-1}\mbox{ as }z\to\xi\mbox{ non-tangentially}\end{cases}

In a forthcoming paper
[

]
with F. Bracci we prove that in convex domains of finite type the function

solves an equation analogous to (

).

Definition 11.8

(Normalized dilation)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains and
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a holomorphic map.
Let
\xi\in\partial D,\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
be points of locally finite type.
Assume that
\xi
is a regular contact point with
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta
. We define the
normalized dilation
of
f
at
\xi
as

\alpha_{\xi}:=\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}\frac{\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(p)}{\Omega^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta}(p^{\prime})}\in(0,+\infty),

where
p\in D,p^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
.

The next result shows that the normalized dilation is well-defined, that is, it does not depend on the chosen base-points
p\in D,p^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}.

Lemma 11.9
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d},D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains and let
\xi\in\partial D,\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
be points of locally finite type.
Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a holomorphic map, and assume that
\xi
is a regular contact point with
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta
.
Then the positive real number

\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}\frac{\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(p)}{\Omega^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta}(p^{\prime})}\in(0,+\infty)

is independent on
p\in D,p^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
p,q\in D,p^{\prime},q^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
. By Lemma
10.5
and (
11.3
) we have

\displaystyle\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}-\log\lambda_{\xi,q,q^{\prime}}+\log|\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(p)|-\log|\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(q)|-\log|\Omega^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta}(p^{\prime})|+\log|\Omega^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta}(q^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{D}_{\xi,q}(p)+h^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta,p^{\prime}}(q^{\prime})+h^{D}_{\xi,p}(q)+h^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta,q^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})=0.

∎

Remark 11.10
.

Notice that, if
D^{\prime}=\mathbb{D}
, then
\alpha_{\xi}=\lambda_{\xi,p,0}|\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(p)|
.

Remark 11.11
.

It immediately follows from Corollary
10.13
that the normalized dilation satisfies a chain rule:

\alpha_{\xi}(g\circ f)=\alpha_{\xi}(f)\cdot\alpha_{\eta}(g).

Lemma 11.12
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.
Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to D
be a complex geodesic with endpoint
\xi
. Then
1
is a regular contact point of
\varphi
and

\alpha_{1}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{|\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(\varphi(0))|}=\varphi_{N}^{\prime}(1).

Moreover, if

\tilde{\rho}\colon D\to\mathbb{D}
is a left inverse of
\varphi
,
then
\xi
is a regular contact point of
\tilde{\rho}
, and

\alpha_{\xi}(\tilde{\rho})=|\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(\varphi(0))|=\frac{1}{\varphi_{N}^{\prime}(1)}.

Proof.

Notice that since
\varphi
is a complex geodesic, we have
\lambda_{1,0,\varphi(0)}(\varphi)=1.
Hence

\alpha_{1}(\varphi)=\lambda_{1,0,\varphi(0)}(\varphi)\frac{\Omega_{1}^{\mathbb{D}}(0)}{\Omega_{\xi}^{D}(\varphi(0))}=\frac{1}{|\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(\varphi(0))|}.

Similarly one obtains the second statement.

∎

12.
The Julia–Wolff–Carathéodory theorem

12.1.
Statement of the result

Our main theorem is the following generalization of the Julia–Wolff-Carathéodory theorem.

Theorem 12.1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains.
Let
\xi\in\partial D,\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
be points of locally finite type with multitype respectively
(m_{j})_{j=0}^{d-1}
and
(n_{i})_{i=0}^{q-1}
.
Let
(v_{j})_{j=0}^{d-1}
(resp.
(u_{i})_{i=0}^{q-1}
) be an orthonormal multitype basis at
\xi
(resp. at
\eta
).
Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a holomorphic map and assume that
\xi
is a regular contact point with
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta
.
Then for all
0\leq j\leq d-1
and
0\leq i\leq q-1
,

\langle df_{z}(v_{j}),u_{i}\rangle=O_{K}\biggl(\delta_{D}(z)^{\frac{1}{n_{i}}-\frac{1}{m_{j}}}\biggr)

(12.1)

and

(i)

K^{\prime}\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}\langle df_{z}(v_{0}),u_{0}\rangle=\alpha_{\xi}:=\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}\frac{\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(p)}{\Omega^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta}(p^{\prime})}\in(0,+\infty)
;

(ii)

\langle df_{z}(v_{j}),u_{0}\rangle=o_{K^{\prime}}\biggl(\delta_{D}(z)^{1-\frac{1}{m_{j}}}\biggr)
for all
1\leq j\leq d-1
;

(iii)

\langle df_{z}(v_{0}),u_{i}\rangle=o_{K^{\prime}}\biggl(\delta_{D}(z)^{\frac{1}{n_{i}}-1}\biggr)
for all
1\leq i\leq q-1
.

Remark 12.2
.

(cf. Remark
7.11
). It is natural to wonder whether

\langle df_{z}(v),u\rangle=O_{K}\biggl(\delta_{D}(z)^{\frac{1}{m_{\eta}(u)}-\frac{1}{m_{\xi}(v)}}\biggr)

(12.2)

for all
v,u\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. This turns out to be false in general (see e.g. Example
12.9
). However, it follows from (
12.1
) using simple linear algebra that

\langle df_{z}(v),u\rangle=O_{K}\biggl(\delta_{D}(z)^{\frac{1}{M_{\eta}(u)}-\frac{1}{m_{\xi}(v)}}\biggr).

It is worth stating Theorem
12.1
in the particular case of functions with values in the unit disc. This statement will actually play an important role in the proof of Theorem
12.1
.

Theorem 12.3
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type.
Let
f\colon D\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function, and assume that
\xi
is a regular contact point.
Then for all

,

\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}(z)=O_{K}\left(\delta_{D}(z)^{1-\frac{1}{m_{\xi}(v)}}\right).

Moreover

(i)

K^{\prime}\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}\frac{\partial f}{\partial n_{\xi}}(z)=\eta\alpha_{\xi}=\eta|\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(p)|\lambda_{\xi,p},

where
\eta=K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)
,

(ii)

\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}(z)=o_{K^{\prime}}\left(\delta_{D}(z)^{1-\frac{1}{m_{\xi}(v)}}\right)
if
v\in T^{\mathbb{C}}_{\xi}\partial D\setminus\{0\}
.

Hence
if

,

K^{\prime}\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}(z)=\langle v,n_{\xi}\rangle\eta\alpha_{\xi}=\langle v,n_{\xi}\rangle\eta\,|\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(p)|\lambda_{\xi,p}.

We divide the proof of Theorem

in three steps.

12.1
)

If

-\log(1-|\zeta|)\leq k_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\zeta)\leq\log 2-\log(1-|\zeta|).

(12.3)

Lemma 12.5
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}

Let

H
denotes the harmonic mean, then clearly

\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{1}{n_{j}}-\frac{1}{m_{j}}=\frac{d}{H(n_{0},\dots,n_{d-1})}-\frac{d}{H(m_{0},\dots,m_{d-1})}.

In particular, if the harmonic mean of the multitype at
\eta
is strictly smaller than the harmonic mean of the multitype at
\xi
, then

K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}\det df_{z}=0

Theorem
12.1
has an interesting consequence for self-maps.

Corollary 12.12
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and
\xi\in\partial D
a point of locally finite type and let
f\colon D\to D
be a holomorphic self-map.
If
\xi
is a regular contact point with
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\xi
,
then for all
p\in D

K^{\prime}\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}\langle df_{z}(n_{\xi}),n_{\xi}\rangle=\lambda_{\xi,p,p}(f).

We now prove a version of the Julia Lemma for the pluricomplex Poisson kernel.

Proposition 12.13
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains, and let
\xi\in\partial D,\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
be points of locally finite type.
Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a holomorphic map.

(i)

If the function

z\mapsto\frac{\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(z)}{\Omega^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta}(f(z))}

is bounded from above, then

\xi
is a regular contact point for
f
with
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta
.

(ii)

If
\xi
is a regular contact point for
f
with
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta
, then

\sup_{z\in D}\frac{\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(z)}{\Omega^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta}(f(z))}=\alpha_{\xi}=K^{\prime}\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}\langle df_{z}(n_{\xi}),n_{\eta}\rangle.

Proof.

Point (i) follows from the implication
(3)\Rightarrow(4)
in the Julia Lemma (Theorem
10.6
) and from (
11.3
).
We prove (ii). Fix
p\in D,p^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
.
From (
10.4
) and (
11.3
) we obtain

\sup_{z\in D}\left(\log|\Omega^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta}(p^{\prime})|-\log|\Omega^{D^{\prime}}_{\eta}(f(z))|-\log|\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(p)|+\log|\Omega^{D}_{\xi}(z)|\right)=\log\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}},

and the result immediately follows.
∎

Remark 12.14
.

Point (ii) of the previous proposition yields an interesting estimate from below of
K^{\prime}\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}\langle df_{z}(n_{\xi}),n_{\eta}\rangle
.
As an example, if
f\colon\mathbb{E}_{m}\to\mathbb{E}_{m}
if a holomorphic self-map such that
\xi=(1,0)
is a regular contact point with

K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\xi
, then by Example
11.6
if
f(0)=(z_{0},z_{1})
is the image of the origin, we have

K^{\prime}\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}\langle df_{z}(e_{0}),e_{0}\rangle\geq\frac{|1-z_{0}|^{2}}{1-|z_{0}|^{2}-|z_{1}|^{m}}.

Corollary 12.15
.

Let
D,D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains, and let
\xi\in\partial D,\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
be points of locally finite type. Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a biholomorphism and assume that there exists a sequence
(z_{n})
in
D
which is
K
-converging to
\xi
such that
f(z_{n})\to\eta
. Then for all
w\in D

\frac{\Omega_{\eta}(w)}{\Omega_{\xi}(f(w))}=\alpha_{\xi}=K^{\prime}\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}\langle df_{z}(n_{\xi}),n_{\eta}\rangle.

Proof.

It is enough to notice that, since
f
is a biholomorphism,

\log\lambda_{\xi,w,f(w)}=\liminf_{z\to\xi}k_{D}(z,w)-k_{D^{\prime}}(f(z),f(w))=0

for all
w\in D
.
∎

We conclude with two extrinsic characterizations of regular contact points (notice the analogy with
[
1
, Propositions 1.2.6, 1.2.8]
).

Corollary 12.16
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain and
\xi\in\partial D
be a point of locally finite type. Let

f\colon D\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic map, then
\xi
is a regular contact point if and only if

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}|f(\xi-tn_{\xi})|=1

(12.5)

and

\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial n_{\xi}}(\xi-tn_{\xi})\right|<\infty.

(12.6)

Proof.

Assume that
\xi
is a regular contact point for
f
, then (
12.5
) follows from the definition while (
12.6
) follows from Theorem
12.3
.

Conversely, assume (
12.5
) and (
12.6
).
Let
t_{0}>0
and
M>0
such that for all
0\leq t\leq t_{0}
we have
\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial n_{\xi}}(\xi-tn_{\xi})\right|\leq M.

Up to taking a smaller
t_{0}
we have for all
0\leq t\leq t_{0}

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{\mathbb{D}}(f(\xi-tn_{\xi}))}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-|f(\xi-tn_{\xi})|}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{{\operatorname{Re}\,}(\overline{f(\xi-sn_{\xi})}\frac{\partial f}{\partial n_{\xi}}(\xi-sn_{\xi}))}{|f(\xi-sn_{\xi})|}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial n_{\xi}}(\xi-sn_{\xi})\right|ds\leq M,

which implies by Proposition
10.15
that
\xi
is a regular contact point.

Corollary 12.17
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
D^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{q}
be
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domains and let
\xi\in\partial D,\eta\in\partial D^{\prime}
be points of locally finite type. Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be a holomorphic map, then
\xi
is a regular contact point with
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta
if and only if

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}f(\xi-tn_{\xi})=\eta

(12.7)

and

\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}|\langle df_{\xi-tn_{\xi}}(n_{\xi}),n_{\eta}\rangle|<\infty.

(12.8)

Proof.

Assume that
\xi
is a regular contact point with
K\textrm{-}\lim_{z\to\xi}f(z)=\eta
.
Then (
12.7
) is obvious and (
12.8
) follows from (i) in Theorem
12.1
.

Conversely, assume (
12.7
) and (
12.8
). Assume
\eta=0
,
n_{\eta}=e_{0}
.
Let
\pi\colon\mathbb{C}^{q}\to\mathbb{C}
be the projection on the first component. By the convexity of
D^{\prime}
we have
\pi(D^{\prime})\subseteq\mathbb{H}
. Notice that

\frac{\partial(\pi\circ f)}{\partial n_{\xi}}(z)=\langle df_{z}(n_{\xi}),n_{\eta}\rangle

so by Corollary
12.16

\xi
is a regular contact point for the function
\mathscr{C}^{-1}\circ\pi\circ f\colon D\to\mathbb{D}
(and thus also for
\pi\circ f\colon D\to\mathbb{H}
). Now fix
p\in D
and
p^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
, then

\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}k_{D}(p,\xi-tn_{\xi})-k_{D^{\prime}}(p^{\prime},f(\xi-tn_{\xi}))\leq\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}k_{D}(p,\xi-tn_{\xi})-k_{\mathbb{H}}(\pi(p^{\prime}),\pi(f(\xi-tn_{\xi})))<+\infty,

hence
\lambda_{\xi,p,p^{\prime}}(f)<+\infty
. Finally, the Julia Lemma (Proposition
10.6
) yields the result.
∎

Our results also give us information at points of infinite type.

Corollary 12.18
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-proper convex domain, and let
\xi\in\partial D
be a smooth point of infinite line type. Let
f\colon D\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}|f(\xi-tn_{\xi})|=1

and

\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial n_{\xi}}(\xi-tn_{\xi})\right|<\infty.

Let
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
with
m_{\xi}(v)=+\infty
, then for all
s<1
and for all sequences
(z_{n})
converging non-tangentially to
\xi
we have

\frac{\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}(z_{n})}{\delta_{D}(z_{n})^{s}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to+\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Proof.

First of all assume that
\xi
is the origin and
n_{\xi}=e_{0}
. Let
r\colon\mathbb{C}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth convex defining function of
D
(for instance, the signed distance function), and define

r_{L}(z):=r(z)+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}|z_{j}|^{L}

where
L
is an even integer. Notice that the origin is a point of locally finite type for
D_{L}:=\{r_{L}<0\}
with line type
L
. Moreover, since
r\leq r_{L}
we have
D_{L}\subset D
.
Now for all
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, by definition we have

m^{D_{L}}_{\xi}(v)=\min\{m^{D}_{\xi}(v),L\}.

By Corollary
12.16
the origin is a regular contact point for the map
f|_{D_{L}}:D_{L}\to\mathbb{D}
. Then by Theorem
12.3
and recalling that a sequences which converges non-tangentially to
\xi
is contained in a
K
-region of
D_{L}
(Corollary
7.15
), we have that for all sequence
(z_{n})
converging non-tangentially to
\xi

\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}(z_{n})\delta_{D}(z_{n})^{\frac{1}{\min\{m_{\xi}(v),L\}}-1}

is bounded.
We conclude letting
L\to+\infty
.
∎

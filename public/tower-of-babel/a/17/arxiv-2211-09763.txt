On the growth of the Jacobians in ℤ p l \mathds{Z}_{p}^{l} -voltage covers of graphs
Sören Kleine, Katharina Müller

Abstract
. We investigate the growth of the p p -part of the Jacobians in voltage covers of finite connected multigraphs, where the voltage group is isomorphic to ℤ p l \mathds{Z}_{p}^{l} for some l ≥ 2 {l\geq 2} , and we study analogues of a conjecture of Greenberg on the growth of class numbers in multiple ℤ p \mathds{Z}_{p} -extensions of number fields. Moreover we prove an Iwasawa main conjecture in this setting, and we study the variation of (generalised) Iwasawa invariants as one runs over the ℤ p l \mathds{Z}_{p}^{l} -covers of a fixed finite graph X X . We discuss many examples; in particular, we construct examples with non-trivial Iwasawa invariants.

1.
Introduction

Let
K_{\infty}
be a
\mathds{Z}_{p}
-extension of a number field
K
. Then for each
{m\in\mathds{N}}
there exists a unique subextension
K_{m}/K
of degree
p^{m}
. Let
h_{m}
be the class number of
K_{m}
. Then Iwasawa (see
[
8
]
) proved that we have an asymptotic formula

(1)

\displaystyle v_{p}(h_{m})=\mu(K_{\infty}/K)\cdot p^{m}+\lambda(K_{\infty}/K)\cdot m+\nu(K_{\infty}/K)

for each sufficiently large
m
, where
\mu(K_{\infty}/K)
,
\lambda(K_{\infty}/K)
and
\nu(K_{\infty}/K)
denote the Iwasawa invariants of the
\mathds{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}/K
. More generally, let
K_{\infty}
be a
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
-extension of a number field
K
, with intermediate fields
K_{m}
. Then Greenberg conjectured that there exists a polynomial
{P(X,Y)\in\mathds{Q}[X,Y]}
of total degree at most
l
and of degree at most 1 in
Y
such that

v_{p}(h_{m})=P(p^{m},m)

for each sufficiently large
m
, where
h_{m}
denotes the class number of
K_{m}
,
{m\in\mathds{N}}
. In this paper, we will refer to this conjecture as
Greenberg’s conjecture
.

In Iwasawa theory of graphs one starts with a finite connected graph
X
instead of a number field
K
and considers a sequence of Galois covers

X\leftarrow X_{1}\leftarrow X_{2}\leftarrow X_{3}\leftarrow X_{4}\leftarrow\dots

such that
\textup{Gal}(X_{m}/X)\cong(\mathds{Z}/p^{m}\mathds{Z})^{l}
. Then
X_{m}
is again a finite graph and we will always assume that each
X_{m}
is connected. Instead of the
p
-part of the class group of the field
K_{m}
one considers the
q
-part of the number of spanning trees of
X_{m}
(where
q
is a prime not necessarily distinct from
p
). Vallières and McGown-Vallières proved in a sequence of papers (see
[
18
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
) that (under mild technical hypothesis) the number of spanning trees grows asymptotically as in Greenberg’s conjecture in the case
l=1
and
{p=q}
. In a subsequent work Lei and Vallières considered the case
l=1
and
{q\neq p}
(see
[
13
]
).

In this paper we study analogous growth patterns in the theory of voltage covers of graphs for
l\geq 2
and
{q=p}
. Our approach is purely algebraic and uses the fact that the number of spanning trees is given by the size of the Jacobian of a finite connected graph (see Section
2
for precise definitions). For the case
l=1
this strategy was used to prove an Iwasawa formula like (
1
) for the
p
-part of the number of spanning trees by Gonet
[
6
]
. We are able to prove Greenberg’s conjecture under the assumption that our base graph
X
is connected (see Theorem
4.3
). A result of this form had been obtained before by Vallières and DuBose, generalising the approach of McGown-Vallières, using Ihara L-functions
[
4
]
.

A central aspect of classical Iwasawa theory is the relation between algebraic objects such as certain Galois groups and analytic objects such as
L
-functions (e.g. the Hasse-Weil
L
-function
L(E,s)
for an elliptic curve
E
or the Dedekind zeta-function
\zeta_{K}(s)
for a number field
K
). In many cases one can define a
p
-adic function interpolating special values of these complex analytic functions. Given a finite connected graph
X
and a finite Galois covering
Y/X
we consider the complex analytic Ihara L-function
L_{X}(\chi,s)
for every character
{\chi\in\widehat{\textup{Gal}(Y/X)}}
.

Let now
X
be a finite connected multigraph, and suppose that
X
has
n
vertices. Let
X_{m}
be the
m
-th intermediate graph in a
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
-cover
X_{\infty}
of
X
. Then there exists an element
\Delta_{\infty}\in\textup{Mat}_{n,n}(\mathds{Z}_{p}[[\textup{Gal}(X_{\infty}/X)]])
, such that
\chi(\det(\Delta_{\infty}))
interpolates the "algebraic part" of
L_{X}(\chi,s)
at
s=1
. In this setting we obtain the following

Theorem

(Iwasawa main conjecture (Theorem
5.2
))
.

Let
J(X_{\infty})
be the Jacobian of
X_{\infty}
and let
\Lambda=\mathds{Z}_{p}[[\textup{Gal}(X_{\infty}/X)]]
. Then

\textup{Char}_{\Lambda}(J(X_{\infty})\otimes\Lambda)=\det(\Delta_{\infty}).

Here
\textup{Char}(M)
denotes the characteristic ideal of a finitely generated and torsion
\Lambda
-module
M
(for details we refer to Section
2.1
).

The third central topic we want to address in the present paper is the behaviour of so-called
(generalised) Iwasawa invariants
if we vary the
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
-cover of a given finite connected graph
X
. Let
P(X,Y)
be the polynomial appearing in Greenberg’s conjecture. Then

P(p^{m},m)=m_{0}p^{ml}+l_{0}mp^{m(l-1)}+O(p^{m(l-1)}).

Following Cuoco and Monsky (see
[
3
]
) we call
m_{0}
and
l_{0}
the (generalised) Iwasawa invariants of
J(X_{\infty})\otimes\Lambda
. In Section
6
we define a topology on the set
\mathcal{E}^{l}(X)
of voltage
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
-covers of
X
and we prove the following results.

Theorem

(Theorems
6.4
and
6.5
)
.

We have the following two results.

(1)

Assume that
l=1
and let
{X_{\infty}\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X)}
. Then there exists a (sufficiently small) neighbourhood
U
of
X_{\infty}
such that the following statements hold:

(a)

For each
{\tilde{X}_{\infty}\in U}
, we have

m_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\infty})\leq m_{0}(X_{\infty}).

(b)

{l_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\infty})=l_{0}(X_{\infty})}
for each
{\tilde{X}_{\infty}\in U}
for which
{m_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\infty})=m_{0}(X_{\infty})}
.

(2)

Fix an element
{X_{\infty}\in\mathcal{E}^{l}(X)}
,
{l\geq 2}
. Then there exist an integer
{k\in\mathds{N}}
and a neighbourhood
U
of
K_{\infty}
such that the following two statements hold for each
{\tilde{X}_{\infty}}
in
U
.

(a)

m_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\infty})\leq m_{0}(X_{\infty})
, and

(b)

l_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\infty})\leq k
holds if
{m_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\infty})=m_{0}(X_{\infty})}
.

Let us briefly describe the structure of the article. Section
2
is preliminary in nature – here we introduce the basic notation and the setup. In Section
3
we relate the Jacobians of the Galois covers
X_{m}/X
,
{m\in\mathds{N}}
, to certain quotients of an Iwasawa module. This enables us to describe the growth of these Jacobians by using results of Cuoco and Monsky, and we already obtain a weak result in the direction of Greenberg’s conjecture (see Lemma
3.2
). In Section
4
, we prove Greenberg’s conjecture by studying the voltage
p
-Laplacian of the Galois cover and using a suitable matrix representation in order to describe the growth of the Jacobians in terms of a certain power series. In Section
5
we prove the Iwasawa main conjecture and in Section
6
we prove Theorems
6.4
and
6.5
which have been stated above. The proofs are completely algebraic, and they are based on work of Fukuda (see
[
5
]
) and the first named author (see
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
). In the final two sections, we compute many numerical examples by applying the results from Sections
4
and
5
. In particular, we construct examples of Galois covers
X_{\infty}/X
of graphs with non-trivial
m_{0}
- and
l_{0}
-invariant.

In the classical Iwasawa theory of
\mathds{Z}_{p}
-extensions of number fields, Iwasawa himself was the first who constructed examples (in the case of
{l=1}
) with a non-trivial
m_{0}
-invariant (see
[
9
]
). On the other hand, to the authors’ knowledge no example of a
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
-extension of a number field
K
is known where one can show that the
l_{0}
-invariant is greater than zero (however, there exist such examples in the setting of Selmer groups of elliptic curves, see
[
12
]
). Therefore the construction of an example with non-trivial
l_{0}
-invariant in the final section might be of some interest.

2.
Notation and definitions

2.1.
Iwasawa modules

Let
\Gamma
be a group which is topologically isomorphic to
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
. Then the completed group ring
\mathds{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
can be identified (non-canonically) with the ring
\mathds{Z}_{p}\llbracket T_{1},\ldots,T_{l}\rrbracket
of formal power series in
l
variables, as follows: choose a set of topological generators
{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{l}}
of
\Gamma
, and consider the bijective homomorphism between the group rings which is induced by mapping
\gamma_{i}
to
{T_{i}+1}
, respectively.

In the following we always denote by
\Lambda_{l}=\mathds{Z}_{p}\llbracket T_{1},\ldots,T_{l}\rrbracket
the Iwasawa algebra in
l
variables. If
l
is clear from the context, then we abbreviate
\Lambda_{l}
to
\Lambda
. Sometimes we will also work with the ring
{R=\mathds{Z}_{p}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{l}]}
.

Now we describe the structure theory of
Iwasawa modules
. In this article, any finitely generated
\Lambda
-module
A
will be called an Iwasawa module. An Iwasawa module is called
pseudo-null
if it is annihilated by two relatively prime elements of the unique factorisation domain
\Lambda
. A
pseudo-isomorphism
of finitely generated
\Lambda
-modules
A
and
B
is a
\Lambda
-module homomorphism
{\varphi\colon A\longrightarrow B}
such that the kernel and the cokernel of
\varphi
are pseudo-null.

Let now
A
be any Iwasawa module. Then by the general structure theory (see
[
16
, Proposition 5.1.7(ii)]
) there exists an
elementary

\Lambda
-module

E_{A}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(p^{e_{i}})\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(g_{j})

and a pseudo-isomorphism
{\varphi\colon A\longrightarrow E_{A}}
. Here
g_{1},\ldots,g_{t}
are elements of
\Lambda
which are coprime with
p
. The
characteristic power series

F_{A}
attached to
A
is the element

F_{A}=p^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}e_{i}}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{t}g_{j},

and the
characteristic ideal

\textup{Char}(E_{A})
of
E_{A}
(and
A
respectively) is the principal ideal
(F_{A})
. The characteristic power series is determined up to units of
\Lambda
by the Iwasawa module
A
, and therefore the characteristic ideal is well-defined.

Moreover, we can define the
(generalised) Iwasawa invariants
of
A
as follows. To this purpose, we fix an isomorphism between
\mathds{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
and
\Lambda
, as above, and we consider the characteristic power series
F_{A}
attached to
A
. Let

m_{0}(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}e_{i},

i.e.
p^{m_{0}(A)}
is the exact power of
p
which divides
F_{A}
. Moreover, we write

F_{A}=p^{m_{0}(A)}\cdot G_{A},

i.e.
G_{A}
is coprime with
p
, and we consider the coset
\overline{G_{A}}
of
G_{A}
in the quotient algebra
\overline{\Lambda}=\Lambda/(p)
. Then we define

l_{0}(A)=\sum_{\mathcal{P}}v_{\mathcal{P}}(\overline{G_{A}}),

where the sum runs over all the prime ideals of
\overline{\Lambda}
of the form
{\mathcal{P}=(\overline{\sigma-1})}
for some element
{\sigma\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{p}}
, and where
v_{\mathcal{P}}
denotes the
\mathcal{P}
-adic valuation. Note that this sum is finite since
\overline{\Lambda}
is again a unique factorisation domain.

In the case of
{l=1}
, the structure theory of Iwasawa modules is better understood. In this case, an Iwasawa module is pseudo-null if and only if it is
finite
. Moreover, it follows from the Weierstraß Preparation Theorem for
{\Lambda=\mathds{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket}
(see
[
19
, Theorem 7.3]
) that the characteristic power series
F_{A}
is associated to a
polynomial
of the form

p^{m_{0}(A)}\cdot G_{A},

where
G_{A}
is
distinguished
. This means that
G_{A}
is monic, and that each coefficient of
G_{A}
, apart from the leading one, is divisible by
p
. Then we define the
classical Iwasawa invariants
of
A
as

\mu(A)=m_{0}(A),\quad\lambda(A)=\deg(G_{A})=l_{0}(G_{A}).

If
A=\varprojlim A_{n}
is the classical Iwasawa module of ideal class groups in the intermediate layers of a
\mathds{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}/K
, then the Iwasawa invariants of
A
are the constants from the asymptotic growth formula (
1
) mentioned in the introduction (see
[
8
]
or
[
19
, Chapter 13]
for more details).

We conclude the current section by introducing some more notation. For any
{i\in\{1,\ldots,l\}}
and every
{m\in\mathds{N}}
, we let

\omega_{m}(T_{i})=(T_{i}+1)^{p^{m}}-1.

We also denote by
I_{m}
the ideal of
{R\subseteq\Lambda}
generated by all the
\omega_{m}(T_{i})
, and we denote the quotient
{R/I_{m}}
by
R_{m}
,
{m\in\mathds{N}}
.

2.2.
Graphs

In this article a graph
Y
will always be a connected multigraph.
We denote the edges between the vertices
v
and
w
by
e_{i}(v,w)
. Note that for fixed
v
and
w
the set
E(v,w)
of edges between
v
and
w
might contain more than one element. For any graph
Y
we make the following definitions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
Y
be a (not necessarily finite) graph. Then we denote by
V(Y)
the set of vertices of
Y
. We assume that
\deg(v)
is finite for each
{v\in V(Y)}
(such graphs are called locally finite), where

\deg(v)=\sum_{w\in V(Y),w\neq v}|E(v,w)|+2|\{\textup{loops from $v$ to $v$}\}|

means the
degree
of
v
and
\textup{mult}(v,w)=|E(v,w)|
means the number of edges between
v
and
w
(i.e.
\textup{mult}(v,w)=0
if
v
and
w
are not adjacent). We define

\displaystyle\textup{Div}(Y)

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{v\in V(Y)}a_{v}v\;\Big|\;a_{v}\in\mathds{Z}_{p},a_{v}=0\textup{ for all but finitely many }v\right\}

\displaystyle\textup{Div}^{0}(Y)

\displaystyle=\left\{d=\sum_{v\in V(Y)}a_{v}v\in\textup{Div}(Y)\;\Big|\;\sum_{v\in V(Y)}a_{v}=0\right\}

\displaystyle\textup{Pr}(Y)

\displaystyle=\{d\in\textup{Div}^{0}(Y)\,\mid\,d\sim 0\},

where
d\sim d^{\prime}
if there is a sequence of firing moves that transforms
d
into
d^{\prime}
(see
[
2
, Definition 1.5]
).
We further define the two quotient groups

\displaystyle\textup{Pic}_{p}(Y)

\displaystyle=\textup{Div}(Y)/\textup{Pr}(Y),

\displaystyle J_{p}(Y)

\displaystyle=\textup{Div}^{0}(Y)/\textup{Pr}(Y).

Note that
J_{p}(Y)
is the
p
-Sylow subgroup of the
Jacobian
of
Y
. As the prime
p
is fixed once and for all we will write
J(Y)
instead of
J_{p}(Y)
from now on. If
Y
is finite and connected, then it is well-known that
J(Y)
is a finite abelian group (see
[
2
, Proposition 2.37]
). Furthermore, the group
\textup{Pr}(Y)
is generated by the elements

p_{v}=\deg(v)v-\sum_{w\sim v,w\neq v}\textup{mult}(v,w)w-2|\{\textup{loops from $v$ to $v$}\}|v\quad\forall v\in V(Y).

Let now
X
be a finite graph with
n
vertices. We specialise to the situation of voltage covers
X_{m}
(see also
[
6
]
). Let
\mathbf{E}_{X}
and
E_{X}
be the sets of directed and undirected edges, of
X
and let
\gamma\colon E_{X}\longrightarrow\mathbf{E}_{X}
be a section of the natural map
\mathbf{E}_{X}\longrightarrow E_{X}
. Let
S=\gamma(E_{X})
. We fix a
voltage assignment
, i.e. a map

\alpha\colon S\longrightarrow\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}.

Using the natural projection maps
{\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}\longrightarrow(\mathds{Z}/p^{m}\mathds{Z})^{l}}
,
{m\in\mathds{N}}
,
\alpha
induces well-defined assignments

\alpha_{m}\colon S\longrightarrow(\mathds{Z}/p^{m}\mathds{Z})^{l}.

Since the target groups of
\alpha
in our applications usually will be Galois groups, we write the target groups of
\alpha
and of the
\alpha_{m}
multiplicatively (although this might look strangely). Note that for each element
e\in\mathbf{E}_{X}
either
e\in S
or
e^{-1}\in S
. If
e\notin S
, we define
\alpha(e)=\alpha(e^{-1})^{-1}
and analogously for
\alpha_{m}
. Thus, we can interpret
\alpha
and
\alpha_{m}
as maps defined on
\mathbf{E}_{X}
.

Definition 2.2
.

We define the derived graph
{X_{m}:=X((\mathds{Z}/p^{m}\mathds{Z})^{l},S,\alpha_{m})}
on the vertices
(v,g\!\!\pmod{p^{m}})
, where
g\in\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
and
g\!\!\pmod{p^{m}}
denotes the equivalence class of
g
in
(\mathds{Z}/p^{m}\mathds{Z})^{l}
. We draw an edge between
(v,g\!\!\pmod{p^{m}})
and
(v^{\prime},g^{\prime}\!\!\pmod{p^{m}})
if there is an edge
e
from
v
to
v^{\prime}
in
\mathbf{E}_{X}
such that
{g^{\prime}\pmod{p^{m}}=g\cdot\alpha_{m}(e)\pmod{p^{m}}}
.

We abbreviate the vertices
(v,1)
of the base graph to
v
.

We have a natural action of
(\mathds{Z}/p^{m}\mathds{Z})^{l}
on
X_{m}
given by

(g^{\prime}\!\!\!\!\pmod{p^{m}})\circ(x,g\!\!\!\!\pmod{p^{m}})=(x,g\cdot g^{\prime}\!\!\!\!\pmod{p^{m}}).

By using the natural projection
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}\longrightarrow(\mathds{Z}/p^{m}\mathds{Z})^{l}
, we also have a natural action of
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
on
X_{m}
. This induces a well-defined action of
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
on
J(X_{m})
. We also obtain an action of the two canonical group rings

R:=\mathds{Z}_{p}[\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}],\quad\mathds{Z}_{p}[[\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}]].

The ring
\mathds{Z}_{p}[\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}]
is isomorphic non-canonically to the ring
R=\mathds{Z}_{p}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{l}]
which has been defined in Section
2.1
, and the latter completed group ring is isomorphic non-canonically to the Iwasawa algebra
{\Lambda_{l}=\mathds{Z}_{p}[[T_{1},\ldots,T_{l}]]}
, as has been explained in the previous subsection.

Definition 2.3
.

We let
X_{\infty}
be a
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
-voltage cover of
X
, with intermediate graphs
X_{m}
. The projective limit

J_{\infty}(X_{\infty}):=J(X_{\infty})\otimes_{R}\Lambda_{l}

is a finitely generated
\Lambda_{l}
-module, i.e. it is an Iwasawa module in the language of Section
2.1
. Therefore it makes sense to speak of the (generalised) Iwasawa invariants of the graph
X_{\infty}
.

In this article, we always assume that all the graphs
X_{m}
are connected. We will provide a sufficient criterion for this property below (see Lemma
2.5
). Thus,
X_{m}/X
is a Galois cover with group
(\mathds{Z}/p^{m}\mathds{Z})^{l}
. Then as a
\mathds{Z}_{p}[\textup{Gal}(X_{m}/X)]
-module
\textup{Pr}(X_{m})
is generated by the elements

\displaystyle p_{v,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in\textbf{E}_{v}(X_{m})}(t(e)-v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in\textbf{E}_{v}(X),t(e)\neq v}(\alpha_{m}(e)t(e)-v)-\sum_{e\in\textbf{E}_{v}(X),t(e)=v}(\alpha_{m}(e)+\alpha_{m}(e)^{-1}-2)v,

where
\textbf{E}_{v}(Y)
denotes the set of edges with origin
v
in a graph
Y
, and where
t(e)
is the target of an edge
e
.

If
e
is a loop with trivial voltage assignment then this term vanishes in the above sum. The coefficient of
{v=(v,1)}
in
p_{v,0}
(over
\mathds{Z}_{p}
) is given by

\deg(v)v-2|\{e\mid t(e)=v,\alpha_{m}(e)=1\}|.

Recall that we just write
w
instead of
(w,1)
for a vertex of the base graph
X
.

Now suppose that
X
is a finite graph, and let
{n=|V(X)|}
.
Fixing an indexing of the vertices of
X
, say
{V(X)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}}
, we will also write
{p_{i,0}=p_{v_{i},0}}
for these elements. We define a
\mathds{Z}_{p}
-linear map

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\colon\textup{Div}(X_{\infty})\longrightarrow\textup{Pr}(X_{\infty}),\quad v_{i,0}\mapsto p_{i,0}.

It is easy to see from the definitions and the action of
\textup{Gal}(X_{\infty}/X)
on
\textup{Div}(X_{\infty})
that this map is unique and well-defined. It is called the
voltage
p
-Laplacian
, see also
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that a graph
X
contains a non-trivial cycle of length
n>1
if there exist a sequence of vertices
v_{0},v_{1},\dots v_{n-1},v_{n},v_{0}
and a sequence of edges
e_{0},\dots e_{n}
such that
e_{i}
is an edge from
v_{i}
to
v_{i+1}
for
0\leq i\leq n-1
and
e_{n}
is an edge from
v_{n}
to
v_{0}
. We assume furthermore that
e_{i+1}\neq{e_{i}}^{-1}
for
{0\leq i\leq n-1}
and
e_{n}\neq e_{0}^{-1}
.

For any such cycle
C
we write
\beta_{C}
for the group ring element
\prod_{i=0}^{n}\alpha(e_{i})
.

Lemma 2.5
.

X_{m}
is connected for all
m
if and only if we can find cycles
C_{1},\dots,C_{l}
such that
\{\beta_{C_{1}},\dots,\beta_{C_{l}}\}
is a set of topological generators of
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
.

Proof.

Recall that we write
V(X_{m})
for the set of vertices of
X_{m}
. We say that two vertices
(v,\gamma)
and
(v^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
of
X_{m}
are connected if there is a path connecting
(v,\gamma)
and
(v^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
. It is easy to check that this defines an equivalence relation on
V(X_{m})
.

Assume first that there are cycles
C_{1},\dots,C_{l}
satisfying the above condition. In what follows, we abbreviate
\beta_{C_{i}}
to
\beta_{i}
for each
{1\leq i\leq l}
.
Let
v\in V(X)
. We will first show that
(v,1)
is connected to
(v,\gamma)
for every
\gamma\in(\mathds{Z}/p^{m}\mathds{Z})^{l}\setminus\{1\}
. As
{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{l}}
are topological generators of
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
it suffices to consider
{\gamma=\beta_{i}^{a_{i}}}
for
1\leq i\leq l
and
a_{i}\in\mathds{Z}/p^{m}\mathds{Z}
. Let
v_{0}
be a vertex lying on
C_{i}
. Then
(v_{0},\gamma^{\prime})
is connected to
(v_{0},\gamma^{\prime}\cdot\beta_{i}^{a_{i}})
for every choice of
\gamma^{\prime}
. As
X
is connected we can find a path
e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}
from
v
to
v_{0}
and we see that
(v,1)
is connected to
{(v_{0},\prod_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{m}(e_{j}))}
and
(v_{0},\tilde{\gamma})
is connected to
(v,\tilde{\gamma}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{m}(e^{-1}_{j}))
for every choice of
\tilde{\gamma}
. We obtain the following relations

\left(v,0\right)\sim\left(v_{0},\prod_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{m}(e_{j})\right)\sim\left(v_{0},\prod_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{m}(e_{j})\beta^{a_{i}}_{i}\right)\sim\left(v,\beta_{i}^{a_{i}}\right)

proving our first claim.

For every
v^{\prime}\in V(X)
we can find an element
\gamma\in(\mathds{Z}/p^{m}\mathds{Z})^{l}
such that
(v,1)
is connected to
(v^{\prime},\gamma)
– using that
X
is connected. Using the first part of the proof we see that
(v,1)\sim(v^{\prime},\gamma)\sim(v^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
for every choice of
\gamma^{\prime}
. Therefore
X_{m}
is indeed connected.

Assume now that all the
X_{m}
are connected. In particular
X_{1}
is connected. Let
v\in V(X)
and
\gamma\in(\mathds{Z}/p\mathds{Z})^{l}\setminus\{1\}
be arbitrary. Then
(v,1)
is connected to
(v,\gamma)
. Thus, there is a path
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
from
v
to
v
such that
\gamma=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{1}(e_{j})
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
e_{i}\neq e^{-1}_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
. Let
v_{i}
be the end point of
e_{i}
and set
v=v_{0}
. Let
k_{v}
be the minimal index such that
e_{k_{v}}\neq e^{-1}_{k-k_{v}+1}
. Such an index exists, as
{\gamma\neq 1}
. Then
e_{k_{v}},\dots,e_{k-k_{v}+1}
is a path from
v_{k_{v}-1}
to itself and it is indeed a cycle. Furthermore,
{\gamma=\prod_{j=k_{v}}^{k-k_{v}+1}\alpha_{1}(e_{j})}
.
∎

3.
The module theoretic perspective in the multidimensional case

In this section we generalise the module-theoretic point of view from
[
6
]
to the case of arbitrary
l
: we will explain the structure of the Iwasawa module
\varprojlim_{m}J(X_{m})
and its relation to the asymptotic growth of

.

Let
R=\mathds{Z}_{p}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{l}]
and
\Lambda=\mathds{Z}_{p}[[T_{1},\ldots,T_{l}]]
be as before, and let
I_{m}
and
{R_{m}=R/I_{m}}
be defined as in Section
2.1
. We write
\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
,
\textup{Div}^{0}_{\Lambda}
and
\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}
for
\textup{Div}(X_{\infty})\otimes\Lambda
,
\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})\otimes\Lambda
and
\textup{Pr}(X_{\infty})\otimes\Lambda
. It is easy to see that
\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
is
\Lambda
-free of rank
n
. Let
V
be the submodule of
\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
generated by
p_{i,0}
, i.e.
V
is the image of
\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}
in
\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
. As
\textup{Div}(X_{m})
is generated by
v_{i,0}
as an
R
-module, there is clearly a surjective map

\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}/I_{m}\to\textup{Div}(X_{m}).

If there are elements
F_{i}\in\Lambda
such that
\sum F_{i}\cdot p_{i,0}=0
in
\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
, then

np^{ml}-1=\mathds{Z}_{p}\textup{-rank}(\textup{Pr}(X_{m}))\leq(n-1)p^{ml}+O(p^{m(l-1)})

yielding a contradiction. Thus,
V
is
\Lambda
-free as well and
\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}\to\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
is injective.
Let

be the submodule of
\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
generated by the elements
p_{i,0}
, the elements
v_{i,0}-v_{j,0}
for
{1\leq j<i\leq n}
and
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{l})\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}

Theorem 3.1
.

We have
J(X_{m})\cong M_{\Lambda}/((\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}+\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda})
.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{m}\colon\textup{Div}(X_{\infty})\longrightarrow\textup{Div}(X_{m})
be the canonical map of voltage covers. Note that we can see this as an
R
-module homomorphism. We start by the following series of observations:

(a)

\pi_{m}
is surjective and induces surjective maps
\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})\longrightarrow\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{m})
and
{\textup{Pr}(X_{\infty})\longrightarrow\textup{Pr}(X_{m})}
.

(b)

The kernel of
\pi_{m}
is given by
(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}\cap\textup{Div}(X_{\infty})
.

(c)

We have
(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}+\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})=M_{\Lambda}
.

(d)

(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}+\textup{Pr}(X_{\infty})=\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}+(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
.

Let us first see that this series of observations suffices to prove the theorem.
By points a) and b) we have an isomorphism

\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{m})\cong\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})/((\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}\cap\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})),

where the right hand term is isomorphic to

(\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})+(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda})/(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}.

Combining this with c) and d), we have

\displaystyle J(X_{m})

\displaystyle=\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{m})/\textup{Pr}(X_{m})

\displaystyle=M_{\Lambda}/(\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}+(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda})

It remains to check the observations a)-d). In what follows we write
{|X|=n}
. For observation a) note that
v_{i,0}
,
{1\leq i\leq|X|}
, is a set of generators for
X_{m}
(seen as an
R
-module).
To see that it remains surjective when restricted to
\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})
, it suffices to note that the elements
{v_{j,0}-v_{i,0}}
and
{v_{j,\tau_{k}}-v_{j,0}}
for a set of topological generators
{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{l}}
of
\textup{Gal}(X_{\infty}/X)
generate
\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{m})
as well as
\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})
as
R
-modules. Using a similar argument for the generators
p_{i,0}
instead shows the corresponding claim for
\textup{Pr}(X_{\infty})
and
\textup{Pr}(X_{m})
.

For point b) note that
\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
is a free
\Lambda
-module of rank
|X|
with generators
v_{j,0}
for
1\leq j\leq|X|
. Furthermore,
\textup{Div}(X_{m})
is a free
R_{m}
-module with the same generators. As
\textup{Div}(X_{\infty})
contains the
R
-module generated by
v_{j,0}
, we see that
\textup{Div}(X_{\infty})
lies densely in
\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
. We obtain the following isomorphisms, where we write
{n=|X|}
.

\displaystyle\textup{Div}(X_{m})

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}R_{m}v_{i,0}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda/(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\Lambda v_{i,0}

\displaystyle\cong\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}/(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))

\displaystyle\cong\textup{Div}(X_{\infty})/(\textup{Div}(X_{\infty})\cap(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}),

which proves claim b).

Now we prove c). By construction
\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})\subseteq M_{\Lambda}
, and as our chosen generators of
M_{\Lambda}
lie in
\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})
, we see that
\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})
lies dense in
M_{\Lambda}
. Note that

(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}

is a neighbourhood of
0
in
M_{\Lambda}
. Let

be an arbitrary element, then there exists
z\in\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})\cap(y+(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda})
.
Thus,

\displaystyle y+(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle\subseteq z+(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle\subseteq\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})+(\omega_{m}(T_{1}),\ldots,\omega_{m}(T_{l}))\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}.

Varying
y
in
M_{\Lambda}
gives the claim.

Claim d) can be proved similarly to point c) by using the generators
p_{i,0}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

4.
Computation of the order of
J(X_{m})

Let
X
be a finite graph, and let
\alpha\colon S\to\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
be as in Section
2

Recall the definition of the
\mathds{Z}_{p}
-linear map
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
from Section
2

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
to a
\Lambda
-linear map.
Recall that
\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}\cong\Lambda^{n}
with basis
\{v_{1,0},\ldots,v_{n,0}\}

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
with respect to this basis. Let
D
be the degree matrix of the base graph

and define the voltage assignment matrix

\alpha_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\sum_{\{e(v_{i},v_{j})\in E(v_{i},v_{j})\}}\alpha(e(v_{i},v_{j}))\quad&\textup{if }i\neq j\\
\sum_{e(v_{i},v_{i})\in E(v_{i},v_{i})\}}\alpha(e(v_{i},v_{i}))+\alpha^{-1}(e(v_{i},v_{i}))\quad&\textup{if }i=j\end{cases}.

Then
\alpha_{i,j}\in\Lambda
for all
i,j
. It is easy to verify that
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
is represented by the matrix
\Delta_{\infty}=(D-A_{\alpha})^{t}

p_{v_{i},0}=-\sum_{e\in E_{v_{i}}(X_{\infty})}(\alpha(e)t(e)-v_{i})=\deg(v_{i})v_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{i,j}v_{j},

i

and not by the
i
-th column. Note that this ambiguity does not occur if we understand
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
only as a
\mathds{Z}_{p}
-linear map. In this case a

\textup{Div}(X_{m})

\{(v_{i},g)\mid v_{i}\in V(X),g\in(\mathds{Z}/p^{m}\mathds{Z})^{l}\}

In what follows, we fix some
{m\in\mathds{N}}
. Recall the definition of
{R_{m}=R/I_{m}}
from Section

to be the representing matrix for the map

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\colon\textup{Div}(X_{m})\longrightarrow\textup{Div}(X_{m})

(again in the

is the image of
\Delta_{\infty}
in
\textup{Mat}_{n,n}(R_{m})

Let
\Omega
be the group of all
p
-power roots of unity in some fixed algebraic closure
\overline{\mathds{Q}_{p}}
of
\mathds{Q}_{p}
. We say that two elements in

Using that
\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
is
\Lambda
-free of rank
n
, we can define

\phi_{m}\colon\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}/I_{m}\longrightarrow(\bigoplus\mathds{Z}_{p}[\zeta])^{n},

where each component map is defined as in
[

]
. Here the sum on the right hand side runs over all equivalence classes of
\Omega^{l}[p^{m}]
.
Let
\tilde{\Lambda}_{m}
be the image of
\phi_{m}
. Note that by
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]

\phi_{m}
has a finite cokernel. Recall that
\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\textup{Div}_{\Lambda})
and that
\Delta^{t}_{\infty}
is a matrix representing the map
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
.

It is immediate that
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(I_{m}\textup{Div}_{\Lambda})\subseteq I_{m}\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}

Note that
\tilde{\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}}
is a linear map of

-modules.

Lemma 4.1
.

We can choose a basis of

Let

\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}

\phi_{m}\circ\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(F)

\Delta^{t}_{\infty}(\zeta_{i}-1)F(\zeta_{i}-1)

We fix an index

at

.

v_{p}(|J(X_{m})|)=\sum_{\zeta\neq 1}v_{p}(\det(\Delta^{t}_{\infty}(\zeta-1)))+v_{p}(|J(X_{0})|).

\tilde{\Lambda}_{m}

in this basis. Then
A_{m}
and

\textup{Div}(X_{m})
is a free
\mathds{Z}_{p}

(\textup{Div}(X_{m})/\Delta^{t}_{m}\textup{Div}(X_{m}))[p^{\infty}]

B_{m}
and
B^{\prime}_{m}
be the matrices representing

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\colon(\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}/I_{m}\otimes\mathds{Q}_{p})/\ker(A_{m})\longrightarrow(\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}/I_{m}\otimes\mathds{Q}_{p})/\ker(A_{m})

and

\tilde{\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}}\colon\tilde{\Lambda}_{m}/\ker(A^{\prime}_{m})\longrightarrow\tilde{\Lambda}_{m}/\ker(A^{\prime}_{m})

in our chosen basis. Then
B_{m}
and
B^{\prime}_{m}
are conjugate and we obtain

|(\textup{Div}(X_{m})/\Delta^{t}_{m}\textup{Div}(X_{m}))[p^{\infty}]|=v_{p}(\det(B^{\prime}_{m}))=v_{p}(\det(B_{m})).

We let
\zeta_{k}=1
. Then
B_{m}
has the form

\begin{pmatrix}\Delta^{t}_{\infty}(\zeta_{1}-1)&0&\dots&\dots&\dots&0\\
0&\Delta^{t}_{\infty}(\zeta_{1}-1)&\dots&\dots&\dots&0\\
\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots\\
0&0&\dots&\Delta^{t}_{\infty}(\zeta_{2}-1)&\dots&0\\
\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots\\
0&\dots&\dots&\dots&0&B_{0}\end{pmatrix},

where
v_{p}(\det(B_{0}))=v_{p}(|J(X_{0})|)
.
Using the structure of the matrix
B_{m}
, the claim follows.
∎

In particular, this proves Greenberg’s conjecture, i.e. we obtain the following

Theorem 4.3
.

Greenberg’s conjecture holds true for the growth of
v_{p}(|J(X_{m})|)
, i.e. there exists a polynomial
{Q(X,Y)\in\mathds{Z}_{p}[X,Y]}
of total degree
l
and degree at most one in the second variable such that

v_{p}(|J(X_{m})|)=Q(p^{m},m)

for all sufficiently large
{m\in\mathds{N}}
.

Remark 4.4
.

Our method gives the same polynomial as the method using Ihara L-functions from
[
4
, Theorem 6.1]
.

5.
An Iwasawa main conjecture

Let as before
X
be a finite connected multigraph. Let
X_{\infty}
be a
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
-voltage cover of
X
. For every finite graph we define the zeta-function and the Ihara
L
-functions as in
[
4
]
.
Let
\Delta_{\infty}
be defined as in Section
4
. Let
\psi
be a character of
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
with finite image and let
A_{\psi}
be the matrix defined in
[
4
]
. From the definitions in
[
4
]
we see that

P_{\psi}(u):=\det(I-A_{\psi}u+(D-I)u^{2})=\frac{(1-u^{2})^{\chi(X)}}{L_{X}(u,\psi)}.

In this section we assume that no vertex has valency
1
. If
\psi
is not the trivial character then

. Using the definition of

, it follows that

If we interpret the function

as the algebraic part of the Ihara

-function, then

can be seen as a

-adic

-series interpolating special values of the algebraic part of

.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X_{m}
be the level
m
subcover of
X_{\infty}/X
. Then we have

J_{\infty}:=J(X_{\infty})\otimes\Lambda=\varprojlim_{m}J(X_{m})\cong\varprojlim_{m}N/(I_{m}L)\cong N.

Proof.

By definition
J(X_{\infty})=\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})/\textup{Pr}(X_{\infty})
as
R
-modules. Consider the exact sequence

(3)

0\longrightarrow\textup{Pr}(X_{\infty})\longrightarrow\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})\longrightarrow J(X_{\infty})\longrightarrow 0.

Recall that
\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})+I_{m}\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}=M_{\Lambda}
for all

. It follows that the image of
\textup{Div}^{0}_{\Lambda}
in
\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
is equal to
M_{\Lambda}
. Note that
\textup{Div}(X_{\infty})/\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty})
is annihilated by

. But
\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{m})[J_{0}]=(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\nu_{m,i}(T_{i}))\textup{Div}(X_{m})\cap\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{m})
.
It follows that
\textup{Div}^{0}_{\Lambda}
does not contain non-trivial
J_{0}
-torsion. In particular, the image of
\textup{Tor}_{1}^{R}(\textup{Div}(X_{\infty})/\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{\infty}))
inside
\textup{Div}^{0}_{\Lambda}
is trivial and we obtain a natural injection

\textup{Div}^{0}_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}.

Thus,
\textup{Div}^{0}_{\Lambda}\cong M_{\Lambda}
.

Upon tensoring (
3
) with
\Lambda
we obtain an exact sequence

\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow M_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow J_{\infty}\longrightarrow 0.

Recall that
\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\textup{Div}_{\Lambda})\subset M_{\Lambda}
by definition. In particular, the natural map
\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow M_{\Lambda}
is injective and we obtain an isomorphism

(4)

M_{\Lambda}/\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}=J_{\infty}.

Taking projective limits of the exact sequences

0\longrightarrow\textup{Pr}(X_{m})\longrightarrow\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{m})\longrightarrow J(X_{m})\longrightarrow 0

gives us a sequence

0\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{m}\textup{Pr}(X_{m})\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{m}\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{m})\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{m}J(X_{m})

By Theorem
3.1

\varprojlim_{m}J(X_{m})\cong N
. Note that
\textup{Pr}(X_{m})
is generated by
p_{i,0}
and that these generators do not have a relation over
\Lambda
. It follows that
\varprojlim_{m}\textup{Pr}(X_{m})\cong\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}
. The module
\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{m})
is generated by
J_{0}\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{m})
,

for

and the elements

. It follows that
\varprojlim_{m}\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{m})
is generated by the same elements over

and we obtain that
\varprojlim_{m}\textup{Div}^{0}(X_{m})\cong M_{\Lambda}
. Summarising, we obtain an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow M_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{m}J(X_{m})\longrightarrow 0

Together with (

) the claim follows.
∎

Fixing this notation, we can prove an

for graphs:

Theorem 5.2
.

Let

. Then

\textup{Char}(J_{\infty})=(\det(\Delta_{\infty})).

Proof.

Recall that
\Delta^{t}_{\infty}
is the matrix representing the
\Lambda
-linear map

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\colon\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow\textup{Div}_{\Lambda},

such that
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(\textup{Div}_{\Lambda})=\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}

\textup{Div}(X_{\infty})/\textup{Pr}(X_{\infty})

{\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}=\textup{Pr}(X_{\infty})\otimes\Lambda}
is a free

-module. Consider the natural exact sequence

\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow(\textup{Div}(X_{\infty})/\textup{Pr}(X_{\infty}))\otimes\Lambda\longrightarrow 0.

We would like to see that this sequence is still left exact. Note that

is still
\Lambda

As

There is a natural injection

whose cokernel has

.

Note that this proof crucially depends on the fact that

6.
Fukuda’s theorem and local boundedness results

We start from the
\Lambda
-module isomorphisms

J(X_{m})\cong N/L_{m}

from Theorem
3.1
. Let
\mathfrak{m}=(p,T_{1},\ldots,T_{l})
be the maximal ideal of the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda
. For any ideal
{I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}}
of
\Lambda
and any finitely generated torsion
\Lambda
-module
A
, we define

\textup{rank}_{I}(A)=v_{p}(|A/(I\cdot A)|),

whenever this is finite.

The following key result is proven as
[
10
, Theorem 2.5]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose that there exists an ideal
I\subseteq\Lambda
such that

\textup{rank}_{I}(J(X_{m}))=\textup{rank}_{I}(J(X_{m+1}))

for some
{m\in\mathds{N}}
. Then

\textup{rank}_{I}(J(X_{k}))=\textup{rank}_{I}(J(X_{m}))

for every
{k\geq m}
, and in fact
\textup{rank}_{I}(N)
is finite and equal to
\textup{rank}_{I}(J(X_{m}))
.

Proof.

Since
N/(I\cdot N+L_{m})
and
N/(I\cdot N+L_{m+1})
have the same cardinality, we may conclude that

I\cdot N+L_{m}=I\cdot N+L_{m+1}.

Letting
Z:=(I\cdot N+L_{m})/(I\cdot N)
and recalling the definition of
L_{m}
, it follows that

Z\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\cdot Z.

Since
Z
is a compact
\Lambda
-module, Nakayama’s Lemma implies that
{Z=0}
, i.e.
L_{m}
is contained in
I\cdot N
. The assertion of the theorem follows immediately.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
N=M_{\Lambda}/\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}
. Then
N\subseteq L=\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}/\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}
. The two
\Lambda
-modules
N
and
L
do not contain any non-zero pseudo-null submodules.

Proof.

Note that
\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
and
\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}
are both free
\Lambda
-modules of rank
n
. Suppose that
x\in\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
is an element such that
x+\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}
generates a pseudo-null submodule in
L
. Choose two coprime elements
a
and
c
such that
ax,cx\in\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}
. Fix a basis
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
of
\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}
and choose coefficients

ax=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i},\quad cx=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}b_{i}.

Note that the coefficients
a_{i}
and
c_{i}
are unique. It follows that

ca_{i}=ac_{i}\textup{ for }1\leq i\leq n.

As
(c,a)=1
, it follows that
a\mid a_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Therefore we can write

ax=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a\cdot\frac{a_{i}}{a}\cdot b_{i}=a\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{i}}{a}\cdot b_{i},

and thus

a\cdot\left(x-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{i}}{a}\cdot b_{i}\right)=0.

Since
\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
is a free
\Lambda
-module, this is possible only if already

x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{i}}{a}\cdot b_{i}

is contained in
\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}
. Therefore
L
(and
N
) do not contain any non-trivial pseudo-null submodule.
∎

The property proved above has many important consequences. For example, since
L
does not contain any non-trivial pseudo-null submodule, we obtain, starting from a pseudo-isomorphism, an
injection

{\psi\colon L\longrightarrow E}
into some elementary
\Lambda
-module
E
such that the cokernel of
\psi
is pseudo-null.

Now we introduce a topology on the set of
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
-covers of our fixed finite multigraph

. The voltage graphs
X_{\infty}
and
\tilde{X}_{\infty}
assigned to two voltage covers

coincide for a large integer

\mathcal{E}^{l}(X)
denote the set of voltage
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
-covers of

. For any voltage cover
{X_{\infty}\in\mathcal{E}^{l}(X)}
and each
{m\in\mathds{N}}

the set of voltage covers
\tilde{X}_{\infty}
which satisfy the above condition (i.e.
{\alpha_{m}=\tilde{\alpha}_{m}}
). It follows from our definition of the voltage cover graphs
X_{m}
(see Definition
2.2
) that

\tilde{X}_{n}=X_{n}

for each
{n\leq m}
if
{\tilde{X}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{E}(K_{\infty},m)}
.

The sets

and

, form the basis of a topology on

. This topology is motivated by what we call

on the set
\mathcal{E}^{l}(K)
of
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
-extensions of a number field

(the latter topology has been introduced in this classical setting in

]
).

Lemma 6.3
.

The set
\mathcal{E}^{l}(X)
is compact with respect to Greenberg’s topology.

Proof.

Fix a finite graph
X
and a voltage cover

\alpha\colon S\longrightarrow\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}.

For every
{m\in\mathds{N}}
, the set
\mathcal{E}^{l,m}(S)
of maps

\alpha_{m}\colon S\longrightarrow(\mathds{Z}/p^{m}\mathds{Z})^{l}

is finite. Therefore
\mathcal{E}^{l,m}(S)
is compact (with regard to the discrete topology) for each

. Since
{\mathcal{E}^{l}(X)=\varprojlim_{m}\mathcal{E}^{l,m}(S)}

\mathcal{E}^{l}(X)
is also compact.
∎

In the following, we want to compare the (generalised) Iwasawa invariants of voltage covers
{X_{\infty},\tilde{X}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{E}^{l}(X)}
which are close with respect to Greenberg’s topology. We start with the case
{l\geq 2}
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Fix an element
{X_{\infty}\in\mathcal{E}^{l}(X)}
,
{l\geq 2}
. Then there exist integers
{i,k\in\mathds{N}}
such that with
{U:=\mathcal{E}(X_{\infty},i)}
the following two statements hold for each
{\tilde{X}_{\infty}}
in

.

(a)

m_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\infty})\leq m_{0}(X_{\infty})
, and

(b)

l_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\infty})\leq k

{m_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\infty})=m_{0}(X_{\infty})}
.

Proof.

Recall that we want to study the generalised Iwasawa invariants of the
\Lambda
-module
{N=M_{\Lambda}/\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}}
(see also Corollary
3.3
). Let
{f_{N}\in\Lambda}
be the characteristic power series of
{N}
. Recall that
{\Lambda=\mathds{Z}_{p}[[T_{1},\ldots,T_{l}]]}
. We say that
f_{N}
is

T_{l}
if
f_{N}
is associated with a product
{p^{m_{0}(N)}\cdot g_{N}}
for some element
g_{N}
of the form

(5)

\displaystyle g_{N}=T_{l}^{k}+h_{k-1}T_{l}^{k-1}+\ldots+h_{1}\cdot T_{l}+h_{0},

where
{k\geq 1}
and
{h_{0},\ldots,h_{k-1}\in(p,T_{1},\ldots,T_{l-1})\subseteq\mathds{Z}_{p}[[T_{1},\ldots,T_{l-1}]]}
. It follows from
[
1
, Lemma 1]
that given any set
{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{l}}
of topological generators of

\textup{Gal}(X_{\infty}/K)\cong\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l},

we can change this set of generators to a set of generators
{\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\gamma_{l-1}^{\prime},\gamma_{l}}
(i.e. without changing the last element) such that with respect to these new generators,

is in Weierstraß normal form with respect to the last variable
T_{l}
.

Now let
E_{N}
be an elementary
\Lambda
-module attached to
N
, and fix a pseudo-isomorphism
{\varphi:E_{N}\longrightarrow N}
. Then the cokernel
{B:=N/\varphi(E_{N})}
is a pseudo-null
\Lambda
-module, and it follows from
[
11

that the topological generators
{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{l}}
of
{\textup{Gal}(X_{\infty}/X)\cong\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}}
can be chosen such that

•

remains unchanged,

is still in Weierstraß normal form with respect to
T_{l}
, and the degree
k
in the representation (
5

does not change, and

•

B/(T_{1},\ldots,T_{l-2},T_{l-1}-p^{x})

.

Fix such an integer

. It follows from
[
11
, Lemma 4.5]
that

\textup{rank}_{n}(N):=v_{p}(|N/(T_{1},\ldots,T_{l-2},T_{l-1}-p^{x},\nu_{2n,n}(T_{l})|)

{n\in\mathds{N}}
. Moreover, it follows from the short exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{N}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

that

\textup{rank}_{n}(N)\leq\textup{rank}_{n}(E_{N})+\textup{rank}_{n}(B)

(in particular, all the terms in this inequality are finite; cf. also the proof of

, Theorem 3.2]
). Note that

\textup{rank}_{n}(B)=v_{p}(|B/(T_{1},\ldots,T_{l-2},T_{l-1}-p^{x})|)=:C

(it will suffice if

\textup{rank}_{n}(B)
is bounded in

.

From now on we will consider

large enough to ensure that

(a)

\textup{rank}_{n}(N)

(b)

, and

Then it follows from the proof of

(cf. in particular equation (4.4)) that

\textup{rank}_{n}(E_{N})=m_{0}(X_{\infty})\cdot(p^{2n}-p^{n})+n\cdot k.

Since
{J(X_{m})\cong N/L_{m}}
and therefore
{\textup{rank}_{n}(J(X_{m}))\leq\textup{rank}_{n}(N)}
for each
{m\in\mathds{N}}
, it follows that we can choose

sufficiently large such that

\textup{rank}_{n}(J(X_{m}))=\textup{rank}_{n}(J(X_{k}))=\textup{rank}_{n}(N)

for each

(cf. also the proof of Theorem

, and consider
{U:=\mathcal{E}^{l}(X_{\infty},m+1)}

\textup{rank}_{n}(E_{\tilde{N}})\leq\textup{rank}_{n}(\tilde{N}),

\textup{rank}_{n}(E_{\tilde{N}})\leq m_{0}(X_{\infty})\cdot(p^{2n}-p^{n})+n\cdot k+C,

{m_{0}(\tilde{X}_{\infty})=m_{0}(X_{\infty})}

In the case
{l=1}
of
\mathds{Z}_{p}
-covers of

Theorem 6.5

Assume that

{X_{\infty}\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(X)}
. Then there exists an integer
{m\in\mathds{N}}
such that the following statements hold:

(a)

For each
{\tilde{X}_{\infty}\in U:=\mathcal{E}^{1}(X_{\infty},m)}

\mu(\tilde{X}_{\infty})\leq\mu(X_{\infty}).

(b)

{\lambda(\tilde{X}_{\infty})=\lambda(X_{\infty})}
for each
{\tilde{X}_{\infty}\in U}
which satisfies
{\mu(\tilde{X}_{\infty})=\mu(X_{\infty})}

Proof.

For statement (a), we could use the previous theorem, since

\mu(N)=\mu(\varprojlim_{m}J(X_{m}))

holds also in the case
{l=1}
(see also Corollary
3.3
). However, it is not hard to give an argument which will prove both (a) and (b).

Fix a pseudo-isomorphism
{\varphi:N\longrightarrow E_{N}}
, where

is an elementary
\Lambda
-module. Then
{N^{\circ}=\ker(\varphi)}
is trivial, i.e.
\varphi
is an injection. Indeed,

is actually equal to the maximal finite
\Lambda
-submodule of

; therefore the claim follows from Lemma

. We stress that when compared to the proof of Theorem

, we have interchanged the roles of the set of definition and the image set in the definition of
\varphi
.

Moreover, the quotient
N/\nu_{2n,n}(T)
is finite for each sufficiently large

since
N
is a torsion
\Lambda
-module and the polynomials
\nu_{n+1,n}(T)
are pairwise coprime.

Letting

\textup{rank}_{n}(N):=v_{p}(|N/\nu_{2n,n}(T)|)

(and similarly for other torsion
\Lambda
-modules), we have

\displaystyle\textup{rank}_{n}(N)

\displaystyle=\textup{rank}_{n}(E_{N})+\textup{rank}_{n}(N^{\circ})

\displaystyle=\textup{rank}_{n}(E_{N})+v_{p}(|N^{\circ}|),

where the last equality holds for each sufficiently large
n
(see
[
10
, proof of Theorem 3.10]
for the first equation). Since
N^{\circ}
is trivial, we actually may conclude that

\textup{rank}_{n}(N)=\textup{rank}_{n}(E_{N})

for each
{n\in\mathds{N}}
.

Now let
n
be large enough such that

(a)

\textup{rank}_{n}(N)
is finite, and

(b)

p^{n}(p-1)>n\cdot\lambda(N)
.

As in the proof of Theorem
6.4
we can use the stabilisation property from Theorem
6.1
in order to define a neighbourhood
{U=\mathcal{E}^{1}(X_{\infty},m)}
of
X_{\infty}
such that

\textup{rank}_{n}(\tilde{X}_{\infty})=\textup{rank}_{n}(X_{\infty})

for each
{\tilde{X}_{\infty}\in U}
. By the above, since the maximal pseudo-null submodule of
\tilde{N}
is also trivial, we have

\displaystyle\textup{rank}_{n}(E_{\tilde{N}})

\displaystyle=\textup{rank}_{n}(E_{N})

(6)

\displaystyle=\mu(N)\cdot(p^{2n}-p^{n})+\lambda\cdot n

for each
{\tilde{X}_{\infty}\in U}
, where
E_{\tilde{N}}
is an elementary
\Lambda
-module attached to
\tilde{N}
, and where the last equation holds in view of the property (b) above (see the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 3.10]
).

Since
{\textup{rank}_{n}(E_{\tilde{N}})\geq\mu(\tilde{N})\cdot(p^{2n}-p^{n})}
, the choice of
n
implies that
{\mu(\tilde{N})\leq\mu(N)}
. This proves (a).

Now suppose that
{\mu(\tilde{N})=\mu(N)}
. If
{\lambda(\tilde{N})\geq p^{n}(p-1)}
, then

\textup{rank}_{n}(E_{\tilde{N}})\geq\mu(\tilde{N})\cdot(p^{2n}-p^{n})+p^{n}(p-1)

by the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 3.10]
. Since this is not possible in view of our choice of
n
, it follows that
{\lambda(\tilde{N})<p^{n}(p-1)}
. Then the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 3.10]
implies that

\textup{rank}_{n}(E_{\tilde{N}})=\mu(\tilde{N})\cdot(p^{2n}-p^{n})+n\cdot\lambda(\tilde{N}).

Since
{\textup{rank}_{n}(E_{\tilde{N}})=\textup{rank}_{n}(E_{N})}
by the above, we may conclude that
{\lambda(\tilde{N})=\lambda(N)}
.
∎

Corollary 6.6
.

The
m_{0}
-invariant is bounded globally on
\mathcal{E}^{l}(X)
, i.e. there exists a constant

such that

for each
{X_{\infty}\in\mathcal{E}^{l}(X)}
.

Moreover, if the
m_{0}
-invariant is constant on
\mathcal{E}^{l}(X)
, then the
l_{0}
-invariant is bounded globally on
\mathcal{E}^{l}(X)
.

Proof.

This follows by combining Theorem
6.4
with Lemma
6.3
.
∎

Again, we prove a more precise statement in the one-dimensional case.

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
{l=1}
, and let
{X_{\infty}\in\mathcal{E}^{l}(X)}
. We fix a neighbourhood
U
of
X_{\infty}
as in Theorem

. Then there exists a potentially smaller neighbourhood
{U^{\prime}\subseteq U}
of
X_{\infty}
with the following property:

Either
{\mu(\tilde{X}_{\infty})=\mu(X_{\infty})}
for each
{\tilde{X}_{\infty}\in U^{\prime}}
, or
\lambda
is unbounded on
U^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We will use the notation from the proof of Theorem
6.5
. Suppose that

has been chosen such that
{p^{n}(p-1)>n\cdot\lambda(N)}
, and that
{\mu(\tilde{N})<\mu(N)}
. For any
{\tilde{X}_{\infty}\in U}
with
\mu(\tilde{N})<\mu(N)
, we have that either
{\lambda(\tilde{N})\geq p^{n}(p-1)}
, or the equation

implies that

\displaystyle\lambda(\tilde{N})

\displaystyle\geq\lambda(N)+\Big\lfloor\frac{p^{2n}-p^{n}}{n}\Big\rfloor\cdot(\mu(N)-\mu(\tilde{N}))

Now choose an integer

Letting

-invariant is unbounded on a neighbourhood of

.
∎

7.
A weak control theorem

Before we can prove a control theorem we need to introduce one further group associated to our voltage graphs: We write
{\textup{Pic}(X_{m})=\textup{Div}(X_{m})/\textup{Pr}(X_{m})}
. Note that this is an infinite abelian group of
\mathds{Z}_{p}
-rank
1
. We define

\textup{Pic}_{\infty}=\textup{Pic}(X_{\infty})\otimes\Lambda.

Analogously to Lemma
5.1
one can show that
{\textup{Pic}_{\infty}\cong\varprojlim_{m}\textup{Pic}(X_{m})}
. In particular, we have a exact sequence of
\Lambda
-modules

0\longrightarrow\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}\longrightarrow\textup{Pic}_{\infty}\longrightarrow 0.

Recall that we have seen in the proof of Lemma
5.1
that the natural map
{\textup{Div}^{0}_{\Lambda}\to\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}}
is injective. Thus, we obtain a natural injection

J_{\infty}\longrightarrow\textup{Pic}_{\infty}.

Lemma 7.1
.

The natural maps

r_{m}\colon\textup{Pic}_{\infty}/I_{m}\textup{Pic}_{\infty}\longrightarrow\textup{Pic}(X_{m})

are isomorphisms.

Proof.

Consider the tautological commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Pr}_{\Lambda}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Pic}_{\infty}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Pr}(X_{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Div}(X_{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Pic}(X_{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{m}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{m}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r_{m}}

It follows from the proof of Theorem
3.1
that
h_{m}
and
g_{m}
are surjective. Hence,
r_{m}
is surjective. Applying the snake lemma we obtain a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\ker(h_{m})\longrightarrow\ker(g_{m})\longrightarrow\ker(r_{m})\longrightarrow 0.

Recall that
\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
is
\Lambda
-free in the generators

. Likewise
\textup{Div}(X_{m})
is
\Lambda/I_{m}
-free in the same generators. It follows that
\ker(g_{m})=I_{m}\textup{Div}_{\Lambda}
and the above sequence becomes

Thus,

is the image of

in

, and therefore
\ker(r_{m})\cong I_{m}\textup{Pic}_{\infty}
.
∎

Recall from Section
3
that

J_{\infty}:=J(X_{\infty})\otimes\Lambda=\varprojlim_{m}J(X_{m}),

and that
{I_{m}\subseteq\Lambda}
is an ideal for each
{m\in\mathds{N}}
.

Lemma 7.2
.

The natural maps

Proof.

Consider the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda/J_{0}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r^{\prime}_{m}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r_{m}}

is surjective and

Let

be a generator of

is generated by

∎

We are not only interested in the projection to finite level, but also to the projection to

-subcovers. Let

be a

.

The natural map

8.
Examples

In this section we want to compute the (generalised) Iwasawa invariants of certain concrete examples. The starting point is the main conjecture (see Theorem
5.2
). In view of this result, we can compute the characteristic power series of a
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
-cover (if
{l=1}
, then we have to keep in mind the Remark
5.3
).

In this section, we consider a base graph of the following form:

\textstyle{x_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{x_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{x_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{x_{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{x_{5}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\ldots}
\textstyle{x_{4}}

In other words, we let
{X=X_{0}}
be a graph with
n
edges
{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}}
, which are connected in a cycle, with one multiple edge, say, between
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
. Let

\alpha\colon S\longrightarrow\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}

be a voltage assignment. We write the Galois group multiplicatively.
We have to study the
n\times n
-matrix

\Delta_{\infty}=D-A_{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}a&x&0&0&0&-1\\
y&a&-1&0&0&0\\
0&-1&2&-1&0&0\\
0&0&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&0&0&-1&2&-1\\
0&0&0&0&-1&2\end{pmatrix}.

Here we denote by 1 the trivial element of the group
\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}
, and
x,y\in\mathds{Z}_{p}[\mathds{Z}_{p}^{l}]
are the group ring elements corresponding to the voltage assignment of the multi-edge. More precisely, if
{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}}
denote the edges between
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
, then

x=-\alpha(e_{1})-\ldots-\alpha(e_{k}),\quad y=-\alpha(e_{1})^{-1}-\ldots-\alpha(e_{k})^{-1},

and
{a=k+1}
.

We want to compute the determinant of
\Delta_{\infty}
, which is almost tridiagonal. To this purpose, we use an idea from the proof of
[
20
, Theorem 1]
: let
\rho
be the permutation matrix which has entries

\rho_{i,j}=\begin{cases}1&i=n,j=1,\\
1&j=i+1,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then the determinant of
\rho
is equal to
(-1)^{n-1}
, and the matrix product
{\Delta_{\infty}\cdot\rho}
is of the form

\Delta_{\infty}\cdot\rho=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc|cccccc}-1&a&x&0&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&0\\
0&y&a&-1&0&\ldots&\ldots&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&-1&2&-1&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&0&-1&2&-1&&\vdots\\
\vdots&&\vdots&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\vdots&&&&-1&2&-1\\
-1&0&\vdots&&&&-1&2\\
2&-1&0&\cdots&&\cdots&0&-1\end{array}\right),

which can be viewed as a block matrix
\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}
.

Now we apply
Schur’s determinant formula
(see
[
17
, Theorem 4.1]
):

\det\left(\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\right)=\det(D)\cdot\det(A-B\cdot D^{-1}\cdot C).

It is easy to see that

D^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-2&-3&-4&\cdots&-n\\
0&-1&\cdots&&&-(n-1)\\
\vdots&&&&&-(n-2)\\
&&&&&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&&&&-1\end{pmatrix}=(m_{ij}),

where

m_{ij}=\begin{cases}0&i>j,\\
-j+i-1&i\leq j.\end{cases}

It follows from a straight-forward computation that

A-BD^{-1}C=\begin{pmatrix}-1+(n+1)x&a-nx\\
-n+(n+1)a&y-1-(a-1)n\end{pmatrix}.

Computing the determinants, we may conclude that

\det(\Delta_{\infty})=-[((n+1)y-1)\cdot x-y+(-a^{2}+2a-1)\cdot n+(1-a^{2})].

Here we have used that
\det(\rho)\cdot\det(D)=(-1)^{n-1}\cdot(-1)^{n-2}=-1
.

In our examples, we first concentrate on the case
{l=1}
.

Example 8.1
.

The easiest case is
a=2
(i.e., no multiple edge). In this case
{y=x^{-1}}
, and the above formula simplifies to

\det(\Delta_{\infty})=2+x+x^{-1}.

In particular, the determinant does not depend on
n
in this special case. Letting
{\tau=T+1}
be a topological generator of the Galois group of
X_{\infty}
over
X
, we have
{x=-\tau=-(T+1)}
and we obtain the characteristic power series

(T+1)^{-1}\cdot(2(T+1)-(T+1)^{2}-1)=(T+1)^{-1}\cdot(-T^{2}).

Note that
T+1
is a unit in
\Lambda
. It follows from Remark
5.3
that the characteristic power series of
J_{\infty}
is equal to
T
.
Thus, in this case we obtain
{\lambda(J_{\infty})=1}
independently of
{n=|X_{0}|}
.

Example 8.2
.

In our second example we consider the case
{a=3}
, i.e. we have two edges between
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
. If the voltage assignments of these two edges are
\tau
and
1
, respectively, then a similar computation yields that the characteristic power series is associated with

(n+2)\cdot T^{2}.

Therefore
{\lambda(J_{\infty})=1}
for all primes
p
, and
{\mu(J_{\infty})=0}
except for the prime divisors of
{n+2}
. In particular, we see that this family of examples includes voltage covers with
arbitrarily large
\mu
-invariant
(for fixed prime

, let
{n=p^{r}-2}
for some large integer

).

If the voltage assignments are
\tau
and
\tau

is associated with

2T^{2},

i.e. it does not depend on the number
n
of vertices of
X_{0}
.

Finally, suppose that
\alpha(e_{1})=\tau
and
\alpha(e_{2})=\tau^{2}
. Then the characteristic power series is associated with

Therefore
{\mu(J_{\infty})=0}
for all primes

, and

\lambda(J_{\infty})=\begin{cases}3&p\mid(n+6);\\
1&p\nmid(n+6).\end{cases}

Example 8.3
.

Now suppose that

is arbitrary, and that each

is equal to

. Then we obtain that

i.e. the characteristic polynomial of

is associated with
{(a-1)T}
in this case; in particular this polynomial does not depend on the number of vertices of
X_{0}
.

Example 8.4
.

Now suppose that
a
is arbitrary and that there exists some integer

In particular, this determinant is independent of the number

Example 8.5
.

In our last example we consider a

-invariant

The main goal of this section is to provide an example of a

For any

.

Example 9.2
.

The following lemma will be the crucial ingredient in the proof of Theorem

Lemma 9.3

\mathds{Z}_{p}^{2}

{\mu(\tilde{Y})=0}

Proof.

Fix
{Y\in\mathcal{E}^{\subseteq X_{\infty}}(X)}
such that
{\mu(Y)=0}
. In what follows we will write
\Lambda_{1}
for any one-dimensional Iwasawa algebra,
{\Lambda=\mathds{Z}_{p}\llbracket S,T\rrbracket}
, and we do not abbreviate
{J_{\infty}(X_{\infty}):=J(X_{\infty})\otimes\Lambda}
to

for clarity.
Moreover, for any
{Y\in\mathcal{E}^{\subseteq X_{\infty}}(X)}

be the

, where
{\Lambda_{1}=\mathds{Z}_{p}\llbracket\textup{Gal}(Y/X)\rrbracket}
.

Suppose that the topological generators
\sigma
and
\tau
of
{\textup{Gal}(X_{\infty}/X)\cong\mathds{Z}_{p}^{2}}
have been chosen such that
{Y=X_{\infty}^{\langle\sigma\rangle}}
, and let
{S=\sigma+1}
. Then Lemma
7.4
implies that the kernel of the canonical map

t^{\prime}\colon J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})/S\longrightarrow J_{\infty}(Y)

has
p
-rank at most 1. Therefore the finitely generated
{\Lambda_{1}}
-module
J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})/S
is
{\Lambda_{1}}
-torsion and has
\mu
-invariant 0. This means that we can choose an annihilator
{f\in{\Lambda_{1}}}
of
J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})/S
which is not divisible by
p
.

Now consider the
\Lambda
-module
J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})
, and suppose that
{m_{0}(X_{\infty})>0}
. Since the Fitting ideal
\textup{Fitt}_{\Lambda}(J_{\infty}(X_{\infty}))
is contained in the annihilator ideal of
J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})
, this assumption would imply that

\textup{Fitt}_{\Lambda}(J_{\infty}(X_{\infty}))\subseteq(p).

But it follows from the general properties of Fitting ideals (see also
[
12
, Proposition 2.1]
) that

(i)

\pi(\textup{Fitt}_{\Lambda}(J_{\infty}(X_{\infty}))=\textup{Fitt}_{\Lambda_{1}}(J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})/S)
and

(ii)

\textup{Ann}_{\Lambda_{1}}(J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})/S)^{l}\subseteq\textup{Fitt}_{\Lambda_{1}}(J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})/S)
for all sufficiently large
{l\in\mathds{N}}
, where

\pi\colon\Lambda\longrightarrow{\Lambda_{1}}

denotes the surjection induced by mapping
S
to 0, and where

\textup{Ann}_{\Lambda_{1}}(J_{\infty}(X_{\infty}/S))

denotes the annihilator ideal.

Therefore each annihilator of
J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})/S
would be divisible by
p
, which is a contradiction to the existence of the annihilator
f
above.

We have shown that
{m_{0}(X_{\infty})=0}
. Now let
{\tilde{Y}\in\mathcal{E}^{\subseteq X_{\infty}}(X)}
be arbitrary, and choose topological generators
\tilde{\sigma}
and
\tau
of
{\textup{Gal}(X_{\infty}/X)}
such that
{\tilde{Y}=X_{\infty}^{\langle\tilde{\sigma}\rangle}}
. Since
{m_{0}(X_{\infty})=0}
, we can choose an annihilator
{F\in\Lambda}
which is not divisible by
p
. In fact,
F
can be chosen such that
F
is not contained in the ideal
(p,\tilde{S})
,
{\tilde{Y}=X_{\infty}^{\langle\tilde{S}+1\rangle}}
, for all but finitely many choices of
\tilde{Y}
. Indeed, the characteristic power series
F_{J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})}
of
J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})
is contained in finitely many of the ideals
(p,\tilde{S})
(note that each such ideal contributes to the
l_{0}
-invariant of
X_{\infty}
). Suppose now that
\tilde{Y}
has been chosen such that
F_{J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})}
is not contained in the corresponding ideal
(p,\tilde{S})
. Since
J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})
does not contain any non-trivial pseudo-null submodules by Lemma
6.2
, it follows from the general structure of
\Lambda
-modules that
F_{J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})}
annihilates
J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})
(see also
[
12
, Proposition 2.1(3)]
). Therefore we can choose
{F=F_{J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})}}
above; this proves our claim.

Again, a suitable power of
F
(the exponent of this power depends on the number of generators of
J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})
over
\Lambda
) is contained in the Fitting ideal
\textup{Fitt}_{\Lambda}(J_{\infty}(X_{\infty}))
, and

\tilde{\pi}(F)\in\textup{Fitt}_{\Lambda_{1}}(J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})/\tilde{S})\subseteq\textup{Ann}_{\Lambda_{1}}(J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})/\tilde{S}),

where now
\tilde{\pi}\colon\Lambda\longrightarrow{\Lambda_{1}}
is induced by mapping
\tilde{S}
to zero.

If
\tilde{\pi}(F)
is not divisible by
p
for our particular choice of
\tilde{S}
(by the above, this can be achieved for all but finitely many
{\tilde{Y}\in\mathcal{E}^{\subseteq X_{\infty}}(X)}
), then it follows that
{\mu(J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})/\tilde{S})=0}
, i.e.
J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})/\tilde{S}
is a finitely generated
\mathds{Z}_{p}
-module. By Lemma
7.4
, the canonical map

J_{\infty}(X_{\infty})/\tilde{S}\longrightarrow J_{\infty}(\tilde{Y})

is surjective. This shows that
J_{\infty}(\tilde{Y})
is also finitely generated over
\mathds{Z}_{p}
, and therefore
{\mu(J_{\infty}(\tilde{Y}))=0}
.
∎

.

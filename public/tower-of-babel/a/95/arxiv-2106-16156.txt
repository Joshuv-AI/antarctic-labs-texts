A semi-finite proof of Jacobi’s triple product identity Thanks: Keywords: Jacobi’s triple product identity; Euler’s q q -exponential function; semi-finite. MSC (2010): 33D15, 11F27.
Jun-Ming Zhu

Abstract
. Jacobi’s triple product identity is proved from one of Euler’s q q -exponential functions in an elementary way.



1.
Introduction

We suppose
|q|<1
and, as usual, the
q
-Pochhammer symbols are
defined respectively by

(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{n})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad(a;q)_{n}=\frac{(a;q)_{\infty}}{(aq^{n};q)_{\infty}}

for any integer
n
. Jacobi’s triple product identity is one of the
most important series-product identities. We state it in the
following theorem.

Theorem
1.1
.

For any complex number
z\neq 0
, we have

(1.1)

(q;q)_{\infty}(-q/z;q)_{\infty}(-z;q)_{\infty}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n(n-1)/2}z^{n}.

Bellman said in
[
3
, p. 42]
that there are no simple proofs
known of the complete result. After that, Andrews
[
1
]
proved
Theorem
1.1
using both of Euler’s
q
-exponential functions,
which are, respectively,

(1.2)

(-z;q)_{\infty}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n(n-1)/2}z^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}},~~z\in\mathbf{C},

and

\frac{1}{(-z;q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}z^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}},~~|z|<1.

In this short note, we will prove Jacobi’s triple product identity
only using (
1.2
). This seems also a proof of the complete
triple product identity required by Bellman. We use a method which
is often called semi-finite method (see
[
3
]
).

2.
The proof

Proof.

For a nonnegative integer
m
and
z\neq 0
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=-m}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n(n-1)/2}z^{n}}{(q^{m+1};q)_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{(n-m)(n-m-1)/2}z^{n-m}}{(q^{m+1};q)_{n-m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q^{m(m+1)/2}z^{-m}}{(q^{m+1};q)_{-m}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n(n-1)/2}{(zq^{-m})}^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m(m+1)/2}z^{-m}(q;q)_{m}(-zq^{-m};q)_{\infty}\qquad\mbox{(by \eqref{E1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m(m+1)/2}z^{-m}(q;q)_{m}(-zq^{-m};q)_{m}(-z;q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q;q)_{m}(-q/z;q)_{m}(-z;q)_{\infty}.

Note that
\frac{1}{(q^{m+1};q)_{-n}}=(q^{m+1-n};q)_{n}=0
when

n>m
.
The left hand side of (
2.1
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n(n-1)/2}z^{n}}{(q^{m+1};q)_{n}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(q^{m+1-n};q)_{n}q^{n(n+1)/2}z^{-n}.

Restricting
z
in any compact subset
of
0<|z|<\infty
and letting
m\rightarrow\infty
in (
2.1
),
we get (
1.1
). By analytic continuation, the restriction on

z
may be relaxed.

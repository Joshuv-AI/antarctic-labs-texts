2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on the method introduced in
[
2
]
, in which a Burgess type bound was obtained for
K=\chi
a multiplicative character of prime modulus
q
, by unexpectedly utilizing a
“trivial” delta method
. We demonstrate in this work once again that the trivial delta method is strong enough to reach a Weyl type bound when the moduli
q
factors in an appropriate way (see
[
26
,
3
]
for other interesting applications of the trivial delta symbol).

Let
(\lambda_{f}(n))_{n\geqslant 1}
be the Hecke eigenvalues of a
\rm GL_{2}
cusp form
f
. Let
q={q_{0}}{q_{1}}
. Let
K\bmod q
be a trace function.
From the definition (
1.10
) we have the twisted multiplicativity

(2.1)

\widehat{K}(b)=\widehat{K_{0}}(\overline{{q_{1}}}b)\widehat{K_{1}}(\overline{{q_{0}}}b).

Consider

(2.2)

S(X):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)K(n)V\left(\frac{n}{X}\right),

where
V
satisfies the bound (
1.11
).
Then

S(X)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)W\left(\frac{n}{X}\right)\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}K(r)V\left(\frac{r}{X}\right)\delta_{n=r}.

Here
W
is a smooth function supported in
(1/100,100)
and satisfying
W^{(j)}(x)\ll_{j}1
for
j\geqslant 0
and
W(x)=1
for
x\in[1,2]
, and
\delta_{n=r}
is the Kronecker delta symbol.

Following
[
2
]
, if we assume that
p
is a prime coprime with
q
and such that

(2.3)

p{q_{0}}>1000X

then for
|n-r|\leqslant 100X
, we have

\begin{split}\delta_{n=r}=&\frac{1}{p{q_{0}}}\sum_{u(p{q_{0}})}e\left(\frac{u(n-r)}{p{q_{0}}}\right)\\
=&\frac{1}{p{q_{0}}}\sum_{c|p{q_{0}}}\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\alpha(c)}e\left(\frac{\alpha(n-r)}{c}\right),\end{split}

and by summing over all
p\in{\rm P}:=\{p\in[P,2P[:p\,\text{prime},\ (p,q)=1\}
, we have

\delta_{n=r}=\frac{1}{|\rm P|}\sum_{p\in\rm P}\frac{1}{pq_{0}}\sum_{c|pq_{0}}\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\alpha(c)}e\left(\frac{\alpha(n-r)}{c}\right).

with
|{\rm P}|\gg P/\log P
from the prime number theorem.

Remark 2.1
.

Choosing the parameter
q_{0}
in place of
q
is one chief difference between this paper and
[
2
]
.

Applying the above expression of

\delta_{n=r}
to the sum
S(X)
, we get

S(X)=\frac{1}{|{\rm P}|q_{0}}\sum_{p\in\rm P}\frac{1}{p}\sum_{c|pq_{0}}\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\alpha(c)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)e\left(\frac{\alpha n}{c}\right)W\left(\frac{n}{X}\right)\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}K(r)e\left(\frac{-\alpha r}{c}\right)V\left(\frac{r}{X}\right).

Applying the Voronoi summation formula (
[
19
, Thm. A.4]
), we obtain

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)e\left(\frac{\alpha n}{c}\right)W\left(\frac{n}{X}\right)=\frac{X}{c}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\lambda_{f}(n)}e\left(\frac{\mp\bar{\alpha}n}{c}\right)\widehat{W}^{\pm}\left(\frac{n}{c^{2}/X}\right).

Here
\widehat{W}^{\pm}(\cdot)
are defined as in
[
2
, Lem. 2.2]
.
Applying the Poisson summation formula (modulo
[c,q]
), the
r
-sum

\sum_{r\geqslant 1}K(r)e\left(\frac{-\alpha r}{c}\right)V\left(\frac{r}{X}\right)

is transformed into

(2.4)

\frac{X}{[c,q]}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{\beta\bmod[c,q]}K(\beta)e\left(\frac{-\alpha\beta}{c}\right)e\left(\frac{r\beta}{[c,q]}\right)\right)\widehat{V}\left(\frac{rX}{[c,q]}\right),

where

\widehat{V}
denotes the Fourier transform of the function
V
.

Following
[
2
]
, we introduce the useful notation
a_{b}:=\frac{a}{(a,b)}
. Then, using the relation
c=\frac{qc_{q}}{q_{c}}
we can write

e\left(\frac{\bullet}{c}\right)=e\left(\frac{\bullet q_{c}}{qc_{q}}\right)=e\left(\frac{\bullet q_{c}\overline{c_{q}}}{q}\right)e\left(\frac{\bullet q_{c}\bar{q}}{c_{q}}\right).

Similarly, using the relation
[c,q]=qc_{q}
we have the reciprocity relation

e\left(\frac{r\beta}{[c,q]}\right)=e\left(\frac{r\beta\overline{c_{q}}}{q}\right)e\left(\frac{r\beta\bar{q}}{c_{q}}\right).

Then the
\beta
-sum in (
2.4
) can be rewritten as

\begin{split}&\sum_{\beta\bmod q}K(\beta)e\left(\frac{(r-\alpha q_{c})\overline{c_{q}}\beta}{q}\right)\times\sum_{\beta\bmod c_{q}}e\left(\frac{(r-\alpha q_{c})\overline{q}\beta}{c_{q}}\right)\\
&=q^{1/2}c_{q}\widehat{K}((r-\alpha q_{c})\overline{c_{q}})\times\delta_{r-\alpha q_{c}\equiv 0\bmod c_{q}},\end{split}

where
\widehat{K}
is the normalized Fourier transform (
1.10
) and
\overline{c_{q}}
is the multiplicative inverse of

modulo
q
. We also note that the weight function
\widehat{V}\left(\frac{rX}{[c,q]}\right)
restricts the effective range of the

-sum in (

) to

By this we mean that for any

, the contribution of the terms satisfying

is bounded by
O_{A,\varepsilon}(X^{-A})
for any

.

Notation
.

To lighten the expressions to come we write

Therefore (

\frac{X}{q^{1/2}}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}|r|\lesssim Z[c,q]/X\\
r-\alpha q_{c}\equiv 0\bmod c_{q}\end{subarray}}{\sum}\widehat{K}((r-\alpha q_{c})\overline{c_{q}})\widehat{V}\left(\frac{rX}{[c,q]}\right)+O_{A}\left(X^{-A}\right).

Therefore one obtains that

S(X)=\frac{1}{|{\rm P}|q_{0}}\sum_{p\in\rm P}\frac{1}{p}\sum_{c|pq_{0}}\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\alpha(c)}\frac{X}{c}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\lambda_{f}(n)}e\left(\frac{\mp\bar{\alpha}n}{c}\right)\widehat{W}^{\pm}\left(\frac{n}{c^{2}/X}\right)\\
\times\frac{X}{q^{1/2}}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}|r|\lesssim Z[c,q]/X\\
r-\alpha q_{c}\equiv 0\bmod c_{q}\end{subarray}}{\sum}\widehat{K}((r-\alpha q_{c})\overline{c_{q}})\widehat{V}\left(\frac{rX}{[c,q]}\right)+O_{A}\left(X^{-A}\right).

If

, then in the above notation

and

. The congruence condition

becomes

.
Furthermore, from the twisted multiplicativity in (

\begin{split}\widehat{K}((r-\alpha{q_{1}})\bar{p})=\widehat{K_{0}}((r-\alpha{q_{1}})\overline{{q_{1}}p})\widehat{K_{1}}(r\overline{{q_{0}}p});\end{split}

similarly we have

\begin{split}e\left(\frac{\mp\bar{\alpha}n}{pq_{0}}\right)=e\left(\frac{\mp\bar{\alpha}n\overline{q_{0}}}{p}\right)e\left(\frac{\mp\bar{\alpha}n\bar{p}}{q_{0}}\right).\end{split}

Therefore we can further rewrite

as

(2.5)

S(X)=\frac{X^{2}}{|{\rm P}|{q_{0}}^{2}q^{1/2}}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\lambda_{f}(n)}\sum_{p\in\rm P}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\sum_{|r|\lesssim Zpq/X}\widehat{K_{1}}(r\overline{{q_{0}}p})e\left(\frac{\mp\overline{r{q_{0}}}n{q_{1}}}{p}\right)\widehat{V}\left(\frac{rX}{pq}\right)\\
\times\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\alpha({q_{0}})}\widehat{K_{0}}((r-\alpha{q_{1}})\overline{{q_{1}}p})e\left(\frac{\mp\bar{\alpha}n\bar{p}}{{q_{0}}}\right)\widehat{W}^{\pm}\left(\frac{n}{p^{2}{q_{0}}^{2}/X}\right)+S(X)_{c=p}+S(X)_{c=q_{0}}+O\left(X^{-A}\right),

where the term

and

Estimating trivially (assuming the sum over

\displaystyle S(X)\ll

2.1.1.
The case
\tilde{n}\not\equiv 0\bmod q_{0}

By a change of variable, the correlation sum
can be written

\mathfrak{C}_{\gamma,{q_{0}}}(\tilde{n})=\frac{1}{q_{0}^{1/2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\alpha({q_{0}})}\widehat{K_{0}}(\gamma_{1}\cdot\alpha)\overline{\widehat{K_{0}}(\gamma_{2}\cdot\alpha)}=\frac{1}{q_{0}^{1/2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\alpha({q_{0}})}\widehat{K_{0}}(\alpha)\overline{\widehat{K_{0}}(\gamma_{2}.\gamma_{1}^{-1}\cdot\alpha)}+O(\frac{\|\widehat{K}_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}}{q_{0}^{1/2}})

where

\gamma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}-q_{1}&r_{1}\\
0&p_{1}q_{1}\end{pmatrix},\ \gamma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\mp\tilde{n}r_{2}-p_{1}q_{1}&p_{2}r_{2}\\
\mp\tilde{n}p_{2}q_{1}&p_{2}^{2}q_{1}\end{pmatrix}\in\GL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{q_{0}})

and where we have set for

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\GL_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{q_{0}}),\ \gamma\cdot\alpha=\frac{a\alpha+b}{c\alpha+d}.

If
\tilde{n}\not\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q_{0}})
, the
matrix
\gamma_{2}.\gamma_{1}^{-1}
is not a scalar matrix. Therefore, by our assumption that the group of automorphisms of the Fourier transform sheaf
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
is trivial and by
[
8
, Prop. 7.2]
, we obtain

(2.8)

\mathfrak{C}_{\gamma,{q_{0}}}(\tilde{n})\ll_{C_{0}}1\quad\text{whenever}\,\,\tilde{n}\not\equiv 0\bmod q_{0}.

It follows that

S_{\text{off},1}(X)\ll\frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{P^{3}{q_{0}}^{2}q^{1/2}}\times\\
\sup_{N\lesssim\frac{P^{2}{q_{0}}^{2}}{X}}N\bigg(q_{0}^{1/2}\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathrm{P}}\sum_{0\neq|\tilde{n}|\lesssim P^{2}{q_{0}}/N}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\scriptstyle|r_{1}|\lesssim ZPq/X}}}{{{\scriptstyle r_{1}\equiv\mp\overline{\tilde{n}}p_{2}q_{1}\bmod p_{1}}}}}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\scriptstyle|r_{2}|\lesssim ZPq/X}}}{{{\scriptstyle r_{2}\equiv\pm\overline{\tilde{n}}p_{1}q_{1}\bmod p_{2}}}}}\|\widehat{K_{1}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

which gives

\begin{split}S_{\text{off},1}(X)\ll&\frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{P^{3}{q_{0}}^{2}q^{1/2}}\sup_{N\lesssim P^{2}{q_{0}}^{2}/X}N\bigg(q_{0}^{1/2}P^{2}\frac{P^{2}{q_{0}}}{N}\frac{Zq}{X}\frac{Zq}{X}\|\widehat{K_{1}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
\ll&\frac{\|\widehat{K_{1}}\|_{\infty}ZX^{1+o(1)}q^{1/2}}{P{q_{0}}^{5/4}}\sup_{N\lesssim P^{2}{q_{0}}^{2}/X}N^{1/2}\\
\ll&\|\widehat{K_{1}}\|_{\infty}ZX^{1/2+o(1)}q^{1/2}{q_{0}}^{-1/4}.\end{split}

2.1.2.
The case
\tilde{n}\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q_{0}})

If
\tilde{n}\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q_{0}})
we have

(2.9)

\mathfrak{C}_{\gamma,{q_{0}}}(0)=\frac{1}{q_{0}^{1/2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{\alpha({q_{0}})}\widehat{K_{0}}(\alpha)\overline{\widehat{K_{0}}(\begin{pmatrix}p_{1}^{2}q_{1}&-(p_{1}r_{1}-p_{2}r_{2})\\
0&p_{2}^{2}q_{1}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\alpha)}+O(\frac{\|\widehat{K}_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}}{q_{0}^{1/2}}).

The matrix
\begin{pmatrix}p_{1}^{2}q_{1}&-(p_{1}r_{1}-p_{2}r_{2})\\
0&p_{2}^{2}q_{1}\end{pmatrix}
is scalar if and only if

p_{1}r_{1}-p_{2}r_{2}\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q_{0}})\hbox{ and }p_{1}\equiv\pm p_{2}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q_{0}}).

Again, by our assumption that the group of automorphisms of the Fourier transform sheaf
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
is trivial and by
[
8
, Prop. 7.2]
, we obtain

(2.10)

\mathfrak{C}_{\gamma,{q_{0}}}(0)\ll_{C_{0}}q_{0}^{1/2}\delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1}p_{1}\equiv r_{2}p_{2}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q_{0}})\\
p_{1}\equiv\pm p_{2}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q_{0}})\end{subarray}}+1.

Hence for the terms such that
\tilde{n}\equiv 0\bmod q_{0}
, we replace the previous
\tilde{n}
by
q_{0}\tilde{n}
with
\tilde{n}\lesssim 1+P^{2}/N
and apply the bound (
2.10
) to obtain

\begin{split}S_{\text{off},2}(X)\ll&\frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{P^{3}{q_{0}}^{2}q^{1/2}}\sup_{N\lesssim\frac{P^{2}{q_{0}}^{2}}{X}}N\bigg(q_{0}^{1/2}\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathrm{P}}\sum_{0<|\tilde{n}|\lesssim 1+P^{2}/N}\\
&\ \ \ \times\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\scriptstyle|r_{1}|\lesssim ZPq/X}}}{{{\scriptstyle r_{1}\equiv\mp\overline{q_{0}\tilde{n}}p_{2}q_{1}\bmod p_{1}}}}}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\scriptstyle|r_{2}|\lesssim ZPq/X}}}{{{\scriptstyle r_{2}\equiv\pm\overline{q_{0}\tilde{n}}p_{1}q_{1}\bmod p_{2}}}}}\|\widehat{K_{1}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\big(q_{0}^{1/2}\delta_{r_{1}p_{1}\equiv r_{2}p_{2}\bmod{q_{0}}}+1\big)\bigg)^{1/2}\\
\ll&\|\widehat{K_{1}}\|_{\infty}\frac{X^{2+o(1)}}{P^{3}{q_{0}}^{2}q^{1/2}}\sup_{N\lesssim P^{2}{q_{0}}^{2}/X}N\bigg(q_{0}^{1/2}P^{2}\big(1+\frac{P^{2}}{N}\big)\frac{Zq}{X}\frac{Zq}{X}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
\ll&\|\widehat{K_{1}}\|_{\infty}ZX^{o(1)}\Bigl(q^{1/2}{q_{0}}^{1/4}+X^{1/2}q^{1/2}{q_{0}}^{-3/4}\Bigr).\end{split}

Combining the above estimates for
S_{\text{off},1}(X)
and
S_{\text{off},2}(X)
, we have therefore proved that the
\tilde{n}\not=0
frequencies

in (
2.7
) contribute at most

(2.11)

\begin{split}S_{\text{off}}(X)\ll X^{o(1)}\|\widehat{K_{1}}\|_{\infty}\Bigl(ZX^{1/2}q^{1/2}{q_{0}}^{-1/4}+Zq^{1/2}{q_{0}}^{1/4}\Bigr).\end{split}

4.1.
First transformations

From now on we assume that

As in

On the Harnack inequality for antisymmetric s s -harmonic functions
Serena Dipierro, Jack Thompson, Enrico Valdinoci

Abstract
. We prove the Harnack inequality for antisymmetric s s -harmonic functions, and more generally for solutions of fractional equations with zero-th order terms, in a general domain. This may be used in conjunction with the method of moving planes to obtain quantitative stability results for symmetry and overdetermined problems for semilinear equations driven by the fractional Laplacian. The proof is split into two parts: an interior Harnack inequality away from the plane of symmetry, and a boundary Harnack inequality close to the plane of symmetry. We prove these results by first establishing the weak Harnack inequality for super-solutions and local boundedness for sub-solutions in both the interior and boundary case. En passant, we also obtain a new mean value formula for antisymmetric s s -harmonic functions.

1.
Introduction and main results

Since the groundbreaking work
[
Har87
]
, Harnack-type inequalities have become an essential tool in the analysis of partial differential equations (
PDE
). In
[
ABR99
,
CMV16
,

]
, Harnack inequalities have been applied to antisymmetric functions—functions that are odd with respect to reflections across a given plane, for more detail see Section

and the

; see also
[

In recent years, there has been consolidated interest in overdetermined problems driven by nonlocal operators, particularly the fractional Laplacian; however, standard Harnack inequalities for such operators are incompatible with antisymmetric functions since they require functions to be non-negative in all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this paper, we will address this problem by proving a Harnack inequality for antisymmetric
s
-harmonic functions with zero-th order terms which only requires non-negativity in a halfspace. By allowing zero-th order terms this result is directly applicable to symmetry and overdetermined problems for semilinear equations driven by the fractional Laplacian.

For equations arising from jump processes, colloquially known as nonlocal

\gamma_{n,s}
is given in (
1.7
).

We remark that
since
y\mapsto\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}-\frac{1}{|x_{\ast}-y|^{n}}
is antisymmetric for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, we can rewrite (
B.1
) as

(B.2)

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}}\bigg(\frac{r^{2}-|x|^{2}}{|y|^{2}-r^{2}}\bigg)^{s}\bigg(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}-\frac{1}{|x_{\ast}-y|^{n}}\bigg)u(y)\mathop{}\!dy.

Proof of Lemma
.

The Poisson representation formula
[
Rie38
]
gives

(B.3)

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}}\bigg(\frac{r^{2}-|x|^{2}}{|y|^{2}-r^{2}}\bigg)^{s}\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\mathop{}\!dy

where
\gamma_{n,s}=\frac{\sin(\pi s)\Gamma(n/2)}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}+1}}
; see also
[
Lan72
, p.112,p.122]
and
[
Gar19
, Section 15]
. Splitting the integral in (
B.3
) into two separate integrals over
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus B_{r}^{+}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{-}\setminus B_{r}^{-}
respectively, then making the change of variables
y\to y_{\ast}
in the integral over
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{-}\setminus B_{r}^{-}
and using that
u
is antisymmetric, we obtain

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus B_{r}^{+}}\bigg(\frac{r^{2}-|x|^{2}}{|y|^{2}-r^{2}}\bigg)^{s}\bigg(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}-\frac{1}{|x_{\ast}-y|^{n}}\bigg)u(y)\mathop{}\!dy.\qed

Now that we have proven the Poisson kernel formula for antisymmetric functions in Lemma
,
we now establish Proposition
.

Proof of Proposition
.

Let
h\in(0,1)
. It follows from Lemma
that

(B.4)

\displaystyle\frac{u(he_{1})}{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\gamma_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus B_{r}^{+}}\bigg(\frac{r^{2}-h^{2}}{|y|^{2}-r^{2}}\bigg)^{s}\bigg(\frac{1}{|he_{1}-y|^{n}}-\frac{1}{|he_{1}+y|^{n}}\bigg)u(y)\mathop{}\!dy

for all
r>h
. Taking a Taylor series in
h
about
0
, we have the pointwise limit

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{h}\bigg(\frac{1}{|he_{1}-y|^{n}}-\frac{1}{|he_{1}+y|^{n}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{2ny_{1}}{|y|^{n+2}}.

Moreover, by a similar argument to (
2.5
),

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1}{(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{s}}\bigg(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}-\frac{1}{|x_{\ast}-y|^{n}}\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2nh|y_{1}|}{(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{s}|he_{1}-y|^{n+2}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}).

Thus, we obtain the result by taking the limit
h\to 0
in (
B.4
) and applying the Dominated Convergence Theorem to justify swapping the limit and the integral on the right-hand side.
∎

From Proposition
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary B.3
.

Let
u\in C^{2s+\alpha}(B_{1})\cap\mathscr{L}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\alpha>0
and
2s+\alpha
not an integer. Suppose that
u
is antisymmetric and
(-\Delta)^{s}u=0
in
B_{1}
.

Then there exists a radially symmetric function
\psi_{s}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying

(B.5)

\displaystyle\frac{C^{-1}}{1+|y|^{n+2s+2}}\leqslant\psi_{s}(y)\leqslant\frac{C}{1+|y|^{n+2s+2}}\qquad\text{for all }y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for some
C>1
, such that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}y_{1}\psi_{s}(y)u(y)\mathop{}\!dy.

In particular, if
u
is non-negative in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
then

C^{-1}\|u\|_{\mathscr{A}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}}(0)\leqslant C\|u\|_{\mathscr{A}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\psi_{s}(y)

\displaystyle:=n(n+2)\gamma_{n,s}\int_{0}^{\min\{1/|y|,1\}}\frac{r^{2s+n+1}}{(1-r^{2})^{s}}\mathop{}\!dr.

It is clear that
\psi_{s}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and that there exists
C>1
such that (
B.5
) holds. Since
u\in\mathscr{L}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have that
\|u\|_{\mathscr{A}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<+\infty
, so it follows that

(B.6)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}y_{1}\psi_{s}(y)u(y)\mathop{}\!dy\bigg|\leqslant C\|u\|_{\mathscr{A}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<+\infty.

If we multiply
\partial_{1}u(0)
by
r^{n+1}
then integrate from 0 to 1, Proposition
and (
B.2
) give that

(B.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}}(0)=\int_{0}^{1}r^{n+1}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}}(0)\mathop{}\!dr=n\gamma_{n,s}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}}\frac{r^{2s+n+1}y_{1}u(y)}{(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{s}|y|^{n+2}}\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dr.

At this point, let us observe that if we formally swap the integrals in (
B.7
) and then make the change of variables
r=|y|\tilde{r}
, we obtain

\displaystyle n\gamma_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{2s+n+1}y_{1}u(y)}{(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{s}|y|^{n+2}}\mathop{}\!dr\mathop{}\!dy+n\gamma_{n,s}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{0}^{|y|}\frac{r^{2s+n+1}y_{1}u(y)}{(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{s}|y|^{n+2}}\mathop{}\!dr\mathop{}\!dy

\displaystyle=n\gamma_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1/|y|}\frac{\tilde{r}^{2s+n+1}}{(1-\tilde{r}^{2})^{s}}\mathop{}\!d\tilde{r}\bigg)y_{1}u(y)\mathop{}\!dy+n\gamma_{n,s}\int_{B_{1}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\tilde{r}^{2s+n+1}}{(1-\tilde{r}^{2})^{s}}\mathop{}\!d\tilde{r}\bigg)y_{1}u(y)\mathop{}\!dy

(B.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}y_{1}\psi_{s}(y)u(y)\mathop{}\!dy.

By (
B.6
), equation (
B.8
) is finite. Hence, Fubini’s theorem justifies changing the order of integration in (
B.7
) and that the right-hand side of (
B.7
) is equal to (
B.8
) which proves the result.
∎

At this point, we can give the proof of Theorem
.

Proof of Theorem
.

We will begin by proving that

(B.9)

\displaystyle u(x)\geqslant Cx_{1}\|u\|_{\mathscr{A}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\qquad\text{for all }x\in B_{1/2}^{+}.

To this end, we observe that since
|x_{\ast}-y|^{n+2}\leqslant C|y|^{n+2}
for all
x\in B_{1}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}
, (
2.6
) gives that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}-\frac{1}{|x_{\ast}-y|^{n}}\geqslant C\frac{x_{1}y_{1}}{|y|^{n+2}}\qquad\text{for all }x\in B_{1}^{+}{\mbox{ and }}y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus B_{1}^{+}.

Hence, by Lemma
, for all
x\in B_{1/2}^{+}
,

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\bigg(\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{|y|^{2}-1}\bigg)^{s}\bigg(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}-\frac{1}{|x_{\ast}-y|^{n}}\bigg)u(y)\mathop{}\!dy

\displaystyle\geqslant Cx_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{y_{1}u(y)}{\big(|y|^{2}-1\big)^{s}|y|^{n+2}}\mathop{}\!dy

where we used that
(-y_{1})u(y_{\ast})=y_{1}u(y)
and that
u\geqslant 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Then
Proposition
with
r=1
gives that

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant Cx_{1}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}}(0)\qquad\text{for all }x\in B_{1/2}^{+}.

Finally, Corollary
gives (
B.9
).

Next, we will prove that

(B.10)

\displaystyle u(x)\leqslant Cx_{1}\|u\|_{\mathscr{A}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\qquad\text{for all }x\in B_{1/2}^{+}.

Similar to above, for all
x\in B_{1/2}^{+}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus B_{1}^{+}
, we have that
|x-y|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|y|
, so (
2.5
) gives that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}-\frac{1}{|x_{\ast}-y|^{n}}\leqslant\frac{Cx_{1}y_{1}}{|y|^{n+2}}\qquad\text{for all }x\in B_{1/2}^{+}{\mbox{ and }}y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus B_{1}^{+}.

As before, using Lemma
, we have that, for all
x\in B_{1/2}^{+}
,

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\bigg(\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{|y|^{2}-1}\bigg)^{s}\bigg(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}-\frac{1}{|x_{\ast}-y|^{n}}\bigg)u(y)\mathop{}\!dy

\displaystyle\leqslant Cx_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{y_{1}u(y)}{\big(|y|^{2}-1\big)^{s}|y|^{n+2}}\mathop{}\!dy.

Then Proposition
and Corollary
give that

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant Cx_{1}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}}(0)\leqslant Cx_{1}\|u\|_{\mathscr{A}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\qquad\text{for all }x\in B_{1/2}^{+},

which is (
B.10
).

From (
B.9
) and (
B.10
) the result follows easily.
∎

Appendix C
A proof of (
1.3
) when
c:=0

that relies on extension methods

We consider the extended variables
X:=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Then, a solution
u
of
(-\Delta)^{s}u=0
in
\Omega^{+}
can be seen as the trace along
\Omega^{+}\times\{0\}
of its
a
-harmonic extension
U=U(x,y)
satisfying

{\rm div}_{\!X}\big(|y|^{a}\nabla_{\!X}U\big)=0\quad{\mbox{ in }}\,\mathbb{R}^{n+1},

where
a:=1-2s
, see Lemma 4.1 in
[
CS07
]
.

We observe that the function
V(x,y):=x_{1}
is also a solution of the above equation.
Also, if
u
is antisymmetric, then so is
U
, and consequently
U=V=0
on
\{x_{1}=0\}
.

As a result, by the boundary Harnack inequality (see
[
FKJ83
]
),

(C.1)

\sup_{\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\times(0,1)}\frac{U}{V}\leqslant C\inf_{\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\times(0,1)}\frac{U}{V}.

In addition,

\sup_{\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\times(0,1)}\frac{U}{V}\geq\sup_{\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\times\{0\}}\frac{U}{V}=\sup_{\tilde{\Omega}^{+}}\frac{u(x)}{x_{1}}

and similarly

\inf_{\tilde{\Omega}^{+}\times(0,1)}\frac{U}{V}\leq\inf_{\tilde{\Omega}^{+}}\frac{u(x)}{x_{1}}.

From these observations and (
C.1
) we obtain (
1.3
)
in this case.

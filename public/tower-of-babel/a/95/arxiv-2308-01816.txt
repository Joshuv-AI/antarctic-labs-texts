Fixed point results for set-contractions on semi-metric space with a directed graph
Talat Nazir, Zakaria Ali, Shahin Nosrat Jogan, Sergei Silvestrov

Abstract
Fixed point results with respect to generalized rational contractive mappings in semi-metric spaces endowed with a directed graph are proved. Some examples are provided to illustrate the results. The obtained results extend, improve and generalize many results in the existing literature. MSC 2020 Classification: 47H09, 47H10, 54C60, 54H25

Introduction

Among various different kinds of extensions or restrictions of the definition of a metric one of them which we consider in this chapter is the Hausdorff semi-metric
\mathrm{d}_{s}
imposed on a nonempty set
Y
. The main restriction of such a metric is that it does not satisfy the
triangle inequality
. We prove our results by employing the generalized rational graph map on the closed and bounded subsets of the semi-metric space
(Y,\mathrm{d}_{s})
.
Frechet
Frechet1906
studied
the concept of metric space defined on a nonempty set X that induces the Hausdorff topology on
X
. Consequently, there have been several generalizations of a metric function. Among which, one of the important generalization is the concept of a semi-metric or symmetric metric space which gives rise to a topology which is not a Hausdorff.
Zhu et al.
Zhu2005
studied the equivalent contractive conditions in symmetric spaces. Imdad
Imdad2007
obtained coincidence and fixed points of mappings in symmetric spaces under strict contractions. Further useful results in this direction were proved in
Aamri2005
;
Aamri2003
;
Aranelovic2012
;
Cho2008
;
Shahzad2016
.

Definition 1
(semi-metric space,
Wilson1931
)

Let
Y
be a nonempty set. A function
\mathrm{d}_{s}:Y\times Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R_{+}}
is known as (Hausdorff) semi-metric on
Y
if for all
\ r,t\in Y
, the following conditions hold:

(i)

\mathrm{d}_{s}(r,t)\geq 0
and equal to zero if and only if
r=t
;

(ii)

\mathrm{d}_{s}(r,t)=\mathrm{d}_{s}(t,r)
.

(Y,\mathrm{d}_{s})
is called a (Hausdorff) semi-metric space.

Let
(Y,\mathrm{d}_{s})
be a semi-metric space. Let
y\in Y
and any
\varrho>0
.
An open ball centered at
y
with radius
\varrho
is defined as follows,

\mathrm{B}(y,\varrho)=\{x\in Y:\mathrm{d}_{s}(y,x)<\varrho\}.

A topology
\tau_{\mathrm{d}_{s}}
on
Y
is represented by open sets as

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{d}_{s}}=\{U\subseteq Y:\forall\text{ }u\in U~\exists\text{ }\varrho\in\mathbb{R_{+}}\text{ such that }B(u,\varrho)\subseteq U\}.

Definition 2

Let
(Y,\mathrm{d}_{s})
be a semi-metric space and a sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subseteq(Y,\mathrm{d}_{s})
. Then for every
u\in Y
and
\varrho>0
, the open ball
\mathrm{B}(u,\varrho)
is a neighbourhood of
u
with respect to the topology
\tau_{\mathrm{d}_{s}}
. Also, we say that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{d}_{s}(u_{n},u)=0
if and only if
\{u_{n}\}
converges to
u
in
\tau_{\mathrm{d}_{s}}
.

The following properties are in place of the
traingle inequality
for semi-metric spaces. Let
\{u_{n}\},\{v_{n}\}
and
\{w_{n}\}
be sequence in a semi-metric space
(Y,\mathrm{d}_{s})
and
u,v\in Y
. Then

a)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{d}_{s}(u_{n},u)=0
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{d}_{s}(u_{n},v)=0\Longrightarrow u=v
.

b)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{d}_{s}(u_{n},u)=0
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{d}_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})=0\Longrightarrow\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{d}_{s}(v,u)=0
.

c)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{d}_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})=0
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{d}_{s}(v_{n},w_{n})=0\Longrightarrow\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(u_{n},w_{n})=0
.

d)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{d}_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})=0
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{d}_{s}(v_{n},w_{n})=0\Longrightarrow\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{d}_{s}(u_{n},w_{n})<\infty
.

e)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{d}_{s}(u_{n},u)=0\Longrightarrow\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{d}_{s}(u_{n},v)=\mathrm{d}_{s}(u,v)
.

, where

Lemma 1

Let
(Y,\mathrm{d}_{s})
be a semi-metric space and
U,V\in CB(Y)
. Then
\mathrm{d}_{s}(u,V)\leq\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V)
, whenever
u\in U
.

Proof

If
u\in U
, then

\mathrm{d}_{s}(u,V)=\inf\limits_{v\in V}\mathrm{d}_{s}(u,v)\leq\sup\limits_{u\in U}\mathrm{d}_{s}(u,V)\leq\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V).

Lemma 2

Let
(Y,\mathrm{d}_{s})
be a semi-metric space and
U,V\in CB(Y)
. Then for any
u\in U
, there exists
v\in V
such that for any
\mu>1
implies
\mathrm{d}_{s}(u,v)\leq\mu\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V)
.

Proof

Suppose by way of contradiction that there exists
u\in U
such that for every
v\in V
, we have
d(u,v)>\mu\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V)
. By Lemma
1

\displaystyle\mu\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V)<\mathrm{d}_{s}(u,V)\leq\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V)

which is a contradiction.

Definition 7

Let

\mathrm{c}\in[0,1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{s}(fx,fy)\leq\mathrm{c}\mathrm{d}_{s}(x,y)\text{ for all }x,y\in Y.

Graphic Contractive Mappings in Semi Metric Spaces

Jachymski in
Jachymski2008
describes his construction of imposing a graph structure on a metric space. Ran and Reurings’ result in
RanReurings2004
, as mentioned previously was the main motivation for such an effort towards generalization of the order or poset structure of a metric space. The usefulness of imposing a graph structure on
(Y,\mathrm{d}_{s})
is that it generalizes the ordered structure on
(Y,\mathrm{d}_{s})
. One the first result in fixed-point theory with a poset structure was obtained by Ran and Reurings in
RanReurings2004
. The latter result was the main thrust in the generalization of equipping spaces with a graph structure which was motivated by Jachymski in
Jachymski2008
. Recently, Latif et al.
Latif2019
obtained some fixed point results for class of set-contraction mappings endowed with a directed graph. In this chapter, we established several fixed point results with respect to generalized
rational contractive mappings in semi-metric spaces endowed with a directed graph. These results extend and generalized several comparable results in
Frechet1906
;
Hicks1998
;
Latif2012
;
MotPetrusel2009
.

Below we define on how to identify a metric space with a graph
G
.

Definition 8

Let
Y
be a non-empty set. We identify
Y
with a directed graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
, as follows:

1)

Y=V(G)
,
where
V(G)
is the vertex set
;

2)

\Delta\subseteq E(G)
, where
\Delta\subseteq Y\times Y
is the diagonal
and
E(G)
is the edge set
;

3)

Loops at a vertex are allowed, for all
v\in V(G)
;

4)

Parallel edges between distinct vertices are prohibited.

Since
V(G)=Y
, we shall freely refer to elements of
V(G)
or
Y
interchangeably.

The semi-metric space
(Y,\mathrm{d}_{s})
is identified with a directed graph
G
as in Definition
8
such that
G
is weighted, that is, for each
(u,v)\in E(G)
we assign the weight
\mathrm{d}_{s}(u,v)
. Where loops have weight
\mathrm{d}_{s}(u,u)=0
for all
u\in V(G)
. Moreover, the
PH
-semi-metric induced by the semi-metric
\mathrm{d}_{s}
assigns a non-zero
PH
-weight to every
U,V\in CB(Y)
and
\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V)=0
, whenever
U=V
.
If
u,v\in V(G)
, then a (directed) path in
G
between
u
and
v

having length
l\in\mathbb{N}
is a finite sequence
\{y_{n}\}

(n\in\{0,\dots,l\})
of
the vertices so that
u=y_{0}
,
v=y_{l}
also
(y_{i-1},y_{i})\in E(G)
for

If between every two vertices there is a (directed) path, then
G

is called connected. If
\widetilde{G}
is connected, then
G
is said to
be weakly connected, where
\widetilde{G}
represents the undirected graph

.
We also use notation

Main Fixed Point Result on Semi Metric Space

Theorem 3.1

Let
(Y,\mathrm{d}_{s})
be a
\mathrm{d}_{s}
-Cauchy complete semi-metric space endowed with a directed graph, such that
V(G)=Y
and
\Delta\subseteq E(G)
. If
T:CB(Y)\rightarrow CB(Y)
is a generalized rational
\lambda
-contraction mapping and
F(T)=\{U\in CB(Y):T(U)=U\}
, then the following holds:

1)

If
F(T)\neq\emptyset
is complete, then the
PH
-weight assigned to
U,V\in F(T)
is zero.

2)

If
F(T)\neq\emptyset
, then
Y_{T}\neq\emptyset
.

3)

If
Y_{T}\neq\emptyset
and the weakly connected graph
\widetilde{G}
satisfies
\mathcal{\overset{*}{P}}
, then
T
has a fixed point.

4)

F(T)\neq\emptyset
is complete if and only if
F(T)
is a singleton.

Proof

∎
1
) Suppose otherwise, that there exists
U,V\in F(T)
such that
\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V)>0
. Now, since
T
is a generalized
\lambda
-contraction, it follows that

\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V)=\mathcal{H}_{s}(T(U),T(V))\leq\lambda M_{T}(U,V)

(2)

for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
, where

\displaystyle M_{T}(U,V)

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V),\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,T(U)),\mathcal{H}_{s}(V,T(V)),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(V,T(V))[1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,T(U))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V)},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(V,T(U))[1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,T(U))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V)}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V).

The above inequality (
2
) implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V)\leq\lambda\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,V).

This is a contraction. Hence our result follows.

2
) Let
U\in F(T)
, then since
(u,u)\subseteq E(G)
for all
u\in V(G)
. That is
(U,U)=(U,T(U))\subseteq E(G)
. Therefore, we have
U\in CB(Y)
such that
(U,T(U))\subseteq E(G)
. Hence,
U\in Y_{T}
.

3
) Let
U\in Y_{T}\neq\emptyset
. Then since
U\in CB(Y)
, and
\widetilde{G}
is weakly connected, it follows that
CB(Y)\subseteq[U]_{\widetilde{G}}=\mathrm{P}(Y)
, a nonempty power set on
Y
. Since
T
is a self-map and the equivalence class satisfies the transitive property on
CB(Y)
, we have
T(U)\in[U]_{\widetilde{G}}
. As such by a similar argument, we have
T(U_{i})\in[U]_{\widetilde{G}}
for each
U_{i}\in[U]_{\widetilde{G}}
. By definition of
T
, we have
\big(T^{n}(U),T^{n+1}(U)\big)\in E(\widetilde{G})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We now define a recursive iterative sequence, as follows:

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=U_{0},

\displaystyle T(U_{0})

\displaystyle=U_{1},

\displaystyle T^{2}(U_{0})

\displaystyle=T(U_{1})=U_{2},

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle T^{n}(U_{0})

\displaystyle=T(U_{n-1})=U_{n}.

We assume that
U_{n+1}\neq U_{n}
for all
n\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}
. In case
U_{k+1}=U_{k}
for some
k
, then
T(U_{k})=U_{k+1}=U_{k}
, that is,
U_{k}
is the fixed point of
T
. Since
\widetilde{G}
is weakly connected, then there exists a sequence
\{y_{i}\}
such that
(y_{i},y_{i+1})\in E(\widetilde{G})
, for
i=0,\dots,n-1
such that
y_{i}\in U_{i}
for
i=0,\dots,n
. Again by definition of
T
we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{H}_{s}\Big(T^{n}(U),T^{n+1}(U)\Big)=\mathcal{H}_{s}\big(U_{n},U_{n+1}\big)&=\mathcal{H}_{s}\big(T(U_{n-1}),T(U_{n})\big)\\
&\leq\lambda\Big(M_{T}(U_{n-1},U_{n})\Big)\end{array}

(3)

for some
\lambda\in[0,1)
, where,

\displaystyle M_{T}(U_{n-1},U_{n})

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{H(U_{n-1},U_{n}),H(U_{n-1},T(U_{n-1})),H(U_{n},T(U_{n})),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{H(U_{n},T(U_{n})[1+H(U_{n-1},T(U_{n-1}))])}{1+H(U_{n-1},U_{n})},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{H(U_{n},T(U_{n-1}))[1+H(U_{n-1},T(U_{n-1}))])}{1+H(U_{n-1},U_{n})}\Big\}.

Applying the mapping, we get

\displaystyle M_{T}(U_{n-1},U_{n})

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{n-1},U_{n}),\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{n-1},U_{n}),\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{n},U_{n+1}),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{n},U_{n+1})[1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{n-1},U_{n})])}{1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{n-1},U_{n})},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{n},U_{n})[1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{n-1},U_{n})])}{1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{n-1},U_{n})}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\max\{\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{n-1},U_{n}),\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{n},U_{n+1})\}.

Now, if
M_{T}(U,V)=\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{n},U_{n+1})
, then by inequality (
3
), we have a contradiction. Therefore the only value
M_{T}(U_{n-1},U_{n})
can obtain is
\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{n-1},U_{n})
. It now follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{s}\Big(T^{n}(U),T^{n+1}(U)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{s}\big(U_{n},U_{n+1}\big)=\mathcal{H}_{s}\big(T(U_{n-1}),T(U_{n})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\mathcal{H}_{s}\big(U_{n-1},U_{n}\big)

\displaystyle=\lambda\mathcal{H}_{s}\big(T(U_{n-2}),T(U_{n-1})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{n-2},U_{n-1})

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{n}\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{0},U_{1})=\lambda^{n}\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,T(U)).

Now, for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{s}\Big(T^{n}(U),T^{m+n}(U)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{n}\mathcal{H}_{s}(U_{0},U_{m})=\lambda^{n}\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,U_{m}).

Taking the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
yields
\lambda^{n}\mathcal{H}_{s}(U,U_{m})\rightarrow 0
. Therefore,
\{T^{n}(U)\}
is a
\mathrm{d}_{s}
-Cauchy sequence in
CB(Y)
. And since
(Y,d)
is
\mathrm{d}_{s}
-Cauchy complete, there exists
U^{*}
such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}T^{n}(U)=U^{*}
.

Now, we show that
U^{*}
is a fixed point of
T
. Since,
\{T^{n}\}
with
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}T^{n}(U)=U^{*}
is a
\mathrm{d}_{s}
-Cauchy sequence and by our assumption we have
(T^{n}(U),T^{n+1}(U))\in E(\tilde{G})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, by property
\mathcal{\overset{*}{P}}
, we have a subsequence
\{T^{n_{k}}(U)\}
of
\{T^{n}(U)\}
such there is an edge between
T^{n_{k}}(U)
and
U^{*}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{s}\Big(T(U^{*}),T^{n_{k}}(U^{*})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda M_{T}(U^{*},T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}))

for some
\lambda\in[0,1)
. Note that

M_{T}(U^{*},T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}))=M_{T}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})
, where

\displaystyle M_{T}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*}),\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}))),\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(U^{*})),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(U^{*}))[1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*})))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*})))[1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*})))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})}\Big\}.

Applying the mapping
T
, we get

\displaystyle M_{T}(U^{*},T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}))

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*}),\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T^{n_{k}}(U^{*})),\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(U^{*})),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(U^{*}))[1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}))[1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*})))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})}\Big\}.

We now consider the following cases.

Case 1
: If
M_{T}(U^{*},T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}))=\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})
, taking the limit for
n\rightarrow\infty
yields

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{H}_{s}\Big(T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}),T(U^{*})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle=\lambda\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},U^{*})=0.

Hence, we must have
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{H}_{s}\Big(T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}),T(U^{*})\Big)=0
, that is
T(U^{*})=U^{*}
.

Case 2
: If
M_{T}(U^{*},T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}))=\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}))
, taking the limit for
n\rightarrow\infty
yields

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{H}_{s}\Big(T(U^{*}),T^{n_{k}}(U^{*})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(U^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}))

\displaystyle=\lambda\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},U^{*})=0.

We must have
\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(U^{*}))=0
, hence
T(U^{*})=U^{*}
.

Case 3
: If
M_{T}(U^{*},T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}))=\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(U^{*}))
taking the limit for
n\rightarrow\infty
yields

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{H}_{s}\Big(T(U^{*}),T^{n_{k}}(U^{*})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(U^{*}))\leq\lambda\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(U^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(U^{*})).

Therefore
\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(U^{*}))=0
, and we conclude:
T(U^{*})=U^{*}
.

Case 4
: If
M_{T}(U^{*},T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}))=\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(U^{*}))[1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})}
, taking the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
yields

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{H}_{s}\Big(T(U^{*}),T^{n_{k}}(U^{*})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(U^{*}))[1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})}

\displaystyle=\lambda\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T(U^{*})).

As in
Case 3
, we have
T(U^{*})=U^{*}
.

Case 5
: If
M_{T}(U^{*},T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}))=\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}))[1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*})))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})}
, taking the limit for
n\rightarrow\infty
yields

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{H}_{s}\Big(T(U^{*}),T^{n_{k}}(U^{*})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}))[1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*})))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{s}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})}

\displaystyle=\lambda(0)=0.

As in
Case 3
, we conclude
T(U^{*})=U^{*}
. Therefore,
U^{*}\in F(T)
.
We now show that the fixed point
U^{*}
is unique. Suppose by way of contradiction that we have
U^{*},V^{*}\in F(T)
and let
\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},V^{*})>0
. Then by a similar argument to (
1
) of Theorem
3.1
, we have
\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},V^{*})\leq\lambda\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},V^{*})
, whence
\mathcal{H}_{s}(U^{*},V^{*})=0
implying
U^{*}=V^{*}
. We also conclude that
|F(T)|=1
.

4
) Letting
F(T)\neq\emptyset
be complete, then by (
1
) of Theorem
3.1
, we conclude that
F(T)
is a singleton. This proves the necessity. Proving the sufficiency, let
F(T)
be a singleton with
U\in F(T)
, then by (
2
) of Theorem
3.1
, we have
U\in Y_{T}
, hence
F(T)
is complete.
∎

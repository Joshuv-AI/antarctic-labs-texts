Deciding whether two quadratic surfaces actually intersect
Huu-Quang Nguyen, Ruey-Lin Sheu, Yong Xia

Abstract
We answer an open question proposed by Pólik and Terlaky in 2007 that: how we can decide whether two quadratic surfaces intersect without actually computing the intersections?



1
Introduction

Quadratic surfaces (also known as
quadrics
) are common in mechanical
CAD/CAM
. The quadrics intersection problem (QSIC) arises in computer graphics; see Levin
(
L79
, 1979)
for a standard reference. It studies the morphology of the intersection curve, and finds ways to compute the mutual curve.
Due to the complication of all possible types of intersections there might arise,
deciding as a priori whether the quadrics actually intersect (the decision version of (QSIC)) helps to render
the intersection in a faster way (Wilf and Manor
(
Wilf-Manor
, 1993)
).

The decision version of (QSIC) turns out to be
difficult in its own right. Even for two ellipsoids in the three dimensional space, the classical approaches to determine whether they intersect - either to do it by a constrained
numerical optimization or to rely on numerical observations of the eigenvalues, both are not satisfactory solutions. We need an analytical mathematical solution to answer the intersection of all types, not just for ellipsoids in 3D, but for all cases in general
n
dimensions (Chan
(
Chan06
, 2006)
). It was also posed formally as an open question by Pólik and Terlaky
(
Polik-Terlaky07
, 2007)
that:
how we can decide whether two quadratic surfaces intersect without actually computing the intersections?
We shall answer the question in this paper.

Let the two quadratic surfaces be represented by equations
f_{i}(x)=0,i=1,2,
where
f_{1}(x)=x^{T}A_{1}x+2a^{T}x+a_{0},f_{2}(x)=x^{T}A_{2}x+2b^{T}x+b_{0}
are real-valued quadratic functions of
n
-variables with symmetric matrices
A_{1},A_{2};

a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
a_{0},b_{0}\in\mathbb{R}.
Let

\mathcal{P}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f_{1}(x)=f_{2}(x)=0\}.

The quadrics intersection problem is now the feasibility problem of the set
\mathcal{P}
. To deal with it, we consider a type of polynomial optimization problem of degree 4:

\displaystyle{\rm(Po4)}\hskip 56.9055pt\begin{array}[]{lll}v{\rm(Po4)}=&\inf\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}&(f_{1}(x))^{2}+(f_{2}(x))^{2}.\end{array}

It is obvious that
\mathcal{P}=\emptyset
if and only if either
v(\rm Po4)>0
or
v(\rm Po4)=0
but unachieved. The following example shows that the unattainability of (Po4) is possible.

Example 1

Let
f_{1}(x,y)=x^{2},f_{2}(x,y)=xy-1.
Consider the sequence

\{(\frac{1}{n},n)^{T}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Since

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}(f_{1}(x_{n},y_{n}))^{2}+(f_{2}(x_{n},y_{n}))^{2}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}=0,

we know
v{\rm(Po4)}=0.
However,
\mathcal{P}=\{(x,y):x=0,xy-1=0\}=\emptyset
implies that
v(\rm Po4)=0
is not attained.

Our approach relies on several known results in literature.

•

Attainability of quadratic programming subject to one quadratic inequality constraint:

\displaystyle{\rm(QP1QC)}\hskip 8.5359pt\begin{array}[]{lll}&\inf\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}&f_{1}(x)\\
&~~{\rm s.t.}&f_{2}(x)\leq 0.\end{array}

The problem (QP1QC) can be solved by the classical S-lemma
MJJ
;
Polik-Terlaky07
;
Y03
, while the attainability of (QP1QC) was completely characterized with a set of necessary and sufficient conditions by Hsia, Lin and Sheu
(
Xia2014
, Theorem 4.4, Remark 4.5, 2014)

•

Quadratic programming subject to one quadratic equality constraint:

\displaystyle{\rm(QP1EQC)}\hskip 8.5359pt\begin{array}[]{lll}&\inf\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}&f_{1}(x)\\
&~~{\rm s.t.}&f_{2}(x)=0.\end{array}

The problem has been completely solved by Xia, Wang and Sheu
(
XWS
, 2016)
by their version of S-lemma with equality.

•

Geometrical representation of “the S-lemma with equality” and “the convexity of the joint numerical range of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}.
” While the former was first characterized analytically by Xia et al.
(
XWS
, 2016)
and the latter by Flores-Bazán and Opazo
(
FO16
, 2016)
, we have provided alternative geometrical version of them by the notation of “separation of quadratic level sets” in
(
Q-S2019
, 2019)
and
(
Q-C-S2020
, 2020)
, respectively. More separation properties about the quadratic level sets shown in
(
Q-S2020
, 2020)
(by Nguyen and Sheu) will be also quoted and used.

Combining all the above results, our scheme to check the feasibility of the set
\mathcal{P}
contains the following procedures.

\bullet

If
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are linearly independent, we develop a new type of
\mathcal{S}
-procedure with
2
quadratic constraints (Theorem
3.1
of this paper). It enables us to compute
v(\rm Po4)
via an SDP.

\bullet

If
A_{1}=t^{*}A_{2}
(or
A_{2}=t^{*}A_{1}
) with
t^{*}\in\mathbb{R},
by elementary analysis, we show that computing
v(\rm Po4)
can be reduced to solve an (QP1EQC).

\bullet

If
v(\rm Po4)=0,
we show the attainability of (Po4) can be either reduced to checking whether
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f_{i}(x)=0\}
separates
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f_{j}(x)=0\}
for some
i,j\in\{1,2\}
; or be reduced to check the attainability of some (QP1QC)’s.

3
Computing
v(\rm Po4)

3.1
A new S-procedure

Let
f_{1}(x)=x^{T}A_{1}x+2a^{T}x+a_{0},~f_{2}(x)=x^{T}A_{2}x+2b^{T}x+b_{0}
and
F:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as
F(z)=z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2},~z=(z_{1},z_{2})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.
We have the following new type of
\mathcal{S}
-procedure.

Theorem 3.1

Suppose that
\{A_{1},A_{2}\}
are linearly independent and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}.
The following two statements are equivalent:

{\rm(G_{1})}

(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},z\in\mathbb{R}^{2})

f_{1}(x)-z_{1}=0,f_{2}(x)-z_{2}=0

~\Rightarrow~F(z)-\gamma\geq 0.

{\rm(G_{2})}

(\exists\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R})

F(z)-\gamma+\alpha(f_{1}(x)-z_{1})+\beta(f_{2}(x)-z_{2})\geq 0,~~\forall(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proof

Note that
\rm(G_{2})\Rightarrow\rm(G_{1})
is trivial. We only prove that
\rm(G_{1})\Rightarrow\rm(G_{2})
.

Since
\{A_{1},A_{2}\}
are linearly independent, by Theorem
2.6
,

\mathbf{R}(f_{1},f_{2})=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}|~z_{1}=f_{1}(x),~z_{2}=f_{2}(x),~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}

is convex. Due to
\rm(G_{1}),
we have
F(z)-\gamma\geq 0,~\forall z\in\mathbf{R}(f_{1},f_{2}),

which implies that

\mathbf{R}(f_{1},f_{2})\cap D=\emptyset,

where
D:=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}|~F(z)-\gamma<0\}.

Since
F(z)
is convex,
D
is convex and open. By the separating hyperplane theorem, there exists
v=(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta})^{T}\not=0
such that

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}z_{1}+\bar{\beta}z_{2}+\bar{\gamma}\geq 0,

\displaystyle~\forall~z\in\mathbf{R}(f_{1},f_{2}),

(11)

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}z_{1}+\bar{\beta}z_{2}+\bar{\gamma}<0,

\displaystyle~\forall~z\in D.

(12)

From (
12
),
\bar{\alpha}z_{1}+\bar{\beta}z_{2}+\bar{\gamma}\geq 0\Rightarrow z\not\in D\Rightarrow F(z)-\gamma\geq 0.
By the classical S-lemma, there exists
t\geq 0
such that

F(z)-\gamma-t(\bar{\alpha}z_{1}+\bar{\beta}z_{2}+\bar{\gamma})\geq 0,~\forall~z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(13)

If
t=0
, choose
\alpha=\beta=0
. Then,
\rm(G_{2})
holds.

If
t>0
, by (
11
), one has,
\forall~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle t\bar{\alpha}f_{1}(x)+t\bar{\beta}f_{2}(x)+t\bar{\gamma}\geq 0

(14)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle t\bar{\alpha}f_{1}(x)+t\bar{\beta}f_{2}(x)+(t\bar{\alpha}-t\bar{\alpha})z_{1}+(t\bar{\beta}-t\bar{\beta})z_{2}+t\bar{\gamma}\geq 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle t\bar{\alpha}(f_{1}(x)-z_{1})+t\bar{\beta}(f_{2}(x)-z_{2})+(t\bar{\alpha}z_{1}+t\bar{\beta}z_{2}+t\bar{\gamma})\geq 0.

Let
\alpha=t\bar{\alpha},~\beta=t\bar{\beta}.
Then, (
14
) renders
\rm(G_{2})
by
(
13
). The proof is complete.
\Box

Example 2

(15)

Note that (

When

3.2
Solving the optimal value of (Po4)

Let us first write the problem (Po4) as a quadratic programming with two quadratic equality constraints as follows.

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}&\inf\limits_{(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}&(z_{1})^{2}+(z_{2})^{2}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt{\rm s.t.}&\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{1}(x)-z_{1}=0,\\
f_{2}(x)-z_{2}=0.\end{array}\end{cases}\end{array}

Theorem 3.2

Under the condition

are linearly independent, the optimal value of

can be computed by

\displaystyle v(\rm{Po4})=\underset{\tiny\begin{array}[]{lll}\gamma,\,\alpha,\,\beta\in\mathbb{R}\end{array}}{\sup}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\gamma&|~M\succeq 0\end{array}\right\},

where
M\in\mathbb{R}^{(n+3)\times(n+3)}
is

\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&&0\\
0&&1\end{array}&~~[0]_{2\times n}&\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{-\alpha}{2}\\
\frac{-\beta}{2}\end{array}\\
~~~[0]_{n\times 2}&\quad\alpha A_{1}+\beta A_{2}&\alpha a+\beta b\\
\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{-\alpha}{2}&\quad\frac{-\beta}{2}\end{array}&\quad~\alpha a^{T}+\beta b^{T}&\quad\alpha a_{0}+\beta b_{0}-\gamma\end{pmatrix},

(21)

where

[0]=(0_{ij})_{2\times n}
with
0_{ij}=0~\forall i,j.

Proof

Since (Po4) can be formulated as (
3.2
), we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}v{\rm(Po4)}&=&\inf\limits_{(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(z)\\
&~~~{\rm s.t.}&\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{lll}f_{1}(x)-z_{1}=0\\
f_{2}(x)-z_{2}=0\end{array}\end{cases}\\
&=&{\sup}\left\{\gamma\left|~\left\{(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\left|~\begin{array}[]{ll}F(z)<\gamma\\
f_{1}(x)=z_{1}\\
f_{2}(x)=z_{2}\end{array}\right.\right\}=\emptyset\right.\right\}\\
&=&\underset{\tiny\begin{array}[]{lll}\gamma,\,\alpha,\,\beta\in\mathbb{R}\end{array}}{\sup}\big\{\gamma|~\overline{F}(x,z)\geq 0~\forall(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big\},\end{array}

(22)

where
\overline{F}(x,z)=F(z)-\gamma+\alpha(f_{1}(x)-z_{1})+\beta(f_{2}(x)-z_{2})
, and the last equality in (

) holds by Theorem
3.1
. Note that
\overline{F}(x,z)\geq 0
can be written as a linear matrix inequality (
3.2
) with a matrix
M
defined in (
21
).
\Box

(23)

Define

;
\bar{a}^{T}=[a^{T},-1,0],

h^{T}=[b^{T}-t^{*}a^{T},t^{*},-1]
,
h_{0}=b_{0}-t^{*}a_{0}
;
and

\bar{A}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{ll}[0]_{n\times n}&[0]_{n\times 2}\\[0.0pt]
{}_{2\times n}&I_{2\times 2}\end{array}\end{pmatrix},~\bar{A_{1}}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{1}&[0]_{n\times 2}\\[0.0pt]
{}_{2\times n}&[0]_{2\times 2}\end{array}\end{pmatrix}\not=0.

Then, (
3.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+2}}

\displaystyle y^{T}\bar{A}y

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle y^{T}\bar{A_{1}}y+\bar{a}^{T}y+a_{0}=0,~h^{T}y+h_{0}=0.

By the null space representation, one can write the hyperplane
h^{T}y+h_{0}=0

as
y=y_{0}+Vz
where
V\in\mathbb{R}^{(n+2)\times(n+1)}
is the matrix basis of
\mathcal{N}(h),~z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
y_{0}=-\frac{h_{0}}{h^{T}h}h
.
Then, (
3.2
) is reduced to the following (QP1EQC) problem:

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle~(y_{0}+Vz)^{T}\bar{A}(y_{0}+Vz)\hskip 42.67912pt

(24)

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle~(y_{0}+Vz)^{T}\bar{A_{1}}(y_{0}+Vz)+\bar{a}^{T}(y_{0}+Vz)+a_{0}=0,

which can be solved by applying the S-lemma with equality. See
XWS

4
Determining the feasibility of the set
\mathcal{P}

Suppose the optimal value of problem (Po4) has been computed. If
v(\rm Po4)>0
, we know immediately that the two quadrics
\{f_{1}=0\}
and
\{f_{2}=0\}
have no intersection. Otherwise,
v(\rm Po4)=0.
It remains to study whether
v(\rm Po4)=0
is attainable or not. Our analysis relies on whether the two hypersurfaces
\{f_{1}=0\}
and
\{f_{2}=0\}
“separate” one by the other. See Definition
1
in Section 2. To this end,
we need to assume that both
f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x)
satisfy two-side Slater’s condition, i.e., there are
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such
f_{i}(x)<0<f_{i}(y),i\in\{1,2\}
.

If not, let
f_{1}(x)\geq 0,~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Namely,
f_{1}(x)
is a convex function. Then
A_{1}\succeq 0,a\in\mathcal{R}(A_{1}),a_{0}-a^{T}A_{1}^{+}a\geq 0.
It indicates that the first order equation
A_{1}x+a=0
has a solution, say
x_{0}=-A_{1}^{+}a,
and
f(x_{0})=-a^{T}A^{+}_{1}a+a_{0}=\inf\{f(x):~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}.

If
a_{0}-a^{T}A^{+}a>0,
then
\{f_{1}=0\}=\emptyset.
In this case, the two quadrics
\{f_{1}=0\}
and
\{f_{2}=0\}
do not intersect.

Otherwise,
a_{0}-a^{T}A^{+}a=0.
Then,
\{f_{1}=0\}=\{x:Ax+a=0\}=\{-A_{1}^{+}a+Zy:y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\}
is affine in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
is a matrix basis of
\mathcal{N}(A_{1})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\{f_{1}=0\}\cap\{f_{2}=0\}=\emptyset

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\{y:(-A_{1}^{+}a+Zy)^{T}A_{2}(-A_{1}^{+}a+Zy)+2b^{T}(-A_{1}^{+}a+Zy)+b_{0}=0\}=\emptyset.

Let
C=Z^{T}A_{2}Z,c=-a^{T}A_{1}^{+}A_{2}Z+b^{T}Z,c_{0}=a^{T}A_{1}^{+}A_{1}^{+}a-2b^{T}A_{1}^{+}a+b_{0}.

Then, the two quadrics do not intersect if,
either
C\succeq 0,c\in\mathcal{R}(C)
and
c_{0}-c^{T}Cc>0;
or
C\preceq 0,c\in\mathcal{R}(C)
and
c_{0}-c^{T}Cc<0.

In order to get the main result of this section, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 1

Assume that
f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x)
both satisfy two-side Slater’s condition;
v(\rm Po4)=0
;
\mathcal{P}=\emptyset
and there doesn’t exist
i\neq j
in
\{1,2\}
such that
\{f_{i}=0\}
separates
\{f_{j}=0\}
. Then, the joint range set
\mathbf{R}(f_{1},f_{2})=\{(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x))^{T}:x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
is convex.

Proof

Suppose on the contrary that
\mathbf{R}(f_{1},f_{2})
is not convex. By Theorem
2.3
in Section 2, there are
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
i\neq j
in
\{1,2\}
, say
i=1,j=2
such that
\{f_{1}=\alpha\}
separates
\{f_{2}=\beta\}
.
By Theorem
2.4
, there is some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that
A_{1}=\lambda A_{2}
and

\text{the hyperplane }\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}-\alpha+\lambda\beta=0\}\text{ separates }\{f_{2}-\beta=0\}.

(25)

By Theorem
2.5
, there exists a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f_{2}-\beta
or
-(f_{2}-\beta)
has the form

-x_{1}^{2}+\delta(x_{2}^{2}+\cdots+x_{m}^{2})+c^{\prime},~\text{ where }\delta\in\{0,1\},~c^{\prime}>0

and

f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}-\alpha+\lambda\beta=\nu_{1}x_{1}+\delta(\nu_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+\nu_{m}x_{m})+\nu_{0},~\nu_{1}\neq 0.

(26)

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

f_{2}(x)-\beta=-x_{1}^{2}+\delta(x_{2}^{2}+\cdots+x_{m}^{2})+c^{\prime},~c^{\prime}>0

since

-(f_{2}(x)-\beta)=-x_{1}^{2}+\delta(x_{2}^{2}+\cdots+x_{m}^{2})+c^{\prime}
can be similarly proved.
Due to
\mathcal{P}=\emptyset
, i.e.
\{f_{1}=0\}\cap\{f_{2}=0\}=\emptyset
, it implies immediately that

\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}=0\}\cap\{f_{2}=0\}=\emptyset.

(27)

\bullet
Case 1:
{\rm rank}(A_{2})\geq 2
. That is,
\delta=1,~m\geq 2.

We first claim that, from (
27
) there indeed is

\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}=0\}\subset\{f_{2}>0\}\text{ or }\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}=0\}\subset\{f_{2}<0\}.

(28)

Indeed, by (
27
), if (
28
) failed, there would be
\bar{x},\hat{x}\in\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}=0\}
such that
f_{2}(\bar{x})<0<f_{2}(\hat{x})
.
From (
26
),
\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}=0\}
is connected, so there exists
\tilde{x}\in\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}=0\}
such that
f_{2}(\tilde{x})=0,
a contradiction to (
27
). Therefore, (
28
) must hold true.

- If
\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}=0\}\subset\{f_{2}>0\},
namely,
f_{2}(x)|_{\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}=0\}}>0,
the first statement of S-lemma with equality,
(\rm E_{1})
, holds. Due to

f_{2}(x)=-x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\cdots+x_{m}^{2}+c^{\prime}+\beta,~c>0;

and

f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}
is non-constant affine (see (
26
)), there is no
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
such that
f_{1}(x)-\lambda f_{2}(x)+\gamma f_{2}(x)\geq 0,~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
The second statement of S-lemma with equality,
(\rm E_{2})
, fails. By Theorem
2.2
and Theorem
2.1
, we know
\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}=0\}\text{ separates }\{f_{2}<0\},
and
c^{\prime}+\beta\geq 0.
In fact, there is
c^{\prime}+\beta>0.
Otherwise, as we can see that the following system of equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}f_{2}(x)=-x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\cdots+x_{m}^{2}=0\\
\left(f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}\right)(x)=\nu_{1}x_{1}+\nu_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+\nu_{m}x_{m}+\nu_{0}+\alpha-\lambda\beta=0,~\nu_{1}\not=0\end{array}\right.

has a solution

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}x_{1}=x_{2}=\frac{-\nu_{0}-\alpha+\lambda\beta}{\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}},x_{3}=\cdots=x_{n}=0,&\text{if }\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}\not=0\\
x_{1}=-x_{2}=\frac{-\nu_{0}-\alpha+\lambda\beta}{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}},x_{3}=\cdots=x_{n}=0,&\text{if }\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}\not=0,\end{array}\right.

which is a contradiction to (
27
).

By
c^{\prime}+\beta>0;

f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}
is non-constant affine;
f_{2}(x)|_{\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}=0\}}>0,
we conclude, by Theorem
2.5
,
that
\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}=0\}
separates
\{f_{2}=0\}.
It follows from Proposition
1
that
\{f_{1}=0\}
separates
\{f_{2}=0\},

which contradicts to the hypothesis of the lemma.

- If
\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}=0\}\subset\{f_{2}<0\},
then
\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2}=0\}\subset\{-f_{2}>0\}.
By the same above arguments for the pair
\{f_{1}-\lambda f_{2},-f_{2}\}
, we also arrive a contradiction. In a short summary, Case 1 cannot happen.

\bullet
Case 2:
{\rm rank}(A_{2})=1
. Then
\pm(f_{2}-\beta)
has the form
-x_{1}^{2}+1

so that

Theorem 4.1

Assume that
f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x)
both satisfy two-side Slater’s condition and
v(\rm Po4)=0
. Then
\mathcal{P}=\emptyset
if and only if either one of the two cases happen.

(a)

\{f_{i}=0\}
separates
\{f_{j}=0\}
for some
i\neq j
in
\{1,2\}
;

(b)

there exists
i\neq j\in\{1,2\}
such that at least one of the four (QP1QC)’s happened:
\inf\{\pm f_{i}(x):\pm f_{j}(x)\leq 0\}=0
. Moreover, for the above (QP1QC)’s that do happen, they must be unattainable.

Proof

(\Rightarrow)
If (a) does not hold, we have to prove that (b) is true.
By Lemma (
1
), the joint range set
\mathbf{R}(f_{1},f_{2})=\{(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x))^{T}:x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
is convex. Moreover, the range set does not contain the origin due to
\mathcal{P}=\emptyset
. By the separation hyperplane theorem, there exists
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\not=(0,0)
such that

\mu_{1}f_{1}(x)+\mu_{2}f_{2}(x)\geq 0,~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(29)

In fact, one has
\mu_{1}\neq 0
,
\mu_{2}\neq 0.
Otherwise, say
\mu_{1}=0,\mu_{2}\neq 0.
Then
\mu_{2}f_{2}(x)\geq 0,~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
would have failed the two-side Slater’s condition of
f_{2}(x)
. Now, dividing
|\mu_{1}|
at both sides of (
29
), we have

sign(\mu_{1})f_{1}(x)+\dfrac{|\mu_{2}|}{|\mu_{1}|}(sign(\mu_{2})f_{2}(x))\geq 0,~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(30)

Applying the classical S-lemma to (
30
),
\{sign(\mu_{2})f_{2}\leq 0\}\subset\{sign(\mu_{1})f_{1}\geq 0\}
. Or equivalently,

\{sign(\mu_{2})f_{2}\leq 0\}\cap\{sign(\mu_{1})f_{1}<0\}=\emptyset.

Then,

\displaystyle\inf\{sign(\mu_{1})f_{1}(x):x\in\{sign(\mu_{2})f_{2}\leq 0\}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{\gamma:\{sign(\mu_{1})f_{1}<\gamma\}\cap\{sign(\mu_{2})f_{2}\leq 0\}=\emptyset\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0.

We are going to claim that, indeed,

\inf\{sign(\mu_{1})f_{1}(x):x\in\{sign(\mu_{2})f_{2}\leq 0\}\}=0.

(31)

Suppose contrarily that
\inf\{sign(\mu_{1})f_{1}(x):x\in\{sign(\mu_{2})f_{2}\leq 0\}\}=\epsilon>0.
Then,

\{sign(\mu_{1})f_{1}<\epsilon/2\}\cap\{sign(\mu_{2})f_{2}\leq 0\}=\emptyset.

By S-lemma, there is
\lambda\geq 0
such that
sign(\mu_{1})f_{1}(x)-\epsilon/2+\lambda\left(sign(\mu_{2})f_{2}(x)\right)\geq 0,~\forall x
. Hence,

\displaystyle 0<\epsilon/2

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|sign(\mu_{1})f_{1}(x)+\lambda\left(sign(\mu_{2})f_{2}(x)\right)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}\sqrt{(f_{1}(x))^{2}+(f_{2}(x))^{2}},~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

which leads to
v({\rm Po4})=\inf\{(f_{1}(x))^{2}+(f_{2}(x))^{2}\}\geq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4(1+\lambda^{2})}>0
, a contradiction to our assumption.
So we have proved (
31
) that one of the four (QP1QC)’s happened:
\inf\{\pm f_{i}(x):\pm f_{j}(x)\leq 0\}=0
.

Finally, for the (QP1QC) that happened, say
\inf\{f_{1}(x):f_{2}(x)\leq 0\}=0
, it must be unattainable. Otherwise, let
\hat{x}
be an optimal solution to
\inf\{f_{1}(x):f_{2}(x)\leq 0\}=0
such that
f_{2}(\hat{x})\leq 0
and
f_{1}(\hat{x})=0.
Since
\mathcal{P}=\emptyset,
there must be
f_{2}(\hat{x})<0.
Then,
\inf\{f_{1}(x):f_{2}(x)\leq 0\}=0
attains its minimum value
0
at an interior point. It could happen only when
f_{1}(x)
is convex and
f(x)\geq 0,~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

It violates the assumption that
f_{1}
satisfies the two-side Slater’s condition.
Therefore,
\inf\{f_{1}(x):f_{2}(x)\leq 0\}=0
is unattainable.

(\Leftarrow)
It is trivial. Indeed,

- If (a) holds, by Definition
1
, we immediately have
\{f_{1}=0\}\cap\{f_{2}=0\}=\emptyset.

- If (b) holds, without loss of generality, we assume that
\inf\{f_{1}(x):f_{2}(x)\leq 0\}=0.
Suppose
x^{*}\in\{f_{1}=0\}\cap\{f_{2}=0\}\not=\emptyset,

x^{*}
must be a minimizer for
\inf\{f_{1}(x):f_{2}(x)\leq 0\}=0,
contradicting to the assumption that
\inf\{f_{1}(x):f_{2}(x)\leq 0\}=0
is not attainable. Hence,
\{f_{1}=0\}\cap\{f_{2}=0\}=\emptyset.

\Box

5
Conclusion and Discussion

In this paper, we propose a scheme to answer conclusively whether two given quadrics intersect or not. We describe the problem as a special type of (unconstrained) polynomial optimization problem of degree 4, which we call it (Po4). The quadric intersection problem is then transformed to solve (Po4) and to detect whether its optimal value
v({\rm Po4})
is attainable or not. Technically, we develop a new S-procedure to compute
v({\rm Po4})
, while having to
borrow a relatively new concept about the separation of quadratic level sets to conquer the attainability issue of it. We agree that our method still requires further computational details to make it practical, for example, how
v({\rm Po4})=0
can be rigidly distinguished from
v({\rm Po4})>0
using today’s finite-digit precision machine. However, our purpose is mainly to show that there is a nice, mathematically sensible, approach to the open problem whose effective solution methods were not known to people before. On the other hand,
we feel that the composition of “quadratic with quadratics” occurs naturally in many applications, such as those of nonlinear least squares. We hope that our approach can be extended to solving optimization problems involving other quartic polynomials.

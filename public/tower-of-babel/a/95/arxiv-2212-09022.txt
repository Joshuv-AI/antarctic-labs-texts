Weyl’s Lemma on R ​ C ​ D ​ ( K , N ) RCD(K,N) Metric Measure Spaces
Yu Peng, Hui-Chun Zhang, Xi-Ping Zhu

Abstract
. In this paper, we extend the classical Weyl’s lemma to R ​ C ​ D ​ ( K , N ) RCD(K,N) metric measure spaces. As its applications, we show the local regularity of solutions for Poisson equations and a Liouville-type result for L 1 L^{1} very weak harmonic functions on R ​ C ​ D ​ ( K , N ) RCD(K,N) spaces. Meanwhile, a byproduct is that we obtain a gradient estimate for solutions to a class of elliptic equations with dis-continuous coefficients.



1.
Introduction

The classical Weyl’s lemma states that any very weakly harmonic function in the Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{n}
must be smooth. Namely,

Theorem 1.1

(Weyl
[
50
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. Suppose that
u\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\Omega)
and

\int_{\Omega}u\Delta\varphi=0

for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. Then
u
is smooth (I.e. there exists a
C^{\infty}
-representative).

Extensions of Weyl’s lemma are widely studied. For examples, Burch
[
6
]
extended it to elliptic operators with variable coefficients, Hörmander
[
21
]
proved Weyl’s lemma for hypoelliptic operators, and Elson
[
16
]
developed a theory of distributions on separable real Banach spaces and proved an analogy of Weyl’s lemma. We refer to
[
44
]
for a survey.
Recently, Di Fratta-Fiorenza
[
14
]
gave a short proof for the regularity of very weak solutions of Poisson equations via Weyl’s lemma.

In this paper, we will extend Weyl’s lemma to non-smooth settings. More precisely, we will prove Weyl’s lemma on
RCD(K,N)
spaces. Given a metric measure space
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
, the notion
RCD(K,N)
is a synthetic notion of lower Ricci bounds for
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
, which has been developed in
[
47
,
48
,
34
,
2
,
3
,
18
,
4
,
17
]
. The parameters
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,+\infty]
play the role of “Ricci curvature
\geqslant K
and dimension
\leqslant N
”. We refer to the survey
[
1
]
for the improvements of geometric analysis on
RCD(K,N)
spaces.
Important examples of
RCD(K,N)
spaces include Ricci limit spaces
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
and finite-dimensional Alexandrov spaces with curvature bounded from below with respect to their Hausdorff measures
[
40
,
51
]
.

Let
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
be an
RCD(K,N)
space. The Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(X)
is a Hilbert space, and then the inner product
\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla v}\rangle\in L^{1}(X,\mathfrak{m})
makes sense for any
u,v\in W^{1,2}(X)
. Given an open subset
\Omega\subset X
, a function
u\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
is called a (weakly)
harmonic function
on
\Omega
if

\int_{X}\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\varphi}\rangle d\mathfrak{m}=0,\ \ \forall\varphi\in Lip_{c}(\Omega),

where
Lip_{c}(\Omega)
denotes the space of Lipschitz functions defined on
X
with compact supported in the interior of
\Omega
. The Lipschitz regularity of harmonic functions on
RCD(K,N)
spaces has been established in
[
19
,
24
,
22
]
.

In order to extend Weyl’s lemma to the setting of
RCD(K,N)
spaces, let us begin with the notion of “very weakly harmonic function”. Let
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
be an
RCD(K,N)
space. We denote by
P_{t}
the heat flow on
L^{2}(X,\mathfrak{m})
and by
\Delta
the associated infinitesimal generator with domain
D(\Delta)
.
Recall that the class of
test functions
on
RCD(K,N)
spaces is given in
[
20
]
by

\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X):=\left\{\varphi\in D(\Delta)\cap L^{\infty}(X):\left\lvert\nabla\varphi\right\rvert\in L^{\infty}(X),\ \Delta\varphi\in L^{\infty}(X)\cap W^{1,2}(X)\right\}.

Given any open domain
\Omega\subset X
, denote by

(1.1)

\mathrm{Test}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega):=\left\{\varphi\in\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X)\big|{\rm\ supp}(\varphi)\subset\Omega\ {\rm and\ supp}(\varphi)\ {\rm is\ compact}\right\}.

It is well-known
[
20
]
that the space
\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X)
is dense in
W^{1,2}(X)
and the space
\mathrm{Test}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega).

Definition 1.2
.

Let
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
be an
RCD(K,N)
space for some
1\leqslant N<\infty
and
K\in\mathbb{R}
and let
\Omega\subset X
be an open domain. A function
u\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\Omega)
is called a
very weakly subharmonic function
on
\Omega
if

(1.2)

\int_{\Omega}u\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}\geqslant 0,\qquad\forall\varphi\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \varphi\geqslant 0.

A function
u\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\Omega)
is called a
very weakly superharmonic function
on
\Omega
if
-u
is very weakly subharmonic. A function
u\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\Omega)
is called a
very weakly harmonic function
on
\Omega
if both
u
and
-u
are very weakly subharmonic.

The Weyl’s lemma is concerned with the regularity of a very weak harmonic function
u
on
\Omega
. If
u\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
then it is a (weakly) harmonic, and hence it is locally Lipschitz continuous (see
[
19
,
24
,
22
]
). This remains the gap between the
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
and the
u\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.

The major obstacle of the improvement from
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
to
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
for very weak harmonic functions on a singular space can be understood as follows.
For the convenience of the discussion, we consider a very weak harmonic function

u:(\Omega,g)\to\mathbb{R}
from a domain
\Omega\!\subset\!\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a
singular
Riemannian metric
g=(g_{ij})
. Then
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
is a very weak solution of the
elliptic equation of divergence form

(1.3)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{i}\left(a^{ij}\partial_{j}u\right)=0\quad{\rm with}\ \ a^{ij}:=\sqrt{g}g^{ij},

in the sense of distributions, where
\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
,
g=\det(g_{ij})
and
(g^{ij})
is the inverse matrix of
(g_{ij})
. It seems that Zhang-Bao’s work
[
53
]
is the only result fixing the whole gap from
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
to
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. They proved
[
53
]
that if the coefficients
a^{ij}\in Lip_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, then every
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
-solution of (
1.3
) must be in
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. Brezis
[
5
]
showed that if all
a^{ij}
are Dini continuous then every
BV_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
-solution of (
1.3
) must be in
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. Recently, Manna-Leone-Schiattarella in
[
35
]
proved that if
a^{ij}\in C^{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,n}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
satisfying a doubling Dini condition, then every
L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
-solution of (
1.3
) is in
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. The
Dini-type condition of the coefficients
a^{ij}
is necessary: Jin-Maz’ya-Schaftingen in
[
28
]
constructed an equation (
1.3
) with
a^{ij}\in C^{0}(\Omega)
and that it has a solution
u\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\setminus W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.
On the other hand, we assume even that
\Omega
is a small neighborhood near a regular point in a finite dimensional Alexandrov space with curvature bounded from below.
According to
[
38
,
39
]
, there is
a
BV_{\rm loc}
-Riemannian metric
(g_{ij})
on
\Omega
.
However, it is well-known
[
38
]
that these
g_{ij}
may not be continuous on a
dense
subset of
\Omega
. Thus, It seems the mentioned works
[
28
,
53
,
5
]
do not support that one can improve a
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
very weak harmonic function to be in
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, even on an Alexandrov space with curvature bounded from below.

The main result of this paper is the following Weyl’s lemma on RCD-setting.

Theorem 1.3

(Weyl’s lemma)
.

Let
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
be an
RCD(K,N)
space for some
1\leqslant N<\infty
and
K\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open domain. If
u\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\Omega)
is a very weakly harmonic function on
\Omega
,
then
u
is locally Lipschitz continuous on
\Omega
(i.e. it has a locally Lipschitz continuous representative). In particular, it is in
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.

Remark 1.4
.

When
X=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the regularity for very weak solutions of (
1.3
) relies on the well-known Calderón-Zygmund theory which states if
\Delta u\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}
with
p>1
, then
u\in W^{2,p}_{\rm loc}
. In particular, if
p>n
then
|\nabla u|
is in
C^{\alpha}_{\rm loc}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Recently, G. De Philippis and J. Núnez-Zimbrón
[
13
]
have shown that this statement does not hold for general Alexandrov spaces with curvature bounded from below. For example, let us consider the space
X^{2}
constructed by Ostu-Shioya in
[
38
]
, which is a two-dimensional Alexandrov space with the property that the set of singular points is dense in
X^{2}
.
Given any non-constant harmonic function
u
on a domain
\Omega\subset X^{2}
, it was shown in
[
13
, Theorem 1]
that
\lim_{R\to 0}\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x))}\int_{B_{R}(x)}|\nabla u|^{2}(x)=0
at every point
x\in\Omega
with
{\rm diam}(\Sigma_{x})<\pi
, where
\Sigma_{x}
is the space of directions at
x
. In the two-dimensional Alexandrov space
X^{2}
, a point
x
satisfying
{\rm diam}(\Sigma_{x})<\pi
is equivalent to that
x
is a singular point. Since the set of singular points on
X^{2}
is dense, if
|\nabla u|
were assumed to be continuous, it would follow
|\nabla u|\equiv 0
on
\Omega
. That is a contradiction.

The first application of Theorem
1.3
is the following local regularity result for the very weak solutions of Poisson equations on
RCD
spaces.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
be an
RCD(K,N)
space with
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,\infty)
, and let
\Omega\subset X
be an open subset. Suppose
f\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. If
u\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\Omega)
is a very weak solution of Poisson equation on
\Omega
in the sense of

(1.4)

\int_{\Omega}u\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}=\int_{\Omega}f\varphi d\mathfrak{m},\qquad\forall\varphi\in\mathrm{Test}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \varphi\geqslant 0.

Then
u
is in
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.

The second application of Theorem
1.3
is the following Liouville-type result for
L^{1}
very weakly subharmonic functions.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
be an
RCD(K,N)
space with
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq N<\infty
, then any
L^{1}(X)
very weakly subharmonic function on
X
must be identically constant (namely, it has a constant representative).

Recall that Li-Schoen
[
32
]
and Chung
[
11
]
gave some examples that a complete Riemannian manifold admits non-constant nonnegative
L^{1}
harmonic functions. Under an assumption of Ricci lower bounds, P. Li
[
31
]
proved a Liouville-type result for
L^{1}
subharmonic functions on complete Riemannian manifolds. We will prove Corollary
1
along the same line in
[
31
]
and combine Theorem
1.3
.

At last, as a byproduct, we give a remark on the Lipschitz estimates for weak solutions of a class of elliptic equations on Euclidean spaces with
dis-continuous coefficients
.
Let
n\geqslant 2
and let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain.
Suppose that
u\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(D)
is a weak solution of elliptic
equations of divergence form

(1.5)

Lu:={\rm div}(A(x)Du)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{i}(a^{ij}\partial_{j}u)=0,\quad 1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n,

where the matrix of coefficients
A(x)=(a^{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
is symmetric, bounded, measurable and uniformly elliptic, i.e.

(1.6)

\|a^{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\leqslant\Lambda,\qquad\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leqslant\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a^{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j},\quad{\rm a.e.}\ x\in D,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

for some
\lambda,\Lambda>0
. It is well known
[
6
]
that any weak solution
u
of
Lu=0
is in
C^{1}(D)
provided that
A
satisfies the Dini continuity condition. Recently, this Dini continuity condition were weaken to others Dini-type conditions by Y. Li
[
33
]
, Dong-Kim
[
15
]
and Maz’ya-McOwen
[
36
]
. However, an example constructed by Jin-Maz’ya-Schaftingen
[
28
]
shows that, general speaking, the continuity of the coefficient is not sufficient to ensure
u\in Lip_{\rm loc}(D)
.

Inspired by the regularity of harmonic functions on
RCD
spaces, we first introduce a class of models for elliptic coefficient, which plays the role of constant coefficient
in the classical regularity theory.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
\overline{A}=(\overline{a}^{ij}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
be symmetric, bounded, measurable and uniformly elliptic on the each ball
B_{R}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
R>0
. Let
n\geqslant 3
. We first introduce a Riemannian metric
\overline{g}_{ij}(x)
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

(1.7)

(\overline{g}_{ij})^{-1}(x)=\overline{g}^{ij}(x):=\frac{\overline{a}^{ij}(x)}{\left[{\rm det}(\overline{a}^{ij}(x))\right]^{\frac{1}{n-2}}},\qquad 1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n.

We denote by
d_{\overline{g}}
the distance function induced by Riemannian metric
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\overline{g}_{ij})
.

The coefficient
\overline{A}
is called a
conical coefficient of nonnegative curvature
if the metric space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},d_{\overline{g}})
is a cone with nonnegative curvature in the sense of Alexandrov.

We remark that if
\bar{a}^{ij}
is constant coefficient, then the Riemannian metric
\bar{g}_{ij}=const
, and then
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\bar{g}_{ij})
is flat (i.e., sectional curvature is zero).

There are also some
dis-continuous
conical coefficient of nonnegative curvature. For example, consider the graph
\Gamma_{f}
of a convex function

f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})=\left(a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+a_{2}x^{2}_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}x^{2}_{n}\right)^{1/2},

where
a_{i}\in(0,+\infty)
for all
i=1,\cdots,n
. It is well known that
\Gamma_{f}
is a cone of nonnegative curvature in the sense of Alexandrov. Under the coordinate system
(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})
, its Riemannian metric is given by

\bar{g}_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+\frac{a_{i}x_{i}}{f}\cdot\frac{a_{j}x_{j}}{f},

for all
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n
. Then
\bar{A}(x)=(\bar{a}^{ij}(x))
with

\bar{a}^{ij}:=\sqrt{{\rm det}(\bar{g}_{ij})}\bar{g}^{ij}=\left(1+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i}x_{i})^{2}}{f^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\delta_{ij}+\frac{a_{i}x_{i}}{f}\cdot\frac{a_{j}x_{j}}{f}\right)^{-1}

is a conical coefficient of nonnegative curvature, and
\bar{a}^{ij}(x)
is not continuous at
0.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(B_{1}(0))
be a weak solution of elliptic equations of divergence form
{\rm div}(A(x)Du)=0,

where the matrix of coefficients
A(x)=(a^{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
is symmetric, bounded, measurable and uniformly elliptic. Let
n\geqslant 3.

Suppose that
A
is Dini asymptotic to a conical coefficient of nonnegative
curvature in the sense that there exists a conical coefficient of nonnegative curvature
\overline{A}=(\overline{a}^{ij})
such that

(1.8)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega_{A,\overline{A}}(t)}{t}dt<\infty,\quad\ where\ \ \omega_{A,\overline{A}}(t):=\|A(x)-\overline{A}(x)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{t}(0))}.

Then

(1.9)

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B_{r}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mathcal{L}^{n}\leqslant C_{0}\fint_{B_{1}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mathcal{L}^{n},

where the constant
C_{0}
depends only on
n,\lambda,\Lambda
and
\omega_{A,\overline{A}}
, and
\mathcal{L}^{n}
is the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

This extends the result in
[
6
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space, i.e.
(X,d)
is a complete and separable metric space endowed with a non-negative Borel metric which is finite on bounded sets.
Throughout this paper, we always assume that
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
is an
RCD(K,N)
space with
N\in[1,+\infty)
and
K\in\mathbb{R}
. For explicit definitions, one can refer to
[
18
,
4
,
17
]
and the survey
[
1
]
.

Let
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
be an
RCD(K,N)
space with
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,+\infty)
. The generalized Bishop-Gromov inequality (see
[
34
,
47
]
) implies a local measure doubling: for any
R>0
, there exists a constant
C_{N,K,R}>0
such that

(2.1)

\frac{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r_{2}}(x))}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{r_{1}}(x))}\leq C_{N,K,R}\cdot\left(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\right)^{N},\quad\forall r_{1},r_{2}\in(0,R)\ \ {\rm with}\ \ r_{1}<r_{2}.

The Sobolev spaces on
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
were established in
[
7
,
43
,
2
,
18
]
via several different approaches. We refer the readers to the textbook
[
20
, Chapter 2]
for basic definitions and properties of
W^{1,2}(X):=W^{1,2}(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
and
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(X):=W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
. For any
f\in W^{1,2}(X),
we denote by
|\nabla f|\in L^{2}(X)
the minimal weak upper gradient of
f
(see for example
[
20
, Sect. 2.1]
). For any
f,h\in W^{1,2}(X)
, the inner product

\langle{\nabla f},{\nabla h}\rangle
is given by

\langle{\nabla f},{\nabla h}\rangle=\frac{1}{4}\left(|\nabla(f+h)|^{2}-|\nabla(f-h)|^{2}\right)\ \in L^{1}(X).

The inner product
\langle{\nabla f},{\nabla h}\rangle
is symmetric and bi-linear, and satisfies Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Chain rule and Leibniz rule (see
[
45
, Appendix]
or
[
20
]
).

For any open domain
\Omega\subset X
, the space
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
is the closure of
Lip_{c}(\Omega)
with support in
\Omega
under
W^{1,2}(X)
-norm.
The space
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
is the space of Borel functions
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f\chi\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(X)
for any Lipschitz function
\chi:X\to[0,1]
such that
d\big({\rm supp}(\chi),X\setminus\Omega\big)>0
, where a function
f\chi
is taken
0
by definition on
X\setminus\Omega.

Let
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
be the heat flow on
X
associated with the energy
\int_{X}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mathfrak{m}
for any
f\in W^{1,2}(X)
, and let
\Delta
be the infinitesimal generator of
P_{t}
with domain

D(\Delta)
. It is well know that (see for instance
[
20
, Chapter 5]
)

(2.2)

\int_{X}h\Delta fd\mathfrak{m}=-\int_{X}\langle{\nabla f},{\nabla h}\rangle d\mathfrak{m},\quad\ \forall f\in D(\Delta),\ \ h\in W^{1,2}(X).

For any
f\in L^{2}(X)
, it is well known that
P_{t}f\in D(\Delta)
, and that the curve
t\mapsto P_{t}f
is in
C^{1}\left((0,+\infty),L^{2}(X)\right)
with

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}P_{t}f=\Delta P_{t}f.

If
f\in D(\Delta)
, then

P_{t}(\Delta f)=\Delta(P_{t}f),\qquad\forall t>0.

Since
P_{t}
is a family of linear contractions on
L^{2}(X)
for any
t>0
, it can be uniquely extended to be a family of linear contractions on
L^{p}(X)
for any
p\in[1,+\infty]
.

Since
RCD(K,N)
spaces are locally doubling and satisfy a local Poincaré inequality (see
[
49
,
41
]
), according to
[
45
,
46
,
3
]
, there exists a heat kernel

p_{t}(x,y)
on
(0,+\infty)\times X\times X
such that

P_{t}f(x)=\int_{X}p_{t}(x,y)f(y)d\mathfrak{m}(y)\ \ \ \ \forall\ t>0

for every
f\in L^{2}(X)
. It was proved
[
46
,
3
]
that
(x,y)\mapsto p_{t}(x,y)
is locally Hölder continuous on
X\times X
for each
t>0
, and that
t\mapsto p_{t}(x,y)
is analytic in
(0,+\infty)
for each
x,y\in X
.
Moreover, it was showed in
[
26
]
that there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
depending only on
N
and
K
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{1}\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x)\right)}\exp

\displaystyle\left\{-\frac{d^{2}(x,y)}{3t}-C_{2}t\right\}\leq p_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x)\right)}\exp\left\{-\frac{d^{2}(x,y)}{5t}+C_{2}t\right\}

for all
t>0
and all
x,y\in X
, and

(2.4)

\left\lvert\nabla p_{t}(\cdot,y)\right\rvert(x)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{t}\cdot\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x)\right)}\exp\left\{-\frac{d^{2}(x,y)}{5t}+C_{2}t\right\}

for all
t>0
and
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x,y\in X
.
Combining Davies
[
12
, Theorem 4]
and (
2.3
), the estimate

(2.5)

\left\lvert\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p_{t}(x,y)\right\rvert=\left\lvert\Delta p_{t}(\cdot,y)\right\rvert(x)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{t\cdot\mathfrak{m}\left(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x)\right)}\exp\left\{-\frac{d^{2}(x,y)}{5t}+C_{2}t\right\}

holds for all
t>0
and
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x,y\in X
. An important property of
p_{t}(x,y)
is the stochastic completeness (see
[
45
]
):

(2.6)

\int_{X}p_{t}(x,y)d\mathfrak{m}(y)=1,\quad\forall t>0\ {\rm and}\ x\in X.

The class of
test functions
on
RCD(K,N)
spaces was introduced in
[
20
]
:

\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X):=\left\{f\in D(\Delta)\cap L^{\infty}(X):\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert\in L^{\infty}(X),\ \Delta f\in L^{\infty}(X)\cap W^{1,2}(X)\right\},

which is an algebra and is dense in
W^{1,2}(X)
(see
[
20
, Sect. 6.1.3]
). Given any
\varphi\in\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X)
, it is well-known
[
4
]
that it has a (Lipschitz) continuous representative. Hence we always assume that every function in
\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X)
is continuous. The following lemma is a standard fact about the heat kernel.

Lemma 2.1
.

(1)
For any
t>0
and
x_{0}\in X
, the function
p_{t}(x_{0},\cdot)\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}(X)
.

(2)
For any
\varphi\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}(X)
and
t>0
, it holds
P_{t}\varphi\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}(X).

Proof.

The assertion (1) follows from
p_{t/2}(x_{0},\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(X)\cap L^{1}(X)
,

p_{t}(x_{0},x)=\big[P_{t/2}\big(p_{t/2}(x_{0},\cdot)\big)\big](x)

and the estimates (
2.3
)-(
2.5
).

For (2), since
\varphi\in D(\Delta)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
and
\Delta\varphi\in L^{\infty}(X)
, by the contraction of
P_{t}
on
L^{\infty}(X)
, we have
P_{t}\varphi\in L^{\infty}(X)
and
\Delta(P_{t}\varphi)=P_{t}(\Delta\varphi)\in L^{\infty}(X)
. From
\Delta\varphi\in L^{2}(X)
, we obtain
\Delta(P_{t}\varphi)=P_{t}(\Delta\varphi)\in W^{1,2}(X).

Finally, since
|\nabla\varphi|\in L^{\infty}(X)
, by using the Bakry-Ledoux inequality on
RCD(K,N)
spaces (see
[
17
, Theorem 4.3]
) and the contraction of
P_{t}
on
L^{\infty}(X)
again, we conclude that

|\nabla P_{t}\varphi|^{2}\leqslant e^{-2Kt}P_{t}(|\nabla\varphi|^{2})\in L^{\infty}(X).

The proof of (2) is finished.
∎

The existence of good cut-off functions is an important ingredient in geometric analysis. For an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
M^{n}
with Ricci curvature bounded from below, one can estimate merely the upper bounds for the Laplacian of distance functions, by the Laplace comparison theorem. In this aspect, Schoen-Yau in
[
42
, Theorem 4.2 in Chapter I]
constructed distance-like functions on
M^{n}
having
L^{\infty}
-bounds of the Laplacian. Cheeger-Colding
[
8
]
found good cut-off functions with
L^{\infty}
estimate on the Laplacian, which is a key technical tool in the Cheeger-Colding theory of
Ricci limit spaces (see
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
).
The existence of a regular cut-off function in
RCD
setting was proved in
[
4
,
22
,
37
]
.
We need the following variant of
[
37
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
be an
RCD(K,N)
space with
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq N<\infty
.
For each
r_{0}>0
, there exists a constant
C_{K,N,r_{0}}>0
such that for any ball
B_{R}(x)\subset X
with radius
R\geqslant r_{0}
, there exists a cut-off function
\eta:X\to\left[0,1\right]
such that

\mathrm{(i)}

\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{R}(x)
and
{\rm supp}(\eta)\subset B_{2R}(x)
,

\mathrm{(ii)}

\eta\in\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X)
, and moreover for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in X
,

|\Delta\eta|(x)+|\nabla\eta|(x)\leqslant\frac{C_{N,K,r_{0}}}{R}.

(
Remark that the constant
C_{N,K,r_{0}}
does not depend on
R
.
)

Proof.

This is a slight modification of
[
37
, Lemma 3.1]
. For the convenience of readers, we give the proof as follows. Fix a ball
B_{2R}(x)\subset X
with radius
R\geqslant r_{0}
and let
\varphi
be a Lipschitz continuous function defined as
\varphi\equiv 1
on
B_{R}(x)
,
\varphi\equiv 0
on
X\setminus B_{2R}(x)
and
\varphi(y)=\frac{2R-d(x,y)}{R}
on
B_{2R}(x)\setminus B_{R}(x)
. As in
[
37
, Lemma 3.1]
, we consider the heat flow regularization
\varphi_{t}:=P_{t}\varphi
. According to
[
17
]
, we can choose
continuous representatives of
\varphi_{t},|\nabla\varphi_{t}|
and
|\Delta\varphi_{t}|
; and moreover, by the Bakry-Ledoux’s inequality (see
[
17
, Theorem 4.3]
), we have for everywhere

(2.7)

|\nabla\varphi_{t}|^{2}+\frac{4Kt^{2}}{N(e^{2Kt}-1)}|\Delta\varphi_{t}|^{2}\leqslant e^{-2Kt}P_{t}(|\nabla\varphi|^{2})\leqslant\frac{e^{-2Kt}}{R^{2}},

where we have used
|\nabla\varphi|\leqslant 1/R
and
L^{\infty}
-contraction of
P_{t}
.
It follows that

|\varphi_{t}(y)-\varphi(y)|\leqslant\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{\frac{N(e^{2Ks}-1)e^{-2Ks}}{4Ks^{2}}}ds:=\frac{F_{K,N}(t)}{R}\leqslant\frac{F_{K,N}(t)}{r_{0}},\quad\forall y\in X,

by
R\geqslant r_{0}
, where
F_{K,N}(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is continuous and
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}F_{K,N}(t)=0.

It is clear that there exists
t_{K,N,r_{0}}>0
such that
F_{K,N}(t_{K,N,r_{0}})\leqslant r_{0}/4
. Therefore we have
\varphi_{t_{K,N,r_{0}}}(y)\in[3/4,1]
for every
y\in B_{R}(x)
and
\varphi_{t_{K,N,r_{0}}}(y)\in[0,1/4]
for every
y\in X\setminus B_{2R}(x)
. As in
[
37
, Lemma 3.1]
, the desired cut-off
function
\eta
can be given by composition with a
C^{2}
-function
f:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
such that
f\equiv 1
on

[3/4,1]
and
f\equiv 0
on
[1/4,0]
; indeed,
\eta:=f\circ\varphi_{t_{K,N,r_{0}}}
is now identically equal to one
on
B_{R-r_{0}}(x)
, vanishes identically on
X\setminus B_{R}(x)
and, by (
2.7
) and the Chain rule (see for instance
[
20
, Proposition 5.2.3]
), it satisfies
the estimate
|\nabla\eta|+|\Delta\eta|\leqslant C(K,N,r_{0})/R
as desired.
∎

We recall the following measure-valued Laplacian introduced in
[
18
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open subset and let
f\in W_{\rm loc}^{1,2}(\Omega)
. It is called
f\in D(\bm{\Delta})
on
\Omega
if there exists a signed Radon measure
\mu
on
\Omega
such that

\int_{\Omega}\varphi d\mu=-\int_{\Omega}\langle{\nabla\varphi},{\nabla f}\rangle d\mathfrak{m},\quad\forall\varphi\in Lip_{c}(\Omega).

Such
\mu
is unique and is denoted by
\bm{\Delta}f
(which depends on
\Omega
).

Both the Chain rule and the Leibniz rule for this
{\bf\Delta}
were proved in
[
18
, Chapter 4]
. When
\Omega=X
, it was showed
[
18
, Proposition 4.24]
that for any
f\in W^{1,2}(X)
,

f\in D(\Delta)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f\in D({\bf\Delta})\ {\rm and}\ {\bf\Delta}f=g\cdot\mathfrak{m}\ {\rm for\ some}\ g\in L^{2}(X).

In this case
{\bf\Delta}f=\Delta f\cdot\mathfrak{m}
.

The combination of
[
52
, Theorem 1.5]
and
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
states the following.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be a domain and
f\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. Suppose that

\mathbf{\Delta}f=g\cdot\mathfrak{m}
on
\Omega
for some
g\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. Then
f\in Lip_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, and moreover for any ball
B_{R}(x)\subset\subset\Omega
, it holds

\sup_{B_{R/2}(x)}|\nabla f|\leqslant C_{N,K,R}\big(\|f\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x))}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x))}\big)

for some constant
C_{N,K,R}.
In particular, any harmonic function
f
(namely,
{\bf\Delta}f=0
on
\Omega
) is locally Lipschitz continuous.

At last, we recall a simple fact as follows.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be open. If
f\in D({\bf\Delta})\cap C(\Omega)
then
{\bf\Delta}f\big(\Omega\setminus{\rm supp}(f)\big)=0
. In particular, if
\Omega=X
and if
f\in D(\Delta)\cap C(X)
then
\Delta f(x)=0
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\not\in{\rm supp}(f)
.

Proof.

Given any
\varphi\in Lip_{c}(\Omega)
with
{\rm supp}(\varphi)\subset\Omega\setminus{\rm supp}(f)
, by applying
[
7
, Corollary 2.25]
, we have that

|\nabla\varphi|=0

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. in
\Omega\setminus{\rm supp}(\varphi)\supset{\rm supp}(f)
and that
|\nabla f|=0

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. in
\Omega\setminus{\rm supp}(f)
. It follows that
\langle{\nabla f},{\nabla\varphi}\rangle=0

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. in
\Omega
. Indeed, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
[
45
, Appendix]
implies

(2.8)

|\langle{\nabla f},{\nabla\varphi}\rangle|\leqslant|\nabla f|\cdot|\nabla\varphi|\quad\mathfrak{m}{\rm-a.e.\ in}\ \Omega.

Therefore, by the definition of
{\bf\Delta}f
, we have

{\bf\Delta}f(\varphi)=0,
which shows the first assertion.

If
\Omega=X
and
f\in D(\Delta)
, by
[
18
, Proposition 4.24]
, we have
{\bf\Delta}f=\Delta f\cdot\mathfrak{m}.
Applying the first assertion, we have
\Delta f(x)=0
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in X\setminus{\rm supp}(f).
The proof is finished.
∎

3.
Distributions and Weyl’s lemma

Let
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
be an
RCD(K,N)
space for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,+\infty)
. Recall that the class of
test functions

\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X):=\left\{\varphi\in D(\Delta)\cap L^{\infty}(X):\left\lvert\nabla\varphi\right\rvert\in L^{\infty}(X),\ \Delta\varphi\in L^{\infty}(X)\cap W^{1,2}(X)\right\},

is dense in
W^{1,2}(X)
and is an algebra (see
[
20
, Sect 6.1]
).
We equip
\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X)
with the norm

\|\varphi\|_{E}:=\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}+\||\nabla\varphi|\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}+\|\Delta\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(X)},

and denote by
E(X)
the linear normed space
\left(\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X),\|\cdot\|_{E}\right)
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open domain, we set

\begin{split}\mathrm{Test}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)&:=\left\{\varphi\in\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X)\big|\ {\rm supp}(\varphi)\subset\Omega\ {\rm and\ supp}(\varphi)\ {\rm is\ compact}\right\}\\
{\rm and}\quad\ \mathrm{Test}_{c,+}^{\infty}(\Omega)&:=\{\varphi\in\mathrm{Test}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)|\ \varphi\geqslant 0\}.\end{split}

From Lemma
2
, for each open domain
\Omega\subset X
and every compact set
Q\subset\Omega
, there exists
\eta\in\mathrm{Test}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
\eta\equiv 1
on
Q
and
0\leqslant\eta\leqslant 1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The space
\mathrm{Test}_{c,+}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
\{f\in Lip_{c}(\Omega)|f\geqslant 0\}
. Consequently,
the space
\mathrm{Test}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

This lemma is somewhat known. For the completeness, we present a proof here.
Let
f\in Lip_{c}(\Omega)
be nonnegative. Here
f
is taken
0
by the definition on
X\setminus\Omega
. According to
[
20
, Proposition 6.1.8]
), we take a sequence of nonnegative
f_{j}\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}(X)
such that
f_{j}\to f
in
W^{1,2}(X)
, as
j\to+\infty
.

Since
{\rm supp}(f)\subset\Omega
is compact, we have
d\big({\rm supp}(f),X\setminus\Omega\big)>0.
According to Lemma
2
, there exists a cut-off function
\eta\in{\rm Test}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
0\leqslant\eta\leqslant 1
and
\eta\equiv 1
, on some domain containing
{\rm supp}(f)
. Then we have
f\eta\equiv f
on
X
and
f_{j}\eta\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
for all
j
. By

|f_{j}\eta-f|=|(f_{j}-f)\eta|\leqslant|f_{j}-f|
and

|\nabla(f_{j}\eta-f)|^{2}\leqslant 2|\nabla(f_{j}-f)|^{2}\eta^{2}+2(f_{j}-f)^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2},

We conclude that
f_{j}\eta\to f
in
W^{1,2}(X)
as
j\to+\infty
, since
\eta\in Lip(X)
. Notice that
f_{j}\eta\geqslant 0
. The proof of the first assertion is finished.

The second assertion follows from the first one by noticing the facts that the space
Lip_{c}(\Omega)
is dense in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
and that for any
f\in Lip_{c}(\Omega)
it holds
f=f^{+}-f^{-}
and
f^{\pm}\in Lip_{c}(\Omega)
.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
.
A
distribution

T
in
\Omega
is a linear functional on
\mathrm{Test}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that for every compact set
Q\subset\Omega
there exists a constant

C_{Q}
such that

|T(\varphi)|\leqslant C_{Q}\|\varphi\|_{E},\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathrm{Test}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\ {\rm with}\ {\rm supp}(\varphi)\subset Q.

It is clear that every
element
f\in E^{\prime}(X)
, the dual space of
E(X)
, defines a distribution on
X
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, the
distributional Laplacian
of
f
, denoted by
\Delta_{\mathscr{D},\Omega}f
, is defined as a linear functional on
\mathrm{Test}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
by

\Delta_{\mathscr{D},\Omega}f(\varphi):=\int_{\Omega}f\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m},\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathrm{Test}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

If
\Omega=X
, we denote
\Delta_{\mathscr{D}}f:=\Delta_{\mathscr{D},X}f
for any
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(X)
.

For each
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, it is easy to check that
\Delta_{\mathscr{D},\Omega}f
is a distribution in
\Omega
.
Given two domains
\Omega\subset\Omega^{\prime}
and a function
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega^{\prime})
, it is not hard to check that
\Delta_{\mathscr{D},\Omega}f=\Delta_{\mathscr{D},\Omega^{\prime}}f
in
\Omega
. In fact, for any
\varphi\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, Lemma
2
implies that
\Delta\varphi(x)=0
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega^{\prime}\setminus\Omega
. It follows
that
\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}f\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}=\int_{\Omega}f\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}
.

First of all, we show that the distribution Laplacian is compatible with the measure-valued Laplacian in
[
18
]
, and also, when
\Omega=X
, with the generator of
L^{p}(X)
-semigroup
P_{t}
for each
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open domain.
If
f\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, then
\Delta_{\mathscr{D},\Omega}f={\bf\Delta}f
on
\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).
In particular, if
u
is subharmonic/superharmonic on
\Omega
then it is very weak subharmonic/superharmonic on
\Omega
.

Proof.

Take any
\varphi\in\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).
Since
d\big({\rm supp}(\varphi),X\setminus\Omega\big)>0
, there exists a Lipschitz function
\chi:X\to[0,1]
such that
\chi\equiv 1
on
{\rm supp}(\varphi)
and
{\rm supp}(\chi)\subset\Omega
. Noticing that
{\rm supp}(\varphi)
is compact, we can choose
\chi
such that
{\rm supp}(\chi)
is compact too. From the definition of
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
and
f\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, we have
h:=f\cdot\chi\in W^{1,2}(X)
; moreover, since
h(x)=f(x)
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in{\rm supp}(\varphi)
, by using
[
7
, Corollary 2.25]
, we get
|\nabla f|(x)=|\nabla h|(x)
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in{\rm supp}(\varphi)
and
|\nabla\varphi|(x)=0
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\not\in{\rm supp}(\varphi)
. It follows that (see (
2.8
))

\langle{\nabla h},{\nabla\varphi}\rangle=\langle{\nabla f},{\nabla\varphi}\rangle\quad\mathfrak{m}{\rm-a.e.\ in}\ \ X.

By the definition of
{\bf\Delta}f
(see Definition
2
), we obtain

(3.1)

\begin{split}{\bf\Delta}f(\varphi)&=-\int_{\Omega}\langle{\nabla f},{\nabla\varphi}\rangle d\mathfrak{m}=-\int_{X}\langle{\nabla f},{\nabla\varphi}\rangle d\mathfrak{m}\\
&=-\int_{X}\langle{\nabla h},{\nabla\varphi}\rangle d\mathfrak{m}.\end{split}

Since
h\in W^{1,2}(X)
and
\varphi\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}(X)\subset D(\Delta)
, we have

\int_{X}h\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}=-\int_{X}\langle{\nabla h},{\nabla\varphi}\rangle d\mathfrak{m}.

Together this with (
3.1
) and the facts that
f=h
on
{\rm supp}(\varphi)
and that
\Delta\varphi(x)=0
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\not\in{\rm supp}(\varphi)
(see Lemma
2
), we conclude that

\begin{split}{\bf\Delta}f(\varphi)&=\int_{X}h\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}=\int_{{\rm supp}(\varphi)}h\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}\\
&=\int_{{\rm supp}(\varphi)}f\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}=\int_{\Omega}f\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}=\Delta_{\mathscr{D},\Omega}f(\varphi).\end{split}

The proof is finished.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For each
p\in[1,\infty)
, if
f\in D(\Delta^{(p)})
, then
\Delta_{\mathscr{D}}f=\Delta^{(p)}f
in the sense of distributions on
X
. Here

\Delta^{(p)}
is the generator of
P_{t}
as semi-group acting on
L^{p}(X)
. That is,

\begin{split}D(\Delta^{(p)})&:=\left\{f\in L^{p}(X)\Big|\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{P_{t}f-f}{t}\ {\rm exists\ in}\ L^{p}\right\}\ \quad{\rm and}\\
\Delta^{(p)}f&:=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{P_{t}f-f}{t}.\end{split}

Proof.

Take any
\varphi\in{\rm Test}_{c}^{\infty}(X)
. Since
\Delta\varphi\in L^{2}(X)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
, we have

\left\|\frac{P_{t}\varphi-\varphi}{t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\leqslant\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\|P_{s}(\Delta\varphi)\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}ds\leqslant\|\Delta\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(X)},

where we have used the contraction of
P_{s}
on
L^{\infty}(X)
for any
s>0
. Notice that
\frac{P_{t}\varphi-\varphi}{t}\to\Delta\varphi
in
L^{2}
as
t\to 0^{+}
implies that there exist a sequence
t_{j}\to 0^{+}
such that
\frac{P_{t_{j}}\varphi(x)-\varphi(x)}{t_{j}}\to\Delta\varphi(x)
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in X
. Since
f\in L^{p}(X)
, the dominated converging theorem implies that

(3.2)

\int_{X}f\frac{P_{t_{j}}\varphi-\varphi}{t_{j}}d\mathfrak{m}\ \to\ \int_{X}f\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m},\quad{\rm as}\ \ t_{j}\to 0^{+}.

On the other hand, recalling that
f\in D(\Delta^{(p)})
states
\frac{P_{t}f-f}{t}\to\Delta^{(p)}f
in
L^{p}(X)
as
t\to 0^{+}
, and by using
p_{t}(x,y)
is symmetric, we get

\int_{X}f\frac{P_{t}\varphi-\varphi}{t}d\mathfrak{m}=\int_{X}\frac{P_{t}f-f}{t}\varphi d\mathfrak{m}\ \to\ \int_{X}\Delta^{(p)}f\cdot\varphi d\mathfrak{m}\quad{\rm as}\ \ t\to 0^{+}.

Therefore, by combining with (
3.2
), we conclude that

\int_{X}f\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}=\int_{X}\Delta^{(p)}f\cdot\varphi d\mathfrak{m},\quad\forall\varphi\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}_{c}(X).

This is
\Delta_{\mathscr{D}}f=\Delta^{(p)}f
in the sense of distributions.
∎

We can now show the following special case of Weyl’s lemma for
L^{1}
functions with compact support, which is the core of this paper.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
v\in L^{1}(X)
such that
v(x)=0
a.e.
x\in X\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})
for some ball
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset X.
Suppose that
\Delta_{\mathscr{D}}v(\varphi)=0
for any nonnegative
\varphi\in\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}_{c}(X)
with
{\rm supp}(\varphi)\subset B_{R/2}(x_{0})
.
Then
v\in Lip\big(B_{R/8}(x_{0})\big)
.

Proof.

Let
p_{t}(x,y)
be the heat kernel on
X
. For any
t>0
, we set

v_{t}(x):=P_{t}v(x)=\int_{X}p_{t}(x,y)v(y)d\mathfrak{m},\quad\forall t>0.

Claim 1: For each
t\in(0,R^{2})
, we have
v_{t}\in L^{\infty}(X)\cap W^{1,2}(X)
.

Fix any
x\in X
. Since
v(y)=0
for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
y\in X\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})
, by the upper bound of heat kernel in (
2.3
), we have for any
x\in X
that

(3.3)

|v_{t}(x)|\leqslant\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{C_{1}|v(y)|}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{t}}(y))}d\mathfrak{m}(y)\leqslant\frac{C_{1}}{\inf_{y\in B_{R}(x_{0})}\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{t}}(y))}\cdot\|v\|_{L^{1}(X)}.

Here and in the following, we denote
C_{1},C_{2},\cdots
for varying constants depending only on
K,N
, and
R
.
The local measure doubling property (
2.1
) implies for
t<R^{2}

\inf_{y\in B_{R}(x_{0})}\mathfrak{m}(B_{\sqrt{t}}(y))\geqslant\inf_{y\in B_{R}(x_{0})}C_{2}\mathfrak{m}(B_{2R}(y))\cdot\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{2R}\Big)^{N}\geqslant C_{3}\mathfrak{m}(B_{R}(x_{0}))\cdot t^{N/2}.

By combining this with (
3.3
), we get
v_{t}\in L^{\infty}(X)
for any
t\in(0,R^{2}).

Since
P_{t}
is contraction on
L^{1}
, we have

. By combining with

Claim 2: There exists a constant

such that for any
t\in(0,R^{2})
it holds

(3.4)

\int_{B_{R/4}(x_{0})}\langle{\nabla v_{t}},{\nabla\varphi}\rangle d\mathfrak{m}\leqslant c\int_{B_{R/4}(x_{0})}|\varphi|d\mathfrak{m},\quad\forall\varphi\in Lip_{c}\big(B_{R/4}(x_{0})\big).

Take any nonnegative
\varphi\in\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}_{c}\big(B_{R/4}(x_{0})\big)
. Since
\varphi\in D(\Delta)
and
v_{t}\in W^{1,2}(X)
(from
Claim 1
), by using (

) and
\Delta(P_{t}\varphi)=P_{t}(\Delta\varphi)

(3.5)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{R/4}(x_{0})}\langle{\nabla v_{t}},{\nabla\varphi}\rangle d\mathfrak{m}&=\int_{X}\langle{\nabla v_{t}},{\nabla\varphi}\rangle d\mathfrak{m}=-\int_{X}v_{t}\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}\\
&=-\int_{X}vP_{t}(\Delta\varphi)d\mathfrak{m}=-\int_{X}v\Delta(P_{t}\varphi)d\mathfrak{m}.\end{split}

According to Lemma
2
, there exist two cut-off functions

in
{\rm Test}^{\infty}(X)
,

, such that:

(i)

and
\eta_{1}\equiv 1

where

.

Next we want to estimate

Since

where in the second inequality we have used (

Similarly, by (

for almost every

4.
Regularity of very weak solutions for Poisson equations

Let
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
be an
RCD(K,N)
space for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,+\infty)
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open domain. A function
u\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\Omega)
is called a
very weakly subsolution (resp. supersolution)
of Poisson equation
\Delta_{\mathscr{D},\Omega}u=f
on
\Omega
for some
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
if

(4.1)

\int_{\Omega}u\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}\geqslant\ ({\rm resp}.\leqslant)\ \int_{\Omega}f\varphi d\mathfrak{m},\qquad\forall\varphi\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)\ {\rm and}\ \varphi\geqslant 0.

A function
u\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\Omega)
is called a
very weakly solution
of
\Delta_{\mathscr{D},\Omega}u=f
on
\Omega
if it is both very weakly subsolution and supersolution.

Next we will show Corollary
1
.

Proof of Corollary
1
.

Take any ball
B:=B_{R}(x)\subset\subset\Omega
. Since
f\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, we have
f\in L^{2}(B)
. Let
w
be the solution of the
relaxed Dirichlet problem (see
[
7
, Theorem 7.12]
for the solvability):

\begin{cases}{\bf\Delta}w=f\cdot\mathfrak{m}\quad{\rm on}\ \ B,\\
w\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B).\end{cases}

By Lemma
3
, we get that
\Delta_{\mathscr{D},B}w={\bf\Delta}w
, i.e.,

\int_{B}w\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}={\bf\Delta}w(\varphi)=\int_{B}f\varphi d\mathfrak{m},\quad\ \forall\varphi\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}_{c}(B)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \varphi\geqslant 0.

By combining with (
1.4
) and Lemma
2
, we have

\int_{B}(u-w)\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}=0,\quad\ \forall\varphi\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}_{c}(B)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \varphi\geqslant 0.

Therefore the function
u-w\in L^{1}(B)
is a very weak harmonic function on
B
. From Theorem
1.3
, we know that
u-w\in Lip_{\rm loc}(B)
. It follows
u=(u-w)+w\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(B).
The proof is finished.
∎

5.
The Liouville property for
L^{1}
(sub)harmonic functions

In this section, we will prove the Liouville-type theorem for
L^{1}
subharmonic/harmonic functions on
RCD(K,N)
space, which is an extension of Peter Li’s result in
[
31
]
. Let
(X,d,\mathfrak{m})
be an
RCD(K,N)
space with
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,+\infty)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
u
and
f
be in
L^{1}(X)
. Suppose that
u
is a very weakly subsolution of Poisson equation
\Delta_{\mathscr{D}}u=f
on
X
. Then

(5.1)

\int_{X}u\Delta hd\mathfrak{m}\geqslant\int_{X}fhd\mathfrak{m},\quad\ \forall h\in\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X)\ {\rm and}\ h\geqslant 0.

Proof.

If
X
is compact, then we have
\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}_{c}(X)=\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X)
. This assertion follows from the Definition
4
with
\Omega=X
.

Suppose that
X
is noncompact. Take any
h\in\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X)
and
h\geqslant 0
.
For each
R>1
, by using Lemma
2
(with
r_{0}=1
therein), we have a cut-off function
\eta:X\to[0,1]
in
{\rm Test}^{\infty}(X)
such that
\eta\equiv 1
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
,
\eta\equiv 0
outside
B_{2R}(x_{0})
and

(5.2)

|\nabla\eta|+|\Delta\eta|\leqslant\frac{C_{K,N}}{R}\leqslant C_{K,N}\quad\mathfrak{m}{-a.e.\ in}\ X,

where the constant
C_{K,N}
is independent of
R
.
We set
\varphi:=h\eta
, and then
\varphi\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}_{c}(X)
. Since
\varphi=h
on
B_{R}(x_{0})
, from Lemma
2
, we have

(5.3)

\Delta\varphi(x)=\Delta h(x)\quad{\rm for}\ \mathfrak{m}{\rm-a.e.}\ x\in B_{R}(x_{0}).

Since
h,\eta\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}(X)
, by using Leibniz rule (see
[
20
, Proposition 5.2.3]
) to
\varphi=h\eta
, we get that for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in X\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})
,

\begin{split}|\Delta\varphi|(x)&\leqslant h(x)|\Delta\eta|(x)+2|\langle{\nabla h},{\nabla\eta}\rangle|(x)+\eta(x)|\Delta h|(x)\\
&\leqslant 2C_{K,N}\big(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}+\||\nabla h|\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\big)+\|\Delta h\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}:=c_{K,N,h},\end{split}

where we have used (
5.2
).
By combining this with (
5.3
), we get

\begin{split}\int_{X}u\Delta hd\mathfrak{m}-\int_{X}u\Delta\varphi d\mathfrak{m}&=\int_{X\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}u(\Delta h-\Delta\varphi)d\mathfrak{m}\\
&\geqslant-c^{\prime}_{K,N,h}\cdot\|u\|_{L^{1}(X\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})},\end{split}

where
c^{\prime}_{K,N,h}:=c_{K,N,h}+\|\Delta h\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}
. Since
\varphi=h
on
B_{R}(x_{0})
, we have

\int_{X}f\varphi d\mathfrak{m}-\int_{X}fhd\mathfrak{m}\geqslant-\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{1}(X\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}.

From the combination of these two inequalities and (
4.1
) with
\Omega=X
(noticing that
\varphi\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}_{c}(X)
), we get

\int_{X}u\Delta hd\mathfrak{m}\geqslant\int_{X}fhd\mathfrak{m}-c^{\prime}_{K,N,h}\cdot\|u\|_{L^{1}(X\setminus B_{R}(x_{0}))}-\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{1}(X\setminus B_{R}(x_{0}))}.

Letting
R\to+\infty
, it follows that (
5.1
), since
u\in L^{1}(X)
and
h\in L^{1}(X)
.
∎

To show the
L^{1}
-Liouville property, we need the following proposition.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
u\in L^{1}(X)
be a very weakly subharmonic (or superharmonic) function on
X
. Then it is a very weak harmonic function on
X
. Furthermore,
u
is in
Lip_{\rm loc}(X)
and is a harmonic function on
X
, namely,

u\in D({\bf\Delta})
and
{\bf\Delta}u=0
on
X
.

Proof.

We first consider that
u
is a
L^{1}
very weakly subharmonic function on
X
.
Since by (
2.5
),
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p_{t}(x,\cdot)(y)\in{L^{\infty}(X)}
for each
t\in(0,\infty)
, we have for
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.
x\in X
that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P_{t}u(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p_{t}(x,y)u(y)d\mathfrak{m}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\Delta_{y}p_{t}(x,y)u(y)d\mathfrak{m}(y).

From Lemma
2
, we have
p_{t}(x,\cdot)\in\mathrm{Test}^{\infty}(X)
. Since
u
is a
L^{1}
very weakly subharmonic function on
X
, by using Lemma
5
, we conclude that
P_{t}u(x)
is a monotone nondecreasing function in
t
, for almost all
x\in X
.
On the other hand, By the stochastic completeness (
2.6
) and Fubini’s theorem, we get

\int_{X}P_{t}u(x)d\mathfrak{m}(x)=\int_{X}\int_{X}p_{t}(x,y)u(y)d\mathfrak{m}(y)d\mathfrak{m}(x)=\int_{X}u(x)\mathfrak{m}(x).

Therefore, we have

P_{t}u(x)=u(x)\quad\mathfrak{m}{\rm-a.e.}\ x\in X.

This implies
u\in D(\Delta^{(1)})
and
\Delta^{(1)}u=0
, since
u\in L^{1}(X)
. From Lemma
3
, we know that
\Delta_{\mathscr{D}}u=0
on
X
in the sense of distributions. That is,
u
is a very weak harmonic function on
X
.

On the other hand, if
u
be a
L^{1}
very weakly superharmonic function on
X
, then
-u
is a very weakly subharmonic, and hence
-u
is a very weak harmonic function on
X
.

Now we want to show the second statement. From the first one,
u
is very weak harmonic function on
X
.
By applying Theorem
1.3
, we get that
u\in Lip_{\rm loc}(X)
. Hence, by Lemma
3
, we get that
u\in D({\bf\Delta})
and
{\bf\Delta}u(\varphi)=0
for any
\varphi\in{\rm Test}^{\infty}_{c}(X)
. Since
{\rm Test}^{\infty}_{c}(X)
is dense in
W^{1,2}_{0}(X)
(see Lemma
3
), we conclude that
u
is a harmonic function on
X
.
∎

Now we are in the position to show Corollary
1

Proof of Corollary
1
.

Let
u
be a
L^{1}
very weakly subharmonic function on
X
. From Proposition
5
, we know in fact that
u
is in
Lip_{\rm loc}(X)
and is a harmonic function on
X
.

We consider the function
u_{1}:=\min\{u,a\}
for arbitrary

. Since

is harmonic, according to
[
45
, Lemma 2]
, we have that
u_{1}
is superharmonic. In particular, it is very weakly superharmonic on
X
(by Lemma
3
). Noticing that
u_{1}\in L^{1}(X)
, by using Proposition

again, we get that
u_{1}
is again harmonic on
X
. We further put
u_{2}:=\max\{u_{1},-a\}
. Similar, from the harmonicity of
u_{1}
,
[
45
, Lemma 2]
implies that
u_{2}
is subharmonic on
X
; and then again, Proposition

yields that
u_{2}
is also harmonic on
X
. From
[
45
, Lemma 2]
, we know that
|u_{2}|
is a nonnegative subharmonic function on

. Since

, we get that

.
According to
[
45
, Theorem 1]
, we now conclude that

is a constant. It follows from the arbitrariness of

that

is a constant. The proof of Corollary

is finished.
∎

6.
A Remark on gradient estimates for solutions of elliptic equations

In this section, we will show Theorem
1.8
.

We first deal with the harmonic functions on
RCD(0,N)
cones. We recall
that if
N\geqslant 2
and if
(\Sigma,d_{\Sigma},\mathfrak{m}_{\Sigma})
is a metric measure space, then the cone
Cone^{N}_{0}(\Sigma):=(C,d_{c},\mathfrak{m}_{c})
is
the metric measure space given by the warped product metric
\Sigma\times_{r^{N-1}}\mathbb{R}
and
d\mathfrak{m}_{c}(r,\xi):=r^{N-1}drd\mathfrak{m}_{\Sigma}(\xi)
.
It was proved
[
29
]
that
Cone^{N}_{0}(\Sigma)
is an
RCD(0,N)
space if and only if
(\Sigma,d_{\Sigma},\mathfrak{m}_{\Sigma})
is an
RCD(N-2,N-1)
space.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
(C,d_{c},\mathfrak{m}_{c})
be an
RCD(0,N)
cone with vertex
o
, over
(\Sigma,d_{\Sigma},\mathfrak{m}_{\Sigma})
. Suppose that
u\in W^{1,2}(B_{R}(o))
is a (weakly) harmonic function, then

(6.1)

\fint_{B_{r_{1}}(o)}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mathfrak{m}_{c}\leqslant\fint_{B_{r_{2}}(o)}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mathfrak{m}_{c}

for all
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<R
.

Proof.

According to
[
23
, Theorem 3.1]
, there exist
\{c_{i}\}_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that

u(r,\xi)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}c_{i}r^{\alpha_{i}}\varphi_{i}(\xi),

where
\varphi_{i}(\xi)
is an eigenfunction of
-\Delta_{\Sigma}
with respect to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
, and
\alpha_{i}>0
are given by
\lambda_{i}=\alpha_{i}(N+\alpha_{i}-2)
.
From
[
30
,
27
]
, we have the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}\geqslant N-1
. It follows
\alpha_{i}\geqslant 1
for all
i\geqslant 1.

A direct calculation (see
[
13
, Eq. (2.5)]
) implies that

\frac{1}{r^{N}}\int_{B_{r}(o)}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mathfrak{m}_{c}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c^{2}_{i}\alpha_{i}\cdot r^{2\alpha_{i}-2}.

The desired monotonicity (
6.1
) follows from this and the fact
\mathfrak{m}_{c}(B_{r}(o))=\frac{r^{N}}{N}\cdot\mathfrak{m}_{\Sigma}(\Sigma).

∎

Let
n\geqslant 3
. Let
u\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(B_{1}(0))
be a weak solution of elliptic equations of divergence form
{\rm div}(A(x)Du)=0,

where the matrix of coefficients
A(x)=(a^{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
is symmetric, bounded measurable and satisfies (
1.6
).

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

According to the assumption of
A
in Theorem
1.8
,
there exists a conical coefficient of nonnegative curvature
\overline{A}=(\overline{a}^{ij})
such that (
1.8
) holds.
Since
\omega_{A,\overline{A}}(r)\to 0
as
r\to 0
, there is
r_{1}>0
such that

\|\overline{a}^{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r_{1}}(0))}\leqslant 2\Lambda,\qquad\frac{\lambda}{2}|\xi|^{2}\leqslant\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{a}^{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j},\quad{\rm a.e.}\ x\in B_{r_{1}}(0),\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

It is easy to check that the the distance
d_{\overline{g}}
induced from the Riemannian metric
\overline{g}_{ij}
in (
1.7
) is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to the Euclidean metric on
B_{r_{1}}(0)
. The bi-Lipschitz constant depends only on
\lambda
and
\Lambda
.

We denote by
B^{\overline{g}}_{r}(0)
the ball with respect the metric
d_{\overline{g}}
, for any
r>0
.
Then there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(\lambda,\Lambda)>1
such that

(6.2)

B_{r/C_{1}}(0)\subset B_{r}^{\overline{g}}(0)\subset B_{C_{1}r}(0),\qquad\forall r>0.

The bi-Lipschitz equivalence between
d_{\overline{g}}
and the Euclidean distance implies that

W^{1,2}(B_{r}^{\overline{g}}(0),d_{\overline{g}},{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}})=W^{1,2}(B_{r}(0))

for any
r\in(0,r_{1}/C_{1})
, where the Riemannian measure

(6.3)

{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}=\sqrt{G}\cdot\mathcal{L}^{n},\qquad G:={\rm det}(\overline{g}_{ij}),

and
\mathcal{L}^{n}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
From (
1.7
), we have

(6.4)

G=\left({\rm det}{(\overline{a}^{ij})}\right)^{\frac{2}{n-2}},\qquad\overline{a}^{ij}=\overline{g}^{ij}\cdot\sqrt{G}

for all
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n.

Denote
B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell}:=B^{\overline{g}}_{\rho^{\ell}}(0),
where
\rho:=1/C_{1}<1
. Let
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\ell_{0}>2
, such that
\rho^{\ell_{0}}<r_{1}/C_{1}
. For each
\ell=\ell_{0},\ell_{0}+1,\cdots,
let
u_{\ell}
be the solution
of Dirichlet problem

\partial_{i}\left(\overline{a}^{ij}\partial u_{\ell}\right)=0\quad{\rm with}\quad u-u_{\ell}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell})\ \ \left(=W^{1,2}_{0}(B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell},d_{\overline{g}},{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}})\right).

Noticing that
\partial_{i}(a^{ij}\partial_{j}u)=0
in the weak sense and letting
v_{\ell}=u-u_{\ell}
, we have

\int_{B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell}}\overline{a}^{ij}\partial_{j}v_{\ell}\partial_{i}v_{\ell}d\mathcal{L}^{n}=\int_{B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell}}\overline{a}^{ij}\partial_{j}u\partial_{i}v_{\ell}d\mathcal{L}^{n}=\int_{B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell}}(\overline{a}^{ij}-a^{ij})\partial_{j}u\partial_{i}v_{\ell}d\mathcal{L}^{n}.

Together this, (
6.2
), (
6.3
), (
6.4
) and (
1.8
), we get

\begin{split}\|\nabla_{\overline{g}}v_{\ell}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}},{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}})}&=\int_{B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell}}\overline{g}^{ij}\partial_{j}v_{\ell}\partial_{i}v_{\ell}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}=\int_{B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell}}\overline{a}^{ij}\partial_{j}v_{\ell}\partial_{i}v_{\ell}d\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&\leqslant C_{2}\cdot\omega_{A,\overline{A}}(C_{1}\rho^{\ell})\cdot\|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}},{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}})}\cdot\|\nabla_{\overline{g}}v_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}},{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}})},\end{split}

where the constant
C_{2}
depends only on
\lambda,\Lambda
, and we have used that
\|\partial_{j}u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}},\mathcal{L}^{n})}\leqslant C_{\lambda,\Lambda}\|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}},{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}})}
for some constant
C_{\lambda,\Lambda}>0
. Hence, for each
\ell=\ell_{0},\ell_{0}+1,\cdots,
we have (noticing that
C_{1}=\rho^{-1}
)

(6.5)

\|\nabla_{\overline{g}}v_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}},{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}})}\leqslant C_{2}\cdot\omega_{A,\overline{A}}(\rho^{\ell-1})\cdot\|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}},{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}})}.

On the other hand, we claim that, for each
\ell=\ell_{0},\ell_{0}+1,\cdots,

u_{\ell}
is a (weakly) harmonic function on
B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell}
in metric measure space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},d_{\overline{g}},{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}})
. Indeed, for any
\varphi\in Lip_{0}(B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell})
, we have

\int_{B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell}}\langle{\nabla_{\overline{g}}u_{\ell}},{\nabla_{\overline{g}}\varphi}\rangle d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}=\int_{B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell}}\overline{a}^{ij}\partial_{j}u_{\ell}\partial_{i}\varphi d\mathcal{L}^{n}=0.

Since
(\mathbb{R}^{n},d_{\overline{g}})
has nonnegative curvature in the sense of Alexandrov, the metric measure space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},d_{\overline{g}},{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}})
is an
RCD(0,n)
cone (see
[
40
,
51
]
). From Lemma
6
, we have

\fint_{B_{\ell+1}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u_{\ell}|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\leqslant\fint_{B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u_{\ell}|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}

for each
\ell=\ell_{0},\ell_{0}+1,\cdots.
By combining with (
6.5
) and
u=u_{\ell}+v_{\ell}
on
B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell}
, we get

\begin{split}&\left(\fint_{B_{\ell+1}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\right)^{1/2}\\
\leqslant&\left(\fint_{B_{\ell+1}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u_{\ell}|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\fint_{B_{\ell+1}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}v_{\ell}|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\right)^{1/2}\\
\leqslant&\left(\fint_{B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u_{\ell}|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}(B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell})}{{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}(B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell+1})}\cdot\left(\fint_{B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}v_{\ell}|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\right)^{1/2}\\
\leqslant&\left(\fint_{B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{C_{2}}{\rho^{n}}\cdot\omega_{A,\overline{A}}(\rho^{\ell-1})\left(\fint_{B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\right)^{1/2}\\
\leqslant&\left(1+C_{3}\omega_{A,\overline{A}}(\rho^{\ell-1})\right)\left(\fint_{B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\right)^{1/2},\end{split}

for any
\ell=\ell_{0},\ell_{0}+1,\cdots,
where we have used the
\|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}},{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}})}\leqslant\|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}},{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}})}
by the property that
u_{\ell}
is the energy minimizing function, and the doubling property of
{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}
(In fact
\frac{{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}(B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell})}{{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}(B^{\overline{g}}_{\ell+1})}=\rho^{-n}
).
By the elementary inequality
\ln(1+s)\leqslant s
for
s>0
, we have

\ln\left(\fint_{B_{\ell+1}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\right)\leqslant\ln\left(\fint_{B_{\ell}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\right)+2C_{3}\omega_{A,\overline{A}}(\rho^{\ell-1})

for any
\ell=\ell_{0}.\ell_{0}+1,\cdots.
Therefore, we have

\limsup_{\ell\to+\infty}\ln\left(\fint_{B_{\ell+1}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\right)\leqslant\ln\left(\fint_{B_{\ell_{0}}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\right)+2C_{3}\sum_{\ell=\ell_{0}}^{\infty}\omega_{A,\overline{A}}(\rho^{\ell-1}).

Since
\omega_{A,\overline{A}}(t)
is nondecreasing in
t>0
, we have

\sum_{\ell=\ell_{0}-1}^{\infty}\omega_{A,\overline{A}}(\rho^{\ell})\leqslant\sum_{\ell=\ell_{0}-1}^{\infty}\int_{\rho^{\ell}}^{\rho^{\ell-1}}\frac{\omega_{A,\overline{A}}(t)}{\rho^{\ell-1}-\rho^{\ell}}\cdot\frac{\rho^{\ell-1}}{t}dt=\frac{1}{1-\rho}\int_{0}^{\rho^{\ell_{0}-2}}\frac{\omega_{A,\overline{A}}(t)}{t}dt.

Therefore, we conclude that

\limsup_{\ell\to+\infty}\left(\fint_{B_{\ell+1}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\right)\leqslant\left(\fint_{B_{\ell_{0}}^{\overline{g}}(0)}|\nabla_{\overline{g}}u|^{2}d{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}\right)\cdot\exp\left(\frac{2C_{3}}{1-\rho}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega_{A,\overline{A}}(t)}{t}dt\right).

It follows the desired estimate (
1.9
), by combining the doubling property of
{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}
and (
6.2
)-(
6.4
). The proof is finished.
∎

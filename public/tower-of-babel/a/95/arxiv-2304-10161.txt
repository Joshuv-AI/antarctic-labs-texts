Erratum and addendum to the paper: Abundance for Kähler threefolds
Frédéric Campana, Andreas Höring, Thomas Peternell

Abstract
. In this text we signal a serious gap in the proof of the main theorem of our paper and explain which parts of the statement remain valid. In fact, the main theorem remains valid unless possibly the variety does not admit positive-dimensional subvarieties through a very general point and is not bimeromorphic to a quotient of a torus. This latter case would be ruled out by a Chern class inequality which holds in the algebraic case but is still unknown in the Kähler setting.



The proof of Theorem 8.2 in
[
CHP16
]
contains a gap. We explain here how to fix this in most cases, the only remaining case being "simple" threefolds
X
of Kodaira dimension
0
, hence through a very general point of
X
there is no proper positive-dimensional compact analytic subvariety.
In this case it is expected that
X
is bimeromorphic to a torus quotient. We explain further how to reduce Theorem 8.2 to an orbifold Chern class inequality of Bogomolov-Gieseker type which in the algebraic setting is well-known and proved by reducing to dimension
2
by taking hyperplane sections. For recent developments towards the inequality in the Kähler case, we refer to
[
JC23
]
.

The issue is concerned with the proof of Equality (33). To be precise, we are in the following situation.
Let
X
be a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial compact Kähler threefold such that
(X,0)
is klt. Further,
X
carries a
divisior
D\in|mK_{X}|
with the following properties.

a)

Set
B:={\rm Supp}D
. Then
(X,B)
is lc and
X\setminus B
has terminal singularities.

b)

K_{X}+B
is nef with
\nu(K_{X}+B)=2
; further,
\kappa(X)=\kappa(K_{X}+B)
.

c)

For all irreducible components
T\subset B
, we have
(K_{X}+B)_{T}\neq 0
.

d)

(K_{X}+B)\cdot K_{X}^{2}\geq 0
.

Then we claim that
\kappa(X)\geq 1
.

To prove this, we consider a terminal modification
\mu:X^{\prime}\to X
, set
L:=m(K_{X}+B)
;
L^{\prime}=\mu^{*}(L)

and things come down to prove Equality (33) in
[
CHP16
]
, stating

(1)

L^{\prime}\cdot(K_{X^{\prime}}^{2}+c_{2}(X^{\prime}))\geq 0.

To prove Equation (
1
), we claimed that we may assume that
X
has canonical singularities in codimension two. This however is not true in general.
Once Equation (
1
) is settled (which holds when

X
has canonical singularities), the proof given in
[
CHP16
]
is complete.
Thus we have to treat the case that
X
has (possibly) non-canonical singularities. In this case
X
has quotient singularities in codimension two.
Instead of taking a terminalization, we let
\mu:X^{\prime}\to X
be a desingularization.
We proceed following the lines of
[
Kwc92
, chapter14]
. As in
[
Kwc92
, Lemma 14.3.1]
, things come down to prove

(2)

L\cdot(K_{X}^{2}+\hat{c}_{2}(X))\geq 0.

Since

L\cdot(K_{X}+B)\cdot B=0,

this comes
by
[
Kwc92
, Lemma 14.3.2]
down to show

(3)

L\cdot\hat{c}_{2}(\Omega_{X}(\log B))\geq 0.

To prove Equation
3
, we proceed as in
[
CHP16
]
in an orbifold context.
We refer to
[
GK20
]
for the notion of orbifold Chern classes in an analytic context.
In particular, we
may define the intersection
\omega\cdot\hat{c}_{2}({\mathcal{F}})
for a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
and a reflexive sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
which is a
Q-sheaf on
X\setminus S
:
the orbifold Chern class
\hat{c}_{2}({\mathcal{F}})\in H^{4}(X\setminus S,\mathbb{R})
extends uniquely to a class
\gamma
on
X
, and we simply define

\omega\cdot\hat{c}_{2}({\mathcal{F}}):=[\omega]\cdot\gamma,

where
[\omega]\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
is the class of
\omega
.

In a first step, we need the following orbifold version of
[
CHP16
, Theorem 7.1]
.

1
.

Conjecture.

Let
X
be a normal compact Kähler threefold with only isolated non-quotient singularities,
and let
\alpha
be a Kähler class on
X
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be an
\alpha-
stable non-zero torsion-free coherent
Q
-sheaf on
X
.
Then we have

\alpha\cdot\hat{c}_{2}({\mathcal{F}})\geq\big(\frac{r-1}{2r}\big)\alpha\cdot c_{1}^{2}({\mathcal{F}}).

Of course, Conjecture
1
should be true in all dimensions under the assumption that
X
has only quotient singularities in codimension
2
.
If
X
is projective, see
[
Kwc92
, chap.10]
for the surface case and
[
GKPT19
, Thm.6.1]

If Conjecture
1
holds for klt spaces, it yields the following orbifold version of

[
CHP16
, Theorem 7.2]
.

2
.

Theorem.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler threefold with isolated non-quotient singularities, and let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a non-zero reflexive coherent
Q
-sheaf
on
X
such that
\det{\mathcal{F}}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. Suppose that there exists a pseudoeffective class
P\in N^{1}(X)
such that

L:=c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})+P

is a nef class. Suppose furthermore that for all
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
the sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
is
(L+\varepsilon\omega)
-generically nef. Then we have

L\cdot\hat{c}_{2}({\mathcal{F}})\geq\frac{1}{2}(L\cdot c_{1}^{2}({\mathcal{F}})-L^{3}).

In particular, if
L\cdot c_{1}^{2}({\mathcal{F}})\geq 0
and
L^{3}=0
, then

(4)

L\cdot\hat{c}_{2}({\mathcal{F}})\geq 0.

We aim to apply Theorem
2
for the reflexive sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}=\Omega_{X}(\log B)
to prove Equation
3
. Therefore, we need to check generic nefness of
{\mathcal{F}}
which
follows from the generic nefness of
\Omega_{X}
.
This is done by the following adaption of
[
CHP16
, Proposition 8.2]
.

3
.

Proposition.

Let
X
be a normal non-algebraic compact Kähler space of dimension
n
with klt singularities.
Suppose that
\kappa(X)\geq 0
.
Then
\Omega_{X}
is generically nef with respect to any nef class
\alpha
, i.e. for every torsion-free quotient sheaf

\Omega_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow 0,

we have
\alpha^{n-1}\cdot c_{1}(\mathcal{Q})\geq 0
.

Proof.

We proceed as in
[
CHP16
]
. Let
\pi:\hat{X}\to X
be a desingularization. Using the same notations as in the proof of
[
CHP16
, Proposition 8.2]
,
we need to show that

\pi^{*}(\alpha)^{n-1}\cdot c_{1}(\hat{Q})\geq 0.

To do this, we apply Enoki’s theorem
[
Eno88
, Theorem 1.4]
, and need to write as
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors,

K_{\hat{X}}=L+D

with
L
nef and
D
effective.
Setting
L=0
, we need to show that
\kappa(\hat{X})\geq 0
. Since
X
is klt, this is not automatic from
\kappa(X)=\kappa(K_{X})\geq 0
.
Assuming to the contrary that
\kappa(\hat{X})=-\infty
. Then by
[
HP16
]
,
\hat{X}
is uniruled. Since
\hat{X}
is not algebraic,
\hat{X}
cannot be rationally connected, hence we have
a rational almost holomorphic (MRC) quotient

\hat{f}:\hat{X}\dasharrow\hat{S}

with
\dim\hat{S}=1,2
. Since
H^{2}(\hat{X},{\mathcal{O}}_{\hat{X}})\neq 0
, we must have
\dim\hat{S}=2
. Since
\hat{X}
is not algebraic, the exceptional set of
\pi
cannot surject onto
\hat{S}
and
therefore
\hat{f}
descends to an almost holomorphic map
f:X\dasharrow\hat{S}
whose general fiber is a smooth rational curve. This contradicts
\kappa(K_{X})\geq 0
.

∎

Proof of Theorem 8.2 in the non-simple case.

In the rest of the paper we point out how directly prove Theorem 8.2 in
[
CHP16
]
- without using a Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality - in case
X
has algebraic dimension
a(X)\geq 1
or carries a family of curves covering
X
. For the theory of algebraic reductions, which we will use freely, we refer e.g. to
[
CP94
]
.
We will write
{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}(L)
.

1st case:
We assume that there exists an almost holomorphic elliptic fibration

f:X\dasharrow S,

over a smooth
Kähler surface. Since
f
cannot be a Moishezon morphism,
{\mathcal{L}}
must be numerically trivial on the general fiber of
f
.
We now consider the graph of the closure of the family of elliptic curves (fibers of
f
) in
X
and obtain a birational map
p:Z\to X
and an equidimensional morphism

q:Z\to T
, where
T
is the parameter space of the family (birational to
S
). We may assume
T
smooth and
Z
normal. The line bundle
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}=p^{*}({\mathcal{L}})

is
q
-numerically trivial, actually trivial on the general fiber of
q
since
H^{0}(X;{\mathcal{L}})\neq 0
. Using Zariski’s lemma
[
BHPVdV04
, III, Lemma 8.2]
we obtain that (up to replacing
L
by a multiple) we have
{\mathcal{L}}\simeq q^{*}({\mathcal{G}})
with a line bundle
{\mathcal{G}}
on
T
.
Since
\nu(\tilde{\mathcal{L}})=2
,
{\mathcal{G}}
is big and nef. Thus
S
is Moishezon and
\kappa(\tilde{\mathcal{L}})=2
, hence
\kappa({\mathcal{L}})=2
.

Note that this first case settles in particular the case where the algebraic dimension

a(X)
is equal to two: in this case the algebraic reduction defines an elliptic fibration as above.

2nd case: assume that
a(X)=1
.
Here the algebraic reduction is a holomorphic fiber space

f:X\to C

over a smooth curve
C
. Let
X_{c}
be a general fiber of
f
, a klt surface, and
\hat{X}_{c}\to X_{c}
the minimal desingularization.
Since
X
is not uniruled and since
\kappa(\hat{X}_{c})\leq 0
for the general fiber of any algebraic reduction, we conclude
\kappa(\hat{X}_{c})=0
, hence
\kappa(X_{c})\geq 0
.
If the general fiber
X_{c}
is algebraic,
f
cannot have a generic multisection, hence
X_{c}
must be smooth and minimal. Thus
B_{|X_{c}}=0
and
{\mathcal{L}}|_{X_{c}}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{c}}
.
Since we may assume
h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes k})=1
for all
k\geq 1
, we have
f_{*}({\mathcal{L}})={\mathcal{O}}_{C}
(anyway we must have
C=\mathbb{P}_{1}
by
C_{3,1}
),
and thus
{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\sum a_{j}D_{j})
with
a_{j}>0
and
D_{j}
fiber components. Since
{\mathcal{L}}
is nef and numerically trivial on the general fibre, this implies
\nu({\mathcal{L}})=1
.
This contradicts our assumption
\nu({\mathcal{L}})=2
.

If the general fiber
X_{c}
is not algebraic, we have
L^{2}\cdot X_{c}=0
. In fact,

L_{|X_{c}}
cannot be big, otherwise
X
would be projective.
Then we see immediately that

K_{X_{c}}^{2}=K_{X_{c}}\cdot B_{|X_{c}}=B_{|X_{c}}^{2}=0.

Further,
K_{X_{c}}
must be nef, otherwise
X_{c}
has a minimal model whose canonical divisor is big. By abundance for surfaces,
K_{X_{c}}
is semiample.

If the Kodaira fibration defines an elliptic fibration on
X_{c}
we obtain a covering family of elliptic curves on
X
and conclude as in the previous case.

If
K_{X_{c}}
is torsion, the same holds for
{\mathcal{L}}_{|X_{c}}
.
Hence
f_{*}({\mathcal{L}}^{k})={\mathcal{O}}_{C}
for some
k
, and we conclude as before.

3rd case: assume that
a(X)=0
and
X
is not simple
. In this case
X
is Kummer, i.e., bimeromorphic to a quotient of a torus - and there is nothing to prove - or
X
has a covering family of elliptic curves, see
[
CP00
]
.
We consider again - as in the first case - the graph
p:Z\to X
. Since
a(X)=0
,
X
has only finitely many divisiors and therefore
p
is generically finite.
As before, we deduce
\kappa(p^{*}({\mathcal{L}}))=2
, hence
\kappa({\mathcal{L}})=2
.

∎

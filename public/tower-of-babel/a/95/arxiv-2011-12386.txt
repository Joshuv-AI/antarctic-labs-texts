Confinement studies in QCD with Dyson-Schwinger equations
Marco Frasca

Abstract
We provide a study of quantum chromodynamics with the technique of Dyson-Schwinger equations in differential form. In this way, we are able to approach the non-perturbative limit and recover, with some approximations, the ’t Hooft limit of the theory. Quark mass in the propagator term goes off-shell at low-energies signaling confinement. A condition for such occurrence in the theory is provided.



1
Introduction

Yang-Mills set of Dyson-Schwinger equations can be solved through a class of exact solutions of the 1-point function, similarly to the
\phi^{4}
theory
Frasca:2015yva
. Indeed, both theories can map each other. Adding quarks to the Lagrangian makes the theory no more amenable to an exact treatment. But, notwithstanding such a difficulty, full QCD can be treated with the identical approach through Dyson-Schwinger equations.

The technique we use is due to Bender, Milton and Savage
Bender:1999ek
. This method has the important advantage that the differential form of the Dyson-Schwinger equations is retained. This is especially useful when, as in our case, we have exact solutions for the 1-point and 2-point equations in the classical case.

Therefore, starting from the results for Yang-Mills theory without quarks, the set of Dyson-Schwinger equations for quantum chromodynamics (QCD) becomes amenable to a perturbative treatment in the strong coupling limit provided the ’t Hooft limit
N\rightarrow\infty,\ Ng^{2}=constant,\ Ng^{2}\gg 1
is taken.

Our main conclusion is that the low-energy limit of QCD yields a confining non-local Nambu-Jona-Lasinio approximation
Frasca:2019ysi
when the condition for confinement is assumed to have the quark propagator off-shell due to the behaviour of the mass function of quarks
Roberts:1994dr
,
Gribov:1998kb
. Therefore, no free quark is observable being not anymore a state of the theory. In this paper we will follow the derivation given in Ref.
Frasca:2019ysi
.

2
Bender-Milton-Savage method

The principal point in the Bender-Milton-Savage (BMS) technique is to derive the Dyson-Schwinger equations retaining their PDE form
Bender:1999ek
. In this way, vertexes are never introduced and there is no need to move to momenta space obtaining cumbersome integral expressions.

We consider the partition function of a given theory, e..g a scalar field theory to fix the ideas, given by

Z[j]=\int[D\phi]e^{iS(\phi)+i\int d^{4}xj(x)\phi(x)}.

(1)

For the 1P-function, it is

\left\langle\frac{\delta S}{\delta\phi(x)}\right\rangle=j(x)

(2)

being

\left\langle\ldots\right\rangle=\frac{\int[D\phi]\ldots e^{iS(\phi)+i\int d^{4}xj(x)\phi(x)}}{\int[D\phi]e^{iS(\phi)+i\int d^{4}xj(x)\phi(x)}}

(3)

Then, we set
j=0
. We derive this equation again with respect to
j
to get the equation for the 2P-function. We assume the following definition of the nP-functions

\langle\phi(x_{1})\phi(x_{2})\ldots\phi(x_{n})\rangle=\frac{\delta^{n}\ln(Z[j])}{\delta j(x_{1})\delta j(x_{2})\ldots\delta j(x_{n})}.

(4)

This will yield

\frac{\delta G_{k}(\ldots)}{\delta j(x)}=G_{k+1}(\ldots,x).

(5)

This procedure can be iterated to any desired order giving, in principle, all the hierarchy of the Dyson-Schwinger equations in PDE form
Frasca:2015yva
. This is advantageous when the solutions for 1P- and 2P-functions are known in the classical case.

4
’t Hooft limit

’t Hooft limit corresponds to solve the theory when
tHooft:1973alw

tHooft:1974pnl

Frasca:2013tma

we re-scale

\displaystyle(i\not{\partial}-{\hat{M}}_{q}^{i}){\hat{q}}_{1}^{i}(x)=0.

5
Non-local NJL approximation

From eq.(
4
) we can define the the self-energy

\displaystyle\Sigma(x,x)=g^{2}\int d^{4}y^{\prime}\Delta_{0}(x-y^{\prime})T^{a}\gamma^{\nu}\times

\displaystyle\sum_{k}{\bar{\hat{q}}}_{1}^{k}(y^{\prime})\gamma_{\nu}T^{a}{\hat{S}}^{ki}_{q}(y^{\prime}-x)

(27)

From this, we can introduce a non-local-Nambu-Jona-Lasinio model (nlNJL)
Frasca:2019ysi

(i\not{\partial}-m_{q}+\Sigma^{i}_{NJL}(x,x)){\hat{S}}^{ij}_{q}(x-y)=\delta_{ij}\delta^{4}(x-y)

(28)

being now the quark self-energy computed at the first iteration by the free quark propagator giving

\displaystyle\Sigma^{i}_{NJL}(x,x)=g^{2}\int d^{4}y^{\prime}\Delta_{0}(x-y^{\prime})T^{a}\gamma^{\nu}\times

\displaystyle\sum_{j}{\bar{\hat{q}}}_{0}^{j}(y^{\prime})\gamma_{\nu}T^{a}{\hat{S}}^{ji}_{0q}(y^{\prime}-x)

(29)

or, in momentum space,

\displaystyle\Sigma^{i}_{NJL}(p)=g^{2}\int\frac{d^{4}p_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\Delta_{0}(p_{1})T^{a}\gamma^{\nu}\times

\displaystyle\sum_{j}{\bar{\hat{q}}}_{0}^{j}(p)\gamma_{\nu}T^{a}{\hat{S}}^{ji}_{0q}(p_{1}-p).

(30)

This nlNJL-model gives rise, as usual, to a mass gap equation for quarks implying the formation of a condensate. This generally grants a pole in the quark propagator. Failing to find such a pole means that the quark propagator has no physical mass states and the quarks are confined
Roberts:1994dr
,
Gribov:1998kb
.
Then, at very low energies

M_{q}=m_{q}-{\rm Tr}\Sigma^{i}_{NJL}(0)

(31)

where the trace is over flavors, colors and spinor indexes. This yields

\displaystyle M_{q}=m_{q}+\frac{N_{f}(N^{2}-1)Ng^{2}}{2}\times

(32)

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\Delta_{0}(p)\frac{M_{q}}{p^{2}+M^{2}_{q}}

We consider the gluon propagator neglecting the mass shift, as this is generally small as shown in
Frasca:2017slg
,

\Delta_{0}(p)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{n}}{p^{2}+m_{n}^{2}}.

(33)

Therefore, we have to compute

\displaystyle M_{q}=m_{q}+\frac{N_{f}(N^{2}-1)Ng^{2}}{2}\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\times

(34)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{n}}{p^{2}+m_{n}^{2}}\frac{M_{q}}{p^{2}+M^{2}_{q}}.

The corresponding integral can be evaluated exactly when a cut-off
\Lambda
is used, as usual for Nambu-Jona-Lasinio models that are generally expected not to be renormalizable
Klevansky:1992qe
. This yields

\displaystyle M_{q}=m_{q}+\frac{N_{f}(N^{2}-1)Ng^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{n}M_{q}}{2(m_{n}^{2}-M_{q}^{2})}\times

\displaystyle\left[m_{n}^{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{n}^{2}}\right)-M_{q}^{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{M_{q}^{2}}\right)\right].

(35)

This equation is amenable to a numerical treatment provided that
M_{q}\geq m_{q}
and
M_{q}\ll\Lambda

\Lambda\approx 1\ {\rm GeV}

x=m_{0}/\Lambda
and
y=M_{q}/\Lambda
having set
m_{n}=(2n+1)m_{0}
. The mass gap

can be assumed to be that of the
\sigma

m_{0}=0.417\ {\rm GeV}

Then, the function to study is

\displaystyle y=\frac{m_{q}}{\Lambda}+\kappa\alpha_{s}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{n}y}{(2n+1)^{2}x^{2}-y^{2}}\times

\displaystyle\left[(2n+1)^{2}x^{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{(2n+1)^{2}x^{2}}\right)-y^{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{y^{2}}\right)\right].

From this, we derive the mass function

6
Conclusions

We have derived the set of Dyson-Schwinger equations, till to 2P-functions, for QCD with the Bender-Milton-Savage technique.
Then, we were able to solve them in the ’t Hooft limit. We recognized that the low-energy limit is given by a nonlocal-Nambu-Jona-Lasinio approximation.
Consequently, in the low-energy limit, we were able to show that the model is confining. A confinement condition was also obtained obtained.

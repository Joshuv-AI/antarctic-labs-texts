Learning optimal policies in potential Mean Field Games: Smoothed Policy Iteration algorithms
Qing Tang, Jiahao Song

Abstract
We introduce two Smoothed Policy Iteration algorithms ( SPI s) as rules for learning policies and methods for computing Nash equilibria in second order potential Mean Field Games (MFGs). Global convergence is proved if the coupling term in the MFG system satisfy the Lasry Lions monotonicity condition. Local convergence to a stable solution is proved for system which may have multiple solutions. The convergence analysis shows close connections between SPI s and the Fictitious Play algorithm, which has been widely studied in the MFG literature. Numerical simulation results based on finite difference schemes are presented to supplement the theoretical analysis.

Literature.
The forward backward structure of system (
1.1

[
1

2
]

[

2
]

[
11

[

[

is evaluated. With some quantitative assumptions, one can show that the policy iteration operator in
[
11
,
12
]
and its generalizations
[
35
]
are contractive and thereby obtain a linear rate of convergence.

Fictitious Play algorithm (

[
26
,
39
]

[

28

29

for the MFG PDE systems and in

,

for MFG reinforcement learning problems. Local convergence of

[

and the selection of equilibria with

Contributions.
Our main contributions are introducing Smoothed Policy Iteration algorithms (henceforth referred to as
SPI
s) for solving system (
1.1
). Our main assumptions on the Hamiltonian and the nonlocal couplings follow closely
[
10
,
14
]
. Some possible generalizations to MFG with local couplings are discussed, based on ideas from
[
17
]
. The main advantage of
SPI
s vis-à-vis
FP
is that they solve the MFG model in a single iteration loop, since the HJB equation at each stage is already linearized. A minor difference from
[
10
,
14
]
is that we prove all results with a learning rate corresponding to an average with increasingly large weights on more recent updates, rather than using simple average. For numerical methods, we use implicit finite difference schemes which are reminiscent of those from
[
1
,
2
]
. Following
[
11
]
, we use the Engquist-Osher flux for the
\rm{div}(\cdot)
term in the FPK equation in order to retain the adjoint structure to the discretized (and linearized) HJB equation. The key idea and difference from policy iteration in
[
11
,
12
]
is using some forms of weighted average policies
\bar{q}^{(n)}
or
\hat{q}^{(n)}
, in order to penalize deviation from the previous step and thereby stabilize the learning procedures. This methodology is also reflected in algorithms used for MFG reinforcement learning, e.g. TRPO
[
27
]
and online mirror descent
[
28
,
40
]
. For the main results we consider coupling terms which are globally Lipschitz, whereas the couplings are (local) uniformly bounded in
[
11
]
. Numerical results show that convergence of
SPI
s are sublinear. Importantly, convergences of
SPI
s are global, i.e. from any initial guesses. This fact is clear from theoretical analysis and supplemented by numerical examples.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
we give the main notation, functional space setting, assumptions on the data and some preliminary results. We propose two different

Generate the distribution from the current policy
. Solve

\left\{\begin{split}&\partial_{t}m^{(n)}-\sigma\Delta m^{(n)}-\text{div}(m^{(n)}\bar{q}^{(n)})=0,\qquad&\text{ in }Q,\\
&m^{(n)}(0,x)=m_{0}(x)&\text{ in }\mathbb{T}^{d}.\end{split}\right.

(3.1)

(ii)

Policy evaluation
. Solve

\left\{\begin{split}&-\partial_{t}u^{(n)}-\sigma\Delta u^{(n)}+\bar{q}^{(n)}\cdot\nabla u^{(n)}-L(x,\bar{q}^{(n)})=f[m^{(n)}](x)&\text{ in }Q,\\
&u^{(n)}(T,x)=g[m^{(n)}(T)](x)&\text{ in }\mathbb{T}^{d}.\end{split}\right.

(3.2)

(iii)

Policy update
.

q^{(n+1)}(t,x)={\arg\max}_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,|q|\leq R}\left\{q\cdot\nabla u^{(n)}(t,x)-L(x,q)\right\}\qquad\text{ in }Q.

(3.3)

(iv)

Smoothing
.
Iteration terminates if
\|q^{(n+1)}-q^{(n)}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is small enough, else

\bar{q}^{(n+1)}=(1-\frac{2}{n+2})\bar{q}^{(n)}+\frac{2}{n+2}q^{(n+1)}.

(3.4)

The interpretation is that, in each stage
n
, the agent holds the belief that other agents are acting according to the smoothed policy
\bar{q}^{(n)}
, hence she generates from
\bar{q}^{(n)}
the probability distribution
m^{(n)}
. She then evaluates
\bar{q}^{(n)}
by solving for the value function
u^{(n)}
. She finds a greedy update
q^{(n+1)}
based on
u^{(n)}
and use it to update
\bar{q}^{(n+1)}
.

We denote:
\delta u^{(n+1)}=u^{(n+1)}-u^{(n)},\,\,\delta m^{(n+1)}=m^{(n+1)}-m^{(n)}
.

Remark 3.1
.

We can interpret the learning rate by rewriting (
3.4
) as,
\forall n\geq 1
,

\bar{q}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}k}\sum_{k=1}^{n}kq^{(k)}.

(3.5)

As an agent plays the game repeatedly, she puts increasingly larger weights in more recent observations of
q^{(n)}
while updating the “belief"
\bar{q}^{(n)}
, cf.
[
22
, p. 191]
.
It is to be noted that all the following results in this subsection hold for
SPI1
if we replace the learning rate
2/(n+2)
by
1/(n+2)
, i.e.
\forall n\geq 1
,

\tilde{q}^{(n+1)}=(1-\frac{1}{n+2})\tilde{q}^{(n)}+\frac{1}{n+2}q^{(n+1)}=\frac{1}{n+2}\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}q^{(k)}.

We focus on the
2/(n+2)
learning rate as it speeds up the convergence from a numerical point of view, for comparison with learning rates in
FP
we refer to
[
9
]
.

.

We use an a posterior argument to check that
(u^{(n)},m^{(n)})
terminates at a solution to (

), if ideally,

. (

Theorem 3.5
.

Under the assumptions (A1), (A2), (A3) and with
R
sufficiently large, the family
\{(u^{n},m^{n})\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, in the algorithm
SPI1
is uniformly continuous and any cluster point is a solution to the second order MFG (
1.1
). If, in addition, the monotonicity condition (A4) holds, then the whole sequence converges to the unique solution of (
1.1
).

Proof.

From the Bernstein estimate (
Remark

2.3
) there exists a sufficiently large
R
such that

\|H_{p}(\cdot,\nabla u)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq R.

We show that
\{H_{p}(x,\nabla u^{(n)})-\bar{q}^{(n)}\}
converges to
0
uniformly. From the convexity of
L(x,q)
in the
q-
variable, we have

L(x,\bar{q}^{(n+1)})\leq\frac{n}{n+2}L(x,\bar{q}^{(n)})+\frac{2}{n+2}L(x,q^{(n+1)}).

We can then obtain from equations (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) ,

\begin{split}&\frac{2}{n+2}H(x,\nabla u^{(n)})\\
={}&\frac{2}{n+2}q^{(n+1)}\cdot\nabla u^{(n)}-\frac{2}{n+2}L(x,q^{(n+1)})\\
={}&\big(\bar{q}^{(n+1)}-\frac{n}{n+2}\bar{q}^{(n)}\big)\cdot\nabla u^{(n)}-\frac{2}{n+2}L(x,q^{(n+1)})\\
\leq{}&-\frac{n}{n+2}\bar{q}^{(n)}\cdot\nabla u^{(n)}+\bar{q}^{(n+1)}\cdot\nabla u^{(n)}+\frac{n}{n+2}L(x,\bar{q}^{(n)})-L(x,\bar{q}^{(n+1)}).\end{split}

We can then obtain from (
3.2
),

\begin{split}&\frac{2}{n+2}H(x,\nabla u^{(n)})-\frac{2}{n+2}\big(\bar{q}^{(n)}\cdot\nabla u^{(n)}-L(x,\bar{q}^{(n)}\big)\\
\leq{}&-\bar{q}^{(n+1)}\cdot\nabla\delta u^{(n+1)}+\bar{q}^{(n+1)}\cdot\nabla u^{(n+1)}-L(x,\bar{q}^{(n+1)})-\bar{q}^{(n)}\cdot\nabla u^{(n)}+L(x,\bar{q}^{(n)})\\
\leq{}&-\bar{q}^{(n+1)}\cdot\nabla\delta u^{(n+1)}+\partial_{t}\delta u^{(n+1)}+\sigma\Delta\delta u^{(n+1)}+f[m^{(n+1)}](x)-f[m^{(n)}](x)\end{split}

(3.10)

We can obtain from
Lemma

2.7
,
Lemma

3.3
and Cauchy Schwartz inequality that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\bar{C}n}|H_{p}(x,\nabla u^{(n)})-\bar{q}^{(n)}|^{2}\\
={}&\frac{2}{2\bar{C}(n+2)}|H_{p}(x,\nabla u^{(n)})-\bar{q}^{(n)}|^{2}+\frac{2|H_{p}(x,\nabla u^{(n)})-\bar{q}^{(n)}|^{2}}{\bar{C}n(n+2)}\\
\leq{}&\frac{2}{2\bar{C}(n+2)}|H_{p}(x,\nabla u^{(n)})-\bar{q}^{(n)}|^{2}+\frac{4|H_{p}(x,\nabla u^{(n)})|^{2}+4|\bar{q}^{(n)}|^{2}}{\bar{C}n(n+2)}\\
\leq{}&\frac{2}{2\bar{C}(n+2)}|H_{p}(x,\nabla u^{(n)})-\bar{q}^{(n)}|^{2}+\frac{C}{n^{2}}\\
\leq{}&\frac{2}{n+2}H(x,\nabla u^{(n)})-\frac{2}{n+2}\big(\bar{q}^{(n)}\cdot\nabla u^{(n)}-L(x,\bar{q}^{(n)})\big)+\frac{C}{n^{2}}\\
\leq{}&\partial_{t}\delta u^{(n+1)}+\sigma\Delta\delta u^{(n+1)}-\bar{q}^{(n+1)}\cdot\nabla\delta u^{(n+1)}+f[m^{(n+1)}](x)-f[m^{(n)}](x)+\frac{C}{n^{2}},\end{split}

(3.11)

where the last inequality is obtained from (
3.10
).
From integration by parts and (
3.1
),

\begin{split}&\int_{Q}m^{(n+1)}\left(\partial_{t}\delta u^{(n+1)}+\sigma\Delta\delta u^{(n+1)}-\bar{q}^{(n+1)}\cdot\nabla\delta u^{(n+1)}\right)dxdt\\
={}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}m^{(n+1)}(T)\big(g[m^{(n+1)}(T)](x))-g[m^{(n)}(T)](x)\big)dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}m_{0}(x)\delta u^{(n+1)}(0,x)dx.\end{split}

We multiply both sides of (
3.11
) by
m^{(n+1)}
, integrate on
Q
to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\bar{C}}\int_{Q}\frac{1}{n}m^{(n+1)}|H_{p}(x,\nabla u^{(n)})-\bar{q}^{(n)}|^{2}dxdt\\
\leq{}&\int_{Q}\left(m^{(n+1)}f[m^{(n+1)}](x)-m^{(n)}f[m^{(n)}](x)\right)dxdt-\int_{Q}f[m^{(n)}](x)\delta m^{(n+1)}dxdt\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(m^{(n+1)}(T)g[m^{(n+1)}(T)](x)-m^{(n)}(T)g[m^{(n)}(T)](x)\right)dx\\
&-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g[m^{(n)}(T)](x)\delta m^{(n+1)}(T,x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}m_{0}(x)\delta u^{(n+1)}(0,x)dx+\frac{C}{n^{2}},\end{split}

where we used the trivial identity:

m^{(n+1)}\left(f[m^{(n+1)}]-f[m^{(n)}]\right)=m^{(n+1)}f[m^{(n+1)}]-m^{(n)}f[m^{(n)}]-f[m^{(n)}]\delta m^{(n+1)}.

We can then use (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) to obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\bar{C}}\int_{Q}\frac{1}{n}m^{(n+1)}|H_{p}(x,\nabla u^{(n)})-\bar{q}^{(n)}|^{2}dxdt\\
\leq{}&\int_{0}^{T}\left(F[m^{(n)}(t)]-F[m^{(n+1)}(t)]\right)dt+G[m^{(n)}(T)]-G[m^{(n+1)}(T)]+\frac{C}{n^{2}}\\
{}&+\int_{Q}\left(m^{(n+1)}f[m^{(n+1)}](x)-m^{(n)}f[m^{(n)}](x)\right)dxdt-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}m_{0}(x)\delta u^{(n+1)}(0,x)dx\\
{}&+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(m^{(n+1)}(T)g[m^{(n+1)}(T)](x))-m^{(n)}(T)g[m^{(n)}(T)](x)\right)dx.\end{split}

(3.12)

Let

a_{n}=\int_{Q}m^{(n+1)}|H_{p}(x,\nabla u^{(n)})-\bar{q}^{(n)}|^{2}dxdt,

(3.13)

by telescoping we can see that,
\forall N\geq 2
,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\bar{C}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{a_{n}}{n}\leq{}&\int_{0}^{T}\left(F[m^{(1)}(t)]-F[m^{(N+1)}(t)]\right)dt+G[m^{(1)}(T)]-G[m^{(N+1)}(T)]\\
{}&+\int_{Q}\left(m^{(N+1)}f[m^{(N+1)}](x)-m^{(1)}f[m^{(1)}](x)\right)dxdt\\
{}&-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}m_{0}(x)\left(u^{(N+1)}(0,x)-u^{(1)}(0,x)\right)dx\\
{}&+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(m^{(N+1)}(T)g[m^{(N+1)}(T)](x)-m^{(1)}(T,x)g[m^{(1)}(T)](x)\right)dx+C,\end{split}

hence,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}/n<+\infty
. One can obtain from Lemma
3.3
and
3.4
that
|a_{n}-a_{n+1}|<C/n
. Then we obtain
a_{n}\rightarrow 0
from Lemma
2.8
. From
Lemma

3.3
we have
m^{(n)}(t,x)\geq 1/C
,
\forall(t,x)\in Q
, hence

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{Q}|H_{p}(x,\nabla u^{(n)})-\bar{q}^{(n)}|^{2}dxdt=0.

From
Lemma

3.3
, the sequence
\{H_{p}(x,\nabla u^{(n)})-\bar{q}^{(n)}\}
is uniformly continuous, hence converges to
0
uniformly on
Q
.

From
Lemma

3.3
, the sequence
\{\bar{q}^{(n)}\}
is pre-compact for uniform convergence, one can extract a uniformly convergent subsequence
\{\bar{q}^{(n_{i})}\}
. From
Proposition

A.3
and
Remark

A.4
,
\{m^{(n_{i})}\}
converges uniformly. It then follows from (
A3
) that
\{f[m^{(n_{i})}]\}
converges uniformly. From Proposition
A.2
and the fact

converges to

uniformly, one can pass to the limit in (

) and (

). Any cluster point of

, denoted by

, is then a classical solution to the system (

).

holds, then the MFG system has a unique classical solution, so that the whole sequence

.
∎

\|\nabla u^{(n)}-\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2\hat{C}\|\hat{q}^{(n-1)}-H_{p}(\cdot,\nabla u^{(n-1)})\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{2C}{(n-1)^{2}}+\frac{2\hat{C}}{n-1}.

Let
n\rightarrow\infty
, then
\|\nabla u^{(n)}-\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\rightarrow 0
. Moreover from
(A2)
,
H_{p}(x,\nabla u^{(n)})
converges uniformly to
H_{p}(x,\nabla u)
, from Proposition
A.3
and Remark
A.4
we have
\{m^{(n)}\}
converges to
m
in
\mathcal{C}^{0}(Q)
. From Proposition
A.2
and (
A.2
),
\{u^{(n)}\}
convergences to
u
in
\mathcal{C}^{0,1}(Q)
.
∎

for

Discretization
. We discrete the problem on
Q=[0,T]\times[-1,1]
with grid
\mathcal{G}
.
h=2/\mathsf{I}
,
i=0,...,\mathsf{I}
.
\Delta t=T/\mathsf{T}
,
\tau=0,...,\mathsf{T}
. Set
t_{\tau}=\tau\Delta t
and

. The values of

and

at

are approximated by

and

and

for all

. Denote by

and
(\cdot)^{-}=\min\left\{\cdot,0\right\}
for the positive and negative part respectively of a real number. We define the following discrete operators:

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\sharp}U_{\tau})_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h^{2}}\left(U_{\tau,[i-1]}-2U_{\tau,i}+U_{\tau,[i+1]}\right)\,,

\displaystyle(D_{\sharp}U_{\tau})_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(D_{L}U_{\tau,i}\,,\,D_{R}U_{\tau,i}\right)=\frac{1}{h}\left(U_{\tau,i}-U_{\tau,[i-1]}\,,\,U_{\tau,[i+1]}-U_{\tau,i}\right)\,,

\begin{split}\text{div}_{\sharp}(M_{\tau+1}\,D_{\sharp}U_{\tau})_{i}={}&\frac{1}{h}\left(M_{\tau+1,[i+1]}D_{L}U_{\tau,[i+1]}^{+}-M_{\tau+1,i}D_{L}U_{\tau,i}^{+}\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{h}\left(M_{\tau+1,i}D_{R}U_{\tau,i}^{-}-M_{\tau+1,[i-1]}D_{R}U_{\tau,[i-1]}^{-}\right),\end{split}

where the index operator
[\cdot]=\left\{(\cdot+I)\,mod\,I\right\}
accounts for the periodic boundary conditions. The two sided discrete gradient operator
(D_{\sharp}U_{\tau})_{i}
is commonly used in construction of numerical schemes for MFGs
[

2
]
. The discrete divergence operator captures the essential structural features of the MFG system
[

2
]
. This motivates, as in
[

\mathsf{Q}_{\tau,i}=\left(\mathsf{Q}_{\tau,i,L}\,,\,\mathsf{Q}_{\tau,i,R}\right),\,\,\mathsf{Q}_{\tau,i,\pm}=\left(\mathsf{Q}^{+}_{\tau,i,L}\,,\,\mathsf{Q}^{-}_{\tau,i,R}\right).

We denote

|\mathsf{Q}_{\tau,i,\pm}|^{2}=\left(\mathsf{Q}_{\tau,i,L}^{+}\right)^{2}+\left(\mathsf{Q}_{\tau,i,R}^{-}\right)^{2},\,\,|\mathsf{Q}_{\tau,i,\pm}|_{\infty}=\sup\left\{\mathsf{Q}^{+}_{\tau,i,L}\,,\,-\mathsf{Q}^{-}_{\tau,i,R}\right\}

and the discrete divergence operator

\begin{split}\text{div}_{\sharp}(M_{\tau+1}\,\mathsf{Q}_{\tau})_{i}={}&\frac{1}{h}\left(M_{\tau+1,[i+1]}\mathsf{Q}_{\tau,[i+1],L}^{+}-M_{\tau+1,i}\mathsf{Q}_{\tau,i,L}^{+}\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{h}\left(M_{\tau+1,i}\mathsf{Q}_{\tau,i,R}^{-}-M_{\tau+1,[i-1]}\mathsf{Q}_{\tau,[i-1],R}^{-}\right).\end{split}

(4.1)

Following

, we approximate the coupling

and

(4.2)

denotes the piecewise constant function taking the value

Consider the linear parabolic equation:

Optimal recovery and generalized Carlson inequality for weights with symmetry properties
K. Yu. Osipenko

Abstract
. The paper concerns problems of the recovery of operators from noisy information in weighted L q L_{q} -spaces with homogeneous weights. A number of general theorems are proved and applied to finding exact constants in multidimensional Carlson type inequalities with several weights and problems of the recovery of differential operators from a noisy Fourier transform. In particular, optimal methods are obtained for the recovery of powers of generalized Laplace operators from a noisy Fourier transform in the L p L_{p} -metric.

The quantity

E(p,q,r)\geq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x(\cdot)\in W\\

3.
Main results

Set

\chi_{0}(t)=\begin{cases}1,&t\in T_{0},\\
0,&t\notin T_{0},\end{cases}\quad\sigma_{r}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}|\varphi_{j}(t)|^{r}.

Theorem 1
.

Let
1\leq q<p,r
,
\lambda_{j}\geq 0
,
j=0,1,\ldots,n
,
\lambda_{0}+\sigma_{r}(t)\neq 0
for almost all
t\in T_{0}
,
\sigma_{r}(t)\neq 0
for almost all
t\in T\setminus T_{0}
,
\widehat{x}(t)\geq 0
be a solution of equation

(4)

-q|\psi(t)|^{q}+p\lambda_{0}x^{p-q}(t)\chi_{0}(t)+r\sigma_{r}(t)x^{r-q}(t)=0,

\overline{\lambda}
such that

(5)

\int_{T_{0}}\widehat{x}^{p}(t)\,d\mu(t)\leq\delta^{p},\quad\int_{T}|\varphi_{j}(t)|^{r}\widehat{x}^{r}(t)\,d\mu(t)\leq 1,\ j=1,\ldots,n,\\
\lambda_{0}\biggl(\int_{T_{0}}\widehat{x}^{p}(t)\,d\mu(t)-\delta^{p}\biggr)=0,\quad\lambda_{j}\biggr(\int_{T}|\varphi_{j}(t)|^{r}\widehat{x}^{r}(t)\,d\mu(t)-1\biggr)=0,\ j=1,\ldots,n.

Then

(6)

E(p,q,r)=\biggl(q^{-1}p\lambda_{0}\delta^{p}+q^{-1}r\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\biggr)^{1/q},

and the method

(7)

\widehat{m}(y)(t)=\begin{cases}q^{-1}p\lambda_{0}\widehat{x}^{p-q}(t)|\psi(t)|^{-q}\psi(t)y(t),&t\in T_{0},\ \psi(t)\neq 0,\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

is optimal recovery method.

To prove this theorem we need some preliminary results. The first one is actually a sufficient condition in the Kuhn-Tucker theorem (the only difference is that we do not require convexity of functions).

Let
f_{j}\colon A\to\mathbb{R}
,
j=0,1,\ldots,k
, be functions defined on some set

. Consider the extremal problem

(8)

f_{0}(x)\to\max,\quad f_{j}(x)\leq 0,\quad j=1,\ldots,k,\quad x\in A,

and write down its Lagrange function

\mathcal{L}(x,\lambda)=-f_{0}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}f_{j}(x),\quad\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}).

Lemma 2
.

Assume that there exist
\widehat{\lambda}_{j}\geq 0
,
j=1,\ldots,k
, and an element
\widehat{x}\in A
,
admissible for problem (
8
), such that

\displaystyle(a)

\displaystyle\quad\min_{x\in A}\mathcal{L}(x,\widehat{\lambda})=\mathcal{L}(\widehat{x},\widehat{\lambda}),\quad\widehat{\lambda}=(\widehat{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\lambda}_{k}),

\displaystyle(b)

\displaystyle\quad\widehat{\lambda}_{j}f_{j}(\widehat{x})=0,\ j=1,\ldots,k.

Then
\widehat{x}
is an extremal element for problem (
8
).

Proof.

For any
x
admissible for problem (
8
) we have

-f_{0}(x)\geq\mathcal{L}(x,\widehat{\lambda})\geq\mathcal{L}(\widehat{x},\widehat{\lambda})=-f_{0}(\widehat{x}).

∎

Put

F(u,v,\alpha)=-((1-\alpha)u+\alpha v)^{q}+av^{p}+bu^{r},\quad u,v\geq 0,\quad\alpha\in[0,1],

where
a,b\geq 0
, and

Lemma 3

(
[
8
]
)
.

For all
a,b\geq 0
,
a+b>0
, and all
1\leq q<p,r<\infty
, there exists the unique solution
\widehat{u}>0
of the equation

-q+pau^{p-q}+rbu^{r-q}=0.

Moreover, for all
u,v\geq 0

\alpha=q^{-1}pa\widehat{u}^{p-q}=1-q^{-1}rb\widehat{u}^{r-q}

F(\widehat{u},\widehat{u},\alpha)\leq F(u,v,\alpha).

In particular, for all
u\geq 0

-\widehat{u}^{q}+a\widehat{u}^{p}+b\widehat{u}^{r}\leq-u^{q}+au^{p}+bu^{r}.

Proof of Theorem
1

3

\int_{T}|\psi(t)x(t)|^{q}\,d\mu(t)\to\max,\quad\int_{T_{0}}|x(t)|^{p}\,d\mu(t)\leq\delta^{p},\\

Put

The value of this problem does not exceed the value of the problem

\int_{T}|\psi(t)|^{q}((1-\alpha(t))u(t)+\alpha(t)v(t))^{q}\,d\mu(t)\to\max,\\

\mathcal{L}(u(\cdot),v(\cdot),\overline{\lambda})=\int_{T}L(t,u(t),v(t),\overline{\lambda})\,d\mu(t),

\mathcal{L}(\widehat{x}(\cdot),\widehat{x}(\cdot),\overline{\lambda})\leq\mathcal{L}(u(\cdot),v(\cdot),\overline{\lambda}).

It follows by Lemma

\int_{T_{0}}|\psi(t)|^{p}|x(t)-\alpha(t)y(t)|^{p}\,d\mu(t)+\int_{T\setminus T_{0}}|\psi(t)x(t)|^{p}\,d\mu(t)\to\max,\\
\int_{T_{0}}|x(t)-y(t)|^{p}\,d\mu(t)\leq\delta^{p},\quad\int_{T}|\varphi_{j}(t)x(t)|^{r}\,d\mu(t)\leq 1,\ j=1,\ldots,n.

Putting
z(\cdot)=x(\cdot)-y(\cdot)
this problem may be rewritten in the form

\int_{T_{0}}|\psi(t)|^{p}|(1-\alpha(t))x(t)+\alpha(t)z(t)|^{p}\,d\mu(t)+\int_{T\setminus T_{0}}|\psi(t)x(t)|^{p}\,d\mu(t)\to\max,\\
\int_{T_{0}}|z(t)|^{p}\,d\mu(t)\leq\delta^{p},\quad\int_{T}|\varphi_{j}(t)x(t)|^{r}\,d\mu(t)\leq 1,\ j=1,\ldots,n.

The value of this problem evidently coincides with the value of the problem

(19)

\int_{T}|\psi(t)|^{p}((1-\alpha(t))v(t)+\alpha(t)u(t))^{p}\,d\mu(t)\to\max,\\
\int_{T_{0}}u^{p}(t)\,d\mu(t)\leq\delta^{p},\quad\int_{T}|\varphi_{j}(t)|^{r}v^{r}(t)\,d\mu(t)\leq 1,\ j=1,\ldots,n,\\
u(t),v(t)\geq 0,\ \mbox{for almost all }t\in T.

The Lagrange function of (
19
) has the form

\mathcal{L}(u(\cdot),v(\cdot),\overline{\lambda})=\int_{T}L(u(t),v(t),\overline{\lambda})\,d\mu(t),

where

L(u,v,\overline{\lambda})=\begin{cases}-|\psi(t)|^{p}((1-\alpha(t))v+\alpha(t)u)^{p}+\lambda_{0}u^{p}+\sigma_{r}(t)v^{r},&t\in T_{0},\\
-|\psi(t)|^{p}v^{p}+\sigma_{r}(t)v^{r},&t\in T\setminus T_{0}.\end{cases}

For
t\in T_{0}
and
|\psi(t)|^{p}>\lambda_{0}
we have

\frac{\partial L}{\partial u}=p\lambda_{0}(u^{p-1}-((1-\alpha(t))v+\alpha(t)u)^{p-1}).

Consequently, in this case for any
v>0
the function
L(u,v,\overline{\lambda})
,
v\in(0,+\infty)
, reaches a minimum at
v=u
. If
t\in T_{0}
,
0<|\psi(t)|^{p}\leq\lambda_{0}
, then
\alpha(t)=1
and
L(u,v,\overline{\lambda})\geq 0
. If
t\in T_{0}
and
\psi(t)=0
, then again
L(u,v,\overline{\lambda})\geq 0
. Set

T_{1}=\{t\in T_{0}:|\psi(t)|^{p}>\lambda_{0}\}
. Then for all
u(t),v(t)\geq 0
we have

\mathcal{L}(u(\cdot),v(\cdot),\overline{\lambda})\geq\int_{T_{1}}L(v(\cdot),v(\cdot),\overline{\lambda})\,d\mu(t)+\int_{T\setminus T_{0}}L(v(\cdot),v(\cdot),\overline{\lambda})\,d\mu(t).

It is easy to check that for all
v(t)\geq 0

L(v(\cdot),v(\cdot),\overline{\lambda})\geq L(\widehat{x}(\cdot),\widehat{x}(\cdot),\overline{\lambda}).

Therefore,

\mathcal{L}(u(\cdot),v(\cdot),\overline{\lambda})\geq\int_{T_{1}\cup(T\setminus T_{0})}L(\widehat{x}(\cdot),\widehat{x}(\cdot),\overline{\lambda})\,d\mu(t)=\mathcal{L}(\widehat{x}(\cdot),\widehat{x}(\cdot),\overline{\lambda}).

Taking into account (
5
) we obtain by Lemma
2
that
u(\cdot)=v(\cdot)=\widehat{x}(\cdot)
are extremal functions in (
19
). Consequently,

e^{p}(p,p,r,\widehat{m})=\int_{T}|\psi(t)\widehat{x}(t)|^{q}\,d\mu(t)=\lambda_{0}\delta^{p}+\frac{r}{p}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\leq E^{p}(p,p,r).

It means that the method
\widehat{m}
is optimal and the optimal recovery error is as stated.
∎

=\frac{B\left(q_{1}\gamma_{1}/p+1,q_{1}(1-\gamma_{1})/2\right)}{2|\nu-\eta-d/2|}.

Thus,

E_{p\infty}(\Lambda,\mathcal{D})\geq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d(1+\gamma_{1})/2}}\widetilde{C}_{p}(\nu,\eta)I^{1/q_{1}}\delta^{\gamma_{1}}.

Moreover, it follows from the same Theorem
5
that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\psi(\xi)F(\xi)-m(y)(\xi)\right|\,d\xi\leq E(p,1,2),

where

m(y)(t)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}k\left(\xi_{1}^{\frac{1}{\nu+d(1/2-1/p)}}t\right)\psi(t)y(t),

and

\xi_{1}=\frac{\delta}{\gamma_{1}^{1/p}}\left(\frac{1-\gamma_{1}}{n}\right)^{1/2}\Biggl(\frac{B\left(q_{1}\gamma_{1}/p,q_{1}(1-\gamma_{1})/2\right)I(2\pi)^{-dq_{1}(1+\gamma_{1})/2}}{|\nu+d(1/2-1/p)|(2\gamma_{1}+(1-\gamma_{1})p)}\Biggr)^{1/2-1/p}\\
=\delta\gamma_{1}^{-\frac{q_{1}}{2p}}\left(\frac{(1-\gamma_{1})\widetilde{C}_{p}(\nu,\eta)I^{1/q_{1}}}{n(2\pi)^{d(1+\gamma_{1})/2}}\right)^{q_{1}(1/2-1/p)}.

Consequently,

\biggl|\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(\xi)F(\xi)e^{i\langle t,\xi\rangle}\,d\xi-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}m(y)(\xi)e^{i\langle t,\xi\rangle}\,d\xi\biggr|\\
\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\psi(\xi)F(\xi)-m(y)(\xi)\right|\,d\xi\leq E(p,1,2)\leq E_{p\infty}(\Lambda,\mathcal{D}).

It follows that the method
\widehat{m}(y)(\cdot)
is optimal, and the error of optimal recovery
coincides with
E(p,1,2)
.

Now we consider the case when

. Put

s(\xi)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\psi(\xi)}{|\psi(\xi)|},&\psi(\xi)\neq 0,\\
1,&\psi(\xi)=0.\end{cases}

Let

\dfrac{\delta\overline{\psi(\xi)}}{\lambda s_{2}(\xi)},&|\psi(\xi)|<\lambda s_{2}(\xi).\end{cases}

such that

\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi_{j}(\xi)|^{2}|F\widehat{x}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=1,\quad j=1,\ldots,n.

Now, to find
\lambda

\frac{\delta^{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{|\psi(\xi)|\geq\lambda s_{2}(\xi)}|\varphi_{j}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi+\frac{\delta^{2}\lambda^{-2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{|\psi(\xi)|<\lambda s_{2}(\xi)}\frac{|\varphi_{j}(\xi)|^{2}|\psi(\xi)|^{2}}{s_{2}^{2}(\xi)}\,d\xi=1.

\gamma_{1}\in(0,1)

\eta>-d
. In this case it is easy to check that
2\nu>\eta

2\nu+d>0
. Passing to the polar transformation we obtain

\frac{\delta^{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\Pi_{d-1}}\widetilde{\varphi}^{2}_{j}(\omega)J(\omega)\,d\omega\int_{0}^{\Phi_{2}(\omega)}\rho^{2\nu+d-1}\,d\rho\\
+\frac{\delta^{2}\lambda^{-2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\Pi_{d-1}}\frac{\widetilde{\varphi}^{2}_{j}(\omega)\widetilde{\psi}^{2}(\omega)}{\widetilde{s}_{2}^{2}(\omega)}J(\omega)\,d\omega\int_{\Phi_{2}(\omega)}^{+\infty}\rho^{-2\nu+2\eta+d-1}\,d\rho=1,

where

\Phi_{2}(\omega)=\left(\frac{\widetilde{\psi}(\omega)}{\lambda\widetilde{s}_{2}(\omega)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\nu-\eta}}.

Thus,

\frac{\delta^{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\lambda^{-\frac{2\nu+d}{2\nu-\eta}}\frac{4\nu-2\eta}{(2\nu+d)(2\nu-2\eta-d)}I_{j}=1.

If

\gamma_{1}\in(0,1)

\eta<-d
,

\frac{\delta^{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\Pi_{d-1}}\widetilde{\varphi}^{2}_{j}(\omega)J(\omega)\,d\omega\int_{\Phi_{2}(\omega)}^{+\infty}\rho^{2\nu+d-1}\,d\rho\\
+\frac{\delta^{2}\lambda^{-2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\Pi_{d-1}}\frac{\widetilde{\varphi}^{2}_{j}(\omega)\widetilde{\psi}^{2}(\omega)}{\widetilde{s}_{2}^{2}(\omega)}J(\omega)\,d\omega\int_{0}^{\Phi_{2}(\omega)}\rho^{-2\nu+2\eta+d-1}\,d\rho=1,

For this case we have

Combining both of these cases and taking into account that

,

+\frac{\delta}{\lambda(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{|\psi(\xi)|<\lambda s_{2}(\xi)}\frac{|\psi(\xi)|^{2}}{s_{2}(\xi)}\,d\xi.

Using calculations similar to those that were above, we obtain

+\frac{\lambda}{\delta(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}s_{2}(\xi)Fx(\xi)\overline{F\widehat{x}(\xi)}e^{i\langle t,\xi\rangle}\,d\xi.

holds. Indeed,

\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{|\psi(\xi)|\geq\lambda s_{2}(\xi)}\left(\psi(\xi)-\lambda s(\xi)s_{2}(\xi)\right)Fx(\xi)e^{i\langle t,\xi\rangle}\,d\xi\\
+\frac{\lambda}{\delta(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}s_{2}(\xi)Fx(\xi)\overline{F\widehat{x}(\xi)}e^{i\langle t,\xi\rangle}\,d\xi\\
=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{|\psi(\xi)|\geq\lambda s_{2}(\xi)}\left((\psi(\xi)-\lambda s(\xi)s_{2}(\xi)\right)Fx(\xi)e^{i\langle t,\xi\rangle}\,d\xi\\

x(\cdot)

What would the rational Urysohn space and the random graph look like if they were uncountable?
Ziemowit Kostana

Abstract
Building on the work of Avraham, Rubin, and Shelah, we aim to build a variant of the Fraïssé theory for uncountable models built from finite submodels. With this aim, we generalize the notion of an increasing set of reals to other structures. As an application, we prove that the following is consistent: there exists an uncountable, separable metric space X X with rational distances, such that every uncountable partial 1-1 function from X X to X X is an isometry on an uncountable subset. We aim for a general theory of structures with this kind of properties. This includes results about the automorphism groups, and partial classification results.



Keywords:
Fraïssé limit, Martin’s Axiom, homogeneous structure, generic structures

History and Motivation

It is well-known that
\operatorname{CH}
entails the existence of
\omega_{1}
-saturated models of size
\omega_{1}
, for all first order theories admitting infinite models (
[
6
]
, ch. 10). In many important cases, such models are unique up to isomorphism. This observation is a cornerstone of the Fraïssé-Jónsson theory, developed in
[
7
]
. These models are built as
\omega_{1}
-chains of countable structures, and so it seems interesting to ask whether we can build uncountable models by "glueing together" finite structures. One indication that such theory should be possible to formulate is the celebrated theorem of Baumgartner, about separable
\omega_{1}
-dense linear orders. Recall that a linear order is
\omega_{1}
-dense if each non-empty open interval has cardinality
\omega_{1}
.

Theorem

(Baumgartner,
[
3
]
)
.

It is consistent with
\operatorname{ZFC}
that there exists a unique up to isomorphism separable
\omega_{1}
-dense linear order.

Once the conclusion of this theorem holds, we can take any subfield of the reals of size
\omega_{1}
as a model of this ordering. This shows that the (unique)
\omega_{1}
-dense separable linear order is homogeneous with respect to finite subsets (as is any subfield of the reals). Ideas of Baumgartner were extended by Avraham, Rubin, and Shelah in
[
2
]
and
[
1
]
. Among other things, they show that Baumgartner’s Theorem does not follow from
\operatorname{MA}+\lnot\operatorname{CH}
, but the latter axiom already ensures some approximation of the Baumgartner’s Theorem. They introduce another axiom,
\operatorname{OCA}_{ARS}
, and show that
\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}+\operatorname{OCA}_{ARS}
implies that either the conclusion of Baumgartner’s Theorem holds, or there are up to isomorphism exactly three homogeneous separable
\omega_{1}
-dense linear orders (
[
1
]
, Sec. 6). This gives some insight that
\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}
might be used in place of induction in some uncountable variant of the Fraïssé theory.

We continue the line of research started in
[
8
]
. We study the structures we introduced therein, and apply to them the ideas from
[
2
]
and
[
1
]
. For example, the generic structures from
[
8
]
, although initially rigid, become highly homogeneous in suitable forcing extensions satisfying
\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}
. Inductive arguments from the classical Fraïssé theory are replaced by Martin’s Axiom, and in some cases it is enough to ensure some form of uniqueness.

Even if aiming towards a new variant of the Fraïssé theory is perhaps too ambitious, we still find new applications for some techniques developed in
[
2
]
. As an example, let us look at a Ramsey-type result from
[
2
]
.

Theorem

(Thm. 2,
[
2
]
)
.

It is consistent with
\operatorname{ZFC}+\operatorname{MA}+"2^{\omega}=\omega_{2}"
that there exists an uncountable set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
with the property that each uncountable 1-1 function
f\subseteq A\times A
is strictly increasing on an uncountable set.

In this spirit, we prove in Section 2

Theorem
.

It is consistent with
\operatorname{ZFC}+\operatorname{MA}+"2^{\omega}=\omega_{2}"
that there exists an uncountable, separable rational metric space
(X,d)
such that each uncountable 1-1 function
f\subseteq X\times X
is an isometry on an uncountable set.

This phenomenon is more general. In Section 3, we prove similar results for other classes of structures, although they are more technical, and require some introduction (what is a "separable" graph?). In Section 4, we prove some results on the number of isomorphism types of models we built.

Preliminaries

By an
embedding
we always mean an isomorphism into its image. A subset of a model is a
substructure
if the identity inclusion is an embedding. We will say that a language is

if it doesn’t contain any function or constant symbols. We write
\operatorname{dom}{f}
and

to denote respectively the domain and the image of a function

.

We study structures added generically by forcings

\operatorname{Fn}(S,\mathcal{K},\lambda),

that we introduced in
[
8
]
. The variable
\mathcal{K}
always stands for a class of structures in some purely relational language. All unspecified forcing terminology follows
[
11
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\lambda
be an infinite cardinal, and
S
an infinite set. For any model
A\in\mathcal{K}
we denote by
F(A)
its underlying set. We introduce the forcing
\operatorname{Fn}(S,\mathcal{K},\lambda)
as the set

\{A\in\mathcal{K}|\;F(A)\in[S]^{<\lambda}\},

ordered by the reversed substructure relation.

We proved in
[
8
]
that models added generically by

\operatorname{Fn}(\omega_{1},\mathcal{K},\omega)

tend to be rigid, while those added by

\operatorname{Fn}(\omega,\mathcal{K},\omega)

are isomorphic to the usual Fraïssé limits of
\mathcal{K}
. We must impose some restrictions on the class
\mathcal{K}
for
\operatorname{Fn}(\omega,\mathcal{K},\omega)
to be an interesting forcing notion. The following is sufficient to ensure the c.c.c. property in all cases in the range of our interest. The name

was coined by W. Kubiś. Two embeddings

and

, such that

.

Definition 1.2
.

\mathcal{K}
has the Splitting Property (SP) if for all
R\in\mathcal{K}
, all pairs of isomorphic extensions
R\subseteq X
,
R\subseteq Y
, there exists
Z\in\mathcal{K}
, containing
X\cup Y
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a class of structures in a purely relational language, that satisfies the following

•

\mathcal{K}
has countably many isomorphism types of finite models,

•

\mathcal{K}
is hereditary (if
A\in\mathcal{K},\;B\subseteq A,
then

),

•

\mathcal{K}
has the SP.

Then the forcing

has the c.c.c. for any cardinal

.

Consider a family of conditions

. Using

-system Lemma, we can trim the sequence, so that the sets

-system with the root

. There are at most countably many structures from

with the universe

, and for all

We can also assume that the extensions

are isomorphic.
Now we can use the SP for the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A}_{\eta}}

2.1
Rectangular Metric Spaces

We adapt the technology from
[
2
]
and
[
1
]
to prove that it is relatively consistent with
\operatorname{ZFC}
that there exists a separable rational metric space
(X,d)
of size
\omega_{1}
, such that any uncountable 1-1 function from
X
to itself is an isometry on an uncountable subset.
The original result states that it is relatively consistent with
\operatorname{ZFC}
that there exists an uncountable set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, such that every function
f:A\rightarrow A
is non-decreasing on an uncountable set. Unlike the latter statement, our result does not seem to follow from Semiopen Coloring Axiom, Open Coloring Axiom or any other combinatorial principle described in
[
1
]
.

We first introduce a metric analog of a
k
-increasing linear order, introduced in
[
2
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space.

•

We call a pair of tuples
\overline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),\overline{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in X^{n}

alike
if they satisfy the following axioms:

A1

\forall\;i,j=1,\ldots,n\;(d(x_{i},y_{i})=d(x_{j},y_{j}))

A2

\forall\;i,j=1,\ldots,n\;(d(x_{i},x_{j})=d(y_{i},y_{j}))

A3

\forall\;i,j=1,\ldots,n\;(x_{i}\neq x_{j}\implies d(x_{i},x_{j})=d(x_{i},y_{j}))

We then write
\overline{x}\circledast\overline{y}
.

•

We call
(X,d)

rectangular
if it is uncountable, and for any sequence of pairwise disjoint tuples
\{(x_{1}^{\xi},\ldots,x_{n}^{\xi})|\;\xi<\omega_{1}\}\subseteq X^{n}
, there are
\xi\neq\eta<\omega_{1}
, such that
(x_{1}^{\xi},\ldots,x_{n}^{\xi})\circledast(x_{1}^{\eta},\ldots,x_{n}^{\eta})
.

Denote by
Metr
the class of rational metric spaces. Keeping up with the general notation,
\operatorname{Fn}(\kappa,Metr,\omega)
is the partial order

\{(Y,d)|\;Y\in[\kappa]^{<\omega},\text{ and $(Y,d)$ is a rational metric space}\},

with the ordering relation being the reversed inclusion preserving the metric.

We begin with a technical Lemma to ensure that we can amalgamate metric spaces in a specific way.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(R,d_{R}),(X,d_{X}),(Y,d_{Y})
be finite metric spaces, such that
(X,d_{X})\cap(Y,d_{Y})=(R,d_{R})
, and suppose that
h:(X,d_{X})\rightarrow(Y,d_{Y})
is an isometric bijection, which is identity on
R
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X,d_{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(R,d_{R})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X\cup Y,d^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(Y,d_{Y})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

and

is a bijective enumeration of

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\circledast(h(x_{1}),\ldots,h(x_{n}))

Proof.

Let

. Given that

must extend the metrics of

The argument showing that

Proposition 2.6
.

Finite support iterations of
(\mathcal{M},d)
-c.c. posets are

-c.c..

Proof.

Assume that
\mathbb{P}
is
(\mathcal{M},d)
-c.c., and
\mathbb{P}\Vdash\text{"$\dot{\mathbb{Q}}$ is $(\mathcal{M},d)${-c.c.}"}
. Take a sequence

\{(p_{\xi},\dot{q}_{\xi},x_{1}^{\xi},\ldots,x_{n}^{\xi})|\;\xi<\omega_{1}\},

and towards contradiction assume that it witnesses
\mathbb{P}\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
not being
(\mathcal{M},d)
-c.c.. Let
\dot{\sigma}
be a
\mathbb{P}
-name defined
\dot{\sigma}=\{(\xi,p_{\xi})|\;\xi<\omega_{1}\}
. If
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}
is an
M

M[G]\models(\xi\in\sigma\iff p_{\xi}\in G).

We claim that in

, for any two conditions

, if
(x_{1}^{\xi},\ldots,x_{n}^{\xi})\circledast(x_{1}^{\eta},\ldots,x_{n}^{\eta})
, then
q_{\xi}
and
q_{\eta}
are inconsistent. For otherwise, there exist

, and

, which forces it. Since

and

are in

, which is a filter, we may choose

. Then
(p,\dot{q})\leq(p_{\xi},\dot{q}_{\xi}),(p_{\eta},\dot{q}_{\eta})
, and
(x_{1}^{\xi},\ldots,x_{n}^{\xi})\circledast(x_{1}^{\eta},\ldots,x_{n}^{\eta})
contrary to the choice of the sequence
\{(p_{\xi},\dot{q}_{\xi},x_{1}^{\xi},\ldots,x_{n}^{\xi})|\;\xi<\omega_{1}\}

G
was an arbitrary generic filter, and remember that
\mathbb{P}\Vdash\text{"$\dot{\mathbb{Q}}$ is $(\mathcal{M},d)${-c.c.}"}
. The conclusion of this is that
\mathbb{P}\Vdash|\dot{\sigma}|<\omega_{1}
. Then there exists a

-name for a countable ordinal

, for which

. Since
\mathbb{P}
is c.c.c. there are only countably many possible values of

, and by taking supremum of them, we can replace

Lemma 2.7
.

It is consistent with
\operatorname{ZFC}+\operatorname{MA}(\text{$(\mathcal{M},d)${-c.c.}})+``2^{\omega}=\omega_{2}"

In fact, full Martin’s Axiom will hold in such model, what we are going to show. Recall that if
\mathbb{P}
is a forcing notion, a set

is
predense
if each element of
\mathbb{P}
is comparable with an element of
D
. A set

is

if each element of
\mathbb{P}
stronger that

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a c.c.c. forcing notion of size
\omega_{1}
. There exists
p\in\mathbb{P}
and a family of
\omega_{1}
many subsets of
\mathbb{P}
, predense below
p
, such that any filter
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}
containing
p
and intersecting all of them is uncountable.

Proof.

Enumerate bijectively
\mathbb{P}
as
\{p_{\gamma}|\;\gamma<\omega_{1}\}
. Let
D_{\alpha}=\{p_{\gamma}|\;\alpha<\gamma<\omega_{1}\}
. We aim to find
p\in\mathbb{P}
such that uncountably many of sets

are predense below
p
– clearly this will finish the proof. If
p
with this property doesn’t exist, then the following assertion holds:

\forall{\gamma<\omega_{1}}\;\exists{\gamma^{\prime}>\gamma}\;\text{"$D_{\gamma^{\prime}}$ is not predense below $p_{\gamma}$"},

and consequently

\forall{\gamma<\omega_{1}}\;\exists{p_{\gamma}^{\prime}\leq p_{\gamma}}\;\exists{\gamma^{\prime}>\gamma}\;\forall{\eta>\gamma^{\prime}}\;p_{\gamma}^{\prime}\bot p_{\eta}.

Using this, we can easily define an uncountable antichain
\{p_{\gamma}|\;\gamma\in E\}
, ensuring at each step of induction that

\forall{\gamma\in E}\;\exists{\gamma^{\prime}>\gamma}\;\forall{\eta>\gamma^{\prime}}\;p_{\gamma}\bot p_{\eta}.

∎

What is clear, is that

2.2
Rectangular Models for General Binary Relations

The reader might have noticed that in the previous section the triangle inequality for the space
(\mathcal{M},d)
was never applied. The crucial property we used was a variant of the SP, which ensures that "remainders" will be alike (see Lemma
2.2
). In this section we will define a version of the SP that allows to proceed with the proof of Theorem
2.12
in case of other classes of structures. One assumption which seems hard to be removed is that the language consists only of binary relational symbols.

Each language consisting of finitely many binary relational symbols can be identified with a finite coloring of ordered pairs – given a model
A
, we assign to each element of
A^{2}
its isomorphism type. There are only finitely many symbols in the language, so this coloring is indeed finite, and moreover, it determines the model
A
completely. Also, any function is a homomorphism precisely when it preserves this coloring. This observation allows to generalize results from Section 2 to other classes, besides metric spaces. In fact, the finiteness of language is not relevant as long as there are only countably many isomorphism types of 2-element models. Let
\mathcal{K}
be some class of structures in a countable language
\{\mathcal{R}_{i}\}_{i<\omega}
consisting of binary relational symbols. Assume also that
\mathcal{K}
has only countably many isomorphism types of finite models. Let
c
be the corresponding coloring of pairs in models from
\mathcal{K}
– by the remark above we may forget about the relational symbols, and think of models from
\mathcal{K}
as having only one "relation", namely
c
. We introduce the
\circledast
relation by axioms similar to the metric case, but unlike metrics, the coloring
c
might not be symmetric. This is the only significant difference.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{K}
, and
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in X^{n}
be disjoint. We will say that they are

, and write
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\circledast(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
, if the following axioms are satisfied

A1a

\forall\;i,j=1,\ldots,n\;c(x_{i},y_{i})=c(x_{j},y_{j})

A1b

\forall\;i,j=1,\ldots,n\;c(y_{i},x_{i})=c(y_{j},x_{j})

A2a

\forall\;i,j=1,\ldots,n\;c(x_{i},x_{j})=c(y_{i},y_{j})

A3a

\forall\;i,j=1,\ldots,n\;(x_{i}\neq x_{j}\implies c(x_{i},x_{j})=c(x_{i},y_{j})=c(y_{i},x_{j}))

If all relations
\mathcal{R}_{k}
are anti-reflexive (
\forall x\;\lnot\mathcal{R}_{k}(x,x)
), then we can omit the clause
(x_{i}\neq x_{j})\implies
in A3a. It is standard to check that

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\circledast(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\iff(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\circledast(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

Definition 2.16

(x_{1}^{\xi},\ldots,x_{n}^{\xi})\circledast(x_{1}^{\eta},\ldots,x_{n}^{\eta}).

\mathcal{K}

, for all pairs of isomorphic extensions

,

enumerating bijectively

The RSP ensures the conclusion of Lemma

Proposition 2.24
.

The classes of graphs, directed graphs, tournaments, linear orders, and partial orders have the RSP.

Proof.

We will prove the RSP for linear and partial orders. Arguments for other classes are easy and left to the reader. For each partial order
\leq
there exists a corresponding quasi-ordering relation
<
, which is anti-reflexive.

Suppose we have a diagram of linear quasi-orders
R,X,Y
, and
h:X\rightarrow Y
, like in the definition of the RSP. We define a quasi-ordering on
Z=X\cup Y
, extending both
<_{X}
and
<_{Y}
, by conditions:

•

\forall{i<\omega}\;(x_{i}<h(x_{i}))
,

•

\forall{i\neq j<\omega}\;(x_{i}<h(x_{j})\iff x_{i}<_{X}x_{j}).

Clearly
<
is an anti-reflexive relation on
Z
. For checking transitivity we must go through several (somewhat boring) cases.

1.

x_{i}<h(x_{j}),\;h(x_{j})<x_{k}
. Either
x_{i}=x_{j}
or
x_{i}<x_{j}
. In the first case
h(x_{i})<x_{k}
, and so
x_{i}<x_{k}
. In the second,
x_{i}<x_{k}
, and
x_{i}<x_{k}
follows from transitivity of
<
on
X
.

2.

h(x_{i})<x_{j},\;x_{j}<x_{k}
. In this case
x_{i}<x_{j}
, so
x_{i}<x_{k}
, and
h(x_{i})<x_{k}
.

3.

x_{i}<x_{j},\;x_{j}<h(x_{k})
. Either
x_{j}=x_{k}
or
x_{j}<x_{k}
. In both cases
x_{i}<x_{k}
, so
x_{i}<h(x_{k})
.

4.

h(x_{i})<h(x_{j}),\;h(x_{j})<x_{k}
. In this case
x_{i}<x_{j}
, and
x_{j}<x_{k}
. By transitivity
x_{i}<x_{k}
, and
h(x_{i})<x_{k}
follows.

5.

x_{i}<h(x_{j}),\;h(x_{j})<h(x_{k})
. If
x_{i}<x_{j}
, then we proceed like before. If
x_{i}=x_{j}
,
h(x_{i})<h(x_{k})
. It follows that
x_{i}<x_{k}
, and
x_{i}<h(x_{k})
.

6.

h(x_{i})<x_{j},\;x_{j}<h(x_{k})
. We see that
x_{i}<x_{j}
. If
x_{j}<x_{k}
, we use transitivity of
<
on
X
. If
x_{j}=x_{k}
, then
h(x_{i})<x_{k}
, and so
x_{i}<x_{k}
. It follows that
h(x_{i})<h(x_{k})
.

The proof for partial orders is strictly simpler – we define the quasi-ordering by conditions:

•

\forall{i<\omega}\quad x_{i}\text{ is incomparable with }h(x_{i})
,

•

\forall{i,j<\omega}\quad x_{i}<h(x_{j})\iff x_{i}<x_{j},

•

\forall{i,j<\omega}\quad\lnot h(x_{i})<x_{j}.

Verification of transitivity is a run through the same cases, except this time we do not have to care if
x_{i},x_{j},x_{k}
are distinct.
∎

2.3
HSS Models

After forcing with
\operatorname{Fn}(\omega_{1},\mathcal{LO},\omega)
, each infinite subset of
\omega_{1}
from the ground model becomes a dense subset of the generic structure. This can be checked by a standard density argument, and analogous property holds for other classes. Specifically, uncountable generic models contain many countable subsets which realize every finite type. We present one way to capture this property. It will be useful in proving classification theorems in the next Section.

Definition 2.26
.

Let
X
be a structure in some relational language. A subset
D\subseteq X
is a
saturating subset
if for any finite subset
E\subseteq X
, for any single-point extension
E\subseteq E\cup\{f\}
, there exists
d\in D
such that the following diagram commutes
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E\cup\{f\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E\cup\{d\}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\mapsto d}

To phrase it shortly, every finite type can be realized in
D
. In case of metric spaces, it this is just to say that any finite configuration of distances from points in
X
can be realized inside
D
. A saturating subset of a metric space is dense in a very strong sense.

Definition 2.27
.

Let
X
be any structure in a relational language.

1.

X
is
separably saturated
if it has a countable saturating subset.

2.

X
is
hereditarily separably saturated
(HSS) if for any countable subset
A\subseteq X
,
X\setminus A
is separably saturated.

Proposition 2.28
.

If
\mathcal{K}
is a class with the RSP then
\operatorname{Fn}(\kappa,\mathcal{K},\omega)
forces the generic structure to be HSS, for any uncountable cardinal
\kappa
.

Proof.

A standard density argument shows that every infinite set from the ground model is a saturating subset.
∎

3

If the uncountable models we are considering are to resemble Fraïssé limits, we should be able to do some kind of "back-and-forth" arguments, like in the classical theory. There is no way inductive arguments can work, but thanks to Theorem

We will be looking at metric spaces with distances in a given countable set

Theorem 3.1
.

\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}
. Let
(X,d)
be any rectangular HSS metric space of size

and

\operatorname{dom}{p_{\gamma}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},x_{k+1}^{\gamma},\ldots,x_{m}^{\gamma}),

\displaystyle d(x_{i}^{\eta},x_{j}^{\eta})=

\displaystyle d(y_{i}^{\eta},y_{j}^{\eta})=

\displaystyle d(y_{i}^{\eta},y_{j}^{\xi})

Also for

\displaystyle d(y_{i}^{\eta},y_{i}^{\xi})

Still, this kind of homogeneity is not sufficient to prove uniqueness, like for the rational Urysohn space.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume
\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}
. If there exists a rectangular HSS rational metric space of size

Proof.

If

is a rectangular, hereditarily separably saturated rational metric space of size
\omega_{1}
, we introduce a family of metrics on

d_{k}(x,y)=k\cdot d(x,y),

for positive integers

k<l
are positive integers, the spaces

and

are not isometric. Indeed, if

is a bijection, then by Theorem

is an isometry of the space

on some pair of distinct points

.
∎

In contrast to Theorem

we have

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume
\operatorname{CH}
, and suppose
(X,d)
is a HSS metric space of size
\omega_{1}
, with distances in
K
. There is an uncountable HSS subspace

not isometric with

.

Proof.

Let
\{f_{\alpha}|\;\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
be an enumeration of all isometries between countable subspaces of

, so that for every

The choice of each

3.3
Separable
\omega_{1}
-dense Linear Orders

What do rectangular linear orders look like? After unwinding the axioms for the
\circledast
relation, we see that

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\circledast(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
translates to the following three axioms:

A1c

\forall\;i,j=1,\ldots,n\;(x_{i}<y_{i}\iff x_{j}<y_{j})

A2c

\forall\;i,j=1,\ldots,n\;(x_{i}<x_{j}\iff y_{i}<y_{j})

A3c

\forall\;i,j=1,\ldots,n\;((x_{i}\neq x_{j})\implies(x_{i}<x_{j}\iff x_{i}<y_{j}\iff y_{i}<x_{j}))

If we omitted A3c, we would obtain what the authors of
[
1
]
call an
increasing order
(actually, one can show that in the class of separable, dense linear orders these two notions coincide). An order added by
\operatorname{Fn}(\omega_{1},\mathcal{LO},\omega)
is a rectangular, separable,
\omega_{1}
-dense linear order, which under
\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}
is also homogeneous.

\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}
for this class, as well as other axioms like

[
1
]

More About Rectangularity

We will be considering models of the form
\chi=(\omega_{1},c)
, where
c:\omega_{1}\times\omega_{1}\rightarrow K
for some, usually finite, set
K
. A set
A\subseteq\omega_{1}
is called
monochromatic
if

c\restriction A\times A\setminus\{(a,a)|\;a\in A\}

is constant. A set
A\subseteq\omega_{1}

hits
a color

, if there are points
a_{0}\neq a_{1}\in A
for which
c(a_{0},a_{1})=k
. If
c
will be symmetric, we will regard it as a function with the domain
[\omega_{1}]^{\leq 2}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
. By a
rectangle
we understand a set of the form
I\times J
, for
I,J\subseteq\omega_{1}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume
\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}
. If
\chi
is a rectangular model in a language consisting of finitely many symmetric binary relations, then
\chi
is a countable union of monochromatic sets.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the hypothesis of Theorem
4.1
, there exists exactly one color
k\in K
that is hit on every uncountable subset of
\omega_{1}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
|K|
. For
|K|=1
the conclusion is obvious, so let us assume that
|K|>1
, and fix any
k_{0}\in K
. First, we show that at least one such color exists. If every uncountable set
X\subseteq\omega_{1}
hits
k_{0}
, we are done. Otherwise, there exists an uncountable set
X
, for which we have

c\restriction[X]^{2}:\;[X]^{2}\rightarrow K\setminus\{k_{0}\}.

Notice that any uncountable subset of a rectangular model is rectangular itself. Therefore, by the induction hypothesis, there exists some color
k_{1}\in K\setminus\{k_{0}\}
, that is hit on every uncountable subset of
X
. We will show that
k_{1}
is also hit on every uncountable subset of
\omega_{1}
. Indeed, fix an uncountable set
A\subseteq\omega_{1}
. By Proposition
2.23
, any bijection
\phi:A\hookrightarrow X
preserves the coloring

on some uncountable set

. But we know that
\phi[A^{\prime}]
hits

, and so does

. By applying Martin’s Axiom to the set of finite

-monochromatic sets, we see that

contains an uncountable

-monochromatic set (see the proof of Theorem

). This shows that the conclusion cannot hold for any color other than

Proof of Theorem

.

Let us fix a color

Recall that a graph

is

if it can be decomposed into a countable union of anticliques, i.e. there exist a function

is

.

Assume
\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}
. If

, then either

The next theorem holds in a broader generality, not only for symmetric relations.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume
\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}
. If
\chi=(\omega_{1},c)
is a rectangular model, then
c
is a countable union of rectangles.

Proof.

We apply Martin’s Axiom to the forcing consisting of finite partial functions from
\omega
to the set
([\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}\setminus\{\emptyset\})^{2}
, with the following additional properties

•

\forall n\in\operatorname{dom}{f}
, if
f(n)=(f^{n}_{0},f^{n}_{1})
, then
f^{n}_{0}\cap f^{n}_{1}=\emptyset
,

•

\forall n\in\operatorname{dom}{f}
,
c\restriction f^{n}_{0}\times f^{n}_{1}
is constant.

This forcing produces a partition of
c
into countable many rectangles: for every pair
(v_{0},v_{1})
of distinct ordinals, the set of conditions containing
(v_{0},v_{1})
in some coordinate is dense. The non-trivial part is to prove the c.c.c. Let us fix a family of conditions
\{f_{\xi}|\;\xi<\omega_{1}\}
. Without loss of generality, they all have the same domain
R\subseteq\omega
. Let us fix
n\in R
. By
\Delta
-system Lemma, we can assume that

\forall\xi<\omega_{1}\quad f_{\xi}(n)=(\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{s},a_{s+1}^{\xi},\ldots,a_{t}^{\xi}\},\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{s^{\prime}},b_{s^{\prime}+1}^{\xi},\ldots,b_{t^{\prime}}^{\xi}\}),

for distinct elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{t}^{\xi},b_{1},\ldots,b_{t^{\prime}}^{\xi}\in\omega_{1}
. Next, we can trim our sequence again, so that for all
\xi\neq\eta<\omega_{1}
, we have

(a_{s+1}^{\xi},\ldots,a_{t}^{\xi},b_{s^{\prime}+1}^{\xi},\ldots,b_{t^{\prime}}^{\xi})\circledast(a_{s+1}^{\eta},\ldots,a_{t}^{\eta},b_{s^{\prime}+1}^{\eta},\ldots,b_{t^{\prime}}^{\eta}).

Note that this implies, in particular, that the sets

\{a_{s+1}^{\xi},\ldots,a_{t}^{\xi},a_{s+1}^{\eta},\ldots,a_{t}^{\eta}\},

\{b_{s^{\prime}+1}^{\xi},\ldots,b_{t^{\prime}}^{\xi},b_{s^{\prime}+1}^{\eta},\ldots,b_{t^{\prime}}^{\eta}\}

are disjoint. We perform the similar trimming procedure for each element of
R
. Finally, we choose any distinct
\xi,\eta<\omega_{1}
, and define

g(n)=f_{\xi}(n)\cup f_{\eta}(n),

for all
n\in R
.
It is straightforward that
g\leq f_{\xi},f_{\eta}
.
∎

Take note that when we say

is a countable union of rectangles
, we refer to both the connectedness relation, and its complement. This means that the graph is both closed and open in the topology generated by the sides of the (countably many) rectangles. If

, we can easily refine this topology to be Hausdorff.

Definition 4.5
.

A graph

is

(resp.

), if

is an open (resp. closed) subset of

with respect to some second countable Hausdorff topology on

.

A graph

is

, if both

and

are open subsets of

with respect to some second countable Hausdorff topology on

.

3.

There exists an
\omega_{1}
-preserving partial order forcing that
G
is countably chromatic.

Proof.

"1.\Rightarrow 2."
We use the forcing

\mathbb{P}=\{f:\operatorname{dom}{f}\rightarrow\omega|\;\operatorname{dom}{f}\in[V]^{<\omega}\quad\forall x\neq y\in\operatorname{dom}{f}\;xEy\implies f(x)\neq f(y)\}.

In order to prove the c.c.c. fix a family
\{f_{\xi}|\;\xi<\omega_{1}\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}
. Without loss of generality the domains of
f_{\xi}
form a
\Delta
-system

\operatorname{dom}{f_{\xi}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},x_{k+1}^{\xi},\ldots,x_{m}^{\xi}).

We can moreover assume that the values
f_{\xi}(x_{i})
and
f_{\xi}(x_{j}^{\xi})
are independent of
\xi
, for all
i=1,\ldots,k
,
j=k+1,\ldots,m
. For every
\xi
, we fix a family of pairwise disjoint basic open neighbourhoods

\{U_{x}^{\xi}\ni x|\;x\in\operatorname{dom}{f_{\xi}}\},

with the property that

\forall x\neq y\in\operatorname{dom}{f_{\xi}}\quad\neg\;x\;E\;y\implies\forall x^{\prime}\in U^{\xi}_{x}\quad\forall y^{\prime}\in U^{\xi}_{y}\quad\neg\;x^{\prime}\;E\;y^{\prime}.

By another trimming, we can assume that the sets
U^{\xi}_{x}
are independent of
\xi
. Finally, we choose
\xi\neq\eta<\omega_{1}

satisfying
1.
It is standard to check that
f_{\xi}
and
f_{\eta}
are compatible.

"2.\Rightarrow 3."
Clear.

"3.\Rightarrow 1."
By the pigeonhole principle, any countably chromatic graph
G
satisfies
1.
Therefore
G
will satisfy
1.
after forcing with
\mathbb{P}
. But
1.
is a
\Pi_{1}
statement in the language of set theory with
\omega_{1}
as a parameter, therefore it must have been true in the ground model.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Assume
\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}
. Then any rectangular tournament of size
\omega_{1}
is a countable union of transitive tournaments.

Proof.

We apply Martin’s Axiom to the forcing

\{f:\operatorname{dom}{f}\rightarrow[\omega_{1}]^{<\omega}|\;\operatorname{dom}{f}\in[\omega]^{<\omega},\;\forall n\in\operatorname{dom}{f}\;f(n)\text{ is transitive.}\}

∎

For the proof of the c.c.c. just recall that transitive tournaments are essentially linear orders, so they satisfy the Strong Amalgamation Property.

5
Rectangular Structures from
\operatorname{CH}

We prove that non-trivial (i.e. HSS) rectangular models exist assuming
\operatorname{CH}
. This is essentially an abstract version of a similar theorem for linear orders, proved in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume
\operatorname{CH}
. Let
\mathcal{K}
be a class of structures in a language consisting of countably many binary relations, and assume that
\mathcal{K}
satisfies RSP and SAP. Then
\mathcal{K}
has a HSS rectangular model of size
\omega_{1}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that every
A\in\mathcal{K}
is of the form
(A,c)
, where
A
is a set, and
c:A^{2}\rightarrow\omega
. Let
\{M_{\alpha}|\;\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
be a continuous sequence of countable elementary submodels of
H(\omega_{1})
, and put
C=\{\alpha<\omega_{1}|\;M_{\alpha}\cap\omega_{1}=\alpha\}
. It is well-known that
C
is a closed unbounded subset of
\omega_{1}
. For
\alpha\in C
, we denote by
\alpha^{\prime}
the successor of
\alpha
in
C
. By induction on
\alpha\in C
we define an increasing sequence of filters
G_{\alpha}\subseteq\operatorname{Fn}(\alpha,\mathcal{K},\omega)
, ensuring that for any
\alpha\in C

G_{\alpha^{\prime}}\subseteq\operatorname{Fn}(\alpha^{\prime},\mathcal{K},\omega):G_{\alpha}\quad\text{ is }\quad\operatorname{Fn}(\alpha^{\prime},\mathcal{K},\omega):G_{\alpha}\text{-generic over }M_{\tau{(\alpha)}},

where
\tau{(\alpha)}
is big enough, so that
\{\alpha^{\prime},G_{\alpha}\}\in M_{\tau({\alpha})}
(recall that
\operatorname{Fn}(\alpha^{\prime},\mathcal{K},\omega):G_{\alpha}
denotes the set of those conditions from
\operatorname{Fn}(\alpha^{\prime},\mathcal{K},\omega)
that are compatible with every element from
G_{\alpha}
). Note that each of these filters is definable from a real, so
\tau(\alpha)
is well defined. Let
X=(\omega_{1},c)
be the union of all filters
G_{\alpha}
.

Why is
X
HSS? We will show that for any
\alpha\in C
, each final segment of
\alpha^{\prime}
is a saturating subset of
X
. Let
E\subseteq(\omega_{1},c)
be a finite substructure, with an extension
E\subseteq(E\cup\{e^{*}\},c^{*})
. For
e\in E
we set

l_{e}=c^{*}(e,e^{*}),

l^{*}_{e}=c^{*}(e^{*},e).

Case 1.

Suppose that
E\subseteq\alpha^{\prime}
. We will show that for every
\beta<\alpha^{\prime}
there exists
\delta\in(\beta,\alpha^{\prime})
, such that for any
e\in E
, we have

c(e,\delta)=l_{e},

c(\delta,e)=l_{e}^{*}.

Indeed, it is sufficient to show that the following set (belonging to
M_{\tau(\alpha)}
) is dense in the partial order
\operatorname{Fn}(\alpha^{\prime},\mathcal{K},\omega):G_{\alpha}
:

\{p\in\operatorname{Fn}(\alpha^{\prime},\mathcal{K},\omega):G_{\alpha}|\;\exists\;\delta\in(\beta,\alpha^{\prime})\quad\forall\;e\in E\quad p\Vdash\dot{c}(e,\delta)=l_{i},\;\dot{c}(\delta,e)=l^{*}_{e}\}.

To see this, fix some
p\in\operatorname{Fn}(\alpha^{\prime},\mathcal{K},\omega):G_{\alpha}
, and assume that
E\subseteq\operatorname{dom}{p}
(otherwise we can extend
p
, so that this is satisfied). Next, we fix
\delta\in(\beta,\alpha^{\prime})\setminus\operatorname{dom}{p}
and extend
p
to
p^{*}
by amalgamating the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p\ }
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{*}=p\cup\{\delta\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E\cup\{e^{*}\}}

To make sure that
p^{*}
is compatible with any condition from
G_{\alpha}
, notice that for any
q\in G_{\alpha}
we can find
p^{\prime}\leq p,q
, not containing
\delta
, and disjointly amalgamate over the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{*}\cap p^{\prime}=p}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\prime}}

Case 2.

Let
\alpha_{1}
be the minimal ordinal in
C
such that
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha
, and
E\subseteq\alpha_{1}^{\prime}
. We will prove by induction on
\alpha_{1}
that

\forall\;\beta<\alpha^{\prime}\;\exists\;\delta\in(\beta,\alpha^{\prime})\;\forall\;e\in E\quad c(e,\delta)=l_{e},\;c(\delta,e)=l_{e}^{*}.

If
\alpha_{1}=\alpha
, our task reduces to the previous case, so let as assume that whenever
\alpha_{0}\in C\cap\alpha_{1}
, the conclusion holds for every
E\subseteq\alpha_{0}^{\prime}
. In particular, it holds for
E\cap\alpha_{1}
. Define

I=\{\delta\in(\beta,\alpha^{\prime})|\;\forall\;e\in E\cap\alpha_{1}\quad c(e,\delta)=l_{e},\;c(\delta,e)=l_{e}^{*}\}.

By the inductive hypothesis,
I
is nonempty for any value of
\beta
, and so
|I|=\omega
. We must verify that the following set (belonging to
M_{\tau(\alpha_{1})}
) is dense in the partial order
\operatorname{Fn}(\alpha_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{K},\omega):G_{\alpha_{1}}
:

\{p\in\operatorname{Fn}(\alpha_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{K},\omega):G_{\alpha_{1}}|\;\exists\;\delta\in I\quad\forall\;e\in E\setminus\alpha_{1}\quad p\Vdash\dot{c}(e,\delta)=l_{i},\;\dot{c}(\delta,e)=l^{*}_{e}\}.

Notice that this set is in
M_{\tau(\alpha_{1})}
. The density argument exactly follows the Case 1, with
\alpha_{1}
in place of
\alpha
, and
E\setminus\alpha_{1}
in place of
E
.

Why is
X
rectangular? Consider a pairwise disjoint family

\{(x_{0}^{\xi},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\xi})|\;\xi<\omega_{1}\}\subseteq X^{n}.

We will be working with a fixed ordinal
\alpha\in C
with an additional property that
\alpha=N\cap\omega_{1}
, for some countable elementary submodel
N\prec H(\omega_{2})
, which contains the set

\{X,\{(x_{0}^{\xi},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\xi})|\;\xi<\omega_{1}\}\}.

This property of
N
ensures the following

Claim
.

1.

\forall\;\beta<\alpha\quad\{x_{0}^{\beta},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\beta}\}\subseteq\alpha
,

2.

\forall\;\beta>\alpha\quad\{x_{0}^{\beta},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\beta}\}\cap\alpha=\emptyset.

Proof of the Claim.

1.

For any
\beta<\alpha
,
\{x_{0}^{\beta},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\beta}\}\in N
. Since
\alpha=N\cap\omega_{1}
, the conclusion follows.

2.

Suppose towards contradiction that
\gamma\in\alpha\cap\{x_{0}^{\beta},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\beta}\}
, for some
\beta>\alpha
. By the elementarity,

N\models\exists\;\delta<\omega_{1}\quad\gamma\in\{x_{0}^{\delta},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\delta}\}.

Therefore we can choose
\delta<\alpha
, such that

\gamma\in\{x_{0}^{\beta},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\beta}\}\cap\{x_{0}^{\delta},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{\delta}\}.

But this contradicts the assumption that the tuples are pairwise disjoint.

∎

Fix some ordinal
\beta<\alpha
. We want to show that the following set is dense in
\operatorname{Fn}(\alpha^{\prime},\mathcal{K},\omega):G_{\alpha}
:

\{p\in\operatorname{Fn}(\alpha^{\prime},\mathcal{K},\omega):G_{\alpha}|\;\exists\;l<\omega\quad x^{\alpha+l}_{0},\ldots,x^{\alpha+l}_{n-1},x^{\beta}_{0},\ldots,x^{\beta}_{n-1}\in\operatorname{dom}{p},\quad

p\Vdash(x^{\alpha+l}_{0},\ldots,x^{\alpha+l}_{n-1})\circledast(x^{\beta}_{0},\ldots,x^{\beta}_{n-1})\}.

To see that this is the case, fix
p\in\operatorname{Fn}(\alpha^{\prime},\mathcal{K},\omega):G_{\alpha}
. We can of course assume that

\{x^{\beta}_{0},\ldots,x^{\beta}_{n-1}\}\subseteq\operatorname{dom}{p}
, and choose
l<\omega
such that
\{x^{\alpha+l}_{0},\ldots,x^{\alpha+l}_{n-1}\}\cap\operatorname{dom}{p}=\emptyset
. Let
p^{\circ}
denote the condition obtained by replacing each
x_{i}^{\beta}\in\operatorname{dom}{p}
with
x_{i}^{\alpha+l}
, and defining relations so that the extensions

p\setminus\{x^{\beta}_{0},\ldots,x^{\beta}_{n-1}\}\subseteq p,

and

p\setminus\{x^{\beta}_{0},\ldots,x^{\beta}_{n-1}\}\subseteq(p\setminus\{x^{\beta}_{0},\ldots,x^{\beta}_{n-1}\})\cup\{x^{\alpha+l}_{0},\ldots,x^{\alpha+l}_{n-1}\}

become isomorphic (via the function
x_{i}^{\beta}\mapsto x_{i}^{\alpha+l}
). We extend
p
to
p^{*}
by applying the RSP to the following diagram of isomorphic extensions
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p\ }
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p\setminus\{x^{\beta}_{0},\ldots,x^{\beta}_{n-1}\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\circ}}

By the RSP, we can amalgamate so that
p^{*}\Vdash(x^{\alpha+l}_{0},\ldots,x^{\alpha+l}_{n-1})\circledast(x^{\beta}_{0},\ldots,x^{\beta}_{n-1})
. To see that
p^{*}
is compatible with every element of
G_{\alpha}
, note that whenever
q\in G_{\alpha}
, we can find
p^{\prime}\leq p,q
disjoint with
\{x^{\alpha+l}_{0},\ldots,x^{\alpha+l}_{n-1}\}
, and then disjointly amalgamate over the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\prime}\cap p^{*}=p}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\prime}}

∎

6
Conclusions

Is the theory we develop entitled to be called "the uncountable Fraïssé theory with finite supports"? We left it to the reader to decide. Obviously, similarity of forcing notions

\operatorname{Fn}{(\omega,\mathcal{K},\omega)}

and

\operatorname{Fn}{(\omega_{1},\mathcal{K},\omega)}

shows that some "genericity" is common to Fraïssé limits and rectangular models. On the other hand, properties of models added by the latter forcing seem heavily dependent on the set-theoretic background, unlike those of countable homogeneous models. Moreover, it looks like Proposition
2.23
exhibits some strange asymmetry of rectangular models in the presence of
\operatorname{MA}_{\omega_{1}}
, which distinguishes them from their countable counterparts.

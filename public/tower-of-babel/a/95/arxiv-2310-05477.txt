Introduction

This paper is devoted to the following fractional
N/s
-Laplacian Choquard equation

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}_{N/s}u=\lambda|u|^{\frac{N}{s}-2}u+(I_{\mu}*F(u))f(u),\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.1)

where
s\in(0,1)
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
I_{\mu}(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{\mu}}
with
\mu\in(0,N)
,
F
is the primitive function of
f
, and
f
is a continuous function with exponential critical growth.

(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}
is the fractional
p
-Laplacian operator defined by

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x):=C(N,s)\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\mathrm{d}y,\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},

for
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
where
B_{\varepsilon}(x)
denotes the ball of radius
\varepsilon
around
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For further considerations about the fractional
p
-Laplacian operator,
we refer the readers to
[
9
]
.

We emphasize that the fractional
p
-Laplacian is nonlinear and nonlocal, so they bring additional difficulties. Another motivation to investigate (
1.1
) comes from the Choquard nonlinearity involving exponential critical growth. For
s\in(0,1)
, if
N>ps
, we know the classical Sobolev embedding that
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is continuous for
q\in[p,p_{s}^{*}]
, where
p_{s}^{*}:=Np/({N-ps})
is called the Sobolev critical exponent. However, if
N=ps
, then
p_{s}^{*}=\infty
and
W^{s,N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is not continuously embedded in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, see
[
9
]
for more details. In the case
N=ps
, the maximum growth that allows us to treat (
1.1
) variationally in the Sobolev space
W^{s,N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which is motivated by
the fractional Trudinger-Moser inequality first given by Ozawa
[
19
]
and later improved by Kozono et al.
[
14
]
(see also
[
22
,
10
,
28
]
). More precisely, inspired by
[
14
]
, we say
f(t)
has exponential critical growth if there exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{|t|\to+\infty}\frac{|f(t)|}{e^{\alpha|t|^{\frac{N}{N-s}}}}=\begin{cases}0,\quad&\mbox{for $\alpha>\alpha_{0}$,}\\
+\infty,\quad&\mbox{for $0<\alpha<\alpha_{0}$.}\end{cases}

To get solutions of
(\ref{pro1})
, one way is to fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and look for critical points of the energy functional
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}:W^{s,N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
(see e.g.
[
4
,
7
,
15
,
27
]
)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}(u)=

\displaystyle\frac{s}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{N/s}}{|x-y|^{2N}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-\frac{s\lambda}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{N/s}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))F(u)\mathrm{d}x,

where
W^{s,N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined by

W^{s,N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\big\{u\in L^{N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):[u]_{s,N/s}<\infty\big\},

here
[u]_{s,N/s}
denotes the Gagliardo seminorm, that is

[u]_{s,N/s}=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{N/s}}{|x-y|^{2N}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\bigg)^{s/{N}}.

It is well known that the space
\big(W^{s,N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\|\cdot\|_{W^{s,N/s}}\big)
, where
\|\cdot\|_{W^{s,N/s}}^{N/s}=[\cdot]_{s,N/s}^{N/s}+\|\cdot\|_{N/s}^{N/s}
, is a uniformly
convex Banach space, particularly reflexive, and separable (see
[
20
]
). We also remind the readers that
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is dense in
W^{s,N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
(see
[
1
]
).

Alternatively, from a physical point of view, it is interesting to find solutions of
(\ref{pro1})
having prescribed mass

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{N/s}\mathrm{d}x=a^{N/s},\quad\text{with $a>0$}.

(1.2)

In this direction, the parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
appears as a Lagrange multiplier, which depends on the solution and is not a priori given. This type of solution is called the normalized solution, and can be obtained by
looking for critical points of the following energy functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(u)=\frac{s}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{N/s}}{|x-y|^{2N}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))F(u)\mathrm{d}x

on the
L^{N/s}
-sphere

S(a)=\Big\{u\in W^{s,N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{N/s}\mathrm{d}x=a^{N/s}\Big\}.

In the past few years, many researchers have studied normalized solutions for nonlinear elliptic equations, see
[

,
6
,
3
,

,

,
23
,

]
for Laplacian equations,
[

Considering the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation with a
L^{2}
-constraint

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u=\lambda u+f(u),\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=a^{2}.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(1.3)

If
f(u)=|u|^{p-2}u
,
p\in(2,2^{*}]
with
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
if
N\geq 3
,
2^{*}=+\infty
if
N=1,2
, by using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

]
, a new critical

which is called

-critical exponent appears. In this case, the associated energy functional of (
1.3
) is defined by

\widehat{\mathcal{J}}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x.

If
2<p<2+\frac{4}{N}
(
L^{2}
-subcritical), then
\widehat{\mathcal{J}}
is bounded from below on
\widehat{S}(a):=\big\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=a^{2}\big\}

\widehat{\mathcal{J}}|_{\widehat{S}(a)}
as a solution of (
1.3

[

,
21
]
. While, if
2+\frac{4}{N}<p\leq 2^{*}
(
L^{2}
-supercritical),

is unbounded from below on
\widehat{S}(a)
, so it seems impossible to search for a global minimizer to obtain a solution.
Jeanjean
[

first obtained a normalized solution of (

-supercritical growth.
By using a minimax principle,

[

presented a new approach, which is based on a natural constraint, and also proved the existence of normalized solutions in this case. Inspired by
[
11
,
3
]
, Soave
[
23
]
studied (
1.3

f(u)=\omega|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{r-2}u
,

,
2<q\leq 2+4/N\leq r<2^{*}
and
q<r
.
Existence and asymptotic properties of normalized solutions, as well as stability and instability results were established in
[
23
]
. The Sobolev critical case

with
N\geq 3
was considered by Soave
[
24
]
. In the case
N=2
and

has exponential critical growth, the existence of normalized solutions of (

) has been discussed by Alves et al.
[

]
.
Besides, Chen et al.
[

]
considered normalized solutions of Schrödinger equations with exponential critical nonlinearities in

.

For

-Laplacian equations,
[

is the first paper to study normalized solutions of

-Laplacian equations with a

-constraint

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u+\omega|u|^{q-2}u+f(u),\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x=a^{p},\end{cases}

where

,

Furthermore, Deng and Wu
[

considered (

Motivated by the results already mentioned above, especially
[
2
,
8
]
, a natural question is whether the normalized solutions exist for problem (
1.1
) -(
1.2
). To reach the conclusion, we
give the following assumptions.

Assume that
f
satisfies:

(f_{1})

f\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
, and
\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\frac{|f(t)|}{|t|^{\kappa}}=0
for some
\kappa>\frac{3N-2s-\mu}{2s}

(f_{2})

\alpha_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{|t|\to+\infty}\frac{|f(t)|}{e^{\alpha|t|^{\frac{N}{N-s}}}}=\begin{cases}0,\quad&\mbox{for $\alpha>\alpha_{0}$,}\\
+\infty,\quad&\mbox{for $0<\alpha<\alpha_{0}$;}\end{cases}

(f_{3})
there exists a constant
\theta>\frac{3N-\mu}{2s}
such that

for any

;

(f_{4})
there exist
\tau>0
and
\sigma>0
such that
F(t)\geq\frac{\tau}{\sigma}|t|^{\sigma}
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Our main result can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
(f_{1})
-
(f_{4})
hold, then there exists
\tau_{*}>0
such that for any
\tau\geq\tau_{*}
, problem

-(

) has a radial solution.

.

A typical example satisfying
(f_{1})
-
(f_{4})
is

\displaystyle f(t)=\tau|t|^{\sigma-2}te^{\alpha_{0}|t|^{\frac{N}{N-s}}}

for any
\sigma>\max\{\kappa+1,\theta\}
.

3
Variational framework

First of all, to make the notation concise, for
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we set

R(\alpha,t)=e^{\alpha|t|^{\frac{N}{N-s}}}-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{j_{N,s}-2}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!}|t|^{\frac{Nj}{N-s}}=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j_{N,s}-1}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!}|t|^{\frac{Nj}{N-s}},

where
j_{N,s}=\min\{j\in\mathbb{N}^{+}:j\geq N/s\}
.

By using assumptions
(f_{1})
and
(f_{2})
, it follows that for any
\zeta>0
,
q>\frac{3N}{2s}
and
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
, there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|f(t)|\leq\zeta|t|^{\kappa}+C|t|^{q-1}R(\alpha,t),\quad\mbox{for all $t\in\mathbb{R}$,}

and using
(f_{3})
, we have

\displaystyle|F(t)|\leq\zeta|t|^{\kappa+1}+C|t|^{q}R(\alpha,t),\quad\mbox{for all $t\in\mathbb{R}$.}

(3.1)

Using (
2.1
), (
3.1
) and Lemma
2.2
, we know
\mathcal{J}
is well defined in
W^{s,N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and of class

with

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\frac{N}{s}-2}[u(x)-u(y)][v(x)-v(y)]}{|x-y|^{2N}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))f(u)v\mathrm{d}x,

for any

. Hence, a critical point of
\mathcal{J}|_{S(a)}

corresponds to a solution of problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

To understand the geometry of
\mathcal{J}|_{S(a)}
, for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in W^{s,N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we define

\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)(x):=e^{s\beta}u(e^{\beta}x),\quad\text{for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

One can easily check that
\|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{N/s}=\|u\|_{N/s}
for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus, we can investigate the structure of
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta))
to speculate the structure of
\mathcal{J}|_{S(a)}
. Denote
S_{r}(a)=S(a)\cap W_{rad}^{s,N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
W_{rad}^{s,N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the subset of the radially symmetric functions in
W^{s,N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
(f_{1})
-
(f_{3})
hold. Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{r}(a)
be a sequence and satisfy
\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}[u_{n}]_{s,N/s}^{N/s}<\big[\frac{(2N-\mu)\alpha_{*}}{2N\alpha_{0}}\big]^{(N-s)/{s}}
. If

in

, then there exists
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
close to
\alpha_{0}
such that for all

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{q}R(\alpha,u_{n})\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}R(\alpha,u)\mathrm{d}x,\,\,\,\text{as}\,\,\,n\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

First, we claim that there exist
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
close to
\alpha_{0}
,
\nu>1
close to
1
such that
R(\alpha,u_{n})
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Indeed, fix
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
close to
\alpha_{0}
, there exists
\nu>1
close to
1

\nu\alpha\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}[u_{n}]_{s,N/s}^{N/(N-s)}<\alpha_{*}.

Hence, by Lemma
2.2
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}R(\alpha,u_{n})^{\nu}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}R\Big(\alpha[u_{n}]_{s,N/s}^{N/(N-s)},\frac{u_{n}}{[u_{n}]_{s,N/s}}\Big)^{\nu}\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}R\Big(\nu\alpha[u_{n}]_{s,N/s}^{N/(N-s)},\frac{u_{n}}{[u_{n}]_{s,N/s}}\Big)\mathrm{d}x\leq C,

this proves the claim.
Since
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
W_{rad}^{s,N/s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then
u_{n}\rightarrow u
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By Lemma

R(\alpha,u_{n})\rightharpoonup R(\alpha,u)
in

. Moreover, for

Thus, by the definition of weak convergence, using the Hölder inequality, we infer that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{q}R(\alpha,u_{n})\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}R(\alpha,u)\mathrm{d}x\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big||u_{n}|^{q}-|u|^{q}\big|R(\alpha,u_{n})\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}|R(\alpha,u_{n})-R(\alpha,u)|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big||u_{n}|^{q}-|u|^{q}\big|^{\nu^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{\nu^{\prime}}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}R(\alpha,u_{n})^{\nu}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}|R(\alpha,u_{n})-R(\alpha,u)|\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow 0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

First, we claim that

\displaystyle\big|I_{\mu}\ast F(u_{n})\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\zeta|u_{n}|^{\kappa+1}+C|u_{n}|^{q}R(\alpha,u_{n})}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

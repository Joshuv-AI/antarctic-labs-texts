Weak convergence of delay SDEs with applications to Carathéodory approximation
T. C. Son, N. T. DungN. V. Tan, T. M. Cuong, H. T. P. Thao, P. D. Tung

Abstract
In this paper, we consider a fundamental class of stochastic differential equations with time delays. Our aim is to investigate the weak convergence with respect to delay parameter of the solutions. Based on the techniques of Malliavin calculus, we obtain an explicit estimate for the rate of convergence. An application to the Carathéodory approximation scheme of stochastic differential equations is provided as well.



Keywords:
Delay SDEs, Weak convergence, Carathéodory approximation, Malliavin calculus.

2010 Mathematics Subject Classification:
65C30, 60H10, 60H07.

1
Introduction

It is known that the Carathéodory approximation scheme was introduced by Carathéodory in the early part of 20th century for deterministic differential equations, see e.g.
[
5
]
. In the context of stochastic equations, the first Carathéodory approximation results were obtained by Bell and Mohammed
[
3
]
(also see Section 2.6 in
[
14
]
for a general formulation). Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R},n\geq 1
and
(B(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
be a standard Brownian motion. We consider stochastic differential equation (SDE)

x(t)=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,x(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,x(s))dB(s),\,\,\,t\in[0,T]

(1.1)

and its Carathéodory approximation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x^{n}(t)=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,x^{n}(s-1/n))ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,x^{n}(s-1/n))dB(s),\,\,\,t\in[0,T]\\
\\
x^{n}(t)=x_{0},\,\,\,t\in[-1/n,0].\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

It was proved in
[
3
,
14
]
that, when the coefficients are Lipschitz and have linear growth,
x^{n}(t)
strongly converges to
x(t)
as
n\to\infty.
Moreover, the following estimate for the strong rate of convergence holds

E\left[\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}|x^{n}(t)-x(t)|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{C}{n},\,\,\,n\geq 1,

(1.3)

where
C
is a positive constant not depending on
n

As discussed in Section 2.6 of
[
14
]
, the advantage of Carathéodory approximation is that we do not need to compute
x^{1}(t),\cdots,x^{n-1}(t)
but compute
x^{n}(t)
directly (this reduces a lot of calculations on stochastic integrals). In addition, the Carathéodory approximation also works well for SDEs with non-Lipschitz coefficients. In the last decades, the Carathéodory approximation has been considered for various stochastic differential equations. Among others, we mention Turo
[
19
]
for stochastic functional differential equations, Mao
[
12
,
13
]
and Liu
[
11
]
for semilinear stochastic evolution equations with time delays, Ferrante & Rovira
[
8
]
for delay differential equations driven by fractional Brownian motion, Faizullah
[
7
]
for SDEs under G-Brownian motion, Benabdallah & Bourza
[
2
]
for perturbed SDEs with reflecting boundary, Mao et al.
[
15
]
for doubly perturbed SDEs, etc.

It is also known that the weak convergence rate of numerical approximations is very useful in practical applications (see, e.g.
[
1
]
for a short discussion). In fact, for certain numerical schemes such as Milstein scheme, Runge-Kutta scheme and Euler-Maruyama scheme, etc. many weak convergence results can be found in the literature, see e.g.
[
1
,
4
,
9
,
10
]
. However, to the best of our knowledge, the weak convergence results of the Carathéodory approximation are scarce even for the system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). Motivated by this observation, our aim is to partially fill up this gap.

In this paper, to make the problem more interesting, we consider delay stochastic differential equations of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}X_{\tau_{1}}(t)=\varphi(0)+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X_{\tau_{1}}(s),X_{\tau_{1}}(s-\tau_{1}))ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X_{\tau_{1}}(s),X_{\tau_{1}}(s-\tau_{1}))dB(s),\,t\in[0,T]\\
\\
X_{\tau_{1}}(t)=\varphi(t),\,\,\,t\in[-\tau_{1},0]\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}X_{\tau_{2}}(t)=\varphi(0)+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X_{\tau_{2}}(s),X_{\tau_{2}}(s-\tau_{2}))ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X_{\tau_{2}}(s),X_{\tau_{2}}(s-\tau_{2}))dB(s),\,t\in[0,T]\\
\\
X_{\tau_{2}}(t)=\varphi(t),\,\,\,t\in[-\tau_{2},0],\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

where
0\leq\tau_{1},\tau_{2}<\infty
and
\varphi:(-\infty,0]\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded deterministic function. Our aim is to study the weak convergence of
X_{\tau_{2}}(t)
to
X_{\tau_{1}}(t)
as
\tau_{2}\to\tau_{1}.
More specifically, we will employ the techniques of Malliavin calculus to provide a quantitative estimate for the quantity

|Eg(X_{\tau_{2}}(t))-Eg(X_{\tau_{1}}(t))|,

where
g
is a bounded and measurable function, see Theorem
3.1
. Our method is different from the existing ones in the literature and based on a new result established recently in
[
6
]
. The restatement of this new result and further comments will be given in Lemma
2.1
and Remark
2.1
.

Our results applied to the Carathéodory approximation system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) yield

\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}|Eg(x^{n}(t))-Eg(x(t))|\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}},\,\,\,n\geq 1.

(1.6)

It should be noted that, when the test function
g
is Lipschitz continuous, the strong rate (
1.3
) implies the weak rate (
1.6
). However, for bounded and measurable test functions, there is no such implication and hence, the novelty of our results lies in the fact that (
1.6
) holds true for any bounded and measurable test function. The price to pay is that, besides Lipschitz and linear growth conditions, we have to impose additional assumptions on the coefficients, see conditions
(ii)
-
(iii)
of Corollary
3.3
.

The rest of this article is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some concepts of Malliavin calculus and a general result obtained in our recent work
[
6
]
. Our main results are then stated and proved in Section 3. The conclusion and some remarks are given in Section 4.

2
Preliminaries

As we have said in the Introduction, this paper is based on techniques of Malliavin calculus. For the reader’s convenience, let us recall some elements of Malliavin calculus (for more details see
[
16
]
). We suppose that
(B(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
is defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},P)
, where
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a natural filtration generated by the Brownian motion
B.
For
h\in L^{2}[0,T],
we denote by
B(h)
the Wiener integral

B_{h}=\int_{0}^{T}h(t)dB(t).

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the dense subset of
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
consisting of smooth random variables of the form

F=f(B_{h_{1}},...,B_{h_{n}}),

(2.1)

where
n\in\mathbb{N},h_{1},...,h_{n}\in L^{2}[0,T]
and
f
is an infinitely
differentiable function such that together with all its partial derivatives
has at most polynomial growth order. If
F
has the form (
2.1
), we define its Malliavin derivative as the process
DF:=\{D_{t}F,t\in[0,T]\}
given by

D_{t}F=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}(B_{h_{1}},...,B_{h_{n}})h_{k}(t).

More generally, for each
k\geq 1,
we can define the iterated derivative operator by setting
D^{k}_{t_{1},...,t_{k}}F=D_{t_{1}}...D_{t_{k}}F.
For any
p,k\geq 1,
we shall denote by
\mathbb{D}^{k,p}
the closure of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to the norm

\|F\|^{p}_{k,p}:=E|F|^{p}+E\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|D_{t_{1}}F|^{p}dt_{1}\bigg]+...+E\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}...\int_{0}^{T}|D^{k}_{t_{1},...,t_{k}}F|^{p}dt_{1}...dt_{k}\bigg].

A random variable
F
is said to be Malliavin differentiable if it belongs to
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}.
For the convenience of the reader, we recall that the derivative
operator
D
satisfy the chain rule, i.e.

D\varphi(F)=\varphi^{\prime}(F)DF.

(2.2)

Moreover, we have the following relations between Malliavin derivative and the integrals

D_{r}\left(\int_{0}^{T}u(s)ds\right)=\int_{r}^{T}D_{r}u(s)ds,

D_{r}\left(\int_{0}^{T}u(s)dB(s)\right)=u_{r}+\int_{r}^{T}D_{r}u(s)dB(s)

for all
0\leq r\leq T,
where
(u(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted and Malliavin differentiable stochastic process.

An important operator in the Malliavin’s calculus theory is the divergence operator
\delta
, it is the adjoint of the derivative
operator
D
. The domain of
\delta
is the set of all functions
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,L^{2}[0,T])
such that for all
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
it holds that

E|\langle DF,u\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}|\leq C(u)\|F\|_{L^{2}[0,T]},

where
C(u)
is some positive constant depending on
u
. In particular, if
u\in dom\delta
, then
\delta(u)
is characterized by following duality relationship

E\langle DF,u\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}=E[F\delta(u)]\text{ for any }F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}.

(2.3)

Let
\mathcal{B}
denote the space of measurable functions
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\|g\|_{\infty}:=\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|g(x)|\leq 1.
We have the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
F_{1}\in\mathbb{D}^{2,2}
satisfy that
\|DF_{1}\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}>0\,\,a.s.
Then, for any random variable
F_{2}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and any
g\in\mathcal{B},
we have

\displaystyle|Eg(F_{1})-Eg(F_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(E\|DF_{1}\|^{-8}_{L^{2}[0,T]}E\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\theta}D_{r}F_{1}|^{2}d\theta dr\right)^{2}+(E\|DF_{1}\|^{-2}_{L^{2}[0,T]})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{1,2},

provided that the expectations exist, where
C
is an absolute constant.

Proof.

This lemma comes from Theorem 3.1 in our recent paper
[
6
]
. Here, for the reader’s convenience, we recall its proof. We write
\langle.,.\rangle
instead of
\langle.,.\rangle_{L^{2}[0,T]}
and
\|.\|
instead of
\|.\|_{L^{2}[0,T]}.
By the routine approximation argument, we can assume that
g
is continuous. Indeed, for example, we can approximate
g
by bounded and Lipschitz continuous functions defined by

g_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm 1\hskip-3.87495pt1}_{\{|y|<\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\}}g(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)dy,\,\,\varepsilon>0,

where
\rho_{\varepsilon}
is the standard mollifier:
\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{\rho(x/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon},
where
\rho(x)=C{\rm 1\hskip-3.87495pt1}_{\{|x|<1\}}e^{\frac{1}{x^{2}-1}}
and
C
is a constant such that
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\rho(x)dx=1.

The function
\psi(y):=\int_{-\infty}^{y}g(z)dz
is differentiable with bounded derivative. Hence,
\psi(F_{i})\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
for
i=1,2.
We obtain

\langle D\psi(F_{i}),DF_{1}\rangle=g(F_{i})\langle DF_{i},DF_{1}\rangle,\,\,\,i=1,2,

or equivalently

\langle D\int_{-\infty}^{F_{i}}g(z)dz,DF_{1}\rangle=g(F_{i})\langle DF_{i},DF_{1}\rangle,\,\,\,i=1,2.

As a consequence, we get

\displaystyle\langle

\displaystyle D\int_{F_{2}}^{F_{1}}g(z)dz,DF_{1}\rangle=g(F_{1})\langle DF_{1},DF_{1}\rangle-g(F_{2})\langle DF_{2},DF_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(g(F_{1})-g(F_{2})\right)\langle DF_{1},DF_{1}\rangle+g(F_{2})\langle DF_{1}-DF_{2},DF_{1}\rangle,

and hence,

g(F_{1})-g(F_{2})=\dfrac{\langle D\int_{F_{2}}^{F_{1}}g(z)dz,DF_{1}\rangle}{\|DF_{1}\|^{2}}-\dfrac{g(F_{2})\langle DF_{1}-DF_{2},DF_{1}\rangle}{\|DF_{1}\|^{2}}.

Taking the expectation yields

\displaystyle Eg(F_{1})-Eg(F_{2})

\displaystyle=E\left[\dfrac{\langle D\int_{F_{2}}^{F_{1}}g(z)dz,DF_{1}\rangle}{\|DF_{1}\|^{2}}\right]-E\left[\dfrac{g(F_{2})\langle DF_{1}-DF_{2},DF_{1}\rangle}{\|DF_{1}\|^{2}}\right].

(2.4)

Now we consider the stochastic process

u_{r}:=\dfrac{D_{r}F_{1}}{\|DF_{1}\|^{2}},\ \ 0\leq r\leq T

and use Proposition 1.3.1 in
[
16
]
to get

\displaystyle E[\delta(u)^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}E|u_{r}|^{2}dr+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}E|D_{\theta}u_{r}|^{2}d\theta dr

\displaystyle=E\|DF_{1}\|^{-2}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}E|D_{\theta}u_{r}|^{2}d\theta dr

(2.5)

By the chain rule (
2.2
) for Malliavin derivatives, we have

D_{\theta}u_{r}=\dfrac{D_{\theta}D_{r}F_{1}}{\|DF_{1}\|^{2}}-2\dfrac{D_{r}F_{1}\langle D_{\theta}DF_{1},DF_{1}\rangle}{\|DF_{1}\|^{4}},\ \ 0\leq\theta,r\leq T.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}E|D_{\theta}u_{r}|^{2}d\theta dr

\displaystyle\leq 2E\left[\dfrac{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\theta}D_{r}F_{1}|^{2}d\theta dr}{\|DF_{1}\|^{4}}\right]+8E\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\dfrac{|D_{r}F_{1}|^{2}\|D_{\theta}DF_{1}\|^{2}}{\|DF_{1}\|^{6}}d\theta dr

\displaystyle=2E\left[\dfrac{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\theta}D_{r}F_{1}|^{2}d\theta dr}{\|DF_{1}\|^{4}}\right]+8E\left[\dfrac{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\theta}D_{r}F_{1}|^{2}d\theta dr}{\|DF_{1}\|^{4}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 10\left(E\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\theta}D_{r}F_{1}|^{2}d\theta dr\right)^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(E\left[\|DF_{1}\|^{-8}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.6)

Inserting (
2.6
) into (
2.5
) yields

E[\delta(u)^{2}]\leq E\|DF_{1}\|^{-2}+10\left(E\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\theta}D_{r}F_{1}|^{2}d\theta dr\right)^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(E\left[\|DF_{1}\|^{-8}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.7)

By using the relation (
2.3
), the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
\|g\|_{\infty}\leq 1,
it follows from (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle|Eg(F_{1})-Eg(F_{2})|\leq E\left|\int_{F_{2}}^{F_{1}}g(z)dz\delta\left(u\right)\right|+E\left|\dfrac{g(F_{2})\langle DF_{1}-DF_{2},DF_{1}\rangle}{\|DF_{1}\|^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(E|F_{1}-F_{2}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(E[\delta\left(u\right)^{2}]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+E\left|\dfrac{\langle DF_{1}-DF_{2},DF_{1}\rangle}{\|DF_{1}\|^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(E|F_{1}-F_{2}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(E[\delta\left(u\right)^{2}]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+E\left[\dfrac{\|DF_{1}-DF_{2}\|}{\|DF_{1}\|}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(E|F_{1}-F_{2}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(E[\delta\left(u\right)^{2}]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(E\|DF_{1}-DF_{2}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(E\|DF_{1}\|^{-2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.8)

So we can obtain the desired conclusion by inserting (
2.7
) into (
2.8
) and then using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Lemma
2.1
shows that the weak convergence can be controlled by the norm
\|.\|_{1,2}
in the space of Malliavin differentiable random variables. In its statement, we do not require any special structure of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}.
We therefore believe that Lemma
2.1
can be applied to other numerical schemes such as Euler-Maruyama and Runge-Kutta schemes. In the present paper, the main reason for choosing Carathéodory scheme is due to the lack of weak convergence results for this scheme in the literature. It is also worth mentioning that the proof of Lemma
2.1
heavily relies on dimension one. The generalization to higher dimensions will be a difficult and interesting problem.

3
The main results

In the whole paper, we are going to impose the following assumptions:

Assumption 3.1
.

The functions
b,\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
are Lipschitz and have linear growth, i.e. there exists
L>0
such that

|b(t,x_{1},y_{1})-b(t,x_{2},y_{2})|+|\sigma(t,x_{1},y_{1})-\sigma(t,x_{2},y_{2})|\leq L(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|y_{1}-y_{2}|)

for all
t\in[0,T],x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and

|b(t,x,y)|+|\sigma(t,x,y)|\leq L(|x|+|y|+1),\,\,\forall\,t\in[0,T],x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

Assumption 3.2
.

For every
t\in[0,T],

b(t,.,.),\sigma(t,.,.)
are twice differentiable functions with the partial derivatives bounded by
L
.

Hereafter, we denote by
C
(with or without an index) a generic constant which may vary at each appearance. For any function
h
of
n
variables, we denote

h^{\prime}_{i}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}):=\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{i}}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}),\quad h^{\prime\prime}_{ij}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}):=\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}).

For any
a,b\in\mathbb{R},
we denote
a\vee b=\max\{a,b\}.
In the proofs, we frequently use the fundamental inequality

(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})^{p}\leq n^{p-1}(a_{1}^{p}+\cdots+a_{n}^{p})

for all
a_{1},...,a_{n}\geq 0
and
p\geq 2.

3.1
Malliavin derivative of delay SDEs

In this Subsection, given
\tau\geq 0,
we investigate Malliavin derivatives of the solution to the following delay SDE

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}X_{\tau}(t)=\varphi(0)+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X_{\tau}(s),X_{\tau}(s-\tau))ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X_{\tau}(s),X_{\tau}(s-\tau))dB(s),\,\,\,t\in[0,T],\\
\\
X_{\tau}(t)=\varphi(t),\quad t\in[-\tau,0].\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

For the Malliavin differentiability of the solutions, we have the following.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let Assumption
3.1
hold. Then, the equation (
3.1
) has a unique solution
(X_{\tau}(t))_{t\in[-\tau,T]}
and this solution is Malliavin differentiable. Moreover, the derivative
D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(t)
satisfies

(i) When
t\in[-\tau,0],

D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(t)=0
for all
0\leq\theta\leq T,

(ii) When
t\in(0,T],

D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(t)=0
for
\theta>t
and

\displaystyle D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(t)

\displaystyle=\sigma(\theta,X_{\tau}(\theta),X_{\tau}(\theta-\tau))+\int_{\theta}^{t}\bar{b}^{\tau}_{2}(s)D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s)ds+\int_{\theta+\tau}^{t}\bar{b}^{\tau}_{3}(s)D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s-\tau)ds

\displaystyle+\int_{\theta}^{t}\bar{\sigma}^{\tau}_{2}(s)D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s)dB(s)+\int_{\theta+\tau}^{t}\bar{\sigma}^{\tau}_{3}(s)D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s-\tau)dB(s),\,\,\,0\leq\theta\leq t-\tau,

(3.2)

\displaystyle D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(t)=\sigma(\theta,X_{\tau}(\theta),X_{\tau}(\theta-\tau))+\int_{\theta}^{t}\bar{b}^{\tau}_{2}(s)D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s)ds+\int_{\theta}^{t}\bar{\sigma}^{\tau}_{2}(s)D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s)dB(s),\,\,\,(t-\tau)\vee 0<\theta\leq t,

(3.3)

where
\bar{b}^{\tau}_{2}(s)
,
\bar{b}^{\tau}_{3}(s)
,
\bar{\sigma}^{\tau}_{2}(s)
,
\bar{\sigma}^{\tau}_{3}(s)
are adapted stochastic processes and bounded by the Lipschitz constant
L.
Here, we use the convention
[0,t-\tau]=\emptyset
if
t<\tau.

Proof.

For
t\in[-\tau,0]
, we have
X_{\tau}(t)=\varphi(t)
is deterministic. Hence, the Malliavin derivative of
X_{\tau}(t)
always vanishes. On the other hand, since the solution
(X_{\tau}(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
is
\mathbb{F}
-adapted, we always have
D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(t)=0
for
\theta>t
. When
\theta\leq t
, by using the same argument as in the proof of Theorem 2.2.1 in
[
16
]
, we can show that the solution
(X_{\tau}(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
is also Malliavin differentiable. Applying the operator
D
to the equation (
3.1
) we deduce

\displaystyle D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(t)

\displaystyle=\sigma(\theta,X_{\tau}(\theta),X_{\tau}(\theta-\tau))

\displaystyle+\int_{\theta}^{t}D_{\theta}[b(s,X_{\tau}(s),X_{\tau}(s-\tau))]ds+\int_{\theta}^{t}D_{\theta}[\sigma(s,X_{\tau}(s),X_{\tau}(s-\tau))]dB_{s},\,\,\,t\in(0,T].

(3.4)

By Proposition 1.2.4 in
[
16
]
and Lipchitz property of
b
and
\sigma
, there exist adapted processes
\bar{b}^{\tau}_{2}(s)
,
\bar{b}^{\tau}_{3}(s)
,
\bar{\sigma}^{\tau}_{2}(s)
and
\bar{\sigma}^{\tau}_{3}(s)
, uniformly bounded by
L
such that

D_{\theta}\left[b(s,X_{\tau}(s),X_{\tau}(s-\tau))\right]=\bar{b}^{\tau}_{2}(s)D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s)+\bar{b}^{\tau}_{3}(s)D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s-\tau),

(3.5)

D_{\theta}\left[\sigma(s,X_{\tau}(s),X_{\tau}(s-\tau))\right]=\bar{\sigma}^{\tau}_{2}(s)D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s)+\bar{\sigma}^{\tau}_{3}(s)D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s-\tau).

(3.6)

Because
D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s-\tau)=0
for
\theta>s-\tau,
we obtain the equations (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) by inserting (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) into (
3.4
). So the proof of Proposition is complete.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

If
b(t,\cdot,\cdot)
and
\sigma(t,\cdot,\cdot)
are continuously differentiable, then
\bar{b}^{\tau}_{2}(s)=b_{2}^{\prime}(s,X_{\tau}(s),X_{\tau}(s-\tau))
,
\bar{b}^{\tau}_{3}(s)=b_{3}^{\prime}(s,X_{\tau}(s),X_{\tau}(s-\tau))
,
\bar{\sigma}^{\tau}_{2}(s)=\sigma_{2}^{\prime}(s,X_{\tau}(s),X_{\tau}(s-\tau))
,
\bar{\sigma}^{\tau}_{3}(s)=\sigma_{3}^{\prime}(s,X_{\tau}(s),X_{\tau}(s-\tau))
. Consequently, under Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(t)

\displaystyle=\sigma(\theta,X_{\tau}(\theta),X_{\tau}(\theta-\tau))+\int_{\theta}^{t}b_{2}^{\prime}(s,X_{\tau}(s),X_{\tau}(s-\tau))D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s)ds

\displaystyle+\int_{\theta+\tau}^{t}b_{3}^{\prime}(s,X_{\tau}(s),X_{\tau}(s-\tau))D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s-\tau)ds+\int_{\theta}^{t}\sigma_{2}^{\prime}(s,X_{\tau}(s),X_{\tau}(s-\tau))D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s)dB(s)

\displaystyle+\int_{\theta+\tau}^{t}\sigma_{3}^{\prime}(s,X_{\tau}(s),X_{\tau}(s-\tau))D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(s-\tau)dB(s),\,\,0\leq\theta\leq t-\tau

(3.7)

\displaystyle D_{\theta}X_{\tau}(t)

E|I_{2,\tau_{1}}(\theta,t)|^{2}+E|J_{2,\tau_{1}}(\theta,t)|^{2}\leq C_{L,T}(t-\theta-\tau_{1})
and hence,

\int_{t-\tau_{2}}^{t-\tau_{1}}[E|I_{2,\tau_{1}}(\theta,t)|^{2}+E|J_{2,\tau_{1}}(\theta,t)|^{2}]d\theta\leq C_{L,T}|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{2}\leq C_{L,T}\,t|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|.

Combining the above estimates yields

\displaystyle E\|D

\displaystyle X_{\tau_{1}}(t)-DX_{\tau_{2}}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}

\displaystyle\leq C_{L,T}\left(t^{2}|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{2}+t\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{1}}|\varphi(s-\tau_{1})-\varphi(s-\tau_{2})|^{4}ds+t\int_{t\wedge\tau_{1}}^{t\wedge\tau_{2}}|\varphi(0)-\varphi(s-\tau_{2})|^{4}ds\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+C_{L,T}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\theta}^{t}E|D_{\theta}X_{\tau_{1}}(s)-D_{\theta}X_{\tau_{2}}(s)|^{2}dsd\theta

\displaystyle=C_{L,T}\left(t^{2}|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{2}+t\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{1}}|\varphi(s-\tau_{1})-\varphi(s-\tau_{2})|^{4}ds+t\int_{t\wedge\tau_{1}}^{t\wedge\tau_{2}}|\varphi(0)-\varphi(s-\tau_{2})|^{4}ds\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+C_{L,T}\int_{0}^{t}E\|DX_{\tau_{1}}(s)-DX_{\tau_{2}}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}ds,\,\,\,t>\tau_{2},

which, by the Gronwall-type lemma (see, Theorem 1.4.2 in

), gives us (

) when
t>\tau_{2}.

We omit the detailed proof of
Case 2:
\tau_{1}<t\leq\tau_{2}
and
Case 3:
t\leq\tau_{1}
because the arguments of

can be replicated.

The proof of Proposition is complete.
∎

We now are ready to state and prove the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let Assumption
3.1
and
3.2
hold. We assume, in addition, that

|\sigma(t,x,y)|\geq\sigma_{0}>0,\,\,\forall\,t\in[0,T],x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

Then, for any
g\in\mathcal{B}
and
t\in(0,T],
we have

\displaystyle|Eg(X_{\tau_{1}}(t))

\displaystyle-Eg(X_{\tau_{2}}(t))|\leq C_{T,L}\bigg(|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle+t^{-1}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{1}}|\varphi(s-\tau_{1})-\varphi(s-\tau_{2})|^{4}ds+t^{-1}\int_{t\wedge\tau_{1}}^{t\wedge\tau_{2}}|\varphi(0)-\varphi(s-\tau_{2})|^{4}ds\bigg)^{1/4},

(3.32)

where
C_{L,T}
is a positive constant.

Proof.

Fixed
t\in(0,T],
we consider the random variables
F_{1}=X_{\tau_{1}}(t)
and
F_{2}=X_{\tau_{2}}(t).
Thanks to Propositions
3.2
,
3.3
,
3.4
and
3.5
we have the following

E\|DF_{1}\|^{-8}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\leq C_{T,L}\,t^{-4},\,\,\,(E\|DF_{1}\|^{-2}_{L^{2}[0,T]})^{2}\leq C_{T,L}\,t^{-2},

\displaystyle E\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\theta}D_{r}F_{1}|^{2}d\theta dr\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=E\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}|D_{\theta}D_{r}E|X_{\tau_{1}}(t)|^{2}d\theta dr\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq t^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}E|D_{\theta}D_{r}E|X_{\tau_{1}}(t)|^{4}d\theta dr

\displaystyle\leq C_{L,T}\,t^{4},

(3.33)

and

\displaystyle\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{1,2}=\left(E|X_{\tau_{1}}(t)-X_{\tau_{2}}(t)|^{2}+E\|DX_{\tau_{1}}(t)-DX_{\tau_{2}}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,L}\left(t^{2}|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{2}+t\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{1}}|\varphi(s-\tau_{1})-\varphi(s-\tau_{2})|^{4}ds+t\int_{t\wedge\tau_{1}}^{t\wedge\tau_{2}}|\varphi(0)-\varphi(s-\tau_{2})|^{4}ds\right)^{1/4}.

Consequently, in view of Lemma

\displaystyle|Eg(X_{\tau_{1}}(t))-Eg(X_{\tau_{2}}(t))|=|Eg(F_{1})-Eg(F_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(E\|DF_{1}\|^{-8}_{L^{2}[0,T]}E\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|D_{\theta}D_{r}F_{1}|^{2}d\theta dr\right)^{2}+(E\|DF_{1}\|^{-2}_{L^{2}[0,T]})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{1,2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,L}\left(|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{2}+t^{-1}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{1}}|\varphi(s-\tau_{1})-\varphi(s-\tau_{2})|^{4}ds+t^{-1}\int_{t\wedge\tau_{1}}^{t\wedge\tau_{2}}|\varphi(0)-\varphi(s-\tau_{2})|^{4}ds\right)^{1/4}.

Corollary 3.2
.

Suppose the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
. In addition, we assume that the initial data
\varphi
is Hölder continuous with exponent
\beta\in(0,1).
Then, for any
g\in\mathcal{B},
we have

\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}|Eg(X_{\tau_{1}}(t))-Eg(X_{\tau_{2}}(t))|\leq C_{T,L}\,|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{\beta\wedge\frac{1}{2}},

(3.34)

where
C_{L,T}
is a positive constant.

Proof.

We have

t^{-1}\int_{t\wedge\tau_{1}}^{t\wedge\tau_{2}}(\tau_{2}-s)^{4\beta}ds\leq t^{-1}\int_{t\wedge\tau_{1}}^{t\wedge\tau_{2}}(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{4\beta}ds\leq(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{4\beta}.

Hence, it follows from (
3.32
) that

\displaystyle|Eg(X_{\tau_{1}}(t))-Eg(X_{\tau_{2}}(t))|

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,L}\left(|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{2}+t^{-1}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{1}}|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{4\beta}ds+t^{-1}\int_{t\wedge\tau_{1}}^{t\wedge\tau_{2}}(\tau_{2}-s)^{4\beta}ds\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,L}\,|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|^{\beta\wedge\frac{1}{2}},\,\,\,0<t\leq T.

This, together with the fact that
|Eg(X_{\tau_{1}}(t))-Eg(X_{\tau_{2}}(t))|=0

∎

When the coefficients

Corollary 3.3
.

Consider the Carathéodory approximation system (
1.1
)-(

\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}|Eg(x^{n}(t))-Eg(x(t))|\leq\frac{C_{L,T}}{\sqrt{n}},\,\,\,n\geq 1,

is a positive constant.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we employed the technique of Malliavin calculus to study the weak convergence of delay SDEs. The interesting point of our results lies in the fact that we are able to provide an explicit estimate for the rate of convergence and we only require the test function
g
to be bounded. Furthermore, the method introduced in the paper can be used to investigate the weak convergence and the Carathéodory approximation for more general equations such as SDEs with multiple delays

dX(t)=b(t,X(t),X(t-\tau_{1}),\cdots,X(t-\tau_{k}))dt+\sigma(t,X(t),X(t-\tau_{1}),\cdots,X(t-\tau_{k}))dB(t),\,\,\,t\in[0,T]

or SDEs with variable delays

dX(t)=b(t,X(t),X(t-\tau(t)))dt+\sigma(t,X(t),X(t-\tau(t)))dB(t),\,\,\,t\in[0,T].

However, the computations will be more complex. Hence, in the present paper, we have chosen the simplest class of delay SDEs to illustrate the main features of the method rather than getting bogged down with complex notations.

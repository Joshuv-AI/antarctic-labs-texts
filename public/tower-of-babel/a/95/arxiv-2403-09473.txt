Abstract
We consider a set of consumers in a city or town (who thus generate pollution) whose opinion is governed by a continuous opinion and discrete action (CODA) dynamics model. This dynamics is coupled with an observation signal dynamics, which defines the information the consumers have access to regarding the common pollution. We show that the external observation signal has a significant impact on the asymptotic behavior of the CODA model. When the coupling is strong, it induces either a chaotic behavior or convergence towards a limit cycle. When the coupling is weak, a more classical behavior characterized by local agreements in polarized clusters is observed. In both cases, conditions under which clusters of consumers don’t change their actions are provided.Numerical examples are provided to illustrate the derived analytical results.

When the coupling between the dynamics is weak, one recovers the asymptotic behavior of the original CODA model in

It is noteworthy that even in the simplified case when all the agents have the same initial opinion, the strong coupling with the external dynamics hampers the convergence toward a steady state and may lead to chaotic oscillations.

The main contributions of this paper are: i) the introduction of a mathematical model capturing the coupling between the CODA dynamics and an external one; ii) the analysis of the asymptotic behavior of the aforementioned model; iii) and the characterization of the coupling strength leading to different asymptotic behaviors.

Model description

We consider the classical multi-agent modeling in which
n
individuals/agents belong to the set
\mathcal{V}=\left\{1,\dots,N\right\}
and interact according to a fixed graph
\mathcal{G}=\left(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}\right)
that can be directed or not. The neighborhood of the agent

is denoted by
\mathcal{N}_{i}
and represents the set of agents that influence
i
according to the graph
\mathcal{G}
(i.e
j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\Leftrightarrow(j,i)\in\mathcal{E}
). We also denote by
n_{i}
the cardinality of
\mathcal{N}_{i}
. We assign to each agent
i\in\mathcal{V}
an opinion
\theta_{i}\in\left[-1,1\right]
that evolves in time according to a discrete time protocol defined further in (
3
). Let
\theta_{i}(k)
be the opinion of the agent
i\in\mathcal{V}
at time
k

\theta(k)
the opinion of all individuals at time
k

q_{i}(k)\in\left\{-1,1\right\}
as a quantized version of

defined by

q_{i}(k)=\begin{cases}1\hskip-5.69054pt&\text{if }(\theta_{i}(k)>0)\lor(\theta_{i}(k)=0\wedge q_{i}(k-1)=1)\\
-1\hskip-5.69054pt&\text{if }(\theta_{i}(k)<0)\lor(\theta_{i}(k)=0\wedge q_{i}(k-1)=-1)\end{cases}

We assume that the action of individual
i
at time

generates an emission
e_{i}(k)\in\left[e_{\text{min}},e_{\text{max}}\right]\subset\mathbb{R}
where

are the minimum and maximum emissions, respectively. The emission is given by the following equation

e_{i}(k)=\begin{cases}e_{\text{min}}\mbox{ if }q_{i}(k)=-1\\
e_{\text{max}}\mbox{ if }q_{i}(k)=1\end{cases}.

(1)

We add an external state
p\in\mathbb{R}
, referred to as pollution, that captures the environment state under the emission of everyone. The pollution evolves according to the following discrete-time dynamics:

p(k+1)=\gamma p(k)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}e_{i}(k),

(2)

where
\gamma\in(0,1)
is an autonomous decay rate.

We assume that individuals cannot observe
p(k)
but they can sense a quantized value
q_{p}(k)\in\left\{-1,1\right\}
. Let us define
q_{p}(k)
as a function of a threshold
\bar{p}\in\mathbb{R}
as follows:

q_{p}(k)=\begin{cases}-1\hskip-5.69054pt&\text{if }(p(k)>\bar{p})\lor(p(k)=\bar{p}\wedge q_{p}(k-1)=-1)\\
1\hskip-5.69054pt&\text{if }(p(k)<\bar{p})\lor(p(k)=\bar{p}\wedge q_{p}(k-1)=1).\end{cases}

We are now ready to describe the opinion dynamics model that we consider in this work. This dynamics adapts the CODA model in
[
12
]
to include the external dynamics of

:

\begin{split}\theta_{i}(k+1)={}&\theta_{i}(k)+\left(1-\theta_{i}(k)^{2}\right)\big[\beta\left(q_{p}(k)-\theta_{i}(k)\right)\\
&+\left(1-\beta\right)\frac{1}{n_{i}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\left(q_{j}(k)-\theta_{i}(k)\right)\big],\end{split}

(3)

where
0\leq\beta\leq 1
encapsulates the trade-off between the environment state observed through
q_{p}(k)
and the opinions of the neighbors.
We note that the complete model coupling CODA and the external dynamics is described by (
1
)-(
3
).

We emphasize a natural partition of
\mathcal{V}
in two subsets
\mathcal{N}^{-}(k)=\left\{i\in\mathcal{V}\mid q_{i}(k)=-1\right\}
and
\mathcal{N}^{+}(k)=\left\{i\in\mathcal{V}\mid q_{i}(k)=1\right\}
. In the following, we denote by
n^{-}(k)
and
n^{+}(k)
the cardinality of
\mathcal{N}^{-}(k)
and
\mathcal{N}^{+}(k)
, respectively. Similarly, for an agent
i
we denote by
\mathcal{N}_{i}^{-}(k)=\mathcal{N}_{i}\cap\mathcal{N}^{-}(k)
and
\mathcal{N}_{i}^{+}(k)=\mathcal{N}_{i}\cap\mathcal{N}^{+}(k)
and by

and

the cardinalities of these sets.

III-A

Characterization of opinion equilibria

In the following, we analyze the asymptotic behavior of opinions that follows the dynamics (
3
). In other words, we assume that the external signal has an exogenous decoupled evolution.

To simplify our further reasoning, we introduce the following notation

f_{i}(k)=\left(1-\beta\right)\frac{n_{i}^{+}(k)-n_{i}^{-}(k)}{n_{i}}+\beta q_{p}(k).

(4)

Lemma 1
.

Let
i\in\mathcal{V}
,
\theta_{i}(0)\in\left(-1,1\right)
. Then for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, one of the following relation holds

\theta_{i}(k)<\theta_{i}(k+1)<f_{i}(k),

(5)

\theta_{i}(k)>\theta_{i}(k+1)>f_{i}(k),

(6)

or,

\theta_{i}(k)=\theta_{i}(k+1)=f_{i}(k).

(7)

Proof.

Let us first observe that
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}q_{j}(k)=n_{i}^{+}(k)-n_{i}^{-}(k)=2n_{i}^{+}(k)-n_{i}
, since
n_{i}^{+}(k)+n_{i}^{-}(k)=n_{i}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, using (
4
), one rewrites (
3
) as:

\displaystyle\theta_{i}(k+1)={}

\displaystyle\theta_{i}(k)+\left(1-\theta_{i}(k)^{2}\right)\big[\beta\left(q_{p}(k)-\theta_{i}(k)\right)

\displaystyle{}+\left(1-\beta\right)\frac{1}{n_{i}}\left(n_{i}^{+}(k)-n_{i}^{-}(k)-n_{i}\theta_{i}(k)\right)\big]

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\theta_{i}(k)+\left(1-\theta_{i}(k)^{2}\right)\big[f_{i}(k)-\theta_{i}(k)\big]

(8)

We continue our reasoning by induction. From equation (
8
) it is straightforward that if
\theta_{t}(k)<f_{i}(k)
then
\theta_{i}(k)<\theta_{i}(k+1)
and
\theta_{i}(k+1)<f_{i}(k)
. Reversely, if
\theta_{i}(k)>f_{i}(k)
then
\theta_{i}(k+1)>\theta_{i}(k)
and
\theta_{i}(k+1)>f_{i}(k)
. Finally, if
\theta_{i}(k)=f_{i}(k)
then
\theta_{i}(k+1)=f_{i}(k)
.

∎

Proposition 1
.

Let
i\in\mathcal{V}
and assume that Assumption
1
holds. If
\left(q_{p}(k)\right)_{k\geq 0}
and
\left(n_{i}^{+}(k)\right)_{k\geq 0}
are stationary sequences with limit
q_{p}^{*}
and
n_{i}^{+}{}^{*}
, respectively. Then the sequence of opinion
\left(\theta_{i}(k)\right)_{k\geq 0}
converges to

\theta_{i}^{*}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\theta_{i}(k)=\left(1-\beta\right)\frac{2n_{i}^{+}{}^{*}-n_{i}}{n_{i}}+\beta q_{p}^{*}\in\mathbb{Q}+\beta\mathbb{Q}

Proof.

Let us note
f_{i}^{*}:=\left(1-\beta\right)\left(2n_{i}^{+}{}^{*}-n_{i}\right)/n_{i}+\beta q_{p}^{*}\in\left[-1,1\right]
and remarks that
n_{i}^{+}{}^{*}
and
q_{p}^{*}\in\mathbb{N}
since those are stationary sequences in
\mathbb{N}
. Then
f_{i}^{*}\in\mathbb{Q}+\beta\mathbb{Q}
.
With Assumption
1
, we have
\theta_{i}(0)\in\left(-1,1\right)
, so that Lemma
1
applies. Then by induction

\forall k\geq k^{*},\quad\theta_{i}(k)\leq\theta_{i}(k+1)\leq f_{i}^{*}\leq 1

or

\forall k\geq k^{*},\quad\theta_{i}(k)\geq\theta_{i}(k+1)\geq f_{i}^{*}\geq 1.

Then since
\left(\theta_{i}(k)\right)_{k\geq 0}
is a bounded monotonous sequence, it converges. Let denote
\theta_{i}^{*}
that limit. Now from above inequalities, if
\theta_{i}^{*}=1
then
f_{i}^{*}=1
. Conversely, if
\theta_{i}^{*}=-1
, we have that
f_{i}^{*}=-1
. Finally, if
\theta_{i}^{*}\in\left(-1,1\right)
then (
8
) rewrite as

\frac{\theta_{i}(k+1)-\theta_{i}(k)}{\left(1-\theta_{i}(k)^{2}\right)}+\theta_{i}(k)=f_{i}^{*}.

Taking the limit of the previous cancel the first term of the left-hand side and we have that
\theta_{i}^{*}=f_{i}^{*}
.
∎

We will later see that opinions can either have an oscillatory chaotic behavior or they converge to a limit cycle. For a discrete-time system given by
x(k+1)=H(x(k))
, if there is a natural number

for which there exist

,

, …,

, then the overall sequence converges to a limit cycle of length

Corollary 1
.

Under Assumption

\left(q_{p}(k)\right)_{k\geq 0}
is a stationary sequence and
\left(n_{i}^{+}(k)\right)_{k\geq 0}
converges to a limit cycle of length

III-B

Asymptotic behavior of the external/pollution dynamics

As pointed out in the previous subsection, the stationarity of
\left(q_{p}(k)\right)_{k\geq 0}
plays a major role in the asymptotic behavior of the opinions
\bm{\theta}
. Consequently, in this subsection we provide a sufficient condition ensuring that
\left(q_{p}(k)\right)_{k\geq 0}
is a stationary sequence. In this setting, the opinions behave as in the CODA model provided in
[
12
]
since the players are influenced by the external dynamics uniformly with respect to time after the sequences become stationary. We can rewrite the dynamics (
2
) by injecting (
1
)

p(k+1)=\gamma p(k)+n^{+}(k)e_{\text{max}}+n^{-}(k)e_{\text{min}}

(9)

Therefore, the pollution
p
reaches an equilibrium only if
(n^{+}(k))_{k\geq 0}
(and implicitly
(n^{-}(k))_{k\geq 0}
) is stationary. Let us suppose that

\lim_{k\to\infty}n^{+}(k)=n^{+},\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}n^{-}(k)=n^{-}

In this case the equilibrium
p^{*}
is given by

p^{*}=\frac{n^{+}e_{\text{max}}+n^{-}e_{\text{min}}}{1-\gamma},

(10)

which now only depends on the partition of actions of the individuals in the social network.

In order to guarantee that
\left(q_{p}(k)\right)_{k\geq 0}
is stationary one needs to ensure that
\left(\text{sgn}\left(p(k)-\bar{p}\right)\right)_{k\geq 0}
is stationary. In other words, for
k
sufficiently large the value of
p(k)
does not cross the threshold
\bar{p}
.

Lemma 2
.

The sequence
\left(q_{p}(k)\right)_{k\geq 0}
is stationary for any graph
\mathcal{G}
with
N
individuals if there exists
k
such that either
\Big(p(k)\leq\frac{Ne_{\text{max}}}{1-\gamma}\wedge\frac{Ne_{\text{max}}}{1-\gamma}\leq\bar{p}\Big)
or

\Big(p(k)\geq\frac{Ne_{\text{min}}}{1-\gamma}\wedge\frac{Ne_{\text{max}}}{1-\gamma}\geq\bar{p}\Big)
.

III-C

Preservation of action

In the following, we investigate under which condition we have that
q_{i}(k)=q_{i}(k+1)
. The following result is instrumental for our purposes.

Lemma 3
.

Let
i\in\mathcal{V}
, then the following statements hold true:

1.

f_{i}(k)\geq 0
and
q_{i}(k)=1
then
q_{i}(k+1)=1
,

2.

if
f_{i}(k)\leq 0
and

q_{i}(k+1)=-1
.

Proof.

As proven in Lemma

one of (
5
), (

) or (

) holds true.

) is verified one has

meaning that

implies
q_{i}(k+1)=1
.

If (
6
) or (
7
) holds, then
\theta_{i}(k)\geq f_{i}(k)
. Consequently,

ensures

or equivalently

.

2) If (

) or (

) holds, then

. Therefore, if

yields
\theta_{i}(k+1)<0\Leftrightarrow q_{i}(k+1)=-1
.

If (
6
) holds, one has that
\theta_{i}(k+1)<\theta_{i}(k)
meaning that
q_{i}(k)=-1
implies
q_{i}(k+1)=-1
.

When
\beta<1/(1+n_{i})
Lemma

can be refined as follows.

Lemma 4
.

Let
i\in\mathcal{V}
and assume that
\beta<1/(1+n_{i})
. The following statements hold:

-

n_{i}^{+}(k)>n_{i}^{-}(k)
and

then,

,

-

n_{i}^{-}(k)<n_{i}^{+}(k)

Proof.

Notice that
\beta<1/(1+n_{i})

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}<\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{i}}
. Recalling that

. Notice also that

\displaystyle f_{i}(k)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\beta\right)\frac{n_{i}^{+}(k)-n_{i}^{-}(k)}{n_{i}}+\beta q_{p}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\beta}{n_{i}}\left(n_{i}^{+}(k)-n_{i}^{-}(k)+\frac{n_{i}q_{p}(k)\beta}{1-\beta}\right)

Therefore

and taking into account that

.

Throughout the paper, we denote by
|A|
the cardinality of a set
A
. We provide a definition for some cluster in the graph such that the opinion will not change through time

Definition 1
.

We say that a subset of agents
A\subset\mathcal{V}
is a weakly robust polarized cluster if the following hold:

•

\forall i,j\in A
,
q_{i}(0)=q_{j}(0)
,

•

\forall i\in A
,
|\mathcal{N}_{i}\cap A|\geq|\mathcal{N}_{i}\setminus A|-\beta/(1-\beta)|\mathcal{N}_{i}|
.

Proposition 2
.

If
A
is a weakly robust polarized cluster and
q_{p}(k)=q_{i}(0)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

, then

q_{i}(k)=q_{i}(0),\ \forall i\in A,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

The proof will be done by induction. Let us suppose that
\forall i\in A
one has
q_{i}(0)=1
. Assume that for a given
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
one has
q_{i}(k^{\prime})=1,\ \forall i\in A
. Since the interaction graph is fixed and
A
is a weakly robust polarized cluster the following holds true
|\mathcal{N}_{i}\cap A|\geq|\mathcal{N}_{i}\setminus A|-\beta/(1-\beta)|\mathcal{N}_{i}|
. Noticing that
\mathcal{N}_{i}\cap A\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{i}^{+}(k^{\prime})
one obtains that
n_{i}^{+}(k^{\prime})\geq n_{i}^{-}(k^{\prime})-n_{i}\beta/(1-\beta)
which is equivalent to

. Applying Lemma

one gets
q_{i}(k^{\prime}+1)=1
and the induction is complete. Similar reasoning applies when
q_{i}(0)=-1,\ \forall i\in A
.
∎

The preservation of action in a weakly polarized cluster is subject to a constant value of

over the time. In order to get rid of this constraint, we introduce the following concept.

.

We say that a subset of agents

is a strongly robust polarized cluster if the following hold:

•

\forall i,j\in A
,
q_{i}(0)=q_{j}(0)
,

•

\forall i\in A
,
|\mathcal{N}_{i}\cap A|\geq|\mathcal{N}_{i}\setminus A|+\beta/(1-\beta)|\mathcal{N}_{i}|
.

Proposition 3
.

If
A
is a robust polarized cluster then

\forall i\in A
,
\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
,
q_{i}(k)=q_{i}(0)
.

Proof.

Apply proof of Proposition
2
while considering
{|\mathcal{N}_{i}\cap A|\geq|\mathcal{N}_{i}\setminus A|+\beta/(1-\beta)|\mathcal{N}_{i}|}
.
∎

It is worth noting that for

Definition 3
.

We say that an opinion state
\bm{\theta}=\left(\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{N}\right)^{\top}
is Fully Synchronized (FS) if
\forall i,j\in\mathcal{V}
,
\theta_{i}=\theta_{j}
.

When the opinion state is FS, the opinion of an agent
i\in\mathcal{V}
is equal to the opinion of any other agent in
\mathcal{V}
. Therefore, in the remainder of this section, we will denote
\theta(k)
and

the common opinion and action of all the agents at time
k
. In other words, we omit the agent index when referring to its opinion or action.

Proposition 4
.

The FS property is forward invariant over time, i.e. if
\bm{\theta}(k)
is FS at time
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\bm{\theta}(k+1)
is FS.

Remark 1
.

If
\bm{\theta}(k)
is FS then
f(k)=(1-\beta)q(k)+\beta q_{p}(k)
.

Indeed, under the assumption of FS, one has that for all
i\in\mathcal{V}
,
(n_{i}^{+}(k)-n_{i}^{-}(k))/n_{i}=q_{i}(k)=q(k)
.

In the FS regime, the action space reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\left\{(q,q_{p})\mid q\in\{-1,1\},q_{p}\in\{-1,1\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(-1,-1),(-1,1),(1,-1),(1,1)\right\}.

Notice that each point in
\mathcal{S}
corresponds to a partition of the state space in four sets. For instance
(1,1)
corresponds to
\{\theta_{i}(k)\geq 0,\forall kp\leq\bar{p}\}
In order to prove the oscillatory behavior of the system (
2
)-(
3
) we show that in general
\mathcal{S}
does not contain equilibrium points. This means that the trajectory of the system cannot remain in a certain partition which means it cannot converge towards a steady state.

Proposition 5
.

Assume that
\beta>1/2
and the opinion state is FS at time
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the points
\left\{(1,-1),(1,-1)\right\}
are not equilibrium in the action space.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction. The reasoning is similar for each of the two points so we will focus on the first one. Let us assume that
(1,-1)
is an equilibrium point. This means that if there exists
k^{*}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
q(k^{*})=1,\ q_{p}(k^{*})=-1
then for any
k>k^{*}
we have
q(k)=1
and
q_{p}(k)=-1
. We notice that in this case, one has
f(k)=1-2\beta,\ \forall k>k^{*}
. Then the dynamics (
8
) becomes

\displaystyle\theta(k+1)=\theta(k)+\left(1-\theta(k)^{2}\right)\left(1-2\beta-\theta(k)\right),\ \forall k>k^{*}.

Recalling that
q(k)=1,\ \forall k>k^{*}

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we have introduced and analyzed a CODA model coupled with an external dynamic state. Specifically, we consider that the external dynamics represent a very simple pollution model in which the emission level depends on the actions of the individuals in the social network. Conversely, the opinions are both influenced by the actions of the neighbors and the pollution level (above or below a given threshold). We have shown that different behaviors are possible, ranging from convergence to a steady state to chaotic behavior of the coupled dynamics. Numerical examples illustrate our theoretical results.

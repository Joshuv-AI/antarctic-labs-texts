A direct and elementary proof of the well-definedness of the interior and exterior polynomials of hypergraphs
Xiaxia Guan, Xian’an Jin, Tianlong Ma School of Mathematical Sciences, Xiamen University, Xiamen, Fujian 361005, China E-mails: gxx0544@126.com; xajin@xmu.edu.cn; tianlongma@aliyun.com

Abstract
T. Kálmán (A version of Tutte’s polynomial for hypergraphs, Adv. Math. 244 (2013) 823-873.) introduced the interior and exterior polynomials which are generalizations of the Tutte polynomial T ⁡ ( x , y ) T(x,y) on plane points ( 1 / x , 1 ) (1/x,1) and ( 1 , 1 / y ) (1,1/y) to hypergraphs. The two polynomials are defined under a fixed ordering of hyperedges, and are proved to be independent of the ordering using techniques of polytopes. In this paper, similar to the Tutte’s original proof we provide a direct and elementary proof for the well-definedness of the interior and exterior polynomials of hypergraphs. Keywords: Hypergraphs; Bipartite graphs; Interior polynomial; Exterior polynomial; Well-definedness AMS subject classification 2020: 05C31; 05C65

Introduction

It is well-known that the Tutte polynomial
T(x,y)

[
6
]
is an important invariant of graphs. Motivated by the study of the HOMFLY polynomial
[
1
,
4
]
, which is an important invariant in knot theory, Kálmán, in
[
2
]
, introduced the interior polynomial
I_{\mathcal{H}}(x)
and the exterior polynomial
X_{\mathcal{H}}(y)
which are generalization of
T(1/x,1)
and
T(1,1/y)
to hypergraphs
\mathcal{H}
, respectively.

A
hypergraph
is a pair
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
, where
V
is a finite set and
E
is a finite multiset of non-empty subsets of
V
. Elements of
V
are called
vertices
and elements of
E
are called
hyperedges
. For a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
, its associated bipartite graph
Bip\mathcal{H}
is a bipartite graph which the sets
V
and
E
are the colour classes of
Bip\mathcal{H}
, and an element
v
of
V
is connected to an element
e
of
E
if and only if
v\in e
. In this paper, we consider a connected hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
, that is,
Bip\mathcal{H}
is connected.

A hypertree in a connected hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is a function
f
:
E\rightarrow N=\{0,1,2,\cdots\}
such that a spanning tree
\tau
of its associated bipartite graph
Bip\mathcal{H}
can be found with the degree of
e
in
\tau

d_{\tau}(e)=f(e)+1
for any
e\in E
. We call that
\tau

realises
or
induces

f
. We denote the set of all hypertrees in
\mathcal{H}
with
B_{\mathcal{H}}
.
Let
f
be a hypertree and
e
,
e^{\prime}
be two distinct hyperedges of
\mathcal{H}
. We say that
f
is the hypertree such that
a transfer of valence
is possible from
e
to
e^{\prime}
if the function
f^{\prime}
obtained from
f
by decreasing
f(e)
by 1 and increasing
f(e^{\prime})
by 1 is also a hypertree.

Given an order on
E
, let
f
be a hypertree. A hyperedge
e\in E
is
internally active
with respect to the hypertree
f
if for any hyperedge
e^{\prime}<e
,
e^{\prime}
can not be transferred valence from
e
. We say that with respect to
f
, a hyperedge
e\in E
is
internally inactive
if it is not internally active. Let
\iota(f)
(
\overline{\iota}(f)
, resp.) denote the number of internally active (inactive, resp.) hyperedges with respect to
f
. This value is called the
internal activity
(
internal inactivity
, resp.) of
f
.
A hyperedge
e\in E
is
externally active
with respect to
f
if for any hyperedge
e^{\prime}<e
,
e^{\prime}
can not transfer valence to
e
. We say that with respect to
f
, a hyperedge
e\in E
is
externally inactive
if it is not externally active. Let
\epsilon(f)
(
\overline{\epsilon}(f)
, resp.) denote the number of externally active (inactive, resp.) hyperedges with respect to
f
. This value is called the
external activity
(
external inactivity
, resp.) of
f
.
We denote the
interior polynomial

I_{\mathcal{H}}(x)=\sum\limits_{f\in B_{\mathcal{H}}}x^{\overline{\iota}(f)}
and the
exterior polynomial

X_{\mathcal{H}}(y)=\sum\limits_{f\in B_{\mathcal{H}}}y^{\overline{\epsilon}(f)}
.

In
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
, Kálmán and Postnikov proved that
the interior polynomial and the exterior polynomial are two invariants of hypergraphs by a straightforward argument using technique of polytope and indirect approach by counting Ehrhart-type lattice point, respectively, which can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1

(
[
2
]
)
.

The interior and exterior polynomials of a connected hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
do not depend on the chosen order on
E
.

In this paper, similar to the idea that Tutte proves the independence of the ordering of edges of his polynomial in
[
6
]
, we provide a direct proof of Theorem
1
. Our proof is also elementary, without the use of polytopes.

Preliminaries

In this section, to prove our Theorem
1
, we first introduce several definitions and significant conclusions. Moreover, we shall list some known results and prove them by the basic technique.

Definition 2
.

Let
G=(E\cup V,\varepsilon)
be a connected bipartite graph. For a subset
E^{\prime}\subset E
, let
G|_{E^{\prime}}
denote the bipartite graph formed by
E^{\prime}

E^{\prime}
and their endpoints in

.
We denote
\mu(E^{\prime})=0

\mu(E^{\prime})=|\bigcup E^{\prime}|-c(E^{\prime})
for
E^{\prime}\neq\emptyset

and
\bigcup E^{\prime}=V\cap(G|_{E^{\prime}})

In
[
2
]
, the sufficient and necessary conditions of hypertrees of a hypergraph was obtained.

Theorem 3

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a connected hypergraph and
Bip\mathcal{H}
be the bipartite graph associated to the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
. Let
f
be a hypertree of
\mathcal{H}
. Then

(1)
0\leq f(e)\leq d_{Bip\mathcal{H}}(e)-1
for all

;

(2)
\sum\limits_{e\in E}f(e)=|V|-1
;

(3)
\sum\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}f(e)\leq\mu(E^{\prime})
for all
E^{\prime}\subset E
.

Lemma 4

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a connected hypergraph and
Bip\mathcal{H}
be the bipartite graph associated to
\mathcal{H}
. If
g:E\rightarrow N
is a function satisfying two conditions as follows: (i)
g(e)\geq 0
for any hyperedge
e\in E
; (ii)
\sum\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}g(e)\leq\mu(E^{\prime})
for any nonempty subset
E^{\prime}\subset E
but not necessarily

, then there exists a cycle-free subgraph

of
Bip\mathcal{H}
with

.

It is clear that
G^{\prime}
is a spanning tree of
Bip\mathcal{H}
if
\sum\limits_{e\in E}f(e)=|V|-1
holds. Then we have the following conclusion.

Lemma 5
.

If
g:E\rightarrow N
is a function satisfying three conditions as follows: (i)
g(e)\geq 0
for any hyperedge
e\in E
; (ii)
\sum\limits_{e\in E}g(e)=|V|-1
; (iii)
\sum\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}g(e)\leq\mu(E^{\prime})
for any nonempty subset
E^{\prime}\subset E
, then

is a hypertree.

For any nonempty subset
E^{\prime}\subset E
, if there is a hypertree

such that
\sum\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}g(e)\leq\sum\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}f_{i}(e)
, then
\sum\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}g(e)\leq\mu(E^{\prime})
by Theorem
3
(3). Thus we have the following result.

Lemma 6

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a connected hypergraph, and
{
f_{i}
}
be the set of hypertrees of
\mathcal{H}
.
Let
g:E\rightarrow N
is a function satisfying two conditions as follows: (i)
g(e)\geq 0
for any hyperedge
e\in E
; (ii)
\sum\limits_{e\in E}g(e)=|V|-1
. If for any nonempty subset

such that
\sum\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}g(e)\leq\sum\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}f_{i}(e)

is a hypertree.

One sufficient condition that a hyperedge can transfer valence to another hyperedge for a hypertree, which is proved by technique of the hypertree polytope in
[
2
]
, is given as follows. However, we prove it again based on Lemma

Lemma 7

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a connected hypergraph, and
f
be a hypertree of
\mathcal{H}
. Let
e_{i}

(i=1,2,3)
be distinct hyperedges of
\mathcal{H}
. With respect to
f
, if
e_{1}
can transfer valence to
e_{2}
, and
e_{2}
can transfer valence to
e_{3}
with respect to

, then
e_{1}
can transfer valence to
e_{3}
with respect to
f
.

Proof.

Let the hypertree
f
be (
\cdots,f(e_{3}),\cdots,f(e_{2}),\cdots,f(e_{1}),\cdots
). Since with respect to
f
,
e_{1}
can transfer valence to
e_{2}
, and
e_{2}
can transfer valence to
e_{3}
, there are the following two hypertrees.

f_{1}
: (
\cdots,f(e_{3}),\cdots,f(e_{2})+1,\cdots,f(e_{1})-1,\cdots
);

f_{2}
: (
\cdots,f(e_{3})+1,\cdots,f(e_{2})-1,\cdots,f(e_{1}),\cdots
).

Next we prove that
f_{3}
: (
\cdots,f(e_{3})+1,\cdots,f(e_{2}),\cdots,f(e_{1})-1,\cdots
) is a hypertree of
\mathcal{H}
.

It is clear that
f_{3}
satisfies
f_{3}(e)\geq 0
for any hyperedge
e\in E
and
\sum\limits_{e\in E}f_{3}(e)=|V|-1
. For any nonempty subset
E^{\prime}\in E
, if
e_{1}\in E^{\prime}
or

, then
\sum\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}f_{3}(e)\leq\sum\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}f_{2}(e)
. If
e_{1}\notin E^{\prime}
and
e_{2}\in E^{\prime}
, then
\sum\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}f_{3}(e)\leq\sum\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}f_{1}(e)
.
Thus, by Lemma
6
,
f_{3}
is a hypertree of
\mathcal{H}
. It implies that
e_{1}
can transfer valence to
e_{3}
with respect to
f
.
∎

Recall that for a hypertree
f
, by Theorem
3
, we have
\sum\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}f(e)\leq\mu(E^{\prime})
for all
E^{\prime}\subset E

Definition 8

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a connected hypergraph. Let
f
be a hypertree of
\mathcal{H}
. For a subset

\sum\limits_{e\in E^{\prime}}f(e)=\mu(E^{\prime})

It is clear that if

is tight at

. Moreover, the following theorem holds immediately from Theorem 44.2 in

]
.

Theorem 9

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a connected hypergraph. Let
f
be a hypertree of
\mathcal{H}
. If the subsets
A\subset E
and

are both tight at

, then
A\cap B
and

are both tight at
f
.

In
[
2
]
, the following result was presented by using Theorem

Lemma 10

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
G=(V\cup E,\varepsilon)
be a connected bipartite graph. Let
f
be a hypertree of

. Then for any non-empty subset

, if

is not tight at

.

7

Lemma 11
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a connected hypergraph, and let

be a hypertree of
\mathcal{H}
. Let

be two distinct hyperedges of
\mathcal{H}
.

with respect to

and every subset

The necessity is obvious. For sufficiency, let us take

. Since

is not tight at

can transfer valence to

for

be the set consisting of all elements of

for

. If

, then we take

. Note that

is not tight at

for

. Let
U_{2}
be the set consisting of all elements of

that can transfer valence to some element of

for

. It is obvious that
E_{1}
is a proper subset of
E_{2}
. Moreover, all elements of
E_{3}=U_{2}\cup E_{2}
(except for

) can transfer valence to

for

by Lemma

is a proper subset of

. Continue the above process, we will eventually obtain that

can transfer valence to
e^{\prime}
for

.
∎

\mathcal{H}=(V,E)

and

\mathcal{H}

(

to

, then

to

(2) If

to

(1) It is clear that

. We claim that any subset
E_{1}\subset E
which contains

(2) Since

. (i) Assume that

. Then

is not tight at

can be transferred valence from

. In this case,

. Then

.

3
Proof of Theorem 1

It suffices to prove that the interior and exterior polynomials are equal under the following distinct orders
O
and
O^{\prime}
on
E
, respectively, that is,
I_{\mathcal{H},O}(x)=I_{\mathcal{H},O^{\prime}}(x)
and

y^{\overline{\epsilon}(g_{1})}+y^{\overline{\epsilon}(g_{l})}
. We have that
\sum\limits_{g\in\mathcal{F}}x^{\overline{\iota}(g)}
is equal under two distinct orders
O
and
O^{\prime}
, and it is similar for
\sum\limits_{g\in\mathcal{F}}y^{\overline{\epsilon}(g)}
.

Thus,
I_{\mathcal{H},O}(x)=I_{\mathcal{H},O^{\prime}}(x)
and
X_{\mathcal{H},O}(y)=X_{\mathcal{H},O^{\prime}}(y)
.
This completes the proof.

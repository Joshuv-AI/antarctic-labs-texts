Abstract
Let 𝒞 \mathscr{C} be a ( d + 2 ) (d+2) -angulated category with d d -suspension functor Σ d \Sigma^{d} . Our main results show that every Serre functor on 𝒞 \mathscr{C} is a ( d + 2 ) (d+2) -angulated functor. We also show that 𝒞 \mathscr{C} has a Serre functor 𝕊 \mathbb{S} if and only if 𝒞 \mathscr{C} has Auslander–Reiten ( d + 2 ) (d+2) -angles. Moreover, τ d = 𝕊 ​ Σ − d \tau_{d}=\mathbb{S}\Sigma^{-d} where τ d \tau_{d} is d d -Auslander-Reiten translation. These results generalize work by Bondal-Kapranov and Reiten-Van den Bergh. As an application, we prove that for a strongly functorially finite subcategory 𝒳 \mathscr{X} of 𝒞 \mathscr{C} , the quotient category 𝒞 / 𝒳 \mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X} is a ( d + 2 ) (d+2) -angulated category if and only if ( 𝒞 , 𝒞 ) (\mathscr{C},\mathscr{C}) is an 𝒳 \mathscr{X} -mutation pair, and if and only if τ d ​ 𝒳 = 𝒳 \tau_{d}\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{X} . Introduction

Unless otherwise specified
k

all categories in this article will be
k

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an additive category. We say that
\mathscr{C}
has a Serre functor
\mathbb{S}
, that is, an auto-equivalence for which there exists a natural equivalence

\mbox{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(X,Y)\simeq\mbox{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(Y,\mathbb{S}X)^{\ast}

for any

, where
(-)^{\ast}=\mbox{Hom}_{k}(-,k)

Bondal and Kapranov showed the following result. Van den Bergh has also given other proof of this result in the appendix of this article
[
B
, Theorem A.4.4]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
BK
, Proposition 3.3]

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a triangulated category. Then every Serre functor is a
triangulated functor.

Reiten and Van den Bergh gave a connection between Auslander-Reiten triangles and Serre functor.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
RVB
, Theorem I.2.4]

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a triangulated category with shift functor
\Sigma
. Then

\mathscr{C}
has a Serre functor
\mathbb{S}
if and only if
\mathscr{C}
has Auslander-Reiten triangles.
Moreover,
\tau=\mathbb{S}\Sigma^{-1}
where
\tau
is the Auslander-Reiten translation.

Quotient categories come in a number of different flavours. The one to be considered here is probably the most basic. Let
\mathscr{C}
be an additive category with a subcategory
\mathscr{X}
. For two objects
A,B\in\mathscr{C}
, denote by
\mathscr{X}(A,B)
all the morphisms from

A
to
B
which factor through an object of
\mathscr{X}
. Then the quotient category
\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
has
the same objects as
\mathscr{C}
, and its morphism spaces are defined by

\mbox{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}}(A,B)=\mbox{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(A,B)/\mathscr{X}(A,B).

Jørgensen gave a necessary and sufficient condition for quotient categories to be triangulated categories.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
J
, Theorem 3.3]

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a triangulated category with Serre functor and
\mathscr{X}
be a functorially finite subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
. Then
the quotient category
\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
is a triangulated category if and only if
\tau\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{X}
.

In
[
GKO
]
, Geiss, Keller and Oppermann introduced
(d+2)
-angulated categories. These are
generalizations of triangulated categories, in the sense that triangles are replaced by

(d+2)
-angles, that is, morphism sequences of length

. In particular, a
3
-angulated category is precisely
a triangulated category.
The notion of Auslander-Reiten
(d+2)
-angles in a special
(d+2)
-angulated category was first introduced by

[

]
, and then was generalized by Fedele
[

]
to arbitrary
(d+2)
-angulated category.

In this article we will generalize these results into the higher homological case.
Moreover, our proof is not far from the usual triangulated case. We
hope that our work would motivate further study on
(d+2)
-angulated categories.

.

(see Theorem

-angulated category with

1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper we denote by
{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Z}}^{*},{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},{\mathbb{N}},{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}},
and
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
the sets of integers, nonzero integers, non-negative integers, positive integers, real numbers, complex numbers, and nonzero complex numbers, respectively. All vector spaces and algebras are assumed to be over
{\mathbb{C}}
.
For a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
is denoted by
U(\mathfrak{g})
.

In this paper we will introduce and study the Lie superalgebras
\mathcal{S}(0)
and
\mathcal{S}(\frac{1}{2})
, which we consider to be the super version of twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
\mathfrak{D}
and are called Fermion-Virasoro algebras. We first recall the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{D}
from
[
1
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

The
twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra

{{\mathfrak{D}}}
is a Lie algebra with a basis

\left\{d_{m},h_{r},c_{1},c_{2},{c}_{3}:m,r\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}

and subject to the commutation relations

(1.1)

\displaystyle[d_{m},d_{n}]=(m-n)d_{m+n}+\delta_{m+n,0}\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}c_{1},

\displaystyle[d_{m},h_{r}]=-rh_{m+r}+\delta_{m+r,0}(m^{2}+m)c_{2},

\displaystyle[h_{r},h_{s}]=r\delta_{r+s,0}{c}_{3},

\displaystyle[c_{1},{{\mathfrak{D}}}]=[c_{2},{{\mathfrak{D}}}]=[c_{3},{{\mathfrak{D}}}]=0,

for
m,n,r,s\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

It is clear that
{{\mathfrak{D}}}
contains a copy of the Virasoro subalgebra
\mathrm{Vir}=\text{span}\{c_{1},d_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
and the Heisenberg algebra
{\mathcal{H}}=\bigoplus_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{C}}h_{r}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}{c}_{3}
.

Similarly, for
\delta=0
or
\frac{1}{2}
, the Fermion-Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
contains a copy of the Virasoro subalgebra
\mathrm{Vir}
and the Fermion algebra
F(\delta)
, see Definition 2.1.

The representation theory over various infinite-dimensional Lie (super)algebras has been studied by many mathematicians and physicists recently. For example, Simple smooth modules for the Virasoro algebra, the Neveu-Schwarz algebra were determined in
[
13
,
7
]
. Y. Billig and V. Futorny classified the simple Harish-Chandra modules over the Witt algebra of rank
n
in
[
2
]
; H. Tan, Y. Yao and K. Zhao classified the simple smooth modules over the mirror and twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras with nonzero level in
[
14
]
. For more related results, see the references within these papers.

Let
\delta=0
or
\frac{1}{2}
. In this paper, we classify all simple smooth
F(\delta)
-modules, all simple weight
F(\delta)
-modules, all simple smooth
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
-modules of nonzero level, all simple Harish-Chandra
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
-modules, and all
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
-modules structures on free
{\mathbb{C}}[L_{0}]
-modules of rank
2
with non-trivial odd spaces.

This paper is arranged as follows. In Section 2, the Fermion algebras and Fermion-Virasoro algebras are introduced, and related definitions are collected. In Section 3, we give some property for the representations of
F(\delta)
. In particular, we classify all simple smooth
F(\delta)
-modules in Lemma
3.4
and Lemma
3.6
, and classify
simple weight
F(\delta)
-modules in Lemma
3.11
. Also we show that any smooth
F(\delta)
-module of nonzero level is completely reducible in Lemma
3.9
. In Section 4, using vertex operators (infinite sum of fermionic oscillators) we classify all simple smooth
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
-modules of nonzero level in Theorems
4.3
and
4.4
. In Section 5, using properties of weight
F(\delta)
-modules we classify all simple Harish-Chandra
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
-modules in Theorems
5.4
and
5.5
. In Section 6, we determine all
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
-modules structures on free
{\mathbb{C}}[L_{0}]
-modules of rank
2
with non-trivial odd spaces. Surprisingly we have found four different classes of such modules in Theorems
6.12
.

At the end of this paper we propose two questions which may be interesting to readers.

2.
The Fermion-Virasoro algebras

Throughout this paper,
\delta=0
or
\frac{1}{2}
.
Now we define the Fermion algebra
F(\delta)
that was used implicitly in Section 3.4 to construct unitary highest weight representation of the Virasoro algebra in
[
5
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
Fermion algebra

F(\delta)=F(\delta)_{\bar{0}}\oplus F(\delta)_{\bar{1}}
is a Lie superalgebra with a basis

\left\{\psi_{m},z:m\in{\mathbb{Z}}+\delta\right\}

and subject to the commutation relations

(2.1)

[\psi_{m},\psi_{n}]=\delta_{m,-n}z,\,\,[F(\delta),z]=0,

for
m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}+\delta
, where
F(\delta)_{\bar{1}}=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}+\delta}{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{i}
and
F(\delta)_{\bar{0}}={\mathbb{C}}z
.

Write
F:=F(0)
for short.
We can consider the Fermion algebra
F
as the super version of the Heisenberg algebra
{\mathcal{H}}
. The operators
\psi_{m}
are generally called fermionic oscillators.

Now we define the Fermion-Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
that was implicitly used in Section 3.4 in
[
5
]
although the algebra
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
was not defined there.

Definition 2.2
.

The
Fermion-Virasoro algebra

\mathcal{S}(\delta)=\mathcal{S}(\delta)_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathcal{S}(\delta)_{\bar{1}}
is the Lie superalgebra with basis
\{L_{m},\psi_{n},c,z:m\in{\mathbb{Z}},n\in{\mathbb{Z}}+\delta\}
, where
L_{m},c,z\in\mathcal{S}(\delta)_{\bar{0}},\psi_{n}\in\mathcal{S}(\delta)_{\bar{1}}
, and subject to the commutation relations

\displaystyle{}[L_{m},L_{n}]

\displaystyle=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\delta_{m,-n}\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}c,

\displaystyle[L_{m},\psi_{n}]

\displaystyle=(-n-\frac{m}{2})\psi_{m+n},

\displaystyle[\psi_{m},\psi_{n}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{m,-n}z,

\displaystyle[\mathcal{S}(\delta),c]

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{S}(\delta),z]=0.

Write
\mathcal{S}:=\mathcal{S}(0)
for short. The results in this paper are proved for
\mathcal{S}
and
F
while their proofs are also applicable to those for
\mathcal{S}(\frac{1}{2})
and
F(\frac{1}{2})
. The main results are given in both
\delta=0
and
\frac{1}{2}
.

The Lie superalgebra
\mathcal{S}(0)/{\mathbb{C}}z
is isomorphic to a quotient algebra of a special case of the Lie superalgebras studied in
[
3
]
. The Lie superalgebras
\mathcal{S}(\delta)/{\mathbb{C}}z
were discussed in
[
4
]
, where their simple Harish-Chandra modules were classified.

It is clear that
\mathcal{S}
contains a copy of the Virasoro subalgebra
\mathrm{Vir}=\text{span}\{c,L_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
and a copy of the Fermion algebra
F=\bigoplus_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{r}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}z
.
We know that
\mathcal{S}
has a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-gradation
\mathcal{S}=\oplus_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathcal{S}_{k}
, where

\mathcal{S}_{k}={\mathbb{C}}L_{k}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{k},\forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{*};\,\,\,\,\mathcal{S}_{0}={\mathbb{C}}L_{0}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{0}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}c\oplus{\mathbb{C}}z.

Write
\mathcal{S}_{-}:=\oplus_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal{S}_{k},\mathcal{S}_{+}:=\oplus_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal{S}_{k}
. Then
\mathcal{S}
has a triangular decomposition

\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{-}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{+}.

Set
\mathrm{Vir}_{\pm}=\mathrm{Vir}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\pm},F_{\pm}=F\cap\mathcal{S}_{\pm}
.

For any
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, define the following subalgebras of
\mathcal{S}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(m,-\infty)}=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}{\mathbb{C}}L_{m+i}\oplus\bigoplus_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{j}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}c\oplus{\mathbb{C}}z,

\displaystyle F^{(n)}=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{n+i}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}z,

\displaystyle F_{[m]}=\bigoplus_{i=-m}^{m}{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{i}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}z.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{S}(\delta),F(\delta)
or
F_{[m]},m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.
For any
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, there is an automorphism
\sigma_{\mu}
of
\mathfrak{g}
given by

\sigma_{\mu}(L_{k})=L_{k},\sigma_{\mu}(\psi_{l})=\mu\psi_{l},\sigma_{\mu}(c)=c,\sigma_{\mu}(z)=\mu^{2}z,k\in{\mathbb{Z}},l\in{\mathbb{Z}}+\delta.

For any
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
and any
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, define the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V^{\mu}
by

x\cdot v=\sigma_{\mu}(x)v,x\in\mathfrak{g},v\in V.

Definition 2.3
.

An
\mathcal{S}
-module
V
is called to be of
level

\ell
and with
central charge

\dot{c}
if
z,c
acts on
V
as scalar
\ell,\dot{c}
respectively.

Definition 2.4
.

An
\mathcal{S}
-module
V
is called a
weight module
if
L_{0}
acts on
V
diagonally, i.e.,
V=\oplus_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}}V_{\alpha}
, where
V_{\alpha}=\{v\in V:L_{0}\cdot v=\alpha v\}
. For a weight
\mathcal{S}
-module
V
, write

\mathrm{Supp}(V)=\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}:V_{\alpha}\neq 0\},

which is called the
support set
of
V
. A weight
\mathcal{S}
-module
V
is called a
highest (resp. lowest) weight module
if
\mathrm{Supp}(V)\subset\alpha+{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
(resp.
\alpha-{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
) for some
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

3.
Representations of
F(\delta)

Definition 3.1
.

Let
V
be a module over a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{h}
be a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, and
S
be a subset of
V
. We denote

\mathrm{Ann}_{V}(\mathfrak{h})=\{v\in V:\mathfrak{h}v=0\},\,\,\,\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathfrak{g}}(S)=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}:xS=0\}.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded Lie superalgebra. A
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
is called a
smooth module
if for any
v\in V
there exists
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
such that
\mathfrak{g}_{i}v=0
for
i>n
. The category of smooth modules over
\mathfrak{g}
will be denoted by
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Smooth modules for affine Kac-Moody algebras
\mathfrak{g}
were introduced and studied by D. Kazhdan and G. Lusztig in
[
6
]
.

Let
\mathscr{V}(\delta)
be the exterior algebra generated by the odd elements
\xi_{i},i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}+\delta
. Then
\mathscr{V}(\delta)
has a basis

1,\xi_{i_{1}}\cdots\xi_{i_{k}},\,\,\,0\leqslant i_{1}<\dots<i_{k},k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Now
\mathscr{V}(\delta)
has an irreducible
F(\delta)
-module structure defined by

\displaystyle\psi_{n}\cdot v=\partial/\partial\xi_{n}\cdot v,

\displaystyle\psi_{-n}\cdot v=\xi_{n}\cdot v,

\displaystyle\psi_{0}\cdot v=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\xi_{0}+\partial/\partial{\xi_{0}})\cdot v\,\,(\text{if }\delta=0),

\displaystyle z\cdot v=v,

where
n\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{+}+\delta)\setminus\{0\},v\in\mathscr{V}(\delta)
. Write
\mathscr{V}:=\mathscr{V}(0)
for short.

Definition 3.3
.

For
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, define in the same manner above the
F_{[m]}
-submodule
\mathscr{V}_{[m]}
of
\mathscr{V}
by

\mathscr{V}_{[m]}=\mathrm{span}\{1,\xi_{i_{1}}\cdots\xi_{i_{k}}\ :\ 0\leqslant i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leqslant m\}.

Then
\mathscr{V}_{[m]}
is a finite-dimensional simple
F_{[m]}
-module.

Lemma 3.4
.

(1)

Suppose
V
is a simple
F_{[m]}
-module such that
zV=0
. Then
F_{[m]}V=0
. Consequently
\dim V=1
.

(2)

Suppose
V
is a simple
F(\delta)
-module such that
zV=0
. Then
F(\delta)V=0
. Consequently
\dim V=1
.

Proof.

Since
z=0
,
\psi_{i}V
is a submodule of
V
, which implies
\psi_{i}V=0
or
\psi_{i}V=V
. The latter cannot occur since
\psi_{i}^{2}V=0
. ∎

Lemma 3.5
.

(1)

Suppose
V
is a simple
F_{[m]}
-module such that
z
acts on
V
as a scalar
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. If there is a nonzero vector
v\in V_{\bar{0}}
such that
\psi_{k}\cdot v=0
for all
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, then
V\cong\mathscr{V}_{[m]}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}
for some
\sqrt{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
(\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}=\lambda
.

(2)

Suppose
V
is a simple
F
-module such that
z
acts on
V
as a scalar
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. If there is a nonzero vector
v\in V_{\bar{0}}
such that
\psi_{k}\cdot v=0
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then
V\cong\mathscr{V}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}
for some
\sqrt{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
(\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}=\lambda
.

Proof.

(1) It suffices to prove that
V^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}}\cong\mathscr{V}_{[m]}
. Note that
z
acts on
V^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}}
as the identity map. Without loss of generality, assume that
\lambda=1
.

Since
\psi_{0}^{2}\cdot v=\frac{1}{2}[\psi_{0},\psi_{0}]\cdot v=\frac{1}{2}v
,
{\mathbb{C}}v\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{0}v
is a simple
F_{[0]}
-module. Also, the fact that
\psi_{k}\cdot({\mathbb{C}}v\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{0}v)=0
for all
k=1,\dots,m
implies that
{\mathbb{C}}v\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{0}v
is a simple
F_{[0]}\oplus F_{[m]+}
-module, where
F_{[m]+}=F_{[m]}\cap F_{+}
. So
V
is a simple quotient of
\rm{Ind}_{F_{[0]}\oplus F_{[m]+}}^{F_{[m]}}({\mathbb{C}}v\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{0}v)
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{C}}v\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{0}v\rightarrow\mathscr{V}_{[0]}
be a linear map defined by

f(v)=1,f(\psi_{0}v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\xi_{0}
. Then
f
is an isomorphism of
(F_{[0]}\oplus F_{[m]+})
-modules. Thus,
f
induces an
F_{[m]}
-module homomorphism

\begin{split}\tilde{f}:\rm{Ind}_{F_{[0]}\oplus F_{[m]+}}^{F_{[m]}}({\mathbb{C}}v\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{0}v)&\rightarrow\mathscr{V}_{[m]}\\
\psi_{i_{1}}\dots\psi_{i_{k}}v&\mapsto\xi_{-i_{1}}\dots\xi_{-i_{k}},\\
\psi_{i_{1}}\dots\psi_{i_{k}}\psi_{0}v&\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\xi_{-i_{1}}\dots\xi_{-i_{k}}\xi_{0},i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}<0.\\
\end{split}

Note that
\tilde{f}
maps a basis to a basis. So
\tilde{f}
is an
F_{[m]}
-module isomorphism. Since
\mathscr{V}_{[m]}
is a simple
F_{[m]}
-module, we have
\rm{Ind}_{F_{[0]}\oplus F_{[m]+}}^{F_{[m]}}({\mathbb{C}}v\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{0}v)
is a simple module and
V\cong\mathscr{V}_{[m]}
.

(2) This could be proved similarly.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

(1)

Suppose
V
is a simple
F_{[m]}
-module for some
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
such that
z
acts on
V
as a scalar
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. Then
V\cong\mathscr{V}_{[m]}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}
for some
\sqrt{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
(\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}=\lambda
.

(2)

Suppose
V
is a simple smooth
F
-module such that
z
acts on
V
as a scalar
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. Then
V\cong\mathscr{V}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}
for some
\sqrt{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
(\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}=\lambda
.

Proof.

(1) Note that
\psi_{i}^{2}\cdot V=0
and that the action of
\psi_{i}
and
\psi_{j}
are anti-commutative for all
i,j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. So there exists a nonzero homogeneous vector
v\in V
such that
\psi_{k}\cdot v=0,k=1,\dots,m
. Replace
v
with
\psi_{0}v
if necessary, and assume that
v\in V_{\bar{0}}
. Then (1) follows from Lemma
3.5
(1).

(2) Let
v
be a nonzero homogeneous vector in
V
. Since
V
is a smooth
F
-module, there exists an
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\psi_{k}\cdot v=0
for any
k>m
. Then
\psi_{k},k>m,
acts trivially on the finite-dimensional
F_{[m]}
-module
U(F_{[m]})v
. Clearly,
U(F_{[m]})v
has a nonzero even vector
w
such that
\psi_{k}\cdot w=0,k=1,\dots,m
. So
\psi_{k}\cdot w=0
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then (2) follows from Lemma
3.5
(2).
∎

With similar arguments, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose
V
is a simple smooth
F(\frac{1}{2})
-module such that
z
acts on
V
as a scalar
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. Then
V\cong\mathscr{V}(\frac{1}{2})^{\sqrt{\lambda}}
for some
\sqrt{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
(\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}=\lambda
.

Note that the Fermion algebra
F_{[m]}
has very good properties such that every
F_{[m]}
-module with nonzero action of the center over
F_{[m]}
is completely reducible.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, and
V
be an
F_{[m]}
-module such that
z
acts on
V
as a scalar
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. Then
V
is completely reducible.

Proof.

\mathbf{Claim}
.
U(F_{[m]})v
is completely reducible for any homogeneous
v\in V
.

For any
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, let
X_{k}=\{\psi_{k},\psi_{-k}\psi_{k}\},Y_{k}=\{\psi_{-k},\psi_{k}\psi_{-k}\}
. Then for any
Z_{k}\in\{X_{k},Y_{k}\},k=1,\dots,m
, the subspace

(3.1)

\text{span}\{x_{1}\dots x_{m}v,\psi_{0}x_{1}\dots x_{m}v:x_{j}\in Z_{j},j=1,\dots,m\}

is an
F_{[m]}
-submodule of
U(F_{[m]})v
. By Lemma
3.6
(1), any simple
F_{[m]}
-module is isomorphic to
\mathscr{V}_{[m]}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}
for some
\sqrt{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
(\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}=\lambda
. Comparing the dimensions of
\mathscr{V}_{[m]}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}
and (
3.1
), we have (
3.1
) is zero or is a simple module. Note that
\psi_{-k}\psi_{k}+\psi_{k}\psi_{-k}=z
in the enveloping algebra for any
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. Then

\bigoplus_{Z_{1}\in\{X_{1},Y_{1}\}}\dots\bigoplus_{Z_{m}\in\{X_{m},Y_{m}\}}\mathrm{span}\{x_{1}\dots x_{m}v,\psi_{0}x_{1}\dots x_{m}v:x_{j}\in Z_{j},j=1,\dots,m\}

contains
v
, and therefore equals
U(F_{[m]})v
. Thus,
U(F_{[m]})v
is completely reducible.

By the Claim, for any homogeneous
v\in V\setminus\{0\}
,
U(F_{[m]})v
is a sum of simple modules. Then
V
is also a sum of simple modules, which means that
V
is completely reducible.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
V
be a smooth
F
-module such that
z
acts on
V
as a scalar
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. Then
V
is completely reducible.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that
U(F)v
is a sum of simple
F
-modules for any homogeneous
v\in V
. Let
v
be a nonzero homogeneous vector in
V
. Then there exists
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\psi_{m+k}v=0
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. By Lemma
3.8
,
U(F_{[m]})v
is completely reducible. From Lemma
3.6
(1), there are
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\in U(F_{[m]})v
satisfying
\psi_{k}x_{i}=0
for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
such that
U(F_{[m]})v=U(F_{[m]})x_{1}+\dots+U(F_{[m]})x_{r}
. We see that

U(F)v=U(F)x_{1}+\dots+U(F)x_{r}.

Note that each
U(F)x_{i}
is isomorphic to
\mathscr{V}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}
for some
\sqrt{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
(\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}=\lambda
. So
U(F)v
is a sum of simple
F
-modules.
∎

For any
I\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, let

S_{I}=\{J\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}:|I\setminus J|<\infty,|J\setminus I|<\infty\}.

Define the
F
-module
\mathscr{V}_{I}
as follows:
\mathscr{V}_{I}
has a basis
\{\xi_{J}:J\in S_{I}\}
, the parity of
\xi_{J}
is defined as
|\xi_{J}|=\overline{|I\setminus J|+|J\setminus I|}
, and the action of
F
on
\mathscr{V}_{I}
is given by

\displaystyle\psi_{k}\cdot\xi_{J}=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} (-1)^{|\{i\in J:i<k\}|}\xi_{J\setminus\{k\}},&\ \text{if}\ k\in J,\\
0,&\ \text{if}\ k\notin J,\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\psi_{-k}\cdot\xi_{J}=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} (-1)^{|\{i\in J:i<k\}|}\xi_{J\cup\{k\}},&\ \text{if}\ k\notin J,\\
0,&\ \text{if}\ k\in J,\end{aligned}\right.

, and set
\lambda^{r}=\lambda^{r-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\lambda}
for any
r\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Lemma 6.9
.

Suppose
d=2
. Then
a=1
or
\frac{5}{2}
, and
V
is isomorphic to
\Omega^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,b)
or
\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,b)
for some
b\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
r\in\delta+{\mathbb{Z}}
with
r>0
. Suppose
d=2
. By Lemma
6.7
, the roots of
f_{r}(x)
are
2ra^{\prime}
and
2ra-3r
. Then

f_{r}(x)=\mu(x-2ra^{\prime})(x-2ra+3r)

for some
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.
From (
6.2
) we have

(6.3)

\begin{split}(-r-\frac{1}{2})f_{r+1}(L_{0})=&\mu\lambda(L_{0}+a^{\prime})(L_{0}+1-2ra^{\prime})(L_{0}+1-2ra+3r)\\
&-\mu\lambda(L_{0}+r+a)(L_{0}-2ra^{\prime})(L_{0}-2ra+3r).\end{split}

By Lemma
6.7
, the roots of
f_{r+1}(x)
are
(r+1)(2a-5)
and
(r+1)(2a-3)
. Then

0=(-r-\frac{1}{2})f_{r+1}((r+1)(2a-5))=\mu\lambda(a-1)(2a-5)(2r+1).

So
a=1
or
\frac{5}{2}
.

Let
b=\mu\lambda^{-r}
. Then

f_{r}(x)=b\lambda^{r}(x-2ra^{\prime})(x-2ra+3r).

From Lemma
6.6
we have

f_{m}(L_{0})=b\lambda^{m}(L_{0}+3m)(L_{0}+m),\forall m\in\delta+{\mathbb{Z}}

if
a=1
, and

f_{m}(L_{0})=b\lambda^{m}L_{0}(L_{0}-2m),\forall m\in\delta+{\mathbb{Z}}

if
a=\frac{5}{2}
. By the definition of
\Omega^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,b)
and
\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,b)
,
V
is isomorphic to
\Omega^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,b)
or
\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,b)
as
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
-modules.
∎

Lemma 6.10
.

Suppose
d=1
. Then
V
is isomorphic to
\Omega^{\prime}_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,a,b)
for some
b\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
r\in\delta+{\mathbb{Z}}
with
r>0
. By Lemma
6.7
, the root of
f_{r}(x)
is
2ra-3r
. Then

f_{r}(L_{0})=\mu(L_{0}-2ra+3r)

for some
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

By Lemma
6.6
, for any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we have

\begin{split}(-r-\frac{n}{2})f_{n+r}(L_{0})=&\lambda^{n}(L_{0}+na^{\prime})f_{r}(L_{0}+n)-\lambda^{n}(L_{0}+na+r)f_{r}(L_{0})\\
=&\lambda^{n}\mu(L_{0}+na^{\prime})(L_{0}-2ra+3r+n)\\
&-\lambda^{n}\mu(L_{0}+na+r)(L_{0}-2ra+3r)\\
=&\lambda^{n}\mu(-r-\frac{n}{2})(L_{0}-(2a-3)(n+r)).\end{split}

So

f_{n+r}(L_{0})=\lambda^{n}\mu(L_{0}-(2a-3)(n+r))

if
n\neq-2r
. In particular,

f_{3r}(L_{0})=\lambda^{2r}\mu(L_{0}-3(2a-3)r).

Let
m=3r,n=-4r
in Lemma
6.6
and we get

\begin{split}-rf_{-r}(L_{0})=&\lambda^{-4r}(L_{0}-4ra^{\prime})f_{3r}(L_{0}-4r)-\lambda^{-4r}(L_{0}+3r-4ra)f_{3r}(L_{0})\\
=&-r\mu\lambda^{-2r}(L_{0}+2ra-3r).\end{split}

Hence,

f_{n+r}(L_{0})=\lambda^{n}\mu(L_{0}-2(n+r)a+3(n+r)),\forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Then

f_{m}(L_{0})=b\lambda^{m}(L_{0}-2ma+3m),\forall m\in\delta+{\mathbb{Z}},

where
b=\mu\lambda^{-r}
.
By the definition of
\Omega^{\prime}_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,a,b)
,
V
is isomorphic to
\Omega^{\prime}_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,a,b)
as
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
-modules.
∎

Lemma 6.11
.

Suppose
d=0
. Then
V
is isomorphic to
\Omega_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,a,b)
for some
b\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
r\in\delta+{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
. Then
f_{r}(L_{0})=b\lambda^{r}
for some
b\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.
By Lemma
6.6
, for any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we have

(-r-\frac{n}{2})f_{r+n}(L_{0})=b\lambda^{n+r}(L_{0}+na^{\prime})-b\lambda^{n+r}(L_{0}+r+na)=b\lambda^{n+r}(-r-\frac{n}{2}).

So
f_{r+n}(L_{0})=b\lambda^{n+r}
for any
n\neq-2r
.
In particular,
f_{3r}(L_{0})=b\lambda^{3r}
. Let
m=3r,n=-4r
in Lemma
6.6
and we get

-rf_{-r}(L_{0})=\lambda^{-4r}(L_{0}-4ra^{\prime})f_{3r}(L_{0}-4r)-\lambda^{-4r}(L_{0}+3r-4ra)f_{3r}(L_{0})=-rb\lambda^{-r}.

Hence

f_{m}(L_{0})=b\lambda^{m},\forall m\in\delta+{\mathbb{Z}}.

By the definition of
\Omega_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,a,b)
,
V
is isomorphic to
\Omega_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,a,b)
as
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
-modules.
∎

From the discussion above, we have

Theorem 6.12
.

Suppose
V
is an
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
-module such that
V_{\bar{0}}
and
V_{\bar{1}}
are free
{\mathbb{C}}[L_{0}]
-modules of rank
1
by restriction. If
\mathcal{S}(\delta)_{\bar{1}}V\neq 0
, then
V
is isomorphic to
\Omega_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,a,b)
,
\Omega^{\prime}_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,a,b),\Omega^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,b)
or
\tilde{\Omega}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{S}(\delta)}(\lambda,b)
up to a parity-change for some
\lambda,b\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
and some
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

It is easy to see that all these
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
-modules are not simple since
V_{\bar{1}}
is a
\mathcal{S}(\delta)
-submodule of
V
.

\mathcal{S}(\delta)

•

Is there any way to construct new simple
F(\delta)
-modules of nonzero level? Or can we classify such
\mathcal{S}(\delta)

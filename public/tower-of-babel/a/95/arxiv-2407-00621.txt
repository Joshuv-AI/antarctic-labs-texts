A further q q -analogue of a formula due to Guillera


Abstract
Hou, Krattenthaler, and Sun have introduced two q q -analogues of a remarkable series for π 2 \pi^{2} due to Guillera, and these q q -identities were, respectively, proved with the use of a q q -analogue of a Wilf–Zeilberger pair provided by Guillera and with the use of ϕ 2 3 {}_{3}\phi_{2} -transforms. We prove a q q -analogue of Guillera’s formula for π 2 \pi^{2} that is inequivalent to previously known q q -analogues of the same formula due to Guillera, including the Hou–Krattenthaler–Sun q q -identities and a subsequent q q -identity due to Wei. In contrast to previously known q q -analogues of Guillera’s formula, our new q q -analogue involves another free parameter apart from the q q -parameter. Our derivation of this new result relies on the q q -analogue of Zeilberger’s algorithm.

Introduction

\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha,\beta,\ldots,\gamma\vskip 2.84526pt\\

A remarkable result due to Guillera

\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{n}\left[\begin{matrix}1,1,1\vskip 2.84526pt\\
\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}\end{matrix}\right]_{n}(3n+2)

(1)

and is closely related to Ramanujan’s series

\frac{4}{\pi}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{n}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\vskip 2.84526pt\\

Background and preliminaries

The
\pi

Also, Campbell and Levrie applied a series acceleration method due to Wilf

See also

These past references

The
q
-shifted factorial
is such that

(a;q)_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{k})

(2)

for integers
n\geq 0
and for complex
q
such that
|q|<1
, with
(a;q)_{0}=1
by convention. This provides an analogue of the Pochhammer
symbol in the sense that
\lim_{q\to 1}\frac{\left(q^{x};q\right)_{n}}{(1-q)^{n}}=(x)_{n}
.
With regard to the notation in
(
2
), we also write

(a;q)_{\infty}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(a;q)_{n}
,
so that

(a;q)_{n}=\frac{\left(a;q\right)_{\infty}}{\left(aq^{n};q\right)_{\infty}}
.
The
q
-binomial coefficient
may be defined via (
2
)
so that

\binom{n}{k}_{q}=\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{k}(q;q)_{n-k}}
,
with the limiting
q\to 1
case yielding the binomial coefficient
\binom{n}{k}
.
A key to our derivation of a new
q
-analogue of
Guillera’s formula in (
1
)
is given by the
QDifferenceEquations
package for the Maple Computer Algebra System.

Theorem 1.2 from the work of Hou et al.
[
6
]

gives us that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{2n(n+1)}(1+q^{2n+2}-2q^{4n+3})\frac{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n}^{3}}{\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{n+1}^{3}\left(-1;q\right)_{2n+3}}=\\
&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2n}}{(1-q^{2n+1})^{2}}\end{split}

(3)

and that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}\frac{1-q^{3n+2}}{1-q}\frac{\left(q;q\right)_{n}^{3}\left(-q;q\right)_{n}}{\left(q^{3};q^{2}\right)_{n}^{3}}=(1-q)^{2}\frac{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}^{4}}{\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{\infty}^{4}}.

(4)

As expressed by Hou et al.
[
6
]
,
by multiplying both sides of (
3
) by
(1-q)^{2}
and by setting
q\to 1
,
this provides an equivalent version of Guillera’s formula in (
1
). Similarly,
setting
q\to 1
in (
4
),
and by applying a property associted with the
q
-Gamma function that gives us that

\lim_{q\to 1}(1-q)\frac{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}^{2}}{\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{\infty}^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{2}
,
we again obtain an equivalent copy of Guillera’s formula.

3
Main result

Instead of using a
q
-analogue
(F_{q},G_{q})
of the WZ pair
(F,G)
employed by Guillera, as in the work of Hou et al.
[
6
]
, with
\lim_{q\to 1}F_{q}(n,k)=F(n,k)
and similarly for
G_{q}
, we apply a
q
-analogue of Zeilberger’s algorithm
based on the
q
-binomial identity such that
\sum_{k=0}^{n}q^{k^{2}}\binom{n}{k}_{q}^{2}=\binom{2n}{n}_{q}
. This gives us a pair of bivariate

q
-hypergeometric functions inequivalent to that inolved in
[
6
]
.

Our main result is given by the
q
-identity highlighted as Theorem
1
below. Theorem
1
can be shown, as we later
demonstrate, to provide an equivalent version of Guillera’s formula in (
1
), by setting
q\to 1
and
k=\frac{1}{2}
. Theorem

1
is inequivalent to previously known
q
-analogues of (
1
) given in

[
2
,
6
,
11
,
12
]
. An advantage to our main result, as below, is given by how it involves

another
free parameter
k
, apart from the
q
-parameter, but this is not the case for previously known
q
-analogues of Guillera’s formula.

Theorem 1
.

The identity

\displaystyle q\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n-2k+k^{2}}\left(2q^{k}-q^{n+1}-q^{2n+2}\right)}{1+q^{n+1}}\frac{(q;q)_{n}^{4}}{(q;q)_{k-1}^{2}(q;q)_{2n+1}(q;q)_{n-k+1}^{2}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{4}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{(k+n)^{2}}\left((q;q)_{\infty}^{3}-\left(q^{1-n-k};q\right)_{\infty}^{2}\right)\left(q^{1+n+k};q\right)_{\infty}^{2}

holds for complex
q
and
k
such that
|q|<1
and
|k|<1
.

Proof.

By setting

F(n,k):=\frac{q^{k^{2}}\binom{n}{k}_{q}^{2}}{\binom{2n}{n}_{q}},

(5)

an application of the
q
-analogue of Zeilberger’s algorithm produces the certificate

R(n,k):=\frac{q^{n+3}\left(1-q^{k}\right)^{2}\left(q^{n+1}+q^{2n+2}-2q^{k}\right)}{\left(q^{k}-q^{n+1}\right)^{2}\left(1+q^{n+1}\right)\left(1-q^{2n+1}\right)},

which leads us to set
G(n,k):=R(n,k)F(n,k)
. We may thus verify that the difference equation

q^{2}F(n+1,k)-q^{2}F(n,k)=G(n,k+1)-G(n,k)

(6)

holds true. By summing both sides of (
6
) with respect to

n
, a telescoping phenomenon gives us that

q^{2}F(m+1,k)-q^{2}F(0,k)=\sum_{n=0}^{m}(G(n,k+1)-G(n,k))

for nonnegative integers
m
.
From the definition in (
5
) together with
the given constraints on
q
and
k
, we find, by setting
m\to\infty
, that

\frac{q^{k^{2}+2}\left((q;q)_{\infty}^{3}-\left(q^{1-k};q\right)_{\infty}^{2}\right)\left(q^{k+1};q\right)_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{4}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(G(n,k+1)-G(n,k)).

Writing
H(k)
in place of the left-hand side of the above equality, a telescoping phenomenon then gives us that

\sum_{n=0}^{m}H(k+n)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(G(n,k+m+1)-G(n,k)).

(7)

We find that
\lim_{m\to\infty}G(n,k+m+1)
vanishes,
again subject to the given constraints on
q
and
k
.
Setting
m\to\infty
in (
7
),
an application of the Dominated Convergence Theorem allows us to interchange the limiting operations
resulting on the right-hand side,
yielding

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H(k+n)=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}G(n,k)
,
which is equivalent to the desired result.
∎

Setting
q\to 1
on both sides of the
q
-identity given in Theorem
1
, through an application of reindexing and Carlson’s theorem to
the classical identity
\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}^{2}}

derived from the generating function
for the sequence enumerating integer partitions of orders
n\geq 0
,
we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(2-2k+3n)\frac{(1)_{n}^{4}}{(1)_{k-1}^{2}(1)_{2n+1}(1)_{-k+n+1}^{2}}=

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1)_{-k-n}^{2}(1)_{k+n}^{2}}=

(8)

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\psi^{(1)}(k)\sin^{2}(k\pi)}{\pi^{2}},

where
\psi^{(1)}
denotes the trigamma function

\psi^{(1)}(z)=\frac{d^{2}}{dz^{2}}\ln\Gamma(z)
,
and where this last equality may be verified inductively, by taking the partial sums
of the series in (
8
).
In particular, setting
k=\frac{1}{2}
gives us that

\pi=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(3n+2)(1)_{n}^{4}}{(1)_{n+\frac{1}{2}}^{2}(1)_{2n+1}}

and this is equivlaent to Guillera’s formula in
(
1
), according to the Gauss multiplication formula
and the relation
(x)_{n}=\frac{\Gamma(x+n)}{\Gamma(x)}
.

Conclusion

Research related to the Hou–Krattenthaler–Sun

,

For example, by again setting
q\to 1
, and
by applying the operator
\frac{d}{dk}\cdot\big|_{k=\frac{1}{2}}

to both sides of the resultant identity,
we obtain

\frac{7\zeta(3)}{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{n}\left[\begin{matrix}1,1,1\vskip 2.84526pt\\
\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}\end{matrix}\right]_{n}((6n+4)O_{n+1}-1)

odd harmonic number, and where
\zeta(3)=\frac{1}{1^{3}}+\frac{1}{2^{3}}+\cdots

denotes Apéry’s constant.
This provides a harmonic sum
analogue of Guillera’s formula, and we encourage a full exploration of the derivation of such results.

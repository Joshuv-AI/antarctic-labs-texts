Nonlinear BSDEs in general filtration with drivers depending on the martingale part of a solution
Tomasz Klimsiak and Maurycy Rzymowski

Abstract
. In the present paper, we consider multidimensional nonlinear backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) with a driver depending on the martingale part M M of a solution. We assume that the nonlinear term is merely monotone continuous with respect to the state variable. As to the regularity of the driver with respect to the martingale variable, we consider a very general condition which permits path-dependence on "the future" of the process M M as well as a dependence of its law (McKean-Vlasov-type equations). For such driver, we prove the existence and uniqueness of a global solution (i.e. for any maturity T > 0 T>0 ) to BSDE with data satisfying natural integrability conditions.

1.
Introduction

Consider a filtered probability space
(\Omega,{\cal F},P,\mathbb{F}:=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0})
, maturity
T>0
,
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable terminal condition
\xi
, and a driver

f:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{l}

which is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable with respect to the first two variables.
Here
{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
denotes the space of
l
-dimensional càdlàg
\mathbb{F}
-martingales starting at zero with square integrable supremum over
[0,T]
. In the present paper, we study backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs) of the form

(1.1)

Y_{t}=\xi+\int^{T}_{t}f(r,Y_{r},M)\,dr-\int^{T}_{t}\,dM_{r},\quad t\in[0,T].

A solution to (
1.1
) consists of an
l
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-adapted càdlàg process
Y
, and
M\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})

such that (
1.1
) holds
P
-a.s.
We follow and generalize here the framework considered by Liang, Lyons and Qian in
[
14
]
, and Bensoussan, Li and Yam in
[
4
]
. In these papers, the authors considered a special class of (
1.1
) with a driver of the form

(1.2)

f(r,Y_{r},M)=g(r,Y_{r},h(M)_{r}),

where
h
is an operator, which maps
{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
in the space
{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})
- consisting of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable processes that are square integrable with respect to
dt\otimes dP
- and
g:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{l}
.

The purpose of the present paper is to address the conjecture made in
[
14
]
about the existence
of a
global solution
to (
1.1
) with
f
given by (
1.2
) under the following condition on the operator
h
: for some
\gamma_{h}>0
, and any
t\in[0,T]
,

(1.3)

E\int_{t}^{T}|h(M)_{r}-h(N)_{r}|^{2}\,dr\leq\gamma_{h}E\int_{t}^{T}\,d[M-N]_{r},\quad M,N\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}).

Under this condition, and Lipschitz continuity of
g

with respect to the second and the third variable, the authors proved in
[
14
]
that for small enough
T>0
, there exists a
solution to (
1.1
) - the so called
local solution
. For the existence of
global solutions
(i.e. for any
T>0
)
h
is additionally assumed in
[
14
]
to satisfy
local-in-time property
: for any
a<b,\,a,b\in[0,T]
, and any
M\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
,

(1.4)

h(M)_{t}=h(M^{a,b})_{t},\quad t\in(a,b)\quad\mbox{with}\quad M^{a,b}_{t}:=M_{(a\vee t)\wedge b},\quad t\in[0,T].

The above condition stands that the evaluation of
h(M)_{t}
depends only on values of
M_{s},\,s\in[t,t+\varepsilon)
for however small
\varepsilon>0
. In the paper, the authors conjectured (see the comments at the end of Section 3 in
[
14
]
) that
regarding operator
h
, condition (
1.3
) alone is not sufficient to guarantee the existence of a
global solution
to (
1.1
), and suggested that the following operator may serve as an example supporting their conjecture:

(1.5)

h_{1}(M)_{t}=E\big(\int_{t}^{T}\,d[M]_{r}|{\cal F}_{t}),\quad t\in[0,T].

We see that the evaluation of
h_{1}(M)_{t}
depends on the whole future, i.e. it depends on the values
M_{s},\,s\in[t,T]
,
so it does not share
local-in-time property
.

Recently, Cheridito and Nam in
[
8
]
, considered even more general framework than presented above. They introduced the notion of backward stochastic equations (BSEs) of the form

(1.6)

Y_{t}+F_{t}(Y,M)+M_{t}=\xi+F_{T}(Y,M)+M_{T},\quad t\in[0,T]

for given generator
F
.
If we take
F_{t}(Y,M)=\int_{0}^{t}f(r,Y_{r},M)\,dr
, then the above BSE becomes BSDE (
1.1
). One of the results
obtained in
[
8
, Proposition 3.7]
applies to BSDEs (
1.1
) with
f
given by (
1.2
), and
h
of a special form. However, a close inspection of the proof of
[
8
, Proposition 3.7]
reveals that it in fact applies not only to
h

specified therein but to any
h
satisfying (
1.3
) and the following additional condition:

(1.7)

\mbox{if}\,\,\,M,N\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})\,\,\,\mbox{are strongly orthogonal, then}\,\,\,h(M+N)=h(M)+h(N).

Therefore, from
[
8
, Proposition 3.7]
it follows that if
g
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to the second and the third variable (uniformly in
t
), and
h
satisfies (
1.3
),(
1.7
), then there exists a
global solution
to (
1.1
).
Observe that operator
h_{1}
satisfies the latter condition. Thereby, it does not support the conjecture stated in
[
14
]
.

The main result of the present paper (Theorem
6.3
) disproves the conjecture formulated in
[
14
]
, and shows that in fact condition (
1.3
) and Lipschitz continuity of driver
g
with respect to the second and the third variable (uniformly in
t
) guarantee the existence
of a unique
global solution
to (
1.1
) with
f
of the form (
1.2
). In fact, we shall prove our existence and uniqueness result of
global solutions
under considerable weaker hypotheses, and
in general framework (
1.1
).
We assume that
f
is merely continuous monotone in the second variable, i.e. for some
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and any
t\in[0,T]
,

\langle f(t,y_{1},M)-f(t,y_{2},M),y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle\leq\mu|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2},\quad y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{l},\,M\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}).

Moreover, the growth of
f
with respect to
y
is subject to no restriction except the local behavior: for any
R>0
,

E\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{|y|\leq R}|f(t,y,0)|\,dt\Big)^{2}<\infty.

Instead of (
1.3
), which only fits into the framework (
1.2
), we consider a more general condition
of the following form: for any
t\in[0,T]
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
,

(1.8)

E\int_{t}^{T}|f(r,y,M)-f(r,y,M^{\prime})|^{2}\,dr\leq\lambda E\int_{t}^{T}\,d[M-M^{\prime}]_{r},\quad M,M^{\prime}\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}),

which holds in particular when
f
is given by (
1.2
),
h
satisfies (
1.3
), and
g
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to
h(M)_{t}
.
When
\mathbb{F}
is generated by a Brownian motion
B
,
f
is given by (
1.2
), and
h(M)=Z^{M}
,
where
Z^{M}
is a unique progressively measurable process such that
M_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}Z_{r}^{M}\,dB_{r},\,t\in[0,T]
, then our assumptions
agree with the ones considered in
[
6
]
(with
p=2
in
[
6
]
; see also
[
12
, Section 6]
, where reflected BSDEs on general filtered spaces are considered).

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the basic notation and hypotheses.
Section
3
is devoted to presenting several examples of driver
f
satisfying (
1.8
).
They show that the approach and framework
considered in the present paper provide a unified way of treating a wide variety of seemingly disparate classes of BSDEs.
The definition of a solution and a priori estimates are contained in Section
4
. In Section
5
we give an existence and uniqueness result for BSDEs with a driver independent of
M
. Finally, Section
6
contains the proof of the paper’s main result. In Section
7
, we demonstrate that the proof technique used in Section
6
may also be utilized to BSDEs
of the form

(1.9)

Y_{t}=\xi+\int^{T}_{t}f(r,Y,M)\,dr-\int^{T}_{t}\,dM_{r},\quad t\in[0,T],

where now the driver may also depend on the future evaluation of
Y
. We prove
that under the following Lipschitz type condition:

(A)

There exists
L>0
such that for any
t\in[0,T]
,
M,M^{\prime}\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
,
Y,Y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})

E\int_{t}^{T}|f(r,Y,M)-f(r,Y^{\prime},M^{\prime})|^{2}\,dr\leq L(E\int_{t}^{T}\,d[M-M^{\prime}]_{r}+E\int_{t}^{T}|Y_{r}-Y^{\prime}_{r}|^{2}\,dr),

and standard integrability assumptions on the data, there exists a unique
global solution
to (
1.9
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Basic notation

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
,
|x|
denotes the Euclidean norm and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

the natural inner product in
{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
. We set
\mbox{\rm sgn}(x)=\mathbf{1}_{|x|\neq 0}\,x/|x|
. Let
(\mathcal{X},\rho)
be a separable metric linear space. We denote by
L^{2}(\Omega,P;\mathcal{X})
the set of
{\cal F}_{T}
-measurable
\mathcal{X}
-valued random variables
X
such that

E\rho^{2}(X,0)<\infty.

We let

\rho_{L^{2}}(X^{1},X^{2}):=\big(E\rho^{2}(X^{1},X^{2})\big)^{1/2},

and if
\mathcal{X}
is a normed space, then we let
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
denote the norm generated by
\rho_{L^{2}}
.
By
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathcal{X})
, we denote the set of all
\mathcal{X}
-valued
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable processes
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, such that

E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\rho^{2}(Y_{t},0)<\infty.

We set

\rho_{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}}(Y^{1},Y^{2}):=\big(E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\rho^{2}(Y^{1}_{t},Y^{2}_{t})\big)^{1/2}

If
\mathcal{X}
is a normed space, then by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}}
we denote the norm generated by
\rho_{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}}
.
We let
\mathcal{M}_{loc}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})
denote the set of all càdlàg processes
M=(M^{1}_{t},\dots,M^{l})_{t\in[0,T]}

such that for any
i\in\{1,\dots,l\}
process
M^{i}
is a local
\mathbb{F}
-martingale. By
\mathcal{M}^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
we denote the subset of
\mathcal{M}_{loc}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})
consisting of processes
M
such that

\|M\|_{{\cal M}^{2}}:=(E[M]_{T})^{1/2}<\infty,

where
[M]_{T}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}[M^{i}]_{T}
and
[M^{i}]
is the
square bracket
of
M^{i},\,i=1,\dots,l
.
By
{\cal M}^{2}_{0,loc}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})
(resp.
{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
) we denote the subspace of
{\cal M}^{2}_{loc}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
(resp.
{\cal M}^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
)
consisting of those processes
M
for which
M_{0}=0
.
We let
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathcal{X})
be the set of all

\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable
processes
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
taking values in
\mathcal{X}
, such that

E\int^{T}_{0}\rho^{2}(X_{r},0)\,dr<\infty.

We let

\rho_{{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}}(X^{1},X^{2}):=\Big(E\int^{T}_{0}\rho^{2}(X^{1}_{r},X^{2}_{r})\,dr\Big)^{1/2}.

When
\mathcal{X}
is a normed space, then by
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}}
we denote the norm generated by
\rho_{{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}}
.
By
B_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega,P;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}))
, we denote the set of all
\mathbb{F}
-adapted
\mathbb{R}^{l}
-valued processes
V=(V_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, such that

\|V\|_{B_{\mathbb{F}}}:=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big(E|V_{t}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}<\infty.

In the whole paper all relations between random variables hold

P
-a.s. For
\mathbb{R}^{l}
-valued processes
X
,
Y
we write
X_{t}=Y_{t},\,t\in[0,T]
(or simply
X=Y
) iff

P(\exists_{t\in[0,T]}\,\,X_{t}\neq Y_{t})=0.

For given
d\in\mathbb{N}
we let
\ell^{d}
denote
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

2.2.
Assumptions on the data

The generator (driver) is the mapping

f:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{l}\times{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})\to\mathbb{R}^{l},

which is
\mathbb{F}
-adapted for fixed
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{l},M\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
.

We shall need the following hypotheses:

(H1)

E|\xi|^{2}<\infty,\,E(\int_{0}^{T}|f(r,0,0)|\,dr)^{2}<\infty
,

(H2)

There exists
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that for any
t\in[0,T]
,
y,y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
,
M\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
,

\langle y-y^{\prime},f(t,y,M)-f(t,y^{\prime},M)\rangle\leq\mu|y-y^{\prime}|^{2},

(H3)

There exists
\lambda>0
such that for any
t\in[0,T]
,
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
,
M,M^{\prime}\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
,

E\int_{t}^{T}|f(r,y,M)-f(r,y,M^{\prime})|^{2}\,dr\leq\lambda E\int_{t}^{T}\,d[M-M^{\prime}]_{r},

(H4)

For every
(t,M)\in[0,T]\times{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
the
mapping
\mathbb{R}^{l}\ni y\rightarrow f(t,y,M)
is continuous
P
-a.s.,

(H5)

For each
r>0
,

E\big(\int^{T}_{0}\psi_{r}(t)\,dt\big)^{2}<\infty,

where
\psi_{r}(t)=\sup_{|y|<r}|f(t,y,0)-f(t,0,0)|
.

3.
Examples of generators satisfying (H3)

In this section we shall give several examples of generators satisfying (H3). They show that the approach and framework
considered in the present paper provides a unified way of treating a wide variety of seemingly disparate classes of BSDEs.
We start with generators of the form (
1.2
) (Examples
3.1
–
3.7
). Then BSDE (
1.1
) is of the following form

(3.1)

Y_{t}=\xi+\int^{T}_{t}g(r,Y_{r},h(M)_{r})\,dr-\int^{T}_{t}\,dM_{r},\quad t\in[0,T].

In fact we consider slightly more general case, when
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is replaced by the metric space
\mathcal{X}
.
In this case Lipschitz continuity of
g
with respect to the third variable and condition (
1.3
), which now admits the following form

(3.2)

E\int_{t}^{T}\rho^{2}(h(M)_{r},h(N)_{r})\,dr\leq\gamma_{h}E\int_{t}^{T}\,d[M-N]_{r},\quad M,N\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}),

imply (H3). In Example
3.8
we show that more general than (
3.1
) framework (
1.1
) allows us in particular to
cover a class of McKean-Vlasov-type equations.

Example 3.1

(Classical BSDEs)
.

In the pioneering paper
[
18
]
Pardoux and Peng introduced the notion of BSDEs, with a filtration
\mathbb{F}

that was assumed to be generated by a given
d
-dimensional Brownian motion
B
, of the following form

(3.3)

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r})\,dr-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{r}\,dB_{r},\quad t\in[0,T].

A solution of equation (
3.3
) consists of a continuous process
Y\in\mathcal{S}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
, and

Z\in{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Observe that BSDE (
3.1
) becomes BSDE (
3.3
)
if we consider
\mathbb{F}
as specified above,
\mathcal{X}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and

h(M)=Z^{M},

where
Z^{M}

is a unique process in
{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
M_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}Z^{M}_{r}\,dB_{r},\,t\in[0,T]
.
Clearly,
h
satisfies (
3.2
).

Example 3.2

(Lévy processes and Teugels martingales)
.

In
[
15
]
Nualart and Schoutens considered filtration
\mathbb{F}
generated by a
d
-dimensional Lévy process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}

with the Lévy triplet
(a,\sigma,\nu)
. They assumed additionally that for some
\alpha>0
and any
\varepsilon>0
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}e^{\alpha|x|}\,\nu(dx)<\infty.

Then there exists an orthonormal sequence
(H^{i})_{i\geq 0}\subset{\cal M}^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
, built up
from Teugels martingales, that furnishes the following representation property: for any
M\in{\cal M}_{0}^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
there exists a unique sequence
(Z^{M,i})_{i\geq 1}\subset{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that

M_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}Z^{M,i}_{r}\,dH^{i}_{r},\quad t\in[0,T].

Based on this representation the authors in
[
15
]
introduced and studied BSDEs of the form

(3.4)

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(r,Y_{r},(Z^{i}_{r})_{i\geq 1})\,dr-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{t}^{T}Z^{i}_{r}\,dH^{i}_{r},\quad t\in[0,T].

Observe that equation (
3.1
) becomes (
3.4
) if we take
\mathcal{X}=l^{2}:=\{(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}:\|(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\|^{2}_{l^{2}}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{2}<\infty\}
, and

h(M)=(Z^{M,i})_{i\geq 1}.

One easily checks that
h
fulfills (
3.2
).

Remark 3.3
.

A more advanced model of the above type on general filtered spaces was considered in
[
12
, Section 6]

for reflected BSDEs (see also
[
9
]
for BSDEs).

Example 3.4

(Poisson random measure)
.

Assume that
B
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion with respect to
\mathbb{F}
and
N
is a
d
-dimensional Poisson
random measure on
E:={\mathbb{R}}^{m}\setminus\{0\}
(independent of

), with an intensity

\ell^{1}\otimes\nu
(
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{d})
) satisfying

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{E}1\wedge|x|^{2}\,\nu_{i}(dx)<\infty,

such that for any

,
\tilde{N}([0,t],A):=N([0,t],A)-t\nu(A)
is an

-valued martingale with respect to
\mathbb{F}
.
In this case any martingale
M\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
admits a unique representation (see e.g.
[
10
, Section III.4]
)

M_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}Z^{M}_{r}\,dB_{r}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{E}U_{r}(x)\,\tilde{N}(dr,dx)+L^{M}_{t},\quad t\in[0,T],

where
Z^{M}\in{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
U\in{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(E,\nu;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
, and

is strongly orthogonal to
B
and
\tilde{N}
. There are numerous results in the literature (see e.g.
[
2
,
13
,
21
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
) concerning BSDEs of the form

(3.5)

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(r,Y_{r},Z_{r},U_{r})\,dr-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{r}\,dB_{r}-\int_{t}^{T}\int_{E}U_{r}(x)\,\tilde{N}(dr,dx),\quad t\in[0,T].

This equation fits into our framework again. To see this take
\mathcal{X}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times L^{2}(E,\nu;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d})

with natural metric
\rho
, and

(3.6)

h(M)=(Z^{M},U^{M}).

One easily checks that (
3.2
) holds.

Example 3.5

(Carré du champs operators)
.

One of the major advantage of BSDE’s theory is that it provides an easy path for proving
probabilistic interpretation of solutions to a class of semilinear PDEs.
Let
\mathbb{F}
be the filtration generated by a Markov process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, say Feller process, associated to
Feller generator
(A,D(A))
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subset D(A)
. It is well known that any such operator is of the form

\displaystyle Au(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}q_{i,j}(x)u_{x_{i}x_{j}}(x)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{i}(x)u_{x_{i}}(x)+c(x)u(x)

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(u(x+y)-u(x)-\mathbf{1}_{\{|y|\leq 1\}}\langle\nabla u(x),y\rangle\big)\,N(x,dy),

with
x
-dependent Lévy triplet
(b(x),Q(x),N(x,dy))
, and
c\leq 0
(see e.g.
[
5
]
).
When
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a diffusion process, i.e.
N\equiv 0
, then solutions to the Cauchy problem

(3.7)

-\partial_{t}u-Au=g(t,x,u,\sigma\nabla u),\quad u(T,\cdot)=\varphi,

where
\sigma\cdot\sigma^{T}=Q
,
are related to BSDEs of the form (
3.3
) with
\xi=\varphi(X_{T})
and
f(r,y,z)=\hat{f}(r,X_{r},y,z)
(see e.g.
[
1
,
16
,
11
,
17
,
22
]
).
However, for general operator
A
it is more natural to consider
\sqrt{\Gamma(u)}
in place of
\sigma\nabla u
in the nonlinear part
of equation (
3.7
), where
\Gamma
is the so called
carré du champs operator
:

\Gamma(u):=A(u^{2})-2uAu,\quad u\in D(A).

Solutions of such equations are related to BSDE (
3.1
), where
\xi,f
are as in the foregoing, and

h(M)_{r}=\sqrt{\frac{d\langle M\rangle_{r}}{dr}}

(see e.g.
[
3
]
). Observe that (
3.2
) is satisfied once again.

In all the above examples operator
h
fulfills
local-in-time property
(
1.4
). Furthermore, it satisfies condition (
1.7
). Now, we shall give simple examples of operator
h
which fulfills (
1.3
), and at the same time does not satisfy (
1.4
) nor (
1.7
). In the sequel we denote by
D(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
the set of all càdlàg
{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
-valued functions on
[0,T]
.

Example 3.6

(Beyond local-in-time property; path dependent BSDEs)
.

Consider a functional
\phi:D(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
such that for some
L>0
and any
a,b\in D(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
,

|\phi(a)-\phi(b)|\leq L\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|a(t)-b(t)|.

Then operator
h:{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})\rightarrow{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}})
, defined by

h(M)_{t}=E(\phi(M_{\cdot\wedge t}-M_{t})|{\cal F}_{t}),

satisfies (
1.3
), however in general it does not satisfy (
1.4
) nor (
1.7
) (take e.g.
\phi(a)=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|a(t)|
).

Example 3.7

(Beyond local-in-time property; anticipated BSDEs)
.

Let
\zeta,\delta:[0,T]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
be continuous functions. With the notation of Example
3.4
, we set

h(M)_{t}=(E(Z^{M}_{t+\zeta(t)}|{\cal F}_{t}),E(U^{M}_{t+\delta(t)}|{\cal F}_{t})),\quad t\in[0,T],

where
(Z^{M}_{t},U^{M}_{t})=(\eta_{t},\hat{\eta}_{t}),\,t\geq T
for given stochastic processes
\eta,\hat{\eta}
.
BSDEs of the form (
3.1
) with
h
as above were introduced (for Brownian filtration) by Peng and Yang in
[
20
]
.
Observe that
h
satisfies (
3.2
) and does not satisfy (
1.4
). If we consider in the definition of
h

nonlinear expectation (see
[
19
]
), then condition (
1.7
) will be also violated.

The last example exhibits the advantage of the general framework (
1.1
) over the framework (
3.1
).
By
\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathcal{X})
we denote the set of all probability measures
\mu
on
\mathcal{X}
for which

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\rho(x,0)\,\mu(dx)<\infty.

We let
W_{\mathcal{X}}
denote the
Wasserstein metric
on
\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathcal{X})
:

\displaystyle W_{\mathcal{X}}(\mu,\nu)=\inf\{\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}\rho(x,y)\,P(dx,dy):P\in{\cal P}_{\mu,\nu}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X})\},

where
{\cal P}_{\mu,\nu}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X})=\{P\in{\cal P}_{1}(\mathcal{X})\times{\cal P}_{1}(\mathcal{X}):P(dx,\mathcal{X})=\mu(dx),\,P(\mathcal{X},dy)=\nu(dy)\}
.

Example 3.8

(McKean-Vlasov-type equations)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=D(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
, and
\rho
be the Skorokhod metric on
D(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
.
Consider a function

\hat{f}:\Omega\times[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{l}\times{\cal P}_{1}(\mathcal{X})\to{\mathbb{R}}^{l}

such that for some
L>0
and any
t\in[0,T]
,
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
,

|\hat{f}(t,y,\mu)-\hat{f}(t,y,\nu)|\leq LW_{\mathcal{X}}(\mu,\nu),\quad\mu,\nu\in{\cal P}_{1}(\mathcal{X}).

For given martingale
M\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
, and
t\in[0,T]
we denote
M^{t}=M_{\cdot\wedge t}-M_{t}
.
We set

f(t,y,M):=\hat{f}(t,y,\mathcal{L}(M^{t})),\quad t\in[0,T],y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{l},M\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}).

Observe that

|f(r,y,M)-f(r,y,N)|\leq LW_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{L}(M^{r}),\mathcal{L}(N^{r}))\leq LE\rho(M^{r},N^{r})\leq LE\sup_{s\in[r,T]}|M^{r}_{s}-N^{r}_{s}|.

Therefore, by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, we easily obtain (H3). We may also get an interesting example
of generator
f
by taking
\mathcal{X}=({\mathbb{R}}^{l}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times L^{2}(E,\nu;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
(cf.
[
7
,
8
]
), and natural metric
\rho

on
\mathcal{X}
(under assumptions and notation of Example
3.4
).
Then, by using representation (
3.6
), we may define

f(t,y,M):=\hat{f}(t,y,\mathcal{L}(Z^{M}_{t},U^{M}_{t})),\quad t\in[0,T],y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{l},M\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}).

One easily checks that
f
satisfies (H3).

4.
Definition of a solution to BSDE and a priori estimates

Definition 4.1
.

We say that a pair
(Y,M)
of
\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes is a solution of the backward stochastic differential equation with right-hand side
f
and terminal value
\xi
(BSDE(
\xi,f
) for short) if

(a)

Y
is càdlàg and
M\in\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
,

(b)

\int^{T}_{0}|f(r,Y_{r},M)|\,dr<+\infty
,

(c)

Y_{t}=\xi+\int^{T}_{t}f(r,Y_{r},M)\,dr-\int^{T}_{t}\,dM_{r},\quad t\in[0,T].

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that
(H1)–(H3)
are in force. Then there exists
C>0
, depending only on
T,\mu^{+},\lambda
, such that for any solution
(Y,M)\in{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
to
BSDE
(\xi,f)
,

E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y_{t}|^{2}+E\int^{T}_{0}\,d[M]_{r}\leq CE\Bigg(|\xi|^{2}+\Big(\int^{T}_{0}|f(r,0,0)|\,dr\Big)^{2}\Bigg).

Proof.

All the constants in the proof labelled
C_{i}
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
, shall depend only on
\lambda,\mu^{+},T
.
By Itô’s formula

(4.1)

|Y_{t}|^{2}+\int^{T}_{t}\,d[M]_{r}=|\xi|^{2}+2\int^{T}_{t}\langle Y_{r},f(r,Y_{r},M)\rangle\,dr-2\int^{T}_{t}\langle Y_{r-},dM_{r}\rangle.

By (H2)

(4.2)

\begin{split}&\langle Y_{r},f(r,Y_{r},M)\rangle\leq|Y_{r}||f(r,Y_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},0)|+\mu^{+}|Y_{r}|^{2}+|Y_{r}||f(r,0,0)|\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+\nu)|Y_{r}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2(1+\nu)}|f(r,Y_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},0)|^{2}+\mu^{+}|Y_{r}|^{2}+|Y_{r}||f(r,0,0)|,\end{split}

for every
\nu\geq 0
.
From (
4.1
), (
4.2
) we get

(4.3)

\begin{split}|Y_{t}|^{2}&+\int^{T}_{t}\,d[M]_{r}\leq|\xi|^{2}+(1+\nu+2\mu^{+})\int^{T}_{t}|Y_{r}|^{2}\,dr\\
&+\frac{1}{1+\nu}\int^{T}_{t}|f(r,Y_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},0)|^{2}\,dr\\
&+2\int^{T}_{t}|Y_{r}||f(r,0,0)|\,dr-2\int^{T}_{t}\langle Y_{r-},dM_{r}\rangle.\end{split}

Since
Y\in\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
, and
M\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
, we have that
\int_{0}^{\cdot}\langle Y_{r-},dM_{r}\rangle

is a martingale.
Thus, taking the expectation, and using (H3), we obtain

(4.4)

\begin{split}E|Y_{t}|^{2}&+E\int^{T}_{t}\,d[M]_{r}\leq E|\xi|^{2}+(1+\nu+2\mu^{+})E\int^{T}_{t}|Y_{r}|^{2}\,dr\\
&+\frac{\lambda}{1+\nu}E\int^{T}_{t}\,d[M]_{r}+2E\int^{T}_{t}|Y_{r}||f(r,0,0)|\,dr.\end{split}

Hence, for
\nu:=2\lambda
, we get

(4.5)

\begin{split}E|Y_{t}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}E\int^{T}_{t}\,d[M]_{r}&\leq E|\xi|^{2}+(1+\nu+2\mu^{+})E\int^{T}_{t}|Y_{r}|^{2}\,dr\\
&\quad+2E\int^{T}_{0}|Y_{r}||f(r,0,0)|\,dr.\end{split}

Set
\beta_{\nu}:=(1+\nu+2\mu^{+})
. By the Gronwall lemma

E|Y_{t}|^{2}\leq e^{\beta_{\nu}T}(E|\xi|^{2}+2E\int^{T}_{0}|Y_{r}||f(r,0,0)|\,dr).

This combined with (
4.5
) yields

(4.6)

\begin{split}E|Y_{t}|^{2}+E\int^{T}_{0}\,d[M]_{r}\leq C_{1}EX,\end{split}

for some
C_{1}>0
, where
X:=|\xi|^{2}+2\int^{T}_{0}|Y_{r}||f(r,0,0)|\,dr
. From (
4.3
), (H3), (
4.6
), and the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality,
we conclude that for any
\beta>0
,

(4.7)

\begin{split}E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y_{t}|^{2}&\leq\frac{\lambda}{1+\nu}E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M]_{r}\,dr+C_{2}EX+2E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int^{T}_{t}\langle Y_{r-},dM_{r}\rangle\Big|\\
&\leq\frac{\lambda}{1+\nu}E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M]_{r}\,dr+C_{2}EX+C_{3}E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y_{t}|\left(\int^{T}_{0}\,d[M]_{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq(\frac{1}{2}+C_{3}\beta^{-1})E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M]_{r}\,dr+C_{2}EX+C_{3}\beta E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y_{t}|^{2}\\
&\leq\big[(\frac{1}{2}+C_{3}\beta^{-1})C_{1}+C_{2}\big]EX+C_{3}\beta E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y_{t}|^{2}.\end{split}

Taking
\beta=\frac{1}{2C_{3}}
we get that for some
C_{4}>0
,

(4.8)

\begin{split}E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y_{t}|^{2}+E\int^{T}_{0}\,d[M]_{r}\leq C_{4}EX\end{split}

Observe that for any
\alpha>0
,

EX\leq\alpha E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y_{t}|^{2}+\alpha^{-1}E\Bigg(|\xi|^{2}+\Big(\int^{T}_{0}|f(r,0,0)|\,dr\Big)^{2}\Bigg).

Taking
\alpha=\frac{1}{2C_{4}}
we conclude from (
4.8
) the desired inequality.
∎

When
l=1
we are able to say something more about integrability of the driver
f
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
l=1
. Assume that
(H1)–(H3)
are in force. Then there exists
C>0
, depending only on
T,\mu^{+},\lambda
, such that for any solution
(Y,M)\in{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
to
BSDE
(\xi,f)
,

E\Big(\int^{T}_{0}|f(r,Y_{r},M)|\,dr\Big)^{2}\leq CE\Bigg(|\xi|^{2}+\Big(\int^{T}_{0}|f(r,0,0)|\,dr\Big)^{2}\Bigg).

Proof.

Throughout the proof
C
denotes a constant which can vary from line to line but depends only on
T,\mu^{+},\lambda
.
By the Itô-Meyer formula

(4.9)

\begin{split}|Y_{t}|\leq|\xi|+\int^{T}_{t}\mbox{\rm sgn}(Y_{r})f(r,Y_{r},M)\,dr-\int^{T}_{t}\mbox{\rm sgn}(Y_{r-})\,dM_{r},\quad t\in[0,T].\end{split}

Hence

(4.10)

\begin{split}|Y_{t}|\leq|\xi|+\int^{T}_{t}\mbox{\rm sgn}(Y_{r})f(r,Y_{r},M)\,dr+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int^{T}_{t}\mbox{\rm sgn}(Y_{r-})\,dM_{r}\Big|,\quad t\in[0,T].\end{split}

By (H2)

\begin{split}-\mbox{\rm sgn}(Y_{r})f(r,Y_{r},M)&\geq-\mbox{\rm sgn}(Y_{r})(f(r,Y_{r},0)-f(r,0,0))+\mu^{+}|Y_{r}|\\
&\quad-|f(r,Y_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},0)|-|f(r,0,0)|-\mu^{+}|Y_{r}|\\
&=\big|\mbox{\rm sgn}(Y_{r})(f(r,Y_{r},0)-f(r,0,0))-\mu^{+}|Y_{r}|\big|\\
&\quad-|f(r,Y_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},0)|-|f(r,0,0)|-\mu^{+}|Y_{r}|\\
&\geq\big|f(r,Y_{r},0)-f(r,0,0)\big|-\mu^{+}|Y_{r}|\\
&\quad-|f(r,Y_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},0)|-|f(r,0,0)|-\mu^{+}|Y_{r}|\\
&\geq|f(r,Y_{r},0)|-|f(r,Y_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},0)|-2|f(r,0,0)|-2\mu^{+}|Y_{r}|.\end{split}

From this inequality and (
4.10
) we infer that

(4.11)

\begin{split}&\Big(\int^{T}_{0}|f(r,Y_{r},0)|\,dr\Big)^{2}\leq C\Bigg(|\xi|^{2}+\int^{T}_{0}|f(r,Y_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},0)|^{2}\,dr\\
&\quad\quad+\Big(\int^{T}_{0}|f(r,0,0)|\,dr\Big)^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|Y_{r}|\,dr+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int^{T}_{t}\mbox{\rm sgn}(Y_{r-})\,dM_{r}\Big|^{2}\Bigg).\end{split}

Thus, applying the expectation to (
4.11
), and using (H3) and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality yields

(4.12)

\begin{split}E\Big(\int^{T}_{0}|f(r,Y_{r},0)|\,dr\Big)^{2}\leq CE\Big(|\xi|^{2}+\Big(\int^{T}_{0}|f(r,0,0)|\,dr\Big)^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|Y_{r}|\,dr+\int^{T}_{0}d[M]_{r}\Big).\end{split}

Therefore, by (H3) and Proposition
4.2
we get the desired inequality.
∎

5.
BSDEs with driver independent of the martingale part

In the present section we shall give an existence result for BSDEs in case the driver
f

does not depend on the martingale part of a solution. The difficulty lies in the fact that

f
is assumed only monotone with respect to
y
with no assumptions on the growth.
In the proof, among others, we adapt and combine the techniques applied in
[
17
]
and
[
6
]
, where the authors considered BSDEs
on Brownian filtration.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
f
does not depend on
M
and
(H1),(H2),(H4),(H5)
are in force. Then there exists
(Y,M)
- a solution to BSDE
(\xi,f)
- such that
Y\in\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
.

Proof.

Since
f
does not depend on
M
, (H5) is of the form

(H5)

for any
r>0
,
E(\int^{T}_{0}\psi_{r}(t)\,dt)^{2}<\infty
, where
\psi_{r}(t)=\sup_{|y|<r}|f(t,y)-f(t,0)|
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mu\leq 0
. We divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1.
In the first step, we need the following additional assumptions: there exist
\alpha,\beta>0
such that

(5.1)

|f(t,y)|\leq\alpha|f(t,0)|+\beta,\quad t\in[0,T],\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{l},

and there exist
C>0
, such that

(5.2)

|\xi|+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|f(t,0)|\leq C

In particular, the above conditions imply that
|f|
is bounded. Let
\varrho
be a non-negative smooth function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
with support in
B(0,1):=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{l}:|x|\leq 1\}
such that
\varrho(0)=1
, and
\varrho(x)<1,\,x\neq 0
.
We set

f_{n}(t,y):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{l}}f(t,x)\varrho_{n}(y-x)\,dx,\quad t\in[0,T],y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{l},

where
\varrho_{n}(x)=a_{n}\rho(nx)
,
a_{n}>0
, and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{l}}\varrho_{n}(x)\,dx=1,\quad n\geq 1.

It is well known that
f_{n}
is smooth and
f_{n}\rightarrow f
uniformly on compacts (both properties with respect to
y
under fixed
\omega\in\Omega
and
t\in[0,T]
).
Observe that by assumptions made in the present step

|f_{n}(t,y)-f_{n}(t,y^{\prime})|\leq 2\ell^{l}(B(0,1))(\alpha C+\beta)\|\nabla\varrho_{n}\|_{\infty}|y-y^{\prime}|,\quad t\in[0,T],\,y,y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{l},

where
\ell^{l}
is
l
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Furthermore,
f_{n}
satisfies (H2) with
\mu\leq 0
, and

(5.3)

|f_{n}(t,y)|\leq(\alpha C+\beta).

By
[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
the problem BSDE
(\xi,f_{n})
admits a unique solution
(Y^{n},M^{n})\in{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
.
By Ito’s formula

(5.4)

\begin{split}e^{t}|Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}+\int^{T}_{t}e^{r}|Y^{n}_{r}|^{2}\,dr+\int^{T}_{t}e^{r}\,d[M^{n}]_{r}&=e^{T}|\xi|^{2}+2\int^{T}_{t}e^{r}\langle Y^{n}_{r},f_{n}(r,Y^{n}_{r})\rangle\,dr\\
&\quad-2\int^{T}_{r}e^{r}\langle Y^{n}_{r-},dM^{n}_{r}\rangle,\quad t\in[0,T].\end{split}

Using (H2) (cf. the comment preceding (
5.3
)), we get

2\langle Y^{n}_{r},f_{n}(r,Y^{n}_{r})\rangle\leq|Y^{n}_{r}|^{2}+|f_{n}(t,0)|^{2}.

Therefore, applying conditional expectation to both sides of (
5.4
) yields

e^{t}|Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}\leq E\Big(e^{T}|\xi|^{2}+\int^{T}_{0}e^{r}|f_{n}(r,0)|^{2}\,dr|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big).

From this, (
5.2
) and (
5.3
),

(5.5)

|Y^{n}_{t}|\leq e^{T}[C+\sqrt{T}(\alpha C+\beta)],\quad t\in[0,T].

Let
U^{n}_{t}=f_{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t})
,
t\in[0,T]
. By (
5.3
), Proposition
4.2
, and the
Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Bigg(E\int^{T}_{0}|U^{n}_{t}|^{2}\,dt+E\int^{T}_{0}\,d[M^{n}]_{r}+E\sup_{t\leq T}|M^{n}_{t}|^{2}\Bigg)<\infty.

Since both
{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
,
L^{2}(\Omega,P;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
are Hilbert spaces, they are reflexive. Therefore, by the Banach-Alaoglu theorem there exists a
subsequence (not relabelled) such that
(U^{n},M^{n})
converges weakly in the space
{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
to
(U,\tilde{M})
and
M^{n}_{T}
converges weakly in the space
L^{2}(\Omega,P;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
to
N
. Let
M
be a càdlàg version of the martingale
E(N|\mathcal{F}_{t})
,
t\in[0,T]
.
We shall prove that for any
\tau\in\mathcal{T}

(5.6)

M^{n}_{\tau}\rightarrow M_{\tau},\quad\mbox{weakly in }\quad L^{2}(\Omega,P;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}).

Let
X\in L^{2}(\Omega,P;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
, then

E\langle M^{n}_{\tau},X\rangle=E\langle E(M^{n}_{T}|\mathcal{F}_{\tau}),X\rangle=E\langle M^{n}_{T},E(X|\mathcal{F}_{\tau})\rangle\rightarrow E\langle M_{T},E(X|\mathcal{F}_{\tau})\rangle=E\langle M_{\tau},X\rangle.

Observe that
\tilde{M}=M
in
{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
.
Indeed, let
Z\in{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
. Then
Z_{r}\in L^{2}(\Omega,P;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
for a.e.
r\in[0,T]
.
By (
5.6
),

E\langle M^{n}_{T},Z_{r}\rangle\to E\langle M_{T},Z_{r}\rangle,\quad\mbox{for a.e. }\,\,r\in[0,T].

Therefore,

\displaystyle E\int_{0}^{T}\langle\tilde{M}_{r},Z_{r}\rangle\,dr

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}E\int_{0}^{T}\langle M^{n}_{r},Z_{r}\rangle\,dr

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}E\langle M^{n}_{T},Z_{r}\rangle\,dr=\int_{0}^{T}E\langle M_{T},Z_{r}\rangle\,dr=E\int_{0}^{T}\langle M_{r},Z_{r}\rangle\,dr.

In the third equation we applied the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem by using the following estimation

E\langle M^{n}_{T},Z_{r}\rangle\leq\sup_{n\geq 1}E\sup_{t\leq T}|M^{n}_{t}|^{2}+E|Z_{r}|^{2}.

The next convergence property we need is as follows: for any
t\in[0,T]

(5.7)

\int^{T}_{t}U^{n}_{r}\,dr\rightarrow\int^{T}_{t}U_{r}\,dr\quad\mbox{ weakly in }\quad L^{2}(\Omega,P;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}).

This easily follows from the following calculation.
Let
X\in L^{2}(\Omega,P;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
, then

\begin{split}E\langle X,\int^{T}_{t}U^{n}_{r}\,dr\rangle&=E\int^{T}_{t}\langle X,E(U^{n}_{r}|\mathcal{F}_{r})\rangle\,dr\\
&\quad=E\int^{T}_{0}\langle U^{n}_{r},\mathbf{1}_{[t,T]}(r)E(X|\mathcal{F}_{r})\rangle\,dr\rightarrow E\int^{T}_{0}\langle U_{r},\mathbf{1}_{[t,T]}(r)E(X|\mathcal{F}_{r})\rangle\,dr\\
&\quad=E\langle X,\int^{T}_{t}U_{r}\,dr\rangle.\end{split}

Set

Y_{t}:=\xi+\int^{T}_{t}U_{r}\,dr-\int^{T}_{t}\,dM_{r},\quad t\in[0,T].

By (
5.6
) and (
5
) for any
t\in[0,T]

(5.8)

Y^{n}_{t}\rightarrow Y_{t}\quad\mbox{ weakly in }\quad L^{2}(\Omega,P;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}).

We shall prove that
U=f(\cdot,Y)
in
{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
. Let
X\in{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
. By (
5.3
), we have
f_{n}(\cdot,X)\rightarrow f(\cdot,X)
in
{\cal H}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
.
From this and (H2) we conclude

(5.9)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}E\int^{T}_{0}\langle Y^{n}_{t}-X_{t},f_{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t})-f(t,X_{t})\rangle\,dt\leq 0.

By Ito’s formula

(5.10)

2E\int^{T}_{0}\langle Y^{n}_{r},f_{n}(r,Y^{n}_{r})\rangle\,dr=E|Y^{n}_{0}|^{2}-E|\xi|^{2}+\int^{T}_{0}\,d[M^{n}]_{r}.

Now, we apply the very well known fact saying that in Hilbert spaces the norm generated by the inner product is weakly lower semicontinuous.
Thus, by (
5.8
)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}E|Y^{n}_{0}|^{2}\geq E|Y_{0}|^{2},

and by (
5.6
)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M^{n}]_{r}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}E|M^{n}_{T}|^{2}\,dr\geq E|M_{T}|^{2}\,dr=E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M]_{r}.

The above two inequalities when applied to (
5.10
) give

(5.11)

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}E\int^{T}_{0}\langle Y^{n}_{t},f(t,Y^{n}_{t})\rangle\,dt\geq E|Y_{0}|^{2}-E|\xi|^{2}+\int^{T}_{0}\,d[M]_{r}=E\int^{T}_{0}\langle Y_{t},U_{t}\rangle\,dt.

It follows from this and (
5.9
) that

(5.12)

\begin{split}&E\int^{T}_{0}\langle Y_{t}-X_{t},U_{t}-f(t,X_{t})\rangle\,dt\\
&\quad\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}E\int^{T}_{0}\langle Y^{n}_{t}-X_{t},f_{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t})-f(t,X_{t})\rangle\,dt\leq 0.\end{split}

Let us choose
X_{t}=Y_{t}-\varepsilon(U_{t}-f(t,Y_{t}))
,
t\in[0,T]
,
\varepsilon>0
. Dividing (
5.12
) by
\varepsilon
and letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain

E\int^{T}_{0}|U_{t}-f(t,Y_{t})|^{2}\,dt\leq 0.

This concludes the proof of Step 1.

Step 2.

In this step we dispense with (
5.1
) but we shall keep in force (
5.2
).
Let
a>0
be such that

e^{T/2}(|\xi|+\sqrt{T}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|f(t,0)|)\leq a.

Let
\theta_{a}
be a smooth function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
such that
0\leq\theta_{a}\leq 1
,
\theta_{a}(y)=1
for
|y|\leq a
and
\theta_{a}(y)=0
for
|y|\geq a+1
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
let us consider (cf. (H5))

\hat{f}_{n}(t,y)=\theta_{a}(y)\cdot(f(t,y)-f(t,0))\cdot\frac{n}{\psi_{a+1}(t)\vee n}+f(t,0).

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\hat{f}_{n}
satisfies (H1), (H4), (H5). Furthermore,
\hat{f}_{n}
also satisfies (H2) but with the positive constant
\mu
.
Indeed, let us take
t\in[0,T]
,
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
and assume that
|y|\geq a+1
and
|y^{\prime}|\geq a+1
. Then the inequality in (H2) is trivially satisfied with any
\mu\geq 0
since
\theta_{a}(y)=\theta_{a}(y^{\prime})=0
. Suppose that
|y|<a+1
. Since
f
satisfies (H2) with
\mu\leq 0
we have

\begin{split}\langle y-y^{\prime},\hat{f}_{n}(t,y)-\hat{f}_{n}(t,y^{\prime})\rangle\leq\frac{n(\theta_{a}(y)-\theta_{a}(y^{\prime}))}{\psi_{a+1}(t)\vee n}\langle y-y^{\prime},f(t,y)-f(t,0)\rangle.\end{split}

By the very definition of
\psi_{a+1}
, we have
|f(t,y)-f(t,0)|\leq\psi_{a+1}(t)
. Therefore

\begin{split}\langle y-y^{\prime},\hat{f}_{n}(t,y)-\hat{f}_{n}(t,y^{\prime})\rangle\leq n\|\nabla\theta_{a}\|_{\infty}|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}.\end{split}

Thus,
\hat{f}_{n}
satisfies (H2) with
\mu=n\|\nabla\theta_{a}\|_{\infty}
. Furthermore, since
\theta_{a}(y)|f(t,y)-f(t,0)|\leq\psi_{a+1}(t),\,t\in[0,T],\,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{l}
,
we also have

|\hat{f}_{n}(t,y)|\leq n+|f(t,0)|,\quad t\in[0,T],\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{l}.

Therefore, by Step 1, the problem BSDE
(\xi,\hat{f}_{n})
admits a unique solution
(Y^{n},M^{n})\in{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
.
By Ito’s formula

(5.13)

\begin{split}&e^{t}|Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}+\int^{T}_{t}e^{r}\,d[M^{n}]_{r}+\int^{T}_{t}e^{r}|Y^{n}_{r}|^{2}\,dr=e^{T}|\xi|^{2}+2\int^{T}_{t}e^{r}\langle Y^{n}_{r},\hat{f}_{n}(r,Y^{n}_{r})\rangle\,dr\\
&\quad-2\int^{T}_{t}e^{r}\langle Y^{n}_{r-},dM^{n}_{r}\rangle,\quad t\in[0,T].\end{split}

Since
f
satisfies (H2) with
\mu\leq 0
, we have

2\langle Y^{n}_{t},\hat{f}_{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t})\rangle\leq 2\langle Y^{n}_{t},f(t,0)\rangle\leq|Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|f(t,0)|^{2}.

Therefore, applying the conditional expectation to (
5.13
), we get

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}\leq e^{T}E(|\xi|^{2}+T\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|f(t,0)|^{2}|{\cal F}_{t})\leq a^{2}.

As a result, we obtain that in fact
(Y^{n},M^{n})
is a solution to BSDE
(\xi,f_{n})
, where

f_{n}(t,y)=(f(t,y)-f(t,0))\frac{n}{\psi_{a+1}(t)\vee n}+f(t,0),\quad t\in[0,T],\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{l}.

Note that
f_{n}
satisfies (H2) with
\mu\leq 0
.
Set
U=Y^{n+m}-Y^{n}
and
V=M^{n+m}-M^{n}
. By Ito’s formula

(5.14)

\begin{split}|U_{t}|^{2}+\int^{T}_{t}\,d[V]_{r}&=2\int^{T}_{t}\langle U_{r},f_{n+m}(r,Y^{n+m}_{r})-f_{n}(r,Y^{n}_{r})\rangle\,dr\\
&\quad-2\int^{T}_{t}\langle U_{r-},dV_{r}\rangle,\quad t\in[0,T].\end{split}

By (H2) (applied to
f_{n+m}
) and the fact that
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|U_{t}|\leq 2a
, we have

(5.15)

\langle U_{r},f_{n+m}(r,Y^{n+m}_{r})-f_{n}(r,Y^{n}_{r})\rangle\leq 2a|f_{n+m}(r,Y^{n}_{r})-f_{n}(r,Y^{n}_{r})|.

From this and (
5.14
), we conclude

(5.16)

E\int^{T}_{0}\,d[V]_{r}\leq 4aE\int^{T}_{0}|f_{n+m}(r,Y^{n}_{r})-f_{n}(r,Y^{n}_{r})|\,dr.

By (
5.14
), (
5.15
)

(5.17)

E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|U_{t}|^{2}\leq 4aE\int^{T}_{0}|f_{n+m}(r,Y^{n}_{r})-f_{n}(r,Y^{n}_{r})|\,dr+E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Bigg|2\int^{T}_{t}\langle U_{r-},dV_{r}\rangle\Bigg|.

By the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality and Young’s inequality there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

(5.18)

\begin{split}E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Bigg|2\int^{T}_{t}\langle U_{r-},dV_{r}\rangle\Bigg|&\leq C_{1}E\Bigg(\int^{T}_{0}|U_{r}|^{2}\,d[V]_{r}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{1}E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|U_{r}|\Bigg(\int^{T}_{0}\,d[V]_{r}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|U_{t}|^{2}+C^{2}_{1}E\int^{T}_{0}\,d[V]_{r}.\end{split}

By (
5.16
)–(
5.18
)

E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|U_{t}|^{2}+E\int^{T}_{0}\,d[V]_{r}\leq 8a(C^{2}_{1}+1)E\int^{T}_{0}|f_{n+m}(r,Y^{n}_{r})-f_{n}(r,Y^{n}_{r})|\,dr.

Observe that

|f_{n+m}(t,Y^{n}_{t})-f_{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t})|\leq 2\psi_{a}(t)\mathbf{1}_{\{\psi_{a}(t)>n\}}.

Therefore, using (H5), we conclude that
(Y^{n},M^{n})
is a Cauchy sequence in the space
{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
.
Thus, there exists
(Y,M)\in{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
such that

(5.19)

E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{n}_{t}-Y_{t}|^{2}+E\int^{T}_{0}\,d[M^{n}-M]_{r}\rightarrow 0,\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

By the definition of a solution to BSDE
(\xi,f_{n})
,

(5.20)

Y^{n}_{t}=\xi+\int^{T}_{t}f_{n}(r,Y^{n}_{r})\,dr-\int^{T}_{t}\,dM^{n}_{r},\quad t\in[0,T].

By (
5.19
), up to subsequence,
Y^{n}_{t}(\omega)\rightarrow Y_{t}(\omega)
for
\ell^{1}\otimes P
-a.e.
(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
.
Thus, by (H4),
f_{n}(\cdot,Y^{n})\rightarrow f(\cdot,Y),\,\ell^{1}\otimes P
-a.e.
Furthermore, since
|Y^{n}_{t}|\leq a,\,t\in[0,T],\,n\geq 1
, we have that for any
t\in[0,T]
,

(5.21)

|f_{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t})|\leq\psi_{a}(t)+|f(t,0)|,\quad n\geq 1.

Therefore, from (H5), and the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we infer that

(5.22)

\sup_{t\leq T}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}f_{n}(r,Y^{n}_{r})\,dr-\int_{t}^{T}f(r,Y_{r})\,dr\Big|\to 0,\quad n\to\infty.

Thanks to this convergence and (
5.19
), we may pass to the limit in (
5.20
) getting that
(Y,M)
is a solution to BSDE
(\xi,f)
.

Step 3.

Finally, we shall dispense with (
5.2
). For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
set

\xi_{n}:=\Pi_{n}(\xi),\quad f_{n}(t,y):=f(t,y)-f(t,0)+\Pi_{n}(f(t,0)),

where
\Pi_{n}(y)=y\min(n,|y|)|y|^{-1}
.
Obviously,

(5.23)

E|\xi-\xi_{n}|^{2}\rightarrow 0,\quad E\Bigg(\int^{T}_{0}|f(t,0)-f_{n}(t,0)|\,dt\Bigg)^{2}\rightarrow 0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. We see that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the pair
(\xi_{n},f_{n})
satisfies the assumptions of Step 2. Therefore, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a unique solution
(Y^{n},M^{n})\in{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
to BSDE
(\xi_{n},f_{n})
.
Let

F(r,y):=f_{n}(r,y+Y^{m}_{r})-f_{m}(r,Y^{m}_{r}),\quad r\in[0,T],\,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{l}.

Observe that
(Y^{n}-Y^{m},M^{n}-M^{m})
is a solution to BSDE
(\xi_{n}-\xi_{m},F)
, and data
\xi_{n}-\xi_{m},F
satisfy (H1)–(H2) (in place of
\xi
and
f
, respectively).
Thus, by Proposition
4.2

(5.24)

\begin{split}E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{n}_{t}-Y^{m}_{t}|^{2}&+E\int^{T}_{0}\,d[M^{n}-M^{m}]_{r}\leq C\Big(E|\xi^{n}-\xi^{m}|^{2}+E\big(\int_{0}^{T}|F(r,0)|\,dr\big)^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad=C\Bigg(E|\xi_{n}-\xi_{m}|^{2}+\Big(\int^{T}_{0}|f_{n}(r,0)-f_{m}(r,0)|\,dr\Big)^{2}\Bigg).\end{split}

From this and (
5.23
), we conclude that

E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{n}_{t}-Y^{m}_{t}|^{2}+E\int^{T}_{0}\,d[M^{n}-M^{m}]_{r}\rightarrow 0,\quad n,m\rightarrow\infty.

This means that
(Y^{n},M^{n})
is a Cauchy sequence in
{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
.
Thus, there exists
(Y,M)\in{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
such that

(5.25)

E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{n}_{t}-Y_{t}|^{2}+E\int^{T}_{0}\,d[M^{n}-M]_{r}\rightarrow 0,\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

By the definition of a solution to BSDE
(\xi_{n},f_{n})
,

(5.26)

Y^{n}_{t}=\xi_{n}+\int^{T}_{t}f_{n}(r,Y^{n}_{r})\,dr-\int^{T}_{t}\,dM^{n}_{r},\quad t\in[0,T].

The proof shall be concluded by passing to the limit in (
5.26
). The only term that requires care is the integral of
f_{n}
.
By (
5.25
), there exists a subsequence (not relabelled) such that

\sup_{t\leq T}|Y^{n}_{t}-Y_{t}|\rightarrow 0\quad a.s.

Therefore,
X:=\sup_{n\geq 1}\sup_{t\leq T}|Y^{n}_{t}|
is finite a.s. Observe that for any
a>0
, (
5.21
) holds on the set
\{X\leq a\}
.
Thus repeating the reasoning following (
5.20
)
we get that (
5.22
) holds on the set
\{X\leq a\}
. Since
a>0
was arbitrary, and
X
is finite a.s., we easily deduce that the convergence (
5.22
)
holds in probability
P
. Using this and (
5.25
), and letting
n\rightarrow\infty
in (
5.26
), we get that
(Y,M)
is a solution to BSDE
(\xi,f)
.
∎

6.
Proof of the main result

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume that
(H1)–(H5)
are satisfied. Let
H^{1},H^{2}\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
and
(Y^{i},M^{i})\in{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
be a solution to BSDE
(\xi,f(\cdot,\cdot,H^{i}))
,

i=1,2.
Let
0\leq a<b\leq T
and
(b-a)\leq\frac{1}{2(1+16\lambda+\mu^{+})}
. Then there exists
\beta\geq 1
depending only on
\lambda,\mu^{+},(b-a)
such that

(6.1)

\sup_{a\leq t\leq b}E|Y^{1}_{t}-Y^{2}_{t}|^{2}+E\int_{a}^{b}\,d[M^{1}-M^{2}]_{r}\leq\beta E|Y^{1}_{b}-Y^{2}_{b}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}E\int_{a}^{T}\,d[H^{1}-H^{2}]_{r}.

Proof.

By Itô’s formula

(6.2)

\begin{split}&|Y^{1}_{t}-Y^{2}_{t}|^{2}+\int^{b}_{t}\,d[M^{1}-M^{2}]_{r}=|Y^{1}_{b}-Y^{2}_{b}|^{2}\\
&\quad+2\int^{b}_{t}\langle Y^{1}_{r}-Y^{2}_{r},f(r,Y^{1}_{r},H^{1})-f(r,Y^{2}_{r},H^{2})\rangle\,dr\\
&\quad-2\int^{b}_{t}\langle Y^{1}_{r-}-Y^{2}_{r-},d(M^{1}_{r}-M^{2}_{r})\rangle,\quad t\in[a,b].\end{split}

By (H2) for any
\nu\geq 0
,

(6.3)

\begin{split}&\langle Y^{1}_{r}-Y^{2}_{r},f(r,Y^{1}_{r},H^{1})-f(r,Y^{2}_{r},H^{2})\rangle\\
&\quad\leq|Y^{1}_{r}-Y^{2}_{r}||f(r,Y^{1}_{r},H^{1})-f(r,Y^{1}_{r},H^{2})|+\mu^{+}|Y^{1}_{r}-Y^{2}_{r}|^{2}\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+\nu)|Y^{1}_{r}-Y^{2}_{r}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2(1+\nu)}|f(r,Y^{1}_{r},H^{1})-f(r,Y^{1}_{r},H^{2})|^{2}+\mu^{+}|Y^{1}_{r}-Y^{2}_{r}|^{2}.\end{split}

Due to the assumptions made on
(Y^{i},M^{i})
it is routine to verify that the process
\int^{\cdot}_{0}(Y^{1}_{r-}-Y^{2}_{r-})\,d(M^{1}_{r}-M^{2}_{r})
is a martingale. Therefore, applying the expectation to (
6.2
) and (
6.3
), and combining these two inequalities, we get by (H3)

(6.4)

\begin{split}&E|Y^{1}_{t}-Y^{2}_{t}|^{2}+E\int^{b}_{t}\,d[M^{1}-M^{2}]_{r}\leq E|Y^{1}_{b}-Y^{2}_{b}|^{2}\\
&\quad+\beta_{\nu}E\int^{b}_{t}|Y^{1}_{r}-Y^{2}_{r}|^{2}\,dr+\frac{\lambda}{1+\nu}E\int^{T}_{a}d[H^{1}-H^{2}]_{r},\end{split}

where
\beta_{\nu}:=(1+\nu+2\mu^{+})
.
By Gronwall’s lemma

(6.5)

E|Y^{1}_{t}-Y^{2}_{t}|^{2}\leq e^{\beta_{\nu}(b-a)}\Big(E|Y^{1}_{b}-Y^{2}_{b}|^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{1+\nu}E\int^{T}_{a}d[H^{1}-H^{2}]_{r}\Big).

This combined with (
6.4
) yields

\displaystyle E|Y^{1}_{t}-Y^{2}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle+E\int^{b}_{t}\,d[M^{1}-M^{2}]_{r}\leq E|Y^{1}_{b}-Y^{2}_{b}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\beta_{\nu}(b-a)e^{\beta_{\nu}(b-a)}\Big(E|Y^{1}_{b}-Y^{2}_{b}|^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{1+\nu}E\int^{T}_{a}d[H^{1}-H^{2}]_{r}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\lambda}{1+\nu}E\int^{T}_{a}d[H^{1}-H^{2}]_{r}

\displaystyle=(1+\beta_{\nu}(b-a)e^{\beta_{\nu}(b-a)})E|Y^{1}_{b}-Y^{2}_{b}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda}{1+\nu}(1+\beta_{\nu}(b-a)e^{\beta_{\nu}(b-a)})E\int^{T}_{a}d[H^{1}-H^{2}]_{r}.

Set
\nu:=16\lambda
, and
\beta:=1+\beta_{\nu}(b-a)e^{\beta_{\nu}(b-a)}
.
Then we get the desired inequality.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

Observe that the last integral on the right-hand side of (
6.1
) is made over
[a,T]
and not over
[a,b]
.
This is the best we can get due to the assumption (H3).

Theorem 6.3
.

Assume that
(H1)–(H5)
are satisfied. Then there exists a solution
(Y,M)
to
BSDE
(\xi,f)
such that
Y\in\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
.

Proof.

Set
(Y^{0},M^{0})=(0,0)
. By Proposition
5.1
for each
n\geq 1
there exists a unique solution
(Y^{n},M^{n})\in{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
to BSDE
(\xi,f(\cdot,\cdot,M^{n-1}))
.
Consider a partition of the interval
[0,T]
:
0=a_{1}<a_{2}<\dots<a_{p-1}<a_{p}=T
, such that

(a_{i+1}-a_{i})\leq\frac{1}{2(1+16\lambda+\mu^{+})},\quad i=1,\dots,p-1.

Set

b_{i}:=E\int_{a_{i-1}}^{a_{i}}\,d[M^{2}-M^{1}]_{r},\quad i=1,\dots,p,\qquad b:=\sum_{i=1}^{p}b_{i}.

By Lemma
6.1
for each
i\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{a_{i}\leq t\leq a_{i+1}}E|Y^{n+1}_{t}-Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle+E\int_{a_{i}}^{a_{i+1}}\,d[M^{n+1}-M^{n}]_{r}

(6.6)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\beta E|Y^{n+1}_{a_{i+1}}-Y^{n}_{a_{i+1}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}E\int_{a_{i}}^{T}\,d[M^{n}-M^{n-1}]_{r}.

In particular for any
n\geq 2
,

(6.7)

\sup_{a_{p-1}\leq t\leq T}E|Y^{n+1}_{t}-Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}+E\int_{a_{p-1}}^{T}\,d[M^{n+1}-M^{n}]_{r}\leq\frac{1}{4}E\int_{a_{p-1}}^{T}\,d[M^{n}-M^{n-1}]_{r}.

From this we conclude that

(6.8)

\sup_{a_{p-1}\leq t\leq T}E|Y^{n+1}_{t}-Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}+E\int_{a_{p-1}}^{T}\,d[M^{n+1}-M^{n}]_{r}\leq(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b_{p}\leq(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b,\quad n\geq 2.

We let

I^{M}_{n}(k)=E\int_{a_{k-1}}^{a_{k}}\,d[M^{n}-M^{n-1}]_{r},\quad I^{Y}_{n}(k)=\sup_{a_{k-1}\leq t\leq a_{k}}E|Y^{n}_{t}-Y^{n-1}_{t}|,

and

I_{n}(k)=I^{M}_{n}(k)+I^{Y}_{n}(k).

Under this notation (
6.8
) becomes

(6.9)

I_{n+1}(p)\leq(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b,\quad n\geq 2.

Observe also that
b_{i}=I^{M}_{2}(i),\,i=1,\dots,p
. By Lemma
6.1
and (
6.9
)

\displaystyle I_{n+1}(p-1)

\displaystyle\leq\beta E|Y^{n+1}_{a_{p-1}}-Y^{n}_{a_{p-1}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}E\int_{a_{p-2}}^{T}\,d[M^{n}-M^{n-1}]_{r}

\displaystyle\leq\beta\sup_{a_{p-1}\leq t\leq T}E|Y^{n+1}_{t}-Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}E\int_{a_{p-1}}^{T}\,d[M^{n}-M^{n-1}]_{r}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}E\int_{a_{p-2}}^{a_{p-1}}\,d[M^{n}-M^{n-1}]_{r}

(6.10)

\displaystyle\leq(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta)+\frac{1}{4}E\int_{a_{p-2}}^{a_{p-1}}\,d[M^{n}-M^{n-1}]_{r},\quad n\geq 2.

Thus,

I_{n+1}(p-1)\leq(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta)+\frac{1}{4}I^{M}_{n}(p-1),\quad n\geq 2.

Consequently,

\displaystyle I_{n+1}(p-1)

\displaystyle\leq(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta)+\frac{1}{4}I^{M}_{n}(p-1)\leq(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta)+\frac{1}{4}I_{n}(p-1)

\displaystyle\leq(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta)+\frac{1}{4}\Big((\frac{1}{4})^{n-2}b(1+\beta)+\frac{1}{4}I^{M}_{n-1}(p-1)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta)+\frac{1}{4^{2}}I^{M}_{n-1}(p-1).

Continuing in this fashion, we get that for each
n\geq 2
,

(6.11)

\displaystyle I_{n+1}(p-1)\leq(n-1)(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(\beta+1)+(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b_{p-1}\leq n(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta).

For the sake of clarity of the reasoning, we shall make one more step.
By Lemma
6.1
,

\displaystyle I_{n+1}(p-2)

\displaystyle\leq\beta E|Y^{n+1}_{a_{p-2}}-Y^{n}_{a_{p-2}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}E\int_{a_{p-3}}^{T}\,d[M^{n}-M^{n-1}]_{r}

(6.12)

\displaystyle\leq\beta I^{Y}_{n+1}(p-1)+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=p-2}^{p}I^{M}_{n}(i),\quad n\geq 2.

By (
6.9
), (
6.11
) for each
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle I_{n+1}(p-2)\leq n(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta)\beta+(n-1)(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta)+(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b+\frac{1}{4}I^{M}_{n}(p-2).

Hence

I_{n+1}(p-2)\leq 2n(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}I^{M}_{n}(p-2),\quad n\geq 2.

Therefore

\displaystyle I_{n+1}(p-2)

\displaystyle\leq 2n(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\Big(2(n-1)(\frac{1}{4})^{n-2}b(1+\beta)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}I^{M}_{n-1}(p-2)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot(2n)(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta)^{2}+\frac{1}{4^{2}}I^{M}_{n-1}(p-2).

Continuing in this fashion we get that for each
n\geq 2
,

(6.13)

\displaystyle I_{n+1}(p-2)\leq 2n^{2}(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta)^{2}.

Proceeding as above for
I_{n+1}(p-i)
with
i=3,\dots,p-1
, we get

(6.14)

I_{n+1}(k)\leq pn^{p-k}(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta)^{p-k},\quad k=1,\dots,p,\quad n\geq 2.

Thus,

(6.15)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}E|Y^{n+1}_{t}-Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}+E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M^{n+1}-M^{n}]_{r}\leq p^{2}n^{p}(\frac{1}{4})^{n-1}b(1+\beta)^{p}=:q^{2}_{n}.

Clearly
\sum_{n\geq 1}q_{n}
is convergent. For
Y\in B_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega,P;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}))
and
M\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})

we set

\|(Y,M)\|:=\sqrt{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}E|Y_{t}|^{2}+E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M]_{r}}.

It is routine to verify that
(B_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega,P;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}))\times{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}),\|\cdot\|)

is a Banach space. Observe that for
n<m
,

(6.16)

\|(Y^{m},M^{m})-(Y^{n},M^{n})\|\leq\sum_{i=n}^{m-1}q_{i}\leq\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}q_{i}.

Thus,
(Y^{n},M^{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in
B_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega,P;{\mathbb{R}}^{l}))\times{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
. By Itô’s formula

\begin{split}|Y^{m}_{t}-Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}&+\int_{t}^{T}\,d[M^{m}-M^{n}]_{r}\\
&=2\int_{t}^{T}\langle f(r,Y^{m}_{r},M^{m-1})-f(r,Y^{n}_{r},M^{n-1}),Y^{m}_{r}-Y^{n}_{r}\rangle\,dr\\
&-2\int_{t}^{T}\langle Y^{m}_{r-}-Y^{n}_{r-},d(M^{m}_{r}-M^{n}_{r})\rangle,\quad t\in[0,T].\end{split}

By (H2)

\displaystyle\langle f(r,Y^{m}_{r},M^{m-1})

\displaystyle-f(r,Y^{n}_{r},M^{n-1}),Y^{m}_{r}-Y^{n}_{r}\rangle

(6.17)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\mu^{+})|Y^{m}_{r}-Y^{n}_{r}|^{2}+|f(r,Y^{m}_{r},M^{m-1})-f(r,Y^{m}_{r},M^{n-1})|^{2}.

This combined with the previous inequality yields

\begin{split}E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{m}_{t}-Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}&+E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M^{m}-M^{n}]_{r}\\
&\quad\leq 4(1+\mu^{+})E\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{m}_{r}-Y^{n}_{r}|^{2}\,dr\\
&\quad+4E\int_{0}^{T}|f(r,Y^{m}_{r},M^{m-1})-f(r,Y^{m}_{r},M^{n-1})|^{2}\,dr\\
&\quad+4E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}(Y^{m}_{r-}-Y^{n}_{r-})\,d(M^{m}_{r}-M^{n}_{r})\Big|.\end{split}

By (H3) and the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality there exists
C>0
such that

\begin{split}E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{m}_{t}&-Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}+E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M^{m}-M^{n}]_{r}\\
&\leq 4(1+\mu^{+})E\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{m}_{r}-Y^{n}_{r}|^{2}\,dr+4\lambda E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M^{m-1}-M^{n-1}]_{r}\\
&+4CE\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{m}_{r-}-Y^{n}_{r-}|^{2}\,d[M^{m}_{r}-M^{n}_{r}]\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq 4(1+\mu^{+})E\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{m}_{r}-Y^{n}_{r}|^{2}\,dr+4\lambda E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M^{m-1}-M^{n-1}]_{r}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{m}_{t}-Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}+8C^{2}E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M^{m}-M^{n}]_{r}.\end{split}

Hence,

\begin{split}E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{m}_{t}&-Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}+E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M^{m}-M^{n}]_{r}\\
&\leq 8(1+\mu^{+})E\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{m}_{r}-Y^{n}_{r}|^{2}\,dr+8\lambda E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M^{m-1}-M^{n-1}]_{r}\\
&+16C^{2}E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M^{m}-M^{n}]_{r}\\
&\leq(8\mu^{+}T+8T+16C^{2})\|(Y^{m},M^{m})-(Y^{n},M^{n})\|^{2}\\
&+8\lambda\|(Y^{m-1},M^{m-1})-(Y^{n-1},M^{n-1})\|^{2}.\end{split}

From this and (
6.16
) we conclude that
(Y^{n},M^{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in the space
{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
.
Therefore, there exists a pair of càdlàg processes
(Y,M)\in{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
such that

(6.18)

E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{n}_{t}-Y_{t}|^{2}+E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M^{n}-M]_{r}\rightarrow 0.

By the definition of a solution to BSDE
(\xi,f(\cdot,\cdot,M^{n-1}))
,

(6.19)

Y^{n}_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(r,Y^{n}_{r},M^{n-1})\,dr-\int_{t}^{T}\,dM^{n}_{r},\quad t\in[0,T]

The final step in the proof thus shall be passing to the limit in (
6.19
). For this we have to cope with the nonlinear term of the equation.
Clearly,

\displaystyle|f(r,Y^{n}_{r},M^{n-1})-f(r,Y_{r},M)|

\displaystyle\leq|f(r,Y^{n}_{r},M)-f(r,Y^{n}_{r},M^{n-1})|

(6.20)

\displaystyle\quad+|f(r,Y^{n}_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},M)|.

By (H3) and (
6.18
)

(6.21)

\begin{split}E\int_{0}^{T}|f(r,Y^{n}_{r},M)-f(r,Y^{n}_{r},M^{n-1})|\,dr\leq\sqrt{\lambda}\sqrt{T}\Big(E\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M^{n-1}-M]_{r}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow 0.\end{split}

Now we shall focus on the second term on the right-hand side of (
6
). Let
(n_{k})
be a subsequence of
(n)
.
Then by (
6.18
) there exists a further subsequence
(n_{k_{l}})
such that

(6.22)

\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{n_{k_{l}}}_{t}-Y_{t}|<\infty.

Set
l:=n_{k_{l}}
. For
\nu>0
set

A_{\nu}:=\{\omega\in\Omega:\exists_{t\in[0,T]}\,\,\exists_{l\geq 1}\,\,|Y^{l}_{t}(\omega)|\geq\nu\}.

We have

(6.23)

\begin{split}P(\int_{0}^{T}|f(r,Y^{l}_{r},M)&-f(r,Y_{r},M)|\,dr>\varepsilon)\leq P(A_{\nu})\\
&+P(\mathbf{1}_{A^{c}_{\nu}}\int_{0}^{T}|f(r,Y^{l}_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},M)|\,dr>\varepsilon).\end{split}

By (
6.22
)

(6.24)

\begin{split}P(A_{\nu})&=P(\exists_{l\geq 1}\,\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{l}_{t}|\geq\nu)\\
&\leq P(\exists_{l\geq 1}\,\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{l}_{t}-Y_{t}|\geq\nu/2)+P(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y_{t}|\geq\nu/2)\\
&\leq\frac{2}{\nu}E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y_{t}|+\frac{2}{\nu}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{l}_{t}-Y_{t}|\rightarrow 0,\quad\nu\rightarrow\infty.\end{split}

By (
6.22
) and (H4)

(6.25)

|f(r,Y^{l}_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},M)|\rightarrow 0,\quad l\rightarrow\infty,\quad\ell^{1}\otimes P\mbox{-a.e.}

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{A^{c}_{\nu}}|f(r,Y^{l}_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},M)|

\displaystyle\leq|f(r,Y_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},0)|+|f(r,Y^{l}_{r},M)-f(r,Y^{l}_{r},0)|

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbf{1}_{A^{c}_{\nu}}|f(r,Y^{l}_{r},0)|+\mathbf{1}_{A^{c}_{\nu}}|f(r,Y_{r},0)|

\displaystyle\leq|f(r,Y_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},0)|+|f(r,Y^{l}_{r},M)-f(r,Y^{l}_{r},0)|

\displaystyle\quad+2\sup_{|y|\leq\nu}|f(r,y,0)|=:g^{\nu}_{l}(r).

By (H3) and (H5), the family
(g^{\nu}_{l})_{l\geq 1}
is uniformly integrable with respect to the measure
\ell^{1}\otimes P
.
From this, (
6.25
), and the Vitali convergence theorem,
we conclude that the second term on the right-hand side of (
6.23
) goes to zero as
l\rightarrow\infty
.
Therefore, letting
l\rightarrow\infty
in (
6.23
) we get

\limsup_{l\rightarrow\infty}P(\int_{0}^{T}|f(r,Y^{l}_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},M)|\,dr>\varepsilon)\leq P(A_{\nu}).

Then, letting
\nu\rightarrow\infty
and using (
6.24
) we obtain

\int_{0}^{T}|f(r,Y^{l}_{r},M)-f(r,Y_{r},M)|\,dr\rightarrow 0,\quad l\rightarrow\infty

in probability
P
. Since
(n_{k})
was an arbitrary subsequence of
(n)
, we get that the above convergence holds with

l
replaced by
n
. This combined with (
6
), (
6.21
) implies

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}f(r,Y^{n}_{r},M^{n-1})-\int_{t}^{T}f(r,Y_{r},M)\,dr\Big|\rightarrow 0,\quad l\rightarrow\infty

in probability
P
. By using this convergence and (
6.18
), we let
n\rightarrow\infty
in (
6.19
)
to get

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(r,Y_{r},M)\,dr-\int_{t}^{T}\,dM_{r},\quad t\in[0,T].

Thus,
(Y,M)
is a solution to BSDE
(\xi,f)
.
∎

7.
Lipschitz type condition on the driver

The purpose of this section is to demonstrate that the method presented in Section
6

also works for generators of the form

f:[0,T]\times\Omega\times{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{l}.

As usual we assume that for any
(Y,M)\in{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
process
f(\cdot,Y,M)
is progressively measurable.

Definition 7.1
.

We say that a pair
(Y,M)\in{\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})\times\mathcal{M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})}
is a solution of BSDE(
\xi,f
) if

(a)

Y
is càdlàg,

(b)

\int^{T}_{0}|f(r,Y,M)|\,dr<+\infty
,

(c)

Y_{t}=\xi+\int^{T}_{t}f(r,Y,M)\,dr-\int^{T}_{t}\,dM_{r},\quad t\in[0,T].

As to the regularity of
f
with respect to
Y
-variable, we consider an analogue
of condition (H3). We formulate it as a one condition which expresses regularity of
f
with respect to
Y
and
M
-variable.

(A)

There exists
L>0
such that for any
t\in[0,T]
,
M,M^{\prime}\in{\cal M}^{2}_{0}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})
,
Y,Y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}}^{l})

E\int_{t}^{T}|f(r,Y,M)-f(r,Y^{\prime},M^{\prime})|^{2}\,dr\leq L(E\int_{t}^{T}\,d[M-M^{\prime}]_{r}+E\int_{t}^{T}|Y_{r}-Y^{\prime}_{r}|^{2}\,dr).

This is thus a type of Lipschitz continuity, so in some sense stronger than (H2), however it permits
f(t,Y,M)

to depend not only on the future of the process
M
but also on the future of the process
Y
.
This generalization allows us in particular to consider in place of
Y_{t}
in Examples
3.1
–
3.8
,
Y_{t+\delta(t)}

or
\mathcal{L}(Y_{t})
. An existence result for (
1.9
) with
f
satisfying condition of type (A) was provided in
[
8
]
.
The authors, however, assumed in
[
8
]
that
f
is of the special form (
1.2
) with
h
defined in Example
3.4
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Assume that (H1) and (A) are in force. Then there exists a unique solution to BSDE
(\xi,f)
.

Proof.

It is enough to repeat the arguments of Section
6
with small modifications.
The proof of Lemma
6.1
may be repeated step by step with one exception that
instead of (
6.3
) one should apply

(7.1)

\begin{split}&E\int_{t}^{T}\langle Y^{1}_{r}-Y^{2}_{r},f(r,Y^{1},H^{1})-f(r,Y^{2},H^{2})\rangle\,dr\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+\nu+L)E\int_{t}^{T}|Y^{1}_{r}-Y^{2}_{r}|^{2}\,dr+\frac{L}{2(1+\nu)}E\int_{t}^{T}\,d[H^{1}-H^{2}]_{r},\end{split}

which follows directly from (A). The whole proof of Theorem
6.3
up to (
6.19
)
may be repeated under assumptions (H1), (A) with one exception that instead of (
6
)
one should employ the following inequality

\displaystyle E\int_{0}^{T}|\langle f(r,Y^{m},M^{m-1})

\displaystyle-f(r,Y^{n},M^{n-1}),Y^{m}_{r}-Y^{n}_{r}\rangle|\,dr

(7.2)

\displaystyle\leq(1+L)E\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{m}_{r}-Y^{n}_{r}|^{2}\,dr+LE\int_{0}^{T}\,d[M^{m-1}-M^{n-1}]_{r},

which once again follows directly from (A). Here note that the existence of
(Y^{n},M^{n})

follows from
[
8
, Proposition 3.7]
. Now we can easily pass to the limit in (
6.19
)
by using condition (A) again.
∎

Koszul-Tate resolutions and decorated trees
Aliaksandr Hancharuk, Camille Laurent-Gengoux, Thomas Strobl

Abstract
. Given a commutative algebra 𝒪 \mathcal{O} , a proper ideal ℐ \mathcal{I} , and a resolution of 𝒪 / ℐ \mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I} by projective 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -modules, we construct an explicit Koszul-Tate resolution. We call it the arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution since it is indexed by decorated trees. When the 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -module resolution has finite length, only finitely many operations are needed in our constructions—this is to be compared with the classical Tate algorithm, which requires infinitely many such computations if ℐ \mathcal{I} is not a complete intersection. As a by-product of our construction, the initial projective 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -module resolution becomes equipped with an explicit A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebra.

Construction 1.1
.

(a)

We choose generators

d\,\colon\,\sum_{j\in J}f_{j}[j]\mapsto\sum_{j\in J}f_{j}E_{j}.

Moreover, there are wide classes of algebras

A classical example of a Syzygy algebra is the algebra

N

\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. Also, germs at

\mathbb{R}^{N}

[

]
.

Koszul-Tate resolutions can be constructed by the
Tate algorithm
, that we now briefly describe.

Construction 1.2
.

Tate algorithm:

(a)

The module
\mathcal{E}_{1}
can be constructed exactly as the module
\mathfrak{M}_{1}
in Construction
1.1
:
\mathcal{E}_{1}:=\mathfrak{M}_{1}
is a free module equipped with an
\mathcal{O}
-linear map
d_{1}:\mathfrak{M}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}
such that
d_{1}(\mathfrak{M}_{1})=\mathcal{I}
.

(b)

The differential
d_{1}\colon{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to\mathcal{O}
extends to
S({\mathcal{E}}_{1})
as a graded derivation of degree
-1
squaring to zero that we denote by
\delta_{1}
. By construction,
H_{0}(S({\mathcal{E}}_{1}),\delta_{1})=\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
. Of course, in general
H_{1}(S({\mathcal{E}}_{1}),\delta_{1})\neq 0
but it is an
\mathcal{O}
-module. Hence, the free
\mathcal{O}
-module
{{\mathcal{E}}}_{2}
generated by generators of
H_{1}(S({\mathcal{E}}_{1}),\delta_{1})
comes equipped with an
\mathcal{O}
-linear map

whose image is
H_{1}(S({\mathcal{E}}_{1}),\delta_{1})
. The maps

then extend to a degree

\delta_{2}
of

that squares to zero. Now, by construction,

H_{0}(S({\mathcal{E}}_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{2}),\delta_{2})=\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}\hbox{ and }H_{1}(S({\mathcal{E}}_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{2}),\delta_{2})=0.

(c)

The procedure is repeated in order to construct a sequence

of free
\mathcal{O}
-modules and a sequence

of
\mathcal{O}
-linear maps such that for every

the image of

generates

. Here
\delta_{k-1}

is

It is easily proven by recursion that for all

extend to a degree

\delta_{k}^{2}=0
and

H_{0}\left(S({\mathcal{E}}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{k}),\delta_{k}\right)=\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}\hbox{ and }H_{i}\left(S({\mathcal{E}}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{k}),\delta_{k}\right)=0\hbox{ for all $i\leq k$ .}

This implies that the pair

(S({\mathcal{E}}_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\oplus\cdots),\delta),

with
\delta
being a degree

\mathcal{E}_{i}
is
d_{i}
for all

, is a Koszul-Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
.

Although both algorithms seem to be similar at first sight, the Tate algorithm used to produce a free Koszul-Tate resolution is in general significantly more complicated than the algorithm constructing an
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution. Generically, even for a Syzygy algebra
\mathcal{O}
, there exist infinitely many
\mathcal{E}_{k}
appearing in the Tate algorithm. This means that one has to compute infinitely many homologies and needs to make equally many choices of generating sets for those homologies. Let us remark in parentheses that there is also the following more subtle difference: If, when computing an
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution, one finds, for example,
\mathfrak{M}_{16}=0
, then one can stop the computation there. In contrast, for the Tate algorithm, if one arrives at the possible choice
\mathcal{E}_{16}=0
, one still can be forced to put
\mathcal{E}_{17}\neq 0
; so there is no obvious manner to say ”we can stop there”.

In conclusion,
even for Syzygy algebras whose
\mathcal{O}
-module resolutions can be found by making finitely many operations, the Tate algorithm obliges one to compute infinitely many homologies
and to do infinitely many choices.
As a result, for most ideals
\mathcal{I}
of an algebra
\mathcal{O}
, no Koszul-Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
is explicitly known, although it is possible to find an explicit
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution.

The purpose of the present article is mainly to answer the following natural question:

Question 1.3
.

Is there a way to construct a Koszul-Tate resolution by performing only finitely many homology computations (and having therefore only finitely many choices to take)?

We will provide a positive answer to this question in the case when an
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution of finite length and of finite ranks exists, so, e.g., always if
\mathcal{O}
is a Syzygy algebra.

In addition, with our construction, there are not only finitely many computations and choices to be made, but we also know when we can stop the construction: at the
N
-th step where
N
is the length of
(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)
.

We now explain how to answer Question
1.3
. We start with given a projective
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution
(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)
as in (
1
). Then we consider the free
\mathcal{O}
-module
\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]
generated by rooted trees with leaves decorated by homogeneous elements of
\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}
, e.g. elements of the form:

with

of fixed degree for

.
Such trees have a natural
\mathcal{O}
-module structure. It becomes graded by declaring the degree of a decorated tree to be the sum of the degrees of the decorations in
\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}
plus the number of vertices.
The main point of the present article is to equip the graded symmetric algebra
S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}])
of
\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]
, thought of as a forest of such trees,
with an explicit differential

\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}

\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
(attached to the free or projective
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution

1.3

Moreover, this differential has several components, which are of the following types:

(1)

The first component consists of acting on the decorations attached to the leaves, i.e. on the corresponding elements in
\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}
. Here
\delta
acts in most cases simply as
d
—up to appropriate signs determined from the tree.

(2)

The second component operates on the trees only: Either by removing the root, resulting in a contribution in terms of a forest, or by
removing inner edges (adding appropriate signs).

(3)

The last and the most intriguing component is the following one: For a tree
t
with
n
leaves and
k
inner vertices it is determined by an
n
-linear map
\psi_{t}\colon\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}\to\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}
of degree
k
.

We will call the maps
\psi_{t}
, indexed by trees
t
,
arborescent operations
. Their construction is the only one which requires choices. Moreover and most importantly, for degree reasons, if the resolution
(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)
has finite length, there exist only finitely many non-trivial arborescent operations, resulting in only a finite number of computations and choices.

It is a natural question to ask what is the structure of these ”mysterious” arborescent operations.
A complete answer probably involves operads, but we have an answer which is sufficient to explain their importance. Since any Koszul-Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
is in particular a projective
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
, and since any two projective
\mathcal{O}
-module resolutions of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
are homotopy equivalent,

(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)
is homotopy equivalent to any Koszul-Tate resolution.
As a consequence, the homotopy transfer theorem
[

]
applies
and yields an
A_{\infty}
-algebra structure on the complex

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}}

Koszul-Tate resolutions play an essential role in the Batalin-(Fradkin)-Vilkoviskiy formalism: It was created in the physics literature in
[
BV77
,
BF83
]
to handle the omni-present gauge theories, then formalized, e.g., in
[
HT92
,
Sta97
,
FKS14
,
ML17
]
, where Koszul-Tate resolutions are often taken as a given starting point.
Koszul-Tate resolutions are also used for PDE’s with symmetries, see, e.g.,
[
GK19
]
.
Finally, they also appear, under different names, in deformation theory of algebraic varieties, see, e.g.,
[
FFP22
]
.

In the literature there exists an explicit construction of an algebra resolution by Chuang and King
[

]
, which even works for non-commutative
\mathcal{O}
.
However,
their algebra is not a graded symmetric algebra—and thus not a Koszul-Tate resolution.

In this article, we provide answers to several side questions which are of interest in their own: For instance, we define the notion of a

which has the following property:
Its homology counts the minimal number of generators needed in a Koszul-Tate resolution at each degree. While arborescent Koszul-Tate resolutions are not minimal, they are very useful in determining lower bounds to the number of generators of any Koszul-Tate resolution.

Convention 1.6

Let us define what we call an
oriented planar tree
t\in{\mathrm{Tree}}
.
It is a connected finite planar graph with oriented edges. Its vertices

are of three possible types: internal vertices, denoted by
{\mathrm{InnVer}}(t)
, have one in-going and at least two out-going edges. The external vertices have either no in-going edge, in which case it is called the
root
of
t
, or no out-going edge, in which case this vertex is called a
leaf
of
t

A

of
A

A

descendants
of
A

We also assume that the leaves are totally ordered. This induces a total order on the outgoing edges of each inner vertex as well as of the root.
We say that a tree is

A

left

right

The

We now present the main theorem of the article.

Theorem 2.28
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal in a commutative algebra
\mathcal{O}
.
For any free or projective
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{M}_{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{M}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}\,\,,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}

(1)

there exist arborescent operations
\psi\colon t\mapsto\psi_{t}
such that the derivation
\delta_{\psi}

described in Section
2.2.2

squares to zero.

(2)

For any such a choice of
\psi
, the pair
(S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]),\delta_{\psi})
is a Koszul-Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

The first item was established in Corollary
2.25
.
In order to prove the second item, it suffices to check that for any such a choice
\psi
, the homology of
\delta_{\psi}
is zero in every positive degree and
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
in degree
0
.

Since
S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}])_{1}=|\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\mathfrak{M}_{1}
,
\delta_{\psi}(|\otimes a)=da
for every
a\in\mathfrak{M}_{1}
, and the image of
d
is
\mathcal{I}
, the degree
0
homology of
\delta_{\psi}
is
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
.

Let us now prove that any
\delta_{\psi}
-closed element of degree
i\geq 1
is exact.
Let
a\in S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}])_{i}
be a
\delta_{\psi}
-closed element of degree
i
,
\delta_{\psi}a=0
. Let us decompose
a
as a sum of two terms:

(32)

a=a^{\geq 2}+a^{1}

with

a^{\geq 2}\in S^{\geq 2}(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}])
and
a^{1}\in S^{1}(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}])\simeq\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]
.

Let us compute
\delta_{\psi}(\mathfrak{r}(a^{\geq 2}))
. It admits a component in
S^{\geq 2}(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}])
, which is
a^{\geq 2}
by definition of
\delta_{\psi}
, see Equation (
17
).
As a consequence,

a^{\prime}:=a-\delta_{\psi}\left(\mathfrak{r}(a^{\geq 2})\right)

and
\delta_{\psi}
-closed element in
S^{1}(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}])_{i}\simeq\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]_{i}
.

Recall from Equation (
17
) that the component of
\delta_{\psi}
that goes from
\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]_{i}
to
S^{\geq 2}(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet})_{i-1}
is the unroot map
\mathfrak{r}^{-1}
.
Since
a^{\prime}
is
\delta_{\psi}
-closed, it belongs to the kernel of the unroot map
\mathfrak{r}^{-1}
. The kernel of
\mathfrak{r}^{-1}
is the space of trivial decorated trees; this implies that there is
b\in\mathfrak{M}_{i}
such that
a^{\prime}=|\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}b
.
Since
0=\delta_{\psi}(a^{\prime})=|\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}db
, the element
b
has to be
d
-closed. By exactness of
\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}
,
b=db^{\prime}
for some

. By construction

\delta_{\psi}(|\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}b^{\prime})=|\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}b=a^{\prime}
.
This proves that

a=\delta_{\psi}(\mathfrak{r}(a_{\geq 2})+|\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}b^{\prime})

is exact, so that the homology of
\delta_{\psi}
is zero.
∎

The

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\mathrm{Incl}\colon&\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{O}&\to&S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}]_{\bullet})\\

\displaystyle\mathrm{Proj}_{\psi}\left(t_{1}[a_{1}^{1},\dots,a_{n_{1}}^{1}]\odot\cdots\odot t_{j}[a_{1}^{j},\dots,a_{n_{j}}^{j}]\right)=\psi_{\mathfrak{r}(t_{1},\dots,t_{j})}(a_{1}^{1},\dots,a_{n_{1}}^{1},\dots,a_{1}^{j},\dots,a_{n_{j}}^{j})\,,

where the tree

is obtained by rooting the trees

altogether.

Proposition 3.1
.

Consider an arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
associated to a free or projective
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution
(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)
with arborescent operations
\psi
and differential
\delta_{\psi}
.
The
\mathcal{O}
-linear maps

S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}])
\mathfrak{M}
\mathrm{Proj}_{\psi}
\mathrm{Incl}

are homotopy inverse one to an other. More precisely,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\psi}\circ{\mathrm{Incl}}&=&{\mathrm{id}}\\
{\mathrm{Incl}}\circ{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\psi}&=&{\mathrm{id}}-\left(h\circ\delta_{\psi}+\delta_{\psi}\circ h\right)\end{array}\right.

where the homotopy
h
is given by
h:=\mathfrak{r}\circ\mathrm{p}^{\geq 2}
.
Moreover, the so-called side relations (see
[
EM53
]
) are satisfied, i.e.:

(34)

h^{2}=0\,,\,h\circ{\mathrm{Incl}}=0\,,\,{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\psi}\circ h=0.

Proof.

A direct computation gives the three relations listed in Equation (
34
). Noting that

{\mathrm{Incl}}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathrm{id}}\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}

the relation
{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\psi}\circ{\mathrm{Incl}}={\mathrm{id}}
is evident.
We are left with proving:

(35)

{\mathrm{Incl}}\circ{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\psi}={\mathrm{id}}-\left(h\circ\delta_{\psi}+\delta_{\psi}\circ h\right).

For the left-hand side we have

{\mathrm{Incl}}\circ{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\psi}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{id}&0&\psi\circ\mathfrak{r}\\[10.0pt]
0&0&0\\[10.0pt]
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

On the other hand, using (
24
) to represent
\delta_{\psi}
and noting that
h
becomes

h=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&\mathfrak{r}\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}

in the decomposition (
23
),
the matrix multiplications yield

h\circ\delta_{\psi}+\delta_{\psi}\circ h=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-\psi\circ r\\[10.0pt]
0&\mathfrak{r}\circ\mathfrak{r}^{-1}&\mathfrak{r}\circ\mathrm{p}^{\geq 2}\circ\delta_{\psi}+*\circ\mathfrak{r}\\[10.0pt]
0&0&\mathfrak{r}^{-1}\circ\mathfrak{r}\end{pmatrix}.

By definition,
\mathfrak{r}\circ\mathfrak{r}^{-1}
is the identity on
{{\mathcal{T}}ree}^{\geq 2}[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]
, while
\mathfrak{r}^{-1}\circ\mathfrak{r}
is the identity on
{S}^{\geq 2}({\mathcal{T}}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}])
.
Equation (
35
) is now seen to hold true provided only that
*\circ\mathfrak{r}+\mathfrak{r}\circ\mathrm{p}^{\geq 2}\circ\delta_{\psi}=0
on
{S}^{\geq 2}({\mathcal{T}}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}])
, which follows immediately upon using the definition of

given in Equation (

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{J}
be a graded differential ideal of an arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution
(S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]),\delta_{\psi})
, which is
h
-stable,
\delta_{\psi}
-stable, and contained in the kernel of
\mathrm{Proj}_{\psi}
.
Then
S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}])/\mathfrak{J}
, equipped with the differential
\overline{\delta}_{\psi}
induced from
\delta_{\psi}
, is a graded commutative algebra whose homology is
0
in every positive degree and
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
in degree
0
.

Proof.

The short exact sequence of differential graded
\mathcal{O}
-modules

0\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{J},\left.\delta_{\psi}\right|_{\mathfrak{J}})\longrightarrow(S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]),\delta_{\psi})\longrightarrow(S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}])/\mathfrak{J},\underline{\delta}_{\psi})\longrightarrow 0

yields a long exact sequence

\cdots\longrightarrow H_{i}(\mathfrak{J},\left.\delta_{\psi}\right|_{\mathfrak{J}})\longrightarrow H_{i}(S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]),\delta_{\psi})\longrightarrow H_{i}(S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}])/\mathfrak{J},\underline{\delta}_{\psi})\longrightarrow H_{i-1}(\mathfrak{J},\left.\delta_{\psi}\right._{|_{\mathfrak{J}}})\longrightarrow\cdots.

Since
\mathfrak{J}
is in the kernel of
\mathrm{Proj}_{\psi}
and is
h
-stable, the relation
\left.\delta_{\psi}\right|_{\mathfrak{J}}\circ\left.h\right|_{\mathfrak{J}}+\left.h\right|_{\mathfrak{J}}\circ\left.\delta_{\psi}\right|_{\mathfrak{J}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{J}}
holds. We conclude that
H_{i}(\mathfrak{J},\left.\delta_{\psi}\right|_{\mathfrak{J}})=0
for all
i\geq 0
. Hence the homologies of

S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]),\delta_{\psi})
and
S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}])/\mathfrak{J},\underline{\delta}_{\psi})
are isomorphic. This proves that
(S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}])/\mathfrak{J},\left.\delta_{\psi}\right|_{\mathfrak{J}})
has trivial homology except in degree
0
where it is
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
. ∎

3.2.

(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)

It is an old problem (see, e.g.,
[
Avr79
]
and
[
Kus94
]

\mathcal{O}
-resolutions
(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)
of

-algebra structure

Kad09

CG08

\psi

(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{O},d)

A_{\infty}
-algebra using the sign and degree conventions of
[
CG08
]
(for the original definition
[
Sta63a

Sta63b

Kel99
]

.

[

]
,
[
Mar06
]

[

[

4.

In section
4.1
, we describe examples of arborescent Koszul-Tate resolutions of ideals
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}

4.2.
Resolutions having a DGCA structure

4.2.1.
An explicit arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution

Several
\mathcal{O}
-module resolutions
(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)
of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
admit a structure of a differential graded commutative algebra (DGCA). Still, they may not be Koszul-Tate resolutions, because they may not be graded symmetric algebras. However, such
\mathcal{O}
-module resolutions
(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)
allow the construction of an explicit arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution in quite a straightforward manner.
Before stating our result, let us introduce a convention: for all
n\geq 2
, denote by
t_{n}
the tree with
n
leaves and one root:

(48)

\begin{array}[]{c}t_{n}=\\
\end{array}\overbrace{\begin{forest}}^{\hbox{$n$ leaves}}.

Notice that
t_{2}
and
t_{3}
already appeared as
\vee
and

{forest}

in the expressions
\psi_{\vee}
and
\psi_{\scalebox{0.15}{\, \, \begin{forest}}}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)
be a free
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{O}
is equipped with a graded commutative associative multiplication
\star
turning it into a DGCA. Here the multiplication
\star
is assumed to extend the given
\mathcal{O}
-module structure:

F*a=a*F=Fa
for all
F\in\mathcal{O}
,
a\in\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{O}
. Choose the arborescent operations
\psi\colon t\mapsto\psi_{t}
as follows

\psi_{t_{n}}\left(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n}\right)=a_{1}\star\cdots\star a_{n}\hbox{ for all $a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}$}

and
\psi_{t}=0
for any tree not of the previous form.
Then the pair
(S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]),\delta_{\psi})

is an arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

It suffices to check Equation (

) for

the trees

with

and for
ii)
the trees obtained by plugging

on some leaf of

for
n,m\geq 2
.

i)
holds true because
d
is a derivation of
*
and
ii)
holds true because
*
is associative.
∎

We now have two DGCAs:
(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathcal{O},\star,d)
and
(S(\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]),\odot,\delta_{\psi})
. Proposition
3.1
provides a projection
{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\psi}
from the second complex to the first complex which is a chain map.

Proposition 4.5
.

For an arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution as in Proposition

, the projection

is a morphism of differential graded commutative algebras.

Proof.

Let us check that

is a morphism of graded commutative algebras,
i.e.

{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\psi}(T_{1}\odot\cdots\odot T_{k})={\mathrm{Proj}}_{\psi}(T_{1})\star\cdots\star{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\psi}(T_{k})

for all

in

.
If

,
\ldots
,

are all trivial trees, then, by the definition of the projection in Equation (

{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\psi}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\\
\scalebox{0.5}{\begin{forest}}\begin{array}[]{c}\odot\dots\odot\\
\end{array}\scalebox{0.5}{\begin{forest}}\\
\end{array}\right)\begin{array}[]{c}\\
=\psi_{t_{k}}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})=\\
\\
\end{array}\begin{array}[]{c}\\
a_{1}\star\cdots\star a_{k}\,.\end{array}

If, on the other hand, one of them is not a trivial tree,
then
{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\psi}(T_{1}\odot\dots\odot T_{k})=(\psi\circ\mathfrak{r})(T_{1}\odot\cdots\odot T_{k})=0
since

is not a tree of the form of Equation (

∎

Note that the kernel of

satisfies the requirements on the ideal

Example 4.7
.

Consider
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{K}[x,y]/\langle xy\rangle
with the two generators
\overline{x}
and

. Let

be the ideal generated by

.
Let

-module with one generator

Then

becomes an

-module resolution by choosing the boundary map

is turned into a DGCA by equipping it with the product

\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{1}{2}(i+j)\\

where

This can be verified by a direct calculation.

Let us remark that this DGCA is a Koszul-Tate resolution, since it is isomorphic, as a graded algebra, to the symmetric algebra of the free

-module with one generator of degree

and one of degree

.

4.2.2.
Koszul complex

Assume that an ideal
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}
is generated by
r
elements
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{r}
.
Let
V
be a vector space of dimension
k
with basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
.
The pair
(K_{\bullet}(k),\delta)
given by
K_{\bullet}(k):=\mathcal{O}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\wedge V^{*}
, and
\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\varphi_{i}\otimes\mathfrak{i}_{e_{i}}
is a differential graded commutative algebra called
Koszul complex
.
The following result, attributed to Jean-Louis Koszul, is well-known.
Recall that
\varphi
is said to be a
regular sequence
if
\phi_{1}\hbox{ is not a zero divisor in }\mathcal{O}
, and for all
i\in\{2,\dots,k\}
,
\phi_{i}
is not a zero divisor in
\mathcal{O}/\langle\phi_{1},\dots\phi_{i-1}\rangle
.

Proposition 4.8
.

[
Eis95
,
Mat89
]
Let
\mathcal{O}
be a Noetherian ring. The Koszul complex
(K_{\bullet}(k),\delta)
is a Koszul-Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
if and only if
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k}\in\mathcal{O}
is a regular sequence.

Example 4.9
.

Consider
\mathcal{I}
an ideal generated by a non-zero divisor
\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{O}
. Then
\varphi_{1}
is a regular sequence of length one. As an
\mathcal{O}
-module,
K_{\bullet}(1)
has therefore two generators: the first one is the unit
1
and we denote the second one by
\theta
.
The differential is given by
\delta(1)=0
and
\delta(\theta)=\varphi_{1}
.

Example 4.10
.

Consider a polynomial
\psi\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
which is homogeneous of degree
>0
. If
\psi
has an isolated singularity at
0
, then it is a classical result
[
Sha13
]
that the sequence

\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\psi}{\partial x_{n}}
is a regular sequence. Its Koszul resolution is canonically isomorphic to the graded algebra of polynomial polyvector fields on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
equipped with the contraction by the exact
1
-form
d\psi
.

Since the Koszul resolution is an
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution, one can construct its arborescent Kozul-Tate resolution as in Proposition
4.4
.

Example 4.11
.

The Koszul resolution of Example
4.9
is an
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution for which
\mathfrak{M}_{i}=0
for all
i\geq 2
while
\mathfrak{M}_{1}
has one generator
\theta
.
Since this generator
\theta
is odd, for every decorated tree
t
with
n\geq 2
leaves,
t[\theta,\dots,\theta]
has a class in the quotient space
\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]
which is equal to
0
. As a consequence, the whole construction of Section
2
becomes trivial, and the arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution coincides with the initial Koszul resolution.

Example
4.11
is a very particular case. It follows from Proposition
4.5
that the construction of Section
2
, applied to the
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution given by the Koszul resolution
(K_{\bullet}(k),\delta)
of a regular sequence of length
k\geq 2
yields an arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution that will be much larger than
(K_{\bullet}(k),\delta)
.
For Koszul complexes, there is no gain in considering arborescent Kozul-Tate resolution, even on the contrary, it makes things more complicated there. However, in the following we provide examples for which our construction is very useful: one is given an
\mathcal{O}
-resolution that happens to be a DGCA but not a Koszul-Tate resolution and obtain an aborescent Koszul-Tate resolution by the use of Proposition
4.4
.

4.2.3.
Taylor resolutions of monomial ideals

Let
\mathcal{O}
be the polynomial ring
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. An ideal
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}
is said to be a
monomial ideal
if it admits a set
\varphi=\{\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k}\}
of monomial generators. Then
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
admits resolutions equipped with a differential graded commutative algebra structure,
the so-called
Taylor resolutions
, that we now describe following
[
Pee10
]
.

Construction 4.12

(Taylor resolutions)
.

The
Taylor complex
is the chain complex

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{i}(k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{0}(k)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}

of free
\mathcal{O}
-modules
C_{i}(\varphi)
constructed as follows:

•

The graded vector space
C_{\bullet}(k)
only depends on the number
k
of monomials generating
\mathcal{I}
.
Let
C_{\bullet}
be the free
\mathcal{O}
-module generated by elements
e_{\tau}
indexed by subsets
\tau\subset\{1,\dots,k\}
:

C_{\bullet}(k)=\oplus_{\tau\subset\{1,\dots,k\}}\mathcal{O}\,e_{\tau}.

We equip
C_{\bullet}
with a grading by imposing the degree of
e_{\tau}
to be the cardinality of
\tau
. For instance,
C_{0}(k)=\mathcal{O}\,e_{{\emptyset}}\simeq\mathcal{O}
while
C_{1}(k)=\oplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{O}\,e_{\{i\}}
.

•

For every subset
\tau
of
\{1,\dots,k\}
, let
m_{\tau}
be
the least common multiple of the monomials
\varphi_{i}
whose index
i
is in
\tau
. Explicitly, for
\tau=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\}\subset\{1,\dots,k\}
, we have
m_{\tau}=\mathrm{lcm}\left(\varphi_{i_{1}},\ldots,\varphi_{i_{m}}\right)
.
Then the differential
d
is defined by:

d(e_{\tau})=\sum_{j\in\tau}\,\mathrm{sign}(j,\tau)\,\frac{m_{\tau}}{m_{\tau\backslash\{j\}}}\,e_{\tau\backslash\{j\}}

where
\mathrm{sign}(j,\tau)
is
+1
if
\tau
contains an even number of elements strictly smaller than
j
and
-1
otherwise.

The complex
(C_{\bullet}(k),d)
is an
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
, see e.g. Theorem 26.7 in
[
Pee10
]
(the minimality condition on the generators imposed there can be dropped). This complex is called the
Taylor resolution
of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
.

Remark 4.13
.

Taylor resolutions need not be minimal
\mathcal{O}
-resolutions in general. For instance, for the ideal of
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
generated by
x^{2},xy,y^{2}
, the Taylor resolution has length three, while the
\mathcal{O}
-resolution in Section
4.1
case I has length two.

As explained in
[
BPS98
]
,
the Taylor resolution is equipped with an associative and graded-commutative multiplication
\star
: for every subsets
\sigma,\tau\subset\{1,\dots,k\}
we pose

(50)

e_{\sigma}\star e_{\tau}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{sign}(\sigma,\tau)\;\dfrac{\,m_{\sigma}\,m_{\tau}\,}{m_{\sigma\cup\tau}}\;\>e_{\sigma\cup\tau},&\hbox{if $\sigma\cap\tau=\varnothing$}\\[10.0pt]
0&\hbox{if $\sigma\cap\tau\neq\varnothing$.}\end{array}\right.

Here
\mathrm{sign}(\sigma,\tau)=(-1)^{N}
where
N
is the cardinality of pairs
(i,j)
with
i\in\sigma,j\in\tau
such that
i>j
. It is routine to check that this multiplication is compatible with
d
as defined above, turning
the Taylor resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
into a DGCA.

Remark 4.14
.

If
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k}
is a regular sequence of monomials, then the Taylor resolution coincides with the Koszul complex
(K_{\bullet}(k),\delta)
of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
as DGCAs.

In general, Taylor resolutions are
not
Koszul-Tate resolutions, because they may not be symmetric algebras.
Being free
\mathcal{O}
-module resolutions, Proposition
4.4
allows us to construct an arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution out of them.
As far as we know, this is the first explicit construction of a Koszul-Tate resolution which works for any monomial ideal even if the Koszul complex does not provide a resolution.

5.
Complexity of Koszul-Tate resolutions

5.1.
Counting computations needed for constructing Koszul-Tate resolutions

Arborescent Koszul-Tate resolutions are explicit in the sense that finitely many computations often suffice to construct it.

Recall that an
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution
(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)
of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
is said to have
finite length
if
\exists N\in\mathbb{N}
s.t.
\forall i>N
,
\mathfrak{M}_{i}=0
.

Theorem 5.1
.

For every
\mathcal{O}
-resolution
(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)
of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
of finite length, its arborescent Koszul-Tate resolutions can be computed by solving finitely many
\mathcal{O}
-linear problems of the second type.

Proof.

The recursion describing the construction of the arborescent operations
\psi
such that
\delta_{\psi}=0
is given in Equation (
30
). It consists in solving an
\mathcal{O}
-linear problem of the second type.
Let
N
be the length of
(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)
. For degree reasons,
\psi_{t}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
is zero if
t[a_{1},\dots,a_{n}]
is of degree greater than
N+1
. There are therefore finitely many trees for which
\psi_{t}\neq 0
.
One therefore needs to solve finitely many
\mathcal{O}
-linear problems of the second type only.
∎

We say that a Noetherian algebra
\mathcal{O}
is a
Syzygy algebra
if every finitely generated free
\mathcal{O}
-module has a finite length
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution by finitely generated free
\mathcal{O}
-modules.
For example, by Hilbert’s Syzygy theorem and its generalizations
[
Eis95
]
, polynomial rings in
N
variables, germs at a point of holomorphic or real analytic functions in
N
variables, or formal power series in
N
variables are all Syzygy algebras
[
Tou76
]
. Moreover every finitely generated module over the previous Syzygy algebras admit free
\mathcal{O}
-module resolutions of length less than or equal to
N
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a Syzygy algebra.
For every ideal
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}
, one can construct an arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
by solving a finite number of
\mathcal{O}
-linear problems involving free
\mathcal{O}
-modules of finite ranks only.

Proof.

The
n
first terms of a free
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
can be computed for all
n
by solving finitely many
\mathcal{O}
-linear problems of the first type. Since
\mathcal{O}
is a Syzygy algebra, this free
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution
(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)
can be chosen of finite length. The result then follows from Theorem
5.1
.
∎

If one restricts further to the polynomial algebra,
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, one can obtain even a much stronger statement than
Corollary
5.2
.

\mathcal{O}
-linear problems of the first or the second type, with all
\mathcal{O}
-modules involved being of finite ranks, can be solved by an algorithm that uses only finitely many algebraic operations
+,-,\times,/
. This follows from the theory of Gröbner basis and Schreyer’s theorem, see e.g. Theorems 2.4.2 and 2.4.4 in
[
AL94
]
, or Chapter 15 in
[
Eis95
]
.

Corollary 5.3
.

For any ideal
\mathcal{I}
of
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, an arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
can be constructed through an algorithm that uses finitely many algebraic operations.

In contrast, the Tate algorithm requires solving infinitely many
\mathcal{O}
-linear problems of the first type, even for a Syzygy or polynomial algebra. The next proposition and the discussion below make this statement precise. The ranks of free
\mathcal{O}
-modules
\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}=\oplus_{i\geq 1}{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
entering a Koszul-Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
can not be arbitrarily small.
In Appendix
C
, we give a lower bound to these ranks. It goes through the notion of a reduced complex
(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I},\underline{\delta})
of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
, which is a complex of free or projective
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
-modules.
We show that it is unique up to homotopy equivalence. Therefore, its
homology
does not depend on the choice of a Koszul-Tate resolution.
The same holds for its homology of the complex of
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces obtained by tensoring by
\mathbb{K}\simeq\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{J}
with
\mathcal{J}
a maximal ideal containing
\mathcal{I}
. This homology is a graded vector space of dimension
b_{i}(\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{J},\mathcal{I})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cap\{+\infty\}
in degree
i\geq 1
. By Theorem
C.10
, for any free
\mathcal{O}
-module
\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}=\oplus_{i\geq 1}{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
entering a Koszul-Tate resolution of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
, the rank
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
is greater or equal to
b_{i}(\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})
. This has consequences for the complexity of the Tate algorithm.

Proposition 5.4
.

If infinitely many of the integers
(b_{i}(\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}))_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are non-zero for a maximal ideal
\mathcal{J}
containing
\mathcal{I}
, then the Tate algorithm requires to solve an infinite number of
\mathcal{O}
-linear problems of the first type.

Proof.

Consider a Koszul-Tate resolution
(S({\mathcal{E}}),\delta)
coming from the Tate algorithm as in Construction
1.2
.
Theorem
C.10
implies that
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\neq 0
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
b_{i}(\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})\neq 0
.
When
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
is non-zero, the
i
-th step of the Tate algorithm requires to solve an
\mathcal{O}
-linear problem of the first type, since the
i
-th step consists in finding generators of the kernel of
\delta_{i-1}
.
∎

Proposition
5.4
is useful when one can compute the homology of the reduced complex of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
tensored with
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{J}
for some maximal ideal
\mathcal{J}
.
Arborescent Koszul-Tate resolutions allow precisely to make such computations, since it allows to describe explicitly a reduced complex of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
.

We conclude this section by applying this strategy to monomial ideals of a polynomial algebra.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a monomial ideal minimally generated by a sequence
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k}
which is not regular.
Then the Tate algorithm requires infinitely many algebraic operations.

We start with a technical lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k}
be a not a regular sequence of monomials, then there exist distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
such that
\mathrm{lcm}\{\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j}\}\neq\varphi_{i}\cdot\varphi_{j}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k}
be a not regular sequence in
\mathcal{O}
. By definition, there exists
m\geq 2
(case
m=1
is excluded) such that
a\cdot\varphi_{m}=\alpha_{1}\cdot\varphi_{1}+\dots\alpha_{m-1}\cdot\varphi_{m-1}
,
a,\alpha_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{O}
and
a\notin\langle\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{m}\rangle_{\mathcal{O}}
. Without any loss of generality we choose
a,\alpha_{\bullet}
to be monomials and we impose that any non-zero summand
\alpha_{i}\varphi_{i}
has the same multipolynomial degree as
a\varphi_{m}
. Then it easily follows if
\alpha_{i}\varphi_{i}\neq 0
there exists
b\in\mathbb{K}^{*}
such that
b\alpha_{i}\varphi_{i}=a\varphi_{m}
. Thus
\varphi_{i}
divides
a\varphi_{m}
. Since
\varphi_{i}
does not divide
a
alone according to the assumptions, the greatest common divisor of
\varphi_{i}
and
\varphi_{m}
is non-trivial and therefore their least common multiplier is not just given by their product. Therefore there exists a monomial
\chi\in\mathcal{J}
such that
\varphi_{i}\cdot\varphi_{j}=\chi\cdot\,\mathrm{lcm}\{\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j}\}
.
∎

5.2.
A word of conclusion about simplicity

We now have two main types of Koszul-Tate resolutions: the arborescent Kozul-Tate resolutions obtained of a well-chosen free or projective
\mathcal{O}
-module resolution, and those obtained out of the Tate algorithm. Hence a natural question: Which of the two is simpler?

From the point of view of complexity, i.e. the quantity of computations, arborescent Koszul-Tate resolutions are significantly simpler, as can be seen by comparing, e.g., Theorem
5.1
with Proposition
5.5
.
In particular, they can often be computed using finitely many elementary operations, see, e.g. Corollary
5.3
.

However,
as we see in Appendix
D
, if properly constructed, the Tate algorithm is minimal. It suffices to choose a minimal set of generators of the homology of the truncated complex

H_{k}\left(S({\mathcal{E}}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{k}),\delta_{k}\right)

at each step of Construction
1.2
, thus forming a ”smallest possible” free
\mathcal{O}
-module
{\mathcal{E}}_{k+1}
.
In contrast, the arborescent Koszul-Tate resolution is almost never minimal.

In conclusion, arborescent Koszul-Tate resolutions are simpler from the point of view of complexity, but not from the point of view of size.

Also, arborescent Koszul-Tate resolutions have easier homotopies.
Assume a module resolution
(\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet},d)
of
\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}
of length
N
is given.

First,
both its arborescent Koszul-Tate resolutions and any Koszul-Tate resolution obtained by the Tate algorithm are homotopy equivalent to
(FM_{\bullet},d)
. While in our construction this homotopy is completely explicit, see Proposition
3.1
, while in the second case, the construction of such a homotopy equivalence would again need infinitely many computations.

Also, Proposition
3.1
gives explicit expression of a homotopy
h
for
\delta_{\psi}
upon restricting to a subspace of the kernel of
{\mathrm{Proj}}_{\psi}
, i.e. in particular to
\mathcal{T}ree[\mathfrak{M}_{\bullet}]_{\geq N+1}
. This is very useful to have in applications like the BV or BFV constructions, see
[
HS
]
.

Appendix A
An ab initio proof of Theorem
3.5

The purpose of this Appendix is to give a detailed proof of Theorem
3.5
without using the homotopy transfer theorem. To be more in line with the rest of the paper, we keep unshifted degrees.
This requires to reformulate the defining properties of
A_{\infty}
and
C_{\infty}
algebras correspondingly. For the case of the
A_{\infty}
algebra this then actually agrees with its original definition in the literature.

Definition A.1
.

[
Sta63a
,
Sta63b
,
Kel99
]
.
Let
(\mathfrak{A},d)
be a complex of
\mathcal{O}
-modules. A sequence of
\mathcal{O}
-linear maps
\mu_{i}\colon\otimes^{i}C_{\bullet}\to C_{\bullet}
,
with
\mu_{i}
of degree
i-2

is said to be a
A_{\infty}
-algebra if it satisfies the ”higher associativity” conditions for all
n\geq 1
:

(53)

\sum_{\scalebox{0.5}{$\begin{array}[]{c}i,k\geq 0,j\geq 1\\
i+j+k=n\end{array}$}}(-1)^{i+jk}\mu_{n-j+1}(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes i}\otimes\mu_{j}\otimes\mathrm{id}^{\otimes k})=0

with the convention that
\mu_{1}=-d
.
An

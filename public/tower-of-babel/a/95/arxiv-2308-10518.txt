Heun-type solutions for the Dirac particle on the curved background of Minkowski space-times
© 1999, Springer Verlag Heidelberg All rights reserved, S Rahmani, H Panahi, and A Najafizade Department of Physics, Faculty of Basic Science, University of Guilan, Rasht 41635-1914, Iran.

Abstract
In the present paper, we study the Dirac equation in the background of Minkowski space-time on a light cone. With the help of the coupling of the radial parts, the system of 4 equations is reduced to two different second-order differential equations, which coincide with a particle in present potentials. It turns out that the central equation reduces to Heun-type equations and provides us with energy spectra. Also, it is shown that the Schrödinger equation like with a combination of the external field is related to a second-order differential equation. In another way to solve the problem, results are valid for a spinless charged particle in the context of the magnetic field. Keywords: Heun function, Dirac equation, curved space-time, light cone

Dirac equation in the constrained metric on the cone

Let us start with a metric in the spherically symmetric space-times. In simple transformation, evaluation for the line element taken in the 2-surface
S

[
23
,
24
]
, reads by

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-2\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v+\frac{1}{2}(u-v)^{2}(\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\varphi^{2}),

(1)

where
(\theta,\varphi)
are the spherical coordinates with Riemannian metric
\mathrm{d}\Omega^{2}=r^{2}(\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\varphi^{2})
which is the intersection of two hypersurfaces in which constants of
u
or
v
are null cones (light-like). We have
-\infty<u\leq v<\infty
for the retarded and advanced times, respectively, in which the radius at any value of
u
and
v
is given by
r=(v-u)/\sqrt{2}\geq 0
. Choosing a non-diagonal tetrad according to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&e_{(0)}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,0,0,1),\qquad e_{(1)}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,0,0,-1),\\
&e_{(2)}^{\alpha}=(0,\frac{\sqrt{2}}{u-v},0,0),\qquad e_{(3)}^{\alpha}=(0,0,\frac{\sqrt{2}}{u-v}\sin\theta,0),\end{split}

(2)

therefore, Christoffel symbols are

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Gamma^{u}_{\theta,\theta}=\frac{1}{2}(v-u),\qquad\Gamma^{u}_{\varphi,\varphi}=\frac{1}{2}(v-u)\sin^{2}(\theta),\\
&\Gamma^{v}_{\theta,\theta}=\frac{1}{2}(u-v),\qquad\Gamma^{v}_{\varphi,\varphi}=\frac{1}{2}(u-v)\sin^{2}(\theta),\\
&\Gamma^{\theta}_{\varphi,\varphi}=-\cos\theta\sin\theta,\qquad\Gamma^{\varphi}_{\theta,\varphi}=\cot\theta,\\
&\Gamma^{\theta}_{u,\theta}=\frac{1}{u-v},\qquad\Gamma^{\theta}_{v,\theta}=\frac{1}{v-u},\\
&\Gamma^{\varphi}_{u,\varphi}=\frac{1}{u-v},\qquad\Gamma^{\varphi}_{v,\varphi}=\frac{1}{v-u}.\\
\end{split}

(3)

The Dirac equation is written as
[
24
]

[i\hbar e^{\mu}_{a}\gamma^{a}(\partial_{\mu}+\Omega_{\mu})-mc-V]\psi(x)=0,\qquad\mu=(u,v,\theta,\varphi)

(4)

with

\Omega_{\mu}=\frac{1}{8}\omega_{ab\mu}[\gamma^{a},\gamma^{b}],\qquad\text{so that}\qquad\omega^{a}_{b\mu}=e^{a}_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}e^{\nu}_{b}+e^{a}_{\nu}e^{\sigma}_{b}\Gamma^{\nu}_{\sigma\mu}

where
\gamma^{\mu}
denotes the Dirac matrices and
m
represents the mass of the particle. The potential is considered in the form

V=\gamma^{\mu}A_{\mu}+V_{s}I,

(5)

where
A_{\mu}
and
V_{s}
are the vectors and scalar potentials, respectively. Units are chosen so that
c=\hbar=1
. The Dirac matrices satisfy

\mathcal{J}^{ab}=[\gamma^{a},\gamma^{b}].

(6)

Therefore, eq. (
4
) takes the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big[\frac{i(u-v)}{2}\big[(\gamma^{0}+\gamma^{3})\partial_{u}+(\gamma^{0}-\gamma^{3})\partial_{v}\big]-\frac{(u-v)}{2}\big[(\gamma^{0}+\gamma^{3})A_{u}+(\gamma^{0}-\gamma^{3})A_{v}\big]-\big[\gamma^{1}A_{\theta}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\sin\theta}A_{\varphi}\big]\\
&+i\big[\gamma^{2}\mathcal{J}^{32}-\gamma^{1}\mathcal{J}^{31}\big]+i\gamma^{1}\partial_{\theta}+i\gamma^{2}\frac{\partial_{\varphi}-\mathcal{J}^{12}\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}-\frac{(m+V_{s}I)(u-v)}{\sqrt{2}}\Big]\psi=0.\end{split}

(7)

Our purpose is the solution of eq. (
7
) in the background of the Minkowski metric. The presence of the term
(v-u)
throughout the reduced Dirac equation is a fundamental difficulty. Hence, let us consider the Dirac equation in coordinates
u
and
v
by so-called null coordinates as

u=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(t-r),\qquad v=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(t+r),

(8)

label null reflects that
u
and
v
are constant along future and past null cones centered
r=0
. Subsequently, the infinitely for region
r\rightarrow\infty
corresponds to taking one of the coordinates to infinity and another fixed. Substituting this transformation into eq. (
7
) leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big[-ir\gamma^{0}\partial_{t}+ir\gamma^{3}\partial_{r}+i\gamma^{1}\partial_{\theta}+i\gamma^{2}\frac{\partial_{\varphi}-\mathcal{J}^{12}\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big[(\gamma^{0}+\gamma^{3})A_{u}+(\gamma^{0}-\gamma^{3})A_{v}\big]-\gamma^{1}A_{\theta}\\
&-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\sin\theta}A_{\varphi}+(m+V_{s}I)r\Big]\psi=0.\end{split}

(9)

In the usual method, one can use the separation of variables method to obtain solutions at this stage. So, if we make the following ansatz for the spinor
\psi
in terms of components of 4-vector in basis

\psi(t,r,\theta,\varphi)=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}(t,r,\theta,\varphi)\\
\Phi_{2}(t,r,\theta,\varphi)\\
\Phi_{3}(t,r,\theta,\varphi)\\
\Phi_{4}(t,r,\theta,\varphi)\end{pmatrix}=r^{4}e^{i\ell\varphi}e^{-i\varepsilon t}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}(r,\theta)\\
\phi_{2}(r,\theta)\\
\mathcal{X}_{1}(r,\theta)\\
\mathcal{X}_{2}(r,\theta)\end{pmatrix},

(10)

with
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
the energy and the azimuthal quantum number of the particle, respectively. The constraint equation (
9
) along the cone then leads to the following coupled linear system with first-order differential equations

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big[-r\partial_{r}+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(A_{u}-A_{v})\Big]\phi_{1}+\Big[-\partial_{\theta}+\frac{\ell+A_{\varphi}}{\sin\theta}+2\cot\theta-iA_{\theta}\Big]\phi_{2}=i\Big[(\varepsilon+m+V_{s})r-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(A_{u}+A_{v})\Big]\mathcal{X}_{1},\\
&\Big[-r\partial_{r}+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(A_{u}-A_{v})\Big]\mathcal{X}_{2}+\Big[-\partial_{\theta}-\frac{\ell+A_{\varphi}}{\sin\theta}+2\cot\theta-iA_{\theta}\Big]\mathcal{X}_{1}=i\Big[(-\varepsilon+m+V_{s})r+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(A_{u}+A_{v})\Big]\phi_{2},\\
&\Big[r\partial_{r}-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(A_{u}-A_{v})\Big]\phi_{2}+\Big[-\partial_{\theta}-\frac{\ell+A_{\varphi}}{\sin\theta}+2\cot\theta-iA_{\theta}\Big]\phi_{1}=i\Big[(\varepsilon+m+V_{s})r-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(A_{u}+A_{v})\Big]\mathcal{X}_{2},\\
&\Big[r\partial_{r}-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(A_{u}-A_{v})\Big]\mathcal{X}_{1}+\Big[-\partial_{\theta}+\frac{\ell+A_{\varphi}}{\sin\theta}+2\cot\theta-iA_{\theta}\Big]\mathcal{X}_{2}=i\Big[(-\varepsilon+m+V_{s})r+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(A_{u}+A_{v})\Big]\phi_{1},\end{split}

(11)

which are now clearly integrable in the cone. Thus, with the assumption of taking the results in the product form of the radial and angular components

\Phi=\begin{bmatrix}\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{2}\\
\mathcal{X}_{1}\\
\mathcal{X}_{2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}S_{1}(r)T_{1}^{+}(\theta)\\
S_{2}(r)T_{2}^{+}(\theta)\\
S_{3}(r)T_{1}^{-}(\theta)\\
S_{4}(r)T_{2}^{-}(\theta)\end{bmatrix}.

(12)

Upon substitution of the separated wavefunction in (

), one obtains 4 coupled radial equations
\{\phi_{j},\mathcal{X}_{j}\}
and the pair of angular components
T^{+}_{j}
with

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big[-r\partial_{r}+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(A_{u}-A_{v})\Big]S_{1}(r)-\lambda S_{2}(r)-i\Big[\varepsilon+m+V_{s}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}r}(A_{u}+A_{v})\Big]rS_{3}(r)=0,\\

(13)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big(\partial_{\theta}+\frac{\ell+A_{\varphi}}{\sin\theta}-2\cot\theta+iA_{\theta}\Big)T_{2}^{+}=\lambda T_{1}^{+},\\
\Big(\partial_{\theta}-\frac{\ell+A_{\varphi}}{\sin\theta}-2\cot\theta+iA_{\theta}\Big)T_{1}^{+}=-\lambda T_{2}^{+},\end{split}

(14)

where

is the separation constant. In which the angular parts

related to the components of

Using

2.1
The Cornell potential

The Cornell potential is a combination of a Coulomb and linear potential. The Coulombic term describes a gluon exchange between the quark and its antiquark
[
26
]
. While, the linear term is related to the lattice QCD measurement
[
27
]
. It is mainly used to describe the spectra of heavy quarkonium systems
[
28

29
]
as

V_{s}(r)=-\frac{A}{r}+Br

(20)

where
A
and
B
are constant coefficients. Substitution of (
20

19

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\Gamma_{1}}{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}+2(E-V_{eff}(r))\Gamma_{1}=0,

(21)

which is the reduced one-dimensional stationary Schrödinger equation, such that the energy and effective potential read as

E=(\varepsilon^{2}-m^{2})/2+AB,\qquad V_{eff}(r)=\frac{\lambda(\lambda+1)+A^{2}}{2r^{2}}-\frac{mA}{r}+\frac{B^{2}}{2}r^{2}+mBr

(22)

Let us now consider an ansatz of the form

\Gamma_{1}(r)=\exp\Big[-\frac{B}{2}r^{2}-mr+\Lambda\log r\Big]f(r),

(23)

By means of the function (
23
) into the Schrödinger equation (
22
), we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}f(r)}{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}+\Big(\frac{\gamma}{r}+\delta+\epsilon r\Big)\frac{\mathrm{d}f(r)}{\mathrm{d}r}+\frac{\alpha r-q}{r}f(r)=0

(24)

such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&2\Lambda=1+\sqrt{1+4A^{2}+4\lambda^{2}+4\lambda},\qquad\delta=-2m,\qquad\epsilon=-2B,\\
&\alpha=\varepsilon^{2}+B(2A-2\Lambda-1),\qquad q=2m(A-\Lambda)\end{split}

(25)

Therefore, we obtained the energy eigenvalue expression for the Cornell potential as

\varepsilon=\sqrt{B(2n+2\Lambda-2A+1)}

(26)

Also, eq. (
24
) has the solution being expressed in terms of the bi-confluent Heun function
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
as

\Gamma_{1}(r)=Ne^{\frac{\epsilon}{4}r^{2}+(\delta/2)r+\Lambda\log r}HeunB\Big[\gamma,\delta,\epsilon;\alpha,q,r\Big]

where
N
is a normalization constant. Fig.

2.2
The Kratzer potential

The Kratzer potential is one of the most important potentials for describing the vibrational and rotational structures of the diatomic molecules, and is discussed in three dimensions by Landau and Lifshitz
[
33
]
. However, it can be the Coulomb plus inverse-square potential written as

V_{s}(r)=\frac{C}{r^{2}}-\frac{D}{r},

(28)

It should be noted that adding the inverse-square potential to the Coulomb potential can describe the electrons in the outer shell of an alkaline metal atom. After substitution of (
19
) in the original equation and in writing it to form (
21
), we have

E=(\varepsilon^{2}-m^{2})/2,\qquad V_{eff}(r)=\frac{C^{2}}{2r^{4}}-\frac{CD}{r^{3}}+\frac{\lambda(\lambda+1)+2mC+D^{2}}{2r^{2}}-\frac{mD}{r}.

(29)

Here, we again follow the another ansatz approach in the following form

\Gamma_{1}(r)=\exp\Big[\sqrt{m^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}r+(1-D)\log r-\frac{C}{r}\Big]g(r).

(30)

Hence, the Heun equation is given by,

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}g(r)}{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}+\Big(\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{r^{2}}+\frac{\delta^{\prime}}{r}+\epsilon^{\prime}\Big)\frac{\mathrm{d}g(r)}{\mathrm{d}r}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}r-q^{\prime}}{r^{2}}g(r)=0,

(31)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\gamma^{\prime}=2C,\qquad\delta^{\prime}=2(1-D),\qquad\epsilon^{\prime}=2\sqrt{m^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}},\\
&q^{\prime}=\lambda(\lambda+1)-2C\Big(\sqrt{m^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}-m\Big)+D,\qquad\alpha^{\prime}=2(1-D)\sqrt{m^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}+2Dm.\end{split}

(32)

For the derived eigenvalues

\varepsilon=m\left[1-\frac{D^{2}}{(n+1-D)^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(33)

The solution of the stationary Heun equation (
31
) for the presented potential (
28

[
34
]
as

\Gamma_{1}(r)=N^{\prime}e^{\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{2}r+\frac{\delta^{\prime}}{2}\log r-\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{2r}}HeunD\Big[\gamma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime};\alpha^{\prime},q;r\Big],

(34)

where
N^{\prime}
is a normalization constant. Fig.

3
Solutions of the Heun-type in an approach to the potential fields

In spherical coordinates, the Hamiltonian of a charged particle under the influence of an external magnetic field may be expressed as

\displaystyle\Biggl\{

\displaystyle-i\gamma^{0}(\partial_{t}-ieA_{t})+i\left(\gamma^{3}(\partial_{r}-ieA_{r})-\frac{\gamma^{1}\mathcal{J}^{31}+\gamma^{2}\mathcal{J}^{32}}{r}\right)+\frac{1}{r}\left(i\gamma^{1}(\partial_{\theta}-ieA_{\theta})+\gamma^{2}\frac{i(\partial_{\varphi}-ieA_{\varphi})+\mathcal{J}^{21}\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}\right)

\displaystyle+mc\Biggr\}\psi=0,

(35)

where
A_{\mu},\ \mu=t,r,\theta,\varphi
is the 4-vector potential. After separating the variables and using the before section relations, we get four radial equations

\displaystyle(\partial_{r}-ieA_{r})S_{1}+\frac{\lambda}{r}S_{2}+i(\varepsilon+m+\phi)S_{3}=0,

(36)

\displaystyle(\partial_{r}-ieA_{r})S_{4}+\frac{\lambda}{r}S_{3}-i(\varepsilon-m+\phi)S_{2}=0,

(37)

\displaystyle(\partial_{r}-ieA_{r})S_{2}+\frac{\lambda}{r}S_{1}-i(\varepsilon+m+\phi)S_{4}=0,

(38)

\displaystyle(\partial_{r}-ieA_{r})S_{3}+\frac{\lambda}{r}S_{4}+i(\varepsilon-m+\phi)S_{1}=0.

(39)

Here, for simplicity of calculations, we assume that the angular component of the potential is
A_{\theta}=A_{\varphi}=0
. Now let us consider the following condition for radial functions as

S_{1}=S_{2},\qquad S_{4}=-S_{3},

(40)

by taking
eA_{t}=\xi(r)
, we get

\displaystyle\left[(\partial_{r}-ieA_{r})+\frac{\lambda}{r}\right]S_{1}=-i(\varepsilon+m+\xi)S_{3},

(41)

\displaystyle\left[(\partial_{r}-ieA_{r})-\frac{\lambda}{r}\right]S_{3}=-i(\varepsilon-m+\xi)S_{1},

(42)

Substitution first function into the second function provides us with a second-order differential equation to the form

\displaystyle S^{\prime\prime}_{1}(r)-2ieA_{r}S^{\prime}_{1}(r)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r\left(\varepsilon+\xi(r)+m\right)}\left(rS^{\prime}_{1}(r)-(ierA_{r}+\lambda)S_{1}(r)\right)\xi^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle+\left((\varepsilon+\xi(r))^{2}-m^{2}-e^{2}A_{r}^{2}-\frac{\lambda(\lambda-1)}{r^{2}}\right)S_{1}(r)=0.

(43)

Among the usual applications of the Dirac equation in an external field are those in which the spatial components
A_{r}
vanish and
\xi(r)
depends only on the magnitude
r
of the position vector as happens in Ref.
[
35
]
. Therefore, equation (
3
) becomes simpler

\displaystyle S^{\prime\prime}_{1}(r)+\frac{1}{r\left(\varepsilon+\xi(r)+m\right)}\left(rS^{\prime}_{1}(r)-\lambda S_{1}(r)\right)\xi^{\prime}(r)+\left((\varepsilon+\xi(r))^{2}-m^{2}-\frac{\lambda(\lambda-1)}{r^{2}}\right)S_{1}(r)=0.

(44)

Let us consider the problem of the Dirac equation in the presence of the external Coulomb field

\xi(r)=-\frac{z}{r}
. With changing the variable

x=\frac{m+\varepsilon}{z}r
, and using a substitution
S_{1}(x)=x^{-w}e^{-\tau x}h(x)
,
in which

\qquad w=\frac{1}{2}(-1+\sqrt{1-4\lambda+4\lambda^{2}-4z^{2}-8\eta-4\eta\lambda}),\qquad\tau=\sqrt{z\eta(m-\varepsilon)},\qquad\text{so that}\qquad\eta=\frac{z}{m+\varepsilon},

(45)

so we get to the

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}h(x)}{\mathrm{d}x^{2}}+\Big(\frac{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}{x}+\frac{\delta^{\prime\prime}}{x-1}+\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\Big)\frac{\mathrm{d}h(x)}{\mathrm{d}x}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime\prime}x-q^{\prime\prime}}{x(x-1)}h(x)=0,

(46)

which is a confluent Heun differential equation and in within we have the parameters

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\gamma^{\prime\prime}=-2w,\qquad\delta^{\prime\prime}=-\eta,\qquad\epsilon^{\prime\prime}=2\tau,\\
&\alpha^{\prime\prime}=-\eta(2\varepsilon z+\tau)-2\tau w,\qquad q^{\prime\prime}=-\eta(2\varepsilon z+w+\lambda)-2\tau w.\end{split}

(47)

Based on these parameter values, we find the confluent Heun functions
HeunC\Big[\gamma^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime},\epsilon^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime},q^{\prime\prime},x\Big]
have the following forms
[
36
,
37
]
:

S_{1}(x)=N^{\prime\prime}e^{\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{2}r+\frac{\delta^{\prime}}{2}\log r-\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{2r}}HeunC\Big[\gamma^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime},\epsilon^{\prime\prime};\alpha^{\prime\prime},q^{\prime\prime};x\Big],

(48)

where
N^{\prime\prime}
is a normalization constant.

One may also consider the situation when
\xi
vanish and only the
A_{r}
radial term remains, we have

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{r}^{2}-2ieA_{r}\partial_{r}+\left(\varepsilon^{2}-m^{2}-e^{2}A_{r}^{2}-\frac{\lambda(\lambda-1)}{r^{2}}\right)\right]S_{1}(r)=0,

Writing now

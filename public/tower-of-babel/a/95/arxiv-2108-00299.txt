Introduction

We consider the following system of singularly perturbed elliptic equations

(
\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,\Omega}
)

\qquad\begin{cases}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u_{i}+u_{i}=\mu_{i}|u_{i}|^{p-2}u_{i}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\not=i\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\lambda_{ij}\beta_{ij}|u_{j}|^{\alpha_{ij}}|u_{i}|^{\beta_{ij}-2}u_{i},\\
u_{i}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\quad u_{i}\neq 0,\qquad i=1,\ldots,\ell,\end{cases}

where
\varepsilon>0
is a small parameter,
\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
which contains the origin,
N\geq 2
,
\mu_{i}>0
,
\lambda_{ij}=\lambda_{ji}<0
,
\alpha_{ij},\beta_{ij}>1
,
\alpha_{ij}=\beta_{ji}
,
\alpha_{ij}+\beta_{ij}=p\in(2,2^{*})
, and
2^{*}
is the critical Sobolev exponent (i.e.,
2^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-2}
if
N\geq 3
and
2^{*}:=\infty
if
N=2
).

This system arises as a model for various physical phenomena, in particular in the study of standing waves for a mixture of Bose–Einstein condensates of
\ell
different hyperfine states which overlap in space, see for example
[
10
]
. Here we consider the case in which the interaction between particles in the same state is attractive (
\mu_{i}>0
) and the interaction between particles in any two different states is repulsive (
\lambda_{ij}<0
).

In their seminal paper
[
12
]
Lin and Wei described the behavior of positive least energy solutions for the system (

S

ε
,
Ω

) with cubic nonlinearity (
\alpha_{ij}=\beta_{ij}=2
) as
\varepsilon\to 0
. They showed that each component is a spike, i.e., a rescaling of the positive ground state solution to the problem

(
\mathscr{P}_{i}
)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+u=\mu_{i}|u|^{p-2}u,\\
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad u\neq 0,\end{cases}

and that, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the centers of the spikes approach a sphere-packing position in
\Omega
, i.e., a configuration of points maximizing the distances among them and to the boundary of
\Omega
. Multiple positive solutions and a numerical description of them is given in
[
25
]
.

Our main objective is to study the existence and profile of solutions to (

S

ε
,
Ω

) some of whose components can be positive while others change sign. It is reasonable to expect that there will be solutions with sign-changing spikes, i.e., solutions whose sign-changing components look like rescalings of a sign-changing solution to the problem (

P
i

). The existence of nonradial sign-changing solutions to (

P
i

) was first established by Bartsch and Willem in
[
2
]
for dimensions
N=4
and
N\geq 6
, and by Lorca and Ubilla for
N=5
in
[
16
]
, taking advantage of some symmetry properties of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Other solutions of this type were found in
[
7
]
.

On the other hand, rescaling the components by
\widetilde{u}_{i}(x):=u_{i}(\varepsilon x)
the system (

S

ε
,
Ω

) becomes

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{i}+u_{i}=\mu_{i}|u_{i}|^{p-2}u_{i}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\not=i\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\lambda_{ij}\beta_{ij}|u_{j}|^{\alpha_{ij}}|u_{i}|^{\beta_{ij}-2}u_{i},\\
u_{i}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}),\quad u_{i}\neq 0,\qquad i=1,\ldots,\ell,\end{cases}

in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\varepsilon x\in\Omega\}
. As
\varepsilon\to 0
these domains cover the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. So it is natural to ask whether the system (

S

ε
,
Ω

) has a solution that, after rescaling, approaches a solution to the system

(
\mathscr{S}_{\infty,\ell}
)

\quad\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{i}+u_{i}=\mu_{i}|u_{i}|^{p-2}u_{i}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\not=i\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\lambda_{ij}\beta_{ij}|u_{j}|^{\alpha_{ij}}|u_{i}|^{\beta_{ij}-2}u_{i},\\
u_{i}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad u_{i}\neq 0,\qquad i=1,\ldots,\ell.\end{cases}

As shown by Lin and Wei in
[
13
, Theorem 1]
, this system does not have a ground state solution. On the other hand, Sirakov showed in
[
23
]
that it does have a positive least energy radial solution (i.e., every component

is positive and radial); see also
[
4
,
26
]
and the references therein.

One might expect to obtain solutions with positive and sign-changing components for the system (

S

ε
,
Ω

) whose limit profile is a solution of the same type for the system (

S

∞
,
ℓ

). For this last system with cubic nonlinearity (hence,

) Sato and Wang
[

]
established the existence of least energy solutions of this type whose components are radial; see also
[

,
14
]
. The following result provides solutions with nonradial sign-changing components. It is proved in Section

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N=4
or
N\geq 6
. Then, for any given
0\leq m\leq\ell
, the system (

S

∞
,
ℓ

) has a solution
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{\ell})
whose first
m
components

are positive and whose last
\ell-m
components

are nonradial and change sign. Furthermore,

satisfies

(1.1)

\begin{cases}w_{i}(z_{1},z_{2},x)=w_{i}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\vartheta}z_{1},\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\vartheta}z_{2},gx)&\text{for all \ }\vartheta\in[0,2\pi),\ g\in O(N-4),\ i=1,\ldots,\ell,\\

w_{i}(z_{1},z_{2},x)=-w_{i}(z_{2},z_{1},x)&\text{if \ }i=m+1,\ldots,\ell,\end{cases}

for all

, and it has least energy among all nontrivial solutions with these symmetry properties.

The symmetries of the sign-changing components

are basically the same as those considered in

2

. So one may wonder whether one can obtain a similar result for

using the symmetries introduced in

[

For the singularly perturbed system (

S

ε
,

,

in

centered at the origin. For

\|u\|_{\varepsilon}^{2}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{N}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big[\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}\Big]\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|u\|:=\|u\|_{1}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

, and

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

These last two results exhibit some interesting facts. The solutions given by Theorem
1.3
are least energy solutions to the system (

S

ε
,
Ω

) having some specific symmetries described in Example
2.1
(ii)
below. Their components behave as expected in the repulsive case

, i.e., they concentrate at points that are far from each other and from the boundary of the ball. On the other hand, the solutions given by Theorem

behave as shown by Lin and Wei
[

for the attractive case

enjoy the same symmetries as those given by Theorem
1.3
(and more), but they have higher energy. This shows that the sign of the interaction coefficient
\lambda_{ij}
is not determinant in the segregation behavior of higher energy solutions.

Another interesting feature of the solutions given by Theorem
1.2
is that the limit profiles of their components are coupled, i.e., they solve the system (

S

∞
,
ℓ

), up to rescaling. A similar behavior can be seen for least energy positive radial solutions to (

S

,
Ω

S

∞
,
ℓ

), see Theorem

. As far as we know, these are the first examples in the literature that show this kind of asymptotic behavior.

To prove our results we follow the approach introduced in
[
7
]
. Using concentration compactness techniques, we carry out a careful analysis of the behavior of minimizing sequences for the system (

S

∞
,
ℓ

) that have a specific type of symmetries, described in Section
2
. The symmetries can be chosen to produce a change of sign by construction. As
\varepsilon\to 0
the components of the system concentrate at fixed points of the group action. If the space of fixed points is trivial, all components will necessarily concentrate at the origin, but they will move far away from each other when the fixed-point space has positive dimension. A detailed description is given in Theorem
3.3
.

We note that for
N=3
there are no symmetries with the properties required to produce nonradial sign-changing solutions. For the single equation (

P
i

[

using the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction procedure.

We wish to point out that solutions with sign-changing components for a system related to the existence of optimal partitions for the eigenvalue problem were obtained by Tavares and Terracini in
[
24
]
. For the system
(\mathscr{S}_{1,\Omega})
with fixed

and cubic nonlinearity (hence,

) in a bounded domain

, existence and multiplicity of sign-changing and semi-nodal solutions (some components change sign and others are positive) have been established by several authors, see, e.g.,
[

22
,
5
]

2
Minimizers with positive and sign-changing components

Let
G
be a closed subgroup of the group
O(N)
of linear isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and denote by
Gx:=\{gx:g\in G\}
the
G
-orbit of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Let
\phi:G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}:=\{-1,1\}
be a continuous homomorphism of groups with the following property:

(A_{1})

If
\phi
is surjective, then there exists
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
Kx_{0}\neq Gx_{0}
where
K:=\ker\phi
.

Let
\Theta
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
which is
G
-invariant
, i.e.,
Gx\subset\Theta
for every
x\in\Theta
. Then, a function
u:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}
is called
G
-invariant
if it is constant on
Gx
for every
x\in\Theta
and will be called
\phi
-equivariant
if

u(gx)=\phi(g)u(x)\text{ \ for all \ }g\in G,\ x\in\Theta.

Define

H_{0}^{1}(\Theta)^{\phi}:=\{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Theta):u\text{ is }\phi\text{-equivariant}\}.

Assumption
(A_{1})
guarantees that
H_{0}^{1}(\Theta)^{\phi}
has infinite dimension, see
[
3
]
. If
\phi\equiv 1
is the trivial homomorphism, then
H_{0}^{1}(\Theta)^{\phi}
is the space of
G
-invariant functions in
H_{0}^{1}(\Theta)
. On the other hand, if
\phi
is surjective, then every nontrivial function
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Theta)^{\phi}
is nonradial and changes sign. The following examples are of relevance to our main results.

Examples 2.1
.

Consider the following examples of groups of isometries and homomorphims satisfying

(i)

For any group
G

is not surjective, so

(ii)

Note that

, where
\Omega_{\varepsilon_{k}}:=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\varepsilon_{k}z\in\Omega\}
. So (

would be zero in some open subset of

. Rescaling yields

and

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}\mathrm{dist}(\zeta_{ik},\partial\Omega)=\infty
for all

, as claimed. This completes the proof.
∎

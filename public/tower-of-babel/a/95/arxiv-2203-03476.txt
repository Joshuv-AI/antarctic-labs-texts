Monotone cohomologies and oriented matchings
Luigi Caputi, Daniele Celoria, Carlo Collari

Abstract
. In this paper, we extend the definition of cohomology associated to monotone graph properties, to encompass twisted functor coefficients. We introduce oriented matchings on graphs, and focus on their (twisted) cohomology groups. We characterise oriented matchings in terms of induced free-flow pseudoforests, and explicitly determine the homotopy type of the associated simplicial complexes. Furthermore, we provide a connection between the cohomology of oriented matchings with certain functor coefficients, and the recently defined multipath cohomology. Finally, we define a further oriented homology for graphs and interpret it as a count of free-flow orientations.



1.
Introduction

In recent years, the study of graphs by means of techniques rooted in combinatorial algebraic topology has developed at a very rapid pace. One of the main avenues of research focuses on the interplay between graphs and simplicial complexes; indeed, there are many distinct natural ways to associate a simplicial complex to a graph. Combinatorial, homotopical, and homological properties of these complexes encode interesting information about the graph.
The study of these simplicial complexes is now an established and active area of research
[
Wac03
,
Koz08
,
Jon08
]
, with deep connections with other areas of mathematics – see
[
Jon08
, Chapter 1]
for an overview.

In this paper we focus on a special class of simplicial complexes associated to certain graph matchings. As most complexes related to matchings on graphs, they arise as a special case of
monotone complexes
; that is simplicial complexes of subgraphs defined by a property which is closed under edge removal. Such properties are collectively called
monotone properties
.
Monotone complexes have been extensively studied, both from a combinatorial and topological perspective
[
Jon08
]
.

In most cases, investigations of monotone complexes have been carried out by applying standard homological methods.
We start our analysis with the observation that, quite often, more refined invariants arise when considering twisted coefficients. Paralleling well-known constructions in topology (
cf
.
[
Qui73
]
and
[
Ric20
, Theorem 16.2.3]
), our interest lies in extending these constructions to
functors coefficient.
To this end, using techniques borrowed from the recently defined poset homology
[
Cha19
,
CCDT21b
]
– for a given monotone property
\mathscr{M}
on a graph
{\tt G}
and a certain functor
\mathcal{F}_{A}
(depending on an algebra
A
) – we construct a cohomology theory
\mathrm{H}_{\mathscr{M}}({\tt G};A)
which we call
monotone cohomology
.
This approach provides a novel and comprehensive tool to study monotone complexes.
By varying the choice of monotone property, one can realise, for example, the chromatic homology of graphs
[
HGR05
]
and the multipath cohomology of directed graphs
[
CCDT21b
]
as monotone cohomologies.

We focus our approach on the special case of
oriented matchings
, and show that their associated monotone cohomology can be effectively computed.
More precisely, for any oriented graph
{\tt G}
, we define the oriented matching complex
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})
; this is obtained by considering a subset of matchings on the face poset of
{\tt G}
that are “compatible” with the given orientation.
Oriented matchings are naturally related to pseudoforests endowed with a specific type of orientations, which we call
free-flow
(
cf.
Definition
3.6
).
As a first result, we provide a complete characterisation of these matchings in terms of the subgraphs they induce, in analogy with
[
Koz99
,
Cha00
]
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
{\tt G}
be an oriented graph.
Simplices in
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})
are in bijection with rooted spanning pseudoforests in
{\tt G}
whose induced orientation is free-flow.

The homotopical and combinatorial properties of the oriented matching complexes, turn out to be tightly intertwined. Indeed, the homotopy type of
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})
is completely determined by simple quantities associated to the orientation of
{\tt G}
. The explicit description that we obtain exhibits these complexes as iterated suspensions, whose parameters are related to the graph’s indegrees (
cf.
Proposition
5.3
).

Proposition 1.2
.

For a connected oriented graph
{\tt G}
, the complex
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})
is either contractible or homotopic to a wedge of spheres. More precisely, if a vertex in
{\tt G}
has indegree
1
, then
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})
is contractible. Otherwise, we have the homotopy equivalence

\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})\simeq\bigvee^{q}\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\ ,

where
q
and
N
are natural numbers depending on the indegrees of vertices in
{\tt G}
.

Our proof of the above result makes use of a correspondence between matchings on graphs and multipaths – some special subgraphs satisfying a monotone property
[
TW12
]
.
A similar correspondence between oriented matchings and multipaths can be used to shed light on the simplicial structure of
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})
, allowing us to obtain the following result:

Corollary 1.3
.

Oriented matching complexes are either strongly shellable or contractible.

In particular, all non-contractible matching complexes are Cohen-Macaulay.

For an oriented graph
{\tt G}
and a commutative algebra
A
, let
C_{o}^{*}({\tt G};A)
and
C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G};A)
be the cochain complexes computing the cohomologies associated to the oriented matching and multipath complexes, respectively. The relation between these two complexes is not direct.
Rather, to an oriented graph
{\tt G}
we associate its “source resolution”
{\tt G}_{sr}
(Definition
4.6
), then the relation is established as follows:

Theorem 1.4
.

The association
{\tt G}\mapsto{\tt G}_{sr}
yields the following isomorphism of cochain complexes

C_{o}^{*}({\tt G};A)\cong C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G}_{sr};A)\otimes A^{\otimes s}

where
s
is the number of vertices of indegree
0
in
{\tt G}
.

Another interesting aspect of the oriented matching complexes
\mathrm{M}^{o}
, is that they provide a decomposition in subcomplexes of the matching complex of
{\tt G}
.
In the spirit of
[
CCC22
]
, we combine them in a single object;
given an oriented graph
{\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}}
, we consider all possible orientations on the underlying unoriented graph
{\tt G}
. The choice of
\mathfrak{o}
provides a natural identification of this set with a Boolean poset.
We decorate the vertices of this poset with the simplices of the corresponding oriented matching complex.
We can then define

, the oriented homology of
{\tt G}
with respect to the orientation
\mathfrak{o}
.
Surprisingly, it turns out that the structure of this homology is very simple, and tightly related to free-flow orientations:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
{\tt G}
be a connected unoriented graph, and let
\mathfrak{o}
be an orientation on
{\tt G}
. Then, generators of
\mathrm{OH}^{*}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}})
are in bijection with free-flow orientations on
{\tt G}
.

Despite the fact that the dimension of
{\rm OH}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}})
depends only on the underlying unoriented graph, the grading in
{\rm OH}
can distinguish between different orientations.

2.1.
Poset homology

We start by reviewing the definition of poset homology for a finite poset
P

\mathcal{F}

e.g.

[

]
) which is defined as the homology of the nerve associated to the poset. We refer to
[
Cha19
,
CCDT21b
]

For a poset
(P,<)

denote the

associated to
<
,
i.e.

x\prec y
if and only if
x<y
and there is no
z

x<z<y

We say that
P
is
ranked
if there is a rank function
\ell\colon P\to\mathbb{N}
such that
x\prec y
implies
\ell(y)=\ell(x)+1
. We say that
P
is
squared
if, for each triple
x,y,z\in P
such that
z\prec y\prec x
, there is a unique
y^{\prime}\neq y
such that
z\prec y^{\prime}\prec x
. Such elements
z,y,y^{\prime},x
, together with their covering relations in
P
, will be called a
square
.
In what follows, we will assume all posets to be ranked and squared.

Example 2.1
.

Recall that a
regular
CW-complex is a CW-complex for which all attaching maps are homeomorphisms.
A
CW-poset
(
i.e.
the collection of the cells in a regular CW complex, ordered by containment
[
Bjo84
, Definition 2.1 & Proposition 3.1]
) is ranked and squared; the rank function is given by the dimension of the cells. In particular, the face poset
F(X)
of a simplicial complex
X
is ranked and squared.

A finite poset
(P,<)
can be seen as a (small) category
\mathbf{P}
; the set of objects of
P
is the set
P
, and there is a unique morphism
x\to y
if and only if
x\leq y
.
Functors on the category associated to the poset
P
preserve commutative squares:
For each
x,z\in P
,
x\leq z
, there is a unique mapping
f_{x,z}\colon x\to z
in the category
P
.
Assume there is a square between
x
and
z
; the existence of such a square implies that
f_{x,z}
factors as follows

f_{x,z}=f_{y,z}\circ f_{x,y}=f_{y^{\prime},z}\circ f_{x,y^{\prime}}\ .

Therefore, given a covariant functor
\mathcal{F}:{\bf P}\to{\bf C}
, we must have:

\mathcal{F}(f_{y,z})\circ\mathcal{F}(f_{x,y})=\mathcal{F}(f_{y,z}\circ f_{x,y})=\mathcal{F}(f_{x,z})=\mathcal{F}(f_{y^{\prime},z}\circ f_{x,y^{\prime}})=\mathcal{F}(f_{y^{\prime},z})\circ\mathcal{F}(f_{x,y^{\prime}})\ .

In other words, all functors preserve the commutativity of the squares in
P
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
be the cyclic group on two elements.

Definition 2.2
.

A
sign assignment
on a poset
(P,<)
is an assignment of elements
\varepsilon({x,y})\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
to each pair of elements
x,y\in P
with
x\prec y
, such that

\varepsilon({x,y})+\varepsilon({y,z})\equiv\varepsilon({x,y^{\prime}})+\varepsilon({y^{\prime},z})+1\mod 2

holds for each square
x\prec y,\penalty\ y^{\prime}\prec z
.

In general, the existence of a sign assignment on a poset
P
depends on the topology of a certain CW-complex associated to

– see,
e.g.

[
CCDT21b
, Section 3.2]
,
[
Put14
, Section 5]
.

Remark 2.3
.

Every CW-poset admits a sign assignment, which is unique up to (a suitable notion of) isomorphism – see,
e.g.

[
Cha19
, Section 4]
.

We can now recall the definition of poset homology of a poset
P
with coefficients in a functor
\mathcal{F}
.

Let
{\bf A}
be an Abelian category – such as the category of left modules on a commutative ring
R
–
P
a ranked squared poset with rank function
\ell
, and
\varepsilon
a sign assignment on
P
.
Given a covariant functor

\mathcal{F}\colon{\bf P}\to{\bf A}
,
we define the cochain groups

(1)

C^{n}_{\mathcal{F}}(P)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in P\\
\ell(x)=n\end{subarray}}\mathcal{F}(x),

and the differentials

(2)

d^{n}=d^{n}_{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in P\\
\ell(x)=n\end{subarray}}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{\prime}\in P\\
x\prec x^{\prime}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\varepsilon(x,x^{\prime})}\mathcal{F}(x\prec x^{\prime})\ .

With these definitions in place, we can state one of the main results from
[
Cha19
]
and
[
CCDT21b
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
{\bf A}
be an Abelian category,
P
be a ranked squared poset, and
\varepsilon
be a sign assignment on
P
. Then, for any

and any functor
\mathcal{F}\colon{\bf P}\to{\bf A}
we have

. In particular,
(C^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}(P),d^{*})
is a cochain complex.

The differentials

, and therefore the cochain complexes, depend
a priori
upon the choice of the sign assignment
\varepsilon
. In the cases of interest to us, that is CW-posets, the choice of the sign assignment does not affect the isomorphism type of the cochain complexes

– see, for instance,
[

3.
Oriented matching complexes

The purpose of this section is twofold; first, we introduce several classical concepts related to graphs, such as matchings and their associated simplicial complexes. Then, we show how to extend this to the oriented setting by defining oriented matchings. These matchings turn out to be related to a special kind of orientations, called free-flow.

3.1.
Matchings

4.
Relations between matchings and multipaths

In this section we prove that, in certain cases, matchings on graphs and path posets can be identified. First, we study the case of graph matching complexes on unoriented graphs. Then, we provide an isomorphism between oriented matching complexes on oriented graphs and path posets.

Call an orientation
\mathfrak{o}\in O({\tt G})
on an unoriented graph
{\tt G}

alternating
if there exists a partition
V\sqcup W
of
V({\tt G})
such that all elements of
V
have indegree
0
and all elements of
W
have outdegree
0
. Note that the existence of an alternating orientation implies that
{\tt G}
is a bipartite graph.

Recall that, for an oriented graph
{\tt G}
,
P({\tt G})
denotes its path poset (
cf.
Example
2.9
) and that
F(S)
denotes the face poset of a simplicial complex
S
. Then, we have the following result:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\tt G}
be an unoriented graph. Then, we have an isomorphism
F(\overline{\mathrm{M}}({\tt G}))\cong P({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}})
if and only if
\mathfrak{o}
is alternating.

Proof.

First, note that every graph matching in
\overline{\mathrm{M}}({\tt G})
can be regarded as being a multipath in
{\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}}
, independently on the chosen orientation
\mathfrak{o}\in O({\tt G})
.
In particular,
|P({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}})|\geq|F(\overline{\mathrm{M}}({\tt G}))|
.
We claim that, if we fix an arbitrary alternating orientation
\mathfrak{o}\in\mathcal{O}({\tt G})
, then every multipath induces a matching on
{\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}}
.

Since
\mathfrak{o}
is alternating, there is a partition
V\sqcup W=V({\tt G})
such that all the edges of
{\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}}
are of the form
(v,w)
with
v\in V,
and
w\in W
.
We only have to observe that the connected components of any multipath in
{\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}}
are either vertices or single edges. For the sake of contradiction, assume there exists at least one component
c
of a certain multipath
{\tt H}
that is not a single edge or a vertex. Then, there are at least two edges in
c
which share a vertex; in particular the target of one edge, which is a vertex in
W
, must be the source of another edge, and therefore it is in
V
. This is a contradiction since
V\cap W
is empty, and the “if” part of the statement follows.

For the converse, assume
\mathfrak{o}
is not alternating. Then, there exists a vertex which is both a source and a target –
otherwise, the partition of
V({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}})
into indegree
0
and outdegree
0
vertices would imply that
\mathfrak{o}
is alternating.
Therefore, there is at least a multipath of length two which is not made of disjoint edges. Thus,
|P({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}})|>|F(\overline{\mathrm{M}}({\tt G}))|
, concluding the proof.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Given a graph

This association can be promoted to a functor

To this end, we have to prove that a regular morphism of oriented graphs

Recall that a vertex

or of the form

(f(v),f(e))&\text{if }v_{sr}=(v,e)\in V({\tt G})\times E({\tt G}).\end{cases}

The definition of

is well-posed; if

, then

is a vertex of

It follows directly from the definitions that

is a regular morphism of oriented graphs, and that

preserves compositions, proving the desired functoriality.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
{\tt G}
be an oriented graph.
There is an isomorphism
F(\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G}))\cong P({\tt G}_{sr})
between the face poset of
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})
and the path poset of
{\tt G}_{sr}
.

Proof.

The map

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\,

\displaystyle E({\tt G})\longrightarrow E({\tt G}_{sr})

\displaystyle e\longmapsto(({\tt s}(e),e),{\tt t}(e)),

is a bijection, with inverse
((v,e),w)\mapsto e
.
Therefore, we have a bijection between the (coherently oriented) edges in the barycentric subdivision of
{\tt G}
and the edges of
{\tt G}_{sr}
.

We claim that this bijection sends an oriented matching in
{\tt G}
to the set of edges in a multipath in
{\tt G}_{sr}
. An oriented matching is a collection of coherently oriented edges in the barycentric subdivision of
{\tt G}
not sharing the same target. These can be seen as a collection of edges in
{\tt G}
not sharing the same target. Since
{\tt t}(e)\neq{\tt t}(e^{\prime})
if and only if
{\tt t}(\Phi(e))\neq{\tt t}(\Phi(e^{\prime}))
, our claim is a consequence of Remark
4.7
.
A similar reasoning, replacing

Finally, the bijection between
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})
and
P({\tt G}_{sr})
just described clearly respects inclusions, and the statement follows.
∎

5.
Oriented matchings and monotone cohomology

The aim of this section is to study the homotopy type of the oriented matching complexes. We show that the oriented matching complexes are homotopy equivalent to wedges of spheres; futher, the sphere’s dimensions are related to simple combinatorial information of graphs. By providing an isomorphism with multipath cohomology, we show that computations can be carried on also in the case of non-constant functor coefficients.

5.1.
Homotopy type of oriented matchings

Recall that if

3

3

.

This next result implies that the whole homotopy type of the complexes
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})
is completely determined by the collection of indegrees of the vertices in
{\tt G}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
{\tt G}
be an oriented graph on
n
vertices. Then, the oriented matching complex of
{\tt G}
is either contractible or a wedge of spheres. More precisely, if there is
v
such that
\mathrm{indeg}(v)=1
, then
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})
is contractible. Otherwise,

\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})\simeq\bigvee^{q}\mathbb{S}^{N-1}

where

q\coloneqq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V({\tt G})\\
\mathrm{indeg}(v)>1\end{subarray}}(\mathrm{indeg}(v)-1),

and
N
is the number of vertices whose indegree is positive.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.8
we have the following isomorphism of simplicial complexes

\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})\cong X({\tt G}_{sr})\ .

Observe that
{\tt G}_{sr}
is the disjoint union of graphs
{\tt H}_{v}
, for
v\in V({\tt G})
with
\mathrm{indeg}(v)\geq 1
, each one isomorphic to a sink with
\mathrm{indeg}(v)+1
vertices.
The multipath complex of a disjoint union is the join of the corresponding multipath complexes, and the multipath complex of a sink with
m
vertices is the disjoint union of
m
points – see
[
CCDT21a
, Subsection 6.4]
. The statement now follows from Remark
5.2
.
∎

A simplicial complex
X
is
shellable
if there exists an ordering
\Delta_{1}
, …,
\Delta_{k}
of the maximal simplices in
X
such that the complex

Y_{h}=\Delta_{h}\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{h-1}\Delta_{i}

is pure (
i.e.
all its maximal simplices have the same dimension) of dimension
\mathrm{dim}(\Delta_{h})-1
, for each
h
.
There is also a related notion of
strong shellability
; we will not recall it here and refer the reader to
[
GSW19
, Definition 2.2]
. For a simplicial complex, strong shellability implies shellability.

It is well-known that shellable (and strongly shellable) complexes are homotopy equivalent to wedges of spheres, one for each maximal simplex (see, for instance,
[
Koz08
, Theorem 12.3]
).
Therefore, it is natural to ask whether non-contractible oriented matching complexes are shellable. We affermatively answer this question;

Corollary 5.4
.

Oriented matching complexes are either strongly shellable or contractible.

In particular, all non-contractible matching complexes are Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

The join of strongly shellable complexes is strongly shellable by
[
GSW19
, Proposition 2.16]
(see also
[
BW97
, Remark 10.22]
for “non-strong” shellability). Wedges of
0
-dimensional spheres, that is finite unions of more than one points, are strongly shellable.
The oriented matching complex can be obtained as the iterated join of wedges of
0
-dimensional spheres if and only if there are no vertices with indegree
1
(
cf.
the proof of Proposition
5.3
). Since, by Proposition
5.3
, the presence of vertices of indegree
1
is equivalent to the oriented matching complex being contractible, we obtain the first part of the statement.

Oriented matching complexes are pure; the dimension of each maximal simplex is the number of vertices with positive indegree. It was observed in
[
GSW19
]
that pure strongly shellable complexes are Cohen-Macaulay, giving the second part of the statement.
∎

Following this last proposition, it is easy to give a complete characterisation of the graphs whose oriented matching complex is homotopically equivalent to a sphere.

Corollary 5.5
.

The oriented matching complex
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})
of an oriented graph
{\tt G}
is homotopically equivalent to a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
if and only if all vertices have indegree
0
or
2
.

Proof.

Apply Proposition
5.3
. The number of vertices of indegree
2
gives the dimension of the sphere.
∎

5.2.
Oriented matching homology with non-constant coefficients

We proved in Proposition
4.8
that the isomorphism between the face poset of
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G})
and the path poset of
{\tt G}_{sr}
induces an isomorphism of the associated monotone cohomologies with constant coefficients. It is however possible to show that this isomorphism cannot be straightforwardly extended to an isomorphism between the respective monotone cohomologies groups with arbitrary functor-valued coefficients.

One easy example is given by the graph
{\tt G}
shown in Figure
9
(A); let
A
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{F}
-algebra of dimension
\alpha
. Consider
C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G};A)
, the multipath cochain complex of
{\tt G}
with coefficients in the functor
\mathcal{F}_{A}\colon\mathbf{P}({\tt G})\to\mathbf{Vect}_{\mathbb{F}}
and denote by
C_{o}^{*}({\tt G};A)
the monotone cochain complex associated to oriented matchings (
cf.
Definition
2.13
).
The chain complex
C_{o}^{*}({\tt G};A)
admits the following description:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\otimes 6}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\otimes 5}\oplus A^{\otimes 5}\oplus A^{\otimes 5}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\otimes 4}\oplus A^{\otimes 4}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}

where
A^{\otimes 6}
sits in degree
0
. The Euler characteristic of
C_{o}^{*}({\tt G};A)
is easily computed to be
\alpha^{4}(\alpha-2)(\alpha-1)
.
On the other hand, the cochain complex associated to
{\tt G}_{sr}
, using the poset in Figure
9
(C) is

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\otimes 5}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\otimes 4}\oplus A^{\otimes 4}\oplus A^{\otimes 4}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\otimes 3}\oplus A^{\otimes 3}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}

where
A^{\otimes 5}
sits in cohomological degree
0
. This time, the Euler characteristic of
C_{o}^{*}({\tt G};A)
is given by
\alpha^{3}(\alpha-2)(\alpha-1)
. Therefore, these complexes must have distinct homologies for any choice of
A
of dimension
\alpha\geq 3
.

Despite being distinct, the above example suggests
that the multipath cohomology groups of
{\tt G}_{sr}
and the oriented matching cohomology groups of
{\tt G}
might still be closely related. We will make use of the following general observation.

Remark 5.6
.

Let
{\tt G}
and
{\tt G}^{\prime}
two graphs, and let
P_{\mathscr{M}}\subseteq SS({\tt G})
and
P_{\mathscr{M}^{\prime}}\subseteq SS({\tt G}^{\prime})
be posets associated to the monotone properties
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
, respectively.
If there exists an isomorphism of posets

\varphi:P_{\mathscr{M}}\longrightarrow P_{\mathscr{M}^{\prime}}

such that there is a graph isomorphism
\varphi({\tt H})\cong{\tt H}
for each
{\tt H}\in P_{\mathscr{M}}
, then we have an isomorphism

(C^{*}_{\mathcal{F}_{A}}(P_{\mathscr{M}}({\tt G})),d^{*})\cong(C^{*}_{\mathcal{F}_{A}}(P_{\mathscr{M}^{\prime}}({\tt G}^{\prime})),d^{*})

of cochain complexes.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
{\tt G}
be an oriented graph. Then, there is an isomorphism of cochain complexes

C_{o}^{*}({\tt G};A)\cong C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G}_{sr};A)\otimes A^{\otimes s}

where
s
is the number of vertices of indegree
0
in
{\tt G}
.

Proof.

Consider the graph
{\tt G}^{\prime}_{sr}
obtained from
\mathtt{F}({\tt G})
by removing all “non-coherent” edges,
i.e.
the edges of the form
((v_{i},v_{j}),v_{i})\in E(\mathtt{F}({\tt G}))
–
cf.
Definition
3.3
.
Then,
{\tt G}_{sr}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
{\tt G}_{sr}
together with the disjoint union of
s
isolated vertices,
s
being is the number of vertices of indegree
0
in
{\tt G}
.

The graph
{\tt G}^{\prime}_{sr}
is bipartite, and its orientation is alternating.
This yields a bijection
\Phi
between the set of multipaths in
{\tt G}^{\prime}_{sr}
and the set of oriented matching in
{\tt G}
. The bijection is such that a multipath is isomorphic, as a graph, to the corresponding oriented matching on
{\tt G}
, thus providing an identification of the two. Moreover, inclusions of multipaths correspond to inclusions of matchings. Therefore, as an application of Remark
5.6
, the associated cochain complexes are isomorphic. We conclude by noting that the multipath cochain complex of
{\tt G}^{\prime}_{sr}
is isomorphic to the tensor product
C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G}_{sr};A)\otimes A^{\otimes s}
, since the multipath cochain complex of a disjoint union is the tensor product of the multipath cochain complexes (
cf.

[
CCDT21a
, Remark 3.2]
).
∎

Theorem
5.7
shows how to carry out computations with general functor coefficients; for sake of completeness, we provide an example:

Example 5.8
.

Consider the
3
-clique graph in Figure
9
(A), and set
A=\mathbb{F}[X]/(X^{2})
, for some field
\mathbb{F}
. By Theorem
5.7
, we know that
C_{o}^{*}({\tt G};A)\cong C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G}_{sr};A)\otimes A^{\otimes s}
, where
s=1
and
{\tt G}_{sr}={\tt A}_{2}\sqcup{\tt L}_{1}
is the graph in in Figure
9
(B).
As mentioned in the proof above, the multipath cochain complex of a disjoint union is the tensor product of the multipath cochain complexes. It follows that

C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt G}_{sr};A)\cong C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt A}_{2};A)\otimes C_{\mu}^{*}({\tt L}_{1};A)\ ,

where the tensor product is a graded tensor product.
Using
[
CCDT21b
, Example 4.16]
we can deduce that the multipath cohomology
\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{*}({\tt A}_{2};A)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}^{2}
in degree
0
, and
\mathbb{F}^{2}
in degree
1
. It is then easy to see that
\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{*}({\tt L}_{1};A)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}^{2}
in degree
0
.
Putting everything together, we see that the cohomology of the cochain complex
C_{o}^{*}({\tt G};A)
is

{\rm H}_{o}^{i}({\tt G};A)\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{F}^{8}&i=0,1,\\
(0)&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

concluding the computation.

6.
Oriented homology and free-flow orientations

In this final section, we introduce a special instance of the general constructions outlined in Section
2
. This is a novel homology theory for oriented graphs; its construction is formally similar to the homology theories introduced in a different context in
[
CCC22
]
.

Let
{\tt G}
be an unoriented graph. Recall that
O({\tt G})
denotes the set of all possible orientations on
{\tt G}
. For a fixed orientation
\mathfrak{o}
, we can define a poset

\mathcal{O}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})\coloneqq(O({\tt G}),<_{\mathfrak{o}})

where
<_{\mathfrak{o}}
is the order relation associated to the covering relation
\prec_{\mathfrak{o}}
defined as follows: given
\mathfrak{o}_{1},\mathfrak{o}_{2}\in O({\tt G})

\mathfrak{o}_{1}\prec_{\mathfrak{o}}\mathfrak{o}_{2}
if and only if the oriented graphs
{\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}_{1}}
and
{\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}_{2}}
differ only on a single edge
e
, and the orientation of
e
in
{\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}_{1}}
is the same as in
{\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}}
. Clearly, we have a poset isomorphism between
\mathcal{O}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})
and the Boolean poset
(\wp(|E({\tt G})|),\subseteq)
.

Consider the functor
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathcal{O}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})\to\mathbf{grVect}_{\mathbb{F}}
to graded vector spaces over the field
\mathbb{F}
, defined on each object
\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}
of (the category associated to)
\mathcal{O}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})
as

(9)

\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{o}^{\prime})\coloneqq\mathbb{F}\langle\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}})\rangle

where
\mathbb{F}\langle\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}})\rangle
is the
\mathbb{F}
-vector space spanned by the simplices in the oriented matching complex
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}})
, graded by the simplices’ dimension.

For each covering relation
\mathfrak{o}_{1}\prec_{\mathfrak{o}}\mathfrak{o}_{2}
, we set

(10)

\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{o}_{1}\prec_{\mathfrak{o}}\mathfrak{o}_{2})\colon\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{o}_{1})\to\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{o}_{2})

to be the map induced by the inclusion
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}_{1}})\cap\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}_{2}})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}_{2}})
. Arguing as in Proposition
2.11
, this data describes a functor on the category associated to
\mathcal{O}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})
.

Now, for any sign assignment
\varepsilon
on
\mathcal{O}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})
, we consider the associated poset homology as the homology of the associated cochain complex
\mathrm{OC}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})\coloneqq(C_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}(\mathcal{O}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})),d^{*})
–
cf.
Theorem
2.4
. Note that this homology is bigraded; the first degree increases by one under the action of the differential. The second degree, called
simplicial degree
, is just given by the simplices’ dimensions. This latter degree is preserved under the differential, making the homology bigraded.

Definition 6.1
.

We define the oriented homology of
{\tt G}
with respect to
\mathfrak{o}
,
\mathrm{OH}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}})
, as the homology of the bigraded cochain complex
(\mathrm{OC}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o}),d^{*})
.

\mathrm{OH}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})
admits an extremely simple and explicit description, regardless of the initial orientation
\mathfrak{o}
chosen. In particular, we show that
\mathrm{OH}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})
is simply a count of the free-flow orientations on
{\tt G}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
{\tt G}
be a connected unoriented graph, and let
\mathfrak{o}
be an orientation on
{\tt G}
. Then, the generators of
\mathrm{OH}^{*}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}})
are in bijection with free-flow orientations on
{\tt G}
.
More precisely,
\dim({\rm OH}^{i}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}}))
is the number of free-flow orientations on
{\tt G}
obtained from
\mathfrak{o}
by changing the orientation of exactly
i
edges.
In particular,
{\rm OH}^{*}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}})
is non-trivial if and only if
{\tt G}
is a pseudotree.

Proof.

By definition,
{\rm OC}^{i}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})
is the
\mathbb{F}
-vector space spanned by oriented matchings on
{\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}}
, for all orientations
\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}
differing from
\mathfrak{o}
on precisely
i
edges.

We call a chain complex
Boolean of dimension
n+1
if it is isomorphic to the reduced simplicial chain complex of the standard
n
-simplex (the
-1
-simplex being the empty set).
We claim that for each matching
m
in
\mathrm{M}({\tt G})=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{o}}\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}})
(
cf.
Equation (
7
)), we have a Boolean subcomplex
C(m)
of
{\rm OC}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})
, and that moreover
{\rm OC}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})
splits as the direct sum of these complexes.

Fix
m\in\mathrm{M}({\tt G})
, and define

C(m)=\mathbb{F}\langle m^{\prime}\in\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o}^{\prime})\mid\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}({\tt G}),\ m^{\prime}=m\rangle\subseteq{\rm OC}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})\ .

Since the differential
d
of
{\rm OC}^{*}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})
is induced by the inclusions
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}_{1}})\cap\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}_{2}})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}_{2}})
(
cf.
Equations (
2
) and (
10
)), it follows that
C(m)
, endowed with its induced differential, is a subcomplex. Furthermore, if
d(x)\in C(m)
then
x\in C(m)
. This last fact implies that
{\rm OC}({\tt G},\mathfrak{o})
splits as the direct sum of these complexes.
It is not hard to see that
C(m)
is Boolean; first, consider the set
F
of edges of
{\tt G}
identified by the matching
m\subset E(\mathtt{F}({\tt G}))
. For an orientation
\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}
on
{\tt G}
,
m
will belong to
\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}})
if and only if the target of each edge in
F
is the same as the corresponding edge in
m
(
cf.
Definition
3.3
).
Let
\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}
be the unique orientation on
{\tt G}
such that the edges in
F
are coherent with
m
, and the remaining edges are oriented as in
\mathfrak{o}
. Starting from
\widehat{\mathfrak{o}}
and changing the orientations of the edges in
E({\tt G})\setminus F
we get all orientations
\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}
such that

C(m)\cap\mathbb{F}\langle\mathrm{M}^{o}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}})\rangle\neq(0)\ .

In particular,
C(m)
is Boolean of dimension
|E({\tt G})\setminus F|
.

Boolean complexes have trivial homology, unless they are of dimension
0
– in which case the homology is free of rank
1
. Therefore, the generators of
{\rm OH}({\tt G}_{\mathfrak{o}})
correspond precisely to the oriented matchings on
{\tt G}
with the same number of edges as
{\tt G}
. In turn, each of these matchings induce an orientation on
{\tt G}
where all vertices have indegree
1
or
0
. Thus, by Lemma
3.7
these orientations are exactly the free-flow orientations on
{\tt G}
.
∎

This last result implies in particular that the rank of the oriented homology is independent of the initial choice of orientation. However, as showed in the previous example and in Figure
10
, the oriented homology of a given graph with respect to two distinct orientations can be different. Namely, the generators might be supported in different degrees.

Coloring Grids Avoiding Bicolored Paths
Derman Keskinkılıç, Lale Özkahya

Abstract
. The star chromatic number on a graph is the minimum number of colors in a proper vertex coloring forbidding any P 4 P_{4} with two colors (bicolored). This problem was introduced by Grünbaum (1973) together with the acyclic coloring of graphs, where bicolored cycles are avoided. In this paper, we study a generalization of this problem, by considering proper vertex coloring on graphs forbidding bicolored paths of a fixed length that was initially discussed by Alon, Mcdiarmid, and Reed (1991). Here, we study this problem on products of two paths. We show that at least 4 colors are needed to properly color the product of paths, P m ​ □ ​ P n P_{m}\square P_{n} , avoiding a bicolored P k , P_{k}, unless n < k − 2 n<k-2 or m < k − 2 . m<k-2. With this result, the above question is settled for all k k on 2-dimensional grids.

The

Similarly,

. Both, the star coloring and

[
2
]
and Kostochka
[
20
]
, respectively.

The star coloring problem has been studied widely on many different graph families
such as product of graphs, planar and outerplanar graphs
[

,

,

,

,

[

. Acyclic coloring of products of graphs, such as grid and tori, are extensively studied in
[

]
, and
[

]
.
Alon, Mcdiarmid, and Reed
[

proved that there exist graphs

In
[
3

, it is also shown that for any graph

Recently, there have been some improvements in the constant factor of the
upper bound in
[

,
11
,
22
]

by using the entropy compression method.
Similar results for the star chromatic number of graphs
are obtained by Fertin et al.
[

]
,
showing

for any graph

Alon, Mcdiarmid, and Reed claim in

]
that an upper bound similar to above can be shown when a bicolored path,

, is not allowed in a proper vertex coloring.
In
[

]
, this chromatic number is studied for paths of even order, and
later studied for all paths in

]

]
.
This problem has been also generalized to subgraphs other than paths.
For example, in
[

,

]
and
[

]
, further bounds are shown introducing the chromatic number for
(2,
\mathcal{F}
)-subgraph coloring

Similarly, Aravind and Subramanian show upper and lower bounds as an expression of the maximum degree

Gonçalves, Montassier, and Pinlou

In this paper, we study this problem on products of paths, in particular on 2-dimensional grids.

a

-coloring

The minimum number of colors needed for a

In

\begin{matrix}1&2&3&1&2&3&...\\
2&3&1&2&3&1&...\\
\end{matrix}\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\begin{matrix}1&2&3&1&2&3&\dots\\

Type 1:

Type 2:

Rademacher Random Projections with Tensor Networks
Beheshteh T. Rakhshan, Guillaume Rabusseau

Abstract
Random projection (RP) have recently emerged as popular techniques in the machine learning community for their ability in reducing the dimension of very high-dimensional tensors. Following the work in [ 30 ] , we consider a tensorized random projection relying on Tensor Train (TT) decomposition where each element of the core tensors is drawn from a Rademacher distribution. Our theoretical results reveal that the Gaussian low-rank tensor represented in compressed form in TT format in [ 30 ] can be replaced by a TT tensor with core elements drawn from a Rademacher distribution with the same embedding size. Experiments on synthetic data demonstrate that tensorized Rademacher RP can outperform the tensorized Gaussian RP studied in [ 30 ] . In addition, we show both theoretically and experimentally, that the tensorized RP in the Matrix Product Operator (MPO) format is not a Johnson-Lindenstrauss transform (JLT) and therefore not a well-suited random projection map.

Our contribution is two-fold. Experiments show that in practice, the performance of the tensorized RP with Rademacher random variables outperforms tensorized Gaussian RP since it reduces the number of operations as it does not require any multiplication.

2.2
Random Projection

Random projections (RP) are efficient tools for projecting linearly high-dimensional data down into a lower dimensional space while preserving the pairwise distances between points. This is the classical result of the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma
[
17
]
which states that any
n
-point set
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be projected linearly into a
k
-dimensional space with
k=\Omega(\varepsilon^{-2}\log{(n)})
. One of the simplest way to generate such a projection is using a
d\times k
random Gaussian matrix
\mathbf{A}
, i.e., the entries of
\mathbf{A}
are drawn independently from a standard Gaussian distribution with mean zero and variance one. More precisely, for any two points
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
the following inequality holds with high probability

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\|^{2}\leq\|f(\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{v})\|^{2}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\|^{2},

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}\ (k\ll d)
is a linear map
f(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
is a random matrix. We also call
f
a Johnson-Lindenstrauss transform (JLT).
To have a JLT, the random projection map
f
must satisfy the following two properties: (i) Expected isometry, i.e.,
\mbox{}\mathbb{E}\left[\|f(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}\right]=\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}
and (ii) Vanishing variance, that is
\mbox{}\textup{Var}\left(\|f(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}\right)
decreases to zero as the embedding size
k
increases.

\bm{\mathcal{T}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{R^{(N-1)}}}\langle\!\langle\bm{\mathcal{G}}^{1},\bm{\mathcal{G}}^{2},\cdots,\bm{\mathcal{G}}^{N}\rangle\!\rangle,

where
\bm{\mathcal{G}}^{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times d_{1}\times R},\bm{\mathcal{G}}^{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{R\times d_{2}\times R},\cdots,\bm{\mathcal{G}}^{N-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{R\times d_{N-1}\times R},\bm{\mathcal{G}}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{R\times d_{N}\times 1}
and the entries of each
\bm{\mathcal{G}}^{n}
for
n\in[N]
are drawn independently from the standard normal distribution (resp. the Rademacher distribution).

Definition 2
.

A
TT Gaussian (resp. TT Rademacher) random projection of rank
R
is a linear map
f_{\mathrm{TT}(R)}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times\dots\times d_{N}}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
defined component-wise by

\left(f_{\mathrm{TT}(R)}(\bm{\mathcal{X}})\right)_{i}:=~\frac{1}{\sqrt{kR^{(N-1)}}}\langle\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{i},\bm{\mathcal{X}}\rangle,\ i\in[k],

where
\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{i}\sim\mathrm{TT}_{\mathcal{N}}(R)
(resp.
\bm{\mathcal{T}}_{i}\sim\mathrm{TT}_{\text{Rad}}(R)
).

Our main results show that the tensorized Rademacher random projection still benefits from JLT properties as it is an expected isometric map and the variance decays to zero as the random dimension size grows. The following theorems state that using Rademacher random variables instead of standard Gaussian random variables gives us the same results for the bound of the variance while preserving the same lower bound for the size of the random dimension
k
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\bm{\mathcal{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}\times\cdots\times d_{N}}
and let
f_{\mathrm{TT}(R)}
be either a tensorized Gaussian RP or a tensorized Rademacher RP of rank
R
(see Definition
2
) . The random projection map
f_{\mathrm{TT}(R)}
satisfies the following properties:

\bullet~\mbox{}\mathbb{E}\left[\|f_{\mathrm{TT}(R)}(\bm{\mathcal{X}})\|_{2}^{2}\right]=\|\bm{\mathcal{X}}\|_{F}^{2}

\bullet~\mbox{}\textup{Var}\left(\|f_{\mathrm{TT}(R)}(\bm{\mathcal{X}})\|_{2}^{2}\right)\leq\frac{1}{k}(3\left(1+\frac{2}{R}\right)^{N-1}-1)\left\|{\bm{\mathcal{X}}}\right\|_{F}^{4}

Proof.

The proof for the Gaussian TT random projection is given in
[
30
]
. We now show the result for the tensorized Rademacher RP. The proof of the expected isometry part follows the exact same technique as in
[
30
]
(see section 5.1, expected isometry part), we thus omit it here.
Our proof to bound the variance of
f_{\mathrm{TT}(R)}
when the core elements are drawn independently from a Rademacher distribution relies on the following lemmas.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
be a random matrix whose entries are i.i.d Rademacher random variables with mean zero and variance one, and let
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
be a (random) matrix independent of
\mathbf{A}
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\rangle^{4}\leq 3\mathbb{E}\left\|{\mathbf{B}}\right\|_{F}^{4}.

Proof.

Setting
\mathbf{a}=\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A})\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}
and
\mathbf{b}=\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{B})\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\rangle^{4}=\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\rangle^{4}=\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbf{a}^{\otimes 4},\mathbf{b}^{\otimes 4}\rangle=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{1}},\mathbf{a}_{i_{2}},\mathbf{a}_{i_{3}},\mathbf{a}_{i_{4}}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}},\mathbf{b}_{i_{2}},\mathbf{b}_{i_{3}},\mathbf{b}_{i_{4}}],

we can see that in four cases we have non-zero values for
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{1}},\mathbf{a}_{i_{2}},\mathbf{a}_{i_{3}},\mathbf{a}_{i_{4}}]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{1}},\mathbf{a}_{i_{2}},\mathbf{a}_{i_{3}},\mathbf{a}_{i_{4}}]=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }i_{1}=i_{2}=i_{3}=i_{4}~~\text{or}\\
&i_{1}=i_{2}\neq i_{3}=i_{4}~~\text{or}\\
&i_{1}=i_{3}\neq i_{2}=i_{4}~~\text{or}\\
&i_{1}=i_{4}\neq i_{2}=i_{3}.\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\rangle^{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{1}}^{4}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{4}]+\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{3}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{1}}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{3}}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{3}}^{2}]+\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{4}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{1}}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{4}}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{4}}^{2}]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{1}}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{2}}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{2}}^{2}].

Since
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{1}}^{4}]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{1}}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{2}}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{3}}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{a}_{i_{4}}^{2}]=1
, the equation above can be simplified as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\rangle^{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{4}]+\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{3}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{3}}^{2}]+\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{4}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{4}}^{2}]+\sum_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{b}_{i_{2}}^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_{1}}\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{4}+\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_{1},i_{3}}\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{2}\mathbf{b}_{i_{3}}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_{1}=i_{3}}\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{4}+\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_{1},i_{4}}\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{2}\mathbf{b}_{i_{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_{1}=i_{4}}\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{4}+\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}}\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{2}\mathbf{b}_{i_{2}}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_{1}=i_{2}}\mathbf{b}_{i_{1}}^{4}=3\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{B}\|_{F}^{4}-2\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{b}\|_{4}^{4}\leq 3\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{B}\|_{F}^{4}.

∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times R}
be a random matrix whose entries are i.i.d Rademacher random variables with mean zero and variance one, and let
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}
be a random matrix independent of
\mathbf{A}
, then

\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}\|_{F}^{4}\leq R(R+2)\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{B}\|_{F}^{4}.

Proof.

Setting
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{B}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}\right\|_{F}^{4}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\left(\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\left(\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbf{M},\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}}\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{E}[(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}})_{i_{1},i_{2}}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}})_{i_{3},i_{4}}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\mathbf{M}_{i_{3}i_{4}}]

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}}\sum_{j,k}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}j}\mathbf{A}_{i_{2}j}\mathbf{A}_{i_{3}k}\mathbf{A}_{i_{4}k}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\mathbf{M}_{i_{3}i_{4}}].

Since the components of
\mathbf{A}
are drawn from a Rademacher distribution, the non-zero summands in the previous equation can be grouped in four cases (which follows from Eq. (
2
)):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}\right\|_{F}^{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}\in[d]}\sum_{j,k\in[R]}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}j}^{2}\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}k}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{1}}^{2}]

\displaystyle(i_{1}=i_{2}=i_{3}=i_{4})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i_{1}\in[d],\atop i_{3}\in[d]\setminus\{i_{1}\}}\sum_{j,k\in[R]}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}j}^{2}\mathbf{A}_{i_{3}k}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{1}}\mathbf{M}_{i_{3}i_{3}}]

\displaystyle(i_{1}=i_{2}\neq i_{3}=i_{4})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i_{1}\in[d],\atop i_{2}\in[d]\setminus\{i_{1}\}}\sum_{j,k\in[R]}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}j}\mathbf{A}_{i_{2}j}\mathbf{A}_{i_{2}k}\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}k}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\mathbf{M}_{i_{2}i_{1}}]

\displaystyle(i_{1}=i_{4}\neq i_{2}=i_{3})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i_{1}\in[d],\atop i_{4}\in[d]\setminus\{i_{1}\}}\sum_{j,k\in[R]}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}j}\mathbf{A}_{i_{4}j}\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}k}\mathbf{A}_{i_{4}k}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{4}}^{2}]

\displaystyle(i_{1}=i_{3}\neq i_{2}=i_{4})

Now by splitting the summations over
j,k\in[R]
in two cases
j=k
and
j\neq k
, and observing that the 3rd and 4th summands in the previous equation vanish when
j\neq k
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}\right\|_{F}^{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}\in[d]}\sum_{j\in[R]}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}j}^{4}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{1}}^{2}]+\!\!\!\!\sum_{i_{1}\in[d]}\sum_{j\in[R],\atop k\in[R]\setminus\{j\}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}j}^{2}\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}k}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{1}}^{2}]

\displaystyle+\!\!\!\!\sum_{i_{1}\in[d],\atop i_{3}\in[d]\setminus\{i_{1}\}}\sum_{j\in[R]}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}j}^{2}\mathbf{A}_{i_{3}j}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{1}}\mathbf{M}_{i_{3}i_{3}}]+\!\!\!\!\sum_{i_{1}\in[d],\atop i_{3}\in[d]\setminus\{i_{1}\}}\sum_{j\in[R],\atop k\in[R]\setminus\{j\}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}j}^{2}\mathbf{A}_{i_{3}k}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{1}}\mathbf{M}_{i_{3}i_{3}}]

\displaystyle+\!\!\!\!\sum_{i_{1}\in[d],\atop i_{2}\in[d]\setminus\{i_{1}\}}\sum_{j\in[R]}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}j}^{2}\mathbf{A}_{i_{2}j}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\mathbf{M}_{i_{2}i_{1}}]+\!\!\!\!\sum_{i_{1}\in[d],\atop i_{4}\in[d]\setminus\{i_{1}\}}\sum_{j\in[R]}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}j}^{2}\mathbf{A}_{i_{4}j}^{2}]\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{4}}^{2}].

Since
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}j}^{4}]=1
and
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}j}^{2}\mathbf{A}_{i_{1}k}^{2}]=1
whenever
j\neq k
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}\right\|_{F}^{4}=R^{2}\left(\sum_{i_{1}\in[d]}\!\!\!\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{1}}^{2}]+\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{i_{1}\in[d],\atop i_{3}\in[d]\setminus\{i_{1}\}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{1}}\mathbf{M}_{i_{3}i_{3}}]\right)\!\!+\!R\!\left(\!\sum_{i_{1}\in[d],\atop i_{2}\in[d]\setminus\{i_{1}\}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\mathbf{M}_{i_{2}i_{1}}]+\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{i_{1}\in[d],\atop i_{4}\in[d]\setminus\{i_{1}\}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{4}}^{2}]\right)

\displaystyle=R^{2}~\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_{1},i_{3}\in[d]}\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{1}}\mathbf{M}_{i_{3}i_{3}}+R~\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}\in[d]}\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\mathbf{M}_{i_{2}i_{1}}+R~\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_{1},i_{4}\in[d]}\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{4}}^{2}-2R~\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_{1}\in[d]}\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq R^{2}~\mathbb{E}[\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathbf{M})^{2}]+R~\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}\in[d]}\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\mathbf{M}_{i_{2}i_{1}}+R~\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_{1},i_{4}\in[d]}\mathbf{M}_{i_{1}i_{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle=R^{2}\mathbb{E}[\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{B})^{2}]+2R\mathbb{E}[\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}((\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{B})^{2})],

where in the last equation, we used the fact that
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{B}
is symmetric.
Finally, by the submultiplicavity property of the Frobenius norm, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}\right\|_{F}^{4}

\displaystyle=R^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathbf{B}\right\|_{F}^{4}+2R\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{B}\right\|_{F}^{2}\leq R^{2}\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathbf{B}\right\|_{F}^{4}+2R\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathbf{B}\right\|_{F}^{4}=R(R+2)\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathbf{B}\right\|_{F}^{4}.\qed

By using these lemmas and the exact same proof technique as in

part).

mutatis mutandi

5
Conclusion

We presented an extension of the tensorized Gaussian embedding proposed in
[
30
]
for high-order tensors: Tensorized Rademacher random projection map. Our theoretical and empirical analysis show that the Gaussian tensorized RP in
[
30
]
can be replaced by the tensorized Rademacher RP while still benefiting from the JLT properties.We also showed, both in theory and practice, the RP in an MPO format is not a suitable dimension reduction map.
Future research directions include leveraging and developing efficient sketching algorithms relying on tensorized RPs to find theoretical guarantees for randomized SVD and regression problems of very high-dimensional matrices given in the TT format.

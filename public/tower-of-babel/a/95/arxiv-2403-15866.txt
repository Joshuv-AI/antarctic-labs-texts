Existence and multiplicity of solutions for the logarithmic Schrödinger equation with a potential on lattice graphs
Zhentao He, Chao Ji

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the existence and multiplicity of solutions for the logarithmic Schrödinger equation on lattice graphs ℤ N \mathbb{Z}^{N} − Δ ​ u + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ u = u ​ log ⁡ u 2 , x ∈ ℤ N , -\Delta u+V(x)u=u\log u^{2},\quad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}, When the potential V V is coercive, we obtain infinitely many solutions by adapting some arguments of the Fountain theorem. In the cases of periodic potential, asymptotically periodic potential and bounded potential, we first investigate the existence of ground state solutions via the variation methods, and then we generalize these results from ℤ N \mathbb{Z}^{N} to quasi-transitive graphs. Finally, we extend the main results of the paper to the p p -Laplacian equation with the logarithmic nonlinearity.

1.
Introduction and main results

In the past decade, a vast literature is dedicated to the following nonlinear logarithmic Schrödinger equation

(1.1)

-\Delta u+V(x)u=u\log u^{2},\,\,\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Because this class of equations has some important physical applications, such as quantum mechanics, quantum optics, nuclear physics, transport and diffusion phenomena, open quantum systems, effective quantum gravity, theory of superfluidity and Bose-Einstein condensation (see
[
26
]

C^{1}

H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)

such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\log u^{2}dx=-\infty
. In order to overcome this technical difficulty, some authors have used different techniques to study the existence, multiplicity and concentration of the solutions under some assumptions on the potential
V
, which can be seen in
[

,

,

,

,
17
,

In recent years, the various mathematical problems on graphs have been extensively investigated (see

[

[

on the lattice graph

where

is periodic or bounded. Due to our
scope, we would like to mention the recent contribution
[

where Chang et al. studied the following logarithmic Schrödinger equations on locally finite graphs

(1.2)

-\Delta u+V(x)u=u\log u^{2}\,\,\text{ in }\,\mathbb{V},

where

G=(\mathbb{V},E)
is a connected locally finite graph, the potential

is bounded from below and may change sign. We know that one of the significant challenges in studying the above problem is the lack of compactness. Under two appropriate assumptions on potential

Motivated by
[

(1.3)

-\Delta u+V(x)u=u\log u^{2},\quad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}.

Specifically, when the potential is coercive, we establish the existence of infinitely many solutions by adapting some arguments of the Fountain theorem and a Sobolev compact embedding theorem, and in the case of periodic potential, asymptotic periodic potential and bounded potential, we obtain the existence of the ground state solutions via variational methods. Here it is essential to note that, in the case of periodic potential, asymptotic periodic potential and bounded potential, the compact embedding theorems in
[

are absent, even on lattice graph. Therefore, our problem is more challenging and complicated. Another novelty arises when the potential

(PS)
, for short) sequence. In the case of a coercive potential, the previous arguments dees not work, we establish logarithmic Sobolev inequality on lattice graph to show the boundedness of the
(PS)
sequence. We believe that this has independent interest and can be applied to the research of other related problems.

2.
The variational framework and some preliminaries

Throughout this paper, let
G
be a uniformly locally finite graph, i.e.,

\#\{y\in\mathbb{V}:y\sim x\}\leq C,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{V},

where
C
is a uniform constant. It is clear that
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
is a special case of uniformly locally finite graph with a uniform constant
2N
.

For any function
u,v\in C(\mathbb{V})
, we define the associated gradient form as

\Gamma(u,v)(x):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\sim x}(u(y)-u(x))(v(y)-v(x)).

Let
\Gamma(u)=\Gamma(u,u)
and

|\nabla u|(x):=\sqrt{\Gamma(u)(x)}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\sim x}(u(y)-u(x))^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

We shall work in Hilbert space

X=\left\{u\in H^{1}\left(\mathbb{V}\right);\int_{\mathbb{V}}(V(x)+1)u^{2}d\mu<\infty\right\}

with the inner product given by

\langle u,v\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{V}}\Gamma(u,v)+(V(x)+1)uvd\mu.

If the potential
V
is coercive,
X
is a proper subspace of
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{V}\right)
and compactly embedded in
\ell^{p}\left(\mathbb{V}\right)
for all
2\leq p\leq\infty
, see the details in
[
9
, Lemma 2.2]
.

If the potential function
V:\mathbb{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is bounded uniformly, and there exist
C_{1},C_{2}>-1
, such that

C_{1}\leq V(x)\leq C_{2},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{V},

it is easy to show that the spaces
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{V}\right)
and
X
are equivalent. If
G
is a uniformly locally finite graph, we may show that the space
H^{1}(\mathbb{V})
is equivalent to
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V})
. Therefore, spaces
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{V}\right)
,
X
and
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V})
are pairwise equivalent to each other.

Lemma 2.1
.

The spaces
H^{1}(\mathbb{V})
and
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V})
are equivalent.

Proof.

Let
u\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V})
. For any
x\in\mathbb{V}
, we have

\sum_{y\sim x}\lvert u(y)-u(x)\rvert^{2}\leqslant 2C\lvert u(x)\rvert^{2}+2\sum_{y\sim x}\lvert u(y)\rvert^{2}.

Thus,

\int_{\mathbb{V}}{\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}}\,d\mu=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\sim x}\lvert u(y)-u(x)\rvert^{2}\leqslant C\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{2}+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\sum_{y\sim x}\lvert u(y)\rvert^{2}\leq 2C\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{2}.

We conclude that
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{V})
and

\lVert u\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert u\rVert\leq\sqrt{2C+1}\lVert u\rVert_{2}.

∎

The following embedding is very important for our problem, or the detail of proof, please refer to
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

X
is continuously embedded into
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V})
for any
q\in[2,\infty]
. Moreover, for any bounded sequence
\left\{u_{k}\right\}\subset X
, there exists
u\in X
such that, up to a subsequence,

\begin{cases}u_{k}\rightharpoonup u&\text{ in }\quad X,\\
u_{k}(x)\rightarrow u(x)&\forall x\in\mathbb{V},\\
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u&\text{ in }\quad\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V}).\end{cases}

To prove Theorem
1.1
, the following logarithmic Sobolev inequality is crucial for proving the boundedness of
(PS)
sequences when the potential is coercive.

Lemma 2.3
.

For all
u\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V})
such that
\lVert u\rVert_{2}=1
, there holds

(2.1)

\int_{\mathbb{V}}{u^{2}\log{u^{2}}}\,d\mu\leq 0.

Proof.

Let
u\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V})
such that
\lVert u\rVert_{2}=1
. Since
\lvert u(x)\rvert^{2}\leq\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{2}=1
for all
x\in\mathbb{V}
, we have
u^{2}(x)\log{u^{2}(x)}\leq 0
for any
x\in\mathbb{V}
and
u(x)\neq 0
. Thus

\int_{\mathbb{V}}{u^{2}\log{u^{2}}}\,d\mu\leq 0.

∎

It follows from (
2.1
) that, for any
u\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V})
with
\lVert u\rVert_{2}\neq 0
, we have

(2.2)

\int_{\mathbb{V}}u^{2}\log{u^{2}}\,d\mu\leq\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{2}\log{\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{2}}.

When the potential
V(x)
is bounded in
\mathbb{V}
, it is not necessary to need the logarithmic Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{V}
to prove the boundedness of
(PS)
sequences, here we only use the equivalence of spaces
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V})
and
X
. I think that this is a big difference between the the continuous case
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and the problem in graphs.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that the potential
V(x)
is bounded and satisfies
\inf_{\mathbb{V}}V(x)>-1
. If
\left(u_{n}\right)
is a sequence such that
\left(J\left(u_{n}\right)\right)
is bounded above and
J^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right)\rightarrow 0
, then
\left(u_{n}\right)
is bounded. Moreover, since
\Psi\geq 0,\left(J\left(u_{n}\right)\right)
is also bounded below and hence it is a
(PS)
sequence.

Proof.

Let
\left(u_{n}\right)\subset X
be a sequence with
\left(J\left(u_{n}\right)\right)
bounded above and
J^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right)\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Then, choosing
v=u_{n}
as test function, we have

(2.3)

\lVert u_{n}\rVert_{2}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{V}}{u_{n}^{2}}\,d\mu=2J\left(u_{n}\right)-\left\langle J^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right),u_{n}\right\rangle\leq C+o_{n}(1)\lVert u_{n}\rVert\leqslant C+o_{n}(1)\lVert u_{n}\rVert_{2},\quad\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

where we use the fact that the spaces
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V})
and
X
are equivalent, proving the first assertion. Then the second assertion immediately follows.
∎

Following the approach explored in
[
13
,
17
]
, due to the lack of smoothness of
J
, let us decompose it into a sum of a
C^{1}
functional and a convex lower semicontinuous functional. For
\delta>0
, let us define the following functions:

F_{1}(s)=\begin{cases}0,&s=0\\
-\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\log s^{2}&0<|s|<\delta\\
-\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\left(\log\delta^{2}+3\right)+2\delta|s|-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2},&|s|\geq\delta\end{cases}

and

F_{2}(s)=\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\log s^{2}+F_{1}(s),\quad s\in\mathbb{R}.

It was proved in
[
13
,
17
]
that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
verify the following properties:

F_{1},F_{2}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\text{. }

If
\delta>0
is small enough,
F_{1}
is convex, even,
F_{1}(s)\geq 0
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and

(2.4)

F_{1}^{\prime}(s)s\geq 0,s\in\mathbb{R}.

Fix
p\in(2,+\infty)
, there exists
C>0
such that

(2.5)

\left|F_{2}^{\prime}(s)\right|\leq C|s|^{p-1},\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

Now, we define

\Phi(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{V}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+(V(x)+1)\right)|u|^{2})d\mu-\int_{\mathbb{V}}F_{2}(u)d\mu,

and

\Psi(u)=\int_{\mathbb{V}}F_{1}(u)d\mu,

then

(2.6)

J(u)=\Phi(u)+\Psi(u),\quad u\in X.

Using the above information, it follows that
\Phi\in C^{1}\left(X,\mathbb{R}\right),\Psi
is convex and lower semicontinuous, but
\Psi
is not a
C^{1}
functional, in fact, it is not continuous in
X
. Due to this fact, we will look for a critical point in the sub-differential. Here we state some definitions that can be found in
[
19
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
E
be a Banach space,
E^{\prime}
be the dual space of
E
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be the duality paring between
E^{\prime}
and
E
. Let
J:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a functional of the form
J(u)=\Phi(u)+\Psi(u)
, where
\Phi\in C^{1}(E,\mathbb{R})
and
\Psi
is convex and lower semicontinuous. Let us list some definitions:

(i) The sub-differential
\partial J(u)
of the functional
J
at a point
u\in E
is the following set

\left\{w\in E^{\prime}:\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u),v-u\right\rangle+\Psi(v)-\Psi(u)\geq\langle w,v-u\rangle,\forall v\in E\right\}.

(ii) A critical point of
J
is a point
u\in E
such that
J(u)<+\infty
and
0\in\partial J(u)
, i.e.

\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u),v-u\right\rangle+\Psi(v)-\Psi(u)\geq 0,\forall v\in E.

(iii) A
(PS)
sequence at level
d
for
J
is a sequence
\left(u_{n}\right)\subset E
such that
J\left(u_{n}\right)\rightarrow d
and there is a numerical sequence
\tau_{n}\rightarrow 0^{+}
with

(2.7)

\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right),v-u_{n}\right\rangle+\Psi(v)-\Psi\left(u_{n}\right)\geq-\tau_{n}\left\|v-u_{n}\right\|,\forall v\in E.

(iv) The functional
J
satisfies the
(PS)
condition if all
(PS)
sequences has a convergent subsequence.

(v) The effective domain of
J
is the set
D(J)=\{u\in E:J(u)<+\infty\}
.

To proceed further we gather and state below some useful results that leads to a better understanding of the problem and of its particularities. In what follows, for each
u\in D\left(J\right)
, we set the functional
J^{\prime}(u):C_{c}(\mathbb{V})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

\left\langle J^{\prime}(u),z\right\rangle=\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u),z\right\rangle-\int_{\mathbb{V}}F_{1}^{\prime}(u)zd\mu,\quad\forall z\in C_{c}(\mathbb{V})

and define

Proposition 3.1

\displaystyle 2d\geq 2J\left(u_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\|u_{n}\right\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{V}}u_{n}^{2}\log u_{n}^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq\left\|u_{n}\right\|^{2}-\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}\log{\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}}\geq\left\|u_{n}\right\|^{2}-C_{1}\left(1+\left\|u_{n}\right\|^{r}\right),

where we take

Passing to a subsequence,

4.
The periodic case

First of all, we show that the functional

satisfies the mountain pass geometry
[

]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The functional
J
satisfies the following conditions:

(i) there exist
\alpha,\rho>0
such that
J(u)\geq\alpha
for any
u\in X
with
\|u\|=\rho
;

(ii) there exists
e\in X
with
\|e\|>\rho
such that
J(e)<0
.

Proof.

(i) Since
F_{1}(s)\geq 0
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

J(u)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{V}}F_{2}(u)d\mu.

Now, from (
2.5
) and Lemma
2.2
, fixed
p\in(2,+\infty)
, it follows that

J(u)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-C\|u\|^{p}\geq\alpha>0,

for some
\alpha>0
and
\|u\|=\rho>0
small enough.

(ii)Fix
u\in D(J)\backslash\{0\}
. Then we have

J(su)=J(u)s^{2}-s^{2}\log s\int_{\mathbb{V}}u^{2}d\mu=s^{2}\left(J(u)-\log{s}\int_{\mathbb{V}}u^{2}d\mu\right)

for all
s>0
. Thus,
J(su)\to-\infty
as
s\to+\infty
.
∎

From Lemma
4.1
, we can define

\Gamma:=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],X):\gamma(0)=0,J(\gamma(1))<0\}

and

c:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sup_{s\in[0,1]}J(\gamma(s)).

Now, let

\mathcal{N}:=\left\{u\in D(J)\backslash\{0\}:\left\langle J^{\prime}(u),u\right\rangle=0\right\}

be the Nehari manifold for
J
and

c_{\mathcal{N}}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}J(u).

Now we show the relation between the minimax level of
J
and ground state energy of
J
which is useful below.

Lemma 4.2
.

c=c_{\mathcal{N}}.

Proof.

Let
u\in D(J)\backslash\{0\}
. Then the map
s\mapsto J(su)
admits a unique maximum point on
(0,\infty)
. In fact, if
\varphi:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the map defined by

\varphi(s):=J(su)=J(u)s^{2}-s^{2}\log s\int_{\mathbb{V}}u^{2}d\mu,\quad s>0,

we have
\varphi(s)>0
for
s>0
sufficiently small and
\varphi(s)<0
for all
s>0
large enough. Moreover,
\varphi^{\prime}(s)=0
with
s>0
if and only if

(4.1)

J(u)=\frac{2\log s+1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{V}}u^{2}d\mu,

which proves the claim.

Let
u\in\mathcal{N}
and let us consider
J\left(t^{*}u\right)<0
, for some
t^{*}>0
. If
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow X
is the continuous path
\gamma(t)=t\cdot t^{*}u
, then

c\leq\max_{t\in[0,1]}J\left(\gamma(t)\right)\leq\max_{t\geq 0}J(tu)=J\left(tu\right)=J(u)

and consequently
c\leq c_{\mathcal{N}}
.

To prove the reverse inequality, by
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
, there exists a
(PS)
sequence
\left(u_{n}\right)\subset X
for
J
. By Lemma
2.4
, the sequence
\left(u_{n}\right)
is bounded in
X
. A key point in our approach is the following result

u_{n}\nrightarrow 0,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\ell^{2}(\mathbb{V}).

Indeed, on the contrary, by interpolation, we would have
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V})
, for some
p\in(2,+\infty)
, then, by (
2.5
) we have

(4.2)

\int_{\mathbb{V}}F_{2}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right)u_{n}d\mu\rightarrow 0.

On the other hand, by (
4.2
), we have

\left\|u_{n}\right\|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{V}}F_{1}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right)u_{n}d\mu=\left\langle J^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right)u_{n}\right\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{V}}F_{2}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right)u_{n}d\mu=o_{n}(1)\left\|u_{n}\right\|+\int_{\mathbb{V}}F_{2}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right)u_{n}d\mu=o_{n}(1),

from where it follows that
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
X
and
F_{1}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right)u_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{V})
. Since
F_{1}
is convex, even and
F(t)\geq F_{1}(0)=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we derive that
0\leq F_{1}(t)\leq F_{1}^{\prime}(t)t
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence,
F_{1}\left(u_{n}\right)\rightarrow 0
in
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{V})
, and so,
J\left(u_{n}\right)\rightarrow J(0)=0
, but this contradicts the fact that

. Hence there are constants

and
b
such that

(4.3)

0<a\leq\lVert u_{n}\rVert_{2}\leq b,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

For each
u_{n}
, let
t_{n}>0
be such that

. Recalling that

(4.4)

J\left(t_{n}u_{n}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{V}}\left|t_{n}u_{n}\right|^{2}d\mu,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

or equivalently

\int_{\mathbb{V}}\Big(\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{2}+\left(V(x)+1\right)\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}\Big)\ d\mu-\int_{\mathbb{V}}u_{n}^{2}\log\left|t_{n}u_{n}\right|^{2}d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{V}}\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}d\mu,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

and

\left\langle J^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right),u_{n}\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{V}}\Big(\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{2}+V(x)\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}\Big)d\mu-\int_{\mathbb{V}}u_{n}^{2}\log u_{n}^{2}d\mu=o_{n}(1),

we have that

This equality combines with (

. On the other hand, by (

Passing to the limit in this inequality, the reverse inequality

holds.

\tilde{u}_{n}\rightarrow\tilde{u}\,\,\text{ in }\,\,X

F_{1}^{\prime}\left(\tilde{u}_{n}\right)\tilde{u}_{n}\rightarrow F_{1}^{\prime}\left(\tilde{u}\right)\tilde{u}\,\,\text{ in }\,\,\ell^{1}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{N}\right).

Since

is convex, even and
F(0)=0

F_{1}^{\prime}(t)t\geq F_{1}(t)\geq 0
for all

. Thus, the last limit together with Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem yields

Thus,

is a critical point of

with

.

Finally, we show that the ground state solution

has the constant sign, that is,

for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
or
u(x)<0
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. First of all, since

for all

, we have

for all

, where
u^{+}=\max\{u,0\}
and
u^{-}=\min\{u,0\}
.

Let
u
be the ground state solution of (
1.3
). Argue by contradiction, suppose
u^{+},u^{-}\neq 0
. From (
4.4
), there exists unique
t_{\pm}
be such that
t_{\pm}u^{\pm}\in\mathcal{N}
. Recalling that

and
J(t_{\pm}u^{\pm})=\frac{t_{\pm}^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\lvert u^{\pm}\rvert^{2}d\mu
. Then we obtain

J(u^{\pm})=\frac{2\log t_{\pm}+1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\lvert u^{\pm}\rvert^{2}d\mu
. Thus

which is a contradiction. Thus,

does not change sign.

If
u(x_{0})=0
for some fixed
x_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
,
then (

) yields that

for all

is connected, we obtain

, which leads to a contradiction with

∎

Remark 4.3
.

In the continuous case
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, from
[

]
, we know that the ground state solution

Assume that

and

, then

. By direct computation,

and

. Moreover, let

be Nehari manifold and

The asymptotically periodic case and the potential well case

In this section, we firstly consider the existence of ground states for problem (

) with the asymptotically periodic potential

.
Let

\Gamma:=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],X):\gamma(0)=0,J(\gamma(1))<0\},

Clearly,

by
(V_{1})

-periodic potential

The energy functional corresponding to (

Now, we have the following important lemma which is useful below.

Lemma 5.1
.

On lattice graph
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
,

(i)

If

, then

, where

(ii)

after passing to a subsequence,

is
a
critical point of

(i) Since

Let

Thus,

c_{p}=J_{p}(u_{p})\geq J_{p}(s_{p}u_{p})>J(s_{p}u_{p})\geq c_{\mathcal{N}}.

(ii)By Lemma
2.4
,
\{u_{n}\}
is a bounded
(PS)

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=J\left(u_{n}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle J^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right),u_{n}\right\rangle+o(1)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}u_{n}^{2}d\mu+o(1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}u^{2}d\mu+o(1)=J(u)-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle J^{\prime}(u),u\right\rangle+o(1)=J(u)+o(1).

and it remains to show that

. Argue by contradiction, suppose

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle J^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right)-J_{p}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right),w\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\left(V_{p}(x)-V(x)\right)u_{n}^{2}d\mu\right)^{1/2}.

As the right-hand side tends to 0 uniformly in

Appendix

Example 6.8
.

Let
p>1
, using the fact

(6.5)

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n(\log n)^{q}}\begin{cases}<\infty,&\text{ if }q>1,\\
=\infty,&\text{ if }0\leq q\leq 1,\end{cases}

we can show that there exists
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})

with
\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\log\lvert u\rvert^{p}d\mu=-\infty
.

Let

e:=(1,0,...,0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},

and

u(x)=\begin{cases}|x|^{-\frac{1}{p}}(\log|x|)^{-\frac{2}{p}},&x=ne,n\geqslant 3,\\
0,&else.\end{cases}

Then we have

\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\lvert u\rvert^{p}d\mu=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}=\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n(\log n)^{2}}<\infty.

Thus
u\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. Similar to Lemma
2.1
, we have
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. However,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\log\lvert u\rvert^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\log\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle=-\left(\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n\log n}+\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{2\log(\log n)}{n(\log n)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\infty

where we use the fact (
6.5
) and
\frac{2\log(\log n)}{n(\log n)^{2}}>0
for
n\geq 3
.

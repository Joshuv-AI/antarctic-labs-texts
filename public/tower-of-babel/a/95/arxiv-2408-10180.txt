The Riemann-Liouville fractional integral in Bochner-Lebesgue spaces III
Paulo M. de Carvalho-Neto, Renato Fehlberg Júnior

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

Historically, Hardy and Littlewood (see
[
12
]
for details) were among the first to initiate discussions regarding the boundedness of the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of order
\alpha>0
on the Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1})
. They managed to obtain several interesting results that laid the foundation for this theory in Harmonic Analysis.

In recent years, the authors have begun to study the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of order
\alpha>0
as an operator over the Bochner-Lebesgue spaces (see
[
5
,
6
]
). This exploration was motivated by the existing gaps in the literature, which sometimes posed obstacles in other studies involving the theory of fractional calculus. In the author’s previous work
[
6
]
, they studied the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of order
\alpha\in(0,1/p)
, with
p>1
, from
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
into
L^{p/(1-p\alpha)}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. However, the critical case, where
p=1/\alpha
, remained unexplored in that work. Initially, one might reasonably expect the Riemann-Liouville integral of order
1/p
maps
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
into
L^{\infty}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. However, this expectation does not align with reality. While Hardy-Littlewood previously addressed this issue when
X=\mathbb{R}
, showing that the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of order
p=1/\alpha
from
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1})
into
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1})
, but not maps to
L^{\infty}(t_{0},t_{1})
, their discussion lacked a complete answer, that we can summarize as: what is the space in the intersection of the
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1})
spaces in which fractional integral of order
p=1/\alpha
would map
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1})
continuously? This is why we are now motivated to delve deeper into this matter. In this work, we establish that the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of order
1/p
is a bounded operator when considered from
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
into
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)\cap K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, for
\gamma\geq 1/p^{\prime}
. It is worth mentioning that there are older partial references on this subject, but they lack completeness (see Subsection
5
).

The other scenario, referred to here as the “Regular Case”, delves into the regularity of the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of order
\alpha\geq 1
from
L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
into the RL fractional Sobolev space denoted by
W^{s,p}_{RL}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. This case is obtained as a consequence of the author’s previous work
[
5
,
6
]
. It is important to emphasize that the concept of non-standard Sobolev spaces has been previously explored in the literature, particularly when
X=\mathbb{R}
; see, for instance,
[
3
,
4
]
.

To summarize the article’s organization, Section 2 introduces classical concepts related to Riemann-Liouville fractional integration and differentiation of order
\alpha>0
. In Section 3, we provid a description of
W^{s,p}_{RL}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, along with a proof for the “Regular Case”. Section 4, we describe the spaces
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and
K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, given some relation between this spaces. Section
5
, we study the “Critical Case” that discusses the critical aspect of the problem as previously explained. In Section 6 we address some open questions.

2.
On the Riemann-Liouville Fractional Integration

Throughout this manuscript, we assume that
t_{0}<t_{1}
are fixed real numbers, and
X
represents a Banach space. To ensure the completeness of this work, we revisit the definitions and notations of some specific classical vector spaces and operators. We begin presenting the classical Bochner-Lebesgue spaces (for further details, see
[
2
]
).

Definition 1
.

If
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we represent the set of all Bochner measurable functions
f:[t_{0},t_{1}]\rightarrow X
, for which
\|f(t)\|_{X}\in L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};\mathbb{R})
, by the symbol
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};{X})
. Moreover,
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};{X})
is a Banach space when considered with the norm

\|f\|_{L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bigg[\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{\|f(s)\|^{p}_{X}}\,ds\bigg]^{1/p},&\textrm{ if }p\in[1,\infty),\\
\esssup_{s\in[a,b]}\|f(s)\|_{X},&\textrm{ if }p=\infty.\end{array}\right.

We use the symbol
L_{loc}^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, to denote the set of functions that belong to
L^{p}(K;X)
for any compact
K\subset(t_{0},t_{1})
.

We also recall the Bochner-Sobolev spaces (for more details see
[
7
]
).

Definition 2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
and
p\geq 1
. The set
W^{k,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
denotes the subspace of
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
of every function
f:[t_{0},t_{1}]\rightarrow X
that has
k-
weak derivatives in
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. By considering the norm

\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}:=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\big\|f^{(j)}\big\|_{L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}

the set
W^{k,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
becomes a Banach space. For the completeness of the definition we assume that
W^{0,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)=L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
.

Now we present the notions of Riemann-Liouville fractional integral and derivative.

Definition 3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
and
f:[t_{0},t_{1}]\rightarrow{X}
. The Riemann-Liouville (RL for short) fractional integral of order
\alpha
at
t_{0}
of function
f
is defined by

(1)

J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f(t):=\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}f(s)}\,ds,

for every
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
such that integral (
2
) exists. Above
\Gamma
denotes the classical Euler’s gamma function.

Remark 4
.

We emphasize the following properties of the RL fractional integral:

(i)

For
\alpha>0
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, it holds that

\left\|J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f(t)\right\|_{L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\leq\left[\dfrac{(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\right]\|f\|_{L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}.

In other words,
J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}
is a bounded linear operator from
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
into itself. For details see
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
.

(ii)

For
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, we have that

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\big\|J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f-f\big\|_{L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}}=0.

This is the reason we define
J_{t_{0},t}^{0}f(t):=f(t)
. For details see
[
5
, Theorem 3.10]
.

(iii)

For
1\leq p<\infty
we have that
\{J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}:\alpha\geq 0\}\subset\mathcal{L}(L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X))
defines a
C_{0}
-semigroup. For details see
[
5
, Theorem 3.15]
.

Definition 5
.

Assume that
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
and consider
f:[t_{0},t_{1}]\rightarrow{X}
. The Riemann-Liouville (RL for short) fractional derivative of order
\alpha
at
t_{0}
of function
f
is defined by

(2)

D_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f(t):=\dfrac{d^{[\alpha]}}{dt^{[\alpha]}}\left[J_{t_{0},t}^{[\alpha]-\alpha}f(t)\right]=\dfrac{d^{[\alpha]}}{dt^{[\alpha]}}\left[\dfrac{1}{\Gamma([\alpha]-\alpha)}\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}{(t-s)^{[\alpha]-\alpha-1}f(s)}\,ds\right],

for every
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
such that the right side of (
2
) exists. Above the derivative is considered in the weak sense and the symbol
[\cdot]
is used to denote the least integer greater than
\alpha
.

Remark 6
.

(i)

If
1\leq p<\infty
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in W^{k,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, follows from the definition of RL fractional derivative and item
(ii)
of Remark
4
that

D_{t_{0},t}^{k}f(t)=\dfrac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[J_{t_{0},t}^{0}f(t)\right]=f^{(k)}(t),

for a.e.
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
.

(ii)

By item
(iii)
of Remark
4
, if
0<\beta\leq\alpha
and
f\in L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
then

D_{t_{0},t}^{\beta}\left[J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f(t)\right]=J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha-\beta}f(t),

for a.e.
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
.

3.
The Boundedness of RL Fractional Integral - Regular Case

In this short section we improve the result cited in Remark
4
. For this purpose, we introduce the space
W_{RL}^{\alpha,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
.

Definition 7
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
p\geq 1
. The set
W_{RL}^{\alpha,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
denotes the subspace of
W^{[\alpha]-1,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
which is composed of every function
f:[t_{0},t_{1}]\rightarrow X
such that
D_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f\in L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
.

Proposition 8
.

By considering the norm

\|f\|_{W_{RL}^{\alpha,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}:=\|f\|_{W^{[\alpha]-1,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}+\|D_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)},

the set
W_{RL}^{\alpha,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
becomes a Banach space. We call
W_{RL}^{\alpha,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
the RL fractional Sobolev spaces.

Proof.

If
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset W_{RL}^{\alpha,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
is a Cauchy sequence, then there should exist
g\in W^{[\alpha]-1,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and
h\in L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
such that
\phi_{j}\rightarrow g
in the topology of
W^{[\alpha]-1,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and
D_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}\phi_{j}\rightarrow h
in the topology of
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. Hence, item
(i)
of Remark
4
ensures that
J_{t_{0},t}^{[\alpha]-\alpha}\phi_{j}\rightarrow J_{t_{0},t}^{[\alpha]-\alpha}g
in the topology of
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. Finally, the definition of weak derivative and a classical argument of convergence allows us to conclude that
D_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}g(t)=h(t)
for a.e.
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
, proving that
g\in W_{RL}^{\alpha,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and
\phi_{j}\rightarrow g
in the topology of
W_{RL}^{\alpha,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, as we wanted.
∎

Remark 9
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\geq 1
, we have that
W_{RL}^{\alpha,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)=W^{\alpha,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and
\|\cdot\|_{W_{RL}^{\alpha,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}=\|\cdot\|_{W^{\alpha,p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}
. Just recall item
(i)
of Remark
6
.

As a consequence of Remarks
4
and
6
, we obtain:

Theorem 10
.

If
\alpha\in[1,\infty)
, then
J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}:L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\rightarrow W^{\alpha,1}_{RL}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
is a bounded linear operator.

Proof.

Observe that the assertion
(ii)
in Remark
6
guarantees that

\dfrac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f(t)\right]=J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha-k}f(t),

is valid for every
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,[\alpha]-1\}
. Therefore, since item
(i)
of Remark
4
ensures that
J_{t_{0},t}^{\beta}:L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\rightarrow L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
is bounded for any
\beta>0
, it follows that
J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f(t)\in W^{[\alpha]-1,1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. Again, from item
(ii)
of Remark
6
, note that

D_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}\left[J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f(t)\right]=f(t)\in L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X).

Therefore we have that
J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f\in W^{\alpha,1}_{RL}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
.

Finally, from item
(i)
of Remark
4
we have that

\|J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f\|_{W^{\alpha,1}_{RL}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}=\sum_{k=0}^{[\alpha]-1}\left\|\dfrac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f\right]\right\|_{L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}+\left\|D_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}\left[J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}f\right]\right\|_{L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\\
=\sum_{k=0}^{[\alpha]-1}\left\|J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha-k}f\right\|_{L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\leq\left[1+\sum_{k=0}^{[\alpha]-1}\dfrac{(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\alpha-k}}{\Gamma(\alpha-k+1)}\right]\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}.

In other words,
J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}:L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\rightarrow W^{\alpha,1}_{RL}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
is a bounded linear operator.
∎

We conclude this section by showing that the space
W^{\alpha,1}_{RL}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
is sharp in the following sense:

Theorem 11
.

If
\alpha\in[1,\infty)
,
\eta_{1}\in[\alpha,\infty)
and
\eta_{2}\in[1,\infty)
are such that
\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}>\alpha+1
, then
J_{t_{0},t}^{\alpha}\big(L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\big)\not\subset W^{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}_{RL}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
.

Proof.

Assume, without loss of generality, that
t_{0}=0
,
t_{1}=1
and
X=\mathbb{R}
.

(i)

Observe that
J_{0,t}^{\alpha}\big(L^{1}(0,1;\mathbb{R})\big)\not\subset W^{\alpha,\eta_{2}}_{RL}(0,1;\mathbb{R})
if
\eta_{2}>1
, since function
f(t)=t^{\gamma}
, for
\gamma=-(\eta_{2}+1)/(2\eta_{2})
, belongs to
L^{1}(0,1;\mathbb{R})
however
D_{0,t}^{\alpha}\left[J_{0,t}^{\alpha}f(t)\right]=t^{\gamma}\not\in L^{\eta_{2}}(0,1;\mathbb{R}).

(ii)

If
\alpha<\eta_{1}\leq 2
and
f(t)=t^{\gamma}
, with
-1<\gamma<\eta_{1}-\alpha-1,

then:

(a)

f\in L^{1}(0,1;\mathbb{R})
, since
\gamma+1>0
;

(b)

Item
(i)
of Remark
4
ensures that
J_{0,t}^{\alpha-1}f\in L^{1}(0,1;\mathbb{R})
. Thus, item
(ii)
of Remark
6
ensures that
(d/dt)J_{0,t}^{\alpha}f=J_{0,t}^{\alpha-1}f
, that is,
(d/dt)J_{0,t}^{\alpha}f\in L^{1}(0,1;\mathbb{R})
;

(c)

Since
0<1-\eta_{1}+\alpha<\gamma+2-\eta_{1}+\alpha<1
, we have

\hskip 28.45274ptD_{0,t}^{\eta_{1}}\big[J_{0,t}^{\alpha}f\big]=(d^{2}/dt^{2})\big[J_{0,t}^{2-\eta_{1}+\alpha}t^{\gamma}\big]=M_{1}(d^{2}/dt^{2})t^{\gamma+2-\eta_{1}+\alpha}=M_{2}\,t^{\gamma-\eta_{1}+\alpha},

for some
M_{1},M_{2}>0
. Therefore we have that
D_{0,t}^{\eta_{1}}\big[J_{0,t}^{\alpha}f\big]\not\in L^{1}(0,1;\mathbb{R})
, since
1+\gamma-\eta_{1}+\alpha<0
.

In other words,
J_{0,t}^{\alpha}\big(L^{1}(0,1;\mathbb{R})\big)\not\subset W^{\eta_{1},1}_{RL}(0,1;\mathbb{R})
.

(iii)

If
\max\{\alpha,2\}<\eta_{1}
, then
[\eta_{1}]-1\geq 2
and therefore
W^{\eta_{1},1}_{RL}(0,1;\mathbb{R})\subset W^{2,1}(0,1;\mathbb{R})
. Hence,
J_{0,t}^{\alpha}\big(L^{1}(0,1;\mathbb{R})\big)\not\subset W^{\eta_{1},1}_{RL}(0,1;\mathbb{R})
, since otherwise
J_{0,t}^{\alpha}\big(L^{1}(0,1;\mathbb{R})\big)\subset W^{2,1}_{RL}(0,1;\mathbb{R})
, which would contradicts item
(ii)
.

(iv)

Finally, for
\alpha<\eta_{1}
and
1<\eta_{2}
, since
W^{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}_{RL}(0,1;\mathbb{R})\subset W^{\eta_{1},1}_{RL}(0,1;\mathbb{R}),

we cannot have
J_{0,t}^{\alpha}\big(L^{1}(0,1;\mathbb{R})\big)\subset W^{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}_{RL}(0,1;\mathbb{R})
, since otherwise
J_{0,t}^{\alpha}\big(L^{1}(0,1;\mathbb{R})\big)\subset W^{\eta_{1},1}_{RL}(0,1;\mathbb{R})
, which would contradicts items
(ii)
or
(iii)
.

∎

4.
Some Special Spaces

In this section, we present the classical BMO space and introduce the
K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
space. To justify studying the operator
J^{\alpha}
from
L^{p}
into these spaces, we first need to understand their relation to the
L^{p}
spaces. This is our main objective here.

4.1.
The BMO space

We begin with the spaces introduced by F. John and L. Nirenberg in
[
17
]
, here adapted to vector-valued functions.

Definition 12
.

If
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and

[f]_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}:=\sup_{[a,b]\subset(t_{0},t_{1})}{\left[\dfrac{1}{(b-a)}\int_{a}^{b}{\|f(s)-\operatorname{avg}_{[a,b]}(f)\|_{X}}\,ds\right]}<\infty,

where

\operatorname{avg}_{[a,b]}(f):=\dfrac{1}{(b-a)}\int_{a}^{b}{f(s)}\,ds,

we say that
f
is a vector-valued function whose mean oscillation is bounded (finite). We denote the vector space of all those functions by
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)
.

Functions in
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)
are important in harmonic analysis and partial differential equations, occasionally serving as a substitute for the space
L^{\infty}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. Not being different here, this space ends up demonstrating great importance in our discussion.

Remark 13
.

Notice that
[\,\cdot\,]_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}
does not define a norm in
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, since for any vector
x\in X
we have that

[x]_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}=0.

Proposition 14
.

It holds that
L^{\infty}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\subsetneq BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)

Proof.

The definition of
[\,\cdot\,]_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}
readily implies, for any
f\in L^{\infty}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, the inequality

[f]_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\leq 2\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(t_{0},t_{1};X)},

establishing the inclusion
L^{\infty}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\subset BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. To prove the strictness of the inclusion, we provide an adaptation of
[
14
]
. Suppose that
t_{0}=0
and
t_{1}=1
. Consider
x\in X\setminus\{0\}
and
\phi:(0,1)\rightarrow X
given by
\phi(t)=\big(\log{t}\big)x
. Clearly
\phi\not\in L^{\infty}(0,1;X)
. On the other hand, for
\varepsilon>1
and
[a,a\varepsilon]\subset(0,1)
, we have that

\hskip 65.44142pt\dfrac{\|x\|_{X}}{(a\varepsilon-a)}\int_{a}^{a\varepsilon}{\big|\phi(s)-\operatorname{avg}_{[a,a\varepsilon]}(\phi)\big|}\,ds\\
=\|x\|_{X}\underbrace{\left(\dfrac{2}{e}\right)\left[\dfrac{(\varepsilon-1)\varepsilon^{\varepsilon/(\varepsilon-1)}-e[\varepsilon\log\varepsilon]}{(\varepsilon-1)^{2}}\right]}_{=\eta(\varepsilon)}.

Observing that function
\eta(\varepsilon)
is both monotonically increasing and continuous in
(1,\infty)
, it follows that

\hskip 65.44142pt[\phi]_{BMO(0,1;X)}=\|x\|_{X}\sup{\{\eta(\varepsilon):\varepsilon>1\textrm{ such that }[a,a\varepsilon]\subset(0,1)\}}\\
=\|x\|_{X}\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow\infty}\eta(\varepsilon)=(2/e)\|x\|_{X}<\infty.\vskip 5.69046pt

∎

Since
L^{\infty}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\subset L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, a natural question that arises is the relation between BMO spaces and
L^{p}
spaces.

Remark 15
.

As demonstrated by the Johnson-Neugebauer example (refer to
[
18
, Remark (2) of Theorem 3]
for more details), which is given by

\phi(t)=\max\{\log(1/t),1/(\log(te^{2}))\},

we can observe that
\phi\in BMO(0,1;\mathbb{R})
but
\phi\not\in L^{p}(0,1;\mathbb{R})
. Therefore, we obtain
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)\nsubseteq L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, for
1\leq p<\infty
.

Despite the previous Remark, if we consider
L_{\text{loc}}^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
we have:

Proposition 16
.

It holds that
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)\subset\bigcap_{r\geq 1}L_{\text{loc}}^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X).

Proof.

For any interval
[a,b]\subset(t_{0},t_{1})
and
f\in BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, it holds that:

\big|\{s\in[a,b]:\|f(s)-\operatorname{avg}_{[a,b]}(f)\|_{X}>\lambda\}\big|\leq c_{1}(b-a)e^{-c_{2}\lambda/\|f\|_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}},

for
c_{1},c_{2}>0
(see
[
17
]
). From this inequality, we can prove that

(3)

\hskip 28.45274pt\int_{a}^{b}\|f(s)-\operatorname{avg}_{[a,b]}(f)\|^{r}_{X}\,ds\leq(t_{1}-t_{0})c_{1}r\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{r-1}e^{-c_{2}s/\|f\|_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}}\,ds\\
=(t_{1}-t_{0})c_{1}\left(\dfrac{\|f\|_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}}{c_{2}}\right)^{r}\Gamma(r+1),

for any
r\geq 1
. Furthermore, for any compact set
K\subset(t_{0},t_{1})
, there exists an interval
[a,b]\subset(t_{0},t_{1})
such that
K\subset[a,b].
Consequently, for
r\geq 1
, we can conclude:

\hskip 28.45274pt\|f\|_{L^{r}(K;X)}\leq\|f-\operatorname{avg}_{[a,b]}(f)\|_{L^{r}(a,b;X)}+\|\operatorname{avg}_{[a,b]}(f)\|_{L^{r}(a,b;X)}\\
\leq\dfrac{\big[(t_{1}-t_{0})c_{1}\Gamma(r+1)\big]^{1/r}\|f\|_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}}{c_{2}}+(b-a)^{(1/r)-1}\|f\|_{L^{1}(a,b;X)}<\infty,

This implies that
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)\subset\bigcap_{r\geq 1}L_{\text{loc}}^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X).

∎

To relate
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and

spaces, we have:

Theorem 17
.

It holds that
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)\cap L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\subsetneq\bigcap_{r\geq 1}L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. Moreover, for any

and

there exists

such that

(4)

\|f\|_{L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\leq Cr\|f\|^{1/r}_{L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\|f\|^{1-(1/r)}_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}.

Proof.

The proof of the inclusion and the inequality are simply adaptations of a classical result (see

, Theorem 2]
).

Now to prove the strictness of the inclusion, assume without loss of generality that
t_{0}=0
,
t_{1}=1
and
x\in X\setminus\{0\}
. Then consider function

where

denotes the characteristic function of

Remark 18
.

We can summarize the above results as:

L^{\infty}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\subsetneq BMO(0,1;X)\cap L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\subsetneq\bigcap_{r\geq 1}L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X).

4.2.
The
K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
space

Now we introduce a vector-valued version of a function space, which was approached by Karapetyants and Rubin in
[
19
,
20
]
. These are the oldest references we have found.

Definition 19
.

For
\gamma>0
, the Karapetyants-Rubin space (KR-space, for short) is the collection of functions
f\in\bigcap_{r\geq 1}L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
that satisfy

\sup\{r^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}:r\geq 1\}<\infty.

We denote this space by
K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
.

From the above definition, several noteworthy aspects become apparent. It is crucial to emphasize that all the subsequent proofs and conclusions, to the best of the author’s knowledge, have not been previously proved in the literature.

Theorem 20
.

In
K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, if we consider

\|f\|_{K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}:=\sup\{r^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}:r\geq 1\},

then it becomes a Banach space.

Proof.

It is not difficult to conclude that
K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
is a normed vector space. Now consider the Cauchy sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. Observe that for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\geq 1
we have

\|f_{j}\|_{L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}=r^{\gamma}\big[r^{-\gamma}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\big]\leq r^{\gamma}\|f_{j}\|_{K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)},

what allows us to deduce that
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. Due to the arbitrary nature of
r\geq 1
and the boundedness of the interval
(t_{0},t_{1})
, we deduce the existence of
f\in\bigcap_{r\geq 1}L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
such that
f_{j}\rightarrow f
in
L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, for each
r\geq 1
. Consequently, we have that

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}r^{-\gamma}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}=r^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)},

for each
r\geq 1
. On the other hand, since
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and

\|f_{j}\|_{K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}=\sup\{r^{-\gamma}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}:r\geq 1\},

we deduce that
\{r^{-\gamma}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
B([1,\infty);\mathbb{R})
. Therefore, there exists
M\in B([1,\infty);\mathbb{R})
such that

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\Big[\sup\big\{\big|r^{-\gamma}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}-M(r)\big|:r\geq 1\big\}\Big]=0.

But then

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}r^{-\gamma}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}=M(r),

for each
r\geq 1
, and therefore
M(r)=r^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}
, what completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 21
.

For
0<\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}
, we have that
K_{\gamma_{1}}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\subsetneq K_{\gamma_{2}}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
continuously.

Proof.

The inclusion of spaces is proven straightforwardly, and the strictness of this inclusion is a direct consequence of the following Lemmas.
∎

It is important to note that, in the following results, we assume that
\psi_{0}(z)
and
\psi_{1}(z)
represent the analytical Digamma and Trigamma functions, respectively. For more comprehensive information on these functions, we refer to
[
1
]
.

Lemma 22
.

For
\zeta\geq 1
, consider
\rho_{\zeta}:[1,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

\rho_{\zeta}(s)=\log\big(\Gamma(s)\big)-(s-1)\big[\psi_{0}(s)-\zeta\big].

Then
\rho_{\zeta}(s)>0
for every
t>1
.

Proof.

Observe that
\rho_{\zeta}^{\prime}(s)=\zeta-(s-1)\psi_{1}(s)
. Now, since Guo-Qi established in
[
11
, Lemma 2]
that
\psi_{1}(s)<e^{1/s}-1
for every
s>1
, we can straightforwardly deduce that
\psi_{1}(s)<1/(s-1)
for every
s>1
. Therefore, for any
\zeta\geq 1
we have that
\psi_{1}(s)<\zeta/(s-1)
for every
s>1
, what allows us to conclude that
\rho_{\zeta}(s)
strictly increases in
(1,\infty)
. Given that
\rho_{\zeta}(1)=0
, we conclude that
\rho_{\zeta}(s)>0
for all
s>1
.
∎

Lemma 23
.

For
x\in X
and
\gamma>0
, define function
\phi_{\gamma}:(0,1)\rightarrow X
by

\phi_{\gamma}(t)=\big[\log{(1/t)}\big]^{\gamma}x.

Then
\phi_{\gamma}\in\bigcap_{r\geq 1}L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and

\|\phi_{\gamma}\|_{K_{\sigma}(0,1;X)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Gamma(\gamma+1),&\textrm{ if }\sigma\geq\gamma,\\
\infty,&\textrm{ if }0<\sigma<\gamma.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

A direct computation shows that
\phi_{\gamma}\in\bigcap_{r\geq 1}L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. For
\sigma>0
, consider function
\upsilon_{\sigma}:[1,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, given by
\upsilon_{\sigma}(s)=s^{-\sigma}\big[\Gamma(s\gamma+1)\big]^{1/s}
.

When
\sigma\geq\gamma
, since
\upsilon_{\sigma}^{\prime}(s)=-s^{-\sigma-2}\big[\Gamma(s\gamma+1)\big]^{1/s}\rho_{\sigma/\gamma}(s\gamma+1)
(where
\rho
is given in Lemma
22
), Lemma
22
ensures that
\upsilon_{\sigma}(s)
is decreasing in
(1,\infty)
. From this we may verify that

\|\phi_{\gamma}\|_{K_{\sigma}(0,1;X)}=\|x\|_{X}\sup\{r^{-\sigma}\big[\Gamma(r\gamma+1)\big]^{1/r}:r\geq 1\}=\Gamma(\gamma+1)<\infty.

On the other hand, when
0<\sigma<\gamma
, the lower bound of the Gamma function (for details we refer to
[
13
]
) allows us to conclude that there exists
C>0
such that

\hskip 31.2982pt\|\phi_{\gamma}\|_{K_{\gamma}(0,1;X)}\geq s^{-\sigma}\big[\Gamma(\gamma s+1)\big]^{1/s}\|x\|\geq Cs^{\gamma-\sigma},

for sufficiently large values of
s\geq 1
. Therefore
\|\phi\|_{K_{\gamma}(0,1;X)}=\infty.

∎

Since, by definition,
K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\subset L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, a natural question that arises is the relation with

.

Theorem 24
.

For all
\gamma>0
,
L^{\infty}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\subsetneq K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
.

Proof.

The inclusion of the spaces is straightforwardly proven. Now the strictness of this inclusion is a direct consequence of Lemma

.
∎

To conclude our discussion in this subsection, let us now delve into the relationship between the spaces
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)\cap L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and
K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)

and are properly contained in

Theorem 25
.

For
\gamma\geq 1
,
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)\cap L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\subsetneq K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)\cap L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and

. Then (

which allow us to deduce that

\sup\{r^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}:r\geq 1\}\leq C(1+\|f\|_{L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)})(1+\|f\|_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)})<\infty,

which implies that

.

To prove that
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)\cap L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
is strictly contained in

, let us assume without loss of generality that
t_{0}=0
and
t_{1}=1
. Now let

and define

as follows:

where

∎

Corollary 26
.

For
0<\gamma<1
, it holds that constant functions belong to

and
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)\cap L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)

BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)\cap L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)

and similarly,

.

Let us assume
t_{0}=0
and

. Consider an arbitrary element

Theorem 27
.

For
0<\gamma<1
we have that
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)\cap K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
is a Banach space when considered with the norm

\|\phi\|_{\gamma}:=\|\phi\|_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}+\|\phi\|_{K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}.

Proof.

A direct computation shows that
\|\cdot\|_{\gamma}
defines a norm. Let us assume that
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)\cap K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
is a Cauchy sequence with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\gamma}
. Then, it follows from Theorem
20
that there exists
f\in K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
such that
\phi_{j}\rightarrow f
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}
. Now, since we have

\|\phi_{j}-f\|_{L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\leq\|\phi_{j}-f\|_{K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)},

we can deduce that the convergence
\phi_{j}\rightarrow f
in

implies that there exists
\{\phi_{j_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
such that
\phi_{j_{k}}(t)\rightarrow f(t)
for almost every
t\in(t_{0},t_{1})
.

Now, by recalling
[
16
, Remarks 1.5 and 1.6]
, we know that
BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)
modulo the space of constant functions is a Banach space. Therefore, since
\{\phi_{j_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}
, there exists
g\in BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)
such that
\phi_{j_{k}}\rightarrow g
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}
. Then, if we take
[a,b]\subset(t_{0},t_{1})
, thanks to (
3
), there exists
\{\phi_{j_{k_{l}}}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}\subset\{\phi_{j_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that

(5)

\phi_{j_{k_{l}}}(t)-\operatorname{avg}_{[a,b]}(\phi_{j_{k_{l}}})\rightarrow g(t)-\operatorname{avg}_{[a,b]}(g)

for almost every

. Therefore, (
5
), the fact that
\phi_{j_{k}}\rightarrow f
in
L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and also
\phi_{j_{k}}(t)\rightarrow f(t)
for almost every

f(t)-\operatorname{avg}_{[a,b]}(f)=g(t)-\operatorname{avg}_{[a,b]}(g)

for almost every
t\in[a,b]
. Since

was an arbitrary closed interval contained in
(t_{0},t_{1})
, we can conclude that
\phi_{j_{k}}\rightarrow f
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}
. From this last argument we may infer that
\phi_{j_{k}}\rightarrow f
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\gamma}

\phi_{j}\rightarrow f
with respect to

5.
Boundeness of the RL Fractional Integral - The Critical Case

In this section, we address the continuity of the RL fractional integral operator of order
\alpha=1/p
in
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, specifically when
p>1
. Since the existing literature on this subject is sparse and incomplete, we dedicate this section to shed as much light as possible on this topic.

Notably, for the Riesz fractional integral
I^{\alpha}
, Stein and Zygmund in
[
23
]
have already established the continuity of
I^{\alpha}
from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Other contributors to this line of study include Muckenhoupt-Wheeden in
[
15
]
, Samko in
[
22
]
and Rafeiro-Samko in
[
21
]
.

On the other hand, when considering the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral, references become more restricted and obscure. The primary reference (known to the authors) on this topic is the classical book
[
9
]
by Edmunds, Kokilashvili, and Meskhi. In Section 2.15 of their book, the authors mention that the proof of the continuity of the RL fractional integral with order
\alpha=1/p
from
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};\mathbb{R})
into
K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};\mathbb{R})
, is given in
[
19
]
. However, despite thorough investigation, we were unable to locate this particular article.

Nonetheless, we did find a work,
[
20
]
, presenting statements akin to those mentioned by Edmunds, Kokilashvili, and Meskhi in their book and authored by the same individuals they cite; namely, N. K. Karapetiantz and B. S. Rubin. This work could serve as an official reference on this topic. It is essential to note that, while this source describes some intriguing results, it does not provide any proofs.

With that in mind, in what follows, we not only establish some of the results presented by Karapetiantz and Rubin in their work
[
20
]
, extending them to vector-valued functions, but we also refine these findings to derive a novel result concerning the continuity of the RL fractional integral.

With that in mind, in what follows, we not only proof some of the results presented by Karapetiantz and Rubin in their work [19] but also provide a completely new approach compared to the one suggested by them (interpolation results from
[
6
, Theorem 5]
). Furthermore, we extend these results to vector-valued functions and refine them to derive a novel result concerning the continuity of the RL fractional integral.

Theorem 28
.

For
p>1
and
q\geq 1
, it holds that

(6)

\|J_{t_{0},t}^{1/p}f\|_{L^{q}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\leq C(p,q)(t_{1}-t_{0})^{1/q}\|f\|_{L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)},

for every
f\in L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, where

(7)

C(p,q)=\dfrac{2p^{(p+(p/q)-(1/q))/(p+q-1)}(q-1)^{(q-1)(1-(1/p))/(p+q-1)}}{\Gamma(1/p)}.

Moreover, it holds that
J_{t_{0},t}^{1/p}\big(L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\big)\not\subset L^{\infty}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. We observe that
[
6
, item
(iii)
of Remark 4]
ensures

(8)

\left\|J^{1/p}_{t_{0},t}f\right\|_{L^{q}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\leq p^{1/q}(t_{1}-t_{0})^{1/pq}\left[J^{1/p}_{t_{0},t}f\right]_{L_{w}^{pq/(p-1)}(t_{0},t_{1};X)},

where
{L_{w}^{pq/(p-1)}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}
denotes the weak Lebesgue space (see
[
6
, item
(iv)
of Remark 4]
for details). Also note that
[
6
, Corollary 1]
ensures that

(9)

\left[J^{1/p}_{t_{0},t}f\right]_{L_{w}^{pq/(p-1)}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\\
\leq\left[\dfrac{2p^{(p-1)/(p+q-1)}(q-1)^{(q-1)(1-(1/p))/(p+q-1)}}{\Gamma(1/p)}\right]\|f\|_{L^{pq/(p+q-1)}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}.

Hence, (
8
), (
9
), and the continuous inclusion of
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
in
L^{pq/(p+q-1)}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
show that
(
6
) holds.

The proof that
J_{t_{0},t}^{1/p}
does not maps the space
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
into
L^{\infty}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
is an adaptation of an argument by Hardy and Littlewood; see
[
12
, p. 578]
for details.
∎

The last theorem demonstrated that the RL fractional integral of order
1/p
maps
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
into
\cap_{r\geq 1}L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
but not into
L^{\infty}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. This leads us to inquire about a Banach space strictly between
L^{\infty}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and
\cap_{r\geq 1}L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and where we can establish that the RL fractional integral of order
1/p
indeed maps
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
into this space.

Theorem 29
.

For
p>1
, it holds that
J^{1/p}_{t_{0},t}:L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\rightarrow BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)
and that

[J_{t_{0},t}^{1/p}f]_{BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\leq\dfrac{4}{\Gamma\big((1/p)+1\big)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)},

for every
f\in L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
.

Proof.

Let
[a,b]\subset(t_{0},t_{1})
and notice that

(10)

\left[\dfrac{1}{b-a}\right]\int_{a}^{b}\left\|J_{t_{0},t}^{1/p}f(t)-\operatorname{avg}_{[a,b]}(J_{t_{0},s}^{1/p}f)\right\|_{X}\,dt\\
\leq\left[\dfrac{1}{(b-a)^{2}}\right]\int_{a}^{b}\int_{a}^{b}\left\|J_{t_{0},t}^{1/p}f(t)-J_{t_{0},s}^{1/p}f(s)\right\|_{X}\,ds\,dt\leq\left[\dfrac{2}{(b-a)}\right]\int_{a}^{b}\left\|J_{t_{0},t}^{1/p}f(t)\right\|_{X}\,dt.

Now observe that

(11)

\int_{a}^{b}\left\|\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(1/p)}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(t-s)^{(1/p)-1}f(s)\,ds\right\|_{X}\,dt\\
\leq\int_{a}^{b}\left\|\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(1/p)}\int_{t_{0}}^{a}(t-s)^{(1/p)-1}f(s)\,ds\right\|_{X}\,dt\\
+\int_{a}^{b}\left\|\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(1/p)}\int_{a}^{t}(t-s)^{(1/p)-1}f(s)\,ds\right\|_{X}\,dt=:\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J}.

It is not difficult to see that Fubini-Tonelli Theorem, Hölder’s inequality and the continuous inclusion of
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
into
L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
ensure that

\mathcal{I}\leq\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(1/p)}\int_{t_{0}}^{a}\left[\int_{a}^{b}(t-s)^{(1/p)-1}\,dt\right]\|f(s)\|_{X}\,ds\\
=\dfrac{1}{\Gamma((1/p)+1)}\int_{t_{0}}^{a}\left[(b-s)^{(1/p)}-(a-s)^{(1/p)}\right]\|f(s)\|_{X}\,ds\\
\leq\dfrac{(b-a)^{1/p}}{\Gamma((1/p)+1)}\|f\|_{L^{1}(a,b;X)}\leq\dfrac{(b-a)}{\Gamma((1/p)+1)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(a,b;X)}.

On the other hand, it follows from item
(i)
of Remark
4
and the continuous inclusion of
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
into
L^{1}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
that

\mathcal{J}\leq\dfrac{(b-a)^{1/p}}{\Gamma((1/p)+1)}\|f\|_{L^{1}(a,b;X)}\leq\dfrac{(b-a)}{\Gamma((1/p)+1)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(a,b;X)}.

Therefore, (
10
) and (
11
) allow us to deduce that

\dfrac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}\left\|J_{t_{0},t}^{1/p}f(t)-\operatorname{avg}_{[a,b]}(J_{t_{0},s}^{1/p}f)\right\|_{X}\,dt\\
\leq\dfrac{4}{\Gamma\big((1/p)+1\big)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)},

as we wanted.

∎

Theorem 30
.

Let
p>1
and
\gamma\geq(p-1)/p
. Then
J^{1/p}_{t_{0},t}:L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\rightarrow K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
is a bounded linear operator, i.e., there exists
M>0
such that

\|J_{t_{0},t}^{1/p}f\|_{K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\leq M\|f\|_{L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)},

for every
f\in L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
.

Proof.

Consider
h:(1,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

h(t)=\dfrac{e^{[(1-(1/p))(t-1)/(p+t-1)]\log(t-1)}}{t^{(p-1)/p}},

and observe that
h(t)
is continuous and that

\lim_{t\rightarrow 1^{+}}h(t)=1\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}h(t)=0.

Therefore
h(t)
is bounded in
(1,\infty)
. Hence, since for
f\in L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
, it follows from from (
6
) and (
7
) that

r^{-{(p-1)/p}}\|J_{t_{0},t}^{1/p}f\|_{L^{r}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\\
\leq 2p^{(p+(p/r)-(1/r))/(p+r-1)}\left(\dfrac{(t_{1}-t_{0})^{1/r}}{\Gamma(1/p)}\right)h(r)\|f\|_{L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)},

for any
r\geq 1
, we deduce the existence of
M\geq 0
such that

\|J_{t_{0},t}^{1/p}f\|_{K_{(p-1)/p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\leq M\|f\|_{L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)},

proving the continuity of the RL fractional integral of order
1/p
from
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
into
K_{(p-1)/p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. To obtain the general case we just apply Theorem
21
.

∎

Our final and main result can now be stated as a direct consequence of Theorems
29
and
30
.

Theorem 31
.

Let
p>1
and
\gamma\geq(p-1)/p
. Then

J^{1/p}_{t_{0},t}:L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\rightarrow BMO(t_{0},t_{1};X)\cap K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)

is a bounded linear operator, i.e., there exists
K>0
such that

\|J_{t_{0},t}^{1/p}f\|_{K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)}\leq K\|f\|_{\gamma},

for every
f\in L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
.

6.
Open Problems

An intriguing question that arose during the proof of Theorem
28
is the task of identifying a function in
L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
for which the RL fractional integral of order
1/p
becomes unbounded. Throughout this study, we have come to appreciate the complexity of this problem, recognizing that our efforts thus far have not produced a solution. However, this is not unexpected, as even Hardy and Littlewood, in their pioneering study
[
12
]
, did not provide such an example. Instead of presenting an explicit example, they resorted to a proof by contradiction to affirmatively address this question. This approach, in some sense, underscores the considerable difficulty in discovering such a function.

Another unresolved issue we encountered pertains to proving that, for any
\gamma\in[0,(p-1)/p)
, it holds that
J_{t_{0},t}^{1/p}\big(L^{p}(t_{0},t_{1};X)\big)\not\subset K_{\gamma}(t_{0},t_{1};X)
. Successfully addressing this would complete our study. However, this problem hinges on the existence of the elusive function mentioned earlier, as it requires a detailed analysis of how the
L^{r}
norm of a function changes with varying
r\geq 1
, and eventually tends to infinity faster then
r^{\gamma}
. We acknowledge that the resolution of this particular problem remains elusive.

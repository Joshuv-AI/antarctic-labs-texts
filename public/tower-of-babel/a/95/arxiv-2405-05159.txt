Finding all solutions to the KZ equations in characteristic p p
Alexander Varchenko ⋆, Vadim Vologodsky ∘

Abstract
. The KZ equations are differential equations satisfied by the correlation functions (on the Riemann sphere) of two-dimensional conformal field theories associated with an affine Lie algebra at a fixed level. They form a system of complex partial differential equations with regular singular points satisfied by the n n -point functions of affine primary fields. In [ SV1 ] the KZ equations were identified with equations for flat sections of suitable Gauss-Manin connections, and solutions of the KZ equations were constructed in the form of multidimensional hypergeometric integrals. In [ SV2 ] the KZ equations were considered modulo a prime number p p , and, for rational levels, polynomial solutions of the KZ equations modulo p p were constructed by an elementary procedure as suitable p p -approximations of the hypergeometric integrals. In this paper we study in detail the first nontrivial example of the KZ equations in characteristic p p . In particular, if the level is irrational, we prove a version of the steepest descent result that relates the KZ local system to the space of functions on the critical locus of the master function. We use this result to prove the generic irreducibility of the KZ local system at any irrational level. If the level is rational, we describe all solutions of the KZ equations in characteristic p p by demonstrating that they all stem from the p p -hypergeometric solutions. Finally, we prove a Lagrangian property of the subbundle of the KZ bundle spanned by the p p -hypergeometric sections.

1.
Introduction

The Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations, or KZ equations, are linear differential equations satisfied by the correlation functions (on the Riemann sphere) of two-dimensional conformal field theories associated with an affine Lie algebra at a fixed level.
They form a system of complex partial differential equations with regular singular points satisfied by the
n
-point functions of affine primary fields.
The equations were derived by physicists Knizhnik and Zamolodchikov in their seminal work
[
KZ
]
.
In
[
SV1
]
the KZ equations were identified with equations for flat sections of suitable Gauss-Manin connections, and solutions of the KZ equations were constructed in the form of multidimensional hypergeometric integrals.
In
[
SV2
]
the KZ equations were considered modulo a prime number
p
, and polynomial solutions of the KZ equations modulo
p
were constructed by an elementary procedure as suitable
p
-approximations of the hypergeometric integrals.
In this paper we address the problem of whether all solutions of the KZ equations modulo
p
are generated by the
p
-hypergeometric
solutions.
We consider the first nontrivial example of the KZ equations and
demonstrate that, indeed, in this case, all solutions of the KZ equations modulo
p
stem from the
p
-hypergeometric solutions.

1.1.
KZ equations

The KZ equations considered in this paper have the following form.
Let
\Omega_{ij}
be the
n\times n
-matrix with only four nonzero elements given by the formula

(1.1)

\Omega_{ij}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}&\vdots^{i}&&\vdots^{j}&\\
{\scriptstyle i}\cdots&{-1}&\cdots&1&\cdots\\
&\vdots&&\vdots&\\
{\scriptstyle j}\cdots&1&\cdots&{-1}&\cdots\\
&\vdots&&\vdots&\end{pmatrix}.

Let
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
.
Define

\displaystyle H_{i}(z)=\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\Omega_{ij}}{z_{i}-z_{j}},\quad i=1,\dots,n,

called the Gaudin Hamiltonians.
Consider the system of differential and algebraic equations for
column vectors
I(z)=(I_{1}(z)
, …,
I_{n}(z))^{\intercal}
:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaa}\partial_{i}I\ +\ h\,H_{i}(z)\,I\,=\,0,\quad i=1,\dots,n,\quad I_{1}(z)+\dots+I_{n}(z)=0,

where
\partial_{i}
denotes
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}
;
M^{\intercal}
denotes the transpose matrix of a matrix
M
;

h
is a parameter of the system.
In this paper this systems of equations is called the KZ equations
and denoted
K\!Z(h)
.

Let
\mathbb{A}^{n}
be affine space with coordinates
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
.
Let

S
be the complement to the union of all diagonal hyperplanes defined by equations

z_{i}=z_{j}
,
i\neq j
.
The KZ equations define a flat KZ connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h}

on the trivial bundle
\pi:V\times S\to S
whose fiber is
the vector space
V
of
n
-vectors with the zero sum of coordinates. We denote the sheaf of sections of this bundle
by

{\mathcal{V}}
.

Remark
.

The system of equations (
1.2
) is the system of the original KZ equations
associated with the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and the subspace of singular vectors of weight
n-2

of the tensor power
({\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{{\otimes}n}
of the two-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-module, up to a gauge
transformation, see this example in
[
V2
, Section 1.1]
.

In this paper we study the KZ connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h}
in characteristic
p>0
. In particular, we describe explicitly all flat sections of
the vector bundle

{\mathcal{V}}
with connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h}

and compute the
p
-curvature of
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h}
.

1.2.
Dual connections

The vector space
V
has a non-degenerate bilinear form

(1.3)

\displaystyle(x,y)=x_{1}y_{1}+\dots+x_{n}y_{n}\,,

the Shapovalov form. The Gaudin Hamiltonians are symmetric,

\displaystyle(H_{i}(z)x,y)=(x,H_{i}(z)y)

for all
i
,
x,y
.
The KZ connections
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h}
and
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},-h}
are dual, that is,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(x(z),y(z))=(\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h}_{i}x(z),y(z))+(x(z),\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},-h}_{i}y(z))

for any
x(z),y(z)
. Equivalently, form (
1.3
) defines an isomorphism of bundles with flat connections

(1.5)

({\mathcal{V}},\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},-h})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}({\mathcal{V}},\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h})^{*}.

In particular, if
x(z)
is a flat section of the connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h}

and
y(z)
is a flat section of the connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},-h}
, then

\partial_{i}(x(z),y(z))=0
.

Let
y(z)
be a nonzero
V
-valued function. For a fixed
z
let
W_{y(z)}\subset V
be the hyperplane defined by the formula

\displaystyle W_{\!y(z)}=\{x\in V\mid(x,y(z))=0\}.

Let
\mathcal{W}_{y(z)}
be the codimension one subbundle of the trivial bundle

\pi:\mathbb{A}^{n}\times V\to\mathbb{A}^{n}
with fibers
W_{\!y(z)}
.

If
y(z)
is a flat section of the KZ connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},-h}
, then the subbundle

\mathcal{W}_{y(z)}
is invariant with respect to the KZ connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h}
,
namely, if
x(z)
is a section of
\mathcal{W}_{y(z)}
, then
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h}_{i}x(z)
also is a section of

\mathcal{W}_{y(z)}
for any
i
.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\big(\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h}_{i}x(z),y(z)\big)=\big(\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h}_{i}x(z),y(z)\big)+\big(x(z),\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},-h}_{i}y(z))=\partial_{i}\big(x(z),y(z)\big)=0.

1.3.
Solutions over
{\mathbb{C}}

Assume that

\displaystyle h\ =\ -\,\frac{r}{q}\,,

where
r,q
are coprime positive integers,
1\leq r<q
. Define the master function

(1.6)

\displaystyle\Phi(x,z)=\prod_{a=1}^{n}(x-z_{a})^{-r/q}

and the
{n}
-vector of hypergeometric integrals

(1.7)

\displaystyle I^{(\gamma)}(z)=(I_{1}(z),\dots,I_{n}(z))^{\intercal},\qquad I_{j}=\int_{\gamma(z)}\frac{\Phi(x,z)dx}{x-z_{j}},

where
\gamma(z)
is a Pochhammer contour in
{\mathbb{C}}-\{z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The vector
I^{(\gamma)}(z)
satisfies the KZ equations
K\!Z(-r/q)
.

Theorem
1.1
is a classical statement.
Much more general algebraic and differential equations satisfied by analogous multidimensional hypergeometric integrals were considered in
[
SV1
]
. Theorem
1.1
is discussed as an example in
[
V2
, Section 1.1]
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
V1
, Formula (1.3)]
)
.

If
nr/q
is not a positive integer, then all local solutions of the equations
K\!Z(-r/q)
have this form.
In particular, the complex vector space of local solutions of the form (
1.7
) is
n-1
-dimensional.

1.4.
Solutions in finite characteristic

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of finite characteristic
p
.
Consider the equations
K\!Z(h)
over
\mathbb{K}
.

The case
h=0
is trivial. Assume that
h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\subset\mathbb{K}
,
h\neq 0
.
Let
\tilde{h}
be the representative of
h
in
\mathbb{Z}
such that

(1.8)

\displaystyle 1\leq\tilde{h}\leq p-1.

Define

(1.9)

\displaystyle P(x,z)=\prod_{s=1}^{n}(x-z_{s})\ \in\ \mathbb{K}[x,z].

The function
P(x,z)^{\tilde{h}}
is called the master function.
Consider the vector of polynomials

(1.10)

\displaystyle Q(x,z,h)=P(x,z)^{\tilde{h}}\Big(\frac{1}{x-z_{1}},\dots,\frac{1}{x-z_{n}}\Big)^{\intercal}\,=\,\sum_{i}Q^{(i)}(z,h)\,x^{i},

where

(1.11)

\displaystyle Q^{(i)}(z,h)=(Q^{(i)}_{1}(z,h),\dots,Q^{(i)}_{n}(z,h))^{\intercal}

are vectors of polynomials in
z
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
SV2
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

For any positive integer
\ell
, the vector of polynomials

Q^{(\ell p-1)}(z,h)
satisfies the equations
K\!Z(h)
over
\mathbb{K}
.

The vector
Q^{(\ell p-1)}(z,h)
is zero if
\ell\not\in\big\{1,\dots,\big[\frac{n{\tilde{h}}}{p}\big]\big\}
for degree reasons.
The vectors

(1.12)

\displaystyle Q^{(\ell p-1)}(z,h),\qquad\ell=1,\dots,\Big[\frac{n{\tilde{h}}}{p}\Big],

are called the
p
-hypergeometric solutions of
the equations
K\!Z(h)
over
\mathbb{K}
.

Notice that if
\frac{n{\tilde{h}}}{p}<1
, then there are no
p
-hypergeometric solutions.

Denote

\mathbb{K}[z^{p}]=\mathbb{K}[z_{1}^{p},\dots,z_{n}^{p}]
.
The set of all solutions of
K\!Z(h)
over
\mathbb{K}
is a module over the ring
\mathbb{K}[z^{p}]
.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
SlV
]
)
.

Let
p>n
.
Then the
p
-hypergeometric solutions in (
1.12
)
are linear independent over the ring
\mathbb{K}[z^{p}]
,
that is, if

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\big[\frac{n{\tilde{h}}}{p}\big]}c_{\ell}(z)Q^{(\ell p-1)}(z,h)=0

for some
c_{\ell}(z)\in\mathbb{K}[z^{p}]
, then
c_{\ell}(z)=0
for all
\ell
.

Consider the dual equations
K\!Z(-h)
over
\mathbb{K}
.
The previous constructions give us the following corollary.

Corollary 1.5
.

The vectors

(1.13)

\displaystyle Q^{(\ell p-1)}(z,-h),\qquad\ell=1,\dots,\Big[\frac{n(p-\tilde{h})}{p}\Big],

satisfy the equations
K\!Z(-h)
. If
p>n
, then these solutions are linear independent over
\mathbb{K}[z^{p}]
.

The total number of
p
-hypergeometric solutions of the equations
K\!Z(h)
and
K\!Z(-h)
equals

(1.14)

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{n{\tilde{h}}}{p}\Big]+\Big[\frac{n(p-{\tilde{h}})}{p}\Big]=n-1=\dim V.

1.5.
What is done in this paper?

There are two cases:
h\in\mathbb{K}\backslash{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and
h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

If
h\in\mathbb{K}\backslash{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, we prove that the KZ connection is irreducible and, in particular, the KZ
equations have no formal solutions.
This is a striking difference with the picture over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

If
h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, we show that all solutions are
p
-hypergeometric.
We shall discuss each case in more detail in the remaining part of the introduction.

1.5.1.
Case

.

Let

be a
\mathbb{K}
-point of

. Recall that a

In Corollary

Theorem 1.6
.

Let

be a prime number that does not divide

Assume that

over

.

In fact, we prove a stronger result. Consider the vector space

Assume that

-module

We refer the reader to Theorem

1.5.2.
Case

.

In this case we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
p
be a prime number
that does not divide

, and

1.5.3.
p
-curvature

In characteristic
p
, the operators

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(z)=\left(\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h}_{k}\right)^{p},\qquad k=1,\dots,n,

commute with multiplication by functions.
The linear operators
\Psi_{k}(z)
are called the
p
-curvature operators
of the KZ connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h}
.

It is known that the intersection of kernels
\cap_{k=1}^{n}\operatorname{ker}\Psi_{k}(z)
of the
p
-curvature operators
coincides with the space generated by flat sections of
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},h}
, see the Cartier descent theorem
[
K70
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Theorem 1.13
.

Assume that
p
does not divide
n
and
h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Then

(1.16)

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(z)\Psi_{\ell}(z)=0

for
k,\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

(\operatorname{i})

If
h=0
, then all operators
\Psi_{k}(z)
are equal to zero.

(\operatorname{ii})

If
h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
and
\big[\frac{n\tilde{h}}{p}\big]=0

or
\big[\frac{n\tilde{h}}{p}\big]=n-1
, then all operators
\Psi_{k}(z)
are equal to zero.

(\operatorname{iii})

If
0<\big[\frac{n\tilde{h}}{p}\big]<n-1
, then every

p
-curvature operator
\Psi_{k}(z)
is of rank 1.
Its kernel is defined by the following linear equation:

(1.17)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaa}\operatorname{ker}\Psi_{k}(z)=\left\{(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})^{\intercal}\in V\ \Big|\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\sum_{m=1}^{\big[\frac{n(p-\tilde{h})}{p}\big]}z_{k}^{{p}(m-1)}Q^{(mp-1)}_{i}(z,-h)=0\right\},

and its image is generated by the vector

(1.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{\phantom{aa}\ell=1}^{\big[\frac{n\tilde{h}}{p}\big]}z_{k}^{{p}(\ell-1)}Q^{(\ell p-1)}(z,h),

see formula (
5.14
).

Formula (
1.16
) and parts (i), (ii) follow easily from Theorem
1.8
; see Lemma
4.8
for details.
Part (iii) is proved in Theorem
5.3
and Corollary
5.4
, as a consequence of the Katz p-curvature theorem
[
K72
]
.

Notice that the linear equation in (
1.17
) is satisfied by all
p
-hypergeometric flat sections

Q^{(mp-1)}(z,h)
,
m=1,\dots,\big[\frac{nh}{p}\big]
, by Orthogonality Corollary
1.10
.

Using the linear equation in (
1.17
) and orthogonality relations (
1.15
),
it is easy to check directly that the intersection
\cap_{k=1}^{n}\operatorname{ker}\Psi_{k}(z)
of kernels of the

-curvature operators
coincides with the span
of
p
-hypergeometric flat sections.

1.6.
Geometric interpretation of the KZ connection

Our approach to the results stated above is based on the geometric interpretation
of the KZ connection as the Gauss-Manin connection. To explain this interpretation, fix a ground field
\mathbb{K}
,
h\in\mathbb{K}
and consider the polynomial
P=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x-z_{i})
viewed as a rational function on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}:={\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times S
.
Define an integrable logarithmic connection on the trivial bundle by the formula

{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}(\log T),\quad\nabla(f)=df+hf\frac{dP}{P},

where
T\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}
is the support of
\operatorname{div}P
. We denote by
{\mathcal{P}}^{h}
the logarithmic local system
({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}},\nabla)
over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}
. Then
({\mathcal{V}},\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ},-h})
is identified with
the relative logarithmic de Rham cohomology
E_{h}:=H^{1}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h})
, see Proposition
2.3
. This interpretation goes back to
[
DF
,
CF
,
DJMM
,
SV1
]
at least when base field is the field of complex numbers.
For
\Char\mathbb{K}=p
and
h\not\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, we prove the following version of the steepest descent result for
E_{h}
. Let
\text{Crit}_{P}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}
be
the critical locus of the polynomial
P(x)
and
S^{\circ}\subset S

the open subset over which the projection
\pi:\text{Crit}_{P}\to S

is étale. In Theorem
3.5
we show that
E_{h}
over
S^{\circ}
is isomorphic
to
{\mathcal{P}}^{h}|_{\pi^{-1}(S^{\circ})}
(viewed as an
{\mathcal{O}}_{S^{\circ}}
-module with a connection). Then the irreducibility result, Theorem
1.7
, is derived from geometric properties of the finite map
\pi
,
see Lemmas
3.8
and
3.9
.

For
h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, we identify in Section
4
the local system
E_{h}
with an isotypic component of the de Rham cohomology (with constant coefficients) of a smooth projective curve
X
over
S
acted upon by a finite group. The flat subbundle
\mathcal{U}
from Theorem
1.8
is constructed using the conjugate filtration on the de Rham cohomology and its Lagrangian property.
Finally, the description of the flat sections of
E_{h}
is proven using a result of Katz relating the p-curvature of the Gauss-Manin connection and the Kodaira-Spencer operator and using the key non-degeneracy property of the latter,
see Corollary
4.6
and Lemma
4.9
.

2.
Logarithmic local system over

and its cohomology

In this section we explain a geometric interpretation
of the KZ connection as the Gauss-Manin connection and prove a few elementary results not specific to the positive characteristic case.

2.1.
Local system
{\mathcal{P}}^{h}
and its logarithmic de Rham cohomology

Fix an integer
n>1
. Let
S\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n}=\Spec{\mathbb{Z}}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]

be the complement to the union of hyperplanes
z_{i}-z_{j}=0
,

1\leq i,j\leq n
, that is,

S=\Spec{\mathbb{Z}}[z_{i},(z_{i}-z_{j})^{-1},\;1\leq i\neq j\leq n].

Set
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}={\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times S
. We denote by
x
the coordinate on
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

Define divisors
T_{i}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{S}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, by equations
x=z_{i}
. Set

\displaystyle T_{\infty}=\{\infty\}\times S\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S},\quad T=T_{\infty}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}.

Note that divisors
T_{i}
,
T_{\infty}
are pairwise disjoint and each of them projects isomorphically to
S
.

We denote by
\Omega^{m}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}(\log T)
the sheaf of logarithmic differential form on
({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S},T)
. Thus,

\Omega^{m}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}(\log T)
is an
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}
-submodule of

\Omega^{m}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(\infty[T])
[1]
[1]

[1]

Where
{\mathcal{O}}(\infty[T])
stands for the sheaf of rational functions
regular outside of
T
,
,
whose sections on
an open subset
U\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}

are differential forms
\mu
on
U\setminus(U\cap T)
such that
f\mu
and
fd\mu
are both
regular on
U
. Here
f=0
is a local equation of the divisor
T
.

Define a closed logarithmic
1
-form on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}
by the formula

(2.1)

\eta=\frac{dP}{P}\in\Gamma({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S},\Omega^{m}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}(\log T)),\qquad P=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x-z_{i}).

Define a logarithmic de Rham local system
{\mathcal{P}}^{h}
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}
:

{\mathcal{P}}^{h}={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}[h,h^{-1}]
as an
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}
-module;
the logarithmic connection is given by the formula:

(2.2)

{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}[h,h^{-1}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{h}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}(\log T)[h,h^{-1}],\quad\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{h}}(f)=df+hf\eta.

Since
\eta
is closed the connection
\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{h}}
is flat.

Remark
.

Our notation for the local system is inspired by the formula:

d(P^{h}f)=P^{h}\Big(df+hf\frac{dP}{P}\Big).

Let
H^{\bullet}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h})
be the relative logarithmic de Rham cohomology of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}
with coefficients in
{\mathcal{P}}^{h}
.
Recall that the latter is defined to be the hypercohomology of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}
with coefficients in the complex (
2.2
)

Since the higher cohomology groups of
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}
and
\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S}(\log T)

vanish,

H^{\bullet}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h})
is just the cohomology of the complex

(2.3)

\Gamma({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}})[h,h^{-1}]\ \xrightarrow{\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{h}}}\ \ \Gamma({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S},\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S}(\log T))[h,h^{-1}].

Observe, that
\Gamma({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S},\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S}(\log T))
is a free
\Gamma(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S})
-module on

\eta_{i}=\frac{d(x-z_{i})}{x-z_{i}},\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

Thus, complex (
2.3
) has the form

(2.4)

{\mathfrak{O}}\ \to\ \bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}{\mathfrak{O}}\cdot\eta_{i}\,,\quad f\mapsto h\sum_{i}f\eta_{i}\,,

where

{\mathfrak{O}}={\mathbb{Z}}[h,h^{-1},z_{i},(z_{i}-z_{j})^{-1},\;1\leq i\neq j\leq n].

In particular, we infer the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

The following statements are true.

(1)

H^{0}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h})=0
.

(2)

E:=H^{1}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h})

is a free
{\mathfrak{O}}
-module generated by classes
[\eta_{i}]

with a single relation

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,[\eta_{i}]=0.

The relative logarithmic de Rham cohomology is equipped with the Gauss-Manin connection
\nabla^{E}
. We refer the reader to
[
K72
, Section 1.4]
for a definition of the Gauss-Manin connection on the logarithmic de Rham cohomology.
An explicit formula for the connection on
E
is
given in Section
2.5
.

2.2.
Calibration

For a Cartier divisor
D

on a scheme
X
we denote by
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)
the corresponding invertible sheaf.
For an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
{\mathcal{F}}
, we shall write
{\mathcal{F}}(D)
for
{\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)
.

Let
m_{\infty}
,
m_{1},\dots,m_{n}
be integers. Denote by

C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}\ :\ C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},{\dots},m_{n}}^{0}\ \to\ C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},{\dots},m_{n}}^{1}

the complex

{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}\Big((m_{\infty}-1)T_{\infty}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(m_{i}-1)T_{i}\,\Big)[h,h^{-1}]\ \xrightarrow{\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{h}}}\ \Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S}\Big(\,m_{\infty}T_{\infty}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}T_{i}\,\Big)[h,h^{-1}]

of sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}
. The differential is well-defined because
\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{h}}=d+h\frac{dP}{P}
and
\frac{dP}{P}
is a section of

\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S}(T_{\infty}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}\,)
.
Note that
C_{1,1,{\dots},1}
is the relative logarithmic de Rham complex (
2.2
).

Lemma 2.1
.

(i)

Fix an integer
1\leq i\leq n
.
Then the embedding of complexes
C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}\hookrightarrow C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{i}+1,\dots,m_{n}}
induces a quasi-isomorphism after inverting
m_{i}-h\in{\mathbb{Z}}[h]
:

C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}[(m_{i}-h)^{-1}]\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{i}+1,\dots,m_{n}}[(m_{i}-h)^{-1}].

(ii)

The embedding of complexes
C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}\hookrightarrow C_{m_{\infty}+1,m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}

induces a quasi-isomorphism after inverting
m_{\infty}+nh\in{\mathbb{Z}}[h]
.

Proof.

For part (i) we have to show that the complex

(2.5)

C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{i}+1,\dots,m_{n}}^{0}/C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}^{0}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{h}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{i}+1,\dots,m_{n}}^{1}/C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}^{1}

becomes acyclic after inverting
m_{i}-h
.
Note that the quotient sheaves in (
2.5
) are supported on
T_{i}
.
The restriction of

C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{i}+1,\dots,m_{n}}^{0}/C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}^{0}

to
T_{i}

is a free
{\mathcal{O}}_{T_{i}}[h,h^{-1}]
-module on

\frac{1}{(x-z_{i})^{m_{i}}}
. The restriction of

C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{i}+1,\dots,m_{n}}^{1}/C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}^{1}

is a free
{\mathcal{O}}_{T_{i}}[h,h^{-1}]
-module on

\frac{d(x-z_{i})}{(x-z_{i})^{m_{i}+1}}
.

Note that

\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{h}}\left(\frac{1}{(x-z_{i})^{m_{i}}}\right)=d\left(\frac{1}{(x-z_{i})^{m_{i}}}\right)+\frac{h}{(x-z_{i})^{m_{i}}}\eta=(h-m_{i})\frac{d(x-z_{i})}{(x-z_{i})^{m_{i}+1}}

in the
quotient
C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{i}+1,\dots,m_{n}}^{1}/C_{m_{\infty},m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}^{1}
.
Thus, complex (
2.5
) is isomorphic to the complex

{\mathcal{O}}_{T_{i}}[h,h^{-1}]\ \xrightarrow{(h-m_{i})\Id}\ {\mathcal{O}}_{T_{i}}[h,h^{-1}]\,.

This proves part (i). For part (ii) the
argument is similar using that the residue of
\eta
at
T_{\infty}
is
-n
.
∎

2.3.
Log de Rham cohomology vs. ordinary

Let
R
be a commutative ring equipped with a homomorphism

s:{\mathbb{Z}}[h,h^{-1}]\to R.

We denote by
\bar{h}\in R^{\times}
the image of
h
.
Since the cohomology
H^{\bullet}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h})
is flat over
{\mathbb{Z}}[h,h^{-1}]

the base change morphism is an isomorphism:

H^{\bullet}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[h]}R\ \mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}\ H^{\bullet}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S},{\mathcal{P}}^{h}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[h]}R).

Let
U
be the complement to
T
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}
. Consider the restriction morphism

(2.6)

H^{\bullet}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[h]}R)\ \to\ H^{\bullet}_{dR}(U/S,({\mathcal{P}}^{h}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[h]}R)|_{U}).

Recall that (
2.6
) commutes with the Gauss-Manin connection.

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume that for every positive integer
m
the
elements
\bar{h}-m
,
n\bar{h}+m
are invertible in
R
.
Then morphism (
2.6
) is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.1
the morphism

C_{1,1,{\dots},1}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[h]}R\ \to\ \mathop{\lim\limits_{\longrightarrow}}\limits_{\raise 3.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle m$}}(C_{m,m,{\dots},m}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[h]}R)

is a quasi-isomorphism.
To complete the proof it remains to observe that

R^{\bullet}\Gamma({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S},\mathop{\lim\limits_{\longrightarrow}}\limits_{\raise 3.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle m$}}(C_{m,m,\dots,m}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[h]}R))\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}H^{\bullet}_{dR}(U/S,({\mathcal{P}}^{h}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[h]}R)|_{U}).

∎

2.4.
Local system
{\mathcal{P}}^{h+1}

Define a logarithmic local system
{\mathcal{P}}^{h+1}
:
{\mathcal{P}}^{h+1}={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}[h,h^{-1}]
as an
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}
-module. The logarithmic connection is given by the formula:

(2.7)

{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}[h,h^{-1}]\ \xrightarrow{\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{h+1}}}\ \ \Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}(\log T)[h,h^{-1}],\quad\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{h+1}}(f)=df+(h+1)f\eta.

Note that the restrictions of
{\mathcal{P}}^{h}
,
{\mathcal{P}}^{h+1}
to

are isomorphic:

(2.8)

{\mathcal{P}}^{h+1}\big|_{U}\ \mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}\ {\mathcal{P}}^{h}\Big|_{U},\quad f\mapsto fP.

Observe that multiplication by
P
yields a commutative diagram

(2.9)

\begin{matrix}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}[h,h^{-1}]&\xrightarrow{\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{h+1}}}&\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S}(\log T)[h,h^{-1}]\cr\Big\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle P$}}$\hss}&&\Big\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle P$}}$\hss}\cr{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}(-\operatorname{div}P)[h,h^{-1}]&\xrightarrow{\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{h}}}&\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S}(T-\operatorname{div}P)[h,h^{-1}],\end{matrix}

where the vertical arrows are isomorphisms.
This defines an isomorphism

(2.10)

H^{\bullet}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h+1})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}R^{\bullet}\Gamma({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S},C_{n+1,0,\dots,0}),

where the complex

is defined in Section
2.2
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Denote
a=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(i+nh)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[h]
. Then
morphism (
2.10
) induces an isomorphism

(2.11)

H^{\bullet}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h+1})[a^{-1}]\ \mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}\ H^{\bullet}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h})[a^{-1}]

of local systems on
S
.

Proof.

Consider the morphisms of complexes

C_{n+1,0,{\dots},0}\ \leftarrow\ C_{1,0,{\dots},0}\ \to\ C_{1,1,{\dots},1}\,.

The right arrow is a quasi-isomorphism by part (i) of Lemma
2.1
.
The second morphism becomes a
quasi-isomorphism after inverting

by
part (ii) of Lemma
2.1
. Since
C_{1,1,{\dots},1}
is the relative logarithmic de Rham complex of

, we infer

R^{\bullet}\Gamma({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S},C_{n+1,0,{\dots},0})[a^{-1}]\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}H^{\bullet}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h})[a^{-1}].

2.5.
Explicit formula for Gauss-Manin connection on
E

For
1\leq i,j\leq n
, let
\Omega_{ij}
be the
n\times n
matrix defined in (
1.1
).
Set

A\,=\,\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{\Omega_{ij}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}dz_{i}\,\in\,\Mat_{n\times n}\big(\,\Gamma(S,\Omega^{1}_{S})\,\big).

Consider the connection on the free

\Gamma(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S})
-module

{\mathfrak{O}}^{\oplus n}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{i=n}{\mathfrak{O}}\cdot e_{i},\quad{\mathfrak{O}}:=\Gamma(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S})[h,h^{-1}]

given by the formula

(2.12)

\nabla=d-hA.

One verifies that the connection is flat:

d(A)-hA\wedge A=0.\,

Proposition 2.3
.

The morphism

({\mathfrak{O}}^{\oplus n},\nabla)\to(E,\nabla^{E}),\quad e_{i}\mapsto[\eta_{i}],\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

is flat.

Proof.

A direct computation of the Gauss-Manin connection on
E
is involved: in general, the vector field
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}
can not be lifted to a vector field on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{Z}
preserving the divisor
V
. However,
arguing as in the proof of Theorem
2.2
it suffices to check
that the composition

({\mathfrak{O}}^{\oplus n},\nabla)\to(E,\nabla^{E})\to(H^{1}_{dR}(U/S,\,{\mathcal{P}}^{h}),\nabla^{GM}),

is flat.
Here the second arrow depicts the restriction map (which is an isomorphism for generic
h
) and
\nabla^{GM}
stands for the Gauss-Manin connection on
H^{1}_{dR}(U/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h})
. Computing the latter is easy:
for every
i\neq j
, we have

\nabla^{GM}_{\!\!\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}}([\eta_{j}])=\left[\left(\left(\frac{1}{x-z_{j}}\right)^{\prime}_{z_{i}}+h\frac{P^{\prime}_{z_{i}}}{(x-z_{j})P}\right)dx\right]=\left[-h\frac{\eta_{i}-\eta_{j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}dx\right].

Since
\sum_{i}[\eta_{i}]=0
the formula above determines the connection.
∎

2.6.
Duality

Denote by
{\mathcal{P}}^{-h}
(resp.
{\mathcal{P}}^{-h}(-T)
) the logarithmic local system
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}[h,h^{-1}]
(resp.
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}(-T)[h,h^{-1}]

\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{-h}}(f)=df-hf\eta.

\begin{split}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}[h,h^{-1}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{h}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S}(\log T)[h,h^{-1}])\otimes&({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}(-T)[h,h^{-1}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla^{{\mathcal{P}}^{-h}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S}[h,h^{-1}])\\

(2.16)

supported in cohomological degrees
1
and
2
.
The Serre duality induces a
{\mathfrak{O}}
-linear duality between complexes (
2.3
) and (
2.16
) which reduces to (
2.15
) in degree
1
. In the other words, if
\eta_{i}^{*}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, is a basis for

dual to

is identified with

via the trace map, then complex (

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}{\mathfrak{O}}\cdot\eta_{i}^{*}\ \to\ {\mathfrak{O}},\quad\sum f_{i}\eta_{i}^{*}\mapsto-h\sum_{i}f_{i}\,.

In particular, we see that

{\mathfrak{O}}
-module and (

Using Lemma
2.1

(2.18)

\gamma:\ H^{1}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{-h}(-T))\ \to\ H^{1}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{-h}),

and

The logarithmic Poincare duality (

3.
Study of
{\mathcal{P}}^{h}

For the duration of this section, let
p

. We denote by

{\mathcal{P}}^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

(resp.

{\mathcal{P}}^{h}

.

In Section

we study the specialization of

to

3.1.
Review of
p
-curvature and Cartier isomorphism

Recall from
[
K72
, Section 3.0]
the notion of
p
-curvature of an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
M
over a smooth scheme
X
over a base
Y
of characteristic
p
equipped with an integrable connection
\nabla
relative to
Y
. For any vector field
\theta\in\text{Der}(U/Y)
on an open subset
U\subset X
the
p
-curvature operator

\Psi_{\theta}:M|_{U}\to M|_{U}

is defined by the formula

\Psi_{\theta}=\nabla_{\theta}^{p}-\nabla_{\theta^{[p]}},

where
\theta^{[p]}
is the
p
-th iterate of the derivation
\theta

(which is again a derivation) and

\nabla_{\theta}
is the covariant derivative along
\theta
. One verifies that
\Psi_{\theta}

is
{\mathcal{O}}_{U}
-linear and flat with respect to
\nabla
. Moreover, one has

\Psi_{\theta+\theta^{\prime}}=\Psi_{\theta}+\Psi_{\theta^{\prime}},\quad\Psi_{\theta}\Psi_{\theta^{\prime}}=\Psi_{\theta^{\prime}}\Psi_{\theta},\quad\Psi_{f\theta}=f^{p}\Psi_{\theta}\,.

The
p
-curvature operators can be organized into a single
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear map

(3.2)

\Psi:M\to F_{\text{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}\otimes M,

where
F_{\text{abs}}:X\to X
is the absolute Frobenius. Homomorphism (
3.2
) is uniquely characterised by the property that, for every vector field
\theta\in\text{Der}(U/Y)
on an open subset
U\subset X
, the composition of

\Psi
with the interior product
\iota_{\theta}
is
\Psi_{\theta}
.

We will use a generalization of the Cartier isomorphism for the de Rham cohomology with coefficients in a module equipped with an integrable connection due to Ogus
[
O
]
. Let us recall the statement. Given
(M,\nabla)
as above consider the
F
-Higgs complex

(3.3)

M\to F_{\text{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}\otimes M\to F_{\text{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{2}_{X/Y}\otimes M\to\cdots

where the differential carries a germ
\omega\otimes v
of

F_{\text{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{m}_{X/Y}\otimes M
to the image of

\omega\otimes\Psi(v)
under the wedge product map

F_{\text{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{m}_{X/Y}\otimes F_{\text{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}\otimes M\to F_{\text{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{m+1}_{X/Y}\otimes M.

Recall that, for every quasi-coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
\Omega
on
X
, its Frobenius pullback

F_{\text{abs}}^{*}\Omega
has a canonical integrable connection, called the Frobenius descent connection, which is uniquely characterized by the property that sections of
F_{\text{abs}}^{-1}\Omega\subset F_{\text{abs}}^{*}\Omega
are flat.
Define an integrable connection on
F_{\text{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{m}_{X/Y}\otimes M
to be
the tensor product of the Frobenius descent connection on
F_{\text{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{m}_{X/Y}
and
\nabla
on
M
. Then
the differential in (
3.3
) is flat.
In particular, cohomology sheaves
{\mathcal{H}}^{m}_{\psi}(X/Y,M)
of
(
3.3
) are endowed with a connection (that we also denote by
\nabla
).
Ogus proved in
[
O
, Theorem 1.2.1]
that the
p
-curvature of
{\mathcal{H}}^{m}_{\psi}(M,\nabla)
is zero and there is a canonical isomorphism:

(3.4)

C:{\mathcal{H}}^{m}_{dR}(X/Y,M)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}{\mathcal{H}}^{m}_{\psi}(X/Y,M)^{\nabla=0}.

Here the superscript
\nabla=0
stands for the subsheaf of flat sections and
{\mathcal{H}}^{m}_{dR}(X/Y,M)
is the de Rham cohomology sheaf (on
X
).

Let us explain an explicit construction of the Cartier isomorphism (
3.4
) for
X
being an open subscheme of
{\mathbb{A}}_{Y}^{1}=Y\times\Spec{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x].
For
m=0
the Cartier operator carries a flat local section of
M
to itself. For
m=1
and a cohomology class represented by a local section
dx\otimes v
of

\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}\otimes M
, we have that

(3.5)

C([dx\otimes v])=-[dx\otimes\Big(\nabla\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Big)\Big)^{p-1}(v)].

In the logarithmic context the
p
-curvature is defined similarly yielding a complex

(3.6)

M\to F_{\text{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}(\log T)\otimes M\to F_{\text{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{2}_{X/Y}(\log T)\otimes M\to\cdots

see
[
O
, Section 3.1]
. There is also a logarithmic version of the generalized Cartier isomorphism:

(3.7)

C:{\mathcal{H}}^{m}_{dR,\log}(X/Y,M)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}{\mathcal{H}}^{m}_{\psi,\log}(X/Y,M)^{\nabla=0};

see
[
O
, Theorem 3.1.1]
.

3.2.
De Rham cohomology of
E\otimes{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

Recall that
E\otimes{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
is a module over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[h,h^{-1}]
. Set

(3.8)

E^{\flat}:=E\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[h]}{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[h,(h^{p}-h)^{-1}].

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
p
does not divide
n
. Then, for every integer
m\geq 0
, we have

H^{m}_{dR}(S,E^{\flat})=0.

Proof.

Set

(3.9)

{\mathcal{P}}^{h,\flat}:={\mathcal{P}}^{h}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[h]}{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[h,(h^{p}-h)^{-1}].

By Proposition
2.2
the restriction morphism

H^{\bullet}_{dR,\log}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h,\flat})\to H^{\bullet}_{dR}(U/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h,\flat}|_{U})

is an isomorphism. In particular,

E^{\flat}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}H^{1}_{dR}(U/S,{\mathcal{P}}^{h,\flat}|_{U}).

Since the relative de Rham cohomology groups are trivial in all other degrees using
the Leray spectral sequence we conclude that

H^{m}_{dR}(S,E^{\flat})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}H^{m+1}_{dR}(U,{\mathcal{P}}^{h,\flat}|_{U}).

Thus, it is enough to show that

(3.10)

H^{\bullet}_{dR}(U,{\mathcal{P}}^{h,\flat}|_{U})=0

in all degrees. We will do this using the generalized Cartier isomorphism.

The
p
-curvature of the local system
{\mathcal{P}}^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
can be described explicitly. Since
{\mathcal{P}}^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
is trivial rank
1
module over
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}}\otimes{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[h,h^{-1}]

the morphism
\Psi
is necessarily a multiplication by a section of

F_{\text{abs}}^{*}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}}(\,\log(T_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})\,)\otimes{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[h,h^{-1}]={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}}[h,h^{-1}]\otimes_{F_{\text{abs}}}\Omega^{1}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}}(\,\log(T_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})\,).

According to
[
K72
, Prop. 7.2.2]
this section is equal to

(h^{p}-h)\otimes\eta.

In local coordinates

on

the above formula reads as follows:

(3.12)

\Psi_{\!\!\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}}=(h^{p}-h)\Big(P^{-1}\frac{\partial P}{\partial z_{i}}\Big)^{p},\quad\Psi_{\!\!\frac{\partial}{\partial x}}=(h^{p}-h)\Big(P^{-1}\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}\Big)^{p}.

The key property of

that we need

does not vanish on

. Indeed, the interior product of

with the vector field

which is an invertible function on

) of

is acyclic in all degrees. Indeed, the latter is obtained
from the acyclic complex of
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules

by pulling it back along

and then tensoring with

Applying the result of Ogus (

we conclude that the de Rham cohomology sheaves

are trivial in all degrees.
This proves the cohomology vanishing (

) and, thus, completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Regular forms

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
1\leq r<q
be an integer congruent to
\bar{r}
modulo
q
. Then the differential forms

(4.20)

\displaystyle\mu^{(r)}_{i}=\frac{x^{i-1}dx}{y^{r}}\,,\qquad i=1,\dots,\Big[\frac{nr}{q}\Big],

form a basis for the
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-module
F^{1}_{\bar{r}}

Hence

is a free module of rank
\big[\frac{nr}{q}\big]

Proof.

Easy calculations in local coordinates show that every such

over each closed point of

Clearly the forms

This proves the lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

\displaystyle\nabla_{\!\!\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}}[\mu_{k}^{(r)}]

4.7.
Poincaré pairing

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
r=1,\dots,q-1
,
k=1,\dots,n
,

j=1,\dots,\big[\frac{n(q-r)}{q}\big]
.
Then the Poincaré pairing
([\omega_{k}^{(r)}],[\mu_{j}^{(q-r)}])

is given by the formula

(4.23)

\displaystyle([\omega_{k}^{(r)}],[\mu_{j}^{(q-r)}])\,=\,-\,\frac{q}{rC_{k}(z)}\,z_{k}^{j-1}\,,

where
C_{k}(z)=(z_{k}-z_{1})\dots(z_{k}-z_{k-1})(z_{k}-z_{k+1})\dots(z_{k}-z_{n})
.

Proof.

Recall that
X
is a compactification of the affine curve defined by the equation
y^{q}=\prod_{a=1}^{n}(x-z_{a})
.
Define an open cover of
X
,

\displaystyle U_{1}=X-D_{k},\qquad U_{2}=X-{\bigcup}_{{i=1\atop i\neq k}}^{n}D_{i}.

Elements of
H^{1}_{dR}(X)
can be represented by cochains

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
consisting of a regular 1-form
\alpha
on
U_{1}
,
a regular 1-form
\beta
on
U_{2}
, and a regular function
\gamma
on
U_{1}\cap U_{2}

such that
d\gamma=\alpha-\beta
. The Poincare pairing of two cochains

(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\gamma_{1})
and
(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2},\gamma_{2})
can be computed as

\displaystyle\Res_{D_{k}}\big(\gamma_{1}\alpha_{2}-\gamma_{2}\alpha_{1}\big).

The cohomology classes
\omega_{1}^{(r)}
and
\mu_{k}^{(q-r)}
are represented by the cochains:

\displaystyle\Big(\omega_{k}^{(r)},-\sum_{{i=1\atop i\neq k}}^{n}\omega_{i}^{(r)},-\frac{q}{ry^{r}}\Big),\qquad(\mu_{j}^{(q-r)},\mu_{j}^{(q-r)},0).

Hence

\displaystyle([\omega_{k}^{(r)}],[\mu_{j}^{(q-r)}])=\Res_{D_{k}}\Big(\frac{-q}{ry^{r}}\cdot\frac{x^{j-1}dx}{y^{q-r}}\Big)=\frac{-q}{rC_{k}(z)}\,z_{k}^{j-1}\,.

∎

4.8.
Fundamental exact sequence

Let
p
be a prime number that does not divide
nq

For the rest of this paper we let

(resp.
X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}=X\times\Spec{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

The relative de Rham cohomology

is equipped with the Gauss-Manin connection
\nabla
, Hodge and conjugate filtrations (
[
K72
, Section 2.3]
).
For dimension reason, the conjugate spectral sequence degenerates at the
E_{1}
-page and
yields a short exact sequence of vector bundles:

(4.24)

0\to F^{*}_{S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}H^{1}(S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle F^{*}_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}_{dR}(X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle C}}{{\longrightarrow}}F^{*}_{S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}H^{0}(S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},\Omega^{1}_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}})\to 0.

Recall the construction of the maps in (
4.24
). For every function
f\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}
, we have
d(f^{p})=0
. Thus the
p
-th power map
F^{*}_{abs}

induces a morphism of complexes
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle F^{*}_{abs}}}{{\longrightarrow}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{1}_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}})
. Passing to cohomology we get an
{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}
-linear
map
H^{1}(S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}})\to F_{*,S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}H^{1}_{dR}(X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})
which by adjunction gives

factors through the absolute de Rham cohomology

\nabla

The second morphism in (
4.24
) arises from the Cartier isomorphism

C:{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\Omega_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}^{\bullet},d)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}\Omega^{1}_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}^{\prime}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}\,,

where

denotes the Frobenius twist

Since the Hodge spectral sequence also degenerates at the

-page, we have

H^{1}(S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}H^{1}_{dR}(X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})/F^{1},\qquad H^{0}(S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},\Omega^{1}_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{}_{\sim}}}F^{1},

where

is the Hodge filtration.
Thus, the above short exact sequence takes the form:

(4.25)

0\to F^{*}_{S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}(H^{1}_{dR}(X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})/F^{1})\xrightarrow{F_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}^{*}}H^{1}_{dR}(X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle C}}{{\longrightarrow}}F^{*}_{S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}F^{1}\to 0.

We remark that morphisms

and

are adjoint with respect to the Poincaré pairing:

(4.26)

(F^{*}_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}(\alpha),\beta)=(\alpha,C(\beta)),\quad\alpha\in F^{*}_{S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}(H^{1}_{dR}(X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})/F^{1}),\;\;\beta\in H^{1}_{dR}(X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}).

Recall that, for every vector bundle
V
over
S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
its Frobenius pullback

is equipped with the Frobenius descent connection characterized by the property that the pullback of any local section of

is flat.
If we equip the boundary terms of sequence (
4.25
) with the Frobenius descent connection and the middle term with the Gauss-Manin connection, then all the differentials in (
4.25
) are flat.

The exact sequence (
4.25
) splits into isotypic components

(4.27)

\displaystyle\qquad 0\to(F^{*}_{S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}(H^{1}_{dR}(X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})/F^{1}))_{\bar{r}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle F_{X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}_{dR}(X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})_{\bar{r}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle C}}{{\longrightarrow}}(F^{*}_{S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}F^{1})_{\bar{r}}\to 0.

Note that

(4.28)

\displaystyle(F^{*}_{S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}F^{1})_{\bar{r}}=F^{*}_{S_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}(F^{1}_{{\bar{r}/p}})\,.

Appendix A
Orthogonality relations

We give an elementary proof of a stronger version of Corollary
1.10
.

Theorem A.1
.

The
p
-hypergeometric solutions of the equations
K\!Z(h)
and
K\!Z(-h)
are orthogonal
under the Shapovalov form. Namely,
for any
\ell\in\big\{1,\dots,\big[\frac{n\tilde{h}}{p}\big]\big\}
and

(A.1)

\displaystyle\big(Q^{(\ell p-1)}(z,h),Q^{(mp-1)}(z,-h)\big)=0.

Proof.

By formula (
1.4
), the left-hand side in (
A.1
)
lies in
\mathbb{K}[z^{p}]
. It remains to show that the left-hand side
cannot be a nonzero element of
\mathbb{K}[z^{p}]

Let

. Let

be the
j
-th coordinate of

and

Let

The polynomials

\displaystyle\deg_{t,z}Q(t,z,-h)=n(p-\tilde{h})-1,\qquad\deg_{z}Q_{j}^{(mp-1)}(z,-h)=n(p-\tilde{h})-mp.

Clearly,

\displaystyle k\tilde{h}=n\tilde{h}-\ell p,\qquad k(p-\tilde{h})=n(p-\tilde{h})-mp,

∎

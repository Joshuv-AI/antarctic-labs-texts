Abstract
. We introduce two families of generators (functions) 𝒢 \mathcal{G} that consist of entire and meromorphic functions enjoying a certain periodicity property and contain the classical Gaussian and hyperbolic secant generators. Sharp results are proved on the density of separated sets that provide non-uniform sampling for the shift-invariant and quasi-shift-invariant spaces generated by elements of these families. As an application, new sharp results are obtained on the density of semi-regular lattices for the Gabor frames generated by elements from these families.

1.
Introduction and main results

A countable set
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is called separated if

\inf_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma,\gamma\neq\gamma^{\prime}}|\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}|>0.

Given a generator (function)
\mathcal{G}
with a ”reasonably fast” decay at
\pm\infty
and a number
p,1\leq p\leq\infty
, the shift-invariant space
V_{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}(\mathcal{G})
consists of all functions
f
of the form

f(x)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{n}\mathcal{G}(x-n),\quad\{c_{n}\}\in l^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}).

More generally, given a separated set
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, the quasi shift-invariant space
V_{\Gamma}^{p}(\mathcal{G})
consists of all functions of the form

f(x)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma),\quad\{c_{\gamma}\}\in l^{p}(\Gamma).

An important class of generators is the Wiener amalgam space
W_{0}
, which consists of measurable functions
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}},
satisfying

(1.1)

\|\mathcal{G}\|_{W}:=\sum\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{\infty}(k,k+1)}<\infty.

Shift-invariant and quasi shift-invariant spaces have important applications in mathematics and engineering, in particular since they are often used as models for spaces of signals and images. It is also well known that there is a close connection between the Gabor frames and sampling sets for the shift-invariant spaces.

A classical example is the Paley–Wiener space
PW^{2}_{\pi}
which is exactly the shift-invariant space
V^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{G})
generated by the sinc function
\mathcal{G}(x)=\sin(\pi x)/(\pi x).

The remarkable result in digital signal processing is the Shannon–Whittaker–Kotelnikov sampling theorem that states that every
f\in PW^{2}_{\pi}
can be reconstructed from its values at the integers:

f(x)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f(n)\,\mbox{sinc}(x-n).

This implies that the set of integers
{\mathbb{Z}}
is a stable sampling set for
PW^{2}_{\pi}
.

In this paper, we introduce two families of generators. As an application, we obtain new sharp results on the density of semi-regular lattices for the Gabor frames with generators from these families.

1.1.
Sampling sets and Beurling densities

A separated set
\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is called a (stable) sampling set for
V^{p}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{G})
if the following sampling inequalities

A\|f\|_{p}^{p}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|f(\lambda)|^{p}\leq B\|f\|_{p}^{p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty,

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq K\sup\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|f(\lambda)|,\quad p=\infty,

hold true with some positive constants
A,B,K
and for every
f\in V_{\Gamma}^{p}(\mathcal{G}).

The
lower and upper uniform densities
of a separated set
\Lambda
(sometimes called the Beurling densities) are defined by

(1.2)

D^{-}(\Lambda):=\lim_{R\to\infty}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\#(\Lambda\cap[x-R,x+R])}{2R},

(1.3)

D^{+}(\Lambda):=\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\#(\Lambda\cap[x-R,x+R])}{2R}.

These densities play a key role in the study of sampling and interpolation sets.

Throughout the paper,
\Gamma
denotes a separated set of translates.
To avoid trivial remarks, in what follows we always assume that
\Gamma
is
relatively dense
, i.e.
D^{-}(\Gamma)>0.

We will now introduce two classes of generators.

\mathcal{K}(\alpha)

Given a number
\alpha>0
and a rational function
\mathcal{R}=P/Q
,

2\pi i/\alpha

\mathcal{G}(z)=\mathcal{R}(e^{\alpha z})=\frac{P(e^{\alpha z})}{Q(e^{\alpha z})}.

Definition 1.1
.

We denote by

) where

and

(

(

(

One may check that conditions

Note that the classical hyperbolic secant generator belongs to

For example,

•

•

•

Then, clearly, the generator

defined in (

) satisfies
\mathcal{G}(z+2\pi i/k\alpha)=c\,\mathcal{G}(z),z\in{\mathbb{C}}.

Assume

(i)
We denote by

is the greatest integer

(ii)
We set
q(\mathcal{G})=

deg

is the denominator in (

1.4.
Stability of
\Gamma
-shifts

Given a generator
\mathcal{G}
, a basic property of quasi shift-invariant space
V^{p}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{G})
is that the
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are
l^{p}
-stable, i.e. there exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

(1.8)

C_{1}\|{\bf c}\|_{p}\leq\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}(\,\cdot-\gamma)\right\|_{p}\leq C_{2}\|{\bf c}\|_{p},\quad\mbox{for every }{\bf c}=\{c_{\gamma}\}\in l^{p}(\Gamma).

This property implies that
V^{p}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{G})
is a closed subspace of
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
and that the system
\{\mathcal{G}(\,\cdot-\gamma)\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
forms an unconditional basis in this space.

The stability property of
{\mathbb{Z}}
-shifts is well-studied.
The following is an immediate corollary of Theorem 3.5 in
[
18
]
:

Lemma 1.5
.

Assume
\mathcal{G}\in W_{0}
and
p\in[1,\infty]
. Then the integer-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are
l^{p}
-stable if and only if the Fourier transform
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
of
\mathcal{G}
satisfies

(1.9)

\hat{\mathcal{G}}\mbox{ does not vanish on any set }{\mathbb{Z}}+b,\ 0\leq b<1.

Throughout the paper, we consider the standard form of Fourier transform,

\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2\pi ixt}\mathcal{G}(x)\,dx.

We also mention paper
[
13
]
, which proves the
l^{2}
-stability of
\Gamma
-shifts for certain generators
\mathcal{G}
under the condition that
\Gamma
is a complete interpolating sequence for the Paley–Wiener space
PW_{\pi}^{2}
.

We would like to get conditions on
\Gamma
and
\mathcal{G}
that imply property (
1.8
) for every value of
p\in[1,\infty].

In fact, the right hand-side inequality in (
1.8
) is true for every separated set
\Gamma
, every generator
\mathcal{G}\in W_{0}
and every
p\in[1,\infty]
, see Lemma
8.1
below. On the other hand, under a mild additional condition on the generator, it suffices to check that the left hand-side inequality is true for
p=\infty
:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a separated set and
\mathcal{G}\in W_{0}\cap C({\mathbb{R}})
. If the left hand-side inequality in (
1.8
) is true for
p=\infty
, then it is true for every
p\in[1,\infty]
.

In what follows, we will say that
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are stable, if they are
l^{p}
-stable, for every
p\in[1,\infty].

Let us now recall Beurling’s notion of the weak limit of a sequence of
sets. A sequence
\{\Gamma(n):n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
of separated subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}
is said to converge weakly to a separated set

\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, if for every
R>0
and
\epsilon>0
,
there exists
n_{\epsilon,R}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
n\geq n_{\epsilon,R}
we have

\Gamma(n)\cap(-R,R)\subset\Gamma+(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\ \mbox{ and }\ \Gamma\cap(-R,R)\subset\Gamma(n)+(-\epsilon,\epsilon).

Given a separated and relatively dense set
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, it is easy to check that every real sequence
\{s_{j}\}
contains a subsequence
\{s_{j_{n}}\}
such that the translates
\Gamma+s_{j_{n}}
converge weakly to some separated relatively dense set
\Gamma^{\prime}
as
n\to\infty
.
Let
W(\Gamma)
denote the collection of all such weak limits. It is well-known (and easy to check) that

(1.10)

D^{-}(\Gamma^{\prime})\geq D^{-}(\Gamma)\ \mbox{and }D^{+}(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq D^{+}(\Gamma),\quad\mbox{for every }\ \Gamma^{\prime}\in W(\Gamma).

As a simple example, we observe that

(1.11)

\mbox{The set }W({\mathbb{Z}})\mbox{ consists of all sets }{\mathbb{Z}}+a,\ a\in[0,1).

We will now formulate some sufficient conditions for the stability of
\Gamma
-shifts for the generators from the families
\mathcal{K}(\alpha)
and
\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
defined above. These will be given in terms of the set

\Gamma
and the poles
w_{j}
of the rational function
\mathcal{R}
in (
1.4
) and (
1.7
).

Denote by
d(w_{j})
the order of the pole
w_{j}
and set
d:=\max\limits_{j}d(b_{j})
. Let
{\rm pol}_{d}(\mathcal{R})
denote the set of poles of
\mathcal{R}
of order
d.

We will consider the following assumptions:

(
\Xi^{\prime}
)

The set
{\rm pol}_{d}(\mathcal{R})
consists precisely of one element;

(
\Xi^{\prime\prime}
)

For every
\Gamma^{\prime}\in W(\Gamma)
there exists
w\in{\rm pol}_{d}(\mathcal{R})
and
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{\prime}
such that

(1.12)

\log(w)-\log(w')\notin\alpha(\Gamma^{\prime}-\gamma^{\prime})\quad\text{for any }w^{\prime}\in{\rm pol}_{d}(\mathcal{R}),\,w\neq w^{\prime}.

When
\Gamma={\mathbb{Z}}
, it follows easily from (
1.11
) that (
1.12
) is equivalent to the simpler condition

(
\Xi^{\prime\prime\prime}
)

\log(w)-\log(w')\notin\alpha{\mathbb{Z}}
,
for every

w^{\prime}\in{\rm pol}_{d}(\mathcal{R}),\,w\neq w^{\prime}.

Proposition 1.7
.

(i)
If
\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
satisfies either
(\Xi^{\prime})
or
(\Xi^{\prime\prime\prime})
, then
{\mathbb{Z}}
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are stable.

(ii)
If
\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{K}(\alpha)
satisfies either
(\Xi^{\prime})
or
(\Xi^{\prime\prime})
, then
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are stable.

1.5.
Sampling for generators from
\mathcal{K}(\alpha)

\mathcal{C}(\alpha)

Our first sampling theorem concerns the class of generators
\mathcal{K}(\alpha)

Theorem 1.8
.

Given a generator
\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{K}(\alpha),\alpha>0
, and two separated sets
\Lambda,\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}

\Gamma
-shifts of

are stable and that

D^{-}(\Lambda)>\frac{q(\mathcal{G})}{k(\mathcal{G})}D^{+}(\Gamma).

Then
\Lambda
is a sampling set for
V^{p}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{G})
, for every
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Recall that the numbers
q(\mathcal{G})
and

are defined in Definition

.

Remark 1.9
.

Note that condition (
1.13

\alpha
. However, above we assume the stability of
\Gamma
-shifts. This property can be violated for certain values of
\alpha

Let

\mathcal{H}(x):=\frac{e^{x}}{e^{2x}+1}\in\mathcal{K}(1)

from the class

1.7.
Gabor frames

Our results for the Gabor frames follow from the sampling theorems formulated above.
We use the connection between Gabor frames and sampling theorems for shift-invariant spaces that previously turned out to be very fruitful, see e.g.
[
9
,
11
]
, and
[
26
]
for a multi-dimensional setting.

Fix the standard notation for the operators of translation and modulation:

M_{\xi}f=e^{2\pi i\xi\,\cdot}f\quad\text{and}\quad T_{\lambda}f=f(\,\cdot-\lambda),\quad\text{where }f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),\,(\lambda,\xi)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

Let
\Lambda,\Psi
be separated real sets. For a generator
\mathcal{G}
the
Gabor system

G(\mathcal{G},\Lambda\times\Psi)
is the collection of all time-frequency shifts

(1.17)

G(\mathcal{G},\Lambda\times\Psi):=\{M_{\xi}T_{\lambda}\mathcal{G},\,\,(\lambda,\xi)\in\Lambda\times\Psi\}.

The system
G(\mathcal{G},\Lambda\times\Psi)
forms a
frame
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
if there exist finite positive constants
A,B
such that

A\|f\|^{2}_{2}\leq\sum\limits_{(\lambda,\xi)\in\Lambda\times\Psi}|\langle f,M_{\xi}T_{\lambda}\mathcal{G}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}_{2},

for every
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}).

For the Gabor systems with generators from the families
\mathcal{K}(\alpha)
and
\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
,
we study the case of semi-regular lattices, i.e.
\Psi={\mathbb{Z}}
.
Our main result is as follows

Theorem 1.13
.

(i)

Assume the Fourier transform
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
of a generator
\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{K}(\alpha),\alpha>0,
satisfies (
1.9
).
If a separated set
\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies
D^{-}(\Lambda)>q(\mathcal{G})/k(\mathcal{G})
,
then the system
G(\mathcal{G},\Lambda\times{\mathbb{Z}})
is a frame in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.

(ii)

There exist a generator
\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{K}(\alpha),\alpha>0,
satisfying (
1.9
) and a separated set
\Lambda
with the critical density
D^{-}(\Lambda)=q(\mathcal{G})/k(\mathcal{G})
such that
G(\mathcal{G},\Lambda\times{\mathbb{Z}})
is not a frame in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.

(iii)

Assume a generator
\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{C}(\alpha),\alpha>0,
satisfies (
1.9
). If a separated set
\Lambda
satisfies
D^{-}(\Lambda)>q(\mathcal{G})+1
, then the system
G(\mathcal{G},\Lambda\times{\mathbb{Z}})
is a frame in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.

(iv)

There exist a generator
\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{C}(\alpha),\alpha>0,
satisfying (
1.9
) and a separated set
\Lambda
with the critical density
D^{-}(\Lambda)=q(\mathcal{G})+1
such that

is not a frame in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Lemma 2.1

Proof.

8.1.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
\Gamma
be a separated set and
\mathcal{G}\in W_{0}
.
Then

(8.1)

\left\|\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}(\cdot-\gamma)\right\|_{p}\leq N^{1/q}(\Gamma)\|\mathcal{G}\|_{W_{0}}\|{\bf c}\|_{p},\quad\mbox{for every }{\bf c}\in l^{p}(\Gamma),

where
q=p/(p-1)
and
N(\Gamma)
is the covering constant

N(\Gamma):=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}\sum_{\Gamma}{\bf 1}_{[0,1]}(x+\gamma).

See Theorem 2.1 in
[
18

for a slightly more general result for the multi-dimensional integer-shifts.

Proof.

Since the set
\Gamma
is separated, we have
N(\Gamma)<\infty.

The proof is obvious when
p=\infty.
Therefore, we assume that
1\leq p<\infty
.

Clearly, for every
h\in L^{q}({\mathbb{R}})
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\int_{k+\gamma}^{k+1+\gamma}|h(x)|^{q}\,dx\leq N(\Gamma)\|h\|_{q}^{q}.

We will use this inequality at the end of the following calculations:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\sum_{\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma)h(x)\right|\,dx\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\Gamma}|c_{\gamma}|\int_{k}^{k+1}|\mathcal{G}(x)h(x+\gamma)|\,dx\leq

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\Gamma}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{\infty}(k,k+1)}|c_{\gamma}|\int_{k+\gamma}^{k+1+\gamma}|h(x)|dx\leq

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{L^{\infty}(k,k+1)}\sum_{\Gamma}|c_{\gamma}|\left(\int_{k+\gamma}^{k+1+\gamma}|h(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\leq N(\Gamma)^{1/q}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{W_{0}}\|{\bf c}\|_{l^{p}}\|h\|_{q}.

Finally,

\left\|\sum_{\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma)\right\|_{p}=\sup_{0<\|h\|_{q}<\infty}\frac{1}{\|h\|_{q}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\sum_{\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma)h(x)\right|dx\leq N(\Gamma)^{1/q}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{W_{0}}\|{\bf c}\|_{l^{p}}.

∎

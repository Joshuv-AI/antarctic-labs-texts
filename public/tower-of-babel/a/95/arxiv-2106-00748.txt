Abstract
. We study Hardy space H L 1 ​ ( X ) H^{1}_{L}(X) related to a self-adjoint operator L L defined on an Euclidean subspace X X of ℝ d {\mathbb{R}^{d}} . We continue study from [ 27 ] , where, under certain assumptions on the heat semigroup exp ⁡ ( − t ​ L ) \exp(-tL) , the atomic characterization of local type for H L 1 ​ ( X ) H^{1}_{L}(X) was proved. In this paper we provide additional assumptions that lead to another characterization of H L 1 ​ ( X ) H^{1}_{L}(X) by the Riesz transforms related to L L . As an application, we prove the Riesz transform characterization of H L 1 ​ ( X ) H^{1}_{L}(X) for multidimensional Bessel and Laguerre operators, and the Dirichlet Laplacian on ℝ + d \mathbb{R}^{d}_{+} .



1.
Introduction and statement of results

1.1.
Introduction.

Let
H_{t}=\exp(t\Delta)
be the heat semigroup on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, i.e.
H_{t}f(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}H_{t}(x-y)f(y)\,dy
and

(1.1)

H_{t}(x-y)=(4\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}\right),\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\ t>0.

The classical Hardy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be defined by the maximal operator related to the operators
H_{t}
and plays an important role in harmonic analysis. We say that a function
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
is in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if

\left\|f\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\left\|\sup_{t>0}\left|H_{t}f(\cdot)\right|\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty.

There are many equivalent definitions of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
related to various objects in harmonic analysis. The interested reader is referred to
[
37
]
and references therein. Let us recall that the Riesz transforms
\widetilde{R}_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}(-\Delta)^{-1/2}
,
j=1,...,d
, are given by

\begin{split}\widetilde{R}_{j}f(x)&=C_{d}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{|x-y|>\varepsilon}\frac{x_{j}-y_{j}}{|x-y|^{d+1}}f(y)\,dy,\end{split}

where
x=(x_{1},...,x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. One of the classical results states that one can give equivalent definition of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in terms of the Riesz transforms, c.f.
[
22
]
. More precisely a function
f
belongs to
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
if and only if all the functions:
f,\widetilde{R}_{1}f,...,\widetilde{R}_{d}f
belong to
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and

(1.2)

\left\|f\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\simeq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|\widetilde{R}_{j}f\right\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}.

On the other hand, a function
f
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be decomposed as an infinite linear combination of simple functions called atoms, see
[
14
]
and
[
28
]
. More precisely, for a function
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we can write

(1.3)

f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}a_{k},

where
\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|<\infty
and
a_{k}
are
atoms
, i.e. there exist balls
B_{k}
in
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
such that:

(1.4)

\mathrm{supp}\,a_{k}\subseteq B_{k},\qquad\left\|a_{k}\right\|_{\infty}\leq|B_{k}|^{-1},\qquad\int_{B_{k}}a_{k}(x)dx=0.

Here
|B_{k}|
is the Lebesgue measure of the ball
B_{k}
. For more properties of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we refer the reader to
[
37
]
and references therein.

One can consider
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as related to the classical Laplacian
\Delta
on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, since many possible definitions of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are given in terms of
\Delta
. Since the 60’s many researchers considered the Hardy spaces
H^{1}_{L}(X)
related to various self-adjoint operators
L
on some metric-measure spaces
X
, see e.g.
[
38
,
28
,
36
,
15
,
22
,
2
,
6
,
20
,
21
,
16
,
18
,
25
,
23
]
. A natural question in this theory is the following: can we have decompositions of the type (
1.3
) for
f\in H^{1}_{L}(X)
? Also, whether the equivalence similar to (
1.2
) holds or not? It appears that now we have many general results concerning atomic decompositions for
H^{1}_{L}(X)
, see e.g.
[
25
,
20
,
34
,
38
]
. However, the characterization of
H^{1}_{L}(X)
in terms of the Riesz transforms is not known in such generality.

In the present paper we shall continue study in the context introduced in
[
27
]
. Recall, that in
[
27
]
we consider a space
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and a nonnegative self-adjoint operator
L
on
L^{2}(X)
. The semigroup
\exp(-tL)
satisfy the upper Gaussian estimates and, roughly speaking, the kernel
T_{t}(x,y)
of
\exp(-tL)
is similar to
H_{t}(x-y)
for local times and
T_{t}(x,y)
decays faster for global times, where the scale of time is adjusted to some covering
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X
. For a precise statement of these assumptions see
[
27
]
or Section
1.2
below. The main issue considered in
[
27
]
was the characterization of
H_{L}^{1}(X)
in terms of the atomic decompositions. It was proved there that in this context one has atoms for
H^{1}_{L}(X)
that are either classical atoms (as in (
1.4
)) or atoms of the form
a(x)=|Q|^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{Q}(x)
,
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
. The latter atoms are called ”local atoms”, c.f.
[
23
]
.

Our goal here is to characterise
H^{1}_{L}(X)
by the Riesz transforms
D_{j}L^{-1/2}
,
j=1,...,d
, where
D_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}+V_{j}
is a derivative adapted to
L
. To this end we add additional assumptions for the kernels:
\partial_{x_{j}}T_{t}(x,y)
,
V_{j}(x)T_{t}(x,y)
. Using this we show a result similar to (
1.2
), i.e. the Hardy space
H^{1}_{L}(X)
is characterized by appropriate Riesz transforms. For other results concerning this question, see e.g.
[
3
,
6
,
33
,
22
,
29
,
17
,
24
,
19
,
32
]
.

Our main motivation here is to give an uniform approach that will work in different contexts to study operators such as: multidimensional Bessel and Laguerre operators, and the Dirichlet Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
. In the last and most technical section we verify that our assumptions are indeed satisfied for these examples.

1.2.
Assumptions

In this section we state assumptions that will be used throughout the paper. Let
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a space that is a product of: finite intervals, half-lines, or lines equipped with the Lebesgue measure, i.e.
X=(a_{1},b_{1})\times...\times(a_{d},b_{d})
, where
a_{j}\in[-\infty,\infty)
and
b_{j}\in(-\infty,\infty]
. We shall study a non-negative self-adjoint operator
L
that is densely defined on
L^{2}(X)
. The semigroup generated by
-L
will be denoted by
T_{t}=\exp(-tL)
and we further assume that there exists an integral kernel
T_{t}(x,y)
, such that for
f\in L^{p}(X)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we have

T_{t}f(x)=\int_{X}T_{t}(x,y)f(y)\,dy,\qquad\text{a.e. }x\in X.

The Hardy space
H^{1}_{L}(X)
related to
L
is defined in terms of the maximal operator related to
T_{t}
, namely

H^{1}_{L}(X)=\left\{f\in L^{1}(X)\ :\ \left\|f\right\|_{H^{1}_{L}(X)}:=\left\|\sup_{t>0}\left|T_{t}f\right|\right\|_{L^{1}(X)}<\infty\right\}.

In this paper we shall study the spaces
H^{1}_{L}(X)
that will be related to some coverings
\mathcal{Q}=\left\{Q_{k}\ :\ k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
of
X
, where
Q_{k}
are cuboids. We assume that
\mathcal{Q}
is
an admissible covering
in the sense of Definition
2.1
below. Let
d_{Q}
be the diameter of
Q
and denote by
Q^{*}
a slight enlargement of
Q
, see the comments after Definition
2.1
below. Following
[
27
]
we assume that there exists
\gamma\in(0,1/3)
and
C,c>0
, such that
T_{t}(x,y)
satisfies:

(
A_{0}
)

\displaystyle 0\leq T_{t}(x,y)\leq Ct^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\right),

\displaystyle x,y\in X,t>0,

(
A_{1}
)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in Q^{**}}\int_{(Q^{***})^{c}}\sup_{t>0}t^{\delta}T_{t}(x,y)\,dx\leq Cd_{Q}^{2\delta},

\displaystyle\delta\in[0,\gamma),Q\in\mathcal{Q},

(
A_{2}
)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in Q^{**}}\int_{Q^{***}}\sup_{t\leq d_{Q}^{2}}t^{-\delta}\left|T_{t}(x,y)-H_{t}(x-y)\right|\,dx\leq Cd_{Q}^{-2\delta},

\displaystyle\delta\in[0,\gamma),Q\in\mathcal{Q}.

In
[
27
]
the authors studied
H^{1}_{L}(X)
for operators satisfying (

A
0

)–(

A
2

). It was proved that
H^{1}_{L}(X)
can be characterized by atomic decompositions with local atoms of the form
|Q|^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{Q}
, where
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
, see
[
27
, Thm. A]
and Theorem
2.3
below.

In the present paper we shall study the Riesz transform characterization of
H^{1}_{L}(X)
, when
L
satisfies (

A
0

)–(

A
2

) and the following assumptions that are inspired by certain known examples like: Bessel, Laguerre, or Schrödinger operators. On
L^{2}(X)
consider the operators
R_{j}
formally given by:

R_{j}=(\partial_{x_{j}}+V_{j})L^{-1/2},\qquad j=1,...,d,

where
\partial_{x_{j}}
is the standard derivative and
V_{j}
is a function that depends only on
x_{j}
. Suppose that
T_{t}(x,y)
satisfy:

(
A_{3}
)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in Q^{**}}\int_{(Q^{***})^{c}}\int_{0}^{d_{Q}^{2}}\left|\partial_{x_{j}}T_{t}(x,y)\right|\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,dx\leq C,

\displaystyle Q\in\mathcal{Q},j=1,...,d,

(
A_{4}
)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in Q^{**}}\int_{X}\int_{d_{Q}^{2}}^{\infty}\left|\partial_{x_{j}}T_{t}(x,y)\right|\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,dx\leq C,

\displaystyle Q\in\mathcal{Q},j=1,...,d,

(
A_{5}
)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in Q^{**}}\int_{Q^{***}}\int_{0}^{d_{Q}^{2}}\left|\partial_{x_{j}}\left(T_{t}(x,y)-H_{t}(x-y)\right)\right|\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,dx\leq C,

\displaystyle Q\in\mathcal{Q},j=1,...,d,

(
A_{6}
)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in X}\int_{X}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|V_{j}(x)\right|T_{t}(x,y)\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,dx\leq C,

\displaystyle j=1,...,d.

For
j=1,...,d
define the kernels

(1.5)

R_{j}(x,y):=\pi^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\partial_{x_{j}}+V_{j}(x_{j})\right)T_{t}(x,y)\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}.

Notice that our assumptions guarantee that the integral above exists for a.e.
(x,y)
. The operators
R_{j}
are defined as follows:

R_{j}f(x)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{|x-y|>\varepsilon}R_{j}(x,y)f(y)\,dy,\qquad x\in X.

We always assume that
R_{j}
are bounded on
L^{2}(X)
.

1.3.
Results

Our first main result is the following theorem, that describes the Hardy space
H^{1}_{L}(X)
in terms of the Riesz transforms.

Theorem A
.

Assume that there is an operator
L
and an admissible covering
\mathcal{Q}
as in Section
1.2
. In particular, we assume that (

A
0

)–(

A
6

) are satisfied. Then
f\in H^{1}_{L}(X)
if and only if
f,R_{1}f,...,R_{d}f\in L^{1}(X)
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

C^{-1}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{1}_{L}(X)}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(X)}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|R_{j}f\right\|_{L^{1}(X)}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{H^{1}_{L}(X)}.

The proof of Theorem
A
is given in Section
3.1
below and it is based on known techniques. The main idea is to compare (locally)
R_{j}
with the classical Riesz transforms
\widetilde{R}_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}(-\Delta)^{1/2}
and use additional decay as
t\to\infty
.

One of our main motivations is to study product cases. Assume that for
i=1,...,N
we have operators
L_{i}
satisfying the assumptions as in Section
1.2
. In particular,
L_{i}
is associated with the semigroup
T_{t}^{[i]}
that has a kernel
T_{t}^{[i]}(x_{i},y_{i}),\ x_{i},y_{i}\in X_{i}
. Then we can define

(1.6)

X=\prod_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}\subseteq\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{R}^{d_{i}}={\mathbb{R}^{d}}

and

(1.7)

L=L_{1}+...+L_{N},

such that each
L_{i}
acts only on the variable
x_{i}\in X_{i}
. For more precise description see Section
2.2
below. The following theorem gives the Riesz transform characterization for
H^{1}_{L}(X)
in the product case.

Theorem B
.

Let
X
and
L
be as in (
1.6
)–(
1.7
) and assume that for each
i=1,...,N
the semigroup kernel
T_{t}^{[i]}(x_{i},y_{i})
together with an admissible covering
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
of
X_{i}
satisfy the conditions (

A
0

)–(

A
6

). Then
f\in H^{1}_{L}(X)
if and only if
f,R_{1}f,...,R_{d}f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
. Moreover,

C^{-1}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{1}_{L}(X)}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|R_{j}f\right\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{H^{1}_{L}(X)}.

The proof of Theorem
B
is given in Section
3.2
below. We shall use
[
27
, Thm. B]
, where we proved that assuming (

A
0

)–(

A
2

) for
T_{t}^{[i]}(x_{i},y_{i})
and
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
we can define an admissible covering
\mathcal{Q}_{1}\boxtimes...\boxtimes Q_{N}
that describes
H^{1}_{L}(X)
for
L=L_{1}+...+L_{N}
, see
[
27
, Def. 1.5]
.

As an example of applications of Theorem
B
we study certain multidimensional Bessel and Laguerre operators. Thanks to Theorem
B
it is enough to verify (

A
0

)–(

A
6

) only in the one-dimensional case. Then, the Riesz transform characterization for
H^{1}_{L}(X)
for the multidimensional case (when
L
is the sum of Bessel or Laguerre operators) follows from Theorem
B
. Furthermore, a similar argument (see Section
4
) allows us to study the Dirichlet Laplacian on the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
. Below we briefly recall the operators that we work with and state the results.

Bessel operator.
Let
X=(0,\infty)^{d}
. For
\beta=(\beta_{1},...,\beta_{d})
assume
\beta_{i}>0
,

, and consider the multidimensional Bessel operator

(1.8)

L_{B}^{[\beta]}=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\frac{d^{2}}{dx_{i}}-\frac{\beta_{i}^{2}-\beta_{i}}{x_{i}^{2}}\right),\qquad x_{1},...,x_{d}>0.

More precisely, by

we shall denote a proper self-adjoint operator defined on

, see e.g.

. Harmonic analysis related to

Denote

(1.9)

\mathcal{Q}_{B}=\left\{[2^{n},2^{n+1}]\,:n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

Then
\mathcal{Q}_{B}
is an admissible covering for
(0,\infty)

Theorem C
.

Let

,

and

2.1.
Admissible coverings

Let
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

Q(z,r_{1},...,r_{d})=\left\{x\in X\ :\ |x_{i}-z_{i}|\leq r_{i}\text{ for }i=1,...,d\right\},

Q(z,r)=Q(z,r,...,r).
The following definition will be used throughout the paper, c.f.
[

.

Definition
\the\thmcount
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a set of cuboids in
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. We call
\mathcal{Q}
an admissible covering if:

1.

X=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}Q
,

2.

if
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}
and
Q_{1}\neq Q_{2}
, then
|Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}|=0
,

3.

if
Q=Q(z,r_{1},...,r_{d})\in\mathcal{Q}
, then
r_{i}\simeq r_{j}
for
i,j\in\left\{1,...,d\right\}
,

4.

if
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}
and
Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}\neq\emptyset
, then
d_{Q_{1}}\simeq d_{Q_{2}}
,

5.

if
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
, then
\mathrm{dist_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}}(Q,{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\setminus X)\gtrsim d_{Q}
.

Having an admissible covering
\mathcal{Q}
and
Q=(z,r_{1},...,r_{d})\in\mathcal{Q}
, we define

Q^{*}:=Q(z,\kappa r_{1},...,\kappa r_{d}),

where
\kappa>1
is chosen so that for
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}
,

Q_{1}^{***}\cap Q_{2}^{***}\neq\emptyset\quad\iff\quad Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}\neq\emptyset

and

\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}(Q^{***},{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\setminus X)>0.

The family
\left\{Q^{***}\right\}_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}
is a finite covering of
X
, namely

(2.1)

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\mathbbm{1}_{Q^{***}}(x)\leq C,\qquad x\in X.

Let us notice that we have a flexibility in choosing the enlargements
Q^{*}
,
Q^{**}
,
Q^{***}
etc. In particular the notation in
[
27
]
is slightly different. Recall that having admissible coverings
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
of
X_{i}
,
i=1,...,N
, we can produce a natural admissible covering
\mathcal{Q}_{1}\boxtimes...\boxtimes\mathcal{Q}_{N}
of
X
as in (
1.6
), see
[
27
, Def. 1.5]
.

2.2.
Products

In this subsection
i
will be always an index from
\left\{1,...,N\right\}
. Let
X_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{i}}
and
L_{i}
are as in Section
1.2
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{i}})
. Set
d=d_{1}+...+d_{N}
and let
X
be as (
1.6
). Now, we shall explain the precise meaning of (
1.7
).
Slightly abusing the notation we keep the symbol
L_{i}
for the operator

\underbrace{I\otimes...\otimes I}_{i-1\text{ times}}\otimes L_{i}\otimes\underbrace{I\otimes...\otimes I}_{N-i\text{ times}}

on
L^{2}(X)
, where
I
denotes the identity operator on the corresponding subspace, and we define

Lf(x)=L_{1}f(x)+\ldots+L_{N}f(x),\qquad x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in X.

Since the operators
L_{i}
are self-adjoint, the operator
L
is well defined and essentially self-adjoint, see e.g.
[
35
, Thm. 7.23]
.

Recall that the semigroups
T_{t}^{[i]}=\exp\left(-tL_{i}\right)
on

have the kernels
T_{t}^{[i]}(x_{i},y_{i})
,
x_{i},y_{i}\in X_{i},t>0
, so that the semigroup
T_{t}=\exp(-tL)
is related to the kernel

T_{t}(x,y)=T_{t}^{[1]}(x_{1},y_{1})\cdot...\cdot T_{t}^{[N]}(x_{N},y_{N}).

2.3.
Local atomic Hardy spaces

For an admissible covering
\mathcal{Q}
of
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
(see Definition
2.1
) we shall define
the local atomic Hardy space

H^{1}_{at}(\mathcal{Q})
related to
\mathcal{Q}
as follows.

Definition
\the\thmcount
.

A function
a\,:\,X\to\mathbb{C}
is called a
\mathcal{Q}-atom
if either:

(i)

there is
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
and a cube
K\subset Q^{**}
, such that:

\mathrm{supp}\,a\subseteq K,\ \ \left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\leq|K|^{-1},\ \ \int a(x)\,dx=0;

or

(ii)

there exists
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
such that

a(x)=|Q|^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{Q}(x).

Then, the atomic space
H^{1}_{at}(\mathcal{Q})
, is defined in a standard way. Namely, we say that a function
f
is in
H^{1}_{at}(\mathcal{Q})
if
f=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}a_{k}
with
\mathcal{Q}
-atoms
a_{k}
and
\sum_{k}|\lambda_{k}|<\infty
. Moreover, the norm of
H^{1}_{at}(\mathcal{Q})
is given by

\left\|f\right\|_{H^{1}_{at}(\mathcal{Q})}=\inf\sum_{k}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|,

where the infimum is taken over all possible representations of
f=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}a_{k}
as above. A standard argument shows that
H^{1}_{at}(\mathcal{Q})
is a Banach subspace of
L^{1}(X)
.

Here we state the atomic decomposition result that follows from
[
27
, Thm. A]
. This will be needed later on in the proof of Theorem
A
.

Theorem
\the\thmcount
.

Assume that for
L,T_{t}
, and an admissible covering
\mathcal{Q}
the assumptions (

A
0

)–(

A
2

) are satisfied. Then
H^{1}_{L}(X)=H^{1}_{at}(\mathcal{Q})
and the corresponding norms are equivalent.

2.4.
Classical local Hardy spaces.

In this section we recall briefly some theory related to the classical local Hardy spaces on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, c.f.
[
23
,
37
]
. In particular, we shall present the relation between classical local Hardy spaces and local Riesz transforms in Proposition
2.4
.

Recall that the kernel of the Riesz transform
\widetilde{R}_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}(-\Delta)^{-1/2}
can be given by
\widetilde{R}_{j}(x,y)=\pi^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{x_{j}}H_{t}(x-y)\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}
and for
\tau>0
denote

\widetilde{R}^{j}_{\tau,loc}(x,y)=\pi^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{\tau^{2}}\partial_{x_{j}}H_{t}(x-y)\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}},\qquad\widetilde{R}^{j}_{\tau,glob}(x,y)=\pi^{-1/2}\int_{\tau^{2}}^{\infty}\partial_{x_{j}}H_{t}(x-y)\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}.

It is well known that these kernels are related (in the principal value sense) with the operators
\widetilde{R}^{j}_{\tau,loc}
and
\widetilde{R}^{j}_{\tau,glob}
that are well-defined and bounded on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
(uniformly in
\tau>0
). In what follows we shall need the following version of the characterization of the local Hardy spaces.

Proposition
\the\thmcount
.

There exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
that does not depend on
\tau>0
such that:

1.

If
a
is either a classical atom or local atom of the form
a=|Q|^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{Q}
, where
Q=Q(z,r_{1},...,r_{d})
,
r_{1}\simeq...\simeq r_{d}\simeq\tau
, we have

\left\|a\right\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|\widetilde{R}^{j}_{\tau,loc}a\right\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}\leq C_{1}.

2.

Assume that
\mathrm{supp}f\subseteq Q^{*}
, where
Q=Q(z,r_{1},...,r_{d})
,
r_{1}\simeq...\simeq r_{d}\simeq\tau
, and

M:=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(Q^{*})}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|\widetilde{R}^{j}_{\tau,loc}f\right\|_{L^{1}(Q^{**})}<\infty.

Then there exist sequences
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k}
and
\{a_{k}\}_{k}
, such that:
f=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}a_{k}
,
\sum_{k}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|\leq C_{2}M
, and
a_{k}
are either the classical atoms supported in a cube
K\subseteq Q^{**}
or
a_{k}=|Q|^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{Q}
.

Sketch of the proof.

This fact is well known and has quite standard proof. For the convenience of the reader we provide a sketch of the proof. Notice that

\widetilde{R}^{j}_{\tau,loc}(x,y)=c_{d}\frac{x_{j}-y_{j}}{|x-y|^{d+1}}\psi\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\tau}\right),

where
\psi
is smooth on
[0,\infty)
,
\psi(0)=c_{d}^{\prime}
and
\psi(s)\simeq e^{-s^{2}}
as
s\to\infty
.

Part
1.
follows by a standard Calderón-Zygmund argument. The main idea is to use the
L^{2}
-estimate on
Q(x_{0},2\tau)
and the estimate
\widetilde{R}^{j}_{\tau,loc}(x,y)\leq\tau|x-y|^{-d-1}
for
y\in Q(x_{0},\tau)
and
x\not\in Q(x_{0},2\tau)
.

In order to prove
2.
define
\lambda_{0}=\int f
and let

g(x)=f(x)-\lambda_{0}|Q|^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{Q}(x).

Then
a_{0}(x)=|Q|^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{Q}(x)
is one of our atoms,
|\lambda_{0}|\leq M
,
\mathrm{supp}\,g\subseteq Q^{*}
and
\int g=0
. By standard computations one may check that

\left\|g\right\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|\widetilde{R}_{j}g\right\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}\lesssim M.

Using the classical characterization of
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
by means of the Riesz transforms, see (
1.2
), we obtain

g(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}a_{k}(x),

where
a_{k}
are classical atoms on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|\lesssim M.

Then

f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}a_{k}(x),\qquad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|\lesssim M.

This may look that we are done, but notice that we also want to have atoms
a_{k}
supported in
Q^{**}
(not anywhere in
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
). This can be done by a standard procedure, for details see e.g.
[
26
, Thm. 2.2(b)]
. Let us notice, that here we make use of the property
5.
of Definition
2.1
, i.e. we enlarge
Q
in
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, but we want to have atoms supported in
Q^{**}
that is still in
X
.
∎

2.5.
Partition of unity

In what follows we shall decompose functions using an admissible covering
\mathcal{Q}
of

2.7.
Riesz transforms.

For

and

(2.2)

\begin{split}R^{j}_{\tau,loc}(x,y)=\pi^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{\tau^{2}}\partial_{x_{j}}T_{t}(x,y)\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}},\qquad x,y\in X,\\

Here we shall prove some preliminary estimate that will be needed later on.

Lemma
\the\thmcount
.

Suppose that (

A
3

) – (

A
6

) are satisfied for

and

. Then

\sup_{y\in X}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\int_{Q^{**}}\left|R_{j}(x,y)\right|\left|\psi_{Q}(x)-\psi_{Q}(y)\right|\,dx\leq C.

Proof.

Fix
y\in X
and
Q_{0}\in\mathcal{Q}
such that

. Write

\begin{split}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\int_{Q^{**}}\left|R_{j}(x,y)\right|&\left|\psi_{Q}(x)-\psi_{Q}(y)\right|\,dx\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\int_{Q^{**}}\left|R^{j}_{d_{Q_{0}},glob}(x,y)\right|\left|\psi_{Q}(x)-\psi_{Q}(y)\right|\,dx\\
&+\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\int_{Q^{**}\cap(Q_{0}^{***})^{c}}\left|R^{j}_{d_{Q_{0}},loc}(x,y)\right|\left|\psi_{Q}(x)-\psi_{Q}(y)\right|\,dx\\
&+\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\int_{Q^{**}\cap Q_{0}^{***}}\left|R^{j}_{d_{Q_{0}},loc}(x,y)\right|\left|\psi_{Q}(x)-\psi_{Q}(y)\right|\,dx\\
&+\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\int_{Q^{**}}\left|R^{j}_{V}(x,y)\right|\left|\psi_{Q}(x)-\psi_{Q}(y)\right|\,dx\\
=&S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}+S_{4}.\end{split}

Using
\|\psi_{Q}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, (
2.1
), (

A
4

), (

A
3

) and (

A
6

) we have

\displaystyle S_{1}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{X}\int_{d_{Q_{0}}^{2}}^{\infty}\left|\partial_{x_{j}}T_{t}(x,y)\right|\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,dx\lesssim 1,

\displaystyle S_{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{(Q_{0}^{***})^{c}}\int_{0}^{d_{Q_{0}}^{2}}\left|\partial_{x_{j}}T_{t}(x,y)\right|\,\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,dx\lesssim 1,

\displaystyle S_{4}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{X}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|V_{j}(x)\right|T_{t}(x,y)\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,dx\lesssim 1.

For

such that

. The number of such

, and

. Applying (

\begin{split}S_{3}\lesssim&\int_{Q_{0}^{***}}\int_{0}^{d_{Q_{0}}^{2}}\left|\partial_{x_{j}}\left(T_{t}(x,y)-H_{t}(x-y)\right)\right|\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,dx\\

&\lesssim 1+d_{Q_{0}}^{-1}\int_{Q_{0}^{***}}|x-y|^{-d+1}\,dx\lesssim 1.\end{split}

∎

3.1.
Proof of Theorem

Proof.

Denote

\left\|f\right\|_{H^{1}_{L,\mathrm{Riesz}}(X)}:=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(X)}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|R_{j}f\right\|_{L^{1}(X)}.

First inequality:

We shall show that

(3.1)

for

and a
\mathcal{Q}
-atom

with

independent of

. In general, (
3.1
) may not be enough to prove boundedness of an operator on

, see
[

. However, here Theorem

, (

), and a standard continuity argument imply

), according to Definition

, suppose that

is an
\mathcal{Q}
-atom associated with

. Let

,
R^{j}_{d_{Q},glob}
and

denote the operators with the integral kernels defined in (

). Applying (

A
6

), (

A
4

), (

A
3

), (

5

), and part

of Proposition

\begin{split}\left\|R_{j}a\right\|_{L^{1}(X)}&\leq\left\|R^{j}_{V}a\right\|_{L^{1}(X)}+\left\|R^{j}_{d_{Q},glob}a\right\|_{L^{1}(X)}+\left\|R^{j}_{d_{Q},loc}a\right\|_{L^{1}((Q^{***})^{c})}\\
&+\left\|\left(R^{j}_{d_{Q},loc}-\widetilde{R}^{j}_{d_{Q},loc}\right)a\right\|_{L^{1}(Q^{***})}+\left\|\widetilde{R}^{j}_{d_{Q},loc}a\right\|_{L^{1}(Q^{***})}\leq C\end{split}

and (

Second inequality:

.
Assume that

, see Section

, with

and

. Notice that

\begin{split}\widetilde{R}^{j}_{d_{Q},loc}f_{Q}=&\left(\widetilde{R}^{j}_{d_{Q},loc}-R^{j}_{d_{Q},loc}\right)f_{Q}+\left(R_{j}f_{Q}-\psi_{Q}R_{j}f\right)\\
&-R^{j}_{d_{Q},glob}f_{Q}-R^{j}_{V}f_{Q}+\psi_{Q}R_{j}f.\end{split}

We use (

5

), Lemma

, (

4

), (

6

) getting

\begin{split}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\left\|\widetilde{R}^{j}_{d_{Q},loc}f_{Q}\right\|_{L^{1}(Q^{**})}\leq&\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\left[\left\|\left(\widetilde{R}^{j}_{d_{Q},loc}-R^{j}_{d_{Q},loc}\right)f_{Q}\right\|_{L^{1}(Q^{**})}+\left\|R_{j}f_{Q}-\psi_{Q}R_{j}f\right\|_{L^{1}(Q^{**})}\right.\\
&\left.+\left\|R^{j}_{d_{Q},glob}f_{Q}\right\|_{L^{1}(Q^{**})}+\left\|R^{j}_{V}f_{Q}\right\|_{L^{1}(Q^{**})}+\left\|\psi_{Q}R_{j}f\right\|_{L^{1}(Q^{*})}\right]\\
\lesssim&\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(Q^{*})}+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(X)}+\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\left\|R_{j}f\right\|_{L^{1}(Q^{*})}\\

for every

. Now we use part

of Proposition

for each

, getting

&+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{1\vee\ln(\sqrt{xy})}...+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{1\vee\ln(\sqrt{xy})}^{\infty}...\\
=&A_{12}+A_{13}+A_{14}+A_{15}+A_{16}+A_{17}+A_{18}.\end{split}

&\lesssim y^{-1}\int_{0}^{2^{-n}}\,dx\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{ct}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim 2^{-2n}\lesssim 1.\end{split}

For
A_{13}

xy\lesssim t

\begin{split}A_{13}&\lesssim y^{\beta}\cdot\int_{0}^{2^{-n}}x^{\beta-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\beta}\exp\left(-\frac{y^{2}}{ct}\right)\frac{dt}{t}\,dx\\
&\lesssim y^{-\beta}\cdot\int_{0}^{2^{-n}}x^{\beta-1}\,dx\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\beta}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{ct}\right)\frac{dt}{t}\\
&\lesssim 2^{-2\beta n}\lesssim 1,\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used that
\beta>0

For
A_{14}
we have
xy\gtrsim t
,
|x-y|\simeq x
, and
x>y
, so that
|F_{2}(t,x,y)|\lesssim x/t
. Using (
5.15
), (
5.17
), and (
5.20

\begin{split}A_{14}\lesssim\int_{2^{n+2}}^{\infty}x\int_{0}^{1}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{ct}\right)\frac{dt}{t^{2}}\,dx\lesssim\int_{2^{n+2}}^{\infty}x^{1-2N}\,dx\cdot\int_{0}^{1}t^{N-2}\,dt\lesssim 1.\end{split}

For
A_{15}
we have that
xy\gtrsim t
,
x\simeq y\simeq 2^{n}
, and
|F_{3}(t,x,y)|\lesssim xt
. Using (
5.16
), (
5.17
), and (
5.21

\begin{split}A_{15}\lesssim&\int_{\left\{|x-y|<2^{-n}\right\}}\int_{2^{-2n}}^{1}\exp\left(-cty^{2}\right)\left(\frac{|x-y|}{t}+xt\right)\frac{dt}{t}\,dx\\
\lesssim&y^{-2N}\cdot\int_{\left\{|x-y|<2^{-n}\right\}}|x-y|\,dx\cdot\int_{2^{-2n}}^{\infty}t^{-N-2}\,dt\\
&+y^{-2N}\cdot\int_{\left\{|x-y|<2^{-n}\right\}}x\,dx\cdot\int_{2^{-2n}}^{\infty}t^{-N}\,dt\lesssim 1.\end{split}

For
A_{16}
we have that
xy\gtrsim t
,
t\leq 1
,
x\lesssim y
, and
\left|F_{3}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim x^{-1}+xt
. Using (
5.16
), (
5.17
), and (
5.21
),

\begin{split}A_{16}\lesssim&\int_{(2^{-n},2^{n+2})\cap\left\{|x-y|>2^{-n}\right\}}\int_{2^{-2n}}^{1}e^{-ty^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\right)\left(\frac{|x-y|}{t}+x^{-1}+tx\right)\frac{dt}{t}\,dx\\
=&A_{16,1}+A_{16,2}+A_{16,3},\end{split}

where
A_{16,1},A_{16,2},A_{16,3}
are the integrals with:
|x-y|t^{-1},x^{-1},xt
, respectively.

\begin{split}A_{16,1}&\lesssim y^{-2N}\int_{\left\{|x-y|>2^{-n}\right\}}|x-y|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-N-1}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}\,dx\\
&\lesssim 2^{-2nN}\int_{\left\{|x-y|>2^{-n}\right\}}|x-y|^{-2N-1}\,dx\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-N-2}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{ct}\right)\,dt\lesssim 1.\end{split}

Notice that
x^{-1}\leq 2^{n}
, thus

\begin{split}A_{16,2}&\lesssim y^{-2N}\int_{(2^{-n},\infty)\cap\left\{|x-y|>2^{-n}\right\}}x^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-N}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}\,dx\\
&\lesssim 2^{n(1-2N)}\int_{\left\{|x-y|>2^{-n}\right\}}|x-y|^{-2N}\,dx\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-N}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{ct}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim 1.\end{split}

\begin{split}A_{16,3}&\lesssim\int_{0}^{2^{n+2}}x\,dx\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-cty^{2}}\,dt\lesssim 2^{2n}\cdot 2^{-2n}\lesssim 1.\end{split}

For
A_{17}
we have that
xy\gtrsim\mathrm{sh}(2t)
,
t\geq 1
, and
\left|F_{2}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim x+y\lesssim y(x+1)
. Using (
5.15
), (
5.18
), and (
5.20
),

\begin{split}A_{17}\lesssim&ye^{-cy^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(x+1)e^{-cx^{2}}\,dx\cdot\int_{1}^{\infty}(\mathrm{sh}(2t))^{-1/2}\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\lesssim 1.\end{split}

For
A_{18}
we have that
xy\lesssim\mathrm{sh}(2t)
,
t\geq 1
, and
\left|F_{1}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim(xy/\mathrm{sh}(2t))^{\beta-1/2}\cdot(x+x^{-1})
. Using (
5.14
) and (
5.19
),

\begin{split}A_{18}\lesssim&\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-c(x^{2}+y^{2})}\left(\frac{xy}{\mathrm{sh}(2t)}\right)^{\beta}(x+x^{-1})\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t\cdot\mathrm{sh}(2t)}}\,dx\\
\lesssim&y^{\beta}e^{-cy^{2}}\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\beta}(x+x^{-1})e^{-cx^{2}}\,dx\cdot\int_{1}^{\infty}(\mathrm{sh}(2t))^{-\beta-1/2}\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\lesssim 1.\end{split}

Let us write

Our goal is to prove

. Observe that by using (

), (

Similarly, we get the estimates

(5.25)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1\land xy}T_{t}(x,y)\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\lesssim e^{-cy^{2}},

\displaystyle 2x\leq y,\ y\geq 1,

(5.26)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1\land xy}T_{t}(x,y)\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\lesssim e^{-cx/y},

\displaystyle 2x/3\geq y,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1\land xy}T_{t}(x,y)\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\lesssim e^{-cx^{2}},

Moreover, by (

), (

(5.28)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1\land xy}T_{t}(x,y)\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}&\lesssim\int_{0}^{xy}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\right)\frac{dt}{t}\\
&\lesssim\int_{|x-y|^{2}/(xy)}^{\infty}e^{-ct}\,\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim\ln\left(\frac{y}{|x-y|}\right),\end{split}

and, for
|x-y|\leq y/2

&\lesssim\frac{\ln(2+(y|x-y|)^{-1})}{1+y^{2}|x-y|^{2}}.\end{split}

Consider first

in

&+\int_{3y/2}^{\infty}xe^{-cx/y}\,dx+\int_{3y/2}^{\infty}x^{-1}e^{-cx/y}\,dx\lesssim 1.\end{split}

Now consider
A_{19}
in
the case
y\geq 1
. Using (
5.24
), (
5.25
), (
5.29
), and (

\begin{split}A_{19}\lesssim&\int_{0}^{(2y)^{-1}}e^{-cy/x}\,\frac{dx}{x}+\int_{(2y)^{-1}}^{y/2}(x+x^{-1})e^{-cy^{2}}\,dx\\

&\lesssim e^{-cy}+(y^{2}+\ln y)e^{-cy^{2}}+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\ln(2+|x|^{-1})}{1+x^{2}}dx+\int_{1}^{\infty}xe^{-cx^{2}}\,dx\lesssim 1.\end{split}

Recall that
\beta>0

Dirichlet Laplacian on

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem

and the Laplacian

with the Dirichlet boundary condition at

where

,
H_{t}
is as in (
1.1

(5.30)

\begin{split}T_{t,D}(x,y)=H_{t}(x-y)-H_{t}(x+y),\qquad x,y,t>0.\end{split}

Observe that
L_{D}
is of the form as in Section
4
. Therefore, due to Theorem
4
, it is enough to prove Theorem
E
in the case

.

Assume then that
d=1
,
X=(0,\infty)
, and
L_{D}
that is related to the kernel (
5.30
). It is sufficient to check that for
T_{t,D}
and the admissible covering

(5.31)

\begin{split}\mathcal{Q}_{D}=\left\{[2^{n},2^{n+1}]\ :\ n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\end{split}

of
(0,\infty)
the conditions (

A
0

)–(

A
5

) are satisfied (obviously, in this case (

A
6

) is automatically satisfied). This is stated in the following lemma.

Lemma
\the\thmcount
.

Let
T_{t,D}(x,y)
and
\mathcal{Q}_{D}
be as in (
5.30
) and (
5.31
), respectively. Then (

A
0

)–(

5

Sketch of the proof.

Notice that

\begin{split}T_{t,D}(x,y)=H_{t}(x,y)\Omega(t,x,y),\qquad x,y,t>0,\end{split}

where

\Omega(t,x,y)=1-\exp(-xy/t)\simeq\min(1,xy/t).

Moreover,

(5.32)

\begin{split}\partial_{x}T_{t,D}(x,y)&=H_{t}(x,y)\left(\Omega(t,x,y)\frac{x-y}{t}+\frac{y}{t}\exp\left(-\frac{xy}{t}\right)\right).\end{split}

The conditions (

A
0

)–(

A
5

) can be proved by using standard estimates. For the convenience of the reader, we shall present the proof of (

A
4

). Let
I\in\mathcal{Q}_{D}
and assume that
I=[2^{n},2^{n+1}]
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Fix
y\in I^{**}
. From (
5.32
) we have

We write

\begin{split}\int_{X}\int_{d_{I}^{2}}^{\infty}\left|\partial_{x}T_{t,D}(x,y)\right|\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,dx&=\int_{0}^{2^{n+2}}\int_{d_{I}^{2}}^{\infty}...+\int_{2^{n+2}}^{\infty}\int_{d_{I}^{2}}^{\infty}...\\
=A_{1}+A_{2}.\end{split}

For
A_{1}
we have that
xy\lesssim t
and
x\lesssim\sqrt{t}
. From (
5.32
) we have
\left|\partial_{x}T_{t,D}(x,y)\right|\lesssim yt^{-3/2}(x/\sqrt{t}+1)\lesssim yt^{-3/2}
and

A_{1}\lesssim\int_{0}^{2^{n+2}}\int_{2^{2n}}^{\infty}yt^{-3/2}\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\,dx\lesssim 2^{n}\int_{0}^{2^{n+2}}\,dx\cdot\int_{2^{2n}}^{\infty}t^{-2}\,dt\lesssim 1.

For
A_{2}
we have that
|x|\simeq|x-y|
and
y\lesssim\sqrt{t}
. From (
5.32
),

\begin{split}\left|\partial_{x}T_{t,D}(x,y)\right|&\lesssim t^{-1}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\right)\left(\min(1,xy/t)+y/\sqrt{t}\right)\\
&\lesssim t^{-1}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{ct}\right)\cdot\begin{cases}\frac{y}{\sqrt{t}}&\text{ \ \ for }\frac{xy}{t}\lesssim 1\\
1&\text{ \ \ for }\frac{xy}{t}\gtrsim 1\end{cases}.\end{split}

Therefore,

\begin{split}\int_{d_{I}^{2}}^{\infty}\left|\partial_{x}T_{t,D}(x,y)\right|\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}&\lesssim\int_{d_{I}^{2}}^{xy}...+\int_{xy}^{\infty}...\\
&\lesssim\int_{0}^{xy}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{ct}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t\sqrt{t}}+y\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{ct}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\int_{0}^{xy}\left(\frac{t}{x^{2}}\right)^{3/2}\,\frac{dt}{t\sqrt{t}}+yx^{-2}\lesssim yx^{-2}.\end{split}

Hence,

A_{2}\lesssim 2^{n}\int_{2^{n+2}}^{\infty}x^{-2}\,dx\lesssim 1.

This finishes the proof of (

A
4

).

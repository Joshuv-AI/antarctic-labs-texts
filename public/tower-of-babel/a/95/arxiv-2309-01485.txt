Abstract
. Quantum complete intersections A = A ⁡ ( 𝐪 , 𝐚 ) A=A({\bf q,a}) are Frobenius algebras, but in the most cases they can not become Hopf algebras. This paper aims to find bi-Frobenius algebra structures on A A . A key step is the construction of comultiplication, such that A A becomes a bi-Frobenius algebra. By introducing compatible permutation and permutation antipode, a necessary and sufficient condition is found, such that A A admits a bi-Frobenius algebra structure with permutation antipode; and if this is the case, then a concrete construction is explicitly given. Using this, intrinsic conditions only involving the structure coefficients ( 𝐪 , 𝐚 ) ({\bf q,a}) of A A are obtained, for A A admitting a bi-Frobenius algebra structure with permutation antipode. When A A is a symmetric algebra, then A A admits a bi-Frobenius algebra structure with permutation antipode if and only if there exists a compatible permutation π \pi with A A such that π 2 = Id \pi^{2}={\rm Id} . Keywords: quantum complete intersection, coalgebra, bi-Frobenius algebra, Nakayama automorphism, compatible permutation, permutation antipode

Introduction

Frobenius algebras appear in various areas of mathematics, and play important roles in particular in representation theory, Hopf algebras, triangulated categories,
and topological quantum field theory (see e.g.

A bi-Frobenius algebra, introduced by Y. Doi and M. Takeuchi

together with an antipode, satisfying some compatible conditions (Definition

A bi-Frobenius algebra is a Hopf algebra if and only if its comultiplication is an algebra homomorphism (

A Bi-Frobenius algebra shares many pleasant properties similar to Hopf algebras, and its basic theory is developed in

[

,
[

]
,
[

]
, and
[

]
.

A main problem in bi-Frobenius algebras is to find bi-Frobenius algebras which are not Hopf algebras.
The first such example is constructed in
[
DT
]
; more examples
using group-like algebras or quiver techniques are given in
[

]
,
[
WZ
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
, and
[

]

Let

,

A quantum complete intersection

Originated from quantum planes (

It is a quotient algebra of a quantum affine space (

Quantum complete intersections reveal many exotic homological and representation properties of algebras (

Quantum complete intersections
A=A({\bf q,a})
are Frobenius algebras ([B]); however, in the most cases they can not become Hopf algebras.
In fact,
A
admits a Hopf algebra structure if and only if it is commutative, the characteristic of the base field
\mathbb{K}
is a prime

in vector

In particular, if Char

then

can not become a Hopf algebra.
Thus, it is natural to study their possible bi-Frobenius structures. When a quantum complete intersection admits a bi-Frobenius algebra structure,
we simply call it
a bi-Frobenius quantum complete intersection.

This paper aims to study and construct bi-Frobenius quantum complete intersections.
If
A
admits such a structure, then
A
has some remarkable properties. For example, the canonical Nakayama automorphism
of
A
is of order
2
: this is not true in general (see Proposition
4.1
and Remark
4.2
).
A key step of constructing bi-Frobenius algebra structure on
A
is the one of comultiplication,
such that
A
becomes a bi-Frobenius algebra. Since actions of antipodes of all the known bi-Frobenius algebra structures on
A

are given by scalar multiplications, on the standard basis
\mathcal{B}
, up to a permutation
\pi
in the symmetric group
S_{n}
, we study a permutation antipode (see Definition
5.1
).
Such a permutation
\pi
is compatible with the structure coefficients
({\bf q,a})
of
A
(see Definition
3.3
, Theorem
5.2
, and Proposition
5.3
).
For quantum affine spaces, an analogue of compatible permutation and the automorphisms of permutation type have been already studied, e.g. in
[
KKZ
]
,
[
G
]
,
[
CPWZ
]
, and
[
BZ
]
. See also Remark
5.5
.

The main result Theorem

gives a necessary and sufficient condition, such that

admits a bi-Frobenius algebra structure
with permutation antipode. This condition involves

q_{\pi}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}

where

is a compatible permutation in

(for details see Remark

and Subsection 2.5). This element

is in fact

admits a bi-Frobenius algebra structure with permutation antipode
if and only if there exist a compatible permutation

with

and

, such that

If this is the case, then a concrete construction is explicitly given. For details see Theorem
6.1
(and Subsection 2.5).
When

is a symmetric algebra, then

admits a bi-Frobenius algebra structure with permutation antipode
if and only if there exists a compatible permutation

with

Based on Theorem

are obtained, for

is in

The results obtained in this paper also provide a large class of bi-Frobenius algebras which are not Hopf algebras.

The paper is organized as follows. Preliminaries are recalled in Section 2.
In Section 3 we study the structure coefficients

2.
Preliminaries

We consider a finite-dimensional algebra
A
over a field
\mathbb{K}
.
The
\mathbb{K}
-linear dual
A^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(A,\ \mathbb{K})
of
A
has a canonical
A
-
A
-bimodule structure:
(a\rightharpoonup f\leftharpoonup b)(x)=f(bxa),\ \forall\ a,b,x\in A,\ \forall\ f\in A^{*}
, where
\rightharpoonup
and
\leftharpoonup
denote the left and the right action of
A
on
A^{*}
, respectively.

2.1.
Frobenius algebras and Nakayama automorphisms

Recall that
A
is
a Frobenius algebra
if
A\cong A^{*}
as left
A
-modules, or equivalently, as right
A
-modules.
If
A\cong A^{*}
as
A
-
A
-bimodules, then
A
is called
a symmetric algebra
.
A more explicit (and equivalent) definition of a Frobenius algebra is a pair
(A,\phi)
, where
\phi\in A^{*}
such that
A^{*}=A\rightharpoonup\phi
, or equivalently,
A^{*}=\phi\leftharpoonup A
.
If this is the case, then
\phi
is referred to
a Frobenius form
of
A
.
For more equivalent definitions and properties of a Frobenius algebra, we refer to K. Yamagata [Y], or A. Skowronski and K. Yamagata [SY].

Let
(A,\ \phi)
be a Frobenius algebra. Then there is a unique algebra isomorphism
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}:A\longrightarrow A

such that

\phi(xy)=\phi(y\mathcal{N}_{\phi}(x)),\ \forall\ x,\ y\in A.

It is called
the Nakayama automorphism of
A
with respect to
\phi
. Let
U(A)
denote the set of invertible elements of
A
. One can find the following fact e.g. in [D3, Lemma 1.4(2)].

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(A,\phi)
be a Frobenius algebra,
\phi^{\prime}\in A^{*}
. Then
\phi^{\prime}
is also a Frobenius form of
A

if and only if there is
z_{1}\in U(A)
such that
\phi^{\prime}=z_{1}\rightharpoonup\phi
; if and only if there is
z_{2}\in U(A)
such that
\phi^{\prime}=\phi\leftharpoonup z_{2}.

If this is the case, then the Nakayama automorphism
\mathcal{N}_{\phi^{\prime}}
with respect to
\phi^{\prime}
is given by

x\mapsto z_{1}\mathcal{N}_{\phi}(x)z_{1}^{-1}
and also given by
x\mapsto\mathcal{N}_{\phi}(z_{2}xz_{2}^{-1})
.

2.2.
Frobenius coalgebras

Assume that
(C,\Delta,\varepsilon)
is a coalgebra over field
\mathbb{K}
. All tensor products are over
\mathbb{K}
. We use the Heyneman-Sweedler notation
\Delta(c)=\sum c_{1}\otimes c_{2}
. Then
C^{*}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(C,\ \mathbb{K})
is an algebra; and
C
becomes a left
C^{*}
-module:
f\rightharpoonup c=\sum c_{1}f(c_{2}),\ \forall\ f\in C^{*},\ \forall\ c\in C
; and
C
becomes a right
C^{*}
-module:
c\leftharpoonup f=\sum f(c_{1})c_{2}
, where
\rightharpoonup
and
\leftharpoonup
denote the left and the right action of
C^{*}
on
C
, respectively.

A finite-dimensional coalgebra
C
is
a Frobenius coalgebra
if
C^{*}\cong C
as left
C^{*}
-modules, or equivalently, as right
C^{*}
-modules.
Also,
C
is a Frobenius coalgebra if and only if there is an element

t\in C
such that
C=t\leftharpoonup C^{*}
, or equivalently,
C=C^{*}\rightharpoonup t
. If this is the case, the Frobenius coalgebra
C
will be denoted by the pair
(C,t)
.

2.3.
Bi-Frobenius algebras

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
DT
]
)
Let
A
be a finite-dimensional algebra and a coalgebra over a field
\mathbb{K}
,
t\in A
, and
\phi\in A^{*}
.
Let
S:A\longrightarrow A
be the
\mathbb{K}
-linear map defined by
S(a)=\sum\phi(t_{1}a)t_{2},\ \forall\ a\in A.

The quadruple
(A,\ \phi,\ t,\ S)
, or simply,
A
is a bi-Frobenius algebra, if the conditions are satisfied
:

(i)
The counit
\varepsilon:A\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
is an algebra homomorphism
(
thus
\varepsilon(1_{A})=1_{A});
and the identity
1_{A}
is a group-like element

(
i.e.,
\Delta(1_{A})=1_{A}\otimes 1_{A});

(ii)

(A,\ \phi)
is a Frobenius algebra
;
and
(A,\ t)
is a Frobenius coalgebra
;

(iii)

S
is an algebra anti-homomorphism of
A

(
i.e.,
S(1)=1
and
S(ab)=S(b)S(a),\ \forall\ a,b\in A)
,
and
S
is a coalgebra anti-homomorphism of
A

(
i.e.,
\varepsilon\circ S=\varepsilon
, and
\Delta(S(a))=\sum S(a_{2})\otimes S(a_{1}),\ \forall\ a\in A)
.

In this case,
S
is called the antipode of bi-Frobenius algebra
(A,\ \phi,\ t,\ S)
.

The antipode
S
of a bi-Frobenius algebra
(A,\phi,t,S)
is bijective, since
S=\kappa\circ\theta
, where
\theta:A\longrightarrow A^{*},\ 1\mapsto\phi
is the isomorphism of left
A
-modules,
and
\kappa:A^{*}\longrightarrow A,\ f\mapsto t\leftharpoonup f
(or equivalently,
\varepsilon\mapsto t
) is the isomorphism of right
A^{*}
-modules. Usually
S^{-1}
is called

the composite inverse
of
S
, and denoted by
\overline{S}
.

Let
(A,\phi,t,S)
be a bi-Frobenius algebra. By identifying
A
with
A^{**}
, then
(A^{*},\ t,\ \phi,\ S^{*})
is a bi-Frobenius algebra. Also,

(A^{\rm op},\ \phi,\ \overline{S}(t),\ \overline{S})
and
(A^{\rm cop},\ \phi\circ\overline{S},\ t,\ \overline{S})
are bi-Frobenius algebras,
where
A^{\rm op}
means the multiplication is the opposite and the comultiplication is the same,
A^{\rm cop}
means the multiplication is the same and the comultiplication is the opposite.

2.4.
The Nakayama automorphism and the
S^{4}
-formula for a Bi-Frobenius algebra

A
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
A
is
augmented
, if there is an algebra homomorphism
\varepsilon:A\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
, which is called
an augmentation
.
For an augmented algebra
(A,\varepsilon)
, an element
t\in A
is said to be
a right
(respectively,
left
)
integral
of
A
if
tx=t\varepsilon(x)
(respectively,
xt=\varepsilon(x)t
), for all
x\in A
. If the right integral space coincides with the left integral space, then
A
is called
unimodular
.

Let
(A,\phi)
be an augmented Frobenius algebra with augmentation
\varepsilon:A\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}.
Then
\varepsilon=t\rightharpoonup\phi=\phi\leftharpoonup s
for some unique
t,s\in A
;
it can be easily deduced that
t
and
s
are respectively left and right integrals of
A
.

For a finite-dimensional coalgebra
C
, the algebra
C^{*}
(its identity is
\varepsilon
) is augmented if and only if
C
has a group-like element
h
.
If this is the case, then a right (respectively, left) integral of algebra
C^{*}
can be interpreted as an element
\phi\in C^{*}
, such that

x\leftharpoonup\phi=\phi(x)h
(respectively,
\phi\rightharpoonup x=h\phi(x)
), for all
x\in C
.

Let
(C,\ t)
be a Frobenius coalgebra with group-like element
h.
Then
h=\phi\rightharpoonup t=t\leftharpoonup\psi
for some unique
\phi,\psi\in C^{*}
; it can be easily deduced that
\phi
and
\psi
are respectively left and right integrals of
C^{*}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
DT
]
,
[
Haim
]
)

(1)
If
A
is an augmented Frobenius algebra, then the space of right
(
respectively, left
)
integrals is one-dimensional.

(2)
Let
(A,\ \phi,\ t,\ S)
be a bi-Frobenius algebra. Then

(i)

\varepsilon=\phi\leftharpoonup t;

(ii)

\phi(t)=1;

(iii)

t
is a right integral of
A;
and the space of right integrals of

is
\mathbb{K}t;

(iv)

1_{A}=t\leftharpoonup\phi;

(v)

\phi
is a right integral of
A^{*};
and the space of right integrals of

is
\mathbb{K}\phi
.

(vi)
If

.

(3)
If
(A,\ \phi,\ t,S)
is a bi-Frobenius algebra, then
(A,\ c\phi,\ \frac{1}{c}t,S)

Proof.

The fact that

is a right integral of

mainly use

By the definition of

is a right integral of

.

We include a proof of (vi) in (2). By definition

Since

By (ii) in (2) one has

, and hence

. ∎

2.5.
Quantum complete intersections.

Denote by
\mathbb{N}_{0}
the set of non-negative integers. Let
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
and all
a_{i}\geq 2
, and
{\bf q}=(q_{ij})
an
n\times n
matrix
over
\mathbb{K}
with
q_{ii}=1
and
q_{ij}q_{ji}=1
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
.
A quantum complete intersection

A=A({\bf q},{\bf a})
is the
\mathbb{K}
-algebra

\mathbb{K}\langle x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\rangle/\langle x_{i}^{a_{i}},\ x_{j}x_{i}-q_{ij}x_{i}x_{j},\ 1\leq i,\ j\leq n\rangle.

If all
a_{i}=2
, then it is called
a quantum exterior algebra
.

Put

V=\{\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\ |\ v_{i}\leq a_{i}-1,\ 1\leq i\leq n\}.

For
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, write
x_{\mathbf{v}}=x_{1}^{v_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{v_{n}}\in A
. Then
\mathcal{B}=\{x_{\mathbf{v}}\ |\ \mathbf{v}\in V\}
is a basis of
A
, which will be called
the standard basis
.
The dual basis of
A^{*}
is
\{x_{\bf v}^{*}\ |\ {\bf v}\in V\}
, where

x_{\bf v}^{*}(x_{\bf u})=\delta_{{\bf v},{\bf u}},\ \forall\ {\bf v},{\bf u}\in V
,
\delta_{{\bf v},{\bf u}}
is the Kronecker symbol. Note that
A
is a local algebra, with the unique maximal ideal
\rm radA=\langle x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\rangle
.

The degree
of
x_{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathcal{B}
is defined as
|x_{\mathbf{v}}|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}
,
and the degree of
x_{\mathbf{u}}\otimes x_{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{B}
is defined as
|x_{\mathbf{u}}|+|x_{\mathbf{v}}|
.
Then
A
is an
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded algebra with respect to the degree. A
\mathbb{K}
-linear map
S:A\longrightarrow A
is said to
graded
,
provided that
S
preserves the degrees of all elements
x_{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathcal{B}
.

Put
{\bf 0}=(0,\cdots,0),\ \ {\bf 1}=(1,\cdots,1)
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
{\bf e}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
be the vector with the
i
-th component
1
and other components
0
.
Define a partial order on
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
by
\mathbf{u}\leq\mathbf{v}
if and only if
u_{i}\leq v_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
Note that
x_{\bf v}\neq 0
if and only if
{\bf v}\leq\mathbf{a-1}
. Thus, if
|x_{\mathbf{v}}|>(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i})-n
then

x_{\bf v}=0
.

For
\mathbf{u,v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, following S. Oppermann
[
O
, Section 2]
, put

{\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{u}|\mathbf{v}\rangle}=\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}q_{ij}^{u_{j}v_{i}};\ \ \ \ \ h_{\bf v}:=\frac{{\bf q}^{\bf\langle a-1-v|v\rangle}}{{\bf q}^{\bf\langle v|a-1-v\rangle}}.

In particular,

{\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{0}|\mathbf{v}\rangle}=1={\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{u}|\mathbf{0}\rangle};\ \ \ h_{\bf 0}=1=h_{\bf a-1};\ \ \ h_{{\bf e}_{i}}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}q_{ij}^{a_{j}-1},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n.

For convenience, we will call
{\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{u}|\mathbf{v}\rangle},\ \forall\ \mathbf{u,v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},

the structure coefficients
of
A
.

Lemma 2.5
.

(S. Oppermann)
The following equalities hold for any
{\bf u,v,w}\in\ \mathbb{Z}^{n}:

{\rm(1)}

x_{\mathbf{u}}x_{\mathbf{v}}={\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{u}|\mathbf{v}\rangle}x_{\mathbf{u+v}},\ \forall\ {\bf u,v}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}.

{\rm(2)}

{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{j}|{\bf e}_{k}\rangle}={\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{k}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle}q_{kj};\ \ \ {\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{j}|{\bf e}_{k}\rangle}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}1,\ \ \ j\leq k;\\
q_{kj},\ j>k.\end{array}\right.

{\rm(3)}

{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf-u|v}\rangle}={\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u|-v}\rangle}=\frac{1}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u|v}\rangle}};

{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u+v|w}\rangle}={\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u|w}\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf v|w}\rangle};

{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u|v+w}\rangle}={\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u|v}\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u|w}\rangle}.

{\rm(4)}

{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u|v}\rangle}=\prod\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle{{\bf e}_{i}|{\bf e}_{j}}\rangle})^{u_{i}v_{j}}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j<i\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle{{\bf e}_{i}|{\bf e}_{j}}\rangle})^{u_{i}v_{j}}.

{\rm(5)}

h_{\bf u+v}=h_{\bf u}h_{\bf v};\ \ \ h_{\bf u}=\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}h_{{\bf e}_{i}}^{u_{i}}.

Proof.

All these equalities can be directly verified. For example, by definition one has

{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u+v|w}\rangle}=\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}q_{ij}^{(u_{j}+v_{j})w_{i}}=\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}q_{ij}^{u_{j}w_{i}}\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}q_{ij}^{v_{j}w_{i}}={\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u|w}\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf v|w}\rangle}.

∎

It is known (see e.g. the proof of [B, Lemma 3.1]) that
x_{\mathbf{a-1}}^{*}
is a Frobenius form of
A
.
Thus, any Frobenius form of
A
is of the form
\phi=x_{\mathbf{a-1}}^{*}\leftharpoonup z
with
z\in U(A)
(cf. Lemma
2.1
).
Since
A
is finite-dimensional and local,

z=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in V}c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{v}}x_{\mathbf{v}}\in A
with each
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{K}
and
c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{0}}\neq 0
.
Using
x_{\mathbf{a-1}}^{*}\leftharpoonup x_{\mathbf{v}}={\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{v}|\mathbf{a-1-v}\rangle}x^{*}_{\mathbf{a-1-v}}
one has

\phi=x_{\mathbf{a-1}}^{*}\leftharpoonup z=x_{\mathbf{a-1}}^{*}\leftharpoonup\sum\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in V}c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{v}}x_{\mathbf{v}}=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in V}c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{v}}{\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{v}|\mathbf{a-1-v}\rangle}\ x^{*}_{\mathbf{a-1-v}}.

Write
\phi=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in V}c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{v}}{\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{v}|\mathbf{a-1-v}\rangle}\ x^{*}_{\mathbf{a-1-v}}
as

\sum\limits_{\mathbf{u}\in V}c_{\mathbf{u}}x_{\mathbf{u}}^{*}
. Then
c_{\mathbf{a-1}}=c^{\prime}_{\mathbf{0}}\neq 0
.

Lemma 2.6
.

(
P. A. Bergh [B]
)
Quantum complete intersection
A=A({\bf q,a})
is a local Frobenius algebra.
It is symmetric if and only if
h_{{\bf e}_{i}}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}(q_{ij})^{a_{j}-1}=1,\ \ 1\leq i\leq n.

Any Frobenius form of
A
is of the form
\phi=x^{*}_{\bf a-1}\leftharpoonup z
with
z\in U(A);

and
\phi=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in V}c_{\mathbf{v}}x_{\mathbf{v}}^{*}\ \ \mbox{with}\ c_{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{K}\ \mbox{and}\ c_{\mathbf{a-1}}\neq 0

gives all the Frobenius homomorphisms of
A
.

The Nakayama automorphism
\mathcal{N}_{x_{\bf a-1}^{*}}
of
A
with respect to the Frobenius form
x_{\bf a-1}^{*}
is given by

\mathcal{N}_{x_{\bf a-1}^{*}}(x_{\bf v})=\frac{{\bf q}^{\bf\langle a-1-v|v\rangle}}{{\bf q}^{\bf\langle v|a-1-v\rangle}}x_{\bf v}=h_{\bf v}x_{\bf v},\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V.

We will call
\mathcal{N}_{x_{\bf a-1}^{*}}

the canonical Nakayama automorphism
of
A
, and denote it simply by
\mathcal{N}
. Thus

\mathcal{N}(x_{\bf v})=h_{\bf v}x_{\bf v},\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V.

3.
Acting with
S_{n}

In order to study the bi-Frobenius algebra structure of a quantum complete intersection
A=A({\bf q,a})
with permutation antipode
(see Definition
5.1
), we need to study the structure coefficients
{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(u)|\pi(v)}\rangle}
for each permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
.

The equations we present here will be referenced later and are of independent interest. The reader may choose to skip this section initially and refer to it later when necessary.

3.1.
Structure coefficients under permutations

Let
\pi\in S_{n}
. Then
\pi
induces an action on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, still denoted by
\pi
, by

\displaystyle\pi({\bf v})=(v_{\pi^{-1}(1)},\cdots,v_{\pi^{-1}(n)}),\ \ \forall\ {\bf v}=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

(3.1)

We will use this induced action throughout, without further explanations.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\pi\in S_{n}
and
{\bf u,v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then

(1)

\pi({\bf 0})={\bf 0};\ \ \pi({\bf 1})={\bf 1};\ \ \pi({\bf e}_{i})={\bf e}_{\pi(i)},\ 1\leq i\leq n;\ \ \pi({\bf u+v})=\pi({\bf u})+\pi({\bf v}).

(2)
For
{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, there holds the equality in quantum complete intersection
A=A(\bf{q,a}):

\displaystyle x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n})}^{v_{n}}x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n-1})}^{v_{n-1}}\cdots x_{\pi({\bf e}_{1})}^{v_{1}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({{\bf e}_{k}})|\pi({{\bf e}_{j}})\rangle})^{v_{k}v_{j}}x_{\pi({\bf v})}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({{\bf e}_{k}})|\pi({{\bf e}_{j}})\rangle})^{v_{k}v_{j}}x_{1}^{v_{\pi^{-1}(1)}}\cdots x_{n}^{v_{\pi^{-1}(n)}}.

(3)

{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(u)|\pi(v)}\rangle}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j,k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{j})|\pi({\bf e}_{k})\rangle})^{u_{j}v_{k}}.

Proof.
The assertion (1) follows directly from (3.1).

(2) Using
x_{\bf\pi(u)}x_{\bf\pi(v)}={\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(u)|\pi(v)}\rangle}x_{\bf\pi(u+v)}
(cf. Lemma
2.5
(1)), one gets

x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n})}^{v_{n}}\cdots x_{\pi({\bf e}_{1})}^{v_{1}}=({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({{\bf e}_{n}})|\pi({{\bf e}_{n-1}})\rangle})^{v_{n}v_{n-1}}x_{\pi(v_{n}{{\bf e}_{n}}+v_{n-1}{{\bf e}_{n-1}})}x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n-2})}^{v_{n-2}}\cdots x_{\pi({\bf e}_{1})}^{v_{1}}.

Again by
x_{\bf\pi(u)}x_{\bf\pi(v)}={\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(u)|\pi(v)}\rangle}x_{\bf\pi(u+v)}
one has

\displaystyle x_{\pi(v_{n}{{\bf e}_{n}}+v_{n-1}{{\bf e}_{n-1}})}x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n-2})}^{v_{n-2}}\cdots x_{\pi({\bf e}_{1})}^{v_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf q}^{\langle\pi(v_{n}{{\bf e}_{n}}+v_{n-1}{{\bf e}_{n-1}})|\pi(v_{n-2}{{\bf e}_{n-2}})\rangle}x_{\pi(v_{n}{{\bf e}_{n}}+v_{n-1}{{\bf e}_{n-1}}+v_{n-2}{{\bf e}_{n-2}})}x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n-3})}^{v_{n-3}}\cdots x_{\pi({\bf e}_{1})}^{v_{1}}

Continuing this process, and using
{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u+v|w}\rangle}={\bf q}^{\langle\bf{u|w}\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle\bf v|w\rangle}
(cf. Lemma
2.5
(3)), one gets

\displaystyle x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n})}^{v_{n}}x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n-1})}^{v_{n-1}}\cdots x_{\pi({\bf e}_{1})}^{v_{1}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({{\bf e}_{k}})|\pi({{\bf e}_{j}})\rangle})^{v_{k}v_{j}}x_{\pi(v_{n}{{\bf e}_{n}}+\cdots+v_{1}{{\bf e}_{1}})}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({{\bf e}_{k}})|\pi({{\bf e}_{j}})\rangle})^{v_{k}v_{j}}x_{\pi({\bf v})}.

(3) By
{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u|v}\rangle}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j,k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{j}|{\bf e}_{k}\rangle})^{u_{j}v_{k}}
(cf. Lemma
2.5
(4)) one has

\displaystyle{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(u)|\pi(v)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{1\leq j,k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{j}|{\bf e}_{k}\rangle})^{u_{\pi^{-1}(j)}v_{\pi^{-1}(k)}}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{1\leq s,t\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{\pi(s)}|{\bf e}_{\pi(t)}\rangle})^{u_{s}v_{t}}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{{\bf e}_{\pi(s)}=\pi({\bf e}_{s})}}\prod\limits_{1\leq s,t\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{s})|\pi({\bf e}_{t})\rangle})^{u_{s}v_{t}},\ \forall\ {\bf u,v}\in V.\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \square

Remark 3.2
.

Taking
{\bf v}={\bf a-1}
in
Lemma
3.1
(2)
one gets the equality in

x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n})}^{a_{n}-1}x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n-1})}^{a_{n-1}-1}\cdots x_{\pi({\bf e}_{1})}^{a_{1}-1}=\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({{\bf e}_{k}})|\pi({{\bf e}_{j}})\rangle})^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}x_{\pi({\bf a-1})}.

This will be used later. For convenience, denote by

It will play an important role in studying bi-Frobenius algebra structures on

.

3.2.
Structure coefficients under compatible permutations

In order to study bi-Frobenius algebra structure with permutation antipode, we need the following

Definition 3.3
.

A permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
is called a compatible permutation with
A(\bf q,a)
, or simply, a compatible permutation,
provided that

a_{\pi(i)}=a_{i},\ \ \ \ q_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}=q_{ji},\ \ \ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

Compatible permutations exist in many situations. Note that when
\bf q
is a symmetric matrix then
\pi=\rm Id
is a compatible permutation.
A similar notion of compatible permutation has been already used, e.g. in
[
KKZ
]
,
[
G
]
,
[
CPWZ
]
, and
[
BZ
]
,
in studying isomorphisms of quantum affine spaces.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
A=A({\bf q,a})
, let
\pi
be a compatible permutation in
S_{n}
.
Then the following equalities hold for
{\bf u,v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:

(1)

\pi({\bf a-1})={\bf a-1}.

(2)

{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u|v}\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(u)|\pi(v)}\rangle}={\bf q}^{\langle{\bf v|u}\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(v)|\pi(u)}\rangle}.

(3)

{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(v)|\pi(u)}\rangle}={\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u|v}\rangle}\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{u_{j}v_{k}+u_{k}v_{j}}.

(4)

h_{\bf v}h_{\bf\pi(v)}=1
.

Proof.

(1) Since
a_{\pi(i)}=a_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq n
, thus
a_{\pi^{-1}(i)}=a_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq n
, and hence

\pi({\bf a-1})=(a_{\pi^{-1}(1)}-1,\cdots,\ a_{\pi^{-1}(n)}-1)=(a_{1}-1,\cdots,\ a_{n}-1)={\bf a-1}.

(2) By Lemma
2.5
(2) and the assumption
q_{\pi(j)\pi(k)}=q_{kj},\ 1\leq j,k\leq n
, and note that
\pi({\bf e}_{j})={\bf e}_{\pi(j)}
, one has

\frac{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{j}|{\bf e}_{k}\rangle}}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{k}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle}}=q_{kj}=q_{\pi(j)\pi(k)}=\frac{{\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle}}{{\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{j})|\pi({\bf e}_{k})\rangle}},\ \ 1\leq j,k\leq n.
Then by Lemma
3.1
(3) one has

\displaystyle\frac{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(u)|\pi(v)}\rangle}}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(v)|\pi(u)}\rangle}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod\limits_{1\leq j,k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{j})|\pi({\bf e}_{k})\rangle})^{u_{j}v_{k}}}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j,k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{j})|\pi({\bf e}_{k})\rangle})^{v_{j}u_{k}}}=\frac{\prod\limits_{1\leq j,k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{u_{k}v_{j}}}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j,k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{j})|\pi({\bf e}_{k})\rangle})^{v_{j}u_{k}}}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{1\leq j,k\leq n}(\frac{{\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle}}{{\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{j})|\pi({\bf e}_{k})\rangle}})^{u_{k}v_{j}}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j,k\leq n}(\frac{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{j}|{\bf e}_{k}\rangle}}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{k}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle}})^{u_{k}v_{j}}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{{\rm By\ Lem.\ref{propeqq}(4)}}}\frac{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf v|u}\rangle}}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u|v}\rangle}}.

(3) By Lemma
3.1
(3) one has

\displaystyle{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(v)|\pi(u)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{1\leq j,k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{j})|\pi({\bf e}_{k})\rangle})^{v_{j}u_{k}}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{j})|\pi({\bf e}_{k})\rangle})^{v_{j}u_{k}}\prod\limits_{1\leq k<j\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{j})|\pi({\bf e}_{k})\rangle})^{v_{j}u_{k}}.

By (2) one has
{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{j}|{\bf e}_{k}\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{j})|\pi({\bf e}_{k})\rangle}={\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{k}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle},\ \ 1\leq j,k\leq n.
Using
{\bf q}^{{\langle{\bf e}_{j}|{\bf e}_{k}\rangle}}=1
if
1\leq j\leq k\leq n
, one has

\displaystyle{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(v)|\pi(u)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{j})|\pi({\bf e}_{k})\rangle})^{v_{j}u_{k}}\prod\limits_{1\leq k<j\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{j})|\pi({\bf e}_{k})\rangle})^{v_{j}u_{k}}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{j}|{\bf e}_{k}\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{j})|\pi({\bf e}_{k})\rangle})^{v_{j}u_{k}}\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{k}u_{j}}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{k}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{j}u_{k}}\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{k}u_{j}}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{k}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle})^{v_{j}u_{k}}\prod\limits_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{j}u_{k}}\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{k}u_{j}}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{{\rm Lem.\ref{propeqq}(4)}}}{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u}|{\bf v}\rangle}\prod\limits_{1\leq j\leq k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{u_{j}v_{k}+u_{k}v_{j}}.

(4) Replacing
\bf u
by
{\bf a-1-v}
in (2) and using
{\bf a-1}=\pi(\bf a-1)
, one gets

\displaystyle h_{\pi({\bf v})}=\frac{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf a-1-\pi(v)|\pi(v)}\rangle}}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(v)|a-1-\pi(v)}\rangle}}{\mathrel{\to}^{{\rm By\ (1)}}}\frac{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(a-1-v)|\pi(v)}\rangle}}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(v)|\pi(a-1-v)}\rangle}}{\mathrel{\to}^{{\rm By\ (2)}}}\frac{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf v|a-1-v}\rangle}}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf a-1-v|v}\rangle}}=\frac{1}{h_{\bf v}}.

This completes the proof. ∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
\pi
is a compatible permutation in
S_{n}
with
\pi^{2}=\rm Id
. Put

I=\{i\ |\ 1\leq i\leq n,\ \pi(i)=i\}
and
J=\{i\ |\ 1\leq i\leq n,\ \pi(i)\neq i\}
. Then

(1)

\{i\ |\ i\in J,\ i<\pi(i)\}\cap\{\pi(i)\ |\ i\in J,\ i<\pi(i)\}=\emptyset;

J=\{i\ |\ i\in J,\ i<\pi(i)\}\cup\{\pi(i)\ |\ i\in J,\ i<\pi(i)\}.

(2)

q_{ij}^{2}=1
,
\forall\ i,j\in I.

(3)

h_{{\bf e}_{i}}=\prod\limits_{j\in I}q_{ij}^{a_{j}-1},\ \forall\ i\in I.

(4)
Let
q_{\pi}
be as in
Remark
3.2
, i.e.,
q_{\pi}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({{\bf e}_{k}})|\pi({{\bf e}_{j}})\rangle})^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}.
Then

q_{\pi}=\prod\limits_{j,k\in I,\ 1\leq j<k\leq n}q_{kj}^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}=\pm 1.

(
If
I=\emptyset
, then
q_{\pi}
is understood to be
1
.
)

Proof.

(1). Assume that otherwise there is an element
i
in the intersection. Then
i<\pi(i)
and
i=\pi(j)
for some
j\in J
with
j<\pi(j)
. Since
\pi^{2}=\rm Id
, one gets a contradiction

i=\pi(j)>j=\pi(i).

For any
j\in J
with
j\notin\{i\ |\ i\in J,\ i<\pi(i)\}
, then
j>\pi(j)
. By
\pi^{2}=\rm Id
one has
\pi(\pi(j))=j>\pi(j)
. By definition
j=\pi(\pi(j))\in\{\pi(i)\ |\ i\in J,\ i<\pi(i)\}
. This justifies the second equality.

(2). For
i,j\in I
, one has
q_{ij}^{2}=q_{ij}q_{ij}=q_{ij}q_{\pi(j)\pi(i)}=q_{ij}q_{ji}=1.

(3). By definition
h_{{\bf e}_{i}}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}q_{ij}^{(a_{j}-1)}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Now let
i\in I
.
For any
j
with
1\leq j\leq n
, by
q_{i\pi(j)}=q_{ji}
and
a_{\pi(j)}=a_{j}
one has
q_{ij}^{a_{j}-1}q_{i\pi(j)}^{a_{\pi(j)}-1}=(q_{ij}q_{ji})^{a_{j}-1}=1
. By (1) one has

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j\in J}q_{ij}^{(a_{j}-1)}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{j\in J,j<\pi(j)}q_{ij}^{(a_{j}-1)}\prod\limits_{j=\pi(s),s\in J,s<j}q_{ij}^{(a_{j}-1)}=\prod\limits_{j\in J,j<\pi(j)}q_{ij}^{(a_{j}-1)}\prod\limits_{s\in J,s<\pi(s)}q_{i\pi(s)}^{(a_{\pi(s)}-1)}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{j\in J,j<\pi(j)}q_{ij}^{(a_{j}-1)}q_{i\pi(j)}^{(a_{\pi(j)}-1)}=1.

Thus

\displaystyle h_{{\bf e}_{i}}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}q_{ij}^{(a_{j}-1)}=\prod\limits_{j\in I}q_{ij}^{(a_{j}-1)}\prod\limits_{j\in J}q_{ij}^{(a_{j}-1)}=\prod\limits_{j\in I}q_{ij}^{(a_{j}-1)},\ \forall\ i\in I.

(4). Since
{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf e}_{k}|{\bf e}_{j}\rangle}=1
for
k<j
, one has

\displaystyle q_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<k\leq n\\
1\leq\pi(j)<\pi(k)\leq n\end{subarray}}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{{\rm Lem.\ref{propeqq}(2)}}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<k\leq n\\
1\leq\pi(j)<\pi(k)\leq n\end{subarray}}(q_{\pi(j)\pi(k)})^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<k\leq n\\
1\leq\pi(j)<\pi(k)\leq n\end{subarray}}q_{kj}^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}.

Assume that
1\leq j<k\leq n
and
1\leq\pi(j)<\pi(k)\leq n
. Then
1\leq\pi^{2}(j)<\pi^{2}(k)\leq n
since
\pi^{2}=\rm Id
. Using the same idea in the proof of (3), we have the following discussions.

If
j\in J
or
k\in J
, then
q_{kj}^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}
and
q_{\pi(k)\pi(j)}^{(a_{\pi(k)}-1)(a_{\pi(j)}-1)}
are different two terms occurring in the product
\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<k\leq n\\
1\leq\pi(j)<\pi(k)\leq n\end{subarray}}q_{kj}^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}.
Using
a_{\pi(i)}=a_{i}
and
q_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}=q_{ji}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
, the product of these two terms is

q_{kj}^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}q_{\pi(k)\pi(j)}^{(a_{\pi(k)}-1)(a_{\pi(j)}-1)}=(q_{kj}q_{jk})^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}=1.

Thus, in the product

\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<k\leq n\\
1\leq\pi(j)<\pi(k)\leq n\end{subarray}}q_{kj}^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}
, we only need to consider the product of those terms

(q_{kj})^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}
with
1\leq j<k\leq n,\ 1\leq\pi(j)<\pi(k)\leq n
and
j\in I,\ k\in I
. Note that if
1\leq j<k\leq n
and
j\in I,\ k\in I
, then

1\leq\pi(j)<\pi(k)\leq n
holds automatically. It follows that

q_{\pi}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<k\leq n\\
1\leq\pi(j)<\pi(k)\leq n\end{subarray}}(q_{kj})^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}=\prod_{j,k\in I,j<k}(q_{kj})^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}.

By (2), one gets
q_{\pi}=\pm 1
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Bi-Frobenius quantum complete intersections

For a bi-Frobenius algebra
(A,\phi,t,S)
, the Frobenius form
\phi
, the right integral
t
, Nakayama automorphisms, the antipode
S
, the right modular function
\alpha
,
and the right modular element
\mathfrak{a}
, provide fundamental structural information. The following result collects these for bi-Frobenius quantum complete intersections,
where (i), (ii) and (iii) below are known.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(A,\ \phi,\ t,\ S)
be a bi-Frobenius algebra structure on
A=A({\bf q,a})
.
Then

(i)

\phi=x_{\bf a-1}^{*}\leftharpoonup z
for some
z\in U(A);
and
\phi=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in V}c_{\mathbf{v}}x_{\mathbf{v}}^{*}
with
c_{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{K}
and
c_{\mathbf{a-1}}\neq 0.

(ii)

\alpha=\varepsilon;
and
\varepsilon(x_{\mathbf{v}})=\delta_{\mathbf{v,0}}\ \ \text{for}\ \ \mathbf{v}\in V.

(iii)

t=cx_{\mathbf{a-1}}
with
c\in\mathbb{K}-\{0\};
and
A
is unimodular with the space of right integrals
\mathbb{K}x_{\bf a-1}
.

(iv)

\mathcal{N}^{2}=\rm Id
, i.e.,
h^{2}_{{\bf e}_{i}}=1,\ 1\leq i\leq n
, where
\mathcal{N}
is the canonical Nakayama automorphism of
A
.

(v)
If
S
is a graded map, then
S^{2}=\mathcal{N}
and
S^{4}=\rm Id
.

(vi)
If
{\rm Char}\mathbb{K}=0,
then
\mathfrak{a}=1,\ \mathcal{N}_{\phi}^{2}=\rm Id
and
S^{4}=\rm Id
.

Proof.

For (i) see Lemma
2.6
. The assertion (ii) follows from the fact that algebra homomorphism
A\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
is unique, for a local finite-dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
A
.
If fact, the right modular function
\alpha:A\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
is an algebra homomorphism (cf. Lemma
2.4
(1)). Since
A
is local,
{\rm Ker}\alpha=\rm radA
and
\alpha(1_{A})=1_{A}
.
While
\varepsilon:A\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
is also an algebra homomorphism, thus
\alpha=\varepsilon
.

(iii) Write
t=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in V}c_{\mathbf{v}}x_{\mathbf{v}}
with each
c_{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{K}
. Since
t
is a right integral of
A
(cf. Lemma
2.3
(2)(iii)),
one has
tx=t\varepsilon(x)=x^{*}_{\mathbf{0}}(x)t
for all
x\in A
. Thus, if

\mathbf{v}<\mathbf{a-1}
, then

0=x^{*}_{\mathbf{0}}(x_{\mathbf{a-1-v}})t=tx_{\mathbf{a-1-v}}=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{u}\in V}c_{\mathbf{u}}x_{\mathbf{u}}x_{\mathbf{a-1-v}}=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{u}\in V}c_{\mathbf{u}}{\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{u}|\mathbf{a-1-v}\rangle}x_{\mathbf{a-1+u-v}}.

In particular,
c_{\mathbf{v}}{\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{v}|\mathbf{a-1-v}\rangle}x_{\mathbf{a-1}}=0
, which implies
c_{\mathbf{v}}=0
, for all
\mathbf{v}<\mathbf{a-1}
. Thus
t=cx_{\mathbf{a-1}}
with
c\in\mathbb{K}
. Since
A=t\leftharpoonup A^{*}
,
c\neq 0
.
This also proves that the space of right integrals is
\mathbb{K}x_{\bf a-1}
.

Similarly, the space of left integrals is
\mathbb{K}x_{\bf a-1}
. Thus
A
is unimodular.

(iv) By (ii),
\alpha=\varepsilon
. Thus for
x\in A
one has

x\leftharpoonup\alpha=x\leftharpoonup\varepsilon=\sum\varepsilon(x_{1})x_{2}=(\varepsilon\otimes\rm Id)\Delta(x)=x

and
\alpha\rightharpoonup x=\varepsilon\rightharpoonup x=\sum x_{1}\varepsilon(x_{2})=x.

Using Lemma
2.4
(3) twice one gets

\mathcal{N}_{\phi}^{2}(x_{i})=\mathcal{N}_{\phi}(\mathfrak{a}^{-1}S^{2}(\alpha\rightharpoonup x_{i})\mathfrak{a})=\mathcal{N}_{\phi}(\mathfrak{a}^{-1}S^{2}(x_{i})\mathfrak{a})=\mathfrak{a}^{-1}S^{2}(\mathfrak{a}^{-1}S^{2}(x_{i})\mathfrak{a})\mathfrak{a}.

By Lemma
2.4
(2) one has

S^{2}(\mathfrak{a}^{-1})=\mathfrak{a}^{-1}
and
S^{2}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathfrak{a}
. Thus

\mathcal{N}_{\phi}^{2}(x_{i})=\mathfrak{a}^{-1}S^{2}(\mathfrak{a}^{-1}S^{2}(x_{i})\mathfrak{a})\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}^{-1}S^{2}(\mathfrak{a}^{-1})S^{4}(x_{i})S^{2}(\mathfrak{a})\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}^{-2}S^{4}(x_{i})\mathfrak{a}^{2}.

Note that the identity of algebra
A^{*}
is
\varepsilon
. Thus
\alpha^{-1}=\varepsilon^{-1}=\varepsilon
.
It follows from Lemma
2.4
(4) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\phi}^{2}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{a}^{-2}S^{4}(x_{i})\mathfrak{a}^{2}=\mathfrak{a}^{-1}(\alpha^{-1}\rightharpoonup x_{i}\leftharpoonup\alpha)\mathfrak{a}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{a}^{-1}(\varepsilon\rightharpoonup x_{i}\leftharpoonup\varepsilon)\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}^{-1}x_{i}\mathfrak{a}.

Since
A
is local and
\mathfrak{a}
is an invertible element of
A
,
\mathfrak{a}=c1_{A}+r
with
c\in\mathbb{K}-\{0\},\ r\in\rm radA
.
Since
\mathfrak{a}
is a group-like element, thus
\varepsilon({\mathfrak{a}})=1
and
{\mathfrak{a}}=1+r
. Thus

\mathcal{N}_{\phi}^{2}(x_{i})=\mathfrak{a}^{-1}x_{i}\mathfrak{a}=x_{i}+\Sigma_{i},\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq n

where
\Sigma_{i}\in\rm rad^{2}A
.

On the other hand, by [B, Lemma 3.1],
\mathcal{N}(x_{i})=h_{{\bf e}_{i}}x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Since
\phi=x_{\bf a-1}^{*}\leftharpoonup z
for some
z\in U(A)
, it follows from Lemma
2.1

that

\mathcal{N}_{\phi}(x_{i})=z\mathcal{N}(x_{i})z^{-1}=zh_{{\bf e}_{i}}x_{i}z^{-1}=h_{{\bf e}_{i}}x_{i}+\Sigma^{\prime}_{i}

with
\Sigma^{\prime}_{i}\in\rm rad^{2}A
. Since
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
is an algebra automorphism,
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}(\rm rad^{2}A)\subseteq\rm rad^{2}A
. Thus

\mathcal{N}_{\phi}^{2}(x_{i})=\mathcal{N}_{\phi}(h_{{\bf e}_{i}}x_{i}+\Sigma^{\prime}_{i})=h^{2}_{{\bf e}_{i}}x_{i}+\Sigma^{\prime\prime}_{i}

with
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}_{i}\in\rm rad^{2}A
. Comparing with
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}^{2}(x_{i})=x_{i}+\Sigma_{i}
one gets

h_{{\bf e}_{i}}^{2}=1,\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq n.

Thus
\mathcal{N}^{2}(x_{i})=h_{{\bf e}_{i}}^{2}x_{i}=x_{i}
for each
i
. Since
\{x_{i}\ |\ 1\leq i\leq n\}
is a set of generators of
A
, one gets
\mathcal{N}^{2}=\rm Id.

(v) Assume that
S
is a graded map. By Lemma
2.4
(4) we have that

S^{4}(x_{i})=\mathfrak{a}(\alpha^{-1}\rightharpoonup x_{i}\leftharpoonup\alpha)\mathfrak{a}^{-1}=\mathfrak{a}(\alpha\rightharpoonup x_{i}\leftharpoonup\alpha)\mathfrak{a}^{-1}=\mathfrak{a}x_{i}\mathfrak{a}^{-1}=x_{i}+\Sigma_{i},\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq n

with
\Sigma_{i}\in\rm rad^{2}A
. Since
S
is graded,
S^{4}(x_{i})=x_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq n)
. Hence
S^{4}=\rm Id
.

Let
\phi=x_{\bf a-1}^{*}\leftharpoonup z
with
z\in U(A)
. Then
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}(x_{\mathbf{v}})=\mathcal{N}(zx_{\mathbf{v}}z^{-1}).

By Lemma
2.4
(3) and the assertion (ii) above one has

\displaystyle S^{2}(x_{\mathbf{v}})

\displaystyle=S^{2}(\varepsilon\rightharpoonup x_{\mathbf{v}})=S^{2}(\alpha\rightharpoonup x_{\mathbf{v}})=\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{N}_{\phi}(x_{\mathbf{v}})\mathfrak{a}^{-1}=\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{N}(zx_{\mathbf{v}}z^{-1})\mathfrak{a}^{-1}.

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{N}(z)\mathcal{N}(x_{\mathbf{v}})\mathcal{N}(z^{-1})\mathfrak{a}^{-1}=h_{\mathbf{v}}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{N}(z)x_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{N}(z))^{-1}.

Note that
\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{N}(z)=c1_{A}+r\in U(A)
with
(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{N}(z))^{-1}=c^{-1}1_{A}+r^{\prime}
, where
r,r^{\prime}\in{\rm rad}(A)
.
Thus

S^{2}(x_{\mathbf{v}})=h_{\mathbf{v}}x_{\mathbf{v}}+\Sigma_{\mathbf{v}}

with
\Sigma_{\mathbf{v}}\in{\rm rad}^{|x_{\mathbf{v}}|+1}(A)
. Since
S
is graded, it follows that

\Sigma_{\mathbf{v}}=0
and hence
S^{2}(x_{\mathbf{v}})=h_{\mathbf{v}}x_{\mathbf{v}}=\mathcal{N}(x_{\mathbf{v}}),\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V
.
Thus
S^{2}=\mathcal{N}
.

(vi) Note that
\mathfrak{a}^{n}=1
for some positive integer
n
. In fact,
{\mathfrak{a}}^{m}
is a group-like element for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
(cf. Lemma
2.4
(1)). Thus
\varepsilon({\mathfrak{a}})=1
and
{\mathfrak{a}}=1+r
for some
r\in\rm rad(A)
.
Since distinct group-like elements are linearly independent, there are positive integers
m_{1}\neq m_{2}
such that
\mathfrak{a}^{m_{1}}=\mathfrak{a}^{m_{2}}
.
Since
\mathfrak{a}
is invertible,
\mathfrak{a}^{n}=1
for some positive integer
n
. Thus

0=(1+r)^{n}-1=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}r^{i}-1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}r^{i}=r(n1_{A}+r^{\prime}),

where
r^{\prime}=\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}\binom{n}{i}r^{i-1}\in\rm rad(A)
is nilpotent. Since
{\rm char}\mathbb{K}=0
,
n1_{A}+r^{\prime}
is invertible, thus
r=0
and
\mathfrak{a}=1
.
Then from the proof of (iv) one gets
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}^{2}=\rm Id
and
S^{4}=\rm Id
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The fact that the canonical Nakayama automorphism
\mathcal{N}
is of order
2
, for those quantum complete intersections
A
which admit a bi-Frobenius algebra structure,
is a quite special property. Indeed, even for a quantum complete intersection,
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
is not in general of order
2
for
\phi\neq x_{{\bf a-1}}^{*}
.
See
Example
6.10
.

5.
Quantum complete intersections with permutation antipodes

Since actions of antipodes of all the known bi-Frobenius algebra structures on quantum complete intersections
are given by scalar multiplications on the standard basis
\mathcal{B}=\{x_{\mathbf{v}}\ |\ \mathbf{v}\in V\}
up to a permutation, it is natural to consider the following notion.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(A,\ \phi,\ t,\ S)
be a bi-Frobenius algebra structure on quantum complete intersection
A=A({\bf q,a})
.
Then
S
is said to be a permutation antipode,
provided that there is a permutation
\pi
on
V
and
c_{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{K},\ \forall\ \mathbf{v}\in V
, such that
S(x_{\mathbf{v}})=c_{\mathbf{v}}x_{\pi(\mathbf{v})},\ \forall\ \mathbf{v}\in V.

If this is the case, we also call
(S,\pi)

the antipode with permutation
\pi
.

5.1.
Bi-Frobenius quantum complete intersections with permutation antipode

The following result gives properties of bi-Frobenius quantum complete intersections
A=A({\bf q,a})
with permutation antipode; in the next
section, by construction, we will see they are also sufficient for
A
to become a bi-Frobenius algebra with permutation antipode.

Recall that
\pi\in S_{n}
is a compatible permutation,
provided that
a_{\pi(i)}=a_{i}
and
q_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}=q_{ji}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
(cf. Definition
3.3
).

Theorem 5.2
.

If
A=A({\bf q,a})
admits a bi-Frobenius algebra structure with permutation antipode
(S,\pi)
.
Then

(i)

S(x_{\bf{a-1}})=x_{\bf{a-1}};

\pi(\bf{a-1})=\bf{a-1};

\pi^{2}=\rm Id;

S
is a graded map,
S^{2}=\mathcal{N}
and
S^{4}=\rm Id
.

(ii)

\pi
induces a compatible permutation in
S_{n}
, still denoted by
\pi
, such that

\pi({\bf v})=(v_{\pi^{-1}(1)},\cdots,v_{\pi^{-1}(n)}),\ \ \ \ x_{\pi(\mathbf{v})}=x_{1}^{v_{\pi^{-1}(1)}}\cdots x_{n}^{v_{\pi^{-1}(n)}},\ \ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V;

and
that
S(x_{i})=c_{i}x_{\pi(i)},
where each
c_{i}\in\mathbb{K},
satisfying

c_{i}c_{\pi(i)}=h_{{\bf e}_{i}},\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq n;\ \ \ \ \ \ \ q_{\pi}\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}=1

where
q_{\pi}
is defined in
Remark
3.2
, i.e.,
q_{\pi}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
(iii),
t=cx_{\bf{a-1}}
with
c\in\mathbb{K}-\{0\}
. By Lemma
2.3
(2)(vi) and Proposition
4.1
(iii) one has
S(t)=t
. Thus

cx_{\bf{a-1}}=t=S(t)=S(cx_{\bf{a-1}})=cS(x_{\bf{a-1}}).

Hence
S(x_{\bf{a-1}})=x_{\bf{a-1}}.
Therefore

x_{\bf{a-1}}=S(x_{\bf{a-1}})=c_{\bf{a-1}}x_{\pi(\bf{a-1})}.

Thus
x_{\pi(\bf{a-1})}=x_{\bf{a-1}}
and
c_{\bf{a-1}}=1
, so
\pi(\bf{a-1})=\bf{a-1}.

By

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\neq S(x_{\mathbf{a-1}})=S(x_{1}^{a_{1}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}-1})=S(x_{n})^{a_{n}-1}\cdots S(x_{1})^{a_{1}-1}

\displaystyle=S(x_{\mathbf{e}_{n}})^{a_{n}-1}\cdots S(x_{\mathbf{e}_{1}})^{a_{1}-1}=cx_{\pi({\bf e}_{n})}^{a_{n}-1}\cdots x_{\pi({\bf e}_{1})}^{a_{1}-1}

with
c\in\mathbb{K}
, we see that each degree
|x_{\pi({\bf e}_{i})}|=1
, since otherwise one has

|x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n})}^{a_{n}-1}\cdots x_{\pi({\bf e}_{1})}^{a_{1}-1}|>\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i}-1),

and then

S(x_{\mathbf{a-1}})=cx_{\pi({\bf e}_{n})}^{a_{n}-1}\cdots x_{\pi({\bf e}_{1})}^{a_{1}-1}=0,
a contradiction!
Thus,
\pi
induces a permutation on the subset
\{{\bf e}_{1},\ \cdots,\ {\bf e}_{n}\}
of
V
, still denoted by
\pi
, namely,
\pi\in S_{n}
, such that

\pi({\bf e}_{i})={\bf e}_{\pi(i)},\ 1\leq i\leq n.

Using the defining relation
x_{j}x_{i}=q_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}
, one can write
x_{\pi(n)}^{v_{n}}\cdots x_{\pi(1)}^{v_{1}}=c^{\prime\prime}x_{1}^{v_{\pi^{-1}(1)}}\cdots x_{n}^{v_{\pi^{-1}(n)}}
with
c^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{K}
. Therefore, for
{\bf v}\in V
one has

\displaystyle c_{\mathbf{v}}x_{\pi(\mathbf{v})}

\displaystyle=S(x_{\mathbf{v}})=S(x_{1}^{v_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{v_{n}})=S(x_{\mathbf{e}_{n}})^{v_{n}}\cdots S(x_{\mathbf{e}_{1}})^{v_{1}}

\displaystyle=c^{\prime}x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n})}^{v_{n}}\cdots x_{\pi({\bf e}_{1})}^{v_{1}}=c^{\prime}x_{{\bf e}_{\pi(n)}}^{v_{n}}\cdots x_{{\bf e}_{\pi(1)}}^{v_{1}}

\displaystyle=c^{\prime}x_{\pi(n)}^{v_{n}}\cdots x_{\pi(1)}^{v_{1}}=c^{\prime}c^{\prime\prime}x_{1}^{v_{\pi^{-1}(1)}}\cdots x_{n}^{v_{\pi^{-1}(n)}}

with
c^{\prime}c^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{K}.
Thus
x_{\pi(\mathbf{v})}=x_{1}^{v_{\pi^{-1}(1)}}\cdots x_{n}^{v_{\pi^{-1}(n)}}
and hence
\pi({\bf v})=(v_{\pi^{-1}(1)},\cdots,v_{\pi^{-1}(n)})
.

In particular, one has

S(x_{i})=S(x_{\mathbf{e}_{i}})=c_{\mathbf{e}_{i}}x_{\pi(\mathbf{e}_{i})}=c_{\mathbf{e}_{i}}x_{{\bf e}_{\pi(i)}}=c_{i}x_{\pi(i)},\ 1\leq i\leq n

with
c_{i}=c_{\mathbf{e}_{i}}
. By the argument above one sees that

x_{\pi({\bf e}_{i})}^{a_{i}-1}=x_{\pi(i)}^{a_{i}-1}\neq 0,\ \ 1\leq i\leq n.

Thus
a_{\pi(i)}\geq a_{i},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n
. Since
\pi\in S_{n}
is of finite order, it follows that
a_{\pi(i)}=a_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq n.

For
1\leq i,j\leq n
, one has

\displaystyle S(x_{i})S(x_{j})

\displaystyle=S(x_{j}x_{i})=q_{ij}S(x_{i}x_{j})=q_{ij}S(x_{j})S(x_{i})=c_{i}c_{j}q_{ij}x_{\pi(j)}x_{\pi(i)}

\displaystyle=c_{i}c_{j}q_{ij}q_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}x_{\pi(i)}x_{\pi(j)}=q_{ij}q_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}S(x_{i})S(x_{j}).

Since
q_{ij}q_{ji}=1,\ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j\leq n,
one gets
q_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}=q_{ji},\ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.
Thus,
\pi\in S_{n}
is a compatible permutation.

By
S(x_{i})=c_{i}x_{\pi(i)},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n,
we know that
S
is a graded map, and hence
S^{2}=\mathcal{N}
and
S^{4}=\rm Id
, by Proposition
4.1
(v).

By
S(x_{\mathbf{v}})=c_{\mathbf{v}}x_{\pi(\mathbf{v})}
and
S^{2}=\mathcal{N}
one has
S^{2}(x_{\mathbf{v}})=c_{\mathbf{v}}c_{\pi(\mathbf{v})}x_{\pi^{2}(\mathbf{v})},\ \forall\ \mathbf{v}\in V,
and

S^{2}(x_{\mathbf{v}})=\mathcal{N}(x_{\mathbf{v}})=h_{\mathbf{v}}x_{\mathbf{v}}.

It follows that

c_{\mathbf{v}}c_{\pi(\mathbf{v})}=h_{\mathbf{v}},\ \ x_{\pi^{2}(\mathbf{v})}=x_{\mathbf{v}},\ \forall\ \mathbf{v}\in V.
Thus
c_{i}c_{\pi(i)}=h_{{\bf e}_{i}},\ 1\leq i\leq n
and
\pi^{2}(\mathbf{v})=\mathbf{v},\ \forall\ \mathbf{v}\in V,
i.e.,
\pi^{2}=\rm Id
.

Finally, since
S(x_{\bf a-1})=x_{\bf a-1}
, one has

\displaystyle x_{\bf a-1}

\displaystyle=S(x_{\bf a-1})=S(x_{n})^{a_{n}-1}S(x_{n-1})^{a_{n-1}-1}\cdots S(x_{1})^{a_{1}-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n})}^{a_{n}-1}x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n-1})}^{a_{n-1}-1}\cdots x_{\pi({\bf e}_{1})}^{a_{1}-1}

By Remark
3.2
one has

x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n})}^{a_{n}-1}x_{\pi({\bf e}_{n-1})}^{a_{n-1}-1}\cdots x_{\pi({\bf e}_{1})}^{a_{1}-1}=q_{\pi}x_{\pi({\bf a-1})}=q_{\pi}x_{\bf a-1}.

It follows that
q_{\pi}\prod\limits\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}=1
.
This completes the proof. ∎

5.2.
Equivalent descriptions of a permutation antipode

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(A,\ \phi,\ t,\ S)
be a bi-Frobenius algebra structure on
A=A({\bf q,a})
.
Then the following statements are equivalent
:

(1)

S
is a permutation antipode
;

(2)
There is
\pi\in S_{n}
and
c_{i}\in\mathbb{K},\ 1\leq i\leq n
, such that

S(x_{i})=c_{i}x_{\pi(i)},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n;

(3)
There is a compatible permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
and
c_{i}\in\mathbb{K},\ 1\leq i\leq n
, such that

S(x_{i})=c_{i}x_{\pi(i)},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n.

Proof.

The implication (1)
\Longrightarrow
(3) follows from Theorem
5.2
; (3)
\Longrightarrow
(2) is trivial. We show (2)
\Longrightarrow
(1).
Assume that there is
\pi\in S_{n}
and
c_{i}\in\mathbb{K},\ 1\leq i\leq n
, such that

S(x_{i})=c_{i}x_{\pi(i)},\ 1\leq i\leq n.
As in the proof of Theorem
5.2
one has
a_{\pi(i)}=a_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq n.
In fact,
Since
S
is bijective, one has

0\neq S(x_{i})^{a_{i}-1}=c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}x_{\pi(i)}^{a_{i}-1},\ 1\leq i\leq n.

Thus
a_{\pi(i)}\geq a_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq n
. Since
\pi\in S_{n}
is of finite order,
a_{\pi(i)}=a_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq n.

Define a permutation on
V
, still denoted by
\pi
, as follows:

\pi({\bf v})=(v_{\pi^{-1}(1)},\cdots,v_{\pi^{-1}(n)}),\ \ \forall\ {\bf v}=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})\in V.

Since
v_{i}\leq a_{i}-1
and
a_{\pi(i)}=a_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
, it follows that

v_{\pi^{-1}(i)}\leq a_{\pi^{-1}(i)}-1=a_{i}-1,\ 1\leq i\leq n.

This implies that
\pi({\bf v})\in V
, i.e.,
\pi
is a well-defined permutation on
V
.
Thus, for
{\bf v}\in V
one has

\displaystyle S(x_{\mathbf{v}})

\displaystyle=S(x_{1}^{v_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{v_{n}})=S(x_{n})^{v_{n}}\cdots S(x_{1})^{v_{1}}

\displaystyle=c_{1}\cdots c_{n}x_{\pi(n)}^{v_{n}}\cdots x_{\pi(1)}^{v_{1}}=c_{1}\cdots c_{n}c^{\prime}x_{1}^{v_{\pi^{-1}(1)}}\cdots x_{n}^{v_{\pi^{-1}(n)}}

\displaystyle=c_{\mathbf{v}}x_{\pi(\mathbf{v})}

where
c_{\mathbf{v}}=c_{1}\cdots c_{n}c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{K}
. By definition
S
is a permutation antipode.
∎

5.3.
The case of
a_{i}\geq 3

Proposition 5.4
.

For
A=A({\bf q,a})
with
a_{i}\geq 3
and
q_{ij}\neq 1
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, if
A
admits a bi-Frobenius algebra structure such that
S
a graded map,
then
S
is necessarily a permutation antipode.

Proof.

Since
S
is graded, one can assume that
S(x_{i})=\sum\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}m_{ij}x_{j},\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq n.
Since
S
is bijective,
M=(m_{ij})
is an invertible matrix. By
x_{j}x_{i}-q_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}=0
one has

S(x_{i})S(x_{j})=q_{ij}S(x_{j})S(x_{i}),\ \forall\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

That is

\sum\limits_{1\leq s,t\leq n}m_{is}m_{jt}x_{s}x_{t}=q_{ij}\sum\limits_{1\leq s,t\leq n}m_{js}m_{it}x_{s}x_{t},\ \forall\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

Since
a_{i}\geq 3
for
1\leq i\leq n
, it follows that
x_{s}^{2}\neq 0,\ 1\leq s\leq n
.
Comparing the coefficient of
x_{s}^{2}
in the both sides of the equality above, one gets

m_{is}m_{js}=q_{ij}m_{is}m_{js},\ \forall\ 1\leq i,j,s\leq n.

Since by assumption
q_{ij}\neq 1
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, one gets

m_{is}m_{js}=0,\ \forall\ 1\leq i,j,s\leq n,\ i\neq j.

This implies that in the
s
-th (
1\leq s\leq n
) column of
M
there is at most one nonzero element.
Since
M
is invertible, in each column of
M
there is a unique nonzero element; and hence in each row of
M
there is a unique nonzero element.
Thus, there is
\pi\in S_{n}
such that
m_{i\pi(i)}\neq 0,\ 1\leq i\leq n,
and
S(x_{i})=m_{i\pi(i)}x_{\pi(i)},\ 1\leq i\leq n
. By Proposition
5.3
,
S
is
a permutation antipode.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

Proposition
5.4
actually shows that when
a_{i}\geq 3
and
q_{ij}\neq 1
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, any graded anti-automorphism
S
of
A
has the property
S(x_{i})=c_{i}x_{\pi(i)}
for some
c_{i}\in\mathbb{K},

1\leq i\leq n
, and some compatible permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
. The similar result for graded automorphisms of quantum affine
n
-space has been obtained by

S. Ceken, J. H. Palmieri, Y. H. Wang, J. J. Zhang
in
[
CPWZ
, Proposition 3.9]
.
The similar considerations have also been discussed for affine connected graded algebras and some quotients of quantum affine
n
-space e.g. in
[
G
]
,
[
BZ
]
.

6.
Construction of bi-Frobenius quantum complete intersections

We will construct a class of bi-Frobenius algebra structures
on
A=A({\bf q,a})
with permutation antipode.
A main difficulty is the construction of comultiplications such that
A
becomes
a bi-Frobenius algebra.

Recall that
\pi\in S_{n}
is a compatible permutation,
if
a_{\pi(i)}=a_{i}
and
q_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}=q_{ji}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
.
The following main result gives a sufficient and necessary condition such that
A
becomes
a bi-Frobenius algebra with permutation antipode.

Theorem 6.1
.

Quantum complete intersection
A({\bf q,a})
admits a bi-Frobenius algebra structure with permutation antipode
if and only if there is a compatible permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
with
\pi^{2}=\rm Id
and there are
c_{i}\in\mathbb{K},\ 1\leq i\leq n
, such that

c_{i}c_{\pi(i)}=h_{{\bf e}_{i}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ q_{\pi}\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}=1

where
q_{\pi}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}.

If this is the case, then
(A,\ x_{\bf a-1}^{*},\ x_{\bf a-1},\ S)
is a bi-Frobenius algebra,
where the comultiplication
\Delta
is given by
(6.3)
and
S
is given by
(\ref{equationS})
below. In particular,
S(x_{i})=c_{i}x_{\pi(i)},\ 1\leq i\leq n
.

Remark 6.2
.

If
\mathbb{K}
contains
\sqrt{-1}
and
q_{ij}=\pm 1,\ 1\leq i,j\leq n
, then one can take
\pi=\rm Id
and choose some
c_{i}\in\mathbb{K},\ 1\leq i\leq n
, such that all the conditions
in
Theorem
6.1
are satisfied. This special case has been treated in
[JZ, Theorem 3.2]
.

To prove Theorem
6.1
, we need some preparations.

6.1.
Structure constants
g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}

To get coalgebra structures on
A=A({\bf q,a})
, one needs to define elements
g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}\in\mathbb{K}
for each
{\bf v}\in V
,
which will be taken as the structure constants.

Setting up:
From now on, we consider
A=A({\bf q,a})
such that
there is a compatible permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
with
\pi^{2}=\rm Id
, and there are
c_{i}\in\mathbb{K},\ 1\leq i\leq n
, such that

c_{i}c_{\pi(i)}=h_{{\bf e}_{i}},\ 1\leq i,j\leq n;\ \ \ \ \ \ q_{\pi}\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}=1.

Since
a_{\pi(i)}=a_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq n,
it follows that if
{\bf v}=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})\in V
then

0\leq v_{\pi^{-1}(i)}\leq a_{\pi^{-1}(i)}-1=a_{i}-1,\ 1\leq i\leq n.

Thus
\pi(V)\subseteq V
, and hence
\pi
induces an action on
V
as follows, still denoted by
\pi
,

\pi({\bf v})=(v_{\pi^{-1}(1)},\cdots,v_{\pi^{-1}(n)}),\ \ \forall\ {\bf v}=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})\in V.

Thus
|x_{\pi({\bf v})}|=|x_{\bf v}|,\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V
, and
\pi({\bf 0})={\bf 0},\ \pi({\bf e}_{i})={\bf e}_{\pi(i)},\ 1\leq i\leq n.
By Lemma
3.4
(1) one has
\pi({\bf a-1})={\bf a-1}.

We will use this induced action throughout, without further explanations.

For
{\bf v}\in V
, define
g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}\in\mathbb{K}
as follows:

\displaystyle g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}=\frac{1}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf a-1-v|v}\rangle}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{v_{i}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{j}v_{k}},\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V.

(6.1)

Then one has
g_{{\bf a-1}-{\bf e}_{i},\pi({\bf e}_{i})}=\frac{c_{i}}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf a-1}-{\bf e}_{i}|{\bf e}_{i}\rangle}},\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq n.

By
\pi^{2}=\rm Id
one has

\displaystyle g_{\bf a-1-\pi(v),v}=\frac{1}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf a-1-\pi(v)|\pi(v)}\rangle}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{v_{\pi(i)}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{\pi(j)}v_{\pi(k)}},\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V.

For later use we need an alternative expression of
g_{\bf a-1-\pi(v),v}
, for which we now deduce as follows.
Note that
{\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle}=1
if
1\leq\pi(k)\leq\pi(j)\leq n
. Thus

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{\pi(j)}v_{\pi(k)}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n,\ 1\leq\pi(j)<\pi(k)\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{\pi(j)}v_{\pi(k)}}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{{\rm Lem.}\ \ref{propeqq}(2)}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n,\ 1\leq\pi(j)<\pi(k)\leq n}(q_{\pi(j)\pi(k)})^{v_{\pi(j)}v_{\pi(k)}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq\pi^{-1}(s)<\pi^{-1}(t)\leq n,\ 1\leq s<t\leq n}(q_{st})^{v_{s}v_{t}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq\pi(j)<\pi(k)\leq n,\ 1\leq j<k\leq n}(q_{jk})^{v_{j}v_{k}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq\pi(j)<\pi(k)\leq n,\ 1\leq j<k\leq n}(q_{\pi(k)\pi(j)})^{v_{j}v_{k}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq\pi(j)<\pi(k)\leq n,\ 1\leq j<k\leq n}(\frac{1}{{\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle}})^{v_{k}v_{j}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\frac{1}{{\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle}})^{v_{j}v_{k}}.

This proved the equality (6.2) below.

Lemma 6.3
.

For
A=A({\bf q,a})
, assume that
there is a compatible permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
with
\pi^{2}=\rm Id
and
c_{i}\in\mathbb{K},\ 1\leq i\leq n
, such that

c_{i}c_{\pi(i)}=h_{{\bf e}_{i}},\ 1\leq i,j\leq n;\ \ \ \ \ \ \ q_{\pi}\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}=1.

Let
g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}\in\mathbb{K}
be defined as in
(6.2)
.
Then

\displaystyle g_{\bf a-1-\pi(v),v}=\frac{1}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf a-1-\pi(v)|\pi(v)}\rangle}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{\pi(i)}^{v_{i}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\frac{1}{{\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle}})^{v_{j}v_{k}},\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V.

(6.2)

And one has
g_{\bf a-1,0}=1=g_{\bf 0,a-1}
.

Proof.

By (6.2) it is clear that
g_{\bf a-1,0}=1
. By (6.2) one has

g_{\bf 0,a-1}=\frac{1}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf 0|a-1}\rangle}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{(a_{j}-1)(a_{k}-1)}.

By assumption

\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{(a_{j}-1)(a_{k}-1)}=q_{\pi}\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}=1

one gets
g_{\bf 0,a-1}=1
.
∎

6.2.
Coalgebra structures on
A=A({\bf q,a})

Define
\varepsilon:A\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
and
\Delta:A\longrightarrow A\otimes A
to be the
\mathbb{K}
-linear maps as follows

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\varepsilon(x_{\mathbf{v}})=\delta_{\mathbf{v,0}}\ \ \text{for}\ \ \mathbf{v}\in V;\\
\Delta(1_{A})=1_{A}\otimes 1_{A};\\
\Delta(x_{\mathbf{v}})=1_{A}\otimes x_{\mathbf{v}}+x_{\mathbf{v}}\otimes 1_{A}\ \ \text{for}\ \ \mathbf{v}\in V-\{\bf 0,a-1\};\\
\Delta(x_{\mathbf{a-1}})=\sum\limits_{{\bf v}\in V}g_{{\bf a-1-v},\pi({\bf v})}\ x_{\bf a-1-v}\otimes x_{\pi(\mathbf{v})}\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ =1_{A}\otimes x_{\mathbf{a-1}}+x_{\mathbf{a-1}}\otimes 1_{A}+\sum\limits_{{\bf v}\in V-\{\bf 0,\ a-1\}}g_{{\bf a-1-v},\pi({\bf v})}\ x_{\bf a-1-v}\otimes x_{\pi(\mathbf{v})}.$$\end{array}\right.

(6.3)

where
g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}
are given by (
6.1
), see also Lemma
6.3
.

Recall that for a coalgebra structure on
A
with comultiplication
\Delta
, an element
x\in A
is
primitive
, if
\Delta(x)=1_{A}\otimes x+x\otimes 1_{A}
.
All the primitive elements of
A
form a
\mathbb{K}
-linear space, denoted by
P(A)
.

Lemma 6.4
.

The algebra
A=A({\bf q,a})
admits a coalgebra structure,
with
\varepsilon
and
\Delta
as in
(\ref{eqcoalgonA});
and
{\rm dim}P(A)\geq(\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}a_{i})-2.

Proof.

One directly verifies the coassociativity
(\Delta\otimes{\rm Id})\Delta=({\rm Id}\otimes\Delta)\Delta
and the counit property
(\varepsilon\otimes{\rm Id})\Delta\cong{\rm Id}\cong({\rm Id}\otimes\varepsilon)\Delta
. For example,

\displaystyle(

\displaystyle\Delta\otimes{\rm Id})\Delta(x_{\bf a-1})=1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}\otimes x_{\bf a-1}+1_{A}\otimes x_{\bf a-1}\otimes 1_{A}+x_{\bf a-1}\otimes 1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}

\displaystyle+\sum_{{\bf u,v}\in V-\{\bf 0,\ a-1\}}g_{{\bf a-1-v},\pi({\bf v})}(x_{\bf a-1-v}\otimes x_{\pi({\bf v})}\otimes 1_{A}+1_{A}\otimes x_{\bf a-1-v}\otimes x_{\pi({\bf v})}+x_{\bf a-1-v}\otimes 1_{A}\otimes x_{\pi({\bf v})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\rm Id}\otimes\Delta)\Delta(x_{\bf a-1}).

By (6.3), all
x_{\bf v}
with
{\bf v}\in V-\{\bf 0,a-1\}
are
\mathbb{K}
-linear independent primitive elements of
A
. Hence
dim
P(A)\geq(\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}a_{i})-2.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

The comultiplication
\Delta
in
(6.3)
is different from
the one given in
[AS2, Example 1.9]:
there
{\rm dim}P(A)=n
. Also, it generalizes one in
[JZ, (3.1)]
where

.

6.3.
Bi-Frobenius structures on
A=A({\bf q,a})

Fix the setting up in Subsection 6.1.
Choosing
\phi=x_{\bf a-1}^{*}
and
t=x_{\bf a-1}
. Consider the
\mathbb{K}
-linear map
S:A\longrightarrow A
given by

\displaystyle S(x_{\bf v})

\displaystyle=\sum\phi(t_{1}x_{\bf v})t_{2}=g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi({\bf v})}{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf a-1-v|v}\rangle}x_{\pi({\bf v})}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{v_{i}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{j}v_{k}}x_{\bf\pi(v)},\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V.

(6.4)

In particular,
S(1_{A})=1_{A};\ \ S(x_{{\bf a-1}})=g_{\bf 0,a-1}x_{{\bf a-1}}=x_{{\bf a-1}}
, and

S(x_{i})=g_{{\bf a-1}-{\bf e}_{i},\pi({\bf e}_{i})}{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf a-1}-{\bf e}_{i}|{\bf e}_{i}\rangle}x_{\pi(i)}=c_{i}x_{\pi(i)},\ 1\leq i\leq n.

Theorem 6.6
.

For
A=A({\bf q,a})
, assume that
there is a compatible permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
with
\pi^{2}=\rm Id
, and that there are
c_{i}\in\mathbb{K}\ (1\leq i\leq n)
such that

c_{i}c_{\pi(i)}=h_{{\bf e}_{i}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ q_{\pi}\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}=1

where
q_{\pi}=\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}.

Then
(A,\ \phi=x_{\bf a-1}^{*},\ t=x_{\bf a-1},\ S)
is a bi-Frobenius algebra,
where the coalgebra structure is given by
(6.3)
and
S
is given by
(\ref{equationS})
. In particular,
S(x_{i})=c_{i}x_{\pi(i)},\ 1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.4
,
\varepsilon
and
\Delta
in (
6.3
) give a coalgebra structure on
A
. It remains to check that the conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) in Definition
2.2

are satisfied. By definition
\varepsilon:A\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
is an algebra map, and
1_{A}
is a group-like element. By Lemma
2.6
,
(A,\ \phi)
is a Frobenius algebra.

By (6.3) one has

\displaystyle t\leftharpoonup x_{\bf a-1-v}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum x_{\bf a-1-v}^{*}(t_{1})t_{2}=\begin{cases}1,\ &\mbox{if}\ \bf v=\bf 0;\\
x_{\bf a-1},\ &\mbox{if}\ \bf v={\bf a-1};\\
g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}x_{\pi({\bf v})},\ &\mbox{if}\ {\bf v}\in V-\{\bf 0,a-1\}\end{cases}.

\displaystyle=g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}x_{\pi({\bf v})},\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V.

Since
\pi
induces a permutation of
V
and
g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}\neq 0
for
{\bf v}\in V
, one has
A=t\leftharpoonup A^{*}
, i.e.,
(A,\ x_{\bf a-1})
is a Frobenius coalgebra.

To show
S
is an algebra anti-homomorphism of
A
, it suffices to show
S(x_{\bf u}x_{\bf v})=S(x_{\bf v})S(x_{\bf u})
for
{\bf u},{\bf v}\in V
. By (
6.4
) one has

\displaystyle S(x_{\mathbf{v}})=\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{v_{i}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{j}v_{k}}x_{\bf\pi(v)};

\displaystyle S(x_{\mathbf{v}})S(x_{\mathbf{u}})={\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(v)|\pi(u)}\rangle}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{u_{i}+v_{i}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{j}v_{k}+u_{j}u_{k}}x_{\pi({\bf u+v})};

\displaystyle S(x_{\mathbf{u}}x_{\mathbf{v}})={\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{u|v}\rangle}S(x_{\bf u+v})

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ ={\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{u|v}\rangle}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{u_{i}+v_{i}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{(u_{j}+v_{j})(u_{k}+v_{k})}x_{\pi({\bf u+v})}.

By Lemma
3.4
(3) one has

{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(v)|\pi(u)}\rangle}={\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u|v}\rangle}\prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{u_{j}v_{k}+v_{j}u_{k}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle S(x_{\mathbf{u}}x_{\mathbf{v}})

\displaystyle={\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{u|v}\rangle}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{u_{i}+v_{i}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{(u_{j}+v_{j})(u_{k}+v_{k})}x_{\pi({\bf u+v})}

\displaystyle={\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{u|v}\rangle}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{u_{i}+v_{i}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{j}v_{k}+u_{j}u_{k}}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{u_{j}v_{k}+v_{j}u_{k}}x_{\pi({\bf u+v})}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\bf q}^{\langle\mathbf{u|v}\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(v)|\pi(u)}\rangle}}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf u|v}\rangle}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{u_{i}+v_{i}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{j}v_{k}+u_{j}u_{k}}x_{\pi({\bf u+v})}

\displaystyle={\bf q}^{\langle{\bf\pi(v)|\pi(u)}\rangle}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{u_{i}+v_{i}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{j}v_{k}+u_{j}u_{k}}x_{\pi({\bf u+v})}

\displaystyle=S(x_{\mathbf{v}})S(x_{\mathbf{u}}).

Now we verify that
S
is a coalgebra anti-homomorphism of
A
, i.e.,
\varepsilon\circ S=\varepsilon
and
(S\otimes S)\circ\tau\circ\Delta=\Delta\circ S.

Since
|x_{\pi({\bf v})}|=|x_{\bf v}|,\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V
, one has

\varepsilon\circ S(x_{\bf v})=\delta_{\mathbf{v,0}}=\varepsilon(x_{\bf v}),\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V

i.e.,
\varepsilon\circ S=\varepsilon
. Also, one has
(S\otimes S)\circ\tau\circ\Delta(1)=1\otimes 1=(\Delta\circ S)(1).
For
{\bf v}\in V-\{\bf 0,a-1\}
, one has

\pi({\bf v})\in V-\{\bf 0,a-1\}
and

\displaystyle(S\otimes S)\circ\tau\circ\Delta(x_{\bf v})=1\otimes S(x_{\bf v})+S(x_{\bf v})\otimes 1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi({\bf v})}{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf a-1-v|v}\rangle}(1\otimes x_{\bf\pi(v)}+x_{\bf\pi(v)}\otimes 1)=g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi({\bf v})}{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf a-1-v|v}\rangle}\Delta(x_{\bf\pi(v)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Delta\circ S)(x_{\bf v}).

Note that
(\Delta\circ S)(x_{\bf a-1})=\Delta(x_{\bf a-1})=\sum\limits_{{\bf v}\in V}g_{{\bf a-1-v},\pi({\bf v})}\ x_{\bf a-1-v}\otimes x_{\pi(\mathbf{v})}.
Since
\pi^{2}=\rm Id
, one has

\displaystyle(S\otimes S)\circ\tau\circ\Delta(x_{\bf a-1})=\sum_{{\bf v}\in V}g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}S(x_{\bf\pi(v)})\otimes S(x_{\bf a-1-v})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf v}\in V}g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}g_{\bf a-1-\pi(v),v}g_{\bf v,a-1-\pi(v)}{\bf q}^{\langle\bf a-1-\pi(v)|\pi(v)\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle\bf v|a-1-v\rangle}x_{\bf v}\otimes x_{\bf a-1-\pi(v)}.

For each
{\bf v}\in V
, comparing the coefficients of
x_{\bf v}\otimes x_{\bf a-1-\pi(v)}
, it suffices to verify

g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}g_{\bf a-1-\pi(v),v}g_{\bf v,a-1-\pi(v)}{\bf q}^{\langle\bf a-1-\pi(v)|\pi(v)\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle\bf v|a-1-v\rangle}=g_{{\bf v},{\bf a-1-\pi(v)}},\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V

or, equivalently

g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}g_{\bf a-1-\pi(v),v}{\bf q}^{\langle\bf a-1-\pi(v)|\pi(v)\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle\bf v|a-1-v\rangle}=1,\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V.

By construction (
6.1
) and (
6.2
), one has

\displaystyle g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}=\frac{1}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf a-1-v|v}\rangle}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{v_{i}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}({\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle})^{v_{j}v_{k}},\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V

and

g_{\bf a-1-\pi(v),v}=\frac{1}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf a-1-\pi(v)|\pi(v)}\rangle}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{\pi(i)}^{v_{i}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(\frac{1}{{\bf q}^{\langle\pi({\bf e}_{k})|\pi({\bf e}_{j})\rangle}})^{v_{j}v_{k}},\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V.

Thus, by definition
h_{\bf v}=\frac{{\bf q}^{\bf\langle a-1-v|v\rangle}}{{\bf q}^{\bf\langle v|a-1-v\rangle}},\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V,
one has

\displaystyle g_{\bf a-1-v,\pi(v)}g_{\bf a-1-\pi(v),v}{\bf q}^{\langle\bf a-1-\pi(v)|\pi(v)\rangle}{\bf q}^{\langle\bf v|a-1-v\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\bf q}^{\langle\bf v|a-1-v\rangle}}{{\bf q}^{\langle{\bf a-1-v|v}\rangle}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}(c_{i}c_{\pi(i)})^{v_{i}}=\frac{1}{h_{\bf v}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}(c_{i}c_{\pi(i)})^{v_{i}},\ \forall\ {\bf v}\in V.

By Lemma
2.5
(5),
h_{\bf v}=\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}{h_{{\bf e}_{i}}}^{v_{i}}
for
{\bf v}\in V
. By assumption
c_{i}c_{\pi(i)}=h_{{\bf e}_{i}},\ 1\leq i\leq n

6.4.
Symmetric case

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
A=A({\bf q,a})
be a symmetric algebra. Then
A
admits a bi-Frobenius algebra structure with permutation antipode
if and only if there is a compatible permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
with
\pi^{2}=\rm Id
.

If this is the case, then
(A,\ x_{\bf a-1}^{*},\ x_{\bf a-1},\ S)
is a bi-Frobenius algebra,
where the comultiplication
\Delta
is given by
(6.3)
,
S
is given by
(\ref{equationS})
, and
S(x_{i})=c_{i}x_{\pi(i)}
for some
c_{i}\in\mathbb{K},\ 1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.1
, it suffices to show the existence of
\{c_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}\subset\mathbb{K}-\{0\}
satisfying the conditions

c_{i}c_{\pi(i)}=h_{{\bf e}_{i}},\ 1\leq i\leq n;\ \ \ \ \ \ q_{\pi}\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}=1.

Since
A
is a symmetric algebra, each
h_{{\bf e}_{i}}=1
(cf. Lemma
2.6
). By Lemma
3.5
(4), these conditions can be rewritten as

c_{i}c_{\pi(i)}=1,\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq n;\ \ \ \ \ \prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<k\leq n,\\
j,\ k\ \in\ I.\end{subarray}}(q_{kj})^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}=1

where
I
is the same as in Lemma
3.5
, i.e.,
I=\{i\ |\ 1\leq i\leq n,\ \pi(i)=i\}
. Also, recall that
J=\{i\ |\ 1\leq i\leq n,\ \pi(i)\neq i\}
.
We choose
c_{i}
as follows:

c_{i}=\begin{cases}\prod\limits_{j\geq i,j\in I}(q_{ij})^{(a_{j}-1)},&\mbox{if}\ i\in I;\\
1,&\mbox{if}\ i\in J.\end{cases}

If
i\in J
, then
\pi(i)\in J
, and hence
c_{i}c_{\pi(i)}=1
for
i\in J
.

If
i\in I
, then
\pi(i)=i
and

c_{i}c_{\pi(i)}=c_{i}^{2}=\prod\limits_{j\geq i,\ j\in I}(q_{ij})^{2(a_{j}-1)}=\prod\limits_{j\geq i,\ j\in I}(q_{ij}q_{\pi(i)\pi(j)})^{(a_{j}-1)}=\prod\limits_{j\geq i,\ j\in I}(q_{ij}q_{ji})^{(a_{j}-1)}=1.

On the other hand, one has

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<k\leq n,\\
j,\ k\ \in\ I\end{subarray}}q_{kj}^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}=\prod\limits_{i\in I}c_{i}^{a_{i}-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<k\leq n,\\
j,\ k\ \in\ I\end{subarray}}q_{kj}^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{i\in I}\prod\limits_{j\geq i,j\in I}q_{ij}^{(a_{i}-1)(a_{j}-1)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<k\leq n,\\
j,\ k\ \in\ I\end{subarray}}q_{kj}^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq j\leq n\\
j,\ k\ \in\ I\end{subarray}}q_{kj}^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<k\leq n\\
j,\ k\ \in\ I\end{subarray}}q_{kj}^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}=\prod_{j,k\in I}q_{kj}^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{k\in I}\prod_{j\in I}q_{kj}^{(a_{k}-1)(a_{j}-1)}{\mathrel{\to}^{{\rm Lem.}\ \ref{lemmaI}(3)}}\prod_{k\in I}h_{{\bf e}_{k}}^{a_{k}-1}=1.

This completes the proof.
∎

A

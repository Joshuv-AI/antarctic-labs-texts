Local well-posedness of the capillary-gravity water waves with acute contact angles
Mei Ming, Chao Wang, [2mm] School of Mathematics and Statistics,Yunnan University, Kunming 615000, P.R.China [2mm] E-mail: mingmei@ynu.edu.cn, [2mm] School of Mathematical Science, Peking University, Beijing 100871, P.R.China [2mm] E-mail: wangchao@math.pku.edu.cn

Abstract
We consider the two-dimensional capillary-gravity water waves problem where the free surface Γ t \Gamma_{t} intersects the bottom Γ b \Gamma_{b} at two contact points. In our previous works [ 52 , 53 ] , the local well-posedness for this problem has been proved with the contact angles less than π / 16 \pi/16 . In this paper, we study the case where the contact angles belong to ( 0 , π / 2 ) (0,\pi/2) . It involves much worse singularities generated from corresponding elliptic systems, which have a strong influence on the regularities for the free surface and the velocity field. Combining the theory of singularity decompositions for elliptic problems with the structure of the water waves system, we obtain a priori energy estimates. Based on these estimates, we also prove the local well-posedness of the solutions in a geometric formulation.

Introduction

We consider the irrotational incompressible capillary-gravity water waves problem in a two-dimensional domain
\Omega_{t}
, where
\Omega_{t}
is a bounded domain with an upper free surface
\Gamma_{t}
and a fixed bottom
\Gamma_{b}
. This moving domain contains two moving contact points
p_{l},p_{r}
(left and right) with the contact angles
\omega_{l},\omega_{r}\in(0,\pi/2)
, which are the intersection points of
\Gamma_{t},\Gamma_{b}
:

\Gamma_{t}\cap\Gamma_{b}=\{p_{l},p_{r}\}.

Moreover, the fixed bottom
\Gamma_{b}
is assumed to be smooth enough, and it becomes straight near the contact points
p_{i}
(
i=l,r
) for the sake of simplicity.

The water waves problem has been widely studied in centuries, see for example
[
63
,
49
]
. This problem focuses on the motion of an ideal fluid and describes the evolution of the free surface
\Gamma_{t}
as well as the velocity field
v
. Mathematically, it is described by Euler’s equation with boundary conditions and initial conditions, and in our case, we also need some boundary conditions at contact points.

We express the water waves problem on the corner domain
\Omega_{t}
as the following system
(WW)
of velocity
v
and pressure
P
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v=-\nabla P+{\bf g}\qquad\hbox{in}\quad\Omega_{t},

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits v=0,\quad\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits v=0\qquad\hbox{in}\quad\Omega_{t},

(1.2)

\displaystyle P|_{\Gamma_{t}}=\sigma\kappa,

(1.3)

\displaystyle D_{t}=\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla\ \textrm{is tangent to}\ \{(t,X)|X\in\partial\Omega_{t}\},

(1.4)

\displaystyle v\cdot n_{b}|_{\Gamma_{b}}=0,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\beta_{c}v_{i}=\sigma(\cos{\omega_{s}}-\cos{\omega_{i}})\qquad\hbox{at}\quad p_{i}\ (i=l,r).

(1.6)

Here (
1.1
) from
(WW)
is the Euler’s equation where
{\bf g}=-g{\bf e_{z}}
is the vertical gravity vector; (
1.2
) describes the incompressibility and irrotationality;
(
1.3
) is the condition of the pressure on the free surface in the case with surface tension, where
\sigma
is the coefficient of surface tension and
\kappa
is the mean curvature of
\Gamma_{t}
(see Section 1.2); (
1.4
) is the classical kinematic condition on the free surface
\Gamma_{t}
with
D_{t}
the material derivative; Meanwhile, (
1.5
) describes that the velocity along the fixed bottom
\Gamma_{b}
is always tangential, where
n_{b}
is the unit outward normal vector of
\Gamma_{b}
. These equations and conditions are standard in water waves, see
[
45
,
46
]
.

In particular, (
1.6
) gives the conditions at contact points, which come from
[
58
]
. We denote by
v_{i}
the upward tangential components of the velocity at the corner points along
\Gamma_{b}
:

v_{l}=-v\cdot\tau_{b}\quad\hbox{at}\ p_{l},\quad\hbox{and}\quad v_{r}=v\cdot\tau_{b}\quad\hbox{at}\ p_{r}.

Here
\omega_{s}
is the stationary contact angle decided by the materials of the bottom and the fluid (see
[
76
]
), and
\beta_{c}
denotes the effective friction coefficient. This condition shows that the slip velocity is dominated by the unbalanced Young stress, and it is indeed an effective variation of Young’s law (1805) for stationary contact angles
[
76
]
. In fact, this kind of conditions are commonly seen, see
[
11
,
12
,
62
,
27
]

Compared to the rich literature on the well-posedness of classical water waves, the research on the well-posedness of water waves problem with non-smooth boundaries (we call it “non-smooth water waves”) just started several years ago and there are a lot of open questions . In general, there are two kinds of non-smooth water waves problems: The first kind of problems has contact points (or contact lines) between the free surface and the bottom, i.e.
\Gamma_{t}\cap\Gamma_{b}\neq\emptyset
; The other kind contains crests or cusps on the free surface, i.e. the surface is Lipschitz. We would like to mention that in the case with large crest angles, the famous Stokes waves can be dated back to papers by G. Stokes
[
63
,
64
]
which obtained traveling-wave solutions with limit crest angle
2\pi/3
. Obviously, the main difference here compared to classical water waves lies in the corners on boundaries. As a result, the analysis involving the corners (i.e. domain singularities) becomes the key point in the non-smooth water waves.

Now, we are in a position to state an informal version of the main result.
To begin with, we introduce the following compatibility conditions at
t=0

\beta_{c}\partial_{t}^{k}v_{i}(0)=\sigma\partial_{t}^{k}\big(\cos{\omega_{s}}-\cos{\omega_{i0}}\big)\quad\hbox{at}\quad p_{i}\,(i=l,r),\quad k=0,1,2,3.

(1.8)

These conditions are needed since we will reduce system
\mbox{(}WW)
into initial-boundary value problem (see (
3.35
), (
5.30
) and (
6.44
)).

Theorem 1.1

Let the initial data belong to a suitable space and the initial contact angles
\omega_{i0}\in(0,\pi/2)
for
i=l,r
. If the compatibility conditions (
1.8
) are satisfied, there exists a time interval depending on the initial data such that system
(WW)
is locally well-posed in a suitable space.

Remark 1.1

The suitable space is defined by
\Sigma_{h}
in Section 6.3 for some good unknowns. In fact, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is divided into three parts. In Section 4 and Section 5, we obtain a priori estimates (see Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
5.1
). In the last section, we construct approximate solutions to prove the well-posedness based on the a priori estimates. The precise statement of local well-posedness for a geometric form of
\mbox{(}WW)
is given in Theorem
6.1
(Section 6.3).

Remark 1.2

When there is no surface tension, the authors of
[
40
,
19
]
studied the case with contact angles. They assume that the wall
\Gamma_{b}
is vertical and the contact angle
\omega\in(0,\pi/4)
. Then, by a symmetric extension, they reduced the problem to the crest case with the crest angle less than
\pi/2
. As a result,
[
40
,
19
]
do not contain the case of limit Stokes waves with the “
2\pi/3
” crest. In that case, one needs a contact angle of
\pi/3
even with the help of a symmetric extension. In this paper, we only require that the contact angles
\omega_{i}\in(0,\pi/2)
, which brings us some useful experiences to deal with domain singularities in a more general case for water waves.

Remark 1.3

By continuity argument of the contact angle in time similarly as in the last part of
[
52
]
, one can see that as long as initial contact angles
\omega_{i0}\in(0,\pi/2)
, we will have
\omega_{i}(t)\in(0,\pi/2)

We recall some works concerning the local well-posedness of non-smooth water waves.
In the case where there are crests or cusps on the free surface (the “crest” case), Alazard, Burq and Zuily
[
5
]
study a special case (without surface tension) when the contact angle is equal to
\pi/2
(the right angle), where they used symmetric and periodic extension to turn this problem into a classical smooth periodic case. A breakthrough in this subject is made by Kinsey and Wu, see
[
40
,
70
]
.
They focus on gravity water waves where the crest angle is less than
\pi/2
. The main difficulty is that the free surface is a non-
C^{1}
interface with angled crest and the Taylor sign
-\nabla_{n_{t}}P
degenerates at the crest point. To be more precise, they start with reducing the water waves problem into the following equation in
[
40
,
70
]
:

\partial_{t}^{2}u+\mathfrak{a}\nabla_{n}u=f(u,\partial_{t}u)

where
\mathfrak{a}=-\nabla_{n_{t}}P
is the Taylor sign. When
\mathfrak{a}
degenerates at the crest point, the above system will loss its hyperbolicity, and classical analysis does not apply any more. To solve the problem, Kinsey and Wu flatten the domain with a Riemann mapping, and some singular weights appear naturally in their equation. As a result, they introduce some weighted Sobolev spaces accordingly for energy estimates to deal with these singular weights. Based on these works, Agrawal
[
1
]
show that these singularities are “rigid”, which means that the angle of these crests can not change in time. Very recently,
Córdoba, Enciso and Grubic
[
19
]
study a similar case with cusps and crests without gravity, where the angles of these crests are less than
\pi/2
and change in time.

For the other case where there are contact angles (that is
\Gamma_{t}\cap\Gamma_{b}\neq\emptyset
), things become different from the “crest” case. First, the corners appear due to intersections of the free surface and the bottom (or wall); Second, there are different boundary conditions in corresponding elliptic systems compared to the crest case. In fact, various boundary conditions may have no big difference if we only focus on the elliptic theory, but there will be a series of consequences in water waves when boundary conditions change. For example, the evolution of the free surface is different from the crest case. Moreover, when there is surface tension, boundary conditions at the contact points as (
1.6
) are needed in order to close the system, and dissipations appear at the contact points too (See Section 4 and
[
52
]
).

In the case with contact angles, de Poyferré
[
23
]
prove a priori estimates in bounded n-dimensional corner domains without surface tension. The contact angle is assumed to be small to ensure sufficient Sobolev regularity near the corner. Meanwhile, under a similar assumption of small contact angles, we obtain the local well-posedness in a two-dimensional corner domain (beach type) with surface tension, see
[
52
,
53
]
. Meanwhile, we notice that
[
23
]
and
[
52
,
53
]
use similar geometry formulations introduced in
[
60
]
.

To explain why small contact angles are needed in
[
52
,
53
,
23
]
, we look at a typical mixed-boundary system in water waves:

\begin{cases}{}\Delta u=0\qquad in\quad\Omega_{t},\\
u|_{\Gamma_{t}}=f,\quad\nabla_{n_{b}}u|_{\Gamma_{b}}=0.\end{cases}

In fact, the elliptic theory on corner domains is well known already, see for example
[
43
,
30
,
44
]
. Generally, one still has variational solution
u\in H^{1}(\Omega_{t})
if the right-side functions lie in proper Sobolev spaces. If one seeks for
H^{2}
and above regularities, singularity decompositions are needed naturally for the solution, which decompose the solution into a singular part
u_{s}
(i.e. not good enough) near the corners and a regular part
u_{r}
:

u=u_{s}+u_{r}\qquad\hbox{according to the required regularity.}

For the mixed-boundary system above, the most singular part in
u_{s}
is like
r^{\pi/2\omega}
, where
r
is the radius with respect to the corner point and
\omega
is the contact angle. Consequently, when
\omega
is small enough, the singular part
u_{s}
will be good enough so that we will have enough regularities from elliptic systems as in classical water waves to close the energy estimates. One can also find a singularity decomposition for
v
in Proposition
2.3
(Section 2).

In contrast, when the contact angle is larger or more general, the idea of taking small angles to improve regularities in
[
23
,
52
,
53
]
does not work any more. Meanwhile, there is no obvious weighted space to use due to the structure of the water waves problem.

We want to show here the main ingredients of this paper.
Similarly as in our previous works
[
52
,
53
]
, we still adopt the geometric formulation from Shatah and Zeng
[
60
,
61
]
. In fact, we rewrite
(\hbox{WW})
into an equation for
\mathfrak{J}=\nabla\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}
with boundary conditions, where
\mathfrak{K}
is the modified mean curvature on
\Gamma_{t}
and
{\cal H}
means the harmonic extension in
\Omega_{t}
(see Section 3, and we only present a simpler form for this equation here):

D^{2}_{t}\mathfrak{J}+\sigma{\cal A}\mathfrak{J}=R\quad\hbox{with}\quad{\cal A}\mathfrak{J}=\nabla{\cal H}(-\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}}\mathfrak{J}^{\perp}),

(1.9)

where
R
is some remainder part.

The trouble here is that singularities from the domain
\Omega_{t}
affect directly the regularity of the solution to
(WW)
, which means singularities always appear in related elliptic systems even if the boundary conditions are good enough in Sobolev spaces (see for example
[
30
]
). More precisely, the natural norm
\|{\cal A}^{k}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}
with
k\geq 1
arising from this equation above is not equivalent with
\|f\|_{H^{3k}(\Omega_{t})}
due to larger contact angles, and apparently “singular parts” are contained in this norm.

Due to this kind of singularities from elliptic systems, we only have limited regularities for some quantities (for example, the velocity
v
) in Sobolev spaces.
Compared to
[
52
,
53
]
, we must make full use of the maximal regularity for each quantity (especially for
v
) while the contact angles
\omega_{i}\in(0,\pi/2)
. To do this, some delicate estimates together with singular parts from singularity decompositions (see
[
51
,
30
]
) are carefully used. Meanwhile, it is also very important to gain more information from the structure of
(WW)
.

The main part of the (lower-order) energy functional is defined as

E(t)=\|D_{t}\mathfrak{J}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|\mathfrak{J}\cdot n_{t}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{t})},

which gives us the following estimate (see Section 4.1)

\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|D_{t}v\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|D_{t}^{2}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}+\|\kappa\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}\leq P(E(t))

with
P(E(t))
the positive-coefficient polynomial of
E(t)
.
This means that the free surface
\Gamma_{t}
still has enough regularity and
v
is Lipschitz.

In our previous work
[
52
,
53
]
, one main part from the remainder term
R
in (
1.9
) is about the higher-order derivative terms of
P_{v,v}
, where
P_{v,v}
is defined by an elliptic system with mixed-boundary conditions. According to the elliptic theory on corner domains (see for example
[
30
]
), the system of
P_{v,v}
only gives
P_{v,v}
limited regularity around
H^{2}(\Omega_{t})
, when the contact angles are less than
\pi/2
. To improve its regularity, we modify the definition of
P_{v,v}
in this paper to have a Neumann-boundary system, see
(
3.21
). In fact, thanks to the elliptic theory, solutions of Neumann-boundary system (or Dirichlet system) may become more regular than solutions of mixed-boundary problem in Sobolev spaces, while the right-side data have the same regularity. As a result, we obtain a bit more regularity from the elliptic system of
P_{v,v}
(we have
P_{v,v}\in H^{3}(\Omega_{t})
indeed), which is important in the energy estimates.

Moreover, another main part of
R
lies in the higher-order derivative terms of the velocity
v
. Here, we point out that compared to
[
52
,
53
]
,
v
loses some regularity due to the existence of corners. Fortunately,
D_{t}v
and
D_{t}^{2}v
have the same regularities as before. As a result, when we deal with
v
, sometimes we need to use material derivatives
D_{t}
instead of spatial derivatives. Meanwhile, we also need to apply singularity decompositions to
v
and its potential
\phi
in the estimates, see for example Lemma
4.5
.

For the higher-order energy estimates, we use the material derivative
D_{t}
instead of
{\cal A}^{1/2}
from
[
60
]
. The higher-order energy is defined as

E_{1}(t)=\|\nabla_{\tau_{t}}D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}\cdot n_{t})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|D^{2}_{t}\mathfrak{J}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}.

In fact, one will see that
D_{t}
is convenient to use when there are contact points. For example, taking
D_{t}
on elliptic systems does not violate boundary conditions, while it will change boundary conditions if one takes spatial derivatives. Moreover, one will find in the higher-order energy estimate that taking
D_{t}
leads to better regularities for some quantities than their own regularities (like
D_{t}v
for the velocity
v
). However, due to the singularities of the boundary (contact points), it is not as convenient as before to turn these regularities into spatial regularities, which explains the reason why we need to choose very carefully for “a suitable space” in Theorem
1.1
. As long as the energy estimates for
E(t),E_{1}(t)
are finished, we use the equation of
\mathfrak{J}
to gain more spatial regularities.

[

[

proved a priori estimates for the contact line problem for two-dimensional Navire-Stokes flow. For Darcy’s flow, one can see
[
41
,

]
.

Organization of the paper

In Section 2, we present various useful lemmas including singularity decompositions and estimates for elliptic systems. In Section 3, we derive the equation for the good unknown
\mathfrak{J}
from

with modified curvature

and modified pressure

u|_{\Gamma_{t}}=0,\qquad\nabla_{n_{b}}u|_{\Gamma_{b}}=g.\end{array}\right.

- The Sobolev norm

for the boundary
\Gamma_{t}

-

2
Preliminaries

To get started, we recall some useful estimates for Sobolev space.

Lemma 2.1

(Sobolev Embeddings) One has the following inclusion:

W^{s,p}(\Omega)\subseteq L^{q}(\Omega)

for
1/q=1/p-s/n
and
\Omega
any bounded open subset of
\mathop{\bf R\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{n}
with a Lipschitz boundary. Moreover, one also has

W^{s,p}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{t,q}(\bar{\Omega})

for
t\leq s,q\geq p
such that
s-n/p=t-n/q
.

Lemma 2.2

(Product estimates)
(1) For functions
f\in H^{1/2}(\Omega_{t})
and
g\in H^{1}(\Omega_{t})\cup L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})
, one has the following product estimate:

\|f\,g\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega_{t})}\big(\|g\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\big)

with a constant
C
independent of
f,g
;

(2) For functions
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{t})\cup H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{t})
, one has

\|f\,g\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{t})}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{t})}\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|f\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{t})}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{t})}\big)

with a constant
C
independent of
f,g
.

Proof.

(1) In fact, one firstly extends
f,g
to be defined on the full plane
\mathop{\bf R\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}
with a control of their corresponding norms. Secondly, one can apply standard para-product analysis to prove the estimate on
\mathop{\bf R\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}
. The details are omitted here. (2) The proof is similar to the proof of (1).

\blacksquare

We quote some Hardy inequalities here.

Lemma 2.3

(Hardy inequalities) (1)(Corollary 2.3
[
15
]
) Let
f\in H^{1}(0,d)\cup C^{0}(0,d)
with
d>0
and
f(0)=0
. Then there exists a positive constant
C=C(\epsilon,d)
such that

\int^{d}_{0}r^{-2\epsilon}|f(r)|^{2}dr\leq C\int^{d}_{0}r^{-2\epsilon+2}|f^{\prime}(r)|^{2}dr

for
\epsilon\in(1/2,1)
;

(2) (Fractional-order version, see
[
26
,
29
]
) For a number
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and any function
f\in H^{\epsilon}(\mathop{\bf R\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{+})
with
f(0)=0
, there exists a positive constant

C=C(\epsilon)
such that

\int_{\mathop{\bf R\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{+}}r^{-2\epsilon}|f(r)|^{2}dr\leq C\int_{\mathop{\bf R\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{+}}\int_{\mathop{\bf R\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{+}}\frac{|f(r)-f(\rho)|^{2}}{|r-\rho|^{1+2\epsilon}}drd\rho.

Lemma 2.4

(Traces on
\Gamma_{t}
or
\Gamma_{b}
, Theorem 5.3
[
52
]
)
The maps

u\mapsto\{u,\nabla_{n_{j}}u\}|_{\Gamma_{j}},\quad\hbox{for}\quad j=t,b

have unique continuous extensions as operators from

H^{s}(\Omega_{t})
onto
\Pi^{1}_{i=1}H^{s-i-1/2}(\Gamma_{j})
for

.

Moreover, one has the estimate :

\|u\|_{H^{s-1/2}(\Gamma_{j})}+\|\nabla_{n_{j}}u\|_{H^{s-3/2}(\Gamma_{j})}\leq C\big(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{s-1/2}}\big)\|u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega_{t})}.

and

\blacksquare

R_{0}
that

n_{t}\cdot\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}}\nabla P_{v,v}=[n_{t},\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}}]\cdot\nabla P_{v,v}.

Moreover, we use the following Hodge decomposition from
[
60
,
52
]
:

{\cal D}_{t}\mathfrak{J}=D_{t}\mathfrak{J}+\nabla{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}

(3.33)

such that
{\cal D}_{t}\mathfrak{J}
satisfies

\nabla\cdot{\cal D}_{t}\mathfrak{J}=0\quad\mbox{on}\ \Omega_{t},\quad\hbox{and}\quad n_{b}\cdot{\cal D}_{t}\mathfrak{J}\big|_{\Gamma_{b}}=0,

and

{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}
satisfies the Neumann-boundary system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}=-tr(\nabla\mathfrak{J}\nabla v)\qquad\hbox{in}\quad\Omega_{t}\\
\nabla_{n_{t}}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}|_{\Gamma_{t}}=C_{\mathfrak{J},v}(t)-\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\mathfrak{K}\nabla_{\tau_{t}}v\cdot n_{t},\quad\nabla_{n_{b}}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}|_{\Gamma_{b}}=\mathfrak{J}\cdot\nabla_{v}n_{b}\end{array}\right.

(3.34)

with

|\Gamma_{t}|C_{\mathfrak{J},v}(t)=-\int_{\Omega_{t}}tr(\nabla\mathfrak{J}\nabla v)dX+\int_{\Gamma_{t}}\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\mathfrak{K}\nabla_{\tau_{t}}v\cdot n_{t}ds-\int_{\Gamma_{b}}\mathfrak{J}\cdot\nabla_{v}n_{b}ds.

Here the assumption

\int_{\Omega_{t}}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}dX=0

holds similarly to guarantee the uniqueness as for
P_{v,v}
system.

This implies immediately

\begin{split}D_{t}{\cal D}_{t}\mathfrak{J}=D_{t}(D_{t}\mathfrak{J}+\nabla{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v})=D^{2}_{t}\mathfrak{J}+D_{t}\nabla{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}.\end{split}

As a result, we finally conclude the equation for
\mathfrak{J}
in the following form based on (
3.32
):

D_{t}\big[{\cal D}_{t}\mathfrak{J}-\nabla{\cal H}\big(D_{t}P_{v,v}-v\cdot(\nabla P_{v,v}|_{c})\big)\big]+\sigma{\cal A}\mathfrak{J}={\cal R}

(3.35)

where the third-order elliptic operator
{\cal A}
is defined in the same way as in
[
60
,
52
]
:

{\cal A}(w)=\nabla{\cal H}\big(-\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}}(w|_{\Gamma_{t}})^{\perp}\big)

for any smooth-enough function
w
defined on
\Omega_{t}
,
and the remainder term
{\cal R}
is

{\cal R}=R_{0}+D_{t}\nabla{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}+D_{t}\nabla{\cal H}\big(v\cdot(\nabla P_{v,v}|_{c})\big).

Moreover, one can find the definition of
\nabla P_{v,v}|_{c}
in the notation part.

Remark 3.1

Instead of (
3.32
), we write the equation of
\mathfrak{J}
in a more complicated form (
3.35
). This happens because of technical reasons. In fact,
{\cal D}_{t}\mathfrak{J}
is more handy to use in the energy estimates, while as a price we have the remainder
{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}
part to deal with. Meanwhile, the part
v\cdot(\nabla P_{v,v}|_{c})
is added to derive better estimate for
D_{t}P_{v,v}
, see Lemma
4.7
.

4
Lower-order energy estimates

The lower-order energy
E(t)
is defined as

E(t)=\|\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\mathfrak{J}^{\bot}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|{\cal D}_{t}\mathfrak{J}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|\Gamma_{t}\|^{2}_{H^{5/2}}+\|v\|^{2}_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{t})},

where we write

E_{l}(t)=\|\Gamma_{t}\|^{2}_{H^{5/2}}+\|v\|^{2}_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{t})}.

Moreover, recalling from
[
52
,
53
]
that our
(WW)
system has some dissipation at corner points, we have the following dissipation term at corner points

F(t)=\sum_{i=l,r}\big|(\sin\omega_{i})\nabla_{{\bf\tau}_{t}}\mathfrak{J}^{\perp}|_{p_{i}}\big|^{2}.

Theorem 4.1

Let the contact angles
\omega_{i}\in(0,\pi/2)
. Assume that
E(t),\int_{0}^{T}F(t)dt
are both bounded above in
[0,T]
for some
T>0
. Then the following a priori estimate holds for system
(WW)
:

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}E(t)+\int_{0}^{T}F(t)dt\leq P(E(0))+\int_{0}^{T}P(E(t))dt,

where
P(\cdot)
is some polynomial with positive constant coefficients depending on
\sigma,\beta_{c}
.

One can see immediately that, our energy is defined mainly in forms of
\mathfrak{J}
. As a result, we need to related
(v,P)
,
\Gamma_{t}
and other quantities to
\mathfrak{J}
firstly before we prove this energy estimate.

4.1
Dependence on
E(t)

We show in this part that all the quantities related to our problem can be controlled by
E(t)
.
To start with, the following proposition focuses on the estimates for
P_{v,v},n_{t},\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H},\mathfrak{J}
.

Lemma 4.1

Assuming that
E(t)\in L^{\infty}[0,T]
for some
T>0
, one has the following estimates

\|P_{v,v}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq P(E_{l}(t))

and

\|n_{t}\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|\mathfrak{J}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}\|_{H^{5/2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq P(E(t)).

Proof.
(1) The first inequality. Applying Proposition
2.2
with an interpolation to
P_{v,v}
system (
3.21
), we get

\|P_{v,v}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\big(\|tr(\nabla v\nabla v)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+|\Gamma_{t}|^{1/2}|C_{v,v}(t)|+\|v\cdot\nabla_{v}n_{b}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{b})}+\|P_{v,v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\big).

For the right-hand side, we have firstly by Lemma
2.1
that

\|tr(\nabla v\nabla v)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\|v\|^{2}_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{t})}.

Second, it’s straightforward to find

|C_{v,v}(t)|\leq C\|v\|^{2}_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{t})}

and

\begin{split}\|P_{v,v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}&=\big\|P_{v,v}-\int_{\Omega_{t}}P_{v,v}dX\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C\|\nabla P_{v,v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\big(\|tr(\nabla v\nabla v)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+|\Gamma_{t}|^{1/2}|C_{v,v}(t)|+\|v\cdot\nabla_{v}n_{b}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{b})}\big),\end{split}

where (
3.22
) is used and the variational estimate above is proved similarly as Proposition
2.2
(1).

Consequently, summing up all these inequalities above, we conclude that

\|P_{v,v}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\|v\|^{2}_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq P(E_{l}(t)).

(2) The second inequality. In fact, the key point of the proof lies in the estimates for mean curvature
\kappa
and
\mathfrak{K}=\sigma\kappa-P_{v,v}
.

To begin with, one can see immediately that
\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}
satisfies the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}=0\qquad\hbox{in}\quad\Omega_{t}\\
\nabla_{n_{t}}\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}|_{\Gamma_{t}}=\mathfrak{J}^{\perp}|_{\Gamma_{t}}\in H^{1}(\Gamma_{t}),\qquad\nabla_{n_{b}}\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}|_{\Gamma_{b}}=0.\end{array}\right.

(4.36)

Applying Proposition
2.2
(2), we find

\begin{split}\|\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}\|_{H^{5/2}(\Omega_{t})}&\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})(\|\mathfrak{J}^{\perp}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})})\\
&\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})(\|\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\mathfrak{J}^{\bot}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}),\end{split}

where interpolation for
\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}
is applied to
\|\mathfrak{J}^{\perp}\|_{L^{2}}
.

Moreover, from the definition of
\mathfrak{K}
and Lemma
2.7
we have

\|\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\big(\|\kappa\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|P_{v,v}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{t})}\big),

which together with the previous inequality leads to

\|\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}\|_{H^{5/2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})(1+\|\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\mathfrak{J}^{\bot}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|P_{v,v}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega_{t})}).

(4.37)

This implies immediately the desired
H^{3/2}
estimate for
\mathfrak{J}
.

For the estimate for
n_{t}
, we find by Lemma
2.4
, (
4.37
) and part (1) that

\begin{split}\|\kappa\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{t})}&\leq\sigma^{-1}(\|\mathfrak{K}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|P_{v,v}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{t})})\\
&\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{\frac{5}{2}}})(1+\|\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\mathfrak{J}^{\bot}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|P_{v,v}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{t})})\leq P(E(t)).\end{split}

(4.38)

As a result, the proof is finished thanks to the fact

\kappa=\nabla_{\tau_{t}}n_{t}\cdot\tau_{t}\quad\hbox{where}\ \nabla_{\tau_{t}}n_{t}\|\tau_{t}\quad\hbox{on}\ \Gamma_{t}.

\blacksquare

Lemma 4.2

Assuming that
E(t)\in L^{\infty}[0,T]
for some
T>0
, one has the following estimates for
v
:

\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|v^{\perp}\|_{H^{5/2}(\Gamma_{t})}\leq P(E(t)).

Meanwhile, one also has

\|D_{t}P_{v,v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}+\|{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}\leq P(E(t))

Proof.
-
H^{1}
estimate for
{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}
.
From system (
3.34
) of
{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}
, we can have the following variational estimate by Proposition
2.2
(1) and Remark
2.1
:

\begin{split}\|{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}\leq&C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\big(\|tr(\nabla\mathfrak{J}\nabla v)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+|\Gamma_{t}|^{1/2}|C_{\mathfrak{J},v}(t)|+\|\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\mathfrak{K}\cdot\nabla_{\tau_{t}}v\cdot n_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}\\
&+\|\mathfrak{J}\cdot\nabla_{v}n_{b}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{b})}\big),\end{split}

which together with Lemma
4.1
leads to the desired estimate for
{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}
.

-
v^{\perp}
estimate. In fact, it is straightforward to get by Lemma
2.4
that

\|D_{t}\kappa\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\|D_{t}\kappa_{\cal H}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})(\|\nabla D_{t}\kappa_{\cal H}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|D_{t}\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}).

Rewriting
\nabla\kappa_{\cal H}
in terms of
\mathfrak{J},\nabla P_{v,v}
leads to

\begin{split}\|D_{t}\kappa\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{t})}&\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\big(\|D_{t}\mathfrak{J}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|D_{t}\nabla P_{v,v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|\nabla v\cdot\mathfrak{J}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|\nabla v\cdot\nabla P_{v,v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\qquad+\|D_{t}\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}\big)\\
&\leq P(E(t))\big(1+\|\nabla{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|D_{t}P_{v,v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}+\|P_{v,v}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|\mathfrak{J}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\qquad+\|D_{t}\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}\big).\end{split}

Applying Lemma
4.1
, we have

\|D_{t}\kappa\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{t})}\leq P(E(t))\big(1+\|\nabla{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|D_{t}P_{v,v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}+\|D_{t}\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}\big).

On the other hand, we rewrite (
3.25
) into

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}}v^{\perp}=-D_{t}\kappa-v^{\perp}|\nabla_{\tau_{t}}n_{t}|^{2}+(\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\nabla_{v^{\top}}n_{t})\cdot\tau_{t}-(\nabla_{\tau_{t}}v^{\top})^{\top}\cdot\nabla n_{t}\cdot\tau_{t},

which together with the inequality above for
\|D_{t}\kappa\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{t})}
and the estimate for
{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}
imply

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}}v^{\perp}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{t})}&\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}},\|n_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}(\Gamma_{t})})\big(\|D_{t}\kappa\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\big)\\
&\leq P(E(t))\big(1+\|\nabla{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|D_{t}P_{v,v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}+\|D_{t}\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\big)\\
&\leq P(E(t))\big(1+\|D_{t}P_{v,v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}+\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\big).\end{split}

(4.39)

Here we use (
3.26
) to obtain

\|D_{t}\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}.

As a result, the estimate for
v^{\perp}
depends on the estimates for
\|D_{t}P_{v,v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})},\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}
.

-
H^{2}
estimate for
v
. Recalling system (
4.44
) for velocity potential
\phi
with
v=\nabla\phi
, we have by Proposition
2.2
that

\begin{split}\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}&\leq\|\phi\|_{H^{3}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\big(\|v^{\perp}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\big)\\
&\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\big(\|v^{\perp}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\big)\\
&\leq P(E(t))\big(1+\|v^{\perp}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Gamma_{t})}\big).\end{split}

Bringing the above estimate into (
4.39
) and applying interpolations to
\|v^{\perp}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Gamma_{t})}
, we arrive at

\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}}v^{\perp}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|v\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq P(E(t))\big(1+\|D_{t}P_{v,v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}\big).

(4.40)

Therefore, it remains to deal with
\|D_{t}P_{v,v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}
.

-
D_{t}P_{v,v}
estimates. From the definition of
P_{v,v}
, we derive the system for
D_{t}P_{v,v}
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta D_{t}P_{v,v}=-D_{t}tr(\nabla v\nabla v)+2tr(\nabla v\nabla^{2}P_{v,v})\qquad\hbox{in}\quad\Omega_{t}\\
\nabla_{n_{t}}D_{t}P_{v,v}|_{\Gamma_{t}}=C^{\prime}_{v,v}(t)+\nabla_{n_{t}}v\cdot\nabla P_{v,v}|_{\Gamma_{t}},\quad\nabla_{n_{b}}D_{t}P_{v,v}|_{\Gamma_{b}}=D_{t}(v\cdot\nabla_{v}n_{b})+\nabla_{n_{b}}v\cdot\nabla P_{v,v}|_{\Gamma_{b}}.\end{array}\right.

(4.41)

with

\int_{\Omega_{t}}D_{t}P_{v,v}dX=0.

It is easy to see from Euler’s equation that

D_{t}tr(\nabla v\nabla v)=-2tr(\nabla v\cdot\nabla v\,\nabla v)-2tr\big(\nabla(\nabla P_{v,v}+\nabla\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H})\nabla v\big),

(4.42)

and similar expression can be derived for
D_{t}(v\cdot\nabla_{v}n_{b})
on
\Gamma_{b}
.

Moreover, we have

C^{\prime}_{v,v}=|\Gamma_{t}|^{-1}\int_{\Omega_{t}}2tr(D_{t}\nabla v\nabla v)dX-|\Gamma_{t}|^{-2}\partial_{t}|\Gamma_{t}|\int_{\Omega_{t}}tr(\nabla v\nabla v)dX,

where
\frac{d}{dt}|\Gamma_{t}|=\int_{\Gamma_{t}}\big(v^{\perp}\kappa+\nabla_{\tau_{t}}(v\cdot\tau_{t})\big)ds
. So we obtain by Lemma
4.1
, (
4.37
) and (
4.38
) that

|C^{\prime}_{v,v}|\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}},\|v\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{t})},\|P_{v,v}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})},\|\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})},\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}\big)\leq P(E(t)).

(4.43)

To prove
H^{1}
estimate for
D_{t}P_{v,v}
, we use the following form of variation equation:

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\big|\nabla D_{t}P_{v,v}\big|^{2}dX=&-\int_{\Omega_{t}}(\Delta D_{t}P_{v,v})D_{t}P_{v,v}dX+\int_{\Gamma_{t}}(\nabla_{n_{t}}D_{t}P_{v,v})D_{t}P_{v,v}ds\\
&+\int_{\Gamma_{b}}(\nabla_{n_{b}}D_{t}P_{v,v})D_{t}P_{v,v}ds.\end{split}

Substituting the expressions in (
4.41
) into the equality above, we can deal with the integrals one by one, where Lemma
2.1
is applied. For example, for the highest-order terms of
v
on the boundary, we use Green’s Formula and Lemma
2.1
to find

\begin{split}&\int_{\Gamma_{t}}\nabla_{n_{t}}v\cdot P_{v,v}\,D_{t}P_{v,v}ds+\int_{\Gamma_{b}}\nabla_{n_{b}}v\cdot P_{v,v}\,D_{t}P_{v,v}ds\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{t}}tr(\nabla v\nabla^{2}P_{v,v})D_{t}P_{v,v}dX+\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla v\cdot\nabla P_{v,v}\cdot\nabla D_{t}P_{v,v}dX\\
&\leq\|\nabla v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{t})}\|\nabla^{2}P_{v,v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\|D_{t}P_{v,v}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{t})}+|\nabla v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{t})}\|\nabla P_{v,v}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{t})}\|\nabla D_{t}P_{v,v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\|v\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{t})}\|P_{v,v}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\|D_{t}P_{v,v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}.\end{split}

The other integrals above can be handled similarly and the details are omitted.

As a result, we obtain by Lemma
4.1
the following desired estimate:

\|D_{t}P_{v,v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}\leq P(E(t))\big(\|P_{v,v}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}\|_{H^{5/2}(\Omega_{t})}+|C^{\prime}_{v,v}|\big)\leq P(E(t)).

Consequently, applying (
4.40
) leads to the estimates for
v^{\perp}
and
v
.

\blacksquare

Remark 4.1

We know immediately from the proof of Lemma
4.2
the following estimate

\|D_{t}\kappa\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{t})}\leq P(E(t)).

Moreover, we also have

\|D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}\leq\sigma\|D_{t}\kappa_{\cal H}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}+\|D_{t}P_{v,v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{t})}\leq P(E(t)).

Based on these lemmas above, we are ready to show some higher-order estimates.

Lemma 4.3

Let
E(t)\in L^{\infty}[0,T]
for some

, then one has

\|P_{v,v}\|_{H^{5/2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|n_{t}\|_{H^{3}(\Gamma_{t})}\leq P(E(t)).

For

, similar arguments as in the proof of Lemma

Applying Lemma
4.2
, we have the desired estimate.

Again, similar arguments as (

\displaystyle\|\kappa\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}\leq\sigma^{-1}\big(\|\mathfrak{K}\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|P_{v,v}\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}\big)\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})(1+\|\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\mathfrak{J}^{\bot}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|P_{v,v}\|_{H^{5/2}(\Omega_{t})}),

Therefore, combining this with the estimate for

, the proof is finished.

\blacksquare

We also present more estimates for

.

Lemma 4.4

Assuming that
E(t)\in L^{\infty}[0,T]
for some

, one has

\|D_{t}v\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|D^{2}_{t}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq P(E(t))

.

.

\blacksquare

We now consider the following Neumann system for velocity potential
\phi
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta\phi=0,\qquad\hbox{on}\quad\Omega_{t}\\

(4.44)

where in order to obtain the uniqueness we chose

without loss of generality.

We show by singularity decompositions that

lies in

, which is a key ingredient in the following estimates.

Lemma 4.5

Assume that
E(t)\in L^{\infty}[0,T]
for some
T>0
, then there exists a unique
\phi\in H^{3}(\Omega_{t})
to system (
4.44
) and one finds the following singular decomposition:

\phi=\phi_{r}+\phi_{s}

where the regular part

, and the singular part is expressed in the same way as

in Proposition

.
Moreover, one has

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq P(E(t)).

Proof.

In fact, applying Proposition
2.3
, we know immediately about the existence of
\phi\in H^{3}(\Omega_{t})
and the singular decomposition. Therefore, we have

v=\nabla\phi_{r}+\nabla\phi_{s}=v_{r}+v_{s},\qquad\hbox{with}\quad v_{r}\in H^{3}(\Omega_{t}).

(4.45)

It only remains to show the estimate for
\nabla v
. Since we have

\phi_{s}=\chi_{\omega}(\omega_{l})\chi_{l}\,c_{l}r^{\pi/\omega_{l}}\circ T_{l}+\chi_{\omega}(\omega_{r})\chi_{r}\,c_{r}r^{\pi/\omega_{r}}\circ T_{r}

where the singular part exists when

. In this case, we find by Proposition

that

\begin{split}\|\nabla^{2}\phi_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}&\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{4}})\big(\|v^{\perp}\|_{H^{5/2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\big)\big(\|\chi_{l}r^{\pi/\omega_{l}-2}\circ T_{l}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\\
&\quad+\|\chi_{r}r^{\pi/\omega_{r}-2}\circ T_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\big)\end{split}

with
\pi/\omega_{i}-2\in(0,1)
. Consequently, we obtain

\|\nabla^{2}\phi_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{4}})\big(\|v^{\perp}\|_{H^{5/2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\big)\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{4}})\|v^{\perp}\|_{H^{5/2}(\Gamma_{t})}

thanks to a direct variational estimate

\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\|v^{\perp}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}.

Moreover, we also have by Proposition
2.3
the following estimate:

\|\phi_{r}\|_{H^{4}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{4}})\big(\|v^{\perp}\|_{H^{5/2}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\big)\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{4}})\|v^{\perp}\|_{H^{5/2}(\Gamma_{t})}.

As a result, we find that

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq\|\nabla^{2}\phi_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}+\|\nabla^{2}\phi_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{4}})\|v^{\perp}\|_{H^{5/2}(\Gamma_{t})},

which together with Lemma
4.2
lead to the desired estimate.

\blacksquare

Lemma 4.7

Assuming

\displaystyle\big\|D_{t}P_{v,v}-v\cdot(\nabla P_{v,v}|_{c})\big\|_{H^{5/2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq P(E(t)).

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta w=-trD_{t}(\nabla v\nabla v)+[\Delta,v]\cdot(\nabla P_{v,v}-\nabla P_{v,v}|_{c}\big)-v\cdot\Delta(\nabla P_{v,v}|_{c})\quad\hbox{in}\quad\Omega_{t}\\

(4.46)

Applying Proposition
2.2
to system (
4.46
), we have

\|w\|_{H^{5/2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C(\|\Gamma_{t}\|_{H^{5/2}})\big(\|\Delta w\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|\nabla_{n_{t}}w\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{t})}+\|\nabla_{n_{b}}w\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{b})}+\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}\big).

4.2
, Lemma
4.3

\displaystyle\|D_{t}tr(\nabla v\nabla v)\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega_{t})}+\big\|[\Delta,v]\cdot(\nabla P_{v,v}-\nabla P_{v,v}|_{c})\big\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega_{t})}+\|v\cdot\Delta(\nabla P_{v,v}|_{c})\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega_{t})}\leq P(E(t)).

for the product estimate of

Second, for the boundary terms, we only need to take care of the following estimate from
\|\nabla_{n_{t}}v\cdot(\nabla P_{v,v}-\nabla P_{v,v}|_{c})\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{t})}

\begin{split}&\big\|\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\nabla_{n_{t}}v\cdot(\nabla P_{v,v}-\nabla P_{v,v}|_{c})\big\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}\\

(4.47)

Here the estimate for the second term in the equality above holds thanks to (
4.45
) and Proposition
2.3
.

To handle the first term in (
4.47

T_{i}
from Proposition
2.3
and (

\begin{split}&\big\|\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\nabla_{n_{t}}v\cdot\chi_{i}(\nabla P_{v,v}-\nabla P_{v,v}|_{p_{i}})\big\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{t})}\\

In the end, the estimate for

Lemma 4.8

Let
E(t)\in L^{\infty}[0,T]
for some
T>0
. Then, we have the following estimate:

\Big|\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla D_{t}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\cdot D_{t}\mathfrak{J}dX-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\cdot\nabla v\cdot\nabla\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}dX\Big|\leq P(E(t)).

Recalling system (
3.34
), we get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta D_{t}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}=-D_{t}tr(\nabla\mathfrak{J}\nabla v)+2tr(\nabla v\nabla^{2}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v})\qquad\hbox{in}\quad\Omega_{t}\\

\nabla_{n_{b}}D_{t}P_{\mathfrak{J},v}|_{\Gamma_{b}}=D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}\cdot\nabla_{v}n_{b})+\nabla_{n_{b}}v\cdot\nabla P_{\mathfrak{J},v}.\end{array}\right.

(4.49)

Therefore, we have directly

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla D_{t}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\cdot D_{t}\mathfrak{J}dX=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla D_{t}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\cdot D_{t}\nabla\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}dX\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla D_{t}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\cdot\nabla D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}dX+\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla D_{t}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\cdot\nabla v\cdot\nabla\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}dX\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla D_{t}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\cdot\nabla D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}dX+\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\cdot\nabla v\cdot\nabla\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}dX-\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\cdot D_{t}(\nabla v\cdot\nabla\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}})dX\\

which leads to the following equality:

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla D_{t}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\cdot D_{t}\mathfrak{J}dX-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\cdot\nabla v\cdot\nabla\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}dX\\

To finish the proof, the key lies in the analysis for the first integral. The remainder part is controlled by
P(E(t))
thanks to Lemma
4.1
, Lemma
4.2
, Remark
4.1
and Lemma
4.6
.

For the first integral on the right side of the equality above, we have by Green’s Formula that

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla D_{t}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\cdot\nabla D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}dX\\
&=\int_{\Gamma_{t}}(\nabla_{n_{t}}D_{t}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v})D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}ds+\int_{\Gamma_{b}}(\nabla_{n_{b}}D_{t}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v})D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}ds-\int_{\Omega_{t}}\Delta D_{t}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}dX\\
&=\int_{\Gamma_{t}}\big[C^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{J},v}-D_{t}(\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}\cdot\nabla_{\tau_{t}}v\cdot n_{t})+\nabla_{n_{t}}v\cdot\nabla{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\big]D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}ds+\int_{\Gamma_{b}}D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}\cdot\nabla_{v}n_{b})D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}ds\\
&\quad+\int_{\Gamma_{b}}\nabla_{n_{b}}v\cdot\nabla P_{\mathfrak{J},v}\,D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}ds+\int_{\Omega_{t}}D_{t}tr(\nabla\mathfrak{J}\nabla v)\,D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}dX-\int_{\Omega_{t}}2tr(\nabla v\nabla^{2}{\cal P}_{\mathfrak{J},v})\,D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}dX\\
&=C^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{J},v}\int_{\Gamma_{t}}D_{t}\mathfrak{K}ds-\int_{\Gamma_{t}}\big(\nabla_{\tau_{t}}D_{t}\mathfrak{K}\cdot\nabla_{\tau_{t}}v\cdot n_{t}\big)D_{t}\mathfrak{K}ds+\int_{\Gamma_{b}}(D_{t}\mathfrak{J}\cdot\nabla_{v}n_{b})D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{{\cal H}}ds\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega_{t}}tr(\nabla D_{t}\mathfrak{J}\nabla v)D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}dX+\hbox{l.o.t.},\end{split}

(4.50)

where the remainder lower-order terms can be controlled by
P(E(t))
in a similar way as before.

Now we deal with the terms in (
4.50
) one by one. To begin with, we write from the definition of
C_{\mathfrak{J},v}
in system (
3.34
) that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\big(|\Gamma_{t}|C_{\mathfrak{J},v}\big)&=-\int_{\Omega_{t}}D_{t}tr(\nabla\mathfrak{J}\nabla v)dX+\int_{\Gamma_{t}}D_{t}(\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\mathfrak{K}\nabla_{\tau_{t}}v\cdot n_{t}ds)-\int_{\Gamma_{b}}D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}\cdot\nabla_{v}n_{b}ds)\\
&=-\int_{\Omega_{t}}tr(\nabla D_{t}\mathfrak{J}\nabla v)dX+\int_{\Gamma_{t}}\nabla_{\tau_{t}}D_{t}\mathfrak{K}\nabla_{\tau_{t}}v\cdot n_{t}ds-\int_{\Gamma_{b}}D_{t}J\cdot\nabla_{v}n_{b}ds+\hbox{l.o.t.},\end{split}

where the lower-order terms can be controlled again and hence the details are omitted. Applying Green’s Formula again and using the decompositions

\begin{split}&\nabla D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}=(\nabla_{\tau_{t}}D_{t}\mathfrak{K})\tau_{t}+(\nabla_{n_{t}}D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H})n_{t}\qquad\hbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{t},\\
&\nabla D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}=(\nabla_{\tau_{b}}D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H})\tau_{b}+(\nabla_{n_{b}}D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H})n_{b}\qquad\hbox{on}\quad\Gamma_{b},\end{split}

we find

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\big(|\Gamma_{t}|C_{\mathfrak{J},v}\big)\\

&\quad-\int_{\Gamma_{b}}\nabla D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}\cdot\nabla_{v}n_{b}ds+\hbox{l.o.t.}\\

(4.51)

Here, for the last three terms on
\Gamma_{b}
, thanks to the assumption that
n_{b}
is constant near the contact points
p_{i}
and

, we know that
\nabla_{\tau_{b}}v\cdot n_{b}=-v\cdot\nabla_{\tau_{b}}n_{b}

\nabla_{n_{b}}D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}|_{\Gamma_{b}}=[\nabla_{n_{b}},D_{t}]\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}=\nabla_{n_{b}}v\cdot\nabla\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}.

2.5
and Lemma
2.6
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\Gamma_{b}}\nabla_{\tau_{b}}D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}\cdot\nabla_{\tau_{b}}v\cdot n_{b}ds+\int_{\Gamma_{b}}\nabla D_{t}\mathfrak{K}_{\cal H}\cdot\nabla_{v}n_{b}ds\\

Meanwhile, the condition (
4.52
) at the contact points are rewritten as

D_{t}\mathfrak{J}_{a}=\pm\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\beta_{c}}\sin\omega_{i}(\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\mathfrak{J}_{a})^{\bot}\tau_{b}+R_{c1,a}\quad\hbox{at}\quad p_{i}(i=l,r)

with

R_{c1,a}=R_{c1}+\sigma aD_{t}\nabla{\cal H}(d_{\Gamma_{t}}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}}^{-1})+\sigma a\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\beta_{c}}(n_{t}\cdot\tau_{b})\big(\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\nabla{\cal H}(d_{\Gamma_{t}}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}}^{-1})\big)^{\perp}.

Next, we consider how to recover the free surface and the domain from
\mathfrak{K}_{a}
, which is slightly different from
[
53
]
. In
[
53
]
, we use the equation of
\mathfrak{N}_{a}={\cal N}(\kappa+a\,d_{\Gamma_{t}}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}}^{-1})
together with the boundary information
d_{i}=d_{\Gamma_{t}}|_{p_{i*}}
of
d_{\Gamma_{t}}
to recover the free surface
d_{\Gamma_{t}}
, so the system of
(\mathfrak{K}_{a},d_{l},d_{r})
is needed; In this paper, we use the quantity
\mathfrak{K}_{a}
instead of
\mathfrak{N}_{a}
, where an extra
P_{v,v}
is added here in (
6.42
).
Moreover, we will need to use the equation and norms of
\mathfrak{J}_{a}
in the iteration scheme, where
\mathfrak{K}_{a}
can be retrieved. In fact, we have
\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{J}_{a}\cdot n_{t}={\cal N}\mathfrak{K}_{a}
on
\Gamma_{t}
. Therefore, to identify
\mathfrak{K}_{a}
, we look at the Neumann-boundary elliptic system of
{\cal H}(\mathfrak{K}_{a})
with the compatibility condition
\int_{\Gamma_{t}}\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{\perp}ds=0
. We know immediately that there exists a unique solution
{\cal H}(\mathfrak{K}_{a})
up to an additive constant to this system.
As a result, as long as we have
\mathfrak{J}_{a}
, we obtain
\mathfrak{K}_{a}
. (One can also check Lemma 2.5 in
[
53
]
.)

Consequently, to recover
d_{\Gamma_{t}}
(which is the key to recover the water-waves system), we need the system of
(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})
. As long as we have proved the existence of the solution to this system, we obtain immediately the following quantity

\kappa+a\,d_{\Gamma_{t}}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}}^{-1}=\sigma^{-1}(\mathfrak{K}_{a}+P_{v,v})\quad\hbox{with the boundary information}\ d_{l},d_{r}.

As a result, the the desired function
d_{\Gamma_{t}}
can be solved directly from these quantities above in a similar way as in Proposition 4.2
[
53
]
, and then we can finally recover our water-waves system
(WW)
.

Based on the analysis above, we need to give the boundary condition of
d_{\Gamma_{t}}
which is deduce from (
6.39
) (for more details, see (4.27) in
[
53
]
). In fact, one has the following evolution equations for
d_{i}(t)=d_{\Gamma_{t}}(p_{i*})
(
i=l,r
):

d_{i}^{\prime\prime}(t)=\mathfrak{B}_{i},\quad i=l,r,

(6.43)

where

\begin{split}\mathfrak{B}_{i}&=-\frac{1}{\mu\cdot(n_{t}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}})}\Big(\mu\cdot(n_{t}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}})\nabla_{v^{*}}\partial_{t}d_{\Gamma_{t}}+\nabla_{v^{*}}\mu\cdot(n_{t}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}})\partial_{t}d_{\Gamma_{t}}+\sigma\mathfrak{K}_{a}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}}\\
&\quad-\sigma^{2}a{\cal N}(d_{\Gamma_{t}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{S_{t}})\circ\Phi_{S_{t}}+(\nabla P_{v,v}+{\bf g})\circ\Phi_{S_{t}}\cdot(n_{t}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}})\Big)\big|_{p_{i*}}.\end{split}

We rewrite the equation for
P_{v,v}
by (
4.41
):

D_{t}P_{v,v}=\Delta^{-1}_{N}(h_{p},f_{p},g_{p}),

where
\Delta^{-1}_{N}
means solving the Neumann-boundary system (
4.41
) with
\int_{\Omega_{t}}D_{t}P_{v,v}dX=0
, and the right-side functions are

h_{p}=2tr(\nabla v\cdot\nabla v\,\nabla v)+2tr\big(\nabla(\nabla P_{v,v}+\mathfrak{J})\nabla v\big)+2tr(\nabla v\nabla^{2}P_{v,v}),\quad f_{p}=C^{\prime}_{v,v}(t)+\nabla_{n_{t}}v\cdot\nabla P_{v,v}\big|_{\Gamma_{t}}

and

g_{p}=-(mJ+\nabla P_{v,v}+{\bf g})\cdot\big(\nabla n_{b}+(\nabla n_{b})^{*}\big)\cdot v+v\cdot D_{t}(\nabla n_{b})\cdot v+\nabla_{n_{b}}v\cdot\nabla P_{v,v}.

As a result, we sum up the system of
(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})
as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{K}_{a}+\sigma(a-\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}}){\cal N}(\mathfrak{K}_{a})=R_{a,0},\\
D_{t}\mathfrak{J}_{a}=\pm\sigma^{2}\beta^{-1}_{c}\sin\omega(\nabla_{\tau_{t}}\mathfrak{J}_{a})^{\bot}\tau_{b}+R_{c1,a},\quad\textrm{at}\quad p_{i}(i=l,r),\\
D_{t}P_{v,v}=\Delta^{-1}_{N}(h_{p},f_{p},g_{p}),\\
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}d_{i}(t)=\mathfrak{B}_{i},\quad i=l,r.\end{array}\right.

(6.44)

Based on these preparations above, we are finally ready to state our precise form of Theorem
1.1
. We start with introducing the space
\Sigma
for given
T,L>0
:

\Sigma=\big\{(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})\big|\|(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})\|_{\Sigma}\leq L\big\}

where the norm

\begin{split}\|(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})\|_{\Sigma}&\triangleq\|\partial_{t}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}})\|_{C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{*}))}+\|(\mathfrak{J}_{a}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}})\cdot n_{t*}\|_{C([0,T];H^{1}(\Gamma_{*}))}\\
&\quad+\|P_{v,v}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}}\|_{C([0,T];H^{5/2}(\Omega_{*}))}+\sum_{i=l,r}\Big(|d_{i}|_{C([0,T])}+|d(d_{i})/dt|_{C([0,T])}\Big).\end{split}

Meanwhile, according to the higher-order energy
E_{h}(t)
, we also define the space (for given
L_{1}>0
)

\Sigma_{h}=\big\{(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})\big|\|(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})\|_{\Sigma_{h}}\leq L_{1}\big\}

by the norm

\begin{split}\|(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})\|_{\Sigma_{h}}&\triangleq\|(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})\|_{\Sigma}+\|\partial_{t}\big((\mathfrak{J}_{a}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}})\cdot n_{t*}\big)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Gamma_{*}))}\\
&\quad+\|\partial^{2}_{t}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}})\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{*}))}.\end{split}

The initial data is given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}})\cdot n_{t*}\big|_{t=0}=\bar{\mathfrak{J}}_{a,0},\quad\partial_{t}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}})\big|_{t=0}=\bar{\mathfrak{J}}_{a,1},\\
P_{v,v}\circ\Phi_{S_{t}}\big|_{t=0}=\bar{P}_{v,v,0},\quad d_{i}(0)=d_{i,0},\quad\frac{d}{dt}d_{i}(0)=d_{i,1}.\end{array}\right.

Now we can present the local well-posedness theorem.

Theorem 6.1

Assume that the initial data
\big(\bar{\mathfrak{J}}_{a,0},\bar{\mathfrak{J}}_{a,1},\bar{P}_{v,v,0},d_{i,0},d_{i,1}\big)\in H^{1}(\Gamma_{*})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{*})\times H^{5/2}(\Omega_{*})\times\mathop{\bf R\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}
and initial contact angles
\omega_{i0}\in(0,\pi/2)
for
i=l,r
. When the compatibility conditions (
1.8
) at
t=0
are satisfied for
k=0,1,2,3
, there exists a unique solution
(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})\in\Sigma_{h}
to system (
6.44
). Moreover, system (
6.44
) is locally well-posed with
(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})
depending continuously on the initial data in
\Sigma
.

6.4
Iteration scheme

In this subsection, we present the iteration scheme. First of all, we set the initial boundary
S_{0}=S_{*}
without loss of generality. To simplify the notations, we denote by

D_{t*}=\partial_{t}+v^{k}_{*}\cdot\nabla,\quad D_{t}=\partial_{t}+v^{k}\cdot\nabla,

when no confusion will be made.

When we have
(\mathfrak{K}^{k}_{a},P^{k}_{v,v},d^{k}_{l},d^{k}_{r})
, the linear system of
(\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a},P^{k+1}_{v,v},d^{k+1}_{l},d^{k+1}_{r})
for the iteration scheme is set to be

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}D_{t*}^{2}(\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}\circ\Phi^{k}_{S_{t}})+\sigma(a-\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}}){\cal N}(\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a})\circ\Phi^{k}_{S_{t}}=R^{k}_{a,0}\circ\Phi^{k}_{S_{t}},\\
D_{t*}(\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}_{a}\circ\Phi^{k}_{S_{t}})=\pm\sigma^{2}\beta^{-1}_{c}\sin\omega^{k}_{i}(\nabla_{\tau^{k}_{t}}\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1})^{\bot}\tau_{b}\circ\Phi^{k}_{S_{t}}+R^{k}_{c1,a}\circ\Phi^{k}_{S_{t}}\quad\textrm{at}\quad p_{i*},\quad i=l,r,\\
D_{t*}(P^{k+1}_{v,v}\circ\Phi^{k}_{S_{t}})=\Delta^{-1}_{N}(h^{k}_{p},f^{k}_{p},g^{k}_{p})\circ\Phi^{k}_{S_{t}}\\
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}d^{k+1}_{i}(t)=\mathfrak{B}^{k}_{i},\qquad i=l,r.\end{array}\right.

(6.45)

Here we use the superscript
k
on
R_{a,0}
(for example) to denote that all the quantities there are obtained using
(\mathfrak{K}^{k}_{a},P^{k}_{v,v},d^{k}_{l},d^{k}_{r})
.

Moreover, we point out that the velocity
v
in the definition of
R^{k}_{a,0}
is given by

D_{t}\tilde{v}^{k}=-J^{k}-\nabla P_{v^{k},v^{k}}-{\bf g},

while in the other quantities we use
v^{k}
defined by (
6.39
)-(
6.41
). This happens due to the difference of regularities using these two definitions, which can be seen already in the previous sections.

Besides, the initial data is given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}_{a}\circ\Phi^{k}_{S_{t}})\cdot n^{k}_{t*}\big|_{t=0}=\bar{\mathfrak{J}}_{a,0},\quad\partial_{t}(\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}_{a}\circ\Phi^{k}_{S_{t}})\big|_{t=0}=\bar{\mathfrak{J}}_{a,1},\\
P^{k+1}_{v,v}\circ\Phi^{k}_{S_{t}}\big|_{t=0}=\bar{P}_{v,v,0},\quad d^{k+1}_{i}(0)=d_{i,0},\quad\frac{d}{dt}d^{k+1}_{i}(0)=d_{i,1}.\end{array}\right.

(6.46)

As a result, a similar proof as the proof of Proposition 5.1 in
[
53
]
, we show the existence of the solution
(\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a},P^{k+1}_{v,v},d^{k+1}_{l},d^{k+1}_{r})
to the linear system (
6.45
)–(
6.46
). The details for the proof are omitted.

Proposition 6.1

Let
\big(\bar{\mathfrak{J}}_{a,0},\bar{\mathfrak{J}}_{a,1},\bar{P}_{v,v,0},d_{i,0},d_{i,1}\big)\in H^{1}(\Gamma_{*})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{*})\times H^{5/2}(\Omega_{*})\times\mathop{\bf R\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}
,
\omega_{i0}\in(0,\pi/2)
and
(\mathfrak{K}^{k}_{a},P^{k}_{v,v},d^{k}_{l},d^{k}_{r})
be given correspondingly. Moreover we assume that the conditions for the corner points from (
6.45
) hold at
t=0
. Then there exists a small
T>0
such that the system (
6.45
)–(
6.46
) has a unique solution on
[0,T]
.

6.5
Uniform estimates

Now we are ready to give the uniform estimates for the linear system (
6.45
)–(
6.46
). To begin with, we define the energy functional for
k\in\mathop{\bf N\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
as below:

E^{k+1}(t)=E^{k+1}_{low}(t)+E^{k+1}_{high}(t)

where
E^{k+1}_{low}(t)
and
E^{k+1}_{high}(t)
are defined by

\begin{split}E^{k+1}_{low}(t)&=a\|(\mathfrak{J}^{k+1})^{\bot}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{k}_{t})}+\|\nabla_{\tau^{k}_{t}}(\mathfrak{J}^{k+1})^{\bot}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{k}_{t})}+\|D_{t}\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{k}_{t})}+\|P^{k+1}_{v,v}\|^{2}_{H^{5/2}(\Omega^{k}_{t})}\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=l,r}\big(|d^{k+1}_{i}(t)|^{2}+\Big|\frac{d}{dt}d^{k+1}_{i}(t)\Big|^{2}\big),\end{split}

and

E_{high}(t)=a\|D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}^{k})^{\bot}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{k}_{t})}+\|\nabla_{\tau^{k}_{t}}D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}^{k+1})^{\bot}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{k}_{t})}+\|D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega^{k}_{t})}.

Moreover, the dissipation
F^{k+1}(t)
is

F^{k+1}(t)=F^{k+1}_{low}(t)+F^{k+1}_{high}(t),

where

F^{k+1}_{low}(t)=\sum_{i=l,r}\big|(\sin\omega_{i}^{k})\nabla_{\tau^{k}_{t}}(\mathfrak{J}^{k+1})^{\perp}|_{p_{i}}\big|^{2},\quad F^{k+1}_{high}(t)=\sum_{i=l,r}\big|(\sin\omega_{i}^{k})\nabla_{\tau^{k}_{t}}D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}^{k+1})^{\perp}|_{p_{i}}\big|^{2}.

Meanwhile, we define some more auxiliary functions. Recalling from (
5.25
), here
{\cal P}^{k}_{t,1}
and
{\cal P}^{k}_{t,2}
are defined by

{\cal P}^{k}_{t,1}=D_{t}P_{\tilde{v}^{k},\tilde{v}^{k}}-v^{k}\cdot(\nabla P_{\tilde{v}^{k},\tilde{v}^{k}}|_{c}),\quad{\cal P}^{k}_{t,2}=D_{t}{\cal P}^{k}_{t,1}-v^{k}\cdot({\cal P}^{k}_{t,1}|_{c}).

The following proposition is our main result on the uniform estimates:

Proposition 6.2

Let
\big(\bar{\mathfrak{J}}_{a,0},\bar{\mathfrak{J}}_{a,1},\bar{P}_{v,v,0},d_{i,0},d_{i,1}\big)
and
(\mathfrak{K}^{k}_{a},P^{k}_{v,v},d^{k}_{l},d^{k}_{r})
be given as in Proposition
6.1
. Then there exists constants
T>0
small enough and
A>0
large enough such that when
a\geq A
, the inequality below holds

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E^{k+1}(t)+\int_{0}^{T}F^{k+1}(t)dt\leq P(E(0)).

Proof.

Since the main steps of the proof follow Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
5.1
, we only present the sketch of the proof here.

First, we consider the basic energy estimates
E^{k+1}_{l}
, where we only focus on the estimates for
\mathfrak{K}_{a}
or
\mathfrak{J}_{a}
and the other estimates follow from Lemma
4.6
and
[
53
]
.

To begin with, acting
(\Phi^{k}_{S_{t}})^{-1}
on both sides of (
6.45
), one has

D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}+\sigma(a-\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}}){\cal N}(\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a})=R^{k}_{a,0},

which implies that

D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}_{a}+\sigma\nabla{\cal H}\big[(a-\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}})(\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}_{a})^{\perp}+h_{\tilde{v}^{k}}\big]=\nabla{\cal H}(R^{k}_{a,0}+\sigma h_{\tilde{v}^{k}})-[\nabla{\cal H},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}.

(6.47)

Using the same arguments as in Section 4 and Section 5, we have

\big\|\nabla{\cal H}(R^{k}_{a,0}+\sigma h_{\tilde{v}^{k}})-[\nabla{\cal H},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t}^{k})}\leq P(E^{k}(t))(1+E^{k+1}_{low}(t)).

Next, we define
P_{J^{k+1},v^{k}}
on
\Omega_{t}^{k}
by system (
3.34
). Taking the
L^{2}(\Omega^{k}_{t})
inner product of (
6.47
) with
D_{t}\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}_{a}+\nabla P_{J^{k+1},v^{k}}
, one has

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{t}^{k}}\Big(D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}_{a}+\nabla{\cal H}\big[(a-\Delta_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}})(\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}_{a})^{\perp}+h_{\tilde{v}^{k}}\big]\Big)\cdot(D_{t}\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}_{a}+\nabla P_{J^{k+1},v^{k}})dX\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{t}^{k}}\big(\nabla{\cal H}(R^{k}_{a,0}+\sigma h_{\tilde{v}^{k}})-[\nabla{\cal H},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}\big)\cdot(D_{t}\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}_{a}+\nabla P_{J^{k+1},v^{k}})dX.\end{split}

Following the energy estimates in Section 4, there exists a constant
A
large enough such that when
a\geq A
, one concludes

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E^{k+1}_{low}(t)+\int_{0}^{T}F^{k+1}_{low}(t)\leq P(E(0))+\int_{0}^{T}P(E^{k}(t)).

On the other hand, for the higher-order energy
E_{h}^{k+1}(t)
, we get similarly as (
5.30
) the equation

D_{t}\big[D^{2}_{t}\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1}-\nabla{\cal H}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})+[\nabla{\cal H},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}\big]+\sigma\nabla{\cal H}\big((a-\Delta_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}})D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1})^{\bot}+D_{t}h_{\tilde{v}^{k}}\big)=R^{k}_{a,1},

where
R^{k}_{a,1}
is given by

\begin{split}R^{k}_{a,1}&=D_{t}\nabla{\cal H}(R^{k}_{a,0}+\sigma h_{\tilde{v}_{k}})-D_{t}\nabla{\cal H}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})+\sigma[\nabla{\cal H},D_{t}]{\cal H}\big((a-\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}})(\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}_{a})^{\perp}+\sigma h_{\tilde{v}^{k}}\big)\\
&\quad-\sigma\nabla{\cal H}[\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}},D_{t}](\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}_{a})^{\perp}.\end{split}

Compared to the energy estimate of Theorem
5.1
, the main difference lies in the term
D_{t}([\nabla{\cal H},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a})
which contains a term like
D_{t}(\partial D_{t}v^{k})\partial{\cal H}(\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a})
. In Theorem
5.1
, since
v
satisfies Euler’s equation, we have the estimate for
D_{t}^{2}\nabla v
. But here in the iteration scheme,
D_{t}^{2}\partial v^{k}
acts like
\partial\partial_{t}^{3}d_{\Gamma_{t}}
, which can not be controlled by the energy. Therefore, we put
D_{t}([\nabla{\cal H},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a})
together with
D^{2}_{t}\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1}
to go back to the form
D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}
:

D^{2}_{t}\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1}+[\nabla{\cal H},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}=\nabla{\cal H}(D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}).

Taking the
L^{2}(\Omega^{k}_{t})
inner product of the above equation with
\nabla{\cal H}(D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a})-\nabla{\cal H}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})
, we derive

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{t}^{k}}D_{t}\big(\nabla{\cal H}(D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a})-\nabla{\cal H}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})\big)\cdot\big(\nabla{\cal H}(D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a})-\nabla{\cal H}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})\big)dX\\
&+\sigma\int_{\Omega_{t}^{k}}\nabla{\cal H}\big((a-\Delta_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}})D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1})^{\bot}-D_{t}h_{\tilde{v}^{k}}\big)\cdot\big(\nabla{\cal H}(D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a})-\nabla{\cal H}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})\big)dX\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{t}^{k}}R^{k}_{a,1}\cdot\big(\nabla{\cal H}(D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a})-\nabla{\cal H}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})\big)dX.\end{split}

For the second integral on the left side of the above equation, we have by Green’s Formula that

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{t}^{k}}\nabla{\cal H}\big((a-\Delta_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}})D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1})^{\bot}+D_{t}h_{\tilde{v}^{k}}\big)\cdot\big(\nabla{\cal H}(D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a})-\nabla{\cal H}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})\big)dX\\
&=\int_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}}\big((a-\Delta_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}})D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1})^{\bot}+D_{t}h_{\tilde{v}^{k}}\big)\cdot{\cal N}(D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}-{\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})ds\\
&=\int_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}}\big((a-\Delta_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}})D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1})^{\bot}+D_{t}h_{\tilde{v}^{k}}\big)\cdot D_{t}^{2}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1})^{\bot}ds\\
&\quad+\int_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}}\big((a-\Delta_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}})D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1})^{\bot}+D_{t}h_{\tilde{v}^{k}}\big)\cdot\big([{\cal N},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}-{\cal N}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})\big)ds,\end{split}

and proceeding in a similar way as before, we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{t}^{k}}\nabla{\cal H}\big((a-\Delta_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}})D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1})^{\bot}+D_{t}h_{\tilde{v}^{k}}\big)\cdot\big(\nabla{\cal H}(D_{t}^{2}\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a})-\nabla{\cal H}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})\big)dX\\
&=\frac{d}{dt}\Big(a\|D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}^{k})^{\bot}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{k}_{t})}+\big\|\nabla_{\tau^{k}_{t}}D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}^{k+1})^{\bot}\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{k}_{t})}\Big)+\nabla_{\tau^{k}_{t}}D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}_{a})^{\perp}\cdot D^{2}_{t}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1})^{\perp}\big|^{p_{r}}_{p_{l}}\\
&\quad+\int_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}}(a-\Delta_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}})D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1})^{\bot}\cdot\big([{\cal N},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}-{\cal N}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})\big)ds\\
&\quad+\int_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}}D_{t}h_{\tilde{v}^{k}}\cdot\big(D_{t}^{2}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1})^{\bot}+[{\cal N},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}-{\cal N}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})\big)ds.\end{split}

On one hand, since the commutator
[D_{t},\,{\cal N}]
is already expressed in (
2.17
),
one can conclude that

\|[{\cal N},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{t}^{k})}\leq P(E^{k+1}(t)).

Consequently, we have

\int_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}}\big((a-\Delta_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}})D_{t}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1})^{\bot}\big)\cdot\big([{\cal N},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}-{\cal N}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})\big)ds\leq\frac{1}{8}F_{h}^{k+1}(t)+P(E^{k+1}(t)).

On the other hand, using similar arguments as in Lemma
5.5
, we get

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}}D_{t}h_{\tilde{v}^{k}}\cdot\big(D_{t}^{2}(\mathfrak{J}_{a}^{k+1})^{\bot}+[{\cal N},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k+1}_{a}-{\cal N}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,2})\big)dsdt\\
&\leq P(E(0))+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(\frac{1}{8}E_{high}^{k+1}(t)+P(E_{low}^{k+1}(t))\big)+\int_{0}^{T}P(E^{k}(t))dt+\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{T}F_{high}^{k+1}(t)dt.\end{split}

In the end, using similar arguments as in Section 5.3, we can show that when
T
small enough, the following estimate holds

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E^{k+1}_{high}(t)+\int_{0}^{T}F^{k+1}_{high}(t)dt\leq P(E(0))+\int_{0}^{T}P(E^{k}(t))dt+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}P(E_{low}^{k+1}(t)).

Therefore, by a bootstrap argument, we can prove the desired result.

\blacksquare

6.6
Cauchy sequence and going back to
(WW)

In this part, we are finally in a position to prove that the sequence of
(\mathfrak{K}^{k}_{a},P^{k}_{v,v},d^{k}_{l},d^{k}_{r})
is indeed a Cauchy sequence. In fact, we know from the previous subsection that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E^{k+1}(t)+\int_{0}^{T}F^{k+1}(t)dt\leq C\quad\hbox{for all}\ k\in\mathbb{N},

(6.48)

where
C>0
is a constant depending on
E(0)
.

To simplify the notation, we denote by

\overline{f}^{k+1}=f^{k+1}\circ\Phi^{k}_{S_{t}},\qquad\delta_{\overline{f}^{k}}=\overline{f}^{k+1}-\overline{f}^{k},

and

(a-\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}})g^{k}=h_{\tilde{v}^{k}}\quad\hbox{with}\quad g^{k}|_{p_{i}}=0.

Using (
6.47
) and rewriting it similarly as (
3.35
), we have the equation of
\delta_{\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}}
:

D_{t*}(D_{t*}\delta_{\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}}-\delta_{\overline{\nabla{\cal H}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,1})}})+\sigma{\cal A}(d_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}})(\delta_{\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}}-\delta_{\overline{g}^{k}})=D_{R^{k}},

(6.49)

where we note

{\cal A}(d_{\Gamma_{t}})f=\big({\cal N}(a-\Delta_{\Gamma_{t}})(f\circ\Phi^{-1}_{S_{t}})\big)\circ\Phi_{S_{t}},

and

\begin{split}D_{R^{k}}&=\big((\partial_{t}+v^{k}_{*}\cdot\nabla)^{2}-(\partial_{t}+v^{k-1}_{*}\cdot\nabla)^{2}\big)\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}+\big({\cal A}(d_{\Gamma^{k}_{t}})-{\cal A}(d_{\Gamma^{k-1}_{t}})\big)(\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}-\overline{g}^{k})+\delta_{\overline{\nabla{\cal H}(R^{k-1}_{a,0}+\sigma h_{\tilde{v}^{k-1}})}}\\
&\quad-\delta_{\overline{[\nabla{\cal H},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k}_{a}}}-D_{t*}\delta_{\overline{\nabla{\cal H}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,1})}}.\end{split}

Besides, similar but simpler equations for
(\delta_{P^{k}_{v,v}},\delta_{d^{k}_{l}},\delta_{d^{k}_{r}})
can be derived, and we omit the details here.

Moreover, we define the energy of the difference according to the definition of
\Sigma
as

\begin{split}E^{k}_{\delta}(t)&=\|\partial_{t}\delta_{\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}}\|_{C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{*}))}+a\|\delta_{\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}}\cdot n_{t*}\|_{C([0,T];L^{2}(\Gamma_{*}))}+\|\delta_{\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}}\cdot n_{t*}\|_{C([0,T];H^{1}(\Gamma_{*}))}\\
&\quad+\|\delta_{P^{k}_{v,v}}\|_{C([0,T];H^{5/2}(\Omega_{*}))}+\sum_{i=l,r}\big(|\delta_{d^{k}_{i}}|_{C([0,T])}+\Big|\delta_{d(d^{k}_{i})/dt}\Big|_{C([0,T])}\big).\end{split}

Before we consider the convergence, we need to deal with
D_{R^{k}}
first.

Lemma 6.1

The right side of (
6.49
) satisfies the following estimate:

\|D_{R^{k}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{*})}\leq CE^{k}_{\delta}(t)

with the positive constant
C
depending on
E(0)
.

Proof.

Here, we only give the outline of the proof, and one can see similar details in
[
53
]
.
First, we consider the estimate for
\big((\partial_{t}+v^{k}_{*}\cdot\nabla)^{2}-(\partial_{t}+v^{k-1}_{*}\cdot\nabla)^{2}\big)\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}
. Using similar arguments as Proposition 4.12 and Corollary 4.13 in
[
53
]
and applying (
6.45
) and (
6.48
), we can have

\begin{split}\big\|\big((\partial_{t}+v^{k}_{*}\cdot\nabla)^{2}-(\partial_{t}+v^{k-1}_{*}\cdot\nabla)^{2}\big)\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{*})}&\leq C\|\partial_{t}^{2}\delta_{d_{\Gamma^{k-1}_{t}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{*})}+C\|\partial_{t}\delta_{d_{\Gamma^{k-1}_{t}}}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{*})}\\
&\leq C\big(a^{-1}\|\delta_{\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}}\cdot n_{*}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{*})}+C\|\partial_{t}\delta_{\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{*})}\big)\leq CE^{k}_{\delta}(t).\end{split}

Second, for the term
({\cal A}(d_{\Gamma_{t}})-{\cal A}(d_{\Gamma^{k-1}_{t}}))(\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}-\overline{g}^{k})
, we notice by (
5.19
) that

\big\|\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}-\overline{g}^{k}\big\|_{H^{5/2}(\Gamma_{*})}\leq C.

As a result, by similar arguments as in the proof of Proposition 6.3
[
53
]
, we have

\big\|\big({\cal A}(d_{\Gamma_{t}})-{\cal A}(d_{\Gamma^{k-1}_{t}})\big)(\overline{\mathfrak{J}}^{k}_{a}-\overline{g}^{k})\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{*})}\leq CE^{k}_{\delta}(t).

In the end, similar arguments as in Lemma 4.6
[
53
]
, we can have

\big\|\delta_{\overline{\nabla{\cal H}(R^{k-1}_{a,0}+\sigma h_{\tilde{v}^{k-1}})}}-\delta_{\overline{[\nabla{\cal H},D_{t}^{2}]\mathfrak{K}^{k}_{a}}}-D_{t*}\delta_{\overline{\nabla{\cal H}({\cal P}^{k}_{t,1})}}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{*})}\leq CE^{k}_{\delta}(t).

Combining all these estimates above, the proof is finished.

\blacksquare

Now, we are able to conclude about the convergence result.

Proposition 6.3

The sequence
(\mathfrak{K}^{k}_{a},P^{k}_{v,v},d^{k}_{l},d^{k}_{r})
is a Cauchy sequence.

Proof.

We follow the steps in Theorem
4.1
and Section 6.3 in
[
53
]
to conclude that there exists a constant
T
small enough and
A
large enough such that when
a\geq A
, we have

E^{k}_{\delta}(t)\leq C\int_{0}^{T}E^{k}_{\delta}(t)dt.

As a result, this implies immediately that
(\mathfrak{K}^{k}_{a},P^{k}_{v,v},d^{k}_{l},d^{k}_{r})
is convergent.

\blacksquare

We are finally in a position to finish the proof for Theorem
6.1
.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.
We only present the sketch for the proof here. In fact, since we have proved in Proposition
6.3
that
(\mathfrak{K}^{k}_{a},P^{k}_{v,v},d^{k}_{l},d^{k}_{r})\in\Sigma
is a Cauchy sequence, we know immediately that there exists
(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})\in\Sigma
satisfying

(\mathfrak{K}^{k}_{a},P^{k}_{v,v},d^{k}_{l},d^{k}_{r})\to(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})\qquad\hbox{in}\quad\Sigma.

As a result, one can show in a standard way that
(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})
satisfies system (
6.44
). Moreover, one also has
(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})\in\Sigma_{h}
in the proof of Proposition
6.3
.

\blacksquare

In the end, we go back to our water-waves system
(WW)
. In fact, thanks to system (
6.44
) for
(\mathfrak{K}_{a},P_{v,v},d_{l},d_{r})\in\Sigma
, we derive the mean curvature
\kappa
, which defines the free surface. Based on the knowledge of
\Gamma_{t}
, we also obtain
v
by Section 6.2.
Therefore, using similar arguments as in Section 6.4
[
53
]
and thanks to discussions in Section 6.3, we can finally retrieve the solution
(v,P)
to the water-waves system
(WW)
.

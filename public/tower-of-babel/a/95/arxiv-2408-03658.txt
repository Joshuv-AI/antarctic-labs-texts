Abstract
Formal languages over infinite alphabets serve as abstractions of structures and processes carrying data. Automata models over infinite alphabets, such as classical register automata or, equivalently, nominal orbit-finite automata, tend to have computationally hard or even undecidable reasoning problems unless stringent restrictions are imposed on either the power of control or the number of registers. This has been shown to be ameliorated in automata models with name allocation such as regular nondeterministic nominal automata, which allow for deciding language inclusion in elementary complexity even with unboundedly many registers while retaining a reasonable level of expressiveness. In the present work, we demonstrate that elementary complexity survives under extending the power of control to alternation : We introduce regular alternating nominal automata (RANAs) , and show that their non-emptiness and inclusion problems have elementary complexity even when the number of registers is unbounded. Moreover, we show that RANAs allow for nearly complete de-alternation, specifically de-alternation up to a single deadlocked universal state. As a corollary to our results, we improve the complexity of model checking for a flavour of Bar- μ \mu TL , a fixed-point logic with name allocation over finite data words, by one exponential level.



I
Introduction

Automata models over infinite alphabets, such as the classical register
automata
[
19
]
, or automata over nominal sets
[
31
]
such as
nondeterministic orbit-finite automata (NOFA)
[
3
]
, have received long-standing
interest. Infinite alphabets may be understood as representing data, for example, nonces in
cryptographic protocols
[
22
]
, data values in XML documents
[
30
]
, object
identities
[
14
]
, or parameters of method calls
[
17
]
. Generally, a
challenge posed by the use of infinite alphabets is that decision problems such as emptiness or
inclusion checking are often either undecidable or computationally very hard, even under severe
restrictions (see the related work section). Schröder et al.
[
33
]
have introduced

regular nondeterministic nominal automata
(RNNA), which on one hand extend NOFAs by name
allocating transitions but on the other hand impose finite (rather than orbit-finite)
branching; name-allocating transitions may be thought of as storing letters read from the input
in a register. Despite that restriction, RNNAs retain a reasonable level of expressivity. In
particular, they feature unrestricted nondeterminism and hence can express languages that are
not acceptable by deterministic or unambiguous register automata such as ‘some letter occurs
twice’; moreover, they allow an unbounded number of registers, and as such accept languages
that are not acceptable by, for instance, alternating 1-register
automata
[
33
]
. Nevertheless, inclusion checking of RNNAs is in parametrized
PSpace
(the parameter being the number of registers), in sharp contrast to the computational hardness
of other non-deterministic register automaton models.

In the present work, we move from nondeterminism to fully fledged
alternation
; as in the
classical setting, this allows for a more succinct presentation of automata. We introduce

regular alternating nominal automata
(RANA), a natural common generalization of
classical alternating finite automata
[
6
]
and RNNAs, and we demonstrate that the
pleasant algorithmic properties of RNNAs are retained when moving to RANAs. In
infinite-alphabet automata models, expressiveness typically increases strictly with the power
of control (e.g. extending deterministic to nondeterministic and further to alternating
computation), and indeed RANAs are strictly more expressive than RNNAs. Remarkably, however, we
show (
Theorem

VI.6
) that RANAs can be de-alternated to so-called
extended RNNAs
,
with (only) doubly exponential blowup. Extended RNNAs, introduced in work on nominal temporal
logics
[
15
]
, extend the nondeterministic RNNA model only by
\top
-states,
i.e. deadlocked universal states. No similar result appears to be known for alternating
register automata. Furthermore, we show that full de-alternation of RANAs to RNNAs is possible
under a specific form of data language semantics. As a corollary, under this semantics RANAs
are expressively equivalent to a subclass of non-guessing NOFAs
[
3
]
and
register automata with nondeterministic reassignment
[
3
,
20
]
.

In addition, we show that emptiness and inclusion checking (relevant for
model checking) of RANAs
are decidable in elementary complexity. More precisely, RANAs, like
RNNAs, support two different forms of freshness semantics for letters
read from the input word:
global freshness
(as found, for
instance, in session automata
[
5
]
) requires letters to
be distinct from all previous letters in the input, while
local
freshness
requires distinctness from letters currently stored in
the registers (as in register automata). Under global freshness,
emptiness and inclusion of RANAs are decidable in exponential space, and in
fact in parametrized polynomial space with the number of registers as
the parameter
(
Theorems

VII.6
and
VII.7
),
while under local freshness, these problems are decidable in doubly
exponential (and in parametrized singly exponential),
space. Notably, these results hold without any bound on the number of
registers or restrictions on alternation.

Both de-alternation and our decidability results hinge on
the notion of name dropping
[
33
]
, which we adapt to RANAs:
Every RANA has an equivalent
name-dropping
modification
(
Theorem

V.4
) in which all transitions may
nondeterministically lose any number of names from the support of states
(analogous to losing register contents in a register automaton).

Our results impact on model checking in the finite-word linear-time nominal
fixed-point logic
Bar-
\mu
TL

[
15
]
: While the original algorithm for model checking
Bar-
\mu
TL
over RNNAs was based on a direct translation from
Bar-
\mu
TL
to extended
RNNAs, we factor this translation through a simple translation from

Bar-
\mu
TL
to RANAs. For local freshness, our method then proceeds by
de-alternating the obtained RANA, yielding the same complexity as
before. Contrastingly, model checking under global freshness can be
performed directly by inclusion checking of RANAs, and hence is in
parametrized
PSpace
by our above-mentioned results; this improves on the
original bound
[
15
]
by one exponential level.
The translation above also retains the complexity of checking satisfiability
of
Bar-
\mu
TL
for both local and global freshness.

In summary, our contributions are the following:

•

We introduce regular alternating nominal automata (RANA), an
alternating automata model for bar languages (
Section

III
) that
allows unrestricted alternation and unboundedly many registers.

•

We show their correspondence to the linear-time nominal
temporal logic
Bar-
\mu
TL

[

]
(
Section

),
improving the complexity of model checking under global freshness.

•

We prove that RANAs can be de-alternated to extended RNNAs (

), which are
purely nondeterministic except for a single deadlocked universal

•

We show that checking non-emptiness and inclusion of RANAs are decidable in

Related Work

The expressive power of automata models over infinite alphabets
generally increases with the power of control
(deterministic/nondeterministic/alternating)
[
21
]
. In
deterministic models, language inclusion can often be decided in

automata

. For nondeterministic

]

). Similarly, language inclusion

. Safety

. Automata models for infinite alphabets outside

8

, as well as the highly

[
2
]
, whose nonemptiness
problem is decidable but, again, at least as hard as Petri net

[
9
]
, namely
Ackermann-complete
[
25
,
24
,
10

.

As mentioned above, our results relate to work on the

\mu

[

hand, both the central positivization construction
(

III.20
) and our algorithmic treatment of inclusion
checking by reduction to alternating finite automata are new.

Nominal Sets

The theory of nominal sets
[
31
]
offers a convenient framework for dealing with names, and related concepts such as binding and freshness. For our purpose, names play the role of data.
In the following, we briefly recall some terminology and basic facts.

For the rest of the paper, we fix a countably infinite set

The group

Recall that

swaps
a
and

A

denoted by

for every

This implies that

Informally, we think of a nominal set

An element

Remark III.8

Remark III.11

The above definitions involve a few subtleties that are worth pointing out:

(1)

and

(4)

w\smash{{}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\vphantom{$\textstyle\models$}}}\vbox{\halign{\hfil\kern 1pt$\tsstyle#\vphantom{by}$\hfil\cr.0pt$\scriptstyle?\cr\kern 1.0pt\hrule\kern 2.5pt2\hrule\kern 1.0pt\cr.0pt$\scriptstyle\cr}}}{}}\varphi_{i}

The evaluation dag keeps track of the dependency of evaluations: A path from

Although we do not currently phrase acceptance in
game-theoretic terms, the evaluation dag is effectively the
arena in which an acceptance game would be played.

Definition III.15

(Escape letter)
.

Let
A=(Q,\delta,q_{0})
be an explicit-dual RANA. Given
a bar string
w\in{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}^{\star}
and a
formula
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathsf{d}}(1+\mathds{A}\times Q+[\mathds{A}]Q)
,
a free name

is an

w
at
\varphi
if from the node

w\smash{{}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\vphantom{$\textstyle\models$}}}\vbox{\halign{\hfil\kern 1pt$\tsstyle#\vphantom{by}$\hfil\cr.0pt$\scriptstyle?\cr\kern 1.0pt\hrule\kern 2.5pt2\hrule\kern 1.0pt\cr.0pt$\scriptstyle\cr}}}{}}\varphi
in the evaluation dag,
a leaf of the form
av\smash{{}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\vphantom{$\textstyle\models$}}}\vbox{\halign{\hfil\kern 1pt$\tsstyle#\vphantom{by}$\hfil\cr.0pt$\scriptstyle?\cr\kern 1.0pt\hrule\kern 2.5pt2\hrule\kern 1.0pt\cr.0pt$\scriptstyle\cr}}}{}}\Box_{\alpha}q^{\prime}
(in
which case
a\neq\alpha
) or
av\smash{{}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\vphantom{$\textstyle\models$}}}\vbox{\halign{\hfil\kern 1pt$\tsstyle#\vphantom{by}$\hfil\cr.0pt$\scriptstyle?\cr\kern 1.0pt\hrule\kern 2.5pt2\hrule\kern 1.0pt\cr.0pt$\scriptstyle\cr}}}{}}\neg\varepsilon

is an

at a state

if it is one at
\delta(q)
.

Intuitively, an escape letter is a letter

at which the processing of the input word ends
immediately: when checking whether
av\models\Box_{\alpha}q^{\prime}
with
a\neq\alpha
holds for a
suffix
av
of the input,
\Box_{\alpha}q^{\prime}
is vacuously satisfied because the next letter
a

to be read is distinct from
\alpha
.
Similarly,
\neg\varepsilon
is vacuously satisfied for a suffix
av
of the input.
We will later use these escape letters to ‘replace’ box modalities by a disjunction
of diamond modalities under preservation of accepted words. This also explains why these
escape letters do not include those arising from diamond modalities, that are those letters

a
for which a leaf of the form
av\smash{{}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\vphantom{$\textstyle\models$}}}\vbox{\halign{\hfil\kern 1pt$\tsstyle#\vphantom{by}$\hfil\cr.0pt$\scriptstyle?\cr\kern 1.0pt\hrule\kern 2.5pt2\hrule\kern 1.0pt\cr.0pt$\scriptstyle\cr}}}{}}\Diamond_{\alpha}q^{\prime}
is reached.

Lemma III.16
.

Let
A=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q{,\>}\delta{,\>}q_{0}}}\right)
be an explicit-dual RANA.
Then the set of escape letters for

at
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathsf{d}}(1+\mathds{A}\times Q+[\mathds{A}]Q)
is contained in
\supp(\varphi)\cup\{a\}

for some
a\in\mathds{A}
.

Proof:

Suppose that there is at least one escape letter for
w
at
\varphi
(otherwise the statement is trivial). Let
a
be the escape letter that occurs last in
w
. Fix a path
from
w\smash{{}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\vphantom{$\textstyle\models$}}}\vbox{\halign{\hfil\kern 1pt$\tsstyle#\vphantom{by}$\hfil\cr.0pt$\scriptstyle?\cr\kern 1.0pt\hrule\kern 2.5pt2\hrule\kern 1.0pt\cr.0pt$\scriptstyle\cr}}}{}}\varphi
to a leaf witnessing

as an escape letter. Then for every escape letter

, the path passes a non-leaf node

bv\smash{{}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\vrule\vphantom{$\textstyle\models$}}}\vbox{\halign{\hfil\kern 1pt$\tsstyle#\vphantom{by}$\hfil\cr.0pt$\scriptstyle?\cr\kern 1.0pt\hrule\kern 2.5pt2\hrule\kern 1.0pt\cr.0pt$\scriptstyle\cr}}}{}}\heartsuit_{b}q^{\prime}

. Since

is free in

.
∎

Example III.17
.

Note, that the additional name
a
in the bound for escape letters
(

) depends on the bar string

as seen in the
following small example. Take the formula

in some

Indeed, every escape letter must occur at some position in

We are prepared to prove that the three types of RANAs are expressively equivalent:

Theorem III.18
.

Ordinary,
positive, and explicit-dual RANA are equivalent under
literal language semantics, hence also under bar language, global and local
freshness semantics.

Since every positive RANA is a RANA, it remains to show that every RANA can be transformed into an equivalent explicit-dual RANA, and every explicit-dual RANA can be transformed into an equivalent positive RANA. The first transformation is straightforward:

Proposition III.19
.

For every RANA
A
, there exists an explicit-dual RANA
{A}^{\mathsf{d}}
that accepts the same
literal language, has twice as many orbits and the same degree as
A
.

Proof:

The explicit-dual RANA
{A}^{\mathsf{d}}
has two states
q
,
q_{\mathsf{n}}

for each state
q
of
A
. The state
q
accepts the same literal
language as in
A
, while
q_{\mathsf{n}}
accepts the complement of
this language. The transition formulae of
{A}^{\mathsf{d}}
emerge
from those of
A
by familiar dualities, e.g. replacing

\neg\Diamond_{\alpha}q
with
\Box_{\alpha}q_{\mathsf{n}}
.
∎

The second transformation is more involved.
Using escape letters, we can restrict the number of ways a box modality can be
satisfied: For instance, a bar string can satisfy
\Box_{a}q
either by starting
with
a
and continuing with a word accepted by
q
(thus satisfying

\Diamond_{a}q
), by being empty (thus satisfying
\varepsilon
) or by
starting with a letter
\beta\in{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}
distinct from
a
(thus
satisfying
\Diamond_{\beta}q_{\top}
where
q_{\top}
accepts every word).
The bound on escape letters (
Lemma

III.16
) allows us to keep this
implicit disjunction finite.

Theorem III.20
.

For every explicit-dual RANA
A
of degree
k
and with
n
orbits, there exists a positive RANA
{A}^{+}
that accepts the same literal language, has degree
2k+1
, and at most
n\cdot(k+2)\cdot(2k+1)^{2k+1}+1

Proof:

Let
A=(Q,\delta,q_{0})
. The positive RANA
{A}^{+}
has pairs
(q,S)
as states, where
q\in Q
and
S\subseteq\mathds{A}
is the current set of escape letters. There are only orbit-finitely many states since the number of escape letters for any
bar string at any state

is bounded by the size of the support of that state with one additional
letter (
Lemma

III.16
). Therefore, we only need to store at most
k+1
letters
simultaneously. Additionally,
{A}^{+}
has a single equivariant state
q_{\top}

with transition function
{\delta}^{+}(q_{\top})=\top
which is the ‘totally
accepting’ state. Given a state
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(q{,\>}S}}\right)
in
{A}^{+}
,
the transition formula is defined by modification of
\delta(q)
in
A
. We sketch the
different possibilities: Every box modal atom
\Box_{a}q^{\prime}
is replaced by the disjunction

\varepsilon\vee\Diamond_{a}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(q^{\prime}{,\>}S}}\right)\vee\bigvee_{\sigma\in S\setminus\{a\}}\Diamond_{\sigma}q_{\top}\vee\Diamond_{{\hskip 0.60275pt{\rule[-0.3014pt]{0.6pt}{3.31529pt}}\hskip 0.60275pt}a}q_{\top}
, whereas diamond modalities
for plain names are not changed. Whenever a bar modal atom
\Diamond_{{\hskip 0.60275pt{\rule[-0.3014pt]{0.6pt}{3.31529pt}}\hskip 0.60275pt}a}
or

\Box_{{\hskip 0.60275pt{\rule[-0.3014pt]{0.6pt}{3.31529pt}}\hskip 0.60275pt}a}

to the currently stored set

of escape letters, while possibly
removing previously stored names to ensure adherence to the bound

This nondeterminism ensures that any set of escape letters can be reached. Moreover, when encountering
\Box_{{\hskip 0.60275pt{\rule[-0.3014pt]{0.6pt}{3.31529pt}}\hskip 0.60275pt}a}

\varepsilon

and
\bigvee_{\sigma\in S}\Diamond_{\sigma}q_{\top}
as disjuncts to the newly generated
formula. It is
then not difficult to verify that a state

in

accepts a bar string

iff
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(q{,\>}S_{q,w}}}\right)

does in
{A}^{+}

is the set of escape letters for

at

. Thus, setting the initial state to
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(q_{0}{,\>}\emptyset}}\right)
(and observing that closed bar strings have no escape letters), we see that

and

accept the same literal languages.
∎

An immediate consequence of the above equivalence is:

Theorem III.21

(Closure properties)
.

Bar and literal
languages acceptable by (positive/ explicit-dual) RANAs are closed
under intersection, union, and complement. More precisely, given RANAs
A_{i}
with

orbits and degree

(

orbits and degree

(L_{0}(A_{1}))^{\complement}
.

IV
Correspondence between RANAs and
Bar-
\mu
TL

Applications for alternating automata have been found with constructing equivalent
automata for logical formulae like with LTL and alternating Büchi automata
[
29
,
36
]
, enabling usage of automata theoretic methods
for logical problems.
Our motivation for introducing alternation to nominal automata with name allocation is to
make translations between a nominal fixed-point logic with name allocation (namely
Bar-
\mu
TL

[
15
]
)
and such nominal automata more succinct. We show that for every
Bar-
\mu
TL
formula, an equivalent
RANA can be constructed with a number of orbits linear in the formula size.

We recap the syntax and semantics of
Bar-
\mu
TL
from Hausmann et al.
[
15
]
:

Definition IV.1
.

We fix a countably infinite set
\mathsf{V}
of fixed-point variables. The set
\mathsf{Bar}
of bar formulae
is generated by the grammar
\varphi,\psi::=\varepsilon\,|\,\neg\varepsilon\,|\,\varphi\vee\psi\,|\,\varphi\wedge\psi\,|\,\heartsuit_{\sigma}\varphi\,|\,X\,|\,\mu X.\varphi
where

\heartsuit\in\{\Diamond,\Box\}
,
\sigma\in{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}
and
X\in\mathsf{V}
. We put

\top:=\varepsilon\vee\neg\varepsilon
and
\bot:=\varepsilon\wedge\neg\varepsilon
and
refer to
\Diamond_{\sigma}
and
\Box_{\sigma}
as
\sigma
-modalities
.

A name
a
is
free
in a formula
\varphi
if
\varphi
contains an
a
-modality at a position
not in the scope of any
{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a
-modality; that is,
{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a
-modalities bind the name
a
.
We write
\mathsf{FN}(\varphi)
for the set of
free names
in
\varphi
and
\mathsf{BN}(\varphi)
for
the set of
bound names
in
\varphi
, i.e. names
a
such that
\varphi
contains a
{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a
-modality.
The
degree
of a formula
\varphi
is the number of its free and bound names:
\deg(\varphi)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathsf{FN}(\varphi)\cup\mathsf{BN}(\varphi)}}\right|
.
Additionally, we require that all fixed points
\mu X.\varphi
are
guarded
, that is, all free occurrences of
X
lie within the scope of some
\sigma
-modality in
\varphi
.
A bar formula is
closed
, if every free occurrence of a fixed-point variable
X
lies within the
scope of a fixed-point expression
\mu X.\psi
. We denote by
\varphi[\nicefrac{{\psi}}{{X}}]
the
formula obtained from
\varphi
by replacing every free occurrence of the fixed-point variable
X
in
\varphi
with
\psi

in the usual manner (capture-avoiding).

Formulae are evaluated over
finite
bar strings. This means that least and greatest fixed points coincide; therefore the syntax only features least fixed points.

\mathsf{Bar}
can be regarded as a nominal set by ‘annotating’ every fixed-point variable
X

(with enclosing fixed-point expression
\mu X.\,\varphi
) with the set
A=\mathsf{FN}(\mu X.\varphi)
of free names
in the enclosing expression. The
\mathsf{Perm}(\mathds{A})
-action then replaces names in the usual manner:

\pi\cdot\varphi
is obtained from
\varphi
by replacing plain names
a\in\mathds{A}
with

\pi(a)
, bar names
{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a\in{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}
with
{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}\pi(a)
and annotations

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X{,\>}A}}\right)
with
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X{,\>}\pi\cdot A}}\right)
. Additionally, we assume all formulae
to be
clean
, that is, fixed-point variables are bound at most once.

Definition IV.2
.

Bar formulae
\varphi
are interpreted as bar languages. We define
satisfaction
w\models\varphi
of a closed formula
\varphi
by a bar string
w\in{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}^{\star}
recursively
by the usual clauses for the Boolean connectives, and

\displaystyle w\models\varepsilon

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle w=\varepsilon;

\displaystyle w\models\neg\varepsilon

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle w\neq\varepsilon;

\displaystyle w\models\mu X.\varphi

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle w\models\varphi[\nicefrac{{\mu X.\varphi}}{{X}}];

\displaystyle w\models\Diamond_{a}\varphi

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\exists v\in{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}^{\star}.\ w=av\text{ and }v\models\varphi;

\displaystyle w\models\Diamond_{{\hskip 0.60275pt{\rule[-0.3014pt]{0.6pt}{3.31529pt}}\hskip 0.60275pt}a}\varphi

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\begin{IEEEeqnarraybox}[]{[}][t][0.4]{l}\exists v,v^{\prime}\in{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}^{\star},b,c\in\mathds{A},\psi\in\mathsf{Bar}.\\

\displaystyle\forall v\in{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}^{\star}.\ w=av\implies v\models\varphi;

\displaystyle w\models\Box_{{\hskip 0.60275pt{\rule[-0.3014pt]{0.6pt}{3.31529pt}}\hskip 0.60275pt}a}\varphi

w\alphaequiv{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}cv^{\prime},\ \langle a\rangle\varphi=\langle c\rangle\psi\text{ and }\\
v^{\prime}\models\psi\big).\end{IEEEeqnarraybox}

Given a closed formula
\varphi
, its

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{0}
consists of
all closed bar strings

such that

, and

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket=\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{0}/{\alphaequiv}
.

Definition IV.5

\displaystyle\psi\ast\chi\in\operatorname{\mathsf{cl}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\text{${}\implies\psi,\chi\in\operatorname{\mathsf{cl}}(\varphi)$ for $\ast\in\{\vee,\wedge\}$};

\displaystyle{}\implies\psi\in\operatorname{\mathsf{cl}}(\varphi)\ \text{for $\heartsuit\in\{\Diamond,\Box\}$ and $\sigma\in{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}$};

V
Name-Dropping Modification and Finite Automata

We have seen in
Remark

III.11
that the possibility
of
\alpha
-renaming bar strings when encountering bar modalities is necessary for

\alpha
-invariance of negation.
In the following, we show that every bar language accepted by a positive RANA is also
accepted by a
name-dropping
positive RANA that does not need any implicit renamings.
Additionally, we show that such name-dropping RANAs admit a finite representation in terms
of
bar alternating finite automata
, which are subsequently employed for our decidability
results in
Section

VII
.

Name-Dropping Modification

We recall that implicit renamings of bar strings
are needed whenever the renaming of a modal atom is ‘blocked’, i.e. has no fitting representative.
To mitigate this, we allow the automaton to forget specific names in the support of a state. This is inspired by a similar construction by Urbat et
al.
[
35
]
for nominal Büchi automata with name allocation. We will use the key fact that every RANA is equivalent to one with a strong
nominal state set.
A nominal set
X
is
strong

[
34
]
if, for all
x\in X

and
\pi\in\mathsf{Perm}(\mathds{A})
, one has
\pi\cdot x=x
if and only if
\pi

fixes every element of
\supp(x)
. (The ‘if’ direction holds in every
nominal set.) For instance,
\mathds{A}^{\#n}
,
\mathds{A}^{n}
and

\mathds{A}^{\!\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scriptsize$\star$}}
are strong nominal sets. Up to isomorphism, orbit-finite strong nominal
sets are precisely coproducts
\coprod_{i=1}^{n}\mathds{A}^{\#n_{i}}

where
n_{i}\in\mathds{N}
and
n
is the number of orbits of
the nominal set; see e.g.
[
27
, Cor. B.27]
.

Theorem V.1
.

For every RANA with
n
orbits and degree
k
, there exists a RANA with
n
orbits and degree
k

that accepts the same literal language and has a strong nominal state set.

A RANA with states
Q=\coprod_{i=1}^{n}\mathds{A}^{\#n_{i}}
can be interpreted as a register automaton with a finite set
C=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\}
of control states where
c_{i}
is equipped with
n_{i}
registers.
In
Construction

V.3
below, we will modify such an automaton, preserving the accepted literal language, to become lossy in the sense that after each transition some of the
register contents may nondeterministically be erased. Our construction uses the following nominal sets:

Definition V.2
.

For

, we write
\mathbf{n}=\{0,\dots,n-1\}
. We denote by

\mathds{A}^{\$\mathbf{n}}
the nominal set of all

from

to
\mathds{A}

The idea of the name-dropping construction is to use partial injective maps (representing the contents of possibly empty registers) as states, which allow to nondeterministically erase stored names.
For transitions, we will replace every occurring modal atom with a disjunction of modal atoms, each of which corresponds to a possible erasure. The details are as follows:

, which implies that, with a classical definition of acceptance, we can only have a bounded number of different names occuring before a
\top
-state is reached.
The above RANA accepts (amongst others) all bar strings of the form

w={\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}aa({\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}b_{i}b_{i})_{i=1}^{m}{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}c{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}dcab_{1}\cdots b_{m}

for any
m
, where all letters are distinct. It therefore accepts strings with an unbounded number of names. However, the prefix of
w
that is processed before reaching the
\top
-state is given by
v={\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}aa({\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}b_{i}b_{i})_{i=1}^{m}{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}c{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}dc
,
which is
\alpha
-equivalent to a bar string with only two
distinct
letters. The above
definition of accepted language via pre-words addresses this
situation by taking all bar strings
v
which are
\alpha
-equivalent to pre-words of words accepted by
\top
in the pre-language of a bar AFA, and then postcomposing
v
with any other bar string to obtain the literal language.
Note, that this RANA is also an ERNNA (as no conjunction occurs in any transition formula). We remark that a
similar problem arose in the definition of extended bar NFAs
[
15
]
, which can be resolved in a similar manner.

We now show how to turn a RANA into an bar language equivalent bar AFA. We consider (w.l.o.g.) name-dropping RANAs, which
allows us work with restricted satisfaction (
Proposition

V.7
), thus avoid implicit
\alpha
-renamings. This simplifies the construction and its correctness proof.

Construction V.10

(RANA to bar AFA)
.

Given the name-dropping modification
A_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
of a RANA, where
A_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}

has degree
k
and
n
orbits, we construct a bar AFA
A_{0}
as follows. If
\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q_{0})\equiv\top
, put
A_{0}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\{q_{0}\}{,\>}\{{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}\ast\}{,\>}\delta_{0}{,\>}q_{0}{,\>}\{q_{0}\}}}\right)

with
\delta_{0}(q_{0},{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}\ast)=\top
. If
\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q_{0})\not\equiv\top
, put
A_{0}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q_{0}{,\>}{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}_{0}\cup\{{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}\ast\}{,\>}\delta_{0}{,\>}q_{0}{,\>}F_{0}}}\right)
with the following data:

(1)

Let
\mathds{A}_{0}
be a fixed
k
-element subset of
\mathds{A}
,
\ast\in\mathds{A}\setminus\mathds{A}_{0}
and
put
{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}_{0}:=\mathds{A}_{0}\cup\{{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a\,:\,a\in\mathds{A}_{0}\}
.

(2)

Q_{0}:=\{q\in Q_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}\,:\,\supp(q)\subseteq\mathds{A}_{0}\,\wedge\,\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q)\not\equiv\top\}
.

(3)

F_{0}:=\{q\in Q_{0}\,:\,\varepsilon\models\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q)\}
.

(4)

Define the function

f\colon({\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}_{0}\cup\{{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}\ast\})\times\mathcal{B}_{+}(1+\mathds{A}\times Q_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}+[\mathds{A}]Q_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}})\to\mathcal{B}_{+}(Q_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}})

recursively by
{f(\alpha,\varepsilon)=\bot}
,
f(\alpha,\top)=\top
,

f(\alpha,\bot)=\bot
,
f(\alpha,\Diamond_{\beta}q)=z
, where
z
is given as follows

\begin{array}[]{@{}c|l@{}}z&\text{if \ldots}\\
\hline\cr\bot&\text{$\Diamond_{\beta}q\neq\Diamond_{\alpha}q^{\prime}$ for all $q^{\prime}$,}\\
\bot&\text{$\alpha={\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}\ast$, $\Diamond_{\beta}q=\Diamond_{\alpha}q^{\prime}$
for some $q^{\prime}$ s.th.~$\ast\in\supp(q^{\prime})$,}\\
\top&\text{$\Diamond_{\beta}q=\Diamond_{\alpha}q^{\prime}$ for some $q^{\prime}$ and $\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q^{\prime})\equiv\top$,}\\
q^{\prime}&\text{$\Diamond_{\beta}q=\Diamond_{\alpha}q^{\prime}$ for some $q^{\prime}$ and $\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q^{\prime})\not\equiv\top$;}\end{array}

and
f(\alpha,\varphi\ast\psi)=f(\alpha,\varphi)\ast f(\alpha,\psi)

for
\ast\in\{\vee,\wedge\}
.

(5)

Define the transition function
\delta_{0}
by
\delta_{0}(q,\alpha)=f(\alpha,\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q))
for
q\in Q_{0}
and
\alpha\in{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}_{0}\cup\{{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}\ast\}
. Note that
every variable
q^{\prime}
in
f(\alpha,\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q))
is an element of
Q_{0}
because of the support principle
(
Lemma

III.13
).

The key observation towards the correctness of the construction is that every bar string accepted by
A_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
has an accepted prefix that is
\alpha
-equivalent to a bar string over
{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}_{0}\cup\{{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}\ast\}
. The additional bar name ‘
{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}\ast
’ is needed to capture bar strings such as
{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}aa_{1}\cdots a_{k}
where
{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a
is not matched by an occurrence of
a
.

Remark V.11
.

(1)

Note that every state contained in the formula
f(\alpha,\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q))
lies in
Q_{0}
due to the support principle (
Lemma

III.13
).

(2)

For a name-dropping RANA
A_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
with degree
k
and
n
orbits, the bar AFA
A_{0}
has an alphabet of size
2\cdot k+1
and at most
n\cdot k!
states: each orbit has support size
\ell\leqslant k
and contributes as many elements to
Q_{0}
as there are injections
from the ordinal set
\ell
to
\mathds{A}_{0}
, at most the number of injections from
k
to
\mathds{A}_{0}
, which is
k!
. Thus,
|Q_{0}|\leqslant n\cdot k^{k}=n\cdot 2^{k\cdot\log k}
.

(3)

We remark about the exclusion of ‘
\top
-states’, i.e. states
q\in Q
where

\supp(q)\subseteq\mathds{A}_{0}
but
\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q)\equiv\top
. The proposed automata model does not allow
a single transition formula for the complete state, but only a transition formula per state and
input letter. In the worst case, as the counterexample from
Remark

V.9
shows,
at a
\top
-state no additional letter can be read without increasing the alphabet simultaneously.
To counteract this, we replace any such occurrences of
\top
-states with
\top

at the respective positions in the transition formula. This is also the reason for the separate
treatment when
\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q_{0})\equiv\top
.

Theorem V.12
.

For
A_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
and
A_{0}
as in
Construction

V.10
, we have
L_{\alpha}(A_{0})=L_{\alpha}(A_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}})
.

Remark V.13
.

Conversely, every bar AFA can be transformed into an equivalent name-dropping RANA (see
Appendix

C
). Hence, RANAs and bar AFAs are expressively equivalent.

VI
De-Alternation of RANAs

We next present our first main result: The de-alternation of RANAs to
ERNNAs, that is, RANAs whose transition formulae only use the Boolean connectives
\bot
,
\top
,
\vee
(
Definition

III.2
).
Without loss of generality, we will work under the following
assumptions.

Assumption VI.1
.

We fix a positive RANA
A
with a strong nominal state set and continue to denote its name-dropping modification by
A_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
(
Construction

V.3
).
In addition, we assume that every transition formula
\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q)
for
q\in Q_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
is in reduced DNF (
Remark

III.10
) and that all disjuncts are
modal atoms of the same kind, that is, of the form
\varepsilon
or
\bigwedge_{j}\Diamond_{\alpha}q_{j}
for the same
\alpha
.

Our de-alternation procedure uses a powerset construction to keep track of all states that the original automaton currently can be in,
just like for the de-alternation of classical alternating finite automata (AFA)
[
12
]
.
However, in order to preserve orbit-finiteness, our construction needs to restrict the number of states that are tracked simultaneously (see
Remark

VI.8
below).
For this, we note that if we fix a single bar string to be read, and if two states
q,q^{\prime}\in Q_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
from the same orbit but with different
supports can be reached by reading a prefix of the bar string, then one of the states can be support-restricted to
\supp(q)\cap\supp(q^{\prime})
.

To formalize this idea, we need to introduce some notation. For
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(i{,\>}r}}\right)\in Q_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
and
J\subseteq\mathds{A}
, put

{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(i{,\>}r}}\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}}}\right|{}^{J}}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(i{,\>}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\kern-1.2ptr\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}}}\right|{}^{J}}}}\right)
(
Definition

V.2
).
Given a finite set
\Phi
of finite subsets of
Q_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
, the set

\operatorname{\mathsf{rest}}(\Phi)
to which
\Phi

restricts
is obtained by
repeatedly replacing
S\in\Phi
with all the sets

S\setminus\{q\}\cup\{{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\kern-1.2ptq\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}}}\right|{}^{J}}\}
and

S\setminus\{q^{\prime}\}\cup\{{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\kern-1.2ptq^{\prime}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}}}\right|{}^{J}}\}
, where
q,q^{\prime}\in S
is
a pair of states that lies in the same orbit of
Q_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
with

\supp(q)\neq\supp(q^{\prime})
, and
J=\supp(q)\cap\supp(q^{\prime})
. (A similar
support restriction technique has been applied to nominal fixed-point logics
[
15
]
.)

Lemma VI.2
.

The restriction process defined above terminates after finitely many steps.

Note that, by definition, no
S\in\operatorname{\mathsf{rest}}(\Phi)
contains two states
q,q^{\prime}
that lie in same orbit of
Q_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
and satisfy
\supp(q)\neq\supp(q^{\prime})
.
This implies that each
S\in\operatorname{\mathsf{rest}}(\Phi)
contains at most
n\cdot k!
elements, where
n
is the number of orbits of
A
and
k
its degree.

Lemma VI.3
.

Let
S\subseteq Q_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
be a finite set of states. Then a bar string
w\in{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}^{\star}

is accepted by
all

q\in S
iff
w
is accepted by
all

q\in S^{\prime}

for some
S^{\prime}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{rest}}(\{S\})
.

Proof:

One only needs to show that two states
q
and
q^{\prime}
accept a bar string iff
{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\kern-1.2ptq\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}}}\right|{}^{J}}

and
q^{\prime}
or
q
and
{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\kern-1.2ptq^{\prime}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}}}\right|{}^{J}}
do for
J=\supp(q)\cap\supp(q^{\prime})
. This
is readily verified by use of
Lemma

V.5
in combination with
Proposition

V.7
. The
statement follows then directly from the definition of
\operatorname{\mathsf{rest}}(\{S\})
and an iterative
argument.
∎The above lemma is the key ingredient to the de-alternation of a RANA to an ERNNA:

Construction VI.4

(RANA to ERNNA)
.

Given a RANA
A_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
as in
Assumption

VI.1

with
n
orbits and degree
k
, we define the ERNNA
A_{E}=(Q_{E},\delta_{E},\{q_{0}\})
as follows:

(1)

The nominal set
Q_{E}
of states is the set of all finite subsets

\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{j}\}\subseteq Q_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
with
j\leqslant n\cdot k!
,
\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q_{i})\not\equiv\top

for all
i
and

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\{q_{\ell}\,:\,\ell\leqslant j,\exists\pi.\ q_{\ell}=\pi\cdot q_{i}\}}}\right|\leqslant k!\qquad\text{for all $i\leqslant j$}.

The
group action on
Q_{E}
acts as expected for subsets of nominal sets.

(2)

The equivariant transition function
\delta_{E}\colon Q_{E}\to\mathcal{B}_{+}(1+\mathds{A}\times Q_{E}+[\mathds{A}]Q_{E})
is defined
as follows: Put
\delta_{E}(\emptyset)=\top
.
For
S=\{q_{1},\dots,q_{j}\}\in Q_{E}
with
j\geqslant 1
:

(a)

If
all

\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q_{i})
’s contain an
\varepsilon
-disjunct, add one to

\delta_{E}(S)
.

(b)

For all
\alpha\in{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}
, if
every

\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q_{i})
contains an
\alpha
-disjunct
C_{i}=\bigwedge_{\ell\in I_{i}}\Diamond_{\alpha}q^{i}_{\ell}
, then for every choice
C_{1},\ldots,C_{j}
of
\alpha
-disjuncts, one from every
q_{i}
, the following
\alpha
-disjuncts are added:
put
\Delta_{\alpha}^{S}:=\{q^{i}_{\ell}\,:\,1\leqslant i\leqslant j,\ell\in I_{i}\}
and add an
\alpha
-disjunct
\Diamond_{\alpha}(S^{\prime}\setminus\{q\in S^{\prime}\,:\,\delta_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}(q)\equiv\top\})
for every
S^{\prime}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{rest}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\{\Delta_{\alpha}^{S}\}}}\right)
.

(c)

If none of the above two cases applies, put
\delta_{E}(S)=\bot
.

The ERNNA
A_{E}
has a degree of at most
n\cdot k\cdot k!\leqslant n\cdot 2^{(k+1)\log(k)}
, since each element of
Q_{E}
contains at
most
n\cdot k!
elements whose least supports are, in the worst
case, mutually disjoint and of size
k
.
To obtain an upper bound on the number of orbits of
Q_{E}
, we record

Lemma VI.5
.

Given a nominal set with
n
orbits and degree
k
, the nominal set of all subsets of size at most
\ell
has at most
\ell\cdot n^{\ell}\cdot 2^{k\cdot\ell\cdot\log(k\cdot\ell)}
orbits.

Applying the lemma to
\ell=n\cdot k!
shows that
Q_{E}
has at most

n\cdot k!\cdot n^{n\cdot k!}\cdot 2^{k\cdot n\cdot k!\cdot\log(k\cdot n\cdot k!)}
orbits, which is exponential in
n
and doubly exponential in
k
, using
k!\leqslant k^{k}\leqslant 2^{k\cdot\log k}
.

The bound on the sets of states in
A_{E}
is necessary since we do not only need to keep track
of states in different orbits but also states in the same orbits having the same support.
All other states of a single orbit get removed by the state restriction process. It is easy
to verify that
Construction

VI.4
yields an ERNNA. That
\delta_{E}
is well-defined follows
from the bounds of sets obtained in the state restriction process. (Namely, a finite subset
of
Q_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
such that any two elements in the same orbit have the same support has
cardinality at most
n\cdot k!
.) The correctness of the construction in the sense that

A_{E}
accepts the same literal language as the RANA
A_{\operatorname{\mathsf{nd}}}
is readily verified by
induction on the word length using the correctness of the state restriction process
(
Lemma

VI.3
). Hence, we obtain

Theorem VI.6
.

RANAs can be de-alternated to ERNNAs with exponential blowup of the degree
and doubly exponential blowup of the number of orbits.

In fact, the latter blowup is parametrized singly exponential with the
degree as parameter.

Remark VI.7

(Need for
\top
-states)
.

A ‘full’ de-alternation from RANAs to RNNAs (that is, RANAs whose transition formulae only use the Boolean connectives
\bot
,
\vee
) under preservation of the literal language is not possible. Consider the RANA
A=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\{q_{0}\}{,\>}\delta{,\>}q_{0}}}\right)
with

\delta(q_{0})=\top
, which accepts the universal bar language, i.e. every closed bar string.
There exists no RNNA accepting that language. Indeed, suppose that such an RNNA
A
exists.
Then, for each natural number
n
, it accepts the closed bar string

w_{n}={\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a_{1}a_{1}{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a_{2}a_{1}a_{2}\cdots{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a_{n}a_{1}\cdots a_{n}
.
Hence
A
has a state
q
accepting the suffix
a_{1}\cdots a_{n}
. The support principle for RNNAs
(cf.
[
33
, Lemma 5.4]
) entails that supports can only grow along bar transitions. This
implies that
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
must be contained in
\supp(q)
. Choosing
n>\deg(A)
yields a contradiction.

Under global freshness semantics, a similar argument shows that a translation from RANAs
to RNNAs is impossible. However, a translation is possible under local freshness semantics,
where ERNNAs and RNNAs are equivalent
[
15
, Rem. 5.2]
.

Remark VI.8
.

To see that naïve de-alternation via an unrestricted powerset construction would not work,
consider the positive RANA
A=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q{,\>}\delta{,\>}q_{0}}}\right)
with the set of states
Q=\{q_{0}\}+\{q_{1}\}\times\mathds{A}
and transitions
\delta(q_{0})=\Diamond_{{\hskip 0.60275pt{\rule[-0.3014pt]{0.6pt}{4.97289pt}}\hskip 0.60275pt}a}q_{0}\wedge\Diamond_{{\hskip 0.60275pt{\rule[-0.3014pt]{0.6pt}{4.97289pt}}\hskip 0.60275pt}a}q_{1}(a)
and
\delta(q_{1}(a))=\Diamond_{{\hskip 0.60275pt{\rule[-0.3014pt]{0.6pt}{4.97289pt}}\hskip 0.60275pt}b}q_{1}(a)
.
On input
w={\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a_{1}\cdots{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a_{n}
, the naïve powerset construction reaches the set
\{q_{0},q_{1}(a_{1}),\ldots,q_{1}(a_{n})\}
; hence it needs to store unboundedly many states, contradicting the required orbit-finiteness of
the state set.

Construction

VI.4
avoids this by reducing the above
(n+1)
-element set to a two-element set.

VII
Decidability of Inclusion

We now show our second main result:
Language inclusion of RANAs is decidable under the bar language, the global and the local freshness semantics.
To derive the corresponding complexity bounds, we will use the equivalence to bar AFAs (
Definition

V.8
).

Remark VII.1
.

For algorithms deciding properties of RANAs, a finite representation
of the underlying nominal set of states and transitions is
required.
We assume without loss of generality that RANAs have a strong nominal
state set, which clearly have a finite representation. Since the transition
function is equivariant, we can represent it via finite representations of
finitely many formulae, namely one for every orbit.

Remark VII.2
.

We summarize the complexities of constructions in previous sections.
Given a RANA with
n
orbits and degree
k
, its name-dropping modification (of an equivalent positive RANA) has at most
(2n(k+2)+1)\cdot 2^{(2k+1)\cdot\log(4k+2)}
orbits, which is linear in
n
and exponential in
k
, and a degree of
2k+1
(
III.19
,
III.20
and
V.3
).
After de-alternation (
Construction

VI.4
), the resulting ERNNA has a degree that is linear in
n
and exponential in
k
, and a number of orbits that is exponential in
n
and doubly exponential in
k
.

We first consider the
non-emptiness problem
, which asks whether the accepted language of a RANA
A=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q{,\>}\delta{,\>}q_{0}}}\right)
is non-empty. We will reduce this problem to the non-emptiness problem for bar AFAs. We first introduce a different,
finite
, semantics for bar AFAs:

Definition VII.3

(Finite semantics)
.

Given a bar AFA
A=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q{,\>}{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}_{0}{,\>}\delta{,\>}q_{0}{,\>}F}}\right)
, its
AFA-language

L_{{\textsf{\small\scriptsize AFA}}}(A)
consists of those bar strings
w\in{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}^{\star}_{0}
that are accepted by
A
when
interpreted as a classical AFA
[
6
]
over the finite alphabet
{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}_{0}
.

Lemma VII.4
.

For every bar AFA
A
, we have
L_{0}(A)=\emptyset
iff

L_{{\textsf{\small\scriptsize AFA}}}(A)\cap\mathsf{bs}(\emptyset)=\emptyset
.

Proof:

There are two difference between the AFA-semantics and the bar language semantics (
Definition

V.8
):
Firstly, a bar AFA can classically (with the AFA-semantics) accept bar strings
w
that are not closed, i.e. have
free names (
\mathsf{FN}(w)\neq\emptyset
), for which we use the intersection; indeed,
L_{{\textsf{\small\scriptsize AFA}}}(A)\cap\mathsf{bs}(\emptyset)
only contains closed bar strings.

Secondly, the bar language semantics handles
\alpha
-equivalent words and
states with
\delta(q,\alpha)=\top
(for some
\alpha\in{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}_{0}
) differently. More concretely, with the
AFA-semantics, a bar AFA can only accept bar strings over
{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}_{0}
, not over
{\mathrlap{\hskip 1.25pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$\mathds{A}$}}}}\mathds{A}}
, which is possible
using the bar language semantics. However, every closed bar string accepted with the AFA-semantics is also accepted
using the bar semantics, yielding
L_{{\textsf{\small\scriptsize AFA}}}(A)\cap\mathsf{bs}(\emptyset)\subseteq L_{0}(A)
and in turn the
‘if’ direction.

For ‘only if’, let
L_{0}(A)\neq\emptyset
and
w\in L_{0}(A)
. Note, that
w
is closed by design.
Then either there is a word
w^{\prime}\alphaequiv w
such that
w^{\prime}
is accepted in whole by
A
, or there
is a prefix
v
and an
\alpha
-equivalent
v^{\prime}
such that

VIII
Conclusion

We have introduced
regular alternating nominal automata

(RANAs), an alternating nominal automata model with name
allocation. RANAs generalize both their nondeterministic restriction,

regular nondeterministic nominal automata
(

)
[
33
]
, and the extension of RNNAs with deadlocked

inclusion problems of the RANA model are decidable in

under local freshness. Lastly, we improved the
model-checking problem for the linear-time nominal fixed-point logic

by one exponential level.

In future work we will extend our approach to infinite data words, aiming for an
alternating extension of
Büchi RNNA

[

. This will provide an
automata-theoretic basis for specification logics over infinite data words, with a perspective to
obtain elementary decidability of model checking and satisfiability checking.

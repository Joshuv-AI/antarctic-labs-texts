1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivations

The article contributes to the theory of entropy of semiclassical measures which sits within the broader study of quantum Hamiltonian systems. We give some review of these notions and pertinent results before stating our main theorem.

1.1.1.
Eigenfunctions and Equidistribution

The semiclassical correspondence principle in quantum mechanics states that in the high-energy limit, the long-time classical mechanics is reflected in the corresponding quantum phenomena. In this article, we focus on the case when the classical system is chaotic. In particular, we focus on the geometric setting of
(M,g)
being a compact, boundaryless, Riemannian manifold whose geodesic flow (slightly abusing notation)
g^{t}
on the unit sphere bundle is Anosov; compact manifolds with negative sectional curvatures form a rich class of examples. The
Quantum Unique Ergodicity (QUE)
conjecture of Rudnick-Sarnak
[
30
]
states that on manifolds
M
with negative sectional curvatures, eigenfunctions of the (semiclassical) quantum Hamiltonian
P(\hbar):=-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{g}
, that is functions
\psi_{\hbar}
satisfying

P(\hbar)\psi_{\hbar}=E\psi_{\hbar}

for
E>0
fixed, become equidistributed as
\hbar\rightarrow 0
. This conjecture is believed to hold more generally for their Wigner distributions
W_{\hbar}
. These are quantities on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
that simultaneously describe the localization of the sequence
\{\psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
in position and momentum.

The Wigner distribution associated to a
L^{2}(M,d\rm{vol}_{g})
-normalized sequence of states
\{\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
is defined as

W_{\hbar}(a):=\langle{\rm{Op}}_{\hbar}(a)\Psi_{\hbar},\Psi_{\hbar}\rangle

(1.1.1)

where
a\in C^{\infty}_{0}(T^{*}M)
and
{\rm{Op}}_{\hbar}(\bullet)
is a quantization procedure that sends
a
to a particular bounded operator on
L^{2}(M,d\rm{vol}_{g})
; this procedure is described in further detail in Appendix
A
. We call
semiclassical measures

\mu_{sc}
the limit points of the sequence
\{W_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
in the weak-* topology on the space of distributions. It is well-known
[
39
]
that semiclassical measures
\mu_{sc}
associated to eigenfunctions of
P(\hbar)
are
g^{t}
-invariant and are supported on the energy shell

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}:=S^{*}_{E}M=\{(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M\,|\,\|\xi\|_{g(x)}=E\};

(1.1.2)

here
\|\bullet\|_{g}
is the norm on
T^{*}_{x}M
generated by the metric
g
. QUE in its fullest strength states the following: in the case of eigenfunctions on manifolds with negative sectional curvatures,
W_{\hbar}
converges to the Liouville measure
dL
on
\mathcal{E}
. This better explains the word “equidistribution”. An important measure of localization is the notion of
Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy
and our main result (appearing in Section
1.2
) concerns this quantity.

At present, only a few cases of QUE have been successfully resolved: so-called Arithmetic QUE proven by Lindenstrauss
[
26
]
in the compact case, Soundararajan
[
35
]
in the non-compact case, and Silberman-Venkatesh for locally symmetric spaces
[
33
]
. These results take place on congruence surfaces that carry additional number-theoretic structure and admit operators which enjoy these symmetries, namely Hecke operators. Specifically, Arithmetic QUE holds for joint eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator and the Hecke operators. We highlight the role entropy plays in the proofs of Lindenstrauss and Silberman-Venkatesh. An analogue of Arithmetic QUE was established by Kurlberg-Rudnick
[
24
]
for the model of the quantum cat map on the 2-torus. See the work of Zelditch
[
37
]
for QUE in the context of random bases on spheres.

Counterexamples to QUE have appeared, albeit in inequivalent models such as the Bunimovich stadium
[
22
]
and the quantum cat map
[
18
,
19
]
. The proofs for the mentioned models have utilized large spectral multiplicities (or degeneracies, as they will be referred to in upcoming discussion and motivate the main result of our paper) in effective ways to demonstrate the existence of exceptional subsequences and therefore the failure of QUE. In fact, Arithmetic QUE was disproved by Kelmer
[
23
]
on higher dimensional tori even without the use of large multiplicities.

In the context of QUE on negatively curved surfaces, it is important to emphasize two relatively recent results that were tremendous steps in our understanding of high-frequency spectral asymptotics. The first was the breakthrough of Dyatlov-Jin
[
14
]
, underpinned by an earlier breakthrough of Bourgain-Dyatlov
[
5
]
on resonance gaps for hyperbolic surfaces with funnels, proving that sequences of eigenfunctions on compact hyperbolic surfaces have
{\rm{supp}}\,\mu_{sc}=S^{*}_{E}M
. This was later generalized to surfaces of variable negative curvature by Dyatlov-Jin-Nonnenmacher
[
15
]
.

Note that the looser notion of
quantum ergodicity
states the existence of a density-one subsequence of
\{\psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
that equidistributes. In fact, S̆chnirel’man-Zelditch-Colin de Verdiére
[
32
,
36
,
12
]
established quantum ergodicity on compact manifolds having ergodic geodesics flows with respect to
dL
. Quantum ergodicity was established on billiards with ergodic flows in
[
20
,
38
]
and for cat maps in
[
10
]
.

1.1.2.
Spectral degeneracies and quasimodes

The Laplace-Beltrami operator on manifolds with Anosov geodesic flows is
not expected
to have large spectral multiplicities, thus one might expect the equidistribution phenomena. In response, one can study a related form (or depending on one’s perspective, a replacement) of this spectral degeneracy by considering functions that act as approximations to true eigenfunctions and are called
quasimodes
. The order of approximation can be quantified with the notion of
width
and is given in the following definition:

Definition
1.1.3

(Quasimodes)
.

For a given energy level
E>0
, we say that a semiclassical family
\{\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
of
L^{2}
-normalized states is a family of
quasimodes of
P(\hbar)=-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{g}
of central energy
E
and
width

w(\hbar)
, if and only if there exists
\hbar_{0}(E)>0
such that

\|(P(\hbar)-E)\Psi_{\hbar}\|_{L^{2}(M)}\leq w(\hbar)\,,\qquad\forall\hbar\leq\hbar_{0}\,.

(1.1.4)

Linear combinations of eigenfunctions corresponding to eigenvalues in a
w(\hbar)
-width window form a class of examples. Note that quasimodes of width
o(\hbar)
have semiclassical measures
\mu_{sc}
also being
g^{t}
-invariant and supported on
\mathcal{E}
.

There is a desire to elucidate the connection between degeneracies in the spectrum of chaotic geometries and QUE, which in its current status is not completely clear. This article attempts to make progress in this direction. A trio of results due to Brooks
[
8
]
, Eswarathasan-Nonnenmacher
[
16
]
, and Eswarathasan-Silberman
[
17
]
show how (the proposed form of) spectral degeneracy can lead to phenomena other than equidistribution, as inspired by the work of Faure-Nonnenmacher-de Bièvre
[
18
]
on the quantum cat map. In particular on a compact surface having a closed hyperbolic geodesic,
[
16
]
constructs a sequence of quasimodes of width
C\frac{\hbar}{|\log\hbar|}
with central energy
E
and whose semiclassical measure is a delta function on that geodesic; in fact, this result continues to hold for more general quantum Hamiltonians whose associated classical dynamics has at least one closed hyperbolic orbit. The works
[
8
,
17
]
are particular to constant negative curvature with that of Eswarathasan-Silberman holding in higher dimensions. Note these results are consistent with that of Dyatlov-Jin-Nonnenmacher
[
14
,
15
]
which show the full support of
\mu_{sc}
extends to quasimodes of width
o\left(\frac{\hbar}{|\log\hbar|}\right)
. We are now in position to introduce our main objects of study:

Definition
1.1.5

(
\varepsilon
-logarithmic modes)
.

For a given energy level
E>0
and width parameter
\varepsilon>0
, we say that a semiclassical family
\{\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
of
L^{2}
-normalized states is a family of
\varepsilon
-logarithmic modes
for
P(\hbar)=-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{g}
if there exist intervals

I(\varepsilon,\hbar):=\left[E-\varepsilon\,\frac{\hbar}{|\log\hbar|},E+\varepsilon\,\frac{\hbar}{|\log\hbar|}\right]

and an
\hbar_{0}(E,\varepsilon)>0
such that for all
\hbar\leq\hbar_{0}
, the modes
\Psi_{\hbar}
are spectrally supported in
I(\varepsilon,\hbar)
: that is,
\Pi_{I(\hbar)^{\complement}}\Psi_{\hbar}=0
. In other words,
\{\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
is a
L^{2}
-normalized family consisting of linear combinations of eigenfunctions whose eigenvalues sit inside of
I(\varepsilon,\hbar)
therefore making it a family of quasimodes for
P(h)
with energy
E
and width
w(\hbar)=\varepsilon\frac{\hbar}{|\log\hbar|}
.

Remark 1.1.6
.

We emphasize that the results in this manuscript continue to hold for normalized modes

\displaystyle\Psi_{\hbar}\in\chi\left(\frac{|\log\hbar|(P(\hbar)-E)}{\varepsilon\hbar}\right)L^{2}(M)

where
\chi
is a Schwartz function with
\chi(0)=1
. The main reason for considering the types of spectral localization seen in Definition
1.1.5
is simply to guarantee
WF_{\hbar}(\Psi_{\hbar})\subset\mathcal{E}
and then easily employ the so-called entropic uncertainty principle (see Section
5
).
\varepsilon
-logarithmic modes are slightly more convenient when using spectral calculus at various places in our argument.

1.2.
Main result

We recall that in this manuscript,
(M,g)
is a
d
-dimensional, compact, and boundaryless manifold whose geodesic flow
g^{t}
on
\mathcal{E}
is Anosov. To better understand the various semiclassical measures
\mu_{sc}
of
\varepsilon
-logarithmic modes, we aim to compute their
Kolmogorov-Sinai entropies
. The Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy of a
g^{t}
-invariant probability measure
\mu
is a non-negative number
H_{KS}(\mu)
describing the long-time, average information carried by
\mu
-typical geodesics. A more precise definition of
H_{KS}
is provided in Section
B
with further background found in
[
6
, Chapter 9]
. Throughout the article,
P(\hbar)
will always denote the semiclassical Laplace-Beltrami operator
-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{g}
.

A classical upperbound on
H_{KS}
for a
g^{t}
-invariant probability measure
\mu
is to due to Ruelle
[
31
]
:

H_{KS}(\mu)\leq\int_{\mathcal{E}}\log J^{u}(\rho)\,d\mu(\rho).

Here,

J^{u}(\rho):=|\det D(g^{1})_{\upharpoonright E^{u}(\rho)}|

is the
unstable Jacobian for
g^{t}
at
\rho\in\mathcal{E}
with
E^{u}(\rho)
being the unstable manifold at
\rho\in\mathcal{E}
; recall that in our geometry,
\mathcal{E}
admits a foliation into unstable manifolds of the flow
[
6
, Chapter 5]
. Note that the unstable Jacobian takes a slightly different definition in the previous works
[
1
,
3
,
2
,
29
]
and that we use the formulation given in
[
27
, Section 1.2]
.

In the case of Anosov systems
[
25
]
, equality in Ruelle’s upper bound occurs if and only if
\mu=dL
. When the sectional curvatures
\mathcal{K}(M)=-1
, the measure of maximal entropy is
dL
and thus
H_{KS}(\mu)\leq d-1
for any
g^{t}
-invariant probability measure
\mu
. Thus, QUE conjecture can be reformulated as claiming
H_{KS}(\mu_{sc})=d-1
, or that
\mu_{sc}
is the measure of maximal entropy in constant curvature.

Now, define

\lambda_{\max}:=\lim_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}\sup_{\rho\in\mathcal{E}}t^{-1}\log|\det D(g^{t})|

to be the
maximal expansion rate on
\mathcal{E}
. The analogous definition for the maximal expansion rate on different energy shells is immediate. Also, define
H_{\max}
to be the maximal entropy amongst all
g^{t}
-invariant measures on
\mathcal{E}
; we remind ourselves that any smooth map of a compact manifold has measures of maximal entropy, a consequence in part to the upper-semicontinuity of
H_{KS}
. Our main result is:

Theorem
1.2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
d
-dimensional compact, boundaryless, Riemannian manifold whose geodesic flow on
\mathcal{E}
is Anosov. Consider an
\varepsilon
-logarithmic family
\{\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
and let
\mu_{sc}
be its semiclassical measure. Finally, let
H_{0}\in(0,H_{\max}]
.

Then there exists a splitting
\mu_{sc}=a\mu_{sc,\rm{L}}+b\mu_{sc,\rm{H}}
into components of entropy
<H_{0}
and
\geq H_{0}
, signed measures
\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{sc}
and
\overline{\tilde{\mathfrak{F}}}_{sc}
generated from
\{\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
, and non-negative functionals
\bm{R}
and
\tilde{\bm{R}}
such that

\displaystyle H_{KS}(\mu_{sc})\geq

\displaystyle\max\Bigg\{\int_{\mathcal{E}}\log J^{u}(\rho)\,d\mu_{sc}(\rho)-\frac{(d-1)}{2}\lambda_{\max}-\frac{1}{2}\left|\int_{\mathcal{E}}\log J^{u}(\rho)\,d\left(\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{sc}+\overline{\tilde{\mathfrak{F}}}_{sc}\right)(\rho)\right|

\displaystyle-H_{\max}\left(\frac{2\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\max}}+\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\max}}\right)^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}(\bm{R}+\tilde{\bm{R}}),\,\,bH_{0}\Bigg\}.

Here we have
|\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{sc}(\mathcal{E})|\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\max}}+\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\max}}\right)^{2}
and
|\overline{\tilde{\mathfrak{F}}}_{sc}(\mathcal{E})|\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\max}}+\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda_{\max}}\right)^{2}
. The functionals
\bm{R},\tilde{\bm{R}}
describe the difference of (local) entropies corresponding to Schrödinger-propagated states and stationary states over sets contained in the supports of
\mu_{sc,\rm{L}}
and
\mu_{sc,\rm{H}}
; they also implicitly depend on the splitting and therefore on
H_{0}
.

If
\varepsilon=o(1)
in
\hbar
,
\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{sc}=\bm{R}=\overline{\tilde{\mathfrak{F}}}_{sc}=\tilde{\bm{R}}=0
and therefore the Anantharaman-Koch-Nonnenmacher bound (without
bH_{0}
) is recovered:

H_{KS}(\mu_{sc})\geq\int_{\mathcal{E}}\log J^{u}(\rho)\,d\mu_{sc}(\rho)-\frac{(d-1)}{2}\lambda_{\max}.

See Section
7.1
, particularly Proposition
7.1.1
, for the definitions of
\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{sc}
and
\overline{\tilde{\mathfrak{F}}}_{sc}
. See Section
8.2
, especially equation (
8.2.3
), for the definition of
\bm{R}
(and
\tilde{\bm{R}}
) and Proposition
8.2.4
for upper bounds; a further discussion on it occurs in Section
1.5
. At the present time we are unable to quantitatively bound
\bm{R}
for fixed
\varepsilon
, hence the shortcoming of not establishing positive entropy without using Anantharaman’s bound
[
1
, Theorem 1.1.2]
. Our bound could be seen as a trade-off though: while Anantharaman’s is positive and less explicit, ours is not clearly positive but more quantitative in some respect. The hope is that Proposition
8.2.4
, providing simpler expressions bounding
\bm{R}
, can be carried further with refined ergodic-theoretic methods which unfortunately are outside the scope of this article.

While Theorem
1.2.1
takes place in the context of the semiclassical Laplace-Beltrami operator
-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{g}
, the result should easily generalize to other quantum Hamiltonians
P(\hbar)
satisfying some basic non-degeneracy and spectral conditions near a fixed energy
E>0
. We leave these to the interested reader.

1.3.
Partial localization

We want to apply Theorem
1.2.1
to logarithmic quasimodes that partially localize:

Theorem
1.3.1

(Partial localization on closed, lower-dimensional, invariant subsets in constant negative curvature
[
17
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
d
-dimensional compact hyperbolic manifold. Let
E>0
be an energy level of
P(\hbar)=-\hbar^{2}\Delta
and
\mu_{\Gamma}
be a
g^{t}
-invariant measure on
\mathcal{E}
with support
\Gamma
a closed set of Hausdorff dimension strictly less than
2d-1
. Then there exists
\varepsilon^{0}>0
and a family of quasimodes
\{\bm{\Psi}_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
of width
\varepsilon^{0}\frac{\hbar}{|\log\hbar|}
with central energy
E
and
\mu_{sc}=\mu_{\Gamma}
. Furthermore, given
\varepsilon_{1}>0
, there exists an
\varepsilon_{1}
-logarithmic family
\{\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
with central energies
E_{\hbar}=E+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\hbar}{|\log\hbar|}\right)
where
\mu_{sc}
contains a component
w_{\Gamma}\cdot\mu_{\Gamma}
in its ergodic decomposition and

w_{\Gamma}\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\varepsilon^{0}}\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\varepsilon^{0}}\right)^{2}\right).

The implicit constant in the expression for
E_{\hbar}
can be taken close from above to
\varepsilon^{0}
.

Noting that our result should easily extend to
\varepsilon
-logarithmic modes but associated to central energies
E_{\hbar}\sim E
, we obtain a new entropy lower bound for
\varepsilon
-logarithmic modes that partially localize.

1.4.
On the proof

In this section, we touch upon our new contributions and give an outline of our proof. First, let
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and consider
q\in\mathbb{N}
such that
qn_{0}\leq\lfloor\frac{|\log\hbar|}{\lambda_{\max}}\rfloor
, the right-hand being twice the “Ehrenfest time”. Next, set
\Sigma(qn_{0})=\{0,1\}^{qn_{0}}
and consider this as the set of binary words of length
qn_{0}
. These will correspond to particular dynamical quantities, specifically
g^{t}
-refined partition elements at time
qn_{0}
.

One can attach a bounded operator
\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}
to such a
qn_{0}
-length word
\bm{\beta}
and which serves as a refined partition element at the quantum level. Moreover, the quantity
\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}
is the quantum analogue of a
g^{t}
-invariant probability measure evaluated at the word
\bm{\beta}
. These notions allow us to analyse a type of quantum entropy at time
qn_{0}
, namely
H_{0}^{qn_{0}-1}(\Psi_{\hbar})
. Finally, let
J_{\bm{\beta}}^{u}
be a discretized version of the unstable Jacobian
J^{u}(\rho)
associated to the cylinder
\bm{\beta}
. All of these objects are defined Sections
4
-
5
and Appendix
B.1
.

The work of Anantharaman-(Koch)-Nonnenmacher
[
2
,
3
]
establishes that within the described regime of
n_{0}
and
\hbar
(modulo errors in a variety of parameters including
\hbar
and abusing some invariance statements),

\displaystyle H_{0}^{qn_{0}-1}(\Psi_{\hbar})-\sum_{\bm{\beta}\in\Sigma(qn_{0})}\log(J_{\bm{\beta}}^{u})\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}\geq-\frac{(d-1)\lambda_{\max}}{2}qn_{0}

thanks to a combination of the entropic uncertainty principle (Section
5.2
) and the hyperbolic dispersive estimate (Appendix
C
). In order to make rigorous the passage from quantum entropies at long times
H_{0}^{qn_{0}-1}(\Psi_{\hbar})
to actual metric entropy for
\mu_{sc}
at finite times
n_{0}
, one uses a sub-additivity argument to reduce the above inequality to

\displaystyle H_{0}^{n_{0}-1}(\Psi_{\hbar})+\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}H_{0}^{n_{0}-1}(e^{-i\frac{jn_{0}P(\hbar)}{\hbar}}\Psi_{\hbar})-q\sum_{\bm{\beta}\in\Sigma(n_{0})}\log(J_{\bm{\beta}}^{u})\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\bm{\beta}\in\Sigma(n_{0})}\log(J_{\bm{\beta}}^{u})\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}F_{1}(\hbar,\bm{\beta},jn_{0})\geq-\frac{(d-1)\lambda_{\max}}{2}qn_{0}

where the operators
\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}
are now pseudodifferential, thanks to the word lengths being independent of
\hbar
, and
F_{1}(\hbar,\bm{\beta},jn_{0})=\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}-\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}e^{-i\frac{jn_{0}P(\hbar)}{\hbar}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}
. At this stage one can make further use of semiclassical methods. One goes from these quantum entropies to metric entropies after dividing both sides by
qn_{0}
and carefully taking the limits
\hbar\rightarrow 0
and
n_{0}\rightarrow\infty
(amongst others limits).

When
\Psi_{\hbar}
is an eigenfunction, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}H_{0}^{n_{0}-1}(e^{-i\frac{jn_{0}P(\hbar)}{\hbar}}\Psi_{\hbar})=(q-1)H^{n_{0}-1}_{0}(\Psi_{\hbar})\mbox{ and }\sum_{\bm{\beta}\in\Sigma(n_{0})}\log(J_{\bm{\beta}}^{u})\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}F_{1}(\hbar,\bm{\beta},jn_{0})=0

modulo small errors in various parameters, including
\hbar
.
In the case of
\varepsilon
-logarithmic modes, these quantities are more complicated with the bulk of our article attempting to control them in
\varepsilon
and other variables.
In particular, we reduce the defect

\displaystyle D(h,n_{0}):=q^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\left(H_{0}^{n_{0}-1}(e^{-i\frac{jn_{0}P(\hbar)}{\hbar}}\Psi_{\hbar})-H_{0}^{n_{0}-1}(\Psi_{\hbar})\right)

into functionals depending on the high and low entropy components of
\mu_{sc}
. From here, we work out quantities that are simpler in expression and whose sum forms an upper bound for
D(\bullet)
in the relevant limiting regime. This is where the terms
\bm{R}
and
\tilde{\bm{R}}
arise and where we can describe the true contributions of this article.

Our first biggest departure
is in establishing a sharper form of the shift-invariance formula for quantum measures (Proposition
4.2.1
) associated to
\varepsilon
-logarithmic modes and addresses
F_{1}(\bullet)
; this should be contrasted with
[
3
, Proposition 4.1]
for eigenfunctions and
[
1
, Proposition 1.3.1]
for
\varepsilon
-logarithmic modes. Our more general invariance statement leads to more precise sub-additivity statements for the entropies
H_{0}^{qn_{0}-1}(\Psi_{\hbar})
(and the associated potentials), themselves proved in Sections
6
and which constitute our
second biggest departure
. The
third and final departure
occurs in Sections
7
and
8
particularly in how we treat our words at time
n_{0}
in the expression for

: that is, we take advantage of a non-ergodic version of Shannon-McMillan-Brieman with the approach inspired largely by the work of Brooks
[
7
]
on entropy for quantum cat maps. Next, we sift out
\bm{R}
amongst other quantities that can be bounded in terms of the spectral width parameter

. Section

gives an exposition of a microlocal version of the Entropic Uncertainty Principle. The appendices give standard definitions for the various entropy and pressure functionals we will use. They also reproduce some necessary statements from semiclassical analysis as well as one of the main results in

, the hyperbolic dispersive estimate. Given the technical nature of the machinery setup in
[

,

, a large portion of this paper’s appendix reiterates important statements produced in those works.

Those readers familiar with this machinery can skip immediately to Sections

,

,

, and

We emphasize that much of our notation was taken directly from the excellent lecture notes of Nonnenmacher

1.5.
The term
R
and future directions

A shortcoming of Theorem
1.2.1
is the lack of a satisfactory estimate on the function
\bm{R}
which loosely measures, over certain sets of dynamical words, the difference of entropies between semiclassical measures generated by sequences
\{\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
and
\{e^{-inP(\hbar)/\hbar}\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
for
n
less than twice the Ehrenfest time. This stems from our application of the (non-ergodic) Shannon-McMillan-Brieman Theorem which is limited in providing non-trivial upper/lower bounds for
\mu_{sc}
on certain sets of words. Proposition
8.2.4
presents functionals in the form of “expected values” of
-\log(\mu_{sc,\rm{H}/\rm{L}})
with respect to
\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{sc}
, which we conjecture are amenable to methods from dynamical systems. A second possible way forward is to implement Anantharaman’s strategy in her original breakthrough
[
1
]
(see also
[
14
]
) in combination with non-ergodic SMB. We hope that our reduction of
\bm{R}
can be carried further by interested readers with the relevant expertise.

A larger issue is in analysing the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy with respect to the total variation norm: that is, understanding the differences of
H_{KS}
over small total-variation-norm neighbourhoods of
\mu_{sc}
. Weak
∗
distances between measures work poorly in general with most ergodic properties; for instance, Sigmund’s Theorem states that zero entropy measures are weak
∗
dense in the space of invariant measures for Axiom A flows. Furthermore, we only know that
H_{KS}
is upper semi-continuous with respect to the weak
∗
topology.

A better way to control entropy, and therefore the term
D(\hbar,n_{0})
, is through the
\overline{d}
metric of Ornstein: Entropy is now uniformly continuous
[
28
, Section 6.4]
. To obtain an improvement on Theorem
1.2.1
, one only needs to show the measures generated by
\Psi_{\hbar}
and
e^{-inP(\hbar)/\hbar}\Psi_{\hbar}
are close in the
\overline{d}
metric for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. Furthermore, this would allows us to generalize the result to
any
non-spectrally localized quasimodes of logarithmic width via simple projection arguments. However, implementing the
\overline{d}
metric in practice is difficult with one form using the theory of joinings by Furstenberg. As this is beyond the scope of our paper, we leave this as a future project.

3.
Microlocalization

Recall that on compact
(M,g)
, the spectrum of
P(\hbar)=-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{g}
is discrete and infinite, which we denote by
\{E_{j}(\hbar)\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Our space
L^{2}(M,d{\operatorname{Vol}}_{g})
admits an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions which we write individually as
\psi_{j}
. In particular, if
\Psi_{\hbar}
is
L^{2}
-normalized, then

\Psi_{\hbar}=\sum_{j}c_{j}(\hbar)\,\psi_{j}

where
\sum_{j}c_{j}(\hbar)^{2}=1
.

3.1.
Microlocalization properties

The main statement of this section is a lemma on the wavefront set for our
\varepsilon
-logarithmic modes. We direct the reader towards Appendix
A.2
for the definition of
\chi^{(n)}
.

Lemma
3.1.1

(
\varepsilon
-logarithmic modes are “microlocalized near
\mathcal{E}
at scale
\hbar^{\delta_{long}}
”)
.

Consider
\chi^{(n)}
as in (
A.2.1
) and
\delta_{long}>0
be given. Let
\{\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
be a family of
\varepsilon
-logarithmic modes and
C_{\delta_{long}}<(\delta_{long})^{-1}-1
. Then there exists
\hbar_{0}(\varepsilon,\delta_{long})
such that for all
\hbar\leq\hbar_{0}
and
n\leq C_{\delta_{long}}|\log\hbar|
, we have

\left(Id-\chi^{(n)}(P(\hbar)-E)\right)\Psi_{\hbar}=\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty}).

In fact, if
\chi^{(n)}(P(\hbar)-E)
is defined through spectral calculus, then the left-hand side is 0. As a consequence,
WF_{\hbar}(\Psi_{\hbar})\subset\mathcal{E}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1.1
.

This is a simple computation via spectral calculus after using the support properties of
\chi^{(n)}
. Thanks to
\chi^{(n)}\left(P(\hbar)-E\right)
being a function of
P(\hbar)
, we have

\chi^{(n)}\left(P(\hbar)-E\right)\Psi_{\hbar}=\sum_{E_{j}\in I(\varepsilon,h)}\chi^{(n)}\left(P(\hbar)-E\right)c_{j}\psi_{j}

where
\psi_{j}
is an eigenfunction with eigenvalue
E_{j}
. Thus,
\sum_{E_{j}\in I^{\complement}(h)}\left(1-\chi^{(n)}\left(E_{j}-E\right)\right)c_{j}\psi_{j}=0
as
c_{j}=0
thanks to
\chi^{(n)}
having width
h^{1-\delta_{long}}
and
I
having width
\frac{\hbar}{|\log\hbar|}
.
∎

Remark 3.1.2
.

One can also use the second-microlocal calculus developed extensively by Sjöstrand-Zworski
[
34
]
and some standard arguments. Consider the eigenvalue
E_{j_{0}}\in I_{0}(\hbar)
that is furthest from the energy
E
. From
[
3
, Proposition 2.5]
, we know that

\|\left(I-\chi^{(n)}((P(\hbar)-E))\right)\psi_{j_{0}}\|=\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{\infty})

by constructing a left elliptic parametrix for
P(\hbar)-E
, and then utilizing a property for symbols of disjoint support. As
I\subset[E-h^{\delta_{long}},E+h^{\delta_{long}}]
for
\hbar\leq\hbar_{0}
, we have that
\|\left(I-\chi^{(n)}((P(\hbar)-E)\right)\Psi_{\hbar}\|=\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{\infty})
by simply applying the triangle inequality
\#\{E_{j}\,|\,E_{j}\in I\}
times and yielding to the Weyl Law.

Note that for more general quasimodes of width as fine as
o\left(\frac{\hbar}{|\log\hbar|}\right)
but without spectral cutoffs mentioned in Remark
1.1.6
, the wavefront set may not be contained inside of
\mathcal{E}
: in fact, one gets the same
o\left(\frac{\hbar}{|\log\hbar|}\right)
estimate on
\Psi_{\hbar}
away from the support of
\chi^{(n)}((P(\hbar)-E)
. This will be important in applying the entropic uncertainty principle found in Proposition
5.2.1
.

, it is strongly suggested they familiarize themselves with various dynamical and operator-theoretic quantities (and the passages between them) including the initial dynamical partition

and
\hbar>0
be given; from here onward we set
\delta_{long}=\delta_{Ehr}
as in the hypothesis of Lemma

T_{\delta_{Ehr},\varepsilon_{slab},\hbar}:=\frac{(1-\delta_{Ehr})}{2\lambda_{\max}(\varepsilon_{slab})}|\log\hbar|

on the energy slab
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon_{slab}}=\{(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M\,|\,E-\varepsilon_{slab}\leq\|\xi\|_{g(x)}\leq E+\varepsilon_{slab}\}.

4.1.
Quantum measures and words

We have a natural notion for a probability measure on the space of words
\Sigma^{n}
, as follows:

Definition
4.1.1

(Quantum measures)
.

Let
\{\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
be an
\varepsilon
-logarithmic family. Then for any
\bm{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{n}
with
n\leq C_{\delta}|\log\hbar|
, we write

\mu_{\hbar}(\bm{\alpha}):=\|\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2},

(4.1.2)

which itself we call the
forward (quantum) measure
. Analogously, we can define the
backwards (quantum) measure
for
\{\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
as

\tilde{\mu}_{\hbar}(\bm{\alpha}):=\|\Pi_{\alpha_{-n}}(-n)\dots\Pi_{\alpha_{-1}}(-1)\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2},

More formally,
\mu_{\hbar}
and
\tilde{\mu}_{\hbar}
can be seen as equivalence classes of
\hbar
-dependent probability measures on words of length
n
, modulo
\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{\infty})
quantities. Moreover, for
n
fixed,
\mu_{\hbar}(\bm{\alpha})\rightarrow\mu_{sc}(\pi_{\bm{\alpha}})
as
\hbar\rightarrow 0
thanks to Egorov’s Theorem for short times with a similar formula for
\tilde{\mu}_{\hbar}
. We will toggle between the notations of
\mu_{\hbar}(\bullet)
and
\|\bullet\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}
depending on the calculation.

Lemma
4.1.3

(Compatibility conditions for logarithmic modes)
.

Let
\{\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
be an
\varepsilon
-logarithmic family. Let
\delta_{Ehr}\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon_{slab}>0
be given. Then there exists
\hbar_{0}(\varepsilon,\delta_{Ehr},\varepsilon_{slab},K)
such that for
\hbar\leq h_{0}
and for all
n\leq\lfloor 2T_{\delta_{Ehr},\varepsilon_{slab},\hbar}\rfloor\leq\hbar^{-(1-\delta_{Ehr})/2}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{n}}\|\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}=1+\mathcal{O}_{K}(\hbar^{\infty})

and therefore
\forall\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{n-2}\in\Sigma^{1}
,

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{n-2}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}=\sum_{\alpha_{n-1}}\|\Pi_{\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+\mathcal{O}_{K}(h^{\infty}).

Proof.

This is an immediate implication of Proposition
B.2.4
, the microlocalization statement Lemma
3.1.1
, and
[
3
, Remark 2.4]
showing that
\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}
continues to be microlocalized near
\mathcal{E}
at scale
\hbar^{\delta_{Ehr}}
. Thanks to Lemma
3.1.1
, for any
\chi\in S^{-\infty,0}(T^{*}M)
supported on
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon_{slab}/2}
,
{\rm{Op}}_{\hbar}(\chi)\Psi_{\hbar}=\Psi_{\hbar}+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{\infty})
. Now apply Proposition
B.2.4
with the same choice of
\chi
.
∎

4.2.
Shift invariance

For
\bm{\beta}\in\Sigma^{n_{0}}
, define
\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n):=U^{-n}\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}
and
\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(-n):=U^{n}\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{-n}
which represent a “forward shift” and “backwards shift” of the quantum partition element. Similarly to
[
3
, Proposition 4.1]
and
[
27
, Lemma 3.14]
, we have a more general statement for the invariance under the shift:

Proposition
4.2.1

(Approximate shift-invariance for logarithmic modes)
.

Consider an
\varepsilon
-logarithmic family
\{\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
. Fix some
n_{0}\geq 0
,
\delta_{Ehr}>0
, and
\varepsilon_{slab}>0
. Then, there exists
\hbar_{0}(n_{0},\varepsilon,\delta_{Ehr},\varepsilon_{slab},K)
such that for all
\hbar\leq\hbar_{0}
, any
\bm{\beta}\in\Sigma^{n_{0}}
, and
n
in the range
[0,2T_{\delta_{Ehr},\varepsilon_{slab},\hbar}-n_{0}]
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{n}}\left\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)\left[\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(n-1)\dots\Pi_{\alpha_{0}}(0)\right]\Psi_{\hbar}\right\|^{2}=\langle\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar},U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\rangle+\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(n\cdot\hbar^{\delta_{Ehr}})

\displaystyle=\mu_{\hbar}\left([\cdot\bm{\beta}]\right)+F_{1}(\hbar,\bm{\beta},\varepsilon,n)+G(\hbar,\bm{\beta},n),

(4.2.2)

where

\displaystyle F_{1}(\hbar,\bm{\beta},\varepsilon,n)=\langle|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}|^{2}\Psi_{\hbar},\Phi_{\hbar}[n]\rangle+\langle|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}|^{2}\Phi_{\hbar}[n],\Psi_{\hbar}\rangle+\langle|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}|^{2}\Phi_{\hbar}[n],\Phi_{\hbar}[n]\rangle,

(4.2.3)

\Phi_{\hbar}[n](x):=\frac{-i}{\hbar}\int_{0}^{n}\left(P(\hbar)-E\right)U^{s}e^{isE/\hbar}\Psi_{\hbar}\,ds,

and

\displaystyle G(\hbar,\bm{\beta},\varepsilon,n):=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Bigg[\sum_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{j-1}}

\displaystyle\Big[\langle R_{\hbar}|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)|^{2}\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\hbar},\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\hbar}\rangle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha_{j-1}\in\Sigma}\langle\Pi_{\alpha_{j-1}}(j-1)^{*}\left[|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)|^{2},\Pi_{\alpha_{j-1}}(j-1)\right]\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\hbar},\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\hbar}\rangle\Big]\Bigg]=\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(n\cdot\hbar^{\delta_{Ehr}})

with
R_{\hbar}
being a smoothing operator that is independent of
n
and the set of words
\Sigma^{0}
being the empty set. Finally,

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\beta}}F_{1}(\hbar,\bm{\beta},\varepsilon,n)=\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{\infty})

in the given parameter range. Lastly,

\displaystyle|F_{1}(\hbar,\bm{\beta},\varepsilon,n)|\leq 2\||\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}|^{2}\Psi_{\hbar}\|\cdot\frac{\varepsilon n}{|\log\hbar|}+\left(\frac{\varepsilon n}{|\log\hbar|}\right)^{2}.

A similar formula, up to errors in
\hbar
, holds for the “backwards” quantities:

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{n}}\left\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(-n)\left[\Pi_{\alpha_{-n}}(-n)\dots\Pi_{\alpha_{-1}}(-1)\right]\Psi_{\hbar}\right\|^{2}=\langle\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{-n}\Psi_{\hbar},U^{-n}\Psi_{\hbar}\rangle

with the formula equating this to
\tilde{\mu}_{\hbar}(\bm{\beta})
having the defect
\tilde{F}_{1}(\bullet)
and errors of the same magnitude in
\hbar
.

Our proposition should be contrasted against the shift-invariance statement in Anantharaman’s work
[
1
, Proposition 1.3.1]
and Nonnenmacher’s exposition
[
27
, Lemma 3.14]
. Moreover, this proposition holds for more general quasimodes of width
\varepsilon\frac{\hbar}{|\log\hbar|}
that microlocalize around
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

We proceed almost exactly the same as in
[
27
, Lemma 3.14]
until the last few steps. We will do the calculation for the forward measure as that for the backwards measure is identical. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{n}}\langle|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)|^{2}\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar},\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\rangle=\sum_{\bm{\alpha^{\prime}}\in\Sigma^{n-1}}\sum_{\alpha_{n-1}\in\Sigma}\left(\langle\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(n-1)^{*}|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)|^{2}\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(n-1)\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\hbar},\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\hbar}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{n-1}}\sum_{\alpha_{n-1}\in\Sigma}\langle\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(n-1)^{*}\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(n-1)|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)|^{2}\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{n-1}}\Psi_{\hbar},\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}\in\Sigma}\Psi_{\hbar}\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{n-1}}\sum_{\alpha_{n-1}\in\Sigma}\langle\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(n-1)^{*}\left[|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)|^{2},\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(n-1)\right]\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\hbar},\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\hbar}\rangle

(4.2.4)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{n-1}}\langle|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)|^{2}\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\hbar},\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\hbar}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(\hbar^{\delta_{Ehr}})\cdot\sum_{\alpha_{n-1}\in\Sigma^{1}}\|\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(n-1)\|\right)+K^{n-1}\cdot\mathcal{O}\left(\hbar^{\infty}\right)+K\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\nu,K}(\hbar^{\infty})

(4.2.5)

The error terms in the final line is deduced as follows in the next two paragraphs.

First, we use that
\sum_{\alpha_{n-1}}\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(n-1)\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(n-1)^{*}=\tilde{S}_{n}+R_{\hbar}
, thanks to the proof of Proposition
B.2.4
found in
[
27
, Proposition 3.1]
and where
\tilde{S}_{n}
satisfies the same properties as
S_{n}
. Apply the microlocalization property for logarithmic modes Lemma
3.1.1
; see
[
3
, Remark 2.4]
for why
\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\hbar}
continues to be microlocalized near
\mathcal{E}
at scale
\hbar^{\delta_{Ehr}}
. In (
4.2.5
), this explains the first and third terms of
\sum_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{n-1}}\langle|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)|^{2}\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\hbar},\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\hbar}\rangle
and
K^{n-1}\cdot\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{\infty})
, respectively.

We now work from the second term in (
4.2.4
) to second and fourth terms in (
4.2.5
). The assumption that
n+n_{0}\leq\lfloor 2T_{\delta_{Ehr},\varepsilon_{slab},\hbar}\rfloor
is crucial in estimating our commutator

\displaystyle\left[|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)|^{2},\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(n-1)\right]

\displaystyle=U^{-n/2}\left(|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}|^{2}(\frac{n}{2})U^{n/2}U^{-n/2}\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(\frac{n}{2}-1)-\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(\frac{n}{2}-1)U^{n/2}U^{-n/2}|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}|^{2}(\frac{n}{2})\right)U^{n/2};

the estimate now follows from an application of Egorov’s Theorem on the operator

\displaystyle|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}|^{2}(\frac{n}{2})\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(\frac{n}{2}-1)-\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}(\frac{n}{2}-1)|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}|^{2}(\frac{n}{2}),

and the invariance of norms under conjugation by
U^{-n/2}
. Perform the following steps: sum first in
\alpha_{n-1}
in order gain the term
\sum_{\alpha_{n-1}\in\Sigma}\|\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}\|
, and then use that
\sum_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\|\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}=1+\mathcal{O}_{\nu,K}(\hbar^{\infty})
à la Lemma
4.1.3
; this leads to the last displayed term of
K\cdot\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{\infty})
.

We can repeat this procedure
n-1
more times to obtain that
\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{n}}\langle|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)|^{2}\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar},\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\rangle
equals

\displaystyle\langle|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}|^{2}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar},U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\rangle

\displaystyle+n\cdot\left(\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(\hbar^{\delta_{Ehr}})\cdot\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{1}}\|\Pi_{\alpha}\|\right)+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}K^{j}\right)\cdot\mathcal{O}\left(\hbar^{\infty}\right)+K\cdot n\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\nu,K}\left(\hbar^{\infty}\right).

Lastly, we use the simple operator formula of

\displaystyle U^{n}-e^{-inE/\hbar}Id=\frac{-i}{\hbar}\int_{0}^{n}\left(P(\hbar)-E\right)U^{s}e^{isE/\hbar}e^{-inE/\hbar}ds

giving a bound on
F_{1}
in terms of
\varepsilon
for
\hbar\leq\hbar_{0}
small enough.

Note that

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+G(\hbar,\bm{\beta},n)=\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+F_{1}(\hbar,\bm{\beta},\varepsilon,n)+G(\hbar,\bm{\beta},n).

(4.2.6)

The equation
\sum_{\bm{\beta}}F_{1}(\hbar,\bm{\beta},\varepsilon,n)=\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{\infty})
now follows immediately from Proposition
B.2.4
applied to both sides of (
4.2.6
), Lemma
3.1.1
applied to
U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}
as
U^{n}
is unitary, and our states being normalized. Finally, we remind ourselves that
\sum_{\bm{\beta}}\langle|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}|^{2}\Phi_{\hbar},\Phi_{\hbar}\rangle=\langle\Phi_{\hbar},\Phi_{\hbar}\rangle\leq\frac{2\varepsilon n}{|\log\hbar|}+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{\infty})
with each summand itself being real and positive.
∎

4.3.
A remark on the continuity of
F_{1}

Consider Proposition
4.2.1
. Suppose that given
\overline{\varepsilon}>0
, there exists a possibly smaller
\hbar_{0}
and a
\overline{\delta}(\overline{\varepsilon})>0
such that for all quasiprojectors
\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}
where
\mu_{\hbar}(\bm{\beta})<\overline{\delta}
when
\hbar\leq\hbar_{0}
and
\bm{\beta}\in\Sigma^{n_{0}}
, we have that

\sup_{n\leq\lfloor 2T_{\delta_{Ehr},\varepsilon_{slab},\hbar}\rfloor-n_{0}}|F_{1}(\hbar,\bm{\beta},\varepsilon,n)|<\overline{\varepsilon}.

This statement is trivial when
\varepsilon=o(1)
in
\hbar
. Moreover, it is simply the limiting definition of absolute continuity of measures. Such a statement appears to require a knowledge of the asymptotics of
\mu_{\hbar}
away from the support of
\mu_{sc}
, but contained in
\mathcal{E}
, for which our current microlocal technology does not penetrate. A previous version of this manuscript assumed this continuity-type condition on
F_{1}
. The author was later informed by Stéphane Nonnenmacher that the assumption fails for the toy model of the quantum cat map, making a stronger case to state our Theorem
1.2.1
as generally as possible.

5.
Quantum entropies and the entropic uncertainty principle

5.1.
Definitions: Quantum entropies and pressures

As mentioned at the top of Section
4
, we urge the readers who are not fluent with the machinery found in
[
3
,
2
]
to carefully read Appendix
B
to familiarize themselves with the notions of classical metric entropy of a measure with respect to a partition
H_{0}^{n-1}(\bullet)
, entropy of a measure with respect to a smooth partition
H_{0}^{n-1}(\bullet)
(a convenient abuse of notation), and classical/quantum pressure functionals
p^{n-1}_{0}(\bullet)
. We give a brief introduction to the quantum versions of entropy and pressure now:

Definition
5.1.1
.

Given an
L^{2}
-normalized quantum state
\Psi_{\hbar}
, a smooth quantum partition
\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm},\rm{q}}
, and an associated set of positive weights
\bm{w}
, we define the
quantum entropy
as

H_{0}^{n-1}(\Psi_{\hbar},\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm,q}}):=\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{n}}\eta\left(\|\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}\right)

and the
quantum pressure
as

p_{0}^{n-1}(\Psi_{\hbar},\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm,q}},\bm{w}):=\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{n}}\eta\left(\|\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}\right)-\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{n}}\|\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}\log\left(\bm{w}_{\bm{\alpha}}^{2}\right).

We suppress the mention of
\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm,q}}
in our quantum entropies/pressures until the end of our argument when both the smoothing parameter
\eta
and the diameter
\zeta
of the initial partition
\mathcal{P}
go to 0.

5.2.
Entropic uncertainty principle

The following proposition is a recording of
[
27
, Proposition 3.12]
and forms a crucial component of the final stages of the proof of Theorem
1.2.1
. We keep the more abstract form of the statement for a cleaner exposition. Towards the end of this section is it explained why we work with spectrally localized modes rather than more general ones of logarithmic width. We suggest readers familiarize themselves with the hyperbolic dispersive estimate of Anantharaman-Nonnenmacher in Appendix
C
before applying the following proposition:

Proposition
5.2.1

(Entropic uncertainty principle for microlocal weighted partitions)
.

On a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, consider two approximate quantum partitions of unity of bounded operators
\bm{\rho}=\{\rho_{i},\,i\in I\}
and
\bm{\tau}=\{\tau_{j},\,j\in J\}
satisfying the identities

\sum_{i\in I}\rho_{i}^{*}\rho_{i}=S_{\bm{\rho}},\,\,\,\sum_{j\in J}\tau_{j}^{*}\tau_{j}=S_{\bm{\tau}},

and two families of weights
\bm{v}=\{v_{i},i\in I\}
,
\bm{w}=\{w_{j},j\in J\}
satisfying
V^{-1}\leq v_{i},w_{j}\leq V
for some
V\geq 1
.

Assume that for some
0\leq\mathscr{R}\leq\min\{|I|^{-2}V^{-2},|J|^{-2}V^{-2}\}
, the above sum of operators satisfies
0\leq\|S_{\bm{\rho}}\|_{op},\|S_{\bm{\tau}}\|_{op}\leq 1+\mathscr{R}
. Furthermore, let
S_{c_{1}},S_{c_{2}}
be two Hermitian operators on
\mathcal{H}
satisfying
0\leq\|S_{c_{1}}\|_{op},\|S_{c_{2}}\|_{op}\leq 1+\mathscr{R}
and the relations

\displaystyle\|\left(S_{c_{2}}-\rm{Id})\rho_{i}\,S_{c_{1}}\right)\|_{op}\leq\mathscr{R},\,\mbox{ for }i\in I

\displaystyle\|\left(S_{\bm{\rho}}-\rm{Id})\,S_{c_{1}}\right)\|_{op}\leq\mathscr{R},\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\|\left(S_{\bm{\tau}}-\rm{Id})\,S_{c_{1}}\right)\|_{op}\leq\mathscr{R}.

Let us set

c_{\rm{cone}}:=\max_{i\in I,j\in J}v_{i}w_{j}\|\tau_{j}\rho_{i}^{*}S_{c_{2}}\|_{op}.

For any
\Psi\in\mathcal{H}
normalized such that
\|(\rm{Id}-S_{c_{1}})\Psi\|_{op}\leq\mathscr{R}
, the quantum pressures with respect to the weighted partitions
(\bm{\rho},\bm{v})
and
(\bm{\tau},\bm{w})
satisfy the bound

p(\Psi,\bm{\rho},\bm{v})+p(\Psi,\bm{\tau},\bm{w})\geq-2\log\left(c_{\rm{cone}}+3|I|V^{2}\mathscr{R}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\mathscr{R}^{1/5}\right)

with the implicit constant in the big-O notation being independent of the weighted partitions or cutoff operators
S_{c_{1}},S_{c_{2}}
.

The formula for
c_{\rm{cone}}
determines our choice of quantum partitions and weights; in particular, we need that
\tau_{j}\rho_{i}^{*}S_{c_{2}}
be equal to
\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}U^{n}\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{-n}\chi^{(n)}(P(\hbar)-E)
(see Proposition
C.0.5
) where
|\bm{\alpha}|=|\bm{\beta}|=n
and
n=\lfloor 2T_{\delta_{Ehr},\varepsilon_{slab},\hbar}\rfloor
. The following list sets the remaining choices in place:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\bm{\tau}=\{\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\,|\,|\bm{\alpha}|=n\}\\
&\bm{\rho}=\{U^{-n}\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}^{*}U^{n}\,|\,|\bm{\beta}|=n\}\\
&\bm{v}=\bm{w}=\{w_{\bm{\beta}}:=\sqrt{J_{n}^{u}(\bm{\beta})}\,|\,|\bm{\beta}|=n\}\\
&S_{c_{1}}=\chi^{(0)}(P(\hbar)-E),\,S_{c_{2}}=\chi^{(n)}(P(\hbar)-E)\end{cases}

(5.2.2)

The formulas for the weights are given in Definition
C.0.4
which immediately implies
\sqrt{J_{2n}^{u}(\bm{\alpha}\bm{\beta})}=\sqrt{J_{n}^{u}(\bm{\beta})}\sqrt{J_{n}^{u}(\bm{\alpha})}
. Moreover,
|I|=|J|=K^{n}
with
\sqrt{J_{n}^{u}(\bm{\beta})}\leq e^{n\lambda_{\rm{max}}(d-1)/2}
. Note that thanks to Proposition
C.0.5
,

c_{\rm{cone}}\leq Ch^{-(d-1+c_{0}\delta_{long})/2}

for all
\hbar
sufficiently small. For our
\varepsilon
-logarithmic modes
\Psi=\Psi_{\hbar}
, we can set
\mathscr{R}=\hbar^{L}
for
L
sufficiently large thanks to our microlocalization property Lemma
3.1.1
. Therefore, for
\hbar\leq\hbar_{0}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}},\delta_{long},\delta_{Ehr},\varepsilon_{slab}\right)
, we have established a bound of

p_{0}^{n-1}(\Psi_{\hbar},\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm,q}},\bm{v})+p_{-n}^{-1}(\Psi_{\hbar},\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}}^{*},\bm{w})\geq-(d-1+c_{0}\delta_{long})|\log\hbar|-2\log C+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{L/3})

where
\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}}^{*}
is the set of adjoints of operators in
\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}}
, the implicit constant is independent of
n
and the power of
L/3
arising from our bounds on
V
and
|I|=K^{n}
. Recall that
\delta_{long}=\delta_{Ehr}
in Section
4
.

Remark 5.2.3
.

Here we see the reasoning behind choosing spectrally localized modes (or more generally projections
\chi\left(\frac{|\log\hbar|(P(\hbar)-E)}{\hbar}\right)f
where
f\in L^{2}(M)
): to have
\mathscr{R}
small enough to dominate the growth of
V^{2}=\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{-(d-1)})
, as dictated by the choice of weights
w_{\bullet},v_{\bullet}
, and make the corresponding product smaller than
c_{\rm{cone}}
. Were
\{\Psi_{\hbar}\}_{\hbar}
a more general logarithmic family, then
\mathscr{R}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\hbar}{|\log\hbar|}\right)
is our best current estimate and this is insufficient.

6.1.
Sub-additivity of quantum entropies

Lemma
6.1.1

(Approximate sub-additivity of quantum entropies)
.

Fix some
n_{0}\geq 0
,
\delta_{Ehr}\in(0,1)
, and
\varepsilon_{slab}
. Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
. Given
\varepsilon
-logarithmic modes
\Psi_{\hbar}
, there exists
\hbar_{0}(\varepsilon,K,n_{0},\delta_{Ehr},\varepsilon_{slab},\varepsilon^{\prime})
such that for all
\hbar\leq\hbar_{0}
, any
\bm{\beta}\in\Sigma^{n_{0}}
, and integers
n
in the range
[0,2T_{\delta_{Ehr},\varepsilon_{slab},\hbar}-n_{0}]
we have the estimate

\displaystyle H_{0}^{n+n_{0}-1}(\Psi_{\hbar})\leq-\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=n}\eta\left(\|\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})\right)+H_{0}^{n_{0}-1}(U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar})+R_{1}\left(\hbar,n,n_{0}\right)

where

\displaystyle R_{1}(\hbar,n,n_{0}){}:={}

\displaystyle-\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|=n_{0}}\left(\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+G(\hbar,\beta,\varepsilon,n)\right)\log\left(\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+G(\hbar,\beta,\varepsilon,n)+\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(\hbar^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})\right)

\displaystyle-H^{n_{0}-1}_{0}(U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(\hbar^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}).

Here,
G
is as in Proposition
4.2.1
. A similar formula holds for
H_{-1}^{-n-n_{0}}(\Psi_{\hbar})
.

Proof.

First, we keep in mind

\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|=n_{0}}\left(\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+G(\hbar,\beta,\varepsilon,n)\right)=1+\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(\hbar^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})

thanks to the shift-invariance statement in Proposition
3.1.1
. We write

\displaystyle H_{0}^{n+n_{0}-1}(\Psi_{\hbar})=

\displaystyle-\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=n}\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|=n_{0}}\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}\,\times

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}}{\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+G(\hbar,\beta,\varepsilon,n)+\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(\hbar^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})}\cdot\left(\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+G(\hbar,\beta,\varepsilon,n)+\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(\hbar^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\right)\right).

Using the homomorphism property of logarithms and again re-expressing 1 in terms of
\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+G(\hbar,\beta,\varepsilon,n)+\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(\hbar^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})
we arrive at

\displaystyle-\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=n}\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|=n_{0}}\left(\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+G(\hbar,\beta,\varepsilon,n)+\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(\hbar^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})\right)\frac{\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}}{\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+G(\hbar,\beta,\varepsilon,n)+\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(\hbar^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})}\times

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}}{\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+G(\hbar,\beta,\varepsilon,n)+\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(\hbar^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=n}\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|=n_{0}}\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}\,\log\left(\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+G(\hbar,\beta,\varepsilon,n)+\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(\hbar^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})\right)

(6.1.2)

for all
\hbar\leq\hbar_{0}
small enough.

On the first summation in (
6.1.2
), we use that
\eta(\bullet)
is a convex function and apply Jensen’s Inequality in
\bm{\beta}
, resulting in it being less than or equal to

\displaystyle-\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=n}\eta\left(\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|=n_{0}}\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}(n)\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}\right)=-\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=n}\eta\left(\|\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(h^{\infty})\right)

after using Proposition
B.2.4
via summing in
\bm{\beta}
index. For the second summation, we sum in
\bm{\alpha}
first as in the proof of Lemma
4.2.1
, leading us to an equality with

\displaystyle-\sum_{|\bm{\beta}|=n_{0}}\left(\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+G(\hbar,\beta,\varepsilon,n)\right)\log\left(\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+G(\hbar,\beta,\varepsilon,n)+\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(\hbar^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})\right)

\displaystyle=H_{0}^{n_{0}-1}(U^{n}\Psi_{\hbar})+R_{1}(\hbar,n,n_{0}).

Recall that
G=\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(n\hbar^{\delta_{Ehr}})
. Consider the inequality
|\eta(s+s^{\prime})-\eta(s)|\leq\max\{-\log(s)-\log(1+\frac{s^{\prime}}{s}),-\log(s)\}|s^{\prime}|
as
|s^{\prime}|<s
. These two facts combined retrieve that
R_{1}=\mathcal{O}(K^{n_{0}}\eta(n\hbar^{\delta_{Ehr}}))=\mathcal{O}_{n_{0}}(\hbar^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})
for
\hbar_{0}
possibly smaller.
∎

\varepsilon^{\prime}>0

\varepsilon

j\leq q

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bm{\beta}\in\mathfrak{L}_{-}\cap B\cup\mathfrak{L}^{\complement}_{-}\cap B}\log(1-b+be^{-n_{0}(H_{0}-\delta/3)})\cdot(-\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{sc}(\varepsilon,\pi_{\bm{\beta}},n_{0}))\leq 0

but is convergent for all

sufficiently large thanks to
|F_{1}(\bullet)|\leq\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{jn_{0}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}+\|\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}\Psi_{\hbar}\|^{2}
. Once again as we do not have a satisfactory estimate on this functional, we settle for the trivial bound of 0. The final region of words

gives

\displaystyle D_{6}(\delta,\overline{\varepsilon},\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}},n_{0},H_{0},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle:=\limsup_{\hbar}\sum_{\bm{\beta}\in\mathfrak{L}^{\complement}_{-}\cap B^{\complement}}q^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}I(\hbar,\bm{\beta},j,n_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bm{\beta}\in\mathfrak{L}^{\complement}_{-}\cap B^{\complement}}\log(1-b+\max\{be^{-n_{0}(H_{0}-\delta/3)},\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{\prime})\})(-\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{sc}(\varepsilon,\pi_{\bm{\beta}},n_{0}))\leq 0.

Finally, we collect the positive functionals and subsequently define the pre-limit remainder functional

\displaystyle R(\delta,\overline{\varepsilon},\mathcal{P}_{sm},n_{0},H_{0},\varepsilon):=D_{2}+D_{3}+D_{4}

(8.2.2)

and write

\displaystyle\bm{R}(H_{0},\varepsilon):=\sup_{\mathcal{P}}\lim_{\zeta\rightarrow 0}\lim_{\overline{\varepsilon}}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}R(\delta,\overline{\varepsilon},\mathcal{P}_{sm},n_{0},H_{0},\varepsilon).

(8.2.3)

We sum up our findings with

Proposition
8.2.4
.

Given
\overline{\varepsilon}>0
, there exists
\delta(\overline{\varepsilon})
and
N_{0}(\overline{\varepsilon},\delta,\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}})
such that for all

\displaystyle\limsup_{\hbar}D(\hbar,n_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bm{\beta}\in\mathfrak{L}_{+}\cap B}-\log(ae^{-(H_{\max}-2\delta/5)n_{0}}+be^{-(H_{\max}+\delta/5)n_{0}})\,\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{sc}(\varepsilon,\pi_{\bm{\beta}},n_{0})+R(\delta,\overline{\varepsilon},\mathcal{P}_{sm},n_{0},H_{0},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq(a+b)\,(H_{\max}+\frac{\delta}{5})n_{0}\sum_{\bm{\beta}\in\mathfrak{L}_{+}\cap B}\,\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{sc}(\varepsilon,\pi_{\bm{\beta}},n_{0})

\displaystyle+\sum_{\bm{\beta}\in\mathfrak{L}_{+}\cap B^{\complement}}-\log(ae^{-(H_{0}-2\delta/5)n_{0}}+b\mu_{sc,\rm{H}})\,\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{sc}(\varepsilon,\pi_{\bm{\beta}},n_{0})

\displaystyle+\sum_{\bm{\beta}\in\mathfrak{L}^{\complement}_{+}\cap B}-\log(a\mu_{sc,\rm{L}}+be^{-(H_{\max}+\delta/5)n_{0}})\,\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{sc}(\varepsilon,\pi_{\bm{\beta}},n_{0})

\displaystyle+D_{4}.

(8.2.5)

We make note that the calculations were done for the forward quantum measure

superscript as before.

We remind ourselves that

Appendix A
On the semiclassical calculus

In this appendix we recall the concepts and definitions from semiclassical analysis needed in our work. The notations are drawn from the monographs
[
13
,
39
]
as well as
[
3
]
.

A.1.
Semiclassical calculus on
M

Recall that we define on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
the following class of symbols for
m\in{\mathbb{R}}
:

\displaystyle S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}):=\{a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times(0,1]):|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}a|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-|\beta|}\}.

(A.1.1)

Symbols in this class can be quantized through the
\hbar
-Weyl quantization into the following pseudodifferential operators acting on
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
:

{\rm{Op}_{\hbar}}^{w}(a)\,u(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi\hbar)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}\,a\big(\frac{x+y}{2},\xi;\hbar\big)\,u(y)dyd\xi\,.

(A.1.2)

One can adapt this quantization procedure to the case of the phase space
T^{*}M
, where
M
is a smooth compact manifold of dimension
d
(without boundary). Consider a smooth atlas
(f_{l},V_{l})_{l=1,\ldots,L}
of
M
, where each
f_{l}
is a smooth diffeomorphism from
V_{l}\subset M
to a bounded open set
W_{l}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. To each
f_{l}
corresponds a pullback
f_{l}^{*}:C^{\infty}(W_{l})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(V_{l})
and a symplectic diffeomorphism
\tilde{f}_{l}
from
T^{*}V_{l}
to
T^{*}W_{l}
:

\tilde{f}_{l}:(x,\xi)\mapsto\left(f_{l}(x),(Df_{l}(x)^{-1})^{T}\xi\right).

Consider now a smooth partition of unity
(\varphi_{l})
adapted to the previous atlas
(f_{l},V_{l})
.
That means
\sum_{l}\varphi_{l}=1
and
\varphi_{l}\in C^{\infty}(V_{l})
. Then, any observable
a
in
C^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
can be decomposed as:
a=\sum_{l}a_{l}
, where

a_{l}=a\varphi_{l}
. Each
a_{l}
belongs to
C^{\infty}(T^{*}V_{l})
and can be pushed to a function
\tilde{a}_{l}=(\tilde{f}_{l}^{-1})^{*}a_{l}\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}W_{l})
.
We may now define the class of symbols of order
m
on
T^{*}M
(after slightly abusing notation and treating
(x,\xi)
as coordinates on
T^{*}W_{l}
)

\displaystyle S^{m}(T^{*}M):=\{a\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}M\times(0,1]):a=\sum_{l}a_{l},\ \text{ such that }\tilde{a}_{l}\in S^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})\quad\text{for each }l\}.

This class is independent of the choice of atlas or smooth partition.
For any
a\in S^{m}(T^{*}M)
, one can associate to each component
\tilde{a}_{l}\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
its Weyl quantization
{\rm{Op}_{\hbar}}^{w}(\tilde{a}_{l})
, which acts on functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}
.
To get back to operators acting on
M
, we consider smooth cutoffs
\psi_{l}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(V_{l})
such that
\psi_{l}=1
close to the support of
\varphi_{l}
, and define the operator:

{\rm{Op}_{\hbar}}(a)u:=\sum_{l}\psi_{l}\times\left(f_{l}^{*}{\rm{Op}}_{\hbar}^{w}(\tilde{a}_{l})(f_{l}^{-1})^{*}\right)\left(\psi_{l}\times u\right),\quad u\in C^{\infty}(M)\,.

(A.1.3)

This quantization procedure maps (modulo smoothing operators with seminorms
\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{\infty})
) symbols
a\in S^{m}(T^{*}M)
onto the space
\Psi^{m}_{\hbar}(M)
of semiclassical pseudodifferential
operators of order
m
. The dependence in the cutoffs
\varphi_{l}
and
\psi_{l}
only appears at order

\hbar\Psi^{m-1}_{\hbar}

[
39
, Thm 9.10]
), so that the principal symbol map
\sigma_{0}:\Psi^{m}_{\hbar}(M)\rightarrow S^{m}(T^{*}M)/\hbar S^{m-1}(T^{*}M)
is
intrinsically defined. Most of the rules and microlocal properties (for example the composition of operators, the Egorov and Calderón-Vaillancourt Theorems) that hold on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be extended to the manifold case.

An important example of a pseudodifferential operator is the semiclassical Laplace-Beltrami operator
P(\hbar)=-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{g}
. In local coordinates
(x;\xi)
on
T^{*}M
, the operator can be expressed as
{\rm{Op}}_{h}^{w}\big(|\xi|^{2}_{g}+\hbar(\sum_{j}b_{j}(x)\xi_{j}+c(x))+\hbar^{2}d(x)\big)
for some functions
b_{j},c,d
on
M
. In particular, its semiclassical principal symbol is the function
|\xi|^{2}_{g}\in S^{2}(T^{*}M)
.

We will need to consider a slightly more general class of symbols than those in (
A.1.1
). Following
[
13
]
, for any
0\leq\nu<1/2
we introduce the symbol class

\displaystyle S^{m,k}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}):=\{a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times(0,1]):|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}a|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\hbar^{-k-\nu|\alpha+\beta|}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-|\beta|}\}.

These symbols are allowed to oscillate more strongly when
\hbar\to 0
.
All the previous remarks regarding the case of
\nu=0
transfer over in a straightforward manner. This slightly “exotic” class of symbols can be adapted on
T^{*}M
as well. For more details, see
[
39
, Section 14.2]
.

Appendix B
Entropy, Pressure, and Quasiprojectors

The material in this appendix is a summary of
[
27
, Section 3]
. For more information on entropy for dynamical systems, see the classical texts of
[
21
]
and
[
6
, Chapter 9]
. Throughout,
g^{t}:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}
is the geodesic flow on the energy shell
\mathcal{E}
.

B.1.
Classical entropy and pressure

B.1.1.
Classical entropy

Definition
B.1.1
.

Let
\mu
be a
g^{t}
-invariant probability measure on
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{P}=\{E_{1},\dots,E_{K}\}
be a
\mu
-measurable partition on
\mathcal{E}
. We say the
(metric) entropy
of
\mu
with respect to
\mathcal{P}
is the quantity

H(\mu,\mathcal{P}):=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\eta(\mu\left(E_{k}\right))\mbox{ where }\eta(s):=-s\log s\mbox{ and }s\in[0,1]

and call
\eta
the
local (metric) entropy at
s
.

It is important to emphasize that in our article, we first take an open cover
\{\mathscr{O}_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k^{\prime}}
of
M
with
M=\sqcup_{k=1}^{K}E^{\prime}_{k}
, a partition consisting of Borel sets
E_{k}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{k}^{\prime}
. Next, we take the elements
E_{k}
as
\pi^{-1}(E^{\prime}_{k})
where
\pi:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow M
is the canonical projection. In order to compute what is referred to as the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy of
\mu
, we first need to consider dynamical refinements
\mathcal{P}^{\vee n}
(or alternatively written as
[\mathcal{P}]_{0}^{n-1}
) where
n\geq 1
and whose elements take the form

E_{\bm{\alpha}}:=g^{-(n-1)}E_{\alpha_{n-1}}\cap\dots g^{-1}E_{\alpha_{1}}\cap E_{\alpha_{0}}

where

\displaystyle\bm{\alpha}:=\alpha_{0}\dots\alpha_{n-1}\in\Sigma^{n}:=\{1,\dots,K\}^{n}

is a
word of length
n
with symbols
\alpha_{k}\in\{1,\dots,K\}
. Note that many such
E_{\bm{\alpha}}
will be empty however we continue to sum over all possible words of length
n
when computing the metric entropies. Moreover, we write
[\mathcal{P}]_{-n}^{-1}
to be the refinement with elements

g^{n}E_{\bm{\alpha}}:=g^{n}E_{\alpha_{-n}}\cap\dots\cap g^{1}E_{\alpha_{-1}}.

The elements
E_{\bm{\alpha}}
represent points
\rho
in
E_{\alpha_{0}}
such that
g^{j}(\rho)\in E_{\alpha_{j}}
for
j\leq n-1
with a similar description for
g^{n}E_{\bm{\alpha}}
. With all this notation in place, if the initial partition
\mathcal{P}
is unambiguous, we simply write

H_{0}^{n-1}(\mu):=H\left(\mu,\mathcal{P}^{\vee n}\right).

Definition
B.1.2
.

Given an invariant probability measure
\mu
, the
Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy
H_{KS}(\mu)
is the number

H_{\rm{KS}}(\mu):=\sup_{\mathcal{P}}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{0}^{n-1}(\mu)<\infty.

For an Anosov flow, the existence of a partition
\mathcal{P}
that obtains this supremum is guaranteed
[
27
, Section 3.1.1]
.

B.1.2.
Classical pressure

Given our
g^{t}
-invariant probability measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{E}
and a partition
\mathcal{P}=\{E_{1},\dots,E_{K}\}
, we can associate a set of weights
\bm{w}=\{w_{i}>0:k=1,\dots,K\}
to this pairing. This leads to the notion of pressure.

Definition
B.1.3
.

We define the
pressure
as

p(\mu,\mathcal{P},\bm{w}):=-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mu(E_{k})\log\left(w_{k}^{2}\,\mu(E_{k})\right).

.

This bears the question of how to define our weights when the partition
\mathcal{P}
is refined via the dynamics of
g^{t}
. Thus, we introduce the next definition:

Definition
B.1.4
.

Given a set of weights
\bm{w}=\{w_{i}>0:k=1,\dots,K\}
associated to a partition
\mathcal{P}
, we define the
n-th refinement
of
\bm{w}
as the set consisting of the elements

w_{\bm{\alpha}}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}w_{\alpha_{j}},\,\mbox{ with }\,|\bm{\alpha}|=n,

where
w_{\bm{\alpha}}
is attached to the element
E_{\bm{\alpha}}\in\mathcal{P}^{\vee n}
. Thus, we write the pressure associated to the partition
\mathcal{P}^{\vee n}
and these refined weights as

\displaystyle p_{0}^{n-1}(\mu,\mathcal{P},\bm{w}).

B.1.3.
Entropy of smooth partitions

First let
\mathcal{P}=\{E_{1},\dots,E_{K}\}
be the
\mu
-measurable partition of
\mathcal{E}
discussed directly after Definition
B.1.1
. Let
\mathbf{1}_{k}
be the characteristic function on
E_{k}
and define

\mathbf{1}_{\bm{\alpha}}:=\mathbf{1}_{\alpha_{n-1}}\circ g^{n-1}\times\dots\times\mathbf{1}_{\alpha_{1}}\circ g^{1}\times\mathbf{1}_{\alpha_{0}}

(B.1.5)

which is the characteristic function of
E_{\bm{\alpha}}
. Note
\mu(E_{\bm{\alpha}})=\int_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{1}_{\bm{\alpha}}\,d\mu
. This leads us to our next definition:

Definition
B.1.6
.

Given any
\varepsilon_{slab}>0
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon_{slab}}=\{(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M\,|\,E-\varepsilon_{slab}\leq\|\xi\|_{g(x)}\leq E+\varepsilon_{slab}\}.

There exists a family of functions
\{\pi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
and neighbourhoods
\{\mathscr{O}_{k}\}_{k}
in
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon_{slab}}
with the following properties:
\pi_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon_{slab}},[0,1])
,
{\operatorname{Supp\,}}\pi_{k}\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{k}
,
E_{k}\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{k}
with
\pi_{k\upharpoonright E_{k}}=1
, and

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_{k}:=\chi_{\varepsilon_{slab}}\mbox{ with }\,{\operatorname{Supp\,}}\chi_{\varepsilon_{slab}}\subset\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon_{slab}}\,\mbox{ and }\chi_{\varepsilon_{slab}}=1\mbox{ on }\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon_{slab}/2}.

(B.1.7)

We call such a family
\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}}:=\{\pi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
a
smooth partition of unity
near
\mathcal{E}
. Note we take the diameters of the
\mathscr{O}_{k}
less than the small parameter
\zeta
.

In fact, given our choice of
\mathcal{P}
and taking
\max_{k}\mbox{diam}(\mathscr{O}_{k}^{\prime})<\zeta
small so we can work in charts on
M
, one can set
\pi_{k}=f_{\eta}*\mathbf{1}_{E_{k}}
where
f_{\eta}
is a family of smooth, compactly supported, approximation-to-the-identities where
\lim_{\eta}f_{\eta}*\mathbf{1}_{E_{k}}=\mathbf{1}_{E_{k}}
. At the final steps of our argument, we take
\zeta\rightarrow 0
.

We can easily extend the notion of entropy to smooth partitions (useful in the context of semiclassical analysis) by simply setting
\mu=0
outside of
\mathcal{E}
:

Definition
B.1.8
.

Let
\varepsilon_{slab}>0
be given. Consider a corresponding smooth partition
\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}}
near
\mathcal{E}
and some weights
\bm{w}
. We define the
entropy of a smooth partition
\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}}
as

H(\mu,\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}}):=-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\eta(\mu(\pi_{k}))

(B.1.9)

and
pressure of a (weighted) smooth partition
\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}}

p(\mu,\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}},\bm{w}):=-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mu(\pi_{k})\log\left(w_{k}^{2}\,\mu(\pi_{k})\right).

(B.1.10)

The corresponding definitions of
\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}}^{\vee n}
,
H_{0}^{n-1}
, and
p_{0}^{n-1}
are clear.

Thanks to this setup and assuming that
\mu
does not charge the boundaries
\partial E_{k}
for all
k=1,\dots,K
, we have that given any
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
and
n\geq 1
, we can choose
\eta
small enough and thus
\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}}
such that

\left|H(\mu,\mathcal{P}^{\vee n}_{\rm sm})-H(\mu,\mathcal{P}^{\vee n})\right|\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}.

See
[
27
, Section 3.1.3]
for more details on the compatibility of this assumption with semiclassical measures
\mu_{sc}
, itself based off
[
1
, Appendix A2]
and
[
3
, Section 2.2.8]
.

B.2.
Quantum partitions

B.2.1.
Quasiprojectors

With Definition (
B.1.6
) in hand, we form a notion of an approximate quantum partition of unity:

Definition
B.2.1
.

An
approximate quantum partition of unity
is a collection
\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm},\rm{q}}=\{\Pi_{k}:={\rm{Op}}_{\hbar}(\tilde{\pi}_{k})\}_{k}
, whose individual elements
\Pi_{k}
we call
quasiprojectors
with corresponding symbols
\tilde{\pi}_{k}\in S^{-\infty,0}(T^{*}M)
that satisfy the following properties:

(1)

for each
k
, the full symbol
\tilde{\pi}_{k}
is constructed so that:
{\operatorname{Supp\,}}\tilde{\pi}_{k}\subset\mathscr{O}_{k}
,
\sqrt{\pi}_{k}
is the principal symbol, and
\Pi_{k}
is selfadjoint.

(2)

the family of operators
\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm},\rm{q}}
satisfies the relation

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Pi_{k}^{2}={\rm{Op}}_{\hbar}(\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{slab}})+\mathcal{O}_{K}(\hbar^{\infty})

(B.2.2)

where
\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{slab}}\in S^{-\infty,0}(T^{*}M)
itself satisfies

\|{\rm{Op}}_{\hbar}(\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{slab}})\|=1+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{\infty}),\,\,{\operatorname{Supp\,}}\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{slab}}\subset\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon_{slab}},\,\mbox{ and }\tilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon_{slab}}=1\mbox{ on }\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon_{slab}/2}.

Notice that
\Pi_{k}
cannot simply be
{\rm{Op}}_{\hbar}(\sqrt{\pi_{k}})
as we need lower order symbols in the expansion in order to guarantee property (
B.2.2
); otherwise, our error term would be
\mathcal{O}(h)
.

B.2.2.
Refined quantum partitions

Analogous to the refinements
\mathcal{P}^{\vee n}_{\rm sm}
of an initial smooth partition
\mathcal{P}_{\rm sm}
, we consider their quantum versions.

Definition
B.2.3
.

Consider the Schrödinger propagator
U=e^{-iP(\hbar)/\hbar}
. Then for a word
\bm{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{n}
, we define the refined (forward) quasiprojectors as

\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}:=U^{-(n-1)}\Pi_{\alpha_{n-1}}U\Pi_{\alpha_{n-2}}\dots\Pi_{\alpha_{1}}U\Pi_{\alpha_{0}}

The family
\mathcal{P}^{\vee n}_{sm,q}:=\{\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\}_{\bm{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{n}}
is called the
n
-th refinement of the quantum partition
\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm},\rm{q}}
. The definition for refined backwards quasiprojectors uses
U^{-1}
and is analogous.

For words
\bm{\alpha}
of length twice the “Ehrenfest time”, the quasiprojectors
\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}
fall outside of the scope of Egorov’s Theorem. Despite this, our refined quasiprojectors continue to form quantum partitions of unity, as encompassed in the next proposition.

Proposition
B.2.4

(Refined quasiprojectors form an approximate quantum partition of unity)
.

(cf
[
27
, Prop 3.1]
)
Let
\delta_{long}>0
be given and choose
C
such that
C+1<\delta_{long}^{-1}
. Then there exists a regime of
n
and
\hbar
such that the refined quantum partition
\mathcal{P}^{\vee n}_{sm,q}
continues to form a quantum partition of unity microlocally near
\mathcal{E}
. That is, for any symbol
\chi\in S^{-\infty,0}(T^{*}X)
supported inside of
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon_{slab}/2}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{n}}\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}=S_{n},\,\,\,\hfill\|S_{n}\|=1+\mathcal{O}_{K}(\hbar^{\infty})

\displaystyle(Id-S_{n})\,{\rm{Op}}_{\hbar}(\chi)=\mathcal{O}_{K}(\hbar^{\infty})

for all
\hbar\leq\hbar_{0}(\varepsilon_{slab},\delta_{long},K)
and
1\leq n\leq\lfloor C|\log\hbar|\rfloor
.

Appendix C
Ehrenfest time and the hyperbolic dispersive estimate

Definition
C.0.1
.

Let
\delta_{Ehr}\in(0,1),\varepsilon_{slab}>0,
and
\hbar>0
be given. We call

T_{\delta_{Ehr},\varepsilon_{slab},\hbar}:=\frac{(1-\delta_{Ehr})}{2\lambda_{\max}(\varepsilon_{slab})}|\log\hbar|

(C.0.2)

the
Ehrenfest time
on the energy neighbourhood
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon_{slab}}
. The number
\lambda_{\max}(\varepsilon_{slab})
is the maximal expansion rate across the entire slab
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon_{slab}}
. Note that for
\varepsilon_{slab}
small enough (depending on
M
),
\lambda_{\max}(\varepsilon_{slab})=\lambda_{\max}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon_{slab}).

Definition
C.0.3

(Coarse-grained unstable Jacobian)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a partition of
\mathcal{E}
. For
E_{\alpha_{j}}\in\mathcal{P}
, we define
J^{u}(\alpha_{j}):=\inf\{J^{u}(\rho)\,|\,\rho\in E_{\alpha_{j}}\}
. We call the quantity

J_{n}^{u}(\bm{\alpha}):=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}J^{u}(\alpha_{i})

(C.0.4)

the
coarse-grained unstable Jacobian at
\bm{\alpha}\in\Sigma^{n}
.

The following is from
[
27
, Corollary 3.7]
and
[
3
, Theorem 2.7]
:

Proposition
C.0.5

(Hyperbolic dispersive estimate)
.

Let
\delta_{Ehr}\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon_{slab}>0
be given. Choose
\delta_{{long}}>0
small enough with
\frac{4}{\lambda_{\max}}+1<\delta_{{long}}^{-1}
. Furthermore, consider a smooth partition
\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}}
near
\mathcal{E}
. Then, there exists
\hbar_{0}(\mathcal{P}_{\rm{sm}},\delta_{long},\delta_{Ehr},\varepsilon_{slab})>0
,
C>0
, and
c_{0}>0
such that for any
0<\hbar\leq\hbar_{0}
, any integer
n\in[0,2T_{\delta_{Ehr},\varepsilon_{slab},\hbar}]
and any two sequences
\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}
of length
n
, the following estimate holds:

\|\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}U^{n}\,\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}U^{-n}\chi^{(n)}(P(\hbar)-E)\|\leq C\,h^{-(d-1+c_{0}\delta_{long})/2}J_{n}^{u}(\bm{\alpha})^{-1/2}\,J_{n}^{u}(\bm{\beta})^{-1/2}

(C.0.6)

The entropic uncertainty principle (described in Section
5.2
) requires that we estimate the operator norm
\Pi_{\bm{\alpha}}\,U^{n}\,\Pi_{\bm{\beta}}\,U^{-n}\chi^{(n)}(P(\hbar)-E)
, resulting from our choice of partitions
\bm{\rho}
and
\bm{\tau}
, therefore feeding the right-hand side of (
C.0.6
) into the crucial quantity
c_{cone}
. In fact,
[
14
, Section 3.2]
and
[
29
, Theorem 7.1]
show this operator is actually pseudodifferential.

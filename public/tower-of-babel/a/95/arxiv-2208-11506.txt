Electrodynamics of Accelerated-Modulation Space-Time Metamaterials Preprint: APS/123-QED
Amir Bahrami, Zoé-Lise Deck-Léger, Christophe Caloz

Abstract
Space-time varying metamaterials based on uniform-velocity modulation have spurred considerable interest over the past decade. We present here the first extensive investigation of accelerated-modulation space-time metamaterials. Using the tools of general relativity, we establish their electrodynamic principles and describe their fundamental phenomena, in comparison with the physics of moving-matter media. We show that an electromagnetic beam propagating in an accelerated-modulation metamaterial is bent in its course, which reveals that such a medium curves space-time for light, similarly to gravitation. Finally, we illustrate the vast potential diversity of accelerated-modulation metamaterial by demonstrating related Schwarzschild holes.

V
Accelerated-Modulation Medium

Let us move on now to the case of the accelerated-modulation medium, which is the main object of this paper. The corresponding analysis is considerably more involved than that of the accelerated-matter medium, due to both the more complex, bilayer structure of the medium [Fig.
1
(b)] and the inexistence of a motion-less frame, as we shall see next. Here, in the
K

1

In the

VI
Formal Modulation-Matter Equivalence

Comparing Eq. (
3

1

K

, where
\theta_{\mathbf{k}}=\arctan(k_{z}/k_{x})
is the phase angle. Using the identity
\tan^{-1}(x)-\tan^{-1}(y)=\tan^{-1}((x-y)/(1+xy))
, this angle may be expressed as

\theta_{\mathrm{defl}}=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{(\mu_{\perp}-\mu_{\|})k_{x}k_{z}-\mu_{\perp}k_{x}\omega\overline{\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}}/c}{\mu_{\perp}k_{x}^{2}+\mu_{\perp}k_{z}^{2}-k_{z}\omega\overline{\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}}/c}\right).

(47)

C.3
Fields for Gaussian Beam Excitation

We consider the propagation of an s polarized plane-wave Gaussian beam that is launched in the
x

x=0

(48)

where
E_{0}

(49)

The spectral field in any plane
x

49

where

to which we apply the inverse Fourier transform to provide the field at any point

Given the assumed
x

(

Inserting this expression into (
52

-independent terms yields

\begin{split}E_{y}(z,x)=&\frac{E_{0}W_{0}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\text{e}^{\mathrm{i}\sqrt{\overline{\epsilon}_{\|}\overline{\mu}_{\perp}}k_{0}\left(1-\tfrac{\overline{\chi}^{2}k_{0}}{2\overline{\epsilon}_{\|}\overline{\mu}_{\|}}\right)x}\\
&\quad\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\exp\left(-k_{z}^{2}\left(\frac{W_{0}^{2}}{4}+\mathrm{i}\frac{\sqrt{\overline{\epsilon}_{\|}\overline{\mu}_{\perp}}}{\overline{\epsilon}_{\|}\overline{\mu}_{\|}}\frac{x}{2k_{0}}\right)\right)\\
&\quad\quad\text{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\sqrt{\overline{\epsilon}_{\|}\overline{\mu}_{\perp}}}{\overline{\epsilon}_{\|}\overline{\mu}_{\|}}\overline{\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}}xk_{z}}\text{e}^{ik_{z}z}dk_{z}.\end{split}

Calculating the integral in (

\begin{split}E_{y}(z,x)=&E_{0}\frac{\text{e}^{\mathrm{i}\sqrt{\overline{\epsilon}_{\|}\overline{\mu}_{\perp}}k_{0}\left(1-\tfrac{\overline{\chi}^{2}k_{0}}{2\overline{\epsilon}_{\|}\overline{\mu}_{\|}}\right)x}}{1+\mathrm{i}\frac{\sqrt{\overline{\epsilon}_{\|}\overline{\mu}_{\perp}}}{\overline{\epsilon}_{\|}\overline{\mu}_{\|}}\frac{2x}{k_{0}W_{0}^{2}}}\\

Appendix D
Modulation Parameters for Black and White Holes

The electromagnetic parameters corresponding to a given gravitational metric
g_{\mu\nu}
are
Plebanski 1960

\epsilon^{ij}=\mu^{ij}=-\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{g_{00}}g^{ij}

(58a)

for the permittivity and permeability, and

\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{j}=\frac{g_{0j}}{g_{00}}

(58b)

for the chirality.

The metric of black or white holes is found from the Schwarzschild space-time interval

ds^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2MG}{r}\right)(cdt)^{2}+\left(1-\frac{2MG}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(59a)

where

d\Omega^{2}=d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2},

(59b)

as

g_{00}=-\left(1-\frac{2MG}{r}\right)

(60a)

and

g_{11}=\left(1-\frac{2MG}{r}\right)^{-1},\quad g_{22}=r^{2},\quad g_{33}=r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta.

(60b)

The Schwarzschild metric (
60
) has two singularities, one at the origin,
r=0
, and one at the “Schwarzschild radius”,
r=2MG
. The former is an essential singularity
Penrose 1965
, whereas the latter is an artifact of the coordinates system in (
59
)
6
6

6

The metric associated with (
59
) is not conformally flat, i.e., its manifold cannot be mapped to a flat space via a conformal transformation map
Tu 2017
and can therefore be removed with a proper change of coordinates
Kruskal 1960
. An appropriate coordinate system to remove the spatial singularity would be one with the radial coordinate being transformed as
Weinberg 1972

r=\hat{r}\left(1+\frac{2MG}{4\hat{r}}\right),

(61)

where
\hat{r}
is the new radial coordinate. Inserting (
61
) into (
59a
) leads to the new space-time interval

ds^{2}=-\left(\frac{1-\frac{2MG}{4\hat{r}}}{1+\frac{2MG}{4\hat{r}}}\right)^{2}(cdt)^{2}+\left(1+\frac{2MG}{4\hat{r}}\right)^{4}(d\hat{r}^{2}+\hat{r}^{2}d\Omega^{2}),

(62a)

with its corresponding metric components

g_{00}=-\left(\frac{1-\frac{2MG}{4\hat{r}}}{1+\frac{2MG}{4\hat{r}}}\right)^{2}

(62b)

and

\begin{split}&g_{11}=\left(1+\frac{2MG}{4\hat{r}}\right)^{4},\quad g_{22}=\left(1+\frac{2MG}{4\hat{r}}\right)^{4}r^{2},\quad\\
&g_{33}=\left(1+\frac{2MG}{4\hat{r}}\right)^{4}r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta.\end{split}

(62c)

and the equivalent medium parameters obtained by inserting (
62b
) and (
62c
) into (
58
)

\epsilon_{\mathrm{H}}=\mu_{\mathrm{H}}=\frac{(1+\frac{2MG}{4\hat{r}})^{3}}{1-\frac{2MG}{4\hat{r}}},

(63a)

\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{\mathrm{H}}=0.

(63b)

For 2D, we transform the coordinates from spherical (
\hat{r},\theta,\phi
) to polar (
\rho,\phi
). Now given the circular symmetry of the medium parameter in the new coordinates, we choose a circularly-symmetric modulation. Therefore, the modulation has to move in radial direction (
\rho
).

For modulation with radial velocity (
\beta=\beta_{\rho}

z-x

35a

E_{\phi}

H_{\phi}

\overline{\epsilon}_{y}=\overline{\epsilon}_{\phi}=\overline{\epsilon}_{\|}=\frac{\Sigma_{\epsilon}-\beta^{2}\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\Sigma_{\mu}}{1-\beta^{2}\Sigma_{\epsilon}\Sigma_{\mu}},

(64a)

\overline{\epsilon}_{\rho}=\overline{\epsilon}_{\perp}=\frac{2\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}{\Sigma_{\epsilon}},

(64b)

\overline{\mu}_{y}=\overline{\mu}_{\phi}=\overline{\mu}_{\|}=\frac{\Sigma_{\mu}-\beta^{2}\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\Sigma_{\epsilon}}{1-\beta^{2}\Sigma_{\epsilon}\Sigma_{\mu}},

(64c)

\overline{\mu}_{\rho}=\overline{\mu}_{\perp}=\frac{2\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}{\Sigma_{\mu}}

(64d)

and

\overline{\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}}_{\rho}=\overline{\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}}=\beta\frac{\Delta_{\epsilon}\Delta_{\mu}}{1-\beta^{2}\Sigma_{\epsilon}\Sigma_{\mu}},

where

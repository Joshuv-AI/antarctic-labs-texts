Follow the flow: Proximal flow inspired multi-step methods
Yushen Huang yushen.huang@stonybrook.edu, Yifan Sun yifan.sun@stonybrook.edu

Abstract
We investigate a family of approximate multi-step proximal point methods, accelerated by implicit linear discretizations of gradient flow. The resulting methods are multi-step proximal point methods, with similar computational cost in each update as the proximal point method. We explore several optimization methods where applying an approximate multistep proximal points method results in improved convergence behavior. We argue that this is the result of the lowering of truncation error in approximating gradient flow.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following optimization problem:

\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;F({\mathbf{x}})=f({\mathbf{x}})+h({\mathbf{x}})

(1)

where
f({\mathbf{x}})
is an
L
- smooth function,
h({\mathbf{x}})
is a closed convex but not neccessary smooth function and
F({\mathbf{x}})
is bounded below.
The problem with the following settings has been raised in many applications
Tibshirani 1996
;
Yuan and Lin 2006
;
Evgeniou et al. 2005
;
Candes and Recht 2012
;
Friedman et al. 2008
.
In this paper, we consider a family of multi-step proximal point updates.
The algorithm is a generalization of the proximal point method (PPM)
Moreau 1965
where we use a linear combination of the previous
\tau
steps in each iteration, as follows:

\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\xi_{i}{\mathbf{x}}^{(k-\tau+i)},\qquad{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+1)}=\mathcal{F}(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{(k)})

(2)

Here,
\mathcal{F}
is an approximate proximal point step. When
\tau=1
and
\xi_{1}=1
, (
2
) reduces to the “vanilla” approximate proximal point method, of which there are many works
Moreau 1965
;
Asi and Duchi 2019
;
Nesterov 2021
;
Asi et al. 2020
.
In this paper, we investigate improvements garnered by higher order
\tau>1
. Note that unlike nonlinear explicit discretization methods (like Runge-Kutta), there is very little overhead in increasing
\tau
, as the averaging is done in an online manner.

However, there are two questions that could arise naturally.
First, suppose we are given
\tau
; how do we choose
\xi_{i}
optimally? Second, can increasing
\tau
always improve performance?

For the first question, we link the multi-step proximal methods to the discretization of gradient flow. Using dynamical systems to interpret optimization methods has garnered considerable interest
Su et al. 2015
;
Shi et al. 2019
;
Zhang et al. 2018
;
Romero and Benosman 2020
.
To optimize the performance in practice, the coefficient should be chosen to minimize the truncation error, which leads to the so-called backward differential formula (BDF) Scheme.
For the second question,
we show that in several important cases,
the methods give significantly better results, such as proximal gradient in compressed sensing with both convex and nonconvex penalties, alternating projections over linear subspaces, and alternating minimization for matrix factorization.

1.1
Related Work

Proximal point method

The proximal point method is originally from
Moreau 1965
where one mimimizes the following subproblem at each iteration;

\min_{{\mathbf{y}}}F({\mathbf{y}})+\frac{1}{2\beta}\|{\mathbf{y}}-{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}.

When
F
is convex, this subproblem has a larger strong convexity parameter thereby facilitating faster numerical methods. In practice, the subproblem will be solved approximately at each iteration; for example, using stochastic projected subgradient
(
Davis and Grimmer 2019
;
Asi and Duchi 2019
)
, prox-linear algorithm
(
Drusvyatskiy and Lewis 2018
)
and catalyst generic acceleration schema
(
Lin et al. 2015
)
. The proximal point method can also be generalized
(
Nesterov 2021
)
by changing the penalty norm to
\|\cdot\|_{2}^{p+1}
with
p\geq 1
which have have faster convergence rates than the vanilla proximal point methods.

Dynamical systems inspired methods

The idea behind the approach is that minimizing a function is equivalent to find the stationary point of a dynamical system. For example,
Su et al. 2014
analyzed Nesterov accelerated gradient descent method as the discretization of a second order ordinary differential equation, and
Shi et al. 2019
analyzed the method formed by using a higher order symplectic discretization of a related differential equations
Shi et al. 2021
, to provide acceleration. Others have attempted the same using Runge-Kutta explicit discretizations
Zhang et al. 2018
. There are also work finding an optimization methods by discretizing other flow such as rescaled gradient flow
Wilson et al. 2019
.
A special example of discretization-inspired optimization improvements is the extragradient method
Korpelevich 1976
, which is widely used in min-max optimization problems
(
Du and Pardalos 1995
)
and variational inequality problems
(
Facchinei and Pang 2003
)
.
The connection between dynamical systems ad optimization methods are also studied in other works
(
Schropp and Singer 2000
;
Wibisono and Wilson 2015
;
Krichene et al. 2015
;
Orecchia and Diakonikolas 2018
;
Sundaramoorthi and Yezzi 2018
)
.

1.2
Contributions

In this paper, we do the following.

•

We propose a dynamical system that, when discretized either exlicitly or implicitly, leads to gradient descent, proximal gradient method, or the proimal point method.

•

We propose a higher order implicit discretization scheme, based on the backward differentiation formulas, which are computationally trivial extensions of existing methods, but whose performance can be significantly improved.

•

We apply our methods to several problems, such as proximal gradient over nonconvex sparse regularization, alternating minimization and alternating projections. Numerically, we see that in these scenarios, multistep methods perform an order of magnitude better than their vanilla versions.

•

We give a preliminary convergence analysis on smooth quadratic problems, as well as convex, strongly convex, and nonconvex smooth problems.

Function conditions

Definition 0

Definition 0
.

f({\mathbf{y}})\geq f({\mathbf{x}})+\langle\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}),{\mathbf{y}}-{\mathbf{x}}\rangle+\frac{\mu}{2}\|{\mathbf{y}}-{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}.

-L

\mu
-convex with a negative

Dynamical System Approach for Optimization

Consider (
1

is a
L
-smooth and

The local minimum of

\dot{{\mathbf{x}}}(t)=\lim_{\beta\to 0}\frac{\text{prox}_{\beta F}({\mathbf{x}})-{\mathbf{x}}}{\beta}.

This DE has a close connection with the Moreau envelope of

\nabla g_{\beta}({\mathbf{x}})=\frac{1}{\beta}\left({\mathbf{x}}-\text{prox}_{\beta F}({\mathbf{x}})\right).

Note that this gradient exists and is unique for all

.
Also, when

\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)=-\nabla F({\mathbf{x}}(t))

2.3
Existance and uniqueness

To show the existance and uniqueness of (
3
), we will first show that its right-hand-side always exists, and that it is a special instance of subgradient flow.

Lemma 0
.

Consider
F=f+h
where
f
is
L
-smooth and
h
is convex. Then the right-hand-side of (
3
) always exists, and satisfies

\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)\in-\partial F({\mathbf{x}}(t))

(4)

where
\partial F({\mathbf{x}}(t))
is the Clarke subdifferential of
F
at
{\mathbf{x}}(t)

(
Clarke 1990
)

\partial F({\mathbf{x}}):=\mathbf{conv}\left(\{g:\exists{\mathbf{u}}_{i}\to{\mathbf{x}},\nabla F({\mathbf{u}}_{i})\to g\}\right)

and is always a closed and convex set.

Proof.

By construction,

F
is
(\mu=-L)
-convex, and thus for all
0<\beta<1/L
,
F_{\beta}({\mathbf{x}})
is strongly convex in
{\mathbf{x}}
, and a unique minimizer
{\mathbf{x}}_{\beta}
always exists; moreover,

\frac{{\mathbf{x}}_{\beta}-{\mathbf{x}}}{\beta}\in-\partial F({\mathbf{x}}_{\beta}),\quad\forall 0<\beta<\frac{1}{L}.

(5)

Next, because
F
is composed of a smooth and convex function, all its nondifferentiable points are isolated. So, assume that for some
{\mathbf{x}}
,
\nabla F({\mathbf{x}})
exists; then (
5
) extends to
\beta=0
. But if
\nabla F({\mathbf{x}})
does not exist, there still exists
\bar{\beta}
small enough that, for all
0<\beta<\bar{\beta}
,
\nabla F({\mathbf{x}}_{\beta})
exists. By definition of the Clarke subdifferential, the limiting gradient
\lim_{\beta\to 0}\nabla F({\mathbf{x}}_{\beta})\in\partial F({\mathbf{x}})
.
∎

System (
3
) is a differential inclusion. Solutions of such systems exist when
\partial F
is an upper hemicontinuous map, and is unique when it satisfies a one-sided Lipschitz condition.

Lemma 0
.

For
F=f+g
where
f
is
L
-smooth and
g
is convex, then the negative of the Clarke subdifferential
(
Clarke 1990
)

\partial F({\mathbf{x}}):=\mathbf{conv}\left(\{g:\exists{\mathbf{u}}_{i}\to{\mathbf{x}},\nabla F({\mathbf{u}}_{i})\to g\}\right)

is always a closed and convex set, and is upper hemicontinuous

\forall{\mathbf{x}},\;\exists\epsilon>0,\;\forall{\mathbf{u}}:\|{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon,\quad\partial F({\mathbf{u}})\subset\partial F({\mathbf{x}}),

and satisfies the one-sided Lipschitz condition, e.g.

\langle g_{x}-g_{y},{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\rangle\leq L\|{\mathbf{y}}-{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2},

(6)

for all
g_{x}\in-\partial F({\mathbf{x}})
,
g_{y}\in-\partial F({\mathbf{y}})
.

Proof.

By construction,
\partial F({\mathbf{x}})
is always closed and convex.
Note that
-\partial F({\mathbf{x}})
is upper hemicontinuous if
\partial F({\mathbf{x}})
is upper hemicontinuous. To see that this is true, note that
F=f+g
where
\nabla f({\mathbf{x}})
always exists and is continuous everywhere, and
\nabla g({\mathbf{x}})
exists for all but isolated points; moreover, by convexity,
g
is locally Lipschitz.
Therefore, for all but isolated points,
\nabla g({\mathbf{x}})
is also continuous.
Assume that
{\mathbf{x}}
is differentiable; then there must exist some
\epsilon
neighborhood around
{\mathbf{x}}
such that for all
{\mathbf{u}}
,
\|{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon
,
\nabla F({\mathbf{u}})
exists, and
\nabla F({\mathbf{u}})\overset{{\mathbf{u}}\to{\mathbf{x}}}{\to}\nabla F({\mathbf{x}})
.
Now assume that
{\mathbf{x}}
is not differentiable. Again, there is a neighborhood
\epsilon
where for all
\|{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon
,
\nabla F({\mathbf{u}})
exists, and by definition,
\nabla F({\mathbf{u}})\overset{{\mathbf{u}}\to{\mathbf{x}}}{\to}\nabla F({\mathbf{x}})
.

On the other hand, because for any
g_{x}\in-\partial F({\mathbf{x}}),g_{y}\in-\partial F({\mathbf{y}})
, we have

\displaystyle F({\mathbf{y}})\geq F({\mathbf{x}})-\langle g_{x},{\mathbf{y}}-{\mathbf{x}}\rangle-\frac{L}{2}\|{\mathbf{y}}-{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle F({\mathbf{x}})\geq F({\mathbf{y}})-\langle g_{y},{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{y}}\rangle-\frac{L}{2}\|{\mathbf{y}}-{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}

By combining the above equations, we recover (
6
).
As a result, the Clarke subdifferential is one sided Lipschitz.
∎

Theorem 5
.

The proximal flow defined by (
3
) always admits an existing and unique solution
\theta(t)
.

Proof.

Since (
3
) is an instance of subgradient flow (
4
), and since the Clarke subdifferential is upper hemicontinuous, then (
3
) always admits an existing solution.
Additionally,
-\partial F({\mathbf{x}})
satisfies the one-sided Lipschitz condition, and therefore the solution is unique.
∎

2.4
Optimality

Next, we show that the stationary point of the proximal flow (e.g.
\lim_{t\to\infty}{\mathbf{x}}(t)
) is equivalent to find the stationary point of
F({\mathbf{x}})
.

Theorem 6
.

Consider the proximal flow in (
3
) with
{\mathbf{x}}(0)={\mathbf{x}}_{0}
.
Then
\lim_{t\to\infty}{\mathbf{x}}(t)
converges to stationary point of
F({\mathbf{x}})
.

Proof.

Recall that if
\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)=0
, this implies

\lim_{\beta\to 0}\frac{\text{prox}_{\beta F}({\mathbf{x}})-{\mathbf{x}}}{\beta}=0

which is equivalent to

0=\lim_{\beta\to 0}-\nabla F_{\beta}({\mathbf{x}})\in\lim_{\beta\to 0}\partial F(\text{prox}_{\beta F}({\mathbf{x}}))=\partial F({\mathbf{x}}).

Hence
0
is a subgradient of
\partial F({\mathbf{x}})
and thus
{\mathbf{x}}
is a stationary point of
F
.
∎

To give a more intuitive understanding of the proximal flow, we give the several examples of
F({\mathbf{x}})
and demostrate how to calculate its corresponding proximal flow.

Example: Smooth minimization.

Suppose
F(x)=f(x)
is differentiable everywhere. Then,

\lim_{\beta\to 0}\frac{\text{prox}_{\beta f}({\mathbf{x}})-{\mathbf{x}}}{\beta}=-\nabla f({\mathbf{x}})

and the proximal flow reduces to gradient flow

\dot{{\mathbf{x}}}(t)=-\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}).

(7)

Example: Smooth + nonsmooth.

Now suppose that
f
is not only smooth, but is second-order continuous, and
where
h
is nonsmooth but convex. Then, in the limit of
\beta\to 0
, we may use the Taylor approximation for
{\mathbf{u}}\approx{\mathbf{x}}

f({\mathbf{u}})\approx\nabla f({\mathbf{x}})^{T}({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{x}})

and therefore, in this limit,

\text{prox}_{\beta f}({\mathbf{x}})\\
=\underset{{\mathbf{u}}}{\mathrm{argmin}}\;\beta\nabla f({\mathbf{x}})^{T}({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{x}})+\beta h({\mathbf{u}})+\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{u}}\|_{2}^{2}\\
=\text{prox}_{\beta h}({\mathbf{x}}-\beta\nabla f({\mathbf{x}})).

In other words, in the limit of
\beta\to 0
, the proximal gradient method also reduces to the proximal point flow.

Example: Shrinkage.

Let us consider
h({\mathbf{x}})=\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}
. Then

\text{prox}_{h}(x)_{i}=\begin{cases}x_{i}-\mathbf{sign}(x_{i})\beta,&|x_{i}|>\beta,\\
0&\text{ else.}\end{cases}

and therefore, in the limit
\beta\to 0
,

\dot{x}_{i}=\begin{cases}-\mathbf{sign}(x_{i}),&|x_{i}|\neq 0,\\
0&\text{ else.}\end{cases}

Note that in this limit,
\dot{x}_{i}
is not continuous (although
x_{i}
is still continuous).
And, for all convex nonsmooth
h
, by using the proximal operator and not the subgradient, we ensure that
\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)
is always attaining a unique value.

Example: LSP.

Let us consider a (
\mu=-1
)-convex loss (e.g., nonconvex)
h({\mathbf{x}})=\sum_{i}\log(1+b^{-1}|x_{i}|)
. Again, this proximal flow may be computed element-wise. For
x_{i}\neq 0
,

\dot{x}_{i}=-\frac{\partial h({\mathbf{x}})}{\partial x_{i}}=-\frac{\mathbf{sign}(x_{i})}{b+|x_{i}|}.

Now consider
x_{i}=0
. Then

\text{prox}_{\beta F}({\mathbf{x}})_{i}=\underset{u}{\mathrm{argmin}}~\beta\log(1+|u|/b)+u^{2}

which is monotonically increasing in
u

for any value of
b>0
,
\beta>0
, and is minimized at
u=0
. Therefore,

\dot{x}_{i}=\begin{cases}-\frac{\mathbf{sign}(x_{i})}{b+|x_{i}|},&x_{i}>0,\\
0&\text{ else.}\end{cases}

Discretize Proximal Flow for Optimization

Introducing prox grad and prox point

At this point, we have now observed three different possible discretizations of the right hand side of (
3

\frac{{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}}{\alpha}=\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k})

where for
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}={\mathbf{x}}_{k}
, the choice of
\mathcal{F}({\mathbf{x}}_{k})
leads to three well-known methods

\displaystyle-\nabla F({\mathbf{x}}_{k}),

(Gradient descent)

\displaystyle\frac{\text{prox}_{\alpha h}({\mathbf{x}}-\alpha\nabla f({\mathbf{x}})}{\alpha}

(Proximal gradient method)

\displaystyle\frac{\text{prox}_{\alpha F}({\mathbf{x}}_{k})-{\mathbf{x}}_{k}}{\alpha}

(Proximal point method).

where the last case reduces to the well-known proximal point method

{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}=\text{prox}_{\alpha F}(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}_{k}).

(8)

3
Accelerated methods

In this section, we empirically validate our proposed methods by considering several optimization problems: proximal gradient with
\ell_{1}
norm, proximal gradient with LSP penalty, alternating minimization for matrix factorization, and alternating projection on linear subspaces.
For those experiments, we calculate the equation (
2
) based on the following three approaches:

Multistep prox gradient

The idea of the first approach is based on approximating

\displaystyle\text{prox}_{\alpha f+\alpha h}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{k})=\underset{{\mathbf{x}}}{\mathrm{argmin}}\overbrace{\underbrace{\alpha f({\mathbf{x}})+\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{x}}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k,1)}\|_{2}^{2}}_{\text{smooth term}}+\underbrace{\alpha h({\mathbf{x}})}_{\text{prox term}}}^{F_{k}({\mathbf{x}})}.

(9)

This is done by first initializing
\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{k,1}=\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{k}
based on left side of (
2
) and perform
m
iterations of the following update

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k,j+1)}=\text{prox}_{\beta\alpha h}(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k,j)}-\alpha\beta\nabla f(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k,j)})-\beta(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k,j)}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k,1)})).

Then update
{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+1)}=\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k,m+1)}
.

Multistep alternating minimization

The second approach is based on alternating minimization
(
Shi et al. 2016
;
Nesterov 2012
;
Liu et al. 2014
;
Fercoq and Richtárik 2015
)
.
The idea is to first interpret the (regularized) alternating minimization steps

\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{{\mathbf{x}}_{1}}{\mathrm{argmin}}\;f({\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2}^{(k)})+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{1}-{\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(k)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}_{2}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{{\mathbf{x}}_{2}}{\mathrm{argmin}}\;f({\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(k)},{\mathbf{x}}_{2})+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{2}-{\mathbf{x}}_{2}^{(k)}\|^{2}

as approximations of the proximal point operations

({\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(k+1)},{\mathbf{x}}_{2}^{(k+1)})\approx\underset{{\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2}}{\mathrm{argmin}}\;f({\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2})+\\
\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{1}-{\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(k)}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{2}-{\mathbf{x}}_{2}^{(k)}\|^{2}.

That is, the approximation is done by splitting the variable into blocks and minimizing each block separately.

The multistep extension is then done by
first initializing
\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}^{(k,1)}_{1},\hat{{\mathbf{x}}}^{(k,1)}_{2}=\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{(k)}

and then performing

m
iterations of

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(k,j+1)}_{1}

\displaystyle=\underset{\mathbf{x}_{1}}{\arg\min}f({\mathbf{x}}_{1},\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(k,j)}_{2})+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\mathbf{x}_{1}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(k,j)}_{1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(k,j+1)}_{2}

\displaystyle=\underset{\mathbf{x}_{2}}{\arg\min}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(k,j)}_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2})+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\mathbf{x}_{2}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(k,j)}_{2}\|^{2}.

Then update
{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+1)}=\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(k,m+1)}
. Although higher
m
can often produce more precise proximal steps, in practice we find often
m=1
to be sufficient for acceleration.

Multistep alternating projections

The third approach is to minimize the optimization

\min_{{\mathbf{x}}}~\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}\bigcap\mathcal{C}_{2}}({\mathbf{x}})

where
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
are closed linear subspaces. We do this by encoding

f({\mathbf{x}})=0,\quad h({\mathbf{x}})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\bigcap\mathcal{C}_{2}\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

The approach is inspired by the alternating projection method
(
Von Neumann 1949
)
which performs the following updates, where
\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{(k)}
is constructed as the left hand side of
2
:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{y}}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=\text{proj}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{(k)})

\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=\text{proj}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}({\mathbf{y}}^{(k+1)}).

Note that here, as in all cases, the major multistep extension is in the construction of
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k)}
, as shown in (
2
). That is, the
per-iteration complexity
of computing
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{(k)}
differently does
not
significantly add computational overhead – but it does significantly affect convergence results.

Proximal gradient with 1-norm

The proximal gradient with

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}}\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{b}}\|^{2}+\lambda\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}

The entries of

We use multistep proximal gradient to approximate the proximal mapping, with

4.2
Proximal gradient with LSP penalty

The proximal gradient with LSP penalty over compressed sensing is formulated as follows:

For the experiment settings, we choose

We choose

For matrix

\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+\tau+1)}\\

{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+\tau-1)}\\

{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+2)}\\

Conclusion and Future Work

The goal of this work is to investigate the use of approximate implicit discretizations of (
3
) in badly conditioned non-smooth problem settings. In this work, we found that the higher-order approximate implicit discretization helps in many optimization problems. However, it is worth pointing out that it is also important to find an efficient manner of choosing the approximate methods. In our work, we provide multiple approaches that approximate the implicit updates, and both work well in practice.

Lemma 0

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{*}\|\leq\left(\gamma+\frac{1+\gamma}{1+\alpha\mu}\right)^{[\frac{k+\tau+1}{\tau}]}\max_{j\in\{0,1,\cdots\tau-1\}}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{j}-{\mathbf{x}}^{*}\|

for all
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,[\frac{n}{\tau}]\}
.
Hence, we prove the desired result.
∎

Corollary 0
.

Let
f({\mathbf{x}})
be a
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth function.
Suppose that we use a
\tau
multi-step proximal point methods with update (
16
),where (
16
) is computed approximately
by using
n
steps of gradient descent with step size
\beta\leq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha L+1}
.
This method
will converge linearly when
\alpha
is large enough with rate

\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{*}\|\leq\left((1-\beta\mu-\frac{\beta}{\alpha})^{n}+\frac{1+(1-\beta\mu-\frac{\beta}{\alpha})^{n}}{1+\alpha\mu}\right)^{[\frac{k+\tau+1}{\tau}]}\max_{j\in\{0,1,\cdots\tau-1\}}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{j}-{\mathbf{x}}^{*}\|

Proof.

Recall that for a
\rho
-strongly convex function
g
, then
n
steps of gradient descent with appropriate step size
\beta
gives convergence guarantee

\|{\mathbf{x}}^{n}-{\mathbf{x}}^{*}\|\leq(1-\rho\beta)^{n}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{0}-{\mathbf{x}}^{*}\|.

Now, note that

g({\mathbf{x}})=\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|{\mathbf{x}}-\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\xi_{i}{\mathbf{x}}^{k+i}\|^{2}+f({\mathbf{x}})

is
\rho=\mu+\frac{1}{\alpha}
strongly convex function. Then, using
n
steps of gradient descent with starting
point
\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\xi_{i}{\mathbf{x}}^{k+i}

gives

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}_{n}-\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{k+\tau+1}\|\leq(1-\beta\mu-\frac{\beta}{\alpha})^{n}\|\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\xi_{i}{\mathbf{x}}^{k+i}-\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{k+\tau+1}\|

where
\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{k+\tau+1}
fully minimizes
g
.
As a result, (
22
) is satisfied with
\gamma=(1-\beta\mu-\frac{\beta}{\alpha})^{n}
, and by invoking Theorem
17
, we will have the desired result.
∎

The following two theorems are for the case when
f({\mathbf{x}})
is
L-
weakly convex for both exact and inexact case. We will also need the following simple fact:

Fact 1
.

\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\|^{2}\leq n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|a_{i}\|^{2}

Theorem 19
.

Suppose that
f({\mathbf{x}})
is an
L-
weakly convex function. Then for any fixed step size
\alpha>0
, the update rule from (
16
),
given
\xi_{i}
, will have:

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq s\leq k}\|\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1})\|=O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}})

if
\alpha<\frac{2-2(\tau-1)\delta}{L}
and
\delta<\frac{1}{\tau-1}
where

\delta=(\tau-1)\sum_{j=1}^{\tau-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{j}(\tau-i)\xi_{i}^{2}\right).

Proof.

By
L
-smoothness, we will have:

\displaystyle f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau})

\displaystyle\geq f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1})+\langle\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}),{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}\rangle-\frac{L}{2}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1})+\frac{1}{\alpha}\langle\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\xi_{i}{\mathbf{x}}^{k+i}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1},{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}\rangle-\frac{L}{2}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(*)}{=}f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1})+\frac{1}{\alpha}\underbrace{\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\xi_{i}{\mathbf{x}}^{k+i}\|^{2}}_{=\alpha\|\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{(k+\tau+1)})\|^{2}}+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{L}{2}\right)\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\sum_{i=1}^{\tau-1}\xi_{i}({\mathbf{x}}^{k+i}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau})\|^{2})

where (*) is
2a^{T}b=\|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}-\|a-b\|^{2}
.
Telescoping,

f({\mathbf{x}}^{\tau})-f({\mathbf{x}}^{*})\geq\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{T}\|\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{(k+\tau+1)})\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{L}{2}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{T}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{T}\|\sum_{i=1}^{\tau-1}\xi_{i}({\mathbf{x}}^{k+i}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau})\|^{2}

Now from Fact
1
,
notice that:

\displaystyle\|\sum_{i=1}^{\tau-1}\xi_{i}({\mathbf{x}}^{k+i}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\tau-1)\sum_{i=1}^{\tau-1}\xi_{i}^{2}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+i}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(*)}{\leq}(\tau-1)\sum_{i=1}^{\tau-1}\xi_{i}^{2}\|\sum_{j=i}^{\tau-1}{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+j)}-{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+j-1)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Fact \ref{fact:variance}}}{\leq}(\tau-1)\sum_{i=1}^{\tau-1}(\tau-i)\sum_{j=i}^{\tau-1}\xi_{i}^{2}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+j}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+j+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\tau-1)\sum_{j=1}^{\tau-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{j}(\tau-i)\xi_{i}^{2}\right)\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+j}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+j+1}\|^{2}

where (*) is from reverse telescoping

{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+i)}-{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+\tau)}=\sum_{j=i}^{\tau-1}({\mathbf{x}}^{(k+j)}-{\mathbf{x}}^{(k+j-1)})

Hence for
c_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}(\tau-i)\xi_{i}^{2}
we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{k}\|\sum_{i=1}^{\tau-1}\xi_{i}({\mathbf{x}}^{s+i}-{\mathbf{x}}^{s+\tau})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\tau-1)\sum_{r=0}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{\tau-1}c_{j}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{r+j}-{\mathbf{x}}^{r+j+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\tau-1)\sum_{j=1}^{\tau-1}c_{j}\sum_{r=0}^{k}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{r+j}-{\mathbf{x}}^{r+j+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{s=r+j}{=}(\tau-1)\sum_{j=1}^{\tau-1}c_{j}\sum_{s=j}^{j+k}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{s}-{\mathbf{x}}^{s+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{(\tau-1)\sum_{j=1}^{\tau-1}c_{j}}_{=\delta}\sum_{s=0}^{k+\tau-1}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{s+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{s}\|^{2}

As a result, we will have:

f({\mathbf{x}}^{\tau})-f({\mathbf{x}}^{*})\geq\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{T}\|\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{(k+\tau+1)})\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{L}{2}-\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{T}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|^{2}-\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\sum_{s=0}^{\tau-1}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{s+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{s}\|^{2}

Now suppose we pick
\alpha,\delta
such that
\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{L}{2}-\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\geq 0
(e.g.
\alpha<\frac{2-2(\tau-1)\delta}{L}
) then

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=0}^{T}\|\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{(k+\tau+1)})\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{T\alpha}(f({\mathbf{x}}^{\tau})-f({\mathbf{x}}^{*}))+\frac{\delta}{T\alpha^{2}}\sum_{s=0}^{\tau-1}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{s+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{s}\|^{2}=O(1/T).

and we have the desired result.
∎

Theorem 20
.

Suppose that
f({\mathbf{x}})
is an
L
-weakly convex function. Then for any fixed step size
\alpha>0
,suppose that the exact solution to

\tau
multi-step proximal points methods are

\displaystyle\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{k+\tau+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\xi_{i}{\mathbf{x}}^{k+i}-\alpha\nabla f(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{k+\tau+1})

(23)

where
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}=1
and
\xi\geq 0
. Suppose that an approximate solution
{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}
of
\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{k+\tau+1}
satisfies the following contraction with respect to
{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}
:

\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{k+\tau+1}\|\leq\gamma\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|

(24)

Then, we will have:

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq s\leq k}\|\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1})\|=O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}})

if
\alpha<\min({\frac{2-2(\tau-1)\delta-16\gamma^{2}}{L},\frac{1}{L}})
and
\delta<\frac{1}{2\tau-2}
where

\delta=(\tau-1)\sum_{j=1}^{\tau-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{j}(\tau-1-i)\xi_{i}^{2}\right).

Proof.

By
L
-smoothness, we will have:

\displaystyle f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau})

\displaystyle\geq f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1})+\frac{1}{\alpha}\langle\ \alpha\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}),{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}\rangle-\frac{L}{2}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(*)}{=}f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1})+\alpha\|\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{(k+\tau+1)})\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{L}{2}\right)\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\alpha}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-\alpha\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1})\|^{2})

\displaystyle=f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1})+\alpha\|\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{(k+\tau+1)})\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{L}{2}\right)\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\alpha}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-\alpha\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1})+\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{k+\tau+1}-\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\xi_{i}{\mathbf{x}}^{k+i}+\alpha\nabla f(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{k+\tau+1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1})+\alpha\|\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{(k+\tau+1)})\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{L}{2}\right)\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\alpha}\|\sum_{i=1}^{\tau-1}\xi_{i}({\mathbf{x}}^{k+i}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau})\|^{2}-\frac{2}{\alpha}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}+\alpha\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1})-\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{k+\tau+1}-\alpha\nabla f(\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{k+\tau+1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1})+\alpha\|\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{(k+\tau+1)})\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{L}{2}\right)\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\alpha}\|\sum_{i=1}^{\tau-1}\xi_{i}({\mathbf{x}}^{k+i}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau})\|^{2}-\frac{2(1+\alpha L)^{2}}{\alpha}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-\tilde{{\mathbf{x}}}^{k+\tau+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq f({\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1})+\alpha\|\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{(k+\tau+1)})\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{L}{2}\right)\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\alpha}\|\sum_{i=1}^{\tau-1}\xi_{i}({\mathbf{x}}^{k+i}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau})\|^{2}-\frac{2\gamma^{2}(1+\alpha L)^{2}}{\alpha}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|^{2}

Now we repeat the step from
19
, we will have:

f({\mathbf{x}}^{\tau})-f({\mathbf{x}}^{*})\geq\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{T}\|\nabla f({\mathbf{x}}^{(k+\tau+1)})\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{L}{2}-\frac{2\gamma^{2}(1+\alpha L)^{2}}{\alpha}-\frac{2\delta}{\alpha}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{T}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+\tau}\|^{2}-\frac{2\delta}{\alpha}\sum_{s=0}^{\tau-1}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{s+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{s}\|^{2}

Hence, we prove the desired result.
∎

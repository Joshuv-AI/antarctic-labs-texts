Hopf algebras, Steinberg modules, and the unstable cohomology of SL n ⁡ ( ℤ ) \SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}) and GL n ⁡ ( ℤ ) \GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}) Thanks: Jeremy Miller was supported by NSF Grant DMS-2202943 Thanks: Peter Patzt was supported by a Simons Foundation Collaboration Grant
Avner Ash, Jeremy Miller, Peter Patzt

Abstract
. We prove that the direct sum of all homology groups of the integral general linear groups with Steinberg module coefficients form a commutative Hopf algebra, in particular a free graded commutative algebra. We use this to construct new infinite families of unstable cohomology classes of SL n ⁡ ( ℤ ) \SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}) .



1.
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to prove a new structural result on the rational cohomology of general and special linear groups over the integers. Let
\St_{n}\mathbb{Q}
denote the
n
th Steinberg module of the rationals (top degree reduced homology of the poset of subspaces of
\mathbb{Q}^{n}
) and
\det
denote the determinant representation (unless stated otherwise, all homology will be taken with coefficients in a field of characteristic zero). By work of Borel–Serre
[
BS73
]
(see also Putman–Studenmund
[
PS22
]
), the twisted homology groups

H_{*}(\GL_{n}\mathbb{Z};\St_{n}\mathbb{Q})\text{ and }H_{*}(\GL_{n}\mathbb{Z};\St_{n}\mathbb{Q}\otimes\det)

determine the cohomology of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
:

\cdot

H^{\binom{n}{2}-k}(\SL_{n}\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{k}(\GL_{n}\mathbb{Z};\St_{n}\mathbb{Q})\oplus H_{k}(\GL_{n}\mathbb{Z};\St_{n}\mathbb{Q}\otimes\det)
for
n>0
,

\cdot

H^{\binom{n}{2}-k}(\GL_{n}\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{k}(\GL_{n}\mathbb{Z};\St_{n}\mathbb{Q})
for
n
odd,

\cdot

H^{\binom{n}{2}-k}(\GL_{n}\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{k}(\GL_{n}\mathbb{Z};\St_{n}\mathbb{Q}\otimes\det)
for
n
even.

There is a well-known equivariant product structure on the Steinberg modules (see e.g.
[
MNP20
]
and
[
MPW
]
). In particular, there is a
\GL_{n}\mathbb{Z}\times\GL_{m}\mathbb{Z}
-equivariant map

(1)

\St_{n}\mathbb{Q}\otimes\St_{m}\mathbb{Q}\longrightarrow\St_{n+m}\mathbb{Q}

which induces ring structures on

H:=\bigoplus_{k,n\geq 0}H_{k}(\GL_{n}\mathbb{Z};\St_{n}\mathbb{Q})\quad\text{and}\quad H^{\det}:=\bigoplus_{k,n\geq 0}H_{k}(\GL_{n}\mathbb{Z};\St_{n}\mathbb{Q}\otimes\det).

We define coproduct structures on these rings which make them into Hopf algebras.

Theorem A
.

H
and
H^{\det}
have the structure of graded commutative Hopf algebras.

Using a theorem of Leray, we deduce the following.

Corollary 1.1
.

H
and
H^{\det}
are free graded commutative algebras.

Our freeness result lets us easily produce new nonzero classes in the cohomology of
\SL_{n}\mathbb{Z}
and
\GL_{n}\mathbb{Z}
simply by multiplying classes that were previously known to be nonzero. Moreover, using elementary results on Hopf algebras, one can check algebraic independence of cohomology classes using the coproduct. We apply our theory to the so-called wheel classes. Brown–Schnetz
[
BS

showed that there are nonzero classes

t_{3}
and

both generate the one-dimensional vector space
H_{3}(\GL_{3}\mathbb{Z};\St_{3}\mathbb{Q})

be a generator of the one-dimensional vector space.

Remark 1.3

Remark 1.4
.

The bialgebra structure on
H
is the shadow of a bialgebra structure on the Steinberg modules themselves. Steinberg modules assemble to form a representation of the groupoid
\bigsqcup_{n}\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
. This category of representations carries two monoidal structures, one often called the Day convolution product considered for example in
[
MNP20
]
and one based on parabolic induction considered for example by Nagpal
[
Nag19
]
. These monoidal structures are compatible in the sense that they form a duoidal category. The Steinberg modules form an algebra with respect to the first product, a coalgebra with respect to the second product, and bialgebra in a duoidal sense. For the definitions of a duoidal category and a bialgebra therein, see Böhm–Chen–Zhang
[
BCZ13
]
. To keep this paper less abstract, we will only work with classical Hopf algebras. However, we plan to revisit these ideas to study the unstable cohomology of congruence subgroups.

[
Rog92

2.
A differential graded bialgebra that computes Steinberg homology

Let
R
be a PID and let
F
be its field of fractions. Further, let
K
be our coefficient ring, a field of characteristic zero.

The
Tits building
of
F^{n}
denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{n}(F)
is the simplicial complex whose vertices are nonzero proper subspaces
0\subsetneq V\subsetneq F^{n}
and
\{V_{0},\dots,V_{p}\}
is a simplex if

V_{0}\subset\dots\subset V_{p}.

The Solomon–Tits Theorem
[
Sol69
]
implies that
\mathcal{T}_{n}(F)
is a wedge of
(n-2)
-dimensional spheres and its only nonzero reduced homology group is called the Steinberg module

\St_{n}(F):=\tilde{H}_{n-2}(\mathcal{T}_{n}(F);K).

By convention, we define
\St_{0}(F)
to be
K
.

Lee–Szczarba
[
LS76
]
gave a flat
K\GL_{n}(R)
-resolution of
\St_{n}(F)
. We use a slight variant of it here, which can be established with the same proof. See also Ash–Gunnells–McConnell
[
AGM12
]
. Let
V_{n}
be the set of all nonzero vectors in
R^{n}\subset F^{n}
and
V^{\pm}_{n}
be the quotient of
V_{n}
identifying negatives. By abuse of notation, we denote an element of
V^{\pm}_{n}
simply by its representative
v\in R^{n}
. Let
X_{n}
be the simplicial complex whose vertices are the elements of
V^{\pm}_{n}
and all (finite) sets of vertices are simplices. Let
L_{n}
the subcomplex of all simplices
\{v_{0},\dots,v_{p}\}
such that the
F
-span of
v_{0},\dots,v_{p}
is not all of
F^{n}
. Clearly,
X_{n}
is contractible and using Lee–Szczarba’s argument it follows that
L_{n}
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathcal{T}_{n}(F)
, which implies that

H_{i}(X_{n},L_{n};K)\cong\tilde{H}_{i-1}(L_{n};K)\cong\begin{cases}\St_{n}(F)&i=n-1\\
0&i\neq n-1\end{cases}.

Because
C_{i}(X_{n},L_{n};K)=0
for
i<n-1
, we get an exact sequence

\dots\longrightarrow C_{n}(X_{n},L_{n};K)\longrightarrow C_{n-1}(X_{n},L_{n};K)\longrightarrow\St_{n}(F)\longrightarrow 0.

[
CP17
, Lemma 3.2]
implies that
C_{i}(X_{n},L_{n};K)
is a flat
K\GL_{n}(R)
-module.

We will use the notation

\Sh_{n,k}=C_{n+k-1}(X_{n},L_{n};K)

and refer to
\Sh_{n,*}\to\St_{n}(F)
as the
Sharbly resolution
of
\St_{n}(F)
. Given nonzero vectors
v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}\in R^{n}
, we get a canonical element

[v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}]\in\Sh_{n,k}

given by the corresponding simplex
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}\}
in
X_{n}
. These elements are nonzero if and only if the
F
-span of
v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}
is
F^{n}
. We call such an element then a
basic sharbly
.

Let
\chi\colon R^{\times}\to K^{\times}
be a character (i.e. a group homomorphism), which we will view as a one-dimensional
K\GL_{n}(R)
-module that factors through the determinant. We will use the notation

[v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}]^{\chi}:=[v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}]\otimes 1\in\Sh^{\chi}_{n,k}:=\Sh_{n,k}\otimes_{K}\chi.

We will further consider the
\GL_{n}(R)
-coinvariants of
\Sh^{\chi}_{n,k}
and call its elements
coinvariant sharblies
. In particular, we will denote the image of a basic sharbly
[v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}]^{\chi}\in\Sh^{\chi}_{n,k}
by

[v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}]^{\chi}_{\GL_{n}(R)}\in(\Sh^{\chi}_{n,k})_{\GL_{n}(R)}

and call it a
basic coinvariant sharbly
. Because
\GL_{n}(R)
acts on the set of all basic sharblies, which up to signs

[v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}]^{\chi}
is antisymmetric, if we choose a representative from each
S_{n+k}
-orbit of
(n+k)

\Sh^{\chi}_{n,k}

\Sh^{\chi}_{n,k}

Let us denote by

B^{\chi}_{n,k}:=(\Sh^{\chi}_{n,k})_{\GL_{n}(R)}\quad\text{and}\quad B^{\chi}_{m}:=\bigoplus_{n+k=m}B^{\chi}_{n,k}

the coinvariants of the sharblies and by

B^{\chi}:=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}B^{\chi}_{m}=\bigoplus_{n,k\geq 0}B^{\chi}_{n,k}

the (bi-)graded vector space of all coinvariant sharblies. It comes with a differential

\displaystyle\partial\colon B^{\chi}_{n,k}=(\Sh^{\chi}_{n,k})_{\GL_{n}(R)}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow(\Sh^{\chi}_{n,k-1})_{\GL_{n}(R)}=B^{\chi}_{n,k-1}

\displaystyle[v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}]^{\chi}_{\GL_{n}(R)}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}(-1)^{i}[v_{1},\dots,\hat{v}_{i},\dots,v_{n+k}]^{\chi}_{\GL_{n}(R)}

of degree
-1
, such that its homology is

H(B^{\chi})_{n,k}\cong H_{k}(\GL_{n}(R);\St_{n}(F)\otimes_{K}\chi).

Definition 2.1
.

We define

\nabla\colon B^{\chi}_{n,k}\otimes B^{\chi}_{m,l}\longrightarrow B^{\chi}_{n+m,k+l}

by sending a tensor product of basic coinvariant sharblies
x=[v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}]^{\chi}_{\GL_{n}(R)}
and
y=[w_{1},\dots,w_{m+l}]^{\chi}_{\GL_{m}(R)}
to

\nabla(x\otimes y)=xy=[\phi_{n,n+m}(v_{1}),\dots,\phi_{n,n+m}(v_{n+k}),\psi_{m,n+m}(w_{1}),\dots,\psi_{m,n+m}(w_{m+l})]^{\chi}_{\GL_{n+m}(R)},

where
\phi_{n,n+m}\colon R^{n}\to R^{n+m}
sends the standard basis vector
e_{i}\in R^{n}
to
e_{i}\in R^{n+m}
and
\psi_{m,n+m}\colon R^{m}\to R^{n+m}
sends
e_{i}\in R^{m}
to
e_{n+i}\in R^{n+m}
.

The following lemma is easy to check.

Lemma 2.2
.

\nabla\colon B^{\chi}\otimes B^{\chi}\to B^{\chi}
defines a (bi-)graded algebra.

We call
\chi\colon R^{\times}\to K^{\times}

odd
if
\chi(-1)=-1
and
even
if
\chi(-1)=1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\chi
is odd and
n
is odd, then
B^{\chi}_{n,k}=0
.

Proof.

Let
x=[v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}]^{\chi}
be a basic sharbly in
\Sh^{\chi}_{n,k}
. Then

(-\id)\cdot x=\chi(-1)^{n}\cdot[-v_{1},\dots,-v_{n+k}]^{\chi}=-[v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}]^{\chi}=-x

and thus vanishes in the coinvariants
B^{\chi}_{n,k}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

B^{\chi}
is graded commutative, i.e. for
x\in B^{\chi}_{n,k}
and
y\in B^{\chi}_{m,l}
, we get

xy=(-1)^{(n+k)(m+l)}yx.

Proof.

Let
x=[v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}]^{\chi}_{\GL_{n}(R)}
and
y=[w_{1},\dots,w_{m+l}]^{\chi}_{\GL_{m}(R)}
be basic coinvariant sharblies. We observe that

xy=[\phi_{n,n+m}(v_{1}),\dots,\phi_{n,n+m}(v_{n+k}),\psi_{m,n+m}(w_{1}),\dots,\psi_{m,n+m}(w_{m+l})]^{\chi}_{\GL_{n+m}(R)}\\
=(-1)^{(n+k)(m+l)}[\psi_{m,n+m}(w_{1}),\dots,\psi_{m,n+m}(w_{m+l}),\phi_{n,n+m}(v_{1}),\dots,\phi_{n,n+m}(v_{n+k})]^{\chi}_{\GL_{n+m}(R)}.

Let
T_{n,m}
be the permutation matrix sending
e_{i}
to
e_{m+i}
if
i\leq n
and to
e_{i-n}
if
i>n
. We can use
T_{n,m}\in\GL_{n+m}(R)
to swap
\phi
and
\psi
in the sense that

T_{n,m}\phi_{n,n+m}=\psi_{n,n+m}\quad\text{and}\quad T_{n,m}\psi_{m,n+m}=\phi_{m,n+m}.

The determinant of
T_{n,m}
is
(-1)^{nm}
, so in particular
1
if
n
or
m
are even. We then get that

[\psi_{m,n+m}(w_{1}),\dots,\psi_{m,n+m}(w_{m+l}),\phi_{n,n+m}(v_{1}),\dots,\phi_{n,n+m}(v_{n+k})]^{\chi}_{\GL_{n+m}(R)}\\
=T_{n,m}\chi(-1)^{nm}[\phi_{m,n+m}(w_{1}),\dots,\phi_{m,n+m}(w_{m+l}),\psi_{n,n+m}(v_{1}),\dots,\psi_{n,n+m}(v_{n+k})]^{\chi}_{\GL_{n+m}(R)}\\
=T_{n,m}\chi(-1)^{nm}yx.

Therefore

xy=(-1)^{(n+k)(m+l)}\cdot\chi(-1)^{nm}\cdot yx

in the coinvariants
B^{\chi}_{n+m,k+l}
. The factor
\chi(-1)^{nm}
is of course
1
if
\chi(-1)=1
, and it is also
1
if
\chi(-1)=-1
because then
nm
is even or
xy=yx=0
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

B^{\chi}
together with the differential
\partial
is a differential graded algebra.

Proof.

Given basic coinvariant sharblies
x=[v_{1},\dots,v_{n+k}]^{\chi}_{\GL_{n}(R)}
and
y=[w_{1},\dots,w_{m+l}]^{\chi}_{\GL_{m}(R)}
, we need to prove that

\partial(xy)=(\partial x)y+(-1)^{n+k}x(\partial y).

This follows as

\displaystyle\partial(xy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phantom{+}\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}(-1)^{i}[\phi_{n,n+m}(v_{1}),\dots,\widehat{\phi_{n,n+m}(v_{i})},\dots,\phi_{n,n+m}(v_{n+k}),\psi_{m,n+m}(w_{1}),\dots,\psi_{m,n+m}(w_{m+l})]^{\chi}_{\GL_{n+m}(R)}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+k}\sum_{i=1}^{m+l}(-1)^{i}[\phi_{n,n+m}(v_{1}),\dots,\phi_{n,n+m}(v_{n+k}),\psi_{m,n+m}(w_{1}),\dots,\widehat{\psi_{m,n+m}(w_{i})},\dots,\psi_{m,n+m}(w_{m+l})]^{\chi}_{\GL_{n+m}(R)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phantom{+}(\partial x)y+(-1)^{n+k}x(\partial y)\qed.

.

Lemma 2.8
.

The map

\Delta\colon\Sh^{\chi}_{n,k}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\subset F^{n}\\

Proof.

Proof.

where

omits the vector
v_{i}
from
x^{\prime}_{U}
if

and from
x^{\prime\prime}_{U}
if

.

On the right-hand-side, we can index the summands by

and the

v_{i}
in

3.
Hopf algebras

In this section, we recall several definitions and facts about Hopf algebras and then prove a generalization of
Theorem A
.

A graded bialgebra
B=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}B_{n}
is called
connected
if
B_{0}=K
.

Usually, a Hopf algebra is defined to be a bialgebra that has an antipode. We will mainly cite Milnor–Moore
[
MM65
]
in this section, who do not require an antipode, i.e. their “Hopf algebras” are simply bialgebras. But it turns out that every connected bialgebra is automatically a Hopf algebra which follows from
[
MM65
, Proposition 8.2]
. Together with
Section 2
, this implies the following generalization of
Theorem A
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every PID
R
and every character
\chi\colon R^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,

H(B^{\chi})\cong\bigoplus_{n,k\geq 0}H_{k}(\GL_{n}(R);\St_{n}(F)\otimes_{K}\chi)

is a graded commutative Hopf algebra.

The
primitives
of a bialgebra
B
are given by

P(B)=\{x\in B\mid\Delta(x)=x\otimes 1+1\otimes x\}.

For a connected bialgebra
B
, let
B_{+}
denote the augmentation ideal
\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}B_{n}
. The

of

Q(B)=B_{+}/(B_{+})^{2}.

Because

, there is a canonical map

Proposition 3.2

(Milnor–Moore
[
MM65
, Proposition 4.17]
)
.

A connected Hopf algebra

is injective.

Theorem 3.3

(Leray
[
MM65
, Theorem 7.5]
)
.

A connected graded commutative Hopf algebra

over a characteristic zero field is isomorphic to the free graded commutative algebra generated by

.

Therefore we get the following generalization of

.

Corollary 3.4
.

For every PID
R
and every character
\chi\colon R^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,

H(B^{\chi})\cong\bigoplus_{n,k\geq 0}H_{k}(\GL_{n}(R);\St_{n}(F)\otimes_{K}\chi)

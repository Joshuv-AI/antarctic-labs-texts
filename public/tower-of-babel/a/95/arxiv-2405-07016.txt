Generalized de Branges-Rovnyak spaces
Alexandru Aleman, Frej Dahlin

Abstract
Given the reproducing kernel k k of the Hilbert space ℋ k {{\mathcal{H}}_{k}} we study spaces ℋ k ​ ( b ) {{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}(b) whose reproducing kernel has the form k ⁡ ( 1 − b ​ b ∗ ) k(1-bb^{*}) , where b b is a row-contraction on ℋ k {{\mathcal{H}}_{k}} . In terms of reproducing kernels this is the most far-reaching generalization of the classical de Branges-Rovnyaks spaces, as well as their very recent generalization to several variables. This includes the so called sub-Bergman spaces [ 31 ] in one or several variables. We study some general properties of ℋ k ​ ( b ) {{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}(b) e.g. when the inclusion map into ℋ k {{\mathcal{H}}_{k}} is compact. Our main result provides a model for ℋ k ​ ( b ) {{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}(b) reminiscent of the Sz.-Nagy-Foiaş model for contractions (see also [ 7 ] ). As an application we obtain sufficient conditions for the containment and density of the linear span of { k w : w ∈ 𝒳 } \{k_{w}:w\in{\mathcal{X}}\} in ℋ k ​ ( b ) {{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}(b) . In the standard cases this reduces to containment and density of polynomials. These methods resolve a very recent conjecture [ 13 ] regarding polynomial approximation in spaces with kernel ( 1 − b ⁡ ( z ) ​ b ​ ( w ) ∗ ) m ( 1 − z ​ w ¯ ) β , 1 ≤ m < β , m ∈ ℕ \frac{(1-b(z)b(w)^{*})^{m}}{(1-z\overline{w})^{\beta}},1\leq m<\beta,m\in{\mathbb{N}} .

Introduction

Motivated by models for contractions, de Branges and Rovnyak initiated
in the 60’s
[
11
,
9
]
the study of the Hilbert spaces with a reproducing
kernel of the form

(1)

k(z,w)=\frac{1-b(z)b(w)^{*}}{1-z\overline{w}},\quad z,w\in{\mathbb{D}},

Here, as usual,
{\mathbb{D}}
denotes the unit disc in the complex plane and the
operator-valued function
b:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{C}},l^{2})
is analytic with operator
norm bounded by one in
{\mathbb{D}}
. These ideas have been considerably developed by
Sarason
[
25
]
who studied extensively these spaces in the case when

b
is a scalar-valued analytic function, and called them

sub-Hardy spaces

{\mathcal{H}}(b)
. For example, he represented them as ranges
of the defect operators
(I-T_{b}T_{b}^{*})^{1/2}
endowed with the norm making
these operators co-isometric. Here
T_{b}
denotes the (analytic) Toeplitz
operator with symbol
b
on the Hardy space
H^{2}
.

{\mathcal{H}}(b)
-spaces attracted a lot of attention during the years, a good
account on those developments can be found in the book by Fricain and
Mashreghi
[
12
]
and in the references therein. An
important idea of Sarason
[
25
]
reveals a kind of dichotomy
regarding the structure of these spaces. Roughly speaking, it turns out that
the properties of
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
are either similar to those of a shift invariant
subspace of
H^{2}
, or to those of the orthogonal complement of such a
subspace (model space). The first alternative occurs when
b
is not an
extreme point of the unit ball of
H^{\infty}
, the algebra of bounded analytic
functions in
{\mathbb{D}}
, while the second occurs when
b
is an extreme point of
that set. In fact, it is proved in
[
25
]
that the following are
equivalent:

(i)

b\in{\mathcal{H}}(b)
,

(ii)

{\mathcal{H}}(b)
is invariant under the shift operator defined by
Sf(z)=zf(z)
,

(iii)

the polynomials are contained in
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
and are dense in the space,

(iv)

b
is not an extreme point of the unit ball of
H^{\infty}
.

We shall refer to the above situation as Sarason’s dichotomy.

Zhu
[
31
]
,
[
30
]
developed these ideas by
considering Toeplitz operators
T_{b}
acting on the Bergman space, i.e. the
range of
(I-T_{b}T_{b}^{*})^{1/2}
with the appropriate norm. We should point out
that these ranges are quite different. For example, it turns out that

\Ran(I-T_{b}T_{b}^{*})=\Ran(I-T_{b}^{*}T_{b})
, which fails dramatically in the Hardy
space context.

Such spaces are called
sub-Bergman
spaces and by analogy to
(
1
) they have the reproducing kernel

(2)

k(z,w)=\frac{1-b(z)\overline{b(w)}}{(1-z\overline{w})^{2}},

i.e. it is obtained by replacing in (
1
) the Szegő kernel by the
Bergman kernel. All of these spaces are invariant for the forward shift and
contain the polynomials. For our purposes it is important to note that in

[
10
]
Chu proved the density of polynomials in all sub-Bergman
spaces.

This idea generated a lot of further work

[
30
,
18
,
1
,
23
]
mainly devoted to
extensions to weighted Bergman spaces in one or several variables.

Another important and very recent development regards the generalization of the
original de Branges-Rovnyak spaces to the context of several complex
variables. From an operator-theoretic point of view, the most natural
generalization of the Szegő kernel to the unit ball in several complex
variables is the Drury-Arveson kernel

k(z,w)=\frac{1}{1-\langle z,w\rangle},\quad z,w\in{\mathbb{B}}_{d}.

Jury and Martin considered in
[
16
]

reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces with kernel of the form

(3)

k_{b}(z,w)=\frac{1-b(z)b(w)^{*}}{1-\langle z,w\rangle},

where
b
is a row-vector pointwise multiplier of the Drury-Arveson space.
They characterized the multipliers
b
for which the equivalent assertions
(i), or (ii) from above hold, showing in particular, that the Sarason dichotomy
does occur in this case. Shortly after, Hartz
[
15
]

completed their work by showing that the equivalent statements (i) and (ii)
above hold precisely when
b
is an extreme point of the unit ball of the
multiplier space. However, the density of polynomials (assertion (iii)) when
(i) or (ii) hold, remains open in this context.

Spaces with kernel of the form (
3
) appear also in the recent doctoral
thesis of Sautel as models for certain commuting tuples which are
expansive on the complement of their null-space. Moreover,
in
[
26
]
they provide sufficient conditions under which a
kernel of the form (
3
) is a complete Nevanlinna-Pick kernel.

The present paper is concerned with a very general construction of this type.
More precisely, we consider a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with
reproducing kernel
k
on a non-void set
{\mathcal{X}}
, denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
. In order
to deal with vector-valued
b
’s as well, we assume that
b
is a
non-constant contractive multiplier of
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}
into
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
.
This implies (see §2.1 below) that

k^{b}(x,y)=(1-b(x)b(y)^{*})k(x,y),\quad x,y\in{\mathcal{X}},

is the reproducing kernel of a Hilbert function space on
{\mathcal{X}}
which we
denote by
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
. It could be called a
sub-
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
space
.

Adopting this general point of view reveals that a number of properties of
the particular spaces mentioned above, especially in the sub-weighted Bergman
case, only depend on general results about reproducing kernels. For example,
in §3.2 we consider the embedding of
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
into
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
which is
always contractive. We prove that a necessary condition for the compactness
of this embedding is that
\|b(x_{n})\|\to 1
, whenever
k(x_{n},x_{n})\to\infty
.

In the case when
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
is a space of analytic functions on the unit disc

{\mathbb{D}}
with
k(z_{n},z_{n})\to\infty
whenever
|z_{n}|\to 1
and
b
is a
scalar-valued multiplier, the above condition implies that
b
is a finite
Blaschke product. In Theorem
4
we show that this condition
is also sufficient provided that it is sufficient in the simplest case

b(z)=z
. This is a fairly far-reaching generalization of results of Zhu

[
31
,
18
]
proved for standard weighted
Bergman spaces.

The results extend also to the context of several complex
variables, namely when
k
is a power of the Drury-Arveson kernel and
b
is
an automorphism of the unit ball. We also supply some examples showing that,
in general, the above condition fails to be sufficient.

Beyond extending these interesting results, the study of general
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
-
spaces has additional motivations. In this paper we shall focus on the
following two aspects:

1)

Reproducing kernel structure induced by operator inequalities,

2)

Reproducing kernels with a complete Nevanlinna-Pick factor.

1) When
b(0)=0
the original de Branges-Rovnyak spaces
{\mathcal{H}}(b)

with
b
scalar-valued are precisely the spaces of analytic functions on
{\mathbb{D}}

with reproducing kernel normalized at
0
where the backward shift
L
is
contractive and, in addition,
I-LL^{*}
has rank one. The recent paper

[
7
]
provides a thorough study of such spaces without
the last assumption, which yields Hilbert spaces with reproducing kernel of
the general form (
1
). Moreover, it turns out that the case when

\Rank(I-LL^{*})<\infty
leads to a theory analogous to the one of sub-Hardy
spaces, in particular Sarason’s dichotomy holds. Extending these ideas to
the context of several variables is obviously very complicated due to the
intricate structure of left inverses to the row operator given by
multiplication of the coordinates. For this reason, the expansivity of that
row operator plays the central role in Sautel’s work
[
26
]
mentioned above.

The second hereditary inequality is more involved and was derived by Shimorin
in
[
28
]
. If
S
denote the forward shift on a space of
analytic functions
{\mathcal{H}}
on the disc with normalized reproducing kernel at
the origin, Shimorin’s condition reads

(4)

\|Sf+g\|^{2}\leq 2(\|f\|^{2}+\|Sg\|^{2}).

It turns out (see also §3.1 below) that it characterizes general
sub-Bergman spaces obtained by replacing the scalar function
b
in
(
2
) by an operator-valued one. In §3.1 we prove a generalization
of both inequalities. Assume that
{\mathcal{H}}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert
space of analytic functions in
{\mathbb{D}}
with normalized kernel at the origin.
We show that the inequality

\left\|f_{0}+\sum_{n\geq 1}u_{n}f_{n}\right\|^{2}\leq\|\varphi f_{0}\|^{2}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\|f_{n}\|^{2},\quad f_{n}\in{\mathcal{H}},\,n\geq 0,

where
\varphi,u_{n},~n\geq 1
are fixed multipliers,
characterizes
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
-spaces, with
k
a kernel of Bergman-type, a class
introduced by McCullough and Richter in
[
19
]
.

2) By a normalized complete Nevanlinna-Pick (CNP) kernel on the non-void set

{\mathcal{X}}
, normalized at
z_{0}\in{\mathcal{X}}
we mean a kernel of the form

(z,w)\mapsto\frac{1}{1-b(z)b(w)^{*}},\qquad b(z)b(w)^{*}=\langle b(z),b(w)\rangle_{\ell^{2}},\quad z,w\in{\mathcal{X}},

where
\|b(z)\|_{l^{2}}\leq 1,~z\in{\mathcal{X}},~b(z_{0})=0
. Such reproducing kernels
arise from solving certain interpolation problems for multipliers

[
2
]
. These kernels have a number of remarkable properties
and have attracted a lot of attention recently. Here we are interested in a
much larger class, namely those which have a normalized CNP factor, i.e. the
reproducing kernels
k
for which there is a normalized CNP kernel
s
such
that
k/s
is a reproducing kernel as well (positive definite). Such kernels
play a crucial role for the very general commutant lifting theorem by S. Shimorin

[
27
]
, and very recently in
[
3
]
it has
been shown that functions in spaces with such kernels can be represented as
quotients of multipliers. Given
k
with the CNP factor

\frac{1}{1-b(z)b(w)^{*}}
the ”quotient” kernel

k_{b}(z,w)=k(z,w)(1-b(z)b(w)^{*}),

corresponds to the
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
space which actually appears in Shimorin’s
commutant lifting theorem as well. One of the main difficulties in dealing
with such spaces is to understand which functions belong to them other than
the reproducing kernels. In §4.1 we give sufficient conditions such that

{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
contains the kernels
k_{y}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
or even more, such that the span
of these kernels is dense in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
(Theorem
6
).
The proof of this approximation theorem is based on an analogue of the
Sz.-Nagy-Foiaş model inspired by the work in
[
7
]
.
The idea is interesting in its own right and is presented in §3.3.

There is a large class of examples satisfying these conditions, like the
sub-Hardy spaces
{\mathcal{H}}(b)
with
b
non-extremal in the unit ball of

H^{\infty}
and all
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
spaces with
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
, essentially every weighted
Bergman space on a domain in
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
and
b
a non-constant analytic row
contraction on that domain. Intuitively speaking, the kernels

k_{y}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k},~y\in{\mathcal{X}}
play the same role as polynomials in the theory of
spaces of analytic functions on the
d
-dimensional unit ball. In particular,
these results extend Chu’s density theorem
[
10
]
to sub-Bergman
spaces on very general domains in
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
. In fact, for the standard weighted
Bergman spaces on the ball we show in §5.1 that Sarason’s dichotomy does
not occur, that is conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) hold in any of such sub-
weighted Bergman spaces.

Finally, these results and ideas apply to an interesting class of kernels
considered recently in
[
13
]
, namely positive
integer powers of the sub-weighted Bergman kernels

\frac{(1-b(z)b(w)^{*})^{m}}{(1-z\overline{w})^{\beta}}\qquad m\in{\mathbb{N}},m<\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}.

These kernels are naturally connected to
m
-hypercontractive operators on
standard weighted Bergman spaces. In
[
13
]
it was
conjectured that polynomials are dense in the corresponding spaces of
analytic functions on
{\mathbb{D}}
. As an application of the results mentioned above
we prove that the conjecture holds true. Our methods yield a proof in the
higher dimensional case as well.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 serves to a preliminary purpose.
Section 3 contains general properties of
{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}(b)
, in §3.1 we discuss the
operator inequalities mentioned before. §3.2 is devoted to the embedding
of
{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}(b)
in
{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}
and the cases where it is compact. The analogue of
the Sz.-Nagy-Foiaş model is described in §3.3. Section 4 contains the
general approximation results, while in Section 5 we apply these ideas to
general weighted Bergman spaces. The proof of the conjecture in

[
13
]
is deferred to section 6.

Preliminaries

2.1
Reproducing kernels

For the sake of completeness we recall some
basic properties of these objects. The material is standard and can be found in

[
8
]
and
[
21
]
. If
{\mathcal{X}}
denotes a non-empty set, a
function
k:{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is called
positive

(write
k\gg 0
) if for all finite subsets
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\subset{\mathcal{X}}
the
matrix
[k(x_{i},x_{j})]_{i,j=1}^{n}
is positive semi-definite. Given
k\gg 0

there exists a unique Hilbert space
{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}
consisting of complex-valued functions on
{\mathcal{X}}
,
such that point-evaluations are bounded linear functionals on
{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}
given by

f(y)=\langle f,k_{y}\rangle_{{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}},\quad x,y\in{\mathcal{X}},

where we have used the common notation
k_{y}=k(\cdot,y)
. For this reason,
k

is called a
reproducing kernel
.

It is easy to verify that if
k,h
are reproducing kernels on
{\mathcal{X}}
with

k-h\gg 0
, then
{\mathcal{H}}_{h}
is contractively contained in
{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}
. In fact, the
map
k_{y}\to h_{y}
extends by linearity to a contraction, whose adjoint is the
inclusion map. This yields the following result.

Proposition 1
.

Let
k
be a reproducing kernel on
{\mathcal{X}}
, then
f:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
if
and only if

c^{2}k(x,y)-f(x)\overline{f(y)}\gg 0

for some
c>0
and the least such
c
is equal to
\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}
.

The classical Schur’s product theorem for positive semi-definite matrices
applies to reproducing kernels and gives the following result.

Theorem 1

(Schur’s product theorem)
.

Let
k
and
l
be reproducing kernels on
{\mathcal{X}}
. Then
kl
is also a
reproducing kernel.

Definition 1
.

Let
k
be a reproducing kernel on
{\mathcal{X}}
. A function
\varphi:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is
a
multiplier
of
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
if
\varphi f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
for every
f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
.
The algebra of these operators is denoted
\Mult({{\mathcal{H}}_{k}})
.

Note that if
\varphi\in\Mult({{\mathcal{H}}_{k}})
, then the induced linear map
M_{\varphi}
on

{\mathcal{H}}_{k},~M_{\varphi}f=\varphi f
is bounded by the closed graph theorem. Moreover,
we have

(5)

M_{\varphi}^{*}k_{y}=\overline{\varphi(y)}k_{y}.

Consequently

|\varphi(x)|\leq\|M_{\varphi}\|,\qquad x\in{\mathcal{X}},\,k_{x}\neq 0.

Using also the fact
that the linear span of
\{k_{y}:y\in{\mathcal{X}}\}
is dense in
{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}
one can derive
the following result.

Proposition 2
.

Let
k
be a reproducing kernel on
{\mathcal{X}}
, then
\varphi
is a multiplier of

{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
if and only if

k(x,y)(c^{2}-\varphi(x)\overline{\varphi(y)})\gg 0

for some
c>0
and the least such
c
is equal to
\|M_{\varphi}\|
.

Given two sets
{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}
of complex-valued functions on
{\mathcal{X}}
we shall
consider the linear span of their pointwise products, that is,

\Span{\mathcal{F}}\cdot{\mathcal{G}}=\Span\{fg:f\in{\mathcal{F}},g\in{\mathcal{G}}\}.

The next result is essentially a consequence of the above. We include a short
proof for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition 3
.

Let
s,t
be reproducing kernels on
{\mathcal{X}}
and
k=st
. Then:

(i)

\Span\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{s})\cdot\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{t})\subset\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
,
in particular
\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{s})\subset\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
contractively,

(ii)

If
f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{s}
and
g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{t}
, then
fg\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
and

\|fg\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}\leq\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{s}}\|g\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{t}}
,
in particular if the constant function
1\in{\mathcal{H}}_{t}
, then

{\mathcal{H}}_{s}
is boundedly contained in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
,

(iii)

If
{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}
are dense sets in
{\mathcal{H}}_{s}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{t}
,
respectively, then
\Span{\mathcal{F}}\cdot{\mathcal{G}}
is dense in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
.

Proof.

(i)
Let
\varphi\in\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{s})
and
\psi\in\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{t})
,
then by Proposition
2

\displaystyle s(x,y)\|M_{\varphi}\|^{2}\gg s(x,y)\varphi(x)\overline{\varphi(y)}

\displaystyle\gg 0,

\displaystyle t(x,y)\|M_{\psi}\|^{2}\gg t(x,y)\psi(x)\overline{\psi(y)}

\displaystyle\gg 0.

By Schur’s product theorem, the order is preserved when multiplying the
left-hand side and the right-hand side, respectively. That is

k(x,y)\|M_{\varphi}\|^{2}\|M_{\psi}\|^{2}\gg k(x,y)\varphi(x)\psi(x)\overline{\varphi(y)\psi(y)}

and it follows that
\varphi\psi\in\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
.
Clearly, any element in
\Span\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{s})\cdot\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{t})
is a sum of
products as above. The contractive containment follows by letting above

\psi=1
which gives

k(x,y)(\|M_{\varphi}\|^{2}-\varphi(x)\overline{\varphi(y)})\gg 0.

(ii)
is very similar and will be omitted.

(iii)

By
(ii)
we have that
{\mathcal{F}}\cdot{\mathcal{G}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
. If

y\in{\mathcal{X}},~\varepsilon>0
, and
f\in{\mathcal{F}},~g\in{\mathcal{G}}
with

\|f-s_{y}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{s}}<\varepsilon,\quad\|g-t_{y}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{t}}<\varepsilon,

by the first part of
(ii)
we have

\displaystyle\|fg-k_{y}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{t}}\|f-s_{y}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{s}}+\|s_{y}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{s}}\|g-t_{y}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{t}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon(\|t_{y}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{t}}+\|s_{y}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{s}}+\epsilon).

which gives the desired result by the density of the linear span of the kernel
functions
k_{y},\,y\in{\mathcal{X}}
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
. ∎

A repeated direct application of the last part of the proposition leads to
the following result.

Corollary 1
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be an algebra of functions on
{\mathcal{X}}
and let
s^{j},~j=1,\ldots,n

be reproducing kernels on
{\mathcal{X}}
such that
{\mathcal{F}}
is dense in

{\mathcal{H}}_{s^{j}},j=1,\ldots,n
. If
k=\prod_{j=1}^{n}s^{j}
then
{\mathcal{F}}
is dense in

{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
.

We close the paragraph with a few words about the vector-valued case. The
reproducing kernel of a Hilbert spaces of vector-valued functions is an
operator valued function of two variables with similar properties to the
scalar-valued version.

In this paper we shall be concerned with a very special case. Given a scalar
reproducing kernel
k
on
{\mathcal{X}}
, we shall consider the Hilbert space

{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}\otimes\ell^{2}
, that is, the space of functions
f:{\mathcal{X}}\to\ell^{2}
such that

f=\begin{pmatrix}f_{0}\\
f_{1}\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix},\,f_{j}\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}},j\geq 0,\qquad\|f\|_{{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}\otimes\ell^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\|f_{j}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}^{2}<\infty.

This Hilbert space has the operator-valued reproducing kernel

(x,y)\mapsto I_{\ell^{2}}k(x,y)
. In other words, given

f\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}\otimes\ell^{2},~y\in{\mathcal{X}},~e\in\ell^{2}
, we have

\langle f(y),e\rangle_{\ell^{2}}=\langle f,k_{y}e\rangle_{{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}\otimes\ell^{2}}.

A multiplier
\varphi
from
{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}\otimes\ell^{2}
to
{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}
is row-vector valued
function such that
\varphi f=\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi_{j}f_{j}\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}
for every
f\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}\otimes\ell^{2}
.
We write
\varphi\in\Mult({{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}\otimes\ell^{2},{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}})
. In this case (
5
)
becomes

(6)

M_{\varphi}^{*}k_{y}=\varphi(y)^{*}k_{y}.

Note that
\varphi(y)^{*}
can be interpreted as a column-vector. Finally, let us
also mention that Proposition
2
extends verbatim to
this context by simply replacing
\overline{\varphi(y)}
by
\varphi(y)^{*}
.

2.2
Some unitarily invariant kernels on the unit ball of
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}

Let
d
be a positive integer and

{\mathbb{B}}_{d}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}:\|z\|<1\},

be the open unit ball in
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
. A kernel
k
on
{\mathbb{B}}_{d}
is invariant under
the unitary group on
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
if and only if
k(z,w)
is a function of

\langle z,w\rangle.

An important class of examples, see
[
5
]
, are reproducing
kernels of radially weighted Besov spaces

B_{\omega}^{\beta}=\left\{f\in\Hol({\mathbb{B}}_{d}):~\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}|R^{\beta}f|^{2}\omega dV<\infty\right\}.

Here
R^{\beta}
is a power of the radial derivative operator

R=\sum_{j=1}^{d}z_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}
,
V=V_{d}
is the Lebesgue measure
on the ball, and
\omega
is a radial integrable function on
{\mathbb{B}}_{d}
. It is
easy to verify that monomials form an orthogonal basis in these spaces.
The particular case
\beta=\frac{d}{2},\,\omega=1
corresponds to the
kernel on
{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}
, which is defined by

s(z,w)\coloneq\frac{1}{1-\langle z,w\rangle},\qquad z,w\in{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}.

When
d=1
it is the reproducing kernel for the Hardy space
H^{2}
on the unit
disk
{\mathbb{D}}
and
s
is called the Szegő kernel. For
d>1
, the
corresponding space is called the Drury-Arveson space and then
s
is called the
Drury-Arveson kernel. This space plays a key role
in the theory of commuting operator tuples. The recent survey

[
14
]
contains a lot of interesting material in this direction.

For any
\beta>0
observe that

s(z,w)^{\beta}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\langle z,w\rangle^{n},

where
a_{n}\geq 0
therefore by Schur’s product theorem
s^{\beta}
is a
reproducing kernel.

Definition 2
.

Let
\beta>0
we write
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}}
for the space of holomorphic functions on

{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}
that corresponds to the reproducing kernel

s^{\beta}(z,w)\coloneq\frac{1}{(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^{\beta}}

These spaces are extensively studied in the literature. If
\beta>d
, then

{\mathcal{H}}_{s^{\beta}}
is a (standard) weighted Bergman space on
{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}
as noted in
the next paragraph.

2.3
Weighted Bergman spaces on a domain of
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
and let
\mu
be a regular Borel
positive measure on
\Omega
such that the analytic functions in
L^{2}(\mu)

form a closed subspace where point-evaluations are continuous linear
functionals. These spaces are usually called weighted Bergman spaces and we
denote them by
L^{2}_{a}(\mu)
. By assumption the space
L^{2}_{a}(\mu)
has a
reproducing kernel which we denote by
k^{\mu}
, and, as pointed out before,
the norm is given by

\|f\|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}|f(z)|^{2}d\mu(z),\quad f\in L_{a}^{2}(\mu).

If
\beta>d
, the spaces
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}}
from the previous paragraph satisfy

{{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}}=L^{2}_{a}(\mu)
with
\Omega={\mathbb{B}}_{d}
,

d\mu=c_{\alpha}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dV
, where
\alpha=\beta-(d+1)
, and

c_{\alpha}
is a normalizing constant.

The vector valued version
L^{2}_{a}(\mu)\otimes\ell^{2}
is defined with help of
the norm

\|f\|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\|f(z)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}d\mu(z)<\infty.

The following simple observation plays an important role for the sequel. We
denote throughout by
V
the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
.

Proposition 4
.

If
L_{a}^{2}(\mu)
is a reproducing kernel closed subspace of
L^{2}(\mu)
then
there exists a measure
\tilde{\mu}
such that

L_{a}^{2}(\tilde{\mu})\otimes\ell^{2}=L_{a}^{2}(\mu)\otimes\ell^{2}
and for each
compact subset
K
of
\Omega
there exists
c(K)>0
with

(7)

\tilde{\mu}|K\geq c(K)V|K.

Proof.

Choose
v\in L^{1}(V),~v>0
a.e. with
\text{essinf }v|K>0
for any
compact subset
K
of
\Omega
and set

d\tilde{\mu}=d\mu+\frac{v(z)}{1+\|k^{\mu}_{z}\|^{2}}dV.

Since for every
f\in L_{a}^{2}(\mu)\otimes\ell^{2}
and
z\in\Omega
we have

\frac{\|f(z)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}}{1+\|k^{\mu}_{z}\|^{2}}\leq\|f\|,

we obtain

L_{a}^{2}(\tilde{\mu})\otimes\ell^{2}=L_{a}^{2}(\mu)\otimes\ell^{2}
and that the
norms are comparable.
∎

One verifies that
\Mult(L^{2}_{a}(\mu))
equals the algebra of bounded
analytic functions on
\Omega
with equality of norms. Similarly,

\Mult(L^{2}_{a}(\mu)\otimes\ell^{2},L^{2}_{a}(\mu))
consists of bounded analytic
row-vectors and the multiplier norm coincides with the supremum norm. For our
purposes it is important to note that these multipliers never attain their
norm unless the symbols are constant. Indeed, if
\|bf\|=\|b\|_{\infty}\|f\|

for some non-zero
f\in L^{2}_{a}(\mu)\otimes\ell^{2}
, then

\|b\|_{\infty}=\|b(z)\|_{\ell^{2}}
for all
z\in\Omega
, which easily implies
that
b
is constant. In the terminology of
[
4
]
these
Bergman spaces have no non-trivial
sub-inner
multipliers. This is in
strong contrast to smaller reproducing kernel spaces on the ball. In fact,
more than that is true for measures
\mu
as above.

Proposition 5
.

Let
b:\Omega\to\ell^{2}
be analytic non-constant and such that
b(z)
is a
row-contraction for every
z\in\Omega
. If
\mu
satisfies
(
7
), then for every
z\in\Omega
there exists
c_{z}>0
such
that

\|f\|^{2}-\|bf\|^{2}\geq c_{z}\|f(z)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2},

for all
f\in L^{2}_{a}(\mu)\otimes\ell^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
z\in\Omega
and
B_{z}
be an open ball in
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
centered at
z
with

\overline{B_{z}}\subset\Omega
. By the assumption on
b
there is
a_{z}>0
such
that
1-\|b(\zeta)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\geq a_{z}
,
\zeta\in B_{z}
. This implies for
each
e\in\ell^{2}
,

\|e\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}-|b(\zeta)e|^{2}\geq(1-\|b(\zeta)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2})\|e\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\geq a_{z}\|e\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

Using also (
7
) we have for
f\in L^{2}_{a}(\mu)\otimes\ell^{2}

\displaystyle\|f(z)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{V(B_{z})}\int_{B_{z}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}dV

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{V(B_{z})c(\overline{B_{z}})}\int_{B_{z}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{V(B_{z})c(\overline{B_{z}})a_{z}}\int_{B_{z}}(\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}-|bf|^{2})d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|f\|^{2}-\|bf\|^{2}}{V(B_{z})c(\overline{B_{z}})a_{z}},

which completes the proof.
∎

3
Basic properties of generalized de Branges-Rovnyak spaces

Let us recall the general notion of a de Branges-Rovnyak space pointed out in
the Introduction. Given a scalar-valued reproducing kernel
k
on the
non-empty set
{\mathcal{X}}
let
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
denote the corresponding Hilbert function space.

Definition 3
.

Let
b\in\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2},{\mathcal{H}}_{k})
with
\|M_{b}\|\leq 1
.
The sub-
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
space, or the de Branges-Rovnyak space with respect to

{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
and
b
is the Hilbert space corresponding to the reproducing kernel

k^{b}(x,y)\coloneq k(x,y)(1-b(x)b(y)^{*}),\qquad b(x)b(y)^{*}=\langle b(x),b(y)\rangle_{\ell^{2}},

which is positive due to the multiplier criterion. We denote this space by

{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
.

One can verify that as a set,
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
is equal to

\Ran(I-M_{b}M_{b}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}
and its norm satisfies

\langle(I-M_{b}M_{b}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}f,(I-M_{b}M_{b}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}g\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)}=\langle f,g\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}},

whenever
f,g\in\Ran(I-M_{b}M_{b}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}={\mathcal{H}}_{k}\ominus\ker(I-M_{b}M_{b}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Furthermore,
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
is contractively contained in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
, this can be
seen either from the above formula, or the fact that

k(z,w)-k^{b}(z,w)=b(x)b(y)^{*}k(x,y),

which is positive by Schur’s product theorem.

3.1
Examples via operator inequalities

It is well known that the backward shift

Lf(z)=\frac{f(z)-f(0)}{z},

is a contraction on any de Branges-Rovnyak space
{\mathcal{H}}(b)={\mathcal{H}}_{s}(b)
, with

s(z,w)=\frac{1}{1-\overline{w}z}
and
b(0)=0
. The starting point of the
work in
[
7
]
is the observation that the converse holds
true as well, as the following result shows.

Proposition 6

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a Hilbert space of analytic functions on
{\mathbb{D}}
with a reproducing
kernel
k
normalized at
0
.
L
is contractive on
{\mathcal{H}}
if and only if there
exists an analytic row-contraction
b
with
b(0)=0
such that

k(z,w)=\frac{1-b(z)b(w)^{*}}{1-z\overline{w}}.

For reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces of analytic functions in
{\mathbb{D}}
having
the identity function
z
as a multiplier and a reproducing kernel normalized
at the origin, the contractivity of
L
is equivalent to the fact that the
forward shift
M_{z}
is expansive, that is,
\|zf\|\geq\|f\|
. In particular,
such spaces have a general de Branges-Rovnyak kernel as above.

There is a very recent extension of this idea to the context of several
complex variables due to Sautel
[
26
]
. It uses
the Drury-Arveson kernel
s(z,w)=\frac{1}{1-\langle z,w\rangle}

instead of the Szegö kernel. The proof is much more involved in this case.

Theorem 2
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}):{\mathcal{H}}^{d}\to{\mathcal{H}}
be a bounded row operator on an infinite
dimensional Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, and let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. T is unitarily equivalent
to
(M_{z},{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(b))
for some
b\in\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{D},{\mathcal{H}}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{E})

and Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{E}
such that
b(0)=0
,

\dim\mathcal{E}=m
, and that
M_{z}
is a bounded row operator if and only if
T

satisfies the following four conditions:

(i)

T_{i}T_{j}=T_{j}T_{i}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
,

(ii)

\dim([\Ran(T-\lambda)]^{\perp})=m
for every
\lambda\in{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}
,

(iii)

\|Tx\|_{\mathcal{H}}\geq\|x\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{d}}
for all
x\in\Ker(T)^{\perp}
,

(iv)

\bigcap\limits_{n\geq 0}\sum\limits_{|w|=n}T_{w}{\mathcal{H}}=\{0\}
.

Sub-Bergman spaces are also related to an operator inequality which is
considerably more subtle. It is due to S. Shimorin
[
28
]
and
it asserts that on the (unweighted) Bergman space on
{\mathbb{D}}
, the forward shift

M_{z}f=zf
satisfies

(8)

\|zf+g\|^{2}\leq 2(\|f\|^{2}+\|zg\|^{2}).

As the previous inequalities, (
8
) is
hereditary
in
the sense that it continues to hold for restrictions of the operator involved
to its invariant subspaces. This estimate has far-reaching consequences
regarding invariant subspaces of the Bergman shift. A crucial fact
(see also
[
6
]
) is that (
8
) is closely related to
sub-Bergman kernels in the following way.

Proposition 7

(Shimorin’s inequality )
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}={{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}
be a Hilbert space of analytic functions on
{\mathbb{D}}
such that
the reproducing kernel
k
is normalized at
0
,
M_{z}
and
L
are bounded
on
{\mathcal{H}}
. Then the inequality (
8
) holds on
{\mathcal{H}}
if and
only if there exists an analytic row-contraction
b
with
b(0)=0
such that

(9)

k(z,w)=\frac{1-b(z)b(w)^{*}}{(1-z\overline{w})^{2}},

In order to study
M_{z}
-invariant subspaces of the Bergman space one needs to
drop both assumptions in the above proposition, that is, to consider
non-normalized kernels, as well as left inverses of the forward shift with a null
space of arbitrary dimension.

Several interesting extensions of Shimorin’s inequality have been found by Olofsson
and Wennman (see
[
20
]
and the references therein).

A very general class of kernels containing all the above examples are the so-
called Bergman-type kernels introduced by McCullough and Richter in

[
19
]
. These are reproducing kernels in
{\mathbb{D}}
of the form

(10)

k(z,w)=\frac{1}{1-\varphi(z)\overline{\varphi(w)}(1-u(z)u(w)^{*})},\quad z,w\in{\mathbb{D}},

with
\varphi(0)=0
and
k(z,z)\to\infty
when
|z|\to 1^{-}
. It is shown in

[
19
]
that
\rho(z)=\frac{\varphi(z)}{z}
can be chosen to be outer
and bounded from above and below. Clearly, the Szegö kernel is obtained for

\varphi(z)=z,u=0
, and the unweighted Bergman kernel for

\varphi(z)=z\sqrt{2},u(z)=\frac{z}{\sqrt{2}}
.

Also, if
k
is of Bergman-type we have
Mult({\mathcal{H}}(k))=H^{\infty}
with
equality of norms (see again
[
19
]
). The following theorem extends all
previous results in one complex variable to the general context of
Bergman-type kernels.

Theorem 3
.

Let
k
be a Bergman-type kernel on
{\mathbb{D}}
of the form (
10
).
Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a Hilbert space of analytic functions on
{\mathbb{D}}
whose reproducing
kernel is normalized at
0
and assume that
\varphi\in Mult({\mathcal{H}})
, the range
of
M_{\varphi}
is closed of co-dimension
1
, and that
u\in Mult({\mathcal{H}}\otimes\ell^{2})
.
Then
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
for an analytic row-contraction
b
in
{\mathbb{D}}
with
b(0)=0
, if and only

(11)

\left\|f_{0}+\sum_{n\geq 1}u_{n}f_{n}\right\|^{2}\leq\|\varphi f_{0}\|^{2}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\|f_{n}\|^{2},

whenever
f_{0},f_{1}\ldots,f_{n},\ldots\in{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.

The assumptions on
M_{\varphi}
imply that its range equals the space of
functions in
{\mathcal{H}}
vanishing at the origin. Consequently, the operator

L_{\varphi}
defined by

L_{\varphi}f=\frac{f-f(0)}{\varphi},\quad f\in{\mathcal{H}},

is a bounded left-inverse of
M_{\varphi}
. Then the inequality
(
11
) is equivalent to

(12)

\left\|L_{\varphi}f_{0}+\sum_{n\geq 1}u_{n}f_{n}\right\|^{2}\leq\|f_{0}\|^{2}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\|f_{n}\|^{2}.

Indeed, if (
11
) holds, we apply it to
L_{\varphi}f_{0},f_{1},\ldots
.
From the fact that the kernel of
{\mathcal{H}}
is normalized at
0
we obtain

\|\varphi L_{\varphi}f_{0}\|^{2}=\|f_{0}-f_{0}(0)\|^{2}\leq\|f_{0}\|^{2},

and (
12
) follows.

Conversely, if (
12
) holds we apply it to

\varphi f_{0},f_{1},\ldots
and obtain (
11
).
Now (
12
) is further equivalent to the fact that
the operator
T:\bigoplus_{\mathbb{N}}{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
defined by

T\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}=L_{\varphi}f_{0}+\sum_{n\geq 1}u_{n}f_{n},

is contractive. This can be re-written as

I-TT^{*}\geq 0\,\Leftrightarrow(I-TT^{*})h\gg 0\,\Leftrightarrow(I-TT^{*})h=bb^{*},

where
h
is the reproducing kernel in
{\mathcal{H}}
and
b
is an analytic row-contraction
in
{\mathbb{D}}
. Since

L_{\varphi}L_{\varphi}^{*}h(z,w)=\frac{h(z,w)-1}{\varphi(z)\overline{\varphi(w)}},\quad M_{u}M_{u}^{*}h(z,w)=u(z)u(w)^{*}h(z,w),\,u\in\Mult({\mathcal{H}}\otimes\ell^{2},{\mathcal{H}}),

we can solve for
h
in
(I-TT^{*})h=bb^{*}
and obtain after a straightforward
calculation

h(z,w)=\frac{1-\varphi(z)\overline{\varphi(w)}b(z)b(w)^{*}}{1-\varphi(z)\overline{\varphi(w)}(1-u(z)u(w)^{*})}=k(z,w)(1-\varphi(z)\overline{\varphi(w)}b(z)b(w)^{*})

i.e.
h
is the reproducing kernel of
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(\varphi b)
.
∎

Besides the Szegő, or the unweighted Bergman kernel, some standard examples
of Bergman-type kernels are

k_{\beta}(z,w)=\frac{1}{(1-z\overline{w})^{\beta}},\quad z,w\in{\mathbb{D}},\,1<\beta<2.

These examples are discussed in
[
20
]
. The corresponding Hilbert spaces
are standard weighted Bergman spaces. Of course, there are many other
examples in this class, in particular in
[
19
]
there are produced
examples such that the forward shift on the corresponding space is not
subnormal.

3.2
Compact embedding

As mentioned in the beginning of this section
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
is always contained
contractively in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
. It is natural to ask when the contractive inclusion
map is compact. As pointed out in the Introduction this question attracted
attention in the context of sub-weighted Bergman spaces.

If the original space
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
is smaller, then the question is less interesting as
we shall see below. We start with a simple necessary condition for the
compactness of the inclusion map which holds under mild assumptions in the
most general context of a kernel
k
on the non-void set
{\mathcal{X}}
.

Proposition 8
.

Let
k
be a reproducing kernel on a non-empty set
{\mathcal{X}}
with the property
that
k_{y}
is a bounded function for every
y\in{\mathcal{X}}
. Let

b\in\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2},{\mathcal{H}}_{k})
with
\|M_{b}\|\leq 1
and suppose that the
inclusion map from
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
is compact. Then for every sequence

(x_{n})
in
{\mathcal{X}}
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}k(x_{n},x_{n})=\infty
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|b(x_{n})\|_{\ell^{2}}=1.

Proof.

Let us begin with the simple observation that if

J:{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)\to{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
is the inclusion map,then its adjoint
J^{*}
satisfies

J^{*}k_{y}(x)=(1-b(x)b^{*}(y))k_{y}(x),\quad x,y\in{\mathcal{X}}.

Now let
(x_{n})
be a sequence as in the statement and set

f_{n}=\frac{k_{x_{n}}}{\|k_{x_{n}}\|}
, so that
\|f_{n}\|=1
. Furthermore, by the
assumption on
k
we have

\langle f_{n},k_{y}\rangle=\frac{k(x_{n},y)}{\|k_{x_{n}}\|}=\frac{\overline{k_{y}(x_{n})}}{\|k_{x_{n}}\|}\to 0\text{ as $n\to\infty$}.

This proves that
(f_{n})
tends weakly to
0
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
, hence if
J
is
compact, so is
J^{*}
, that is,
J^{*}f_{n}
must tend in norm to 0. Thus,

\|J^{*}f_{n}\|^{2}=1-\|b(x_{n})\|^{2}\to 0.

and the result follows.
∎

The result is of particular interest for most common reproducing kernel
Hilbert spaces of analytic functions in the unit disc, in the case when
b

is scalar-valued. Indeed, if
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
is such a space with

k(z,z)\to\infty,\quad|z|\to 1^{-},

and
b\in Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
is contractive, by
proposition

8
the
compactness of the inclusion
J:{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)\to{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
implies that the bounded
analytic function
b
satisfies for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}

\lim_{z\to\lambda}|b(z)|=1,

hence
b
is a finite Blaschke product. However, in this generality, finite
Blaschke products may fail to be contractive multipliers so the corresponding
generalized de Branges-Rovnyak spaces might not exist.

Example 1
.

a)

In the Dirichlet space
D
with kernel

-\frac{1}{z\overline{w}}\log(1-z\overline{w}),\quad z,w\in{\mathbb{D}},

every finite Blaschke product
b
is a multiplier, but (see
[
22
]
)

\|bf\|>\|f\|,\quad f\in D\setminus\{0\}.

b)

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
be the unweighted Bergman space and
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}=H^{2}
be the
Hardy space on the unit disc.
Consider the reproducing kernel Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
consisting of analytic
functions
f
in
{\mathbb{D}}
which can be written (uniquely) as

f(z)=u_{1}(z^{2})+zu_{2}(z^{2}),\quad u_{1}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{1},\,u_{2}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{2},

with norm

\|f\|^{2}=\|u_{1}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{1}}+\|u_{2}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{2}}.

Then obviously,
M_{z}
is unbounded on
{\mathcal{H}}
while if
\varphi\in H^{\infty}
, the
function
\psi(z)=\varphi(z^{2})
is a multiplier with
\|M_{\psi}\|=\|\psi\|_{\infty}.

Proposition 9
.

Given a reproducing kernel Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
of analytic functions in

{\mathbb{D}}
, the finite Blaschke products are contractive multipliers if and only
if the identity function
\zeta(z)=z
is a contractive multiplier.

Proof.

One direction is obvious since the identity is a finite
Blaschke product, while the converse follows by standard functional calculus
for contractions from the equality
b(M_{z})=M_{b}
valid for any finite Blaschke
product.
∎

A similar result can be derived for the compactness of the embedding of

{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
into
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
, but the proof is somewhat more involved. Our next
result provides a far-reaching generalization of the result in
[
18
]
.

Theorem 4
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
is a reproducing
kernel Hilbert space of analytic functions on
{\mathbb{D}}
such that

(i)

M_{z}
is contractive on
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
,

(ii)

k(z,z)\to\infty
as
|z|\to 1^{-}
.

Then if
b\in Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
is contractive, the inclusion
map From
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
into
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
is compact if and only if
I-M_{z}M_{z}^{*}
is
compact and
b
is a finite Blaschke product.

The proof is essentially based on the identity below. A similar formula appears
in
[
13
]
. Given
a\in{\mathbb{D}}
we denote by
\varphi_{a}
the
disc-automorphism

\varphi_{a}(z)=\frac{z-a}{1-\overline{a}z},\quad z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Lemma 1
.

Let
s(z,w)=\frac{1}{1-z\overline{w}}
and let
b
be a finite Blaschke product with
zeros
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in{\mathbb{D}}
. Then

(13)

(1-b(z)\overline{b(w)})s(z,w)=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(\varphi_{a_{l}}^{\prime}(z)\overline{\varphi_{a_{l}}^{\prime}(w)})^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{l-1}\varphi_{a_{j}}(z)\overline{\varphi_{a_{j}}(w)}.

Proof.

We proceed by induction. For
a\in{\mathbb{D}}
a direct computation
gives

(1-\varphi_{a}(z)\overline{\varphi_{a}(w)})s(z,w)=(\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(z)\overline{\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(w)})^{\frac{1}{2}},

which is the case
n=1
. Suppose that (
13
) holds for
any Blaschke product with
n
zeros, and let
b
be the finite Blaschke
product with zeros
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}\in{\mathbb{D}}
. Let
b_{0}=1
and let

b_{k},~k\geq 1,
denote the Blaschke product having only the first
k
zeros,

a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
. Write

1-b(z)\overline{b(w)}=1-b_{n}(z)\overline{b_{n}(w)}+b_{n}(z)\overline{b_{n}(w)}(1-\varphi_{a_{n+1}}(z)\overline{\varphi_{a_{n+1}}(w)}).

By the induction hypotheses

(1-b_{n}(z)\overline{b_{n}(w)})s(z,w)=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(\varphi_{a_{l}}^{\prime}(z)\overline{\varphi_{a_{l}}^{\prime}(w)})^{\frac{1}{2}}b_{l-1}(z)\overline{b_{l-1}(w)},

and by the case
n=1
,

b_{n}(z)\overline{b_{n}(w)}(1-\varphi_{a_{n+1}}(z)\overline{\varphi_{a_{n+1}}(w)})s(z,w)=(\varphi_{a_{n+1}}^{\prime}(z)\overline{\varphi_{a_{n+1}}^{\prime}(w)})^{\frac{1}{2}}b_{n}(z)\overline{b_{n}(w)},

which proves the induction step and the result follows.
∎

Proof of
theorem

4
.

The assumption
\|M_{z}\|\leq 1
implies that for all
a\in{\mathbb{D}}
, the functions

u_{a}(z)=(1-\overline{a}z)^{-1},~z\in{\mathbb{D}}
are multipliers, in fact

M_{u_{a}}=(I-\overline{a}M_{z})^{-1}
. Assume that
I-M_{z}M_{z}^{*}
is compact and let

b
be a finite Blaschke product with zeros
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in{\mathbb{D}}
.
Multiply both sides of (
13
) by
k(z,w)/s(z,w)
, use
the identity
u(z)\overline{u(w)}k(z,w)=M_{u}M_{u}^{*}k_{w}(z)
together with the density
of the linear span of
\{k_{w}:~w\in{\mathbb{D}}\}
to obtain an equality of the form

(14)

I-M_{b}M_{b}^{*}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}M_{v_{l}}(I-M_{z}M_{z}^{*})M_{v_{l}}^{*},

with
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{k})
. Thus, by assumption, each operator in
the sum is compact, hence, so is
I-M_{b}M_{b}^{*}
. Now if
J:{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)\to{\mathcal{H}}_{k}

is the inclusion map, as above it follows that

J^{*}k_{w}(z)=(1-b(z)\overline{b(w)})k(z,w),

hence
JJ^{*}=I-M_{b}M_{b}^{*}
is compact. Conversely, if
JJ^{*}=I-M_{b}M_{b}^{*}
is
compact, by
proposition

8
,
b
must be a Blaschke product, so
that (
14
) holds. Note that all operators on the right
hand side are positive, and in the first term we have

v_{1}(z)=(\varphi_{a_{1}}^{\prime}(z))^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{(1-|a_{1}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{1-\overline{a_{1}}z},

is an invertible multiplier. This leads to

M_{v_{1}}^{-1}(I-M_{b}M_{b}^{*})(M_{v_{1}}^{-1})^{*}\geq I-M_{z}M_{z}^{*}.

Since the left hand side is compact and the right hand side is positive it
follows that
I-M_{z}M_{z}^{*}
is compact and the proof is complete. ∎

The theorem applies not only to arbitrary weighted Bergman spaces on
{\mathbb{D}}
,
or the Hardy space. The result holds in many other reproducing kernel spaces,
for example those corresponding to radial kernels

k(z,w)=\sum_{n\geq 0}k_{n}(z\overline{w})^{n},\quad z,w\in{\mathbb{D}},

with
0<k_{n}\leq k_{n+1},~\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{k_{n}}{k_{n+1}}=1.

It is an interesting question to characterize the compact embedding of

{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
into
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
for a a contractive row multiplier
b
.

The situation is much more complicated for analytic reproducing kernels on
the unit ball
{\mathbb{B}}_{d},~d>1
. A particular case that can be treated with the
method above is when
b
is an automorphism of
{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}={\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}

for some
\beta>1
. In this case we have the following identity similar to
(
13
) (see for example
[
24
]
),

1-\langle b(z),b(w)\rangle=\frac{(1-\langle a,a\rangle)(1-\langle z,w\rangle)}{(1-\langle z,a\rangle)(1-\langle a,w\rangle)}

where
a=b^{-1}(0)
. This leads to

s^{\beta}(z,w)(1-b(z)b(w)^{*})=s^{\beta-1}(z,w)\varphi(z)\overline{\varphi(w)},

where
\varphi(z)=\frac{(1-\langle a,a\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{1-\langle z,a\rangle}
is an
invertible multiplier of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta-1}
. In other words,

{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}(b)={\mathcal{H}}_{\beta-1}
with equivalent norms. This proves the
following result.

Proposition 10
.

If
\beta>1
and
b
is an automorphism of
{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}
, then
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}}(b)
is
compactly embedded in
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}}
.

3.3
A model for
{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}(b)

One of the main difficulties that occur
when dealing with de-Branges-Rovnyak spaces and their generalizations is
finding functions in the space other than finite linear combinations of
reproducing kernels. In some cases this led to a number of recent non-trivial
results. For example, the main result in
[
7
]
shows
that in one variable,
{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(b)
contains a dense subset of functions that
extend continuously to
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
. Further refinements of that result have
been recently obtained by Limani and Malman
[
17
]
. The method in
[
7
]
relies on a special formula for the norm in
{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(b)

which turns out to be essentially related to the famous Nagy-Foiaş model
for the backward shift on these spaces (see
[
29
]
).
Some of these ideas do extend to the general context of
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
-spaces and
provide useful information about their structure. To illustrate this,
consider a scalar-valued reproducing kernel
k
on the non-empty set
{\mathcal{X}}

with corresponding Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
and fix a contractive row-multiplier

b\in\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2},{\mathcal{H}}_{k})
. The considerations below apply to a
scalar, or finite dimensional vector
b
in a trivial way. Therefore for the
rest of the subsection we shall consider the case when

b\in\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2},{\mathcal{H}}_{k})
.

Denote by
\Delta=(I-M_{b}^{*}M_{b})^{\frac{1}{2}}:{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}
.
If
\Delta
(or
I-M^{*}_{b}M_{b}
) is injective, then

\|f\|_{\Delta}=\|\Delta f\|,

is a norm on
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}
. In this case we let
{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}
be the
completion of
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\Delta}
. We
shall be interested in the closed subspace
{\mathcal{M}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}

defined by

(15)

{\mathcal{M}}=\{(bf,f):f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}\}

Let us record two simple facts about this subspace.

Proposition 11
.

Suppose that
\Delta
is injective and let
{\mathcal{M}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}

be the subspace given by (
15
). Then:

(i)

{\mathcal{M}}
is closed,

(ii)

For
(u,v)\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}
its orthogonal projection onto
{\mathcal{M}}
,
P_{\mathcal{M}}(u,v)
is given by

(16)

P_{\mathcal{M}}(u,v)=(bw,w),\qquad w=M_{b}^{*}u+\Delta^{2}v.

Proof.

(i) If
((bf_{n},f_{n}))
converges in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}
then from

\|(bf_{n},f_{n})-(bf_{m},f_{m})\|^{2}=\|b(f_{n}-f_{m})\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}+\|\Delta(f_{n}-f_{m})\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}}=\|f_{n}-f_{m}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}},

we see that
(f_{n})
is Cauchy in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}
, hence convergent.

(ii) With
f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}
and
w
as in the statement we have

\displaystyle\langle(bf,f),(bw,w)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}}

\displaystyle=\langle f,w\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle f,M_{b}^{*}u+\Delta^{2}v\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle(bf,f),(u,v)\rangle{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}}.

∎

The main result of this subsection is given below.

Theorem 5
.

Suppose that
\Delta
is injective and let

{\mathcal{M}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}
be the subspace given by (
15
).
Then the map
J:{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
,
J(u,v)=u
is unitary. Equivalently,
for every
f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
there exists a unique
g\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}
such that

(f,g)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp}
and

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)}^{2}=\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}^{2}+\|g\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}}^{2}.

Proof.

Let us note first that if
(0,g)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp},~g\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}
, then

g=0
. Indeed, in this case
g
is orthogonal in
{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}
to the dense
subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}
, hence
g=0
. In particular, if

P_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp}}=I-P_{\mathcal{M}}
, this implies that the linear span of

\{P_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp}}(k_{y},0):~y\in{\mathcal{X}}\}
is dense in
{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp}
.
By
proposition

11
(ii) we can easily calculate the elements of this set.
Indeed, according to (
16
) we have

P_{\mathcal{M}}(k_{y},0)=(bw,w),\qquad w=M_{b}^{*}k_{y},

so that

(I-P_{\mathcal{M}})(k_{y},0)=P_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp}}(k_{y},0)=(k_{y}-bb^{*}(y)k_{y},-b^{*}(y)k_{y})=(k_{y}^{b},-b^{*}(y)k_{y}),

where
k^{b}
denotes the reproducing kernel in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
. But then

\displaystyle\langle P_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp}}(k_{y},0),P_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp}}(k_{z},0)\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}}

\displaystyle=\langle k_{y}-bb(y)^{*}k_{y},k_{z}-bb(z)^{*}k_{z}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}+\langle\Delta^{2}b(y)^{*}k_{y},b(z)^{*}k_{z}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=k_{y}(z)-b(z)b(y)^{*}k_{y}(z)-\overline{b(y)b(z)^{*}k_{z}(y)}+\langle bb(y)^{*}k_{y},bb(z)^{*}k_{z}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}

\displaystyle\quad+b(z)b(y)^{*}k_{y}(z)-\langle bb(y)^{*}k_{y},bb(z)^{*}k_{z}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}

\displaystyle=k_{y}(z)-b(z)b(y)^{*}k_{y}(z)=k_{y}^{b}(z).

Thus the map
J
in the statement preserves the norm on a dense subset of

{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp}
and maps it onto a dense subset of
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
. The result follows.

∎

The hypotheses that
\Delta
is injective might seem restrictive. It does not
apply to inner functions
b
in the case of the Hardy space on
{\mathbb{D}}
, or,
more generally to sub-inner multipliers of
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
. It turns out that smaller
spaces than
H^{2}
on
{\mathbb{D}}
, like for example standard weighted Dirichlet
spaces possess a large set of sub-inner multipliers
(see
[
4
]
, Theorem 14.9). On the other hand, recall that
weighted Bergman spaces do not have such multipliers.

4
Approximation results

4.1
The general approximation theorem

Given a space
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
, one
of the main difficulties in understanding its structure is the lack of
knowledge about its elements other than the reproducing kernels. Therefore
it is natural to relate this problem to elements in the larger (”known”)
space
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
, in particular to the original kernels
k_{y},~y\in X
. We
shall consider the map
J
from Theorem
5
, more precisely,
its inverse
J^{-1}:{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)\to{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}
and denote, as in the
previous subsection, by
{\mathcal{M}}
the closed subspace of
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}

with

{\mathcal{M}}=\{(bg,g):~g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}\}.

Proposition 12
.

Assume that
\Delta
is injective. If
y\in{\mathcal{X}}
then
k_{y}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
if
and only if there exists
c_{y}>0
such that

(17)

|b(y)g(y)|\leq c_{y}\|g\|_{\Delta},\quad g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}.

In this case, there exists a unique
l_{y}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}
with

(18)

\langle g,l_{y}\rangle_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}}=-b(y)g(y),\quad g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2},

and
J^{-1}k_{y}=(k_{y},l_{y})
.

Proof.

If
k_{y}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
and
J^{-1}k_{y}=(k_{y},l_{y})\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp}
, then
l_{y}

must satisfy (
18
) which implies (
17
).
Conversely, if (
17
) holds, the existence and uniqueness of
l_{y}
follows by the Riesz representation theorem and Theorem

5
gives
k_{y}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
.
∎

Under the assumption
k_{y}\in{\mathcal{H}}(b),~y\in{\mathcal{X}}
, the natural question
regarding approximation in this space is whether the linear span of these
functions is dense. We provide a sufficient condition for this conclusion.

Theorem 6
.

Suppose that
\Delta
is injective and that:

(i)

If
y\in{\mathcal{X}},~e\in\ell^{2}
there exists
d_{y}>0
such that

(19)

|\langle g(y),e\rangle_{\ell^{2}}|\leq d_{y}\|g\|_{\Delta}\|e\|_{2},\quad g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}.

Equivalently, point evaluations are continuous on
{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}
.

(ii)

If
f\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}
and
bf\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
then
f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}.

Then the linear span of
\{k_{y}:y\in{\mathcal{X}}\}
is contained and dense in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
.

Proof.

The hypothesis (i) together with
proposition

12
imply that

k_{y}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b),\,y\in{\mathcal{X}}
. Now suppose that
f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)
annihilates

k_{y},~y\in{\mathcal{X}}
. Let

J^{-1}f=(f,g),\,\,J^{-1}k_{y}=(k_{y},l_{y})\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp},\quad y\in{\mathcal{X}},

and apply Proposition
12
to conclude that
(
18
) holds for all
y\in{\mathcal{X}}
. Thus

0=\langle f,k_{y}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(b)}=\langle J^{-1}f,J^{-1}k_{y}\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}}=f(y)-b(y)g(y),

for all
y\in{\mathcal{X}}
, i.e.
f=bg\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
. But then by assumption (ii), we
have
g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\otimes\ell^{2}
and
J^{-1}f=(f,g)=(bg,g)\in{\mathcal{M}}
which
implies
(f,g)=0
and the result follows.
∎

Remark 1
.

1)

In the classical case when
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
equals the Hardy space
H^{2}
and
b

is a scalar analytic function bounded by
1
in
{\mathbb{D}}
, assumption (i) is
equivalent to the fact that
b
is not extremal in
H^{\infty}
. This follows
immediately by the standard Szegö theorem. Moreover, in this case there
exists an outer function
a
with
|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}=1
a.e. on the unit circle,
which easily implies that (ii) holds automatically.

2)

In the general case, an analogue of the above condition is to assume
that there exists a multiplier
a
such that
I-M_{b}^{*}M_{b}\geq M_{a}^{*}M_{a}

(see for example
[
16
]
and the discussion in the
Introduction). If
a
is zero-free then this assumption implies
assumption (i) in the theorem above. However, we do not know whether
it implies (ii) as well.

5
Weighted sub-Bergman spaces

Let
\Omega
be a domain of
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
,
\mu
be a positive measure on
\Omega

such that the Bergman space
L^{2}_{a}(\mu)
is a closed subspace of
L^{2}(\mu)

with reproducing kernel
k^{\mu}
(see
section

2.3
). For an
analytic row-contraction
b
on
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
we consider the sub-
Bergman space
{\mathcal{H}}_{k^{\mu}}(b)
. The aim of this subsection is to show that
the general result given in
theorem

6
does always apply to these
spaces.

Theorem 7
.

Assume that
\mu
satisfies (
7
) and
let
b
be any analytic row-contraction on
\Omega
with
\|b(z_{0})\|<1
for
some
z_{0}\in\Omega
. Then the linear span of
\{k^{\mu}_{z}:~z\in\Omega\}
is
dense in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k^{\mu}}(b)
.

Proof.

The statement is obvious when
b
is a constant with
\|b\|<1
, so we may
assume
b
is not constant. We want to apply Theorem
6
.
To this end we need to verify the assumptions (i) and (ii) in that theorem.
Assumption (i) follows by an application of Proposition
5
.
To verify (ii), let
g\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\Delta}
with
bg\in L_{a}^{2}(\mu)
and let
(g_{n})

be a sequence in
L_{a}^{2}(\mu)\otimes\ell^{2}
with
\|g_{n}-g\|_{\Delta}\to 0
. By
assumption (i) we have for each
z\in\Omega
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}bg_{n}(z)=bg(z)
, and that
(g_{n}(z))
converges weakly to

g(z)
in
\ell^{2}
, in particular,
\|g(z)\|_{2}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|g_{n}(z)\|_{2}
.
Then since
b(z)
is a contractive row contraction we can apply Fatou’s lemma
to obtain

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\Delta}^{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|g_{n}\|_{\Delta}^{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}(\|g_{n}(z)\|_{2}^{2}-|bg_{n}(z)|^{2})d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\int_{\Omega}\liminf_{n\to\infty}(\|g_{n}(z)\|_{2}^{2}-|bg_{n}(z)|^{2})d\mu(z)\geq\int_{\Omega}(\|g(z)\|_{2}^{2}-|bg(z)|^{2})d\mu(z).

This gives

\int_{\Omega}\|g(z)\|^{2}d\mu(z)\leq\|g\|_{\Delta}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|bg(z)|^{2}d\mu(z),

that is, assumption (ii) is verified as well. Then the result follows by a
direct application of Theorem
6
.
∎

5.1
Approximation by polynomials in standard weighted sub-Bergman spaces on the unit ball

In this subsection we shall specialize to unitarily invariant weighted
Bergman spaces on
{\mathbb{B}}_{d}
, the unit ball in
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
. These are the spaces

{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}
defined in §2.2, where
\beta>d
corresponding to measures
that satisfy (
7
). Recall that their reproducing kernel is
given by

s^{\beta}(z,w)=\frac{1}{(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^{\beta}},\quad z,w\in{\mathbb{B}}_{d}.

For such
\beta
we let
b
be an analytic row-contraction with
\|b(0)\|<1
.
The sub-Bergman space
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}}(b)
is the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{s^{\beta}}(b)
,
that is, the Hilbert space with reproducing kernel

s^{\beta}(z,w)(1-b(z)b(w)^{*})=\frac{1-b(z)b(w)^{*}}{(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^{\beta}}.

Let us note
first the following property of the multipliers of these spaces.

Proposition 13
.

Let
\beta>d
and let
b
an analytic row-contraction with
\|b(0)\|<1
. Then
for every
0<\gamma<\beta-d
,
\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{\gamma})
is contractively contained
in
\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}(b))
. In particular, if
u
is analytic in a neighborhood
of
\overline{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}
then
u\in\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}(b))
.

Proof.

The first assertion is a direct application of Proposition

3
(i). The fact that analytic functions in a
neighborhood of
\overline{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}
are multipliers of

{\mathcal{H}}_{\gamma},~\gamma>0
, is well known and easy to prove. We omit the details.
∎

With this observation in hand we can turn to the approximation result for
these spaces. It actually shows that within this context Sarason’s dichotomy
does not occur.

Corollary 2
.

Let
\beta>d
and let
b
an analytic row-contraction with
\|b(0)\|<1
. Then
polynomials form a dense subset of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}(b)
. Moreover, for any
e\in\ell^{2}
,
the function
z\mapsto b(z)e
belongs to
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}}(b)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
7
,
{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}(b)
contains all kernels

s_{w}^{\beta},~w\in{\mathbb{B}}_{d}
, of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}
. By
proposition

13
we
have
s_{w}^{-\beta}\in\Mult({\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}(b))
, hence

1=s_{w}^{-\beta}s_{w}^{\beta}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}(b)
. The same argument shows that

1\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta-\gamma}(b)
whenever
\gamma>0,~\beta-\gamma>d
. But then

proposition

3
(ii) implies that for such
\gamma
,
{\mathcal{H}}_{\gamma}

is continuously contained in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}(b)
. Since the kernels
s_{w}^{\beta}

can be approximated by polynomials in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\gamma}
the same holds in

{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}(b)
and another application of Theorem
7

shows that the closure of polynomials in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}(b)
contains the dense
set
\text{span}\{s_{w}^{\beta}:~w\in{\mathbb{B}}_{d}\}
which concludes the proof.

The previous paragraph shows that
z\mapsto b(z)b(w)^{*}
belongs to
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}}(b)

for any
w\in{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}
. Indeed, this follows by multiplying the kernel function

z\mapsto s^{\beta}_{w}(1-b(z)b(w)^{*})(z)

by
s^{-\beta}_{w}
, this proves that
z\mapsto 1-b(z)b(w)^{*}
belongs to
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}}(b)
,
but so does the constant function
1
. To conclude, let
e\in\ell^{2}
be decomposed
as
e=e^{\prime}+e^{\prime\prime}
where
e^{\prime}\in\Span\{b(w)^{*}:w\in{\mathbb{B}}_{d}\}
and
e^{\prime\prime}\perp b(w)^{*}
for
all
w\in{\mathbb{B}}_{d}
. Note that
e\perp b(w)^{*}
is equivalent to
b(w)e=0
, hence the
function
z\mapsto b(z)e=b(z)e^{\prime}
belongs to
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\beta}}(b)
.
∎

Using a completely different method, Chu
[
10
]
proved the
density of polynomials for scalar-valued
b
in the case when
d=1
and

\beta=2
.

6
Approximation by polynomials in higher order de Branges-Rovnyak spaces

Let
k
be a reproducing kernel on a non-empty set
{\mathcal{X}}
, and let
b
be a
contractive multiplier of
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
. In this section we shall consider kernels
of the form

(20)

k^{b,m}(x,y)=k(x,y)(1-b(x)\overline{b(y)})^{m},\quad x,y\in{\mathcal{X}},

where
m
is a fixed positive integer. Such kernels have been recently
introduced and thoroughly studied in
[
13
]
, in
connection with
m-
hypercontractive operators.

Definition 4
.

A bounded operator
A
on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
is called an

m
-hypercontraction
if

(21)

\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}A^{*j}A^{j}\geq 0,

for all
0\leq n\leq m
, where
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

The result below is a slight simplification of
[
13
, Corollary 3.6]
.

Proposition 14
.

Let
b
be a contractive multiplier of
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
such that
|b(x)|<1
for every

x\in{\mathcal{X}}
. Then
M_{b}^{*}
is a
m
-hypercontraction if and only if

(22)

k^{b,m}(x,y)=k(x,y)(1-b(x)\overline{b(y)})^{m}\gg 0.

In this case,

k(x,y)(1-b(x)b(y))^{n}\gg 0,

for every integer
n
with
0\leq n\leq m
.

Proof.

We use repeatedly that the bounded operator
T
on the reproducing kernel
Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
is positive if and only if
Tk(x,y)\gg 0
. Thus if

M_{b}^{*}
is
m-
hypercontractive then (
21
) holds for
n=m
,
hence

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}M_{b}^{j}M_{b}^{*j}k(x,y)

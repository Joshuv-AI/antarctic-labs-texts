Excitation Spectrum for Bose Gases beyond the Gross–Pitaevskii Regime
Christian Brennecke, Marco Caporaletti, Benjamin SchleinDepartment of Mathematics, Harvard University, One Oxford Street, Cambridge MA 02138, USAInstitute of Mathematics, University of Zurich, Winterthurerstrasse 190, 8057 Zurich, Switzerland

Abstract
We consider Bose gases of N N particles in a box of volume one, interacting through a repulsive potential with scattering length of order N − 1 + κ N^{-1+\kappa} , for κ > 0 \kappa>0 . Such regimes interpolate between the Gross-Pitaevskii and thermodynamic limits. Assuming that κ \kappa is sufficiently small, we determine the ground state energy and the low-energy excitation spectrum, up to errors vanishing in the limit N → ∞ N\to\infty .



1
Introduction and main result

We consider systems of
N\in\mathbb{N}
bosons in the three-dimensional box
\Lambda=[-1/2,1/2]^{3}
, with periodic boundary conditions, interacting through a repulsive potential with effective range of the order
N^{-1+\kappa}
, for
\kappa\geq 0
small enough. The Hamilton operator of the system has the form

H_{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}-\Delta_{x_{i}}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}N^{2-2\kappa}V(N^{1-\kappa}(x_{i}-x_{j}))

(1.1)

and it is densely defined on
L^{2}_{s}(\Lambda^{N})
, the subspace of
L^{2}(\Lambda^{N})
consisting of permutation-invariant functions on
\Lambda^{N}
. Throughout the paper we will assume that the potential
V\in L^{3}(\mathbb{R})
is non-negative, radial and compactly supported. We recall the zero-energy scattering equation

\left[-\Delta+{1\over 2}V(x)\right]f(x)=0,

with the boundary condition
f(x)\to 1
as
\left|x\right|\to\infty
. It is easy to see that, outside of the range of the potential
V
, the solution must have the form
f=1-\mathfrak{a}_{0}/\left|x\right|
for some unique positive real number
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
called the scattering length of
V
. By scaling,

\left[-\Delta+{1\over 2}N^{2-2\kappa}V(N^{1-\kappa}x)\right]f(N^{1-\kappa}x)=0,

so that the scattering length of the rescaled potential is given by
\mathfrak{a}_{N}=\mathfrak{a}_{0}/N^{1-\kappa}
. For
\kappa<2/3
the Hamiltonian (
1.1
) represents a “dilute limit”, in the sense that the rescaled density
\rho\mathfrak{a}_{N}^{3}=N^{-2+3\kappa}
tends to zero, as
N\to\infty
.

For
\kappa=0
, (
1.1
) describes a Bose gas in the so-called Gross-Pitaevski regime, for which several rigorous results have been obtained, including convergence of the many-body dynamics towards the time-dependent Gross-Pitaevskii equation
[
13
,
26
,
3
,
10
]
, complete Bose-Einstein condensation
[
20
,
21
,
25
,
4
,
7
,
24
,
17
,
11
]
, bounds on the ground state energy to leading order
[
22
,
25
]
and, recently, up to errors vanishing in the limit of large
N

[
6
]
, as well as estimates on the low-energy excitation spectrum
[
6
]
. For
\kappa=2/3
, the Hamilton operator (
1.1
) describes instead (after appropriate rescaling) a gas in the thermodynamic limit, at fixed density. In this regime, only the ground state energy has been determined rigorously, at leading order
[
23
]
and, recently, including next-order corrections
[
15
]
. In this regime, the existence of Bose-Einstein condensation and the form of the excitation spectrum are still open and in fact they represent very challenging goals in mathematical physics (a review of a long-term project based on a renormalization group approach to these questions can be found in
[
2
]
).

In this paper, we choose
\kappa\in(0;2/3)
small enough, interpolating between the Gross-Pitaevskii and the thermodynamic limit. Our main theorem extends precise estimates on the ground state energy and on low-lying excitations from the Gross-Pitaevskii limit to regimes with
\kappa>0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
V\in L^{3}(\mathbb{R})
be pointwise nonnegative, spherically symmetric and compactly supported and denote by
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
its scattering length, and let
E_{N}
be the ground state of the Hamiltonian
H_{N}
defined in (
1.1
). For
\kappa\geq 0
small enough, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that, in the limit
N\to\infty
,

\begin{split}E_{N}=\;&4\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}N^{\kappa}(N-1)+e_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{a}_{0}N^{\kappa})^{2}\\
&+{1\over 2}\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}\left[\sqrt{|p|^{4}+16\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}p^{2}N^{\kappa}}-p^{2}-8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}N^{\kappa}+{(8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}N^{\kappa})^{2}\over 2p^{2}}\right]+\mathcal{O}(N^{-\varepsilon})\,,\end{split}

(1.2)

with the notation
\Lambda^{*}_{+}=2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
and

e_{\Lambda}=2-\lim_{M\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}_{+}\\
\left|p_{1}\right|,\left|p_{2}\right|,\left|p_{3}\right|\leq M\end{subarray}}{4\cos(\left|p\right|)\over\left|p\right|^{2}}\,,

(1.3)

where, in particular, the limit exists. Moreover, for if
\kappa,\mu>0
are small enough, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that the spectrum of
H_{N}-E_{N}
below the threshold
N^{\kappa/2+\mu}
consists of eigenvalues given, in the limit
N\to\infty
, by

\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}n_{p}\sqrt{|p|^{4}+16\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}N^{\kappa}p^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-\varepsilon})\,,

(1.4)

where
n_{p}\in\mathbb{N}
for all
p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}
and
n_{p}\neq 0
only for a finite number of
p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}
.

Remark:
Approximating the sum on the r.h.s. of (
1.2
) with an integral, we find

\begin{split}E_{N}&=4\pi a_{0}N^{1+\kappa}+\frac{N^{5\kappa/2}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\int\left[\sqrt{|p|^{4}+16\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}p^{2}}-p^{2}-8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}+\frac{(8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0})^{2}}{2p^{2}}\right]dp+\mathcal{O}(N^{2\kappa})\\
&=4\pi a_{0}N^{1+\kappa}+4\pi\cdot\frac{128}{15\sqrt{\pi}}\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{5/2}N^{5\kappa/2}+\mathcal{O}(N^{2\kappa})\end{split}

which is consistent with the Lee-Huang-Yang formula, derived in
[
15
]
for
\kappa=2/3
(after appropriate rescaling). The expansion (
1.2
) is more precise, since it identifies all contributions to the ground state energy that do not vanish as
N\to\infty
(but, of course, it only
holds for
\kappa>0
small enough).

Remark:
Eq. (
1.4
) implies that low-lying excited eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian
H_{N}
have energy of order
N^{\kappa/2}
(for this reason, the interval
[0;N^{\kappa/2+\mu}]
considered in (
1.4
) contains several eigenvalues of the operator
H_{N}
, for any
\mu>0
, in the limit of large
N
). In fact, (
1.4
) also shows that, to leading order, the dispersion of the excitations is linear in
|p|
. By Landau’s criterion, this gives a heuristic explanation of the emergence of superfluidity
[
18
]
.

Remark:
The bounds (
1.2
), (
1.4
) establish the validity of the predictions of Bogoliubov theory
[
8
]
for
\kappa>0
small enough, extending the results obtained in
[
6
]
for
\kappa=0
(and previous results obtained in
[
28
,
16
,
19
,
12
,
27
,
9
]
for mean-field bosons and in
[
5
]
for regimes interpolating between the mean-field and the Gross-Pitaevskii limit).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
follows the strategy developed in
[
7
]
for the Gross-Pitaevskii regime with
\kappa=0
. It makes use, crucially, of recent estimates obtained in
[
1
]
, for sufficiently small
\kappa>0
, for the expectation of the number and the energy of excitations of the condensate (with a rate that becomes optimal as
\kappa\to 0
).

First of all, we factor out the Bose-Einstein condensate, focusing on its orthogonal excitations. To this end, we introduce a unitary operator
U_{N}
, mapping the Hilbert space
L^{2}_{s}(\Lambda^{N})
into the truncated Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{+}^{\leq N}
, constructed on the orthogonal complement of the condensate wave function. This leads us to an excitation Hamiltonian
\mathcal{L}_{N}=U_{N}H_{N}U_{N}^{*}
, acting on
\mathcal{F}_{+}^{\leq N}
.

As a second step, we conjugate
\mathcal{L}_{N}
with a (generalized) Bogoliubov transformation, defining the renormalized excitation Hamiltonian
\mathcal{G}_{N}=e^{-B}\mathcal{L}_{N}e^{B}
. The antisymmetric operator
B
is quadratic in (modified) creation and annihilation operators. Conjugation with
e^{B}
acts on high momenta; it creates short-scale correlations among particles. Even after conjugation with
e^{B}
, there are still some important contributions to the energy hidden in parts of
\mathcal{G}_{N}
that are cubic and quartic in creation and annihilation operators. For this reason, we need to conjugate
\mathcal{G}_{N}
with another unitary operator of the form
e^{A}
, this time with
A
cubic, rather than quadratic, in creation and annihilation operators. We obtain the twice renormalized Hamiltonian
\mathcal{J}_{N}=e^{-A}\mathcal{G}_{N}e^{A}
. Up to error terms that can be estimated with the a-priori bounds from
[
1
]
,
\mathcal{J}_{N}
is the sum of a constant term, a quadratic and a quartic contribution. The quartic part is positive and can be neglected, when proving lower bounds on the ground state energy and on the excited eigenvalues. To show matching upper bounds, we only have to control the quartic potential on appropriate trial states. To conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we still need to diagonalize the quadratic part of
\mathcal{J}_{N}
. To this end, we use a last (generalized) Bogoliubov transformation to define the final excitation Hamiltonian
\mathcal{M}_{N}=e^{-T}\mathcal{J}_{N}e^{T}
, with a diagonal quadratic part.

While the previous steps of the analysis are quite similar to what was done in
[
6
]
for
\kappa=0
, this last step differs substantially. The reason is that, for
\kappa>0
, the (generalized) Bogoliubov transformation
e^{T}
diagonalizing the quadratic component of
\mathcal{J}_{N}
creates a large number of excitations (the number of excitations of the Bose-Einstein condensate is of the order
N^{3\kappa/2}
, and thus diverges, as
N\to\infty
, for any
\kappa>0
). For this reason, it is more difficult to determine the action of
e^{T}
(being
e^{T}
a generalised Bogoliubov transformation, its action is not explicit; it has to be computed through an expansion, whose convergence depends on the size of
T
) and to control the resulting growth of error terms. This part of the analysis, which is carried out in Section
4
and leads to the proof of Theorem
1.1
in Section
5
, is the main novelty of our work. Together with the a-priori bounds on the number and the energy of excitations of the Bose-Einstein condensate from
[
1
]
, this is also the main reason why we have to restrict our analysis to very small values of the parameter
\kappa>0
. While we did not try to optimize the choice of
\kappa
, it is clear that to make it substantially larger and to approach the thermodynamic limit at
\kappa=2/3
, genuinely new ideas are needed. In this direction, let us mention the recent result obtained in
[
14
]
, where the techniques of
[
15
]
have been applied to show the existence of Bose-Einstein condensation up to
\kappa<2/5
; compared with the bounds of
[
1
]
, this approach only controls the expectation of the number of particles and therefore it cannot be directly applied to show Theorem
1.1
. It would in fact be possible to combine the estimate in
[
14
]
with the bounds in
[
1
, Proposition 3.3]
to get a better control of energy and high powers of number of excitations. Since, however, we would still have to restrict to very small values of
\kappa
, we decided to use directly the bounds in
[
1
]
, to keep our analysis as short as possible.

2
Fock Space and the Excitation Hamiltonian

We describe excitations of the Bose-Einstein condensate on the truncated Fock space

\mathcal{F}_{+}^{\leq N}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{N}L^{2}_{\perp}(\Lambda)^{\otimes_{s}k}

built over the orthogonal complement
L^{2}_{\perp}(\Lambda)
of the zero-momentum mode
\varphi_{0}\equiv 1
. We map the original
N
-particle Hilbert space
L^{2}_{s}(\Lambda^{N})
into
\mathcal{F}_{+}^{\leq N}
through the unitary operator
U_{N}:L^{2}_{s}(\Lambda^{N})\to\mathcal{F}_{+}^{\leq N}
, defined by
U_{N}\psi_{N}=\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{N}\}
, with
\alpha_{j}\in L^{2}_{\perp}(\Lambda)^{\otimes_{s}j}
, if

\psi_{N}=\alpha_{0}\varphi_{0}^{\otimes N}+\alpha_{1}\otimes_{s}\varphi_{0}^{\otimes_{s}(N-1)}+\dots+\alpha_{N}

where
\otimes_{s}
denotes the symmetrized tensor product. The map
U_{N}
factors out the Bose-Einstein condensate and allows us to focus on its orthogonal excitations. With
U_{N}
, we can define the excitation Hamiltonian
\mathcal{L}_{N}=U_{N}H_{N}U_{N}^{*}
as a self-adjoint operator on a dense subspace of
\mathcal{F}_{+}^{\leq N}
. Proceeding as in
[
1
, Sect. 2]
, we find

\mathcal{L}_{N}=\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(0)}+\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(2)}+\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(3)}+\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(4)}\,,

with

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(0)}=\,&{N-1\over 2N}N^{\kappa}\widehat{V}(0)(N-\mathcal{N}_{+})+{N^{\kappa}\widehat{V}(0)\over 2N}\mathcal{N}_{+}(N-\mathcal{N}_{+})\\
\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(2)}=\,&\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}p^{2}a_{p}^{*}a_{p}+\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}N^{\kappa}\widehat{V}(p/N^{1-\kappa})\left[b_{p}^{*}b_{p}-{1\over N}a_{p}^{*}a_{p}\right]\\
&+{N^{\kappa}\over 2}\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}\widehat{V}(p/N^{1-\kappa})[b_{p}^{*}b_{-p}^{*}+b_{p}b_{-p}]\\
\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(3)}=\,&{N^{\kappa}\over\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p,q\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}\\
p\neq-q\end{subarray}}\widehat{V}(p/N^{1-\kappa})\left[b_{p+q}^{*}a_{-p}^{*}a_{q}+a_{q}^{*}a_{-p}b_{p+q}\right]\\
\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(4)}=\;&{N^{\kappa}\over 2N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p,q\in\Lambda^{*}_{+},r\in\Lambda^{*}\\
r\neq-p,-q\end{subarray}}\widehat{V}(r/N^{1-\kappa})a_{p+r}^{*}a_{q}^{*}a_{p}a_{q+r}\,,\end{split}

(2.1)

where
\Lambda_{+}^{*}=2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{3}\backslash\{0\}
is the set of possible momenta of excitations of the condensate and where, for any
p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}
,
a^{*}_{p},a_{p}
are the usual creation and annihilation operators. Moreover, for
p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}
, we introduced generalized creation and annihilation operators

\begin{split}b_{p}^{*}&=U_{N}a_{p}^{*}\frac{a_{0}}{\sqrt{N}}U_{N}^{*}=a_{p}^{*}\sqrt{\frac{N-\mathcal{N}_{+}}{N}}\\
b_{p}&=U_{N}\frac{a_{0}^{*}}{\sqrt{N}}a_{p}U_{N}^{*}=\sqrt{\frac{N-\mathcal{N}_{+}}{N}}a_{p}\end{split}

satisfying the approximate CCR

[b_{p},b_{q}]=[b_{p}^{*},b_{q}^{*}]=0,\qquad[b_{p},b_{q}^{*}]=\delta_{p,q}\left(1-{\mathcal{N}_{+}\over N}\right)-{1\over N}a_{q}^{*}a_{p},

(2.2)

together with the useful commutation relations

[b_{p},a^{*}_{q}a_{r}]=\delta_{p,q}b_{r},\qquad[b_{p}^{*},a_{q}^{*}a_{r}]=-\delta_{p,r}b_{q}^{*}.

Observe that, while
a_{p},a_{p}^{*}
do not preserve the truncation on the number of particles, the operators
b_{p},b_{p}^{*}
(and also products of the form
a_{p}^{*}a_{q}
) are well-defined on
\mathcal{F}_{+}^{\leq N}
.

In the following, we will also use the notation

\mathcal{K}=\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}p^{2}a_{p}^{*}a_{p},\qquad\mathcal{V}_{N}=\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(4)}=\;{N^{\kappa}\over 2N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p,q\in\Lambda^{*}_{+},r\in\Lambda^{*}\\
r\neq-p,-q\end{subarray}}\widehat{V}(r/N^{1-\kappa})a_{p+r}^{*}a_{q}^{*}a_{p}a_{q+r}

(2.3)

for the kinetic and the potential energy operators. Moreover, we set
\mathcal{H}_{N}=\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{V}_{N}
. It will sometimes also be useful to switch to position space, introducing, for
x\in\Lambda
, operator-valued distributions
\check{a}_{x},\check{a}^{*}_{x},\check{b}_{x},\check{b}^{*}_{x}
and writing the interaction as

\mathcal{V}_{N}=\frac{1}{2}\int dxdy\,N^{2-2\kappa}V(N^{1-\kappa}(x-y))\check{a}_{x}^{*}\check{a}_{y}^{*}\check{a}_{y}\check{a}_{x}

(2.4)

3
Renormalized Hamiltonians

The vacuum expectation of
\mathcal{L}_{N}
still differs from its ground state (hence that of
H_{N}
) to the leading order
N^{1+\kappa}
. This is because correlations among particles still carry an energy of order
N^{1+\kappa}
in low-energy states. To extract the relevant contributions to the energy from the cubic and quartic terms, we are going to conjugate
\mathcal{L}_{N}
with a generalized Bogoliubov transformation. To choose the appropriate Bogoliubov transformation, we consider, for a fixed
\ell\in(0;1/2)
, the ground state
f_{\ell}
of the Neumann problem

\left[-\Delta+{1\over 2}V\right]f_{\ell}=\lambda_{\ell}f_{\ell}

(3.1)

on
B_{\ell N^{1-\kappa}}
with boundary condition
f_{\ell}(x)=1
for
\left|x\right|=\ell N^{1-\kappa}
. By scaling,
f_{\ell}(N^{1-\kappa}\cdot)
satisfies the equation

\left[-\Delta+N^{2-2\kappa}{1\over 2}V(N^{1-\kappa}\cdot)\right]f_{\ell}(N^{1-\kappa}\cdot)=N^{2-2\kappa}\lambda_{\ell}f_{\ell}(N^{1-\kappa}\cdot)

on
B_{\ell}
. We now define
f_{N}
to be the extension of
f_{\ell}(N^{1-\kappa}\cdot)
to
\Lambda
, obtained by setting
f_{N}(x)=1
if
x\in\Lambda\backslash B_{\ell}
. With
\chi_{\ell}
denoting the characteristic function of
B_{\ell}
we have

\left[-\Delta+N^{2-2\kappa}{1\over 2}V(N^{1-\kappa}\cdot)\right]f_{N}=N^{2-2\kappa}\lambda_{\ell}\chi_{\ell}f_{N}\,.

(3.2)

We further define
w_{\ell}=1-f_{\ell}
on
B_{\ell N^{1-\kappa}}
and its rescaled version
w_{N}=w_{\ell}(N^{1-\kappa}\cdot)=1-f_{N}
on
\Lambda
, with Fourier coefficients

\widehat{w}_{N}(p)=\int_{\Lambda}e^{-ip\cdot x}w_{N}(x)dx={1\over N^{3-3\kappa}}\widehat{w}_{\ell}(p/N^{1-\kappa})=\delta_{p,0}-\widehat{f}_{N}(p)\,.

Some important properties of these functions are collected in the next lemma, whose proof is a straightforward adaptation of
[
6
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix
\ell\in(0;1/2)
and denote by
f_{\ell}
the solution of (
3.1
). For
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough the following properties hold true:

i)

We have

\lambda_{\ell}={3\mathfrak{a}_{0}\over(\ell N^{1-\kappa})^{3}}\left(1+{9\over 5}{\mathfrak{a}_{0}\over\ell N^{1-\kappa}}+\mathcal{O}\left({\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{2}\over(\ell N^{1-\kappa})^{2}}\right)\right)\,.

(3.3)

ii)

We have
0\leq f_{\ell}
,
w_{\ell}\leq 1
, and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left|\int V(x)f_{\ell}(x)dx-8\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}\left(1+{3\over 2}{\mathfrak{a}_{0}\over\ell N^{1-\kappa}}\right)\right|\leq{C\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{3}\over(\ell N^{1-\kappa})^{2}}\,.

(3.4)

iii)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

0\leq w_{\ell}\leq{C\over 1+\left|x\right|}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left|\nabla w_{\ell}\right|\leq{C\over 1+\left|x\right|^{2}}\,,

(3.5)

and precisely

\left|{1\over(\ell N^{1-\kappa})^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w_{\ell}(x)dx-{2\over 5}\pi\mathfrak{a}_{0}\right|\leq{C\mathfrak{a}_{0}\over\ell N^{1-\kappa}}\,.

(3.6)

iv)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\widehat{w}_{N}(p)\leq{CN^{\kappa}\over N\left|p\right|^{2}}

(3.7)

for all
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough (such that
N^{\kappa}/N\leq 1/\ell
).

We define now
\eta:\Lambda^{*}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
through

\eta_{p}=-N\widehat{w}_{N}(p)=-{N^{\kappa}\over N^{2-2\kappa}}\widehat{w}_{\ell}(p/N^{1-\kappa})\,,

In particular,
\widehat{f}_{N}(p)=\delta_{p,0}+\eta_{p}/N
. In position space, we have

\check{\eta}(x)=-Nw_{\ell}(N^{1-\kappa}x)

for
x\in\Lambda
. From Lemma
3.1
, and in particular Eq. (
3.5
), (
3.7
), we obtain

\eta_{p}\leq{CN^{\kappa}\over p^{2}}\,,\qquad{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\eta\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{2}\leq CN^{\kappa}\,,\qquad{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\check{\eta}\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{\infty}\leq CN

(3.8)

Moreover, from (
3.5
) we deduce

{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\eta\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{H^{1}}^{2}=N^{4-2\kappa}{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\nabla w_{\ell}(N^{1-\kappa}\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{2}^{2}\leq\int_{B_{\ell}}dx{CN^{4-2\kappa}\over(1+N^{2-2\kappa}\left|x\right|^{2})^{2}}\leq CN^{1+\kappa}.

The scattering equation (
3.2
) translates to Fourier space into

p^{2}\widehat{f}_{N}(p)+{N^{\kappa}\over 2N}(\widehat{V}(\cdot/N^{1-\kappa})\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{p}=N^{2-2\kappa}\lambda_{\ell}(\widehat{\chi}_{\ell}\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{p}\,,

(3.9)

for
p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}
or, equivalently,

\begin{split}p^{2}\eta_{p}+{1\over 2}N^{\kappa}\widehat{V}(p/N^{1-\kappa})+{1\over 2N}&\sum_{q\in\Lambda^{*}}N^{\kappa}\widehat{V}((p-q)/N^{1-\kappa})\eta_{q}\\
&=N^{3-2\kappa}\lambda_{\ell}\widehat{\chi}_{\ell}(p)+N^{2-2\kappa}\lambda_{\ell}\sum_{q\in\Lambda^{*}}\widehat{\chi}_{\ell}(p-q)\eta_{q}.\end{split}

(3.10)

In order to make the
\ell^{2}
-norm of
\eta
small (which is important to control the action of the corresponding generalized Bogoliubov transformation), we introduce an infrared cutoff, restricting
\eta
to high momenta. For an
\alpha>0
to be specified below, we define
P_{H}:=\{p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}\,:\,\left|p\right|\geq N^{\alpha}\}
and the coefficients

\eta_{H}(p)=\eta_{p}\chi_{P_{H}}(p)\,.

Then, we have

\begin{split}{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\eta_{H}\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{2}&\leq CN^{\kappa-\alpha/2},\hskip 14.22636pt{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\eta_{H}\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{H^{1}}\leq CN^{(1+\kappa)/2},\hskip 14.22636pt{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\eta_{H}\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{\infty}\leq CN^{\kappa-\alpha}\,,\end{split}

and moreover, from Lemma
3.1
and (
3.8
),

\left|\check{\eta}_{H}(x)\right|=\Big|\sum_{\left|p\right|\geq N^{\alpha}}e^{ip\cdot x}\eta_{p}\Big|\leq\left|\check{\eta}(x)\right|+\sum_{\left|p\right|<N^{\alpha}}\left|\eta_{p}\right|\leq C(N+N^{\kappa+\alpha})\,.

We assume throughout the following analysis that
\alpha>2\kappa,\,\kappa+\alpha<1
so that

\begin{gathered}{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\eta_{H}\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{2}\rightarrow 0\,,{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\eta_{H}\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{\infty}\rightarrow 0\,\quad\mbox{as }N\rightarrow\infty\,,\hskip 28.45274pt{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\check{\eta}_{H}\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{\infty}\leq CN\,.\end{gathered}

(3.11)

With the coefficients
\eta_{H}
, we define the antisymmetric operator

on powers of the number of particles operator. The proof of the following lemma can be found in
[

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
\alpha>2\kappa
. For every

e^{-B}(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)^{j}e^{B}\leq C(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)^{j}\,.

On states with few excitations, the generalized Bogoliubov transformation

acts approximately like a standard Bogoliubov transformation. To make this statement more precise, for

\gamma_{p}=\cosh(\eta_{H}(p))

\sigma_{p}=\sinh(\eta_{H}(p))

\begin{split}e^{-B}b_{p}e^{B}&=\gamma_{p}b_{p}+\sigma_{p}b_{-p}^{*}+d_{p}\\

\left|\sigma_{p}\right|\leq{CN^{\kappa}\over\left|p\right|^{2}},\,\qquad\left|\sigma_{p}-\eta_{p}\right|\leq{CN^{3\kappa}\over\left|p\right|^{6}}\,,\qquad\left|\gamma_{p}\right|\leq C\,,\qquad\left|\gamma_{p}-1\right|\leq{CN^{2\kappa}\over\left|p\right|^{4}}\,.

(3.14)

In position space, we have

{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\check{\sigma}\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{2}={\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\sigma\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{2}\leq CN^{\kappa-\alpha/2}\rightarrow 0\,,\qquad{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\check{\sigma}\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{\infty}\leq CN\,,\qquad{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\check{\sigma}\star\check{\gamma}\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{\infty}\leq CN\,.

In the next lemma, taken from

Lemma 3.3
.

For

be defined as in (

(3.16)

Moreover, defining

\begin{split}\|(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)^{n/2}\check{d}_{x}\xi\|&\leq{C\over N}\left[\|(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)^{(n+3)/2}\xi\|+\|\check{b}_{x}(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)^{(n+2)/2}\xi\|\right]\\

(3.17)

We can now define the renormalized excitation Hamiltonian

\mathcal{G}_{N}=e^{-B}\mathcal{L}_{N}e^{B}.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that
2\kappa<\alpha<1/2

\mathcal{G}_{N}=C_{\mathcal{G}_{N}}+Q_{\mathcal{G}_{N}}+\mathcal{H}_{N}+\mathcal{C}_{N}+\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}_{N}}\,,

\begin{split}Q_{\mathcal{G}_{N}}=\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}\left[\Phi_{p}b_{p}^{*}b_{p}+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{p}\left(b_{p}^{*}b_{-p}^{*}+b_{p}b_{-p}\right)\right]\,\end{split}

with (recall the convention that

While the vacuum expectation of the renormalized excitation Hamiltonian (

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

Proposition 3.7

Let

\mathcal{J}_{N}=C_{\mathcal{J}_{N}}+\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{J}_{N}}+\mathcal{V}_{N}+\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{J}_{N}}\,,

4
Diagonalization of the Quadratic Hamiltonian

In this section we apply a final (generalized) Bogoliubov transformation to the renormalized Hamiltonian
\mathcal{J}_{N}
in order to diagonalize its quadratic part. First, we prove some bounds on the coefficients
F_{p},\,G_{p}
defined in (
3.26
), (
3.27
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume (
3.24
). There exist constants
C>c>0
independent of
N
, such that for
N
large enough, we have:

i)

c(p^{2}+N^{\kappa})\leq F_{p}\leq C(p^{2}+N^{\kappa})
for all
p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}
,

ii)

For all
p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}
,

c\leq 1+\frac{G_{p}}{F_{p}}\leq 2\,,\quad c\cdot\min\Big[1,p^{2}/N^{\kappa}\Big]\leq 1-\frac{G_{p}}{F_{p}}\leq C.

(4.1)

In particular, this implies that
|G_{p}|/F_{p}\leq 1-cN^{-\kappa}
, for all
p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}
.

iii)

\left|G_{p}\right|\leq CN^{\kappa}
for
p\in P_{H}^{c}
,

iv)

\left|G_{p}\right|\leq CN^{2\kappa}\left|p\right|^{-2}
for
p\in P_{H}
.

Proof.

We start by proving
i)
. For
p\in P_{H}^{c}
, we write

F_{p}=p^{2}+N^{\kappa}(\widehat{V}(\cdot/N^{1-\kappa})\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{0}+N^{\kappa}\left((\widehat{V}(\cdot/N^{1-\kappa})\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{p}-(\widehat{V}(\cdot/N^{1-\kappa})\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{0}\right)

and we observe that, by (
3.4
),
(\widehat{V}(\cdot/N^{1-\kappa})\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{0}\geq C>0
, uniformly in
N
. With

\left|(\widehat{V}(\cdot/N^{1-\kappa})\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{p}-(\widehat{V}(\cdot/N^{1-\kappa})\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{0}\right|\leq C\left|p\right|/N^{1-\kappa}\leq CN^{\alpha+\kappa-1},

for all
p\in P_{H}^{c}
, we conclude that

\widetilde{c}(p^{2}+N^{\kappa})-CN^{2\kappa+\alpha-1}\leq F_{p}\leq\widetilde{C}(p^{2}+N^{\kappa})+CN^{2\kappa+\alpha-1}.

This gives
i)
for
N
sufficiently large, thanks to
2\kappa+\alpha-1<0
. For
p\in P_{H}
we proceed similarly, using the fact that
\big|(\widehat{V}(\cdot/N^{1-\kappa})\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{p}\big|\leq C
, uniformly in
N
. As for
ii)
, we compute
F_{p}-G_{p}=(\gamma_{p}-\sigma_{p})^{2}p^{2}
(which in particular implies that
G_{p}\leq F_{p}
). With
i)
, distinguishing the cases
|p|\leq N^{\kappa/2}
and
|p|>N^{\kappa/2}
(and recalling that
\gamma_{p}=1
,
\sigma_{p}=0
for
p\in P_{H}^{c}
), we obtain (
4.1
). Part
iii)
follows immediately from
\big|(\widehat{V}(\cdot/N^{1-\kappa})\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{p}\big|\leq C
. To prove
iv)
, we can use the scattering equation. Indeed, for
p\in P_{H}
, we have by (
3.14
) that

\begin{split}2p^{2}\left|\sigma_{p}\gamma_{p}-\eta_{p}\right|&\leq CN^{3\kappa}\left|p\right|^{-4}\leq CN^{2\kappa}\left|p\right|^{-2}\,,\\
N^{\kappa}(\widehat{V}(\cdot/N^{1-\kappa})\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{p}\left|(\gamma_{p}+\sigma_{p})^{2}-1\right|&\leq CN^{\kappa}(\widehat{V}(\cdot/N^{1-\kappa})\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{p}\left|\sigma_{p}\gamma_{p}+\sigma_{p}^{2}\right|\\
&\leq CN^{2\kappa}\left|p\right|^{-2}\,.\end{split}

Thus, it is enough to prove that
p^{2}\eta_{p}+N^{\kappa}(\widehat{V}(\cdot/N^{1-\kappa})\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{p}
satisfies the desired bound. By the scattering equation (
3.9
) and the bound (
4.3
), we find

p^{2}\eta_{p}+N^{\kappa}(\widehat{V}(\cdot/N^{1-\kappa})\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{p}=N^{3-2\kappa}\lambda_{\ell}(\widehat{\chi}_{\ell}\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{p}\leq CN^{\kappa}\left|p\right|^{-2}.

(4.2)

To show the last inequality, we remark that

\widehat{\chi}_{\ell}(p)=\int_{\left|x\right|\leq\ell}e^{-ip\cdot x}={4\pi\over\left|p\right|^{2}}\left({\sin(\ell\left|p\right|)\over\left|p\right|}-\ell\cos(\ell\left|p\right|)\right)\,,

which in particular implies that
|\widehat{\chi}_{\ell}(p)|\leq C/p^{2}
(recall that
\ell<1/2
). Moreover, recalling that
\check{\eta}
is supported in
B_{\ell}
, we find, with (

|(\widehat{\chi}_{\ell}\star\eta)_{q}|=|\widehat{(\chi_{\ell}\check{\eta})}(q)|=|\eta_{q}|\leq CN^{\kappa}/q^{2}
for all

\left|(\widehat{\chi}_{\ell}\star\widehat{f}_{N})_{p}\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left|p\right|^{2}}\,.

(4.3)

which implies (

). This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

w-v\in P_{H}\end{subarray}}N^{\kappa}\widehat{V}((v-w)/N^{1-\kappa})\eta_{r}b_{r+v}^{*}b_{-r}^{*}\left(1-{\mathcal{N}_{+}\over N}\right)b_{w-v}^{*}b_{w},

\displaystyle\Pi_{5}=\,

\displaystyle N^{-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in P_{H}\\
v\in P_{L}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in P_{H}\\
w\in P_{L}\end{subarray}}N^{\kappa}\widehat{V}(s/N^{1-\kappa})\eta_{r}b_{v}^{*}(b_{-r}a_{s+w}^{*}a_{r+v}+a_{s+w}^{*}a_{-r}b_{r+v})b_{-s}^{*}b_{w},

\displaystyle\Pi_{6}=\,

\displaystyle-N^{-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in P_{H}\\
v\in P_{L}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in P_{H}\\
w\in P_{L}\end{subarray}}N^{\kappa}\widehat{V}(s/N^{1-\kappa})\eta_{r}b_{r+v}^{*}b_{-r}^{*}a_{s+w}^{*}a_{v}b_{-s}^{*}b_{w},

\displaystyle\Pi_{7}=\,

\displaystyle-N^{-1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in P_{H}\\
v\in P_{L}\end{subarray}}N^{\kappa}\widehat{V}(r/N^{1-\kappa})b_{r+v}^{*}[b_{-r}^{*},A]b_{v},

\displaystyle\Pi_{8}=\,

\displaystyle-N^{-1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in P_{H}\\
v\in P_{L}\end{subarray}}N^{\kappa}\widehat{V}(r/N^{1-\kappa})b_{r+v}^{*}b_{-r}^{*}[b_{v},A]\,.

This is (
B.12
), with
\delta_{\Theta_{0}}=\delta+\sum_{j=1}^{8}[\Pi_{j}+\mathrm{h.c.}]
. We proceed to bound the terms in the error to show (
B.14
). The characteristic function in
\delta
vanishes for any
v
, if
\left|r\right|>N^{\alpha}+N^{\beta}
: in particular,

\langle{\xi},\delta{\xi}\rangle\leq CN^{-1+\kappa}\langle{\xi},\mathcal{N}{\xi}\rangle\Big(\sum_{|r|\leq 2N^{\alpha}}|\eta_{r}|\Big)\leq CN^{-1+2\kappa+\alpha}\langle{\xi},\mathcal{N}{\xi}\rangle.

The term
\Pi_{1}
is easily bounded by Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality and
\sum_{r\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}|\widehat{V}(r/N^{1-\kappa})\eta_{r}|\leq CN
: we find
\pm\Pi_{1}\leq CN^{-1+\kappa}(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)^{2}\leq CN^{-1/2}(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)^{2}
. As for
\Pi_{2}
we estimate

\begin{split}|\langle{\xi},\Pi_{2}{\xi}\rangle|\leq\,&CN^{-1+\kappa}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in P_{H}\\
v\in P_{L}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in P_{L}\\
w-r-v\in P_{H}\end{subarray}}\left|\eta_{r}\right|^{2}{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|b_{w-r-v}b_{v}\xi\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
&\qquad\times\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in P_{H}\\
v\in P_{L}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in P_{L}\\
w-r-v\in P_{H}\end{subarray}}{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|b_{-r}b_{-w}\xi\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
\leq\,&CN^{-1+\kappa}{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|\eta_{H}\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}_{2}\left|P_{L}\right|^{1/2}{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)^{2}\xi\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}^{2}\\
\leq\,&CN^{-1/2}{\left|\hskip-0.50003pt\left|(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)^{2}\xi\right|\hskip-0.50003pt\right|}^{2}\,,\end{split}

where we used
\alpha-\beta>2\beta+4\kappa-1
in the last step. The terms
\Pi_{3},\Pi_{4}
can be bounded similarly. The terms
\Pi_{5},\Pi_{6}
are first rearranged in normal order, producing additional terms from commutators, and then they are bounded as we did for
\Xi_{5},\Xi_{6}
in the proof of Lemma
B.3
. We find
\Pi_{5},\Pi_{6}\leq CN^{-1/2}(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)^{3}
. Expanding the commutator in
\Pi_{7}
we find terms similar to
\Pi_{1},...,\Pi_{6}
, which are bounded in the same way. Finally, the term
\Pi_{8}
is analogous to
\Xi_{8}
in Lemma
B.3
and can be bounded similarly, using the kinetic energy operator. This yields
\Pi_{8}\leq CN^{-1/2}(\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{N}_{+}^{2}+1)(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)
. Collecting all bounds, we get (
B.14
). Finally, the bound (
B.15
) follows from the fact that
\Pi_{0}=-\Xi_{0}
and from (
B.7
).
∎

Applying the last two lemmas, we can now control the action of

on

Lemma B.7
.

We have

\begin{split}e^{-A}\mathcal{H}_{N}e^{A}=\,&\mathcal{H}_{N}-N^{-1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in P_{H}\\
v\in P_{L}\end{subarray}}N^{\kappa}\widehat{V}(r/N^{1-\kappa})\Big[b_{r+v}^{*}b_{-r}^{*}b_{v}+\mathrm{h.c.}\Big]\\
&-N^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in P_{H}\\
v\in P_{L}\end{subarray}}N^{\kappa}\left(\widehat{V}(r/N^{1-\kappa})+\widehat{V}((r+v)/N^{1-\kappa})\right)\eta_{r}b_{v}^{*}b_{v}+\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}_{N}}\,,\end{split}

with

\pm\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}_{N}}\leq CN^{-1/2+\beta/2+2\kappa}(\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{N}_{+}^{2}+1)(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)\,.

Proof.

With Lemma
B.5
and Lemma
B.6
, we find

\begin{split}e^{-A}\mathcal{H}_{N}e^{A}=\,&\mathcal{H}_{N}+\int_{0}^{1}e^{-sA}\Theta_{0}e^{sA}ds+\int_{0}^{1}e^{-sA}\delta_{\mathcal{H}_{N}}e^{sA}ds\\
=\,&\mathcal{H}_{N}+\Theta_{0}+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}e^{-s_{2}A}\Pi_{0}e^{s_{2}A}ds_{2}ds_{1}\\
&+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}e^{-s_{2}A}\delta_{\Theta_{0}}e^{s_{2}A}ds_{2}ds_{1}+\int_{0}^{1}e^{-sA}\delta_{\mathcal{H}_{N}}e^{sA}ds\\
=\,&\mathcal{H}_{N}+\Theta_{0}+{1\over 2}\Pi_{0}+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\int_{0}^{s_{2}}e^{-s_{3}A}[\Pi_{0},A]e^{s_{3}A}ds_{3}ds_{2}ds_{1}\\
&+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}e^{-s_{2}A}\delta_{\Theta_{0}}e^{s_{2}A}ds_{2}ds_{1}+\int_{0}^{1}e^{-sA}\delta_{\mathcal{H}_{N}}e^{sA}ds\\
=\,&\mathcal{H}_{N}+\Theta_{0}+{1\over 2}\Pi_{0}+\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}_{N}},\end{split}

and the claim follows by (
B.10
) (
B.13
), the bounds (
B.11
), (
B.14
) and Lemma
3.6
.
∎

B.4
Proof of Proposition
3.7

Bringing together the results of Lemma
B.2
, Lemma
B.4
and Lemma

B.7
, we find that

\begin{split}e^{-A}\mathcal{G}_{N}e^{A}=\,&C_{\mathcal{G}_{N}}+\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{G}_{N}}+\mathcal{C}_{N}+\mathcal{H}_{N}\\
&+N^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in P_{H}\\
v\in P_{L}\end{subarray}}N^{\kappa}\left(\widehat{V}(r/N^{1-\kappa})+\widehat{V}((r+v)/N^{1-\kappa})\right)\eta_{r}b_{v}^{*}b_{v}\\
&-N^{-1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in P_{H}\\
v\in P_{L}\end{subarray}}N^{\kappa}\widehat{V}(r/N^{1-\kappa})\Big[b_{r+v}^{*}b_{-r}^{*}b_{v}+\mathrm{h.c.}\Big]+\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{J}_{N}}+e^{-A}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}_{N}}e^{A}\,,\end{split}

(B.16)

with

\pm\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{J}_{N}}\leq CN^{-{1\over 2}+{\beta\over 2}+2\kappa}(\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{N}_{+}^{2}+1)(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)\,.

We also notice that, by Equation (
3.22
) and Lemma
3.6
, we have

\pm e^{-A}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}_{N}}e^{A}\leq CN^{-{1\over 2}+5\kappa/2+\alpha}(\mathcal{H}_{N}+\mathcal{N}_{+}^{2}+1)(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)\,.

We consider

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}_{N}-N^{-1/2}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in P_{H}\\

It is simple to check that

\pm(Z_{2}+\mathrm{h.c.})\leq CN^{-{1\over 2}+{\alpha\over 2}}(\mathcal{K}+1)(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1).

The term

, on the other hand, can be bounded in two ways, leading to the estimates (

\begin{split}|\langle{\xi},Z_{1}{\xi}\rangle|\leq\,&N^{-1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\Lambda^{*}_{+},v\in P_{L}^{c}\\

In the second case, we switch to position space and find for any

\begin{split}\big|\langle\xi|Z_{1}|\xi\rangle\big|&\leq\int dxdy\,N^{5/2-2\kappa}V(N^{1-\kappa}(x-y))|\Big|\langle\xi,\check{b}^{*}_{x}\check{b}^{*}_{y}\sum_{v\in P_{L}^{c}}e^{ivy}(\gamma_{v}b_{v}+\sigma_{v}b_{-v}^{*})|\xi\rangle\Big|\\

\begin{split}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{2}=&-N^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in P_{H},v\in P_{L}^{c}\end{subarray}}N^{\kappa}\left(\widehat{V}(r/N^{1-\kappa})+\widehat{V}((r+v)/N^{1-\kappa})\right)\eta_{r}b_{v}^{*}b_{v}\\

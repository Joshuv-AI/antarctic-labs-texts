Alternating Multiple Mixed Values: Regularization, Special Values, Parity and Dimension Conjectures
Ce Xu, Lu Yan and Jianqiang Zhao[1mm] a. School of Mathematics and Statistics, Anhui Normal University, Wuhu 241002, P.R. Chinab. School of Mathematical Sciences, Tongji University, Shanghai 200092, P.R. Chinac. Department of Mathematics, The Bishop’s School, La Jolla, CA 92037, United States of America

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we define and study a variant of multiple zeta values (MZVs) of level four, called alternating multiple mixed values or alternating multiple
M
-values (AMMVs), forming a
\mathbb{Q}[i]
-subspace of the colored MZVs of level four. This variant includes the alternating version of Hoffman’s multiple
t
-values, Kaneko-Tsumura’s multiple
T
-values, and the multiple
S
-values studied by the authors previously as special cases. We exhibit nice properties similar to the ordinary MZVs such as the generalized duality, integral shuffle and series stuffle relations. After setting up the algebraic framework we derive the regularized double shuffle relations of the AMMVs by adopting the machinery from color MZVs of level four. As an important application, we prove a parity result for AMMVs previously conjectured by us. We also investigate several alternating multiple
S
- and
T
-values by establishing some explicit relations of integrals involving arctangent function. At the end, we compute the dimensions of a few interesting subspaces of AMMVs for weight less than 9. Supported by theoretical and numerical evidence aided by numerical and symbolic computation, we formulate a few conjectures concerning the dimensions of the above-mentioned subspaces of AMMVs. These conjectures hint at a few very rich but previously overlooked algebraic and geometric structures associated with these vector spaces.

Keywords
: (colored) multiple zeta values; (alternating) multiple mixed values; (alternating) multiple
S
-values; (alternating) multiple
T
-values; regularization; duality; parity.

One can extend these to iterated integrals over any piecewise smooth path on the complex plane via pull-backs.
We refer the interested reader to Chen’s original work

The primary goals of this paper are to study the explicit relations of AMMVs and some of their special types, and

3
Regularization for divergent AMMVs and double shuffle relations

Since there are no weight 1 MZVs (as
\zeta(1)
diverges), it is impossible to prove the identity

\zeta(2,1)=\zeta(3)
via a finite DBSF. However, one can remedy this by considering the

regularized double shuffle relation
(regularized DBSF) produced by the following mechanism.

First, combining Prop.
2.2
and Prop.
2.5
and using
the algebra structures of
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\shuffle$}}}^{1}
(resp.
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\shuffle}^{1}
) over
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\shuffle$}}}^{0}
(resp.
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\shuffle}^{0}
)
we can prove the following algebraic result without too much difficulty.

Proposition 3.1
.

We have an algebra homomorphism:

{\text{{{M}}}}_{\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\shuffle$}}}:({\mathfrak{A}}_{\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\shuffle$}}}^{1},\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\shuffle$}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}[T]

which is uniquely determined by the properties that it extends the evaluation map
M:{\mathfrak{A}}_{\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\shuffle$}}}^{0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
by sending

\emph{\texttt{z}}_{1}
to
T
and
\emph{\texttt{z}}_{\wc{1}}
to
T+2\log 2
. Further, we have an algebra homomorphism:

{\text{{{M}}}}_{\shuffle}:({\mathfrak{A}}_{\shuffle}^{1},\shuffle)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}[T]

which is uniquely determined by the properties that it
extends the evaluation map
{\text{{{M}}}}:{\mathfrak{A}}_{\shuffle}^{0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
by sending

\emph{\texttt{z}}_{1}
to
T-\log 2
and
\emph{\texttt{z}}_{\wc{1}}
to
T+\log 2
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as that for the corresponding results of MMVs.
See
[
29
, Prop. 2.7 and Prop. 2.8]
. The key observations for
the stuffle regularization are

\displaystyle M_{N}(1)=\,\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\displaystyle\frac{2}{2k}\,=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}=\log N+\gamma+

\displaystyle O(N^{-1}\log N),

\displaystyle M_{N}(\wc{1})=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\displaystyle\frac{2}{2k-1}=\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{2N}\displaystyle\frac{2}{l}-S_{N}(1)=

\displaystyle 2(\log 2N+\gamma)-\log N-\gamma+O(N^{-1}\log N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log N+\gamma+2\log 2+O(N^{-1}\log N).

By the usual regularization process we see that
{\texttt{M}}_{\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\shuffle$}}}({\texttt{z}}_{1;1}^{1})=T
and
{\texttt{M}}_{\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\shuffle$}}}(\wc{{\texttt{z}}}_{1;1}^{-1})=T+2\log 2
.

For the shuffle regularization, we take an arbitrarily small
\lambda>0
and find that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-\lambda}{\omega}^{+1}_{+1}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-\lambda}\displaystyle\frac{2tdt}{1-t^{2}}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-\lambda}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1-t}-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-\lambda}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1+t}=-\log\lambda-\log 2+O(\lambda),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-\lambda}{\omega}^{-1}_{+1}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-\lambda}\displaystyle\frac{2dt}{1-t^{2}}=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-\lambda}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1-t}+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-\lambda}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{1+t}=-\log\lambda+\log 2+O(\lambda).

By the usual regularization process we see that
{\texttt{M}}_{\shuffle}({\omega}^{+1}_{+1})=T-\log 2
and
{\texttt{M}}_{\shuffle}({\omega}^{-1}_{+1})=T+\log 2
.
The rest of the proof is essentially the same as that for the corresponding result of MZVs and is thus left
to the interested reader.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The following question was posed by the referee: is it possible to choose the images of
M(1)
and
M(\wc{1})

independently? This may be possible and may play some important roles when we consider generating functions
of AMMVs in general. However, the additional complication is unnecessary for our purpose in this paper.

The following structural result is useful in proving the comparison theorem between the two different ways
of regularization of AMMVs. Let
\mathsf{AMMV}_{w}
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space generated by all the AMMVs of weight
w

and denote all its subspaces similarly.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any
w\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{Q}[i]}\mathsf{AMMV}_{w}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[i]=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}[i]}\mathsf{CMZV}^{4}_{w}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[i].

Proof.

For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
,
{\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}}}=({\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,{\varepsilon}_{r})\in\{\pm 1\}^{r}
, and
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r})\in\{\pm 1\}^{r}
, the partial sum of AMMV truncated at
2N
is

\displaystyle M_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})_{N}:={}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{2N\geq m_{1}>\cdots>m_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{(1+{\varepsilon}_{1}(-1)^{m_{1}})\sigma_{1}^{(2m_{1}+1-{\varepsilon}_{1})/4}\cdots(1+{\varepsilon}_{r}(-1)^{m_{r}})\sigma_{r}^{(2m_{r}+1-{\varepsilon}_{r})/4}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{2N\geq m_{1}>\cdots>m_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{(1+{\varepsilon}_{1}(-1)^{m_{1}})\mu_{1}^{m_{1}+(1-{\varepsilon}_{1})/2}\cdots(1+{\varepsilon}_{r}(-1)^{m_{r}})\mu_{r}^{m_{r}+(1-{\varepsilon}_{r})/2}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}^{(1-{\varepsilon}_{j})/2}\bigg)\displaystyle\sum_{2N\geq m_{1}>\cdots>m_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{(1+{\varepsilon}_{1}(-1)^{m_{1}})\mu_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots(1+{\varepsilon}_{r}(-1)^{m_{r}})\mu_{r}^{m_{r}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}

(3.22)

where
\mu_{j}=i
if
\sigma_{j}=-1
and
\mu_{j}=1
if
\sigma_{j}=1
. Taking
N\to\infty
we see that
\mathsf{AMMV}_{w}\subseteq\mathsf{CMZV}^{4}_{w}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[i]
.

On the other hand, for any
\xi_{j}\in\{e^{\pi i/2},e^{\pi i},e^{3\pi i/2},e^{2\pi i}\}=\{\pm 1,\pm i\}
, we have

\displaystyle\xi_{j}^{n_{j}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{\pm 1,\pm i(-1)^{{(n_{j}-1)}/{2}}\}&\quad\hbox{if $n_{j}$\ \text{odd};}\\
\{1,\pm(-1)^{{n_{j}}/{2}}\}&\quad\hbox{if $n_{j}$\ \text{even},}\end{array}\right.

and therefore according to the definition of colored MZVs of level four,

\displaystyle\Li_{k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{r}}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{r})={}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots\xi_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\delta_{1}=0}^{1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum_{\delta_{r}=0}^{1}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0\\
n_{j}\equiv\delta_{j}\pmod{2}\ \forall j\end{subarray}}\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots\xi_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}\in\mathsf{AMMV}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[i].

(3.23)

Hence,
\mathsf{CMZV}^{4}_{w}\subseteq\mathsf{AMMV}_{w}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[i]
. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We can now apply the same mechanism as in
[
16
]
to derive the following regularized DBSFs.

Theorem 3.4
.

Define an
\mathbb{R}
-linear map
\rho:\mathbb{R}[T]\to\mathbb{R}[T]
by

\rho(e^{Tu})=\exp\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\zeta(n)u^{n}\right)e^{(T-\log 2)u},\qquad|u|<1.

Then for any
{\bf w}\in{\mathfrak{A}}^{1}
one has

{\text{{{M}}}}_{\shuffle}({\bf w};T)=\rho\circ{\text{{{M}}}}_{\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\shuffle$}}}({\bf w};T).

Proof.

It is possible to modify the proof contained in
[
16
]
and adapt to our situation.
However, the process is tedious and requires a lot computation and estimates. See
[
20
]

for a detailed presentation for the so-called
\mu
-multiple Hurwitz zeta values. By taking
\mu=2

and
m
’s to be arbitrary positive integers in
[
20
]
we recover the proof of the comparison theorem
for the regularization of the non-alternating MMVs.

Instead of reinventing the wheel for AMMVs, we can use Theorem
3.3
and the regularization
of CMZVs to prove the current theorem. Indeed, using the same notation as in Theorem
3.3
,
by (
3
), we see that

\displaystyle M_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})_{N}={}

\displaystyle\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}^{(1-{\varepsilon}_{j})/2}\bigg)\displaystyle\sum_{\delta_{1}=\pm 1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum_{\delta_{r}=\pm 1}\Big(\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{1}(\delta_{1})\cdots\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{r}(\delta_{r})\Big)\Li^{(2N)}_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}(\delta_{1}\mu_{1},\dots,\delta_{r}\mu_{r})

(3.24)

where
\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{j}(1)=1
and
\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{j}(-1)={\varepsilon}_{j}
,
\mu_{j}=i
or 1 according to
\mu_{j}^{2}=\sigma_{j}
, and
\Li^{(2N)}
denotes the partial sum of
the CMZV truncated at
2N
. By the regularization scheme for CMVZs (see
[
40
, Ch. 13]
), as
N\to\infty
,

\Li^{(2N)}_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}(\delta_{1}\mu_{1},\dots,\delta_{r}\mu_{r})=\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}^{\ast}(\delta_{1}\mu_{1},\dots,\delta_{r}\mu_{r};T+\log 2)\big|_{T=\log N+\gamma}+O(N^{-1}\log^{r}N),

where
\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}^{\ast}(\delta_{1}\mu_{1},\dots,\delta_{r}\mu_{r};T)\in\mathbb{C}[T]
. Therefore, setting
{\bf w}={\omega}_{0}^{k_{1}-1}{\omega}_{\sigma_{1}}^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\cdots{\omega}_{0}^{k_{r}-1}{\omega}_{\sigma_{r}}^{{\varepsilon}_{r}}
, we get

{\texttt{M}}_{\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\shuffle$}}}({\bf w};T)=\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}^{(1-{\varepsilon}_{j})/2}\bigg)\displaystyle\sum_{\delta_{1}=\pm 1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum_{\delta_{r}=\pm 1}\Big(\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{1}(\delta_{1})\cdots\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{r}(\delta_{r})\Big)\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}^{\ast}(\delta_{1}\mu_{1},\dots,\delta_{r}\mu_{r};T+\log 2).

(3.25)

Moreover, if we define the
\mathbb{C}
-linear map
\tau:\mathbb{C}[T]\to\mathbb{C}[T]

such that
\tau(T)=T-\log 2
and define the
\mathbb{C}
-linear map
\tilde{\rho}:\mathbb{C}[T]\to\mathbb{C}[T]
such that

\tilde{\rho}(e^{Tu})=\exp\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\zeta(n)u^{n}\right)e^{Tu},\qquad|u|<1,

then for
\rho=\tilde{\rho}\circ\tau
we get

\rho(\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}^{\ast}(\delta_{1}\mu_{1},\dots,\delta_{r}\mu_{r};T))=\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}^{\shuffle}(\delta_{1}^{\prime}\mu_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\delta_{r}^{\prime}\mu_{r}^{\prime};T)

(3.26)

where
\delta_{j}^{\prime}=\prod_{n=1}^{j}\delta_{n}
,
\mu_{j}^{\prime}=\prod_{n=1}^{j}\mu_{n}
and
\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}^{\shuffle}({\boldsymbol{\xi}};T)\in\mathbb{C}[T]
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-\lambda}{\omega}_{0}^{k_{1}-1}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{\xi_{1}^{-1}-t}\cdots{\omega}_{0}^{k_{1}-1}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{\xi_{r}^{-1}-t}=\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}^{\shuffle}({\boldsymbol{\xi}};-\log\lambda)+O(\lambda\log^{r}\lambda)

as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
, for all
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in\{\pm 1,\pm i\}^{r}
.
It is clear that
\rho=\tilde{\rho}\circ\tau
satisfies the conditions given in the statement of the theorem.

On the other hand, setting
\kappa_{j}=\prod_{n=1}^{j}\sigma_{n}
for
j=1,\dots,r
,
we obtain from (
1.4
) the shuffle regularized value of

M_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{{\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-\lambda}{\omega}_{0}^{k_{1}-1}W_{\kappa_{1}}^{{\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2}}\cdots{\omega}_{0}^{k_{r-1}-1}W_{\kappa_{r-1}}^{{\varepsilon}_{r-1},{\varepsilon}_{r}}{\omega}_{0}^{k_{r}-1}{\omega}_{\kappa_{r}}^{{\varepsilon}_{r}}.

(3.27)

To save space, for any 4-th root of unity
\mu
we put

{\texttt{x}}(\mu)=\displaystyle\frac{dt}{\mu^{-1}-t}.

Then by definition (
1.2
)

\displaystyle{\omega}_{+1}^{-1}:={}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{2dt}{1-t^{2}}={\texttt{x}}(1)-{\texttt{x}}(-1),{}

\displaystyle\quad{\omega}_{-1}^{-1}:={}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{-2dt}{1+t^{2}}=i\big({\texttt{x}}(i)-{\texttt{x}}(-i)\big),

\displaystyle{\omega}_{+1}^{+1}:={}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{2tdt}{1-t^{2}}={\texttt{x}}(1)+{\texttt{x}}(-1),{}

\displaystyle\quad{\omega}_{-1}^{+1}:={}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{-2tdt}{1+t^{2}}={\texttt{x}}(i)+{\texttt{x}}(-i).

Let
\eta_{j}={\varepsilon}_{j}{\varepsilon}_{j+1}
for all
j=1,\dots,r
, where
{\varepsilon}_{r+1}=1
.
We see that for all
j=1,\dots,r-1
we always have

W_{\kappa_{j}}^{{\varepsilon}_{j},{\varepsilon}_{j+1}}=(\mu_{j}^{\prime})^{({\varepsilon}_{j+1}-{\varepsilon}_{j})/2}\big({\texttt{x}}(\mu_{j}^{\prime})a+\eta_{j}{\texttt{x}}(-\mu_{j}^{\prime})\big)

where
\mu_{j}^{\prime}=\prod_{n=1}^{j}\mu_{n}
for all
j=1,\dots,r
.
Further, the last 1-form in (
3.27
) can be written as

{\omega}_{\kappa_{r}}^{{\varepsilon}_{r}}=(\mu^{\prime}_{r})^{(1-{\varepsilon}_{r})/2}\big({\texttt{x}}(\mu_{r}^{\prime})a+\eta_{r}{\texttt{x}}(-\mu_{r}^{\prime})\big).

Hence the shuffle regularized value of
M_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{{\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})
is (setting
\eta_{r+1}=1
)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-\lambda}{\omega}_{0}^{k_{1}-1}W_{\kappa_{1}}^{{\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2}}\cdots{\omega}_{0}^{k_{r-1}-1}W_{\kappa_{r-1}}^{{\varepsilon}_{r-1},{\varepsilon}_{r}}{\omega}_{0}^{k_{r}-1}{\omega}_{\kappa_{r}}^{{\varepsilon}_{r}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{r}(\mu^{\prime}_{j})^{(1-\eta_{j})/2-(1-\eta_{j+1})/2}\bigg)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-\lambda}{\omega}_{0}^{k_{1}-1}\big({\texttt{x}}(\mu_{1}^{\prime})a+\eta_{1}{\texttt{x}}(-\mu_{1}^{\prime})\big)\cdots{\omega}_{0}^{k_{r}-1}\big({\texttt{x}}(\mu_{r}^{\prime})a+\eta_{r}{\texttt{x}}(-\mu_{r}^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{r}(\mu^{\prime}_{j}/\mu^{\prime}_{j-1})^{(1-\eta_{j})/2}\bigg)\displaystyle\sum_{\delta_{1}^{\prime}=\pm 1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum_{\delta_{r}^{\prime}=\pm 1}\Big(\tilde{\eta}_{1}(\delta_{1}^{\prime})\cdots\tilde{\eta}_{r}(\delta_{r}^{\prime})\Big)\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}^{\shuffle}(\delta_{1}^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\delta_{r}^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}_{r};-\log\lambda)+O(\lambda\log^{r}\lambda)

where
\mu^{\prime}_{0}=1
,
\tilde{\eta}_{j}(1)=1
and
\tilde{\eta}_{j}(-1)=\eta_{j}
for all
j=1,\dots,r
.
Moreover, it is easy to check that for all
j=1,\dots,r

\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{j}(\delta_{1}\delta_{2})=\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{j}(\delta_{1})\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{j}(\delta_{2}),\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{{\varepsilon}_{j}{\varepsilon}_{j+1}}(\delta)=\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{j}(\delta)\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{j+1}(\delta)\quad\forall\delta,\delta_{1},\delta_{2}=\pm 1.

(3.28)

Thus by the change of indices
\delta_{j}^{\prime}=\prod_{n=1}^{j}\delta_{n}
for all
j=1,\dots,r
we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{1}(\delta_{1}^{\prime})\cdots\tilde{\eta}_{r}(\delta_{r}^{\prime})={}

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\varepsilon}_{1}{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\delta_{1})\widetilde{{\varepsilon}_{2}{\varepsilon}_{3}}(\delta_{1}\delta_{2})\cdots\widetilde{{\varepsilon}_{r-1}{\varepsilon}_{r}}(\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\cdots\delta_{r-1})\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{r}(\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\cdots\delta_{r})=\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{1}(\delta_{1})\cdots\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{r}(\delta_{r}).

Hence

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-\lambda}{\omega}_{0}^{k_{1}-1}W_{\kappa_{1}}^{{\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2}}\cdots{\omega}_{0}^{k_{r-1}-1}W_{\kappa_{r-1}}^{{\varepsilon}_{r-1},{\varepsilon}_{r}}{\omega}_{0}^{k_{r}-1}{\omega}_{\kappa_{r}}^{{\varepsilon}_{r}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}^{(1-\eta_{j})/2}\bigg)\displaystyle\sum_{\delta_{1}=\pm 1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum_{\delta_{r}=\pm 1}\Big(\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{1}(\delta_{1})\cdots\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{r}(\delta_{r})\Big)\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}^{\shuffle}(\delta_{1}^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\delta_{r}^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}_{r};-\log\lambda)+O(\lambda\log^{r}\lambda)

where we recall that
\delta_{j}^{\prime}=\prod_{n=1}^{j}\delta_{n}
and
\mu^{\prime}_{j}=\prod_{n=1}^{j}\mu_{n}
. This implies that

{\texttt{M}}_{\shuffle}({\bf w};T)=\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}^{(1-\eta_{j})/2}\bigg)\displaystyle\sum_{\delta_{1}=\pm 1}\cdots\displaystyle\sum_{\delta_{r}=\pm 1}\Big(\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{1}(\delta_{1})\cdots\tilde{{\varepsilon}}_{r}(\delta_{r})\Big)\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}^{\shuffle}(\delta_{1}^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\delta_{r}^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}_{r};T).

(3.29)

The theorem now follows from (
3.25
), (
3.26
) and (
3.29
).
As a final note, since all AMMVs are real numbers, we see that the theorem holds over
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We know there should be
\mathbb{Q}
-linear relations among Euler sums or AMZVs that are not consequences
of the regularized DBSF, see
[
37
, Remark 3.5]
. Further, there are some octahedral relations
discovered in
[
36
]
that are not consequences of regularized DBSF. Therefore, the same should hold for AMMVs by Theorem
3.3
.

Next, let’s consider a weight three example using a regularized AMMV as defined by
[
33
, Definition 3.2]
.

Example 3.6
.

As a simple example of the above theorem, we first have

\displaystyle{\texttt{M}}_{\shuffle}(\wc{1},\bar{2})={}

\displaystyle\,{\texttt{M}}_{\shuffle}\big({\bf p}({\omega}_{1}^{-1}{\omega}_{0}{\omega}^{1}_{-1})\big)={\texttt{M}}_{\shuffle}({\omega}_{1}^{-1}{\omega}_{0}{\omega}^{1}_{-1})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\,{\texttt{M}}_{\shuffle}({\omega}_{1}^{-1}\shuffle{\omega}_{0}{\omega}^{1}_{-1}-{\omega}_{0}{\omega}_{1}^{-1}{\omega}^{1}_{-1}-{\omega}_{0}{\omega}^{1}_{-1}{\omega}_{1}^{-1})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\,{\texttt{M}}_{\shuffle}({\omega}_{1}^{-1})M(\wc{2})-M({\bf q}({\omega}_{0}{\omega}_{1}^{-1}{\omega}^{1}_{-1}))-M({\bf q}({\omega}_{0}{\omega}^{1}_{-1}{\omega}_{1}^{-1})))

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\,(T+\log 2)M(\bar{2})-M(\wc{2},\bar{1})-M(\wc{\bar{2}},\wc{\bar{1}}).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle{\texttt{M}}_{\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\shuffle$}}}(\wc{1},\bar{2})={}

\displaystyle\,{\texttt{M}}_{\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\shuffle$}}}\big({\bf p}({\texttt{z}}_{1,1}^{-1}{\texttt{z}}_{2,-1}^{1})\big)={\texttt{M}}_{\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\shuffle$}}}\big({\bf p}({\texttt{z}}_{1,1}^{-1}\ast{\texttt{z}}_{2,-1}^{1}-{\texttt{z}}_{2,-1}^{1}{\texttt{z}}_{1,1}^{-1})\big)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\,{\texttt{M}}_{\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\shuffle$}}}\big({\texttt{z}}_{1,1}^{-1}\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\shuffle$}}{\texttt{z}}_{2,-1}^{1}-{\bf p}({\texttt{z}}_{2,-1}^{1}{\texttt{z}}_{1,1}^{-1})\big)=(T+2\log 2)M(\bar{2})-M(\bar{2},\wc{1}),

since no stuffing can appear. By Theorem
3.4
, we get

\displaystyle(T+\log 2)M(\bar{2})-M(\wc{2},\bar{1})-M(\wc{\bar{2}},\wc{\bar{1}})

\displaystyle\,=\rho\Big((T+2\log 2)M(\bar{2})-M(\bar{2},\wc{1})\Big)

\displaystyle\,=(T+\log 2)M(\bar{2})-M(\bar{2},\wc{1})

\displaystyle\,\Longrightarrow M(\wc{2},\bar{1})+M(\wc{\bar{2}},\wc{\bar{1}})=M(\bar{2},\wc{1}).

We can check this identity numerically and find that both sides are
\approx-0.7739912
.

For more complicated examples, it is in fact easier to use (
3.25
) and (
3.29
)
to express the regularized AMMVs in terms of regularized CMZVs.

Theorem 3.7
.

(Generalized Duality Relation)

Let
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}),{\boldsymbol{\sl{l}}}=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}}}\in\{\pm 1\}^{r}
with
{\varepsilon}_{1}=-1
.
Then

M\big({\omega}_{0}^{k_{r}}({\omega}_{\sigma_{r}}^{{\varepsilon}_{r}})^{l_{r}}\cdots({\omega}_{\sigma_{2}}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}})^{l_{2}}{\omega}_{0}^{k_{1}}({\omega}_{\sigma_{1}}^{-1})^{l_{1}}\big)=M\big((u_{\sigma_{1}}^{-1})^{l_{1}}u_{0}^{k_{1}}(u_{\sigma_{2}}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}})^{l_{2}}\cdots(u_{\sigma_{r}}^{{\varepsilon}_{r}})^{l_{r}}u_{0}^{k_{r}}\big),

(3.30)

where
u_{0}={\omega}_{+1}^{-1}
,
u_{+1}^{-1}={\omega}_{0}
,
u_{-1}^{-1}={\omega}_{-1}^{-1}
,
u_{+1}^{+1}={\omega}_{0}+{\omega}_{+1}^{+1}-{\omega}_{+1}^{-1}

and
u_{-1}^{+1}={\omega}_{+1}^{+1}-{\omega}_{+1}^{-1}-{\omega}_{-1}^{+1}
.

Proof.

Under the substitution
t\to\displaystyle\frac{1-t}{1+t}
, we get

\displaystyle dt\to\displaystyle\frac{-2dt}{(1+t)^{2}},\quad 1-t^{2}\to\displaystyle\frac{4t}{(1+t)^{2}},\quad 1+t^{2}\to\displaystyle\frac{2(1+t^{2})}{(1+t)^{2}}.

Thus

\displaystyle{\omega}_{0}=\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\to-\displaystyle\frac{2dt}{1-t^{2}}=-{\omega}_{+1}^{-1},\quad{\omega}_{+1}^{-1}=\displaystyle\frac{2dt}{1-t^{2}}\to-{\omega}_{0},\quad{\omega}_{-1}^{-1}=\displaystyle\frac{-2dt}{1+t^{2}}\to-{\omega}_{-1}^{-1},

\displaystyle\quad{\omega}_{+1}^{+1}=\displaystyle\frac{2tdt}{1-t^{2}}\to\displaystyle\frac{-(1-t)dt}{t(1+t)}={\omega}_{+1}^{-1}-{\omega}_{0}-{\omega}_{+1}^{+1},

\displaystyle\quad{\omega}_{-1}^{+1}=\displaystyle\frac{-2tdt}{1+t^{2}}\to\displaystyle\frac{2(1-t)dt}{(1+t^{2})(1+t)}={\omega}_{+1}^{-1}-{\omega}_{+1}^{+1}+{\omega}_{-1}^{+1}.

The theorem follows immediately.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

(1). Note that on the left-hand side of the generalized duality relation (
3.30
) the last 1-form
must be
{\omega}_{\pm 1}^{-1}
. It is not difficult to adopt a regularization procedure to derive the generalized
duality relation with ending 1-form equal to
{\omega}_{\pm 1}^{+1}
. We will leave this to the interested reader.

(2). If
{\omega}_{\pm 1}^{+1}
appears on the left-hand side of (
3.30
) then there are always more than
one term on the right since the terms produced on the right-hand side are all distinct and cannot cancel each other.

Example 3.9
.

To illustrate Remark
3.8
concretely, we have found the following
generalized duality relations first of which does not involve

Supported by theoretical and numerical evidence presented in this paper, we now formulate a few conjectures concerning the dimensions of some subspaces of AMMVs. These conjectures hint at a few very rich but previously overlooked algebraic and geometric structures associated with these vector spaces. It is our hope that these unsolved problems will provoke further development and more detailed studies of the motivic theory of the AMMVs.

Conjecture 5.9
.

(cf.
[
30
, Conj. 5.2]
)

We have the following generating function

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathsf{AMTV}_{n})t^{n}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-t-t^{2}-t^{3}}.

Namely, the dimensions form the tribonacci sequence
\{d_{w}\}_{w\geq 1}=\{1,2,4,7,13,24,\dotsc\}
, see A000073 at oeis.org.

Conjecture 5.10
.

We have
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathsf{AMSV}_{1}=1
,
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathsf{AMSV}_{2}=3
, and

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathsf{AMSV}_{n}=2\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathsf{AMSV}_{n-1}-2\Big\lfloor\displaystyle\frac{n+1}{2}\Big\rfloor+4\quad\text{for all $n\geq 3$}.

Conjecture 5.11
.

For
n\geq 2
, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathsf{AMtV}_{n}=3\times 2^{n-2}.

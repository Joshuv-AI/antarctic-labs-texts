Reconstructing isotropic and anisotropic f ⁡ ( 𝒬 ) f(\mathcal{Q}) cosmologies
Fabrizio Esposito, Sante Carloni, Roberto Cianci, Stefano Vignolo

Abstract
We present a reconstruction algorithm for cosmological models based on f ⁡ ( 𝒬 ) f(\mathcal{Q}) gravity. We specifically focus on Bianchi Type-I and Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker spacetimes, obtaining exact solutions that might have application in a variety of scenarios such as spontaneous isotropization of Bianchi Type-I models, dark energy, and inflation as well as pre-Big Bang cosmologies.



I
Introduction

General Relativity (GR) is a geometric theory of gravitation based on the Equivalence Principle
Wald 1984
;
Weinberg and Steven 1972
. In GR, the dynamics of the gravitational field is described by the Einstein equations, which relate the curvature of spacetime to the energy-matter sources.

Despite the great successes of Einstein’s theory, some shortcomings of GR have emerged over the years. For example, in cosmology, no known matter source can generate the accelerated expansion phase that our Universe is experiencing. The currently most widely accepted model for cosmology, the
\Lambda
CDM model, assumes that the accelerated expansion is due to the cosmological constant
\Lambda

Carroll 2001
;
Peebles and Ratra 2003
;
Bamba et al. 2012
. However, the observed value of
\Lambda
disagrees with the theoretical prediction by 120 orders of magnitude
Weinberg 1989
. This result led the community to consider more general fluids, generally known as dark energy, with the same key property as
\Lambda
, i.e. negative pressure. To this day, the nature of dark energy is still a matter of debate. At the quantum level, the main conceptual problem is that in GR the metric is the field describing both the dynamics of gravity and the spacetime background. However, quantum theories are formulated on a fixed background. In order to solve the above problems, an interaction between geometry and quantum fields can be introduced, which is expressed by a modification of the Hilbert-Einstein Lagrangian through geometry-geometry or geometry-matter interaction terms. It is just in this field of research that the Extended Theories of Gravity were introduced
Capozziello and De Laurentis 2011
;
Sotiriou and Faraoni 2010
;
Clifton et al. 2012
.

In GR, gravity is modeled in terms of geometrical properties of spacetime, represented by the Riemann tensor. The Riemann tensor (or more in general the curvature tensor), together with torsion and non-metricity, express the properties of a given affine connection defined on the spacetime. The set made up of curvature, torsion and non-metricity tensors is usually referred to as the “Trinity of Gravity”
Jiménez et al. 2019
. It has been known for some time that different representations of gravity can be obtained depending on the geometrical quantity considered, indeed, torsion and non-metricity contain enough degrees of freedom to describe the geometry of spacetime entirely. For instance, the Teleparallel Gravity (TG)
Krššák et al. 2019
is a gravitational gauge theory where spacetime is assumed flat and torsion describes the gravitation through the Weitzenböck connection. Instead, the Symmetric Teleparallel Gravity (STG)
Nester and Yo 1999
;
Adak et al. 2013
;
Adak et al. 2006
;
Bombacigno et al. 2021
;
Conroy and Koivisto 2018
describes gravity through a torsion-free and curvature-free connection but with non-metricity different from zero. As well as for GR, extensions of these theories have been developed, like the
f(T)
(where
T
is the torsion scalar)
Cai et al. 2016
and
f(\mathcal{Q})
(where
\mathcal{Q}
is the non-metricity scalar) theories
Jiménez et al. 2018
.

A significant advantage of both
f(T)
and
f(\mathcal{Q})
theories, with respect to other “geometric” extensions of GR (like
f(R)
gravity), is that the background field equations are always of second order, so there are no instability problems related to the Ostrogradsky’s theorem
Motohashi and Suyama 2015
;
Woodard 2015
.

In this paper, we focus on one of these recent modifications of GR:
f(\mathcal{Q})
theory. Several studies have been published on this kind of theory, mainly in connection with cosmological applications
Harko et al. 2018
;
Beltrán Jiménez et al. 2020
;
Mandal et al. 2020
;
Barros et al. 2020
;
Lazkoz et al. 2019
;
Soudi et al. 2019
;
Frusciante 2021
;
Hassan et al. 2021
;
Bajardi et al. 2020
. In particular, we aim to explore exact isotropic and anisotropic cosmological solutions using the so-called reconstruction methods.

Reconstruction methods were used for the first time by G. F. R. Ellis and M. S. Madsen
Ellis and Madsen 1991
to find the potential functions needed in models of inflationary universes.
The general idea consists in reversing the usual procedure of resolution: a given form for the spatial scale factor is assumed and, once substituted into the cosmological equations, further information is derived on the remaining unknown functions of the theory, for instance, the inflaton potential function in
Ellis and Madsen 1991
.
Subsequently, reconstruction was used in various frameworks, like in the cosmology of
f(R)
gravity
Carloni et al. 2012
, where the generic function of the Ricci scalar is reconstructed starting from given scale factors, in scalar-tensor cosmologies
Vignolo et al. 2013
and in the study of static and spherically symmetric spacetimes
Carloni and Vernieri 2018a
;
Carloni and Vernieri 2018b
;
Carloni 2014
;
Naidu et al. 2021
.

In the following, we will consider Bianchi Type-I (BI)
Ellis and MacCallum 1969
;
Ellis 2006
and spatially flat Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) cosmologies with a focus on the role that non-metricity plays in cosmic history. The interest in the FLRW universe is evident due to its role in modern cosmology. On the other hand, the BI metric, which corresponds to the simplest anisotropic generalization of the spatially flat FLRW spacetime, finds its interest in the analysis of the behavior of anisotropies in cosmology. In fact, even though the current Universe is believed to be essentially isotropic, it may not have been so at its beginning, nor does it necessarily have to be in the future. Furthermore, by studying less symmetrical metrics, we can better understand the isotropic ones, which can be considered their special subcase.

The layout of the paper is the following. In Sec.
II
we review some generalities on
f(\mathcal{Q})
theory. In Sec.
III
we derive the cosmological equations for the BI metric, and then we restrict to the spatially flat FLRW one. In Sec.
IV
and Sec.
V
we apply the reconstruction method to
f(\mathcal{Q})
cosmology. We devote Sec.
VI
to a final discussion of the obtained results. Throughout the paper we use natural units (
c=8\pi G=1
) and the metric signature (
-,+,+,+
).

II
f(\mathcal{Q})
theory

We assume a spacetime endowed with a metric tensor
g_{ij}
and an affine connection
\Gamma_{ij}{}^{k}
that introduces a corresponding covariant derivative
\nabla
. Given a metric
g_{ij}
, any affine connection can be decomposed as follows:

\Gamma_{ij}{}^{k}=\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}{}^{k}+K_{ij}{}^{k}+N_{ij}{}^{k},

(1)

where
\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}{}^{k}
is the Levi-Civita connection,

\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}{}^{k}=\frac{1}{2}g^{kh}\left(\partial_{i}g_{jh}+\partial_{j}g_{ih}-\partial_{k}g_{ij}\right),

(2)

K_{ij}{}^{k}
is the contorsion tensor,

K_{ij}{}^{k}=\frac{1}{2}\left(T_{ij}{}^{k}-T_{i}{}^{k}{}_{j}-T_{j}{}^{k}{}_{i}\right),

(3)

with the torsion tensor defined as

{T_{ij}}^{k}={\Gamma_{ij}}^{k}-{\Gamma_{ji}}^{k},

(4)

and
N_{ij}{}^{k}
is the disformation tensor,

N_{ij}{}^{k}=\frac{1}{2}\left(Q^{k}{}_{ij}-Q_{i}{}^{k}{}_{j}-Q_{j}{}^{k}{}_{i}\right),

(5)

defined in terms of the non-metricity tensor

Q_{kij}=\nabla_{k}g_{ij}.

(6)

The curvature tensor associated with the connection
\Gamma_{ij}{}^{k}
is expressed as

R^{h}{}_{kij}=\partial_{i}\Gamma_{jk}{}^{h}-\partial_{j}\Gamma_{ik}{}^{h}+\Gamma_{ip}{}^{h}\Gamma_{jk}{}^{p}-\Gamma_{jp}{}^{h}\Gamma_{ik}{}^{p}.

(7)

We will focus on
f(\mathcal{Q})
gravity, namely a generalization of STG based on an action of the form

A=\int d^{4}x\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}f(\mathcal{Q})+\lambda_{a}{}^{bij}R^{a}{}_{bij}+\lambda_{a}{}^{ij}T_{ij}{}^{a}\right]+A_{m}

(8)

where
A_{m}
indicates a generic matter action,
\lambda_{a}{}^{bij}
and
\lambda_{a}{}^{ij}
are Lagrange multipliers introduced to impose the vanishing of curvature and torsion, and
f(\mathcal{Q})
is a generic function of the non-metricity scalar,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=

\displaystyle-Q_{hij}P^{hij}=

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}Q_{hij}Q^{hij}-\frac{1}{2}Q_{hij}Q^{ijh}-\frac{1}{4}q_{h}q^{h}+\frac{1}{2}q_{h}Q^{h},

In Eq. (
9
), the tensor

\displaystyle P^{h}{}_{ij}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}Q^{h}{}_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{(ij)}{}^{h}+\frac{1}{4}q^{h}g_{ij}-\frac{1}{4}Q^{h}g_{ij}-\frac{1}{4}\delta^{h}_{(i}q_{j)}

(10)

is defined as the conjugate of the non-metricity tensor, and

q_{h}=Q_{hi}{}^{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad Q_{h}=Q_{ih}{}^{i}.

(11)

are the two independent traces of
Q_{hij}
.
The expression for the non-metricity scalar (
9
) has been chosen in such a way that when setting
f(\mathcal{Q})=\mathcal{Q}
we obtain a theory that is equal to GR modulo a boundary term.

We develop the theory in the metric-affine framework where metric and connection are independent variables. By varying with respect to the Lagrange multipliers, we obtain the constraints

R^{a}{}_{bij}=0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad T_{ij}{}^{a}=0,

(12)

whereas variations with respect to the metric and the connection yield field equations of the form:

\begin{split}\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\nabla_{h}\left(\sqrt{-g}f^{\prime}P^{h}{}_{ij}\right)+\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}f(\mathcal{Q})+\quad\\
+f^{\prime}\left(P_{iab}Q_{j}{}^{ab}-2Q^{ab}{}_{i}P_{abj}\right)=\Sigma_{ij}\end{split}

(13)

and

\nabla_{p}\lambda_{h}{}^{jip}+\lambda_{h}{}^{ij}-\sqrt{-g}f^{\prime}P^{ij}{}_{h}=\Phi^{ij}{}_{h},

(14)

with

\Sigma_{ij}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{m}}{\delta g^{ij}}\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi^{ij}{}_{h}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{m}}{\delta{\Gamma_{ij}{}^{h}}}.

(15)

\mathcal{L}_{m}
is a generic matter Lagrangian density that includes
\sqrt{-g}
in its definition.

The flatness and torsionless conditions (
12
) ensure the existence of local coordinates in which
\Gamma_{ij}{}^{h}=0
. In the following, we will systematically adopt this choice of coordinates, usually referred to as the “coincident gauge.” We also note that, due to the flatness and torsionless conditions, Eq. (
14
) can be written as follows:

\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\left(\sqrt{-g}f^{\prime}P^{ij}{}_{h}\right)+\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\Phi^{ij}{}_{h}=0.

(16)

Using Eq. (
16
) into the Levi-Civita divergence of Eq. (
13
), we derive the energy-momentum conservation:

\tilde{\nabla}_{i}\Sigma^{i}{}_{h}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\Phi^{ij}{}_{h}=0,

(17)

where
\tilde{\nabla}
denotes the Levi-Civita covariant derivative. Since we will consider only perfect fluids as source of the gravitational field equations, we will assume that the matter action does not depend on the connection. Therefore, from now on we will consider
\Phi^{ij}{}_{h}=0
.

III
f(\mathcal{Q})
cosmology

Let us now specialize the field equations of (
13
) to the study of cosmological models. In particular, we focus on BI and FLRW universes. These equations will constitute the base of the reconstruction method we intend to apply.

III.1
Bianchi Type-I metric

The BI metric represents spatially flat, homogeneous but not isotropic spacetimes. The most common realization of this metric is

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)dx^{2}+b^{2}(t)dy^{2}+c^{2}(t)dz^{2},

(18)

where
a(t)
,
b(t)
and
c(t)
are the scale factors associated to each space direction.
Remembering we are using the coincident gauge, in this metric the non-metricity scalar is

\mathcal{Q}=2\left(\frac{\dot{a}\dot{b}}{ab}+\frac{\dot{a}\dot{c}}{ac}+\frac{\dot{b}\dot{c}}{bc}\right).

(19)

III.1.1
Cosmological equations

To derive the cosmological equations, we assume that, at the cosmological level, matter is described by the energy-momentum tensor of a perfect fluid,

\Sigma_{ij}=\left(\rho+p\right)u_{i}u_{j}+pg_{ij},

(20)

where pressure
p
and the energy density
\rho
are related by the equation of state
p=w\rho
, with
w
the barotropic factor, and
\rho
satisfies the continuity equation derived from Eq. (
17
),

\dot{\rho}+\frac{\dot{\tau}}{\tau}\left(1+w\right)\rho=0,

(21)

whose solution in terms of the volume of the Universe
\tau(t)=a(t)b(t)c(t)
is

\rho=\rho_{0}\tau^{-\left(1+w\right)},

(22)

where
\rho_{0}
is the density at a given initial time. We derive the cosmological equations from the temporal and spatial part of Eq. (
13
), whereas Eq. (
16
) is identically satisfied,

\frac{1}{2}f-2f^{\prime}\left(\frac{\dot{a}\dot{b}}{ab}+\frac{\dot{b}\dot{c}}{bc}+\frac{\dot{a}\dot{c}}{ac}\right)=-\rho,

(23)

\dot{f}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}-\frac{\dot{\tau}}{\tau}\right)-f^{\prime}\left(\frac{\ddot{b}}{b}+\frac{\ddot{c}}{c}+\frac{\dot{a}\dot{b}}{ab}+\frac{\dot{a}\dot{c}}{ac}+2\frac{\dot{b}\dot{c}}{bc}\right)+\frac{1}{2}f=p,

(24)

\dot{f}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\dot{b}}{b}-\frac{\dot{\tau}}{\tau}\right)-f^{\prime}\left(\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}+\frac{\ddot{c}}{c}+\frac{\dot{a}\dot{b}}{ab}+\frac{\dot{b}\dot{c}}{bc}+2\frac{\dot{a}\dot{c}}{ac}\right)+\frac{1}{2}f=p,

(25)

\dot{f}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\dot{c}}{c}-\frac{\dot{\tau}}{\tau}\right)-f^{\prime}\left(\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}+\frac{\ddot{b}}{b}+\frac{\dot{a}\dot{c}}{ac}+\frac{\dot{b}\dot{c}}{bc}+2\frac{\dot{a}\dot{b}}{ab}\right)+\frac{1}{2}f=p.

(26)

We can recast the above equations in a more useful form by performing some simple operations.

Let us consider the combination of the Eqs. (
23
)-(
26
) given by
Eq. (
23
) multiplied by
-\dot{\tau}/\tau
and added to Eqs. (
24
), (
25
), and (
26
) multiplied by
3\dot{a}/a
,
3\dot{b}/b
, and
3\dot{c}/c
, respectively.
The resulting expression is an equivalent of the Raychaudhuri equation,

f\frac{\dot{\tau}}{\tau}-f^{\prime}\left(\frac{3}{2}\dot{\mathcal{Q}}+2\mathcal{Q}\frac{\dot{\tau}}{\tau}\right)-3f^{\prime\prime}\mathcal{Q}\dot{\mathcal{Q}}=\frac{\dot{\tau}}{\tau}\left(\rho+3p\right).

(27)

However, we can also obtain Eq. (
27
) using only Eqs. (
21
) and (
23
) taking the time derivative of Eq. (
23
) multiplied by
3
and adding Eq. (
23
) multiplied by
2\dot{\tau}/\tau
.
Thus, if the Eqs. (
21
) and (
23
) are satisfied, so is Eq. (
27
). This step will be crucial for developing the reconstruction algorithm as it allows us to remove one equation.

Instead, subtracting Eq. (
25
) and Eq. (
26
) from Eq. (
24
), we get, respectively:

\frac{\dot{a}}{a}-\frac{\dot{b}}{b}=\frac{k_{ab}}{f^{\prime}\tau}\quad\to\quad\frac{a}{b}=e^{d_{1}}\exp\int\frac{k_{ab}}{f^{\prime}\tau}dt

(28)

and

\frac{\dot{a}}{a}-\frac{\dot{c}}{c}=\frac{k_{ac}}{f^{\prime}\tau}\quad\to\quad\frac{a}{c}=e^{d_{2}}\exp\int\frac{k_{ac}}{f^{\prime}\tau}dt,

(29)

where
k_{ab},k_{ac},d_{1}
, and
d_{2}
are constants of integration.

We can, therefore, consider the equivalent set of independent cosmological equations given by:

\dot{\rho}+\frac{\dot{\tau}}{\tau}\left(1+w\right)\rho=0,

(30)

\frac{1}{2}f-2f^{\prime}\left(\frac{\dot{a}\dot{b}}{ab}+\frac{\dot{b}\dot{c}}{bc}+\frac{\dot{a}\dot{c}}{ac}\right)=-\rho,

(31)

\frac{\dot{a}}{a}-\frac{\dot{b}}{b}=\frac{k_{ab}}{f^{\prime}\tau},

(32)

\frac{\dot{a}}{a}-\frac{\dot{c}}{c}=\frac{k_{ac}}{f^{\prime}\tau}.

(33)

One of the last two equations can be replaced, depending on the situation we are analyzing, by the relation that we derive with simple algebraic steps from Eqs. (
32
) and (
33
),

(k_{ab}-k_{ac})\frac{\dot{a}}{a}+k_{ac}\frac{\dot{b}}{b}-k_{ab}\frac{\dot{c}}{c}=0.

(34)

Notice that the number of cosmological equations has decreased. This is possible because one of the Eqs. (
24
)-(
26
) can be replaced by Eq. (
27
), which is always satisfied given the solutions of the Eqs. (
21
) and (
23
).

III.2
FLRW metric

If we require isotropy in the BI metric, that is
a(t)=b(t)=c(t)
in (
18
), we obtain the spatially flat FLRW metric. The non-metricity scalar is related to the Hubble parameter
H=\dot{a}/{a}
by

\mathcal{Q}=6H^{2}

(35)

and the Raychaudhuri equation (
27
) is equal to

\frac{1}{6}f-f^{\prime}\left(\dot{H}+2H^{2}\right)-12f^{\prime\prime}H^{2}\dot{H}=\frac{1}{6}\left(\rho+3p\right).

(36)

The cosmological equations (
30
)-(
33
) reduce to the set

\frac{1}{2}f-6H^{2}f^{\prime}=-\rho,

(37)

\dot{\rho}+3H(1+w)\rho=0.

(38)

In order to facilitate the study of the examples that will be considered, it is useful to define the deceleration parameter,

q=-\frac{\ddot{a}a}{\dot{a}^{2}},

(39)

and rewrite Eqs. (
36
) and (
37
) in a more expressive form,

\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}=-\frac{1}{6}\left(\hat{\rho}_{M}+3\hat{p}_{M}\right)-\frac{1}{6}\left(\hat{\rho}_{f}+3\hat{p}_{f}\right),

(40)

\displaystyle H^{2}=\frac{1}{3}\left(\hat{\rho}_{M}+\hat{\rho}_{f}\right),

(41)

where

\hat{\rho}_{M}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rho}{f^{\prime}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\hat{p}_{M}=\frac{p}{f^{\prime}}

(42)

represent the standard energy density and pressure, with an effective gravitational constant regulated by
f^{\prime}(\mathcal{Q})
, while

\hat{\rho}_{f}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{f}{f^{\prime}},\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{p}_{f}=2\left[\frac{\mathcal{Q}}{4}-\frac{1}{4}\frac{f}{f^{\prime}}+\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}H\dot{\mathcal{Q}}\right]

(43)

represent the energy density and pressure of an effective fluid associated with the presence of non-metricity.

IV
Reconstruction method: Bianchi Type-I

In this section, we will apply the reconstruction algorithm to investigate some exact cosmological models of the type BI. Given suitable scale factors, we will find the function
f(\mathcal{Q})
, which admits such scale factors as solutions of the corresponding cosmological equations.

IV.1
Example 1: Power law scale factors

Let us start by assuming each scale factor as a power law as in the classical Kasner solution
Stephani 2004
(but without restrictions on the exponents),

\displaystyle a(t)=a_{0}t^{n}\qquad b(t)=b_{0}t^{m},\qquad c(t)=c_{0}t^{l},

(44)

\displaystyle\tau=abc=a_{0}b_{0}c_{0}t^{N}

(45)

where
N=n+m+l
and
a_{0}
,
b_{0}
and
c_{0}
are dimensional constants. In such a circumstance, the non-metricity scalar takes the form

\mathcal{Q}=2\left(nm+nl+ml\right)t^{-2}=\xi t^{-2},

(46)

with
\xi=2(nm+nl+ml)
. Making use of Eq. (
46
), from the definition of the spatial volume and the continuity equation we obtain the expressions of
\tau
and
\rho
as functions of
\mathcal{Q}
,

\tau(\mathcal{Q})=\tau_{0}\left(\frac{\xi}{\mathcal{Q}}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}},

(47)

\rho(\mathcal{Q})=\rho_{0}\tau^{-(1+\bar{w})}=\rho_{0}\tau_{0}^{-(1+\bar{w})}\left(\frac{\mathcal{Q}}{\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\bar{w})N},

(48)

where
\tau_{0}=a_{0}b_{0}c_{0}
.
In the above expression, and in the following, we will use
\bar{w}
instead of
w
to emphasize the fact that
\bar{w}
is just a parameter for the theory we will reconstruct, and it is not related to any matter source the final reconstructed theory might be coupled with.
Inserting Eq. (
48
) into Eq. (
31
), we get the differential equation

\frac{f(\mathcal{Q})}{2}-\mathcal{Q}f^{\prime}(\mathcal{Q})=-\epsilon\mathcal{Q}^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\bar{w})N}

(49)

with

\epsilon=\rho_{0}\tau_{0}^{-(1+\bar{w})}\xi^{-\frac{1}{2}(1+\bar{w})N}.

(50)

Equation (
49
) admits the solution,

f(\mathcal{Q})=f_{0}\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}+2\epsilon\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\bar{w})N}}{N(1+\bar{w})-1},

(51)

where
f_{0}
is a constant of integration and will be so throughout the paper

51
) is only valid for the fluid chosen in the reconstruction process, however, such a conclusion would be incorrect. In fact, if we use Eq. (

Using Eqs. (

) and (

), Eqs. (

f_{0}=0,\qquad n+m+l=\frac{1}{\bar{w}},

k_{ab}=\frac{\bar{w}(1+\bar{w})\rho_{0}\tau_{0}^{-\bar{w}}(n-m)}{2\left[m+n-\bar{w}\left(m^{2}+mn+n^{2}\right)\right]},

(53)

Notice that relation (

IV.2
Example 2: More complex scale factors.

In this second example, we choose the scale factors as follows:

\begin{split}a(t)=\alpha\texttt{a}(t)\sqrt[3]{\tau(t)},\qquad b(t)=\beta\texttt{b}(t)\sqrt[3]{\tau(t)},\\
c(t)=\gamma\texttt{c}(t)\sqrt[3]{\tau(t)},\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \end{split}

(56)

which is an interesting template for BI solutions (see, e.g.
Stephani 2004
). The quantities
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\gamma
are generic constants.

The first step is to derive
\texttt{b}(t)
from Eq. (
34
),

\texttt{b}=b_{0}\texttt{a}^{\frac{k_{ac}-k_{ab}}{k_{ac}}}\texttt{c}^{\frac{k_{ab}}{k_{ac}}},

(57)

where
b_{0}
is a constant of integration. Then, using the definition of
\tau
and
\mathcal{Q}
, we obtain the scale factor

\texttt{a}=a_{0}\texttt{c}^{\frac{k_{ab}+k_{ac}}{k_{ab}-2k_{ac}}}

(58)

and the relation

\frac{\dot{\texttt{c}}^{2}}{\texttt{c}^{2}}=\frac{(k_{ab}-2k_{ac})^{2}}{18\Omega^{2}}\left(2\frac{\dot{\tau}^{2}}{\tau^{2}}-3\mathcal{Q}\right)

(59)

a_{0}=\left(b_{0}\alpha\beta\gamma\right)^{\frac{k_{ac}}{k_{ab}-2k_{ac}}}

(60)

and

\Omega=\sqrt{k_{ab}^{2}-k_{ab}k_{ac}+k_{ac}^{2}}.

(61)

from Eq. (
31

(62)

and replace all in Eq. (
32
), then we find that particular cosmological solutions

f=K_{f}\tau^{-1-\bar{w}},

(63)

\frac{\dot{\tau}^{2}}{\tau^{2}}=\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{Q}\left(K_{\tau}^{2}\ \mathcal{Q}\ \tau^{2\bar{w}}+6\right),

(64)

K_{\tau}(K_{f}+2\rho_{0})-4\Omega=0.

(65)

63

\tau=\pm\left(\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{0}}{\mathcal{Q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}},

(66)

f(\mathcal{Q})=K_{f}\left[\pm\left(\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{0}}{\mathcal{Q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\right]^{-1-\bar{w}},

(67)

which, when substituted into Eq. (

K_{f}=\frac{2n\rho_{0}}{2\bar{w}+2-n}

V
Reconstruction method: FLRW

In this section, we will consider spatially flat FLRW cosmologies. As it will be evident, the higher symmetry of the FLRW spacetime, compared to BI, will make it easier to apply the reconstruction method.

V.1
Example 1: reconstruction from a time dependent scale factor

To start with, we take two scale factors
a=a(t)
into account: a power law and an exponential function of time.

V.1.1
Scale factor as a power law

Setting

a(t)=a_{0}t^{n},

(102)

with
a_{0}

\mathcal{Q}=6H^{2}=6n^{2}t^{-2}.

(103)

Inverting relation (
103

\rho

\mathcal{Q}

a(\mathcal{Q})=a_{0}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathcal{Q}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}

(104)

\rho=\rho_{0}a^{-3(1+\bar{w})}=\rho_{0}a_{0}^{-3(1+\bar{w})}\left(\frac{\mathcal{Q}}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}n(1+\bar{w})},

(105)

\alpha=6n^{2}

105

37

f_{1}=\frac{2\rho_{0}}{3n(1+\bar{w})-1}a_{0}^{-3(1+\bar{w})}\alpha^{-\frac{3}{2}(1+\bar{w})n}.

(107)

The function (
106

Jiménez et al. 2018
;
Beltrán Jiménez et al. 2020
2
2

2

106

. This result implies that
f(\mathcal{Q})
gravity can have, at most, one cosmological solution in common with GR.
.

Now we will show that, as anticipated in Sec.

\frac{1}{a}=\Lambda+\frac{\Lambda\mathcal{Q}}{24\Omega^{2}}

(121)

(122)

Replacing Eq. (
122

37

\displaystyle f(\mathcal{Q})=

\displaystyle f_{0}\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}+\,_{2}F_{1}\left[\frac{5}{2},-3\bar{w};\frac{7}{2};-\frac{\mathcal{Q}}{24\Omega^{2}}\right]f_{1}\mathcal{Q}^{3}+

(123)

\displaystyle+\,_{2}F_{1}\left[\frac{3}{2},-3\bar{w};\frac{5}{2};-\frac{\mathcal{Q}}{24\Omega^{2}}\right]f_{2}\mathcal{Q}^{2}+

\displaystyle+\,_{2}F_{1}\left[\frac{1}{2},-3\bar{w};\frac{3}{2};-\frac{\mathcal{Q}}{24\Omega^{2}}\right]f_{3}\mathcal{Q}+

\displaystyle-\,_{2}F_{1}\left[-\frac{1}{2},-3\bar{w};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{\mathcal{Q}}{24\Omega^{2}}\right]f_{4},

where

(

\Lambda

VI
Discussion and conclusions

In this paper, we derived and analyzed some exact cosmological solutions in the context of
f(\mathcal{Q})
theory, with the aim to understand the role the non-metricity might play in the evolution of the Universe.

The primary tools to perform this investigation were reconstruction techniques, in which the form of the scale factor(s) is assumed and the form of the function
f
is recovered
a posteriori
. An essential step for developing the reconstruction algorithm is to reduce the cosmological equation to the simplest set of independent equations. In this respect, the relation between the different components of the Einstein equations is pivotal. We were able to show that, as it is well known in the case of FLRW metrics, also in the case of BI metrics one of the Einstein equations is dependent on the other when the conservation laws are taken into account. This feature has allowed us to reduce the number of equations to be solved in this case. In their original form, reconstruction techniques require some inversion of the scale factor or other related quantities. We have been able to go around this difficulty assigning a differential relation rather than an exact expression for the scale factors. This approach led to the derivation of several nontrivial solutions.

We started by studying the case of an anisotropic universe endowed with a BI metric. In this context, we found several solutions, such as universes where initial and final states are singular configurations with only one or two spatial dimensions (Secs.

In almost all of these solutions, we found that the non-metricity scalar presents some special features when the difference between the behavior of the scale factors (and hence anisotropy) has maximum or minimum. For example, in Fig.

Unfortunately, within the framework of this work, it was not possible to derive a formal relationship between these two quantities. Future works will aim at the clarification of such relation with more sophisticated tools.

We conclude remarking that the relation between non-metricity, anisotropy, and expansion of the Universe that we have seen above resembles the one that ties the Ricci scalar to those quantities in metric
f(R)
gravity. In this respect, therefore, in spite of the reduced degrees of freedom,

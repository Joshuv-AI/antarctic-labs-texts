Abstract
. We establish new mean curvature rigidity theorems of spin fill-ins with non-negative scalar curvature using two different spinorial techniques. Our results address two questions by Miao and Gromov, respectively. The first technique is based on extending boundary spinors satisfying a generalized eigenvalue equation via the Fredholm alternative for an APS boundary value problem, while the second is a comparison result in the spirit of Llarull and Lott using index theory. We also show that the latter implies a new Witten-type integral inequality for the mass of an asymptotically Schwarzschild manifold which holds even when the scalar curvature is not assumed to be non-negative.

1.
Introduction

Fill-ins and extensions with non-negative scalar curvature play a major role in mathematical relativity and geometry.

On the one hand, they provide an important tool in many proofs.
In
[
9
,
13
,
16
,
23
]
manifolds with boundary are filled in by disks, in
[
33
]
a collar fill-in is used to connect the boundary to a minimal surface, and in
[
37
,
42
]
asymptotically flat extensions of compact manifolds with boundary are constructed.
The common thread in all of these constructions is that the fill-ins and extensions allow the applications of theorems which hold for manifolds without boundary or with minimal boundary such as the positive mass theorem or the Riemannian Penrose inequality.
The above methods can even be combined as in
[
16
,
32
]
.
Moreover, fill-ins can be used to simplify the topology of the underlying manifold, see for instance
[
1
,
10
]
.

On the other hand, fill-ins and extensions are used directly in several important definitions such as Bray’s inner mass
[
9
]
and the Bartnik mass
[
8
]
which is in the subject of much current research.

In view of this plethora of applications, it is of great significance to better understand when fill-ins with non-negative scalar curvature exist and what their properties are.
In this article, we address two questions asked by Miao
[
34
, Question 2]
and Gromov
[
19
, page 3]
concerning such fill-ins.

Let us start by recalling the notion of a fill-in with non-negative scalar curvature.
For a Riemannian manifold
M
with boundary
\partial M
, we denote by
\mean_{\partial M}
the mean curvature of
\partial M
.
We adopt the convention that the boundary of the unit disk
\mathrm{D}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
has mean curvature
n-1
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be an
(n-1)
-dimensional connected closed manifold.
Given a metric
g_{\Sigma}
on
\Sigma
and a smooth function
h\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
, we say that an
n
-dimensional connected compact Riemannian manifold with boundary
(M,g)
is a non-negative scalar curvature (NNSC) fill-in for
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},h)
if
(\partial M,g|_{\partial M})=(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
,
\scal_{M}\geq 0
and
\mean_{\partial M}=h
.
We say that
h
is the mean curvature of the fill-in
(M,g,h)
.
When
\Sigma
is spin, we say that
(M,g)
is a spin NNSC fill-in if
M
is spin and induces the given spin structure on
\Sigma=\partial M
.

Shi–Wang–Wei’s extension theorem
[
38
, Theorem 1]
establishes that, if
M
is a compact manifold with boundary
\Sigma
and
g_{\Sigma}
is a Riemannian metric on
\Sigma
, then
g_{\Sigma}
extends to a metric of positive scalar curvature on
M
.
This shows that every closed null-bordant Riemannian manifold admits an NNSC fill-in.
However, their construction produces a fill-in with negative mean curvature.

[
34
, Question 2]
asked whether every closed null-bordant Riemannian manifold
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
admits a NNSC fill-in with positive mean curvature.
Our first result answers Miao’s question in the negative in the spin setting.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a closed spin manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with
n\equiv 0,1,3
or
7\mod 8
.
Then there exists a metric
g_{\Sigma}
on
\Sigma
such that every spin NNSC fill-in of
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
with non-negative mean curvature is Ricci-flat with minimal boundary.
Every Berger sphere
(\mathrm{S}^{3},g)
with scalar curvature
\scal\leq-24
has this property.

Theorem

1.2
relies on a general extension principle for spinors exhibited in
Lemma

3.1
(similar to earlier ideas of
[
35
,
21
]
, see
Remark

3.4
), combined with classical existence results of harmonic spinors on these Berger spheres due to Hitchin
[
25
]
and, in the more general cases due to further results of both
[
25
]
as well as
[
3
]
.
We remark that harmonic spinors can often be produced independently of the metric using the Atiyah–Singer index theorem, e.g. if
\widehat{\mathrm{A}}({\Sigma})\neq 0
, but then
\Sigma
would not be null-bordant (see
[
27
, Chapter III, §11, (11.21)]
), so it would not make sense to talk about fill-ins in these situations.
The significance of
Lemma

3.1
is that it does not rely on the index theorem and in
Theorem

1.2
we can thus treat special metrics on null-bordant manifolds such as the spheres
\mathrm{S}^{4k+3}
.

This method also shows that mean-convex spin domains admitting a parallel spinor are extremal with respect to spin fill-ins as in the next theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(N,g_{N})
be a spin manifold which admits a parallel spinor and has a compact mean-convex boundary
\Sigma=\partial N
, that is,
\mean_{\partial N}\geq 0
.
Let
g_{\Sigma}
be the induced metric on
\Sigma
and let
h\in\Ct^{\infty}(\Sigma,\mathbb{R})
with
h\geq\mean_{\partial N}
.
Then every spin NNSC fill-in of
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},h)
admits a parallel spinor and in this case
h=\mean_{\partial N}
.

Note that, in general, it is important that a spin NNSC fill-in requires an extension of the given spin structure on
\Sigma
to the filled-in manifold
M
, see also the discussion in
Remark

3.7
below.
However, in those cases where
\Sigma
only admits one spin structure—such as the main example
\mathrm{S}^{3}
of
Theorem

1.2
—the theorem thus already precludes the existence of a fill-in with positive mean curvature admitting any spin structure.

Furthermore, we establish that the extension principle of
Lemma

3.1
implies a certain comparison result in the spirit of
[
17
,
31
]
without using index theory, see
Theorem

3.5
.

Next, we investigate a positive upper bound for a spin NNSC fill-in of a closed Riemannian spin manifold
\Sigma
in terms of metric properties of
\Sigma
which—in contrast to the previous results—is based on index theory.
To this end, let us start with recalling the following notion due to Gromov
[
19
, Section 1]
,
[
20
, Section 4.3]
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
(Y,g_{Y})
be a
k
-dimensional closed orientable Riemannian manifold. The
hyperspherical radius
of
(Y,g_{Y})
, denoted by
\rad_{\mathrm{S}^{k}}(Y,g_{Y})
, is the supremum of the numbers
R>0
such that there exists a smooth
\tfrac{1}{R}
-Lipschitz map
f\colon(Y,g_{Y})\to(\mathrm{S}^{k},g_{\mathrm{S}^{k}})
of non-zero degree.

Gromov
[
19
, Section 2]
proved that if
(\Sigma^{n-1},g_{\Sigma})
is a closed Riemannian spin manifold,
h
is a smooth function on
\Sigma
, and
M
is a spin fill-in for
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},h)
, then
\min_{\Sigma}h\leq(n-1)/R
, where
R
is the hyperspherical radius of
\Sigma
.
Moreover, he conjectured
[
19
, Page 3]
that disks are rigid, that is, equality is achieved if and only if
M
is a disk in Euclidean space.
Our second main result establishes this conjecture.

Theorem 1.5
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
be an
(n-1)
-dimensional closed connected Riemannian spin manifold.
Let
h\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function, and let
(M,g)
be a spin NNSC fill-in of
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},h)
.
Then

(1.1)

\min_{p\in\Sigma}h(p)\leq\frac{n-1}{\rad_{\mathrm{S}^{n-1}}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})}.

Furthermore, equality in
1.1
is achieved if and only if
(M,g)
is the round disk in Euclidean space of radius
R=\rad_{\mathrm{S}^{n-1}}(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
and
h=\frac{n-1}{R}
.

One wishes to apply a scalar- and mean curvature rigidity result in the spirit of
[
17
,
31
]
, for flatness see specifically
[
41
, Theorem 3.2]
, to a map
\Sigma\to\mathrm{S}^{n-1}
realizing the hyperspherical radius.
However, due to its definition in terms of a supremum,
a priori
we do not have a
smooth
map precisely realizing the hyperspherical radius.
We solve this issue by establishing the following almost rigidity statement for maps to a fixed bounded strictly convex smooth domain in Euclidean space from a fixed spin NNSC manifold with boundary, which is also of independent interest.

Theorem 1.6
.

For
n\geq 3
, let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with smooth strictly convex boundary.
Let
M
be a connected compact Riemannian spin manifold with connected boundary such that
\scal_{M}\geq 0
.
Fix
p\in[1,\infty)
.
Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
\delta=\delta(M,\Omega,p,\varepsilon)>0
such that the following holds:

For every smooth map
f\colon\partial M\to\partial\Omega
satisfying

•

\mathrm{Lip}(f)\leq 1+\delta
,

•

\mean_{\partial M}\geq\mean_{\partial\Omega}\circ f-\delta
,

•

\deg(f)\neq 0
,

there exists an isometry
\phi\colon\partial M\to\partial\Omega
with
\II_{\partial M}=\phi^{\ast}\II_{\partial\Omega}
such that
f
is
\varepsilon
-close to
\phi
in
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(\partial M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Moreover, in this case
M
is flat and isometric to
\Omega
.

The proof of this theorem is based on carefully analyzing the fundamental spinorial integral inequality established in
Proposition

4.4
, which can be interpreted as a version of a result of Lott
[
31
, Theorem 1.1]

“with coefficients”
in the spirit of Listing
[
29
]
, a special case of which is also used by Brendle in
[
11
]
.
The existence of spinors used in this theorem is provided by index theory which in this case works regardless of the dimension’s parity (see
Appendix

A
).

Another possible approach to
Theorem

1.5
would be to first extract a convergent subsequence from a sequence of maps almost realizing the hyperspherical radius and then prove rigidity directly for the limiting Lipschitz map.
Then the potential difficulty would lie in the fact that this map is
a priori
not necessarily smooth.
In the situation of Llarull’s theorem, a related low regularity Lipschitz rigidity result was recently established by
[
14
]
, where also singular metrics of
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}
-regularity (
p>n
) are considered.
In our present situation,
Theorem

1.6
immediately implies a rigidity result for low-regularity Lipschitz maps with smooth metrics as stated in the next corollary.
However,
Theorem

1.6
provides stronger control than merely a Lipschitz rigidity result because using an a priori compactness argument only yields
\Ct^{0,\alpha}
-subconvergence to a Lipschitz map rather than in
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}
.

Corollary 1.7
.

For
n\geq 3
, let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, strictly convex domain with smooth boundary.
Let
M
be a connected compact Riemannian spin manifold with boundary such that
\scal_{M}\geq 0
.
If
f\colon\partial M\to\partial\Omega
is a
(
not necessarily smooth
)
1-Lipschitz map of non-zero degree such that
\mean_{\partial M}\geq\mean_{\partial\Omega}\circ f
, then
f
is an isometry and
M
is flat and isometric to
\Omega
.

In the case when the map
f
in
Corollary

1.7
is
smooth
, its statement already follows from results of
[
41
, Theorem 3.2]
, see also
[
40
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Finally, we show in
Section

6
that the same techniques—that is, an index-theoretic existence result of a spinor and the integral inequality from
Proposition

4.4
—also imply a Witten-type integral formula
[
43
]
for the ADM-mass of an asymptotically Schwarzschild manifold.
Interestingly, unlike Witten’s approach, this formula does not use non-negative scalar curvature, and it is still valid when the underlying manifold is only asymptotically Schwarzschild up to first order instead of second order.
In particular this gives yet another proof of the Riemannian positive mass theorem and thereby addresses another question of
[
19
, 3]
on the relation between the theorems of Llarull, Goette–Semmelmann and Lott
[
30
,
17
,
31
]
to the positive mass theorem.

The paper is organized as follows.
In
Section

2
, we recall the basic notation and convention on spinor bundles and Dirac operators.
In
Section

3
, we prove the extension result for spinors (
Lemma

3.1
) and use it to deduce
Theorems

1.2
and
1.3
and the comparison result à la Lott (
Theorem

3.5
).

Section

4
is dedicated to proving the fundamental integral inequality (
Proposition

4.4
) needed for the remaining results.
In
Section

5
, we establish
Theorem

1.6
, from which we derive
Theorem

1.5
and
Corollary

1.7
.
Finally, in
Section

6
, we discuss the application to asymptotically flat manifolds.

2.
Notation and conventions

2.1.
The boundary Dirac operator and Weitzenböck formula

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold and
S\to M
a Dirac bundle in the sense of Gromov–Lawson
[
18
,
27
]
(for instance the spinor bundle if
M
is a spin manifold).
We denote with
\clm
the Clifford multiplication and with
\mathcal{D}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\clm(e_{i})\nabla_{e_{i}}
the Dirac operator on
S
.
We will study boundary value problems in this context, for the general theory of which we refer to
[
5
,
6
]
.
To this end, we denote the
interior
unit normal field of
\partial M
by
\nu_{M}
and denote by
\mean_{\partial M}
the mean curvature with respect to
\nu_{M}
.
Next, we fix the
boundary Dirac operator

(2.1)

\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}\clm^{\partial}(e_{i})\nabla^{\partial}_{e_{i}}=\frac{1}{2}\mean_{\partial M}-\clm(\nu_{M})\mathcal{D}-\nabla_{\nu_{M}}\colon\Ct^{\infty}(\partial M,S^{\partial})\to\Ct^{\infty}(\partial M,S^{\partial}),

where
S^{\partial}=S|_{\partial M}
,
\clm^{\partial}(\omega)=\clm(\omega)\clm(\nu)
,
\nabla^{\partial}_{\xi}=\nabla_{\xi}+\frac{1}{2}\clm^{\partial}(\nabla_{\xi}\nu)
.
This operator
\mathcal{A}
can be used as a canonical adapted operator on the boundary, see
[
6
, Appendix 1]
to study boundary value problems for
\mathcal{D}
.
Note that
\mathcal{A}\clm(\nu_{M})=-\clm(\nu_{M})\mathcal{A}
.

We then have the integrated Bochner–Lichnerowicz–Weitzenböck formula for
\Psi\in\Ct^{\infty}(M,S)
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}\Psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}^{2}=\|\nabla\Psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\mathcal{R}^{S}\Psi,\Psi\right)_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial M}\frac{1}{2}\mean_{\partial M}|\Psi|^{2}-\langle\mathcal{A}\Psi,\Psi\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}

see
[
6
, Appendix 1, equation (27)]
.
Here
\mathcal{R}^{S}
is given by

\mathcal{R}^{S}=\sum_{i<j}\clm(e_{i})\clm(e_{j})\mathrm{R}^{S}_{e_{i},e_{j}}.

In the case that
M
is spin and
S
its spinor bundle, we have
\mathcal{R}^{S}=\frac{\scal}{4}
, in which case the formula
2.2
is called the integrated
Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz formula
.

2.2.
Generalized APS boundary conditions

We need to impose boundary conditions to work with the Dirac operator on manifolds with boundary.
Given any self-adjoint first-order operator
A
on the boundary adapted to
\mathcal{D}
in the sense of
[
6
, §3.2]
, which might not necessarily be the canonical adapted operator described in
Section

2.1
above, we can define the corresponding (generalized)
[
2
]
(APS) boundary condition by imposing on sections
\Psi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(M,S)
that

\chi_{[0,\infty)}(A)\left(\Psi|_{\partial M}\right)=0,

where
\chi_{[0,\infty)}(A)
is the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto the non-negative part of the spectrum of
A
.
This is always an elliptic boundary condition
[
6
, Example 4.21]
.

In
Section

3
we will apply this to the case
A=\mathcal{A}+h
, where
h\in\Ct^{\infty}(\partial M,\mathbb{R})
is some smooth function on the boundary and
\mathcal{A}
is the canonical adapted operator.
The adjoint condition of
\chi_{[0,\infty)}(\mathcal{A}+h)\left(\Psi|_{\partial M}\right)=0
is given by

\chi_{(0,\infty)}(\mathcal{A}-h)\left(\Psi|_{\partial M}\right)=0,

compare
[
6
, §4.3]
, where
\chi_{(0,\infty)}
denotes the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto the positive part of the spectrum.
Note the subtle but important difference between non-negative and positive parts of the spectrum as well as the change in sign in front of the function
h
.

2.3.
Spin maps and local boundary conditions

In this subsection, we set up another boundary value problem, namely the one we need for the proof of
Theorem

1.5
.
This is a conceptual elaboration of the approach used by
[
31
]
that has also been used by several other authors in recent times
[
12
,
11
,
41
]
.

Let
f\colon(M,\partial M)\to(N,\partial N)
be a smooth
spin map
between Riemannian manifolds with boundary of the same dimension
n
.
Being a spin map means that
w_{1}(\mathrm{T}M)=f^{\ast}w_{1}(\mathrm{T}N)
and
w_{2}(\mathrm{T}M)=f^{\ast}w_{2}(\mathrm{T}N)
, or equivalently that the bundle
\mathrm{T}M\oplus f^{\ast}\mathrm{T}N
endowed with the pseudo-Riemannian bundle metric
g_{M}\oplus(-g_{N})
admits a spin structure.
1
1

1

If both
M
and
N
are oriented, this is by coincidence equivalent to
\mathrm{T}M\oplus f^{\ast}\mathrm{T}N
having a spin structure also with respect to a positive definite bundle metric.
But in general the pseudo-Riemannian perspective is the topologically appropriate point of view, compare also
[
39
, §1.2 and §4]
, and it avoids having to introduce factors of
\sqrt{-1}
at some places in our proofs.

We say that a
spin structure on
f
is a spin structure on
(\mathrm{T}M\oplus f^{\ast}\mathrm{T}N,g_{M}\oplus(-g_{N}))
.

Recall that the Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{n,n}=\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{n},(\delta,-\delta))
has a canonical irreducible representation
\mathbf{c}\colon\mathrm{Cl}_{n,n}\xrightarrow{\cong}\End(\bigwedge\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is generated by the standard Clifford actions on the exterior algebra

(2.3)

\displaystyle\textbf{c}(v,0)\alpha

\displaystyle\coloneqq\clm(v)\alpha\coloneqq v\wedge\alpha-\ins_{v}\alpha,

\displaystyle\textbf{c}(0,v)\alpha

\displaystyle\coloneqq\clms(v)\alpha\coloneqq v\wedge\alpha+\ins_{v}\alpha.

Let
S
be the complexified spinor bundle associated to a chosen spin structure on
\mathrm{T}M\oplus f^{\ast}\mathrm{T}N
and the canonical representation
c
, that is,

S=\left(\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{K}_{n,n}}(\mathrm{T}M\oplus f^{\ast}\mathrm{T}N)\times_{\mathrm{K}_{n,n},\textbf{c}}\bigwedge\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\otimes\mathbb{C},

where
\mathrm{K}_{n,n}\subset\Spin_{n,n}\subset\mathrm{Cl}_{n,n}^{\times}
denotes the maximal compact subgroup covering
\mathrm{S}(\Orth_{n}\times\Orth_{n})\subset\SO_{n,n}
.
Note that
S
admits two different Clifford actions, one for vector fields
\xi\in\Vfds(M)
on
M
which we denote by
\clm(\xi)=\textbf{c}(\xi,0)\in\End(S)
and the other for vector fields
\eta\in\Vfds(N)
on
N
which we denote by
\clms(\eta)=\textbf{c}(0,f^{\ast}\eta)\in\End(S)
.
By construction, the usual Clifford algebra relations hold which we spell out in the following for convenience of the reader.

\displaystyle\clm(\xi)^{2}=-|\xi|^{2},\quad\clms(\eta)^{2}=|\eta|^{2},\quad\clm(\xi)\clms(\eta)=-\clms(\eta)\clm(\xi),

\displaystyle\clm(\xi)^{\ast}=-\clm(\xi),\quad\clms(\eta)^{\ast}=\clms(\eta).

With respect to the Clifford action of
M
, the bundle
S
is a Dirac bundle in the sense of Gromov–Lawson and we have the Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\clm(e_{i})\nabla_{e_{i}}
.
In this case, the interior curvature term
\mathcal{R}^{S}
in the Bochner–Lichnerowicz–Weitzenböck formula
2.2
is explicitly given by
\mathcal{R}^{S}=\frac{\scal}{4}+\mathcal{R}^{N}
, where

(2.4)

\mathcal{R}^{N}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}\clm(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})\clms\left(\mathrm{R}^{\mathrm{T}N}(\mathrm{d}{f}(e_{i})\wedge\mathrm{d}{f}(e_{j}))\right).

Again we need to impose suitable boundary conditions.
In addition to APS-type conditions, in this case there are specific local boundary conditions well-adapted to the situation at hand.
To describe these, let
\nu_{M}
and
\nu_{N}
denote the interior unit normals of the boundaries of
M
and
N
, respectively, and let
s\colon\partial M\to\{\pm 1\}
be a choice of sign for each connected component of
\partial M
.
Then we may impose the local boundary condition

(2.5)

\clm(\nu_{M})\psi=s\clms(\nu_{N})\psi\quad\text{on $\partial M$.}

Rewriting this as
\chi(\psi)=s\psi
with
\chi=\clms(\nu_{N})\clm(\nu_{M})
(and using
\chi\clm(\nu_{M})=-\clm(\nu_{M})\chi
as well as
Lemma

4.1
below), one verifies that this is a self-adjoint elliptic boundary condition.
Note that for
s=1
, this corresponds to the boundary condition used by Lott
[
31
]
.
Moreover, the bundle
S
inherits a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-grading
S=S^{+}\oplus S^{-}
from the standard even/odd grading on
\bigwedge\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to which
\mathcal{D}
is odd. As usual, we can thus define the index

\ind(\mathcal{D},s)=\dim\ker(\mathcal{D}^{+},s)-\dim\ker(\mathcal{D}^{-},s),

where we take the kernel on smooth (or equivalently
\mathrm{H}^{1}
-) sections satisfying the boundary condition
2.5
.
A similar setup in a related context was considered in
[
15
, §2–3]
, compare also
[
7
, Appendix B]
.

We consider two standard examples.
The first is that this reduces to differential forms in case
M=N
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
M=N
be the same Riemannian manifold and
f=\id
.
Then
\mathrm{T}M\oplus f^{\ast}(\mathrm{T}M)=\mathrm{T}M\oplus\mathrm{T}M
and this bundle admits a canonical spin structure with respect to the bundle metric
g_{M}\oplus(-g_{M})
.
The exterior algebra
\bigwedge\mathrm{T}{M}
is itself a fiberwise irreducible bundle of
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathrm{T}M\oplus\mathrm{T}M,g_{M}\oplus(-g_{M}))
-modules, so we may just take
S=\bigwedge\mathrm{T}{M}\otimes\mathbb{C}
and, in light of
2.3
,
\mathcal{D}=d+d^{\ast}
is the de Rham operator on complex-valued differential forms.
By the same token, for
s=1
the boundary condition
2.5
then becomes the
\ins_{\nu_{M}}\alpha=0
—in other words, absolute boundary conditions.
In particular,
\ker(\mathcal{D},1)
is isomorphic to the de Rham cohomology of
M
and so
\ind(\mathcal{D},1)=\chi(M)
.
Similarly, for
s=-1
it means
\nu_{M}\wedge\alpha=0
—that is, relative boundary conditions.
For a general sign choice
s
, it would mean using absolute boundary conditions on some boundary components and relative on the others.

Convention 2.2
.

In the remaining part of this paper, we will assume that every domain in Euclidean space is bounded and has smooth boundary.

The second example is where
N=\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex domain, which is the case we will use in the proof of
Theorem

1.5
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
N=\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex domain.
Then
\mathrm{T}\Omega=\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the trivial bundle and hence
\mathrm{T}M\oplus f^{\ast}\mathrm{T}\Omega=\mathrm{T}M\oplus\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In this case, the condition of
f
being spin is equivalent to
M
admitting a spin structure.
Moreover, given a spin structure on
M
with its principal
\Spin_{n}
-bundle, we then obtain

S=\left(\mathrm{P}_{\Spin_{n}}(\mathrm{T}M)\times_{\Spin_{n},\clm}\bigwedge\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\otimes\mathbb{C}.

Note that as a
\mathrm{Cl}_{n,0}
-module the exterior algebra
\bigwedge\mathbb{R}^{n}
is not irreducible and it splits into multiple irreducible submodules.
For instance, if
n
is even, we can identify
S
with
\not{S}_{M}\otimes\not{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, where
\not{S}
refers to the usual complex spinor bundles.
The latter being trivial, we can further observe that
S\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\not{S}_{M}
, where
m=\dim\not{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=2^{\frac{n}{2}}
, so a section of
S
can be identified with a system of spinors on
M
—this is essentially the point of view taken by
[
11
]
.
However, we will not make use of this observation and instead work with the abstract construction of the bundle, which works regardless of the dimension’s parity.

We also note that the bundle
S
and its Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}
do not depend on the map
f
or the domain
\Omega
at all, but the boundary condition
2.5
does.
We then have

\ind(\mathcal{D},1)=\deg(f).

In the even-dimensional case, this is a consequence of
[
31
]
, but again there is no reason to restrict to the even-dimensional case and we provide a direct argument of this index formula in
Appendix

A
independently of the dimension’s parity, see
Theorem

A.3
.

3.
NNSC fill-ins with non-negative mean curvature

In this section, we answer Miao’s question
[
34
, Question 2]
restricted to spin fill-ins, establish fill-in extremality of spin domains admitting a parallel spinor and estabish a comparison result in the spirit of
[
31
]
.
All of these results rely on the following general extension lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
be a compact connected Riemannian manifold with boundary endowed with a Dirac bundle
S\to M
such that
\mathcal{R}^{S}\geq 0
on
M
.
Let
h\colon\partial M\to[0,\infty)
be a smooth non-negative function and let
\psi_{0}\in\Ct^{\infty}(\partial M,S)
be a non-zero section satisfying
\mathcal{A}\psi_{0}=\frac{1}{2}h\psi_{0}
.
Furthermore, assume that
\mean_{\partial M}\geq h
.
Then the bundle
S\to M
always admits a non-zero parallel section and
\mean_{\partial M}=h
.
Moreover, if
\mean_{\partial M}\neq 0
, then
\psi_{0}
itself extends to a parallel section of
S\to M
.

Proof.

We consider the Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}(M,S;B)\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(M,S)
subject to the boundary condition
B
given by the spectral projection
\chi_{[0,\infty)}(\mathcal{A}-\frac{1}{2}h)
as described in
Section

2.2
.
Then the adjoint boundary condition
B^{\mathrm{ad}}
is determined by the projection
\chi_{(0,\infty)}(\mathcal{A}+\frac{1}{2}h)
.
We now apply the Fredholm alternative.

First consider the case that the operator
\mathcal{D}\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}(M,S;B)\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(M,S)
is surjective.
Then choose a section
\Psi_{0}\in\Ct^{\infty}(M,S)
such that
\Psi_{0}|_{\partial M}=\psi_{0}
.
By surjectivity, we can furthermore choose
\Psi_{1}
such that
\mathcal{D}\Psi_{1}=-\mathcal{D}\Psi_{0}
and
\chi_{[0,\infty)}(\mathcal{A}-\frac{1}{2}h)(\psi_{1})=0
with
\psi_{1}\coloneqq{\Psi_{1}}|_{\partial M}
.
We then set
\Psi=\Psi_{0}+\Psi_{1}
. Then
\mathcal{D}\Psi=0
and hence by (
2.2
) we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla\Psi\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\int_{\partial M}\langle(\tfrac{1}{2}\mean_{\partial M}-\mathcal{A})\Psi,\Psi\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}

\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla\Psi\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\int_{\partial M}\langle(\tfrac{1}{2}h-\mathcal{A})\Psi,\Psi\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla\Psi\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\int_{\partial M}\langle(\tfrac{1}{2}h-\mathcal{A})\psi_{1},\psi_{1}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}+\int_{\partial M}\langle(\tfrac{1}{2}h-\mathcal{A})\psi_{0},\psi_{0}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla\Psi\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\int_{\partial M}\langle(\tfrac{1}{2}h-\mathcal{A})\psi_{1},\psi_{1}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}\geq\|\nabla\Psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\lambda_{0}\int_{\partial M}|\psi_{1}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}\geq 0,

where
\lambda_{0}>0
is the smallest positive eigenvalue of the operator
\tfrac{1}{2}h-\mathcal{A}
.
This means that
\nabla\Psi=0
,
\psi_{1}=0
and hence
\Psi|_{\partial M}=\psi_{0}
.
So we have found the desired parallel section
\Psi
and it extends
\psi_{0}
.
Moreover, it then follows from
2.1
that
\mathcal{A}\psi_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\mean_{\partial M}\psi_{0}
.
But since by assumption we have
\mathcal{A}\psi_{0}=\frac{1}{2}h\psi_{0}
, this means that
h=\mean_{\partial M}
.

On the other hand, if the operator
\mathcal{D}\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}(M,S;B)\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(M,S)
is not surjective, then the operator
\mathcal{D}
, subject to the adjoint condition
B^{\mathrm{ad}}
, must have a non-trivial element in the kernel
\Psi
, that is,
\mathcal{D}\Psi=0
and
\chi_{(0,\infty)}(\mathcal{A}+\frac{1}{2}h)(\Psi|_{\partial M})
= 0.
By (
2.2
),

\displaystyle 0=\|\mathcal{D}\Psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla\Psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\partial M}\langle(\tfrac{1}{2}\mean_{\partial M}-\mathcal{A})\Psi,\Psi\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\partial M}\tfrac{1}{2}\mean_{\partial M}|\Psi|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}+\int_{\partial M}\langle(-\tfrac{1}{2}h-\mathcal{A})\Psi,\Psi\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}\geq 0,

where we crucially use non-negativity of
h
and
\mean_{\partial M}
.
We conclude that
\nabla\Psi=0
, which provides the desired parallel section for this case, as well as
0=\mean_{\partial M}\geq h\geq 0
and so also
\mean_{\partial M}=h=0
.
In particular, this second case can only occur if
\mean_{\partial M}=0
.
∎

Applying
Lemma

3.1
to the spinor bundle itself yields the following statement.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(\Sigma^{n-1},g_{\Sigma})
be a closed spin manifold, and let
h\colon\Sigma\to[0,\infty)
be a smooth non-negative function such that there exists a non-trivial spinor
\psi\in\Ct^{\infty}(\Sigma,\not{S}_{\Sigma})
satisfying
\not{D}_{\Sigma}\psi=\frac{1}{2}h\psi
.
Then any NNSC spin fill-in
(M,g)
of
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},h)
with
\mean_{\partial M}\geq h
admits a parallel spinor and thus is Ricci-flat. Moreover, in this case
\mean_{\partial M}=h
.

Proof.

Let
S\to M
denote the spinor bundle of
M
with Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}
.
Then its restriction to the boundary
S^{\partial}
can be identified with one or two copies, depending on dimension parity, of the spinor bundle
\not{S}_{\Sigma}
such that the canonical boundary operator
\mathcal{A}
identifies with
\not{D}_{\Sigma}
on each copy.
Thus by assumption there exists a section
\psi_{0}\in\Ct^{\infty}(\Sigma,S^{\partial})
such that
\mathcal{A}\psi_{0}=\frac{1}{2}h\psi_{0}
.
Thus the statement follows from
Lemma

3.1
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the first two of our main theorems.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

On the Berger sphere with constant scalar curvature
\scal\leq-24
there is a solution to the Dirac equation according to
[
25
, page 37]
.
Similarly, Bär showed in
[
3
]
that on every closed spin manifold of dimension
n\equiv 3\mod 4
, there exists a metric admitting a harmonic spinor, and Hitchin did so in
[
25
, Theorem 4.5]
for dimension
n\equiv 0,1,7\mod 8
.
Hence, the result follows from
Proposition

3.2
with
h=0
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

Given a parallel spinor on
N
, its restriction to
\Sigma
provides a spinor
\psi_{0}
satisfying
\mathcal{A}\psi_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\mean_{\partial N}\psi_{0}
because of
2.1
.
Then we are in the situation of
Proposition

3.2
and any spin NNSC fill-in
(M,g)
of
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},h)
with
h\geq\mean_{\partial N}
admits a parallel spinor and satisfies
h=\mean_{\partial N}
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The proof of
Lemma

3.1
has the curious feature that both cases of the Fredholm alternative lead to existence of a spinor with the desired property.
One might be tempted to ask more generally whether in the setting of the theorem any spinor on the boundary
\psi_{0}\in\Ct^{\infty}(\partial M,S)
satisfying
\mathcal{A}\psi_{0}=\frac{1}{2}h\psi_{0}
can be extended to a parallel spinor on a spin NNSC fill-in with
\mean\geq h
.

The proof already shows that this is the case whenever the first part of the Fredholm alternative applies.
Moreover, the second case can only occur if
h=0
.
So if
h\neq 0
, any such spinor indeed extends to a parallel spinor on the fill-in.

However, if
h=0
and we have a non-trivial spinor
\psi_{0}
at the boundary satisfying
\mathcal{A}\psi_{0}=0
, then
\psi_{1}=\clm(\nu_{M})\psi_{0}
also satisfies
\mathcal{A}\psi_{1}=0
.
But it is not possible to simultaneously extend both
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{1}
to harmonic spinors on the spin fill-in
M
, because if
\Psi_{i}
was a harmonic spinor on
M
extending
\psi_{i}
for
i=0,1
, then we would have

0=(\mathcal{D}\Psi_{0},\Psi_{1})_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}-(\Psi_{0},\mathcal{D}\Psi_{1})_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}=\int_{\partial M}\langle\psi_{0},\clm(\nu_{M})\psi_{1}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}=-\int_{\partial M}\lvert\psi_{0}\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}\neq 0.

Relatedly, non-negativity of
h
cannot be dropped from the hypotheses of
Lemma

3.1
because if
\mathcal{A}\psi_{0}=\frac{1}{2}h\psi_{0}
, then we have
\mathcal{A}\psi_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(-h)\psi_{1}
for
\psi_{1}=\clm(\nu_{M})\psi_{0}
.
For instance, the sphere
\mathrm{S}^{n-1}
for
n\geq 3
admits a spinor
\psi_{1}
with
\not{D}_{\mathrm{S}^{n-1}}\psi_{1}=-\frac{n-1}{2}\psi_{1}
even though the hemisphere in
\mathrm{S}^{n}
is a spin PSC fill-in of
\mathrm{S}^{n-1}
with
\mean=0\geq-\frac{n-1}{2}
.
More drastically, the boundary Dirac operator always has arbitrarily negative eigenvalues.

Remark 3.4
.

A similar result as in
Proposition

3.2
was established by

provided that

does not vanish identically.
However, for the rigidity statement in our main application,

A closely related statement was also claimed by

formulated in terms of the smallest non-negative eigenvalue

However, the statement and proof of

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
f\colon(M,\partial M)\to(N,\partial N)
be a smooth spin map between connected Riemannian manifolds with boundary such that

(i)

the restricted map
\partial f\coloneqq f|_{\partial M}\colon\partial M\to\partial N
is an isometry,

(ii)

the map
f
admits a spin structure which extends the canonical spin structure of
\partial f
from
Example

2.1
,

(iii)

f\colon M\to N
is area non-increasing,

(iv)

\scal_{M}\geq\scal_{N}\circ f
and the curvature operator of
N
is non-negative,

(v)

\mean_{\partial M}\geq\mean_{\partial N}\circ\partial f
and
\mean_{\partial N}\geq 0
.

Then
\scal_{M}=\scal_{N}\circ f
and
\mean_{\partial M}=\mean_{\partial N}\circ\partial f
.
Moreover, if one of the two following additional conditions holds,

•

0<\Ric_{N}<\frac{\scal_{N}}{2}
,

•

or
0<\Ric_{N}
and
f
is distance non-increasing,

then
f\colon M\to N
is an isometry.

Remark 3.6
.

The condition on the spin structure

is automatically satisfied if the restriction to the boundary induces a surjective map
\HZ^{1}(N;\mathbb{Z}/2)\to\HZ^{1}(\partial N;\mathbb{Z}/2)
on the first
\mathbb{Z}/2

.

Theorem

3.5
.

We work with the Dirac bundle

as in

2.3
, where we choose a spin structure on

We use the normal field
\nu_{M}
to identify

Remark 3.7
.

The concept of a NNSC spin fill-in in the statement of

That this is a necessary restriction can already be seen in the case of

In particular, the presence of the latter spin structure does not contradict the fact that the disk is an NNSC fill-in of
\mathrm{S}^{1}
with positive mean curvature.
Indeed, following

which visibly does not have a kernel.
A more intrinsic description is that sections of the spinor bundle on
\mathrm{S}^{1}
associated to the spin structure restricted from the disc can be identified with

-anti-periodic functions on
\mathbb{R}
.
The other spin structure on
\mathrm{S}^{1}
—the one that does

extend to the disc—corresponds to the trivial principal spin bundle and hence is just the trivial bundle
\mathrm{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
on which the Dirac operator

acts. It clearly has a kernel given by the constant functions.
See for instance
[

]
for more details on these and other examples of this nature.

Of course, this aspect not relevant in the case of the main example in

1.2
, the Berger sphere, since
\mathrm{S}^{3}
has only one spin structure.

In the case of

3.5
, there is also spin extension condition as part of

, but it is unclear if this is necessary.

Lemma 4.1
.

\mathcal{A}\clms(\nu_{N})=\clms(\nu_{N})\mathcal{A}+\clm(\nu_{M})\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\clm(e_{i})\clms(-\nabla_{\mathrm{d}f(e_{i})}\nu_{N})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\clm(e_{i})\clm(\nu_{M})\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\partial}\clms(\nu_{N})\psi

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\clm(e_{i})\clm(\nu_{M})\clms(\nabla_{\mathrm{d}{f}(e_{i})}\nu_{N})\psi+\clm(e_{i})\clm(\nu_{M})\clms(\nu_{N})\nabla^{\partial}_{e_{i}}\psi\right)

To estimate the curvature terms further, we will use various norms of a linear map

Lemma 4.2

Suppose that

It follows from the fact that
\mathcal{A}

\clm(\nu_{M})
and
\clms(\nu_{N})\clm(\nu_{M})\psi=s\psi
that

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}\psi,\psi\rangle=

\displaystyle s\langle\mathcal{A}\psi,\clms(\nu_{N})\clm(\nu_{M})\psi\rangle=s\langle\clms(\nu_{N})\mathcal{A}\psi,\clm(\nu_{M})\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\langle(\clms(\nu_{N})\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{A}\clms(\nu_{N}))\psi,\clm(\nu_{M})\psi\rangle-s\langle\mathcal{A}\clms(\nu_{N})\clm(\nu_{M})\psi,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\langle(\clms(\nu_{N})\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{A}\clms(\nu_{N}))\psi,\clm(\nu_{M})\psi\rangle-\langle\mathcal{A}\psi,\psi\rangle.

Hence,
Lemma

4.1
implies

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}\psi,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{s}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left\langle\clm(\nu_{M})\clm(e_{i})\clms((\Shape_{\partial N}\circ\mathrm{d}{f})(e_{i}))\psi,\clm(\nu_{M})\psi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{s}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left\langle\clm(e_{i})\clms((\Shape_{\partial N}\circ\mathrm{d}{f})(e_{i}))\psi,\psi\right\rangle.

Now choose an orthonormal basis
(e_{i})
of
\mathrm{T}_{x}(\partial M)
such that
(\Shape_{\partial N}\circ\mathrm{d}{f})(e_{i})=\sigma_{i}\bar{e}_{i}
, where
\bar{e_{i}}=Ue_{i}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathrm{T}_{f(x)}(\partial N)
and
\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i}(\Shape_{\partial N}\circ\mathrm{d}_{x}{f})\geq 0
are the singular values of
\Shape_{\partial N}\circ\mathrm{d}_{x}f
.
Then
\lvert\Shape_{\partial N}\circ\mathrm{d}{f}\rvert_{\tr}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{i}
.
We thus obtain

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}\psi,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{s}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left\langle\clm(e_{i})\clms((\Shape_{\partial N}\circ\mathrm{d}{f})(e_{i}))\psi,\psi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{s}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{i}\underbrace{\left\langle\clm(e_{i})\clms(\bar{e}_{i})\psi,\psi\right\rangle}_{-|\psi|^{2}\leq\dots\leq|\psi|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lvert\Shape_{\partial N}\circ\mathrm{d}{f}\rvert_{\tr}\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{i}\underbrace{\left(\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}+s\left\langle\clm(e_{i})\clms(\bar{e}_{i})\psi,\psi\right\rangle\right)}_{\geq 0}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\lvert\Shape_{\partial N}\circ\mathrm{d}{f}\rvert_{\tr}\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}-\frac{\sigma_{\min}}{2}\sum_{i}\left(\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}+s\left\langle\clm(e_{i})\clms(\bar{e}_{i})\psi,\psi\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lvert\Shape_{\partial N}\circ\mathrm{d}{f}\rvert_{\tr}\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}-\frac{\sigma_{\min}}{2}\sum_{i}\left(\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}-s\left\langle\clms(\bar{e}_{i})\psi,\clm(e_{i})\psi\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lvert\Shape_{\partial N}\circ\mathrm{d}{f}\rvert_{\tr}\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}-\frac{\sigma_{\min}}{4}\sum_{i}\lvert\clms(Ue_{i})\psi-s\clm(e_{i})\psi\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lvert\Shape_{\partial N}\circ\mathrm{d}{f}\rvert_{\tr}\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}-\frac{\sigma_{\min}}{4}\ \lvert\clms(U{-})\psi-s\clm({-})\psi\rvert^{2}.\qed

The same argument also shows:

Lemma 4.3
.

For a section

, pointwise on

\langle\mathcal{R}^{N}\Psi,\Psi\rangle\geq-\frac{1}{2}\lvert\mathrm{R}^{\mathrm{T}N}\circ(\mathrm{d}{f}\wedge\mathrm{d}{f})\rvert_{\tr}\lvert\Psi\rvert^{2}.

Proof.

Choose an orthonormal basis
(\omega_{\alpha})
of
\Lambda^{2}\mathrm{T}_{x}M
such that
\mathrm{R}^{\mathrm{T}N}\circ(\mathrm{d}{f}\wedge\mathrm{d}{f})(\omega_{\alpha})=\lambda_{\alpha}\bar{\omega}_{\alpha}
, where
(\bar{\omega}_{\alpha})
is an orthonormal basis of
\Lambda^{2}\mathrm{T}_{f(x)}N
and
\lambda_{\alpha}\geq 0
.
Then
\lvert\mathrm{R}^{\mathrm{T}N}\circ(\mathrm{d}{f}\wedge\mathrm{d}{f})\rvert_{\tr}=\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}
.
It follows from
2.4
that

\langle\mathcal{R}^{N}\Psi,\Psi\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\langle\clm(\omega_{\alpha})\clms(\bar{\omega}_{\alpha})\Psi,\Psi\rangle

and thus
\langle\mathcal{R}^{N}\Psi,\Psi\rangle\geq-\frac{1}{2}\lvert\mathrm{R}^{\mathrm{T}N}\circ(\mathrm{d}{f}\wedge\mathrm{d}{f})\rvert_{\tr}\lvert\Psi\rvert^{2}
.
∎

Combining

Corollary 4.5
.

In particuar, if the boundary

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}\Psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla\Psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left(\scal_{M}\Psi,\Psi\right)_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\lvert\mathrm{R}^{\mathrm{T}N}\circ\mathrm{d}{f}\wedge\mathrm{d}{f}\rvert_{\tr}\lvert\Psi\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{V}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial M}\left(\mean_{\partial M}-(\mean_{\partial N}\circ f)\cdot\lambda\right)|\Psi|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\partial M}\frac{\sigma_{\min}(\Shape_{\partial N})}{\lambda}\lvert\mathrm{d}{f}-\lambda U\rvert_{2}^{2}|\Psi|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\partial M}\sigma_{\min}(\Shape_{\partial N}\circ\mathrm{d}{f})\lvert\clms(U{-})\Psi-s\clm({-})\Psi\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S},

where

is a bundle isometry and we have

on
\partial M

5.
Almost rigidity for maps to Euclidean domains

In this section, we prove our main almost rigidity theorem for maps to Euclidean domains and deduce rigidity for Gromov’s hyperspherical radius estimate.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with smooth strictly convex boundary
\partial\Omega
.
Let
M
be a connected compact Riemannian spin manifold with connected boundary such that
\scal_{M}\geq 0
.
Let
f_{i}\colon\partial M\to\partial\Omega
be a sequence of smooth maps such that, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

•

\operatorname{Lip}(f_{i})\leq 1+\frac{1}{i}
,

•

\mean_{\partial M}\geq\mean_{\partial\Omega}\circ f_{i}-\frac{1}{i}
,

•

\deg(f_{i})\neq 0
.

Then there exists a smooth isometry
f\colon\partial M\to\partial\Omega
such that
\II_{\partial M}=f^{\ast}\II_{\partial\Omega}
and a subsequence
(f_{i_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
f_{i_{k}}\to f
in
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(\partial M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
p<\infty
.

Proof.

Extend the maps
f_{i}
to obtain maps
F_{i}\colon(M,\partial M)\to(\Omega,\partial\Omega)
of the same degrees and such that
\mathrm{d}{F_{i}}(\nu_{M})=\nu_{\Omega}
.
Since by
Example

2.3
we have
\ind(\mathcal{D},1)=\deg(F_{i})=\deg(f_{i})\neq 0
, we find non-trivial harmonic spinors
\Psi_{i}
with
\|\Psi_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}^{2}=1
satisfying the boundary condition
\clm(\nu_{M})\Psi_{i}=\clms_{i}(\nu_{\Omega})\Psi_{i}
.
Now the estimate given by
Corollary

4.5
(applied with
\lambda=1+\frac{1}{i}
and
s=1
) shows that there exists some fixed constant
\kappa>0
(not depending on
i
) such that for all
i
we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{i}\int_{\partial M}\lvert\Psi_{i}\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}\geq\|

\displaystyle\nabla\Psi_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\lvert\mathrm{d}{f_{i}}-(1+\tfrac{1}{i})U_{i}\rvert_{2}|\Psi_{i}|\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\partial M)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\|\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}(\Shape_{\partial\Omega}\circ\mathrm{d}{f_{i}})}\left(\clms(U_{i}{-})\Psi_{i}-\clm({-})\Psi_{i}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\partial M)}^{2}.

Here
U_{i}
denotes a measurable bundle isometry
\mathrm{T}(\partial M)\to f_{i}^{\ast}\mathrm{T}(\partial\Omega)
such that
\Shape_{\partial\Omega}\circ\mathrm{d}{f_{i}}=P_{i}\circ U_{i}
is a polar decomposition of
\Shape_{\partial\Omega}\circ\mathrm{d}{f_{i}}
.
Note that while
U_{i}
is uniquely determined and smooth on any subset where
\mathrm{d}{f_{i}}
is invertible because we have assumed
\Shape_{\partial\Omega}
to be strictly positive, we may have non-uniqueness and jumps in the presence of non-regular points of
f_{i}
, which is why a priori we can only assume
U_{i}
to be a measurable section.

By the trace inequality applied to the function
|\Psi_{i}|
, we also obtain a constant
C_{\mathrm{T}}\geq 0
not depending on
i
such that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}|\Psi_{i}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}\leq C_{\mathrm{T}}\|\Psi_{i}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(M)}^{2}=C_{\mathrm{T}}\left(1+\|\nabla\Psi_{i}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}\right)

for all
i
.
Plugging
5.2
into
5.1
shows that the right-hand side of
5.1
tends to zero as
i\to\infty
, that is,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Psi_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\to 0,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\|\lvert\mathrm{d}{f_{i}}-(1+\tfrac{1}{i})U_{i}\rvert_{2}|\Psi_{i}|\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\partial M)}

\displaystyle\to 0,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}(\Shape_{\partial\Omega}\circ\mathrm{d}{f_{i}})}\left(\clms(U_{i}{-})\Psi_{i}-\clm({-})\Psi_{i}\right)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\partial M)}

\displaystyle\to 0.

In particular, the sequence
(\Psi_{i})
is uniformly bounded in
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(M)
, so after passing to a subsequence we may assume that
\Psi_{i}\to\Psi
in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)
for some spinor
\Psi
with
\|\Psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}=1
by the Rellich theorem.
But then
5.3
further implies that this convergence already takes place in
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(M)
and
\nabla\Psi=0
, in particular
\Psi
is smooth on all of
M
and
\lvert\Psi\rvert
is constant.
Moreover, by the trace theorem,

(5.6)

\Psi_{i}|_{\partial M}\to\Psi|_{\partial M}\text{ in $\mathrm{L}^{2}(\partial M)$.}

Since
\mathrm{d}{f}_{i}
and
U_{i}
are uniformly bounded in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\partial M)
, the convergence
5.6
together with
5.4
and
5.5
means that as
i\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{d}{f_{i}}-U_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\partial M)}

\displaystyle\to 0,

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\sigma_{\min}(\Shape_{\partial\Omega}\circ\mathrm{d}{f_{i}})}\left(\clms(U_{i}{-})\Psi-\clm({-})\Psi\right)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\partial M)}

\displaystyle\to 0,

respectively.
After passing to a further subsequence, we can then ensure that pointwise almost everywhere on
\partial M
we have

(5.7)

\displaystyle|\mathrm{d}{f_{i}}-U_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle\to 0,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\min}(\Shape_{\partial\Omega}\circ\mathrm{d}{f_{i}})\ |\clms(U_{i}{-})\Psi-\clm({-})\Psi|^{2}

\displaystyle\to 0.

At a point
x\in\partial M
where
|\mathrm{d}_{x}{f_{i}}-(U_{i})_{x}|\to 0
, we have

\liminf_{i\to\infty}\sigma_{\min}((\Shape_{\partial\Omega}\circ\mathrm{d}{f_{i}})_{x})>0

because
\liminf_{i\to\infty}\sigma_{\min}((\Shape_{\partial\Omega})_{f_{i}(x)})>0
everywhere due to strict convexity of the boundary.
So we deduce that

(5.8)

|\clms(U_{i}{-})\Psi-\clm({-})\Psi|\to 0\quad\text{pointwise almost everywhere on $\partial M$.}

Next
5.8
implies that there exists a measurable section
U
of
\Hom(\mathrm{T}(\partial M),\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is a fiberwise isometric embedding
\mathrm{T}_{x}(\partial M)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that that
U_{i}\to U
pointwise almost everywhere.
This is because we have an injective bundle map

(5.9)

\Hom(\mathrm{T}_{x}(\partial M),\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow\Hom(\mathrm{T}_{x}(\partial M),S_{x}),\quad T\mapsto\clms(T{-})\Psi.

Together with
5.7
this implies
\mathrm{d}{f}_{i}\to U
pointwise almost everywhere on
\partial M
.
Again using that
\mathrm{d}{f_{i}}
and
U
are uniformly bounded
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\partial M)
, this implies that
\mathrm{d}{f}_{i}\to U
in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\partial M,\Hom(\mathrm{T}(\partial M),\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for every
p<\infty
by dominated convergence.

Finally, we use Arzelà–Ascoli to pass to another subsequence such that
f_{i}\to f
in
\Ct^{0}
, where
f\colon\partial M\to\partial\Omega
is a Lipschitz map.
Since we already know that
\mathrm{d}{f_{i}}
converges in
\mathrm{L}^{p}
, it follows that
(f_{i})
is a Cauchy sequence also in
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(\partial M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and hence the convergence
f_{i}\to f
takes place in
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(\partial M,\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
p<\infty
.
Furthermore, it follows that
\mathrm{d}{f}=U
almost everywhere and so
\mathrm{d}{f}
is an isometry almost everywhere.

Finally, we deduce from
5.8
that

(5.10)

\clms(\mathrm{d}{f}(\xi))\Psi=\clm(\xi)\Psi

almost everywhere for any smooth vector field
\xi
tangent to
\partial M
.
Now since the parallel spinor
\Psi
is smooth, this means already that
\mathrm{d}{f}\colon\mathrm{T}(\partial M)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is smooth, again because of the smooth bundle embedding
5.9
.
Thus
f
is smooth on
\partial M
.
But then
f\colon\partial M\to\partial\Omega
is a smooth local isometry and hence an isometry because
\partial\Omega
is diffeomorphic to the sphere.

To obtain the result on the second fundamental forms, first observe that
\Psi
also satisfies the boundary condition
\clms(\nu_{\Omega})\Psi=\clm(\nu_{M})\Psi
, where
\clms(\nu_{\Omega})
is defined in terms of the limiting map
f
.
Differentiating this equality in the direction of a vector field
\xi
tangential to
\partial M
, we obtain

\clm(\nabla_{\xi}\nu_{M})\Psi=\clms(\nabla_{\mathrm{d}{f}(\xi)}\nu_{\Omega})\Psi\underset{\lx@cref{refnum}{eq:clifford_action}}{=}\clm((\mathrm{d}{f})^{-1}\nabla_{\mathrm{d}{f}(\xi)}\nu_{\Omega})\Psi,

and thus
\nabla_{\xi}\nu_{M}=(\mathrm{d}{f})^{-1}\nabla_{\mathrm{d}{f}(\xi)}\nu_{\Omega}
which proves the desired statement
\II_{\partial M}=f^{\ast}\II_{\partial\Omega}
.
∎

The next proposition already follows from
[
41
, Theorem 3.2]
.
We include a self-contained proof, which does not make use of the analysis on manifolds with corners for the odd-dimensional case.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with smooth strictly convex boundary
\partial\Omega
.
Let
M
be a connected compact Riemannian spin manifold with boundary such that
\scal_{M}\geq 0
.
Let
f\colon\partial M\to\partial\Omega
be an isometry satisfying
\II_{\partial M}=f^{\ast}\II_{\partial\Omega}
.
Then
M
is isometric to
\Omega
.

Proof.

We work in the same setup as the proof of
Proposition

5.1
above.
That is, extend
f
to a smooth map
F\colon(M,\partial M)\to(\Omega,\partial\Omega)
of the same degree such that
\mathrm{d}{F}(\nu_{M})=\nu_{\Omega}
.
Since by
Example

2.3

\ind(\mathcal{D},1)=\deg(F)=\deg(f)\neq 0
, we find a non-trivial harmonic spinor
\Psi
satisfying the boundary condition
\clm(\nu_{M})\Psi=\clms(\nu_{\Omega})\Psi
.
By
Corollary

4.5
,
\Psi
is parallel and it satisfies

(5.11)

\clm(\xi)\Psi=\clms(\mathrm{d}{F}(\xi))\Psi\quad\text{on $\partial M$ for any $\xi\in\Ct^{\infty}(\partial M,\mathrm{T}M)$,}

compare
5.10
above and which for the normal component
\xi=\nu_{M}
is just the boundary condition.

Let
\bar{e}_{1},\dotsc,\bar{e}_{n}
denote a constant orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For each
i=1,\dotsc,n
define a vector field
\xi_{i}\in\Vfds(M)
by
\langle\xi_{i},{-}\rangle=\langle\clms(\bar{e}_{i})\Psi,\clm({-})\Psi\rangle
.
Since
\Psi
and
\bar{e}_{i}
are parallel, the vector field
\xi_{i}
is also parallel.
Using
5.11
on
\partial M
we have

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{i},\xi\rangle=\langle\clms(\bar{e}_{i})\Psi,\clms(\mathrm{d}{F}(\xi))\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\clms(\bar{e}_{i})\clms(\mathrm{d}{F}(\xi))\Psi,\Psi\rangle+2\langle\bar{e}_{i},\mathrm{d}{F}(\xi)\rangle\lvert\Psi\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=-\langle\clms(\bar{e}_{i})\clm(\xi)\Psi,\Psi\rangle+2\langle\bar{e}_{i},\mathrm{d}{F}(\xi)\rangle\lvert\Psi\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=-\langle\xi_{i},\xi\rangle+2\langle\bar{e}_{i},\mathrm{d}{F}(\xi)\rangle\lvert\Psi\rvert^{2},

where we have used the Clifford relation
\clms(v)\clms(w)+\clms(w)\clms(v)=2\langle v,w\rangle
.
Thus

(5.12)

\langle\xi_{i},\xi\rangle=\langle\bar{e}_{i},\mathrm{d}{F}(\xi)\rangle\lvert\Psi\rvert^{2}\quad\text{on $\partial M$ for any $\xi\in\Ct^{\infty}(\partial M,\mathrm{T}M)$.}

In particular, assuming the parallel spinor
\Psi
is normalized such that
|\Psi|=1
, then
5.12
implies that
\mathrm{d}{F}(\xi_{i})=\bar{e}_{i}
on
\partial M
.
Since
\mathrm{d}{F}
is an isometry at the boundary, this means that
\xi_{1},\dotsc,\xi_{n}
is an orthonormal frame of
\mathrm{T}M
along
\partial M
.
But the vector fields
\xi_{i}
are parallel, so they form a parallel orthonormal frame of
\mathrm{T}M
on all of
M
. This means that the metric on
M
is flat.

Using that
\II_{\partial M}=f^{\ast}\II_{\partial\Omega}
, we glue
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
to
M
, obtaining an open manifold equipped with a complete flat
\Ct^{2}
-metric (see e.g.
[
37
, Lemma 4.1]
).
By the classification theorem for flat manifolds, we deduce that
M
is a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Using again that
\II_{\partial M}=f^{\ast}\II_{\partial\Omega}
, we deduce that
M
and
\Omega
are related via a Euclidean motion by the fundamental theorem of hypersurfaces.
∎

1.6
.

.

and

.

Equivalently, we show that, if

By

6.
Applications to asymptotically flat manifolds

Witten
[
43
]
showed that, on an asymptotically flat spin manifold
M^{n}
of non-negative scalar curvature, the following identity holds:

(6.1)

\displaystyle m=\frac{1}{2(n-1)\omega_{n-1}}\int_{M}(4|\nabla\psi|^{2}+\scal|\psi|^{2})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{V}.

Here,
\psi
is a harmonic spinor asymptotic to a constant spinor with norm
1
at
\infty
.
For the definitions of the mass
m
and asymptotic flatness, we refer to
[
28
]
.
One drawback of this formula is that it needs non-negative scalar curvature since otherwise the existence of
\psi
is not guaranteed.
Having such a mass formula without the
\scal\geq 0
requirement has been for instance exploited in
[
24
, Theorem 1.5]
.
Interestingly,
Proposition

4.4
implies such a Witten-type integral formula without imposing any conditions on the scalar curvature.

Given
k\geq 1
and
s>n-2
, we say that an asymptotically flat manifold
(M^{n},g)
is
\Ct^{k}_{-s}
-asymptotically Schwarzschild of mass
m
if in the asymptotically flat coordinate system

(6.2)

\displaystyle\left(g-\left(1-\frac{2m}{r^{n-2}}\right)^{-1}\mathrm{d}r^{2}-r^{2}g_{\mathrm{S}^{n-1}}\right)\in\Ct^{k}_{-s}.

Moreover, we denote with
M_{r}
the portion of
M
where in the asymptotically flat coordinate system
|x|\leq r
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\delta>0
and let
(M^{n},g)
be a smooth spin manifold which is
\Ct^{1}_{-(n-2)-\delta}
-asymptotically Schwarzschild of mass
m
.
Then for every radius
r\gg 1
there exists a harmonic spinor
\psi_{r}
on
M_{r}
normalized to
\|\psi_{r}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\partial M_{r})}=|\partial M_{r}|
such that

(6.3)

\displaystyle m\geq\frac{1}{2(n-1)\omega_{n-1}}\int_{M_{r}}(4|\nabla\psi_{r}|^{2}+\scal|\psi_{r}|^{2})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{V}+\bigO(r^{-\delta}).

Since manifolds which are asymptotically Schwarzschild are dense within arbitrary asymptotically flat manifolds, the above theorem implies the Riemannian positive mass theorem.
We also remark that the spinors in our proof live in a different bundle compared to the ones in Witten’s argument.

Proof.

Let
r\gg 1
and consider the coordinate sphere
\partial M_{r}
in the asymptotically flat end.
Since
(M^{n},g)
is asymptotically Schwarzschild, we obtain

(6.4)

\displaystyle\mean_{r}=\frac{n-1}{r}\left(1-\frac{m}{r^{n-2}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-(n-2)-\delta})\right)

for the mean curvature
\mean_{r}
of
\partial M_{r}
.
Moreover, the induced metric
g_{r}
on
\partial M_{r}
satisfies

(6.5)

\displaystyle g_{r}=g_{\mathrm{S}^{n-1}_{r}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-(n-2)-\delta}),

where
g_{\mathrm{S}^{n-1}_{r}}=r^{2}g_{\mathrm{S}^{n-1}}
is the round metric on the sphere of radius
r
.
The latter condition implies that the map
f_{r}=\id:\partial M_{r}\to S_{r}
is Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
L_{r}
satisfying

(6.6)

\displaystyle L_{r}=1+\mathcal{\bigO}(r^{-(n-2)-\delta}).

Now as described in Section
5
, we can extend
f_{r}
to a map
F_{r}
from
M_{r}
to the ball
\mathrm{B}_{r}
of radius
r
, and solve on the corresponding twisted spin bundle
S
the Dirac equation
\mathcal{D}\psi_{r}=0
with boundary condition
\clm(\nu_{M_{r}})\psi_{r}=\clms(\nu_{\mathrm{B}_{r}})\psi_{r}
.
Combining equations (
6.4
), (
6.5
) and (
6.6
) with
Proposition

4.4
, which in this situation states

\displaystyle\int_{M_{r}}(4|\nabla\psi_{r}|^{2}+\scal|\psi_{r}|^{2})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{V}\leq 2\int_{\partial M_{r}}\left(L_{r}\frac{n-1}{r}-\mean_{r}\right)|\psi_{r}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S},

the result follows.
∎

We expect that this approach to also work in the initial data set setting, and we refer

for the corresponding Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz formula.
Also, compare this to

Appendix A
The index theorem

In this appendix, we exhibit a quick cut-and-paste argument to derive the index formula for the boundary value problem studied in
Section

2.3
for the case of maps to Euclidean domains in any dimension parity.
Suppose from now on that we are in the setting of
Section

2.3
.
Given a vector field
\eta\in\Vfds(N)
, we consider the following perturbed Dirac operator

\mathcal{D}_{\eta}=\mathcal{D}+\clms(\eta).

Expanding the term
|\mathcal{D}_{\eta}\psi|^{2}
and integrating by parts the term
\langle\clms(\eta)\psi,\mathcal{D}\psi\rangle
, we obtain the following formula

(A.1)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{\eta}\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}^{2}=\|\mathcal{D}\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{M}\langle(\clms(\eta)\mathcal{D}+\mathcal{D}\clms(\eta))\psi,\psi\rangle+\lvert\eta\rvert^{2}\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{V}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial M}\langle\clm(\nu_{M})\clms(\eta)\psi,\psi\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathrm{S}.

Note that
\clms(\eta)\mathcal{D}+\mathcal{D}\clms(\eta)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\clm(e_{i})\clms(\nabla_{\mathrm{d}{f}(e_{i})}\eta)
by a similar argument as in the proof of
Lemma

4.1
.
Moreover, if
\psi
satisfies the boundary condition
\clm(\nu_{M})\psi=s\clms(\nu_{N})\psi
, then

(A.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\clm(\nu_{M})\clms(\eta)\psi,\psi\rangle=&-\langle\clms(\eta)\clm(\nu_{M})\psi,\psi\rangle\\
=&-s\langle\clms(\eta)\clms(\nu_{N})\psi,\psi\rangle\\
=&s\langle\clms(\nu_{N})\clms(\eta)\psi,\psi\rangle-2s\langle\eta,\nu_{N}\rangle\lvert\psi\rvert^{2}\\
=&-\langle\clm(\nu_{M})\clms(\eta)\psi,\psi\rangle+2s\langle\eta,-\nu_{N}\rangle\lvert\psi\rvert^{2},\end{split}

where we have used the Clifford relation
\clms(v)\clms(w)+\clms(w)\clms(v)=2\langle v,w\rangle
.
Thus we conclude that
\langle\clm(\nu_{M})\clms(\eta)\psi,\psi\rangle=s\langle\eta,-\nu_{N}\rangle|\psi|^{2}
.

We obtain the following lemma:

Lemma A.1
.

Let
s\colon\partial N\to\{\pm 1\}
and suppose there exists a nowhere-vanishing vector field
\eta\in\Vfds(N)
such that
s\langle\eta,-\nu_{N}\rangle\geq 0
on
\partial N
.
Then the operator
\mathcal{D}
subject to the boundary condition
\clm(\nu_{M})\psi=s\clms(\nu_{N})\psi
has vanishing index.

Proof.

We have
\ind(\mathcal{D},{s})=\ind(\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\eta},s)
for any
\lambda\geq 0
by homotopy invariance of the index.
But
A.1
implies that under the boundary condition given by
s
the operator
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda\eta}
is invertible for
\lambda
sufficiently large and hence has vanishing index.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose that we have a decomposition
N=N_{0}\cup_{\Upsilon}N_{1}
and
M=M_{0}\cup_{\Sigma}M_{1}
respecting all structures, where
M_{i}
and
N_{i}
are codimension zero submanifolds with boundary and the gluing happens along the additional boundary components not present in
M
and
N
, denoted by
\Upsilon\subseteq\partial N_{i}
and
\Sigma\subseteq\partial M_{i}
.
We also assume that the map
f\colon M\to N
restricts to
f_{i}\colon(M_{i},\partial M_{i})\to(N_{i},\partial N_{i})
.
Let
s\colon\partial N\to\{\pm 1\}
and extend this to
s_{i}\colon\partial N_{i}\to\{\pm 1\}
such that
s_{0}|_{\Upsilon}=-s_{1}|_{\Upsilon}
.
Then

\ind(\mathcal{D},s)=\ind(\mathcal{D}_{0},s_{0})+\ind(\mathcal{D}_{1},s_{1}).

Proof.

This is a consequence of the splitting theorem of
[
6
, Theorem 6.5]
, compare also
[
15
, Theorem 3.6]
.
∎

Theorem A.3
.

Let
N=\Omega
be a convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
s=1
. Then

\ind(\mathcal{D},1)=\deg(f)

Proof.

We proceed in several steps of successive generality.

(1)

The case where
M=N
is a convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with identical metrics and
f=\id
reduces to
Example

2.1
because
\chi(N)=1
.

(2)

Now assume that
f\colon M\to N
is a diffeomorphism, where
N
is a convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then by homotopy invariance of the index we may assume that
f
is an isometry.
Thus we obtain a bundle isomorphism
\mathrm{T}{M}\oplus f^{\ast}\mathrm{T}{N}\cong\mathrm{T}{M}\oplus\mathrm{T}{M}
which preserves the pseudo-Riemannian metric and connection.
If
f
is also orientation preserving, this is an orientation preserving bundle isomorphism and hence lifts to the associated spinor bundles, in which case we are immediately reduced to
1
because then
\ind(\mathcal{D},1)=1=\deg{f}
.
Otherwise, if
f
is orientation reversing, then the induced isomorphism
\mathrm{T}{M}\oplus f^{\ast}\mathrm{T}{N}\cong\mathrm{T}{M}\oplus\mathrm{T}{M}
is also orientation reversing and the effect on the spinor bundle is a reversal of the
\mathbb{Z}/2
-grading.
Hence
\ind(\mathcal{D},1)=-1=\deg(f)
.

(3)

Next we consider a map
f\colon M\to N
which on each connected component of

restricts to a diffeomorphism onto the convex domain
N
.
Then the desired index formula follows directly from
2
by additivity over connected components.

(4)

Now we are ready to treat the general case.
To this end, let
y_{0}\in N
be an interior point of
N
which is a regular value of
f
.
Choose a smooth vector field
\eta
on
N
which satisfies
\eta|_{\partial N}=-\nu_{N}
and which is equal to the vector field

in a disk neighborhood

around

and such that
\eta
has no zeroes other than

.
By possibly shrinking the disk
N_{0}
further, we can also assume that

is a diffeomorphism on each connected component of

and
M_{0}
has a smooth boundary
\Sigma\coloneqq\partial M_{0}
.
Let
N_{1}
denote the closure of the complement of
N_{0}
and
M_{1}\coloneqq f^{-1}(N_{1})
as well as
\Upsilon\coloneqq\partial N_{0}
.
Let

be defined by setting

,

and

.
Then by construction

and we have

Moreover,

∎

Appendix B
A quantitative matrix Hölder inequality

Let
W,V
be real Euclidean vector spaces and assume for simplicity that
\dim W=\dim V=n<\infty
.
Let
T\colon W\to V
be a linear map.
We can always find a polar decomposition
T=P\circ U
, where
U\colon W\to V
is an isometry and
P\colon V\to V
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator.
The operator
P
is always uniquely determined by
T
and given by the formula
P=\sqrt{T\circ T^{\ast}}
, whereas the isometry
U
is only unique if
T
is invertible.
The eigenvalues
\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\sigma_{n}\geq 0
of
P
are called the
singular values
of the operator
T
.
The
trace norm
of
T
is defined as
|T|_{\tr}=\tr(P)
, or in other words as the sum of the singular values.
The
operator norm
of
T
, denoted by
|T|_{\mathrm{op}}
, is the maximum of its singular values.
More generally, we may also define the
Schatten norms
by
|T|_{p}=\tr(P^{p})^{1/p}
for any
1\leq p<\infty
.
For
p=2
, this is known as the
Hilbert–Schmidt norm
.
Given linear maps between
T\colon W\to V
and
S\colon V\to V
, we have the following
“Hölder inequality”

(B.1)

|S\circ T|_{1}\leq|S|_{p}\ |T|_{q},

whenever
1/p+1/q=1
.

We will only use the case
p=1,q=\infty
and when
S
is a non-negative endomorphism.
In this case we have the following quantitative estimate which we will need in our (almost) rigidity argument:

Lemma B.1
.

Let
V,W
be finite dimensional real Euclidean vector spaces of the same dimension,
T\colon W\to V
a linear map and
S\colon V\to V
a self-adjoint non-negative endomorphism.
Let
\sigma_{\min}=\sigma_{\min}(S)\geq 0
denote the smallest eigenvalue of
S
.
Furthermore, let
P\circ U
be a polar decomposition of
S\circ T
, that is,
P\circ U=S\circ T
with
U\colon W\to V
an isometry and
P\colon V\to V
a self-adjoint non-negative endomorphism.
Then for any
\lambda\geq|T|_{\mathrm{op}}
, we have

\tfrac{\sigma_{\min}}{2}\lvert T-\lambda U\rvert_{2}^{2}\leq\lvert S\rvert_{\tr}\ \lambda^{2}-\lvert S\circ T\rvert_{\tr}\lambda.

In particular, if
T\neq 0
,

\tfrac{\sigma_{\min}}{2|T|_{\mathrm{op}}}\lvert T-|T|_{\mathrm{op}}U\rvert_{2}^{2}\leq\lvert S\rvert_{\tr}\ \lvert T\rvert_{\mathrm{op}}-\lvert S\circ T\rvert_{\tr}.

Proof.

Let
(e_{i})
be an orthonormal basis of
W
. Then

\displaystyle\tfrac{\sigma_{\min}}{2}\lvert T-\lambda U\rvert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\sigma_{\min}}{2}\sum_{i}\lvert T(e_{i})-\lambda U(e_{i})\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\sigma_{\min}}{2}\sum_{i}\lvert T(e_{i})\rvert^{2}+\lambda^{2}\lvert U(e_{i})\rvert^{2}-2\lambda\langle T(e_{i}),U(e_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{\min}\sum_{i}\left(\lambda^{2}-\lambda\langle U^{\ast}T(e_{i}),e_{i}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\min}\tr\left(\lambda^{2}\id_{W}-\lambda U^{\ast}\circ T\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\min}\tr\left(\lambda^{2}\id_{V}-\lambda T\circ U^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\tr\left(\lambda^{2}S-\lambda S\circ T\circ U^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=\tr\left(\lambda^{2}S-\lambda P\right)=\lvert S\rvert_{\tr}\ \lambda^{2}-\lvert S\circ T\rvert_{\tr}\lambda.\qed

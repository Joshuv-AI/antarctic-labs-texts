The blow-down map in Lie algebroid cohomology
Andreas Schüßler

Abstract
We study the blow-down map in cohomology in the context of real projective blowups of Lie algebroids. Using the blow-down map in cohomology we compute the Lie algebroid cohomology of the blowup of transversals of arbitrary codimension, generalising the Mazzeo-Melrose theorem on b-cohomology. To prove the result we develop a Gysin sequence for Lie algebroids. As another example we use the developed tools to compute the Lie algebroid cohomology of the action Lie algebroid 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 ​ ( 3 ) ⋉ ℝ 𝟛 \mathfrak{so}(3)\ltimes\ch@airxmathbbm{R}^{3} , a result known in Poisson geometry literature. Moreover, we use similar techniques to compute the de Rham cohomology of real projective blowups.

Blowup constructions are well-known in algebraic geometry (see e.g.

2.1

For a Lie algebroid

using the first part.
If

\phi^{\sharp}L
is trivial. In a trivialisation
\phi^{\sharp}L=\tilde{M}\times\ch@airxmathbbm{R}
for every

we have that

(\phi^{\sharp})^{-1}(\{v_{p}\})=\{(p^{+},r(v_{p})),(p^{-},-r(v_{p}))\}\subseteq\tilde{M}\times\ch@airxmathbbm{R},

(
\ast
)

where
\{p^{+},p^{-}\}=\phi^{-1}(\{p\})
, and the
\ch@airxmathbbm{Z}_{2}
-action flips the two points. Let
\omega=\eta\mathbin{\otimes}\ell\in\Omega^{k}(A,E\mathbin{\otimes}L)
be given, where
\eta\in\Omega^{k}(A,E)
and
\ell\in\Gamma^{\infty}(L)

Then we can define a form
\tilde{\omega}\in\Omega^{k}(\phi^{!}A,\phi^{\sharp}E)

\tilde{\omega}(\tilde{a}_{1},\dots,\tilde{a}_{k})\colon\tilde{M}\ni p^{\pm}\mapsto\pm r\big(\ell(\phi(p^{\pm}))\big)\eta\big(\phi^{!}(\tilde{a}_{1}(p^{\pm})),\dots,\phi^{!}(\tilde{a}_{k}(p^{\pm}))\big)\in E_{\phi(p^{\pm})}=\phi^{\sharp}E_{p^{\pm}},

where
\tilde{a}_{1},\dots,\tilde{a}_{k}\in\Gamma^{\infty}(\phi^{!}A)

\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M)
-linear extension as a module morphism along
\phi^{\ast}
gives a map
\Omega^{\bullet}(A,E\mathbin{\otimes}L)\to\Omega^{\bullet}(\phi^{!}A,\phi^{\sharp}E)
which maps into the subcomplex of anti-invariant forms.
To show that it is a bijection let
\tilde{\omega}\in\Omega^{k}(\phi^{!}A,\phi^{\sharp}E)=\Omega^{k}(\phi^{!}A,\phi^{\sharp}(E\mathbin{\otimes}L))
be given. For
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\Gamma^{\infty}(A)

\omega(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\colon p\mapsto\phi^{\sharp}\big(\tilde{\omega}_{\tilde{p}}(\tilde{a}_{1}(\tilde{p}),\dots,\tilde{a}_{k}(\tilde{p}))\big),

(2.15)

where
\tilde{a}_{j}(\tilde{p})

\phi^{!}(\tilde{a}_{j}(\tilde{p}))=a_{j}(p)

\ch@airxmathbbm{Z}_{2}

and defines a smooth section since

Finally, we note that the (anti-) invariant part of the cohomology is the cohomology of the (anti-) invariant subcomplex.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
A\Rightarrow M
,
\phi\colon\tilde{M}\to M
,

\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\phi^{!}A,\phi^{\sharp}E)_{\pm}=\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\Omega^{\bullet}(\phi^{!}A,\phi^{\sharp}E)_{\pm}).

(2.16)

Proof
:

This is obtained by averaging the
\ch@airxmathbbm{Z}_{2}
-action: For example, to see that the natural map for the invariant eigenspaces is injective let
[\omega]_{+}\in\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\Omega^{\bullet}(\phi^{!}A,\phi^{\sharp}E)_{+})
be given. If
[\omega]=0
there exists

with

3
Blowup of Lie algebroids

The idea of the blowup of a Lie algebroid is the following. Suppose that
(A,B)\Rightarrow(M,N)
is a pair of Lie algebroids. Then blowing up will lead to a new Lie algebroid
\mathrm{Blup}(A,B)\Rightarrow\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)
together with a morphism of Lie algebroids
p_{A}\colon\mathrm{Blup}(A,B)\to A
. This means we get an induced map in cohomology
p_{A}^{\ast}\colon\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(A)\to\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Blup}(A,B))
. Now two scenarios are possible: Either the blown-up Lie algebroid is of interest and we would like to understand its cohomology in relation to
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(A)
, or we are interested in computing
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(A)
. In the second case, one chooses the Lie subalgebroid
B
in such a way that
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Blup}(A,B))
is easier to compute and then draws conclusions regarding
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(A)
via the blow-down map.

3.1
Real projective blowups of smooth manifolds

Throughout this section let
(M,N)
be a pair of manifolds. The blowup
\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)
of
N
in
M
is as a set given by

\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)=(M\setminus N)\cup\ch@airxmathbbm{P},

(3.1)

i.e. by replacing
N
with
\ch@airxmathbbm{P}=\ch@airxmathbbm{P}(\nu)
, the projectivisation of the normal bundle

\nu=\nu(M,N)=TM|_{N}/TN

of
N
in
M
. However, (
3.1
) does as written does not carry an obvious smooth structure. There are several equivalent ways to define the blowup as a manifold.
Since they are all useful to keep in mind we list them here, but define the blowup using a universal property
[
33
, Proposition 5.30]
.

Definition 3.1
.

The
blowup
of
N
in
M
is given by a pair of manifolds
(B,P)
together with a map of pairs
p\colon(B,P)\to(M,N)
such that

1.

P\subseteq B
is a submanifold of codimension
1
,

2.

p^{-1}(N)=P
and the normal derivative
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}p\colon\nu(B,P)\to\nu(M,N)
is fiberwisely injective,

3.

it satisfies the following universal property: If
(X,Y)
is another pair of manifolds and
q\colon(X,Y)\to(M,N)
satisfies the first two conditions, there exists a unique map of pairs
\Phi\colon(X,Y)\to(B,P)
such that
q=p\circ\Phi
.

The map
p\colon B\to P
is called the
blow-down map
.

In accordance to the description in (
3.1
) we write
\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)=B
and
\ch@airxmathbbm{P}=P
for the codimension
1
submanifold.
From Definition
3.1
we see that if
\operatorname{\mathrm{codim}}N=1
then
\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)=M
are isomorphic via the blow-down map, while it hides that the blow-down map is proper (see
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
and
[
33
, Proposition 5.34]
for a proof), and that the restriction

p\big|_{\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)\setminus\ch@airxmathbbm{P}}\colon\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)\setminus\ch@airxmathbbm{P}\to M\setminus N

(3.2)

is a diffeomorphism.
One way to construct the blowup is via the
deformation to the normal cone

[
8
, Section 4]
, which also gives insights into the functorial properties of the blowup. As a set the deformation to the normal cone is given by

\mathrm{DNC}(M,N)=M\times(\ch@airxmathbbm{R}\setminus\{0\})\cup(\nu\times\{0\})

(3.3)

and is endowed with a smooth structure that magnifies normal directions for
\ch@airxmathbbm{R}\ni t\to 0
, which can be understood in terms of smoothness of a
\ch@airxmathbbm{R}^{\times}
-action given by

\lambda.(v,t)=\begin{cases}(v,\lambda^{-1}t)&\text{ if }t\neq 0\\
(\lambda v,0)&\text{ if }t=0.\end{cases}

(3.4)

This action is proper and free on
\mathrm{DNC}(M,N)\setminus(N\times\ch@airxmathbbm{R})
. Here we consider
N\times\{0\}
to sit inside the normal bundle
\nu
as the zero section. The blowup of
N
in
M
is then given by the quotient

\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)=\frac{\mathrm{DNC}(M,N)\setminus(N\times\ch@airxmathbbm{R})}{\ch@airxmathbbm{R}^{\times}}=M\setminus N\cup\ch@airxmathbbm{P}(\nu)

(3.5)

and the blow-down map on the projectivisation of the normal bundle is just the fibre projection.

Remark 3.2
.

Even though the deformation to the normal cone constitutes a functor from the categories of pairs of manifolds to the category of manifolds, the same is not quite true for blowups, see
[
8
, Definition 4.8]
. If
f\colon(M,N)\to(X,Y)
is a map of pairs of manifolds, we can only define a blowup of
f
on

\mathrm{Blup}(f)\colon\mathrm{Blup}_{f}(M,N):=\frac{\mathrm{DNC}(M,N)\setminus\mathrm{DNC}(f)^{-1}(Y\times\ch@airxmathbbm{R})}{\ch@airxmathbbm{R}^{\times}}\to\mathrm{Blup}(X,Y).

(3.6)

In particular,
\mathrm{Blup}(f)
is defined on all of
\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)
iff
f^{-1}(Y)=N
and the normal derivative of
f
is fiberwisely injective.

For local computations charts describing the smooth structure of the blowup are most useful
[
33
, Remark 5.29]
.

Remark 3.3
(Charts for
\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)
).

Let
(M,N)
be a pair of manifolds and let
(U,(x,y))
be a submanifold chart, i.e.
U\cap N=\{x=0\}
, where for simplicity we assume
(x,y)(U)=\ch@airxmathbbm{R}^{n}
. Then the collection
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,k}
defined by

U_{i}=p^{-1}(\{x_{i}\neq 0\})\cup\{[v]_{q}\in\ch@airxmathbbm{P}_{U\cap N}\colon\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}x_{i}(v)\neq 0\}

(3.7)

yields an open cover of
p^{-1}(U)
and for
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
the maps

\displaystyle\Phi_{i}\colon U_{i}

\displaystyle\to\ch@airxmathbbm{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}(y(p(\xi)),\tfrac{x_{1}(p(\xi))}{x_{i}(p(\xi))},\dots,\tfrac{x_{i-1}(p(\xi))}{x_{i}(p(\xi))},x_{i}(p(\xi)),\tfrac{x_{i+1}(p(\xi))}{x_{i}(p(\xi))},\dots\tfrac{x_{k}(p(\xi))}{x_{i}(p(\xi))})&\text{ for }y_{i}(p(\xi))\neq 0\\
(y(p(\xi)),\tfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}x_{1}(v)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}x_{i}(v)},\dots,\tfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}x_{i-1}(v)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}x_{i}(v)},0,\tfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}x_{i+1}(v)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}x_{i}(v)},\dots\tfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}x_{k}(v)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}x_{i}(v)})&\text{ for }\xi=[v]_{p(\xi)}\end{cases}

yield charts for
\mathrm{Blup}(U,U\cap N)\subseteq\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)
.

The final point of view we want to take is locally around the submanifold
N
in a tubular neighbourhood. Using gluing, this is enough to define the blowup even globally, see e.g.
[
27
, Section 2]
. Moreover, it implies that every tubular neighbourhood of
N
in
M
induces a tubular neighbourhood of
\ch@airxmathbbm{P}
in
\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)
, see Corollary
3.6
.

Remark 3.4
.

Let
E\to N
be a vector bundle and view
N
as a subset of
E
via the image of the zero section. Then

\mathrm{Blup}(E,N)\simeq\ch@airxmathbbm{L}(E)=\ch@airxmathbbm{L},

(3.8)

where
\ch@airxmathbbm{L}(E)
is the tautological line bundle over the projectivisation of
E
, i.e. it is the line bundle over
\ch@airxmathbbm{P}
with fibres given by

\ch@airxmathbbm{L}_{[v]}=\{v^{\prime}\in E\colon v^{\prime}=\lambda v\text{ for some }\lambda\in\ch@airxmathbbm{R}\}

(3.9)

for
v\in E\setminus N
. In this case, the blow-down map is given by
v^{\prime}_{[v]}\mapsto v^{\prime}
.

Thus every tubular neighbourhood of
N
in
M
induces a tubular neighbourhood of
\ch@airxmathbbm{P}
in
\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)
. We show that the respective Euler vector fields are related by the blow-down map. For that, we first note that vector fields on
M
that are tangent to
N
lift to the blowup in a unique way.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
N\subseteq M
be a submanifold,
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and write
p\colon\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)\to M
for the blow-down map. Then there is a vector field
\tilde{X}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathrm{Blup}(M,N))
with
\tilde{X}\sim_{p}X
if and only if
X\in\Gamma^{\infty}(TM,TN)
, i.e.
X
is tangent to
N
. In that case,
\tilde{X}
is unique and tangent to
\ch@airxmathbbm{P}\subseteq\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)
.

Proof
:

First suppose that
\tilde{X}
exists. Note that for
[v]\in\ch@airxmathbbm{P}
we have
\operatorname{\mathrm{im}}(T_{[v]}p)=T_{p([v])}N\oplus\ch@airxmathbbm{R}v
, making use of a tubular neighbourhood. Then

\bigcap_{[v]\in\ch@airxmathbbm{P}_{p}}\operatorname{\mathrm{im}}(T_{[v]}\rho)=T_{p}N,

thus
X\in\Gamma^{\infty}(TM,TN)
follows immediately. Conversely let
X\in\Gamma^{\infty}(TM,TN)
be given. Take an adapted chart
(U,(y,x))
of
N
in
M
with
N\cap U=\{x=0\}
. Then
X
is locally given by

X\big|_{U}=f^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{j}}+g^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}}

for
f^{j},g^{k}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(M)
, where all
g^{k}
vanish on
N
. Fix
i\in\{1,\dots,\operatorname{\mathrm{codim}}N\}
and consider the chart
(U_{i},\Phi_{i}=(\tilde{y},\tilde{x}))
of the blowup adapted to
\ch@airxmathbbm{P}
(given by
\tilde{x}_{i}=0
). Then on
U_{i}\setminus\ch@airxmathbbm{P}
we have

p^{\ast}X=p^{\ast}f^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{y}^{j}}+p^{\ast}g^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{x}^{i}}+\sum_{k\neq i}\frac{1}{\tilde{x}^{i}}(p^{\ast}g^{k}-p^{\ast}g^{i}\tilde{x}^{k})\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{x}^{k}}.

(
\ast
)

Since for all
k\in\{1,\dots,\operatorname{\mathrm{codim}}N\}
we have
g^{k}\big|_{N}=0
,
p^{\ast}g^{k}\big|_{\ch@airxmathbbm{P}}=0
follows. Thus there are functions
\tilde{g}^{k}\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(U_{i})
such that
p^{\ast}g^{k}=\tilde{x}^{i}\tilde{g}^{k}
. Inserting this in
(\ast)
shows that
p^{\ast}X\big|_{U\setminus N}
extends smoothly to a vector field on
U_{i}
, hence
p^{\ast}X\big|_{M\setminus N}
extends smoothly to
\ch@airxmathbbm{P}
.

\ch@irxboxempty

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
E\to N
be a vector bundle with Euler vector field
\xi\in\mathfrak{X}(E)
. Then the lift of
\xi
is the Euler vector field of the line bundle
\ch@airxmathbbm{L}=\mathrm{Blup}(E,N)\to\ch@airxmathbbm{P}
.

Proof
:

Lemma
3.5
implies that
\xi
lifts to the blowup. Since the blow-down map is a vector bundle morphism and a diffeomorphism on
E\setminus N
, we can compare the flows of the vector fields there to see that the lift is indeed the Euler vector field.

3.2
Blowups of Lie algebroids

Starting from a Lie algebroid
A\Rightarrow M
and a Lie subalgebroid
B\Rightarrow N
, the blowup
\mathrm{Blup}(A,B)
according to Definition
3.1
will in general not carry a natural structure of a Lie algebroid. In fact, if
\operatorname{\mathrm{rank}}(B)<\operatorname{\mathrm{rank}}(A)
, by Remark
3.2
already the vector bundle projection
\pi\colon A\to M
of
A
will not lift to the blowup. Instead, one has to consider
\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B)
.

We start by blowing up vector subbundles, then anchored subbundles, and finally Lie subalgebroids. Even though each construction will bring its own universal property, we only state it for the blowup of Lie algebroids.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\pi\colon(A,B)\to(M,N)
be a pair of vector bundles. Then
\mathrm{Blup}(\pi)\colon\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B)\to\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)
is a vector bundle with sections given by the
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathrm{Blup}(M,N))
-span of

\{\mathrm{Blup}(s)\colon\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)\to\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B)\colon s\in\Gamma^{\infty}(A,B)\},

(3.10)

considering
s\in\Gamma^{\infty}(A,B)
as a map of pairs
(M,N)\to(A,B)
.

Proof
:

See
[
33
, Section 5.4.2]
. Note that
\mathrm{Blup}(s)\colon\mathrm{Blup}_{s}(M,N)\to\mathrm{Blup}(A,B)
is actually defined on all of
\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)
and maps into
\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B)
since
s
is a section.

\ch@irxboxempty

From Lemma
3.7
we can immediately write down local frames for the blowup.

Remark 3.8
(Local frames for
\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B)
).

Let
B\to N
be a vector subbundle of corank
k
of
\pi\colon A\to M
. Let
(U,(x,y))
be a submanifold chart of
N
in
M
, i.e.
U\cap N=\{x=0\}
. Moreover, let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k},f_{1},\dots,f_{\operatorname{\mathrm{rank}}B}\}
be a local frame of
A_{U}
adapted to
B
, meaning
\{f_{1}\big|_{N},\dots f_{\operatorname{\mathrm{rank}}B}\big|_{N}\}
is a local frame for
B_{U\cap N}
. Then the collection

\{\mathrm{Blup}(x_{i}e_{1}),\dots,\mathrm{Blup}(x_{i}e_{k}),\mathrm{Blup}(f_{1}),\dots,\mathrm{Blup}(f_{\operatorname{\mathrm{rank}}B})\}

(3.11)

yields a local frame for
\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B)_{U_{i}}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\pi\colon(A,B)\to(M,N)
be a pair of vector bundles and let
p\colon\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)\to M
denote the blow-down map of the base.

1.

As vector bundles,
\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,A_{N})=p^{\sharp}A
. Under this identification we have
p_{A}=p^{\sharp}
.

2.

As vector bundles,
\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi_{p^{\sharp}A}}(p^{\sharp}A,p^{\sharp}B)=\mathrm{Blup}(A,B)
.

Proof
:

Both statements follow from Remark
3.8
using their respective local frames.

\ch@irxboxempty

Given a map of pairs of vector bundles
\Phi\colon(A,B)\to(E,F)
over a base map
\phi\colon(M,N)\to(X,Y)
we obtain an induced map of vector bundles

\mathrm{Blup}(\Phi)\colon\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi_{A}}(A,B)_{\mathrm{Blup}_{\phi}(M,N)}\to\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi_{E}}(E,F).

(3.12)

Next, we can include an anchor map into the construction, i.e. a vector bundle morphism
\rho\colon A\to TM
over the identity.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
(\pi\colon A\to M,\rho)
be an anchored vector bundle and
B\to N
a subbundle with
\rho(B)\subseteq TN
. Then there is an induced anchor on
\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B)
which on blowup sections is given by

\rho_{\mathrm{Blup}}(\mathrm{Blup}(s))=\widetilde{\rho(s)},

(3.13)

where
s\in\Gamma^{\infty}(A,B)
and
\widetilde{\rho(s)}
is the extension from Lemma
3.5
. In particular, the blow-down map is a morphism of anchored vector bundles.

Alternatively, one can blowup the map of pairs
\rho\colon(A,B)\to(TM,TN)
and consider the concatenation

\rho_{\mathrm{Blup}}\colon\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B)\to\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi_{TM}}(TM,TN)\to T\mathrm{Blup}(M,N),

(3.14)

where the second map is given by the identity in fibres over
M\setminus N
and by the differential of the projection
\nu(M,N)\setminus 0\to\ch@airxmathbbm{P}(M,N)
over the projective bundle
[
33
, Proposition 5.58]
. Lastly, the universal property of the blowup of Lie algebroids is the following.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
(A,B)\Rightarrow(M,N)
be a pair of Lie algebroids. Then the
blowup of
B
in
A
is a pair of Lie algebroids
(\tilde{A},\tilde{B})
together with a morphism
p_{A}\colon(\tilde{A},\tilde{B})\to(A,B)
of pairs of Lie algebroids such that

1.

\tilde{B}
is a full rank Lie subalgebroid over a base of codimension
1
,

2.

p_{A}^{-1}(B)=\tilde{B}
and the normal derivative
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}p_{A}\colon\nu(\tilde{A},\tilde{B})\to\nu(A,B)
is fiberwisely injective,

3.

it satisfies the following universal property: If
(E,F)
is another pair of Lie algebroids and
\phi\colon(E,F)\to(A,B)
a morphism of pairs of Lie algebroids such that the first two conditions are satisfied, there exists a unique morphism of pairs of Lie algebroids
\Phi\colon(E,F)\to(\tilde{A},\tilde{B})
such that
\phi=p_{A}\circ\Phi
.

One can obtain an explicit model for the blowup of Lie algebroids by equipping
\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B)
with the anchor from Lemma
3.10
and bracket on blowup sections given by

[\mathrm{Blup}(a),\mathrm{Blup}(b)]=\mathrm{Blup}([a,b])

(3.15)

for
a,b\in\Gamma^{\infty}(A,B)
. Together with the Leibniz rule, (
3.15
) defines the bracket uniquely as these sections generate
\Gamma^{\infty}(\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B))
. Then the blow-down map
p_{A}\colon\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B)\to A
becomes a Lie algebroid morphism over
p\colon\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)\to M
. Proving that this construction satisfies the universal property is analog to the case of blowups of manifolds, for which it is given in
[
33
, Proposition 5.30]
.

Example 3.12
(Elementary modifications).

Elementary modifications
[
22
,
14
,
23
]
can be expressed in terms of blowups. If
N\subseteq M
has
\operatorname{\mathrm{codim}}(N)=1
, the blow-down map is a diffeomorphism. In particular, when blowing up the base manifold does not change, and in this case the sections of the Lie algebroid blowup are precisely given by

\Gamma^{\infty}(\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B))=\Gamma^{\infty}(A,B)

(3.16)

by Lemma
3.7
. Examples of these modifications are

1.

the log-tangent bundle
T^{b}_{N}M=\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi_{TM}}(TM,TN)
,

2.

the scattering tangent bundle
\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi_{T^{b}_{N}M}}(T^{b}_{N}M,0_{N})
.

4
The blow-down map in cohomology

Since for a pair of Lie algebroids
(A,B)\Rightarrow(M,N)
the only interesting blowup to consider is
\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B)
, we drop the additional subscript
\pi
from now on.

The blow-down map
p_{A}\colon\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B)\to\mathrm{Blup}(M,N)
is a morphism of Lie algebroids, hence induces a map in Lie algebroid cohomology

p_{A}^{\ast}\colon\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(A)\to\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Blup}_{\pi}(A,B)).

(4.1)

In this section we study the blow-down map in cohomology by first considering the pullback by the blow-down map

p_{A}^{\ast}\colon\Omega^{\bullet}(A)\to\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Blup}(A,B))

(4.2)

on the level of forms. For a pair of Lie algebroids
(A,B)\Rightarrow(M,N)
the pullback (
4.2
) will not be surjective unless both
N\subseteq M
is of codimension
1
and
B=A_{N}
. However, if we restrict to forms that are flat along
N
and
\ch@airxmathbbm{P}=p^{-1}(N)
, respectively,
p_{A}^{\ast}
becomes an isomorphism. Here we mean flatness in the following sense:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
E\to M
be a vector bundle and
N\subseteq M
a submanifold. Let
\mathcal{I}_{N}
denote the vanishing ideal of
N
.

1.

The space of
flat sections along

5
Blowups of transversals

One class of Lie subalgebroids of

Appendix A
A Gysin sequence for Lie algebroids

We develope a Gysin sequence for Lie algebroids, which we made use of in Section
5
. To be able to formulate the result we first introduce a notion of fibre integration for Lie algebroids.

A.1
Integration along fibres

Throughout this section let
\mathrm{pr}\colon F\to N
denote a locally trivial fibre bundle with orientable and connected fibres and

a Lie algebroid. We aim to define a notion of integrating along fibres

(\pi^{!})_{\ast}\colon\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cv}}(\pi^{!}B)\to\mathrm{H}^{\bullet-\operatorname{\mathrm{rank}}F}(B,o(F)),

(A.1)

where
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cv}}(\pi^{!}B)
denotes compact vertical cohomology, see Definition
5.3
.
Here,
o(F)\to N
denotes the orientation bundle of
F
. This is standard for vector bundles, but can also be made sense of in our more general situation. Since the fibres are orientable we can construct a double cover
\tilde{N}
of
N
by

\tilde{N}_{p}=\{\lambda\colon\lambda\text{ is an orientation on the manifold }F_{p}\}

(A.2)

\tilde{N}\times\ch@airxmathbbm{R}\to\tilde{N}

\ch@airxmathbbm{Z}_{2}

(-1).(\lambda,t)=(\bar{\lambda},-t),

(A.3)

where
\bar{\lambda}
denotes the opposite orientation. Then

o(F)=\tilde{N}\times\ch@airxmathbbm{R}/\ch@airxmathbbm{Z}_{2}\to N.

(A.4)

The bundle (
A.4

F\to N

Remark A.1

If

is not the trivial bundle, pulling back

to

will result in an orientable fibre bundle.

The orientation bundle carries a canonical flat connection of

(induced by the trivial

-connection on

[
5

in the case of vector bundles, and the representation of

To define fibre integration we first consider the case

\pi^{!}B=\mathcal{F}(\pi)
is the Lie algebroid corresponding to the foliation of

into the fibres of
\pi

Definition A.2

Let

be a locally trivial fibre bundle of rank

\int_{\mathcal{F}(\pi)}\omega=\begin{cases}\Big(N\ni p\mapsto\int_{F_{p}}\iota_{p}^{\ast}\omega\Big)\in\Gamma^{\infty}(o(F))&\text{ if }\omega\in\Omega^{k}_{\mathrm{cv}}(\mathcal{F}(\pi))\\

(A.5)

Remark A.3

Fibre integration is well defined and coincides with the standard notion of fibre integration in the following sense: Since

Proposition A.4
.

Fibre integration descends to a map

\int_{\mathcal{F}(\pi)}\colon\mathrm{H}^{k}_{\mathrm{cv}}(\mathcal{F}(\pi))\to\Gamma^{\infty}(o(F)).

(A.6)

Proof
:

We only need to check that integration vanishes on exact forms. But, since we have for foliated forms

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{F_{p}}\iota^{\ast}_{p}\omega=\iota^{\ast}_{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{F}}\omega,

\ch@irxboxempty

Recall that in case of an orientable vector bundle

With the fibre integration on cohomology defined, we get a Thom isomorphism for Lie algebroids as a consequence of Lemma

Lemma A.9
(Thom isomorphism for Lie algebroids).

Let
\pi\colon E\to N
be a vector bundle of rank
k
with orientation bundle
o(E)
and
B\Rightarrow N
a Lie algebroid. Then fibre integration

(\pi^{!})_{\ast}\colon\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{cv}}^{\bullet}(\pi^{!}B)\to\mathrm{H}^{\bullet-k}(B,o(E))

(A.18)

is an isomorphism. If
\theta\in\mathrm{H}^{k}_{\mathrm{cv}}(E,\pi^{\sharp}o(E)^{\ast})
denotes the Thom class of the vector bundle, the inverse of
(\pi^{!})_{\ast}
is given by

\mathrm{H}^{\bullet-k}(B,o(E))\ni\omega\mapsto\rho_{\pi^{!}B}^{\ast}\theta\wedge(\pi^{!})^{\ast}\omega\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{cv}}^{\bullet}(\pi^{!}B).

(A.19)

Proof
:

The statement follows from a spectral sequence argument. Consider the filtration on
\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cv}}(\pi^{!}B)
given by

\mathcal{F}^{p}\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cv}}(\pi^{!}B)=(\Omega_{\mathrm{cv}}(\mathcal{F}(\pi))^{\wedge p}\wedge\Omega_{\mathrm{cv}}(\pi^{!}B))^{\bullet}

And the induced spectral sequence
\{E_{r}^{p,q}\}_{r\geq 0}
. By standard arguments we obtain

E_{1}^{p,q}\simeq\mathrm{H}^{q}(B,\mathrm{H}^{p}_{\mathrm{cv}}(\mathcal{F}(\pi))).

Then by Lemma
A.5
the statement follows.

A.2
The Gysin sequence

We prove a Gysin-like sequence, which we have used in Section
5
, for the cohomology of
\pi^{!}B
, where
B\Rightarrow N
is a Lie algebroid and
\pi\colon\ch@airxmathbbm{S}\to N
is a sphere bundle of rank
k
, i.e. a locally trivial fibre bundle with a
k
-dimensional sphere as typical fibre. In this case, by the Serre spectral sequence for fibre bundles
[
28
, Theorem 5.7]
we obtain a spectral sequence converging to the Lie algebroid cohomology of
\pi^{!}B
with second page given by

E_{2}^{p,q}=\mathrm{H}^{p}(B,\mathcal{H}^{q}(\ch@airxmathbbm{S})).

(A.20)

Here,
\mathcal{H}^{q}(\ch@airxmathbbm{S})\to N
denotes a smooth vector bundle with fibres given by

\mathcal{H}^{q}(\ch@airxmathbbm{S})_{x}=\mathrm{H}^{q}(\pi^{-1}(x))
for
x\in N
.
Thus,
E_{2}^{p,q}
has nontrivial entries only if
q=0
or
q=k
.
Therefore the next (and last) nontrivial differential is
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{k+1}
. Recall that in case of
B=TN
and trivial orientation bundle it is given by
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{k+1}=\wedge e
, where
e\in\mathrm{H}^{k+1}(N)
is the Euler class of the sphere bundle
[
5
, Chapter 11]
. If
o(\ch@airxmathbbm{S})
is nontrivial, we can consider the pullback to a trivialising double cover
\tilde{N}\to N
and find that
e\in\mathrm{H}^{k+1}(\tilde{N})_{-}=\mathrm{H}^{k+1}(N,o(\ch@airxmathbbm{S}))
instead.

Moduli spaces of residueless meromorphic differentials and the KP hierarchy
Alexandr Buryak, Paolo Rossi, Dimitri Zvonkine

Abstract
. We prove that the cohomology classes of the moduli spaces of residueless meromorphic differentials, i.e., the closures, in the moduli space of stable curves, of the loci of smooth curves whose marked points are the zeros and poles of prescribed orders of a meromorphic differential with vanishing residues, form a partial cohomological field theory (CohFT) of infinite rank. To this partial CohFT we apply the double ramification hierarchy construction to produce a Hamiltonian system of evolutionary PDEs. We prove that its reduction to the case of differentials with exactly two zeros and any number of poles coincides with the KP hierarchy up to a change of variables.



Introduction

In recent years several constructions of moduli spaces of meromorphic differentials on smooth Riemann surfaces, where both the differential and the curve are allowed to vary, have appeared in the literature. In particular, in
[
BCGGM18
,
BCGGM19
,
Sau19
]
the authors constructed, with different techniques, smooth Deligne–Mumford moduli stacks parameterizing families of stable curves of genus
g
and with
n
markings, together with a meromorphic differential with poles and zeros of prescribed orders
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=2g-2
, on their
n
marked points and studied their geometry and topology. Such families have a natural univocal definition as long as the underlying curve is smooth, in which case their moduli stack, up to projectivization with respect to the multiplicative
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on the differential, can be seen as a substack
\mathcal{H}_{g}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
inside
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. The above constructions provide different compactifications and all possess natural forgetful maps to the moduli space of stable curves
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
with respect to which their image is simply the closure
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, which is pure dimensional, but is not in general irreducible. This is in contrast, for instance, with
[
FP18
]
where the authors construct a closed pure dimensional substack
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
of
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
as a proper moduli space of twisted canonical divisors containing
\mathcal{H}_{g}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
as an open subset, but having in general irreducible components that do not lie in
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. In the strictly meromorphic case, where there exists an
a_{i}<0
, the moduli space
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
carries a natural weighted fundamental class
\mathsf{H}_{g}(a_{1}\ldots,a_{n})
which was shown in
[
BHPSS20
]
to equal Pixton’s
1
-twisted double ramification (DR) cycle
\mathrm{DR}_{g}^{1}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, defined in
[
JPPZ17
]
as an explicit sum over stable graphs of tautological classes.

While Pixton’s formula is expected to provide the weighted fundamental classes
\mathsf{H}_{g}(a_{1}\ldots,a_{n})
with the structure of an infinite rank partial cohomological field theory (CohFT), as already proven for the (untwisted) DR cycle in
[
BR21c
]
(see also
[
BR21b
]
), we cannot expect the same from the fundamental classes of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, simply for dimensional reasons. The situation however improves if we demand that all residues of the meromorphic differentials vanish. The corresponding moduli stacks and compactifications were constructed in
[
Sau19
,
CMZ20
]
and the corresponding substack of
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
is denoted by
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g}^{\res}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.

Our first result is that the fundamental classes of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g}^{\res}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
(with
a_{i}\neq-1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
) do indeed form an infinte rank partial CohFT. We show this in Section
1
, after introducing the necessary geometric notions and results from the aforementioned papers.

At this point the possibility of employing integrable systems techniques to study the intersection theory of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g}^{\res}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
arises. In Section
2
we define the corresponding DR hierarchy and prove some of its properties, including homogeneity with respect to the appropriate grading.

Finally, our main result is found in Section
3
, where we prove that a reduction of the DR hierarchy corresponding to moduli spaces of meromorphic differentials with exactly two zeros and any number of poles with no residues coincides with the celebrated Kadomtsev–Petviashvili (KP) hierarchy up to a Miura transformation.

The precise indentification of the aforementioned reduction of the DR hierarchy for residueless meromorphic differentials with the KP hierarchy constructed via Lax operators is achieved thanks to a reconstruction theorem, also proved in Section
3
of independent interest: the KP hierarchy can be uniquely reconstructed, using the properties of commutativity of the flows, homogeneity, tau-symmetry and compatibility with spatial translations, from exactly three coefficients in each component of the first nontrivial flow together with the linear terms in the dispersionless limit of all other flows.

Natural future developments include the identification of the full DR hierarchy for the spaces of residueless meromorphic differentials and the investigation of the Dubrovin–Zhang
[
DZ01
]
side of the correspondence of this partial cohomological field theory with integrable systems, guided by the DR/DZ equivalence conjecture
[
Bur15
,
BDGR18
]
which predicts that the KP hierarchy and its parent hierarchy for differentials with any number of zeros should compute all intersection numbers of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g}^{\res}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
with any monomial in the psi classes. This is material for a future work.

In the

\mathrm{gl}\colon{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g_{1},n_{1}+1}\times{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g_{2},n_{2}+1}\to{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g_{1}+g_{2},n_{1}+n_{2}}
is the corresponding gluing map and where

is defined by
\eta^{\alpha\mu}\eta_{\mu\beta}=\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}
for
\alpha,\beta\in A
.

Definition 1.6
.

A
CohFT
is a partial CohFT

such that the following extra axiom is satisfied:

(iv)

\mathrm{gl}^{*}c_{g+1,n}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}})=c_{g,n+2}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes e_{\mu}\otimes e_{\nu})\eta^{\mu\nu}
, where
\mathrm{gl}\colon{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n+2}\to{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g+1,n}
is the gluing map, which increases the genus by identifying the last two marked points.

Definition 1.7
.

A partial CohFT
c_{g,n}\colon V^{\otimes n}\to H^{\mathrm{even}}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
is called
homogeneous
if

is a graded vector space with a homogeneous basis
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
, with
q_{\alpha}:=\deg e_{\alpha}
, the metric
\eta
on
V
, seen as the map

, is homogeneous with
\delta:=-\deg\eta
,
\deg e_{1\!\!1}=0
and complex constants

for
\alpha\in A
and
\gamma
exist such that the following condition is satisfied:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Deg c_{g,n}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}})+\pi_{*}c_{g,n+1}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes r^{\alpha}e_{\alpha})=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{\alpha_{i}}+\gamma g-\delta\right)c_{g,n}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}}),

where
\Deg\colon H^{*}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})\to H^{*}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
is the operator that acts on
H^{i}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
by multiplication by
\frac{i}{2}
and
\pi\colon{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n+1}\to{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
forgets the last marked point. The constant
\gamma

is called the

of our partial CohFT.

When a homogeneous partial CohFT is a CohFT, the loop axiom enforces the condition

.

As remarked in
[
BR21b
, Section 3]
, a sufficient condition for the definition of a partial CohFT to make sense when

is countably generated, say
V:=\mathrm{span}\left(\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}}\right)
, i.e.

in the above definition, is that the set
\{\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}|c_{g,n}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}})\neq 0\}
is finite for every
g,n
in the stable range and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and that
\eta_{\alpha\beta}
has a unique two-sided inverse

.

Let us introduce the notation
\mathbb{Z}^{\star}:=\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{-1\}
.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
V:=\mathrm{span}\left(\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{\star}}\right)
and let
\eta
be the nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on

given by
\eta_{\alpha\beta}=\eta(e_{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}):=\delta_{\alpha+\beta,-2}
. For
g,n\geq 0
and

, the classes

, with

(1.5)

c_{g,n}(e_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{\alpha_{n}}):=[\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g}^{\res}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})]\in H^{2(g-1+N_{\alpha_{[n]}})}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}),\qquad\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\star},

form an infinite rank homogeneous partial CohFT with unit
e_{0}
, metric
\eta
, and, with notations as in Definition
1.7
,
q_{\alpha}=0
if
\alpha\geq 0
and
q_{\alpha}=1
if
\alpha\leq-2
,

for all

Proof.

First note that for fixed
g
,
n
in the stable range and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
the set
\{\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\star}|c_{g,n}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}})\neq 0\}
is indeed finite (actually composed of one element) thanks to the fact that
[\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g}^{\res}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})]=0
unless
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}=2g-2
. Further,
\eta_{\alpha\beta}=\delta_{\alpha+\beta,-2}
has a unique two-sided inverse, namely
\eta^{\alpha\beta}=\delta_{\alpha+\beta,-2}
.

S_{n}
-equivariance of the linear maps
c_{g,n}
is clear from the definition.

On the marked curve
(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1};0,\infty,1)
a (unique up to a multiplicative constant) meromorphic differential whose divisor is
\alpha[0]+\beta[\infty]+0[1]
exists if
\beta=-\alpha-2
and is given by
\omega=z^{\alpha}dz
, which shows that
c_{0,3}(e_{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\otimes e_{0})=\delta_{\alpha+\beta,-2}
. Let us compute
c_{g,n+1}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes e_{0})
when
2g-2+n>0
. Consider the lift
\widetilde{\pi}\colon\overline{B}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},0)\to\overline{B}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
of
\pi\colon{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n+1}\to{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
through
p\colon\overline{B}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\to{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
. Since
\overline{B}_{g}^{\res}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},0)
is the moduli stack of projectivized multiscale differentials where the last marked point is unconstrained (neither a zero nor a pole), we have that
\widetilde{\pi}
is faithfully flat. Consider then the fiber product
X
of
\overline{B}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
and
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n+1}
over
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
, denoting the two projections by
a
and
b
, respectively. Since
\pi
is faithfully flat and
p
is proper, then
a
is faithfully flat and
b
is proper and we have
\pi^{*}p_{*}=b_{*}a^{*}
in the Chow group. Moreover the maps
\widetilde{\pi}
and
p
induce a proper birational morphism
f\colon\overline{B}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},0)\to X
with
p=bf
and
\widetilde{\pi}=af
. Now, always working in the Chow group, we have
\widetilde{\pi}^{*}[\overline{B}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})]=[\overline{B}_{g}^{\res}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},0)]
and
a^{*}[\overline{B}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})]=[X]
by faithful flatness of
\widetilde{\pi}
and
a
, while
f_{*}[\overline{B}_{g}^{\res}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},0)]=[X]
by birationality of

. Then we conclude that
c_{g,n+1}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes e_{0})=p_{*}[\overline{B}_{g}^{\res}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},0)]=p_{*}\widetilde{\pi}^{*}[\overline{B}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})]=b_{*}f_{*}\widetilde{\pi}^{*}[\overline{B}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})]=b_{*}a^{*}[\overline{B}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})]=\pi^{*}p_{*}[\overline{B}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})]=\pi^{*}c_{g,n}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}})
in Chow and hence in cohomology.

Next, we are interested in
\sigma^{*}c_{g,n}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}})
where
\sigma\colon{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g_{1},|I|+1}\times{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g_{2},|J|+1}\to{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
is the natural boundary map with
g_{1}+g_{2}=g
and

. The preimage
p^{-1}\left(\sigma\left({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g_{1},|I|+1}\times{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g_{2},|J|+1}\right)\right)
is a normal crossing divisor of
\overline{B}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
, which is the union of strata of the form
D_{\Gamma}
with
\Gamma
being either a one level connected graph with two vertices and one horizontal edge, a two level connected graph with one vertex per level, one vertical edge and no horizontal edges, or a two level connected graph with at least two vertices on at least one of the levels and no horizontal edges.

In the first case
D_{\Gamma}
is actually empty: horizontal nodes correspond to simple poles and these are forbidden by the residue theorem, since all other poles are at marked points, where residues are set to zero.

In the third case the stratum
D_{\Gamma}
projects to a stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
of codimension at least
2
because the fibers of

are of dimension at least
1
(given a multiscale differential whose underlying level graph has at least two vertices on the same level not connected by horizontal nodes, one can always rescale the meromorphic differential on one vertex relative to the ones on vertices of the same level without changing the underlying stable curve).

In the second case, notice that if
D_{\Gamma}\neq\emptyset
, then for the only edge
e\in E(\Gamma)
identifying the two points
q^{-}\in C_{(-1)}
and
q^{+}\in C_{(0)}
, we have
\kappa_{e}=|2g_{1}-1-\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}|=|2g_{2}-1-\sum_{j\in J}a_{j}|\neq 0
and
\res_{q^{-}}\omega_{v(q^{-})}=0
, and moreover
2g_{1}-1-\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}
is positive if and only if the vertex of
\Gamma
of level
0
is incident to the legs marked by
I
. Since
T_{\Gamma}=\mathbb{C}^{*}
in this case, this shows that there is a morphism
\widetilde{\sigma}\colon\overline{B}^{\res}_{g_{1}}(\alpha_{I},2g_{1}-2-\sum_{i\in I}a_{i})\times\overline{B}^{\res}_{g_{2}}(\alpha_{J},2g_{2}-2-\sum_{j\in J}a_{j})\to\overline{B}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
lifting
\sigma
, which is an isomorphism onto its image

, and therefore
\sigma^{-1}\left(\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\right)=\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\res}_{g_{1}}(\alpha_{I},2g_{1}-2-\sum_{i\in I}a_{i})\times\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\res}_{g_{2}}(\alpha_{J},2g_{2}-2-\sum_{j\in J}a_{j})
.

The above considerations show that, denoting

, we have

\sigma^{*}c_{g,n}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $\kappa=0$},\\

and the fact that

in the second case is equivalent to the fact that the intersection of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\res}_{g}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
with the image of
\sigma
along
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g_{1}}^{\res}(\alpha_{I},\kappa-1)\times\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\res}_{g_{2}}(\alpha_{J},-\kappa-1)
is generically transversal.

Denote by
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
the smooth parts of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\res}_{g_{1}}(\alpha_{I},\kappa-1)
and
\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\res}_{g_{2}}(\alpha_{J},-\kappa-1)
, respectively. Denote also
S:=\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g}^{\res}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
for brevity. Let us show that the intersection of
S
with the image of
\sigma
is transversal along
S_{1}\times S_{2}
.

Pick points
p_{1}\in S_{1}
and
p_{2}\in S_{2}
. Denote by
p\in{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
the point
\sigma(p_{1},p_{2})
. By the smoothness of stratum
S_{1}
, we can choose local coordinates
U_{1}\times V_{1}
on

p_{1}
such that

. We choose local coordinates

in the neighborhood of
p_{2}
in
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g_{2},|J|+1}

\Delta\subset\mathbb{C}
the unit disc. We claim that we can choose local coordinates

on
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
in the neighborhood of
p
such that the stratum
S
is

and the image of
\sigma
is
U_{1}\times V_{1}\times U_{2}\times V_{2}\times\{0\}
. The transversality of the intersection is then obvious. So let us describe the choice of local coordinates.

Every curve
C_{1}
in
U_{1}\times\{0\}
carries a residueless meromorphic differential. It is unique up to a multiplicative constant. Choose this constant in some way over
U_{1}
and denote the meromorphic differential by
\alpha
. Similarly, denote by
\beta
the meromorphic differential on a curve

of

on

and

,

and
U_{2}
. We extend the local coordinates

and

\sigma^{*}c_{g,n}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{\star}}c_{g_{1},|I|+1}(\otimes_{i\in I}e_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes e_{\alpha})c_{g_{2},|J|+1}(\otimes_{j\in J}e_{\alpha_{j}}\otimes e_{-\alpha-2})

Finally, from formula (

2.
The DR hierarchy for the cycle of residueless meromorphic differentials

Here we briefly review the notion of double ramification (DR) hierarchy for a partial CohFT and then apply this construction to the partial CohFT formed by the cycles of residueless meromorphic differentials.

In
[
Bur15
]
, the first named author introduced a construction associating an integrable Hamiltonian system of evolutionary PDEs to a given CohFT. In
[
BDGR18
]
it was proved that the same construction also works for partial CohFTs and, in
[
BR21b
]
, the first example of DR hierarchy associated to an infinite rank partial CohFT was computed. Finally, in
[
BR21a
,
ABLR21
]
, the construction was generalized to associate an integrable system of evolutionary PDEs to any F-CohFT (a generalization of the notion of partial CohFT introduced in
[
BR21a
]
and further studied in
[
ABLR20
]
). Although this last generalization will not be needed in this paper, it has several points in common with a reduction of the DR hierarchy associated to the infinte rank partial CohFT (
1.5
) (the reduction corresponding to only considering the spaces of meromorphic differentials with exactly two zeros), which we will study in Section
3
.

Let
\psi_{i}\in H^{2}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
be the
i
-th
psi class
, i.e. the first Chern class of the tautological line bundle over
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
whose fiber at a stable curve is the cotangent line at its
i
-th marked point. Let
\lambda_{j}\in H^{2j}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
be the
j
-th
Hodge class
, i.e. the
j
-th Chern class of the Hodge bundle
\mathbb{E}
, which is the rank
g
vector bundle over
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
whose fiber at a stable curve is its space of holomorphic one-forms.

For any
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=0
, let
\mathrm{DR}_{g}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in H^{2g}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
be the (untwisted)
double ramification (DR) cycle
. The DR cycle is the pushforward, through the forgetful map to
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
, of the virtual fundamental class of the moduli space of projectivized stable maps to
{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}^{1}
relative to
0
and
\infty
, with ramification profile
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
at the marked points (see, e.g.,
[
BSSZ15
]
for more details). More precisely, the pushforward itself lies in
H_{2(2g-3+n)}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
, while its Poincaré dual cohomology class lies in
H^{2g}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
. By abuse of notation, we will denote both the pushforward and its Poincaré dual by
\mathrm{DR}_{g}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.

The restriction
\mathrm{DR}_{g}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\big|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{ct}}}
, where we recall that
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{ct}}
is the moduli space of stable curves of compact type, is a homogeneous polynomial in
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
of degree
2g
with the coefficients in
H^{2g}({\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{ct}})
(see, e.g.,
[
JPPZ17
]
). Polynomiality of the DR cycle on
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{ct}}_{g,n}
together with the fact that
\lambda_{g}
vanishes on
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{ct}}
(see, e.g.,
[
FP00
, Section 0.4]
) implies that the cohomology class
\lambda_{g}\mathrm{DR}_{g}\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in H^{4g}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n+1})
is a degree
2g
homogeneous polynomial in the coefficients
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{A}
and
\widehat{\Lambda}_{A}
be the spaces of
differential polynomials
and
local functionals
in formal variables
u^{\alpha}_{k}
,
\alpha\in A
,
k\geq 0
, and
\varepsilon
, where
A
is an index set (as above, finite or countable) and, in the case of finite
A
, the definitions and the notations are taken from the paper
[
Ros17
, Section 2.1]
. A minor adjustment is needed in order to include the case of countable
A
in our considerations. The ring
\mathbb{C}[[u^{*}_{*}]]
is graded by the differential grading
\deg_{{\partial}_{x}}u^{\alpha}_{k}:=k
, and the degree
d
part of it is denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{A}^{[d]}
. We then define
\mathcal{A}_{A}:=\oplus_{d\geq 0}\mathcal{A}_{A}^{[d]}
,
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{A}:=\mathcal{A}_{A}[[\varepsilon]]
, and
\widehat{\Lambda}_{A}:=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{A}\left/\left({\partial}_{x}\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{A}\oplus\mathbb{C}[[\varepsilon]]\right)\right.
where
{\partial}_{x}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}u^{\alpha}_{k+1}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}u^{\alpha}_{k}}
. We denote the image of
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{A}
through the natural projection to
\widehat{\Lambda}_{A}
by
\overline{f}=\int fdx
. Assigning
\deg_{{\partial}_{x}}\varepsilon:=-1
, the degree
d
parts of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{A}
and
\widehat{\Lambda}_{A}
are denoted by
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{A}^{[d]}
and
\widehat{\Lambda}^{[d]}_{A}
, respectively.

Remark 2.1
.

In the case of finite
A
we have
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{A}=\mathbb{C}[[u^{*}]][u^{*}_{>0}][[\varepsilon]]
where
u^{\alpha}:=u^{\alpha}_{0}
, which is the standard way to introduce the space of differential polynomials, but for countable
A
we have
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{A}\neq\mathbb{C}[[u^{*}]][u^{*}_{>0}][[\varepsilon]]
.

Given a partial CohFT
c_{g,n}\colon V^{\otimes n}\to H^{\mathrm{even}}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
with
V=\operatorname{span}\left(\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}\right)
, unit
e_{1\!\!1}
, and metric
\eta
, the Hamiltonian densities for the associated DR hierarchy are the generating series
[
BR16a
]

(2.1)

g_{\alpha,d}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g,n\geq 0\\
2g-1+n>0\end{subarray}}\frac{\varepsilon^{2g}}{n!}\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\geq 0}\mathrm{Coef}_{a_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots a_{n}^{k_{n}}}\left(\int_{\mathrm{DR}_{g}\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i},a_{1}\ldots,a_{n}\right)}\hskip-56.9055pt\lambda_{g}\psi_{1}^{d}c_{g,n+1}(e_{\alpha}\otimes\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}})\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}u^{\alpha_{i}}_{k_{i}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{[0]}_{A},

where
\alpha\in A
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. To this definition one can add
g_{\alpha,-1}:=\eta_{\alpha\mu}u^{\mu}
,
\alpha\in A
. The
Hamiltonians
of the DR hierarchy are the local functionals
\overline{g}_{\alpha,d}\in\widehat{\Lambda}^{[0]}_{A}
,
\alpha\in A
,
d\geq-1
. By a result of
[
Bur15
]
, the Hamiltonians of the DR hierarchy are in involution with respect to the Poisson brackets on
\widehat{\Lambda}_{A}
defined by
\{\overline{f},\overline{g}\}=\int\left(\frac{\delta\overline{f}}{\delta u^{\alpha}}\eta^{\alpha\beta}{\partial}_{x}\frac{\delta\overline{g}}{\delta u^{\beta}}\right)dx
for any two local functionals
\overline{f},\overline{g}\in\widehat{\Lambda}_{A}
, that is
\{\overline{g}_{\alpha_{1},d_{1}},\overline{g}_{\alpha_{2},d_{2}}\}=0
for all
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in A
and
d_{1},d_{2}\geq-1
.

This implies that the infinite system of evolutionary PDEs, called the
DR hierarchy
,

(2.2)

\frac{{\partial}u^{\alpha}}{{\partial}t^{\beta}_{d}}=\eta^{\alpha\mu}{\partial}_{x}\frac{\delta\overline{g}_{\beta,d}}{\delta u^{\mu}},\qquad\alpha,\beta\in A,\quad d\geq 0,

where, for any
\overline{f}\in\widehat{\Lambda}_{A}
,

\frac{\delta\overline{f}}{\delta u^{\alpha}}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-{\partial}_{x})^{k}\frac{{\partial}f}{{\partial}u^{\alpha}_{k}},\qquad\alpha\in A,

satisfies the compatibility conditions
\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t^{\beta_{2}}_{d_{2}}}\frac{{\partial}u^{\alpha}}{{\partial}t^{\beta_{1}}_{d_{1}}}=\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t^{\beta_{1}}_{d_{1}}}\frac{{\partial}u^{\alpha}}{{\partial}t^{\beta_{2}}_{d_{2}}}
for all
\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in A
and
d_{1},d_{2}\geq 0
.

In
[
BR16a
,
BDGR18
,
BDGR20
]
the authors showed that the DR hierarchy of a partial CohFT is a hierarchy of DR type, which means in particular that it is a tau-symmetric Hamiltonian system and its Hamiltonian densities can be reconstructed uniquely from the Hamiltonian
\overline{g}_{{1\!\!1},1}
only, via a universal recursion equation.

If the partial CohFT
c_{g,n}\colon V^{\otimes n}\to H^{\mathrm{even}}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
is homogeneous, with notations as in Definition
1.7

3.
A reduction to meromorphic differentials with two zeros and the KP hierarchy

In this section we describe a reduction of the DR hierarchy for the cycles of residueless meromorphic differentials. As we will see, this reduction does not respect the Poisson structure, in the sense that it is only defined at the level of vector fields. As the main result of the paper, we will prove that the reduction coincides with the KP hierarchy up to a Miura transformation.

The KP hierarchy

Let us briefly recall the construction of the KP hierarchy and some of its properties. A more detailed introduction can be found, for example, in
[

Consider formal variables
f_{i}^{(j)}
,
i\geq 1

pseudo-differential operator

A
is a Laurent series

Let
A_{+}:=\sum_{n=0}^{m}a_{n}{\partial}_{x}^{n}
and
\res A:=a_{-1}
. The product of pseudo-differential operators is defined by the following commutation rule:

\displaystyle{\partial}_{x}^{k}\circ a\coloneqq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{k(k-1)\ldots(k-l+1)}{l!}({\partial}_{x}^{l}a){\partial}_{x}^{k-l},\qquad a\in\mathcal{R}_{f},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},

which endows the space of pseudo-differential operators with the structure of an associative algebra.

Let

The
KP hierarchy
is the system of evolutionary PDEs with dependent variables
f_{i}
defined by

\frac{{\partial}L}{{\partial}T_{n}}=[(L^{n})_{+},L],\qquad n\geq 1.

Example 3.3

Using that

\displaystyle L^{2}={\partial}_{x}^{2}+2f_{1}+\left(2f_{2}+f_{1}^{(1)}\right){\partial}_{x}^{-1}+\left(2f_{3}+f_{1}^{2}+f_{2}^{(1)}\right){\partial}_{x}^{-2}+\ldots,

Lemma 3.9
.

We have
R_{\alpha\beta}\in\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ev}}_{w;\geq 1}
.

Proof.

There is an involution on the space of pseudo-differential operators given by

\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{m}a_{n}{\partial}_{x}^{n}\right)^{\dagger}:=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{m}(-{\partial}_{x})^{n}\circ a_{n}.

It satisfies the properties
(A\circ B)^{\dagger}=B^{\dagger}\circ A^{\dagger}
and
\res A^{\dagger}=-\res A
for any two pseudo-differential operators
A
and
B
.

Consider the change of variables
f_{i}\mapsto\widetilde{f}_{i}(f^{(*)}_{*})
given by

\displaystyle L={\partial}_{x}+\sum_{i\geq 1}f_{i}{\partial}_{x}^{-i}\,\mapsto

\displaystyle\mapsto\,

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}={\partial}_{x}+\sum_{i\geq 1}\widetilde{f}_{i}(f^{(*)}_{*}){\partial}_{x}^{-i}:=-L^{\dagger}={\partial}_{x}+f_{1}{\partial}_{x}^{-1}+(-f_{2}-f_{1}^{(1)}){\partial}_{x}^{-2}+(f_{3}+2f_{2}^{(1)}+f_{1}^{(2)}){\partial}_{x}^{-3}+\ldots.

It is clearly invertible and it induces a change of variables

, for which we compute

\widetilde{w}_{\alpha}(w^{(*)}_{*})=\res\widetilde{L}^{a}=(-1)^{a}\res(L^{\dagger})^{a}=(-1)^{a}\res(L^{a})^{\dagger}=(-1)^{a+1}\res L^{a}=(-1)^{a+1}w_{a}.

Therefore, the KP hierarchy written in the variables

has the form

(3.31)

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}\widetilde{w}_{\alpha}}{{\partial}T_{\beta}}={\partial}_{x}\widetilde{R}_{\alpha\beta},\qquad\widetilde{R}_{\alpha\beta}=(-1)^{\alpha+1}\left.R_{\alpha\beta}\right|_{w_{\gamma}^{(k)}=(-1)^{\gamma+1}\widetilde{w}_{\gamma}^{(k)}}\in\mathcal{R}_{\widetilde{w};\geq 1}.

On the other hand, we compute

\frac{{\partial}\widetilde{L}}{{\partial}T_{\beta}}=-\left(\frac{{\partial}L}{{\partial}T_{\beta}}\right)^{\dagger}=-[(L^{\beta})_{+},L]^{\dagger}=\left[\left((L^{\dagger})^{\beta}\right)_{+},L^{\dagger}\right]=(-1)^{\beta+1}\left[(\widetilde{L}^{\beta})_{+},\widetilde{L}\right],

and, therefore,
\frac{{\partial}\widetilde{w}_{\alpha}}{{\partial}T_{\beta}}=(-1)^{\beta+1}\res\left[(\widetilde{L}^{\beta})_{+},\widetilde{L}^{\alpha}\right]
. Hence,
\widetilde{R}_{\alpha\beta}=\left.(-1)^{\beta+1}R_{\alpha\beta}\right|_{w^{(k)}_{\gamma}=\widetilde{w}^{(k)}_{\gamma}}
. Combining this with (
3.31
) we obtain
\left.(-1)^{\alpha+\beta}R_{\alpha\beta}\right|_{w^{(k)}_{\gamma}\mapsto(-1)^{\gamma+1}w^{(k)}_{\gamma}}=R_{\alpha\beta}
. Together with the property
\deg R_{\alpha\beta}=\alpha+\beta
this implies that
\left.R_{\alpha\beta}\right|_{w^{(k)}_{\gamma}\mapsto(-1)^{k}w^{(k)}_{\gamma}}=R_{\alpha\beta}
, which gives

, as required.

r_{\gamma}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{\gamma-1})\in\mathcal{R}_{v}
,
\tdeg r_{\gamma}=2
, as follows:

Q_{\gamma,2}=v_{\gamma+1}+r_{\gamma}+\left(\text{monomials of $\tdeg\geq 3$}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Lemma 3.14
.

We have
r_{\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+k=\gamma}v_{i}v_{k}
.

Proof.

We already know this for
\gamma=1,2
, so we need to prove it for
\gamma\geq 3
. Consider equation (
3.42
), where we recall that
\beta\geq 4
. Let
r_{i,k}:=\left.\frac{{\partial}^{2}Q_{\beta-1,2}}{{\partial}v_{i}{\partial}v_{k}}\right|_{v_{*}=0}
. Note that
r_{i,k}=r_{k,i}
, and that
r_{i,k}=0
unless
i+k=\beta-1
. We know that
r_{1,\beta-2}=1
. Equation (
3.42
) in particular means that

\displaystyle\int\left(\sum_{i,k=1}^{\beta-2}\sum_{j,l\geq 0}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v^{(j)}_{i}}\left(\frac{{\partial}\widetilde{Q}_{2,\beta-1}}{{\partial}v^{(l)}_{k}}{\partial}_{x}^{l+1}Q_{k,2}\right){\partial}_{x}^{j+1}Q_{i,2}-v_{2}^{(1)}v_{\beta-1}^{(1)}\right)dx=0\quad\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\int\left(\sum_{i,k=1}^{\beta-2}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v^{(j)}_{i}}\left(\frac{{\partial}r_{\beta-1}}{{\partial}v_{k}}v^{(1)}_{k+1}\right)v^{(j+1)}_{i+1}-v_{2}^{(1)}v_{\beta-1}^{(1)}\right)dx=0.

The last integral is equal to

\displaystyle\int\left(\sum_{i,k=1}^{\beta-2}\frac{{\partial}^{2}r_{\beta-1}}{{\partial}v_{i}{\partial}v_{k}}v_{i+1}^{(1)}v^{(1)}_{k+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{\beta-3}\frac{{\partial}r_{\beta-1}}{{\partial}v_{k}}v^{(2)}_{k+2}-v_{2}^{(1)}v_{\beta-1}^{(1)}\right)dx=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\beta-2}\sum_{k=1}^{\beta-3}r_{i,k}\left(v_{i+1}^{(1)}v^{(1)}_{k+1}-v_{i}^{(1)}v_{k+2}^{(1)}\right)\right)dx.

Note that if for a quadratic polynomial
p
in the variables
v_{1}^{(1)},\ldots,v_{\beta-2}^{(1)}
we have
\int pdx=0
, then
p=0
. Therefore, we have

0=\sum_{i=1}^{\beta-2}\sum_{k=1}^{\beta-3}r_{i,k}\left(v_{i+1}^{(1)}v^{(1)}_{k+1}-v_{i}^{(1)}v_{k+2}^{(1)}\right)=\sum_{i,k=2}^{\beta-3}(r_{i,k}-r_{i+1,k-1})v_{i+1}^{(1)}v^{(1)}_{k+1}+(r_{\beta-2,1}-r_{2,\beta-3})v_{2}^{(1)}v_{\beta-1}^{(1)},

which implies

\displaystyle r_{1,\beta-2}=r_{2,\beta-3},

\displaystyle r_{i+1,k-1}+r_{i-1,k+1}=2r_{i,k},\qquad 2\leq i,k\leq\beta-3,\quad i+k=\beta-1.

Since
r_{1,\beta-2}=1
, this immediately gives that
r_{i,k}=1
for
i+k=\beta-1
, as required.
∎

Consider relation (

\displaystyle\Bigg[\sum_{i,k=1}^{\beta-2}\sum_{j,l\geq 0}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v^{(j)}_{i}}\left(\frac{{\partial}R}{{\partial}v^{(l)}_{k}}{\partial}_{x}^{l+1}Q_{k,2}\right){\partial}_{x}^{j+1}Q_{i,2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle+{\partial}_{x}\left[-\sum_{i=1}^{\beta-3}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{{\partial}R}{{\partial}v^{(j)}_{i}}{\partial}_{x}^{j}\left({\partial}_{x}Q_{i+1,2}+\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\sum_{l\geq 0}\frac{{\partial}\widetilde{Q}_{2,i}}{{\partial}v^{(l)}_{k}}{\partial}_{x}^{l+1}Q_{k,2}-v_{1}v_{i}^{(1)}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{12}v_{i}^{(3)}\right)-v_{1}{\partial}_{x}R+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{12}{\partial}_{x}^{3}R\right]

Generalized Iterated Function System and Common Attractors of
Generalized Hutchinson Operators in Dislocated Metric Spaces

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we present the
generalized iterated function system for constructing of common fractals of
generalized contractive mappings in the setup of dislocated metric spaces.
The well-posedness of attractors based problems of rational contraction maps
in the framework of dislocated metric spaces is also established. Moreover,
the generalized collage theorem is also established in dislocated metric
spaces.

Keywords
: Hutchinson operator;
generalized iterated function system; fractal, generalized rational
contraction; Set-valued mapping; common fixed point.

Introduction and preliminaries

Metric fixed point theory serves as an essential tool for solving problems
arising in various branches of mathematical analysis for instance, split
feasibility problems, variational inequality problems, nonlinear
optimization problems, equilibrium problems, complementary problems,
selection and matching problems, and problems of proving an existence of
solution of integral and differential equations, adaptive control systems,
fractal image decoding, convergence of recurrent networks and many more. In
particular, it has deep roots in nonlinear functional analysis.

Hitzler and Seda
[
4
]
introduced the notion of dislocated
metric space and proved a fixed point result as an interesting
generalization of the Banach contraction principle. Then several useful
results of fixed point theory were established,
[

,
2
,
6
,
8
]
introduced the notion of contractions and related
fixed point results in dislocated metric spaces.

By using the concept of Hausdorff dislocated metric and generalized
contraction mappings, we establish the existence of attractors of Hutchinson
operators in the framework of Dislocated metric spaces. First we recall the
definition of dislocated metric space.

Theorem 1.11
. A dislocated metric space
(X,\delta)

Proof.

\{F_{n}\}

If

Generalized Iterated Function System

a finite collection of generalized rational contraction mappings on a
dislocated metric space. We start with the following result.

\displaystyle\frac{H_{\delta}(V,S\left(V\right))[1+H_{\delta}(U,T\left(U\right))]}{1+H_{\delta}(U,V)},\frac{H_{\delta}(V,T\left(U\right))[1+H_{\delta}(V,T\left(U\right))]}{1+H_{\delta}(U,V)},

\displaystyle\frac{H_{\delta}(V,T\left(U\right))[1+H_{\delta}(U,T\left(U\right))]}{1+H_{\delta}(U,V)}\}.

The above defined operator

is also known as a generalized
rational contractive Hutchinson operator which is the extension of
Hutchinson operator given in

(T,S)

defined in Proposition 2.1 is generalized contraction, then it is trivially
generalized rational contraction and so
(T,S)
is generalized rational
contractive Hutchinson operator.

Definition 2.3.
Let
X
be a dislocated metric space.
If
f_{n},g_{n}:X\rightarrow X
,

are generalized contraction
mappings, then
(X;f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{N};g_{1,}g_{2},...,g_{N})
is called
generalized iterated function system (GIFS).

Definition 2.4.
A nonempty closed and bounded subset
U
of
X
is called a common attractor of
T
and
S\
generated by GIFS if

(a)

T(U)=S(U)=U
and

(b)

there exist an open subset

of
X
such that
U\subseteq V

and
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}T^{k}(B)=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}S^{k}(B)=U
for any closed and bounded subset

of
V

limit is applied with respect to the dislocated Hausdorff metric.

The largest open set
V
satisfying (b) is called a basin of
attraction.

\displaystyle\frac{H_{\delta}\left(B_{2n+1},A^{\ast}\right)[1+H_{\delta}\left(B_{2n+1},T\left(A^{\ast}\right)\right)]}{1+H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+1})},

\displaystyle\frac{H_{\delta}(B_{2n+1},T\left(A^{\ast}\right))[1+H_{\delta}(B_{2n+1},T\left(A^{\ast}\right))]}{1+H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+1})},

\displaystyle\frac{H_{\delta}(B_{2n+1},T\left(A^{\ast}\right))[1+H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T\left(A^{\ast}\right))]}{1+H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+1})}\}.

Now, we have again the following cases:

(i)

If
M_{T,S}\left(B_{2n+1},A^{\ast}\right)=H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+1}),
then by (3), we have

H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))\leq H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+2})+\alpha H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+1})

and on taking limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
implies

H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))=0,

which implies
A^{\ast}=T\left(A^{\ast}\right)
.

(ii)

If
M_{T,S}\left(B_{2n+1},A^{\ast}\right)=H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast})),
then by (3), we get

H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))\leq H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+2})+\alpha H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))

and on taking limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
implies

H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))\leq 0+\alpha H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast})),

that is,

(1-\alpha)H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))\leq 0

and since
(1-\alpha)>0
, so we get
A^{\ast}=T\left(A^{\ast}\right).

(iii)

If
M_{T,S}\left(B_{2n+1},A^{\ast}\right)=H_{\delta}\left(B_{2n+1},B_{2n+2}\right),
then by (3), we obtain that

H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))\leq H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+2})+\alpha H_{\delta}\left(B_{2n+1},B_{2n+2}\right)

and on taking limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
implies

H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))=0,

that is,
A^{\ast}=T\left(A^{\ast}\right)
.

(iv)

If
M_{T,S}\left(B_{2n+1},A^{\ast}\right)=\dfrac{H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+2})+H_{\delta}(B_{2n+1},T\left(A^{\ast}\right))}{2},

then

H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))\leq H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+2})+\frac{\alpha}{2}[H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+2})+H_{\delta}(B_{2n+1},T\left(A^{\ast}\right))]

and on taking limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
implies

H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))\leq 0+\frac{\alpha}{2}H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast})),

that is,

(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))\leq 0

and since
1-\dfrac{\alpha}{2}>0
, so we get
A^{\ast}=T\left(A^{\ast}\right).

(v)

If
M_{T,S}\left(B_{2n+1},A^{\ast}\right)=\dfrac{H_{\delta}\left(B_{2n+1},A^{\ast}\right)[1+H_{\delta}\left(B_{2n+1},T\left(A^{\ast}\right)\right)]}{1+H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+1})},
we have

H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))\leq H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+2})+\alpha\frac{H_{\delta}\left(B_{2n+1},A^{\ast}\right)[1+H_{\delta}\left(B_{2n+1},T\left(A^{\ast}\right)\right)]}{1+H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+1})}

and on taking limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
implies

H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))=0,

that is,
A^{\ast}=T\left(A^{\ast}\right)
.

(vi)

If
M_{T,S}\left(B_{2n+1},A^{\ast}\right)=\dfrac{H_{\delta}(B_{2n+1},B_{2n+2})(1+H_{\delta}\left(A^{\ast},T\left(A^{\ast}\right)\right)}{1+H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+1})},

and on taking limit as

H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))=0,

that is,
A^{\ast}=T\left(A^{\ast}\right).

(vii)

If
M_{T,S}\left(B_{2n+1},A^{\ast}\right)=\dfrac{H_{\delta}(B_{2n+1},T\left(A^{\ast}\right))[1+H_{\delta}(B_{2n+1},T\left(A^{\ast}\right))]}{1+H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+1})},

\displaystyle H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))\leq H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+2})+\alpha\frac{H_{\delta}(B_{2n+1},T\left(A^{\ast}\right))[1+H_{\delta}(B_{2n+1},T\left(A^{\ast}\right))]}{1+H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+1})}

\displaystyle H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+2})+\alpha\frac{[H_{\delta}(B_{2n+1},A^{\ast})+H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T\left(A^{\ast}\right))][1+H_{\delta}(B_{2n+1},T\left(A^{\ast}\right))]}{1+H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+1})}

and on taking limit as

H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))\leq\alpha H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast})),

that is,

(1-\alpha)H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))\leq 0

and since

, so we get
A^{\ast}=T\left(A^{\ast}\right).

(viii)

Finally if

\displaystyle H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T(A^{\ast}))\leq H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+2})+\alpha\frac{H_{\delta}(B_{2n+1},T\left(A^{\ast}\right))[1+H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},T\left(A^{\ast}\right))]}{1+H_{\delta}(A^{\ast},B_{2n+1})}

and since

which gives

Thus,

is the common attractor of
T
and
S.

Theorem 3.5
(

Let
(X,\delta)
be a dislocated metric space and

and

.

U
is the

that is,

4
Well-posedness of Common Attractors

In this section, we will define the well-posedness of attractors based
problems of rational contraction maps in the framework of dislocated metric
spaces.

Definition 4.1.
Let
(X,\delta)
be a dislocated
metric space. An attractor based problem of mapping
T:\mathcal{C}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(X)\
is called well-posed if
T
has a unique
attractor
A^{\ast}\in\mathcal{C}(X)
and for any sequence
\{A_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{C}(X)
such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}H_{\delta}(T(A_{n}),A_{n})=0
implies that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}A_{n}=A^{\ast}.

Definition 4.2.
Let
(X,\delta)
be a dislocated
metric space. A common attractor based problem of mappings
T,S:\mathcal{C}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(X)\
is called well-posed if
T
and
S
have a
unique common attractor
A^{\ast}\in\mathcal{C}(X)
and for any sequence
\{A_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{C}(X)
such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}H_{\delta}(T(A_{n}),A_{n})=0
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}H_{\delta}(S(A_{n}),A_{n})=0
implies that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}A_{n}=A^{\ast}.

Let
(X,\delta)

\displaystyle H_{\delta}(B^{\ast},B_{n})

\displaystyle 0,

(vi)

If
M_{T,S}(B^{\ast},B_{n})=\dfrac{H_{\delta}(B_{n},S\left(B_{n}\right))[1+H_{\delta}(B^{\ast},T\left(B^{\ast}\right))]}{1+H_{\delta}(B^{\ast},B_{n})}

Recovering R R -symbols from modular data
Siu-Hung Ng, Eric C. Rowell, Xiao-Gang Wen

Abstract
. Given a premodular category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , we show that its R R -symbols can be recovered from its T T -matrix, fusion coefficients and some 2nd generalized Frobenius-Schur indicators. In particular, if 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is modular, its R R -symbols for a certain gauge choice are explicitly determined by its modular data. Thus, R R -symbols cannot distinguish distinct topological orders with the same modular data. Our approach suggests that in the multiplicity-free case the modular data determines 𝒞 \mathcal{C} .



Introduction
:
Modular tensor categories (MTCs) are spherical nondegenerate braided fusion
categories. These categories naturally arise in both mathematics and physics
such that the theory of subfactors, rational conformal theory
[
1
]
,
topological quantum field theory
[
2
]
, topological orders

[
3
,
4
]
, and many more. Recently, MTC theory becomes more
important due to a realization that symmetries in 1-dimensional quantum systems
are actually described by MTCs in trivial Witt class

[
5
,
6
,
7
]
, rather than by groups which can
only describe a subset of symmetries.

Associated to an MTC
\mathcal{C}
is a pair
(S,T)
of complex square matrices, called the
modular data
of
\mathcal{C}
, which are indexed by a complete set
{\operatorname{Irr}}(\mathcal{C})
of simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
, and uniquely determined by
\mathcal{C}
up to some permutations on
{\operatorname{Irr}}(\mathcal{C})
. The number
|{\operatorname{Irr}}(\mathcal{C})|
is called the
rank
of the modular data
(S,T)
.

There have been numerous efforts to generate modular data of small ranks (cf.
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
). However, it has been shown by examples that modular data of an MTC does not determine the underlying MTC
[
12
]
. More precisely, the nonabelian group
G
of order
55
admits two non-cohomologous 3-cocycles
{\omega}
and
{\omega}^{\prime}
such that the centers
Z({\operatorname{Vec}}_{G}^{\omega})
and
Z({\operatorname{Vec}}_{G}^{{\omega}^{\prime}})
are inequivalent braided fusion categories but their modular data are the same, up to a permutation.

To completely determine an MTC
\mathcal{C}
, up to equivalence, one may need its associativity constraints and braidings. It is natural to ask whether there is any invariant of MTCs beyond modular data without knowing the complete information of its associativity constraints and braiding. The Whitehead
[
13
]
and the Borromean links
[
14
]
colored by simple objects of an MTC were shown to be invariants beyond modular data, and they can distinguish
Z({\operatorname{Vec}}_{G}^{{\omega}})
for any 3-cocycle
{\omega}
of the nonabelian group
G
above.

These invariants motivate us to ask if the gauge equivalence classes of
R
-symbols of an MTC are invariants beyond modular data. However, we find that, for some
gauge choice
, the
R
-symbols can be determined by the modular data of any MTC. We conjecture that in the multiplicity-free case, modular data
does
determine the MTC uniquely.

In general,
R
-symbols of a premodular category
\mathcal{C}
can be determined by its
T
-matrix, fusion rules and the second generalized Frobenius-Schur indicators. When
\mathcal{C}
is modular, its fusion rules and generalized Frobenius-Schur indicators can be expressed in terms of its modular data.

The article assumes some basic knowledge on modular tensor categories, graphical calculus and Frobenius-Schur indicators for pivotal categories. The readers are referred to
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
for more details. The categorical development of Frobenius-Schur indicators can be found in
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
.

\frac{\sqrt{\theta_{c}}}{\theta_{a}}
of the matrix
[R_{a,a}^{c}]
presented in Proposition
1
up to an ordering of the gauge choice. This proves the first part of following statement.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a premodular category over
{\mathbb{C}}
. The braiding of
\mathcal{C}
is completely determined by its fusion rules, its ribbon structure and the generalized Frobenius-Schur indicators
\nu_{2,1}^{\iota(c)}(a)
for all
a,c\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(\mathcal{C})
. The value
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\theta_{c}}}\nu_{2,1}^{\iota(c)}(a)
is an integer bounded by
N_{c}^{a,a}
and has the same parity as
N_{c}^{a,a}
for any
a,c\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(\mathcal{C})
.

The matrix
[R_{a,a}^{c}]
in Proposition
1
is
\frac{\sqrt{\theta_{c}}}{\theta_{a}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}I_{d_{+}}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&I_{d_{-}}\end{array}\right]
for some basis
B^{a,a}_{c}
of
\mathcal{C}(a{\otimes}a,c)
, and

(7)

\displaystyle d_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(N^{a,a}_{c}\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{\theta_{c}}}\nu_{2,1}^{\iota(c)}(a))\,.

In particular, if
\mathcal{C}
is modular, the braiding of
\mathcal{C}
is completely determined by the modular data
(S,T)
of
\mathcal{C}
and

(8)

d_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left(N^{a,a}_{c}\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{\theta_{c}}\dim\mathcal{C}}\sum_{k,l\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(\mathcal{C})}d_{k}\overline{S}_{c,l}N^{kl}_{a}\frac{\theta^{2}_{k}}{\theta^{2}_{l}}\right)\,,

where
d_{k}
is the categorical dimension of the object
k

Proof.

For the last assertion, it follows from
[
20
, Prop. 6.1]
that

(9)

\nu_{2,1}^{\iota(c)}(a)=\frac{1}{\dim\mathcal{C}}\sum_{k,l\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(\mathcal{C})}d_{k}\overline{S}_{c,l}N^{kl}_{a}\frac{\theta^{2}_{k}}{\theta^{2}_{l}},

where
S_{c,l}
and
T_{c,l}=\delta_{c,l}\theta_{l}
are the modular data of
\mathcal{C}
. By the Verlinde formula, the fusion rules of
\mathcal{C}
are determined by the
S
-matrix. Therefore, the braiding of
\mathcal{C}
is completely determined by the modular data
(S,T)
of
\mathcal{C}
.

\square

Remark 4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a modular tensor category. It was conjectured in
[
21
]
that

Y_{a,b}^{c}:=\frac{\sqrt{\theta_{b}}}{\sqrt{\theta_{c}}\dim\mathcal{C}}\sum_{k,l\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(\mathcal{C})}\overline{S}_{b,k}\overline{S}_{c,l}N^{kl}_{a}\frac{\theta^{2}_{k}}{\theta^{2}_{l}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}

for all
a,b,c\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(\mathcal{C})
. It was further conjectured in
[
22
]
that

\dim\mathcal{C}(c,a{\otimes}a{\otimes}b)\pm Y_{a,b}^{c}\geq 0\,.

Both conjectures were proved in
[
20
, §8]
, and it was further proved in
[
23
, Prop. 4.2]
that
\dim\mathcal{C}(c,a{\otimes}a{\otimes}b)\pm Y_{a,b}^{c}
is always an even integer. The second statement of Theorem
3
could be considered as an extension of
[
23
, Prop. 4.2]
to premodular categories but only for
b={\mathbb{1}}
.

The expression (
5
) also appeared in
[
13
, Prop. 2.3 (1)]
and
[
24
, Prop. 2.4 (1)]
with
\nu_{2,1}^{\iota(c)}(a)
being substituted by (
9
) and the Verlinde formula. Equation (
8
) can also be found in
[
25
]
. A similar conclusion for UMTC as the last part of Theorem 3 was also obtained in
[
24
, Prop. 3.2]
.

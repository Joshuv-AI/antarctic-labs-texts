Refined Verlinde formulas for Hilbert schemes of points and moduli spaces of sheaves on K3 surfaces
Lothar Göttsche

Abstract




Abstract.
We compute generating functions for elliptic genera with values in line bundles on Hilbert schemes of points on surfaces.
As an application we also compute generating functions for elliptic genera with values in determinant line bundles on moduli spaces of sheaves on K3 surfaces.

Keywords.
Hilbert schemes; moduli spaces of sheaves; elliptic genus; Verlinde formula

1. Introduction

The celebrated Verlinde formula (see
[
Ver88
,
NR93
,
BL94
,
Fal94
]
) is a formula for the generating function for dimensions of spaces of sections of line bundles on moduli spaces of vector bundles on algebraic curves.

Now let
S
be a smooth projective surface over
\mathbb C
and
S^{[n]}
the Hilbert scheme of
n
points on
S
.
For every vector bundle
V
on
S
there is a corresponding tautological bundle
V^{[n]}
of rank
\operatorname{rk}(V^{[n]})=n\operatorname{rk}(V)
, whose fibre
over
Z\in S^{[n]}
is
H^{0}(Z,V|_{Z})
. The map
V\mapsto V^{[n]}
extends to a homomorphism from the Grothendieck group
K^{0}(S)
of vector bundles on
S
to
K^{0}(S^{[n]})
.
For
L\in S^{[n]}
denote
\mu(L):=\det((L-\mathcal{O}_{S})^{[n]})
and
E:=\det(\mathcal{O}^{[n]}_{S})
.
Then it is well known that
\operatorname{Pic}(S^{[n]})=\mu(\operatorname{Pic}(S))\oplus\mathbb ZE
.
The analogue of the Verlinde formula for Hilbert schemes of points is a formula for the generating function for holomorphic Euler characteristics
\chi(S^{[n]},\mu(L)\otimes E^{r})
.
In
[
EGL01
]
such a formula was proven in the cases
r=-1,0,1
or

K_{S}^{2}=K_{S}L=0
.
On the other hand the celebrated Dijkgraaf-Moore-Verlinde-Verlinde formula
[
DMVV97
]
, shown in
[
BL00
,
BL03
,
BL05
]
, relates the generating function of the elliptic genera
\Ell(S^{[n]})
of Hilbert schemes of points to Siegel modular forms.

In this short note we interpolate between these two results, by proving a formula for
\Ell(S^{[n]},\mu(L)\otimes E^{r})
, the elliptic genus with values in the line bundle
\mu(L)\otimes E^{r}
.
To state these results we introduce the following power series.

\displaystyle\phi_{-2,1}(q,y)

\displaystyle:=(y^{1/2}-y^{-1/2})^{2}\prodop\displaylimits_{n>0}\frac{(1-q^{n}y)^{2}(1-q^{n}/y)^{2}}{(1-q^{n})^{4}},

\displaystyle\wp(q,y)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{12}+\frac{1}{(y^{1/2}-y^{-1/2})^{2}}+\sumop\displaylimits_{n>0}\sumop\displaylimits_{d|n}d(y^{d}-2+y^{-d})q^{n},

\displaystyle\phi_{0,1}(q,y)

\displaystyle:=12\wp(x,y)\phi_{-2,1}(x,y),

\displaystyle h(q,y)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{12}+\sumop\displaylimits_{n>0}\sumop\displaylimits_{d|n}\frac{n}{d}(y^{d}+y^{-d})x^{n}.

\wp(q,y)
is the Weierstrass
\wp
-function.
It is standard that

\Ell(S)=-(\wp+h)\phi_{-2,1}K_{S}^{2}+\phi_{0,1}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S}).

We also introduce the following Borcherds type lifts. For
f:=\sumop\displaylimits_{m,n}c_{m,n}q^{m}y^{n}\in\mathbb Q[[y,q]]
, we put

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}(f,p)

\displaystyle:=\prodop\displaylimits_{l>0,m\geq 0,n\in\mathbb Z}(1-p^{l}q^{m}y^{n})^{c_{lm,n}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}^{(a,b)}(f,p)

\displaystyle:=\prodop\displaylimits_{l>0,m\geq 0,n\in\mathbb Z}(1-p^{l}q^{m}y^{n})^{l^{a}n^{b}c_{lm,n}},\quad a,b\in\mathbb Z.

Then the DMVV formula says

\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}\Ell(S^{[n]})p^{n}=\frac{1}{\mathbf{L}(\Ell(S),p)}=\frac{\mathbf{L}((\wp+h)\phi_{-2,1},p)^{K_{S}^{2}}}{\mathbf{L}(\phi_{0,1},p)^{\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S})}}.

The first theorem deals with the case
r=0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
S
be an algebraic surface,
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)
.
Then

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}\Ell(S^{[n]},\mu(L))p^{n}=

\displaystyle\frac{\big(\mathbf{L}^{(2,0)}(-\phi_{-2,1},p)\big)^{\frac{L^{2}}{2}}\big(\mathbf{L}^{(1,1)}(-\phi_{-2,1},p)\big)^{\frac{LK_{S}}{2}}}{\mathbf{L}(\Ell(S),p)}.

Specializing to
L=\mathcal{O}_{S}
, we recover the DMVV formula. Specializing to
q=0
yields an infinite product formula for the
\chi_{y}
-genera with values in a
\mu(L)
, which in turn recovers for
L=\mathcal{O}_{S}

the formula for the
\chi_{-y}
-genera of Hilbert schemes from
[
GS93
]
.
We write

\widetilde{\Delta}(p,y):=\prodop\displaylimits_{n>0}(1-p^{n})^{20}(1-p^{n}y)^{2}(1-p^{n}/y)^{2},\quad\overline{\eta}(p)=\prodop\displaylimits_{n>0}(1-p^{n}).

Corollary 1.2
.

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}\overline{\chi}_{-y}(S^{[n]},\mu(L))p^{n}

\displaystyle=\prodop\displaylimits_{n>0}\left(\frac{(1-p^{n})^{2}}{(1-p^{n}y)(1-\frac{p^{n}}{y})}\right)^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}L^{2}}\prodop\displaylimits_{n>0}\left(\frac{1-\frac{p^{n}}{y}}{1-p^{n}y}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}LK_{S}}\frac{\overline{\eta}(p)^{K_{S}^{2}}}{\widetilde{\Delta}(p,y)^{\frac{\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S})}{2}}}.

For general line bundles on Hilbert schemes of points we can partially determine the generating function.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every
r\in\mathbb Z
there are universal power series

A_{r},B_{r}\in\mathbb Q[y^{\pm 1}][[z,q]]
such that for every smooth projective surface
S
and every
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)
we have

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}\Ell(S^{[n]},\mu(L)\otimes E^{r})p^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{L}^{(2,0)}(-\phi_{-2,1},z)^{\frac{1}{2}(L^{2}+r^{2}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S}))}A_{r}^{LK_{S}}B_{r}^{K_{S}^{2}}}{\left(\mathbf{L}(2\phi_{0,1},z)\left(1-r^{2}z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\log\mathbf{L}^{(2,0)}(\phi_{-2,1},z)\right)\right)^{\frac{\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S})}{2}}},

with the change of variables

p=z\mathbf{L}^{(2,0)}(-\phi_{-2,1},z)^{r^{2}}
.

Specialising to
q=0
again yields a formula for the
\chi_{-y}
-genus with values in
\mu(L)\otimes E^{r}
.

Corollary 1.4
.

For every smooth projective surface
S
and every
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)
we have

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}\overline{\chi}_{-y}(S^{[n]},\mu(L)\otimes E^{r})p^{n}

\displaystyle=\prodop\displaylimits_{n>0}\left(\frac{(1-z^{n})^{2}}{(1-z^{n}y)(1-z^{n}/y)}\right)^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}(L^{2}+r^{2}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S}))}

\displaystyle\times\frac{A_{r}(y,z,0)^{LK_{S}}B_{r}(y,z,0)^{K_{S}^{2}}}{\left(\widetilde{\Delta}(z,y)\left(1+r^{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 1}(y^{d}-2+y^{-d})\frac{n^{3}}{d^{3}}z^{n}\right)\right)^{\frac{\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S})}{2}}}.

with the change of variables

p=z\prodop\displaylimits_{n>0}\left(\frac{(1-z^{n})^{2}}{(1-z^{n}y)(1-z^{n}/y)}\right)^{n^{2}r^{2}}
.

Finally we show an analogue of Theorem
1.3
for moduli spaces of sheaves on K3 surfaces
S
.
Fix
s\in\mathbb Z_{>0}
. Let
H
be an ample line bundle on
S
and

M_{S}^{H}(s,c_{1},c_{2})
the moduli space of
H
-semistable sheaves on
S
of rank
s
with Chern classes
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
.
We assume that
M_{S}^{H}(s,c_{1},c_{2})
consists only of stable sheaves.
For any choice of
s,c_{1},c_{2}
, we denote

\operatorname{vd}=\operatorname{vd}(s,c_{1},c_{2}):=2sc_{2}-(s-1)c_{1}^{2}-2(s^{2}-1).

Let
r\in\mathbb Z
. Let
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)\otimes\mathbb Q
with
c_{1}(L)-\frac{r}{s}c_{1}\in H^{2}(S,\mathbb Z)
.
If
s
divides
(c_{1}(L)-\frac{r}{s}c_{1})c_{1}+r(\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{2}-c_{2})
, we define a determinant line bundle
\mu(L)\otimes E^{r}\in Pic(M^{H}_{S}(s,c_{1},c_{2}))
.
This is
the generalization of the line bundle with the same name on
S^{[n]}=M^{H}_{S}(1,0,n)
.
We obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
S
be a K3 surface,
s\in\mathbb Z_{>0}
. Under the assumptions above we have

\displaystyle\Ell(M^{H}_{S}(s,c_{1},c_{2}),\mu(L)\otimes E^{r})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Coeff}_{p^{\operatorname{vd}/2}}\left[\frac{\mathbf{L}^{(2,0)}(-\phi_{-2,1},z)^{\frac{L^{2}}{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{s^{2}}}}{\mathbf{L}(2\phi_{0,1},z)\left(1-\frac{r^{2}}{s^{2}}z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\log\mathbf{L}^{(2,0)}(\phi_{-2,1},z)\right)}\right],

with the change of variables

p=z\mathbf{L}^{(2,0)}(-\phi_{-2,1},z)^{\frac{r^{2}}{s^{2}}}
, and

\displaystyle\overline{\chi}_{-y}(M^{H}_{S}(s,c_{1},n),\mu(L)\otimes E^{r})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Coeff}_{p^{\operatorname{vd}/2}}\left[\frac{\prodop\displaylimits_{n>0}\left(\frac{(1-z^{n})^{2}}{(1-z^{n}y)(1-z^{n}/y)}\right)^{n^{2}(\frac{L^{2}}{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{s^{2}})}}{\left(\widetilde{\Delta}(z,y)\left(1+\frac{r^{2}}{s^{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 1}\sumop\displaylimits_{d|n}(y^{d/2}-y^{-d/2})^{2}\frac{n^{3}}{d^{3}}z^{n}\right)\right)}\right],

with the change of variables

p=z\prodop\displaylimits_{n>0}\left(\frac{(1-z^{n})^{2}}{(1-z^{n}y)(1-z^{n}/y)}\right)^{\frac{n^{2}r^{2}}{s^{2}}}
.

In the special case of K3 surfaces Theorem
1.5
in particular confirms the conjectures of
[
GKW
]
about refinements of Verlinde formulas for moduli spaces of rank 2 sheaves on surfaces in the case of K3 surfaces.
The specialization
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}
reproduces in the case of K3 surfaces the formulas of
[
GK
]
on the elliptic genus of moduli spaces of sheaves on surfaces.

2. Background material

2.1. Hilbert schemes of points

Let
S
be a smooth projective surface. We denote
S^{[n]}
the Hilbert scheme of
n
points on
S
. It is a smooth projective variety of dimension
2n
.
Let
S^{(n)}
be the
n
-th symmetric power of
S
. The Hilbert-Chow morphism
\pi:S^{[n]}\to S^{(n)},Z\mapsto supp(Z)
, sending a zero dimensional scheme to its support with multiplicities is a crepant resolution of
S^{(n)}
, i.e. it is birational and
\pi^{*}K_{S^{(n)}}=K_{S^{[n]}}
.
Let
Z_{n}(S)\subset S\times S^{[n]}
be the universal subscheme, with projections
p:Z_{n}(S)\to S^{[n]}
,
q:Z_{n}(S)\to S
.
For a vector bundle
V
of rank
r
on
S
the corresponding tautological vector bundle is

V^{[n]}:=p_{*}q^{*}V
, a vector bundle of rank
rn
on
S^{[n]}
. This extends to a homomorphism
\vphantom{S}{}^{[n]}:K^{0}(S)\to K^{0}(S^{[n]})
between the
Grothendieck groups of vector bundles.
We put
E:=\det(\mathcal{O}_{S}^{[n]})
, and for a line bundle
L
on
S
we put
\mu(L):=\det((L-\mathcal{O}_{S})^{[n]})
.
Let
\eta:S^{n}\to S^{(n)}
be the natural projection.
Let
L_{n}:=\eta_{*}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}pr_{i}^{*}L)^{\mathfrak S_{n}}
be the
\mathfrak S_{n}
-equivariant pushforward, where
pr_{i}:S^{n}\to S
is the
i
-th projection.
Then it is well-known that
\mu(L)=\eta^{*}(L_{n})
, and from the definitions it follows that
\det(V^{[n]})=\mu(\det(V))\otimes E^{\operatorname{rk}(V)}
.

2.2. Elliptic genus.

For a compact complex manifold
M
, the
\chi_{-y}
-genus is

\chi_{-y}(M):=\sumop\displaylimits_{p}(-y)^{p}\chi(M,{}^{p}_{M}).

Usually we consider the normalized version

\overline{\chi}_{-y}(M):=(-y)^{-\frac{\dim(M)}{2}}\chi_{-y}(M)
.
For
V\in K^{0}(M)
the
\chi_{-y}
-genus with values in
V
is

\overline{\chi}_{-y}(M,V):=y^{-\frac{\dim(M)}{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{p}(-y)^{p}\chi(M,{}^{p}_{M}\otimes V).

For a rank
r
vector bundle
V
on
M
put

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}V:=\sumop\displaylimits_{n=0}^{r}[\Lambda^{n}V]\,t^{n},\quad\operatorname{Sym}_{t}V:=\sumop\displaylimits_{n=0}^{\infty}[\operatorname{Sym}^{n}V]\,t^{n}.

Write
y=e^{2\pi iz}
,
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
.
Then for
W\in K^{0}(M)
, the elliptic genus and the elliptic genus with values in
W
are defined by

\displaystyle\Ell(M)

\displaystyle:=\Ell(M,z,\tau):=\overline{\chi}_{-y}(M,\mathcal{E}(T_{M})),

\displaystyle\Ell(M,W)

\displaystyle:=\Ell(M,W,z,\tau):=\overline{\chi}_{-y}(M,\mathcal{E}(T_{M})\otimes W),

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(V)

\displaystyle:=\bigotimesop\displaylimits_{n=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{-yq^{n}}V^{\vee}\otimes\Lambda_{-y^{-1}q^{n}}V\otimes\operatorname{Sym}_{q^{n}}(V\oplus V^{\vee}).

Let

\theta(z,\tau):=-iq^{\frac{1}{8}}(y^{\frac{1}{2}}-y^{-\frac{1}{2}})\prodop\displaylimits_{n>0}(1-q^{n})(1-q^{n}y)(1-q^{n}/y)

be the classical Jacobi theta function, where we write
y=e^{2\pi iz}
,
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
.
Let
c(M)=\prodop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}(1+x_{j})
be a formal splitting of the total Chern class of
M
. Putting

\ELL(M):=\ELL(M,z,\tau)=\prodop\displaylimits_{j}x_{j}\frac{\theta(\frac{x_{j}}{2\pi i}-z,\tau)}{\theta(\frac{x_{j}}{2\pi i},\tau)}\in H^{*}(X,\mathbb Q)[y^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}][[q]],

it
follows from the definitions and the Riemann-Roch theorem that

\Ell(M)=\intop\nolimits_{M}\ELL(M),\quad\Ell(M,W)=\intop\nolimits_{M}\ELL(M)\operatorname{ch}(W).

2.3. Beauville-Bogomolov quadratic form

Let
X
be a compact holomorphic symplectic manifold of dimension
\dim(X)=2m
.
We briefly review some properties of the Beauville-Bogomolov quadratic form
q_{X}:H^{2}(X)\to\mathbb Q
on
X
from
[
Huy99
, Sections 1.9–1.11]
.
Note that the odd Chern classes of
X
vanish.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any
\beta\in H^{4k}(X,\mathbb Q)
in the sub-algebra generated by the Chern classes of
X
, there is a constant
c(\beta)\in\mathbb Q
, such that for all
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb Q)

\intop\nolimits_{X}\beta\alpha^{\dim(X)-2k}=c(\beta)q_{X}(\alpha)^{m-k}.

The quantity
c(\beta)
is invariant under deformation of
X
.

Corollary 2.2
.

There exists a polynomial
h_{X}=h_{X}(z)
with coefficients in
\mathbb Q[y^{\pm 1}][[q]]
such that

\intop\nolimits_{X}\ELL(X)\exp(\alpha)=h_{X}(q_{X}(\alpha)),\quad\hbox{for all }\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb Z).

The polynomial
h
is invariant under deformation of
X
.

3. The case
r=0
.

In this section we will prove Theorem
1.1
.
We start by reviewing some of the ideas and definitions of
[
BL05
]
.
For a Kawamata log-terminal pair
(Z,D)
of a projective variety and a divisor
D
in
Z
with an action of a finite group
G
,
Borisov and Libgober define in
[
BL05
, Definition 3.6]
the orbifold elliptic class
\Ell_{\orb}(Z,D,G)=\Ell_{\orb}(Z,D,G,z,\tau)
.
In fact they first define it in
[
BL05
, Definition 3.2]
in case
X
is nonsingular and the pair
(X,E)
is also
G
-normal (see
[
BL05
, Definition 3.1]
for the definitions) by an explicit formula.
In the general case, they define in
[
BL05
, Definition 3.6]

\Ell_{\orb}(Z,D,G):=\rho_{*}\Ell_{\orb}(X,E,G)
for
\rho:(X,E)\to(Z,D)
a
G
-normal equivariant resolution.
We will write
\Ell_{\orb}(Z,G)
,
\Ell_{\orb}(Z,D)
,
\Ell_{\orb}(Z)
in case
D=0
and/or
G
is the trivial group.

If
X
is a nonsingular projective variety, with an action of a finite group
their formula specializes to

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Ell_{\orb}(X,G):=\frac{1}{|G|}\sumop\displaylimits_{gh=hg}\sumop\displaylimits_{Z}[Z]\Big(\prodop\displaylimits_{\lambda(g)=\lambda(h)=0}\prodop\displaylimits_{j}x_{\lambda,j}\Big)\prodop\displaylimits_{\lambda,j}\frac{\theta(\frac{x_{\lambda,j}}{2\pi i}+\lambda(g)-\tau\lambda(h)-z)}{\theta(\frac{x_{\lambda,j}}{2\pi i}+\lambda(g)-\tau\lambda(h))}e^{2\pi i\lambda(h)z}.

Here
Z
runs over the irreducible components of the common fixpoint set of
g
and
h
, and
[Z]
is the class of
Z
in the Chow group of
X
.
The restriction of
T_{X}
to Z splits into linearized bundles according to the
[0,1)
-valued characters
\lambda
of
\langle g,h\rangle
. We denote by
x_{\lambda,j}
the elements of a formal splitting of the total Chern class of the bundle with character
\lambda
.
If
G
acts effectively on
(Z,D)
and
(Z/G,D/G)
is the quotient pair in the sense of
[
BL05
, Definition 2.7]
, they show in
[
BL05
, Theorem. 5.3]
that

\psi_{*}\Ell_{\orb}(Z,D)=\Ell_{\orb}(Z/G,D/G)
for the quotient morphism
\psi:Z\to Z/G
. This is in particular true for the pairs
(Z,0)
,
(Z/G,0)
, if
Z
is nonsingular and
G
is acting freely in codimension
1
.

Now consider the action of
\mathfrak S_{n}
on
S^{n}
, and recall the quotient morphism
\eta:S^{n}\to S^{(n)}
and the Hilbert Chow morphism
\pi:S^{[n]}\to S^{(n)}
.
As
S^{[n]}
is nonsingular we have
\ELL(S^{[n]})=\Ell_{\orb}(S^{[n]})
. As
\pi
is a crepant resolution,
[
BL05
, Theorem 3.5]
implies that
\pi_{*}\ELL(S^{[n]})=\Ell_{\orb}(S^{(n)})
.
Thus we find by the above

(3.2)

\pi_{*}\ELL(S^{[n]})=\Ell_{\orb}(S^{(n)})=\eta_{*}\Ell_{\orb}(S^{n},\mathfrak S_{n}).

Now let
L
be a line bundle on
S
, then we have

\displaystyle\Ell(S^{[n]},\mu(L))

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{S^{[n]}}\ELL(S^{[n]})\operatorname{ch}(\mu(L))

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{S^{(n)}}\pi_{*}\big(\ELL(S^{[n]})\big)\operatorname{ch}(L_{n})

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{S^{n}}\Ell_{\orb}(S^{n},\mathfrak S_{n})\operatorname{ch}\Big(\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}pr_{i}^{*}L\Big).

In the second line we have used
\pi^{*}(L_{n})=\mu(L)
and the projection formula, and in the third line we have used (
3.2
),
\eta^{*}(L_{n})=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}pr_{i}^{*}L
and again the projection formula.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}
be the Chern roots of
T_{S}
. Then

(3.3)

\begin{split}&\Ell(S,L)=\intop\nolimits_{S}(1+L+\frac{L^{2}}{2})\prodop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{2}x_{i}\frac{\theta(\frac{x_{j}}{2\pi i}-z,\tau)}{\theta(\frac{x_{j}}{2\pi i},\tau)}\\
&=\Ell(S)-\frac{K_{S}L}{2}\mathrm{Coeff}_{x^{1}}\left(x\frac{\theta(\frac{x}{2\pi i}-z,\tau)}{\theta(\frac{x}{2\pi i},\tau)}\right)^{2}+\frac{L^{2}}{2}\mathrm{Coeff}_{x^{2}}\left(x\frac{\theta(\frac{x}{2\pi i}-z,\tau)}{\theta(\frac{x}{2\pi i},\tau)}\right)^{2}\\
&=\Ell(S)+\frac{K_{S}L}{2}y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\phi_{-2,1}+\frac{L^{2}}{2}\phi_{-2,1}.\end{split}

Now we prove Theorem
1.1
by adapting the proof of
[
BL05
, Theorem 6.1]
. Note that in the notations of
[
BL05
]
we have
D=0
, which leads to many simplifications.

Let
(g,h)
be a commuting pair in
\mathfrak S_{n}
. We sum up the description of the action of
g,h
and their fixpoint sets in the proof of
[
BL05
, Theorem 6.1]
.
We have a decomposition
\{1,\ldots,n\}=J_{1}\cup\ldots\cup J_{m}
into the orbits of the subgroup generated by
g,h
. Thus
the action of
(g,h)
on
S^{n}
restricts to an action on each of the corresponding products
S^{J_{l}}
.
Furthermore we can write
|J_{l}|=a_{l}b_{l}
for positive integers
a_{l},b_{l}
, and up to reordering of the elements of
J_{l}
the action of
(h,g)
on
S^{J_{l}}
can be described as follows. Write
(y_{i,j})_{i\in\mathbb Z/a_{l}Z,\ j\in\mathbb Z/b_{l}\mathbb Z}
the components of elements
of
y\in S^{J_{l}}
. Then the action of
g,h
on
S^{J_{l}}
is given by
h(y_{i,j})=y_{i,j+1}
,
g(y_{i,j})=y_{i+1,j}
for
0\leq i<a_{l}-1
,
g(y_{a_{l}-1,j})=y_{0,j+s}
, for some
s\in\{0,\ldots,b_{l}-1\}
, and
s
determines the
action of
(g,h)
on
J_{l}
uniquely.
The fixpoint set
(S^{J_{l}})^{g,h}
is
S
embedded via the diagonal map
j_{l}:S\to S^{J_{l}}
.

Changing their notation slightly, we denote for
(a,b,s):=(a_{l},b_{l},s_{l})
by

4. The case of Hilbert schemes of points on K3 surfaces

Now we want to consider the case of Hilbert schemes of points on K3 surfaces. We obtain a formula for all
r
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
S
be a K3 surface and
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)
. Then

\Ell(S^{[n]},\mu(L)\otimes E^{r})=\mathrm{Coeff}_{p^{n}}\left[{\big(\mathbf{L}^{(2,0)}(-\phi_{-2,1},p)\big)^{\frac{L^{2}}{2}-r^{2}(n-1)}\mathbf{L}(-2\phi_{0,1},p)}\right].

Proof.

The Hilbert scheme
S^{[n]}
is a holomorphic symplectic manifold; let
q_{S^{[n]}}
be its Beauville-Bogolomov quadratic form.
By Corollary
2.2
there exists a polynomial
h_{S^{[n]}}(z)
with coefficients in
\mathbb Q[y^{\pm 1}][[q]]
such that

(4.1)

\Ell(S^{[n]},M)=h_{S^{[n]}}\big(q_{S^{[n]}}(c_{1}(M))\big),\quad\hbox{for all }M\in\operatorname{Pic}(S^{[n]}).

It is shown in
[
Bea83
, lem. 9.1]
that for
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)
we have

(4.2)

q_{S^{[n]}}(L+rE)=L^{2}-2r^{2}(n-1).

Therefore Proposition
4.1
follows from Theorem
1.1
.∎

Now we want to deduce Theorem
1.3
from Proposition
4.1
.
We know

\Ell(S^{[n]},\mu(L)\otimes E^{r})=\intop\nolimits_{S^{[n]}}\ELL(S^{[n]})\operatorname{ch}(\mu(L)\otimes E^{r}),

where
\ELL
is the genus associated to a power series, and
\mu(L)\otimes E^{r}=\det((L+(r-1)\mathcal{O}_{S}))^{[n]}
.
Therefore
[
EGL01
, Theorem 4.2]
applies and gives the following.

Corollary 4.2
.

For every
r\in\mathbb Z
, there are universal power series
F_{r,1},F_{r,2},F_{r,3},F_{r,4}\in\mathbb Q[y^{\pm 1}][[q,p]]
such that for every smooth projective surface
S
and every

L\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)
we have

\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}\Ell(S^{[n]},\mu(L)\otimes E^{r})p^{n}=F_{r,1}^{L^{2}/2}F_{r,2}^{LK_{S}/2}F_{r,3}^{K_{S}^{2}}F_{r,4}^{\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S})}.

Using Corollary
4.2
, in order to prove Theorem
1.3
, we only need show the formulas for
F_{r,1}
and
F_{r,4}
, which
are determined by their values for
S
a K3 surface.
So let again
S
be a K3 surface, and
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)
.
Then by Proposition
4.1
we get

\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}\Ell(S^{[n]},\mu(L)\otimes E^{r})p^{n}=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}p^{n}\mathrm{Coeff}_{p^{n}}\left[\big(\mathbf{L}^{(2,0)}(-\phi_{-2,1})(p)^{\frac{L^{2}}{2}-(n-1)r^{2}}\mathbf{L}(-2\phi_{0,1})(p)\big)\right].

Thus Theorem
1.3
follows from the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
g(p)=1+\sumop\displaylimits_{i\geq 1}a_{i}p^{i}
be a power series starting with
1
, let
f(p)
be a power series.
Fix
w,k\in\mathbb Q
. Put

h(p):=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}p^{n}\mathrm{Coeff}_{p^{n}}\big[g(p)^{w-kn}f(p)\big].

Then

h(p)=\frac{g(z)^{w}f(z)}{1+kz\frac{d}{dz}\log(g(z))}\quad\hbox{where }p=zg(z)^{k}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
w=0
(otherwise replace
f(p)
by
g(p)^{w}f(p)
),
and
k=1
(otherwise replace
g(p)
by
g(p)^{k}
and note that
z\frac{d}{dz}\log(g(z)^{k})=kz\frac{d}{dz}\log(g(p))
).
We can describe
h(p)
as follows: for a variable
u
write
g(p)^{-u}f(p)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}h_{n}(u)p^{n}
, with
h_{n}(u)
a polynomial in
u
, then
h(p)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}h_{n}(p\frac{d}{dp})p^{n}
, i.e. move all factors of
u
to the left and then
replace
u
by
p\frac{d}{dp}
.
We make the variable transformation
p=e^{x}
, so that
p\frac{p}{dp}=\frac{d}{dx}
, and we write
g(p)=e^{a(x)}
,
f(p)=\phi(x)
.
Then we obtain

\displaystyle h(p)

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\big(a(x)^{n}\phi(x)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi(\eta)}{1+a^{\prime}(\eta)}\Big|_{\eta=x-a(\eta)}

\displaystyle=\frac{f(z)}{1+z\frac{d}{dz}\log g(z)}\Big|_{p=zg(z)}.

In the second line we have used the Lagrange inversion formula

\sumop\displaylimits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\big(a(x)^{n}f(x)\big)=\frac{f(z)}{1-a^{\prime}(z)}\Big|_{z=x+a(z)}.

In the third line we put
z:=e^{\eta}
, thus
p=e^{x}=e^{\eta+a(\eta)}=zg(z)
.
∎

5. Moduli of sheaves on K3 surfaces

In this section we extend our results to moduli spaces of sheaves on K3 surfaces.
First we briefly recall determinant line bundles on moduli spaces of sheaves, for details see e.g.
[
GNY09
, Section 1.1]
,
[
HL10
, Chapter 8]
.

For a Noetherian scheme
Y
denote by
K(Y)
and
K^{0}(Y)
the Grothendieck groups of coherent sheaves and locally free sheaves.
If
Y
is nonsingular and projective, then
K(Y)=K^{0}(Y)
. We denote by
[\mathcal{F}]
the class of a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
in
K(Y)
.
For a proper morphism
f:Z\to Y
the pushforward
f_{!}:K(Z)\to K(Y)
is defined by
f_{!}([\mathcal{F}]):=\sumop\displaylimits_{i}(-1)^{i}[R^{i}f_{*}\mathcal{F}]
.
For any morphism
f:Z\to Y
the pullback
f^{*}:K^{0}(Y)\to K^{0}(Z)
is defined by
f^{*}[\mathcal{F}]=[f^{*}\mathcal{\mathcal{F}}]
for
\mathcal{F}
a locally free sheaf on
Y
.

Now let
S
be a smooth projective surface. On
K(S)
there is a quadratic form
(u,v)\mapsto\chi(S,u\otimes v)
to be denoted by
\chi(u\otimes v)
. Classes

u,v\in K(S)
are called numerically equivalent if
u-v
is in the radical of this quadratic form.
Let
K(S)_{\num}
be the set of numerical equivalence classes.
Let
c\in K(S)_{\num}
. For a flat family
\mathcal{E}
of coherent sheaves on
S
of class
c\in K(S)_{\num}
parametrized by a scheme
T
, let
q:S\times T\to S
,
p:S\times T\to T
be the projections
and define

\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}:K(S)\to\operatorname{Pic}(S)
by the composition

K(S)\xrightarrow{q^{*}}K^{0}(S\times T)\xrightarrow{\otimes[\mathcal{E}]}K^{0}(S\times T)\xrightarrow{p_{!}}K^{0}(T)\xrightarrow{\det^{-1}}\operatorname{Pic}(T).

For
c\in K_{\num}(S)
let
K_{c}:=\big\{v\in K(S)\bigm|\chi(v\otimes c)=0\big\}
. Let
H\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)
be ample, denote
M^{H}_{S}(c)
the moduli space of
H
-semistable sheaves of class
c
.
Assume that
H
is general, i.e. if
\mathcal{F}\in M^{H}_{S}(c)
is strictly
H
-semistable, then it is strictly semistable for all
H^{\prime}
in a neighbourhood of
H
.
Then there exists a homomorphism
\lambda:K_{c}\to\operatorname{Pic}(M^{H}_{S}(c))
, such that if
\mathcal{E}
is a flat family of coherent sheaves on
S
of class
c
parametrized by
T
,
then
\phi_{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(\lambda(v))=\lambda_{\mathcal{E}}(v)
, where
\phi_{\mathcal{E}}:T\to M^{H}_{S}(c)
is the classifying morphim associated to
\mathcal{E}
.
For a class
v\in K(S)
denote

\operatorname{vd}(v):=2\operatorname{rk}(v)c_{2}(v)-(\operatorname{rk}(v)-1)c_{1}(v)^{2}-(\operatorname{rk}(v)^{2}-1)

the expected dimension of
M^{H}_{S}(v)
.
We obtain the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
S
be a K3 surface and
c\in K_{\num}(S)
, with
r(c)>0
or with
r(c)=0
and
c_{1}(c)
nef and big.
Let
H
ample on
S
such that
M^{H}_{S}(c)
only consists of stable sheaves.
Assume furthermore
\operatorname{vd}(c)>1
. Then

\displaystyle\Ell(M^{H}_{S}(c),\lambda(v))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Coeff}_{p^{\frac{\operatorname{vd}(c)}{2}}}\left[{\big(\mathbf{L}^{(2,0)}(-\phi_{-2,1},p)\big)^{\frac{\operatorname{vd}(v)}{2}-1}\mathbf{L}(-2\phi_{0,1},p)}\right],

\displaystyle\overline{\chi}_{-y}(M^{H}_{S}(c),\lambda(v))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Coeff}_{p^{\frac{\operatorname{vd}(c)}{2}}}\left[\frac{1}{\widetilde{\Delta}(p,y)}\prodop\displaylimits_{n>0}\left(\frac{(1-p^{n})^{2}}{(1-p^{n}y)(1-p^{n}/y)}\right)^{n^{2}(\frac{\operatorname{vd}(v)}{2}-1)}\right].

Proof.

We adapt the arguments in
[
GNY09
, Section 1.5]
.
The Mukai lattice of
S
is
H^{*}(S,\mathbb Z)
with the symmetric bilinear form

\langle w,w^{\prime}\rangle=\intop\nolimits_{S}(c_{1}c_{1}^{\prime}-ra^{\prime}-r^{\prime}a),\quad w=(r,c_{1},a),\ w^{\prime}=(r^{\prime},c_{1}^{\prime},a^{\prime}).

Let
\phi:K(S)\to H^{*}(S,\mathbb Z)
be the homomorphism defined by
\phi(E)=\operatorname{ch}(E)\sqrt{\mathrm{td}(S)}.
Let
w:=\phi(c)
, then
\phi
induces a injective homomorphism
\phi:K_{c}\to w^{\perp}
.
There is a homomorphism
\theta_{w}:w^{\perp}\to H^{2}(M_{S}^{H}(c),\mathbb Z)
, such that
\theta_{w}(\phi(v))=c_{1}(\lambda(v))
for all
v\in K_{c}
.
We have assumed that
\operatorname{rk}(c)>0
or that
\operatorname{rk}(c)=0
and
c_{1}(c)
is nef and big, furthermore
\operatorname{vd}(c)>1
, and
M^{H}_{S}(c)
consists only of stable sheaves.
Under these assumptions we know (
[
GNY09
, Theorem 1.14]
,
[
Yos01a
]
,
[
Yos01b
]
), that
M^{H}_{S}(c)
is an irreducible symplectic manifold which is deformation equivalent to
S^{[\operatorname{vd}(c)/2]}
,

\theta_{w}:w^{\perp}\to H^{2}(M_{S}^{H}(c),\mathbb Z)
is surjective, and for
x
in
w^{\perp}
we have
\langle x,x\rangle=q_{M^{H}_{S}(c)}(\theta_{w}(x))
.

Let
v\in K_{c}
.
By Corollary

we have

\Ell(M^{H}_{S}(c),\lambda(v))=\intop\nolimits_{M^{H}_{S}(c)}\ELL(M^{H}_{S}(c))\exp(c_{1}(\lambda(v)))=h_{M^{H}_{S}(c)}\big(q_{M^{H}_{S}(c)}(\theta_{w}(c_{1}(\lambda(v))))\big).

As
M^{H}_{S}(c)
is deformation equivalent to

We compute

q_{M^{H}_{S}(c)}\big(\theta_{w}(c_{1}(\lambda(v)))\big)=\langle\phi(v),\phi(v)\rangle=\operatorname{vd}(v)-2.

Thus we get by Proposition
4.1
and (

),(

\Ell(M^{H}_{S}(c),\lambda(v))=h_{S^{[\operatorname{vd}(c)/2]}}(\operatorname{vd}(v)-2)=\mathrm{Coeff}_{p^{\frac{\operatorname{vd}(c)}{2}}}\left[{\mathbf{L}^{(2)}(-\phi_{-2,1},p)^{\frac{\operatorname{vd}(v)}{2}-1}\mathbf{L}(-2\phi_{0,1},p)}\right].

∎

In order to express this formula in terms of generating functions we denote the line bundles
\lambda(v)
on different moduli spaces

in a unified way, generalizing our notation on Hilbert schemes of points.

Notation 5.2
.

For fixed
c\in K(S)_{\num}
we write
s=\operatorname{rk}(c)
,
c_{1}=c_{1}(c)
,
c_{2}=c_{2}(c)
. Let us denote by

and

, and denote
\operatorname{vd}:=\operatorname{vd}(c)
.
Let

.
If

divides

\mu(L)\otimes E^{r}\in\operatorname{Pic}(M^{H}_{S}(c))\in\operatorname{Pic}(M^{H}_{S}(c))
by

for

with

(5.1)

\operatorname{rk}(v)=r,\quad c_{1}(v)=M,\quad c_{2}(v)=\frac{M^{2}}{2}+2r+\frac{Mc_{1}}{s}+\frac{r}{s}\big(\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{2}-c_{2}\big).

Note that the condition on

, so that

Remark 5.3

(1)

When

(see e.g.
[

]
,
[

]
).

(2)

When

on

under the identification

, for any first Chern class

.

(3)

If all sheaves in

are slope stable, then twisting by a line bundle

\phi_{A}:M^{H}_{S}(c)\to M^{H}_{S}(c\otimes A)

Now we prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem

.

Fix

\operatorname{rk}(c)=s>0
,
c_{1}(c)=c_{1}
,
c_{2}(c)=c_{2}
, fix

, let

, denote by

, and assume that

divides

.
Let

fullfill (

\mu(L)\otimes E^{r}=\lambda(v)

Plugging the relations

into the formula

\operatorname{vd}(v)=L^{2}+2-\frac{r^{2}}{s^{2}}(\operatorname{vd}(c)-2).

Thus
Proposition

gives

\displaystyle\Ell(M^{H}_{S}(c),\mu(L)\otimes E^{r})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Coeff}_{p^{\operatorname{vd}(c)/2}}\left[\big(\mathbf{L}^{(2,0)}(-\phi_{-2,1},p)\big)^{\frac{L^{2}}{2}-\frac{r^{2}}{s^{2}}(\operatorname{vd}(c)/2-1)}\mathbf{L}(-2\phi_{0,1},p)\right]

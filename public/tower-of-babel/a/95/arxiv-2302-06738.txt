Regularity of minimizing p p -harmonic maps into spheres and sharp Kato inequality
Katarzyna Mazowiecka, Michał Miśkiewicz

Abstract
. We study regularity of minimizing p p -harmonic maps u : B 3 → 𝕊 3 u\colon B^{3}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{3} for p p in the interval [ 2 , 3 ] [2,3] . For a long time, regularity was known only for p = 3 p=3 (essentially due to Morrey [ 24 ] ) and p = 2 p=2 (Schoen–Uhlenbeck [ 29 ] ), but recently Gastel [ 7 ] extended the latter result to p ∈ [ 2 , 2 + 2 15 ] p\in[2,2+\frac{2}{15}] using a version of Kato inequality. Here, we establish regularity for a small interval p ∈ [ 2.961 , 3 ] p\in[2.961,3] by combining Morrey’s methods with Hardt and Lin’s Extension Theorem [ 11 ] . We also improve on the other result by obtaining regularity for p ∈ [ 2 , p 0 ] p\in[2,p_{0}] with p 0 = 3 + 3 2 ≈ 2.366 p_{0}=\frac{3+\sqrt{3}}{2}\approx 2.366 . In relation to this, we address a question posed by Gastel and prove a sharp Kato inequality for p p -harmonic maps in two-dimensional domains, which is of independent interest.

1.
Introduction

The object of our study are minimizing
p
-harmonic maps, which are defined as minimizers of the Dirichlet
p
-energy

(1.1)

E_{p}(u)\coloneqq\int_{\mathcal{M}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}V\qquad\text{for }u\in W^{1,p}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})

with fixed boundary data
u|_{\partial\mathcal{M}}
, where
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{N}
are smooth Riemannian manifolds,
\mathcal{M}
has a non-empty boundary and
\mathcal{N}
is closed. The regularity theory of these maps started with studying the more classical case
p=2
, in which they are simply called
minimizing harmonic maps
. The classical result of Morrey
[
24
]
states that all minimizing harmonic maps are regular if
n=2
,
n
being the domain dimension. In the seminal paper
[
28
]
Schoen and Uhlenbeck showed that in higher dimensions, these maps are regular in the interior outside of a singular set of Hausdorff dimension at most
n-3
. Moreover, the singular set is discrete if
n=3
.

The result of Schoen and Uhlenbeck
[
28
]
is sharp: for
3
-dimensional domains minimizing harmonic maps may have point singularities, as shown by the “hedgehog” map

u\colon B^{3}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{2},\quad u(x)=\frac{x}{|x|};

see, e.g.,
[
2
]
for the proof of minimality. Similar examples are available for general
n\geq 3
, showing that the singular set may indeed be
(n-3)
-dimensional.

Since singularities may appear, one has to be careful with the statement of the problem. Postponing a detailed discussion until Section
2
, let us mention that we assume
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
to be isometrically embedded, and
W^{1,p}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
is defined as the set of all
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying
u(x)\in\mathcal{N}
a.e. Moreover, since the regularity considerations are local in nature, we may assume for simplicity — as is customary in the literature — that
\mathcal{M}=\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a Euclidean domain.

Although the cited result is sharp in general, regularity properties of harmonic maps depend crucially on the geometry of the target manifold
\mathcal{N}
. For example, solutions are regular if
\mathcal{N}
has non-positive sectional curvature
[
28
]
, see also related earlier works
[
5
,
12
]
. In a subsequent paper Schoen and Uhlenbeck
[
29
]
studied the case when the target manifold is a sphere
\mathcal{N}={\mathbb{S}}^{k}
. Their result, combined with a further improvement of Okayasu
[
27
]
, gives:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
29
]
,
[
27
]
)
.

Any minimizing harmonic map
u\colon B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{k}
is regular whenever
n\leq d(k)
, where

d(k)=\begin{cases}k&\text{ for }2\leq k\leq 5\\
5&\text{ for }6\leq k\leq 9\\
6&\text{ for }k\geq 10.\end{cases}

If we specialize this result to the case
n=k
, we deduce that all minimizing harmonic maps

u\colon B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}

must be regular for
n=2,3,4,5
. It is an open problem whether minimizing harmonic maps
u\colon B^{6}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{6}
are continuous. In higher dimensions, Jäger and Kaul
[
18
]
proved that the (singular) equatorial map defined by

v\colon B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n},\qquad v(x)\coloneqq\left(\frac{x}{|x|},0\right)

is a minimizing harmonic map if and only if
n\geq 7

[
18
]
. In particular, the equatorial map is
not
a minimizing harmonic map for
n=6
.

Let us return to
p
-harmonic maps for general exponent
p
. Here, regularity theory for minimizing
p
-harmonic maps was developed in analogy to the case
p=2
, at least to some extent. Hardt and Lin
[
11
]
showed that minimizing
p
-harmonic maps are regular outside of a singular set, which in general has Hausdorff dimension at most
n-\lfloor p\rfloor-1
, and is discrete in the case
n=\lfloor p\rfloor+1
. In particular, these maps are regular when
p\geq n
; note that this special case can also be obtained by an adaption of Morrey’s classical argument.

This result may be improved for special target manifolds, as shown by Nakauchi
[
26
]
and Xin–Yang
[
31
]
. In the case of the target manifold being a sphere we have

Theorem 1.2

(
[
26
,
31
]
)
.

Any minimizing
p
-harmonic map
u\colon B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{k}
is regular whenever
n\leq d(k,p)
, where

d(k,p)=\begin{cases}\lfloor p\rfloor+1&\text{ for }p+2\leq k<p+4\\
\lfloor p\rfloor+2&\text{ for }p+4\leq k<p+6\\
\min\{\lfloor p\rfloor+3,\lambda(p)\}&\text{ for }p+6\leq k<p+8\\
\min\{\lfloor p\rfloor+4,\lambda(p)\}&\text{ for }p+8\leq k,\end{cases}

and
\lambda(p)=p+2\sqrt{1-\frac{p-2}{(p-1)^{2}}}+\frac{2}{p-1}
.

Unfortunately, this estimate is not optimal and in particular it leaves an open question whether minimizing
p
-harmonic maps are regular for
u\colon B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
and
p\in[n-1,n)
.

In a recent paper Gastel
[
7
]
was exploiting the connection between the theory of Cosserat micropolar elasticity and
p
-harmonic maps. He showed that the only obstacle for the regularity of minimizers in a geometrically nonlinear Cosserat model for micropolar elasticity of continua is the possible existence of non-constant
p
-minimizing tangent maps from
B^{3}
into
{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
.

In this setting Gastel improved
Theorem

1.2
by showing that all minimizing
p
-harmonic maps from
B^{3}
to
{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
must be regular when
2\leq p\leq\frac{32}{15}\approx 2.133
. Combined with the regularity theory of Hardt and Lin
[
11
]
, it implies that minimizing
p
-harmonic maps from
B^{3}
to
{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
must be regular for all
p
in
[2,\frac{32}{15}]\cup[3,\infty)
. Let us mention that Gastel’s results were extended to the case of stable-stationary solutions by Li and Wang
[
19
]
.

Our main goal is to study the regularity of minimizing
p
-harmonic maps from
B^{3}
to
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
. We obtain:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p_{0}=\frac{3+\sqrt{3}}{2}\approx 2.366
and
p_{1}=3-\frac{17-24\ln(2)}{3^{3/2}2^{5/6}}\approx 2.961
. If
p\in[2,p_{0}]\cup[p_{1},3]
, then any minimizing
p
-harmonic map
u\colon B^{3}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
is regular.

This result improves on the previous work of Gastel and Hardt–Lin. Interestingly, it leaves a gap
(p_{0},p_{1})
of length approximately
0.6
, in which regularity is still an open problem. It is our belief that that regularity holds for these values of
p
as well, and we provide certain motivation by showing a certain “counterexample candidate” fails (see
Lemma

3.1
). However, the problem of regularity in this interval may be as difficult as determining whether minimizing harmonic mappings
u\colon B^{6}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{6}
must be regular.

In the following, we decided to break
Theorem

1.3
into two separate statements:
Theorem

5.1
for
p\in[2,p_{0}]
and
Theorem

4.1
for
p\in[p_{1},3]
. The first reason for this is that the two regimes require very different techniques. And the second, that in both cases the method of proof actually leads to a more general result.

Proof outline for
Theorem

1.3
.

Regularity for
p=3
follows along the lines of Morrey’s proof
[
24
]
. Given a minimizing
u\colon B^{3}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
and considering its restriction
v\coloneqq u\big\rvert_{\partial B_{r}}
to a sphere (
r\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
), we may extend
v
to a map
\overline{v}\colon B_{r}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
with
p
-energy controlled by that of
v
. For a generic slice, the latter is controlled by the
p
-energy of
u
on
B_{1}\setminus B_{1/2}
and hence minimality gives

\int_{B_{1/2}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla\overline{v}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{1/2}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

The hole-filling trick now leads to polynomial decay of
E_{p}(u)
on smaller and smaller balls. In other words,
\nabla u
lies in a Morrey space
L^{p,\lambda}
with some small
\lambda>0
, which implies continuity of
u
.

The crucial part of this argument is the ability to extend
v
with energy control, and Morrey’s construction of this extension does not apply for
p<3
. However, there is another construction available in the literature: Hardt-Lin’s extension theorem
[
11
, Theorem 6.2]
. This more refined topology-sensitive tool applies with a uniform constant for all
p\in[2,3]
, thus enabling us to proceed with the argument. In result, we obtain
\nabla u\in L^{p,\lambda}
for each such
p

with uniform
\lambda>0
. If only
p\in(3-\lambda,3]
, such regularity is enough to conclude the continuity of
u
. The argument we give in
Section

4
is slightly different: we decided to exploit the notion of tangent maps, which simplifies the exposition and lets us keep track of constants more easily.

The method we employ in the regime
p\in[2,\frac{3+\sqrt{3}}{2}]
dates back to Schoen and Uhlenbeck
[
29
]
. For simplicity, we will only discuss the classical example
p=2
here and mention other authors’ contributions when necessary. The proof follows by excluding the existence of non-constant tangent maps, i.e., minimizers with symmetry
u(\lambda x)=u(x)
for
\lambda>0
. Such maps are determined by their restriction to
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
, and Schoen and Uhlenbeck derived the following estimates:

\frac{1}{3}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{4}-\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2},\quad\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{4}-\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}.

One can combine these two by the simple pointwise inequality
|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\leq|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}
, leading to an inequality of the form

A\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{4}+B\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq 0.

If only
A,B>0
, this would prove that
u
is necessarily constant, and indeed an analogous argument works for maps into
{\mathbb{S}}^{4},{\mathbb{S}}^{5},{\mathbb{S}}^{6},\ldots
. However, here we have
A=-\frac{1}{6}<0
and additional insight is needed: Okayasu
[
27
]
noted that for harmonic maps, the pointwise inequality is improved to
|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}
— what we call a Kato inequality. That way, one obtains
A=\frac{1}{12}
and
B=\frac{1}{4}
, which completes the proof (note that Schoen and Uhlenbeck covered the case of maps
B^{3}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
too, but with entirely different methods).

Gastel
[
7
]
followed the same line of reasoning for
p>2
, and to this end he proved a non-optimal Kato inequality
|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\leq\kappa(p)|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}
, with
\kappa(p)\xrightarrow{p\to 2}\frac{1}{2}
. That way, regularity is still obtained for
p
close to
2
(one can check numerically that the argument covers
p\leq\frac{32}{15}\approx 2.133
). Our proof differs from Gastel’s in two ways. First, in the context of maps
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
we obtain a Kato inequality with the
optimal
constant, which by itself would enlarge the regularity interval to
p\leq\frac{5+\sqrt{17}}{4}\approx 2.281
. Second, we note a possible — quite technical in nature — improvement in one of the other inequalities, which enables us to cover all
p\leq\frac{3+\sqrt{3}}{2}\approx 2.366
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In
Section

2
we review the notions of
p
-harmonic maps that we will be using. As a motivation to study the regularity problem, in
Section

3
we prove that the natural candidate for a singular
p
-harmonic map — the equatorial map — is not minimizing
p
-harmonic when
p\in[2,3)
. In
Section

4
we show how the extension theorem of Hardt and Lin
[
11
]
can be used to obtain regularity of maps into
\mathcal{N}={\mathbb{S}}^{3}
for exponents
p
close to
3
. Next, in
Section

5
we modify Gastel’s argument
[
7
]
, which is itself based on the original idea of Schoen and Uhlenbeck
[
29
]
, and establish regularity for
p\in[2,\frac{3+\sqrt{3}}{2}]
. Finally, in
Section

6
we discuss sharp Kato inequality for
p
-harmonic maps in
2
-dimensional domains, as well as its relation to the regularity problem, and comment on the inequality in higher dimensions. We close the paper with
Appendix

, in which explicit constant estimates are given for the inequalities used in
Section

4
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Weakly, stationary, and stable
p
-harmonic maps

In the world of non-smooth solutions, it is important to distinguish between different classes of
p
-harmonic maps. We will only consider minimizing and stable stationary
p
-harmonic maps in the sequel, but we also define weakly
p
-harmonic maps for a complete picture. To keep it simple, we restrict here to maps from an open domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
into
{\mathbb{S}}^{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A (weakly)
p
-harmonic map
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{S}}^{k})
is defined as a critical point of the
p
-energy with respect to variations in the range, i.e.,

(2.1)

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg\rvert_{t=0}E_{p}(u_{t})=0\quad\text{where}\quad u_{t}=\frac{u+t\varphi}{|u+t\varphi|}

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k+1})
. Equivalently, as a solution the Euler–Lagrance equation

-\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=|\nabla u|^{p}u\quad\text{weakly}.

Moreover, it is called:

•

stationary
if it is also a critical point with respect to variations of the domain, i.e.,

(2.2)

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg\rvert_{t=0}E_{p}(u_{t})=0\quad\text{where}\quad u_{t}(x)=u(x+t\xi(x))

for all
\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

•

stable
if it the energy has a non-negative second variation at
u
:

(2.3)

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\bigg\rvert_{t=0}E_{p}(u_{t})\geq 0\quad\text{where}\quad u_{t}=\frac{u+t\varphi}{|u+t\varphi|},

or equivalently

(2.4)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-|\nabla u|^{2}|\varphi|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+(p-2)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-4}\left(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq 0

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k+1})
satisfying
\varphi(x)\cdot u(x)=0
a.e.

•

minimizing
if
E_{p}(u)\leq E_{p}(v)
for all
v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{S}}^{k})
with the same trace on
\partial\Omega
.

We have the following inclusions between the different classes of
p
-harmonic maps:

\text{minimizing}\subset\text{stable stationary}\subset\text{stationary}\subset\text{weak}.

We note here that in the case
k>2p-1
stable
p
-harmonic maps are quasiminimizers
[
14
, Proposition 2]
.

2.2.
Tangent maps

Definition 2.2
.

A
p
-harmonic map
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathcal{N}
is called
a tangent map
if it is
0
-homogeneous, meaning that
u(\lambda x)=u(x)
for all
\lambda>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, we say it is a
p
-minimizing tangent map if it minimizes the
E_{p}
-energy (
1.1
) on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Most of the regularity theory of minimizing harmonic maps relies on the study of minimizing tangent maps. The following theorem of Hardt and Lin (which is a generalization of
[
28
, Theorem IV]
) allows us to deduce regularity by ruling out the existence of non-constant tangent maps.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
11
, Theorem 4.5]
)
.

Assume that the only
p
-minimizing tangent maps from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into
\mathcal{N}
are constant maps. Then all minimizing
p
-harmonic maps
u\colon\Omega\to\mathcal{N}
on
n
-dimensional domains are regular.

Remark 2.4
.

Using the compactness lemma for stable-stationary
p
-harmonic maps
[
15
, Lemma 4.3]

the latter theorem may be improved to the following:

Assume
p<3
. If every stable-stationary
p
-harmonic tangent map from
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to
{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
is constant then every stable-stationary
p
-harmonic map
u\colon B^{3}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
is regular.

3.
Motivation: the equatorial map

In this section we will study the equatorial map
v\colon B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
defined by

v(x)\coloneqq\left(\frac{x}{|x|},0\right).

It is easy to verify that
v
is weakly
p
-harmonic for any
p<n
. For
p\in(n-2\sqrt{n-1},n)
, we will show that it is not a minimizing
p
-harmonic map because it is not stable. As mentioned in the introduction, the map
v
above is an important counterexample to regularity in many cases. Hence, our result motivates an attempt to prove that minimizing
p
-harmonic maps
B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
in general are regular for
p
close to
n
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The equatorial map
v
is unstable for
p\in(n-2\sqrt{n-1},n)
.

In particular,
v
is unstable for every
p\in[2,3]
when
n\leq 6
. Let us compare this result with minimality of the “hedgehog” map
\frac{x}{|x|}\colon B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
, for which we have:

Theorem 3.2
.

The map
\frac{x}{|x|}\colon B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
is a minimizing
p
-harmonic map for every
1\leq p<n
.

Theorem

3.2
is due to Jäger–Kaul
[
18
]
in the case
p=2
,
n\geq 7
; Brezis–Coron–Lieb
[
2
]
for
n=3
,
p=2
; Coron–Gulliver
[
4
]
when
p=1,2,\ldots,n-1
; Lin
[
21
]
for
n\geq 3
,
p=2
; Hardt–Lin–Wang
[
10
]
in the case
p\in[n-1,n)
; Hong
[
13
]
when
1<p\leq n-1
.

Let us also recall that for
p=2
we have

Theorem 3.3

(
[
18
, Theorem 2, §3]
)
.

The equatorial map
\left(\frac{x}{|x|},0\right)\colon B^{n}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
is minimizing harmonic if and only if
n\geq 7
.

Proof of
Lemma

3.1
.

We follow the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 2]
.

Assume on the contrary that
v
satisfies the stability inequality (
2.4
) for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({B}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
satisfying
v(x)\cdot\varphi(x)=0
a.e. Choosing

\varphi(x)\coloneqq\eta(|x|)e_{n+1},

where
\eta\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
, we easily have
v(x)\cdot\varphi(x)=0
. Additionally we have
\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi=0
as
\varphi
is radial and
v
is constant on the radii. Since
|\nabla v(x)|^{2}=\frac{n-1}{|x|^{2}}
, the stability inequality for our choice of
\varphi
would give

(3.1)

\int_{B^{n}}|x|^{2-p}\left((\eta^{\prime}(|x|))^{2}-\frac{n-1}{|x|^{2}}\eta^{2}(|x|)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq 0,

which after integrating over spheres
S_{r}
is equivalent to

(3.2)

\int_{0}^{1}r^{n-p+1}\left((\eta^{\prime}(r))^{2}-\frac{n-1}{r^{2}}\eta^{2}(r)\right)\,\mathrm{d}r\geq 0.

In order to obtain a contradiction, we will construct a non-zero function
\eta\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the ODE

(3.3)

\begin{cases}\eta^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{n-p+1}{r}\eta^{\prime}(r)+\frac{n-1-\varepsilon}{r^{2}}\eta(r)=0&\text{ for }r\in(r_{0},1),\\
\eta(r_{0})=\eta(1)=0.\end{cases}

for some small parameters
\varepsilon(p,n),r_{0}(p,n)>0
. Assuming that we have a solution
\eta
of (
3.3
) extended by zero to
[0,r_{0}]
, integration by parts leads to

(3.4)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}&r^{n-p+1}\left((\eta^{\prime}(r))^{2}-\frac{n-1}{r^{2}}\eta^{2}(r)\right)\,\mathrm{d}r\\
&=-\int_{r_{0}}^{1}r^{n-p+1}\eta(r)\left(\eta^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{n-p+1}{r}\eta^{\prime}(r)+\frac{n-1}{r^{2}}\eta(r)\right)\,\mathrm{d}r\\
&=-\varepsilon\int_{r_{0}}^{1}r^{n-p-1}{\varepsilon}\eta^{2}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r<0,\end{split}

which contradicts (
3.2
).

It remains to prove that there is a non-zero solution to (
3.3
). Using the substitution
\zeta(t)=\eta(e^{t})
we arrive at the ODE

(3.5)

\zeta^{\prime\prime}(t)+(n-p)\zeta^{\prime}(t)+(n-1-\varepsilon)\zeta(t)=0,

whose solutions are explicit:

\zeta(t)=\begin{cases}c_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}t}+c_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}t}&\text{ if }\Delta>0,\\
e^{\lambda t}(c_{1}+c_{2}t)&\text{ if }\Delta=0,\\
e^{\lambda t}(c_{1}\cos(\mu t)+c_{2}\sin(\mu t))&\text{ if }\Delta<0,\end{cases}

depending on the sign of the determinant
\Delta_{\varepsilon}=(n-p)^{2}-4(n-1-\varepsilon)
. Note however that the boundary conditions
\zeta(\ln r_{0})=\zeta(0)=0
force
\zeta
to be identically zero when
\Delta_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
.

Thus, we restrict to the range
p\in(n-2\sqrt{n-1},n+2\sqrt{n-1})
, in which we have
\Delta_{0}<0
. Fixing small
\varepsilon(n,p)>0
, we still have
\Delta_{\varepsilon}<0
, and thus
\zeta(t)=e^{\lambda t}\sin(\mu t)
is a solution of (
3.5
) for some suitable
\lambda,\mu
(
\lambda
may be small but positive). We now choose
r_{0}\in(0,1)
so that
\ln r_{0}
is a negative multiple of
\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}
, in which case
\zeta
satisfies the boundary conditions and produces a solution of (
3.3
):

\eta(r)=r^{\frac{p-n}{2}}\sin(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\varepsilon}/4}\cdot\ln r).

As discussed earlier, Kato inequalities are an important tool in studying regularity of

Definition 6.1
.

Let

, and consider a

\kappa(p,n,d)>0

(6.1)

\kappa(p,n,d)|\nabla|\nabla u|(0)|^{2}\leq|\nabla^{2}u(0)|^{2}

Remark 6.2

For

Remark 6.3

Lemma 6.7

The optimal Kato constant

in (

Proof.

We follow the proof strategy of

, suitably adapting some technical details.

We may choose an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for which the partial derivatives
u_{1},u_{2}
are orthogonal, and then choose an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in which
u_{1}=(\alpha_{1},0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
u_{2}=(0,\alpha_{2},0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Denoting the desired constant as
\kappa(p):=\min(2,1+(p-1)^{2})
, the Kato inequality
\kappa(p)|\nabla|\nabla u|(0)|^{2}\leq|\nabla^{2}u(0)|^{2}
decouples into two separate inequalities:

(6.4)

\begin{split}\kappa(p)(\alpha_{1}u_{11}^{1}+\alpha_{2}u_{12}^{2})^{2}&\leq(u_{11}^{1})^{2}+2(u_{12}^{2})^{2}+(u_{22}^{1})^{2},\\
\kappa(p)(\alpha_{2}u_{22}^{2}+\alpha_{1}u_{12}^{1})^{2}&\leq(u_{22}^{2})^{2}+2(u_{12}^{1})^{2}+(u_{11}^{2})^{2}.\end{split}

The (non-negative) terms
\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\sum_{k=3}^{d}(u_{ij}^{k})^{2}
of
|\nabla^{2}u(0)|^{2}
will not appear on the right-hand side of the inequality (

). For the sake of clarity, we shall focus on proving the first inequality of (
6.4
).

Note that the first equation of the
p
-harmonic system

0=u_{11}^{1}+u_{22}^{1}+(p-2)\alpha_{1}(\alpha_{1}u_{11}^{1}+\alpha_{2}u_{12}^{2})

can be used to express
u_{22}^{1}
in terms of
u_{11}^{1},u_{12}^{2}
. Introducing auxiliary variables
x:=u_{11}^{1}
,
y=u_{12}^{2}
and then translating to rotated variables

,

, we transform the desired inequality to the form

\displaystyle\kappa(p)z^{2}

\displaystyle\leq x^{2}+2y^{2}+(x+(p-2)\alpha_{1}z)^{2}

\displaystyle=2z^{2}+2w^{2}-x^{2}+(x+(p-2)\alpha_{1}z)^{2}

\displaystyle=(2+p(p-2)\alpha_{1}^{2})z^{2}+2(p-2)\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}zw+2w^{2}.

Now we need to distinguish between the two cases:

Case 1:
p>2
,
\kappa(p)=2
.
Here, the inequality simplifies further to

0\leq p\alpha_{1}^{2}z^{2}+2\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}zw+\frac{2}{p-2}w^{2}.

As a quadratic form in
z
and
w
, the right-hand side has the discriminant
4\alpha_{1}^{2}(\alpha_{2}^{2}-\frac{2p}{p-2})
, which is nonpositive for all
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in[0,1]
and

. Hence, the inequality always holds.

Case 2:
1\leq p\leq 2
,
\kappa(p)=1+(p-1)^{2}
.
The inequality takes the form

(1+(p-1)^{2})z^{2}\leq(2+p(p-2)\alpha_{1}^{2})z^{2}+2(p-2)\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}zw+2w^{2},

which is easiest to analyse as a quadratic inequality in

. Indeed, it can be rewritten as
(1-\alpha_{1}^{2})z^{2}\cdot p^{2}+ap+b\leq 0
(for some coefficients
a,b
). Since the leading coefficient
(1-\alpha_{1}^{2})z^{2}
is nonnegative, by convexity it is enough to consider the edge cases:

p=1
and
p=2
. If only one recalls the equality
\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}=1
, both cases are trivial to check (the first one reduces to
(\alpha_{2}z-\alpha_{1}w)^{2}+(1+\alpha_{2}^{2})w^{2}\geq 0
, the second one to

). This finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem A.1
.

Let

.

Proof.

Let us denote

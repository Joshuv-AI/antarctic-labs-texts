Z

.

6.
Moore-Gibson-Thompson-type equations

In this subsection we present boundary-initial value problems associated with a Moore-Gibson-Thompson equation with fractional damped and strongly damped linear wave equation, where our results from previous section can be applied. Namely, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geqslant 3
, be a bounded smooth domain, and the initial-boundary value problems

(6.1)

\begin{cases}u_{ttt}-\Delta u+\eta(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{3}}u_{tt}+\eta(-\Delta)^{\frac{2}{3}}u_{t}=f(u),&\ t>0,\ x\in\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=\varphi(x),\ u_{t}(0,x)=\xi(x),\ u_{tt}(0,x)=\psi(x),&\ x\in\Omega,\\
u(t,x)=0,&\ t\geqslant 0,\ x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\eta>1
.

The nonlinearity
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
in (
6.1
) is a continuously differentiable function satisfying for some
1<\rho<\frac{3N+4}{3N-8}
the growth condition

(6.2)

|f^{\prime}(s)|\leqslant C(1+|s|^{\rho-1}).

Here we consider
X=L^{2}(\Omega)
and the negative Laplacian operator

Au=-\Delta u,

with domain

D(A)=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

which is a sectorial operator and it bounded imaginary powers, and consequently the spaces
X^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in[0,1]
, are characterized with the aid of complex interpolation as

X^{\alpha}=[H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),L^{2}(\Omega)]_{\alpha}=H^{2\alpha}_{\{I\}}(\Omega)

and

X^{-\alpha}=(H^{2\alpha}_{\{I\}}(\Omega))^{\prime}

where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\alpha}
denotes the complex interpolation function (see
[
2
]
and
[
13
]
). In particular
X=X^{0}=L^{2}(\Omega)
,
X^{\frac{1}{2}}=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
X^{-\frac{1}{2}}=(H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))^{\prime}
and
X^{1}=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

With this set-up we will consider problem (
6.1
) in the form (
2.8
)-(
2.9
) with
u_{0}=\varphi,v_{0}=A^{\frac{1}{3}}\varphi+\xi
, and
w_{0}=A^{\frac{1}{3}}\xi+\psi

Let
F:Z_{-1}^{1}\to Z_{-1}^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\geqslant 0

2.5
). Recall that a mild solution of (

v_{0}\\
w_{0}\end{smallmatrix}\Big]+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(t-s)}F\Big(\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}u\\
v\\
w\end{smallmatrix}\Big]\Big(s,\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}u_{0}\\
v_{0}\\
w_{0}\end{smallmatrix}\Big]\Big)\Big)ds,

for

. We say that (

)-(
2.9

Z_{-1}^{1}
is for any
\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}u_{0}\\
v_{0}\\

As a consequence of the Sobolev embeddings we obtain the following result cf.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain of class
C^{m}
and
(A,D(A))
be a sectorial operator in

m\geqslant 1
. Then for
\alpha\in[0,1]
the following inclusion holds.

X^{\alpha}\subset W^{s,q}(\Omega)

if
2m\alpha-\frac{N}{p}\geqslant s-\frac{N}{q}
,
1<p\leqslant q<\infty
,

.

From we have

Theorem 6.2
.

The problem (
2.8
)-(
2.9
) with
\eta>1
is locally posed in
Z_{-1}^{1}
whenever
f
satisfies (
6.2
) for some
1<\rho<\frac{3N+4}{3N-8}

Proof:
The map
F
defined as in (
2.5
) is Lipschitz continuous on bounded sets from
Z_{-1}^{1}
into
Z_{-1}^{\sigma}=X^{\frac{1+\sigma}{3}}\times X^{\frac{\sigma}{3}}\times X^{\frac{-1+\sigma}{3}}
whenever
0<\sigma\leqslant\tilde{\sigma}
, and
\tilde{\sigma}=\min\{1,(\rho-1)(2-\frac{3}{4}N)+1\}
(

B
is a bounded subset of

\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}u_{1}\\
v_{1}\\

Since
L^{\frac{6N}{3N+4(1-\sigma)}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{\frac{2(1-\sigma)}{3}}_{\{I\}}(\Omega)=X^{\frac{-1+\sigma}{3}}
we obtain

\Big\|F\Big(\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}u_{1}\\
v_{1}\\
w_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\Big]\Big)-F\Big(\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}u_{2}\\
v_{2}\\
w_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\Big]\Big)\Big\|_{Z_{-1}^{\sigma}}\leqslant c_{2}\|f(u_{1})-f(u_{2})\|_{L^{\frac{6N}{3N+4(1-\sigma)}}(\Omega)}

and thanks to (

) there exists

such that

\forall s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R},\quad|f(s_{1})-f(s_{2})|\leqslant C|s_{1}-s_{2}|(1+|s_{1}|^{\rho-1}+|s_{2}|^{\rho-1})

and consequently

\begin{split}\Big\|F\Big(\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}u_{1}\\

&\leqslant c_{4}\Big\|\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}u_{1}\\

An exponentially shrinking problem
Mumtaz Hussain, Junjie Shi

Abstract
. The Jarník-Besicovitch theorem is a fundamental result in metric number theory which concerns the Hausdorff dimension for some limsup sets. We investigate a related problem of estimating the Hausdorff dimension of a liminf set. Let τ > 0 \tau>0 and { q j } j ≥ 1 \{q_{j}\}_{j\geq 1} be a sequence of integers. We calculate the Hausdorff dimension of the set Λ d 𝜽 ( τ ) = { 𝐱 ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) d : ∥ q j x i − θ i ∥ < q j − τ for all j ≥ 1 , i = 1 , 2 , ⋯ , d } , \Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau)=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1)^{d}:\|q_{j}x_{i}-\theta_{i}\|<q_{j}^{-\tau}\ \text{for all }j\geq 1,i=1,2,\cdots,d\right\}, where ‖ ⋅ ‖ \left\|\cdot\right\| denotes the distance to the nearest integer and 𝜽 ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) d {\boldsymbol{\theta}}\in[0,1)^{d} is fixed. We also give some heuristics for the Hausdorff dimension of the corresponding multiplicative set ℳ d 𝜽 ( τ ) = { 𝐱 ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) d : ∏ i = 1 d ∥ q j x i − θ i ∥ < q j − τ for all j ≥ 1 } . \mathcal{M}_{d}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\tau)=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1)^{d}:\prod_{i=1}^{d}\|q_{j}x_{i}-\theta_{i}\|<q_{j}^{-\tau}\ \text{for all }j\geq 1\right\}.

Introduction

How well a real number can be approximated by rational numbers is a fundamental problem in metric number theory. Dirichlet’s theorem (1842) states that for any
\mathbf{x}:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in[0,1)^{d}
and any
Q\in\mathbb{N}
, there exixts
1\leq q\leq Q
such that

\|qx_{i}\|\leq Q^{-\frac{1}{d}},\quad 1\leq i\leq d,

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|
denotes the distance to the nearest integer. A consequence of Dirichlet’s theorem is that for
\mathbf{x}\in[0,1)^{d}
, there exists infinitely many
q\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(1)

\|q\mathbf{x}\|:=\max\left\{\|qx_{1}\|,\ldots,\|qx_{d}\|\right\}\leq q^{-\frac{1}{d}}.

Naturally, one can ask what happens when the right hand side of (
1
) is replaced by some general function
\psi:\mathbb{N}\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\psi(q)\to 0
as
q\to\infty
. This setup, called the set of
\psi
-approximable numbers, is defined as

W_{d}(\psi)=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1)^{d}:\|q\mathbf{x}\|<\psi(q)\ \text{for infinitely many }q\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Results on the metrical size of
W_{d}(\psi)
are well-known. These include fundamental results such as Khintchine’s theorem (1924) and Jarník’s theorem (1932) on the Lebesgue measure and Hausdorff measure of
W_{d}(\psi)
respectively. For recent advances in this area, we refer the reader to the recent breakthrough result of Maynard-Koukouloupolous
[
3
]
for the measure of
W_{1}(\psi)
without the monotonicity assumption on
\psi
.

It should be clear that any point
\mathbf{x}
in the limsup set
W_{d}(\psi)
can be well approximated by infinitely many rational points, however there is no further information about the rational numbers approximating
\mathbf{x}
, the rational numbers are rather generic. So a natural question is “What is the size of the set
W_{d}(\psi)
if
infinitely many
is replaced by
for all
within the set
W_{d}(\psi)

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
h>\tau+1
. Then

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau)=\frac{d(1-\tau\alpha)}{\tau+1},

where

(2)

h=\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{\log q_{j+1}}{\log q_{j}},\ \ \ \alpha=\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{\log q_{1}+\cdots+\log q_{j-1}}{\log q_{j}}.

Remark 1.2
.

When
h\leq\tau+1
, the structure of the set
\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau)
is quite complex and, hence, poses a challenging question of the Hausdorff dimension of the
\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau)
for this case. We present two examples to highlight the complexity for the one dimensional homogeneous case only. Fix a real number
\tau>0
and let
\theta_{j}=0
for all
j\geq 1
.

(1)

Assume that the integer sequence
\{q_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
satisfies

\frac{1}{8}q_{j}^{\tau+1}\leq q_{j+1}\leq\frac{1}{4}q_{j}^{1+\tau},\ \ {\text{for all}}\ j\geq 1.

Then
h=1+\tau
. We claim that
\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau)

contains at most finitely many points.

More precisely, for each
j\geq 1
, let

E_{j}=\{x\in[0,1):\|q_{j}x\|<q_{j}^{-\tau}\}.

Then
E_{j}
consists of
q_{j}
intervals of maximum length
2q_{j}^{-1-\tau}
. At the same time, the intervals contained in
E_{j+1}
are separated by a gap at least
q_{j+1}^{-1}-2q_{j+1}^{-1-\tau}
.
Note that if
q_{1}
is large enough then for all
j\geq 1

\frac{2}{q_{j}^{1+\tau}}\leq\frac{1}{q_{j+1}}-\frac{2}{q_{j+1}^{1+\tau}}.

This implies that each interval inside
E_{j}
can intersect at most one interval in
E_{j+1}
. Thus
E_{j}\cap E_{j+1}
will be contained in at most
q_{j}
intervals of length
2q_{j+1}^{-1-\tau}
. Continuing in this way, we see that the set

E_{1}\cap E_{2}\cap\cdots\cap E_{j}

will be contained in at most
q_{1}
intervals of length
2q_{j}^{-1-\tau}
. Taking the limit as
j
tends to infinity shows the claim.

(2)

Let
\eta>1+\tau
. Define a sequence of integers
\{q_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
in the following way:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\liminf\limits_{j\to\infty}\frac{\log q_{j+1}}{\log q_{j}}=1+\tau,\ q_{j+1}\leq\frac{1}{4}q_{j}^{1+\tau},\ q_{j}|q_{j+1},&\hbox{when $j$ is even;}\\
\liminf\limits_{j\to\infty}\frac{\log q_{j+1}}{\log q_{j}}=\eta,&\hbox{when $j$ is odd.}\end{array}\right.

With the same argument as in the first example, we note that for all
j\geq 1
,

\displaystyle E_{2j}\cap E_{2j+1}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in[0,1):\|q_{2j}x\|<q_{2j+1}^{-\tau}\right\}

\displaystyle\approx\left\{x\in[0,1):\|q_{2j}x\|<q_{2j}^{-\tau(2+\tau)}\right\}.

By taking
\bar{q}_{j}=q_{2j}
, we have

\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau)=\Big\{x\in[0,1):\|\bar{q}_{j}x\|<(\bar{q}_{j})^{-\tau(2+\tau)},\ {\text{for all}}\ j\geq 1\Big\}.

This falls into the case that
h>1+\tau
for the sequence
\{\bar{q}_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
by noting that
h=\eta(1+\tau)
, and our new
\hat{\tau}\mapsto\tau(2+\tau)
. So
h=\eta(1+\tau)>(1+\tau)^{2}=\hat{\tau}+1
. Thus, by Theorem
1.1
we note that the set
\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau)

is of positive Hausdorff dimension.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We split the proof into two parts: the upper and lower bounds. For all
j\geq 1
, let

E_{j}=\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1)^{d}:\|q_{j}x_{i}-\theta_{i}\|<q_{j}^{-\tau}\ i=1,2,\cdots,d\}.

Then,

\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau)=\bigcap_{j\geq 1}E_{j}.

By viewing
[0,1]^{d}
as the unit torus, it is clear that
E_{j}
is a collection of squares:

E_{j}=\bigcup_{0\leq p_{i,j}<q_{j},1\leq i\leq d}\ \prod_{i=1}^{d}B\left(\frac{p_{i,j}+\theta_{i}}{q_{j}},\frac{1}{q_{j}^{1+\tau}}\right).

The following simple fact will be used in the upper and lower bound estimates below.

Fact: Let
(a,b)
be an interval in
\mathbb{R}
. For any
\theta\in[0,1)
and
q\in\mathbb{N}
, the interval
(a,b)
can contain

{\text{at most}}\ (b-a)\cdot q+2,\ \ \ {\text{and at least}}\ (b-a)q-2

shifted rationals
(p+\theta)/q
.

2.1.
The upper bound

It is trivial that
E_{1}\cap\cdots\cap E_{j}
is a cover for
\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau)
. Next we search for a suitable cover for
E_{1}\cap\cdots\cap E_{j}
.

To elaborate the main strategy, we first consider
E_{1}\cap E_{2}
. Note that
E_{1}
consists of
q_{1}^{d}
squares of side length
2q_{1}^{-1-\tau}
, and the squares in
E_{2}
are separated by a gap of at least
q_{2}^{-1}-q_{2}^{-1-\tau}\geq 1/(2q_{2})
in each coordinate axis. Thus each square in
E_{1}
can intersect at most

\Big(2q_{1}^{-1-\tau}\cdot 2q_{2}+2\Big)^{d}

squares in
E_{2}
. Hence, by the
Fact
stated at the beginning,
E_{1}\cap E_{2}
can be covered by

q_{1}^{d}\cdot\Big(2q_{1}^{-1-\tau}\cdot 2q_{2}+2\Big)^{d}

squares of side length
2q_{2}^{-1-\tau}
.

Thus by an iterative process,
E_{1}\cap\cdots\cap E_{j}
can be covered by at most

q_{1}^{d}\cdot\Big({2}q_{1}^{-1-\tau}\cdot 2q_{2}+2\Big)^{d}\cdots\Big({2}q_{j-1}^{-1-\tau}\cdot 2q_{j}+2\Big)^{d}

squares of side length
2q_{j}^{-1-\tau}
.

Recall the definition of
h
and the assumption
h>1+\tau
. For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
k_{o}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq k_{o}
,

4q_{k}^{-1-\tau}\cdot q_{k+1}+2\leq q_{k}^{-1-\tau}\cdot q_{k}^{1+\epsilon}.

As a result, for some constant
c=c(\epsilon)
, one has

\displaystyle q_{1}^{d}\cdot\Big(q_{1}^{-1-\tau}\cdot 2q_{2}+2\Big)^{d}\cdots\Big(q_{j-1}^{-1-\tau}\cdot 2q_{j}+2\Big)^{d}\leq\Big[c(\epsilon)\cdot q_{1}^{-\tau+\epsilon}\cdots q_{j-1}^{-\tau+\epsilon}\cdot q_{j}^{1+\epsilon}\Big]^{d}.

Thus,

\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau))\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\Big[c(\epsilon)\cdot q_{1}^{-\tau+\epsilon}\cdots q_{j-1}^{-\tau+\epsilon}\cdot q_{j}^{1+\epsilon}\Big]^{d}\cdot\left(q_{j}^{-s(\tau+1)}\right).

Therefore
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau))=0,
whenever

\displaystyle s>

\displaystyle\frac{d}{\tau+1}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{(-\tau+\epsilon)(\log q_{1}+\cdots+\log q_{j-1})+(1+\epsilon)\log q_{j}}{\log q_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{\tau+1}\Big(1+\epsilon-(\tau-\epsilon)\alpha\Big).

Hence, from the definition of Hausdorff dimension and the arbitrariness of
\epsilon>0
, it follows that

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau)\leq\frac{d(1-\tau\alpha)}{\tau+1}.

2.2.
The lower bound

The main tool in establishing the lower bound for the Hausdorff dimension of
\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau)
is the following well-known mass distribution principle
[
1
]
.

Proposition 2.1

(Mass Distribution Principle
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure supported on a measurable set
F
. Suppose there are positive constants
c
and
r_{0}
such that

\mu(B(x,r))\leq cr^{s}

for any ball
B(x,r)
with radius
r\leq r_{0}
and center
x\in F
. Then
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}F\geq s
.

To use this principle, we construct a suitable Cantor subset of
\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau)
satisfying conditions of the mass distribution principle.

•

Level one,
F_{1}
, of the Cantor subset is nothing but
E_{1}
, that is

F_{1}=\bigcup_{0\leq p_{i,1}<q_{1},1\leq i\leq d}\ \prod_{i=1}^{d}B\left(\frac{p_{i,1}+\theta_{i}}{q_{1}},\frac{1}{q_{1}^{1+\tau}}\right).

We also write

\mathcal{F}_{1}=\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{d}B\left(\frac{p_{i,1}+\theta_{i}}{q_{1}},\tfrac{1}{q_{1}^{\tau+1}}\right):0\leq p_{i,1}<q_{1},\ 1\leq i\leq d\right\},

for the collection of squares constituting
E_{1}
.

•

For level two of the Cantor subset we first count the number of squares in
E_{2}
contained in each square of
\mathcal{F}_{1}.
Let
B_{1}
be a square in
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and write it as

B_{1}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}B\left(\frac{p_{i,1}+\theta_{i}}{q_{1}},\frac{1}{q_{1}^{1+\tau}}\right):=\prod_{i=1}^{d}B_{i,1}.

by the
Fact
stated at the beginning, for any
i\in\{1,\cdots,d\},

\displaystyle\#P_{i,2}:

\displaystyle=\#\left\{0\leq p_{i,2}<q_{2}:B\left(\frac{p_{i,2}+\theta_{i}}{q_{2}},\frac{1}{q_{2}^{\tau+1}}\right)\cap B_{i,1}\neq{\diameter}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{1}^{\tau+1}}-2\geq\frac{q_{2}}{q_{1}^{\tau+1}}.

Thus, there are at least
[{q_{2}}\cdot{q_{1}^{-(\tau+1)}}]^{d}
squares contained within each square of
\mathcal{F}_{1}.
Choose a sub-collection of these squares with cardinality
[{q_{2}}\cdot{q_{1}^{-(\tau+1)}}]^{d}
and denote this sub-collection by
\mathcal{F}_{2}(B_{1})
. Then define

\mathcal{F}_{2}=\bigcup_{B_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{1}}\mathcal{F}_{2}(B_{1}),\ F_{2}=\bigcup_{B_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{2}}B_{2},

as the second level of the Cantor subset.
Note that every pair of squares in
E_{2}
is separated by a gap of at least
\tfrac{1}{2q_{2}},
so are those in
\mathcal{F}_{2}
.
This is because for any integer vectors

(p_{1,2},\cdots,p_{d,2})\neq(p^{\prime}_{1,2},\cdots,p^{\prime}_{d,2})
, at least for one index
i
,

\displaystyle\left|\prod_{i=1}^{d}B\left(\tfrac{p_{i,2}+\theta_{i}}{q_{2}},\tfrac{1}{q_{2}^{\tau+1}}\right),\prod_{i=1}^{d}B\left(\tfrac{p^{\prime}_{i,2}+\theta_{i}}{q_{2}},\tfrac{1}{q_{2}^{\tau+1}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\left|B\left(\tfrac{p_{i,2}+\theta_{i}}{q_{2}},\tfrac{1}{q_{2}^{\tau+1}}\right),B\left(\tfrac{p^{\prime}_{i,2}+\theta_{i}}{q_{2}},\tfrac{1}{q_{2}^{\tau+1}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\tfrac{p_{i,2}-p^{\prime}_{i,2}}{q_{2}}-\tfrac{2}{q_{2}^{\tau+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{q_{2}}-\tfrac{2}{q_{2}^{\tau+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{2q_{2}}.

Continuing in this way, we have a sequence
\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
consisting of squares such that

\mathcal{F}_{k}=\bigcup_{B_{k-1}\in\mathcal{F}_{k-1}}\mathcal{F}_{k}(B_{k-1}).

For each
B_{k-1}\in\mathcal{F}_{k-1}
, the family
\mathcal{F}_{k}(B_{k-1})
contains
[{q_{k}}\cdot{q_{k-1}^{-(\tau+1)}}]^{d}
squares of side length
q_{k}^{-(1+\tau)}
and separated by at least
\tfrac{1}{2q_{k}}
gap between them. Then define

\mathcal{F}_{k}=\bigcup_{B_{k}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}B_{k},\ {\text{and}}\ \mathcal{F}_{\infty}=\bigcap_{k\geq 1}F_{k},

as the
k
th level of the Cantor subset and the Cantor subset of
\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau)
respectively.

Next, we distribute the mass uniformly on the Cantor subset. For any
B_{k}\in\mathcal{F}_{k},
let
B_{k-1}
be the square in
\mathcal{F}_{k-1}
such that
B_{k}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(B_{k-1})
(when
k=1
, define
B_{0}=[0,1]^{d}
). Then define

\displaystyle\mu(B_{k})

\displaystyle=\mu(B_{k-1})\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{F}_{k}(B_{k-1})}

\displaystyle=\mu(B_{k-1})\left[\frac{q_{k}}{q_{k-1}^{\tau+1}}\right]^{-d}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left[\frac{q_{i-1}^{\tau+1}}{q_{i}}\right]\right)^{d}

\displaystyle\asymp{q_{k}^{-d}\cdot(q_{1}\cdots q_{k-1})^{d\tau}}.

Having defined the measure on the Cantor subset, we now calculate the Hölder exponent of the measure
\mu
, which determines how the measure of a ball is related to its diameter. At first, we consider the measure of balls in
\mathcal{F}_{k}
:

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\mu(B_{k})}{|B_{k}|}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{d\log q_{k}-d\tau(\log q_{1}+\cdots+\log q_{k-1})}{(\tau+1)\log q_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d(1-\alpha)}{\tau+1},

where
|B_{k}|
denotes the radius of the ball
B_{k}
. Thus for any
s<\frac{d(1-\alpha)}{\tau+1}
, there exists
k_{o}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq k_{o}

\mu(B_{k})\leq|B_{k}|^{s},\ \ {\text{for all}}\ B_{k}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}.

For any
x\in\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
and
r<1/(4q_{k_{o}})
, we consider the measure of the ball
B(x,r)
. Assuming the ball
B(x,r)
can intersect only one element in
\mathcal{F}_{k-1},
say
B_{k-1},
and at least two elements in
\mathcal{F}_{k}.
Hence all the elements in
\mathcal{F}_{k}
which can intersect
B(x,r)
are contained in
\mathcal{F}_{k}(B_{k-1}).
Since any two elements in
\mathcal{F}_{k}(B_{k-1})
are
\tfrac{1}{2q_{k}}
-separated, we have
r\geq\tfrac{1}{4q_{k}}.
Thus
k\geq k_{o}
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
r\leq|B_{k-1}|,
otherwise, we have

\mu(B(x,r))\leq\mu(B_{k-1})\leq|B_{k-1}|^{s}\leq r^{s}.

We count how many elements in
\mathcal{F}_{k}(B_{k-1})
which can intersect
B(x,r).
Note that all elements in
\mathcal{F}_{k}(B_{k-1})
are

\tfrac{1}{2q_{k}}
-separated, which mean that their
\tfrac{1}{4q_{k}}
-thickenings are still disjoint. Moreover, since
r\geq\tfrac{1}{4q_{k}},
all the
\tfrac{1}{2q_{k}}
-thickenings of these elements in
\mathcal{F}_{k}(B_{k-1})
are still contained in
B(x,8r).
Thus, a volume argument gives

\#\{B_{k}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(B_{k-1}):B_{k}\cap B(x,r)\neq{\diameter}\}\leq 16^{d}\cdot r^{d}\cdot q_{k}^{d}.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\mu(B(x,r))

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{\mu(B_{k-1}),\ \ 16^{d}\cdot r^{d}q_{k}^{d}\left(\tfrac{q_{k}}{q_{k-1}^{\tau+1}}\right)^{-d}\mu(B_{k-1})\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\mu(B_{k-1}),\ 16^{d}\cdot r^{d}q_{k-1}^{d(\tau+1)}\mu(B_{k-1})\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 16^{d}\cdot\left({q_{k-1}^{-(\tau+1)}}\right)^{s}\min\left\{1,r^{d}q_{k-1}^{d(\tau+1)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 16^{d}\cdot\left({q_{k-1}^{-(\tau+1)}}\right)^{s}\cdot 1^{(1-s/d)}\left(r^{d}q_{k-1}^{d(\tau+1)}\right)^{s/d}

\displaystyle=16^{d}\cdot r^{s}.

The Mass Distribution Principle implies that

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d}(\tau)\geq\frac{d(1-\alpha)}{\tau+1}.

This completes the proof.

3.
Concluding remarks and open problems

Finally, we list a couple of open problems which are worth investigating. The first problem may be termed as the weighted inhomogeneous exponentially shrinking problem. Fix
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}:=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{d})\in[0,1)^{d}
and
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{d})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
. Let the integer sequence
\{q_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
be as in (
2
). Then, what is the Hausdorff dimension of the set

\Lambda_{d}^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})=\left\{x\in[0,1)^{d}:\|q_{j}x_{i}-\theta_{i}\|<q_{j}^{-\tau_{i}}\ \text{for all }j\geq 1,i=1,2,\cdots,d\right\}?

We expect that the ideas presented in this paper combined with techniques in
[
5
]
may yield a solution to this problem. A more general problem may be to estimate the Hausdorff measure of

\Lambda_{d}^{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(\psi)=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1)^{d}:\|q_{j}x_{i}-\theta_{i}\|<\psi(q_{j})\ \text{for all }j\geq 1,i=1,2,\cdots,d\right\}

for some function
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

In another direction, the same problem may be considered within the multiplicative settings. For any
\tau>0
and fixed
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\in[0,1)^{d}
, consider the set

\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\tau)=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1)^{d}:\prod_{i=1}^{d}\|q_{j}x_{i}-\theta_{i}\|<q_{j}^{-\tau}\ \text{for all }j\geq 1\right\}.

We note that, since
\|q_{j}x_{1}-\theta_{1}\|<1
, we trivially have that

\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\theta_{1}}(\tau)\times[0,1)^{d-1}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\bf{\theta}}(\tau).

Hence giving the lower bound of the Hausdorff dimension to be

d-1+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\theta}(\tau)=d-1+\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\Lambda_{1}^{\theta}(\tau)=d-1+\tfrac{1-\tau\alpha}{\tau+1},

where

\alpha=\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{\log q_{1}+\cdots+\log q_{j-1}}{\log q_{j}},\quad\text{and}\quad\tau+1<h=\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{\log q_{j}}{\log q_{j-1}}.

So the main problem is to estimate the upper bound for
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\bf{\theta}}(\tau).
We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
h>\tau+1
, then

d-1+\tfrac{1-\tau\alpha}{\tau+1}\leq\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\tau)\leq d-1+\frac{1}{\tau+1}.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

For the upper bound, we proceed as in
[
2
]
. Let
h>0
and define the sequence of positive integers as in (
2
). Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1)^{d}:\prod_{i=1}^{d}\|q_{j}x_{i}-\theta_{i}\|<q_{j}^{-\tau}\ \text{for all }j\geq 1\right\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcap_{q\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{p\in\mathcal{Z}_{q}}\tfrac{1}{q_{j}}(\mathbf{p}+{\boldsymbol{\theta}}+M(q_{j}^{-\tau})),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{q_{j}}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:-1\leq p_{i}+\theta_{i}\leq q_{j}+1\quad\forall i=1,\ldots,d\}

\displaystyle M(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1)^{d}:\prod_{i}|x_{i}|\leq\gamma\}.

For each
q_{j}
, let
\{B_{{q_{j}},i}:i=1,\ldots,N_{q_{j}}\}
be the collection of
d
-dimensional hypercubes covering
M(q_{j}^{-\tau})
as in
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
. Then the collection

\left\{\tfrac{1}{q_{j}}(\mathbf{p}+{\boldsymbol{\theta}}+B_{q_{j},i}):q_{j}\in\mathbb{N},\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{Z}_{q_{j}}\right\}

is a fine cover for
\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\tau)
.
Therefore, the
s
-dimensional cost of the cover can be estimated as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathcal{M}_{d}(\tau))

\displaystyle=\liminf_{j\to\infty}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{Z}_{q_{j}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\left|\tfrac{1}{q_{j}}(\mathbf{p}+{\boldsymbol{\theta}}+B_{q_{j},i})\right|^{s}

\displaystyle\ll\liminf_{j\to\infty}q_{j}^{d}q_{j}^{-s}q_{j}^{-\tau(s-d+1)}

\displaystyle\ll\liminf_{j\to\infty}q_{j}^{d-s-\tau(s-d+1)}.

Hence, when
s>d-1+\tfrac{1}{\tau+1}
,
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathcal{M}_{d}(\tau))\to 0
. Thus

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{M}_{d}(\tau)\leq d-1+\tfrac{1}{\tau+1}.

Finally, note that the dimension bounds are not sharp. A careful covering argument may lead to the dimension to be

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{M}_{d}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\tau)=d-1+\tfrac{1-\tau\alpha}{\tau+1}.

Finally, it is worth noting that for
h
large enough
\alpha\approx\frac{1}{h}
. So for
\tau
large enough (
\tau+1\approx\tau
) with
\tau+1=h-\epsilon
, both the dimension bounds are close enough.

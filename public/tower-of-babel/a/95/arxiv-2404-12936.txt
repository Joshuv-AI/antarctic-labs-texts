A Shintani lift for rigid cocycles
Isabella Negrini

Abstract
. We construct a Shintani lift for rigid analytic cocycles of higher weight, attaching modular forms of half-integral weight to such cocycles. The expression for the Fourier coefficients of the modular form ℛ ​ 𝒮 ​ ( J ) \mathcal{RS}(J) attached to a cocycle J J is given in terms of the residues of J J , and shares a striking similarity with the expression for the coefficients of the classical Shintani lift 𝒮 ⁡ ( f ) \mathcal{S}(f) of an integral weight modular form f f . This work aligns with the ideas of the nascent p p - adic Kudla program and strengthens the analogy between rigid cocycles and modular forms.



Introduction

This work mirrors the classical theory of theta correspondences between automorphic forms in the new setting of rigid cocycles. As such, it fits into the emerging
p
-
adic Kudla program
initiated in
[
DGL
]
, one of whose goals is to build modular generating series from rigid cocycles.

Rigid cocycles were introduced in
[
DV1
]
with the idea to extend the theory of complex multiplication to real quadratic fields. They were initially defined as elements of the parabolic cohomology
H^{1}_{par}(\Gamma,M)
, where
\Gamma:=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])
and
M
is a
\Gamma
-module of certain functions on Drinfeld’s
p
-adic upper half-plane
\mathcal{H}_{p}
(see Section
1
for a detailed introduction on rigid cocycles). The values of rigid
meromorphic
cocyles were conjectured in
[
DV1
]
to be analogues of singular moduli for real quadratic fields. In
loc. cit.
and other works, rigid cocycles behave like modular forms or at least modular functions. As an example, in
[
DV2
]
certain rigid cocycles are seen as analogues of some modular functions with CM divisor by means of a rigid analytic Borcherds lift. In
[
Neg
]
, some rigid
analytic
cocycles play the role of integral weight modular forms in the construction of a rigid analytic Shimura lift. The present work strengthens this analogy between rigid cocycles and modular forms.

Our main result is as follows. Let
k\geq 0
be an integer. Given a suitable weight
2k
rigid cocycle
J
, we construct a modular form
\mathcal{RS}(J)
of weight
k+1/2
attached to
J
. In order to give a rough statement, consider the following set of binary quadratic forms

\mathcal{F}_{p}:=\{ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}|\;a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z};\;b^{2}-4ac>0;\;p\nmid a,p|b,p|c\},

and let
D(Q):=b^{2}-4ac
.

Theorem.

Given a suitable rigid cocycle
J
, we can construct a half-integral weight modular form
\mathcal{RS}(J)
as

\mathcal{RS}(J):=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{p}/\Gamma_{0}(p)}\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q)q^{D(Q)/p},

where the coefficient
\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q)
is obtained by pairing
Q
with a certain residue polynomial of
J
. Here
q:=e^{2\pi i\tau}
with
\tau\in\mathcal{H}
. The assignment
J\mapsto\mathcal{RS}(J)
is Hecke-equivariant away from
p
.

The residue polynomials mentioned in the statement will be defined in Section
1.1
, here we will limit ourselves to mention that they are
p
-adic counterparts for period polynomials of modular forms. A more precise, though still not completely accurate, statement of the theorem above will be given in Section
1.2.2
. For the fully rigorous statement, see Theorem
3.1
.
Note that
\mathcal{RS}(J)
is a classical modular form but its coefficients are written in terms of
J
, a
p
-adic object.

The series above gives the
rigid Shintani
map of the cocycle
J
, which is an analogue of the classical Shintani lift
\mathcal{S}
of
[
Shn
]
. The map
\mathcal{S}
associates a modular form of weight
k+1/2
to a modular form of weight
2k
, so in our analogy rigid cocycles play the role of integral weight modular forms. Moreover, the functions
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{RS}
have a similar construction: indeed, given
f
of weight
2k
, the Fourier coefficients of the Shintani lift
\mathcal{S}(f)
are given by period integrals of
f
. This analogy between residues of rigid cocycles and periods of modular forms was already exploited in
[
Neg
]
, where an analogue for rigid cocycles of the Shimura lift of
[
Shim
]
was contructed by providing a theta kernel function with coefficients in rigid cocycles. The rigid analytic analogues of
[
Neg
]
and of the present work behave similarly to their classical counterparts, and their constructions shed light on some parallels between the classical setting and that of rigid cocycles. In particular the coefficients of the modular form
\mathcal{RS}(J)
share a striking similarity with the ones of the Shintani lift
\mathcal{S}(f)
. For more details on the analogy between classical and rigid analytic Shimura and Shintani lifts, see Section
1.2
.

Both the present work and
[
Neg
]
fit into the
p
-adic Kudla program, the ideas of which were already present in
[
DV2
]
, as they construct modular generating series whose coefficients arise from rigid cocycles, and use them to define maps which are counterparts to theta correspondences in this new setting.

1.
Motivation and background

1.1.
Rigid cocycles

Let
\mathcal{H}_{p}
and
\Gamma
be as defined in the Introduction, and let
k\geq 0
be an integer. We will denote by
\mathcal{A}_{2k}
the additive group of rigid analytic functions on
\mathcal{H}_{p}
, the Drinfeld’s
p
-adic upper-half plane, with the weight
2k
action of the group
\Gamma
defined as

f|\gamma(z)=(cz+d)^{-k}f\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right),\quad\mbox{ for }f\in\mathcal{A}_{2k}\>\text{ and }\>\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma.

. For more details on
\mathcal{H}_{p}
and rigid functions see
[
DT
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A weight
2k
rigid analytic cocycle is an element of the
\mathbb{C}_{p}
-vector space
H^{1}_{par}(\Gamma,\mathcal{A}_{2k})
.

Here
H^{1}_{par}(\Gamma,\mathcal{A}_{2k})\subset H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathcal{A}_{2k})
is the parabolic cohomology of
\Gamma
, defined as usual (see for example
[
Hida
]
).
Although the definition above agrees with the one of
[
DV1
]
, in the present work we will adopt an equivalent definition given in terms of
\mathcal{A}_{2k}
-valued modular symbols. These are functions

m:\mathbb{P}_{1}(\mathbb{Q})\times\mathbb{P}_{1}(\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{2k}

such that

m\{r,s\}=-m\{s,r\}\>\>\>\>\text{ and }\>\>\>\>m\{r,s\}+m\{s,t\}=m\{r,t\},\qquad\mbox{ for all }r,s,t\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\mathbb{Q}).

Such a modular symbol is called
\Gamma
-invariant if

m\{\gamma r,\gamma s\}|\gamma=m\{r,s\},\mbox{ for all }\gamma\in\Gamma.

We will denote by
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
the
\mathbb{C}_{p}
vector space of
\Gamma
-invariant modular symbols with values in
\mathcal{A}_{2k}
. Lemmas 1.3 and 1.9 of
[
DV1
]
imply that there is an isomorphism between
H^{1}_{par}(\Gamma,\mathcal{A}_{2k})
and
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
, so we can give the following definition, which we will adopt for the rest of the paper, noting that it is much more explicit and convenient for computations.

Definition 1.2
.

A weight
2k
rigid analytic cocycle is an element of the
\mathbb{C}_{p}
-vector space
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
. This definition is equivalent to Definition
1.1
.

We will now define a rational structure on
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
using the
residues
of rigid cocycles. This is an anlogue of the rational structure on classical modular forms defined via their periods (see
[
KZ
]
). We need to recall from
[
Neg
]
the definition of the residue map on
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}
be the homogeneous polynomials with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
and degree
2k
, with the action of
\Gamma
defined as

(h|\gamma)(X,Y):=h(aX+bY,cX+dY),\>\>\>\>\>\>\text{for }h\in\mathcal{P}_{2k-2},\>\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma.

Denote by
\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})
the space of
\Gamma_{0}(p)
-invariant modular symbols with values in
\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be the Buhat-Tits tree of
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. This object can be seen as a
skeleton
for
\mathcal{H}_{p}
by means of a
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-equivariant reduction map
red:\mathcal{H}_{p}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
. We will not use any property of
\mathcal{T}
or the reduction map in the present work, but we will sometimes refer to the edges of
\mathcal{T}
. In particular,
e_{0}
will denote the
standard edge
of the Bruhat-Tits tree. Given
f
a rigid function and
e
an edge of
\mathcal{T}
, one can define the
annular residue

\Res_{e}(f)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
of
f
at
e
using the reduction map from
\mathcal{H}_{p}
to
\mathcal{T}
. For more on
\mathcal{T}
and its relationship with
\mathcal{H}_{p}
, see
[
DT
]
.

Definition 1.3
.

The residue map
\Res_{0}:\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})\rightarrow\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})
is defined as
\Res_{0}(J):=\mu
, where

(1)

\mu\{r,s\}(X,Y):=\sum_{i=0}^{2k-2}{2k-2\choose i}(-1)^{i}\Res_{e_{0}}(z^{2k-2-i}J\{r,s\}(z))X^{i}Y^{2k-i}.

Note that expression (
1
) will never be used in this paper and we only included it to provide some background. We are now ready to define a rational structure on
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
.

Definition 1.4
.

We denote by
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})^{(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})}
the rigid analytic cocycles
J
such that
\Res_{0}(J)
is a modular symbol with values in polynomials with coefficients in
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
. In other words,
J\in\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})^{(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})}
if and only if the numbers
\Res_{e_{0}}(z^{2k-2-i}J\{r,s\}(z))
are in
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
for
i=0,...,2k-2
.

Note that
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})^{(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})}
is not empty, as it contains the cocycles
J_{k,D}
of
[
Neg
]
(see
loc. cit.
, Theorem 4.1).

We will now define the space of
harmonic cocycles
on the edges of
\mathcal{T}
, which is used in some proofs of Section
2
and was used in
[
Neg
]
to give an alternate decription of
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
. For any
\Gamma
-module
\Omega
, the harmonic cocycles
C_{har}(\Omega)
are
\Omega
-valued functions on the edges of
\mathcal{T}
satisfying certain
harmonicity conditions
. More precisely any
c\in C_{har}(\Omega)
is such that

\sum_{s(e)=v}c(e)=0,

where the sum is taken over all the edges
e
having the same vertex
v=s(e)
as their starting point, and

c(\bar{e})=-c(e),

where
\bar{e}
is the edge obtained by flipping the orientation of
e
(i.e. by switching the starting and ending points of
e
). The action of
\Gamma
on
C_{har}(\Omega)
is given by

(c|\gamma)(e):=c(\gamma e)|\gamma.

For any edge of
\mathcal{T}
, the evaluation map
C_{har}(\Omega)\rightarrow\Omega
is defined as
ev_{e}(c):=c(e)
. We will work with harmonic cocycles with
\Omega=\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}
, and more precisely with modular symbols values in such harmonic cocycles.

Definition 1.5
.

We will denote by
\MS^{\Gamma}(C_{har}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}))
the space of
\Gamma
-invariant modular symbols with values in
C_{har}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})
.

It was shown in
[
Neg
]
that there is an isomorphism
ST:\MS^{\Gamma}(C_{har}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}))\rightarrow\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
, called the Schneider-Teitelbaum lift. Its inverse
\Res
is the generalized residue map, which we do not need to define here. We will limit ourselves to show the following diagram for the sake of clarity

(2)

\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})\xrightarrow{Res}\MS^{\Gamma}(C_{har}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}))\xrightarrow{ev_{e_{0}}}\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}),

where, with a slight abuse of notation,
ev_{e_{0}}
is the map induced on modular symbols by the
\Gamma_{0}(p)
-equivariant evaluation of harmonic cocycles at the edge
e_{0}
.

Remark 1.1
.

The residue map
\Res_{0}
of Definition
1.3
can be seen as
\Res_{0}=ev_{e_{0}}\circ\Res
(see Section 4 of
[
Neg
]
for more details). This will be used in the proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Finally, we can consider a different action on
\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}
, given by

(h\star\gamma)(X,Y):=h(dX-cY,-bX+aY),\>\>\>\>\text{ with }\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma.

Note that
h\star\gamma=h|(\gamma^{T})^{-1}
and let
S:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Then
(\gamma^{T})^{-1}=S^{-1}\gamma S
and we can pass from one action to the other with the following
\Gamma
-equivariant map

\displaystyle\alpha:(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2},|)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2},\star)

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle h|S.

We consider both actions in order to be consistent with the literature: indeed
[
Shn
]
and
[
Ste
]
use
\star
while the action used in
[
Neg
]
and in the literature on rigid cocycles is the
|
one. Consequently, the proofs involving polynomials in Section
3
use the
\star
action, while the definitions and proofs in Section
2
use the
|
one. This is not an issue, as the map
\alpha
is used in Section
3
to phrase everything in the
\star
notation.

As
\alpha
is
\Gamma
-equivariant, it induces a map on
\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})
, so let
\widetilde{\Res}_{0}:=\alpha\circ\Res_{0}
. We will often adopt the notation
\mu_{J}:=\Res_{0}(J)
and
\widetilde{\mu}_{J}:=\widetilde{\Res}_{0}(J)
.

1.2.
Classical and rigid analytic Shimura-Shintani lifts

In this Section we are going to recall some facts about the classical Shimura and Shintani lifts, and compare them to their analogues in the framework of rigid cocycles.

1.2.1.
The Shimura lift.

Let
k
be an even integer. Given a half-integral weight modular form
g\in S_{k+1/2}(\Gamma_{0}(4))
, Shimura (
[
Shim
]
) constructed an integral weight form
\mathcal{SH}(g)\in S_{2k}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
. The latter can be obtained by pairing
g
with a theta kernel function
\Omega_{k}
via the Petersson inner product. More precisely,
\Omega_{k}
is a function of two variables
z,\tau\in\mathcal{H}
, given by

(3)

\Omega_{k}(z,q):=\sum_{D>0}D^{k-1/2}f_{k,D}(z)q^{D},

where
q:=e^{2\pi i\tau}
and
f_{k,D}\in S_{2k}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
are the so-called
“Zagier forms”
defined in
[
Za
]
. The series
\Omega_{k}
is a half-integral weight modular form with coefficients in
S_{2k}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
, meaning that
\Omega_{k}\in S_{k+1/2}(\Gamma_{0}(4))\otimes S_{2k}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
. In simpler terms, the series
\Omega_{k}
belongs to
S_{k+1/2}(\Gamma_{0}(4))
as a function of
\tau
. Then, up to a multiplicative constant,

(4)

\mathcal{SH}(g)=\langle g,\Omega_{k}\rangle_{Pet}=\int_{\Gamma_{0}(4)\backslash\mathcal{H}}g(\tau)\overline{\Omega_{k}(-\overline{z},\tau)}v^{k-3/2}dudv,\quad\tau=u+iv.

In order to compare
\mathcal{SH}
with its rigid analytic analogue, it will be useful to give a different expression for (
4
). Let
\{g_{j}\}_{j=1,...,N}
be an orthonormal basis for
S_{k+1/2}(\Gamma_{0}(4))
, and let
g=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}g_{j}
and
\Omega_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}g_{j}\otimes f_{j}
with
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
and
f_{j}\in S_{2k}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
. Then we can write

(5)

\mathcal{SH}(g)=\langle g,\Omega_{k}\rangle_{Pet}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}f_{j}.

In
[
Neg
]
, an analogue
\mathcal{RSH}
of
\mathcal{SH}
was defined in the setting of rigid cocycles. More precisely, let
k
be odd now, then
\mathcal{RSH}:S_{k+1/2}^{(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})}(\Gamma(4p^{2}))\rightarrow\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
, where
S_{k+1/2}^{(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})}(\Gamma(4p^{2}))
are the modular forms of weight
k+1/2
and level
4p^{2}
whose Fourier coefficients are in
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
. The key ingredient for the construction of
\mathcal{RSH}
is a theta kernel function, i.e. a half-integral weight modular form with coefficients in rigid cocycles. To define this, begin with the formal
q
-series

(6)

\hat{\Omega}_{k}(q):=\sum_{D>0}D^{k-1/2}J_{k,D}q^{D}\in\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})\big[\big[q\big]\big],

where
J_{k,D}\in\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
are
p
-adic analogues of the Zagier forms (see Section 2 of
[
Neg
]
for their explicit definition). Consider the natural map

\Psi:S_{k+1/2}^{(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})}(\Gamma(4p^{2}))\otimes_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})\rightarrow\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})\big[\big[q\big]\big].

The main result of
[
Neg
]
is that

(7)

\hat{\Omega}_{k}\in\Psi\big(S_{k+1/2}^{(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})}(\Gamma(4p^{2}))\otimes_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})\big).

For more details see Theorems 6.1, 6.2, 6.3 in
loc. cit.
The preimage under
\Psi
of the series
\hat{\Omega}_{k}(q)
will be denoted by
\hat{\Omega}_{k}(\tau)
, where
\tau\in\mathcal{H}
.
Let now
\{g_{j}\}_{j=1,...,M}
be a basis of
S_{k+1/2}^{(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})}(\Gamma(4p^{2}))
and write

(8)

\hat{\Omega}_{k}(\tau)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}g_{j}(\tau)\otimes m_{j}

with
m_{j}\in\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
. Now let
g:=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\alpha_{j}g_{j}\in S_{k+1/2}^{(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})}(\Gamma(4p^{2}))
with
\alpha_{j}\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then in light of (
5
) it is natural to define

(9)

\mathcal{RSH}(g):=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\alpha_{j}m_{j}\in\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k}).

Comparing (
3
) and (
6
) as well as (
5
) and (
9
), we see that in our analogy rigid analytic cocycles play the role of modular forms.

1.2.2.
The Shintani lift.

In
[
Shn
]
Shintani constructed a map going from integral weight modular forms to half-integral weight forms. We are now going recall the classical Shintani lift for level
p
forms and compare it with our main result.

Let
\chi
be the Dirichlet character modulo
4p
defined as

(10)

\chi(d):=\Big(\frac{(-1)^{k}p}{d}\Big),\;\;d\in(\mathbb{Z}/4p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}.

Given a binary quadratic form
Q(x,y):=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}
, we will adopt the notation
Q:=[a,b,c]
. The discriminant of
Q
will be denoted by
D(Q)
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{p}
be the set of binary quadratic forms defined as

(11)

\mathcal{F}_{p}:=\{[a,b,c]|\;a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z};\;D(Q)>0;\;p\nmid a,p|b,p|c\}.

A matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma
acts on
Q(x,y)
by

(Q\star\gamma)(x,y):=Q(\delta x-\gamma y,-\beta x+\alpha y).

This action is compatible with the
\star
action on polynomials defined in Section
1.1
, and in the present section the action on
\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}
is the
\star
one.
Note that
\Gamma_{0}(p)
preserves
\mathcal{F}_{p}
. The stabilizer
\Gamma_{Q}
of
Q
in
\Gamma
is generated up to torsion by the
automorph

\gamma_{Q}
of
Q
, and
\gamma_{Q}\in\Gamma_{0}(p)
if
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{p}
(see
[
Shn
]
). We recall now the definition of the
Shintani cycle

C_{Q}
associated to
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{p}
, following
[
Shn
]
.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
\omega
be an arbitrary point in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
. The Shintani cycle associated to
Q:=[a,b,c]\in\mathcal{F}_{p}
is the oriented geodesic
C_{Q}:=(r_{Q},s_{Q})
in the upper half-plane, where

(r_{Q},s_{Q})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(x_{Q},y_{Q})&\text{if }D(Q)\text{ is a square},\\
(\omega,\gamma_{Q}(\omega))&\text{otherwise},\\
\end{array}\right.

with

(x_{Q},y_{Q})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Big(\frac{b+\sqrt{D_{Q}}}{2c},\frac{b-\sqrt{D_{Q}}}{2c}\Big)&\text{if }c\neq 0,\\
\big(\infty,\frac{a}{b}\big)&\text{if }c=0,\>b>0,\\
\big(\frac{a}{b},\infty\big)&\text{if }c=0,\>b<0.\\
\end{array}\right.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Shintani)

Let
f\in S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
and let
\chi
be as in (
10
).
Let

I_{k}(f,Q):=\int_{C_{Q}}f(\tau)Q(1,-\tau)^{k-1}d\tau,\quad\text{with }Q\in\mathcal{F}_{p}.

Then

\mathcal{S}(f):=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{p}/\Gamma_{0}(p)}I_{k}(f,Q)q^{D(Q)/p}

is a cusp form in
S_{k+1/2}(\Gamma_{0}(4p),\chi)
. Here
q:=e^{2\pi i\tau}
with
\tau\in\mathcal{H}
. The map

\mathcal{S}:S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))\rightarrow S_{k+1/2}(\Gamma_{0}(4p),\chi)

is
\bar{\mathbb{C}}
-linear and
\mathcal{S}(T_{m}(f))=T_{m^{2}}(\mathcal{S}(f))
for any
m
coprime with
p
.

We will now state our main result. Although more precise than the one in the Introduction, the statement below is still paraphrasing certain points. A more rigorous statement will be given in Theorem
3.1
, after having introduced all the necessary notation. Let
\langle\>,\>\rangle
be the pairing on
\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}
defined as

\langle X^{i}Y^{2k-2-i},X^{j}Y^{2k-2-j}\rangle={2k-2\choose i}^{-1}(-1)^{i}\delta_{i,2k-2-j}.

Main Theorem.

Let
J\in\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
be a suitable rigid cocycle and let
\chi
be the character defined in (
10
). Let

\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q):=\langle\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{r_{Q},s_{Q}\}(x,y),Q^{k-1}(x,y)\rangle,\quad\text{with }Q\in\mathcal{F}_{p}.

Then

\mathcal{RS}(J):=(2k-2)!\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{p}/\Gamma_{0}(p)}\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q)q^{D(Q)/p}

is a cusp form in
S_{k+1/2}(\Gamma_{0}(4p),\chi)
. Here
q:=e^{2\pi i\tau}
with
\tau\in\mathcal{H}
. The assignment
J\mapsto\mathcal{RS}(J)
is Hecke-equivariant away from
p
.

To see the similarity between
I_{k}(f,Q)
and
\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q)
, consider the period map

f\mapsto\widetilde{per}(f):=\int_{r}^{s}f(z)(zX+Y)^{2k-2}dz,

which will be further described in the next Section.

Then
I_{k}(f,Q)
can be characterized as

(12)

I_{k}(f,Q)=(2k-2)!\langle(\widetilde{per}(f)\{r_{Q},s_{Q}\})(x,y),Q^{k-1}(x,y)\rangle.

See for example Section 4.3 of
[
Ste
]
for more details. Comparing (
12
) with
\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q)
in the Main Theorem, we see that the only difference between them is that in the rigid analytic case the residues
\widetilde{\mu}_{J}
of
J
take the place of the periods of modular forms. (The factor
(2k-2)!
is not important, as it simply arises because the pairing
\langle\>,\rangle
that we chose is slightly different from the one used by Shintani). This parallel between periods of modular forms and residues of rigid cocycles already appeared in
[
Neg
]
, where the residues of the cocycles
J_{k,D}
of (
3
) were shown to be equal to the periods of certain modular forms related to the Zagier forms
f_{k,D}
of (
6
) (see Sections 4 and 5 of
[
Neg
]
).

1.3.
Eichler-Shimura isomorphism and cuspidal cocycles

We briefly recall some facts about the Eichler-Shimura isomorphism and introduce some notation that will be needed to prove our main result in Section
3
. For a similar treatment of this topic and for more details see Section 4 of
[
Ste
]
.

Consider the involution on
\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})
given by

(m|w_{\infty})\{r,s\}:=(m\{w_{\infty}r,w_{\infty}s\})|w_{\infty},\>\>\text{with }w_{\infty}:=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

We will call
even
(resp.
odd
) elements of
\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})
which are in the
+1
(resp.
-1
) eigenspace for
w_{\infty}
.
Every modular symbol in
\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})
can be written as a sum of an even and an odd modular symbol, and we have

(13)

\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})=\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})^{+}\oplus\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})^{-}.

Clearly the same definitions could be given using the
\star
action.

We will denote by
\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}^{(\mathbb{C})}
the homogeneous polynomials with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
and degree
2k-2
, with the
|
action of
\Gamma_{0}(p)
. Recall the Eichler-Shimura isomorphism, in particular the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))\oplus S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\Gamma_{0}(p),\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}^{(\mathbb{C})})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}^{(\mathbb{C})})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ES}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces per}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

Here
ES
is an isomorphism,
\beta
associates to a modular symbol
\mu
the cocycle
\Phi_{\mu}(\gamma):=\mu\{x_{0},\gamma x_{0}\}
for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
, and
per
is given by

f\mapsto per(f):=\int_{r}^{s}f(z)(X-zY)^{2k-2}dz.

Moreover
per=per^{+}\oplus per^{-}
, where
per^{+}
(resp.
per^{-}
) denotes the map given by taking even (resp. odd) periods of cusp forms. These maps land in the spaces of even and odd modular symbols, which are the eigenvalues of
w_{\infty}
acting on
\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}^{(\mathbb{C})})
. By the Manin-Drinfeld principle there is a unique Hecke-equivariant section
s_{\beta}
of
\beta
such that the diagram commutes and
\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}^{(\mathbb{C})})=Ker(\beta)\oplus Im(s_{\beta})
. We have
s_{\beta}=per\circ ES^{-1}
.

If we consider
\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}^{(\mathbb{C})}
with the
\star
action instead, we get an analogue diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))\oplus S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\Gamma_{0}(p),\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}^{(\mathbb{C})})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}^{(\mathbb{C})})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{ES}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{per}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

Now the period map is given by

f\mapsto\widetilde{per}(f):=\int_{r}^{s}f(z)(zX+Y)^{2k-2}dz.

The maps
\widetilde{ES}
and
\widetilde{s}_{\beta}
are defined like in the previous case. To relate the objects in
[
Neg
]
with the ones of
[
Shn
]
and
[
Ste
]
, we will use the fact that
\widetilde{per}(f)=\alpha\circ per(f)
and
\widetilde{per}^{\pm}(f)=\alpha\circ per^{\pm}(f)
.

If
J\in\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})^{(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})}
, then
\Res_{0}(J)
can be seen as an element of
\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}^{(\mathbb{C})})
, hence the following definition makes sense.

Definition 1.7
.

A rigid analytic cocycle
J\in\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})^{(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})}
is called cuspidal if
\Res_{0}(J)\in Im(s_{\beta})
or equivalently if
\widetilde{\Res}_{0}(J)\in Im(\widetilde{s}_{\beta})
. The space of cuspidal cocycles will be denoted by
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})^{\text{cusp}}
.

Note that the cocycles
J_{k,D}
of
[
Neg
]
are cuspidal as
\Res_{0}(J_{k,D})=per^{-}(f_{k,D}^{(p)})
, for certain cusp forms
f_{k,D}^{(p)}\in S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
.

2.
Hecke operators on rigid analytic cocycles

In this Section we will provide two different definitions of Hecke operators on rigid analytic cocycles: the first one will be given using the map
\Res_{0}:\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})\rightarrow\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})
of
[
Neg
]
, the second one will be more explicit. Proposition
2.2
will show that the two definitions coincide.

Let
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j=0,...,p}
be the representatives given by
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})=\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{p}\gamma_{j}\Gamma_{0}(p)
and let
w_{p}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
p&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

Definition 2.1
.

The space
\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})^{p\text{-}new}
is the subspace given by the modular symbols
\psi\in\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{p}\psi|\gamma_{j}=0\>\>\>\>\text{ and }\>\>\>\>\sum_{j=0}^{p}\psi|(w_{p}\gamma_{j}^{-1})=0.

Lemma 2.1
.

The map
\Res_{0}
gives an isomorphism
\Res_{0}:\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})\rightarrow\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})^{p\text{-}new}
.

Proof.

Recall from (
2
) that
\Res_{0}=ev_{e_{0}}\circ\Res
. As
\Res
is the inverse of the Schneider-Teitelbaum lift
ST
, it must be injective. Moreover,
ev_{e_{0}}(c)
belongs to
\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})^{p\text{-}new}
because of the harmonicity properties of
c
. Indeed, the conditions in Definition
2.1
can be rewritten as sums of
c
evaluated at all the edges leaving the standard vertex
v_{0}
of
\mathcal{T}
. So the only thing left to prove is that
ev_{e_{0}}
is surjective on
\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})^{p\text{-}new}
. Any edge
e
of
\mathcal{T}
can be written as
e=\gamma e_{0}
for some
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Then, given
c_{0}\in\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})^{p\text{-}new}
, we can define

c\{r,s\}(e):=c_{0}\{\gamma r,\gamma s\}|\gamma.

Using the fact that
c_{0}
satisfies the formulas in Definition
2.1
one can show that
c\in\MS^{\Gamma}(C_{har}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}))
. It is easy to check that
ev_{e_{0}}(c)=c_{0}
.
∎

Recall that there is an action of Hecke operators on
\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})
, induced by the Hecke action on
S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
via the Eichler-Shimura isomorphism
ES
. For
J\in\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
and any positive integer
m
with
(m,p)=1
we can now define Hecke operators
T_{m}
as

(14)

T_{m}(J):=\Res_{0}^{-1}(T_{m}(\Res_{0}(J))).

Similarly, let

(15)

U_{p}(J):=\Res_{0}^{-1}(U_{p}(\Res_{0}(J))).

Alternatively, we can give more concrete definitions as follows. Let
l
be a prime different from
p
. Define the sets of matrices

(16)

\Gamma_{l}:=\Big\{\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\
0&l\end{pmatrix},\>a=0,...,l-1\Big\}\cup\Big\{\begin{pmatrix}l&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\Big\}.

Any
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{l}
acts on
f\in\mathcal{A}_{2k-2}
by

(f|\gamma)(z):=det(\gamma)^{k-1}(cz+d)^{2-2k}f(\gamma z),

and on
h\in\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}
by

(f|\gamma)(z):=det(\gamma)^{1-k}(cX+dY)^{2k-2}h(aX+bY,cX+dY).

These induce actions of
\gamma
on
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
,
\MS^{\Gamma}(C_{har}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}))
and
\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})
.

Then, we can define

(17)

T_{l}(J):=l^{k-1}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{l}}J|\gamma.

Note that the
T_{l}
’s defined in (
17
) preserve the space
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
, by the same argument used to show that Hecke operators preserve the space of modular forms.
To define the operator
U_{p}
, consider the involution on
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
defined by

(J|w_{p})\{r,s\}:=(J\{w_{p}r,w_{p}s\})|w_{p},\>\>\text{with }w_{p}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
p&0\end{pmatrix},

where
J\in\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})
. Then let

(18)

U_{p}(J):=-J|w_{p}.

In order to prove that the definitions given in (
14
), (
15
) and (
17
), (
18
) coincide, we need some notation.
As
w_{p}
is an involution, we can write

\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})=\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})^{p,+}\oplus\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})^{p,-},

where
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})^{p,+}
(resp.
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})^{p,-}
) is the eigenspace on which
w_{p}
acts with eigenvalue
+1
(resp.
-1
). The two subspaces that we just defined are called the spaces of
p
-
even
and
p
-
odd
rigid analytic cocycles. Similarly, as
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})\cong\MS^{\Gamma}(C_{har}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}))
, we can define
\MS^{\Gamma}(C_{har}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}))^{p,+}
and
\MS^{\Gamma}(C_{har}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}))^{p,-}
.

For any
c\in C_{har}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})
and for any edge
e
of the Bruhat-Tits tree, recall from Section
1.1
that
ev_{e}
is the evaluation map at
e
, i.e.
ev_{e}(c):=c(e)
.

Finally, given the sign
\epsilon=+
(resp.
\epsilon=-
), let

\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})^{U_{p}=\epsilon}

be the subspace of
\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})
on which
U_{p}
acts as multiplication by
+1
(resp.
-1
).

Proposition 2.1
.

The map
ev_{e_{0}}
induces Hecke-equivariant inclusions

ev_{e_{0}}:\MS^{\Gamma}(C_{har}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}))^{p,\epsilon}\hookrightarrow\MS^{\Gamma_{0}(p)}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2})^{U_{p}=-\epsilon}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one of Proposition 3.3 of
[
DV2
]
.

For any edge
e
of the Bruhat-Tits tree, let
U(e)\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
be the
p
-adic ball attached to

. Then the complement of
U(e_{0})
is

U(e_{0})^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\bigsqcup_{j=1,...,p}U(e_{j}),

where

U(e_{j}):=\alpha_{j}^{-1}\mathbb{Z}_{p},\>\>\>\>\text{ with }\alpha_{j}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&j-1\\
0&p\end{pmatrix}.

Let
c\in\MS^{\Gamma}(C_{har}(\mathcal{P}_{2k-2}))^{p,\epsilon}
. The harmonicity of

implies that

(ev_{e_{0}}(c))\{r,s\}=-\sum_{j=1,...,p}(ev_{e_{j}}(c))\{r,s\}.

From here we can proceed as
[

]
, so the quantity above can be written as

-\sum_{j=1,...,p}c\{r,s\}(e_{j})=-\sum_{j=1,...,p}((c\{r,s\}|\alpha_{j}^{-1})(e_{0}))|\alpha_{j}=-\epsilon\sum_{j=1,...,p}(c\{\alpha_{j}r,\alpha_{j}s\}(e_{0}))|\alpha_{j}.

The result follows from the definition of
U_{p}
given in (
18
).
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

The definition of
T_{l}
(resp.
U_{p}
) given in (
14
) (resp. (
15
)) coincides with the one given in (

) (resp. (

)).

Proof.

The Schneider-Teitelbaum lift

and its inverse

are
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-equivariant, and the map

is equivariant for the matrices

defined in (

. This implies that the map
\Res_{0}=ev_{e_{0}}\circ\Res
is

-equivariant for

defined in (

implies that

∎

3.
Construction of the Shintani map for rigid cocycles

In this Section we will construct a map
\mathcal{RS}:\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})^{cusp}\rightarrow S_{k+1/2}(\Gamma_{0}(4p),\chi)
, where
\chi
was defined in (
10
).

We can finally give a rigorous statement of the main theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
J\in\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})^{cusp}
be a cuspidal cocycle and let
\chi
be the character defined in (
10
). Let

\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q):=\langle\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{r_{Q},s_{Q}\}(x,y),Q^{k-1}(x,y)\rangle,\quad\text{with }Q\in\mathcal{F}_{p}.

Then

\mathcal{RS}(J):=(2k-2)!\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{p}/\Gamma_{0}(p)}\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q)q^{D(Q)/p}

is a cusp form in
S_{k+1/2}(\Gamma_{0}(4p),\chi)
. Here
q:=e^{2\pi i\tau}
with
\tau\in\mathcal{H}
. The map

\mathcal{RS}:\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})^{cusp}\rightarrow S_{k+1/2}(\Gamma_{0}(4p),\chi)

is
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear and

\mathcal{RS}(T_{m}(J))=T_{m^{2}}(\mathcal{RS}(J))

for any
m
coprime with
p
.

To prove Theorem
3.1
we will need the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

The quantity
\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q)
is well defined and depends only on the
\Gamma_{0}(p)
-orbit of
Q
.

Proof.

We will start by proving that
\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q)
does not depend on the choice of the point
\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
that we used to define it. Let
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
. Then using the properties of
\Gamma_{0}(p)
-invariant modular symbols we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{\omega,\gamma_{Q}(\omega)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{\omega^{\prime},\gamma_{Q}(\omega^{\prime})\}+\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{\gamma_{Q}(\omega^{\prime}),\gamma_{Q}(\omega)\}-\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{\omega^{\prime},\omega\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{\omega^{\prime},\gamma_{Q}(\omega^{\prime})\}+\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{\omega^{\prime},\omega\}\star\gamma_{Q}^{-1}-\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{\omega^{\prime},\omega\}.

Now note that

\displaystyle\langle(\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{\omega^{\prime},\omega\}\star\gamma_{Q}^{-1})(x,y),Q^{k-1}(x,y)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{\omega^{\prime},\omega\}(x,y),(Q\star\gamma_{Q})^{k-1}(x,y)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{\omega^{\prime},\omega\}(x,y),Q^{k-1}(x,y)\rangle.

From this one can immediately conclude that
\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q)
does not depend on
\omega
or
\omega^{\prime}
.

We will now show that
\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q)=\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q\star\gamma)
for any
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(p)
. At first note that
\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q\star\gamma)
can be written as

(19)

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{r_{Q\star\gamma},s_{Q\star\gamma}\},(Q\star\gamma)^{k-1}(x,y)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{r_{Q\star\gamma},s_{Q\star\gamma}\}\star\gamma^{-1})(x,y),Q^{k-1}(x,y)\rangle

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{\mu}_{J}\{\gamma(r_{Q\star\gamma}),\gamma(s_{Q\star\gamma})\}(x,y),Q^{k-1}(x,y)\rangle.

We are going to treat separately the cases in which
D(Q)
is or is not a square. Let us assume at first that
D(Q)
is not a square, i.e.
\{r_{Q\star\gamma},s_{Q\star\gamma}\}=\{\omega,\gamma_{Q\star\gamma}(\omega)\}
for some
\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
. The automorph of
Q\star\gamma
is
\gamma_{Q\star\gamma}=\gamma^{-1}\gamma_{Q}\gamma
, so the pair appearing in (
20
) is
\{\gamma(r_{Q\star\gamma}),\gamma(s_{Q\star\gamma})\}=\{\gamma\omega,\gamma_{Q}(\gamma\omega)\}
. This implies that the quantity in (
20
) is equal to
\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q)
.

We will now consider the case in which
D(Q)
is a square. In this case the Shintani cycle
(r_{Q},s_{Q})
is defined as
(g(\infty),g(0))
for
g\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
(Q\star g)(x,y)=\sqrt{D}xy
(see
[
Shn
]
,page 101). If
g
is such a matrix, then
(Q\star\gamma)\star(\gamma^{-1}g)(x,y)=\sqrt{D}xy
, hence
(r_{Q\star\gamma},s_{Q\star\gamma})=(\gamma^{-1}g(\infty),\gamma^{-1}g(0))=(\gamma^{-1}r_{Q},\gamma^{-1}s_{Q})
. This fact, together with equations (
19
), (
20
) implies that also in this case
\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q)=\hat{I}_{k}(J,Q\star\gamma)
.
∎

Expression (
12
) implies that we can write
I_{k}(f,Q)=I_{k}(f,Q)^{+}+I_{k}(f,Q)^{-}
, where

I_{k}(f,Q)^{\pm}=(2k-2)!\langle(\widetilde{per}^{\pm}(f)\{r_{Q},s_{Q}\})(x,y),Q^{k-1}(x,y)\rangle.

We will now prove that the even part
I_{k}(f,Q)^{+}
of
I_{k}(f,Q)
does not contribute to the Shintani lift
\mathcal{S}(f)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f\in S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
. Then

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{p}/\Gamma_{0}(p)}I_{k}(f,Q)^{+}q^{D(Q)/p}=0,

and

\mathcal{S}(f)=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{p}/\Gamma_{0}(p)}I_{k}(f,Q)^{-}q^{D(Q)/p}.

Proof.

Recall that
w_{\infty}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and let
Q^{\prime}:=Q|w_{\infty}
for
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{p}
. Then

\widetilde{per}^{\pm}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\widetilde{per}(f)\>\pm\>\widetilde{per}(f)|w_{\infty}\Big),

and

I_{k}(f,Q)^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(I_{k}(f,Q)\>\pm\>(2k-2)!\langle(\widetilde{per}(f)\{-r_{Q},-s_{Q}\})(x,y),{Q^{\prime}}^{k-1}(x,y)\rangle\Big).

Using the definition of the Shintani cycle one can show that
\{-r_{Q},-s_{Q}\}=\{s_{Q^{\prime}},r_{Q^{\prime}}\}
, hence

I_{k}(f,Q)^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(I_{k}(f,Q)\>\mp\>I_{k}(f,Q^{\prime})\Big).

So
I_{k}(f,Q)^{+}
does not affect the sum giving
\mathcal{S}(f)
, as the contributions from
I_{k}(f,Q)
and
I_{k}(f,Q^{\prime})
cancel out.
∎

Now we can finally prove our main result.

Proof.

(Proof of Theorem
3.1
)

As the cocycle
J
is cuspidal, there are
(g,f)\in S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))\oplus S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
such that
\mu_{J}=per^{+}(g_{J})+per^{-}(f_{J})
, or equivalently
\widetilde{\mu}_{J}=\widetilde{per}^{+}(g_{J})+\widetilde{per}^{-}(f_{J})
. By Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
3.1
we have

\mathcal{RS}(J)=\mathcal{S}(f_{J})

and the result follows.
∎

For
J
a cuspidal cocycle in
\MS^{\Gamma}(\mathcal{A}_{2k})^{(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})}
, write
\widetilde{\mu}_{J}=\widetilde{\mu}_{J}^{+}+\widetilde{\mu}_{J}^{-}
as a sum of an even and an odd modular symbol. Then the proof above has the following consequences.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let

\mathcal{RS}^{\pm}(J):=(2k-2)!\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{p}/\Gamma_{0}(p)}\langle\widetilde{\mu}_{J}^{\pm}\{r_{Q},s_{Q}\}(x,y),Q(x,y)^{k-1}\rangle q^{D(Q)/p}.

Then

\mathcal{RS}(J)=\mathcal{RS}^{-}(J)\>\>\>\text{ and }\>\>\>\mathcal{RS}^{+}(J)=0.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\widetilde{s_{\beta}}^{-}
be the map given by composing
\widetilde{s}_{\beta}\circ\widetilde{Res}_{0}
with the projection on the second component of
S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))\oplus S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
. Then
\mathcal{RS}
factors through
S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
via
\widetilde{s_{\beta}}^{-}
.

Example 3.1
.

We can apply our main theorem to the rigid cocycle
J_{k,D}
of equation (
6
). It was shown in
[
Neg
]
that
\mu_{J_{k,D}}=per^{-}(f_{k,D}^{(p)})
, where
f_{k,D}^{(p)}\in S_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
is a level
p
analogue of the Zagier form
f_{k,D}
of equation (
3
) (see
[

]
, Section 5). Hence
\mathcal{RS}(J_{k,D})=\mathcal{S}(f_{k,D}^{(p)})
.

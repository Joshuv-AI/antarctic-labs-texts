Nonmesonic Quantum Many-Body Scars in a 1D Lattice Gauge Theory
Zi-Yong Ge, Yu-Ran Zhang, Franco Nori

Abstract
We investigate the meson excitations (particle-antiparticle bound states) in quantum many-body scars of a 1D ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} lattice gauge theory coupled to a dynamical spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} chain as a matter field. By introducing a string representation of the physical Hilbert space, we express a scar state | Ψ n , l ⟩ \ket{\Psi_{n,l}} as a superposition of all string bases with an identical string number n n and a total length l l . For the small- l l scar state | Ψ n , l ⟩ \ket{\Psi_{n,l}} , the gauge-invariant spin exchange correlation function of the matter field hosts an exponential decay as the distance increases, indicating the existence of stable mesons. However, for large l l , the correlation function exhibits a power-law decay, signaling the emergence of nonmesonic excitations. Furthermore, we show that this mesonic-nonmesonic crossover can be detected by the quench dynamics, starting from two low-entangled initial states, respectively, which are experimentally feasible in quantum simulators. Our results expand the physics of quantum many-body scars in lattice gauge theories and reveal that the nonmesonic state can also manifest ergodicity breaking.

—We introduce string bases in the

A state with one string excitation can be written as

\displaystyle\ket{\mathcal{S}_{k,\ell}}:=\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{k,\ell}^{\dagger}\ket{\Omega}=\hat{\sigma}_{k}^{+}\big(\prod_{k\leq j<k+\ell}\hat{\tau}^{\text{M}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)\hat{\sigma}_{k+\ell}^{+}\ket{\Omega},

(3)

where

An arbitrary gauge invariant basis can be written as

Mesonic-nonmesonic crossover.
—Mesons, as a type of particle-antiparticle bound states, play an important role in the dynamics of LGTs.
If the system is in a confined phase, the low-energy excitation is described by mesons.
In addition, in a high-energy regime, meson dynamics also closely relate to the ETH.
Previous works have shown that almost all of the towers of QMBSs in LGTs originate from meson excitations
James et al. 2019
;
Robinson et al. 2019
;
Surace et al. 2020
;
Iadecola and Schecter 2020
;
Kormos et al. 2017
;
Birnkammer et al. 2022
.
For the Hamiltonian (
Nonmesonic Quantum Many-Body Scars in a 1D Lattice Gauge Theory
), the

,
where the low-energy excitation is a meson.
However, it is still unclear whether mesons can describe the high-energy dynamics, especially the scar dynamics.

According to Eq. (
4

For small

This suggests that these scar states should be described by stable mesons.
However, as

Hence, intuitively, isolated

However, for large-

Summary.
—We have investigated the mesons in QMBSs of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
LGT.
By introducing the string representation, we express the wave function of each QMBS as an equal superposition of all string
bases with an identical string number and total string length.
We demonstrate that scar states with a small total string length are described by stable mesons, like conventional towers of QMBSs in LGTs,
while we find the nonmesonic excitations in the scar states with large total string length.
Furthermore, this mesonic-nonmesonic crossover in QMBSs can be observed from the quench dynamics with two experimentally accessible initial states.
Our results bring new insights into QMBSs in LGTs and reveal that the nonmesonic states can also host ergodicity breaking in LGTs,
which can be experimentally verified with quantum simulators.

Here, the mesonic-nonmesonic crossover is reminiscent of the asymptotic freedom of quarks
Gross and Wilczek 1973
;
Politzer 1973
.
The eigenenergy of the scar state
\ket{\Psi_{n,l}}
becomes large when increasing the total string length
l
.
Thus, the nonmesonic excitation in large-
l
QMBSs is in analogy with the asymptotic freedom of quarks
in the high-energy regime of quantum chromodynamics.
The conventional QMBSs are generated by local operators, which correspond to nonfractionalized excitations
Chandran et al. 2023
,
e.g., magnons in
PXP

Iadecola et al. 2019
and spin-
1

XY
models
Schecter and Iadecola 2019
, and
\eta
-pairs in Hubbard-like models
Mark and Motrunich 2020
;
Moudgalya et al. 2020
.
However, the nonmesonic excitations have some analogies with spinons
Fradkin 2013
, which are fractionalized excitations and different from conventional QMBSs.
Therefore, our results also reveal that nonlocal generating operators may lead to fractionalized excitations in QMBSs,
providing an inspiration for studying the nontrivial excitation in QMBSs.
Here, nonlocal generating operators are necessary for the nonmesonic QMBSs,
since they can separate two matter particles of a meson.
However, it is still unclear which types of generating operators can induce these nontrivial physics.
Another interesting issue is whether the above physics can be generalized to other gauge groups or high-dimensional LGTs
Dagotto et al. 1988
;
Banuls et al. 2020
.
These open questions deserve further study.

Proof of
\hat{H}_{K}\ket{\Psi_{n,l}}=0

In the main text, we show that the wave function

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{n,l}}=\mathcal{N}_{n,l}\sum P_{\{\mathcal{S}_{k_{j},\ell_{j}}\}_{n}^{l}}\ket{\{\mathcal{S}_{k_{j},\ell_{j}}\}_{n}^{l}},

(S1)

is an exact eigenstate of
\hat{H}
.
Here we present details for proving this result.
It is not difficult to find that
\hat{H}_{E}\ket{\Psi_{n,l}}=h(2l-L)\ket{\Psi_{n,l}}
,
so we only need to prove
\hat{H}_{K}\ket{\Psi_{n,l}}=0
.
Since the action of
\hat{H}_{K}
is increasing or reducing the total string length by one, while keeping
n
invariant, we have

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{K}\ket{\Psi_{n,l}}=\sum c_{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{l-1}}\ket{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{l-1}}+\sum c_{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{l+1}}\ket{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{l+1}}.

(S2)

Here, the factors have forms

\displaystyle c_{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{l-1}}=\mathcal{N}_{n,l}\sum[

\displaystyle(1-\delta_{k^{\prime}_{1}+\ell^{\prime}_{1}+1,k^{\prime}_{2}})(P_{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{1},\ell^{\prime}_{1}+1},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{2},\ell^{\prime}_{2}}...}+P_{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{1},\ell^{\prime}_{1}},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{2}-1,\ell^{\prime}_{2}+1}...})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(1-\delta_{k^{\prime}_{2}+\ell^{\prime}_{2}+1,k^{\prime}_{3}})(P_{...,\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{2},\ell^{\prime}_{2}+1},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{3},\ell^{\prime}_{3}}...}+P_{...,\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{2},\ell^{\prime}_{2}},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{3}-1,\ell^{\prime}_{3}+1}...})+...

\displaystyle c_{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{l+1}}=\mathcal{N}_{n,l}\sum[

\displaystyle(1-\delta_{\ell^{\prime}_{1},1})(P_{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{1}-1,\ell^{\prime}_{1}-1},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{2},\ell^{\prime}_{2}}...}+P_{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{1},\ell^{\prime}_{1}-1},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{2},\ell^{\prime}_{2}}...})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(1-\delta_{\ell^{\prime}_{2},1})(P_{...,\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{2}-1,\ell^{\prime}_{2}-1},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{3},\ell^{\prime}_{3}}...}+P_{...,\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{2},\ell^{\prime}_{2}-1},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{3},\ell^{\prime}_{3}}...})+....

(S3)

Since the parity satisfies
P_{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{1},\ell^{\prime}_{1}},...,\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{1}},...,\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{n},\ell^{\prime}_{n}}}=\exp{(i\pi\sum_{j}k^{\prime}_{j})}
,
we have

\displaystyle P_{...\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j-1},\ell^{\prime}_{j-1}},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}+1},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j+1},\ell^{\prime}_{j+1}}...}=-P_{...\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j-1},\ell^{\prime}_{j-1}},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j+1}-1,\ell^{\prime}_{j+1}+1}...}

\displaystyle P_{...\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j-1},\ell^{\prime}_{j-1}},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j}-1,\ell^{\prime}_{j}-1},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j+1},\ell^{\prime}_{j+1}}...}=-P_{...\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j-1},\ell^{\prime}_{j-1}},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}-1},\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j+1},\ell^{\prime}_{j+1}}...}.

(S4)

Therefore,
c_{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{l-1}}=c_{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{l+1}}=0
, i.e.,
\hat{H}_{K}\ket{\Psi_{n,l}}=0

Proof of
\ket{\Psi_{n,n+m}}=\mathcal{D}_{n,m}\hat{L}_{m}^{\dagger}\ket{\Psi_{n,n+m-1}}

Next we show the detail of proving Eq. (8) in the main text, i.e,

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{n,n+m}}=\mathcal{D}_{n,m}\hat{L}_{m}^{\dagger}\ket{\Psi_{n,n+m-1}},

(S5)

where
\mathcal{D}_{n,m}
is a normalization factor, and

\displaystyle\hat{L}^{\dagger}_{m}=\sum_{j}\big(\sum_{k\leq m}\prod_{\ell\leq k}\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}-\ell}\big)\hat{\sigma}^{-}_{j}\hat{\tau}^{\text{M}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\sigma}^{+}_{j+1}

(S6)

It is not difficult to demonstrate

\displaystyle\big(\sum_{k\leq m}\prod_{\ell\leq k}\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}-\ell}\big)\hat{\sigma}^{-}_{j}\hat{\tau}^{\text{M}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\sigma}^{+}_{j+1}\ket{\mathcal{S}_{k,\ell}}=\begin{cases}\ell\delta_{j,k+\ell}\ket{\mathcal{S}_{k,\ell+1}}\quad\ell\leq m\\
m\delta_{j,k+\ell}\ket{\mathcal{S}_{k,\ell+1}}\quad\ell>m.\end{cases}

(S7)

Thus, the action of
\hat{L}^{\dagger}_{m}
is increasing the total string length of a basis without changing the parity and string number.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{m}^{\dagger}\ket{\Psi_{n,n+m-1}}=\sum\alpha_{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{n+m}}\ket{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{n+m}}.

(S8)

For the wave function
\ket{\Psi_{n,n+m-1}}=\mathcal{N}_{n,n+m-1}\sum P_{\{\mathcal{S}_{k_{j},\ell_{j}}\}_{n}^{n+m-1}}\ket{\{\mathcal{S}_{k_{j},\ell_{j}}\}_{n}^{n+m-1}}
,
the length of each string satisfies
\ell_{j}\leq m
.
Hence, the factor has the form

\displaystyle\alpha_{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{n+m}}=\mathcal{N}_{n,n+m-1}P_{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{n+m}}[(1-\delta_{\ell^{\prime}_{1},1})(\ell^{\prime}_{1}-1)+(1-\delta_{\ell^{\prime}_{2},1})(\ell^{\prime}_{2}-1)+...+(1-\delta_{\ell^{\prime}_{n},1})(\ell^{\prime}_{n}-1)].

(S9)

If
\ell^{\prime}_{j}=1

(1-\delta_{\ell^{\prime}_{j},1})(\ell^{\prime}_{j}-1)=(\ell^{\prime}_{j}-1)=0
, and if
\ell^{\prime}_{j}\neq 1

(1-\delta_{\ell^{\prime}_{j},1})(\ell^{\prime}_{j}-1)=(\ell^{\prime}_{j}-1)
.
Thus

\displaystyle\alpha_{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{n+m}}=\mathcal{N}_{n,n+m-1}P_{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{n+m}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\ell^{\prime}_{j}-1)=\mathcal{N}_{n,n+m-1}P_{\{\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}_{j},\ell^{\prime}_{j}}\}_{n}^{n+m}}(m-1).

(S11)

That is, Eq. (8) is proved, and the normalization factor satisfies

Jordan ∗ * -homomorphisms on the spaces of continuous maps taking values in C ∗ C^{*} -algebras
Shiho Oi

Abstract
. Let 𝒜 \mathcal{A} be a unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebra. We consider Jordan ∗ * -homomorphisms on C ⁡ ( X , 𝒜 ) C(X,\mathcal{A}) and Jordan ∗ * -homomorphisms on Lip ⁡ ( X , 𝒜 ) \operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A}) . More precisely, for any unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebra 𝒜 \mathcal{A} , we prove that every Jordan ∗ * -homomorphism on C ⁡ ( X , 𝒜 ) C(X,\mathcal{A}) and every Jordan ∗ * -homomorphism on Lip ⁡ ( X , 𝒜 ) \operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A}) is represented as a weighted composition operator by using the irreducible representations of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . In addition, when 𝒜 1 \mathcal{A}_{1} and 𝒜 2 \mathcal{A}_{2} are primitive C ∗ C^{*} -algebras, we characterize the Jordan ∗ * -isomorphisms. These results unify and enrich previous works on algebra ∗ * -homomorphisms on C ⁡ ( X , 𝒜 ) C(X,\mathcal{A}) and Lip ⁡ ( X , 𝒜 ) \operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A}) for several concrete examples of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} .

1.
Introduction

For any elements
a,b
in a
*
-algebra, their Jordan product is defined by
a\circ b=\frac{ab+ba}{2}
. Let
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2}
be
*
-algebras. A Jordan
*
-homomorphism from
\mathcal{B}_{1}
into
\mathcal{B}_{2}
is a linear map
J:\mathcal{B}_{1}\to\mathcal{B}_{2}
such that
J(a\circ b)=J(a)\circ J(b)
for any
a,b\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
( or equivalently,
J(a^{2})=J(a)^{2}
for any
a\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
)
and
J(a)^{*}=J(a^{*})
for any
a\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
. A well-known theorem of Kadison in
[
6
]
tells that every unital surjective linear isometry between unital
C^{*}
-algebras is a Jordan
*
-isomorphism. Accordingly, Jordan
*
-homomorphisms are quite important and basic to an investigation. They form the foundation of
*
-algebra theories.
Thus the study of Jordan
*
-homomorphisms between
*
-algebras has caused a great deal of interest.

We now consider
*
- algebras of vector-valued continuous functions. We
emphasize that on the one hand, the algebra
*
- homomorphisms on
*
- algebras of vector-valued continuous functions are well studied, on the other hand there are few results on Jordan
*
-homomorphisms on
*
- algebras of vector-valued continuous functions.
The purpose of this paper is to investigate Jordan
*
-homomorphisms on two
*
-algebras of vector-valued functions. The first
*
-algebra is
C(X,\mathcal{A})
which is the space of all continuous maps from a compact Hausdorff space
X
into a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
. More precisely, let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space and
\mathcal{A}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}}
. Then

C(X,\mathcal{A})=\{F:X\to\mathcal{A}\ |\ F\text{ is continuous}\}.

It is easy to see that
C(X,\mathcal{A})
is a unital
C^{*}
-algebra with the norm
\|F\|=\sup_{x\in X}\|F(x)\|_{\mathcal{A}}
for any
F\in C(X,\mathcal{A})
. The second
*
-algebra is
\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
, which is the space of all Lipschitz maps from a compact metric space
X
into a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
. More precisely, let
X
be a compact metric space and
\mathcal{A}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra. Then

\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})=\{F:X\to\mathcal{A}\ |\ L(F):=\sup_{x\neq y\in X}\frac{\|F(x)-F(y)\|_{\mathcal{A}}}{d(x,y)}<\infty\}.

With
\|F\|_{L}=\sup_{x\in X}\|F(x)\|_{\mathcal{A}}+L(F)
for any
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
,
\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
is a Banach algebra. Since we define the involution
*
on
\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
by
F^{*}(x)=[F(x)]^{*}
for all
x\in X
,
\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
is a Banach
*
-algebra.
Throughout the paper,
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
will be unital
C^{*}
-algebras.

In
[
5
, Theorem 12]
, Leung and Tang proved the following. For
i=1
or
2
, let
X_{i}
be realcompact spaces. Suppose that
T:C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})\to C(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
is an algebra
*
-isomorphism so that
Z(F)\neq\emptyset\Leftrightarrow Z(TF)\neq\emptyset
, where
Z(F)=\{x\in X_{i}|F(x)=0\}
for each
F\in C(X_{i},\mathcal{A}_{i})
for
i=1,2
. Then there is a homeomorphism
\varphi:X_{2}\to X_{1}
and an algebra
*
-isomorphism
V_{y}:\mathcal{A}_{1}\to\mathcal{A}_{2}
for each
y\in X_{2}
such that

(1)

TF(y)=V_{y}(F(\varphi(y))),\quad F\in C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})\text{ and all }y\in X_{2}.

Let us recall that an algebra
*
-homomorphism is a multiplicative linear operator that preserves the involution
*
. It is natural to ask whether a Jordan
*
-isomorphism from
C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
onto
C(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
can be represented as a weighted composition operator, which has a similar form to (
1
). In this paper, we prove that every Jordan
*
-isomorphism from
C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
onto
C(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
is represented as a weighted composition operator by using the irreducible representations of
\mathcal{A}_{2}
. In addition we characterize Jordan
*
-isomorphisms from
C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
onto
C(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
if
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
are primitive
C^{*}
-algebras.

We now introduce the study of Jordan
*
-homomorphisms on
\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
. As pointed out by Botelho and Jamison in
[
1
]
, it is not known that whether algebra
*
-homomorphisms on
\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
are bounded with
\|\cdot\|_{L}
or not. Therefore there are some results for continuous algebra
*
-homomorphisms on
\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
. For example, a result of Botelho and Jamison
[
1
]
says that if
T:\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},B(H))\to\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},B(H))
is a ps-continuous algebra

-homomorphism that preserves constant functions with

, then there exist a Lipschitz function
\varphi:X_{2}\to X_{1}
and an algebra
*
-homomorphism
V:B(H)\to B(H)
such that
T(F)(y)=V(F(\varphi(y)))
for all
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},B(H))
and
y\in X_{2}
. In
[
2
]
, Botelho and Jamison also proved that if
T:\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))\to\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
is an algebra
*
-homomorphism and
T(1)=1
then there exist an algebra
*
-homomorphism
V_{y}:M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
for each
y\in X_{2}
and a continuous function
\varphi:X_{2}\to X_{1}
such that
T(F)(y)=V_{y}(F(\varphi(y)))
for all
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
and
y\in X_{2}
. As in the case of commutative
C^{*}
-algebras, in
[
7
]
the author proved the following. Suppose that
X_{2}
is connected. Let
M_{i}
be compact Hausdorff spaces for
i=1,2
. If
T:\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},C(M_{1}))\to\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},C(M_{2}))
is an algebra homomorphism with
T(1)=1
then there exist a continuous map
\tau:M_{2}\to M_{1}
, and a Lipschitz map
\varphi_{\phi}:X_{2}\to X_{1}
for each
\phi\in M_{2}
such that
T(F)(y)(\phi)=F(\varphi_{\phi}(y))(\tau(\phi))
holds for any
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},C(M_{1}))
,
\phi\in M_{2}
and
y\in X_{2}
.
The above results tell us that there are a variety of forms of algebra
*
-homomorphisms. We see that forms of algebra
*
-homomorphisms and Jordan
*
-homomorphisms depend on the classes of
C^{*}
-algebras
\mathcal{A}
. In Section
3
, we consider Jordan
*
-homomorphisms on
\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
for any unital
C^{*}
-algebras
\mathcal{A}
. We obtain a characterization of the Jordan
*
-isomorphisms from
\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
onto
\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
when
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
are primitive
C^{*}
-algebras. This is an generalization of the above results by Botelho and Jamison in
[
1
,
2
]
.

To state our theorem, we need some notations. We say a state
\rho
on
\mathcal{A}
is pure if it has the property that whenever
\tau
is a positive linear functional on
\mathcal{A}
such that
\tau\leq\rho
, there is a number
t\in[0,1]
such that
\tau=t\rho
. We denote the set of all pure states on
\mathcal{A}
by
\operatorname{PS}(A)
.
If
\rho
is a state on
\mathcal{A}
, then there is a cyclic representation
(\pi_{\rho},H_{\rho})
of
\mathcal{A}
with a unit cyclic vector
x_{\rho}
such that
\rho(a)=<\pi_{\rho}(a)x_{\rho},x_{\rho}>
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
.
In this paper, we denote the identity operator on a set
B
by
\operatorname{Id}_{B}
.
We also denote the unity of a unital Banach algebra
B
by
1_{B}
. If there is no cause for confusion, we write just
1
.

2.
Jordan
*
-homomorphisms on
C(X,\mathcal{A})

In this section we state the theorems for Jordan
*
-homomorphisms on
C(X,\mathcal{A})
. In general, every Jordan
*
-homomorphism between two unital
C^{*}
-algebras is contractive. More
precisely, let
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
be unital
C^{*}
-algebras. Suppose that
J:\mathcal{A}_{1}\to\mathcal{A}_{2}
is a Jordan
*
-homomorphism. Since
J(1)
is a projection in
\mathcal{A}_{2}
, we have
\|J\|\leq 1
by
[
8
, Corollary 1]
.

We first introduce some additional notations and terminology.
In this section,
X
,
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are compact Hausdorff spaces. For a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, if its zero ideal is primitive, we call it a primitive
C^{*}
-algebra.
Let
f\in C(X)
and
a\in\mathcal{A}
, where
C(X)
is a commutative
C^{*}
-algebra of all complex valued continuous functions on
X
. We define the algebraic tensor product
f\otimes a:X\to\mathcal{A}
by

(f\otimes a)(x)=f(x)a,\quad x\in X.

We have
f\otimes a\in C(X,\mathcal{A})
such that
\|f\otimes a\|=\|f\|_{\infty}\|a\|_{\mathcal{A}}
. We denote the algebraic tensor product space by
C(X)\otimes\mathcal{A}
.

For any unital
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, the following proposition is well-known.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{B}
is a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}
that contains the identity, every pure state on
\mathcal{B}
is extend to a pure state on
\mathcal{A}
.

We see a proof of Proposition
2.1
e.g. in
[
3
, Corollary 32.8]
. On the other hand,
we also have the next proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
\mathcal{B}
is a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}
, every pure state on
\mathcal{B}
can be extend to a pure state on
\mathcal{A}
.

Proposition
2.2
means that even if
\mathcal{B}
does not contain the identity of
\mathcal{A}
, each pure state on
\mathcal{B}
can be extend.
Unfortunately we could not find a proof of Proposition
2.2
in the literature, so we present proofs in detail for completeness. To prove Proposition
2.2
, we apply Proposition
2.1
.

Proof.

Let
\psi
be a pure state on
\mathcal{B}
. If
\mathcal{B}
has the identity of
\mathcal{A}
, by Proposition
2.1
there exists a pure state
\phi
on
\mathcal{A}
such that
\phi|_{\mathcal{B}}=\psi
. Thus suppose that
\mathcal{B}
does not have the identity. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
be the unitization of
\mathcal{B}
. We define a linear functional
\psi_{1}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
by
\psi_{1}(b+\beta)=\psi(b)+\beta
for
b\in\mathcal{B}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
. Then for any positive element
(b+\beta)^{*}(b+\beta)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
, we have
\psi_{1}((b+\beta)^{*}(b+\beta))\geq(|\psi(b)|-|\beta|)^{2}+2(|\overline{\psi(b)}\beta|+Re(\overline{\psi(b)}\beta))\geq 0
and
\psi_{1}(1)=1
. Thus
\psi_{1}
is a state on
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
. Let
\tau
be a positive linear functional on
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
such that
\tau\leq\psi_{1}
. For any
b\in\mathcal{B}
, we get
\tau(b^{*}b)\leq\psi_{1}(b^{*}b)=\psi(b^{*}b)
. Thus
\tau|_{\mathcal{B}}
is a positive linear functional on
\mathcal{B}
such that
\tau|_{\mathcal{B}}\leq\psi
. Since
\psi
is a pure state on
\mathcal{B}
, there is
t\in[0,1]
such that
\tau|_{\mathcal{B}}=t\psi
. Let
(u_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be an approximate unit for
\mathcal{B}
. As
\psi(u_{\lambda})^{2}\leq\psi(u_{\lambda}^{2})\leq 1
, and
\lim_{\lambda}\psi(u_{\lambda})=\|\psi\|=1
, we get
\lim_{\lambda}\psi(u_{\lambda}^{2})=1
. As
\tau\leq\psi_{1}
, for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
we have

0\leq(\psi_{1}-\tau)((u_{\lambda}+\beta)^{*}(u_{\lambda}+\beta))=(\psi_{1}-\tau)(u_{\lambda}^{2}+2\beta u_{\lambda}+\beta^{2})\\
=(1-t)(\psi(u_{\lambda}^{2}))+(1-t)(2\psi(u_{\lambda})\beta)+(1-\|\tau\|)\beta^{2}.

Thus
0\leq(1-t)+2(1-t)\beta+(1-||\tau\|)\beta^{2}
for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. This implies that
(1-t)^{2}-(1-t)(1-\|\tau\|)=(1-t)(\|\tau\|-t)\leq 0
. As
t\in[0,1]
, we obtain
\|\tau\|\leq t
.
For any
b\in\mathcal{B}
, we have
\|t\psi(b)\|=\|\tau(b+0)\|\leq\|\tau\|\|b\|
. As
\|\psi\|=1
we get
t\leq\|\tau\|
. Thus
\|\tau\|=t
. Then for any
b\in\mathcal{B}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
, we get
\tau(b+\beta)=\tau(b)+\beta\tau(1)=t\psi(b)+\beta t=t(\psi(b)+\beta)=t\psi_{1}(b+\beta)
. Hence
\tau=t\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{1}
is a pure state on
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
. Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
contains the identity of
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
, by Proposition
2.1
, there is a pure state
\psi_{1}^{\prime}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
such that
\psi_{1}^{\prime}|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}}=\psi_{1}
. We shall show that
\psi_{1}^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{A}}
is a pure state on
\mathcal{A}
. Let
\tau
be a positive linear functional such that
\tau\leq\psi_{1}^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{A}}
. Then we define a positive linear functional
\tau^{\prime}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
by
\tau^{\prime}(a+\beta)=\tau(a)+\beta\|\tau\|
for any
a+\beta\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
. Then
\|\tau^{\prime}\|=\|\tau\|
. For any positive element
(a+\beta)^{*}(a+\beta)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
, we have
(\psi_{1}^{\prime}-\tau^{\prime})((a+\beta)^{*}(a+\beta))=(\psi_{1}^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{A}}-\tau)((a+\beta 1_{\mathcal{A}})^{*}(a+\beta 1_{\mathcal{A}}))\geq 0
. Hence
\psi_{1}^{\prime}\geq\tau^{\prime}
. Since
\psi_{1}^{\prime}
is a pure state on
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
, there exists
t\in[0,1]
such that
\tau^{\prime}=t\psi_{1}^{\prime}
. For any
a\in\mathcal{A}
we have
\tau(a)=\tau^{\prime}(a)=t\psi_{1}^{\prime}(a)=t\psi_{1}^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{A}}(a)
. Thus
\psi_{1}^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{A}}
is a pure state on
\mathcal{A}
. We get
\psi(b)=\psi_{1}(b)=\psi_{1}^{\prime}|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}}(b)=\psi_{1}^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{A}}(b)
for any
b\in\mathcal{B}
. Therefore
\psi_{1}^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{A}}=\psi
on
\mathcal{B}
.
∎

For
y\in X
, let
\delta_{y}
denote the evaluation function on
C(X,\mathcal{A})
defined by
\delta_{y}(F)=F(y)
. We now describe the pure states on a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
C(X,\mathcal{A})
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
C(X,\mathcal{A})
. Then
\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{B})\subset\{\rho\circ\delta_{y}|\ \rho\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{A}),y\in X\}
.

Proof.

Let
p\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{B})
. By Proposition
2.2
, there exists a pure state
\phi
on
C(X,\mathcal{A})
such that
\phi|_{\mathcal{B}}=p
. Suppose that there are
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
in
C(X,\mathcal{A})^{*}_{1}
, which is the closed unit ball of the dual of
C(X,\mathcal{A})
, such that
\phi=\frac{\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}}{2}
. Then we have
1=\phi(1)=\frac{\tau_{1}(1)+\tau_{2}(1)}{2}
, we have
\tau_{1}(1)=\tau_{2}(1)=1
. Thus
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
are states on
C(X,\mathcal{A})
. As
\phi
is a pure state, we get
\phi=\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}
. Therefore
\phi
is an extreme point of
C(X,\mathcal{A})^{*}_{1}
. By Corollary 2.3.6. in
[
4
]
, there exist an extreme point
\rho
of the closed unit ball of the dual of
\mathcal{A}
and
y\in X
such that
\phi=\rho\circ\delta_{y}
. As
1=\phi(1)=\rho\circ\delta_{y}(1)=\rho(1)
,
\rho
is a pure state on
\mathcal{A}
.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
J
be a Jordan
*
-homomorphim of
C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
into
C(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by
J(C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}))
. For any
\rho\circ\delta_{y}\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{B})
, let
Y_{\rho}=\{x\in X_{2}|\ \rho\circ\delta_{x}\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{B})\}
. Then there exist a continuous map
\varphi_{\rho}:Y_{\rho}\to X_{1}
and a Jordan
*
-homomorphism
V_{y}:\mathcal{A}_{1}\to\mathcal{A}_{2}
for each
y\in Y_{\rho}
such that

\pi_{\rho}(JF(y))=\pi_{\rho}(V_{y}(F(\varphi_{\rho}(y))))

for all
F\in C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
and all
y\in Y_{\rho}
.

We note that as
\rho\circ\delta_{y}\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{B})
,
y\in Y_{\rho}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
, fix
\rho\circ\delta_{y}\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{B})
. Then
\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}
is an irreducible representation of
\mathcal{B}
. Thus
\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(\mathcal{B})
acts irreducibly on
H_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}
. Then
\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(\mathcal{B})^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}\operatorname{Id}_{H_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}}
, where
\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(\mathcal{B})^{\prime}
is the commutant of
\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(\mathcal{B})
in
B(H_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}})
. By Corollary 3.4. in
[
9
]
,
\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}\circ J:C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})\to B(H_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}})
is either a
*
-homomorphism or an anti
*
-homomorphism. For any
f\in C(X_{1})
, if it is a homomorphism, we have

\begin{split}\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(J(F))\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(J(f\otimes 1))=\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(J(F\cdot f\otimes 1))\\
=\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(J(f\otimes 1\cdot F))=\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(J(f\otimes 1))\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(JF)\end{split}

for all
F\in C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
. Thus
J(f\otimes 1)\in\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(\mathcal{B})^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}\operatorname{Id}_{H_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}}
. If
\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}\circ J
is an anti
*
-homomorphism, we obtain
J(f\otimes 1)\in\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(\mathcal{B})^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}\operatorname{Id}_{H_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}}
similarly. There is
\lambda_{f}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(2)

\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}\circ J(f\otimes 1)=\lambda_{f}\cdot\operatorname{Id}_{H_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}}.

If
f=1\in C(X_{1})
, there is
\lambda_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}\circ J(1)=\lambda_{1}\cdot\operatorname{Id}_{H_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}}
. We have
\lambda_{1}\cdot\operatorname{Id}_{H_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}}=\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}\circ J(1)=\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}\circ J(1^{2})=(\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}\circ J(1))(\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}\circ J(1))=\lambda_{1}^{2}\cdot\operatorname{Id}_{H_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}}
. This implies that
\lambda_{1}=1
or
\lambda_{1}
=0. Suppose that
\lambda_{1}=0
. Then we get
\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(J(1))=0
. As
\rho\circ\delta_{y}
is a pure state on
\mathcal{B}
, there exists
F\in C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
such that
\rho\circ\delta_{y}(JF)\neq 0
. Therefore
\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(JF)\neq 0
. Then we have
0\neq\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(JF)=\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(J(F\cdot 1))=\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(JF)\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(J1)=0
. This is a contradiction. Hence
\lambda_{1}=1
. Then
\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}\circ J(1)=\operatorname{Id}_{H_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}}
and by (
2
) we get
\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}\circ J(f\otimes 1)=\lambda_{f}\cdot\operatorname{Id}_{H_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}}=\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}\circ J(\lambda_{f}\cdot 1)
. So we get
\pi_{\rho\circ\delta_{y}}(J(f\otimes 1)-J(\lambda_{f}1))=0
. This implies
\rho\circ\delta_{y}((J(f\otimes 1)-J(\lambda_{f}1))^{*}(J(f\otimes 1)-J(\lambda_{f}1)))=0
. Therefore we get

(3)

J^{*}(\rho\circ\delta_{y})(f\otimes 1-\lambda_{f}\cdot 1)=0

and

(4)

\pi_{\rho}(J(f\otimes 1)(y))=\lambda_{f}(\pi_{\rho}(J(1)(y)))

for any
f\in C(X_{1})
. Since
\rho\circ\delta_{y}\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{B})
, we have
J^{*}(\rho\circ\delta_{y})\in\operatorname{PS}(C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}))
. There exist
\phi_{\rho,y}\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{A}_{1})
and
x\in X_{1}
such that
J^{*}(\rho\circ\delta_{y})=\phi_{\rho,y}\circ\delta_{x}
. Now we define
\varphi_{\rho}:Y_{\rho}\to X_{1}
by
J^{*}(\rho\circ\delta_{y})=\phi_{\rho,y}\circ\delta_{\varphi_{\rho}(y)}
. Then by (
3
) and
\phi_{\rho,y}(1)=1
, we obtain
f(\varphi_{\rho}(y))=\lambda_{f}
. By (
4
), we have

(5)

\pi_{\rho}(J(f\otimes 1)(y))=f(\varphi_{\rho}(y))\pi_{\rho}(J(1)(y))

for any
f\in C(X_{1})
and
y\in Y_{\rho}
.

Fix
y\in Y_{\rho}
. We define
V_{y}:\mathcal{A}_{1}\to\mathcal{A}_{2}
by

V_{y}(a)=J(1\otimes a)(y)

for any
a\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
. It is clear that
V_{y}
is a Jordan
*
-homomorphism.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{y}
be the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by
J(C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}))(y)
. As
\rho\circ\delta_{y}\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{B})
, we shall show that
\rho
is a pure state on
\mathcal{B}_{y}
. As
\rho\circ\delta_{y}\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{B})
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
G\in\mathcal{B}
with
\|G\|_{\infty}\leq 1
such that
|\rho\circ\delta_{y}(G)|\geq 1-\epsilon
. This implies that
|\rho(G(y))|\geq 1-\epsilon
. Since
\|G(y)\|\leq\|G\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, we get
\|\rho|_{\mathcal{B}_{y}}\|\geq 1
. On the other hand, as
\rho\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{A}_{2})
,
\rho
is a state on
\mathcal{B}_{y}
. Let
\tau
be a positive linear functional such that
\tau\leq\rho
on
\mathcal{B}_{y}
. Then for any positive element
F\in C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
,
\tau(JF(y))\leq\rho(JF(y))
. This implies that
\tau\circ\delta_{y}\leq\rho\circ\delta_{y}
on
\mathcal{B}
. Since
\rho\circ\delta_{y}
is a pure state on
\mathcal{B}
, there is
t\in[0,1]
such that
\tau\circ\delta_{y}=t\rho\circ\delta_{y}
. For any
F\in C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
, we get
\tau(JF(y))=t\rho(JF(y))
. So
\tau=t\rho
on
\mathcal{B}_{y}
. Thus
\rho
is a pure state on
\mathcal{B}_{y}
. Since a map from
C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
into
\mathcal{B}_{y}
by
F\mapsto J(F)(y)
is a Jordan
*
-homomorphism, the map
F\mapsto\pi_{\rho}(J(F)(y))
is either a homomorphism or an anti-homomorphism. For any
f\in C(X_{1})
and
a\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
, if it is a homomorphism, by (
5
) we have

\pi_{\rho}(J(f\otimes a)(y))=\pi_{\rho}(J(f\otimes 1)(y))\pi_{\rho}(J(1\otimes a)(y))\\
=f(\varphi_{\rho}(y))\pi_{\rho}(J(1)(y))\pi_{\rho}(J(1\otimes a)(y))=f(\varphi_{\rho}(y))\pi_{\rho}(V_{y}(a))\\
=\pi_{\rho}(V_{y}(f(\varphi_{\rho}(y))a))=\pi_{\rho}(V_{y}(f\otimes a(\varphi_{\rho}(y)))).

If it is an anti
*
-homomorphism, for any
f\in C(X_{1})
and
a\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
,
\pi_{\rho}(J(f\otimes a)(y))=\pi_{\rho}(V_{y}(f\otimes a(\varphi_{\rho}(y))))
holds similarly as above. This implies that
\pi_{\rho}(J(F)(y))=\pi_{\rho}(V_{y}(F(\varphi_{\rho}(y))))
holds for any
F\in C(X_{1})\otimes\mathcal{A}_{1}
. Since
C(X_{1})\otimes\mathcal{A}_{1}
is dense in
C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
, for any
F\in C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
, there exists
G_{n}\in C(X_{1})\otimes\mathcal{A}_{1}
such that
\|F-G_{n}\|_{\infty}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Since every Jordan
*
-homomorphism between
C^{*}
-algebras is contractive, we have

\begin{split}&\|\pi_{\rho}(JF(y))-\pi_{\rho}(V_{y}(F(\varphi_{\rho}(y)))\|_{B(H_{\rho})}\\
&\leq\|\pi_{\rho}(JF(y))-\pi_{\rho}(JG_{n}(y))\|_{B(H_{\rho})}+\|\pi_{\rho}(V_{y}(G_{n}(\varphi_{\rho}(y))))-\pi_{\rho}(V_{y}(F(\varphi_{\rho}(y)))\|_{B(H_{\rho})}\\
&\leq\|JF(y)-JG_{n}(y)\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}+\|V_{y}(G_{n}(\varphi_{\rho}(y)))-V_{y}(F(\varphi_{\rho}(y))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\\
&\leq\|JF-JG_{n}\|_{\infty}+\|G_{n}(\varphi_{\rho}(y)))-F(\varphi_{\rho}(y))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\\
&\leq 2\|F-G_{n}\|_{\infty}\to 0\end{split}

as
n\to\infty
. Thus for any
F\in C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
we get

\pi_{\rho}(JF(y))=\pi_{\rho}(V_{y}(F(\varphi_{\rho}(y)))),\quad y\in Y_{\rho}.

Finally we shall show that
\varphi_{\rho}:Y_{\rho}\to X_{1}
is continuous. Since
\rho\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{B}_{y})
for any
y\in Y_{\rho}
, we have
\pi_{\rho}(J(1)(y))=\operatorname{Id}_{H_{\rho}}
. By (
5
), we get
\pi_{\rho}(J(f\otimes 1)(y))=f(\varphi_{\rho}(y))\operatorname{Id}_{H_{\rho}}
for any
f\in C(X_{1})
and
y\in Y_{\rho}
. Let
\{y_{\lambda}\}\subset Y_{\rho}
be a net with
y_{\lambda}\to y_{0}\in Y_{\rho}
. For any
f\in C(X_{1})
we have
\pi_{\rho}(J(f\otimes 1)(y_{\lambda}))\to\pi_{\rho}(J(f\otimes 1)(y_{0}))
. Hence we get
f(\varphi_{\rho}(y_{\lambda}))\to f(\varphi_{\rho}(y_{0}))
for any
f\in C(X_{1})
. Let
U
be a neighbourhood of
\varphi_{\rho}(y_{0})
. By Uryzorn’s lemma there exists
g\in C(X_{1})
such that
g=0
on
X_{1}\setminus U
and
g(\varphi_{\rho}(y_{0}))=1
. Thus there is
\lambda_{0}
such that
|g(\varphi_{\rho}(y_{\lambda}))|\geq\frac{1}{2}
for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
. This implies that
\varphi_{\rho}(y_{\lambda})\in U
if
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
. We conclude that
\varphi_{\rho}
is continuous. We complete the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

.

Since

∎

Theorem 2.8
.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
are primitive. Then
J:C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})\to C(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
be a Jordan
*
-isomorphism if and only if there exist a homeomorphism
\varphi
from
X_{2}
onto
X_{1}
and a Jordan
*
-isomorphism
V_{y}:\mathcal{A}_{1}\to\mathcal{A}_{2}
for each
y\in X_{2}
so that the maps
y\mapsto V_{y}
and
x\mapsto V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(x)}
are continuous with respect to the strong operator topology such that

(6)

JF(y)=V_{y}(F(\varphi(y)))

for all
F\in C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
and
y\in X_{2}
.

Proof.

Firstly, suppose that
J
is of the form described as (
6
) in the statement. We shall prove that
JF\in C(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
for any
F\in C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
. Let
\{y_{\lambda}\}\subset X_{2}
be a net with
y_{\lambda}\to y_{0}
. Then

\begin{split}&\|JF(y_{\lambda})-JF(y)\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\\
&=\|V_{y_{\lambda}}(F(\varphi(y_{\lambda})))-V_{y}(F(\varphi(y)))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\\
&\leq\|V_{y_{\lambda}}(F(\varphi(y_{\lambda})))-V_{y_{\lambda}}(F(\varphi(y)))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}+\|V_{y_{\lambda}}(F(\varphi(y)))-V_{y}(F(\varphi(y)))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\\
&=\|F(\varphi(y_{\lambda}))-F(\varphi(y))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}+\|V_{y_{\lambda}}(F(\varphi(y)))-V_{y}(F(\varphi(y)))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\to 0,\end{split}

since the map
y\mapsto V_{y}
is continuous with respect to the strong operator topology. For any
F,G\in C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
, we have

J(F\circ G)(y)=V_{y}((F\circ G)(\varphi(y)))=V_{y}(F(\varphi(y))\circ G(\varphi(y)))\\
=V_{y}(F(\varphi(y)))\circ V_{y}(G(\varphi(y)))=JF(y)\circ JG(y)=(JF\circ JG)(y).

In addition we have
JF^{*}(y)=V_{y}(F^{*}(\varphi(y)))=(V_{y}(F(\varphi(y))))^{*}=(JF)^{*}(y)
for any
F\in C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
and
y\in X_{2}
. Thus
J
is a Jordan
*
- homomorphism. For any
G\in C(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
, we define a map
F
on
X_{1}
by

. Let
\{x_{\lambda}\}\subset X_{1}
be a net with
x_{\lambda}\to x_{0}
. Then

\begin{split}&\|F(x_{\lambda})-F(x_{0})\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\\
&=\|V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(x_{\lambda})}(G(\varphi^{-1}(x_{\lambda})))-V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(x_{0})}(G(\varphi^{-1}(x_{0})))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\\
&\leq\|V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(x_{\lambda})}(G(\varphi^{-1}(x_{\lambda})))-V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(x_{\lambda})}(G(\varphi^{-1}(x_{0})))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\|V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(x_{\lambda})}(G(\varphi^{-1}(x_{0})))-V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(x_{0})}(G(\varphi^{-1}(x_{0})))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\\
&=\|G(\varphi^{-1}(x_{\lambda}))-G(\varphi^{-1}(x_{0}))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}+\|V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(x_{\lambda})}(G(\varphi^{-1}(x_{0})))-V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(x_{0})}(G(\varphi^{-1}(x_{0})))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\\
&\to 0,\end{split}

as

is continuous with respect to the strong operator topology. This implies that
F(x_{\lambda})\to F(x_{0})
as
x_{\lambda}\to x_{0}
. Thus
F\in C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
. Moreover for any
y\in X_{2}
we have

\begin{split}J(F)(y)&=J(V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(\cdot)}(G(\varphi^{-1}(\cdot))))(y)\\
&=V_{y}(V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(y))}(G(\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(y)))))=V_{y}(V^{-1}_{y}(G(y)))=G(y).\end{split}

So
J
is surjective. Suppose that

, then there is

such that

. Then there exists

such that
\varphi(y)=x
and

. Thus
JF\neq JG
. So
J
is injective. Thus
J
is a Jordan
*
-isomorphism.

We shall show that the converse holds. By Theorem
2.5
, there exist a continuous map
\varphi_{\rho}:X_{2}\to X_{1}
for each
\rho\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{A}_{2})
and a Jordan
*
-homomorphism
V_{y}:\mathcal{A}_{1}\to\mathcal{A}_{2}
for each
y\in X_{2}
such that

\pi_{\rho}(JF(y))=\pi_{\rho}(V_{y}(F(\varphi_{\rho}(y))))

for all
F\in C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
and
y\in X_{2}
. Since
\mathcal{A}_{2}
is a primitive
C^{*}

such that
\pi_{\rho}
is faithful. We define

by

. Then we have

We show that
\varphi
is a homeomorphism. Let

such that
\varphi(y_{1})=\varphi(y_{2})
. As
\mathcal{A}_{1}
is a primitive
C^{*}
- algebra, applying the similar argument to
J^{-1}
, there exist a continuous map

and a Jordan

S_{x}:\mathcal{A}_{2}\to\mathcal{A}_{1}
for each

such that

, for all

. Then we have

(7)

F(x)=J^{-1}JF(x)=S_{x}(JF(\psi(x)))=S_{x}(V_{\psi(x)}F(\varphi(\psi(x))))

for all

.
In addition we also have

for all

3.
Jodan
*
-homomorphisms on
\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})

In this section we investigate Jordan
*
-homomorphisms between spaces of Lipschitz maps taking values in a unital
C^{*}
-algebra.
Throughout this section,
X
,
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are compact metric spaces. We see that
\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
is a non-commutative Banach
*
-algebra with identity. In addition we also define the algebraic tensor product. Let
f\in\operatorname{Lip}(X)
and
a\in\mathcal{A}
, where
\operatorname{Lip}(X)
is the space of all complex valued Lipschitz functions on
X
. We define
f\otimes a(x)=f(x)a
for any
x\in X
. The algebraic tensor product space of
\operatorname{Lip}(X)
and
\mathcal{A}
will denoted by
\operatorname{Lip}(X)\otimes\mathcal{A}
. It is easy to see that
\operatorname{Lip}(X)\otimes\mathcal{A}\subset\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
.
Let
J:\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})\to\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
be a Jordan
*
-homomorphism. Because it is not known whether Jordan
*
-homomorphisms in this setting are continuous or not, we assume that
J
is continuous with
\|\cdot\|_{L}
. If
a
is an element of a unital Banach algebra,
\sigma(a)
will denote the spectrum of
a
and
r(a)
its spectral radius.

Lemma 3.1
.

Then
F
is an invertible element in
\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
if and only if
F(x)
is an invertible element in
\mathcal{A}
for all
x\in X
.

Proof.

Let
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
such that
F(x)
is an invertible element in
\mathcal{A}
for all
x\in X
. Given
x_{0}\in X
, let
\{x_{n}\}
be a sequence of
X
with
x_{n}\to x_{0}
. Then
F(x_{n})\to F(x_{0})
. We set
\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{A})=\{a\in\mathcal{A}|\ a\text{\ is \ invertible}\}.
Since the map from
\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{A})
onto
\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{A})
by
a\mapsto a^{-1}
is continuous, we get
F(x_{n})^{-1}\to F(x_{0})^{-1}
. Thus
F^{-1}\in C(X,\mathcal{A})
and
\|F^{-1}\|_{\infty}<\infty
. For any
x,y\in X
, we get

\|F^{-1}(x)-F^{-1}(y)\|=\|F^{-1}(x)(F(y)-F(x))F^{-1}(y)\|\\
\leq\|F^{-1}(x)\|\|F(y)-F(x)\|\|F^{-1}(y)\|\leq\|F^{-1}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|F(y)-F(x)\|.

We have
L(F^{-1})\leq\|F^{-1}\|_{\infty}^{2}L(F)<\infty
and consequently,
F^{-1}\in\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
. The converse is clear.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that a Jordan
*
-homomorphism
J:\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})\to\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
is continuous with
\|\cdot\|_{L}
. Then
J
is continuous with
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Let
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
such that
F(x)^{*}=F(x)
for all
x\in X_{1}
. We firstly show that
r(F)=\|F\|_{\infty}
.
By Lemma
3.1
, for any
\lambda\in\sigma(F)
, there exists
x\in X_{1}
such that
F(x)-\lambda\cdot 1
is not invertible, and
\lambda\in\sigma(F(x))
. This implies that
|\lambda|\leq r(F(x))=\|F(x)\|\leq\|F\|_{\infty}
. As
\lambda\in\sigma(F)
, we have
r(F)\leq\|F\|_{\infty}
. On the other hand, there is
x_{0}\in X_{1}
such that
\|F(x_{0})\|=\|F\|_{\infty}.
Hence
\|F\|_{\infty}=\|F(x_{0})\|=r(F(x_{0}))=\sup\{|\lambda||\lambda\in\sigma(F(x_{0}))\}\leq\sup\{|\lambda||\lambda\in\sigma(F)\}=r(F)
. We conclude that
\|F\|_{\infty}=r(F)
. Fix
y\in X_{2}
. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(9)

\|(JF)^{n}(y)\|^{\frac{1}{n}}=\|J(F^{n})(y)\|^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\|J(F^{n})\|_{L}^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\|J\|^{\frac{1}{n}}\|F^{n}\|_{L}^{\frac{1}{n}}.

As
F(x)^{*}=F(x)
for all
x\in X_{1}
, we have
(JF)^{*}(y)=JF(y)
. By (
9
) we obtain

\|JF(y)\|\leq r(F)=\|F\|_{\infty}
, because
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|(JF)^{n}(y)\|^{\frac{1}{n}}=r(JF(y))=\|JF(y)\|
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|J\|^{\frac{1}{n}}\|F^{n}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}_{L}=1\cdot r(F)
. As
y\in X_{2}
is arbitrary, we have

\|JF\|_{\infty}\leq\|F\|_{\infty}.

For any
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
, we set
F_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(F+F^{*})
and
F_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}i(F-F^{*})
. Then
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are self adjoint elements of
\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
and
F=F_{1}+iF_{2}
. In addition, we have
\|F_{1}\|_{\infty}\leq\|F\|_{\infty}
and
\|F_{2}\|_{\infty}\leq\|F\|_{\infty}
. Thus
\|JF\|_{\infty}\leq\|JF_{1}\|_{\infty}+\|JF_{2}\|_{\infty}\leq\|F_{1}\|_{\infty}+\|F_{2}\|_{\infty}\leq 2\|F\|_{\infty}
and
J
is bounded with
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}.

∎

Since
\operatorname{Lip}(X,\mathcal{A})
is uniformly dense in
C(X,\mathcal{A})
, Lemma
3.2
implies that every continuous Jordan
*
-homomorphism from
\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
into
\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
admits a unique extension to a Jordan
*
-homomorphism
J^{\infty}:C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})\to C(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
J
be a continuous Jordan
*
- homomorphism from
\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
into
\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
. Then we define a Jordan
*
-homomorphism
J^{\infty}:C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})\to C(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
as a unique extension of
J
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by
J^{\infty}(C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}))
. For any
\rho\circ\delta_{y}\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{B})
, let
Y_{\rho}=\{x\in X_{2}|\ \rho\circ\delta_{x}\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{B})\}
. Then there exist a Lipschitz map
\varphi_{\rho}:Y_{\rho}\to X_{1}
and a Jordan
*
-homomorphism
V_{y}:\mathcal{A}_{1}\to\mathcal{A}_{2}
for every
y\in Y_{\rho}
such that

\pi_{\rho}(JF(y))=\pi_{\rho}(V_{y}(F(\varphi_{\rho}(y))))

for all
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
and
y\in Y_{\rho}
.

Proof.

Let
\rho\circ\delta_{y}\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{B})
. By applying Theorem
2.4
for a Jordan
*
- homomorphism
J^{\infty}
from
C(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
into
C(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
, we only need to show that the continuous map
\varphi_{\rho}:Y_{\rho}\to X_{1}
is Lipschitz continuous. For any
y,z\in Y_{\rho}
and
f\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1})
, we have

\|\pi_{\rho}(J^{\infty}(f\otimes 1)(y))-\pi_{\rho}(J^{\infty}(f\otimes 1)(z))\|_{B(H_{\rho})}\\
\leq\|J(f\otimes 1)(y)-J(f\otimes 1)(z)\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\leq L(J(f\otimes 1))d(y,z).

We get
\pi_{\rho}(J^{\infty}(f\otimes 1)(z))=\pi_{\rho}(V_{z}(f\otimes 1(\varphi_{\rho}(z))))=\pi_{\rho}(f(\varphi_{\rho}(z))V_{z}(1))=f(\varphi_{\rho}(z))\operatorname{Id}_{H_{\rho}}
for any
f\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1})
and
z\in Y_{\rho}
. Fix
y\in Y_{\rho}
. We set
f\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1})
by
f(x)=d(x,\varphi_{\rho}(y))
. For any
z\in Y_{\rho}
, we have

d(\varphi_{\rho}(z),\varphi_{\rho}(y))=\|\pi_{\rho}(J^{\infty}(f\otimes 1)(z))-\pi_{\rho}(J^{\infty}(f\otimes 1)(y))\|_{B(H_{\rho})}\\
\leq L(J(f\otimes 1))d(z,y)\leq\|J\|\|f\|_{L}d(z,y)\\
=\|J\|(\operatorname{diam}(X_{2})+1)d(z,y).

This implies that
\varphi_{\rho}:Y_{\rho}\to X_{1}
is a Lipschitz map.
∎

As a corollary of Theorem
3.3
, we have the following. This is a
slight extension of

7
, Theorem 1]
because we do not assume that
T(1)=1

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
be compact Hausdorff spaces. Suppose that
T:\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},C(M_{1}))\to\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},C(M_{2}))
is an algebra homomorphism. Let
\mathcal{B}
be the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by
T^{\infty}(C(X_{1},C(M_{1})))
. Fix
\delta_{\phi}\circ\delta_{y}\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{B})
, where
y\in X_{2}
and
\phi\in M_{2}
. Then there exist a Lipschitz map
\varphi_{\delta_{\phi}}:Y_{\delta_{\phi}}\to X_{1}
and a continuous map
\tau:M_{2}\to M_{1}
such that

TF(y)(\phi)=F(\varphi_{\delta_{\phi}}(y)))(\tau_{y}(\phi))

for all
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},C(M_{1}))
and
y\in Y_{\delta_{\phi}}
.

Proof.

As
\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},C(M_{1}))
and
\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},C(M_{2}))
are semi-simple commutative Banach algebras which are symmetric, every algebra homomorphism is an algebra
*
- homomorphism. Moreover any algebra homomorphism between semi-simple commutative Banach algebras is continuous. Hence,
T
is continuous with
\|\cdot\|_{L}
. Thus Theorem
3.3
implies that there exist a Lipschitz map
\varphi_{\delta_{\phi}}:Y_{\delta_{\phi}}\to X_{1}
and an algebra
*
- homomorphism
V_{y}:C(M_{1})\to C(M_{2})
for each
y\in Y_{\delta_{\phi}}
such that

\pi_{\delta_{\phi}}(TF(y))=\pi_{\delta_{\phi}}(V_{y}(F(\varphi_{\delta_{\phi}}(y))))

for all
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},C(M_{1}))
. For each
y\in Y_{\delta_{\phi}}
there is a continuous map
\tau_{y}
from
M_{2}
into
M_{1}
such that
V(a)(\phi)=a(\tau_{y}(\phi))
for any
a\in C(M_{1})
and
\phi\in M_{1}
. Hence we have

TF(y)(\phi)=F(\varphi_{\delta_{\phi}}(y))(\tau_{y}(\phi))

for all
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},C(M_{1}))
and
y\in Y_{\delta_{\phi}}
.
∎

V_{x}(a)=T(1\otimes a)(x)=T(1\otimes a)(z)=V_{z}(a)

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
are primitive. Then
J:\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})\to\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
is a continuous Jordan
*
-isomorphism if and only if there exist a lipeomorphism
\varphi:X_{2}\to X_{1}
and a Jordan
*
-isomorphism
V_{y}:\mathcal{A}_{1}\to\mathcal{A}_{2}
for each
y\in X_{2}
, where the map
y\mapsto V_{y}
and
x\mapsto V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(x)}
are Lipschitz continuous with respect to the strong operator topology such that

JF(y)=V_{y}(F(\varphi(y)))

for all
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
and
y\in X_{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
J
is of the form described in the statement. Since
\varphi
is a lipeomorphism, there is a positive number
L(\varphi)
such that
d(\varphi(y),\varphi(z))\leq L(\varphi)d(y,z)
for any
y,z\in X_{2}
.
For any
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
, we have

\|JF(y)-JF(z)\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}=\|V_{y}(F(\varphi(y)))-V_{z}(F(\varphi(z)))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\\
\leq\|V_{y}(F(\varphi(y)))-V_{y}(F(\varphi(z)))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}+\|V_{y}(F(\varphi(z)))-V_{z}(F(\varphi(z)))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\\
\leq\|F(\varphi(y))-F(\varphi(z))\|_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}+K\|F(\varphi(z))\|d(y,z)\\
\leq L(F)L(\varphi)d(y,z)+K\|F\|_{\infty}d(y,z)=(L(F)L(\varphi)+K\|F\|_{\infty})d(y,z).

This shows that
JF\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
. In addition we have
\|JF\|_{L}\leq(1+\max\{L(\varphi),K\})\|F\|_{L}
. Applying similar arguments, we obtain that
J^{-1}F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
for all
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
. Thus
J
is a continuous Jordan
*
-isomorphism from
\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
onto
\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})
.

Here, we prove the converse. By Theorem
2.8
, it remains only to show that
\varphi
is a lipeomorphism and the maps
y\mapsto V_{y}
and
x\mapsto V_{\varphi^{-1}(x)}^{-1}
are Lipschitz continuous with respect to the strong operator topology. We define
\varphi=\varphi_{\rho}
, where
\rho\in\operatorname{PS}(\mathcal{A}_{2})
such that
\pi_{\rho}
is a faithful non-zero irreducible representation of
\mathcal{A}_{2}
. Thus Theorem
3.3
asserts
\varphi
is a Lipschitz map and
JF(y)=V_{y}(F(\varphi(y)))
for any
F\in\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
. With a
similar argument as for
J^{-1}:\operatorname{Lip}(X_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2})\to\operatorname{Lip}(X_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1})
, there exists a Lipschitz map
\psi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
and a Jordan
*
-isomorphism
S_{x}:\mathcal{A}_{2}\to\mathcal{A}_{1}
for each
x\in X_{1}
such that
(J^{-1}F)(x)=S_{x}(F(\psi(x)))
. Since
\operatorname{Lip}(X_{i},\mathcal{A}_{i})
separates the points of
X_{i}
, we get
\varphi\circ\psi=\operatorname{Id}_{X_{1}}
and
\psi\circ\varphi=\operatorname{Id}_{X_{2}}
(This is discussed in more
detail in the proof of Theorem
2.8
). Thus
\varphi^{-1}=\psi
. We conclude that
\varphi
is a lipeomorphism. For any
a\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
,

\|V_{y}(a)-V_{z}(a)\|=\|J(1\otimes a)(y)-J(1\otimes a)(z)\|\\
\leq L(J(1\otimes a))d(y,z)\leq\|J\|\|a\|d(y,z)

for each
y,z\in X_{2}
. Since
V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(x)}=S_{x}
for any
x\in X_{1}
, we have

\|V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(x)}(a)-V^{-1}_{\varphi^{-1}(z)}(a)\|=\|S_{x}(a)-S_{z}(a)\|=\|J^{-1}(1\otimes a)(x)-J^{-1}(1\otimes a)(z)\|\\
\leq L(J^{-1}(1\otimes a))d(x,z)\leq\|J^{-1}\|\|a\|d(x,z)

for any
a\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
and any
x,z\in X_{1}
. Hence we finish the proof.
∎

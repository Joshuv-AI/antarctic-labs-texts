On singularly perturbed systems that are monotone with respect to a matrix cone of rank k k Thanks: This work was partially supported by a research grant from the ISF.
Ron Ofir, Pietro Lorenzetti, Michael Margaliot

Abstract
We derive a sufficient condition guaranteeing that a singularly perturbed linear time-varying system is strongly monotone with respect to a matrix cone C C of rank k k . This implies that the singularly perturbed system inherits the asymptotic properties of systems that are strongly monotone with respect to C C , which include convergence to the set of equilibria when k = 1 k=1 , and a Poincaré-Bendixson property when k = 2 k=2 . We extend this result to singularly perturbed nonlinear systems with a compact and convex state-space. We demonstrate our theoretical results using a simple numerical example.

a\leq^{\scriptstyle C}b,\;a\not=b\implies\Phi_{t}(a)\ll^{\scriptstyle C}\Phi_{t}(b)\text{ for all }t>0.

\displaystyle(\Phi_{t}(b)-\Phi_{t}(a))^{\mathsf{T}}P(\Phi_{t}(b)-\Phi_{t}(a))

\displaystyle=(\exp(At)(b-a))^{\mathsf{T}}P(\exp(At)(b-a))

PA+A^{\mathsf{T}}P\preceq-2sP-\sigma I_{n},\text{ for some }s\geq 0,\sigma>0.

II-A

Singular Perturbation Theory for LTV Systems

Consider the LTV singularly perturbed system

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=A(t)x(t)+B(t)z(t),

\displaystyle-{\varepsilon}\dot{H}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=H_{\varepsilon}D-B+{\varepsilon}H_{\varepsilon}L_{\varepsilon}B-{\varepsilon}(A-BL_{\varepsilon})H_{\varepsilon}.

{\varepsilon}\dot{z}_{\delta}=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}(x,z)x_{\delta}+\frac{\partial g}{\partial z}(x,z)z_{\delta},\end{gathered}

(18)

where the Jacobians are evaluated along the trajectory
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x(t)\\
z(t)\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\Omega_{x}\times\Omega_{z}

of (
III-B
). Note that (

) is a singularly perturbed LTV system.

We assume that there exists
h\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{r}};\mathbb{R}^{n_{f}})

g(x,h(x))=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{r}}

Then, the reduced model associated with (
18
) is

\dot{x}_{s}=\left[\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x,z)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}(x,z)\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}(x)\right]x_{s},

(19)

where

\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}(x)=-\left[\frac{\partial g}{\partial z}(x,h(x))\right]^{-1}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}(x,h(x)).

(20)

The boundary-layer associated to (
18
) is

{\varepsilon}\dot{z}_{f}=\frac{\partial g}{\partial z}(x,z_{f}+h(x))z_{f},

(21)

where here
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{r}}
is treated as a fixed parameter. Let

\begin{gathered}A:=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x,z),\quad B:=\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}(x,z),\\
C:=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}(x,z),\quad D:=\frac{\partial g}{\partial z}(x,z),\end{gathered}

(22)

where for simplicity we omit
the dependence of

on
(x(t),z(t))

\begin{gathered}L_{0}:=D^{-1}C\quad\text{and}\quad A_{0}:=A-BL_{0}.\end{gathered}

Assumption 9
.

The matrices
A,B,C,D

are continuously differentiable, bounded, and with bounded derivatives for any initial condition

(x_{0},z_{0})\in\Omega_{x}\times\Omega_{z}
of (
III-B

Theorem 10
.

Consider the singularly perturbed nonlinear system (
III-B
), and assume that
Assumption
9
holds. Suppose that there exist symmetric matrices
P_{r}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{r}\times n_{r}}

with
\operatorname{{\mathrm{I}n}}(P_{r})=(p,0,n_{r}-p)

P_{f}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{f}\times n_{f}}
with

\operatorname{{\mathrm{I}n}}(P_{f})=(0,0,n_{f})

\lambda_{r},\lambda_{f}\geq 0
and
\sigma_{r},\sigma_{f}>0
such that

P_{r}A_{0}+(A_{0})^{\mathsf{T}}P_{r}\preceq-2\lambda_{r}P_{r}-\sigma_{r}I_{n_{r}},

(24a)

P_{f}D+D^{\mathsf{T}}P_{f}\preceq-2\lambda_{f}P_{f}-\sigma_{f}I_{n_{f}},

(24b)

for all
(x,z)
emanating from initial conditions
(x_{0},z_{0})\in\Omega_{x}\times\Omega_{z}
of (
III-B
).
Let

\mathcal{P}:=\begin{bmatrix}P_{r}&0\\
0&P_{f}\end{bmatrix}.

Then there exists
{\varepsilon}^{*}>0
such that for any
{\varepsilon}\in(0,{\varepsilon}^{*})
, the system (
III-B
) is
p

\mathcal{P}
with rate
\lambda_{r}
.

Proof.

Fix
(x_{0},z_{0}),(\tilde{x}_{0},\tilde{z}_{0})\in\Omega_{x}\times\Omega_{z}

).

. Then

\displaystyle\dot{v}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(\bar{x},\bar{z})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\;v+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}(\bar{x},\bar{z})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\;w,

(25)

\displaystyle{\varepsilon}\dot{w}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}(\bar{x},\bar{z})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\;v+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial g}{\partial z}(\bar{x},\bar{z})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\;w.

By Theorem
7

24
)
imply that
(

{\varepsilon}>0

{\varepsilon}^{*}>0

) is

,

IV
Conclusion

Systems that are monotone w.r.t. a cone of rank
k
satisfy useful asymptotic properties. This follows from the fact that, roughly speaking, almost all bounded solutions of such a system can be injectively projected into a
k
-dimensional subspace, so essentially the dynamics is
k
-dimensional
[
16
]
.

We derived a sufficient condition guaranteeing that a singularly perturbed nonlinear system is monotone w.r.t. a matrix cone of rank
k
. The analysis is relatively simple and does not require using geometric singular perturbations theory. Another advantage of our approach is that it provides an explicit expression for the matrix
k
-cone
C_{\mathcal{P}}^{-}
.

We demonstrated our results using a synthetic example, but we believe that
it may be useful in the analysis of real-world systems, e.g., power systems.
Another possible direction for future research is to study the relation of our results to singularly perturbed
k
-contractive systems.

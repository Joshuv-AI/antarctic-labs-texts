Abstract
. We define a type of modulus dMod p \operatorname{dMod}_{p} for Lipschitz surfaces based on L p L^{p} -integrable measurable differential forms, generalizing the vector modulus of Aikawa and Ohtsuka. We show that this modulus satisfies a homological duality theorem, where for Hölder conjugate exponents p , q ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p,q\in(1,\infty) , every relative Lipschitz k k -homology class c c has a unique dual Lipschitz ( n − k ) (n-k) -homology class c ′ c^{\prime} such that dMod p 1 / p ⁡ ( c ) ​ dMod q 1 / q ⁡ ( c ′ ) = 1 \operatorname{dMod}_{p}^{1/p}(c)\operatorname{dMod}_{q}^{1/q}(c^{\prime})=1 and the Poincaré dual of c c maps c ′ c^{\prime} to 1. As dMod p \operatorname{dMod}_{p} is larger than the classical surface modulus Mod p \operatorname{Mod}_{p} , we immediately recover a more general version of the estimate Mod p 1 / p ⁡ ( c ) ​ Mod q 1 / q ⁡ ( c ′ ) ≤ 1 \operatorname{Mod}_{p}^{1/p}(c)\operatorname{Mod}_{q}^{1/q}(c^{\prime})\leq 1 , which appears in works by Freedman and He and by Lohvansuu. Our theory is formulated in the general setting of Lipschitz Riemannian manifolds, though our results appear new in the smooth setting as well. We also provide a characterization of closed and exact Sobolev forms on Lipschitz manifolds based on integration over Lipschitz k k -chains.

1.
Introduction

In
[
7
]
, Fuglede defined the modulus for a family of measures. He used this to define the modulus for a family of Lipschitz surfaces, extending the classical definition for a family of paths. In this paper, we give a definition for a new version of the modulus of a family of surfaces using differential forms, which we denote
\operatorname{dMod}
. We are able to prove that our definition possesses duality properties that Fuglede’s classical definition of the modulus of surfaces does not satisfy. The main theorem of the paper is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a closed Lipschitz Riemannian manifold. Let
c\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
be a non-torsion element of the rank
k
Lipschitz homology group. For all
p\in(1,\infty)
, there exists a unique element
c^{\prime}\in H^{\lip}_{n-k}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
so that the Poincaré dual of
c
maps
c^{\prime}
to
1
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(c)^{1/p}\operatorname{dMod}_{q}(c^{\prime})^{1/q}=1,

where
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
.

1.1.
Modulus and duality

The study of path modulus was initiated by Beurling and Ahlfors
[
1
]
in order to study quasiconformal maps. If
\Gamma
is a family of paths in
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}
, then the
p
-modulus
is defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Gamma)=\inf\bigg\{\int_{\Omega}\rho^{p}:\rho\in L^{p}(\Omega)\text{ and }\int_{\gamma}\rho\geq 1\text{ for }\gamma\in\Gamma\bigg\}.

The
2
-modulus of path families is preserved by planar conformal maps and is preserved up to a constant by planar quasiconformal maps.
It is also connected to capacity problems and the calculus of variations.

The
p
-modulus satisfies a duality property for topological quadrilaterals. Let
Q
be a topological quadrilateral with sides
E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}
and
E_{4}
arranged cyclically. If
\Gamma_{1}
is the family of paths connecting
E_{1}
to
E_{3}
in
Q
and
\Gamma_{2}
is the family of paths connecting
E_{2}
to
E_{4}
in
Q
, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{1})^{1/p}\operatorname{Mod}_{q}(\Gamma_{2})^{1/q}=1,

where
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
.

Fuglede
[
7
]
defined the modulus for a family of measures in an analogous way. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a family of measures on a set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. The
p
-modulus of
\mathcal{M}
is defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\mathcal{M})=\inf\bigg\{\int_{\Omega}\rho^{p}:\rho\in L^{p}(\Omega)\text{ and }\int_{\Omega}\rho d\mu\geq 1\text{ for }\mu\in\mathcal{M}\bigg\}.

Since Lipschitz surfaces always have corresponding surface measures, this definition also gives a definition for a family of Lipschitz surfaces. Fuglede was interested in duality results that resemble the duality for quadrilaterals in the curves setting and was able to prove such a theorem for the
2
-modulus of surfaces. In particular, he proved that the collection of all curves that connect a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\infty
has dual
2
-modulus to the collection of Lipschitz hypersurfaces that separate
K
from
\infty

[
7
, Theorem 9]
.

Gehring
[
8
]
and Ziemer
[
20
]
proved a similar duality results for the exponent
p=n
. Gehring showed, under smoothness assumptions on the surfaces, that the
n
-modulus of a family of curves connecting two compact continua is dual to the
n/(n-1)
-modulus of the family of surfaces separating them.
Ziemer extended the result to remove the smoothness assumption on the surface family.

A general duality theory for families of surfaces does not hold. This was observed by Freedman and He in
[
6
]
. However, they pose similar capacity problems, which do satisfy duality properties in the cases they are interested in, namely solid tori in
3
-dimensional space. They are also able to show that for families of surfaces
A
and
B
which are dual in a homological sense, one has

\displaystyle\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(A)^{1/p}\operatorname{Mod}_{q}(B)^{1/q}\leq 1,

where
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
.

In
[
13
]
, Lohvansuu studied the modulus of surfaces in Lipschitz cubes. Let
Q=h(I^{n})
, where
I=[0,1]
and
h
is a bilipschitz map. Let
A=h(\partial I^{k}\times I^{n-k})
and
B=h(I^{k}\times\partial I^{n-k})
. In this case, the relative homology satisfies
H^{k}(Q,A;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H^{k}(Q,B;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
. If
\Gamma_{A}
and
\Gamma_{B}
are the generators of the homology groups, Lohvansuu proved that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{A})^{1/p}\operatorname{Mod}_{q}(\Gamma_{B})^{1/q}\leq 1,

where
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
.

1.2.
Modulus with differential forms

The above results indicate that finding a modulus theory that gives a duality property is not trivial even in settings where the topology is not complicated, like a cube or torus. For this reason, in this paper we focus on a modulus that uses differential forms instead of densities.
In
[
2
]
, Aikawa and Ohtsuka defined a vector modulus that assigned a modulus to a family of vector valued measures. This is very similar to the modulus we define, however they only consider families of curves. They are able to show duality properties in this setting and relate their theory to capacity properties of sets.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a family of Lipschitz
k
-chains in
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. We define the
p
-differential form modulus
as

\displaystyle\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(\mathcal{S})=\inf\int_{\Omega}|\omega|^{p},

where the infimum is taken over all
k
-forms
\omega\in L^{p}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma}\omega\geq 1,

for
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}
, outside a set of zero
p
-modulus in
\mathcal{S}
. Excluding an exceptional set in
\mathcal{S}
is necessary, as noted in
[
2
]
and discussed in Section
8
. The differential form modulus is particularly suited to proving duality theorems.

We prove Theorem
1.1
by proving a more general theorem that includes manifolds with boundary. Let
M
be a closed
n
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold of an oriented boundaryless Lipschitz Riemannian reference
n
-manifold
\mathcal{R}
. Additionally, suppose that
\partial M=D\cup E
, where
D
and
E
are closed
(n-1)
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifolds of
\partial M
with
\partial D=\partial E
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
satisfy
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
. If
c\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;{\mathbb{Z}})
is a non-torsion element, then there exists a unique element
c^{\prime}\in H_{n-k}^{\lip}(M,E;{\mathbb{R}})
such that the Poincaré dual of
c
maps
c^{\prime}
to
1
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(c)^{1/p}\operatorname{dMod}_{q}(c^{\prime})^{1/q}=1.

Theorem
1.1
follows from Theorem
1.2
by letting
M=\mathcal{R}
and having
\partial M=D=E=\emptyset
. In the case where
H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_{n-k}^{\lip}(M,E;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
, as in the setting of
[
13
]
, we can strengthen our theorem to ensure that
c^{\prime}\in H_{n-k}^{\lip}(M,E;{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
satisfy
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
. If
H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_{n-k}^{\lip}(M,E;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
and
c\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;{\mathbb{Z}})
and
c^{\prime}\in H_{n-k}^{\lip}(M,E;{\mathbb{Z}})
are the generators of the homology groups, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(c)^{1/p}\operatorname{dMod}_{q}(c^{\prime})^{1/q}=1.

An immediate consequence of Theorem
1.3
is the following corollary, which generalizes the main result in
[
13
]
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
with
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
. Suppose that
H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{n-k}^{\lip}(M,E;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
. Let
c
and
c^{\prime}
be the generators of
H_{k}^{\lip}(M;\mathbb{Z})
and
H_{n-k}^{\lip}(M;\mathbb{Z})
, respectively. Then

\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(c)^{\frac{1}{p}}\operatorname{Mod}_{q}(c^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq 1.

As mentioned above, a version of Theorem
1.3
does not in general hold for the classical surface modulus defined by Fuglede, due to a counterexample given by Freedman and He in
[
6
]
. This counterexample is a solid torus with a
180^{\circ}
twist, the slices of which are dumbbell-shaped surfaces isometric to
S=B^{2}((1,0),2^{-1})\cup B^{2}((-1,0),2^{-1})\cup([-1,1]\times[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon])
. See Figure
1
for an illustration. A flat version of this example can be defined by
M=(S\times[0,1])/\sim
, with the equivalence relation
\sim
given by
(z,0)\sim(-z,1)
.

Figure 1.
The dumbbell-shaped twisted solid torus of Freedman and He that acts as a counterexample to a version of Theorem
1.3
for the classical modulus.

Indeed, in the context of Theorem
1.3
,
M
is an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian manifold with
H_{1}(M,\emptyset;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
. If
c\in H_{1}(M,\emptyset;{\mathbb{Z}})
is a generator, then the density
\rho
which is
1
in the handle
[-2^{-1},2^{-1}]\times[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\times[0,1]
and
0
elsewhere will be admissible for
c
, as any loop winding around
M
exactly once that doesn’t stay in the handle has to cross it. Hence,
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(c)\leq\varepsilon
. However, for a generator
c^{\prime}
of
H_{2}(M,\partial M;{\mathbb{Z}})
, by taking a constant density
\rho^{\prime}
in the end-part
(B^{2}((1,0),2^{-1})\cup B^{2}((-1,0),2^{-1}))\times[0,1]
of the dumbbell, one finds an upper bound for
\operatorname{Mod}_{q}(c^{\prime})
independent of
\varepsilon
. Thus, for small enough
\varepsilon
, one has
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(c)^{1/p}\operatorname{Mod}_{q}(c^{\prime})^{1/q}<1
.

Similarly to the planar case discussed earlier, the classical path modulus is connected to the theory of quasiconformal maps in that quasiconformal maps on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
preserve the
n
-modulus, up to a multiplicative constant. For surface modulus, only partial results have been shown. For example, Rajala in
[
15
]
showed a result for the image of sphere families by quasiregular maps. It is an interesting question whether there exists a class of
k
-surfaces that is invariant under quasiconformal transformations and has a reasonable definition for the
n/k
-modulus that is quasi-preserved by quasiconformal maps. A similar question can also be asked for the differential form modulus.

1.3.
Ideas of the proofs

We now outline the proofs of the main theorems in this paper. Since our setting is that of Lipschitz manifolds, there are technical difficulties in defining the integration theory for surfaces. This is mainly due to the fact that the Riemannian metric
g
for a Lipschitz manifold
M
is defined almost everywhere. Thus, the surface measure of an arbitrary Lipschitz
k
-chain
\sigma
on
M
, which is used to define the
p
-modulus and consequently
p
-exceptional sets, is not necessarily defined in an intrinsic way. We are however able to define
p
-exceptional families of Lipschitz
k
-chains on
M
without first defining the
p
-modulus of surfaces on
M
, essentially relying on the general modulus of measures and the quasi-preservation of surface measures under bilipschitz maps.

This definition of
p
-exceptional sets unlocks the requisite integration theory of
L^{p}
-forms over
p
-almost every Lipschitz
k
-chain on
M
and moreover allows us to define the classical
p
-modulus on
M
using the fact that
p
-almost every Lipschitz
k
-chain has a surface measure. Following this, we obtain versions of the de Rham theorem and Stokes’ theorem in the setting of Sobolev forms and Lipschitz chains. Our arguments take advantage of the integration theory of flat forms by Whitney
[
19
]
and Federer
[
5
]
, which also applies in our setting of Lipschitz manifolds.

We then prove a characterization of weak exactness and closedness for Sobolev differential forms via integration over Lipschitz
k
-chains. This characterization is similar in spirit to Fuglede’s original motivation for defining
p
-modulus. In
[
7
, Section 3]
, he showed that the
p
-modulus can be used to detect when vector fields have zero divergence or curl. In particular, our version generalizes Fuglede’s results to differential forms of arbitrary degree, applies on Lipschitz manifolds and also includes the characterization of weakly vanishing boundary values. The following theorem is a simplified version of this characterization not involving boundary values; the full statement can be found in Section
7
. While the theorem does not use differential form modulus, it is an essential ingredient in its study.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
M
be an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian
n
-manifold without boundary. Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
. The form
\omega
is weakly closed if and only if

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma}\omega=0,

for all
\sigma\in\partial C_{k+1}^{\lip}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
outside a
p
-exceptional set. Additionally,
\omega
is weakly exact if and only if

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma}\omega=0,

for all
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\cap\ker(\partial)
outside a
p
-exceptional set.

Following this, we define the modulus
\operatorname{dMod}_{p}
of differential forms and study admissible forms for a homology class
c
. An
L^{p}
-differential form
\omega
is
admissible
for
c
if

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma}\omega\geq 1

for
\sigma\in c
, outside family that is
p
-exceptional in terms of the classical modulus of surfaces. The key observation is that in this case, we can use the characterization theory outlined in Theorem
1.5
to show that
\omega
is in fact weakly closed, despite us having no regularity assumptions on
\omega
beyond
L^{p}
-integrability. This directly connects admissibility of forms for homology classes to Sobolev-de Rham cohomology theory.

With these preliminaries we are finally able to show the duality theorems, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. The proof uses the connection between
p
-differential form modulus and the capacity of differential forms, which was observed in
[
6
]
.
We first show that for a Lipschitz homology class
c
, the modulus problem admits a unique minimizer
\omega
. In particular, we prove in Section
8
that this minimizer satisfies the following properties.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
M
be a closed
n
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold in an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian reference
n
-manifold
\mathcal{R}
and let
D
be a closed
(n-1)
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold of
\partial M
. Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
c\in H^{\lip}_{k}(M,D;{\mathbb{Z}})
, with
0<k<n
. If
c
is not a torsion element, then there exists a unique differential form
\omega
such that
\omega
is
L^{p}
-integrable, weakly closed,
p
-harmonic,
\omega
has weakly vanishing tangential part in
D
,
|\omega|^{p-2}\omega
has weakly vanishing normal part in
E=\partial M\setminus D
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(c)=\int_{M}|\omega|^{p}\operatorname{vol}_{M}=\inf_{\tau}\int_{M}|\omega+d\tau|^{p}\operatorname{vol}_{M},

where the infimum is over all
L^{p}
-integrable
\tau
, with an
L^{p}
-integrable weak exterior derivative
d\tau
and with weakly vanishing tangential part in
D
.

We then connect the form
\omega
to Poincaré duality. In particular, if
M
is compact, then the Poincaré dual of
c
corresponds to a Sobolev-de Rham cohomology class
[\zeta]
, where we may assume that
\zeta
solves the capacity problem,

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left|{\zeta}\right|^{q}\operatorname{vol}_{M}=\inf_{\tau}\int_{M}\left|{\zeta+d\tau}\right|^{q}\operatorname{vol}_{M},

with
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
and with the infimum taken over Sobolev forms
\tau
. We prove that
\zeta
and
\omega
are in fact related to each other via the formula

\displaystyle\omega=\frac{|\zeta|^{q-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\zeta}{\|\zeta\|_{q}^{q}}.

This indicates the connection between capacity problems and the differential form modulus. The dual homology class
c^{\prime}
of
c
is then found as the Poincaré dual of
[\omega]
up to a sign and we prove that
\zeta
is a
q
-modulus minimizing admissible form for
c^{\prime}
. The class
c^{\prime}
a priori has real coefficients, but in the case where
H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{n-k}^{\lip}(M,E;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
, we are able to show that
c^{\prime}
in fact corresponds to a generator of
H_{n-k}^{\lip}(M,E;\mathbb{Z})
.

We remark that key parts of our theory rely on a technical result that Lipschitz homology classes on Lipschitz manifolds are never
p
-exceptional in the sense of the classical modulus of surfaces. Our proof of this fact ended up sufficiently involved to deserve its own section. Indeed, even though the ideas behind this are clear locally,
p
-exceptionality is a global property, so most standard local proofs appear to fail. Our approach uses a technique based on mollification and leads to a working proof that is mostly local, although the method becomes increasingly technical in the case involving relative homology.

Structure of this paper

In Section
2
, we recall Fuglede’s modulus and present the relevant theorems. In Section
3
, we recall the necessary background information regarding Lipschitz Riemannian manifolds and in Section
4
we discuss integration theory on Lipschitz Riemannian manifolds. In Section
5

6
, we prove that Lipschitz homology classes are not
p
-exceptional, a technical result we require for the duality theory. In Section
7
, we prove the characterization of weak exactness and closedness of differential forms outlined in Theorem
1.5
. In Section
8
, we define the differential form modulus and prove its basic properties. This includes a proof for Theorem
1.6
. Section
9
then includes the proofs of the main duality theorems for differential form modulus.

2.
Modulus of measures

We recall Fuglede’s definition for the modulus of a family of measures (see
[
7
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a complete measure space and let
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
be the family of all measures
\nu
on
X
such that every
\mu
-measurable set is
\nu
-measurable for all
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
. If
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
, then a measurable function
f\colon X\to[0,\infty]
is called
admissible for
\Sigma
if

(1)

\int_{X}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu\geq 1

for every
\nu\in\Sigma
. We denote the set of admissible functions for
\Sigma
by
\operatorname{adm}(\Sigma)
. The
p
-modulus
of
\Sigma
for
p\in[1,\infty)
is given by

\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma)=\inf_{f\in\operatorname{adm}(\Sigma)}\int_{X}\left|{f}\right|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu.

A family of measures
\Sigma
on
(X,\mu)
is called
p
-exceptional
if
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma)=0
. A property of measures is said to hold for
p
-almost every (
p
-a.e.)
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
if it holds outside a
p
-exceptional subset of
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
.

We recall some basic properties of the
p
-modulus (see
[
7
, Chapter I]
). Given a measure
\nu
, we use
\overline{\nu}
to denote its completion. Moreover, if
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
, then we denote
\Sigma\geq\Sigma^{\prime}
if, for every
\sigma\in\Sigma
, there exists
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
such that
\sigma(A)\geq\sigma^{\prime}(A)
for all
\mu
-measurable
A\subset X
. In this case, we say that
\Sigma^{\prime}

minorizes

\Sigma
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a complete measure space and let
p\in[1,\infty)
.

(1)

The modulus
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}
is an outer measure on
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
.

(2)

If
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
and
\Sigma\geq\Sigma^{\prime}
, then
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma)\leq\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma^{\prime})
.

(3)

If
E\subset X
is such that
\mu(E)=0
, then
\nu(E)=0
for
p
-a.e.
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
.

(4)

If
f\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
, then
f\in L^{1}(X,\overline{\nu})
for
p
-a.e.
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
.

(5)

If
f\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
and
f_{i}\to f
in
L^{p}(X,\mu)
, then there exists a subsequence
f_{i_{j}}
of
f_{i}
such that
f_{i_{j}}\to f
in
L^{1}(X,\overline{\nu})
for
p
-a.e.
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
.

The following lemma gives a characterization for
p
-exceptional sets.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
7
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a complete measure space, let
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
and let
p\in[1,\infty)
. The set
\Sigma
is
p
-exceptional if and only if there exists a function
f\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
such that
f\geq 0
and

\int_{X}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma=\infty

for every
\sigma\in\Sigma
.

For two sets
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
, we say that
\Sigma^{\prime}

weakly minorizes

\Sigma
if, for every
\sigma\in\Sigma
, there exists
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
and
c_{\sigma}\in(0,1)
such that
\sigma(A)\geq c_{\sigma}\sigma^{\prime}(A)
for all
\mu
-measurable
A\subset X
. In this case, we denote
\Sigma\gtrsim\Sigma^{\prime}
. Since the constant
c_{\sigma}
in the definition of weak minorization can depend on
\sigma
, there is no estimate on
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma)
in terms of
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma^{\prime})
if
\Sigma\gtrsim\Sigma^{\prime}
. However, we do get the following result for exceptional sets as an immediate corollary of Lemma
2.3
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a complete measure space, let
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
and let
p\in[1,\infty)
. If
\Sigma^{\prime}
is
p
-exceptional and
\Sigma\gtrsim\Sigma^{\prime}
, then
\Sigma
is
p
-exceptional.

A measurable function
f\colon X\to[0,\infty]
is
p
-weakly admissible
for
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
if (
1
) holds for
p
-almost every
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
.
The set of such functions is denoted by
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}(\Sigma)
.
It follows that

(2)

\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma)=\inf_{f\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}(\Sigma)}\int_{X}\left|{f}\right|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu.

If
p>1
, the infimum in (
2
) is in fact a minimum with a unique minimizer (see the discussion in
[
7
, pp. 181–182]
).

3.
Lipschitz manifolds

3.1.
Lipschitz manifolds without boundary

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a topological
n
-manifold without boundary, where we assume all manifolds are Hausdorff and second-countable. An atlas
\mathcal{A}
of charts
\phi\colon\Omega_{\phi}\to U_{\phi}
with
\Omega_{\phi}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
U_{\phi}\subset\mathcal{R}
is a
Lipschitz atlas
if, for any two charts
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{A}
, the transition map
\phi^{-1}\circ\psi\colon\psi^{-1}(U_{\phi}\cap U_{\psi})\to\phi^{-1}(U_{\phi}\cap U_{\psi})
is bilipschitz. A topological manifold
\mathcal{R}
equipped with a Lipschitz atlas
\mathcal{A}
is called a
Lipschitz manifold
. The charts
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
are called
bilipschitz charts
.

A
Lipschitz Riemannian metric

g
on
\mathcal{R}
is a choice of a measurable Riemannian metric
\phi^{*}g\in\Gamma(T^{*}\Omega_{\phi}\otimes T^{*}\Omega_{\phi})
for every chart
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
. . The metric satisfies that for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{A}
and
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, there exists
C_{\phi}\in[1,\infty)
so that
C_{\phi}^{-1}\left|{v}\right|^{2}\leq\phi^{*}g(v,v)\leq C_{\phi}\left|{v}\right|^{2}
a.e. on
\Omega_{\phi}
and
(\phi^{-1}\circ\psi)^{*}(\phi^{*}g)=\psi^{*}g
a.e. on
\psi^{-1}(U_{\phi}\cap U_{\psi})
.
A Lipschitz manifold
\mathcal{R}
equipped with a Lipschitz Riemannian metric
g
is a
Lipschitz Riemannian manifold
.

With a Lipschitz structure, we may define the set of measurable differential forms,
\Gamma(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
in the usual way, for
M\subset\mathcal{R}
measurable (see e.g.
[
16
]
). If
M
is orientable, we may also define a volume form, denoted
\operatorname{vol}_{g}
.
If
f\colon\mathcal{R}\to[0,\infty]
is a measurable function we can define the usual integral of
f
against the volume form using a continuous partition of unity. In particular,
\operatorname{vol}_{g}
induces a measure on
\mathcal{R}
. Moreover, if
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\Gamma(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\mathcal{R})
, we obtain a well-defined measurable inner product
\left<\omega,\omega^{\prime}\right>\colon\mathcal{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.
This allows us to define point-wise norms
\left|{\omega}\right|\colon\mathcal{R}\to[0,\infty)
for measurable
k
-forms a.e. on
\mathcal{R}
. We thus obtain the spaces
L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}D)
and
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}D)
for any open set
D\subset\mathcal{R}
in the usual way, where the
L^{p}
-norm is given by

\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}=\left(\int_{\mathcal{R}}\left|{\omega}\right|^{p}\operatorname{vol}_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

3.2.
Sobolev spaces on Lipschitz manifolds

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We recall that if
\omega\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
, then
d\omega\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k+1}T^{*}\Omega)
is a
weak differential of
\omega
if

(3)

\int_{\Omega}d\omega\wedge\eta=(-1)^{k+1}\int_{\Omega}\omega\wedge d\eta

for all
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\wedge^{n-k-1}T^{*}\Omega)
. The Sobolev space of all
\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
with a weak differential
d\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
is denoted by
W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
. Locally integrable versions
W^{d,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
are defined accordingly.

If
\Upsilon\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is another domain and
f\colon\Omega\to\Upsilon
is bilipschitz, then the Lusin
(N^{-1})
-property of
f
along with the almost everywhere differentiability of
f
yields a measurable pull-back
f^{*}\omega
on
\Omega
for any measurable
k
-form
\omega
on
\Upsilon
. We recall the following chain rule for such pull-backs, sketching its proof for the convenience of the reader (See also
[
4
, Lemma 2.22]
and
[
11
, Lemma 3.6]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega,\Upsilon\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be connected open domains, let
f\colon\Omega\to\Upsilon
be bilipschitz and let
p\in[1,\infty]
. If
\omega\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Upsilon)
, then
f^{*}\omega\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
and
df^{*}\omega=f^{*}d\omega
.

Proof.

Let
L
be the bilipschitz constant of
f
. If
\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Upsilon)
, then
f^{*}\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
for
p\in[1,\infty)
by the following standard change of variables argument:

(4)

\int_{\Omega}\left|{f^{*}\omega}\right|^{p}\leq\int_{\Omega}(\left|{\omega}\right|^{p}\circ f)\left|{Df}\right|^{kp}\leq L^{n+kp}\int_{\Omega}(\left|{\omega}\right|^{p}\circ f)J_{f}=L^{n+kp}\int_{f(\Omega)}\left|{\omega}\right|^{p}.

The same holds for
p=\infty
by using
\left|{f^{*}\omega}\right|\leq L^{k}(\left|{\omega}\right|\circ f)
and the Lusin properties of
f
.

Checking that
df^{*}\omega=f^{*}d\omega
weakly is also a standard approximation argument. Indeed, one selects
\Omega^{\prime}
compactly contained in
\Omega
, chooses a compactly supported
\omega^{\prime}\in W^{d,p}_{0}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n})
that coincides with
\omega
on
f(\Omega^{\prime})
and mollifies both
f
and
\omega^{\prime}
to obtain
f_{i}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\omega_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We have
df_{i}^{*}\omega_{j}=f_{i}^{*}d\omega_{j}
. Since
\left|{f_{i}-f}\right|\to 0
uniformly,
\left|{Df_{i}}\right|\leq L
a.e. on
\Omega^{\prime}
,
Df_{i}\to Df
pointwise a.e. on
\Omega
and each
\omega_{j}
and
d\omega_{j}
is uniformly continuous, dominated convergence yields that
f_{i}^{*}\omega_{j}\to f^{*}\omega_{j}
and
f_{i}^{*}d\omega_{j}\to f^{*}d\omega_{j}
in the
L^{1}
-norm on
\Omega^{\prime}
. Hence, it follows that
f^{*}d\omega_{j}=df^{*}\omega_{j}
. Then since
\omega_{j}\to\omega^{\prime}
and
d\omega_{j}\to d\omega^{\prime}
in the
L^{1}
-norm, (
4
) yields that
f^{*}\omega_{j}\to f^{*}\omega^{\prime}=f^{*}\omega
and
f^{*}d\omega_{j}\to f^{*}d\omega^{\prime}=f^{*}d\omega
in the
L^{1}
-norm on
\Omega^{\prime}
. Hence,
df^{*}\omega=f^{*}d\omega
weakly on
\Omega^{\prime}
. This generalizes to
\Omega
by the locality of weak differentials.
∎

For an open
U
in an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian manifold, we define that
d\omega\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k+1}T^{*}U)
is a weak differential of
\omega\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
if
\phi^{*}d\omega
is a weak differential of
\phi^{*}\omega
, for every bilipschitz chart
\phi
with
U_{\phi}\subset U
. This lets us define Sobolev spaces
W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
and
W^{d,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
. The space
W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
has the norm
\lVert\omega\rVert_{W^{d,p}}=\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}+\lVert d\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}
. It can be shown that
d\omega
is a weak differential of
\omega\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
precisely if it satisfies (
3
) for test forms
\eta\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{n-k-1}T^{*}U)
with
\spt\eta\subset U
compact.

We also state the following approximation results.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
U
be an open set in an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{R}
and let
p\in[1,\infty)
. The space
W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
is dense in both
L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
and
W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
.

Proof.

We sketch the case
\omega\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
, as the proof of the other case is analogous. Let
\varepsilon>0
. We take a countable locally finite cover of
U
with charts
\phi_{i}\colon\Omega_{i}\to U_{i}
,
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
and use a subordinate
W^{d,\infty}
-partition of unity to find
\omega_{i}\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
such that
\omega=\sum_{i}\omega_{i}
and
\spt\omega_{i}\subset U_{i}
. We take a smooth convolution approximation
\eta_{i}
of
\phi_{i}^{*}\omega_{i}
such that
\lVert\eta_{i}-\phi_{i}^{*}\omega_{i}\rVert_{W^{d,p}}<C_{i}^{-(k+1)p-n}2^{-i}\varepsilon
, where
C_{i}
is the constant such that
C_{i}^{-1}\left|{v}\right|\leq\sqrt{(\phi_{i}^{*}g)_{x}(v,v)}\leq C_{i}\left|{v}\right|
for every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a.e.
x\in\Omega_{i}
. The extension of
(\phi_{i}^{-1})^{*}\eta_{i}
by zero defines a
W^{d,\infty}
-form on
U
such that
\lVert\omega_{i}-(\phi_{i}^{-1})^{*}\eta_{i}\rVert_{W^{d,p}}<2^{-i}\varepsilon
. The sum of these forms gives the desired approximation of
\omega
.
∎

3.3.
Lipschitz domains and submanifolds

A connected, closed region
M\subset\mathcal{R}
in a Lipschitz
n
-manifold
\mathcal{R}
is called a
(weakly) Lipschitz domain
if it satisfies the following condition: For any
x\in\partial M
, there exists a bilipschitz chart
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
such that
x\in U_{\phi}
,
\phi(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}\cap\Omega_{\phi})=\intr(M)\cap U_{\phi}
,
\phi(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{-}\cap\Omega_{\phi})=(\mathcal{R}\setminus M)\cap U_{\phi}
and
\phi(\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}\cap\Omega_{\phi})=\partial M\cap U_{\phi}
. Here,
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>0\}
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{-}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}<0\}
are half-spaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A chart
\phi
as above is denoted a
bilipschitz boundary chart of
M
. Note that we allow for a Lipschitz domain
M
to be non-compact. More generally, a
closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold

M
of
\mathcal{R}
is a locally finite disjoint union of Lipschitz domains in
\mathcal{R}
; this is equivalent to the definition of a Lipschitz domain without the connectedness assumption. If a closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold is compact, we refer to it as a
compact Lipschitz
n
-submanifold
.

The boundary
\partial M
of a closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold
M
is a Lipschitz
(n-1)
-manifold, as the restrictions of
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
to
\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
provide a Lipschitz atlas
\mathcal{A}_{\partial M}
. For subsets
A\subset\partial M
, we use
\intr(A)
and
\partial A
to refer to the interior and boundary, respectively, in
\partial M
.

We can define
L^{p}
and
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-spaces of differential forms on a closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold
M
in the usual way. We consequently define Sobolev spaces
W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
by requiring that
d\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k+1}T^{*}M)
is a weak differential of
\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
in
\intr(M)
and also
W^{d,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-spaces with a similar definition. For the rest of this section, we let
M
be a closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold in
\mathcal{R}
,
U^{\prime}
be an open subset of
\mathcal{R}
and
U=U^{\prime}\cap M
.

Suppose that
D
is a closed Lipschitz
(n-1)
-submanifold in
\partial M
. Let
\omega\in W^{d,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
.
The tangential part
\omega_{T}
of
\omega
vanishes weakly on
D
if the extension of
d\omega
by zero to
(U^{\prime}\setminus\partial M)\cup(U\cap\intr(D))
is the weak differential of the extension of
\omega
by zero to
(U^{\prime}\setminus\partial M)\cup(U\cap\intr(D))
. This is only dependent on
U
,
M
and
D
and not the choice of
U^{\prime}
. The space of
\omega\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
with a weakly vanishing
\omega_{T}
on
D
is denoted
W^{d,p}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
and a similar notation
W^{d,p}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
is used for the locally integrable version.

The Lipschitz Riemannian metric
g
on
\mathcal{R}
defines a Hodge star operator
\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt_{g}
on
\mathcal{R}
. The weak codifferential is defined by
d^{*}\omega=(-1)^{n(k-1)+1}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt_{g}d\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt_{g}\omega
, whenever
\omega\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
is such that
\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt_{g}\omega
is weakly differentiable. The space of all forms
\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
with a weak codifferential
d^{*}\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}U)
is denoted
W^{d^{*},p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
and locally integrable versions of the spaces are defined accordingly.

For
\omega\in W^{d^{*},1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
, the
normal part
\omega_{N}
of
\omega
vanishes weakly on
D
if the tangential part
(\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt_{g}\omega)_{T}
of
\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt_{g}\omega
vanishes weakly on
D
. In particular, this occurs if the extension of
d^{*}\omega
by zero to
(U^{\prime}\setminus\partial M)\cup(U\cap\intr(D))
is the weak codifferential of the extension of
\omega
by zero to
(U^{\prime}\setminus\partial M)\cup(U\cap\intr(D))
. We denote the space of Sobolev
k
-forms
\omega\in W^{d^{*},p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
with weakly vanishing normal part on
D
by
W^{d^{*},p}_{N(D)}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
and a corresponding locally integrable version
W^{d^{*},p}_{N(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
is also defined.

We note that the spaces
W^{d,p}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
and
W^{d^{*},p}_{N(D)}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
are complete. Indeed, this is a direct consequence of the completeness of
W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
and
W^{d^{*},p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
, as taking extensions by zero past
\intr(D)
has no effect on
L^{p}
-convergence.

We prove a version of Lemma
3.2
for
W^{d,p}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
. For smooth domains
M
in smooth manifolds
\mathcal{R}
, the case
D=\partial M
can be found in
[
12
, Corollary 3.8]
. Our proof is somewhat similar to theirs, except the handling of charts that meet
\partial D
requires some technical modifications.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
M
be a closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold in an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian
n
-manifold
\mathcal{R}
without boundary, let
U\subset M
be open in
M
and let
D
be a closed Lipschitz
(n-1)
-submanifold in
\partial M
. If
p\in[1,\infty)
, then every
\omega\in W^{d,p}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
can be approximated in
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{W^{d,p}}
-norm by
\omega_{j}\in W^{d,\infty}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
with
\spt\omega_{j}\subset U\setminus D
.

Before we begin the proof, we recall the following reflection property of
W^{d,p}
-forms in the Euclidean setting. It is discussed in
[
12
, p.48, 50]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
B
be the unit ball
B^{n}(0,1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and suppose that
\omega\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}(B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}))
for some
p\in[1,\infty]
. Extend
\omega
to
B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{-}
by
\Psi^{*}(\omega)=-\omega
, where
\Psi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the reflection map defined by
\Psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\Psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},-x_{n})
. Then the extended
\omega
is in
W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Let
B
be the unit ball
B^{n}(0,1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We can take a locally finite cover of
U
with bilipschitz charts which are of the following three types:

(1)

\phi_{i}\colon B\to U_{i}
, where
U_{i}\subset\intr(M)
;

(2)

\phi_{i}\colon B\to U_{i}
, where
\phi_{i}(B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\subset\intr M
,
\phi_{i}(B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{-})\subset\mathcal{R}\setminus M
and
\phi_{i}(B\cap\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\subset\intr D
;

(3)

\phi_{i}\colon B\to U_{i}
, where
\phi_{i}(B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\subset\intr M
,
\phi_{i}(B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{-})\subset\mathcal{R}\setminus M
and
\phi_{i}(B\cap\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\subset\partial M\setminus D
;

(4)

\phi_{i}\colon B\to U_{i}
, where

•

\phi_{i}(B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\subset\intr M
,

•

\phi_{i}(B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{-})\subset\mathcal{R}\setminus M
,

•

\phi_{i}(B\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}=0,x_{1}>0\})\subset\intr D
,

•

\phi_{i}(B\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}=0,x_{1}<0\})\subset\partial M\setminus D
,

•

and
\phi_{i}(B\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}=0,x_{1}=0\})\subset\partial D
.

Similarly as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we can use a
W^{d,\infty}
partition of unity to reduce the question into the case where
\spt\omega
is compact and contained in a single chart neighborhood
U_{i}
. We only present the last case (
4
), as it demonstrates all the required techniques and the other cases can be shown by leaving out parts of the argument.

Suppose we are in the last case. We use another bilipschitz chart to further modify our domain. Define
\phi_{i}^{\prime}\colon B\to U_{i}
by

•

\phi_{i}^{\prime}(B\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}\geq\max(0,x_{1})\})=M\cap U_{i}
,

•

\phi_{i}^{\prime}(B\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}=0,x_{1}<0\})=U_{i}\cap(\partial M\setminus D)
,

•

\phi_{i}^{\prime}(B\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}=0,x_{1}=0\})=U_{i}\cap\partial D
,

•

\phi_{i}^{\prime}(B\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}=x_{1},x_{1}>0\})=U_{i}\cap\intr D
.

We extend
\omega
by zero outside
M
and let
\omega^{\prime}=(\phi_{i}^{\prime})^{*}\omega
on
B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
. By the weak vanishing of
\omega_{T}
on
D
, we have that this extension by zero is in
W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\phi_{i}(B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}))
. Consequently,
d\omega^{\prime}=(\phi_{i}^{\prime})^{*}d\omega
weakly,
\omega^{\prime}\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}(B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}))
and
\omega^{\prime}\equiv 0
on
\{x\in B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}:x_{n}<x_{1}\}
.

We then extend
\omega^{\prime}
to
B
via the reflection Lemma
3.4
. The form
\omega^{\prime}
is compactly supported in
W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
and
\omega^{\prime}
vanishes on the set of points
\{x\in B:\left|{x_{n}}\right|<x_{1}\}
. Let
\tau_{t}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the translation
x\mapsto(x_{1}-t,x_{2},x_{3},\dots,x_{n})
. If
j>0
, then for small enough
t
,
\tau_{t}^{*}\omega^{\prime}
and
\tau_{t}^{*}d\omega^{\prime}
are well-defined compactly supported forms in
B^{n}(0,1)
and

\int_{B^{n}(0,1)}(\left|{\omega^{\prime}-\tau_{t}^{*}\omega^{\prime}}\right|^{p}+\left|{d\omega^{\prime}-d\tau_{t}^{*}\omega^{\prime}}\right|^{p})<\varepsilon_{j}.

Moreover, the translated form
\tau_{t}^{*}\omega^{\prime}
vanishes in a neighborhood of the set
B\cap\{x:\left|{x_{n}}\right|\leq x_{1}\}
. Hence, this remains true for convolutions with a small enough mollifying kernel and we may use a convolution to pick a smooth approximation
\omega_{j}^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}(B\setminus\{x:\left|{x_{n}}\right|\leq x_{1}\}))
with
\lVert\omega_{j}^{\prime}-\tau_{t}^{*}\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{p}+\lVert d\omega_{j}^{\prime}-d\tau_{t}^{*}\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{p}<\varepsilon_{j}
. Then
((\phi_{i}^{\prime})^{-1})^{*}\omega_{j}^{\prime}
yields the desired approximation in the chart
U_{i}
, concluding the proof of this case.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Note that the same argument as above can be used in the smooth manifold setting to prove a smooth approximation version of Lemma
3.3
. The only change one has to do is to replace the condition
x_{n}\geq x_{1}
in the definition of
\phi_{i}^{\prime}
by a condition of the form
x_{n}\geq f(x_{1})
, where
f
is a smooth non-decreasing function such that
f(t)=0
if
t\leq 0
and
f(t)>0
if
t>0
.

We end this section with a lemma about wedge products and vanishing tangential parts, the proof of which takes advantage of Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
M
be a closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold in an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian
n
-manifold
\mathcal{R}
without boundary, let
p,q,r\in[1,\infty]
with
p^{-1}+q^{-1}\geq r^{-1}
and let
U\subset M
be open in
M
. Let
D\subset\partial M
be a closed Lipschitz
(n-1)
-submanifold and let
E=\partial M\setminus\intr D
. If
\omega\in W^{d,p}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
and
\tau\in W^{d,q}_{T(E)}(\wedge^{l}T^{*}U)
, where
0\leq l+k\leq n
, then
\omega\wedge\tau\in W^{d,r}_{T(\partial M)}(\wedge^{l+k}T^{*}U)
.

Proof.

By standard properties of wedge products of weakly differentiable forms, we have
\omega\wedge\tau\in W^{d,r}(\wedge^{k+l}T^{*}U)
with
d(\omega\wedge\tau)=d\omega\wedge\tau+(-1)^{k}\omega\wedge d\tau
. By Lemma
3.3
, we may approximate
\omega
by
\omega_{i}\in W^{d,\infty}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
such that
\spt\omega_{i}\subset M\setminus D
and
\tau
by
\tau_{i}\in W^{d,\infty}_{T(E)}(\wedge^{l}T^{*}U)
such that
\spt\tau_{i}\subset M\setminus E
. Consequently,
\spt(\omega_{i}\wedge\tau_{i})
does not meet
\partial M
and hence
\omega_{i}\wedge\tau_{i}\in W^{d,\infty}_{T(\partial M)}(\wedge^{k+l}T^{*}U)
. Since also
\omega_{i}\wedge\tau_{i}\to\omega\wedge\tau
in the
W^{d,r}
-norm on
U
, the claim follows by completeness of
W^{d,r}_{T(\partial M)}(\wedge^{k+l}T^{*}U)
.
∎

4.
Lipschitz chains and integration

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a Lipschitz
n
-manifold without boundary. A map
\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to\mathcal{R}
that is Lipschitz, where
\Delta_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
is the (closed) standard
k
-simplex, is called a
singular Lipschitz
k
-simplex
. Given a field of coefficients
\mathbb{K}
, a
Lipschitz
k
-chain on
\mathcal{R}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{K}
is a formal sum
\sigma=k_{1}\sigma_{1}+\dots+k_{m}\sigma_{m}
of singular Lipschitz
k
-simplices
\sigma_{i}
, where
k_{i}\in\mathbb{K}
. We denote the space of all such Lipschitz
k
-chains by
C^{\lip}_{k}(\mathcal{R};\mathbb{K})
. If
U
is a subset of
\mathcal{R}
, then
C^{\lip}_{k}(U;\mathbb{K})
is the space of all
\sigma=k_{1}\sigma_{1}+\dots+k_{m}\sigma_{m}\in C^{\lip}_{k}(\mathcal{R};\mathbb{K})
such that the image of each
\sigma_{i}
is contained in
U
. We also obtain a boundary map
\partial\colon C^{\lip}_{k+1}(U;\mathbb{K})\to C^{\lip}_{k}(U;\mathbb{K})
in the usual way.

4.1.
Lipschitz chains in Euclidean domains

We first go over the key properties of the space
C^{\lip}_{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a connected open domain.

Suppose that
\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to\Omega
is a singular Lipschitz
k
-simplex. Rademacher’s theorem yields a derivative
D\sigma(x)\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
for almost every
x\in\Delta_{k}
. We may define a corresponding Borel measure
\nu_{\sigma}
for
\sigma
on
\Omega
by

(5)

\int_{\Omega}\eta\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{\sigma}=\int_{\Delta_{k}}(\eta\circ\sigma)\left|{J_{\sigma}}\right|\operatorname{vol}_{k}.

for any Borel measurable function
\eta\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
. Here, the absolute value of the Jacobian
\left|{J_{\sigma}}\right|
is defined a.e. on
\Delta_{k}
by

(6)

\left|{J_{\sigma}}\right|(x)=\sqrt{\det D^{T}\sigma(x)D\sigma(x)}.

Moreover, we can extend this definition to Lipschitz
k
-chains
\sigma=k_{1}\sigma_{1}+\dots+k_{m}\sigma_{m}
by
\nu_{\sigma}=k_{1}\nu_{\sigma_{1}}+\dots+k_{m}\nu_{\sigma_{m}}
. Using the measure
\nu_{\sigma}
, we define the modulus of a family
\mathcal{S}
of Lipschitz
k
-chains, denoted

\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\mathcal{S})=\operatorname{Mod}_{p}\left(\left\{\overline{\nu_{\sigma}}:\sigma\in\mathcal{S}\right\}\right).

Let
\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to\Omega
be a singular Lipschitz
k
-simplex in
\Omega
and let
\omega
be a Borel differential
k
-form on
\Omega
such that
\left|{\omega}\right|\in L^{1}(\nu_{\sigma})
. We may define the integral of
\omega
over the surface
\sigma
. The pull-back
\sigma^{*}\omega
is a well-defined measurable form since
\omega
is Borel. Moreover, since
\sigma^{*}\omega
is a top-dimensional form in
\Delta_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|{\sigma^{*}\omega_{x}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|{\omega_{\sigma(x)}}\right|\left|{J_{\sigma}}\right|

and

(7)

\int_{\Delta_{k}}\left|{\sigma^{*}\omega}\right|\operatorname{vol}_{k}\leq\int_{\Delta_{k}}(\left|{\omega}\right|\circ\sigma)\left|{J_{\sigma}}\right|\operatorname{vol}_{k}=\int_{\Omega}\left|{\omega}\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{S}<\infty.

Thus,
\sigma^{*}\omega
is integrable. So

(8)

\int_{\sigma}\omega:=\int_{\Delta_{k}}\sigma^{*}\omega.

The definition extends to
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
linearly.

We crucially obtain a version of the Fuglede lemma for differential forms.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a connected open domain and let
p\in[1,\infty)
. If
\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
and
\omega_{i}\to\omega
in
L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
, then there exists a subsequence
\omega_{i_{j}}
of
\omega_{i}
such that

\int_{\sigma}\omega_{i_{j}}\to\int_{\sigma}\omega

for
p
-a.e.
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

The forms
\omega
and
\omega_{i}
are integrable over
\sigma
for
p
-a.e.
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
. Lemma
2.2
, (
5
) yields a subsequence
\omega_{i_{j}}
such that

\int_{\Omega}\left|{\omega_{i_{j}}-\omega}\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{\sigma}\to 0

for
p
-a.e.
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
. It follows using (
7
) and (
8
) that

\int_{\sigma}(\omega_{i_{j}}-\omega)\to 0

for
p
-a.e.
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
.
∎

4.2.
Wolfe’s theorem

We have a reasonable integral for
\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
over
p
-a.e. Lipschitz
k
-chain
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
. However, a much stronger result can be achieved for
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
. According to
Wolfe’s theorem
, there is a definition of an integral of
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
over
all
Lipschitz
k
-chains
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, despite the fact that
\omega
is defined only up to a set of measure zero. Standard references for this can be found in the books of Whitney
[
19
, Chapter IX]
and Federer
[
5
, 4.1.19]
. See also the work of Petit, Rajala and Wenger
[
14
]
, which uses methods closer to the setting of this paper.

We state the precise properties of the integral we require. The following Proposition
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
are reasonably immediate consequences from the results in
[
5
]
and
[
19
]
. However, for the reader unfamiliar with this theory, we have given a more detailed account of the proof with precise literary references in the appendix of this paper.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a connected open domain. There exists a unique bilinear map

W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)\times C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R},\quad(\omega,\sigma)\mapsto\int_{\sigma}\omega

with the following properties.

(1)

If
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
is smooth, then

\int_{\sigma}\omega=\int_{\Delta_{k}}\sigma^{*}\omega.

for all singular Lipschitz
k
-simplices
\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to\Omega
.

(2)

If
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
is Borel, then for every
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\int_{\sigma}\omega=\int_{\Delta_{k}}\sigma^{*}\omega,

for
p
-a.e. singular Lipschitz
k
-simplices
\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to\Omega
.

(3)

For all
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
and
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}\Omega)
,

\int_{\partial\sigma}\omega=\int_{\sigma}d\omega.

(4)

For all
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
and
\omega,\omega_{j}\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
where
j\in\mathbb{N}
, if
\omega_{j}\to\omega
almost everywhere pointwise and
\sup_{j}\lVert\omega_{j}\rVert_{W^{d,\infty}}<\infty
, then

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\sigma}\omega_{j}=\int_{\sigma}\omega.

(5)

If
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
,
\tau\in C_{k+1}^{\lip}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
and
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
, then

\left|{\int_{\sigma+\partial\tau}\omega}\right|\leq(\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\lVert d\omega\rVert_{L^{\infty}})(\nu_{\sigma}(\Omega)+\nu_{\tau}(\Omega)).

(6)

If
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is another domain,
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
and
\omega^{\prime}\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega^{\prime})
are such that
\omega|_{\Omega\cap\Omega^{\prime}}=\omega^{\prime}|_{\Omega\cap\Omega^{\prime}}
and
\sigma\in C^{\lip}_{k}(\Omega\cap\Omega^{\prime};\mathbb{R})
, then

\int_{\sigma}\omega=\int_{\sigma}\omega^{\prime}.

Suppose that
f\colon\Omega\to\Upsilon
is a Lipschitz map between two Euclidean domains. The push-forward
f_{*}\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\Upsilon;\mathbb{R})
for every
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
is given by
f_{*}\sigma=\sigma\circ f
for any Lipschitz
k
-simplex
\sigma
. The definition is extended to chains linearly.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega,\Upsilon\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be connected open domains, let
f\colon\Omega\to\Upsilon
be bilipschitz and let
p\in[1,\infty]
. If
\omega\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Upsilon)
, then

\int_{\sigma}f^{*}\omega=\int_{f_{*}\sigma}\omega.

for
p
-a.e.
\sigma\in C^{\lip}_{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
and for all
\sigma\in C^{\lip}_{k}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
when
p=\infty
.

Finally, in our applications, we will also have to consider integration of
W^{d,\infty}
-forms over Lipschitz
k
-chains that meet the boundary of a Lipschitz domain
D
. The following property of the integral will play a significant role in making that boundary part of the theory work.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and let
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\Omega)
. If
\omega\equiv 0
on
\Omega\cap\mathbb{H}_{+}^{n}
, then the integral of
\omega
over every
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega\cap\overline{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}};\mathbb{R})
vanishes.

Proof.

The claim is clear for all
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+};\mathbb{R})
by Proposition
4.2
part (
6
), since
\omega
vanishes in a neighborhood of the total image of
\sigma
. The part that requires attention is proving the result for a Lipschitz
k
-simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to\Omega\cap\overline{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}
that meets
\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
. Let
\sigma
be such a
k
-simplex.

The proof is via a standard flat approximation argument. For every
\varepsilon>0
, we define a map
H_{\varepsilon}\colon\Delta_{k}\times[0,\varepsilon]\to\Omega\cap\overline{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}
by
H_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\sigma(x)+(0,0,\dots,0,t)
; for small enough
\varepsilon>0
the image is indeed in
\Omega\cap\overline{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}
. Let
\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)=H_{\varepsilon}(x,\varepsilon)
. The map
H_{\varepsilon}
can be understood as a Lipschitz
(k+1)
-chain; its total measure is
\nu_{H_{\varepsilon}}(\Omega)=\varepsilon\nu_{\sigma}(\Omega)
and its boundary consists of
\sigma-\sigma_{\varepsilon}
and a part with total measure
\nu_{\partial H_{\varepsilon}-(\sigma-\sigma_{\varepsilon})}(\Omega)=\varepsilon\nu_{\partial\sigma}(\Omega)
. By Proposition
4.2
part (
5
), we get

\left|{\int_{\sigma-\sigma_{\varepsilon}}\omega}\right|\leq(\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\lVert d\omega\rVert_{L^{\infty}})(\varepsilon\nu_{\sigma}(\Omega)+\varepsilon\nu_{\partial\sigma}(\Omega))\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}0.

On the other hand,
\omega
vanishes in a neighborhood of
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
, so

\int_{\sigma-\sigma_{\varepsilon}}\omega=\int_{\sigma}\omega,

completing the proof.
∎

4.3.
Integration of
W^{d,\infty}
-forms in Lipschitz manifolds

We now have the necessary preliminaries to discuss integration over Lipschitz
k
-chains on Lipschitz manifolds. Suppose that
\mathcal{R}
is a Lipschitz Riemannian manifold without boundary with metric
g
and let
A\subset\mathcal{R}
be any subset of
\mathcal{R}
. Since we have given a definition for Lipschitz maps
\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to\mathcal{R}
in Section
3.1
, we obtain the spaces
C^{\lip}_{k}(A;\mathbb{K})
of Lipschitz chains on
A
with
\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Z}\}
.

Suppose that
U\subset\mathcal{R}
is an open subset of
\mathcal{R}
and that
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}(U)
. If
\sigma\in C^{\lip}_{k}(U;\mathbb{K})
, then we may subdivide
\sigma
into a
\sigma^{\prime}=k_{1}\sigma_{1}+\dots+k_{i}\sigma_{i}
such that every
\sigma_{j}
is a Lipschitz
k
-simplex contained in a single chart neighborhood
U_{\phi_{j}}\subset U
. Then

\int_{\sigma}\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{i}k_{j}\int_{\phi_{j}^{-1}\circ\sigma_{j}}\phi_{j}^{*}\,\omega,

where we use Wolfe’s canonical integral on the right hand side. By Lemma
4.3
, the right hand side integrals are independent of the choice of chart
\phi_{j}
. By considering a common refinement of two subdivisions, we see that the integral depends only on
\sigma
.

Next, we consider integration on Lipschitz submanifolds. Let
M
be a closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold in
\mathcal{R}
, let
U\subset M
be open in
M
and let
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
. Our previous definition lets us integrate
\omega
over all
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(U\cap\intr M;\mathbb{R})
, but we wish to extend this to all
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(U;\mathbb{R})
. By subdivision, it is again enough to consider integration over a single Lipschitz
k
-simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to U
contained in a chart neighborhood
U_{\phi}
, where the remaining non-trivial case is that
\phi\colon B^{n}(0,1)\to U_{\phi}
maps
B^{n}(0,1)\cap\overline{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}
into
M
and
B^{n}(0,1)\setminus\overline{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}
outside
M
.

The form
\phi^{*}\omega
is in
W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}(B^{n}(0,1)\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}))
. Hence, we can use the reflection Lemma
3.4
to extend
\phi^{*}\omega
to a form
\alpha\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B^{n}(0,1))
. The integral of
\omega
over
\sigma
is the integral of
\alpha
over
\phi^{-1}\circ\sigma
. In order to see that this is independent of our choices, we let
\psi
be another such chart and let
\alpha^{\prime}
be the extension of
\psi^{*}\omega
by reflection. Then
\alpha-(\psi^{-1}\circ\phi)^{*}\alpha^{\prime}
vanishes in the subset of
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
where it is defined. Lemma
4.4
shows that the integral of
\alpha-(\psi^{-1}\circ\phi)^{*}\alpha^{\prime}
over
\phi^{-1}\circ\sigma
vanishes and the same integral is obtained by using
\psi
and
\alpha^{\prime}
instead of
\phi
and
\alpha
. So the integral of
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
is well-defined over every
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(U;\mathbb{R})
, when
U
is an open subset of a closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold
M
.

We also note that the definition extends to forms
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
. This is since there exists an increasing sequence of sets
U_{i}\subset U
such that
U_{i}
are open in
M
,
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U_{i})
and
U=\bigcup_{i}U_{i}
. Every Lipschitz
k
-simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to U
is then contained in one of the sets
U_{i}
by compactness.

By using bilipschitz charts, subdivision of Lipschitz
k
-simplices and Proposition
4.2
part (
3
), we immediately have the following version of Stokes’ theorem for the integral of
W^{d,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-forms.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
M
be a closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold in an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian
n
-manifold
\mathcal{R}
, let
U\subset M
be open in
M
,
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(U;\mathbb{R})
be a singular Lipschitz
k
-chain and
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}U)
. Then

\int_{\partial\sigma}\omega=\int_{\sigma}d\omega.

We also require the following result for the canonical integral regarding boundary simplices.

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose that
M
is a closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold in an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian
n
-manifold
\mathcal{R}
without boundary and that
U\subset M
is an open subset of
M
. Let
D\neq\emptyset
be a closed Lipschitz
(n-1)
-submanifold in
\partial M
and let
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
. Then

\int_{\sigma}\omega=0

for every
\sigma\in C^{\lip}_{k}(D\cap U;\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Let
B=B^{n}(0,1)
. By subdivision, we may assume that
\sigma
is a Lipschitz
k
-simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to D
that is contained in the image of a boundary chart
\phi\colon B\to V
with
\phi(B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\subset U
and
\phi(B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{-})\subset\mathcal{R}\setminus M
. We wish to show that the integral of
\phi^{*}\omega
over
\sigma^{\prime}=\phi^{-1}\circ\sigma
vanishes. The case where
V\cap\partial M\subset D
is simple, as we may just extend
\phi^{*}\omega
by zero into
B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{-}
and use Lemma
4.4
.

The other case to consider is when
V
meets
\partial D
, in which case we can set
\phi
up so that
V\cap D=\phi(\{x\in B:x_{n}=0,x_{1}>0\})
. Let
\tau_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the translation map by
(\varepsilon,0,\dots,0)
. For small enough
\varepsilon>0
,
(\tau_{\varepsilon})_{*}\sigma^{\prime}
remains in
B
and the integral of
\phi^{*}\omega
over
(\tau_{\varepsilon})_{*}\sigma^{\prime}
again vanishes by a use of Lemma
4.4
. Similarly as in the proof of Lemma
4.4
, we can define
H_{\varepsilon}\colon\Delta_{k}\times[0,\varepsilon]\to B
by
H_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=(\tau_{t})_{*}\sigma^{\prime}(x)
and we have by Proposition
4.2
part (
5
) that

\displaystyle\left|{\int_{\sigma^{\prime}-(\tau_{\varepsilon})_{*}\sigma^{\prime}}\phi^{*}\omega}\right|

\displaystyle\leq(\lVert\phi^{*}\omega\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\lVert d\phi^{*}\omega\rVert_{L^{\infty}})(\nu_{H_{\varepsilon}}(B)+\nu_{\partial H_{\varepsilon}-(\sigma^{\prime}-(\tau_{\varepsilon})_{*}\sigma^{\prime})}(B))

\displaystyle\leq(\lVert\phi^{*}\omega\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\lVert d\phi^{*}\omega\rVert_{L^{\infty}})(\varepsilon\nu_{\sigma^{\prime}}(B)+\varepsilon\nu_{\partial\sigma^{\prime}}(B))\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}0.

We conclude that the integral of
\phi^{*}\omega
over
\sigma^{\prime}
vanishes.
∎

4.4.
Integration of
L^{p}
-forms in Lipschitz manifolds

Next, we consider integration of
\omega\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}\mathcal{R})
over Lipschitz
k
-chains on a Lipschitz Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{R}
. For this, we need to first define
p
-exceptional families of surfaces. The main technical complication is that you cannot use (
6
) to define
\left|{J_{\sigma}}\right|
for every Lipschitz
\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to\mathcal{R}
due to
g
only being defined almost everywhere.

We approach this as follows. Let
\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to\mathcal{R}
be Lipschitz. We can choose a subdivision of
\Delta_{k}
into finitely many
\Delta_{k,i}\subset\Delta_{k}
such that
\sigma(\Delta_{k,i})
is contained in the image
U_{i}
of a bilipschitz chart
\phi_{i}\colon\Omega_{i}\to U_{i}
for every
i
. Now, for every
i
, the map
\sigma_{i}=\phi_{i}^{-1}\circ\sigma|_{\Delta_{k,i}}
is Lipschitz and we can define a measure
\nu_{\sigma_{i}}
on
\Omega_{i}
with respect to the Euclidean metric. We then define

(9)

\mu_{\sigma}=\sum_{i}(\phi_{i})_{*}\nu_{\sigma_{i}}.

Once we have fixed a measure
\mu_{\sigma}
for every Lipschitz
k
-simplex, we can similarly define measures
\mu_{\sigma}
for every formal linear combination
\sigma=c_{1}\sigma_{1}+\dots+c_{k}\sigma_{k}\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\mathcal{R};\mathbb{R})
of Lipschitz
k
-simplices
\sigma_{i}
. We note that the measures
\mu_{\sigma}
depend on the choices of subdivisions
\Delta_{k,i}
and charts
\phi_{i}
for each
\sigma
. However, we still have the following.

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose
\mathcal{R}
is a Lipschitz Riemannian manifold and that
\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to\mathcal{R}
is a Lipschitz
k
-simplex. Suppose that
\mu_{\sigma}
and
\mu_{\sigma}^{\prime}
are both defined using (
9
), with different subdivisions and bilipschitz charts. There exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that
C^{-1}\mu_{\sigma}^{\prime}\leq\mu_{\sigma}\leq C\mu_{\sigma}^{\prime}
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{R}
is a smooth Riemannian manifold and if we define
\nu_{\sigma}
using (
5
) and (
6
) where
D^{T}\sigma(x)
is the adjoint satisfying
g(D\sigma(x)v,w)=g_{\text{eucl}}(v,D^{T}\sigma(x)w)
, then there exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that
C^{-1}\nu_{\sigma}\leq\mu_{\sigma}\leq C\nu_{\sigma}
.

Proof.

By a finite subdivision and use of bilipschitz charts, the claims reduce to the fact that if
\sigma^{\prime}\colon\Delta_{k}\to\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is Lipschitz and
f\colon(\Omega,g_{\text{eucl}})\to(\Omega^{\prime},g)\subset(\mathbb{R}^{n},g)
is bilipschitz, where the Riemannian metric
g
in the target is smooth, then
C^{-1}\left|{J_{f\circ\sigma^{\prime}}}\right|\leq\left|{J_{\sigma^{\prime}}}\right|\leq C\left|{J_{f\circ\sigma^{\prime}}}\right|
a.e. in
\Delta_{k}
. For a simple proof of this fact, let
x
be a point in the interior of
\Delta_{k}
such that
f\circ\sigma^{\prime}
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are differentiable at
x
and use the singular value decomposition of matrices to select an orthonormal basis
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that the image vectors
w_{i}=D(f\circ\sigma^{\prime})(x)v_{i}
are
g
-orthogonal. Now, the bilipschitz condition of
f
and Hadamard’s inequality yield that

\displaystyle\left|{J_{f\circ\sigma^{\prime}}}\right|

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left|{w_{i}}\right|_{g}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{d_{g}(f\circ\sigma^{\prime}(x+tv_{i}),f\circ\sigma^{\prime}(x))}{t}

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{i=1}^{k}C^{-1}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\left|{\sigma^{\prime}(x+tv_{i})-\sigma^{\prime}(x)}\right|}{t}

\displaystyle=C^{-k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left|{D\sigma^{\prime}(x)v_{i}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq C^{-k}\left|{J_{\sigma^{\prime}}(x)}\right|.

The other direction of the estimate is analogously obtained by using the singular value decomposition theorem on
D\sigma^{\prime}(x)
instead.
∎

In particular, Lemma
4.7
states that different choices in the construction of the measures
\mu_{\sigma}
lead to families
\mathcal{M}=\{\mu_{\sigma}:\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\mathcal{R};\mathbb{R})\}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\{\mu_{\sigma}^{\prime}:\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\mathcal{R};\mathbb{R})\}
such that we have weak minorizations
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\lesssim\mathcal{M}\lesssim\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. Therefore, these families have the same
p
-exceptional sets by Corollary

in the smooth setting. Hence, we can define that a subset

if the family of measures

is
p
-exceptional.

Before we proceed to define integration for

via

if there exists a constant

such that

. By Lemma

, this is independent on the choice of
\mu_{\sigma}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose
\mathcal{R}
is a Lipschitz Riemannian manifold and
\phi\colon\Omega\to U
is a chart.

(1)

Let
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\mathcal{R};\mathbb{R})
and
\sigma^{\prime}\colon\Delta_{k}\to\Omega
be a Lipschitz
k
-simplex in
\Omega
. If

(or any bilipschitz reparameterization) occurs in a subdivision of
\sigma
with

, then
\sigma
covers
\sigma^{\prime}
via
\phi
.

(2)

Let
\Sigma^{\prime}\subset C_{k}^{\lip}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
and

\Sigma=\left\{\sigma\in C_{k}^{\lip}(\mathcal{R};\mathbb{R}):\sigma\text{ covers }\sigma^{\prime}\text{ via }\phi\text{ for some }\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}\right\}.

If
\Sigma^{\prime}
is
p
-exceptional, then

is
p
-exceptional.

Proof.

If

occurs in a subdivision of

, then by further subdividing one can make it so that

is one of the measures added together to form

.
This gives the first property.

For the second, since
\phi^{*}\operatorname{vol}_{g}
is a.e. uniformly comparable with the Lebesgue volume, it follows that the family of measures

-exceptional in

-exceptional by Corollary

.
∎

5.
Sobolev cohomology theories

We next discuss the relevant parts of Sobolev cohomology theory on Lipschitz manifolds.

5.1.
Local Sobolev cohomology

Let
M
be a closed Lipschitz
n
submanifold in an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian
n
-manifold
\mathcal{R}
without boundary, let
D
be a closed Lipschitz
(n-1)
-submanifold in
\partial M
and let
p\in[1,\infty]
. We note that the map
U\mapsto W^{d,p}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
is a sheaf on
M
.

In order to define a cohomology theory in the Sobolev setting the only analytic tool needed is a local exactness result. This is provided by a Sobolev variant of the Poincaré lemma. A version in the Euclidean setting with
D=\emptyset
can be found in
[
10
, Section 4]
. We give the version of the result that we require, relying heavily on the exposition in
[
10
, Section 4]
for the proof.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
M
be a closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold in an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian
n
-manifold
\mathcal{R}
without boundary, let
p\in[1,\infty]
and let
D
be a closed Lipschitz
(n-1)
-submanifold in
\partial M
.
Let
x\in M
,
k\geq 1
,
U
a neighborhood of
x
in
M
and suppose that
\omega\in W^{d,p}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
is such that
d\omega=0
. There exists a neighborhood
V\subset U
of
x
and a form
\tau\in W^{d,p}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}V)
such that
\omega|_{V}=d\tau
.

Proof.

We first recall an explicit construction for
\tau
in the Euclidean setting. Let
B=B^{n}(0,1)
. Fix
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B)
such that
\eta\geq 0
and
\int_{B}\eta=1
and define a linear operator
T\colon C^{\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)\to C^{\infty}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}B)
by

(10)

(T\omega)_{x}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})=\int_{B}\eta(y)\int_{0}^{1}t^{k-1}\omega_{tx+(1-t)y}(x-y,v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Then
\omega=dT(\omega)+T(d\omega)
and
\lVert T\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq C_{\eta}\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and all
\omega\in C^{\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
(see
[
10
, (4.6) and (4.15)]
). If
p\in[1,\infty)
, then
C^{\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
is dense in
L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
and hence
T
extends to a bounded linear map
T\colon L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)\to L^{p}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}B)
.
Moreover, a similar extension remains valid for
p=\infty
even though
C^{\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
is not dense in
L^{\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
.
Indeed, we may smoothly approximate
\omega\in L^{\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
with
\omega_{j}\in C^{\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
such that
\lVert\omega_{j}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
and
\lVert\omega_{j}-\omega\rVert_{L^{1}}\to 0
.
In which case,
T(\omega_{j})
converges pointwise to a unique
T(\omega)\in L^{1}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
and the uniform bound
\lVert T(\omega_{j})\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{\eta}\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
yields that
\lVert T(\omega)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{\eta}\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
.

If
p\in[1,\infty)
, it follows by smooth approximation that
T(\omega)\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}B)
and
\omega=dT(\omega)+T(d\omega)
if
\omega\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
(see
[
10
, Lemma 4.2]
). The same is also true for
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
, since
W^{d,\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)\subset W^{d,1}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
implies that
dT(\omega)=\omega-T(d\omega)
weakly and hence
\lVert dT(\omega)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\lVert T(d\omega)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
. In the Euclidean setting, if
\omega\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
weakly, then a choice of
\tau=T(\omega)
yields
\tau\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}B)
and
d\tau=\omega
weakly.

We return to the Lipschitz manifold setting. Suppose first that
x\in\intr(M)
. We can choose a bilipschitz chart
\phi\colon B\to V
such that
x\in V\subset U\cap\intr(M)
. The Euclidean result implies that
\phi^{*}\omega\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
and the desired
\tau\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}V)
is given by
\phi^{*}\tau=T(\phi^{*}\omega)
.

The next case we consider is when
x\in\partial M\setminus D
. There exists a bilipschitz chart
\phi\colon B\to V
such that
x\in V
,
V\cap M\subset U
,
V\cap D=\emptyset
,
\phi(B\cap\overline{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}})=V\cap M
and
\phi(B\setminus\overline{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}})=V\setminus M
. In this case, the reflection Lemma
3.4
extends
\phi^{*}\omega
to
B
. A valid choice of
\tau
is given by
\phi^{*}\tau=T(\phi^{*}\omega)
on
B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
and
\phi^{*}\tau=0
on
B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{-}
.

Next, we consider the case
x\in\intr D
. There exists a bilipschitz chart
\phi\colon B\to V
such that
x\in V
,
V\cap M\subset U
,
V\cap\partial M\subset\intr D
,
\phi(B\cap\overline{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}})=V\cap M
and
\phi(B\setminus\overline{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}})=V\setminus M
. If we extend
\phi^{*}\omega
by zero to
B
, the extension is in
W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
with
\spt(\phi^{*}\omega)\subset\overline{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}
. By selecting the
\eta
in the definition of

to satisfy
\spt\eta\subset B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{-}
and since

is convex, it follows from (

) that
T(\phi^{*}\omega)=0
on
B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{-}
(see also the comment before
[
10
, (4.14)]
). Thus,
\phi^{*}\tau=T(\phi^{*}\omega)
remains a valid choice that vanishes weakly on
D
.

We have one more remaining case, which is

. For this case, we select the bilipschitz chart
\phi\colon B\to V
, where
\phi(B\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}=0,x_{1}<0\})=V\cap(\partial M\setminus D)
and
\phi(B\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}=x_{1},x_{1}>0\})=V\cap\intr D
. By extending
\phi^{*}\omega
by zero to
B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
and then further extending to
B
by Lemma
3.4
, we obtain an extension
\phi^{*}\omega\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}B)
that vanishes in
H=B\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left|{x_{n}}\right|<x_{1}\}
. Choosing
\eta
such that
\spt\eta\subset H
,

vanishes on
H
due to convexity of
H
. The desired
\tau
is given by
\phi^{*}\tau=T(\phi^{*}\omega)
in
B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
and
\phi^{*}\tau=0
in
B\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{-}
.
∎

Let
H_{p}^{k}(M,D)
denote the degree

W^{d,p}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{*}T^{*}M)
. We call

the degree
k

-cohomology of the pair
(M,D)
. Due to the Poincaré lemma shown in Lemma
5.1
, the methods of sheaf cohomology now apply to the spaces

. We begin with the following corollary of the Poincaré lemma. The proof assumes familiarity with sheaf theory and sheaf cohomology; for standard reference works on the topic, we refer the reader to the comprehensive treatment in
[
3
]
, as well as the more focused introductions in
[
17
, Chapter 5]
and
[
18
, Chapter II]
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
M
be a closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold in an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian
n
-manifold
\mathcal{R}
without boundary, let
p\in[1,\infty)
and let
D
be a closed Lipschitz
(n-1)
-submanifold in
\partial M
. The relative
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-cohomology spaces
H_{p}^{k}(M,D)
and

are isomorphic as vector spaces, with the isomorphism induced by the inclusion maps
W^{d,\infty}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)\hookrightarrow W^{d,p}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{*}T^{k}M)
.

Proof.

Based on the Poincaré Lemma
5.1
, we obtain that the graded differential sheaves
\mathcal{W}_{p,D}^{*}\colon U\mapsto W^{d,p}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{*}T^{*}U)
and
\mathcal{W}_{\infty,D}^{*}\colon U\mapsto W^{d,\infty}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{*}T^{*}U)
on

each form a fine torsionless resolution of the sheaf
\mathcal{R}_{D}
of locally constant functions on

which vanish on

. Since the inclusion maps
W^{d,\infty}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)\hookrightarrow W^{d,p}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{*}T^{k}M)
form a sheaf homomorphism of resolutions, they must induce an isomorphism in cohomology by
[
3
, II.4.2]
.
∎

5.2.
Lipschitz homology and the de Rham theorem

On smooth manifolds
M
, the de Rham theorem shows that the cohomology of the complex
C^{\infty}(\wedge^{*}T^{*}M)
is dual to singular homology and the duality map is explicitly given by integration over smooth simplices. Since we can integrate
W^{d,\infty}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{*}T^{*}M)
-forms over Lipschitz simplices, a similar explicit duality relation between Lipschitz homology and
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-cohomology exists.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
M
be a closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold in an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian
n
-manifold
\mathcal{R}
without boundary, let
p\in[1,\infty]
and let
D
be a closed Lipschitz
(n-1)
-submanifold in
\partial M
. There is a canonical isomorphism

H^{k}_{\infty}(M,D)\cong\hom(H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{R})\cong\hom(H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{R}),

which, for
[\omega]\in H^{k}_{\infty}(M,D)
and
[\sigma]\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{K})
with
\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}\}
, is given by

(11)

[\omega]([\sigma])=\int_{\sigma}\omega.

Moreover, this isomorphism maps the wedge product to the cup product on the level of cohomology classes: if
E=\partial M\setminus D
, then
[\omega]\cup[\omega^{\prime}]=[\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime}]
for all
[\omega]\in H^{k}_{\infty}(M,D),[\omega^{\prime}]\in H^{l}_{\infty}(M,E)
, where

Proposition 6.1

Let

We present the argument in increasingly general cases, building up to Proposition

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
M
be an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian manifold without boundary and let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
k\in\{0,\dots,\infty\}

[\sigma]\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M;\mathbb{K})

\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}\}

[\sigma]
is not
p
-exceptional.

Idea of proof.

Suppose that
c\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M;\mathbb{K})
is not
p
-exceptional. Then there is a function
\rho\in L^{p}(M)
such that the integral of
\rho
over the measure of every
\sigma\in c
is infinite. The idea is to select an arbitrary chain
\sigma\in c
and to construct a function
\rho^{\prime}
by smoothing
\rho
in a neighborhood of the total image of
\sigma
. By the averaging properties of the convolution, the property that the integral of
\rho^{\prime}
over the measure of
\sigma
is infinite remains true. However, the resulting

will be continuous and finite-valued on the compact total image of

and

will therefore have a maximal value on it. This is a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Lemma
6.2
.

Suppose towards contradiction that

\rho

\mu_{\sigma}
,
\sigma\in c

We select bilipschitz charts

We fix

(12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{M}\rho_{i}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{\sigma}(x)&=\int_{M}\int_{B^{n}(0,1)}\rho_{i-1}\circ F_{i,v}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{\sigma}(x)\\

\displaystyle\left|{\rho_{i}\circ\phi_{i}(x)-\rho_{i}\circ\phi_{i}(x_{k})}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{\eta^{n}(x)}\int_{(B_{x}\cup B_{x,k})\setminus(B_{x}\cap B_{x,k})}\rho_{i-1}\circ\phi_{i}+\left|{\frac{1}{\eta^{n}(x_{k})}-\frac{1}{\eta^{n}(x)}}\right|\int_{B^{n}(0,R)}\rho_{i-1}\circ\phi_{i},

Idea of the proof of Proposition
6.1
.

We use a locally Lipschitz collar neighborhood to find a smaller submanifold

\rho
by zero to all of

\sigma_{\varepsilon}\in c_{\varepsilon}

The strategy is again to construct a

The idea for this is that after any of the translations used to perform the convolution, the part of

will be an element of

and hence

will have infinite integral over it. A major technical issue, however, is that the intersection of a Lipschitz chain and a Lipschitz subdomain is not always a Lipschitz chain: a simple example is the graph of a Lipschitz function

and the fact that any boundary translations in our convolution are inwards into

Since

8.
Modulus with differential forms

In this section we define a version of modulus based on differential forms. This modulus will exhibit duality properties for dual homology classes, which we will prove in the following section. For the rest of this section, let
M
be an closed Lipschitz
n
-submanifold in an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian
n
-manifold
\mathcal{R}
without boundary and let
D\subset\partial M
be a closed Lipschitz
(n-1)
-submanifold of
\partial M
, with all the cases
\partial M=\emptyset
,
D=\emptyset
and
\partial D=\emptyset
permitted.

Before we give the definition for differential form modulus, we define the standard surface modulus
\operatorname{Mod}(\Sigma)
for families
\Sigma
of Lipschitz
k
-chains on
M
. The difficulty in the definition of the standard modulus is that the measure of a Lipschitz
k
-chain is not canonically defined, as discussed in Section
4.4
. However, we have given a valid definition of
p
-exceptional families of
k
-chains in Section
4.4
and by Lemma
4.11
and the surrounding discussion, we may define
\nu_{\sigma}
for
p
-a.e. Lipschitz
k
-chain
\sigma
.

Hence, for any
\Sigma\subset C^{\lip}_{k}(M;\mathbb{R})
, we select a
p
-exceptional family
\Sigma_{p}\subset\Sigma
such that, for every
\sigma\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{p}
and every Lipschitz
k
-simplex
\sigma_{i}
in the sum representation of
\sigma
, we have that
\nu_{\sigma_{i}}
exists and is uniformly comparable with
\mu_{\sigma_{i}}
, where
\mu_{\sigma_{i}}
is the measure on
\sigma_{i}
that is defined via charts in Section
4.4
. We then define

\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\{\nu_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{p}\}).

We also denote
\rho\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}(\Sigma)
if
\rho\in\operatorname{adm}(\{\nu_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{p}\})
for some
p
-exceptional
\Sigma_{p}
as above. This definition of
p
-modulus of surface families is consistent with the Euclidean one by (
2
). Moreover, for another choice of exceptional set
\Sigma_{p}
, we observe that
\{\nu_{\sigma}:\sigma\in(\Sigma_{p}\setminus\Sigma_{p}^{\prime})\cup(\Sigma_{p}^{\prime}\setminus\Sigma_{p})\}
is
p
-exceptional: Indeed, if
\sigma\in\Sigma_{p}^{\prime}\setminus\Sigma_{p}
, then
\nu_{\sigma}
is uniformly comparable with
\mu_{\sigma}
and hence
\{\nu_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\Sigma_{p}^{\prime}\setminus\Sigma_{p}\}\gtrsim\{\mu_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\Sigma_{p}^{\prime}\}
where the latter is
p
-exceptional. Hence, the value of
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma)
is independent on the choice of
\Sigma_{p}
.

We also give a lemma related to exceptional sets of formal sums that is of use to us later.
Given two families of formal sums
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
, we denote by
\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime}
the family of all formal sums
\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}
where
\sigma\in\Sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
be families of Lipschitz
k
-chains on
M
with
k<n
and let
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then

\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime})\geq 2^{-p}\min(\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma),\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma^{\prime})).

In particular, if
\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime}
is
p
-exceptional, then
\Sigma
or
\Sigma^{\prime}
is
p
-exceptional.

Proof.

We fix
p
-exceptional families
\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma}
,
\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime}}
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\{\nu_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\Sigma\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma}\}),\qquad\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma^{\prime})=\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\{\nu_{\sigma^{\prime}}:\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\}),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime})=\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\{\nu_{\sigma}:\sigma\in(\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime})\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime}}\}).

Note that by shrinking
\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime}}
, we may assume that
\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime}}\cap((\Sigma\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma})+(\Sigma^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}))=\emptyset
. Indeed, if we suppose that
\sigma\in\Sigma\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma}
and
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
, then for all singular
k
-simplices
\sigma_{i}
in the sum representations of
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
, the surface measures
\nu_{\sigma_{i}}
exist and are comparable to
\mu_{\sigma_{i}}
. Hence, we may remove
\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}
from
\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime}}
if needed.

Next, let
\sigma=a_{1}\sigma_{1}+\dots+a_{m}\sigma_{m}\in\Sigma\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma}
. Let
\Sigma^{\prime}_{\sigma}
denote the collection of all
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
such that
\nu_{\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}}\neq\nu_{\sigma}+\nu_{\sigma^{\prime}}
.
Every
\sigma^{\prime}=a_{1}^{\prime}\sigma_{1}^{\prime}+\dots+a^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}\sigma^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{\sigma}
shares a positive-measured surface with
\sigma
; i.e.
\sigma_{i}=\sigma^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}
for some indices
i
and
i^{\prime}
,
a_{i}
and
a^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}
have opposite signs and
\nu_{\sigma_{i}}\neq 0
.

The family
\Sigma^{\prime}_{i}
of all
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
containing
\sigma_{i}
in their formal sum representation is in fact
p
-exceptional, as long as
\nu_{\sigma_{i}}\neq 0
. Indeed, the density
\rho
which is
\infty
on the image of
\sigma_{i}
and
0
elsewhere is essentially admissible for
\Sigma^{\prime}_{i}
.
Since
k<n
, the surface
\sigma_{i}
has zero
n
-dimensional volume and the integral of
\rho
over
M
vanishes, implying
p
-exceptionality of
\Sigma^{\prime}_{i}
. Since
\Sigma^{\prime}_{\sigma}
is contained in the finite union of all
\Sigma^{\prime}_{i}
where
\nu_{\sigma_{i}}\neq 0
, we conclude that
\Sigma^{\prime}_{\sigma}
is
p
-exceptional.

Next, suppose that
\rho
is admissible for
\{\nu_{\sigma}:\sigma\in(\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime})\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime}}\}
and let
\sigma\in\Sigma\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma}
. Using
\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime}}\cap((\Sigma\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma})+(\Sigma^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}))=\emptyset
, it follows that for every
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
we have

\int_{M}\rho\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}}\geq 1.

If
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}\setminus(\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\cup\Sigma^{\prime}_{\sigma})
, we then have
\nu_{\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}}=\nu_{\sigma}+\nu_{\sigma^{\prime}}
and therefore one of the following two conditions is true:

\displaystyle\int_{M}2\rho\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{\sigma}\geq 1\quad\text{or}\quad\int_{M}2\rho\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{\sigma^{\prime}}\geq 1.

If the latter is the case for every
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}\setminus(\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\cup\Sigma^{\prime}_{\sigma})
, then since
\Sigma^{\prime}_{\sigma}
is
p
-exceptional, it follows that
2\rho\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}(\{\nu_{\sigma^{\prime}}:\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\})
and therefore

(17)

\int_{M}\rho^{p}\operatorname{vol}_{M}\geq 2^{-p}\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma^{\prime}).

Hence, either the integral of
2\rho
over
\nu_{\sigma}
is at least 1 or (
17
) holds. By repeating this for all
\sigma\in\Sigma\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma}
, we conclude that either
2\rho\in\operatorname{adm}(\{\nu_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\Sigma\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma}\})
or (
17
) holds.
In the former case, we have

(18)

\int_{M}\rho^{p}\operatorname{vol}_{M}\geq 2^{-p}\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\Sigma).

So either (
17
) or (
18
) holds for an arbitrary
\rho\in\operatorname{adm}(\{\nu_{\sigma}:\sigma\in(\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime})\setminus\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma+\Sigma^{\prime}}\})
, completing the proof.
∎

We now proceed to discuss modulus with differential forms. Suppose that
\mathcal{S}
is a family of Lipschitz
k
-chains on
M
. If
p\in[1,\infty)
, then we say that a
k
-form
\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
is
p
-weakly admissible
for
\mathcal{S}
if

(19)

\int_{\sigma}\omega\geq 1,

for
p
-a.e.
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}
. The set of such
\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
is denoted
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
. We define the
differential
p
-modulus
of
\mathcal{S}
by

\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(\mathcal{S})=\inf_{\omega\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})}\int_{M}\left|{\omega}\right|^{p}\operatorname{vol}_{M}.

The case
k=1
corresponds to an unweighted version of the vector modulus defined by Aikawa and Ohtsuka
[
2
]
.
Unlike in the case of the standard modulus, the use of
p
-weakly admissible
k
-forms results in a different theory than if (
19
) were required for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}
, as noted by Aikawa and Ohtsuka
[
2
, p. 64]
.
For example, consider the case when
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\}
, where
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\colon\overline{B_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(0,1)}\to M
and
\sigma(x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1},x_{k})=\sigma^{\prime}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1},-x_{k})
.
No form can possibly satisfy (
19
) for both
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
, as the integrals are equal but with opposite signs.
This would result in an infinite modulus for
\mathcal{S}
under a definition requiring (
19
) for every element of
\mathcal{S}
. However, assuming
k<n
, the shared image of
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
has zero volume and hence
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\mathcal{S})=0
.
Consequently
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})=L^{p}(\wedge^{k}M)
and hence
\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(\mathcal{S})=0
.

The moduli
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\mathcal{S})
and
\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(\mathcal{S})
may in general assume different values (see e.g.
[
6
, p. 201]
). However, for every
\omega\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
, we have that
\left|{\omega}\right|\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}(\mathcal{S})
and

(20)

\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\mathcal{S})\leq\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(\mathcal{S}).

8.1.
Existence of Minimizers

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and let
\mathcal{S}\subset C_{k}^{\text{lip}}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
. Then
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
is a closed subset of
L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
. Additionally, if
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})\neq\emptyset
, then there exists a form
\omega\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
such that

(21)

\int_{M}\left|{\omega}\right|^{p}\operatorname{vol}_{M}=\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(\mathcal{S}).

If
p>1
,
\omega
is unique.

Proof.

For the closedness of
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
, if
\omega_{i}\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
and
\omega_{i}\to\omega
in
L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
, then by Lemma
4.9
,

\int_{\sigma}\omega\geq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{\sigma}\omega_{i}\geq 1

for
p
-a.e.
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}
.

Let
(\omega_{i})
be a norm-infimizing sequence in
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
. The Banach-Alaoglu theorem yields a subsequence
(\omega_{i_{j}})
and a limit
\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}M)
such that
\omega_{i_{j}}\to\omega
weakly.
By Mazur’s lemma, there exists a sequence
(\omega_{l}^{\prime})
such that every
\omega_{l}^{\prime}
is a convex combination of elements in the sequence
(\omega_{i_{j}})
with
j\geq l
and
\omega_{l}^{\prime}\to\omega
strongly. Since
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
is closed under convex combinations, it follows that
\omega_{l}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
for every
l
.
We have already shown in this case that
\omega\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
.
Finally,
\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}=\lim_{l\to\infty}\lVert\omega_{l}^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\lVert\omega_{i_{j}}\rVert_{p}
, which implies that
\omega
satisfies (
21
).

Assume now that
p>1
, with goal of showing uniqueness of
\omega
. We may assume
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\mathcal{S})>0
, as otherwise
\omega=0
is clearly the unique element of
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
with the minimal
L^{p}
-norm. Suppose that
\omega^{\prime}\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
is another minimizer of the
L^{p}
-norm in
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
.
Since
p>1
, the
L^{p}
-norm is strictly convex. So either
\omega^{\prime}=t\omega
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
or
\lVert(\omega^{\prime}+\omega)/2\rVert_{L^{p}}<\lVert\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{p}}/2+\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}/2
.
The latter is impossible since
(\omega^{\prime}+\omega)/2\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
and
\omega,\omega^{\prime}
are
L^{p}
-norm minimizers in
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(\mathcal{S})
.
In the remaining case
\omega^{\prime}=t\omega
and
\left|{t}\right|=1
since
\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}=\lVert\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{p}}\neq 0
. The admissibility of
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
gives that
t=1
.
∎

8.2.
Properties of Admissible Forms for Homology Classes

We next prove that admissible forms for a homology family of surfaces are closed and satisfy appropriate boundary conditions.
Even though the family of admissible forms
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
for
\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(c)
, where
c
is a homology class, includes no assumptions on their differentiability, any
p
-weakly admissible form
\omega
will satisfy
d\omega=0
weakly.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
c\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{Z})
with
k<n
. Then every
\omega\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
satisfies
d\omega=0
in the weak sense and
\omega\in W^{d,p}_{T(D),\text{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
.

Proof.

Suppose that either conclusion does not hold for
\omega\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
. By Theorem
7.2
, there exists a family
\mathcal{S}\subset C_{k}^{\text{lip}}(D;{\mathbb{Z}})+\partial C_{k+1}^{\lip}(M;\mathbb{Z})
such that
\mathcal{S}
is not
p
-exceptional and

\int_{\sigma}\omega\neq 0,

for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}
.
For all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, define the family
\mathcal{S}_{k}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sigma\in C_{k}^{\text{lip}}(D;{\mathbb{Z}})+\partial C_{k+1}^{\lip}(M;\mathbb{Z}):\int_{\sigma}\omega\leq-\frac{1}{k}\right\}.

We also denote
-\mathcal{S}_{k}=\{-\sigma:\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\}
and
a\mathcal{S}_{k}=\{a\sigma:\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\}
for
a\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Since
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}
is an outer measure and
\mathcal{S}\subset\bigcup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal{S}_{k}\cup(-\mathcal{S}_{k})
, we have that
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\mathcal{S}_{k})>0
or
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(-\mathcal{S}_{k})>0
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. However,
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\mathcal{S}_{k})=\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(-\mathcal{S}_{k})
, which implies that
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\mathcal{S}_{k})>0
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
It follows that for this specific
k
,
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(a\mathcal{S}_{k})>0
for every
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Additionally,
a\mathcal{S}_{k}\subset C_{k}^{\text{lip}}(D;{\mathbb{Z}})+\partial C_{k+1}^{\lip}(M;\mathbb{Z})
.

For all
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
, define the sets
\mathcal{C}_{l}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{l}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sigma\in c:\int_{\sigma}\omega\leq l\right\}.

We have that
c=\mathcal{C}\cup\bigcup_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal{C}_{l}
, where
\mathcal{C}
is the
p
-exceptional set of elements of
c
where the integral of
\omega
is not defined. Since
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(c)>0
by Proposition
6.1
, the subadditivity of
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}
again implies that
\operatorname{Mod}_{p}(\mathcal{C}_{l})>0
for some
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Let
a
be large enough so that
l-a/k<1
and consider the set
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{C}_{l}+a\mathcal{S}_{k}
. Then
\mathcal{E}\subset c
and moreover, for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}
, we have

\int_{\sigma}\omega\leq l-\frac{a}{k}<1.

Since
\omega\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
, we must have that the set
\mathcal{E}
is
p
-exceptional. However,
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{C}_{l}+a\mathcal{S}_{k}
, where neither of the sets
\mathcal{C}_{l}
nor
a\mathcal{S}_{k}
is
p
-exceptional. This contradicts Lemma
8.1
, completing the proof of the claim. ∎

We also have a converse result concerning exact forms.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
c\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{Z})
with
k>0
. If
\omega\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
and
\tau\in W^{d,p}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}M)
, then
\omega+d\tau\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
.

Proof.

We have, due to Lemma
7.6
, that the integral of
d\tau
over
p
-a.e.
\sigma\in c
is zero. It follows that the integrals of
\omega+d\tau
and
\omega
coincide over
p
-a.e.
\sigma\in c
and hence
\omega+d\tau\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
.
∎

8.3.
Minimizers are
p
-harmonic

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, a
k
-form
\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}M)
is
p
-harmonic
if

(22)

\displaystyle d\omega=0\quad\text{and}

(23)

\displaystyle d(\left|{\omega}\right|^{p-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\omega)=0.

We next prove the following fact about cohomology minimizing forms on closed manifolds with boundary. Let
E=\partial M\setminus\intr D
, which is a closed Lipschitz
(n-1)
-submanifold of
\partial M
.

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
\omega\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
with
d\omega=0
weakly, where
0<k<n
. Then there exists a unique
\omega_{0}\in\omega+dW^{d,p}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}M)
such that
\omega_{0}
is
p
-harmonic and
\left|{\omega_{0}}\right|^{p-2}\omega_{0}\in W^{d^{*},q}_{N(E)}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
, where
q^{-1}+p^{-1}=1
. Moreover,
\omega_{0}
is the unique
L^{p}
-norm minimizing element in
\omega+dW^{d,p}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}M)
.

Proof.

Let
\omega+d\tau_{i}
be an
L^{p}
-norm minimizing sequence. By the Banach-Alaoglu theorem, there exists a subsequence that converges to
\omega_{0}\in L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
. By taking convex combinations and applying Mazur’s lemma, we may assume that the sequence converges strongly to a form
\omega_{0}\in W^{d,p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
.

We need to show that
\omega-\omega_{0}\in dW^{d,p}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}M)
,
\omega_{0}
is
p
-harmonic with
\left|{\omega_{0}}\right|^{p-2}\omega_{0}\in W^{d^{*},q}_{N(E)}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
and that no other element in
\omega+dW^{d,p}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}M)
minimizes the
L^{p}
-norm. Out of these, the uniqueness of the minimizer follows from the strict concavity of the
L^{p}
-norm.

For exactness, consider
\sigma\in C_{k}^{\text{lip}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
such that
\partial\sigma\in C_{k}^{\text{lip}}(D;{\mathbb{Z}})
. By Lemma
4.9
, there exists a subsequence so that

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma}\omega-\omega_{0}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\sigma}d\tau_{i_{j}}=0,

for
p
-a.e. such
\sigma
. By Theorem
7.2
,
\omega-\omega_{0}=d\tau_{0}
is exact and
\tau_{0}\in W^{d,p}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}M)
.
Since
\omega
is closed, we have that
\omega_{0}
is also closed.

To prove that
\omega_{0}
is
p
-harmonic we use a variational argument. Fix
\phi\in W^{d,\infty}_{T(\partial M)}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}M)
. By the minimizing property of
\omega_{0}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\int_{M}|\omega_{0}+\epsilon d\phi|^{p}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}

\displaystyle=p\int_{M}d\phi\wedge|\omega_{0}|^{p-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{0}.

Since
\phi
was an arbitrary flat form, we have that
\omega_{0}
is
p
-harmonic.
In the case where
E=\partial M\setminus D
is a closed Lipschitz
(n-1)
-submanifold of
\partial M
, let
\phi\in W^{d,\infty}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}M)
. The calculation above still holds and we see that
|\omega_{0}|^{p-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{0}
has weakly vanishing tangential part on
E
. It follows that
\left|{\omega_{0}}\right|^{p-2}\omega_{0}\in W^{d^{*},q}_{N(E)}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
, completing the proof of existence for
\omega_{0}
as in the statement.

For uniqueness, suppose that
\omega^{\prime}
is
p
-harmonic with
\left|{\omega^{\prime}}\right|^{p-2}\omega^{\prime}\in W^{d^{*},q}_{N(E)}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
and that
\omega_{0}=\omega^{\prime}+d\tau
for some
\tau\in W^{d,p}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}M)
. We compute that

\displaystyle\lVert\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\omega^{\prime}\wedge(\left|{\omega^{\prime}}\right|^{p-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(\omega_{0}-d\tau)\wedge(\left|{\omega^{\prime}}\right|^{p-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\omega_{0}\wedge(\left|{\omega^{\prime}}\right|^{p-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\omega^{\prime})+(-1)^{k}\int_{M}\tau\wedge d(\left|{\omega^{\prime}}\right|^{p-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left<\omega_{0},\left|{\omega^{\prime}}\right|^{p-2}\omega^{\prime}\right>\leq\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p-1}.

Note that the integration by parts in the third line is justified: we can use Lemma
3.6
to conclude that
\left|{\omega^{\prime}}\right|^{p-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\omega^{\prime}\wedge\tau
has weakly vanishing tangential part on
\partial M
and consequently the integral of
d(\left|{\omega^{\prime}}\right|^{p-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\omega^{\prime}\wedge\tau)
over
M
vanishes by a use of Theorem
7.2
.
It follows that
\lVert\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{p}\leq\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert_{L^{p}}
. Since
\omega_{0}
is the unique
L^{p}
-norm minimizing element in
\omega+dW^{d,p}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}M)
, we must have
\omega^{\prime}=\omega_{0}
.
∎

Our observations regarding the set
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
for a homology class
c
imply that the minimal element in
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
is
p
-harmonic.

Proposition 8.6
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
c\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{Z})
with
0<k<n
. Suppose that
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)\neq\emptyset
and let
\omega\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
be the unique element with

\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(c)=\int_{M}\left|{\omega}\right|^{p}\operatorname{vol}_{M}.

Then
\omega
is
p
-harmonic,
\omega
has weakly vanishing tangential part on
D
and
\left|{\omega}\right|^{p-2}\omega
has weakly vanishing normal part on
E
.

Proof.

Recall that
\omega
exists and is unique by Proposition
8.2
.
By Lemma
8.3
,
d\omega=0
in the weak sense and
\omega
has vanishing tangential part on
D
. For every
\tau\in W^{d,p}_{T(D)}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}M)
, we have that
\omega+d\tau\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
by Lemma
8.4
. Among such
\omega+d\tau
, there exists a unique
p
-harmonic form by Lemma
8.5
with the correct boundary values. This
p
-harmonic element has the minimal
L^{p}
-norm. Since
\omega
has the minimal
L^{p}
-norm in
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
, it follows that
\omega
must be
p
-harmonic.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Proposition
8.6
immediately implies the Theorem as long as
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)\neq\emptyset
. Since
c
is not a torsion element, this follows from Proposition
5.3
.
∎

9.
Duality

In this section we connect the modulus-minimizing forms with their Poincaré duals using the Hodge duality of
p
-harmonic and
q
-harmonic forms. In particular, we show for compact
M
that the
q
-harmonic Hodge dual of the modulus-minimizer of
c\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{Z})
is a
q
-harmonic representative of the Poincaré dual
c^{*}_{PD}\in H^{n-k}_{\lip}(M,E;\mathbb{Z})
of
c
. For a general reference of such Poincaré duals, we refer to
[
9
, Theorem 3.43]
.

For the duration of this section, let
M
be a compact Lipschitz
n
-submanifold in an oriented Lipschitz Riemannian
n
-manifold
\mathcal{R}
without boundary. The set
D\subset\partial M
will be a closed Lipschitz
(n-1)
-submanifold of
\partial M
with boundary. Additionally, we have that
E=\partial M\setminus D
is also a closed Lipschitz
(n-1)
-submanifold with boundary.

We begin with a Lemma connecting the Poincaré dual to integration.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
with
p^{-1}+q^{-1}\leq 1
, let
c\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{Z})
, let
c_{\mathbb{R}}
be the element of
H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{R})
containing
c
and let
c^{*}_{PD,\mathbb{R}}\in H^{n-k}_{\lip}(M,E;\mathbb{R})
be the Poincaré dual of
c_{\mathbb{R}}
. Suppose that
c^{*}_{PD,\mathbb{R}}
is mapped to
[\alpha]\in H_{q}^{n-k}(M,E)
in the isomorphisms of Proposition
5.3
and Corollary
5.2
and suppose that
\omega\in W^{d,p}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
with
d\omega=0
. Then for
p
-a.e.
\sigma\in c
, we have

\int_{\sigma}\omega=\int_{M}\alpha\wedge\omega

Proof.

The Poincaré duality of
c_{\mathbb{R}}
and
c^{*}_{PD,\mathbb{R}}
means that
c_{\mathbb{R}}=[M]\cap c^{*}_{PD,\mathbb{R}}=[M]\cap[\alpha]
, where
[M]
is the orientation class of
H_{n}^{\lip}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})
. In particular, since the isomorphism of Proposition
9.2
takes the wedge product to the cup product and since the isomorphisms of Corollary
5.2
respect the wedge product
W^{d,p}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)\times W^{d,q}_{T(E),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{n-k}T^{*}M)\to W^{d,1}_{T(\partial M),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{n}T^{*}M)
provided by Lemma
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle[\omega](c)=[\omega](c_{\mathbb{R}})=[\omega]([M]\cap[\alpha])=([\alpha]\cup[\omega])([M])=[\alpha\wedge\omega](M)=\int_{M}\alpha\wedge\omega,

where
[\omega]
denotes the cohomology class of
\omega
in
H_{p}^{k}(M,D)
. On the other hand, relying on Corollary
5.2
, we may select
\omega^{\prime}\in[\omega]\cap W^{d,\infty}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
, in which case
\omega=\omega^{\prime}+d\tau
for some
\tau\in W^{d,p}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k-1}T^{*}M)
. Thus, by Proposition
5.3
and Theorem
7.2
, we have

\displaystyle[\omega](c)

\displaystyle=[\omega^{\prime}](c)=\int_{\sigma}\omega^{\prime}=\int_{\sigma}\omega+d\tau=\int_{\sigma}\omega

for
p
-a.e.
\sigma\in c
.
∎

Proposition 9.2
.

Let
c\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{Z})
, let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
with
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
. Suppose that
c\notin\tor(H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{Z}))
. Then
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)\neq\emptyset
and for
q
-a.e.
\sigma^{\prime}\in C_{k}^{\lip}(M;\mathbb{Z})
with
\partial\sigma^{\prime}\in C_{k+1}^{\lip}(E;\mathbb{Z})
, we have

\int_{\sigma^{\prime}}\frac{(-1)^{k(n-k)}\left|{\omega}\right|^{p-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\omega}{\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}}=c_{\text{PD}}^{*}(\sigma^{\prime}),

where
c_{\text{PD}}^{*}\in H_{\lip}^{n-k}(M,E;\mathbb{Z})
is the Poincaré dual of
c
and
\omega
is the unique element in
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
such that
\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}=\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(c)
.

Proof.

Let
c_{\mathbb{R}}\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{R})
be the real homology class containing the integral homology class
c
and let
c_{PD,\mathbb{R}}^{*}\in H^{n-k}_{\lip}(M,E;\mathbb{R})
be the Poincaré dual of
c_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
c_{PD}(\sigma^{\prime})=c_{PD,\mathbb{R}}(\sigma^{\prime})
for every
\sigma^{\prime}\in C_{k}^{\lip}(M;\mathbb{Z})
with
\partial\sigma^{\prime}\in C_{k+1}^{\lip}(E;\mathbb{Z})
. By the de Rham theorems of Proposition
5.3
and Corollary
5.2
,
c_{PD,\mathbb{R}}
is represented by a unique
[\zeta]\in H_{q}(M,E)
. By Lemma
8.5
, there exists a unique
q
-harmonic element in
[\zeta]
, which we may assume to be
\zeta\in W^{d,p}_{T(E),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{n-k}T^{*}M)
. Since
c\notin\tor(H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;\mathbb{Z}))
, we have
c_{PD,\mathbb{R}}\neq 0
and consequently
\zeta
is not identically zero.

Our goal is to show that
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
is non-empty and that the
L^{p}
-norm minimizing element
\omega
satisfies

(24)

\zeta=\frac{(-1)^{k(n-k)}\left|{\omega}\right|^{p-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\omega}{\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}}.

We show (
24
) by showing the equivalent equality:

(25)

\omega=\frac{\left|{\zeta}\right|^{q-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\zeta}{\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}^{q}}.

Let
\omega^{\prime}=\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}^{-q}\left|{\zeta}\right|^{q-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\zeta
, with the aim of showing
\omega^{\prime}=\omega
. We note that

(26)

\lVert\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{p}}=\frac{1}{\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}^{q}}\left(\int_{M}\left|{\zeta}\right|^{(q-1)p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\frac{1}{\lVert\zeta\rVert^{q}_{L^{q}}}\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}^{\frac{q}{p}}=\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}^{\frac{q(1-p)}{p}}=\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}^{-1}.

Since
\zeta
is
q
-harmonic, a calculation shows that
d\omega^{\prime}=0
. Hence, Lemma
9.1
yields that

\int_{\sigma}\omega^{\prime}=\int_{M}\zeta\wedge\omega^{\prime}=\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}^{-q}\int_{M}\zeta\wedge\left|{\zeta}\right|^{q-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\zeta=1

for
p
-a.e.
\sigma\in c
. Therefore
\omega^{\prime}\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
and notably
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
is non-empty.

Finally, we show that
\lVert\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}
, which will imply that
\omega^{\prime}=\omega
since
\omega
is the unique minimizer in
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
.
We have again by Lemma
9.1
that

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma}\omega=\int_{M}\zeta\wedge\omega,

for
p
-a.e.
\sigma\in c
. Since
c
is not
p
-exceptional by Proposition
6.1
, we may choose a
\sigma\in c
so that this holds and

\displaystyle 1\leq\int_{\sigma}\omega\leq\|\omega\|_{L^{p}}\|\zeta\|_{L^{q}}.

By (
26
),

\displaystyle\|\omega^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\omega\|_{L^{p}},

and the proof is therefore complete.

∎

The proof of Proposition
9.2
provides a way to find the minimizer of
\operatorname{dMod}_{p}
using the Hodge star and Poincaré duality.
Let
c\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;{\mathbb{Z}})
, then
c_{PD}^{*}
is represented by a
q
-harmonic form
\zeta
. The minimizer of
\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(c)
is

\displaystyle\omega=\frac{\left|{\zeta}\right|^{q-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\zeta}{\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}^{q}}.

Since both of these operations are injective, we have shown the following proposition.

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{p}\colon H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;{\mathbb{Z}})\to L^{p}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}M)
give the unique minimizer of
\operatorname{dMod}_{p}
for a homology class. The operator
\mathcal{F}_{p}
is injective.

We can now prove Theorem
1.2
for a general compact, oriented Lipschitz manifold
M
of dimension
n
that is a subset of
\mathcal{R}
, an
n
-dimensional Lipschitz Riemannian manifold without boundary.

Theorem
1.2
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
satisfy
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
. If
c\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;{\mathbb{Z}})
is a non-torsion element, then there exists a unique element
c^{\prime}\in H_{n-k}^{\lip}(M,E;{\mathbb{R}})
such that the Poincaré dual of

maps

to

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(c)^{1/p}\operatorname{dMod}_{q}(c^{\prime})^{1/q}=1.

Proof.

Let
c_{PD}^{*}
be the Poincaré dual of

. Then
c_{PD}^{*}
is a nontorsion element of
H^{n-k}_{\lip}(M,E;\mathbb{Z})

Let
\omega

\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{p}^{k}(c)
and let
\zeta=(-1)^{k(n-k)}\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}^{-p}\left|{\omega}\right|^{p-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\omega
.
Due to Proposition
9.2
,

maps to

via the canonical map

We let

be the Poincaré dual of the cohomology class
[(-1)^{k(n-k)}\omega]\in H_{p}^{k}(M,D;\mathbb{R})
. By Lemma
9.1
, we conclude that for

-a.e. every

\int_{\sigma^{\prime}}\zeta=\int_{M}(-1)^{k(n-k)}\omega\wedge\zeta=\int_{M}\omega\wedge\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}^{-p}\left|{\omega}\right|^{p-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\omega=1.

It follows that
\zeta
is weakly

has integral

over

-a.e. element of

.

Suppose that
\zeta^{\prime}
is the unique minimizer of the

. Then

is

-harmonic and has weakly vanishing tangential part on

by Proposition

is not

. Hence, there exists

such that the following computation, which again uses Lemma

along with (

), is valid:

1=\int_{\sigma^{\prime}}\zeta^{\prime}=\int_{M}(-1)^{k(n-k)}\omega\wedge\zeta^{\prime}\leq\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\zeta^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{q}}=\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}^{-1}\lVert\zeta^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{q}}.

Thus,
\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}\leq\lVert\zeta^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{q}}
. But since
\zeta\in\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{q}^{n-k}(c^{\prime})
and since
\zeta^{\prime}
is the unique
L^{q}
-norm minimizer in
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{q}^{n-k}(c^{\prime})
, we must have
\zeta=\zeta^{\prime}
. In conclusion, (
26
) implies that

\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(c)^{\frac{1}{p}}\operatorname{dMod}_{q}(c^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{q}}=\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\zeta^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{q}}=\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}=1.

It remains to show that
c^{\prime}
is unique. For this, suppose that
a\in H_{n-k}^{\lip}(M,E;{\mathbb{R}}),c_{PD}^{*}(a)=1
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(c)^{1/p}\operatorname{dMod}_{q}(a)^{1/q}=1.

Let
\xi
denote the
L^{q}
-norm minimizing element in
\operatorname{ess\,adm}_{q}^{n-k}(a)
, in which case our assumption on the moduli yields
\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{q}}=\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}^{-1}
. If we denote
\omega^{\prime}=(-1)^{k(n-k)}\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{q}}^{-q}\left|{\xi}\right|^{q-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\xi
, then
d\omega^{\prime}=0
and
\omega^{\prime}
has vanishing tangential part on
D
by Proposition
8.6
.
Additionally, by (
26
), the norms must satisfy
\lVert\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{p}}=\lVert\xi\rVert_{L^{q}}^{-1}=\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}
. Moreover, by Proposition
9.2
,
[\omega^{\prime}]
is the Poincaré dual of
a
.

By our assumption that
c_{PD}^{*}(a)=1
and since
c_{PD}^{*}
corresponds to
[\zeta]
in the de Rham map, we may again select a suitable
\sigma^{\prime}\in a
and use Lemma
9.1
and (
26
) to obtain that

1=\int_{\sigma^{\prime}}\zeta=\int_{M}\omega^{\prime}\wedge\zeta\leq\lVert\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}=\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}^{-1}=1.

It follows that

\int_{M}\omega^{\prime}\wedge\zeta=\lVert\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}.

By the equality condition of Hölder’s inequality,

\lVert\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{p}}^{-1}\omega^{\prime}=(-1)^{k(n-k)}\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}^{-q+1}\left|{\zeta}\right|^{q-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\zeta.

By using (
26
) and
\omega=\lVert\zeta\rVert_{L^{q}}^{-q}\left|{\zeta}\right|^{q-2}\mathtt{\star}\hskip 1.0pt\zeta
, it follows that
\lVert\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{p}}^{-1}\omega^{\prime}=(-1)^{k(n-k)}\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}^{-1}\omega
and since
\lVert\omega^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{p}}=\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{p}}
, we therefore have
\omega^{\prime}=(-1)^{k(n-k)}\omega
. Since
a
is the Poincaré dual of
[\omega^{\prime}]
and
c^{\prime}
is the Poincaré dual of
[(-1)^{k(n-k)}\omega]
, we conclude that
a=c^{\prime}
.

∎

As a consequence, we prove Theorem
1.3
, a duality result for a special class of manifolds. As in Theorem
1.2
, let
M
be a compact, oriented Lipschitz Riemannian
n
-manifold that is a subset of
\mathcal{R}
, a Lipschitz Riemannian
n
-dimensional manifold.

Theorem
1.3
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
satisfy
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
. If
H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_{n-k}^{\lip}(M,E;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
and
c\in H_{k}^{\lip}(M,D;{\mathbb{Z}})
and

are the generators of the homology groups, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(c)^{1/p}\operatorname{dMod}_{q}(c^{\prime})^{1/q}=1.

Proof.

Let
a\in H_{n-k}^{\lip}(M,E;\mathbb{R})
be the unique element provided by Theorem
1.2
such that
c^{*}_{PD}(a)=1
and
\operatorname{dMod}_{p}(c)^{1/p}\operatorname{dMod}_{q}(a)^{1/q}=1
. Since

and

c^{*}_{PD}(c^{\prime})=\pm 1
. Moreover,
c^{*}_{PD}
when understood as a map

is a non-zero linear map between 1-dimensional spaces, so it is necessarily injective. It follows that the only classes in
H_{n-k}^{\lip}(M,E;\mathbb{R})
that

maps to
\pm 1
are given by
\pm c^{\prime}
after the relevant natural identifications. Hence, we must have
a=\pm c^{\prime}
. In particular,
a
has integer coefficients due to
c^{\prime}
having integer coefficients and the desired duality property holds for
c
and
c^{\prime}
due to it holding for
c
and
a
. ∎

Corollary

[

10.2.
Proof of the Sobolev de Rham theorem in Section 5

In this second part of the appendix, we sketch the main points of the proof of Proposition
5.3
. The reader is assumed to be familiar with the standard terminology of sheaf theory and sheaf cohomology.

Sketch of proof of Proposition
5.3
.

By Lemma
4.6
, the integration map over Lipschitz chains yields a linear presheaf morphism from the sheaves
\mathcal{W}_{\infty,D}^{k}\colon U\mapsto W^{d,\infty}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
to the presheaves
\mathcal{L}_{D}^{k}=\{f\in\hom(C_{k}^{\lip}(U;\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{R}):f|_{C_{k}^{\lip}(U\cap D;\mathbb{Z})}\equiv 0\}
. Moreover, by the Stokes theorem of Proposition
4.5
, this map commutes with the differentials of the corresponding complexes. The sheaves
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{D}^{k}
generated by
\mathcal{L}_{D}^{k}
are flabby, as Lipschitz cochains can be trivially zero-extended to larger domains. Moreover, like
\mathcal{W}_{\infty,D}^{k}
, the sheaves
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{D}^{k}
also form a resolution of
\mathcal{R}_{D}
, since Lipschitz cochains on balls/half-balls of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy a Poincaré lemma. Hence, relying on
[
3
, II.4.2]
, the integration map yields an isomorphism from
H^{\infty}(M,D;\mathbb{R})
to the cohomology of
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{D}^{k}(M)
. Moreover, the cohomologies of
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{D}^{k}(M)
and
\mathcal{L}_{D}^{k}(M)
are naturally isomorphic by an argument based on subdivision of simplices: an exposition of the argument in the standard case of continuous singular cochains can be found in
[
17
, Section 5.32]
, requiring essentially no changes for our case of Lipschitz singular cochains vanishing on
D
.

For the product structure, we note that
\wedge
induces a product on
W^{d,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{*}T^{*}U)
which satisfies
f\wedge g=fg
for functions
f,g\in W^{d,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{0}T^{*}U)
and
d(\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime})=d\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime}+(-1)^{k}\omega\wedge d\omega^{\prime}
for
\omega\in W^{d,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}U)
,
\omega^{\prime}\in W^{d,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{l}T^{*}U)
. Since the graded differential sheaf
\mathcal{W}_{\infty}^{*}:U\mapsto W^{d,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{*}T^{*}U)
forms a fine torsionless resolution of the constant sheaf on
M
, we have that
\mathcal{W}_{\infty}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{A}
is a fine resolution for any sheaf
\mathcal{A}
of vector spaces (see e.g.
[
3
, Corollary II.9.18]
). By the discussion in
[
3
, II.7.5]
, it follows that
\wedge
computes the cup product of sheaf cohomology. In particular, the wedge product
(\mathcal{W}_{\infty}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{D})(M)\otimes(\mathcal{W}_{\infty}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{E})(M)\to(\mathcal{W}_{\infty}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{\partial M})(M)
computes the cup product
H^{*}(M;\mathcal{R}_{D})\otimes H^{*}(M;\mathcal{R}_{E})\to H^{*}(M;\mathcal{R}_{\partial M})
in sheaf cohomology, where we use the identification
\mathcal{R}_{D}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{E}\cong\mathcal{R}_{\partial M}
.

The space
(\mathcal{W}_{\infty}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{D})(U)
is isomorphic to
\{\omega\in W^{d,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{*}T^{*}U):\spt\omega\cap D=\emptyset\}
. Hence,
\mathcal{W}_{\infty}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{D}
is naturally isomorphic to a sub-resolution of
\mathcal{W}_{\infty,D}^{*}
. The same is true for
E
and
\partial M
and the embeddings respect wedge products. Since the wedge product
W^{d,\infty}_{T(D),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{*}T^{*}M)\times W^{d,\infty}_{T(E),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{*}T^{*}M)\to W^{d,\infty}_{T(\partial M),\mathrm{loc}}(\wedge^{*}T^{*}M)
is well defined by Lemma
3.6
, we may use
[
3
, II.4.2]
to conclude that this wedge product computes the cup product
H^{*}(M;\mathcal{R}_{D})\times H^{*}(M;\mathcal{R}_{E})\to H^{*}(M;\mathcal{R}_{\partial M})
.

Similar ideas show that the cup product of singular Lipschitz cohomology computes the cup product of sheaf cohomology. Indeed, recall that the cup product of relative Lipschitz cohomology is defined by a cup product map
\mathcal{L}_{D}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{E}^{*}\to\mathcal{L}_{D+E}^{*}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{D+E}^{*}
denotes cochains vanishing on every element of
C_{*}^{\lip}(D;\mathbb{Z})+C_{*}^{\lip}(E;\mathbb{Z})
. By a similar proof as for
\mathcal{L}_{D}^{*}
, the sheaves
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{D+E}^{*}
generated by
\mathcal{L}_{D+E}^{*}
form a resolution of
\mathcal{R}_{\partial M}
and the cohomologies of
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{D+E}^{*}(M)
and
\mathcal{L}_{D+E}^{*}(M)
are naturally isomorphic. Since
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\emptyset}^{*}
form a flabby resolution of the constant sheaf of
M
, the same argument as above leads to the conclusion that the cup product
(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\emptyset}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{D})(M)\otimes(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\emptyset}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{E})(M)\to(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\emptyset}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{\partial M})(M)
computes the cup product of sheaf cohomology. We then observe similarly as before that
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\emptyset}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{D}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\emptyset}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{E}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\emptyset}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{\partial M}
can be identified with sub-resolutions of
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{D}^{*}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{E}^{*}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{D+E}^{*}
, respectively. Hence, the cup products
\mathcal{L}_{D}^{*}(M)\otimes\mathcal{L}_{E}^{*}(M)\to\mathcal{L}_{D+E}^{*}(M)
and
(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\emptyset}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{D})(M)\otimes(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\emptyset}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{E})(M)\to(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\emptyset}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{R}_{\partial M})(M)
yield the same cup product in map cohomology, completing the proof.
∎

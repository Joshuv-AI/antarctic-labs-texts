Abstract
. We prove an L p L^{p} -spectral multiplier theorem for sub-Laplacians on Heisenberg type groups under the sharp regularity condition s > d ​ | 1 / p − 1 / 2 | s>d\left|1/p-1/2\right| , where d d is the topological dimension of the underlying group. Our approach relies on restriction type estimates where the multiplier is additionally truncated along the spectrum of the Laplacian on the center of the group.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Statement of the main result

Let
G
be a two-step stratified Lie group, that is, a connected, simply connected, two-step nilpotent Lie group whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
with
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]=\mathfrak{g}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
being contained in the center of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. This basis can be identified with a system of left-invariant vector fields on
G
via the Lie derivative. Let
L
be the sub-Laplacian associated with the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
, that is, the second order, left-invariant differential operator given by

L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2}).

This operator is positive and self-adjoint on
L^{2}(G)
, where
G
is endowed with a left-invariant Haar measure. Via functional calculus, one can define for every Borel measurable function
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
the operator
F(L)
on
L^{2}(G)
, which is a bounded operator on
L^{2}(G)
whenever the spectral multiplier
F
is bounded. Regarding multipliers
F
for which
F(L)
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{p}(G)
, sufficient conditions can be given in terms of differentiability properties of the multiplier
F
, usually expressed by the scale-invariant localized Sobolev norms
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}
,
s\geq 0
, given by

\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}:=\sup_{t>0}{\lVert F(t\,\cdot\,)\eta\rVert_{L_{s}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}}.

Here,
\eta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a smooth non-zero function with compact support in
(0,\infty)
and
L_{s}^{2}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the Sobolev space of (fractional) order
s\geq 0
. Due to a celebrated theorem of Christ
[
8
]
and of Mauceri and Meda
[
25
]
,
F(L)
extends to a bounded operator on all
L^{p}
-spaces for
1<p<\infty
whenever

\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}<\infty\quad\text{for some }s>Q/2,

where
Q=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{1}+2\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is the homogeneous dimension of the underlying Lie group. Moreover,
F(L)
is of weak type
(1,1)
, i.e., bounded from
L^{1}(G)
to the Lorentz space
L^{1,\infty}(G)
. (Actually, the theorem holds true for stratified Lie groups of arbitrary step, but our focus lies on stratified Lie groups of step two.)

In the case of Heisenberg (-type) groups, Müller and Stein
[
28
]
, and independently Hebisch
[
13
]
showed that the threshold
s>Q/2
can even be pushed down to
s>d/2
, where
d
is the topological dimension of the underlying group. This result has been extended to other specific classes of two-step stratified Lie groups
[
23
,
19
,
24
]
(and also to other settings, cf.
[
9
,
18
,
5
,
1
]
), but up to now, it is still open whether the threshold
s>d/2
is sufficient for any two-step stratified Lie group. However, Martini and Müller
[
20
]
were able to show that for all two-step stratified Lie groups and left-invariant sub-Laplacians, the sharp threshold is strictly less than
Q/2
, but not less than
d/2
.

On the other hand, instead of asking for boundedness on all
L^{p}
-spaces for
1<p<\infty
simultaneously, one can ask for the minimal threshold
s_{p}\in[0,d/2]
such that
F(L)
is bounded on
L^{p}
whenever
\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}<\infty
for some
s>s_{p}
. In
[
17
]
, Martini, Müller, and Nicolussi Golo showed for a large class of smooth second-order real differential operators associated with a sub-Riemannian structure on smooth
d
-dimensional manifolds that at least regularity of order
s\geq d\left|1/p-1/2\right|
is necessary for having
L^{p}
-spectral multiplier estimates. On the opposite, one expects this threshold also to be essentially sufficient. Sufficiency results featuring the regularity condition
s>Q\left|1/p-1/2\right|
, where
Q\geq d
is the homogeneous dimension, are available in various settings
[
6
,
16
,
35
]
, but beyond the Euclidean setting (where
d=Q
), to the best of my knowledge, sufficiency of the threshold
s\geq d\left|1/p-1/2\right|
has so far only been proven in exceptional cases, see
[
7
,
32
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to extend the results for Grushin operators of
[
32
]
to sub-Laplacians on Heisenberg type groups, which is the following subclass of two-step stratified Lie groups: Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is endowed with an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for which the stratification
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is orthogonal. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
denote the dual of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. For any
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, we have a skew-symmetric bilinear form
\omega_{\mu}:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(1.1)

\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime}):=\mu([x,x^{\prime}]),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}.

Then, for any
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, there is a skew-symmetric endomorphism
J_{\mu}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
such that
\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime})=\langle J_{\mu}x,x^{\prime}\rangle
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. Let
|\cdot|
denote the norm on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
induced by the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. Then the group
G
is called a
Heisenberg type group
if
J_{\mu}
is orthogonal for
|\mu|=1
, that is,

J_{\mu}^{2}=-|\mu|^{2}\id_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}.

Note that this implies in particular that
\mathfrak{g}_{2}=[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]
is the center of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Given any orthonormal basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
with respect to the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, we consider the associated sub-Laplacian

(1.2)

L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2}).

Our main result is the following spectral multiplier estimate, together with a corresponding result for Bochner–Riesz multipliers. By the result of
[
17
]
, the threshold
s>d\left(1/p-1/2\right)
is optimal up to the endpoint and cannot be decreased. Note that the required order of regularity in the second part of
Theorem

1.1
is the same as in the Bochner–Riesz conjecture, see for example
[
36
]
. However, note also that
Theorem

1.1
only makes a statement in the range
1\leq p\leq 2(d_{2}+1)/(d_{2}+3)
, where
d_{2}
is the dimension of the center of the group.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a Heisenberg type group of topological dimension
d=\dim G
and center of dimension
d_{2}
, and let
L
be a sub-Laplacian as in
Equation

1.2
. Suppose that
1\leq p\leq 2(d_{2}+1)/(d_{2}+3)
. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

If
p>1
and if
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded Borel function such that

\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}<\infty\quad\text{for some }s>d\left(1/p-1/2\right),

then the operator
F(L)
is bounded on
L^{p}(G)
, and

\lVert F(L)\rVert_{L^{p}\to L^{p}}\leq C_{p,s}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}.

(2)

For any
\delta>d\left(1/p-1/2\right)-1/2
, the Bochner–Riesz means
(1-tL)^{\delta}_{+}
,
t\geq 0
, are uniformly bounded on
L^{p}(G)
.

If
s>d/2
in the first part of
Theorem

1.1
, then the operator
F(L)
is of weak type
(1,1)
by
[
13
]
. The condition
1\leq p\leq 2(d_{2}+1)/(d_{2}+3)
in
Theorem

1.1
derives from the Stein-Tomas restriction estimate on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, which is used for the proof of the spectral multiplier estimates. Due to this assumption, the first part of the theorem only gives results when
d_{2}\geq 2
, which means that
G
must be a Heisenberg type group which is not a Heisenberg group. This reflects the phenomenon that there are no good analogues of Fourier restriction estimates available in the case of Heisenberg groups, except for the trivial
L^{1}
-
L^{\infty}
estimates, which is due to the fact that the Heisenberg group admits only a one-dimensional center
[
29
]
. It remains an open question whether such
p
-specific spectral multiplier estimates as above also hold in the setting of Heisenberg groups.

The spectral multiplier results of
Theorem

1.1
are extended in a follow-up paper
[
33
]
to the class of Métivier groups. However, the approach of
[
33
]
requires additional methods due to the fact that the matrices
J_{\mu}
from above are no longer orthogonal and the spectral decomposition into eigenspaces, which depend on
\mu
, is more complicated.

1.2.
Structure of the proof

Building on methods of
[
4
,
7
,
32
]
, the proof of the spectral multiplier estimates of
Theorem

1.1
relies on restriction type estimates, a fundamental connection that was first discovered by C. Fefferman
[
10
]
and has since then been exploited by many other authors, see
[
34
,
12
,
6
]
. The key idea can be illustrated as follows. Suppose that we want to derive
L^{p}
-boundedness of the Bochner–Riesz means
(1-L)_{+}^{\delta}
. If
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is the multiplier given by

F(\lambda)=(1-\lambda^{2})^{\delta}_{+},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

then
F(\sqrt{L})=(1-L)_{+}^{\delta}
. We decompose
F
as

F=\sum_{\iota=0}^{\infty}F^{(\iota)},

where
|\tau|\sim 2^{\iota}
whenever
\tau\in\supp\widehat{F^{(\iota)}}
for
\iota\geq 1
. It suffices to show

\lVert F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\rVert_{p\to p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\quad\text{for some }\varepsilon>0.

Let
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}
be the convolution kernel associated with
F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})
, that is,

F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f=f*\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}\quad\text{for }f\in\mathcal{S}(G),

where
*
denotes the group convolution. By the Fourier inversion formula, we have

F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|\tau|\sim 2^{\iota}}\chi(\tau/2^{\iota})\hat{F}(\tau)\cos(\tau\sqrt{L})\,d\tau,

where
\chi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is some smooth function which is compactly supported in
(0,\infty)
. Thanks the frequency localization of the dyadic pieces
F^{(\iota)}
, since solutions of the wave equation associated with
L
possess finite propagation speed, the convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}
is supported in a Euclidean ball of dimension
B_{R}\times B_{R^{2}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
centered at the origin with radius
R\sim 2^{\iota}
. Now having a restriction type estimate which is essentially of the form

(1.3)

\lVert F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f\rVert_{2}\lesssim\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\lVert f\rVert_{p},

then, given a function
f
with
\supp f\subseteq B_{R}\times B_{R^{2}}
(which one can assume without loss of generality), Hölder’s inequality provides

(1.4)

\lVert F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f\rVert_{p}\lesssim R^{Q/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f\rVert_{2}\lesssim R^{Q/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\lVert f\rVert_{p},

where
Q=d_{1}+2d_{2}
and
1/q=1/p-1/2
. On the other hand, one can show that
F\in L^{2}_{Q/q+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})
for some
\varepsilon>0
if
\delta>Q/q-1/2
, whence the last term of
Equation

1.4
can be estimated via

R^{Q/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\lVert f\rVert_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{Q/q+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\lVert f\rVert_{p}.

However, this approach does in general only provide thresholds featuring the homogeneous dimension
Q
in place of the topological one. Employing the approach of
[
32
]
, we additionally decompose the multipliers
F^{(\iota)}
dyadically along the spectrum of the Laplacian

U=(-(U_{1}^{2}+\dots+U_{d_{2}}^{2}))^{1/2},

where
U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
is a basis of the second layer
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. More precisely, we decompose
F^{(\iota)}
such that

(1.5)

F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\chi_{\ell}(L/U),

where
(\chi_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a dyadic decomposition of
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
such that
|\lambda|\sim 2^{\ell}
if
\lambda\in\supp\chi_{\ell}
. The convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell}
of the operator
F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)=F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\chi_{\ell}(L/U)
can be explicitly written down in terms of the Fourier transform and rescaled Laguerre functions
\varphi_{k}^{|\mu|}
(for their definition, see
Equation

2.9
below), namely,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell}(x,u)=(2\pi)^{-d_{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}}

\displaystyle F^{(\iota)}\big(\sqrt{[k]|\mu|}\big)\chi_{\ell}([k])\varphi_{k}^{|\mu|}(x)e^{i\langle\mu,u\rangle}\,d\mu,

where
[k]:=2k+d_{1}/2
. Thus, due to the factor
\chi_{\ell}([k])
in the integral, the truncation achieved by the function
\chi_{\ell}
corresponds to taking in
Equation

1.6
only the summands with
[k]\sim 2^{\ell}
. Assuming in the following that
F^{(\iota)}
is compactly supported around
1
(which is of course in general not true in view of the Paley–Wiener theorem but can be achieved by a cut-off), we have
|\mu|\sim[k]^{-1}
and thus
|\mu|\sim 2^{-\ell}
on the support of
F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}
, whence the truncation afforded by the functions
\chi_{\ell}
is also referred to as a
truncation along the spectrum of
U
. Pointwise estimates for Laguerre functions (cf. Eq. (1.1.44)

e^{-x}

\varphi_{k}(x):=\varphi_{k}^{1}(x)

|x|\gtrsim[k]^{1/2}

|\mu|\sim[k]^{-1}

\varphi_{k}^{|\mu|}

is supported where
|x|\lesssim|\mu|^{-1/2}[k]^{1/2}\sim 2^{\ell}=:R_{\ell}

\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell}

supported in a Euclidean ball of dimension
B_{R_{\ell}}\times B_{R^{2}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
centered at the origin. Now, instead of
Equation

1.3
, suppose we had a restriction type estimate of the form

(1.7)

\lVert F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f\rVert_{2}\lesssim R_{\ell}^{-d_{2}/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\lVert f\rVert_{p}.

We distinguish the cases
0\leq\ell\leq\iota
and
\ell>\iota
. In the former case, we decompose the ball
B_{R}\times B_{R^{2}}
into a grid of balls
B_{m}^{(\ell)}
of dimension
R_{\ell}\times R^{2}
with respect to the Euclidean distance on the layers
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, respectively. Correspondingly, we decompose the function
f
supported in
B_{R}\times B_{R^{2}}
into a sum of functions
f_{m}
supported in balls of dimension
R_{\ell}\times R^{2}
. Then Hölder’s inequality, applied with
1/p=1/q+1/2
, combined with the restriction type estimate
Equation

1.7
would provide

\displaystyle\lVert F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f_{m}\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim(R_{\ell}^{d_{1}}R^{2d_{2}})^{1/q}\lVert F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f_{m}\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(R_{\ell}^{d_{1}-d_{2}}R^{2d_{2}})^{1/q}\lVert F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\lVert f_{m}\rVert_{p}.

Since
G
is a Heisenberg type group, we have
d_{2}<d_{1}
. Together with
\ell\leq\iota
, we obtain

R_{\ell}^{d_{1}-d_{2}}R^{2d_{2}}\sim 2^{(\ell-\iota)(d_{1}-d_{2})}2^{\iota(d_{1}+d_{2})}\leq 2^{\iota(d_{1}+d_{2})}.

Hence we may estimate the last term of
Equation

1.8
by

R^{d/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\lVert f\rVert_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{d/q+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\lVert f\rVert_{p}.

The case
\ell>\iota
can be treated similarly by using
Equation

1.4
in conjunction with
Equation

1.7
and summing the geometric series over all
\ell>\iota
. As a consequence, only regularity
\delta>d/q-1/2
of the multiplier
F(\lambda)=(1-\lambda^{2})_{+}^{\delta}
is necessary for the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the Bochner–Riesz mean
F(\sqrt{L})=(1-L)_{+}^{\delta}
. Thus, at least by our heuristics, the truncation of
Equation

1.5
ultimately provides thresholds with the topological dimension instead of the homogeneous one.

It should be emphasized that the present approach benefits from the fact that the dimension
d_{2}
of the second layer
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is smaller than the dimension of the first layer. Doing the decomposition in the first layer reflects a phenomenon that has already been prominent in the setting of Grushin operators
-\Delta_{x}-|x|^{2}\Delta_{u}
,
(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
, which are closely related to sub-Laplacians on Heisenberg type groups. In
[
21
]
, Martini and Sikora proved a Mikhlin–Hörmander type result with threshold
s>D/2
for Grushin operators, where
D:=\max\{d_{1}+d_{2},2d_{2}\}
, which was later improved by Martini and Müller
[
18
]
to hold for the topological dimension
d
in place of
D
. Both approaches rely on weighted Plancherel estimates for the associated integral kernels, but differ by the employed weights. While
[
21
]
uses the weight
|x|^{\gamma}
in the first layer in conjunction with a sub-elliptic estimate,
[
18
]
employs the weight
|u|^{\gamma}
in the second layer. A similar phenomenon occurred later in the articles
[
7
]
and
[
32
]
, where spectral multiplier theorems with
p
-specific regularity bounds were proved. The approach of Chen and Ouhabaz
[
7
]
relies on weighted restriction type estimates using the weight
|x|^{\gamma}
in the first layer, while
[
32
]
employs the weighted Plancherel estimate of
[
18
]
with weight in the center to express support conditions of integral kernels. In accordance with the phenomenon of
[
21
]
and
[
18
]
, the result of
[
7
]
needs
s>D\left(1/p-1/2\right)
as regularity condition, while
[
32
]
only needs regularity of order
s>d\left(1/p-1/2\right)
. The present article relies on the same key idea as in
[
32
]
, but uses instead a weighted Plancherel estimate featuring the weight
|x|^{\gamma}
. However, this approach still provides the optimal threshold
s>d\left(1/p-1/2\right)
in
Theorem

1.1
and is in line with the phenomena described above, since
d_{2}<d_{1}
and thus
D=d
, due to the fact that
G
is a Heisenberg type group.

1.3.
Structure of the paper

Section

2
and
5
are preliminary sections dealing with the spectral theory and sub-Riemannian geometry of sub-Laplacians. In
Section

3
, we prove the previously mentioned truncated restriction type estimates.
Section

4
is devoted to proving a weighted Plancherel estimate with weight
|x|^{\gamma}
. In
Section

6
we reduce the proof of
Theorem

1.1
to spectral estimates for multipliers whose Fourier transform is supported on dyadic scales. The proof of this reduced version of
Theorem

1.1
is given in
Section

7
.
Section

8
is an additional section, where we show that a sub-elliptic estimate for the sub-Laplacian with the help of which one could directly transfer the approach of
[
7
]
to the setting of Heisenberg type groups is in general false.

Notation

We let
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
. The space of (equivalence classes of) integrable simple functions on a two-step stratified Lie group
G
will be denoted by
D(G)
, while
\mathcal{S}(G)
shall denote the space of Schwartz functions on
G\cong\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The indicator function of a subset
A

\textbf{1}_{A}
. Given two suitable functions

on

denote the group convolution given by

f*g(x,u)=\int_{G}f(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\,g\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\,d(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}),\quad(x,u)\in G,

where
d(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
G
. For a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the Fourier transform
\hat{f}
is defined by

\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)e^{-i\xi x}\,dx,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

while the inverse Fourier transform
\check{f}
is given by

\check{f}(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\xi)e^{ix\xi}\,d\xi,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We write
A\lesssim B
if
A\leq CB
for a constant
C
. If
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
, we write
A\sim B
. Moreover, we fix the following dyadic decomposition throughout this article: Let
\chi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be an even and smooth function such that
1/2\leq|\lambda|\leq 2
for all
\lambda\in\supp\chi
and

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{j}(\lambda)=1\quad\text{for }\lambda\neq 0,

where
\chi_{j}
is given by

(1.9)

\chi_{j}(\lambda)=\chi(\lambda/2^{j})\quad\text{for }j\in\mathbb{Z}.

2.
Spectral theory of sub-Laplacians on Heisenberg type groups

Let
G
be a two-step stratified Lie group. Via exponential coordinates, we may identify
G
with its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, which is the tangent space at the identity of
G
. Since
G
is stratified of step 2,
\mathfrak{g}
can be decomposed as
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
with
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]=\mathfrak{g}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
being contained in the center of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{*}=\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. For any
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
, let
\omega_{\mu}
be the skew-symmetric bilinear form given by

\omega_{\mu}(x,x^{\prime})=\mu([x,x^{\prime}]),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}.

Then
G
is called a
Heisenberg type group
if there is an inner product

, Minkowski’s integral inequality yields

(3.5)

\int_{\dot{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{*}}\lVert g_{k}^{\mu}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})}^{2}\,d\mu\leq\bigg(\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\bigg(\int_{\dot{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{*}}|g_{k}^{\mu}(x)|^{2}\,d\mu\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{2}{p}}.

Let
f_{x}:=f(x,\cdot)
and
\widehat{\cdot}
denote the Fourier transform on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Using polar coordinates and applying the Stein–Tomas restriction estimate
[
38
]
yields

\displaystyle\int_{\dot{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{*}}|g_{k}^{\mu}(x)|^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{d_{2}-1}}|F(\sqrt{[k]r})\widehat{f_{x}}(r\omega)|^{2}r^{d_{2}-1}\,d\sigma(\omega)\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}|F(\sqrt{[k]r})|^{2}r^{-d_{2}-1}\int_{S^{d_{2}-1}}\big|\big(f_{x}(r^{-1}\,\cdot\,)\big)^{\wedge}(\omega)\big|^{2}\,d\sigma(\omega)\,dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}|F(\sqrt{[k]r})|^{2}r^{-d_{2}-1}\lVert f_{x}(r^{-1}\,\cdot\,)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{2})}^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}|F(\sqrt{[k]r})|^{2}r^{2d_{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})-1}\,dr\,\lVert f_{x}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim[k]^{-2d_{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\lVert f_{x}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{2})}^{2}.

In combination with
Equation

3.3
,
Equation

3.4
and
Equation

3.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert F_{\ell}(L,U)f\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{[k]\sim 2^{\ell}}[k]^{-2d_{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})-1}\lVert F\rVert_{2}^{2}\lVert f\rVert_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim 2^{-2\ell d_{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\lVert F\rVert_{2}^{2}\lVert f\rVert_{p}^{2}.

This proves
Equation

3.2
.
∎

4.
A weighted Plancherel estimate

In this section, we prove a weighted Plancherel estimate for convolution kernels associated with the sub-Laplacian
L
on the Heisenberg type group
G
. Usually, those estimates are the crux of the matter when proving Mikhlin–Hörmander results featuring the threshold
s>d/2
, where
d
is the topological dimension of the underlying space, see for example
[
23
, Thm. 4.6]
or
[
24
, Prop. 3]
. However, in the present setting, the weighted Plancherel estimate
Equation

4.1
will serve a different purpose, namely turning support conditions in conjunction with convolution kernels into some sort of rapid decay.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
F
and
F_{\ell}
be defined as in
Theorem

3.2
, and
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}
be the convolution kernel of the operator
F_{\ell}(L,U)
. Then, for all
\alpha\geq 0
,

(4.1)

\int_{G}\big||x|^{\alpha}\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(x,u)\big|^{2}\,d(x,u)\leq C_{\alpha}2^{\ell(2\alpha-d_{2})}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\quad\text{for all }\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
. Using
Equation

2.14
in combination with Plancherel’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{G}\big||x|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(x,u)\big|^{2}\,d(x,u)

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{\dot{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{*}}\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\Big||x|^{\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{\ell}\big([k]|\mu|,|\mu|\big)\varphi_{k}^{|\mu|}(x)\Big|^{2}\,dx\,d\mu.

Given
\mu\in\dot{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{*}
, we consider the rescaled Hermite operator
H^{\mu}=-\Delta_{z}+\tfrac{1}{4}|z|^{2}|\mu|^{2}
acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
. By Proposition 3.3 of
[
7
]
,

(4.3)

\left\lVert|\cdot|^{\alpha}g\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}\lesssim|\mu|^{-\alpha}\lVert(H^{\mu})^{\alpha/2}g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}.

On the other hand, by Equation (1.3.25) of
[
37
]
, the functions
\Phi^{|\mu|}_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}
defined by
Equation

2.6
are also eigenfunctions of
H^{\mu}
, with

H^{\mu}\Phi^{|\mu|}_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}=(|\nu|+|\nu^{\prime}|+n)|\mu|\Phi^{|\mu|}_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\quad\text{for all }\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}.

By the definition
Equation

2.11
of
\varphi_{k}^{|\mu|}
, this implies in particular

H^{\mu}\varphi_{k}^{|\mu|}=[k]|\mu|\varphi_{k}^{|\mu|}.

(Alternatively, one could use
Equation

2.7
by exploiting that
\varphi_{k}^{|\mu|}
is radial-symmetric by
Equation

2.9
and that the operators
H^{\mu}
and
L_{0}^{|\mu|}
coincide on such functions.) Hence, together with
Equation

4.3
, the right-hand side of
Equation

4.2
can be dominated by a constant times

(4.4)

\int_{\dot{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\Big|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}[k]^{\alpha/2}|\mu|^{-\alpha/2}F_{\ell}\big([k]|\mu|,|\mu|\big)\varphi_{k}^{|\mu|}(x)\Big|^{2}\,dx\,d\mu.

Using
[k]|\mu|\sim 1
for
\sqrt{[k]|\mu|}\in\supp F
,
|F_{\ell}(\lambda,\rho)|\leq|F(\lambda)|
and orthogonality of the functions
\varphi_{k}^{|\mu|}
,
Equation

4.4
can be estimated by a constant times

(4.5)

\sum_{[k]\sim 2^{\ell}}[k]^{2\alpha}\int_{\dot{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\big|F\big(\sqrt{[k]|\mu|}\big)\varphi_{k}^{\mu}(x)\big|^{2}\,dx\,d\mu.

Since the functions
\Phi_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}^{|\mu|}
form an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
,

|\mu|^{-n}\lVert\varphi_{k}^{|\mu|}\rVert_{2}^{2}=|\{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:|\nu|_{1}=k\}|=\binom{k+n-1}{k}\sim(k+1)^{n-1}.

Hence
\lVert\varphi_{k}^{|\mu|}\rVert_{2}^{2}\sim[k]^{-1}
for
[k]|\mu|\in\supp F
. Thus
Equation

4.5
is comparable to

(4.6)

\sum_{[k]\sim 2^{\ell}}[k]^{2\alpha-1}\int_{\dot{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{*}}\big|F\big(\sqrt{[k]|\mu|}\big)\big|^{2}\,d\mu.

Using polar coordinates and a substitution in the integral over
\dot{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{*}
shows that
Equation

4.6
in turn is comparable to

\sum_{[k]\sim 2^{\ell}}[k]^{2\alpha-1-d_{2}}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\sim 2^{\ell(2\alpha-d_{2})}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

This proves
Equation

4.1
.
∎

5.
The sub-Riemannian geometry of the sub-Laplacian

In this section we summarize the main properties of the sub-Riemannian geometry associated with left-invariant sub-Laplacians on two-step stratified groups. Let
G
be a two-step stratified Lie group and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be a stratification of its Lie algebra. Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2})
be the associated sub-Laplacian. We again identify
G\cong\mathfrak{g}
via the exponential map and
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d}
by means of the basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and a basis
U_{1},\dots,U_{d_{2}}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
.

Let
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
denote the Carnot–Carathéodory distance associated with the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
. By definition, this means that for
g,h\in G
, the distance
d_{\mathrm{CC}}(g,h)
is given by the infimum over all lengths of horizontal curves
\gamma:[0,1]\to G
joining
g
with
h
, see for instance
[
39
, Section III.4]
. Since the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
are left-invariant and
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]=\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, they satisfy Hörmander’s condition
[
14
]
, that is, the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
along with their iterated commutators

[X_{i},X_{j}],[X_{i},[X_{j},X_{l}]],\dots

span the tangent space
\mathfrak{g}=T_{e}G
of
G
at the identity
e\in G
, and hence at every point
g\in G
. (In our two-step setting, the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
together with their commutators
[X_{i},X_{j}]
already span the tangent space.) Hence, due to the Chow–Rashevskii theorem
[
39
, Proposition III.4.1]
,
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
is indeed a metric on
M
, which induces the (Euclidean) topology of
G=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Since
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
are left-invariant vector fields,
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
is left-invariant, that is,

(5.1)

d_{\mathrm{CC}}(ag,ah)=d_{\mathrm{CC}}(g,h)\quad\text{for all }a,g,h\in G.

On the other hand,

\lVert(x,u)\rVert:=(|x|^{4}+|u|^{2})^{1/4},\quad(x,u)\in G

defines a homogeneous norm in the sense of Folland and Stein
[
11
]
with respect to the dilations
\delta_{R}
given by

(5.2)

\delta_{R}(x,u)=(Rx,R^{2}u),\quad R\geq 0.

Hence
G\times G\ni(g,h)\mapsto\lVert g^{-1}h\rVert
is a left-invariant (quasi-)distance on
G
. Since any two homogeneous norms on a homogeneous Lie group are equivalent
[
11
, Lemma 1.4]
, we have

(5.3)

d_{\mathrm{CC}}(g,h)\sim\lVert g^{-1}h\rVert\quad\text{for all }g,h\in G.

Let
B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(g)
denote the ball of radius
R\geq 0
centered at
g\in G
with respect to
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
. Then
Equation

5.1
and
Equation

5.2
yield

(5.4)

|B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(g)|=R^{Q}|B_{1}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)|,

where we identify
0
with the identity element
e\in G
via
G\cong\mathfrak{g}
, and
Q=d_{1}+2d_{2}
is the homogeneous dimension. Note that
Equation

5.4
yields in particular that the metric space
(G,d_{\mathrm{CC}})
equipped with the the Lebesgue measure (which is a bi-invariant Haar measure on
G
) is a space of homogeneous type with homogeneous dimension
Q
.

Furthermore, the sub-Laplacian
L
possesses the finite propagation speed property with respect to the Carnot-Carathéodory distance
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
, which will be of fundamental importance in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
f,g\in L^{2}(G)
are supported in open subsets
U,V\subseteq G
, then

(\cos(t\sqrt{L})f,g)=0\quad\text{for all }|t|<d_{\mathrm{CC}}(U,V).

For a proof, see
[
26
]
or
[
30
, Corollary 6.3]
.

6.
Reduction of
Theorem

1.1
to dyadic spectral multipliers

To prove
Theorem

1.1
, we use the following general spectral multiplier result of
[
6
]
, which allows us to reduce the spectral multiplier estimates of
Theorem

1.1
to estimates for spectral multipliers whose Fourier transforms are supported on dyadic scales. Very similar arguments are used in
[
7
, Section 5]
and
[
32
, Section 4]
, but we give a detailed discussion for the convenience of the reader. Given a suitable multiplier
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, we use the notation

(6.1)

F^{(\iota)}:=(\hat{F}\chi_{\iota})^{\vee}\quad\text{for }\iota\in\mathbb{Z},

where
\widehat{\cdot}
and
\cdot^{\vee}
denote the Fourier transform and its inverse on
\mathbb{R}
, respectively, and
(\chi_{\iota})_{\iota\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the dyadic decomposition from
Equation

1.9
.

Proposition 6.1
.

[
6
, Proposition I.22]

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a metric measure space of homogeneous type and
Q\geq 0
such that

\mu(B_{\lambda r}(x))\leq C\lambda^{Q}\mu(B_{r}(x))\quad\text{for all }x\in X,r>0,\lambda\geq 1,

where
B_{s}(x)
denotes the ball of radius
s>0
centered at
x\in X
.
Let
1\leq p_{0}<p<2
. Suppose that
L
is a positive self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(X)
such that the following statements are satisfied:

(i)

L
possesses the finite propagation speed property, that is,

(\cos(t\sqrt{L})f,g)_{L^{2}(X)}=0\quad\text{for all }|t|<\rho(U,V)

whenever
f,g\in L^{2}(X)
are supported in open subsets
U,V\subseteq X
, where

\rho(U,V):=\inf\{\rho(u,v):u\in U,v\in V\}.

(ii)

L
satisfies the Stein–Tomas restriction type condition
(\mathrm{ST}^{\infty}_{p_{0},2})
of
[
6
]
, that is, for any
R>0
and all bounded Borel functions
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
supported in
[0,R]
,

(6.2)

\|F(\sqrt{L})(\mathbf{1}_{B_{r}(x)}f)\|_{2}\leq C\mu(B_{r}(x))^{{\frac{1}{2}}-{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}}(Rr)^{Q({\frac{1}{p_{0}}}-{\frac{1}{2}})}\|F\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{p_{0}}

for all
x\in X
, all
r\geq 1/R
, and all
f\in L^{p_{0}}(X)
.

(iii)

There is some
\beta>Q/2
such that

\sup_{t>0}\|F(t\sqrt{L})\|_{p\to p}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{\infty}_{\beta}}

for all even bounded Borel functions
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
with
\supp F\subseteq[-1,1]
.

Suppose that
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is an even bounded Borel function and that there is a bounded sequence
(\alpha(\iota))_{\iota\in\mathbb{Z}}
with
\sum_{\iota\geq 0}(\iota+1)\alpha(\iota)<\infty
such that

(6.3)

\lVert(F\chi_{i})^{(j)}(\sqrt{L})\rVert_{p\to p}\leq\alpha(i+j)\quad\text{for all }i,j\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then the operator
F(\sqrt{L})
is of weak-type
(p,p)
.

Remark
.

To be precise, in
[
6
]
, Proposition I.22 requires the condition
(\mathrm{E}_{p_{0},2})
in place of the Stein–Tomas type restriction condition
(\mathrm{ST}^{\infty}_{p_{0},2})
. However, both conditions are equivalent by Proposition I.3 of the same paper. Moreover, the notation of our decomposition indexed by
i
and
j
differs slightly from that of
[
6
]
since
(F\eta_{i})^{(j)}=(F\chi_{-i})^{(j)}
, where
(\eta_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the dyadic decomposition from
[
6
, Eq. (I.3.3)]
. The somewhat artificial requirement that
F
shall be an even function is linked to the finite propagation speed property. This will become apparent at the beginning of the proof of
Proposition

7.1
.

We apply
Proposition

6.1
in the setting where
X=G
is a two-step stratified Lie group and

(6.4)

L=-(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2})

is the sub-Laplacian associated with a basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
of the first layer of the stratification
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
of
G
. The measure
\mu
in
Proposition

6.1
will be the Lebesgue measure on
G
.

For our purposes of proving
Theorem

1.1
, we only need
Corollary

6.2
for the case where
L
is a sub-Laplacian on a Heisenberg type group, but the more general version for arbitrary two-step stratified Lie groups is readily available. Note, however, that
Proposition

6.1
requires the restriction type condition
(\mathrm{ST}^{\infty}_{p_{0},2})
in the setting of arbitrary two-step stratified Lie groups. However, instead of using the restriction type estimates of
Theorem

3.2
, we use a Plancherel estimate for the associated convolution kernel, which in turn implies a restriction type estimate from
L^{1}
to
L^{2}
.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
G
be a two-step stratified Lie group and
L
be a sub-Laplacian as in
Equation

6.4
. Let
p_{*}\in[1,2]
and
s>1/2
. Suppose that for all
1\leq p\leq p_{*}
there exists some
\varepsilon>0
such that

(6.5)

\|F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\|_{p\to p}\leq C_{p,s}2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\quad\text{for all }\iota\in\mathbb{N}

and all even bounded Borel functions
F\in L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
supported in
[-2,-1/2]\cup[1/2,2]
. Then the statements (
1
) and (
2
) of
Theorem

1.1
hold for all
1\leq p\leq p_{*}
.

Remark
.

The assumption
1\leq p\leq 2(d_{2}+1)/(d_{2}+3)
of
Theorem

1.1
automatically implies that
s>1/2
if
s>d\left(1/p-1/2\right)
since

d\bigg(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)\geq d_{2}\bigg(\frac{(d_{2}+3)}{2(d_{2}+1)}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)=\frac{d_{2}}{d_{2}+1}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

However, in
Corollary

6.2
, we only require
1\leq p\leq p_{*}
for some
p_{*}\in[1,2]
, which is why we additionally assume
s>1/2
to make sure that
\|F|_{(0,\infty)}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}
.

Proof.

The result for Bochner–Riesz multipliers in the second part of
Theorem

1.1
is a direct consequence of (
6.5
) without
Proposition

6.1
involved, which can be seen in the same way as in the proof of Theorem 1.2 in
[
32
, Section 4]
.

For the first part of
Theorem

1.1
, we observe that

\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}\sim\|\tilde{F}\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}\quad\text{for }F(\lambda)=\tilde{F}(\sqrt{\lambda}).

Thus, we may pass from
F(L)
to the operator
F(\sqrt{L})
. We use
Proposition

6.1
for
p_{0}=1
.
Proposition

6.1
only shows that the operator
F(\sqrt{L})
is of weak type
(p,p)
, but the boundedness on
L^{p}
can be easily recovered as follows: First, note that the case
p=1
is excluded in the first part of
Theorem

1.1
. Thus, suppose that the interval
(1,p_{*}]
is non-empty and let
1<p\leq p_{*}
. Suppose that
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded Borel function satisfying

\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}<\infty\quad\text{for some }s>d\left(1/p-1/2\right).

We can choose
1<\tilde{p}<p
such that
s>d\left(1/\tilde{p}-1/2\right)
. Applying
Proposition

6.1
implies that
F(\sqrt{L})
is of weak type
(\tilde{p},\tilde{p})
. On the other hand, the assumption
s>1/2
in
Corollary

6.2
ensures that

\|F|_{(0,\infty)}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}.

Hence, via interpolation with the
L^{2}
-
L^{2}
bound provided by the spectral theorem, we may conclude that
F(\sqrt{L})
is bounded on
L^{p}
. The claimed estimate

\|F(\sqrt{L})\|_{p\to p}\leq C_{p,s}\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}

in the first part of
Theorem

1.1
follows by the closed graph theorem applied to the map
F\mapsto F(\sqrt{L})
. Alternatively, this estimate can also be derived by inspecting the arguments of
[
6
]
(see also
[
31
, Section 7]
).

Now we verify the assumptions of
Proposition

6.1
. Let
p_{0}=1
. The finite propagation speed property in (i) holds due to
Lemma

5.1
, and the estimate in (iii) is automatically fulfilled by Theorem I.5 of
[
6
]
. Since
p_{0}=1
, the restriction type condition
(\mathrm{ST}^{\infty}_{p_{0},2})
is a consequence of a Plancherel estimate for the associated convolution kernel, see also
[
6
, Section III.5]
. More precisely, given a bounded Borel measurable function
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, since
L
is a left-invariant operator, there is a convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}_{F(\sqrt{L})}
such that
F(\sqrt{L})f=f*\mathcal{K}_{F(\sqrt{L})}
for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. By
[
8
, Proposition 2]
, there is some constant
C>0
such that we have the Plancherel estimate

\|\mathcal{K}_{F(\sqrt{L})}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}=C\int_{0}^{\infty}|F(\sqrt{\lambda})|^{2}\lambda^{Q/2}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda},

where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of
G
. If
R>0
and
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded Borel function supported in
[0,R]
, then

\int_{0}^{\infty}|F(\sqrt{\lambda})|^{2}\lambda^{Q/2}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\lesssim R^{Q}\|F\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Thus, we obtain

\|F(\sqrt{L})f\|_{2}=\|f*\mathcal{K}_{F(\sqrt{L})}\|_{2}\leq\|f\|_{1}\|\mathcal{K}_{F(\sqrt{L})}\|_{2}\lesssim R^{Q/2}\|F\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{1}.

By
Equation

5.4
, we have
|B_{r}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(x,u)|=r^{Q}|B_{1}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)|
. Hence, for
p_{0}=1
, the factor on the right-hand side of

is given by

|B_{r}(x,u)|^{-1/2}(Rr)^{Q/2}\sim R^{Q/2},

which verifies the restriction type condition
(\mathrm{ST}^{\infty}_{p_{0},2})
. Thus the assumptions of
Proposition

6.1
are satisfied.

Now suppose that the dyadic estimate
Equation

6.5
of
Corollary

6.2
holds. Let
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be a bounded Borel function such that

\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}<\infty\quad\text{for some }s>d\left(1/p-1/2\right).

To show that
F(\sqrt{L})
is of weak type
(p,p)
, we verify the required estimate

6.3
of
Proposition

6.1
. Note that we may assume without loss of generality that
F
is an even function since
L
is a positive operator. For
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we let
F_{i}:=F\chi_{i}
, where
(\chi_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the dyadic decomposition from
Equation

1.9
. Given

, let
\iota:=i+j
and

G(\lambda):=F(2^{i}\lambda)\chi(\lambda),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

where

is the bump function from

1.9
. Then

is an even function, and

\displaystyle(F_{i})^{(j)}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(\widehat{F_{i}}\chi_{j})^{\vee}(\lambda)=(2^{i}\hat{G}(2^{i}\cdot)\chi_{j})^{\vee}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(\hat{G}\chi_{\iota})^{\vee}(2^{-i}\lambda)=G^{(\iota)}(2^{-i}\lambda).

Let again
\delta_{R}
be the dilation given by
\delta_{R}(x,u)=(Rx,R^{2}u)

(F(\sqrt{L})f)\circ\delta_{R^{-1}}=F(R\sqrt{L})(f\circ\delta_{R^{-1}}).

This implies

\lVert G^{(\iota)}(2^{-i}\sqrt{L})\rVert_{p\to p}=\lVert G^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\rVert_{p\to p}.

Hence, for
\iota\geq 0
,

6.5
yields

\displaystyle\lVert(F\chi_{i})^{(j)}(\sqrt{L})\rVert_{p\to p}

\displaystyle=\lVert G^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\rVert_{p\to p}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\|G^{(\iota)}\|_{L_{s}^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}.

The case
\iota<0
can be treated by the Mikhlin–Hörmander type result of
[
8
]
and
[
25
]
. Suppose
\iota<0
. Let
\psi:=\sum_{i\leq 2}\chi_{i}
. Then
\psi
is supported in
[-8,8]
. We decompose
G^{(\iota)}
as
G^{(\iota)}=G^{(\iota)}\psi+G^{(\iota)}(1-\psi)
. Since
G^{(\iota)}=G*\check{\chi}_{\iota}
,
\supp G\subseteq[-2,2]
and
\check{\chi}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\Big(\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Big)^{\alpha}G^{(\iota)}(\lambda)\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\Big(\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Big)^{\alpha}\int_{-2}^{2}2^{\iota}G(\tau)\check{\chi}(2^{\iota}(\lambda-\tau))\,d\tau\Big|

(6.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N}2^{\iota(\alpha+1)}\int_{-2}^{2}\frac{|G(\tau)|}{(1+2^{\iota}|\lambda-\tau|)^{N}}\,d\tau,\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
Q
denote again the homogeneous dimension of
G
. Choosing
N:=0
in
Equation

6.6
and using
2^{\iota(\alpha+1)}\leq 1
, we obtain

\|G^{(\iota)}\psi\|_{L^{2}_{Q/2+1,\mathrm{sloc}}}\lesssim_{\psi}\lVert G\rVert_{2}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}.

On the other hand, choosing
N:=\alpha+1
in
Equation

6.6
yields in particular

\Big|\Big(\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Big)^{\alpha}G^{(\iota)}(\lambda)\Big|\lesssim|\lambda|^{-\alpha}\lVert G\rVert_{2}\quad\text{for }|\lambda|\geq 4.

Since all derivatives of
1-\psi
are Schwartz functions, Leibniz rule yields

\lVert G^{(\iota)}(1-\psi)\rVert_{L^{2}_{Q/2+1,\mathrm{sloc}}}\lesssim_{\psi}\lVert G\rVert_{2}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}.

Hence, applying the Mikhlin–Hörmander type result of
[
8
]
and
[
25
]
yields

\displaystyle\lVert(F_{i})^{(j)}(\sqrt{L})\rVert_{p\to p}

\displaystyle=\lVert G^{(\iota)}(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\rVert_{p\to p}

\displaystyle=\lVert G^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\rVert_{p\to p}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{2}_{s,\mathrm{sloc}}}.

This establishes the required condition
Equation

6.3
of
Proposition

6.1
. Thus we can apply
Proposition

6.1
and we get that the operator
F(\sqrt{L})
is of weak type
(p,p)
.
∎

7.
Proof of the reduction of
Theorem

1.1

Now suppose that
L=(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{d_{1}}^{2})
is a sub-Laplacian on a Heisenberg type group
G
, where
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
is an orthonormal basis of the first layer
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
of the stratification
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Let again
d_{\mathrm{CC}}
denote the Carnot–Carathéodory distance associated with the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{d_{1}}
, let
d=d_{1}+d_{2}
be the topological dimension, and
Q=d_{1}+2d_{2}
be the homogeneous dimension of
G
. By
Corollary

6.2
, the proof of
Theorem

1.1
can be reduced to proving the statement. Given a suitable multiplier

, we write again

F^{(\iota)}:=(\hat{F}\chi_{\iota})^{\vee}\quad\text{for }\iota\in\mathbb{Z},

where
(\chi_{\iota})_{\iota\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the dyadic decomposition of
Equation

1.9
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Suppose that
1\leq p\leq 2(d_{2}+1)/(d_{2}+3)
. If
s>d\left(1/p-1/2\right)
, then there exists some
\varepsilon>0
such that

\|F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})\|_{p\to p}\leq C_{p,s}2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\quad\text{for all }\iota\in\mathbb{N}

and any even bounded Borel function

supported in
[-2,-1/2]\cup[1/2,2]
.

Proof.

Let
\iota\in\mathbb{N}
and
R:=2^{\iota}
. We proceed in several steps.

(1)
Reduction to compactly supported functions.
Let
f\in D(G)
be an integrable simple function on
G
. We first show that we may restrict to the case where
f
is supported in
B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)
. Since the metric space
(G,{d_{\mathrm{CC}}})
endowed with the Lebesgue measure is a space of homogeneous type and separable, we may choose a decomposition into disjoint sets
B_{j}\subseteq B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(x^{(j)},u^{(j)})
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
(x^{(j)},u^{(j)})\in G
such that for every
\lambda\geq 1
, the number of overlapping dilated balls

is bounded by a constant
C(\lambda)\sim\lambda^{Q}
, which is independent of
\iota
. We decompose

as

Since
F
is even, so is
\hat{F}
. As
\chi_{\iota}
is even as well, the Fourier inversion formula provides

F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f_{j}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{2^{\iota-1}\leq|\tau|\leq 2^{\iota+1}}\chi_{\iota}(\tau)\hat{F}(\tau)\cos(\tau\sqrt{L})f_{j}\,d\tau.

Since

is supported in

by the formula above. Together with the bounded overlap of these balls, we obtain

\lVert F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\|F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}.

Altogether, since

is left-invariant, it suffices to show

(7.1)

\big\|\textbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}F^{(\iota)}(\sqrt{L})f\big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\lVert f\rVert_{p}

whenever our initial function

is supported in

.

(2)
Localizing the multiplier.
Next we show that we may replace the multiplier
F^{(\iota)}
by
F^{(\iota)}\psi
, where
\psi
is a smooth cut-off function which is compactly supported away from the origin. Using the dyadic decomposition
(\chi_{\iota})_{\iota\in\mathbb{Z}}
from
Equation

1.9
, we put

\psi:=\sum_{j=-2}^{2}\chi_{j}.

Then
1/8\leq|\lambda|\leq 8
whenever
\lambda\in\supp\psi
, and
|\lambda|\notin(1/4,4)
if
\lambda\in\supp(1-\psi)
. We decompose
F^{(\iota)}
as

F^{(\iota)}=F^{(\iota)}\psi+F^{(\iota)}(1-\psi).

The second part of this decomposition can be treated by the Mikhlin–Hörmander type result of
[
8
]
and
[
25
]
. Note that
F^{(\iota)}=F*\check{\chi}_{\iota}
, and
\check{\chi}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Thus, given
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and

, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\Big(\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Big)^{\alpha}F^{(\iota)}(\lambda)\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\Big(\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Big)^{\alpha}\int_{-2}^{2}2^{\iota}F(\tau)\check{\chi}(2^{\iota}(\lambda-\tau))\,d\tau\Big|

(7.2)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N}2^{\iota(\alpha+1)}\int_{-2}^{2}\frac{|F(\tau)|}{(1+2^{\iota}|\lambda-\tau|)^{N}}\,d\tau.

Since
F
is supported in

in

\Big|\Big(\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Big)^{\alpha}F^{(\iota)}(\lambda)\Big|\lesssim 2^{-\iota}\min\{|\lambda|^{-\alpha},1\}\lVert F\rVert_{2}\quad\text{whenever }|\lambda|\notin(1/4,4).

This implies

\lVert F^{(\iota)}(1-\psi)\rVert_{L^{2}_{Q/2+1,\mathrm{sloc}}}\lesssim_{\psi}2^{-\iota}\lVert F\rVert_{2}.

Hence, the Mikhlin–Hörmander type result of
[
8
]
and
[
25
]
yields

\|(F^{(\iota)}(1-\psi))(\sqrt{L})\|_{p\to p}\lesssim 2^{-\iota}\lVert F\rVert_{2}.

Thus, instead of
Equation

7.1
, we are left proving

(7.3)

\big\|\textbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})f\big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\lVert f\rVert_{p}

for all
f\in D(G)
that are supported in

.

(3)
Truncation along the spectrum of
U
.
Next we decompose the operator
(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})
by a dyadic decomposition of
U
. For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, let the function
F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be given by

F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(\lambda,\rho)=(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{\lambda})\chi_{\ell}(\lambda/\rho)\quad\text{for }\lambda\geq 0,\rho\neq 0

and
F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(\lambda,\rho)=0
else.
We decompose the function on the left-hand side of
Equation

7.3
as

\displaystyle\textbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}(F^{(\iota)}\psi)(\sqrt{L})f

\displaystyle=\textbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}\bigg(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\iota}+\sum_{\ell=\iota+1}^{\infty}\bigg)F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f

(7.4)

\displaystyle=:g_{\leq\iota}+g_{>\iota}.

The sum over
\ell>\iota
can be treated directly by the restriction type estimate of
Theorem

3.2
. Recall that
|B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)|\sim R^{Q}
by
Equation

5.4
. Hence, Hölder’s inequality with

and the restriction type estimate

3.2
imply

\displaystyle\|g_{>\iota}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{Q/q}\|g_{>\iota}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq R^{Q/q}\bigg\|\sum_{\ell=\iota+1}^{\infty}F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)f\bigg\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\iota(Q-d_{2})/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\psi\rVert_{2}\lVert f\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\psi}2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\lVert f\rVert_{p}

if we choose

. (Note that
\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{2}\sim 2^{-\iota s}\|F^{(\iota)}\|_{L^{2}_{s}}
due to the localization in frequency.) Thus, we are done once we have also treated the sum over
\ell\in\{-1,\dots,\iota\}
, that is, it remains to show

(7.5)

\|g_{\leq\iota}\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\lVert f\rVert_{p}.

(4)
The support of the convolution kernel.

Let
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell}
be the convolution kernel of the operator
F^{(\iota)}_{\ell}(L,U)
. By
Equation

5.3
, there is a constant
C>0
such that

B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)\subseteq B_{CR}(0)\times B_{CR^{2}}(0).

Hence the function

is supported in a Euclidean ball of dimension
R\times R^{2}
. In view of the finite propagation speed property which we exploited in part (1) of the proof, we may think of
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell}
being supported in a ball of dimension
R\times R^{2}
as well (which is of course not quite true since we replaced the multiplier
F^{(\iota)}
by
F^{(\iota)}\psi
). In the following, we show that the convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}^{(\iota)}_{\ell}
of the truncated multiplier is essentially supported in an even smaller ball of dimension
R_{\ell}R^{\gamma}\times R^{2}
, where
R_{\ell}:=2^{\ell}
and
\gamma>0
will be a number chosen sufficiently small, depending only on the parameters
s,p,d_{1},d_{2}
. For convenience, we introduce the following notation: We will write

A\lesssim_{\iota}B

whenever
A\leq R^{C(p,d_{1},d_{2})\gamma}B
for some constant
C(p,d_{1},d_{2})>0
depending only on the parameters
p,d_{1},d_{2}
.

Given
\ell\in\{0,\dots,\iota\}
, we split the Euclidean ball
B_{CR}(0)\times B_{CR^{2}}(0)
into a grid with respect to the first layer, which gives a decomposition of
\supp f\subseteq B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)
such that

\supp f=\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}B_{m}^{(\ell)},

where
B_{m}^{(\ell)}\subseteq B_{CR_{\ell}}(x_{m}^{(\ell)})\times B_{CR^{2}}(0)
are disjoint subsets, and
|x_{m}^{(\ell)}-x_{m^{\prime}}^{(\ell)}|>R_{\ell}/2
for
m\neq m^{\prime}
. Then the number
M_{\ell}
of balls in this decomposition is bounded by

(7.6)

M_{\ell}\lesssim(R/R_{\ell})^{d_{1}}=2^{d_{1}(\iota-\ell)}.

Moreover, given
\gamma>0
, the number of overlapping balls

\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}:=B_{2CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}}(x_{m}^{(\ell)})\times B_{9CR^{2}}(0),\quad 1\leq m\leq M_{\ell}

can be bounded by a constant
N_{\gamma}\lesssim_{\iota}1
(which is independent of
\ell
). We decompose the function
f
as

f=\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}}.

In the following, we show that the function

g_{m}^{(\ell)}:=\textbf{1}_{B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}})

is essentially supported in the ball
\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}
. We decompose the function
g_{\leq\iota}
of
Equation

7.4
as

(7.7)

g_{\leq\iota}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\iota}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}g_{m}^{(\ell)}|_{\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\iota}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}g_{m}^{(\ell)}|_{\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}}=:g_{\leq\iota}^{(1)}+g_{\leq\iota}^{(2)}.

To show that the second summand is negligible (in the sense of
Equation

7.13
), we interpolate between
L^{1}
and
L^{2}
via the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem. For the
L^{1}
-
L^{1}
estimate, note that
(x,u)\in(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)})\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)
and
(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\in B_{m}^{(\ell)}
imply

|x-x^{\prime}|\geq CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}
be the convolution kernel associated with
F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)
. Then

F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}})(x,u)=(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}})*\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(x,u),

and we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert g_{\leq\iota}^{(2)}\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\iota}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}\textbf{1}_{(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)})\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}(x,u)\,\big|(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}})*\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(x,u)\big|\,d(x,u)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\iota}\int_{A_{\ell}(x^{\prime})}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}\big|f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}}(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\big|

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\big|\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\big|\,d(x,u)\,d(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})

(7.8)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}|f(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})|\,\kappa_{\gamma}(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\,d(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}),

where

A_{\ell}(x^{\prime}):=\{(x,u)\in B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0):|x-x^{\prime}|\geq CR_{\ell}R^{\gamma}\}

and

\kappa_{\gamma}(x^{\prime},u^{\prime}):=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\iota}\int_{A_{\ell}(x^{\prime})}\big|\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)\big|\,d(x,u).

Given
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields

\displaystyle\int_{A_{\ell}(x^{\prime})}

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}\big((x^{\prime},u^{\prime})^{-1}(x,u)\big)|\,d(x,u)

\displaystyle\lesssim(R_{\ell}R^{\gamma})^{-N}\int_{A_{\ell}(x^{\prime})}\big||x-x^{\prime}|^{N}\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(x-x^{\prime},u-u^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}[x^{\prime},x])\big|\,d(x,u)

(7.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim(R_{\ell}R^{\gamma})^{-N}R^{Q/2}\bigg(\int_{G}\big||x|^{N}\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(x,u)\big|^{2}\,d(x,u)\bigg)^{1/2}.

In the last line we used again that
|B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)|\sim R^{Q}
by
Equation

5.4
.
By
Proposition

4.1
, the second factor of
Equation

7.9
can be estimated by

\int_{G}\big||x|^{N}\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(x,u)\big|^{2}\,d(x,u)\lesssim_{N}R_{\ell}^{2N-d_{2}}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Hence

\kappa_{\gamma}(x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\lesssim_{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\iota}R^{-\gamma N+Q/2}R_{\ell}^{-d_{2}/2}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\lesssim R^{-\gamma N+Q/2}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}.

Altogether, with
Equation

7.8
, we have

(7.10)

\lVert g_{\leq\iota}^{(2)}\rVert_{1}\lesssim_{N}R^{-\gamma N+Q/2}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\lVert f\rVert_{1}.

For the
L^{2}
-
L^{2}
estimate, we use the trivial estimate

(7.11)

\lVert g_{\leq\iota}^{(2)}\rVert_{2}=\bigg\|\sum_{\ell=0}^{\iota}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}g_{m}^{(\ell)}|_{\mathfrak{g}\setminus\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}}\bigg\|_{2}\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\iota}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}\|g_{m}^{(\ell)}\|_{2}.

Since
\lVert\chi_{\ell}\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1
, each summand of
Equation

7.11
can be estimated by

\lVert g_{m}^{(\ell)}\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}})\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}}\rVert_{2}.

Using Hölder’s inequality on the right-hand side of
Equation

7.11
yields

\lVert g_{\leq\iota}^{(2)}\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{\infty}(\iota+1)M_{\ell}^{1/2}\lVert f\rVert_{2}.

Together with
Equation

7.6
and the Sobolev embedding

\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{\infty}\lesssim\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}\sim R^{\alpha}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2},\quad\alpha>1/2,

we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert g_{\leq\iota}^{(2)}\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{\alpha}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}(\iota+1)M_{\ell}^{1/2}\lVert f\rVert_{2}

(7.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{\alpha^{\prime}}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\lVert f\rVert_{2}\quad\text{for }\alpha^{\prime}>\alpha+d_{1}/2.

Applying the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem with
Equation

7.10
and
Equation

7.12
and choosing
N=N(\gamma)\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large in
Equation

7.10
yields

(7.13)

\lVert g_{\leq\iota}^{(2)}\rVert_{p}\lesssim_{N}2^{-\iota N}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\lVert f\rVert_{p}.

In view of the decomposition
Equation

7.7
, for showing
Equation

7.5
, it thus remains to prove

(7.14)

\|g_{\leq\iota}^{(1)}\|_{p}=\bigg\|\sum_{\ell=0}^{\iota}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}\tilde{g}_{m}^{(\ell)}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\lVert f\rVert_{p},

where

\tilde{g}_{m}^{(\ell)}:=\textbf{1}_{\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}\cap B_{3R}^{d_{\mathrm{CC}}}(0)}F_{\ell}^{(\iota)}(L,U)(f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}}).

On a formal level, this means that we may indeed assume that the convolution kernel
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}^{(\iota)}
is supported in a ball of dimension
R_{\ell}R^{\gamma}\times R^{2}
.

(5)
The main contribution.
Hölder’s inequality and the bounded overlapping property of the balls
\tilde{B}_{m}^{(\ell)}
imply

(7.15)

\lVert g_{\leq\iota}^{(1)}\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim_{\iota}(\iota+1)^{p-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\iota}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\ell}}\|\tilde{g}_{m}^{(\ell)}\|_{p}^{p}.

Using Hölder’s inequality together with the restriction type estimate
Equation

3.2
yields

\displaystyle\|\tilde{g}_{m}^{(\ell)}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim((R_{\ell}R^{\gamma})^{d_{1}}R^{2d_{2}})^{1/q}\lVert g_{m}^{(\ell)}\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\iota}(R_{\ell}^{d_{1}}R^{2d_{2}})^{1/q}\lVert g_{m}^{(\ell)}\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(R_{\ell}^{d_{1}-d_{2}}R^{2d_{2}})^{1/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}\lVert f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}}\rVert_{p}.

Plugging this estimate into the right-hand side of
Equation

7.15
and using the fact that the functions
f|_{B_{m}^{(\ell)}}
have disjoint support, we obtain

\lVert g_{\leq\iota}^{(1)}\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim_{\iota}(\iota+1)^{p-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\iota}(R_{\ell}^{d_{1}-d_{2}}R^{2d_{2}})^{p/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{2}^{p}\lVert f\rVert_{p}^{p}.

Choosing
\gamma>0
small enough, we may conclude that

\displaystyle\lVert g_{\leq\iota}^{(1)}\rVert_{p}^{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon\iota}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\iota}(R_{\ell}^{d_{1}-d_{2}}R^{d_{2}-d_{1}})^{p/q}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{p}\lVert f\rVert_{p}^{p}

for some
0<\varepsilon<s-d/q
. Recall that
R_{\ell}=2^{\ell}
and
R=2^{\iota}
. Since
d_{1}>d_{2}
, we have

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\iota}\Big(\Big(\frac{R_{\ell}}{R}\Big)^{d_{1}-d_{2}}\Big)^{p/q}\leq C_{p}.

Altogether, we obtain

\lVert g_{\leq\iota}^{(1)}\rVert_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\tilde{\varepsilon}\iota}\lVert F^{(\iota)}\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\lVert f\rVert_{p}

for some
\tilde{\varepsilon}>0
. This is
Equation

7.14
, so the proof is concluded.
∎

8.
Remarks on weighted restriction type estimates for sub-Laplacians

In
[
7
]
, Chen and Ouhabaz proved a spectral multiplier theorem for the Grushin operator
\mathcal{G}=-\Delta_{x}-|x|^{2}\Delta_{u}
acting on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
by using a weighted restriction type estimate of the form

\lVert|x|^{\alpha}F(\sqrt{\mathcal{G}})f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})}\leq C_{p,\alpha}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})},

where
\alpha>0
and
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a bounded Borel function supported in
[1/4,1]
. Let
L
denote again a sub-Laplacian on a Heisenberg type group
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
with layers of dimension
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
, respectively. Then

Lf=\mathcal{G}f

for any
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
-radial function on the Heisenberg type group
G
, i.e., a function on
G
which only depends on
|x|
(with
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
) and
u\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
(where we identify again
G
with its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, which is in turn identified with
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
). In view of this close relationship, one might hope that the approach of Chen and Ouhabaz can also be applied in the setting of Heisenberg type groups. However, a crucial ingredient of their approach is the sub-elliptic estimate

(8.1)

\lVert|x|^{\alpha}g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})}\leq C_{\alpha}\lVert|\mu|^{-\alpha}(H^{\mu})^{\alpha/2}g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})},\quad g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}),

where
H^{\mu}=-\Delta_{x}+\tfrac{1}{4}|x|^{2}|\mu|^{2}
denotes again the rescaled Hermite operator on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. Unfortunately, the analogous estimate of
Equation

8.1
in our setting, where
H^{\mu}
is replaced by the
\mu
-twisted Laplacian
L^{\mu}
of
Equation

2.2
, fails. We will prove in the following that the estimate
Equation

8.1
where
H^{\mu}
is replaced by
L^{\mu}
is false for
\alpha=1
. (The approach of
[
7
]
requires to choose
0<\alpha<d_{2}(1/p-1/2)
as large as possible, so large values of
\alpha
are the crucial ones.)

Via
Equation

2.4
and a linear substitution, the estimate

\lVert|x|^{\alpha}g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})}\leq C_{\alpha}\lVert|\mu|^{-\alpha}(L^{\mu})^{\alpha/2}g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})}

is equivalent to

\lVert|z|^{\alpha}g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}\leq C_{\alpha}\lVert|\mu|^{-\alpha}(L_{0}^{|\mu|})^{\alpha/2}g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})},

where
L_{0}^{|\mu|}
is the twisted Laplacian of
Equation

2.5
. Rescaling with
|\mu|
, we may restrict to the case
|\mu|=1
. Let
A:=L_{0}^{1}
. Then, by
Equation

2.5
,

A=-\Delta_{z}+\tfrac{1}{4}|z|^{2}-iN,

where, when writing
z=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
,

N=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(a_{j}\partial_{b_{j}}-b_{j}\partial_{a_{j}}).

Now, suppose that

(8.2)

\lVert|z|g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}\leq C\lVert A^{1/2}g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}\quad\text{for all }g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}).

Recall that the matrix coefficients
\Phi_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}
of the Schrödinger representation
\pi_{1}(\cdot,0)
given by
Equation

2.6
are eigenfunctions of
A
with

(8.3)

A\Phi_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}=(2|\nu^{\prime}|_{1}+n)\Phi_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}.

On the other hand, the functions
\Phi_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}
are also eigenfunctions of
H=-\Delta_{z}+\tfrac{1}{4}|z|^{2}
by Equation (1.3.25) of
[
37
]
, with

(8.4)

H\Phi_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}=(|\nu|_{1}+|\nu^{\prime}|_{1}+n)\Phi_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}.

When writing
\zeta=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})
, direct computation shows

\widehat{Ag}(\zeta)=\hat{A}\hat{g}(\zeta),

where the operator
\hat{A}
is given by

\hat{A}:=|\zeta|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{4}\Delta_{\zeta}+i\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\partial_{\alpha_{j}}\beta_{j}-\partial_{\beta_{j}}\alpha_{j})

and
\widehat{\cdot}
denotes the
2n
-dimensional Fourier transform given by

\hat{g}(\zeta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g(z)e^{-i\langle\zeta,z\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}}\,dz,\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}.

Since
\hat{A}(g(2\zeta))=(Ag)(2\zeta)
, the estimate
Equation

8.2
together with Plancherel’s theorem implies

(8.5)

\lVert(-\Delta_{z})^{1/2}g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}\leq C\lVert A^{1/2}g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}\quad\text{for all }g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}).

Setting
g:=\Phi_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}
and using
Equation

8.4
,
Equation

8.2
,
Equation

8.5
,
Equation

8.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle(|\nu|_{1}+|\nu^{\prime}|_{1}+n)\lVert g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert H^{1/2}g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big((-\Delta_{z}+\tfrac{1}{4}|z|^{2})g,g\big)

\displaystyle=\big\|(-\Delta_{z})^{1/2}g\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}\big\||z|g\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\lVert A^{1/2}g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=C(2|\nu^{\prime}|_{1}+n)\lVert g\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}^{2}.

Now fixing
\nu^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and letting
|\nu|\to\infty
yields a contradiction, whence the assumed estimate
Equation

8.2
is indeed false.

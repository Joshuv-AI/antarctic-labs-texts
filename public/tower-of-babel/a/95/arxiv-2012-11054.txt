Abstract
. We study left invariant conformal Killing-Yano (CKY) 2 2 -forms on Lie groups endowed with a left invariant metric. We classify all 5 5 -dimensional metric Lie algebras carrying CKY tensors that are obtained as a one-dimensional central extension of 4-dimensional metric Lie algebras endowed with a invertible parallel skew-symmetric tensor. On the other hand, we also classify 5 5 -dimensional metric Lie algebras with center of dimension greater than one admitting strict CKY tensors. In addition, we determine all possible CKY tensors on these metric Lie algebras. In particular, we exhibit the first examples of CKY 2 2 -forms on metric Lie algebras which do not admit any Sasakian structure.



1.
Introduction

A differential
p
-form
\eta
on a
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is called
conformal Killing-Yano
(CKY for short) if it satisfies for any vector field
X
the following equation

(1)

\nabla_{X}\eta=\dfrac{1}{p+1}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\eta-\dfrac{1}{n-p+1}X^{*}\wedge\mathrm{d}^{*}\eta,

where
X^{*}
is the dual 1-form of
X
,
\mathrm{d}^{*}
is the co-differential operator,
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection associated to
g
and
\iota_{X}
is the interior product with
X
. If
\eta
is co-closed (
\mathrm{d}^{*}\eta=0
) then
\eta
is said to be a Killing-Yano
p
-form (KY for short). Closed conformal Killing-Yano forms will be called
*
-Killing-Yano
p
-forms. Note that the Hodge-star operator
applied to a CKY
p
-form determines a CKY
(n-p)
-form on
M
. In particular, the Hodge-star operator interchanges closed and co-closed CKY forms on
M
.

Killing-Yano
p
-forms were defined by K. Yano in 1951
[
28
]
, as a natural generalization of Killing vector fields. Indeed, for
p=1
they are dual to Killing vector fields.
Later, Kashiwada and Tachibana studied CKY forms in
[
26
]
,
[
16
]
as a generalization of KY forms. Indeed, it is easy to see that a CKY
1
-form is dual to a conformal vector field.

This topic has taken a lot of interest not only in geometry but also in physics, since it has applications in the study of black holes. A CKY form describes hidden symmetries, that is symmetries that cannot be identified directly. In 1970, Walker and Penrose noticed in
[
27
]
that some conserved quantity in the Kerr metric is originated from a Killing tensor of rank two. Since then, the study of CKY forms is in continuous growth in black holes theory.

In the literature, there are more results concerning the (non-)existence of KY forms than “strict” conformal Killing-Yano forms, that is, CKY forms which are not KY. Notable examples of KY
2
-forms are given by nearly Kähler manifolds
(M,g,J)
whose fundamental
2
-form
\omega
given by
\omega(X,Y)=g(JX,Y)
is KY. For a compact simply connected symmetric space
M
, it was shown in
[
8
]
that
M
admits a non-parallel Killing-Yano
p
-form,
p\geq 2
, if and only if it is isometric to a Riemannian product
S^{k}\times N
, where
S^{k}
is a round sphere and
k>p
. Another important result states that every Killing-Yano
p
-form on a compact quaternion Kähler manifold is parallel for any
p\geq 2
, see
[
19
]
.

In the case of CKY forms, the following results were proved in
[
24
]
: the canonical
2
-form of a Sasakian manifold is a strict CKY
2
-form; a CKY
p
-form (
p\neq 3,4
) on a compact manifold with holonomy
G_{2}
is parallel; the vector space of CKY
p
-forms on a
n
-dimensional connected Riemannian manifold has dimension at most
{n+2}\choose{p+1}
; and there are no CKY forms on compact
manifolds of negative constant sectional curvature.
In
[
20
]
a description of CKY
p
-forms on a compact Riemannian product was given, proving that such a form is a sum of forms of the following types: parallel forms, pull-back of Killing-Yano forms on the factors, and their Hodge duals.

In this article we study left invariant CKY
2
-forms on Lie groups endowed with a left invariant metric. The study of KY 2-forms in this setting began in
[
7
]
and was continued in
[
5
]
and
[
11
]
.
CKY
2
-forms (not necessary left invariant) on four dimensional simply connected Lie groups with left invariant metrics were classified in
[
3
]
, where it is also shown that those
2
-forms which are left invariant are parallel. Recently, in
[
12
]
CKY
2
- and
3
-forms were studied in
2
-step nilpotent Lie groups.

Some general properties of strict CKY
2
-forms were described in
[
4
]
. In particular
[
4
, Theorem
4.3
]
shows that a metric Lie algebra admitting a strict CKY tensor has odd dimension. In dimension
3
, all CKY
2
-forms were classified in
[
1
]
. It is not clear yet what happens in dimensions greater than three in general. It is known that the only
2
-step nilpotent Lie algebras with a strict CKY 2-form are the Heisenberg Lie algebras of dimension
2n+1

[
1
, Theorem 4.4]
(see also
[
12
]
), which also admit Sasakian structures.

Besides this progress, it is clear that there are not many explicit examples of strict CKY
2
-forms on Lie groups, except for the ones induced by left invariant Sasakian structures; and the next natural case is to consider
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebras.
Therefore, the first goal of this work is to fill this gap by giving examples of non-Sasakian Lie algebras that admit a CKY
2
-form. Secondly, we provide a classification of
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebras admitting strict CKY
2
-forms distinguishing between two cases according to the dimension of their center.

This work is organized as follows: In Section

2
we recall some basic definitions about left invariant CKY 2-forms on Lie groups endowed with a left invariant metric. We also review some results from
[
4
]
that will be useful in this work.
In Section
3
we use the recent classification of
4
-dimensional metric Lie algebra carrying parallel skew-symmetric endomorphisms in
[
15
]
, to classify all
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebras with one dimensional center admitting a CKY
2
-form whose co-differential lies in the dual of the center (Theorem
3.2
).

In Section
4
we exhibit some general results on metric Lie algebras with center of dimension greater than one carrying a CKY
2
-form.
We continue in Section
5
with a complete classification, up to isometric isomorphism and scaling, of
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebras with center of dimension greater than one admitting a CKY
2
-form (Theorem
5.4
). In particular, we obtain the first explicit examples of CKY tensors on metric Lie algebras which do not admit any Sasakian structure.

In Section
6
we study the vector space of left invariant CKY
2
-forms on each family of metric Lie algebras obtained in Section
3
and Section
5
.
In the former case we show that the CKY
2
-forms are always closed (Proposition
6.1
), in contrast with the latter case when they are never closed (Proposition
6.3
). Moreover, in Theorem
6.2
and Theorem
6.5
, full descriptions of the vector spaces of left invariant CKY
2
-forms (including KY
2
-forms) are given, proving that they are one dimensional.

2.
Left invariant CKY 2-forms on Lie groups

2.1.
Left invariant structures on Lie groups

Let
G
be a Lie group and
{\mathfrak{g}}
its Lie algebra of left invariant vector fields. It is known that there is a linear isomorphism between
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
T_{e}G
where
e
is the identity in
G
.
We denote by
g
a left invariant Riemannian metric on
G
satisfying
{L_{a}}^{*}g=g
, where
L_{a}
denotes the left translation by
a\in G
. There is a well-known correspondence between left invariant metrics on
G
and inner products on
{\mathfrak{g}}\cong T_{e}G
defined as
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:=g_{e}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Every left invariant metric
g
defines a unique Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
on
G
, and for every
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
it has the following simple expression

(2)

2\langle\nabla_{x}y,z\rangle=\langle[x,y],z\rangle-\langle[y,z],x\rangle+\langle[z,x],y\rangle.

In particular, it is easy to see that for any
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, the endomorphism
\nabla_{x}:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
is skew-symmetric with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

A
2
-form
\omega
is left invariant if
L_{a}^{*}\omega=\omega
for all
a\in G
, and every left invariant
2
-form
\omega
determines a skew-symmetric bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, which we also denote by
\omega
.
Reciprocally, every skew-symmetric bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}
defines a left invariant 2-form on
G
.

There is a left invariant skew-symmetric
(1,1)
tensor
T:TG\to TG
associated to each left invariant
2
-form
\omega
, such that,
\omega(X,Y)=g(TX,Y)
for all
X,Y
vector fields on
G
. In particular, this induces a skew-symmetric endomorphism on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, which we still denote by
T
. Reciprocally, every skew-symmetric endomorphism
T
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
defines a skew-symmetric bilinear form
\omega
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and as a consequence induces a left invariant 2-form on
G
.
We will study left invariant
2
-forms on
G
satisfying condition (
1
), and the associated endomorphism
T
will be called a CKY tensor. We will make no distinction between the CKY
2
-form
\omega
and the associated CKY tensor
T
.

2.2.
CKY tensors on Lie algebras

In
[
24
]
, Semmelmann gives an equivalent definition for CKY
2
-forms on Riemannian manifolds
(M,g)
, that is,
\omega
is a CKY
2
-form on
(M,g)
if and only if there exists a
1
-form
\theta
such that

(3)

(\nabla_{X}\omega)(Y,Z)+(\nabla_{Y}\omega)(X,Z)=2g(X,Y)\theta(Z)-g(Y,Z)\theta(X)-g(X,Z)\theta(Y)

for all
X,Y,Z
vector fields on
M
. If such a
1
-form exists, then it is uniquely determined by
\omega
and it is given by

\theta(X)=-\displaystyle{\frac{1}{n-1}\mathrm{d}^{*}\omega(X)},

where
n
denotes the dimension of
M
.
We consider now a Lie group
G
equipped with a left invariant metric
g
, and let
\omega
be a left invariant CKY
2
-form on
(G,g)
with associated CKY tensor
T
. Since
\omega
is left invariant, then
\theta
is left invariant as well. Moreover, equation (
3
) is equivalent to the following expression at the Lie algebra level

(4)

\langle\left(\nabla_{x}T\right)y,z\rangle+\langle\left(\nabla_{y}T\right)x,z\rangle=2\langle x,y\rangle\theta(z)-\langle y,z\rangle\theta(x)-\langle x,z\rangle\theta(y),

for some
\theta\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, and we will refer to (
4
) as the CKY condition along this work. Note that (
4
) is symmetric with respect to
x
and
y
.
We denote by
\xi
the unique element of
{\mathfrak{g}}
such that

\theta(x)=\langle\xi,x\rangle

for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, and we will refer to
\xi
as the vector associated to
T
.
We are interested in strict CKY tensors, that is,
\theta\neq 0
. We recall now some general results of CKY tensors from
[
4
]
that will be useful later.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
T
is a CKY tensor on a metric Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
with associated vector
\xi
, then
T\xi=0
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
T
be a CKY tensor on the metric Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, with associated vector
\xi
. If
\xi\neq 0
, then
\dim{\mathfrak{g}}
is odd and
T|_{\xi^{\perp}}:{\xi}^{\perp}\to{\xi}^{\perp}
is a linear isomorphism. Moreover,

(\nabla_{\xi}T)\xi=\nabla_{\xi}\xi=0
, and
{\xi}^{\perp}
is stable by the operator
\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
T
is a CKY tensor on a given metric Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, and
\xi
is the unique element such that
\theta(x)=\langle\xi,x\rangle
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
as described above, then
\theta(\xi)=\|\xi\|^{2}
.
If we consider
cT
with
c\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
, then
cT
is a CKY tensor as well, since it is easily verified that the space of CKY tensors is a vector space.
Then, the
1
-form associated to
cT
is given by
\widehat{\theta}=c\theta
. Moreover, the unique
element
\widehat{\xi}
such that
\widehat{\theta}(x)=\langle\widehat{\xi},x\rangle
is
\widehat{\xi}=c\xi
.
Therefore, without loss of generality, we may assume that
\xi
is unitary, since we can replace
T
by
\frac{1}{\|\xi\|}T
, if necessary. From now on, we will assume
\|\xi\|=1
, in particular
\theta(\xi)=1
.

In
[
4
]
it was proven that if
{\mathfrak{g}}
is unimodular, then
\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
. Here, we consider the general case when
{\mathfrak{g}}
is not necessarily unimodular and we obtain the following result:

Lemma 2.3
.

If
T
is a strict CKY tensor on a metric Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
with associated vector
\xi
, then
\langle\xi,{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}\rangle\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{i}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, then

\displaystyle\theta(\xi)

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle{\frac{1}{n-1}\mathrm{d}^{*}\omega(\xi)}=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\nabla_{e_{i}}\omega)(e_{i},\xi)}=-\displaystyle{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega(\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{i},\xi)+\omega(e_{i},\nabla_{e_{i}}\xi)}

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle T\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{i},\xi\rangle+\langle Te_{i},\nabla_{e_{i}}\xi\rangle}=-\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle[Te_{i},e_{i}],\xi\rangle}.

Since,
\theta(\xi)\neq 0
, then
\langle\xi,{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}\rangle\neq 0
.
∎

As said in the introduction,
in
[
4
, Theorem
4.3
]
, Andrada and Dotti proved that a metric Lie algebra admitting a strict CKY tensor has odd dimension. Therefore, we look for CKY
2
-forms on odd dimensional metric Lie algebras.

The first step was taken in
[
1
]
, where all CKY
2
-forms in dimension
3
were classified: the Lie algebras
\mathfrak{su}(2)
,
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
,
\mathfrak{h}_{3}
and
\mathfrak{aff}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}
admit strict CKY 2-forms for certain metrics. We follow the notation in
[
18
]
and in
[
2
]
where
\mathfrak{su}(2)
is the Lie algebra of the Lie group
SU(2)
,
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
is the Lie algebra of the Lie group
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
,
\mathfrak{h}_{3}
denotes the
3
-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{aff}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the 2-dimensional non-abelian Lie algebra.

Therefore, the next natural step is to consider
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebras; we analyze them taking into account the dimension of their center.
In Section
3
we study the case with
1
-dimensional center, while in Section
5
we focus on the Lie algebras with center of dimension greater that one.

3.
CKY tensors on
5
-dimensional Lie algebras with
\dim{\mathfrak{z}}=1

In this section we classify all
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebras
admitting a strict CKY
2
-form whose co-differential lies in the dual of the center, in particular the center
is
1
-dimensional according to
[
4
, Theorem 4.6]
.
In addition, we determine all possible strict CKY tensors on these metric Lie algebras (see Table
1
).

First, let us recall a result from
[
4
]
, where the authors characterize

n
-dimensional metric Lie algebras admitting strict CKY tensors with associated vector
\xi
in the center, as a central extension of
(n-1)
-dimensional Lie algebras equipped with an invertible KY tensor.
For the sake of completeness we include this result, see
[
4
, Theorem 4.6 and Theorem 4,8]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
S
be an invertible KY tensor on the metric Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot],\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
such that the
2
-form
\mu(x,y)=-2\langle S^{-1}x,y\rangle
is closed. Set
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{h}\oplus_{\mu}\mathbb{R}\xi
the central extension of
\mathfrak{h}
by the
2
-form
\mu
, that is the vector space
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbb{R}\xi
equipped with the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mu}
defined by

(5)

[\mathfrak{h},\xi]_{\mu}=0,\ \ [x,y]_{\mu}=[x,y]+\mu(x,y)\xi,\ x,y\in\mathfrak{h};

the inner product on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is defined by extending the one on
\mathfrak{h}
by
\langle\mathfrak{h},\xi\rangle=0
, with
\|\xi\|>0
arbitrary. Then, the endomorphism
T
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
given by
T|_{\mathfrak{h}}=S
and
T\xi=0
is a strict CKY tensor on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Conversely, any metric Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
admitting a strict CKY tensor
T
with associated vector
\xi
in the center is obtained in this way, where
\mathfrak{h}=\xi^{\perp}
,
S:=T|_{\mathfrak{h}}
, and the Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{h}
is the
\mathfrak{h}
-component of the Lie bracket on
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Moreover, the center of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is generated by
\xi
.

Second, every KY tensor on a
4
-dimensional metric Lie algebra is parallel according to
[
3
, Lemma 3.7]
. Notice that any parallel skew-symmetric endomorphism is KY, so in particular if it is invertible it satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
3.1
(see also
[
4
, Corollary 2.2]
).
More recently, in
[
15
, Section 3]
a classification of all non-abelian
4
-dimensional metric Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{h},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
that carry parallel skew-symmetric endomorphisms was made.
This classification is up to isometric isomorphisms and for each fixed metric Lie algebra all parallel skew-symmetric tensors are given up to equivalence, where,
two parallel skew-symmetric tensors
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are said to be
equivalent
if there exists an isometric isomorphism of Lie algebras

(6)

\varphi:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}\;\text{such that}\;\varphi H_{1}=H_{2}\varphi.

These results altogether allow us to give a full classification of
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebras admitting a strict CKY
2
-form whose co-differential lies in the dual of the center.
Before we state the main result of this section we give an example of this construction.

Example 1
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be the
4
-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathbb{R}\times\mathfrak{e}(2)
with Lie brackets given by
[e_{1},e_{2}]=-f_{2}
and
[e_{1},f_{2}]=e_{2}
. The metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{t}
is defined by
\text{diag}(t,t,t,t)
in the basis
\{e_{1},f_{1},e_{2},f_{2}\}
, for
t>0
.
According to
[
15
]
, any invertible parallel skew-symmetric endomorphism
H
on
(\mathfrak{h},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{t})
is given by

Se_{1}=f_{1}
,
Se_{2}=a_{2}f_{2}

for some non-zero
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, it follows from Theorem
3.1
and the discussion above that the central extension metric Lie algebra
(\mathbb{R}\times\mathfrak{e}(2)\oplus_{\mu}\mathbb{R}\xi,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{t})
with
\|\xi\|=1
,
\mu(x,y)=-2\langle S^{-1}x,y\rangle
, and Lie brackets given by

[e_{1},e_{2}]_{\mu}=-f_{2},\;[e_{1},f_{2}]_{\mu}=e_{2},\;[e_{1},f_{1}]_{\mu}=2t{a_{1}}^{-1}\xi,\;[e_{2},f_{2}]_{\mu}=2t{a_{2}}^{-1}\xi

admits a CKY tensor
T
given by
T|_{\mathfrak{h}}=S
and
T(\xi)=0
. In particular, the associated
2
-form is given by
\omega=a_{1}te^{1}\wedge f^{1}+a_{2}te^{2}\wedge f^{2}
, where
\{e^{1},f^{1},e^{2},f^{2}\}
is the metric dual basis of
\{e_{1},f_{1},e_{2},f_{2}\}
. We denote
\mathbb{R}\times\mathfrak{e}(2)\oplus_{\mu}\mathbb{R}\xi
by
{\mathfrak{g}}_{a_{1},a_{2},t}
to emphasize that the structure constants depend on those parameters.

Next, we have the main result of this section:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebra and denote by
{\mathfrak{z}}
its center.
If
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
admits a strict CKY tensor
T
with associated vector
\xi\in{\mathfrak{z}}
, then
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is isometrically isomorphic to one and only one of the metric Lie algebras in Table
1
.

Moreover, the CKY tensor
T
is uniquely determined, up to scaling, by the corresponding metric Lie algebra and it
is
given in the last column of Table
1
.

Proof.

Proceeding as in Example
1
with all metric Lie algebras admitting an invertible parallel skew-symmetric tensor classified in
[
15
]
, we obtain all possible strict CKY tensors on a
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebra with
\xi\in{\mathfrak{z}}
.
Moreover,
T
is uniquely determined, up to scaling, by the corresponding metric Lie algebra (see Remark
2.1
).

It remains to show that each metric Lie algebras
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
in Table
1
are pairwise non-isometrically isomorphic. Clearly, elements from different families (rows) are not isomorphic.
Now we check metric Lie algebras within the same family. Since they are similar, we only explain one of those families.

Consider the metric Lie algebra from Example
1
with parameters
a_{1},a_{2},t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, and assume
\Phi:({\mathfrak{g}}_{a_{1},a_{2},t},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{t})\to({\mathfrak{g}}_{a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2},t^{\prime}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{t^{\prime}})
is an isometric isomorphism. First, we show that
t=t^{\prime}
. Indeed, note that
\Phi(\xi)=\pm\xi
, and
\langle\Phi(x),\xi\rangle_{t^{\prime}}=0
for all
x\in\mathfrak{h}=\xi^{\perp}
, thus
\Phi|_{\xi^{\perp}}
preserves
\xi^{\perp}
.
Moreover,

\Phi|_{\mathfrak{h}}:(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{t})\to(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{t^{\prime}})
is an isometric automorphism as well.
According to
[
15
, Proposition 3.6]
we have that
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{t})
is isometrically isomorphic to
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{t^{\prime}})
if and only of
t=t^{\prime}
.

Now we fix
t
and we show that
(a_{1},a_{2})=(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2})
. Using (
5
) and the fact that
\Phi|_{\mathfrak{h}}
is an isomorphism, we have that
\mu^{\prime}(\Phi(x),\Phi(y))=\mu(x,y)
, or equivalently,
S^{\prime}=\Phi^{t}S\Phi
. Thus,
S
and
S^{\prime}
are equivalent according to (
6
). Again, according to
[
15
, Proposition 3.6]
,
S
is equivalent to
S^{\prime}
if and only if
(a_{1},a_{2})=(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2})
. ∎

Metric Lie algebra

strict CKY
2
-form
\omega

(\mathbb{R}\times\mathfrak{e}(2)\oplus_{\mu}\mathbb{R}\xi,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{t})

4.
CKY tensors on Lie algebras with
\dim{\mathfrak{z}}>1

In this section we study Lie algebras with
\dim{\mathfrak{z}}>1
admitting CKY tensors, where
{\mathfrak{z}}
denotes the center of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
In particular, such a Lie algebra cannot admit any Sasakian structure, since the center of a Sasakian Lie algebra is trivial or has dimension
1
. As a consequence of this condition on the center of the Lie algebra, we have the following result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T
be a strict CKY tensor on the metric Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, with associated 1-form
\theta
and dual vector
\xi
. If
\operatorname{dim}{\mathfrak{z}}\geq 2
, then
\theta(z)=0
for all
z\in\mathfrak{z}
. In particular
\xi\perp{\mathfrak{z}}
.

Proof.

Let
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathfrak{z}
, such that
z_{1},z_{2}\neq 0
and
\langle z_{1},z_{2}\rangle=0
. From (
4
) applied to
z_{1},z_{2}
we have

\displaystyle 2\langle\left(\nabla_{z_{1}}T\right)z_{1},z_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle z_{1},z_{1}\rangle\theta(z_{2})-\langle z_{1},z_{2}\rangle\theta(z_{1})-\langle z_{1},z_{2}\rangle\theta(z_{1}).

Since
\langle\left(\nabla_{z_{1}}T\right)z_{1},z_{2}\rangle=\langle\nabla_{z_{1}}Tz_{1},z_{2}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{z_{1}}z_{1},Tz_{2}\rangle=0
,
we obtain that
\theta(z_{2})=0
.
∎

As a consequence of
\xi\in{\mathfrak{z}}^{\perp}
we have the following condition:

Lemma 4.2
.

If
T
is a strict CKY tensor on the metric Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, then
T
does not preserve the decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{z}}\oplus{\mathfrak{z}}^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Let
\theta
and
\xi
be the
1
-form and dual vector associated to
T
. Suppose
z
is a unitary vector on the
{\mathfrak{z}}
, then from
(
4
) we obtain

2\langle(\nabla_{z}T)z,\xi\rangle=2\|z\|^{2}\theta(\xi)=2,

which implies
\langle\nabla_{z}Tz,\xi\rangle=1
.
Then, it follows from equation (
2
) that
\langle[Tz,\xi],z\rangle=-2
, and therefore
T{\mathfrak{z}}\nsubseteq{\mathfrak{z}}
.
∎

In the next lemma we apply (
4
) to different choices of
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, according to the decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{z}}\oplus{\mathfrak{z}}^{\perp}
.
We summarize the conditions that a Lie algebra must satisfy in order to admit a strict CKY tensor. These conditions will be useful in the next section. Note that if we have three central elements, then both sides of CKY condition (
4
) are identically zero, since
{\mathfrak{z}}\perp\xi
.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)

(7)

We check the CKY condition (

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\langle[\xi,Tx],\xi\rangle=\langle\nabla_{\xi}Tx-\nabla_{Tx}\xi,\xi\rangle=\langle\nabla_{\xi}Tx,\xi\rangle+\langle\nabla_{\xi}x,T\xi\rangle=\langle(\nabla_{\xi}T)x,\xi\rangle=-\langle(\nabla_{\xi}T)\xi,x\rangle.

Consider now
(x,y,\xi)

, since

\displaystyle-2\langle x,y\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{\xi}Tx,y\rangle-\langle\nabla_{Tx}{\xi},y\rangle-\langle\nabla_{Tx}y,\xi\rangle+\langle\nabla_{y}Tx,\xi\rangle+\langle\nabla_{y}\xi,Tx\rangle-\langle\nabla_{\xi}y,Tx\rangle+

\displaystyle=2\langle\left(\nabla_{\xi}T\right)x,y\rangle+2\langle\left(\nabla_{x}T\right)\xi,{y}\rangle

In the same way, from (

with

Similarly, using that

Therefore, the CKY condition is satisfied for all

and

Now for any

Next, as (

Finally, the CKY condition (
4

Since (

8
), (

), (
10

Proof.

Remark 5.1

As a consequence of Lemma

Lemma 5.3

Let
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be as above, then

({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)

Let

Since

is a Lie isomorphism,

preserves the center, i.e.
\varphi({\mathfrak{z}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s}))={\mathfrak{z}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}})

and the fact that

is an isometry, then

can be written in the orthonormal basis

\varphi=\begin{pmatrix}a&0&0&0&0\\

0&v&bu&0&0\\
0&0&0&g&-ch\\

,
for some

with

, and

. From

and

, and from

we obtain that

. Moreover, using that

and

\varphi
has the following form

\varphi=\begin{pmatrix}a&0&0&0&0\\
0&a\cos t&-\sin t&0&0\\
0&a\sin t&\cos t&0&0\\
0&0&0&\cos t&-a\sin t\\
0&0&0&\sin t&a\cos t\end{pmatrix}

with
a^{2}=1
.
∎

can be used to produce examples of compact manifolds admitting CKY tensors.
Recall that any left invariant Riemannian metric on a Lie group
G
defines a Riemannian metric on any compact quotient
\Gamma\backslash G
, such that the natural projection
p:G\to\Gamma\backslash G
is a locally isometric covering.
Therefore, any CKY tensor on a metric Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)

induces a CKY tensor on any compact quotient
\Gamma\backslash G
, where
\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of
G
.

Consider
the metric Lie algebra
(L_{5,9},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r})
with the CKY tensor given in Table
3
.
This is an example of a
3
-step nilpotent non-Sasakian Lie algebra admitting a CKY tensor. The associated simply connected Lie group admits lattices (
[
17
]
). Moreover, any lattice in the associated simply connected Lie group determines a nilmanifold admitting a CKY tensor induced by
T
.
In
[
10
]
it is shown that the only nilmanifolds admitting a Sasakian structure (not necessarly invariant) are quotients of the Heisenberg Lie group. Therefore, any nilmanifold obtained as a quotient of the simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
L_{5,9}
does not admit any Sasakian structure.

For the second metric Lie algebra
(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathfrak{su}(2),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r,s})
, any lattice in the associated simply connected Lie group
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times SU(2)
determines a compact manifold admitting a CKY tensor induced by
T
. In particular, the product
\mathcal{S}^{3}\times\mathcal{T}^{2}
of the
3
-dimensional sphere with the
2
-dimensional torus equipped with the metric induced by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r,s}
admits a CKY tensor; in contrast with the nilmanifold in the first example, this Lie group does admit Sasakian structures (which are non-invariant), according to
[
9
]
.

Finally, the metric Lie algebra
(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r,s})
is the third example of a non-Sasakian Lie algebra admitting a CKY tensor.
The Lie group
SL(2,\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
admits lattices (see
[
21
]
).
Moreover, any lattice determines a compact manifold admitting a CKY tensor induced by
T
.
We do not known in this case if these compact manifolds admit non-invariant Sasakian structures.

6.
The vector space of CKY 2-forms

The space of CKY 2-forms on a given metric Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a vector space (see
[
25
]
,
[
24
]
), we denote it by
\mathcal{CKY}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
. It contains two distinguished vector subspaces: the vector space of KY
2
-forms and the vector space of
*
-KY
2
-forms denoted by
\mathcal{KY}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
and
\mathcal{*KY}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
respectively.
It is known that the sum of any
*
-KY
2
-form and any KY
2
-form is a CKY
2
-form, but the converse does not hold in general, see
[
16
,
26
]
. In this section we investigate these subspaces on the metric Lie algebras in Table
1
, and then any metric Lie algebra in Table
3
.

Regarding the metric Lie algebras from Section 3, we can characterize the CKY
2
-forms classified in Theorem
3.2
further. Indeed, it is clear that those CKY
2
-forms are not Killing-Yano; however, in the next proposition, we show that they are always closed.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebra,
then any strict CKY
2
-form, such that its co-differential lies in the dual of the center, is a
*
-KY
2
-form.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a strict CKY
2
-form with associated tensor
T
and central vector
\xi
.
It follows from Theorem
3.1
and the discussion there, that
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is as central extension of a
4
-dimensional Lie algebra equipped with a parallel skew-symmetric tensor.
Moreover, using the fact that
T\xi=0
we obtain
\mathrm{d}\omega(x,y,z)=\mathrm{d}^{\mathfrak{h}}\omega(x,y,z)
for any
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{h}
, where
\mathrm{d}^{\mathfrak{h}}
denotes the derivative with respect to
(\mathfrak{h},[\,,\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle|_{\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}})
.

Since
T|_{\mathfrak{h}}
is a parallel tensor on
(\mathfrak{h},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle|_{\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}})
, we have that
\mathrm{d}^{\mathfrak{h}}\omega=0
(see
[
4
, Corollary 2.2]
).
In addition,
\mathrm{d}\omega(x,y,\xi)=-\omega([x,y],\xi)=\langle[x,y],T\xi\rangle=0
. Therefore,
\omega
is a closed, that is,
\omega
is a
*
-KY
2
-form.
∎

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebra admitting a strict CKY
2
-form such that its co-differential lies in the dual of the center, or equivalently a metric Lie algebra classified in Theorem
3.2
; we notice that there is only one strict CKY
2
-form, up to scaling. Indeed, any associated tensor in the third column of Table
1
is completely determined by the corresponding metric Lie algebra, and any non-zero multiple of
T
is again a strict CKY tensor according to Remark
2.1
.

In the next result we show that each metric Lie algebra in Table
1
does not admit any other CKY
2
-forms, including KY
2
-forms and strict CKY
2
-forms whose co-differential is not necessary in the dual of the center.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebra admitting a strict CKY
2
-form, such that its co-differential lies in the dual of the center, then

\mathcal{KY}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)=0
and
\mathcal{*KY}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)=\mathcal{CKY}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is
1
-dimensional. In particular,
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
does not admit non-zero parallel tensors.

Proof.

It follows from Section 3 that any
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebra admitting a CKY
2
-form such that its co-differential lies in the dual of the center is listed in Table
1
. Thus, let us fix one of the Lie algebras
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
in Table
1
, and we proceed case by case.
By a direct calculation assisted by the software system Sage
[
23
]
we see that an arbitrary
2
-form on
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is CKY, if and only if, its associated tensor is the one listed in Table
1
, up to scaling.

In particular,
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
does not admit neither non-trivial KY
2
-forms nor strict CKY
2
-forms such that their co-differential is not in the dual of the center (we are happy to share the Sage’s implementation with anybody who might be interested). Therefore,
\mathcal{KY}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)=0
,
\mathcal{CKY}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is
1
-dimensional, and using Proposition
6.1
it follows that
\mathcal{*KY}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)=\mathcal{CKY}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
.
∎

Remark 6.1
.

It follows from Theorem
6.2
that any
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)

admitting a strict CKY
2
-form, such that its co-differential lies in the dual of the center, does not admit any other strict CKY
2
-form
\omega
such that the associated vector
\xi
is not in the center.
However, it is still an open problem to determine the existence of CKY
2
-forms on an arbitrary metric Lie algebra with
1
-dimensional center, which is not necessarily generated by
\xi
.
In particular, it would be interesting to study other metrics on those
5
-dimensional Lie algebras in Table
2
and determine if they admit a CKY
2
-form with
\xi\notin{\mathfrak{z}}
. In that case, the metric will not be isometric to the ones described in Table
2
.

We consider now a
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebra with
\dim{\mathfrak{z}}>1
admitting a strict CKY tensor, that is,
a metric Lie algebra classified in Theorem
5.4
, see Table
3
. Since they are isometrically isomorphic to
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
for some
r,s>0
we can work with the latter for simplicity.
Given
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, a strict CKY tensor
T
is completely determined by
r,s
according to (
21
).
Moreover, it follows from Remark
2.1
that any multiple of that
T
is a strict CKY tensor as well.
Thus, we can conclude that there is only one, up to scaling, strict CKY 2-form for each
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, in particular,
1\leq\dim\,\mathcal{CKY}({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)\leq

{7}\choose{3}
, according to
[
24
, Theorem 5.2]
. We show in the following proposition that these strict CKY 2-forms are never
*
-KY (compare with Proposition
6.1
).

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a
5
-dimensional metric Lie algebra with
\dim{\mathfrak{z}}>1
, then any strict CKY
2
-form
\omega
is never closed.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.3
, a metric Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
admitting a strict CKY
2
-form
\omega
is isomorphically isometric to
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
for some
r,s>0
with Lie brackets as (
20
), and
\omega
is given by (
21
).
Then, by a direct computation, we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\omega(x,z_{2},\xi)=\langle[\xi,x],Tz_{2}\rangle=-\frac{{6}r}{s},

and therefore
\omega
is never closed.
∎

Now, in order to determine the vector space
\mathcal{CKY}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, we investigate KY
2
-forms on the metric Lie algebras
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, or equivalently KY tensors on
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
. Note that the KY condition on a metric Lie algebra is given by the CKY condition (
4
) with
\theta=0
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be the metric Lie algebra with Lie brackets given by (
20
).
If
T
is a KY tensor on
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, then
T=0
. In particular,
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
does not admit non-zero parallel tensors.

Proof.

Let
T
be a KY tensor on
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
with associated
2
-form
given by

\omega=\theta\wedge(az^{1}+bz^{2}+cx^{*}+dy^{*})+z^{1}\wedge(ez^{2}+fx^{*}+gy^{*})+z^{2}\wedge(hx^{*}+iy^{*})+x^{*}\wedge y^{*},

where
\{\theta,z^{1},z^{2},x^{*},y^{*}\}
is the metric dual
of the orthonormal basis
\{\xi,z_{1},z_{2},x,y\}
.
Then
\omega
must be co-closed, which is equivalent to
c=j=d=0
.
On the other hand, from (
7
) (with
\theta=0
) we have that
\langle[Tz_{1},u],z_{2}\rangle=\langle[Tz_{2},u],z_{1}\rangle=0
and
\langle[Tz_{i},u],z_{i}\rangle=0
for
i=1,2
and any
u\in{\mathfrak{z}}^{\perp}
.
Then, using these equations with
u

w\in\{\xi,x,y\}
, we get that
a=b=f=g=h=i=0
.
Finally, applying (
4
) (with
\theta=0
) for
\{\xi,z_{2},x\}
we obtain
e=0
, and therefore
T=0
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

An interesting question would be to try to find another metric on the Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s}
such that it admits a non-zero KY tensor. Of course, this metric Lie algebra will not admit any strict CKY tensor.

As a direct consequence of Proposition
6.4
and Proposition
6.3
we have:

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a metric Lie algebra with Lie bracket as in (
20
). Then,
\mathcal{KY}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)=\mathcal{*KY}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)=0
and the space
\mathcal{CKY}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
has dimension one.

Remark 6.3
.

As a consequence of Theorem
6.5
, any CKY
2
-forms on
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
cannot be written as a linear combination of Killing forms and Hodge dual of Killing forms. Compare with
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
where CKY
2
-forms on
2
-step nilpotent Lie algebras are studied, and it is shown that the space of CKY
2
-forms coincides with the space of KY
2
-forms or with the space of
*
-KY
2
-forms.

It is known that the Hodge-star operator interchanges
*
-KY forms and KY forms (see
[
25
, Theorem 2]
).
Therefore, the next result follows from Theorem
6.5
.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a metric Lie algebra with Lie bracket by (
20
).
Then,
\mathcal{KY}^{3}({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)=\mathcal{*KY}^{3}({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)=0
and the space
\mathcal{CKY}^{3}({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
has dimension one.

Remark 6.4
.

In
[
11
]
, the authors give a characterization of KY
3
-forms on
2
-step nilpotent nilmanifolds.

Remark 6.5
.

Each metric Lie algebra in Table
3
is isometrically isomorphic to one of the metric Lie algebras
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
described above. Then it follows from Theorem
6.5
and Corollary
6.6
that the vector spaces
\mathcal{CKY}^{p}(L_{5,9},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r})
,
\mathcal{CKY}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathfrak{su}(2),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r,s})
and
\mathcal{CKY}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r,s})
have dimension one, for
p=2
and
3
. In the same way, the vector spaces
\mathcal{KY}^{p}(L_{5,9},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r})
,
\mathcal{KY}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathfrak{su}(2),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r,s})
,
\mathcal{KY}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r,s})
,
\mathcal{*KY}^{p}(L_{5,9},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r})
,
\mathcal{*KY}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathfrak{su}(2),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r,s})
and
\mathcal{*KY}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{r,s})
are trivial for
p=2
and
3
.

Remark 6.6
.

It can be shown that the Lie algebra of the holonomy group of all examples from Sections
3
and
5
is generic, i.e.
\mathfrak{hol}(G,g)\cong\mathfrak{so}(5)
and
\mathfrak{hol}(G_{r,s},g)\cong\mathfrak{so}(5)
, where
(G,g)
is the simply connected Lie group with left invariant metric associated to any metric Lie algebra in Table
1
and
(G_{r,s},g)
is the simply connected Lie group with left invariant metric associated to any metric Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}}_{r,s},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
. Then
(G,g)
and
(G_{r,s},g)
are irreducible as Riemannian manifolds.

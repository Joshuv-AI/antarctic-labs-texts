( n , σ ) − (n,\sigma)- equivalence relation between skew constacyclic codes
Hassan Ou-azzou, Mustapha Najmeddine, Nuh Aydin

Abstract
In this paper we generalize the notion of n n -equivalence relation introduced by Chen et al. in [ 12 ] to classify constacyclic codes of length n n over a finite field 𝔽 q , \mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}, where q = p r q=p^{r} is a prime power, to the case of skew constacyclic codes without derivation. We call this relation ( n , σ ) (n,\sigma) -equivalence relation, where n n is the length of the code and σ \sigma is an automorphism of the finite field. We compute the number of ( n , σ ) (n,\sigma) -equivalence classes, and we give conditions on λ \lambda and μ \mu for which ( σ , λ ) (\sigma,\lambda) -constacyclic codes and ( σ , λ ) (\sigma,\lambda) -constacyclic codes are equivalent with respect to our ( n , σ ) (n,\sigma) -equivalence relation. Under some conditions on n n and q q we prove that skew constacyclic codes are equivalent to cyclic codes. We also prove that when q q is even and σ \sigma is the Frobenius autmorphism, skew constacyclic codes of length n n are equivalent to cyclic codes when gcd ⁡ ( n , r ) = 1 \gcd(n,r)=1 . Finally we give some examples as applications of the theory developed here.

Introduction

Coding theory plays a fundamental role in various applications, such as error detection and correction, data transmission, data storage and reliable communication. It involves the study of efficient encoding and decoding methods for transmitting data reliably over noisy channels. Cyclic codes are one of the most important families of linear codes for both theoretical and practical reasons. They establish a key link between coding theory and algebra, and their structure makes them convenient for implementation. Many of the best-known codes are either cyclic or related to cyclic codes. Cyclic codes were introduced in the 1950s by Prange in
[
21
]
as linear codes with the property that the cyclic shift of any codeword is another codeword. One of the important generalizations of cyclic codes is the class of constacyclic codes, first introduced in
[
8
]
. Like cyclic codes, constacyclic codes also are also conveniently implemented using simple shift registers. Their rich algebraic structure makes them a major subject of study for theoretical reasons. There are useful generalizations of cyclic codes such as quasi-cyclic (QC) and quasi-twisted (QT) codes that have been used to obtain a large number of new linear codes with better parameters e.g.,
[
2
,
1
,
3
,
6
,
19
]
. One of the generalizations of cyclic codes is skew-cyclic codes, which were introduced in
[
9
]
. Their study necessitates the use of the Ore ring
R:=\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}[x,\sigma]
, where
\sigma
is an automorphism on
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
, and the multiplication is defined by the extension of the rule
x\cdot a=\sigma(a)x
for all
a\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
.

For a nonzero element
\lambda
of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
,
\lambda
-constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
are characterized as the ideals
\langle g(x)\rangle
of the quotient ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-\lambda\rangle
where the generator polynomial
g(x)
is the unique monic polynomial of minimum degree in the code, and it is a divisor of
x^{n}-\lambda
. Cyclic codes (
\lambda=1
) are a particular case of constacyclic codes. Although the quotient space
R/R\cdot\langle x^{n}-\lambda\rangle
is not always a ring, hence skew cyclic codes are not necessarily ideals, they can be viewed as left
R
-submodules
\langle g(x)\rangle
of the left
R
-module
R/R\cdot\langle x^{n}-\lambda\rangle
where
g(x)
is a right divisor of
x^{n}-\lambda
. Other characterizations of skew cyclic and skew constacyclic codes can be found in
[
16
,
6
,
9
,
10
]
.

In a previous work
[
12
]
, Chen et al. introduced an equivalence relation “
\sim_{n}
” called
n
-equivalence for the nonzero elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to classify constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that the constacyclic codes belonging to the same equivalence class have the same minimum distance. For
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}
,
\lambda\sim_{n}\mu
means the existence of a nonzero scalar
a\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}
such that the map
\psi_{a}
from the ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-\mu\rangle
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-\lambda\rangle
, defined by
\psi_{a}(f(x))=f(ax)
is an isometry with respect to the Hamming distance. Equivalently,
\lambda\sim_{n}\mu
if the polynomial
\lambda x^{n}-\mu
has at least one root in
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}[x].
So, it is easy to relate the generator polynomial of a
\lambda
-constacyclic code
C=\langle g(x)\rangle
with that of
\psi_{a}(C)=\langle g(ax)\rangle
.
Some papers of interest about the equivalence of constacyclic codes can be found in
[
4
,
5
,
11
,
13
,
15
]
.

In this paper we generalize the notion of
n
-equivalence relation introduced by Chen et al. in
[
12
]
to classify constacyclic codes of length
n
over finite field
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
to the case of skew constacyclic codes without derivation. We call this relation

-equivalence relation, where

\lambda

\mu

\sigma

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section
2

2
Skew
(\sigma,\lambda)-
constacyclic codes

Let
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
be a finite field with
q
elements, where
q=p^{r}
for some prime number
p
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, and let
\sigma:\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
be an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
. Recall that the
Frobenius automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
is defined as the automorphism
\theta:\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
that maps
a
to
a^{p}
and generates the Galois group
\aut(\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}).
Then each automorphism
\sigma\in\aut(\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}})
can be expressed as a power of
\theta
, which means there exists an integer
s
,
0\leq s<r,
such that
\sigma(a)=a^{p^{s}}
for all
a\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.
It is important to note also that
\theta
keeps
\mathbb{F}_{p}
fixed i.e.,
\theta(\mathbb{F}_{p})=\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and generates the cyclic group of automorphisms of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
.

Definition 1
(Skew
(\sigma,\lambda)
-constacyclic code).

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}
and
\sigma\in\aut(\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}})
be an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.
A linear code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{n}
is called a skew
(\sigma,\lambda)
-constacyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
if for each codeword
(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})
of
C,
we have

\left(\lambda\sigma(c_{n-1}),\sigma(c_{0}),\sigma(c_{1}),\ldots,\sigma(c_{n-2})\right)\in C.

For
\lambda=\pm 1
we obtain skew
\sigma
-cyclic codes and
\sigma
-negacyclic codes as special cases.

Let us recall that a map
T:\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{{}^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{{}^{n}}
is called
\sigma
-semi linear transformation if it is an additive map satisfying
T(\alpha u)=\sigma(\alpha)T(u)
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
and
u\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{n}.
In the particular case
\sigma=\id,\ T
is a linear transformation. We can obtain some important classes of codes from semi linear transformations.

Remark 1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}
and
\sigma\in\aut(\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}})
be an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.

1.

Skew
(\sigma,\lambda)
-constacyclic codes are invariant under the
\sigma-
semi linear transformation
T_{\sigma,\lambda},
called the
(\sigma,\lambda)-
constacyclic shift, defined by:

T_{\sigma,\lambda}(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1})=\left(\lambda\sigma(v_{n-1}),\sigma(v_{0}),\sigma(v_{1}),\ldots,\sigma(v_{n-2})\right),\text{for all }\ v=(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{n}.

2.

\lambda
-constacyclic codes are invariant under the linear transformation
T_{\lambda}
defined by:

T_{\lambda}(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1})=\left(\lambda v_{n-1},v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-2}\right),\text{for all }\ v=(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{n}.

3.

When
\sigma=\id,
we get
T_{\lambda}=T_{\sigma,\id}.

To investigate the structure of skew
(\sigma,\lambda)-
constacyclic codes we need to recall the notion of skew polynomials.
A
\sigma
-skew polynomial ring over
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
is the set

\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}[x,\sigma]=\left\{a_{0}+a_{1}x+\ldots+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}:a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\right\}

endowed with the usual polynomial addition and the multiplication is defined by

xa=\sigma(a)x.

In the following proposition, we collect some properties of the skew polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}[x,\sigma]
.

Proposition 1
(
[
20
]
, p. 483-486).

Let
s
be the order of the automorphism
\sigma,
and denote by
\Fix_{\sigma}(\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}})
the subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
fixed by
\sigma
.

1.

The ring
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}[x,\sigma]
is, in general, a non-commutative ring unless
\sigma
is the identity automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.

2.

An element
f\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}[x,\sigma]
is central if and only if
f
belongs to
\Fix_{\sigma}(\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}})[x^{s}]
i.e., the centre of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}[x,\sigma]
is
\Z(R)=\Fix_{\sigma}(\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}})[x^{s}].

3.

Two-sided ideals of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}[x,\sigma]
are generated by elements of the form
h(x^{s})x^{m}
, where
m
is an integer and
h(x)\in\Fix_{\sigma}(\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}})[x].

4.

The ring
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}[x,\sigma]
is a right (resp. left) Euclidean domain.

It is important to note that in the polynomial ring
R
, the evaluation of skew polynomials is different from the evaluation of polynomials in the usual polynomial rings over commutative rings.

Definition 2
(
[

).

For

and

,
the right evaluation of

at

f(a):=r
such that

In
[

, Lam and Leroy proved that, for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
we have

f(\alpha)=a_{0}+a_{1}N_{1}(\alpha)+\ldots+a_{n-1}N_{n-1}(\alpha)=\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{n}}a_{i}N_{i}(\alpha)

(1)

where
N_{0}(\alpha)=1,
and for each
i
in
\mathbb{N}^{*}

N_{i}(\alpha)=\sigma^{i-1}(\alpha)\ldots\sigma(\alpha)\alpha.

By taking
\sigma(a)=a^{p^{s}}
for all

we obtain for each

N_{i}(\alpha)=\alpha^{p^{s(i-1)}}\alpha^{p^{s(i-2)}}\ldots\alpha^{p^{s}}\alpha=\alpha^{\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}p^{sj}}=\alpha^{{\frac{p^{si}-1}{p^{s}-1}}}.

(2)

Set
[i]_{s}=\dfrac{p^{si}-1}{p^{s}-1}
. Then the right evaluation of

f(\alpha)=\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{n}}a_{i}\alpha^{[i]_{s}}.

(3)

Now we recall the following characterization of skew
(\sigma,\lambda)-
constacyclic codes.

Theorem 1
( Theorem 3, Theorem 4 in

A linear code

Moreover,

1.

There is a monic polynomial of least degree

2.

The set

3.

A generator matrix

(4)

where

4.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between right divisors of

and skew
(\sigma,\lambda)-
constacyclic codes of length

\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}

Definition 3
( Section 1.6, and 1.7 in

Let

Remark 2
.

Two equivalent codes have the same minimum Hamming distance and in general same parameters.

Theorem 2

Let

Let

Since

\displaystyle=x^{1+\alpha_{2}n}(c_{0}+c_{1}x+\ldots+c_{n-1}x^{n-1})

3
Equivalence between skew constacyclic codes

In
[
12
]
Chen et al. introduced the notion of
n
-equivalence relation on
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}
for the purpose of classifying ordinary constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.
In this section we generalize this notion to the case of skew constacyclic codes by defining an equivalence relation on
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}
called
(n,\sigma)
-equivalence relation, where
\sigma\in\aut(\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}})
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.

Definition 4
(
(\sigma,n)-
equivalence relation).

Let
n
be a positive integer. We say that
\lambda
and
\mu
are
(n,\sigma)
-equivalent in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, denoted by
\lambda\sim_{n,\sigma}\mu
, if the skew polynomial
\lambda x^{n}-\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}[x,\sigma]
has at least one root in
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}
, i.e., there is
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}
such that
\lambda N_{n}(\alpha)=\mu.

Proposition 2
.

The relation
\sim_{n,\sigma}
is an equivalence relation on
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}
.

Proof
.

1.

\lambda\sim_{n,\sigma}\lambda
since
1
is a root of
\lambda x^{n}-\lambda
in
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
.

2.

\lambda\sim_{n,\sigma}\mu\iff
there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
such that
\alpha
is a root of the polynomial
f(x)=\lambda x^{n}-\mu
. By equation (1), this is equivalent to

\lambda N_{n}(\alpha)=\mu\iff\lambda=\mu N_{n}(\alpha^{-1})\iff\mu\sim_{n,\sigma}\lambda.

3.

If
\lambda\sim_{n,\sigma}\mu
and
\mu\sim_{n,\sigma}\xi
then there are
\alpha,\beta
in
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}
such that

\lambda N_{n}(\alpha)=\mu\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mu N_{n}(\beta)=\xi

Therefore

\lambda N_{n}(\alpha)=N_{n}(\beta^{-1})\xi

and so

\lambda N_{n}(\alpha)N_{n}(\beta)=\lambda N_{n}(\alpha\beta)=\xi.

Hence
\lambda\sim_{n,\sigma}\xi.

The following theorem gives characterizations of
(n,\sigma)
-equivalence between two elements
\lambda
and
\mu
of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\sigma\in\aut(\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}})
be an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
such that
\sigma(a)=a^{p^{s}}
for all
a\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
, and let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. The following four statements are equivalent:

1.

\lambda\sim_{n,\sigma}\mu

2.

There is an element
\alpha
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
such that
\lambda N_{n}(\alpha)=\mu
and the following map

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\varphi_{\alpha}:&\mathbb{F}_{q}[x,\sigma]/\langle x^{n}-\mu\rangle&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{F}_{q}[x,\sigma]/\langle x^{n}-\lambda\rangle,\\
&&&\\
&f(x)&\longmapsto&f(\alpha x)\end{array}

is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra isometry with respect to the Hamming distance.

3.

\lambda^{-1}\mu\in\langle N_{n}(\xi)\rangle
, where
\xi
is a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.

4.

\lambda^{-1}\mu\in\langle\xi^{[n]_{s}}\rangle
, where
\xi
is a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.

5.

\left(\lambda^{-1}\mu\right)^{d}=1
, where
d=\dfrac{q-1}{\operatorname{gcd}([n]_{s},q-1)}
.

In particular, the number of
(n,\sigma)
-equivalence classes in

\operatorname{gcd}([n]_{s},q-1)

Proof
.

1.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2).
Suppose that
\lambda\sim_{n,\sigma}\mu
then there is
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
such that
\lambda N_{n}(\alpha)=\mu
. We will prove that
\varphi_{\alpha}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
-algebra isometry. Let us first show that
\varphi_{\alpha}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
-algebra isomorphism. Let
f(x)=\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}}a_{i}x^{i}
and
g(x)=\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}}b_{i}x^{i}
in
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}[x,\sigma]/\langle x^{n}-\mu\rangle.
On the one hand we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}f(x)g(x)&=\displaystyle{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}}\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{j}}a_{i}\sigma^{i}\left(b_{j-i}\right)x^{j}+\displaystyle{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}}\displaystyle{\sum_{i=j+1}^{n-1}}a_{i}\sigma^{i}\left(b_{n-i+j}\right)x^{j+n}\\
&=\displaystyle{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}}\left(\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{j}}a_{i}\sigma^{i}\left(b_{j-i}\right)+\displaystyle{\sum_{i=j+1}^{n-1}}a_{i}\sigma^{i}(b_{n-i+j})\sigma^{j}(\mu)\right)x^{j},\\
&(\text{ since $x^{j+n}=\sigma^{j}(\mu)x^{j}(\mod x^{n}-\mu).$ })\par\\
\end{array}

As
\varphi_{\alpha}\left(x^{j}\right)=N_{j}(\alpha)x^{j}
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{rl}\varphi_{\alpha}(f(x)g(x))&=\displaystyle{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}}\left(\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{j}}a_{i}\sigma^{i}\left(b_{j-i}\right)+\displaystyle{\sum_{i=j+1}^{n-1}}a_{i}\sigma^{i}(b_{n-i+j})\sigma^{j}(\mu)\right)N_{j}(\alpha)x^{j}\\
&\\
\end{array}

On the other hand we have:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\varphi_{\alpha}(f(x))\varphi_{\alpha}(g(x))=&\displaystyle{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}}\left(\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{j}}a_{i}N_{i}(\alpha)\sigma^{i}\left(b_{j-i}N_{j-i}(\alpha)\right)\right)x^{j}+\\
&\displaystyle{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}}\left(\displaystyle{\sum_{i=j+1}^{n-1}}a_{i}N_{i}(\alpha)\sigma^{i}\left(b_{n-i+j}N_{n+j-i}(\alpha)\right)\right)x^{j+n}.\\
\end{array}

As
x^{j+n}=\sigma^{j}(\lambda)x^{j}(\mod x^{n}-\lambda)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}\varphi_{\alpha}(f(x))\varphi_{\alpha}(g(x))&=\displaystyle{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}}\left(\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{j}}a_{i}\sigma^{i}\left(b_{j-i}\right)N_{i}(\alpha)\sigma^{i}\left(N_{j-i}(\alpha)\right)\right)x^{j}+\\
&\displaystyle{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}}\left(\displaystyle{\sum_{i=j+1}^{n-1}}a_{i}\sigma^{i}\left(b_{n-i+j}\right)N_{i}(\alpha)\sigma^{i}\left(N_{n+j-i}(\alpha)\right)\sigma^{j}(\lambda)\right)x^{j}.\\
\end{array}

According to
[
14
, Proposition 2.1]
we have
N_{i}(\alpha)\sigma^{i}\left(N_{j-i}(\alpha)\right)=N_{j}(\alpha),
and so

N_{i}(\alpha)\sigma^{i}\left(N_{n+j-i}(\alpha)\right)\sigma^{j}(\lambda)=N_{j+n}(\alpha)\frac{\sigma^{j}(\mu)}{\left(\sigma^{j}\left(N_{n}(\alpha)\right)\right)}=\sigma^{j}(\mu)N_{j}(\alpha),\ \text{since }\lambda N_{n}(\alpha)=\mu.

It follows that

\begin{array}[]{rl}\varphi_{\alpha}(f(x))\varphi_{\alpha}(g(x))&=\displaystyle{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}}\left(\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{j}}a_{i}\sigma^{i}\left(b_{j-i}\right)+\displaystyle{\sum_{i=j+1}^{n-1}}a_{i}\sigma^{i}\left(b_{n-i+j}\right)\sigma^{j}(\mu)\right)N_{j}(\alpha)x^{j}\\
&=\varphi_{\alpha}(f(x)g(x)),\end{array}

which means that
\varphi_{\alpha}
is a morphism of an
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
-algebra.

Finally we observe that for any
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x,\sigma]/\langle x^{n}-\mu\rangle
,
f(x)
and
f(\alpha x)
have the same weight. Therefore,
\varphi_{\alpha}
preserves the Hamming weight and so it is an
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
-algebra isometry with respect to Hamming distance.

2.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3). Suppose there exists an
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
such that the map

\varphi:\mathbb{F}_{q}[x,\sigma]/\langle x^{n}-\mu\rangle\to\mathbb{F}_{q}[x,\sigma]/\langle x^{n}-\lambda\rangle,\ \ f(x)\mapsto f(\alpha x)

is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra isometry with respect to the Hamming distance. From the fact that
\mu=\varphi_{\alpha}(\mu)
and
x^{n}=\lambda\mod(x^{n}-\lambda)
we obtain

\mu=\varphi_{\alpha}(\mu)=\varphi_{\alpha}\left(x^{n}\right)=\varphi_{\alpha}(x)^{n}=(\alpha x)^{n}=N_{n}(\alpha)x^{n}=N_{n}(\alpha)\lambda.

This gives
\lambda^{-1}\mu=N_{n}(\alpha).
Let
\xi
be a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
then
\alpha=\xi^{i}
for some
0\leq i\leq q-1.
It follows that

\lambda^{-1}\mu=N_{n}(\xi^{i})=N_{n}(\xi)^{i},\ \text{ which means that }\ \lambda^{-1}\mu\in\langle N_{n}(\xi)\rangle.

3.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(4). Since
N_{n}(\xi)=\xi^{[n]_{s}}
we have
\langle\xi^{[n]_{s}}\rangle=\langle N_{n}(\xi)\rangle
and so
\lambda^{-1}\mu\in\langle\xi^{[n]_{s}}\rangle.

4.

(4)
\Rightarrow
(5). Since
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=\langle\xi\rangle
is a cyclic group,
\langle\xi^{[n]_{s}}\rangle
is the unique subgroup of order
d=\dfrac{q-1}{\operatorname{gcd}([n]_{s},q-1)}
and any subgroup with order dividing
d
is contained in the subgroup
\langle\xi^{[n]_{s}}\rangle
. It follows that
(\lambda\mu^{-1})^{d}=1.

5.

(5)
\Rightarrow
(1). Let
\lambda
and
\mu
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
such that
(\lambda\mu^{-1})^{d}=1.
That means
\lambda\mu^{-1}
belongs to the cyclic subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}
of order
d.
However, the unique subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}
of order
d
is
\langle\xi^{[n]_{s}}\rangle
.
It follows that
\lambda^{-1}\mu=\xi^{i[n]_{s}}\in\langle\xi^{[n]_{s}}\rangle,
for some integer
0\leq i\leq q-1.
Let
\alpha=\xi^{i},
then
\lambda^{-1}\mu=\alpha^{[n]_{s}}=N_{n}(\alpha),
and so
\lambda N_{n}(\alpha)=\mu
which means that
\lambda\sim_{n,\sigma}\mu.

Finally, by the equivalence of (1) and (5), the number of
(n,\sigma)
-equivalence classes in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
is equal to the index of
\langle\xi^{[n]_{s}}\rangle
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, which is given by

\left|\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\ :\ \langle\xi^{[n]_{s}}\rangle\right|=\frac{q-1}{\left|\langle\xi^{[n]_{s}}\rangle\right|}=\dfrac{q-1}{\frac{q-1}{\operatorname{gcd}([n]_{s},q-1)}}=\operatorname{gcd}([n]_{s},q-1).

\sigma(a)=a^{2}

In the following theorem we investigate equivalence among
(\sigma,\delta)

Theorem 5
.

Let
q=2^{r}
and
\sigma\in\aut(\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}})
be the Frobenuis automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.
Let
C
be a
(\sigma,\lambda)
-constacyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.

The number of
(n,\sigma)
-equivalence classes is equal
2^{\gcd(n,r)}-1
, and

1.

If
\gcd(n,r)=1,
then
C
is equivalent to a cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.

2.

If
\gcd(n,r)=d\neq 1
, then there is a unique
0\leq j\leq 2^{d}-2
such that
C
is equivalent to a skew
(\sigma,\xi^{j})
-constacyclic code, where
\xi
is a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.

Proof
.

We have
\gcd([n]_{1},q-1)=\gcd(2^{n}-1,2^{r}-1)=2^{\gcd(n,r)}-1.
Then according to Theorem
4
, the number of
(n,\sigma)
-equivalence classes is
2^{\gcd(n,r)}-1.

1.

If
n=1\mod r
, then
\gcd([n]_{1},q-1)=1
and so we have only one equivalence class which means that all
(\sigma,\lambda)
-constacylic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
are equivalent to skew cyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.
On the other hand the order of
\sigma
is
r
and
\gcd(n,2k)=1,
then by Theorem
2
,
C
is equivalent to a cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.

2.

If
\gcd(n,r)=d\neq 1,
and
\gcd([n]_{1},q-1)=2^{d}-1,

we can decompose
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}
into a disjoint union of cosets of the subgroup
\langle\xi^{2^{d}-1}\rangle
as follows:

\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}=\langle\xi\rangle=\langle N_{n}(\xi)\rangle\cup\xi\langle N_{n}(\xi)\rangle\cup\ldots\cup\xi^{2^{d}-2}\langle N_{n}(\xi)\rangle.

So the element
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}^{*}
belongs to exactly one of the cosets, i.e., there is a unique integer
0\leq j\leq 2^{d}-2,
such that
\lambda\in\xi^{j}\langle N_{n}(\xi)\rangle
and so
\lambda
is
(\sigma,n)
-equivalent to
\xi^{j}
.

When
q=2^{r}
and
r
is an even number we have the following result.

Corollary 4
.

Let
q=2^{2k}

\sigma

-constacyclic codes of odd length

.

Let
C
be a
(\sigma,\lambda)

n
over
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.
On the one hand we have
\gcd([n]_{1},2k)=2^{\gcd(n,2k)}-1=2^{1}-1=1,
hence

is equivalent to a skew cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.
On the other hand the order of
\sigma
is
2k
and
\gcd(n,2k)=1
. Then by Theorem
2
,
C
is equivalent to a cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}.

All skew

Example 1

Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced the
(n,\sigma)
-equivalence relation, where
n
is the length of the code and
\sigma
is an automorphism of the finite field. We computed the number of
(n,\sigma)
-equivalence classes, and we give conditions on
\lambda
and
\mu
for which
(\sigma,\lambda)
-constacyclic codes and
(\sigma,\lambda)
-constacyclic codes to be equivalent using the properties of our
(n,\sigma)
-equivalent relation. Under some conditions on
n
and
q
, we proved that skew constacyclic codes are equivalent to cyclic codes. For future work, it would be interesting to introduce the concept of “skew cyclotomic cosets” to facilitate the factorization of
x^{n}-1
and
x^{n}-a
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x,\sigma].
This may give an efficient way to classify skew constacyclic codes of length
n
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{{}_{q}}
.

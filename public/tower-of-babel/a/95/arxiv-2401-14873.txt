Abstract
. Motivated by issues in the
context of asymptotically flat spacetimes at null infinity, we discuss in
the simplest example of a free massless scalar field in two dimensions
several subtleties that arise when setting up the canonical formulation on a
single or on two intersecting null hyperplanes with a special emphasis on
the infinite-dimensional global and conformal symmetries and their canonical
generators, the free data, a consistent treatment of zero modes, matching
conditions, and implications for quantization of massless versus massive
fields.

Introduction

When studying asymptotically flat spacetimes at future null infinity, outgoing
radiation is readily accessible whereas incoming radiation is somewhat hidden.
In the Bondi-Sachs approach

[

pages 520-523 for a discussion of incoming and outgoing waves in this context).
This asymmetry is due to the fact that the standard coordinate systems used in
both of these approaches involve a null retarded time coordinate
u
and a
radial coordinate
r
that controls the asymptotics, the area-radius in the
former and the affine parameter for null generators in the latter. In order to
achieve an understanding of scattering in this framework, the analysis is
repeated by using a null advanced time coordinate

incoming and outgoing radiation, with important antipodal matching conditions

[
13

. Rather than discovering the effects of incoming,
respectively outgoing, radiation layer by layer in an asymptotic expansion, it
seems more efficient to cut through the layers and use double-null light-cone
coordinates to set up the problem (see e.g.
[
14
]
, section 11.1 for
an introduction).

Since asymptotically flat general relativity is to a large extent controlled by
the linearized theory, we will at this stage not attempt directly to identify
incoming and outgoing gravitons in full general relativity (see
e.g.
[
15
]
), but instead point out subtleties that arise when
setting up the canonical formalism for free massless fields on a null
hypersurface. Whereas in the instant form of dynamics, first class constraints
generate gauge symmetries under standard assumptions, this is not necessarily
the case in the front form: the first class constraints associated to the
definition of the canonical momenta generate infinite-dimensional global
symmetries of the type that have attracted considerable attention in recent
investigations. This is ultimately due to the fact that the initial data that
one fixes on a null hyperplane do not determine the wave that moves along the
direction of this hyperplane (see e.g.
[
16
]
, section 6, paragraph
2).

In the present note, we explicitly study these subtleties in all detail in the
well-understood and completely tractable example of a free massless scalar in

[

ingredient when considering spin 0,1 and 2 fields on a flat four dimensional
Minkowski background, where the intersection of null hyperplanes is
two-dimensional Euclidean space rather than a point. Our aim is thus not to
learn something new about the free massless boson but rather about the canonical
formalism on null hypersurfaces.

We start by studying the canonical formulation on a single light front
(hereafter simply ‘front’), i.e., a null line where initial data is provided.
Whereas in the instant form of dynamics, a free massless scalar field is
equivalent to a left and a right moving chiral boson, together with a
(space-independent) zero mode (see e.g.

[

chiral boson, together with a zero mode sector. As shown in

[
30

, this sector is associated with a first class
constraint that appears in connection with the well-known second class
constraint of the light-front analysis. This sector contains a Lagrange
multiplier with an arbitrary time dependence that encodes the data on the other
front, in-line with a characteristic initial value problem. Even though this
sector has the same form than that of a canonical pair that is pure gauge, the
main point is that it has to be interpreted differently because it contains the
wave moving along the front. What is missing in this approach is a Poisson
bracket structure in this sector.

Once it becomes clear that half of the physical degrees of freedom of the
problem, e.g., the left movers if choosing
x^{+}
as evolution time, are encoded
in a zero mode, it is convenient to set up the problem on a finite

interval in order to cleanly separate the zero from the other modes, before
taking the large interval limit if needed. This procedure of putting the system
in a null box requires suitable boundary conditions. The case of periodic or
anti-periodic ones is referred to as discrete light cone quantization

to use the Peierls bracket, or its quantum version, the Pauli-Jordan commutation
function. It allows one to directly work on the level of the general solution to
the equations of motion, which is explicitly known. This is not a necessity
however. If one chooses to work with standard equal time Poisson / Dirac

In order to get correct interpretations in both formulations and ensure

As a consistency check, we provide in double front form, the mode expansion, the

reproduce this specific observable in this approach because there is a tension:
while solutions and symmetries simplify in light-cone coordinates, the boundary
conditions are not well adapted, and are natural in instant form. Only a
detailed understanding of all sectors, i.e., left and right movers, together
with particle zero modes and their matching conditions, yields correct results.

provide the well-known partition function that we are reproducing in the main

non-trivial mixing through conformal transformations of the left and right

In a section devoted to the massive case, we quickly review the well-known

2.2
Symmetries and Noether currents

Our convention for global symmetries and Noether currents are as follows. If

\delta_{Q}\phi^{i}=Q^{i}
is a symmetry of the action,
\delta_{Q}\mathcal{L}=\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}_{Q}
for some
k^{\mu}_{Q}
, the Noether current for a
Lagrangian that depends at most on first order derivatives of the fields is

j^{\mu}_{Q}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}\phi^{i}}Q^{i}-k^{\mu}_{Q}

and satisfies

Q^{i}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\phi^{i}}+\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{Q}=0.

(2.8)

The correspondence between global symmetries and Noether currents is unique only
when defining equivalence classes (see e.g.
[
51
,
52
]

for details). In the current context where there are no non-trivial gauge
symmetries, global symmetries of the form

Q^{i}_{T}=M^{[ij]}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\phi^{j}}+M^{i,j\mu}\partial_{\mu}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\phi^{j}}+\partial_{\mu}\big[M^{j,i\mu}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\phi^{j}}\big]-\partial_{\mu}[M^{[i\mu,j\nu]}\partial_{\nu}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\phi^{j}}\big],

(2.9)

where
M^{[ij]}=-M^{[ji]}
,
M^{[i\mu,j\nu]}=-M^{[j\nu,i\mu]}
with associated
Noether currents

j^{\mu}_{Q_{T}}=-\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\phi^{i}}M^{j,i\mu}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\phi^{j}}+\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\phi^{i}}M^{[i\mu,j\nu]}\partial_{\nu}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\phi^{j}},

(2.10)

are trivial. More generally, on the level of currents, weakly vanishing currents
and currents that are divergences of superpotentials,
j^{\mu}=\partial_{\nu}k^{[\mu\nu]}
, are
trivial. As we will see below, additional considerations may require specific
“improved” representatives.

If
\epsilon^{+}=\epsilon^{+}(x^{+})
,
\epsilon^{-}=\epsilon^{-}(x^{-})
, the chiral shift transformations

\delta_{\epsilon^{\pm}}\phi=\epsilon^{\pm},

(2.11)

map solutions to solutions. Furthermore, they are global symmetries of the
action since

\delta_{\epsilon^{+}}\mathcal{L}=\partial_{-}(\phi\partial_{+}\epsilon^{+}),\quad\delta_{\epsilon^{-}}\mathcal{L}=\partial_{+}(\phi\partial_{-}\epsilon^{-}),

(2.12)

and the associated Noether currents are

j^{+}_{L,\epsilon^{+}}=\partial_{-}\phi\epsilon^{+},\ j^{-}_{L,\epsilon^{+}}=\partial_{+}\phi\epsilon^{+}-\phi\partial_{+}\epsilon^{+},\ j^{+}_{L,\epsilon^{-}}=\partial_{-}\phi\epsilon^{-}-\phi\partial_{-}\epsilon^{-},\ j^{-}_{L,\epsilon^{-}}=\partial_{+}\phi\epsilon^{-}.

(2.13)

In particular, the chiral shifts contain the constant shift
\delta_{c}\phi=c
with Noether
current
j^{\mu}_{c}=\partial^{\mu}\phi c
.

Furthermore, the Noether currents associated to conformal symmetries,

\delta_{\xi}\phi=\xi^{\rho}\partial_{\rho}\phi,\quad\xi^{+}=\xi^{+}(x^{+}),\ \xi^{-}=\xi^{-}(x^{-}),

(2.14)

are given in terms of the energy momentum tensor

T_{\pm\pm}=(\partial_{\pm}\phi)^{2},\quad T_{\pm\mp}=0,

(2.15)

as

j^{+}_{L,\xi}=\xi^{-}T_{--},\quad j^{-}_{L,\xi}=\xi^{+}T_{++}.

(2.16)

3.1
Dynamics

We start by following the discussion in
[
23
,
30
]
.
We will mark with a
+
all quantities to keep track of our choice of evolution
time. This is not necessary in this section and makes the notation cumbersome,
but it will pay off in the next sections when we will consider both fronts at
once. When taking
x^{+}
as the direction of evolution, the definition of the
canonical momentum
\pi^{+}
on the
x^{+}=c^{+}
front leads to the primary
constraints

g^{+}=\pi^{+}-\partial_{-}\phi\approx 0,

(3.1)

which hold at each point
x^{-}
. The canonical Hamiltonian,

G^{+}[\lambda^{+}]=\int dx^{-}g^{+}\lambda^{+},

(3.2)

vanishes on the primary constraint surface.
The first order constrained action that is equivalent to the
Lagrangian one by elimination of the auxiliary fields given by the canonical
momentum
\pi^{+}(x^{+},x^{-})
and the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda^{+}(x^{+},x^{-})
is
then

S_{H}[\phi,\pi^{+},\lambda^{+}]=\int dx^{+}\int dx^{-}\ \mathcal{L}_{H}^{+},\quad\mathcal{L}_{H}^{+}=\pi^{+}\partial_{+}\phi-\lambda^{+}(\pi^{+}-\partial_{-}\phi).

(3.3)

By construction, the solution of the associated Euler-Lagrange
equations of motion

\pi^{+}=\partial_{-}\phi,\quad\lambda^{+}=\partial_{+}\phi,\quad\partial_{+}\pi^{+}+\partial_{-}\lambda^{+}=0,

(3.4)

give not only (
2.5
), but fix also the auxiliary fields in terms of this
solution,

\pi^{+}=\partial_{-}\phi^{S}_{-},\quad\lambda^{+}=\partial_{+}\phi^{S}_{+}.

(3.5)

Let us now see how these equations appear in terms of the canonical structure.
The non-vanishing equal
x^{+}
time Poisson bracket associated to the first order
action (
3.3
) is

\{\phi(x^{+},x^{-}),\pi^{+}(x^{+},y^{-})\}_{+}=\delta(x^{-},y^{-}).

(3.6)

The Poisson brackets of the constraints smeared by Lagrange
multipliers,
are given by

\{G^{+}[\lambda^{+1}],G^{+}[\lambda^{+2}]\}_{+}=\int dx^{-}(\lambda^{+2}\partial_{-}\lambda^{+1}-\lambda^{+1}\partial_{-}\lambda^{+2}).

(3.7)

When following Dirac’s algorithm, there are no secondary constraints. Instead
there are restrictions on the Lagrange multipliers from the requirement that

\{g^{+}(x^{+},x^{-}),\int dy^{-}\lambda^{+}(x^{+},y^{-})g^{+}(x^{+},y^{-})\}_{+}=-2\partial_{-}\lambda^{+}\approx 0\Longrightarrow\partial_{-}\lambda^{+}=0,

(3.8)

so that
\lambda^{+}=\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}(x^{+})
. This implies that the
x^{-}
zero modes of the local
constraint
g^{+}
,

\bar{g}^{+}_{+}=\int dy^{-}g^{+},

(3.9)

is first class, and so is

G^{+}[\bar{\lambda}^{+}]=\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}\bar{g}^{+}_{+}.

(3.10)

By construction, the Hamiltonian equations of motion that are equivalent to the
Lagrangian ones are the constraints (
3.1
) together
with the evolution equations

\partial_{+}\phi=\{\phi,G^{+}[\bar{\lambda}^{+}]\}_{+}=\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+},\quad\partial_{+}\pi^{+}=\{\pi^{+},G^{+}[\bar{\lambda}^{+}]\}_{+}=0.

(3.11)

In terms of
\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}(x^{+})
, the general solution of the evolution equation in

x^{+}
is

\phi(x^{+},x^{-})=\int^{x^{+}}_{c^{+}}dy^{+}\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}(y^{+})+\phi(c^{+},x^{-}).

(3.12)

When using the last of (
3.11
), the constraints (
3.1
) are solved in
terms of
\pi^{+}(x^{-})
as

\phi(x^{+},x^{-})=\int^{x^{-}}_{c^{-}}dy^{-}\pi^{+}(y^{-})+\phi(x^{+},c^{-}).

(3.13)

Matching at either
c^{+}
or
c^{-}
then gives

\phi(x^{+},x^{-})=\int^{x^{+}}_{c^{+}}dy^{+}\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}(y^{+})+\int^{x^{-}}_{c^{-}}dy^{-}\pi^{+}(y^{-})+\phi(c^{+},c^{-}).

(3.14)

The general solution is thus determined by the free data

\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}(x^{+})
at
x^{-}=c^{-}
,
\pi^{+}(x^{-})
at
x^{+}=c^{+}
and a constant at the
intersection.

Two lessons can be drawn from this analysis:

•

The Dirac algorithm on a null hyperplane provides the correct free data
for the characteristic initial value problem.

•

Contrary to what happens in the instant form where the Lagrange multipliers for
first class constraints are fixed on-shell in terms of pure gauge degrees of
freedom, they now encode information on the physical degree of freedom that
cannot be seen on the front. These constraints have thus to be treated differently
than first class constraints in instant form.

3.3
Zero mode and chiral boson sectors

The
x^{-}
zero mode of the Lagrange multiplier is defined as

\bar{\lambda}_{+}^{+}(x^{+})=\frac{1}{L_{-}}\int^{{L_{-}}/{2}}_{-{L_{-}}/{2}}dx^{-}\lambda^{+}(x^{+},x^{-}),

(3.20)

so that

\lambda^{+}=\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}+\tilde{\lambda}_{+}^{+},\quad\int^{{L_{-}}/{2}}_{-{L_{-}}/{2}}dx^{-}\tilde{\lambda}_{+}^{+}=0.

(3.21)

The canonical variables may be decomposed in the same way as the Lagrange multipliers,

\begin{split}&\phi(x^{+},x^{-})=\bar{\phi}_{+}(x^{+})+\tilde{\phi}_{+}(x^{+},x^{-}),\quad\pi^{+}(x^{+},x^{-})=\frac{1}{L_{-}}\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}(x^{+})+\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}(x^{+},x^{-}),\\
&\bar{\phi}_{+}=\frac{1}{L_{-}}\int^{{L_{-}}/{2}}_{-{L_{-}}/{2}}dx^{-}\phi,\ \bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}=\int^{{L_{-}}/{2}}_{-{L_{-}}/{2}}dx^{-}\pi^{+},\ \int^{{L_{-}}/{2}}_{-{L_{-}}/{2}}dx^{-}\tilde{\phi}_{+}=0=\int^{{L_{-}}/{2}}_{-{L_{-}}/{2}}dx^{-}\,\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}.\end{split}

(3.22)

Note that the non-periodicity goes into the field without the
x^{-}
zero mode,

\tilde{\phi}_{+}=\Delta_{+}\frac{x^{-}}{L_{-}}+\tilde{\varphi}_{+}.

(3.23)

\begin{split}&\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}=\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}(0)=\Delta_{+},\quad\tilde{\phi}_{+}=\phi^{R}(x^{-}),\quad\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+}=0,\\

Decomposing in turn the

zero mode into a constant part and a fluctuating
field,

\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}=\frac{1}{L_{+}}\int^{L_{+}/2}_{-L_{+}/2}dy^{+}\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}(y^{+})+\tilde{\bar{\lambda}}^{+}_{+}
,
one finds

\bar{\phi}_{+}=\bar{\bar{\phi}}_{+}+\int^{L_{+}/2}_{-L_{+}/2}dy^{+}\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}(y^{+})\frac{x^{+}}{L_{+}}+\partial_{+}^{-1}\tilde{\bar{\lambda}}^{+}_{+},

(3.26)

where
\bar{\bar{\phi}}_{+}
is a constant and
\partial_{+}^{-1}\tilde{\bar{\lambda}}^{+}_{+}
is the primitive
of
\tilde{\bar{\lambda}}^{+}_{+}
that does not contain a constant,

\partial_{+}^{-1}\tilde{\bar{\lambda}}^{+}_{+}(x^{+})=\int^{x^{+}}_{0}dy^{+}\tilde{\bar{\lambda}}^{+}_{+}(y^{+})-\frac{1}{L_{+}}\int^{L_{+}/2}_{-L_{+}/2}dy^{+}\int^{y^{+}}_{0}dy^{+}\tilde{\bar{\lambda}}^{+}_{+}(z^{+})=\phi^{L}(x^{+}).

(3.27)

Finally, in order for the solution to have the correct entangled periodicity,
one needs the “matching” condition

\Delta_{+}=\int^{L_{+}/2}_{-L_{+}/2}dy^{+}\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}(y^{+}),

(3.28)

and, provided that the integration constants are identified,

\Delta_{+}=\frac{\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}(0)}{2}
,
\bar{\bar{\phi}}_{+}=\bar{\phi}_{0}(0)
, the
solution to the Hamiltonian equations of motion agrees with
(
3.16
).

3.4
Improved action principle

A way to get a well-defined action principle from which equations (
3.24
)
derive is the following. We first note that the constraints (
3.1
) split
into the first class constraint (
3.9
) and the second class constraints

\tilde{g}^{+}_{+}=g^{+}-\frac{\bar{g}^{+}_{+}}{L_{-}},\quad\int^{{L_{-}}/{2}}_{-{L_{-}}/{2}}dx^{-}\,\tilde{g}^{+}_{+}=0,

(3.29)

where

\bar{g}^{+}_{+}=\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}-\Delta_{+},\quad\tilde{g}^{+}_{+}=\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}-\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}.

(3.30)

We then set

\bar{\phi}_{+}=\Delta_{+}\frac{x^{+}}{L_{+}}+\bar{\varphi}_{+},

(3.31)

with
\bar{\varphi}_{+}
periodic in
x^{+}
. After substitution of these decompositions
into (
3.3
) the improved first order action is

\begin{split}&S^{I+}_{H}[\bar{\varphi}_{+},\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+},\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+},\tilde{\varphi}_{+},\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+},\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+};\Delta_{+}]=\int dx^{+}L^{+}_{H},,\quad L^{+}_{H}=\bar{L}^{+}_{H}+\int^{{L_{-}}/{2}}_{-{L_{-}}/{2}}dx^{-}\,\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{+}_{H},\\
&\bar{L}^{+}_{H}=\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}\partial_{+}\bar{\varphi}_{+}-\bar{\lambda}_{+}^{+}\bar{g}^{+}_{+}+\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}\frac{\Delta_{+}}{L_{+}}-2\frac{\Delta^{2}_{+}}{L_{+}},\\
&\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{+}_{H}=\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}\partial_{+}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}-\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+}\tilde{g}^{+}_{+},\end{split}

(3.32)

where the tilded fields are periodic in
x^{-}
, while the barred fields are
assumed periodic in
x^{+}
, and the constant
-2\frac{\Delta^{2}_{+}}{L_{+}}
has been added for
later convenience.

In terms of the fields of the improved action principle, the non-vanishing equal

x^{+}
-time brackets are

\{\bar{\varphi}_{+}(x^{+}),\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}(x^{+})\}_{+}=1,\quad\{\tilde{\varphi}_{+}(x^{+},x^{-}),\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}(x^{+},y^{-})\}_{+}=\delta(x^{-},y^{-})-\frac{1}{L_{-}}.

(3.33)

This variational principle correctly reproduces
all equations of motion, including the matching condition (
3.28
) which
follows from the
x^{+}
zero mode part of the equation of motion for

\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}
. If one extracts the constant
x^{+}
zero mode of the Lagrange
multiplier for the first class constraint,

\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}(x^{+})=\bar{\bar{\lambda}}^{+}_{+}+\tilde{\bar{\lambda}}^{+}_{+}(x^{+})
, this equation splits into the
matching condition (
3.28
), which reads
\Delta_{+}=L_{+}\bar{\bar{\lambda}}^{+}_{+}
and

\partial_{+}\varphi_{+}=\tilde{\bar{\lambda}}^{+}_{+}
, which contains the right movers.

The improved action thus decouples into a zero mode sector and the starting
point Lagrangian action (
2.3
) for
\tilde{\varphi}_{+}
instead of
\phi
.
The latter encodes the dynamics of the right moving chiral bosons. If one
forgets that the coordinates
x^{\pm}
are null, and identifies
x^{+}\to x^{0}
,

x^{-}\to x^{1}
, the former looks like a simple first class constraint system
consisting of a single degree of freedom that is pure gauge because of the first
class constraint
\bar{g}^{+}_{+}\approx 0
. As emphasized before, this
interpretation for the zero mode sector is not correct however, since it
contains the information on the physical relevant left mover, including part of
the particle zero mode of the instant form in the non-periodic case.

A convenient way to restrict the theory described by
\bar{L}^{+}_{H}
to just the
degrees of freedom of a particle, without the left movers, is to declare the
constant
\Delta_{+}
to be dynamical,
\Delta_{+}=\Delta_{+}(x^{+})
. In this case, solving the constraint

\bar{g}^{+}_{+}
in the action yields that of a free particle,

S^{+}_{P}=\int dx^{+}[\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}\partial_{+}\varphi_{+}-\frac{(\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+})^{2}}{L_{+}}].

(3.34)

3.5
Symmetries and generators

Consider first the periodic case. The transformations generated by the
differentiable first class constraint
G^{+}[\epsilon^{+}]
, where

\epsilon^{+}=\epsilon^{+}(x^{+})
, are

\delta_{\epsilon^{+}}\phi=\{\phi,G^{+}[\epsilon^{+}]\}_{+}=\epsilon^{+},\quad\delta_{\epsilon^{+}}\pi^{+}=\{\pi^{+},G^{+}[\epsilon^{+}]\}_{+}=0.

(3.35)

Even though the transformations are generated by a first class constraints, it has
been argued in
[
30
]
that these transformations cannot be a
gauge symmetries since they transform solutions to inequivalent solutions. The detailed
explanation is as follows:

The expectation that first class constraints generate transformations that
do not change the physical state is based on the assumption that initial data
on a constant time surface are enough to fully determine the solution (see
e.g. section 1.2.1 of
[
37
]
). There is thus no contradiction
because, in the massless case, fixing data on a single front is not enough to
completely determine the solution.

Note also that the mechanism here is different and not directly related to the
case of large gauge transformations where suitably improved differentiable
generators associated with first class constraints do change the physical state
on account of non trivial boundary conditions

[
32
,
55
]
.

At this stage, we go a step further and show that these shift transformations
are global symmetries, both in the periodic and the non-periodic case. In order to
do so, the transformations are extended to the Lagrange multipliers in such a way
that the variation of the integrand of the first order action (
3.3
) is a
divergence (see e.g.
[
37
]
, chapter 3.2.2). This fixes

\delta_{\epsilon^{+}}\lambda^{+}=\partial_{+}\epsilon^{+},\quad\delta_{\epsilon^{+}}\mathcal{L}_{H}^{+}=\partial_{-}(\phi\partial_{+}\epsilon^{+}),

(3.36)

with associated Noether currents

j^{+}_{\epsilon^{+}}=\pi^{+}\epsilon^{+},\quad j^{-}_{\epsilon^{+}}=\lambda^{+}\epsilon^{+}-\phi\partial_{+}\epsilon^{+}.

(3.37)

Note that the Hamiltonian form of the currents
j^{\pm}_{\epsilon^{+}}
agree with
the Lagrangian
j^{\pm}_{L,\epsilon^{+}}
in (
2.13
) when using the last two
Hamiltonian equations of motion in (
3.4
).

In the periodic case, both
G^{+}[\epsilon^{+}]
and
Q^{+}_{\epsilon^{+}}
are
differentiable generators. They generate the same symmetry
transformations as
G^{+}[\epsilon^{+}]
, while the associated Noether currents
differ by the divergence of a superpotential,

g^{+}\epsilon^{+}=j^{+}_{\epsilon^{+}}-\partial_{-}(\phi\epsilon^{+}),\quad j^{\prime-}_{\epsilon^{+}}=j^{-}_{\epsilon^{+}}+\partial_{+}(\phi\epsilon^{+})=(\lambda^{+}+\partial_{+}\phi)\epsilon^{+}.

(3.38)

The other shift transformations
\delta_{\epsilon^{-}}\phi=\epsilon^{-}
may again be
extended to the auxiliary fields so that the variation of integrand of the first
order action is a divergence,

\delta_{\epsilon^{-}}\pi^{+}=\partial_{-}\epsilon^{-},\quad\delta_{\epsilon^{-}}\lambda^{+}=0,\quad\delta_{\epsilon^{-}}\mathcal{L}^{+}_{H}=\partial_{+}(\phi\partial_{-}\epsilon^{-}),

(3.39)

with associated Noether currents,

j^{+}_{\epsilon^{-}}=\pi^{+}\epsilon^{-}-\phi\partial_{-}\epsilon^{-},\quad j^{-}_{\epsilon^{-}}=\lambda^{+}\epsilon^{-},

(3.40)

that agree with their Lagrangian counterparts when using the equations of motion
for the auxiliary fields. On the phase space variables, these symmetries are
canonically generated by the associated charges

Q^{+}_{\epsilon^{-}}=\int^{{L_{-}}/{2}}_{-{L_{-}}/{2}}dx^{-}j^{+}_{\epsilon^{-}}
,
which are differentiable generators also in the non-periodic case. One
may also define the equivalent Noether currents

j^{\prime+}_{\epsilon^{-}}=(\pi^{+}+\partial_{-}\phi)\epsilon^{-}=j^{+}_{\epsilon^{-}}+\partial_{-}(\phi\epsilon^{-}),\ j^{\prime-}_{\epsilon^{-}}=j^{-}_{\epsilon^{-}}-\partial_{+}(\phi\epsilon^{-})=(\lambda^{+}-\partial_{+}\phi)\epsilon^{-}.

(3.41)

The associated charges
Q^{\prime+}_{\epsilon^{-}}=\int^{{L_{-}}/{2}}_{-{L_{-}}/{2}}dx^{-}j^{\prime+}_{\epsilon^{-}}
are only
differentiable in the periodic case.

The extension of the conformal symmetries

\delta_{\xi}\phi=\xi^{\rho}\partial_{\rho}\phi
to the auxiliary fields is

\delta_{\xi}\pi^{+}=\partial_{-}(\delta_{\xi}\phi),\quad\delta_{\xi}\lambda^{+}=\partial_{+}(\delta_{\xi}\phi),

(3.42)

with Noether currents

j^{+}_{\xi}=\pi^{+}\delta_{\xi}\phi-\xi^{+}\partial_{+}\phi\partial_{-}\phi,\quad j^{-}_{\xi}=\lambda^{+}\delta_{\xi}\phi-\xi^{-}\partial_{-}\phi\partial_{+}\phi.

(3.43)

Representatives for observables such as Noether charges that are adapted to the
Hamiltonian formalism should not involve time derivatives of
the canonical variables. This leads to

Q^{\prime+}_{\xi^{+}}=\int^{{L_{-}}/{2}}_{-{L_{-}}/{2}}dx^{-}j^{\prime+}_{\xi^{+}},\quad Q^{\prime+}_{\xi^{-}}=\int^{{L_{-}}/{2}}_{-{L_{-}}/{2}}dx^{-}j^{\prime+}_{\xi^{-}}.

(3.44)

where one finds for the left chiral half of the conformal symmetries,

\begin{split}&j^{\prime+}_{\xi^{+}}=\xi^{+}\lambda^{+}(\pi^{+}-\partial_{-}\phi),\quad j^{\prime-}_{\xi^{+}}=\xi^{+}\lambda^{+}\partial_{+}\phi,\\
&\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{+}}\phi=\xi^{+}\lambda^{+}=\{\phi,Q^{\prime+}_{\xi^{+}}\}_{+},\quad\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{+}}\pi^{+}=-\xi^{+}\partial_{-}\lambda^{+}=\{\pi^{+},Q^{\prime+}_{\xi^{+}}\}_{+},\\
&\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{+}}\lambda^{+}=\partial_{+}(\xi^{+}\lambda^{+}),\end{split}

(3.45)

and

\begin{split}&j^{\prime+}_{\xi^{-}}=\xi^{-}\pi^{+}\partial_{-}\phi,\quad j^{\prime-}_{\xi^{-}}=\xi^{-}\pi^{+}(\lambda^{+}-\partial_{+}\phi),\\
&\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{-}}\phi=\xi^{-}\partial_{-}\phi=\{\phi,Q^{\prime+}_{\xi^{-}}\}_{+},\ \delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{-}}\pi^{+}=\partial_{-}(\xi^{-}\pi^{+})=\{\pi^{+},Q^{\prime+}_{\xi^{-}}\}_{+},\\
&\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{-}}\lambda^{+}=\xi^{-}\partial_{-}\lambda^{+},\end{split}

(3.46)

for the right chiral half. The generators in (
3.44
) are differentiable
only in the periodic case. In addition, the right chiral half of the conformal
algebra is canonically realized in terms of Poisson brackets of the associated
Noether charges, whereas the left is not,

\{Q^{\prime+}_{\xi_{1}^{-}},Q^{\prime+}_{\xi_{2}^{-}}\}_{+}=Q^{\prime+}_{[\xi_{1}^{-},\xi^{-}_{2}]},\quad\{Q^{\prime+}_{\xi_{1}^{+}},Q^{\prime+}_{\xi_{2}^{+}}\}_{+}=0\neq Q^{\prime+}_{[\xi_{1}^{+},\xi^{+}_{2}]}.

(3.47)

In the non-periodic case, differentiable generators can be obtained on the level
of the improved first order action principle (
3.32
) and its variables.
The left chiral shift symmetries with parameter
\epsilon^{+}(x^{+})
, which we
assume to be periodic in
x^{+}
, act non-trivially only on the zero mode sector as

\delta_{\epsilon^{+}}\bar{\varphi}^{+}=\epsilon^{+},\quad\delta_{\epsilon^{+}}\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}=0,\quad\delta_{\epsilon+}\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}=\partial_{+}\epsilon^{+}.

(3.48)

On the phase space
variables, these transformations are canonically generated by the differentiable
generator

Q^{+I}_{\epsilon^{+}}=\bar{g}^{+}_{+}\epsilon^{+}.

(3.49)

For the right chiral shift symmetries, we require the parameter
\epsilon^{-}(x^{-})
to be
periodic in
x^{-}
, and decompose into a constant mode and the non-constant ones,

\epsilon^{-}=\bar{\epsilon}^{-}_{+}+\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{+}
, with

\bar{\epsilon}^{-}_{+}=\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\epsilon^{-}/L_{-}
,

\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{+}=0
.
They act as

\delta_{\epsilon^{-}}\bar{\varphi}_{+}=\bar{\epsilon}^{-}_{+},\quad\delta_{\epsilon^{-}}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}=\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{+},\quad\delta_{\epsilon^{-}}\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}=\partial_{-}\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{+},\quad\delta_{\epsilon^{-}}\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}=0=\delta_{\epsilon^{-}}\bar{\lambda}_{+}^{+}=\delta_{\epsilon^{-}}\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+}.

(3.50)

On the phase space variables, these transformations are canonically generated by
the differentiable generators,

Q^{+I}_{\epsilon^{-}}=\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}\bar{\epsilon}^{-}_{+}+\int^{L_{-}/2}_{-L_{-}/2}dx^{-}(\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}+\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+})\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{+}.

(3.51)

For the conformal symmetries, we assume
\xi^{+}(x^{+}),\xi^{-}(x^{-})
to be periodic in the
their arguments. For the left chiral half of the conformal symmetries, we find

\begin{split}&\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{+}}\bar{\varphi}_{+}=\xi^{+}\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+},\quad\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{+}}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}=\xi^{+}\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+},\quad\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{+}}\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}=0,\quad\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{+}}\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}=-\xi^{+}\partial_{-}\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+},\\
&\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{+}}\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}=\partial_{+}(\xi^{+}\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}),\quad\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{+}}\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+}=\partial_{+}(\xi^{+}\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+}),\end{split}

(3.52)

with differentiable canonical generators,

Q^{I+}_{\xi^{+}}=\xi^{+}\bar{g}^{+}_{+}\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}+\xi^{+}\int^{L_{-}/2}_{-L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+}(\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}-\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}),

(3.53)

for the transformations on the phase space variables. They do not provide a
canonical realization of the left chiral half of the conformal algebra.

For the right chiral half, the mixing between sectors is more involved.
One finds

\begin{split}&\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{-}}\bar{\varphi}_{+}=\int^{L_{-}/2}_{-L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{L_{-}}(\frac{\Delta_{+}}{L_{-}}+\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}),\ \delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{-}}\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}=0,\ \delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{-}}\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}=\int^{L_{-}/2}_{-L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{L_{-}}\partial_{-}\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+},\\
&\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{-}}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}=\xi^{-}(\frac{\Delta_{+}}{L_{-}}+\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+})-\int^{L_{-}/2}_{-L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{L_{-}}(\frac{\Delta_{+}}{L_{-}}+\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}),\\
&\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{-}}\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}=\partial_{-}[\xi^{-}(\frac{\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}}{L_{-}}+\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+})],\quad\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{-}}\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+}=\xi^{-}\partial_{-}\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+}-\int^{L_{-}/2}_{-L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{L_{-}}\partial_{-}\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+}.\end{split}

(3.54)

The associated Noether charges

Q^{+}_{\xi^{-}}=\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\xi^{-}\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}+\Delta_{+}\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{L_{-}}\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}\\
+\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{L_{-}}\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}+\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}\frac{\Delta_{+}}{L_{-}}\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{L_{-}},

(3.55)

are the improved differentiable canonical generators of the right chiral half of
the conformal transformations on the canonical variables. They agree on-shell
with the charges computed in instant form in (
A.55
), when the latter is
restricted to the right chiral half and after performing the change of
integration variables
x^{1}=\sqrt{2}x^{-}
. Furthermore, they provide a canonical
realization of the right chiral half of the conformal transformations,

\{Q^{+}_{\xi^{-}_{1}},Q^{+}_{\xi^{-}_{2}}\}=\delta^{\prime}_{\xi^{-}_{2}}Q^{+}_{\xi^{-}_{1}}=Q^{+}_{[\xi^{-}_{1},\xi^{-}_{2}]}.

(3.56)

Note that the right chiral half of the shift and the conformal symmetries are
generated on the canonical variables by charges that vanish on the constraint
surface.

3.6
Reduced theory and Dirac brackets

Solving the second class constraints in the action, i.e., eliminating the
auxiliary fields
\tilde{\lambda}_{+}^{+},\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}
gives the equivalent reduced
action principle,

\begin{split}&S_{R}^{+}[\bar{\varphi}_{+},\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+},\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+},\tilde{\varphi}_{+};\Delta_{+}]=\int_{-L_{+}/2}^{L_{+}/2}dx^{+}L^{+}_{R},\\
&L^{+}_{R}=\bar{L}^{+}_{H}+\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\,\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{+}_{R},\quad\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{+}_{R}=\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}\partial_{+}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}.\end{split}

(3.57)

One may follow for instance
[
31
]
,
[
56
]
, section
5.D or
[
29
]
, section 2.6, and compute the Dirac brackets
associated with the second class constraints. When using that

\{\tilde{g}^{+}_{+}(x^{-}),\tilde{g}^{+}_{+}(y^{-})\}_{+}=-2\partial_{-}^{x}\delta(x^{-},y^{-}),

(3.58)

and

\int dy^{-}\partial_{-}^{x^{-}}\delta(x^{-},y^{-})\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon(y^{-}-z^{-})=\delta(x^{-},z^{-}),

(3.59)

where
\varepsilon(x)
is the sign function, it follows that the non-vanishing
Dirac brackets are given by

\begin{split}&\{\tilde{\varphi}_{+}(x^{-}),\tilde{\varphi}_{+}(y^{-})\}^{*}_{+}=-\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon(x^{-}-y^{-})+\frac{x^{-}-y^{-}}{2L_{-}},\\
&\{\tilde{\varphi}_{+}(x^{-}),\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}(y^{-})\}^{*}_{+}=\frac{1}{2}[\delta(x^{-},y^{-})-\frac{1}{L_{-}}],\\
&\{\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}(x^{-}),\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}(y^{-})\}^{*}_{+}=\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\prime}(x^{-},y^{-}),\\
&\{\bar{\varphi}_{+},\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}\}^{*}_{+}=1,\end{split}

(3.60)

where by convention
\delta^{\prime}(x,y)=\partial_{x}\delta(x,y)=-\partial_{y}\delta(x,y)
.
More details can be found in Appendix
A.1
.

When working with Dirac brackets, the second class constraints may be imposed
strongly and only the first class constraint
\bar{g}^{+}_{+}\approx 0
of the zero mode sector
remains. The Hamiltonian reduces to

H^{+*}=\bar{g}^{+}_{+}\bar{\lambda}_{+}^{+}.

(3.61)

It vanishes on the first class constraint surface and generates no time
evolution in the chiral sector, but only in the zero mode sector,

\partial_{+}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}=\{\tilde{\varphi}_{+},H^{+*}\}^{*}_{+}=0,\ \partial_{+}\bar{\varphi}_{+}=\{\bar{\varphi}_{+},H^{+*}\}^{*}_{+}=\bar{\lambda}_{+}^{+},\ \partial_{+}\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}=\{\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+},H^{+*}\}^{*}_{+}=0,

(3.62)

so that the initial data
\tilde{\varphi}_{+}(x^{-})
are constants of the motion.

The generator
Q^{+I}_{\epsilon^{+}}
is unaffected by the pull-back to the second class
constraint surface, while the generator
Q^{+I}_{\epsilon^{-}}
becomes

Q^{+*}_{\epsilon^{-}}=\bar{\pi}^{+}\bar{\epsilon}^{-}_{+}+\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\,2\partial_{-}\tilde{\phi}\tilde{\epsilon}^{-}_{+}.

(3.63)

For the left chiral half of the conformal symmetries, the reduced generator is

Q^{+*}_{\xi^{+}}=\bar{g}^{+}_{+}{\bar{\lambda}_{+}^{+}}\xi^{+}.

(3.64)

It does no longer induce a transformation on
\tilde{\varphi}_{+}
and does not provide a
canonical realization of the left chiral half of the conformal algebra in the
Dirac bracket.

The reduced transformations for the right chiral half of the conformal
symmetries are given by

\begin{split}&\delta^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}=\xi^{-}(\frac{\Delta_{+}}{L_{-}}+\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+})-\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{L_{-}/2}dy^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{L_{-}}(\frac{\Delta_{+}}{L_{-}}+\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}),\\
&\delta^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\bar{\varphi}_{+}=\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{L_{-}/2}dy^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{L_{-}}(\frac{\Delta_{+}}{L_{-}}+\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}),\quad\delta^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}=0,\\
&\delta^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\bar{\lambda}_{+}^{+}=\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{L_{-}/2}dy^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{L_{-}}\partial_{+}\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+},\end{split}

(3.65)

and the reduced Noether charge is

Q^{+*}_{\xi^{-}}=\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\xi^{-}(\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+})^{2}+(\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}+\Delta_{+})\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{L_{-}}\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}\\
+\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}\frac{\Delta_{+}}{L_{-}}\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{L_{-}}.

(3.66)

When using that

\frac{\delta L_{R}^{+}}{\delta\bar{\lambda}_{+}^{+}}\delta^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\bar{\lambda}_{+}^{+}=\bar{g}^{+}_{+}\int_{-L-/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{2L_{-}}\frac{\delta\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{+}_{R}}{\delta\tilde{\varphi}_{+}},

(3.67)

the equation for the Noether charge,

\partial_{+}Q^{+*}_{\xi^{-}}+\frac{\delta L_{R}^{+}}{\delta\bar{\varphi}_{+}}\delta^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\bar{\varphi}_{+}+\frac{\delta L_{R}^{+}}{\delta\bar{\lambda}_{+}^{+}}\delta^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\bar{\lambda}_{+}^{+}+\int_{-L-/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\frac{\delta\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{+}_{R}}{\delta\tilde{\varphi}_{+}}\delta^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}=0,

(3.68)

may be re-written as

\partial_{+}Q^{+*}_{\xi^{-}}+\frac{\delta L_{R}^{+}}{\delta\bar{\varphi}_{+}}\hat{\delta}^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\bar{\varphi}_{+}+\int_{-L-/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\frac{\delta\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{+}_{R}}{\delta\tilde{\varphi}_{+}}\hat{\delta}^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}=0,

(3.69)

where the “improved” transformations are defined as

\begin{split}\hat{\delta}^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\bar{\varphi}_{+}&=\delta^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\bar{\varphi}_{+},\quad\hat{\delta}^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}=0=\hat{\delta}^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+},\\
\hat{\delta}^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}&=\delta^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}+\bar{g}^{+}_{+}(\frac{\xi^{-}}{2L_{-}}-\int_{-L-/2}^{L_{-}/2}dy^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{2L_{-}^{2}})\\
&=\xi^{-}\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}-\int_{-L-/2}^{L_{-}/2}dy^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{L_{-}}\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}+\frac{\pi^{+}_{+}+\Delta_{+}}{2L_{-}}(\xi^{-}-\int_{-L-/2}^{L_{-}/2}dy^{-}\frac{\xi^{-}}{L_{-}}).\end{split}

(3.70)

These improved transformations are canonically generated in the Dirac bracket by
the reduced Noether charge,

\hat{\delta}^{R}_{\xi^{-}}\cdot=\{\cdot,Q^{+*}_{\xi^{-}}\}^{*}_{+}.

(3.71)

The latter form a canonical realization of the conformal algebra in the Dirac
bracket on the first class constraint surface,

\{Q^{+*}_{\xi^{-}_{1}},Q^{+*}_{\xi^{-}_{2}}\}^{*}_{+}=\hat{\delta}^{R}_{\xi^{-}_{2}}Q^{+*}_{\xi^{-}_{1}}=Q^{+*}_{[\xi^{-}_{1},\xi^{-}_{2}]}+\frac{(\bar{g}^{+}_{+})^{2}}{4L_{-}}\int_{-L-/2}^{L_{-}/2}dx^{-}\frac{[\xi_{1}^{-},\xi^{-}_{2}]}{L_{-}}.

(3.72)

3.7
Preliminary attempt at quantization

Even though encoding half of the relevant data in a Lagrange multiplier is fine
insofar as the characteristic initial value problem is concerned, the lack of a
symplectic structure for these data means that one does not know how to quantize
this sector. We illustrate this by providing the partition function in the
single front formulation, while treating the sector associated with the first
class constraints as pure gauge.

We consider the theory with periodic boundary conditions on the
x^{+}=0
front,

x^{-}\sim x^{-}+L_{-}
. Our aim here is to attempt to compute the standard partition
function,

Z(\beta,\alpha)={\rm Tr}\,e^{-\beta\hat{H}+i\alpha\hat{P}},

(3.73)

where the observables are the Hamiltonian and linear momentum in instant form,
from the viewpoint of canonical quantization on a single light front
x^{+}=c^{+}
.
Details on how to obtain this partition function in terms of chiral bosons in
instant form are reviewed in Appendix
A
.

When using that
\partial_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\partial_{+}+\partial_{-})
,

\partial_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\partial_{+}-\partial_{-})
, the generator

Q^{+}_{\partial_{0}}
of evolution in
x^{0}
time and the generator

Q^{+}_{\partial_{1}}
of spatial translation in
x^{1}
are

\begin{split}&Q_{\partial_{0}}^{+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big[\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}\bar{g}^{+}_{+}+\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}\frac{\Delta_{+}}{L_{-}}+\int dx^{-}\,(\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+})^{2}\big],\\
&Q_{\partial_{1}}^{+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big[\bar{\lambda}_{+}^{+}\bar{g}^{+}_{+}+\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}\frac{\Delta_{+}}{L_{-}}-\int dx^{-}\,(\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+})^{2}\big].\end{split}

(3.74)

If
\tau=\frac{\alpha+i\beta}{L}
, for some
L
, the observable involved in the partition
function is

-\beta Q_{\partial_{0}}^{+}-i\alpha Q_{\partial_{1}}^{+}=\frac{i\tau L}{\sqrt{2}}H^{+R}-\frac{i\bar{\tau}L}{\sqrt{2}}H^{+*},

(3.75)

where

H^{+R}=\int dx^{-}\,(\partial_{-}\tilde{\varphi}_{+})^{2},\quad H^{+*}=\bar{\lambda}_{+}^{+}\bar{g}^{+}_{+}+\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}\frac{\Delta_{+}}{L_{-}},

(3.76)

so that the partition function factorizes into contributions from the chiral
sector and the zero mode,

As is clear from Appendix

How to proceed in a way that puts right and left movers on

The main lessons from the analysis of this section are the following

•

When setting up the canonical formalism on a single null hyperplane,
none of the solutions or of the degrees of freedom of the system are lost.
In the case of the
x^{+}
-front, the right movers and their Poisson structure
are correctly reproduced and lead to a correct quantization of this sector.
It turns out however that the left movers together with the momentum of the
particle zero mode of the instant form are encoded in the Lagrange
multiplier for a first class constraint. What is missing is a suitable
Poisson structure that allows one to correctly quantize these degrees
of freedom. In the case of the
x^{-}
front, the role of the left and right
movers are exchanged.

•

All the global symmetries of the system are reproduced and can be
represented in terms of suitable conserved Noether currents. It turns out
however that the left chiral shift symmetry is generated by a charge that
vanishes on the constraint surface. The same applies for the charges
generating the left chiral half of the conformal symmetries. There is no
canonical realization for the left chiral half of the conformal symmetries.
This is because some of the information on the full charge and the correct
Poisson structure is missing.

4
Double front Hamiltonian analysis

We now want to recover the full phase space from a Hamiltonian viewpoint in a
symmetrical way for left and right movers. In order to do so, one sets up the

planes is equivalent to the phase space in instant form associated to a

a region of spacetime (see e.g.

4.2
Conserved currents and Stokes’ theorem

In order to relate to the well-understood principles in instant form, one uses
Stokes’ theorem on the level of conserved currents. Indeed, consider a conserved
current
j^{\mu}
with conservation equation
\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}\approx 0
, where the weak equality
now denotes an equality that holds when the equations of motion (and their
linearization) are satisfied. Defining the associated
n-1
-form on Minkowsi
spacetime by

j=d^{n-1}x_{\mu}j^{\mu}.

(4.5)

with

d^{n-k}x_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{k}}=\frac{1}{(n-k)!}\epsilon_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{k}\nu_{k+1}\dots\nu_{n}}dx^{\nu_{k+1}}\dots dx^{\nu_{n}},

(4.6)

with
\epsilon_{01\dots n-1}=1

\epsilon_{\mu_{0}\dots\mu_{n-1}}

(4.7)

It then follows from Stokes’ theorem that

\int_{\partial V}j=\int_{V}dj\approx 0.

(4.8)

In our case

\begin{split}j&=dx^{1}j^{0}-dx^{0}j^{1}=dx^{-}j^{+}-dx^{+}j^{-},\quad j^{\pm}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(j^{0}\pm j^{1}),\\
dj&=dx^{0}dx^{1}(\partial_{0}j^{0}+\partial_{1}j^{1})=dx^{+}dx^{-}(\partial_{+}j^{+}+\partial_{-}j^{-}).\end{split}

(4.9)

In instant front, the relevant conserved quantity is

\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}j^{0}|_{x^{0}=0}

(4.10)

In the particular case when

may be evaluated at any constant line

4.3

In order to be able to deal with Poisson brackets, one may consider (odd) field

(4.11)

(4.12)

In our case of action

respectively
x^{-}

4.6
Alternative double front constrained Hamiltonian analysis

Even though the exact Peierls bracket for free field theories is very appealing,
it is possible to formulate all results consistently from the knowlegde
of the brackets on equal “time” slices, because the Peierls bracket is
uniquely determined by suitable initial conditions and the fact that it is a
solution to the homogeneous equations of motion (cf. (
B.2
)). In
addition, such a formulation has the advantage that it extends to interacting
theories where an exact, non-perturbative solution of the equations of motion is
usually not readily accessible.

One may now develop in parallel the constrained Hamiltonian analysis on the two
intersecting fronts
x^{+}=L_{+}/2
and
x^{-}=L_{-}/2

single fronts in the previous section. We will “match” two equivalent

More concretely, the system described by action (

\pi^{\mp}

\begin{split}S_{H}^{I+}&=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{+}\big[2\bar{L}^{+}_{H}+\int_{x^{+}-L_{-}}^{x^{+}+L_{-}}dx^{-}[\tilde{\pi}^{+}_{+}\partial_{+}\tilde{\varphi}_{+}-\tilde{\pi}^{-}_{+}\tilde{g}^{+}_{+}]\big],\\

by cutting with constant

The lessons that can drawn from the double front analysis are:

•

The missing part of the symplectic structure is determined by equal time
Poisson brackets on the other front. The left chiral shifts and the left

•

In the case of the time-like cylinder, the

If one works separately on each of the fronts, due care has to be taken

5
Massive case

We now illustrate how some of the subtleties related to zero modes are absent if one adds a mass term.

5.1
Dynamics, symmetries and Noether currents

The action is now

S=\int dx^{+}dx^{-}[\partial_{+}\phi\partial_{-}\phi-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\phi^{2}].

(5.1)

There are no longer any shift symmetries while the conformal symmetries reduce
to the Poincaré symmetries in
1+1
spacetime dimensions. The latter are
described by
\delta_{\xi}\phi=\xi^{\rho}\partial_{\rho}\phi
where

\xi^{+}=a^{+}+\omega x^{+},\quad\xi^{-}=a^{-}-\omega x^{-},

(5.2)

or equivalently, by
\partial_{-}\xi^{+}=0=\partial_{+}\xi^{-}
and in addition

\partial_{+}\xi^{+}+\partial_{-}\xi^{-}=0
. The associated Noether current is given in
terms of the trace-full energy momentum tensor

T_{\pm\pm}=(\partial_{\pm}\phi)^{2},\quad T_{\mp\pm}=\frac{m^{2}}{2}\phi^{2},

(5.3)

as

j^{+}_{L,\xi}=T_{--}\xi^{-}+T_{-+}\xi^{+},\quad j^{-}_{L,\xi}=T_{++}\xi^{+}+T_{+-}\xi^{-}.

(5.4)

5.2
Single front Hamiltonian analysis

In the canonical analysis on the
x^{+}=c^{+}
front, the constraint
(
3.1
) is unchanged but the primary Hamiltonian is no longer proportional
to this constraint, but rather

H=\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{{L_{-}/2}}dx^{-}\frac{m^{2}}{2}\phi^{2}.

(5.5)

The equivalent first order action principle is now

S_{H}[\phi,\pi^{+},\lambda^{+}]=\int dx^{+}\int_{-L_{-}/2}^{{L_{-}/2}}dx^{-}[\pi^{+}\partial_{+}\phi-\frac{m^{2}}{2}\phi^{2}-\lambda^{+}(\pi^{+}-\partial_{-}\phi)].

(5.6)

Conservation in time of the constraints
\{g^{+},H+G[\lambda^{+}]\}\approx 0
now imposes

\partial_{-}\lambda^{+}=-\frac{m^{2}}{2}\phi.

(5.7)

In the following, we assume all fields to be periodic in
x^{-}
and decompose, as
in the massless case, into a zero mode that is constant on the
x^{+}
front and
only depends on
x^{+}
, and a field without zero mode.

It then follows that there is a single secondary constraint
that sets to zero the zero mode of
\phi
as defined in (
3.22
),

\bar{\phi}_{{+}}\approx 0.

(5.8)

Furthermore, it follows from (
5.7
) that
\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+}
is uniquely fixed
by
\tilde{\phi}_{+}
,

\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{+}(\tilde{\phi}_{+})=-\frac{m^{2}}{2}\partial_{-}^{-1}\tilde{\phi}_{+},

(5.9)

Conservation in time of the secondary constraint,

\{\bar{\phi}_{{+}},H+G[\lambda^{+}]\}\approx 0
, then requires the zero mode of
the Lagrange multiplier to vanish,
\bar{\lambda}^{+}_{+}=0
. All the
constraints, the primary ones
\tilde{g}^{+}_{+},\bar{g}^{+}_{+}=\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}
in (
3.30
)
together with the secondary one
\bar{\phi}_{+}
are second class. Note that the
absence of a first class constraint is consistent with the absence of a shift
symmetry. The total, first class, Hamiltonian is

H^{\prime}=H+G^{+}[\tilde{\lambda}^{+}_{{+}}(\tilde{\phi}_{+})].

(5.10)

When imposing all second class constraints strongly and working with Dirac
brackets, the zero modes
\bar{\phi}_{+},\bar{\pi}^{+}_{+}
are eliminated from the theory.
The only variables that remain are
\tilde{\phi}(x^{-})
with Dirac brackets given in
the first equation of (
3.60
). The Hamiltonian reduces to the one
given in (
5.5
) and the general solution to the evolution equations

\partial_{+}\tilde{\phi}_{+}(x^{-})=\{\tilde{\phi}_{+}(x^{-}),H\}^{*}_{+}=m^{2}\int^{L_{-}/2}_{-L_{-}/2}dy^{-}[-\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon(x^{-}-y^{-})+\frac{x^{-}-y^{-}}{2L_{-}}]\tilde{\phi}_{+}(y^{-}),

(5.11)

is, for periodic boundary conditions, entirely determined by the free data

\tilde{\phi}(c^{+},x^{-})
. It may be represented formally through the series

\tilde{\phi}_{+}(x^{+},x^{-})=e^{-(x^{+}-c^{+})\{H,\cdot\}^{*}_{+}}\tilde{\phi}_{+}(c^{+},x^{-}).

(5.12)

It thus follows that, with periodic boundary conditions in
x^{-}
, the free data
in the massive case can be prescribed on a single null hyperplane.

6
Lessons and outlook

The main lessons for a consistent quantization of fields on null hyperplanes
that can been drawn from this detailed investigation of the simplest example
are the following:

(i)

The Dirac algorithm systematically determines the free data not only of
the Cauchy problem but also of the characteristic initial value problem.

(ii)

The algorithm is very different in the massless and massive case. In the
former, there are primary first class constraints and data is needed on both
of the intersecting null planes. They are absent in the latter case and data
on a single null surface is enough to fully determine the general solution.

(iii)

The first class constraint in the front form of dynamics in the massless
case does not generate a gauge symmetry but rather an infinite number of
global symmetries. The associated Lagrange multiplier corresponds to a
canonical momentum for time evolution along, rather than off, the front
and encodes information on physical degrees of freedom.

(iv)

As in the instant form of dynamics, the problem has to be supplemented
by boundary conditions. The simplest one consists in putting the system
in a box with periodic boundary conditions. This is where discrete
light-cone quantization comes in. Equivalence with quantization in
instant form on a time-like cylinder requires entangled periodic
boundary conditions in the intersecting null directions. The free
particle in the spectrum does not satisfy the periodic boundary
conditions used in discrete light-cone quantization. This is where
improvement terms of Regge-Teitelboim/ Gibbons-Hawking type become
relevant.

(v)

When trying to describe on a single null hyperplane the full system as
understood from the viewpoint of the instant form, one misses part of the
canonical structure. The generators of the left chiral shifts and conformal
transformations vanish on-shell and do not provide a canonical realization of
the left chiral half of the conformal algebra.

(vi)

A formulation on intersecting double fronts is required. The missing part of
the symplectic structure is determined by equal time Poisson brackets on the
other front. The left chiral shifts and the left chiral half of the conformal
symmetries are entirely generated by non-vanishing charges on the other front
that form a canonical realization of the algebra. Suitable matching conditions
become relevant in order not to over-count when re-assembling the descriptions
on the individual fronts.

(vii)

The problem of quantization of a free massless boson on intersecting
null lines turns out to be closely related to the formulation of chiral

p
-forms in terms of non chiral ones (see e.g.
[
69
]

and references therein) in the simplest case with
p=0
. In particular,
the coupling of the chiral sectors with the topological zero mode sector
in the Noether charges for the conformal symmetries is particularly
instructive.

(viii)

In order to get the correct Dirac/ Peierls brackets that canonically generate
the transformations, a covariant first order formulation with suitable
auxiliary fields can be useful.

It should be quite instructive to work out the details on how to deal with
electromagnetism, Yang-Mills and gravitational theories and their intrinsic
gauge invariances from the viewpoint of the canonical approach on intersecting
double fronts, while keeping both physical and unphysical degrees of freedom. We
plan to address these questions elsewhere.

A.1
Covariant Hamiltonian formulation

In two-dimensional Minkowski spacetime with metric

ds^{2}=(dx^{0})^{2}-(dx^{1})^{2}=\eta_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}
, the Lagrangian action for a free
massless scalar field is:

S[\phi]=\frac{1}{2}\int dx^{0}dx^{1}\,\mathcal{L},\quad\mathcal{L}=\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi.

(A.1)

Infinitesimal conformal symmetries acts as

\delta_{\xi}\phi=\xi^{\rho}\partial_{\rho}\phi,\quad\partial_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}\xi_{\mu}=\partial_{\rho}\xi^{\rho}\eta_{\mu\nu},

(A.2)

with

\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\phi}\delta_{\xi}\phi+\partial_{\mu}(T^{\mu}_{\nu}\xi^{\nu})=0,\quad T_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\rho}\phi\partial^{\rho}\phi.

(A.3)

The associated first order action in instant form is

S_{H}[\phi,\pi^{0}]=\int dx^{0}dx^{1}\,\mathcal{L}_{H}^{0},\quad\mathcal{L}_{H}^{0}=\pi^{0}\partial_{0}\phi-\mathcal{H}^{0},\quad\mathcal{H}^{0}=\frac{1}{2}((\pi^{0})^{2}+(\partial_{1}\phi)^{2}).

(A.4)

In order to better compare to the light front analysis, one may introduce an
additional auxiliary field
\pi^{1}
and work with the equivalent “covariant”
first order action

S^{\prime}_{H}[\phi,\pi^{\mu}]=\int dx^{0}dx^{1}\,\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{H},\quad\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{H}=\pi^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{\mu}\pi_{\mu},

(A.5)

with equations of motion

\partial_{\rho}\phi-\pi_{\rho}=0,\quad\partial_{\mu}\pi^{\mu}=0.

(A.6)

The full Hamiltonian treatment of this system then leads to

S^{\prime\prime}_{H}[\phi,\pi^{\mu},\pi_{\pi^{1}},\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2}]=\int dx^{0}dx^{1}\,[\pi^{0}\partial_{0}\phi+\pi_{\pi^{1}}\partial_{0}\pi^{1}+\pi^{1}\partial_{1}\phi-\frac{1}{2}(\pi^{0})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\pi^{1})^{2}\\
-\lambda^{1}\pi_{\pi^{1}}-\lambda^{2}(\pi^{1}+\partial_{1}\phi)],

(A.7)

with two second class constraints and associated Dirac
brackets,
\{\pi_{\pi^{1}}(x^{1}),\cdot\}^{*}_{0}=0
together with

\begin{split}&\{\phi(x^{1}),\pi^{0}(y^{1})\}^{*}_{0}=\delta(x^{1},y^{1}),\quad\{\phi(x^{1}),\pi^{1}(y^{1})\}^{*}_{0}=0,\\
&\{\pi^{1}(x^{1}),\pi^{0}(y^{1})\}^{*}_{0}=-\delta^{\prime}(x^{1},y^{1})=\{\pi^{0}(x^{1}),\pi^{1}(y^{1})\}^{*}_{0}.\end{split}

(A.8)

The Hamiltonian is given by

H^{\prime 0}=\int dx^{1}[\frac{1}{2}(\pi^{0})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\pi^{1})^{2}-\pi^{1}\partial_{1}\phi].

(A.9)

Imposing the second class constraints strongly
then leads one back to the theory defined by (
A.4
).

One may now also easily work out what happens if one sets up the Hamiltonian
formalism at a constant
x^{1}
, for instance
x^{1}=0
, which is a time-like
surface, so that

is the evolution parameter. For want of a better name,

\phi(x^{0}),\pi^{1}(x^{0})
, the

\pi^{0}
, which is on-shell given by
\pi^{0}=\partial_{0}\phi
,
and forms together with

\{\pi_{\pi^{0}},\cdot\}^{*}_{1}=0

\begin{split}&\{\phi(x^{0}),\pi^{1}(y^{0})\}^{*}_{1}=\delta(x^{0},y^{0}),\quad\{\phi(x^{0}),\pi^{0}(y^{0})\}^{*}_{1}=0,\\
&\{\pi^{0}(x^{0}),\pi^{1}(y^{0})\}^{*}_{1}=\delta^{\prime}(x^{0},y^{0})=\{\pi^{1}(x^{0}),\pi^{0}(y^{0})\}^{*}_{1}.\end{split}

(A.10)

The Hamiltonian is given by

H^{\prime 1}=\int dx^{0}[\frac{1}{2}(\pi^{0})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\pi^{1})^{2}-\pi^{0}\partial_{0}\phi],

(A.11)

and, after imposing the constraints strongly, reduces to

H^{1}=-\frac{1}{2}\int dx^{0}[(\pi^{1})^{2}+(\partial_{0}\phi)^{2}].

(A.12)

A.2
Covariant Hamiltonian representation of shift and

For the shift symmetries, we have

. The Noether
equations become

\begin{split}&\delta_{\epsilon^{\pm}}\phi\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{H}^{0}}{\delta\phi}+\delta_{\epsilon^{\pm}}\pi^{0}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{H}^{0}}{\delta\phi}+\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{\epsilon^{\pm}}=0,\\
&j^{0}_{\epsilon^{\pm}}=\pi^{0}\epsilon^{\pm}-\phi\partial_{0}\epsilon^{\pm},\quad j^{1}_{\epsilon^{\pm}}=-\partial_{1}\phi\epsilon^{\pm}\pm\phi\partial_{0}\epsilon^{\pm}.\end{split}

(A.13)

The associated Noether charges that generate these transformations in the Poisson bracket
are
Q^{0}_{\epsilon^{\pm}}=\int dx^{1}j^{0}_{\epsilon^{\pm}}
.

Infinitesimal conformal transformations are parametrized by the vectors

\xi^{\mu}(x^{\nu})
, with (
A.2
) equivalent to

\partial_{0}\xi^{0}=\partial_{1}\xi^{1}
,
\partial_{0}\xi^{1}=\partial_{1}\xi^{0}
. If

\mathcal{P}^{0}=\pi^{0}\partial_{1}\phi
, the Noether equation adapted to the
Hamiltonian formalism in instant form may be written as

\delta^{0}_{\xi}\phi\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{H}^{0}}{\delta\phi}+\delta^{0}_{\xi}\pi^{0}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{H}^{0}}{\delta\phi}+\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{\xi}=0,

(A.14)

where

\begin{split}&\delta^{0}_{\xi}\phi=\xi^{0}\pi^{0}+\xi^{1}\partial_{1}\phi,\quad\delta^{0}_{\xi}\pi^{0}=\partial_{1}(\xi^{0}\partial_{1}\phi+\xi^{1}\pi^{0}),\\
&j^{0}_{\xi}=\xi^{0}\mathcal{H}^{0}+\xi^{1}\mathcal{P}^{0},\quad j^{1}_{\xi}=-\xi^{0}\mathcal{P}^{0}-\xi^{1}\mathcal{H}^{0}-(\xi^{0}\partial_{1}\phi+\xi^{1}\pi^{0})(\partial_{0}\phi-\pi^{0}).\end{split}

(A.15)

The associated Noether charges
Q^{0}_{\xi}=\int dx^{1}j^{0}_{\xi}
generate the
transformations through the Poisson bracket and realize the conformal
algebra,

\delta_{\xi}^{0}\phi=\{\phi,Q^{0}_{\xi}\}_{0},\quad\delta_{\xi}^{0}\pi^{0}=\{\pi^{0},Q^{0}_{\xi}\}_{0},\quad\{Q^{0}_{\xi_{1}},Q^{0}_{\xi_{2}}\}_{0}=Q^{0}_{[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]}.

(A.16)

In particular, the Hamiltonian evolution equations in
x^{0}
are recovered
from the first two equations by choosing
\xi^{0}=1
,
\xi^{1}=0
.

In the covariant first order formulation, the Noether equation for shift
symmetries is extended to the auxiliary field
\pi^{1}
as

\begin{split}&\delta_{\epsilon^{\pm}}\phi\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{H}}{\delta\phi}+\delta_{\epsilon^{\pm}}\pi^{\rho}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{H}}{\delta\pi^{\rho}}+\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{\epsilon^{\pm}}=0,\\
&\delta_{\epsilon^{\pm}}\phi=\epsilon^{\pm},\quad\delta_{\epsilon^{\pm}}\pi^{\mu}=\partial^{\mu}\epsilon^{\pm},\quad j^{\mu}_{\epsilon^{\pm}}=\pi^{\mu}\epsilon^{\pm}-\phi\partial^{\mu}\epsilon^{\pm}.\end{split}

(A.17)

The associated Noether
Q^{0}_{\epsilon^{\pm}}=\int dx^{1}j^{0}_{\epsilon^{\pm}}
generate
these transformations in the Dirac bracket.

For conformal symmetries, we have

\begin{split}&\delta^{0}_{\xi}\phi\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{H}}{\delta\phi}+\delta^{0}_{\xi}\pi^{\rho}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{H}}{\delta\pi^{\rho}}+\partial_{\mu}\tilde{j}^{\mu}_{\xi}=0,\\
&\delta^{0}_{\xi}\pi^{1}=-\partial_{1}\delta^{0}_{\xi}\phi,\quad\tilde{j}^{0}_{\xi}=j^{0}_{\xi},\quad\tilde{j}^{1}_{\xi}=j^{1}_{\xi}+\delta^{0}_{\xi}\phi\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{H}}{\delta\pi^{1}}.\end{split}

(A.18)

The Noether charges
Q^{0}_{\xi}
generate the conformal transformations through the Dirac
bracket and realize the conformal algebra

\delta_{\xi}^{0}\phi=\{\phi,Q^{0}_{\xi}\}_{0}^{*},\quad\delta_{\xi}^{0}\pi^{\rho}=\{\pi^{\rho},Q^{0}_{\xi}\}_{0}^{*},\quad\{Q^{0}_{\xi_{1}},Q^{0}_{\xi_{2}}\}^{*}_{0}=Q^{0}_{[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]}.

(A.19)

This computation relies on the assumption that one may integrate by parts in
space.

A more covariant way to write Noether’s theorem for conformal symmetries in
first order form is

\begin{split}&\delta^{\prime}_{\xi}\phi\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{H}}{\delta\phi}+\delta^{\prime}_{\xi}\pi^{\rho}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{H}}{\delta\pi^{\rho}}+\partial_{\mu}j^{\prime\mu}_{\xi}=0,\quad\delta^{\prime}_{\xi}\phi=\xi^{\rho}\pi_{\rho},\quad\delta^{\prime}_{\xi}\pi^{\rho}=\partial_{\mu}(\xi^{\mu}\pi^{\rho}-\xi^{\rho}\pi^{\mu}),\ \\
&j^{\prime\mu}_{\xi}=\pi^{\mu}\xi^{\rho}\pi_{\rho}-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{\mu}\pi^{\rho}\pi_{\rho}-(\xi^{\mu}\pi^{\rho}-\xi^{\rho}\pi^{\mu})\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{H}}{\delta\pi^{\rho}}.\end{split}

(A.20)

The associated Noether charge is
Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi}=\int dx^{1}j^{\prime 0}_{\xi}
with

j^{\prime 0}_{\xi}=\pi^{0}\xi^{\rho}\pi_{\rho}-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{0}\pi^{\rho}\pi_{\rho}-(\xi^{0}\pi^{1}-\pi^{0}\xi^{1})(\pi^{1}+\partial_{1}\phi),

(A.21)

and

Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi}=Q^{0}_{\xi}-\frac{1}{2}\int dx^{1}\xi^{0}(\pi^{1}+\partial_{1}\phi)^{2}.

(A.22)

It generates the
\delta^{0}_{\xi}
transformations, and the
\delta^{\prime}_{\xi}

transformations on-shell,

\{\phi,Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi}\}^{*}_{0}=\delta^{0}_{\xi}\phi\approx\delta^{\prime}_{\xi}\phi,\ \{\pi^{0},Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi}\}^{*}_{0}=\delta^{0}_{\xi}\pi^{0}\approx\delta^{\prime}_{\xi}\pi^{0},\ \{\pi^{1},Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi}\}^{*}_{0}=\delta^{0}_{\xi}\pi^{1}\approx\delta^{\prime}_{\xi}\pi^{1},

(A.23)

where the two first relations only require the second class constraint

\pi^{1}=-\partial_{1}\phi
, while the last relation needs all equations of motion.
Furthermore, the Dirac bracket algebra of the Noether charges represents the
conformal algebra on the second class constraint surface,

\{Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi_{1}},Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi_{2}}\}^{*}_{0}=Q^{0}_{[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]}=Q^{\prime 0}_{[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]}+\frac{1}{2}\int dx^{1}[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]^{0}(\pi^{1}+\partial_{1}\phi)^{2}.

(A.24)

Finally, note that in terms of the standard Poisson bracket, if one denotes by

\delta^{0,\pi^{1}}_{\xi}
a variation that acts only on
\pi^{1}
according to

\delta^{0,\pi^{1}}_{\xi}\pi^{1}=\delta^{0}_{\xi}\pi^{1}=-\partial_{1}(\delta^{0}_{\xi}\phi)
, one may write
the Dirac bracket of the Noether charges in terms of a “modified” bracket as

\{Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi_{1}},Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi_{2}}\}^{*}_{0}=\{Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi_{1}},Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi_{2}}\}_{0}\\
+\int dx\int dy\big[\frac{\delta Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi_{1}}}{\delta\pi^{0}(x)}\{\pi^{0}(x),\pi^{1}(y)\}^{*}_{0}\frac{\delta Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi_{2}}}{\delta\pi^{1}(y)}+\frac{\delta Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi_{1}}}{\delta\pi^{1}(x)}\{\pi^{1}(x),\pi^{0}(y)\}^{*}_{0}\frac{\delta Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi_{2}}}{\delta\pi^{0}(y)}\big]\\
=\{Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi_{1}},Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi_{2}}\}_{0}-\delta^{0,\pi^{1}}_{\xi_{1}}Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi_{2}}+\delta^{0,\pi^{1}}_{\xi_{2}}Q^{\prime 0}_{\xi_{1}}.

(A.25)

In other words, the modified bracket arises here when one works with the standard
Poisson bracket and one forgets that the transformation law of the auxiliary field

\pi^{1}
is canonically generated by the appropriate Dirac bracket.

In space form, the Noether charges for shift symmetries are

Q^{1}_{\epsilon^{\pm}}=\int dx^{0}j^{1}_{\epsilon^{\pm}}
and generate the
transformations in the associated Dirac brackets. For conformal symmetries, they
are
Q^{\prime 1}_{\xi}=\int dx^{0}j^{\prime 1}_{\xi}
where

j^{\prime 1}_{\xi}=\pi^{1}\xi^{\rho}\pi_{\rho}-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{1}\pi^{\rho}\pi_{\rho}-(\xi^{1}\pi^{0}-\xi^{0}\pi^{1})\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{H}}{\delta\pi^{0}}.

(A.26)

They may be written as

Q^{\prime 1}_{\xi}=Q^{1}_{\xi}+\frac{1}{2}\int dx^{0}\xi^{1}(\pi^{0}-\partial_{0}\phi)^{2}.

(A.27)

where

Q^{1}_{\xi}=\int dx^{0}[\xi^{1}\mathcal{H}^{1}+\xi^{0}\mathcal{P}^{1}],\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}=-\frac{1}{2}[(\pi^{1})^{2}+(\partial_{0}\phi)^{2}],\quad\mathcal{P}^{1}=\pi^{1}\partial_{0}\phi,

(A.28)

are the charges in the reduced theory without the auxiliary field
\pi^{0}
. The
conformal transformations generated in the Dirac bracket are

\begin{split}&\delta^{1}_{\xi}\phi=\{\phi,Q^{\prime 1}_{\xi}\}^{*}_{1}=-\xi^{1}\pi^{1}+\xi^{0}\partial_{0}\phi,\quad\delta^{1}_{\xi}\pi^{1}=\{\pi^{1},Q^{\prime 1}_{\xi}\}^{*}_{1}=\partial_{0}(-\xi^{1}\partial_{0}\phi+\xi^{0}\pi^{1}),\\
&\delta^{1}_{\xi}\pi^{0}=\{\pi^{0},Q^{\prime 1}_{\xi}\}^{*}_{1}=\partial_{0}(\delta^{1}_{\xi}\phi),\end{split}

(A.29)

and

\{Q^{\prime 1}_{\xi_{1}},Q^{\prime 1}_{\xi_{2}}\}^{*}_{1}=Q^{1}_{[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]}=Q^{\prime 1}_{[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]}-\frac{1}{2}\int dx^{0}[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]^{1}(\pi^{0}-\partial_{0}\phi)^{2}.

(A.30)

Note that these computations rely on the assumption that one may integrate by
parts in time.

A.3
Decomposition on a time-like cylinder

The analysis starts by following the discussion in
[
29
]
on how to
perform an off-shell split of a free massless boson in two dimensions in terms
of left and right moving chiral fields and an
x^{1}
zero mode.

We consider the system (
A.4
) on a spatial circle of length
L
. In other
words, the points
(x^{0},x^{1})\sim(x^{0},x^{1}+L)
are identified and all fields
satisfy periodic boundary conditions in
x^{1}
.

First, one separates the
x^{1}
zero mode from the rest of
the fields,

\begin{split}\phi(x^{0},x^{1})=\bar{\phi}_{0}(x^{0})+\tilde{\phi}_{0}(x^{0},x^{1}),\quad\pi^{0}(x^{0},x^{1})=\frac{1}{L}\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}(x^{0})+\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}(x^{0},x^{1}),\\
\bar{\phi}_{0}=\frac{1}{L}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\,\phi,\quad\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}=\int^{L/2}_{-L/2}dx^{1}\,\pi^{0},\quad\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\,\tilde{\phi}_{0}=0=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\,\tilde{\pi}_{0}^{0}.\end{split}

(A.31)

In terms of this decomposition, the action (
A.4
) splits as

S_{P}[\bar{\phi}_{0},\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}]+S_{H}[\tilde{\phi}_{0},\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}]
with

S^{P}[\bar{\phi}_{0},\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}]=\int dx^{0}[\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}\partial_{0}\bar{\phi}_{0}-\frac{1}{2L}(\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0})^{2}].

(A.32)

The equations of motion become

\partial_{0}\bar{\phi}_{0}=\frac{1}{L}\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0},\ \partial_{0}\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}=0,\ \tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}=\partial_{0}\tilde{\phi}_{0},\ \partial_{0}\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}-\partial_{1}^{2}\tilde{\phi}_{0}=0,

(A.33)

and imply the wave equation
\partial_{0}^{2}\tilde{\phi}_{0}=\partial_{1}^{2}\tilde{\phi}_{0}
.

The non-vanishing equal
x^{0}

time Poisson brackets are

\{\phi(x^{1}),\pi^{0}(y^{1})\}_{0}=\delta^{P}(x^{1},y^{1}),\quad\delta^{P}(x^{1},y^{1})=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{ik(x^{1}-y^{1})}.

(A.34)

After the split, they become

\{\tilde{\phi}_{0}(x^{1}),\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}(y^{1})\}_{0}=\delta^{P}(x^{1},y^{1})-\frac{1}{L},\quad\{\bar{\phi}_{0},\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}\}_{0}=1.

(A.35)

Then the chiral fields are defined as

\phi^{L}=\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{\phi}_{0}+\partial_{1}^{-1}\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}),\quad\phi^{R}=\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{\phi}_{0}-\partial_{1}^{-1}\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}).

(A.36)

Note that on fields without a zero mode,
there is a precise definition of
\partial_{1}^{-1}
that does not contain a zero mode,

\partial^{-1}_{1}\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}(x^{0},x^{1})=\int^{x^{1}}_{0}dy^{1}\,\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}(x^{0},y^{1})-\frac{1}{L}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dy^{1}\int_{0}^{y^{1}}dz^{1}\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}(x^{0},z^{1}).

(A.37)

Alternatively, for periodic fields in
x^{1}
, one integrates the zero mode free
Fourier series term by term in such a way that the result is again a zero
mode free Fourier series.

This definition guarantees (i) that the chiral fields do not involve a zero
mode, (ii) a simple invertible off-shell relation for the fields and their
momenta in terms of the chiral fields

\tilde{\phi}_{0}=\phi^{L}+\phi^{R},\quad\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}=\partial_{1}\phi^{L}-\partial_{1}\phi^{R},

(A.38)

and, (iii) that the equations of motion
(
A.33
) imply that the chiral fields are left and right movers,

\partial_{0}\phi^{L}=\partial_{1}\phi^{L},\quad\partial_{0}\phi^{R}=-\partial_{1}\phi^{R}.

(A.39)

The general solution to the equations of motion is

\phi^{L}=\phi^{L}(x^{+}),\quad\phi^{R}=\phi^{R}(x^{-}),\quad\bar{\pi}_{0}^{0}=\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}(c_{0}),\quad\bar{\phi}_{0}=\bar{\phi}_{0}(c^{0})+\frac{x^{0}}{L}\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}(c_{0}).

(A.40)

Up to a boundary term in time, there is an off-shell split of the action
principle into left and right moving chiral bosons,

S_{H}[\tilde{\phi}_{0},\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}]=S^{L}[\phi^{L}]+S^{R}[\phi^{R}]+S^{B},

(A.41)

with

\begin{split}S^{L}[\phi^{L}]&=\int_{x^{0}_{i}}^{x^{0}_{f}}dx^{0}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\big[\partial_{1}\phi^{L}\partial_{0}\phi^{L}-(\partial_{1}\phi^{L})^{2}\big],\\
S^{R}[\phi^{R}]&=\int_{x^{0}_{i}}^{x^{0}_{f}}dx^{0}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\big[-\partial_{1}\phi^{R}\partial_{0}\phi^{R}-(\partial_{1}\phi^{R})^{2}\big],\\
S^{B}&=\Big[\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{1}\phi^{L}\phi^{R}-\phi^{L}\partial_{1}\phi^{R})\Big]^{x^{0}_{f}}_{x^{0}_{i}}.\end{split}

(A.42)

Note that, while action (
A.4
) is invariant under parity transformations,

S_{H}[\hat{\phi},\hat{\pi}]=S_{H}[\phi,\pi],\quad\hat{\phi}(x^{0},x^{1})=\phi(x^{0},-x^{1}),\ \hat{\pi}(x^{0},x^{1})=\pi(x^{0},-x^{1}),

(A.43)

they exchange the left and right chiral actions,

\begin{split}&S^{L}[\hat{\phi}^{L}]=S^{R}[\phi^{R}],\quad S^{R}[\hat{\phi}^{R}]=S^{L}[\phi^{L}],\\
&\hat{\phi}^{L}(x^{0},x^{1})=\phi^{R}(x^{0},-x^{1}),\quad\hat{\phi}^{R}(x^{0},x^{1})=\phi^{L}(x^{0},-x^{1}).\end{split}

(A.44)

Note also that the primitive

\partial_{1}^{x}u^{P}(x^{1},y^{1})=-\partial_{1}^{y}u^{P}(x^{1},y^{1})=\delta^{P}(x^{1},y^{1})-\frac{1}{L}

that does not contain
x^{1}
zero modes is

u^{P}(x^{1},y^{1})=\sum_{n>0}\frac{1}{\pi n}\sin{k(x^1-y^1)}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon(x^{1}-y^{1})-\frac{x^{1}-y^{1}}{L},\quad k=\frac{2\pi n}{L},

(A.45)

where the last equality follows from the Fourier transforms of the sign function

\varepsilon(x)
and of the linear function
x
on the interval

[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}]
, and extended as periodic functions beyond,

\varepsilon(x)=\sum_{n>0}\frac{2(1-\cos\pi n)}{\pi n}\sin{kx},\quad\frac{x}{L}=-\sum_{n>0}\frac{\cos\pi n}{\pi n}\sin{kx}.

(A.46)

It follows that the equal
x^{0}
time canonical Poisson brackets (
A.35
) become

\begin{split}&\{\phi^{R}(x^{1}),\phi^{R}(y^{1})\}_{0}=\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon(x^{1}-y^{1})-\frac{x^{1}-y^{1}}{2L}=-\{\phi^{L}(x^{1}),\phi^{L}(y^{1})\}_{0},\\
&\{\phi^{L}(x^{1}),\phi^{R}(y^{1})\}_{0}=0.\end{split}

(A.47)

One may also introduce the auxiliary field
\pi^{1}
(and its conjugate momentum),
and split off their zero modes. It then follows that
\bar{\pi}^{1}_{0}=0
,

\tilde{\pi}^{1}_{0}+\partial_{1}\phi^{L}+\partial_{1}\phi^{R}=0
together with the analogous spit of

\pi_{\pi^{1}}
are all second class constraints. If one decides to
keep
\bar{\pi}^{1}_{0}
,
\tilde{\pi}^{1}_{0}
, it follows that the Dirac brackets of

\bar{\pi}^{1}_{0}
with all variables vanish, while those of
\tilde{\pi}^{1}_{0}
are given by

\{\tilde{\pi}^{1}_{0}(x^{1}),\phi^{R}(y^{1})\}^{*}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(\delta(x^{1},y^{1})-\frac{1}{L})=-\{\tilde{\pi}^{1}_{0}(x^{1}),\phi^{L}(y^{1})\}^{*}_{0}.

(A.48)

The Hamiltonian and linear momentum

H=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\,[(\pi^{0})^{2}+(\partial_{1}\phi)^{2}],\quad P=-\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\,\pi^{0}\partial_{1}\phi,

(A.49)

split as
H=H^{L}+H^{R}+H^{P}
,
P=P^{L}+P^{R}
, with

\begin{split}H^{L}&=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\,(\partial_{1}\phi^{L})^{2},\quad H^{R}=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\,(\partial_{1}\phi^{R})^{2},\quad H^{P}=\frac{1}{2L}(\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0})^{2},\\
P^{L}&=-\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\,(\partial_{1}\phi^{L})^{2},\quad P^{R}=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\,(\partial_{1}\phi^{R})^{2}.\end{split}

(A.50)

Of interest to us here is the extended partition function

Z(\beta,\alpha)={\rm Tr}\,e^{-\beta\hat{H}+i\alpha\hat{P}}.

(A.51)

In terms of the modular parameter
\tau=\frac{\alpha+i\beta}{L}=\tau_{1}+i\tau_{2}
,
the combination of Hamiltonian and linear momentum that is involved decomposes
into right and left chiral pieces together with a free particle contribution,

-\beta H+i\alpha P=i\tau LH^{R}-i\bar{\tau}LH^{L}-\tau_{2}LH^{P}.

(A.52)

A.4
Decomposition of conformal transformations

It is also useful to see how the infinitesimal conformal transformations mix the
different sectors.

This leads to

\begin{split}&\delta_{\xi}\bar{\phi}_{0}=\frac{\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}}{L^{2}}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\xi^{0}+\frac{1}{L}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dy^{1}\,(\xi^{0}\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}+\xi^{1}\partial_{1}\tilde{\phi}_{0}),\quad\delta_{\xi}\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}=0,\\
&\delta_{\xi}\tilde{\phi}_{0}=\xi^{0}\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}+\xi^{1}\partial_{1}\tilde{\phi}_{0}-\frac{1}{L}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dy^{1}\,(\xi^{0}\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}+\xi^{1}\partial_{1}\tilde{\phi}_{0})+(\xi^{0}-\frac{1}{L}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\xi^{0})\frac{\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}}{L},\\
&\delta_{\xi}\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}=\partial_{1}(\xi^{0}\partial_{1}\tilde{\phi}_{0}+\xi^{1}\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0})+\frac{1}{L}\partial_{1}\xi^{1}\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}.\end{split}

(A.53)

These transformations are canonically generated by the Noether charge

Q_{\xi}=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}[\xi^{0}\tilde{\mathcal{H}}+\xi^{1}\tilde{\mathcal{P}}]+\frac{\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}}{L}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}[\xi^{0}\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}+\xi^{1}\partial_{1}\tilde{\phi}_{0}]+\frac{(\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0})^{2}}{2L^{2}}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\xi^{0},

(A.54)

with
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}=\frac{1}{2}[(\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0})^{2}+(\partial_{1}\tilde{\phi}_{0})^{2}]
,

\tilde{\mathcal{P}}=\tilde{\pi}^{0}_{0}\partial_{1}\tilde{\phi}_{0}
. For the transformation
not to mix the zero and non-zero mode sectors, the middle term has to vanish,
which is the case if
\partial_{1}\xi^{\mu}=0
. Because of the conformal Killing
equations (
A.2
), this requires the
\xi^{\mu}
to be constants so that
the transformations are infinitesimal translations.

In the general case, when expressed in terms of the chiral
fields, the charge becomes

Q_{\xi}={\sqrt{2}}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}[\xi^{+}(\partial_{1}\phi^{L})^{2}+\xi^{-}(\partial_{1}\phi^{R})^{2}]+\frac{\sqrt{2}\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}}{L}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}[\xi^{+}\partial_{1}\phi^{L}-\xi^{-}\partial_{1}\phi^{R}]\\
+\frac{(\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0})^{2}}{2L^{2}}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{1}\xi^{0}.

(A.55)

The transformation of
\phi^{L},\phi^{R}
, which only involve
\xi^{+}
and
\xi^{-}
respectively,
can then be obtained by using (
A.47
).

A.5
Mode expansions and partition function

Let us choose initial data at
x^{0}=0
and define

k=\frac{2\pi n}{L}=-k_{1},\quad k_{0}=|k|,\quad k_{\mu}x^{\mu}=|k|x^{0}-kx^{1}.

(A.56)

The standard mode expansion for the on-shell
fields and momenta is

\begin{split}\phi(x^{0},x^{1})=\bar{\phi}_{0}(0)+\frac{x^{0}}{L}\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}(0)+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\Big[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2|k|L}}a_{k}e^{-ik_{\mu}x^{\mu}}+{\rm c.c.}\Big],\\
\pi(x^{0},x^{1})=\frac{1}{L}\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}(0)-i\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\Big[\sqrt{\frac{|k|}{2L}}a_{k}e^{-ik_{\mu}x^{\mu}}-{\rm c.c.}\Big].\end{split}

(A.57)

In terms of oscillators, the equal
x^{0}
time canonical Poisson brackets
(
A.34
) are given by

\begin{split}\{a_{k},a^{*}_{k^{\prime}}\}_{0}&=-i\delta_{n,n^{\prime}},\ \{a_{k},a_{k^{\prime}}\}_{0}=0=\{a^{*}_{k},a^{*}_{k^{\prime}}\}_{0},\\
\{\bar{\phi}_{0},\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}\}_{0}&=1,\ \{\bar{\phi}_{0},\bar{\phi}_{0}\}_{0}=0=\{\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0},\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}\}_{0}.\end{split}

(A.58)

From (
A.38
), it follows that the expansions of the derivatives of the
on-shell chiral fields are

\partial_{1}\phi^{R}=i\sum_{n>0}\sqrt{\frac{k}{2L}}(a_{k}e^{ik(x^{1}-x^{0})}-{\rm c.c.}),\quad\partial_{1}\phi^{L}=-i\sum_{n>0}\sqrt{\frac{k}{2L}}(\tilde{a}_{k}e^{-ik(x^{1}+x^{0})}-{\rm c.c.}),

(A.59)

where
\tilde{a}_{k}=a_{-k}
. One may now integrate, which determines
\phi^{R},\phi^{L}

up to functions of time
f^{R}(x^{0}),f^{L}(x^{0})
. These functions have to vanish because
the chiral fields have been defined so as not to contain an
x^{1}
zero mode. Hence,

\phi^{R}(x^{-})=\sum_{n>0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi n}}(a_{k}e^{-ik_{-}x^{-}}+{\rm c.c.}),\quad\phi^{L}(x^{+})=\sum_{n>0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi n}}(\tilde{a}_{k}e^{-ik_{+}x^{+}}+{\rm c.c.}),

(A.60)

where

x^{\pm}=2^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x^{0}\pm x^{1}),\quad L_{\pm}=\frac{L}{\sqrt{2}},\quad k_{\pm}=\frac{2\pi n}{L_{\pm}}.

(A.61)

This also follows directly from the definition (
A.36
). In particular,
having extracted the
x^{1}
zero mode from the chiral fields uniquely fixes the
prescription for the action of
\partial_{1}^{-1}
on those fields.
As a consequence, the on-shell field may be decomposed as

\phi(x^{0},x^{1})=\bar{\phi}_{0}(0)+\frac{x^{0}}{L}\bar{\pi}^{0}_{0}(0)+\phi^{R}(x^{-})+\phi^{L}(x^{+}).

(A.62)

One then recovers from the mode expansions and (
A.58
) the equal
x^{0}
time
Poisson brackets of the chiral fields,

\begin{split}\{\phi^{R}(x^{1}),\phi^{R}(y^{1})\}_{0}&=\sum_{n>0}\frac{\sin{k(x^1-y^1)}}{2\pi n}=-\{\phi^{L}(x^{1}),\phi^{L}(y^{1})\}_{0},\\
\{\phi^{L}(x^{1}),\phi^{R}(y^{1})\}_{0}&=0,\end{split}

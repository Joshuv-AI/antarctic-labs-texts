Cartan subgroups in connected locally compact groups
Arunava Mandal, Riddhi Shah

Abstract
. We define Cartan subgroups in connected locally compact groups, which extends the classical notion of Cartan subgroups in Lie groups. We prove their existence and justify our choice of the definition which differs from the one given by Chevalley on general groups. Apart from proving some properties of Cartan subgroups, we show that the Cartan subgroups of the quotient groups are precisely the images of Cartan subgroups of the ambient group. We establish the so-called ‘Levi’ decomposition of Cartan subgroups which extends Wüstner’s decomposition theorem and our earlier results about the same proven for Lie groups. We also show that the centraliser of any maximal torus of the radical is connected and its Cartan subgroups are also the Cartan subgroup of the ambient group; moreover, every Cartan subgroup arises this way. We prove that Cartan subalgebras defined by Hofmann and Morris in pro-Lie algebras are the same as those corresponding to Cartan subgroups in case of pro-Lie algebras of connected locally compact groups, and that they are nilpotent. We characterise density of the image of a power map in the group in terms of its surjectivity on all Cartan subgroups, and show that weak exponentiality of the group is equivalent to the condition that all its Cartan subgroups are connected.

Introduction

A Cartan subgroup is a classical object in Lie theory
defined by C. Chevalley
[
Ch
]
in 1955. Chevalley defined a purely intrinsic notion of the
Cartan subgroups
C
of an abstract group
G
: It is a maximal nilpotent subgroup of
G
with
the additional property that every subgroup which is normal and of finite index in
C
is also of
finite index in its own normaliser in
G
. It is shown in
[
Ch
]
that the Cartan subgroups of a
Zariski connected algebraic group
G
are exactly those irreducible algebraic subgroups of
G

whose Lie algebras are the Cartan subalgebras of the Lie algebras of
G
. Note that Catran subgroups
of a connected Lie group
G
are closed in
G
, but need not be connected, and their Lie algebras
are the Cartan subalgebras of the Lie algebra of
G
; more precisely, each Cartan subalgebra is
the Lie algebra of exactly one Cartan subgroup. The structural aspect of Cartan subgroups have
been studied extensively in algebraic groups or Lie groups by Borel
[
Bo
]
, Goto
[
G
]
,
Togo
[
T
]
, Prasad, Raghunathan
[
PR
]
and many others. Cartan subgroups also play
a major role in the study of weak exponentiality and power maps
(see for e.g.
[
HoMu
]
,
[
N
]
,
[
Wu1
]
,
[
DM
]
,
[
BM
]
,
[
M1
]
,
[
M2
]
,
[
MS
]
).

In this article, we explore the appropriate notion of Cartan subgroups in a connected locally
compact group which is consistent with the well-established definition of Cartan subgroups in a
connected Lie group. Our definition of Cartan subgroups slightly differs from Chevalley’s definition
mentioned in the beginning. To put it in a suitable framework, let us recall the other well-known
definition of Cartan subgroups in connected Lie groups given in
[
N
]
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group with the Lie algebra
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathcal{G}}
, i.e. it is a (maximal) nilpotent Lie
subalgebra which coincides with its own normaliser. Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{C}}

(resp.
{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathbb{C}}
) be the complexification of
{\mathcal{C}}
(resp.
{\mathcal{G}}
)
and let
\Delta
be the set of roots of
{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathbb{C}}
belonging to
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Then
{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{C}}+\Sigma_{\alpha\in\Delta}{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}
. Recall that

N_{G}({\mathcal{C}}):=\{g\in G\mid{\rm Ad}(g)({\mathcal{C}})={\mathcal{C}}\}.

Definition 1.1

(
[
N
]
)
.

A closed subgroup
C
of a Lie group
G
is called a Cartan subgroup if the following hold:

{\rm(I)}

The Lie algebra of
C
, denoted
by
{\mathcal{C}}
, is a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathcal{G}}
.

{\rm(II)}

C({\mathcal{C}}):=\{g\in N_{G}({\mathcal{C}})\mid\alpha\circ{\rm Ad}(g)|_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathbb{C}}}=\alpha\mbox{ for all }\alpha\in\Delta\}=C.

Definition
1.1
is equivalent to the definition given by Chevalley on any connected Lie group
[
N
]
. The absence of
root space decomposition in a connected locally compact group is a primary obstruction to extending Definition
1.1
, whereas,
apriori, there is no problem to consider Chevalley’s definition of Cartan subgroups as it is in the context of connected locally compact groups,
since it is purely group-theoretic. However, Chevalley’s definition does not work for even compact connected (non-Lie) groups as illustrated
by Example
2.6
below. This suggests that Chevalley’s definition of Cartan subgroups needs to be modified
in case of general connected locally compact groups. In this framework, we define Cartan subgroup as
a maximal nilpotent subgroup, with the modified condition that for any closed normal subgroup
L
of
C
, if
C/L
is
compact and totally disconnected, then
N_{G}(L)/L
is compact and totally disconnected (see Definition
2.1
).

First, we show the existence of Cartan
subgroups in a connected locally compact group
G
(see Theorem
2.5
). We prove that every
Cartan subgroup
C
is a projective limit of Cartan subgroups in Lie groups (see Proposition
3.1
). There is a notion of
Cartan subalgebras of a pro-Lie algebra introduced by Hofmann and Morris
[
HoMo2
]
which are pro-nilpotent. Here, we prove
that the Lie algebra of Cartan subgroup is a Cartan sublagebra of the pro-Lie algebra of the connected locally compact group.
In fact, we show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between Cartan subalgebras and Cartan subgroups (see Theorem

7.1
). This implies in particular that the Cartan subalgebras in this case are nilpotent.

It is well known that a connected locally compact group
G
is a projective limit of connected Lie groups, i.e.
G=\varprojlim_{\alpha}G/K_{\alpha}
, where
K_{\alpha}
’s are compact normal subgroups of
G
with

\cap_{\alpha}K_{\alpha}=\{e\}
and each
G/K_{\alpha}
is a Lie group. Therefore,
one expects to extend various structural results about Cartan subgroups from Lie groups to all connected locally compact groups.
Here, we obtain various characterisation of Cartan subgroups, extending our recent results obtained in
[
MS
]
. One would
expect to prove results on such groups

by a technique of passing through the limit and using the results in Lie groups at hand.
Although this technique works in some cases, however, to a large extent, it does not help to prove many of our results. Instead,
we need to use various structural results on connected locally compact groups, Levi decompositions, and reduction processes.

As in the case of connected Lie groups, Cartan subgroups of a connected locally compact group which is a compact extension
of a closed connected solvable normal subgroup turn out to be connected, and conjugate to each other. For compact groups,
they are precisely the maximal connected abelian subgroups (see Proposition
3.3
). Unlike in the case of Lie groups,
a connected locally compact group

can be an arbitrary product of nontrivial connected locally compact groups

G_{\alpha}

of

is again a Cartan subgroup of

is of this form
(see Proposition

Cartan subgroups in the quotient groups. More precisely, if

with the additional property that
(i)
elements of

,

(ii)

of

Moreover, we prove that

We also characterise Cartan subgroups
of

, where

is any maximal compact subgroup of

R
in

is the centraliser of

in

We show that each Cartan subgroup of

As an application, we give a characterisation of the density of the image of a power map on a locally compact group

2.
Cartan subgroups in a connected locally compact group

In this section, we define the notion of Cartan subgroups in the context of connected locally compact groups.
We also prove the existence of Cartan subgroups in a connected locally compact group.

Definition 2.1
.

A subgroup
C
of a connected locally compact group
G
is said to be a Cartan subgroup of
G
if the following conditions
are satisfied:

{\rm(I)}

C
is a maximal nilpotent subgroup of
G
.

{\rm(II)}

If
L
is any closed normal subgroup of
C
such that

C/L
is compact and totally disconnected, then

N_{G}(L)/L
is compact and totally disconnected.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that if
G
is a connected Lie group, then the quotients
C/L
and
N_{G}(L)/L
are Lie groups and any
compact totally disconnected Lie group is discrete and finite. Therefore, Definition

2.1
for Cartan subgroups extends the one given by
Chevalley in the case of Lie groups. We will also show in Proposition
3.1
, that in a connected
locally compact group, every Cartan subgroup arises as a projective limit of Cartan subgroups of Lie groups.
Example
2.6
will justify our choice of condition
(II)
for Cartan subgroups in connected locally compact

(
non-Lie
)
groups, as in a compact connected infinite dimensional group
G
, which is a product of
certain semisimple Lie groups, the maximal nilpotent group is a compact connected infinite dimensional
abelian group which has infinite index in its normaliser and the quotient is totally disconnected.

As the closure of a nilpotent group is a nilpotent group, every Cartan subgroup is closed. Since it is a maximal nilpotent group in
G
,
it contains the center
Z(G)
of
G
.

We will prove the existence of a Cartan subgroup in a connected locally compact group. Before that we prove some preliminary
results which are needed for this purpose.
The following lemma is known in the case of connected Lie groups.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact group. Let
Z
be a closed central subgroup of
G
and let
C
be a Cartan
subgroup of
G
. Then the following hold:

(1)

Z\subset C
.

(2)

C/Z
is a Cartan subgroup of
G/Z
. Conversely, for any subgroup
C^{\prime}
which contains
Z
, if
C^{\prime}/Z
is a
Cartan subgroup of
G/Z
, then
C^{\prime}
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
.

(3)

Z_{n}\subset C
and
C/Z_{n}
is a Cartan subgroup of

of

Z_{0}=Z(G)
,

and
Z_{n+1}/Z_{n}
is the center of
G/Z_{n}
,

Proof.

(1):
Since
C
is a maximal nilpotent group,
Z\subset Z(G)\subset C
.

(2):
Let
C

is of the form

, where

,

. Suppose that

, being isomorphic to

, is also compact and totally disconnected, and hence

N_{G}(L)/L
. Therefore,
\pi(N_{G}(L))/\pi(L)
is compact and totally disconnected. Since

,
we have
\pi(N_{G}(L))=N_{G_{1}}(L_{1})
. Thus
N_{G/Z}(L_{1})/L_{1}
is compact and totally disconnected.
This proves that
C/Z
is a Cartan subgroup in

.

Conversely, suppose
C^{\prime}
contains
Z
and
C^{\prime}/Z
is a Cartan subgroup of
G/Z
. Then it is easy to see that
C^{\prime}

is a maximal nilpotent subgroup of

. Let

be a closed normal subgroup of

such that

is compact
and totally disconnected. Then
\overline{LZ}
is a closed normal subgroup of

and
C^{\prime}/\overline{LZ}
, being a quotient
group of
C^{\prime}/L
, is compact totally disconnected. Moreover,

is
isomorphic to
(C^{\prime}/Z)/(\overline{LZ}/Z)
. Since
C^{\prime}/Z
is a Cartan subgroup of

, we get that

N_{G/Z}(\overline{LZ}/Z)/\overline{(LZ}/Z)
is compact and totally disconnected.
Since

and
\overline{LZ}\subset N_{G}(L)\subset N_{G}(\overline{LZ})
,
we get that
N_{G}(\overline{LZ})/\overline{LZ}
, and hence
N_{G}(L)/\overline{LZ}
is compact and totally disconnected.
As
\overline{LZ}/L
, being a subset of

, is compact and totally disconnected, so is

. This proves that

is a Cartan subgroup of

.

(3):
It follows easily from (1) and (2).
∎

]
and some simple observations.
For any subgroup

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

be a connected locally compact group and let

be a compact normal subgroup of

.
Then the following hold:

(i)

Z(K)\subset Z(G)
,
Z(K^{0})\subset Z(G)
and
Z(D(K^{0}))\subset Z(G)
.

(ii)

G=Z_{G}(K)K=Z_{G}(K)K^{0}=Z_{G}^{0}(K)K^{0}=Z^{0}_{G}(K)D(K^{0})
.

(iii)

G=Z_{G}(K^{0})K^{0}=Z_{G}^{0}(K^{0})K^{0}=Z_{G}(D(K^{0}))D(K^{0})

G=Z_{G}(K^{0})K^{0}=Z_{G}^{0}(K^{0})K^{0}

=Z^{0}_{G}(D(K^{0}))D(K^{0})
.

(iv)

K=Z(K)D(K^{0})
.

(v)

Z_{G}(K)=Z_{G}^{0}(K)Z(K)=Z_{G}^{0}(K^{0})Z(K^{0})=Z_{G}^{0}(K)Z(D(K^{0}))

Z_{G}(K)=Z_{G}^{0}(K)Z(K)=Z_{G}^{0}(K^{0})Z(K^{0})

=Z_{G}^{0}(D(K^{0}))Z(D(K^{0}))

(vi)

is a connected semisimple group with trivial center.

(vii)

R\subset Z_{G}^{0}(K)
where

Proof.

(i):
This follows from Theorem 4 of
[

]
since

,

,

.

(ii):
Theorem 2 of

]

. Note that as

is normal in

, so is

and

. As

is connected, so is

, and hence

. Moreover,

is also connected, and we get that

.
We know from Lemma 2.4 of

]

).

(iii):
The proof follows in the same way as in (ii), using Theorem 2 of

]
and the facts that

and

are compact normal subgroups of

(iv):

(vi):

Existence of Cartan subgroups:

It is well-known that Cartan subgroups exist in connected Lie groups. We now prove the existence of Cartan subgroups in
connected locally compact groups as in Definition
2.1
. Note again that this definition agrees with
the original definition of Cartan subgroups in case of connected Lie groups. We also justify the choice of conditions in
the definition by an example.

Theorem 2.5
.

Every connected locally compact group admits a Cartan subgroup.

Proof.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact group. Then
G
admits a unique maximal compact normal subgroup
K
such that

G/K
is a Lie group. By Lemma
2.4
(vi),
K/Z(K)
is a connected semisimple group with trivial center. By
Lemma
2.4
(i),
Z(K)
is central in
G
, and hence by Lemma
2.3
, we may replace
G
by

G/Z(K)
and
K
by
K/Z(K)
, and assume that
Z(K)
is trivial. Now
K=K^{0}=D(K^{0})
is connected and has
trivial center, and
G=Z^{0}_{G}(K)\times K
(cf. Lemma
2.4
(ii)). Note that,
Z^{0}_{G}(K)
is a connected Lie group
as
Z^{0}_{G}(K)
has no nontrivial compact normal subgroups. Let
C_{0}
be a Cartan subgroup in
Z^{0}_{G}(K)
.
Since
K
is a connected semisimple compact group with trivial center, by Theorem 9.19 (iv) of
[
HoMo1
]
,

K=\Pi_{d}K_{d}
, where each
K_{d}
is a compact connected simple Lie group with trivial center. Now

G=Z^{0}_{G}(K)\times\Pi_{d}K_{d}
. Let
C_{d}
be a Cartan subgroup of
K_{d}
for each
d
. By Proposition 6 of
[
W
]
,
each
C_{d}
is compact, connected and abelian, and hence so is

C^{\prime}:=\Pi_{d}C_{d}
. Let
C=C_{0}\times C^{\prime}=C_{0}\times\Pi_{d}C_{d}
, where

C_{0}
as above is a Cartan subgroup of the Lie group
Z_{G}^{0}(K)
. We show that
C
satisfies conditions (I) and (II)
and hence it is a Cartan subgroup of
G
. As
C_{0}
is nilpotent and
C^{\prime}
is abelian, it follows that
C
is nilpotent.
Let
\pi_{0}:G\to Z^{0}_{G}(K)
and
\pi_{d}:G\to K_{d}
be natural projections. Then
\pi_{0}
and each
\pi_{d}
are open
continuous group homomorphisms and
G=\pi_{0}(G)\times\Pi_{d}\pi_{d}(G)
. Now
C
is a
maximal nilpotent group as
\pi_{0}(C)
and each
\pi_{d}(C)
are so in
\pi_{0}(G)
and
K_{d}
respectively. That is,

C
satisfies condition (I).

Let
L
be a closed normal subgroup in
C
such that
C/L
is compact and totally disconnected. Then

C^{0}=C_{0}^{0}\times C^{\prime}=L^{0}
, and it follows that
L=\pi_{0}(L)\times C^{\prime}
. Therefore,
C/L
is isomorphic to
a subgroup of
C_{0}/C_{0}^{0}
which is discrete, and hence
C/L
is finite.
Now we get that
C_{0}/\pi_{0}(L)
is finite. Hence
N_{\pi_{0}(G)}(\pi_{0}(L))/\pi_{0}(L)
is finite. Also,

\pi_{d}(L)=\pi_{d}(C^{0})=C_{d}
for each
d
. Therefore,
N_{K_{d}}(\pi_{d}(L))/\pi_{d}(L)=N_{K_{d}}(C_{d})/C_{d}
is finite, and
hence
N^{0}_{K}(C^{\prime})=C^{\prime}
and
N^{0}_{G}(L)\cap K=C^{\prime}
. Now
N_{G}(L)=N_{\pi_{0}(G)}(\pi_{0}(L))\times\Pi_{d}N_{K_{d}}(C_{d})
,
and it follows that
N_{G}(L)K/LK
is isomorphic to
N_{\pi_{0}(G)}(\pi_{0}(L))/\pi_{0}(L)
which is finite. As
K
is compact,
the preceding assertion implies that
N_{G}(L)/L
is compact and also that

N^{0}_{G}(L)\subset L^{0}(N^{0}_{G}(L)\cap K)=L^{0}C^{\prime}=L^{0}
. Therefore,
N_{G}(L)/L
is totally disconnected,
i.e.
C
satisfies condition (II) and
C
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
.
∎

We now give an example of a connected compact group
G
, whose Cartan subgroups
C
are connected, but
N_{G}(C)/C
is (compact)
totally disconnected and infinite. The example justifies the choice of conditions in the definition of Cartan subgroups in a
general connected locally compact group. Note that a maximal torus in a compact connected group is a maximal connected
abelian subgroup.

Example 2.6
.

Take a compact connected Lie group
K
and a maximal torus
T
such that
N_{K}(T)/T
is nontrivial and finite.
Many such groups exist, for example one can take
K=U(n)
, a unitary group in
{\rm GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
and

T=U(1)\times\cdots\times U(1)

(n
-copies
)
for any
n\geq 2
. Then
N_{K}(T)/T
is isomorphic to
S_{n}

(
the permutation group of
n
elements
)
. Recall that
T
is a Cartan subgroup of
K
. Let
G
be an infinite
countable product of
K
. Then it is easy to see that if
C
is an infinite countable product of
T
, then
C
is a
Cartan subgroup of
G

(
see also Proposition
4.2
)
. Here,
N_{G}(C)/C
is an infinite compact totally disconnected group.

3.
Some properties of Cartan subgroups

In this section, we establish a relation between Cartan subgroups in a connected locally compact group and those of its
quotient groups modulo compact normal subgroups in Proposition
3.1
(1) below; we will later generalise it for
quotient groups modulo any closed normal subgroups; see Theorem
4.1
. We also show in Proposition
3.1
(2a)
below that in a connected locally compact group, every Cartan subgroup arises as a projective limit of Cartan subgroups
of Lie groups. Proposition
3.1
(2b) will be useful in the proof of Theorem
7.1
.
We also list and prove some properties related to the structure of Cartan subgroups which will be useful.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact group. Then the following hold:

(1)

Let
K
be any compact normal subgroup of
G
. If
C
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
, then
CK/K

is a Cartan subgroup of
G/K
. Conversely, given a Cartan subgroup
C^{\prime}
in
G/K
, there exists a Cartan
subgroup
C
in
G
such that
CK/K=C^{\prime}
.

(2)

Let
G=\varprojlim_{\alpha}G/K_{\alpha}
, a projective limit of Lie groups
G_{\alpha}:=G/K_{\alpha}
, where

\{K_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
is a collection of compact normal subgroups of
G
such that
\cap_{\alpha\in\Lambda}K_{\alpha}=\{e\}
. Let
\pi_{\alpha}:G\to G_{\alpha}
be the
natural projection,
\alpha\in\Lambda
. Then the following hold:

(a)

C
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
if and only if
\pi_{\alpha}(C)
is a Cartan subgroup of
G_{\alpha}
for
each
\alpha\in\Lambda
.

(b)

For every pair
\alpha,\beta
in
\Lambda
such that
K_{\alpha}\subset K_{\beta}
,
let
\pi_{\alpha\beta}:G_{\alpha}\to G_{\beta}
be the natural quotient map. Suppose
C_{\alpha}
is a Cartan subgroup of

G_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
such that
\pi_{\alpha\beta}(C_{\alpha})=C_{\beta}
for all pairs
\alpha,\beta
as above.
Then
C=\cap_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\,\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(C_{\alpha})
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
and
\pi_{\alpha}(C)=C_{\alpha}

for every
\alpha\in\Lambda
.

Proof.

(1):
Let
C
be a Cartan subgroup of
G
. By Lemma
2.4
(i, vi),
Z(K)
is central in
G
and the center of

K/Z(K)
is trivial. As

is isomorphic to
(G/Z(K))/(K/Z(K))
, by Lemma
2.3
, we may replace

G
by
G/Z(K)
and
K
by
K/Z(K)
, and assume that
Z(K)
is trivial. By Lemma
2.4
(vi), we have that

K=D(K^{0})
is connected (with trivial center), and
G=Z^{0}_{G}(K)\times K
. It is easy to see that any Cartan
subgroup
C
of

is of the form
C=C_{1}\times C_{2}
, where
C_{1}
is a Cartan subgroup of
Z_{G}^{0}(K)
and

C_{2}
is a Cartan subgroup of
K
. Hence
CK/K
, being isomorphic to
C_{1}
, is a Cartan subgroup of
G/K
.

Conversely, let
C^{\prime}
be a Cartan subgroup of
G/K
. By Lemma
2.4
,
Z(K)
is central in
G
. By
Lemma
2.3
, it is enough to prove the statement for
G/Z(K)
and
K/Z(K)
(instead of
G
and
K
).
Hence we may replace
G
by
G/Z(K)
and
K
by
K/Z(K)
, and assume
that
K=D(K^{0})
is connected and has trivial center. By Lemma
2.4
,

G=Z_{G}^{0}(K)\times K
. Let
C_{1}=\pi^{-1}(C^{\prime})\cap Z_{G}^{0}(K)
. where

\pi:G\to G/K
is the natural projection. As the restriction of
\pi
to
Z_{G}^{0}(K)
is a homeomorphism onto
G/K
and
\pi(C_{1})=C^{\prime}
,
we get that
C_{1}
is a Cartan subgroup of
Z_{G}^{0}(K)
. Let
C:=C_{1}\times C_{2}
, where

C_{2}
is any Cartan subgroup of
K
. It is easy to see that
C
is a Cartan subgroup of
G

and
CK/K=\pi(C)=\pi(C_{1})=C^{\prime}
.

(2\mbox{a}):
Let
C
be a Cartan subgroup of
G
. Then by
(1)
, we have
\pi_{\alpha}(C)
is a Cartan subgroup of

\pi_{\alpha}(G)
for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
.

Conversely, let
C
be a subgroup of
G
such that
\pi_{\alpha}(C)
is a Cartan subgroup of
\pi_{\alpha}(G)

for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
. As
\pi_{\alpha}(C)
is a maximal nilpotent group in
G_{\alpha}
, it contains
Z(G_{\alpha})
, the
center of
G_{\alpha}
. As
\pi_{\alpha}(Z(G))\subset Z(G_{\alpha})
for each
\alpha
, we get that
Z(G)\subset C
.

Let
K
be the maximal compact normal subgroup of
G
. By Lemma
2.4
, we have that
Z(K)\subset Z(G)
and by
Lemma
2.3
, it is enough to prove that
C/Z(K)
is a Cartan subgroup of
G/Z(K)
. By Lemma
2.4
(vi).

K/Z(K)
has trivial center, and hence
G/Z(K)
has no nontrivial compact central subgroup. We also get from (1) that for
each
\alpha
,
(CK_{\alpha}Z(K))/(K_{\alpha}Z(K)
) is a
Cartan subgroup of
G/(K_{\alpha}Z(K))
. Now we may replace
G
by
G/Z(K)
and
K_{\alpha}
by
(K_{\alpha}Z(K))/Z(K)

and assume that the maximal compact normal subgroup of

(which we denote by

again) has trivial center.
By Lemma
2.4
,
K=D(K^{0})
is connected and has trivial center and

.
As
K_{\alpha}\subset K
and
K
is connected, we get that

Z(K_{\alpha})\subset Z(K)=\{e\}
for each
\alpha
. Therefore,
K_{\alpha}
has trivial center and

K_{\alpha}
are connected (cf. Lemma
2.4
(vi)). Now
K=Z^{0}_{K}(K_{\alpha})\times K_{\alpha}
and we get that

G=Z_{G}^{0}(K)\times K=Z_{G}^{0}(K)\times K^{\prime}_{\alpha}\times K_{\alpha},

where
K^{\prime}_{\alpha}:=Z^{0}_{K}(K_{\alpha})
is a connected semisimple Lie group with trivial center for each
\alpha
.
Therefore,
\pi_{\alpha}(G)
is isomorphic to the direct product
Z_{G}^{0}(K)\times K^{\prime}_{\alpha}
, and hence

\pi_{\alpha}(C)=C_{\alpha}
is isomorphic to
C^{\prime}\times C^{\prime}_{\alpha}
, where
C^{\prime}
(resp.
C^{\prime}_{\alpha}
) is a Cartan subgroup of
Z_{G}^{0}(K)

(resp.
K^{\prime}_{\alpha}
). As

is semisimple and has trivial center, as shown in the proof of Theorem
2.5
,

C^{\prime}_{\alpha}
is abelian. Therefore, we have that the length of the central series of each

is same as that of
C^{\prime}
.
This implies that

is nilpotent. It is easy to see that

is a maximal nilpotent group in

, since each
\pi_{\alpha}(C)
is
so in
G_{\alpha}
. Suppose
L\subset C
is a closed
normal subgroup of

such that

is compact and totally disconnected. Then for each
\alpha

\pi_{\alpha}(C)/\pi_{\alpha}(L)
is isomorphic to

, which is a quotient of

.
Therefore
\pi_{\alpha}(C)/\pi_{\alpha}(L)
is compact and totally disconnected, and being a Lie group, it is finite. Hence,

N_{G_{\alpha}}(\pi_{\alpha}(L))/\pi_{\alpha}(L)
is finite. Since
\pi_{\alpha}(N_{G}(L))\subset N_{G_{\alpha}}(\pi_{\alpha}(L))
, we get that

\pi_{\alpha}(N_{G}(L))/\pi_{\alpha}(L)

.
Therefore,

is compact and

. Hence

and

is a Cartan subgroup of

.

(2
b
): Let
\{C_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
and
C=\cap_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\,\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(C_{\alpha})
be as in the hypothesis.
As each
C_{\alpha}
is a Cartan subgroup of
G_{\alpha}
, to show that
C
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
, by (2a),
it is enough to show that
\pi_{\alpha}(C)=C_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
. Let
\alpha\in\Lambda
. By (1), there exists a Cartan
subgroup
C^{\prime}_{\alpha}
in
G
such that
\pi_{\alpha}(C^{\prime}_{\alpha})=C_{\alpha}
. By Lemma
2.4
, we have that
G=Z_{\alpha}K^{0}_{\alpha}
, with

Z_{\alpha}=Z^{0}_{G}(K_{\alpha})
. Here
Z_{\alpha}
and
K^{0}_{\alpha}
are normal in
G
and they centralise each other, and

Z_{\alpha}\cap K^{0}_{\alpha}\subset Z(K^{0}_{\alpha})\subset Z(G)
. Hence we get that
C^{\prime}_{\alpha}=C_{\alpha 1}C_{\alpha 2}
, where

C_{\alpha 1}=C^{\prime}_{\alpha}\cap Z_{\alpha}
(resp.
C_{\alpha 2}=C^{\prime}_{\alpha}\cap K^{0}_{\alpha}
) is a Cartan subgroup of
Z_{\alpha}
(resp.
K^{0}_{\alpha}
).
Since
C^{\prime}_{\alpha}K_{\alpha}=C_{\alpha 1}K_{\alpha}
and since
C_{\alpha 1}\cap K_{\alpha}\subset Z(K_{\alpha})\subset Z(G)
, the following holds:
For any Cartan subgroup
L
of
G
such that
\pi_{\alpha}(L)=C_{\alpha}
, we get that
L\cap Z_{\alpha}=C_{\alpha 1}
.

We put a natural order on the index set
\Lambda
as follows: For
\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda
,
\alpha\leq\beta
if

K_{\alpha}\subset K_{\beta}
. Let
\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda
be such that
\alpha\leq\beta
. Then
\pi_{\alpha\beta}\circ\pi_{\alpha}=\pi_{\beta}
and

\pi_{\beta}(C^{\prime}_{\alpha})=\pi_{\alpha\beta}\pi_{\alpha}(C^{\prime}_{\alpha})=\pi_{\alpha\beta}(C_{\alpha})=C_{\beta}
. Hence we get that

C^{\prime}_{\alpha}\cap Z_{\beta}=C_{\beta 1}
, i.e.
C_{\beta 1}\subset C^{\prime}_{\alpha}
.
Moreover,
C^{\prime}_{\alpha}K_{\alpha}\subset C^{\prime}_{\alpha}K_{\beta}=C^{\prime}_{\beta}K_{\beta}
,
i.e.
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(C_{\alpha})\subset\pi_{\beta}^{-1}(C_{\beta})
. This implies that
C=\cap_{\alpha\leq\beta}\,\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(C_{\alpha})

for each
\beta\in\Lambda
.
Now as
C_{\beta 1}\subset C^{\prime}_{\alpha}
for each

, we get that
C_{\beta 1}\subset C
. Therefore,

C^{\prime}_{\beta}K_{\beta}=C_{\beta 1}K_{\beta}\subset CK_{\beta}
. Conversely,
CK_{\beta}\subset\pi_{\beta}^{-1}(C_{\beta})=C^{\prime}_{\beta}K_{\beta}
.
Therefore,
\pi_{\beta}(C)=C_{\beta}
for each

is a Cartan subgroup of

by (2a).
∎

In a connected locally compact group
G
, there exists a largest (closed) connected nilpotent normal subgroup which is known
as the nilradical of
G
. Note that the maximal compact (central) subgroup
T
of the nilradical
N
of
G
is connected and
central in
G
and
N/T
is simply connected and nilpotent. Hence, as in section 3 of
[

]
, one can have a recipe for
constructing a Cartan subgroup containing a nilpotent subgroup which complements the nilradical in some sense in a connected
locally compact solvable group. However, one can easily deduce the following directly by using the same result on Lie groups
in
[
MS
]
and Proposition
3.1
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact solvable group and let
N
be the nilradical of
G
.
If
L
is a (closed) nilpotent subgroup of
G
such that
G=LN
. Then there exists a Cartan subgroup
C
of
G

such that
L\subset C
.

Proof.

Let
K
be the maximal compact normal subgroup of
G
. Since
G
is solvable and Lie projective, it is easy to see that

K
central in
G
, and hence, it is contained in every Cartan subgroup of
G
. Let
\pi:G\to G/K
be the natural
projection. Then
G/K
is a Lie group, with
\pi(N)
as its nilradical and
G/K=\pi(L)\pi(N)
, where
\pi(L)
is nilpotent.
Now by Proposition 3.1 of
[
MS
]
,
\pi(L)
is contained in a Cartan subgroup
(say)
C^{\prime}
of
G/K
. Therefore,
L\subset LK\subset\pi^{-1}(C^{\prime}):=C
which is a Cartan subgroup of
G
by
Lemma
2.3
(2).
∎

Note that Cartan subgroups need not be connected and any conjugate of a Cartan subgroup is also a Cartan subgroup.
The following is known for connected Lie groups.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact group such that
G/R
is compact, where
R
is the solvable radical of
G
.
Then its Cartan subgroups are connected and conjugate to each other. Moreover, if
G
is compact, then its Cartan
subgroups are precisely the maximal connected abelian subgroups.

Proof.

As
G
is Lie projective, we have compact normal subgroups
\{K_{\alpha}\}
and natural projections
\pi_{\alpha}:G\to G/K_{\alpha}

with the property that
G/K_{\alpha}
are Lie groups and
G=\varprojlim_{\alpha}G/K_{\alpha}
.
Let
C
be a Cartan subgroup of
G
. By Proposition
3.1
,
\pi_{\alpha}(C)
is a Cartan subgroup of
\pi_{\alpha}(G)
for each
\alpha
.
Note that
C=\varprojlim_{\alpha}\pi_{\alpha}(C)
.
Now
\pi_{\alpha}(R)
is the radical of
G_{\alpha}
and the condition on
G
implies that
G_{\alpha}/\pi_{\alpha}(R)
is compact,
and hence
\pi_{\alpha}(C)
is connected for each
\alpha
(cf.
[
W
]
, Proposition 6). Hence
C
is connected.

Now suppose
G
is compact. Then so is
G_{\alpha}
, and
\pi_{\alpha}(C)
is a maximal connected abelian subgroup of
\pi_{\alpha}(G)
for
each
\alpha
, and hence
C
is a maximal (connected) abelian subgroup of
G
. Conversely, it is easy to see that if
A
is a
maximal connected abelian subgroup in
G
, then so is
\pi_{\alpha}(A)
in
\pi_{\alpha}(G)
, and it is a Cartan
subgroup of
\pi_{\alpha}(G)
for each
\alpha
. By Proposition
3.1
(2a),
A
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
.

Now we prove that all that Cartan subgroups of such a
G
are conjugate. This holds in case
G
as above is a Lie group
(cf.
[
W
]
, Proposition 6). Now suppose
G
is not a Lie group.
Note that all the conjugates of
C
are also Cartan subgroups of
G
. Let
C^{\prime}
be any Cartan subgroup of
G
. We will
show that it is conjugate to
C
. Let
K
be the largest compact normal subgroup of
G
. Then
K
is nontrivial and
K=Z(K)D(K^{0})

(see Lemma
2.4
(iv)). Since
Z(K)\subset Z(G)
is contained in all Cartan subgroups, by
Lemma
2.3
, we can replace
G
by
G/Z(K)
and
K
by
K/Z(K)
and assume that

G=Z^{0}_{G}(K)\times K
,
K=D(K^{0})=\Pi_{d}(R_{d})
, where
Z^{0}_{G}(K)
is a connected Lie group and each
R_{d}
is a compact
simple Lie group with a trivial center. Now
C=C_{1}\times C_{2}
and
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}_{1}\times C^{\prime}_{2}
where
C_{1}
and
C^{\prime}_{1}

(resp.
C_{2}
and
C^{\prime}_{2}
) are Cartan subgroups of
Z^{0}_{G}(K)
(resp.
K
). As the Cartan subgroups of

are product
of Cartan subgroups of connected compact Lie groups

, we have that
C_{2}
and
C^{\prime}_{2}
are conjugate. Now the

of

is contained in

(cf. Lemma

(vii)), it is the radical of
Z^{0}_{G}(K)
, and

is compact. Hence we get that

and

are also conjugate. This implies that

and

are conjugate.
∎

Now we list some properties of Cartan subgroups of a connected locally compact group
G
. It is well-known that any closed solvable
(as well as nilpotent) subgroup of
G
is compactly generated (see Main Theorem of
[
HoN
]
), and hence all Cartan subgroups
of
G
are compactly generated.
The following is known for Lie groups (see
[
MS
]
, Corollary 3.7, Lemma 3.9 and Remark 3.10).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact group and let
C
be a Cartan subgroup of
G
.
Let
R
and
N
be respectively the radical and the nilradical of
G
. Then the following hold:

(1)

N_{G}(C^{0})=N_{G}(C)
,
N_{R}(C^{0})=N_{R}(C)=C\cap R
and
N_{N}(C^{0})=N_{N}(C)=C\cap N
.

(2)

Z_{G}(C^{0})\subset C
. Moreover, if
G
is semisimple, then
Z_{G}(C^{0})=C
.

(3)

If
H
is a closed connected subgroup of
G
such that
C\subset H
, then
C
is a Cartan subgroup of
H
.

In Lemma
3.4
, statements in (1) and (2) can be deduced easily from the same statements for Lie groups
in
[
MS
]
and Proposition
3.1
, and (3) follows easily from the definition of Cartan subgroups.We will not go into details.

Now we give an equivalent criteria to determine Cartan subgroups in connected locally compact groups, which is known for Lie groups
(see Corollary 3.2 and Remark 3.10 of
[
MS
]
).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact group. Then

C
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
if and only if
C
is a maximal nilpotent group and

N_{G}(C^{0})/C
is compact and totally disconnected. If
G
is solvable, then
C
is a Cartan subgroup
of
G
if and only if
C
is connected and nilpotent, and
N_{G}(C)=C
.

Proof.

Let
C
be a maximal nilpotent subgroup of
G
. Let
L
be any closed normal subgroup of
C
such that

C/L
is compact and totally disconnected. Then
L^{0}=C^{0}
and
N_{G}(L)\subset N_{G}(C^{0})
. Suppose
N_{G}(C^{0})/C
is compact
and totally disconnected. Then so is
N_{G}(C^{0})/L
, and hence also,
N_{G}(L)/L
. Thus
C
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
.
The converse follows easily as
N_{G}(C)=N_{G}(C^{0})
by Lemma
3.4
(1); which also implies that
N_{G}(C^{0})/C
is a
group. Now suppose
G
is solvable. By Proposition
3.3
, any Cartan subgroup
C
of
G
is connected, and by
Lemma
3.4
(1), we have it
N_{G}(C)=C
. The converse can easily be deduced using Proposition
3.1
and
Corollary 3.2 of
[
MS
]
.
∎

4.
Cartan subgroups in quotient groups

In
[
MS
]
, for a connected Lie group
G
, Cartan subgroups of quotient groups of
G
are characterised as
precisely the images of Cartan subgroups of
G
(see Theorem 1.5 in
[

]
). The following generalises the
same from Lie groups to locally compact groups.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G
a connected locally compact group and let
H
be a closed normal subgroup of
G
.
Then the following hold:

(1)

If
C
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
, then
CH/H
is a Cartan subgroup of
G/H
.

(2)

If
C^{\prime}
is a Cartan subgroup of
G/H
, then there exists a Cartan subgroup of
C
of
G
such that
CH/H=C^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(1):
This can be proven easily easily using Theorem 1.5 (a) of
[
MS
]
and Proposition
3.1
. We
give a proof for the sake of completeness.
Let
G=\varprojlim_{\alpha}G/K_{\alpha}
, a projective limit of Lie groups
G_{\alpha}=G/K_{\alpha}
where
K_{\alpha}
are
compact normal subgroups of
G
. Let
\pi_{\alpha}:G\to G_{\alpha}
be the natural projection map for each
\alpha
.
Let
C
be a Cartan subgroup of
G
. By Proposition
3.1
,
\pi_{\alpha}(C)
is a Cartan subgroup of
G_{\alpha}
,
for each
\alpha
. As
\pi_{\alpha}(H)
is closed normal subgroup of
G_{\alpha}
, by Theorem 1.5 of
[
MS
]
,

(\pi_{\alpha}(C)\pi_{\alpha}(H))/\pi_{\alpha}(H)
is a Cartan subgroup of

(2):

Using Theorem

, Proposition

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

be a connected locally compact group such that

with
the product topology
(
\Lambda
is an arbitrary indexing set
)
, where all but finitely many

compact. Then the following hold:

(i)

Any Cartan subgroup
C
of

, where

\psi_{\alpha}:G\to G_{\alpha}
is the natural projection and
\psi_{\alpha}(C)
is a Cartan subgroup
of
G_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
.

(ii)

If
C_{\alpha}
is a Cartan subgroup of
G_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
, then

C=\Pi_{\alpha\in\Lambda}C_{\alpha}
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
.

Proof.

(i):
Let
C
be any Cartan subgroup of
G
. Then
C
is nilpotent,
and hence
\psi_{\alpha}(C)
is nilpotent and
l(\psi_{\alpha}(C))\leq n=l(C)
. Therefore
\Pi_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\psi_{\alpha}(C)

is also nilpotent, and as it contains the Cartan subgroup
C
which is a maximal nilpotent group in
G
, we have that

C=\Pi_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\psi_{\alpha}(C)
. Note that each
G_{\alpha}
is isomorphic to
G/\ker\psi_{\alpha}
. Hence by
Theorem
4.1
,
\psi_{\alpha}(C)
is Cartan subgroup of
G_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha
.

(ii):
Let
C_{\alpha}
be a Cartan a subgroup of
G_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\Lambda
, and let

C=\Pi_{\alpha\in\Lambda}C_{\alpha}
as in the hypothesis. Since all but finitely many
G_{\alpha}
’s are compact,
we have by Proposition
3.3
that all but finitely many
C_{\alpha}
’s are abelian. Therefore,

\{l(C_{\alpha})\mid\alpha\in\Lambda\}
is finite, and hence
C
is nilpotent. Moreover,
C
is a maximal nilpotent
subgroup in
G
as each
C_{\alpha}
is so in
G_{\alpha}
. By Lemma
3.5
, it is enough to show that

N_{G}(C^{0})/C
is compact and totally disconnected. As
C^{0}=\Pi_{\alpha\in\Lambda}C^{0}_{\alpha}
,

N_{G}(C^{0})=\Pi_{\alpha\in\Lambda}N_{G_{\alpha}}(C^{0}_{\alpha})
and
N_{G}(C^{0})/C
is isomorphic to

\Pi_{\alpha\in\Lambda}(N_{G_{\alpha}}(C^{0}_{\alpha})/C_{\alpha})
. As

is a Cartan subgroup of

,
by Corollary

,

The following lemma which is known for Lie groups will be useful for the proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact group and let
R
and
N
be respectively the radical
and the nilradical of
G
. Then
R=(C\cap R)N
and both
C\cap R
and
C\cap N
are connected.

Proof.

We first show that
R/N
is central in
G/N
. This is known if
G
is a Lie group.
Let
\{K_{\alpha}\}
be a collection of compact normal subgroups in
G
such that each
G/K_{\alpha}
is a Lie group and

\cap_{\alpha}K_{\alpha}=\{e\}
.
Let
\pi_{\alpha}:G\to G/K_{\alpha}
be the natural projection for each
\alpha
. As
\pi_{\alpha}(R)
and
\pi_{\alpha}(N)
are respectively the radical
and the nilradical of
G/K_{\alpha}
, we get that
R\subset\{x\in G\mid xgx^{-1}g^{-1}\in NK_{\alpha}\mbox{ for all }g\in G\}
. Since
this is true for all
K_{\alpha}
, we get that
R/N
is central in
G/N
. By Theorem
4.1
,
CN/N
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
,
and hence it contains
R/N
. Therefore,
R\subset CN
, i.e.
R=(C\cap R)N
, which proves the first assertion.
Since
R
is connected, we get that
R=(C\cap R)^{0}N
, and hence that
C\cap R=(C\cap R)^{0}(C\cap N)
. Therefore,

C\cap R
is connected if
C\cap N
is connected.

If
G
is compact, then
N=Z^{0}(G)\subset C
, and hence
C\cap N=N
is connected. Suppose
G
is not compact.
Let
K
be the largest compact normal subgroup of
G
. By Lemma
2.4
,

K=Z(K)D(K^{0})
,
Z(K)\subset Z(G)
and
G=G_{1}D(K^{0})=D(K^{0})G_{1}
, where
G_{1}:=Z^{0}_{G}(D(K^{0}))
. As

G_{1}\cap D(K^{0})
is central in
D(K^{0})
, and hence in
G
, one can use Proposition
3.1
and
Lemma
2.3
to get that
C=C_{1}C_{2}=C_{2}C_{1}
, where
C_{1}

(resp.
C_{2}
) is a Cartan subgroup of
G_{1}
(resp.
D(K^{0})
). Note that since
D(K^{0})
is semisimple,
its nilradical is trivial and it is easy to see that
N
is the nilradical of
G_{1}
. Since

C\cap G_{1}=C_{1}
, we get that
C\cap N=C_{1}\cap N
, and hence without loss of any generality we may
replace
G
by
G_{1}
and assume that the maximal compact normal subgroup
K
of
G
is abelian,
and hence and central in
G
.

Let
\{K_{\alpha}\}
and
\pi_{\alpha}:G\to G/K_{\alpha}
be as above.
As
K
is central in
G
, so is each
K_{\alpha}
, and hence
K_{\alpha}\subset C
. By Proposition
3.1
, we have that

\pi_{\alpha}(C)
is a Cartan subgroup of the Lie group
G/K_{\alpha}
. As
\pi_{\alpha}(N)
is the nilradical of
G/K_{\alpha}
, we have
that
\pi_{\alpha}(C)\cap\pi_{\alpha}(N)
is connected (cf.
[
Wu2
]
, Theorem 1.9). Now

CK_{\alpha}\cap NK_{\alpha}=(CK_{\alpha}\cap N)K_{\alpha}=(C\cap N)K_{\alpha}
. Therefore,
C\cap N=(C\cap N)^{0}(K_{\alpha}\cap N)
.
Since this is true for all

and it is connected.

∎

5.

In

, a Levi decomposition for a connected locally compact group

, where

is the radical of

and

Recall that a connected locally compact group is said to be semisimple, if it has no proper connected abelian (solvable) normal
subgroup, i.e. its radical is trivial. Equivalently, a connected subgroup

of

is semisimple if for all
\alpha

\pi_{\alpha}(S)
is
semisimple in the Lie group
\pi_{\alpha}(G)

is any compact

is a Lie group. Also,

is a

is a compact connected semisimple group,

(see
[
Mat
]
). It is also shown in
[

]
, that all Levi subgroups are conjugate to each
other by elements of the radical

. Note that a Levi subgroup
S
of
G
, being a product of a connected (semisimple)

K
be any compact normal subgroup
of
G
. Then
S\cap K
is normal in
S
. Since
K=Z(K)D(K^{0})
and

, being semisimple and normal in

, is
contained
S
, and hence
D(K^{0})\subset K_{0}
. Now we show that

is compact. It is enough to show that

is compact. In fact,

is central in

and as

(cf.

S\cap Z(K)
,
and hence

is compact. It follows that

is a projective limit of Lie groups
S/(S\cap K_{\alpha})
which are isomorphic
to
\pi_{\alpha}(S)
. In fact, for all small

K_{\alpha}\cap S_{0}=\{e\}
and

.
Therefore,
\pi_{\alpha}(S)
is isomorphic to
S_{0}(K_{0}/K_{\alpha})
for all small

It has been shown by Wüstner that any Cartan subgroup

. Here, we extend

. Note that the Levi subgroup

, an almost direct

S

product of Cartan subgroups of
S_{0}
and
K_{0}
. We need the following fact: For any Levi subgroup

is contained in the nilradical
N
of

; this can be
deduced by using the same assertion for connected Lie groups and Lie projectivity of

. We also need the following:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

be a connected locally compact group and let

be a totally disconnected compact

be a Levi decomposition with a Levi subgroup

and

, the centraliser of

in

Let

where

and
d_{r}\in R
. Then
d_{r}d_{s}=d_{s}d_{r}
. For

Generalising Wüstner’s decomposition theorem for Cartan subgroups in Lie groups and Theorem 1.1 of
[

‘Levi’ decomposition of Cartan subgroup in the following theorem. Recall that for subgroups

and

of

.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact group and let
R
be the radical of

.

(1)

Given a Cartan subgroup
C
of
G
, there exists a Levi decomposition

G=SR
such that
C=(C\cap S)(C\cap R)
, where
C\cap S
is a Cartan
subgroup of
S
and
C\cap S
centralises

C\cap R
. Moreover,
Z_{R}(C\cap S)
is connected and
C\cap R
is a Cartan subgroup of

(2)

Given any Levi subgroup
S
of
G
and a Cartan subgroup

of
S
,

is connected and for any Cartan subgroup

C_{Z_{R}(C_{S})}
of it,
C_{S}C_{Z_{R}(C_{S})}

.

Proof.

(1):
If

is solvable, then
S
is trivial and the assertions follow easily. Suppose

is not solvable.
If

is compact, then
G=D(G)Z^{0}(G)
, where

is a compact connected

Step 1:
We now want to prove (1) for a Cartan subgroup

of

G=Z_{G}(T_{R})
, and the assertion holds trivially. Now
suppose
G
is not compact.

Let
C^{\prime}
be a Cartan subgroup of
Z_{G}(T_{R})
. Let
\{K_{\alpha}\}
be a collection of compact normal subgroups in
G
such that

G/K_{\alpha}
are Lie groups and

G=\varprojlim_{\alpha}G/K_{\alpha}
. Let
\pi_{\alpha}:G\to G/K_{\alpha}
be the natural projections. By Lemma
6.1
,
\pi_{\alpha}(T_{R})
is a
maximal torus in the radical
\pi_{\alpha}(R)
of
\pi_{\alpha}(G)
and
\pi_{\alpha}(Z_{G}(T_{R}))=Z_{\pi_{\alpha}(G)}(\pi_{\alpha}(T_{R}))
. By Proposition
3.1
(1),

\pi_{\alpha}(C^{\prime})
is a Cartan subgroup of
Z_{\pi_{\alpha}(G)}(\pi_{\alpha}(T_{R}))
. Now by Theorem 1.2 of
[
MS
]
,
\pi_{\alpha}(C^{\prime})
is a Cartan subgroup
of
\pi_{\alpha}(G)
. Since this holds for every
\alpha
, we get from Proposition
3.1
(2a) that
C^{\prime}
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
.

We now show the converse. Let
C
be a Cartan subgroup of
G
and let
M
be the maximal compact connected subgroup of

C\cap R
. We first show that
M
is a maximal torus in
R
. Let
\pi:G\to G/Z(K)
be the natural projection, where
K
is the
maximal compact normal subgroup of
G
. By Lemma
2.4
,
Z(K)
is central in
G
and
\pi(G)=L\times\pi(K)
, where

\pi(K)
is compact, connected and semisimple with trivial center and

L=Z^{0}_{\pi(G)}(\pi(K))
is a Lie group. Now
\pi(R)\subset L
and it is the radical of
\pi(G)
. By Proposition
3.1
(1),

\pi(C)
is a Cartan subgroup of
\pi(G)
,
and
\pi(C)=C_{1}\times C_{2}
, where
C_{1}
(resp.
C_{2}
) is a Cartan subgroup of
L
(resp.
\pi(K)
).
As
L
is a Lie group and
\pi(C)\cap\pi(R)=C_{1}\cap\pi(R)
is connected, and as
Z(K)\subset C
, we get that

\pi(C)\cap\pi(R)=\pi(C\cap R)
. In particular,
\pi(M)
is a maximal compact subgroup of
C_{1}\cap\pi(R)
, and it is
a maximal torus of
\pi(R)
(cf.
[
G
]
, Theorem 3 (ii)). As all maximal tori in
R
(resp.
\pi(R)
) are conjugate to each other,
by Lemma
6.1
there exists a maximal torus
T_{R}
in
R
such that
\pi(T_{R})=\pi(M)
. As
\ker\pi=Z(K)\subset C
, we get that

T_{R}\subset C
, and hence
T_{R}=M
. Thus
M
is a maximal torus of
R
. By Lemma
6.1
,
Z_{G}(M)
is connected.
As
C\subset Z_{G}(M)
,
C
is a Cartan subgroup of
Z_{G}(M)
(cf. Lemma
3.4
(3)).
∎

7.
Cartan subalgebras

The Lie algebra of a connected locally compact group can be defined as follows:
A connected locally compact group
G
is Lie projective and if
G=\varprojlim_{\alpha}G_{\alpha}
for Lie groups
G_{\alpha}
, then the
Lie algebra
L(G)
of
G
is defined as
L(G)=\varprojlim_{\alpha}L(G_{\alpha})
, the projective limit of
L(G_{\alpha})
, the Lie algebras
of
G_{\alpha}
, and it is independent of the choice of
\{G_{\alpha}\}
(see
[
L
]
where it is called LP-Lie algebras). Note that
L(G)

defined this way is isomorphic to the one defined in Ch. 2 of
[
HoMo2
]
, where it is defined as the set of all continuous real
one-parameter subgroups of
G
endowed with a Lie algebra structure and a topology of uniform convergence on compact sets.
Since
L(G)
is a projective limit of finite dimensional real Lie algebras, it is also called pro-Lie algebra in
[
HoMo2
]
.
A Lie algebra of a locally compact group
G
is defined as the Lie algebra
L(G^{0})
of
G^{0}
.

Note that if
\pi_{\alpha}:G\to G_{\alpha}
denotes the natural projection and
\ker\pi_{\alpha}=K_{\alpha}
is a compact normal subgroup
of
G
for each
\alpha
, then
{\rm d}\,\pi_{\alpha}:L(G)=\varprojlim_{\alpha}L(G_{\alpha})\to L(G_{\alpha})
is the corresponding projection
(in terms of the definition of the Lie algebra in
[
HoMo2
]
, if for
X\in L(G)
is a continuous real one-parameter
subgroup of
G
, then
\pi_{\alpha}\circ X
is a continuous real one-parameter subgroup of
G_{\alpha}
and

{\rm d}\,\pi_{\alpha}(X)=\pi_{\alpha}\circ X\in L(G_{\alpha})
, see
[
HoMo2
]
). Also, if
\ker\pi_{\alpha}=K_{\alpha}\subset K_{\beta}=\ker\pi_{\beta}
,
then there is a natural projection
\pi_{\alpha\beta}:G_{\alpha}\to G_{\beta}
and the corresponding Lie algebra homomorphism

{\rm d}\,\pi_{\alpha\beta}:L(G_{\alpha})\to L(G_{\beta})
such that
\pi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha\beta}=\pi_{\beta}

and
{\rm d}\,\pi_{\alpha}\circ{\rm d}\,\pi_{\alpha\beta}={\rm d}\,\pi_{\beta}
.

A Lie algebra is said to be pro-nilpotent if it is a projective limit of nilpotent Lie algebras. A
Cartan subalgebra

of a pro-Lie algebra is a closed pro-nilpotent subalgebra that agrees with its own normaliser
(see Definition 7.88 of
[
HoMo2
]
for details, and for its existence see Theorem 7.93 of
[
HoMo2
]
).

In the following theorem, we prove that Lie algebra of a Cartan subgroup exists and it is a Cartan subalgebra as defined in
[
HoMo2
]
.
Moreover, there is a one-to-one correspondence between Cartan subgroups and Cartan subalgebras. We will also show
that every Cartan subalgebra of
\mathcal{G}
is nilpotent.
For a locally compact group
G
, we denote the Lie algebra
L(G)
of
G
by
{\mathcal{G}}
and
L(G_{\alpha})
by
{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact group with the Lie algebra
{\mathcal{G}}
. Let
C
be a Cartan subgroup of
G
.
Then the Lie algebra
L(C)
of
C

is a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathcal{G}}
. Moreover,
L(C)
is nilpotent. Conversely, given a Cartan subalgebra
\mathcal{C}
of

\mathcal{G}
there exists a unique Cartan subgroup
C
of
G
whose Lie algebra is
\mathcal{C}
.
In particular, all Cartan subalgebras of
{\mathcal{G}}
are nilpotent.

Proof.

Let
C
be a Cartan subgroup of
G
. Let
L(C)
be the subalgebra of
\mathcal{G}
such that it is the Lie algebra of
C^{0}
.
As
G
is a projective limit
of Lie groups
G_{\alpha}=G/K_{\alpha}
where
K_{\alpha}
are compact normal subgroup of
G
, by Proposition
3.1
we have

C=\varprojlim_{\alpha}CK_{\alpha}/K_{\alpha}
. Since
C
is a projective limit of Lie groups
C^{\prime}_{\alpha}:=CK_{\alpha}/K_{\alpha}
, its Lie algebra
is a pro-Lie algebra, i.e.
L(C)=\varprojlim_{\alpha}L(C^{\prime}_{\alpha})
,
where
L(C^{\prime}_{\alpha})
is a Lie subalgebra of
{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha
. Now
L(C)
is a Cartan
subalgebra by Theorem 7.93 of
[
HoMo2
]
as
L(C^{\prime}_{\alpha})
is a Cartan subalgebra for each
\alpha
.
As
C
is nilpotent, the length
l(C)
of the central series of
C
is finite. Then

l(C_{\alpha}^{0})\leq l(C_{\alpha})\leq l(C)
for all
\alpha
. Therefore,
l(L(C^{\prime}_{\alpha}))=l(C^{\prime}_{\alpha})\leq l(C)
for every
\alpha
, where

l(L(C^{\prime}_{\alpha}))
is the length of the central series of the Lie subalgebras in
L(C^{\prime}_{\alpha})
, and hence

L(C)
is nilpotent.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{C}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathcal{G}}
. Then its projection
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
in
{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha}
is a
Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha
, and
\mathcal{C}=\varprojlim_{\alpha}\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}

(cf.
[
HoMo1
]
, Proposition 7.90). For each
\alpha
, there exists a unique Cartan subgroup
C_{\alpha}
of
G_{\alpha}
,
whose Lie algebra is
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
and
C_{\alpha}=Z_{G_{\alpha}}(C_{\alpha}^{0})C_{\alpha}^{0}
(cf.
[
MS
]
, Lemma 3.9 (3),
see also Corollary
5.4
above).
For
\alpha,\beta
such that
K_{\alpha}\subset K_{\beta}
,
since
{\rm d}\,\pi_{\alpha\beta}(\mathcal{C}_{\alpha})=\mathcal{C}_{\beta}
and the exponential map
is surjective for any connected nilpotent Lie group, we have that
\pi_{\alpha\beta}(C_{\alpha}^{0})=C_{\beta}^{0}
. Now

\pi_{\alpha\beta}(Z_{G_{\alpha}}(C_{\alpha}^{0}))\subset Z_{G_{\beta}}(C_{\beta}^{0})
. Hence,
\pi_{\alpha\beta}(C_{\alpha})\subset C_{\beta}
.
By Theorem
4.1
(see also Theorem 1.5 in
[
MS
]
),
\pi_{\alpha\beta}(C_{\alpha})
is a Cartan subgroup of
G_{\beta}
,
and hence it is equal to
C_{\beta}
. By Proposition
3.1
(2b),
C=\cap_{\alpha}\,\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(C_{\alpha})
is a Cartan subgroup of
G
and

\pi_{\alpha}(C)=C_{\alpha}
for every
\alpha
. Now
\mathcal{C}=\varprojlim_{\alpha}\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
,
where
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}=L(C_{\alpha})=L(\pi_{\alpha}(C))
for each
\alpha
.
Hence we get that
\mathcal{C}
is the Lie algebra of
C
. From the earlier
part of the proof, it follows that
\mathcal{C}
is nilpotent. Now
\pi_{\alpha}(C)=C_{\alpha}
is a unique Cartan subgroup in the Lie group

G_{\alpha}
such that
L(C_{\alpha})=\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}={\rm d}\,\pi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{C})
, hence we get that
C
is a unique Cartan subgroup of
G

such that
L(C)=\mathcal{C}
.
∎

8.
Power maps, Cartan subgroups and weak exponentiality

Let
G
be a group. For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
k
-th power map
P_{k}:G\to G
is defined by
g\mapsto g^{k}
for all
g\in G
.

In this section, we characterise the dense image of the power maps in a connected locally compact
group in terms of Cartan subgroups.

Note that a connected locally compact group
G
is Lie projective and it can be expressed as
G=\varprojlim_{\alpha}G_{\alpha}

where each
G_{\alpha}
is a Lie group.
If
\pi_{\alpha}:G\to G_{\alpha}
denote natural projection with
\ker\pi_{\alpha}
being a compact normal subgroup of
G
, then
any set
D
is dense in
G
if and only if
\pi_{\alpha}(D)
is dense in

G_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha
(this is easy to show, see for example a proposition on pp 327 in
[
HoMo2
]
).
Now the following lemma can be proven easily using the proposition mentioned above, as
\pi_{\alpha}(P_{k}(G))=P_{k}(G_{\alpha})

for each
\alpha
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
G=\varprojlim_{\alpha}G/K_{\alpha}
, a projective limit of Lie groups
G_{\alpha}=G/K_{\alpha}
where
K_{\alpha}

are compact normal subgroups in
G
. Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then
P_{k}(G)
is dense in
G
if and only if
P_{k}(G_{\alpha})
is dense in
G_{\alpha}
for every
\alpha
.

We now extend Theorem 1.6 of
[
MS
]
to any connected locally compact group
G
as follows:

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
H
be a closed normal subgroup of a locally compact group
G
. Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If

P_{k}(H)

(resp.
P_{k}(G/H)
) is dense in
H
(resp.
G/H
), then
P_{k}(G)
is dense in
G
.

Proof.

We have that
G=\varprojlim_{\alpha}G/K_{\alpha}
, where each
K_{\alpha}
is a compact normal
subgroup of
G
such that
G/K_{\alpha}
is a Lie group.
Then
G/HK_{\alpha}
(resp.
HK_{\alpha}/K_{\alpha}
) is isomorphic to
(G/K_{\alpha})/(HK_{\alpha}/K_{\alpha})
(resp.
H/(H\cap K_{\alpha})
).
As
P_{k}(H)
is dense in
H
, we get that

P_{k}(HK_{\alpha})/K_{\alpha}
is dense in
(HK_{\alpha})/K_{\alpha}
. Moreover, as
P_{k}(G/H)
is dense in
G/H
,
we get that
P_{k}(G/HK_{\alpha})
is dense in
G/HK_{\alpha}
. Then, by
Theorem 1.6 of
[
MS
]
, we get that
P_{k}(G/K_{\alpha})
is dense in
G/K_{\alpha}
. In particular,
\pi_{\alpha}(P_{k}(G))
is dense
in
\pi_{\alpha}(G)
for each
\alpha
. By Lemma
8.1
,
P_{k}(G)
is dense in
G
.
∎

We recall that there exists a characteristic connected closed subgroup
G_{s}
of
G
containing
R={\rm Rad}(G)

such that
G_{s}/R
is compact and
G/G_{s}
is a semisimple Lie group without compact factors (see
[
HoMu
]
).

Let
S(C)
be the union of all Cartan subgroups of a connected locally compact group
G
. It is known that

S(C)
is dense in
G
if
G
is a Lie group. It is also true for any
G
, as shown in the proof of the following theorem,
which co-relates the density of image of a power map
P_{k}
with the surjectivity of its restriction to Cartan subgroups.
The theorem below is essentially known for Lie groups. Note that any connected locally compact nilpotent group is divisible.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact group. Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

P_{k}(G)
is dense in
G
.

(2)

P_{k}(C)=C
for every Cartan subgroup
C
of
G
.

(3)

P_{k}(C/C^{0})=C/C^{0}
for every Cartan subgroup
C
of
G
,

(4)

P_{k}(S(C))=S(C)
.

(5)

P_{k}(G/K)
is dense in
G/K
for a compact connected normal subgroup

K
of
G
.

(6)

P_{k}(G/R)
is dense in
G/R
, where
R
is the radical of
G
.

(7)

P_{k}(G/G_{s})
is dense in
G/G_{s}
, where
G_{s}
is as above.

(8)

P_{k}(G/H)
is dense in
G/H
, where
H
is a closed connected normal
subgroup of
G
such that
R\subset H
and
H/R
is compact.

Proof.

Since
G
is a connected locally compact group, we can write
G=\varprojlim_{\alpha}G/K_{\alpha}
, where
K_{\alpha}
are
compact normal subgroups in
G
with
\cap_{\alpha}K_{\alpha}=\{e\}
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2):

Suppose that
P_{k}(G)
is dense in
G
. Let
C
be any Cartan subgroup of
G
. Let

G_{\alpha}=G/K_{\alpha}
and
G=\varprojlim_{\alpha}G_{\alpha}
, where
G_{\alpha}
are connected Lie groups. Then
by Proposition
3.1

(2a)
,
C_{\alpha}=CK_{\alpha}/K_{\alpha}
is a Cartan subgroup of
G_{\alpha}
. Now

P_{k}(G_{\alpha})
is dense in
G_{\alpha}
, and by Theorem 1.1 of
[
BM
]
we have
P_{k}(C_{\alpha})=C_{\alpha}

for every
\alpha
. Now
P_{k}(C)K_{\alpha}=CK_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha
. Since
C
is compactly generated and nilpotent,
by Theorem 3.1.17 of
[
H
]
,
C
is root compact, and hence
P_{k}(C)
is closed. Therefore,
P_{k}(C)=C
.

(2)\Leftrightarrow(3):
Note that
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
is obvious. Let
C
be any Cartan subgroup of
G
.
Suppose
(3)
holds, i.e.
P_{k}(C/C^{0})=C/C^{0}
. Since
C^{0}
is connected and nilpotent,
P_{k}(C^{0})=C^{0}
. Also, by
Corollary
5.4
(1),
C=Z_{G}(C^{0})C^{0}
. Then (3) implies that
P_{k}(Z_{G}(C^{0}))C^{0}=C
. As
C^{0}
is
centralises
Z_{G}(C^{0})
we get that

P_{k}(C)=P_{k}(Z_{G}(C^{0})C^{0})=P_{k}(Z_{G}(C^{0}))P_{k}(C^{0})=P_{k}(Z_{G}(C^{0}))C^{0}=C.

Hence (2) holds. That is,
(3)\Rightarrow(2)
.

(2)\Rightarrow(4)
is obvious. Now we show that
(4)\Rightarrow(1)
. Suppose
P_{k}(S(C))=S(C)
. It is enough
to show that
S(C)
is dense in
G
. Here,

G=\varprojlim_{\alpha}G_{\alpha}
where
G=G/K_{\alpha}
are Lie groups, where
K_{\alpha}
are compact normal subgroup of
G
.
By Proposition
3.1
(1), the image of
S(C)
in
G_{\alpha}
is the union of Cartan subgroups in

G_{\alpha}
, which is dense in
G_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha
. Hence, by a proposition on page 327 of

[
HoMo2
]
that
S(C)
is dense in
G
. Thus,
(1-4)
are equivalent.

(1)\Leftrightarrow(5):
Here,
(1)\Rightarrow(5)
is obvious. For the converse, suppose
(5)
holds.
By Proposition
3.3
, the Cartan subgroup of
K
is connected. Now using
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
for

K
instead of
G
, we get that
P_{k}(K)
is dense in
K
. Now by Proposition
8.2
,
P_{k}(G)
is dense in
G
,
i.e. (1) holds.

(1)\Leftrightarrow(6):
Note that
(1)\Rightarrow(6)
is obvious. Since the Cartan subgroups of
the radical
R
are connected by Proposition
3.3
, arguing as above, we get that
(6)\Rightarrow(1)
.

(1)\Leftrightarrow(7)
and
(1)\Leftrightarrow(8)
can be proven the same way as
(1)\Leftrightarrow(5)
since
the Cartan subgroups of
G_{s}
, as well as those of the subgroup
H
as in
(8)
are connected by Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Remark 8.4
.

As in the case of Lie groups, the density of the image of
P_{k}
in a connected locally compact group is equivalent to
that of the restriction of
P_{k}
to a Levi subgroup of it. More generally, if
G=SR
is a Levi decomposition, with a Levi subgroup
S
,
where
S=S_{0}K_{0}=K_{0}S_{0}
,
S_{0}
is a connected Lie group without any compact factors and
K_{0}
is the largest compact
connected normal subgroup of
S
(cf.
[
Mat
]
), then
G_{s}
as mentioned in Theorem
8.3

(7)
is the same as
K_{0}R
,
and it follows from Theorem
8.3
and Theorem
5.2
that
P_{k}(G)
is dense in
G
if and only if
P_{k}(S_{0})
is
dense in
S_{0}
.

An element
x
in a connected locally compact group
G
is said to be
exponential if it is contained in a continuous real one-parameter subgroup of
G
.
A subgroup
H
of
G
is said to be exponential (resp. weakly exponential), if every element in
H
is exponential,
(resp. the set of exponential elements in
H
is dense in
H
). Any (weakly) exponential group is necessarily connected.
It is known that any compactly generated locally compact nilpotent group
G
is connected
if and only if it is divisible (see also Theorem
8.3

(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
). This is also equivalent to saying that
such a
G
is weakly exponential. More generally, we state the following which is known for Lie groups (see
[
N
]
and
[
BM
]
).

Corollary 8.5
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact group. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

G
is weakly exponential.

(ii)

P_{k}(G)
is dense in
G
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(iii)

Every Cartan subgroup of
G
is connected.

In the Corollary
8.5
,
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
is obvious,
(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
follows easily from Theorem
8.3

and
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
follows from Lemma
8.1
, Corollary 1.3 of
[
BM
]
and Lemma 3.2 of
[
HoMu
]
. We will not go into
details here. Note that every divisible element in a connected Lie group is exponential (cf.
[
Mc
]
), and hence every connected
nilpotent Lie subgroup is exponential. However, there are compact connected abelian groups which are not exponential.
Thus the condition (iii) in Corollary
8.5
is equivalent to the condition that every Cartan subgroup is weakly exponential.

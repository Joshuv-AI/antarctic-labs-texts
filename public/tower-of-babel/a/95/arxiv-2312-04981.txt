Joint moments of derivatives of characteristic polynomials of random symplectic and orthogonal matrices
Julio C. Andrade, Christopher G. Best

Abstract
. We investigate the joint moments of derivatives of characteristic polynomials over the unitary symplectic group S ​ p ​ ( 2 ​ N ) Sp(2N) and the orthogonal ensembles S ​ O ​ ( 2 ​ N ) SO(2N) and O − ​ ( 2 ​ N ) O^{-}(2N) . We prove asymptotic formulae for the joint moments of the n 1 n_{1} -th and n 2 n_{2} -th derivatives of the characteristic polynomials for all three matrix ensembles. Our results give two explicit formulae for each of the leading order coefficients, one in terms of determinants of hypergeometric functions and the other as combinatorial sums over partitions. We use our results to put forward conjectures on the joint moments of derivatives of L L -functions with symplectic and orthogonal symmetry.

1.
Introduction

Let
G(2N)\in\{Sp(2N),\ SO(2N),\ O^{-}(2N)\}
, where
Sp(2N)
is the the group of
2N\times 2N
unitary symplectic matrices and
SO(2N)
and
O^{-}(2N)
are the subsets of
2N\times 2N
orthogonal matrices with determinant
+1
and
-1
, respectively. Also, denote the characteristic polynomial of a matrix
A\in G(2N)
by

\Lambda_{A}(s)=\det\left(I-As\right).

In this paper we consider the joint moments

\int_{G(2N)}\left(\Lambda_{A}^{(n_{1})}(1)\right)^{k_{1}}\left(\Lambda_{A}^{(n_{2})}(1)\right)^{k_{2}}dA

(1.1)

of the
n_{1}
-th and
n_{2}
-th derivatives of the characteristic polynomials, where
n_{1},n_{2}
are non-negative integers and
dA
denotes the Haar measure on the relevant matrix ensemble. Using techniques developed in
[
1
,
9
,
21
]
, we obtain asymptotic formulae for (
1.1
) for each
G(2N)
and for all non-negative integers
k_{1},k_{2}
. Our main results give two explicit expressions for the leading order coefficients for each of the matrix ensembles under consideration and are detailed in section
2
.

The problem we study here is part of a general problem to obtain exact formulae for the complex moments of the derivatives of characteristic polynomials. A key motivation is the link between random matrix theory and the study of families of
L
-functions and their value distribution in analytic number theory. Specifically, one can use formulae obtained for characteristic polynomials of the various matrix ensembles to predict formulae for the corresponding quantities for
L
-functions with the same symmetry type. The complex moments of the derivatives of characteristic polynomials and
L
-functions can then be used to infer information on the zeros of the derivatives through Jensen’s formula. For results on the radial distribution of the zeros of the derivative of characteristic polynomials and on the horizontal distribution of the zeros of the derivative of the Riemann zeta function, see, for example,
[
11
,
23
]
and
[
25
,
26
]
, respectively.

Additionally on the number theory side, the order of vanishing of an
L
-function at the central point, which is controlled by the derivatives of the
L
-function, is widely believed to contain deep arithmetic and geometric information. The Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture for example, famously states that the order of vanishing of an
L
-function attached to an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
is equal to the rank of the curve.

For the ensemble of random unitary matrices
U(N)
, Conrey, Rubinstein and Snaith
[
9

proved that for integer

\int_{U(N)}|\Lambda_{A}^{\prime}(1)|^{2k}dA\sim c_{k}N^{k^{2}+2k},

where

c_{k}=(-1)^{k(k+1)/2}\sum_{h=0}^{k}\binom{k}{h}\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{k+h}\left(e^{-x}x^{-k^{2}/2}\det_{k\times k}\left(I_{i+j-1}(2\sqrt{x})\right)\right),

with

denoting the modified Bessel function of the first kind. Here, and throughout the paper, the indices

and

of the matrix in the determinant range from 1 to

. Also proven in
[
9
]
is a similar asymptotic formula for the

-th moment of the derivative of an analogue of Hardy’s

-function. As an application, the authors use their results to make a conjecture for the moments of the derivative of the Riemann zeta function and of the
Z
-function. Forrester and Witte
[

]
have given alternate expressions for the leading order coefficients obtained in
[
9
]
in terms of solutions to Painlevé III differential equations.

Concerning the joint moments over the unitary ensemble, one is interested in the quantity

\int_{U(N)}|\Lambda_{A}^{(n_{1})}(1)|^{2M}|\Lambda_{A}^{(n_{2})}(1)|^{2M-2k}dA.

(1.2)

When

)
/N^{k^{2}+2M}

In the case of general

and

Turning to the symplectic and orthogonal matrix ensembles, Altuğ et al.

identity matrix. The special orthogonal group
SO(2N)
and
O^{-}(2N)
are the subsets of orthogonal
2N\times 2N
matrices with determinant
+1
and
-1
, respectively. Each of these matrix ensembles is endowed with the normalised Haar measure
dA

We will set
\char 16\relax^{2}=-1
and the variable

for the set of permutations on

The multinomial coefficient is defined as

\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}=\frac{n!}{l_{1}!\cdots l_{k}!},

for integers

For any

2.
Main results

We now state our main results. Our first two theorems give an asymptotic formula for the joint moments of derivatives of characteristic polynomials of matrices over
Sp(2N)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
0\leq n_{1}\leq n_{2}
be integers and let
k_{1},k_{2}
be non-negative integers, not both
0
. Set
k=k_{1}+k_{2}
. Then, we have

\int_{Sp(2N)}\left(\Lambda_{A}^{(n_{1})}(1)\right)^{k_{1}}\left(\Lambda_{A}^{(n_{2})}(1)\right)^{k_{2}}dA=b_{k_{1},k_{2}}^{Sp}(n_{1},n_{2})\cdot(2N)^{k(k+1)/2+k_{1}n_{1}+k_{2}n_{2}}\left(1+O(N^{-1})\right),

where

\displaystyle b_{k_{1},k_{2}}^{Sp}(n_{1},n_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}n_{1}+k_{2}n_{2}}}{2^{k(k+1)/2+k_{1}n_{1}+k_{2}n_{2}}}\sum_{u_{1}+\cdots+u_{P}=k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}}{u_{1}\dots,u_{P}}\frac{(n_{1}!)^{k_{1}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{P}(\textbf{a}_{i}!)^{u_{i}}(\prod_{j=1}^{[n_{1}/2]}j^{\sum_{i=1}^{P}u_{i}a_{i,j}})}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{v_{1}+\cdots+v_{Q}=k_{2}}\binom{k_{2}}{v_{1}\dots,v_{Q}}\frac{(n_{2}!)^{k_{2}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{Q}(\textbf{b}_{i}!)^{v_{i}}(\prod_{j=1}^{[n_{2}/2]}j^{\sum_{i=1}^{Q}v_{i}b_{i,j}})}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{s,i}=m_{s}\\
s=2,\dots,n\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{s=2}^{n}\binom{m_{s}}{m_{s,1},\dots,m_{s,k}}\right)\left(\frac{d}{du}\right)^{m_{1}}\left.\det_{k\times k}\left(g_{2i-j+2\sum_{s=2}^{n}sm_{s,i}}(u)\right)\right|_{u=0}.

Here, for
i=1,\dots,P
, we define
{\bf a}_{i}=(a_{i,0},a_{i,1},\dots,a_{i,[n_{1}/2]})
to be the
P
distinct tuples of integers satisfying

a_{i,j}\geq 0\ \textup{and}\ a_{i,0}+2\sum_{j=1}^{[n_{1}/2]}ja_{i,j}=n_{1}.

Similarly, the
{\bf b}_{i}=(b_{i,0},b_{i,1},\dots,b_{i,[n_{2}/2]})
for
i=1,\dots,Q
are defined to be the
Q
tuples of integers satisfying

b_{i,j}\geq 0\ \textup{and}\ b_{i,0}+2\sum_{j=1}^{[n_{2}/2]}jb_{i,j}=n_{2}.

Also, we define
{\bf a}_{i}!:=\prod_{j=1}^{[n_{1}/2]}a_{i,j}!
and
{\bf b}_{i}!:=\prod_{j=1}^{[n_{2}/2]}b_{i,j}!
. Finally,
m_{j}:=\sum_{i=1}^{P}u_{i}a_{i,j}+\sum_{i=1}^{Q}v_{i}b_{i,j}
for
j=1,\dots,[n_{1}/2]
and
m_{j}:=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}v_{i}b_{i,j}
for
j=[n_{1}/2]+1,\dots,[n_{2}/2]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
0\leq n_{1}\leq n_{2}
be integers and let
k_{1},k_{2}
be non-negative integers, not both

where

\displaystyle b_{k_{1},k_{2}}^{Sp}(n_{1},n_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k(k-1)/2+k_{1}n_{1}+k_{2}n_{2}}}{2^{k(k+1)/2+k_{1}n_{1}+k_{2}n_{2}}}(n_{1}!)^{k_{1}}(n_{2}!)^{k_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\sum_{j=1}^{k}l_{i,j}\leq n_{1}\\

Here

for

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
1\leq n_{1}\leq n_{2}
be integers and let
k_{1},k_{2}
be non-negative integers, not both 0. Set

and
2.4

are large and

(2.1)

Proposition 2.7

For

Also, for

A mixed second moment involving a second derivative of these quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions over function fields was considered by Djanković and Đokić
[
10
]
. In particular, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.2 in
[
10
]
, along with Florea’s asymptotic formula for the second moment of
L(1/2,\chi_{D})
in
[
12
]
, imply that

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{2g+1}}\frac{L(1/2,\chi_{D})L^{\prime\prime}(1/2,\chi_{D})}{\log^{2}q}\sim\frac{(1-q^{-1})}{80}\mathcal{B}(1/q)\cdot(2g+1)^{5}.

Similarly to the previous asymptotic formula,
\mathcal{B}(1/q)
is given as an Euler product over monic, irreducible polynomials and it agrees with the arithmetic factor appearing in the asymptotic formula for the second moment of
L(1/2,\chi_{D})
. This result is therefore consistent with the prediction of our conjecture as we compute that
b_{1,1}^{Sp}(0,2)=1/80

One can naturally use our Theorems

to make analogous conjectures for the joint moments of derivatives at the central point for any family of

We begin with the following two lemmas concerning Vandermonde determinants of differential operators. The first is quoted from
[
1
, Lemma 2.8]
and follows from the definition.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f_{1}(x),\dots,f_{k}(x)
be
k-1
times differentiable. Then

\Delta\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(x_{i})=\det_{k\times k}\left(f_{i}^{(j-1)}(x_{i})\right).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f_{1}(x,y),\dots,f_{k}(x,y)
be
k-1
times differentiable in
x
and
y
. Then

\Delta\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)\Delta\left(\frac{d}{dy}\right)\left.\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(x_{i},y_{i})\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}=\cdots=x_{k}=X,\\
y_{1}=\cdots=y_{k}=Y\end{subarray}}=\sum_{\mu\in S_{k}}\det_{k\times k}\left(\frac{d^{i+j-2}}{dX^{i-1}dY^{j-1}}f_{\mu(i)}(X,Y)\right).

In particular, when
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{k}=f
, we have

\Delta\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)\Delta\left(\frac{d}{dy}\right)\left.\prod_{i=1}^{k}f(x_{i},y_{i})\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}=\cdots=x_{k}=X,\\
y_{1}=\cdots=y_{k}=Y\end{subarray}}=k!\det_{k\times k}\left(\frac{d^{i+j-2}}{dX^{i-1}dY^{j-1}}f(X,Y)\right).

Proof.

The case when
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{k}=f
is the result of
[
1
, Lemma 2.9]
and the proof of the general case follows the same lines. By Lemma
3.1
, we have

\Delta\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(x_{i},y_{i})=\det_{k\times k}\left(\frac{d^{j-1}}{dx_{i}^{j-1}}f_{i}(x_{i},y_{i})\right)=\sum_{\mu\in S_{k}}\textrm{sign}(\mu)\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{d^{\mu(i)-1}}{dx_{i}^{\mu(i)-1}}f_{i}(x_{i},y_{i}).

Then, by Lemma
3.1
again, we have that

\displaystyle\Delta\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)\Delta\left(\frac{d}{dy}\right)\left.\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(x_{i},y_{i})\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i}=X,\\
y_{i}=Y\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\in S_{k}}\textrm{sign}(\mu)\,\Delta\left(\frac{d}{dy}\right)\left.\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{d^{\mu(i)-1}}{dx_{i}^{\mu(i)-1}}f_{i}(x_{i},y_{i})\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i}=X,\\
y_{i}=Y\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\in S_{k}}\textrm{sign}(\mu)\left.\det_{k\times k}\left(\frac{d^{\mu(i)+j-2}}{dx_{i}^{\mu(i)-1}dy_{i}^{j-1}}f_{i}(x_{i},y_{i})\right)\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i}=X,\\
y_{i}=Y\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\in S_{k}}\textrm{sign}(\mu)\det_{k\times k}\left(\frac{d^{\mu(i)+j-2}}{dX^{\mu(i)-1}dY^{j-1}}f_{i}(X,Y)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\in S_{k}}\det_{k\times k}\left(\frac{d^{i+j-2}}{dX^{i-1}dY^{j-1}}f_{\mu(i)}(X,Y)\right),

where we have interchanged the rows of the matrix to obtain the final line.
∎

We next express a certain contour integral in terms of the hypergeometric functions
g_{m}(u)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and let
n\geq 1
be an integer. Then, for complex numbers
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}
, we have

\frac{1}{2\pi\char 16\relax}\oint_{|w|=1}\exp\left(w+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{u_{j}}{w^{2j}}\right)\frac{dw}{w^{k+1}}=\sum_{m_{2},\dots,m_{n}=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{u_{j}^{m_{j}}}{m_{j}!}\right)g_{k+2\sum_{j=2}^{n}jm_{j}}(u_{1}),

where
g_{m}(u)
is the hypergeometric function defined in
(
1.1
)
.

Proof.

We compute the integral by determining the coefficient of
w^{k}
in the exponential factor of the integrand. So, let
a_{n}(k)
be the coefficient of
w^{k}
in
\exp(w+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{u_{j}}{w^{2j}})
. Then,

\displaystyle\exp\left(w+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{u_{j}}{w^{2j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{u_{n}}{w^{2n}}\right)\exp\left(w+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{u_{j}}{w^{2j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{u_{n}^{m}}{m!}w^{-2nm}\right)\left(\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{n-1}(m)w^{m}\right).

From this it follows that

\displaystyle a_{n}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{n}=0}^{\infty}\frac{u_{n}^{m_{n}}}{m_{n}!}a_{n-1}(k+2nm_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{2},\dots,m_{n}=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{u_{j}^{m_{j}}}{m_{j}!}\right)a_{1}(k+2\sum_{j=2}^{n}jm_{j}).

We then see that by definition,
a_{1}(k+2\sum_{j=2}^{n}jm_{j})=g_{k+2\sum_{j=2}^{n}jm_{j}}(u_{1})
and hence

a_{n}(k)=\sum_{m_{2},\dots,m_{n}=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{u_{j}^{m_{j}}}{m_{j}!}\right)g_{k+2\sum_{j=2}^{n}jm_{j}}(u_{1}),

as required.
∎

Lemma 3.5

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
n\geq 0
and

be integers. Then

\frac{d^{n}}{d\alpha^{n}}\left.\frac{e^{-N\alpha}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(w_{i}^{2}-\alpha^{2})}\right|_{\alpha=0}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2}}\right)\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}(-N)^{n-m}\,m!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=m\\
l_{j}\ \textup{even}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{l_{i}}}.

Proof.

This follows from the proof of

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
n\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
be integers. Then

\frac{d^{n}}{d\alpha^{n}}\left.\frac{e^{-N\alpha}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(w_{i}^{2}-\alpha^{2})}\right|_{\alpha=0}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2}}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n\\
m_{3}=m_{5}=\cdots=0\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!}(-N)^{m_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\left(\frac{1}{j}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2j}}\right)^{m_{2j}}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[
21
, Lemma 9]
. First, we have

\frac{d}{d\alpha}\frac{e^{-N\alpha}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(w_{i}^{2}-\alpha^{2})}=\frac{e^{-N\alpha}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(w_{i}^{2}-\alpha^{2})}f_{1}(\alpha),

where

f_{1}(\alpha)=-N+2\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2}-\alpha^{2}}.

We can then write

\frac{d^{n}}{d\alpha^{n}}\frac{e^{-N\alpha}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(w_{i}^{2}-\alpha^{2})}=\frac{e^{-N\alpha}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(w_{i}^{2}-\alpha^{2})}f_{n}(\alpha),

(3.1)

where
f_{n}(\alpha)
is defined recursively by

f_{n+1}(\alpha)=f_{n}(\alpha)f_{1}(\alpha)+f_{n}^{\prime}(\alpha).

Now, let
g(\alpha)
be a function such that
g^{\prime}(\alpha)=f_{1}(\alpha)
. Then, we have that

\frac{d^{n}}{d\alpha^{n}}e^{g(\alpha)}=e^{g(\alpha)}f_{n}(\alpha).

But, by Faà di Bruno’s formula, we also have that

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{d\alpha^{n}}e^{g(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=e^{g(\alpha)}\sum_{m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{g^{(j)}(\alpha)}{j!}\right)^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle=e^{g(\alpha)}\sum_{m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{f_{1}^{(j-1)}(\alpha)}{j!}\right)^{m_{j}}.

Comparing the above two expressions for
(d/d\alpha)^{n}e^{g(\alpha)}
, we see that

f_{n}(\alpha)=\sum_{m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{f_{1}^{(j-1)}(\alpha)}{j!}\right)^{m_{j}}.

One can check that for
j\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle f_{1}^{(j)}(0)=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{if}\quad j\quad\textrm{even},\\
2j!\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}^{-(1+j)}&\textrm{if}\quad j\quad\text{odd}.\end{cases}

Hence, we have that

f_{n}(0)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+nm_{n}=n\\
m_{3}=m_{5}=\cdots=0\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!}(-N)^{m_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\left(\frac{1}{j}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{w_{i}^{2j}}\right)^{m_{2j}}.

Evaluating (
3.1
) at
\alpha=0
using this expression for
f_{n}(0)
yields the desired result.

∎

Proposition 3.11

Proof.

Y_{i}=N\end{subarray}}.

\frac{1}{(2\pi\char 16\relax)^{k}}\oint\cdots\oint\frac{\Delta(w)\Delta(w^{2})\,e^{N\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}^{2k+m_{j}}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}dw_{i}=\Delta\left(\frac{d}{dX}\right)\Delta\left(\frac{d}{dY}\right)\left.\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(X_{i},Y_{i})\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{i}=0,\\
Y_{i}=N\end{subarray}},

where

So, by Lemma
3.2

\frac{1}{(2\pi\char 16\relax)^{k}}\oint\cdots\oint\frac{\Delta(w)\Delta(w^{2})\,e^{N\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}^{2k+m_{j}}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}dw_{i}=\sum_{\mu\in S_{k}}\left.\det_{k\times k}\left(\frac{d^{i+j-2}}{dX^{i-1}dY^{j-1}}f_{\mu(i)}(X,Y)\right)\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}X=0,\\
Y=N\end{subarray}}.

\displaystyle\frac{d^{i+j-2}}{dX^{i-1}dY^{j-1}}\left.f_{\mu(i)}(X,Y)\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}X=0,\\
Y=N\end{subarray}}=\frac{1}{2\pi\char 16\relax}\oint_{|w|=1}\frac{e^{Nw}}{w^{2k+m_{\mu(i)}-2i-j+3}}dw=\frac{N^{2k+m_{\mu(i)}-2i-j+2}}{\Gamma(2k+m_{\mu(i)}-2i-j+3)},

and the proposition follows.

Lastly, we include a lemma that allows us to explicitly evaluate certain determinants whose entries are reciprocals of the Gamma function.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
m_{j}\geq 0
be integers for
j=1,\dots,k
. Then, we have

\det_{k\times k}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(2k+m_{i}-2i-j+2)}\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{(2k+m_{j}-2j)!}\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq k}(m_{j}-m_{i}-2j+2i).

Proof.

With our notation, equation (4.13) in
[
24
]
can be written as

\det_{k\times k}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(z_{i}-j+1)}\right)=\frac{\Delta(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Gamma(z_{j})}.

We take
z_{i}=2k+m_{i}-2i+1
for
i=1,\dots,k
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\det_{k\times k}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(2k+m_{i}-2i-j+2)}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma(2k+m_{i}-2i+1)^{-1}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(m_{j}-2j-m_{i}+2i).

Since
2k+m_{i}-2i+1\geq 1
for
1\leq i\leq k
, we have that
\Gamma(2k+m_{i}-2i+1)=(2k+m_{i}-2i)!
which completes the proof.

∎

\displaystyle b_{0,1}^{Sp}(0,n)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}n!}{2^{n+1}}\sum_{2l\leq n}\frac{1}{(n-2l)!(2l+1)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}n!}{2^{n+1}(n+1)!}\sum_{2l\leq n}\binom{n+1}{2l+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}(n+1)}\left(\sum_{2l\leq n}\binom{n}{2l}+\sum_{2l\leq n-1}\binom{n}{2l+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}(n+1)}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2(n+1)},

where we have used standard properties of the binomial coefficient. In the same manner, by Theorem
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle b_{0,1}^{SO}(0,n)

\displaystyle=2^{1-n}n!\sum_{2l\leq n}\frac{1}{(n-2l)!(2l)!}

\displaystyle=2^{1-n}\sum_{2l\leq n}\binom{n}{2l}

\displaystyle=2^{1-n}\left(\sum_{2l\leq n-1}\binom{n-1}{2l}+\sum_{2l\leq n}\binom{n-1}{2l-1}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{1-n}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{l}

\displaystyle=1.

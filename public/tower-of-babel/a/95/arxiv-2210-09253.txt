Interacting Jump Processes Preserve Semi-Global Markov Random Fields on Path Space
Ankan Ganguly, Kavita Ramanan

Abstract
Consider a system of interacting particles indexed by the nodes of a graph whose vertices are equipped with marks representing parameters of the model such as the environment or initial data. Each particle takes values in a countable state space and evolves according to a (possibly non-Markovian) continuous-time pure jump process whose jump intensities depend only on its own state (or history) and marks as well as the states (or histories) and marks of particles and edges in its neighborhood in the graph. Under mild conditions on the jump intensities, it is shown that the trajectories of the interacting particle system exhibit a certain local or semi-global Markov random field property whenever the initial condition satisfies the same property. Our results complement recent works that establish the preservation of a local second-order Markov random field property for interacting diffusions. Our proof methodology in the context of jump processes is different, and works directly on infinite graphs, thereby bypassing any limiting arguments. Our results apply to models arising in diverse fields including statistical physics, neuroscience, epidemiology and opinion dynamics, and have direct applications to the study of marginal distributions of interacting particle systems on Cayley trees.

Introduction

A pure jump interacting particle system (IPS) describes a collection of randomly evolving particles indexed
by the nodes of an underlying graph, where the dynamics of each particle in the collection is described by a pure jump process on a discrete state space, with jump rates depending not only on its own state but
also on the states of neighboring particles in the graph.
Commonly studied IPS include the voter model, contact process and Glauber dynamics for various
statistical physics spin models like the Ising and Potts models
[
30
]
, as well as many other models
arising in engineering and operations research (see
[
17
]
for a list of references).
Several works over the last two decades have shown that such IPS do not preserve Gibbsianness
[
13
,
25
,
26
,
12
,
14
,
23
,
15
]
. In fact, as demonstrated in
[
13
,
12
,
25
,
14
,
15
]
, even if the initial states of particles form a Markov random field (MRF) with respect to the underlying interaction graph,
the collection of states of all particles at some future time may fail to form a Markov random field (MRF)
of any order (with respect to the same graph). In fact, this can occur even if the IPS is ergodic with a stationary distribution that is an MRF (see, e.g.
[
13
]
). As is well known, given a locally finite graph
G=(V,E)
and a Polish space
{\mathcal{Z}}
, the
{\mathcal{Z}}^{V}
-valued random element
Z
is said to form a (local) MRF if for any disjoint partition
A,B,S
of
V
such that
S
is equal to
{\mathcal{N}}_{A}(G)
, the neighborhood of the finite set
A
in
G
,

Z_{A}\perp\!\!\!\perp Z_{B}|Z_{S}.

(1.1)

Furthermore, for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
Z
is said to form an
\alpha
-MRF if instead
S
is equal to
{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}_{A}(G)
, the
\alpha
-neighborhood of the finite set
A
in
G
(which is the set of vertices in
A^{c}
that lie at a distance of at most
\alpha
from
A
) above. On infinite graphs, one can also consider global MRFs: an MRF or
\alpha
-MRF is said to be
global
if (
1.1
) holds even when
A
is infinite. Instead, we introduce the intermediate notion of a semi-global MRF (SGMRF), which will turn out to be more relevant for our purposes. An SGMRF or
\alpha
-SGMRF is defined analogously to the MRF (respy.
\alpha
-MRF) property except that (
1.1
) must hold even for infinite
A
whose
\alpha
-neighborhood
S
is finite. In this article we show (under general conditions on the jump intensities and the interaction graph) that
if the initial states
form an
\alpha
-MRF (respy.
\alpha
-SGMRF) with
\alpha\geq 2
, then the
trajectories
also form an
\alpha
-MRF (respy.
\alpha
-SGMRF).
In particular, this shows that
there is preservation of the
\alpha
-MRF and
\alpha
-SGMRF properties at the level of trajectories, even if
not at the level of states.
In addition, we also show this in general fails to hold if
\alpha=1
(see Example
3.8
).

The definition of an
\alpha
-SGMRF is a natural extension of the definition of a “Markov chain on a tree,” as stated in
[
35
, Section 2]
and
[
20
, Chapter 12]
, to higher-order Markov chains and general graphs (see Appendix
B
for further discussion). An important motivation for establishing the second-order
SGMRF property is that it can be used to obtain autonomous descriptions of
marginal dynamics for IPS on trees as unique solutions to certain associated local equations
[
19
, Chapter 6]
.
As shown in
[
17
, Theorem 4.3 and Corollary 4.7]
,
the local equations describe the limit of both the neighborhood empirical measure
as well as the marginal dynamics
at the root of IPS on sequences of uniformly rooted random regular graphs whose sizes
grow to infinity, much in the spirit of mean-field limits for IPS on complete graphs
[
32
]
.
The MRF property by itself is insufficient for such a characterization (as also observed in
[
28
]
in the context of diffusions).

Our results in fact apply to a far more general class of IPS characterized as solutions to Poisson-driven stochastic differential equations (SDEs) that may be non-Markovian or heterogeneous. Non-Markovian dynamics are crucial to model a variety of applications in
neuroscience, epidemiology and engineering,
and heterogeneities arise naturally in many settings, including load balancing models
[
1
,
16
,
34
]
. We capture heterogeneities in the dynamics by equipping the interaction graph with
(possibly random) marks on the vertices, which
specify the initial states and/or
initial histories of the particles, as well as heterogeneities in the dynamics,
random environments and asymmetries in the local interactions with respect to the neighboring particles.
The jump rates of
each particle are allowed to depend on the histories of neighboring particles as well as the marks
of vertices in the neighborhood (a precise model description is given in Section
3.1
).
When the rates satisfy some mild regularity conditions (stated in Assumptions
3.1
and

3.4
), our main result (Theorem
3.7
) shows that if the random marks form an MRF or SGMRF of order

\alpha\geq 2
, then the trajectories of the IPS also exhibit the same MRF property.

To the best of our knowledge, this article is the first exploration of MRF properties of trajectories of IPS described by jump processes. However, there exist results of a similar flavor in the context of diffusions (see
[
11
,
10
,
7
,
27
]
and references therein). Specifically, Theorem 2.7 of
[
27
]
establishes conditions under which trajectories of interacting diffusions preserve the second-order
local

MRF property. Our results generalize those of
[
27
]
in the jump process context by considering higher-order MRFs, as well as SGMRFs
rather than just MRFs, and
weakening assumptions on the initial data, allowing for more general initial data than the initial state of the process.
Specifically, unlike in
[
27
]
, we do not require that the initial conditions be absolutely continuous with respect to any product measure. This is of particular interest in the study of marginals of stationary Markov processes, as well as non-Markov processes, for which the initial data includes the history of the process before time zero.
These “infinite histories” are typically highly singular so that even on finite graphs, the initial data will typically fail to be absolutely continuous with respect to any product measure.

Despite some similarity in the results, it is worth emphasizing that our proof technique differs from that used for diffusions in
[
27
]
. In the latter work, the trajectories of interacting diffusions are first shown to preserve the 2-MRF property on finite graphs, and then a limiting argument
is used to extend to infinite graphs. This argument exploits the fact that interacting diffusions on infinite graphs arise as local weak limits of interacting diffusions on finite graphs
[
29
, Theorem 3.7]
, and shows that the

2
-MRF property is preserved along suitably constructed convergent sequences. This approach requires one to impose certain assumptions about the continuity of the dynamics with respect to the initial condition (to ensure the aforementioned local weak convergence).
In contrast, we directly prove our main result for IPS on infinite graphs without invoking of limiting arguments. A brief outline of our approach, which allows us to handle both the MRF and SGMRF properties in a unified manner, is as follows. First, given an IPS
we construct an associated sequence of reference processes on infinite graphs, whose (initial data and) trajectories satisfy a certain conditional independence property that is akin to an MRF property. We then show that the law of the IPS is absolutely continuous with respect to that of each of the reference processes and use the form of the Radon-Nikodym derivative to deduce the MRF or SGMRF property
of the IPS from the conditional independence properties of the reference processes. An intermediate step in this process that may be of independent interest is an infinite-dimensional Girsanov theorem for IPS on possibly non-locally finite graphs even when the defining SDE may have multiple weak solutions (see Proposition
4.9
). The proof of this proposition proceeds by first establishing a duality between the IPS and a point process and then applying extensions of standard results for non-explosive point processes to the explosive marked point process setting to deduce the resulting Radon-Nikodym derivative.

In Section
2
we establish basic definitions and notations that will be in use throughout the article. In Section
3
, we introduce the model, state our main results and provide certain counterexamples that suggest our results cannot, in general, be reasonably strengthened. In Section
4
, we prove the main result taking for granted the absolute continuity of the IPS with respect to the reference processes and the form of the associated Radon-Nikodym derivatives. The latter are derived in Section
5
. In Appendix
A
, we prove a technical lemma that is used to describe the conditional structure of the reference processes. In Appendix
B
we provide an alternate characterization of the SGMRF property and derive associated properties. In Appendix
C
we show that the point process dual is a well-defined property. Lastly, in Appendix
D
, we apply the results of
[
17
]
to prove that the the sequence of reference IPS is well defined under the conditions we impose upon it.

Model Description and Main Results

3.1
Model Description and Assumptions

We consider IPS in which each particle takes
values in a countable state space
{\mathcal{X}}
(viewed without loss of generality as a subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}
) and has state
transitions that lie in some finite jump set

{\mathcal{J}}\subseteq\{i-j:i,j\in{\mathcal{X}},i\neq j\}
. We restrict consideration to the case
|{\mathcal{J}}|<\infty
because this setting leads to simpler and more transparent expressions and
seems to cover most examples of interest, although it is straightforward to generalize our results to the case of a countable jump set
{\mathcal{J}}
. The data specifying the model consists of a deterministic (simple, locally finite, undirected) graph
G=(V,E)
that encodes the interaction structure, the initial data
\mathbf{\kappa}\in{\mathcal{K}}^{V}
, where
{\mathcal{K}}
is a Polish space,
and a family of jump rate functions

r^{v}_{j}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times({\mathcal{K}}\times{\mathcal{D}})^{V}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+},j\in{\mathcal{J}},v\in V
, that specifies the dynamics, where
{\mathcal{D}}
is the space of càdlàg functions taking values in
{\mathcal{X}}
(using the notation from Section
2
). The initial data
\mathbf{\kappa}
can not only capture the initial state at time zero (for a Markov process) or history before time zero (for a non-Markovian process), it can also be used to encode other state parameters of the model such as random environments and heterogeneities in particle dynamics (see
[
17
, Section 4.4]
for concrete examples).
We assume interactions between particles are local (with respect to the graph
G
), predictable and with regular paths,
as encapsulated in the following assumption.

Assumption 3.1
.

The family of rate functions

\mathbf{r}:=\{r^{v}_{j}\}_{v\in V,j\in{\mathcal{J}}}
consists of Borel measurable functions from
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times({\mathcal{K}}\times{\mathcal{D}})^{V}
to
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
that satisfy the following three conditions:

1.

(locality)
for every
v\in V
and
j\in{\mathcal{J}}
, there exists a function
\widetilde{r}^{v}_{j}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times({\mathcal{K}}\times{\mathcal{D}})^{\text{cl}_{v}}
such that for every
(t,\vartheta,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times({\mathcal{K}}\times{\mathcal{D}})^{V}
,

r^{v}_{j}(t,\vartheta,x)=\widetilde{r}^{v}_{j}(t,\vartheta_{\text{cl}_{v}},x_{\text{cl}_{v}})\qquad\mbox{ for all }(t,\vartheta,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times({\mathcal{K}}\times{\mathcal{D}})^{V};

2.

(predictability)
for every

\vartheta\in{\mathcal{K}}^{V}
,
t>0
,
v\in V
and
x,y\in{\mathcal{D}}^{V}
,

x(s)=y(s):\quad s\in[0,t)\quad\Rightarrow\quad r^{v}_{j}(t,\vartheta,x)=r^{v}_{j}(t,\vartheta,y);

3.

(regularity) for every
v\in V,j\in{\mathcal{J}}
and
(\vartheta,x)\in({\mathcal{K}}\times{\mathcal{D}})^{V}
, the map
(0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto r^{v}_{j}(t,\vartheta,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is càglàd.

In what follows,
{\rm Leb}
is Lebesgue measure on

and

.

Definition 3.2
.

The
solution space
associated with the
{\mathcal{K}}^{V}
-valued (random) initial data
\mathbf{\kappa}

is a complete filtered probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
with
{\mathbb{F}}
satisfying the usual conditions, which supports the initial data
\mathbf{\kappa}
with
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\supseteq\sigma(\mathbf{\kappa})
, and a collection of i.i.d.
{\mathbb{F}}
-Poisson processes
\mathbf{N}:=\{\mathbf{N}_{v}\}_{v\in V}
on the space
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}\times{\mathcal{J}}
with intensity measure
{\rm Leb}^{2}\otimes\#_{{\mathcal{J}}}
, referred to as
driving Poisson processes
.

Given the solution space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
, initial data
\mathbf{\kappa}
and jump rate function family
\mathbf{r}:=\{r^{v}_{j}\}_{v\in V,j\in{\mathcal{J}}}
that satisfy Assumption
3.1
, we now describe the associated IPS
X
as a solution to the following Poisson-driven SDE:

X_{v}(t)=X_{v}(0)+\int_{(0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathcal{J}}}j{\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{r\leq r^{v}_{j}(s,\mathbf{\kappa},X)\right\}}\,\mathbf{N}_{v}(ds,dr,dj),\quad v\in V,t\in[0,\infty).

(3.1)

Note that Assumption
3.1
implies that
(s,\mathbf{\kappa},X)\mapsto r^{v}_{j}(s,\mathbf{\kappa},X)
only
depends on
X
and
\mathbf{\kappa}
only via
X_{\text{cl}_{v}}[s)
and
\mathbf{\kappa}_{\text{cl}_{v}}
.
Since, as mentioned above, the initial data
\mathbf{\kappa}
may contain more than the initial state, we will find it convenient to express the latter as a Borel measurable function
\xi:{\mathcal{K}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
of the initial data:

X_{v}(0):=\xi(\mathbf{\kappa}_{v}),\quad v\in V.

(3.2)

We call
\xi
the
initial condition map
, and we will refer to
(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
as the initial data pair.

Definition 3.3
.

A
weak solution
to the SDE (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) for the initial data
\mathbf{\kappa}
is a
{\mathbb{F}}
-adapted càdlàg stochastic process
X
defined on an associated solution space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})

that satisfies (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) almost surely. The SDE (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) is said to be
strongly well-posed
for the initial data
\mathbf{\kappa}
if on any solution space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
associated with
\mathbf{\kappa}
,
there exists a weak solution
X
to (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) for the initial data
\mathbf{\kappa}
and the SDE (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) is pathwise unique in the sense that
given any other weak solution
Y
to (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) on the same solution space (and hence, with the same driving Poisson processes and initial data) it follows
that
X=Y
almost surely.

Our main result holds under the following mild condition that the jump rate functions at a vertex
satisfy a certain degree-dependent bound.

Assumption 3.4
.

There exists a function
C:{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
that is non-decreasing in each of its arguments and such that for any
v\in V,j\in{\mathcal{J}},k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
r^{v}_{j}(t,\cdot,\cdot)\leq C(|\text{cl}_{v}|,t)
.

Remark 3.5
.

Most IPS of interest satisfy
Assumptions
3.1
and
3.4
(e.g., see
[
17
, Section 4.4]
).
As shown in Lemma
4.2
, under these assumptions it follows from

[
17
, Theorem 4.2, Propositions 5.15 and 5.17]
that
strong well-posedness of the IPS holds on a very large class of graphs that includes bounded degree graphs
and almost sure realizations of Galton-Watson trees
whose offspring distributions have finite first moments. Thus, the main result, Theorem
3.7
, in this article pertains to the case in which the SDE (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) is strongly well-posed.
However, the change of measure result in Proposition
4.9
and the duality result in
Proposition
5.2
are established under more general assumptions that do not require (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) to be well-posed.

Remark 3.6
.

The description of a weak solution in Definition
3.3
corresponds to a
({\mathbb{F}},\mathbf{N})
-weak solution in

[
17
, Remark 3.5]
. However, when we consider settings in which the SDE (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) is strongly well-posed, it follows from

[
17
, Lemma 3.10]
that every such weak solution is necessarily also
strong
in the sense that it is adapted to the filtration
{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathbf{\kappa}}\vee{\mathbb{H}}^{\mathbf{N}}
. Given this equivalence, we omit the qualifier “weak” or “strong” for solutions to (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) when the SDE is strongly well-posed.

Unless otherwise specified, we use
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
and
\mathbf{N}
to denote the solution space and driving Poisson processes of (
3.1
)-(
3.2
).

3.2
Main Result and Counterexamples

Our main result shows that the trajectories of the IPS propagate certain MRF properties. To state these precisely, recall the definition of MRF,
\alpha
-MRF, SGMRF and
\alpha
-SGMRF from Section
1
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Suppose the jump rate function family
\mathbf{r}:=\{r^{v}_{j}\}_{v\in V,j\in{\mathcal{J}}}
satisfies Assumptions
3.1
and
3.4
, and
G
is either a graph of bounded degree or
an a.s. realization
of a Galton-Watson tree whose offspring distribution has a finite first moment. Given an initial data pair
(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
, let
X=X[\infty)
be a solution to (
3.1
)-(
3.2
).
If
\mathbf{\kappa}
forms an
\alpha
-MRF (respy.
\alpha
-SGMRF) with respect to
G
for some integer
\alpha\geq 2
, then for each
t\in(0,\infty]
,
(\mathbf{\kappa},X[t))
forms an
\alpha
-MRF (respy.
\alpha
-SGMRF) with respect to
G
.

Theorem
3.7
is a direct consequence of a more general result, Proposition
4.3
, which
establishes
this “preservation of MRF” property for IPS on a broader class of graphs (that satisfy the condition stated in
Assumption
4.1
), and Lemma
4.2
, which shows that this class
includes the graphs mentioned in Theorem
3.7
.
The proof of Proposition
4.3
is presented in Section
4.4
.

Theorem
3.7

is used in forthcoming work
[
18
]
to obtain an autonomous characterization of the marginal distribution on the root neighborhood of an IPS with homogeneous jump rate functions on
the
d
-regular tree. As elaborated in the next section,
the theorem is also relevant to the study of Gibbs-non Gibbs transitions of spin models.

We now describe two counterexamples that demonstrate that the results in
Theorem
3.7
cannot in general be improved.
Specifically, the first example shows that the
analog of Theorem
3.7
does not in general hold when
\alpha=1
.

Example 3.8
.

There exists an IPS
X
on a finite graph
G=(V,E)
with jump rate functions
\mathbf{r}
and initial data pair
(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
such that the components of
\mathbf{\kappa}
are mutually independent and for which
(\mathbf{\kappa},X[t))
does not form an MRF for any
t>0
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be the path on three vertices with
V=\{1,2,3\}
and
E=\{\{1,2\},\{2,3\}\}
. Let
{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{X}}=\{0,1\}
,
{\mathcal{J}}=\{1\}
. Also, let
\xi
be the identity map on
{\mathcal{K}}
. Set
X_{2}(0)=\mathbf{\kappa}_{2}:=0
and let
(X_{1}(0),X_{3}(0))=(\mathbf{\kappa}_{1},\mathbf{\kappa}_{3})
be i.i.d. Bernoulli(
1/2
) random variables. Note that then
\{\mathbf{\kappa}_{v}\}_{v=1,2,3}
are mutually independent.

Consider the following (Markovian) jump rate functions,
r^{v}_{1}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathcal{D}}^{V}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
:

r^{1}_{1}\equiv r^{3}_{1}\equiv 0\quad\text{ and }\quad r^{2}_{1}(t,x)={\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{x_{1}(0)\neq x_{3}(0),x_{2}(t-)=0\right\}}.

This model satisfies Assumptions
3.1

and
3.4
, and the maximum degree of any vertex in
G
is
2
. Now,
X_{1}(0)\neq X_{3}(0)
with probability
1/2
and
X_{2}\equiv 0
on the event
\{X_{1}(0)=X_{3}(0)\}
. Note that the function
f:{\mathcal{D}}_{t-}\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined by
f(y)=y(0)
is bounded and
measurable, and on the event
\{X_{2}(t-)=1\}
, which implies
X_{1}(0)\neq X_{3}(0)
, it is easily verified that

\begin{array}[]{rll}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(X_{1}[t))|X_{2}[t)\right]&={\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{1}(0)=1|X_{2}[t)\right)&=\frac{1}{2},\\
{\mathbb{E}}\left[f(X_{1}[t))|X_{\{2,3\}}[t)\right]&={\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{1}(0)=1|X_{\{2,3\}}[t)\right)&=1-X_{3}(0).\end{array}

Since
X_{3}(0)
is random,
\text{Law}(X_{1}[t)|X_{\{2,3\}}[t))\neq\text{Law}(X_{1}[t)|X_{2}[t))
. Hence, the trajectories
X[t)
do not form an MRF for any
t>0
.

Next we observe that for some
t>0
, the states
X(t)=\{X_{v}(t)\}_{v\in V}
(as opposed to the trajectories)
may fail to form an
\alpha
-MRF for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
even if the initial data
\mathbf{\kappa}
forms an SGMRF.
Indeed, this follows from the substantial literature on the topic of dynamic transitions of IPS from Gibbs to non-Gibbs states as demonstrated in the example below.

Example 3.9
.

Fix
d>2
. Let
G
be the infinite
d
-regular tree and let
\nu
be the positive-boundary ferromagnetic Ising model for an inverse temperature-magnetic field pair
(\beta,h)
at which the Ising model experiences a phase transition (as described in
[
20
, Section 12.2]
).
If
X
is the IPS corresponding to infinite-temperature Glauber dynamics (as described in
[
13
]
) with initial condition

X(0)\sim\nu
, then
X(0)
forms a 1-SGMRF, but there exists an interval
[t_{1},t_{2})\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that for all
t\in[t_{1},t_{2})
,
\{X_{v}(t)\}_{v\in V}
does not form an
\alpha
-order MRF for any
\alpha\geq 1
.

The fact that
X(0)
is an MRF follows from the fact that it is a Gibbs measure associated with a Markovian specification (see
[
20
, Section 12.2]
). Furthermore, as an extremal countable state MRF
[
20
, Theorem 12.31]
, it is also a Markov chain on the tree
[
35
, Corollary 2]
. By Lemma
B.2
, this implies that it is also an SGMRF and therefore an
\alpha
-SGMRF for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
The assertion of the example then follows from
[
12
, Theorem 3.9 and Remark 3.10]
. Since
\text{Law}(X(t))
is non-Gibbs (i.e., non-quasilocal) in the stated interval,
X(t)
fails to form an
\alpha
-MRF for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

4
A Generalization of the Main Result

4.1
Statement of the More General Result

For simplicity of formulation, in Theorem
3.7
we only addressed certain classes of graphs
G
.
However, as shown in Proposition
4.3
below,
the conclusion of Theorem
3.7
in fact holds
for any graph-jump rate function pair that satisfies the following more general
(but less transparent) assumption.
The assumption is expressed in terms of a certain family of reference processes which are modified versions of the original
family of jump rate functions
\mathbf{r}:=\{r^{v}_{j}\}_{v\in V,j\in{\mathcal{J}}}

defined as follows:
for any
W\subset V
, let
\widehat{\mathbf{r}}^{W}:=\{\widehat{r}^{W,v}_{j}\}_{v\in V,j\in{\mathcal{J}}}
be the family of jump rate functions defined by

\widehat{r}^{W,v}_{j}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot):=\begin{cases}r^{v}_{j}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)&\text{ if }v\notin W,\\
1&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(4.1)

The property that each reference IPS is a
strong
solution to the
associated SDE plays a crucial role in the proof of
Proposition
4.3
(see Remark
3.6
and
Proposition
4.4
). This motivates the following assumption.

Assumption 4.1
.

The graph
G
, the initial data pair
(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
and the family of jump rate functions
\mathbf{r}:=\{r^{v}_{j}\}_{v\in V,j\in{\mathcal{J}}}
are
such that for every finite (and possibly empty)
W\subseteq V
,
the SDE (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) is strongly well-posed for the initial data

\mathbf{\kappa}
when
\mathbf{r}
is replaced with the modified
jump rate function family
\widehat{\mathbf{r}}^{W}:=\{\widehat{r}^{W,v}_{j}\}_{v\in V,j\in{\mathcal{J}}}
defined in (
4.1
).

The next lemma shows that this assumption holds under the conditions of
Theorem
3.7
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
\mathbf{r}:=\{r^{v}_{j}\}_{v\in V,j\in{\mathcal{J}}}
satisfies
Assumptions
3.1
and
3.4
and

G
is either a graph with finite maximal degree or an
a.s. realization of a Galton-Watson tree whose offspring distribution
has a finite first moment.
Then
G
,
(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
and
\mathbf{r}
satisfy
Assumption
4.1
.

The proof of Lemma
4.2
is a technical extension of results in
[
17
]
, and thus deferred to Appendix
D
. As shown therein, Assumption
4.1
in fact holds for the large class of finitely dissociable graphs introduced in
[
17
, Definition 5.11]
whenever the jump rate function family
\mathbf{r}
satisfies Assumptions
3.1
and
3.4
.

We now state the generalization of Theorem
3.7
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose the graph
G
, initial data pair
(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
and jump rate function family
\mathbf{r}
satisfies Assumptions
3.1
,
3.4
and
4.1
. Let
X
be the solution to the SDE (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) and let
\alpha\geq 2
be an integer.
If
\mathbf{\kappa}
forms an
\alpha
-MRF (respy.
\alpha
-SGMRF) with respect to
G
then for each
t\in(0,\infty]
,
(\mathbf{\kappa},X[t))
forms an
\alpha
-MRF (respy.
\alpha
-SGMRF) with respect to
G
.

The proof of Proposition
4.3
is given in Section
4.4
. Its outline is as follows. Fix
G
,
(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
and
\mathbf{r}
as in the proposition, and let
X=\{X_{v}\}_{v\in V}
be a solution to the associated SDE (
3.1
)-(
3.2
).
Also, fix
\alpha\geq 2
and assume that
\mathbf{\kappa}
forms an
\alpha
-MRF on
{\mathcal{K}}^{V}

with respect to
G
.
For any
t\in(0,\infty)
, our proof that
(\mathbf{\kappa},X[t))
also forms an
\alpha
-MRF can be broken into four main steps. First, in Section
4.2
,
we construct a sequence of
{\mathcal{X}}^{V}
-valued “reference processes”
\{\widehat{X}^{n}\}_{n\in\ N},
with initial data pair
(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
having the property that
for any partition
A,B,S
of
V
for which
S={\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}_{A}
and
A
is finite, we have

(\mathbf{\kappa}_{A},\widehat{X}^{n}_{A}[t))\perp\!\!\!\perp(\mathbf{\kappa}_{B},\widehat{X}^{n}_{B}[t))|(\mathbf{\kappa}_{S},\widehat{X}^{n}_{S}[t)),\quad t\in(0,\infty)

(4.2)

for all
n
sufficiently large (depending on
S
).
Next, in Section
4.3
we compute the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the law on path space of the IPS
X
with respect to that of
the reference process

\widehat{X}^{n}
for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
by first establishing a duality relation between IPS and point processes (see Proposition
5.2
) and then leveraging results from point process theory. Next, combining an explicit factorization of this Radon-Nikodym derivative with
a slight modification of a result from
[
33
]
(see Lemma
4.12
), we prove in Section
4.4
that (
4.2
) must hold with
\widehat{X}^{n}
replaced by
X
, which implies that
(\mathbf{\kappa},X[t))
forms an
\alpha
-MRF with respect to
G

for all
t\in(0,\infty)
. Finally, we extend the result to the case
t=\infty
via a standard martingale argument.
The proof of preservation of the SGMRF property proceeds in a similar fashion, first assuming
\mathbf{\kappa}
forms an
\alpha
-SGMRF and following the above argument, where now
A
may be infinite, but
S
must still be finite.

4.2
Reference Processes and their Conditional Independence Properties

For the remainder of the article, we assume that
{\mathcal{X}}={\mathbb{Z}}
. Note that this is without loss of generality because
the IPS
X
may be regarded as a process with
state space
{\mathbb{Z}}^{V}
such that
X_{v}(t)\in{\mathcal{X}}
almost surely for all
v\in V
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. Given initial data pair
(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
from (
3.2
), for any finite vertex set
W\subseteq V
, let
\widehat{X}^{W}
be the solution to the SDE

\widehat{X}^{W}_{v}(t)=\begin{cases}\xi(\mathbf{\kappa}_{v})+\int_{(0,t]\times(0,1]\times{\mathcal{J}}}j\,\mathbf{N}_{v}(ds,dr,dj)&\text{ if }v\in W,\\
\xi(\mathbf{\kappa}_{v})+\int_{(0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathcal{J}}}j{\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{r\leq r^{v}_{j}(s,\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{W})\right\}}\,\mathbf{N}_{v}(ds,dr,dj)&\text{ if }v\notin W.\end{cases}

(4.3)

The equation (
4.3
) can be rewritten in terms of the modified rate function family
\widehat{\mathbf{r}}^{W}:=\{\widehat{r}^{W,v}_{j}\}_{v\in V,j\in{\mathcal{J}}}
as follows:

\widehat{X}^{W}_{v}(t)=\xi(\mathbf{\kappa}_{v})+\int_{(0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathcal{J}}}j{\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{r\leq\widehat{r}^{W,v}_{j}(s,\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{W})\right\}}\,\mathbf{N}_{v}(ds,dr,dj),\quad v\in V.

Thus, by Assumption
4.1
, the SDE (
4.3
) is strongly well-posed and
\widehat{X}^{W}
is a.s. uniquely defined.

Fix an arbitrary vertex
\o\in V
and define

\widehat{X}^{n}:=\widehat{X}^{V_{n}}\quad\text{ for }\quad V_{n}:=\{v\in V:d_{G}(v,\o)\leq n\},\qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(4.4)

The main result of this section, Proposition
4.4
below,
shows that
(\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{n})
has a conditional structure that partially resembles an
\alpha
-SGMRF.

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose
G,(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
and
\mathbf{r}
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
,
3.4
and
4.1
, and for each
n\geq 2
let
\widehat{X}^{n}
be as defined in (
4.3
) and (
4.4
). Fix an integer
\alpha\geq 1
and let

A,B,S\subseteq V
form a partition of
V
such that
S={\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}_{A}\subseteq V_{n-1}
and also suppose

\mathbf{\kappa}_{A}\perp\!\!\!\perp\mathbf{\kappa}_{B}|\mathbf{\kappa}_{S}
. Then for any
t\in(0,\infty)
,

(\mathbf{\kappa}_{A},\widehat{X}^{n}_{A}[t))\perp\!\!\!\perp(\mathbf{\kappa}_{B},\widehat{X}^{n}_{B}[t))|(\mathbf{\kappa}_{S},\widehat{X}^{n}_{S}[t)).

(4.5)

The proof of Proposition
4.4
is given after the following abstract technical lemma, which provides sufficient conditions under which conditional independence properties can be transferred from one collection of random elements to another.

Lemma 4.5
.

For each
i,j=1,2,3
, let
Z^{j}_{i}
be a random element taking values in some Polish space
{\mathcal{Z}}^{j}_{i}
that satisfies the following properties:

1.

\{Z^{2}_{i}\}_{i=1,2,3}
is a set of mutually independent random elements that is independent of
\{Z^{1}_{i}\}_{i=1,2,3}
;

2.

{\mathcal{H}}^{Z^{1}_{i}}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}^{Z^{3}_{i}}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}^{Z^{\{1,2\}}_{i}}
for
i=1,2,3,
where
Z^{\{1,2\}}_{i}=(Z^{1}_{i},Z^{2}_{i})
.

Then

Z^{1}_{1}\perp\!\!\!\perp Z^{1}_{2}|Z^{1}_{3}

(4.6)

implies
Z_{1}^{3}\perp\!\!\!\perp Z_{2}^{3}|Z^{3}_{3}
.

Relegating the proof of the lemma to Appendix
A
,
we first apply it to prove Proposition
4.4
.

Proof of Proposition
4.4
:

Fix a partition
A,B,S\subseteq V
satisfying the conditions of the proposition, and for notational convenience, set
D_{1}:=A,D_{2}:=B,D_{3}:=S
. To prove Proposition
4.4
, it clearly suffices to show that the conditions of Lemma
4.5
are satisfied by
Z^{1}_{i}=\mathbf{\kappa}_{D_{i}}
,
Z^{2}_{i}=\mathbf{N}_{D_{i}}
and
Z^{3}_{i}=(\mathbf{\kappa}_{D_{i}},\widehat{X}^{n}_{D_{i}}[t))
,
i=1,2,3
, since then Lemma
4.5
implies that
Z_{1}^{3}\perp\!\!\!\perp Z_{2}^{3}|Z_{3}^{3}
, which is equivalent to (
4.5
).

First, note that
\mathbf{\kappa}_{D_{1}}\perp\!\!\!\perp\mathbf{\kappa}_{D_{2}}|\mathbf{\kappa}_{D_{3}}
by assumption and so
(Z^{1}_{j})_{j=1,2,3}
satisfies (
4.6
). Further, since
D_{i}
,
i=1,2,3,
are disjoint,

\{\mathbf{N}_{D_{i}}\}_{i=1,2,3}
are mutually independent and, by assumption, also independent of
\{\mathbf{\kappa}_{D_{i}}\}_{i=1,2,3}
. Thus,
Z^{1}
and
Z^{2}
satisfy
property 1 of Lemma
4.5
.
It only remains to verify the measurability condition stated in property 2.
The first inclusion

{\mathcal{H}}^{Z_{i}^{1}}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}^{Z_{i}^{3}}
holds trivially for
i=1,2,3
.
To prove the second inclusion, note that

\widehat{X}^{n}
is the a.s. unique solution to the SDE
(
4.3
) with
W=V_{n}
and so substituting the local jump rate functions
\widetilde{\mathbf{r}}:=\{\widetilde{r}^{v}_{j}\}_{v\in V,j\in{\mathcal{J}}}
from condition 1 of Assumption
3.1
, we see that its marginal

\widehat{X}^{n}_{A}
on the set
A
solves the following SDE:

\widehat{X}^{n}_{v}(t)=\begin{cases}\xi(\mathbf{\kappa}_{v})+\int_{(0,t]\times(0,1]\times{\mathcal{J}}}j\mathbf{N}_{v}(ds,dr,dj)&\text{ if }v\in V_{n}\cap A,\\
\xi(\mathbf{\kappa}_{v})+\int_{(0,t]\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathcal{J}}}j{\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{r\leq\widetilde{r}^{v}_{j}(s,\widehat{X}^{n}_{\text{cl}_{v}},\mathbf{\kappa}_{\text{cl}_{v}})\right\}}\mathbf{N}_{v}(ds,dr,dj)&\text{ if }v\in A\setminus V_{n}.\end{cases}

(4.7)

We now claim that
\text{cl}_{v}\subseteq A
for every
v\in A\setminus V_{n}
,
and thus the SDE (
4.7
) for the marginal
\widehat{X}_{A}
is autonomously defined.
The claim holds because for any
v\in A\setminus V_{n}
, the fact that
v\in A
,
S={\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}_{A}\subseteq V_{n-1}
and
S\cap B=\emptyset
imply

\text{cl}_{v}\cap B\subseteq S\cap B=\emptyset
, whereas the fact that
v\notin V_{n}
implies
\text{cl}_{v}\cap S\subseteq\text{cl}_{v}\cap V_{n-1}=\emptyset
. Since
A,B
and
S
form a partition of

V
, this shows that
\text{cl}_{v}\subseteq A
.

We now show that the SDE (
4.7
) is strongly well-posed.
Fix any solution space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
supporting the driving Poisson processes
\widetilde{\mathbf{N}}_{A}
(in the sense of Definition
3.2
) and
\mathbf{\kappa}_{A}
, and consider an extension
(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{{\mathcal{F}}},\tilde{\mathbb{F}},\tilde{\mathbb{P}})
of the solution space that
also supports i.i.d. Poisson processes
\widetilde{\mathbf{N}}_{V\setminus A}
and
\mathbf{\kappa}_{V\setminus A}
such that
\widetilde{\mathbf{N}}:=(\widetilde{\mathbf{N}}_{v})_{v\in V}
is a collection of driving Poisson processes of (
4.3
) with
W=V_{n}
.
Let
\widetilde{Y}^{A}
and
\widetilde{Z}^{A}
be two weak solutions to (
4.7
) on the solution space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
with the same initial
data
\mathbf{\kappa}_{A}
, and, recalling that (
4.3
) is strongly well-posed, let
\widetilde{X}=\widetilde{X}^{n}
be the a.s. unique solution to (
4.3
) with
W=V_{n}
and initial data
\mathbf{\kappa}
. Then, using the property that the marginal on
A
of any solution to (
4.7
) is autonomously defined, the processes

\widetilde{Y}_{v}:=\begin{cases}\widetilde{Y}^{A}_{v}&\text{ if }v\in A\\
\widetilde{X}_{v}&\text{ if }v\notin A\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{Z}_{v}:=\begin{cases}\widetilde{Z}^{A}_{v}&\text{ if }v\in A\\
\widetilde{X}_{v}&\text{ if }v\notin A\end{cases},

are both solutions to (
4.3
) on
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
with
W=V_{n}
. Therefore
\widetilde{X}=\widetilde{Y}=\widetilde{Z}
a.s., and so in particular

\widetilde{Y}^{A}=\widetilde{Z}^{A}
a.s.. Thus, (
4.7
) is strongly well-posed.
In turn, the strong well-posedness of (
4.7
) implies
\widehat{X}^{n}_{A}
is the a.s. unique solution to (
4.7
) driven by
\mathbf{N}_{A}
, and hence by Remark
3.6
,
(\mathbf{\kappa}_{A},\widehat{X}^{n}_{A}[t))
must be
{\mathcal{H}}_{t-}^{\mathbf{N}_{A}}\vee{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathbf{\kappa}_{A}}
-measurable. This proves the second inclusion in property 2 for the case
i=1
.

The case
i=2
can be argued similarly. We first claim that
{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}_{B}(G)\subseteq S
. Indeed, if the claim were not true, then since
A,B,S
is a (disjoint) partition of
V
,
it must be true that
{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}_{B}(G)\cap A\neq\emptyset
or equivalently, there must exist
u\in A
and
v\in B

such that
d_{G}(u,v)\leq\alpha
. However, since
S={\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}_{A}(G)
by assumption, this implies

v\in{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}_{A}(G)\cap B=S\cap B=\emptyset
, which is a contradiction. This proves the claim.
Given the claim, an identical argument as that used for
i=1
shows that
\widehat{X}^{n}_{B}
is autonomously defined and
(\mathbf{\kappa}_{B},\widehat{X}^{n}_{B}[t))
is
{\mathcal{H}}_{t-}^{\mathbf{N}_{B}}\vee{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathbf{\kappa}_{B}}
-measurable, thus proving the second inclusion
in property 2 of Lemma
4.5
when
i=2
. The proof for the case
i=3
is much simpler. By
(
4.3
) with
W=V_{n}
,
for each
v\in S\subset V_{n}
,
\widehat{X}^{n}_{v}
is given by the one-dimensional,
{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathbf{\kappa}_{S}}\vee{\mathbb{H}}^{\mathbf{N}_{S}}
-adapted process
t\mapsto\xi(\mathbf{\kappa}_{v})+\int_{(0,t]\times(0,1]\times{\mathcal{J}}}j\mathbf{N}_{v}(ds,dr,dj)
. This shows that
\widehat{X}^{n}_{v}
is a measurable function of
(\mathbf{\kappa}_{v},\mathbf{N}_{v})
. Since this is true for each
v\in S
, this proves that
property 2 of Lemma
4.5
also holds for
i=3
.
This completes the verification of the conditions of Lemma
4.5
for the
\{Z^{j}_{i}\}_{i,j\in\{1,2,3\}}
defined at the start of the proof,
and thus proves the proposition.
∎

4.3
Change of Measure Results on Infinite Graphs

In this section, we identify the form of the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the law of the solution to the SDE (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) with respect to that of the reference process
\widehat{X}^{W}
for finite
W
. To state our result, we first introduce the notions of proper trajectories and
their so-called jump characteristics. Recall the definition of
{\rm Disc}_{t}\left(x\right)
and
\Delta x
from Section
2
.

Definition 4.6
.

For any
U\subseteq V
, we say a càdlàg function
x\in{\mathcal{D}}^{U}
is proper if for every
u\neq v\in U
and
t<\infty
,
{\rm Disc}_{t}\left(x_{v}\right)\cap{\rm Disc}_{t}\left(x_{u}\right)=\emptyset
.

Definition 4.7
.

Fix
U\subseteq V
finite,
t\in(0,\infty)
, and let
x\in{\mathcal{D}}_{t-}^{U}
be proper. Then the
jump characteristics
of
x
are the elements
\{(t_{k}(x),j_{k}(x),v_{k}(x))\}\subset(0,\infty)\times{\mathcal{J}}\times U
, where
\{t_{k}(x)\}:={\rm Disc}_{\infty}\left(x\right)

is an increasing sequence, and
\Delta x_{v_{k}(x)}(t_{k}(x))=j_{k}(x)
for each
k<|{\rm Disc}_{\infty}\left(x\right)|+1
. When the trajectory
x
is clear from the context, we simply write
\{(t_{k},j_{k},v_{k})\}
for
\{(t_{k}(x),j_{k}(x),v_{k}(x))\}
.

We now establish conditions under which the jump characteristics of a process exist.

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose the jump rate function family
\mathbf{r}
satisfies conditions 2 and 3 of Assumption
3.1
. Given the initial data pair
(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
, for any finite
W\subseteq V
, let
\widehat{X}^{W}
be any weak solution to the SDE (
4.3
) and let
X
be any weak solution to (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) for the same initial data pair
(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
. Then for any finite
U\subseteq V
, the jump characteristics of
\widehat{X}^{W}_{U}
and
X_{U}
are almost surely well defined.

Proof.

Note that
\widehat{X}^{\emptyset}
is a weak solution to (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) so we may write
X=\widehat{X}^{\emptyset}
without loss of generality. Thus, for any fixed, finite
U,W\subseteq V
, it suffices to prove that the jump characteristics of
\widehat{X}^{W}_{U}
exist. This occurs precisely when
\widehat{X}^{W}_{U}
is proper and its discontinuity times can be enumerated in increasing order. Suppose
\widehat{X}^{W}
solves (
4.3
) on the solution space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
supporting the driving Poisson processes
\mathbf{N}
. Fix any
u,v\in V
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. Note that by (
4.3
),

{\rm Disc}_{t}\left(\widehat{X}^{W}_{v}\right)\subseteq{\mathcal{E}}_{v}
for each
v\in V
,
where
{\mathcal{E}}_{v}:=\{s\in[0,t]:\mathbf{N}_{v}(\{s\}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathcal{J}})=1\}
. Thus, for any
u,v\in V
with
u\neq v
,

{\rm Disc}_{t}\left(\widehat{X}^{W}_{u}\right)\cap{\rm Disc}_{t}\left(\widehat{X}^{W}_{v}\right)\subseteq{\mathcal{E}}_{u}\cap{\mathcal{E}}_{v}=\emptyset\text{ a.s.}

by the independence of
\mathbf{N}_{u}
and
\mathbf{N}_{v}
. Therefore
\widehat{X}^{W}
is almost surely proper, which implies that
\widehat{X}^{W}_{U}
is proper. Furthermore, because
U
is finite,
{\mathcal{X}}^{U}
is discrete and equipped with a complete metric. Therefore,
|{\rm Disc}_{t}\left(\widehat{X}^{W}_{U}\right)|<\infty
for every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, which shows that the jump characteristics of
\widehat{X}^{W}_{U}
exist.
∎

Our next result is a general change of measure result that characterizes the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the law of the solution to the SDE (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) with respect to the reference process
\widehat{X}^{W}
in (
4.3
) in terms of the jump characteristics of
\widehat{X}^{W}_{W}
, which are a.s. well defined by Lemma
4.8
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
G
be a deterministic (not necessarily locally finite) graph. Suppose the jump rate function family
\mathbf{r}:=\{r^{v}_{j}\}_{v\in V,j\in{\mathcal{J}}}
satisfies conditions 2 and 3 of Assumption
3.1
. Let
W\subseteq V
be any finite set and denote
\widehat{\mu}^{W}:=\text{Law}(\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{W})
where
\widehat{X}^{W}
is any weak solution to the SDE (
4.3
) for the initial data pair
(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
. Also assume that for any
(t,j,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathcal{J}}\times V
,

\int_{0}^{t}r^{v}_{j}(s,\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{W})\,ds<\infty\text{ a.s..}

(4.8)

Define the filtration
{\mathbb{G}}:={\mathcal{H}}^{\mathbf{\kappa}}\vee{\mathbb{H}}^{\widehat{X}^{W}}
and define the process
L^{W}=(L_{t}^{W})_{t\geq 0}
as follows:

L^{W}_{t}:=\left[\prod_{0<t_{k}\leq t}r^{v_{k}}_{j_{k}}(t_{k},\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{W})\right]\exp\left(-\sum_{(j,v)\in{\mathcal{J}}\times W}\int_{(0,t]}\left(r^{v}_{j}(s,\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{W})-1\right)\,ds\right),

(4.9)

where
\{(t_{k},j_{k},v_{k})\}
are the jump characteristics of
\widehat{X}^{W}_{W}
.
Then
L^{W}
is a
{\mathbb{G}}
-local martingale. Moreoever, if
L^{W}
is also a
{\mathbb{G}}
-martingale, then there exist a measure
\widetilde{\mu}\in{\mathcal{P}}(({\mathcal{K}}\times{\mathcal{D}})^{V})
and a weak solution
X
to the SDE (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) for the initial data pair
(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
such that

\frac{d\widetilde{\mu}_{t-}}{d\widehat{\mu}^{W}_{t-}}(\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{W}[t))=L^{W}_{t-},\quad t>0,\mbox{a.s.},

where
\widetilde{\mu}_{t-}
and
\widehat{\mu}^{W}_{t-}
are the restrictions of the respective measures
\widetilde{\mu}
and
\widehat{\mu}^{W}
to the space
{\mathcal{B}}(({\mathcal{K}}\times{\mathcal{D}}_{t-})^{V})
, and
\text{Law}(\mathbf{\kappa},X)=\widetilde{\mu}
.

When
G
is finite, this result is well known if the IPS is Markov (e.g.
[
6
, Example 15.2.10]
). When
G
is finite and the IPS is non-Markov, the result can be deduced by combining duality characterizations of the IPS in terms of a point process (as discussed in Section
5.1
) with standard change of measure theorems for point processes
(e.g.,
[
6
, Theorem 15.2.7]
,
[
9
, Proposition 14.4.III]
or
[
5
, VIII T10]
) and
simple estimates to prove that the candidate Radon-Nikodym derivative is indeed a martingale (rather than just a local martingale). However, the case when
G
is infinite is more subtle even for Markov IPS due to the possibility of explosions,
and Proposition
4.11
in fact addresses the more general
case when the graph may not even be locally finite, necessitating further care.
This more general setting is
of interest for the study of analogous properties of IPS on random graphs. In fact, by letting
G
be the infinite, complete graph, Proposition
4.9
becomes applicable to solutions of a more general class of infinite-dimensional Poisson-driven SDEs not necessarily arising as locally interacting processes with
respect to some graph.
The proof of Proposition
4.9
relies on duality characterizations and is hence deferred to Section
5.2
.

Remark 4.10
.

Note that Proposition
4.9
does not require uniqueness in law of solutions to the SDEs (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) and (
4.3
). It simply establishes a correspondence between the laws of specific weak solutions to the two SDEs when

L^{W}
is a
{\mathbb{G}}
-Martingale.

However, in the presence of well-posedness, as a corollary of Proposition
4.9
, we obtain
the following change of measure result, which is used to prove Proposition
4.3
.

Corollary 4.11
.

Suppose
G
,
(\mathbf{\kappa},\xi)
and
\mathbf{r}
satisfy Assumptions
3.1
,
3.4
and
4.1
. For any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
let
V_{n}
be as in (
4.4
), let
\widehat{X}^{n}:=\widehat{X}^{V_{n}}
and
X
be solutions to (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) and (
4.3
), respectively, and let
\widehat{\mu}^{n}=\text{Law}(\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{n})
and
\mu=\text{Law}(\mathbf{\kappa},X)
. Then, for any
t\in(0,\infty),

\frac{d\mu_{t-}}{d\widehat{\mu}^{n}_{t-}}(\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{n}[t))=\left[\prod_{0<t_{k}^{n}<t}r^{v_{k}^{n}}_{j_{k}^{n}}(t_{k}^{n},\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{n})\right]\exp\left(-\sum_{(j,v)\in{\mathcal{J}}\times V_{n}}\int_{(0,t)}\left(r^{v}_{j}(s,\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{n})-1\right)\,ds\right)a.s.,

(4.10)

where
\{(t_{k}^{n},j_{k}^{n},v_{k}^{n})\}_{k}
is the set of jump characteristics of
\widehat{X}^{n}_{V_{n}}
in the sense of Definition
4.7
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
{\mathbb{G}}:={\mathcal{H}}^{\mathbf{\kappa}}\vee{\mathbb{H}}^{\widehat{X}^{n}}
. Applying Proposition
4.9
with
W=V_{n}
, the process

L^{n}:=L^{V_{n}}
in (
4.9
)
is a
{\mathbb{G}}
-local martingale. We first prove that it is in fact a
{\mathbb{G}}
-martingale. Let
\{\theta_{\ell}\}_{{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a localizing sequence for
L^{n}
and for each
{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\tau_{\ell}:=\inf\{t:|{\rm Disc}_{t}\left(\widehat{X}^{n}_{V_{n}}\right)|\geq{\ell}\}
where the infimum of an empty set is taken to be infinite. Note that
\tau_{\ell}
is a
{\mathbb{G}}
-stopping time. Because
\widehat{X}^{n}_{V_{n}}
is a.s. càdlàg and
{\mathcal{X}}^{V_{n}}
is a discrete space,
\widehat{X}^{n}_{V_{n}}
can only have finitely many discontinuities in any finite time interval. Thus,
\lim_{{\ell}\to\infty}\tau_{\ell}=\infty
a.s., so
\{\tau_{\ell}\wedge\theta_{\ell}\}_{{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is also a localizing sequence for
L^{n}
. Recall the definition of
C:{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
from Assumption
3.4
and let
d:=\max\{|\text{cl}_{v}|:v\in V_{n}\}
. Then for each
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,

\sup_{{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}}}|L^{n}_{t\wedge\tau_{\ell}\wedge\theta_{\ell}}|\leq|C(d,t)|^{{\ell}\wedge|{\rm Disc}_{t}\left(\widehat{X}^{n}_{V_{n}}\right)|}\exp\left(t|V_{n}||{\mathcal{J}}|\right)\leq|C(d,t)|^{|{\rm Disc}_{t}\left(\widehat{X}^{n}_{V_{n}}\right)|}\exp\left(t|V_{n}||{\mathcal{J}}|\right).

However, note that
|{\rm Disc}_{t}\left(\widehat{X}^{n}_{V_{n}}\right)|\sim\text{Poiss}(|V_{n}||{\mathcal{J}}|t)
because by (
4.1
)
\widehat{r}^{V_{n},v}_{j}(\cdot,\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{n})=1
whenever
(j,v)\in{\mathcal{J}}\times V_{n}
. Since
{\mathcal{J}}
is assumed to be finite, this implies

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}}}|L^{n}_{t\wedge\tau_{\ell}\wedge\theta_{\ell}}|\right]\leq\exp\left(t|V_{n}||{\mathcal{J}}|\right){\mathbb{E}}\left[|C(d,t)|^{|{\rm Disc}_{t}\left(\widehat{X}^{n}_{V_{n}}\right)|}\right]<\infty,

which shows that for each
t\geq 0
, the sequence
\{L^{n}_{t\wedge\tau_{\ell}\wedge\theta_{\ell}}\}_{{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is dominated by an integrable random variable, and is therefore uniformly integrable. By
[
8
, Proposition 1.8]
, it follows that
L^{n}
is a
{\mathbb{G}}
-martingale and for any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
{\mathbb{E}}[L^{n}_{t}]=1
. Proposition
4.9
then implies that the measure
\widetilde{\mu}\in{\mathcal{P}}(({\mathcal{K}}\times{\mathcal{D}})^{V})
defined by

d\widetilde{\mu}_{t-}/d\widehat{\mu}^{n}_{t-}(\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{n})=L^{n}_{t-}
a.s. for all
t>0
is the law of a weak solution
\widetilde{X}
to (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) for the initial data
\mathbf{\kappa}
on the graph
G
. By Assumption
4.1
, the SDE (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) is
well-posed, which implies that
(\mathbf{\kappa},\widetilde{X})\overset{\text{(d)}}{=}(\mathbf{\kappa},X)
.
This shows

d\mu_{t-}/d\widehat{\mu}^{n}_{t-}(\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{n})=d\widetilde{\mu}_{t-}/d\widehat{\mu}^{n}_{t-}(\mathbf{\kappa},\widehat{X}^{n})=L^{n}_{t-}
a.s. for every
t>0
, as desired.
∎

4.4
Proof of Proposition
4.3

As mentioned earlier, the proof of the preservation of the MRF property over any finite time interval proceeds by transfering analogous conditional
independence properties for the reference processes established in Proposition
4.4
to the original
IPS. This makes use of a modified version of a result from
[
33
]

stated in Lemma
4.12
below.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}})
be a measurable space, let

\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{i}\subset\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
,
i=0,1,2
, be sub-
\sigma
-algebras, and let

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}
be two probability measures on

(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}})
such that
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\ll\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
.
Assume that under
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
,
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{1}
and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{2}
are
conditionally independent given
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}
. If in addition, the Radon-Nikodym derivative
\rho:=d\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}/d\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
with respect to

\vee_{i=1}^{3}\widetilde{\mathbb{F}}_{i}
satisfies
\rho=\rho_{1}\rho_{2}
almost surely for some
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{i}\vee\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}
-measurable random variables
\rho_{i}
,
i=1,2,
then under
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}
,
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{1}
and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{2}
are also
conditionally independent given
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}
.

Proof.

If the filtrations were required to be complete, then this result would follow from
[
33
, Theorem 3.6]
under the stronger assumption that
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}
is equivalent to
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
. However, as elaborated below, the same argument used in
[
33
]
also shows that the result holds under the weaker assumption of a not necessarily complete filtration and only absolute continuity of
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}
with respect to
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
(rather than equivalence). Let
Z
be any bounded,
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{1}
-measurable random variable. Then, applying
[
3
, Proposition B.41]
and using the
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
-conditional independence of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{1}
and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{2}
given
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}
and the
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{i}
-measurability of
\rho_{i}
,
i=1,2,
we have
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}}\left[Z|\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}\vee\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}^{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{0}}\left[Z\rho_{1}\rho_{2}|\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}\vee\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{2}\right]}{{\mathbb{E}}^{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{0}}\left[\rho_{1}\rho_{2}|\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}\vee\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{2}\right]}=\frac{\rho_{2}{\mathbb{E}}^{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{0}}\left[Z\rho_{1}|\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}\vee\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{2}\right]}{\rho_{2}{\mathbb{E}}^{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{0}}\left[\rho_{1}|\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}\vee\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{2}\right]}=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}^{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{0}}\left[Z\rho_{1}|\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}\right]}{{\mathbb{E}}^{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{0}}\left[\rho_{1}|\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}\right]}.

Thus,
{\mathbb{E}}^{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}}\left[Z|\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}\vee\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{2}\right]
is
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}
-measurable and therefore
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}
-a.s. equal to
{\mathbb{E}}^{\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}}\left[Z|\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}\right]
. Since
Z
is arbitrary,
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{1}
and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{2}
are conditionally independent given
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{0}
under
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}
as well.
∎

partition

and for

\displaystyle=\prod_{v\in V_{n}}g_{v}\left(\mathbf{\kappa}_{\text{cl}_{v}},\widehat{X}^{n}_{\text{cl}_{v}}[t)\right)\text{ a.s..}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{v\in\text{cl}_{A}\cap V_{n}}g_{v}\left(\vartheta_{\text{cl}_{v}},x_{\text{cl}_{v}}\right)\right)\left(\prod_{u\in V_{n}\setminus\text{cl}_{A}}g_{u}\left(\vartheta_{\text{cl}_{u}},x_{\text{cl}_{u}}\right)\right).

(4.12)

Since

(\mathbf{\kappa}_{A},X_{A}[t))\perp\!\!\!\perp(\mathbf{\kappa}_{B},X_{B}[t))|(\mathbf{\kappa}_{S},X_{S}[t)).

(4.13)

\alpha
-MRF (respy.
\alpha
-SGMRF) with respect to

.

We now extend this to the infinite time interval to show that the same is true for
(\mathbf{\kappa},X)
.
The argument we use is similar to the one applied in the proof of
[

, Theorem 2.4]
. Fix an element

. For any

and

as

in
{\mathcal{D}}^{a}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[f_{A}(\mathbf{\kappa}_{A},X_{A})f_{B}(\mathbf{\kappa}_{B},X_{B})f_{S}(\mathbf{\kappa}_{S},X_{S})\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 24.0pt=\lim_{s_{2}\to\infty}\lim_{s_{1}\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f_{A}(\mathbf{\kappa}_{A},X_{A}[s_{2}))f_{B}(\mathbf{\kappa}_{B},X_{B}[s_{2}))f_{S}(\mathbf{\kappa}_{S},X_{S}[s_{1}))\right]

5
Proof of the Radon-Nikodym Derivative Characterization

The goal of this section is to prove the change of measure result in
Proposition
4.9
. The proof, which is given in Section
5.2
,
relies on a key duality characterization of the IPS that is first established in Proposition
5.2
of Section
5.1
.

5.1
Dual Processes

We start in Section
5.1.1
by introducing some standard notation related
to point processes.

5.1.1
Point Processes

Fix a filtered probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
supporting a filtration
{\mathbb{G}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}
and a Polish space
{\mathcal{Z}}
. Recall from Section
2
that
{\mathscr{N}}({\mathcal{Z}})
is the (Polish) space of locally finite, nonnegative integer-valued measures on
{\mathcal{Z}}
, equipped with the weak-hash topology. A point process
P
on
{\mathcal{Z}}
is a random element taking values in
{\mathscr{N}}({\mathcal{Z}})
. For every bounded set
B\subseteq{\mathcal{Z}}
, there exists a finite set of points
\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subseteq B
such that
P(\{z_{i}\})>0
for all
i=1,\dots,N
, and
P(B\setminus\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N})=0
. These points are called
events
. A point process
P
on
{\mathcal{Z}}
is
simple
if
\sup_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}P(\{z\})\in\{0,1\}
almost surely. For
\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}:={\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathcal{Z}}
, a point process
P
on
\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}
is referred to as a
marked
point process on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with marks in
{\mathcal{Z}}
. If
P
has events
\{(t_{i},\rho_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}
, then
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are said to be the
marks
of
P
. If
P([0,T]\times{\mathcal{Z}})<\infty
a.s. for all
T\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, then
P
is said to be
non-explosive
or
locally finite
. All point processes
P
that we consider
will be
simple
in the sense that
\sup_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}P(\{t\}\times{\mathcal{Z}})\in\{0,1\}
almost surely. A marked point process
P
on
\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}
is said to be
{\mathbb{G}}
-adapted if for every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
A\in{\mathcal{B}}([0,t]\times{\mathcal{Z}})
,
P(A)
is
{\mathcal{G}}_{t}
-measurable. For any point process
P
on
\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}
,
{\mathbb{H}}^{P}
is defined to be the minimal filtration satisfying the usual conditions such that
P
is
{\mathbb{H}}^{P}
-adapted.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{Z}}
is equipped with a nonnegative, locally finite
reference measure

\ell
. Then a random function
\Gamma:\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathcal{Z}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is said to be
{\mathbb{G}}
-
mark predictable
if it is measurable with respect to
{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbb{G}})\otimes{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{Z}})
, where
{\mathscr{P}}({\mathbb{G}})
is the predictable
\sigma
-algebra generated by
{\mathbb{G}}

[
9
, page 379]
. As an immediate consequence, it follows that
t\mapsto\int_{z\in A}\Gamma(t,z)\,\ell(dz)
is
{\mathbb{G}}
-predictable for all
A\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{Z}})
. The
{\mathbb{G}}
-
intensity
of
P
with respect to the measure
\ell
is a
{\mathbb{G}}
-mark predictable process
\Gamma
such that for each bounded
A\subseteq{\mathcal{Z}}
, the process
t\mapsto P([0,t]\times A)-\int_{z\in A}\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma(s,z)\,ds\,\ell(dz)
is a
{\mathbb{G}}
-local martingale
[
9
, Definitions 14.1.I,14.3.I]
.

5.1.2
Dual Characterizations

Recalling the Definition
4.7
of the jump characteristics
\{(t_{k}(x),j_{k}(x),v_{k}(x))\}

of any proper trajectory
x
, we now define the notion of a dual to

.

Definition 5.1
.

For any (not necessarily finite)
U\subseteq V
, and any proper
x\in{\mathcal{D}}^{U}
, the
dual
of
x
is the simple marked point process
p\in{\mathscr{N}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathcal{J}}\times U)
with event times
\bigcup_{v\in U}\{t_{k}(x_{v})\}
and marks
\bigcup_{v\in U}\{(j_{k}(x_{v}),v_{k}(x_{v}))\}
.

It is easy to see that the duals of weak solutions to (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) generally exist, see Lemma
C.1
for a complete proof.
In what follows,
\#_{{\mathcal{S}}}
denotes the counting measure on any countable set
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose that
G=(V,E)
is a deterministic (but not necessarily locally finite) graph, and
the jump rate function family
\mathbf{r}:=\{r^{v}_{j}\}_{v\in V,j\in{\mathcal{J}}}
satisfies conditions 2 and 3 of Assumption

and the

Let

be any weak solution to the SDE (

(a)

The dual

has a
{\mathbb{G}}
-intensity on

\Lambda_{U}(t,\rho):=r^{v}_{j}(t,\mathbf{\kappa},X)\quad\text{ for }\quad\rho=(j,v)\in{\mathcal{J}}\times U,\text{ }t>0.

(5.1)

(b)

Let
\widetilde{X}
be an a.s. proper
{\mathcal{X}}^{V}
-valued càdlàg process and let
\widetilde{\mathbf{\kappa}}
be a
{\mathcal{K}}^{V}
-valued random element, defined on a common probability space

and

\widetilde{{\mathbb{G}}}
-predictable intensity

then it is possible to extend the probability space

When

is finite and the IPS is Markov, duality results analogous to Proposition

are well known (e.g.
[
9

follow easily from scaling arguments (applied to the driving Poisson processes of the SDE (

)-(
3.2

[
6

. However, when

and

are infinite, the dual point processes

and
\widetilde{P}_{U}
of

and

respectively may be explosive, in which case standard embedding theorems cannot be applied directly (although see
[
22

(a), this is easily resolved by noting that for all finite

to
(0,\infty)\times{\mathcal{J}}\times W

\widehat{{\mathbb{G}}}

\widetilde{\mathbf{N}}^{W}
for finite

[
6

[
4

Proof of (a):

We start with a basic measure-theoretic result,

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
be a complete probability space and let

be a sub-

{\mathbb{E}}\left[Z|{\mathcal{G}}\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[Z|\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}\right]

Proof.

By definition,

is a bounded,
\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}
-measurable function. Therefore, by
[
24

{\mathcal{G}}
-measurable function

{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s.. Thus for any

,

and hence,

{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{A\right\}}g\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{A\right\}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[Z|\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}\right]\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{A\right\}}Z|\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}\right]\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathbb{I}}_{\left\{A\right\}}Z\right].

This proves that

immediately implies that conditional independence relations of

Appendix B
Supplementary Properties of SGMRFs

Recall the definition of SGMRFs given in the Introduction. We now describe some properties of SGMRFs. We begin with Lemma
B.1
, which gives an equivalent definition of the SGMRF property. Lemma
B.2
then applies this definition to prove the assertion made in Section
1
that the
\alpha
-SGMRF property extends the notion of tree-indexed Markov chains.

In the following lemma, we use the terminology of
\alpha
-separation. For a positive integer
\alpha
, given three disjoint subsets

,

is said to
\alpha
-separate

and

if any path between a vertex in
A
and a vertex in
B
contains a consecutive sequence of
\alpha
distinct vertices in
S
.

.

Let

be a locally finite graph, and let
{\mathcal{Z}}
be a Polish space. Then a
{\mathcal{Z}}^{V}
-random element

forms an
\alpha
-SGMRF if and only if relation (
1.1
), namely

Z_{A}\perp\!\!\!\perp Z_{B}|Z_{S}
, holds for all finite, disjoint

such that
S

\alpha
-separates
A
and
B
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, fix the
{\mathcal{Z}}^{V}
-random vector
Z
. First, assume that
Z_{A}\perp\!\!\!\perp Z_{B}|Z_{S}
for all finite, disjoint
A,B,S\subseteq V
such that
S

\alpha
-separates
A
and
B
in
G
. Then for any disjoint partition

of
V
such that

is finite and any finite

and

, all paths from
A
to

must contain
\alpha

, and so

\alpha
-separates

and

in

. Thus,

-SGMRF.

To show the reverse implication, now assume that

forms an
\alpha
-SGMRF and let

be finite, disjoint vertex sets such that
S

\alpha
-separates

and

\displaystyle Z_{A^{\prime}}\perp\!\!\!\perp Z_{(S\cup B^{\prime})\setminus{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}_{A^{\prime}}(G)}|Z_{{\mathcal{N}}^{\alpha}_{A^{\prime}}(G)}

If the claim holds, then an application of Lemma

\alpha
-separates

\alpha

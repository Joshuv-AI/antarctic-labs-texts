Suzuki Type Estimates for Exponentiated Sums and Generalized Lie-Trotter Formulas in Banach Algebras
Zhenhua Wang

Abstract
The Lie-Trotter formula has been a fundamental tool in quantum mechanics, quantum computing, and quantum simulations. The error estimations for the Lie-Trotter product formula play a crucial role in achieving scalability and computational efficiency. In this note, we present two error estimates of Lie-Trotter product formulas, utilizing Jordan product within Banach algebras. Additionally, we introduce two generalized Lie-Trotter formula and provide two explicit estimation formulas. Consequently, the renowned Suzuki symmetrized approximation for the exponentiated sums follows directly from our main Theorem.

Introduction

The Trotter product formula
[
12
]
has played a pivotal role in the fields of quantum mechanics, quantum computing, and quantum simulationsdue to its capability to decompose complex quantum systems into simpler subsystems. Accurate estimation of errors associated with the Lie-Trotter product formula is crucial for achieving scalability and computational efficiency in various quantum applications.

The theory of Trotter product formulas in Banach algebras was significantly advanced by Suzuki. In 1976, Suzuki introduced estimates for the product formula approximation, extending the Lie-Trotter formula to exponentiated sums of an arbitrary number of elements in a Banach algebra
[
10
]
. He later refined these estimates in 1985 by presenting a symmetrized approximation method for exponentiated sums
[
11
]
. The applications of his results are extensive, notably in quantum chemistry, condensed matter physics, and quantum computing
[
9
]
.

The extension of Trotter product formulas to Jordan-Banach algebras was first achieved by Peralta and collaborators
[
7
]
.
Inspired by their work, we recently developed Trotter product formulas in JB-algebras, adopting a distinct approach from Peralta et al. Remarkably, Suzuki-type estimates remain valid even in the non-associative context of JB-algebras
[
5
]
. This theory has further implications for quantum information science
[
16
]
. Building on our results
[
5
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
, we introduced the concept of Lie-Trotter means within JB-algebras
[
17
]
.

In this note, we present two error estimates for Lie-Trotter product formulas using Jordan product within Banach algebras. Additionally, we establish two generalized Lie-Trotter formulas and explicitly provide two estimation formulas with an order of
\displaystyle 1/n^{2}
. Consequently, the notable Suzuki estimate for symmetrized Lie-Trotter formula
[
11
, Formula 3, Equation (1.15)]
follows directly from our results. Note that our main results,
2.1
and
3.1
, partially answer the open problem regarding the existence of Trotter error estimates for Jordan-Trotter product formulas in Jordan-Banach algebras, initially posed in
[
5
]
, since every Banach algebra is also a Jordan-Banach algebra. This open problem has recently been fully solved in
[
6
]
.

We now introduce necessary notation. Let
\Al
be a unital Banach algebra. It possesses an underlying Jordan algebra structure with the Jordan product defined by

A\circ B=(AB+BA)/{2},\,\mbox{for any}\,A,B\in\Al.

Note that
A\circ B=B\circ A
and
\left\|A\circ B\right\|\leq\left\|A\right\|\cdot\left\|B\right\|.

The Jordan triple product on
\Al
is defined by

\displaystyle\{ABC\}:=(A\circ B)\circ C+(C\circ B)\circ A-(A\circ C)\circ B=(ABC+CBA)/2.

It follows readily that
\displaystyle\left\|\{ABC\}\right\|\leq\left\|A\right\|\left\|B\right\|\left\|C\right\|.
For further details on Jordan products, we refer readers to, we refer the reader to

Error Estimations for Second Order Jordan-Trotter Product Formulas

In this section, we utilize the Jordan product structure within Banach algebras to establish Suzuki-type estimations for Trotter product formulas and explore their implications.

The following theorem provides Suzuki-type estimations for exponentiated sums using Jordan product decompositions in Banach algebras, with an error order of
1/n^{2}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any finite number of elements
\displaystyle A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{m}
in a Banach algebra
\Al,

\displaystyle\left\|\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}A_{j}\right)-g_{n}(\{A_{j}\})\right\|\leq\dfrac{1}{3n^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|A_{j}\|\right)^{3}\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|A_{j}\|\right),

where

\displaystyle g_{n}\left(\{A_{j}\}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\left[\left(\exp\left(\frac{A_{1}}{n}\right)\circ\exp\left(\frac{A_{2}}{n}\right)\right)\circ\cdots\right]\circ\exp\left(\frac{A_{m}}{n}\right)\right)^{n}

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}A_{j}\right),

(2.1)

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\left[\left(\exp\left(\frac{A_{1}}{n}\right)\circ\exp\left(\frac{A_{2}}{n}\right)\right)\circ\cdots\right]\circ\exp\left(\frac{A_{m}}{n}\right).

Then,

\displaystyle\|C\|\leq\exp\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|A_{j}\|\right)\,\ \mbox{and}\,\ \|D\|\leq\exp\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|A_{j}\|\right).

We observe that

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=\left\|\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}A_{j}\right)-g_{n}(\{A_{j}\})\right\|=\left\|C^{n}-D^{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\|C-D\|\left(\|C\|^{n-1}+\|C\|^{n-2}\|D\|+\cdots+\|D\|^{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq n\|C-D\|\left(\max\{\|C\|,\|D\|\}\right)^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq n\|C-D\|\exp\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|A_{j}\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq n\left(\|C-F\|+\|D-F\|\right)\exp\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|A_{j}\|\right),

(2.2)

where

\displaystyle F=I+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{A_{j}}{n}\right)+\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}A_{j}/n\right)^{2}/{2!}.

(2.3)

Note that

\displaystyle\|C-F\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\left(\frac{A_{1}+A_{2}\cdots+A_{m}}{n}\right)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\right\|

where the inequality

Following a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem 3.1 in
[
5
]
, the degree 2 Taylor polynomial of
D
is
F.
Thus,

\displaystyle\|D-F\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\left(\mbox{sum of all terms of degree $k$ of Taylor expansion of}\,D\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=3}^{\infty}\dfrac{\left[(A_{1}^{k_{1}}\circ A_{2}^{k_{2}})\circ\cdots\right]\circ A_{m}^{k_{m}}}{n^{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{m}}\cdot k_{1}!\cdot k_{2}!\cdots k_{m}!}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=3}^{\infty}\dfrac{\|A_{1}\|^{k_{1}}\|A_{2}\|^{k_{2}}\cdots\|A_{m}\|^{k_{m}}}{n^{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{m}}\cdot k_{1}!\cdot k_{2}!\cdots k_{m}!}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3!\cdot n^{3}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|A_{j}\|\right)^{3}\exp\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|A_{j}\|\right)

(2.5)

[

Combining

As a corollary, we have the following generalized Lie-Trotter formula for an

Corollary 2.2
.

For any finite number of elements
A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{m}
in a Banach algebra

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left\{\left[\left(\exp\left(\frac{A_{1}}{n}\right)\circ\exp\left(\frac{A_{2}}{n}\right)\right)\circ\cdots\right]\circ\exp\left(\frac{A_{m}}{n}\right)\right\}^{n}=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}A_{j}\right).

\displaystyle\exp(A+B)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\exp\left(\frac{A}{n}\right)\exp\left(\frac{B}{n}\right)+\exp\left(\frac{B}{n}\right)\exp\left(\frac{A}{n}\right)\right)\right]^{n}

3
Error Estimations for Jordan-Trotter Triple Product Formulas

In this section, we further extend our analysis by introducing Suzuki-type estimations for exponentiated sums decomposed via the Jordan triple product in Banach algebras.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any finite number of elements
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}\cdots,A_{2p},A_{2p+1}

\displaystyle\left\|\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2p+1}A_{j}\right)-h_{n}(\{A_{j}\})\right\|\leq\frac{1}{3n^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2p+1}\|A_{j}\|\right)^{3}\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2p+1}\|A_{j}\|\right)

where

\displaystyle h_{n}(\{A_{j}\})=\left\{\exp\left(\frac{A_{2p}}{n}\right)\cdots\left\{\exp\left(\frac{A_{2}}{n}\right)\exp\left(\frac{A_{1}}{n}\right)\exp\left(\frac{A_{3}}{n}\right)\right\}\cdots\exp\left(\frac{A_{2p+1}}{n}\right)\right\}^{n}.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}A_{j}\right),

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\left\{\exp\left(\frac{A_{2p}}{n}\right)\cdots\left\{\exp\left(\frac{A_{2}}{n}\right)\exp\left(\frac{A_{1}}{n}\right)\exp\left(\frac{A_{3}}{n}\right)\right\}\cdots\exp\left(\frac{A_{2p+1}}{n}\right)\right\}.

\displaystyle\|G\|\leq\exp\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{2p+1}\|A_{j}\|\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|H\|\leq\exp\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{2p+1}\|A_{j}\|\right).

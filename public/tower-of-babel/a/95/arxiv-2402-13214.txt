Application of the Inclusion-Exclusion Principle to Prime Number Subsequences
Michael P. May

Abstract
We apply the Inclusion-Exclusion Principle to a unique pair of prime number subsequences to determine whether these subsequences form a small set or a large set and thus whether the infinite sum of the inverse of their terms converges or diverges. In this paper, we analyze the complementary prime number subsequences ℙ ′ \mathbb{P}^{\prime} and ℙ ′′ \mathbb{P}^{\prime\prime} as well as revisit the twin prime subsequence ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}_{2} .

]

[

Proof 5.2

\displaystyle{j+k}\;\;

\displaystyle{=\dfrac{\ln{x}}{\ln{x}+1}+\dfrac{\ln{x}}{\ln{x}(\ln{x}+1)}}

\displaystyle{=\dfrac{\ln{x}(\ln{x}+1)+\ln{x}\ln{x}(\ln{x}+1)}{\ln{x}(\ln{x}+1)^{2}}}

\displaystyle{=\dfrac{\ln{x}(\ln{x}+1)(\ln{x}+1)}{\ln{x}(\ln{x}+1)^{2}}}

\displaystyle{=1}.

Since
j+k=1
is valid in the lemma for all
x>1
, we have established that

\displaystyle{\dfrac{1}{{\ln^{j}{p_{r}}}\cdot{\ln^{k}{p_{r}}}}}\;\;

\displaystyle{=\dfrac{1}{\ln{p_{r}}}\cdot\biggl[{j+k}\biggr]}

\displaystyle{=\dfrac{1}{\ln{p_{r}}}\cdot\biggl[\dfrac{\ln{p_{r}}}{\ln{p_{r}}(\ln{p_{r}}+1)}+\dfrac{\ln{p_{r}}}{\ln{p_{r}}+1}\biggr]}.

Thus, in harmony with the lemma, we have

{\pi{{}^{\prime\prime}(x)}\leq r^{\prime\prime}+\dfrac{x}{\ln{p_{r}}(\ln{p_{r}}+1)}+2^{r^{\prime\prime}}}

(15)

where
r^{\prime\prime}=
the number of
p^{\prime\prime}\leq p_{r}
and
2^{r^{\prime\prime}}=
the maximum error resulting from the main term. We now proceed with our estimate of
15
. We know that

{r^{\prime\prime}\leq{\lfloor{\dfrac{r}{2}}\rfloor}\;\;\;\text{for}\;\;\;p^{\prime\prime}>2}

because it was proven that there are fewer primes in the subsequence
\mathbb{P^{\prime\prime}}
than in the complementary prime subsequence
\mathbb{P^{\prime}}
(recall Eq.
4
). Thus,

\displaystyle{\pi{{}^{\prime\prime}(x)}}

\displaystyle{\leq r^{\prime\prime}+\dfrac{x}{\ln{p_{r}}(\ln{p_{r}}+1)}+2^{r^{\prime\prime}}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;(\ref{eq:14})

\displaystyle{<r^{\prime\prime}+\dfrac{x}{\ln{r}(\ln{r}+1)}+2^{r^{\prime\prime}}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\left(r<p^{r}\right)

\displaystyle{\leq\lfloor{\dfrac{r}{2}}\rfloor}+{\dfrac{x}{\ln{r}(\ln{r}+1)}+2^{\lfloor{\frac{r}{2}}\rfloor}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\left({\lfloor{\dfrac{r}{2}}\rfloor}\geq r^{\prime\prime}\right)

\displaystyle{<\dfrac{x}{\ln{r}(\ln{r}+1)}+2^{\lfloor{\frac{r}{2}}\rfloor+1}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\left(2^{\lfloor{\frac{r}{2}}\rfloor}>{\lfloor{\dfrac{r}{2}}\rfloor}\right)

\displaystyle{<\dfrac{x}{\ln{r}(\ln{r}+1)}+2^{{\frac{r}{2}}+1}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\left({\dfrac{r}{2}}>{\lfloor{\dfrac{r}{2}}\rfloor}\right).

Now, let

{r=x^{m}\;\;\;\text{such that}\;\;\;m={\frac{1}{c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}}}}\;\;\;\text{for some positive constant}\;\;c.

We now have

\displaystyle{\pi{{}^{\prime\prime}(x)}}

\displaystyle{<\dfrac{x}{\ln{x^{m}}({\ln{x^{m}}+1})}+2^{\frac{1}{2}(x^{m}+2)}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\left(m={\frac{1}{c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}}}\right)

\displaystyle{=\dfrac{x}{\left(\dfrac{\ln{x}}{c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}}\right)^{2}+\dfrac{\ln{x}}{c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}}}+2^{\frac{1}{2}(x^{m}+2)}}

\displaystyle{=\dfrac{x}{\dfrac{c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}\cdot\ln^{2}{x}+(c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}})^{2}\cdot\ln{x}}{(c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}})^{3}}}+2^{\frac{1}{2}(x^{m}+2)}}

\displaystyle{=x\cdot\dfrac{(c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}})^{2}}{\ln^{2}{x}+c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}\cdot\ln{x}}+2^{\frac{1}{2}(x^{m}+2)}}

\displaystyle{<C\cdot x\cdot\dfrac{(\ln{\ln{x}})^{2}}{\ln^{2}{x}+c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}\cdot\ln{x}}+2^{\frac{1}{2}(x^{m}+2)}}.

Since
\ln{\ln{x}}\cdot\ln{x}<c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}\cdot\ln{x}
for
c\geq 1
, and since
2^{\frac{1}{2}(x^{m}+2)}\ll
than the main term when
c\geq 5
, we finally arrive at

{\pi^{\prime\prime}{(x)}<C\cdot x\cdot\dfrac{(\ln{\ln{x}})^{2}}{(\ln{x})^{2}+\ln{\ln{x}}\cdot\ln{x}}}.

(16)

Thus, we see that for some positive constant
C<+\infty
, the sum of the reciprocals of the infinite subsequence of prime numbers
\mathbb{P^{\prime\prime}}
converges, and this is confirmed when we compare
16
to the count of
p_{2}\leq x
(see
20

6
Estimating
{\pi{{}^{\prime}(x)}}

In order to calculate an estimate of
\pi{{}^{\prime}(x)}
, we invoke the lemma and begin with

{\pi{{}^{\prime}(x)}\leq r^{\prime}+\dfrac{x}{\ln{p_{r}}+1}+2^{r^{\prime}}}.

(17)

Proceeding as before, we know that

{r^{\prime}\leq{\lfloor{r}\rfloor}\;\;\;\text{for}\;\;\;p^{\prime}\geq 2}

since there are fewer primes in the subsequence
\mathbb{P^{\prime}}
than in the set of all prime numbers
\mathbb{P}
. Thus,

\displaystyle{\pi{{}^{\prime}(x)}}

\displaystyle{\leq r^{\prime}+\dfrac{x}{\ln{p_{r}}+1}+2^{r^{\prime}}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;(\ref{eq:16})

\displaystyle{<r^{\prime}+\dfrac{x}{\ln{r}+1}+2^{r^{\prime}}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\left({r<p^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle{\leq\lfloor{{r}}\rfloor}+{\dfrac{x}{\ln{r}+1}+2^{\lfloor{{r}}\rfloor}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\left({\lfloor{{r}}\rfloor}\geq r^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle{<\dfrac{x}{\ln{r}+1}+2^{\lfloor{{r}}\rfloor+1}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\left(2^{\lfloor{{r}}\rfloor}>{\lfloor{{r}}\rfloor}\right)

\displaystyle{<\dfrac{x}{\ln{r}+1}+2^{{{r}}+1}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\left({r}>{\lfloor{{r}}\rfloor}\right).

Now, let

{r=x^{m}\;\;\;\text{such that}\;\;\;m={\frac{1}{c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}}}}\;\;\;\text{for some positive constant}\;\;c.

We now have

\displaystyle{\pi{{}^{\prime}(x)}}

\displaystyle{<\dfrac{x}{{\ln{x^{m}}+1}}+2^{x^{m}+1}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\left(m={\frac{1}{c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}}}\right)

\displaystyle{=\dfrac{x}{\dfrac{\ln{x}}{c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}}+1}+2^{x^{m}+1}}

\displaystyle{=\dfrac{x}{\dfrac{\ln{x}+c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}}{c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}}}+2^{x^{m}+1}}

\displaystyle{=x\cdot\dfrac{c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}}{\ln{x}+c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}}+2^{x^{m}+1}}

\displaystyle{<C\cdot x\cdot\dfrac{\ln{\ln{x}}}{\ln{x}+c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}}+2^{x^{m}+1}}.

Since
\ln{\ln{x}}<c\cdot\ln{\ln{x}}
for
c\geq 1
, and since
2^{x^{m}+1}\ll
than the main term when
c\geq 5
, we finally arrive at

{\pi^{\prime}{(x)}<C\cdot x\cdot\dfrac{\ln{\ln{x}}}{\ln{x}+\ln{\ln{x}}}}.

Since we’ve shown that
\mathbb{P^{\prime\prime}}
is a small set in that the infinite sum of its reciprocals converges, and since it is known that the sum of the reciprocals of the set of all prime numbers
\mathbb{P}
diverges, we can deduce from the relation

{\mathbb{P{}}}={\mathbb{P{{}^{\prime}}}+\mathbb{P{{}^{\prime\prime}}}}

that the prime number subsequence
\mathbb{P^{\prime}}
is a large set and that the infinite sum of its reciprocals diverges.

{\pi{{}_{2}(x)}}

9
Conclusion

In this paper, we applied the Inclusion-Exclusion Principle to the complementary prime number subsequences
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\prime\prime}
to derive the respective prime counting functions
\pi^{\prime}{(x)}
and
\pi^{\prime\prime}{(x)}
to determine whether these subsequences form a small set or a large set and thus whether the infinite sum of the inverse of their terms converges or diverges. In this study, we concluded that the sum of the reciprocals of the prime number subsequence
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
diverges, similar to that for the set of all prime numbers
\mathbb{P}
, while the sum of the reciprocals of the prime number subsequences
\mathbb{P}^{\prime\prime}
converges, similar to that for the set of all twin primes
\mathbb{P}_{2}
.

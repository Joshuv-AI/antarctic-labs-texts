1.
Introduction

For a complex variety
X
, intersection cohomology
IH^{\cdot}(X)
coincides with singular cohomology with rational coefficients
H^{\cdot}(X)
when
X
is smooth and has better properties than
H^{\cdot}(X)
when
X
is singular, for example it satisfies Poincaré duality and the Hard Lefschetz theorem.
Many applications of intersection cohomology, for example in representation theory
[
18
]
,
[
7
, Section 4]
, are through the decomposition theorem of Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber
[
3
]
.

A construction of intersection
K
-theory is expected to have applications in computations of
K
-theory via a
K
-theoretic version of the decomposition theory, and in representation theory, for example in the construction of representations of vertex algebras using (framed) Uhlenbeck spaces
[
5
]
.
The Goresky–MacPherson construction of intersection cohomology
[
15
]
does not generalize in an obvious way to
K
-theory.

1.1.
The perverse filtration and intersection cohomology

For
S
a variety over
\mathbb{C}
, intersection cohomology
IH^{\cdot}(S)
is a subquotient of
H^{\cdot}(X)
for any resolution of singularities
f:X\to S
.
The decomposition theorem implies that
IH^{\cdot}(S)
is a (non-canonical) direct summand of
H^{\cdot}(X)
. Consider the perverse filtration

{}^{p}H^{\leqslant i}_{f}(X):=H^{\cdot}\left(S,{}^{p}\tau^{\leqslant i}Rf_{*}IC_{X}\right)\hookrightarrow H^{\cdot}(S,Rf_{*}IC_{X})=H^{\cdot}(X).

For
V\hookrightarrow S
, denote by
X_{V}:=f^{-1}(V)
. Let
A_{V}
be the set of irreducible components of
X_{V}
and let
c^{a}_{V}
be the codimension on
\iota^{a}_{V}:X_{V}^{a}\hookrightarrow X
for
a\in A_{V}
. Consider a resolution of singularities
\pi^{a}_{V}:Y_{V}^{a}\to X_{V}^{a}
.
Let
g^{a}_{V}:=f\pi^{a}_{V}:Y_{V}^{a}\to V
. Define

\displaystyle{}^{p}\widetilde{H}_{f,V}^{\leqslant i}:=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{a\in A_{V}}\iota^{a}_{V*}\pi_{V*}^{a}\,{}^{p}H^{\leqslant i-c^{a}_{V}}_{g^{a}_{V}}(Y_{V}^{a})\subset{}^{p}H_{f}^{\leqslant i}(X),

\displaystyle{}^{p}\widetilde{H}_{f}^{\leqslant i}:=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{V\subsetneqq S}{}^{p}\widetilde{H}_{f,V}^{\leqslant i}\subset{}^{p}H_{f}^{\leqslant i}(X).

The decomposition theorem implies that

IH^{\cdot}(S)\cong{}^{p}H_{f}^{\leqslant 0}H^{\cdot}(X)\big/{}^{p}\widetilde{H}_{f}^{\leqslant 0}H^{\cdot}(X).

1.3.
Properties of the perverse filtrations and intersection
K
-theory

The perverse filtrations in
K
-theory and intersection
K
-theory have similar properties to their counterparts in cohomology.

For a map
f:X\to S
, let

s:=\dim X\times_{S}X-\dim X
be its defect of semismallness. In Theorem
3.11
, we show that

\displaystyle\textbf{P}^{\leqslant-s-1}_{f}\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{0}(X)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\textbf{P}^{\leqslant s}_{f}\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{0}(X)=P^{\leqslant s}_{f}\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{0}(X)

\displaystyle=\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{0}(X).

This implies that

\displaystyle\text{gr}^{\cdot}IK_{\cdot}(S)=\text{gr}^{\cdot}\textbf{I}K_{\cdot}(S)=\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(S)\text{ for }S\text{ smooth,}

\displaystyle\text{gr}^{\cdot}\textbf{I}K_{0}(S)=\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{0}(X)\text{ if }S\text{ has a small resolution }f:X\to S.

For more computations of perverse filtrations in
K
-theory and intersection
K
-theory, see Subsections
3.7
and
4.4
.

Let
d=\dim S
. In cohomology, there are natural maps

\displaystyle H^{i}(S)\to IH^{i}(S)\to H^{\text{BM}}_{2d-i}(S)

\displaystyle IH^{i}(S)\otimes IH^{j}(S)\to H^{\text{BM}}_{2d-i-j}(S).

The composition in the first line is the natural map
H^{i}(S)\to H^{\text{BM}}_{2d-i}(S)
. The second map is non-degenerate for cycles of complementary dimensions.
In Subsection
4.3
we explain that there exist natural maps

\displaystyle\text{gr}_{i}IK_{\cdot}(S)

\displaystyle\to\text{gr}_{i}G(S)

\displaystyle\text{gr}^{i}IK_{\cdot}(S)\times\text{gr}^{j}IK_{\cdot}(S)

\displaystyle\to\text{gr}_{d-i-j}G_{\cdot}(S)

and their analogues for
\textbf{I}K
.
The above filtration on
G
-theory is by dimension of supports, see
[
13
, Section 5.4]
.

1.4.
The decomposition theorem for semismall maps

As mentioned above, many applications of intersection cohomology are based on the decomposition theorem. When the map

f:X\to S

is semismall, the statement of the decomposition theorem is more explicit, which we now explain. Let
\{S_{a}|\,a\in I\}
be a stratification of
S
such that
f_{a}:f^{-1}(S_{a}^{o})\to S_{a}^{o}
is a locally trivial fibration, where
S_{a}^{o}=S_{a}-\bigcup_{b\in I}\left(S_{a}\cap S_{b}\right).

Let
A\subset I
be the set of relevant strata, that is, those strata such that for
x_{a}\in S_{a}^{o}
:

\dim f^{-1}(x_{a})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\dim S-\dim S_{a}\right).

For
x_{a}\in S_{a}^{o}
, the monodromy group
\pi_{1}(S_{a}^{0},x_{a})
acts on the set of irreducible components of
f^{-1}(x_{a})
of top dimension; let
L_{a}
be the corresponding local system.
Let
c_{a}
be the codimension of
X_{a}=f^{-1}(S_{a})
in
X
. The decomposition theorem for the map
f:X\to S
says that there exists a canonical decomposition
[
7
, Theorem 4.2.7]
:

H^{j}(X)\cong\bigoplus_{a\in A}IH^{j-c_{a}}(S_{a},L_{a}).

We conjecture the analogous statement in
K
-theory.

Conjecture 1.3
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a semismall map and consider
\{S_{a}|\,a\in I\}
a stratification as above, and let
A\subset I
be the set of relevant strata. There is a decomposition for any integer
j
:

\text{gr}^{j}K_{\cdot}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\bigoplus_{a\in A}\text{gr}^{j-c_{a}}\textbf{I}K_{\cdot}(S_{a},L_{a})_{\mathbb{Q}}.

See Conjecture
5.1
for a more precise statement. In Theorem
5.4
,
we check the above conjecture for
K_{0}
under the extra condition that for any
a\in A
, there are small maps
\pi_{a}:T_{a}\to S_{a}
such that
\pi_{a}^{-1}(S_{a}^{o})\to S_{a}^{o}
is étale with associated local system
L_{a}
. The proof of the above result is based on a theorem of de Cataldo–Migliorini
[
6
, Section 4]
. In Subsection
4.4.4
, we prove the statement for
K_{0}
when
f:X\to S
is a resolution of singularities of a surface.

1.5.
Intersection Chow groups

When
X
is smooth,
\text{gr}^{i}K_{0}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}=CH^{i}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Thus
\text{gr}^{i}IK_{0}(S)_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a candidate for an intersection Chow group of
S
. Corti–Hanamura already defined (rational) intersection Chow groups (or Chow motives) in
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
inspired by the decomposition theorem. One proposed definition assumes conjectures of Grothendieck and Murre and proves a version of the decomposition theorem for Chow groups; the other approach defines a perverse-type filtration on Chow groups by induction on level
i
of the filtration and via correspondences involving all varieties
W\to S
.
These correspondences need to satisfy certain vanishings for the perverse filtration in cohomology. The advantage of the definition we propose is that one uses fewer correspondences to define
P^{\leqslant i}_{f}
and
\textbf{P}^{\leqslant i}_{f}
and this allows for computations, see Subsection
4.4
and Theorem
5.3
.

For varieties
S
with a semismall resolution of singularities
f:X\to S
, de Cataldo–Migliorini
[
6
]
proposed a definition of Chow motives
ICH\,(S)
and proved a version of the decomposition theorem for semismall maps.

1.6.
Intersection
K
-theory

Our approach to define (graded) intersection
K
-theory uses functoriality of the perverse filtration in an essential way. To obtain this functoriality, it is essential to pass to
\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(X)
.
It is a very interesting problem to find a definition of the perverse filtration on
K_{\cdot}(X)
.
We hope that such a definition will provide a version of equivariant intersection
K
-theory with applications to geometric representation theory, for example in understanding the
K
-theoretic version of
[
5
]
.

There are proposed definitions of intersection
K
-theory in some particular cases. Cautis
[
8
]
, Cautis–Kamnitzer
[
9
]
have an approach for categorification of intersection sheaves for certain subvarieties of the affine Grassmannian. Eberhardt defined intersection
K
-theoretic sheaves for varieties with certain stratifications
[
12
]
.
In
[
19
]
, we proposed a definition of intersection
K
-theoretic for good moduli spaces of smooth Artin stacks which has applications to the structure theory of Hall algebras of Kontsevich–Soibelman
[
20
]
.

2.1.
Notations and conventions

All schemes considered in this paper are finite type and defined over
\mathbb{C}
.
The definition of the filtration in Subsection

works over any field, but to define intersection
K
-theory we use resolution of singularities. The definition of intersection
K
-theory works over any field of characteristic zero. A variety is an irreducible reduced scheme. We use quasi-smooth schemes in Subsections

and

, which are the natural extension of lci schemes in derived geometry.

For

a scheme, let

be its subcategory of bounded complexes of locally free sheaves on

. For their analogues for quasi-smooth schemes, see
[

. The functors used in the paper are derived; we sometimes drop

or

instead of

.
When

is smooth, the two categories coincide. Define

\displaystyle G_{\cdot}(S)

\displaystyle:=K_{\cdot}\left(D^{b}\text{Coh}(S)\right),

\displaystyle K_{\cdot}(S)

\displaystyle:=K_{\cdot}\left(\text{Perf}(S)\right).

For

a subvariety of

, let

of complexes supported on

, and

The local systems used in this paper are of the form

For

2.3.
Quasi-smooth schemes

2.3.1.

A morphism
f:X\to Y
of derived schemes is
quasi-smooth
if it is locally of finite presentation and the cotangent complex
\mathbb{L}_{f}
has Tor-amplitude
\leqslant 1
. Alternatively, there is a factorization

X\xrightarrow{\iota}X^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\pi}Y

with
\pi
smooth and
\iota
a quasi-smooth immersion, that is, the complex
\mathbb{L}_{\iota}[-1]
is a vector bundle, see
[
17
, Section 2]
,
[
2
, Subsections 2.1 and 2.2]
. A proper map between smooth varieties is quasi-smooth. Any quasi-smooth map has a well defined relative dimension.

A derived scheme
X
is quasi-smooth if the structure morphism
X\to\text{Spec}\,\mathbb{C}
is quasi-smooth. Any quasi-smooth scheme has a well defined dimension.

2.3.2.

We list some properties satisfied by quasi-smooth morphisms and schemes. We are using them only in the proof of an excess intersection formula, see (

). Consider a cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\prime}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

If

is quasi-smooth, then

is quasi-smooth and

\text{reldim}\,(f)=\text{reldim}\,(f^{\prime}).

Composition of quasi-smooth maps is quasi-smooth.

Let

The perverse filtration in cohomology

Let
S
be a scheme over
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
D^{b}_{c}(S)
be the derived category of bounded complexes of rational constructible sheaves
[
7
, Section 2]
. Consider the perverse
t
-structure
\left(\mathcal{P}^{\leqslant i},\mathcal{P}^{\geqslant i}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
on this category. There are functors:

\displaystyle{}^{p}\tau^{\leqslant i}:

\displaystyle D^{b}_{c}(S)\to\mathcal{P}^{\leqslant i},

\displaystyle{}^{p}\tau^{\geqslant i}:

\displaystyle D^{b}_{c}(S)\to\mathcal{P}^{\geqslant i}

such that
for
F\in D^{b}_{c}(S)
there is a distinguished triangle in
D^{b}_{c}(S)
:

{}^{p}\tau^{\leqslant i}F\to F\to{}^{p}\tau^{\geqslant i+1}F\xrightarrow{[1]}.

For a proper map
f:X\to S
and
F\in D^{b}_{c}(X)
, the perverse filtration on
H^{\cdot}(X,F)
is defined as the image of

{}^{p}H^{\leqslant i}_{f}(X,F):=H^{\cdot}(S,{}^{p}\tau^{\leqslant i}Rf_{*}F)\to H^{\cdot}(S,Rf_{*}F)=H^{\cdot}(X,F).

For
F=IC_{X}
, the decomposition theorem implies that

{}^{p}IH^{\leqslant i}_{f}(X)\hookrightarrow IH^{\cdot}(X).

Let
f:X\to S
be a generically finite morphism from
X
smooth, let
U
be a smooth open subset of
S
such that
f^{-1}(U)\to U
is étale, and let
L=f_{*}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{f^{-1}(U)}\right)
.

For
V\hookrightarrow S
, denote by
X_{V}:=f^{-1}(V)
. Let
A_{V}
be the set of irreducible components of
X_{V}
. Let
c^{a}_{V}
be the codimension on
\iota^{a}_{V}:X_{V}^{a}\hookrightarrow X
for
a\in A_{V}
. Consider a resolution of singularities
\pi_{V}^{a}:Y_{V}^{a}\to X_{V}^{a}
.
Let
g_{V}^{a}:=f\pi_{V}^{a}:Y_{V}^{a}\to V
.
Then

{}^{p}\tau^{\leqslant 0}Rf_{*}IC_{X}\cong\text{ker}\left(Rf_{*}IC_{X}\to\bigoplus_{V\subsetneqq S}\bigoplus_{a\in A_{V}}\left({}^{p}\tau^{>c_{V}^{a}}Rg^{a}_{V*}IC_{Y^{a}_{V}}\right)[c^{a}_{V}]\right).

Define the subspace

{}^{p}\widetilde{\tau}^{\leqslant 0}Rf_{*}IC_{X}:=\text{image}\left(\bigoplus_{V\subsetneqq S}\bigoplus_{a\in A_{V}}\left({}^{p}\tau^{\leqslant-c_{V}^{a}}Rg^{a}_{V*}IC_{Y^{a}_{V}}\right)[-c^{a}_{V}]\to{}^{p}\tau^{\leqslant 0}Rf_{*}IC_{X}\right).

By a computation of Corti–Hanamura
[
11
, Proposition 1.5, Theorem 2.4]
, there is an isomorphism:

(3)

IC_{S}(L)\cong{}^{p}\tau^{\leqslant 0}Rf_{*}IC_{X}\big/{}^{p}\widetilde{\tau}^{\leqslant 0}Rf_{*}IC_{X}.

Further, consider a more general morphism
f:X\to S
with
X
smooth. Let
V\subsetneqq S
be a subvariety. For
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, denote by
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{i}(Rf_{*}IC_{X})_{V}
the direct sum of simple summands of
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{i}(Rf_{*}IC_{X})
with support equal to
V
.
A computation of Corti–Hanamura
[
11
, Proposition 1.5]
shows that:

(4)

{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{i}\left(Rf_{*}IC_{X}\right)_{V}\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{a\in A_{V}}{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{i+c^{a}_{V}}\left(Rg^{a}_{V*}IC_{Y^{a}_{V}}\right).

3.
The perverse filtration in
K
-theory

3.1.
Definition of the filtration
P^{\prime\leqslant\cdot}

Let

f:X\to S
be a proper map between varieties. We define an increasing filtration

P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f}\text{gr}^{\cdot}G_{\cdot}(X)\subset\text{gr}^{\cdot}G_{\cdot}(X).

It induces a filtration on
\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(X)
.
We use the notations from Subsection
2.4
.
Let
Y\hookrightarrow X
be a subvariety and let
T\xrightarrow{\pi}S
be a map generically finite onto its image from
T
smooth.
Consider the diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T\times X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

For a correspondence
\Gamma\in\text{gr}_{\dim X-s}G_{T\times_{S}Y,0}(T\times X)
, define

\Phi_{\Gamma}:=p_{*}(\Gamma\otimes q^{*}(-)):\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{i}(T)\to\text{gr}^{\cdot-s}G_{Y,i}(X).

We usually drop the shift by
s
in the superscript of
\text{gr}^{\cdot}G_{Y}(X)
.
We define the subspace of
\text{gr}^{\cdot}G_{Y}(X)
:

\displaystyle P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f,T}

\displaystyle:=\text{span}_{\Gamma}\left(\Phi_{\Gamma}:\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(T)\to\text{gr}^{\cdot}G_{Y}(X)\right)

\displaystyle P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f}

\displaystyle:=\text{span}\left(P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f,T}\text{ for all maps }\pi\text{ as above}\right),

where the dimension of the correspondence satisfies

(5)

\left\lfloor\frac{i+\dim X-\dim T}{2}\right\rfloor\geqslant s.

We also define a quotient of
\text{gr}^{\cdot}G_{Y}(X)
:

P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f}\text{gr}^{\cdot}G_{Y}(X)\hookrightarrow\text{gr}^{\cdot}G_{Y}(X)\twoheadrightarrow P^{\prime>i}_{f}\text{gr}^{\cdot}G_{Y}(X).

3.2.
Functoriality of the filtration
P^{\prime\leqslant\cdot}

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth varieties with
c=\dim X-\dim Y
.
Consider proper maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

There are induced maps

h^{*}:P^{\prime\leqslant i-c}_{f}\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(X)\to P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{g}\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(Y).

Proof.

Let
T\to S
be a generically finite map onto its image with
T
smooth. It suffices to show that

h^{*}:P^{\prime\leqslant i-c}_{f,T}\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(X)\to P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{g,T}\,\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(Y).

Consider the diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y\times T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\times T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{h}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{X}}

Let
\Theta\in\text{gr}_{\dim X-s}G_{T\times_{S}X,0}(T\times X)
be a correspondence such that

i\geqslant 2s-\dim X+\dim T.

For
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have that:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{gr}^{j}K_{\cdot}(T)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{gr}^{j-s}K_{\cdot}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{gr}^{j-s}K_{\cdot}(Y).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{\Theta}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{\widetilde{h}^{*}\Theta}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h^{*}}

To see this, we compute:

h^{*}\Phi_{\Theta}(F)=h^{*}p_{X*}(\Theta\otimes q_{X}^{*}F)=p_{Y*}\widetilde{h}^{*}(\Theta\otimes q_{X}^{*}F)=p_{Y*}(\widetilde{h}^{*}\Theta\otimes q_{Y}^{*}F)=\Phi_{\widetilde{h}^{*}\Theta}(F).

The correspondence
\widetilde{h}^{*}\Theta\in\text{gr}_{\dim Y-s}G_{T\times_{S}Y}(T\times Y)
satisfies

i+c\geqslant 2s-\dim Y+\dim T,

and this implies the desired conclusion.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be varieties with proper maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Let
c=\dim X-\dim Y
.
There are induced maps

h_{*}:P^{\prime\leqslant i-c}_{g}\text{gr}_{\cdot}G_{\cdot}(Y)\to P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f}\text{gr}_{\cdot}G_{\cdot}(X).

Proof.

Let
T\to S
be a generically finite map onto its image from
T
smooth. We first explain that

h_{*}:P^{\prime\leqslant i-c}_{g,T}\text{gr}_{\cdot}G_{\cdot}(Y)\to P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f,T}\,\text{gr}_{\cdot}G_{\cdot}(X).

We use the notation from the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Consider a correspondence
\Gamma\in\text{gr}_{\dim Y-s}G_{T\times_{S}Y,0}(T\times Y)
such that

i\geqslant 2s-\dim Y+\dim T.

For
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have that:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{gr}_{\dim T-j}K_{\cdot}(T)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{gr}_{\dim Y-j+s}G_{\cdot}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{gr}_{\dim Y-j+s}G_{\cdot}(X).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{\Gamma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{\widetilde{h}_{*}\Gamma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{*}}

To see this, we compute:

h_{*}p_{Y*}(\Gamma\otimes q_{Y}^{*}F)=p_{X*}\widetilde{h}_{*}(\Gamma\otimes\widetilde{h}^{*}q_{X}^{*}F)=p_{X*}(\widetilde{h}_{*}\Gamma\otimes q_{X}^{*}F).

The correspondence

\widetilde{h}_{*}\Gamma\in\text{gr}_{\dim Y-s}G_{T\times_{S}X}(T\times X)=\text{gr}_{\dim X-(c+s)}G_{T\times_{S}X}(T\times X)

satisfies

i+c\geqslant 2(s+c)-\dim X+\dim T,

and thus the conclusion follows.
∎

We continue with some further properties of the filtration
P^{\prime\leqslant\cdot}
. The following is immediate:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a proper map. Let
U
be an open subset of
S
,
X_{U}:=f^{-1}(U)
,
\iota:X_{U}\hookrightarrow X
, and
f_{U}:X_{U}\to U
. Then

\iota^{*}:P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f}\text{gr}_{\cdot}G_{\cdot}(X)\to P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f_{U}}\text{gr}_{\cdot}G_{\cdot}(X_{U}).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a proper map from
X
smooth and consider
e\in\text{gr}^{j}K_{0}(X)
. Then

e\cdot P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f}\text{gr}^{a}K_{\cdot}(X)\subset P^{\prime\leqslant i+2j}_{f}\text{gr}^{a+j}K_{\cdot}(X).

Proof.

Let
T\to S
be a generically finite map onto its image with
T
smooth and let
\Theta\in\text{gr}_{a}G_{T\times_{S}X,0}(T\times X)
. Let
p:T\times X\to X
be the natural projection. Then

p^{*}(e)\cdot\Theta\in\text{gr}_{a-j}G_{T\times_{S}X,0}(T\times X).

For
x\in\text{gr}_{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(T)
, we have that

e\cdot\Phi_{\Theta}(x)=\Phi_{p^{*}(e)\cdot\Theta}(x),

and the conclusion thus follows.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth varieties with proper maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

such that
h
is surjective. Let
c=\dim X-\dim Y
. Then

\displaystyle h_{*}\left(P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f}\text{gr}_{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}\right)

\displaystyle=P^{\prime\leqslant i+c}_{f}\text{gr}_{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}

\displaystyle h^{*}\big(\text{gr}^{\cdot}K(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\cap P^{\prime\leqslant i+c}_{g}\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}

\displaystyle=h^{*}\left(P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f}\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\right).

If there exists
X^{\prime}\to Y
such that the induced map
X^{\prime}\to X
is birational, then the above isomorphisms hold integrally.

Proof.

The statement and its proof are similar to
[
11
, Proposition 3.11]
.

Let
i:X^{\prime}\to Y
be a map such that
hi:X^{\prime}\to X
is generically finite and surjective. Let
f^{\prime}:=fi:X^{\prime}\to S
. Then, by Proposition
3.2
:

P^{\prime\leqslant i+c}_{f^{\prime}}\text{gr}_{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(X^{\prime})\xrightarrow{i_{*}}P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{g}\text{gr}_{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(Y)\xrightarrow{h_{*}}P^{\prime\leqslant i+c}_{f}\text{gr}_{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(X).

The map
h_{*}i_{*}:\text{gr}_{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(X^{\prime})\to\text{gr}_{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(X)
is multiplication by the degree of the map
hi
, so it is an isomorphism rationally. It is an isomorphism integrally if
X^{\prime}\to X
has degree
1
. The pullback statement is similar.
∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S.}

\tau^{a}_{V}\pi=\tau^{\prime a}_{V}\pi^{\prime}

By Proposition

\text{ker}\left(\tau^{a*}_{V}:P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f}\text{gr}^{\cdot}G_{Y}(X)\to P^{\prime>i+c^{a}_{V}}_{\widetilde{f^{a}_{V}}}\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{\cdot}\left(\widetilde{X^{a}_{V}}\right)\right)\cong\\
\text{ker}\left(\pi^{*}\tau^{a*}_{V}:P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f}\text{gr}^{\cdot}G_{Y}(X)\to P^{\prime>i+c^{a}_{V}}_{\widetilde{f^{a}_{V}}}\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(W)\right)\cong\\
\text{ker}\left(\tau^{\prime a*}_{V}:P^{\prime\leqslant i}_{f}\text{gr}^{\cdot}G_{Y}(X)\to P^{\prime>i+c^{a}_{V}}_{\widetilde{f^{a}_{V}}}\text{gr}^{\cdot}K_{\cdot}\left(\widetilde{X^{\prime a}_{V}}\right)\right).

Theorem 3.6
.

be smooth varieties with

.
Consider proper maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

\displaystyle:P^{\leqslant i-c}_{g}\text{gr}_{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(Y)\to P^{\leqslant i}_{f}\text{gr}_{\cdot}K_{\cdot}(X).

.

. We use induction on the dimension of

. The case of

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X.}

\displaystyle\text{gr}^{i}\textbf{I}K_{\cdot}(S)

\displaystyle\to\text{gr}_{d-i}G_{\cdot}(S).

(b) There are natural intersection maps

\displaystyle\text{gr}^{i}IK_{\cdot}(S)\otimes\text{gr}^{j}IK_{\cdot}(S)

\displaystyle\to\text{gr}_{d-i-j}G_{\cdot}(S)

\displaystyle\text{gr}^{i}\textbf{I}K_{\cdot}(S)\otimes\text{gr}^{j}\textbf{I}K_{\cdot}(S)

\displaystyle\to\text{gr}_{d-i-j}G_{\cdot}(S).

Proof.

Let
f:X\to S
be a resolution of singularities. We discuss the claims for
IK_{\cdot}
, the ones for
\textbf{I}K_{\cdot}
are similar. We construct maps as above using
f
. They are independent by
f
by an argument as in Theorem
4.1
.

(a) There is a natural map

\text{gr}^{i}K_{\cdot}(X)=\text{gr}_{d-i}G_{\cdot}(X)\xrightarrow{f_{*}}\text{gr}_{d-i}G_{\cdot}(S),
and we thus obtain a map

\text{gr}^{i}IK_{\cdot}(S)=P^{\leqslant 0}_{f}\text{gr}^{i}K_{\cdot}(X)\big/\left(\widetilde{P}^{\leqslant 0}_{f}\text{gr}^{i}K_{\cdot}(X)\cap\text{ker}\,f_{*}\right)\to\text{gr}_{d-i}G_{\cdot}(S).

(b) Consider the composite map

P^{\leqslant 0}_{f}\text{gr}^{i}K_{\cdot}(X)\boxtimes P^{\leqslant 0}_{f}\text{gr}^{j}K_{\cdot}(X)\to\text{gr}^{i+j}K_{\cdot}(X\times X)\xrightarrow{\Delta^{*}}\text{gr}^{i+j}K_{\cdot}(X)\xrightarrow{f_{*}}\text{gr}_{d-i-j}G_{\cdot}(S).

The subspaces

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{P}^{\leqslant 0}_{f}\text{gr}^{i}K_{\cdot}(X)\cap\text{ker}\,f_{*}\right)\boxtimes P^{\leqslant 0}_{f}\text{gr}^{i}K_{\cdot}(X)

\displaystyle P^{\leqslant 0}_{f}\text{gr}^{i}K_{\cdot}(X)\boxtimes\left(\widetilde{P}^{\leqslant 0}_{f}\text{gr}^{i}K_{\cdot}(X)\cap\text{ker}\,f_{*}\right)

are in the kernel of
f_{*}\Delta^{*}=\Delta^{*}\left(f_{*}\boxtimes f_{*}\right)
. We thus obtain the desired map.
∎

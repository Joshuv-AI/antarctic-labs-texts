Modified contact simplex iteration
Sergei Drozdov

Abstract
We study the iterations of the procedure of taking the contact simplex. We define the concept of the root of the simplex, which is a homothety image of contact simplex with a special coefficient greater than 1. The article shows that once we iterate the root of a given simplex, the circumcenter sequence of these simplices has two partial limits.



1
Introduction

Consider triangle
S,
and its inscribed circle
C.
Then the triangle formed by the points of intersection of
S
and
C
is called the
contact triangle
(also known as Gergonne triangle or intouch triangle) of
S
. If we iterate the procedure of taking the contact triangle, the sequence obtained will converge to the equilateral triangle (if the proper normalisation is applied). This follows, for example, from the fact that on each step we switch from the triangle with the angles

\left(\frac{\pi}{3}+\alpha,\frac{\pi}{3}+\beta,\frac{\pi}{3}+\gamma\right)
to the triangle with the angles

\left(\frac{\pi}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2},\frac{\pi}{3}-\frac{\beta}{2},\frac{\pi}{3}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)
.

Figure 1:
Contact triangle iteration.

The same procedure for the euclidian simplex of the higher dimension is discussed in
[
1
]
, and for the tetrahedrons in
[
2
]
. As shown in these sources, the sequence of simplices does not have a unique limit, but instead, the iterations of the odd and even orders have two limits, that are, generally speaking, different.

We modify the procedure slightly, and instead of normalizing contact simplices, let them grow (in a special way). We prove the technical Lemma
3.1
and afterward the Theorem
4.1
, which shows that the circumcenter sequence of these simplices has two partial limits.

Let

Then

And also,

Proof.

In the notation of the Definition

are

and

Simplex

is the contact simplex of
A_{1}\dots A_{n+1}.

The pointwise inversion image of

is homothetic to

If the apply central symmetry, centered at point

∎

We will further denote simplex

.

In the notation of the Definition

,
\forall i\neq j:1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n+1

\overrightarrow{IC_{i}}\cdot\overrightarrow{IA_{j}}=-R^{2}

(1)

Proof.

(2)

(3)

∎

I\in Int(T)

Proof.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
S_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a simplex with
n+1
vertices. For every

Also, denote
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{r_{k}}{R_{k}}=\rho,

\forall k\enskip\frac{r_{k}}{R_{k}}\leqslant\rho

Proof.

\frac{r_{k}}{R_{k}}

\forall k\enskip r_{k}<R_{k}.

∎

Property 3.4
.

In the notation as above,
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{r_{k}}{R_{k}}\leqslant\frac{1}{n-1}.

Proof.

Since, for any
n+1-
vertex simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{n},
with the radii of inscribed and circumscribed spheres

holds
\frac{r}{R}\leqslant\frac{1}{n-1},

\frac{r_{k}}{R_{k}}\leqslant\frac{1}{n-1},

∎

Property 3.5
.

In the notation as above, if
n=2
(
S_{1}
is a triangle), then
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{r_{k}}{R_{k}}=\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

If

the angles of

converge to

as

In the neighborhood of the equilateral triangle, the factor of the radii of inscribed and circumscribed circles of a triangle is a continuous function of the angles. So this factor converges to the value of the factor in the equilateral triangle.
There it equals

∎

I_{k+1}=O_{k+2},

(54)

and
I_{k+1}
is the center of inscribed sphere of
S^{\prime}_{k+1},
so it lies inside
S^{\prime}_{k+1}
.

Now fix some full-dimensional face of
S_{k+1},
let’s call it
\Gamma.
It divides the space into two half-spaces,
let’s denote one containing
S_{k+1}
by
\Pi.

Let the image of
\Gamma,
after the homothety transforming
S_{k+1}
into
S^{\prime}_{k+1}
be
\Gamma^{\prime},
and
the image of
\Pi
be
\Pi^{\prime}.

Since
I_{k+1},
the center of homothety lies in
\Pi,
then
\Pi^{\prime}\subset\Pi.

From the Lemma
3.1
the circumscribed sphere of
S_{k}
lies inside
S_{k+1},
and so does its center,
O_{k}.

So
O_{k}\in\Pi.

The distance from
O_{k}
to
\Gamma
is in the interval
[R_{k};R_{k}+\frac{3d^{2}}{R_{k}}]
, see (
52
).
This means that since the distance between
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
equals
R_{k}
,
that
O_{k}\in\Pi^{\prime},
and distance from
O_{k}
to
\Gamma^{\prime}
is smaller or equal than
(R_{k}+\frac{3d^{2}}{R_{k}})-R_{k}=\frac{3d^{2}}{R_{k}}.

Repeating this argument for all full-dimension faces of
S_{k+1},
we obtain that

O_{k}
lies inside
S^{\prime}_{k+1},
as
S^{\prime}_{k+1}
is the intersection of
n+1
half-spaces in the family of
\Pi^{\prime}.

Now, as we’ve shown that
O_{k},O_{k+2}\in S^{\prime}_{k+1},
let’s prove that this simplex is indeed small. First, if
S^{\prime}_{k+1}
is a point, then
|O_{k}O_{k+2}|=0,
and any upper bound holds. If
S^{\prime}_{k+1}
is not a point, let’s continue.

The distance from
O_{k}
to all full-dimension faces of
S^{\prime}_{k+1}
is no larger than

\frac{3d^{2}}{R_{k}}.

This means that the radius of the inscribed sphere of
S^{\prime}_{k+1}
is no larger than

\frac{3d^{2}}{R_{k}}

The simplices
S_{k+1}
and
S^{\prime}_{k+1}
are homothetic, so if the radii of inscribed and circumscribed spheres of
S^{\prime}_{k+1}
are
r^{\prime}_{k+1}
and
R^{\prime}_{k+1},
then from (
16
):

\frac{r^{\prime}_{k+1}}{R^{\prime}_{k+1}}=\frac{r_{k+1}}{R_{k+1}}\geqslant\rho(1-\varepsilon).

(55)

So,

R^{\prime}_{k+1}\leqslant\frac{r^{\prime}_{k+1}}{\rho(1-\varepsilon)}\leqslant\frac{3d^{2}}{\rho(1-\varepsilon)R_{k}},

(56)

which means that since both
O_{k},O_{k+2}\in S^{\prime}_{k+1}
then

|O_{k}O_{k+2}|\leqslant 2R^{\prime}_{k+1}\leqslant\frac{6d^{2}}{\rho(1-\varepsilon)R_{k}}=\frac{6|O_{k}O_{k+1}|^{2}}{\rho(1-\varepsilon)R_{k}},\text{ for }k>K_{2}.

(57)

And since for
k>K_{2}
holds
\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2},
then:

|O_{k}O_{k+2}|\leqslant\frac{12|O_{k}O_{k+1}|^{2}}{\rho R_{k}},\text{ for }k>K_{2}.

(58)

3. Sequence
\{O_{2k},k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a Cauchy sequence.

We have shown that points
O_{2k}
and
O_{2k+2}
are close to each other for sufficiently large
k.
If we also can show, that
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|O_{2k}O_{2k+2}|<\infty,
this will lead to
|O_{2k}O_{2l}|\to 0,\enskip k,l\to\infty,
meaning that
O_{2k}
is a Cauchy sequence, and so, has a limit in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

From Property (
2.2
) and Corollary (
3.3
) we know

R_{k+1}=R_{k}\cdot\frac{R_{k}}{r_{k}}\geqslant\frac{R_{k}}{\rho}.

(59)

So, since we know from the Property
3.4
, that
\rho\leqslant\frac{1}{n-1}
,
then
R_{k}
grows fast, more precisely :

R_{k}\geqslant R_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\right)^{k-1}.

(60)

Let
c:=\frac{12}{\rho},
and denote
d_{k}:=|O_{k}O_{k+1}|.

Then for
k>K_{2}
from (
58
) we get:

d_{k+1}=|O_{k+1}O_{k+2}|\leqslant|O_{k+1}O_{k}|+|O_{k}O_{k+2}|\leqslant d_{k}+\frac{cd_{k}^{2}}{R_{k}}.

(61)

Let
\varepsilon_{2}>0
be a small real number, such that

\frac{(1+c\varepsilon_{2})^{2}}{\frac{1}{\rho}}<\frac{1}{2}

(62)

From (
43
) we have: for this
\varepsilon_{2}
there exists such
K_{3}>K_{2},
that for all
k>K_{3}
holds:

\frac{d_{k}}{R_{k}}\leqslant\varepsilon_{2},

(63)

so for
k>K_{3}
the combination of (
61
) and (
63
) gives:

d_{k+1}\leqslant d_{k}+\frac{cd_{k}^{2}}{R_{k}}\leqslant d_{k}+c\varepsilon_{2}d_{k}=(1+c\varepsilon_{2})d_{k},

(64)

so for some large
C>0
and for all
k

d_{k}\leqslant C(1+c\varepsilon_{2})^{k}.

(65)

We can now continue the estimate on
|O_{k}O_{k+2}|,
in (
58
), for
k>K_{2}.

First, equivalently to (
58
):

|O_{k}O_{k+2}|\leqslant\frac{cd_{k}^{2}}{R_{k}},

(66)

then from (
60
) for all
k
:

\frac{cd_{k}^{2}}{R_{k}}\leqslant\frac{cd_{k}^{2}}{R_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\right)^{k-1}},

(67)

then from (
65
), for all
k:

\frac{cd_{k}^{2}}{R_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\right)^{k-1}}\leqslant\frac{c\cdot C^{2}(1+c\varepsilon_{2})^{2k}}{R_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\right)^{k-1}}=\frac{c\cdot C^{2}}{\rho R_{1}}\left(\frac{(1+c\varepsilon_{2})^{2}}{\frac{1}{\rho}}\right)^{k}.

(68)

So, for
k>K_{2}
:

|O_{k}O_{k+2}|\leqslant\frac{c\cdot C^{2}}{\rho R_{1}}\left(\frac{(1+c\varepsilon_{2})^{2}}{\frac{1}{\rho}}\right)^{k}.

(69)

And from (
62
), for
k>K_{2}
:

|O_{k}O_{k+2}|\leqslant\frac{c\cdot C^{2}}{\rho R_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}.

(70)

The sum of the geometric progression with a ratio smaller than 1 is finite. So the sequence
\{O_{2k}\}
is a Cauchy sequence, and so it has a limit in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The same argument holds for
\{O_{2k+1}\},
since it is the sequence of even order circumcenters, with the starting simplex
S_{2}
instead of
S_{1}.

∎

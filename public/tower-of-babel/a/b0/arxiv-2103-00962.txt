High-resolution infrared action spectroscopy of the fundamental vibrational band of CN +
José L. Doménech, Oskar Asvany, Charles R. Markus, Stephan Schlemmer, Sven Thorwirth

Abstract
Rotational-vibrational transitions of the fundamental vibrational modes of the 12 C 14 N + and 12 C 15 N + cations have been observed for the first time using a cryogenic ion trap apparatus with an action spectroscopy scheme. The lines P ⁡ ( 3 ) P(3) to R ⁡ ( 3 ) R(3) of 12 C 14 N + and R ⁡ ( 1 ) R(1) to R ⁡ ( 3 ) R(3) of 12 C 15 N + have been measured, limited by the trap temperature of approximately 4 K and the restricted tuning range of the infrared laser. Spectroscopic parameters are presented for both isotopologues, with band origins at 2000.7587(1) and 1970.321(1) cm -1 , respectively, as well as an isotope independent fit combining the new and the literature data.

Introduction

The first molecules observed in the interstellar medium (ISM) were the carbon containing diatomic species CH,
\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
, and CN, all identified through their electronic spectra
[
1
,
2
]
.
The cyano radical (CN) has been the subject of many laboratory and astronomical investigations, having been found in a variety of astronomical environments including diffuse and dense molecular clouds
[
3
,
4
]
, circumstellar envelopes
[
5
]
, and external galaxies
[
6
]
.
For decades, it has been a valuable probe of the conditions in these environments and has been used to accurately determine the temperature of the cosmic microwave background
[
7
,
8
,
3
]
.
Its cation form,
\text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
, is thought to play an important role in the formation of CN in shielded regions where there is sufficient
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
density
[
9
,
4
]
.
In regions with high
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
density, on the other side, the presence of
\text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
is unlikely,
due to its fast reaction with molecular hydrogen
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.
A preliminary search for
\text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
in the ISM, which relied on predictions of the rotational transitions from ultraviolet measurements
[
14
,
15
]
, ultimately proved unsuccessful
[
16
]
.

\text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
is isoelectronic with
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
, and therefore was expected to have either a
{}^{1}\Sigma^{+}
or
{}^{3}\Pi
ground state.
Quantum-chemical calculations have had difficulty predicting the relative energy of these low lying states, and have not been able to definitively determine which electronic state has the lowest energy
[
17
,
18
]
.
The first laboratory measurements of
\text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
were achieved through ultraviolet emission spectroscopy of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
/He discharges
[
14
,
15
]
,
however, at that time it was not possible to identify the electronic ground state with certainty.
Recently, millimeter- and sub-millimeter wave rotational spectra of both the
\text{C}\hphantom{{}^{\text{14}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{14}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{14}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{14}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{14}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
and
\text{C}\hphantom{{}^{\text{15}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{15}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{15}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{15}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{15}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
isotopologues were observed for the first time using
action spectroscopy via
state-dependent attachment of He-atoms in a cryogenic ion trap
[
19
]

finally confirming a
{}^{1}\Sigma^{+}
ground electronic state.

In the present investigation, we have extended the sub-mm work on
\text{CN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
to the infrared.
Rotational-vibrational transitions of
\text{C}\hphantom{{}^{\text{14}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{14}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{14}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{14}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{14}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}

\mu

Due to its cryogenic operation principle and the small rotational partition function of CN

This investigation provides experimental benchmarks for future high-level computational investigations of

Experimental setup

The experiments were conducted in a cryogenic 22-pole ion trap apparatus (COLTRAP),
which has previously been described in detail
[
21
,
20
]
.
As the recent rotational investigation of CN
+
has been performed in the same machine
with very similar experimental conditions
[
19
]
, only a brief description is given here.
CN
+
ions are created inside a storage ion source through electron impact ionization (
E_{e^{-}}\approx 30
eV) of methyl cyanide vapour, CH
3
CN.
As isotopically enriched methyl cyanide is readily available commercially,
C
15
N
+
was produced from CH
3
C
15
N (Sigma Aldrich, 98 %
15
N).
Using an excess of helium in the source chamber (
p\approx 10^{-4}
mbar) turned out to further enhance CN
+
production, as described in
[
19
]
.
Ion pulses are extracted from the source and mass selected
(e.g.
m/z=26
for
12
C
14
N
+
) in a subsequent quadrupole mass filter.
When the selected ions enter the 22-pole trap, they are slowed down and cooled to the ambient trap temperature of 4 K by collisions with
He gas (the trap is constantly filled with a number density of about
10^{15}
cm
-3
).
Typically, about 10,000 ions are stored inside the trap, and due to the low temperature and large helium number density, also
CN
+
-He complexes are formed via ternary collisions. Because of destruction of these complexes in collisions with He atoms,
an equilibrium

\rm{CN^{+}}+2\,He\,\,\,\mathop{\rightleftarrows}\,\,\,CN^{+}\text{-}He+He\,\,,

(1)

is reached inside the trap after a couple of 10 ms.
A typical distribution of the CN
+
-(He)
n
complexes formed in the trap
is shown in Fig.
1
.

For the detection of the rovibrational transitions, the method of laser induced inhibition of complex growth
(LIICG,
[
20
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
) was applied.
This action spectroscopic method exploits the fact that
vibrational excitation of the bare ion leads to
a suppression of the formation of the cation-helium complex (CN
+
-He in this case).
This action can be detected by extraction of the trap content,
mass selection for CN
+
-He (e.g., at
m/z=26+4=30
, see arrow in Fig.
1
), and counting of these clusters using a Daly-type detector.
A rovibrational line, appearing as dip in the CN
+
-He counts, is recorded by repeating these trap cycles at 1 Hz and
counting CN
+
-He as a function of the laser frequency.

In the present investigation,
a quantum cascade laser (QCL, Daylight Solutions) operating in the range 1961–2205 cm
-1

and providing up to several 100 mW of power, has been applied.
The intrinsic linewidth of the QCL is specified to be smaller than 30 MHz.
A fraction of the mid-IR light was picked off and guided to a wavemeter (Bristol 621 A-IR) for frequency determination, while the rest was sent through the UHV environment of the 22-pole ion trap via a pair of CaF
2
windows.
After leaving the ion trap machine the radiation passed through a 20 cm long cell containing OCS gas at
\sim 1
mbar pressure. A power sensor head (Thorlabs S302C) was placed at the end of the cell to monitor the power of the IR radiation and to record the OCS absorption lines which were used for absolute frequency calibration.

Isotope invariant fitting

The newly obtained data have also been reduced using a global Dunham-Watson type

Fitting was performed using Pickett’s SPFIT/SPCAT

and

Astrochemical considerations and future perspectives

Now that the high resolution pure rotational and rotational-vibrational spectra of CN
+
have been
studied, a closer look at the reaction kinetics may be indicated.
Of particular importance in an astrophysical scenario is the reaction of CN
+

Hydrogenation proceeds in two steps finally leading to the terminal product HCNH

\displaystyle\rm{CN^{+}}+\rm{H}_{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}

\displaystyle\rm{HCN^{+}}+\rm{H}

(2)

\displaystyle\rm{HCN^{+}}+\rm{H}_{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k_{3}}}{{\longrightarrow}}

\displaystyle\rm{HCNH}^{+}+\rm{H}\,\,,

(3)

with a similar reaction chain involving HNC

Consequently, this behaviour

To the best of our knowledge, there are no measurements of these reactions

For such exothermic and direct hydrogen abstraction reactions,
no temperature dependence is expected.
Nonetheless, we caught a preliminary glimpse of this

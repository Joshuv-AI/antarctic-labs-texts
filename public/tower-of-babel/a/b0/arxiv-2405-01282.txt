Oort’s conjecture and automorphisms of supersingular curves of genus four
Dušan Dragutinović

Abstract
. We show that every component of the locus of smooth supersingular curves of genus 4 4 in characteristic p > 2 p>2 has a trivial generic automorphism group. As a result, we prove Oort’s conjecture about automorphism groups of supersingular abelian fourfolds for p > 2 p>2 . Our main idea consists of estimating dimensions of the loci of smooth supersingular curves that admit an automorphism of prime order by considering possible choices of the corresponding quotient curves. This reasoning also results in a new proof of Oort’s conjecture for g = 3 g=3 and p > 2 p>2 , previously proved by Karemaker, Yuboko, and Yu.

1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and
E
be a supersingular elliptic curve over
k
, that is
E[p](\bar{k})=\{O\}
. For any
g\geq 2
, we say that a
g
-dimensional abelian variety
A
over
k
is
supersingular
if there is a
\bar{k}
-isogeny

A\otimes\bar{k}\overset{\sim}{\to}E^{g}\otimes\bar{k}
. Denote by
\mathcal{A}_{g}=\mathcal{A}_{g}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
the moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension
g
, and let
\mathcal{S}_{g}\subset\mathcal{A}_{g}
be the closed locus of all supersingular
g
-dimensional abelian varieties. It is well-known that
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is a smooth irreducible stack of dimension
\dim\mathcal{A}_{g}=\frac{g(g+1)}{2}
. By
[
LO98
, Theorem 4.9]
, we know that
\mathcal{S}_{g}
is pure of dimension
\left\lfloor\frac{g^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor
, while its number of irreducible components is given in terms of certain class numbers. Throughout the paper, by an automorphism, we mean a geometric automorphism. In
[
EMO01
, Problem 4]
, Oort posed the following conjecture and open problem.

Conjecture 1.1

(Oort’s conjecture)
.

For any
g\geq 2
and a prime number
p>0
, every component of the supersingular locus
\mathcal{S}_{g}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{g}
has generic automorphism group
\{\pm 1\}
.

Open problem 1.2
.

Suppose that
g\geq 3
and a
p
is a prime number. Suppose there exists a supersingular curve of genus
g
in characteristic
p
. Is it true that there exists a supersingular curve
C
of genus
g
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
such that
\mathrm{Aut}(C)
is trivial?

So far, it is known that Oort’s conjecture fails for
(g,p)=(2,2)
by
[
Ibu20
]
and for
(g,p)=(3,2)
by
[
Oor91
]
, while it holds when
g=2
or
g=3
for any
p>2
by
[
Ibu20
]
and
[
KYY21
]
. Before we state our main result, let us briefly introduce some notions.
To a stable curve
C
we can associate its Jacobian
\mathcal{J}_{C}=\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)
which is a semi-abelian variety. This induces the Torelli morphism
j:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}\to{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}}_{g}
, where
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
is the moduli space of stable curves of genus
g
and
{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}}_{g}
is a fixed smooth toroidal compactification of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
as in
[
Ale05
, Theorem 4.1]
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{g}
the sublocus of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
consisting of smooth curves, and recall the well-known fact
\dim\mathcal{M}_{g}=3g-3
.
We say that a stable curve
C
is supersingular if its Jacobian is supersingular;
C
is then necessarily of compact type, i.e., its dual graph is a tree. Denote by
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{ss}
the locus of smooth supersingular curves of genus
g
in characteristic
p>0
. In Section
2
, we give an overview of all invariants in characteristic
p>0
that we will use throughout the paper. In Section
4
, we prove the following theorem, our main result.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
p>2
, every component of
\mathcal{M}_{4}^{ss}
has a trivial generic automorphism group.

As an immediate consequence, we get an answer to Open problem
1.2
for
g=4
and
p>2
.

Corollary 1.4
.

For
p>2
, there exists a smooth supersingular curve of genus
4
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
whose automorphism group is trivial.

Proof.

As it was shown in
[
KHS20
, Corollary 1.2]
by construction and later in
[
Pri
, Theorem 1.1]
using a geometric argument, there exists a smooth supersingular curve of genus
4
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
for any
p>0
. In other words,
\mathcal{M}_{4}^{ss}
is always non-empty, so that Theorem
1.3
implies the result.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.3
consists of estimating dimensions of the loci of smooth supersingular curves
C
that admit an automorphism of prime order
l>0
. For any such automorphism
\iota
, we get the quotient curve
D=C/\left\langle\iota\right\rangle
, which is a smooth curve of some genus
g_{D}
. In Section
3.1
, we present some known results about the case
g_{D}=0
for any
l>0
, and in Sections
3.2
and
3.3
, we pay special attention to the case
l=2
with
g_{D}=1
or
g_{D}=2
. As another consequence of the preceding theorem, we have the following result.

Corollary 1.5
.

Oort’s conjecture
1.1
holds for
g=4
and
p>2
.

Proof.

By
[
Igu81
]
, the Torelli locus
\mathcal{J}_{4}=j(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{4})\cap\mathcal{A}_{4}
is an ample divisor in
\mathcal{A}_{4}
. Therefore, similarly as in
[
ST18
, Proposition 2.4]
, we observe that
\mathcal{J}_{4}
has a non-empty intersection with every positive dimensional closed subvariety of
\mathcal{A}_{4}
. In particular,
\mathcal{J}_{4}
intersects every component
\Gamma
of the supersingular locus
\mathcal{S}_{4}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{4}
.

A dimension count tells us that every generic point of
\mathcal{J}_{4}\cap\Gamma
represents a Jacobian of a smooth non-hyperelliptic curve
C
. Indeed, recall that the codimension of an intersection of subvarieties of
\mathcal{A}_{4}
is at most the sum of the codimensions. Therefore, as was already concluded in
[
Pri
, Theorem 1.1]
, the dimension of any component of
\mathcal{J}_{4}\cap\Gamma
is at least
3
, while the locus
\Pi
of products of supersingular Jacobians of lower dimension in
\mathcal{A}_{4}
has dimension
\dim\Pi=2
. This implies that every generic point of the intersection corresponds to a Jacobian of a smooth curve
C
. The curve
C
is non-hyperelliptic by Lemma
3.2
below.

By Torelli’s theorem
[
Mil86
, Theorem 12.1]
, for a smooth non-hyperelliptic curve
C
, it holds that
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{J}_{C})\cong\mathrm{Aut}(C)\times\{\pm 1\}
. Therefore, if there is a component of the supersingular locus
\mathcal{S}_{4}
whose generic automorphism group strictly contains
\{\pm 1\}
, it would follow that there is a component of the supersingular locus of smooth curves
\mathcal{M}_{4}^{ss}
, whose generic automorphism group is non-trivial. In Theorem
1.3
, we show this cannot be the case when
p>2
, so the result follows.
∎

We were informed that another proof of Oort’s conjecture
1.1
for
g=4
was previously obtained by Karemaker and Yu (
[
KY
]
), using different techniques than ours. Note that Corollary
1.5
does not imply Theorem
1.3
. Namely, Oort’s conjecture
1.1
might hold for some pair
(g,p)
, while all smooth supersingular curves of genus
g
in characteristic
p
have non-trivial automorphism groups, giving a negative answer to Open problem
1.2
for this pair
(g,p)
. See also Remark
4.1
, where we comment on the case
g=4
and
p=2
.

In Section
5
, we apply our ideas described above to present a new proof of Oort’s conjecture
1.1
in the case
g=3
and
p>2
. Finally, we present a proof we have not found in the literature of a well-known result by Oort in
[
Oor91
]
that there are no smooth supersingular hyperelliptic curves of genus
3
in characteristic
2
.

2.
Invariants in characteristic
p>0

From now on, let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
, and denote by
\sigma:k\to k
the Frobenius of
k
. The
p\text{-}\mathrm{rank}
of a stable curve
C
of genus
g\geq 2
, denoted by
f_{C}
, is the semisimple rank of the
\sigma
-linear Frobenius operator
F
on
H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
, i.e.,

f_{C}=\mathrm{rank}_{k}F^{g}|_{H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})};

note that
0\leq f_{C}\leq g
. This definition generalizes the well-known notion of the
p
-rank of an abelian variety
A
over
k
as the number
f_{A}
such that
\#A[p](k)=p^{f_{A}}
for the choice
A=\mathcal{J}_{C}
, where
C
is a smooth curve and
f_{C}=f_{\mathcal{J}_{C}}
. It holds that

C\text{ is supersingular }\implies f_{C}=0,

while the reverse implication does not hold for
g\geq 3
. By
[
FvdG04
, Theorem 2.3]
, the locus
V_{f}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
of all stable curves
C
of genus
g
with
f_{C}\leq f
is pure of dimension

(2.1)

\dim V_{f}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}=2g-3+f.

Let
A
be a principally polarized abelian variety (p.p.a.v.) over
k
of dimension
g
. First, let
A[p^{\infty}]=\varinjlim A[p^{n}]
be its
p
-divisible group. By the Dieudonné-Manin classification
[
Man63
]
, there are certain
p
-divisible groups
G_{c,d}
for
c,d\geq 0
relatively prime integers, such that there is an isogeny of
p
-divisible groups

(2.2)

A[p^{\infty}]\sim\bigoplus_{\lambda=\frac{d}{c+d}}G_{c,d}

for a unique choice of
slopes

\lambda
. The
Newton polygon
of
A
is the collection of all slopes
\lambda
that occur in (
2.2
), counted with multiplicities.
A
is supersingular if and only if
A[p^{\infty}]\sim(G_{1,1})^{g}
.

Now, let
A[p]
be the polarized
p
-torsion group subscheme of
A
and let
\mathbb{D}(A)=\mathbb{D}(A[p])
be its polarized Dieudonné module. By
[
Oor01
, Section 5]
or
[
vdG99
, Section 2]
, there is a
1
-to-
1
correspondence between the isomorphism classes of all
\mathbb{D}(A)
and the Young diagrams

\mu=[\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{n}],

for some
n\in\{0,1,\ldots,g\}
and
\mu_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
g\geq\mu_{1}>\ldots\mu_{n}>0
. (
\mu=\varnothing
if
n=0
.) We call the
\mu
associated with
A
via the correspondence above the
Ekedahl-Oort type
of
A
and write
\mu(A)=\mu
. Denote by
Z_{\mu}
the locus in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
consisting of (isomorphism classes of) all p.p.a.v.’s
A
with
\mu(A)=\mu
, and by
\overline{Z}_{\mu}
its closure in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
. By
[
Oor01
, Corollary 11.2, Theorem 1.2]
, each
Z_{\mu}
is a locally closed subset, pure of codimension
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}
in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
, and it is quasi-affine, so it cannot contain a positive-dimensional complete subvariety.
Furthermore, by
[
Oor01
, Proposition 11.1]
, if we introduce a partial ordering by

\mu=[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}]\geq\mu^{\prime}=[\mu^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mu^{\prime}_{m}]\text{ if }n\leq m\text{ and }\mu_{i}\leq\mu_{i}^{\prime}\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n,

as long as
g\geq\max\{\mu_{1},\mu_{1}^{\prime}\}
, we get

\mu^{\prime}\leq\mu\quad\implies Z_{\mu^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{Z}_{\mu}\text{ in }\mathcal{A}_{g}.

If
\mu(A)=[\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{n}]
, then we can compute the
p
-rank of
A
as
f_{A}=g-\mu_{1}
. Finally, if
\mu(A)=[g,g-1,\ldots,2,1]
, then
A\cong E^{g}
with
E
a supersingular elliptic curve and we say that such an
A
is
superspecial
. A stable curve
C
is
superspecial
if its Jacobian
\mathcal{J}_{C}
is superspecial; note that there are at most finitely many smooth superspecial curves of fixed genus
g\geq 2
in characteristic
p>0
.
Here are some properties when

.

3.
Loci of curves with automorphisms and quotient curves

Let
C
be a smooth curve of genus
g
over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p>0
and assume that there exists an automorphism
\gamma
of
C
, which is of prime order
l>0
. For a moment, let us assume that
l\neq p
. Denote
G=\left\langle\gamma\right\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/l\mathbb{Z}
. Then, there exists
D=C/G
, the quotient of
C
by
G
, which is a smooth curve. Its function field
\kappa(D)
is the subfield of
\kappa(C)
fixed by
G
, i.e.,
\kappa(D)=\kappa(C)^{G}
. Consider the canonical map

C\to D=C/G.

Its local description tells us that the ramification points of this map are exactly the points of
C
with a non-trivial stabilizer in
G
so that the ramification indices are either
1
or
l
. In other words, this map is a
(\mathbb{Z}/l\mathbb{Z})
-cover. The Riemann-Hurwitz formula tells us that
2g_{C}-2=l(2g_{D}-2)+|B|(l-1)
, where
g_{C}
and
g_{D}
are the genera of
C
and
D
, and
B
is the set of branch points. Given a smooth curve
D
of genus
g_{D}\geq 0
and a finite set
B
of points on
D
, there are (at most) finitely many
(\mathbb{Z}/l\mathbb{Z})
-covers
C\to D
whose sets of branch points coincide with
B
. One computes the genus of
C
using the mentioned formula.

We recall the following bound on the dimension of the locus of curves with automorphisms obtained by Achter, Glass, and Pries using the description above, which also holds for
l=p
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
AGP08
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
l>0
be any prime number, and let
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{l}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{g}
be the closed locus of curves that admit an automorphism of order
l
and suppose that
\Gamma_{l}
is an irreducible component of
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{l}
with generic point
\eta
. If
D
is the quotient of
C_{\eta}
by a group of order
l
, and
g_{D}
and
f_{D}
are respectively its genus and
p\text{-}\mathrm{rank}
, then

\dim\Gamma_{l}\leq 2(g-g_{D})/(l-1)+f_{D}-1.

3.1.
Cyclic covers of the projective line

In this subsection, we consider the cyclic covers of the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degrees
2
,
3
, and
l=p
, which correspond to hyperelliptic, cyclic trigonal, and Artin-Schreier curves respectively. Recall that there is a unique projective automorphism that sends any triple of distinct points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to the triple
(0,1,\infty)
.

First, we mention a result about smooth
hyperelliptic curves

C
, i.e., the curves with an automorphism
\iota\in\mathrm{Aut}(C)
of degree
2
, such that
C/\left\langle\iota\right\rangle\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{g}
denote the locus of all smooth hyperelliptic curves of genus
g
in characteristic
p>2
. It is well known that

\dim\mathcal{H}_{g}=2g-1,

which corresponds to the choice of
2g+2
branch points of the double cover
C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
C
a smooth hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
, and fixing three of the branch points. Similarly to (
2.1
), it was shown in
[
GP05
, Theorem 1]
that the dimension of any
p
-rank
\leq f
locus
V_{f}\mathcal{H}_{g}
of
\mathcal{H}_{g}
equals

\dim V_{f}\mathcal{H}_{g}=g-1+f.

Denote by
\mathcal{H}_{g}^{ss}
the locus of supersingular curves in
\mathcal{H}_{g}
(possibly empty for
g\geq 4
) and recall
\mathcal{H}_{g}^{ss}\subseteq V_{0}\mathcal{H}_{g}
. The following result is due to Oort for
g=3
and Achter-Pries for
g\geq 4
.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
Oor91
, Theorem 1.12]
,
[
AP11
, Corollary 3.16]
,
p>2
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{g}^{ss}
be the locus of smooth hyperelliptic curves of genus
g\geq 3
which are supersingular. Then
\dim\mathcal{H}_{3}^{ss}=1
and
\dim\mathcal{H}_{g}^{ss}\leq g-2
for
g\geq 4
.

Denote by
\mathcal{T}_{g}
the locus of smooth
cyclic trigonal
curves of genus
g
, i.e., of smooth curves
C
of genus
g
with an automorphism
\iota\in\mathrm{Aut}(C)
of degree
3
such that
C/\left\langle\iota\right\rangle\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Denote
G=\left\langle\iota\right\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
. Assume that
p>0
and
p\neq 3
. We can see that the cover
C\to C/G\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
is ramified at
g+2
points so that
\mathcal{T}_{g}
is pure of dimension

\dim\mathcal{T}_{g}=g-1.

Let
\xi:\{1,2,\ldots,g+2\}\to(\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})^{*}
be a map of sets such that
\sum_{i=1}^{g+2}\xi(i)=0\in\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
called the class vector and let
\overline{\xi}=(|\xi^{-1}(1)|,|\xi^{-1}(2)|)
be its inertia type. By
[
AP07
, Lemma 2.3]
, for any irreducible component of the locus
\mathcal{T}_{g,\mathrm{{ord}}}
of smooth cyclic trigonal curves of genus
g
with ordered ramification points, there is a class vector
\xi
as above. We denote this component by
\mathcal{T}_{g,\mathrm{ord}}^{\xi}
. By forgetting the labeling, we get the map
\mathcal{T}_{g,\mathrm{ord}}^{\xi}\to\mathcal{T}_{g}^{\overline{\xi}}
with
\mathcal{T}_{g}^{\overline{\xi}}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{g}
. We see that
\mathcal{T}_{g}
consists of the union of such
\mathcal{T}_{g}^{\overline{\xi}}
and note that the choice of
\xi
corresponds to encoding the action of
G
on the tangent spaces of

at the ramification points. For more details, see
[
AP07
, Section 2.1]
.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
AP07
]
,

)
.

Every component

of the locus

of smooth supersingular cyclic trigonal curves of genus

has dimension
\dim\Gamma\leq g-2
.

Proof.

By the proof of
[
AP07
, Corollary 3.11]
, every component
\mathcal{T}_{g,\mathrm{ord}}^{\xi}
of
\mathcal{T}_{g,\mathrm{ord}}
contains an element whose underlying smooth curve

satisfies that its Jacobian

is absolutely simple. This curve

is supersingular. Therefore,

, which proves the result.

\mathcal{AS}_{g,f}\subseteq\mathcal{AS}_{g}

, and by

. The

was studied in

]
. It turns out that

and

Lemma 3.4

(
[
PZ12
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
g=d(p-1)/2

f=0
. Then, there is a unique component of the

locus
\mathcal{AS}_{g,0}

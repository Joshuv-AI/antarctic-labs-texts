Involutive Moving Frames II; The Lie-Tresse theorem


Abstract
This paper continues the project, begun in [ 1 ] , of harmonizing Cartan’s classical equivalence method and the modern equivariant moving frame in a framework dubbed involutive moving frames . As an attestation of the fruitfulness of our framework, we obtain a new, constructive and intuitive proof of the Lie-Tresse theorem (Fundamental basis theorem) and a first general upper bound on the minimal number of generating differential invariants for Lie pseudo-groups. Further, we demonstrate the computational advantages of this framework by studying the equivalence problem for first order PDE in two independent variables and one dependent variable under point transformations.

Introduction

This paper continues the project, begun in
[
1
]
, of relating Cartan’s equivalence method to the equivariant moving frame for pseudo-groups. The major step was accomplished in
[
1
, Theorem 3.12]
where Cartan’s classical solution to the congruence problem for submanifolds of finite dimensional Lie groups was extended to the groupoids associated to Lie pseudo-groups. Cartan’s solution in the finite dimensional case underlies his classical method of moving frames as well as the modern equivariant moving frame,
[
2
]
and its infinite dimensional analog harmonized the finite and infinite dimensional methods of equivalence. However, the key theorem
[
1
, Theorem 3.12]
was most directly applicable to so-called horizontal actions which left out some important equivalence problems, particularly those for partial differential equations. The current paper completes this picture by clarifying precisely which equivalence problems can be solved using some version of
[
1
, Theorem 3.12]
and how the theorem applies.

There are two major consequences of this, both of which indicate that our framework is fruitful for equivalence problems for Lie pseudo-groups. First, we obtain a new, constructive and intuitive proof of the Lie-Tresse theorem on the finiteness of generators of the differential invariant algebra of Lie pseudo-groups. Our proof is much simpler than previous modern proofs,
[
3
,
4
,
10
]
, and, interestingly, makes critical use of the finite dimensional theory. Second, there are enormous computational advantages to the combination of the equivariant moving frame and Cartan’s geometry. We study, in Section
2.4
, the general equivalence problem of first order PDE

u_{y}=f(x,y,u,u_{x})

under point transformations and characterize those equations that are point-equivalent to
u_{y}=0
and
u_{y}=u_{x}^{2}
. The branching found here are new. A forthcoming paper is dedicated to the computational advantages of our framework.

The paper’s setup is as follows. In Section
2
we give a quick review of Lie pseudo-groups and equivariant moving frames and refine the definition of a partial moving frame from
[
1
]
. We explain how the key results from
[
1
]
carry over to the class of “vertically integrable” pseudo groups, leading to the equivalence method in Theorem
2.8
. In the following section we nail down the precise condition on pseudo-groups for which Theorem
2.8
is most natural and we demonstrate this application by studying first order PDE under point-transformations. In the final section we give a new, constructive and intuitive proof of the Lie-Tresse theorem. This version of the theorem also gives general upper bounds on the minimal number of generating invariants and we consider a few examples of this bound. In the Appendix we consider the fundamental concept from
[
10
]
of
persistence of freeness
of Lie pseudo-groups. This property of pseudo-groups was proven in
[
10
]
by rather complex means, but we show that it becomes essentially trivial in our framework.

S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are locally congruent under some
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
.

2.

For each section
s_{1}
of
\mathcal{B}^{j^{q}S_{1}}_{p}\to\mathcal{X}
there is a section
s_{2}
of
\mathcal{B}^{j^{q}S_{2}}_{p}\to\mathcal{X}
and a map
f
such that

f^{*}s_{2}^{*}\bar{S}_{2}^{*}\mu^{a}_{A}=s_{1}^{*}\bar{S}_{1}\mu^{a}_{A},\quad|A|<p.

3.

There exists a pair of sections,
s_{j}
of
\mathcal{B}^{j^{q}S_{j}}_{p}\to\mathcal{X}
,
j=1,2
, and a map
f
such that

f^{*}s_{2}^{*}\bar{S}_{2}^{*}\mu^{a}_{A}=s_{1}^{*}\bar{S}_{1}\mu^{a}_{A},\quad|A|<p.

This theorem presents us with two problems. First we must solve the coframe equivalence problem in part 3 of Theorem
2.8
to obtain a function
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
and its corresponding

of Theorem
2.4
. Then we must integrate

and
f_{p}
to a pseudo-group element
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
. In this paper we shall focus on those vertically integrable pseudo-groups where the section

can be

\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
. We shall call these pseudo-groups

and we now turn to characterizing them in terms of their determining equations. Note that for these quasi-horizontal pseudo-groups it is enough to find the

of part 3 of Theorem

in order to completely solve the equivalence problem. Hence, the involutive moving frame method from
[
1
]
will completely carry over to the quasi-horizontal case.

2.3
Quasi-horizontal Lie pseudo groups

We now turn to the characterization of Lie pseudo-groups for which the sections
f_{p}
of Theorem
2.4
can be uniquely integrated to pseudo-group elements.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Lie pseudo-group, of order
t^{*}
, of transformations on
\mathcal{E}
with determining equations
F^{(t)}(t,Z^{(t)})=0
. Given local coordinates
z=(x,u)
on
\mathcal{E}
, the elements of
\mathcal{H}
are local diffeomorphisms
(x,u)=z\mapsto Z=(X,U)
, on
\mathcal{E}
. Then
\mathcal{H}
is called
quasi-horizontal
if there exists an
r\geq t^{*}
such that the following holds.

In the determining equations of
\mathcal{H}
, each derivative of order
r
,
Z^{a}_{A}
,
|A|=r
, which has at least one
u^{\alpha}
derivative can be taken as principal. In other words, for each
A\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r-1}
,
1\leq a\leq n+m
and
1\leq\alpha\leq m
, one of the determining equations
F^{(r)}=0
for
\mathcal{H}
reads

\frac{\partial^{r}Z^{a}}{\partial u^{\alpha}\partial z^{A}}=F^{a}_{\alpha,A}(z,Z^{(r)}).

Notice that if the two conditions hold for some
r
, they also hold for all
r^{\prime}\geq r
. We say
\mathcal{H}
is
quasi-horizontal of order

r
if
r
is the smallest number for which the above holds.

Remark 2.10
.

Importantly, if
\mathcal{H}
is quasi-horizontal in variables
(x,u)
it is also quasi-horizontal (with the same horizontal order) after any fiber preserving change of variables in
\mathcal{E}
,
(x,u)\mapsto(y(x),w(x,u))
. The above definition is therefore consistent on a general bundle
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{X}
.

Remark 2.11
.

Horizontal pseudo-groups, as defined in
[
1
]
, are trivially quasi-horizontal.

Now let
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
and
f_{p}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{H}^{S_{1}}_{p}
satisfy the conditions of Theorems
2.4
and
2.8
where
\mathcal{H}
is quasi horizontal (of order
r\leq p
) and assume the images of
S_{j}
are the graphs of two functions
u_{j}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{U}
,
j=1,2
. We can straighten
S_{1}
out in the
x
-direction by a (fiber-preserving) change of variables,

(y,w)\mapsto(y,u_{1}(y)+w).

We call the coordinates
(y,w)

flat
for
S_{1}
, and by Remark
2.10

\mathcal{H}
is also quasi-horizontal in
(y,w)
. Notice that on
S_{1}
we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial y}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+u_{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial u}.

(2.10)

In the flat coordinates
S_{1}
is the zero section
y\to(y,0)
and we shall treat
\bar{S}_{1}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle\circ\,$}f_{p}(y,0)
as initial conditions for a sequence of normal systems of partial differential equations for the vector

\boldsymbol{Z}:=Z^{(p-1)}=(Z^{1},\ldots,Z^{a}_{A},\ldots)\raise-1.29167pt\hbox{\scriptsize$|$}_{|A|\leq p-1},

where
A=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n+m})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n+m}
now denote derivatives w.r.t.
(y,w)
. The
p^{\text{th}}
order determining equations for
\mathcal{H}
involve some equations that only contain
y
-derivatives of
\boldsymbol{Z}
, but these equations are satisfied by
\bar{S}_{1}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle\circ\,$}f_{p}(y,0)
by construction. The rest of the
p^{\text{th}}
order equations involve at least one
w
-derivative. Since
\mathcal{H}
is quasi-horizontal, using the standard trick of turning a PDE of arbitrary order into a first order system, we can write these latter equations as

\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{1}}\boldsymbol{Z}=\boldsymbol{F}_{1}(y,w,\boldsymbol{Z},\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}\boldsymbol{Z},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{n}}\boldsymbol{Z})

(2.11)

for some function
\boldsymbol{F}_{1}
.

Now, given the map
\bar{S}_{1}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle\circ\,$}f_{p}(y,0)
consider the initial value problem (
2.11

\boldsymbol{Z}(y,0)=\bar{S}_{1}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle\circ\,$}f_{p}(y,0)
. This is obviously a normal system and uniquely integrable to an open set in

by the Cauchy-Kovalevskaya theorem. We can then use the newly integrated

as an initial condition for a system

\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{2}}\boldsymbol{Z}=\boldsymbol{F}_{2}(y,w,\boldsymbol{Z},\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}\boldsymbol{Z},\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{n}}\boldsymbol{Z})

and so on until we have integrated
\boldsymbol{Z}
to the prolongation of a pseudo-group element
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
. This proves the following.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be quasi horizontal of order
r
and let
f
and
f_{p}
satisfy the conditions of Theorems
2.4
and
2.8
with
p\geq r
. Then
f
can be uniquely integrated to a pseudo-group element
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
.

Theorem
2.12
allows us to transfer the equivalence method for horizontal actions from
[

]
to the quasi horizontal case as outlined in the comments following Theorem
2.8
. We now demonstrate the use of Theorem
2.8
by studying the equivalence problem of first order partial differential equations.

2.4
Equivalence of first order PDE

Consider a general first order partial differential equation for a scalar function

of two variables,

u_{y}=f(x,y,u,u_{x}).

We shall study the equivalence of such equations under the pseudo-group,
\mathcal{G}
, of
point transformations
.

Remark 2.13
.

We shall not go into too much detail regarding the actual, nitty-gritty, computations in this example. A forthcoming paper is dedicated to the computational advantages of the involutive moving frame.

Writing
u_{x}=p
and
u_{y}=q

(x,y,u,p,q)\overset{\varphi}{\mapsto}(X,Y,U,P,Q).

Since the above is the first prolongation of

X_{p}=X_{q}=Y_{p}=Y_{q}=U_{p}=U_{q}=0
and

\begin{bmatrix}P\\
Q\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}X_{x}+pX_{u}&X_{y}+qX_{y}\\
Y_{x}+pY_{u}&Y_{y}+qY_{u}\end{bmatrix}^{-T}\begin{bmatrix}U_{x}+pU_{u}\\
U_{y}+qU_{u}\end{bmatrix}.

(2.12)

These are the basic first order equations for
\mathcal{G}
from which all other defining equations are found, by prolongation and projection. Note that differentiating both sides of (
2.12
) w.r.t.
q
will give determining equations
P_{q}=\cdots
and
Q_{q}=\cdots
where the right hand sides are first order, and we easily determine that this Lie pseudo-group is quasi horizontal of horizontal order 1.

Since the entries of the matrix above appear so frequently throughout we denote them by

\begin{bmatrix}g_{11}&g_{12}\\
g_{21}&g_{22}\end{bmatrix}:=\begin{bmatrix}X_{x}+pX_{u}&X_{y}+qX_{y}\\
Y_{x}+pY_{u}&Y_{y}+qY_{u}\end{bmatrix}.

We normalize
(X,Y,U,P,Q)
to zero so that

U_{x}=-pU_{u},\quad U_{y}=-qU_{u}

and the recurrence formula gives
\mu^{u}_{X}=-\omega^{p}
and
\mu^{u}_{Y}=-\sum\widehat{Q}_{i}\omega^{i}

\widehat{Q}_{i}
gives

\displaystyle d\widehat{Q}_{X}

\displaystyle=\sum\widehat{Q}_{Xi}\omega^{i}+\omega^{p}\widehat{Q}_{U}-\widehat{Q}_{P}\mu^{p}_{X}+\widehat{Q}_{P}\widehat{Q}_{X}\mu^{y}_{X}+\widehat{Q}_{X}\mu^{u}_{U}-\widehat{Q}_{X}\mu^{x}_{X}-\widehat{Q}_{X}\mu^{y}_{Y}-\widehat{Q}_{Y}\mu^{y}_{X}+\mu^{p}_{Y},

\displaystyle d\widehat{Q}_{Y}

\displaystyle=\sum\widehat{Q}_{Yi}\omega^{i}+\omega^{q}\widehat{Q}_{U}-\widehat{Q}_{P}\mu^{p}_{Y}+\widehat{Q}_{P}\widehat{Q}_{Y}\mu^{y}_{X}+\widehat{Q}_{Y}\mu^{u}_{U}-\widehat{Q}_{X}\mu^{x}_{Y}-2\widehat{Q}_{Y}\mu^{y}_{Y}+\mu^{q}_{Y},

\displaystyle d\widehat{Q}_{U}

\displaystyle=\sum\widehat{Q}_{Ui}\omega^{i}-\widehat{Q}_{P}\mu^{p}_{U}+\widehat{Q}_{P}\widehat{Q}_{U}\mu^{y}_{X}-\widehat{Q}_{X}\mu^{x}_{U}-\widehat{Q}_{U}\mu^{y}_{Y}-\widehat{Q}_{Y}\mu^{y}_{U}+\mu^{q}_{U},

\displaystyle d\widehat{Q}_{P}

\displaystyle=\sum\widehat{Q}_{Pi}\omega^{i}+\mu^{x}_{X}+\widehat{Q}_{P}^{2}\mu^{y}_{X}-\widehat{Q}_{P}\mu^{y}_{Y}-\mu^{x}_{Y},

where, in the sums above,

for

\displaystyle\mu^{p}_{Y}

\displaystyle=-\sum\widehat{Q}_{Xi}\omega^{i},

\displaystyle\mu^{q}_{Y}

\displaystyle=-\sum\widehat{Q}_{Yi}\omega^{i},

\displaystyle\mu^{q}_{U}

\displaystyle=-\sum\widehat{Q}_{Ui}\omega^{i},

\displaystyle\mu^{x}_{Y}

\displaystyle=-\sum\widehat{Q}_{Pi}\omega^{i}.

Also, the recurrence formula

now becomes
\mu^{u}_{Y}=0

The next step in the equivalence procedure is to compute the structure equations
d\omega^{i}
on our equivariant moving frame
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{1,1}
. Doing so we encounter as structure coefficients the lifted second order invariants
\widehat{Q}_{ij}

\widehat{Q}_{PP}
to zero. At this point, there are
r^{(2)}=13
second order group parameters we have

and the second order jets

Meanwhile,

\widehat{Q}_{PP}=\frac{g_{11}^{2}q_{pp}}{U_{u}(g_{22}-g_{21}q_{p})}

and so this equivalence problem branches at this point, depending on whether
q_{pp}=0
or not.

2.4.1
Branch 1

When
q_{pp}=0
the original PDE has the form

u_{y}=g(x,y,u)u_{x}+h(x,y,u).

On this branch
\widehat{Q}_{PP}=0
and there are no more normalizable structure coefficients in

. We must then check for involutivity of the
\omega^{i}

\displaystyle d\omega^{x}

\displaystyle=-\omega^{x}\wedge\mu^{x}_{X}-\omega^{u}\wedge\mu^{x}_{U},

\displaystyle d\omega^{u}

\displaystyle=-\omega^{x}\wedge\mu^{y}_{X}-\omega^{u}\wedge\mu^{y}_{Y}-\omega^{u}\wedge\mu^{y}_{U},

\displaystyle d\omega^{u}

\displaystyle=\omega^{x}\wedge\omega^{p}-\omega^{u}\wedge\mu^{u}_{U},

\displaystyle d\omega^{p}

\displaystyle=-\omega^{x}\wedge\mu^{p}_{X}-\omega^{u}\wedge\mu^{p}_{U}-\omega^{p}\wedge\mu^{u}_{U}+\omega^{p}\wedge\mu^{x}_{X}.

To perform Cartan’s test for involutivity we compute the reduced Cartan characters
s^{(1)}_{1},s^{(1)}_{2},s^{(1)}_{3},s^{(1)}_{4}
for the above system. The first Cartan character

is the maximum rank of the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}-a&0&0&-c&0&0&0&0\\
0&-a&-b&0&-c&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&0&-c&0&0\\
d&0&0&0&0&-d&-a&-c\\
\end{array}\right)

where

and

,
s^{(1)}_{3}=1
and

Since this is true, the frame

Branch 2

At the next stage, computing

In the case this expression is identically zero, for example for the PDE

\displaystyle d\omega^{x}

\displaystyle d\omega^{y}

\displaystyle=-\omega^{x}\wedge\mu^{y}_{X}-\omega^{u}\wedge\mu^{y}_{Y},

The Lie-Tresse theorem

When constructing a (partial) moving frame for a quasi-horizontal pseudo-group
\mathcal{H}
of order
r
there are two possible outcomes as one navigates the different branches that emerge. The first possibility is that at some stage the Maurer-Cartan forms
\mu^{a}_{A}
,
|A|<p
,
r\leq p
, on our partial moving frame
\mathcal{B}_{p,q}\to\mathcal{S}_{q}
are involutive,
[
1
]
. In this case, usually all sections whose jets lie within the domain of definition
\mathcal{S}_{q}
are locally congruent as there are rarely any invariants present (although there are exceptions,
[
6
, p. 370]
).

Alternatively one manages to normalize all pseudo-group parameters of some order
p
. The moving frame machinery is then continued where we investigate the structure functions of the coframe in a search for further normalizations of group parameters. On some branches these structure functions are all constant and no more normalizations are possible. In these cases, the sections of that branch are all equivalent and have a finite dimensional Lie group of symmetries.

In this section we look at the case when we can normalize all pseudo-group parameters of order
\leq p
to obtain some partial moving frame
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{p,q}\to\mathcal{S}_{q}
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}:=(\pi^{\infty}_{q})^{-1}(\mathcal{S}_{q})
. A differential invariant is a smooth
\mathcal{H}
-invariant function on
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
. The
Lie-Tresse theorem
, or
Fundamental basis theorem
, first proved by Lie in the finite dimensional case,
[
5
, p.760]
, and extended by Tresse to include pseudo-groups,
[
13
]
, states that the algebra (over
\mathbb{R}
) generated by all such differential invariants is
finitely generated
in the following sense. There exist differential invariants
I_{1},\ldots,I_{t}
and invariant differential operators
\mathcal{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{D}_{n}
such that
all
differential invariants can be written as smooth functions of the
I_{i}
and their derivatives w.r.t
\mathcal{D}_{j}
. In
[
10
]
the authors gave a constructive, practical proof of this theorem, which nevertheless relied upon some complicated results on non-standard algebraic objects like “eventual submodules” and required the computation of a Groebner basis for a certain module. In this section we give a straightforward and simple proof that is more practical than the one in
[
10
]
since once we have completed an equivalence problem, we may simply read a generating set of invariants off the final structure equations.

Remark 3.1
.

In the Appendix we show that freeness (i.e. all pseudo-group parameters can be normalized) of a Lie pseudo-group implies quasi-horizontality, and so we are necessarily in the quasi-horizontal case without having restricted the generality of our Lie-Tresse theorem below.

Again, let
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{p,q}\to\mathcal{S}_{q}
be a moving frame where we have normalized all pseudo-group parameters of order
\leq p
so
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{p,q}
is actually diffeomorphic to its domain of definition
\mathcal{S}_{q}\subset J^{q}(\mathcal{E})
. For reasons that become clear in a moment, assume
p=r
(we can do this since all pseudo-group parameters can be normalized by persistence of freeness, see Appendix).

Let us assume, for simplicity that
\mathcal{H}
acts transitively on
\mathcal{E}
and so we can normalize all zero order lifted invariants
Z^{a}
. Then, modulo contact forms on
\mathcal{S}_{q}
, the Maurer-Cartan forms
\mu^{a}_{A}
,
|A|<r
, corresponding to group parameters, once restricted to
B_{r,q}
, become linear combinations

\mu^{a}_{A}\mapsto\sum_{l=1}^{n}I^{a}_{A;l}\omega^{l}

of the horizontal forms
\omega^{l}
, where
I^{a}_{A;l}
are differential invariants on
\mathcal{S}_{q}
. Note that
1\leq l\leq n
. The forms
\omega^{l}
,
1\leq l\leq n
, are a basis of contact invariant horizontal forms in
J^{\infty}(\mathcal{E})
. Let us denote their dual differential operators by
\mathcal{D}_{l}
. Each
\omega^{l}
(
\mathcal{D}_{l}
) is a linear combination of the
dx^{l}
(
D_{x^{l}}
) with coefficients that are functions on
\mathcal{S}_{q}
.

The equivalence problem for sections
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of
\mathcal{E}
(with jets
j^{q}S_{j}
lying in
\mathcal{S}_{q}
) reduces, according to Theorem
2.8
, to the equivalence of the
\mu^{a}_{A}
,
|A|<r
, as pulled back to the spaces
\mathcal{B}^{j^{q}S_{1}}_{r}
and
\mathcal{B}^{j^{q}S_{2}}_{r}
, but both are diffeomorphic to open sets in the
n
dimensional base space
\mathcal{X}
. Let us describe this reduced equivalence problem on
\mathcal{X}
. Denote the pull-back of
\omega^{i}
to
\mathcal{B}^{j^{q}S_{\epsilon}}_{r}
by
\widetilde{\omega}_{\epsilon}^{i}
for
\epsilon=1,2
, and that of the invariants
I^{a}_{A;i}
by
I^{a}_{\epsilon;A;l}
, but notice that
I^{a}_{\epsilon;A;l}
is simply
I^{a}_{A;i}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle\circ\,$}j^{q}S_{\epsilon}
. A local map
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
preserves all the pulled-back Maurer-Cartan forms
\mu^{a}_{A}
,
|A|<r
, if and only if

f^{*}\widetilde{\omega}_{2}^{i}=\widetilde{\omega}_{1}^{i}\quad\text{and}\quad f^{*}I^{a}_{2;A;l}=I^{a}_{1;A;l}.

Élie Cartan solved this
(extended) coframe equivalence problem
and we briefly describe his solution, see
[
6
]
for more. Write the structure functions

d\omega^{i}=J^{i}_{j,k}\omega^{j}\wedge\omega^{k},\quad 1\leq i,j,k\leq n,

for certain differential invariants defined on
(\pi^{q+1}_{q})^{-1}\left(\mathcal{S}_{q}\right)
. Actually, the
J^{i}_{j,k}
are themselves functions of some of the
I
’s since the Maurer-Cartan structure equations on
\mathcal{D}
, (
2.2
) give

d\omega^{i}=-d\mu^{i}=-\sum_{b}\omega^{b}\wedge\mu^{i}_{b}

and so any invariant
J^{i}_{jk}
appearing above will have come from the restriction of
\mu^{i}_{b}
to the moving frame and can be found among the
I^{a}_{A;l}
. Denote the dual differential operators to the coframes
\widetilde{\omega}^{i}_{\epsilon}
by
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\epsilon}_{i}
. Note that
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\epsilon}_{i}
is obtained by evaluating the coefficients of
\mathcal{D}_{i}
at
j^{q}S_{\epsilon}
. The
invariants
of the extended coframe
\{\widetilde{\omega}_{\epsilon}^{i},I^{a}_{\epsilon;A;l}\}
are the
I^{a}_{\epsilon;A;l}
and all their derivatives w.r.t. the
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{\epsilon}_{i}
. Cartan proved that an equivalence map
f
exists if and only if
f
pulls the (infinite) collection of invariants
\{(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{2})^{K}I^{a}_{2;A;l}\}_{K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n},|A|<r}
to
\{(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}^{1})^{K}I^{a}_{1;A;l}\}_{K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n},|A|<r}
. There is a finite process for determining when this happens which has to do with finding a finite subset of the invariants that has maximal rank (again, see
[
6
]
).

Let us denote the collection of invariants and their derivatives by

\mathcal{C}:=\{(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}})^{K}I^{a}_{A;l}\}_{K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n},|A|<r}.

Note that the invariants in
\mathcal{C}
are defined on
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
. Before proceeding to the Lie-Tresse theorem we arrange the functions from
\mathcal{C}
in a map

c_{\infty}:=(I^{a}_{A;l},\ldots,\mathcal{D}^{K}I^{a}_{A;l},\ldots)_{|K|\leq\infty,\penalty\ 1\leq a\leq n+m,\penalty\ 1\leq l\leq n,\penalty\ |A|<r},

(3.1)

where
c_{\infty}:J^{\infty}(\mathcal{E})\to\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
. We shall prove that the fibers of the map
c_{\infty}
are precisely the orbits of
\mathcal{H}
on
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
so that any function, constant on these orbits, must be a function of
c_{\infty}
. Since
c_{\infty}
contains only the invariants
\mathcal{D}^{K}I^{a}_{A;l}
this will prove the Lie-Tresse theorem.

Theorem 3.2

(Lie-Tresse)
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{r,q}\to\mathcal{S}_{q}
be an equivariant moving frame for a quasi-horizontal pseudo-group
\mathcal{H}
of order
r
where we have normalized all the pseudo-group parameters of orders
\leq r
. The invariants

\{I^{a}_{A;l}\}_{1\leq l\leq n,\penalty\ 1\leq a\leq n+m,\penalty\ |A|<r}

generate the differential invariant algebra of
\mathcal{H}
on
(\pi^{\infty}_{q})^{-1}(\mathcal{S}_{q})
.

Proof.

The proof is based on the fact that the invariants

\{I^{a}_{A;l}\}_{1\leq l\leq n,\penalty\ 1\leq a\leq n+m,\penalty\ |A|<r}

determine the local congruence problem for sections in
\mathcal{E}
.

Let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be two sections of
\mathcal{E}
with infinte jets
j^{\infty}S_{j}\raise-1.29167pt\hbox{\scriptsize$|$}_{x_{j}}=z_{j}^{\infty}
at some points
x_{j}
,
j=1,2
, and suppose that all invariants of the collection
\mathcal{C}
agree at the
z_{j}^{\infty}
, i.e.

R\raise-1.29167pt\hbox{\scriptsize$|$}_{z_{1}^{\infty}}=R\raise-1.29167pt\hbox{\scriptsize$|$}_{z_{2}^{\infty}},\quad\forall R\in\mathcal{C}.

(3.2)

(Equivalently,
c_{\infty}(z_{1}^{\infty})=c_{\infty}(z_{2}^{\infty})
.)

First we show that (
3.2
) implies that for each invariant
R\in\mathcal{C}
,
R\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle\circ\,$}j^{\infty}S_{1}
and
R\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle\circ\,$}j^{\infty}S_{2}

overlap
(have the same image set) on some neighborhoods of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
. (Note that, for simplicity, we write
R\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle\circ\,$}j^{\infty}S_{j}
even though
R
only depends on the jets of
S_{j}
up to some finite order.)

Pick
n
invariants
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\in\mathcal{C}
such that

d_{H}A_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge d_{H}A_{n}\raise-1.29167pt\hbox{\scriptsize$|$}_{z^{\infty}_{j}}\neq 0

where
d_{H}
is the horizontal part of the exterior derivative on
J^{\infty}(\mathcal{E})
. (Note that we may have to restrict the
z_{j}^{\infty}
to a dense subset of
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
in order to make sure this is possible.)

The (invariant) derivative with respect to
A_{i}
,
D_{A_{i}}
, is a linear combination of the
\mathcal{D}_{j}
with coefficients depending on
\mathcal{D}_{k}A_{l}
. For a multi-index
J=(J_{1},\ldots,J_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, we shall write
D_{A}^{J}
for
D^{J_{1}}_{A_{1}}\dots D^{J_{n}}_{A_{n}}
. Therefore, by (
3.2
),

(D_{A}^{J}R)\raise-1.29167pt\hbox{\scriptsize$|$}_{z_{1}^{\infty}}=(D_{A}^{J}R)\raise-1.29167pt\hbox{\scriptsize$|$}_{z_{2}^{\infty}}

for all
R\in\mathcal{C}
and
J\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
.

Now take some invariant
R\in\mathcal{C}
and consider the two maps
R_{j}:=R\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle\circ\,$}j^{\infty}S_{j}
after the change of variables
x\mapsto(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle\circ\,$}j^{\infty}S_{j}\raise-1.29167pt\hbox{\scriptsize$|$}_{x}
. Note that
a_{0}:=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle\circ\,$}j^{\infty}S_{1}\raise-1.29167pt\hbox{\scriptsize$|$}_{x_{1}}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle\circ\,$}j^{\infty}S_{2}\raise-1.29167pt\hbox{\scriptsize$|$}_{x_{2}}
. These maps have power series expansions around
a_{0}
given by

R_{j}=\sum\frac{1}{J!}(D_{A}^{J}R)\raise-1.29167pt\hbox{\scriptsize$|$}_{z^{\infty}_{j}}(a-a_{0})^{J}.

Therefore the
R_{j}
’s have the same power series coefficients and, everything being real-analytic, there must be open neighborhoods
V^{R}_{j}
of
x_{j}
such that

R_{1}(V^{R}_{1})=R_{2}(V^{R}_{2}).

(3.3)

The next step in the proof is proving that
z_{1}^{\infty}
and
z_{2}^{\infty}
such that (
3.2
) holds are congruent under
\mathcal{H}
. This actually follows quite easily from what we have shown so far. Recall that local congruence of
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, mapping
x_{1}
to
x_{2}
(and therefore
z_{1}^{\infty}
to
z_{2}^{\infty}
) is equivalent to a local equivalence map (again, mapping
x_{1}\mapsto x_{2}
) between the extended coframes
\{\widetilde{\omega}^{i}_{1},I^{a}_{1;A;l}\}
and
\{\widetilde{\omega}^{i}_{2},I^{a}_{2;A;l}\}
. Following Cartan’s solution, there is a finite set of invariants,
R_{1},\ldots,R_{k}\in\mathcal{C}
such that there exists a local equivalence map between the extended coframes if and only if there are open neighborhoods
V_{j}
of
x_{j}
,
j=1,2
, such that
R_{t}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle\circ\,$}j^{\infty}S_{1}(V_{1})=R_{t}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle\circ\,$}j^{\infty}S_{2}(V_{2})
for all
t=1,\ldots,k
. Well, according to (
3.3
) we can take

V_{j}:=\bigcap_{t=1}^{k}V^{R_{t}}_{j},\quad j=1,2,

proving that
z_{1}^{\infty}
and
z_{2}^{\infty}
are congruent.

We have shown that the the fibers of the invariant map
c_{\infty}
and the orbits of
\mathcal{H}
on
J^{\infty}(\mathcal{E})
are the same thing. This completes the theorem.

∎

3.1
Upper bounds on the size of the generating set

Theorem
3.2
provides an upper bound on the number of generating invariants. We have that

\{I^{a}_{A;l}\}_{1\leq l\leq n,\penalty\ 1\leq a\leq n+m,\penalty\ |A|<r}

(3.4)

is a set of generators, where

\mu^{a}_{A}\mapsto I^{a}_{A;l}\omega^{l}.

Since those
\mu^{a}_{A}
corresponding to principal derivatives in the determining equations for
\mathcal{H}
are constant-coefficient linear combinations of parametric ones, we can focus only on the latter. Therefore, to count the number of
I
’s we simply have to count the number of parametric derivatives of order
<r
in
\mathcal{H}
and multiply by
n
. The former number is equal to the dimension of the groupoid
\mathcal{H}_{r-1}
. So a general upper bound for the
minimal number of generating invariants
is

n\cdot\left(\text{dim}\mathcal{H}_{r-1}\right).

In the case that
\mathcal{H}
is a finite dimensional Lie group its determining equations are maximally over-determined and so the dimension of the Lie group is equal to
\text{dim}\mathcal{H}_{r-1}
. We therefore have, for a Lie group
G
acting freely on
\mathcal{S}_{q}
the upper bound

n\cdot\left(\text{dim}G\right).

Remark 3.3
.

This upper bound, in the Lie group case, has also be deduced using the equivariant moving frame,
[
2
]
. There, the members of the generating set (
3.4
) were called
Maurer-Cartan invariants
.

Further assuming that
\mathcal{H}
acts transitively on
\mathcal{E}
so that normalizing the
X
’s gives
\mu^{i}=-\omega^{i}
and so there are
n
Maurer-Cartan forms that do not provide any invariants we obtain the upper bound

n\cdot\left(\text{dim}\mathcal{H}_{r-1}-n\right)

(3.5)

on the number of generating invariants. Similarly, if
\mathcal{H}
acts transitively on
J^{1}(\mathcal{E})
, normalizing all the first order (and zero order) lifted invariants
\widehat{U}^{\alpha}_{i}\equiv e^{\alpha}_{i}
to constants
e^{\alpha}_{i}
, the recurrence formula gives

0=d\widehat{U}^{\alpha}=\widehat{U}^{\alpha}_{i}\omega^{i}+\text{Maurer-Cartan forms}=e^{\alpha}_{i}\omega^{i}+\text{Maurer-Cartan forms}

By transitivity we can solve these equations for
m
Maurer-Cartan forms which then become
constant coefficient
combinations of
\omega
’s. We can hence subtract a further
n\cdot m
from our upper bound (
3.5
). More generally, using the same argument, if
\mathcal{H}_{r}
acts transitively on
J^{q^{*}}(\mathcal{E})
we have the upper bound

n\cdot\left(\text{dim}\mathcal{H}_{r-1}-\text{dim}J^{q^{*}-1}(\mathcal{E})\right).

Now,
\text{dim}J^{q^{*}-1}=n+m\binom{q^{*}-1+n}{n}
and so the above bound is equal to

n\cdot\left(\text{dim}\mathcal{H}_{r-1}-n-m\binom{q^{*}-1+n}{n}\right).

(3.6)

For a free Lie-group action, we replace
\text{dim}\mathcal{H}_{r-1}
with the dimension of the Lie group.

Example 3.4
.

Consider the Lie group action

(x,u)\mapsto(X,U)=(\lambda x+a,\lambda u+b),\quad\lambda>0.

The defining equations are

X_{u}=X_{xx}=U_{x}=U_{uu}=0,\quad U_{u}=X_{x}

and this pseudo-group is quasi horizontal of order
2
. By Theorem
3.2
, a second order moving frame will provide us with a generating set of invariants. Normalizing
X=U=0
the recurrence formula gives

\mu^{x}=-\omega^{x}=-X_{x}dx,\quad\mu^{u}=-\widehat{U}_{X}\omega^{x}

where
\widehat{U}_{X}=u_{x}
is an invariant. Moving up to the next order, we have

du_{x}=\widehat{U}_{XX}\omega^{x}=\frac{u_{xx}}{X_{x}}\omega^{x}

and so (assuming
u_{xx}>0
) we normalize
\widehat{U}_{XX}=1
to obtain

X_{x}=u_{xx}

and

0=d\widehat{U}_{XX}=\widehat{U}_{XXX}\omega^{x}-\mu^{x}_{X}.

Solving for
\mu^{x}_{X}
we find that

\mu^{x}_{X}=\frac{u_{xxx}}{u_{xx}^{2}}\omega^{x}.

By Theorem
3.2
,
u_{x}
and
\displaystyle\frac{u_{xxx}}{u_{xx}^{2}}
generate the invariant differential algebra. Notice that
u_{x}
alone is not a generator, since

du_{x}=\omega^{x}

and so applying the invariant derivative to
u_{x}
generates no new invariants. Hence, we have a minimal set of generators of size 2.

Now, the upper bound (
3.5
) is

1\cdot\left(3-1\right)=2.

Therefore, the upper bound (
3.5
) is actually optimal in complete generality.

Although, as the last example showed, the upper bound (
3.5
) is optimal it is far from robust; in most cases it will overshoot the actual size of a minimal generating set by quite a lot.

Example 3.5
.

Consider the Lie-group action

(x,u)\mapsto(x+a,u+p(x))

where
x,a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
p(x)
is an arbitrary polynomial of total degree at most
d
. This Lie group has dimension

n+m\binom{n+d}{n}

and acts transitively of
J^{d}(\mathcal{E})
. The upper bound (
3.6
) is

n\cdot\left(n+m\binom{n+d}{n}-n-m\binom{n+d-1}{n}\right)=nm\binom{n+d-1}{n-1}.

(3.7)

It is easily seen that a
minimal
set of generating invariants for this Lie group action is
u^{\alpha}_{J}
where
J\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
with
|J|=d+1
. This minimal generating set has

m\binom{n+d}{d+1}=m\binom{n+d}{n-1}

elements. Hence, the upperbound (
3.7
) is just the simplistic upper bound on the number of monomials in
n
variables of degree
d+1
obtained by taking the number of monomials of degree
d
and multiplying by
n
.

Appendix: Eventually free Lie pseudo-groups

As previously mentioned, the foundational work,
[
9
,
10
]
, on the equivariant moving frame for pseudo-groups focused on pseudo-group actions that were
eventually free
, meaning that at some order, the groupoid action, see (
2.4
), of
\mathcal{H}_{q}
on some subset
\mathcal{S}_{q}\subset J^{q}(\mathcal{E})
was free. Equivalently, each group parameter of order
\leq q
can be normalized in the lifted invariants
\widehat{U}^{\alpha}_{J}
,
|J|\leq q
, restricted to
\mathcal{S}_{q}
. The key result in
[
10
]
, dubbed
persistence of freeness
, says that if the action is free at order
q
in
\mathcal{S}_{q}
, it is also free at order
q+1
on the pre-image
(\pi^{q+1}_{q})^{-1}(\mathcal{S}_{q})
where
\pi^{q+1}_{q}:J^{q+1}(\mathcal{E})\to J^{q}(\mathcal{E})
is the canonical projection. In this section we prove that eventually free actions are quasi-horizontal and that persistence of freeness is a trivial consequence thereof. We used persistence of freeness briefly leading up to the Lie-Tresse theorem above.

As a quick warm up for the general case, consider a Lie pseudo-group acting on graphs of maps
x\mapsto u(x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The total derivative on
J^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is

D_{x}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\sum u_{k+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}},

where
u_{k}
is the
k^{\text{th}}

x
-derivative of u. The first prolongation of a map
\varphi(z)=\varphi(x,u)=(X,U)=Z
is

\widehat{U}_{X}=\frac{D_{x}U}{D_{x}X}=\frac{U_{x}+u_{x}U_{u}}{X_{x}+u_{x}X_{u}},

but notice that the nominator and denominator are the
\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
derivatives of
U
and
X
in flat coordinates for the graph of
x\mapsto u(x)
since, on the graph of
u
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+u_{x}\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u},

(cf. (
3.8
)).

It is easily seen that in general, on the graph of
x\mapsto u(x)
, we have that

\frac{d^{k}}{dy^{k}}Z=D_{x}^{k}Z.

(3.8)

Defining

D_{X}=\frac{1}{D_{x}X}D_{x},

the higher order prolongations of
\varphi
are

\widehat{U}_{k}=D_{X}U,

which are functions of the
y
-derivatives of
\varphi
only. In arbitrary many variables,
n+m
, for
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{U}
, the formulas for
\widehat{U}_{J}
, restricted to a graph of
x\mapsto u(x)
, become rather complicated but they too are functions of the expressions

\frac{\partial^{|K|}}{\partial y^{K}}Z^{a}=D^{K}Z^{a},\quad K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n},

where

\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}+u^{\alpha}_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\alpha}}.

Now assume the action of
\mathcal{H}
on
\mathcal{E}
is free at order
q
in
\mathcal{V}_{q}\subset J^{q}(\mathcal{E})
and
j^{q}u\raise-1.29167pt\hbox{\scriptsize$|$}_{x}\in\mathcal{V}_{q}
. Let
x\mapsto u(x)
be a representing map with
q
-jet
j^{q}u\raise-1.29167pt\hbox{\scriptsize$|$}_{x}
at
x
and such that the entire
q
jet
j^{q}u
lies in
\mathcal{V}_{q}
. Freeness means that it is possible to solve for every group parameter of order
\leq q
in a set of equations (restricted to
x\mapsto u(x)
) of the form

\widehat{U}^{\alpha_{i}}_{J^{i}}=c^{\alpha_{i}}_{J^{i}}=\text{constant},

(3.9)

for
1\leq i\leq\text{dim}\mathcal{H}_{q}
. We shall prove that
\mathcal{H}
is necessarily quasi-horizontal at some order
\leq q
. Writing the determining equations in flat coordinates
(y,w)
w.r.t
x\mapsto u(x)
, we write
\boldsymbol{Y}^{(q)}
for those pseudo-group jet coordinates
Z^{a}_{K}
,
|K|\leq q
, that are pure
y
-derivatives and
\boldsymbol{W}^{(q)}
for at most
q^{\text{th}}
order pseudo-group jet coordinates that are not. We consider two sets of equations; the determining equations for
\mathcal{H}
of order
\leq q
and (
3.9
), in flat coordinates for
u
. These can be written

\displaystyle F(z,Z,\boldsymbol{Y}^{(q)},\boldsymbol{W}^{(q)})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle G(z,Z,\boldsymbol{Y}^{(q)})

\displaystyle=0,

since (
3.9
) only involve
\boldsymbol{Y}^{(q)}
. The fact that this system can be uniquely solved for

\boldsymbol{Y}^{(q)}
and
\boldsymbol{W}^{(q)}
directly implies that

can be solved for
\boldsymbol{W}^{(q)}
. In particular,
each
pseudo-group jet involving a
w
-derivative can be taken as principal. Thus, eventually free Lie pseudo-group actions are quasi-horizontal and they have horizontal order equal to the order of the pseudo-group. In addition, each pseudo-group jet involving a

-derivative can be taken as principal.

.

Example 3.7
.

Consider the Lie group action

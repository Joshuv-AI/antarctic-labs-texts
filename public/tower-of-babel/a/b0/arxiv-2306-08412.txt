Given graphs

Until now, no exact formula is known for general values of

R(3,3)=6,R(3,4)=9,R(3,5)=14,R(3,6)=18,R(3,7)=23,R(3,8)=28,R(3,9)=36,R(4,4)=18

[
10
]
and references therein.

There is a lot of research on Ramsey numbers for non-complete graphs such as complete bipartite graphs, cycles, paths, stars, etc
[

]
.
For disconnected graphs, there are only few results. For example, the exact formula of

]

[

]

For

Theorem 1

[

For

The lower bound of Theorem

To address this issue, we introduce a new method for handling smaller values of

In this paper, we are only interested in the case when

The main goal of this paper is to extend the result of Theorem

Lemma 2

Let

Proof.

We first consider two cases for

Case 1:

We consider three subcases.

can be combined into two parts where each part is at least
\left\lfloor{\frac{n-1}{4}}\right\rfloor+1=\left\lceil{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rceil

\left\lceil{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rceil

Case 1.3:

into three parts, says

This is possible because

Next, we suppose that

.
We have

Then we obtain

z\geq\frac{2n-1}{3}+\frac{1}{3}\left\lfloor{\frac{n-1}{4}}\right\rfloor-\frac{2}{3}\left\lceil{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rceil\geq\left\lceil{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rceil.

Again, we get three parts where each part is at least
\left\lceil{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rceil
.

Case 2:

.

Again, we will split it into three subcases.

Case 2.1:

a_{2},a_{3}\geq\left\lceil{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rceil
.

\left\lceil{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rceil
.

Case 2.2:

a_{2}\geq\left\lceil{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rceil

a_{3}<\left\lceil{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rceil
.
We consider the value of
a_{3}+a_{4}+\cdots+a_{t}

\left\lceil{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rceil
, then combining
a_{3},a_{4},\ldots,a_{t}
into a single part.
So, we obtain three parts of size at least
\left\lceil{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rceil

Otherwise,

.
Then

.
Combining
a_{2},a_{3},\ldots,a_{t}
into a single part, we yield two parts of size at least

.

Case 2.3:

a_{2}<\left\lceil{\frac{n}{4}}\right\rceil
.
Then
n\geq 5
and
a_{2}+a_{3}+\cdots+a_{t}\geq n-2+\left\lfloor{\frac{n-1}{4}}\right\rfloor

When

and

But,

Thus,

Therefore, we can combine

Next, suppose that

From all cases, we can combine

\displaystyle=2n-|U_{4}^{\prime}|-1

\displaystyle=2(n-|U_{4}^{\prime}|)+|U_{4}^{\prime}|-1

. Therefore,

red copies of

in

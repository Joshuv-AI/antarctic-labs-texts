Counterexample to a conjecture about dihedral quandle
Saikat Panja, Sachchidanand Prasad

Abstract
. It was conjectured that the augmentation ideal of a dihedral quandle of even order n > 2 n>2 satisfies | Δ k ​ ( R n ) / Δ k + 1 ​ ( R n ) | = n |\Delta^{k}(\textup{R}_{n})/\Delta^{k+1}(\textup{R}_{n})|=n for all k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 . In this article we provide a counterexample against this conjecture.

1.
Introduction

A
quandle
is a pair
(A,\cdot)
such that ‘
\cdot
’ is a binary operation satisfying

(1)

the map
S_{a}:A\longrightarrow A
, defined as
S_{a}(b)=b\cdot a
is an automorphism for all
a\in A
,

(2)

for all
a\in A
, we have
S_{a}(a)=a
.

To have a better understanding of the structure, a theory parallel to group rings was introduced by Bardakov, Passi and Singh in
[
BPS19
]
. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
denote the cyclic group of order
n
. Then defining
a\cdot b=2b-a
defines a quandle structure on
A=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. This is known as
dihedral quandle
. For other examples see
[
BPS19
]
. The quandle ring of a quandle
A
is defined as follows. Let
R
be a commutative ring. Consider

R[A]\vcentcolon=\left\{\sum_{i}r_{i}a_{i}:r_{i}\in R,a_{i}\in A\right\}.

Then this is an additive group in usual way. Define multiplication as

\left(\sum_{i}r_{i}a_{i}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{j}s_{j}a_{j}\right)\vcentcolon=\sum_{i,j}r_{i}s_{j}(a_{i}\cdot a_{j}).

The
augmentation ideal
of
R[A]
,
\Delta_{R}(A)
is defined as the kernel of the augmentation map

\varepsilon:R[A]\to R,~\sum_{i}r_{i}a_{i}\mapsto\sum_{i}r_{i}.

The powers
\Delta^{k}_{R}(A)
is defined as
\left(\Delta_{R}(A)\right)^{k}
. When
R=\mathbb{Z}
, we will be omitting the subscript
R
. The following proposition gives a basis for
\Delta_{R}(X)
.

Proposition A
.

[
BPS19
, Proposition 3.2, Page 6]

A basis of
\Delta_{R}(X)
as an
R
-module is given by
\{a-a_{0}:a\in A\setminus\{a_{0}\}\}
, where
a_{0}\in A
is a fixed element.

The following has been conjectured in
[
BPS19
, Conjecture 6.5, Page 20]
.

Conjecture
.

Let
\textup{R}_{n}=\{a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{n-1}\}
denote the dihedral quandle of order
n
. Then we have the following statements.

(1)

For an odd integer
n>1
,
\Delta^{k}\left(\textup{R}_{n}\right)/\Delta^{k+1}\left(\textup{R}_{n}\right)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{n}
for all
k\geq 1
.

(2)

For an even integer
n>2
,
\left|\Delta^{k}\left(\textup{R}_{n}\right)/\Delta^{k+1}\left(\textup{R}_{n}\right)\right|=n
for
k\geq 2
.

The first statement has been confirmed by Elhamdadi,
Fernando and Tsvelikhovskiy in
[
EFT19
, Theorem 6.2, Page 182]
. The second statement holds true for
n=4
, see
[
BPS19
]
. Here we have given a counterexample in
2.1
to show that the conjecture is not true in general.

2.
Counterexample

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\textup{R}_{8}
be the dihedral quandle of order
8
. Then

\left|\Delta^{2}\left(\textup{R}_{8}\right)/\Delta^{3}\left(\textup{R}_{8}\right)\right|=16.

From
A
, we get that
\{e_{i}=a_{i}-a_{0}:i=1,2,\cdots,n-1\}
is a basis for
\Delta\left(\textup{R}_{n}\right)
. We will be using this notation in the subsequent computation.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\textup{R}_{2k}
denote the dihedral quandle of order
2k~(k\geq 2)
. Then
e_{i}\cdot e_{k}=0
for all
i=1,2,\cdots,2k-1
.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle e_{i}\cdot e_{k}

\displaystyle=\left(a_{i}-a_{0}\right)\cdot\left(a_{k}-a_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=a_{2k-i}-a_{2k-i}-a_{0}+a_{0}=0.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\textup{R}_{2k}
denote the dihedral quandle of order
2k~(k\geq 2)
. Then
e_{i}\cdot e_{j}=e_{i}\cdot e_{k+j}
for all
j=1,2,\cdots,k-1
and for all
i=1,2,\cdots,2k-1
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle e_{i}\cdot e_{k+j}

\displaystyle=a_{i}a_{k+j}-a_{i}a_{0}-a_{0}a_{k+j}+a_{0}

\displaystyle=a_{i}a_{j}-a_{i}a_{0}-a_{0}a_{j}+a_{0}

\displaystyle=e_{i}\cdot e_{j}.

∎

We will use
2.2
and
2.3
to simplify the multiplication tables.

Proof of
2.1
.

Recall that a basis of
\Delta\left(\textup{R}_{8}\right)
is given by
\mathcal{B}_{1}=\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{7}\}
. The multiplication table for the
e_{i}\cdot e_{j}
is given as follows:

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr&e_{1}&e_{2}&e_{3}\\
\hline\cr e_{1}&e_{1}-e_{2}-e_{7}&e_{3}-e_{4}-e_{7}&e_{5}-e_{6}-e_{7}\\
\hline\cr e_{2}&-e_{2}-e_{6}&e_{2}-e_{4}-e_{6}&-2e_{6}\\
\hline\cr e_{3}&-e_{2}-e_{5}+e_{7}&e_{1}-e_{4}-e_{5}&e_{3}-e_{5}-e_{6}\\
\hline\cr e_{4}&-e_{2}-e_{4}+e_{6}&-2e_{4}&e_{2}-e_{4}-e_{6}\\
\hline\cr e_{5}&-e_{2}-e_{3}+e_{5}&-e_{3}-e_{4}+e_{7}&e_{1}-e_{3}-e_{6}\\
\hline\cr e_{6}&-2e_{2}+e_{4}&-e_{2}-e_{4}+e_{6}&-e_{2}-e_{6}\\
\hline\cr e_{7}&-e_{1}-e_{2}+e_{3}&-e_{1}-e_{4}+e_{5}&-e_{1}-e_{6}+e_{7}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Since
\Delta^{2}\left(\textup{R}_{8}\right)
is generated by
e_{i}\cdot e_{j}
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module, using row reduction over
\mathbb{Z}
one can show that a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}=

\displaystyle\left\{u_{1}=e_{1}-e_{2}-e_{7},u_{2}=e_{2}+e_{6},u_{3}=e_{3}-e_{4}-e_{7},\right.

\displaystyle\kern 14.22636pt\left.u_{4}=e_{4}+2e_{6},u_{5}=e_{5}-e_{6}-e_{7},u_{6}=4e_{6}\right\}.

We now want to express a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\Delta^{3}\left(\textup{R}_{8}\right)
in terms of
\mathcal{B}_{2}
. First we calculate the products
u_{i}\cdot e_{j}
. This is presented in the following table.

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr&e_{1}&e_{2}&e_{3}\\
\hline\cr u_{1}&\noindent\hbox{}\hfill{{\hbox{$\begin{array}[c]{@{}c@{}}2e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{3}\\
+e_{6}-e_{7}\end{array}$}}}\hfill\hbox{}&\noindent\hbox{}\hfill{{\hbox{$\begin{array}[c]{@{}c@{}}e_{1}-e_{2}+e_{3}\\
+e_{4}-e_{5}+e_{6}-e_{7}\end{array}$}}}\hfill\hbox{}&\noindent\hbox{}\hfill{{\hbox{$\begin{array}[c]{@{}c@{}}e_{1}-e_{4}+e_{5}\\
+2e_{6}-2e_{7}\end{array}$}}}\hfill\hbox{}\\
\hline\cr u_{2}&-3e_{2}+e_{4}-e_{6}&-2e_{4}&-e_{2}+e_{4}-3e_{6}\\
\hline\cr u_{3}&\noindent\hbox{}\hfill{{\hbox{$\begin{array}[c]{@{}c@{}}e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{3}\\
+e_{4}-e_{5}-e_{6}+e_{7}\end{array}$}}}\hfill\hbox{}&2e_{1}+2e_{4}-2e_{5}&\noindent\hbox{}\hfill{{\hbox{$\begin{array}[c]{@{}c@{}}e_{1}-e_{2}+e_{3}+e_{4}\\
-e_{5}+e_{6}-e_{7}\end{array}$}}}\hfill\hbox{}\\
\hline\cr u_{4}&-5e_{2}-e_{4}+e_{6}&-2e_{2}-4e_{4}+2e_{6}&-e_{2}-e_{4}-3e_{6}\\
\hline\cr u_{5}&\noindent\hbox{}\hfill{{\hbox{$\begin{array}[c]{@{}c@{}}e_{1}+2e_{2}-2e_{3}\\
-e_{4}+e_{5}\end{array}$}}}\hfill\hbox{}&\noindent\hbox{}\hfill{{\hbox{$\begin{array}[c]{@{}c@{}}e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{3}+e_{4}\\
-e_{5}-e_{6}+e_{7}\end{array}$}}}\hfill\hbox{}&2e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{3}+e_{6}-e_{7}\\
\hline\cr u_{6}&-8e_{2}+4e_{4}&-4e_{2}-4e_{4}+4e_{6}&-4e_{2}-4e_{6}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Hence, a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
\Delta^{3}\left(\textup{R}_{8}\right)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{3}

\displaystyle=\left\{v_{1}=e_{1}-e_{2}+e_{3}+e_{4}-e_{5}+e_{6}-e_{7},v_{2}=e_{2}-e_{3}-2e_{4}+2e_{5}+e_{6}-e_{7},\right.

\displaystyle\kern 14.22636pt\left.v_{3}=-e_{3}-e_{4}+2e_{5}-2e_{6}-e_{7},v_{4}=-2e_{4},v_{5}=-4e_{5}-4e_{6}+4e_{7},v_{6}=8e_{6}\right\}.

Now we will present the elements of
\mathcal{B}_{3}
in terms of
\mathcal{B}_{2}
. We have the following presentation.

\begin{array}[]{c c c c c c c c}v_{1}&=&u_{1}&&&+2u_{4}&-u_{5}&-u_{6}\\
v_{2}&=&&u_{2}&-u_{3}&-u_{4}&+2u_{5}&+u_{6}\\
v_{3}&=&&&-u_{3}&-2u_{4}&+2u_{5}&+u_{6}\\
v_{4}&=&&&&2u_{4}&&-u_{6}\\
v_{5}&=&&&&&-4u_{5}\\
v_{6}&=&&&&&&2u_{6}.\end{array}

Note that we can alter the basis
\mathcal{B}_{2}
of
\Delta^{2}\left(\textup{R}_{8}\right)
as follows:

\displaystyle\left\{u_{1}+2u_{4}-u_{5}-u_{6},u_{2}-u_{3}-u_{4}+2u_{5}+u_{6},u_{3}+2u_{4}-2u_{5}-u_{6},u_{4},u_{5},u_{6}\right\}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\dfrac{\Delta^{2}\left(\textup{R}_{8}\right)}{\Delta^{3}\left(\textup{R}_{8}\right)}

\displaystyle\cong\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}v_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}v_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}v_{3}\oplus\mathbb{Z}u_{4}\oplus\mathbb{Z}u_{5}\oplus\mathbb{Z}u_{6}}{\mathbb{Z}v_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}v_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}v_{3}\oplus\mathbb{Z}(2u_{4}-u_{6})\oplus\mathbb{Z}(-4u_{5})\oplus\mathbb{Z}(2u_{6})}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{4}\oplus\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}u_{4}\oplus\mathbb{Z}u_{6}}{\mathbb{Z}(2u_{4}-u_{6})\oplus\mathbb{Z}(2u_{6})}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{4}\oplus\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}u_{4}\oplus\mathbb{Z}u_{6}}{\mathbb{Z}u_{4}\oplus\mathbb{Z}(4u_{6})}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{4}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{4}.

∎

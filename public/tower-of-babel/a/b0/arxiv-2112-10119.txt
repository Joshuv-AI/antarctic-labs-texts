Explicit multivariate approximations from cell-average data
Sergio Amat, David Levin, Juan Ruiz, Dionisio F. Yáñez

Abstract
Given gridded cell-average data of a smooth multivariate function, we present a constructive explicit procedure for generating a high-order global approximation of the function. One contribution is the derivation of high order approximations to point-values of the function directly from the cell-average data. The second contribution is the development of univariate B-spline based high order quasi-interpolation operators using cell-average data. Multivariate spline quasi-interpolation approximation operators are obtained by tensor products of the univariate operators.



1
Introduction

In some applications, e.g., in some numerical methods for solving flow problems, and in medical imaging, our raw data is in the form of cell-averages on a grid of cells. The underlying function is usually piecewise smooth, and the ultimate challenge is to find a high order piecewise smooth approximation to the function, including a high order approximation of the boundaries between the smooth pieces. This is the subject of several papers
[
3
,
9
]
, including a recent paper by the authors
[
1
]
. A basic ingredient of these works is the simpler problem of approximating a smooth function from cell-average data,
and this is the subject of the present contribution.

Given the cell averages of a smooth function, we derive high order approximations of the values of the function at the middle point of each cell using the cell-average plus a linear combination of local even order central differences of the cell-average data. We start with the univariate case. Let
f
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2m+2}
function, and consider the cell-averages of
f
on intervals of length
h
:

\bar{f}(a)=\frac{1}{h}\int_{a-h/2}^{a+h/2}f(x)dx.

(1)

Under these premises, we show that

f(a)=\bar{f}(a)+\sum_{r=1}^{m}a_{r}\Delta^{2r}\bar{f}(a)+O(h^{2m+2}),

(2)

where
\Delta^{2}g(a)=g(a-h)-2g(a)+g(a+h)
, and we develop a recursive algorithm for computing the coefficients
\{a_{r}\}
.
Next, we extend the above 1D formula to 2D and, by induction, to
k
D. We also show that the
k
D formula is just a tensor product of the 1D formula.

One way of computing a high order approximation of
f
using its cell-averages is by finding a local polynomial whose cell-averages agree with the given cell-average data. This approach is used e.g. in
[
2
]
. Here we aim at getting a global approximation of
f
, and for this purpose we suggest using the quasi-interpolation strategy.

The term quasi-interpolation approximation describes a local high order approximation which is of the same approximation order as an interpolation operator using the same approximation function space. Spline quasi-interpolation operators are well studied, and explicit schemes are developed in
[
8
]
. The
p
-order quasi-interpolation operators in
[
8
]
compute the spline approximation of
f(x)
using a small number of equidistant function values near
x
, such that the approximation is exact if
f\in\Pi^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, the space of polynomials of degree
\leq p
. In the present paper we derive quasi-interpolation operators which use cell-average data. The new quasi-interpolation schemes are local and reproduce
\Pi^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, and we show that they achieve the full approximation order, i.e.,
O(h^{p+1})
.

The derivation for the univariate case is based upon Speleers formulae
[
8
]
together with some basic properties of B-splines. The application to higher dimensions can be easily done by applying the tensor product of the univariate operators.

In practice, the cell-average data is given on a bounded rectangular domain. Thus, the algorithms for approximating the point-values of
f
and for computing the spline approximation need to be changed near the boundaries of the domain. We suggest here a way of treating the approximation near the boundaries, maintaining the same asymptotic approximation error rates, only with larger multiplying constants.
This paper is divided in three sections: Firstly, we present a direct procedure in order to obtain point-wise values of the function from the cell averages, we show the error formula and extend the results to
k
D dimensions. In the second section, quasi-interpolation using B-splines is explained and a new general method to obtain a global approximation to the function is implemented. Also, some specific examples are presented and the leading error coefficient is derived. Finally, some conclusions and future work are commented in section 4.

2
From cell-average to point value from a Lagrange interpolation point of view

Typically, in order to recover the original value of a function in a determined point from cell-average data we solve a system, i.e. if we consider the data
\{\bar{f}(a+jh)\}_{j=-m}^{m}
and we want to approximate
f(a)
, we pose the system with unknowns
c_{j},\,j=0,\ldots,2m+1
:

\int_{a+jh-\frac{h}{2}}^{a+jh-\frac{h}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{2m+1}c_{i}x^{i}\right)dx=\bar{f}(a+jh),\quad j=-m,\ldots,m,

(3)

define the polynomial
g\in\Pi^{2m+1}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
as

g(x):=\sum_{i=0}^{2m+1}c_{i}x^{i},

and calculate an approximation of
f(a)
evaluating
g
at
a
. It is well-known that, if
f\in\mathcal{C}^{2m+2}
, with this procedure we obtain an approximation of order
O(h^{2m+2})
. In this section, we develop a direct method to obtain a general centered expression for a determined order of accuracy which relates the values of a function at a point with the cell-average around this point, Equation (
1
).
The idea is to use the Taylor expansion of
f
in Equation (
1
), replacing the derivatives of
f
by its appropriate finite differences, and then expressing the finite differences of
f
by finite differences of
\bar{f}
. This leads to a triangular system for the coefficients in (2), which allows to design a recursive algorithm to get the solution.
This section is divided into two parts, we develop the method in the 1D case and provide an error formula, then we extend the result to the 2D case via the tensor-product strategy and finally, via an induction process, to
k
D.

2.1
A procedure to obtain a centered expression for any order of accuracy

Along all this section, we will suppose a point
a\in\mathbb{R}
, assume
f\in\mathcal{C}^{2m+2}(\Omega)
,
\Omega=[x_{1},x_{2}]
,
h>0
, and

[a-mh,a+mh]\subset\Omega
. We denote,

\Delta^{2m}\bar{f}(a)=\sum_{j=-m}^{m}(-1)^{m+j}\binom{2m}{j+m}\bar{f}(a+jh).

(4)

To ease the notation, we express the calculations at the point
a=0
. Using Taylor expansions on Equation (
1
), there exists
\xi_{1}\in\Omega
such that

\bar{f}(0)=\sum_{t=0}^{m}\frac{1}{(2t+1)!2^{2t}}h^{2t}f^{(2t)}(0)+\frac{1}{(2m+3)!2^{2m+2}}h^{2m+2}f^{(2m+2)}(\xi_{1}).

(5)

We denote,

b_{m+1}=-\frac{1}{(2m+3)!2^{2m+2}}.

(6)

Let
g\in\mathcal{C}^{2m+2}(\Omega)
be a function with
[-rh,rh]\subset\Omega
and
r\leq m
, using Taylor expansions again we get:

g((j-r)h)+g(-(j-r)h)=2g(0)+2\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{(h(j-r))^{2k}}{(2k)!}g^{(2k)}(0)+O(h^{2m+2}),

(7)

and

\Delta^{2r}g(0)=\binom{2r}{r}(-1)^{r}g(0)+\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}\left(g((j-r)h)+g(-(j-r)h)\right).

(8)

In order to obtain an easier expression for
\Delta^{2r}g(0)
we prove the following well-known auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 2.1

Let
r
be a natural number with
r\geq 1
then

\binom{2r}{r}(-1)^{r}+2\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}=0.

Proof.
From

\binom{2r}{j}=\binom{2r}{2r-j},\quad 0\leq j\leq 2r,

and the Newton binomial expansion:

w(x):=(x-1)^{2r}=\sum_{j=0}^{2r}\binom{2r}{j}x^{2r-j}(-1)^{j},

we have that

0=w(1)=\sum_{j=0}^{2r}\binom{2r}{j}(-1)^{j}=\binom{2r}{r}(-1)^{r}+2\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}.

\blacksquare

Thus, using (
7
), (
8
) and Lemma
2.1
we get:

\begin{split}\Delta^{2r}g(0)=&\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}\left(2\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{(h(j-r))^{2k}}{(2k)!}g^{(2k)}(0)+O(h^{2m+2})\right)\\
=&2\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}(j-r)^{2k}\right)\frac{h^{2k}}{(2k)!}g^{(2k)}(0)+O(h^{2m+2}),\end{split}

(9)

and prove the following direct consequence, Corollary
2.2
.

Corollary 2.2

Let
r,k
be natural numbers with
1\leq k\leq r
then

\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}(j-r)^{2k}=\begin{cases}0,&1\leq k<r,\\[2.0pt]
\frac{(2r)!}{2}&k=r.\end{cases}

Proof.
Let
r\geq 1
be a natural number. We take a function,
g\in\mathcal{C}^{2r+1}(\mathbb{R})
such that
g^{(2k)}(0)\neq 0,\,\,1\leq k\leq r
and
g^{(2r+1)}(0)=0
(for example
g(x)=(x+1)^{2r}
), then we define the polynomial of degree
2r
:

\Delta^{2r}g(0)=h^{2r}g^{(2r)}(0)=:p(h),

and by Equation (
9
) we obtain the following polynomial:

\Delta^{2r}g(0)=2\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}(j-r)^{2k}\right)\frac{h^{2k}}{(2k)!}g^{(2k)}(0)=:q(h),

from
q(h)=p(h)
we get the result.

\blacksquare

Using Corollary
2.2
, we rewrite Equation (
9
) as:

\Delta^{2r}g(0)=2\sum_{k=r}^{m}\left(\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}(j-r)^{2k}\right)\frac{h^{2k}}{(2k)!}g^{(2k)}(0)+O(h^{2m+2}).

(10)

Finally, if we substitute
g=f^{(2t)}
, with
t\leq m
in Equation (
10
) then for each
t

\Delta^{2r}f^{(2t)}(0)=2\sum_{k=r}^{m-t}\left(\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}(j-r)^{2k}\right)\frac{h^{2k}}{(2k)!}f^{(2(k+t))}(0)+O(h^{2m+2}).

(11)

Applying
\Delta^{2r}
to Equation (
5
),

\begin{split}\Delta^{2r}\bar{f}(0)=&\sum_{t=0}^{m}\frac{1}{(2t+1)!2^{2t}}h^{2t}f^{(2t)}(0)+b_{m+1}h^{2m+2}f^{(2m+2)}(\xi_{1}),\\
=&\sum_{t=0}^{m}\frac{h^{2t}}{(2t+1)!2^{2t}}\left(2\sum_{k=r}^{m-t}\left(\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}(j-r)^{2k}\right)\frac{h^{2k}}{(2k)!}f^{(2(k+t))}(0)+O(h^{2m+2})\right)\\
&-b_{m+1}h^{2m+2}f^{(2m+2)}(\xi_{1})\\
=&\sum_{t=0}^{m}\left(\sum_{k=r}^{m-t}\left(\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}(j-r)^{2k}\right)\frac{h^{2(k+t)}}{(2t+1)!(2k)!2^{2t-1}}f^{(2(k+t))}(0)\right)+O(h^{2m+2}).\\
\end{split}

Defining
i=k+t
for
r\leq k\leq m-t
and
0\leq t
, we get

\begin{split}\Delta^{2r}\bar{f}(0)=&\sum_{t=0}^{m}\left(\sum_{k=r}^{m-t}\left(\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}(j-r)^{2k}\right)\frac{h^{2(k+t)}}{(2t+1)!(2k)!2^{2t-1}}f^{(2(k+t))}(0)\right)+O(h^{2m+2})\\
=&\sum_{i=r}^{m}\left(\sum_{t=0}^{i-r}\left(\frac{1}{2^{2t-1}(2t+1)!(2(i-t))!}\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}(j-r)^{2(i-t)}\right)h^{2i}f^{(2i)}(0)\right)+O(h^{2m+2}).\\
\end{split}

(12)

We introduce the notation

\Delta^{2r}_{m}\bar{f}(0)=\sum_{i=r}^{m}\left(\sum_{t=0}^{i-r}\left(\frac{1}{2^{2t-1}(2t+1)!(2(i-t))!}\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}(j-r)^{2(i-t)}\right)h^{2i}f^{(2i)}(0)\right).

(13)

From (
5
), we have that

\bar{f}(0)=f(0)+\sum_{t=1}^{m}\frac{h^{2t}}{(2t+1)!2^{2t}}f^{(2t)}(0)+O(h^{2m+2}).

Now we look for coefficients
\mathbf{a}_{m}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})
such that

\sum_{t=1}^{m}\frac{h^{2t}}{(2t+1)!2^{2t}}f^{(2t)}(0)+\sum_{r=1}^{m}a_{r}\Delta^{2r}_{m}\bar{f}(0)=0.

(14)

In view of the expression (
13
) we obtain:

\mathbf{M}_{m}\mathbf{a}_{m}^{T}=\mathbf{b}_{m}^{T},

(15)

where
\mathbf{b}_{m}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
are the coefficients of the derivatives which appear in the expression of
\bar{f}(0)
, Equation (
6
), i.e.

b_{i}=-\frac{1}{(2i+1)!2^{2i}},\quad 1\leq i\leq m,

(16)

and
\mathbf{M}_{m}=(m_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{m}
are the coefficients of the derivatives of
\Delta^{2r}_{m}
, Equation (
13
), with

m_{i,j}=\sum_{t=0}^{i-j}\frac{1}{2^{2t-1}(2t+1)!(2(i-t))!}\sum_{s=j+1}^{2j}(-1)^{s}\binom{2j}{s}(s-j)^{2(i-t)}.

(17)

The following result is a direct consequence by Corollary
2.2
. This result confirms that there exists a unique solution to our problem. Combining (
5
) and (
14
), it follows that we can approximate the value of the function with a certain order of accuracy using a combination of cell-average data.

Proposition 2.3

Let
m
be a natural number and
\mathbf{M}_{m}=(m_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{m}
the matrix defined in Equation (
17
)
then

m_{i,j}=\begin{cases}0,&i<j,\\
1,&i=j.\end{cases}

Therefore, the system defined in Equation (
15
) has a unique solution.

In order to clarify all the concepts, we introduce an example.

Example 2.1

For example, for
m=5
, we get:

\bordermatrix{~&{\Delta_{5}^{2}\bar{f}(a)}&{\Delta_{5}^{4}\bar{f}(a)}&{\Delta_{5}^{6}\bar{f}(a)}&{\Delta_{5}^{8}\bar{f}(a)}&{\Delta^{10}_{5}\bar{f}(a)}\cr h^{2}f^{(2)}(a)&1&0&0&0&0\cr h^{4}f^{(4)}(a)&\frac{1}{8}&1&0&0&0\cr h^{6}f^{(6)}(a)&\frac{13}{1920}&\frac{5}{24}&1&0&0\cr h^{8}f^{(8)}(a)&\frac{41}{193536}&\frac{23}{1152}&\frac{7}{24}&1&0\cr h^{10}f^{(10)}(a)&\frac{671}{154828800}&\frac{227}{193536}&\frac{77}{1920}&\frac{3}{8}&1\cr}=\mathbf{M}_{5},

(18)

\bordermatrix{~&{f(a)-\bar{f}(a)}\cr h^{2}f^{(2)}(a)&-(24)^{-1}\cr h^{4}f^{(4)}(a)&-(1920)^{-1}\cr h^{6}f^{(6)}(a)&-(322560)^{-1}\cr h^{8}f^{(8)}(a)&-(92897280)^{-1}\cr h^{10}f^{(10)}(a)&-(40874803200)^{-1}}=\mathbf{b}_{5}^{T}.

(19)

It is clear that there exists a unique solution and it is:

\mathbf{a}_{5}=\left(-\frac{1}{24},\frac{3}{640},-\frac{5}{7168},\frac{35}{294912},-\frac{63}{2883584}\right).

Then,

\bar{f}(a)-\frac{1}{24}\Delta^{2}\bar{f}(a)+\frac{3}{640}\Delta^{4}\bar{f}(a)-\frac{5}{7168}\Delta^{6}\bar{f}(a)+\frac{35}{294912}\Delta^{8}\bar{f}(a)-\frac{63}{2883584}\Delta^{10}\bar{f}(a)=f(a)+O(h^{12}).

If we know the coefficients
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m-1}
we can write that

a_{m}=b_{m}-\sum_{r=1}^{m-1}m_{m,r}a_{r},

then we can design a recursive algorithm to solve the problem.
Collecting all the above results, we have proved that if
f\in\mathcal{C}^{2m+2}(\Omega)
then

f(a)=\sum_{r=0}^{m}a_{r}\Delta^{2r}\bar{f}(a)+O(h^{2m+2}),

(20)

being
a_{0}=1
and
\mathbf{a}_{m}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})
the solution of the system in (
15
). We present an error formula for this approximation in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4
(Error formula)

Let
\Omega
be a closed interval,
1\leq m\in\mathbb{N}
;
a,h\in\mathbb{R}
,
h>0
with
[a-mh,a+mh]\subset\Omega
,
f\in\mathcal{C}^{2m+2}(\Omega)
and
||f^{(2m+2)}||_{\infty,\Omega}=\max\{|f^{(2m+2)}(x)|:x\in\Omega\}
, then

\left|f(a)-\sum_{r=0}^{m}a_{r}\Delta^{2r}\bar{f}(a)\right|\leq|a_{m+1}|||f^{(2m+2)}||_{\infty,\Omega}h^{2m+2},

(21)

being
a_{0}=1
and
\mathbf{a}_{m}=(a_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
the solution of the system (
15
), and

a_{m+1}=b_{m+1}-\sum_{r=1}^{m}m_{m+1,r}a_{r},

with
b_{m+1}
and
m_{m+1,j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
defined in Equations (
16
) and (
17
) respectively.

Proof.
For simplicity, we develop the proof for
a=0
. Using Taylor’s expansions, we have that there exists
\xi_{1}\in[-\frac{h}{2},\frac{h}{2}]
such that

\begin{split}\bar{f}(0)&=f(0)+\sum_{t=1}^{m}\frac{h^{2t}}{(2t+1)!2^{2t}}f^{(2t)}(0)+\frac{1}{(2m+3)!2^{2m+2}}f^{(2m+2)}(\xi_{1})h^{2m+2}\\
&=f(0)+\sum_{t=1}^{m}\frac{h^{2t}}{(2t+1)!2^{2t}}f^{(2t)}(0)-b_{m+1}f^{(2m+2)}(\xi_{1})h^{2m+2}\\
&\leq f(0)+\sum_{t=1}^{m}\frac{h^{2t}}{(2t+1)!2^{2t}}f^{(2t)}(0)-b_{m+1}||f^{(2m+2)}||_{\infty,\Omega}h^{2m+2}\end{split}

(22)

with
b_{m+1}
defined in Equation (
16
). Again, using Taylor’s expansions, there exists
\xi_{2}\in[-mh,mh]
such that

\begin{split}\Delta^{2r}\bar{f}(0)=&\sum_{i=r}^{m}\left(\sum_{t=0}^{i-1}\left(\frac{1}{2^{2t-1}(2t+1)!(2(i-t))!}\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}(j-r)^{2(i-t)}\right)h^{2i}f^{(2i)}(0)\right)+\\
&+\sum_{t=0}^{m}\frac{1}{2^{2t-1}(2t+1)!(2(m+1-t))!}\sum_{j=r+1}^{2r}(-1)^{j}\binom{2r}{j}(j-r)^{2(m+1-t)}h^{2m+2}f^{(2m+2)}(\xi_{2})\\
=&\Delta_{m}^{2r}\bar{f}(0)+m_{m+1,r}f^{(2m+2)}(\xi_{2})h^{2m+2}\\
\leq&\Delta_{m}^{2r}\bar{f}(0)+m_{m+1,r}||f^{(2m+2)}||_{\infty,\Omega}h^{2m+2}\\
\end{split}

(23)

with
m_{m+1,r}
,
r=1,\ldots,m
defined in Equation (
17
). Finally, by the construction of the system (
15
) we know:

\sum_{t=1}^{m}\frac{h^{2t}}{(2t+1)!2^{2t}}f^{(2t)}(0)+\sum_{r=1}^{m}a_{r}\Delta^{2r}_{m}\bar{f}(0)=0.

(24)

Therefore, collecting Equations (
22
), (
23
) and (
24
):

\begin{split}\left|\sum_{r=0}^{m}a_{r}\Delta^{2r}\bar{f}(0)-f(0)\right|&\leq\left|\bar{f}(0)-f(0)-\sum_{t=1}^{m}\frac{h^{2t}}{(2t+1)!2^{2t}}f^{(2t)}(0)+\sum_{r=1}^{m}a_{r}m_{m+1,r}||f^{(2m+2)}||_{\infty,\Omega}h^{2m+2}\right|\\
&\leq\left|-b_{m+1}||f^{(2m+2)}||_{\infty,\Omega}h^{2m+2}+\sum_{r=1}^{m}a_{r}m_{m+1,r}||f^{(2m+2)}||_{\infty,\Omega}h^{2m+2}\right|\\
&=\left|-b_{m+1}+\sum_{r=1}^{m}a_{r}m_{m+1,r}\right|||f^{(2m+2)}||_{\infty,\Omega}h^{2m+2}\\
&=|a_{m+1}|||f^{(2m+2)}||_{\infty,\Omega}h^{2m+2}.\\
\end{split}

\blacksquare

Corollary 2.5

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
P\in\Pi^{2m+1}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
, then

\sum_{r=0}^{m}a_{r}\Delta^{2r}\bar{P}(a)=P(a),\,\,\,\forall\,a\in\mathbb{R},

where
a_{0}=1
and
\mathbf{a}_{m}=(a_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
is the solution of the system in (
15
).

Proof.
Let
m
be a natural number and
P(x)\in\Pi^{2m+1}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then the result is a direct consequence of Proposition
2.4
, since

P^{(2m+2)}(x)=0
for all
x\,\in\mathbb{R}
.

\blacksquare

Finally, we show that this new recursive method is equivalent to solving the system introduced in Equation (
3
). Note that with our algorithm it is not necessary to compute any coefficient of the polynomials. As a consequence, we can obtain a high order approximation with a low computational cost.

Proposition 2.6

Let
\Omega
be a closed interval,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
f\in\mathcal{C}^{2m+2}(\Omega)
, we consider
g\in\Pi^{2m+1}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\bar{g}(a+jh)=\bar{f}(a+jh),\quad j=-m,\ldots,m,

(25)

then:

\sum_{r=0}^{m}a_{r}\Delta^{2r}\bar{f}(a)=g(a),

(26)

where
a_{0}=1
and
\mathbf{a}_{m}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})
is the solution of the system (
15
).

Proof.
By hypothesis, (
25
) and by Equation (
4
), if
r\leq m
we have

\Delta^{2r}\bar{f}(a)=\sum_{j=-r}^{r}(-1)^{r+j}\binom{2r}{j+r}\bar{f}(a+jh)=\sum_{j=-r}^{r}(-1)^{r+j}\binom{2r}{j+r}\bar{g}(a+jh),

then by Corollary
2.5
, we get

\sum_{r=0}^{m}a_{r}\Delta^{2r}\bar{f}(a)=\sum_{r=0}^{m}a_{r}\Delta^{2r}\bar{g}(a)=g(a).

\blacksquare

As a conclusion, we have developed a direct method to obtain an approximation to the values of the function
f
from cell-average data. We use a tensor-product strategy to extend the result to 2D; see
A
for more information. With this extension, it is easy to generalise to
k
dimensions.

2.2
From cell-average to point values: The
k
D case

The generalization to
k
D case is easy using an induction process. Therefore, let
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
be an arbitrary point,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be an open set, with

\tilde{\Omega}:=[s_{1}-m^{x_{1}}h_{x_{1}},s_{1}+m^{x_{1}}h_{x_{1}}]\times\ldots\times[s_{k}-m^{x_{k}}h_{x_{k}},s_{k}+m^{x_{k}}h_{x_{k}}]\subset\Omega
and
f\in\mathcal{C}^{p}(\Omega)
,
p\geq 2m^{x_{j}}+2
,
j=1,\ldots,k
. We define:

\bar{f}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k})=\int_{\tilde{\Omega}}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})dx_{1}\ldots dx_{k},

then using the same process as in

A
we have:

\begin{split}f(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k})&=\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{m^{x_{1}}}\sum_{i_{2}=0}^{m^{x_{2}}}\ldots\sum_{i_{k}=0}^{m^{x_{k}}}a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\ldots a_{i_{n}}\Delta^{2i_{1},x_{1}}\Delta^{2i_{2},x_{2}}\ldots\Delta^{2i_{k},x_{k}}\bar{f}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}O(h_{x_{j}}^{2m^{x_{j}}_{0}+2}).\end{split}

(27)

3.1

Given the values of

In this section, we prove a relation between the point-value and cell-average operators depending on the degree of the B-spline used, and we generalise this relation. This section is divided in two parts: In the first we introduce the problem and prove some auxiliary lemmas. In the second part we show the main theorem and introduce some examples.

4
Conclusions

In this work, two main results are presented: We give an explicit method to obtain the values of a function from cell-average data without obtaining the coefficients of the polynomials, we prove the error bound and extend the result to several dimensions. In the second part of the paper, we introduce B-splines theory and a generalisation of the cell-average discretisation using
q
-average based on the repeat convolution of the characteristic function in the interval
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
. We prove a relation between all the
q
-discretisations, offering a simple algorithm to switch between them. As a particular case, we give a formula to obtain an approximation of a function from cell-average data. In all these cases, proofs for the accuracy order are given and some examples are performed. Finally, we extend these results to any dimension using a tensor product.

Rank gain of Jacobians over number field extensions with prescribed Galois groups Thanks: Bo-Hae Im was supported by the National Research Foundation of Korea(NRF) grant funded by the Korea government(MSIT) (No. 2020R1A2B5B01001835).
Bo-Hae Im, Joachim König

Abstract
. We investigate the rank gain of elliptic curves, and more generally, Jacobian varieties, over non-Galois extensions whose Galois closure has Galois group permutation-isomorphic to a prescribed group G G (in short, “ G G -extensions”). In particular, for alternating groups and (an infinite family of) projective linear groups G G , we show that most elliptic curves over (e.g.) ℚ \mathbb{Q} gain rank over infinitely many G G -extensions, conditional only on the parity conjecture. More generally, we provide a theoretical criterion which allows to deduce that “many” elliptic curves gain rank over infinitely many G G -extensions, conditional on the parity conjecture and on the existence of geometric Galois realizations with group G G and certain local properties.

1.
Introduction

The rank of elliptic curves over number fields has been one of the main subjects in the areas of algebraic number theory and arithmetic geometry. In particular, questions about the rank of quadratic twists of a given elliptic curve
E
over a number field
K
have been studied extensively, and are directly related to the question over which quadratic extensions
K(\sqrt{a})/K
the rank of
E
grows, upon recalling that for an elliptic curve
E/K
over a number field
K
and its quadratic twist
E^{a}
by a square-free integer
a
, the rank of
E
over the quadratic extension
K(\sqrt{a})
is equal to the sum of the ranks of
E
and
E^{a}
over
K
, which implies that the positive rank of the twist over
K
gives the rank growth of
E
over the quadratic extension of
K
. For example, in
[
30
]
, a lower bound of density of twists of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
with positive rank has been given, and in
[
6
,
16
,
14
]
, the simultaneous rank growth of more than one elliptic curve over given quadratic extensions has been studied. Some conditional results on the positive rank of twists of elliptic curves, relative to the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture and the finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich group, have been given in
[
24
,
11
]
.

In this paper, we more generally investigate the rank growth of elliptic curves over extensions of the base field whose Galois closure has a prescribed Galois group.

Given a transitive permutation group
G\leq S_{n}
, by a
G
-extension

we mean an (in general, non-Galois) degree-
n
extension over
K
whose Galois closure has Galois group permutation-isomorphic to

.

The following are variants (of increasing strength) of the same question of interest:

Question 1.1
.

Let

be a number field and

a transitive permutation group.

Do there exist infinitely many elliptic curves

(

, such that each

Can the infinite family

in a) even be assumed to contain “most” elliptic curves over

(with a suitable way of counting)?

Can it even be assumed to contain all elliptic curves over

In the key case

, the strongest version Question

(e.g., see
[
21
]
).

Examples where Question

, (for example, see
[
17
,
7
]
) or

(the normalizer of an order

On the other hand for

Note that if a

). We are therefore primarily interested in the case where such intermediate extensions do not exist. Via the Galois correspondence, this is the case if and only if the point stabilizer

is a maximal subgroup inside

is in fact a

to exclude certain trivial answers; it is, however, furthermore useful for theoretical purposes, since it guarantees that the rank of the respective elliptic curve becomes infinite over the compositum of these extensions. See, e.g.,
[

2.
Main results

We attack Question
1.1
(in fact, for very broad classes of elliptic curves), using the root number
W(E/K)
of an elliptic curve defined over
K
. See Section
3.2
for more on root numbers; for now it suffices to note that
W(E/K)
equals
-1
or
1
, and conditional on the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, equals the parity
(-1)^{rank(E(K))}
of the rank of
E
over
K
.

We fix a finite transitive permutation group
G\leq S_{n}
, a number field
K
, an elliptic curve
E
over
K
, and ask the following question: As
F/K
runs through all (degree-
n
)
G
-extensions of
K
, does the sign

differ from

infinitely often? If so, that means in particular that the analytic rank of
E
over
F
is higher than the one over
K
for infinitely many

, and assuming the parity conjecture, the rank of
E
over
F
is larger than the one over
K
. Our goal is to obtain a positive answer for as broad a class of elliptic curves as possible, for prescribed groups
G
.

We focus in particular on non-solvable groups. One of the most successful approaches to realizing such groups as Galois groups over number fields
K
is via
K
-regular realizations over the function field
K(t)
(and subsequent application of Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem), via, e.g., the so-called rigidity method (here, a field extension
L/K(t)
is called
K
-regular, if
K
is algebraically closed in
L
; cf. Section
3.1
).
We provide the following general criterion, which enables us to give positive answers to Question
1.1
for groups
G
conditional on
K
-regular realizations over
K(t)
, possibly with certain local conditions.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
K
be a number field, and let
G\leq S_{n}
be a transitive permutation group occurring as the Galois group of a
K
-regular Galois extension
L/K(t)
.
Assume that one of the following holds:

i)

G
is not contained in
A_{n}
, or

ii)

there exists a
K
-rational branch point
t_{i}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
of
L/K(t)
with the following property: if
I\trianglelefteq D
denote the inertia and decomposition group at
t\mapsto t_{i}
in
L/K(t)
, there exists
x\in D
such that
n-|orb(\langle I,x\rangle)|
is odd, where

is the set of orbits of a subgroup

.

Then under the parity conjecture, there exists a positive density set
\mathcal{S}
of primes of
K
such that, for each

, each elliptic curve over
K
with multiplicative reduction at

gains rank over infinitely many pairwise linearly disjoint

-extensions of

Theorem

means in particular that groups not contained in

are typically easier to deal with; see also Theorem

Regarding the somewhat technical Case ii), we emphasize that it may often be verified purely theoretically, without relying on an explicit polynomial for the Galois extension

. See Section

As a broader application of Theorem

, we show the following result, providing rank gain over extensions of degree

with Galois group (of the Galois closure) equal to

in its natural action.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
K
be a number field not containing
\sqrt{-1}
, let
p
be a prime number congruent to
3
mod
8
, and let
G=PSL_{2}(p)\leq S_{p+1}
.
Then under the parity conjecture, for all but finitely many primes
q
of
K
inert in
K(\sqrt{-1})/K
(with the exceptional set depending on

), the following holds:

Every elliptic curve over
K
with multiplicative reduction at
q
gains rank over infinitely many (pairwise linearly disjoint) degree-
p+1

G
-extensions of
K
.

For the case

, we reach an even stronger conclusion.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let

be a number field, and let

be an elliptic curve over

having at least one prime of multiplicative reduction. Then under the parity conjecture, for each

, there exist infinitely many pairwise linearly disjoint degree-

with Galois group

such that

The proofs of the above results are contained in Section

as a Galois group with certain local conditions. One way to guarantee the latter is to use previous results about the local behavior of specializations of Galois extensions of function fields such as
[

, which is a main ingredient for the proof of Theorem

In fact, the proofs yield an asymptotic result. In all cases above, there exists a constant

(depending on

and

, but
not
on the concrete elliptic curve

!) satisfying the following:
the number of
G
-extensions

with relative discriminant of norm
\leq B
such that

is asymptotically at least

, cf. Remark

For some small alternating and linear groups, we also give explicit sample polynomials over whose root fields the rank of a prescribed elliptic curve will grow. In Section
4.6
, we briefly discuss the frequency of the elliptic curves satisfying the conditions of the above theorems; it turns out that most elliptic curves over

in certain scenarios; notably, via the construction of covers

with Galois group

. These have been used for special cases in, e.g.,
[

]

[
21
]
. However, this approach is only available for rather restricted classes of groups (and then in general suffices only to answer the “weak” Question
1.1

, there are known examples of elliptic curves over number fields with the same root number over all quadratic extensions
[

]

In Section

to give a generalization from elliptic curves to Jacobians of curves of arbitrary genus (although with certain technical assumptions). This allows us to generalize results such as Theorem

Let

1

Then for every integer

See also Theorem

3.
Prerequisites

3.1.
Completion and specialization of
G
-extensions

Let
F
be a field and
L/F
a separable (but not necessarily Galois) extension of degree
n
with Galois closure
\Omega/F
. We call
L/F
a
G
-extension if
Gal(\Omega/F)
is a permutation group isomorphic to
G
via the usual action on conjugates of
L
.

Now let
R
be a Dedekind domain with field of fractions
F
. Let
p
be a prime ideal of
R
with perfect residue field, which we denote by
F_{p}
.
Denote by
L_{p}
the direct product of residue fields
L_{\mathfrak{p}}
where
\mathfrak{p}
runs through the prime ideals extending
p
in the integral closure of
R
in
L
. This is an étale algebra (in the case where
L/F
is Galois, it is a direct product of isomorphic Galois extensions, and for most purposes, we may simply work with one of those extensions and refer to this as
L_{p}
). In the same way, the completion
\widehat{L_{p}}
at
p
may be defined as the direct product of completions
\widehat{L_{\mathfrak{p}}}
at places
\mathfrak{p}
extending
p
. By the decomposition group of (the Galois closure)
L/F
at
p
, we mean the decomposition group at any prime extending
p
, which yields a subgroup of

well-defined up to conjugacy. Notably, the degrees of the completions
\widehat{L_{\mathfrak{p}}}/\widehat{F_{p}}
equal the orbit lengths of the decomposition group at
p
in the Galois closure
\Omega/F
of
L/F
.

We are particularly interested in the case where
F=k(t)
is a rational function field.
A
G
-extension

is called

-regular
, if the algebraic closure of
k
inside
L
equals

.

Now let

be of characteristic zero, and let

be a
k
-regular

-extension with Galois closure
\Omega/k(t)
.
For each
t_{i}\in\overline{k}\cup\{\infty\}
, the

of
\Omega/k(t)
at

is the minimal positive integer

such that

\overline{k}(((t-t_{i})^{1/e_{i}}))

is called a

The set of branch points is always a finite set.
To each branch point

(of ramification index

), we may associate a well-defined conjugacy class
C_{i}
of

, corresponding to the automorphism
(t-t_{i})^{1/e_{i}}\mapsto\zeta(t-t_{i})^{1/e_{i}}
of

\overline{k}(((t-t_{i})^{1/e_{i}}))
, where
\zeta
is a primitive

-th root of unity. The ramification index then equals the order of any element in the class

.

For any

,
the
specialization
of

at

, denoted by

is the residue extension of

at

(where the implicit Dedekind domain

should be taken as the local ring corresponding to the place

).

Now let

be a number field. For our purposes, it is important to gain information about the local behaviour of specializations

Let

, and denote by

Remark 3.3
.

The exceptional sets
\mathcal{S}_{0}
in Theorems

and

are not identical, although both can be made effective (see, e.g.,
[

Finally, we will make use of the following result, which is a consequence of the well-known compatibility of Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem with the weak approximation property (cf., e.g.,
[

.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let

be a

-regular Galois extension with Galois group

, and let

be a finite set of primes of

. For each

Remark 3.5

Concretely, Proposition

3.2.
Root numbers of elliptic curves and abelian varieties

We recall some basics about local root numbers of abelian varieties, and in particular of elliptic curves.
We refer to
[
5
]
for the following.

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over a number field
K
. The (global)
root number

W(A):=W(A/K)\in\{-1,1\}
is the sign in the (conjectural, except for special cases) functional equation for the
L
-function of
A
. By definition
W(A)
is the product of
local root numbers

W_{p}(A)
, i.e., the root numbers associated to
A\otimes_{K}K_{p}
, where
p
runs through all primes of
K
, including the archimedean ones (note that
W_{p}(A)=1
for all but finitely many
p
). The parity conjecture for abelian varieties states that
W(A)=(-1)^{rank(A(K))}
, where
rank(A(K))
is the rank of the Mordell-Weil group of
A
over
K
. Since on the other hand, trivially
rank(A(L))\geq rank(A(K))
for any finite extension
L\supseteq K
, the root number may give information about the rank gain of
A
upon base change to certain extensions (once again, conditional on the parity conjecture).

The following facts about local root numbers of elliptic curves are well-known, cf., e.g.,
[
29
]
(which also includes formulas for the local root numbers in the case of additive reduction).

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
E/K
be an elliptic curve over a number field
K
and
p
a place of
K
. Then

W_{p}(E/K)=\begin{cases}-1,\text{ if }p\text{ is an archimedean place },\\
+1,\text{ if }E/K\text{ has good reduction at }p,\\
-1,\text{ if }E/K\text{ has split multiplicative reduction at }p\\
+1,\text{ if }E/K\text{ has non-split multiplicative reduction at }p\end{cases}
.

In particular, in the case where
E
is a semistable elliptic curve over
K
, one has the following characterization of the root number of
E
over
K
, cf. e.g.,
[
10
]
:

Corollary 3.7
.

For a semistable elliptic curve over a number field
K
,

W(E/K)=(-1)^{u+s}
, where
u
is the number of archimedean places of
K
and
s
is the number of primes of
K
at which
E
has split multiplicative reduction.

4.
Proof of the main results

4.1.
Some preparations

Fix a transitive group
G\leq S_{n}
, a
G
-extension
F/K
of number fields, an elliptic curve
E
defined over
K
and a prime
p
of
K
at which
E
has multiplicative reduction.
Due to Proposition
3.6
, the product of local root numbers
W_{q}(E/F)
at primes
q
of
F
extending
p
is exactly
(-1)^{s_{p}}
, where
s_{p}
is the number of primes of
F
extending
p
at which
E
has split multiplicative reduction. It is well-known (e.g.,
[
32
, Chapter VII.5]
) that
E
has multiplicative reduction at all primes
\mathfrak{p}
extending
p
, and the reduction type at
\mathfrak{p}
is non-split if and only if it is non-split at
p
and the residue degree of
\mathfrak{p}
over
p
is odd.

On the other hand, the places extending

in

correspond one-to-one to the orbits of the decomposition group
D_{p}\leq G
at
p
in the Galois closure
\Omega/K
of
F/K
, and the residue degree of any such place equals the number of
I_{p}
-orbits (
I_{p}
being the inertia group at
p
) into which the respective
D_{p}
-orbit decomposes.
We therefore have the following characterization:

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume the above notation.

a)

If
E
has split multiplicative reduction at
p
, then
s_{p}
equals the number of orbits of the decomposition group
D_{p}
at
p
in
\Omega/K
.

b)

If
E
has non-split multiplicative reduction at
p
, then
s_{p}
equals the number of orbits of
D_{p}
which split into an even number of orbits of the inertia group
I_{p}\trianglelefteq D_{p}
.

Similarly, if
q
is an archimedean place of
K
extended by exactly
u_{q}
places of
F
, the contribution to
W(E/F)
from places extending
q
is simply
(-1)^{u_{q}}
, and this number is determined as follows.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume the above notation. Let
q
be an archimedean place of
K
.

a)

If
q
is real archimedean, then
u_{q}
equals the number of orbits of
\tau\in G
, where
K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the real embedding corresponding to
q
, and
\tau
is the complex conjugation in
\Omega/K
.

b)

If
q
is non-real, then
u_{q}=[F:K]
.

In particular, the values
s_{q}
in Lemma
4.1
as well as
u_{q}
in Lemma
4.2
a) are given group-theoretically in terms of certain orbit numbers.
Therefore, due to Proposition
3.6
, controlling the value of
W(E/F)
reduces to a two-fold problem:
firstly, a purely group-theoretical question about the existence of subgroups of
G
with a certain orbit structure. Secondly, a local-global problem, namely finding
G
-extensions with the prescribed subgroups from the first step as decomposition (and inertia) subgroups at certain prescribed primes.

Indeed, assume that there is a prime
p
of
K
which is either real archimedean, or non-archimedean and of split multiplicative reduction for
E
. Assume furthermore that there exist two subgroups
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
of
G
whose numbers of orbits
|orb(H_{1})|
and
|orb(H_{2})|
have different parity, and such that
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
both occur as decomposition groups of a suitable extension of
K_{p}
. Then
W(E/F)
can in principle be controlled via the prime
p
; concretely, assume for the moment that
H_{1}
is cyclic and contained in
A_{n}
(i.e., in particular
n-|orb(H_{1})|
is even), whereas
n-|orb(H_{2})|
is odd. If there exist two
G
-extensions
F_{1}/K
and
F_{2}/K
with decomposition groups
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
at
p
, then
W_{p}(E/F_{1})\neq W_{p}(E/F_{2})
due to Lemmas
4.1
a) and
4.2
a).
If, additionally,
F_{1}/K
and
F_{2}/K
have identical local behavior at all other primes of multiplicative reduction of
E
as well as at the archimedean primes, then we have
W(E/F_{1})\neq W(E/F_{2})
.

Assume now instead that there exists a prime
p
at which
E
has non-split multiplicative reduction.
Then, from Lemma
4.1
b),
W_{p}(E/K)
can be controlled as soon as there exists pairs of subgroups
(I_{1},H_{1})
and
(I_{2},H_{2})
occurring as inertia group and decomposition group at
p
in suitable
G
-extensions
F_{1}/K
and
F_{2}/K
, and such that the parities of
|orb(H_{1})|-|orb(I_{1})|
and
|orb(H_{2})|-|orb(I_{2})|
are different.
If, additionally,
I_{2}
and
H_{1}
are both cyclic and contained in
A_{n}
(i.e.,
n-|orb(I_{2})|
,
n-|orb(H_{1})|
and
n-|orb(I_{1})|
are all even),
then we obtain exactly the same sufficient condition “
n-|orb(H_{2})|
odd” for the inequality of local root numbers
W_{p}(E/F_{1})\neq W_{p}(E/F_{2})
as above.

In total, we have shown the following simplified assertion:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a number field
K
and
\mathcal{S}_{E}
be any finite set of primes of
K
, containing the archimedean places of
K
and the primes of bad reduction of
E
.
Let
G\leq S_{n}
be a transitive permutation group.

Assume the following:

i)

There exist
p\in\mathcal{S}_{E}
, either real archimedean or of multiplicative reduction for
E
, and a subgroup
H\leq G
which occurs as the Galois group of some extension of
\widehat{K_{p}}
such that
n-|orb(H)|
is odd.

ii)

There exist
G
-extensions
F_{1}/K
and
F_{2}/K
, with isomorphic completions
\widehat{(F_{1})_{q}}\cong\widehat{(F_{2})_{q}}
for all
q\in\mathcal{S}_{E}\setminus\{p\}
, and such that
p
is unramified in
F_{1}/K
with decomposition group contained in
G\cap A_{n}
, whereas the decomposition group at
p
of
F_{2}/K
equals
H
, and the corresponding inertia group
I\trianglelefteq H
is cyclic and contained in
G\cap A_{n}
.

Then
W(E/F_{1})\neq W(E/F_{2})
. In particular, conditional on the parity conjecture,
E
gains rank over at least one of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
.

Remark 4.4
.

Some words on the group-theoretical condition on the existence of a subgroup
H
as in condition i) are in order. Obviously, it can be fulfilled for any subgroup
G\leq S_{n}
not contained in
A_{n}
. On the other hand, it cannot possibly be fulfilled for groups
G
of odd order. An “obvious” class of interesting test cases (i.e., for which the group theoretical condition is neither trivial nor impossible) is then the class of non-abelian simple groups, and we will follow this consideration in Sections
4.4
and
4.5
. The group-theoretical condition still does not hold for all simple groups - e.g., computation with Magma
[
4
]
confirms that
PSL_{2}(11)
acting primitively on
55
points has no metacyclic subgroup (i.e., no subgroup which could occur as the decomposition group at a tamely ramified prime) with
55-|orb(H)|
odd. At least, it seems very frequently fulfilled among primitive simple groups.

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

Deducing positive answers to Question
1.1
a) (and, in fact, b)) for a group

from Proposition

is possible as soon as

contains

as in Proposition

, occurring as a Galois group over suitable completions

, and assuming the existence of

-extensions with suitable local behavior as in Condition ii) of Prop.

. Tools to guarantee the latter are provided by Theorems

, which we now use to prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem

.

In case

is not contained in

, define

and

to be any cyclic subgroup of

not contained in

. If on the other hand

, we are in Alternative ii) of the assumptions of Theorem

, and set

as defined there. Note that in both cases,

is odd, and

is cyclic and contained in

.

We now claim that for all primes

of

inside some (to be determined) set

of primes of positive density, and for all finite sets
\mathcal{S}_{0}
of primes of

, there exist two specializations

and

, both with Galois group

, such that 1)

has decomposition group

and inertia group

at

is unramified at

with Frobenius contained in

;
and 3) both extensions have identical local behaviour at each

.

Since the proof of the claim requires slightly different arguments in the two alternatives of Theorem
2.1
, we assume first
G\leq A_{n}
(whence we are in Alternative ii)).
Then the first requirement of the claim is guaranteed by Theorem
3.2
, assuming that we choose

satisfying both of the following:

a)

p
has Frobenius class (the conjugacy class of)
\overline{x}
in the residue extension of
L/K(t)
at the branch point

, where
\overline{x}
denotes the image of the prescribed element

from Theorem

b)

p
is outside some finite set of primes depending on

Clearly the set

of such primes

is of positive density by Chebotarev’s density theorem, yielding the claim about

Next, the second requirement of the claim simply translates to

by choosing an arbitrary specialization value

. Define

as

minus these finitely many primes.

Now we need to find infinitely many values

(resp.,
t_{1}
)
\in K
whose specialization

(resp.,
L_{t_{1}}/K
) has the same local behavior as

(resp., as

) at

, retains the full Galois group

, and moreover such that

and

have identical local behavior at all

.
This, however, is provided by Proposition

, which also gives linear disjointness.
The assertion now follows immediately from Proposition
4.3
. Concretely, choosing

(resp.,

) as the subfield of

(resp.,

) fixed by a point stabilizer in

, one has
W(E/F_{1})\neq W(E/F_{2})
for all elliptic curves

over

with at least one prime

of multiplicative reduction (of course, we here apply the above with

the union of the set of archimedean primes of

Now assume instead that we are in Alternative i) of the theorem, i.e.,

is not contained in

.
Again, the assertion follows directly from Proposition

, assuming that we can prove the claim. The only changes in the proof are required in the choice of specializations

∎

Note that in the case when

is not contained in

, the above proof actually yields a cofinite set

of primes fulfilling the assertion of Theorem

.
We end this section by pointing out a class of groups for which one has an even stronger conclusion.

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume the parity conjecture.

Let
K
be a number field, and let
G\leq S_{n}
be a transitive group not contained in
A_{n}
and possessing a generic Galois extension over
K
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
K
, and assume that
E
possesses at least one prime of multiplicative reduction.
Then
E
gains rank over infinitely many
G
-extensions of
K
.

Proof.

We may choose a prime
p
of
K
, of multiplicative reduction for
E
, and an element
\sigma\in G
with
\textrm{sgn}(\sigma)=-1
.
Since
G
is assumed to possess a generic Galois extension over
K
, we may invoke a famous result by Saltman
[
31
, Theorem 5.9]
, asserting the solvability of all “Grunwald problems” for
G
over
K
. I.e., given any finite set
S
of places of
K
and Galois extensions
\widehat{L^{q}}/\widehat{K_{q}}
with Galois group embedding into
G
(for all
q\in S
), there exists a
G
-extension of
K
with completion
\widehat{L^{q}}/\widehat{K_{q}}
for all
q\in S
simultaneously.
It now suffices to choose all the
\widehat{L^{q}}/\widehat{K_{q}}
unramified and simply apply Proposition
4.3
with
H=\langle\sigma\rangle
and
I=\{1\}
, yielding
G
-extensions
F_{1}/K
and
F_{2}/K
with
W(E/F_{1})\neq W(E/F_{2})
.
Of course, one even obtains infinitely many pairs of such extensions, e.g., up to increasing the finite set
S
and thus the number of local conditions above suitably. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

a)

Groups for which generic Galois extensions exist over all number fields
K
include the symmetric groups
S_{n}
, whence in particular we immediately regain a positive answer to Question
1.1
over (e.g.)
K=\mathbb{Q}
, although of course this follows unconditionally from
[
21
]
. Another class of examples are dihedral groups

for all primes

, cf.
[

, Proposition 5.5.2]
(and these groups are not contained in

as soon as

mod
4
).

b)

If

is even a semidirect product of

and

(such as the two examples in a)), then even the condition on

to have a prime of multiplicative reduction in Theorem

can be dropped, as long as

has a real embedding. Indeed, one may then choose a real-archimedean prime

and a cyclic subgroup

generated by an involution in

(taking the role of complex conjugation in an extension of

), and carry out the proof in analogy to the above.

In light of the above, it is more challenging to focus on groups

, and for which generic Galois extensions are not known to exist (for example, the existence of generic

-extensions over

is open for all

]
).

4.3.
Application of Theorem
2.1
: A first example

We show the practical applicability of Theorem
2.1
with a first non-trivial example. In the next section we will give an infinite family of examples.
It should be understood that applications of Theorem
2.1
are not limited in any way to these cases, and the interested reader may consult, e.g.,
[
23
]
for many more
\mathbb{Q}
-regular Galois realizations of simple groups
G
, some of which (with suitable permutation representations of
G
) lend themselves to similar applications. Group-theoretical claims in the following example were verified with Magma.

Example 4.7
.

Let
G=M_{24}<A_{24}
be the largest sporadic Mathieu group in its natural primitive action on
24
points. We show the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
can be fulfilled for
G
over
K=\mathbb{Q}
. By
[
23
, Theorem III.7.12]
, there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-regular Galois realization
L/\mathbb{Q}(t)
with group
G
and with four branch points, of ramification index
2
,
2
,
2
and
12
respectively. The branch point
t_{1}
of ramification index
12
is automatically
\mathbb{Q}
-rational. Indeed, this follows from a special case of the so-called branch cycle lemma (e.g.,
[
23
, Chapter I, Theorem 2.6]
), asserting that branch points conjugate to each other must have the same ramification index. Furthermore, the inertia group
I
of order
12
has exactly two orbits (each of length
12
) in the action on
24
points. The residue extension of
L/\mathbb{Q}(t)
at
t\mapsto t_{i}
must contain the
12
-th roots of unity (see, e.g.,
[
19
, Lemma 2.3]
), whence the decomposition group at
t\mapsto t_{i}
is of order divisible by
12\cdot\varphi(12)=48
. On the other hand, the normalizer of the cyclic subgroup
I
in
M_{24}
is only of order
48
, whence equality must hold. This normalizer contains a subgroup
H=\langle I,x\rangle
isomorphic to the dihedral group
D_{12}
, such that
x
switches the two orbits of
I
; in other words,
24-|orb(H)|=23
is odd. This shows that the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
are fulfilled.

Note that the above argument used only ramification indices of a certain
\mathbb{Q}
-regular realization; also, the proof of the existence of this particular realization can be carried out purely group-theoretically (using braid group action). The argument therefore does not require the computation of a concrete polynomial for the extension
L/\mathbb{Q}(t)
.

4.4.
Projective linear groups: Proof of Theorem
2.2

Proof.

We can make Theorem
2.1
more explicit using a concrete
PSL_{2}(p)
-extension
L/\mathbb{Q}(t)
as given in
[
23
, Chapter 1, Cor. 8.10]
. This extension has three branch points, of ramification index

2
,
p
and
p
respectively. Since there is only one branch point
t_{1}
of ramification index
2
, it is automatically
\mathbb{Q}
-rational by the branch cycle lemma
[
23
, Chapter I, Theorem 2.6]
. Furthermore, as shown in the proof of
[
19
, Theorem 5.3]
, the residue extension
L_{t_{1}}/\mathbb{Q}
at this branch point must contain
\sqrt{-1}
. The same then remains true for the
K
-regular
PSL_{2}(p)
-extension
L^{\prime}:=L\cdot K/K(t)
. In particular, for any prime
q
of
K
which is inert in
K(\sqrt{-1})
, the Frobenius of
L^{\prime}_{t_{1}}/K
at
q
has even order. Therefore, we can use Theorem
3.2
to obtain the following:

For all but finitely many primes
q
of
K
inert in
K(\sqrt{-1})
, there exist infinitely many
t_{0}\in K
such that
L^{\prime}_{t_{0}}/K
has inertia group
I
of order
2
and decomposition group
\langle I,x\rangle
of order divisible by
4
at
q
. However, due to our condition
p\equiv 3\pmod{8}
, the only class of elements of order
2
in

is the class of fixed point free involutions, which are contained in a cyclic subgroup of order
\frac{p+1}{2}
. The centralizer of such an involution is the normalizer of such a cyclic subgroup, and is isomorphic to a dihedral group of order
p+1
(acting regularly on
p+1
points). Cf., e.g.,
[
12
, Chapter II.8]
(in particular Satz 8.4 and Satz 8.5), for the above well-known facts on subgroups of
PSL_{2}(p)
.
Now the group
H:=\langle I,x\rangle
is a subgroup of such an involution centralizer, and thus has
\frac{p+1}{|H|}
orbits (each of length

). Since

by assumption and
|H|
is divisible by
4
by the above, this means that the number of orbits of

is odd. On the other hand, we are considering
PSL_{2}(p)\leq S_{p+1}
acting transitively on a set of even cardinality. Now, as in the proof of Theorem

we obtain that for all but finitely many

as above, and for all elliptic curves

with multiplicative reduction at

-extensions of

over which

gains rank. This concludes the proof.
∎

4.5.
Alternating groups: Proof of Theorem
2.3

For the case of alternating groups, we can make use of strong results on regular extensions with prescribed fibers due to Mestre, and thus reach a stronger conclusion than in the previous cases.

The following special existence result on
A_{n}
-extensions with prescribed local conditions will suffice for our purposes.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
K
be a number field,
S=\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{r}\}
be a non-empty finite set of primes of
K
, such that
S\setminus\{p_{1}\}
contains all archimedean primes of
K
. Let
n\geq 4
. Then there exist infinitely degree-
n
extensions
F_{1}/K
and
F_{2}/K
with group
A_{n}
fulfilling the following:

a)

The decomposition group at any prime extending
p_{1}
in the Galois closure of
F_{1}/K
is conjugate in
A_{n}
to the Klein
4
-group on
\langle(1,2)(3,4),(1,3)(2,4)\rangle
, and the respective inertia subgroup is conjugate to
\langle(1,2)(3,4)\rangle
.

b)

p_{i}
splits completely in
F_{j}/K
, for all
i=2,\dots,r
,
j\in\{1,2\}
and
(i,j)\neq(1,1)
.

Theorem
2.3
immediately follows from Lemma
4.9
,
together with Proposition
4.3
. It therefore remains to prove Lemma
4.9
.

Proof.

First, let
n
be odd. Clearly, there is a
V_{4}
-extension of
K
(with
V_{4}
the Klein
4
-group) with local behaviour in
S
as prescribed for
F_{1}/K
in a) and b), e.g., as a very special case of the Grunwald-Wang theorem (e.g.,
[
27
, Theorem 9.2.8]
).
Denote this extension by
F/K
. Now a famous construction of Mestre
[
26
]
ensures the existence of an
A_{n}
-extension
L/K(t)
which specializes to
F/K
at
t\mapsto 0
(and is unramified at
t\mapsto 0
). More precisely,
L/K(t)
can be chosen as the splitting field of a polynomial
f(X)-tg(X)
where
f
is separable of degree
n
with group
V_{4}
, and
\deg(g)<n
.
Now, specialize
L/K(t)
at any value
t_{0}\in K\setminus\{0\}
which is
p
-adically “sufficiently” close to
0
for all
p\in S
.
The assertion for the infinitely many fields
F_{1}/K
then follows from compatibility of weak approximation with Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem, as stated in Proposition
3.4
.
To obtain the same for the fields
F_{2}/K
, simply repeat the above starting with the trivial extension
K/K
instead of
F/K
.

Now let
n
be even. Then choose an extension
L/K(t)
as above for the group
A_{n+1}
, and note that by construction the fixed field of
L
is a rational function field, say
K(X)
. Without loss, assume that
X\mapsto 0
is a point extending

in
K(X)/K(t)
. Then specialization of
L/K(X)
at
X\to 0
yields a
V_{4}
-extension, and we may now specialize
X

p
-adically close to
0
as above to reach the desired conclusion for
A_{n}
with
n
even, completing the proof.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

Note that while the extensions

constructed in the above proof depend on the concrete elliptic curve

, their genus does not, whence Remark

yields an asymptotic lower bound independent of

for the number of

-extensions over which

gains rank.

Example 4.11
.

Given a concrete elliptic curve and a concrete

be the (rank

4.6.
About the elliptic curves satisfying the assumptions of the theorems

As shown in
[
3
, Theorem 8]
, 100 percent of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
have at least one prime of multiplicative reduction, and thus fulfill the conclusion of Theorem
2.3
. In fact, what is shown in
[
3
]
is that for any finitely many prime numbers
p_{1},\dots,p_{r}
, the proportion of elliptic curves which do not have multiplicative reduction at any
p_{i}
is bounded from above by
\prod_{i=1}^{r}(1-1/p_{i}+o(1/p_{i}))
, see the proof of
[
3
, Corollary 28]
.

It then follows easily that the “
100\%
density” conclusion holds even for elliptic curves with at least one prime of multiplicative reduction inside a prescribed positive density subset of all prime numbers. In other words, Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
also apply to 100 percent of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
, ordered by height. It is reasonable to expect the same over arbitrary number fields, although a formal proof of such a statement would lead us too far away.

5.
A generalization to abelian varieties

Of course, it is natural to ask Question

for abelian varieties of dimension
>1
as well. Our methods generalize to this case as long as one has sufficiently explicit formulae linking the local root numbers of such varieties to such group-theoretical invariants as orbit numbers of decomposition groups etc.
We exhibit some cases in which this can be achieved.

5.1.
Semistable Jacobians

For the following, see Section 5 of
[

]

Let

Let

in

(

Under the above assumptions,

We can now state the following somewhat technical result, which may be seen as an analog of Proposition

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

Furthermore, let

For a prime

, or

Then there exist

Proof.

of

be a prime extending

-orbit of singular points

\gcd(d,d(z))

.

be the product of all

-orbit dividing

(1)

\omega_{\mathfrak{q}}([z])=(-1)^{\gcd(d,d(z))}\cdot\tau_{\mathfrak{q}}([z^{\prime}]),

-orbit dividing

.
Let

(2)

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume the parity conjecture for abelian varieties. Let

be a curve over a number field

Proof.

It suffices to verify that the extensions constructed in the proofs of Theorem

and Theorem
2.2
, are such that they fulfill conditions 1) and 2) of Lemma
5.2
for the prescribed prime

(and for

.

For

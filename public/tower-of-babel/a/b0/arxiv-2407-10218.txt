Wavefronts for a degenerate reaction-diffusion system with application to bacterial growth models
Luisa Malaguti, Elisa Sovrano

Abstract
We investigate wavefront solutions in a nonlinear system of two coupled reaction-diffusion equations with degenerate diffusivity: n t = n x ​ x − n ​ b , b t = [ D ​ n ​ b ​ b x ] x + n ​ b , n_{t}=n_{xx}-nb,\hskip 9.24994ptb_{t}=[Dnbb_{x}]_{x}+nb, where t ≥ 0 , t\geq 0, x ∈ ℝ x\in\mathbb{R} , and D D is a positive diffusion coefficient. This model, introduced by Kawasaki et al. (J. Theor. Biol. 188, 1997), describes the spatial-temporal dynamics of bacterial colonies b = b ⁡ ( x , t ) b=b(x,t) and nutrients n = n ⁡ ( x , t ) n=n(x,t) on agar plates. Kawasaki et al. provided numerical evidence for wavefronts, leaving the analytical confirmation of these solutions an open problem. We prove the existence of an infinite family of wavefronts parameterized by their wave speed, which varies on a closed positive half-line. We provide an upper bound for the threshold speed and a lower bound for it when D D is sufficiently large. The proofs are based on several analytical tools, including the shooting method and the fixed-point theory in Fréchet spaces, to establish existence, and the central manifold theorem to ascertain uniqueness. Mathematics Subject Classifications: 35C07, 35K55, 35K57. Keywords: degenerate diffusion, reaction-diffusion equations, wavefront, wave speed.

Introduction and main results

In this article, we are exploring a nonlinear system of two coupled reaction-diffusion equations that models the growth of bacteria on agar plates with nutrients. Based on laboratory experiments and numerical analyses, it has been observed that bacterial evolution can produce various spatiotemporal patterns during bacterial growth, including rings, disks, and dense branching morphologies, which depend on the bacterial species and nutrient conditions (see
[
9
,
12
,
13
,
18
]
). To study the evolution of bacteria and nutrients, we consider the reaction-diffusion model as described in
[
13
]
:

\displaystyle n_{t}=D_{n}n_{xx}-nb,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle b_{t}=[D_{b}nbb_{x}]_{x}+nb,

(1.1b)

where
n=n(x,t)
and
b=b(x,t)
are the concentration of the nutrient and the population density of bacteria, respectively, at time
t\geq 0
and space
x\in\mathbb{R}
. The parameters
D_{n}
and
D_{b}
are the nutrient and bacteria diffusion coefficients, respectively. The diffusivity of bacteria is nonlinear and degenerates to model its trend toward reduced mobility when the nutrient or the bacteria concentration level is low (i.e., if either
n
or
b
tends to zero).

Experimental observations reported in
[
13
]
have shown that system (
1.1b
) can generate spatiotemporal patterns supported by traveling wave solutions. These solutions are characterized by profiles that propagate at a constant speed, specifically represented by:

n(x,t)=\eta(x-ct),\quad b(x,t)=\beta(x-ct),

(1.2)

where
(\eta,\beta)
represents the profile and
c\in\mathbb{R}
denotes the wave speed, which is an unknown of the problem. Despite these experimental observations, there is a lack of theoretical proof for the existence of traveling wave solutions for system (
1.1b
), nor has there been an analytical study of the admissible wave speeds. This work aims to address these theoretical and analytical gaps.

In
[
20
]
, researchers analyzed the case
D_{n}=0
and, through both numerical and analytical approaches, indicated that wavefronts display “sharp” behavior in the bacteria profile at a critical speed (see Definition
1.2
). In
[
19
]
, the studied system is

\begin{cases}n_{t}=-f(n,b),\\
b_{t}=[g(n)h(b)b_{x}]_{x}+f(n,b),\end{cases}

where the diffusion term
g(n)h(b)
degenerates at both
n=0
and
b=0
, and the reaction term
f(n,b)
is positive except when
n=0
or
b=0
. They established the existence of wavefronts for all wave speeds within a closed half-line and, similar to the model in
[
20
]
, showed that the profile exhibits “sharp” behavior at the threshold speed. It has been also demonstrated in
[
19
]
that the profiles for all other speeds are smooth. For the classical non-degenerate case, the analogous system studied in literature is:

\begin{cases}n_{t}=\lambda n_{xx}-f(n,b),\\
b_{t}=b_{xx}+f(n,b),\end{cases}

where
\lambda\geq 0
. Results depend on different properties of the function
f
, which is consistently assumed to be as before (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
5
,
15
,
17
]
). This model applies to contexts such as modeling an isothermal autocatalytic chemical reaction (where
n
and
b
represent the concentrations of the autocatalysts) or modeling thermal diffusive combustion (where
n
is the concentration of the reactant and
b
is the temperature of the mixture). Various results regarding wavefronts have been obtained depending on the value of
\lambda
and the properties of
f
. If
\lambda=0
or
\lambda>0
and
f(n,b)=nb
, an admissible closed half-line of wave speeds has been demonstrated (see
[
4
,
15
]
). Conversely, if
\lambda>0
and
f(n,b)=\tilde{g}(n)b
with
\tilde{g}(0)=0
and
\tilde{g}(s)>0
for all
s\in(0,1]
, then an admissible closed half-line of wave speeds is guaranteed for
0<\lambda<1
(see
[
17
]
). For
\lambda>1
, however, the set of admissible speeds may be disconnected (see
[
5
]
). These results have been further improved in
[
1
]
to include more general reaction terms
f(n,b)
.

Without loss of generality, in the following, we assume in (
1.1b
) that

D_{n}=1\quad\text{and}\quad D_{b}=D>0.

When introducing the wave coordinate
\xi:=x-ct
, the functions
\eta
and
\beta
in (
1.2
) satisfy the system

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime\prime}+c\eta^{\prime}-\eta\beta=0,

(1.3a)

\displaystyle\left(D\eta\beta\beta^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}+c\beta^{\prime}+\eta\beta=0,

(1.3b)

where
{}^{\prime}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\xi}
. The nontrivial stationary solutions associated with (
1.3b
) belong to one of the following sets:

\{(\eta,\beta)\colon\eta\equiv K_{\eta},\,\beta\equiv 0,\text{ with }K_{\eta}\in(0,+\infty)\},\quad\{(\eta,\beta)\colon\eta\equiv 0,\,\beta\equiv K_{\beta},\text{ with }K_{\beta}\in(0,+\infty)\}.

Here, we look for traveling waves of (
1.1b
) that connect nontrivial stationary solutions characterized by profiles
(\eta,\beta)
consisting of a pair of strictly monotone functions (i.e.,
wavefronts
). In the following, we will consider
K_{\eta}=1=K_{\beta}
and look for wavefronts that connect the two stationary solutions
(0,1)
and
(1,0)
. This leads to the boundary conditions:

\displaystyle\left(\eta(-\infty),\beta(-\infty)\right)=(0,1),

(1.4a)

\displaystyle\left(\eta(+\infty),\beta(+\infty)\right)=(1,0),

(1.4b)

where for any function
m(\xi)
with a limit at
\pm\infty
, we abbreviate
\lim_{\xi\to\pm\infty}m(\xi)=m(\pm\infty)
.

Given the presence of a degenerate diffusion term in system (
1.1b
), before stating our results, let us point out the solution notion we are seeking (see
[
8
]
).

Definition 1.1

(Wavefront and semi-wavefront)
.

Let
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an open interval and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Consider two non-constant monotone functions
\eta,\beta\colon J\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(i)

\eta
is of class

and solves (
1.3a
);

(ii)

\beta
is continuous and differentiable a.e. with
\beta\beta^{\prime}\in L^{1}_{loc}(J)
and solves weakly (
1.3b
), namely, for every
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(J)
, it satisfies

\int_{J}\left[\left(D\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)+c\beta(\xi)\right)\psi^{\prime}(\xi)-\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\psi(\xi)\right]\,\mathrm{d}\xi=0.

(1.5)

•

When
J=\mathbb{R}
and
(\eta,\beta)
satisfies (
1.4b
),
(n,b)
with
n(x,t)=\eta(x-ct)=\eta(\xi)
and
b(x,t)=\beta(x-ct)=\beta(\xi)
is called (global)
wavefront
of (
1.1b
).

•

When
J=(-\infty,a)
(or
J=(a,+\infty)
) with
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
(\eta,\beta)
satisfies (
1.4a
) (or (
1.4b
)),
(n,b)
with
n(x,t)=\eta(x-ct)=\eta(\xi)
and
b(x,t)=\beta(x-ct)=\beta(\xi)
is called
semi-wavefront
in
J
of (
1.1b
).

In both cases, the pair
(\eta,\beta)
denotes the wave profile of
(n,b)
, whereas
c
stands for the wave speed.

Warning.
For conciseness, we will refer also to the profile
(\eta,\beta)
as “wavefront” of (
1.1b
) or “semi-wavefront” of (
1.1b
) (satisfying either (
1.4a
) or (
1.4b
)).

Definition 1.2

(Classical and sharp wavefront)
.

A wavefront
(\eta,\beta)
is said to be classical if the function
\beta
is differentiable,
\beta\beta^{\prime}
is absolutely continuous, and the equation (
1.3b
) holds a.e.
On the other hand, a wavefront
(\eta,\beta)
is said to be sharp at the point
\ell\in\{0,1\}
if there exists a real number
\xi_{\ell}
such that
\beta(\xi_{\ell})=\ell
, and the function
\beta
is classical on the set
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\xi_{\ell}\}
, but not differentiable at the point
\xi_{\ell}
.

This work investigates conditions under which wavefronts exist and examines their regularity properties. Specifically, we demonstrate the existence and uniqueness of a semi-wavefront for each positive wave speed in the negative half-line
(-\infty,0]
. Our approach utilizes shooting method and fixed-point theory in Fréchet spaces to establish existence and the central manifold theorem to ascertain uniqueness.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
D_{n}=1
and
D_{b}=D>0
. For each
c>0
, system (
1.1b
) has a unique semi-wavefront in
(-\infty,0]
with wave speed
c
whose profile
(\eta,\beta)
satisfies (
1.4a
). Moreover,
\eta^{\prime}>0
and
\beta^{\prime}<0
.

Further, we extend the solution to the positive half-line
[0,+\infty)
and show that sufficiently large wave speeds yield a classical global wavefront.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
D_{n}=1
and
D_{b}=D>0
. There exists
c_{0}>0
such that, for every
c\geq c_{0}
, system (
1.1b
) has a unique wavefront with wave speed
c
whose profile
(\eta,\beta)
satisfies (
1.4b
). If
c>c_{0}
, the wavefront is classical.
Moreover, the following estimates on
c_{0}
hold:

\max\left\{0,\sqrt{{D}/{15}}-1\right\}<c_{0}\leq 2\sqrt{De^{D}}.

Drawing parallels with the scenario when
D=0
, we conjecture the existence of a threshold wave speed for which a sharp wavefront for system (
1.1b
) can be observed (see
[
19
,
20
]
).

We first describe some properties that wavefront or semi-wavefronts of (

\eta_{0}:=\eta(0),\quad\eta^{\prime}_{0}:=\eta^{\prime}(0),\qquad\beta_{0}:=\beta(0),\quad\beta^{\prime}_{0}:=\beta^{\prime}(0).

(2.1)

Let
(\eta,\beta)
be a solution of (

\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, the shifted pair
\left(\eta(\cdot+\xi_{0}),\beta(\cdot+\xi_{0})\right)
remains a solution of (
1.3b
).
So, with no loss of generality, in the following, we always assume that

\beta_{0}>0.

Furthermore, if
(\eta,\beta)
is a wavefront of (
1.1b
) or a semi-wavefront of (
1.1b
) (satisfying either (
1.4a
) or (
1.4b

1.1

\beta
is non-constant and monotone decreasing. If, moreover,

\tau:=\sup\{\xi>0\colon\beta(\xi)>0\}.

(2.2)

.

The following lemma provides qualitative properties on wavefronts of (

).

Lemma 2.1
.

Every wavefront
(\eta,\beta)
of (
1.1b
) with wave speed

(i)

\eta^{\prime}(-\infty)=0
and
\eta(-\infty)\beta^{\prime}(-\infty)=0
;

(ii)

\eta^{\prime}(+\infty)=0
and
\beta(\tau^{-})\beta^{\prime}(\tau^{-})=0
;

(iii)

c=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\eta(s)\beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s>0
;

(iv)

0<\eta^{\prime}(\xi)<c
and
0<\eta(\xi)<1
,
\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}
;

(v)

\beta^{\prime}(\xi)<0
and
0<\beta(\xi)<1
,
\forall\xi\in(-\infty,\tau)
.

Proof.

By Definition
1.1
, we notice that
\eta
and
\beta
are monotone functions. Thus, they are bounded functions thanks to the boundary conditions (

), namely
\eta(\xi)\in[0,1]

\beta(\xi)\in[0,1]

\xi\in\mathbb{R}

) in
[\xi,0]

\eta^{\prime}(\xi)=\eta^{\prime}_{0}+c\left(\eta_{0}-\eta(\xi)\right)-\int^{0}_{\xi}\eta(s)\beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

and so we deduce that
\eta^{\prime}
has limit as

\eta
is bounded, then
\eta^{\prime}(-\infty)=0
. Similarly, by integrating (
1.3b
) in
[\xi,0]

D\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)=D\eta_{0}\beta_{0}\beta^{\prime}_{0}+c\left(\beta_{0}-\beta(\xi)\right)+\int^{0}_{\xi}\eta(s)\beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)

\eta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)
has limit as

\beta(-\infty)=1

\eta(-\infty)\beta^{\prime}(-\infty)=l<0

\eta(-\infty)\frac{\beta^{\prime}(-\infty)}{\eta(-\infty)}=\frac{l}{\eta(-\infty)}=-\infty,

\beta
is bounded. Thus, we have
\eta(-\infty)\beta^{\prime}(-\infty)=0

The proof that
\eta^{\prime}(+\infty)=0

\beta(+\infty)\beta^{\prime}(+\infty)=0

\tau<+\infty

\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\tau-\varepsilon,\tau+\varepsilon)

\begin{split}0=&\int_{\tau-\varepsilon}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\left[(D\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)+c\beta(\xi))\psi^{\prime}(\xi)-\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\psi(\xi)\right]\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\

Since
\psi(\tau-\varepsilon)=0

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\left[(D\eta(\tau-\delta)\beta(\tau-\delta)\beta^{\prime}(\tau-\delta)+c\beta(\tau-\delta))\right]\psi(\tau-\delta)=0,

\lim_{\xi\to\tau^{-}}\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)=0.

(ii)
.

To prove

, let us integrate (
1.3a
) in
[\xi_{0},\xi]
and pass to the limit when
\xi_{0}\to-\infty

(i)

\eta^{\prime}(\xi)+c\eta(\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}\eta(s)\beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Hence, by

and (

) as

.

To prove the first property in
(iv)
, we observe that

\left(\eta^{\prime\prime}(\xi)+c\eta^{\prime}(\xi)-\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\right)e^{c\xi}=\left(\eta^{\prime}(\xi)e^{c\xi}\right)^{\prime}-\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)e^{c\xi}=0,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

Then, thanks to

, we have

\eta^{\prime}(\xi)=e^{-c\xi}\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}\eta(s)\beta(s)e^{cs}\,\mathrm{d}s.

(2.5)

The case
\eta(\xi)\equiv 0

(-\infty,\xi)
is not possible because it implies

, in contradiction with Definition
1.1
. Hence we obtain that
\eta^{\prime}(\xi)>0
, for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
. Now we prove that

, for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}

\beta^{\prime}(\xi)=\frac{c(1-\beta(\xi))}{D\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)}-\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{D\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)}-c\frac{1}{D\beta(\xi)},\quad\forall\xi<\tau.

At last, the conclusion follows thanks to the regularity of
\eta^{\prime}
(see Definition
1.1
).

The following lemma contains some further important properties satisfied by the semi-wavefronts
(\eta,\beta)
of (
1.1b
) in

with

Lemma 2.5
.

If
(\eta,\beta)
is a wavefront of (
1.1b
) in
(-\infty,\tau)
for some
c>0
,
then

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi\to-\infty}\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}=\frac{2}{c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4}}
;

(ii)

\beta^{\prime}(-\infty)=0
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi\to-\infty}\frac{1-\beta(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}=\frac{2}{c^{2}+c\sqrt{c^{2}+4}}+1
.

Proof.

To prove
(i)
, we assume, by a contradiction, that such a limit does not exist. Therefore, by using Lemma
2.1

(iv)
, we have

0\leq\ell_{1}:=\liminf_{\xi\to-\infty}\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}<\limsup_{\xi\to-\infty}\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}=:\ell_{2}\leq+\infty.

(2.8)

For every
\lambda\in(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
there exist two sequences
(\xi_{n})_{n}
and
(\mu_{n})_{n}
such that
\xi_{n}\to-\infty,

\mu_{n}\to-\infty
as
n\to+\infty
, and the following hold

\displaystyle\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi_{n})}{\eta(\xi_{n})}=\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\mu_{n})}{\eta(\mu_{n})}=\lambda,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}\right)^{\prime}\Big|_{\xi=\xi_{n}}>0,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}\right)^{\prime}\Big|_{\xi=\mu_{n}}<0,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.10)

When computing the derivative in (
2.9
) we have that
\eta^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{n})/\eta(\xi_{n})>\lambda^{2}
for all
n
and then, by (
1.3a
),
-c\lambda+\beta(\xi_{n})>\lambda^{2}
; passing to the limit as
n\to+\infty
we obtain that
\lambda^{2}+c\lambda-1\leq 0
. Since
\lambda
is positive by (
2.8
), this implies

0<\lambda\leq\frac{2}{c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4}}.

Similarly, when considering (
2.10
), we obtain

\lambda\geq\frac{2}{c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4}}.

It follows that
\displaystyle\lambda=\frac{2}{c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4}}
. This is in contradiction with
\lambda
arbitrarily taken in
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
, so we have proved that the limit exists. Now, let

\ell:=\lim_{\xi\to-\infty}\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}.

By Lemma
2.1

(iv)
, we notice that
0\leq\ell\leq+\infty
. Due to
l’Hôspital role, we have that

\lim_{\xi\to-\infty}\frac{1}{\eta(\xi)}\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}\eta(s)\beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\lim_{\xi\to-\infty}\frac{\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)}{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}=\frac{1}{\ell}

with the obvious meaning if
\ell=0^{+}
or
\ell=+\infty
. By (
2.4
)

\ell=\lim_{\xi\to-\infty}\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}=\lim_{\xi\to-\infty}-c+\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}\eta(s)\beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}{\eta(\xi)}=-c+\frac{1}{\ell}.

This proves that
\ell\in(0,+\infty)
and
\ell^{2}+c\ell-1=0
. Condition
(i)
is then satisfied.

To prove
(ii)
, we assume, by a contradiction, that such a limit does not exist and assume the existence of
b_{1},b_{2}
satisfying

b_{1}:=\liminf_{\xi\to-\infty}\beta^{\prime}(\xi)<\limsup_{\xi\to-\infty}\beta^{\prime}(\xi)=:b_{2}.

By Remark
2.2
, we notice that
\beta^{\prime}(\xi)<0
for all
\xi\in(-\infty,\tau)
. This implies
-\infty\leq b_{1}<b_{2}\leq 0
. In addition
b_{2}=0
since, otherwise, we could find
\varepsilon>0
and
\xi_{0}<0
such that
\beta^{\prime}(\xi)<-\varepsilon,
for
\xi\leq\xi_{0}
in contradiction with the boundedness of
\beta
. Due to (
1.4a
), we have that

Db_{1}=\liminf_{\xi\to-\infty}D\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)<\limsup_{\xi\to-\infty}D\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)=0.

Hence, for every
\lambda\in(Db_{1},0)
, there exists a sequence
(\xi_{n})_{n}\subset(-\infty,\tau)
with
\xi_{n}\to-\infty
as

such that

D\beta(\xi_{n})\beta^{\prime}(\xi_{n})=\lambda,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left(D\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)\right)^{\prime}\Big|_{\xi=\xi_{n}}<0,\qquad\forall n.

By writing (

) as

D\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)=\frac{c(1-\beta(\xi))}{\eta(\xi)}-\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}-c,\quad\xi\in(-\infty,\tau),

(2.11)

we have, in the whole interval

\left(D\beta\beta^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}=-\frac{c\beta^{\prime}}{\eta}-\frac{c(1-\beta)}{\eta}\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\eta}-\frac{\eta^{\prime\prime}}{\eta}+\left(\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\eta}\right)^{2}.

According to (
1.3a
), in
(-\infty,\tau)
, we obtain

\left(D\beta\beta^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}=-\frac{c\beta^{\prime}}{\eta}-\frac{c(1-\beta)}{\eta}\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\eta}+\frac{c\eta^{\prime}}{\eta}-\beta+\left(\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\eta}\right)^{2}.

(2.12)

Moreover, if we restrict to the values of
(\xi_{n})_{n},
thanks to (

), we have

\beta^{\prime}(\xi_{n})=\frac{\lambda}{D\beta(\xi_{n})},\qquad\frac{c(1-\beta(\xi_{n}))}{\eta(\xi_{n})}=\lambda+\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi_{n})}{\eta(\xi_{n})}+c.

Therefore, by (
2.12
), it follows

\begin{split}0&>\left(D\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)\right)^{\prime}\Big|_{\xi=\xi_{n}}\\
&=-\frac{c\lambda}{D\eta(\xi_{n})\beta(\xi_{n})}-\left[\lambda+\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi_{n})}{\eta(\xi_{n})}+c\right]\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi_{n})}{\eta(\xi_{n})}+\frac{c\eta^{\prime}(\xi_{n})}{\eta(\xi_{n})}-\beta(\xi_{n})+\left(\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi_{n})}{\eta(\xi_{n})}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Due to property
(i)
and (
1.4a
), when passing to the limit as
n\to+\infty
, we have that

0\geq\lim_{n\to+\infty}D(\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi))^{\prime}|_{\xi=\xi_{n}}=+\infty,

which is a contradiction. Thus, the limit
\beta^{\prime}(-\infty)
exists and is necessarily
0
, so
(ii)
is proved.

Property
(iii)
follows as an immediate consequence of
(i)
and
(ii)
, thanks to (
2.11
).

Proposition 2.6
.

Every wavefront
(\eta,\beta)
of (
1.1b
) with wave speed
c
satisfies:

\eta(\xi)\geq L(c)\left(1-\beta(\xi)\right),\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R},

(2.13)

where
\displaystyle L(c):=\frac{c^{2}+c\sqrt{c^{2}+4}}{2+c^{2}+c\sqrt{c^{2}+4}}
.

Proof.

Firstly, we claim that

\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}\leq\frac{2}{c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4}},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.14)

To prove this assertion, we observe that the function
{\eta^{\prime}}/{\eta}
is always positive. Next, we find its critical points and, by referring to equation (
1.3a
), we deduce

\left(\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\eta}\right)^{\prime}(\xi)=0\Leftrightarrow\left(\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}\right)^{2}+c\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}-\beta(\xi)=0.

This condition is met when

\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}=\frac{2\beta(\xi)}{c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4\beta(\xi)}}.

(2.15)

Given that the function

\varphi(v)=\frac{2v}{c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4v}},\quad v\in[0,1],

satisfies
\varphi^{\prime}(v)>0
for all
v\in[0,1]
, it follows that

\frac{2\beta(\xi)}{c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4\beta(\xi)}}\leq\frac{2}{c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4}},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.16)

Employing Lemma
2.5

(i)
and noting that

\lim_{\xi\to+\infty}\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}=0,

we conclude from (
2.15
) and (
2.16
) that the claim (
2.14
) is valid.

Applying equation (
2.7
), we derive that

\frac{1-\beta(\xi)}{\eta(\xi)}=1+\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{c\eta(\xi)}+\frac{D\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)}{c}<1+\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\xi)}{c\eta(\xi)}\leq 1+\frac{2}{c^{2}+c\sqrt{c^{2}+4}},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R},

which completes the proof.
∎

The following result provides a positive lower bound for wavefront speeds of the system (

Proposition 2.7
.

Every wavefront
(\eta,\beta)
of (
1.1b
) with wave speed

satisfies:

c>\max\left\{0,\sqrt{{D}/{15}}-1\right\}.

Proof.

Firstly, we multiply (
1.3b
) for
\beta
and we integrate in
(\xi_{0},\xi_{1})\subset(-\infty,\tau)
, so that we obtain

\begin{split}0=&\int_{\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{1}}\left(D\eta(s)\beta(s)\beta^{\prime}(s)\right)^{\prime}\beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+c\int_{\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{1}}\beta(s)\beta^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{1}}\eta(s)\beta^{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
=&D\eta(\xi_{1})\beta^{2}(\xi_{1})\beta^{\prime}(\xi_{1})-D\eta(\xi_{0})\beta^{2}(\xi_{0})\beta^{\prime}(\xi_{0})-\int_{\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{1}}D\eta(s)\beta(s)\beta^{\prime 2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&+c\left(\frac{\beta(\xi_{1})^{2}}{2}-\frac{\beta(\xi_{0})^{2}}{2}\right)+\int_{\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{1}}\eta(s)\beta^{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

By letting
\xi_{0}\to-\infty
and
\xi_{1}\to\tau
, and using the boundary conditions (
1.4a
) along with Lemma
2.1
, we have

-\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}D\eta(s)\beta(s)\beta^{\prime 2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s-\frac{c}{2}+\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\eta(s)\beta^{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=0.

(2.17)

By using Lemma
2.1

\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\eta(s)\beta^{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\eta(s)\beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=c,

and so from (

\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}D\eta(s)\beta(s)\beta^{\prime 2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{c}{2}.

(2.18)

Next, we multiply (
1.3b
) for

and we integrate in
(\xi_{0},\xi_{1})\subset(-\infty,+\infty)

\begin{split}0=&\int_{\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{1}}\left(D\eta(s)\beta(s)\beta^{\prime}(s)\right)^{\prime}D\eta(s)\beta(s)\beta^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+c\int_{\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{1}}\beta^{\prime}(s)D\eta(s)\beta(s)\beta^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\\

&+\int_{\xi_{0}}^{\xi_{1}}D\beta^{\prime}(s)\eta^{2}(s)\beta^{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

By letting
\xi_{0}\to-\infty
and
\xi_{1}\to\tau

c\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}D\eta(s)\beta(s)\beta^{\prime 2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}D\beta^{\prime}(s)\eta^{2}(s)\beta^{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=0.

\frac{c^{2}}{2}\geq-\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}D\beta^{\prime}(s)\eta^{2}(s)\beta^{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

2.6

\begin{split}\frac{c^{2}}{2}&\geq\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}D\left(-\beta^{\prime}(s)\right)\eta^{2}(s)\beta^{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\\

with

\xi\to+\infty

\lim_{\xi\to+\infty}\eta^{\prime}(\xi)\geq\lim_{\xi\to+\infty}\frac{\eta_{0}l}{c}(1-e^{-c\xi})=\frac{\eta_{0}l}{c}>0,

which is in contradiction with the boundedness of
\eta(+\infty)
stated in Proposition
2.11
. Hence
\beta(+\infty)=0
. It thus remains to show that
\eta(+\infty)=1.

According to Remark
2.2
and Proposition
2.11
, notice that the limit
\eta(+\infty)
exists and it is finite. Moreover, by (
2.5
) and the boundedness of
\eta
, we get also that
\eta^{\prime}(+\infty)
exists and it is necessarily
0
.
From (
2.7
), we deduce that

\lim_{\xi\to+\infty}D\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)=c\left(1-\eta(+\infty)\right).

If
\eta(+\infty)<1
, then
D\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)>0
for sufficiently large values of
\xi
, which is in contradiction with the sign of the function
D\eta\beta\beta^{\prime}
. On the other hand, if
\eta(+\infty)>1
, then
\beta^{\prime}(+\infty)=-\infty
, which is in contradiction with the boundedness of
\beta
. Hence
\eta(+\infty)=1
and case
(i)
is proved.

Case
(ii)
. Assume
\tau<+\infty
hence implying that
(\eta,\beta)
is a semi-wavefront of (
1.1b
) in
(-\infty,\tau)
. Since
\eta\in C^{1}(-\infty,\tau]
, we obtain from (

) that

has a finite limit, when

and

\lambda:=\lim_{\xi\to\tau^{-}}D\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)=c-\eta^{\prime}(\tau)-c\eta(\tau).

Moreover, by Remark
2.2
, we have
\lambda\leq 0
. Hence,
\eta^{\prime}(\tau)=c-\lambda-c\eta(\tau)
and the function
\eta
satisfies
\eta^{\prime\prime}+c\eta^{\prime}=0
in the half-line
(\tau,+\infty)
. Therefore

\eta(\xi)=1-\frac{\lambda}{c}+\left(\frac{\lambda}{c}-1+\eta(\tau)\right)e^{-c(\xi-\tau)},\qquad\xi\geq\tau.

As a consequence
\eta(+\infty)=1
if and only if
\lambda=0
. In this case
(\eta,\beta)
satisfies conditions (
2.27
) in the half-line
(\tau,+\infty)
.
Now we prove that, when extended as in (
2.27
) on
(\tau,+\infty)
, the couple
(\eta,\beta)
is a solution of (
1.3b
)–(
1.4b
). Indeed,
\beta\in C^{2}(-\infty,\tau)
(see Remark
2.4
) with
\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)\to 0
as
\xi\to\tau^{-}
(see Lemma
2.1

(ii)
), hence
\beta\beta^{\prime}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\tau-\epsilon,\tau+\epsilon)
with
\epsilon>0
and
\delta>0
such that
\tau-\delta>\tau-\epsilon
. It holds

\begin{split}\int^{\tau+\epsilon}_{\tau-\epsilon}&\left[\left(D\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)+c\beta(\xi)\right)\psi^{\prime}(\xi)-\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\psi(\xi)\right]\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int^{\tau-\delta}_{\tau-\epsilon}\left[\left(D\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)+c\beta(\xi)\right)\psi^{\prime}(\xi)-\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\psi(\xi)\right]\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\left[D\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)\psi(\xi)+c\beta(\xi)\psi(\xi)\right]^{\tau-\delta}_{\tau-\epsilon}\\
&\qquad-\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int^{\tau-\delta}_{\tau-\epsilon}\left[\left(D\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)\right)^{\prime}+c\beta^{\prime}(\xi)+\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\right]\psi(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\lim_{\delta\to 0}D\eta(\tau-\delta)\beta(\tau-\delta)\beta^{\prime}(\tau-\delta)\psi(\tau-\delta)=D\eta(\tau)\beta(\tau)\beta^{\prime}(\tau)=0.\end{split}

since
D\eta\beta\beta^{\prime}\to 0
as
\xi\to\tau^{-}
.

It remains to show that
(\eta,\beta)
is a sharp wavefront in
0
. To this aim, we only need to exclude the case when
\beta^{\prime}(\tau^{-})=0
. We reason by contradiction and assume that
\beta^{\prime}(\tau^{-})=0
. Let
\bar{\beta}:=\beta(\bar{\xi})>0
for some

. We have that

\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\tau=\bar{\xi}+\int_{\bar{\beta}}^{0}\dot{\xi}(\beta)\,\mathrm{d}\beta=&\bar{\xi}+\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\beta}}^{0}\frac{\mathrm{d}\beta}{\beta^{\prime}(\xi(\beta))}\\
\\
\displaystyle=&\bar{\xi}+\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\beta}}^{0}\frac{D\beta N(\beta)}{z(\beta)}\,\mathrm{d}\beta=\bar{\xi}+\int_{0}^{\bar{\beta}}\frac{DN(\beta)}{-\frac{z(\beta)}{\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}\beta,\end{array}

with
\xi(\beta),

N(\beta)
, and

defined in (

) and (

).

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,c)
. We claim that there is
\beta_{0}\in(0,\bar{\beta})
such that

-\frac{z(\beta)}{\beta}<\frac{D\eta^{2}(\tau)}{c-\varepsilon}\beta,\quad 0<\beta<\beta_{0}.

(2.28)

By (
1.4a
) and Lemma

(v)

0<\bar{\eta}:=\eta(\bar{\xi})\leq N(\beta)\leq N(0)=\eta(\tau)<1
for

. Hence, if

) is true then

\tau>\bar{\xi}+\int_{0}^{\beta_{0}}\frac{DN(\beta)}{-\frac{z(\beta)}{\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}\beta>\int_{0}^{\beta_{0}}\frac{N(\beta)(c-\varepsilon)}{\eta^{2}(\tau)\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\beta>\int_{0}^{\beta_{0}}\frac{\bar{\eta}(c-\varepsilon)}{\eta^{2}(\tau)\beta}\,\mathrm{d}\beta=+\infty.

Hence, when (

) is valid, the case

and
\beta^{\prime}(\tau^{-})=0
produces a contradiction, and then it is not possible. Notice that
\beta^{\prime}(\tau^{-})<0
(see Lemma
2.9
) and hence the corresponding solution is sharp. Now we show (
2.28
) using a comparison-type argument applied to problem (
2.24c
). Let

\gamma(\beta):=-\frac{D\eta^{2}(\tau)}{c-\varepsilon}\beta^{2},\quad 0<\beta<1,

with
\varepsilon
as above. Since

\dot{\gamma}(\beta)=-2\frac{D\eta^{2}(\tau)}{c-\varepsilon}\beta\to 0,\quad\text{as }\beta\to 0^{+},

while

-c-\frac{DN^{2}(\beta)\beta^{2}}{\gamma(\beta)}=-c+(c-\varepsilon)\frac{N^{2}(\beta)}{\eta^{2}(\tau)}<-c+c-\varepsilon=-\varepsilon,

there is
\tilde{\beta}\in(0,1)
such that

\dot{\gamma}(\beta)>-c-\frac{DN^{2}(\beta)\beta^{2}}{\gamma(\beta)},\quad 0<\beta<\tilde{\beta}.

Then,
\gamma(\beta)

2.24a
) in
(0,\tilde{\beta})

\tilde{\beta}<\bar{\beta}

\beta^{\prime}(\tau^{-})=0

0=\lim_{\xi\to\tau^{-}}\beta^{\prime}(\xi)=\lim_{\beta\to 0^{+}}\beta^{\prime}(\xi(\beta))=\lim_{\beta\to 0^{+}}\frac{z(\beta)}{DN(\beta)\beta}

implying

\lim_{\beta\to 0^{+}}\frac{z(\beta)}{\beta}=0,

since

when

. Corresponding to a decreasing sequence

such that
\beta_{n}\to 0
as

with
0<\sigma_{n}<\beta_{n}
for all

satisfying

\frac{z(\beta_{n})}{\beta_{n}}=\dot{z}(\sigma_{n})=-c-\frac{DN^{2}(\sigma_{n})\sigma_{n}^{2}}{z(\sigma_{n})}\to 0,\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty.

Therefore
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{DN^{2}(\sigma_{n})\sigma_{n}^{2}}{-z(\sigma_{n})}=c
and hence there is
\tilde{n}
such that

\frac{DN^{2}(\sigma_{n})\sigma_{n}^{2}}{-z(\sigma_{n})}>c-\varepsilon,\quad\text{for }n\geq\tilde{n},

again with

as above. This implies

We choose

such that
\sigma_{n_{0}}<\tilde{\beta}
. Notice that

; since, moreover,
\gamma
is and upper-solution to (

) in
(0,\sigma_{n_{0}})\subset(0,\tilde{\beta})
we obtain (see, e.g,
[

) that

for

and (

) is proved with

.
∎

3
Semi-wavefront on the negative half-line

In this section, we demonstrate Theorem
1.1
by proving the existence of a semi-wavefront of (
1.1b
) with positive speed
c
in the half-line
(-\infty,0]
(as will be shown in Proposition
3.1
) and its uniqueness (as will be shown in Proposition
3.10
).

Proposition 3.1
.

For every
c>0
, there exists a semi-wavefront
(\eta,\beta)
of (
1.1b
) in
(-\infty,0]
with wave speed
c
. Moreover,
\eta^{\prime}(\xi)>0
and
\beta^{\prime}(\xi)<0
for all
\xi\in(-\infty,0]
.

The proof is based on comparison results, i.e., we use upper and lower functions (cf.,
[
14
]
). Let
-\infty\leq a_{-}<a_{+}\leq+\infty
and let
f\colon(a_{-},a_{+})\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. We say that
y
is a
lower function
of
u^{\prime\prime}=f(\xi,u,u^{\prime})
if
y\in C^{2}(a_{-},a_{+})
and
y^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\geq f\left(\xi,y(\xi),y^{\prime}(\xi)\right)
for every
\xi\in(a_{-},a_{+})
. If the
\geq
sign is replaced by
\leq
, we say that
y
is an
upper function
.

We first investigate the solvability of (
1.3a
) in
(-\infty,0]
with positive
c
and sufficiently regular
\beta
and establish some necessary conditions for the existence of semi-wavefronts of (
1.1b
) on the half-line
(-\infty,0]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume
\beta\in C(-\infty,0]
satisfying
0<m\leq\beta(\xi)\leq M<+\infty
for all
\xi\in(-\infty,0]
. For every
c>0
and
\eta_{0}\in(0,1)
equation (
1.3a
) has a unique solution such that
\eta^{\prime}(\xi)>0
for all
\xi\in(-\infty,0]
and satisfies
\eta(0)=\eta_{0}
and
\eta(-\infty)=\eta^{\prime}(-\infty)=0.

Proof.

Let

S_{1}(\xi):=\eta_{0}e^{\nu_{1}\xi}\text{ with }\nu_{1}:=\frac{2m}{c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4m}},\quad\text{for }\xi\in(-\infty,0],

and

S_{2}(\xi):=\eta_{0}e^{\nu_{2}\xi}\text{ with }\nu_{2}:=\frac{2M}{c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4M}},\quad\text{for }\xi\in(-\infty,0].

Notice that
S_{1}
is the solution of

\begin{cases}\eta^{\prime\prime}(\xi)+c\eta^{\prime}(\xi)-m\eta(\xi)=0,\\
\eta(0)=\eta_{0},\quad\eta(-\infty)=0,\end{cases}

and also an upper function of (
1.3a
). In fact,
S_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)=-cS_{1}^{\prime}(\xi)+mS_{1}(\xi)\leq-cS_{1}^{\prime}(\xi)+\beta(\xi)S_{1}(\xi),
for all

Analogously,
S_{2}
is a lower function of (
1.3a
), since
S_{2}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)=-cS_{2}^{\prime}(\xi)+MS_{2}(\xi)\geq-cS_{2}^{\prime}(\xi)+\beta(\xi)S_{2}(\xi),
for all
\xi\in(-\infty,0].

Therefore, from the theory of upper and lower functions
[
14
]
, a solution
\eta
to (
1.3a
) exists satisfying

S_{2}(\xi)\leq\eta(\xi)\leq S_{1}(\xi),\quad\forall\xi\in(-\infty,0].

(3.1)

Moreover, by integrating (
1.3a
) in
[\xi,0],
with
\xi<0

2.3
).
Hence,
\eta^{\prime}(\xi)
has limit when

\eta^{\prime}(-\infty)=0

is bounded. In particular, we have that

(3.2)

It remains to prove that (
1.3a
) has a unique solution
\eta
satisfying
\eta(\xi_{0})=\eta_{0}
and
\eta(-\infty)=0
. We reason by contradiction and assume the existence of

solutions to (
1.3a
) with
\eta_{1}(0)=\eta_{2}(0)=\eta_{0}
and
\eta_{1}(-\infty)=\eta_{2}(-\infty)=0
. Since
\eta_{1}^{\prime}(0)\neq\eta_{2}^{\prime}(0)

\eta_{1}^{\prime}(0)>\eta_{2}^{\prime}(0)

0>\eta_{2}^{\prime}(0)-\eta_{1}^{\prime}(0)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left[\eta_{2}(s)-\eta_{1}(s)\right]\beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Hence
\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}<0
in some interval

. The relation between
\eta_{2}^{\prime}(0)
and
\eta_{1}^{\prime}(0)
implies the existence of

such that
\eta_{1}(\xi_{1})=\eta_{2}(\xi_{1})

(3.3)

Therefore, by applying (

to both

0<\int_{\xi_{1}}^{0}\left[\eta_{2}(s)-\eta_{1}(s)\right]\beta(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\eta_{1}^{\prime}(0)-\eta_{2}^{\prime}(0)=-\eta_{2}^{\prime}(\xi_{1})+\eta_{1}^{\prime}(\xi_{1}),

in contradiction with (

At last, if there is

This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.11

For every

\begin{cases}\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)=\dfrac{1}{D\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)},\quad\xi\in(-\infty,0],\\

\begin{split}&p(u):=-\frac{\eta^{\prime}\left(\Phi^{-1}(u)\right)}{c}-\eta\left(\Phi^{-1}(u)\right),\\

\begin{cases}\dot{p}(u)=-\frac{D}{c}q^{2}(u)\left(p(u)+r(u)+1\right)^{2},\\

Proof of Proposition

Consequently, the eigenvalues of

Notice that a solution of (

, we prove that the center manifold is unique. Let us consider two center manifolds

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial k_{i}}{\partial p}(p(u),q(u))\Big(-\frac{D}{c}q(u)^{2}\left(p(u)+k_{i}(p(u),q(u))+1\right)^{2}\Big)\\

Since,

It remains to show that the system

q\in(0,q_{0})
. On the other hand, we compute

\begin{split}\dot{p}_{1}(q)-&\dot{p}_{2}(q)=\frac{q}{c^{2}}\left[\frac{\left(p_{1}(q)+k\left(p_{1}(q),q\right)+1\right)}{p_{1}(q)+q}-\frac{\left(p_{2}(q)+k\left(p_{2}(q),q\right)+1\right)}{p_{2}(q)+q}\right]\\
&=\frac{q}{c^{2}}\left[\frac{\left(p_{2}(q)-p_{1}(q)\right)(1-q)+k\left(p_{1}(q),q\right)\left(p_{2}(q)+q\right)-k\left(p_{2}(q),q\right)\left(p_{1}(q)+q\right)}{\left(p_{1}(q)+q\right)\left(p_{2}(q)+q\right)}\right].\end{split}

By adding the term
\pm(p_{2}(q)+q)k_{2}(p_{2}(q),q)
to the numerator on the left-hand side and dividing both sides by
\left(p_{1}(q)-p_{2}(q)\right)
, we obtain

\frac{\dot{p}_{1}(q)-\dot{p}_{2}(q)}{p_{1}(q)-p_{2}(q)}=A(q)\left[q-1-k\left(p_{2}(q),q\right))+\left(p_{2}(q)+q\right)\frac{k\left(p_{1}(q),q\right)-k\left(p_{2}(q),q\right)}{p_{1}(q)-p_{2}(q)}\right],

where

A(q)=\frac{q}{c^{2}\left(p_{1}(q)+q\right)\left(p_{2}(q)+q\right)\left(p_{1}(q)-p_{2}(q)\right)}>0,\quad\forall q\in(0,1).

We claim that

\lim_{q\to 0^{+}}q-1-k\left(p_{2}(q),q\right))+\left(p_{2}(q)+q\right)\frac{k\left(p_{1}(q),q\right)-k\left(p_{2}(q),q\right)}{p_{1}(q)-p_{2}(q)}=-1.

Once the claim is proved, since
p_{1}(q)-p_{2}(q)>0
for every
q\in(0,q_{0})
, it follows that there is
q_{1}\in(0,q_{0})
such that
\dot{p}_{1}(q)-\dot{p}_{2}(q)<0
for all
q\in(0,q_{1})
. This implies that
p_{1}-p_{2}
is decreasing in
(0,q_{1})
and so
p_{1}(q)-p_{2}(q)>p_{1}(q_{1})-p_{2}(q_{1})
for
q<q_{1}
, leading to a contradiction since
\lim_{q\to 0^{+}}p_{1}(q)-p_{2}(q)=0
.

To prove the claim, notice that

\lim_{q\to 0^{+}}(q-1-k(p_{2}(q),q))=-1,

since
k(0,0)=0
. Given that
p_{2}(q)+q<0
, for all
q\in(0,1)
, it remains to prove that

\lim_{q\to 0^{+}}\frac{k(p_{1}(q),q)-k(p_{2}(q),q)}{p_{1}(q)-p_{2}(q)}=0.

(3.17)

Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. Since
\frac{\partial}{\partial p}k(0,0)=0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that for all
|p|<\delta
and
|q|<\delta
, we have

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial p}k(p,q)\right|<\varepsilon.

(3.18)

Given that
\lim_{q\to 0^{+}}p_{1}(q)=0=\lim_{q\to 0^{+}}p_{2}(q)
, there exists
\delta_{1}=\delta_{1}(\varepsilon)>0
with
\delta_{1}<\delta
such that
|q|<\delta_{1}
implies
|p_{i}(q)|\leq\delta
, for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Therefore, from (
3.18
), it follows that

\left|\frac{k(p_{1}(q),q)-k(p_{2}(q),q)}{p_{1}(q)-p_{2}(q)}\right|=\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial p}k(p(q),q)\right|<\varepsilon,

for some
p(q)
between
p_{1}(q)
and
p_{2}(q)
by the Mean Value Theorem. This proves (
3.17
) and completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The thesis follows by Propositions
3.1
and
3.10
.
∎

4
Existence of wavefront solutions

In this section, we aim to demonstrate Theorem
1.2
.
To proceed with the proof of the theorem, we first consider the semi-wavefront
(\eta,\beta)
of (
1.1b
) in
(-\infty,0]
with wave speed
c>0
, whose unique existence is established by Theorem
1.1
. Next, we extend this semi-wavefront to the interval
[0,\tau)
, where
\tau
is defined as in (
2.2
). Notably,
\eta^{\prime}>0
and
\beta^{\prime}<0
hold on
(-\infty,\tau)
(see Lemma
2.1
and Remark
2.2
), allowing us to define
z_{c}(\beta)
for
0<\beta<1
, as described in (
2.23
), and consider problem (
2.24c
).

Secondly, we introduce the following auxiliary first-order boundary value problem:

\displaystyle\dot{w}(\beta)=-\sigma-\frac{g(\beta)}{w(\beta)},\quad 0<\beta<2,

(4.1a)

\displaystyle\dot{w}(\beta)<0,\quad 0<\beta<2,

(4.1b)

\displaystyle w(0)=w(2)=0,

(4.1c)

where
\dot{w}=\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{\mathrm{d}\beta}
and
g\colon[0,2]\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function satisfying:
g(s)=De^{D}s^{2}
for all
s\in[0,1]
,
0<{g(s)}/{s}<De^{D}
for all
s\in(1,2)
, and
g(2)=0
. We denote with
w_{\sigma}
the solution of (
4.1c
) associated with the parameter
\sigma
and recall the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

(see
[
6
, Theorem 2.6]
and
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
)
There exists a positive constant
\sigma^{*}
satisfying
0<\sigma^{*}\leq 2\sqrt{De^{D}}
such that problem (
4.1c
) has a unique solution if and only if
\sigma\geq\sigma^{*}
. Moreover
\dot{w}_{\sigma^{*}}(0)=-\sigma^{*}
and
\dot{w}_{c}(0)=0
for
c>\sigma^{*}
.

Lastly, in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we make use of the following comparison result between solutions of problems (
2.24c
) and (
4.1c
). For what follows, we recall the definitions of the values
\eta_{0},\eta_{0}^{\prime}
,
\beta_{0}
, and
\beta_{0}^{\prime}
in (
2.1
).

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
w_{\sigma^{*}}
be the solution of problem (
4.1c
) associated with the parameter
\sigma^{*}
. Then, for every
c\geq\sigma^{*}
the solution
z_{c}
of (
2.24c
) satisfies

w_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta)<z_{c}(\beta)<0,\quad\forall\beta\in(0,\beta_{0}].

(4.2)

Proof.

Using the threshold speed
\sigma^{*}
from Proposition
4.1
for problem (
4.1c
), let us define

\mu:=-\max_{\tilde{\beta}\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]}w_{\sigma^{*}}(\tilde{\beta}).

We observe that
\mu<\frac{\sigma^{*}}{2}
. Indeed, from (
4.1a
), we obtain that

\dot{w}_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta)=-\sigma^{*}+\frac{g(\beta)}{-w_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta)}>-\sigma^{*},\quad 0<\beta<2.

Therefore, by integrating in
[0,\beta]
, we obtain

w_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta)=w_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta)-w_{\sigma^{*}}(0)=\int_{0}^{\beta}\dot{w}_{\sigma^{*}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s>-\sigma^{*}\beta,

which implies
\max_{\beta\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]}w_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta)>-\frac{\sigma^{*}}{2}.

We take
c\geq\sigma^{*}
and set

\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(c):=\frac{\mu}{c+\sigma_{2}},

(4.3)

with
\sigma_{2}
defined as in (
3.5
).
First of all, we notice that
\eta_{0}\in\left(0,\frac{c}{c+\sigma_{2}}\right)
since
\mu<\frac{\sigma^{*}}{2}<c
. Then, from Proposition
3.4
, we obtain
\beta_{0}>1-\frac{\eta_{0}(c+\sigma_{2})}{c}=1-\frac{\mu}{c}>1-\frac{\sigma^{*}}{2c}.
Thus, since
c\geq\sigma^{*}
,

\frac{1}{2}<\beta_{0}<1.

(4.4)

Next, we divide the proof into two parts. First, we claim that
z_{c}(\beta_{0})>w_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta_{0})
. To this purpose, we consider
z_{c}(\beta_{0})=DN(\beta_{0})\beta_{0}\beta^{\prime}\left(\xi(\beta_{0})\right)=D\eta_{0}\beta_{0}\beta^{\prime}_{0},

and, by using formula (
2.7
), we obtain

z_{c}(\beta_{0})=D\eta_{0}\beta_{0}\beta^{\prime}_{0}=c(1-\beta_{0})-\eta_{0}^{\prime}-c\eta_{0}>-\eta_{0}^{\prime}-c\eta_{0}.

Since
\eta_{0}^{\prime}\leq\eta_{0}\sigma_{2}
via (
3.6
), we get

z_{c}(\beta_{0})>-\eta_{0}^{\prime}-c\eta_{0}\geq-\eta_{0}(c+\sigma_{2})=-\frac{\mu}{c+\sigma_{2}}(c+\sigma_{2})=-\mu.

Hence,

z_{c}(\beta_{0})>-\mu=\max_{\beta\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]}w_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta)\geq w_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta_{0}),

where the last inequality follows from (
4.4
). Thus, the claim is proved.

As for the second part, we suppose by contradiction that there exists a
\beta_{1}\in(0,\beta_{0})
such that
z_{c}(\beta_{1})=w_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta_{1})
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
z_{c}(\beta)>w_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta)
for
\beta\in(\beta_{1},\beta_{0}]
. Since
\beta_{1}>0
, by Proposition
2.11
we have

N(\beta_{1})=\eta(\xi(\beta_{1}))<\eta(\tau)\leq\sqrt{e^{D}}.

Therefore,
DN^{2}(\beta_{1})\beta_{1}^{2}<De^{D}\beta_{1}^{2}=g(\beta_{1})
. Since
c\geq\sigma^{*}
,

\dot{z}_{c}(\beta_{1})-\dot{w}_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta_{1})=-c+\frac{DN^{2}(\beta_{1})\beta_{1}^{2}}{-z_{c}(\beta_{1})}+\sigma^{*}-\frac{g(\beta_{1})}{-w_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta_{1})}<-c+\sigma^{*}\leq 0.

Therefore, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
z_{c}(\beta)-w_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta)<0
, for all
\beta\in(\beta_{1},\beta_{1}+\varepsilon)
. This contradicts the condition
z_{c}>w_{\sigma^{*}}
in
(\beta_{1},\beta_{0}]
. At last the existence of
\beta_{2}\in(0,\beta_{0})
such that
z_{c}(\beta_{2})=0
implies
\dot{z}_{c}(\beta_{2})=+\infty
, in contradiction with
z_{c}<0
in
(\beta_{2},\beta_{0})
. Hence, (
4.2
) is satisfied, and the proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let us define
c_{0}:=\sigma^{*},
where
\sigma^{*}
is the threshold speed associated to (
4.1c
) (see Proposition
4.1
). Consider the semi-wavefront
(\eta,\beta)
of (
1.1b
) in
(-\infty,0]
with wave speed
c\geq c_{0}
(see Theorem
1.1
) and extend it to
[0,\tau)
.

We consider two cases for
\tau
. If
\tau=+\infty
, then
(\eta,\beta)
is a classical wavefront of (
1.1b
) with wave speed
c
by Proposition (
2.12
)
(i)
. On the contrary, if
\tau<+\infty
, we consider the function
z_{c}
defined in (
2.23
) which satisfies problem (
2.24c
). By Proposition (
4.2
) and Lemma
2.1
, we have that

0\geq\lim_{\xi\to\tau^{-}}D\eta(\xi)\beta(\xi)\beta^{\prime}(\xi)=\lim_{\beta\to 0^{+}}z_{c}(\beta)\geq\lim_{\beta\to 0^{+}}w_{\sigma^{*}}(\beta)=0;

hence,
(\eta,\beta)
is a wavefront of (
1.1b
) with wave speed
c
by Proposition
2.12

(ii)
.

We now show that
\tau<+\infty
is impossible when
c>c_{0}
. Indeed, let
c>c_{0}
. Notice that
\dot{z}_{c}(0)
exists (see
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
) and it is
\dot{z}_{c}(0)=0
or
\dot{z}_{c}(0)=-c
. Since
\dot{w}_{\sigma^{*}}=-\sigma^{*}
(see Proposition
4.1
) the latter case is in contradiction with inequality (
4.2
). Hence
\dot{z}_{c}(0)=0
and then, by (
2.23
), we obtain

\lim_{\xi\to\tau^{-}}\beta^{\prime}(\xi)=\lim_{\beta\to 0^{+}}\frac{z_{c}(\beta)}{D\beta\eta(\xi(\beta))}=0,

which is not compatible with
\tau<+\infty
(as showed in the proof of Proposition
2.12

(ii)
).

Finally, we will refer to Propositions
2.7
and
4.1
for comparison estimates of
c_{0}
. This completes the proof.
∎

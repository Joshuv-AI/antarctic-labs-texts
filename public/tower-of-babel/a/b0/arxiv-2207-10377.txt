1.
Introduction and main results

In this paper, our first aim is to study the existence and
non-existence of normalized solutions to the lower critical
autonomous Choquard equation with a local term

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\lambda u=\gamma(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}})|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}-2}u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u,\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx=c^{2},\end{cases}

where
N\geq 1
,
\gamma,\mu,c>0
,
2<q\leq 2+\frac{4}{N}
,

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is an unknown parameter that appears as a
Lagrange multiplier,
\alpha\in(0,N)
, and
I_{\alpha}
is the
Riesz potential defined for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
by

I_{\alpha}(x):=\frac{A_{\alpha}(N)}{|x|^{N-\alpha}},\ A_{\alpha}(N):=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})\pi^{N/2}2^{\alpha}}

with
\Gamma
denoting the Gamma function (see
[
34
]
,
P.19).

One motivation driving the search for normalized solutions to
(
1.1
) is the nonlinear equation

(1.2)

i\partial_{t}\psi-\Delta\psi-\gamma(I_{\alpha}\ast|\psi|^{p})|\psi|^{p-2}\psi-\mu|\psi|^{q-2}\psi=0,\ (t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\frac{N+\alpha}{N}\leq p\leq\frac{N+\alpha}{(N-2)^{+}}
and

2<q\leq\frac{2N}{(N-2)^{+}}
. The equation (
1.2
) has several
physical origins. When
N=3
,
p=2
,
\alpha=2
and
\mu=0
,
(
1.2
) was investigated by Pekar in
[
32
]
to study
the quantum theory of a polaron at rest. In
[
22
]
,
Choquard applied it as an approximation to Hartree-Fock theory of
one component plasma. It also arises in multiple particles systems

[
12
]
and quantum mechanics
[
33
]
. When
p=2
, equation (
1.2
) reduces to the well-known Hartree
equation. The Choquard equation (
1.2
) with or without a local
perturbation has attracted much attention nowadays, see

[
3
,
5
,
7
,
11
,
25
,
28
]
for the local
existence, global existence, blow up and more in general dynamical
properties.

Searching for standing wave solution
\psi(t,x)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x)

of (
1.2
) leads to

(1.3)

-\Delta u+\lambda u=\gamma(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u,\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

When looking for solutions to (
1.3
) one choice is to fix

\lambda>0
and search for solutions to (
1.3
) as critical
points of the functional

\bar{J}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}|u|^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2p}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{p}-\frac{\mu}{q}|u|^{q}\right)dx,

see for example
[
18
,
19
,
27
,
31
]
and the references therein. Another
choice is to fix the
L^{2}
-norm of the unknown function
u
, that
is, to consider the problem

(1.4)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\lambda u=\gamma(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u,\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx=c^{2}\end{cases}

with fixed
c>0
and unknown
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. This is
meaningful from the physical point of view, as the
L^{2}
-norm is a
preserved quantity of the evolution equation (
1.2
) and the
variational characterization of such solutions is often a strong
help to analyze their orbital stability, see
[
6
]

and the references therein. In this direction, define on

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the energy functional

\hat{J}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{\gamma}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{p})|u|^{p}dx-\frac{\mu}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx.

A critical point of
\hat{J}
constrained to

S(c):=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx=c^{2}\right\}

gives rise to a solution to (
1.4
). Such solution is usually
called a normalized solution, which is the aim of this paper.

The normalized solutions to the Choquard equation have been studied
extensively in these years, see

[
2
,
8
,
9
,
14
,
20
,
26
,
40
,
41
]
and the references
therein. Recently, Li
[
15
,
16
]
studied the existence,
multiplicity, orbital stability and instability of the normalized
solutions to the upper critical Choquard equation (
1.3
),
i.e.,
p=\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2}
. As to the lower critical Choquard
equation, the lower critical exponent
p=\frac{N+\alpha}{N}
seems
to be a new feature for the Choquard equation, which is related to a
new phenomenon of “bubbling at infinity” (see
[
30
]
). So compared with the study developed for the upper
critical Choquard equation, the lower critical problem seems to be
more challenging. In a recent paper
[
39
]
, the authors
explored new methods and ideas to study the normalized solutions to
the lower critical Choquard equation, meanwhile they proposed some
open questions. In this paper, we settle parts of the open
questions.

A solution
u
to the problem (
1.1
) corresponds to a critical
point of the functional

(1.5)

\begin{split}E_{q}(u):&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{\gamma N}{2(N+\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}})|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx\\
&\qquad-\frac{\mu}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx\end{split}

restricted to the sphere
S(c)
. It is easy to see that
E_{q}\in C^{1}(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\mathbb{R})
and

\begin{split}E_{q}^{\prime}(u)\varphi&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\nabla\varphi dx-\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}})|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}-2}u\varphi dx\\
&\qquad-\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q-2}u\varphi dx,\ \text{for\ any\ }\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).\end{split}

For any
2<r<\frac{2N}{(N-2)^{+}}
, we define

\eta_{r}:=\frac{N}{2}-\frac{N}{r}
,

(1.6)

\bar{S}^{-1}:=\inf_{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{r(1-\eta_{r})}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{r\eta_{r}}{2}}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{r}dx},

and

(1.7)

S_{\alpha}:=\inf_{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}})|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx\right)^{\frac{N}{N+\alpha}}}.

The main results to the equation (
1.1
) are as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
c,\gamma,\mu>0
, and

2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}
. Then the infimum

\sigma(c):=\inf_{S(c)}E_{q}(u)<-\frac{\gamma N}{2(N+\alpha)}S_{\alpha}^{-\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}c^{\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}}

is achieved by
u_{0}\in S(c)
with the following properties:

(i)
u_{0}
is a real-valued positive function in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
which is radially symmetric and non-increasing;

(ii)
u_{0}
is a ground state of (
1.1
) with some
\lambda_{c}>\frac{\gamma N}{N+\alpha}S_{\alpha}^{-\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}c^{\frac{2\alpha}{N}}
.
A ground state
v
of (
1.1
) is defined as follows:

E_{q}|_{S(c)}^{\prime}(v)=0\ \text{and}\ E_{q}(v)=\inf\{E_{q}(w):\ w\in S(c),\ E_{q}|_{S(c)}^{\prime}(w)=0\}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
c,\gamma,\mu>0
, and

q=2+\frac{4}{N}
. If
\,0<\mu c^{4/N}\leq\frac{N+2}{N\bar{S}}
,
then (
1.1
) has no solutions.

Remark 1.3
.

(1) Theorem
1.1
was obtained in
[
39
]
under the
assumption that
\mu
is larger than some positive constant. In this
paper, by using the extremal functions of (
1.7
), we improve
their result.

(2) Theorem
1.2
was obtained in
[
39
]
under the
assumption that
0<\mu c^{4/N}<\frac{N+2}{N\bar{S}}
. In this
paper, by using the extremal functions of (
1.6
) and
(
1.7
), we can improve their result. Moreover, we give a
different proof for Theorem
1.2
and further give some
insights for other cases (see Remark
3.3
).

Our second aim is to study the multiplicity of normalized solutions
to the non-autonomous Choquard equation

(1.8)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\lambda u=(I_{\alpha}\ast[h(\epsilon x)|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}])h(\epsilon x)|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}-2}u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx=c^{2},\end{cases}

where
\epsilon>0
,
2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}
,
N,c,\mu,\lambda,\alpha,I_{\alpha}
are as in (
1.1
), and
h
satisfies
the following conditions:

(
h_{1}
)
h\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
and
0<h_{inf}:=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x)\leq\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x)=:h_{{max}}
;

(
h_{2}
)
h_{\infty}:=\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}h(x)<h_{{max}}
;

(
h_{3}
)
h^{-1}(h_{{max}})=\{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{l}\}
with
a_{1}=0
and

a_{j}\neq a_{i}
if
i\neq j
.

There are many researches concerning the non-normalized solutions to
the non-autonomous lower critical Choquard equation in these years,
see
[
17
,
30
,
42
]
and the references therein. Recently, there is a lot of work
going on with normalized solutions to the non-autonomous Choquard
equation, see
[
10
,
13
,
21
,
24
,
35
,
38
]
. However, as to our
knowledge, there are no paper considering the multiplicity of
normalized solutions to the non-autonomous lower critical Choquard
equation. In this paper, based the results obtained to (
1.1
),
and using the method of
[
1
,
4
]
, we
study this problem and obtain the following result to (
1.8
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
c,\epsilon,\mu>0
,

2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}
, and
h
satisfy (
h_{1}
)-(
h_{3}
). Then there
exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that (
1.8
) admits at least
l

couples
(u_{j},\lambda_{j})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times\mathbb{R}
of
weak solutions for
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
with

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{j}|^{2}dx=c^{2}
,
\lambda_{j}>0
and

\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u_{j})<0
for
j=1,2,\cdots,l
, where

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u):&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{\mu}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx\\
&\qquad-\frac{N}{2(N+\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast[h(\epsilon x)|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}])h(\epsilon x)|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx.\end{split}

Remark 1.5
.

The same result as Theorem
1.4
can be proved for the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\lambda u=\gamma(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}})|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}-2}u+h(\epsilon x)|u|^{q-2}u,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx=c^{2}.\end{cases}

2.
Preliminaries

The following Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality can be found in
(
[
36
]
, Theorem B).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
2<r<\frac{2N}{(N-2)^{+}}
, and
\bar{S}
be defined in
(
1.6
). Then
\bar{S}>0
and the minimizer of
\bar{S}
is

u(x)=\left(\frac{\nu_{0}}{\mu_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r-2}}w(\sqrt{\nu_{0}}(x-y)),\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\nu_{0}=\frac{4}{N(r-2)}(1-\frac{(r-2)(N-2)}{4})
,

\mu_{0}=\frac{4}{N(r-2)}
, and
w
is the unique positive radial
solution of the equation

-\Delta u+u=|u|^{r-2}u,\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

The following Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequalities can be found in
(
[
23
]
, Theorem 4.3).

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
N\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
p,r>1
with

1/p+(N-\alpha)/N+1/r=2
. Let
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
v\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then there exists a sharp constant

C(N,\alpha,p)
, independent of
u
and
v
, such that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)v(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dxdy\right|\leq C(N,\alpha,p)\|u\|_{p}\|v\|_{r}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
N\geq 1
,

, and
S_{\alpha}
be defined in
(
1.7

S_{\alpha}>0
and the minimizer of
S_{\alpha}
is

u(x)=a\left(\frac{\delta}{\delta^{2}+|x-y|^{2}}\right)^{N/2},\ a\in\mathbb{R},\ \delta>0,\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The following Brézis-Lieb-type lemma for nonlocal nonlinearities

Let

3.
The autonomous problem

3.1.
The case
2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
c,\gamma,\mu>0
, and

2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}
. Then

(1)
E_{q}
defined in (
1.5
) is bounded below and coercive on

S(c)
;

(2)
\sigma(c)<-\frac{\gamma N}{2(N+\alpha)}S_{\alpha}^{-\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}c^{\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}}
,
where
\sigma(c)
is defined in Theorem
1.1
;

(3) For
0<c_{1}<c_{2}
, there holds
\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{c_{2}^{2}}\sigma(c_{2})<\sigma(c_{1})
.

Proof.

(1) By (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), for any
u\in S(c)
, one has

E_{q}(u)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\gamma N}{2(N+\alpha)}S_{\alpha}^{-\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}c^{\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}}-\frac{\mu}{q}\bar{S}c^{q(1-\eta_{q})}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{q\eta_{q}},

which implies that
E_{q}
is bounded below and coercive on
S(c)
for

q\eta_{q}<2
.

(2) By Lemma
2.3
, we choose
v
such that

S_{\alpha}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|v|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}})|v|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx\right)^{\frac{N}{N+\alpha}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{2}dx.

Define
u:=\frac{cv}{\|v\|_{2}}
and
u_{\tau}(x):={\tau}^{N/2}u(\tau x)
for
\tau>0
, then
u_{\tau}\in S(c)
for all
\tau>0
. By direct
calculations, we deduce that

\begin{split}E_{q}(u_{\tau})&=\frac{1}{2}\tau^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\gamma N}{2(N+\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}})|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx-\frac{\mu}{q}\tau^{q\eta_{q}}\|u\|_{q}^{q}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\tau^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu}{q}\tau^{q\eta_{q}}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{\gamma N}{2(N+\alpha)}S_{\alpha}^{-\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}c^{\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}}\\
&<-\frac{\gamma N}{2(N+\alpha)}S_{\alpha}^{-\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}c^{\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}}\end{split}

for
\tau>0
small enough. This proves (2).

(3) Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset S(c_{1})
be a bounded minimizing sequence
for
\sigma(c_{1})
and let
\nu:=\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}
. Then
\nu>1
,

\{\nu u_{n}\}\subset S(c_{2})
and

\begin{split}E_{q}(\nu u_{n})&=\nu^{2}E_{q}(u_{n})+\frac{\gamma N}{2(N+\alpha)}(\nu^{2}-\nu^{\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}})|u_{n}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx\\
&\qquad+\frac{\mu}{q}(\nu^{2}-\nu^{q})\|u_{n}\|_{q}^{q},\end{split}

which implies that
E_{q}(\nu u_{n})<\nu^{2}E_{q}(u_{n})
. Consequently,

\sigma(\nu c_{1})\leq\nu^{2}\sigma(c_{1})
, where the equality holds
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}})|u_{n}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx+\|u_{n}\|_{q}^{q}\to 0\ \text{as}\ n\to+\infty.

But this is not possible, since otherwise we find that

0>\sigma(c_{1})=\lim_{n\to+\infty}E_{q}(u_{n})=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+o_{n}(1)\right)\geq 0.

Hence
\sigma(\nu c_{1})<\nu^{2}\sigma(c_{1})
, that is,

\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{c_{2}^{2}}\sigma(c_{2})<\sigma(c_{1})
. The proof is
complete.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
N\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
c,\gamma,\mu>0
, and

2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}
. Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset S(c)
be a sequence such
that
E_{q}(u_{n})\rightarrow\sigma(c)
. Then the sequence
\{u_{n}\}

is relatively compact in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
up to translations.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
3.1
(1) that
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We claim that there exits
\beta>0
such that

(3.1)

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B_{R}(y)}|u_{n}|^{2}dx\geq\beta\ \text{for\ some}\ R>0.

Suppose the contrary. Then by the Lions lemma (
[
37
]
,
Lemma 1.21),
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
2<s<\frac{2N}{(N-2)^{+}}
. Using this together with (
1.6
) and
(
1.7
) gives

\begin{split}\sigma(c)+o_{n}(1)&=E_{q}(u_{n})\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\gamma N}{2(N+\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}})|u_{n}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx+o_{n}(1)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\gamma N}{2(N+\alpha)}S_{\alpha}^{-\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}c^{\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N}}+o_{n}(1),\end{split}

which contradicts Lemma
3.1
(2). So (
3.1
) holds.

By (
3.1
), there exists
\{y_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

u_{n}(x+y_{n})\rightharpoonup u\not\equiv 0
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Set
v_{n}=u_{n}(x+y_{n})-u
. If
\|u\|_{2}=b\neq c
, then
b\in(0,c)
.
Setting
d_{n}=\|v_{n}\|_{2}
, and by using

\|u_{n}(x+y_{n})\|_{2}^{2}=\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{2}^{2}+o_{n}(1),

we obtain that
d_{n}\in(0,c)
for
n
large enough and

\|v_{n}\|_{2}\to d
with
c^{2}=d^{2}+b^{2}
. It follows from Lemma

2.4
, Lemma
3.1
(3) and (
[
29
]
, Lemma 2.5) that

\begin{split}\sigma(c)+o_{n}(1)=E_{q}(u_{n}(x+y_{n}))&=E_{q}(v_{n})+E_{q}(u)+o_{n}(1)\\
&\geq\sigma(d_{n})+\sigma(b)+o_{n}(1)\\
&\geq\frac{d_{n}^{2}}{c^{2}}\sigma(c)+\sigma(b)+o_{n}(1).\end{split}

Letting
n\to+\infty
and using again Lemma
3.1
(3), we find
that

\sigma(c)\geq\frac{d^{2}}{c^{2}}\sigma(c)+\sigma(b)>\frac{d^{2}}{c^{2}}\sigma(c)+\frac{b^{2}}{c^{2}}\sigma(c)=\sigma(c),

which is a contradiction. So
\|u\|_{2}=c
and
u_{n}(x+y_{n})\to u
in

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and then
u_{n}(x+y_{n})\to u
in

L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
2\leq s<\frac{2N}{(N-2)^{+}}
. Consequently,
we obtain that

\sigma(c)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}E_{q}(u_{n})=\lim_{n\to+\infty}E_{q}(u_{n}(x+y_{n}))\geq E_{q}(u)\geq\sigma(c).

This shows that
E_{q}(u)=\sigma(c)
and
\|\nabla u_{n}(x+y_{n})\|_{2}\to\|\nabla u\|_{2}
as
n\to+\infty
, that is,
u\in S(c)
is a
minimizer of
\sigma(c)
and
u_{n}(x+y_{n})\to u
in

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
. In view of Lemma

(1), let

,

Proof of Theorem

4.
The non-autonomous problem

To show the dependence on parameters, we rewrite

\begin{split}J_{\gamma}(u):&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{\mu}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx\\
&\qquad-\frac{\gamma^{2}N}{2(N+\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}})|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx\end{split}

and

\Upsilon_{\gamma,c}:=\inf_{u\in S(c)}J_{\gamma}(u).

It is obvious that
\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u)\geq J_{h_{max}}(u)

for any
u\in S(c)
. By Theorem
1.1
, the definition

\Gamma_{\epsilon,c}:=\inf_{u\in S(c)}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u)

is well defined and
\Gamma_{\epsilon,c}\geq\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}
.

The next two lemmas establish the relations of

\Gamma_{\epsilon,c}
,
\Upsilon_{h_{\infty},c}
and

\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
\mu,c>0
,
2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}
,
and
0<\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}
. Then
\Upsilon_{\gamma_{2},c}<\Upsilon_{\gamma_{1},c}
. In particular,

\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}<\Upsilon_{h_{\infty},c}<0
.

Proof.

Let
u\in S(c)
satisfy
J_{\gamma_{1}}(u)=\Upsilon_{\gamma_{1},c}
.
Then,
\Upsilon_{\gamma_{2},c}\leq J_{\gamma_{2}}(u)<J_{\gamma_{1}}(u)=\Upsilon_{\gamma_{1},c}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
\epsilon,\mu,c>0
,

2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}
, and
h
satisfy (
h_{1}
)-(
h_{3}
). Then

\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\Gamma_{\epsilon,c}\leq\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.1
, choose

such that

. Then

\begin{split}\Gamma_{\epsilon,c}\leq\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u)&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{\mu}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx\\
&\qquad-\frac{N}{2(N+\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast[h(\epsilon x)|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}])h(\epsilon x)|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx.\\
\end{split}

Letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, by the Lebesgue dominated convergence
theorem, we deduce that

\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\Gamma_{\epsilon,c}\leq\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u)=J_{h(0)}(u)=J_{h_{max}}(u)=\Upsilon_{h_{max},c},

which completes the proof.
∎

By Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, there exists
\epsilon_{1}>0

such that
\Gamma_{\epsilon,c}<\Upsilon_{h_{\infty},c}
for all

\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{1})

that
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{1})
. The next two lemmas will be used

restricted to
S(c)
at some levels.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that
N\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
\epsilon,\mu,c>0
,

2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}
, and
h
satisfies (
h_{1}
)-(
h_{3}
). Let

\{u_{n}\}\subset S(c)
be such that
\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u_{n})\to a

in

.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
u\equiv 0

\begin{split}a+o_{n}(1)=\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u_{n})=&J_{h_{\infty}}(u_{n})+\frac{N}{2(N+\alpha)}h_{\infty}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}})|u_{n}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx\\

By (

\delta>0

with Lemma

\begin{split}&h_{\infty}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast|u_{n}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}})|u_{n}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast[h(\epsilon x)|u_{n}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}])h(\epsilon x)|u_{n}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx\\

Lemma 4.4
.

,
\alpha\in(0,N)

,

)-(

b=\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{2},\ d_{n}=\|v_{n}\|_{2}
and suppose that

\|v_{n}\|_{2}\to d
, then we get
d\geq\beta>0
and
c^{2}=b^{2}+d^{2}
. From
d_{n}\in(0,c)
for
n
large enough, we have

(4.10)

a+o_{n}(1)=\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u_{n})=\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(v_{n})+\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon})+o_{n}(1).

Since
v_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, similarly to
the proof of Lemma
4.3
, we deduce that

(4.11)

\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(v_{n})\geq J_{h_{\infty}}(v_{n})-\delta C+o_{n}(1)

for any
\delta>0
, where
C>0
is a constant independent of

\delta
and
n
. By (
4.10
), (
4.11
) and Lemma

3.1
(3), we obtain that

\begin{split}a+o_{n}(1)=\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u_{n})&\geq J_{h_{\infty}}(v_{n})+\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon})-\delta C+o_{n}(1)\\
&\geq\Upsilon_{h_{\infty},d_{n}}+\Upsilon_{h_{max},b}-\delta C+o_{n}(1)\\
&\geq\frac{d_{n}^{2}}{c^{2}}\Upsilon_{h_{\infty},c}+\frac{b^{2}}{c^{2}}\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}-\delta C+o_{n}(1).\end{split}

Letting
n\to+\infty
, by the arbitrariness of
\delta>0
, we
obtain that

\begin{split}a&\geq\frac{d^{2}}{c^{2}}\Upsilon_{h_{\infty},c}+\frac{b^{2}}{c^{2}}\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}\\
&=\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}+\frac{d^{2}}{c^{2}}(\Upsilon_{h_{\infty},c}-\Upsilon_{h_{max},c})\\
&\geq\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{c^{2}}(\Upsilon_{h_{\infty},c}-\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}),\end{split}

which contradicts
a<\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}+\rho_{0}
. Thus, we must
have
u_{n}\to u_{\epsilon}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
∎

In what follows, we fix
\tilde{\rho},\tilde{r}>0
satisfying:

\bullet

\overline{B_{\tilde{\rho}}(a_{i})}\cap\overline{B_{\tilde{\rho}}(a_{j})}=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
and

i,j\in\{1,\cdots,l\}
;

\bullet

\cup_{i=1}^{l}B_{\tilde{\rho}}(a_{i})\subset B_{\tilde{r}}(0)
;

\bullet

K_{\frac{\tilde{\rho}}{2}}=\cup_{i=1}^{l}\overline{B_{\frac{\tilde{\rho}}{2}}(a_{i})}
.

We also set the function
Q_{\epsilon}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
by

Q_{\epsilon}(u):=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\chi(\epsilon x)|u|^{2}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx},

where
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is given by

\chi(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x,&\ \text{if}\ |x|\leq\tilde{r},\\
\tilde{r}\frac{x}{|x|},&\ \text{if}\ |x|>\tilde{r}.\end{array}\right.

The next two lemmas will be useful to get important (
PS
) sequences
for
\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}
restricted to
S(c)
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
\epsilon,\mu,c>0
,

2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}
,
h
satisfy (
h_{1}
)-(
h_{3}
), and
\rho_{0}
be
defined in Lemma
4.5
. Then there exist

\epsilon_{2}\in(0,\epsilon_{1}],\rho_{1}\in(0,\rho_{0}]
such that if

\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{2})
,
u\in S(c)
and

\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u)\leq\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}+\rho_{1}
, then

Q_{\epsilon}(u)\in K_{\frac{\tilde{\rho}}{2}}.

Proof.

If the lemma does not occur, there must be
\rho_{n}\to 0,\epsilon_{n}\to 0
and
\{u_{n}\}\subset S(c)
such that

(4.12)

\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon_{n}}(u_{n})\leq\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}+\rho_{n}\ \text{and}\ Q_{\epsilon_{n}}(u_{n})\not\in K_{\frac{\tilde{\rho}}{2}}.

Consequently,

\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}\leq J_{h_{max}}(u_{n})\leq\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon_{n}}(u_{n})\leq\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}+\rho_{n},

then

\{u_{n}\}\subset S(c)\ \text{and}\ J_{h_{max}}(u_{n})\to\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}.

According to Lemma
3.2
, there exists
\{y_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
v_{n}(x):=u_{n}(x+y_{n})\to v
in

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
v\in S(c)
. Now we will study the two
cases: (I)
|\epsilon_{n}y_{n}|\to+\infty
and (II)
\epsilon_{n}y_{n}\to y
for some
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

If (I) holds, the limit
v_{n}\to v
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
provides

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon_{n}}(u_{n})&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}dx-\frac{\mu}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v_{n}|^{q}dx\\
&\quad-\frac{N}{2(N+\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast[h(\epsilon_{n}x+\epsilon_{n}y_{n})|v_{n}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}])h(\epsilon_{n}x+\epsilon_{n}y_{n})|v_{n}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx\\
&\to J_{h_{\infty}}(v)\ \text{as}\ n\to+\infty.\end{split}

Since
\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon_{n}}(u_{n})\leq\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}+\rho_{n}
, we deduce that

\Upsilon_{h_{\infty},c}\leq J_{h_{\infty}}(v)\leq\Upsilon_{h_{max},c},

which contradicts
\Upsilon_{h_{\infty},c}>\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}
in
Lemma
4.1
.

Now if (II) holds, then

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon_{n}}(u_{n})\to J_{h(y)}(v)\ \text{as}\ n\to+\infty,\end{split}

which combined with
\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon_{n}}(u_{n})\leq\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}+\rho_{n}
gives that

\Upsilon_{h(y),c}\leq J_{h(y)}(v)\leq\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}.

By Lemma
4.1
, we must have
h(y)=h_{max}
and
y=a_{i}
for
some
i=1,2,\cdots,l
. Hence,

\begin{split}Q_{\epsilon_{n}}(u_{n})=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\chi(\epsilon_{n}x)|u_{n}|^{2}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{2}dx}&=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\chi(\epsilon_{n}x+\epsilon_{n}y_{n})|v_{n}|^{2}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v_{n}|^{2}dx}\\
&\to\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\chi(y)|v|^{2}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{2}dx}=a_{i}\in K_{\frac{\tilde{\rho}}{2}},\end{split}

which implies that
Q_{\epsilon_{n}}(u_{n})\in K_{\frac{\tilde{\rho}}{2}}
for
n
large enough. That contradicts
(
4.12
). The proof is complete.
∎

From now on, we will use the following notations:

\bullet

\theta_{\epsilon}^{i}:=\{u\in S(c):|Q_{\epsilon}(u)-a_{i}|\leq\tilde{\rho}\}
;

\bullet

\partial\theta_{\epsilon}^{i}:=\{u\in S(c):|Q_{\epsilon}(u)-a_{i}|=\tilde{\rho}\}
;

\bullet

\beta_{\epsilon}^{i}:=\inf_{u\in\theta_{\epsilon}^{i}}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u)
;

\bullet

\tilde{\beta}_{\epsilon}^{i}:=\inf_{u\in\partial\theta_{\epsilon}^{i}}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u)
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
\epsilon,\mu,c>0
,

2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}
,
h
satisfy (
h_{1}
)-(
h_{3}
),
\epsilon_{2}
and

\rho_{1}
be obtained in Lemma
4.6
. Then there exists

\epsilon_{3}\in(0,\epsilon_{2}]
such that

\beta_{\epsilon}^{i}<\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}+\frac{\rho_{1}}{2}\ \text{and}\ \beta_{\epsilon}^{i}<\tilde{\beta}_{\epsilon}^{i},\ \text{for\ any\ }\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{3}).

Proof.

Let
u\in S(c)
be such that

J_{h_{max}}(u)=\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}.

For
1\leq i\leq l
, we define

\hat{u}_{\epsilon}^{i}(x):=u\left(x-\frac{a_{i}}{\epsilon}\right),\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Then
\hat{u}_{\epsilon}^{i}\in S(c)
for all
\epsilon>0
and

1\leq i\leq l
. Direct calculations give that

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\hat{u}_{\epsilon}^{i})&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{\mu}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx\\
&\quad-\frac{N}{2(N+\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast[h(\epsilon x+a_{i})|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}])h(\epsilon x+a_{i})|u|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx,\end{split}

and then

(4.13)

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\hat{u}_{\epsilon}^{i})=J_{h(a_{i})}(u)=J_{h_{max}}(u)=\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}.

Note that

Q_{\epsilon}(\hat{u}_{\epsilon}^{i})=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\chi(\epsilon x+a_{i})|u|^{2}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx}\to a_{i}\text{\ as}\ \epsilon\to 0^{+}.

So
\hat{u}_{\epsilon}^{i}\in\theta_{\epsilon}^{i}
for
\epsilon

small enough, which combined with (
4.13
) implies that there
exists
\epsilon_{3}\in(0,\epsilon_{2}]
such that

\beta_{\epsilon}^{i}<\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}+\frac{\rho_{1}}{2}\text{\ for\ any}\ \epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{3}).

For any
v\in\partial\theta_{\epsilon}^{i}
, that is,
v\in S(c)
and

|Q_{\epsilon}(v)-a_{i}|=\tilde{\rho}
, we obtain that

|Q_{\epsilon}(v)\not\in K_{\frac{\tilde{\rho}}{2}}
. Thus, by Lemma

4.6
,

\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(v)>\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}+\rho_{1}\ \text{for\ all}\ v\in\partial\theta_{\epsilon}^{i}\ \text{and}\ \epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{3}),

which implies that

\tilde{\beta}_{\epsilon}^{i}=\inf_{v\in\partial\theta_{\epsilon}^{i}}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(v)\geq\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}+\rho_{1}\ \text{for\ all}\ \epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{3}).

Thus,

\beta_{\epsilon}^{i}<\tilde{\beta}_{\epsilon}^{i}\ \text{for\ all\ }\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{3}).

∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
. Set

\epsilon_{0}:=\epsilon_{3}
, where
\epsilon_{3}
is obtained in Lemma

4.7
. Let
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
. By Lemma

4.7
, for each
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,l\}
, we can use the
Ekeland variational principle to find a sequence
\{u_{n}^{i}\}\subset\theta_{\epsilon}^{i}
satisfying

\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u_{n}^{i})\to\beta_{\epsilon}^{i}\ \text{and}\ \|\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}|_{S(c)}^{\prime}(u_{n}^{i})\|\to 0\ \text{as}\ n\to+\infty,

that is,
\{u_{n}^{i}\}_{n}
is a
(PS)_{\beta_{\epsilon}^{i}}
sequence for

\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}
restricted to
S(c)
. Since

\beta_{\epsilon}^{i}<\Upsilon_{h_{max},c}+\rho_{0}
, it follows from
Lemma
4.5
that there exists
u^{i}
such that
u_{n}^{i}\to u^{i}

in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Thus

u^{i}\in\theta_{\epsilon}^{i},\ \mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u^{i})=\beta_{\epsilon}^{i}<0\ \text{and}\ \mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}|_{S(c)}^{\prime}(u^{i})=0.

As

Q_{\epsilon}(u^{i})\in\overline{B_{\tilde{\rho}}(a_{i})},\ Q_{\epsilon}(u^{j})\in\overline{B_{\tilde{\rho}}(a_{j})},

and

\overline{B_{\tilde{\rho}}(a_{i})}\cap\overline{B_{\tilde{\rho}}(a_{j})}=\emptyset\ \text{for}\ i\neq j,

we conclude that
u^{i}\not\equiv u^{j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq l
and

i\neq j
. Therefore,
\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}
has at least
l

nontrivial critical points for all
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
.
So there exists
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

-\Delta u^{i}+\lambda_{i}u^{i}=(I_{\alpha}\ast[h(\epsilon x)|u^{i}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}])h(\epsilon x)|u^{i}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}-2}u^{i}+\mu|u^{i}|^{q-2}u^{i},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

By using
\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u^{i})=\beta_{\epsilon}^{i}<0
and

\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u^{i})u^{i}+\lambda_{i}c^{2}=0
, we obtain that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{i}c^{2}&=-\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u^{i})+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u^{i}|^{q}dx\\
&\qquad+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{N}{2(N+\alpha)}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\ast[h(\epsilon x)|u^{i}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}])h(\epsilon x)|u^{i}|^{\frac{N+\alpha}{N}}dx,\end{split}

which implies that
\lambda_{i}>0
for
i=1,2,\cdots,l
. The proof is
complete.

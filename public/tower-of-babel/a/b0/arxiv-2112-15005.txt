The Principle of Linearized Stability in Age-Structured Diffusive Populations
Christoph Walker, Josef Zehetbauer

Abstract
. The principle of linearized stability is established for age-structured diffusive populations incorporating nonlinear death and birth processes. More precisely, asymptotic exponential stability is shown for equilibria for which the semigroup associated with the linearization at the equiblibrium has a negative growth bound. The result is derived in an abstract framework and applied in concrete situations.

Introduction

Let
u=u(t,a,x)\geq 0
denote the density of an age-structured diffusive population at time
t\geq 0
, age
a\in[0,a_{m})
with maximal age
a_{m}\in(0,\infty]
, and spatial position
x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A prototype model
[
6
,
11
,
26
]
for the evolution of

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{a}u

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}_{x}\big(d(a,x)\nabla_{x}u\big)-m\big(\bar{u}(t,x),a,x\big)u\ ,

\displaystyle t>0\,,

\displaystyle a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

\displaystyle x\in\Omega\,,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle u(t,0,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b\big(\bar{u}(t,x),a,x\big)u(t,a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,

\displaystyle t>0\,,

\displaystyle x\in\Omega\,,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\partial_{N}u(t,a,x)

\displaystyle=0\ ,

\displaystyle t>0\,,

\displaystyle a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

\displaystyle x\in\partial\Omega\,,

(1.1d)

\displaystyle u(0,a,x)

\displaystyle=u_{0}(a,x)\ ,

\displaystyle a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

\displaystyle x\in\Omega\,,

where the death and birth rates

and

\bar{u}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\nu(a,x)\,u(t,a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a

with weight
\nu

Spatial dispersal is governed by the diffusion term in (

. The initial distribution of the population is
u_{0}=u_{0}(a,x)\geq 0
, and

Questions related to well-posedness and qualitative aspects of linear and nonlinear population models incorporating age and spatial structure have been addressed by many authors under different assumptions and with different techniques. We mention

In this research we shall investigate stability properties of equilibrium solutions to problems of the form (
1.1
) for which we embed the latter in a more abstract framework. To this end, we set

A(a)w:=\mathrm{div}_{x}\big(d(a,\cdot)\nabla_{x}w\big)\,,\quad w\in E_{1}\,,

where e.g.
E_{1}:=W_{q,\mathcal{B}}^{2}(\Omega)
consists of all functions

in the Sobolev space

with

satisfying the Neumann boundary condition

. For a smooth and positive function

is then the generator of an analytic semigroup in

with domain

(and if

depends smoothly also on

E_{0}
with regularity subspace
E_{1}
).
We shall thus focus in the following on the abstract problem

(1.2a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{a}u\,

\displaystyle=A(a)u-m\big(\bar{u}(t),a\big)u\,,\qquad t>0\,,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

(1.2b)

\displaystyle u(t,0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b\big(\bar{u}(t),a\big)\,u(t,a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\qquad t>0\,,

(1.2c)

\displaystyle u(0,a)

\displaystyle=u_{0}(a)\,,\qquad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

for functions
u=u(t,a):\mathbb{R}^{+}\times[0,a_{m})\rightarrow E_{0}^{+}
, where
a_{m}\in(0,\infty]
and

A(a):E_{1}\subset E_{0}\rightarrow E_{0}

is for each

the generator of an analytic semigroup on some Banach lattice
E_{0}
with domain
E_{1}
. Given such a function

we indicate with a bar its (weighted) integral with respect to

\bar{u}(t)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\nu(\sigma)\,u(t,\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\in E_{0}

for a fixed function
\nu

We shall be more specific about the assumptions when presenting the main results in
Section

2

1.1

Let us emphasize that the “elliptic” operator
A(a)
and the age derivative
\partial_{a}
– being supplemented with a nonlocal boundary condition (
1.2b

It is then natural to consider problem (
1.2
) as an evolution equation in the Banach space
\mathbb{E}_{0}:=L_{1}([0,a_{m}),E_{0})
(actually, on a subspace thereof to have more flexibility with respect to the nonlinearities).
The well-posedness of problems of the form (
1.2

Equilibria – i.e. time-independent solutions – of (
1.2
) are determined from

(1.3a)

\displaystyle\partial_{a}\phi\,

\displaystyle=A(a)\phi-m(\bar{\phi},a)\phi\,,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

(1.3b)

\displaystyle\phi(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(\bar{\phi},a)\,\phi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,.

Clearly,
\phi\equiv 0
is always an equilibrium. In previous research we presented fairly general conditions sufficient for the existence of at least one positive smooth non-trivial equilibrium, e.g. by bifurcation methods
[
17
,

or using fixed point methods in conical shell
[

The main purpose of this research now is to establish the principle of linearized stability for an equilibrium
\phi
of (
1.2
) in the sense that the linearization of (

) at
\phi
controls the stability of
\phi
. Imposing that the nonlinearities are differentiable at

1.2

(1.4a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+\partial_{a}v\,

\displaystyle=A(a)v-m\big(\bar{\phi},a\big)v-\partial m\big(\bar{\phi},a\big)[\bar{v}(t)]\phi(a)\,,\qquad t>0\,,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

(1.4b)

\displaystyle v(t,0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b\big(\bar{\phi},a\big)\,v(t,a)\,\mathrm{d}a+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\partial b\big(\bar{\phi},a\big)[\bar{v}(t)]\,\phi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\qquad t>0\,,

(1.4c)

\displaystyle v(0,a)

\displaystyle=v_{0}(a)\,,\qquad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

with
\partial
indicating Fréchet derivatives with respect to
\bar{\phi}
.
Such linear problems were studied in
[
26
,
24
]
. It was shown, in particular, that (under suitable assumptions) the corresponding solution is given by a strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
(i.e.,
v(t)=\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t)v_{0}
) inheriting the regularizing effect from the parabolic character of the operator
\partial_{t}-A
. Under the premise that this semigroup has an exponential decay we shall show herein that the equilibrium
\phi
is asymptotically exponentially stable. If this condition is not met in the case of the trivial equilibrium
\phi=0
, the associated semigroup has asynchronous exponential growth
[
22
,
24
]
(see also
[
10
]
for a slightly different situation with nonlocal diffusion).

We shall emphasize that our approach to investigate linearized stability is adapted from the case without diffusion
[
13
]
(see also
[
25
]

[
12
]
for the case of a linear birth rate). The idea of the proof presented herein follows closely the corresponding proof of
[
13
,
25
]
. There are, however, additional difficulties and technicalities that have to be dealt with when diffusion is taken into account.

2.
Main Result

We now list our assumptions in detail, state the required well-posedness result, and then present the main result on the asymptotic exponential stability of equilibria.

Preliminaries

Let
J:=[0,a_{m}]
if
a_{m}<\infty
and
J:=[0,\infty)
if
a_{m}=\infty
. We write
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
for the normed vector space of bounded linear operators from a Banach space

E_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\relbar\kern-3.44444pt\joinrel\lhook\joinrel\rightarrow}}}E_{0}\,.

Fixing for

(see
[
2
]

E_{\theta}:=(E_{0},E_{1})_{\theta}
equipped with the order naturally induced by
E_{0}^{+}
.
We suppose that there is
\rho>0
such that

(2.1a)

A\in C^{\rho}\big(J,\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\big)

and

(2.1b)

A(a)\ \text{ is resolvent positive for each $a\in J$}\,,

where

with domain
E_{1}
.
Then, due to (

generates a positive parabolic evolution operator

\{\Pi(a,\sigma)\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0})\,;\,a\in J\,,\,0\leq\sigma\leq a\}

on

with regularity subspace

in the sense of

The evolution operator satisfies useful stability estimates on the interpolation spaces. We fix

and

\|\Pi(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha})}+(a-\sigma)^{\alpha}\,\|\Pi(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\alpha})}\leq M_{\alpha}e^{\varpi(a-\sigma)}\,,\qquad a\in J\,,\quad 0\leq\sigma\leq a\,,

We further assume for the birth rate that

\big[\bar{v}\to b(\bar{v},\cdot)]\in C_{b}^{1-}\big(E_{\alpha},L_{\infty}^{+}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha},E_{0})\big)\big)\,,

and for the death rate that

\big[\bar{v}\to m(\bar{v},\cdot)]\in C_{b}^{1-}\big(E_{\alpha},L_{\infty}^{+}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha},E_{0})\big)\big)\,,

where

stands for locally Lipschitz continuous maps that are bounded on bounded sets.
Finally, we fix a weight function

such that there is
\vartheta\in(0,1)
with

(2.1g)

\nu\in L_{1}^{+}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})\big)\cap L_{\infty}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})\big)\,,\quad\theta\in\{0,\alpha,\vartheta\}\,.

Setting

Well-Posedness

Questions related to well-posedness of nonlinear problems of the form (
1.2
) (and even more general equations) were addressed e.g. in
[
18
]
.
Integrating (
1.2
) formally along characteristics yields that a solution
u:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}

to (
1.2
) with initial value
u_{0}\in\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
satisfies the fixed point equation

(2.6a)

u(t,a)\,=\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Pi(a,a-t)\,u_{0}(a-t)+G_{F(u)}(t,a)\,,&&a\in J\,,\ 0\leq t\leq a\,,\\
&\Pi(a,0)\,B_{u}(t-a)+G_{F(u)}(t,a)\,,&&a\in J\,,\ t>a\,,\end{aligned}\right.

where

(2.6b)

G_{v}(t,a):=\int_{(t-a)_{+}}^{t}\Pi(a,a-t+s)\,v(s,a-t+s)\,\mathrm{d}s

for
v:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}_{0}
,
and where
B_{u}:=u(\cdot,0)
satisfies the nonlinear Volterra equation

(2.6c)

\begin{split}B_{u}(t)\,&=\,\int_{0}^{t}b(\bar{u}(t),a)\,\Pi(a,0)\,B_{u}(t-a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,+\,\int_{t}^{a_{m}}b(\bar{u}(t),a)\,\Pi(a,a-t)\,u_{0}(a-t)\,\mathrm{d}a\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(\bar{u}(t),a)G_{F(u)}(t,a)\,\mathrm{d}a\end{split}

for
t\geq 0
. Here and in the following we put
b(\bar{v},a):=0
whenever
a\notin J
. Note that

for

by (

), while (
2.6c
) ensures

(2.6d)

\begin{split}B_{u}(t)\,&=\,\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(\bar{u}(t),a)\,u(t,a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\quad t\geq 0\,.\end{split}

This is in accordance with the age boundary condition (
1.2b
).

Motivated by these observations we mean in the following by a (local) solution to problem (
1.2
) a function

satisfying (

is an interval in

containing

. We first state a well-posedness result which is mainly due to

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1)
and suppose (
2.1

For every

there exists a unique maximal solution

to problem (
1.2
) on some maximal interval of existence
I(u_{0})=[0,t^{+}(u_{0}))
; that is,

satisfies (

If

\sup_{t\in I(u_{0})\cap[0,T]}\|u(t;u_{0})\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}<\infty\,,\quad T>0\,,

then the solution exists globally, i.e.,

.
Finally, if
u_{0}\in\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}^{+}
, then

for

.

The solution provided by

features further properties, in particular with respect to regularity. We shall not go into details here and refer to

Stability of Equilibria

Let
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{1}\cap C(J,E_{0})
be an equilibrium solution to (
1.2
) (in the sense that it is a mild solution to (
1.3
)).
As mentioned before, existence of non-trivial positive smooth equilibria to problems of the form (
1.2
) was established in previous works
[
17
,
19
,
21
,
23
]
to which we refer.

The main purpose of this research is to establish the principle of linearized stability for such an equilibrium. That is, we want to derive information on the stability of
\phi
from (spectral properties of) the linearized problem (
1.4
).
To make things more precise, we now assume further that

(2.7a)

\begin{split}&E_{\alpha}\rightarrow L_{\infty}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha},E_{0})\big)\,,\ v\mapsto m(v,\cdot)\text{ is differentiable at $\bar{\phi}\in E_{1}$}\end{split}

and

(2.7b)

\begin{split}&E_{\alpha}\rightarrow L_{\infty}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha},E_{0})\big)\,,\ v\mapsto b(v,\cdot)\text{ is differentiable at $\bar{\phi}\in E_{1}$}\,.\end{split}

Moreover, for technical reasons we also assume that (for some
\vartheta\in(0,1)
, see (
2.1g
))

(2.7c)

\begin{split}b(\bar{\phi},\cdot)\in L_{1}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})\big)\cap L_{\infty}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})\big)\,,\quad\theta\in\{0,\alpha,\vartheta\}\,,\end{split}

and

(2.7d)

\big[v\mapsto\partial b(\bar{\phi},\cdot)[v]\phi\big]\in\mathcal{L}\big(E_{\theta},\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\big)\,,\quad\theta\in\{0,\alpha,\vartheta\}\,.

Setting then

(\partial F(\phi)v)(a):=-m\big(\bar{\phi},a\big)v(a)-\partial m\big(\bar{\phi},a\big)[\bar{v}]\phi(a)\,,\qquad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,\quad v\in\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}\,,

and

(2.8)

\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(v):=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(\bar{\phi},a)\,v(a)\,\mathrm{d}a+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\partial b(\bar{\phi},a)[\bar{v}]\phi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\quad v\in\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}\,,

we have

(2.9)

\partial F(\phi)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha},\mathbb{E}_{0})\,,\qquad\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\theta},E_{\theta})\,,\quad\theta\in\{0,\alpha,\vartheta\}\,.

It then follows from
[
24
, Theorem 2.8]
that, for
v_{0}\in\mathbb{E}_{0}
, the linearized Cauchy problem (see (
1.4
))

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+\partial_{a}v\,

\displaystyle=A(a)v+\partial F(\phi)v\,,\qquad t>0\,,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

\displaystyle v(t,0)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(v(t))\,,\qquad t>0\,,

\displaystyle v(0,a)

\displaystyle=v_{0}(a)\,,\qquad a\in(0,a_{m})\,.

defines a strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
; that is,
v(t)=\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t)v_{0}
is its unique (mild) solution in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
. Moreover, the semigroup inherits the regularizing properties from the parabolic part in the sense that there are
N_{\alpha}(\phi)\geq 1
and
\omega_{\alpha}(\phi)\in\mathbb{R}
with

(2.10)

\|\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}+t^{\alpha}\|\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\leq N_{\alpha}(\phi)\,e^{-\omega_{\alpha}(\phi)t}\,,\quad t\geq 0\,.

We shall give more details on all these facts later in the subsequent sections (in particular, see Proposition
5.4
below). The main result of this work regarding the stability of equilibria now states that the equilibrium
\phi
is asymptotically exponentially stable in
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
provided that
\omega_{\alpha}(\phi)>0
:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1)
and suppose (
2.1
). Let
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{1}\cap C(J,E_{0})
be an equilibrium solution to (
1.2
) such that (
2.7
) is satisfied. Moreover, suppose that

\omega_{\alpha}(\phi)>0

in (
2.10
). Then, given any
\omega\in(0,\omega_{\alpha}(\phi))
, there are
r>0
and
M\geq 1
such that, for every
u_{0}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}(\phi,r)
, the solution
u(\cdot;u_{0})
to (
1.2
) exists globally and

\|u(t;u_{0})-\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}\leq M\,e^{-\omega t}\,\|u_{0}-\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}\,,\quad t\geq 0\,.

In particular, the equilibrium
\phi
is asymptotically exponentially stable in
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
.

Note that the assumption
\omega_{\alpha}(\phi)>0
is equivalent to the assumption that the growth bound of the semigroup
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
is negative. In some cases (e.g. for the trivial equilibrium
\phi=0
or if
m=m(a)
is independent of the population) one can show that the semigroup
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
is eventually compact, hence its growth bound coincides with the spectral bound of its generator (see
5.6
and
Section

7
).

It is also worth pointing out that (
2.7c
) and (
2.7d
) are not too restrictive with regard to applications since
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{1}
. Finally, we emphasize that one can choose
\alpha\in(0,1)
positive so that the nonlinearities

and

are defined on a smaller space
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
than
\mathbb{E}_{0}
. This is due to the fact that we exploit the regularizing effects induced from the analytic generator

1.2
).

we revisit the concrete problem (

) and present examples to which our results apply.

3.
Well-Posedness: Proof of
2.1

2.1
is a special case of the results shown in
[
18
]
except for the term
G_{F(u)}
defined in (
2.4
) and (
2.6b
). However, noticing from (
2.1c
) that, for
v\in C\big([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{0}\big)
and
t\in[0,T]
,

\begin{split}\|G_{v}(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}&\leq\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\int_{(t-a)_{+}}^{t}\|\Pi(a,a-t+s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\alpha})}\,\|v(s,a-t+s)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}a\\
&\leq c(T)\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\int_{(t-a)_{+}}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\,\|v(s,a-t+s)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}a\\
&\leq c(T)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\,\|v(s)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\end{split}

we infer from the continuity properties of the evolution operator
\Pi
(see
[
2
, p.45]
) that

(3.1)

[v\mapsto G_{v}]\in\mathcal{L}\big(C\big([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{0}\big),C\big([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}\big)\big)\,,

hence (
2.4
) implies

[v\mapsto G_{F(v)}]\in C_{b}^{1-}\big(C\big([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}\big),C\big([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}\big)\big)\,.

The well-posedness stated in
2.1
then follows from this and (
2.5
) exactly along the lines of
[
18
, Theorem 2.2]
by means of Banach’s fixed point theorem. The positivity is shown as in
[
18
, Propositon 2]
.

4.
Derivation of the Linearization

We prepare the proof of
2.2
by deriving the linearization of problem (
1.2
) at an equiblibrium.

For the remainder of this paper, suppose (
2.1
) and let
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{1}\cap C(J,E_{0})
be a fixed equilibrium solution to (
1.2
) – i.e.
\phi
is a mild solution to (
1.3
) – such that (
2.7
) is satisfied.
We first note the following representation of
\phi
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The equilibrium
\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{1}\cap C(J,E_{0})
satisfies the identity

(4.1)

\phi(a)\,=\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Pi(a,a-t)\,\phi(a-t)+G_{F(\phi)}(t,a)\,,&&a\in J\,,\ t\leq a\,,\\
&\Pi(a,0)\,\phi(0)+G_{F(\phi)}(t,a)\,,&&a\in J\,,\ t>a\,,\end{aligned}\right.

for every
t\geq 0
, where
F
and
G_{F(\phi)}
are defined in (
2.4
) and (
2.6b
), respectively.

Proof.

It readily follows from (
2.2
) and (
1.3a
) that

(4.2)

\phi(a)=\Pi(a,0)\phi(0)+\int_{0}^{a}\Pi(a,\sigma)F(\phi)(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,,\quad a\in J\,.

Let
t\geq 0
be arbitrarily fixed and
a\in J
. If
a\in(0,t)
, then, by formula (
4.2
),

\displaystyle\phi(a)

\displaystyle=\Pi(a,0)\phi(0)+\int_{t-a}^{t}\Pi(a,a-t+s)F(\phi)(a-t+s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\Pi(a,0)\phi(0)+G_{F(\phi)}(t,a)\,.

If
t<a_{m}
and
a\in(t,a_{m})
, then, by formula (
4.2
) and the evolution property (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\phi(a)

\displaystyle=\Pi(a,0)\phi(0)+\left(\int_{0}^{a-t}+\int_{a-t}^{a}\right)\Pi(a,\sigma)F(\phi)(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\Pi(a,a-t)\left(\Pi(a-t,0)\phi(0)+\int_{0}^{a-t}\Pi(a-t,\sigma)F(\phi)(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\Pi(a,a-t+s)F(\phi)(a-t+s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\Pi(a,a-t)\phi(a-t)+G_{F(\phi)}(t,a)\,.

This is the assertion.
∎

Let now
u_{0}\in\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
be fixed and set

w:=u(\cdot;u_{0})-\phi\,,\qquad w_{0}:=u_{0}-\phi\,,

where
u(\cdot;u_{0})\in C\big(I(u_{0}),\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}\big)
is the maximal solution to (
1.2
) provided by
2.1
.
Then
w\in C\big(I(u_{0}),\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}\big)
, and it follows from
4.1
and (
2.6a
) that

(4.3)

w(t,a)\,=\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Pi(a,a-t)\,w_{0}(a-t)+G_{F(u)-F(\phi)}(t,a)\,,&&(t,a)\in I(u_{0})\times J\,,\ t\leq a\,,\\
&\Pi(a,0)\,\big(B_{u}(t-a)-\phi(0)\big)+G_{F(u)-F(\phi)}(t,a)\,,&&(t,a)\in I(u_{0})\times J\,,\ t>a\,.\end{aligned}\right.

We next use the linearizations for
F
and
B_{u}
. To this end, we note from (
2.1g
) that

\|\bar{v}-\bar{\phi}\|_{E_{\alpha}}\leq\|\nu\|_{L_{\infty}(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha}))}\,\|v-\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}

for
v\in\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
so that, using (
2.7b
), we can write

(4.4a)

b(\bar{v},\cdot)v-b(\bar{\phi},\cdot)\phi=b(\bar{\phi},\cdot)(v-\phi)+\partial b(\bar{\phi},\cdot)[\bar{v}-\bar{\phi}]\phi+R_{b}(v-\phi)

with reminder term

(4.4b)

\|R_{b}(v-\phi)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}=o\big(\|v-\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}\big)\ \text{ as }\ \|v-\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}\to 0\,.

Also note that (
2.7b
) entails

(4.4c)

\|\partial b(\bar{\phi},a)[\bar{v}]\phi(a)\|_{E_{0}}\leq c_{b}\,\|\bar{v}\|_{E_{\alpha}}\,\|\phi(a)\|_{E_{0}}\,,\quad a\in J\,,\quad\bar{v}\in E_{\alpha}\,,

with
c_{b}:=\|\partial b(\bar{\phi},\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha},L_{\infty}(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha},E_{0})))}
.
Similarly, due to (
2.7a
),
F:\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{E}_{0}
is differentiable at
\phi
and

(4.5a)

F(v)=F(\phi)+\partial F(\phi)(v-\phi)+R_{F}(v-\phi)

with

(4.5b)

\partial F(\phi)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha},\mathbb{E}_{0})\,,\qquad\|R_{F}(v-\phi)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}=o\big(\|v-\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}\big)\ \text{ as }\ \|v-\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}\to 0\,.

In particular,

(4.6)

G_{F(u)-F(\phi)}=G_{\partial F(\phi)w+R_{F}(w)}\,.

Recalling (
2.6d
) we set (slightly abusing notation)

\displaystyle B_{w}(t):

\displaystyle=B_{u}(t)-\phi(0)\,=\,\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\big[b(\bar{u}(t),a)\,u(t,a)-b(\bar{\phi},a)\phi(a)\big]\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(\bar{\phi},a)\,w(t,a)\,\mathrm{d}a+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\partial b(\bar{\phi},a)[\bar{w}(t)]\,\phi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}R_{b}(w(t))(a)\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(\bar{\phi},a)\,w(t,a)\,\mathrm{d}a+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\partial b(\bar{\phi},\sigma)[\nu(a)w(t,a)]\,\phi(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}R_{b}(w(t))(a)\,\mathrm{d}a

for
t\in I(u_{0})
, where we used (
4.4a
) and the linearity of
\partial b(\bar{\phi},\sigma)[\cdot]
for the third respectively fourth equality. Introducing (see (
2.1g
), (
2.7c
), (
2.7d
))

(4.7a)

\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}\in L_{1}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})\big)\cap L_{\infty}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})\big)\,,\quad\theta\in\{0,\alpha,\vartheta\}\,,

by

(4.7b)

\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)v:=b(\bar{\phi},a)v+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\partial b(\bar{\phi},\sigma)[\nu(a)v]\,\phi(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,,\quad a\in J\,,\quad v\in E_{0}\,,

we obtain

(4.8)

\displaystyle B_{w}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)\,w(t,a)\,\mathrm{d}a+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}R_{b}(w(t))(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\quad t\in I(u_{0})\,.

Consequently, we infer from (
4.3
), (
4.6
), and (
4.8
) the following intermediate result:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
u_{0}\in\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
and let
u(\cdot;u_{0})\in C\big(I(u_{0}),\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}\big)
be the maximal solution to (
1.2
). If

w=u(\cdot;u_{0})-\phi
and
w_{0}=u_{0}-\phi
,
then
w\in C(I(u_{0}),\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})

satisfies

(4.9a)

w(t,a)\,=\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Pi(a,a-t)\,w_{0}(a-t)+G_{\partial F(\phi)w+R_{F}(w)}(t,a)\,,&&(t,a)\in I(u_{0})\times J\,,\ t\leq a\,,\\
&\Pi(a,0)\,B_{w}(t-a)+G_{\partial F(\phi)w+R_{F}(w)}(t,a)\,,&&(t,a)\in I(u_{0})\times J\,,\ t>a\,,\end{aligned}\right.

where
B_{w}
satisfies (
4.8
), i.e.

(4.9b)

\begin{split}B_{w}(t)&=\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\big(w(t)\big)+h_{w}(t)\,,\quad t\in I(u_{0})\,,\end{split}

with
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
being defined in (
2.8
) and

(4.9c)

h_{w}(t):=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}R_{b}(w(t))(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\quad t\in I(u_{0})\,.

It is worth pointing out that
w
is thus the (generalized) solution to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w+\partial_{a}w\,

\displaystyle=A(a)w+\partial F(\phi)w+R_{F}(w)\,,

\displaystyle t\in I(u_{0})\,,\quad a\in J\,,

\displaystyle w(t,0)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(w(t,\cdot))+h_{w}(t)\,,

\displaystyle t\in I(u_{0})\,,

\displaystyle w(0,a)

\displaystyle=w_{0}(a)\,,

\displaystyle a\in J\,,

where
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
from (
2.8
) is the linearization of the right-hand side of the age boundary condition (
1.2b
).
The proof of
2.2
is then based on suitable estimates on
w
given by (
4.9
). To this end it is instrumental to investigate first the linear counterpart of (
4.9
) more generally. This is the purpose of the next section. Regarding the data note that

\partial F(\phi)w+R_{F}(w)\in C\big([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{0}\big)\,,\quad h_{w}\in C\big([0,T],E_{0}\big)\,,\quad w_{0}\in\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}\,.

We then shall return to
4.2
and continue from there in Section
6
.

5.
The Linearized Problem

As just announced it is appropriate at this stage to consider the linear version of (
4.9
). More precisely, given
T>0
, we fix

(5.1)

f\in C\big([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{0}\big)\,,\quad h\in C\big([0,T],E_{0}\big)\,,\quad z\in\mathbb{E}_{0}\,,\quad\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\,,

and set

(5.2)

\Pi_{\gamma}(a,\sigma):=e^{-\gamma(a-\sigma)}\Pi(a,\sigma)\,,\qquad a\in J\,,\quad 0\leq\sigma\leq a\,,

and

(5.3a)

G_{f}^{\gamma}(t,a):=\int_{(t-a)_{+}}^{t}\Pi_{\gamma}(a,a-t+s)\,f(s,a-t+s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\qquad a\in J\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,.

The additional parameter
\gamma
is introduced for technical reasons, its role will become clear later in Section
6
(see (
6.8
) for definiteness). We then define in dependence on these data the function
W=W_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}
by

(5.3b)

W_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}(t,a)\,:=\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Pi_{\gamma}(a,a-t)\,z(a-t)+G_{f}^{\gamma}(t,a)\,,&&(t,a)\in[0,T]\times J\,,\ t\leq a\,,\\
&\Pi_{\gamma}(a,0)\,B_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}(t-a)+G_{f}^{\gamma}(t,a)\,,&&(t,a)\in[0,T]\times J\,,\ t>a\,,\end{aligned}\right.

where
B=B_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}
satisfies

\displaystyle B(t)\,=\,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)\,\Pi_{\gamma}(a,0)\,B(t-a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,+\int_{t}^{a_{m}}\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)\,\Pi_{\gamma}(a,a-t)\,z(a-t)\,\mathrm{d}a\,

(5.3c)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)\,G_{f}^{\gamma}(t,a)\,\mathrm{d}a+h(t)

with the understanding in the following that
\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)=0
whenever
a\notin J
. That is,

(5.4)

\displaystyle B_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}(t)=\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\big(W_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}(t,\cdot)\big)+h(t)\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,.

Let us point out that
W=W_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}
represents the (generalized) solution to the linear problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}W+\partial_{a}W\,

\displaystyle=\big(-\gamma+A(a)\big)W+f(t,a)\,,

\displaystyle t\in[0,T]\,,\quad a\in J\,,

\displaystyle W(t,0)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\big(W(t,\cdot)\big)+h(t)\,,

\displaystyle t\in[0,T]\,,

\displaystyle W(0,a)

\displaystyle=z(a)\,,

\displaystyle a\in J\,,

and is formally obtained by an integration along characteristics. The subsequent auxiliary results are considerably easier to derive on the formal level of this differential equation.

The linear structure of (
5.3
) ensures the superposition

(5.5)

W_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}=W_{z,0}^{\gamma,0}+W_{0,f}^{\gamma,0}+W_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}\,.

The aim now is to give a semigroup based representation formula for
W_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}
related to the data
(z,f,\gamma,h)
which we then shall exploit for the nonlinear problem (
4.9
).

The Linearized Age Boundary Operator

Starting with
B_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}
we show, in particular, that it is well-defined and collect further properties in the next lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose (
5.1
). There is a unique

B=B_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}\in C([0,T],E_{0})

satisfying (
5.3
). Moreover, it decomposes as

(5.6)

B_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}(t)=B_{z,0}^{\gamma,0}(t)+B_{0,f}^{\gamma,0}(t)+B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t)\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

with

(5.7)

B_{z,0}^{\gamma,0}(t)=e^{-\gamma t}\,B_{z,0}^{0,0}(t)\,,\quad t\geq 0\,,

and

(5.8)

B_{0,f}^{\gamma,0}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}B_{f(s),0}^{\gamma,0}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,.

Proof.

It follows as in (
3.1
) that

[f\mapsto G_{f}^{\gamma}]\in\mathcal{L}\big(C\big([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{0}\big),C\big([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}\big)\big)\,.

Setting

\tilde{h}(t):=\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\big(G_{f}^{\gamma}(t,\cdot)\big)+h(t)\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

we thus obtain
\tilde{h}\in C([0,T],E_{0})
due to (
5.1
) and (
2.9
). Therefore,
[
24
, Lemma 6.1]
along with (
2.1a
), (
2.1c
), (
2.1d
), (
2.1g
), (
2.7c
), and (
2.7d
) entails that there exists a unique function
B=B_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}\in C([0,T],E_{0})
satisfying (
5.3
) and

(5.9)

\big[z\to B_{z,0}^{\gamma,0}\big]\in\mathcal{L}\big(\mathbb{E}_{0},C([0,T],E_{0})\big)\,.

The linear structure of (
5.3
) ensures (
5.6
). As for (
5.7
) note first that
T>0
can be chosen arbitrary if
f
and
h
are zero. From (
5.3
) and (
5.2
) we derive

\displaystyle e^{\gamma t}B_{z,0}^{\gamma,0}(t)\,=\,\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)\,\Pi(a,0)\,e^{\gamma(t-a)}\,B_{z,0}^{\gamma,0}(t-a)\,\mathrm{d}a+\int_{t}^{a_{m}}\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)\,\Pi(a,a-t)\,z(a-t)\,\mathrm{d}a

for
t\geq 0
. That is,
t\mapsto e^{\gamma t}B_{z,0}^{\gamma,0}(t)
satisfies the same equation as
B_{z,0}^{0,0}
. Uniqueness implies then (
5.7
).

With the same idea we prove (
5.8
). To this end, we first note that the integral in (
5.8
) is well defined, since

\big[s\mapsto B_{f(s),0}^{\gamma,0}(t-s)\big]\in C\big([0,t],E_{0}\big)\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

as is easily seen by the triangle inequality together with (
5.9
) and the assumption
f\in C\big([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{0}\big)
. Next, by (
5.3
) we have

\displaystyle B_{f(s),0}^{\gamma,0}(t-s)=\,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t-s}\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)\,\Pi_{\gamma}(a,0)\,B_{f(s),0}^{\gamma,0}(t-s-a)\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle+\int_{t-s}^{a_{m}}\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)\,\Pi_{\gamma}(a,a-t+s)\,f(s,a-t+s)\,\mathrm{d}a

so that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}B_{f(s),0}^{\gamma,0}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t-s}\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)\,\Pi_{\gamma}(a,0)\,B_{f(s),0}^{\gamma,0}(t-s-a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{t-s}^{a_{m}}\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)\,\Pi_{\gamma}(a,a-t+s)\,f(s,a-t+s)\,\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}s

for
t\in[0,T]
.
Therefore, applying Fubini’s theorem, we derive

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}B_{f(s),0}^{\gamma,0}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)\,\Pi_{\gamma}(a,0)\,\left(\int_{0}^{t-a}B_{f(s),0}^{\gamma,0}(t-a-s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)\int_{(t-a)_{+}}^{t}\Pi_{\gamma}(a,a-t+s)\,f(s,a-t+s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}a

Consequently, recalling (
5.3
) and (
5.3a
), we see that
t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}B_{f(s)}^{\gamma,0,0}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s
satisfies the same equation as
B_{0,f}^{\gamma,0}
so that (
5.8
) follows by uniqueness.
∎

We next derive an estimate on
B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
h\in C\big([0,T],E_{0}\big)
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there are constants
\mu=\mu(\alpha,b,\phi)>0
and
c_{1}=c_{1}(\alpha,b,\phi)>0
(both independent of
\gamma
and
h
) such that

(5.10)

\displaystyle\|B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t)\|_{E_{0}}\leq c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-a)^{-\alpha}\,e^{(\mu+\varpi-\gamma)(t-a)}\,\|h(a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a\,+\|h(t)\|_{E_{0}}\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,.

Proof.

We use (
5.3
), (
2.1c
), (
2.1e
) and (
4.4c
) to get

\displaystyle\|B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t)\|_{E_{0}}\leq\,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}(a)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha},E_{0})}\,\|\Pi_{\gamma}(a,0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\alpha})}\,\|B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t-a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a\,+\|h(t)\|_{E_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{b}_{\phi}\|_{L_{\infty}(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\alpha},E_{0}))}\,M_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(\varpi-\gamma)a}\,a^{-\alpha}\,\|B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t-a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a\,+\|h(t)\|_{E_{0}}

for
t\in[0,T]
. That is, there is some
c=c(\alpha,b,\phi)>0
such that

\displaystyle e^{-(\varpi-\gamma)t}\|B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t)\|_{E_{0}}\leq c\int_{0}^{t}(t-a)^{-\alpha}\,e^{-(\varpi-\gamma)a}\,\|B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a\,+e^{-(\varpi-\gamma)t}\|h(t)\|_{E_{0}}

for
t\in[0,T]
. Gronwall’s inequality
[
7
, Lemma 7.1.1]
now implies (
5.10
) for some constants
\mu=\mu(\alpha,b,\phi)>0
and
c_{1}=c_{1}(\alpha,b,\phi)>0
.
∎

The Linear Part and its Associated Semigroup

Now that
B_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}
is well-defined we shall focus on
W_{z,f}^{\gamma,h}
. We begin with the linear part
W_{z,0}^{\gamma,0}
(with vanishing
f
and
h
). We first note that it defines a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
and, due to the regularizing effects of the evolution operator
\Pi
, also on
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
. This semigroup was investigated in
[
24
]
. We recall the main results and add some other useful properties.

Proposition 5.3
.

Set

\mathbb{S}(t)z:=W_{z,0}^{0,0}(t,\cdot)\,,\qquad t\geq 0\,,\quad z\in\mathbb{E}_{0}\,.

Then
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
and (its restriction) also on
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
with

(5.11)

\|\mathbb{S}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\leq C_{\alpha}\,t^{-\alpha}e^{\varsigma_{\alpha}t}\,,\quad t>0\,,

for some
C_{\alpha}\geq 1
and
\varsigma_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,

(5.12)

W_{z,0}^{\gamma,0}(t,\cdot)=e^{-\gamma t}\,\mathbb{S}(t)z\,,\qquad t\geq 0\,,\quad z\in\mathbb{E}_{0}\,,

and, for
z\in\mathbb{E}_{0}
and
f\in C\big([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{0}\big)
with
T>0
,

(5.13)

W_{z,f}^{\gamma,0}(t,\cdot)=e^{-\gamma t}\mathbb{S}(t)z+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\mathbb{S}(t-s)f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,.

Proof.

It follows from assumptions (
2.1a
), (
2.1c
), (
2.1d
), (
2.1g
), (
2.7c
), (
2.7d
) together with
[
24
, Theorem 2.8]
that
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
defines a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
and on
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
satisfying (
5.11
). Identity (
5.12
) is easily derived from the definition of
W_{z,0}^{\gamma,0}
in (
5.3b
) along with (
5.2
) and (
5.7
) from
5.1
.
As noted in (
5.5
), the linear structure of (
5.3
) entails

W_{z,f}^{\gamma,0}=W_{z,0}^{\gamma,0}+W_{0,f}^{\gamma,0}

so that, due to (
5.12
), identity (
5.13
) will follow once we have identified the integral term therein as
W_{0,f}^{\gamma,0}
. To this end observe that (
5.12
) ensures

(5.14)

\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\mathbb{S}(t-s)f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}W_{f(s),0}^{\gamma,0}(t-s,\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,.

Let
t\in[0,T]
and
a\in J
. If
t\leq a
, then
t-s\leq a-s\leq a
for
s\in[0,t]
and therefore, by (
5.14
), (
5.3b
), and (
5.3a
), we indeed have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\mathbb{S}(t-s)f(s)\right)(a)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\Pi_{\gamma}(a,a-t+s)f(s,a-t+s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=G_{f}^{\gamma}(t,a)=W_{0,f}^{\gamma,0}(t,a)

in this case. Consider then
a<t
. In this case we infer from (
5.14
) and (
5.3b
) that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\mathbb{S}(t-s)f(s)\right)(a)\,\mathrm{d}s=\,

\displaystyle\Pi_{\gamma}(a,0)\int_{0}^{t-a}B_{f(s),0}^{\gamma,0}(t-a-s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{t-a}^{t}\Pi_{\gamma}(a,s+a-t)f(s,a-t+t)\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

From (
5.8
) in

and (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\mathbb{S}(t-s)f(s)\right)(a)\,\mathrm{d}s=\,

which proves (
5.13
).
∎

As remarked previously the strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
was investigated in

[
24
]
. In particular, it was shown therein that the regularizing effect stated in (

) implies that its generator can be perturbed by an operator belonging to
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha},\mathbb{E}_{0})
and still yields a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
. We use this observation now to derive the following representation for the solution of the perturbed Cauchy problem. So far, we refrained from indicating the dependence of
\mathbb{S}(t)
on the fixed equilibrium
\phi
. For later use, however, we indicate this dependence in the notation of the semigroup associated with the perturbation.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
denote the infinitesimal generator of the strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
introduced in
5.3
and consider
\mathbb{B}:=\partial F(\phi)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha},\mathbb{E}_{0})
. Then
\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
and also on
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
. Moreover, there are
N_{\alpha}:=N_{\alpha}(\phi)\geq 1
and
\omega_{\alpha}:=\omega_{\alpha}(\phi)\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(5.15)

\|\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}+t^{\alpha}\,\|\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\leq N_{\alpha}\,e^{-\omega_{\alpha}t}\,,\quad t\geq 0\,.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
v_{0}\in\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
, and
g\in C([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{0})
. If
v\in C([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})
solves

\displaystyle v(t)=e^{-\gamma t}\mathbb{S}(t)v_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\mathbb{S}(t-s)\,\big((\gamma+\mathbb{B})v(s)+g(s)\big)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

then

\displaystyle v(t)=\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t)v_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t-s)\,g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,.

Proof.

It follows from
[
24
, Theorem 2.8]
that
\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}
with domain
D(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B})=D(\mathbb{A})
(in particular, we have
D(\mathbb{A})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
) generates a strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
and on
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
satisfying (
5.15
).
The remainder of the proof is now the same as in
[
25
, Proposition 4.17]
except that
\mathbb{B}
herein is no bounded perturbation on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
. We thus include the details here.

(i)
Let first
g\in C^{1}([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{0})
and
v_{0}\in D(\mathbb{A})
. Define

(5.16)

\displaystyle x(t):=\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t)v_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t-s)g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

and note that
x\in C^{1}([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{0})\cap C([0,T],D(\mathbb{A}))
is the unique solution to

x^{\prime}=(-\gamma+\mathbb{A})x+(\gamma+\mathbb{B})x+g(t)\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,\qquad x(0)=v_{0}\,.

Hence,

\displaystyle x(t)=e^{-\gamma t}\mathbb{S}(t)v_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\mathbb{S}(t-s)\big((\gamma+\mathbb{B})x(s)+g(s)\big)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

so that, using (
5.11
),

\displaystyle\|x(t)-v(t)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\,\|\mathbb{S}(t-s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\,\|\gamma+\mathbb{B}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha},\mathbb{E}_{0})}\,\|x(s)-v(s)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\,\|\gamma+\mathbb{B}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha},\mathbb{E}_{0})}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\,e^{(\varsigma_{\alpha}-\gamma)(t-s)}\,\|x(s)-v(s)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}s

for
t\in[0,T]
. Gronwall’s inequality
[
2
, II.Theorem 3.3.1]
implies that indeed
v=x
on
[0,T]
.

(ii)
Consider now
g_{k}\in C^{1}([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{0})
and
v_{0,k}\in D(\mathbb{A})
with

g_{k}\to g\ \text{ in }\ C([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{0})\,,\qquad v_{0,k}\to v_{0}\ \text{ in }\ \mathbb{E}_{0}

as
k\to\infty
.
Define
x
again by (
5.16
) and accordingly

\displaystyle x_{k}(t):=\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t)v_{0,k}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t-s)g_{k}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,.

Taking
\beta\in\{0,\alpha\}
and invoking (
5.15
) we obtain

\displaystyle t^{\beta}\,\|x(t)-x_{k}(t)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq t^{\beta}\,\|\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\beta})}\,\|v_{0}-v_{0,k}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}

\displaystyle\quad+t^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t-s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\beta})}\,\|g(s)-g_{k}(s)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq c(T)\,\|v_{0}-v_{0,k}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}+c(T)\|g-g_{k}\|_{C([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{0})}

for
t\in[0,T]
. Consequently, as
k\to\infty
,

(5.17)

t^{\beta}\,\|x(t)-x_{k}(t)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\beta}}\to 0\ \text{ uniformly with respect to $t\in[0,T]$}\,,\qquad\beta\in\{0,\alpha\}\,.

Since

\displaystyle x_{k}(t)=e^{-\gamma t}\mathbb{S}(t)v_{0,k}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\mathbb{S}(t-s)\,\big((\gamma+\mathbb{B})x_{k}(s)+g_{k}(s)\big)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

according to
(i)
, it thus follows from (
5.17
) (using (
5.11
)) that

(5.18)

\displaystyle x(t)=e^{-\gamma t}\mathbb{S}(t)v_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\mathbb{S}(t-s)\,\big((\gamma+\mathbb{B})x(s)+g(s)\big)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,.

Consequently, (
5.18
) and (
5.11
) entail

\displaystyle\|x(t)-v(t)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\,\|\mathbb{S}(t-s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\,\|\gamma+\mathbb{B}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha},\mathbb{E}_{0})}\,\|x(s)-v(s)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq c(T)\,\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\,\|x(s)-v(s)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}s

for
t\in[0,T]
; that is
v=x
on
[0,T]
by Gronwall’s inequality.
∎

Of course,
5.4
is not restricted to the particular choice of
\partial F(\phi)
for the perturbation
\mathbb{B}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha},\mathbb{E}_{0})
.

Remark 5.5
.

It is worth emphasizing that the strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is the solution operator associated with the linearization (see (
1.4
)) of (
1.2
) given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+\partial_{a}v\,

\displaystyle=A(a)v+\partial F(\phi)v\,,\qquad t>0\,,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

\displaystyle v(t,0)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(v(t))\,,\qquad t>0\,,

\displaystyle v(0,a)

\displaystyle=v_{0}(a)\,,\qquad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

that is,
v(t)=\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t)v_{0}
,
t\geq 0
, defines the unique mild solution for each
v_{0}\in\mathbb{E}_{0}
. See
[
24
]
.

The assumption
\omega_{\alpha}(\phi)>0
in (
5.15
) corresponds to an exponential decay of the semigroup
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
and plays an important role in the subsequent stability analysis. We thus add some comments on this issue.

Remark 5.6
.

(a)
Assuming
\omega_{\alpha}(\phi)>0
in (
5.15
) is equivalent to assuming that the growth bound of the semigroup
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
is negative.

Proof.

Let
\omega(\mathbb{T}_{\phi})
be the growth bound of the semigroup
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
. Clearly, (
5.15
) yields that
\omega(\mathbb{T}_{\phi})\leq-\omega_{\alpha}(\phi)
. Assume now that
\omega(\mathbb{T}_{\phi})<0
. Then, for
\omega(\mathbb{T}_{\phi})<-(\omega+\varepsilon)<-\omega<0
there is
N\geq 1
such that

\|\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\leq N\,e^{-(\omega+\varepsilon)t}\,,\quad t\geq 0\,.

This along with (
5.15
) implies

\|\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\leq\|\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t-1)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\,\|\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(1)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})}\leq N\,e^{-(\omega+\varepsilon)(t-1)}\,N_{\alpha}\,e^{|\omega_{\alpha}|}\leq N_{0}\,e^{-\omega t}\,t^{-\alpha}

for
t\geq 1
and some
N_{0}\geq 1
,
while such an estimate is obviously implied by (
5.15
) for
t\in(0,1)
. Hence,
\omega_{\alpha}(\phi)
can be chosen positive in (
5.15
) if
\omega(\mathbb{T}_{\phi})<0
.
∎

(b)
If the semigroup
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
is eventually compact, then the growth bound of the semigroup
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
coincides with the spectral bound of its generator
\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}
, see
[
5
, IV.Corollary 3.12]
. In some cases (e.g. for the trivial equilibrium
\phi=0
, or if
\mathbb{B}=\partial F(\phi)=0
so that
\mathbb{T}_{\phi}=\mathbb{S}
) one can indeed show the eventual compactness of
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}

[
24
]
so that
\omega_{\alpha}(\phi)>0
in (
5.15
) is equivalent to a negative spectral bound of the generator.

We will get back to this point in
Section

7
.

The Nonlinear Part

We next focus on the nonlinear part
W_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}
for which we shall derive an estimate.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let

and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
W_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}\in C([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})
and there are constants
\mu=\mu(\alpha,b,\phi)>0
and

(both independent of

and

(5.19)

\|W_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}\leq c_{0}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(\varpi+\mu-\gamma)(t-a)}\,(t-a)^{-\alpha}\,\|h(a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,.

Proof.

To prove continuity let
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
. Then, by (
5.3b
), (
5.2
) and (
2.1c
),

\displaystyle\|W_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t,\cdot)-W_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(s,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{s}\|\Pi_{\gamma}(a,0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\alpha})}\,\|B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t-a)-B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(s-a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{s}^{t}\|\Pi_{\gamma}(a,0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\alpha})}\,\|B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t-a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq M_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{s}e^{(\varpi-\gamma)a}a^{-\alpha}\,\|B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t-a)-B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(s-a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\quad+M_{\alpha}\int_{s}^{t}e^{(\varpi-\gamma)a}a^{-\alpha}\,\|B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t-a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a\,.

Since

according to
5.1
, we infer that indeed
W_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}\in C([0,T],\mathbb{E}_{\alpha})
.

As for (
5.19
) we use the corresponding estimate on
B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}
established in (
5.10
). More precisely, we use (
5.10
) along with (
2.1c
) and (
5.2
) in the definition (
5.3b
) of

to derive

\displaystyle\|W_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t\wedge a_{m}}\|\Pi_{\gamma}(a,0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\alpha})}\,\|B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t-a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a\,

\displaystyle\leq M_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}(t-a)^{-\alpha}\,e^{(\varpi-\gamma)(t-a)}\,\|B_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a\,

\displaystyle\leq M_{\alpha}\,c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-a)^{-\alpha}\,e^{(\varpi-\gamma)(t-a)}\,\int_{0}^{a}(a-s)^{-\alpha}\,e^{(\mu+\varpi-\gamma)(a-s)}\,\|h(s)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}a\,

\displaystyle\quad+M_{\alpha}\,c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-a)^{-\alpha}\,e^{(\varpi-\gamma)(t-a)}\,\|h(a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq M_{\alpha}\,c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(\mu+\varpi-\gamma)(t-s)}\,\|h(s)\|_{E_{0}}\,\int_{s}^{t}(t-a)^{-\alpha}\,(a-s)^{-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}s\,

\displaystyle\quad+M_{\alpha}\,c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-a)^{-\alpha}\,e^{(\varpi-\gamma)(t-a)}\,\|h(a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a

for
t\in[0,T]
. Now, noticing

\int_{s}^{t}(t-a)^{-\alpha}\,(a-s)^{-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}a=\mathsf{B}(1-\alpha,1-\alpha)\,(t-s)^{1-2\alpha}\leq c_{\mu}e^{\mu(t-s)}\,(t-s)^{-\alpha}\,,\quad 0\leq s<t\,,

with Beta function

, we conclude

\displaystyle\|W_{0,0}^{\gamma,h}(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}}\leq c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-a)^{-\alpha}\,e^{(2\mu+\varpi-\gamma)(t-a)}\,\|h(a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

as claimed.
∎

) entails that

r(Q_{0})=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(0,a)\,e^{-\int_{0}^{a}m(0,s)\mathrm{d}s}\,\mathrm{d}a\,.

Consequently, we obtain from
7.1
:

Corollary 7.2
.

Assume (
7.5
), and (
7.8
). If

\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(0,a)\,e^{-\int_{0}^{a}m(0,s)\mathrm{d}s}\,\mathrm{d}a<1\,,

then
\phi=0
is an asymptotically exponentially stable equilibrium of problem (
7.4
) in the phase space
L_{1}\big((0,a_{m}),W_{q,N}^{2\alpha}(\Omega)\big)
for
2\alpha\in(n/q,2)\setminus\{1+1/q\}
.

For instance, if the death rate dominates the birth rate in the sense that

b(0,a)\leq m(0,a)\,,\quad a\in J\,,

then

\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(0,a)\,e^{-\int_{0}^{a}m(0,s)\mathrm{d}s}\,\mathrm{d}a\leq\int_{0}^{a_{m}}m(0,a)\,e^{-\int_{0}^{a}m(0,s)\mathrm{d}s}\,\mathrm{d}a=1-e^{-\int_{0}^{a_{m}}m(0,s)\mathrm{d}s}<1

Stability of a Nontrivial Equilibrium of Problem (
1.1
)

We only sketch a particular case for a nontrivial positive equilibrium of problem (
1.1
).
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be bounded with smooth boundary and consider

(7.9a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{a}u

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}_{x}\big(d(a,x)\nabla_{x}u\big)-m\big(a,x\big)u\ ,

\displaystyle t>0\,,

\displaystyle a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

\displaystyle x\in\Omega\,,

(7.9b)

\displaystyle u(t,0,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b\big(\bar{u}(t,x),a\big)u(t,a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,

\displaystyle t>0\,,

\displaystyle x\in\Omega\,,

(7.9c)

\displaystyle\partial_{N}u(t,a,x)

\displaystyle=0\ ,

\displaystyle t>0\,,

\displaystyle a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

\displaystyle x\in\partial\Omega\,,

(7.9d)

\displaystyle u(0,a,x)

\displaystyle=u_{0}(a,x)\ ,

\displaystyle a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

\displaystyle x\in\Omega\,,

with

\bar{v}(x)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\nu(a,x)\,v(a,x)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,.

For the data we assume

(7.10a)

\displaystyle d\in C^{\rho,1}(J\times\bar{\Omega},(0,\infty))\,,

(7.10b)

\displaystyle b\in C^{4,0}(\mathbb{R}\times J,\mathbb{R}^{+})\,,\quad b>0\,,

(7.10c)

\displaystyle m\in C^{\rho,2}(J\times\bar{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{+})\,,

(7.10d)

\displaystyle\nu\in C^{0,2}(J\times\bar{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{+})\,.

Note that we assume
m=m(a,x)
to be independent of
u
. Consider now a (nontrivial) positive equilibrium

\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{1}\cap C(J,E_{0})=L_{1}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(W_{q,N}^{2})\big)\cap C([0,a_{m}],L_{q})

and fix again
2\alpha\in(n/q,2)
. Then, as above,

b(\bar{\phi},\cdot)\in L_{\infty}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(W_{q,N}^{2\theta})\big)\,,\quad 2\alpha\leq 2\theta<2\,,

and

\big[v\mapsto\partial b(\bar{\phi},\cdot)[v]\phi\big]\in\mathcal{L}\big(W_{q,N}^{2\theta},L_{1}(J,W_{q,N}^{2\theta})\big)\,,\quad 2\alpha\leq 2\theta<2\,,

that is, (
2.7c
) and (
2.7d
) are satisfied for
\theta\in(\alpha,1)
. Since
m=m(a,x)
is independent of
u
, we may put

A(a)w:=\mathrm{div}_{x}\big(d(a,\cdot)\nabla_{x}w\big)-m(a,\cdot)w\,,\quad w\in W_{q,N}^{2}\,,\quad a\in J\,,

from the very beginning. Then
A\in C^{\rho}\big(J,\mathcal{H}(W_{q,N}^{2},L_{q})\big)
and we may interpret
F=0
in (
2.4
). Then the semigroup
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
coincides with the semigroup
(\mathbb{S}(t))_{t\geq 0}
from
5.3
and is thus
eventually compact on
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
according to
[
24
, Corollary 2.2]
. Hence, its growth bound coincides with the spectral bound
s_{\alpha}
of its generator due to
[
5
, IV. Corollary 3.12]
, and the spectrum of the generator consists of eigenvalues only
[
5
, V. Corollary 3.2]
. Clearly, any such eigenvalue is also an eigenvalue of the generator of the semigroup
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
considered on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
, hence
s_{\alpha}\leq s_{0}
. Assuming further that

(7.10e)

\displaystyle\partial_{1}b(\bar{\phi}(x),a)\geq 0\,,\quad(a,x)\in J\times\Omega\,,

it follows from
[
24
, Theorem 2.8]
that
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a positive semigroup on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
. One then argues as in
[
24
, Proposition 5.2]
to conclude that
s_{0}
is an eigenvalue of the generator of
(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}(t))_{t\geq 0}
.
If
\psi
is a corresponding eigenfunction, it satisfies (see
[
24
]
)

(7.11)

\displaystyle\partial_{a}\psi\,

\displaystyle=(-s_{0}+A(a))\psi\,,\quad a\in J\,,

(7.12)

\displaystyle\psi(0)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(\psi)

with (using (
7.7
))

\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(\psi)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(\bar{\phi},a)\,\psi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\partial_{1}b(\bar{\phi},a)\,\phi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\nu(a)\,\psi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,.

Plugging this into (

\psi(0)=Q_{\phi,{s_{0}}}\psi(0)\,,

where

Q_{\phi,{\lambda}}z:=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(\bar{\phi},a)\,\Pi_{\lambda}(a,0)\,z\,\mathrm{d}a+\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\partial_{1}b(\bar{\phi},a)\,\phi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\nu(a)\,\Pi_{\lambda}(a,0)\,z\,\mathrm{d}a

is a compact operator on
E_{0}=L_{q}
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence,
1
is an eigenvalue of
Q_{\phi,{s_{0}}}
so that

1\leq r(Q_{\phi,{s_{0}}})\,.

By (

) and (
7.10e
),
the operator

\displaystyle r(Q_{\phi,0})<1

s_{0}<0

on
\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}

:

Corollary 7.3
.

Let

The limits of Kähler manifolds under holomorphic deformations
Mu-Lin Li, Wanmin Liu

Abstract
. Under mild assumptions on the metric and topology of the central fiber, we prove that the limit of Kähler manifolds under holomorphic deformation is still Kähler.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
32G05, (Primary); 53C55, 32Q15 (Secondary)

1.
Introduction

A
smooth family of compact complex manifolds
is defined as a proper holomorphic submersion
f:X\to B
between complex manifolds
X
and
B
. The following conjecture regarding families of compact complex manifolds has been a longstanding problem.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
f:X\to\Delta=\{z\in\mathbb{C},|z|<1\}
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds such that all fibers
X_{t}:=f^{-1}(t)
are Kähler for
t\in\Delta^{\star}:=\Delta\setminus\{0\}
. Then the central fiber
X_{0}:=f^{-1}(0)
is a manifold in Fujiki class
{\cal C}
.

The case where
\dim X_{t}=2
has been resolved affirmatively, relying on the
theory of classification of surfaces and Siu’s result
[
21
]
(see also
[
5
]
,
[
15
]
).

We list some results for the case
\dim X_{t}\geq 3
. Hironaka
[
13
]
shows that the limit of Kähler manifolds of dimension three may not be Kähler again. Popovici
[
17
,
18
]
proved that if
X_{t}
is projective for
t\neq 0
, then
X_{0}
is Moishezon under some conditions on the Hodge numbers
h_{\overline{\partial}}^{0,1}(X_{t})
or on the metric on
X_{0}
. Under the same conditions, Barlet
[
1
]
showed that if
X_{t}
is Moishezon for
t\neq 0
, then
X_{0}
is also Moishezon. Rao and Tsai
[
19
]
proved that if there exist uncountably many Moishezon fibers in the family, then any fiber satisfying either of the above two conditions is still Moishezon.

When
X_{t}
are Kähler manifolds for
t\neq 0
with
\dim X_{t}\geq 3
, very few results are known, to the best of the authors’ knowledge. To establish that
X_{0}
is Kähler, we focus on manifolds that satisfy specific cohomological and metric conditions, inspired by the aforementioned results. Our findings are as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f:X\to\Delta
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds, where
X_{t}
is Kähler for
t\neq 0
. Assume the following conditions hold:

•

X_{0}
admits a metric
\omega
satisfying
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
and
\partial\omega\wedge\overline{\partial}\omega=0
.

•

h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0})=h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{t})
for
t\neq 0
.

Then
X_{0}
is a Kähler manifold.

The construction of non-Kähler compact complex manifolds equipped with metrics
\omega
satisfying
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
and
\partial\omega\wedge\overline{\partial}\omega=0
has attracted considerable attention in complex geometry. See the examples of such nilmanifolds in
[
10
, Section 2.3]
.

As a corollary, we provide an alternative proof of the result that the limit of Kähler surfaces under holomorphic deformation is also Kähler (Corollary
1.3
). Our proof does not rely on the classification theory of compact complex surfaces.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
f:X\to\Delta
be a smooth family of complex compact surfaces, where
X_{t}
is Kähler for
t\neq 0
. Then
X_{0}
is also Kähler.

The structure of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we recall a criterion for determining whether a compact complex manifold
X
is Kähler, provided it is equipped with a special metric
\omega
. In Section 3, we construct a special
d
-closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
on the central fiber
X_{0}
, ensuring that the volume
\int_{X_{0}}T_{ac}^{n}>0
, under certain numerical conditions on the dimensions of the cohomologies. This is achieved by applying the result of Demailly and Păun
[
9
, Theorem 0.9]
. In Section 4, we prove the main results of this paper by combining the findings from Sections 2 and 3.

2.
Currents on Complex Manifolds

A compact complex manifold
X
is said to be
in Fujiki class
{\cal C}
if there exists a complex Kähler manifold
Y
and a surjective meromorphic map
h:Y\to X
. These manifolds were first introduced by Fujiki
[
12
]
.

Let
{\cal A}^{i,j}
denote the sheaf of smooth
(i,j)
-forms on
X
, and let
{\cal E}^{i,j}(X):=\Gamma(X,{\cal A}^{i,j})
represent the global smooth
(i,j)
-forms on
X
.

Let
\omega
be a smooth
(1,1)
-form on
X
, which can be locally written as
\omega=\sqrt{-1}h_{i\bar{j}}dz_{i}\wedge d\bar{z}_{j}
. The form
\omega
is called
positive
(resp.
semi-positive
) if

(h_{i\bar{j}})
is a positive (resp.
semi-positive
) definite Hermitian matrix. The space of currents of degree
(p,q)
over
X
is denoted by
{\cal D}^{p,q}(X)
. A current of degree
(p,q)
can be regarded as an element
T
in the dual space
({\cal E}^{n-p,n-q}(X))^{\prime}
.
A current
T
of degree
(p,p)
is said to be
positive
if, for any choice of smooth
(1,0)
-forms
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n-p}
on
X
,

T\wedge\sqrt{-1}\alpha_{1}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\wedge\sqrt{-1}\alpha_{n-p}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{n-p}}

is a positive measure. For a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
on
X
, let
T_{ac}
denote the absolutely continuous part of
T
in its Lebesgue decomposition
T=T_{ac}+T_{sg}
. So
T_{ac}
is considered as a
(1,1)
-form
with
L_{loc}^{1}
coefficients, so that the product
T^{m}_{ac}
is taken pointwise for
0\leq m\leq n
.

A
Kähler current
on a compact complex space
X
is defined to be a closed positive current
T
of bidegree
(1,1)
satisfying
T\geq\epsilon\omega
, where
\epsilon
is a positive number and
\omega
is a smooth positive Hermitian form on
X
.

There are several other methods to guarantee that a manifold belongs to Fujiki class
{\cal C}
. One of them is the following theorem, which combines
[
22
, Theorem 5]
and
[
9
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold of dimension
n
. The
following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

X
is in Fujiki class
{\cal C}
.

(2)

There exists a Kähler current
T
on
X
.

If the compact complex manifold
X
is equipped with a special metric, we can derive a criterion for
X
to be Kähler.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
23
, Theorem 1.2]

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional compact complex manifold with a metric
\omega
satisfying
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
and
\partial\omega\wedge\overline{\partial}\omega=0
. If there is a closed positive current
T
on
X
such that
\int_{X}T_{ac}^{n}>0
, where
T_{ac}
is the absolutely continuous part of the Lebesgue decomposition of
T
, then
X
is Kähler.

3.
Cohomologies

There are several different types of cohomology theories for compact complex manifolds. We consider three of them: the Bott–Chern cohomology, the Dolbeault cohomology and the de Rham cohomology.

3.1.
Cohomologies of Complex Manifolds

Let
{\cal E}^{i,j}(X)=\Gamma(X,{\cal A}^{i,j})
denote the global smooth
(i,j)
-forms on
X
, where
{\cal A}^{i,j}
is the sheaf of smooth
(i,j)
-forms on
X
. Then, we have a double complex
({\cal E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(X)
,
\partial,\overline{\partial})
. Let
{\cal E}^{k}(X)=\oplus_{i+j=k}{\cal E}^{i,j}(X)
, and define the total complex
({\cal E}^{\bullet}(X),d=\partial+\overline{\partial})
. There are two natural filtrations of the complex
({\cal E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(X),\partial,\overline{\partial})
, namely, for
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

{{}^{\prime}F}^{p}({\cal E}^{k}(X)):=\bigoplus_{r+s=k,r\geq p}{\cal E}^{r,s}(X),\quad\text{and}\quad{{}^{\prime\prime}F}^{q}({\cal E}^{k}(X)):=\bigoplus_{r+s=k,s\geq q}{\cal E}^{r,s}(X),

which induce two spectral sequences:

({{}^{\prime}E}^{\bullet,\bullet}_{r},\ {{}^{\prime}d}_{r})\quad\quad\text{and }\quad\quad({{}^{\prime\prime}E}^{\bullet,\bullet}_{r},\ {{}^{\prime\prime}d}_{r}),\quad r\in\mathbb{N}.

These spectral sequences are called the
Hodge spectral sequence
and the
Frölicher spectral sequence
, respectively.
Both spectral sequences converge to the de Rham cohomology
H^{\bullet}_{dR}(X,\mathbb{C})
.

For the differential complex
({\cal E}^{p,\bullet},\overline{\partial})
, the
Dolbeault cohomology
is defined as

H^{\bullet,\bullet}_{\overline{\partial}}(X,\mathbb{C}):=\frac{\ker\overline{\partial}}{\im\overline{\partial}}.

The
Bott–Chern cohomology
of
X
is the bi-graded algebra

H^{\bullet,\bullet}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{C})\;:=\;\frac{\ker\partial\cap\ker\overline{\partial}}{\im\partial\overline{\partial}}.

Let
h_{\overline{\partial}}^{p,q}(X)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{p,q}_{\overline{\partial}}(X,\mathbb{C})
,
h^{k}(X)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{k}_{dR}(X,\mathbb{C})
, and
h_{BC}^{p,q}(X)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{p,q}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{C})
.

The
Aeppli cohomology
of
X
is the bi-graded
H^{\bullet,\bullet}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{C})
-module

H^{\bullet,\bullet}_{A}(X,\mathbb{C})\;:=\;\frac{\ker\partial\overline{\partial}}{\im\partial+\im\overline{\partial}}\;.

The following theorem is well-known, and we refer to
[
20
, Lemma 2.5]
for a proof.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold of complex of dimension n. For any
(p,q)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, the wedge
\wedge
-operator induces a non-degeneration bilinear map

\begin{CD}H^{p,q}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{C})\times H^{n-p,n-q}_{A}(X,\mathbb{C})@>{{}^{\wedge}}>{}>H^{n,n}_{A}(X,\mathbb{C})@>{{}^{\int_{X}}}>{}>\mathbb{C}\end{CD}

\begin{CD}(\alpha,\beta)@>{}>{}>\{\alpha\}\cdot\{\beta\}.\end{CD}

Fix a Hermitian metric on
X
, we can define the following differential operator,

\Delta_{BC}:=(\partial\overline{\partial})(\partial\overline{\partial})^{*}+(\partial\overline{\partial})^{*}(\partial\overline{\partial})+(\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial)(\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial)^{*}+(\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial)^{*}(\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial)+\overline{\partial}^{*}\overline{\partial}+\partial^{*}\partial,

which is a self-adjoint elliptic differential operator. Therefore, the following results hold.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold endowed
with a Hermitian metric. Then there exists an orthogonal decomposition

{\cal E}^{p,q}(X)=\ker\Delta_{BC}\oplus\im\partial\overline{\partial}\oplus(\im\partial^{*}+\im\overline{\partial}^{*}),

and an isomorphism

H^{\bullet,\bullet}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{C})\cong\ker\Delta_{BC}.

Proof.

For a proof, we refer to
[
20
, Theorem 2.2]
.
∎

We also recall the classical result of Kodaira and Spencer concerning the upper semi-continuity of the kernel dimensions.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
f:X\to B
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds, and
X_{b}
be the fiber of
f
over
b\in B
. Then, for every
p\,,q\in\mathbb{N}
, the function

b\in B\mapsto\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{p,q}_{BC}(X_{b},\mathbb{C})

is upper semi-continuous.

Proof.

For a proof, we refer to
[
20
, Lemma 3.2]
.
∎

By applying Kodaira and Spencer’s
[
14
, Theorem 5]
to the differential operator
\Delta_{BC}
, we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
f:X\to B
be a complex analytic family of compact complex manifolds. If, for every
p\,,q\in\mathbb{N}
, the function
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{p,q}_{BC}(X_{b},\mathbb{C})
is a constant function, then
\cup_{b\in B}H^{p,q}_{BC}(X_{b},\mathbb{C})
is a
C^{\infty}
-bundle over
B
.

For a compact complex manifold
X
, we have the following short exact sequence

(3.1)

\begin{CD}H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{C})@>{{}^{\phi}}>{}>H^{2}_{dR}(X,\mathbb{C})@>{{}^{u}}>{}>H^{2,0}_{\partial}(X,\mathbb{C})\oplus H^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X,\mathbb{C}),\end{CD}

where

u:\alpha=\alpha^{2,0}+\alpha^{1,1}+\alpha^{0,2}\mapsto\alpha^{2,0}+\alpha^{0,2}

is the projection. Then the following property holds.

Proposition 3.5
.

[
20
, Lemma 3.3]

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold. Then

h^{2}(X)\leq 2h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X)+h^{1,1}_{BC}(X).

If
X
is Kähler, the above inequality becomes an equality.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
f:X\to B
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds. Suppose that the fibers
X_{b}
are Kähler for all
b\in B
with
b\neq b_{0}
. If
h^{2}(X_{b_{0}})=2h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{b_{0}})+h^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{b_{0}})
, then
\cup_{b\in B}H^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{b},\mathbb{C})
is a
C^{\infty}
-subbundle of
R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{C}
over
B
.

Proof.

By the upper semi-continuity of Bott–Chern cohomology and Dolbeault cohomology, we have the following inequalities:

\displaystyle h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{b})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{b_{0}}),

\displaystyle h^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{b})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{b_{0}}).

Thus for
b\neq b_{0}
,

\displaystyle h^{2}(X_{b})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{b})+h^{2,0}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{b})+h^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{b})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{b})+h^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{b})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{b_{0}})+h^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{b_{0}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{2}(X_{b_{0}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{2}(X_{b}).

Thus,

h^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{b})=h^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{b_{0}}).

It follows that
\cup_{b\in B}H^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{b},\mathbb{C})
forms a
C^{\infty}
-bundle.

Since
h^{2}(X_{b})=2h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{b})+h^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{b})
for all
b\in B
, the morphism
\phi
in the exact sequence (
3.1
) is injective for every
X_{b}
. Therefore,
\cup_{b\in B}H^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{b},\mathbb{C})
is a
C^{\infty}
-subbundle of
R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{C}
over
B
.
∎

3.2.
Construction of Positive Current

To construct a positive current on the central fiber, we first introduce some notation regarding the Barlet cycle space of compact complex manifolds.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
f:X\to B
be a smooth proper morphism. A smooth
B
-relative
(1,1)
-form
\omega_{X/B}
is called a
B
-relative Kähler form if its restriction to each fiber
X_{b}
is a Kähler form for all
b\in B
.

Suppose that the fiber
X_{b}
is Kähler. Then by
[
14
, Section 6]
, there exists an open neighborhood
U_{b}\subset B
containing
b
, such that the restriction family
f^{-1}(U_{b})\to U_{b}
admits a
U_{b}
-relative Kähler form
\omega_{f^{-1}(U_{b})/U_{b}}
.

Let
n=\dim X_{b}
denote the dimension of the fiber
X_{b}
for
b\in B
. For
0\leq k\leq n
, let
C^{k}(X/B)
be the
B
-relative Barlet space, which parametrizes the
k
-dimensional cycles in
X
over
B
. There exists a canonical morphism

\mu:C(X/B):=\cup_{k}C^{k}(X/B)\to B.

For a base change
T\to B
, it follows that

C(X\times_{B}T/T)=C(X/B)\times_{B}T.

We believe that the following properness property of the morphism
\mu
is well-known to experts. However, since we could not find a suitable reference for this result, we state it here along with its proof.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
f:X\to B
be a smooth proper morphism with a
B
-relative Kähler form
\omega_{X/B}
. Suppose
A
is a connected component of
C(X/B):=\cup_{k}C^{k}(X/B)
. Then the restricted morphism
\mu|_{A}:A\to B
is proper.

Proof.

The proof follows a similar approach to
[
1
, Proposition 3.0.6]
, which addresses the case of relative cycle spaces
C^{n-1}(X/B)
.
Since
\omega_{X/B}
is a
B
-relative Kähler form on
X
, the form
\omega_{X/B}^{k}
is closed when restricted to the fiber
X_{b}
, for
1\leq k\leq n
. For a
k
-dimensional relative cycle
Z
, define the quantity
Q(Z):=\int_{Z}\omega^{k}_{X/B}
. This is a continuous function on
Z
. On any given compact set
K\subset B
, the function
Q
is bounded. Since
Q
is locally constant on the fibers of the projection
C^{k}(X/B)\to B
, for a connected component
A
of
C(X/B):=\cup_{k}C^{k}(X/B)
, the restricted morphism
\mu|_{A}:A\to B
is proper by Bishop’s theorem
[
3
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
f:X\to B
be a smooth proper morphism with fibers
X_{b}
being Kähler for all
b\in B
. Suppose
A
is a connected component of
C(X/B):=\cup_{k}C^{k}(X/B)
. Then the restricted morphism
\mu|_{A}:A\to B
is proper.

Proof.

Since the fibers
X_{b}
are Kähler for all
b\in B
, there exists a small neighborhood
U\subset B
of any
b\in B
such that the restricted family
f_{b}:f^{-1}(U)\to U
admits an
U
-relative Kähler form. Consequently, the induced morphism

\mu_{b}:C(f^{-1}(U)/U)\to U

is proper on when restricts on an arbitrary component. It is straightforward to verify that
C(f^{-1}(U)/U)=C(X/B)\times_{B}U
, and
\mu_{b}
is the restriction of
\mu
to
C(f^{-1}(U)/U)
. Therefore, for a connected component
A
of
C(X/B)
, the map
A\times_{B}U\to U
is proper. Thus the restricted morphism
\mu|_{A}:A\to B
is proper.
∎

If all the fibers of the family
f:X\to B
are Kähler manifolds, then the bundles

\cup_{b\in B}H^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{b},\mathbb{C}),\quad\cup_{b\in B}H^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{b},\mathbb{C}),\quad\cup_{b\in B}H^{2,0}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{b},\mathbb{C})

are all
C^{\infty}
-subbundles of
R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{C}
. Denote these subbundles by

{\cal F}_{B}^{p,q},\text{ with }p+q=2,p\geq 0,q\geq 0.

This gives the following
C^{\infty}
-decomposition:

R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{C}={\cal F}^{2,0}_{B}\oplus{\cal F}^{1,1}_{B}\oplus{\cal F}^{0,2}_{B}.

The Gauss–Manin connection
\nabla
on
R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{C}
can then be expressed as

\nabla=\left[{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\nabla^{2,0}&*&*\\
&\nabla^{1,1}&*\\
&*&\nabla^{0,2}\end{array}}\right],

where
\nabla^{p,q}
are the induced connections on the subbundles
{\cal F}^{p,q}_{B}
.

Using the properness of connected component of the Barlet cycle space, Demailly and Păun
[
9
]
proved the following celebrated theorem on the Kähler cone.

Theorem 3.10
.

[
9
, Theorem 0.9]

Let
f:X\to S
be a deformation of compact Kähler manifolds over an irreducible base
S
. Then there exists a countable union
S^{\prime}=\cup S_{\nu}
of analytic subsets
S_{\nu}\subset S
, such that the Kähler cones
{\cal K}_{t}\subset H^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{t},\mathbb{C})
are invariant over
S\setminus S^{\prime}
under parallel transport with respect to the
(1,1)
-projection
\nabla^{1,1}
of the Gauss–Manin connection.

This theorem can be used to produce a closed positive current on the central fiber.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
f:X\to\Delta
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds, with
X_{t}
being Kähler when
t\neq 0
and
\dim X_{t}=n
. Assume that
h^{2}(X_{0})=2h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0})+h^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{0})
. Then there exists a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
on
X_{0}
with
\int_{X_{0}}T_{ac}^{n}>0
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta^{\star}=\Delta\setminus\{0\}
, and consider the restriction
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\triangleq f^{-1}(\Delta^{\star})\to\Delta^{\star}
. The morphism
f^{\prime}
is smooth and all fibers are Kähler manifolds.

By Proposition
3.9
, the morphism
\mu:C(X^{\prime}/\Delta^{\star}):=\cup_{k}C^{k}(X^{\prime}/\Delta^{\star})\to\Delta^{\star}
is proper for
0\leq k\leq n
. Denote by
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
the union of the image in
\Delta^{\star}
of those components of
C(X^{\prime}/\Delta^{\star})
that do not project onto
\Delta^{\star}
. By
[
11
, Theorem]
there are at most countably many components of
C(X^{\prime}/\Delta^{\star})
. Since
\mu
is proper,
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
is an union of countably many proper analytic closed subsets of
\Delta^{\star}
.

Let
\Sigma^{\prime}:=\Delta^{\star}\setminus\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
, and let
\Sigma:=\Sigma^{\prime}\cup\{0\}=\Delta\setminus\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
. It follows that
\Sigma
is arcwise connected by piecewise smooth analytic arcs. For
t_{0}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
, consider a smooth arc

\gamma:[0,1]\to\Sigma,\quad\quad u\mapsto\gamma(u),

satisfying
\gamma(0)=0
and
\gamma(1)=t_{0}
.

Since
f:X\to\Delta
is a smooth family of compact complex manifolds, the sheaf
R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{C}
is locally constant on
\Delta
. There exists a Gauss–Manin connection
\nabla
on
R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{C}
. By the assumption
h^{2}(X_{0})=2h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0})+h^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{0})
, Theorem
3.6
implies that
{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{1,1}=\cup_{t\in\Delta}H^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{t},\mathbb{C})
is a smooth subbundle of
R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{C}
. Let
{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{2}
be the smooth orthogonal complement of
{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{1,1}
in
R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{C}
, so that
{\cal F}_{\Delta,t}^{2}\cong H^{2,0}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{t},\mathbb{C})\oplus H^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{t},\mathbb{C})
for
t\in\Delta^{\star}
. Denote by
\nabla^{1,1}
the restriction of the Gauss–Manin connection
\nabla
to
{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{1,1}
.

Let
\alpha(u)\in H^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{\gamma(u)},\mathbb{C})
for
u\in[0,1]
be a family of real
(1,1)
-cohomology classes obtained by parallel transport under
\nabla^{1,1}
along
\gamma
. From the argument in the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 0.9]
, we can assume that
\alpha(u)
is constant under the parallel transport for
u\in(0,1]
. This implies that
\nabla\alpha(u)\in H^{2,0}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{\gamma(u)},\mathbb{C})\oplus H^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{\gamma(u)},\mathbb{C})
for
u\in(0,1]
. Consequently,

\frac{d}{du}\bigg([X_{\gamma(u)}]\cdot\alpha^{n}(u)\bigg)=n[X_{\gamma(u)}]\cdot\alpha^{n-1}(u)\cdot\nabla\alpha(u)=0,

and thus

[X_{\gamma(u)}]\cdot\alpha^{n}(u)=c

is constant for
u\in(0,1]
.

Suppose that
\alpha(1)
is a Kähler class on
X_{\gamma(1)}
for
t_{0}=\gamma(1)\in\Sigma^{\prime}
.
By Theorem
3.10
, the Kähler cone
{\cal K}_{\gamma(u)}\subset H^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{\gamma(u)},\mathbb{C})\cong H^{1,1}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{\gamma(u)},\mathbb{C})
of the fibers
X_{\gamma(u)}
are invariant along
\gamma((0,1])
under the parallel transport with respect to
\nabla^{1,1}
, thus the classes
\alpha(u)
are Kähler classes for all
u\in(0,1]
.

Let
\omega_{\gamma(u)}
be a smooth positive
(1,1)
-closed form in the class
\alpha(u)
for
u\in(0,1]
. Let
g_{t}
be a smooth family of Gauduchon metrics after possibly shrinking on
\pi:X\to\Delta
. Then

\int_{X_{\gamma(u)}}\omega_{\gamma(u)}\wedge g_{\gamma(u)}^{n-1}=\alpha(u)\cdot\{g_{\gamma(u)}^{n-1}\},

where
\{g_{\gamma(u)}^{n-1}\}\in H^{n-1,n-1}_{A}(X_{\gamma(u)},\mathbb{C})
is the corresponding class.

Since
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H_{BC}^{1,1}(X_{\gamma(u)},\mathbb{C})
is independent of
u\in[0,1]
, there is a
d
-closed smooth
(1,1)
-form
\Omega
on
X|_{\gamma([0,1])}
such that
[\Omega|_{X_{\gamma(u)}}]=\alpha(u)
in
H^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{\gamma(u)},\mathbb{C})
for any
u\in[0,1]
by the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 9]
after replacing the Dolbeault Laplacian by
\Delta_{BC,t}
.
Now we have that

\alpha(u)\cdot\{g_{\gamma(u)}^{n-1}\}=\int_{X_{\gamma(u)}}\Omega\wedge g_{\gamma(u)}^{n-1},

which is point-wisely smoothly dependent on
u\in[0,1]
. Therefore
\alpha(u)\cdot\{g_{\gamma(u)}^{n-1}\}
is finite for
u\in[0,1]
. Thus there exits a constant
C
such that

0<\int_{X_{\gamma(u)}}\omega_{\gamma(u)}\wedge g_{\gamma(u)}^{n-1}<C,

for
u\in(0,1]
after shrinking the disc small enough. Thus there exists a weakly convergent sequence
\omega_{\gamma(u_{k})}\to T
as
u_{k}\to 0
. The limit current
T
is a positive
d
-closed
(1,1)
-currents. Let
T_{ac}
be the absolutely continuous part of
T
. The semi-continuity property
[
4
, Proposition 2.1]
for the top power of the absolutely continuous part in
(1,1)
-currents shows that,

T^{n}_{ac}\geq\lim\sup\omega_{\gamma(u_{k})}^{n}.

Thus

\int_{X_{0}}T^{n}_{ac}\geq\int_{X_{\gamma(u_{k})}}\omega_{\gamma(u_{k})}^{n}=c>0.

∎

3.3.
Cohomology under Special Metrics

In this section we study the behaviors of the cohomologies of compact complex manifolds equipped with special metrics.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold of dimension
n
, equipped with a metric
\omega
such that
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=\partial\overline{\partial}(\omega^{2})=0
. Then for
0\leq p\leq n
the identity map

\iota_{p}:H^{p,0}_{\overline{\partial}}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{p}_{dR}(X,\mathbb{C}),

is well-defined and injective. Similarly, the conjugate map

\psi_{p}:H^{p,0}_{\overline{\partial}}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{0,p}_{\overline{\partial}}(X,\mathbb{C})

is also well-defined and injective.

Proof.

Since
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=\partial\overline{\partial}(\omega^{2})=0
, it follows that
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega^{p}=0
for all
1\leq p\leq n
.
Let
z\in X
and choose local coordinates
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
around
z
such that the metric
\omega
has the form

\omega=\sqrt{-1}\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}(z)dz_{j}\wedge d\bar{z}_{j}

where
\gamma_{j}(z)>0
. Let
\alpha=\partial\phi^{p,0}
, where
\phi^{p,0}
is a
(p,0)
-form, and assume that
\overline{\partial}\alpha=\overline{\partial}\partial\phi^{p,0}=0
. Assume that
\partial\phi^{p,0}=\sum_{I}f_{I}dz_{I}
is a summation of
\overline{\partial}
-closed
(p+1,0)
-forms, where
I=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{p+1})
is an ordered subset of
\{1,\cdots,n\}
with
|I|=p+1
and
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p+1}
. Then

\displaystyle\quad\partial\phi^{p,0}\wedge\overline{\partial}\overline{\phi^{p,0}}\wedge\omega^{n-p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad C\bigg(\sum_{I}|f_{I}|^{2}\gamma_{n-I}(z)\bigg)(\sqrt{-1})^{n}dz_{1}\wedge d\bar{z}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}\wedge d\bar{z}_{n}

where
\gamma_{n-I}(z):=\prod_{k\notin I}\gamma_{k}(z)
and
C=(-1)^{\frac{|I|(|I|-1)}{2}}(-\sqrt{-1})^{|I|}
is a nonzero constant.
So

\displaystyle\quad C\int_{X}\bigg(\sum_{I}|f_{I}|^{2}\gamma_{n-I}(z)\bigg)(\sqrt{-1})^{n}dz_{1}\wedge d\bar{z}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}\wedge d\bar{z}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\int_{X}\partial\phi^{p,0}\wedge\overline{\partial}\overline{\phi^{p,0}}\wedge\omega^{n-p-1}=0.

Thus
\alpha=\partial\phi^{p,0}=0
.

Now let
\alpha^{p,0}\in H_{\overline{\partial}}^{p,0}(X,\mathbb{C})
, then
\overline{\partial}\partial\alpha^{p,0}=0
. So the argument above shows that
\partial\alpha^{p,0}=0
. Therefore, every
\overline{\partial}
-closed
(p,0)
-form is also
d
-closed.
The identity map

\iota_{p}:H^{p,0}_{\overline{\partial}}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{p}_{dR}(X,\mathbb{C}),

is thus well-defined.

To prove that
\iota_{p}
is injective, suppose that
\iota_{p}(\beta^{p,0})=0
for some
\overline{\partial}
-closed
(p,0)
-form
\beta^{p,0}
. Then
\beta^{p,0}=\partial\gamma^{p-1,0}
, where
\gamma^{p-1,0}
is a
(p-1,0)
-form. Using the same argument as above, we conclude that
\beta^{p,0}=0
.
Thus the map
\iota_{p}
is injective.

Because the
\overline{\partial}
-closed
p
-form is also
d
-closed, the conjugate map

\psi_{p}:H^{p,0}_{\overline{\partial}}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{0,p}_{\overline{\partial}}(X,\mathbb{C})

is well-defined. To prove that
\psi_{p}
is injective, suppose that
\psi_{p}(\beta)=\bar{\beta}=0\in H^{0,p}_{\overline{\partial}}(X,\mathbb{C})
for some
\beta\in H^{p,0}_{\overline{\partial}}(X,\mathbb{C})
. Then there exists a form
\alpha^{0,p-1}\in{\cal E}^{0,p-1}(X)
such that
\bar{\beta}=\overline{\partial}\alpha^{0,p-1}
.
So by the same calculation as above, we have

\int_{X}\partial\overline{\alpha^{0,p-1}}\wedge\overline{\partial}\alpha^{0,p-1}\wedge\omega^{n-p}=0,

which implies that
\beta=0
. Therefore the conjugate map
\psi_{p}
is also injective.
∎

Let
{\cal P}
be the sheaf of real-valued pluriharmonic functions over a compact complex manifold
X
. We have the following fine resolution,

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>{\cal P}@>{}>{}>({\cal A}_{X})_{\mathbb{R}}@>{{}^{\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}}}>{}>({\cal A}^{1,1}_{X})_{\mathbb{R}}\\
@>{{}^{d}}>{}>({\cal A}^{2,1}_{X}\oplus{\cal A}^{1,2}_{X})_{\mathbb{R}}@>{{}^{d}}>{}>\cdots.\end{CD}

From
[
16
, Page 20]
, we know that the
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}H^{1}(X,{\cal P})
is finite, and

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}H^{1}(X,{\cal P})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H_{BC}^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{C}).

On the other hand, consider the following short exact sequence.

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>\mathbb{R}@>{}>{}>{\cal O}_{X}@>{}>{}>{\cal P}@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

This gives rise to the following long exact sequence on sheaf cohomology

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>H^{0}(X,\mathbb{R})@>{}>{}>H^{0}(X,{\cal O}_{X})@>{}>{}>H^{0}(X,{\cal P})\\
@>{}>{}>H^{1}(X,\mathbb{R})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(X,{\cal O}_{X})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(X,{\cal P})\\
@>{}>{}>H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})@>{{}^{\pi^{0,2}}}>{}>H^{2}(X,{\cal O}_{X}).\end{CD}

The first three terms in the sequence form a short exact sequence

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>\mathbb{R}@>{}>{}>\mathbb{R}^{\oplus 2}@>{}>{}>\mathbb{R}@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

So we have

(3.2)

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>H^{1}(X,\mathbb{R})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(X,{\cal O}_{X})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(X,{\cal P})\\
@>{}>{}>H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})@>{{}^{\pi^{0,2}}}>{}>H^{2}(X,{\cal O}_{X}).\end{CD}

The morphism
\pi^{0,2}
is the projection of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})\subset H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
to the
(0,2)
-part
H^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X,\mathbb{C})
through the isomorphism
H^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X,\mathbb{C})\cong H^{2}(X,{\cal O}_{X})
.
Let
H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})=\mathrm{ker}\,\pi^{0,2}
be the subgroup of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
consisting of
d
-closed real
(1,1)
-forms. Then one has the
exact sequence of real vector spaces,

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>H^{1}(X,\mathbb{R})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(X,{\cal O}_{X})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(X,{\cal P})\\
@>{}>{}>H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

Thus, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.13
.

[
16
, Theorem 6.3]

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold, then

h^{1,1}_{BC}(X)=2h^{0,1}_{\overline{\partial}}-h^{1}(X)+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R}).

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
f:X\to\Delta
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds. Assume that
h_{\overline{\partial}}^{0,2}(X_{t})
is constant for all
t\in\Delta
, and there exists a Hermitian metric
\omega
on the central fiber
X_{0}
satisfying
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=\partial\overline{\partial}\omega^{2}=0
. Then

h^{2}(X_{0})=2h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0})+h^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{0}).

Proof.

Since the Hermitian metric
\omega
on
X_{0}
satisfies
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=\partial\overline{\partial}\omega^{2}=0
, by Proposition
3.12
, we have the following inequality

h_{\overline{\partial}}^{0,2}(X_{0})\geq h_{\overline{\partial}}^{2,0}(X_{0}).

From the assumptions we know that for
t\neq 0
,

h_{\overline{\partial}}^{2,0}(X_{t})=h_{\overline{\partial}}^{0,2}(X_{t})=h_{\overline{\partial}}^{0,2}(X_{0}).

Thus

h_{\overline{\partial}}^{2,0}(X_{t})\geq h_{\overline{\partial}}^{2,0}(X_{0}).

On the other hand, by the upper semi-continuity of the Dolbeault cohomology, we know that

h_{\overline{\partial}}^{2,0}(X_{t})\leq h_{\overline{\partial}}^{2,0}(X_{0}).

So

h_{\overline{\partial}}^{2,0}(X_{t})=h_{\overline{\partial}}^{2,0}(X_{0}).

It follows that

h_{\overline{\partial}}^{0,2}(X_{0})=h_{\overline{\partial}}^{2,0}(X_{0}).

Combining it with Proposition
3.12
, the morphism
H^{2,0}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0},\mathbb{C})\to H^{2}_{dR}(X_{0},\mathbb{C})
is injective and the morphism
\psi_{2}:H^{2,0}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0},\mathbb{C})\to H^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0},\mathbb{C})
is an isomorphism. Consequently, the projection

H^{2}_{dR}(X_{0},\mathbb{C})\to H^{2}(X_{0},{\cal O}_{X_{0}})\cong H^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0},\mathbb{C})

is surjective. Therefore, the projection
\pi^{0,2}:H^{2}(X_{0},\mathbb{R})\to H^{2}(X_{0},{\cal O}_{X_{0}})
in (
3.2
) is also surjective, yielding the exact sequence:

(3.3)

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>H^{1}(X_{0},\mathbb{R})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(X_{0},{\cal O}_{X})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(X_{0},{\cal P})\\
@>{}>{}>H^{2}(X_{0},\mathbb{R})@>{{}^{\pi^{0,2}}}>{}>H^{2}(X_{0},{\cal O}_{X_{0}})@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{R}}H^{2}(X_{0},\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{R}}H^{2}(X_{0},{\cal O}_{X_{0}})+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}H^{1}(X_{0},{\cal P})

\displaystyle-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}H^{1}(X_{0},{\cal O}_{X_{0}})+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}H^{1}(X_{0},\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0})+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}H^{1}(X_{0},{\cal P})+h^{1}(X_{0})-2h^{0,1}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0}).

By
[
19
, Proposition 4.12]
, the invariance of
h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{t})
implies that
h^{0,1}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{t})
is also invariant.
Hence for
t\neq 0
,

h^{1}(X_{0})=h^{1}(X_{t})=2h^{0,1}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{t})=2h^{0,1}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0}).

So from the exact sequence (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle h^{2}(X_{0})=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}H^{2}(X_{0},\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0})+\dim_{\mathbb{R}}H^{1}(X_{0},{\cal P})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0})+h^{1,1}_{BC}(X_{0}).

∎

Proof of the Theorems

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Since
X_{0}
admits a metric
\omega
such that
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
and

, it follows that
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega^{p}=0
for all
0\leq p\leq n
. By Proposition
3.14
, Lemma
3.11
and Theorem
2.2
, we conclude that

∎

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Since
X_{0}
is a compact complex surface, the Frölicher spectral sequence degenerates at

. By
[
2
, IV, Theorem 2.9, Proposition 2.10]
, we have

H_{dR}^{2}(X_{0},\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{p+q=2}\,^{\prime}F^{p}H^{2}(X_{0},\mathbb{C})\cap\overline{{}^{\prime}F}^{q}H^{2}(X_{0},\mathbb{C}).

Thus

h^{2}(X_{0})=h^{2,0}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0})+h^{1,1}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0})+h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0}).

By the upper semi-continuity of the Dolbeault cohomology, for

, we have

h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{0})=h^{0,2}_{\overline{\partial}}(X_{t}).

Additionally, the Gauduchon metric on
X_{0}
satisfies
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
. Consequently, by Lemma
3.11
and Theorem
2.2
(or
[
6

), we conclude that
X_{0}

∎

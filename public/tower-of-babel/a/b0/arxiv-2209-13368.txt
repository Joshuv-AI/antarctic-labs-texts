Isometric, symmetric and isosymmetric commuting d d -tuples of Banach space operators
B.P. Duggal, I.H. Kim

Abstract
Generalising the definition to commuting d d -tuples of operators, a number of authors have considered structural properties of m m -isometric, n n -symmetric and ( m , n ) (m,n) -isosymmetric commuting d d -tuples in the recent past. This note is an attempt to take the mystique out of this extension and show how a large number of these properties follow from the more familiar arguments used to prove the single operator version of these properties.

Let
B({\cal H})
(resp.,
B({\cal X})
) denote the algebra of operators, i.e. bounded linear transformations, on an infinite dimensional complex Hilbert space
{\cal H}
into itself (resp., on an infinite dimensional comples Banach space
{\cal X}
into itself),
\mathbb{C}

B({\cal H})^{d}

{\mathbb{C}}^{d}

d

B({\cal H})
(resp.,

\overline{z}

and
{\bf z}=(z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{d})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}

{\bf{P}}
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
and a
d
-tuple
{\mathbb{A}}
of commuting operators in
B({\cal H})^{d}
,
{\mathbb{A}}
is a hereditary root of
{\bf{P}}
if
{\bf{P}}({\mathbb{A}})=0
. Two particular operator classes of hereditary roots which have been studied extensively are those of
m
-symmetric (also called
m
-selfadjoint in the literature) and
m
-isometric operators, where
A\in B({\cal H})
is
m
-symmetric (for some integer
m\geq 1
) if

\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right){A^{*}}^{(m-j)}A^{j}=0

and
A\in B({\cal H})
is
m
-isometric if

\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)A^{*j}A^{j}=0.

Combining these two classes, we say
A\in B({\cal H})
is an
(m,n)
-isosymmetry (equivalently, the pair
(A^{*},A)
is
(m,n)
-isosymmetric) for some integers

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)A^{*j}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}n\\
k\end{array}\right)A^{*(n-k)}A^{k}\right)A^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}n\\
k\end{array}\right)A^{*(n-k)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)A^{*j}A^{j}\right)A^{k}=0.

It is clear that
m
-symmetric operators arise as solutions of
P(z)=({\overline{z}}-z)^{m}=0
,

A generalisation of the
m
-isometric property of operators
A\in B({\cal H})
to commuting
d
-tuples
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{d})\in B({\cal H})^{d}
,
[A_{i},A_{j}]=A_{i}A_{j}-A_{j}A_{i}=0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
, is obtained as follows
[
20
]
:
\mathbb{A}
is
m

\sum_{j=0}^{m}{(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)\sum_{|\beta|=j}{\frac{j!}{{\beta}!}}\mathbb{A}^{*\beta}\mathbb{A}^{\beta}}=0,

where

\displaystyle\beta=(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{d}),\ |\beta|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\beta_{i},\ {\beta}!=\Pi_{i=1}^{d}{\beta_{i}}!,

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{\beta}=\Pi_{i=1}^{d}{A_{i}^{\beta_{i}}},\ A^{*{\beta}}=\Pi_{i=1}^{d}{A_{i}^{*{\beta_{i}}}};

\mathbb{A}
is
n
-symmetric if

\sum_{j=0}^{m}{(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)(A_{1}^{*}+\cdots+A^{*}_{d})^{n-j}(A_{1}+\cdots+A_{d})^{j}}=0.

These generalisations, and certain of their variants (including
(m,n)
-isosymmetric operators), have recently been the subject matter of a number of studies, see

Definitions and introductory properties

For
A,B\in B({\cal X})
, let
L_{A}
and
R_{B}\in B(B({\cal X}))
denote respectively the operators

L_{A}(X)=AX\ {\rm and}\ R_{B}(X)=XB

of left multiplication by
A
and right multiplication by
B
. A
d
-tuple
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{d})\in B({\cal X})^{d}
is a commuting
d
-tuple if

[A_{i},A_{j}]=A_{i}A_{j}-A_{j}A_{i}=0,\ {\rm all}\ 1\leq i,j\leq d.

Given commuting
d
-tuples
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{d})
and
\mathbb{B}=(B_{1},\cdots,B_{d})
, define operators
\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}
and
\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}
by

\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}^{\alpha}=\Pi_{i=1}^{d}{L^{\alpha_{i}}_{A_{i}}},\ \mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}^{\alpha}=\Pi_{i=1}^{d}{R_{B_{i}}^{\alpha_{i}}}

where

\alpha=\left(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d}\right),\ |\alpha|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\alpha_{i}},\ \alpha_{i}\geq 0\ {\rm for\ all}\ 1\leq i\leq d.

For
d
-tuples
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
, and an operator
X\in B({\cal X})
, let “*” and “
\times
” denote, respectively, the multiplication operations

\displaystyle(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{j}(X)=\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=j}{{\frac{j!}{{\alpha}!}}\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}^{\alpha}\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}^{\alpha}}\right)(X)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}{L_{A_{i}}R_{B_{i}}}\right)^{j}(X)

\displaystyle(\rm{all\ integers}\ j\geq 0,\ {\alpha}!={\alpha_{1}}!\cdots{\alpha_{d}}!)\ {\rm and}

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}\right)(X)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}L_{A_{i}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}R_{B_{i}}\right)(X).

We say that the
d
-tuples
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
commute,
[\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}]=0
, if

[A_{i},B_{j}]=0\ {\rm for\ all}\ 1\leq i,j\leq d.

Evidently,

[\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}]=0

and if
[\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}]=0
, then

[\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{B}}]=[\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{A}},\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}]=0.

A pair
(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
of commuting
d
-tuples
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
is said to be
(X,m)
-isometric,
(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\in(X,m)
-isometric, for some positive integer
m
and operator
X\in B({\cal X})
, if

\displaystyle\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I-\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{m}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}n\\
j\end{array}\right)\left(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}\right)^{j}\right)(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}n\\
j\end{array}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}L_{A_{i}}R_{B_{i}}\right)^{j}\right)(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}n\\
j\end{array}\right)\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=j}{\frac{j!}{{\alpha}!}}\mathbb{A}^{\alpha}X\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0;

(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
is
(X,n)
-symmetric, for some positive integer
n
and operator
X\in B({\cal X})
, if

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}^{n}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}-\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{n}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}n\\
j\end{array}\right)\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}^{n-j}\times\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}^{j}\right)(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}n\\
j\end{array}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}L_{A_{i}}\right)^{n-j}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}R_{B_{i}}\right)^{j}\right)(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}n\\
j\end{array}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}A_{i}\right)^{n-j}X\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}B_{i}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Commuting tuples of
(X,m)
-isometric, similarly
(X,n)
-symmetric operators, share a large number of properties with their single operator counterparts. However, there are instances where a property holds for the single operator version but fails for the
d
-tuple version. For example, whereas

\ \triangle^{m}_{A,B}(X)=0\Longleftrightarrow\triangle^{m}_{A^{t},B^{t}}(X)\ {\rm for\ all\ integers}\ t\geq 1

and

\triangle^{m}_{A,B}(X)=0\Longleftrightarrow\triangle^{m}_{A^{-1},B^{-1}}(X)=0\ {\rm for\ all\ invertible}\ A\ {\rm and}\ B,

these properties fail for
d
-tuples, as the following example shows.

Example 2.1

If we define operators
A_{i},B_{i}
(
i=1,2
) by
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{B}=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}I,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}I\right)
, then
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},A_{2})
and
\mathbb{B}=(B_{1},B_{2})
are commuting, invertible
2
-tuples such that
(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
is
1
-isometric, i.e.
(I,1)
-isometric, but neither of
(\mathbb{A}^{2},\mathbb{B}^{2})
,
\mathbb{A}^{2}=\mathbb{B}^{2}=(A^{2}_{1},A_{1}A_{2},A_{2}A_{1},A_{2}^{2})
, and
(\mathbb{A}^{-1},\mathbb{B}^{-1})
,
\mathbb{A}^{-1}=\mathbb{B}^{-1}=(\sqrt{2}I,\sqrt{2}I)
, is
m
-isometric for any
m
.

In the following we show that where a property is shared by the single operator and the
d
-tuple versions, a proof of the
d
-tuple version of the result is obtained from the argument of the single operator version of the result (if not by a transliteration of the argument, then by a simple additional argument). We remark here that a number of authors have considered
(X,m)
-isometric and
(X,n)
-symmetric Hilbert space tuples with the operator
X
replaced by a positive operator
P
. The consideration of a general operator
X
, rather than
P\geq 0
, does not involve extra argument and does not, in general, result in loss of information. Any additional information that may result from a consideration with
P\geq 0
is usually a result of additional hypotheses on
P
, such as injectivity, which lead to additional structure on the underlying Hilbert space. We start in the following with a couple of basic observations.
The definitions imply

\triangle^{t}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)=\triangle^{t-m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X\right),\ \delta^{t}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)=\delta^{t-n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right),

\displaystyle\triangle^{t_{1}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{t_{2}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\triangle^{t_{1}-m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left[\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{t_{2}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\triangle^{t_{1}-m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left[\delta^{t_{2}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\triangle^{t_{1}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left[\delta^{t_{2}-n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)\right]

and

\displaystyle\triangle^{t_{1}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{t_{2}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\triangle^{t_{1}-m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left[\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{t_{2}-n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X))\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\triangle^{t_{1}-m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left[\delta^{t_{2}-n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X))\right)\right]

for all integers
t_{1}\geq m
and
t_{2}\geq n
. Thus:

Proposition 2.2

Given commuting
d
-tuples
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}
in
B({\cal X})^{d}
and an operator
X\in B({\cal X})
,

(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\in(X,m)-{\rm isometric}\Longrightarrow(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\in(X,t_{1})-{\rm isometric\ for\ all\ integers}\ t_{1}\geq m;

(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\in(X,n)-{\rm symmetric}\Longrightarrow(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\in(X,t_{2})-{\rm symmetric\ for\ all\ integers}\ t_{2}\geq n;

\displaystyle\{(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle(X,m)-{\rm isometric}\}\vee\{(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\in(X,n)-{\rm symmetric}\}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\in(X,(t_{1},t_{2}))-{\rm isosymmetric}{\rm\ for\ all\ integers\ }t_{1}\geq m,t_{2}\geq n.

If
(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\in(X,m)
-isometric, then

\displaystyle\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(I-\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*R_{\mathbb{B}})\left(\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})\left(\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{2}\left(\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=(\mathbb{L}_{A}*R_{\mathbb{B}})\left(\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{t}\left(\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X).

for all integers
t\geq 0
. Since
\mathbb{L}_{A}*R_{\mathbb{B}}
commutes with
\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}
, we also have

\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left((\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{t}(X)\right)=\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)

for all integers
t\geq 0
.

Again, if
(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\in(X,n)
-symmetric, then

\displaystyle\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}-\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})\left(\delta^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}\left(\delta^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{n-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}^{t}\left(\delta^{n-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=\mathbb{R}^{t}_{\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{n-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)

for all integers
t\geq 0
. Here

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}\left(\delta^{n-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}n-1\\
j\end{array}\right)\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}^{n-1-j}\times\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}^{j}\right)(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}n-1\\
j\end{array}\right)\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}^{n-j}\times\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}^{j}\right)(X)

and

\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{n-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}n-1\\
j\end{array}\right)\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}^{n-1-j}\times\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}^{j+1}\right)(X).

Proposition 2.3

Given commuting
d
-tuples
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}\in B({\cal X})^{d}
and an operator
X\in B({\cal X})
, if
(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\in(X,m)
-isometric, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}{\frac{1}{\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t\\
m-1\end{array}\right)}}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{t}(X)=\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X).

In particular, if
(\mathbb{L}_{A}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})
is invertible, then
\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)=0
.

The identity

(a-1)^{m}=a^{m}-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)(a-1)^{j}

applied to
\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)=(I-\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{m}(X)=0
implies

(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{m}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)\triangle^{j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X).

Observing

\displaystyle(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{m+1}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)\triangle^{j+1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)\triangle^{j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
m-1\end{array}\right)\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m+1\\
j\end{array}\right)\triangle^{j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m+1\\
j\end{array}\right)\triangle^{j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X),

an induction argument shows that

\displaystyle(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{t}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t\\
j\end{array}\right)\triangle^{j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t\\
m-1\end{array}\right)\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)+\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t\\
j\end{array}\right)\triangle^{j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)

for all integers
t\geq m
. Since
\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t\\

is of the order of

\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t\\

\displaystyle\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}{\frac{1}{\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t\\
m-1\end{array}\right)}}\left[(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{t}(X)-\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t\\
j\end{array}\right)\triangle^{j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}{\frac{1}{\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t\\
m-1\end{array}\right)}}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{t}(X).

As seen above
(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{t}\left(\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)
for all integers
t\geq 0
. Hence, if
\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}
is invertible, then
\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)=(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{-t}\left(\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)
for all integers
t\geq 0
. Consequently, if
\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}
is invertible, then

X=\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t\\
m-1\end{array}\right)\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)+\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t\\
j\end{array}\right)(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{-t}\left(\triangle^{j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right).

Since

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}{\frac{1}{\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t\\
m-1\end{array}\right)}}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t\\
j\end{array}\right)(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})^{-t}\left(\triangle^{j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)-X\right]=0,

we have
\triangle^{m-1}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)=0
.

The case
\mathbb{B}=(B_{1},\cdots,B_{d})\in B({\cal H})^{d}

Proposition 2.4

If

(I,2)

\displaystyle 0.

Proposition 2.6

\mathbb{A}_{n}=(A_{1n},\cdots,A_{dn})

\mathbb{B}=(B_{1n},\cdots,B_{dn})

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{d}(A_{in}-A_{i})\right\|=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{d}(B_{in}-B_{i})\right\|=0

for all

. Since

\displaystyle\left\|\triangle^{m_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}_{n},\mathbb{B}_{n}}(X)-\triangle^{m_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}.\mathbb{B}}(X)\right\|

\displaystyle\left\|\triangle^{m_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}_{n},\mathbb{B}_{n}}(X)-\triangle^{m_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}_{n}.\mathbb{B}}(X)\right\|+\left\|\triangle^{m_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}_{n},\mathbb{B}}(X)-\triangle^{m_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}.\mathbb{B}}(X)\right\|

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m_{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m_{1}\\
j\end{array}\right)\left(\left\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}A_{in}\right)^{m_{2}-j}X\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}B_{in}\right)^{j}-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}B_{i}\right)^{j}\right]\right\|+\right.

d
-tuples
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{d})
and
{\mathbb{N}}=(N_{1},\cdots,N_{d})
, we define

\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}=(A_{1}+N_{1},\cdots,A_{d}+N_{d}).

Recall that
[\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{N}}]=0
if and only if
[A_{i},N_{j}]=0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
.

Theorem 3.1

Given commuting
d
-tuples
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{d})
and
\mathbb{B}=(B_{1},\cdots,B_{d})
in
B({\cal X})^{d}
such that
\triangle^{m_{1}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{m_{2}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=0
for some positive integers
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
, let

{\mathbb{N}}_{i}=(N_{i1},\cdots,N_{id}),\ i=1,2,

be two commuting
n_{i}
-nilpotent
d
-tuples such that

[\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{N}}_{1}]=[\mathbb{B},{\mathbb{N}}_{2}]=0.

Then

\triangle^{t_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}\left(\delta^{t_{2}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}(X)\right)=0;\ t_{i}=m_{i}+n_{1}+n_{2}-2,\ i=1,2.

The commutativity hypotheses on
\mathbb{A}
,
\mathbb{B}
,
{\mathbb{N}}_{1}
and
{\mathbb{N}}_{2}
, taken alongwith the commutativity of the left and the right multiplication operators, imply

\triangle^{s_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}\left(\delta^{s_{2}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}(X)\right)=\delta^{s_{2}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}\left(\triangle^{s_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}(X)\right)

for all positive integers
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
. We prove the theorem in two steps. In the first step we let
\delta^{m_{2}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)=Y
; then
\triangle^{m_{1}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(Y)=0
, and we prove that
\triangle^{t_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}(Y)=0
. In the second step, we let
\triangle^{t_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}(Y)=Z
. Then

\triangle^{t_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}(Y)=\delta^{m_{2}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(Z)=0
, and we prove that
\delta^{t_{2}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}(Z)=0
.

Considering
\triangle^{t_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}(Y)
, we have

\displaystyle\triangle^{t_{1}}_{{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1}},{\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I-\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}})^{t_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[(I-\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})-\left((\mathbb{L}_{{\mathbb{N}}_{1}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}})+(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{{\mathbb{N}}_{2}})\right)\right]^{t_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{t_{1}}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t_{1}\\
j\end{array}\right)\triangle^{t_{1}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}j\\
k\end{array}\right)(\mathbb{L}_{{\mathbb{N}}_{1}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}})^{j-k}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{{\mathbb{N}}_{2}})^{k}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{t_{1}}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t_{1}\\
j\end{array}\right)\left[\sum_{k=0}^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}j\\
k\end{array}\right)(\mathbb{L}_{{\mathbb{N}}_{1}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}})^{j-k}(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{{\mathbb{N}}_{2}})^{k}\right]\triangle^{t_{1}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}.

The operator
{\mathbb{N}}_{i}
being
n_{i}
-nilpotent,
(\mathbb{L}_{{\mathbb{N}}_{1}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}})^{k}=0
for all
k\geq n_{1}
and
(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{{\mathbb{N}}_{2}})^{j-k}=0
for all
j-k\geq n_{2}
, or,
k\leq j-n_{2}
. Hence

\triangle^{t_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}(Y)=0

for all
n_{1}\leq k\leq j-n_{2}
. This leaves us with the case
n_{1}-1\geq k\geq j-n_{2}+1
. But then
j\leq n_{1}+n_{2}-2
implies
t_{1}-j\geq m_{1}+n_{1}+n_{2}-2-(n_{1}+n_{2}-2)=m_{1}
, and this, since
\triangle^{m_{1}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)=0
, forces
\triangle^{t_{1}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(Y)=0
. Conclusion:
\triangle^{t_{1}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}(Y)=0
.

Consider now
\delta^{t_{2}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}(Z)
. Since

\displaystyle\delta^{t_{2}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}=\left(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1}}-\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}\right)^{t_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}-\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})+(\mathbb{L}_{{\mathbb{N}}_{1}}-\mathbb{R}_{{\mathbb{N}}_{2}})\right)^{t_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{t_{1}}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t_{2}\\
j\end{array}\right)\left[\sum_{k=0}^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}j\\
k\end{array}\right)(-1)^{k}\mathbb{L}_{{\mathbb{N}}_{1}}^{j-k}\mathbb{R}_{{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}^{k}\right]\delta^{t_{2}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}},

and since
\mathbb{L}_{{\mathbb{N}}_{1}}^{j-k}\mathbb{R}^{k}_{{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}=0
for all
j-k\geq n_{1}
and
k\geq n_{2}
,
\delta^{t_{2}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}(Z)=0
for all
n_{2}\leq k\leq j-n_{1}
. If
j-n_{1}+1\leq k\leq n_{2}-1
(implies
j\leq n_{1}+n_{2}-2
), then
t_{2}-j=m_{2}+n_{1}+n_{2}-2-j\geq m_{2}
and
\delta^{t_{2}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(Z)=0
. Hence
\delta^{t_{2}}_{\mathbb{A}+{\mathbb{N}}_{1},\mathbb{B}+{\mathbb{N}}_{2}}(Z)=0
, and the proof is complete.

4
.
Isosymmetric products

If
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{d_{1}})
and
{\mathbb{S}}=(S_{1},\cdots,S_{d_{2}})
are two commuting
d_{i}
tuples in
B({\cal X})^{d_{i}}
,
i=1,2
, then the product
{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A}
is the operator

{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A}=(S_{1}A_{1},\cdots,S_{1}A_{d_{1}},S_{2}A_{2},\cdots,S_{2}A_{d_{1}},\cdots,S_{d_{2}}A_{1},\cdots,S_{d_{2}}A_{d_{1}}).

The tuples
\mathbb{A}
and
{\mathbb{S}}
commute,
[\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{S}}]=0
, if

[A_{i},S_{j}]=0\ {\rm for\ all}\ 1\leq i\leq d_{1}\ {\rm and}\ 1\leq j\leq d_{2}.

If
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}\in B({\cal X})^{d_{1}}
are commuting
d_{1}
-tuples and
{\mathbb{S,T}}\in B({\cal X})^{d_{2}}
are commuting
d_{2}
-tuples such that

[\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{S}}]=[\mathbb{B},{\mathbb{T}}]=0,

then (
\mathbb{L}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A}}=\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}{\mathbb{S}}}
,
\mathbb{R}_{{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}=\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}{\mathbb{T}}}
and)

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d_{2}}S_{j}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}A_{i}XB_{i}\right]T_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d_{2}}S_{j}\left((\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)T_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{L}_{{\mathbb{S}}}*\mathbb{R}_{{\mathbb{T}}}\right)\left((\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})(X)\right),

\displaystyle\triangle_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I-\mathbb{L}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}})(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I-\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}})(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}})(I-\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})+(I-\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}})\right)(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}})\triangle_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}+\triangle_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}\right)(X)

and

\left((\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}})\triangle_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\right)^{n}(X)=(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}})^{n}\left(\triangle^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right).

Again, if
\mathbb{A}
,
\mathbb{B}
,
{\mathbb{S}}
,
{\mathbb{T}}
are the tuples above, then

\displaystyle\delta_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbb{L}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A}}-R_{{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}})(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{j=1}^{d_{2}}L_{S_{j}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}L_{A_{i}}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{d_{2}}R_{T_{j}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}R_{B_{i}}\right)\right](X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}\times\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}-\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}\right](X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}\times(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{A}}-\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}})+\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}\times(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}-\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}})\right](X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}\times\delta_{\mathbb{A}.,\mathbb{B}}+\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}\times\delta_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}\right](X),

and

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}\times\delta_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\right)^{n}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{j=1}^{d_{2}}L_{S_{j}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}L_{A_{i}}-R_{B_{i}}\right)\right]^{n}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d_{2}}L_{S_{j}}\right)^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}(L_{A_{i}}-R_{B_{i}})\right)^{n}\right](X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\times\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}})(X)=\mathbb{L}^{n}_{\mathbb{S}}\times\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)

and similarly

(\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}\times\delta_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}})^{n}(X)=\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times\delta^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(X).

It is well known, see for example
[
9
,
18
,
16
,
21
,
29
]
, that if
[A_{1},A_{2}]=[B_{1},B_{2}]=0
and
\triangle^{m_{i}}_{A_{i},B_{i}}(X)=0
(similarly,
\delta^{m_{i}}_{A_{i},B_{i}}(X)=0
) for
i=1,2
, then
\triangle^{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}_{A_{1}A_{2},B_{1},B_{2}}(X)=0
(resp.,
\delta^{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}_{A_{1}A_{2},B_{1}B_{2}}(X)=0
). Using an argument similar in spirit to the one used to prove Theorem
3.1
(see also
[
16
]
), we prove in the following an analogous result for products
({\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}{\mathbb{B}})
of commuting
d
-tuples
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B},{\mathbb{S}}
and
{\mathbb{T}}
. We remark that the order
d_{i}
,
i=1,2
of the
d
-tuples plays no role in the workings of our argument: there is no loss of generality in assuming
d_{1}=d_{2}=d
.

Theorem 4.1

Let
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B},{\mathbb{S}}
and
{\mathbb{T}}
be commuting
d
-tuples in
B({\cal X})^{d}
such that

[\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{S}}]=[\mathbb{B},{mathbbS}]=[\mathbb{B},{\mathbb{T}}]=0.

If

\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X))=0=\triangle^{r}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(\delta^{s}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(X))

and

\triangle^{r}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(\delta^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(X))=0=\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(\delta^{s}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X))

for some positive integers
m,n,r
and
s
, then

\triangle^{t_{1}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(\delta^{t_{2}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(X))=0,

where
t_{1}=m+r-1
and
t_{2}=n+s-1
.

The commutativity hypothesis
[\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{S}}]=[\mathbb{B},{\mathbb{T}}]=0
, taken alongwith the commutativity of the left and the right multiplication operators implies

\displaystyle\triangle^{n_{1}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{n_{2}}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(X)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\triangle_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}^{n_{1}-m}\left[\triangle_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}^{m}\left(\delta^{n_{2}-n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\delta^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(X)\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\triangle_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}^{n_{1}-m}\left[\delta^{n_{2}-n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\triangle_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}^{m}\left(\delta^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(X)\right)\right)\right]

for all integers
n_{1}\geq m
and
n_{2}\geq n
. Hence

\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(X)\right)=0\Longrightarrow\triangle^{n_{1}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{n_{2}}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(X)\right)=0

for all integers
n_{1}\geq m
and
n_{2}\geq n
. Similarly,

\triangle^{r}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=0\Longrightarrow\triangle^{n_{1}}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\delta^{n_{2}}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=0

for all integers
n_{1}\geq r
and
n_{2}\geq n
.

The proof below is divided into two parts. In the first part we prove

\triangle^{t_{1}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=0,\ t_{1}=m+r-1,

and in the second part we prove

\delta^{t_{2}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}\left(\triangle^{t_{1}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=0,\ t_{2}=n+s-1.

Set
\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)=Y
. Then
\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(Y)=0
and

\displaystyle\triangle^{t_{1}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\triangle_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}+(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}})\triangle_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\right)^{t_{1}}(Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{t_{1}}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t_{1}\\
j\end{array}\right)(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}})^{t_{1}-j}\triangle^{t_{1}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\triangle^{j}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}\right)(Y).

The commutativity hypotheses ensure

\left[(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}})^{t_{1}-j}\triangle^{t_{1}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\ ,\triangle^{j}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}\right]=0;

hence

\displaystyle(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}})^{t_{1}-j}\triangle^{t_{1}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\triangle^{j}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}})^{t_{1}-j}\left(\triangle^{t_{1}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\triangle^{j}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(Y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}})^{t_{1}-j}\left(\triangle^{j}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}\triangle^{t_{1}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(Y)\right).

Since

\triangle_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}^{j}(Y)=\triangle_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}^{j}\left(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=0

for all
j\geq r
and

\triangle^{t_{1}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(Y)=\triangle^{t_{1}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=0

for all
t_{1}-j\geq m
, equivalently
j\leq t_{1}-m=r-1
, we have

\triangle_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}^{t_{1}}(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X))=0.

Now set
\delta^{s}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(X)=M
. Then
\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(M)=0
. Arguing as above, it is seen that

\triangle_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}^{t_{1}}(M)=\left[\sum_{j=0}^{t_{1}}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t_{1}\\
j\end{array}\right)(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}*\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}})^{t_{1}-j}\triangle^{t_{1}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\triangle^{j}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}\right](M).

Since
\triangle^{j}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(M)=0
for all
j\geq r
and
\triangle^{t_{1}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(M)=0
for all
t_{1}-j\geq m
, equivalently
j\leq r-1
, we have

\triangle_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}^{t_{1}}\left(\delta^{s}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(X)\right)=0.

To conclude the proof, set
\triangle_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}^{t_{1}}(X)=Z
. Then
\triangle_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}^{t_{1}}\left(\delta^{t_{2}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)=\delta^{t_{2}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(Z)
and

\displaystyle\delta^{t_{2}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbb{L}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A}}-\mathbb{R}_{{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}})^{t_{2}}(Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}\times\delta_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}+\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{T}}\times\delta_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}\right)^{t_{2}}(Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{j=0}^{t_{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}t_{2}\\
j\end{array}\right)\left(\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}^{t_{2}-j}\times\delta^{t_{2}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\right)\left(\mathbb{R}^{j}_{\mathbb{T}}\times\delta^{j}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}\right)\right](Z).

Evidently,

\left[\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{S}}\times\delta_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}},\ \mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{S}}\times\delta_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}\right]=0.

Since

R_{\mathbb{S}}\times\delta^{j}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(Z)=R_{\mathbb{S}}\times\left[\delta^{j}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\triangle^{t_{1}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)\right]=0

for all
j\geq s
and

\mathbb{L}^{t_{2}-j}_{\mathbb{S}}\times\delta^{t_{2}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(Z)=\mathbb{L}^{t_{2}-j}_{\mathbb{S}}\times\left(\delta^{t_{2}-j}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\triangle^{t_{1}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)\right)=0

for all
t_{2}-j\geq n
, equivalently for all
j\leq t_{2}-n=s-1
, we have

\displaystyle\delta^{t_{2}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{t_{2}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}\left(\triangle^{t_{1}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\triangle^{t_{1}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{t_{2}}_{{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{B}}(X)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Theorem
4.1
is a generalisation of a number of currently available results on products of operators. The theorem implies, in particular the following corollaries.

Corollary 4.2

If
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B},{\mathbb{S}}
and
{\mathbb{T}}
are commuting
d
-tuples in
B({\cal X})^{d}
such that
[\mathbb{A},{\mathbb{S}}]=[\mathbb{B},{mathbbS}]=[\mathbb{B},{\mathbb{T}}]=0
, then

(i)
\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)=\triangle^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(X)=0\Longrightarrow\triangle^{m+n-1}_{\mathbb{A}{\mathbb{S}},\mathbb{B}{\mathbb{T}}}(X)=0
;

(ii)
\delta^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(X)=\delta^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(X)=0\Longrightarrow\delta^{m+n-1}_{\mathbb{A}{\mathbb{S}},\mathbb{B}{\mathbb{T}}}(X)=0
.

Let
C
be a conjugation of
{\cal H}
. (Thus
C:{\cal H}\rightarrow{\cal H}
is a conjugate linear operator such that
C^{2}=I
and
\langle Cx,y\rangle=\langle Cy,x\rangle
for all
x,y\in{\cal H}
.) The first part of the following corollary has been proved in
[
6
, Theorem 4]
.

Corollary 4.3

Let
{\mathbb{S}}
and
{\mathbb{T}}
be commuting
d
-tuples in
B({\cal X})^{d}
such that
[{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}]=[{\mathbb{S}}^{*},C{\mathbb{T}}C]=0
. Then

(i)
\triangle^{m}_{{\mathbb{S}}^{*},C{\mathbb{S}}C}(X)=\triangle^{n}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{*},C{\mathbb{T}}C}(X)=0\Longrightarrow\triangle^{m+n-1}_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{*}},C{{\mathbb{S}}{\mathbb{T}}}C}(X)=0
;

(ii)
\delta^{m}_{{\mathbb{S}}^{*},C{\mathbb{S}}C}(X)=\delta^{n}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{*},C{\mathbb{T}}C}(X)=0\Longrightarrow\delta^{m+n-1}_{{\mathbb{S}}^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{*},C{\mathbb{S}}{\mathbb{T}}C}(X)=0
.

If
\mathbb{A}=A\in B({\cal X})
and
\mathbb{B}=B\in B({\cal X})
are single operators, then the products
\mathbb{A}{\mathbb{S}}
and
\mathbb{B}{\mathbb{T}}
are the
d
-tuples

\mathbb{A}{\mathbb{S}}=(AS_{1},\cdots,AS_{d})\ {\rm and}\ \mathbb{B}{\mathbb{T}}=(BT_{1},\cdots,BT_{d}).

If also
[A,{\mathbb{S}}]=[B,{\mathbb{T}}]=0
, then:

Corollary 4.4

(i)
\triangle^{m}_{A,B}(X)=\triangle^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(X)=0\Longrightarrow\triangle^{m+n-1}_{A{\mathbb{S}},B{\mathbb{T}}}(X)=0
;

(ii)
\delta^{m}_{A,B}(X)=\delta^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(X)=0\Longrightarrow\delta^{m+n-1}_{A{\mathbb{S}},B{\mathbb{T}}}(X)=0
.

Part (i) of the corollary is a generalisation of
[
6
, Theorem 6]
and part (ii) of the corollary, in so far as the authors can ascertain, is new.

Tensor products
\triangle^{t}_{\mathbb{A}\otimes{\mathbb{S}},\mathbb{B}\otimes{\mathbb{T}}}(I)
and
\delta^{t}_{\mathbb{A}\otimes{\mathbb{S}},\mathbb{B}\otimes{\mathbb{T}}}(I)

Let
{\cal X}\overline{\otimes}{\cal X}
denote the completion, endowed with a reasonable cross norm, of the algebraic tensor product of
{\cal X}
with itself. Let
S\otimes T
denote the tensor product of
S\in B({\cal X})
and
T\in B({\cal X})
. The tensor product of the
d
-tuples
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{d})
and
\mathbb{B}=(B_{1},\cdots,B_{d})
is the
d^{2}
-tuple

\mathbb{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}=\left(A_{1}\otimes B_{1},\cdots,A_{1}\otimes B_{d},A_{2}\otimes B_{1},\cdots,A_{2}\otimes B_{d},\cdots,A_{d}\otimes B_{1},\cdots,A_{d}\otimes B_{d}\right).

Let
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
be commuting
d
-tuples such that
\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(I)=0
(i.e., the pair
(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
is
m
-isometric). Then

\displaystyle\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(I)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(I)\otimes I=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}A_{i}B_{i}\right)^{j}\otimes I

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}(A_{i}\otimes I)(B_{i}\otimes I)\right)^{j}=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A}\otimes I,\mathbb{B}\otimes I}({\mathbb{I}})=0,\ {\mathbb{I}}=I\otimes I.

Similarly,

\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(I)=0\Longleftrightarrow\triangle^{m}_{I\otimes\mathbb{A},I\otimes\mathbb{B}}({\mathbb{I}})=0.

Considering
m
-symmetric pairs
(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
, we have

\displaystyle\delta^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(I)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}A_{i}\right)^{m-j}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}B_{i}\right)^{j}\right]\otimes I=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}A_{i}\otimes I\right)^{m-j}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}B_{i}\otimes I\right)^{j}=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\delta^{m}_{\mathbb{A}\otimes I,\mathbb{B}\otimes I}({\mathbb{I}})=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\delta^{m}_{I\otimes\mathbb{A},I\otimes\mathbb{B}}({\mathbb{I}})=0.

The extension of Theorem
4.1
to tensor products is now almost automatic.

Theorem 4.5

Given tuples
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B},{\mathbb{S}}
and
{\mathbb{T}}
in
B({\cal X})^{d}
, if

(i)
\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(I)=\triangle^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(I)=0
(resp.,
\delta^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(I)=\delta^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(I)=0
), then
\triangle^{m+n-1}_{\mathbb{A}\otimes{\mathbb{S}},\mathbb{B}\otimes{\mathbb{T}}}({\mathbb{I}})=0
(resp.,
\delta^{m+n-1}_{\mathbb{A}\otimes{\mathbb{S}},\mathbb{B}\otimes{\mathbb{T}}}({\mathbb{I}})=0
);

(ii)
\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(I)\right)=\triangle^{r}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(I)=\delta^{s}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(I)=0
for some positive integers
m,n,r
and
s
, then
\triangle^{m+r-1}_{\mathbb{A}\otimes{\mathbb{S}},\mathbb{B}\otimes{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\delta^{n+s-1}_{\mathbb{A}\otimes{\mathbb{S}},\mathbb{B}\otimes{\mathbb{T}}}({\mathbb{I}})\right)=0
.

Define operators
{\bf A}
,
{\bf B}
,
{\bf S}
and
{\bf T}
by

{\bf A}=\mathbb{A}\otimes I,\ {\bf B}=\mathbb{B}\otimes I,\ {\bf S}=I\otimes{\mathbb{S}},\ {\rm and}\ {\bf T}=I\otimes{\mathbb{T}}.

Then
[{\bf A},{\bf S}]=[{\bf B},{\bf S}]=[{\bf B},{\bf T}]=0
,

\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(I)=\triangle^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(I)=0\Longleftrightarrow\triangle^{m}_{{\bf A},{\bf B}}({\mathbb{I}})=\triangle^{n}_{{\bf S},{\bf T}}({\mathbb{I}})=0

and

\delta^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(I)=\delta^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(I)=0\Longleftrightarrow\delta^{m}_{{\bf A},{\bf B}}({\mathbb{I}})=\delta^{n}_{{\bf S},{\bf T}}({\mathbb{I}})=0.

Applying Corollary
4.4
,

\triangle^{m+n-1}_{{\bf A}{\bf S},{\bf B}{\bf T}}({\mathbb{I}})=\triangle^{m+n-1}_{\mathbb{A}\otimes{\mathbb{S}},\mathbb{B}\otimes{\mathbb{T}}}({\mathbb{I}})=0

and

\delta^{m+n-1}_{{\bf A}{\bf S},{\bf B}{\bf T}}({\mathbb{I}})=\delta^{m+n-1}_{\mathbb{A}\otimes{\mathbb{S}},\mathbb{B}\otimes{\mathbb{T}}}({\mathbb{I}})=0.

This proves (i).

To prove (ii), we start by observing that

\displaystyle\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(I)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}n\\
k\end{array}\right)\times

\displaystyle\times\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}L_{A_{i}}R_{B_{i}}\right)^{j}\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}L_{A_{i}}\right)^{n-k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}R_{B_{i}}\right)^{k}\right)\right](I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}m\\
j\end{array}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}(-1)^{k}\left(\begin{array}[]{clcr}n\\
k\end{array}\right)P(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})\right)(I),

where
P(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
is a polynomial with entries which are constant multiples of terms of type
A_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots A_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}B_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots B_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
for some non-negative integers
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq d
). Hence

\displaystyle\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(I)\right)=0

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}\left(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}}(I)\right)\otimes I=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\triangle^{m}_{\mathbb{A}\otimes I,\mathbb{B}\otimes I}\left(\delta^{n}_{\mathbb{A}\otimes I,\mathbb{B}\otimes I}({\mathbb{I}})\right)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\triangle^{m}_{{\bf A},{\bf B}}\left(\delta^{n}_{{\bf A},{\bf B}}({\mathbb{I}})\right)=0.

Again

\displaystyle\triangle^{r}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(I)=0\Longleftrightarrow\triangle^{r}_{{\bf S},{\bf T}}({\mathbb{I}})=0\Longrightarrow\delta^{n}_{{\bf A},{\bf B}}\left(\triangle^{r}_{{\bf S},{\bf T}}({\mathbb{I}})\right)=0,

\displaystyle\delta^{s}_{{\mathbb{S}},{\mathbb{T}}}(I)=0\Longleftrightarrow\delta^{s}_{{\bf S},{\bf T}}({\mathbb{I}})=0\Longrightarrow\triangle^{m}_{{\bf A},{\bf B}}\left(\delta^{s}_{{\bf S},{\bf T}}({\mathbb{I}})\right)=0

and

\triangle^{r}_{{\bf S},{\bf T}}\left(\delta^{s}_{{\bf S},{\bf T}}({\mathbb{I}})\right)=0.

Since
{\bf A}
,
{\bf B}
,
{\bf S}
and
{\bf T}
satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
4.1
, the proof of (ii) follows.

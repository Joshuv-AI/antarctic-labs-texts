Abstract
. In this paper we prove the monotonicity of positive solutions to − Δ p ​ u = f ⁡ ( u ) -\Delta_{p}u=f(u) in half-spaces under zero Dirichlet boundary conditions, for ( 2 ​ N + 2 ) / ( N + 2 ) < p < 2 (2N+2)/(N+2)<p<2 and for a general class of regular changing-sign nonlinearities f f . The techniques used in the proof of the main result are based on a fine use of comparison and maximum principles and on an adaptation of the celebrated moving plane method to quasilinear elliptic equations in unbounded domains.



1.
Introduction and statement of the main result

In this paper we deal with the study of the monotonicity of weak positive solutions of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
to the following quasilinear elliptic problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u),\quad&\text{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u(x^{\prime},y)>0,\quad&\text{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
u(x^{\prime},0)=0,\quad&\text{on}\,\,\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\end{cases}

where
p>1
,
N>1
and
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}:=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\ |\ x_{N}>0\}
and
\Delta_{p}u:=\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
is the usual
p
-Laplace operator.
In what follows, we shall denote a generic point of
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
by
(x^{\prime},y)
with
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
and
y=x_{N}\in(0,+\infty)
.
As well known, according to the regularity results in
[
9
,
26
]
, solutions of equations involving the
p
-Laplace operator are, in general, only continuously differentiable
and, therefore, the equation (
1.1
) has to be understood in the weak sense, see Definition
2.1
below.

The study of monotonicity properties of the solutions is an important task that sometimes appears in many applications such as blow-up analysis, a-priori estimates and also in the proof of Liouville type theorems. Qualitative properties of the solutions to (
1.1
) were firstly studied in the semilinear case
p=2
, in a series of papers due to H. Berestycki, L. Caffarelli and L. Nirenberg
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. More recent results for the case
p=2
can be found in the works
[
11
,
13
,
18
,
19
]
. We also point out
[
4
,
23
]
for results concerning the monotonicity of solutions in half-spaces for equations involving a fully nonlinear uniformly elliptic operator.
The situation in the quasilinear case is much more involved. Indeed, when working with the singular or degenerate
p
-Laplace operator, both the weak and the strong comparison principles might fail. The possible loss of these crucial tools being due either by the presence of critical points, or by the fact that the non-linearity
f
changes sign. Added to this is the fact that we are facing a problem on an unbounded domain. Also, when
p\neq 2
we cannot exploit the usual arguments and tricks related to the linear character of the Laplace operator.

The present work gives new results regarding the monotonicity of positive solutions in half-spaces. Here, we continue the study of qualitative properties in half-spaces started in previous papers. In particular, in
[
14
]
, the authors assumed that
(2N+2)/(N+2)<p<2
with the nonlinearity
f
a positive locally Lipschitz continuous function. Later on, in
[
16
]
, the same results was extended for every
p\in(1,2)
and to equations involving a first order term. A partial answer in the degenerate case
p>2
was obtained in
[
15
]
, where power-like nonlinearities were considered under the restriction
2<p<3
. Some years later in
[
17
]
, the authors removed this restriction and the monotonicity result was extended to a class of positive nonlinearities that are superlinear at zero.
We also mention the works
[
8
,
10
]
, where some (partial) results on half-spaces are obtained under technical and restrictive assumptions on the nonlinear function
f
.

While all these papers assume that
f(u)\geq 0
,
in the present work we are able to consider general genuinely changing-sign nonlinearities.

In particular, we assume that
f
satisfies the following assumptions:

{(h_{f})}\qquad\begin{cases}f\in C^{1}([0,+\infty)),\quad f(0)\geq 0,&\\
\mathcal{N}_{f}:=\{t\in[0,+\infty)\ |\ f(t)=0\}\,\,{\text{is a discrete set.}}&\end{cases}

Since in our problem the right hand side
depends only on
u
, it is possible to introduce the following set

(1.2)

\mathcal{Z}_{f(u)}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\ |\ u(x)\in\mathcal{N}_{f}\}.

We define a strip
\Sigma_{\lambda}
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
as follows

\Sigma_{\lambda}:=\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times(0,\lambda),

where
\lambda>0
. Now, under these assumptions and using these notations, we are ready to state our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
N>1
,
{(2N+2)}/{(N+2)}<p<2
and let
u\in C^{1}_{loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) such that
\nabla u\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\lambda})
, for every strip
\Sigma_{\lambda}
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. If
f
fulfills
(
h_{f}
)
, then
u
is monotone increasing w.r.t. the
y
-direction and

(1.3)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\geq 0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

Moreover

(1.4)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}>0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\mathcal{Z}_{f(u)}.

To get our main result, we use a weak comparison principle for quasilinear equations in strips
[
16
]
to start the moving plane procedure orthogonally to the boundary
\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. Then, by a delicate analysis based on the use of the techniques developed in
[
6
,
7
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
and by the translation invariance of the considered problem, we obtain the monotonicity of the solution w.r.t. the
y
-direction.

2.
Preliminary results and proof of Theorem
1.1

The aim of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
. First of all we recall some results about comparison and maximum principles for quasilinear elliptic equations that will be used several times in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, in the same spirit of
[
12
]
. To this end we give the definition of weak solution to problem (
1.1
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open set of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 1
. We say that
u\in C^{1}(\Omega)
is a

weak subsolution
to

(2.5)

-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega

if

(2.6)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}(\nabla u,\nabla\varphi)\,dx\,\leq\int_{\Omega}f(u)\varphi\,dx\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega),\,\varphi\geq 0.

Similarly, we say that
u\in C^{1}(\Omega)
is a

weak supersolution
to (
2.5
) if

(2.7)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}(\nabla u,\nabla\varphi)\,dx\,\geq\int_{\Omega}f(u)\varphi\,dx\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega),\,\varphi\geq 0.

Finally, we say that
u\in C^{1}(\Omega)
is a
weak
solution
of equation (
2.5
), if
(
2.6
) and (
2.7
) hold.

The first theorem that we are going to recall is a strong comparison principle that holds for quasilinear elliptic equations in any bounded smooth domains
\Omega
under the hypothesis that the nonlinearity
f
has a fixed sign.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
u,v\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be two solutions to

(2.8)

-\Delta_{p}w=f(w)\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

where
\Omega
is a bounded smooth connected domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\frac{2N+2}{N+2}<p<+\infty
.
Assume that at least one of the following two conditions
(f_{u})
,
(f_{v})
holds:

(
f_{u}
):

either

(2.9)

f(u(x))>0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\overline{\Omega}

or

(2.10)

f(u(x))<0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\overline{\Omega};

(
f_{v}
):

either

(2.11)

f(v(x))>0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\overline{\Omega}

or

(2.12)

f(v(x))<0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\overline{\Omega}.

Suppose furthermore that

(2.13)

u\leq v\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega.

Then
u\equiv v
in
\Omega
unless

(2.14)

u<v\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega.

For details regarding the proof of this result we refer to
[
12
]
. We remark that the classical strong comparison principle holds in any connected component
\Omega\setminus\mathcal{Z}
, where
\mathcal{Z}:=\{x\in\Omega\ |\ |\nabla u|=|\nabla v|=0\}
; Theorem
2.2
, instead, is true without any a priori assumption on the set
\mathcal{Z}
.

Now, let us recall that the linearized operator at a fixed solution
w
of (
2.8
),
L_{w}(v,\varphi)
(with weight
\varrho=|\nabla u|^{p-2}
), is well defined for every smooth functions
v\in C^{1}(\hat{\Omega})
and
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\hat{\Omega})
, with
\hat{\Omega}:=\Omega\setminus\{x\in\Omega\ |\ |\nabla w|=0\}
, by

(2.15)

\begin{split}L_{w}(v,\varphi)\equiv&\int_{\hat{\Omega}}|\nabla w|^{p-2}(\nabla v,\nabla\varphi)\,dx+(p-2)\int_{\hat{\Omega}}|\nabla w|^{p-4}(\nabla w,\nabla v)(\nabla w,\nabla\varphi)\,dx\\
&-\int_{\hat{\Omega}}f^{\prime}(w)v\varphi\,dx.\end{split}

As in the case of Theorem
2.2
, it is possible to show a classical strong maximum principle for the linearized equation in any connected component of
\hat{\Omega}
. Although we defined the linearized operator (
2.15
), we need to define it at a fixed solution
w
of (
2.8
) in the whole domain
\Omega
. We point out that under the hypothesis (
2.18
)
and (
2.19
), thanks to
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
, the weight
\varrho=|\nabla u|^{p-2}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
if
p>(2N+2)/(N+2)
. Hence
L_{w}(v,\varphi)
, is well defined, for every
v
and
\varphi
in the weighted Sobolev space
H^{1,2}_{\varrho}(\Omega)
with

\varrho=|\nabla w|^{p-2}
by

\begin{split}L_{w}(v,\varphi)\equiv&\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{p-2}(\nabla v,\nabla\varphi)\,dx+(p-2)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{p-4}(\nabla w,\nabla v)(\nabla w,\nabla\varphi)\,dx\\
&-\int_{\Omega}f^{\prime}(w)v\varphi\,dx.\end{split}

We recall that, for
\varrho\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, the space
H^{1,2}_{\varrho}(\Omega)
is define as the completion of

(or
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
) with the norm

(2.16)

\|v\|_{H^{1,2}_{\varrho}}=\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\varrho)},

where

\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,\varrho)}:=\int_{\Omega}\varrho(x)|\nabla v(x)|^{2}\,dx.

Moreover, the space
H^{1,2}_{0,\varrho}(\Omega)
is consequently defined as the closure of
C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
(or
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
), w.r.t. the norm (
2.16
).
We point out that the linearized operator is also well defined if
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
.

Finally

is a weak solution of the linearized operator if

(2.17)

L_{w}(v,\varphi)=0\qquad{\forall\varphi\in H^{1,2}_{0,\rho}(\Omega)}.

For future use we recall that, from
[

,
24
,
25
]
, it follows that

so that if

(with compact support), the linearized operator

is still well defined. Under this assumption the following result holds true.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
u\in C^{1}(\Omega)
be a solution to problem (
2.8
), with
\frac{2N+2}{N+2}<p<+\infty
. Assume that either

(2.18)

f(u(x))>0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\overline{\Omega}

or

(2.19)

f(u(x))<0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\overline{\Omega}.

If

in

in

Finally, let us recall a weak comparison principle in narrow unbounded domains:

Theorem 2.4

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let
1<p<2
and
N>1
. Fix
\lambda_{0}>0

. Consider

\Sigma_{(\lambda,y_{0})}:=\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times\left(y_{0}-\frac{\lambda}{2},y_{0}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\right),\quad y_{0}\geq\frac{\lambda}{2},\quad y_{0}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\leq\lambda_{0}.

Let
u,v\in C^{1}_{loc}(\overline{\Sigma}_{(\lambda,y_{0})})
such that
\|u\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}\leq L_{0}
,
\|v\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla v\|_{\infty}\leq L_{0}
,

and

(2.20)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u\leq f(u)\quad&\text{in}\ \Sigma_{(\lambda,y_{0})}\\

\end{cases}

where the open set
\mathcal{S}_{(\tau,\varepsilon)}\subseteq\Sigma_{(\lambda,y_{0})}

\mathcal{S}_{(\tau,\varepsilon)}=\bigcup_{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}I_{x^{\prime}}^{\tau,\varepsilon},

and, for

\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(N,p,\lambda_{0},L_{0})>0

and

u\leq v\quad\text{in}\,\,\mathcal{S}_{(\tau,\varepsilon)}.

The proof of this result is contained in
[

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
a connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\mathcal{Z}_{f(u)}
and let us assume that
\partial_{y}u\geq 0
in
\mathcal{U}

\partial_{y}u>0\quad\text{in}\,\,\mathcal{U}.

Proof.

Using Theorem
2.3
we deduce that either
\partial_{y}u>0
in
\mathcal{U}
or
\partial_{y}u\equiv 0
in
\mathcal{U}
. By contradiction, let us assume that
\partial_{y}u\equiv 0
in
\mathcal{U}
. Take
P_{0}=(x^{\prime}_{0},y_{0})\in\mathcal{U}
and let us define

s(t)=P_{0}-ty_{0}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{N},\quad t\in[0,1]

and

(2.21)

t_{0}=\sup\Big\{t\in[0,1]\,:\,s(\vartheta)\in\mathcal{U},\;\forall\vartheta\in[0,t)\Big\}.

We note that the supremum in (

) is well defined, since by definition the connected component
\mathcal{U}
is an open set, and that
t_{0}\in(0,1]
.

In the case
t_{0}=1
, we deduce that
u(P_{0})=0
. Indeed,
u
is constant on

for

(recall that
\partial_{y}u\equiv 0
in
\mathcal{U}
),

on

. But this gives a contradiction with our assumption

in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
.

In the case

, we deduce that

. But

define the unbounded strip

The first step in the proof of the monotonicity is to get a property concerning the local

such that

satisfies the
linearized equation (
2.17
), i.e.

(2.26)

L_{u}(u_{y},\varphi)=0\quad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Sigma_{\lambda}).

Let us set
z_{0}=(z_{0}^{\prime},y_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
.

We have two possibilities:
u_{y}(z_{0})=0
or
u_{y}(z_{0})>0
.

Claim:
We show that the case
u_{y}(z_{0})=0
is not possible.

If
u_{y}(z_{0})=0
, then

(2.27)

u_{y}(x)=0\qquad\text{for every }x\in B_{\hat{\rho}}(z_{0}),

for some positive
{\hat{\rho}}
; to prove this we use the fact that
|\nabla u(z_{0})|\neq 0
,
u
belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{1}
and that it holds the classical strong maximum principle for the linearized operator (see e.g. Theorem 2.5 in
[
12
]
or
[
21
]
).

By construction there exists
0<\varepsilon_{1}<\hat{\rho}
such that every point
z\in\mathcal{S}_{1}:=\{(z^{\prime}_{0},t)\in\mathcal{U}_{0}:y_{0}<t<y_{0}+\varepsilon_{1}\}
has the following properties:

(1)

z\in\overline{\mathcal{U}_{0}}
, since the ball is sliding along the segment
\mathcal{S}_{1}
;

(2)

z\not\in\partial\mathcal{U}_{0}
, since
z_{0}
is the first contact point with
\partial\mathcal{U}_{0}
.

That is, we have

(2.28)

\mathcal{S}_{1}\subset\overline{\mathcal{U}_{0}}\setminus\partial\mathcal{U}_{0}=\mathcal{U}_{0}.

By construction we have that
\overline{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\subset B_{\hat{\rho}}(z_{0})
and by (
2.27
)

u_{y}(z)=0\quad\forall z\in\overline{\mathcal{S}_{1}}.

Hence, we deduce that
u(z)=u(z_{0})
for every
z\in\overline{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
; since
z_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{f(u)}
by our assumptions, we reach a contradiction with (
2.28
).

From what we have seen above, we have
|\nabla u(z_{0})|\neq 0
and
hence there exists a ball
B_{r}(z_{0})
where
|\nabla u(x)|\neq 0

for every
x\in B_{r}(z_{0})
. By the classical strong comparison principle (see e.g. Theorem 2.3 in
[
12
]
or Theorem 1.4 in
[
5
]
or
[
21
]
) it follows that
u\equiv u_{\lambda}
in

B_{r}(z_{0})
namely
u\equiv u_{\lambda}
in a neighborhood of the point
z_{0}\in\partial\mathcal{U}_{0}
. Since
u_{y}(z_{0})>0
and
\mathcal{N}_{f}
is discrete

B_{r}(z_{0})\cap\Big((\Sigma_{\lambda}\setminus\mathcal{Z}_{f(u)})\setminus\mathcal{U}_{0}\Big)\neq\emptyset

and
u_{y}(x)>0
in
B_{r}(z_{0})
, as consequence, the set
\{u=u(z_{0})\}
is a graph in the
y
-direction in a neighborhood of the point
z_{0}
.

Now we have to distinguish two cases:

Case 1:

u(z_{0})=\min\Big[\mathcal{N}_{f}\setminus\{0\}\Big].

Define the sets

\mathcal{C}_{1}:=\Big\{x=(x^{\prime},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\,:\,x^{\prime}\in\Pi_{0}\left(B_{r}(z_{0})\right)\quad\text{and}\quad u(x)<u(z_{0})\Big\}

\mathcal{C}_{2}:=B_{r}(z_{0})\cup\big(\Pi_{0}\left(B_{r}(z_{0})\right)\times(0,y_{0})\big),

where
\Pi_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
is the canonical projection on the hyperplane
\{y=0\}
and

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}.

We observe that
\mathcal{C}
is an open path-connected set (actually a deformed cylinder), see Figure
4
. Since
f(u(z_{0}))
has the right sign, by Theorem
2.2
it follows that
u\equiv u_{\lambda}
in
\mathcal{C}
and this is in contradiction with the fact that
u(x^{\prime},0)=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, while
u_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},0)>0
.

u(z_{0})>\min\Big[\mathcal{N}_{f}\setminus\{0\}\Big]
.

In this case the open ball
B_{r}(z_{0})
must intersect another connected component (i.e.
\not\equiv\mathcal{U}_{0}
) of
\Sigma_{\lambda}\setminus\mathcal{Z}_{f(u)}
, such that
u\equiv u_{\lambda}
in a such component, see Figure

z_{1}
, the corresponding level set is a graph in the

-direction and
u(z_{1})<u(z_{0})
, since
\mathcal{N}_{f}
is a discrete set. Now, it is clear that repeating a finite number of times the argument leading to the existence of the touching point
z_{0}
, we can find a touching point

u(z_{m})=\min\Big[\mathcal{N}_{f}\setminus\{0\}\Big].

u<u_{\lambda}
in

.

With the notation introduced above, we set

(2.29)

\Lambda:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\ |\ u\leq u_{t}\;\text{in}\ \Sigma_{t},\;\forall t<\lambda\}.

Note that, by Theorem
2.4
(with
v=u_{t})
, it follows
that
\Lambda\neq\emptyset
, hence we can define

(2.30)

\bar{\lambda}:=\sup\Lambda.

The proof of the fact that
u(x^{\prime},y)
is monotone increasing in the
y
-direction in the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
is done once we show that
\bar{\lambda}=+\infty
. To this end we assume by contradiction that
0<\bar{\lambda}<+\infty
, and we prove a crucial result,
which allows us to localize the support of
(u-u_{\bar{\lambda}})^{+}
.
This localization, that we are going to obtain, will be useful to
apply Theorem
2.4
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Under the same assumption of Theorem
1.1
, let
u
be a solution to (
1.1
). Assume
0<\bar{\lambda}<+\infty

(see (
2.30
))
and set

W_{\varepsilon}:=(u-u_{\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon})\cdot\chi_{\{y\leq\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon\}}

where
\varepsilon>0
.

Given
0<\delta<\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{2}
and
\kappa>0
, there exists
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
such that for every
0<\varepsilon<\bar{\varepsilon}

(2.31)

\text{\emph{supp}}\,W^{+}_{\varepsilon}\subset\{0\leq y\leq\delta\}\cup\{\bar{\lambda}-\delta\leq y\leq\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon\}\cup\left(\bigcup_{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}B_{x^{\prime}}^{\kappa}\right),

where
B_{x^{\prime}}^{\kappa}
is such that

B^{\kappa}_{x^{\prime}}\subseteq\{y\in(0,\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon)\ :\ |\nabla u(x^{\prime},y)|<\kappa,\;|\nabla u_{\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},y)|<\kappa\}.

Proof.

Since
\bar{\lambda}<+\infty
, the continuity of
u
and
u_{\lambda}
, imply

u\leq u_{\bar{\lambda}}\quad\text{in}\;\Sigma_{\bar{\lambda}}.

Assume by contradiction that (
2.31
) is false, so that there
exist
0<\delta<\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{2}
and
\kappa>0
in such a way that, given any
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
, we find

0<\varepsilon\leq\bar{\varepsilon}
so that there exists a
corresponding
x_{\varepsilon}=(x^{\prime}_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon})

such that

u(x^{\prime}_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon})\geq u_{\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon}(x^{\prime}_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon}),

with
x_{\varepsilon}=(x^{\prime}_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon})

belonging to the set

\{(x^{\prime}_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,\delta<y_{\varepsilon}<\bar{\lambda}-\delta\}

such that
|\nabla u(x_{\varepsilon})|\geq\kappa
(or possibly
|\nabla u_{\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})|\geq\kappa
).

Taking
\bar{\varepsilon}={1}/{n}
, then there exists

\varepsilon_{n}\leq 1/n
going to zero, and a
corresponding sequence

x_{n}=(x^{\prime}_{n},y_{n})=(x^{\prime}_{\varepsilon_{n}},y_{\varepsilon_{n}})

such that

u(x^{\prime}_{n},y_{n})\geq u_{\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon_{n}}(x^{\prime}_{n},y_{n})

with
\delta<y_{n}<\bar{\lambda}-\delta
. Up to subsequences, let us
assume that

y_{n}\rightarrow\bar{y}\;\text{with}\;\delta\leq\bar{y}\leq\bar{\lambda}-\delta.

Let us define

\tilde{u}_{n}(x^{\prime},y):=u(x^{\prime}+x^{\prime}_{n},y)

so that
\|\tilde{u}_{n}\|_{\infty}=\|u\|_{\infty}
. By standard
regularity theory, see
[
9
,
20
,
26
]
, we have that

\|\tilde{u}_{n}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})}\leq C,

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Therefore, by Ascoli’s Theorem we have

\tilde{u}_{n}\overset{C^{1}_{loc}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}})}{\longrightarrow}\tilde{u}

up to subsequences. By construction it follows that

(i)

\tilde{u}>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, with
\tilde{u}(x^{\prime},0)=0
for every
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
;

(ii)

\tilde{u}\leq\tilde{u}_{\bar{\lambda}}\quad\text{in}\ \Sigma_{\bar{\lambda}}
;

(iii)

\tilde{u}(0,\bar{y})=\tilde{u}_{\bar{\lambda}}(0,\bar{y})
;

(iv)

|\nabla\tilde{u}(0,\bar{y})|\geq\kappa
.

We point out that, in (i), by construction
\tilde{u}\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. As a consequence, by applying the strong maximum principle (see
[
21
,
22
,
27
]
) we get that
\tilde{u}>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
. Since
|\nabla\tilde{u}(0,\bar{y})|\geq\kappa
there exists
\rho>0
and a ball
B_{\rho}(0,\bar{y})\subset\Sigma_{\bar{\lambda}}
such that
|\nabla\tilde{u}(x)|\neq 0
for every
x\in B_{\rho}(0,\bar{y})
. Now, if
\tilde{u}(0,\bar{y})\in\mathcal{Z}_{f(u)}
, since
\tilde{u}
is non constant in
B_{\rho}(0,\bar{y})
, there exists
P_{0}\in B_{\rho}(0,\bar{y})
such that
\tilde{u}(P_{0})\not\in\mathcal{Z}_{f(u)}
.
By the classical strong comparison principle we get that

(2.32)

\tilde{u}\equiv\tilde{u}_{\bar{\lambda}}\quad\text{in}\,\,B_{\rho}(0,\bar{y}).

On the other hand, by Proposition
2.6
it follows that

\tilde{u}<\tilde{u}_{\bar{\lambda}}\qquad\text{in}\,\,\Sigma_{\bar{\lambda}}\setminus\mathcal{Z}_{f(u)}.

This gives a contradiction with (
2.32
). Hence we have (
2.31
).

∎

Now we are ready to prove the main result of the paper.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let us assume by contradiction that
0<\bar{\lambda}<+\infty
, see (
2.30
). Set
\lambda_{0}=\bar{\lambda}+2
and

L_{0}:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\{0\leq y\leq 2\bar{\lambda}+10\})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\{0\leq y\leq 2\bar{\lambda}+10\})}+1>0

and take
\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(N,p,\lambda_{0},L_{0})>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(N,p,\lambda_{0},L_{0})>0
as in Theorem
2.4
.

By Proposition
2.7
we have that, given
0<\delta<\min\{\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{2},\frac{\tau_{0}}{4}\}
and
0<\kappa<\varepsilon_{0}
, we find
\bar{\varepsilon}
such that, for any
0<\varepsilon\leq\min\{\bar{\varepsilon},\frac{\tau_{0}}{4},1\}
, it follows

\text{supp}\,W^{+}_{\varepsilon}\subset\{0\leq y\leq\delta\}\cup\{\bar{\lambda}-\delta\leq y\leq\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon\}\cup\left(\bigcup_{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}B_{x^{\prime}}^{\kappa}\right),

where
B_{x^{\prime}}^{\kappa}
is such that

B^{\kappa}_{x^{\prime}}\subseteq\{y\in(0,\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon)\ :\ |\nabla u(x^{\prime},y)|<\kappa,\;|\nabla u_{\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},y)|<\kappa\}.

We claim
u\leq u_{\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon}
in
\Sigma_{\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon}
. Indeed, let us assume that the open set

\mathcal{S}_{(2\delta+\varepsilon,\kappa)}:=\{x\in\Sigma_{\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon}\ :\ u(x)-u_{\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon}(x)>0\}

is not empty, then
u
and
v=u_{\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon}
satisfy (
2.20
) with
\lambda=y_{0}=\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon\ (<\lambda_{0})
. Since by construction
2\delta+\varepsilon<\tau_{0}
and
\kappa<\varepsilon_{0}
we can apply Theorem
2.4
to conclude that
u\leq u_{\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon}
on
\mathcal{S}_{(2\delta+\varepsilon,\kappa)}
. This contradicts the definition of
\mathcal{S}_{(2\delta+\varepsilon,\kappa)}
. Hence
\mathcal{S}_{(2\delta+\varepsilon,\kappa)}=\emptyset
.

The latter proves that
u\leq u_{\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon}
in
\Sigma_{\bar{\lambda}+\varepsilon}
, which contradicts the definition of
\bar{\lambda}
and consequently we deduce that
\bar{\lambda}=+\infty
. This implies the monotonicity of
u
, that is

\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\geq 0\quad\text{in}\,\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}.

Moreover, an application of Lemma
2.5
yields

\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}>0\quad\text{in}\,\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\setminus\mathcal{Z}_{f(u)}.

∎

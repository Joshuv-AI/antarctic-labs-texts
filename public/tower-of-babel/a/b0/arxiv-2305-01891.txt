Weight hierarchies of three-weight p p -ary linear codes from inhomogeneous quadratic functions
Shupeng Hu, Fei Li, Xiumei Li

Abstract
. The weight hierarchy of a linear code have been an important research topic in coding theory since Wei’s original work in 1991. In this paper, choosing D = { ( x , y ) ∈ ( 𝔽 p s 1 × 𝔽 p s 2 ) \ { ( 0 , 0 ) } : f ⁡ ( x ) + Tr 1 s 2 ​ ( α ​ y ) = 0 } D=\Big\{(x,y)\in\Big(\mathbb{F}_{p^{s_{1}}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{s_{2}}}\Big)\Big\backslash\{(0,0)\}:f(x)+\text{\rm Tr}_{1}^{s_{2}}(\alpha y)=0\Big\} as a defining set , where α ∈ 𝔽 p s 2 ∗ \alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{s_{2}}}^{*} and f ⁡ ( x ) f(x) is a quadratic form over 𝔽 p s 1 \mathbb{F}_{p^{s_{1}}} with values in 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} , whether f ⁡ ( x ) f(x) is non-degenerate or not, we construct a family of three-weight p p -ary linear codes and determine their weight distributions and weight hierarchies completely. 1.
Introduction

For an odd prime number
p
and a positive integer
s
, let
\mathbb{F}_{p^{s}}
be the finite field with
p^{s}

elements and
\mathbb{F}_{p^{s}}^{*}
be its multiplicative group.

An
[n,k,d]

p
-ary linear code
C
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
with minimum (Hamming) distance
d
.
For
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
, denote by
A_{i}
the number of codewords in
C
with Hamming weight
i
. The weight distribution of
C
is defined by the sequence
(1,A_{1},\cdots,A_{n})

and the weight enumerator of
C
is defined by the polynomial
1+A_{1}x+A_{2}x^{2}+\cdots+A_{n}x^{n}
.
If the sequence
(A_{1},\cdots,A_{n})
has
t
nonzero
A_{i}
with
i=1,2,\cdots n
, then the code
C
is called a
t
-weight code.
In coding theory, the weight distribution of linear codes is a classical research topic and attracts much attention. Furthermore,
linear codes with a few weights have important applications in authentication codes
[
10
]
,
association schemes
[
3
]
, secret sharing
[
34
]
and strongly regular graphs
[
4
]
.

For an
[n,k,d]
linear code
C
and
1\leq r\leq k
, the concept of generalized Hamming weights (GHW)
d_{r}(C)
can be viewed as an extension of Hamming weight.
We recall the definition of the generalized Hamming weights of linear codes.
Denote by

[C,r]_{p}
the set of all its
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector subspaces with dimension
r
.
For
H\in[C,r]_{p}
, the support
\textrm{Supp}(H)
of
H
is the set of coordinates where not all codewords of
H
are zero, that is,

\textrm{Supp}(H)=\Big\{i:1\leq i\leq n,c_{i}\neq 0\ \ \textrm{for some $c=(c_{1},c_{2},\cdots,c_{n})\in H$}\Big\}.

The
r
-th generalized Hamming weight of
C
, which is also called the
r
-th minimum support weight, is

d_{r}(C)=\min\Big\{|\textrm{Supp}(H)|:H\in[C,r]_{p}\Big\},\ 1\leq r\leq k.

The set
\{d_{1}(C),d_{2}(C),\cdots,d_{k}(C)\}
is called the weight hierarchy of
C
.

The generalized Hamming weights was introduced in 1977 by Helleseth et al.
[
13
,
19
]
and Victor Wei
[
30
]
proved that they can characterize the cryptography performance of a linear code over the wiretap channel of type II and determined the weight hierarchies for some well-known classes of codes, such as Hamming codes, Reed-Muller
codes, Reed-Solomon codes and Golay codes etc. From then on, much more attention was paid to generalized Hamming weights (see
[
5
,
12
,
16
,
30
,
31
]
). To determine the weight hierarchy of linear codes is relatively challenging, during the past decade, there were research results
about weight hierarchies of some classes of linear codes
[
2
,
17
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
27
,
32
,
33
]
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In Sec. 2, we introduce a generalized method of constructing linear code by defining sets and
give a corresponding formula for computing the generalized Hamming weights.
In Sec. 3, we construct
p
-ary linear codes with three weights and determine their weight distributions and weight hierarchies completely.
In Sec. 4, we summarize the paper.

2.1.
The defining-set construction of linear codes

Ding et al.
[
9
]
proposed a generic construction of linear codes as below.
Denote by
\text{\rm Tr}_{1}^{s}
the trace function from
\mathbb{F}_{p^{s}}
onto
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Let
D=\{d_{1},d_{2},\cdots,d_{n}\}
be a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{s}}^{\ast}.

A
p
-ary linear code of length
n
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle C_{D}=\{\left(\text{\rm Tr}_{1}^{s}(xd_{1}),\text{\rm Tr}_{1}^{s}(xd_{2}),\ldots,\text{\rm Tr}_{1}^{s}(xd_{n})\right):x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{s}}\},

and
D
is called the defining set of
C_{D}
. By choosing some proper defining sets, some optimal linear
codes with a few weights can be constructed (see
[
6
,
8
,
11
,
18
,
28
,
35
]
and the references therein).

Li et al. generalized Ding’s constructing method and also constructed some linear codes with a few weights
[
20
]
. The following constructing method can be regarded as a generalized form of Li’s.
For some positive integers
s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{e}
, define
s=\sum_{i=1}^{e}s_{i}
,
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p^{s_{1}}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{s_{2}}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{s_{e}}}
and
\mathbb{F}^{\star}=\mathbb{F}\Big\backslash\{(0,0,\cdots,0)\}
.
For
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{e}),Y=(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{e})\in\mathbb{F}
,
denote by
\text{\rm Tr}(X\odot Y)
as follows,

\text{\rm Tr}(X\odot Y)=\text{\rm Tr}_{1}^{s_{1}}(x_{1}y_{1})+\text{\rm Tr}_{1}^{s_{2}}(x_{2}y_{2})+\cdots+\text{\rm Tr}_{1}^{s_{e}}(x_{e}y_{e}).

Let
D=\{Y_{1},Y_{2},\cdots,Y_{n}\}

be contained in
\mathbb{F}^{\star}
.
Define
a
p
-ary linear code
C_{D}
as follows:

(2.1)

\displaystyle C_{D}=\Big\{\Big(\text{\rm Tr}(X\odot Y_{i}\Big)_{Y_{i}\in D}:X\in\mathbb{F}\Big\}.

Here,
D
is also called the defining set. Now we give a formula to calculate the
r
-th generalized Hamming weight of

C_{\textrm{D}}
in (
2.1
), which
is a generalization of
[
24
, Proposition 1]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For each
r
and
1\leq r\leq s
, if the dimension of
C_{D}

is
s
, then

(2.2)

d_{r}(C_{D})=n-\max\big\{|D\cap H|:H\in[\mathbb{F},s-r]_{p}\big\}.

Proof.

Let
\sigma
be a map from
\mathbb{F}
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
defined by

\sigma(X)=\Big(\text{\rm Tr}(X\odot Y_{1}),\text{\rm Tr}(X\odot Y_{2}),\cdots,\text{\rm Tr}(X\odot Y_{n})\Big).

Obviously,
\sigma
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear homomorphism and the image
\sigma(\mathbb{F})
is
C_{D}
. By the assumed condition, that is, the dimension of
C_{D}

is
s
, we know that
\sigma
is injective. Taking any
r
-dimensional subspace
U_{r}\in[C_{D},r]_{p}
, denote by
H_{r}=\sigma^{-1}(U_{r})

the inverse image of
U_{r}
, then
H_{r}
is an
r
-dimensional
subspace of
\mathbb{F}
. Let
\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{r}\}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-basis of
H_{r}
, we have

d_{r}(C_{\textrm{D}})=n-\max\{N(U_{r}):U_{r}\in[C_{\textrm{D}},r]_{p}\},

where

\displaystyle N(U_{r})

\displaystyle=\Big|\{i:1\leq i\leq n,c_{i}=0\ \ \textrm{for any\ }C=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n})\in U_{r}\}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\{i:1\leq i\leq n,\text{\rm Tr}(X\odot Y_{i})=0\ \ \textrm{for any\ }X\in H_{r}\}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\{i:1\leq i\leq n,\text{\rm Tr}(\beta_{j}\odot Y_{i})=0,j=1,2,\cdots,r\}\Big|.

Then, by the orthogonal property of additive characters, we have

\displaystyle N(U_{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{r}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{x_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\zeta_{p}^{x_{1}\text{\rm Tr}\big((\beta_{1}\odot Y_{i})\big)}\ldots\sum_{x_{r}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\zeta_{p}^{{x_{r}\text{\rm Tr}\big((\beta_{r}\odot Y_{i})\big)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{r}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\zeta_{p}^{\mathrm{Tr}\big((x_{1}\beta_{1}+\ldots+x_{r}\beta_{r})\odot Y_{i}\big)}

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{r}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\beta\in H_{r}}\zeta_{p}^{\mathrm{Tr}(\beta\odot Y_{i})}.

For
H_{r}
,
let
H_{r}^{\perp}=\{X\in\mathbb{F}:\mathrm{Tr}(X\odot Y)=0\ \textrm{for any}\ Y\in H_{r}\}
,
by Theorem 2.37
[
26
]
, one can prove that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(H_{r})+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(H_{r}^{\bot})=s
and
(H_{r}^{\bot})^{\bot}=H_{r}
. So,
for any
Y\in\mathbb{F}
,

(2.4)

\sum_{\beta\in H_{r}}\zeta_{p}^{\text{\rm Tr}(\beta\odot Y)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|H_{r}|,&\textrm{if\ }\ Y\in H_{r}^{\bot},\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise\ }.\end{array}\right.

Combining (
2.1
) with (
2.4
), we have

N(U_{r})=\frac{1}{p^{r}}\sum_{Y\in D\cap H_{r}^{\bot}}|H_{r}|=|D\cap H_{r}^{\bot}|.

Finally, we get the desired result, which follows from the fact that there is a bijection
between
[\mathbb{F},r]_{p}
and
[\mathbb{F},s-r]_{p}
.

∎

2.3.
Quadratic form

Viewing
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q=p^{s}
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear space and fixing
\upsilon_{1},\upsilon_{2},\cdots,\upsilon_{s}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
as its
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-basis, then
for any

there is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear isomorphism

is called the coordinate vector of

of
\mathbb{F}_{q}

Let

be a quadratic form over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with values in
\mathbb{F}_{p}

F(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(f(x+y)-f(x)-f(y)\Big),\textrm{for any}\ x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q},

then

can be represented by

(2.5)

\displaystyle f(x)=f(X)=f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq s}F(v_{i},v_{j})x_{i}x_{j}=XAX^{T},

where
A=(F(v_{i},v_{j}))_{s\times s},F(v_{i},v_{j})\in\mathbb{F}_{p},F(v_{i},v_{j})=F(v_{j},v_{i})
and
X^{T}
is the transposition of

. Denote by

is non-degenerate if

and degenerate, otherwise.
For any
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

can be uniquely expressed as

and

.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle F(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(f(x+y)-f(x)-f(y)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(f(X+Y)-f(X)-f(Y)\Big)

\displaystyle=XAY^{T}=YAX^{T}

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\text{\rm Tr}_{1}^{s}\Big(xL_{f}(y)=\text{\rm Tr}_{1}^{s}\Big(yL_{f}(x)\Big)\Big),

where
L_{f}
is a linearized polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

defined as

(2.7)

\displaystyle L_{f}(\upsilon_{1},\upsilon_{2},\cdots,\upsilon_{s})=(\upsilon_{1},\upsilon_{2},\cdots,\upsilon_{s})\Big(\text{\rm Tr}_{1}^{s}(v_{i}v_{j})\Big)^{-1}A.

, respectively.
If

with satisfying

From eq. (

4.
Concluding Remarks

In the work of
[
25
]
, using a special non-degenerate homogeneous quadratic function we constructed a family of three-weight linear codes and determined their weight distributions and weight hierarchies.
In this paper, our defining set is
D=\Big\{(x,y)\in\Big(\mathbb{F}_{p^{s_{1}}}\times\mathbb{F}_{p^{s_{2}}}\Big)\Big\backslash\{(0,0)\}:f(x)+\text{\rm Tr}_{1}^{s_{2}}(\alpha y)=0\Big\}
,
where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{s_{2}}}^{*}
and

is a quadratic forms over
\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}
with values in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, whether

is non-degenerate or not. We also constructed a family of three-weight linear codes with any dimension by extending known construction and determined their weight distributions and weight hierarchies.

Let

and

denote the minimum and maximum nonzero weight of our obtained code
C_{D}
defined in (

), respectively.
If

, then it can be easily checked that

By the results in
[
1
]
and
[
34
]
, we know that every nonzero codeword of
C_{D}
is minimal and most of the codes we constructed
are suitable for constructing secret sharing schemes with interesting properties.

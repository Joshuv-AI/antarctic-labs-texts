. 1.
Introduction

A smooth metric measure space is a triple
(M,g,d\mu)
, where
g
is a metric,
d\mu=e^{-\phi(x)}d\nu
is the weighted volume measure on
(M,g)
related to function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
d\nu
is the Riemannian volume measure. Such spaces have been used more widely in the work of mathematicians, for instance, Perelman used it in
[
11
]
. Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional closed Riemannian manifold with metric
g
.

Over the last few years the geometric flows as the Ricci-Bourguignon flow have been a topic of active
research interest in both mathematics and physics. A geometric flow is an evolution of a geometric structure under
a differential equation related to a functional on a
manifold, usually associated with some curvature. The family

{g(t)}
of Riemannian metrics on
M

is called a Ricci-Bourguignon flow when it satisfies the equations

(1.1)

\frac{d}{dt}g(t)=-2Ric(g(t))+2\rho R(g(t))g(t)=-2(Ric-\rho Rg),\\
\

with the initial condition

g(0)=g_{0}\\

where
Ric
is the Ricci tensor of
g(t)
,
R
is the scalar curvature and
\rho
is a real constant. When
\rho=0
,
\rho=\frac{1}{2}
,
\rho=\frac{1}{n}
and
\rho=\frac{1}{2(n-1)}
, the tensor
Ric-\rho Rg
corresponds to the Ricci tensor, Einstein tensor, the traceless Ricci tensor and Schouten tensor respectively. In fact the Ricci-Bourguignon flow is a system of partial differential equations which was introduced by Bourguignon for the first time in
1981 (see
[
3
]
).
Short time
existence and uniqueness for
solution to the Ricci-Bourguignon flow on
[0,T)
have been shown by Catino and et’al in
[
6
]
for
\rho<\frac{1}{2(n-1)}
.
When
\rho=0
,
the Ricci-Bourguignon flow is the Ricci flow.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
,
f\in W^{1,p}(M)
where
W^{1,p}(M)
is the Sobolev space.
For,
p\in[1,+\infty)
, the
p
-Laplacian of
f
defined as

(1.2)

\Delta_{p}f=div(|\nabla f|^{p-2}\nabla f)=|\nabla f|^{p-2}\Delta f+(p-2)|\nabla f|^{p-4}(Hessf)(\nabla f,\nabla f).

The Witten-Laplacian is defined by
\Delta_{\phi}=\Delta-\nabla\phi.\nabla
, which is a symmetric diffusion operator on
L^{2}(M,\mu)
and is self-adjoint.
Now, for
p\in[1,+\infty)
and any smooth function
f
on
M
, we define the weighted
p
-Laplacian on
M
by

(1.3)

\Delta_{p,\phi}f=e^{\phi}div\left(e^{-\phi}|\nabla f|^{p-2}\nabla f\right)=\Delta_{p}f-|\nabla f|^{p-2}\nabla\phi.\nabla f.

In
the weighted
p
-Laplacian when
\phi
is a constant function, the weighted
p
-Laplace operator is just the
p
-Laplace operator and when
p=2
, the weighted
p
-Laplace operator is the Witten-Laplace operator.

Let
\Lambda
satisfies in
-\Delta_{p,\phi}f=\Lambda|f|^{p-2}f,
for some
f\in W^{1,p}(M)
, in this case we say
\Lambda
is an eigenvalue of the weighted
p
-Laplacian
\Delta_{p,\phi}
at time
t\in[0,T)
. Notice that
\Lambda
equivalently satisfies in

(1.4)

-\int_{M}f\Delta_{p,\phi}fd\mu=\Lambda\int_{M}|f|^{p}d\mu,

where
d\mu=e^{-\phi(x)}d\nu
and
d\nu
is the Riemannian volume measure and using the integration by parts it results that

(1.5)

\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{p}\,d\mu=\Lambda\int_{M}|f|^{p}d\mu,

in above equation,
f(x,t)
called eigenfunction corresponding to eigenvalue
\Lambda(t)
. The first non-zero eigenvalue
\lambda(t)=\lambda(M,g(t),d\mu)
is defined as follows

(1.6)

\lambda(t)=\mathop{\inf}\limits_{0\neq f\in W_{0}^{1,p}(M)}\left\{\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{p}d\mu:\,\,\,\,\int_{M}|f|^{p}d\mu=1\right\},

where
W_{0}^{1,p}(M)
is the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
with respect Sobolev norm

(1.7)

||f||_{W^{1,p}}=\left(\int_{M}|f|^{p}d\mu+\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{p}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The eigenvalue problem for weighted
p
-Laplacian has been extensively studied in the literature
[

13
]

The problem of monotonicity of the eigenvalue of geometric operator is a known and an intrinsic problem. Recently many mathematicians study properties of evolution of the eigenvalue of geometric operators, for instance, Laplace,
p
-Laplace, Witten-Laplace, along various geometric flows, for example, Yamabe flow, Ricci flow, Ricci-Bourguignon flow, Ricci-harmonic flow and mean curvature flow. The main study of evolution of the eigenvalue of geometric operator along the geometric flow began when Perelman in

]

Then Cao

under the Ricci flow and the Ricci-Bourguignon flow, respectively.

Author in
[
2
]
studied the monotonicity of the first eigenvalue of Witten-Laplace operator

along the Ricci-Bourguignon flow with some assumptions and in

[
1
]
investigate the evolution for the first eigenvalue of
p
-Laplacian along the Yamabe flow.

In
[
9
]
and
[
15
]

have been studied the evolution for the first eigenvalue of geometric operator

along the Yamabe flow and the Ricci flow, respectively.
For the other recent research in this subject, see

,
8
,
16
]

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the Ricci-Bourguignon flow equation (
1.1
), we get

(1)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g^{ij}=2(R^{ij}-\rho Rg^{ij})
,

(2)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(d\nu)=(n\rho-1)Rd\nu

(3)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(d\mu)=(-\phi_{t}+(n\rho-1)R)d\mu

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Gamma_{ij}^{k})=-\nabla_{j}R_{i}^{k}-\nabla_{i}R_{j}^{k}+\nabla^{k}R_{ij}+\rho(\nabla_{j}R\delta_{i}^{k}+\nabla_{i}R\delta_{j}^{k}-\nabla^{k}Rg_{ij})

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}R=[1-2(n-1)\rho]\Delta R+2|Ric|^{2}-2\rho R^{2}

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2R^{ij}\nabla_{i}f\nabla_{j}f-2\rho R|\nabla f|^{2}+2g^{ij}\nabla_{i}f\nabla_{j}f_{t},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\nabla f|^{p-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-2)|\nabla f|^{p-4}\{R^{ij}\nabla_{i}f\nabla_{j}f-\rho R|\nabla f|^{2}+g^{ij}\nabla_{i}f\nabla_{j}f_{t}\},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Delta f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2R^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}f+\Delta f_{t}-2\rho R\Delta f-(2-n)\rho\nabla^{k}R\nabla_{k}f,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Delta_{p}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2R^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z\nabla_{j}f)-2\rho R\Delta_{p}f+g^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z_{t}\nabla_{j}f)

\displaystyle+g^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z\nabla_{j}f_{t})+\rho(n-2)Zg^{ij}\nabla_{i}R\nabla_{j}f,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Delta_{p,\phi}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2R^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z\nabla_{j}f)+g^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z_{t}\nabla_{j}f)+g^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z\nabla_{j}f_{t})

\displaystyle-2\rho R\Delta_{p,\phi}f+\rho(n-2)Zg^{ij}\nabla_{i}R\nabla_{j}f-Z_{t}\nabla\phi.\nabla f

\displaystyle-2ZR^{ij}\nabla_{i}\phi\nabla_{j}f-Z\nabla\phi_{t}.\nabla f-Z\nabla\phi.\nabla f_{t},

where

and

.

Proof.

By direct computation in local coordinates we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(g^{ij}\nabla_{i}f\nabla_{j}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g^{ij}}{\partial t}\nabla_{i}f\nabla_{j}f+2g^{ij}\nabla_{i}f\nabla_{j}f_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2R^{ij}\nabla_{i}f\nabla_{j}f-2\rho R|\nabla f|^{2}+2g^{ij}\nabla_{i}f\nabla_{j}f_{t},

which exactly (
3.1
). We prove (
3.2

) as follows

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\nabla f|^{p-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(|\nabla f|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p-2}{2}(|\nabla f|^{2})^{\frac{p-4}{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(|\nabla f|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p-2}{2}|\nabla f|^{p-4}\left\{2R^{ij}\nabla_{i}f\nabla_{j}f-2\rho R|\nabla f|^{2}+2g^{ij}\nabla_{i}f\nabla_{j}f_{t}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-2)|\nabla f|^{p-4}\left\{R^{ij}\nabla_{i}f\nabla_{j}f-\rho R|\nabla f|^{2}+g^{ij}\nabla_{i}f\nabla_{j}f_{t}\right\},

which is (
3.2

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Delta f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}[g^{ij}(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{k}})]

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g^{ij}}{\partial t}(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{k}})+g^{ij}(\frac{\partial^{2}f_{t}}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial x^{k}})-g^{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Gamma_{ij}^{k})\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2R^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}f-2\rho R\Delta f+\Delta f_{t}-g^{ij}\left\{-\nabla_{j}R_{i}^{k}-\nabla_{i}R_{j}^{k}+\nabla^{k}R_{ij}\right\}\nabla_{k}f

\displaystyle-g^{ij}\rho(\nabla_{j}R\delta_{i}^{k}+\nabla_{i}R\delta_{j}^{k}-\nabla^{k}Rg_{ij})\nabla_{k}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2R^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}f+\Delta f_{t}-2\rho R\Delta f-(2-n)\rho\nabla^{k}R\nabla_{k}f.

Let

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Delta_{p}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\big(div(|\nabla f|^{p-2}\nabla f)\big)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\big(g^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z\nabla_{j}f)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\big(g^{ij}\nabla_{i}Z\nabla_{j}f+g^{ij}Z\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}f\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g^{ij}}{\partial t}\nabla_{i}Z\nabla_{j}f+g^{ij}\nabla_{i}Z_{t}\nabla_{j}f+g^{ij}\nabla_{i}Z\nabla_{j}f_{t}+Z_{t}\Delta f+Z\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Delta f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2R^{ij}\nabla_{i}Z\nabla_{j}f-2\rho Rg^{ij}\nabla_{i}Z\nabla_{j}f+g^{ij}\nabla_{i}Z_{t}\nabla_{j}f+g^{ij}\nabla_{i}Z\nabla_{j}f_{t}+Z_{t}\Delta f

\displaystyle+Z\{2R^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}f+\Delta f_{t}-2\rho R\Delta f-(2-n)\rho\nabla^{k}R\nabla_{k}f\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2R^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z\nabla_{j}f)-2\rho R\Delta_{p}f+g^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z_{t}\nabla_{j}f)

\displaystyle+g^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z\nabla_{j}f_{t})+\rho(n-2)Zg^{ij}\nabla_{i}R\nabla_{j}f.

We have
\Delta_{p,\phi}f=\Delta_{p}f-|\nabla f|^{p-2}\nabla\phi.\nabla f

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Delta_{p,\phi}f)

\displaystyle-Zg^{ij}\nabla_{i}\phi\nabla_{j}f_{t}

\displaystyle 2R^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z\nabla_{j}f)-2\rho R\Delta_{p}f+g^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z_{t}\nabla_{j}f)+g^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z\nabla_{j}f_{t})

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(M,g(t),\phi(t)),\,\,\,t\in[0,T)

Proof.

Let

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\lambda(t,f(t))|_{t=t_{0}}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}f\Delta_{p,\phi}f\,d\mu.

Now by applying condition

and the time derivative, we can have

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}|f|^{p}d\mu=0=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}|f|^{p-2}f^{2}d\mu=\int_{M}(p-1)|f|^{p-2}ff_{t}d\mu+\int_{M}|f|^{p-2}f\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(fd\mu),

(3.9)

\int_{M}|f|^{p-2}f\left[(p-1)f_{t}d\mu+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(fd\mu)\right]=0.

On the other hand, using (
3.5

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}f\Delta_{p,\phi}f\,d\mu

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Delta_{p,\phi}f)f\,d\mu+\int_{M}\Delta_{p,\phi}f\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(f\,d\mu)

\displaystyle+\int_{M}g^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z_{t}\nabla_{j}f)f\,d\mu+\int_{M}g^{ij}\nabla_{i}(Z\nabla_{j}f_{t})f\,d\mu

\displaystyle+\rho(n-2)\int_{M}Z\nabla R.\nabla ff\,d\mu-\int_{M}Z_{t}\nabla\phi.\nabla ff\,d\mu

\displaystyle-\int_{M}Z\nabla\phi_{t}.\nabla ff\,d\mu-\int_{M}Z\nabla\phi.\nabla f_{t}f\,d\mu

By the application of integration by parts we can conclude that

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}Z\nabla_{i}R^{ij}\nabla_{j}ff\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\lambda(t,f(t))|_{t=t_{0}}

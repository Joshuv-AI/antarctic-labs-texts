Dynamic programming in convex stochastic optimization
Teemu Pennanen, Ari-Pekka Perkkiö

Abstract
This paper studies the dynamic programming principle for general convex stochastic optimization problems introduced by Rockafellar and Wets in [ 30 ] . We extend the applicability of the theory by relaxing compactness and boundedness assumptions. In the context of financial mathematics, the relaxed assumption are satisfied under the well-known no-arbitrage condition and the reasonable asymptotic elasticity condition of the utility function. Besides financial mathematics, we obtain several new results in linear and nonlinear stochastic programming and stochastic optimal control.



Keywords.
Dynamic programming, stochastic programming, convexity

An extended real-valued random variable

Conditional expectations of normal integrands

The general theory of dynamic programming studied in this paper builds on conditional expectations of normal integrands. This section reviews the theory that will be used in the analysis in the subsequent sections. Most of the results below can be found in the literature but we include the simple proofs because that allows us to make some extensions to the existing theory.

2.1
Existence

Conditional expectations of convex normal integrands were introduced in
[
7
]
. The article
[
12
]
extended the definition to general
{\cal B}\otimes{\cal F}
-measurable lower bounded integrands. More general conditions for the existence of a conditional expectation of a normal integrand have been given in
[
35
,
36
,
9
]
. Our arguments and conditions for existence in Section

are largely from
[

We say that a normal integrand
h
is
L-bounded
if there exist
\rho,m\in L^{1}
with

h(x)\geq-\rho|x|-m\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Condition 3 in the following lemma was used for the existence of a conditional expectation in
[
7
, Theorem 2]
.

Lemma 1
.

For a convex normal integrand
h
, the following are equivalent:

1.

h
is L-bounded,

2.

there exist
v\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
m\in L^{1}
such that

h(x,\omega)\geq x\cdot v(\omega)-m(\omega),

3.

\mathop{\rm dom}\nolimits Eh^{*}\cap L^{1}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
v\in L^{1}
such that
Eh^{*}(v)<\infty
is finite. By Fenchel’s inequality,

h(x,\omega)\geq x\cdot v-h^{*}(v,\omega)\geq-|v||x|-h^{*}(v,\omega),

so we may choose
\rho=|v|
and
m=h^{*}(v)^{+}
. On the other hand,
h\geq-\rho|\cdot|-m
can be written as
(h+\rho|\cdot|)^{*}(0)\leq m
. By
[
29
, Theorem 16.4]
, this means that

\inf_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\{h^{*}(v)+\delta_{\mathbb{B}}(v/\rho)\}\leq m,

where the infimum is attained. By
[
33
, Theorem 14.37]
, there is a
v\in L^{0}
with
|v|\leq\rho
and
h^{*}(v)\leq m
.
∎

If a normal integrand
h
is L-bounded, then
h(x)
is quasi-integrable and
E^{\cal G}[h(x)]
is well-defined for every
x\in L^{\infty}({\cal G})
. The following lemma shows that, for L-bounded normal integrands, it suffices to test with
x\in L^{\infty}({\cal G})
in the definition of conditional expectation.

Lemma 2
.

Given an L-bounded normal integrand
h
, a
{\cal G}
-normal integrand
\bar{h}
is the
{\cal G}
-conditional expectation of
h
if and only if

\bar{h}(x)=E^{\cal G}[h(x)]\quad a.s.

for all
x\in L^{\infty}({\cal G})
. In particular, if
h(x)
is
{\cal G}
-measurable for every
x\in L^{\infty}({\cal G})
, then
E^{\cal G}h=h
.

Proof.

It is clear that the condition holds if
\bar{h}=E^{\cal G}h
. On the other hand, if
\bar{h}\neq E^{\cal G}h
, then there exists an
x\in L^{0}({\cal G})
such that
h(x)
is quasi-integrable and
\bar{h}(x)\neq E^{\cal G}[h(x)]
. For
\nu
large enough,

\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq\nu\}}\bar{h}(x)\neq\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq\nu\}}E^{\cal G}[h(x)].

The left side equals
\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq\nu\}}\bar{h}(\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq\nu\}}x)
while the right side equals

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq\nu\}}E^{\cal G}[h(x)]

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq\nu\}}E^{\cal G}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq\nu\}}h(x)]

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq\nu\}}E^{\cal G}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq\nu\}}h(\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq\nu\}}x)]

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq\nu\}}E^{\cal G}[h(\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq\nu\}}x)].

Thus,
\bar{h}(\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq\nu\}}x)\neq E^{\cal G}[h(\mathbbm{1}_{\{|x|\leq\nu\}}x)]
so the condition fails. By
[
35
, Lemma 6]
, the condition defines
\bar{h}
uniquely almost surely everywhere.
∎

Given

Theorem 4
.

Let
h
be a real-valued normal integrand such that there exist
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\rho\in L^{1}
with
h(\bar{x})\in L^{1}
and

|h(x)-h(x^{\prime})|\leq\rho|x-x^{\prime}|\quad\forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then
E^{\cal G}h
exists and it is characterized by

(E^{\cal G}h)(x)=E^{\cal G}[h(x)]\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Moreover,

|(E^{\cal G}h)(x-x^{\prime})|\leq(E^{\cal G}\rho)|x-x^{\prime}|\quad\forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1)

Proof.

The assumptions imply that
h(x)\in L^{1}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and that, by Jensen’s inequality,

|E^{\cal G}[h(x)]-E^{\cal G}[h(x^{\prime})]|\leq(E^{\cal G}\rho)|x-x^{\prime}|\quad a.s.

for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
D
be a countable dense set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and define
\tilde{h}(x,\omega):=E^{\cal G}[h(x)](\omega)
for each
x\in D
. By countability of
D
, there is a
P
-null set
N\in{\cal G}
such that, for all
\omega\in\Omega\setminus N
,

|\tilde{h}(x,\omega)-\tilde{h}(x^{\prime},\omega)|\leq(E^{\cal G}\rho)(\omega)|x-x^{\prime}|\quad\forall x,x^{\prime}\in D.

The function
\tilde{h}
has a unique continuous extension to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\Omega\setminus N)
. Finally, we extend the definition of
\tilde{h}
to all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega
by setting
\tilde{h}(\cdot,\omega)=0
for
\omega\in N
. The function
\tilde{h}
thus constructed is a
{\cal G}
-measurable Caratheodory integrand and thus, normal. It is clear that it satisfies (
1
) as well.

If
x=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}x^{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A^{i}}
, where
x^{i}\in D
and
A^{i}\in{\cal G}
form a disjoint partition of
\Omega
, we have

E^{\cal G}[h(x)]=E^{\cal G}[h(\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}x^{i}\mathbbm{1}_{A^{i}})]=E^{\cal G}[\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\mathbbm{1}_{A^{i}}h(x^{i})]=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\mathbbm{1}_{A^{i}}\tilde{h}(x^{i})=\tilde{h}(x).

Any
x\in L^{\infty}({\cal G})
is an almost sure limit of such simple random variables bounded by
\|x\|_{L^{\infty}}
, so dominated convergence for conditional expectations (see e.g.
[
34
, Theorem II.7.2]
) and scenariowise continuity of
h
and
\tilde{h}
imply that
E^{\cal G}[h(x)]=\tilde{h}(x)
. Thus Lemma
implies the claim.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let

be L-bounded normal integrands with

For any

The following result is a monotone convergence theorem for conditional expectations of integrands.

Theorem 7
.

Let
(h^{\nu})_{\nu=1}^{\infty}
be a nondecreasing sequence of L-bounded normal integrands and

h=\sup_{\nu}h^{\nu}.

If each
E^{\cal G}h^{\nu}
exists, then
E^{\cal G}h
exists and

E^{\cal G}h=\sup_{\nu}E^{\cal G}h^{\nu}.

Proof.

For any
x\in L^{\infty}({\cal G})
and
\alpha\in L^{\infty}({\cal G};\mathbb{R}_{+})
, monotone convergence and Lemma
imply that

\displaystyle E[\alpha{h}(x)]

\displaystyle=E[\alpha\sup_{\nu}h^{\nu}(x)]=\sup_{\nu}E[\alpha h^{\nu}(x)]

\displaystyle=\sup_{\nu}E[\alpha E^{\cal G}[h^{\nu}(x)]]=E[\alpha\sup_{\nu}E^{\cal G}[h^{\nu}(x)]].

Thus
\sup_{\nu}E^{\cal G}h^{\nu}=E^{\cal G}h
.
∎

The following is our main result on the existence of conditional normal integrands.

Theorem 8
.

An L-bounded (convex) normal integrand admits a conditional expectation and that is L-bounded (and convex) as well.

Proof.

Let

be an L-bounded normal integrand. Assume first that
h\leq m
for some constant

h^{\nu}(x,\omega):=\inf_{x^{\prime}}\{h(x^{\prime},\omega)+\nu\rho(\omega)|x-x^{\prime}|\}

form a nondecreasing sequence of Caratheodory functions increasing pointwise to

imply that

satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
.
Thus, by Theorems
and
,

exists. To remove the assumption

(which are normal integrands, by

Let

Let

Remark 12
.

Conditional expectation is a linear operator on the linear space of normal integrands that satisfy the assumptions of ?THM?
.

Proof.

If
h
satisfies the assumptions of ?THM?
, then so does

, and

for every

. Thus

E^{\cal G}[-h](x)=E^{\cal G}[-h(x)]=-E^{\cal G}[h(x)]=-(E^{\cal G}h)(x)\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

so
E^{\cal G}[-h]=-E^{{\cal G}}h

∎

The class of normal integrands is not a linear space, so one cannot hope for linearity of the conditional expectation, in general.

The product of two extended real numbers is defined as zero if one of them is zero while the nonnegative scalar multiple of a function

is defined by

(\alpha h)(x):=\begin{cases}\alpha h(x)&\text{if $\alpha>0$},\\
\delta_{\mathop{\rm cl}\nolimits\mathop{\rm dom}\nolimits h}(x)&\text{if $\alpha=0$}.\end{cases}

Equivalently,

(\alpha h)(x):=\alpha h(x)+\delta_{\mathop{\rm cl}\nolimits\mathop{\rm dom}\nolimits h}(x)\quad\alpha\geq 0.

Lemma 58
.

Given extended real-valued random variables
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
, we have

E[\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}]=E[\xi_{1}]+E[\xi_{2}]

under any of the following:

1.

\xi_{1}^{+},\xi_{2}^{+}\in L^{1}
or
\xi_{1}^{-},\xi_{2}^{-}\in L^{1}

2.

\xi_{1}\in L^{1}
or
\xi_{2}\in L^{1}
,

3.

\xi_{1}
or
\xi_{2}
is

Proof.

If both

\xi_{2}

\displaystyle(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})^{+}\leq\xi_{1}^{+}+\xi_{2}^{+},\quad(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})^{-}\leq\xi_{1}^{-}+\xi_{2}^{-},

\displaystyle\xi_{i}^{+}\leq(\xi_{i}+\xi_{j})^{+}+\xi_{j}^{-},\quad\xi_{i}^{-}\leq(\xi_{i}+\xi_{j})^{-}+\xi_{j}^{+}.

\xi_{1}^{+},\xi_{2}^{+}\in L^{1}

\xi_{i}^{-}\notin L^{1}

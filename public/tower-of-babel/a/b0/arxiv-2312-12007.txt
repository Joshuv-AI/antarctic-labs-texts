n n -valued quandles and associated bialgebras
Valeriy G. Bardakov, Tatyana A. Kozlovskaya, Dmitry V. Talalaev

Abstract
The principal aim of this article is to introduce and study n n -valued quandles and n n -corack bialgebras. We elaborate the basic methods of this theory, reproduce the coset construction known in the theory of n n -valued groups. We also consider a construction of n n -valued quandles using n n -multi-quandles. In contrast to the case of n n -valued groups this construction turns out to be quite rich in algebraic and topological applications. An important part of the work is the study of the properties of n n -corack bialgebras those role is analogous to the group bialgebra. Keywords : Multi-set, multivalued group, multi-group, rack, quandle, n n -valued quandle, bi-algebra, rack bi-algebra. Mathematics Subject Classification 2010: Primary 20N20; Secondary Secondary 16S34, 05E30

Proposition 5.4

\Delta f(x,h^{-1}yh)=\sum_{b\in B}f(b^{-1}xb\cdot h^{-1}yh)=\sum_{b\in B}f(hb^{-1}xbh^{-1}\cdot y)=\sum_{b^{\prime}\in B}f((b^{\prime})^{-1}xb^{\prime}\cdot y)=\Delta f(x,y).

5.3

To each finite quandle
(Q,*)
one can associate a bialgebra
\Bbbk[Q]
which is a linear space those basis we denote by the same letters

\displaystyle m(e_{i},e_{j})=e_{i}\ast e_{j};

\displaystyle\Delta e_{i}=e_{i}\otimes e_{i}.

It is easy to check that

1.

The comultiplication

\displaystyle(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta)\Delta.

The multiplication is a homomorphism of coalgebras

\displaystyle\Delta m=(m\otimes m)(\mathrm{id}\otimes\sigma\otimes\mathrm{id})(\Delta\otimes\Delta)

(8)

where
\sigma
is a permutation of the corresponding tensor components;

3.

The self-distributivity condition of

\displaystyle m(m\otimes\mathrm{id})=m(m\otimes m)(\mathrm{id}\otimes\sigma\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathrm{id}\otimes\ id\otimes\Delta).

(9)

The self-distributivity condition (

(c*b)*a=\sum_{(a)}(c*a^{(1)})*(b*a^{(2)})

where we used the Sweedler notations:

\displaystyle\Delta a=\sum_{i}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes a_{i}^{(2)}=\sum a^{(1)}\otimes a^{(2)}.

(10)

Remark 5.7

A similar approaches to rack bialgebras can be found in

\displaystyle\Delta f(x,y)=f(x\ast y).

\displaystyle(m\otimes m)(id\otimes\sigma\otimes id)(\Delta\otimes\Delta)=\Delta m,

where

.

Let

-valued rack, then the algebra of functions

-corack bialgebra.

Proof.

The only thing to prove is the condition that

is an

as an algebra. The demonstration goes through the same lines as in the case of
n
-groups, but we provide it here for the sake of completeness. Let us calculate the derived comultiplications, the 2-nd one is:

\displaystyle\Delta^{(2)}(f_{1},f_{2})=\Delta(f_{1})\Delta(f_{2})-\Delta(f_{1}f_{2}).

This is an element in
C(Q)\otimes C(Q).
We associate this with the space of functions on two arguments. Let us recall that

\displaystyle x\ast y=[z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}]

and

\displaystyle\Delta(f)(x,y)=\sum_{i}f(z_{i})=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \lambda_{i}\in\Bbbk.

(13)

\displaystyle f_{i}(z_{j})=\lambda_{j}^{i},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \lambda_{j}^{i}\in\Bbbk,\penalty\ \penalty\ i=1,2,\ldots,\penalty\ \penalty\ j=1,2,\ldots,n.

\displaystyle f_{1}(x\ast y)f_{2}(x\ast y)-(f_{1}f_{2})(x\ast y)

\displaystyle(\sum_{i}\lambda^{1}_{i})(\sum_{j}\lambda^{2}_{j})-\sum_{i}\lambda^{1}_{i}\lambda^{2}_{i}=\sum_{i\neq j}\lambda_{i}^{1}\lambda_{j}^{2}.

\displaystyle\Delta^{(k)}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})(x,y)=\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}:j_{l}\neq j_{m}}\lambda^{1}_{j_{1}}\ldots\lambda^{k}_{j_{k}}.

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1}}\lambda^{1}_{j_{1}}\sum_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{k+1}:j_{l}\neq j_{m}}\lambda^{2}_{j_{2}}\ldots\lambda^{k+1}_{j_{k+1}}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j_{2},\ldots,j_{k+1}:j_{l}\neq j_{m}}\lambda^{1}_{j_{i+1}}\lambda^{2}_{j_{2}}\ldots\lambda^{k+1}_{j_{k+1}}.

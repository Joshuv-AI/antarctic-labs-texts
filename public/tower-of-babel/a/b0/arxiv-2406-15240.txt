Tree-level
R

Abstract




The tree-level string effective action reduced from
D
to
D-d
dimensions possesses a continuous
O(d,d)
symmetry, closely related to T-duality. A necessary condition for a higher derivative correction to preserve this symmetry is that certain
O(d,d)
violating terms which appear in the dimensional reduction have to cancel out. We use this idea to complete the quartic Riemann correction with all terms involving five NS-NS sector fields. The resulting Lagrangian is considerably simpler than expressions that have previously appeared in the literature.

1
Introduction

The first correction to the tree-level effective action for the type II string has been known for a long time to involve a quartic Riemann invariant at order
\alpha^{\prime 3}
. More explicitly it takes the form
[
1
,
2
,
3
]

\frac{\zeta(3)\alpha^{\prime 3}}{2^{11}}\int d^{D}x\sqrt{-g}\,e^{-2\Phi}L\,,\qquad L=\frac{1}{4!}t_{8}t_{8}R^{4}+\ldots

(1.1)

In fact, a similar correction appears also for the heterotic string
[
4
,
5
]
and the bosonic string
[
6
]
(
D
denotes the critical dimension). This
R^{4}
-term is known to be accompanied by a large number of terms, but the full expression is not known. Here we confine ourselves to the NS-NS sector only. In that case we know from four-point string scattering amplitude calculations that the Riemann tensor should actually be replaced by the torsionful Riemann tensor
1
1

1

This is the Riemann tensor computed from the torsionful spin connection
\omega^{(-)}=\omega-\frac{1}{2}H
(it is related to the one compute from
\omega^{(+)}=\omega+\frac{1}{2}H
by sending
H\rightarrow-H
or, equivalently, exchanging the first two and last two indices).

\mathcal{R}^{ab}{}_{cd}=\mathcal{R}_{S}^{ab}{}_{cd}-(\nabla H)^{ab}{}_{cd}\,,

(1.2)

where

\mathcal{R}_{S}^{ab}{}_{cd}=R_{S}^{ab}{}_{cd}+\frac{1}{2}H^{[a}{}_{ce}H^{b]e}{}_{d}\,,

(1.3)

is symmetric under exchanging the first two and the last two indices, while

(\nabla H)^{ab}{}_{cd}=\nabla^{[a}H^{b]}{}_{cd}

(1.4)

is anti-symmetric. Furthermore, it is known from five-point amplitude calculations that there is also a
\varepsilon_{8}\varepsilon_{8}\mathcal{R}^{4}
term, as well as certain
H^{2}\mathcal{R}^{3}
terms
[
7
]
. Terms involving the derivative of the dilaton and terms quartic in the
B
-field have so far not been computed from amplitudes. In principle one could simply continue and compute all the remaining terms from scattering amplitudes. This would however be very involved for two reasons – (1) one would have to go up to the eight-point amplitude (to get the
H^{8}
terms) and (2) one has to match this to a field theory calculation involving an ansatz with an enormous number of terms. This approach does not seem very practical. Another possible approach is to try to use supersymmetry to find the remaining terms. We will not follow this approach here, instead we refer to
[
8
]
for a recent review of the state of the art. A third possible approach is to try to use duality symmetries to fix the remaining terms. This is the approach we will take here.

The most straightforward way to do this is to take the most general ansatz for the
D
-dimensional action, reduce it on
S^{1}
, and require the result to be invariant under T-duality. This calculation has been carried out by Garousi in a series of papers. Due to the ansatz involving of the order of 1000 terms
[
9
]
the calculation was done using symbolic software. He found that the full NS-NS sector Lagrangian is uniquely fixed by the requirement of T-duality
[
10
]
. While this result is very nice, there is one serious drawback – the resulting Lagrangian contains over 400 terms! While it turns out to be possible to use the previously known structures to simplify the result
[
11
,
12
]
, the Lagrangian is still too unwieldy, involving just under 200 terms. There is good reason to think that the result can be cast in a much better form, but it is very hard to find the right form without some additional hints for what structures should appear. For this reason we will follow a different approach, where it is easier to recognize which structures must appear along the way.

We will use the fact that the tree-level string effective action reduced to
D-d
dimensions has a continuous
O(d,d)
symmetry
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
. This symmetry is closely related to T-duality. The presence of this symmetry in the reduced theory gives very strong constraints on the form of the unreduced theory. However, carrying out the full dimensional reduction and imposing the
O(d,d)
symmetry is even more complicated (for general
d
) than requiring T-duality of the
S^{1}
reduction. But there is a way to simplify the calculations, which was introduced in
[
16
]
.
2
2

2

Other approaches to simplifying the use of duality symmetries to constrain higher derivative corrections include
[
17
]
and
[
18
,
19
]
. These have so far only been applied at the first order in
\alpha^{\prime}
. Other works, e.g.
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
, have considered a “cosmological reduction” to one dimension, but in that case the constraints are not strong enough to fix all terms in the action. Finally, approaches which attempt to make the duality symmetries manifest from the beginning appear to fail at order
\alpha^{\prime 3}

[
23
]
.
The idea is to implement only a necessary condition for
O(d,d)
symmetry. We consider only the terms quadratic in the KK vectors that arise in the dimensional reduction, with no dependence on the scalars (except the dilaton). Dimensional reduction of the NS-NS sector fields gives rise to two vector fields, one coming from the metric and one from the B-field. Taking their sum and difference we have the two fields
A_{a}{}^{c^{\prime}}
and
\hat{A}_{b}{}^{d^{\prime}}
, where unprimed/primed indices are external/internal. Since any internal indices must be contracted in the effective action there are three possible contractions of these that can appear in the reduced action:
A_{a}\cdot A_{b}
,
\hat{A}_{a}\cdot\hat{A}_{b}
and
A_{a}\cdot\hat{A}_{b}
(actually, due to gauge invariance their fields strengths appear instead of
A
and
\hat{A}
). Now, the point is that the KK vectors combine to a single
O(d,d)
vector, with respect to the internal index, but there are only
two
quadratic
O(d,d)
invariants: the contraction with the constant
O(d,d)
invariant metric and the contraction with the so-called generalized metric. One finds that the contractions
A_{a}\cdot A_{b}
and
\hat{A}_{a}\cdot\hat{A}_{b}
respect the
O(d,d)
symmetry, while
A_{a}\cdot\hat{A}_{b}
violates it. Therefore a necessary condition for
O(d,d)
invariance of the reduced action is that all terms proportional to
A_{a}\cdot\hat{A}_{b}
cancel out. This condition turns out to be extremely strong. It fixes the form of the correction essentially completely.
3
3

3

Terms involving only the derivative of the dilaton turn out not to be fixed. Such terms are not expected and indeed they are known not to be present for the correction in question.

In
[
16
]
this condition was used to complete the
R^{4}
terms up to fifth order in fields, up to dilaton terms. The
H^{2}\mathcal{R}^{3}
terms were shown to agree with amplitude calculations
[
7
]
. They also agree with the results of Garousi
[
12
]
. Here we revisit this calculation in order to (1) include also the dilaton terms (2) determine a better form for the
H^{4}\mathcal{R}
terms, whose form in
[
16
]
, while simpler than the expressions found by Garousi, did not have a particularly natural form. Taking into account a similar calculation at order
\alpha^{\prime 2}

[
24
]
, we are able to reduce the
H^{4}\mathcal{R}
terms by almost a factor of two compared to
[
16
]
, as well as find a natural structure for at least some of these terms. The result is a complete form for the completion of
R^{4}
up to fifth order in NS-NS fields, which is by far the simplest form known to date. Ultimately this result should of course be completed to include the higher order terms, which have the form
H^{4}\mathcal{R}^{2}
,
H^{6}\mathcal{R}
and
H^{8}
, either by the same approach or by comparing to the all order results of Garousi. Both approaches involve long calculations, but the latter may easily be done with the aid of a computer. However, there is good reason to expect that these higher order terms will not be too complicated as there are not as many terms with these structures and also given the result at
\alpha^{\prime 2}
in
[
24
]
, where most of the higher order terms could be removed (see also the results of
[
12
]
). Finally, the RR fields also need to be included to have the complete correction in the case of the type II string, for some progress in this direction see
[
25
]
.

Some words of clarification are in order here. Firstly, note that we are not trying to prove the full
O(d,d)
invariance of the reduced action. This was already (indirectly) proven by Sen in
[
15
]
. Instead, we are imposing only a
necessary
condition for
O(d,d)
.
4
4

4

Using our results it would be straightforward (but very tedious) to prove directly the full
O(d,d)
invariance. To do this one of course has to carry out the full dimensional reduction, without truncating the scalars. The argument at the beginning of section 4 of
[
16
]
shows that this is guaranteed to work.
Another important point concerns the uniqueness of the result. As already mentioned the result is not unique since certain dilaton terms, e.g.
((\nabla_{a}^{(+)}\partial_{b}\Phi)^{2})^{2}
, can be added without affecting the results. Besides these terms, which involve only the dilaton and its derivatives, no other non-uniqueness is encountered in the calculations. But we did not prove that there is no other source of non-uniqueness, since this would involve lengthy calculations (and anyway seems very unlikely). Instead we rely on the results of Garousi mentioned above, which show that there is a unique Lagrangian compatible with the
O(1,1)
symmetry of the circle reduction. The only thing that remains to do is then to match our
H^{4}\mathcal{R}
terms to those of Garousi (all other terms already match), which would be straightforward with the aid of a computer. There is very little doubt that the two will match (up to field redefinitions). Indeed, at order
\alpha^{\prime 2}
the two results match perfectly, as shown in
[
24
]
. The point of the present paper is not so much to rederive the results of Garousi in a different way, but rather to find a more suitable form for those results.

The calculations in this paper are essentially straightforward, but in places very long due to a large number of integrations by parts and use of Bianchi identities needed. We give a lot of detail for most steps of the calculation in the bulk of the paper. However, most readers will be mainly interested in the end result and for this reason we summarize it here.

1.1
Summary of results

For ease of presentation we have divided the Lagrangian into seven pieces

L=L_{1}+L_{2}+L_{3}+L_{4}+L_{5}+L_{6}+L_{7}\,,

(1.5)

where

\begin{split}L_{1}&=\frac{1}{4!}t_{8}t_{8}\mathcal{R}^{4}-\frac{1}{2(3!)}t_{8}t_{8}H^{2}\mathcal{R}^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{t}_{8}\tilde{t}_{8}H^{2}(\nabla H)^{2}\mathcal{R}+\frac{1}{4(4!)}(\varepsilon_{8}\varepsilon_{8})^{\prime}\mathcal{R}^{4}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2(3!)^{3}}(\varepsilon_{9}\varepsilon_{9})^{\prime}H^{2}\mathcal{R}^{3}-\frac{1}{2(3!)^{2}}H^{2}(\varepsilon_{6}\varepsilon_{6})^{\prime}\mathcal{R}^{3}+\frac{1}{2(3!)^{3}}(\varepsilon_{9}\varepsilon_{9})^{\prime}[H^{2}]\mathcal{R}^{3}\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2(3!)^{2}}(\varepsilon_{9}\varepsilon_{9})^{\prime}[H^{2}](\nabla H)^{2}\mathcal{R}+2\varepsilon_{4}H^{2}\varepsilon_{4}\mathcal{R}^{2}\mathcal{R}-4\varepsilon_{4}H^{2}\varepsilon_{4}(\nabla H)^{2}\mathcal{R}\,,\end{split}

(1.6)

contains the terms with (more or less) familiar structures. In particular it contains the quartic Riemann terms and all
H^{2}\mathcal{R}^{3}
terms. With the exception of the
H^{2}(\varepsilon_{6}\varepsilon_{6})^{\prime}\mathcal{R}^{3}
term these are the same as in
[
16
]
, but written more compactly, and they match with amplitude calculations as verified there. The former term was not visible in those calculations due to neglecting dilaton terms (related to this term via the equations of motion), it does match with
[
12
]
. We have also included
H^{2}(\nabla H)^{2}\mathcal{R}
terms with similar structures. The tensor structures appearing here are defined as follows

t_{8}M_{1}M_{2}M_{3}M_{4}=8\tr(M_{1}M_{2}M_{3}M_{4})-2\tr(M_{1}M_{2})\tr(M_{3}M_{4})+\mbox{cyclic}(2,3,4)\,,

(1.7)

which is standard, but we also define another version

\begin{split}\tilde{t}_{8}M_{1}M_{2}M_{3}M_{4}&=-8\tr(M_{1}M_{2}M_{3}M_{4})+8\tr(M_{1}M_{3}M_{4}M_{2})+8\tr(M_{1}M_{4}M_{2}M_{3})\\
&\quad+2\tr(M_{1}M_{2})\tr(M_{3}M_{4})-2\tr(M_{1}M_{3})\tr(M_{4}M_{2})+2\tr(M_{1}M_{4})\tr(M_{2}M_{3})\,,\end{split}

(1.8)

which differs from
t_{8}
in the sign of the first, fourth and sixth term. The
\varepsilon\varepsilon
structures are defined as

(\varepsilon_{8}\varepsilon_{8})^{\prime}\mathcal{R}^{4}=-8!\left(\mathcal{R}^{4}\right)^{\prime a_{1}\cdots a_{8}}{}_{[a_{1}\cdots a_{8}]}

(1.9)

and

(\varepsilon_{9}\varepsilon_{9})^{\prime}H^{2}\mathcal{R}^{3}=-9!H^{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}}H_{[a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}}\left(\mathcal{R}^{3}\right)^{\prime a_{4}\cdots a_{9}}{}_{a_{4}\cdots a_{9}]}\,,

(1.10)

which is also standard except for the prime, which means that all terms with self-contraction, i.e. terms involving the (torsionful) Ricci tensor and scalar, are to be removed. Again, this difference is only seen when dilaton terms are included so it was not seen in
[
16
]
or
[
7
]
, but again this agrees with
[
12
]
where the dilaton terms were included. We similarly have

H^{2}(\varepsilon_{6}\varepsilon_{6})^{\prime}\mathcal{R}^{3}=-6!H^{2}\left(\mathcal{R}^{3}\right)^{\prime a_{1}\cdots a_{6}}{}_{[a_{1}\cdots a_{6}]}\,,

(1.11)

where all the indices of the
H
s are contracted, i.e.
H^{2}=H_{abc}H^{abc}
. This term simply serves to remove the term with the
H
s completely contracted coming from
(\varepsilon_{9}\varepsilon_{9})^{\prime}H^{2}\mathcal{R}^{3}
. Finally, we have the term

(\varepsilon_{9}\varepsilon_{9})^{\prime}[H^{2}]\mathcal{R}^{3}=-\frac{6!^{2}}{6}H^{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}}H_{a_{4}a_{5}a_{6}}\left(\mathcal{R}^{3}\right)^{\prime[a_{4}\cdots a_{9}]}{}_{[a_{7}a_{8}a_{9}a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}]}\,,

(1.12)

where the square brackets mean that
H^{2}
should be treated as a single object, i.e. any term with contractions of the two
H
s is also removed. This term simply doubles the corresponding term coming from
(\varepsilon_{9}\varepsilon_{9})^{\prime}H^{2}\mathcal{R}^{3}
(curiously at order
\alpha^{\prime 2}
it has the opposite sign and the two contributions cancel rather than add up
[
24
]
). We have also defined a term with a somewhat different structure

\varepsilon_{4}H^{2}\varepsilon_{4}\mathcal{R}^{2}\mathcal{R}=-4!H_{a}{}^{a_{1}a_{2}}H^{ba_{3}a_{4}}\mathcal{R}_{ce[a_{1}a_{2}}\mathcal{R}_{a_{3}a_{4}]}{}^{ed}\mathcal{R}_{bd}{}^{ac}\,,

(1.13)

which was defined slightly differently, with an anti-symmetrization of five indices, in
[
16
]
.

The remaining terms in the Lagrangian consist of 25 terms of the form
H^{2}(\nabla H)^{2}\mathcal{R}
, which seem not to be expressible in terms of the above structures. Of course we can replace
\mathcal{R}
by
R
, the usual Riemann tensor, in these terms, since they differ only by higher order terms which we neglected here. First we have the four terms (
H^{2}_{abcd}=H_{abe}H^{e}{}_{cd}
)

\begin{split}L_{2}&=-4!(\nabla H)^{abef}(\nabla H)^{cd}{}_{ef}H_{k[ab}\nabla^{2}H^{k}{}_{cd]}+4!\nabla^{e}H^{fab}\nabla_{e}H_{f}{}^{cd}H_{k[ab}\nabla^{2}H^{k}{}_{cd]}\\
&\quad-2(4!)H^{2}_{[abcd]}(\nabla H)^{abef}(\nabla H)_{ghef}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{cdgh}-2(4!)H^{2}_{[abcd]}\nabla^{e}H^{fag}\nabla_{e}H_{f}{}^{bh}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{cd}{}_{gh}\,.\end{split}

(1.14)

The reason we have written them this way is that the first two terms turn out to be precisely the same as two terms that appear at order
\alpha^{\prime 2}

[
24
]
, except for an extra insertion of
\nabla^{2}
. The last two are also of the same form but with an extra insertion of
\mathcal{R}
. At the moment we don’t have a deeper understanding of why these terms should appear. Note that using the fact that
\nabla^{2}H_{abc}=-3R^{de}{}_{[ab}H_{c]de}
, up to higher order terms and terms proportional to the equations of motion (which can be removed by a field redefinition), also the first two terms take the form
H^{2}(\nabla H)^{2}\mathcal{R}
. Which is the right way to write these terms will only be clear once the higher order terms are computed (when working to higher order in fields on has to replace
\nabla^{2}
by
(\nabla_{a}-2\partial_{a}\Phi)\nabla^{a}
in order not to generate extra dilaton terms).

The remaining terms have a less clear structure, but they all have at least two pairs of anti-symmetrized indices contracted, which we refer to loosely as at least two ‘traces’. First we have the terms

\begin{split}L_{3}&=8\nabla_{e}H^{kcd}\nabla^{e}H_{fcd}H_{abk}\nabla^{2}H^{abf}-8\nabla_{e}H_{kcd}H^{fcd}\nabla^{e}H_{abk}\nabla^{2}H^{abf}\\
&\quad+8\nabla^{e}H^{2}_{abcd}H^{agh}\nabla_{e}H_{fgh}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{bfcd}-8H_{abf}H_{kcd}\nabla_{e}H^{abk}\nabla^{e}H^{fgh}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{cd}{}_{gh}\,.\end{split}

(1.15)

Their structure is again similar to terms that appear at order
\alpha^{\prime 2}
, with an extra insertion of
\nabla^{2}
or
\mathcal{R}
. Note that in these terms all derivatives are contracted among themselves. Next we have three terms with both derivatives contracted with
\mathcal{R}

\begin{split}L_{4}&=-16H^{2}_{abcd}\nabla_{e}H^{agh}\nabla_{f}H^{c}{}_{gh}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{bdef}-4H^{2}_{abcd}\nabla_{e}H^{cgh}\nabla_{f}H^{d}{}_{gh}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{abef}\\
&\quad+8H_{abh}H_{kcd}\nabla_{e}H^{abg}\nabla_{f}H_{g}{}^{cd}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{hkef}\,.\end{split}

(1.16)

Then we have five terms whose structure is different

\begin{split}L_{5}&=64H^{abc}H_{def}(\nabla H)_{ck}{}^{gd}(\nabla H)^{hk}{}_{ab}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{ef}{}_{gh}-16H^{abc}H_{def}(\nabla H)^{dk}{}_{ab}(\nabla H)_{ckgh}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{efgh}\\
&\quad+16H_{abe}H_{kcd}\nabla^{k}H^{egh}(\nabla H)^{c}{}_{fgh}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{fdab}+16H^{2}_{abcd}\nabla_{f}H^{abc}(\nabla H)^{efgh}\mathcal{R}_{Sdegh}\\
&\quad+4H_{abc}H^{def}\nabla_{d}H_{k}{}^{ab}(\nabla H)_{efgh}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{ckgh}\,,\end{split}

(1.17)

where the last two involve a ‘Chern-Simons like’ combination
H_{ab[c}\nabla^{e}H_{d]}{}^{ab}
, which also appears at order
\alpha^{\prime 2}
. Finally we have terms involving
H^{2}_{ab}=H_{acd}H_{b}{}^{cd}

\begin{split}L_{6}&=4H^{2}_{ef}\nabla_{k}H^{ecd}\nabla^{f}H^{kgh}\mathcal{R}_{Scdgh}+8H^{2}_{ef}\nabla_{k}H^{eab}(\nabla H)_{abgh}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{kfgh}\\
&\quad-8\nabla^{k}H^{2}_{ef}H_{abk}\nabla^{e}H^{a}{}_{gh}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{bfgh}-4\nabla_{k}H^{2}_{ef}H^{kcd}\nabla_{g}H_{hcd}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{eghf}\end{split}

(1.18)

and terms where two of the
H
s have all their indices contracted (
H^{2}=H_{abc}H^{abc}
)

\begin{split}L_{7}&=\frac{1}{3}\nabla^{e}H^{2}_{abcd}\nabla_{e}H^{2}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{abcd}-\frac{4}{3}H^{2}_{cd}\nabla_{a}H_{efg}\nabla_{b}H^{efg}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{acdb}-\frac{2}{3}H^{2}\nabla_{c}H_{agh}\nabla_{d}H_{b}{}^{gh}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{abcd}\\
&\quad+\frac{4}{3}\nabla^{h}H_{efg}\nabla^{k}H^{efg}H_{kcd}H_{abh}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{cdab}+\frac{4}{3}H^{2}(\nabla H)_{abcd}(\nabla H)^{abgh}\mathcal{R}_{S}^{cd}{}_{gh}\,.\end{split}

(1.19)

Note that there are no terms involving the derivative of the dilaton, which is consistent with the results of Garousi.

This Lagrangian was derived from the requirement that the
O(d,d)
violating terms quadratic in the KK vectors cancel out upon dimensional reduction. More precisely, they cancel out up to total derivative terms, which we drop throughout, and terms proportional to the equations of motion. The latter we keep track of up to first order in the
B
-field. They are given in (
3.42
). The presence of these terms shows that (very complicated) field redefinitions are required in the reduced theory before it becomes compatible with
O(d,d)
symmetry. Their form can be directly read off from (
3.42
). An interesting question, which we plan to return to, is whether these field redefinitions can be lifted to
D
dimensions.

The remainder of this paper is dedicated to the derivation of the Lagrangian presented above. In Sec.
2
we briefly review the Kaluza-Klein reduction focusing on the
O(d,d)
-violating terms quadratic in the KK vectors. In Sec.
3
we analyze the
O(d,d)
-violating terms coming from the terms with the
t_{8}
-structure. Their cancellation turns out to require many terms of different structures, whose dimensional reduction and simplification is deferred to the appendix.

2
Kaluza-Klein reduction and
O(d,d)

A nice discussion of Kaluza-Klein reduction and
O(d,d)
symmetry can be found in
[
26
]
. Here we will mostly be interested in how
O(d,d)
violating contractions of two KK vector fields can appear. We will summarize this briefly here and refer to
[
24
]
for more details.

The starting point is the usual KK ansatz for the vielbein (internal indices are denoted with a prime)

\underline{e}_{\underline{m}}{}^{\underline{a}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e_{m}{}^{a}&A^{(1)n^{\prime}}_{m}e_{n^{\prime}}{}^{a^{\prime}}\\
0&e_{m^{\prime}}{}^{a^{\prime}}\end{array}\right)

(2.1)

and the
B
-field

\underline{B}_{\underline{mn}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{B}_{mn}&A^{(2)}_{mn^{\prime}}+A^{(1)k^{\prime}}_{m}B_{k^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\\
-A^{(2)}_{nm^{\prime}}-A^{(1)k^{\prime}}_{n}B_{k^{\prime}m^{\prime}}&B_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\end{array}\right)\,,

(2.2)

where

\hat{B}_{mn}=B_{mn}-A_{[m}^{(1)m^{\prime}}A_{n]m^{\prime}}^{(2)}+A^{(1)k^{\prime}}_{m}A^{(1)l^{\prime}}_{n}B_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\,.

(2.3)

Besides the lower-dimensional metric,
B
-field and dilaton we obtain
2d
vector fields
A^{(1)m^{\prime}}
and
A_{m^{\prime}}^{(2)}
(we suppress, for the moment, the external vector index) as well as a number of scalar fields contained in
g_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}
and
B_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}
. These can be combined into fields that transform simply under the
O(d,d)
symmetry: an
O(d,d)
vector

\mathcal{A}_{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A^{(1)m^{\prime}}\\
A^{(2)}_{m^{\prime}}\end{array}\right)

(2.4)

and a symmetric
O(d,d)
matrix, sometimes called the generalized metric,

\mathcal{H}_{MN}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g^{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}&-g^{m^{\prime}k^{\prime}}B_{k^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\\
B_{m^{\prime}k^{\prime}}g^{k^{\prime}n^{\prime}}&g_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}-B_{m^{\prime}k^{\prime}}g^{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}B_{l^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\end{array}\right)\,.

(2.5)

What will be important for us is that from two
\mathcal{A}
s we can form precisely two
O(d,d)
scalars, namely

\mathcal{A}_{M}\eta^{MN}\mathcal{A}_{N}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathcal{A}_{M}\mathcal{H}^{MN}\mathcal{A}_{N}\,,

(2.6)

where
\mathcal{H}^{MN}=\eta^{MK}\mathcal{H}_{KL}\eta^{LN}
and we introduced the
O(d,d)
invariant metric

\eta^{MN}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\delta^{m^{\prime}}{}_{n^{\prime}}\\
\delta_{m^{\prime}}{}^{n^{\prime}}&0\end{array}\right)\,.

(2.7)

In the following we will simply set the scalars that arise in the dimensional reduction to zero (this is consistent since we only work up to quadratic order in the KK vectors
[
24
]
). In this case the two
O(d,d)
scalars constructed from the KK vectors become

A_{(m}^{(1)m^{\prime}}A^{(2)}_{n)m^{\prime}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad A_{m}^{(1)m^{\prime}}A^{(1)}_{nm^{\prime}}+A_{m}^{(2)m^{\prime}}A^{(2)}_{nm^{\prime}}\,.

(2.8)

We now define the combinations

A=-\frac{1}{2}(A^{(1)}+A^{(2)})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\hat{A}=\frac{1}{2}(A^{(1)}-A^{(2)})\,.

(2.9)

In terms of these the two
O(d,d)
invariants are

A_{m}\cdot A_{n}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\hat{A}_{m}\cdot\hat{A}_{n}\,,

(2.10)

where the
\cdot
denotes contraction of the internal index. Conversely, the inner product between the two vectors,

A_{m}\cdot\hat{A}_{n}\,,

(2.11)

explicitly
violates

Appendix A
Useful identities

The torsionful Riemann tensor satisfies the following useful identities

\nabla^{(+)}_{[d}\mathcal{R}^{ef}{}_{ef]}=-H_{[de}{}^{g}\mathcal{R}^{ef}{}_{f]g}\,,\qquad\nabla^{(-)[d}\mathcal{R}^{ef]}{}_{ef}=H^{[de}{}_{g}\mathcal{R}^{f]g}{}_{ef}\,,

(A.1)

\begin{split}\nabla^{(-)[b}\nabla^{(+)}_{e}\hat{F}^{cd]}&=-\mathcal{R}^{[bc}{}_{eg}\hat{F}^{d]g}+H^{[bc}{}_{g}\nabla^{(+)}_{e}\hat{F}^{d]g}\,,\\
\nabla^{(+)}_{[b}\nabla^{(-)e}F_{cd]}&=-\mathcal{R}^{eg}{}_{[bc}F_{d]g}-H_{[bc}{}^{g}\nabla^{(-)e}F_{d]g}\,,\end{split}

(A.2)

and

(\nabla^{(-)}_{c}-2\partial_{c}\Phi)\mathcal{R}^{bc}{}_{ef}+H^{b}{}_{cd}\mathcal{R}^{cd}{}_{ef}=-2\nabla^{(+)}_{[e}(\mathcal{R}^{b}{}_{f]}+2\nabla^{(-)b}\partial_{f]}\Phi)+H_{cef}(\mathcal{R}^{bc}+2\nabla^{(-)b}\partial^{c}\Phi)\,,

(A.3)

where the RHS is proportional to the lowest order equations of motion since

\mathcal{R}_{ab}+2\nabla^{(-)}_{a}\partial_{b}\Phi=\mathbbm{G}_{ab}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbbm{B}_{ab}\,.

(A.4)

The lowest order equations of motion are
\mathbbm{G}_{ab}=0
and
\mathbbm{B}_{ab}=0
with

\mathbbm{B}_{ab}=(\nabla^{c}-2\partial^{c}\Phi)H_{abc}\,,\qquad\mathbbm{G}_{ab}=R_{ab}+2\nabla_{a}\partial_{b}\Phi-\frac{1}{4}H^{2}_{ab}

(A.5)

and in the reduced theory we also have the equations of motion of the KK vectors
\mathbbm{A}_{a}=0
and
\hat{\mathbbm{A}}_{a}=0
with

\mathbbm{A}^{b}_{a^{\prime}}=(\nabla^{(-)}_{a}-2\partial_{a}\Phi)F^{ab}_{a^{\prime}}\,,\qquad\hat{\mathbbm{A}}^{b}_{a^{\prime}}=(\nabla^{(+)}_{a}-2\partial_{a}\Phi)\hat{F}^{ab}_{a^{\prime}}\,.

(A.6)

We did not introduce a notation for the dilaton equation of motion since it does not enter our calculations.

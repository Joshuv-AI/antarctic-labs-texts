Reactive Hall and Edelstein effects in a tight-binding model with spin-orbit coupling
Emmanouil K. Kokkinis 1 , Joseph J. Betouras 2 , and Xenophon Zotos 3,4

Abstract
The reactive Hall constant R H R_{H} , described by reactive (non-dissipative) conductivities, is analyzed within linear response theory in the presence of spin-orbit interaction. Within a two dimensional tight-binding model the effect of van Hove singularities is studied. Along the same line a formulation of the Edelstein constant is proposed and studied as a function of coupling parameters and fermion filling.

III
Reactive Hall response

We will analyze the reactive Hall response within
standard linear response theory,

\displaystyle\langle j^{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{j^{x}j^{x}}E^{x}(t)+\sigma_{j^{x}j^{y}_{q}}E^{y}(t)

\displaystyle\langle j^{y}_{q}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{j^{y}_{q}j^{x}}E^{x}(t)+\sigma_{j^{y}_{q}j^{y}_{q}}E^{y}(t)

closely following the development in
znlp
.

Reactive - nondissipative - response occurs in a generic uniform
(without disorder) interacting
system at zero temperature, in which case the brackets

\langle...\rangle
denote ground state average (
znlp
)
or at finite temperatures in a uniform noninteracting system
in which case the brackets
\langle...\rangle
denote thermal average.
Here we study the reactive Hall constant
for the Hamiltonian (
1
) at finite temperatures
in the presence of a magnetic field, with the conductivities
given by,

\displaystyle\sigma_{j^{\alpha}j^{\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{z}(\langle\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\phi^{\alpha}\partial\phi^{\beta}}\rangle-\chi_{j^{\alpha}j^{\beta}}),

\displaystyle\chi_{AB}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int^{\infty}_{0}dte^{izt}\langle[A(t),B]\rangle.

In contrast to the usual derivation of the Hall constant expression,

q-

current-current to current-density correlations using the continuity equation:

\displaystyle\langle j^{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{j^{x}j^{x}}E^{x}(t)+\frac{1}{q}\chi_{j^{x}n_{q}}E^{y}(t)

\displaystyle\langle j^{y}_{q}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{q}\chi_{n_{q}j^{x}}E^{x}(t)+(\frac{z}{q})^{2}\chi_{n_{q}n_{q}}\frac{i}{z}E^{y}(t)~,

with
n_{q}=\sum_{l,m}(-ie^{iqm})c^{\dagger}_{l,m}\cdot c_{l,m}
.

At this point we consider the “screening” (or slow)

{\hat{y}}-

(q,\omega)

q\rightarrow 0

H(\lambda,\mu)

\frac{\partial^{2}E_{n}}{\partial\mu\partial\lambda}=\langle n|\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\mu\partial\lambda}|n\rangle-\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\langle n|\frac{\partial H}{\partial\mu}|m\rangle\langle m|\frac{\partial H}{\partial\lambda}|n\rangle+H.c.}{E_{m}-E_{n}}

(2)

we arrive at,

R_{H}=-\frac{1}{D}\frac{\partial D}{\partial\rho},

(3)

\rho

The Drude weight

To evaluate the eigenvalues/eigenstates we diagonalize

1

\phi_{x}

{\bf k}=(k_{x},k_{y})

\begin{vmatrix}\epsilon^{(0)}_{\bf k}-\epsilon&\Delta_{\bf k}e^{+i\theta_{\bf k}}\\
\Delta_{\bf k}e^{-i\theta_{\bf k}}&\epsilon^{(0)}_{\bf k}-\epsilon\end{vmatrix}=0

\epsilon^{(0)}_{\bf k}(\phi_{x})=-2t_{x}\cos(k_{x}+\phi_{x})-2t_{y}\cos k_{y}.

\Delta_{\bf k}=2\sqrt{\lambda_{x}^{2}\sin^{2}(k_{x}+\phi_{x})+\lambda_{y}^{2}\sin^{2}k_{y}}

\theta_{\bf k}=\tan^{-1}\frac{\lambda_{x}\sin k_{x}}{\lambda_{y}\sin k_{y}}.

We obtain

H=\sum_{{\bf k}\pm}\epsilon_{{\bf k}\pm}c^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}\pm}c_{{\bf k}\pm}

with the two spin states turning by the spin-orbit interaction
to two chirality eigenstates

c_{{\bf k}\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(c_{{\bf k}\uparrow}\pm e^{-i\phi_{\bf k}}c_{{\bf k}\downarrow})

and eigenvalues,

\epsilon_{{\bf k}\pm}(\phi_{x})=\epsilon_{k}(\phi_{x})\pm\Delta_{\bf k}.

Finally the reactive Hall constant is given by,

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\pm}\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi}\frac{dk_{x}}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi}\frac{dk_{y}}{2\pi}f_{{\bf k}\pm}\frac{\partial^{2}\epsilon_{{\bf k}\pm}}{\partial\phi_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle f_{{\bf k}\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta(\epsilon_{{\bf k}\pm}-\mu)}}.

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\Im\alpha_{yx}(\omega)

V
Conclusions

In this work we study the effect of spin-orbit interaction
on the reactive Hall constant
R_{H}
within a noninteracting
fermion Hamiltonian model.
In particular we show the role of van Hove singularities in
the density of states as cusps in the density dependence of
R_{H}
.
Furthermore, we introduce the concept of reactive Edelstein constant,
an effect in spin-orbit coupled systems and derive an expression analogous to
the Kohn formula for the reactive response.

The main open question is the effect of interactions, introduced
e.g. by a Hubbard
U
term in the Hamiltonian.
An emerging Mott gap, typically at half filling, leads to a vanishing
D

as Kohn pointed out, the criterion for a
Mott metal-insulator transition.
Thus the interaction effects in combination with the presence of (higher-order)
van Hove singularities can have drastic consequence on
phase formation
Zervou_Efremov_Betouras_2023
.
At first, this can be studied by a mean field approach
mf1
;
mf2

which essentially leads to effective single particle states allowing
a direct computation of
R_{H}
and
D_{E}
.
Next, the interaction effects can be studied by
either numerical simulations or many-body theory techniques

Zervou_Efremov_Betouras_2023
.
An interesting question is the temperature dependence of the Edelstein
constant. While it is known that the Drude weight vanishes at any
finite temperature in an interacting system evidence of dissipation due
to interactions, the temperature dependence of
D_{E}
is not clear.

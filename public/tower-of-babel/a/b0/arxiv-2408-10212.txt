Abstract
This paper introduces nonlinear fractional Lane-Emden equations of the form, D α ​ y ​ ( x ) + λ x β ​ D β ​ y ​ ( x ) + f ⁡ ( y ) = 0 , 1 < α ≤ 2 , 0 < β ≤ 1 , 0 < x < 1 , D^{\alpha}y(x)+\frac{\lambda}{x^{\beta}}~D^{\beta}y(x)+f(y)=0,~~1<\alpha\leq 2,~~0<\beta\leq 1,~~0<x<1, subject to boundary conditions, y ′ ​ ( 0 ) = 𝐚 , 𝐜 ​ y ′ ​ ( 1 ) + 𝐝 ​ y ​ ( 1 ) = 𝐛 , y^{\prime}(0)=\mathbf{a},~~~\mathbf{c}~y^{\prime}(1)+\mathbf{d}~y(1)=\mathbf{b}, where, D α , D β D^{\alpha},D^{\beta} represent Caputo fractional derivative, 𝐚 , 𝐛 , 𝐜 , 𝐝 ∈ ℝ \mathbf{a,b,c,d}\in\mathbb{R} , λ = 1 , 2 \lambda=1,2 , and f ⁡ ( y ) f(y) is non linear function of y . y. 2.2
Some Definitions

Several definitions of fractional derivatives are available. In this work, we use the following two versions.

Riemann-Liouville Fractional Integral
[
11
,
20
,
16
]
:
The Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of a function

is given as,

I^{\nu}\psi(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma{(\nu)}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\nu-1}\psi(\tau)d\tau,~~~(m-1)<\nu\leq m,~~~m=\lceil\nu\rceil.

(18)

Caputo Fractional Derivative
[
20
,
17
]
:
The Caputo fractional derivative of order
\nu>0,
of a function
\phi(t)
is defined as,

D^{\nu}\phi(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(m-\nu)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{m-\nu-1}\phi^{m}(\tau)d\tau,~~(m-1)<\nu\leq m,~~~m=\lceil\nu\rceil.

(19)

In what follows we recall some important Lemmas from
[
20
]
, that will be used in this paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
\psi(\tau)=\tau^{\nu},
then,

I^{\gamma}\psi(\tau)=\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)}{\Gamma(\nu+\gamma+1)}\tau^{\gamma+\nu}.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\psi(\tau)=c,
where
c
is constant and
\gamma>0,
then,
D^{\gamma}\psi(\tau)=0.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\gamma>0,n=\lceil\gamma\rceil
and
\phi\in AC^{n}[a,b],
then,

I^{\gamma}~D^{\gamma}\phi(\zeta)=\phi(\zeta)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\zeta^{k}}{k!}\phi^{(k)}(0).

3.1
Algorithm for UFHWCM

Here, we outline the steps for our method to solve fractional Lane-Emden equations (
10
). The procedure involves the following key steps;

Step 1(Quasilinearization):
Transform the nonlinear Lane-Emden equations (
10
) into a sequence of linear Lane-Emden equations (
22
) using the quasilinearization scheme.

Step 2(Haar wavelet collocation):
Utilize the Haar wavelet collocation method, incorporating the boundary conditions into the sequence of linear Lane-Emden equations.

Step 3(Substitution and replacement):
We insert equations (
24
), (
28
), and (
29
) into equation (
22
), and we replace the variable

with the collocation points defined in (
17
).

Step 4(Solve linear system):
Following step
3
, we obtain a system of linear algebraic equations. These equations can be solved by initiating with an initial guess

to determine the wavelet coefficients.

Step 5(First approximate solution):
We substitute the obtained wavelet coefficients into (

Step 6(Iterative refinement):
Continue steps

for
r

4.1
Convergence Analysis

To prove the convergence, we require the following result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
r,s>0
and
1<\alpha\leq 2,
then,

r^{\alpha}-s^{\alpha}\leq(r-s)^{\alpha}.

Proof.

Let
\frac{s}{r}=x,
then

(r-s)^{\alpha}+s^{\alpha}=r^{\alpha}\Big[\Big(1-\frac{s}{r}\Big)^{\alpha}+\Big(\frac{s}{r}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big]=r^{\alpha}f(x).

(31)

Since,
f(x)
is continuous and for
1<\alpha\leq 2,
then,
\min_{x\in[0,1]}f(x)=1
.
Hence

r^{\alpha}\Big[\Big(1-\frac{s}{r}\Big)^{\alpha}+\Big(\frac{s}{r}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big]\geq r^{\alpha}\min_{x\in[0,1]}f(x)\geq r^{\alpha}\times 1=r^{\alpha}.\\

Thus,

r^{\alpha}-s^{\alpha}\leq(r-s)^{\alpha}.

This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\hskip 213.39566pt\Big(I^{\alpha}h_{2^{s}+t+1}(x)-\frac{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}I^{\alpha-1}h_{2^{s}+t+1}(1)-I^{\alpha}h_{2^{s}+t+1}(1)\Big)dx,

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{j}-1}\sum_{s=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{t=0}^{2^{s}-1}a_{2^{j}+k+1}a_{2^{s}+t+1}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(p_{\alpha,2^{j}+k+1}(x)-\frac{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}p_{\alpha-1,2^{j}+k+1}(1)-p_{\alpha,2^{j}+k+1}(1)\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 213.39566pt\Big(p_{\alpha,2^{s}+t+1}(x)--\frac{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}p_{\alpha-1,2^{s}+t+1}(1)-p_{\alpha,2^{s}+t+1}(1)\Big)dx.

(32)

Now since,
D^{\alpha}y(x)=\sum_{\varrho=1}^{\infty}a_{\varrho}h_{\varrho}(x)
then, by using the definition of uniform Haar function, we can evaluate coefficients
a_{\varrho}
as,

\displaystyle a_{\varrho}

\displaystyle=2^{j}\int_{0}^{1}D^{\alpha}y(x)h_{\varrho}(x)dx,

\displaystyle a_{\varrho}

\displaystyle=2^{j}\Big(\int_{\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho)}^{\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho)}D^{\alpha}y(x)dx-\int_{\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho)}^{\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho)}D^{\alpha}y(x)dx\Big).

Since,
D^{\alpha}y(x)
be continuous on
[0,1],
so we have,

a_{\varrho}=2^{j}\{(\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho)-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho))D^{\alpha}y(\xi_{1})-(\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho)-\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho))D^{\alpha}y(\xi_{2})\},

where
\xi_{1}\in(\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho),\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho))~\&~~\xi_{2}\in(\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho),\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho)),
then,

\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho)-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho)=\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho)-\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho)=\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}.

So we get,

\displaystyle a_{\varrho}

\displaystyle=2^{j}\Big\{\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}D^{\alpha}y(\xi_{1})-\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}D^{\alpha}y(\xi_{2})\Big\},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\{D^{\alpha}y(\xi_{1})-D^{\alpha}y(\xi_{2})\},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})D^{\alpha+1}y(\xi),~~\xi\in(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}).

Since,
D^{\alpha+1}y(x)
is bounded such that
|D^{\alpha+1}y(x)|\leq\epsilon,
which implies,
a_{\varrho}\leq\epsilon~\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}\Big).
Since, the fractional integral of uniform Haar wavelet is given in (
20
) and (
21
), we first derive the bounds in each of the intervals,
[\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho),\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho)),~~[\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho),\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho))

\&~~\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho)\leq x<1.
Now for,
x\in[\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho),\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho))
, i.e.,
\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho)\leq x<\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho),
we have,

p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)=\frac{(x-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho))^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}.

Here, since,
p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)
is monotonically increasing. So,

\displaystyle 0\leq p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)

\displaystyle\leq p_{\alpha,\varrho}(\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho)),

\displaystyle|p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho)-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho))^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)},

\displaystyle|p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}\Big)^{\alpha}.

Now, since
p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)
is continuous on compact interval
[\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho),\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho)],
so, the function
p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)
attains it’s global maximum in this interval. Since,

\displaystyle p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\{(x-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho))^{\alpha}-2(x-\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho))^{\alpha}\},~~~~~~~~\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho)\leq x\leq\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho).

Then,

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\{\alpha(x-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho))^{\alpha-1}-2\alpha(x-\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho))^{\alpha-1}\},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\{(x-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho))^{\alpha-1}-2(x-\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho))^{\alpha-1}\}.

So,
p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)
will be monotonically increasing if

p^{\prime}_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)\geq 0,

after simplification, we get,

x\leq\frac{2^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho)-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho)}{2^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}-1}.

Thus
p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)
is monotonically increasing if
x\leq\frac{2^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho)-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho)}{2^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}-1}
in
[\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho),\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho)].
By using the method of contradiction, we can easily prove that,

\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho)\leq\frac{2^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho)-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho)}{2^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}-1}.

Therefore, we have,

0\leq p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)\leq p_{\alpha,\varrho}(\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho)),~~~~~~\forall x\in[\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho),\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho)],

and,

\displaystyle|p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\{(\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho)-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho))^{\alpha}-2(\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho)-\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho))^{\alpha}\},

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{2^{\alpha}-2}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}\Big)^{\alpha},~~~~~~\forall x\in[\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho),\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho)].

For
1>x\geq\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho),
we have,

p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma{(\alpha+1)}}\Big[(x-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho))^{\alpha}-2(x-\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho))^{\alpha}+(x-\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho))^{\alpha}\Big].

By using Lemma
4.1
, we get,

\displaystyle p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma{(\alpha+1)}}\Big[(x-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho))^{\alpha}-2(x-\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho))^{\alpha}+(x-\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho))^{\alpha}\Big],

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma{(\alpha+1)}}\Big[(x-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho))^{\alpha}-(x-\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho))^{\alpha}+(x-\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho))^{\alpha}-(x-\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho))^{\alpha}\Big],

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma{(\alpha+1)}}\Big[\{\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho)-\Upsilon_{1}(\varrho)\}^{\alpha}+\{\Upsilon_{2}(\varrho)-\Upsilon_{3}(\varrho)\}^{\alpha}\big],

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma{(\alpha+1)}}\Big[\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}\Big)^{\alpha}+\Big(\frac{-1}{2^{j+1}}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big],

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma{(\alpha+1)}}(1+(-1)^{\alpha})\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}\Big)^{\alpha}.

Hence, we have,

|p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)|\leq k\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}\Big)^{\alpha},~~\forall x\in[0,1].

From the above, we deduce the following,

|p_{\alpha-1,\varrho}(x)|\leq k\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}\Big)^{\alpha-1},~~\forall x\in[0,1].

Then,

\displaystyle|p_{\alpha,\varrho}(1)|\leq k\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}\Big)^{\alpha}.

\displaystyle|p_{\alpha-1,\varrho}(1)|\leq k\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}\Big)^{\alpha-1}.

Now, using inequality,

\Big(p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)-\frac{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}p_{\alpha-1,\varrho}(1)-p_{\alpha,\varrho}(1)\Big)\leq~|p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)|+\frac{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}|p_{\alpha-1,\varrho}(1)|+|p_{\alpha,\varrho}(1)|,

(33)

we get,

\Big(p_{\alpha,\varrho}(x)-\frac{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}p_{\alpha-1,\varrho}(1)-p_{\alpha,\varrho}(1)\Big)\leq k\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}\Big)^{\alpha-1}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j}}+\frac{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}\Big),~~~~~\forall x\in[0,1].

Now, since the Haar function satisfies the orthogonal property, so we have,

\displaystyle a_{2^{j}+k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}\Big),

\displaystyle a_{2^{s}+t+1}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\Big(\frac{1}{2^{s+1}}\Big).

So, (
32
) reduces to,

\displaystyle\|E_{M}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq k^{2}\epsilon^{2}\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{j}-1}\sum_{s=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{t=0}^{2^{s}-1}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{2^{s+1}}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j+1}}\Big)^{\alpha-1}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j}}+\frac{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{2^{s+1}}\Big)^{\alpha-1}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{s}}+\frac{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}\Big),

\displaystyle=k^{2}\epsilon^{2}\Big\{\Big(\frac{1}{2^{J+2}}\Big)^{\alpha}\Big(\frac{2^{\alpha}}{2^{\alpha}-1}\Big)+\frac{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{J+1}}\Big)^{\alpha-1}\frac{1}{2(2^{\alpha-1}-1}\Big\}^{2}.

Hence we arrive at,

\|E_{M}\|_{2}\leq k\epsilon\Big(\frac{1}{2^{J+1}}\Big)^{\alpha-1}\Big\{\Big(\frac{1}{2^{J+1}}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\alpha}-1}\Big)+\frac{\mathbf{c}}{2\mathbf{d}}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{\alpha-1}-1}\Big)\Big\},

(34)

with
M=2^{J}
and
J
denoting the highest resolution level, the equation (
34
) ensures the convergence of UFHWCM.

\displaystyle TV=B,

(35)

\kappa(T)

.

[
13
]

T

, exists for all sufficiently large values of
M(M>\mathcal{M}_{0})
.

There exists a constant
\mathcal{C}
, independent of
M
, such that the norm of the inverse matrix,
||T^{-1}||
, is bounded by
\mathcal{C}
for all
M>\mathcal{M}_{0}
.

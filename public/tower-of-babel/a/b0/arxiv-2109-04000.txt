There does not exist a strongly regular graph with parameters ( 1911,270,105 , 27 ) (1911,270,105,27)
Jack H. Koolen, Brhane Gebremichel

Abstract
In this paper we show that there does not exist a strongly regular graph with parameters ( 1911,270,105 , 27 ) (1911,270,105,27) .

Preliminaries

2.1
Graphs

In this paper all the graphs are finite, undirected and simple. For definitions, we do not define, see
[
2
]
. The eigenvalues of a graph are the eigenvalues of its adjacency matrix
A(G)
indexed by
V(G)
, such that
A_{xy}=1
if
xy\in E(G)
and
0
otherwise. The smallest eigenvalue of a graph is denoted by
\lambda_{\min}(G)
.

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a graph. The valency
k_{x}
of a vertex
x
of
G
is the number of neighbours of
x
, i.e. the vertices
y\in V(G)
such that
xy\in E(G)
. A graph
G
is
k
-regular if
k_{x}=k
for all vertices
x\in V(G)
. A graph
G
is strongly regular with parameters
(n,k,\lambda,\mu)
if
G
has
n
vertices, is
k
-regular and any two distinct vertices have exactly
\lambda
(resp.
\mu
) common neighbours if they are adjacent (resp. non-adjacent). The strongly regular graph
G
is called primitive if
G
and its complement are both connected.

2.2
Interlacing

If
M
(resp.
N
) is a real symmetric
m\times m
(resp.
n\times n
) matrix with
\theta_{1}(M)\geqslant\theta_{2}(M)\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\theta_{m}(M)
(resp.
\theta_{1}(N)\geqslant\theta_{2}(N)\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\theta_{n}(N)
) the eigenvalues of
M
(resp.
N
) in nonincreasing order. Assume
m\leqslant n
. Then we say that the eigenvalues of
M

interlace
the eigenvalues of
N
, if
\theta_{n-m+i}(N)\leqslant\theta_{i}(M)\leqslant\theta_{i}(N)
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.

The following result is a special case of interlacing.

Lemma 2.1

(cf.
[
5
, Theorem 9.1.1]
)
.

Let
B
be a real symmetric
n\times n
matrix and
C
be a principal submatrix of
B
of order
m
, where
m<n
. Then the eigenvalues of
C
interlace the eigenvalues of
B
.

As an easy consequence of Lemma
2.1
, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a graph and
H
a proper induced subgraph of
G
. Denote by
\theta_{\min}(G)
(resp.
\theta_{\min}(H)
) the smallest eigenvalue of
G
(resp.
H
). Then
\theta_{\min}(G)\leqslant\theta_{\min}(H)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
\pi:=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}\}
be a partition of
V
. We say
\pi
is an
equitable partition
with respect to
G
if the number of neighbours in
V_{j}
of a vertex
u
in
V_{i}
is a constant
q_{ij}
, independent of
u
, only dependent on
i
and
j
. For an equitable partition
\pi
with respect to
G
, the quotient matrix
Q
of
G
with respect to
\pi
is defined as
Q=(q_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant r}
.

Lemma 2.3

(cf.
[
5
, Theorem 9.3.3]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph. If
\pi
is an equitable partition of
G
and
Q
is the quotient matrix with respect to
\pi
of
G
, then every eigenvalue of
Q
is an eigenvalue of
G
.

2.3
Terwilliger graphs

A
Terwilliger graph
is a non-complete graph such that, for any two vertices
x
and
y
at distance
2
, the subgraph induced by common neighbours of
x
and
y
forms a clique of order
c
(for some fixed
c\geqslant 0
).

Lemma 2.4

(cf.
[
1
, Corollary 1.16.6 (ii)]
)
.

There is no strongly regular Terwilliger graph with parameters
(n,k,\lambda,\mu)
for
k<50(\mu-1)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

If a strongly regular graph with parameters
(1911,270,105,27)
exist, then it contains an induced quadrangle.

Proof
.

Suppose there exist a strongly regular graph
G
with parameters
(1911,270,105,27)
which does not contain an induced quadrangles. Then
G
is a Terwilliger graph. As
\mu=27
then, by Lemma
2.4
, the valency of
G
is at least
1300
, which is a contradiction as
k=270
. This shows the lemma.
∎

2.4
Join of graphs

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two graphs such that
V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2})=\emptyset
. The
join
of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, denoted by
G_{1}\nabla G_{2}
, has as vertex set
V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
and edge set
E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})\cup\{\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\mid x_{1}\in V(G_{1}),x_{2}\in V(G_{2})\}
. The following lemma is a consequence of Section
2.3.1
of
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G_{i}
be a
k_{i}
-regular graph with
n_{i}
vertices, for
i=1,2
, such that
V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2})=\emptyset
. Then the smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}(G_{1}\nabla G_{2})
of the join
G_{1}\nabla G_{2}
satisfies

\lambda_{\min}=\min\{\lambda_{\min}(G_{1}),\lambda_{\min}(G_{2}),\lambda_{\min}(Q)\}

where

\displaystyle Q=\begin{pmatrix}k_{1}&n_{2}\\
n_{1}&k_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

The following lemma was inspired by Cao, Koolen, Munemasa, Yoshino
[
3
]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be a
k
-regular graph on
n
vertices with smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{\min}(G)\leqslant-1
. Consider
K_{t}\nabla G
for some positive integer
t
. Then
\lambda_{\min}(K_{t}\nabla G)=\lambda_{\min}(G)
if and only if
(\lambda_{\min}(G)-k)(\lambda_{\min}(G)+1-t)\geqslant nt
.

Proof
.

As
\lambda_{\min}(K_{t})\geqslant-1
, by Lemma
2.6
we find
\lambda_{\min}(G)=\lambda_{\min}(K_{t}\nabla G)
if and only if

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}\begin{pmatrix}t-1&n\\
t&k\end{pmatrix}\geqslant\lambda_{\min}(G)

if and only if

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}t-1-\lambda_{\min}(G)&n\\
t&k-\lambda_{\min}(G)\end{pmatrix}\geqslant 0,\text{ as }t\geqslant 1.

This show the lemma.
∎

Let

Using Lemma,

Lemma 3.3

(cf.
[
6

)
.

Let
G
be a strongly regular graph with parameters
(v,k,\lambda,\mu)
having smallest eigenvalue
-m
. Let
C
be a maximal clique of
G
of order
c
. If
\mu>m(m-1)
and
c>\frac{{\mu}^{2}}{\mu-m(m-1)}-m+1
, then

(3)

((c+m-3)(k-c+1)-2(c-1)(\lambda-c+2))^{2}-(k-c+1)^{2}(c+m-1)(c-(m-1)(4m-1))\geqslant 0.

We denote the polynomial on the left hand side of the inequality (
3
) by
M_{G}(c)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

If a strongly regular graph
G
with parameters
(1911,270,105,27)
exists, then any clique in
G
has order at most
32
.

Proof
.

Let
G
be a strongly regular graph with parameters
(1911,270,105,27)
. Then, it has smallest eigenvalue
-3
. Let
C
be a maximal clique in
G
of order
c
. If
c>\frac{27^{2}}{27-6}-3+1=32\frac{5}{7}
, then by Lemma
3.4
, we have

M_{G}(c)=672c^{3}-80784c^{2}+1468512c+3277200\geq 0

as
\mu=27>6
. It is easily checked that
M_{G}(26)<0
and
M_{G}(97)<0
. This means that
c\geqslant 98
. This gives a contradiction, as the Delsarte bound gives
c\leqslant 1+\frac{k}{m}=1+\frac{270}{3}=91
. So we obtain that any clique has order at most
32
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G
be strongly regular graph with parameters
(1911,270,105,27)
. Assume there are two cliques
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that
V(C_{1})\neq V(C_{2})
, each of order at least
29
, intersecting in at least
22
vertices. Then one of the following holds:

(1)

There is at least one vertex
z
in the symmetric difference
V(C_{1})\triangledown V(C_{2})
which is adjacent to all vertices in
V(C_{1})\triangledown V(C_{2})\backslash\{z\}
,

(2)

C_{1}
and
C_{2}
intersect in exactly
27
vertices and both are maximal with order
29
.

Proof
.

Let
H
be the induced subgraph on
V(C_{1})\triangledown V(C_{2})
. If
H
is complete, then we are in Case (1). So we may assume
H
is not complete. This means that
|V(C_{1})\cap V(C_{2})|=:t\in\{22,23,\ldots,27\}
as
\mu=27
.

Assume
t=22
. Let
C^{\prime}_{1}
(resp.
C^{\prime}_{2}
) be a sub clique of
C_{1}
(resp.
C_{2}
) such that
V(C^{\prime}_{1})\supseteq V(C_{1})\cap V(C_{2})
,
V(C^{\prime}_{2})\supseteq V(C_{1})\cap V(C_{2})
and
|V(C^{\prime}_{1})|=|V(C^{\prime}_{2})|=29
. Let
K
be the induced subgraph on
V(C^{\prime}_{1})\cup V(C^{\prime}_{2})
. By Proposition
2.1
we see that
K
has smallest eigenvalue at least
-3
. Let
\pi=\{V(C^{\prime}_{1})\cap V(C^{\prime}_{2}),V(C^{\prime}_{1})\triangledown V(C^{\prime}_{2})\}
of
V(C^{\prime}_{1})\cup V(C^{\prime}_{2})
be a partition of

with quotient matrix

\displaystyle Q=\begin{pmatrix}21&14\\
22&\alpha+6\end{pmatrix}.

By Lemma
2.3
, we see that the smallest eigenvalue of
Q
is at least
-3
. This implies that
24\alpha\geqslant 92
, as
\det(Q+3\mathbf{I})\geqslant 0
. So,
\alpha\geqslant\frac{23}{6}
. This means that there are at least
\lceil\frac{7\times 23}{6}\rceil=27
edges between
V(C^{\prime}_{1})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{2})
and
V(C^{\prime}_{2})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{1})
. If Case (1) of the lemma does not happen, then all vertices
V(C^{\prime}_{1})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{2})
have at most 5 neighbours in
V(C^{\prime}_{2})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{1})
, as
\mu=27
.

We need to consider two cases. There exist a vertex
x\in V(C^{\prime}_{1})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{2})
such that
x
has
5
neighbours in
V(C^{\prime}_{2})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{1})
or there is no vertex
x\in V(C^{\prime}_{1})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{2})
with
5
neighbours in
V(C^{\prime}_{2})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{1})
.

In the first case, let
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{5}
be the
5
neighbours of

in
V(C^{\prime}_{2})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{1})
. Then
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{5}
have each at most
5
neighbours in
V(C^{\prime}_{1})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{2})
and hence there is an edge
zu
between
V(C^{\prime}_{1})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{2})
and
V(C^{\prime}_{2})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{1})
, such that
z\in(V(C^{\prime}_{1})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{2}))\backslash\{x\}
and
u\in(V(C^{\prime}_{2})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{1}))\backslash\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{5}\}
. Then
u
and
x
are at distance two and they have at least
28
common neighbours, a contradiction. Now assume that all vertices of
V(C^{\prime}_{1})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{2})
(resp.
V(C^{\prime}_{2})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{1})
) have at most 4 neighbours in
V(C^{\prime}_{2})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{1})
(resp.
V(C^{\prime}_{1})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{2})
). As there are at least
27
edges between
V(C^{\prime}_{1})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{2})
and
V(C^{\prime}_{2})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{1})
, then there exist
x\in V(C^{\prime}_{1})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{2})
and
y\in V(C^{\prime}_{2})\backslash V(C^{\prime}_{1})
such that
x
and
y
are at distance two and they have at least

common neighbours, a contradiction. This shows that, if

, then we are in Case (1) of the lemma.

In similar fashion, it can be shown that, if
t\in\{23,23,\ldots,26\}
, then we are in Case (1) of the lemma.

Now assume

. If
|V(C_{1})|\geqslant 30
and
|V(C_{2})|\geqslant 29
, then the quotient matrix

of

This means

and hence

But, as

On the local graph of
G

For a graph
G
and

let
\Delta(x)
be the induced subgraph on
\{y\in V(G)\mid x\sim y\}
. The graph
\Delta(x)

G
with respect to

.

Lemma 4.1

(cf.
[
4
,
7
]
)
.

Let
G
be a primitive strongly regular graph with parameters
(v,k,\lambda,\mu)
. Let
x
be a vertex of
G
and
\Delta(x)
be the local graph of
G
with respect to
x
. Let
\bar{C}=\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{c}\}
be an independent set of
\Delta(x)
of order

. Then

(4)

\binom{\bar{c}}{2}(\mu-1)\geqslant\bar{c}(\lambda+1)-k

holds.

For distinct non-adjacent vertices

define
C(w_{1},w_{2}):=\{z\sim x\mid z\sim w_{1},z\sim w_{2}\}
and

.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume a strongly regular graph
G
with parameters

. Then there is no independent set

.

.

Assume that
\Delta(x)
contains an independent set

of order

. Then by Lemma

we have
260=\binom{5}{2}26=5\times 106-270=260
. So we have equality in (

). This means that

in
\Delta(x)
for all

. As
C(u,v)\leqslant 25
in
\Delta(x)
, we obtain that
u
and

are not both elements in an independent set

of order

in
\Delta(x)
. This shows the lemma
∎

and for the other
i
’s in
\{2,3,\ldots,6\}
we have
c(a_{2i-1},a_{2i})=26
.

If
c(u,v)=25
and
\sum_{\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\in\binom{U}{2}}c(u_{1},u_{2})=155
, then
c(a_{2i-1},a_{2i})=26
for all
i=2,3,\ldots,6
and there exists a unique vertex
z
of
\Delta(x)
such that
z
has exactly
3
neighbours in
U
. Any other vertex
w
of
\Delta(x)
has at most
2
neighbours in

. This shows the lemma.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
4.3
we have the following.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume a strongly regular graph
G
exists with parameters
(1911,270,105,27)
such that
G
has an induced quadrangle, say
x\sim u\sim y\sim v\sim x
. Let
W:=\{w\sim x\mid w\neq u,w\neq v,w\not\sim u,w\not\sim v\}
and let
Z:=\{z\in W\mid\text{there exists a vertex }z^{\prime}\in W\backslash\{z\}\text{ such that }z^{\prime}\not\sim z\}
. Let
\Gamma_{W}
be the subgraph induced on
W
. For
w\in W
, let
k_{w}
be the valency of
w
in
\Gamma_{W}
. For
z\in Z
, let
K_{z}
(resp.
\tilde{K}_{z}
) be the subgraph induced on
\{w\in W\mid w\neq z,w\not\sim z\}
(resp.
\{w\in W\mid w\neq z,w\not\sim z\}\cup\{x\}
). Then the following hold:

(1)

The graph
K_{z}
and
\tilde{K}_{z}
are complete and
K_{z}
has at least
28
vertices,

(2)

|W|\in\{82,83\}
,

(3)

For
w\in W
we have
k_{w}\in\{53,54,|W|-1\}
. Moreover, if
|W|=82
, then
k_{w}\in\{53,|W|-1\}
and any two distinct non-adjacent vertices
z,z^{\prime}\in Z
have
c(z,z^{\prime})=26
.

Proof
.

(1): If
K_{z}
is not complete, then there would be an independent set
U
of order
5
containing
u
and
v
, a contradiction with Lemma
4.1
. As
K_{z}
is a subgraph of
\Delta(x)
, it is clear that
\tilde{K}_{z}
is complete as well. We will show later that
K_{z}
has at least
28
vertices.

(2): We have
|W|=k-2(\lambda+1)+c(u,v)
. As
k=270
,
\lambda=105
and
c(u,v)\in\{24,25\}
, we find
|W|\in\{82,83\}
. This shows (2).

(3): Let
w\in W
. If
w\not\in Z
, then
k_{w}=|W|-1
. So, let
w\in Z
and
w^{\prime}\in Z\backslash\{z\}
such that
w\not\sim w^{\prime}
. We have
c(u,v)\in\{24,25\}
.

If
c(u,v)=24
, then
|W|=82
, and
c(a_{1},a_{2})=26
for all
\{a_{1},a_{2}\}\in\binom{\{u,v,w,w^{\prime}\}}{2}
and
\{a_{1},a_{2}\}\neq\{u,v\}
, by Lemma
4.3
(1). In particular, we have
c(w,w^{\prime})=26
and
k_{w}=k_{w^{\prime}}=105-2\times 26=53
. In this case,
K_{w}
has
82-53-1=28
vertices.

If
c(u,v)=25
, then
|W|=83
. We have
c(u,w),c(v,w)\in\{25,26\}
. If one of
c(u,w)
or
c(v,w)
is equal to
25
, then
k_{w}=105-c(u,w)-c(v,w)=105-25-26=54
, by Lemma
4.3
(1). If
c(u,w)=c(v,w)=26
, then
54=105-c(u,w)-c(v,w)+1\geqslant 105-c(u,w)-c(v,w)=53
, by Lemma
4.4
(2). This implies
k_{w}
has at least
83-54-1=28
vertices. This shows the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume a strongly regular graph
G
with parameters
(1911,270,105,27)
exists containing an induced quadrangle, say
x\sim u\sim y\sim v\sim x
. Let
W:=\{w\sim x\mid w\neq u,w\neq v,w\not\sim u,w\not\sim v\}
. Then
|W|\neq 83
.

Proof
.

Let
\Gamma_{W}
be the subgraph of
G
induced on
W
. In Lemma
4.4
(3), we have seen that the valency
k_{w}
in
\Gamma_{W}
of a vertex
w\in W
satisfies
k_{w}\in\{82,54,53\}
.

Let
Y:=\{w\in W\mid k_{w}\in\{54,82\}\}\cup\{x\}
.

Claim 4.6
.

The induced subgraph of
\Gamma_{W}
on
Y
is complete.

Proof of Claim
4.6
.
Clearly
x
is adjacent to all the other vertices in
Y
. Let
w\in W
such that
k_{w}\in\{82,54\}
. If
k_{w}=82
, then
w
is adjacent to all other vertices of
\Gamma_{W}
. So, we only need to show that, if
w
and
w^{\prime}
are distinct vertices in
W
such that
k_{w}=k_{w^{\prime}}=54
, then
w\sim w^{\prime}
.

Let
w
be such that
k_{w}=54
. Let
w^{\prime}\in W\backslash\{w\}
be such that
w\not\sim w^{\prime}
. There are, without loss of generality, two cases namely,
c(u,w)=25
and
c(v,w)=26
or,
c(u,w)=c(v,w)=26
. In the first case
c(u,w^{\prime})=26=c(w^{\prime},v)
and
k_{w^{\prime}}=105-c(u,w^{\prime})-(v,w^{\prime})=53
, by Lemma
4.3
(1). In the second case there exists a vertex
z\sim x
such that
u\sim z\sim v
and
z\sim w
. This means that
c(u,w^{\prime})=26=c(v,w^{\prime})
and
k_{w^{\prime}}=105-c(u,w^{\prime})-c(v,w^{\prime})=53
, by Lemma
4.3
(2). ∎

By Lemma
3.4
, a clique of
G
has order at most
32
, so
|Y|\leqslant 32
. This means that
|\{w\in W\mid k_{w}=53\}|\geqslant 83-31=52
. So there are two distinct non-adjacent vertices
z
and
z^{\prime}
in
W
with
k_{z}=k_{z^{\prime}}=53
. We have
c(z,z^{\prime})\in\{25,26\}
. There is no vertex
\hat{z}
that is adjacent to
u,v
and one of
z
and
z^{\prime}
. This means that, if
c(z,z^{\prime})=26
, then there exists a vertex
\tilde{z}
adjacent to
z,z^{\prime}
and one of
u
and
v
. This means
|C(z,z^{\prime})\cap W|=25
holds whether
c(z,z^{\prime})=25
or
c(z,z^{\prime})=26
. This means that the cliques
K_{z}
and
K_{z^{\prime}}
both have exactly
29
vertices, where
K_{z}
is defined as in Lemma
4.4
.

Assume that there are two distinct vertices in
C(z,z^{\prime})
that are adjacent to all vertices in
K_{z}
. Then they have at least
28
common neighbours and hence are adjacent. This implies there are at most
2
vertices in
C(z,z^{\prime})
such that they are adjacent to all vertices in
\tilde{K}_{z}
and at most
2
vertices in
C(z,z^{\prime})
such that they are adjacent to all vertices in
\tilde{K}_{z^{\prime}}
, where
K_{z}
and
\tilde{K}_{z}
are as defined in Lemma
4.4
. So there exists a vertex
w\in C(z,z^{\prime})
that is not adjacent to all vertices of
\tilde{K}_{z}
and not adjacent to all vertices of
\tilde{K}_{z^{\prime}}
. We find
k_{w}\in\{53,54\}
. Either
w
is adjacent to all other vertices in
C(z,z^{\prime})
or there exist
w^{\prime}\in C(z,z^{\prime})\backslash\{w\}
such that
w\not\sim w^{\prime}
.

Claim 4.7
.

|G(w)\cap G(x)\cap G(z)\cap G(z^{\prime})|\geqslant 22
.

Proof of Claim
4.7
.
We may assume that there exists a vertex
w^{\prime}\in C(z,z^{\prime})\backslash\{w\}
such that
w\not\sim w^{\prime}
, as otherwise we are done. We have
|G(x)\cap G(z)\cap G(z^{\prime})|=c(z,z^{\prime})=25
,
|G(z)\cap G(z^{\prime})|=27
and
|G(w)\cap G(z)\cap G(z^{\prime})|\geqslant 24
. The last statement follows from Lemma
4.3
, as
w\sim z\sim w^{\prime}\sim z^{\prime}\sim w
is an induced quadrangle of
G
. Now
|G(w)\cap G(x)\cap G(z)\cap G(z^{\prime})|\geqslant 25+24-27=22
. This shows Claim
4.7
. ∎

Claim
4.7
implies that
w
has at least
21
neighbours in
W\cap C(z,z^{\prime})
. So, without loss of generality, the vertex
w
has at least
\frac{53-25}{2}=14
neighbours in
K_{z}
and hence at least
15
vertices in
\tilde{K}_{z}
. By Table
2
, we find that
w
has at least
24
neighbours in
\tilde{K}_{z}
and hence at least
23
neighbours in
K_{z}
. This means that
w
has at most
54-21-23=10
neighbours in
K_{z^{\prime}}
, by Claim
4.7
. By Table
2
, we find that
w
has at most
8
neighbours in
\tilde{K}_{z^{\prime}}
.

Now we consider
K_{w}
. Then
K_{w}
has at least
28
vertices, and
\tilde{K}_{w}
and
\tilde{K}_{z^{\prime}}
intersect in at least
30-8=22
vertices.

Now consider a maximal clique
C_{1}
of
G
containing
\tilde{K}_{w}
and a maximal clique
C_{2}
of
G
containing
\tilde{K}_{z^{\prime}}
. We have

. This means that

does not contain

. Also

, so

does not contain

. This means that

. As

and

, by Lemma
3.5
, we find that

or
C_{2}
is not maximal, a contradiction. This shows the lemma.

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Assume a strongly regular graph

with parameters
(1911,270,105,27)
exists containing an induced quadrangle, say

. Let
W:=\{w\sim x\mid w\neq u,w\neq v,w\not\sim u,w\not\sim v\}

.

Proof
.

Let
\Gamma_{W}
be the subgraph of

induced on

. In Lemma
4.4
(3), we have seen that the valency

in

of a vertex

satisfies
k_{w}\in\{82,53\}
. There are two distinct vertices

and hence

, as

If there exists a vertex

, then consider the induced subgraphs
\hat{K}_{z}
(resp.

) on

). As

and

does not lie in an independent set of order

inside

, we see that

in this case, as

So we may assume that all vertices of

have valency

have exactly
26
common neighbours. Note that, by Lemma

, any clique

in

on

Consider the join

by Lemma

be the subgraph on

, we consider the clique

. By Table

, we find that any vertex

of

neighbours or at least

neighbours in

. This means that

has at most
3
neighbours or at least

neighbours in

.

Let

be two distinct non-adjacent vertices in
\Gamma_{W}
. There are at most
3
vertices in

that are adjacent to all vertices in

, and similarly there are at most

vertices in

k_{w^{\prime}}
as

. So there exist a vertex

that is not adjacent to some vertex
p
(resp.

) in

(resp.

). As

has at least
22
neighbours in

, as in Claim

of Lemma

, without loss of generality, we see that

has at most

. So

has at most

neighbours in

. So the two cliques

vertices.

and
\tilde{K}_{w}

and

\tilde{K}_{z}
and

vertices. As

and

we see that
\tilde{K}_{z}
and

must intersect in precisely

vertices and both are maximal, by Lemma

. We have

and

and,

and

intersect in exactly

vertices.

Now consider

. The clique

and

intersects in exactly two vertices under which
p^{\prime}
. Consider any vertex

of the clique

. Then

neighbours in

(2)

, or

Abstract
This paper studies a system of n ∈ ℕ : n ≥ 2 n\in\mathbb{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,n\geq 2 non-relativistic, spinless quantum particles moving on the real line and interacting via a two-body delta potential. The Hamiltonian of such a system is proved to be affiliated to the resolvent algebra of the case, ℛ ⁡ ( ℝ 2 ​ n , σ ) \mathcal{R}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\sigma\right) ; it is further shown the existence of a C ∗ − \text{C}^{\ast}- dynamical system and of a subalgebra π S ​ ( 𝔖 0 ) − 1 ⊂ ℛ ⁡ ( ℝ 2 ​ n , σ ) \pi_{S}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{0}\right)^{-1}\subset\mathcal{R}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\sigma\right) , stable under time evolution, where π S \pi_{S} is the Schrödinger representation of the resolvent algebra.

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{\sigma}}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,\frac{\left(\mathfrak{F}_{\underline{Y}_{\sigma}}\varphi\right)\left(P_{\sigma},\underline{P}_{\sigma}\right)}{\frac{p_{\sigma}^{2}}{2\mu_{\sigma}}+2\mu_{\sigma}\left(Q_{\sigma}-z\right)}dp_{\sigma}=

Proposition 4.26
.

\left(H,\mathcal{D}_{H}\right)
is the unique self-adjoint extension of
\left(H_{0},\ker\tau\right)
.

Proof.

By having established that

\displaystyle\left[\Theta\left(z\right)_{\text{diag}}\right]_{\sigma\sigma}

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}_{\chi^{(\text{red})}_{\sigma}}-g\tau_{\sigma}G_{\sigma}^{\ast}\left(\overline{z}\right)

\displaystyle\left[\Theta\left(z\right)_{\text{off}}\right]_{\sigma\nu}

\displaystyle=-g\tau_{\sigma}G_{\nu}^{\ast}\left(\overline{z}\right)

for
z<0
, the first resolvent formula allows to analytically extend these equalities to
\rho\left(H_{0}\right)
entirely, for all
\sigma,\nu\in\mathcal{I}
. The domain of the operators is independent on
z
and, as long as
z<z_{0}
,
\Theta\left(z\right)
is invertible in
\mathcal{B}\left(\chi^{(\text{red})}\right)
. Then,

1.

for all
z\in\rho\left(H_{0}\right)
,
\sigma\in\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle\left[\Theta\left(z\right)_{(\text{diag})}\right]^{\ast}_{\sigma\sigma}

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}_{\chi^{\text{red}}_{\sigma}}-g\left[\tau_{\sigma}G_{\sigma}^{\ast}\left(\overline{z}\right)\right]^{\ast}=\mathds{1}_{\chi^{\text{red}}_{\sigma}}-g\tau_{\sigma}R_{H_{0}}\left(\overline{z}\right)\tau_{\sigma}^{\ast}=\mathds{1}_{\chi^{\text{red}}_{\sigma}}-g\tau_{\sigma}\left[R_{H_{0}}\left(z\right)^{\ast}\tau_{\sigma}^{\ast}\right]=

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}_{\chi^{\text{red}}_{\sigma}}-g\tau_{\sigma}G_{\sigma}^{\ast}\left(z\right)=\left[\Theta\left(\overline{z}\right)_{\text{diag}}\right]_{\sigma\sigma}.

2.

for all
z\in\rho\left(H_{0}\right)
,
\sigma,\nu\in\mathcal{I}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\sigma\neq\nu

\displaystyle\left[\Theta\left(z\right)_{\text{off}}\right]_{\sigma\nu}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=-gG_{\nu}\left(\overline{z}\right)\tau_{\sigma}^{\ast}=-g\tau_{\nu}R_{H_{0}}\left(\overline{z}\right)\tau_{\sigma}^{\ast}=-g\tau_{\nu}\left[R_{H_{0}}\left(z\right)^{\ast}\tau_{\sigma}^{\ast}\right]=-g\tau_{\nu}G_{\sigma}^{\ast}\left(z\right)=

\displaystyle=\left[\Theta\left(\overline{z}\right)_{\text{off}}\right]_{\nu\sigma}.

Further, given
\sigma\in\mathcal{I}
arbitrary, let
z,w\in\rho\left(H_{0}\right)
be such that
z\neq w
. The first resolvent formula allows to prove that

G_{\sigma}^{\ast}\left(w\right)-G_{\sigma}^{\ast}\left(z\right)=\left(\overline{w}-\overline{z}\right)R_{H_{0}}\left(\overline{w}\right)R_{H_{0}}\left(\overline{z}\right)\tau_{\sigma}^{\ast}\equiv\left(\overline{w}-\overline{z}\right)R_{H_{0}}\left(\overline{w}\right)G_{\sigma}^{\ast}\left(z\right).

Then,

1.

for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{I}
,
z,w\in\rho\left(H_{0}\right)
as above

\left[\Theta\left(z\right)_{\text{diag}}\right]_{\sigma\sigma}-\left[\Theta\left(w\right)_{\text{diag}}\right]_{\sigma\sigma}=g\tau_{\sigma}\left[G_{\sigma}^{\ast}\left(\overline{w}\right)-G_{\sigma}^{\ast}\left(\overline{z}\right)\right]\equiv g\left(w-z\right)G_{\sigma}\left(w\right)G_{\sigma}^{\ast}\left(\overline{z}\right).

2.

for all
\sigma,\nu\in\mathcal{I}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\sigma\neq\nu
,
z,w\in\rho\left(H_{0}\right)
as above

\left[\Theta\left(z\right)_{\text{off}}\right]_{\sigma\nu}-\left[\Theta\left(w\right)_{\text{off}}\right]_{\sigma\nu}=g\tau_{\sigma}\left[G_{\nu}^{\ast}\left(\overline{w}\right)-G_{\nu}^{\ast}\left(\overline{z}\right)\right]=g\left(w-z\right)G_{\sigma}\left(w\right)G_{\nu}^{\ast}\left(\overline{z}\right).

The statement then follows from remark
4.15
.
∎

Lemma 4.27
.

Under the foregoing hypothesis, fixed
z\in\rho\left(H_{0}\right)\cap\rho\left(H\right)

\mathcal{D}_{H}=\left\{\psi\in\mathcal{H}\,\bigg|\,\exists!\,\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{H_{0}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\psi=\varphi+gG\left(\overline{z}\right)^{\ast}\Theta\left(z\right)^{-1}\tau\varphi\right\}.

Proof.

It is well known that
\mathcal{D}_{H}=\text{Ran}\,R_{H}\left(z\right)
for all
z\in\rho\left(H\right)
, independently of
z
. Infact, considered
z_{1}\in\rho\left(H\right)
, let

\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\left(z_{1}\right)}=\left\{\psi\in\mathcal{H}\bigg|\psi=R_{H}\left(z_{1}\right)\varphi,\;\varphi\in\mathcal{H}\right\}

be. Let then
z_{2}\in\rho\left(H\right)
be such that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert z_{1}-z_{2}\right\rvert}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert R_{H}\left(z_{2}\right)\right\rVert}^{-1}
and let
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\left(z_{1}\right)}
be arbitrary. There exists a unique
\varphi_{z_{1}}\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\psi=R_{H}\left(z_{1}\right)\varphi_{z_{1}}
. However, the Neumann expansion formula gives

\psi=R_{H}\left(z_{1}\right)\varphi_{z_{1}}=R_{H}\left(z_{2}\right)\left[\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}{\sum}\,\left(z_{1}-z_{2}\right)^{n}R_{H}\left(z_{2}\right)^{n}\varphi_{z_{1}}\right]\equiv R_{H}\left(z_{2}\right)\varphi_{z_{2}},

i.e.
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\left(z_{2}\right)}
, hence
\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\left(z_{1}\right)}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\left(z_{2}\right)}
. The roles of
z_{1},z_{2}
are nevertheless switchable, therefore
\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\left(z_{1}\right)}=\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\left(z_{2}\right)}
, proving the independence of
\mathcal{D}_{H}
on
z\in\rho\left(H\right)
. The result then follows by arbitrarily fixing
z\in\rho\left(H\right)\cap\rho\left(H_{0}\right)
and by observing that, given
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{H}
generic,
\varphi=R_{H_{0}}\left(z\right)\left(H-z\right)\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{H_{0}}
.
∎

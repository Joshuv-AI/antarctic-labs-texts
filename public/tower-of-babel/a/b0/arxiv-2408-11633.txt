Abstract
. We study moderate deviations from hydrodynamic limits of a reaction diffusion model. The process is defined as the superposition of the symmetric exclusion process with a Glauber dynamics. When the process starts from a product measure with a constant density, which is a non-equilibrium measure for the process, we prove that the re-scaled density fluctuation field satisfies the moderate deviation principle. Our proof relies on the so-called main lemma developed by Jara and Menezes [ 5 , 6 ] .



1.
Introduction

The theory of hydrodynamic limits concerns about deriving macroscopic laws directly from microscopic dynamics. Fluctuations and large deviations from hydrodynamic limits have been investigated intensively, see
[
8
]
. The regime of moderate deviations is between fluctuations and large deviations, and as far as we know, moderate deviations from hydrodynamic limits was only proved for a few models. Gao and Quastel in
[
3
]
first investigated moderate deviations from hydrodynamic limits of the symmetric simple exclusion process (SSEP). Since then, moderate deviation principles (MDP) from hydrodynamic limits was extended to other interacting particle systems, including a special Ginzburg-Landau model
[
10
]
, the SSEP with a slow bond in one dimension
[
13
]
, and the weakly asymmetric simple exclusion process
[
14
]
. The main difficulty is to prove a super-exponential version of the Boltzmann-Gibbs principle. Formally speaking, the Boltzmann-Gibbs principle states that one could replace the non-conserved quantities of the dynamics by its density fluctuation field under the correct time scaling
[
8
, Chapter 11]
; while when considering moderate deviations, one needs to prove such replacement is super-exponentially small.

We underline that the above results are all concentrated on the stationary case,
i.e.
, when the process starts from its stationary measure. It remains challenging to prove MDP from hydrodynamic limits when the process is not stationary as one needs to prove a non-equilibrium version of the super-exponential Boltzmann-Gibbs principle. As far as we know, non-equilibrium MDP from hydrodynamic limits was only proved for the SSEP in one dimension
[
12
]
very recently. The proof uses large deviation estimates for the SSEP. Moreover, since the SSEP is linear, one does not need to prove the super-exponential Boltzmann-Gibbs principle.

The aim of this paper is to investigate non-equilibrium moderate deviations for other interacting particle systems. Precisely, we study the reaction diffusion model in
[
4
]
. The process is composed of the SSEP dynamics and a Glauber dynamics. In the SSEP, particles perform symmetric random walks on the discrete torus subject to the constraint that there is at most one particle at each site. In the Glauber dynamics, particles are created or destroyed at each site according to some rate. It was proved in
[
1
]
that the hydrodynamic limits of this model are governed by the reaction diffusion equation. Non-equilibrium fluctuations were proved in
[
6
]
by using the improved relative entropy method. For dynamical large deviations of this model, we refer the readers to
[
9
,
7
]
. In this article, we proved moderate deviation principles for the rescaled density fluctuation field when the process starts from a special product measure, which is not invariant for the dynamics, see Theorem
2.2
.

As in the theory of large and moderate deviations, for the upper bound, we investigate a super-exponential martingale related to the process, and for the lower bound, we need to study hydrodynamic limits for a perturbed dynamics, see
[
8
]
for example. Compared to the proof in
[
12
]
, we do need to prove the non-equilibrium super-exponential version of the Boltzmann-Gibbs principle, see Lemmas
3.1
and
3.6
, which is the main novelty of the paper. We prove them directly by using the so-called main lemma in
[
4
]
, which is crucial in the improved relative entropy method developed by Jara and Menezes
[
5
,
6
]
.

Since the process is irreducible, it has a unique invariant measure, which is called non-equilibrium stationary state (NESS) in the literature. Fluctuations and large deviations for the NESS were studied in
[
4
,
2
]
. We leave it as a future work to study the MDP for the NESS.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we state the model and results rigorously. The MDP upper and lower bound are proved respectively in Sections
3
and
4
.

1.1.
Notation

We denote
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
. For
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
or
\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}
, write
x\sim y
if
\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}-y_{i}|=1
.
For a probability measure
\mu
on some space, we denote by
E_{\mu}[\,\cdot\,]
the expectation with respect to the measure
\mu
. For two sequences of positive real numbers
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, write
a_{n}\ll b_{n}
or
a_{n}=o(b_{n})
if
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}/b_{n}=0.

2.
Model and results

The state space of the model is
\Omega_{n}:=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}
, where
\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}:=\mathbb{Z}^{d}/(n\mathbb{Z}^{d})
is the
d
-dimensional discrete torus with size
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The generator of the process
\eta(t)\equiv\eta^{n}(t)
is
L_{n}=n^{2}L_{n}^{\rm ex}+L_{n}^{\rm r}
, where for any function
f:\Omega_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle L_{n}^{\rm ex}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}[f(\eta^{x,x+e_{i}})-f(\eta)],

\displaystyle L_{n}^{\rm r}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}c_{x}(\eta)[f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta)].

Above, for
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{n}
,
\eta^{x,y}
is the configuration obtained from
\eta
by swapping the values of
\eta_{x}
and
\eta_{y}
, and
\eta^{x}
is the one by flipping the value of
\eta_{x}
,
i.e.
,

\eta^{x,y}_{z}=\begin{cases}\eta_{x},\quad&\text{if }z=y,\\
\eta_{y},\quad&\text{if }z=x,\\
\eta_{z},\quad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\qquad\eta^{x}_{z}=\begin{cases}1-\eta_{x},\quad&\text{if }z=x,\\
\eta_{z},\quad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The flipping rate
c_{x}(\eta)
is defined as

c_{x}(\eta)=\Big(a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big)(1-\eta_{x})+b\eta_{x},

where
a,b>0
and
\lambda>-a
are given parameters.

For
\rho\in(0,1)
, let
\nu^{n}_{\rho}
be the product measure on
\Omega_{n}
with constant particle density
\rho
,

\nu^{n}_{\rho}(\eta_{x}=1,\,\forall x\in A)=\rho^{|A|},\quad\forall A\subset\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}.

Direct calculations show that

F(\rho):=E_{\nu^{n}_{\rho}}[c_{x}(\eta)(1-2\eta_{x})]=(a+\lambda\rho)(1-\rho)-b\rho.

Since
F
is a quadratic polynomial such that
F(0)=a>0,F(1)=-b<0
, there exists a unique point
\rho_{*}\in(0,1)
such that
F(\rho_{*})=0
.

Let
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
be the space of smooth functions on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
be its topological dual. Fix a time horizon
T>0
. We equip the space
D([0,T],\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
, the space of Càdlàg
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
-valued trajectories on
[0,T]
, with the uniform weak topology: a sequence
\{\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\}_{n\geq 1}
converges to
\mu_{\cdot}
in
D([0,T],\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
if and only if for any
H\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},H\big\rangle-\big\langle\mu_{t},H\big\rangle|=0.

Assume the initial measure of the process is
\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
. We are interested in the rescaled density fluctuation field
\mu^{n}_{t}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
of the process, which acts on
H\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
as

\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},H\big\rangle=\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(t)H(\tfrac{x}{n}),

where
\bar{\eta}_{x}=\eta_{x}-\rho_{*}
, and
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq}
is a sequence of positive real numbers. Define

g_{d}(n)=\begin{cases}n,\quad&\text{if }d=1,\\
\log n,\quad&\text{if }d=2,\\
1,\quad&\text{if }d\geq 3.\end{cases}

We shall need the following assumptions on
a_{n}
.

Assumption 2.1
.

n^{d-1}\sqrt{g_{d}(n)}\ll a_{n}\ll n^{d}.

Remark 2.1
.

In the regime of moderate deviations, one usually assumes
n^{d/2}\ll a_{n}\ll n^{d}
. So, the above assumption is only optimal in dimension one.

Next, we introduce the MDP rate function. For
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, the rate function corresponding to the initial distribution is defined as

(2.1)

\mathcal{Q}_{0}(\mu_{0}):=\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\Big\{\big\langle\mu_{0},\phi\big\rangle-\frac{\chi(\rho_{*})}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\Big\},

where
\chi(\rho_{*})=\rho_{*}(1-\rho_{*})
. Let
C^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
be the space of functions on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
which are continuously derivative in the time variable and smooth in the space variable. For
\mu\in D([0,T],\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
and
H\in C^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
, define

\ell_{T}(\mu,H):=\big\langle\mu_{T},H_{T}\big\rangle-\big\langle\mu_{0},H_{0}\big\rangle-\int_{0}^{T}\big\langle\mu_{s},(\partial_{s}+\Delta+F^{\prime}(\rho_{*}))H_{s}\big\rangle ds.

By simple calculations,
F^{\prime}(\rho_{*})=\lambda-a-b-2\lambda\rho_{*}
.
The rate function corresponding to the dynamics of the process is defined as

\mathcal{Q}_{\rm dyn}(\mu):=\sup_{H\in C^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\Big\{\ell_{T}(\mu,H)-\chi(\rho_{*})\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|\partial_{u_{i}}H\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}-\frac{G(\rho_{*})}{2}\|H\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\Big\},

where
G(\rho):=E_{\nu^{n}_{\rho}}[c_{x}(\eta)]=(a-\lambda\rho)(1-\rho)+b\rho
. Finally, the rate function is defined as

\mathcal{Q}_{T}:=\mathcal{Q}_{0}+\mathcal{Q}_{\rm dyn}.

Denote by
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
the probability measure on
D([0,T],\Omega_{n})
induced by the process
(\eta(t))_{t\geq 0}
with initial distribution
\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
, and by
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
the corresponding expectation.

Below is the main result of the article.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume Assumption
2.1
holds. There exists
\lambda_{c}>0
such that for any
\lambda\in[-\lambda_{c},\lambda_{c}]
, under
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
, the sequence of measures
\{\mu^{n}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfies the moderate deviation principles with decay rate
a_{n}^{2}/n^{d}
and with rate function
\mathcal{Q}_{T}
. Precisely speaking, for any closed set
C
and any open set
O
in
D([0,T],\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\{\mu^{n}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}_{n\geq 1}\in C\Big)\leq-\inf_{\mu\in C}\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu),

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\{\mu^{n}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}_{n\geq 1}\in O\Big)\geq-\inf_{\mu\in O}\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu).

3.
The upper bound

In Subsection
3.1
, we introduce an exponential martingale related to the process and express it as a functional of the fluctuation field
\mu^{n}_{t}
plus super-exponentially small error terms. This is the main step of the upper bound. The main difficulty is to prove two super-exponential estimates, see Lemmas
3.1
and
3.6
. We prove exponential tightness of the rescaled density fluctuation field in Subsection
3.2
. Finally, the proof of the upper bound is concluded in Subsection
3.3
.

3.1.
An exponential martingale

For any
H\in C^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
, by Feynman-Kac formula,

\mathcal{M}^{n}_{t}(H)=\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},H_{t}\big\rangle-\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{0},H_{0}\big\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\tfrac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\rangle}\big(\partial_{s}+L_{n}\big)e^{\tfrac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\rangle}ds\Big\}

is an exponential martingale. By direct calculations,

e^{-\tfrac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\rangle}\partial_{s}e^{\tfrac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\rangle}=\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\partial_{s}H_{s}\big\rangle,

and

\displaystyle e^{-\tfrac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\rangle}L_{n}e^{\tfrac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\rangle}

\displaystyle=n^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big[\exp\big\{\tfrac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\big(\eta_{x}(s)-\eta_{x+e_{i}}(s)\big)\big(H_{s}(\tfrac{x+e_{i}}{n})-H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big)\big\}-1\big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}c_{x}(\eta(s))\big[\exp\big\{\tfrac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}(1-2\eta_{x}(s))H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big\}-1\big].

By Taylor’s expansion up to the second order, and using the summation by parts formula,

e^{-\tfrac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\rangle}L_{n}e^{\tfrac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\rangle}=\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\Big\{\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\Delta_{n}H_{s}\big\rangle+\frac{1}{2n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big(\eta_{x}(s)-\eta_{x+e_{i}}(s)\big)^{2}\big(\nabla_{n,i}H_{s}\big)^{2}(\tfrac{x}{n})\\
+\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}c_{x}(\eta(s))(1-2\eta_{x}(s))H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})+\frac{1}{2n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}c_{x}(\eta(s))H_{s}^{2}(\tfrac{x}{n})+R^{n,1}_{s}(H)\Big\},

where
\Delta_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Delta_{n,i}
is the discrete Laplacian, and

\Delta_{n,i}H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})=n^{2}\big(H_{s}(\tfrac{x+e_{i}}{n})+H_{s}(\tfrac{x-e_{i}}{n})-2H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big),\quad\nabla_{n,i}H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})=n\big(H_{s}(\tfrac{x+e_{i}}{n})-H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big).

The error term satisfies

|R^{n,1}_{s}(H)|\leq C(H)a_{n}/n^{d}.

Since
F(\rho_{\ast})=E_{\nu^{n}_{\rho_{\ast}}}\big[c_{x}(\eta)(1-2\eta_{x})\big]=0
, we have

\displaystyle c_{x}(\eta)(1-2\eta_{x})

\displaystyle=\Big(a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big)(1-\eta_{x})-b\eta_{x}

\displaystyle=-(a+b+\lambda\rho_{*})\bar{\eta}_{x}+\frac{\lambda(1-\rho_{*})}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\bar{\eta}_{y}-\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\bar{\eta}_{y}\bar{\eta}_{x}.

Thus, using the summation by parts formula,

(3.1)

\displaystyle e^{-\tfrac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\rangle}L_{n}e^{\tfrac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\langle\mu^{n}_{s},H_{s}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\Big\{\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},\big((1+\tfrac{\lambda(1-\rho_{*})}{2dn^{2}})\Delta_{n}+F^{\prime}(\rho_{*})\big)H_{s}\big\rangle+\frac{1}{2n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big(\eta_{x}(s)-\eta_{x+e_{i}}(s)\big)^{2}\big(\nabla_{n,i}H_{s}\big)^{2}(\tfrac{x}{n})

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{2da_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{y\sim x}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)\bar{\eta}_{y}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})+\frac{1}{2n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}c_{x}(\eta(s))H_{s}^{2}(\tfrac{x}{n})+R^{n,1}_{s}(H)\Big\}.

In order to further deal with the above terms, we need the following two super-exponential estimates in Lemmas
3.1
and
3.6
.

Lemma 3.1

(A super-exponential estimate for degree-one terms)
.

If
r_{n}\gg a_{n}
, then for any
H\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(3.2)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big|>\varepsilon\Big)=-\infty.

Before proving the above lemma, we first recall the following version of Garsia-Rodemich-Rumsey inequality (see
[
8
, page 182]
), which allows us to remove the supremum over time inside the above inequality.

Lemma 3.2

(Garsia-Rodemich-Rumsey inequality)
.

Let
g:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. Assume
\psi(u),p(u)
are strictly increasing functions satisfying that

\psi(0)=p(0)=0,\quad\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}\psi(u)=\infty.

Then, for any
\delta>0
,

\sup_{|t-s|<\delta\atop 0\leq s,t\leq T}|g(t)-g(s)|\leq 8\int_{0}^{\delta}\psi^{-1}\Big(\frac{4B}{u^{2}}\Big)p(du),

where

B=\int_{0}^{T}ds\int_{0}^{T}dt\psi\Big(\frac{|g(t)-g(s)|}{p(|t-s|)}\Big).

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

First, we adopt the ideas in
[
3
]
, where the Garsia-Rodemich-Rumsey inequality was used to prove exponential tightness of the density fluctuation field, to remove the supremum over time inside the probability in (
3.2
). In Lemma
3.2
, take

g(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds,\quad\psi(u)=u^{2q},\quad p(u)=u^{1/3},

where
q>3
is fixed. Then, there exists some constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(q)
such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big|\leq C_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\frac{B}{u^{2}}\Big)^{\tfrac{1}{2q}}\frac{du}{u^{2/3}}=C_{1}T^{\tfrac{1}{3}-\tfrac{1}{q}}B^{\tfrac{1}{2q}}=:C_{2}(q,T)B^{\tfrac{1}{2q}},

where

B=\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\Big(\frac{1}{|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\big|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\big|\Big)^{2q}.

Thus, by Markov’s inequality, for any
A>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big|>\varepsilon\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(B^{\tfrac{1}{2q}}>\frac{\varepsilon}{C_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{A\varepsilon}{C_{2}T^{1/q}}+\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}}\Big(\frac{B}{T^{2}}\Big)^{\tfrac{1}{2q}}\Big\}\Big].

Since
A
could be taken arbitrarily large, we only need to show that, for any
A>0
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}}\Big(\frac{B}{T^{2}}\Big)^{\tfrac{1}{2q}}\Big\}\Big]=0.

Define

f_{q}(x)=\exp\Big\{\big((2q-1)^{2q}+x\big)^{\tfrac{1}{2q}}\Big\},\quad x\geq 0.

One could check directly that
f_{q}
is convex. Then,

\displaystyle\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}}\Big(\frac{B}{T^{2}}\Big)^{\tfrac{1}{2q}}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq f_{q}\Big(\frac{1}{T^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\Big(\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\big|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\big|\Big)^{2q}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}f_{q}\Big(\Big(\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\big|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\big|\Big)^{2q}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq e^{2q-1}\frac{1}{T^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\big|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\big|\Big\},

where we used the inequality
f_{q}(x)\leq\exp\{(2q-1)+x^{\tfrac{1}{2q}}\}
. Thus, we only need to show that, for any
A>0
,

(3.3)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\Big(\frac{1}{T^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\,\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\big|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\big|\Big\}\Big]\Big)=0.

Next, we bound the expectation

\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\big|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\big|\Big\}\Big].

Without loss of generality, assume
t<t^{\prime}
. Using the basic inequalities
e^{|x|}\leq e^{x}+e^{-x}
and
\log(x+y)\leq\log 2+\max\{\log x,\log y\}
, we can remove the absolute value inside the above exponential. Then, we shall use the following version of Feynman-Kac formula, see
[
4
, Proposition 4.3]
for example. Below, we say
f
is a
\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
-density if
f\geq 0
and
E_{\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}}[f]=1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
W
be a function on
[0,T]\times\Omega_{n}
. Then,

\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}W(t,\eta(t))dt\Big\}\Big]\leq\int_{0}^{T}dt\sup_{f:\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\text{-density}}\Big\{\int\Big(W_{t}+\frac{1}{2}L_{n}^{*}\mathbf{1}\Big)fd\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}-\int\Gamma_{n}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big\},

where
L_{n}^{*}
is the adjoint operator of
L_{n}
in
L^{2}(\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}})
, and
\Gamma_{n}(f)=\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}(f)+\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(f)
, where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big[f(\eta^{x,x+e_{i}})-f(\eta)\big]^{2},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}c_{x}(\eta)\big[f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta)\big]^{2}.

Take

W(s,\eta)=\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}r_{n}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n}),\quad s\in[t,t^{\prime}],

and
W(s,\eta)=0
otherwise. Then, we bound

(3.4)

\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\big|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\big|\Big\}\Big]\leq\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\Lambda^{n}_{s}ds,

where, for
s<t
,

\Lambda^{n}_{s}=\sup_{f:\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\text{-density}}\Big\{\int\frac{1}{2}L_{n}^{*}\mathbf{1}fd\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}-\int\Gamma_{n}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big\},

and for
t\leq s\leq t^{\prime}
,

\Lambda^{n}_{s}=\sup_{f:\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\text{-density}}\Big\{\int\Big(\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}r_{n}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})+\frac{1}{2}L_{n}^{*}\mathbf{1}\Big)fd\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}-\int\Gamma_{n}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big\}.

To bound
\Lambda^{n}_{s}
, we first recall the so-called main lemma, which was proved in
[
4
, Theorem 3.3]
. For
1\leq i\leq d
, let
h^{i}
be functions on
\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}
. Define

V(h):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}h^{i}(x).

Proposition 3.4

(The first main lemma)
.

There exists some constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(d)
such that for any
\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
-density
f
,

\int V(h)fd\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm ex}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}+C_{0}\kappa(\rho_{*})\mathcal{A}(\|h\|_{\infty})\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}+C_{0}\mathcal{A}(\|h\|_{\infty})n^{d-2}g_{d}(n),

where
\mathcal{A}(u):=u(1+u)
,
\|h\|_{\infty}:=\max_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d},1\leq i\leq d}|h^{i}(x)|
, and

\kappa(\rho)=\frac{2\rho(1-\rho)|\log\tfrac{\rho}{1-\rho}|}{\min\{a,a+\lambda,b\}|1-2\rho|}.

To calculate
L_{n}^{*}\mathbf{1}
, we use the following formula, see
[
6
, Proposition 3.3]
for example.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X_{t}
be a continuous markov process on some space
\Omega
with generator

Lf(x)=\sum_{y\in\Omega}r(x,y)[f(y)-f(x)].

Let
\nu
be a probability measure on
\Omega
and
L^{*}
be the adjoint of
L
in
L^{2}(\nu)
. Then,

L^{*}\mathbf{1}(x)=\sum_{y\neq x}\Big\{\frac{\nu(y)r(y,x)}{\nu(x)}-r(x,y)\Big\}.

We first bound the term
\Lambda^{n}_{s}
for
s<t
. Using the above lemma,

\displaystyle L_{n}^{*}\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\Big\{\eta_{x}\Big(\frac{1-\rho_{*}}{\rho_{*}}\Big[a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big]-b\Big)+(1-\eta_{x})\Big(\frac{\rho_{*}}{1-\rho_{*}}b-\Big[a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big]\Big)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\Big(\frac{\eta_{x}}{\rho_{*}}-\frac{1-\eta_{x}}{1-\rho_{*}}\Big)\Big(\Big[a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big](1-\rho_{*})-b\rho_{*}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\chi(\rho_{*})}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}\Big(F(\rho_{*})+\frac{\lambda(1-\rho_{*})}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\bar{\eta}_{y}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{y\sim x}\bar{\eta}_{x}\bar{\eta}_{y}.

Thus, by Proposition
3.4
, for
s<t
,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{n}_{s}\leq\sup_{f:\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\text{-density}}\Big\{\Big[C_{0}\kappa(\rho_{*})\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\Big)-1\Big]\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}+C_{0}\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\Big)n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)\Big\}.

Let
\lambda_{c}>0
be such that

(3.5)

C_{0}\kappa(\rho_{*})\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\Big)<1

for any
\lambda\in[-\lambda_{c},\lambda_{c}]
, which is possible since

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}C_{0}\kappa(\rho_{*})\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\Big)=0.

Then, we bound, for
s<t
,

\Lambda^{n}_{s}\leq C_{0}\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\Big)n^{d-2}g_{d}(n).

Next, we deal with the case
t\leq s\leq t^{\prime}
. We need to consider the extra term

\int\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}r_{n}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})f(\eta)d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}.

By making the change of transformations
\eta\mapsto\eta^{x}

\displaystyle\int\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}r_{n}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}(1-\eta_{x}-\rho_{*})H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})f(\eta^{x})\frac{\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}(\eta^{x})}{\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}(\eta)}d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}r_{n}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})f(\eta^{x})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}r_{n}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})[f(\eta)-f(\eta^{x})]d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}.

By Young’s inequality, for any
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, the last line is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\int\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}c_{x}(\eta)[\sqrt{f(\eta)}-\sqrt{f(\eta^{x})}]^{2}d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{A^{2}a_{n}^{4}}{8\varepsilon_{0}n^{2d}r_{n}^{2}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2/3}}\int\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}c_{x}(\eta)^{-1}\big(\bar{\eta}_{x}\big)^{2}H^{2}_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})[\sqrt{f(\eta)}+\sqrt{f(\eta^{x})}]^{2}d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{0}\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}\big(\sqrt{f}\big)d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}+\frac{C_{3}A^{2}a_{n}^{4}}{\varepsilon_{0}n^{d}r_{n}^{2}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2/3}}

for some constant
C_{3}=C_{3}(\rho_{*},a,b,\lambda,H)
. Thus, for
t\leq s\leq t^{\prime}
, for any
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{n}_{s}\leq\sup_{f:\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\text{-density}}\Big\{

\displaystyle\Big[C_{0}\kappa(\rho_{*})\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\Big)+\varepsilon_{0}-1\Big]\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}

\displaystyle+C_{0}\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\Big)n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)+\frac{C_{3}A^{2}a_{n}^{4}}{\varepsilon_{0}n^{d}r_{n}^{2}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2/3}}\Big\}.

By choosing
\varepsilon_{0}
small enough such that
C_{0}\kappa(\rho_{*})\mathcal{A}(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}})+\varepsilon_{0}<1
, we have that, for
t\leq s\leq t^{\prime}
,

\Lambda^{n}_{s}\leq C_{0}\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\Big)n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)+\frac{C_{3}A^{2}a_{n}^{4}}{\varepsilon_{0}n^{d}r_{n}^{2}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2/3}}.

Recall (
3.4
). Then,

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}A}{n^{d}\sqrt{|t-t^{\prime}|}}\big|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\big|\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}C_{0}\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\Big)n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)ds+\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\Big[C_{0}\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\Big)n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)+\frac{C_{3}A^{2}a_{n}^{4}}{\varepsilon_{0}n^{d}r_{n}^{2}|t-t^{\prime}|^{2/3}}\Big]ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}t^{\prime}\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\Big)n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)+\frac{C_{3}A^{2}a_{n}^{4}}{\varepsilon_{0}n^{d}r_{n}^{2}}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}.

Finally, we bound the limit in (
3.3
) by

(3.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\Big(\frac{1}{T^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\,\exp\Big\{C_{0}t^{\prime}\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\Big)n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)+\frac{C_{3}A^{2}a_{n}^{4}}{\varepsilon_{0}n^{d}r_{n}^{2}}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}\Big\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big\{C_{0}T\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\Big)\frac{n^{2d-2}g_{d}(n)}{a_{n}^{2}}+\frac{C_{3}(2T)^{1/3}A^{2}a_{n}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{0}r_{n}^{2}}\Big\}.

We conclude the proof by using the assumption
n^{d-1}\sqrt{g_{d}(n)}\ll a_{n}\ll r_{n}
.
∎

Lemma 3.6

(A super-exponential estimate for degree-two terms)
.

For any
H\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, for any
1\leq i\leq d
, and for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(3.7)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\rho_{\ast}}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big|>\varepsilon\Big)=-\infty.

Proof.

Following the proof of (
3.3
) line by line, we only need to show that, for any
A>0
,

(3.8)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\Big(\frac{1}{T^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\\
\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}A}{n^{d}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\Big|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)\bar{\eta}_{x+e_{i}}(s)H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\Big|\Big\}\Big]\Big)=0.

As before, we assume
t<t^{\prime}
. By Lemma
3.3
, the expectation in (

) is bounded by
\exp\{\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\Lambda^{n}_{s}ds\}
, where for

and for
s<t

\Lambda^{n}_{s}=\sup_{f:\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\text{-density}}\Big\{\int\frac{1}{2}L_{n}^{*}\mathbf{1}f(\eta)d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}-\int\Gamma_{n}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big\}.

We have shown in the proof of Lemma

that, for

In particular,

(3.10)

\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda^{n}_{s}ds\leq C_{0}T\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}\Big)n^{d-2}g_{d}(n).

For

,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{n}_{s}\leq\sup_{f:\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\text{-density}}\Big\{

\displaystyle\Big[C_{0}\kappa(\rho_{*})\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}+\frac{a_{n}A}{n^{d}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\|H\|_{\infty}\Big)-1\Big]\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}

\displaystyle+C_{0}\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}+\frac{a_{n}A}{n^{d}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\|H\|_{\infty}\Big)n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)\Big\}.

Note that we cannot deal with the coefficient before

by using (
3.5
) directly since there is a singularity at the point

\varepsilon_{0}>0
small enough such that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\Lambda^{n}_{s}ds

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{f}\Big\{C_{0}\kappa(\rho_{*})\Big(\big(1+\tfrac{\lambda}{d\rho_{*}}\big)\frac{a_{n}A|t-t^{\prime}|^{2/3}}{n^{d}}\|H\|_{\infty}+\frac{a_{n}^{2}A^{2}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}{n^{2d}}\|H\|_{\infty}^{2}\Big)\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}

\displaystyle+C_{0}(t^{\prime}-t)\mathcal{A}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2d\rho_{*}}+\frac{a_{n}A}{n^{d}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\|H\|_{\infty}\Big)n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\Big(\frac{a_{n}^{3}}{n^{2d}}+n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)\Big)

for some constant
C_{5}=C_{5}(C_{0},\lambda,d,\rho_{*},H,A,T)
. Above, we used the trivial bound

\int\Gamma_{n}^{\rm r}(\sqrt{f})d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\leq Cn^{d}

for any
\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
-density

. Finally, by (

) and (

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}C_{6}\Big(\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}+\frac{n^{2d-2}g_{d}(n)}{a_{n}^{2}}\Big)=0

for some constant

, thus concluding the proof.
∎

By Lemmas

and

Moreover, the time integral of the third one is super-exponentially small. Therefore, we can rewrite the exponential martingale as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{n}_{t}(H)=\exp\Big\{

\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\Big(\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},H_{t}\big\rangle-\big\langle\mu^{n}_{0},H_{0}\big\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},(\partial_{s}+\Delta+F^{\prime}(\rho_{*}))H_{s}\big\rangle ds

3.2.
Exponential tightness

In this section, we prove the sequence of processes
\{\mu^{n}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}_{n\geq 1}
is exponentially tight. It suffices to prove the following two estimates: for any
H\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, for any
\varepsilon>0

(3.14)

\displaystyle\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big|\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},H\big\rangle\big|>M\Big)=-\infty,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\sup_{|t-s|\leq\delta}\big|\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t}-\mu^{n}_{s},H\big\rangle\big|>\varepsilon\Big)=-\infty.

Recall (
3.12
). Together with (
3.13
), it is easy to see that the three terms in the second line in (
3.12
) satisfy the above two estimates. Thus, we only need to respectively consider the following three terms

\big\langle\mu^{n}_{0},H\big\rangle,\quad\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathcal{M}^{n}_{t}(H),\quad\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},(\Delta+F^{\prime}(\rho_{*}))H_{s}\big\rangle ds.

For the initial term, since
\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
is a product measure, by Taylor’s expansion,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{0},H\big\rangle\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\log E_{\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}H(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\log\Big(1+\frac{a_{n}^{2}\chi(\rho_{*})}{2n^{2d}}H^{2}\big(\tfrac{x}{n}\big)+\mathcal{O}_{H}\big(\frac{a_{n}^{3}}{n^{3d}}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi(\rho_{*})}{2}\|H\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}.

By Markov’s inequality, the term
\big\langle\mu^{n}_{0},H\big\rangle
satisfies the estimate in (
3.14
).

Now, we deal with the martingale term. For the estimate in (
3.14
), by Markov’s inequality, we only need to show that, there exists some constant
C
such that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\exp\{|\log\mathcal{M}^{n}_{t}(H)|\}\Big]\leq C.

As before, we can remove the above absolute value, and only need to prove that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathcal{M}^{n}_{t}(H)\Big]\leq C.

By Doob’s inequality and (
3.12

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\big(\mathcal{M}^{n}_{T}(H)\big)^{2}\Big]\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\mathcal{M}^{n}_{T}(2H)\exp\Big\{\frac{C(H,\rho_{*})a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\Big\}\Big]\leq C(H,\rho_{*}).

For the estimate in (
3.15
), we need to show that, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(3.17)

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\sup_{|t-s|\leq\delta,\atop 0\leq s,t\leq T}\Big|\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\frac{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{t}(H)}{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{s}(H)}\Big|>\varepsilon\Big)=-\infty.

Since

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\frac{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{t}(H)}{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{t-}(H)}\Big|\leq\frac{C(H)}{a_{n}},

we have

\Big\{\sup_{|t-s|\leq\delta,\atop 0\leq s,t\leq T}\Big|\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\frac{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{t}(H)}{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{s}(H)}\Big|>\varepsilon\Big\}\subset\bigcup_{k=0}^{[T/\delta]}\Big\{\sup_{k\delta\leq t\leq(k+1)\delta}\Big|\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\frac{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{t}(H)}{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{k\delta}(H)}\Big|>\varepsilon/4\Big\}.

Thus, we only need to show that

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\,\max_{0\leq k\leq[T/\delta]}\,\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\sup_{k\delta\leq t\leq(k+1)\delta}\Big|\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\frac{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{t}(H)}{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{k\delta}(H)}\Big|>\varepsilon/4\Big)=-\infty.

By Markov’s inequality and Doob’s inequality, it suffices to show that, for any
A>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\,\max_{0\leq k\leq[T/\delta]}\,\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\sup_{k\delta\leq t\leq(k+1)\delta}\Big|\frac{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{t}(H)}{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{k\delta}(H)}\Big|^{A}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\,\max_{0\leq k\leq[T/\delta]}\,\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\Big|\frac{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{(k+1)\delta}(H)}{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{k\delta}(H)}\Big|^{A}\Big]=0.

We finish the proof by observing that

\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\Big|\frac{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{(k+1)\delta}(H)}{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{k\delta}(H)}\Big|^{A}\Big]\leq\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\frac{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{(k+1)\delta}(AH)}{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{k\delta}(AH)}\exp\Big\{\frac{C(H,A)\delta a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\Big\}\Big]=\exp\Big\{\frac{C(H,A)\delta a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}\Big\}.

For the term
\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{s},(\Delta+F^{\prime}(\rho_{*}))H\big\rangle ds
, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.7
.

For any
H\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, and for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big|\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{r},H\big\rangle dr\big|>M\Big)=-\infty,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\sup_{|t-s|\leq\delta}\big|\int_{s}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{r},H\big\rangle dr\big|>\varepsilon\Big)=-\infty.

Proof.

We only prove the second identity and the first one could be proved in the same way. As in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, by Garsia-Rodemich-Rumsey inequality (see Lemma
3.2
),

\sup_{|t-s|\leq\delta}\big|\int_{s}^{t}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{r},H\big\rangle dr\big|\leq C_{1}(q)\delta^{\tfrac{1}{3}-\tfrac{1}{q}}B^{\tfrac{1}{2q}},

where
q>3
is fixed, and

B=\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\Big(\frac{1}{|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\Big|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{r},H\big\rangle dr\Big|\Big).

Similarly to the proof of (
3.3
), we only need to prove that, there exists some constant
C
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\Big(\frac{1}{T^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\,\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}A}{n^{d}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/3}}\big|\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{x}(s)H(\tfrac{x}{n})ds\big|\Big\}\Big]\Big)\leq C.

Note that the above expression is the same as the one in (
3.3
) with
A=1,r_{n}=a_{n}
. By (
3.6
), the limit in the last inequality is bounded by

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}C(C_{0},d,T,\lambda,\rho_{*},\varepsilon_{0})\Big(\frac{n^{2d-2}g_{d}(n)}{a_{n}^{2}}+1\Big)\leq C(C_{0},d,T,\lambda,\rho_{*},\varepsilon_{0}),

thus concluding the proof.
∎

3.3.
Proof of the upper bound

By the exponential tightness of the sequence
\{\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\}
, we only need to prove that for any compact set
K\subset D([0,T],\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
,

(3.20)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\in K\Big)\leq-\inf_{\mu\in K}\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu).

For any
\delta>0
, let
\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
be the event that

\Big|\int_{0}^{T}R^{n}_{s}(H)ds\Big|\leq\delta.

Then, by (
3.13
) and (
3.12
), the
\limsup
in (
3.20
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\in K,\mathcal{B}_{\delta}\Big)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\mathcal{M}^{n}_{T}(H)^{-1}\mathcal{M}^{n}_{H}(H)\mathbf{1}\{\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\in K,\mathcal{B}_{\delta}\}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq-\inf_{\mu\in K}\Big\{\ell_{T}(\mu,H)-\chi(\rho_{*})\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|\partial_{u_{i}}H\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}-\frac{G(\rho_{*})}{2}\|H\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}+\langle\mu_{0},\phi\rangle\Big\}

\displaystyle+\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\langle\mu^{n}_{0},\phi\rangle\Big\}\Big]+\delta

for any
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. By (
3.16
), the
\limsup
in the last line equals
\tfrac{\chi(\rho_{*})}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}
. Letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
and then optimizing over
H\in C^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\in K\Big)\leq-\sup_{H\in C^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\inf_{\mu\in K}\Big\{\ell_{T}(\mu,H)\\
-\chi(\rho_{*})\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|\partial_{u_{i}}H\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}-\frac{G(\rho_{*})}{2}\|H\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}+\langle\mu_{0},\phi\rangle-\frac{\chi(\rho_{*})}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\Big\}.

Note that the term inside the above brace is continuous in
\mu,H,\phi
, linear in
\mu
and concave in
H,\phi
. By the Minimax theorem
[
3
, Page 584]
, we can exchange the order of the above
\sup
and
\inf
, thus concluding the proof of the upper bound.

4.
The lower bound

In subsection
4.1
, we state hydrodynamic limits for a perturbed dynamics corresponding to the exponential martingale constructed previously in Subsection
3.1
. The proof of this hydrodynamic limits is presented in Subsection
4.3
. The straightforward approach is to use the entropy method, see
[
8
, Chapter 5]
for example. However, for the entropy method, one of the steps is to prove the absolute continuity of the limiting point with respect to the Lebesgue measure, which we are not aware of how to prove in this case. For this reason, we adopt the relative entropy method, where we need another version of main lemma, see Proposition
4.5
. With the above hydrodynamic limits, we prove the lower bound in Subsection
4.2
.

4.1.
Hydrodynamic limits for the perturbed dynamics

For any
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, define
\nu^{n}_{\phi,\rho_{*}}
as the product measure on
\Omega_{n}
with marginals

\nu^{n}_{\phi,\rho_{*}}(\eta_{x}=1)=\rho_{*}+\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\phi\Big(\frac{x}{n}\Big),\quad x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}.

For any
H\in C^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
, define

\frac{d\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}}{d\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\rho_{*}}}}=\mathcal{M}^{n}_{T}(H)\frac{d\nu^{n}_{\phi,\rho_{*}}}{d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}}.

Under
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}
,
(\eta(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a time-inhomogeneous Markov process on the state space
\Omega_{n}
with generator
L_{n,t}^{H}
and with initial distribution
\nu^{n}_{\phi,\rho_{*}}
, where for
f:\Omega_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle L_{n,t}^{H}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=n^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\exp\big\{\tfrac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\eta_{x+e_{i}})\big(H_{t}(\tfrac{x+e_{i}}{n})-H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big)\big\}\big[f(\eta^{x,x+e_{i}})-f(\eta)\big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}c_{x}(\eta)\exp\big\{\tfrac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}(1-2\eta_{x})H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big\}\big[f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta)\big],

see
[
8
, Appendix 1, Proposition 7.3]
for example.

The following result concerns hydrodynamic limits for
\mu^{n}_{t}
in the above perturbed dynamics. It can also be regarded as an equilibrium perturbation of the dynamics, see
[
11
]
for example.

Proposition 4.1
.

Under
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}
, the sequence
\{\mu^{n}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}_{n\geq 1}
converges in probability to the deterministic measure
\{\rho(t,u)du,0\leq t\leq T\}
, where
\rho(t,u)
is the unique weak solution to the following PDE:

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho(t,u)=(\Delta+F^{\prime}(\rho_{*}))\rho(t,u)-2\chi(\rho_{*})\Delta H(t,u)+G(\rho_{*})H(t,u),\quad t>0,u\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\\
\rho(0,u)=\phi(u),\quad u\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.\end{cases}

4.2.
Proof of the lower bound

We first introduce some properties of the rate function
\mathcal{Q}_{T}
. For any
H,J\in C^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
, define the scalar product
[\cdot,\cdot]
as

[H,J]:=\chi(\rho_{*})\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial_{u_{i}}H(t,u)\partial_{u_{i}}J(t,u)\,du\,dt+\frac{G(\rho_{*})}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}H(t,u)J(t,u)\,du\,dt.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the Hilbert space defined as the completion of the space
H,J\in C^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
with respect to the above scalar product. With the above notation, we have

\mathcal{Q}_{\rm dyn}(\mu):=\sup_{J\in C^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\Big\{\ell_{T}(\mu,J)-[J,J]\Big\}.

Also recall (
2.1
). Then, by Riesz representation theorem, it is easy to prove the following result. We omit the proof here and refer the readers to
[
3
, Lemma 5.1]
for details.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)<+\infty
, then there exist
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
H\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\big\langle\mu_{0},\varphi\big\rangle=\big\langle\phi,\varphi\big\rangle,\;\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d});\quad\mathcal{Q}_{0}(\mu_{0})=\frac{1}{2\chi(\rho_{*})}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2},

and that

\ell_{T}(\mu,J)=2[H,J],\;\forall J\in C^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d});\quad\mathcal{Q}_{\rm dyn}(\mu)=[H,H].

Now, we prove the lower bound. For any open set
O\in D([0,T],\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
, it suffices to show that for any
\mu\in O
,

(4.2)

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\in O\Big)\geq-\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu).

Using an approximation procedure, we can assume that
\mu(t,du)=\rho(t,u)du
with
\rho(t,u)\in C^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
and that
H\in C^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}),\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, where
H
and
\phi
are identified in Lemma
4.2
, see
[
3
]
for example. Moreover,
\rho(t,u)
is the unique weak solution to (
4.1
). By Jensen’s inequality,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\in O\Big)=\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big[\mathcal{M}^{n}_{T}(H)^{-1}\frac{d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}}{d\nu^{n}_{\phi,\rho_{*}}}\mathbf{1}\{\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\in O\}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\in O\Big)+\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big[\log\mathcal{M}^{n}_{T}(H)^{-1}\Big|\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\in O\Big]+\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big[\log\frac{d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}}{d\nu^{n}_{\phi,\rho_{*}}}\Big|\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\in O\Big].

By Proposition
4.1
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\in O\Big)=1.

In particular,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}\Big(\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\in O\Big)=0.

To deal with the remaining two terms on the right-hand side of (
4.3
), we need the following lemma, which can be proved by using the entropy inequality, see
[
14
, Lemma 4.3]
for example.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in D([0,T],\Omega_{n})
. If

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\rho_{*}}(\mathcal{A})=-\infty,

then,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}(\mathcal{A})=0.

For the martingale term on the right-hand side of (
4.3
),

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\log\mathcal{M}^{n}_{T}(H)^{-1}=-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big\{\ell_{T}(\mu^{n},H)-[H,H]\Big\}=-\Big\{\ell_{T}(\mu,H)-[H,H]\Big\}=-\mathcal{Q}_{\rm dyn}(\mu)

in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}
-probability. In the above first identity we used (
3.12
), (
3.13
) and Lemma
4.3
; in the second one we used Proposition
4.1
; in the last one we used Lemma
4.2
. By Proposition
4.1
and dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big[\log\mathcal{M}^{n}_{T}(H)^{-1}\Big|\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\in O\Big]=-\mathcal{Q}_{\rm dyn}(\mu).

For the last term on the right-hand side of (
4.3
), since
\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}
and
\nu^{n}_{\phi,\rho_{*}}
are both product measures, direct calculations yield that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\mathbb{E}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big[\log\frac{d\nu^{n}_{\rho_{*}}}{d\nu^{n}_{\phi,\rho_{*}}}\Big|\mu^{n}_{\cdot}\in O\Big]=-\frac{1}{2\chi(\rho_{*})}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}=-\mathcal{Q}_{0}(\mu_{0}),

see
[
14
]
for details.

Finally, we conclude the proof of the lower bound by (
4.3
) and the above estimates.

4.3.
Proof of Proposition
4.1

Let
\pi^{n}_{t}
be the distribution of the process at time
t
, and introduce the reference measure
\nu^{n}_{t}
as the product measure on
\Omega_{n}
with marginals

\nu^{n}_{t}(\eta_{x}=1)=\rho^{n}_{x},\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{n},

where we shorten

\rho^{n}_{x}:=\rho^{n}(t,\tfrac{x}{n}):=\rho_{*}+\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\rho(t,\tfrac{x}{n}).

Recall the relative entropy of
\pi^{n}_{t}
with respect to
\nu^{n}_{t}
is defined as

\mathcal{H}_{n}(t):=\mathcal{H}(\pi^{n}_{t}|\nu^{n}_{t})=\int f^{n}_{t}\log f^{n}_{t}d\nu^{n}_{t},\quad f^{n}_{t}=d\pi^{n}_{t}/d\nu^{n}_{t}.

The main aim of this section is to prove the following result.

Proposition 4.4
.

For any
0\leq t\leq T
,
\mathcal{H}_{n}(t)\ll a_{n}^{2}/n^{d}
.

We first prove the hydrodynamic limits from the above result.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
from Proposition
4.4
.

By the entropy inequality, it is directly from the above proposition that, for any
0\leq t\leq T
, for any
H\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},H\big\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho(t,u)H(u)du

in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}
-probability, see
[
11
, Proof of Theorem 2.2]
for example. Thus, to show the sample-path convergence, we only need to show
\{\mu^{n}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}
is tight in the space
D([0,T],\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
. It suffices to show that, for any
H\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big|\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t},H\big\rangle\big|>M\Big)=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}^{n}_{H,\phi}\Big(\sup_{|t-s|\leq\delta}\big|\big\langle\mu^{n}_{t}-\mu^{n}_{s},H\big\rangle\big|>\varepsilon\Big)=0.

The above two estimates follows immediately from (
3.14
), (
3.15
) and Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Now, we prove Proposition
4.4
. By Yau’s relative entropy inequality
[
5
]
,

\mathcal{H}_{n}^{\prime}(t)\leq\int\Big(L_{n,t}^{H,*}\mathbf{1}-\partial_{t}\log\nu^{n}_{t}\Big)f^{n}_{t}d\nu^{n}_{t}-\int\Gamma_{n,t}^{H}(\sqrt{f^{n}_{t}})d\nu^{n}_{t},

where
L_{n,t}^{H,*}
is the adjoint of
L_{n,t}^{H}
in
L^{2}(\nu^{n}_{t})
, and
\Gamma_{n,t}^{H}(f)=\Gamma_{n,t}^{H,{\rm ex}}(f)+\Gamma_{n,t}^{H,{\rm r}}(f)
, where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n,t}^{H,{\rm ex}}(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\exp\big\{\tfrac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}(\eta_{x}-\eta_{x+e_{i}})\big(H_{t}(\tfrac{x+e_{i}}{n})-H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big)\big\}\big[f(\eta^{x,x+e_{i}})-f(\eta)\big]^{2},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n,t}^{H,{\rm r}}(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}c_{x}(\eta)\exp\big\{\tfrac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}(1-2\eta_{x})H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\big\}\big[f(\eta^{x})-f(\eta)\big]^{2}.

By Lemma
3.5
,
L_{n,t}^{H,*}\mathbf{1}=A_{n}(t)+B_{n}(t)
, where
A_{n}(t)

corresponds to the dynamics of the exclusion process and equals

\displaystyle n^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big\{\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})\Big[\exp\Big\{-\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d+1}}\nabla_{n,i}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big\}\frac{\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})}{\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})}-\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d+1}}\nabla_{n,i}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle+\eta_{x+e_{i}}(1-\eta_{x})\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d+1}}\nabla_{n,i}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big\}\frac{\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})}{\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})}-\exp\Big\{-\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d+1}}\nabla_{n,i}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big\}\Big]\Big\},

and the Glauber part
B_{n}(t)
equals

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\Big\{\eta_{x}\Big[\Big(a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big)\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big\}\frac{1-\rho^{n}_{x}}{\rho^{n}_{x}}-b\exp\Big\{-\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle+(1-\eta_{x})\Big[b\exp\Big\{-\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big\}\frac{\rho^{n}_{x}}{1-\rho^{n}_{x}}-\Big(a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big)\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big\}\Big]\Big\}.

The time derivative equals

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\log\nu^{n}_{t}=\partial_{t}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\Big[(1-\eta_{x})\log(1-\rho^{n}_{x})+\eta_{x}\log\rho^{n}_{x}\Big]=\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}w_{x}\partial_{t}\rho(t,\tfrac{x}{n}),

where

w_{x}:=w^{n}(t,x;\eta):=\frac{\eta_{x}-\rho^{n}_{x}}{\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})}.

Next, we deal with the term
A_{n}(t)
. By Taylor’s expansion, we write
A_{n}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}A_{n,i}(t)+\mathcal{O}_{H}(a_{n}^{3}/n^{2d+1})
, where

\displaystyle A_{n,1}(t)

\displaystyle=n^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big\{\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})\Big[\frac{\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})}{\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})}-1\Big]+\eta_{x+e_{i}}(1-\eta_{x})\Big[\frac{\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})}{\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})}-1\Big]\Big\},

\displaystyle A_{n,2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n}n^{2}}{n^{d+1}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla_{n,i}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big\{\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})\Big[-\frac{\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})}{\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})}-1\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\eta_{x+e_{i}}(1-\eta_{x})\Big[\frac{\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})}{\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})}+1\Big]\Big\},

\displaystyle A_{n,3}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n}^{2}n^{2}}{2n^{2d+2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big(\nabla_{n,i}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big)^{2}\Big\{\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})\Big[\frac{\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})}{\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})}-1\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\eta_{x+e_{i}}(1-\eta_{x})\Big[\frac{\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})}{\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})}-1\Big]\Big\}.

Note that
\mathcal{O}_{H}(a_{n}^{3}/n^{2d+1})\ll a_{n}^{2}/n^{d}
. For
A_{n,1}(t)
, we first write the term inside the brace as

\displaystyle(\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}-\rho^{n}_{x})\Big[\frac{\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})}{\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})}-\frac{\eta_{x+e_{i}}(1-\eta_{x})}{\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})}\Big]=(\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}-\rho^{n}_{x})\big[w_{x}-w_{x+e_{i}}-(\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}-\rho^{n}_{x})w_{x}w_{x+e_{i}}\big],

then, using the summation by parts formula,

\displaystyle A_{n,1}(t)=\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}w_{x}\Delta_{n}\rho(t,\tfrac{x}{n})-\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{2d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big(\nabla_{n,i}\rho(t,\tfrac{x}{n})\Big)^{2}w_{x}w_{x+e_{i}}.

By Lemmas
3.6
and
4.3
, it is easy to see that

(4.4)

\int_{0}^{t}E_{\pi^{n}_{s}}\Big[\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{2d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big(\nabla_{n,i}\rho(s,\tfrac{x}{n})\Big)^{2}w_{x}w_{x+e_{i}}\Big]ds\ll\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}.

Similarly, since
|\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}-\rho^{n}_{x}|\leq Ca_{n}/n^{d+1}
,

|A_{n,3}(t)|\leq\frac{C(H)a_{n}^{2}}{n^{2d}}\times n^{d}\times\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d+1}}=\frac{C(H)a_{n}^{3}}{n^{2d+1}}\ll\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{d}}.

For
A_{n,2}(t)
, we write the term inside the brace as

\displaystyle\Big[\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})+\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})\Big]\Big[\frac{\eta_{x+e_{i}}(1-\eta_{x})}{\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})}-\frac{\eta_{x}(1-\eta_{x+e_{i}})}{\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})+\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})\Big]\Big[w_{x+e_{i}}-w_{x}+(\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}-\rho^{n}_{x})w_{x}w_{x+e_{i}}\Big].

Thus,

\displaystyle A_{n,2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d-1}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla_{n,i}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big[\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})+\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})\Big](w_{x+e_{i}}-w_{x})

\displaystyle+\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{2d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla_{n,i}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big[\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})+\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})\Big]\nabla_{n,i}\rho(t,\tfrac{x}{n})w_{x}w_{x+e_{i}}.

By Lemmas
3.6
and
4.3
, the last line satisfies the estimate in (
4.4
). For the first line in the expression of
A_{n,2}(t)
, first replacing
\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})+\rho^{n}_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x+e_{i}})
with
2\chi(\rho_{*})
plus an error term of order
a_{n}/n^{d}
, then using the summation by parts formula, and finally by Lemmas
3.1
and (
4.3
),

A_{n,2}(t)=-2\chi(\rho_{*})\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}w_{x}\Delta_{n}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})

plus an error term satisfying the estimate in (
4.4
). To sum up, we have shown that

A_{n}(t)=\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}w_{x}\Big(\Delta_{n}\rho(t,\tfrac{x}{n})-2\chi(\rho_{*})\Delta_{n}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big)

plus an error term satisfying the estimate in (
4.4
).

Now, we deal with the term
B_{n}(t)
. By Taylor’s expansion,
B_{n}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}B_{n,i}(t)+\mathcal{O}_{H}(a_{n}^{3}/n^{2d})
, where

\displaystyle B_{n,1}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\Big\{\eta_{x}\Big[\Big(a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big)\frac{1-\rho^{n}_{x}}{\rho^{n}_{x}}-b\Big]+(1-\eta_{x})\Big[b\frac{\rho^{n}_{x}}{1-\rho^{n}_{x}}-\Big(a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big)\Big]\Big\},

\displaystyle B_{n,2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big\{\eta_{x}\Big[\Big(a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big)\frac{1-\rho^{n}_{x}}{\rho^{n}_{x}}+b\Big]-(1-\eta_{x})\Big[b\frac{\rho^{n}_{x}}{1-\rho^{n}_{x}}+\Big(a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big)\Big]\Big\},

\displaystyle B_{n,3}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{2n^{2d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}H_{t}^{2}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big\{\eta_{x}\Big[\Big(a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big)\frac{1-\rho^{n}_{x}}{\rho^{n}_{x}}-b\Big]+(1-\eta_{x})\Big[b\frac{\rho^{n}_{x}}{1-\rho^{n}_{x}}-\Big(a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big)\Big]\Big\}.

For
B_{n,1}(t)
, we write the term inside the brace as

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\eta_{x}}{\rho^{n}_{x}}-\frac{1-\eta_{x}}{1-\rho^{n}_{x}}\Big)\Big[\Big(a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big)(1-\rho^{n}_{x})-b\rho^{n}_{x}\Big]

\displaystyle=w_{x}\Big[F(\rho^{n}_{x})+\frac{\lambda(1-\rho^{n}_{x})}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\big\{(\eta_{y}-\rho^{n}_{y})+(\rho^{n}_{y}-\rho^{n}_{x})\big\}\Big].

Since
F(\rho^{n}_{x})=F^{\prime}(\rho_{*})a_{n}\rho(t,\tfrac{x}{n})/n^{d}+\mathcal{O}(a_{n}^{2}/n^{2d})
, by Lemmas
3.1
and
4.3
,

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}w_{x}F(\rho^{n}_{x})=\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}F^{\prime}(\rho_{*})w_{x}\rho(t,\tfrac{x}{n})

plus an error term satisfying the estimate in (
4.4
). Similarly, the term

\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})w_{x}\sum_{y\sim x}(\rho^{n}_{y}-\rho^{n}_{x})=\frac{\lambda a_{n}}{2dn^{d+2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})w_{x}\Delta_{n}\rho(t,\tfrac{x}{n})

also satisfies the estimate in (
4.4
). We are left with the term

\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{y\sim x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})w_{x}(\eta_{y}-\rho^{n}_{y})=\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{y\sim x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})\chi(\rho^{n}_{y})w_{x}w_{y}.

Note that we cannot use Lemma
3.6
to deal with the above term since
a_{n}^{2}/n^{d}\ll a_{n}
. Instead, we need the following version of the main lemma, see
[
5
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Proposition 4.5

(The second main lemma)
.

There exists some constant
C=C(\lambda,d,\rho_{*})
such that

\int\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}\sum_{y\sim x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})\chi(\rho^{n}_{y})w_{x}w_{y}f^{n}_{t}d\nu^{n}_{t}\leq\frac{1}{4}\int\Gamma_{n,t}^{H,{\rm ex}}(\sqrt{f^{n}_{t}})d\nu^{n}_{t}+C\big(\mathcal{H}_{n}(t)+n^{d-2}g_{d}(n)\big).

Since
n^{d-1}\sqrt{g_{d}(n)}\ll a_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}E_{\pi^{n}_{s}}\big[B_{n,1}(s)\big]ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}E_{\pi^{n}_{s}}\Big[\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}F^{\prime}(\rho_{*})w_{x}\rho(s,\tfrac{x}{n})\Big]ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}ds\Big\{\int\Gamma_{n,s}^{H,{\rm ex}}(\sqrt{f^{n}_{s}})d\nu^{n}_{s}\Big\}+\frac{Cn^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}_{n}(s)ds+o_{n}(1).

Comparing the expression of
B_{n,3}(t)
with
B_{n,1}(t)
, it is immediately that
B_{n,3}(t)
satisfies the estimate in (
4.4
). It remains to deal with
B_{n,2}(t)
. We first write the term inside the brace as

\displaystyle w_{x}\Big[\Big(a+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{y}\Big)(1-\rho_{x}^{n})+b\rho^{n}_{x}\Big]=w_{x}G(\rho^{n}_{x})+\frac{\lambda}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})[\eta_{y}-\rho^{n}_{x}].

By Taylor’s expansion, Lemmas
3.1
and
4.3
,

\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})w_{x}G(\rho^{n}_{x})=\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})w_{x}G(\rho_{*})

plus an error term satisfying the estimate in (
4.4
). For the remaining term in
B_{n,2}(t)
, we write

\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\sum_{y\sim x}w_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})[\eta_{y}-\rho^{n}_{x}]

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n}}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})\sum_{y\sim x}w_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})[\eta_{y}-\rho^{n}_{y}]+\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{n^{2d+2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}H_{t}(\tfrac{x}{n})w_{x}(1-\rho^{n}_{x})\Delta_{n}\rho(t,\tfrac{x}{n}).

Note that the first term above can be dealt with by Proposition
4.5
and the second one has order
o(a_{n}^{2}/n^{d})
. Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}E_{\pi^{n}_{s}}\big[B_{n,2}(s)\big]ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}E_{\pi^{n}_{s}}\Big[\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}G(\rho_{*})w_{x}H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big]ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4a_{n}}\int_{0}^{t}ds\Big\{\int\Gamma_{n,s}^{H,{\rm ex}}(\sqrt{f^{n}_{s}})d\nu^{n}_{s}\Big\}+\frac{Cn^{d}}{a_{n}^{3}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}_{n}(s)ds+o_{n}(1).

To sum up, we have shown that

\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}E_{\pi^{n}_{s}}\big[B_{n}(s)\big]ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}E_{\pi^{n}_{s}}\Big[\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}w_{x}\Big(F^{\prime}(\rho_{*})\rho(s,\tfrac{x}{n})+G(\rho_{*})H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})\Big)\Big]ds\\
+\frac{1}{4}\Big(1+\frac{1}{a_{n}}\Big)\int_{0}^{t}ds\Big\{\int\Gamma_{n,s}^{H,{\rm ex}}(\sqrt{f^{n}_{s}})d\nu^{n}_{s}\Big\}+\frac{Cn^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{a_{n}}\Big)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}_{n}(s)ds+o_{n}(1).

Adding up the above estimates, for
n
large enough such that
1+1/a_{n}<2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\mathcal{H}_{n}(t)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\mathcal{H}_{n}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}E_{\pi^{n}_{s}}\Big[\frac{1}{a_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{n}^{d}}w_{x}\Big(\big(\Delta_{n}+F^{\prime}(\rho_{*})\big)\rho(s,\tfrac{x}{n})-2\chi(\rho_{*})\Delta_{n}H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})

\displaystyle+G(\rho_{*})H_{s}(\tfrac{x}{n})-\partial_{s}\rho(s,\tfrac{x}{n})\Big)\Big]ds+2C\int_{0}^{t}\frac{n^{d}}{a_{n}^{2}}\mathcal{H}_{n}(s)ds+o_{n}(1).

Since
\rho(t,u)
is the solution to (
4.1
), together with Lemmas
3.1
and Lemma
4.3
, the above time integral has order
o_{n}(1)
. We finally conclude the proof of Proposition
4.4
by noting that
\mathcal{H}_{n}(0)=0
and by using Grönwall’s inequality.

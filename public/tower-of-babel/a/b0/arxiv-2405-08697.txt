Color Chiral Cherenkov radiation and energy loss in quark-gluon plasma
Jeremy Hansen, Kirill Tuchin

Abstract
We introduce and investigate the Color Chiral Cherenkov effect which consists in radiation of the circularly polarized gluons by a fast color charge moving with constant velocity in the presence of the Chiral Magnetic current. We derive the transition rates for all gluon polarizations. We compute the contribution of the Color Chiral Cherenkov effect to the parton energy loss in the quark-gluon plasma.



I
Introduction

A fast particle carrying electric charge and moving in a medium with chiral fermions can lose a significant portion of its energy by means of the Chiral Cherenkov radiation
Tuchin 2018a
;
Huang and Tuchin 2018
;
Hansen and Tuchin 2021
;
Tuchin 2018b
. It is emitted due to the unique dispersion relation of the electromagnetic field and is closely related to the chiral magnetic
Kharzeev 2006
;
Kharzeev et al. 2008
;
Kharzeev 2010
;
Kharzeev and Zhitnitsky 2007
;
Fukushima et al. 2008
and anomalous Hall effects
Klinkhamer and Volovik 2005
;
Zyuzin and Burkov 2012
;
Grushin 2012
;
Kharzeev and Zhitnitsky 2007
;
Kharzeev 2014
. Thus far all studies of the Chiral Cherenkov radiation focused on the media governed by the Quantum Electrodynamics because of possible technological applications. However, it is clear that the color version of the Chiral Cherenkov effect is expected to give a significant contribution to the energy loss in strongly interacting media with chiral fermions. We therefore set in this paper to derive the color version of the Chiral Cherenkov effect and estimate its magnitude in the quark-gluon plasma.

The color version of the chiral magnetic effect—the induction of the color current in the direction of the color magnetic field—can be described by letting the
\theta
-angle slowly vary with time. The gluon field excitations in the chiral medium supporting the chiral magnetic current are governed by the Lagrangian
Wilczek 1987
;
Carroll et al. 1990
;
Sikivie 1984
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\Tr\left(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\right)-\frac{c_{A}}{2}\theta\Tr\left(F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\right)\,,

(1)

where the field tensor is

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}-ig[A_{\mu},A_{\nu}]\,.

(2)

A_{\mu}=A_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}
and
F_{\mu\nu}=F_{\mu\nu}^{a}t^{a}
, where
t^{a}
are the SU(3) generators.
\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}F^{\lambda\rho}
is the dual field tensor. The external pseudo-scalar field
\theta
is sourced by the topological charge and
c_{A}
is the anomaly coefficient.

The equations of motion derived from the Lagrangian (
1
) depend only on the gradient
\partial_{\mu}\theta
. We adopt a model of the quark-gluon plasma with spatially homogeneous and slowly time dependent
\theta
:
c_{A}\partial_{\mu}\theta=b_{0}\delta_{\mu 0}
, where
b_{0}
is the (constant) chiral magnetic conductivity.
As a result the chiral magnetic current
{\bm{j}}^{a}=b_{0}{\bm{B}}^{a}
emerges as a source of the color magnetic field in Amper’s law. That
\theta
is a slow function of time is indicated by the parametrically large
sphaleron transition time
1/(g^{4}T)
at a given plasma temperature
T

Arnold et al. 1997
;
Arnold et al. 1999
;
Bodeker 1998
.

The chiral magnetic current modifies the spectrum of the gluon excitations, which can be found by solving the equations of motion without the self-interaction terms:

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+b_{\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}=0\,,

(3)

along with the Bianchi identity. In the radiation gauge, the corresponding vector potential obeys the equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}{\bm{A}}-\nabla^{2}{\bm{A}}=b_{0}({\bm{\nabla}}\times{\bm{A}})\,.

(4)

The plane wave solutions of (
4
) are circularly polarized and have the dispersion relation

\displaystyle\omega^{2}={\bm{k}}^{2}-\lambda b_{0}|{\bm{k}}|\,,

(5)

where
k=(\omega,{\bm{k}})
and
\lambda=\pm 1
indicate the right or left polarization. With the account of the screening effects the
in-medium gluons have the dispersion relation

\displaystyle\omega^{2}={\bm{k}}^{2}+\mu^{2}({\bm{k}})={\bm{k}}^{2}-\lambda b_{0}|{\bm{k}}|+\omega_{p}^{2}\,,

(6)

where
\mu
is the gluon mass parameter and
\omega_{p}
is the plasma frequency. Actually, Eq. (
6
) is the short wavelength limit of the full dispersion relation
Akamatsu and Yamamoto 2013
;
Manuel and Torres-Rincon 2014
, which nevertheless suffices for the present calculation.
Throughout the paper we assume that
b_{0}
is positive. Since Eq. (
6
) scales with
\lambda b_{0}
, the negative
b_{0}
case can be obtained by the replacement
b_{0}\to-b_{0}
and
\lambda\to-\lambda
.

The chiral term in the gluon dispersion relation generates the spacelike gluon mode
\omega^{2}-{\bm{k}}^{2}<0
which opens the possibility for novel
1\to 2
processes that are otherwise prohibited in QCD by energy and momentum conservation. This effect is analogous to the Cherenkov radiation where the spacelike excitations of the electromagnetic field produced by a particle moving at a speed greater than the phase velocity of light, represent the propagating wave solution in dielectric materials. In chiral media, the chiral conductivity effectively contributes to the medium dielectric response making possible excitation of the gauge field wave. This effect is referred to as the Chiral Cherenkov radiation. We refer to its QCD version as the Color Chiral Cherenkov radiation.

Unlike QED, where the chrial Cherenkov radiation is described by a single diagram
e\to e\gamma
, where the photon dispersion relation is modified by the anomaly, in QCD there are two possible channels depicted in Fig.
1
. The first of these channels
q\to qg
is quasi-Abelian. The corresponding emission rate can be derived from the Abelian expression by including the appropriate color factors. We performed this calculation in

Hansen and Tuchin 2021
. The novel channel is
g\to gg
where all three gluons are excitations of the chiral medium. It is the focus of the present article.

The paper is structured as follows. The next section deals with the Feynman rule for the triple-gluon vertex stemming from the second term in the right-hand-side of (
1
). The main result, namely, the transition rates for all quark and gluon polarization states is derived in Sec.
III
. Sec.
IV
discusses the energy loss due to the Color Chiral Cherenkov radiation. The summary and outlook are presented in Sec.
V
.

II
The triple-gluon vertex

The diagrams contributing to the Color Chiral Cherenkov radiation are depicted in Fig.
1
. Whereas the anomaly does not affect the Feynman rule of the fermion-gluon vertex, it does modify the triple-gluon one. To derive the rule we
write in the second term in (
1
)

\displaystyle\Tr\left(F^{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\right)=2\partial_{\mu}K^{\mu}

(7)

where

\displaystyle K^{\mu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\Tr\left(A_{\nu}\partial_{\rho}A_{\sigma}-g\frac{2i}{3}A_{\nu}A_{\rho}A_{\sigma}\right)

(8)

and integrate by parts the corresponding term in the action. The result is

\displaystyle S_{\theta}=c_{A}\int\partial_{\mu}\theta\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{2}A^{a}_{\nu}\partial_{\rho}A^{a}_{\sigma}-g\frac{2i}{3}\frac{1}{4}if^{abc}A^{a}_{\nu}A^{b}_{\rho}A^{c}_{\sigma}\right)d^{4}x\,.

(9)

The first term in (
9
) contributes to the equation of motion (
3
), while the second one to the triple-gluon vertex. The variation of action
S_{\theta}
with respect to three gluon fields produces the Feynman rule for the anomalous contribution to the triple-gluon vertex in momentum space:

\displaystyle\text{anomalous triple-gluon vertex}=gb_{\mu}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}f^{abc}\,,

(10)

where we follow the conventions of
Peskin and Schroeder 1995
. This is the only new Feynman rule due to the anomalous term in the Lagrangian (
1
). The red circle in Fig.
1
includes both the conventional and anomalous contributions.

III
The chiral Cherenkov radiation rate

The radiation rates can be computed as

\displaystyle dW_{a\to bc}=\frac{g^{2}C_{a\to bc}}{2(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{ss^{\prime}}\delta(\omega+E^{\prime}-E)\delta({\bm{k}}+{\bm{p}}^{\prime}-{\bm{p}})\frac{1}{8EE^{\prime}\omega}|\mathcal{M}_{a\to bc}|^{2}d^{3}p^{\prime}\,d^{3}k\,,

(11)

where the sum runs over the fermion polarization states,
\mathcal{M}_{a\to bc}
are the amplitudes without color generators
t^{a}
and the structure constants
f^{abc}
, and
C_{a\to bc}
are the color factors. The color factors are given by

\displaystyle C_{q\to qg}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\tr(t^{a}t^{a})=\frac{N_{c}^{2}-1}{2N_{c}}=\frac{4}{3}\,,

(12)

\displaystyle C_{g\to gg}

\displaystyle=\frac{(f^{abc})^{2}}{N_{c}^{2}-1}=N_{c}=3\,.

(13)

The matrix elements read:

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}_{q\to qg}

\displaystyle=i\bar{u}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}s^{\prime}}\not{e}^{*}_{{\bm{k}}\lambda}u_{{\bm{p}}s}\,,

(14)

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}_{g\to gg}

\displaystyle=i\mathcal{M}^{A}_{g\to gg}+i\mathcal{M}^{B}_{g\to gg}

\displaystyle=(e_{{\bm{p}}\lambda_{0}}\cdot e^{*}_{{\bm{k}}\lambda})(p+k)\cdot e^{*}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}+(e^{*}_{{\bm{k}}\lambda}\cdot e^{*}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}})(p^{\prime}-k)\cdot e_{{\bm{p}}\lambda_{0}}-(e_{{\bm{p}}\lambda_{0}}\cdot e^{*}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}})(p+p^{\prime})\cdot e^{*}_{{\bm{k}}\lambda}

\displaystyle-ib_{\mu}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}e_{{\bm{p}}\lambda_{0},\nu}e^{*}_{{\bm{k}}\lambda,\rho}e^{*}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\sigma}\,,

(15)

where
e_{{\bm{p}}\lambda\mu}

A
,
B
refer to the second and third lines of (
15
) respectively. Note that not just the last line in (
15
), but all terms in (
14
) and (
15
) depend on the anomaly by the way of the dispersion relation (
6
).

We perform the calculation in the high-energy approximation, meaning that the momenta of all particles along the jet axis are much larger than the transverse momenta and the effective mass. Let
z
be the jet axis, then

\displaystyle p=\left(E,0,0,E-\frac{k_{\perp}^{2}+x(1-x)\mu^{2}(E)}{2x(1-x)E}\right)\,,

(16a)

\displaystyle k=\left(\omega,k_{\perp},0,\omega-\frac{k_{\perp}^{2}+\mu^{2}(\omega)}{2\omega}\right)\,,

(16b)

\displaystyle p^{\prime}=\left(E^{\prime},-k_{\perp},0,E^{\prime}-\frac{k_{\perp}^{2}+\mu^{2}(E^{\prime})}{2E^{\prime}}\right)\,,

(16c)

where the mass parameter in (
6
) becomes
\mu^{2}(\omega)\approx\omega_{p}^{2}-\lambda b_{0}\omega
and
x=\omega/E
is the fraction of energy of the incident parton carried away by the gluon
k
. Eqs. (
16

k_{\bot}\ll E,E^{\prime},\omega
and
|\mu|\ll E,E^{\prime},\omega
. The latter condition is equivalent to
b_{0}\ll E,E^{\prime},\omega
.

Given (
16

\displaystyle e_{p\lambda_{0}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,1,\lambda_{0}i,0)\,,

(17a)

\displaystyle e_{k\lambda}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(0,1,\lambda i,-\frac{k_{\perp}}{\omega}\right)\,,

(17b)

\displaystyle e_{p^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(0,1,\lambda^{\prime}i,\frac{k_{\perp}}{E^{\prime}}\right)\,,

(17c)

The integral over the
{\bm{p}}^{\prime}
in (
11
) is trivial considering the delta-function expressing the momentum conservation. The remaining delta-function in
q\to qg
rate reads:

\displaystyle\delta(E^{\prime}+\omega-E)

\displaystyle=2x(1-x)E\delta\left[k_{\bot}^{2}+\mu^{2}(\omega)(1-x)+m^{2}x^{2}\right]\,,

\displaystyle=2x(1-x)E\delta\left[k_{\bot}^{2}+(\omega_{p}^{2}-\lambda b_{0}xE)(1-x)+m^{2}x^{2}\right]\,.

(18)

Clearly, the argument of this delta-function can vanish only if
\lambda b_{0}>0
. We assumed in Introduction that
b_{0}>0
. Therefore, only the right-polarized gluons
\lambda>0
can be radiated by the incident quark. Moreover, the energy conservation, expressed by the argument of the delta-function (
III
), can be satisfied only if

\displaystyle x_{-}^{q\to qg}<x<x_{+}^{q\to qg}\,,

(19)

where

\displaystyle x_{\pm}^{q\to qg}=\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}+\lambda b_{0}E\pm\sqrt{(\omega_{p}^{2}-\lambda b_{0}E)^{2}-4m^{2}\omega_{p}^{2}}}{2(m^{2}+\lambda b_{0}E)}\,.

(20)

The requirement that (
20
) have real values sets the infrared threshold for the energy of the incident quark:

\displaystyle E>E_{1}=\frac{2m\omega_{p}+\omega_{p}^{2}}{b_{0}}\,.

(21)

The delta-function in
g\to gg
rate reads:

\displaystyle\delta(E^{\prime}+\omega-E)

\displaystyle=2x(1-x)E\delta\left[k_{\perp}^{2}+x\mu^{2}(E^{\prime})+(1-x)\mu^{2}(\omega)-x(1-x)\mu^{2}(E)\right]

\displaystyle=2x(1-x)E\delta\left[k_{\perp}^{2}+\omega_{p}^{2}(1-x+x^{2})-b_{0}E(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{0})x(1-x)\right]\,.

(22)

In this case the gluon emission is possible only if
\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda_{0}
. Apparently only the following four channels are allowed:
g_{L}\to g_{R}g_{L}

g_{L}\to g_{L}g_{R}

g_{R}\to g_{R}g_{R}

g_{L}\to g_{R}g_{R}

\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{0}=1

\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{0}=3

\displaystyle x_{-}^{g\to gg}<x<x_{+}^{g\to gg}\,,

(23)

where

\displaystyle x_{\pm}^{g\to gg}=\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}+(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{0})b_{0}E\pm\sqrt{(\omega_{p}^{2}-(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{0})b_{0}E)^{2}-4\omega_{p}^{4}}}{2(\omega_{p}^{2}+(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{0})b_{0}E)}\,.

(24)

The requirement that (

\displaystyle E>E_{2}=\frac{3\omega_{p}^{2}}{b_{0}}\,.

(25)

The amplitude

\displaystyle\sum_{ss^{\prime}}|\mathcal{M}_{q\to qg_{R}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=4\left[EE^{\prime}-m^{2}-\frac{({\bm{k}}\cdot{{\bm{p}}})({\bm{k}}\cdot{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}})}{{\bm{k}}^{2}}\right]\,,

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{ss^{\prime}}|\mathcal{M}_{q\to qg_{L}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(27)

For the gluon splitting amplitudes we find

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}^{A}_{g_{R}\rightarrow g_{R}g_{R}}=\frac{k_{\perp}}{x(1-x)}\,,

(28a)

\displaystyle iM^{A}_{g_{L}\rightarrow g_{R}g_{L}}=\frac{(1-x)k_{\perp}}{x}\,,

(28b)

\displaystyle iM^{A}_{g_{L}\rightarrow g_{L}g_{R}}=\frac{xk_{\perp}}{(1-x)}\,,

(28c)

\displaystyle iM^{A}_{g_{L}\rightarrow g_{R}g_{R}}=\mathcal{O}(k_{\perp}^{2}/E,b_{0}/E)\,,

(28d)

\displaystyle iM^{B}_{g\rightarrow gg}=\frac{-b_{0}k_{\perp}(\lambda(1-x)+\lambda^{\prime}x+\lambda_{0})}{x(1-x)E}\,.

(28e)

\displaystyle\frac{dW_{q\rightarrow qg}}{dk_{\perp}^{2}dx}=\frac{\alpha_{s}g^{2}}{3x^{2}(1-x)E}\left[(1+(1-x)^{2})k_{\perp}^{2}+m^{2}x^{4}\right]\delta\left(k_{\perp}^{2}+\mu^{2}(1-x)+m^{2}x^{2}\right)\,,

(29b)

\displaystyle\frac{dW_{g_{L}\rightarrow gg}}{dk_{\perp}^{2}dx}=\frac{3\alpha_{s}g^{2}}{2E}\left[\frac{(1-x)^{2}}{x^{2}}+\frac{x^{2}}{(1-x)^{2}}\right]k_{\perp}^{2}\delta\left(k_{\perp}^{2}+\omega_{p}^{2}x^{2}+(\omega_{p}^{2}-b_{0}\omega)(1-x)\right)\,.

(29c)

One can easily identify in (

Integrating over the transverse momentum

\displaystyle\frac{dW_{q\rightarrow qg}}{dx}=\frac{\alpha_{s}g^{2}}{3x^{2}E}\left\{\left[1+(1-x)^{2}\right]\left(b_{0}xE-\omega_{p}^{2}\right)-2m^{2}x^{2}\right\}\Theta\left(x^{q\to qg}_{+}-x\right)\Theta\left(x-x^{q\to qg}_{-}\right)\,,

(30a)

\displaystyle\frac{dW_{g_{\lambda_{0}}\rightarrow gg}}{dx}=\frac{3\alpha_{s}g^{2}}{4x^{2}(1-x)^{2}E}\left\{\left(b_{0}Ex-\omega_{p}^{2}\right)(1-x)-\omega_{p}^{2}x^{2}\right\}\left\{\left[x^{4}+(1-x)^{4}\right]\delta_{\lambda_{0},-1}+\delta_{\lambda_{0},1}\right\}

Here

\omega_{p}^{2}=\frac{g^{2}T^{2}}{18}(2N_{c}+N_{f})

V
Summary and outlook

We investigated the Color Chiral Cherenkov radiation—the QCD analogue of the Chiral Cherenkov effect in QED—and applied the results to study its role in the energy loss and jet quenching in the quark-gluon plasma. Our main result is
the gluon emission rates (
30
) by quark and gluons in the chiral medium. The resulting energy loss is given by Eqs. (
32
).

A number of approximations were made to derive these results. Firstly, we assumed that the medium is homogeneous such that
{\bm{b}}=c_{A}{\bm{\nabla}}\theta=0
. One can consider a complimentary scenario where
{\bm{b}}
is finite and
b_{0}
vanishes. The corresponding rate of gluon emission by a fast quark can be obtained similarly to QED, by substituting
b_{0}\rightarrow|{\bm{b}}|\cos{\beta}
where
\beta
into (
30
) is the angle between
{\bm{b}}
and the outgoing gluon
Tuchin 2018a
;
Tuchin 2018b
. Gluon radiation by a fast gluon at finite
{\bm{b}}
, on the other hand, requires further analysis. However, it should be noted that while
\mathcal{M}^{B}_{g\to gg}
gives a sub-relativistic correction in a homogeneous medium, it gives no contribution at all in the inhomogeneous case. Indeed, the scattering amplitude
\mathcal{M}^{B}_{g\to gg}=-ib_{\mu}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}e_{{\bm{p}}\lambda_{0},\nu}e^{*}_{{\bm{k}}\lambda,\rho}e^{*}_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\sigma}
is antisymmetric with respect to exchange of
b_{\mu}
and any polarization vector. As such if
b_{0}=0
, then
\mathcal{M}^{B}_{g\to gg}
must be proportional to the zeroth component of one of the polarization vectors. Therefore, given that the zeroth component is zero (see (
17
)),
\mathcal{M}^{B}_{g\to gg}
vanishes.

Secondly, we assumed that the radiating particle is ultra-relativistic throughout the entire process. As a result, the spectrum in the quark channel exhibits discontinuous behavior at
x=x^{q\to qg}_{+}
, as can be seen in Fig.
2
. By including non-relativistic corrections for the outgoing particle, one may expect the rate of gluon emission to drop smoothly to zero as
x
tends to 1. This correction has very little effect on the rate of energy loss, however it may play a role in parton evolution of jet.

Comparing the decay rates of the left-handed and right-handed gluons in Fig.
2
, one finds that in a medium with
b_{0}>0
, the left-handed gluons decay more slowly when compared to right-handed ones. As a result jets develop strong left-hand polarziation. This is the clearest manifestation of the chiral anomaly in the jet structure.

The chiral imbalance is also imprinted into the jet loss pattern seen in Fig.
3
: the right-handed gluons loose a lot more energy than the left-handed ones. However, as the jet energy increases, the difference between the gluon polarizations becomes less pronounced since the energy loss is driven primarily by the large polarization independent logarithms in (
33
). These logarithms also enhance the relative contribution of gluons as compared to quarks to the energy loss at high energies. In contrast, at low energy, the energy loss is dominated by quarks as seen in Fig.
3
. We stress again that these conclusions hold only for energy loss due to the Color Chiral Cherenkov radiation, ignored all other contributions. A recent review of the conventional mechanisms of energy loss can be found in
Cao and Wang 2021
.

In conclusion, energy loss due to the Color Chiral Cherenkov radiation is significant. Therefore a comprehensive phenomenological analysis requires incorporation of the novel energy loss channels into the numerical packages describing jets in hot nuclear medium.

Abstract
. For a prime p p and a commutative ring R R with unity, let W ⁡ ( R ) W(R) denote the group of p p -typical Witt vectors. The group W ⁡ ( R ) W(R) is endowed with a Verschiebung operator W ⁡ ( R ) → 𝑉 W ⁡ ( R ) W(R)\xrightarrow{V}W(R) and a Teichmüller map R → ⟨ ⟩ W ⁡ ( R ) R\xrightarrow{\langle{\ }\rangle}W(R) . One of the properties satisfied by V , ⟨ ⟩ V,\langle{\ }\rangle is that the map R → W ⁡ ( R ) R\to W(R) given by x ↦ V ⁡ ⟨ x p ⟩ − p ⁡ ⟨ x ⟩ x\mapsto V\langle{x^{p}}\rangle-p\langle{x}\rangle is an additive map. In this paper we show that for p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 , this property essentially characterises the functor W W (Theorem 1.6 ). Unlike other characterisations (see [ 1 ] , [ 7 ] ), this is a group-theoretic characterisation, in the sense that it does not use the ring structure of W ⁡ ( R ) W(R) . Most constructions of the group of p p -typical Witt vectors of non-commutative rings do not have a ring structure, and hence the above characterisation is more suitable for generalisation to the non-commutative setup.

Introduction

]

1.1
.

For a commutative ring

-

(3)

(4)

The above properties do not use the ring structure on

We now abstract the properties

in the following definition.

Definition 1.3

(pre-Witt functor)
.

A functor
F:{\mathsf{ComRings}}\to\mathsf{Ab}
is called a pre-Witt functor, if for every

is endowed with a functorial endomorphism

and a functorial map
R\xrightarrow{\langle{\ }\rangle}F(R)

Remark 1.4
.

R\mapsto W(R)
is an example of a pre-Witt functor.

Remark 1.5
.

For every ring

admits one more functorial endomorphism, viz., the Frobenius endomorphism

. However,

.

The following theorem, which is the main result of this paper, shows that the properties as in

characterise the functor

.

Theorem 1.6
.

For
p\neq 2
, the classical functor of
p
-typical Witt vectors

W:{\mathsf{ComRings}}\to Ab

Lemma 2.3

3.
The functor
C:{\mathsf{ComRings}}\to\mathsf{Ab}

Fix a prime
p\neq 2

C:{\mathsf{ComRings}}\to\mathsf{Ab}
which attempts to enforce the properties
1.1

(4) and hence we introduce a temporary notion of a weak pre-Witt functor (see Definition

Definition 3.1

(The functor
C
)
.

For any associative ring
R
and a prime
p\neq 2
, we first define a group
G(R)
as follows. Let

(1)

\widetilde{G}(R)
be the free abelian group generated by symbols
\{V^{n}_{r}\ |\ n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\ 0\neq r\in R\}

(2)

A set map

\langle{r}\rangle:=V^{0}_{r}
.

(3)

A homomorphism
\widetilde{G}(R)\xrightarrow{V}\widetilde{G}(R)

V(V^{n}_{r}):=V^{n+1}_{r}

(4)

\bigcup_{n}\left(\{(V^{n}_{(x+y)^{p}}-pV^{n-1}_{x+y})-(V^{n}_{x^{p}}-pV^{n-1}_{x})-(V^{n}_{y^{p}}-pV^{n-1}_{y})\ |\ x,y\in R\}\bigcup\{V^{n}_{r}+V^{n}_{-r}\ |\ r\in R\}\right)

(5)

\widetilde{H}(R)
be the
p

, i.e.

\widetilde{H}(R):=\{\alpha\in\widetilde{G}(R)\ |\ p^{\ell}\alpha\in H(R)\ \text{for some }\ell>0\}
.

(6)

Let

denote the completion of
\widetilde{G}(R)/\widetilde{H}(R)
by the
V
-filtration. Let
G(R)
be the quotient of

modulo the closed subgroup generated by
p

is functorial in

Let
R\in{\mathsf{ComRings}}
and

0\to I\to{\mathbb{Z}}[R]\to R\to 0

be the canonical free presentation of

.
Define

C(R):=\frac{G({\mathbb{Z}}[R])}{G_{I}({\mathbb{Z}}[R])}

where

is the closed subgroup generated by

\{V_{x}^{n}-V_{y}^{n}\ |\ x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}[R]\text{ such that }x-y\in I,\ n\geq 0\}.

We have a well defined group homomorphism
V:C(R)\to C(R)
and a well defined map of sets
R\xrightarrow{\langle{\ }\rangle}C(R)
induced by the corresponding maps on
G({\mathbb{Z}}[R])
.

Any ring homomorphism

gives a group homomorphism
G(R)\to G(S)
induced by

which is compatible with

and hence a continuous group homomorphism

There is a straight forward modification of the definition of

Theorem 3.6
.

Let

. The functor

It follows from the construction of

\eta:\widetilde{G}({\mathbb{Z}}[R])\to F({\mathbb{Z}}[R]),\ \text{given by}\ V^{n}_{a}\mapsto V^{n}\langle{a}\rangle\ \text{for all}\ a\in{\mathbb{Z}}[R],\ n\geq 0.

In this section we will prove the main theorem

, we have the canonical isomorphism

by
[

, Page 561]

is a pre-Witt functor and that

The first step towards proving this is to show that, for a free commutative polynomial algebra

. The crucial observation for this is the Lemma

. Before proving the Lemma

, we will begin by stating an interesting fact about integers.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p
be any prime and
\{c_{1},c_{2},...,c_{n}\}
be distinct nonzero integers.
Let

M(c_{1},c_{2},...c_{n}):=\left[\begin{matrix}c_{1}&c_{2}&...&c_{n}\\
c_{1}^{p}&c_{2}^{p}&...&c_{n}^{p}\\
\vdots\\
c_{1}^{p^{n-1}}&c_{2}^{p^{n-1}}&...&c_{n}^{p^{n-1}}\end{matrix}\right]

Then columns of
M(c_{1},...,c_{n})
are
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linearly independent in the following cases

(1)

c_{i}
are positive integers for all
i
.

(2)

p\neq 2
and
|c_{i}|
are distinct, i.e.
c_{i}\neq-c_{j}
for any
i\neq j

Proof.

Clearly columns of
M(c_{1},...,c_{n})
are
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linearly independent iff
det(M(c_{1},...,c_{n}))\neq 0
.
The first statement is a well known fact and follows from the exercise as given in
[
8
, page 43]
. The second statement can be deduced from (1) as follows. Since
p
is odd,
det(M(c_{1},c_{2},..,c_{n}))
differs from

at most by a sign. The condition in (2) ensures that
|c_{i}|
are distinct and now apply (1).
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p\neq 2
.
Let
A={\mathbb{Z}}[S]
be a commutative polynomial ring over a set
S
. Let
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
be a finite set of distinct non-zero elements of
A
. Further assume that
f_{i}\neq-f_{j}
for any
i\neq j
. Then the subset
\{\langle{f_{i}}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{r}
of
X(A)
is
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linearly independent.

Proof.

As there are only finitely many variables in each
f_{i}
, we may assume
A={\mathbb{Z}}[X_{1},...,X_{n}]
. Since
{\mathbb{Z}}
is an infinite integral domain, it is well known that a polynomial
f\in A
is zero iff
f(\underline{a})=0
for all
\underline{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Applying this to the polynomial
g:=\Pi_{i<j\leq r}(f_{i}-f_{j})(f_{i}+f_{j})\cdot\Pi_{i}f_{i}
, which is non-zero by hypothesis, we can choose an element
\underline{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
such that
g(\underline{a})\neq 0
. This implies that
c_{i}:=f_{i}(a)
are distinct nonzero integers such that
c_{i}\neq-c_{j}
for
i\neq j
. The linear independence of
\langle{f_{i}}\rangle
now directly follows by Lemma
4.1

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
A={\mathbb{Z}}[S]
be a free polynomial algebra.
There exists a canonical isomorphism
C(A)\cong X(A)
.

Proof.

We know that
X(A)
is a pre-Witt functor by Lemma
2.4
. Since
C
is a universal weak pre-Witt functor there exists a natural transformation
\eta:C\to X
. For a polynomial algebra
A
, we have the group homomorphism
\eta:C(A)\to X(A)
which is given by
V^{n}_{a}\mapsto V^{n}\langle{a}\rangle
. By Lemma
2.3
(1) this map is surjective. By Lemma
3.3
we know that
G(A)\cong C(A)
, thus composing with the group homomorphism
\widetilde{G}(A)/\widetilde{H}(A)\to G(A)
, we get the map

\overline{\eta}:\widetilde{G}(A)/\widetilde{H}(A)\to G(A)\to X(A)

The image of
\overline{\eta}
lands in
\widetilde{X}(A)
. Thus we have the group epimorphism

\widetilde{\eta}:\widetilde{G}(A)/\widetilde{H}(A)\to\widetilde{X}(A);\ \ \ V^{n}_{a}\mapsto V^{n}\langle{a}\rangle.

We will now prove the theorem in following steps.

Step 1
: To prove the theorem it is enough to show that
\widetilde{\eta}
is an isomorphism: This is because, if
\widetilde{\eta}
is an isomorphism then since
\widetilde{\eta}
is compatible with
V
, after taking the
V
-completion we get that

G^{0}(A)\cong X(A).

Since
X(A)
has no
p
-torsion, this implies, using Lemma
3.3

C(A)\cong G(A)\cong X(A).

Thus, it is enough to show that the group epimorphism
\widetilde{\eta}
is injective. This we will prove in the next step.

Step 2
: To show that
\widetilde{\eta}
is injective, we in fact consider the map (again denoted by
\widetilde{\eta}
);

\widetilde{\eta}:\widetilde{G}(A)\to\widetilde{X}(A)

We will show that
{\rm Ker}(\widetilde{\eta})=\widetilde{H}(A)
. It is easy to observe from the definition of
\widetilde{H}(A)
that
\widetilde{H}(A)\subseteq{\rm Ker}(\widetilde{\eta})
.

Let
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}c_{i}V^{n_{i}}_{a_{i}}\in{\rm Ker}(\widetilde{\eta})
. We will prove that
\alpha\in\widetilde{H}(A)
, i.e.

p^{k}\alpha\in H(A)
for some
k\geq 0
. We will establish this in following two steps.

Step 2a
: We first prove the case when
n_{i}=0
for all
i
, i.e.

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}c_{i}V^{0}_{a_{i}}

We may assume without loss of generality that
a_{i}
are all distinct. Moreover if
a_{r}=-a_{s}
for some
r\neq s
then

V^{0}_{a_{r}}=-V^{0}_{a_{s}}\ {\rm mod}\ H(A).

Thus we may replace the expression
c_{r}V^{0}_{a_{r}}+c_{s}V^{0}_{a_{s}}
with
(c_{s}-c_{r})V^{0}_{a_{s}}
. In particular we may assume that not only
a_{i}
are distinct, but
a_{i}\neq-a_{j}
for any
i\neq j
. In this case,
c_{i}
must be zero for all
i
by Lemma
4.2
. Thus
\alpha=0
and hence is in
\widetilde{H}(A)
.

Step 2b
: For
n_{i}\geq 0
, we prove the claim by induction on
n_{\alpha}:={\rm max}_{i}\{n_{i}\}-{\rm min}_{i}\{n_{i}\}
. Since
\widetilde{X}(A)\xrightarrow{V}\widetilde{X}(A)
and
\widetilde{H}(A)\xrightarrow{V}\widetilde{H}(A)
are both injective maps, we may assume without loss of generality that
{\rm min}_{i}\{n_{i}\}=0
. The starting step of induction is proved in Step 1. Now by rearranging, we may assume that
n_{i}=0
for
1\leq i\leq s
and
n_{i}>0
for
s<i\leq\ell
. Thus without loss of generality we have

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}V^{0}_{a_{i}}+\sum_{i>s}^{\ell}c_{i}V^{n_{i}}_{a_{i}}

We need to show
p^{k}\alpha\in H(A)
, for some
k\geq 0
.

\displaystyle p\alpha

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{s}pc_{i}V^{0}_{a_{i}}+\sum_{i>s}^{\ell}pc_{i}V^{n_{i}}_{a_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}(pV^{0}_{a_{i}}-V^{1}_{a_{i}^{p}})+\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}V^{1}_{a_{i}^{p}}+\sum_{i>s}^{\ell}pc_{i}V^{n_{i}}_{a_{i}}

\displaystyle=\beta+\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}V^{1}_{a_{i}^{p}}+\sum_{i>s}^{\ell}pc_{i}V^{n_{i}}_{a_{i}}

\displaystyle=:\beta+\gamma

Since
\alpha\in{\rm Ker}(\widetilde{\eta})
we have

p\widetilde{\eta}(\alpha)=\widetilde{\eta}(\beta)+\widetilde{\eta}(\gamma)=(p\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}a_{i},\cdots)=(0,0,0,\cdots).

This implies that
\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}a_{i}=0
since
X(A)
is p-torsion free.

We now claim that
\beta\in H(A)
: To prove this claim we will use the defining relations of
H(A)
.

Consider the map
\phi:A\to\widetilde{G}(A)
given by
a\mapsto V^{1}_{a^{p}}-pV^{0}_{a}
which is a linear map, i.e.

\phi(\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}a_{i})\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}\phi(a_{i})(\text{mod}\ H(A))

Thus,
\beta\equiv 0(\text{mod}\ H(A))
. This proves that
\beta\in H(A)
and hence is in
\widetilde{H}(A)
. Thus,
\beta\in{\rm Ker}(\widetilde{\eta})
. This implies that
\gamma\in{\rm Ker}(\widetilde{\eta})
. Moreover observe that
n_{\gamma}<n_{\alpha}
, hence by the induction hypothesis,
\gamma\in\widetilde{H}(A)
. Thus,
p^{r}\gamma\in H(A)
for some
r\geq 0
. Thus,
p^{r+1}\alpha\in H(A)
.
Thus,
{\rm Ker}(\widetilde{\eta})\subseteq\widetilde{H}(A)
.

This proves that
\widetilde{G}(A)/\widetilde{H}(A)\cong\widetilde{X}(A)
and hence the theorem.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

The natural transformation
C\to E
is an isomorphism of functors. In particular,
C
is a pre-Witt functor.

Proof.

Let
A
be a commutative polynomial ring. Since
E(A)\cong X(A)
by
[
3
, Cor. 2.10]
and
X(A)\cong C(A)
by Lemma
4.3
, we get that
C(A)\cong E(A)
. For a general commutative ring
R
, consider the presentation,

0\to I\to{\mathbb{Z}}[R]\to R\to 0.

The functor
C
being universal, we have a commutative diagram with exact rows.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{I}({\mathbb{Z}}[R])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G({\mathbb{Z}}[R])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{I}({\mathbb{Z}}[R])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X({\mathbb{Z}}[R])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E(R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}

Now by Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
4.3
, the verticle map

\phi:G({\mathbb{Z}}[R])\to X({\mathbb{Z}}[R])

is an isomorphism. The definition of
G_{I}({\mathbb{Z}}[R])
and
X_{I}({\mathbb{Z}}[R])
shows that the preimage of
X_{I}({\mathbb{Z}}[R])
under
\phi
is exactly
G_{I}({\mathbb{Z}}[R])
. Hence, the induced map
C(R)\to E(R)
is an isomorphism.
∎

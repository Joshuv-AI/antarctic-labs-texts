New sharp bounds for the Jacobi heat kernel via an extension of the Dijksma-Koornwinder formula
Adam Nowak, Peter Sjögren, Tomasz Z. Szarek

Abstract
. We obtain sharp estimates for the Jacobi heat kernel in a range of parameters where the result has not been established before. This extends and completes an earlier result due to the authors. The proof is based on a generalization of the Dijksma-Koornwinder product formula for Jacobi polynomials.

1.
Introduction

Let
\alpha,\beta>-1
. The Jacobi heat kernel is defined by

(1)

G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-tn(n+\alpha+\beta+1)}\frac{P_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)P_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(y)}{h_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}},\qquad x,y\in[-1,1],\quad t>0,

where
P_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}
are the classical Jacobi polynomials and
h_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}
are suitable normalizing
constants. This is the kernel of the Jacobi semigroup generated by the Jacobi operator; see Section
2

for precise definitions. The oscillatory series displayed above cannot be computed.

The kernel
G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)
is an important object in analysis and beyond,
in particular due to its deep connections with, in a sense, more elementary
heat kernels in numerous classical frameworks. These include, among others, the contexts of Euclidean spheres (cf.
[
12
]
),
compact rank-one symmetric spaces (cf.
[
13
]
), Fourier-Bessel expansions (cf.
[
9
]
)
and algebraic contexts related to multi-dimensional
balls, simplices, cones, conical surfaces and other domains, see e.g.
[
13
,
6
,
4
,
2
]
and references therein.
Thus advancing knowledge about
G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)
is widely motivated.

In this paper we complete our study
[
13
]
of the behavior of the Jacobi heat kernel by proving genuinely sharp two-sided
global estimates in the whole range of the parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
indicated above.
In order to state the main result, we define for
t>0
and
\theta,\varphi\in[0,\pi]
the quantity

Z_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(\theta,\varphi)=\big[t+\theta\varphi\big]^{-\alpha-1/2}\,\big[t+(\pi-\theta)(\pi-\varphi)\big]^{-\beta-1/2}\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\bigg({-\frac{(\theta-\varphi)^{2}}{4t}}\bigg).

We will prove the following theorem, which concludes
the long-standing open problem of finding sharp bounds for the Jacobi heat kernel.

Theorem A
.

Let
\alpha,\beta>-1
and
T>0
be fixed. There exists a constant
C=C(\alpha,\beta,T)>1
such that

C^{-1}Z_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(\theta,\varphi)\leq G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta,\cos\varphi)\leq CZ_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(\theta,\varphi)

for
\theta,\varphi\in[0,\pi]
and
0<t\leq T
.

In the restricted range of parameters
\alpha,\beta\geq-1/2
, Theorem
A
was proved by the authors in
[
12
,
13
]
,
see
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
. We remark that only qualitatively sharp, short-time bounds for
G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)

were earlier obtained in
[
1
]
and independently by other methods in
[
10
]
;
in the latter paper under the restriction
\alpha,\beta\geq-1/2
.
The difference between genuinely sharp and qualitatively sharp bounds is that in the latter case the constants appearing
in the exponential factors in the lower and upper estimates (which are
1/4
in our Theorem
A
)
differ from each other and from the optimal one.
This exponential gap swallows various polynomial factors, which in case of genuinely sharp bounds must be determined precisely.

On the other hand, for any
\alpha,\beta>-1
showing the long-time sharp estimates

(2)

C^{-1}\leq G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)\leq C,\qquad x,y\in[-1,1],\quad t\geq T,

with
T>0
fixed and some
C=C(\alpha,\beta,T)>1
independent of
x,y,t
, is much more straightforward, see
[
1
,
10
]
.

The new contribution of Theorem
A
pertains essentially (see
[
13
, Remark 3.2]
)
to the range of parameters when
\alpha<-1/2
or
\beta<-1/2
, in particular to the tricky case when also
\alpha+\beta\leq-3/2
.
These cases are more difficult to handle than the previously solved case
\alpha,\beta\geq-1/2
.
In fact, some crucial tools and techniques elaborated and used earlier in
[
10
,
13
]
break down for the parameters below

-1/2
and hence the proof of Theorem
A
requires some new ideas and tools. One of them is an extended variant
of a
product formula
for Jacobi polynomials due to Dijksma and Koornwinder
[
3
]
. This extension, obtained
by means of analytic continuation,
is contained in Theorem
3.2
, see also Remark
3.3
; we believe that this result is of independent interest.
For the statement of the original Dijksma-Koornwinder formula and further details, see Section
3
.
The extended product formula leads to an extension of a
reduction formula
for the Jacobi heat kernel
from
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
; see Theorem
4.3
, which is the key to proving the desired bounds.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we introduce basic notation and gather various facts and results needed in the sequel.
Section
3
is devoted to the extension of the Dijksma-Koornwinder product formula.
In Section
4
we extend the reduction formula for the Jacobi heat kernel.
Section
5
contains some preparations for the proof of Theorem
A
, given in Section
6
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper we use a standard notation.
We will frequently write
X\lesssim Y
to indicate that
X\leq CY
with a positive constant
C

independent of significant quantities. We shall write
X\simeq Y
when simultaneously
X\lesssim Y
and
Y\lesssim X
.

Varia

For various facts below, see e.g.
[
15
]
. We use the following notation.

\displaystyle P_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}

Jacobi polynomials

\displaystyle C_{n}^{\lambda}\;\;

Gegenbauer/ultraspherical polynomials

\displaystyle P_{n}^{\lambda}\;\;

\displaystyle\qquad\textrm{Jacobi polynomial}\;\;P_{n}^{\lambda,\lambda}.

The Gegenbauer and Jacobi polynomials are linked by

C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+1/2)}{\Gamma(2\lambda)}\,\frac{\Gamma(n+2\lambda)}{\Gamma(n+\lambda+1/2)}\,P_{n}^{\lambda-1/2}(x),\qquad\lambda>-1/2,\quad\lambda\neq 0.

We will use a similar shortened notation for the Jacobi heat kernel,
G_{t}^{\lambda}(x,y):=G_{t}^{\lambda,\lambda}(x,y)
,
and for other objects defined below, e.g.
h_{n}^{\lambda}:=h_{n}^{\lambda,\lambda}
,
J^{\lambda}:=J^{\lambda,\lambda}
.

For
\alpha,\beta>-1
the Jacobi orthogonality measure on the interval
[-1,1]
is

d\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=(1-x)^{\alpha}(1+x)^{\beta}\,dx.

For complex
\alpha
and
\beta
such that
\Re\alpha,\Re\beta>-1
, we let

h_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{2^{\alpha+\beta+1}\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta+1)\Gamma(n+\alpha+\beta+1)\Gamma(n+1)},\qquad n\geq 0,

where in the case
n=0
the product
(2n+\alpha+\beta+1)\Gamma(n+\alpha+\beta+1)
is replaced by
\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+2)
.
For real
\alpha,\beta>-1
, one has
h_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}=\|P_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(d\varrho_{\alpha,\beta})}
.

Note that
P_{0}^{\alpha,\beta}\equiv 1
.
We state some other useful formulas, valid for
n\geq 0
:

(3)

\displaystyle P_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha+1)_{n}}{n!}=\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\alpha+1)},

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}P_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(n+\alpha+\beta+1)P^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}_{n-1}(x).

By convention,
P_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}\equiv 0
for
k<0
.
The Pochhammer symbol is defined as
(a)_{k}=a(a+1)\cdot\ldots\cdot(a+k-1)
for
k\geq 1
, with the convention that
(a)_{0}=1
.

Jacobi polynomials and complex values of the parameters

In this subsection we consider
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
, see
[
15
, Chapter IV, Section 4.22]
.

Jacobi polynomials have natural extensions to all complex
parameters, since, defined a priori as orthogonal polynomials for
\alpha,\beta>-1
,
they are polynomials not only in their argument, but also in the parameters.
One has
P^{\alpha,\beta}_{0}\equiv 1
, and for
n\geq 1

\displaystyle P_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(n+\alpha+\beta+1)_{k}\;(\alpha+k+1)_{n-k}\;\Big(\frac{x-1}{2}\Big)^{k}.

Note that
P_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}
is a polynomial of degree at most
n
in
\alpha
and
\beta
.
And
P^{\alpha,\beta}_{n}(x)
is a polynomial of degree at most
n
in
x

(the degree is equal to
n
if e.g.
\alpha,\beta>-1
; for more details, see
[
15
, Chapter IV, Section 4.22]
).

The formulas (
3
) and (
4
) hold for all complex parameters (understood as appropriate limits whenever necessary).
Moreover, the
P_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}
are eigenfunctions of the Jacobi differential operator given by

J^{\alpha,\beta}=-\big(1-x^{2}\big)\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-\big[\beta-\alpha-(\alpha+\beta+2)x\big]\frac{d}{dx}.

More precisely, one has

(5)

J^{\alpha,\beta}P_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}=n(n+\alpha+\beta+1)P_{n}^{\alpha,\beta},\qquad n\geq 0.

Measures and some integrals

We observe that

\varrho_{\alpha,\beta}\big([-1,1]\big)=h_{0}^{\alpha,\beta}=2^{\alpha+\beta+1}\,\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+2)},\qquad\alpha,\beta>-1.

For complex
\alpha
such that
\Re\alpha>-1
and
\alpha\neq-1/2
, define

\Pi_{\alpha}(u)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\alpha+1/2)}\int_{0}^{u}\big(1-w^{2}\big)^{\alpha-1/2}\,dw.

Notice that
\Pi_{\alpha}
is an odd function in
-1<u<1
, increasing if
\alpha>-1/2
and decreasing if
\alpha<-1/2
.

For real
\alpha>-1/2
the measure
d\Pi_{\alpha}
is a probability measure in the interval
[-1,1]
,
as seen by changing variable to
v=w^{2}
in the integral above.
As
\alpha\searrow-1/2
, the weak limit of
d\Pi_{\alpha}
is

d\Pi_{-1/2}:=\frac{\delta_{-1}+\delta_{1}}{2},

where
\delta_{\pm 1}
is a unit point mass at
\pm 1
.

In general, for all complex
\alpha
such that
\Re\alpha>-1
,
\alpha\neq-1/2
, the distribution derivative
d\Pi_{\alpha}
is
a local complex measure in
(-1,1)
. For real
\alpha\in(-1,-1/2)
its density is negative, even, and not integrable in
(-1,1)
.
Furthermore, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
11
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

Let
\alpha\in(-1,-1/2)
be fixed. Then the density
|\Pi_{\alpha}(u)|
defines a finite measure on
[-1,1]
and

|\Pi_{\alpha}(u)|\simeq|u|\big(1-|u|\big)^{\alpha+1/2}\simeq|u|\frac{d\Pi_{\alpha+1}(u)}{du},\qquad u\in(-1,1).

We define

\mathcal{A}:=\big\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\Re\alpha>-1,\Re\beta>-1\big\}.

Observe that, see
[
11
, Section 2]
, if
\phi
is a continuous function in
[0,1]
satisfying

|\phi(u)|\leq C(1-u)
, with a constant
C=C(\alpha)
that is locally bounded, then the integral

I(\alpha)=\int_{(0,1]}\phi(u)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)

is well defined and analytic in
\{\alpha:\Re\alpha>-1\}
. Moreover,

I(-1/2)=0
, as seen from the bound
|I(\alpha)|\lesssim|\alpha+1/2|\int_{0}^{1}(1-u^{2})^{\Re\alpha+1/2}du
.
This observation generalizes as follows, cf.
[
11
, Section 2]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

(a)

Assume that
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(u)
is continuous in
(u,\alpha,\beta)\in[0,1]\times\mathcal{A}

and analytic in
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}
for each
u\in(0,1)
and satisfies

|\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(u)|\leq C(1-u)
, where the constant
C=C(\alpha,\beta)
is locally bounded in
\mathcal{A}
. Then

I(\alpha,\beta)=\int_{(0,1]}\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(u)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)

is well defined and analytic in
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}
. Moreover,
I(-1/2,\beta)=0
.

(b)

Let
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(u,v)
be continuous in
(u,v,\alpha,\beta)\in[0,1]^{2}\times\mathcal{A}

and analytic in
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}
for each

u,v\in(0,1)
, and assume that
|\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(u,v)|\leq C(1-u)(1-v)
with
C
as in
(a)
.
Then the double integral

I(\alpha,\beta)=\iint_{(0,1]^{2}}\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(u,v)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,d\Pi_{\beta}(v)

is well defined and analytic in
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}
. Furthermore,
I(-1/2,\beta)=I(\alpha,-1/2)=0
.

3.
Dijksma-Koornwinder formula

In
[
3
]
Dijksma and Koornwinder found a symmetric
product formula
for two Jacobi polynomials,
which was originally stated as follows (cf.
[
3
, (2.5)]
):

(6)

\displaystyle P_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}\big(1-2s^{2}\big)P_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}\big(1-2t^{2}\big)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+1)\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{\pi n!\Gamma(n+\alpha+\beta+1)\Gamma(\alpha+1/2)\Gamma(\beta+1/2)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{-1}^{1}C_{2n}^{\alpha+\beta+1}\Big(ust+v\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\Big)\big(1-u^{2}\big)^{\alpha-1/2}\big(1-v^{2}\big)^{\beta-1/2}\,du\,dv.

Here
-1\leq s,t\leq 1
, and
the formula is valid for
n\geq 0
and all complex
\alpha,\beta
such that
\Re\alpha>-1/2
and
\Re\beta>-1/2
.
The proof in
[
3
]
is group theoretic.
An analytic proof was given slightly later by Koornwinder
[
7
]
.
Yet another proof can be found in the more recent paper
[
8
]
.

We will rewrite and extend the product formula to all
\alpha,\beta
such that
\Re\alpha>-1
,
\Re\beta>-1
,
i.e.,
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}
; see Theorem
3.2
below.
For this purpose we define, to begin with for
\alpha,\beta>-1
such that
\alpha+\beta+1/2>-1
and for
\lambda>-1
,

\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}:=\frac{h_{0}^{\alpha,\beta}}{(\alpha+\beta+3/2)h_{0}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}h_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}},\quad n\geq 0,\qquad\quad\mathrm{and}\qquad\quad\;\mathfrak{D}_{k}^{\lambda}:=\frac{P_{k}^{\lambda}(1)}{(\lambda+1)h_{k}^{\lambda}},\quad k\geq 0.

In what follows we use implicitly the duplication formula for the gamma function, written as

\Gamma(2\lambda+2)=\Gamma(\lambda+1)\Gamma(\lambda+3/2)2^{2\lambda+1}/\sqrt{\pi}.

By a direct computation we see that

(7)

\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}=\begin{cases}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)(2n+\alpha+\beta+1)\Gamma(n+\alpha+\beta+1)\Gamma(n+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+5/2)\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\Gamma(n+\beta+1)},&n\geq 1,\\
\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+2)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+5/2)},&n=0,\end{cases}

and this provides an analytic extension of each
\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}
,
n\geq 0
,
to
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}
. Similarly for
\lambda>-1

(8)

\mathfrak{D}_{k}^{\lambda}=\begin{cases}\frac{(2k+2\lambda+1)\Gamma(k+2\lambda+1)}{2^{2\lambda+1}\Gamma(\lambda+2)\Gamma(k+\lambda+1)},&k\geq 1,\\
\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+3/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\lambda+2)},&k=0.\end{cases}

The last expression defines an analytic extension of each

\mathfrak{D}_{2n}^{\lambda}
,
n\geq 0
, to
\Re\lambda>-3/2
.
Thus, from now on, we consider
\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}
and
\mathfrak{D}_{2n}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}
as analytic functions
of
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}
given by (
7
) and (
8
), respectively.
Note that
\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta},\mathfrak{D}_{2n}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}>0
for
\alpha,\beta\in(-1,\infty)
.

We denote

(9)

\Phi_{k}^{\lambda}(\theta,\varphi,u,v):=\mathfrak{D}_{k}^{\lambda}{P}_{k}^{\lambda}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}\Big),

and let
s=\sin\frac{\theta}{2}
and
t=\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}
in (
6
). By means of (
7
) and (
8
),
one then finds the following more compact form of the Dijksma-Koornwinder formula (
6
):

(10)

\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}{P}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta){P}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\varphi)=\iint\Phi_{2n}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,v)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,d\Pi_{\beta}(v),\quad 0\leq\theta,\varphi\leq\pi.

This holds for
\Re\alpha>-1/2
and
\Re\beta>-1/2
, and since Jacobi polynomials are continuous functions of their parameters,
it is also valid when
\alpha=-1/2
or
\beta=-1/2
.

We now proceed essentially as in
[
11
, Section 2]
.
Taking the even parts of
\Phi_{k}^{\lambda}(\theta,\varphi,u,v)
in
u
and
v
, we define

\Phi_{k,E}^{\lambda}(\theta,\varphi,u,v):=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\xi,\eta=\pm 1}\Phi_{k}^{\lambda}(\theta,\varphi,\xi u,\eta v).

Then by (
10
) and for symmetry reasons, one has for
(\alpha,\beta)
such that
\Re\alpha,\Re\beta>-1/2

(11)

\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}{{P}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta){P}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\varphi)}=4\iint_{(0,1]^{2}}\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,v)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,d\Pi_{\beta}(v).

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}
,
n\geq 0
and
\theta,\varphi\in[0,\pi]

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}{{P}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta){P}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\varphi)}

\displaystyle=4\iint_{(0,1]^{2}}\Big[\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,v)-\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,1)

\displaystyle\qquad-\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,1,v)+\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,1,1)\Big]\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,d\Pi_{\beta}(v)

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{(0,1]}\Big[\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,1)-\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,1,1)\Big]\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{(0,1]}\Big[\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,1,v)-\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,1,1)\Big]\,d\Pi_{\beta}(v)

\displaystyle\quad+\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,1,1).

Proof.

When
\Re\alpha,\Re\beta>-1/2
, the claimed identity is a straightforward consequence of (
11
),
since the integrals can be evaluated term by term.
The left-hand side of the identity is analytic in
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}
.
It suffices to verify that the right-hand side is analytic in

\mathcal{A}
, since then the conclusion will follow by analytic continuation.

The last term of the right-hand side is analytic in
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}
, as easily seen from (
9
).
The second term of the right-hand side, i.e., the integral in
u
,
is of the form
I(\alpha,\beta)
from item (a) of Proposition
2.2
, with

\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(u,v)=\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,1)-\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,1,1).

Note that
|\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(u)|\leq C(1-u)
, since the derivative

\partial_{u}\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,1)
is bounded locally uniformly in
\alpha
and
\beta
,
see (
4
). The third term of the right-hand side, i.e., the integral in
v
, is analogous.

Finally, the first term of the right-hand side, i.e., the double integral,
is of the form
I(\alpha,\beta)
from item (b) of Proposition
2.2
, with

\displaystyle\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(u,v)

\displaystyle\quad=\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,v)-\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,1)-\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,1,v)+\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,1,1),

and the condition
|\phi_{\alpha,\beta}(u,v)|\leq C(1-u)(1-v)
is satisfied since the second order derivatives
of
\Phi_{2n,E}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,v)
in
u,v
are bounded locally uniformly in
\alpha
and
\beta
, cf. (
4
).

Altogether, it follows that the right-hand side of the identity is analytic in
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

The next result is the announced extension of the Dijksma-Koornwinder formula. We state it for real values of the parameters.
A variant with complex parameters is indicated in Remark
3.3
below.
For the sake of completeness, the original Dijksma-Koornwinder formula is included as item (i).
By convention,
\Phi_{k}^{\lambda}(\theta,\varphi,u,v)\equiv 0
when
k<0
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
n\geq 0
and
\theta,\varphi\in[0,\pi]
. Then, with all integrations taken over
[-1,1]^{2}
,

(i)

If
\alpha,\beta\geq-1/2
, then

\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}{{P}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta){P}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\varphi)}=\iint\Phi_{2n}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,v)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,d\Pi_{\beta}(v).

(ii)

If
-1<\beta<-1/2\leq\alpha
, then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}{{P}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta){P}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\varphi)}

\displaystyle\quad=\iint\Big\{-2(\alpha+\beta+5/2)\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}\,\Phi_{2n-1}^{\alpha+\beta+3/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,v)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,\Pi_{\beta}(v)\,dv

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\Phi_{2n}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,v)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,d\Pi_{-1/2}(v)\Big\}.

(iii)

If
-1<\alpha<-1/2\leq\beta
, then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}{{P}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta){P}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\varphi)}

\displaystyle\quad=\iint\Big\{-2(\alpha+\beta+5/2)\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}\,\Phi_{2n-1}^{\alpha+\beta+3/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,v)\,\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,du\,d\Pi_{\beta}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\Phi_{2n}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\theta,\varphi,u,v)\,d\Pi_{-1/2}(u)\,d\Pi_{\beta}(v)\Big\}.

(iv)

If
-1<\alpha,\beta<-1/2
, then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}{{P}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta){P}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\varphi)}

Proof.

4.
Reduction formula

Recall that for
\alpha,\beta>-1
,
x,y\in[-1,1]
and
t>0
the Jacobi heat kernel
G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)
is given by (
1
).
For basic information concerning this kernel, see e.g.
[
10
]
.
We will use the following lemma from our earlier paper
[
13
]
.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
13
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

Let
\alpha,\beta>-1
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\,G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,1)=2(\alpha+1)\,e^{-t(\alpha+\beta+2)}\,G_{t}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}(x,1),\qquad x\in[-1,1],\quad t>0.

In particular, the function
x\mapsto G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,1)
is strictly increasing on
[-1,1]
.

We now introduce an auxiliary function
H_{t}^{\lambda}(x)
which can be regarded as an extension
for
-3/2<\lambda\leq-1
of (a constant times) the even part in
x
of the Jacobi-ultraspherical heat kernel
G_{t}^{\lambda}(x,1)
.
Let

H_{t}^{\lambda}(x):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-t2n(2n+2\lambda+1)}\,\mathfrak{D}_{2n}^{\lambda}\,P_{2n}^{\lambda}(x),\qquad x\in[-1,1],\quad t>0,\quad-3/2<\lambda\leq-1.

Note that
H_{t}^{\lambda}(x)
is well defined. Indeed, the constants
\mathfrak{D}_{2n}^{\lambda}
are well defined by
(
8
). By Stirling’s formula, one has
\mathfrak{D}_{2n}^{\lambda}=\mathcal{O}(n^{\lambda+1})
as
n\to\infty
.
Furthermore, the Jacobi polynomials satisfy (see
[
15
, Chapter VII, Section 7.32, Theorem 7.32.2]
)

\big|P_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)\big|\lesssim(k+1)^{\max\{\alpha,\beta,-1/2\}},\qquad k\geq 0,\quad x\in[-1,1],

for any fixed
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Consequently, we see that the series defining
H_{t}^{\lambda}(x)

converges uniformly in
(t,x)\in[T,\infty)\times[-1,1]
for each fixed
T>0
. Using (
4
) we also
see that the series can be differentiated term by term in
x
and
t
arbitrarily many times.
Thus
H_{t}^{\lambda}(x)
is a smooth function of
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times[-1,1]
.

Next, we observe that
H_{t}^{\lambda}
satisfies the heat equation based on the Jacobi-ultraspherical operator
J^{\lambda}
,

(12)

\Big(\frac{d}{dt}+J^{\lambda}\Big)H_{t}^{\lambda}(x)=0,\qquad x\in[-1,1],\quad t>0.

This follows from the fact that each term of the defining series satisfies this equation, see (
5
).
Moreover, we have the following.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
-3/2<\lambda\leq-1
. Then

\frac{d}{dx}\,H_{t}^{\lambda}(x)=2e^{-t(2\lambda+2)}\Big[G_{t}^{\lambda+1}(x,1)\Big]_{\emph{odd}},\qquad x\in[-1,1],\quad t>0,

where the subscript ‘odd’ indicates the odd part of the function in
x
. Similarly,

\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\,H_{t}^{\lambda}(x)=4(\lambda+2)e^{-t(4\lambda+6)}\Big[G_{t}^{\lambda+2}(x,1)\Big]_{\emph{even}},\qquad x\in[-1,1],\quad t>0,

where the subscript ‘even’ indicates the even part of the function in
x
.

Proof.

To verify the first identity,
differentiate the series defining
H_{t}^{\lambda}(x)
term by term and use (
4
) together with the fact that
ultraspherical polynomials of odd orders are odd functions, and those of even orders are even functions.
The second identity follows from the first one and Lemma
4.1
.
We leave the straightforward computations to the reader.
∎

The next result extends a
reduction formula
for the Jacobi heat kernel that was obtained in
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]

by means of the Dijksma-Koornwinder product formula. For the sake of completeness,

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
t>0
and
\theta,\varphi\in[0,\pi]
. Then, with all integrations taken over

(i)

If

\displaystyle\frac{h_{0}^{\alpha,\beta}}{h_{0}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}}\,G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta,\cos\varphi)

\displaystyle\frac{h_{0}^{\alpha,\beta}}{h_{0}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}}\,G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta,\cos\varphi)

\displaystyle\frac{h_{0}^{\alpha,\beta}}{h_{0}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}}\,G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta,\cos\varphi)

\displaystyle\quad=\iint\Big\{-2(\alpha+\beta+3/2)e^{-t(\alpha+\beta+3/2)/2}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+3/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,du\,d\Pi_{\beta}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,d\Pi_{-1/2}(u)\,d\Pi_{\beta}(v)\Big\}.

(iv)

If
-1<\alpha,\beta<-1/2
and
\alpha+\beta>-3/2
, then

\displaystyle\frac{h_{0}^{\alpha,\beta}}{h_{0}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}}\,G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta,\cos\varphi)

\displaystyle\quad=\iint\Big\{(\alpha+\beta+3/2)(\alpha+\beta+5/2)e^{-t(\alpha+\beta+2)}\sin\theta\sin\varphi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+5/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,du\,\Pi_{\beta}(v)\,dv

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-2(\alpha+\beta+3/2)e^{-t(\alpha+\beta+3/2)/2}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+3/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,du\,d\Pi_{-1/2}(v)

(v)

If
-1<\alpha,\beta<-1/2
and
\alpha+\beta\leq-3/2
, then

\displaystyle h_{0}^{\alpha,\beta}\mathfrak{C}_{0}^{\alpha,\beta}G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta,\cos\varphi)

\displaystyle\quad=\iint\Big\{(\alpha+\beta+5/2)e^{-t(\alpha+\beta+2)}\sin\theta\sin\varphi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+5/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,du\,\Pi_{\beta}(v)\,dv

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-2e^{-t(\alpha+\beta+3/2)/2}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+3/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,du\,d\Pi_{-1/2}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-2e^{-t(\alpha+\beta+3/2)/2}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+3/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,d\Pi_{-1/2}(u)\,\Pi_{\beta}(v)\,dv

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+H_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}\Big)\,d\Pi_{-1/2}(u)\,d\Pi_{-1/2}(v)\Big\}.

Proof.

To verify items (i)–(iv) above, one starts by multiplying the identities in (i)–(iv) of Theorem
3.2

by the factor
\exp(-tn(n+\alpha+\beta+1))
. The resulting equations are summed over

n=0,1,2,\ldots
. Then one uses the simple relations

\displaystyle tn(n+\alpha+\beta+1)

\displaystyle\quad=(t/4)2n[2n+(\alpha+\beta+1/2)+(\alpha+\beta+1/2)+1]

\displaystyle\quad=(t/4)(2n-1)[2n-1+(\alpha+\beta+3/2)+(\alpha+\beta+3/2)+1]+t(\alpha+\beta+3/2)/2

\displaystyle\quad=(t/4)(2n-2)[2n-2+(\alpha+\beta+5/2)+(\alpha+\beta+5/2)+1]+t(\alpha+\beta+2)

and the fact that Jacobi-ultraspherical polynomials of even (odd) orders are even (odd) functions.
It follows that the series obtained represents in each case
either the even or the odd part with respect to the first variable of the Jacobi-ultraspherical
heat kernel with appropriate parameter and the second variable fixed at the right endpoint.
Since, for symmetry reasons, the corresponding complementary odd/even parts give no contributions to the integrals,
we end up with the desired conclusions.

Dealing with the last term in item (v) is even more straightforward, since it requires only some of the above arguments.
∎

5.
Preliminaries for the proof of Theorem
A

Initial estimates

The next result is a special case of Theorem
A
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\lambda>-1
. Then

G_{t}^{\lambda}(\cos\theta,1)\simeq t^{-\lambda-1}\exp\bigg(-\frac{\theta^{2}}{4t}\bigg),

uniformly in
0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2
and
0<t\leq 1
.

Remark 5.2
.

This estimate is new for
\lambda<-1/2
, but for
\lambda\geq-1/2
it is contained in the known part of Theorem
A
.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

We first verify that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}G_{t}^{\lambda}(0,1)=0
.
The heat equation yields

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,G_{t}^{\lambda}(0,1)=-J^{\lambda}\,G_{t}^{\lambda}(x,1)\Big|_{x=0}=\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\,G_{t}^{\lambda}(x,1)\Big|_{x=0}.

To estimate this second derivative, we apply Lemma
4.1
twice and then use Remark
5.2
, getting

(13)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,G_{t}^{\lambda}(0,1)\simeq G_{t}^{\lambda+2}(0,1)\simeq t^{-\lambda-3}\,\exp\bigg(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16t}\bigg)>0,\qquad 0<t\leq 1.

Thus
G_{t}^{\lambda}(0,1)
is a non-negative, increasing function of
t
in
(0,1]
, and so the limit

\eta:=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}G_{t}^{\lambda}(0,1)

exists and is non-negative.

Aiming at a contradiction, we assume that
\eta>0
. Since
G_{t}^{\lambda}(x,1)
is increasing in
x
by Lemma
4.1
,
we then have
G_{t}^{\lambda}(x,1)\geq G_{t}^{\lambda}(0,1)>\eta
for
0\leq x\leq 1
and
0<t\leq 1
,
and by symmetry
G_{t}^{\lambda}(1,y)>\eta
for
0\leq y\leq 1
. Now let
\big(T_{t}^{\lambda}\big)

be the Jacobi (ultraspherical) semigroup
and take a nontrivial continuous function
f\geq 0
supported in a compact subinterval of
(0,1)
. Then we get

T^{\lambda}_{t}f(1)=\int_{-1}^{1}G_{t}^{\lambda}(1,y)f(y)\,d\varrho_{\lambda}(y)>\eta\,\|f\|_{L^{1}(d\varrho_{\lambda})}>0

for
0<t\leq 1
. But
\big(T^{\lambda}_{t}\big)
is a symmetric diffusion semigroup in the sense of

[
14
, Chapter 3]
and its maximal operator is
L^{\infty}
-bounded.
By standard arguments, see e.g.
[
5
, Chapter 2, Section 2]
, it follows that

T^{\lambda}_{t}f
converges to
f
as
t\to 0^{+}
, uniformly in
[-1,1]
, since this convergence certainly holds for the
dense subset of
C([-1,1])
consisting of finite linear combinations of Jacobi-ultraspherical polynomials.
This is a contradiction which proves that
\eta=0
.

Then (
13
) and Remark
5.2
imply for
0<t\leq 1

\displaystyle G_{t}^{\lambda}(0,1)

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{-\lambda-3}\,\exp\bigg(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16\tau}\bigg)\,d\tau\simeq\int_{1/t}^{\infty}\sigma^{\lambda+1}\,\exp\bigg(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}\,\sigma\bigg)\,d\sigma

(14)

\displaystyle\simeq t^{-\lambda-1}\,\exp\bigg(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16t}\bigg),

where we made the change of variable
\sigma=1/\tau
.

For
x=\cos\theta\in(0,1]
, we apply first Lemma
4.1
and then Remark
5.2
, to get

\displaystyle G_{t}^{\lambda}(x,1)-G_{t}^{\lambda}(0,1)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{d}{dy}\,G_{t}^{\lambda}(y,1)\,dy\simeq\int_{0}^{x}G_{t}^{\lambda+1}(y,1)\,dy

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\theta}^{\pi/2}G_{t}^{\lambda+1}(\cos\varphi,1)\,\sin\varphi\,d\varphi\simeq t^{-\lambda-2}\,\int_{\theta}^{\pi/2}\,\exp\bigg(-\frac{\varphi^{2}}{4t}\bigg)\,\varphi\,d\varphi

\displaystyle\quad=2\,t^{-\lambda-1}\,\bigg[\exp\bigg(-\frac{\theta^{2}}{4t}\bigg)-\exp\bigg(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16t}\bigg)\bigg].

From this and (
5
), we can finish the proof by separating the case
((\pi/2)^{2}-\theta^{2})/t>1
and the contrary case.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\lambda\in(-3/2,-1]
. Then

H_{t}^{\lambda}(\cos\theta)\simeq t^{-\lambda-1}\,\exp\bigg(-\frac{\theta^{2}}{4t}\bigg),

uniformly in
0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2
and
0<t\leq 1
.

Proof.

We first claim that

H_{t}^{\lambda}(0)\to 0\qquad\mathrm{as}\qquad t\to 0^{+}.

In the formula of Theorem
4.3
(v), we take
\alpha=\beta=\lambda/2-1/4>-1
,
\varphi=0
and
\theta=\pi
.
Then the fourth integral in the right-hand side of this formula is precisely
H_{t/4}^{\lambda}(0)
.
Moreover, the first three integrals in the right-hand side all vanish because of the trigonometric factors.
In the left-hand side, we have constant times
G_{t}^{\alpha}(-1,1)
, and this quantity is non-negative and,
in view of Lemma
4.1
, no larger than
G_{t}^{\alpha}(0,1)
. Lemma
5.1
shows that
G_{t}^{\alpha}(0,1)\to 0
as

t\to 0^{+}
, and the claim follows.

We will next estimate
H_{t}^{\lambda}(x)
for
0\leq x\leq 1
and
0<t\leq 1
, by integrating its derivatives.
Letting first
x=0
, we conclude
from (
12
) and Lemma
4.2
that

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,H_{t}^{\lambda}(0)=-J^{\lambda}H_{t}^{\lambda}(x)\Big|_{x=0}=\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\,H_{t}^{\lambda}(x)\Big|_{x=0}\simeq G_{t}^{\lambda+2}(0,1)\simeq t^{-\lambda-3}\,\exp\bigg(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16t}\bigg),

the last relation by Lemma
5.1
. Integrating as in (
5
), we get for
0<t\leq 1

(15)

H_{t}^{\lambda}(0)\simeq t^{-\lambda-1}\,\exp\bigg(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16t}\bigg).

Let now
0\leq x\leq 1
and
0<t\leq 1
. We write, using Lemma
4.2
and then Lemma
4.1
,

\displaystyle H_{t}^{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=H_{t}^{\lambda}(0)+\int_{0}^{x}\frac{d}{dy}\,H_{t}^{\lambda}(y)\,dy=H_{t}^{\lambda}(0)+e^{-t(2\lambda+2)}\,\int_{0}^{x}\Big[G_{t}^{\lambda+1}(y,1)-G_{t}^{\lambda+1}(-y,1)\Big]\,dy

\displaystyle\simeq H_{t}^{\lambda}(0)+\int_{0}^{x}\,\int_{-y}^{y}G_{t}^{\lambda+2}(z,1)\,dz\,dy\simeq H_{t}^{\lambda}(0)+\int_{0}^{x}\,\int_{0}^{y}G_{t}^{\lambda+2}(z,1)\,dz\,dy,

the last step by the monotonicity of

. Now let

and

.
From Lemma
5.1
, we see that the last double integral is of order of magnitude

(16)

\displaystyle\int^{\pi/2}_{\theta}\int^{\pi/2}_{\varphi}G_{t}^{\lambda+2}(\cos\kappa,1)\sin\kappa\,d\kappa\,\sin\varphi\,d\varphi\simeq t^{-\lambda-3}\,\int^{\pi/2}_{\theta}\int^{\pi/2}_{\varphi}\exp\bigg(-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4t}\bigg)\kappa\,d\kappa\,\varphi\,d\varphi

\displaystyle\qquad=2\,t^{-\lambda-2}\,\int^{\pi/2}_{\theta}\bigg[\exp\bigg(-\frac{\varphi^{2}}{4t}\bigg)-\exp\bigg(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16t}\bigg)\bigg]\,\varphi\,d\varphi

\displaystyle\qquad=4\,t^{-\lambda-1}\,\bigg[\exp\bigg(-\frac{\theta^{2}}{4t}\bigg)-\exp\bigg(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16t}\bigg)\bigg]-t^{-\lambda-2}\,\bigg[\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{2}-\theta^{2}\bigg]\,\exp\bigg(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16t}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad=4\,t^{-\lambda-1}\,\exp\bigg(-\frac{\theta^{2}}{4t}\bigg)\bigg[1-\bigg(1+\frac{(\pi/2)^{2}-\theta^{2}}{4t}\bigg)\,\exp\bigg(-\frac{(\pi/2)^{2}-\theta^{2}}{4t}\bigg)\bigg].

Now if
{((\pi/2)^{2}-\theta^{2})}/{4t}>C
for some suitably large constant
C

the product inside the square bracket in the last expression will be small,
and then the whole expression is of order of magnitude
t^{-\lambda-1}\exp(-\theta^{2}/{4t})
.
Together with (

), this implies the conclusion of the lemma.
And in the contrary case
{((\pi/2)^{2}-\theta^{2})}/{4t}\leq C
, one has

t^{-\lambda-1}\exp(-\theta^{2}/{4t})\simeq t^{-\lambda-1}\exp(-{\pi^{2}}/{16t})\simeq H_{t}^{\lambda}(0)
because of (

).
Since the expressions in (
16
) are non-negative and no larger than a constant times
t^{-\lambda-1}\exp(-\theta^{2}/{4t})
,
the conclusion follows from (
15
).
∎

Further preparations

Let
0\leq\theta,\varphi\leq\pi
. For
0\leq u,v\leq 1
we define

F(u,v):=\bigg[\arccos\bigg(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}\bigg)\bigg]^{2}.

Then
0\leq F(1,1)=(\theta-\varphi)^{2}/4\leq F(u,v)\leq\pi^{2}/4=F(0,0)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

There exist absolute constants
c_{1}>c_{2}>0
such that the bounds

\displaystyle\exp\bigg(-c_{1}\,\frac{(1-u)\theta\varphi}{t}\bigg)\exp\bigg(-c_{1}\,\frac{(1-v)(\pi-\theta)(\pi-\varphi)}{t}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{F(u,v)}{t}\bigg)\,\exp\bigg(\frac{F(1,1)}{t}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\exp\bigg(-c_{2}\,\frac{(1-u)\theta\varphi}{t}\bigg)\exp\bigg(-c_{2}\,\frac{(1-v)(\pi-\theta)(\pi-\varphi)}{t}\bigg)

hold for
\theta,\varphi\in[0,\pi]
,
u,v\in[0,1]
and
t>0
.

Proof.

We have
\cos\sqrt{F(u,v)}=u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}
.
Differentiating this equation with respect to
u
and
v
, we get

\displaystyle-\partial_{u}F(u,v)

\displaystyle=2\,\frac{\sqrt{F(u,v)}}{\sin\sqrt{F(u,v)}}\,\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}\simeq\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}\simeq\theta\varphi;

\displaystyle-\partial_{v}F(u,v)

\displaystyle=2\,\frac{\sqrt{F(u,v)}}{\sin\sqrt{F(u,v)}}\,\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}\simeq\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}\simeq(\pi-\theta)(\pi-\varphi).

Thus for any
0\leq u,v\leq 1
and
0\leq\theta,\varphi\leq\pi

F(u,v)-F(1,1)\simeq(1-u)\theta\varphi+(1-v)(\pi-\theta)(\pi-\varphi).

Dividing by
t
and exponentiating, we obtain the desired conclusion.
∎

We will also need the following.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\gamma\geq-1/2
be fixed. Then uniformly in
\xi\geq 0

\int_{[0,1]}\exp\big(-\xi(1-s)\big)\,d\Pi_{\gamma}(s)\simeq\frac{1}{(1+\xi)^{\gamma+1/2}}.

Proof.

The case
\gamma=-1/2
is trivial, so we assume
\gamma>-1/2
.
Using the definition of
\Pi_{\gamma}
and making the transformation
\sigma=\xi(1-s)
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]}\exp\big(-\xi(1-s)\big)\,d\Pi_{\gamma}(s)

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{0}^{1}\exp\big(-\xi(1-s)\big)\,(1-s)^{\gamma-1/2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\xi^{-\gamma-1/2}\,\int_{0}^{\xi}\exp(-\sigma)\,\sigma^{\gamma-1/2}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\simeq\xi^{-\gamma-1/2}\,\min\big(\xi^{\gamma+1/2},1\big)\simeq\frac{1}{(1+\xi)^{\gamma+1/2}}.

∎

6.
Proof of Theorem
A

In this proof we may assume that
T=1
, since in compact time intervals separated from
0
the short-time and long-time
bounds coincide, and the long-time bounds (
2
) are a simple consequence of the short-time bounds (see
[
10
, p. 233]
).
In what follows all relations
\simeq
and
\gtrsim
are uniform with respect to
\theta,\varphi\in[0,\pi]
and
0<t\leq 1
.

The proof of Theorem
A
is based on the five parts of Theorem
4.3
,
which are defined in terms of
\alpha
and
\beta
.
In
Part (i) of Theorem
4.3
one has
\alpha,\beta\geq-1/2
. This case is contained in the known part of
Theorem
A
and will not be considered here.

STEP 1: Part (ii) of Theorem
4.3

Here
-1<\beta<-1/2\leq\alpha
.

By Theorem
4.3
(ii)

(17)

G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta,\cos\varphi)\simeq I_{1}+I_{2},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=-(\pi-\theta)(\pi-\varphi)\iint G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+3/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,\Pi_{\beta}(v)\,dv;

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\iint G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,d\Pi_{-1/2}(v).

Observe that
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are positive, in the case of
I_{1}
since
G_{t}^{\alpha+\beta+3/2}(\cdot,1)
is increasing and

\Pi_{\beta}
is odd and decreasing. We will estimate
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
separately.

For the simpler
I_{2}
we have (the relations will be justified in a moment)

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\iint_{[0,1]^{2}}G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,d\Pi_{-1/2}(v)

\displaystyle\simeq t^{-\alpha-\beta-3/2}\int_{[0,1]}\exp\bigg(-\frac{F(u,1)}{t}\bigg)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u).

Here we first restrict the set of integration by means of the monotonicity of

G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+1/2}(\cdot,1)
(see Lemma
4.1
) and the symmetry of the measures involved.
The second relation follows from plugging in the bound of Lemma
5.1
.
Applying Lemma
5.4
with
v=1
, we conclude

I_{2}\gtrsim t^{-\alpha-\beta-3/2}\exp\bigg(-\frac{F(1,1)}{t}\bigg)\int_{[0,1]}\exp\bigg(-c_{1}\,\frac{(1-u)\theta\varphi}{t}\bigg)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u),

and this inequality can be reversed if we replace
c_{1}
by
c_{2}
. But for any fixed
c>0
, Lemma
5.5
implies

\int_{[0,1]}\exp\bigg(-c\,\frac{(1-u)\theta\varphi}{t}\bigg)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\simeq\frac{1}{(1+\theta\varphi/t)^{\alpha+1/2}}=t^{\alpha+1/2}(t+\theta\varphi)^{-\alpha-1/2}.

It follows that

(18)

I_{2}\simeq(t+\theta\varphi)^{-\alpha-1/2}\,t^{-\beta-1}\exp\bigg(-\frac{(\theta-\varphi)^{2}}{4t}\bigg).

To treat
I_{1}
, we recall that
\Pi_{\beta}(v)
is an odd function which is negative for
v>0
. Thus we have

I_{1}=(\pi-\theta)(\pi-\varphi)\int_{[-1,1]}\int_{[0,1]}\Big[G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+3/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\\
-G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+3/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}-v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\Big]\,|\Pi_{\beta}(v)|\,dv\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u).

We write the difference here as the integral from
-v
to
v
of the derivative and use Lemma
4.1
, to get

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\simeq(\pi-\theta)^{2}(\pi-\varphi)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{[-1,1]}\int_{[0,1]}\int_{-v}^{v}G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+5/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v^{\prime}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,dv^{\prime}\,|\Pi_{\beta}(v)|\,dv\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u).

Since the integrand here is increasing in
u
and in
v^{\prime}
, the symmetries of the measures
d\Pi_{\alpha}
and
dv^{\prime}

show that one can restrict the integration in these two variables to
[0,1]
and
[0,v]
, respectively.
Then we use Fubini’s theorem and integrate first in
v
,
which produces a factor
\int_{v^{\prime}}^{1}|\Pi_{\beta}(v)|\,dv\simeq(1-v^{\prime})^{\beta+3/2}
. As a result,

I_{1}\simeq(\pi-\theta)^{2}(\pi-\varphi)^{2}\int_{[0,1]}\int_{[0,1]}G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+5/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v^{\prime}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,(1-v^{\prime})^{\beta+3/2}\,dv^{\prime}\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u).

Now Lemma
5.1

tells us that

I_{1}\simeq(\pi-\theta)^{2}(\pi-\varphi)^{2}\,t^{-\alpha-\beta-7/2}\int_{[0,1]}\int_{[0,1]}\exp\bigg(-\frac{F(u,v^{\prime})}{t}\bigg)\,(1-v^{\prime})^{\beta+3/2}\,dv^{\prime}\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u).

From Lemma
5.4
we see that this expression can be estimated from above and below by constant times

\displaystyle(\pi-\theta)^{2}(\pi-\varphi)^{2}\,t^{-\alpha-\beta-7/2}\exp\bigg(-\frac{F(1,1)}{t}\bigg)

\displaystyle\;\;\times\int_{[0,1]}\exp\bigg(-c\,\frac{(1-u)\theta\varphi}{t}\bigg)\,d\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\int_{[0,1]}\exp\bigg(-c\,\frac{(1-v^{\prime})(\pi-\theta)(\pi-\varphi)}{t}\bigg)\,(1-v^{\prime})^{\beta+3/2}\,dv^{\prime},

though with different values of
c>0
in the upper and lower estimates. In the last integral here,
we can replace
(1-v^{\prime})^{\beta+3/2}\,dv^{\prime}
by the comparable measure
d\Pi_{\beta+2}(v^{\prime})
. Then
Lemma
5.5
implies that the product of the two integrals is of order of magnitude

t^{\alpha+1/2}(t+\theta\varphi)^{-\alpha-1/2}\,t^{\beta+5/2}\big[t+(\pi-\theta)(\pi-\varphi)\big]^{-\beta-5/2}.

Summing up, we have

I_{1}\simeq(t+\theta\varphi)^{-\alpha-1/2}(\pi-\theta)^{2}(\pi-\varphi)^{2}\,\,\big[t+(\pi-\theta)(\pi-\varphi)\big]^{-\beta-5/2}\,t^{-1/2}\,\exp\bigg(-\frac{(\theta-\varphi)^{2}}{4t}\bigg).

Adding this and (
18
), we obtain from (
17
)

(19)

G_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(\cos\theta,\cos\varphi)\simeq(t+\theta\varphi)^{-\alpha-1/2}\,\big[t+(\pi-\theta)(\pi-\varphi)\big]^{-\beta-1/2}\,t^{-1/2}\,\exp\bigg(-\frac{(\theta-\varphi)^{2}}{4t}\bigg),

as desired.

STEP 2: Part (iii) of Theorem
4.3

Here
-1<\alpha<-1/2\leq\beta
.

This part is completely analogous to Part (ii).
We need only swap
\alpha
with
\beta
,
u
with
v
and
\theta,\;\varphi
with
\pi-\theta,\;\pi-\varphi
.
The result will coincide with (
19
).

STEP 3: The first three integrals in Parts (iv) and (v) of Theorem
4.3

Here
-1<\alpha,\beta<-1/2
.

Neglecting irrelevant positive factors, these three integrals in Part (iv) coincide with those in Part (v), and they are

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\,\sin\theta\sin\varphi\,\iint G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+5/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,du\,\Pi_{\beta}(v)\,dv;

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\,-\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}\,\iint G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+3/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,\Pi_{\alpha}(u)\,du\,d\Pi_{-1/2}(v);

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=\,-\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2}\,\iint G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+3/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,d\Pi_{-1/2}(u)\,\Pi_{\beta}(v)\,dv.

Each of these three quantities will be seen to be positive in the estimates that follow.

We start with
J_{1}
.
Imitating the first step of the treatment of
I_{1}
above, now for both the variables
u
and
v
, we have

(20)

J_{1}=\,\sin\theta\sin\varphi\,\iint_{[0,1]^{2}}\Delta(u,v)\,|\Pi_{\alpha}(u)|\,du\,|\Pi_{\beta}(v)|\,dv,

where
\Delta(u,v)
is defined as

\displaystyle G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+5/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)-G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+5/2}\Big(-u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)

\displaystyle-\,G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+5/2}\Big(u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}-v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)+G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+5/2}\Big(-u\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}-v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big).

This second difference is also given by

(21)

\Delta(u,v)=\iint_{|u^{\prime}|<u,\,|v^{\prime}|<v}\partial_{u^{\prime}}\partial_{v^{\prime}}\Big[G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+5/2}\Big(u^{\prime}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v^{\prime}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\Big]\,du^{\prime}\,dv^{\prime}.

Applying Lemma
4.1

\displaystyle\partial_{u^{\prime}}\partial_{v^{\prime}}\Big[G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+5/2}\Big(u^{\prime}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v^{\prime}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\simeq\sin\theta\sin\varphi\>G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+9/2}\Big(u^{\prime}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v^{\prime}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big).

By combining this with (
20
) and (
21
), we obtain

J_{1}\simeq\,\sin^{2}\theta\,\sin^{2}\varphi\iint_{[0,1]^{2}}\iint_{|u^{\prime}|<u,\,|v^{\prime}|<v}\\
G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+9/2}\Big(u^{\prime}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v^{\prime}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,du^{\prime}\,dv^{\prime}\,|\Pi_{\alpha}(u)|\,du\,|\Pi_{\beta}(v)|\,dv.

As in the case of
I_{1}
in Step 1, we can restrict the inner integration here to
0<u^{\prime}<u
and

,

5.1
, and finally swap the order of integration. The result will be

J_{1}\simeq\sin^{2}\theta\,\sin^{2}\varphi\;t^{-\alpha-\beta-11/2}\iint_{[0,1]^{2}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{F(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})}{t}\bigg)\,\int_{u^{\prime}}^{1}|\Pi_{\alpha}(u)|\,du\,\int_{v^{\prime}}^{1}|\Pi_{\beta}(v)|\,dv\,du^{\prime}\,dv^{\prime}.

and

Then Lemma

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta\,\sin^{2}\varphi\;t^{-\alpha-\beta-11/2}\exp\bigg(-\frac{F(1,1)}{t}\bigg)\,

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{[0,1]}\,\exp\bigg(-c\,\frac{(1-u^{\prime})\theta\varphi}{t}\bigg)\,d\Pi_{\alpha+2}(u^{\prime})\;\int_{[0,1]}\exp\bigg(-c\,\frac{(1-v^{\prime})(\pi-\theta)(\pi-\varphi)}{t}\bigg)\,d\Pi_{\beta+2}(v^{\prime}),

with constants

(22)

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\simeq\theta^{2}\varphi^{2}\,(t+\theta\varphi)^{-\alpha-5/2}\,(\pi-\theta)^{2}(\pi-\varphi)^{2}\,\big[t+(\pi-\theta)(\pi-\varphi)\big]^{-\beta-5/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+5/2}\Big(u^{\prime}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+v\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,du^{\prime}\,|\Pi_{\alpha}(u)|\,du\,d\Pi_{-1/2}(v),

where we also wrote the difference as an integral of the derivative and used Lemma

Since

and

above, we switch the order of integration in

and

The result will be

J_{2}\simeq\,\theta^{2}\,\varphi^{2}\,\int_{[0,1]}G_{t/4}^{\alpha+\beta+5/2}\Big(u^{\prime}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi}{2}+\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi}{2},1\Big)\,d\Pi_{\alpha+2}(u^{\prime}).

J_{2}\simeq\,\theta^{2}\,\varphi^{2}\;t^{-\alpha-\beta-7/2}\,\exp\bigg(-\frac{F(1,1)}{t}\bigg)\,\int_{[0,1]}\,\exp\bigg(-c\,\frac{(1-u^{\prime})\theta\varphi}{t}\bigg)\,d\Pi_{\alpha+2}(u^{\prime}).

Here
-1<\alpha,\beta<-1/2

This integral is

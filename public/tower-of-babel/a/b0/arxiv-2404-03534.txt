Some remarks on the Gram-Schmidt walk algorithm and consequences for Komlos conjecture
Witold Bednorz and Piotr Godlewski

Abstract
In this paper we improve the best known constant for the discrepancy formulated in the Komlos Conjecture. The result is based on the improvement of the subgaussian bound for the random vector constructed in the Gram-Schmidt Random Walk algorithm. Moreover, we present detailed argument for the smoothed analysis of this random vector. The analysis concerns a modification of a given matrix in the conjecture by a Gaussian type perturbation. Our result improves the recent paper in this direction.

History

In this paper we discuss some improvement on the constant for the discrepancy problem formulated by Komlos.
Suppose that vectors
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\left\lVert v_{i}\right\rVert_{2}\leqslant 1
for
i\in[n]
.
The Komlos conjecture concerns the existence of a sequence of signs
(\varepsilon_{i})^{n}_{i=1}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}

such that

\left\lVert\sum^{n}_{i=1}\varepsilon_{i}v_{i}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant C,

where
C
is a universal constant.
First attempts to show the result reaches early eighties, when Beck and Spencer got some partial results towards the conjecture.
The first important observation was shown in 1985, in papers
[
6
,
7
]
J. Spencer proved the following observation.

Theorem 1

For any
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\left\lVert v_{i}\right\rVert_{2}\leqslant 1
there exists a sequence of signs
(\varepsilon_{i})^{n}_{i=1}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
, such that

\left\lVert\sum^{n}_{i=1}\varepsilon_{i}v_{i}\right\rVert_{2}\leqslant C\sqrt{n}\sqrt{1+\log\left(\frac{d}{n}\right)},

where
C
is a universal constant.

Then, in 1998 W. Banaszczyk
[
2
]
proved the best up now lower bound for the discrepancy.

Theorem 2

For any
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots v_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
there exists
a sequence of signs
(\varepsilon_{i})^{n}_{i=1}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}

such that

\left\lVert\sum^{n}_{i=1}\varepsilon_{i}v_{i}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant C\sqrt{1+\log d}.

Numerous results were proved about the Komlos conjecture to mention the result by Nikolov
[

who showed some convex-type result that supports the conjecture. The huge success was to discover

2
Main results

In this paper we show how to improve the best known constant for the discrepancy formulated in Komlos conjecture. Our idea is based on the Gram-Schmidt Random Walk (GSW) algorithm which gives a random vector
X=(X_{i})^{n}_{i=1}\in\left\{\pm 1\right\}^{n}_{i=1}

for a certain
d\times n
matrix
M
with all columns
M_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
to be of the Euclidean norm smaller or equal
1
. Variable
X
is obtained as the result of a certain number of improvements
X_{t}
,
0\leqslant t\leqslant T\leqslant n
, where
T
is a random number of running time of the algorithm. We explain later that some of the GSW algorithm steps may be trivial - they do not give new orthogonal vectors, that is why we prefer
\hat{T}\leqslant T
which represents number of nontrivial time steps.
The algorithm construction guarantees

\hat{T}\leqslant\mathrm{dim}\left(\mathrm{Lin}\left(M_{1},M_{2},\ldots,M_{n}\right)\right)\leqslant\min\left(d,n\right).

Our goal is to replace
d
in the Banaszczyk lower bound by
\mathbf{E}\hat{T}
. Namely,

Theorem 3

For any
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, there exists a sequence of signs
(X_{i})^{n}_{i=1}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
such that

\left\lVert\sum^{n}_{i=1}v_{i}X_{i}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant 2\max\left(1,\sqrt{2\mathbf{E}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}Z_{e_{i}}}\sqrt{\log\mathbf{E}\hat{T}}\right),

where
Z_{e_{i}}
are random variables satisfying
0\leqslant Z_{e_{i}}\leqslant 1
and
\hat{T}
is the number of nontrivial times steps of GSW algorithm -
\hat{T}\leqslant\min\left\{d,n\right\}
.

Now suppose that
R
is
d\times n
matrix of i.i.d. Gaussian entries
R_{i,j}
of

{\cal N}(0,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{d})
. Let also
Y
be a certain modification of
X

distribution, which we detail later, still defined on
\left\{\pm\right\}^{n}_{i=1}
. Note that in this direction we follow
[
5
]
, however our choice of the modification is more natural and closer to the basic definition of
X
. We show that

Theorem 4

Suppose that
n=\kappa d\log d
,
\kappa,\sigma\geqslant 1
and
\varepsilon=\sigma\sqrt{\log d}d^{-\kappa/32}
, then

\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\mathbf{P}_{Y}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)>0\right)\geqslant 1-c(d),

where
c(d)\rightarrow 0
, when
d\rightarrow\infty
.

In fact we can calculate the value of
c(d)
which is bit complex, what is important however that it behaves like
1/\log d
.
Our idea can be extended for different
R_{i,j}
ensambles, however our aim is to give precise lower bound for the basic
case.

3
GSW algorithm

Our improvement is based on the analysis of the GSW algorithm.
Let us quote the algorithm:

•

Set the initial vector
X_{1}=\mathbf{0}\in[-1,1]^{n}
, time step
t\leftarrow 1
, the set of unfrozen indices
{\cal A}_{1}\leftarrow[n]
and the pivot
p_{1}\leftarrow n
.

•

Until
{\cal A}_{t}\neq\emptyset
do:

1.

Set direction
u_{t}\leftarrow\mathrm{argmin}_{u\in U}\left\lVert Mu\right\rVert_{2}
, where

U=\{u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\;\;u(p_{t})=1,\;\;u(i)=0\;\;\forall i\not\in{\cal A}_{t}\}
.

2.

Set
\boldsymbol{\delta^{+}}\leftarrow\left|\max\Delta\right|
,
\boldsymbol{\delta^{-}}\leftarrow\left|\min\Delta\right|
, where

\Delta=\{\delta\in{\mathbb{R}}:\;\;X_{t}+\delta u_{t}\in[-1,1]^{n}\}
.

3.

Let

\boldsymbol{\delta_{t}}\leftarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\boldsymbol{\delta^{+}}&\mbox{with probab.}&\boldsymbol{\delta^{-}/\left(\delta^{-}+\delta^{+}\right)}\\
\boldsymbol{\delta^{-}}&\mbox{with probab.}&\boldsymbol{\delta^{+}/\left(\delta^{-}+\delta^{+}\right)}\end{array}\right..

4.

Set
X_{t+1}\leftarrow X_{t}+\delta_{t}u_{t}
,
{\cal A}_{t+1}\leftarrow\{i\in[n]:\;\;\left|X_{t+1}(i)\right|<1\}
,
p_{t+1}\leftarrow\max{{\cal A}_{t+1}}
,
t\leftarrow t+1
.

•

Return
X\leftarrow X_{T+1}
, where
{\cal A}_{T+1}=\emptyset
.

Our analysis of the above algorithm starts with an observation that for each time step
\boldsymbol{t}
we have
\boldsymbol{t}\leqslant T+1\leqslant n+1
, where
T
is random.
We also control pivot number
\boldsymbol{p}\in[n]
, which indicated the vector
v_{\boldsymbol{p}}
used by the algorithm as a pivot.
Note that not all vectors may become the pivot and we denote the set of pivots by
{\cal P}
. Furthermore, we denote the time step in which
\boldsymbol{p}
becomes the pivot by
t_{\boldsymbol{p}}
and the pivot number in time step
\boldsymbol{t}
by
\boldsymbol{p_{t}}
.
We say that an index

is frozen in time step
\boldsymbol{t}
if
v_{i}\in{\cal A}_{t}\backslash{\cal A}_{t+1}
.

We define an order
\sigma
on
[n]
in the following way. We start by setting
\sigma(n)=\boldsymbol{p_{1}}=n
. Next, we set
\sigma(n-1)=j
if

is frozen in time step
\boldsymbol{t}=1
.
If there are more frozen coordinates e.g.
j_{1}>j_{2}>\ldots>j_{r}
then
\sigma(n-i)=j_{i}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
. Then we change the time phase and now either we have
a new pivot
\boldsymbol{p_{2}}\neq\boldsymbol{p_{1}}
and then
\sigma(n-r-1)=\boldsymbol{p_{2}}

or
\boldsymbol{p_{2}}=\boldsymbol{p_{1}}
and then once again we consider all other frozen coordinates
in this time step and numerate them by
\sigma
in the same way as described in time step one.
We continue until time step
T+1
in which all coordinates are frozen.

We now start the orthogonalisation, namely we define orthogonal vectors
w_{\sigma(1)},w_{\sigma(2)},\ldots w_{\sigma(n)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by

w_{\sigma(1)}=\frac{v_{\sigma(1)}}{\left\lVert v_{\sigma(1)}\right\rVert_{2}},\;\;w_{\sigma(r)}=\frac{v_{\sigma(r)}-A_{r}v_{\sigma(r)}}{\left\lVert v_{\sigma(r)}-A_{r}v_{\sigma(r)}\right\rVert_{2}},

where
A_{r}=\sum_{s<r}w_{\sigma(s)}w^{T}_{\sigma(s)}
.
Note that
A_{r}
is the orthogonal projection on space

\mathrm{Lin}(v_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(r-1)})
.
By the construction all
w_{\sigma(r)}
are orthogonal, we have either
\left\lVert w_{\sigma(r)}\right\rVert_{2}=1
or
w_{\sigma(r)}=0
, and
Mu_{t}=v_{\boldsymbol{p_{t}}}-A_{t}v_{\boldsymbol{p_{t}}}
.
Moreover,
w_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,w_{\sigma(n)}
form an orthonormal basis of
V=\mathrm{Lin}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
, thus

\displaystyle Mu_{t}=\sum^{n}_{r=1}\left\langle w_{\sigma(r)},v_{\boldsymbol{p_{t}}}-A_{t}v_{\boldsymbol{p_{t}}}\right\rangle w_{\sigma(r)}=\sum^{n}_{r=1}\left[\left\langle w_{\sigma(r)},v_{\boldsymbol{p_{t}}}\right\rangle-\left\langle A_{t}w_{\sigma(r)},v_{\boldsymbol{p_{t}}}\right\rangle\right]w_{\sigma(r)}.

Let
\ell_{t}=\left|{\cal A}_{t}\right|
. It is crucial to observe that
A_{t}w_{\sigma(r)}=w_{\sigma(r)}
if
r<\ell_{t}
since
w_{\sigma(r)}
is already in the space on which
A_{t}
projects.
On the other hand, by the construction
A_{t}w_{\sigma(r)}=0
for all
r\geqslant\ell_{t}
. Finally let
g_{t}=\sigma^{-1}(\boldsymbol{p_{t}})
, for any
r>g_{t}
we have
w_{\sigma(r)}\bot v_{\boldsymbol{p_{t}}}
.
Therefore

Mu_{t}=\sum^{g_{t}}_{r=\ell_{t}}\left\langle w_{\sigma(r)},v_{\boldsymbol{p_{t}}}\right\rangle w_{\sigma(r)}.

(1)

Let us fix pivot
\boldsymbol{p}
and let

I_{\boldsymbol{p}}=\{t\geqslant 1:\;\;\boldsymbol{p_{t}}=\boldsymbol{p}\}=\{t\geqslant t_{\boldsymbol{p}}:\;\;\boldsymbol{p_{t}}=\boldsymbol{p}\}

be the set of time steps for which
\boldsymbol{p}

There are
k+1

is the pivot, i.e.:

w_{\sigma(r-k)},w_{\sigma(r-k-1)},\ldots,w_{\sigma(r)},

(2)

We can split
V
into orthogonal subspaces
\left(V_{\boldsymbol{p}}\right)_{\boldsymbol{p}\in{\cal P}}
, where
V_{\boldsymbol{p}}
is spanned by (
2

(3)

Now we split the set of coordinates

This allows us to split

, spanned by vectors from

P_{Q^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{t}}=\sum_{r\in Q^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{t}}w_{\sigma(r)}w^{T}_{\sigma(r)}.

(4)

For any

(5)

We want to estimate the change in discrepancy for pivot

\sum_{t\in I_{\boldsymbol{p}}}M\left(X_{t+1}-X_{t}\right)=\sum_{t\in I_{\boldsymbol{p}}}M\delta_{t}u_{t}.

Using (

\sum_{t\in I_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\delta_{t}\left\langle Mu_{t},v\right\rangle=\sum^{n}_{t=t_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\delta_{t}\sum^{t-1}_{s=t_{\boldsymbol{p}}-1}\sum_{r\in Q^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{s}}\left\langle w_{\sigma(r)},v_{\boldsymbol{p}}\right\rangle\left\langle w_{\sigma(r)},v\right\rangle.

4
Analysis of the Laplace transform

Let
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}}
be generated by
\delta_{t}
for
t<t_{\boldsymbol{p}}
. We define

\alpha_{r}=\left\langle w_{\sigma(r)},v_{\boldsymbol{p}}\right\rangle,\;\;\beta_{r}=\left\langle w_{\sigma(r)},v\right\rangle.

and

F=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum^{n}_{t=t_{\boldsymbol{p}}-1}\left|\sum_{r\in Q^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{t}}\alpha_{r}\beta_{r}\right|\right)^{2}.

We show that conditionally
F
is the right

Proposition 1

The following inequality holds true

\mathbf{E}\left(\exp\left(\sum_{t\in I_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\delta_{t}\left\langle Mu_{t},v\right\rangle-F\right)\;\middle|\;\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}}\right)\leqslant 1.

Proof.

For any
R=t_{\boldsymbol{p}},\ldots,n
we define

S_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)=\sum^{n}_{t=t_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\delta_{t}\sum^{(R\wedge t)-1}_{s=t_{\boldsymbol{p}}-1}\sum_{r\in Q^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{s}}\alpha_{r}\beta_{r}.

Moreover, let

F_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\sum^{R-1}_{t=t_{\boldsymbol{p}}-1}\left|\sum_{r\in Q^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{t}}\alpha_{r}\beta_{r}\right|\right]^{2}

and

g(R)=\mathbf{E}\left(\exp\left(S_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)-F_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)\right)\;\middle|\;\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}}\right).

We are going to show that

g(n+1)\geqslant g(n)\leqslant g(n-1)\leqslant\ldots g(t_{\boldsymbol{p}})\leqslant 1.

The inequality
g(n+1)\geqslant g(n)
is trivial.
We aim to show that for
R+1\leqslant n
, it holds true that

g(R+1)=\mathbf{E}\left(\exp\left(S_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R+1)-F_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R+1)\right)\;\middle|\;\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}}\right)\leqslant\mathbf{E}\left(\exp\left(S_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)-F_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)\right)\;\middle|\;\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}}\right)=g(R).

(7)

We observe that for
R<n
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left(\exp\left(S_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R+1)-F_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R+1)\right)\;\middle|\;\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum^{R-1}_{t=t_{\boldsymbol{p}]}}\delta_{t}\sum^{t-1}_{s=t_{\boldsymbol{p}}-1}\sum_{r\in Q^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{s}}\alpha_{r}\beta_{r}-F_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R+1)\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\mathbf{E}\exp\left(\sum^{n}_{t=R}\delta_{t}\sum^{R}_{s=t_{\boldsymbol{p}}-1}\sum_{r\in Q^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{s}}\alpha_{r}\beta_{r}\;\middle|\;\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)\right),

where
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)
is generated by all
\delta_{t}
for

Clearly,
\exp\left(S_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)-F_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)\right)

Therefore, to show (

\mathbf{E}\exp\left(\sum^{n}_{t=R}\delta_{t}\sum^{R}_{s=t_{\boldsymbol{p}}-1}\sum_{r\in Q^{\boldsymbol{p}}_{s}}\alpha_{r}\beta_{r}\;\middle|\;\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)\right)\leqslant\exp(F_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R+1)-F_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)).

(8)

Note that under
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)

\alpha_{r},\beta_{r}

\delta_{R}=\sum^{n}_{t=R}\delta_{t}

can attain only two values:
1\pm z_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)
, where
\left|z_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)\right|\leqslant 1
and
\mathbf{E}\left(\delta_{R}\;\middle|\;\Delta_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)\right)=0
. More precisely there exists

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathbf{P}(\delta_{R}=-1-z_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R))&=&\frac{1-z_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)}{2}\\
\mathbf{P}(\delta_{R}=1-z_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R))&=&\frac{1+z_{\boldsymbol{p}}(R)}{2}\end{array}.

(9)

Consequently, the proof of (
8
) reduces to the following result.

Lemma 1

For any
x\in[-1,1]
and
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
it holds true that

\frac{1-x}{2}\exp(-(1+x)(a+b))+\frac{1+x}{2}\exp((1-x)(a+b))\leqslant\exp\left(\left|a\right|\left|b\right|+\frac{1}{2}\left|b\right|^{2}\right).

Proof.

Let us define

L=\frac{(1-x)e^{-(1+x)(a+b)}+(1+x)e^{(1-x)(a+b)}}{(1-x)e^{-(1+x)a}+(1+x)e^{(1-x)a}}.

Observe that

L=pe^{-b(1+x)}+qe^{b(1-x)},

where

p=\frac{(1-x)e^{-a}}{(1-x)e^{-a}+(1+x)e^{a}},\;\;q=\frac{(1+x)e^{a}}{(1-x)e^{-a}+(1+x)e^{a}}.

Obviously,

S=-p(1+x)b+q(1-x)b=\left(1-x^{2}\right)\frac{e^{a}-e^{-a}}{e^{a}+e^{-a}+x\left(e^{a}-e^{-a}\right)}b=\frac{(1-x^{2})\sinh a}{\cosh{a}+x\sinh{a}}b.

We show that

\left|\frac{(1-x^{2})\sinh a}{\cosh{a}+x\sinh{a}}\right|\leqslant F(x)=\frac{(1-x^{2})\left|\sinh{a}\right|}{\cosh{a}-\left|x\right|\left|\sinh{a}\right|}\leqslant\left|a\right|.

The maximum of
F
is attained at

\left|x\right|=\frac{\cosh{a}-1}{\left|\sinh{a}\right|}

and is equal

\frac{2\left(\cosh{a}-1\right)}{\left|\sinh{a}\right|}.

We end up in the well known inequality

\cosh{a}-1\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\left|a\right|\left|\sinh{a}\right|

which proves that
\left|S\right|\leqslant\left|a\right|\left|b\right|
. We can now use Hoeffding lemma to show that

Le^{-S}=pe^{-(1+x)b-S}+qe^{(1-x)b-S}\leqslant e^{\frac{1}{8}(2b)^{2}}=e^{\frac{1}{2}b^{2}}.

Hence

L\leqslant e^{S+\frac{1}{2}b^{2}}\leqslant e^{\left|a\right|\left|b\right|+\frac{1}{2}\left|b\right|^{2}}.

\blacksquare

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{E}\left(\cosh\left(\lambda\left\langle MX,v\right\rangle\right)-1\right)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}Z_{v}\right)&\leqslant\mathbf{E}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}Z_{v}\right)\right)\\
&\leq 1-\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\mathbf{E}Z_{v}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\mathbf{E}Z_{v}.\end{split}

(13)

Let us fix a constant
C>0
. Note that we can split event
\left\{\left\lVert MX\right\rVert_{\infty}>C\right\}
into sets
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots A_{m}
which are pairwise
disjoint and

A_{i}=\left\{\left|\left\langle MX,e_{i}\right\rangle\right|>C\right\}\cap\bigcap^{i-1}_{j=1}\left\{\left|\left\langle MX,e_{i}\right\rangle\right|\leqslant C\right\}.

Let us observe that

\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\mathbf{E}Z_{e_{i}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}/\mathbf{P}(A_{i})\right)\leqslant\mathbf{E}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}Z_{e_{i}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}/\mathbf{P}(A_{i}).

Suppose that
\mathbf{P}\left(\left\lVert MX\right\rVert_{\infty}>C\right)=1
, which means
\sum^{d}_{i=1}\mathbf{P}(A_{i})=1
and by Jensen’s inequality and (
13
) we have

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\sum^{d}_{i=1}\mathbf{E}Z_{e_{i}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum^{d}_{i=1}\mathbf{P}(A_{i})\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\mathbf{E}Z_{e_{i}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}/\mathbf{P}(A_{i})\right)\leqslant\sum^{d}_{i=1}\mathbf{E}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}Z_{e_{i}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\cosh\left(C\lambda\right)-1}\sum^{d}_{i=1}\mathbf{E}\left(\cosh\left(\lambda\left\langle MX,e_{i}\right\rangle\right)-1\right)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}Z_{e_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\left(\cosh\left(C\lambda\right)-1\right)}\sum^{d}_{i=1}\mathbf{E}Z_{e_{i}}\leqslant\frac{\lambda^{2}\mathbf{E}\hat{T}}{2\left(\cosh\left(C\lambda\right)-1\right)}.

On the other hand,

\sum^{d}_{i=1}\mathbf{E}Z_{e_{i}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}\leqslant\mathbf{E}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}Z_{e_{i}}\leqslant 1.

Therefore

\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\mathbf{E}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}Z_{e_{i}}\right)\leqslant\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\left(\cosh\left(C\lambda\right)-1\right)}.

Note that

\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\left(\cosh\left(C\lambda\right)-1\right)}\leqslant\frac{\lambda^{2}}{e^{C\lambda}-1-C\lambda}.

Thus for
C\geqslant 2
the following inequality holds true

e^{C\lambda}-1-C\lambda>\lambda^{2}e^{C\lambda/2}

since

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=2}\frac{\left(C\lambda\right)^{k}}{k!}=\sum^{\infty}_{l=0}\frac{\left(C\lambda\right)^{l+2}}{(l+2)!}=\sum^{\infty}_{l=0}\frac{\left(C\lambda/2\right)^{l}}{l!}\frac{2^{l}\left(C\lambda\right)^{2}}{(l+1)(l+2)}>\lambda^{2}e^{C\lambda/2}.

We have used here that
C\geqslant 2
and
2^{l+2}>(l+1)(l+2)
for all
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Hence

\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\mathbf{E}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}Z_{e_{i}}\right)<\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}C\lambda\right)\mathbf{E}\hat{T}.

The optimal
\lambda
equals
C/\left(2\mathbf{E}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}Z_{e_{i}}\right)
and then

\frac{1}{2}C\lambda-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\mathbf{E}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}Z_{e_{i}}=\frac{C^{2}}{8\mathbf{E}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}Z_{e_{i}}}

So if
C\geqslant 2
then

\exp\left(\frac{C^{2}}{8\mathbf{E}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}Z_{e_{i}}}\right)<\mathbf{E}\hat{T}

so

C<2\sqrt{2\mathbf{E}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}Z_{e_{i}}}\sqrt{\log\mathbf{E}\hat{T}}.

Thus if
C\geqslant 2\max\left\{1,\sqrt{2\mathbf{E}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}Z_{e_{i}}}\sqrt{\log\mathbf{E}\hat{T}}\right\}
, we have a contradiction. This completes the proof.

This shows the main improvement of the Banaszczyk result

Theorem 6

There exists
X=(X_{i})^{n}_{i=1}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
such that

\left\lVert MX\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant 2\max\left(1,\sqrt{2\mathbf{E}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}Z_{e_{i}}}\sqrt{\log\mathbf{E}\hat{T}}\right),

where
K
is a universal constant.

We now turn to the analysis of a perturbation of
M
. Suppose that
\bar{X}

is generated by the GSW algorithm however not for the original matrix
M
but rather for the matrix
\bar{M}=2^{-1/2}(M^{T},\mathrm{Id}_{n})^{T}

Moreover,

\mathbf{E}\exp\left(\lambda\left\langle\bar{X},v\right\rangle\right)\leqslant\exp\left(\lambda^{2}\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}\right),

since
Z_{v}\leqslant\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}

The point is that we have to slightly modify the distribution of
\bar{X}
. More precisely, we use

X
distributed on
\{\pm 1\}^{n}

where

Note that

however much better upper bound for variance is proved in

]

independent of

,

are independent. We require that
R_{i,j}
are independent of Gaussian distribution

.
Note that conditionally on
X

\left\langle M_{i}+R_{i},X\right\rangle
are independent.
We are going to use the second moment approach, namely

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\mathbf{P}_{X}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)>0\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\left[{\mathbf{E}_{R}\mathbf{P}_{X}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)}\right]^{2}}{\mathbf{E}_{R}\left[\mathbf{P}_{X}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\right]^{2}}.

Our main result states that if
n\sim d\log d
, and
\varepsilon=d^{-c}
, where

then the above probability is almost
1
.
Formally, we aim to show Theorem

with

as the required modification.

We observe that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{R}\left[\mathbf{P}_{X}\left(\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\right)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\bigcap^{d}_{i=1}\left\{\left|\left\langle M_{i}+R_{i},X\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap\bigcap^{d}_{i=1}\left\{\left|\left\langle M_{i}+R_{i},Y\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\prod^{d}_{i=1}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\{\left|\left\langle M_{i}+R_{i},X\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap\left\{\left|\left\langle M_{i}+R_{i},Y\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\right).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{E}_{R}\mathbf{P}_{X}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\mathbf{P}_{R^{1}}\left(\bigcap^{d}_{i=1}\left|\left\langle M_{i}+R^{1}_{i},X\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\mathbf{P}_{R^{2}}\left(\bigcap^{d}_{i=1}\left|\left\langle M_{i}+R^{2}_{i},X\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\prod^{d}_{i=1}\mathbf{P}_{R^{1}}\left(\left|\left\langle M_{i}+R^{1}_{i},X\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\mathbf{P}_{R^{2}}\left(\left|\left\langle M_{i}+R^{2}_{i},Y\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right).

6
Main comparison result

We are going to show that that there exists
C_{i}(X,Y)
for each
i\in d

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\{\left|\left\langle M_{i}+R_{i},X\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\}\cap\{\left|\left\langle M_{i}+R_{i},Y\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{i}(X,Y)\mathbf{P}_{R^{1}}\left(\left|\left\langle M_{i}+R^{1}_{i},X\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\mathbf{P}_{R^{2}}\left(\left|\left\langle M_{i}+R^{2}_{i},Y\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right).

(17)

Let us now fix
X=x\in\left\{\pm 1\right\}^{n}_{j=1}
. We choose
R_{ij}=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{d}}G_{ij}
, where
G_{ij}
are independent symmetric Gaussian variables.
Clearly for each
i\in[d]
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{G_{i}}\left(\left|\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{d}}\sum^{n}_{j=1}G_{i,j}x_{j}-\sum^{n}_{j=1}M_{ij}x_{j}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon,\;\;\left|\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{d}}\sum^{n}_{j=1}G_{ij}y_{j}-\sum^{n}_{j=1}m_{ij}y_{j}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}_{g_{1},g_{2}}\left(\left\{\left|\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\sqrt{k}g_{1}+\sqrt{n-k}g_{2}\right)-m_{i}(1)\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap\left\{\left|\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\sqrt{k}g_{1}-\sqrt{n-k}g_{2}\right)-m_{i}(2)\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}_{g_{1},g_{2}}\left(\left|\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{d}}\sqrt{k}g_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{i}(1)+m_{i}(2)\right)\right|+\left|\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{d}}\sqrt{n-k}g_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{i}(1)-m_{i}(2)\right)\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right),

where
g_{1},g_{2}
independent Gaussian r.v.’s and

m_{i}(1)=\left\langle M_{i},x\right\rangle,\;\;m_{i}(2)=\left\langle M_{i},y\right\rangle.

We denote

k=\left|\left\{j\in[n]:\;\;x_{i}=y_{i}\right\}\right|,\;\;n-k=\left|\left\{j\in[n]:\;\;x_{i}=y_{i}\right\}\right|.

(18)

Note that

k=\frac{1}{4}\left|x+y\right|^{2},\;\;n-k=\frac{1}{4}\left|x-y\right|^{2},\;\;2k-n=\left\langle x,y\right\rangle.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{g_{1},g_{2}}\left(\left|\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{d}}\sqrt{k}g_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{i}(1)+m_{i}(2)\right)\right|+\left|\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{d}}\sqrt{n-k}g_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{i}(1)-m_{i}(2)\right)\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{d}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}\sqrt{n-k}}\int_{\left|\bar{u}\right|+\left|\bar{v}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon}\exp\left(-\frac{d}{2\sigma^{2}k}\left|\bar{u}-\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{i}(1)+m_{i}(2)\right)\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\exp\left(\frac{d}{2\sigma^{2}(n-k)}\left|\bar{v}-\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{i}(1)-m_{i}(2)\right)\right|^{2}\right)d\bar{u}d\bar{v}

The main problem in the above formula is
m_{i}(1)m_{i}(2)

Moreover,

\frac{d\left(n-2k\right)^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}nk(n-k)}\left(\left|u-m_{i}(1)\right|^{2}+\left|v-m_{i}(2)\right|^{2}\right)\geqslant\frac{d(n-2k)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}nk(n-k)}\left|u-m_{i}(1)\right|\left|v-m_{i}(2)\right|.

Note that of
k\leqslant n/2
,

\frac{d\left|n-2k\right|n}{4\sigma^{2}nk(n-k)}-\frac{d(n-2k)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}nk(n-k)}=\frac{d\left|n-2k\right|}{2\sigma^{2}n(n-k)}=\frac{d\left|n-2k\right|}{2\sigma^{2}n\max\left\{k,n-k\right\}}.

Similarly, for
k>n/2

\frac{d\left|n-2k\right|n}{4\sigma^{2}nk(n-k)}-\frac{d(n-2k)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}nk(n-k)}=\frac{d\left|n-2k\right|}{2\sigma^{2}nk}=\frac{d\left|n-2k\right|}{2\sigma^{2}n\max\left\{k,n-k\right\}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{d(2k-n)}{4\sigma^{2}k(n-k)}(u-m_{i}(1))(v-m_{i}(2))=\mathrm{sgn}\left(2k-n\right)\frac{d\left|n-2k\right|}{\sigma^{2}k(n-k)}(u-m_{i}(1))(v-m_{i}(2))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{sgn}\left(2k-n\right)\left(\frac{d\left|n-2k\right|}{2n\sigma^{2}\max\left\{k,n-k\right\}}+\frac{d(n-2k)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}nk(n-k)}\right)(u-m_{i}(1))(v-m_{i}(2))

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{d\left(2k-n\right)}{2n\sigma^{2}\max\left\{k,n-k\right\}}(u-m_{i}(1))(v-m_{i}(2))+\frac{d(n-2k)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}nk(n-k)}\left|u-m_{i}(1)\right|\left|v-m_{i}(2)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{d\left(2k-n\right)}{2\sigma^{2}n\max\left\{k,n-k\right\}}(u-m_{i}(1))(v-m_{i}(2))+\frac{d(n-2k)^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}nk(n-k)}\left(\left|u-m_{i}(1)\right|^{2}+\left|v-m_{i}(2)\right|^{2}\right).

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{d}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{k}\sqrt{n-k}}\int_{\left|u\right|\leqslant\varepsilon}\int_{\left|v\right|\leqslant\varepsilon}\exp\left(-\frac{dn}{8\sigma^{2}k(n-k)}\left(\left|u-m_{i}(1)\right|^{2}+\left|v-m_{i}(2)\right|^{2}\right)\right)

(19)

Hence

C(x,y)\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-n^{-2}\left\langle x,y\right\rangle^{2}}}\right)^{d}\exp\left(\frac{d\left|\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\right|}{n^{2}\sigma^{2}}\left(\varepsilon\sqrt{d}\sqrt{8CV}+\varepsilon^{2}d+\left|\left\langle\bar{x},y\right\rangle\right|\right)\right).

(20)

7
How to split the bound

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{G_{i}}\left(\left|\sum^{n}_{j=1}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{d}}G_{ij}x_{j}-\sum^{n}_{j=1}m_{ij}x_{j}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right)=\mathbf{P}_{g}\left(\left|\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{d}}\sqrt{n}g-\left\langle M_{i},x\right\rangle\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{d}}{\sqrt{2\pi n}\sigma}\int^{\varepsilon}_{-\varepsilon}\exp\left(-\frac{d\left(u-\left\langle M_{i},x\right\rangle\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}\right)du\geqslant\exp\left(-\frac{d\left\langle M_{i},x\right\rangle^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}\right)\gamma_{1}(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{1}),

where
K_{1}=[-1,1]
and
\gamma_{1}
- standard Gaussian measure. Therefore, for
x\in A

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)=\prod^{d}_{i=1}\mathbf{P}_{g_{i}}\left(\left|\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{d}}\sqrt{n}g_{i}-\left\langle M_{i},x\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\exp\left(-\sum^{d}_{i=1}\frac{d\left\langle M_{i},x\right\rangle^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}\right)\gamma_{d}(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{d})=\exp\left(-\frac{d\left\lVert Mx\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}\right)\gamma_{d}(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{d}),

where
K_{d}=[-1,1]^{d}
and
\gamma_{d}
is the standard Gaussian measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Thus

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbf{E}_{Y}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)=W^{-1}\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\frac{d\left\lVert M\bar{Y}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}\right)\mathbf{1}_{Y\in B}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)\bar{Y}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\\
&\geqslant W^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{\bar{Y}}\left(Y\in B\right)\gamma_{d}(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{d})\geqslant W^{-1}\left(1-C^{-1}\right)\gamma_{d}\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{d}\right),\end{split}

(21)

where we used that
\mathbf{P}_{\bar{Y}}(\bar{Y}\in B)\geqslant 1-C^{-1}
and that

W=\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\mathbf{1}_{\bar{Y}\in B}\exp\left(\frac{d\left\lVert M\bar{Y}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}\right).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{d}}{\sqrt{2\pi n}\sigma}\int^{\varepsilon}_{-\varepsilon}\exp\left(-\frac{d\left(u-\left\langle M_{i},x\right\rangle\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}\right)du

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-d\frac{\left\langle M_{i},x\right\rangle^{2}}{2n\sigma^{2}}\right)\frac{\sqrt{d}}{\sqrt{2\pi n}\sigma}\int^{\varepsilon}_{-\varepsilon}\exp\left(-\frac{d\left(u\right)^{2}}{2n\sigma^{2}}\right)\cosh\left(\frac{du\left\langle M_{i},x\right\rangle}{\sigma^{2}n}\right)du

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left(-d\frac{\left\langle M_{i},x\right\rangle^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}\right)\gamma_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}\right)K_{1})\cosh\left(\frac{d\varepsilon\left\langle M_{i},x\right\rangle}{\sigma^{2}n}\right).

Consequently, we have on
\bar{Y}\in B
, i.e.
\left\lVert M\bar{Y}\right\rVert\leqslant 2CV
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)=\prod^{d}_{i=1}\mathbf{P}_{g_{i}}\left(\left|\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{d}}\sqrt{n}g_{i}-\left\langle M_{i},x\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left(-\sum^{d}_{i=1}\frac{d\left\langle M_{i},x\right\rangle^{2}}{2n\sigma^{2}}\right)\gamma_{d}\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}\sigma\varepsilon}{\sqrt{d}}K_{d}\right)\prod^{d}_{i=1}\cosh\left(\frac{d\varepsilon\left\langle M_{i},x\right\rangle}{n\sigma^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left(\left(\frac{d\varepsilon^{2}}{\sigma^{2}n}-1\right)\frac{d\left\lVert Mx\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}\right)\gamma_{d}\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{d}\right)\leqslant\exp\left(\frac{d^{2}\varepsilon^{2}CV}{\sigma^{4}n^{2}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{d\left\lVert MY\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}\right)\gamma_{d}\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{d}\right).

It shows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{Y}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)=W^{-1}\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\frac{d\left\lVert M\bar{Y}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\lVert M\bar{Y}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leqslant 2CV}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)\bar{Y}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant W^{-1}\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\mathbf{1}_{Y\in B}\gamma_{d}\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{d}\right)\leqslant W^{-1}\exp\left(\frac{d^{2}\varepsilon^{2}2CV}{2\sigma^{4}n^{2}}\right)\gamma_{d}\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{d}\right).

(22)

Now we split the main bound

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{R}\left[\mathbf{P}_{X}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\right|\leqslant n/2}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\right|>n/2}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\right).

8
Bound for
\left|\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\right|>n/2

We first bound the case of
\left|\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\right|\geqslant n/2
. Note that

\left\lVert M\bar{Y}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\leqslant 2CV
for
\bar{Y}\in B
, so

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{Y}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\right|>n/2}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|>n/2}\mathbf{1}_{\bar{Y}\in B}\exp\left(\frac{d\left\lVert M\bar{Y}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left(\frac{dCV}{\sigma^{2}n}\right)\mathbf{P}_{\bar{Y}}\left(\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|>n/2\right).

This event has a small probability, namely

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\bar{Y}}\left(\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|>n/2\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2e^{-\lambda n/2}\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\lambda\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right)=2e^{-\frac{1}{2}\lambda n+\lambda^{2}n}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2e^{-n/16},\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;\lambda=1/4.

Therefore, by (
21
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\right|>n/2}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}]\cap\left\{\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\right|>n/2}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{X}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\mathbf{E}_{Y}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\right|>n/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2e^{-n/16}\exp\left(\frac{dCV}{\sigma^{2}n}\right)\mathbf{E}_{X}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2e^{-n/16}\exp\left(\frac{dCV}{\sigma^{2}n}\right)W\left(1-C^{-1}\right)^{-1}\gamma^{-1}_{d}\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{d}\right)\left[\mathbf{E}_{X}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\right]^{2}.

In order to bound
W
we can use

W\leqslant\exp\left(\frac{dCV}{\sigma^{2}n}\right),

and thus finally

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\right|>n/2}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}]\cap\left\{\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2(1-C^{-1})^{-1}e^{-n/16}\exp\left(\frac{2dCV}{\sigma^{2}n}\right)\gamma^{-1}_{d}\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{d}\right)\left[\mathbf{E}_{X}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\right]^{2}.

We aim to analyze when

2e^{-n/16}\exp\left(\frac{2dCV}{\sigma^{2}n}\right)\gamma^{-1}_{d}\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{d}\right)\leqslant 2e^{-n/64}.

This can be done be requiring that

\gamma_{d}\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{d}\right)\geqslant e^{-n/32}

(23)

and

\frac{2dCV}{\sigma^{2}n}\leqslant\frac{n}{64},\;\;n\geqslant 8\sqrt{d}\sqrt{2CV}.

(24)

Clearly (
23
) reads as

\gamma_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{1}\right)\geqslant e^{-n/\left(32d\right)},\;\;\mbox{i.e.}\;\;\mathbf{P}\left(\left|g\right|\leqslant\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\right)\geqslant e^{-\frac{n}{32d}}.

Therefore, we require that
\varepsilon\geqslant\varepsilon_{0}
for
\varepsilon_{0}
satisfying

\mathbf{P}\left(\left|g\right|\leqslant\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon_{0}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\right)=e^{-\frac{n}{32d}}.

We require that
\sqrt{d}\varepsilon_{0}/\left(\sigma\sqrt{n}\right)\leqslant c
for a small positive constant
c
, and thus we can use the inequality

P\left(\left|g\right|\leqslant\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon_{0}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\right)\geqslant\frac{2\varepsilon_{0}\sqrt{d}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma\sqrt{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{c^{2}}{2}\right).

Hence

\varepsilon_{0}\leqslant\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{\frac{c^{2}}{2}}\frac{\sigma\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{d}}e^{-\frac{n}{32d}}

and thus we assume that

\varepsilon\geqslant\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{\frac{c^{2}}{2}}\frac{\sigma\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{d}}e^{-\frac{n}{32d}}.

The right interval for
\varepsilon
is

\frac{\sigma\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{d}}e^{-\frac{n}{32d}}\lesssim\varepsilon.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\right|>n/2}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}]\cap\left\{\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2e^{-n/64}\left(1-C^{-1}\right)^{-1}\left[\mathbf{E}_{X}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\right]^{2}.

9
Bound for
\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\leqslant n/2

Now we analyze the case when
\left|\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\right|<n/2
. Here we use the our bound on
C(x,y)
.
Namely, by (
22
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\right|\leqslant n/2}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\right|\leqslant n/2}C(X,Y)\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant W^{-1}\exp\left(\frac{d^{2}\varepsilon^{2}2CV}{2\sigma^{4}n^{2}}\right)\gamma_{d}\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n}\sigma}K_{d}\right).\mathbf{E}_{X}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|\leqslant n/2}C(X,\bar{Y}).

Note that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(1-x^{2}\right)\right)\leqslant e^{\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+\frac{1}{2}x^{4}}\leqslant\exp\left(\frac{5}{8}x^{2}\right),\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;\left|x\right|<1/2.

Recall the definition of
\hat{X}
i.e. (
19
). We have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|\leqslant n/2}C(X,\bar{Y})

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-n^{-2}\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle^{2}}}\right)^{d}\exp\left(\frac{d\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\left(\varepsilon^{2}d+\varepsilon\sqrt{d}\sqrt{8CV}\right)+\left|\left\langle\hat{X},\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\frac{5d}{8n^{2}}\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle^{2}+\frac{d\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|)}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\left(\varepsilon^{2}d+\varepsilon\sqrt{d}\sqrt{8CV}+\left|\left\langle\hat{X},\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|\right)\right).

By Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|\leqslant n/2}C(X,\bar{Y})

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\frac{5d}{4n^{2}}\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle^{2}+\frac{2d\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|)}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\left(\varepsilon^{2}d+\varepsilon\sqrt{d}\sqrt{8CV}\right)\right)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\frac{2d}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|\left|\left\langle\hat{X},\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|\right)\right)^{1/2}.

However,
\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\bar{Y}}\left(\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|>u\right)\leqslant 2e^{-\lambda u}\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\lambda\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right)=2e^{-\lambda u+\lambda^{2}n}\leqslant 2e^{-u^{2}/\left(4n\right)}

for
\lambda=u/(2n)
.
Now for

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(2bt+a\right)e^{at+bt^{2}}e^{-ct^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{-b}{c-b}\left(-2(c-b)t+a\right)e^{at-(c-b)t^{2}}dt+\frac{ca}{c-b}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{at}e^{-(c-b)t^{2}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{c-b}+\frac{ca}{c-b}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{c-b}}\frac{\sqrt{2(c-b)}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{at}e^{-(2(c-b))t^{2}/2}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{c-b}+\frac{ca}{c-b}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{c-b}}e^{\frac{a^{2}}{4(c-b)}}.

In our case

a=\frac{2d}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\left(\varepsilon^{2}d+\varepsilon\sqrt{d}\sqrt{8CV}\right),\;\;b=\frac{5d}{4n^{2}}\,\;\;c=\frac{1}{4n}

The crucial assumption

b\leqslant\frac{1}{2}c,\;\;\mbox{ie.}\;\;10d\leqslant n

and

\frac{a}{\sqrt{b-c}}=\sqrt{8n}\frac{2d}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\left(\varepsilon^{2}d+\varepsilon\sqrt{d}\sqrt{8CV}\right)\leqslant\delta,

where
\delta
is suitably small.
It holds true, for example when

\varepsilon\leqslant\frac{\delta}{32}\sigma^{2}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{3/2}(CV)^{-1/2},\;\;\varepsilon\leqslant\frac{\delta^{1/2}}{4}\sigma\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)n^{-1/4}.

Therefore,

\frac{b}{c-b}+\frac{ca}{c-b}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{c-b}}e^{\frac{a^{2}}{4(c-b)}}\leqslant 10\frac{d}{n}+2\sqrt{\pi}\delta e^{\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}}

It shows that

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\frac{d}{n^{2}}\left(\frac{5}{4}+\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle^{2}+\frac{2d\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|)}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\left(\varepsilon^{2}d+\varepsilon\sqrt{d}\sqrt{8CV}\right)\right)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1+2\int^{\infty}_{0}\left(2bt+a\right)e^{at+bt^{2}}e^{-ct^{2}}dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1+20\frac{d}{n}+4\sqrt{\pi}\delta e^{\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}}\right)^{1/2}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\frac{2d}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|\left|\left\langle\bar{X},\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|\right)

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\frac{2d}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle^{2}\right)\right)^{1/2}\left(\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\frac{2d}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\left\langle\hat{X},\bar{Y}\right\rangle^{2}\right)\right)^{1/2}.

We know that

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\frac{2d}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle^{2}\right)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1+2\int^{\infty}_{0}2bte^{bt^{2}}e^{-ct^{2}}dt\right)^{1/2}=\left(1+2\frac{b}{c-b}\right)^{1/2},

where

b=\frac{2d}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}},\;\;c=\frac{1}{4n}

we need that

Then

\left(\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\frac{2d}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle^{2}\right)\right)^{1/2}\leqslant\left(1+32\frac{d}{n\sigma^{2}}\right)^{1/2}.

Now observe that
\left|\left\langle\hat{X},\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{Y}\left(\left|\left\langle\hat{X},\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|>u\right)\leqslant 2e^{-\lambda u}\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\lambda\left\langle\hat{X},\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=2e^{-\lambda u+\lambda^{2}2CV}\leqslant 2e^{-u^{2}/\left(8CV\right)},\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;\lambda=\frac{u}{4CV}.

We can use our formula with

b=\frac{2d}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}},\;\;c=\frac{1}{8CV}.

and

b<c/2,\;\;n>\frac{4\sqrt{d}\sqrt{CV}}{\sigma}.

Then

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\frac{d}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\left\langle\hat{X},\bar{Y}\right\rangle^{2}\right)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(1+2\int^{\infty}_{0}2bte^{bt^{2}}e^{-ct^{2}}dt\right)^{1/2}=\left(1+2\frac{b}{c-b}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1+\frac{32dCV}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\right)^{1/2}.

It implies that

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\exp\left(\frac{2d}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|\left|\left\langle\hat{X},\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|\right)\right)^{1/2}\leqslant\left(1+32\frac{d}{\sigma^{2}n}\right)^{1/4}\left(1+\frac{32dCV}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\right)^{1/4}.

Hence

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{E}_{\bar{Y}}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,\bar{Y}\right\rangle\right|\leqslant n/2}C(X,\bar{Y})&\leqslant\left(1+20\frac{d}{n}+4\sqrt{\pi}\delta e^{\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}}\right)^{1/2}\left(1+32\frac{d}{n\sigma^{2}}\right)^{1/4}\left(1+\frac{32dCV}{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}\right)^{1/4}\\
&=1+\bar{\delta}.\end{split}

(25)

We collect conditions under which we may claim that
\bar{\delta}
is suitably small. We need that

\displaystyle\frac{n}{d}>L,\;\;\frac{n}{d}>\frac{L}{\sigma^{2}};

\displaystyle\varepsilon\leqslant\frac{\delta}{32}\sigma^{2}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)^{3/2}(CV)^{-1/2},\;\;\varepsilon\leqslant\frac{\delta^{1/2}}{4}\sigma\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)n^{-1/4},

(26)

where
L
is suitably large and
\delta
is suitably small.
Thus by (
22
)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\right|\leqslant n/2}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1+\bar{\delta}\right)\exp\left(\frac{d^{2}\varepsilon^{2}2CV}{2\sigma^{4}n^{2}}\right)W^{-1}\gamma_{d}(\frac{\sqrt{n}\sigma\varepsilon}{\sqrt{d}}K_{d})\mathbf{E}_{X}\mathbf{1}_{X\in A}\mathbf{P}_{R^{1}}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R^{1}\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1+\bar{\delta}\right)\left(1-C^{-1}\right)^{-1}\exp\left(\frac{d^{2}\varepsilon^{2}CV}{\sigma^{4}n^{2}}\right)\left[\mathbf{E}_{X}\mathbf{1}_{X\in A}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\right]^{2}.

Note that by our assumptions

\varepsilon\leqslant\frac{\delta}{32}\sigma^{2}\frac{n}{d}(CV)^{-1/2},\;\;\mbox{so}\;\;\exp\left(\frac{d^{2}\varepsilon^{2}CV}{\sigma^{4}n^{2}}\right)\leqslant\exp\left(\frac{\delta^{2}}{32}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{X,Y}\mathbf{1}_{\left|\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\right|\leqslant n/2}\mathbf{1}_{X\in A}\mathbf{1}_{Y\in A}\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)\mathbf{P}_{R}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)Y\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1+\bar{\delta}\right)\gamma_{d}(\frac{\sqrt{n}\sigma\varepsilon}{\sqrt{d}}K_{d})\mathbf{E}_{X}\mathbf{1}_{X\in A}\mathbf{P}_{R^{1}}\left(\left\lVert\left(M+R^{1}\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right)

Thus matching our bounds

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{R}\left[\mathbf{P}_{X}\left(\left\{\left\lVert\left(M+R\right)X\right\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap\left\{X\in A\right\}\right)\right]^{2}

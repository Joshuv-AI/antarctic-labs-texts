Abstract
. Let G G be a group with a metric invariant under left and right translations, and let 𝔻 r \mathbb{D}_{r} be the ball of radius r r around the identity. A ( k , r ) (k,r) -metric approximate subgroup is a symmetric subset X X of G G such that the pairwise product set X ​ X XX is covered by at most k k translates of X ​ 𝔻 r X\mathbb{D}_{r} . This notion was introduced in [ Tao08 ] and [ Tao14 ] along with the version for discrete groups (approximate subgroups). In [ Hru12 ] , it was shown for the discrete case that, at the asymptotic limit of X X finite but large, the “approximateness” (or need for more than one translate) can be attributed to a canonically associated Lie group. Here we prove an analogous result in the metric setting, under a certain finite covering assumption on X X replacing finiteness. In particular, if G G has bounded exponent, we show that any ( k , r ) (k,r) -metric approximate subgroup is close to a ( 1 , r ′ ) (1,r^{\prime}) -metric approximate subgroup for an appropriate r ′ r^{\prime} .

Introduction

Approximate subgroups were defined in
[
Tao08
]
along with a metric version,

[
Tao14
]

-roughly commensurable

k

T

T
on the right), where
T
is a subset containing the identity. A
(k,T)
-rough approximate subgroup
is a symmetric subset containing the identity which is
(k,T)
-roughly commensurable to its pairwise product set. We simply say that two set are
k
-commensurable if they are
(k,\boldsymbol{1})
-roughly commensurable and, similarly, we say that a set is a
k
-approximate subgroup if it is a
(k,\boldsymbol{1})
-rough approximate subgroup, where
\boldsymbol{1}\coloneqq\{1\}
. As long as there is no confusion, to simplify the notation, we may omit the parameters
k
or

Finite approximate subgroups arose in analysis (see
[
Fre73
]
), and have had deep significance in group theory and additive combinatorics. See especially the paper
[
BGT12
]
that classifies finite approximate subgroups, and the many papers that cite it. There are also connections to basic model theoretic concepts such as connected components and strong types, as discussed, for example, in
[
HKP22
]
. The basic fact is that, in the presence of an invariant measure, approximate subgroups are governed by canonically associated Lie groups
[
Hru12
]
; it is this that we aim to generalise to the metric case.

By a metric group we mean a group together with a metric invariant under left translations. We will write
\mathbb{D}_{r}(1)
for the closed ball of radius
r
at the identity and, similarly,
\mathbb{D}_{r}(X)\coloneqq X\mathbb{D}_{r}(1)
for the (closed)

-thickening of

. In this case, we say that two sets are
(k,r)
-commensurable
if they are
(k,\mathbb{D}_{r}(1))
-roughly commensurable and that a set is a
(k,r)
-metric approximate subgroup
if it is a
(k,\mathbb{D}_{r}(1))
-rough approximate subgroup.

The notion of ultraproduct appropriate to metric groups is similar to their use in continuous logic: from an ordinary ultraproduct of metric groups, we retain only the elements at bounded distance from the identity, and then quotient out by the infinitesimals. This involves what is known as a

in model theory. A systematic theory of piecewise hyperdefinable sets was developed in
[
RF23
]
, following an ad hoc treatment in an early version of this note. Here, we briefly recall in the third section the main results from
[

]
that we will need.

By comparison to the vast field of work on approximate subgroups, there has been little work on the metric case that we are aware of; but two very striking results have appeared which we now describe:

In the abstract setting, there is an extremely interesting foundational work by Gowers and Long
[
GL20
]
regarding approximate associativity in partial Latin squares, that has metric subgroups in its conclusion. It would be very interesting to try to connect a result like ours with their work, to see under what conditions

There has also been a vast field of work on finite approximate subgroups of linear groups, due to Erdös-Szemerédi, Bourgain-Katz-Tao, Helfgott, Pyber-Szabó, Breuillard-Green-Tao, and others. In this line, a strong metric version was proved by de Saxcé

is the one given by making definable each

For example, a

on

is a finitely-additive probability measure defined on the whole boolean algebra of definable subsets of

Fortunately, every finitely-additive probability measure on a subalgebra can be extended to a finitely-additive probability measure on the whole algebra. In that case, for a given Keisler measure

is a Keisler measure on

extending
\nu_{n}
. Then,

is a definable Keisler measure on the expansion

extending the original Keisler measure

— of course, properties such as invariance under a group action need not be preserved without special attention.

Let

4.
Metric Lie Model Theorem

In this section we prove our main theorem.

Fix

and

Remark 19
.

Let

of

Lemma 27

\mathrm{(2)}&U_{n+1}^{2}\subseteq U_{n}\mathrm{\ for\ each\ }n\in\mathbb{N}.\\
\mathrm{(3)}&x^{-1}U_{n+1}x\subseteq U_{n}\mathrm{\ for\ each\ }x\in U_{0}\mathrm{\ and\ }n\in\mathbb{N}.\\
\mathrm{(4)}&[U_{n_{1}},U_{n_{2}}]\subseteq U_{n}\mathrm{\ for\ each\ }n,n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\mathrm{\ with\ }n\leq n_{1}+n_{2}.\\

\mathrm{(6)}&\{x\in U_{0}\mathrel{:}x^{17}\in U_{n}\}=U_{n+4}.\\
\mathrm{(7)}&\mathrm{If\ }x,y\in U_{0}\mathrm{\ with\ }x^{2}=y^{2},\mathrm{\ then\ }x=y.\end{array}

In our case, we pick

.

Now, as

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{(1)}&X^{2}\cdot o_{r}(1)\mathrm{\ and\ }Y_{0}\mathrm{\ are\ }c_{1}\mbox{-}\mathrm{commensurable.}\\

Take

6.
Digression on de Saxcé product theorem

In
[
RF23
]
, the close relationship between the model theoretic Generic Set Lemma
[
RF23
, Theorem 2.1]
and the additive combinatorics Machado’s Closed Approximate Subgroups Theorem
[
Mac23
, Theorem 1.4]
was already discussed; both are special cases of a more general topological result. Here, we take the opportunity to explain the further relation of these two results with de Saxcé’s Product Theorem
[
dS15
, Theorem 1]
, which also has some underlying similarities with the main results of this paper. First of all, we note the following remarkable consequences of Machado’s Closed Approximate Subgroup Theorem by using Poguntke’s Theorem
[
Pog94
, Theorem 3.3]
. Recall that a
semi-simple Lie group
is a connected Lie group that has no non-trivial connected solvable normal subgroups. A
simple Lie group
it is a non-abelian connected Lie group that has no non-trivial connected normal subgroups.

Lemma 29
.

Let
G
be a semi-simple Lie group and
X
a closed approximate subgroup with empty interior:

(1)

Suppose
X
is compact. Then,
X
is contained in finitely many translates of a proper connected closed subgroup
H\lneq G
.

(2)

Suppose
X
is connected. Then,
X
is contained in a proper connected closed subgroup
H\lneq G
.

(3)

Suppose
G
is a simple Lie group. Then,
X
is discrete or contained in a proper closed subgroup
H\lneq G
.

Proof.

By Machado’s Closed Approximate Subgroups Theorem
[
Mac23
, Theorem 1.4]
, there is a subgroup
L\leq G
containing
X
that admits a Lie group structure such that
X
has non-empty interior, the map
\iota\colon L\to G
is a
1
-to-
1
continuous group homomorphism and
X
has the same subspace topology induced by
L
and
G
. By
[
Pog94
, Theorem 3.3]
and
[
Lee03
, Proposition 6.5]
, as
G
is semi-simple,
\iota_{\mid L^{\circ}}
has dense image if and only if it is actually an isomorphism. Since
X\cap L^{\circ}
has empty interior in
G
but not in
L
, we conclude that
H\coloneqq\overline{\iota(L^{\circ})}
is a proper connected closed subgroup of
G
.

(1)

If
X
is compact, finitely many translates of
L^{\circ}
cover it, so finitely many translates of
H
cover
X
.

(2)

If
X
is connected,
X\subseteq L^{\circ}
, so
X\subseteq H
.

(3)

Finally, suppose
G
is simple and
X
is not contained in a proper closed subgroup of
G
. Thus, the group generated by
X
is dense in
G
, so
\iota
has dense image. Since
L^{\circ}
is normal in
L
, so is
H
in
\iota(L)
. As
\iota(L)
is dense and
H
is closed, we conclude that
H
is a normal subgroup of
G
. Since
G
is simple, we conclude that
H
is trivial, so
L^{\circ}
is trivial, concluding that
L
is discrete. Hence,
X
is discrete.∎

As a consequence, by an easy ultraproduct argument, we get the following variations of de Saxcé’s Product Theorem
[
dS15
, Theorem 1]
. Let us restate first the original result by de Saxcé.

Fact

(de Saxcé’s Product Theorem)
.

Let
G
be a simple Lie group and take some left invariant metric. There is a neighbourhood
U
of the identity such that, for any
0<\sigma<\dim(G)
, there are
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\sigma)>0
and
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\sigma)
with the following property: if
X\subseteq U
and
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
satisfy

(1)

\mathrm{N}^{\mathrm{cov}}_{\delta}(X)\leq\delta^{-\sigma-\varepsilon}
,

(2)

\mathrm{N}^{\mathrm{cov}}_{\rho}(X)\geq\delta^{\varepsilon}\rho^{-\sigma}
for any
\rho\geq\delta
and

(3)

\mathrm{N}^{\mathrm{cov}}_{\delta}(X^{3})\leq\delta^{-\varepsilon}\mathrm{N}^{\mathrm{cov}}_{\delta}(X)
,

then there is a closed connected subgroup
H\lneq G
with
X\subseteq\mathbb{D}_{\delta^{\varepsilon}}(H)
.

The third hypothesis in
[
dS15
, Theorem 1]
is very close to assuming that
X
is a metric approximate subgroup, while the other two hypotheses are morally saying that the dimension of
X
looks like
0<\sigma<d
. Our variations are based on these analogies:

Proposition 30

(A Product Theorem for Simple Lie Groups)
.

Let
G
be a simple Lie group and
U
a compact neighbourhood of the identity. Take some left invariant metric. Let
0<\sigma_{0}<\sigma_{1}<\dim(G)
and
c_{0},c_{1},k,s\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for any
(k,2^{-m})
-metric approximate subgroup
X\subseteq U
satisfying
2^{i\sigma_{0}-c_{0}}\leq\mathrm{N}_{2^{-i}}(X)\leq 2^{i\sigma_{1}+c_{1}}
for each
i\leq m
, there is a closed subgroup
H\lneq G
with
X
covered by
\mathbb{D}_{2^{-s}}(H)
.

Proof.

Aiming a contradiction, suppose otherwise. Then, we have a counterexample
X_{m}
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Take an ultraproduct in the sense of (unbounded) continuous logic, i.e. take an ultraproduct, take the subgroup generated by
U
and quotient by the infinitesimals. By compactness of
U
, we end then with a closed subset
X\subseteq U
of
G
. By Łoś’s Theorem,
X
is a
k
-approximate subgroup and satisfies
2^{i\sigma_{0}-c_{0}}\leq\mathrm{N}_{2^{-i}}(X)\leq 2^{i\sigma_{1}+c_{1}}
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,
0<\sigma_{0}\leq\dim(X)\leq\sigma_{1}<d
, where
\dim
denotes the (large) inductive dimension, by
[
HW15
, Theorem VII 2]
and
[
Fal90
, Eq.3.17 p.46]
. Since the inductive dimension of discrete spaces is
0
,
X
is not discrete. Also, by
[
HW15
, Corollary 1 of Theorem IV 3]
,
X
has empty interior in
G
. By
29
, as
G
is simple,
X
is contained in a proper closed subgroup
H\lneq G
. Thus, by Łoś’s Theorem, there is some
m
such that
X_{m}\subseteq\mathbb{D}_{2^{-s}}(H)
, getting a contradiction with our initial assumption.
∎

Proposition 31

(A Product Theorem for Semisimple Lie Groups)
.

Let
G
be a semi-simple Lie group and
U
a compact neighbourhood of the identity. Take some left invariant metric. Let
\sigma<\dim(G)
,
C,k,s\in\mathbb{N}
and

. Then, there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for any
(k,2^{-m})
-metric approximate subgroup
X\subseteq U
satisfying
\mathrm{N}_{2^{-i}}(X)\leq C\cdot 2^{i\sigma}
for each
i\leq m
, there is
c\leq m
and a connected closed subgroup
H\lneq G
with
X
covered by
c
many translates of
\mathbb{D}_{2^{-s(c)}}(H)

Proof.

Aiming a contradiction, suppose otherwise. Then, take an ultraproduct in the sense of (unbounded) continuous logic of a sequence of counterexamples. By compactness of
U
, we end then with a closed subset
X\subseteq U
of
G
. By Łoś’s Theorem,
X
is a
k
-approximate subgroup and satisfies
\mathrm{N}_{2^{-i}}(X)\leq C\cdot 2^{i\sigma}
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,
\dim(X)\leq\sigma<d
, where
\dim
denotes the (large) inductive dimension, by
[
HW15
, Theorem VII 2]
and
[
Fal90
, Eq.3.17 p.46]
. In particular,
X
has empty interior in
G
by
[
HW15
, Corollary 1 of Theorem IV 3]

29
, as
G

X
is contained in finitely many translates of a proper connected closed subgroup

. Thus, by Łoś’s Theorem, there is some
m>c
such that
X_{m}
is covered by
c
many translates of
\mathbb{D}_{2^{-s(c)}}(H)
, getting a contradiction with our initial assumption.
∎

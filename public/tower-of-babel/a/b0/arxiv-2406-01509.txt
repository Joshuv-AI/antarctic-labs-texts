Spectral characterization of the constant sign derivatives of Green’s function related to two point boundary value conditions
Alberto Cabada, Lucía López-Somoza and Mouhcine Yousfi

Abstract
In this paper we will study the set of parameters in which certain partial derivatives of the Green’s function, related to a n n -order linear operator T n ​ [ M ] T_{n}[M] , depending on a real parameter M M , coupled to different two-point boundary conditions, are of constant sign. We will do it without using the explicit expression of the Green’s function. The constant sign interval will be characterized by the first eigenvalue related to suitable boundary conditions of the studied operator. As a consequence of the main result, we will be able to give sufficient conditions to ensure that the derivatives of Green’s function cannot be nonpositive (nonnegative). These characterizations and the obtained results can be used to deduce the existence of solutions of nonlinear problems under additional conditions on the nonlinear part. To illustrate the obtained results, some examples are given.

Introduction

Many papers in the literature have studied the qualitative properties of solutions of differential equations coupled with different boundary conditions. Several techniques have been used to ensure the existence, non-existence and multiplicity of solutions. Among them, stands out the method of lower and upper solutions, coupled with monotone iterative techniques which, as it is very well-known, are equivalent to the constant sign of the Green’s function of the corresponding linear problem. Moreover the method of constructing suitable cones in Banach spaces where to apply the well-known Krasnosel’skiĭ’s fixed-point theorem or the classical index theory, see for instance
[
16
,
24
,
23
,
29
]
, is a powerful tool to deduce the existence of positive solutions of the nonlinear problem. Such cones are constructed using constant sign properties of the Green’s function and some of its derivatives. So, as much information we have about such constant signs, we may construct smaller cones, that allow us to ensure, both the positiveness of the solutions we are looking for, and some of its derivatives. Thus, for instance, we may prove the existence of positive and convex solutions if both the Green’s function and its second partial derivative with respect to
t
are nonnegative on their square of definition.

The study of the sign of the Green’s function has been made for many authors on the literature. Usually some sufficient conditions are obtained to deduce the constant sign of such function and some of its partial derivatives with respect to the first variable. Different kind of two-point boundary conditions have been considered and, usually, the differential linear operator is assumed to be disconjugate or disfocal on a suitable interval. We refer to the reader the classical works
[

[

Step 1.

Study of the function
v_{s}^{q}[M]
at
s=a

Let us consider the function

w_{M,q}(t)=\dfrac{\partial^{\eta}}{\partial s^{\eta}}\Big(\dfrac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)\Big)_{\mid s=a},

where
\eta
has been defined in (

).

If
\eta>0
, since
\{0,\dots,\eta-1\}\subset\{\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n-k}\}
using the equality (

) we obtain that

g_{M}^{1}(t,a)=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}g_{M}^{1}(t,a)=\cdots=\dfrac{\partial^{\eta-1}}{\partial s^{\eta-1}}g_{M}^{1}(t,a)=0\,,\;\;\text{for all}\;t\in(a,b].

Therefore, for all
t\in(a,b]
and
q\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
we infer that

\dfrac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}g_{M}^{1}(t,a)=\dfrac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}\Big(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)\Big)_{\mid s=a}=\cdots=\dfrac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}\Big(\dfrac{\partial^{\eta-1}}{\partial s^{\eta-1}}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)\Big)_{\mid s=a}=0\,,

equivalently,

\dfrac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}g_{M}^{1}(t,a)=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\Big(\dfrac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)\Big)_{\mid s=a}=\cdots=\dfrac{\partial^{\eta-1}}{\partial s^{\eta-1}}\Big(\dfrac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)\Big)_{\mid s=a}=0\,.

Note that it is necessary that
w_{M,q}>0
to guarantee the strongly positive sign of the function
v_{s}^{q}[M]
. Indeed, if there exists
t^{*}\in[a,b]
, such that
w_{M,q}(t^{*})<0
, then there exists
\rho(t^{*})>a
such that
v_{s}^{q}[M](t^{*})<0
for all
s\in(a,\rho(t^{*}))
, which contradicts the strongly positive sign.

Since the function
v_{s}^{q}[M]
is a solution of
T_{n}[M]\,v_{s}^{q}[M](t)=0
for
t\neq s
, we have that

\dfrac{\partial^{\eta}}{\partial s^{\eta}}\left(T_{n}[M]\,v_{s}^{q}[M](t)\right)_{\mid s=a}=T_{n}[M]\,w_{M,q}(t)=0\,,\quad t\in(a,b]\,,

that is, the function
w_{M,q}
solves the equation
T_{n}[M]\,u(t)=0
,
t\in(a,b]
.

It remains to obtain the boundary conditions satisfied by the function
w_{M,q}
.

Using the matrix of the Green’s function given in (
2.4
), the equality (
2.6
), the expression of
g_{n-j}
,
j=1,\dots,n-1
given in (
2.8
) and the following identity

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(\dfrac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}\,G_{M}(t,s)\Big)=A\,\dfrac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}G_{M}(t,s),\quad\text{for all }t\in I\backslash\left\{s\right\},\,q\in\{1,\dots,n-1\},

it follows that

\dfrac{\partial^{\mu_{1}^{q}+q}}{\partial t^{\mu_{1}^{q}+q}}g_{M}^{2}(t,s)_{\mid t=a}=0,\,-\dfrac{\partial^{\mu_{1}^{q}+q+1}}{\partial t^{\mu_{1}^{q}+q}\partial s}g_{M}^{2}(t,s)_{\mid t=a}=0,\,\dots,\,(-1)^{\eta}\dfrac{\partial^{\mu_{1}^{q}+q+\eta}}{\partial t^{\mu_{1}^{q}+q}\partial s^{\eta}}g_{M}^{2}(t,s)_{\mid t=a}=0\,.

Since
(\eta+\mu_{1}^{q}+q)\mod{n}<n-1
, then we are not in an element of the diagonal of
\dfrac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}G_{M}(t,s)
, and taking in account that the above equalities are satisfied for
s=a
, by continuity we deduce that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{r}\dfrac{\partial^{\mu_{1}^{q}+q}}{\partial t^{\mu_{1}^{q}+q}}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)_{\mid(t,s)=(a,a)}=0\,,\\
\\
-\dfrac{\partial^{\mu_{1}^{q}+q+1}}{\partial t^{\mu_{1}^{q}+q}\partial s}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)_{\mid(t,s)=(a,a)}=0\,,\\
\\
\vdots\\
\\
(-1)^{\eta}\dfrac{\partial^{\mu_{1}^{q}+q+\eta}}{\partial t^{\mu_{1}^{q}+q}\partial s^{\eta}}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)_{\mid(t,s)=(a,a)}=0\,.\end{array}\right.

Therefore,

w_{M,q}^{(\mu_{1}^{q})}(a)=\dfrac{\partial^{\mu_{1}^{q}+q+\eta}}{\partial t^{\mu_{1}^{q}+q}\partial s^{\eta}}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)_{\mid(t,s)=(a,a)}=0\,.

Making a similar reasoning we deduce that

w_{M,q}^{(\mu_{2}^{q})}(a)=\cdots=w_{M,q}^{(\mu_{z-1}^{q})}(a)=w_{M,q}^{(\mu_{z+1}^{q})}(a)=\cdots=w_{M,q}^{(\mu_{k}^{q})}(a)=0\,.

For
\mu_{z}^{q}
, we have the following equalities

\dfrac{\partial^{\mu_{z}^{q}+q}}{\partial t^{\mu_{z}^{q}+q}}g_{M}^{2}(t,s)_{\mid t=a}=0,\,-\dfrac{\partial^{\mu_{z}^{q}+q+1}}{\partial t^{\mu_{z}^{q}+q}\partial s}g_{M}^{2}(t,s)_{\mid t=a}=0,\,\dots,\,(-1)^{\eta}\dfrac{\partial^{\mu_{z}^{q}+q+\eta}}{\partial t^{\mu_{z}^{q}+q}\partial s^{\eta}}g_{M}^{2}(t,s)_{\mid t=a}=0\,.

By assumption, we have that
\eta+\mu_{z}^{q}+q=n-1
, so we are in an element of the diagonal of
\dfrac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}G_{M}(t,s)
, and taking into account again that the previous equalities are satisfied for
s=a
and that
\dfrac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}G_{M}(t,s)
has a jump equal to

on the diagonal we infer that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{r}\dfrac{\partial^{\mu_{z}^{q}+q}}{\partial t^{\mu_{z}^{q}+q}}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)_{\mid(t,s)=(a,a)}=0\,,\\
\\
-\dfrac{\partial^{\mu_{z}^{q}+q+1}}{\partial t^{\mu_{z}^{q}+q}\partial s}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)_{\mid(t,s)=(a,a)}=0\,,\\
\\
\vdots\\
\\
(-1)^{\eta}\dfrac{\partial^{\mu_{z}^{q}+q+\eta}}{\partial t^{\mu_{z}^{q}+q}\partial s^{\eta}}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)_{\mid(t,s)=(a,a)}=1\,.\end{array}\right.

Therefore,

w_{M,q}^{(\mu_{z}^{q})}(a)=\dfrac{\partial^{\mu_{z}^{q}+q+\eta}}{\partial t^{\mu_{z}^{q}+q}\partial s^{\eta}}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)_{\mid(t,s)=(a,a)}=(-1)^{\eta}\,.

\dfrac{\partial^{\rho_{1}^{q}+q}}{\partial t^{\rho_{1}^{q}+q}}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)_{\mid t=b}=0,\,-\dfrac{\partial^{\rho_{1}^{q}+q+1}}{\partial t^{\rho_{1}^{q}+q}\partial s}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)_{\mid t=b}=0,\,\dots,\,(-1)^{\eta}\dfrac{\partial^{\rho_{1}^{q}+q+\eta}}{\partial t^{\rho_{1}^{q}+q}\partial s^{\eta}}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)_{\mid t=b}=0\,.

The above equalities are satisfied at

Analogously we obtain that

w_{M,q}^{(\rho_{2}^{q})}(b)=\dots=w_{M,q}^{(\rho_{n-k}^{q})}(b)=0\,.

If
\eta=0

Now, let us see that

cannot be positive for

is positive. Then, by monotony of

.

\widehat{y}_{M,q}^{(\delta_{1})}(b)=\cdots=\widehat{y}_{M,q}^{(\delta_{z-1})}(b)=\widehat{y}_{M,q}^{(\delta_{z+1})}(b)=\cdots=\widehat{y}_{M,q}^{(\delta_{k})}(b)=&\,0\,,\\
y_{M,q}^{(\delta_{z})}(b)=&\,(-1)^{n-\beta^{q}-q+1}\,.\end{split}

(3.19)

Analogously, if
\beta^{q}=0
, then
\widehat{y}_{M,q}(s)=\frac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}g_{M}^{2}(b,s)
satisfies the conditions (
3.19
) by an argument similar to the case
\beta^{q}>0
.

Thus, we deduce that
\widehat{y}_{M,q}>0
on
(a,b)
if
\beta^{q}
is even and
\widehat{y}_{M,q}<0
on
(a,b)
if
\beta^{q}
is odd for all
M\in[0,(\lambda_{2}^{*})^{q}]
, where
(\lambda_{2}^{*})^{q}>0
is the least positive eigenvalue of
\widehat{T}_{n}[0]\equiv T_{n}[0]
in
X_{2}^{*}
defined in (
3.15
).

Again, from Lemma
3.9
we have that
(\lambda_{2}^{*})^{q}=\lambda_{2}^{q}
. Therefore, we conclude from this step that, if
M\in[0,\lambda_{2}^{q}]
, then

\forall s\in(a,b)\,,\quad\exists\rho^{q}(s)>0\ \mid\ v_{s}^{q}[M](t)>0\ \forall t\in(b-\rho^{q}(t),b)\,.

Step 5.

Study of the function
v_{s}^{q}[M]
on
(a,b)\times(a,b)
.

In this step, we verify that the function
v_{s}^{q}[M](t)>0
for all
(t,s)\in I\times I
if
M
belongs to the given intervals. Let us denote
u_{M}^{s}(t)=g_{M}(t,s)
, for all
s\in(a,b)
.

The function
v_{s}^{q}[M](t)
is in
C^{n-2-q}(I)
and it satisfies the boundary conditions (
2.18
)-(
2.19
).

Using the definition of
v_{s}^{q}[M]
, the following equality holds for all
s\in(a,b)
:

T_{n-q}[0]\,v_{s}^{q}[M](t)=-M\,u_{M}^{s}(t)\,,\quad\forall\,t\neq s\,,\ t\in I\,.

(3.20)

Let’s see that for the values of the parameter
M
for which
v_{s}^{q}[M](t)
has constant sign in
I
, it cannot have a double zero in
(a,b)
, and this implies that the change of the sign must be at
t=a
or
t=b
, and the result is proven.

We do the proof for
n-k
even (the case
n-k
odd is similarly proved). In this case, we study the behavior of
v_{s}^{q}[M](t)
for
M>0
and
u_{M}^{s}\geq 0
. From (
3.20
), we have that

\dfrac{d^{n-q}}{dt^{n-q}}\,v_{s}^{q}[M](t)=\dfrac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}\,g_{M}(t,s)\leq 0.

Therefore, using the condition
(T_{d})
, since
v_{1}=\dots=v_{n}=1
, we have that

\dfrac{d^{n-q-1}}{dt^{n-q-1}}\,v_{s}^{q}[M](t)=\dfrac{\partial^{n-1}}{\partial t^{n-1}}\,g_{M}(t,s)

is a decreasing function, with two continuous components and a jump
+1
at
t=s
. Then, it has at most two zeros on
I
(see Figure
1
).

Figure 1:

\dfrac{d^{n-q-1}}{dt^{n-q-1}}\,v_{s}^{q}[M](t)
, maximal oscillation with
t\in I=[0,1].

Now, we have that

\dfrac{d^{n-q-2}}{dt^{n-q-2}}\,v_{s}^{q}[M](t)=\dfrac{\partial^{n-2}}{\partial t^{n-2}}\,g_{M}(t,s)

is a continuous function with at most four zeros on
I
(See Figure
2
).

Figure 2:

\dfrac{d^{n-q-1}}{dt^{n-q-1}}\,v_{s}^{q}[M](t)
, maximal oscillation with
t\in I=[0,1].

By recurrence, we have that

\dfrac{d^{n-q-l}}{dt^{n-q-l}}\,v_{s}^{q}[M](t)=\dfrac{\partial^{n-l}}{\partial t^{n-l}}\,g_{M}(t,s)

has at most
l+2
zeros on
I
. In particular,
v_{s}^{q}[M]
has at most
n-q+2
zeros on
I
. Moreover, since

\displaystyle(v_{s}^{q})^{(\sigma_{1}^{q})}[M](a)=

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle=(v_{s}^{q})^{(\sigma_{c_{q}}^{q})}[M](a)=0\,,

\displaystyle(v_{s}^{q})^{(\varepsilon_{1}^{q})}[M](b)=

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle=(v_{s}^{q})^{(\varepsilon_{d_{q}}^{q})}[M](b)\hskip 1.13791pt=0\,,

v_{s}^{q}[M]
has
c_{q}+d_{q}=n-q
zeros on the boundary. Thus,
v_{s}^{q}[M]
has at most two zeros on
(a,b)
.

Now, making an argument similar to the one made in
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
, we deduce that the maximal oscillation (that is, the maximal number of zeros) is possible if and only if

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(v_{s}^{q})^{(\alpha^{q})}[M](a)\leq 0\,,&\text{if $n-q-c_{q}$ is even,}\\
&\\
(v_{s}^{q})^{(\alpha^{q})}[M](a)\geq 0\,,&\text{if $n-q-c_{q}$ is odd.}\end{array}\right.

(3.21)

Since we are in the case
n-q-c_{q}
even, the maximal oscillation is only possible if
(v_{s}^{q})^{(\alpha^{q})}[M](a)\leq 0
. However, by definition,
(v_{s}^{q})^{(\alpha^{q})}[M](a)\neq 0
and, since
v_{s}^{q}[M]
is nonnegative and
\alpha^{q}
denotes the order of the smallest derivative at
t=a
which is different from zero, necessarily
(v_{s}^{q})^{(\alpha^{q})}[M](a)>0
. Therefore the maximal oscillation can not happen (that is,
v_{s}^{q}[M]
cannot have two zero on
(a,b)
). Thus,
v_{s}^{q}[M]
has at most one simple zero on
(a,b)
, which is not possible due to the constant sign of
v_{s}^{q}[M]
, and we conclude that
v_{s}^{q}[M]
does not have any zero on
(a,b)
.

From this step we deduce that if
M>0
and
u_{M}^{s}\geq 0
, then
v_{s}^{q}[M]>0
on
(a,b)
.

In conclusion, from the previous steps the result is proved.
∎

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
q\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
. Suppose that
{\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k}\}}-{\{\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n-k}\}}
satisfies condition
(N_{a})
,
c_{q}+d_{q}=n-q
,
c_{q}\geq 1
and
d_{q}\geq 1
. If either
\sigma_{k}=q+c_{q}-1
or
\varepsilon_{n-k}=q+d_{q}-1

•

If
n-q-c_{q}
is even, then there is not any
M\in\mathbb{R}
such that
v_{s}^{q}[M]
is strongly negative on
I\times I
.

•

If
n-q-c_{q}
is odd, then there is not any
M\in\mathbb{R}
such that
v_{s}^{q}[M]
is strongly positive on
I\times I
.

Proof.

If
\sigma_{k}=q+c_{q}-1
, then
\eta=n-1-\sigma_{k}=n-q-c_{q}
.

We consider

w_{M,q}(t)=\dfrac{\partial^{\eta}}{\partial s^{\eta}}\Big(\dfrac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}g_{M}^{1}(t,s)\Big)_{\mid s=a},

defined in Step 1 of the proof of Theorem
3.11
.

We know that this function satisfies the following boundary conditions:

\begin{split}w_{M,q}^{(\mu_{1}^{q})}(a)=\cdots=w_{M,q}^{(\mu_{z-1}^{q})}(a)=w_{M,q}^{(\mu_{z+1}^{q})}(a)=\cdots=w_{M,q}^{(\mu_{k}^{q})}(a)=&\,0\,,\\

w_{M,q}^{(\mu_{z}^{q})}(a)=&\,(-1)^{\eta}=(-1)^{n-q-c_{q}}\,.\end{split}

Hence, if
n-q-c_{q}
is even, then there exists
\rho>0
, such that
w_{M,q}(t)>0
for all

. So,
v_{s}^{q}[M]
cannot be negative for any real
M
.

Now, if
n-q-c_{q}
is odd, then there exists
\rho>0
, such that
w_{M,q}(t)<0
for all
t\in(a,a+\rho)
. Thus,
v_{s}^{q}[M]
cannot be positive for any
M\in\mathbb{R}
.

Analogously, if
\varepsilon_{n-k}=q+d_{q}-1
, then
\gamma=n-\varepsilon_{n-k}-1=n-q-d_{q}=c_{q}
. In this case, we consider the function

y_{M,q}(t)=\dfrac{\partial^{\gamma}}{\partial s^{\gamma}}\Big(\dfrac{\partial^{q}}{\partial t^{q}}g_{M}^{2}(t,s)\Big)_{\mid s=b}\,,

defined in Step 2 of the proof of Theorem
3.11
.

We know that for all
M\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
y_{M,q}
satisfies the following boundary conditions:

\begin{split}y_{M,q}^{(\mu_{1}^{q})}(a)=\cdots=y_{M,q}^{(\mu_{k}^{q})}(a)=&\,0\,,\\
y_{M,q}^{(\rho_{1}^{q})}(b)=\cdots=y_{M,q}^{(\rho_{h-1}^{q})}(b)=y_{M,q}^{(\rho_{h+1}^{q})}(b)=\cdots=y_{M,q}^{(\rho_{n-k}^{q})}(b)=&\,0\,,\\

Hence, if

and

are even, then there exists
\rho>0
, such that
y_{M,q}(t)>0

cannot be negative for any real

.

Moreover, if
n-q-c_{q}
is even and
c_{q}

\rho>0
, such that
y_{M,q}(t)<0
for all
t\in(b-\rho,b)
. So,
v_{s}^{q}[M]
cannot be positive for any real
M
.

Now, if
n-q-c_{q}
is odd and
c_{q}

\rho>0
, such that

Now, we give a necessary condition of the nonpositive (nonnegative) sign of

has a nonpositive (nonnegative) sign whose infimum or supremum is given by the first eigenvalue

of

in

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
q\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
. Suppose that
{\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k}\}}-{\{\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n-k}\}}
satisfies condition
(N_{a})
,

,

and

•

Let
n-k
be even:

–

If

is even and

is nonpositive on

).

·

\lambda_{5}^{q}<0

•

Let

*

\lambda_{1}>0

in

Definition 4.5
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
\Omega\subset X
open and
T:\Omega\rightarrow X

T
is compactly fixed if the set of fixed points of
T

We will denote by

the set of fixed points of

Next lemma compiles some classical results regarding the fixed point index formulated in
[
21

in a more general framework.

In particular, given
X
a Banach space,
K\subset X
a cone and
\Omega\subset K
an arbitrary open subset,
\partial\,\Omega
will denote the boundary of
\Omega
in the relative topology in
K
, induced by the topology of
X
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
K\subset X
a cone and
\Omega\subset K
an arbitrary open subset with
0\in\Omega
. Assume that
T\colon\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow K
is a compact and compactly fixed operator such that
x\neq Tx
for all
x\in\partial\,\Omega
.

Then the fixed point index
i_{K}(T,\Omega)
has the following properties:

1.

If
x\neq\mu\,Tx
for all
x\in\partial\,\Omega
and for every
\mu\leq 1
, then
i_{K}(T,\Omega)=1.

2.

If
\Omega
is bounded and there exists
e\in K\setminus\{0\}
such that
x\neq Tx+\lambda\,e
for all
x\in\partial\,\Omega
and all
\lambda>0
, then
i_{K}(T,\Omega)=0
.

3.

If
i_{K}(T,\Omega)\neq 0
, then
T
has a fixed point in
\Omega
.

4.

If
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are two open and disjoint sets such that
\operatorname{Fix}(T)\subset\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}\subset\Omega
, then

i_{K}(T,\Omega)=i_{K}(T,\Omega_{1})+i_{K}(T,\Omega_{2}).

Remark 4.7
.

Note that, in Item
2
in previous lemma, it is required that
\Omega
is bounded. However, the other assertions hold for an arbitrary open set, which might be unbounded.

Using Items

and
2
in Lemma

, it is possible to deduce the following corollary. The proof would be analogous to that of
[

, Theorem 2.3.3]

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
K\subset X
a cone and
\Omega\subset K
an open set such that
0\in\Omega
. Assume that
T\colon\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow K
is a compact and compactly fixed operator such that

for all
x\in\partial\,\Omega
. Then

1.

If
T\,x\not\succeq x
for all
x\in\partial\,\Omega
then
i_{K}(T,\Omega)=1
.

2.

If
\Omega
is bounded and, moreover,
T\,x\not\preceq x
for all

i_{K}(T,\Omega)=0
.

Theorem 4.9

Suppose that the conditions

\lambda^{q_{l}}\;\;\text{if}\;\;l=r,\end{aligned}\right.

the nonlinear problem (

Proof.

Consider the operator

,

We show that

is well-defined in

, that is,
\mathcal{L}_{M}(K)\subset K
.

Let

, it is immediate to verify that
\mathcal{L}_{M}\,u\geq 0
on
I
. Moreover, we have that:

(-1)^{d_{q_{i}}}\,(\mathcal{L}_{M}\,u)^{(q_{i})}(t)=\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}(-1)^{d_{q_{i}}}\,\frac{\partial^{q_{i}}}{\partial t^{q_{i}}}g_{M}(t,s)\,\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds\geq 0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{M}\,u(t):

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}g_{M}(t,s)\,\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}k_{1}(t)\,\phi(s)\,\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{1}(t)}{k_{2}}\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}k_{2}\,\phi(s)\,\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k_{1}(t)}{k_{2}}\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\left\{\max_{t\in I}g_{M}(t,s)\right\}\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k_{1}(t)}{k_{2}}\max_{t\in I}\left\{\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}g_{M}(t,s)\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds\right\}=\frac{k_{1}(t)}{k_{2}}\,\|\mathcal{L}_{M}\,u\|_{\infty},

and

\displaystyle(-1)^{d_{q_{i}}}\,(\mathcal{L}_{M}\,u)^{(q_{i})}(t)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}(-1)^{d_{q_{i}}}\,\frac{\partial^{q_{i}}}{\partial t^{q_{i}}}g_{M}(t,s)\,\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}k_{1}^{q_{i}}(t)\,\phi(s)\,\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{1}^{q_{i}}(t)}{k_{2}^{q_{i}}}\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}k_{2}^{q_{i}}\,\phi(s)\,\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k_{1}^{q_{i}}(t)}{k_{2}^{q_{i}}}\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\left\{\sup_{t\in I}\,(-1)^{d_{q_{i}}}\,\frac{\partial^{q_{i}}}{\partial t^{q_{i}}}g_{M}(t,s)\right\}\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k_{1}^{q_{i}}(t)}{k_{2}^{q_{i}}}\sup_{t\in I}\left\{\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}(-1)^{d_{q_{i}}}\,\frac{\partial^{q_{i}}}{\partial t^{q_{i}}}g_{M}(t,s)\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{1}^{q_{i}}(t)}{k_{2}^{q_{i}}}\,\|(-1)^{d_{q_{i}}}\,(\mathcal{L}_{M}\,u)^{(q_{i})}\|_{\infty}=\frac{k_{1}^{q_{i}}(t)}{k_{2}^{q_{i}}}\,\|(\mathcal{L}_{M}\,u)^{(q_{i})}\|_{\infty},

for all

and
i\in\{1,\dots,l\}
.

On the other hand, for

and

Thus,
\mathcal{L}_{M}(K)\subset K
.

By the continuity of

and using standard techniques one can prove that operator
\mathcal{L}_{M}
is compact.

Consider

. Let us choose

\epsilon_{1}>\frac{k_{2}}{m_{1}^{2}\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}\phi(s)\,ds}.

From assumption
(H_{1})
, there exists

such that when

Now, we show that
\mathcal{L}_{M}\,u\not\preceq u
, for all
u\in K\cap\partial\Omega_{1}
with

\Omega_{1}=\{u\in K:\ \|u\|<p\},

for some
p>0
. Here
\preceq
denotes the order induced by the cone
K
.

Then, we have that for
t\in I_{1}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{M}\,u(t)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}g_{M}(t,s)\,\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}k_{1}(t)\,\phi(s)\,\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}k_{1}(t)\,\phi(s)\,\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\geq m_{1}\,\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}\phi(s)\,\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\geq m_{1}\,\epsilon_{1}\,\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}\phi(s)\,\left(|u(s)|+\cdots+|u^{(n-1)}(s)|\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{m_{1}\,\epsilon_{1}}{k_{2}}\,\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}\phi(s)\,k_{1}(s)\,\|u\|_{\infty}\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{m_{1}^{2}\,\epsilon_{1}}{k_{2}}\,\|u\|_{\infty}\displaystyle\int_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}\phi(s)\,ds>\|u\|_{\infty}.

Therefore,
\mathcal{L}_{M}\,u(t)>u(t)
for all

and so it is proved that

for all

. As a consequence (see

On the other hand, the regularity of the Green’s function

By hypothesis
(H_{2})
, there exists

such that if
{\min\left\{|u(t)|,\ldots,|u^{(n-1)}(t)|\right\}\geq\tilde{M}}

Consider

and let us define the following subset of

Since the set

is unbounded in the cone

with respect to

is only defined in the case that the set of fixed points of operator

in

, that is,

Arguing as in the proof of

can be defined in this case. Indeed, since

Thus, we may assume that such set is bounded, and, from this hypothesis, is not difficult to verify that

Now, we prove that
\|\mathcal{L}_{M}\,u\|\leq\|u\|
for all

.

Consider

. Therefore,

\min\left\{\min_{t\in I}|u^{(q_{i})}(t)|:\,i\in\{0,\dots,r\}\right\}=q>\tilde{M}.

Then,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{M}\,u\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\max_{t\in I}\,\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}g_{M}(t,s)\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds\leq n\,\epsilon_{2}\,\|u\|\max_{t\in I}\,\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}g_{M}(t,s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq n\,\epsilon_{2}\,\|u\|\,N_{0}<\|u\|,

and

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{L}_{M}\,u)^{(q_{i})}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in I}\,\Big|\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial^{q_{i}}}{\partial t^{q_{i}}}g_{M}(t,s)\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds\Big|

\displaystyle\leq n\,\epsilon_{2}\,\|u\|\sup_{t\in I}\,\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}(-1)^{d_{q_{i}}}\,\frac{\partial^{q_{i}}}{\partial t^{q_{i}}}g_{M}(t,s)\,f\left(s,u(s),\dots,u^{(n-1)}(s)\right)\,ds

for all

Thus,

, for all

Therefore, we conclude that the operator

has a fixed point in
{K\cap(\bar{\Omega_{2}}\setminus\Omega_{1})}
which is a solution of problem (

).

Thus, in both situations (

) on the cone

.

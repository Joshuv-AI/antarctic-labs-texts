Proving Conjectures Acquired by Composing Multiple Biases
Jovial Cheukam-Ngouonou, Ramiz Gindullin, Nicolas Beldiceanu, Rémi Douence, Claude-Guy Quimper

Abstract
We present the proofs of the conjectures mentioned in the paper published in the proceedings of the 2024 AAAI conference [ 1 ] , and discovered by the decomposition methods presented in the same paper.

Most of the proofs go along this line.

Proof 1
(Proof of Conj. (
1
))

We associate three conditions
①
v\bmod\overline{c}=0
,
②
\underline{s}\geq(v\bmod\overline{c}=0\;?\;\overline{c}\;:\;v\bmod\overline{c})
,
③
2\cdot\underline{s}\leq\overline{c}

to Conj. (
1

. Note that the case
\mbox{①}\land\mbox{②}\land\mbox{③}
is not feasible.
We now prove each of the seven incompatible cases by contradiction.

Proof by contradiction of case
\mbox{①}\land\mbox{②}\land\neg\mbox{③}
and case
\mbox{①}\land\neg\mbox{②}\land\mbox{③}
:
For all digraphs we have
c\cdot\overline{c}\geq v
. But the negation of Conj. (
1
), namely
c<\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor
, implies that
c\cdot\overline{c}<v
, a contradiction, as the implication assumes that
x<\lfloor y/z\rfloor\Rightarrow x\cdot z<y
.

Proof by contradiction of cases
\neg\mbox{①}\land\mbox{②}\land\mbox{③}
,
\neg\mbox{①}\land\mbox{②}\land\neg\mbox{③}
, and
\neg\mbox{①}\land\neg\mbox{②}\land\mbox{③}
:
For any digraph we have that
c\cdot\overline{c}\geq v
, which implies that
c\geq\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor
.
From the negation of Conj. (
1
) we get

c<\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor+1
, that is
c\leq\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor
.
So finally we have
c=\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor
, and the corresponding maximum number of vertices is
\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor\cdot\overline{c}
,
which is strictly less than
v=\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor\cdot\overline{c}+v\bmod\overline{c}
as, from the hypothesis
\neg
①, we have
v\bmod\overline{c}>0
.

Proof by contradiction of case
\mbox{①}\land\neg\mbox{②}\land\neg\mbox{③}
:

We derive from the negation of Conj. (
1
) a lower bound and an upper bound on the number of vertices
v
and show that the lower bound exceeds the upper bound.

[Computing of a lower bound of
v
]

By simplifying the negation of the conjecture, namely
c<\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor+1
, we obtain
c\leq\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor
, which implies that
c\cdot\overline{c}\leq v
.

[Computing of an upper bound of
v
]

From
\neg
③ we have that each connected component contains exactly one scc. One of these connected components has size
\underline{s}
, while let
|c_{i}|
denote the size of each of the remaining connected components (with
i\in[1,c-1]
). Consequently the total number of vertices is
\underline{s}+\sum_{i=1}^{c-1}|c_{i}|
, which is less than or equal to
\underline{s}+(c-1)\cdot\overline{c}
.

[Deriving a contradiction]

We showed that
v\in[c\cdot\overline{c},\underline{s}+(c-1)\cdot\overline{c}]
.
Hence we should have
c\cdot\overline{c}\leq\underline{s}+(c-1)\cdot\overline{c}
.
But ① implies that
\neg
② can be simplified as
\underline{s}<\overline{c}
, which leads to the fact that the upper bound
\underline{s}+(c-1)\cdot\overline{c}
is strictly less than
c\cdot\overline{c}
, a contradiction.

Proof by contradiction of case
\neg\mbox{①}\land\neg\mbox{②}\land\neg\mbox{③}
:

For any digraph we have that
c\cdot\overline{c}\geq v
.
Moreover,
v
can be decomposed wrt
\overline{c}
in the following way

v=\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor\cdot\overline{c}+v\bmod\overline{c}
.
This leads to the following inequality

c\cdot\overline{c}\geq\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor\cdot\overline{c}+v\bmod\overline{c}
,
which can be rewritten as

c\geq\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor+(v\bmod\overline{c})/\overline{c}
.
From
\neg
①, i.e.
v\bmod\overline{c}>0
,
and therefore

c>\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor
,
i.e.
c\geq\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor+1
.
By considering the negation of Conj. (
1
)
we obtain
c=\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor+1
,
and the corresponding maximum number of vertices is

\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor\cdot\overline{c}+\underline{s}
, as
one of the connected components must have
\underline{s}
vertices since, from hypothesis
\neg
③ each connected component contains exactly one scc.
From hypothesis
\neg
②,
\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor\cdot\overline{c}+\underline{s}
is strictly less than

v=\left\lfloor v/\overline{c}\right\rfloor\cdot\overline{c}+v\bmod\overline{c}

Proof 2
(Proof of Conj. (

Proving a Conjecture on
Rooted Trees
Found Using Decomposition Methods

We give a conjecture about the
Rooted Tree
combinatorial object.
As this proof will also use the
Partition
combinatorial object, we start by introducing the object
Partition
.

Consider the
Partition
combinatorial object, where each instance is a sequence
\mathcal{S}
of integers identified by the following characteristics:

n
the sequence length,

\mathit{nval}
the number of distinct values in
\mathcal{S}
,

\underline{m}
(resp.
\overline{m}
) the number of occurrences of the least (resp. most) frequent integer in
\mathcal{S}
,

\underline{\overline{m}}
the difference
\overline{m}-\underline{m}
.

Example 1

We have the
Partition
instance

\mathcal{S}=[1,1,1,1,1,1,2,2,3,3,4]
,
where the values of characteristics are
n=11
,
\mathit{nval}=4,\underline{m}=1,\overline{m}=6,\underline{\overline{m}}=5
.

A
Rooted Tree
is an acyclic digraph where the unique vertex that has no outgoing arc is called the
root
, and where a
leaf
is a vertex with no incoming arc. An instance of a rooted tree
\mathcal{T}
is identified by the following characteristics:

n
the number of vertices of
\mathcal{T}
,

\ell
the number of leaves of
\mathcal{T}
,

\underline{d}
(resp.
\overline{d}
) the minimum (resp. maximum) number of successors of the vertices of
\mathcal{T}
.
Conj. (
5
) gives a
sharp lower and upper bound on
\ell
wrt
n
,
\underline{d}
, and
\overline{d}
.

\left\{~\begin{aligned} \underline{d}=0\Rightarrow~&\ell=1\\
\underline{d}>0\Rightarrow~&\ell\in\left[\left\lceil\frac{n\cdot\underline{d}+\overline{d}-\underline{d}-n+1}{\underline{d}}\right\rceil,\right.\left.\left\lfloor\frac{n\cdot\overline{d}+\underline{d}-\overline{d}-n+1}{\overline{d}}\right\rfloor\right]\end{aligned}\right.

(5)

Proof 5
(Proof of Conj. (
5
))

The first case is obvious as
\underline{d}=0
means that the tree has just one vertex.

To prove the second case
\underline{d}>0
, we first prove two conjectures of the
Partition
object.
We then represent a
Rooted Tree
as a
Partition
object and finally derive from that representation the Conj. (
5
).

For the
Partition
object, let
o_{i}
be the number of occurrences of the
i
-th integer in
\mathcal{S}
, and
n_{p}
the length of
\mathcal{S}
.
We have

n_{p}=\overline{m}+\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{nval}-1}o_{i}=\underline{\overline{m}}+\underline{m}+\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{nval}-1}o_{i}\geq\underline{\overline{m}}+\mathit{nval}\cdot\underline{m}
.
And as
\mathit{nval}\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain the inequality

\mathit{nval}\leq
\left\lfloor\frac{n_{p}-\underline{\overline{m}}}{\underline{m}}\right\rfloor
, the first true conjecture found in the context of the
Partition
object. We also have

n_{p}=\underline{m}+\sum_{i=2}^{\mathit{nval}}o_{i}=\overline{m}-\underline{\overline{m}}+\sum_{i=2}^{\mathit{nval}}o_{i}\leq\mathit{nval}\cdot\overline{m}-\underline{\overline{m}}
, which leads to the second true conjecture of the
Partition
object
\mathit{nval}\geq
\left\lceil(n_{p}+\underline{\overline{m}})/\overline{m}\right\rceil
.

Now we represent a
Rooted Tree
as a vector
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
of
n-1
components where
\forall j\in[1;n-1]
, the
j
-th component
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[j]
is the father of the vertex
j
when the root is the vertex
j=0
. This configuration shows that a
Rooted Tree
is a
Partition
object where
\mathit{nval}=n-\ell,n_{p}=n-1,\underline{m}=\underline{d}
and
\overline{m}=\overline{d}
. As an illustration, the following
Rooted Tree

1
0
4
2
3
5
6

is represented by the
Partition
object
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=[0,0,2,0,3,3]
as we have
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[1]=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[2]=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[4]=0,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[3]=2,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[5]=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[6]=3
.
So by applying the two bounds of
\mathit{nval}
which was proven earlier, we have

\left\lceil(n_{p}+\underline{\overline{m}})/\overline{m}\right\rceil\leq\mathit{nval}\leq\left\lfloor(n_{p}-\underline{\overline{m}})/\underline{m}\right\rfloor\Leftrightarrow

\left\lceil(n-1+(\overline{d}-\underline{d}))/\overline{d}\right\rceil\leq n-\ell\leq\left\lfloor(n-1-(\overline{d}-\underline{d}))/\underline{d}\right\rfloor\Leftrightarrow

n-\left\lfloor(n-1-(\overline{d}-\underline{d}))/\underline{d}\right\rfloor\leq\hskip 7.0pt\ell\hskip 7.0pt\leq n-\left\lceil(n-1+(\overline{d}-\underline{d}))/\overline{d}\right\rceil\Leftrightarrow

\left\lceil n-(n-1-(\overline{d}-\underline{d}))/\underline{d}\right\rceil\leq\hskip 4.0pt\ell\hskip 4.0pt\leq\left\lfloor n-(n-1+(\overline{d}-\underline{d}))/\overline{d}\right\rfloor

which leads to the Conj. (
5
).

4
Conclusion

In this report, five conjectures found by the decomposition methods introduced in
[
1
]
were proved. Namely, four conjectures on
Digraphs
and one on
Rooted Trees
. To deal with the complexity of those conjectures, various methods were used. The method used to prove conjectures on
Digraphs
is enumerating all the corresponding disjoint and simple cases of the conjectures and prove each simple case by contradiction or deduction. The conjecture on
Rooted Trees
was proved by using some properties of partitioned sets.

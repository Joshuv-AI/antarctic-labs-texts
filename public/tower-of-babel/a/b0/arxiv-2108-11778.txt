Minimal Stinespring Representations of operator valued multilinear maps
Erik Christensen

Abstract
. A completely positive linear map φ \varphi from a C*-algebra 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} into B ⁡ ( ℋ ) B({\mathscr{H}}) has a Stinespring representation as φ ⁡ ( a ) = X ∗ ​ π ​ ( a ) ​ X , \varphi(a)=X^{*}\pi(a)X, where π \pi is a *-representation of 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} on a Hilbert space 𝒦 {\mathscr{K}} and X X is a bounded operator from ℋ {\mathscr{H}} to 𝒦 . {\mathscr{K}}. Completely bounded multilinear operators on C*-algebras as well as some densely defined multilinear operators in Connes’ non commutative geometry also have Stinespring representations of the form Φ ⁡ ( a 1 , … , a k ) = X 0 ​ π 1 ​ ( a 1 ) ​ X 1 ​ … ​ π k ​ ( a k ) ​ X k \Phi(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})=X_{0}\pi_{1}(a_{1})X_{1}\dots\pi_{k}(a_{k})X_{k} such that each a i a_{i} is in a *-algebra A i A_{i} and X 0 , … ​ X k X_{0},\dots X_{k} are densely defined closed operators between the Hilbert spaces. We show that for both completely bounded maps and for the geometrical maps, a natural minimality assumption implies that two such Stinespring representations have unitarily equivalent *-representations in the decomposition.

This minimality condition is not new, it is known from the beginning of the theory of completely positive maps and it fits naturally to the setting of completely bounded maps. It reappears in

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

\displaystyle\{l_{1}{\mathscr{K}}_{1},\dots,l_{k}{\mathscr{K}}_{k},

\displaystyle X_{0}|\{l_{1}{\mathscr{K}}_{1}\},\dots l_{i}X_{i}|\{l_{(i+1)}{\mathscr{K}}_{(i+1)}\},\dots,l_{k}X_{k}\}

We start from the right and let

denote the orthogonal projection in the commutant
\pi_{k}({\mathcal{A}}_{k})^{\prime}

\displaystyle\forall(a_{1},\dots,a_{k}):\Phi(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})

\displaystyle=X_{0}\pi_{1}(a_{1})X_{1}\dots\pi_{k}(a_{k})l_{k}X_{k}

\displaystyle=\,X_{0}\pi_{1}(a_{1})X_{1}\dots X_{(k-1)}l_{k}\pi_{k}(a_{k})l_{k}X_{k}.

So if we replace

\displaystyle\Phi(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\,

\displaystyle=\,X_{0}\pi_{1}(a_{1})X_{1}\dots\pi_{(k-1)}(a_{(k-1)})l_{(k-1)}X_{(k-1)}\pi_{k}(a_{k})l_{k}X_{k}.

We can then replace

by

and

By construction it is minimal at the
(k-1)
’st variable, but we have to see that the minimality at the

\displaystyle X_{0}\pi_{1}(a_{1})X_{1}\dots\pi_{(k-1)}(a_{(k-1)})l_{(k-1)}X_{(k-1)}l_{k}\pi_{k}(a_{k})

In
[

Paulsen and Suen study Stinespring representations of a completely bounded map

of the form

such that

They show that two minimal Stinespring representations of this sort do have unitarily equivalent *-representations in their Stinespring representations.

We will extend this result to unbounded Stinespring representations of multilinear maps. The multilinear case is proved via an induction argument. The induction step and the one variable case is to a large extent proven as an application of the lemma we present below.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
A
be an associative *-algebra over
{\mathbb{C}},

{\mathscr{G}},{\mathscr{K}},{\mathscr{L}}

Hil-bert spaces,
\pi:A\to B({\mathscr{K}}),

\rho:A\to B({\mathscr{L}})
non degenerate *-represen-tations of
A
and
\sigma:=\pi\oplus\rho
the direct sum representation of
A
on
{\mathscr{K}}\oplus{\mathscr{L}}.
Let
X:{\mathscr{G}}\to{\mathscr{K}}
and

be densely defined closed operators, which both have a dense subspace
{\mathscr{E}}
of
{\mathscr{G}}
in their domain of definition. Let

be a projection in the commutant
\sigma(A)^{\prime}
such that all the vectors
\{(\pi(a)X\gamma,\rho(a)Y\gamma)\,:\,a\in A,\gamma\in{\mathscr{E}}\}
are in the space
{\mathscr{N}}:=N({\mathscr{K}}\oplus{\mathscr{L}}).
Let
K
denote the orthogonal projection from
{\mathscr{K}}\oplus{\mathscr{L}}
onto
{\mathscr{K}}.

If

(2.7)

N\wedge K=N\wedge(I-K)=(I-N)\wedge K=(I-N)\wedge(I-K)=0

then there exists a closed densely defined operator
T:{\mathscr{K}}\to{\mathscr{L}},
such that for the polar decomposition
T=W|T|:

(i)

{\mathscr{N}}=N({\mathscr{K}}\oplus{\mathscr{L}})=G(T),
the graph of
T,

(ii)

\forall\gamma\in\mathrm{dom}(T)\,\forall a\in A:\,\pi(a)\gamma\in\mathrm{dom}(T)
and
T\pi(a)\gamma=\rho(a)T\gamma,

(iii)

the isometry
W,
in the polar decomposition
T=W|T|,
maps
{\mathscr{K}}
onto
{\mathscr{H}}
such that
\forall a\in A:\,\,W\pi(a)W^{*}=\rho(a),
and the positive operator
|T|
is affiliated with the commutant
\pi(A)^{\prime},

(iv)

\forall\gamma\in{\mathscr{E}}:\,X\gamma\in\mathrm{dom}(T)\text{ and }TX\gamma=Y\gamma.

Proof.

We will work with operators on the direct sum Hilbert space
{\mathscr{K}}\oplus{\mathscr{L}}
and write them as matrices, when this notation is the easiest to use. The direct sum representation
\sigma:=\pi\oplus\rho
may then be written in the form

\sigma(a)=\begin{pmatrix}\pi(a)&0\\
0&\rho(a)\end{pmatrix}.

We will apply Halmos’ result
[
6
]
describing the relations between two closed subspaces of a Hilbert space to the 2 closed subspaces
{\mathscr{K}}
and
{\mathscr{N}}
of
{\mathscr{K}}\oplus{\mathscr{L}}.
By assumption the two subspaces are in the so-called generic position to each other and by Halmos’ result
[
6
]
there exists a closed densely defined operator
T
from
{\mathscr{K}}
to
{\mathscr{L}}
such that
{\mathscr{N}}=G(T),
the graph of
T,
and
T
is injective with dense range.
The fact that
N
is in the commutant of
\sigma(A)
means that the closed subspace
{\mathscr{N}}=G(T)
is invariant for all the operators
\sigma(a),
so

(2.8)

\forall a\in A\,\forall\xi\in\text{dom}(T):\quad\pi(a)\xi\in\text{dom}(T),\text{ and }T\pi(a)\xi=\rho(a)T\xi.

It follows that for the polar decomposition
T=W|T|,
which in the unbounded case is presented in
[
7
]
6.1.11, the positive operator
|T|
on
{\mathscr{K}}
is affiliated with the commutant
\pi(A)^{\prime}
and
W
is an isometry of
{\mathscr{K}}
onto
{\mathscr{L}}
such that

(2.9)

\forall a\in A;\quad\rho(a)=W\pi(a)W^{*}.

These consequences may be seen from the fact that the positive operator, say
N_{11},
being the upper left hand element in the matrix for the projection
N
is in
B({\mathscr{K}})
and commutes with all the operators in
\pi(A)
. It follows from
[
6
]
that
N_{11}=(I_{\mathscr{K}}+T^{*}T)^{-1},
so we get that
|T|:=(T^{*}T)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is affiliated to
\pi({\mathcal{A}})^{\prime},
and the domain of definition for
T
is left invariant by all the operators
\pi(a).
In the polar decomposition
W|T|:=T,
the isometry part
W
must map
{\mathscr{K}}
isommetrically onto
{\mathscr{L}}
since
T
is injective and the range of
T
is dense. Hence

\displaystyle\forall a\in A:\quad\rho(a)W|T|

\displaystyle=W|T|\pi(a)=W\pi(a)|T|

\displaystyle\forall a\in A:\quad W^{*}\rho(a)W

\displaystyle=\pi(a).

By assumption we know that for
a
in
A
and
\gamma
in
{\mathscr{E}}
we have
\pi(a)X\gamma
in dom
(T)
and
T\pi(a)X\gamma=Y\rho(a)\gamma,
and the statement (iv) of the lemma follows when

is unital. In the non unital case we know by Kaplansky’s density theorem and the assumption on non degenerate representations
\pi,\rho
that there exists a net of contractions
(\pi(a_{i}))_{(i\in J)}
in
\pi(A)
which converges strongly to

Since
\rho(a)=W\pi(a)W^{*}
the net

converges strongly to

and we get for any

\lim(\pi(a_{i})X\gamma,T\pi(a_{i})X\gamma)=\lim(\pi(a_{i})X\gamma,\rho(a_{i})Y\gamma)=(X\gamma,Y\gamma,)

since
T
is closed it follows that

is in dom
(T)
and

The lemma follows.
∎

3.
Equivalence of unbounded Stinespring representations

We will now work with the definitions and results of the previous section to show that two minimal unbounded Stinespring representations of a multilinear map have unitarily equivalent *-representations in their decompositions. The proof is a finite induction argument and the induction step consists to a large extent in an application of Lemma
2.6
. This general theorem may then be applied to the case of two minimal Stinespring representations of one completely bounded map. On the other hand the general unbounded case is filled with small arguments concerning domains of definition for the unbounded operators in the proof. We have chosen to present the completely bounded one variable case first, to make the proof of the general unbounded multilinear case easier to follow. It also turns out that the completely bounded one variable case has a proof which is nearly identical to the proof of the induction step in the general unbounded multilinear case, so we will save a bit in the proof of the general theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a C*-algebra,
{\mathscr{G}},{\mathscr{H}},{\mathscr{K}},{\mathscr{L}},
Hilbert spaces and
\varphi:{\mathcal{A}}\to B({\mathscr{G}},{\mathscr{H}})
a completely bounded map. Let

\{{\mathscr{K}},\pi,X_{0},X_{1}\}
and
\{{\mathscr{L}},\rho,Y_{0},Y_{1}\}

be two minimal Stinespring representations of
\varphi,
then there exists a densely defined, closed, injective operator operator
T:{\mathscr{K}}\to{\mathscr{L}}
with dense range, such that for the polar decomposition
T=W|T|

(i)

\forall\xi\in\mathrm{dom}(T)\,\forall a\in{\mathcal{A}}:\,\pi(a)\xi\in\mathrm{dom}(T)
and
T\pi(a)\xi=\rho(a)T\xi,

(ii)

W
is an isomtery of
{\mathscr{K}}
onto
{\mathscr{L}},
and
\forall a\in{\mathcal{A}}:\,W\pi(a)W^{*}=\rho(a),

(iii)

\forall\gamma\in{\mathscr{G}}:X_{1}\gamma\in\mathrm{dom}(T):TX_{1}\gamma=Y_{1}\gamma,

(iv)

\forall\eta\in{\mathscr{H}}:Y_{0}^{*}\eta\in\mathrm{dom}(T^{*})\text{ and }T^{*}Y_{0}^{*}=X^{*}_{0},

Y_{0}T=X_{0}|\mathrm{dom}(T).

Proof.

Define
{\mathscr{R}}
as the closed linear span inside
{\mathscr{K}}\oplus{\mathscr{L}}
of the vectors in the set
\{(\pi(a)X_{1}\gamma,\rho(a)Y_{1}\gamma)\,:\,a\in{\mathcal{A}},\,\gamma\in{\mathscr{G}}\},
and let
{\mathscr{N}}
denote the closed linear subspace of
{\mathscr{K}}\oplus{\mathscr{L}}
defined as an intersection of perpendicular complements

{\mathscr{N}}:=\underset{a\in{\mathcal{A}},\,\eta\in{\mathscr{H}}}{\cap}(-\pi(a)X_{0}^{*}\eta,\rho(a)Y^{*}_{0}\eta)^{\perp}.

Since we look at two Stinespring representations of
\varphi,

it turns out that
{\mathscr{R}}
is a subspace of
{\mathscr{N}},
then when we define
R
and
N
as the orthogonal projections onto respectively
{\mathscr{R}}
and
{\mathscr{N}}
we get
N\geq R.
As in the lemma above we let
\sigma
denote the representation of
{\mathcal{A}}
on
{\mathscr{K}}\oplus{\mathscr{L}}
given as
\sigma:=\pi\oplus\rho.
By construction both
{\mathscr{N}}
and
{\mathscr{R}}
are invariant under
\sigma({\mathcal{A}}),
so both
R
and
N
belong to the commutant
\sigma({\mathcal{A}})^{\prime}.
We will now apply Lemma
2.6
so we define
K,\,L
as the orthogonal projections from
{\mathscr{K}}\oplus{\mathscr{L}}
onto
{\mathscr{K}}
and
{\mathscr{L}}
respectively.

The minimality condition, span
(\{\pi(a)X_{0}^{*}\eta\,:\,a\in{\mathcal{A}},\,\eta\in{\mathscr{H}}\})
is dense in
{\mathscr{K}},
implies that
K\wedge N=0.

The minimality condition, span
(\{\rho(a)Y_{0}^{*}\eta\,:\,a\in{\mathcal{A}},\,\eta\in{\mathscr{H}}\})
is dense in
{\mathscr{L}},
implies that
(I-K)\wedge N=L\wedge N=0.

The minimality condition, span
(\{\pi(a)X_{1}\gamma\,:\,a\in{\mathcal{A}},\,\gamma\in{\mathscr{G}}\})
is dense in
{\mathscr{K}},
implies that
K\wedge(I-R)=0,
and since
I-N\leq I-R
we have
K\wedge(I-N)=0.

The minimality condition, span
(\{\rho(a)Y_{1}\gamma\,:\,a\in{\mathcal{A}},\,\gamma\in{\mathscr{G}}\})
is dense in
{\mathscr{L}},
implies that
L\wedge(I-R)=0,
and since
I-N\leq I-R
we have
(I-K)\wedge(I-N)=L\wedge(I-N)=0.

Then
N
and
K
are in the generic position,described in the proof of Lemma
2.6
, so that lemma implies that there exists a densely defined closed operator
T:{\mathscr{K}}\to{\mathscr{L}}
such that the conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) of this theorem are fulfilled.

With respect to the statement (iv), we remark that by the definition of
{\mathscr{N}}
and
T
we have

\forall\xi\in\mathrm{dom}(T)\,\forall a\in{\mathcal{A}}\,\forall\eta\in{\mathscr{H}}:\,\,\langle\xi,\pi(a)X_{0}^{*}\eta\rangle\,=\,\langle T\xi,\rho(a)Y_{0}^{*}\eta\rangle,

hence

\forall a\in{\mathcal{A}}\,\forall\eta\in{\mathscr{H}}:\,\,\rho(a)Y^{*}_{0}\eta\in\mathrm{dom}(T^{*})\text{ and }T^{*}\rho(a)Y^{*}_{0}\eta=\pi(a)X^{*}_{0}\eta

Just as in the proof of (iv) in Lemma
2.6
we remark that
T^{*}
is a closed operator and Kaplansky’s density theorem may then be applied again to show

\forall\eta\in{\mathscr{H}}:\,\,Y^{*}_{0}\eta\in\mathrm{dom}(T^{*})\text{ and }T^{*}Y^{*}_{0}\eta=X^{*}_{0}\eta,

then
T^{*}Y^{*}_{0}=X_{0}^{*}
and since in general for closed densely defined operators
A,B
with
AB
densely defined we have
B^{*}A^{*}\subseteq(AB)^{*}
we get
Y_{0}T=X_{0}|\mathrm{dom}(T),

and the theorem follows.
∎

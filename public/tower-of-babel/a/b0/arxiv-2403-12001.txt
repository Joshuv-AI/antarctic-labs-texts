No-gap second-order conditions for minimization problems in spaces of measures
Gerd Wachsmuth, Daniel Walter

Abstract
. Over the last years, minimization problems over spaces of measures have received increased interest due to their relevance in the context of inverse problems, optimal control and machine learning. A fundamental role in their numerical analysis is played by the assumption that the optimal dual state admits finitely many global extrema and satisfies a second-order sufficient optimality condition in each one of them. In this work, we show the full equivalence of these structural assumptions to a no-gap second-order condition involving the second subderivative of the Radon norm as well as to a local quadratic growth property of the objective functional with respect to the bounded Lipschitz norm. Keywords: Optimization in measure space, second-order optimality conditions, second subderivatives, Wasserstein distance. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 46E27,49K27, 49J52, 49J53

Contribution

1.2.
Related work

The present work is mainly influenced by prior work on no-gap second-order conditions utilizing the notion of second subderivatives.
This is a well-known concept in finite-dimensional variational analysis,
see the classical treatment in
RockafellarWets1998
, Definition 13.3

as well as the recent contribution
BenkoMehlitz2022
. In the infinite-dimensional setting,
this derivative has been used in
Do1992

to characterize the directional differentiability of projections.
More recently,
ChristofWachsmuth2017:1
,
second subderivatives have been utilized to derive second-order conditions for infinite-dimensional constrained minimization problems.
Subsequently, this approach has been further developed
in the contributions

wachsmuth2
;
BorchardWachsmuth2023
.

In the present work, we follow an implicit approach to computing
G^{\prime\prime}(\imath_{\mathcal{M}}\bar{u},\bar{p};\cdot)
in order to avoid the necessity of working on the dual space
C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}
. More in detail, we first prove the strong-strong twice epi-differentiability of the preadjoint of
G
, which is now a functional over
C^{1}(\Omega)
, and derive a candidate for
G^{\prime\prime}(\imath_{\mathcal{M}}\bar{u},\bar{p};\cdot)
via arguments from convex duality. Conceptually, this is closest related to the exposition in
wachsmuth2
where the authors consider a similar treatment for integral functionals defined on
L^{1}(\Omega)
.
Finally, we also mention

ChristofWachsmuth2017:3

where the authors address the differentiability of
parameter-to-solution maps associated to parameter-dependent variational inequalities by using second subderivatives.

The notion of non-degeneracy of certain dual variables was possibly first coined in the seminal paper
duval_peyre_2014
where it was used to address the recovery properties of Radon norm variational regularization in inverse problems. Similar applications can be found in, e.g.,
poon_2018
;
poon_2023
;
huynh2023optimal
. Subsequently, these structural assumptions have also become a central pillar for the analysis of finite element discretizations of PDE-constrained optimal control problems with sparse controls, see, e.g.,
vexler
, as well as the derivation of fast convergence results for numerical algorithms such as generalized conditional gradient or exchange type methods,
pieper_walter_2021
;
flinth
, or over-parametrized gradient descent,
chizat
. In this context, we also point out that
chizat
, Proposition 3.1
deduces local quadratic growth w.r.t the bounded Lipschitz norm from a slightly stronger analogue of (
1.4
) and (
1.5
).

If
Y
is finite dimensional and
L
is convex, we also note that Problem (

P

) can be interpreted as the Fenchel dual of a semi-infinite, state-constrained problem. From this perspective,
\bar{u}
corresponds to the Lagrange multiplier for the state constraint imposed on the optimal state

Under the canonical norm

Minimization problems in spaces of measures

In this section, we collect some preliminary results on minimization problems of the form (

Assumption 3.1
.

Throughout the paper, we assume that:

A1

The space of observations

\lVert\cdot\rVert_{Y}=\sqrt{(\cdot,\cdot)_{Y}}
.

A2

The functional

\mathcal{C}^{2}
.

A3

The kernel

A4

The set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is compact,
it equals the closure of its interior

and

is uniformly locally quasiconvex, see

5.1
.

First, we note that the regularity of

implies weak*-to-strong continuity of the integral operator
K
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
3.1
hold. Then the operator
K\colon\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\to Y
as defined in (
1.1
) is linear and sequentially weak*-to-strong continuous. Moreover, we have
K=(K_{*})^{*}
where the preadjoint
K_{*}\colon Y\to C(\Omega)
is given by

\displaystyle[K_{*}y](x)=(k(x),y)_{Y}\quad\forall y\in Y,\penalty\ x\in\Omega.

Proof.

Note that
K_{*}
is linear and bounded since
k(x)\in C(\Omega;Y)
. Moreover, we observe

\displaystyle\langle K_{*}y,u\rangle=\int_{\Omega}(k(x),y)_{Y}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u(x)=\left(y,\int_{\Omega}k(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u(x)\right)_{Y}=(y,Ku)_{Y}

for all
y\in Y
and
u\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
. Thus, we have
(K_{*})^{*}=K
. In particular,
K
is linear and bounded as well as sequentially weak*-to-weak continuous. Let
(u_{k})\subset\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
be a weak* convergent sequence with limit
\bar{u}
. Then there exists a sequence
(y_{k})\subset Y
with
\lVert y_{k}\rVert_{Y}=1
,
\lVert Ku_{k}\rVert_{Y}=(y_{k},Ku_{k})_{Y}
and thus

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lVert K\bar{u}\rVert_{Y}\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lVert Ku_{k}\rVert_{Y}=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}(y_{k},Ku_{k})_{Y}=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\langle K_{*}y_{k},u_{k}\rangle.

Now, consider an arbitrary weakly convergent subsequence of
(y_{k})
, denoted by the same symbol, with limit
\bar{y}\in Y
,
\lVert\bar{y}\rVert_{Y}\leq 1
. We readily verify that
K_{*}y_{k}
converges weakly to
K_{*}\bar{y}
in

. Moreover, since
k\in C^{0,\gamma}(\Omega;Y)
, the sequence
(K_{*}y_{k})
is uniformly bounded in

. Hence, due to Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, we conclude
K_{*}y_{k}\rightarrow K_{*}\bar{y}

and, finally,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\langle K_{*}y_{k},u_{k}\rangle=\langle K_{*}\bar{y},\bar{u}\rangle\leq\lVert K\bar{u}\rVert_{Y}.

Together with (

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lVert Ku_{k}\rVert_{Y}=\lVert K\bar{u}\rVert_{Y}
.
A usual subsequence-subsequence argument yields

for the entire sequence,

is weak*-to-strong continuous.
∎

In what follows, we denote by

.

The Jordan decomposition

\displaystyle\supp\bar{u}_{\pm}\subset\left\{x\in\Omega\nonscript\;\middle|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\bar{p}(x)=\pm\alpha\right\}\quad\text{as well as}\quad|\bar{p}|\leq\alpha\quad\text{on}\penalty\ \Omega

4.
No-gap second-order conditions for abstract minimization problems

Next, we summarize tangible results on no-gap second-order conditions for nonsmooth minimization problems. Subsequently, these will be specialized to the problem at hand. More in detail, we consider

(4.1)

\min_{x\in X}\left[F(x)+G(x)\right].

We start by fixing the setting.

Assumption 4.1
.

The following assumptions are made throughout this section.

(1)

There holds
X=Z^{*}
for a separable Banach space
Z
.

(2)

The functional
G\colon X\to(-\infty,\infty]

and
the point

\bar{x}\in\dom(G)
are
given.

(3)

Associated with the functional
F\colon\dom(G)\to\mathbb{R}
,
there exist

F^{\prime}(\bar{x})\in Z

and
a bounded bilinear form

F^{\prime\prime}(\bar{x})\colon X\times X\to\mathbb{R}

such that

(4.2)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{F(\bar{x}+t_{k}h_{k})-F(\bar{x})-t_{k}F^{\prime}(\bar{x})h_{k}-\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}F^{\prime\prime}(\bar{x})h_{k}^{2}}{t_{k}^{2}}=0

holds
for all sequences
(t_{k})\subset(0,\infty)
,
(h_{k})\subset X

satisfying
t_{k}\searrow 0
,
h_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}h\in X

and

\bar{x}+t_{k}h_{k}\in\dom(G)
.

Loosely speaking, the “smooth” part
F
of the objective admits a second-order Taylor expansion around
\bar{x}
.
The bilinear form
F^{\prime\prime}(\bar{x})
can be interpreted as its Hessian.
Regarding the potentially nonsmooth term
G
, we rely on the notion of the weak* second subderivative.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
x\in\dom(G)
and
p\in Z
be given.
The
weak* second subderivative

\displaystyle G^{\prime\prime}(x,p;\cdot)\colon X\to[-\infty,\infty]

of
G
at
x
for

is given by

\displaystyle G^{\prime\prime}(x,p;h)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{G(x+t_{k}h_{k})-G(x)-t_{k}\langle z,h_{k}\rangle}{t^{2}_{k}/2}\nonscript\;\middle|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}t_{k}\searrow 0,\penalty\ h_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}h\right\}.

The functional
G
is called
strictly twice epi-differentiable
at
x
for

if for every
h\in X
and every sequence
(t_{k})\subset(0,\infty)
with
t_{k}\searrow 0

there exists a sequence
(h_{k})\subset X
with

\displaystyle G^{\prime\prime}(x,p;h)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{G(x+t_{k}h_{k})-G(x)-t_{k}\langle z,h_{k}\rangle}{t^{2}_{k}/2},\quad h_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}h,\penalty\ \lVert h_{k}\rVert_{X}\to\lVert h\rVert_{X}.

We use the following result,
see
BorchardWachsmuth2023
, Theorem 2.20

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
4.1
hold and assume that
h\mapsto F^{\prime\prime}(\bar{x})h^{2}
is sequentially weak* continuous.
Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

There exist
\varepsilon>0
and
\gamma>0

satisfying the second-order growth condition

\displaystyle(F+G)(x)-(F+G)(\bar{x})\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert x-\bar{x}\rVert^{2}_{X}\qquad\forall x\in X,\lVert x-\bar{x}\rVert_{X}\leq\varepsilon.

(ii)

We have
the sufficient second-order condition

(SSC)

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(\bar{x})h^{2}+G^{\prime\prime}(\bar{x},-F^{\prime}(\bar{x});h)>0\qquad\forall h\in X\setminus\{0\}

as well as
the non-degeneracy condition

(NDC)

\displaystyle\text{for all $(t_{k})\subset(0,\infty)$, $(h_{k})\subset X$
with $t_{k}\searrow 0$, $h_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}0$}\text{ and $\lVert h_{k}\rVert_{X}=1$, we have }

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{t_{k}^{2}}\big(G(\bar{x}+t_{k}h_{k})-G(\bar{x})\big)+\langle F^{\prime}(\bar{x}),h_{k}/t_{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime\prime}(\bar{x})h_{k}^{2}\bigg)>0.

5.1.
Uniform local Quasiconvexity

In what follows,
we recall the notion of “uniform local quasiconvexity”
appearing in
3.1

and discuss its consequences.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that a set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is

uniformly locally quasiconvex

with constants

r>0
and
C\geq 1

if for any

x,y\in D
with
\lvert y-x\rvert\leq r

there exists a curve

\gamma\in C([0,1];D)

with
\gamma(0)=x
,
\gamma(1)=y

and Lipschitz constant
at most
C\lvert y-x\rvert
.

Remark 5.2
.

Typically, it is required that the curve
\gamma\in C([0,1];D)

from
Definition

5.1

is rectifiable and of length at most
C\lvert y-x\rvert

(instead of being Lipschitz continuous),
see
HajlaszKoskelaTuominen2008
, p. 1223
.
However, one can perform an arc-length reparametrization
to ensure Lipschitz continuity of
\gamma
.

It is clear that convex sets are uniformly locally quasiconvex.
Further,
the next result shows that
Definition

5.1

is a rather weak requirement.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and bounded.
Further, we require that
D

is a Lipschitz set
in the sense that
for every point

,
there exists
r>0
and
a bijection
l_{p}\colon B_{r}(p)\to B_{1}(0)

such that
l_{p}
and
l_{p}^{-1}
are Lipschitz,

l_{p}(D\cap B_{r}(p))=\{z\in B_{1}(0)\nonscript\;|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}z_{n}>0\}=:B_{1}^{+}(0)\quad\text{and}\quad l_{p}(\partial D\cap B_{r}(p))=\{z\in B_{1}(0)\nonscript\;|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}z_{n}=0\}.

Then,

is uniformly locally quasiconvex.

The proof of
Lemma

5.3

can be found in

We give two important consequences of

For the proofs, we again refer to

The first lemma shows that functions from

Lemma 5.4

Let

Assume that

In case that

5.2.
A primer on the dual space of
C^{1}(\Omega)
and the relation to the bounded Lipschitz norm

We start by giving a precise characterization of the space
C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}
and of its canonical dual norm. For this purpose, note that

\displaystyle C^{1}(\Omega)\cong C\coloneqq\left\{(\varphi,\nabla\varphi)\nonscript\;\middle|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\varphi\in C^{1}(\Omega)\right\}\subset C(\Omega)\times C(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where the product space on the right-hand side is equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\lVert(\varphi,\phi)\rVert_{C\times C^{d}}=\max\left\{\lVert\varphi\rVert_{C},\lVert\phi\rVert_{C^{d}}\right\}\qquad\forall(\varphi,\phi)\in C(\Omega)\times C(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Note that the mapping
C^{1}(\Omega)\ni\varphi\mapsto(\varphi,\nabla\varphi)\in C(\Omega)\times C(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})

is an isometry.
One readily verifies that the dual space
of
C(\Omega)\times C(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})

is given by
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
with associated duality pairing

\langle\varphi,u\rangle+\langle\phi,v\rangle\qquad\forall(\varphi,\phi)\in C(\Omega)\times C(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\penalty\ (u,v)\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})

and norm

\lVert(u,v)\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}+\lVert v\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{d}}=\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}+\lVert\lvert v\rvert\rVert_{\mathcal{M}},

where
\lvert v\rvert\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
is the variation
of the vector-valued measure
v

(w.r.t. the Euclidean norm on its image space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
).

The subset
C
is complete since it is isometrically isomorphic
to the complete space
C^{1}(\Omega)
.
Thus,

C
is a closed subspace of
C(\Omega)\times C(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Consequently, its dual space can be characterized using the annihilator

\displaystyle C^{\bot}\coloneqq\left\{(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\nonscript\;\middle|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\langle\varphi,\mu\rangle+\langle\nabla\varphi,\nu\rangle=0\quad\forall(\varphi,\nabla\varphi)\in C\right\}.

Lemma 5.7

(
Brezis2011
, Proposition 11.10
)
.

There holds

(5.1)

(\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}))/C^{\bot}\cong C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}.

The isometric isomorphism is given by

(\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}))/C^{\bot}\ni(u,v)+C^{\bot}\mapsto\left(C^{1}(\Omega)\ni\varphi\mapsto\langle\varphi,u\rangle+\langle\nabla\varphi,v\rangle\right).

Consequently, we identify the elements of
C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}
with equivalence classes

\llbracket u,v\rrbracket\coloneqq(u,v)+C^{\bot}=\left\{(u,v)+(\mu,\nu)\nonscript\;\middle|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}(\mu,\nu)\in C^{\bot}\right\}\in(\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}))/C^{\bot}

of measures
(u,v)\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})

which act on functions
\varphi\in C^{1}(\Omega)
via the duality pairing

\displaystyle\llangle\varphi,\llbracket u,v\rrbracket\rrangle\coloneqq\langle\varphi,u\rangle+\langle\nabla\varphi,v\rangle.

In what follows, we will always employ the identification (
5.1
),
i.e., bounded functionals on
C^{1}(\Omega)
are always identified with the corresponding equivalence classes.
Consequently, the norm on
C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}
is given by
the quotient norm

\lVert\llbracket u,v\rrbracket\rVert_{(C^{1})^{*}}=\sup_{\lVert\varphi\rVert_{C^{1}}\leq 1}\llangle\varphi,\llbracket u,v\rrbracket\rrangle=\inf\left\{\lVert u+\mu\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}+\lVert v+\nu\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{d}}\nonscript\;\middle|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}(\mu,\nu)\in C^{\bot}\right\}.

In the subsequent sections,
we frequently encounter elements of the form
\llbracket u,0\rrbracket
with
u\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)=C(\Omega)^{*}
.
Note that
\llbracket u,0\rrbracket
is the restriction of the functional
u\colon C(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}

to the domain
C^{1}(\Omega)
.
In what follows, we give
a simpler representation of the dual norm for such elements
\llbracket u,0\rrbracket
.
For this purpose, we denote by
\operatorname{Lip}(\Omega)
the space of Lipschitz continuous functions on
\Omega
which we equip with the canonical norm

\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\operatorname{Lip}}\coloneqq\max\{\lVert\varphi\rVert_{C},\lip(\varphi)\},

where we used the Lipschitz constant
\lip(\varphi)
of
\varphi
defined in (
2.1
).
Naturally, every
u\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
defines a linear function on
\operatorname{Lip}(\Omega)
. The associated dual norm

(5.2)

\lVert u\rVert_{\operatorname{BL}}=\sup_{\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\operatorname{Lip}}\leq 1}\langle\varphi,u\rangle

will be referred to as the
bounded Lipschitz
norm.
Due to our standing assumption that
\operatorname{int}(\Omega)

is uniformly locally quasiconvex,
the space
C^{1}(\Omega)
is embedded into the Lipschitz functions, see
Lemma

5.4
,
and
we obtain the equivalence of the bounded Lipschitz norm with
the natural norm of
C^{1}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 5.8
.

The norms

\lVert\llbracket\cdot,0\rrbracket\rVert_{(C^{1})^{*}}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\operatorname{BL}}

are equivalent on
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

From

Lemma

5.4

and the definition of
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\operatorname{Lip}}

we have

\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\operatorname{Lip}}\leq L\lVert\varphi\rVert_{C^{1}}\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}(\Omega)

for some constant
L>0
.
This yields

\lVert\llbracket u,0\rrbracket\rVert_{(C^{1})^{*}}=\sup_{\lVert\varphi\rVert_{C^{1}}\leq 1}\llangle\varphi,\llbracket u,0\rrbracket\rrangle=\sup_{\lVert\varphi\rVert_{C^{1}}\leq 1}\langle\varphi,u\rangle\leq\sup_{\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\operatorname{Lip}}\leq L}\langle\varphi,u\rangle=L\lVert u\rVert_{\operatorname{BL}}.

For the reverse estimate,
let
\varphi\in\operatorname{Lip}(\Omega)
with
\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\operatorname{Lip}}\leq 1
be given.
Using classical arguments,

\varphi
can be extended to all of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

while preserving its Lipschitz constant.
Now, let
\varepsilon>0
be given and denote by
\Phi_{\varepsilon}

a mollifier
supported on
B_{\varepsilon}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then,

\psi:=\Phi_{\varepsilon}\mathbin{\star}\varphi

satisfies

\psi\in C^{1}(\Omega)
,

\lVert\psi\rVert_{C^{1}}\leq 1

and

\lVert\psi-\varphi\rVert_{C}\leq\varepsilon
.
For an arbitrary
u\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
,
we get

\langle\varphi,u\rangle\leq\varepsilon\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}+\langle\psi,u\rangle=\varepsilon\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}+\llangle\varphi,\llbracket u,0\rrbracket\rrangle\leq\varepsilon\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}+\lVert u\rVert_{(C^{1})^{*}}.

Taking the limit
\varepsilon\searrow 0

and the supremum w.r.t.
\varphi
,
this shows

\lVert u\rVert_{\operatorname{BL}}\leq\lVert u\rVert_{(C^{1})^{*}}
.
∎

Moreover, we require the notion of the Wasserstein-1 distance between positive measures of the same mass.

Definition 5.9
.

Given two measures
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
with
\mu,\nu\geq 0
and
\mu(\Omega)=\nu(\Omega)
, we define the Wasserstein-1 distance between
\mu
and
\nu
as

W_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\min\left\{\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\lvert x-y\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)\nonscript\;\middle|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\right\},

where the minimum is taken over all couplings
\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\subset\mathcal{M}(\Omega\times\Omega)
between
\mu
and
\nu
, i.e., there holds
\gamma\geq 0
as well as

\gamma(B\times\Omega)=\mu(B)\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma(\Omega\times B)=\nu(B)

for all Borel sets
B\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
.

The well known Kantorovich duality
yields

W_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\mu-\nu)\nonscript\;\middle|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\lip(\psi)\leq 1\right\}.

Using this, we can derive an optimal transport based representation of the bounded Lipschitz norm.

Lemma 5.10
.

For every
u\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
we denote by
u=u_{+}-u_{-}
the Jordan decomposition of
u
.
Then, we have

(5.3)

\lVert u\rVert_{\operatorname{BL}}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu,\nu\geq 0\\
\mu(\Omega)=\nu(\Omega)\end{subarray}}\left[W_{1}(\mu,\nu)+\lVert u_{+}-\mu\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}+\lVert u_{-}-\nu\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\right].

Proof.

The formula (
5.3
) will be proven by Fenchel duality.
We define the sets

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\coloneqq C(\Omega)^{3},

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})\in X\nonscript\;|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\lip(\psi_{1})\leq 1,\;\lVert\psi_{2}\rVert_{C}\leq 1,\;\lVert\psi_{3}\rVert_{C}\leq 1\},

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle\coloneqq C(\Omega)^{2},

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\in Y\nonscript\;|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\varphi_{1}\leq 0,\;\varphi_{2}\leq 0\},

as well as the mappings

A\colon X\to Y
,
f\colon X\to(-\infty,\infty]
and
g\colon Y\to(-\infty,\infty]
via

\displaystyle A(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2},-\psi_{1}-\psi_{3}),

\displaystyle g(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})

\displaystyle\coloneqq I_{M}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}),

\displaystyle f(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})

\displaystyle\coloneqq I_{D}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})+\langle\psi_{2},u_{+}\rangle+\langle\psi_{3},u_{-}\rangle.

The functionals
f
and
g
are convex and lower semicontinuous
and
A
is linear and bounded.
The set
A\dom(f)
contains the constant function
(-1,-1)

and this is a continuity point of
g
.
Hence, Fenchel duality gives

j_{1}\coloneqq\inf_{(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})\in X}f(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})+g(A(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3}))=-\min_{(\mu,\nu)\in Y^{*}}f^{*}(-A^{*}(\mu,\nu))+g^{*}(\mu,\nu)\eqqcolon j_{2},

see
BorweinZhu2005
, Theorem 4.4.3
.
It remains to compute
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
.
For
j_{1}
we get

\displaystyle j_{1}

\displaystyle=\inf_{(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})\in X}f(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})+g(A(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3}))

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\langle\psi_{2},u_{+}\rangle+\langle\psi_{3},u_{-}\rangle\nonscript\;|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})\in D,\;-\psi_{3}\leq\psi_{1}\leq\psi_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\langle\psi_{1},u_{+}\rangle+\langle-\psi_{1},u_{-}\rangle\nonscript\;|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}(\psi_{1},\psi_{1},-\psi_{1})\in D\right\},

where we used that
u_{+},u_{-}\geq 0
.
Now,
(\psi_{1},\psi_{1},-\psi_{1})\in D

is equivalent to
\lVert\psi_{1}\rVert_{\operatorname{Lip}}\leq 1

and, thus,

j_{1}=-\lVert u\rVert_{\operatorname{BL}}.

To evaluate
j_{2}
, we first compute

g^{*}(\mu,\nu)=\sup\left\{\langle\varphi_{1},\mu\rangle+\langle\varphi_{2},\nu\rangle\nonscript\;|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\in M\right\}=I_{P}(\mu,\nu),

where
P\subset\mathcal{M}(\Omega)^{2}
is the subset of non-negative measures.
Moreover,

\displaystyle f^{*}(-A^{*}(\mu,\nu))

\displaystyle=f^{*}(\nu-\mu,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\langle\psi_{1},\nu-\mu\rangle+\langle\psi_{2},\mu\rangle+\langle\psi_{3},\nu\rangle-f(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})\nonscript\;|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})\in X\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\langle\psi_{1},\nu-\mu\rangle+\langle\psi_{2},\mu-u_{+}\rangle+\langle\psi_{3},\nu-u_{-}\rangle\nonscript\;|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})\in D\right\}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}W_{1}(\mu,\nu)+\lVert u_{+}-\mu\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}+\lVert u_{-}-\nu\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}&\text{if }\mu(\Omega)=\nu(\Omega)\\
\infty&\text{if }\mu(\Omega)\neq\nu(\Omega)\end{cases}

for all non-negative measures

\mu(\Omega)=\nu(\Omega)\end{subarray}}\left[W_{1}(\mu,\nu)+\lVert u_{+}-\mu\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}+\lVert u_{-}-\nu\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\right].

Remark 5.12

In the case that the diameter of

5.3.
A lifted setting

In the following, let the canonical injection of
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
into
C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}
be denoted by

\Pi\colon\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\to C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}\quad\text{where}\quad\Pi u\coloneqq\llbracket u,0\rrbracket.

Note that the continuity of this injection follows from the definitions.
Moreover, we define the lifted penalty term and forward operator by

(5.4)

G\colon C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}\to[0,\infty]\quad\text{with}\quad G(\llbracket u,v\rrbracket)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\alpha\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}&\exists\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\colon\llbracket u,v\rrbracket=\Pi\tilde{u}\\
\infty&\text{else}\end{cases}

as well as

(5.5)

\mathcal{K}\colon C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}\to Y\quad\text{with}\quad\mathcal{K}(\llbracket u,v\rrbracket)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}k(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u(x)+\int_{\Omega}Dk(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v(x)

where the second integral has to be understood as

\int_{\Omega}Dk(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v(x)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}Dk(x)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lvert v\rvert}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lvert v\rvert(x).

The properties of
G
and
\mathcal{K}
will be investigated in

Lemmas

5.14
and
5.15

below.

The range of
\Pi
is not (weak*) closed.
Thus, it might not be possible to separate
elements
\llbracket u,v\rrbracket\in C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}\setminus\Ran(\Pi)
from
\Ran(\Pi)
by a continuous linear functional.
However, we can utilize the following characterization.

Lemma 5.13
.

An element
\llbracket u,v\rrbracket\in C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}
belongs to the range of
\Pi

if and only if is bounded by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{C}
, i.e., there exists
C>0
such that we have

\lvert\llangle\varphi,\llbracket u,v\rrbracket\rrangle\rvert\leq C\lVert\varphi\rVert_{C}\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}(\Omega).

Proof.

It is clear that “
\Rightarrow
” holds.
In order to prove “
\Leftarrow
”,
let
\llbracket u,v\rrbracket
be bounded by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{C}
.
Thus, this linear functional is a bounded functional
on the dense subspace
C^{1}(\Omega)
of the normed space
C(\Omega)
.
Hence, it can be uniquely extended to a functional
\tilde{u}\in C(\Omega)^{*}=\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
,
i.e., we have

\llangle\varphi,\llbracket u,v\rrbracket\rrangle=\langle\varphi,\tilde{u}\rangle_{C}=\llangle\varphi,\llbracket\tilde{u},0\rrbracket\rrangle\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{1}(\Omega).

This proves
\llbracket u,v\rrbracket=\Pi\tilde{u}
.
∎

Next, we show that
G
is a nice functional.

Lemma 5.14
.

The function
G
from (
5.4
) is proper, convex and weak* lower semi-continuous on
C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}
. Its preconjugate
H\colon C^{1}(\Omega)\to[0,\infty]
is given by

\displaystyle\forall\varphi\in C^{1}(\Omega):\qquad H(\varphi)=\begin{cases}0&\varphi\in\mathbb{B}_{\alpha},\\
\infty&\text{else},\end{cases}

where
\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}:=\{\phi\in C(\Omega)\nonscript\;|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\lVert\phi\rVert_{C}\leq\alpha\}
.

Proof.

We first show
H^{*}=G
. The claimed properties of
G
then follow immediately. Note that

H^{*}(\llbracket u,v\rrbracket)=\sup\left\{\llangle\varphi,\llbracket u,v\rrbracket\rrangle\nonscript\;|\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\varphi\in\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}\right\}.

We argue by a distinction of cases.
First, if
v=0
, we immediately get
H^{*}(\llbracket u,0\rrbracket)=\alpha\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}

due to the density of
\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}\cap C^{1}(\Omega)
in
\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}\subset C(\Omega)
.
This shows that
H^{*}
and
G
coincide on the image of
\Pi
.
In case that
\llbracket u,v\rrbracket\not\in\Ran(\Pi)
,
we can utilize
Lemma

5.13

and obtain that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exists
\varphi_{n}\in C^{1}(\Omega)
with

\lvert\llangle\varphi_{n},\llbracket u,v\rrbracket\rrangle\rvert\geq n\lVert\varphi_{n}\rVert_{C}.

Since
\pm\alpha\varphi_{n}/\lVert\varphi_{n}\rVert_{C}\in\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}
,
this implies
H^{*}(\llbracket u,v\rrbracket)\geq n
.
Thus, we conclude
H^{*}(\llbracket u,v\rrbracket)=G(\llbracket u,v\rrbracket)=\infty
if
\llbracket u,v\rrbracket\not\in\Ran(\Pi)
.
∎

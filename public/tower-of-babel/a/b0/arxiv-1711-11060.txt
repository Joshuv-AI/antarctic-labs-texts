Abstract
. We prove a robust version of Freiman’s 3 ​ k − 4 3k-4 theorem on the restricted sumset A + Γ B A+_{\Gamma}B , which applies when the doubling constant is at most 3 + 5 2 \tfrac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2} in general and at most 3 3 in the special case when A = − B A=-B . As applications, we derive robust results with other types of assumptions on popular sums, and structure theorems for sets satisfying almost equalities in discrete and continuous versions of the Riesz-Sobolev inequality.



1.
introduction

For two sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{Z}
, the complete sumset
A+B
is defined by

A+B:=\{a+b\colon a\in A,b\in B\}.

We have the trivial lower bound
|A+B|\geq|A|+|B|-1
, and the equality holds if and only if
A,B
are arithmetic progressions with the same common difference. Freiman’s
3k-4
theorem goes a step further, and states that if
|A|=|B|=k
and
|A+B|\leq 3k-4
, then
A,B
must have diameters bounded by
|A+B|-k+1
; i.e., there exist arithmetic progressions
P,Q
with the same common difference and sizes at most
|A+B|-k+1
, such that
A\subset P
and
B\subset Q
.

In this paper we aim at proving a robust version of Freiman’s
3k-4
theorem. For a subset
\Gamma\subset A\times B
, the restricted sumset
A+_{\Gamma}B
is defined by

A+_{\Gamma}B=\{a+b\colon(a,b)\in\Gamma\}.

We are mainly concerned with situations when
\Gamma
is almost all of
A\times B
. The advantage of considering a robust version is to relax the rigid conditions and keep the important feature. See
[
14
]
and
[
13
]
for more work in this flavor.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Let
A,B\subset\mathbb{Z}
be subsets with
|A|=|B|=n\geq\max\{3,2\varepsilon^{-1/2}\}
, and let
\Gamma\subset A\times B
be a subset with
|\Gamma|\geq(1-\varepsilon)n^{2}
. If
|A+_{\Gamma}B|<(1+\theta-11\varepsilon^{1/2})n
where
\theta=\tfrac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}
, then there are arithmetic progressions
P,Q
with the same common difference and sizes at most
|A+_{\Gamma}B|-(1-5\varepsilon^{1/2})n
, such that
|A\cap P|\geq(1-\varepsilon^{1/2})n
and
|B\cap Q|\geq(1-\varepsilon^{1/2})n
.

Our primary focus is on the upper bound for
|A+_{\Gamma}B|
in the assumption. While one naturally expects Theorem
1.1
to hold with
1+\theta
replaced by
3
, we are only able to prove this expected result in the special case when
B=-A
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Let
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
be a subset with
|A|=n\geq\max\{3,2\varepsilon^{-1/2}\}
, and let
\Gamma\subset A\times A
be a subset with
|\Gamma|\geq(1-\varepsilon)n^{2}
. If
|A-_{\Gamma}A|<(3-11\varepsilon^{1/2})n
, then there exists an arithmetic progression with length at most
|A-_{\Gamma}A|-(1-5\varepsilon^{1/2})n
such that
|A\cap P|\geq(1-\varepsilon^{1/2})n
.

As an application to Theorem
1.1
, we prove a structure theorem for sets
A
with the quantity

C(A):=\frac{\#\{(a,a^{\prime})\in A\times A:a+a^{\prime}\in A\}}{|A|^{2}}

being close to its maximum value
3/4+o(1)
. The fact that
C(A)
is maximized when
A
is a symmetric interval around the origin can be proved by a simple combinatorial argument; see
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
. The following result asserts that if
C(A)
is close to
3/4
, then
A
must be close to a symmetric arithmetic progression around the origin.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,2^{-50})
. Let
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
be a subset with
|A|=n\geq\varepsilon^{-1}
and
C(A)\geq 3/4-\varepsilon
. Then there exists an arithmetic progression
P
containing
0
and centered at
0
such that
|P|\leq(1+280\varepsilon^{1/4})n
and
|P\cap A|\geq(1-10\varepsilon^{1/4})n
.

We deduce Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
in Section
2
from two technical propositions, Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
2.3
. In Section
3
, we develop robust analgoues of Kneser’s theorem in abelian groups, which are used in Section
4
to deduce the technical propositions. In Section
5
, we relate our main result with similar types of results in the literature
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. Section
6
contains the deduction of Theorem
1.3
from Theorem
1.1
, and finally in Section
7
we deduce a continuous version of Theorem
1.1
, which is a special case of previous works on near equality in the Riesz-Sobolev inequality
[
1
,

.

2.
Technical version of the main theorems

Before stating the technical versions of our main theorems, we need the following definition which also appeared in
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
,
B
be two finite sets (in an ambient abelian group
G
), and let
\Gamma\subset A\times B
. We say that
A+_{\Gamma}B
is
(K,s)
-regular if the following two conditions hold:

(1)

for each
a\in A
there are at most
s
values of
b\in B
with
(a,b)\notin\Gamma
, and similarly for each
b\in B
there are at most
s
values of
a\in A
with
(a,b)\notin\Gamma
;

(2)

if
r_{A+B}(x)\geq K
, then
x\in A+_{\Gamma}B
.

Here
r_{A+B}(x)
denotes the number of ways to write
x=a+b
with
a\in A
and
b\in B
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\ell
be a positive integer and let
A,B\subset\{0,1,\cdots,\ell\}
be subsets with size
|A|=|B|=n\geq 3
. Assume that
0,\ell\in A
,
0\in B
and
\operatorname{gcd}(A\cup B)=1
. Let
\Gamma\subset A\times B
be a subset such that
A+_{\Gamma}B
is
(K,s)
-regular for some
K\geq 2
and
s\geq 0
.
Then

|A+_{\Gamma}B|\geq\begin{cases}\ell+n-2s&\text{if }\ell<2n-2K-2,\\
(\theta+1)n-4s-2K-\theta&\text{if }\ell\geq 2n-2K-2,\end{cases}

where
\theta=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}.

In the special case when
A=B
, this becomes
[
6
, Theorem 1]
. In fact, our proof will follow arguments from
[
6
]
with modifications to accommodate the asymmetric nature of our result. While Proposition
2.2
is the technical version of Theorem
1.1
, the following proposition corresponds to Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\ell
be a positive integer and let
A\subset\{0,1,\cdots,\ell\}
be a subset with size
|A|=n
. Assume that
0,\ell\in A
and
\operatorname{gcd}(A)=1
. Let
\Gamma\subset A\times A
be a subset such that
A-_{\Gamma}A
is
(K,s)
-regular for some
K\geq 2
and
s\geq 0
.
Then

|A-_{\Gamma}A|\geq\begin{cases}\ell+n-2s&\text{if }\ell<2n-2K-2,\\
3n-4s-2K-2&\text{if }\ell\geq 2n-2K-2.\end{cases}

The reason that we are able to get a better result in the case when
A=-B
stems from a special combinatorial argument (see Proposition
3.2
below) that is not available in the general setting.

We will prove Propositions
2.2
and
2.3
in Section
4
, after developing the essential ingredient in Section
3
about restricted sumsets in abelian groups. In the remainder of this section, we deduce Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
from Propositions
2.2
and
2.3
, respectively.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
assuming Proposition
2.2
.

Since
|\Gamma|\geq(1-\varepsilon)n^{2}
, for every
a\in A
outside an exceptional set of size at most
\varepsilon^{1/2}n
, we have
(a,b)\in\Gamma
for all but at most
\varepsilon^{1/2}n
values of
B
. Thus we can find
A^{\prime}\subset A
and
B^{\prime}\subset B
such that
|A^{\prime}|=|B^{\prime}|\geq(1-\varepsilon^{1/2})n
and for any
a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}
we have

\#\{b^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}\colon(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\notin\Gamma\}\leq\varepsilon^{1/2}n.

Let
\Gamma^{*}=(A^{\prime}\times B^{\prime})\cap\Gamma
. Set

s=\varepsilon^{1/2}n
and
K=\varepsilon^{1/2}n
, and take

\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma^{*}\cup\{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in A^{\prime}\times B^{\prime}\colon r_{A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}}(a^{\prime}+b^{\prime})\geq K\}.

Then by construction
A^{\prime}+_{\Gamma^{\prime}}B^{\prime}
is
(K,s)
-regular. After translating and dilating appropriately so that the smallest elements in
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
are both
0
and
\text{gcd}(A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime})=1
, let
\ell
be the largest element in
A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}
. By Proposition
2.2
we have

|A^{\prime}+_{\Gamma^{\prime}}B^{\prime}|\geq\begin{cases}\ell+|A^{\prime}|-2s&\text{if }\ell<2|A^{\prime}|-2K-2,\\
(\theta+1)|A^{\prime}|-4s-2K-\theta&\text{if }\ell\geq 2|A^{\prime}|-2K-2.\end{cases}

Since
|A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\varepsilon^{1/2})n
and
n\geq 2\varepsilon^{-1/2}
, the inequalities above lead to

|A^{\prime}+_{\Gamma^{\prime}}B^{\prime}|\geq\min(\ell+(1-3\varepsilon^{1/2})n,(\theta+1-10\varepsilon^{1/2})n).

Next we claim that

|A+_{\Gamma}B|\geq|A^{\prime}+_{\Gamma^{\prime}}B^{\prime}|-\varepsilon^{1/2}n.

To see this, note that
A^{\prime}+_{\Gamma^{*}}B^{\prime}\subset A+_{\Gamma}B
, and that if
x\in(A^{\prime}+_{\Gamma^{\prime}}B^{\prime})\setminus(A^{\prime}+_{\Gamma^{*}}B^{\prime})
then
r_{A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}}(x)\geq K
by the definition of
\Gamma^{\prime}
, so that the number of such
x
is at most

\frac{|(A^{\prime}\times B^{\prime})\setminus\Gamma^{*}|}{K}\leq\frac{|(A\times B)\setminus\Gamma|}{K}\leq\varepsilon^{1/2}n.

It follows that

|A+_{\Gamma}B|\geq\min(\ell+(1-4\varepsilon^{1/2})n,(\theta+1-11\varepsilon^{1/2})n).

By our assumption on the size of
A+_{\Gamma}B
, we must have

|A+_{\Gamma}B|\geq\ell+(1-4\varepsilon^{1/2})n.

Setting
P
and
Q
to be suitably translated and dilated copies of the interval
\{0,1,2,\cdots,\ell\}
(so that
A^{\prime}\subset P
and
B^{\prime}\subset Q
), we see that they have lengths

\ell+1\leq|A+_{\Gamma}B|-(1-5\varepsilon^{1/2})n,

and moreover
|A\cap P|\geq|A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\varepsilon^{1/2})n
and similarly
|B\cap Q|\geq|B^{\prime}|\geq(1-\varepsilon^{1/2})n
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
assuming Proposition
2.3
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is almost the same as the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The only difference is that we apply Proposition
2.3
intead of Proposition
2.2
in the final step to complete our proof.
∎

3.
Robust results in the abelian group setting

In this section we prove robust analogues of Kneser’s theorem in abelian groups. Our first result is an extension of
[
6
, Theorem 2]
to two distinct sets
A,B
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G
be an abelian group and let
A,B\subset G
be subsets with
n=|A|\leq|B|
. Let
\Gamma\subset A\times B
be a subset such that
A+_{\Gamma}B
is
(K,s)
-regular for some
K,s\geq 0
. Suppose that
A+_{\Gamma}B\neq A+B
. Then

|A+_{\Gamma}B|>\theta n-K-2s,

where
\theta=\tfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
.

Proof.

For the purpose of contradiction, assume that
|A+_{\Gamma}B|\leq\theta n-K-2s
. The proof is split into the following four steps.

Step 1

By the pigeonhole principle, there exists
\sigma\in G
such that

r_{A+B}(\sigma)\geq\frac{|\Gamma|}{|A+_{\Gamma}B|}>\frac{n^{2}-ns}{\theta n-K-2s}\geq\frac{n(n-s)}{\theta(n-s)}=(\theta-1)n.

From now on fix such
\sigma
.

Step 2

We show that
r_{A-A}(c)\geq(2-\theta)n+K
for each
c\in B-B
. Take
c=b^{\prime}-b\in B-B
for some
b,b^{\prime}\in A
. Since
A+_{\Gamma}B
is
(K,s)
-regular, we have

|(A+b)\backslash(A+_{\Gamma}B)|\leq s,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ |(A+b^{\prime})\backslash(A+_{\Gamma}B)|\leq s.

It follows that

|(A+b)\cup(A+b^{\prime})|\leq|A+_{\Gamma}B|+2s\leq\theta n-K,

and thus

|(A+b)\cap(A+b^{\prime})|\geq 2|A|-\theta n+K=(2-\theta)n+K.

Note that each element in the intersection
(A+b)\cap(A+b^{\prime})
is of the form
a+b=a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}
for some
a,a^{\prime}\in A
, and this leads to a representation

c=b^{\prime}-b=a-a^{\prime}.

Hence
r_{A-A}(c)\geq(2-\theta)n+K
as desired. Similarly one can also show that
r_{B-B}(d)\geq 2|B|-\theta n+K
for each
d\in A-A
.

Step 3

We claim that for the above fixed
\sigma
and each
c\in B-B
, there are at least
K
triples
(b,a,a^{\prime})\in B\times A\times A
such that
c=a^{\prime}-a
and
\sigma=a+b
. To see this, note that the number of
(a,a^{\prime})\in A\times A
with
c=a^{\prime}-a
is at least
(2-\theta)n+K
by Step 2, and the number of
(a,b)\in A\times B
with
\sigma=a+b
is at least
(\theta-1)n
by Step 1. Hence the number of such triples
(b,a,a^{\prime})
is at least

(2-\theta)n+K+(\theta-1)n-|A|\geq K,

as claimed. For any such triple we have
c+\sigma=a^{\prime}+b\in A+B
, and thus from the
(K,s)
-regularity assumption we deduce that
c+\sigma\in A+_{\Gamma}B
. This shows that
B-B+\sigma\subset A+_{\Gamma}B
. Similarly, for each
d\in A-A
the number of triples
(b,b^{\prime},a)\in B\times B\times A
with
d=b^{\prime}-b
and
\sigma=a+b
is at least

2|B|-\theta n+K+(\theta-1)n-|B|\geq K,

showing that
A-A+\sigma\subset A+_{\Gamma}B
as well. Hence

(3.1)

|(A-A)\cup(B-B)|\leq|A+_{\Gamma}B|.

Step 4

Finally, we show that (
3.1
) is impossible. Since
A+_{\Gamma}B\neq A+B
, we may find
a\in A
and
b\in B
such that
a+b\notin A+_{\Gamma}B
. Thus
r_{A+B}(a+b)<K
, which implies that

|(-a+A)\cap(b-B)|<K.

Therefore,

\begin{split}|(A-A)\cup(B-B)|&\geq|(-a+A)\cup(b-B)|>2n-K\\
&>\theta n-K-2s\geq|A+_{\Gamma}B|.\end{split}

This contradicts (
3.1
) and the proof is completed.
∎

Motivated by arguments from
[
7
, Section 4]
, we can obtain a better bound in the special case when
B=-A
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be an abelian group and let
A\subset G
be a subset with
|A|=n
. Let
\Gamma\subset A\times A
be a subset such that
A-_{\Gamma}A
is
(K,s)
-regular for some
K,s\geq 0
. Suppose that
A-_{\Gamma}A\neq A-A
. Then

|A-_{\Gamma}A|>2n-K-2s.

Proof.

Since
A-_{\Gamma}A\neq A-A
, there exists
(a,-a^{\prime})\in A\times-A
such that
a-a^{\prime}\notin A-_{\Gamma}A
. This implies that
r_{A-A}(a-a^{\prime})<K
by the definition of
(K,s)
-regular. Let
N(a)
be the set of neighbours of
a
in
\Gamma
:

N(a)=\{n_{a}\in-A\colon(a,n_{a})\in\Gamma\},

and define
N(a^{\prime})
similarly. Since

|(a+N(a))\cap(a^{\prime}+N(a^{\prime})|\leq r_{A-A}(a-a^{\prime})<K,

it follows that

|(a+N(a))\cup(a^{\prime}+N(a^{\prime}))|>|N(a)|+|N(a^{\prime})|-K\geq 2n-2s-K.

The proof is completed by noting that
(a+N(a))\cup(a^{\prime}-N(a^{\prime}))\subset A-_{\Gamma}A
.
∎

4.
Proof of Propositions
2.2
and
2.3

In this section we prove Propositions
2.2
and
2.3
. The proofs roughly follow Lev and Smeliansky’s proof
[
8
]
of Freiman’s
3k-4
theorem by reducing modulo
\ell
and applying Kenser’s theorem in
\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
, with a significant amount of work devoted to analyzing the stabilizer
H
from Kneser’s theorem.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Let
\widetilde{A},\widetilde{B}\subset\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
be the images of
A,B
modulo
\ell
, respectively, so that
|\widetilde{A}|=n-1
and
|\widetilde{B}|\geq n-1
. Let
\widetilde{\Gamma}\subset\widetilde{A}\times\widetilde{B}
be the image of
\Gamma
modulo
\ell
. The
(K,s)
-regularity of
A+_{\Gamma}B
implies that
\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}
is
(2K,s)
-regular:

(1)

for any
\widetilde{a}\in\widetilde{A}
there are at most
s
values of
\widetilde{b}\in\widetilde{B}
such that
(\widetilde{a},\widetilde{b})\notin\widetilde{\Gamma}
, and similarly for any
\widetilde{b}\in\widetilde{B}
there are at most
s
values of
\widetilde{a}\in\widetilde{A}
such that
(\widetilde{a},\widetilde{b})\notin\widetilde{\Gamma}
;

(2)

if
r_{\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}}(\widetilde{x})\geq 2K
, then
\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}
.

Indeed, (1) holds because each bad pair
(a,b)\notin\Gamma
reduces to at most one bad pair
(\widetilde{a},\widetilde{b})\notin\widetilde{\Gamma}
. To show (2), take
\widetilde{x}\notin\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}
, so that any pre-image of
\widetilde{x}
is not in
A+_{\Gamma}B
. If
\widetilde{x}=0
, then

r_{\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}}(0)\leq r_{A+_{\Gamma}B}(0)+r_{A+_{\Gamma}B}(\ell)+r_{A+_{\Gamma}B}(2\ell)\leq 1+(K-1)+1<2K,

since
K\geq 2
. If
\widetilde{x}\neq 0
, then it has two possible pre-images
x,x+\ell
and thus

r_{\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}}(\widetilde{x})\leq r_{A+_{\Gamma}B}(x)+r_{A+_{\Gamma}B}(x+\ell)<K+K=2K.

This proves the
(2K,s)
-regularity of
\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}
.

Note that if
(0,b)
and
(\ell,b)
both lie in
\Gamma
for some
b\in B
, then both
0+b
and
\ell+b
lie in
A+_{\Gamma}B
, but they reduce to the same element modulo
\ell
and are thus counted only once in
\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}
. Since there are at least
n-2s
values of such
b\in B
, we conclude that

(4.1)

|A+_{\Gamma}B|\geq|\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}|+n-2s.

If
\ell<2n-2K-2
, then
\ell+2K<2n-2\leq|\widetilde{A}|+|\widetilde{B}|
. It follows that

|\widetilde{A}\cap(x-\widetilde{B})|\geq|\widetilde{A}|+|\widetilde{B}|-\ell>2K

for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
, and hence
r_{\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}}(x)>2K
for any
x
. Since
\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}
is
(2K,s)
-regular, we have
\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
, and by (
4.1
) we have
|A+_{\Gamma}B|\geq\ell+n-2s
, as desired.

If
\ell\geq 2n-2K-2
and
\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}\neq\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}
, then we may apply Proposition
3.1
to get

|\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}|>\theta(n-1)-2K-2s.

Thus by (
4.1
) we get the desired bound

|A+_{\Gamma}B|\geq(\theta+1)n-2K-4s-\theta.

It remains to consider the case when
\ell\geq 2n-2K-2
and
\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}=\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}
.
We may assume that

(4.2)

|\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}|=|\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}|\leq 2n-2s-4,

since otherwise the conclusion follows from (
4.1
). Let
H=H(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})
be the period of
\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}
, so that
\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}+H=\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}
. By Kneser’s theorem applied to
\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}
, we have

(4.3)

|\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}|\geq|\widetilde{A}+H|+|\widetilde{B}+H|-|H|.

To count the number of elements
x\in A+_{\Gamma}B
, we divide into three cases according to whether
\widetilde{x}
, the projection of
x
modulo
\ell
, lies in
\widetilde{B}
,
(\widetilde{B}+H)\setminus\widetilde{B}
, or
(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})\setminus(\widetilde{B}+H)
. Note that
\widetilde{B}+H\subset\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}
since
\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}+H=\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}
and
0\in\widetilde{A}
. Note also that if
\widetilde{A}\subset H
then one only needs to consider the first two cases as the third set is empty.

Case 1

First we claim that

\#\{x\in A+_{\Gamma}B:\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{B}\}\geq 2n-2s-1.

Write
B=\{b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{n}\}
where
0=b_{1}<b_{2}<\cdots<b_{n}\leq\ell
. Since
0,\ell\in A
, the following
2n-1
sums all lie in
A+B
:

0+b_{1},0+b_{2},\cdots,0+b_{n},\ell+b_{2},\ell+b_{3},\cdots,\ell+b_{n}.

Moreover, at most
2s
of them do not belong to
A+_{\Gamma}B
, and the claim follows.

Case 2

We claim that

\#\{x\in A+_{\Gamma}B:\widetilde{x}\in(\widetilde{B}+H)\setminus\widetilde{B}\}\geq|(\widetilde{B}+H)\backslash\widetilde{B}|=|\widetilde{B}+H|-|\widetilde{B}|.

Indeed, since
(\widetilde{B}+H)\backslash\widetilde{B}\subset\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}=\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}
, every
\widetilde{x}\in(\widetilde{B}+H)\backslash\widetilde{B}
has a pre-image in
A+_{\Gamma}B
.

We may now conclude the proof when
\widetilde{A}\subset H
. If
H=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
, then from (
4.3
) it follows that
|\widetilde{A}+_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\widetilde{B}|=|\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}|\geq\ell
. Hence by (
4.1
), we have

|A+_{\Gamma}B|\geq\ell+n-2s\geq 3n-2K-2s-2>(\theta+1)n-4s-2K-\theta,

as desired. If
H\subsetneq\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
, then
\widetilde{B}+H
must contain at least two cosets of
H
, since otherwise
\widetilde{B}
is contained in a single coset of
H
, contradicting the assumptions that
0\in\widetilde{B}
and
\text{gcd}(A\cup B)=1
. Hence
|\widetilde{B}+H|\geq 2|H|\geq 2|\widetilde{A}|=2n-2
. Combining the bounds from Cases 1 and 2, we have

\begin{split}|A+_{\Gamma}B|&\geq 2n-2s-1+|\widetilde{B}+H|-|\widetilde{B}|\geq 2n-2s-1+(2n-2)-n\\
&>(\theta+1)n-4s-2K-\theta,\end{split}

as desired. From now on assume that
\widetilde{A}\subsetneq H
, and we will need to consider Case 3 as well.

Case 3

Finally we prove that

\#\{x\in A+_{\Gamma}B:\widetilde{x}\in(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})\setminus(\widetilde{B}+H)\}\geq 2|\widetilde{B}|-|\widetilde{B}+H|-2s-1.

Since
\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}+H=\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}
, the set
(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})\setminus(\widetilde{B}+H)
is a disjoint union of
N
cosets of
H
, where

N=\frac{|\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}|-|\widetilde{B}+H|}{|H|}.

Note that
N\geq 1
, since otherwise
|\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}|=|\widetilde{B}+H|
, and by (
4.3
) we get
|\widetilde{A}+H|\leq|H|
, contradicting the assumption that
\widetilde{A}\subsetneq H
. Fix a coset
\widetilde{a}+\widetilde{b}+H
in
(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})\setminus(\widetilde{B}+H)
, and let us count the quantity

M:=\#\{x\in A+_{\Gamma}B:\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{a}+\widetilde{b}+H\}.

Define
A^{\prime}
to be the set of those elements in
A
whose projections modulo
\ell
lie in the coset
\widetilde{a}+H
, and similarly define
B^{\prime}
to be the set of those elements in
B
whose projections modulo
\ell
lie in
\widetilde{b}+H
. Since any sum in
A^{\prime}+_{\Gamma}B^{\prime}
projects to an element in the desired coset
\widetilde{a}+\widetilde{b}+H
, we have

(4.4)

M\geq|A^{\prime}+_{\Gamma}B^{\prime}|\geq|A^{\prime}|+|B^{\prime}|-2s-1,

where the second inequality follows by writing

A^{\prime}=\{x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{|A^{\prime}|}\},\ \ B^{\prime}=\{y_{1}<y_{2}<\cdots<y_{|B^{\prime}|}\},

and noting that among the
|A^{\prime}|+|B^{\prime}|-1
distinct sums

x_{1}+y_{1},x_{1}+y_{2},\cdots,x_{1}+y_{|B^{\prime}|},x_{2}+y_{|B^{\prime}|},\cdots,x_{|A^{\prime}|}+y_{|B^{\prime}|},

at most
2s
of them do not lie in
A^{\prime}+_{\Gamma}B^{\prime}
.

Let
\widetilde{A}^{\prime}
be the image of
A^{\prime}
after modulo
\ell
; i.e.
\widetilde{A}^{\prime}=\widetilde{A}\cap(\widetilde{a}+H)
. Since
(\widetilde{a}+H)\setminus\widetilde{A}=(\widetilde{a}+H)\setminus\widetilde{A}^{\prime}
is contained in
(\widetilde{A}+H)\setminus\widetilde{A}
, we have

|\widetilde{A}+H|-|\widetilde{A}|\geq|\widetilde{a}+H|-|\widetilde{A}^{\prime}|\geq|H|-|A^{\prime}|.

It follows that

|A^{\prime}|\geq|H|+|\widetilde{A}|-|\widetilde{A}+H|,

and similarly,

|B^{\prime}|\geq|H|+|\widetilde{B}|-|\widetilde{B}+H|.

Combining the two bounds above with (
4.4
) and (
4.3
), we obtain

\begin{split}M&\geq 2|H|+|\widetilde{A}|+|\widetilde{B}|-|\widetilde{A}+H|-|\widetilde{B}+H|-2s-1\\
&\geq|H|+|\widetilde{A}|+|\widetilde{B}|-|\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}|-2s-1.\end{split}

Call the lower bound above
M_{0}
so that
M\geq M_{0}
. In particular, we have
M_{0}\geq|H|+1
by (
4.2
). We conclude that the total number of
x\in A+_{\Gamma}B
counted in this case is at least
M_{0}N
, and

\begin{split}M_{0}N&=N|H|+N(M_{0}-|H|)\geq N|H|+M_{0}-|H|\\
&=|\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}|-|\widetilde{B}+H|+(|H|+|\widetilde{A}|+|\widetilde{B}|-|\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B}|-2s-1)-|H|\\
&=|\widetilde{A}|+|\widetilde{B}|-|\widetilde{B}+H|-2s-1.\end{split}

Combining Cases 1, 2, and 3 together we get the desired bound

\begin{split}|A+_{\Gamma}B|&\geq(2n-2s-1)+(|\widetilde{B}+H|-|\widetilde{A}|)+(|\widetilde{A}|+|\widetilde{B}|-|\widetilde{B}+H|-2s-1)\\
&\geq 3n-4s-3.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

One can follow the proof of Proposition
2.2
above, taking
B=\ell-A
and using Proposition
3.2
instead of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

5.
Robust results with other assumptions

Our main theorem deals with the restricted sumset
A+_{\Gamma}B
, where
\Gamma
consists of almost all of the pairs from
A\times B
. One may consider other robust versions of the sumset
A+B
. In this section we deduce two such variants, and compare them with existing results in the literature.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be two sets of integers both with size
n\geq 3
. Let
0<\delta<\tfrac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}
. Assume that

\sum_{x}\min(1_{A}*1_{B}(x),t)\leq(2+\delta)nt,\ \ 1\leq t\leq 2^{-10}\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}-\delta\right)^{2}n.

Then there are arithmetic progressions
P,Q
with the same common difference and sizes at most
(1+\delta)n+10t^{1/2}n^{1/2}
, such that
|A\cap P|\geq n-2t^{1/2}n^{1/2}
and
|B\cap Q|\geq n-2t^{1/2}n^{1/2}
.

The assumption here is motivated by Pollard’s theorem which states that

\sum_{x}\min(1_{A}*1_{B}(x),t)\geq t(2n-t)

for any
0\leq t\leq n
. In the case when
A=B
, this should be compared with
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
, where the assumptions on
\delta
and
t
are:

0<\delta<\tfrac{1}{4},\ \ t\leq\tfrac{1}{5}\left(\tfrac{1}{4}-\delta\right)n.

Thus Corollary
5.1
extends the range of
\delta
, at the cost of requiring
t
to be a much smaller constant times
n
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
be the set of pairs
(a,b)\in A\times B
with
r_{A+B}(a+b)\geq t
. Then

\sum_{x}\min(1_{A}*1_{B}(x),t)=\sum_{x\in A+_{\Gamma}B}t+\sum_{x\notin A+_{\Gamma}B}1_{A}*1_{B}(x)=t|A+_{\Gamma}B|+|(A\times B)\setminus\Gamma|.

By assumption the quantity above is bounded by
(2+\delta)nt
. Thus it follows that

|A+_{\Gamma}B|\leq(2+\delta)n

and

|(A\times B)\setminus\Gamma|\leq(2+\delta)nt\leq 3nt.

We will apply Theorem
1.1
with
\varepsilon=3t/n
. The upper bound on
t
implies that

2+\delta\leq 1+\theta-11\varepsilon^{1/2},

where
\theta=\tfrac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}
, so that the assumption of Theorem
1.1
is satisfied. Thus there are arithmetic progressions
P,Q
with the same common difference and sizes at most

|A+_{\Gamma}B|-(1-5(3t/n)^{1/2})n\leq(1+\delta)n+10t^{1/2}n^{1/2},

such that

|A\cap P|\geq(1-(3t/n)^{1/2})n\geq n-2t^{1/2}n^{1/2},\ \ |B\cap Q|\geq n-2t^{1/2}n^{1/2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

The second corollary deals with popular sums; see
[
10
, Lemma 3.4]
and
[
12
, Theorem 2.4]
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be two sets of integers both with size
n
. Let
\eta
and
\delta
be positive numbers such that
11\eta^{1/2}+\delta<\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}
and
n>2\eta^{-1/2}
. Assume that

\#\left\{x\in A+B\colon r_{A+B}(x)\geq\frac{\eta n^{2}}{|A+B|}\right\}\leq(2+\delta)n.

Then there exist arithmetic progressions
P,Q
with the same common difference and sizes at most
(1+\delta+5\eta^{1/2})n
, such that
|A\cap P|\geq(1-\eta^{1/2})n
and
|B\cap Q|\geq(1-\eta^{1/2})n
.

Compared with
[
10
, Lemma 3.4]
which was deduced from
[
6
, Theorem 1]
, Corollary
5.2
is applicable to two distinct sets
A,B
and can be deduced from our Proposition
2.2
.

Proof.

In this proof, we write
r(x)
for
r_{A+B}(x)
, and let
K=\eta n^{2}/|A+B|
. Let
\Gamma\subset A\times B
be the set of
(a,b)\in A\times B
with
r(a+b)\geq K
. From the inequality

n^{2}=\sum_{x}r(x)=\sum_{x\colon r(x)<K}r(x)+\sum_{x\colon r(x)\geq K}r(x)\leq K|A+B|+|\Gamma|,

it follows that

|\Gamma|\geq n^{2}-K|A+B|=(1-\eta)n^{2}.

We will apply Theorem
1.1
with
\varepsilon=\eta
. Since
2+\delta<1+\theta-11\eta^{1/2}
by assumption, we can apply Theorem
1.1
to conclude that there are arithmetic progressions
P,Q
with the same common difference and sizes at most

(2+\delta)n-(1-5\eta^{1/2})n\leq(1+\delta+5\eta^{1/2})n,

such that

|A\cap P|\geq(1-\eta^{1/2})n,\ \ |B\cap Q|\geq(1-\eta^{1/2})n.

This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we deduce Theorem
1.3
from Theorem
1.1
. The plan is to divide
A
into its positive and negative parts, and apply Theorem
1.1
to each of these two parts (with appropriately chosen
\Gamma
). After that, we need to ensure that the arithmetic progressions containing these two parts can be glued together so that they contain
0
and are symmetric around
0
.

Lemma 6.1
.

If
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
|A|=n
, then
C(A)<1/2
.

Proof.

Write
A=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}<\cdots<a_{n}\}
. For each
i
, the sums
a_{i}+a_{1},\cdots,a_{i}+a_{n}
are all of size greater than
a_{i}
, and thus at most
n-i
of them can lie in
A
. It follows that

C(A)\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(n-i)<\frac{1}{2}.

∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,2^{-20})
. Let
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
be a set of
n\geq 2\varepsilon^{-1/2}
positive integers. If
C(A)\geq(1-\varepsilon)/2
, then there is an arithmetic progression
P
containing
0
with size at most
(1+45\varepsilon^{1/2})n
such that
|A\cap P|\geq(1-\varepsilon^{1/2})n
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma\subset A\times A
be the subset defined by

\Gamma=\{(a,a^{\prime})\in A\times A\colon|a-a^{\prime}|\in A\}.

Since
C(A)\geq(1-\varepsilon)/2
, the number of pairs
(a,a^{\prime})\in A\times A
with
a-a^{\prime}\in A
is at least
(1-\varepsilon)n^{2}/2
, and by symmetry there are also at least
(1-\varepsilon)n^{2}/2
pairs
(a,a^{\prime})\in A\times A
with
a^{\prime}-a\in A
. It follows that
|\Gamma|\geq(1-\varepsilon)n^{2}
. By the construction of
\Gamma
we have

|A-_{\Gamma}A|\leq 2n.

Since
\varepsilon<2^{-20}
, we have
2n\leq(1+\theta-11\varepsilon^{1/2})n
, where
\theta=\tfrac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2}
. Thus by Theorem
1.1
, there is an arithmetic progression
P
with

|P|\leq|A-_{\Gamma}A|-(1-5\varepsilon^{1/2})n\leq(1+5\varepsilon^{1/2})n,

such that
|A\cap P|\geq(1-\varepsilon^{1/2})n
. It remains to show that
P
can be extended to an arithmetic progression
\widetilde{P}
containing
0
, and that
|\widetilde{P}\setminus P|
is small.

Note that if
(a,a^{\prime})\in\Gamma
, then either
a\in A\setminus P
, or
a^{\prime}\in A\setminus P
, or
|a-a^{\prime}|\in A\setminus P
, or
(a,a^{\prime},|a-a^{\prime}|)\in P\times P\times P
. Hence

|\Gamma|\leq 4n|A\setminus P|+\#\{(a,a^{\prime})\in P\times P\colon|a-a^{\prime}|\in P\}.

Since
|\Gamma|\geq(1-\varepsilon)n^{2}
and
|A\setminus P|\leq\varepsilon^{1/2}n
, we have

\#\{(a,a^{\prime})\in P\times P\colon|a-a^{\prime}|\in P\}\geq(1-5\varepsilon^{1/2})n^{2}.

It follows that the elements of
P
must be in the residue class
0\pmod{d}
, where
d
is the common difference of
P
, since otherwise
(P-P)\cap P=\emptyset
. Furthermore, from
|P|\leq(1+5\varepsilon^{1/2})n
we get

\#\{(a,a^{\prime})\in P\times P\colon|a-a^{\prime}|\notin P\}\leq|P|^{2}-(1-5\varepsilon^{1/2})n^{2}\leq 40\varepsilon^{1/2}n|P|.

Let
md
be the smallest element in
P
, so that
P
can be extended to an arithmetic progression
\widetilde{P}
containing
0
, with
|\widetilde{P}|=|P|+m
. Then

\begin{split}\#\{(a,a^{\prime})\in P\times P\colon|a-a^{\prime}|\notin P\}&\geq\#\{(a,a^{\prime})\in P\times P\colon|a-a^{\prime}|<md\}\\
&\geq\min\left(m,|P|\right)|P|.\end{split}

Combining the upper and lower bounds above, we get
m\leq 40\varepsilon^{1/2}n
, and hence

|\widetilde{P}|=|P|+m\leq(1+5\varepsilon^{1/2})n+40\varepsilon^{1/2}n\leq(1+45\varepsilon^{1/2})n.

The proof is completed by replacing
P
by
\widetilde{P}
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
P_{1},P_{2}
be arithmetic progressions of the form

P_{1}=\{0,d_{1},2d_{1},\cdots,(\ell_{1}-1)d_{1}\},\ \ P_{2}=\{-(\ell_{2}-1)d_{2},\cdots,-2d_{2},-d_{2},0\}.

If
d_{1}\neq d_{2}
, then the number of pairs
(a_{1},a_{2})\in P_{1}\times P_{2}
with
a_{1}+a_{2}\notin P_{1}\cup P_{2}
is at least

\tfrac{1}{4}\min(\ell_{1}^{2},\ell_{2}^{2})-\tfrac{1}{2}(\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})^{2}-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}.

Proof.

By dividing all elements in
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
by
(d_{1},d_{2})
, we may assume that
(d_{1},d_{2})=1
. Consider the number of pairs
(a_{1},a_{2})\in P_{1}\times P_{2}
with
a_{1}+a_{2}\in P_{1}
. This is the same as the number of solutions to
x_{1}d_{1}-x_{2}d_{2}=y_{1}d_{1}
, where
0\leq x_{1},y_{1}<\ell_{1}
and
0\leq x_{2}<\ell_{2}
. We must have
x_{1}\equiv y_{1}\pmod{d_{2}}
. Since
x_{1}-y_{1}
must also be non-negative, the number of such solutions is at most

\sum_{y_{1}=0}^{\ell_{1}-1}\left(\frac{\ell_{1}-1-y}{d_{2}}+1\right)\leq\frac{\ell_{1}(\ell_{1}-1)}{2d_{2}}+\ell_{1}.

Similarly, the number of pairs
(a_{1},a_{2})\in P_{1}\times P_{2}
with
a_{1}+a_{2}\in P_{2}
is at most
\ell_{2}(\ell_{2}-1)/(2d_{1})+\ell_{2}
. It follows that the number of pairs
(a_{1},a_{2})\in P_{1}\times P_{2}
with
a_{1}+a_{2}\notin P_{1}\cup P_{2}
is at least

\ell_{1}\ell_{2}-\frac{\ell_{1}(\ell_{1}-1)}{2d_{2}}-\frac{\ell_{2}(\ell_{2}-1)}{2d_{1}}-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}.

The conclusion follows by noting that at least one of
d_{1},d_{2}
is at least
2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be the set of all positive and negative elements in
A
, respectively. Let
n_{1}=|A_{1}|
and
n_{2}=|A_{2}|
. Then
n_{1}+n_{2}\geq n-1
. Let
X_{i}
(
i\in\{1,2\}
) be the number of pairs
(a,a^{\prime})\in A_{i}\times A_{i}
with
a+a^{\prime}\notin A_{i}
. By Lemma
6.1
applied to
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, we have
X_{i}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}n_{i}^{2}
. On the other hand, by assumption we have

X_{1}+X_{2}\leq\left(\tfrac{1}{4}+\varepsilon\right)n^{2}.

It follows that

\left(\tfrac{1}{4}+\varepsilon\right)n^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2})\geq\tfrac{1}{4}(n-1)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}(n_{1}-n_{2})^{2},

which implies that

(n_{1}-n_{2})^{2}\leq 4\varepsilon n^{2}+2n\leq 6\varepsilon n^{2}

since
n\geq\varepsilon^{-1}
. Hence
|n_{1}-n_{2}|\leq 3\varepsilon^{1/2}n
, and thus both
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
lie in the range
[n/2-2\varepsilon^{1/2}n,n/2+2\varepsilon^{1/2}n]
. Furthermore, we have

\begin{split}X_{1}&\leq\left(\tfrac{1}{4}+\varepsilon\right)n^{2}-X_{2}\leq\left(\tfrac{1}{4}+\varepsilon\right)n^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}n_{2}^{2}\\
&\leq\left(\tfrac{1}{4}+\varepsilon\right)n^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\tfrac{n}{2}-2\varepsilon^{1/2}n\right)^{2}\leq\left(\tfrac{1}{8}+2\varepsilon^{1/2}\right)n^{2}\\
&\leq\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+16\varepsilon^{1/2}\right)n_{1}^{2},\end{split}

and similarly we have

X_{2}\leq\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+16\varepsilon^{1/2}\right)n_{2}^{2}.

Since
\varepsilon<2^{-50}
, we may apply Lemma
6.2
to
A_{1}
and
-A_{2}
to find arithmetic progressions
P_{1},P_{2}
containing
0
, such that

|P_{i}|\leq(1+270\varepsilon^{1/4})n_{i},\ \ |A_{i}\cap P_{i}|\geq(1-6\varepsilon^{1/4})n_{i}.

Let
d_{1},d_{2}
be the common differences of
P_{1},P_{2}
, respectively. Consider

T=\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in A_{1}\times A_{2}\colon a_{1}+a_{2}\notin A\}

and

T^{\prime}=\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in P_{1}\times P_{2}\colon a_{1}+a_{2}\notin P_{1}\cup P_{2}\}.

If
(a_{1},a_{2})\in T^{\prime}
, then either
(a_{1},a_{2})\in T
, or
a_{1}\in P_{1}\setminus A_{1}
, or
a_{2}\in P_{2}\setminus A_{2}
, or
a_{1}+a_{2}\in A\setminus(P_{1}\cup P_{2})
.

\begin{split}|T^{\prime}|&\leq|T|+|P_{1}\setminus A_{1}||P_{2}|+|P_{2}\setminus A_{2}||P_{1}|+|A\setminus(P_{1}\cup P_{2})|\min(|P_{1}|,|P_{2}|)\\
&\leq|T|+276\varepsilon^{1/4}n_{1}\cdot 2n_{2}+276\varepsilon^{1/4}n_{2}\cdot 2n_{1}+6\varepsilon^{1/4}n\cdot 2\min(n_{1},n_{2})\\
&\leq|T|+282\varepsilon^{1/4}n^{2}.\end{split}

From the assumption
C(A)\geq 3/4-\varepsilon
and the facts that
C(A_{1})\leq 1/2
and
C(A_{2})\leq 1/2
, it follows that

|T|\leq\left(\tfrac{1}{4}+\varepsilon\right)n^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}n_{1}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}n_{2}^{2}\leq\varepsilon n^{2}+\tfrac{n}{2}\leq 2\varepsilon n^{2}.

Hence
|T^{\prime}|\leq 284\varepsilon^{1/4}n^{2}
. On the other hand, if
d_{1}\neq d_{2}
then we can apply Lemma
6.3
to get

|T^{\prime}|\geq\tfrac{1}{4}\min(|P_{1}|^{2},|P_{2}|^{2})-\tfrac{1}{2}(|P_{1}|-|P_{2}|)^{2}-|P_{1}|-|P_{2}|.

Using the bounds

|P_{i}|\leq(1+270\varepsilon^{1/4})n_{i}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}(1+280\varepsilon^{1/4})n\leq n

and

|P_{i}|\geq(1-6\varepsilon^{1/4})n_{i}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(1-10\varepsilon^{1/4})n\geq\tfrac{1}{3}n,

we conclude that

|T^{\prime}|\geq\tfrac{1}{36}n^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}(290\varepsilon^{1/4}n)^{2}-2n\geq\tfrac{1}{40}n^{2}.

This is a contradiction to the previously obtained upper bound
|T^{\prime}|\leq 284\varepsilon^{1/4}n^{2}
, and hence we must have
d_{1}=d_{2}
. The conclusion follows by taking
P
to be the union
P_{1}\cup P_{2}
,
so that

|P|\leq 2\max(|P_{1}|,|P_{2}|)\leq 2(1+270\varepsilon^{1/4})\max(n_{1},n_{2})\leq(1+280\varepsilon^{1/4})n

and

|A\cap P|=|A_{1}\cap P_{1}|+|A_{2}\cap P_{2}|\geq(1-6\varepsilon^{1/4})(n_{1}+n_{2})\geq(1-10\varepsilon^{1/4})n.

∎

7.
A continuous analogue of Theorem
1.3

For a measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
with finite Lebesgue measure
\lambda(A)
, we study the following quantity related to
C(A)
:

\langle 1_{A}*1_{A},1_{A}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}1_{A}(y)1_{A}(x-y)1_{A}(x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

Theorem 7.1

( Riesz-Sobolev Inequality)
.

Let
f,g,h
be three nonnegative functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then with

I(f,g,h)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y)g(x-y)h(x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

we have
I(f,g,h)\leq I(f^{*},g^{*},h^{*})
, where
f^{*},g^{*},h^{*}
are the corresponding symmetric - decreasing rearangement of functions
f,g,h
.

One can find the proof in
[
9
, Chapter 3]
.
To apply the Riez- Sobolev inequality, we notice that
A^{*}
, the symmetric rearangement of the set
A
in our case is
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, the interval centered at the origin with
\lambda(I)=\lambda(A)
. Thus,

(7.1)

\langle 1_{A}*1_{A},1_{A}\rangle\leq\langle 1_{I}*1_{I},1_{I}\rangle=\tfrac{3}{4}\lambda(A)^{2}.

Using Theorem
1.3
, we may deduce the following structure theorem for sets
A
when the inequality (
7.1
) is almost sharp.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}
be a measurable set with finite Lebesgue measure, and let
\varepsilon\in(0,2^{-100})
. Assume that

\langle 1_{A}*1_{A},1_{A}\rangle\geq(1-\varepsilon)\langle 1_{I}*1_{I},1_{I}\rangle,

where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is the interval centered at the origin with
\lambda(I)=\lambda(A)
. Then there exists an interval
J\subset\mathbb{R}
centred at the origin such that
\lambda(J)\leq(1+561\varepsilon^{1/4})\lambda(A)
and
\lambda(A\cap J)\geq(1-21\varepsilon^{1/4})\lambda(A)
.

A more general result concerning
\langle 1_{A}*1_{B},1_{C}\rangle
for
A,B,C\subset\mathbb{R}
was established in
[
1
,
2
]
, and even more generally in
[
3
,
4
]
for
A,B,C\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

We construct the following discrete version
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}
of
A
in the following standard way (c.f.
[
1
, Section 6]
). Let
\eta,\delta>0
be sufficiently small in terms of
\varepsilon
and
\lambda(A)
. For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
I_{n}
be the interval
(\eta n,\eta(n+1))
. Let

\mathcal{A}=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\colon\lambda(A\cap I_{n})\geq(1-\delta)\lambda(I_{n})\}.

Let
\widetilde{A}=\cup_{n\in\mathcal{A}}I_{n}
, so that
\lambda(\widetilde{A})=\eta|\mathcal{A}|
. By the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, we have
|A\triangle\widetilde{A}|\rightarrow 0
as
\max(\eta,\delta)\rightarrow 0
. Thus by taking
\eta,\delta
small enough, we may assume that

\eta^{-1}\left(1-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{100}\right)\lambda(A)\leq|\mathcal{A}|\leq\eta^{-1}\left(1+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{100}\right)\lambda(A),

and that

\langle 1_{\widetilde{A}}*1_{\widetilde{A}},1_{\widetilde{A}}\rangle\geq\langle 1_{A}*1_{A},1_{A}\rangle-\tfrac{1}{100}\varepsilon\lambda(A)^{2}\geq\left(\tfrac{3}{4}-\tfrac{4\varepsilon}{5}\right)\lambda(A)^{2}.

Now we connect
\langle 1_{\widetilde{A}}*1_{\widetilde{A}},1_{\widetilde{A}}\rangle
with
C(A)
. Note that

\langle 1_{\widetilde{A}}*1_{\widetilde{A}},1_{\widetilde{A}}\rangle=\sum_{a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}}\langle 1_{I_{a}}*1_{I_{b}},1_{I_{c}}\rangle.

The summand above is nonzero only when
a+b=c
or
a+b=c+1
, in which case it has value
\eta^{2}/2
. Hence

\langle 1_{\widetilde{A}}*1_{\widetilde{A}},1_{\widetilde{A}}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}\eta^{2}\left(\sum_{a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}}1_{a+b=c}+\sum_{a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}}1_{a+b=c+1}\right).

It follows that

\sum_{a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}}1_{a+b=c}+\sum_{a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}}1_{a+b=c+1}\geq 2\eta^{-2}\left(\tfrac{3}{4}-\tfrac{4\varepsilon}{5}\right)\lambda(A)^{2}\geq\left(\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{8\varepsilon}{5}\right)|\mathcal{A}|^{2}.

The first sum above is the number of summing triples in
\mathcal{A}
, and the second sum above is the number of summing triples in
\mathcal{A}-1=\{n-1\colon n\in\mathcal{A}\}
. Both of these two sums are bounded above by
\tfrac{3}{4}|\mathcal{A}|^{2}+O(|\mathcal{A}|)
, and thus they are also both bounded below by

\left(\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{8\varepsilon}{5}\right)|\mathcal{A}|^{2}-\tfrac{3}{4}|\mathcal{A}|^{2}-O(|\mathcal{A}|)\geq\left(\tfrac{3}{4}-2\varepsilon\right)|\mathcal{A}|^{2}.

Hence
C(\mathcal{A})\geq 3/4-2\varepsilon
and
C(\mathcal{A}-1)\geq 3/4-2\varepsilon
.

By Theorem
1.3
, there exist arithmetic progressions
P,P^{\prime}
centred at the origin such that

|P|\leq(1+560\varepsilon^{1/4})|\mathcal{A}|,\ \ |P\cap\mathcal{A}|\geq(1-20\varepsilon^{1/4})|\mathcal{A}|,

and

|P^{\prime}|\leq(1+560\varepsilon^{1/4})|\mathcal{A}|,\ \ |P^{\prime}\cap(\mathcal{A}-1)|\geq(1-20\varepsilon^{1/4})|\mathcal{A}|.

Since

\begin{split}|P\cap(P^{\prime}+1)|&\geq|P\cap\mathcal{A}|-|\mathcal{A}\setminus(P^{\prime}+1)|\\
&\geq(1-20\varepsilon^{1/4})|\mathcal{A}|-20\varepsilon^{1/4}|\mathcal{A}|\geq 0.99|\mathcal{A}|\geq 0.9|P|,\end{split}

the common difference of
P
and
P^{\prime}
must be
1
. Let
J
be the smallest interval centred at the origin containing
I_{n}
for all
n\in P
, so that

\lambda(J)\leq\eta(|P|+1)\leq(1+560\varepsilon^{1/4})\eta|\mathcal{A}|+\eta\leq(1+561\varepsilon^{1/4})\lambda(A).

Moreover, we have

\lambda(A\cap J)\geq\sum_{n\in\mathcal{A}\cap P}\lambda(A\cap I_{n}).

Each summand above is at least
(1-\delta)\lambda(I_{n})=(1-\delta)\eta
by the definition of
\mathcal{A}
. Hence

\lambda(A\cap J)\geq(1-\delta)\eta|\mathcal{A}\cap P|\geq(1-\delta)(1-20\varepsilon^{1/4})\eta|\mathcal{A}|\geq(1-21\varepsilon^{1/4})\lambda(A).

This completes the proof.

∎

SCHATTEN CLASSES AND COMMUTATOR IN THE TWO WEIGHT SETTING, I. HILBERT TRANSFORM
Michael Lacey, Ji Li, Brett D. Wick

Abstract
. We characterize the Hilbert–Schmidt class membership of commutator with the Hilbert transform in the two weight setting. The characterization depends upon the symbol of the commutator being in a new weighted Besov space. This follows from a Schatten class S p S_{p} result for dyadic paraproducts, where 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty . We discuss the difficulties in extending the dyadic result to the full range of Schatten classes for the Hilbert transform.

1.
Introduction and Statement of Main Results

Consider the commutator with Hilbert transform
H
defined as follows.

[b,H](f)(x):=b(x)H(f)(x)-H(bf)(x).

In this paper, we are focused on the two weight setting, given as follows.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
,
\mu,\lambda
in the Muckenhoupt weight class
A_{p}
, and
\nu=\lambda^{-{1\over p}}\mu^{1\over p}
.
Bloom
[
2
]
showed that
[b,H]
is bounded from
L^{p}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{p}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})

if and only if
b
is in
{\rm BMO}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R})
.
Just recently, the first and second authors
[
6
]
proved that
[b,H]
is compact from
L^{p}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{p}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})

if and only if
b
is in
{\rm VMO}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R})
.

We characterize the Hilbert–Schmidt property of the commutator above in the two weight setting. Key to this is a the Schatten class membership of the dyadic variants of the commutator.

The two weight case introduces difficulties not present in the unweighted case. Our characterizations depend upon new weighted Besov spaces.
For the dyadic case, we find a convenient characterization for the dyadic commutators to be in any Schatten class for
1<p<\infty
.
Those classes
S^{p}
are defined
in §
2
. See (
2.1
).
But for the continuous case, we have to content ourselves with just the case
p=2
,
that is the Hilbert-Schmidt case.
We state our results here.
Membership of commutators in Schatten classes is characterized by the symbol of the commutator being in Besov spaces. We introduce a new type of weighted Besov spaces with respect to the two weights
\mu
and
\lambda
as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose
\mu,\lambda\in A_{2}
and set
\nu=\mu^{1\over 2}\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}
. Let
b\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then we say that
b
belongs to the weight Besov space
B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
if

\|b\|_{B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\big|b(x)-b(y)\big|^{2}\over|x-y|^{2}}\lambda(x)\mu^{-1}(y)dydx\Bigg)^{1\over 2}<\infty.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\mu,\lambda\in A_{2}
and set
\nu=\mu^{1\over 2}\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}
.
Suppose
b\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then commutator
[b,H]
belongs to
S^{2}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))
if and only if

b\in B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, in this case we have

\|b\|_{B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\approx\|[b,H]\|_{S^{2}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}.

The key new tools to prove this result are

(1)

For sufficiency, the characterisation of dyadic paraproducts
\Pi_{b}
as we establish in Theorem
1.4
. This characterization is
in terms of the dyadic Besov spaces, which contain the continuous version.

(2)

The intersection of shifted dyadic weighted Besov spaces is the continuous weighted Besov space, mimicking a well known property of BMO space. This addresses sufficiency in the continuous case.

(3)

For necessity in the continuous case, the equivalent characterisations of the dyadic weighted Besov norm and the decomposition of dyadic interval via median value.

We emphasize that this strategy also depends upon the Hilbert space structure. The ‘obvious’ generalization to
L^{p}
is problematic. We return to this point in the concluding section of the paper.

We turn to the dyadic setting.
Throughout,
\mathcal{D}
is a choice of a dyadic system or grid in
\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\{h_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{D}\}
be the Haar functions associated to
\mathcal{D}
.
For function, or symbol,
b
, let
\widehat{b}(I)=\langle b,h_{I}\rangle
be the
Haar coefficients of
b
. The Besov spaces are then given by this definition.

Definition 1.3
.

Suppose
\nu\in A_{2}
,
0<p<\infty
. Let
b\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
\mathcal{D}
be an arbitrary dyadic interval system in
\mathbb{R}
. Then we say that
b
belongs to the weight dyadic Besov space
B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D})=B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
if

\displaystyle{\|b\|_{B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}:=\Bigg(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\bigg({|\widehat{b}(I)|\,|I|^{1\over 2}\over{\nu(I)}}\bigg)^{p}\Bigg)^{1\over p}<\infty.}

The paraproduct operator with symbol
b
is defined to be

(1.1)

\Pi_{b}\equiv\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\widehat{b}(I)h_{I}\otimes\frac{\mathsf{1}_{I}}{\left|I\right|}.

A Haar multiplier is defined in terms of an arbitrary function
\epsilon\colon\mathcal{D}\to\{-1,1\}
.

(1.2)

T_{\epsilon}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\epsilon_{I}\widehat{f}(I)h_{I}.

The main result in the dyadic case holds for all Schatten classes
1<p<\infty
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
,
\mu,\lambda\in A_{2}
and set
\nu=\mu^{1\over 2}\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}
.
Suppose
b\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})
.

(1)

\Pi_{b}
is in
S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))
if and only if
b
is in
B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover,

\|\Pi_{b}\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}\approx\|b\|_{B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

(2)
If
b
is in
B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
then
[b,T_{\epsilon}]
is in
S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))
. Conversely, suppose the coefficients
\{\epsilon_{I}\}_{I\in\mathcal{D}}
satisfies the following non-degenerate condition: for each dyadic interval
Q\in\mathcal{D}
, there is another dyadic interval
\hat{Q}\in\mathcal{D}
with
|Q|=|\hat{Q}|
and
{\rm dist}(Q,\hat{Q})\leq|Q|
, such that there exists
I_{0}\in\mathcal{D}
satisfying
Q,\hat{Q}\subset I_{0}
,
|I_{0}|\leq C|Q|
, and

\bigg|\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D},I_{0}\subset I}\epsilon_{I}h_{I}(x)h_{I}(y)\bigg|\geq{C_{0}\over|I_{0}|},

where
C
and
C_{0}
are absolute positive constants. Then, if
[b,T_{\epsilon}]
is in
S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))
, we deduce that
b
is in
B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.

We now establish the dyadic structure for the weighted Besov spaces, in the case
p=2
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose
\mu,\lambda\in A_{2}
and set
\nu=\mu^{1\over 2}\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}
. There are two dyadic systems
\mathcal{D}^{0}
and
\mathcal{D}^{1}
for which we have

B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})=B_{\nu,d}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D}^{0})\cap B_{\nu,d}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D}^{1})
with

\|b\|_{B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\approx\|b\|_{B_{\nu,d}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D}^{0})}+\|b\|_{B_{\nu,d}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D}^{1})}.

There is no mystery as to the selection of the two dyadic grids. The ‘one-third’ shift suffices. See for example
[
7
]
.

In the unweighted case, these results are classical in nature.
The case of the Hilbert transform commutator is equivalent to Schatten class membership for Hankel operators. This was characterized in terms of membership of the symbol in a Besov space by Peller
[
1
,
8
]
.
The characterization holds for all
0<p<\infty
.
The higher dimensional case

, was studied by Janson and Wolff

]
.
Interestingly, for the Riesz transform commutators, one has membership in

for

Due to our difficulties in the continuous case,

The higher dimensional case was further studied Rochberg
[
12
]
, and
Rochberg–Semmes
[
10
,

]
. We will rely upon their innovations.

We will collect known results in §

, including Bloom’s characterization and the Rochberg–Semmes notion of

The next section

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect the necessary background for the theorems to be proved.

2.1.
Schatten Classes

For two separable Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
over
\mathbb{C}
, we let

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H})
be the space of bounded linear operators from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{H}
, and
\mathcal{B}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H})
the space of compact operators. Suppose

T\in\mathcal{B}_{0}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H})
. Then
|T|:=(T^{*}T)^{1\over 2}
is compact and positive.
The eigenvalues of
|T|
, which are non-negative, are called the singular values of
T
. Singular values are listed in decreasing order
according to multiplicity by
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\cdots\geq 0
. For
0<p<\infty
,
the Schatten class
S^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H})
consists of those compact operators
T
whose sequence of singular values (counted according to multiplicity and arranged in decreasing order) belong to
\ell^{p}
. In that case, we write

(2.1)

\lVert T\rVert_{S^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H})}^{p}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\sigma_{j}^{p}.

In their innovative approach to singular values, Rochberg and Semmes
[
11
]
introduced a notion of
nearly weakly orthogonal
(NWO) sequences of functions.
This is an important tool for us, so we recall facts here. We apply the notion in the unweighted section.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and
\mathcal{D}
be a dyadic system, a collection of functions
\{e_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{D}\}
is
NWO
if
each
e_{I}
is supported on
I
, and the maximal function below is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})

(2.2)

\sup_{I}\chi_{I}\frac{\lvert\langle f,e_{I}\rangle\rvert}{\lvert I\rvert^{1/2}}.

An elementary example of NWO sequence is any sequence of functions satisfying

\lvert e_{I}\rvert\lesssim\lvert I\rvert^{-1/2}\chi_{I}
.
Since we work with weights, the NWO sequences we meet will not satisfy this criteria.
Instead, we will have need of this fact.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
11
]
*§7D)
.

If the collection of functions
\{e_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{D}\}
are supported on
I
and satisfy for some
2<r<\infty
,

\lVert e_{I}\rVert_{r}\lesssim\lvert I\rvert^{1/r-1/2}
, then
\{e_{I}\}
is NWO.

The concept of NWO is related to Schatten norms by way of this key inequality. For any
bounded compact operator
A
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
:

(2.3)

\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\lvert\langle Ae_{I},f_{I}\rangle\rvert^{p}\lesssim\lVert A\rVert_{S^{p}}^{p},

where
\{e_{I}\}_{I\in\mathcal{D}}
and
\{f_{I}\}_{I\in\mathcal{D}}
are NWO. This inequality can be found in
[
11
]
*(1.10), p. 242.

2.2.
A_{2}
weights

Definition 2.3
.

Let
w(x)
be a nonnegative locally integrable function
on
\mathbb{R}
. We
say
w
is an
A_{2}

weight
, written
w\in A_{2}
, if

[w]_{A_{2}}\coloneqq\sup_{B}{1\over|B|}\int_{B}w(x)dx\cdot{1\over|B|}\int_{B}w(x)^{-1}dx<\infty.

Here the suprema are taken over all intervals
B\subset\mathbb{R}
.
The quantity
[w]_{A_{2}}
is called the
A_{2}
constant
of
w
.

It is well known that
A_{2}
weights are doubling. Namely,

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
w\in A_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then
for every
\lambda>1
and for every interval
B\subset\mathbb{R}
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle w(\lambda B)\lesssim\lambda\,w(B).

See for example
[
4
]
*Lemma 9.2.1.

2.3.
Weight BMO and VMO spaces

We recall the weighted BMO space and VMO space and their dyadic versions.

Definition 2.5
.

Suppose
w\in A_{\infty}
.
A function
b\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})
belongs to

{BMO}_{w}(\mathbb{R})
if

\|b\|_{{BMO}_{w}(\mathbb{R})}:=\sup_{B}{1\over w(B)}\int_{B}|b(x)-b_{B}|\,dx<\infty,

where
b_{B}:={1\over|B|}\int_{B}b(x)dx
and the supremum is taken over all balls
B\subset\mathbb{R}
. The dyadic weighted BMO space
{BMO}_{w,d}(\mathbb{R})
consists of functions
b\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})

such that
\|b\|_{{BMO}_{w,d}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty
, where the
\|b\|_{{BMO}_{w,d}(\mathbb{R})}
is defined the same as above with intervals replaced by dyadic cubes.

Definition 2.6
.

Suppose
w\in A_{\infty}
.
A function
b\in{BMO}_{w}(\mathbb{R})
belongs to

{VMO}_{w}(\mathbb{R})
if

\displaystyle{\rm(i)}\ \lim_{a\to 0}\sup_{B:\ r_{B}=a}{1\over w(B)}\int_{B}|b(x)-b_{B}|\,dx=0,

\displaystyle{\rm(ii)}\ \lim_{a\to\infty}\sup_{B:\ r_{B}=a}{1\over w(B)}\int_{B}|b(x)-b_{B}|\,dx=0,

\displaystyle{\rm(iii)}\ \lim_{a\to\infty}\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}\backslash B(x_{0},a)}{1\over w(B)}\int_{B}|b(x)-b_{B}|\,dx=0,

where
x_{0}
is any fixed point in
\mathbb{R}
. A function
b\in{BMO}_{w,d}(\mathbb{R})
belongs to the dyadic weighted VMO space

{VMO}_{w,d}(\mathbb{R})
if the above three limits hold with intervals replaced by dyadic cubes.

2.4.
Operator Theory Lemma

We provide a simple lemma that allows us to study Schatten norms on unweighted
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose
1\leq p<\infty
and
\mu,\lambda\in A_{2}
.
Then
T
is in
S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))
if and only if

\lambda^{1\over 2}T\mu^{-{1\over 2}}
is in
S^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
. Moreover, we have

\|T\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}=\|\lambda^{1\over 2}T\mu^{-{1\over 2}}\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}.

Proof.

We first point out that
T
is bounded from
L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if

\lambda^{1\over 2}T\mu^{-{1\over 2}}
is bounded from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover,
T
is compact from
L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if
\lambda^{1\over 2}T\mu^{-{1\over 2}}
is compact from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The case of Schatten membership is equally straightforward.

To show the Schatten class estimate, we treat
\lambda^{1/2}
as a multiplication operator, that is,

M_{\lambda^{1\over 2}}f(x)=\lambda^{1\over 2}(x)f(x),\quad\forall f\in L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}).

Then we see that

M_{\lambda^{1\over 2}}
is a unitary operator from
L_{\lambda}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Similarly,
M_{\mu^{-{1\over 2}}}
is a unitary operator from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to
L_{\mu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Note that unitary operators preserve
S^{p}
classes for
1\leq p<\infty
, we obtain our argument.
∎

3.
Bloom type Besov spaces and dyadic structure

3.1.
Equivalent norms on the dyadic weighted Besov spaces

Similarly to the weighted
BMO
spaces, there are equivalent norms for the
dyadic
Besov spaces defined in
Definition
1.3
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
\mu,\lambda\in A_{2}
and set
\nu=\mu^{1\over 2}\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}
. Then

\displaystyle\|b\|_{B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{p}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\Big|\widehat{b}(I){\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}(\mu^{-{1}}(I))^{{1\over 2}}\over|I|^{{3\over 2}}}\Big|^{p}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\Big|\widehat{b}(I){|I|^{1\over 2}\over{\lambda^{-1}(I)^{1\over 2}(\mu(I))^{{1\over 2}}}}\Big|^{p}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\bigg({|\widehat{b}(I)|\,|I|^{1\over 2}\over{\nu(I)}}\bigg)^{p},

where the implicit constants depend only on
[\mu]_{A_{2}}
,
[\lambda]_{A_{2}}
and
[\nu]_{A_{2}}
.

Proof.

This depends upon elementary properties of
A_{2}
weights. In view of Definition
1.3
, it clearly suffices to argue that
for any dyadic interval
I
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\lvert I\rvert}{\nu(I)}

\displaystyle\approx{\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}(\mu^{-{1}}(I))^{{1\over 2}}\over|I|}\approx{|I|\over{\lambda^{-1}(I)^{1\over 2}(\mu(I))^{{1\over 2}}}}.

But, the definition of the
\sigma\in A_{2}
is

{\sigma(I)\over|I|}{\sigma^{-1}(I)\over|I|}\approx 1.

It follows that

(3.6)

{\lambda(I)\mu^{-{1}}(I)\over|I|\cdot\lvert I\rvert}\approx{|I|\cdot\lvert I\rvert\over{\lambda^{-1}(I)\mu(I)}}.

This is the rightmost equivalence in (
3.5
).

Recalling the definition of
\nu
and using Cauchy–Schwarz, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{\lvert I\rvert^{2}}{\nu(I)}

\displaystyle\approx\nu^{-1}(I)

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\nu^{-1}(x)dx=\int_{I}\mu^{-{1\over 2}}(x)\lambda^{{1\over 2}}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\mu^{-1}(I)^{1\over 2}\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}.

And, again by Cauchy-Schwarz,

\displaystyle{|I|\over{\lambda^{-1}(I)^{1\over 2}(\mu(I))^{{1\over 2}}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\lvert I\rvert}{v(I)},

which completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Dyadic structure of the weighted Besov space
B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})

In this subsection we will prove Theorem
1.5
, namely that the continuous Besov space is the intersection of well chosen dyadic Besov spaces.
There are two halves. In the first half, the continuous Besov space
B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is contained a dyadic Besov space
B_{\nu,d}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
for any choice of dyadic system
\mathcal{D}
. To be more explicit, we have the following.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose
\mu,\lambda\in A_{2}
and set
\nu=\mu^{1\over 2}\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}
.
Then we have
B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})\subset B_{\nu,d}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, where
B_{\nu,d}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is the dyadic weighted Besov space associated with an arbitrary dyadic system
{\mathcal{D}}\subset\mathbb{R}
and we have

\|b\|_{B_{\nu,d}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|b\|_{B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

with the implicit constants depending only on
[\mu]_{A_{2}}
and
[\lambda]_{A_{2}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to see that the dyadic Besov norm is dominated by the continuous Besov norm. The key inequalities here are specific to a choice of dyadic interval
I
.
Below, let
I^{\prime}=I+2\lvert I\rvert
. We will freely use the
A_{2}
property.

\displaystyle{\big|\int_{I}b(x)h_{I}(x)dx\big|\,|I|^{1\over 2}\over{\nu(I)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg|\int_{I}b(x)h_{I}(x)dx\bigg|\,{\nu^{-1}(I)\over|I|^{3\over 2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{y\in I^{\prime}}\bigg|\int_{I}\Big(b(x)-b(y)\Big)h_{I}(x)dx\bigg|\,{\nu^{-1}(I^{\prime})\over|I|^{3\over 2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I^{\prime}}\bigg|\int_{I}\Big(b(x)-b(y)\Big)h_{I}(x)dx\bigg|\,\frac{\nu^{-1}(y)}{|I|^{3\over 2}}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I^{\prime}}\int_{I}\Big|b(x)-b(y)\Big||h_{I}(x)|\lambda^{1\over 2}(x)\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}(x)dx\,\lambda^{1\over 2}(y)\mu^{-{1\over 2}}(y)dy\ {1\over|I|^{3\over 2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{I^{\prime}}\int_{I}\big|b(x)-b(y)\big|^{2}\lambda(x)\mu^{-1}(y)dydx\times\int_{I^{\prime}}\int_{I}|h_{I}(x)|^{2}\lambda^{-1}(x)\lambda(y)dydx\bigg)^{1\over 2}\,\ {1\over|I|^{3\over 2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{I^{\prime}}\int_{I}{\big|b(x)-b(y)\big|^{2}\over|x-y|^{2}}\lambda(x)\mu^{-1}(y)dydx\ {\lambda(I)\lambda^{-1}(I)\over|I|^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{I^{\prime}}\int_{I}{\big|b(x)-b(y)\big|^{2}\over|x-y|^{2}}\lambda(x)\mu^{-1}(y)dydx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Square this, and sum over
I\in\mathcal{D}
, to get an expression dominated by
\lVert b\rVert_{B^{2}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}
.
The proof of Proposition
3.2
is complete.
∎

The second half of the proof is to show that the continuous Besov space is in the intersection of two well chosen dyadic Besov spaces.
The dependence of the dyadic Besov space on the choice of dyadic system is supressed in the notation. For the purposes
of this next proposition, we use the notation
B^{2}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D})
for the dyadic Besov space associated with dyadic system
\mathcal{D}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose
\mu,\lambda\in A_{2}
and set
\nu=\mu^{1\over 2}\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}
.
There are two choices of grids
\mathcal{D}^{0}
and
\mathcal{D}^{1}
for which we have

B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D}^{0})\cap B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D}^{1})=B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Moreover, we have

\|b\|_{B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\approx\|b\|_{B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D}^{0})}+\|b\|_{B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D}^{1})}.

Proof.

We take two dyadic grids
\mathcal{D}^{0}
and
\mathcal{D}^{1}
so that for all intervals
I
there is a
Q\in\mathcal{D}^{0}\cup\mathcal{D}^{1}
with

(3.7)

I\subset Q\subset 4I.

One option is that
\mathcal{D}^{0}
is the standard dyadic system in
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{D}^{1}
is the ‘’one-third shift’
of
\mathcal{D}^{0}
, see for example
[
7
]
.

By Proposition
3.2
, it suffices to show that the continuous Besov norm is dominated by the larger of the two dyadic Besov space norms. Below,
\mathcal{D}
is the standard dyadic grid in
\mathbb{R}
.
Also,
a,n\in\mathbb{N}
are integers that we will specify. Observe that:

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|b\|_{B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\big|b(x)-b(y)\big|^{2}\over|x-y|^{2}}\lambda(x)\mu^{-1}(y)dydx

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{I}\int_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}:2^{a}\lvert I\rvert<|x-y|\leq 2^{a+1}\lvert I\rvert}{\big|b(x)-b(y)\big|^{2}\over|x-y|^{2}}\lambda(x)\mu^{-1}(y)dydx

(3.10)

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\lvert I\rvert^{-2}\int_{I}\int_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}:2^{a}\lvert I\rvert<|x-y|\leq 2^{a+1}\lvert I\rvert}{\big|b(x)-b(y)\big|^{2}}\lambda(x)\mu^{-1}(y)dydx

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\lvert I\rvert^{-2}\sum_{m=n}^{2n-1}\int_{I}\int_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}:2^{a}(m/n)\lvert I\rvert<|x-y|\leq 2^{a}((m+1)/n)\lvert I\rvert}{\big|b(x)-b(y)\big|^{2}}\lambda(x)\mu^{-1}(y)dydx

The second integral above is over a symmetric interval. Consider the two intervals

(3.12)

I,\qquad I+2^{a}\lvert I\rvert[m/n,(m+1)/n],\qquad n\leq m<2n.

Now, we choose

, and

, so the second interval is smaller in length, but still comparable to

in length. And, they are separated by a distance approximately

2^{a}\lvert I\rvert

I

I^{\prime}
, and
I+2^{a}\lvert I\rvert[m/n,(m+1)/n]
the right half.
We can argue similarly for
I-2^{a}\lvert I\rvert[m/n,(m+1)/n]
. Below we continue with
I
and

.
In particular, for the main term in (

Above, we choose

.

It follows that the norm

3.3.
Comparison of

and give equivalent norms for it.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that

Proof.

We first prove that

\displaystyle\|b\|_{{BMO}_{\nu,d}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\approx\sup_{K\in\mathcal{D}}{1\over\mu^{-1}(K)}\sum_{I\subset K}|\widehat{b}(I)|^{2}{\mu^{-1}(I)^{2}\over|I|^{2}}{\lambda(I)\over|I|}.

, we have

\displaystyle{1\over\mu^{-1}(K)}\sum_{I\subset K}|\widehat{b}(I)|^{2}{\mu^{-1}(I)^{2}\over|I|^{2}}{\lambda(I)\over|I|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{I\subset K}|\widehat{b}(I)|^{2}{\mu^{-1}(I)\over|I|^{2}}{\lambda(I)\over|I|}\lesssim\|b\|_{B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{\nu,d}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\lesssim\|b\|_{B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}
. Hence, we see that

where

. Hence,

.
∎

4.
Schatten classes and paraproducts

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be an arbitrary dyadic system in
\mathbb{R}
.
Recall that
\Pi_{b}\equiv\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\widehat{b}(I)h_{I}\otimes\frac{\mathsf{1}_{I}}{\left|I\right|}
is the ‘paraproduct’ operator with symbol function
b
. The adjoint of
\Pi_{b}
on
unweighted

L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is defined by

\Pi_{b}^{\ast}\equiv\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\widehat{b}(I)\frac{\mathsf{1}_{I}}{\left|I\right|}\otimes h_{I}
,
where
\widehat{b}(I)=\langle b,h_{I}\rangle
. Moreover, we see that for
\mu,\lambda\in A_{2}
,

(4.1)

\lVert\Pi_{b}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})}=\lVert\Pi_{b}^{\ast}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\lambda^{-1}}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{2}_{\mu^{-1}}(\mathbb{R})}.

We have the following characterization of the Schatten membership of the paraproduct operators.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
,
\mu,\lambda\in A_{2}
and set
\nu=\mu^{1\over 2}\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}
.
Suppose
b\in{\rm VMO}_{\nu,d}(\mathbb{R})
. Then we have
\Pi_{b}
is in
S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))
if and only if
b
is in
B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover,

\|\Pi_{b}\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}\approx\|b\|_{B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

Proof.

Sufficiency:

Suppose
b\in B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
. By definition we see that

\displaystyle(\lambda^{1\over 2}\Pi_{b}\mu^{-{1\over 2}})(f)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\widehat{b}(I)\lambda^{1\over 2}(x)h_{I}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathsf{1}_{I}(y)}{\left|I\right|}f(y)\,\mu^{-{1\over 2}}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\widehat{b}(I){\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}(\mu^{-{1}}(I))^{{1\over 2}}\over|I|^{1\over 2}}{1\over|I|}\cdot{\lambda^{1\over 2}(x)|I|^{1\over 2}h_{I}(x)\over\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\,\frac{\mathsf{1}_{I}(y)\mu^{-{1\over 2}}(y)}{(\mu^{-{1}}(I))^{{1\over 2}}}\,dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}B(I)\cdot G_{I}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\,H_{I}(y)dy,

where

B(I)=\widehat{b}(I){\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}(\mu^{-{1}}(I))^{{1\over 2}}\over|I|^{{3\over 2}}},\quad G_{I}(x)={\lambda^{1\over 2}(x)|I|^{1\over 2}h_{I}(x)\over\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}},\quad H_{I}(y)=\frac{\mathsf{1}_{I}(y)\mu^{-{1\over 2}}(y)}{(\mu^{-{1}}(I))^{{1\over 2}}}.

The purpose of these equalities is that the last line places us in the position of using the NWO inequality (
2.3
) to conclude the proof.
This depends upon us showing that
\{G_{I}\}
and
\{H_{I}\}
are NWO for
L^{2}
, in the language of Definition
2.1
.
It then follows from (
2.3
) that

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{b}\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}^{p}

\displaystyle=\|\lambda^{1\over 2}\Pi_{b}\mu^{-{1\over 2}}\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}|B(I)|^{p}\approx\|b\|_{B_{\nu}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{p}.

The arguments that
\{G_{I}\}
and
\{H_{I}\}
are NWO depend only on the reverse Hölder inequality for
A_{2}
weights. They are similar, and we address
G_{I}
,
using the sufficient condition from Proposition
2.2
, which requires us to control a local norm in an index
r>2
.
To see this, we first note that
G_{I}
is supported in
I
. Next, as
\lambda
is
A_{2}
, there is a
r=r_{[\lambda]_{A_{2}}}>2
, so
that for each dyadic interval
I

\Bigl[\lvert I\rvert^{-1}\int_{I}\lambda^{r/2}\;dx\Bigr]^{2/r}\lesssim\frac{\lambda(I)}{\lvert I\rvert}.

With this choice of
r
, we can estimate as follows.

\displaystyle\|G_{I}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq{|I|^{1\over 2}\over\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}}\bigg(\int_{I}{\lambda^{r\over 2}(x)|h_{I}(x)|^{r}}dx\bigg)^{1\over r}\leq{|I|^{1\over 2}\over\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}}\bigg({|I|\over|I|^{r\over 2}}\ {1\over|I|}\int_{I}{\lambda^{r\over 2}(x)}dx\bigg)^{1\over r}

\displaystyle={|I|^{1\over 2}\over\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}}\bigg({|I|\over|I|^{r\over 2}}\bigg)^{1\over r}\bigg({1\over|I|}\int_{I}{\lambda^{r\over 2}(x)}dx\bigg)^{{2\over r}\cdot{1\over 2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{|I|^{1\over 2}\over\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}}\bigg({|I|\over|I|^{r\over 2}}\bigg)^{1\over r}\bigg({1\over|I|}\int_{I}{\lambda(x)}dx\bigg)^{{1\over 2}}

\displaystyle=|I|^{{1\over r}-{1\over 2}},

where the last inequality follows from reverse Hölder inequality for
\lambda\in A_{2}
. Hence, the claim holds.

Necessity:

Suppose
\Pi_{b}\in S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))
. Then from Lemma
2.7
we have that
\lambda^{1\over 2}\Pi_{b}\mu^{-{1\over 2}}\in S^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
. Note that for each dyadic interval
I
, we have
\widehat{b}(I)=\langle\Pi_{b}(\chi_{I}),h_{I}\rangle
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\Big|\widehat{b}(I){\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}(\mu^{-{1}}(I))^{{1\over 2}}\over|I|^{{3\over 2}}}\Big|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\Big|\langle\Pi_{b}(\chi_{I}),h_{I}\rangle{|I|^{1\over 2}\over\lambda^{-1}(I)^{1\over 2}(\mu(I))^{{1\over 2}}}\Big|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\Big|\big\langle\lambda^{1\over 2}\Pi_{b}\mu^{-{1\over 2}}(\mu^{{1\over 2}}\chi_{I}),\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}h_{I}\big\rangle{|I|^{1\over 2}\over\lambda^{-1}(I)^{1\over 2}(\mu(I))^{{1\over 2}}}\Big|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\big|\big\langle\lambda^{1\over 2}\Pi_{b}\mu^{-{1\over 2}}G_{I},H_{I}\big\rangle\big|^{p}.

Above, we use the notation

G_{I}:={\mu^{{1\over 2}}\chi_{I}\over\mu(I)^{{1\over 2}}}\quad{\rm and}\quad H_{I}:={\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}h_{I}|I|^{1\over 2}\over\lambda^{-1}(I)^{1\over 2}}.

These two collections of functions are NWO, in the sense of Definition
2.1
.
(We argued along this lines in the sufficiency direction.)
And so we have by (
2.3
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\Big|\widehat{b}(I){\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}(\mu^{-{1}}(I))^{{1\over 2}}\over|I|^{{3\over 2}}}\Big|^{p}\lesssim\|\lambda^{1\over 2}\Pi_{b}\mu^{-{1\over 2}}\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{p}.

That is, we have established that

\|b\|_{B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|\lambda^{1\over 2}\Pi_{b}\mu^{-{1\over 2}}\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}
.
∎

Based on Lemma
4.1
we have the following.

Corollary 4.2
.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
,
\mu,\lambda\in A_{2}
and set
\nu=\mu^{1\over 2}\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}
.
Suppose
b\in{\rm VMO}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R})
. Then we have
\Pi_{b}\in S^{p}(L^{2}(\lambda^{-1}),L^{2}(\mu^{-1}))
if and only if
b\in B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover,

\|\Pi_{b}\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}(\lambda^{-1}),L^{2}(\mu^{-1}))}\approx\|b\|_{B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

Hence, we have

\|\Pi_{b}^{*}\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}\approx\|b\|_{B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

5.
Schatten class and commutator of Haar multiplier

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be an arbitrary dyadic system in
\mathbb{R}
.
Recall that the Haar multiplier is defined in terms of an arbitrary function
\epsilon\colon\mathcal{D}\to\{-1,1\}
with

T_{\epsilon}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\epsilon_{I}\widehat{f}(I)h_{I}.

We have the following characterization of the Schatten membership of the paraproduct operators.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
,
\mu,\lambda\in A_{2}
and set
\nu=\mu^{1\over 2}\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}
.
Suppose
b\in{\rm VMO}_{\nu,d}(\mathbb{R})
. Then we have
[b,T_{\epsilon}]
is in
S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))
if and only if
b
is in
B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover,

\|[b,T_{\epsilon}]\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}\approx\|b\|_{B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

Proof.

Sufficiency:
Suppose
b\in B_{\nu,d}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.

Consider the commutator of Haar multiplier
[b,T_{\varepsilon}]
. We have

\displaystyle[b,T_{\varepsilon}]=\Pi_{b}T_{\varepsilon}-T_{\varepsilon}\Pi_{b}+\Pi^{*}_{b}T_{\varepsilon}-T_{\varepsilon}\Pi_{b}^{*}.

Then it is direct that

\displaystyle\|[b,T_{\varepsilon}]\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Pi_{b}\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}\|T_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})}+\|T_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})}\|\Pi_{b}\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\Pi^{*}_{b}\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}\|T_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})}+\|T_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})}\|\Pi_{b}^{*}\|_{S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}.

Based on the result in Section
4
,
we see that

[b,T_{\varepsilon}]\in{S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}
.

Necessity:
Suppose that

[b,T_{\varepsilon}]\in{S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}
. Then we know that
\lambda^{1\over 2}[b,T_{\varepsilon}]\mu^{-{1\over 2}}\in{S^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}
. Based on the non-degenerated condition on the Haar multiplier coefficients
\{\epsilon_{I}\}_{I}
, by using the same proof as that for Proposition
6.2
, we see that the necessity holds.
∎

6.
Schatten classes and commutator of the Hilbert transform

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
. To show the sufficiency, we use the dyadic result together with the observation of Petermichl
[
9
]
, that the Hilbert transform is an average of Haar shifts. For the necessity, we use the pointwise kernel lower bound and the median to split the Besov norm into the product of the commutator against NWOs.

We now provide the proof of our main theorem.

Sufficiency:

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose
\mu,\lambda\in A_{2}
and set
\nu=\mu^{1\over 2}\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}
.
Suppose that
b\in B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, then

\displaystyle\|[b,H]\|_{S^{2}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}\lesssim\|b\|_{B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

Proof.

Suppose that
b\in B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then we know that
b\in B^{2}_{\nu,d}(\mathbb{R})
for any system of dyadic intervals. That is

\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\Big|\widehat{b}(I){\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}(\mu^{-{1}}(I))^{{1\over 2}}\over|I|^{{3\over 2}}}\Big|^{p}<\infty.

We use Petermichl’s observation that the Hilbert transform can be recovered through an appropriate average of Haar shifts,
[
9
]
. On a fixed dyadic lattice
\mathcal{D}
with Haar basis
\{h_{I}\}_{I\in\mathcal{D}}
, we define a Haar shift operator
\Sh
, following Petermichl.
Set for any Haar function
h_{I}
,

\Sh h_{I}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(h_{I_{-}}-h_{I_{+}})
, which uniquely defines the operator.
Then, the Hilbert transform is an average of shift operators, with the average performed over the class of
all dyadic grids. In particular, to prove norm inequalities for the Hilbert transform, it suffices to prove them
for the Haar shift operator.

The commutator with the Haar shift operator has an explicit expansion in terms of the paraproducts and
\Sh
,

(6.1)

[b,\Sh]f=\Sh(\Pi_{b}f)-\Pi_{b}(\Sh f)+\Sh(\Pi_{b}^{*}f)-\Pi_{b}^{*}(\Sh f)+\Pi_{\Sh f}b-\Sh(\Pi_{f}b).

It is known that for any
w\in A_{2}
,
\left\|\Sh\right\|_{L^{2}(w)\to L^{2}(w)}\lesssim 1
.
There are six terms on the right above. The first four are of the form a paraproduct (or its adjoint) composed with a bounded operator.
But, the Schatten classes are closed under composition with bounded operators. That is, we have, for instance for the first term on the right in (
6.1
),

\lVert\Sh(\Pi_{b}f)\rVert_{S^{2}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq\|\Sh\|_{L^{2}(\lambda)\to L^{2}(\lambda)}\|\Pi_{b}\|_{S^{2}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}\lesssim\|b\|_{B^{2}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}.

We have to appeal to Lemma
4.1
and Corollary
4.2
. The discussion of the next three terms is the same.

The last two terms on the right in (
6.1
) are not of this form, and are treated separately.
Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{b}f

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Pi_{\Sh f}b-\Sh(\Pi_{f}b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{\widehat{b}(I)}{\left|I\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\widehat{f}(I)(h_{I_{+}}(x)-h_{I_{-}}(x))

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{\widehat{b}(I)}{\left|I\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\widehat{f}(I)k_{I}(x).

Expressed in this way, we see that the remainder is better behaved than the paraproducts, since the
k_{I}
have strong orthogonality properties. A straight forward variant of the proof of the Schatten norm estimates for paraproducts will complete the proof. The details are below.

Recall from Lemma
2.7
that
\mathcal{R}_{b}\in S^{2}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))
if and only if
\lambda^{1\over 2}\mathcal{R}_{b}\mu^{-{1\over 2}}\in S^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
. We have that

\displaystyle(\lambda^{1\over 2}\mathcal{R}_{b}\mu^{-{1\over 2}})(f)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{\widehat{b}(I)}{\left|I\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\mu^{-{1\over 2}}(y)h_{I}(y)dy\lambda^{1\over 2}(x)k_{I}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\widehat{b}(I){\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}(\mu^{-{1}}(I))^{{1\over 2}}\over|I|^{{3\over 2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y){\mu^{-{1\over 2}}(y)|I|^{1\over 2}h_{I}(y)\over(\mu^{-{1}}(I))^{{1\over 2}}}\,dy\ {\lambda^{1\over 2}(x)|I_{+}|^{1\over 2}k_{I}(x)\over\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}B(I)\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)H_{I}(y)\,dy\ K_{I}(x),

where the terms above are defined by

(6.2)

\displaystyle B(I):=\widehat{b}(I){\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}(\mu^{-{1}}(I))^{{1\over 2}}\over|I|^{{3\over 2}}},

(6.3)

\displaystyle H_{I}(x):={\mu^{-{1\over 2}}(x)|I|^{1\over 2}h_{I}(x)\over(\mu^{-{1}}(I))^{{1\over 2}}},\quad\textup{and}\quad K_{I}(x):={\lambda^{1\over 2}(x)|I_{+}|^{1\over 2}k_{I}(x)\over\lambda(I)^{1\over 2}}.

Similar to the estimate in Subsection 3.1, we see that both
\{H_{I}\}_{I\in\mathcal{D}}
and
\{K_{I}\}_{I\in\mathcal{D}}
are NWOs.

Combining all the estimates above, we see that
[b,\Sh]\in{S^{p}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}
. Hence, we further have
[b,H]\in{S^{2}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}
with

\|[b,H]\|_{S^{2}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}\lesssim\|b\|_{B^{2}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}.

The proof of Proposition
6.1
is complete.
∎

Necessity:

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose
\mu,\lambda\in A_{2}
,
\nu=\mu^{1\over 2}\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}
.
Suppose
b\in{\rm VMO}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R})
with
\|[b,H]\|_{S^{2}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}<\infty
, then we have

(6.4)

\|b\|_{B_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|[b,H]\|_{S^{2}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}.

Proof.

To begin with, we note that from Lemma
2.7
,

[b,H]\in S^{2}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))
if and only if
\lambda^{1\over 2}[b,H]\mu^{-{1\over 2}}
is in the unweighted
S^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
.

Next, we note that from Theorem
1.5
, we know that the continuous Besov space is the intersection of two dyadic Besov spaces.
Thus, it suffices to show that

\|b\|_{B_{\nu,d}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|[b,H]\|_{S^{2}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))},

for any dyadic Besov space. Below, we consider intervals
Q\in\mathcal{D}
, a fixed dyadic system.

This argument is of a standard nature. For each dyadic interval
Q
, let
\hat{Q}
be the dyadic interval such that:

(1)

|Q|=|\hat{Q}|
, and both
Q
and
\hat{Q}
are contained in the same dyadic interval
\widetilde{Q}
with

|\widetilde{Q}|\lesssim|Q|
.

(2)

The kernel of Hilbert transform
K(x-{\hat{x}})
does not change sign for all
(x,{\hat{x}})\in Q\times\hat{Q}
and

(6.5)

|K(x-{\hat{x}})|\gtrsim\frac{1}{|Q|}.

(3)

Let
\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)
be a median value of
b
over
\hat{Q}
. This means
\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)
is a real number such that

(6.6)

\displaystyle E_{1}^{Q}:=\{y\in Q:b(y)<\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)\}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ E_{2}^{Q}:=\{y\in Q:b(y)>\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)\}.

We note that we have the upper bound
\lvert E^{\hat{Q}}_{j}\rvert\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\lvert\hat{Q}\rvert
for
j=1,2
.
A median value always exists, but may not be unique.

Next we decompose
Q
into two dyadic children by writing
Q=\bigcup_{j=1}^{2}Q_{j}
.
By the cancellation property of
h_{Q}
, we see that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Q}b(x)h_{Q}(x)dx\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{Q}(b(x)-\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b))h_{Q}(x)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq{1\over|Q|^{1\over 2}}\int_{Q}\left|b(x)-\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{2}{1\over|Q|^{1\over 2}}\int_{Q_{j}}\left|b(x)-\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|Q|^{1\over 2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{Q_{j}\cap E_{1}^{Q}}\left|b(x)-\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)\right|dx+\frac{1}{|Q|^{1\over 2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{Q_{j}\cap E_{2}^{Q}}\left|b(x)-\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)\right|dx

(6.7)

\displaystyle=:{\rm Term}_{1}^{Q}+{\rm Term}_{2}^{Q}.

Above, we are using the notation (
6.6
).

Now we denote

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\hat{Q}}:=\{y\in\hat{Q}:b(y)\geq\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)\}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ F_{2}^{\hat{Q}}:=\{y\in\hat{Q}:b(y)\leq\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)\}.

Then by the definition of
\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)
, we have
|F_{1}^{\hat{Q}}|=|F_{2}^{\hat{Q}}|\sim|\hat{Q}|
and
F_{1}^{\hat{Q}}\cup F_{2}^{\hat{Q}}=\hat{Q}
. Note that for
s=1,2
, if
x\in E_{s}^{Q}
and
y\in F_{s}^{\hat{Q}}
, then

\displaystyle\left|b(x)-\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|b(x)-\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)\right|+\left|\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)-b(y)\right|=\left|b(x)-\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)+\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)-b(y)\right|=\left|b(x)-b(y)\right|.

Therefore, for
s=1,2
, by using (
6.5
) and by the fact that
|F_{s}^{\hat{Q}}|\approx|Q|
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Term}_{s}^{Q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|^{1\over 2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{Q_{j}\cap E_{s}^{Q}}\left|b(x)-\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)\right|dx\frac{|F_{s}^{\hat{Q}}|}{|Q|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|Q|^{1\over 2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{Q_{j}\cap E_{s}^{Q}}\ \int_{F_{s}^{\hat{Q}}}\left|b(x)-\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)\right|\ \frac{1}{|Q|}\ dy\ dx\

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|^{1\over 2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{Q_{j}\cap E_{s}^{Q}}\int_{F_{s}^{\hat{Q}}}\left|b(x)-\alpha_{\hat{Q}}(b)\right|\left|K(x-y)\right|dydx

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|^{1\over 2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{Q_{j}\cap E_{s}^{Q}}\int_{F_{s}^{\hat{Q}}}\left|b(x)-b(y)\right|\left|K(x-y)\right|dydx.

To continue, by noting that
K(x-y)
and
b(x)-b(y)
do not change sign for
(x,y)\in(Q_{j}\cap E_{s}^{Q})\times F_{s}^{\hat{Q}}
,
s=1,2
, we have that

\displaystyle{\rm Term}_{s}^{Q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|^{1\over 2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left|\int_{Q_{j}\cap E_{s}^{Q}}\int_{F_{s}^{\hat{Q}}}(b(x)-b(y))K(x-y)dydx\right|

(6.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|Q|^{1\over 2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(b(x)-b(y))K(x-y)\chi_{F_{s}^{\hat{Q}}}(y)\ dy\ \chi_{Q_{j}\cap E_{s}^{Q}}(x)\ dx\right|.

We now insert the weights
\lambda
and
\mu
to get

\displaystyle{\rm Term}_{s}^{Q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|^{1\over 2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(b(x)-b(y))\lambda^{1\over 2}(x)K(x-y)\mu^{-{1\over 2}}(y)\big(\mu^{{1\over 2}}(y)\chi_{F_{s}^{\hat{Q}}}(y)\big)\ dy\ \big(\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}(x)\chi_{Q_{j}\cap E_{s}^{Q}}(x)\big)\ dx\right|.

Thus, we further have

\displaystyle\big|\widehat{b}(Q)\big|^{2}{|Q|\over\lambda^{-1}(Q)\ \mu(Q)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(b(x)-b(y))\lambda^{1\over 2}(x)K(x-y)\mu^{-{1\over 2}}(y)\big(\mu^{{1\over 2}}(y)\chi_{F_{s}^{\hat{Q}}}(y)\big)\ dy\ \frac{\big(\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}(x)\chi_{Q_{j}\cap E_{s}^{Q}}(x)\big)}{\mu(\hat{Q})^{1\over 2}\lambda^{-1}(Q)^{1\over 2}}\ dx\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(b(x)-b(y))\lambda^{1\over 2}(x)K(x-y)\mu^{-{1\over 2}}(y){\mu^{{1\over 2}}(y)\chi_{F_{s}^{\hat{Q}}}(y)\over\mu(\hat{Q})^{1\over 2}}\ dy\ {\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}(x)\chi_{Q_{j}\cap E_{s}^{Q}}(x)\over\lambda^{-1}(Q)^{1\over 2}}\ dx\right|^{2}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{k}}\big|\widehat{b}(Q)\big|^{2}{|Q|\over\lambda^{-1}(Q)\ \mu(Q)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{k}}\sum_{s=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}|\big\langle\lambda^{1\over 2}[b,H]\mu^{-{1\over 2}}G_{\hat{Q}},H_{Q}\big\rangle|^{2}.

On the right above, we are using the notation

G_{\hat{Q}}(x):={\chi_{F_{s}^{\hat{Q}}}(x)\mu^{{1\over 2}}(x)\over\mu(\hat{Q})^{1\over 2}}\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ H_{Q}(x)={\chi_{Q_{j}\cap E_{s}^{Q}}(x)\lambda^{-{1\over 2}}(x)\over\lambda^{-1}(Q)^{1\over 2}}.

Both collections of functions abover are NWO for
L^{2}
. See Definition
2.1
, and Proposition
2.2
.
And both
Q
and
\hat{Q}
are contained in the same dyadic interval
\tilde{Q}
and that the dyadic intervals

Q,\hat{Q}
and
\tilde{Q}
have comparable sizes.

It follows from (
2.3
) that

\|b\|_{B^{2}_{\nu,d}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|[b,H]\|_{S^{2}(L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}))}.

The proof of Proposition
6.2
is complete.
∎

7.
Further discussions on weighted Besov spaces
B^{p}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R})
for
p\not=2

We recall that Peller has characterized the membership of the commutator
[b,H]
in
S^{p}
, for all
0<p<\infty
, in the unweighted case. Namely,

Theorem 7.1
.

[
8
]
For a symbol
b\in VMO(\mathbb{R})
, and
0<p<\infty
, we have

(7.1)

\lVert[b,H]\rVert_{S^{p}(L^{2}\to L^{2})}^{p}\approx\int\int\frac{\lvert b(x)-b(y)\rvert^{p}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}}\;dxdy

Our first question is then: Does this characterization continue to hold in the one weight case? Namely, when one consider the commuator as acting on
L^{2}(w)
,
for an
A_{2}
weight
w
. Our second question is can our dyadic result Theorem
1.4
be extended to the case
0<p\leq 1
?
Our methods used duality, especially through the inequality (
2.3
). So a new approach would be needed.

Also, recall that if one consider the commutator with a Riesz transform
R
in higher dimensions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the only symbols
b
for which the commutator
is in
S^{p}
, for
0<p<d
are the constant symbols. But in that case, the commutator is zero. See the introduction to
[
5
]
.
Thus, given our restriction in our main theorem on the Hilbert transform, the restriction to dimension one is reasonable.

Our main question is how to extend Theorem
1.2
to the case of Schatten classes for
p\neq 2
. Let us describe a difficulty in finding a sufficient condition.
In our current proof, we used a classical characterization of Hilbert–Schmidt operators in terms of the their kernels. Namely, if
T
, an operator acting on a
general measure space has kernel
K(x,y)
, we have

(7.2)

\lVert T\rVert_{S^{2}}^{2}=\int\int\lvert K(x,y)\rvert^{2}\;dx\,dy.

This has an extension to
S^{p}
for
p>2
. Combining observations of Russo
[
13
]
and Goffeng
[
3
]
,
Janson and Wolff
[
5
]
*Lemma 1 and Lemma 2: if
p>2
and
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
, then

(7.3)

\displaystyle\|T\|_{S^{p,\infty}}\leq\|K\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}^{1/2}\|K^{*}\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}^{1/2},

where
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}
denotes the mixed-norm:

\|K\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}:=\big\|\|K(x,y)\|_{L^{p}(dx)}\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}(dy)}.

Consider
T=[b,H]
and
\lambda,\mu\in A_{2}
.
Then,
T^{\prime}=M_{\sqrt{\lambda}}TM_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}

is mapping
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to itself with
the kernel

(7.4)

K(x,y)=\sqrt{\lambda(x)/\mu(y)}\ \frac{b(x)-b(y)}{x-y}

Then, one needs to bound, with
1/q=1-2/p

(7.5)

\displaystyle\lVert K\rVert_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl\lVert\sqrt{\lambda(x)/\mu(y)}\frac{\lvert b(x)-b(y)\rvert}{\lvert x-y\rvert}\Bigr\rVert_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl\lVert\sqrt{\lambda(x)/\mu(y)}\frac{\lvert b(x)-b(y)\rvert}{|x-y|}\Bigr\rVert_{L^{p},L^{p}}\lVert\lvert x-y\rvert^{-1/q}\rVert_{L^{\infty}L^{q,\infty}}

(7.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl\lVert\sqrt{\lambda(x)/\mu(y)}\frac{\lvert b(x)-b(y)\rvert}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{2/p}}\Bigr\rVert_{L^{p}(dxdy)}.

The expression above is problematic, however. The power
p/2
falls on an
A_{2}
weight.
A power weight
\lambda(x)=\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}
is an
A_{2}
weight for
0<\alpha<1/2
. For
\alpha p/2>1
, this weight will not be in
L^{p/2}_{\textup{loc}}
.
One might hope that the the expression in (
7.6
) could be useful, due to the isolated nature of the singularity of the power weight.
But, we also have this folklore fact: The singularities of an
A_{2}
weight can be dense, as detailed in the Proposition below.
For
\lambda
as in Proposition
7.2
, if one uses (
7.6
) as a definition of a Besov space, then only constants are allowed.
This is in sharp contrast to the dyadic Besov spaces, which contain all Haar functions, for instance.

Proposition 7.2
.

For any
r>1
, there is a
\lambda\in A_{2}
for which
\int_{I}\lambda^{r}(x)\;dx=\infty

for all intervals
I
.

Proof.

Fix
r>1
and set
\alpha=(1+1/r)/2
.
Let
0<\delta<1/2
, and consider the function

(7.7)

\phi_{\delta}(x)=\begin{cases}\delta^{-\alpha},&n<x<n+\delta,\ n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
1,&\textup{otherwise}\end{cases}

This is an
A_{2}
weight, with
A_{2}
constant independent of
\delta
. And,

(7.8)

\int_{I}\phi_{\delta}^{r}\;dx\geq\delta^{1-r\alpha}=\delta^{-\frac{r-1}{2}}.

This becomes unbounded as
\delta\downarrow 0
.

Now, let
n_{j}=2^{j}
and
A(1-\alpha)>2
. Then define

(7.9)

\lambda(x)=\max_{j\geq 1}\phi_{2^{-An_{j}}}(2^{n_{j}}x+2^{-n_{j}-1})\eqqcolon\max_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}(x)

The purpose of having
n_{j}
to increase quickly is to have the ‘peaks’ of the weights have rapidly increasing heights, and very short durations.
The cumulative mass that the peaks add an interval
I
with length greater than
2^{-n_{j}}
is

(7.10)

\displaystyle 2^{-An_{j}(1-\alpha)}2^{n_{j}}\lvert I\rvert\lesssim 2^{-n_{j}}\lvert I\rvert.

The shift ‘
+2^{-n_{j}-1}
’ is to make the peaks disjoint.
It is a consequence of (
7.8
) that
\lambda
has infinite
L^{r}
norm on every interval. We need to check that
\lambda\in A_{2}
.

Fix an interval
I
. Certainly, the only interesting case is
\lvert I\rvert<1
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{j}
be the components of the set
\lambda_{j}^{-1}(2^{A\alpha n_{j}})
. Let
k
be the minumum integer
j

so that
I
intersects some interval
P\in\mathcal{P}_{j}
.
If there are no such integers
j
, then
\lambda
is identically one on
I
, and there is nothing to prove.
Otherwise, we have by (
7.10
),

\lambda(I)\lesssim\lambda_{k}(I)
.
And,
\chi_{I}\lambda(x)\geq\chi_{I}\lambda_{k}(x)
, so the
A_{2}
property follows from the fact that the
\lambda_{k}

have uniformly bounded
A_{2}
constant.
∎

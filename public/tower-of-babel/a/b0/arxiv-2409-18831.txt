Abstract
. We characterize when a subfactor N ⊆ M N\subseteq M is oracle computable relative to a presentation of the ambient factor M M in terms of computability of the Jones basic construction, in terms of computable Pismner-Popa bases, and in terms of computability of the conditional expectation map. We illustrate our main theorem with some examples.

Before explaining these three approaches to the complexity of a subfactor inclusion

First, the entire discussion is relative to a given

One reasonable interpretation of this question involves the basic construction of Jones, which is the II

Another perspective on the question comes from considering Pimsner-Popa bases for the inclusion. A Pimsner-Popa basis for the inclusion

One final perspective on the

It follows from our main result that if

M_{n}^{\#}
comprising the Jones tower.

Two of the three equivalent conditions in our main theorem involve a conjunction of two statements. It is natural to wonder if either of those conjunctions can be simplified to one of their conjuncts. We conclude the paper with two partial results along these lines. We show that if

M_{1}^{\#}
is
\mathbf{D}^{\prime\prime}
-computable (so at most two “jumps” of complexity higher). It would be interesting to see if these jumps are actually necessary.

In future work, we plan on studying effective aspects of other objects related to a subfactor, such as its standard invariant and derived tower.

2.
Generalities on Presentations

In this section, we discuss the notion of a presentation of a tracial von Neumann algebra and collect some basic facts to be used throughout the paper.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a tracial von Neumann algebra
M
, a
presentation
of
M
is a countable sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of elements of the unit ball of
M
that generate a (SOT-) dense *-subalgebra of
M
. The elements of the presentation are sometimes called the
special points
of the presentation.

We use letters like
M^{\#}
and
M^{\dagger}
to denote presentations of
M
.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a presentation
M^{\#}
of a tracial von Neumann algebra, the set of elements of
M
obtained by closing the elements of the presentation under addition, multiplication, adjoint, and multiplication by elements of
\mathbb{Q}(i)
are referred to as the
rational points
of
M^{\#}
.

Throughout, we let
\mathcal{F}(k)=\mathrm{C}^{\mbox{$*$}}\langle c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}:{\left\lVert c_{i}\right\rVert}\leq 1\rangle
be the universal contraction C*-algebra on
k
generators.

Given a presentation
M^{\#}
of
M
, there is an associated computable enumeration
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of the rational points of the presentation.
Moreover, there is a computable functional
\cdot^{\flat}
on the rational points which computes a naïve upper bound on the operator norm.
Specifically, if
x_{n}=p(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
is a rational point (so
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
are special points of
M^{\#}
and
p(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})
is a
*
-polynomial with coefficients from
\mathbb{Q}(i)
), then we set

\displaystyle x_{n}^{\flat}

\displaystyle:=\|p(c_{1},\ldots,c_{k})\|_{\mathcal{F}(k)}

\displaystyle=\sup\{{\left\lVert p(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\right\rVert}_{A}:A\text{ is a C*-algebra},{\left\lVert z_{i}\right\rVert}_{A}\leq 1\}.

In particular,
a_{n}^{\flat}=1
for every special point
a_{n}
of the presentation.
Since the standard presentation of
\mathcal{F}(k)
is computable by
[
F
, Corollary 3.8]
, it follows that
x_{n}^{\flat}
is a computable real number, uniformly in
n
.

A key consequence of our set-up is the following:

Lemma 2.3
.

For any rational point
x
of
M^{\#}
with
\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1
, there is a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of rational points of
M^{\#}
such that
\left\lVert x_{n}-x\right\rVert_{\infty}\to 0
with
x_{n}^{\flat}<1
for all
n
.

Proof.

Let
x=p(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
.
Let
\pi:\mathcal{F}(k)\to\mathrm{C}^{\mbox{$*$}}\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\rangle\subseteq M
be the natural surjection defined by
\pi(c_{i})=a_{i}
.
By
[
RLL00
, 2.2.10]
, there exists
z\in\mathcal{F}(k)
such that
{\left\lVert z\right\rVert}=\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1
and
\pi(z)=x
.
Let
(z_{n})
be a sequence of rational *-polynomials in
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}
such that
{\left\lVert z_{n}-z\right\rVert}\to 0
and
{\left\lVert z_{n}\right\rVert}<1
for all
n
.
Let
x_{n}=\pi(z_{n})
, so
x_{n}
is a rational point of
M^{\#}
and
x_{n}^{\flat}={\left\lVert z_{n}\right\rVert}<1
.
It remains to note that
\left\lVert x_{n}-x\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert\pi(z_{n}-z)\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq{\left\lVert z_{n}-z\right\rVert}\to 0
.
∎

Corollary 2.4

For any

For each rational point

3.
Useful technical lemmas

In this section, we collect a number of useful technical lemmas stating that certain types of elements (such as projections and partial isometries) can, under certain assumptions, be found effectively.

Fact 3.1
.

Suppose
0<\epsilon<1
and
0<\delta<\epsilon^{2}/48
.
Then for any tracial von Neumann algebra
M
and
x\in M
, if
\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1
,
\left\lVert x-x^{*}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\delta
, and
\left\lVert x-x^{2}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\delta
, then there is a projection
p\in M
such that
\left\lVert x-p\right\rVert_{2}<\epsilon
.

Proof.

Let
z=x^{*}x
.
Then
0\leq z\leq 1
and

\left\lVert x-z\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert x-x^{2}\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert x-x^{*}\right\rVert_{2}\leq 2\delta.

Hence,

\displaystyle\left\lVert z^{2}-z\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert z^{2}-zx\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert zx-z\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert z-x\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert x^{*}\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert x^{2}-x\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle<\epsilon^{2}/16.

Thus, by
[
C75
, Lemma 1.1.5]
, there is a projection
p
such that
\left\lVert z-p\right\rVert_{2}\leq\epsilon/2
, whence
\left\lVert x-p\right\rVert_{2}<\epsilon
.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

Given a presentation
M^{\#}
of a tracial von Neumann algebra
M
and rational
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, we say a rational point
x
of
M^{\#}
is an
\epsilon
-quasi-projection
if
x^{\flat}<1
,
\left\lVert x-x^{*}\right\rVert_{2}<\epsilon^{2}/48
, and
\left\lVert x-x^{2}\right\rVert_{2}<\epsilon^{2}/48
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix a presentation
M^{\#}
of a tracial von Neumann algebra
M
and rational
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

(1)

Every
\epsilon
-quasi-projection is
\epsilon
-close (in
2
-norm) to an actual projection.

(2)

Every projection in
M
is
\epsilon
-close (in
2
-norm) to an
\epsilon
-quasi-projection.

(3)

If
M^{\#}
is
\mathbf{D}
-computable, then the set of
\epsilon
-quasi-projections of
M^{\#}
is
\mathbf{D}
-c.e.

Proof.

Item (1) follows from Fact
3.1
while item (2) follows from Corollary
2.4
. Item (3) is clear.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
M^{\#}
is a
\bf D
-computable presentation of a tracial von Neumann algebra

. Then
(x,y)\mapsto\tr(x^{*}y):(M^{\#})^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
is a
\mathbf{D}
-computable map, uniformly in a code for
M^{\#}
.

Proof.

This follows from the polarization identity.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
M^{\#}
is a
\bf D
-computable presentation of a tracial von Neumann algebra

. Then
1
is a
\mathbf{D}
-computable point of

Proof.

Given

, search for a rational point

-quasi-projection with

Such a rational point must exist by Corollary

By Fact

Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert p-1\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=1-\tr(p)

\displaystyle\leq|1-\tr(xx^{*}x)|+\left\lVert xx^{*}x-p\right\rVert_{2}(\text{by Cauchy-Schwarz})

\displaystyle<2^{-2k-2}.

Thus

-computable projections of

for

Proof.

As usual, we denote the fact that projections

If

A proof of the non-effective form of the following lemma is essentially contained in the proof of
[

, Proposition 1.1.3(b)]
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
M
be a II
1
factor.
Let
p,q\in M
be projections such that
p\sim q
.
Suppose
0<\epsilon<1
and
0<\delta<\epsilon^{16}/11^{16}
.
Then for any
x\in M
, if
\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1
,
\left\lVert x^{*}x-p\right\rVert_{2}\leq\delta
, and
\left\lVert xx^{*}-q\right\rVert_{2}\leq\delta
, then there is an implement
v\in M
of
p\sim q
with
\left\lVert x-v\right\rVert_{2}<\epsilon
.

Proof.

Let
x\in M
be as in the conclusion of the theorem and set
y:=qxp
.
Then

\left\lVert(1-q)x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\tr((1-q)(xx^{*}-q)(1-q))\leq\left\lVert xx^{*}-q\right\rVert_{2}\leq\delta

and

\left\lVert x(1-p)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\tr((1-p)(x^{*}x-p)(1-p))\leq\left\lVert x^{*}x-p\right\rVert_{2}\leq\delta,

so

\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert x-qx\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert qx-y\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert(1-q)x\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert x(1-p)\right\rVert_{2}\leq 2\delta^{1/2}.

Furthermore,

\left\lVert y^{*}y-p\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert y^{*}y-y^{*}x\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert y^{*}x-x^{*}x\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert x^{*}x-p\right\rVert_{2}\leq 5\delta^{1/2},

whence it follows that

\left\lVert(y^{*}y)^{2}-y^{*}y\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert(y^{*}y)^{2}-y^{*}yp\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert y^{*}y-p\right\rVert_{2}\leq 5\delta^{1/2}.

Let
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
be defined by
f(a)=a^{-1/2}
if
1-\sqrt{5}\delta^{1/4}\leq a\leq 1
and
0
otherwise.
Set
z:=yf(y^{*}y)
.
Then
z^{*}z
and
zz^{*}
are projections satisfying

z^{*}z=y^{*}yf(y^{*}y)^{2}\leq p\text{ and }zz^{*}=y(f^{*}y)^{2}y^{*}\leq q.

By construction,

(1-y^{*}y)^{2}(1-z^{*}z)\geq(5\delta^{1/2})(1-z^{*}z)
and
\tr((y^{*}y)^{2}(1-y^{*}y)^{2})\leq(5)^{2}\delta
,
whence

\displaystyle\left\lVert y(1-z^{*}z)\right\rVert_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle=\tr(y^{*}y(1-z^{*}z))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tr((y^{*}y)^{2}(1-z^{*}z))

\displaystyle\leq(5\delta^{1/2})^{-1}\tr((y^{*}y)^{2}(1-y^{*}y)^{2}(1-z^{*}z))

\displaystyle\leq 5\delta^{1/2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert y-z\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert y-yz^{*}z\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert yz^{*}z-z\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert y(1-z^{*}z)\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert y^{*}yf(y^{*}y)^{2}-f(y^{*}y)\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt[4]{5}\delta^{1/8}+\sqrt{5}\delta^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq 5\delta^{1/8}.

Furthermore,

\left\lVert z^{*}z-p\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert z^{*}z-z^{*}y\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert z^{*}y-y^{*}y\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert y^{*}y-p\right\rVert_{2}\leq 15\delta^{1/8}.

Observe that
\tr(q)-\tr(zz^{*})=\tr(p)-\tr(z^{*}z)=\left\lVert p-z^{*}z\right\rVert_{2}\leq 15\delta^{1/8}
.
Let
w
be a partial isometry such that
w^{*}w=p-z^{*}z
and
ww^{*}=q-zz^{*}
.
Then

\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\tr(p)-\tr(z^{*}z)\leq 15\delta^{1/8},

and so
\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{2}\leq 4\delta^{1/16}
.
Finally, set
v:=z+w
.
Then
v
is an implement of
p\sim q
and

\left\lVert x-v\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert y-z\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{2}\leq 11\delta^{1/16}<\epsilon.

∎

4.
Preliminaries on subfactor theory

In this section, we present the basic facts from subfactor theory from
[
J83
,
PP86
]
needed in the rest of the paper.
Another good reference for this material is
[
P94
]
.

Throughout, by a
subfactor
we mean an inclusion of
\rm II_{1}
factors
N\subseteq M
. We let
e_{N}:L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(N)
(or simply
e
if the context is clear) denote the canonical orthogonal projection map, noting that the restriction
E_{N}
of
e_{N}
to
M
takes values in
N
; the linear map
E_{N}:M\to N
is called the
conditional expectation map
from
M
onto
N
. We note the following properties of
E
:

•

E_{N}(x)=x
for all
x\in N
.

•

E_{N}(xyz)=xE_{N}(y)z
for all
x,z\in N
and
y\in M
.

•

E_{N}(x^{*})=E_{N}(x)^{*}
for all
x\in M
.

•

E_{N}(x)
is the unique
y\in N
such that
\tr((x-y)z)=0
for all
z\in N
.

•

E_{N}(x)
is the unique
y\in N
such that
e_{N}xe_{N}=ye_{N}
.

•

E_{N}
is trace-preserving and contractive with respect to both the operator norm and the 2-norm.

An important invariant of the subfactor
N\subseteq M
is the
index

[M:N]
of
N
in
M
.
In order to avoid introducing modules over a II
1
factor, we give a definition of the index in terms of the conditional expectation map due to Pimsner and Popa

[
PP86
]
, namely

[M:N]^{-1}:=\max\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:\ E_{N}(x)\geq\lambda x\text{ for all }x\geq 0\}.

This can alternatively be described by

[M:N]^{-1}:=\inf\left\{\frac{\|E_{N}(x)\|^{2}_{2}}{\|x\|^{2}_{2}}\ :\ x>0\right\}.

The terminology is inspired by the fact that if
L(H)\subseteq L(G)
is the subfactor induced by a subgroup
H\leq G
, then
[L(G):L(H)]=[G:H]
. However, unlike the case of groups, the index does not necessarily have to be an integer. A landmark result of Jones states that
[M:N]
must always be an element of the set
\{4\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi}{n})\ :n=3,4,5,\ldots\}\cup[4,\infty]
. Moreover, all of these values can be realized as indices of subfactors of
\mathcal{R}
.

Associated to the subfactor
N\subseteq M
is the
Jones basic construction
, which is the von Neumann algebra
M_{1}:=(M\cup\{e_{N}\})^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(M))
generated by
M
and
e_{N}
. We recall the following basic facts about
M_{1}
:

•

M+Me_{N}M
is a weakly dense
*
-subalgebra of
M_{1}
.

•

M_{1}
is a II
1
factor if and only if
[M:N]<\infty
.

Assuming
[M:N]<\infty
, we also have:

•

[M_{1}:M]=[M:N]
.

•

For
x\in M
, we have
\tr(xe_{N})=[M:N]^{-1}\tr(x)
. In particular,

\tr(e_{N})=[M:N]^{-1}

and

\left\lVert xe_{N}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=[M:N]^{-1}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

for any
x\in M
.

•

E_{M}(e_{N})=\tr(e_{N})\cdot 1=[M:N]^{-1}\cdot 1
.

•

For
x\in M_{1}
,
y=[M:N]E_{M}(xe_{N})
is the unique element of
M
such that
xe_{N}=ye_{N}
. In particular,
M_{1}=Me_{N}M:=\operatorname{span}\{xe_{N}y\ :\ x,y\in M\}
.

Since
M\subseteq M_{1}
is a subfactor, one can consider the associated basic construction, which is denoted
M_{2}
. Continuing this process leads to the
Jones tower

N\subseteq M_{0}:=M\subseteq M_{1}\subseteq M_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq M_{n}\subseteq M_{n+1}\subseteq\cdots.

Suppose that
[M:N]<\infty
has integer part
n
. Then there exist elements
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n+1}
of
M
with the following properties:

•

E_{N}(m_{j}^{*}m_{k})=0
for all distinct
j,k\in\{1,\ldots,n+1\}
.

•

E_{N}(m_{j}^{*}m_{j})=1
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

•

E_{N}(m_{n+1}^{*}m_{n+1})
is a projection of trace
[M:N]-n
.

(By convention,
m_{n+1}=0
if
[M:N]\in\mathbb{N}
.)
Any such family of elements is called a
Pimsner-Popa basis of
M
over
N
. The terminology is inspired by the fact that if
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n+1}
is a Pimsner-Popa basis of
M
over
N
, then every
x\in M
can be written uniquely as
x=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}m_{j}x_{j}
with each
x_{j}\in N
for
j=1,\dots,n
and
x_{n+1}\in E_{N}(m_{n+1}^{*}m_{n+1})N
. Other important properties of a Pimsner-Popa basis are the following:

•

\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}m_{j}e_{N}m_{j}^{*}=1
.

•

\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}m_{j}^{*}=[M:N]
.

Pimsner-Popa bases are unique in a sense made precise in
[
PP86
]
. Relevant for us is how they can be constructed: Let
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n+1}
be orthogonal projections in
M_{1}
with
tr(g_{i})=[M:N]^{-1}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
tr(g_{n+1})=1-n[M:N]^{-1}
. For
j=1,\ldots,n+1
, take partial isometries
v_{j}\in M_{1}
such that
v_{j}v_{j}^{*}=g_{j}
,
v_{j}^{*}v_{j}=e_{N}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
, and
v_{n+1}^{*}v_{n+1}\leq e_{N}
.
A Pimsner-Popa basis is then obtained by letting
m_{j}
be the unique element of
M
such that
v_{j}=v_{j}e_{N}=m_{j}e_{N}
.

Moving forward, if the inclusion
N\subseteq M
is clear from context, we simply write
e
for the Jones projection
e_{N}
.

Although we will not study computability of standard invariants in this article, the notion of the standard invariant will arise in examples below.
Jones’
standard invariant
consists of:

•

The two towers of higher relative commutants/centralizer algebras

(M_{0}^{\prime}\cap M_{n})_{n\geq 0}\text{
and }(M_{1}^{\prime}\cap M_{n+1})_{n\geq 0},

which are finite dimensional by
[
J83
]
;

•

the Jones projections
e_{1}\in N^{\prime}\cap M_{1}
,
e_{2}\in M^{\prime}\cap M_{2}\subset N^{\prime}\cap M_{2}
,
e_{3}\in M_{1}^{\prime}\cap M_{3}\subset M^{\prime}\cap M_{3}\subset N^{\prime}\cap M_{3}
, etc., which satisfy the Temperley-Lieb-Jones relations (see Example
8.4
below);
and

•

the Markov trace
\tr
on
M_{\infty}=\varinjlim M_{n}
restricted to the two towers of centralizer algebras.

The standard invariant, and thus the subfactor itself, is called
finite depth
if there is a global bound on the dimensions of the centers of the higher relative commutants.

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose

-computable presentation,

is
\mathbf{D}
-computable, and

, and

6.
The main theorem

In this section, we state and prove the main result of this paper. Before doing so, we introduce some important terminology.

.

Recalling the definition of the induced presentation of a subfactor (Definition

Lemma 6.4

If

is

-computable, then

\mathbf{D}
-computable if and only if

\mathbf{D}
-computably embedded in

.

We can now state our main theorem:

Theorem 6.5
.

Suppose that
N
is a subfactor of
M
such that

. Fix a presentation

of

be the induced presentation of the Jones basic construction

. Let

be a Turing oracle such that
M^{\#}
is
\bf D
-computable. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

M_{1}^{\#}
is
\bf D
-computable.

(2)

The following two statements hold:

(a)

E_{N}:M^{\#}\to M^{\#}
is
\bf D
-computable.

(b)

[M:N]
is a
\bf D
-computable real number.

(3)

The following two statements hold:

(a)

There is a presentation
N^{\dagger}
of
N
which is
\mathbf{D}
-computably embedded in
M^{\#}
.

(b)

There is a
\mathbf{D}
-computable Pimsner-Popa basis of
M^{\#}
over
N
.

Proof.

We first prove the equivalence of (1) and (2).
Suppose that
M_{1}^{\#}
is
\bf D
-computable. Since

is a special point of
M_{1}^{\#}
, we have
[M:N]^{-1}=\tr(e)=\|e\|_{2}^{2}
is
\bf D
-computable, and thus so is
[M:N]
.
Furthermore,
E_{N}:M^{\#}\to M^{\#}
is
\mathbf{D}
-computable: given a rational point
x
of
M^{\#}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, search for a rational point
y
of
M^{\#}
such that
\left\lVert ye-exe\right\rVert_{2}^{2}<[M:N]^{-1}2^{-2k}
, in which case

\left\lVert y-E_{N}(x)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=[M:N]\left\lVert(y-E_{N}(x))e\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=[M:N]\left\lVert ye-exe\right\rVert_{2}^{2}<2^{-2k}.

Conversely, suppose that
E_{N}:M^{\#}\to M^{\#}
is
\bf D
-computable and
[M:N]
is
\bf D
-computable.
Given a rational point
x
of
M_{1}^{\#}
, by Lemma
5.4
we can write
x^{*}x
in syntactic normal form as
a+\sum_{i}b_{i}ec_{i}
.
Then

\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\tr(x^{*}x)=\tr(a)+\sum_{i}\tr(b_{i}ec_{i})=\tr(a)+[M:N]^{-1}\sum_{i}\tr(b_{i}c_{i}),

which is
\mathbf{D}
-computable by Corollary
3.6
and Lemma
6.4
.

We now prove that (1) implies (3). Suppose (1), equivalently (2), holds.
Trivially then,
N^{\#}
is
\mathbf{D}
-computably embedded in
M^{\#}
by Lemma
6.4
.
To finish proving (3), we apply Lemma

to find
\mathbf{D}
-computable projections

of

with

\tr(g_{i})=[M:N]^{-1}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,n,\quad\tr(g_{n+1})=1-n[M:N]^{-1}.

By Lemma
3.13
, we effectively find
\mathbf{D}
-computable partial isometries

of
M_{1}^{\#}
such that

for

v_{j}^{*}v_{j}=e
for

, and
v_{n+1}^{*}v_{n+1}\leq e
.
By Lemma

, we can effectively find
\mathbf{D}
-computable points

of
M^{\#}
, where each
m_{j}
is the unique element of

that satisfies

.
It follows that

is a
\mathbf{D}
-computable Pimsner-Popa basis of
M^{\#}
over
N
.

Finally, we prove that (3) implies (2). Let
N^{\dagger}
be
\mathbf{D}
-computably embedded in
M^{\#}
and let
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n+1}
be a
\mathbf{D}
-computable Pimsner-Popa basis of
M^{\#}
over
N
. The statement that

is
\bf D
-computable is
[
G21
, Proposition 2.9]
, but the argument there is incorrect as it is missing the third item in our definition of a

-computable Pimsner Popa basis of

over

. For that reason, we repeat the argument again. Fix a rational point
x
of
M^{\#}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
By our assumptions, we can find rational points

of

y=\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}y_{j}+m_{n+1}E_{N}(m_{n+1}^{*}m_{n+1})y_{n+1}

Then

\left\lVert E_{N}(x)-E_{N}(y)\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert_{2}<2^{-k},

where

Improvements to the main result?

It becomes natural to wonder if any of the conditions in the main theorem are redundant. While we are unable to accomplish this at the moment, we do mention some partial results in this regard.

Given item (2) in Theorem
6.5
above, one may ask whether or not

must be
\bf D
-computable. This is the best we are able to do at this point:
4
4

4

Recall that

Proposition 7.1

-computable, whence

is
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
-computable and a
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
-computable Pimsner-Popa basis of

over

By the equation
[M:N]^{-1}=\inf\big\{\left\lVert E_{N}(x)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2}^{-2}:x>0\big\}
, we can compute a decreasing sequence which converges to

from
\mathbf{D}
.
Thus

is
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
-computable.
∎

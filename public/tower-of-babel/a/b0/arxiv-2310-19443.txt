Asymptotically accurate and locking-free finite element implementation of first order shear deformation theory for plates Journal: Computers and Structures
K. C. Le a,b , H.-G. Bui c

Abstract
A formulation of the asymptotically exact first-order shear deformation theory for linear-elastic homogeneous plates in the rescaled coordinates and rotation angles is considered. This allows the development of its asymptotically accurate and shear-locking-free finite element implementation. As applications, numerical simulations are performed for circular and rectangular plates, showing complete agreement between the analytical solution and the numerical solutions based on two-dimensional theory and three-dimensional elasticity theory.

As far as the authors of this paper are aware, none of the existing studies on this topic has found a simple formulation of the FSDT that is inherently free of the shear-locking effect, regardless of the discretization scheme and integration technique used. The goal of this paper is therefore twofold. First, we give the formulation of the FSDT for plates in the rescaled coordinates and rotation angles. This formulation occurs naturally when the coordinates in the mid-plane are scaled by the plate thickness
h
(thus becoming dimensionless), while the rotation angles are multiplied by
h
, resulting in equal and finite orders of the bending and shear stiffnesses as well as the scaled rotation angles and bending measures.

Finally, Section 5 concludes the paper.

2
Rescaled variational formulation of FSDT for plates

Let
\Omega
be a two-dimensional domain in the
(x_{1},x_{2})
-plane bounded by a piecewise smooth closed curve
\partial\Omega
. We consider a linear elastic homogeneous plate which in the undeformed stress-free state occupies the 3-D region
\mathcal{V}=\Omega\times(-h/2,h/2)
. Its cross section in the plane
(x_{1},x_{3})
is shown in Fig.
1
. We call
\Omega
the plate mid-plane and
h
its thickness.

Figure 1:
Cross section of a plate

Variational principle of the asymptotically exact two-dimensional first-order shear deformation theory (FSDT) for linearly elastic homogeneous plate
[
18
,
29
]
states that the true deflection and rotation angles of the plate minimize the 2-D average energy functional

J[u,\varphi_{\alpha}]=\int_{\Omega}\Bigl\{\frac{\mu h^{3}}{12}[\sigma(\rho_{\alpha\alpha})^{2}+\rho_{\alpha\beta}\rho_{\alpha\beta}]+\frac{5\mu h}{12}\varphi_{\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha}\Bigr\}\differential{a}-\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(fu+\frac{\sigma h^{2}}{10}f\rho_{\alpha\alpha}\Bigr)\differential{a}

(1)

among all deflections
u(x_{1},x_{2})
and rotation angles
\varphi_{\alpha}(x_{1},x_{2})
satisfying the kinematic boundary conditions. Here
\differential a=\differential x_{1}\differential x_{2}
,
\sigma=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+2\mu}=\frac{\nu}{1-\nu}
, with
\lambda
,
\mu
and
\nu
being the Lame constants and Poisson’s ratio, respectively. Note that
\varphi_{\alpha}
are the rotation angles due to pure shear deformation,
\rho_{\alpha\beta}
are the measures of bending and
f
is the external transverse load. They are given by

\begin{split}&\rho_{\alpha\beta}=u_{,\alpha\beta}-\varphi_{(\alpha,\beta)},\\
&f=\tau_{3}|_{x_{3}=h/2}+\tau_{3}|_{x_{3}=-h/2},\end{split}

(2)

with
\tau_{3}
being the normal traction. In the following, we use Greek indices, running from 1 to 2, to refer to the plane coordinates
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
. The comma before an index denotes differentiation with respect to the corresponding coordinate, the parentheses surrounding a pair of indices denote symmetrization operation and summation over repeated indices is understood.

The FE-implementation of this variational problem is not straightforward due to the different magnitudes of the bending and shear stiffnesses. Since the shear stiffness
(5\mu h/12)
is two orders of magnitude larger than the bending stiffness
(\mu h^{3}/12)
with respect to
h
, its multiplication by very small rotation angles leads to a numerical instability called the shear-locking effect when
h
becomes small. To solve this problem, we first try to get rid of the second derivatives in this variational problem. We introduce the new unknown functions

\psi_{\alpha}=-u_{,\alpha}+\varphi_{\alpha}

(3)

which have the meaning of the total angles of rotation of the transverse fibers due to both bending and shear deformation so that

\begin{split}&\rho_{\alpha\beta}=u_{,\alpha\beta}-\varphi_{(\alpha,\beta)}=-\psi_{(\alpha,\beta)},\\
&\varphi_{\alpha}=u_{,\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha}.\end{split}

(4)

In terms of these new unknown functions, the functional becomes

J[u,\psi_{\alpha}]=\int_{\Omega}\Bigl\{\frac{\mu h^{3}}{12}[\sigma(\psi_{\alpha,\alpha})^{2}+\psi_{(\alpha,\beta)}\psi_{(\alpha,\beta)}]+\frac{5\mu h}{12}(u_{,\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha})(u_{,\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha})\Bigr\}\differential{a}\\
-\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(fu-\frac{\sigma h^{2}}{10}f\psi_{\alpha,\alpha}\Bigr)\differential{a}.

(5)

To avoid the shear-locking effect, we try to make the problem independent of
h
so that the orders of magnitude of the bending and shear stiffnesses become the same. For this purpose, we introduce the rescaled coordinates and rotation angles as follows

\bar{x}_{\alpha}=\frac{x_{\alpha}}{h},\quad\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha}}=h\psi_{\alpha}.

(6)

Note that
\bar{x}_{\alpha}
are dimensionless, while
\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha}}
have the dimension of length and can be interpreted as the longitudinal displacements of the positive face surface of the plate
[
18
,
21
]
(see Fig. 1). Since

\begin{split}&\rho_{\alpha\beta}=-\psi_{(\alpha,\beta)}=-\frac{1}{h^{2}}\bar{\psi}_{(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta})},\\
&u_{,\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{h}(u_{,\bar{\alpha}}+\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha}}),\\
&\differential{a}=\differential{x_1}\differential{x_2}=h^{2}\differential{\bar{x}_1}\differential{\bar{x}_2}=h^{2}\differential{\bar{a}},\end{split}

(7)

we reduce the energy functional to

J[u,\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha}}]=h\int_{\bar{\Omega}}\Bigl\{\frac{\mu}{12}[\sigma(\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}})^{2}+\bar{\psi}_{(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta})}\bar{\psi}_{(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta})}]+\frac{5\mu}{12}(u_{,\bar{\alpha}}+\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha}})(u_{,\bar{\alpha}}+\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha}})\Bigr\}\differential{\bar{a}}\\
-\int_{\bar{\Omega}}h^{2}\Bigl(fu-\frac{\sigma}{10}f\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}}\Bigr)\differential{\bar{a}}.

(8)

Here
\bar{\Omega}=\{(\bar{x}_{1},\bar{x}_{2})\,|\,(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega\}
denotes the rescaled 2-D domain, and components of vectors and their derivatives having Greek indices with bar are related to the rescaled coordinates
\bar{x}_{\alpha}
. We can further simplify this functional by dividing it by
\mu h
. Then the minimization problem reduces to

\bar{J}[u,\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha}}]=\int_{\bar{\Omega}}\Bigl\{\frac{1}{12}[\sigma(\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}})^{2}+\bar{\psi}_{(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta})}\bar{\psi}_{(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta})}]+\frac{5}{12}(u_{,\bar{\alpha}}+\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha}})(u_{,\bar{\alpha}}+\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha}})\Bigr\}\differential{\bar{a}}\\
-\int_{\bar{\Omega}}\Bigl(\bar{f}u-\frac{\sigma}{10}\bar{f}\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}}\Bigr)\differential{\bar{a}}\rightarrow\min_{u,\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha}}},

(9)

where

\bar{f}=\frac{hf}{\mu}.

(10)

Thus,
\bar{f}
is equal to the plate thickness
h
times the characteristic strain
\varepsilon=f/\mu
, and since both the bending and shear stiffness in the rescaled functional (
9
) have the order of unity, the minimizer must be of the same order as
\bar{f}
multiplied by a function depending on the characteristic size of
\bar{\Omega}
. Returning to the original functions, we see that the rotation angles
\psi_{\alpha}
have the order of the characteristic strain (
\varphi_{\alpha}
are even much smaller
[
28
,
29
]
), while the bending measures
\rho_{\alpha\beta}
have the order of the characteristic strain divided by
h
, as discussed in the introduction. In this way, the rescaled problem (
9
) provides an elegant and effective way to avoid the shear-locking effect. Note that the same rescaled formulation can be applied to the FSDT for anisotropic plates, since both the kinematic formulas (
3
)-(
4
) and the orders of the bending and shear stiffnesses with respect to
h
remain unchanged compared to the isotropic plates.

To solve problem (
9
) we need to pose the boundary conditions. If the part of the plate’s edge,
\bar{\partial}_{k}
, is clamped, the admissible functions must satisfy the following “hard” kinematical conditions

u=0,\quad\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha}}=0\quad\text{at $\bar{\partial}_{k}$}.

(11)

If the remaining part of the plate’s edge,
\bar{\partial}_{s}
, is simply supported, then only the kinematical condition
u=0
should be fulfilled, while
\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha}}
can be varied arbitrarily.
3
3

3

This is the so-called “soft” boundary conditions for simply supported edge. About other boundary conditions for FSDT see
[
48
]
.
Finally, if the remaining part
\bar{\partial}_{s}
of the plate’s edge is free, then no constraints are imposed on
u
and
\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha}}
there. After finding the solution, we want also to check the asymptotic accuracy of the theory. For this purpose, we need to compute the true average displacement of the plate by adding the correction term to the previously determined deflection
[
18
]

\check{u}=u+\frac{h^{2}\sigma}{60}\rho_{\alpha\alpha}=u-\frac{\sigma}{60}\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}}.

(12)

The determination of
\varphi_{\alpha}
also requires the first derivatives of
u
. Therefore we would like to have the solution of (
9
) such that
u
and
\bar{\psi}_{\bar{\alpha}}
are both
C^{1}
-functions. To check the asymptotic accuracy we must compare functions
\check{u}
and
\check{\psi}_{\alpha}
found by the 2-D FSDT with

\langle w_{3}(x_{\alpha},x_{3})\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{h}\int_{-h/2}^{h/2}w_{3}(x_{\alpha},x_{3})\differential{x_3}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle w_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha},x_{3})x_{3}\rangle/(h^{2}/12),

(13)

where
w_{i}(x_{\alpha},x_{3})
are the displacements computed by the 3-D exact theory of elasticity. If they agree with the accuracy up to
h^{2}/l^{2}
, then the asymptotic accuracy of our FE-implementation is guaranteed.

3
Finite element implementation

3.1
Weak and strong formulations

Since we will only deal with rescaled coordinates and rotation angles in this Section and in the analytical parts of the next Section, we briefly omit all bars in the functional (
9
). Calculating the first variation of (
9
) and equating it to zero, we obtain the following necessary condition for the minimum

\variation{J}=\int_{\Omega}\Bigl\{\frac{1}{6}[\sigma\psi_{\alpha,\alpha}\variation{\psi}_{\beta,\beta}+\psi_{(\alpha,\beta)}\variation{\psi}_{(\alpha,\beta)}]+\frac{5}{6}(u_{,\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha})(\variation{u}_{,\alpha}+\variation{\psi}_{\alpha})\Bigr\}\differential{a}\\
-\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(f\variation{u}-\frac{\sigma}{10}f\variation{\psi}_{\alpha,\alpha}\Bigr)\differential{a}=0.

(14)

Introducing the notations

\begin{split}&\variation{W}^{b}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{6}[\sigma\psi_{\alpha,\alpha}\variation{\psi}_{\beta,\beta}+\psi_{(\alpha,\beta)}\variation{\psi}_{(\alpha,\beta)}]\differential{a},\\
&\variation{W}^{s}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{5}{6}(u_{,\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha})(\variation{u}_{,\alpha}+\variation{\psi}_{\alpha})\differential{a},\\
&\variation{W}^{e}=\int_{\Omega}(f\variation{u}-\frac{\sigma}{10}f\variation{\psi}_{\alpha,\alpha})\differential{a},\end{split}

(15)

for the variations of the bending energy, shear energy, and the work of external forces, respectively, we recast Eq. (
14
) in the form

\variation{W}^{b}+\variation{W}^{s}=\variation{W}^{e}.

(16)

Denote the space of admissible functions as
\mathcal{K}=\{(v,\chi_{\alpha})\,|\,(v,\chi_{\alpha})|_{\partial_{k}}=0\}
. The weak formulation of the problem is stated as follows: Given
f
, find
(u,\psi_{\alpha})\in\mathcal{K}
such that Eq. (
16
) is satisfied for all
(\variation{u},\variation{\psi}_{\alpha})\in\mathcal{K}
. For the integrals in (
15
) to be meaningful,
v
and
\chi_{\alpha}
must belong at least to the Sobolev’s space of square integrable functions with the square integrable first derivatives,
H^{1}(\Omega)
. However, since the desired asymptotic accuracy of FSDT may require higher smoothness of
u
and
\psi_{\alpha}
, the continuity assumption in
\mathcal{K}
still remains unspecified.

Although not relevant for the FE-implementation, the strong formulation of the problem is also stated for the sake of completeness. Under the assumptions that
u
and
\psi_{\alpha}
are doubly differentiable, we apply the partial integration to Eq. (
14
) and put it in the form

\variation{J}=\int_{\Omega}\Bigl\{\Bigl[-\frac{\sigma}{6}\psi_{\beta,\beta\alpha}-\frac{1}{6}\psi_{(\alpha,\beta)\beta}\Bigr]\variation{\psi}_{\alpha}-\frac{5}{6}(u_{,\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha})_{,\alpha}\variation{u}+\frac{5}{6}(u_{,\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha})\variation{\psi}_{\alpha}\Bigr\}\differential{a}\\
-\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(f\variation{u}+\frac{\sigma}{10}f_{,\alpha}\variation{\psi}_{\alpha}\Bigr)\differential{a}+\int_{\partial_{s}}\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{\sigma}{6}\psi_{\beta,\beta}n_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{6}\psi_{(\alpha,\beta)}n_{\beta}\Bigr)\variation{\psi}_{\alpha}\\
+\frac{5}{6}(u_{,\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha})n_{\alpha}\variation{u}\Bigr]\differential{s}+\int_{\partial_{s}}\frac{\sigma}{10}fn_{\alpha}\variation{\psi}_{\alpha}\differential{s}=0,

(17)

where
n_{\alpha}
is the unit normal outward to the boundary and
\differential s
is the length element. It is assumed here that the remaining part
\partial_{s}
of the plate edge is free. Because of the arbitrariness of the variations
\variation{\psi}_{\alpha}
and
\variation{u}
in
\Omega
and on
\partial_{s}
, from (
17
) follow the second order partial differential equations

\begin{split}&-\frac{\sigma+1}{6}\psi_{\beta,\beta\alpha}-\frac{1}{6}\psi_{\alpha,\beta\beta}+\frac{5}{6}(u_{,\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha})=\frac{\sigma}{10}f_{,\alpha},\\
&-\frac{5}{6}(u_{,\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha})_{,\alpha}=f,\end{split}

(18)

the kinematic boundary conditions (
11
) on
\partial_{k}
, and the natural boundary conditions

\begin{split}&\frac{\sigma}{6}\psi_{\beta,\beta}n_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{6}\psi_{(\alpha,\beta)}n_{\beta}+\frac{5}{6}(u_{,\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha})=-\frac{\sigma}{10}fn_{\alpha},\\
&\frac{5}{6}(u_{,\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha})n_{\alpha}=0\end{split}

(19)

on
\partial_{s}
. Eqs. (
18
), (
11
), and (
19
) constitute the strong formulation of the problem.

3.2
Discretization

To discretize Eq. (
16
), interpolation spaces for displacement and rotation angles are required. In the following, a general interpolation scheme is used without regard to specific requirements on the order and continuity of the function space as mentioned above. In this sense,
u
and
\psi_{\alpha}
(
\alpha=1,2
) are considered as primary variables. Their interpolation is

u=(\mathbf{N}^{u})^{T}\mathbf{u},\quad\psi_{\alpha}=(\mathbf{N}^{\psi})^{T}{\bf\it\psi}_{\alpha},

(20)

where
\mathbf{N}^{u}
and
\mathbf{N}^{\psi}
are column vectors representing the shape functions for
u
and
\psi_{\alpha}
, respectively, while
\mathbf{u}
and
{\bf\it\psi}_{\alpha}
are deflection and rotation angle vectors, respectively. The vector notation is in column ordering unless otherwise stated.

Based on Eq. (
20
) we find the variation of the primary variables

\variation{u}=\variation{\vb{u}}^{T}\mathbf{N}^{u},\quad\variation{\psi}_{\alpha}=\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{\alpha}\mathbf{N}^{\psi},

(21)

and their derivatives

\begin{split}&\variation{u}_{,\alpha}=\variation{\vb{u}}^{T}\mathbf{N}^{u}_{,\alpha},\\
&\variation{\psi}_{\alpha,\alpha}=\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{\alpha}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,\alpha}=\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{1}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1}+\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{2}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2},\\
&\variation{\psi}_{(\alpha,\beta)}=\frac{1}{2}(\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{\alpha}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,\beta}+\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{\beta}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,\alpha}).\end{split}

(22)

Substituting Eqs. (
21

15

\begin{split}\variation{W}^{b}&=\frac{1}{6}\int_{\Omega}[\sigma(\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{1}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1}+\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{2}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2})(\psi_{1,1}+\psi_{2,2})+\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{1}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1}\psi_{1,1}\\
&+\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{2}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2}\psi_{2,2}+(\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{1}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2}+\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{2}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1})\psi_{(1,2)}]\differential{a},\\
\variation{W}^{s}&=\frac{5}{6}\int_{\Omega}[(\variation{\vb{u}}^{T}\mathbf{N}^{u}_{,1}+\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{1}\mathbf{N}^{\psi})(u_{,1}+\psi_{1})\\
&+(\variation{\vb{u}}^{T}\mathbf{N}^{u}_{,2}+\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{2}\mathbf{N}^{\psi})(u_{,2}+\psi_{2})]\differential{a},\\
\variation{W}^{e}&=\int_{\Omega}[\variation{\vb{u}}^{T}\mathbf{N}^{u}f-\frac{\sigma}{10}(\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{1}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1}+\variation{\vb*{\psi}}^{T}_{2}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2})f]\differential{a}.\end{split}

(23)

Consequently, the residual forces with respect to
u
and
\psi_{\alpha}

\begin{split}\mathbf{R}^{u}&=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{N}^{u}f\differential{a}-\frac{5}{6}\int_{\Omega}[\mathbf{N}^{u}_{,1}(u_{,1}+\psi_{1})+\mathbf{N}^{u}_{,2}(u_{,2}+\psi_{2})]\differential{a},\\
\mathbf{R}^{\psi_{1}}&=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\sigma}{10}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1}f\differential{a}-\frac{5}{6}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}(u_{,1}+\psi_{1})\differential{a}\\
&-\frac{1}{6}\int_{\Omega}[\sigma\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1}(\psi_{1,1}+\psi_{2,2})+\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1}\psi_{1,1}+\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2}\psi_{(1,2)}]\differential{a},\\
\mathbf{R}^{\psi_{2}}&=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\sigma}{10}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2}f\differential{a}-\frac{5}{6}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}(u_{,2}+\psi_{2})\differential{a}\\
&-\frac{1}{6}\int_{\Omega}[\sigma\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2}(\psi_{1,1}+\psi_{2,2})+\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2}\psi_{2,2}+\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1}\psi_{(1,2)}]\differential{a}.\end{split}

(24)

Taking the directional derivatives of Eqs. (

\begin{split}\mathbf{K}^{uu}&=\frac{5}{6}\int_{\Omega}\left[\mathbf{N}^{u}_{,1}(\mathbf{N}^{u}_{,1})^{T}+\mathbf{N}^{u}_{,2}(\mathbf{N}^{u}_{,2})^{T}\right]\differential{a},\\

\mathbf{K}^{u\psi_{2}}&=\frac{5}{6}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2}(\mathbf{N}^{\psi})^{T}\differential{a},\end{split}

(25)

\begin{split}\mathbf{K}^{\psi_{1}u}&=\frac{5}{6}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}(\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1})^{T}\differential{a},\\
\mathbf{K}^{\psi_{1}\psi_{1}}&=\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[\frac{5}{6}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}(\mathbf{N}^{\psi})^{T}+\frac{\sigma+1}{6}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1}(\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1})^{T}+\frac{1}{12}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2}(\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2})^{T}\Bigr]\differential{a},\\
\mathbf{K}^{\psi_{1}\psi_{2}}&=\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[\frac{\sigma}{6}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1}(\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2})^{T}+\frac{1}{12}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2}(\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1})^{T}\Bigr]\differential{a},\end{split}

(26)

\begin{split}\mathbf{K}^{\psi_{2}u}&=\frac{5}{6}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}(\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2})^{T}\differential{a},\\
\mathbf{K}^{\psi_{2}\psi_{1}}&=\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[\frac{\sigma}{6}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2}(\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1})^{T}+\frac{1}{12}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1}(\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2})^{T}\Bigr]\differential{a},\\
\mathbf{K}^{\psi_{2}\psi_{2}}&=\int_{\Omega}\Bigl[\frac{5}{6}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}(\mathbf{N}^{\psi})^{T}+\frac{\sigma+1}{6}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2}(\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,2})^{T}+\frac{1}{12}\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1}(\mathbf{N}^{\psi}_{,1})^{T}\Bigr]\differential{a}.\end{split}

(27)

The final form of the discretized linear system reads

\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{K}^{uu}&\mathbf{K}^{u\psi_{1}}&\mathbf{K}^{u\psi_{2}}\\
\mathbf{K}^{\psi_{1}u}&\mathbf{K}^{\psi_{1}\psi_{1}}&\mathbf{K}^{\psi_{1}\psi_{2}}\\
\mathbf{K}^{\psi_{2}u}&\mathbf{K}^{\psi_{2}\psi_{1}}&\mathbf{K}^{\psi_{2}\psi_{2}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{u}\\
{\bf\it\psi}_{1}\\
{\bf\it\psi}_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{F}^{u}\\
\mathbf{F}^{\psi_{1}}\\
\mathbf{F}^{\psi_{2}}\end{pmatrix},

(28)

with the elements of the stiffness matrix being the blocks given in Eqs. (
25
)-(
27
), and
\mathbf{F}^{u}
,
\mathbf{F}^{\psi_{1}}
,
\mathbf{F}^{\psi_{2}}
the first terms in (
24
) representing the external forces.

3.3
Isogeometric analysis

The weak form (
17
) contains the first order derivatives of deflection and rotation angles and their variations and is therefore meaningful only if
(u,\psi_{\alpha})
and
(\variation{u},\variation{\psi}_{\alpha})
belong to the Sobolev space
H^{1}(\Omega)
. However, as mentioned in Section 2, for the desired asymptotic accuracy, the discretization space must be at least
C^{1}
to ensure continuity and smoothness of the solution. In this paper, we use isogeometric analysis with the non-uniform rational
B-splines (NURBS) shape function, which guarantees
C^{1}
-continuity
[
49
]
. This approach also allows to facilitate the refinement of the mesh and the elevation of the discretization order if necessary.

In the context of geometric modeling, a plate can be represented by NURBS surface patches, in which each patch can be described as a tensor product of two univariate B-splines such as

\mathbf{S}\left(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}N_{i}^{p}\left(\xi_{1}\right)N_{j}^{q}\left(\xi_{2}\right)\mathbf{P}_{ij}.

(29)

In Eq. (
29
), the univariate B-spline basis function of order
p
and
q
, denoted as
N_{i}^{p}
and
N_{j}^{q}
, is computed via recursive Cox-de-Boor formula

N_{i}^{p}(\xi)=\dfrac{\xi-\xi_{i}}{\xi_{i+p}-\xi_{i}}N^{p-1}_{i}(\xi)+\dfrac{\xi_{i+p+1}-\xi_{i}}{\xi_{i+p+1}-\xi_{i+1}}N^{p-1}_{i+1}(\xi),\;N_{i}^{0}(\xi)=\begin{cases}1&\xi_{i}\leq\xi\leq\xi_{i+1}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(30)

We use
\{\mathbf{P}_{ij}\}_{0\leq i\leq m,0\leq j\leq n}
to denote the control point grid. The definition of the control point in homogeneous coordinates will facilitate the construction of the NURBS patch. A surface can comprise a single or multiple patches that are connected at the patch interface. In the latter case, the concept is called multipatch analysis. In the typical scenario, the patch information matches at the interface, including the parametric (knot) information and the location of the control points, the multipatch is naturally connected and the
C^{0}
continuity is guaranteed on the patch interfaces, i.e. strong coupling. However, patch-wise parametric matching is not the necessary condition. There are various methods to maintain the patch continuity in a NURBS multipatch, such as the penalty method
[
50
]
, Nitsche method
[
51
]
or bending strip method
[
52
]
. These methods impose weak coupling condition and are more sophisticated to implement.

The design of the B-spline surface allows different interpolation orders in each parametric direction. This feature is particularly useful for analyses of materials with anisotropic behavior where accuracy needs to be improved in a particular direction. In addition, rescaling of the mesh can be conveniently performed by rescaling the control point coordinates. This eliminates the need for remeshing.

The NURBS patches in a multipatch structure are macro elements where the shape function needs the full knot vectors for values and derivatives evaluation. This on one hand prevents the possibility of applying parallelization algorithms to the numerical code, on the other hand it makes the element matrices very dense, which reduces the performance of the sparse direct solver. Bézier decomposition algorithm
[
53
]
is proposed to retain the local characteristic of the finite element. In essence, the Bézier decomposition strategy constructs a local operator for each knot span (in 1D) or knot cell (in 2D and 3D) so that the shape function within the cell can be evaluated independently with the knot vectors. The support domain, i.e. control points influenced by the cell, of each cell and the number of shape functions required for each cell is reduced to
p+1
in each parametric direction. This eliminates the global access to patch-wise data and allows a typical finite element code to work seamlessly with NURBS elements.

4.1
Circular plate under uniform transverse load

As a first test problem, we apply the developed finite element code to the numerical simulation of the axisymmetric bending of a circular plate clamped at the edge and subjected to a constant transverse load
f
. We chose this 2-D problem because it admits an analytical solution, so we can directly compare the numerical solution with it to test the former’s divergence and investigate the optimal choice of interpolation order. As the bending is axisymmetric, we must have in the polar coordinates
(r,\theta)
the vanishing rotation angle
\psi_{\theta}=0
, while
u
and
\psi_{r}
are functions of
r
only. Consequently,

\rho_{rr}=-\psi_{r,r},\quad\rho_{\theta\theta}=-\frac{1}{r}\psi_{r},

(31)

while all other components of
\rho_{\alpha\beta}
vanish. It is easy to show that the problem (
9
) reduces to minimizing the following functional

I[u,\psi_{r}]=\int_{0}^{R}\Bigl\{\frac{1}{12}[\sigma(\psi_{r,r}+\frac{1}{r}\psi_{r})^{2}+(\psi_{r,r})^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}(\psi_{r})^{2}]+\frac{5}{12}(u_{,r}+\psi_{r})^{2}\Bigr\}r\differential{r}\\
-\int_{0}^{R}\Bigl[fu-\frac{\sigma}{10}f\Bigl(\psi_{r,r}+\frac{1}{r}\psi_{r}\Bigr)\Bigr]r\differential{r}\rightarrow\min_{u(R)=\psi_{r}(R)=0},

(32)

where
R
is the dimensionless radius of the plate and all bars are still dropped for short. The standard calculus of variations shows that the minimizer must satisfy the equation

\begin{split}&-\frac{\sigma}{6}\Bigl[(\psi_{r,r}+\frac{1}{r}\psi_{r})r\Bigr]_{,r}+\frac{\sigma}{6}(\psi_{r,r}+\frac{1}{r}\psi_{r})-\frac{1}{6}(\psi_{r,r}r)_{,r}\\
&+\frac{1}{6r}\psi_{r}+\frac{5}{6}(u_{,r}+\psi_{r})r=0,\\
&-\frac{5}{6}\left[(u_{,r}+\psi_{r})r\right]_{,r}=fr,\end{split}

(33)

subjected to the clamped boundary conditions
u(R)=\psi_{r}(R)=0
. Integrating the second equation of (
33
) and using the fact that
\varphi=u_{,r}+\psi_{r}
cannot be singular at
r=0
, we find that

\varphi(r)=u_{,r}+\psi_{r}=-\frac{3}{5}fr.

(34)

Substituting (
34
) into the first of (
33
), we reduce it to

\psi_{r,rr}+\frac{1}{r}\psi_{r,r}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\psi_{r}=-\frac{3}{\sigma+1}fr.

(35)

Integrating this equation and using the boundary condition
\psi_{r}(R)=0
as well as the non-singularity of
\psi
at
r=0
, we get

\psi_{r}(r)=\frac{3}{8(\sigma+1)}f(R^{2}r-r^{3})=\frac{3(1-\nu)}{8}f(R^{2}r-r^{3}).

(36)

Now, the deflection
u
can be found from

u_{,r}=-\frac{3}{5}fr-\psi_{r}=-\frac{3}{5}fr-\frac{3}{8(\sigma+1)}f(R^{2}r-r^{3}).

(37)

Integrating this equation and using the boundary condition
u(R)=0
, we obtain finally (cf.
[
55
,
56
]
)

u(r)=\frac{3}{10}f(R^{2}-r^{2})+\frac{3}{32(\sigma+1)}f(R^{2}-r^{2})^{2}=\frac{3}{10}f(R^{2}-r^{2})+\frac{3(1-\nu)}{32}f(R^{2}-r^{2})^{2}.

(38)

Note that the deflection according to Kirchhoff’s plate theory reads
[
57
]

u_{K}(r)=\frac{3(1-\nu)}{32}f(R^{2}-r^{2})^{2}.

(39)

while the angle of rotation,
-u_{K,r}
, coincides with (
36
).

If we have the simply supported edge instead of the clamped edge, then the deflection becomes

u(r)=\frac{3}{10(1+\nu)}f(R^{2}-r^{2})+\frac{3(1-\nu)}{32}f(R^{2}-r^{2})\Bigl(\frac{5+\nu}{1+\nu}R^{2}-r^{2}\Bigr).

(40)

Since the solution procedure is similar to the previous case, we omit it here for brevity. Note that the second term on the right-hand side of (
40
) represents the deflection according to Kirchhoff’s plate theory
[
57
]
.

4.2
Rectangular plate under uniform transverse load

For the second test problem, we apply the developed finite element code to the numerical simulation of the bending of a rectangular plate subjected to a constant load
f
. We assume that
\Omega
occupies the domain
(0,L)\times(-D/2,D/2)
of the
(x_{1},x_{2})
-plane. In the first case study we let the left edge at
x_{1}=0
be clamped while the three remaining edges of the plate be free (see Fig.
7
(left)).

Figure 7:
Numerical example 4.2(a) (Rectangular plate with one clamped edge and three free edges): Geometry and boundary condition for (left) the analysis using FSDT plate element and (right) the 2D analysis using solid element.

When the depth of the rectangular plate,
D
, becomes large, the solution must exhibit the plane strain state due to the almost translational invariance in the
x_{2}
-direction. For FSDT in the rescaled formulation (
9
), this means that the 2-D problem reduces to the following 1-D variational problem: Minimize the energy functional of the beam-like model

I[u,\psi]=\int_{0}^{L}\Bigl[\frac{1}{2}E_{b}(\psi_{,x})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}E_{s}(u_{,x}+\psi)^{2}\Bigr]\differential{x}\\
-\int_{0}^{L}\Bigl(fu-\frac{\sigma}{10}f\psi_{,x}\Bigr)\differential{x}\rightarrow\min_{u(0)=\psi(0)=0},

(42)

where the bars in this theoretical part
are
again dropped for short,
x\equiv x_{1}
and

E_{b}=\frac{\sigma+1}{6}=\frac{1}{6(1-\nu)},\quad E_{s}=\frac{5}{6}.

(43)

The vanishing first variation leads to the differential equations

\begin{split}&-E_{b}\psi_{,xx}+E_{s}(u_{,x}+\psi)=0,\\
&-E_{s}(u_{,x}+\psi)_{,x}=0,\end{split}

(44)

which are subjected to the boundary conditions

\begin{split}&u(0)=\psi(0)=0,\\
&E_{b}\psi_{,x}(L)=-\frac{\sigma}{10}f,\quad E_{s}(u_{,x}+\psi)|_{x=L}=0.\end{split}

(45)

From the first equation of (
44
) follows

u_{,x}+\psi=\varphi=\frac{E_{b}}{E_{s}}\psi_{,xx}.

(46)

Substituting this into the second equation yield

E_{b}\psi_{,xxx}=-f.

(47)

Integrating Eq. (
47
) and using the boundary conditions, we obtain

\psi(x)=\frac{f}{E_{b}}\Bigl[-\frac{1}{6}x^{3}+\frac{1}{2}Lx^{2}-(\frac{1}{2}L^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{10})x\Bigr].

(48)

The deflection can be found from the equation

u_{,x}=\varphi-\psi=-\psi+\frac{E_{b}}{E_{s}}\psi_{,xx},

(49)

which, together with the boundary condition
u(0)=0
, yields

u(x)=\frac{f}{E_{b}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{24}x^{4}-\frac{1}{6}Lx^{3}+(\frac{1}{4}L^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{20})x^{2}\Bigr]+\frac{f}{E_{s}}(-\frac{x^{2}}{2}+Lx).

(50)

However, this is still not the true average transverse displacement of the plate. The latter should be computed in accordance with Eq. (
12
) giving

\check{u}(x)=\frac{f}{E_{b}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{24}x^{4}-\frac{1}{6}Lx^{3}+(\frac{1}{4}L^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{20})x^{2}\Bigr]+\frac{f}{E_{s}}(-\frac{x^{2}}{2}+Lx)\\
-\frac{\sigma}{60}\frac{f}{E_{b}}\Bigl(-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+Lx-\frac{1}{2}L^{2}-\frac{\sigma}{10}\Bigr).

(51)

Note that the average transverse displacement according to the classical Kirchhoff’s plate theory in this beam-like model is given by

u_{K}(x)=\frac{f}{E_{b}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{24}x^{4}-\frac{1}{6}Lx^{3}+\frac{1}{4}L^{2}x^{2}\Bigr),

(52)

while the angle of rotation reads

\psi_{K}(x)=-u_{K,x}=-\frac{f}{E_{b}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{6}x^{3}-\frac{1}{2}Lx^{2}+\frac{1}{2}L^{2}x\Bigr).

(53)

The presence of the analytical solution given by Eqs. (
48
)-(
51
) for this particular case allows a comparison with the solution of the 2-D FSDT and with the solution of the exact elasticity theory. On one hand, the comparison with the solution of the 2-D FSDT can show that our FE-code is free of shear-locking and allows us to check its convergence and efficiency as
D
becomes large. On the other hand, the comparison with the solution of the exact elasticity theory allows us to verify the asymptotic accuracy of our FE-code. To solve this problem within the exact elasticity theory, we use the geometry and boundary condition shown in Fig.
7
(right). The normal traction is applied to the top and bottom of the plate, as shown in this Figure, to avoid its elongation and at the same time maintain the bending combined with a pure shear
[
18
]
. Under the plane strain condition the problem becomes 2-D, so we can employ 2-D solid elements. Having the numerical solution within the plane strain elasticity theory, the average transverse displacement and rotation angles over the thickness are evaluated using Eq. (
13
). Within 2-D FSDT the deflection and rotation angles are evaluated along the centerline
x_{2}=0
. Then the true average transverse displacement
\check{u}
is computed in accordance with (
12
).

Both the plate analysis and the elastic solid analysis employ the cubic order NURBS discretization with sufficiently fine mesh. Using again the bars for the recalled quantities, we show the results of numerical simulation in Fig.

\bar{L}=3

This also confirms that our rescaled formulation of the FSDT is inherently shear-locking-free and that the FE-code is efficient. Second, the solution of the 2-D FSDT approximates the solution of the exact elasticity theory much better than that of the classical Kirchhoff plate theory. For large
\bar{L}

\bar{L}

\bar{x}_{1}=0
(cf. Eq. (
51

[
18

. Far from the edge, this correction term of order

compared to unity is essential for ensuring the asymptotic accuracy of the FSDT up to that order. Another detail worth mentioning is that the elastic 2-D analysis does not use an approximation, but evaluates the solution fields in a brute force method with a very fine mesh. The average transverse displacements and rotation angles obtained by calculating integrals (
13
) over the thickness in a post-processing represent only the integral characteristics of the FSDT. The comparison between the detailed distributions of displacements across the thickness is left for future investigations.

(55)

\varphi(x)=\frac{f}{E_{s}}\Bigl(-x+\frac{1}{2}L\Bigr).

5
Conclusion

In this work, two advancements were made compared to the available approaches: (i) the inherently shear-locking free formulation of the asymptotically exact FSDT for homogeneous elastic plates in the rescaled coordinates and rotation angles was found, (ii) the asymptotic accuracy of the isogeometric FE-analysis that is computationally efficient and free of high-order interpolation schemes and/or sophisticated integration techniques has been achieved. The requirement that the deflection and rotation angles belong to the
C^{1}
function space is only necessary to achieve asymptotic accuracy but is not relevant to the shear-locking effect, which is absent in this formulation. Since our main focus was on the inherently shear-locking-free formulation and the asymptotic accuracy of its FE-implementation, the simplest linear FSDT for homogeneous elastic plates was chosen intentionally. Once understood, the results obtained in this paper can be extended in many directions. Among others, we will develop FE-implementation based on the inherently shear-locking-free formulation of the asymptotically exact: (i) linear FSDT for homogeneous elastic shells, (ii) linear FSDT for FG-plates and shells, (iii) dynamic FSDT for FG-plates and shells, (iv) nonlinear FSDT for FG-plates and shells with application to buckling analysis. The asymptotically accurate and shear-locking free finite element implementation of the linear FSDT for homogeneous elastic shells will be addressed in our next publication.

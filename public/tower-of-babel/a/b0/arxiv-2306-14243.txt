Introduction

In 1921, Emmy Noether revolutionized Commutative Algebra by establishing the primary decomposition Theorem for Noetherian rings
[
30

I

\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for
k\gg 0
large enough? In 1976, it was predicted by Ratliff
[
31

\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
stabilizes. That is, there exists

\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k+1})=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all

. Another remarkable result of Brodmann says that
\operatorname{depth}(R/I^{k})
is constant for
k\gg 0

[
4
]
. Suppose furthermore that
I
is a graded ideal of a standard graded polynomial ring
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
with coefficients in a field

[

Now, let

Theorem
1.1

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. The following hold.

(a)

If
\mathcal{G}=\{\overline{g_{1}},\dots,\overline{g_{r}}\}

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)=\min\{\deg(g_{i})\ :\ 1\leq i\leq r\ \textit{and}\ (I:g_{i})=\mathfrak{p}\}.

(b)

\operatorname{v}(I)=\min\{\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I):\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\}
.

(c)

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\geq\alpha((I:\mathfrak{p})/I)
, with equality if
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Max}(I)
.

(d)

If
I
has no embedded primes, then
\operatorname{v}(I)=\min\{\alpha((I:\mathfrak{p})/I):\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\}
.

Firstly, we determine the “local” numbers

\operatorname{v}(I)=\min\{\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I):\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\}
.

Let

A priori, it is not clear that

1.
How to compute the
\operatorname{v}
-number of a graded ideal?

Let
I
be an ideal of a Noetherian domain
R
. We denote the set of associated primes of
I
by
\operatorname{Ass}(I)
, and by
\operatorname{Max}(I)
the set of associated primes of
I
that are maximal with respect to the inclusion. It is clear that
I
has no embedded primes if and only if
\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\operatorname{Max}(I)
.

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]=\bigoplus_{d}S_{d}
be the standard graded polynomial ring with
n
variables and coefficients in a field
K
, and let
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be the graded maximal ideal. The concept of
\operatorname{v}
-number was introduced by Cooper
et all
in
[
8
]
. Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. Then, the
\operatorname{v}
-number of
I
at
\mathfrak{p}
is defined as

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\ =\ \min\{d\ :\ \textit{there exists}\ f\in S_{d}\ \textit{such that}\ (I:f)=\mathfrak{p}\}.

Whereas, the
\operatorname{v}
-number of
I
is defined as

\operatorname{v}(I)\ =\ \min\{d\ :\ \textit{there exists}\ f\in S_{d}\ \textit{such that}\ (I:f)\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\}.

Note that if
I=\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, then
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)=\operatorname{v}(I)=0
.

The following result due to Grisalde, Reyes and Villarreal
[
23
, Theorem 3.2]
shows how to compute the
\operatorname{v}
-number of a graded ideal. For a finitely generated graded
S
-module
M=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}M_{d}\neq 0
, we call
\alpha(M)=\min\{d:M_{d}\neq 0\}
the
initial degree
of
M
. In the next theorem, the bar
\overline{\phantom{p}}
denotes the residue class modulo
I
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. The following hold.

(a)

If
\mathcal{G}=\{\overline{g_{1}},\dots,\overline{g_{r}}\}
is a homogeneous minimal generating set of
(I:\mathfrak{p})/I
, then

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)=\min\{\deg(g_{i})\ :\ 1\leq i\leq r\ \textit{and}\ (I:g_{i})=\mathfrak{p}\}.

(b)

\operatorname{v}(I)=\min\{\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I):\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\}
.

(c)

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\geq\alpha((I:\mathfrak{p})/I)
, with equality if
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Max}(I)
.

(d)

If
I
has no embedded primes, then
\operatorname{v}(I)=\min\{\alpha((I:\mathfrak{p})/I):\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\}
.

2.
Asymptotic growth of the modules
(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian domain and
I\subset R
an ideal. It is known by Brodmann
[
3
]
that
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
stabilizes for large
k
. That is,
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k+1})=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
. A prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset R
such that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
, is called a
stable prime of
I
.

The set of the stable primes of
I
is denoted by
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Likewise,
\operatorname{Max}^{\infty}(I)
denotes the set of stable primes of
I
, maximal with respect to the inclusion. The least integer
k_{0}
such that
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k_{0}})
for all
k\geq k_{0}
is denoted by
\operatorname{astab}(I)
.

Now, let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring, with
K
a field, and unique graded maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. In light of Theorem
1.1
and Brodmann result, to understand the asymptotic behaviour of the function
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
, one has to understand the asymptotic growth of the modules
(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}
for
k\gg 0
.

Let
M\neq 0
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module. Let
\omega(M)
be the highest degree of a homogeneous element of the
K
-vector space
M/\mathfrak{m}M
. Equivalently, the highest degree
j
such that the graded Betti number
\beta_{0,j}(M)
is non-zero. Thus

\omega(M)=\max\{d:\beta_{0,d}(M)\neq 0\}=\max\{d:\operatorname{Tor}^{S}_{0}(S/\mathfrak{m},M)_{d}\neq 0\}.

Similarly, one has that
\alpha(M)=\min\{d:\operatorname{Tor}^{S}_{0}(S/\mathfrak{m},M)_{d}\neq 0\}
.

The following theorem provides natural asymptotic upper and lower bounds for the
\operatorname{v}
-function
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
which are linear functions in
k
, for
k\gg 0
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a graded ideal, and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Then, the following holds.

(a)

For all
k\geq 1
, we have

\alpha((I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k})\ \leq\ \operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})\ \leq\ \omega((I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}).

(b)

The functions
\alpha((I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k})
,
\omega((I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k})
are linear in
k
for
k\gg 0
.

(c)

There exist eventually linear functions
f(k)
and
g(k)
such that

f(k)\leq\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\leq g(k),\ \ \textit{for all}\ \ k\gg 0.

Statement (a) follows immediately from Theorem
1.1
(a). Assume for a moment that statement (b) holds, then (c) can be proved as follows. By Brodmann, we have
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\min\{\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k}):\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)\}
for all
k\gg 0
. Thus, by (a), for all
k\gg 0

\min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}\alpha((I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k})\leq\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\leq\min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}\omega((I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}).

Setting
f(k)=\min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}\alpha((I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k})
and
g(k)=\min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}\omega((I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k})
, by statement (b) it follows that
f(k)
and
g(k)
are the required eventually linear functions in
k
. Statement (c) follows.

To prove statement (b), we construct a suitable module that encodes the growth of the modules
(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}
. Indeed, we define it in the following more general context. Let
I
be an ideal of a commutative Noetherian domain
R
and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Then we set

\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\ =\ \bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k},

and
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{k}=(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}
for all
k\geq 0
.

The symbol “
\operatorname{Soc}
” is used, because when
R=S
or
R
is local and
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}
is the (graded) maximal ideal, then
(I^{k}:\mathfrak{m})/I^{k}
is the
socle module
of
S/I^{k}
, see
[
6
]
.

The first step consists in showing that
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
is a finitely generated graded module over a suitable ring. For this aim, we introduce the following ring,

\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\ =\ \bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I^{k}/\mathfrak{p}I^{k}),

and we set
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{k}=I^{k}/\mathfrak{p}I^{k}
. We define addition in the obvious way and multiplication as follows. If
a\in I^{k}/\mathfrak{p}I^{k}
and
b\in I^{\ell}/\mathfrak{p}I^{\ell}
, then
ab\in I^{k+\ell}/\mathfrak{p}I^{k+\ell}
. It is routine to check that this multiplication is well–defined.

As before, we note that if
R=S
or
R
is local and
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}
is the maximal ideal, then
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I^{k}/\mathfrak{m}I^{k})
is the well–known
fiber cone
of
I
.

With the notation introduced, we have

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
I
be an ideal of a Noetherian commutative domain
R
and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Then,
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
is a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
-module.

Proof.

Firstly, we show that
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
has the structure of a graded
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
-module. For this purpose, let
f\in I^{\ell}/\mathfrak{p}I^{\ell}
. It is clear that multiplication by
f
induces a map
(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}\rightarrow(I^{k+\ell}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k+\ell}
for any
k\geq 0
. Hence
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{\ell}\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{k}\subseteq\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{k+\ell}
.

To prove that
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
-module, we consider

J=(0:_{\operatorname{gr}_{I}(R)}\mathfrak{p})=\{f\in\operatorname{gr}_{I}(R):f\mathfrak{p}=0\},

i.e.
, the annihilator of
\mathfrak{p}
in the associated graded ring of
I
,
\operatorname{gr}_{I}(R)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I^{k}/I^{k+1})
. Recall that
\operatorname{gr}_{I}(R)
is a Noetherian ring
[
29
, Proposition (10.D)]
. Thus, as an ideal of
\operatorname{gr}_{I}(R)
,
J
is a finitely generated graded
\operatorname{gr}_{I}(R)
-module. Since
\mathfrak{p}
annihilates
J
, then
J
has also the structure of a finitely generated graded
\operatorname{gr}_{I}(R)/\mathfrak{p}\operatorname{gr}_{I}(R)
-module. But

\displaystyle\operatorname{gr}_{I}(R)/\mathfrak{p}\operatorname{gr}_{I}(R)\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I^{k}/I^{k+1})}{\mathfrak{p}\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I^{k}/I^{k+1})}=\frac{\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I^{k}/I^{k+1})}{\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(\mathfrak{p}I^{k}/I^{k+1})}

\displaystyle=\ \bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\frac{I^{k}/I^{k+1}}{\mathfrak{p}I^{k}/I^{k+1}}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(I^{k}/\mathfrak{p}I^{k})

\displaystyle=\ \mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I).

Consequently,
J
is a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
-module.

Let us show that
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{k+1}=J_{k}
for
k\gg 0
. For this purpose, we compute the
k
th graded component of
J
. We have

\displaystyle J_{k}\

\displaystyle=\ \{f\in\operatorname{gr}_{I}(R)_{k}:f\mathfrak{p}=0\}=\{f\in I^{k}/I^{k+1}\ :f\mathfrak{p}=0\}

\displaystyle=\ \{f\in I^{k}:f\mathfrak{p}\in I^{k+1}\}/I^{k+1}=(\{f\in R:f\mathfrak{p}\in I^{k+1}\}\cap I^{k})/I^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\ ((I^{k+1}:\mathfrak{p})\cap I^{k})/I^{k+1}.

By Ratliff
[
31
, Corollary 4.2]
, there exists
r
such that
(I^{k+1}:I)=I^{k}
for all
k\geq r
. Whereas, by Brodmann
[
3
]
, there exists
b
such that
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
for all
k\geq b
. Let
k^{*}=\max\{r,b\}
. Next, we show that
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{k+1}=J_{k}
for
k\geq k^{*}
.

Let
k\geq k^{*}
. We claim that
\mathfrak{p}
contains
I
. Indeed,
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
, hence
I^{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Let
a\in I
, then
a^{k}\in I^{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Since
\mathfrak{p}
is prime, actually
a\in\mathfrak{p}
and so
I\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Therefore,
(I^{k+1}:\mathfrak{p})\subseteq(I^{k+1}:I)=I^{k}
by the Ratliff property. Hence,

J_{k}=((I^{k+1}:\mathfrak{p})\cap I^{k})/I^{k+1}=(I^{k+1}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k+1}=\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{k+1}.

Consequently, we obtain that
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{\geq k^{*}+1}=J_{\geq k^{*}}
, where
M_{\geq\ell}
denotes
\bigoplus_{k\geq\ell}M_{\ell}
if
M=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}M_{k}
is graded. Since
J
is finitely generated as a
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
-module, it follows that
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
-module as well.

∎

Now, we assume furthermore that
R=S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is the standard graded polynomial ring with
K
a field, that
I
is a graded ideal of
S
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
is a stable prime of
I
. Then,
I^{k}/\mathfrak{p}I^{k}
is a graded
S
-module, for all
k\geq 0
. Therefore,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
is in a natural way a bigraded ring:

\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\ =\ \bigoplus_{d,k\geq 0}(I^{k}/\mathfrak{p}I^{k})_{d}.

In particular, we set
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{(d,k)}=(I^{k}/\mathfrak{p}I^{k})_{d}
and
\textup{bideg}(f)=(d,k)
for
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{(d,k)}
.

Note that each module
(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}
is a graded
S
-module. Thus, we can write

\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\ =\ \bigoplus_{d,k\geq 0}\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{(d,k)}

where
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{(d,k)}=((I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k})_{d}
. Hence,
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
is a bigraded
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
-module, because
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{(d_{1},\ell)}\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{(d_{2},k)}\subseteq\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{(d_{1}+d_{2},k+\ell)}
.

Therefore, we have proved that

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
I
be a graded ideal of
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
with
K
a field and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Then,
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
is a finitely generated bigraded
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
-module.

Let
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
be a minimal system of homogeneous generators of
I\subset S
. It is well–known that the associated graded ring
\operatorname{gr}_{I}(S)
has a presentation

\varphi:T=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]\rightarrow\operatorname{gr}_{I}(S)

defined by setting

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{i})=x_{i}+I\in\operatorname{gr}_{I}(S)_{0}=S/I,\ \

\displaystyle\text{for}\ \ 1\leq i\leq n,

\displaystyle\varphi(y_{i})=u_{i}+I^{2}\in\operatorname{gr}_{I}(S)_{1}=I/I^{2},\ \

\displaystyle\text{for}\ \ 1\leq i\leq m.

Since
I
is graded,
\operatorname{gr}_{I}(S)
is naturally bigraded, with
\operatorname{gr}_{I}(S)_{(d,k)}=(I^{k}/I^{k+1})_{d}
. Moreover,
T
can be made into a bigraded ring by setting
\textup{bideg}(x_{i})=(1,0)
for
1\leq i\leq n
, and
\textup{bideg}(y_{i})=(\deg(u_{i}),1)
for
1\leq i\leq m
, where
\deg(u_{i})>0
is the degree of
u_{i}
in
S
. With these bigradings,
\varphi
is a bigraded surjective ring homomorphism.

In the proof of Theorem
2.2
we have seen that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)=\operatorname{gr}_{I}(S)/\mathfrak{p}\operatorname{gr}_{I}(S)
. Let
\pi:\operatorname{gr}_{I}(S)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
be the canonical epimorphism. Then, the composition map

\psi=\pi\circ\varphi\ :\ T\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)

(1)

is a surjective ring homomorphism. It is clear that
\psi
preserves the bigraded structure. Thus,
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
has also the structure of a bigraded
T
-module, if we set

af=\psi(a)f\ \ \ \text{for all}\ \ a\in T\ \ \text{and all}\ \ f\in\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I).

Since
\psi
is surjective and
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
-module, it follows that
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
is a finitely generated
T
-module, as well.

The following lemma is required. For a bigraded
T
-module
M=\bigoplus_{d,k}M_{d,k}
, we set
M_{(*,k)}=\bigoplus_{d}M_{(d,k)}
. Note that
M_{(*,k)}
becomes a graded
S
-module.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
T=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
be a bigraded polynomial ring, with
K
a field,
\textup{bideg}(x_{i})=(1,0)
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
\textup{bideg}(y_{i})=(d_{i},1)
for
1\leq i\leq m
. Let
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\subset T
. Let
M
be a finitely generated bigraded
T
-module. Then,

\operatorname{Tor}^{S}_{i}(S/\mathfrak{m},M_{(*,k)})\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{T}_{i}(T/\mathfrak{m},M)_{(*,k)}

for all
i
and
k
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}:0\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow F_{j}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
be a minimal bigraded
T
-resolution of
M
. Then,

{\mathbb{F}}_{k}:0\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow(F_{j})_{(*,k)}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow(F_{1})_{(*,k)}\rightarrow(F_{0})_{(*,k)}\rightarrow M_{(*,k)}\rightarrow 0

is a graded (possibly non-minimal) free
S
-resolution of
M_{(*,k)}=\bigoplus_{d}M_{(d,k)}
. Since
\operatorname{Tor}^{T}_{i}(T/\mathfrak{m},M)=H_{i}({\mathbb{F}}/\mathfrak{m}{\mathbb{F}})
we have that
\operatorname{Tor}^{T}_{i}(T/\mathfrak{m},M)_{(*,k)}=H_{i}({\mathbb{F}}_{k}/\mathfrak{m}{\mathbb{F}}_{k})
which in turn is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Tor}^{S}_{i}(S/\mathfrak{m},M_{(*,k)})
. The desired conclusion follows.

∎

Note that
T/\mathfrak{m}=K[y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
and that
\operatorname{Tor}^{T}_{0}(T/\mathfrak{m},\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I))
is a finitely generated bigraded
T/\mathfrak{m}
-module. Therefore, by the above lemma, we have

\displaystyle\alpha((I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k})

\displaystyle=\alpha(\operatorname{Tor}^{S}_{0}(S/\mathfrak{m},(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}))=\alpha(\operatorname{Tor}^{S}_{0}(S/\mathfrak{m},\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{(*,k)}))

\displaystyle=\alpha(\operatorname{Tor}^{T}_{0}(T/\mathfrak{m},\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I))_{(*,k)}).

Similarly,
\omega((I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k})=\omega(\operatorname{Tor}^{T}_{0}(T/\mathfrak{m},\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I))_{(*,k)})
.

From this discussion, Theorem
2.1
(b) follows from the next more general statement, which is a variation of
[
9
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
V=K[y_{1},\dots,y_{s}]
a polynomial ring, with
\textup{bideg}(y_{i})=(d_{i},1)
,
d_{i}\geq 1
, for
1\leq i\leq s
and
K
a field, and let
M
be a finitely generated bigraded
V
-module. Then,
\alpha_{M}(k)=\min\{d:M_{(d,k)}\neq 0\}
and
\omega_{M}(k)=\max\{d:M_{(d,k)}\neq 0\}
are linear functions in
k
for
k\gg 0
.

Proof.

The claim about the linearity of
\omega_{M}(k)
follows from
[
9
, Theorem 3.4]
. The proof of the claim of the linearity of
\alpha_{M}(k)
is similar, but we include here all the details for the convenience of the reader.

For any exact sequence
0\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow P\rightarrow 0
of finitely generated bigraded
V
-modules we have
\alpha_{N}(k)=\min\{\alpha_{M}(k),\alpha_{P}(k)\}
, for all
k
.

Since
M
is a finitely generated
V
-module and
V
is Noetherian, by the bigraded version of
[
11
, Proposition 3.7]
there exists a sequence of bigraded
V
-submodules

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{i-1}\subset M_{i}=M

of
M
such that
M_{j}/M_{j-1}\cong V/\mathfrak{p}_{j}
, with
\mathfrak{p}_{j}
a bigraded prime ideal of
V
, for all
1\leq j\leq i
. Hence, we may suppose that
M=V/J
with
J
a bigraded ideal of
V
. We show that
J
can be replaced by a monomial ideal. For this aim, let
>
be a monomial order on
V
, and let
\textup{in}(J)
be the initial ideal of
J
with respect to
>
. The natural
K
-basis of
V/J
consists of all residue classes (modulo
J
) of all monomials not belonging to
\textup{in}(J)
, see
[
24
, Proposition 2.2.5.(a)]
. The same residue classes modulo
\textup{in}(J)
form a
K
-basis for
V/\textup{in}(J)
. Thus
\alpha_{M}(k)=\alpha_{V/J}(k)=\alpha_{V/\textup{in}(J)}(k)
, and we can assume that
M=V/J
with
J
a monomial ideal of
V
.

Recall that
\textup{bideg}(y_{i})=(d_{i},1)
for
1\leq i\leq s
. For later use, we may suppose that
d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\cdots\leq d_{s}
. Furthermore, we can assume that
J
is minimally generated by the monomials
{\bf y}^{{\bf c}_{i}}=y_{1}^{c_{i,1}}y_{2}^{c_{i,2}}\cdots y_{s}^{c_{i,s}}
, for
1\leq i\leq r
.

Let
{\bf a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{s})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{s}
, we denote by
\overline{{\bf y^{a}}}
the residue class of
{\bf y^{a}}=y_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots y_{s}^{a_{s}}
in
V/J
. Let
k\geq 0
, by
B_{k}
we denote the minimal basis of
(V/J)_{k}
. Then, we can write
\alpha_{M}(k)=\min\{v({\bf a}):\overline{{\bf y^{a}}}\in B_{k}\}
, where

v({\bf a})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}\deg(y_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}d_{i}.

Clearly,
\overline{{\bf y^{a}}}\in B_{k}
if and only if
\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}=k
, and for all
i=1,\dots,s
, there exists
j
such that
a_{j}<c_{i,j}
. Denote by
L
the set of all maps
\{1,\dots,r\}\rightarrow\{1,\dots,s\}
. We can decompose the set
B_{k}
as the union
\bigcup_{f\in L}B_{k,f}
, where

B_{k,f}=\big\{\overline{{\bf y^{a}}}\ :\ \sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}=k\ \text{and}\ a_{f(i)}<c_{i,f(i)},\ i=1,\dots,r\big\}.

With this in mind, we can write
\alpha_{M}(k)=\min_{f\in L}\alpha_{f}(k)
, where
\alpha_{f}(k)
is defined as
\alpha_{f}(k)=\min\{v({\bf a}):\overline{{\bf y^{a}}}\in B_{k,f}\}
. Hence, it is enough to prove that
\alpha_{f}(k)
is a linear function with integer coefficients for all
f\in L
and all
k\gg 0
.

Fix
f\in L
. Let
\{j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{t}\}
be the image of
f
. For
h=1,\dots,t
, we set
c_{j_{h}}=\min\{c_{i,j_{h}}:i=1,\dots,r\}-1
. Then, we have that

B_{k,f}=\big\{\overline{{\bf y^{a}}}\ :\ \sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}=k\ \text{and}\ a_{j_{h}}\leq c_{j_{h}},\ h=1,\dots,t\big\}.

Thus,
\alpha_{f}(k)
is given by the maximum of the
functional

v({\bf a})
on the set

C_{k,f}=\big\{{\bf a}\ :\ \sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}=k\ \text{and}\ a_{j_{h}}\leq c_{j_{h}},\ h=1,\dots,t\big\}.

Let
\ell
be the smallest integer such that
j_{1}=1
,
\dots
,
j_{\ell}=\ell
and
j_{\ell+1}>\ell+1
. Thus, for
{\bf a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{s})\in C_{k,f}
we have
a_{1}<c_{j_{1}},\ a_{2}<c_{j_{2}},\ \dots,\ a_{\ell}<c_{j_{\ell}}
and no bound on
a_{j_{\ell+1}}
, except that
\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}=k
. We distinguish the two possible cases.

Case 1
. Suppose
{\ell}=s
. Then
\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}
can be at most
c_{j_{1}}+c_{j_{2}}+\dots+c_{j_{\ell}}
. Thus, for all
k\gg 0
,
B_{k,f}=\emptyset
and so
\alpha_{f}(k)=0
.

Case 2
. Suppose
\ell<s
. We let
k
such that
k\geq c_{j_{1}}+c_{j_{2}}+\dots+c_{j_{\ell}}
. We claim that the functional
v
has its minimal value for
{\bf a}_{*}=(c_{j_{1}},c_{j_{2}},\dots,c_{j_{\ell}},k-\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}c_{j_{p}},0,0,\dots,0)
.
Then, for all large
k\gg 0
, we have that

\alpha_{f}(k)=v({\bf a}_{*})=\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}c_{j_{p}}d_{j_{p}}+d_{j_{\ell+1}}(k-\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}c_{j_{p}}),

which is a linear function in
k
with integer coefficients, as desired.

Let
{\bf a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{s})\in C_{k,f}
. Assume that for some
1\leq i<j\leq s
we have
a_{i}<c_{i}
and
a_{j}>0
. Then,
{\bf a}^{\prime}=(a_{1},a_{2}\dots,a_{i}+1,\dots,a_{j}-1,\dots,a_{s})

also belongs to
C_{k,f}
and
v({\bf a}^{\prime})\leq v({\bf a})
because we have
d_{i}\leq d_{j}
. Thus, we see that the minimal value of
v
on
C_{k,f}
is achieved when we fill up the first “boxes” of
{\bf a}\in C_{k,f}
as much as possible. Finally, the functional
v
reaches its minimal value when
{\bf a}={\bf a}_{*}
.

∎

Theorem
1.1
(c) combined with Theorem
2.1
(b) yields

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a graded ideal and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Max}^{\infty}(I)
. Then,
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
is a linear function in
k
, for
k\gg 0
.

We conclude this section with the following result that gives an estimate on the growth of the modules
(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}
for large
k
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal, and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Then,

(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}\ =\ (I(I^{k-1}:\mathfrak{p}))/I^{k},\ \ \textit{for all}\ k\gg 0.

In particular,
(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})\ =\ I(I^{k-1}:\mathfrak{p})
, for all
k\gg 0
.

In order to prove the above result, we need the next general property.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
T=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
be a bigraded polynomial ring, with
K
a field,
\textup{bideg}(x_{i})=(1,0)
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
\textup{bideg}(y_{i})=(d_{i},1)
,
d_{i}\geq 1
, for
1\leq i\leq m
. Let
M
be a finitely generated bigraded
T
-module. Then

M_{(*,k)}\ =\ \{y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\}M_{(*,k-1)},\ \ \textit{for all}\ k\gg 0.

Here
\{y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\}M_{(*,k-1)}
denotes the set
\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{i}f_{i}:f_{i}\in M_{(*,k-1)}\}
.

Proof.

Since
M
is finitely generated, there exists bihomogeneous elements
g_{1},\dots,g_{r}
that generate
M
as a bigraded
T
-module. Let
\textup{bideg}(g_{i})=(p_{i},q_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq r
.

For vectors
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
and
{\bf b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{m})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{m}
, we set

{\bf x^{a}y^{b}}\ =\ x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots y_{m}^{b_{m}}.

Then, these elements form a
K
-basis of
T
, and

(2)

where the sum is taken over all

3.
Asymptotic behaviour of the
\operatorname{v}
-number

In this section, we prove the main result in the article:
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
is indeed linear function in
k
for all
k\gg 0
, and all graded ideals
I\subset S
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a graded ideal. Then,
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
, for all for
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
, and
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
are linear functions in
k
, for
k\gg 0
.

In order to prove the theorem, we construct a new module starting from
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal, and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. For all
k
, let
\overline{g}_{k,1},\dots,\overline{g}_{k,r_{k}}
be a minimal homogeneous generating set of
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{(*,k)}=(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}
. We set

\displaystyle A_{k}\

\displaystyle=\ \{\overline{g}_{k,j}\ :\ (I^{k}:g_{k,j})=\mathfrak{p}\},

\displaystyle B_{k}\

\displaystyle=\ \{\overline{g}_{k,j}\ :\ (I^{k}:g_{k,j})\neq\mathfrak{p}\}.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be the submodule of
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
generated by
\bigcup_{k}B_{k}
. Since each element of the set
\bigcup_{k}B_{k}
is bihomogeneous,
\mathcal{N}
is a bigraded submodule of
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
. We define the following
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
-module:

\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}(I)\ =\ \operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)/\mathcal{N}.

By Theorem
2.2
and the fact that
\mathcal{N}
is a bigraded submodule of
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
, it follows that
\operatorname{Soc}^{*}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
is a finitely generated bigraded
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
-module.

Moreover,

\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}(I)_{(*,k)}\ =\ \dfrac{(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}}{\mathcal{N}_{(*,k)}},

for all
k\geq 0
.

Finally, we recall the following basic rules. Let
I,I_{1},I_{2},\{J_{i}\}_{i}
be ideals of a commutative Noetherian ring
R
and let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
R
. Then,

(i)

(I:\sum_{i}J_{i})=\bigcap_{i}(I:J_{i})
,

(ii)

((I:I_{1}):I_{2})=(I:I_{1}I_{2})
,

(iii)

if
\mathfrak{p}=\bigcap_{i}J_{i}
, then
\mathfrak{p}=J_{i}
for some
i
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. By Theorem
1.1
, for all
k\gg 0
we have

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})=\min_{\overline{g}_{k,j}\in A_{k}}\deg(g_{k,j}).

Next, note that
A_{k}\cup B_{k}
generates
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{(*,k)}
and
B_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{N}
. It follows that a minimal homogeneous generating set of
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}(I)_{(*,k)}
is given by the non-zero residue classes, modulo
\mathcal{N}_{(*,k)}
, of the elements of
A_{k}
. We claim that each of these classes is non-zero modulo
\mathcal{N}_{(*,k)}
. Suppose for a contradiction that some
\overline{g}_{k,j}\in A_{k}
belongs to
\mathcal{N}_{(*,k)}
. Since
\mathcal{N}
is generated by
\bigcup_{\ell}B_{\ell}
, we can write

\overline{g}_{k,j}\ =\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell\leq k\\
\overline{g}_{\ell,p}\in B_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\overline{a}_{k-\ell,p}\overline{g}_{\ell,p},

(3)

with each
\overline{a}_{k-\ell,p}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)_{(*,k-\ell)}=I^{k-\ell}/\mathfrak{p}I^{k-\ell}
. Since
\overline{g}_{k,j}\in A_{k}
we have
(I^{k}:g_{k,j})=\mathfrak{p}
. By equation (
3
) we can write
g_{k,j}=\sum a_{k-\ell,p}g_{\ell,p}+z
where
z
is a suitable element of
I^{k}
. Note that
\bigcap(I^{k}:a_{k-\ell,p}g_{\ell,p})
is contained in
(I^{k}:g_{k,j})=\mathfrak{p}
. Indeed, take
h\in\bigcap(I^{k}:a_{k-\ell,p}g_{\ell,p})
. Then
ha_{k-\ell,p}g_{\ell,p}\in I^{k}
for all terms in the intersection. Hence
hg_{k,j}=\sum ha_{k-\ell,p}g_{\ell,p}+hz\in I^{k}
. Since
(I^{\ell}:g_{\ell,p})\subseteq(I^{k}:a_{k-\ell,p}g_{\ell,p})
for all
\ell
and
p
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}\ \subseteq\ \bigcap(I^{\ell}:g_{\ell,p})\ \subseteq\ \bigcap(I^{k}:a_{k-\ell,p}g_{\ell,p})\ \subseteq\ \mathfrak{p}.

Hence
\bigcap(I^{\ell}:g_{\ell,p})=\mathfrak{p}
. By rule (iii), we have
(I^{\ell}:g_{\ell,p})=\mathfrak{p}
for some
\ell
and
p
. But this is a contradiction because
\overline{g}_{\ell,p}\in B_{\ell}
. This proves our claim. As a consequence, we obtain that for all
k\gg 0

\alpha(\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}(I)_{(*,k)})\ =\ \min_{\overline{g}_{k,j}\in A_{k}}\deg(g_{k,j})\ =\ \operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k}).

Using the map (
1
), we see that
\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}(I)
is a finitely generated bigraded
T
-module. Moreover,
\alpha(\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}(I)_{(*,k)})=\alpha(\operatorname{Tor}^{T}_{0}(T/\mathfrak{m},\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}(I))_{(*,k)})
. Applying Proposition
2.5
, we conclude that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})=\alpha(\operatorname{Soc}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}(I)_{(*,k)})
is a linear function in
k
for
k\gg 0
.

Finally,
\operatorname{v}(I)=\min_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)}\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
is a linear function in
k
for
k\gg 0
, for it is given by the minimum of finitely many eventually linear functions in
k
.

∎

We conclude the section with some results which estimate the growth of the functions
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
, when
k\gg 0
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Then,

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-1})+\alpha(I)\ \leq\ \operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})\ \leq\ \operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-1})+\omega(I),\ \ \textit{for all}\ k\gg 0.

In particular,

\operatorname{v}(I^{k-1})+\alpha(I)\leq\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\leq\operatorname{v}(I^{k-1})+\omega(I),\ \ \textit{for all}\ k\gg 0.

Proof.

We first prove that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-1})+\alpha(I)\leq\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
, for
k\gg 0
. By Proposition
2.7
, there exists
k_{0}>0
such that
(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}\ =\ (I(I^{k-1}:\mathfrak{p}))/I^{k}
, for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Let
k\geq k_{0}
. Take
\overline{f}\in(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})/I^{k}
such that
(I^{k}:f)=\mathfrak{p}
and
\deg(f)=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
. Since
(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p})=I(I^{k-1}:\mathfrak{p})
, we can write
f=ug
where
u\in I
and
g\in(I^{k-1}:\mathfrak{p})
. Then

\mathfrak{p}\subseteq(I^{k-1}:g)\subseteq(uI^{k-1}:ug)=(uI^{k-1}:f)\subseteq(I^{k}:f)=\mathfrak{p}.

Here we used that
g\in(I^{k-1}:\mathfrak{p})
and
uI^{k-1}\subseteq I^{k}
. Hence,
(I^{k-1}:g)=\mathfrak{p}
and so

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})=\deg(f)=\deg(ug)=\deg(g)+\deg(u)\geq\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-1})+\alpha(I).

Now, we prove the inequality
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})\leq\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-1})+\omega(I)
, for
k\gg 0
. By Brodmann and Ratliff, there exists
k^{*}>0
such that
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
and
(I^{k}:I)=I^{k-1}
for all
k\geq k^{*}
. Fix
k\geq k^{*}
and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
.

Let
f\in S
be a homogeneous element with
(I^{k-1}:f)=\mathfrak{p}
and
\deg(f)=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-1})
. Let
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
be a minimal homogeneous generating set of
I
. By rules (ii) and (i),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}=(I^{k-1}:f)\

\displaystyle=\ (I^{k}:I):f=(I^{k}:fI)

\displaystyle=\ (I^{k}:\sum_{i=1}^{m}(fu_{i}))=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}(I^{k}:fu_{i}).

Hence, by rule (iii), we have
\mathfrak{p}=(I^{k}:fu_{i})
for some
i
. By the definition of
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})
, this means that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k})\leq\deg(fu_{i})=\deg(f)+\deg(u_{i})\leq\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I^{k-1})+\omega(I)
.

∎

Let

Suppose that

, for all

.

(b)

Suppose that
a_{k}\leq a_{k-1}+a
, for all

. Then, there exists a constant
c
such that
a_{k}\leq ak+c
, for all
k\gg 0
.

(c)

Suppose that
a_{k}=a_{k-1}+a
, for all
k\gg 0
and some integer
a
. Then, there exists a constant
c
such that
a_{k}=ak+c
, for all
k\gg 0
.

(d)

Suppose that
ak+b\leq a_{k}\leq ak+c
, for all

. Then,

Suppose that

4.
The limit behaviour of the sequence
\frac{\operatorname{v}(I^{k})}{k}

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal. Due to Theorem

, there exist integers
a
and
b
such that
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=ak+b
for
k\gg 0
. As a consequence, the limit
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{v}(I^{k})/k
exists and it is equal to
a
. In this section, we prove the following surprising fact.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal. Then

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{v}(I^{k})}{k}\ =\ \alpha(I).

In view of statements (a), (b) and (d) of Lemma
3.4
, to prove the theorem, it is enough to find lower and upper bounds for
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
, which are linear functions in
k
of the type
\alpha(I)k+c
, for some constant
c
, and for all
k\gg 0
.

We begin by providing the linear upper bound.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal. Then, there exists a constant
c
such that

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\ \leq\ \alpha(I)k+c,\ \ \textit{for all}\ k\gg 0.

The proof of this lemma is based upon the next bound which generalizes a result of Saha and Sengupta
[

, Proposition 3.11]

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal and let
f\in S
be a homogeneous element not belonging to
I
. Then

\operatorname{v}(I)\ \leq\ \operatorname{v}(I:f)+\deg(f).

Proof.

If
(I:f)\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
, then
\operatorname{v}(I)\leq\deg(f)
. Since, also
\operatorname{v}(I:f)\geq 0
, the asserted inequality follows. Suppose that
(I:f)\notin\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. Note that
(I:f)
is a proper ideal, because
f\notin I
. From the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow S/(I:f)\rightarrow S/I\rightarrow S/(I,f)\rightarrow 0

it follows that
\operatorname{Ass}(I:f)\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. Let
g\in S
be a homogeneous element such that
((I:f):g)\in\operatorname{Ass}(I:f)
and
\deg(g)=\operatorname{v}(I:f)
. Then

((I:f):g)=(I:(fg))\in\operatorname{Ass}(I).

Thus
\operatorname{v}(I)\leq\deg(fg)=\deg(f)+\deg(g)=\deg(f)+\operatorname{v}(I:f)
.

∎

Now, we are ready for the proof of Lemma
4.2
which follows closely the argument given in
[
2

.

Let

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\leq\operatorname{v}(I^{k}:f^{2})+2\alpha(I)

\displaystyle\operatorname{v}(I^{k})\ \leq\ \alpha(I)k+(\operatorname{v}(I^{k}:f^{k-1})-\alpha(I)).

(4)

On the other hand, consider the ascending chain

(d).

{\bf b}:0\leq b_{1}<b_{2}<\cdots<b_{m}

The associated prime ideals of a monomial ideal
I
are monomial prime ideals, that is, ideals generated by a subset of the variables
[
24
, Corollary 1.3.9]
. Thus, in our case
\operatorname{Ass}(I)\subseteq\{(x),(y),(x,y)\}
. We set
\mathfrak{p}_{x}=(x)
,
\mathfrak{p}_{y}=(y)
and
\mathfrak{m}=(x,y)
.

We can compute
\operatorname{Ass}(I_{\bf a,b})
in terms of the sequences
{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
I=I_{\bf a,b}\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then,

(a)

\mathfrak{p}_{x}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
if and only if
a_{m}>0
.

(b)

\mathfrak{p}_{y}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
if and only if
b_{1}>0
.

(c)

\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
if and only if
m>1
, i.e.,
I
is not a principal ideal.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{p}_{x}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
then
I\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{x}=(x)
. Hence
x
divides all minimal monomial generators of
I
. In particular,
x
divides
x^{a_{m}}y^{b_{m}}
and
a_{m}>0
.

Conversely, let
a_{m}>0
. Then
x
divides all minimal monomial generators of
I
. Hence
I\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{x}=(x)
. Since
\mathfrak{p}_{x}
is of height one, it follows that
\mathfrak{p}_{x}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
.

This proves (a), statement (b) can be proved similarly.

Finally, for the proof of (c), suppose
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. If
I
is a principal ideal, then for some
c
and
d
,
I=(x^{c}y^{d})=(x^{c})\cap(y^{d})=\mathfrak{p}_{x}^{c}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{y}^{d}
is the primary decomposition of
I
, which contradicts our assumption. Hence
I
is not principal.

Conversely, suppose
I
is not principal, but
\mathfrak{m}\notin\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. Then
\operatorname{Ass}(I)\subseteq\{\mathfrak{p}_{x},\mathfrak{p}_{y}\}
. Since
\mathfrak{p}_{x}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{y}
are height one prime ideals,
I=\mathfrak{p}_{x}^{c}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{y}^{d}=(x^{c})\cap(y^{d})=(x^{c}y^{d})
for some
c
and
d
, against our assumption. The assertion follows.

∎

The following lemma is required.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
I=I_{\bf a,b}\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then,

x^{ka_{1}}y^{kb_{1}},x^{ka_{m}}y^{kb_{m}}\in G(I^{k}),\ \ \textit{for all}\ \ k\geq 1.

Proof.

Let
k\geq 1
. We know that

I^{k}\ =\ (\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x^{a_{i}}y^{b_{i}})^{k_{i}}\ :\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}=k).

Let
x^{r}y^{s}
be an arbitrary generator of
I^{k}
different from
x^{ka_{1}}y^{kb_{1}}
. Then, we have
r=k_{1}a_{1}+\dots k_{m}a_{m}
,
s=k_{1}b_{1}+\dots k_{m}b_{m}
,
\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}=k
and
k_{i}>0
for some
i\neq 1
. Thus,

ka_{1}=k_{1}a_{1}+k_{2}a_{1}+\dots k_{m}a_{1}>k_{1}a_{1}+k_{2}a_{2}+\dots k_{m}a_{m}=r

and

kb_{1}=k_{1}b_{1}+k_{2}b_{1}+\dots k_{m}b_{1}<k_{1}b_{1}+k_{2}b_{2}+\dots k_{m}b_{m}=s.

Therefore,
x^{r}y^{s}
does not divide
x^{ka_{1}}y^{kb_{1}}
. This shows that
x^{ka_{1}}y^{kb_{1}}
is a minimal generator of
I^{k}
. By a similar argument we obtain that
x^{ka_{m}}y^{kb_{m}}\in G(I^{k})
.

∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
I=I_{\bf a,b}\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
, for all
k\geq 1
. In particular,
\textup{astab}(I)=1
.

Proof.

Let us prove that
\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 2
. By the previous proposition,
\mathfrak{p}_{x}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
if and only if
x
divides all minimal monomial generators of
I
. Hence, if
\mathfrak{p}_{x}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
, then
\mathfrak{p}_{x}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 2
, as well.

Now, suppose that
\mathfrak{p}_{x}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for some
k\geq 2
, but
\mathfrak{p}_{x}\notin\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. Then, by Proposition
5.1
(a) we have
a_{m}=0
. Hence
y^{b_{m}}\in G(I)
. By the previous corollary,
y^{kb_{m}}\in G(I^{k})
, as well. But this is impossible, because
y^{kb_{m}}\notin\mathfrak{p}_{x}
, but by assumption
\mathfrak{p}_{x}
contains
I^{k}
. Hence
a_{m}>0
and
\mathfrak{p}_{x}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
, as wanted.

The same reasoning can be applied to show that
\mathfrak{p}_{y}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
if and only if
\mathfrak{p}_{y}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
, for any
k\geq 2
.

Finally, by the previous proposition,
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
if and only
I
is not principal. Lemma
5.2
implies that
I
is not principal if and only if
I^{k}
is not principal for any
k\geq 2
. Thus
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
if and only if
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for any
k\geq 2
.

∎

Next, we compute the functions
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}_{x}}(I_{\bf a,b}^{k})
,
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}_{y}}(I_{\bf a,b}^{k})
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
I=I_{\bf a,b}\subset S
be a monomial ideal. The following holds.

(a)

If
a_{m}>0
, then
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}_{x}}(I^{k})=k(a_{m}+b_{m})-1
, for all
k\geq 1
.

(b)

If
b_{1}>0
, then
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}_{y}}(I^{k})=k(a_{1}+b_{1})-1
, for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

The only generator
\overline{u}\in(I:\mathfrak{p}_{x})/I
such that
(I:u)=\mathfrak{p}_{x}
is
x^{a_{m}-1}y^{b_{m}}
, for it has the largest
y
-degree. For each
k\geq 1
, from Lemma
5.2
,
x^{ka_{m}}y^{kb_{m}}\in G(I^{k})
and such generator has the highest
y
-degree. Thus,
\overline{u}=\overline{x^{ka_{m}-1}y^{kb_{m}}}
is the only generator of
(I^{k}:\mathfrak{p}_{x})/I^{k}
such that
(I^{k}:u)=\mathfrak{p}_{x}
. Similarly, one can prove (b).

∎

In the next proposition, we show how to compute
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)
for a non principal monomial ideal
I=I_{\bf a,b}\subset S
. For our convenience, if
c\geq 1
, in the proof of the next result we regard
x^{-c}
and
y^{-c}
as
1
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
I=I_{\bf a,b}\subset S
be a non principal monomial ideal. Then,

(I:\mathfrak{m})/I=(x^{a_{j}-1}y^{b_{j+1}-1}\ :\ 1\leq j\leq m-1)/I.

(5)

In particular,

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)=\min\{a_{j}+b_{j+1}-2\ :\ 1\leq j\leq m-1\}.

Proof.

Firstly, we compute
I:\mathfrak{m}
. We have

\displaystyle I:\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=\ (I:\mathfrak{p}_{x})\cap(I:\mathfrak{p}_{y})

(6)

\displaystyle=\ (x^{a_{1}-1}y^{b_{1}},\dots,x^{a_{m}-1}y^{b_{m}})\cap(x^{a_{1}}y^{b_{1}-1},\dots,x^{a_{m}}y^{b_{m}-1})

\displaystyle=\ (\text{lcm}(x^{a_{i}-1}y^{b_{i}},x^{a_{j}}y^{b_{j}-1})\ :\ 1\leq i\leq m,\ 1\leq j\leq m)

\displaystyle=\ (\text{lcm}(x^{a_{i}-1}y^{b_{i}},x^{a_{j+1}}y^{b_{j+1}-1})\ :\ 1\leq i\leq m,\ 0\leq j\leq m-1)

\displaystyle=\ (x^{\max\{a_{i}-1,a_{j+1}\}}y^{\max\{b_{i},b_{j+1}-1\}}\ :\ 1\leq i\leq m,\ 0\leq j\leq m-1).

Fix
j\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}
and let
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.

If
i\leq j
, we have
a_{i}\geq a_{j}>a_{j+1}
and
b_{i}\leq b_{j}<b_{j+1}
. Therefore
x^{a_{j+1}}|x^{a_{i}-1}
and
y^{b_{i}}|y^{b_{j+1}-1}
. Hence,

x^{a_{j}-1}y^{b_{j+1}-1}\in(I:\mathfrak{m})\ \ \text{divides}\ \ x^{\max\{a_{i}-1,a_{j+1}\}}y^{\max\{b_{i},b_{j+1}-1\}}\ \ \text{for}\ \ i\leq j.

(7)

If
i>j
, we have
a_{i}-1\leq a_{j+1}
and
b_{i}\geq b_{j+1}-1
, so
x^{a_{i}-1}|x^{a_{j+1}}
and
y^{b_{j+1}-1}|y^{b_{i}}
. Hence,

x^{a_{j+1}}y^{b_{i}}\in(I:\mathfrak{m})\ \ \text{divides}\ \ x^{\max\{a_{i}-1,a_{j+1}\}}y^{\max\{b_{i},b_{j+1}-1\}}\ \ \text{for}\ \ i>j.

(8)

Thus, by equations (
6
), (
7
) and (
8
) we have

I:\mathfrak{m}\ =\ (x^{a_{j}-1}y^{b_{j+1}-1},x^{a_{j+1}}y^{b_{i}}\ :\ 1\leq j\leq m-1,\ j+1\leq i\leq m).

Note that for each
i\geq j+1
we have
x^{a_{j+1}}y^{b_{i}}\in I
. It is clear that
x^{a_{j}-1}y^{b_{j+1}-1}\notin I
, for all
j=1,\dots,m-1
. Hence, equation (
5
) follows.

The claim about
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)
follows from (
5
) and Theorem
1.1
(c).

As a consequence of our discussion, we obtain the next formula that shows us how to compute the
\operatorname{v}
-number of
I_{\bf a,b}
solely in terms of the sequences
{\bf a}
and
{\bf b}
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
I=I_{\bf a,b}\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then

\operatorname{v}(I)=\begin{cases}\min\{a_{i}+b_{i+1}-2\ :\ 1\leq i\leq m-1\},\ \text{if}\ b_{1}=0\ \text{and}\ a_{m}=0,\\
\min\{a_{1}+b_{1}-1,a_{i}+b_{i+1}-2\ :\ 1\leq i\leq m-1\},\ \text{if}\ b_{1}\neq 0\ \text{and}\ a_{m}=0,\\
\min\{a_{m}+b_{m}-1,a_{i}+b_{i+1}-2\ :\ 1\leq i\leq m-1\},\ \text{if}\ b_{1}=0\ \text{and}\ a_{m}\neq 0,\\
\min\{a_{1}+b_{1}-1,a_{m}+b_{m}-1,a_{i}+b_{i+1}-2\ :\ 1\leq i\leq m-1\},\ \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Suppose
I
is non principal. Then, the statement follows by combining Proposition
5.1
, Corollary
5.4
and Proposition
5.5
. Now, if
I
is principal, the above formulas also hold. Indeed, in this case
m=1
and in the above last three minimums one does not have to consider the terms
a_{i}+b_{i+1}-2
because
m-1=0
.

∎

Let
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a monomial ideal. Then

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k+b,

for all
k\gg 0
. By
[
13
, Theorem 2.3(c)]
we have that
b\geq-1
. Note also that
a=\alpha(I)\geq 1
. On the other hand, let
f(k)=ak+b
be any linear function in
k
, with
a\geq 1
and
b\geq-1
. In the next theorem, we prove that there exists a monomial ideal
I\subset K[x,y]
such that
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
agrees with

for all
k\geq 1
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
a\geq 1
and
b\geq-1
be integers. Then, there exists a monomial ideal
I\subset S=K[x,y]
such that

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=ak+b,\ \ \textit{for all}\ \ k\geq 1.

Proof.

We claim that
I=(x^{a},x^{a-1}y^{b+2})=x^{a-1}(x,y^{b+2})
satisfies our assertion. For this aim, let us show that
I^{k}=(x^{ka-i}y^{i(b+2)}:0\leq i\leq k)
for all
k\geq 1
. Indeed,

\displaystyle I^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{k(a-1)}(x,y^{b+2})^{k}=x^{k(a-1)}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(x^{k-i}y^{i(b+2)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x^{ka-i}y^{i(b+2)}:0\leq i\leq k).

Since
ka>ka-1>\dots>ka-k
and
b+2<2(b+2)<\dots<k(b+2)
, it follows that
G(I^{k})=\{x^{ka-i}y^{i(b+2)}:0\leq i\leq k\}
.

Note that
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)=\{\mathfrak{p}_{x},\mathfrak{m}\}
if
a>1
, and
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)=\{\mathfrak{m}\}
if
a=1
.

If
a>1
, by Corollary
5.4
(a) we have
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}_{x}}(I^{k})=k(a+b+1)-1
.

Whereas, by Proposition
5.5
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I^{k})\

\displaystyle=\ \min\{(ka-i)+(i+1)(b+2)-2:0\leq i\leq k-1\}

\displaystyle=\ \min\{ka+(i+1)b+i:0\leq i\leq k-1\}

\displaystyle=\ ak+b.

If
a>1
, then
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\min\{\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}_{x}}(I^{k}),\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I^{k})\}=\min\{k(a+b+1)-1,ak+b\}=ak+b
. Otherwise, if
a=1
, then
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{m}}(I^{k})=ak+b
once again.

∎

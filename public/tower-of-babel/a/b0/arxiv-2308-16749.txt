Cyclotomic expansions for double twist knots with an odd number of half-twists
Qingtao Chen, Kefeng Liu and Shengmao Zhu

Abstract
. In this note, we compute the cyclotomic expansion formula for colored Jones polynomial of double twist knots with an odd number of half-twists 𝒦 p , s 2 \mathcal{K}_{p,\frac{s}{2}} by using the Kauffman bracket skein theory. It answers a question proposed by Lovejoy and Osburn in 2019.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the double twist knot
\mathcal{K}_{p,\frac{s}{2}}
with an odd number of half-twists, where
p
is a nonzero integer and
s
is odd. For convenience, we usually write
s=2m-1
with
m
a positive integer.

(1.1)

Figure 1.
Double twist knot
\mathcal{K}_{p,\frac{s}{2}}

The goal of this note is to compute the cyclotomic expansion formula for the colored Jones polynomial of
\mathcal{K}_{p,\frac{s}{2}}
. Hence it answers a question proposed in
[
9
]
.

First, let us fix the notations used in this paper. Let
A
be an indeterminate and
\mathfrak{q}=A^{2}
. For an integer
n
, we define the symbols by

\displaystyle[n]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{n}-\mathfrak{q}^{-n}}{\mathfrak{q}-\mathfrak{q}^{-1}},\qquad\{n\}=\mathfrak{q}^{n}-\mathfrak{q}^{-n}.

Furthermore, for
n\geq 0
and
0\leq i\leq n
, we define the
\mathfrak{q}
-factorials by

\displaystyle[n]!=[n][n-1]\cdots[1],\quad\{n\}!=\{n\}\{n-1\}\cdots\{1\},\quad\left[\begin{array}[]{@{\,}c@{\,}}n\\
i\end{array}\right]=\frac{[n]!}{[i]![n-i]!}.

We introduce the variable
q=\mathfrak{q}^{2}
and the
q
-Pochhammer symbol as follows

(1.2)

\displaystyle(x;q)_{k}=\prod_{j=0}^{k}(1-xq^{j}).

Then we define

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{@{\,}c@{\,}}n\\
i\end{array}\right]_{q}=\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{i}(q;q)_{n-i}}.

Given a knot
\mathcal{K}
, we denote by
J^{\prime}_{N}(\mathcal{K};\mathfrak{q})
the
N
-th normalized colored Jones polynomial of
\mathcal{K}
as in
[
10
]
.
Habiro proved the famous cyclotomic expansion formula for
J^{\prime}_{N}(\mathcal{K};\mathfrak{q})
(see formula (6.5) in
[
5
]
) which states that
there are Laurent polynomials (independent of
N
)

H_{k}(\mathcal{K};\mathfrak{q})\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{q}^{\pm 1}]
, such that

(1.5)

\displaystyle J_{N}^{\prime}(\mathcal{K};\mathfrak{q})=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}H_{k}(\mathcal{K};\mathfrak{q})\frac{\{N+k\}!}{\{N-1-k\}!\{N\}}.

Habiro proved the existence of such polynomial
H_{k}(\mathcal{K};\mathfrak{q})
via quantum group theory
[
5
]
.
However, it is not easy to compute the explicit formula of
H_{k}(\mathcal{K};\mathfrak{q})
for a given knot
\mathcal{K}
. For the twist knot
\mathcal{K}_{p}
( i.e.
\mathcal{K}_{p,1}
in our notation), the explicit formula for
H_{k}(\mathcal{K}_{p};\mathfrak{q})
was first computed by Habiro in
[
4
]
(see also
[
5
]
), and later given by Masbaum in
[
10
]
by using the Kauffman bracket skein theory.

As to the double twist knot

\mathcal{K}_{p,r}
(where
p,r
are two nonzero integers), one can derive the following expression for
H_{k}(\mathcal{K}_{p,r};\mathfrak{q})
with a slight modification of Masbaum’s computations in
[
10
]
for the twist knot
\mathcal{K}_{p}
( cf.
[
7
,
13
,
8
]
etc.)

(1.6)

\displaystyle H_{k}(\mathcal{K}_{p,r};\mathfrak{q})=(-1)^{k}c^{\prime}_{k,p}c^{\prime}_{k,r},

where

(1.7)

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{k,p}=\{k\}!\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{l}\mathfrak{q}^{2pl(l+1)}\{2l+1\}}{\{k+l+1\}!\{k-l\}!}.

Moreover, Masbaum also provided another multiple sum expression for the formula
c^{\prime}_{k,p}
(see formula (46) in
[
10
]
) which implies that

H_{k}(\mathcal{K}_{p,r};\mathfrak{q})\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{q}^{\pm 1}].

Now we consider the double twist knots with an odd number of half-twists
\mathcal{K}_{p,\frac{s}{2}}
.
In this note, we compute an explicit formula for
H_{k}(\mathcal{K}_{p,\frac{s}{2}};\mathfrak{q})
by using the Kauffman bracket skein theory. The main result is

Theorem 1.1
.

The colored Jones polynomial of
\mathcal{K}_{p,\frac{s}{2}}
is given by

(1.8)

\displaystyle J^{\prime}_{N}(\mathcal{K}_{p,\frac{s}{2}};\mathfrak{q})=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}H_{k}(\mathcal{K}_{p,\frac{s}{2}};\mathfrak{q})\frac{\{N+k\}!}{\{N-1-k\}!\{N\}},

where

(1.9)

\displaystyle H_{k}(\mathcal{K}_{p,\frac{s}{2}};\mathfrak{q})=(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}d_{k,j,p}c^{\prime}_{j,p}\tilde{c}^{\prime}_{j,\frac{s}{2}},

with

(1.10)

\displaystyle d_{k,j,p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=j}^{k}(-1)^{i+j}\mathfrak{q}^{-2pi(i+2)}\frac{\{2i+2\}\{i+1+j\}!}{\{k+i+2\}!\{k-i\}!\{i-j\}!},

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{j,p}

\displaystyle=\{j\}!\sum_{l=0}^{j}\frac{(-1)^{l}\mathfrak{q}^{2pl(l+1)}\{2l+1\}}{\{j+l+1\}!\{j-l\}!},

\displaystyle\tilde{c}^{\prime}_{j,\frac{s}{2}}

\displaystyle=\{j\}!\sum_{l=0}^{j}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{sl(l+1)}\{2l+1\}}{\{j+l+1\}!\{j-l\}!}.

Furthermore, by using the technique of Bailley chains
[
1
]
, we can derive the following multiple sum expressions for the above formulas.

.

We have the following identities

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{k,p}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}q^{\frac{k(k+3)}{4}}\sum_{k=k_{|}p|\geq\cdots\geq k_{1}\geq 0}\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}q^{k_{i}^{2}+k_{i}}\left[\begin{array}[]{@{\,}c@{\,}}k_{i+1}\\

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we review the main results in
[
10
]
. Let
M
be an oriented 3-manifold, the Kauffman bracket skeim module
\mathcal{K}(M)
is the free
\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm 1}]
-module generated by isotopy classes of banded links in
M
modulo the submodule generated by the Kauffman bracket skein relation.

By its definition, it is easy to see that the Kauffman bracket
\langle\ \rangle
of the banded links gives that
\mathcal{K}(S^{3})=\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm 1}]
. We use the normalization that the bracket of the empty link is 1.

Furthermore, the skein module of the solid torus
S^{1}\times D^{2}
is given by
\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm 1}][z]
. Usually, we denote this skein module by
\mathcal{B}
. Here
z
is given by the banded link
S^{1}\times J
, where
J
is a small arc lie in the interior of
D^{2}
, and
z^{n}
means
n
-parallel copies of
z
. We define the even part
\mathcal{B}^{ev}
of
\mathcal{B}
to be the submodule generated by the even powers of
z
.

Let
t:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
denote the twist map induced by a full right handed twist on the solid torus. Then there exists a basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
of eigenvectors for the twist map
t
(see e.g
[
2
]
), which is defined recursively by

(2.1)

\displaystyle e_{0}=1,\ e_{1}=z,\ e_{i}=ze_{i-1}-e_{i-2}.

Moreover, the
e_{i}
satisfies

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle e_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}[i+1]

(2.3)

\displaystyle t(e_{i})

\displaystyle=\mu_{i}e_{i}

where
\mu_{i}=(-1)^{i}A^{i^{2}+2i}
is also called the framing factor which provides the contribution of a framing adding to a banded link.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a knot
\mathcal{K}
with zero framing, the
N
-th colored Jones polynomial
J_{N}(\mathcal{K};\mathfrak{q})
of
\mathcal{K}
is defined to be the Kauffman bracket of
\mathcal{K}
cabled by
(-1)^{N-1}e_{N-1}
, i.e.

(2.4)

\displaystyle J_{N}(\mathcal{K};\mathfrak{q})=(-1)^{N-1}\langle\mathcal{K}(e_{N-1})\rangle

where the factor of
(-1)^{N}
is included such that for the unknot
U
,
J_{N}(U;\mathfrak{q})=[N]
. Furthermore, the normalized
N
-th colored Jones polynomial of
\mathcal{K}
is defined as

(2.5)

\displaystyle J^{\prime}_{N}(\mathcal{K};\mathfrak{q})=\frac{\langle\mathcal{K}(e_{N-1})\rangle}{\langle e_{N-1}\rangle}.

Let
\lambda_{i}=-\mathfrak{q}^{i+1}-\mathfrak{q}^{-i-1}
, we define the skein element
R_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
as follow

(2.6)

\displaystyle R_{n}=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(z-\lambda_{2i}).

Then
R_{n}
forms a basis of
\mathcal{B}
. Therefore, there are coefficients
t_{k,i}
and
s_{i,j}
such that

(2.7)

\displaystyle R_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}t_{k,i}e_{i}

and

(2.8)

\displaystyle e_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}s_{i,j}R_{j}.

By induction, we can check that

(2.9)

\displaystyle t_{k,i}=\frac{\{2k+1\}!\{2i+2\}}{\{k+i+2\}!\{k-i\}!}

and

\displaystyle s_{i,j}=(-1)^{i+j}\left[\begin{array}[]{@{\,}c@{\,}}i+1+j\\
i-j\end{array}\right].

We also need the following two properties for
R_{k}
:

(2.12)

\displaystyle\langle R_{k},e_{2i}\rangle=0\ \text{for}\ i<k,

and

(2.13)

\displaystyle\langle R_{k},e_{2k}\rangle=(-1)^{k}\frac{\{2k+1\}!}{\{1\}!}.

Habiro
[
5
]
introduced the following skein element
\omega_{+}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{n,+}R_{k}\in\mathcal{B}
, where
c_{k,+}
is given by

(2.14)

\displaystyle c_{k,+}=(-1)^{k}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{k(k+3)/2}}{\{k\}!}.

A key property that
\omega_{+}
satisfied is that

(2.15)

\displaystyle\langle\omega_{+},x\rangle=\langle t(x)\rangle

for any

We write

Proof of the cyclotomic expansion formula

We write

Lemma 3.1
.

The coefficient

is given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle d_{k,j}^{(p)}=\sum_{i=j}^{k}(-1)^{i(1+p)+j}\frac{\{2k+1\}!\{2i+2\}!}{\{k+i+2\}!\{k-i\}!}\mathfrak{q}^{p(i+2)i/2}\frac{\{i+1+j\}!}{\{i-j\}!\{2j+1\}!}.

Proof.

This formula is a straightforward consequence of the formulas (
2.7
) and (
2.8
). Indeed,

(3.2)

\displaystyle t^{p}(R_{k})

\displaystyle=t^{p}(\sum_{i=0}^{k}t_{k,i}e_{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{k}t_{k,i}t^{p}(e_{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{k}t_{k,i}\mu_{i}^{p}e_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\mu_{i}^{p}\sum_{j=0}^{i}s_{i,j}R_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{i=j}^{k}t_{k,i}\mu_{i}^{p}s_{i,j}R_{j}.

So we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle d_{k,j}^{(p)}=\sum_{i=j}^{k}t_{k,i}\mu_{i}^{p}s_{i,j}

Actually, there is another technique named Bailley chains

4.1.
Bailey chains

We recall some basics of Bailey chains as illustrated in the appendix of
[
12
]
. The details and proofs can be found in
[
1
]
. A pair of sequences
(a_{k},b_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is called a Bailey pair if they are related by

(4.1)

\displaystyle b_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{a_{j}}{(q;q)_{k-j}(xq;q)_{k+j}}

or equivalently

(4.2)

\displaystyle a_{k}=\frac{1-xq^{2k}}{1-x}\sum_{j=0}^{k-j}q^{(k-j)(k-j-1)/2}\frac{(x;q)_{k+j}}{(q;q)_{k-j}}b_{j}.

Given a Bailey pair
(a_{k},b_{k})_{k\geq 0}
, one can construct a new Bailley pair
(a^{\prime}_{k},b^{\prime}_{k})_{k\geq 0}
by

(4.3)

\displaystyle a^{\prime}_{k}=x^{k}q^{k^{2}}a_{k},\ b^{\prime}_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{x^{j}q^{j^{2}}}{(q;q)}b_{j},\ k=0,1,2,3,...

A sequence
(\alpha^{(1)},\beta^{(1)})\rightarrow(\alpha^{(2)},\beta^{(2)})\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow(\alpha^{(l)},\beta^{(l)})\rightarrow\cdots
obtained by applying iteratively is called a Bailey Chain. The key observation is that two Bailey pairs next to each other in a Bailey chain,

(\alpha_{n}^{(l-1)},\beta_{n}^{(l-1)})_{n\geq 0}
and
(\alpha_{n}^{(l)},\beta_{n}^{(l)})_{n\geq 0}
, satisfy the special version of the Bailey’s lemma

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{\alpha_{j}^{(l)}}{(q;q)_{k-j}(xq;q)_{k+j}}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{x^{j}q^{j^{2}}}{(q;q)_{k-j}}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\frac{\alpha_{k}^{(l-1)}}{(q;q)_{j-k}(xq;q)_{j+k}}.

Using the formula (
4.4
) recursively, we obtain

(4.5)

\displaystyle(q;q)_{k_{p}}\sum_{k_{p-1}=0}^{k_{p}}\frac{\alpha_{k_{p-1}}^{(p)}}{(q;q)_{k_{p}-k_{p-1}}(xq;q)_{k_{p}+k_{p-1}}}

\displaystyle=(q;q)_{k_{p}}\sum_{k_{p-1}}^{k_{p}}\frac{x^{k_{p-1}}q^{k_{p-1}^{2}}}{(q;q)_{k_{p}-k_{p-1}}}\sum_{k_{p-2}=0}^{k_{p-1}}\frac{a_{k_{p-2}}^{(p-1)}}{(q;q)_{k_{p-1}-k_{p-2}}(xq;q)_{k_{p-1}+k_{p-2}}}

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=(q;q)_{k_{p}}\sum_{k_{p}\geq\cdots\geq k_{1}\geq 0}\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}x^{k_{i}}q^{k_{i}^{2}}\frac{1}{(q;q)_{k_{p}-k_{p-1}}\cdots(q;q)_{k_{2}-k_{1}}}\sum_{k_{0}=0}^{k_{1}}\frac{\alpha^{(1)}_{k_{0}}}{(q;q)_{k_{1}-k_{0}}(xq;q)_{k_{1}+k_{0}}}

In particular, taking
x=q
in (
4.5
) and by

\displaystyle\alpha_{l}^{(p)}=q^{(l^{2}+l)(p-1)}\alpha^{(1)}_{l}

we obtain

(4.7)

\displaystyle(q;q)_{k}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{q^{(l^{2}+l)(p-1)}\alpha^{(1)}_{l}(1-q)}{(q;q)_{k-l}(q;q)_{k+l+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=k_{p}\geq\cdots\geq k_{1}\geq 0}\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}q^{k_{i}^{2}+k_{i}}\frac{(q;q)_{k}}{(q;q)_{k_{p}-k_{p-1}}\cdots(q;q)_{k_{2}-k_{1}}}\sum_{k_{0}=0}^{k_{1}}\frac{\alpha^{(1)}_{k_{0}}}{(q;q)_{k_{1}-k_{0}}(q;q)_{k_{1}+k_{0}+1}}

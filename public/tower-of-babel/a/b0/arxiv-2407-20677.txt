Complete corrected formula for generating functions of the hypergeometric distribution
Ken Yamamoto

Abstract
The hypergeometric distribution is a popular distribution, whose properties have been extensively investigated. Generating functions of this distribution, such as the probability-generating function, the moment-generating function, and the characteristic function, are known to involve the Gauss hypergeometric function. We elucidate that the existing formula of generating functions is incomplete and provide the corrected formula. In view of the utility and applicability of the hypergeometric distribution, the exact and correct foundation is crucially important.



Keywords:
hypergeometric distribution; Gauss hypergeometric function; probability-generating function; moment-generating function

Introduction

The hypergeometric distribution is a useful and popular discrete probability distribution in probability theory and statistics.
This distribution describes the number of successes in a sample drawn from a finite population without replacement.
The hypergeometric distribution has been applied to biology
Fury
, complex networks
Casiraghi
, and blockchain technology
Hafid
.
Furthermore, the hypergeometric distribution provides the theoretical basis for Fisher’s exact test
Rivals
and the Mantel–Haenszel test
Wittes
used in medical statistics, and mark–recapture methods
Seber
in ecology.
A simple example of the hypergeometric distribution is picking colored balls from an urn
Johnson
;
Balakrishnan
.
Let an urn contain
K
white balls and
N-K
black balls.
We let
X
denote the number of white balls in
n
balls picked randomly.
The probability that
k
balls are white is given by

\mathrm{Pr}\left(X=k\right)=\left.\binom{K}{k}\binom{N-K}{n-k}\middle/\binom{N}{n}\right..

(1)

This is the probability mass function of the hypergeometric distribution.
The support of this distribution, i.e., the set of
k
with
\mathrm{Pr}\left(X=k\right)>0
, consists of integers within
\max\{0,n+K-N\}\leq k\leq\min\{n,K\}
.
If we consider the factorial of negative integers as infinity, Eq. (
1
) is correct for all integers
k
.

The hypergeometric distribution is related to several distributions.
When picking balls
with replacement
, the number of white balls follows the binomial distribution
Johnson
, which is simpler than the hypergeometric distribution.
When we pick balls one by one without replacement until the number of black balls reaches a given value, the number of white balls follows the negative hypergeometric distribution
Mukhopadhyay
.
The multivariate hypergeometric distribution
Bishop
appears when the balls have more than two colors.
In the Pólya urn model
Mahmoud
, at each step, one ball is drawn, its color is observed, and the ball is returned in the urn with additional balls of the same color.
In this model, the number of white balls follows a distribution referred to as the beta-binomial distribution
Johnson
.

Many publications, e.g.,
Johnson
;
Balakrishnan
;
Bishop
;
Patel
, contain the moment-generating function, the characteristic function, and the probability-generating function of the hypergeometric distribution.
The most fundamental function among them is the probability-generating function

G_{X}(z)\coloneqq E\left[z^{X}\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Pr}\left(X=k\right)z^{k}.

Using
G_{X}
, the moment-generating function is expressed as
M_{X}(t)=G_{X}(e^{t})
, the characteristic function as
\varphi_{X}(t)=G_{X}(e^{it})
(
i=\sqrt{-1}
), and the cumulant-generating function as
K_{X}(t)=\ln G_{X}(e^{t})
.
The existing formula for the probability-generating function of the hypergeometric distribution is

G_{X}(z)=\frac{(N-n)!(N-K)!}{N!(N-K-n)!}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-n,-K;N-K-n+1;z\right).

(2)

Here,
{}_{2}F_{1}
is the Gauss hypergeometric function defined by

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;c;z\right)\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{k}(b)_{k}}{(c)_{k}}\frac{z^{k}}{k!},

where
(a)_{k}
is the Pochhammer symbol:

(a)_{0}=1,\quad(a)_{k}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+k-1).

The Gauss hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;c;z\right)
diverges when
c
is zero or negative integer because
(c)_{k}=0
for
k\geq|c|+1
.
Therefore, Eq. (
2
) is not properly defined when
n\geq N-K+1
.
The factorial
(N-K-n)!
also diverges for such
n
, and Eq. (
2
) takes an indeterminate form “
\infty/\infty
.”
The condition
n\geq N-K+1
indicates that the number
n
of balls drawn is greater than the number of black balls.
In this study, we aim to establish the complete formula for
G_{X}(z)
by providing the determinate expression for
n\geq N-K+1
.

Theorem 1.1

(Main result)
.

The complete probability-generating function of
X
becomes

\displaystyle G_{X}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{(N-n)!(N-K)!}{N!(N-K-n)!}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-n,-K;N-K-n+1;z\right)

n\leq N-K
,

(3a)

\displaystyle G_{X}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{n!K!}{N!(n+K-N)!}z^{n+K-N}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(n-N,K-N;n+K-N+1;z\right)

n\geq N-K+1
.

(3b)

Equation (
3b
) is the correct determinate formula for
n\geq N-K+1
.
Although this result might be trivial for expert mathematicians, the explicit expression will be useful for students, engineers, practitioners, and programmers who are not necessarily familiar with mathematics.

\displaystyle=M(M-1)\cdots(M-j+1)=(-1)^{j}(-M)(-M+1)\cdots(-M+j-1)=(-1)^{j}(-M)_{j}

Second proof.

We introduce the random variable

as the number of

The number of white balls left becomes

\displaystyle G_{X^{\prime}}(z)

\displaystyle G_{X}(z)

\displaystyle=E\left[z^{X}\right]=E\left[z^{X^{\prime}+n+K-N}\right]=z^{n+K-N}G_{X^{\prime}}(z)

∎

4
Moments

A typical application of the probability- and moment-generating functions is the calculation of moments.
In many books, e.g.,
Johnson
;
Balakrishnan
;
Mukhopadhyay
;
Bishop
;
Patel
, the mean and variance of the hypergeometric distribution become

E\left[X\right]=\frac{nK}{N},\quad V\left[X\right]=\frac{n(N-n)K(N-K)}{N^{2}(N-1)},

respectively.
More generally, factorial moments,
E\left[X(X-1)\cdots(X-r+1)\right]
for
r=1,2,\ldots
, can be exactly calculated for the hypergeometric distribution:

E\left[X(X-1)\cdots(X-r+1)\right]=\frac{n!K!(N-r)!}{N!(K-r)!(N-r)!}.

(9)

Although direct computation of Eq. (
9
) based on the probability mass function (
1
) is possible, the factorial moments can be systematically calculated using the probability-generating function
G_{X}
as

E\left[X(X-1)\cdots(X-r+1)\right]=\left.\frac{d^{r}G_{X}(z)}{dz^{r}}\right|_{z=1}.

Using the probability-generating function in Eq. (
3a
), the factorial moment in Eq. (
9
) can be easily obtained using formulas (see
Olver
):

\frac{d^{r}}{dz^{r}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;c;z\right)=\frac{(a)_{r}(b)_{r}}{(c)_{r}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a+r,b+r;c+r;z\right)

and

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;c;1\right)=\frac{\Gamma(c)\Gamma(c-a-b)}{\Gamma(c-a)\Gamma(c-b)}.

What computation is performed for
n\geq N-K+1
in which Eq. (
3b
) is applied?
The effective formula
Olver
in this case is

\frac{d^{r}}{dz^{r}}(z^{c-1}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;c;z\right))=(c-r)_{r}z^{c-r-1}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;c-r;z\right).

(10)

For
{}_{2}F_{1}\left(n-N,K-N;n+K-N+1;z\right)
(
a=n-N
,
b=K-N
, and
c=n+K-N+1
) in Eq. (
3b
),
c-r=n+K-N+1-r
becomes
0
or a negative integer when
r\geq n+K-N+1
.
Therefore,
{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;c-r;z\right)
cannot be defined for such
r
, and a special treatment is necessary.

Proposition 4.1

(Supplement formula to Eq. (
10
))
.

For positive integers
m
and
r
which satisfy
m\leq r
,

\frac{d^{r}}{dz^{r}}(z^{m-1}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;m;z\right))=\frac{(m-1)!(a)_{r-m+1}(b)_{r-m+1}}{(r-m+1)!}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a+r-m+1,b+r-m+1;r-m+2;z\right).

Proof.

Using the gamma function instead of the Pochhammer symbol, Eq. (
10
) can be written as

\frac{d^{r}}{dz^{r}}(z^{c-1}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;c;z\right))=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(c-r)}z^{c-r-1}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;c-r;z\right).

In taking the
c\to m
limit, Eq. (
4
) is used because

1.
Introduction

Whittaker modules are important objects in the representation theory since
its emergence in the study of complex semisimple Lie algebras. Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}_{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{+}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra and let
\eta:\mathfrak{n}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a Lie algebra homomorphism. A
\mathfrak{g}
-module is called a Whittaker module if
x-\eta(x)
acts locally nilpotent on it for any
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{+}
. Whittaker modules were first defined for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
by Arnal and Pinzcon
[
2
]
. Kostant
[
18
]
defined
Whittaker modules for all complex semisimple Lie algebras. When
\eta
is non-singular, Kostant showed that simple Whittaker modules are in bijective
correspondence with maximal ideals of the center
Z(\mathfrak{g})
of the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. For quantum groups, Whittaker modules have been studied in
[
26
,
34
,
35
]
. Whittaker modules have also been studied for
the Virasoro algebra in
[
27
,
28
,
15
,
22
,
23
]
, for Heisenberg
algebras in
[
12
]
, for Weyl algebras and generalized Weyl algebras in
[
7
]
, for affine Lie algebras in
[
1
,
10
]
and for classical Lie-super algebras in
[
8
]
. In
[
6
]
, the authors gave a more general picture of Whittaker modules for many Lie algebras which includes, in particular, Lie algebras with triangular decomposition and simple Lie algebras of Cartan type. They described some basic properties of Whittaker modules, including a block decomposition of the category of Whittaker modules and some properties of simple Whittaker modules under some assumptions.

Among the theory of infinite dimensional Lie algebras,
the Lie algebras of polynomial vector fields on irreducible affine algebraic varieties is an important class of Lie
algebras. These algebras contain the Cartan type Lie algebra
W_{n}
which is the derivation Lie algebra of
A_{n}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]
. Weight modules with finite dimensional weight spaces (also called Harish-Chandra modules) for
W_{n}
has been well developed. In 1974-1975, A. Rudakov
addressed the classification of a class of irreducible
W_{n}
-representations
that satisfy some natural topological conditions, see
[
31
,
32
]
.
Tensor modules
T(P,V)
over
W_{n}
were introduced by Shen and Larsson, see
[
33
,
19
]
, for a
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module
P
and
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module V, where
\mathcal{D}_{n}
is the Weyl algebra. In
[
20
]
, the structure of any
T(P,V)
was completely determined. When
V
is finite dimensional, the simplicity of
T(P,V)
over the Witt algebra
on an
n
-dimensional torus was given by Rao, see
[
13
]
.
The
classification of simple Harish-Chandra modules over
W_{1}
was given in
[
24
]
. Very recently,
based on the classification of simple uniformly bounded
W_{n}
modules (see
[
9
]
for
n=2
and
[
36
]

for any
n
) and the description of the weight set of simple weight
W_{n}
modules
(see
[
29
]
), D. Grantcharov and V. Serganova completed the classification of simple Harish-Chandra
W_{n}
-modules, see
[
17
]
. They proved that every such nontrivial module
M
is a simple sub-quotient of some tensor module
T(P,V)
.
In
[
11
]
, tilting modules and their character formulas for category
\mathcal{O}
of
W_{n}
were described.
However non-weight modules and weight modules that are not
Harish-Chandra modules for
W_{n}
are not well developed.

In this paper, we study the category of Whittaker modules for
W_{n}
.
Let

\Delta_{n}=\text{Span}\{\frac{\partial}{\partial{t_{1}}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial{t_{n}}}\}
which is a commutative subalgebra of
W_{n}
.
Then
(W_{n},\Delta_{n})
is a Whittaker pair in the sense of
[
6
]
.
A
W_{n}
-module
M
on which
\Delta_{n}
operates locally finite is called a Whittaker module. We denote the semidirect Lie algebra
W_{n}\ltimes A_{n}
by
\widetilde{W}_{n}
. A
\widetilde{W}_{n}
-module
M
is called an
(A_{n},W_{n})
-module, if the action of
A_{n}
on

is associative.
This kind of modules play an important role in the classification of
simple modules for Cartan type Lie algebras, see
[
3
,
4
,
16
,
14
,
36
]
. For an
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and a Whittaker
W_{n}
-module
M
, a nonzero

is called a Whittaker vector if
\frac{\partial}{\partial{t_{i}}}v=a_{i}v
for any

.
Let
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
(resp.

-modules (resp.
(A_{n},W_{n})

, where

, see Theorem
3.4
. When
\mathbf{a}\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
,

is a free

. Using the covering method introduced in
[

is isomorphic to some simple sub-quotient of

for some finite dimensional simple

-module

and its endomorphism algebra

. When
\mathbf{a}\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
, we show that
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
and the category of finite dimensional

-modules are equivalent, see Theorem

. A similar equivalence for finite

was shown by Serge Skryabin, see the appendix in
[

.

We denote by
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

and

the sets of integers, positive integers, nonnegative
integers, complex numbers, and nonzero complex numbers, respectively. For a Lie algebra

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Witt algebras
W_{n}

Throughout this paper,
n
denotes a positive integer.
We fix the vector space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
n
-dimensional complex vectors.
Denote its standard basis by
\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{n}\}
.

Denote by
A_{n}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n}]
the polynomial algebra in the commuting variables
t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n}
.
Let
\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial{t_{i}}},h_{i}=t_{i}\partial_{i}
for any
i=1,2,\cdots,n
.
For the convenience, set

t^{\mathbf{m}}=t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{m_{n}}
,
h^{\mathbf{m}}=h_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots h_{n}^{m_{n}}
and
|\mathbf{m}|=m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n}
for any
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
.
Recall that the classical Witt algebra
W_{n}=\text{Der}(A_{n})=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}{A}_{n}\partial_{i}
is a free
A_{n}
-module of rank
n
. It has the following Lie bracket:

[t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{i},t^{\mathbf{r}}\partial_{j}]=r_{i}t^{\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\partial_{j}-m_{j}t^{\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\partial_{i},

where all
\mathbf{m},\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
.

It is well-known that
W_{n}
is a simple Lie algebra.
The subspace
\mathfrak{h}_{n}=\mathbb{C}h_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{C}h_{n}
is a Cartan subalgebra (a maximal
abelian subalgebra that is diagonalizable on
W_{n}
with respect to the
adjoint action) of
W_{n}
.

Note that
A_{n}
is a
W_{n}
-module under the derivation action of
W_{n}
. We denote the semidirect Lie algebra
W_{n}\ltimes A_{n}
by
\widetilde{W}_{n}
which is called the extended Witt algebra. Let
W_{n}
-
Mod
be the category of finitely generated left
U(W_{n})
-modules.

Definition 2.1
.

A
W_{n}
-module
M
is called a weight module if the action of
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
on
M

is diagonalizable, i.e.,

M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}}M_{\lambda},
where

M_{\lambda}=\{v\in M\mid hv=\lambda(h)v,\forall\ h\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}\}.

A weight
W_{n}
-module
M
is uniformly bounded if the dimension of each weight space
M_{\lambda}
is smaller than a fixed integer.

Definition 2.2
.

A module
M
over
\widetilde{W}_{n}
is called an
(A_{n},W_{n})
-module if the action of
A_{n}
on
M
is associative, i.e.,
g(fv)=(gf)v
, for any
f,g\in A_{n},v\in M
.

Example 2.1
.

(
a
)

The natural module
A_{n}
is an
(A_{n},W_{n})
-module.

(
b
)

The adjoint
W_{n}
-module
W_{n}
is an
(A_{n},W_{n})
-module under the following action:

f(t)\cdot g(t)\partial_{i}=(f(t)g(t))\partial_{i},\ \ f(t),g(t)\in A_{n},

where
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

2.2.
Whittaker modules

Denote
\Delta_{n}=\text{span}\{\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}\}
which is an abelian subalgebra of
W_{n}
.
Since the adjoint action of
\Delta_{n}
on
W_{n}/\Delta_{n}
is locally nilpotent. Thus
(W_{n},\Delta_{n})
is a Whittaker pair in the sense of
[
6
]
. A
W_{n}
-module
M
is called a Whittaker module if
the action of
\Delta_{n}
on
M
is locally finite.
For an
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we can define a
Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi_{\mathbf{a}}:\Delta_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
\phi_{\mathbf{a}}(\partial_{i})=a_{i}
for any
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
. A Whittaker module
M
is of type
\phi_{\mathbf{a}}
if for any
v\in M
there is a
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(x-\phi_{\mathbf{a}}(x))^{k}v=0
for all
x\in\Delta_{n}
.
We also define the subspace of
M
as follows:

\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)=\{v\in M\mid xv=\phi_{\mathbf{a}}(x)v,\ \forall\ x\in\Delta_{n}\}

of
M
.
An element in
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
is called a Whittaker vector.

We consider Whittaker modules under some natural finite condition. Let
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
be the category consisting of Whittaker
W_{n}
-modules of type
\phi_{\mathbf{a}}
such that
\dim\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)<+\infty
. Similarly let
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{\widetilde{W}}
be the category consisting of Whittaker
(A_{n},W_{n})
-modules of type
\phi_{\mathbf{a}}
such that
\dim\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)<+\infty
.

For a vector
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we call it non-singular if
a_{i}\neq 0
for any
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
.

2.3.
Weighting functor

In this subsection, we recall the weighting functor introduced in

[
25
]
, which establishes a relationship between weight modules and non-weight modules.
For an
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
I_{\mathbf{r}}
be the maximal ideal of
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
generated by

h_{1}-r_{1},\dots,h_{n}-r_{n}.

For any
W_{n}
-module
M
and
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, set
M^{\mathbf{r}}:=M/I_{\mathbf{r}}M
.
Denote

\mathfrak{W}(M):=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}M^{\mathbf{r}}.

By Proposition 8 in
[
25
]
, we have the following construction.

Proposition 2.3
.

The vector space
\mathfrak{W}(M)
becomes a weight
W_{n}
-module under the following action:

(2.1)

t^{\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}\partial_{i}\cdot(v+I_{\mathbf{r}}M):=t^{\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}\partial_{i}v+I_{\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{m}}M,v\in M,\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},

where
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
with
m_{i}\geq-1
.

In Section 4, we will use the weighting functor to show that the
(A_{n},W_{n})
-cover
\widehat{M}
has a finite composition series as an
(A_{n},W_{n})
-module for any module
M
in
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
when
\mathbf{a}
is non-singular.

3.
Whittaker
(A_{n},W_{n})
-modules

In this section, we study Whittaker
(A_{n},W_{n})
-modules. Explicitly, we establish an equivalence between
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{\widetilde{W}}
and
the category of finite dimensional
L_{n}
-modules.

3.1.
Whittaker modules over
\mathcal{D}_{n}

Let
\mathcal{D}_{n}
be the Weyl algebra
over
A_{n}
which is the unital associative algebra
over
\mathbb{C}
generated by
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
,

\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}
subject to
the relations

[\partial_{i},\partial_{j}]=[t_{i},t_{j}]=0,\qquad[\partial_{i},t_{j}]=\delta_{i,j},\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

A
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module
M
is called a Whittaker module of type
\mathbf{a}
if for any
v\in M
there is a
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(\partial_{i}-a_{i})^{k}v=0
for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
.
Let
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbf{a}}^{D}
be the category consisting of finite length Whittaker
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-modules of type
\mathbf{a}
.

Let
\sigma_{\mathbf{a}}
be the algebra automorphism of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
defined by

t_{i}\mapsto t_{i},\partial_{i}\mapsto\partial_{i}+a_{i}.

The
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module
A_{n}
can be twisted by
\sigma_{\mathbf{a}}
to be a new
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module
A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}
. Explicitly the the action of

\mathcal{D}_{n}
on
A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}
is defined by

X\cdot f(t)=\sigma_{\mathbf{a}}(X)f(t),\ \ f(t)\in A_{n},X\in\mathcal{D}_{n}.

Lemma 3.1
.

(
a
)

Any simple module in
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbf{a}}^{D}
is isomorphic to
A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}
.

(
b
)

The category
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbf{a}}^{D}
is semi-simple.

Proof.

(a) For any
f(t)\in A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}
, the formula
(\partial_{i}-a_{i})\cdot t^{\mathbf{m}}=m_{i}t^{\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}
tells us that
the degree of
t_{i}
in
(\partial_{i}-a_{i})f(t)
is smaller than that of
t_{i}
in
f(t)
. By induction on the degree of
f(t)
, any submodule of
A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}

must contain
1
which generates
A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}
. So
A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}
is a simple

\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module. If
V
is a simple module in
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbf{a}}^{D}
, then there is a nonzero
v\in V
such that
\partial_{i}v=a_{i}v
for any
i
. Then the map
V\rightarrow A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}},t^{\mathbf{m}}v\mapsto t^{\mathbf{m}}
defines a
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module isomorphism between

V
and
A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}
.

(b) Since
A^{\mathbf{a}}_{n}
is equivalent to
A_{n}
up to the algebra automorphism
\sigma_{\mathbf{a}}
of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
defined by
t_{i}\mapsto t_{i},\partial_{i}\mapsto\partial_{i}+a_{i}
,
\text{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}(A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}},A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}})\simeq\text{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}(A_{n},A_{n})
. By Lemma 3 in
[
21
]
,
\text{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}(A_{n},A_{n})=0
. So
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbf{a}}^{D}
is semi-simple.
∎

3.2.
The Whittaker category
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{\widetilde{W}}

Since
A_{n}
is a left module algebra over the Hopf algebra
U(W_{n})
, we have the smash product algebra
A_{n}\#U(W_{n})
. An
(A_{n},W_{n})
-module is actually a module
over
A_{n}\#U(W_{n})
.

Let
L_{n}
be the Lie subalgebra of
W_{n}
spanned by
t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n,\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
with
|\mathbf{m}|\geq 1
which is called the jet Lie algebra of
W_{n}
.

The following isomorphism was given in
[
36
]
or
[
5
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The linear map
\phi:A_{n}\#U(W_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(L_{n})
defined by

\displaystyle\phi(t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{k})

\displaystyle=t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{k}\otimes 1+\sum_{\mathbf{r}\neq 0,\atop r_{i}\leq m_{i}}\binom{\mathbf{m}}{\mathbf{r}}t^{\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{r}}\otimes t^{\mathbf{r}}\partial_{k},

\displaystyle\phi(t^{\mathbf{m}})

\displaystyle=t^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes 1,\ \ \ \mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n},k\in\{1,\cdots,n\},

is an associative algebra isomorphism,
where
\binom{\mathbf{m}}{\mathbf{r}}=\binom{m_{1}}{r_{1}}\cdots\binom{m_{n}}{r_{n}}
.

For any
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module
P
, and
L_{n}
-module
V
, the tensor product
P\otimes V
becomes an
(A_{n},W_{n})
-module
T(P,V)
under the map

\phi
.

The isomorphism in Theorem
3.2
tells us that there is a close relationship
between vector field Lie algebras and Weyl algebras.

Let
L_{n}\text{-mod}
be the category of finite dimensional
L_{n}
-modules. When
V
is finite dimensional,
we can check that
T(A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}},V)\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{\widetilde{W}}
and the Whittaker vectors space
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(T(A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}},V))=V
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
M\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{\widetilde{W}}
, the
Whittaker vector subspace
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
is an
L_{n}
-module
V
and
T(A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}},V)\cong M
.

Proof.

Through the isomorphism
\phi
in Theorem
3.2
, we can view
M
as a module over
\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(L_{n})
. In
\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(L_{n})
,
[\mathcal{D}_{n},L_{n}]=0
. So
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
is an
L_{n}
-module.
We claim that the
\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(L_{n})
-module homomorphism

\displaystyle\xi:A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}\otimes\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)

\displaystyle\rightarrow M,

\displaystyle t^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes v

\displaystyle\mapsto t^{\mathbf{m}}v,\ \mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n},

is an isomorphism. By Lemma
3.1
,
\xi
is surjective. Suppose that
w=\sum_{\mathbf{s}\in\Lambda}t^{\mathbf{s}}\otimes v_{\mathbf{s}}\in\ker\xi
, where
\Lambda
is a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
.
For any
\mathbf{m}\in\Lambda
,
by the simplicity of
A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}
and the density theorem, there is some

X\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
such that
Xt^{\mathbf{m}}=1
and
Xt^{\mathbf{s}}=0
for any
\mathbf{s}\in\Lambda
with
\mathbf{s}\neq\mathbf{m}
. Then
X\xi(w)=\xi(Xw)=\xi(1\otimes v_{\mathbf{m}})=v_{\mathbf{m}}=0
. Consequently
w=0
. So
\xi
is also injective. Therefore,
\xi
is an isomorphism.
∎

In the following theorem, we show that each block
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{\widetilde{W}}
is equivalent to

L_{n}\text{-mod}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

The functor

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:L_{n}\text{-mod}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{\widetilde{W}},

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\mapsto T(A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}},V),

is an equivalence of the two categories.

Proof.

By Schur’s Lemma,
\text{End}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}(A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}})=\mathbb{C}
. From Lemma
3.3
, for any
M\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{\widetilde{W}}
,
there is an
L_{n}
-module
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
such that

\mathcal{F}(\text{Wh}(M))\cong M
. From
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(L_{n})}(A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}\otimes V,A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}\otimes W)\cong\text{Hom}_{U(L_{n})}(V,W)
,
we see that
the homomorphism

\mathcal{F}_{V,W}:\text{Hom}_{L_{n}}(V,W)\rightarrow\text{Hom}_{\widetilde{W}_{n}}(\mathcal{F}(V),\mathcal{F}(W))

is an isomorphism,
for any modules
V,W\in L_{n}\text{-mod}
. Therefore
\mathcal{F}
is an equivalence of the two categories.
∎

Next we give all simple Whittaker

-modules. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal of
A_{n}
generated by

t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}

is a Lie subalgebra of

which is equal to
L_{n}
.

The following lemma was given in
[
36
]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

(
a
)

The linear map
\pi:\mathfrak{m}\Delta_{n}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}\Delta_{n}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
such that

\displaystyle t_{i}\partial_{j}

\displaystyle\mapsto e_{ij},\ \ i,j\in\{1,\cdots,n\},

is a Lie algebra isomorphism.

(
b
)

If
V
is a finite dimensional simple
L_{n}
-module, then
\mathfrak{m}^{2}\Delta_{n}V=0
. So

is a simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
via the isomorphism in (a).

By Lemma
3.5
and Theorem
3.2
, for any simple finite dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V
, we can define an
(A_{n},W_{n})
-module structure on

as follows:

\displaystyle t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{k}(p\otimes v)

\displaystyle=t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{k}p\otimes v+\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}t^{\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}p\otimes e_{ik}v,

\displaystyle t^{\mathbf{m}}(p\otimes v)

\displaystyle=(t^{\mathbf{m}}p)\otimes v,\ \ \ \mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n},k\in\{1,\cdots,n\},

where
p\in A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}},v\in V
.

Combining Theorem
3.4
with Lemma
3.5
, we obtain the following description of simple Whittaker
(A_{n},W_{n})
-modules.

Corollary 3.6
.

If
M
is a simple Whittaker
(A_{n},W_{n})
-module of type
\phi_{\mathbf{a}}
such that

for some finite dimensional simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V
.

4.
Whittaker
W_{n}
-modules

Throughout this section, we always assume that
\mathbf{a}
is non-singular, i.e.,
\mathbf{a}\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
. In this case, a module in
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
is said to be non-singular. In this section, we classify simple
W_{n}
-modules in
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
. We also obtain an analogue of Skryabin’s equivalence for the category
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
.

4.1.
Preliminary lemmas

The following result gives an interesting property of non-singular Whittaker modules.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
M
is a Whittaker
W_{n}
-module of type
\phi_{\mathbf{a}}
, then

M=U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M):=\text{Span}\{h^{\mathbf{m}}v\mid v\in\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M),\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}\}.

Moreover, if
\{v_{i}\mid i\in\Lambda\}
is a basis of
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
, then

\{h^{\mathbf{m}}v_{i}\mid\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n},i\in\Lambda\}

is a basis of
M
.

Proof.

For an
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, define

\partial_{\mathbf{m}}=(\partial_{1}-a_{1})^{m_{1}}\cdots(\partial_{n}-a_{n})^{m_{n}}.

Note that the elements
\partial_{\mathbf{m}}
with
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
form a basis for
U(\Delta_{n})
. Consider the total order on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
satisfying the condition:
\mathbf{r}<\mathbf{m}
if
|\mathbf{r}|<|\mathbf{m}|
or

|\mathbf{r}|=|\mathbf{m}|
and
there is an
l\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
such that
r_{i}=m_{i}
when
1\leq i<l
and
r_{l}<m_{l}
. For each
\mathbf{m}
, the set
\{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}\mid\mathbf{r}<\mathbf{m}\}
is a finite set. So
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
as an ordered set.
For a nonzero
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, denote by
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}
the predecessor
of
\mathbf{m}
.

Using the condition that
a_{i}\neq 0
for any
i
and induction on
\mathbf{m}
, we can show that for any
\mathbf{m},\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
and nonzero
v\in\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
, we have that
\partial_{\mathbf{s}}h^{\mathbf{m}}v=0
whenever
\mathbf{s}>\mathbf{m}
,

\partial_{\mathbf{m}}h^{\mathbf{m}}v=k_{\mathbf{m}}v
for some nonzero scalar
k_{\mathbf{m}}
.

For each
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, denote by
\mathbb{I}_{\mathbf{m}}
the ideal of
U(\Delta_{n})
spanned by
\partial_{\mathbf{s}}
with
\mathbf{s}>\mathbf{m}
, and
M_{\mathbf{m}}=\{w\in M\mid\mathbb{I}_{\mathbf{m}}w=0\}
. Clearly
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)=M_{\mathbf{0}}
.
For any nonzero
w\in M
, by the definition of Whittaker modules, there is an

\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
such that
w\in M_{\mathbf{m}}\setminus M_{\mathbf{m}^{\prime}}
, i.e.,

\partial_{\mathbf{m}}w\neq 0
and
\partial_{\mathbf{s}}w=0
for any
\mathbf{s}>\mathbf{m}
. So
\partial_{\mathbf{m}}w\in\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
. By the above discussion,
\partial_{\mathbf{m}}h^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{\mathbf{m}}w=k_{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{\mathbf{m}}w
.
We call
\mathbf{m}
the degree of
w
. We use induction on the degree
\mathbf{m}
of
w
to show that
w\in U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
. Let
w^{\prime}=w-\frac{1}{k_{\mathbf{m}}}h^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{\mathbf{m}}w
. Then

\partial_{\mathbf{m}}w^{\prime}=\partial_{\mathbf{m}}w-\frac{1}{k_{\mathbf{m}}}\partial_{\mathbf{m}}h^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{\mathbf{m}}w=0.

This implies that the degree of
w^{\prime}
is smaller than
\mathbf{m}
. By the induction
hypothesis,
w^{\prime}\in U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
. Hence
w\in U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
.

Suppose that
w:=\sum_{\mathbf{r}\leq\mathbf{m}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{\mathbf{r},i}h^{\mathbf{r}}v_{i}=0
in
M
, where
c_{\mathbf{r},i}\in\mathbb{C}
.
From

\partial_{\mathbf{m}}w=0
, we see that
c_{\mathbf{m},i}=0
. Then by induction on
\mathbf{m}
, we have that

c_{\mathbf{r},i}=0
for any
\mathbf{r}<\mathbf{m}
and
i
. Thus
\{h^{\mathbf{m}}v_{i}\mid\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n},i\in\Lambda\}
is linearly independent. In conclusion, we complete the proof.

∎

From Lemma
4.1
, if
M
is a Whittaker
W_{n}
-module of type
\phi_{\mathbf{a}}
, then

M
is a free
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
-module and
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
is a basis of

M
as a free
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
-module.

Corollary 4.2
.

Any nontrivial
W_{n}
-module
M\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
is a free
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
-module of finite rank.

Corollary 4.3
.

The category
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
is an abelian category.

Proof.

Suppose that
M\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
and
N
is an arbitrary
W_{n}
-submodule of
M
. Since
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(N)\subset\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
,
N\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
. By Lemma
4.1
,
M/N
is a free
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
module whose rank is not bigger than that of
M
.
Thus
\dim\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M/N)<\infty
and hence
M/N\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
.
∎

Next let us recall the
(A_{n},W_{n})
-cover introduced in
[
4
]
in a slightly
different form.
Let
\Delta
be the co-multiplication of
U(\widetilde{W}_{n})
, that is

\Delta:U(\widetilde{W}_{n})\rightarrow U(\widetilde{W}_{n})\otimes U(\widetilde{W}_{n}),X\mapsto X\otimes 1+1\otimes X,\ \ X\in\widetilde{W}_{n}.

Let
\pi:U(\widetilde{W}_{n})\rightarrow U(W_{n})
be the homomorphism such that

\pi|_{W_{n}}=\text{id}_{W_{n}},\pi|_{A_{n}}=0
. Denote the composition

(\text{id}_{\widetilde{W}_{n}}\otimes\pi)\cdot\Delta:U(\widetilde{W}_{n})\rightarrow U(\widetilde{W}_{n})\otimes U(W_{n})

by
\Delta^{\prime}
. Then for any
(A_{n},W_{n})
-module
P
and
W_{n}
-module
M
,
P\otimes M
becomes an
(A_{n},W_{n})
-module under the pulling back of
\Delta^{\prime}
. In particular,

W_{n}\otimes M
can be defined to be an
(A_{n},W_{n})
-module by

t^{\mathbf{r}}\cdot(t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{i}\otimes w)=t^{\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{r}}\partial_{i}\otimes w,\ \ w\in M,\mathbf{r},\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}.

Define a linear map
\theta:W_{n}\otimes M\rightarrow M
such that

t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{i}\otimes w\mapsto t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{i}w,\ w\in M,\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}.

One can check directly that
\theta
is a
W_{n}
-module homomorphism.
Define

K(M)=\{v\in\ker\theta\mid A_{n}\cdot v\subset\ker\theta\}

which is an
(A_{n},W_{n})
-submodule of

.
Let
\widehat{M}=(W_{n}\otimes M)/K(M)
which is called the
(A_{n},W_{n})
-cover of

M
. It is natural that
\theta
induces a
W_{n}
-module homomorphism

\hat{\theta}:\widehat{M}\rightarrow M
. For any
X\in W_{n}
,
v\in M
,
denote the image of
X\otimes v
in
\widehat{M}
by
X\boxtimes v
.

4.2.
Simple Whittaker modules

For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{\beta}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
, in
U(W_{n})
, we denote the following operator:

\omega_{\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{\beta}}^{m,j,l,p}:=\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}t^{\mathbf{\alpha}+(m-i)\mathbf{e}_{j}}\partial_{l}\cdot t^{\mathbf{\beta}+i\mathbf{e}_{j}}\partial_{p}.

The following lemma was given by Xue and Lu, see
[
36
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
M
is a uniformly bounded weight module over
W_{n}
, then
there is an
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\omega_{\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{\beta}}^{m,j,l,p}M=0
for all

j,l,p\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{\beta}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
.

Using Lemma
4.4
, we show that for any simple module
M
in

\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
,
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(\widehat{M})
is finite dimensional.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that
M
is a simple module in
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
.
Then
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(\widehat{M})
is finite dimensional.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
and Corollary
4.2
,
M
is a free
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
-module of finite rank. Thus
\mathfrak{W}(M)
is a uniformly bounded weight
W_{n}
-module. In fact each weight space of

\mathfrak{W}(M)
has dimension no bigger than the rank of
M

as a free
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
-module. From Lemma
4.4
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\omega_{\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s}}^{m,j,l,p}\mathfrak{W}(M)=0,

for all all

j,l,p\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
.

By the definition of
\mathfrak{W}(M)
, we deduce that

\omega_{\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s}}^{m,j,l,p}M\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathbf{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}I_{\mathbf{\alpha}}M=0,\ \forall\ j,l,p\in\{1,\dots,n\},\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0},

where
I_{\mathbf{\alpha}}
is the maximal ideal of
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
generated by
h_{1}-\alpha_{1},\dots,h_{n}-\alpha_{n}
. The result
\bigcap_{\mathbf{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}I_{\mathbf{\alpha}}M=0
follows from that
M
is a finitely generated free
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
-module.

The simplicity of
M
implies that

M=W_{n}M
. Next we prove by induction on
|\mathbf{r}|
that

t^{\mathbf{r}}\partial_{l}\boxtimes t^{\mathbf{s}}\partial_{p}v\in\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{|\mathbf{\alpha}|\leq mn}t^{\mathbf{\alpha}}\partial_{i}\boxtimes M,

for all
v\in M,\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},p,l\in\{1,\cdots,n\}.

This is clear for
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
with
|\mathbf{r}|\leq mn
. Now we assume that
|\mathbf{r}|>mn
. Without lose of generality, we may assume that
r_{1}>m
.

For any
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
, we have

\displaystyle\theta(\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}{m\choose i}t^{\mathbf{r}-i\mathbf{e}_{1}}\partial_{l}\otimes(t^{\mathbf{s}+i\mathbf{e}_{1}}\partial_{p}v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}{m\choose i}t^{\mathbf{r}-i\mathbf{e}_{1}}\partial_{l}t^{\mathbf{s}+i\mathbf{e}_{1}}\partial_{p}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s}}^{m,1,l,p}(v)=0,

which implies

\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}{m\choose i}t^{\mathbf{r}-i\mathbf{e}_{1}}\partial_{l}\otimes t^{\mathbf{s}+i\mathbf{e}_{1}}\partial_{p}v\in K(M).

This means that

\displaystyle t^{\mathbf{r}}\partial_{l}\boxtimes t^{\mathbf{s}}\partial_{p}v=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i}{m\choose i}t^{\mathbf{r}-i\mathbf{e}_{1}}\partial_{l}\boxtimes t^{\mathbf{s}+i\mathbf{e}_{1}}\partial_{p}v,

in

\widehat{M}

which belongs to
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{|\mathbf{r}|\leq mn}t^{\mathbf{r}}\partial_{i}\boxtimes M

by the induction hypothesis.

Therefore
\widehat{M}
is a finitely generated
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
-module. By Lemma
4.1
,
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(\widehat{M})
is finite dimensional. Otherwise,
\widehat{M}
is a free
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
-module of infinite rank.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

If
M
is a simple module in
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
, then
M
is isomorphic to some simple sub-quotient of
T(A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}},V)
for some finite dimensional simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V
.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.5
, there is an
(A_{n},W_{n})
-module
M_{1}
such that
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M_{1})
is finite dimensional and
a
W_{n}
-module epimorphism
\psi:M_{1}\rightarrow M
. We may choose
M_{1}
such that
\dim\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M_{1})
is minimal.
The minimality of
M_{1}
forces that
M_{1}
is a simple
(A_{n},W_{n})
-module. Otherwise,
M_{1}
has a nonzero maximal
(A_{n},W_{n})
-submodule
M_{2}
.
Then from corollary
4.2
,
M_{2}
and
M_{1}/M_{2}
are free
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
module of rank both less than
\dim\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M_{1})
. Because
M
is a simple
W_{n}
-module, we must have either
\psi(M_{2})=M
or
\psi(M_{2})=0
. Then either
M_{2}
or
M_{1}/M_{2}
has a simple
W_{n}
-quotient module isomorphic to
M
, contradicting the minimality of
M_{1}
. From Corollary
3.6
, we know that
M_{1}\cong T(A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}},V)
for a finite dimensional simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V
. Hence
M
is a simple
W_{n}
-quotient of
T(A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}},V)
.∎

4.3.
An analogue of Skryabin’s equivalence

Let
\mathbb{C}_{\mathbf{a}}=\mathbb{C}1_{\mathbf{a}}
be the one dimensional
U(\Delta_{n})
-module such that
\partial_{i}1_{\mathbf{a}}=a_{i}1_{\mathbf{a}}
for any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Let

Q_{\mathbf{a}}=U(W_{n})\otimes_{U(\Delta_{n})}\mathbb{C}_{\mathbf{a}},

an induced
W_{n}
-module, and

H_{\mathbf{a}}=\text{End}_{W_{n}}(Q_{\mathbf{a}})^{\text{op}},

an associative algebra over
\mathbb{C}
. Then
Q_{\mathbf{a}}
is both a left
U(W_{n})
-module and a right
H_{\mathbf{a}}
-module. Let
H_{\mathbf{a}}\text{-mod}
be the category of finite dimensional
H_{\mathbf{a}}
-modules.

Theorem 4.8
.

(
a
)

As a right
H_{\mathbf{a}}
-module,
Q_{\mathbf{a}}
is free. More precisely, the subset

\{h^{\mathbf{m}}\otimes 1_{\mathbf{a}}\mid\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}\}

is
a basis of
Q_{\mathbf{a}}
as an
H_{\mathbf{a}}
-module.

(
b
)

The functors
M\mapsto\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
and
V\mapsto Q_{\mathbf{a}}\otimes_{H_{\mathbf{a}}}V
are inverse equivalence between
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
and
H_{\mathbf{a}}\text{-mod}
.

Proof.

(a). By adjunction and the definition of Whittaker Vectors, we have

\text{End}_{W_{n}}(Q_{\mathbf{a}})\cong\text{Hom}_{\Delta_{n}}(\mathbb{C}_{\mathbf{a}},Q_{\mathbf{a}})\cong\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(Q_{\mathbf{a}}).

Then
\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(Q_{\mathbf{a}})
is a free
H_{\mathbf{a}}
-module of rank
1
.

By Lemma
4.1
,
Q_{\mathbf{a}}=U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(Q_{\mathbf{a}})
.
By PBW Theorem,
Q_{\mathbf{a}}
is
U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
-free. Then (a) follows.

(b).
By (a) and Lemma
4.1
, for any
M\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{W}
,

Q_{\mathbf{a}}\otimes_{H_{\mathbf{a}}}\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)\cong M.

On the other side, for any
V\in H_{\mathbf{a}}\text{-mod}
,

\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(Q_{\mathbf{a}}\otimes_{H_{\mathbf{a}}}V)=\text{Wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\otimes V)\cong V.

Then we can complete the proof.
∎

For finite
W
-algebras
W(\mathfrak{g},e)
, a similar category equivalence was shown by
Serge Skryabin, see the appendix in
[
30
]
.

A DSP shared is a DSP earned: HLS Task-Level Multi-Pumping for High-Performance Low-Resource Designs
Giovanni Brignone, Mihai T. Lazarescu, Luciano Lavagno

Abstract
HLS ( HLS ) enhances digital HW design productivity through a high abstraction level. Even if the HLS abstraction prevents fine-grained manual RTL ( RTL ) optimizations, it also enables automatable optimizations that would be unfeasible or hard to automate at RTL . Specifically, we propose a task-level multi-pumping methodology to reduce resource utilization, particularly DSP , while preserving the throughput of HLS kernels modeled as DFG targeting FPGA . The methodology exploits the HLS resource sharing to automatically insert the logic for reusing the same functional unit for different operations. In addition, it relies on multi-clock DFG to run the multi-pumped tasks at higher frequencies. The methodology scales the pipeline II ( II ) and the clock frequency constraints of resource-intensive tasks by a multi-pumping factor ( M M ). The looser II allows sharing the same resource among M M different operations, while the tighter clock frequency preserves the throughput. We verified that our methodology opens a new Pareto front in the throughput and resource space by applying it to open-source HLS designs using SOTA commercial HLS and implementation tools by Xilinx. The multi-pumped designs require up to 40% fewer DSP resources at the same throughput as the original designs optimized for performance (i.e., running at the maximum clock frequency) and achieve up to 50% better throughput using the same DSP as the original designs optimized for resources with a single clock.

VII
Conclusion

We propose a task-level multi-pumping technique for saving

HW
resources while maintaining the original throughput for

HLS
dataflow designs for
FPGA
.

Given a
SOTA
single-clock
DFG
, our approach first
generalizes it to a multi-clock
DFG
. Secondly, it tunes the
tasks’ high-level parameters (i.e., clock frequency and pipeline

II
) to multi-pump their
FU
. The overhead for
generalization is negligible, thanks to the
DFG
structure,
which consists of independent blocks communicating via

FIFO
, allowing for safe
CDC
, and modern
FPGA

clock architectures, which seamlessly handle multiple clock
domains even if current
HLS
tools do not exploit them.

The experimental results reported in
Section

VI
prove that
our method opens a new Pareto front in the performance versus

DSP
space, saving up to
40\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
of resources at
maximum throughput. Moreover, our method does not require any
manual architecture changes from the designer, since it acts only
on the high-level parameters of the tasks and uses the
HLS

binding algorithm to automatically generate the resource sharing
logic. Finally, the generalization to multi-clock
DFG

simply requires replacing single-clock with multi-clock

FIFO
. Therefore, our technique is well suited for a fully
automated
HLS
optimization pass, which will be the subject
of future work.

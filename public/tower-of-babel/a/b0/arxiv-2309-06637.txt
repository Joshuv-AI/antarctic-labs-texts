Non-associative categories of octonionic bimodules Thanks: This work was supported by the NNSF of China (12171448).
Qinghai Huo, Guangbin Ren

Abstract
. Category is put to work in the non-associative realm in the article. We focus on a typical example of non-associative category. Its objects are octonionic bimodules, morphisms are octonionic para-linear maps, and compositions are non-associative in general. The octonionic para-linear map is the main object of octonionic Hilbert theory because of the octonionic Riesz representation theorem. An octonionic para-linear map f f is in general not octonionic linear since it subjects to the rule Re ​ ( f ⁡ ( p ​ x ) − p ​ f ​ ( x ) ) = 0 . \text{Re}\,\big(f(px)-pf(x)\big)=0. The composition should be modified as f ⊚ g ⁡ ( x ) := f ⁡ ( g ⁡ ( x ) ) − ∑ j = 1 7 e j ​ Re ​ ( f ⁡ ( g ⁡ ( e i ​ x ) ) − f ⁡ ( e i ​ g ​ ( x ) ) ) f\circledcirc g(x):=f(g(x))-\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}\text{Re}\,\Big(f\big(g(e_{i}x)\big)-f\big(e_{i}g(x)\big)\Big) so that it preserves the octonionic para-linearity. In this non-associative category, we introduce the Hom and Tensor functors which constitute an adjoint pair. We establish the Yoneda lemma in terms of the new notion of weak functor. To define the exactness in a non-associative category, we introduce the notion of the enveloping category via a universal property. This allows us to establish the exactness of the Hom functor and Tensor functor.

1.
Introduction

Category theory, initiated by Eilenberg and MacLane in 1945
[
16
]
, provides deep insights and similarities between seemingly different areas of mathematics

[
1
,
16
,
20
,
25
]
.
However, category theory is exclusively restricted to the associative realm since compositions among morphisms are always assumed to be associative.

The purpose of this article is to bring the category into the non-associative realm by
providing a typical example of the non-associative category whose compositions are non-associative. This category comes from octonionic functional analysis.
In octonionic functional analysis, the central objects are
octonionic para-linear functionals (see
[
13
]
).
This notion can be generalized to a more general setting, which leads to a specific non-associative category. The objects of this category are octonionic bimodules, the morphisms are octonionic para-linear maps, and the compositions are regular compositions that are non-associative.

We first recall the notion of para-linearity by focusing our attention on octonionic Hilbert spaces.

Definition 1.1

(
[
13
]
)
.

Suppose that
H
is a left
\mathbb{O}
-module and also a real Hilbert space with a real-valued inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}
. We call
H
an

\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space
if there exists an
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear map

\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle:H\times H\rightarrow\mathbb{O}

such that

Re\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}

and
satisfying axioms

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

(
\mathbb{O}
-para-linearity)

\left\langle\cdot,u\right\rangle
is left
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear for all
u\in H
.

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

(
\mathbb{O}
-hermiticity)

\left\langle u,v\right\rangle=\overline{\left\langle v,u\right\rangle}
for all
u,v\in H
.

(
{\mathrm{iii}}
)

(Positivity)

\left\langle u,u\right\rangle\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\left\langle u,u\right\rangle=0
if and only if
u=0
.

The definition of octonionic Hilbert spaces can trace back to 1964 by Goldstine and Horwitz
[
8
]
.
In fact, they put an extra axiom which states

\left\langle pu,u\right\rangle=p\left\langle u,u\right\rangle

for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
u\in H
. This axiom turns out to be
redundant
[
13
]
.

Definition
1.1
provides a unified viewpoint for the theory of Hilbert spaces over normed division algebras. In contrast to the full development of quaternionic Hilbert spaces
[
11
,
19
,
18
,
23
,
24
,
5
,
6
,
4
,
7
]
,
the research on octonionic Hilbert spaces is very rare.

The map
\left\langle\cdot,u\right\rangle
induced by the
\mathbb{O}
-inner product
is a real linear map but not an
\mathbb{O}
-linear map. Even though this, it satisfies

\mbox{Re}\left\langle px,u\right\rangle=\mbox{Re}(p\left\langle x,u\right\rangle)

for any
p\in\mathbb{O}
.
This motivates us to introduce a notion of
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear function as
a real linear map
f:H\to\mathbb{O}
for which

(1.1)

\text{Re}\,f(px)=\text{Re}\,(pf(x))

for any
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
x\in H
.
The importance of octonionic para-linear maps has been shown in
the octonionic Riesz representation theorem which provides a bijection between
an
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space
H
and
its dual space consisting of
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear functions
[
13
]
.
We will further study
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear operators between
octonionic Hilbert spaces.
To this end, we need to generalize the notion of para-linear functions to para-linear maps between
\mathbb{O}
-modules.

Let
M
and
M^{\prime}
be two
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules.
We want to define the notion of left
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear maps from
M
to
M^{\prime}
in terms of the definition equality (
1.1
).
But in contrast to the case where
M^{\prime}=\mathbb{O}
, the real part operator in (
1.1
) is meaningless for a general
\mathbb{O}
-module
M^{\prime}
.
Therefore, we need to introduce a real part structure in the target module
M^{\prime}
, which is given by a projection map

(1.2)

\mbox{Re}:M^{\prime}\to\mathscr{A}(M^{\prime}).

Here
\mathscr{A}(M^{\prime})
is the set of associative elements of
M^{\prime}
.
Notice that in the specific case
M^{\prime}=\mathbb{O}
we have

(1.3)

\text{Re}\,x=\frac{5}{12}x-\frac{1}{12}\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}xe_{i}.

It turns out that the real part operator on an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule
M^{\prime}
also satisfies (
1.3
). Similar results also hold in quaternionic case
[
17
]
.

The set
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
of all left
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear maps can be endowed with a canonical
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule structure. Its

\mathbb{O}
-scalar multiplications are defined by

\displaystyle(r\odot f)(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle f(xr)+A_{r}(x,f),

\displaystyle(f\odot r)(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle f(xr)+A_{r}(x,f),

where

A_{r}(x,f):=f(rx)-rf(x).

We find that the real part of the bimodule
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
is

\text{Re}\,\big(\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})\big)=\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,M^{\prime}).

This indicates that it is too small to study the
\mathbb{O}
-linear maps in octonionic functional analysis.

Note that the para-linear maps are not preserved under ordinary composition.
We then introduce a new notion of regular composition which preserves octonionic para-linear morphisms.
More precisely,
let
M,M^{\prime},M^{\prime\prime}
be
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules and let
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M^{\prime},M^{\prime\prime})
and
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
.
We define

(f\circledcirc g)(x):=f(g(x))-\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}\text{Re}\,\Big(f\big(g(e_{i}x)\big)-f\big(e_{i}g(x)\big)\Big).

Then we have

f\circledcirc g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime\prime}).

The above definition originates from the specific case of the multiplication of octonionic matrices.
Notice that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(\mathbb{O},\mathbb{O})=\{R_{p}:p\in\mathbb{O}\},

where
R_{p}
is a right multiplication operator on
\mathbb{O}
defined by

\displaystyle R_{p}x=xp.

It turns out that

(1.5)

\displaystyle R_{p}\circledcirc R_{q}=R_{pq}

so that
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(\mathbb{O},\mathbb{O})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{O}
as an algebra.
The multiplications of general matrices have the similar properties.
Notice that the regular composition is generally non-associative. This gives rise to our specific non-associative category. We shall denote by
\mathcal{LO}
the typical non-associative category,
whose objects are octonionic bimodules, whose morphisms are left
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear maps, and whose compositions are left regular compositions.
The category
\mathcal{RO}
of right para-linear maps can be defined similarly.

Now we move on to study functors between these two non-associative categories. We shall introduce several functors, including the conjugate, Hom, and tensor functors.

Firstly, we define the conjugate functor.
For any
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule, we have an induced bimodule, denoted by
M^{C}
, whose bimodule structure is defined as:

p\cdot x=x\overline{p},\quad x\cdot p=\overline{p}x.

This leads to a functor, called the conjugate functor,

C:\mathcal{LO}\to\mathcal{RO},

which is an isomorphism of categories.
The effect of the conjugate functor is that it can translate results from
\mathcal{LO}
to
\mathcal{RO}
and vise versa.

Next, we define
Hom
functors between categories
\mathcal{LO}
and
\mathcal{RO}
. They will play a central role in the study of dual operators in octonionic functional analysis.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule
M
, we have a covariant Hom functor

\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-):\mathcal{LO}\to\mathcal{LO}

and a contravariant Hom functor

\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(-,M):\mathcal{LO}\to\mathcal{RO}.

Moreover, both functors are
\mathbb{O}
-linear.

Finally, we consider the tensor functors. We define the octonionic tensor product of two
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules
M
,
M^{\prime}
as

M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}M^{\prime}:=(\text{Re}\,M\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\text{Re}\,M^{\prime})\otimes\mathbb{O}.

We then have a natural tensor functor:

\displaystyle M\otimes^{ll}_{\mathbb{O}}-:\qquad\mathcal{LO}\qquad

\displaystyle\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad\mathcal{LO}

\displaystyle X\qquad

\displaystyle\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X

\displaystyle X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{\prime}\qquad

\displaystyle\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,f_{M}.

Here the map

f_{M}:\text{Re}\,M\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\text{Re}\,X\to\text{Re}\,M\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\text{Re}\,X^{\prime}

is defined by

f_{M}(m\otimes x):=m\otimes f(x)

for all
m\in\text{Re}\,M
and
x\in\text{Re}\,X
. The map
l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,f_{M}
is a canonical para-linear extension map of
f_{M}
.
Similarly, we can also define functors

M\otimes^{lr}_{\mathbb{O}}-,\qquad\quad M\otimes^{rr}_{\mathbb{O}}-,\qquad M\otimes^{rl}_{\mathbb{O}}-.

It turns out that the two functors

(M\otimes^{ll}_{\mathbb{O}}-,\ \text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-))

constitute an
adjoint pair
.

The above preparation allows us to study further exactness for non-associative categories.
It is hard to consider exactness
in the non-associativity setting. To overcome the difficulty, we associate the non-associative category with an associative category. This associative category is constructed due to a universal property and is called the enveloping category.
The exactness in a non-associative category can thus be defined as the exactness of its enveloping category.

Studying the exactness, we need to introduce the notion of natural associated transformations. Through a natural associated isomorphism, a functor
between non-associative categories naturally corresponds to an associated functor between their enveloping categories.

For example, we can provide the natural associated functors of the three functors
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(-,M)
,
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-)
, and
M\otimes^{ll}_{\mathbb{O}}-
.
It turns out that
the natural associated functors of
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(-,M)

is the usual Hom functor

\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(-,\text{Re}\,M):\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}},

where
\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}
stands for the associative category, whose objects are
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules, morphisms are
\mathbb{R}
-linear maps, and compositions are usual compositions.
The natural associated functor of
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-)
is the usual Hom functor

\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,-):\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}},

and the natural associated functor of
M\otimes^{ll}_{\mathbb{O}}-
is the usual tensor functor

\text{Re}\,M\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}-:\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}.

These yield the exactness of Hom functor and tensor functor in
\mathcal{LO}
.

In the classical theory of categories, the Yoneda lemma
is arguably the most important result.
We can extend this result to non-associative categories.
It gives rise to a new notion called weak functors. It turns out that the usual Hom functor from a non-associative category to the
Sets
category is merely a weak functor other than a functor.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we review some basic properties of octonions and octonionic modules.

2.1.
Octonions

The algebra
\mathbb{O}
of octonions is a non-associative, non-commutative, normed division algebra over
\mathbb{R}
. As a real vector space, it has a basis
[
2
]

e_{0}=1,\quad e_{1},\quad\dots,\quad e_{7},

subject to the multiplication rules

e_{i}e_{j}+e_{j}e_{i}=-2\delta_{ij},\quad i,j=1,\dots,7.

An octonion can be written as

x=x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{7}x_{i}e_{i},\quad x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}.

Its conjugate is defined as

\overline{x}=x_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{7}x_{i}e_{i}.

The norm of
x
can be expressed as

|x|=\sqrt{x\overline{x}}\in\mathbb{R};

and the real part of
x
is given by

\text{Re}\,{x}=x_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(x+\overline{x}).

Following the
\epsilon
-notation from

[
3
]
,
the multiplication rules can be expressed as

(2.1)

\displaystyle e_{i}e_{j}=\epsilon_{ijk}e_{k}-\delta_{ij}.

The symbol
\epsilon
is skew-symmetric in indices. In fact, if we write
e^{ijk}
for the wedge product
e^{i}\wedge e^{j}\wedge e^{k}
in
\Lambda^{3}\left(\left(\mathbb{R}^{7}\right)^{*}\right)
, then we obtain a
3
-form:

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon_{ijk}e^{ijk}.

More precisely, we have

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=e^{123}+e^{145}+e^{167}+e^{246}-e^{257}-e^{347}-e^{356}.

2.2.
\mathbb{O}
-modules

There are abundant results on octonionic bimodules, or more generally, alternative modules. Already in 1952, Schafer
[
21
]
gave the birepresentations of alternative algebras. Subsequently, Jacobson
[
14
]
determined the irreducible representations for finite
dimensional semi-simple alternative algebras. A more general study for alternative bimodules is given in
[
22
]
. Recently, only for the octonionic case, we
[
12
]
introduce the new notions of associative elements and conjugate associative elements, which give rise to a new description of the structure of
\mathbb{O}
-modules.

We now recall some basic notations and results on
\mathbb{O}
-modules.
A real vector space
M
is called a left
\mathbb{O}
-module if it is endowed with an
\mathbb{O}
-scalar multiplication such that the
left associator
is left alternative.
That is, for all
p,q\in\mathbb{O},\ m\in M
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle[p,q,m]=-[q,p,m],

where the left associator is defined by

[p,q,m]=(pq)m-p(qm).

It is easy to check that (
2.2
) is equivalent to

r(rm)=r^{2}m\text{\ \ \ for all \ \ }r\in\mathbb{O},\,\ m\in M.

We recall a useful
identity which holds in any left
\mathbb{O}
-module
M
.

(2.3)

[p,q,r]m+p[q,r,m]=[pq,r,m]-[p,qr,m]+[p,q,rm]

for all
p,q,r\in\mathbb{O}
and
m\in M
.
We shall generalize this identity in many different settings.

The
nucleus
of a left
\mathbb{O}
-module
M
is defined by

\mathscr{A}(M):=\{m\in M\mid[p,q,m]=0,\text{ for all }p,q\in\mathbb{O}\}.

Any element in the nucleus
\mathscr{A}(M)
is called an
associative element
.
We denote by
\mathscr{A}^{-}(M)
the set of all
conjugate associative elements

\mathscr{A}^{-}(M):=\{m\in M\mid(pq)m=q(pm)\text{ for all }p,q\in\mathbb{O}\}.

The irreducible left
\mathbb{O}
-modules are already known to be isomorphic to the regular or conjugate regular modules. We refer to Chapter 11 of the monograph
[
26
]
for detailed results. We
[
12
]
describe the structure of a left
\mathbb{O}
-module in terms of associative elements and conjugate associative elements as follows

Theorem 2.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
M
be a left
\mathbb{O}
-module. Then

M=\mathbb{O}\mathscr{A}(M)\oplus{\mathbb{O}}\mathscr{A}^{-}(M).

We now recall the definition of
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules. Since
\mathbb{O}
is a special alternative algebra, the notion of an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule is just an alternative bimodule over
\mathbb{O}

[
21
,
14
]
. Here we state the definition as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

A left
\mathbb{O}
-module
M
is called an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule
if there is a right
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear multiplications such that for any
p,q\in\mathbb{O}
and
m\in M
,

[p,q,m]=[m,p,q]=[q,m,p].

Here we denote

[q,m,p]:=(qm)p-q(mp)

and denote the similar notation for
[m,p,q]
.

We denote by
\text{Reg}\,{\mathbb{O}}
, or just
\mathbb{O}
if there is no confusion, the regular bimodule with the multiplication given by the product
in
\mathbb{O}
. Clearly, the
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule
\text{Reg}\,{\mathbb{O}}
is
irreducible. Moreover, it is the only
irreducible
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule; see the results by Schafer
[
21
]
and Jacobson
[
14
]
.

3.
\mathbb{O}
-para-linearity

Recently, we
[
13
]
introduce a notion of
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear functional. In fact, we can generalize this notion to a more general case of
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear maps between
\mathbb{O}
-modules.
The
\mathbb{O}
-para-linearity plays a central role in the study of octonionic functional analysis. We shall give a complete discussion about this concept in this section.

3.1.
Real part operator on
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules

To generalize the notion of para-linear functions to para-linear maps, we need to generalize the notion of the real part structure of
\mathbb{O}
to general
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules.

Let
M
be an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule. Since the only irreducible
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule is the regular bimodule
\text{Reg}\,{\mathbb{O}}
, it follows from
Theorem
2.1
that

(3.1)

\displaystyle M=\mathbb{O}\mathscr{A}(M).

One can easily check that

e_{i}\mathscr{A}(M)\cap e_{j}\mathscr{A}(M)=\{0\}

for each
i\neq j\in\{0,\dots,7\}
.
Indeed, supposing on the contrary, we can find

e_{i}x=e_{j}y

for some non-zero elements
x,y\in\mathscr{A}(M)
. Then
x=(\overline{e_{i}}e_{j})y
. It follows that

[p,q,x]=[p,q,\overline{e_{i}}e_{j}]y=0

for arbitrary
p,q\in\mathbb{O}
.
This forces that
y=0
, a contradiction.

Hence we have a direct sum decomposition of
M

(3.2)

\displaystyle M=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}\mathscr{A}(M).

We thus define the real part operator as the projective operator

\text{Re}\,:M\to\mathscr{A}(M).

The right multiplication in an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule is uniquely determined by its left multiplication, which is completely different from the quaternionic case.
We denote the
commutative center
of
M
by:

\mathscr{Z}(M):=\{x\in M\mid px=xp,\text{ for all }p\in\mathbb{O}\}.

Then we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,M=\mathscr{A}(M)=\mathscr{Z}(M).

We collect some properties of the real part operator as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
M
be an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule.
We have a concrete expression of the real part operator in terms of scalar multiplications

(3.4)

\text{Re}\,x=\frac{5}{12}x-\frac{1}{12}\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}xe_{i}.

Moreover, for all
x\in M
, we have

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

\text{Re}\,^{2}x=\text{Re}\,x
.

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

For all
p,q\in\mathbb{O}
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,(p\text{Re}\,(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\text{Re}\,p)\text{Re}\,(x),

(3.6)

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,[p,q,x]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,[p,x]=0.

(
{\mathrm{iii}}
)

x=\sum_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}\text{Re}\,(\overline{e_{i}}x)
.

Proof.

We prove (
3.4
).
One can check directly that (
3.4
) holds for the case
M=\mathbb{O}
. The general case then follows. Indeed, for any
x\in M
, write
x=\sum_{j=0}^{7}e_{j}x_{j}
with
x_{j}\in\mathscr{A}(M)
. Then according to (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{5}{12}x-\frac{1}{12}\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}xe_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{12}\sum_{j=0}^{7}e_{j}x_{j}-\frac{1}{12}\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{7}e_{j}x_{j}\right)e_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{7}\left(\frac{5}{12}e_{j}-\frac{1}{12}\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}e_{j}e_{i}\right)x_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{7}\text{Re}\,(e_{j})x_{j}

\displaystyle=x_{0}.

This proves (
3.4
).

The proof of assertion
({\mathrm{i}})
is trivial. We prove (
3.5
). Without loss of generality we can assume
x\in\mathscr{A}(M)
.
It follows from assertion
({\mathrm{i}})
that

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,(px)

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{12}px-\frac{1}{12}\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}(px)e_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{12}px-\frac{1}{12}\sum_{i=1}^{7}(e_{i}pe_{i})x

\displaystyle=(\text{Re}\,p)x.

Then (
3.6
) follows from (
3.5
) directly.

Let
x=\sum_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}x_{i}
. Then it follows from (
3.5
) that

\text{Re}\,(\overline{e_{i}}x)=\text{Re}\,\sum_{i=0}^{7}(e_{j}\overline{e_{i}})x_{j}=x_{i}.

This proves assertion
({\mathrm{iii}})
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Recall that in a quaternionic bimodule, the real part operator is defined by

\text{Re}\,x=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{e\in B}\overline{e}xe\quad(x\in X).

Where
B:=\{1,i,j,k\}
is a basis of the quaternions
\mathbb{H}
(
[

]
). And the polarization identity holds:

(3.7)

\displaystyle x=\sum_{e\in B}e\text{Re}\,(\overline{e}x).

(
3.4
) is a similar result in octonionic case.

Let
M
,
M^{\prime}
be two
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules.
A map
f:M\to M^{\prime}
is called
\mathbb{O}
-linear
if

f(px)=pf(x),\quad f(xp)=f(x)p

for all
x\in M,\ p\in\mathbb{O}
. Note that the right multiplication in an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule is uniquely determined by its left multiplication. We conclude that

(3.8)

\begin{split}\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,M^{\prime})&=\{f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})\mid f(px)=pf(x)\text{ for all }x\in M,\ p\in\mathbb{O}\}\\
&=\{f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})\mid f(px)=pf(x)\text{ for all }x\in M,\ p\in\mathbb{O}\}.\end{split}

It follows from (
3.4
) that any
\mathbb{O}
-linear map commutes with the real part operator.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
M
,
M^{\prime}
be two
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules and
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,M^{\prime})
.
Then

f(\text{Re}\,x)=\text{Re}\,f(x)

for all
x\in M
.

3.2.
Second associator

To study the para-linear map, we need to introduce the notion of the second associators related to a linear map between
\mathbb{O}
-modules and establish some identities associated with this notion.

Let
M
,
M^{\prime}
be two left
\mathbb{O}
-modules. For any
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})
,
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
x\in M
, we denote

A_{p}(x,f):=f(px)-pf(x)

and call it the
second left associator
of
f
.
Similarly we can introduce the
second right associator
between right
\mathbb{O}
-modules via

B_{p}(f,x):=f(x)p-f(xp).

By definition, we can generalize identity (
2.3
) as follows.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
M
,
M^{\prime}
be two
\mathbb{O}
-modules and
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})
. Assume that
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
p,q\in\mathbb{O}
and
x\in M
. The following statements hold.

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

If
M
,
M^{\prime}
are two left
\mathbb{O}
-modules, then

(3.9)

A_{pq}(x,f)=f([p,q,x])-[p,q,f(x)]+pA_{q}(x,f)+A_{p}(qx,f).

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

If
M
,
M^{\prime}
are two right
\mathbb{O}
-modules, then

(3.10)

B_{pq}(f,x)=f([x,p,q])-[f(x),p,q]+B_{p}(f,x)q+B_{q}(f,xp).

(
{\mathrm{iii}}
)

If
M
,
M^{\prime}
are two
\mathbb{O}
-modules, then

A_{\alpha}(x,f)=B_{\alpha}(f,x)=0.

Proof.

A direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle A_{pq}(x,f)

\displaystyle=f((pq)x)-(pq)f(x)

\displaystyle=f(p(qx)+[p,q,x])-(pq)f(x)

\displaystyle=pf(qx)+A_{p}(qx,f)+f([p,q,x])-(pq)f(x)

\displaystyle=p(qf(x))+pA_{q}(x,f)+A_{p}(qx,f)+f([p,q,x])-(pq)f(x)

\displaystyle=f([p,q,x])-[p,q,f(x)]+pA_{q}(x,f)+A_{p}(qx,f).

This proves assertion
({\mathrm{i}})
. Assertion
({\mathrm{ii}})
can be proved similarly. Assertion
({\mathrm{iii}})
is trivial.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Let
f
be a map between two left (right)
\mathbb{O}
-modules.
We denote the
evaluation of
f
on an element
x
by

(3.11)

\displaystyle\left<x,f\right>:=f(x)\quad(\text{resp. }\left<f,x\right>:=f(x)).

This notation is reminiscent of the inner product on a Hilbert space.

If we write

A_{p}(x,f)=\left<px,f\right>-p\left<x,f\right>=:[p,x,f],

as we have denoted in
[
13
]
, then identity (
3.9
) can be rephrased as

p[q,x,f]+\left<[p,q,x],f\right>=[pq,x,f]-[p,qx,f]+[p,q,\left<x,f\right>],

which is an analogue of identity (
2.3
).

Similarly, we denote

B_{p}(f,x)=\left<f,x\right>p-\left<f,xp\right>=:[f,x,p].

Then identity (
3.10
) can be interpreted as an identity similar to identity (
2.3
).

Although such notations
provide a neat mnemonic, we shall still keep the notations
A_{p}(x,f)
and
B_{p}(f,x)
throughout in order to avoid any confusions. The notation given in (
3.11
) will be used many times in the sequel.

The following identities related to second associators are crucial in applications.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
M,\ M^{\prime}
be two left
\mathbb{O}
-modules. Then for all
p\in\mathbb{O},\ x\in M
, and
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})
, the following identities hold:

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

A_{\overline{p}}(x,f)=-A_{p}(x,f)
;

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

A_{p^{2}}(x,f)=pA_{p}(x,f)+A_{p}(px,f)
;

(
{\mathrm{iii}}
)

A_{p}(\overline{p}x,f)=pA_{p}(x,f),\qquad A_{p}({p}x,f)=\overline{p}A_{p}(x,f)
;

(
{\mathrm{iv}}
)

A_{p}(\overline{p}^{k}x,f)=p^{k}A_{p}(x,f),k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(
{\mathrm{v}}
)

A_{p^{k+1}}(x,f)=pA_{p^{k}}(x,f)+\overline{p}^{k}A_{p}(x,f),k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Similar results hold for the second right associators.

Proof.

Assertion
({\mathrm{i}})
follows from assertion
({\mathrm{iii}})
of Lemma
3.4
. Assertion
({\mathrm{ii}})
follows from
identity (
3.9
) with
q
replaced by
p
. Assertion
({\mathrm{iii}})
follows also from identity (
3.9
) with
q
replaced by
\overline{p}
.

Assertion
({\mathrm{iv}})
can be proved by induction on
k
. Assertion
({\mathrm{v}})
follows by induction on
k
and using assertion
({\mathrm{iv}})
.
∎

To show the
\mathbb{O}
-module structures of the set of para-linear maps,
we shall define several kinds of octonionic scalar multiplications on
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})
according to the types of
\mathbb{O}
-modules
M
and
M^{\prime}
. The notation (
3.11
) will be used in the following proposition
for memory convenience.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
M,M^{\prime}
be two
\mathbb{O}
-modules,
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})
and
r,p,q\in\mathbb{O}
.

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

If
M
is left module and
M^{\prime}
is bimodule, we define

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left<x,f\odot r\right>:=\left<x,f\right>r-A_{r}(x,f).

Then we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle A_{p}(x,f\odot r)

\displaystyle=[p,f(x),r]+A_{p}(x,f)r-A_{r}(px,f)+pA_{r}(x,f),

(3.14)

\displaystyle[f,p,q](x)

\displaystyle=[f(x),p,q]-A_{p}(x,f)q-A_{q}(x,f\odot p)+A_{pq}(x,f).

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

If
M
is bimodule and
M^{\prime}
is left module, we define

(3.15)

\displaystyle\left<x,r\odot f\right>:=\left<xr,f\right>+A_{r}(x,f).

Then we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle A_{p}(x,r\odot f)

\displaystyle=f([p,x,r])+A_{p}(xr,f)+A_{r}(px,f)-pA_{r}(x,f),

(3.17)

\displaystyle[p,q,f](x)

\displaystyle=A_{pq}(x,f)-A_{q}(xp,f)-A_{p}(x,q\odot f)-f([x,p,q]).

(
{\mathrm{iii}}
)

If
M
is right module and
M^{\prime}
is bimodule, we define

(3.18)

\displaystyle\left<r\odot f,x\right>:=r\left<f,x\right>+B_{r}(f,x).

Then we have

(3.19)

\displaystyle B_{p}(r\odot f,x)

\displaystyle=[r,f(x),p]+B_{r}(f,xp)+rB_{p}(f,x)-B_{r}(f,xp),

(3.20)

\displaystyle[p,q,f](x)

\displaystyle=B_{pq}(f,x)-pB_{q}(f,x)-B_{p}(q\odot f,x)+[p,q,f(x)].

(
{\mathrm{iv}}
)

If
M
is bimodule and
M^{\prime}
is right module, we define

(3.21)

\displaystyle\left<f\odot r,x\right>:=\left<f,rx\right>-B_{r}(f,x).

Then we have

(3.22)

\displaystyle B_{p}(f\odot r,x)

\displaystyle=f[r,x,p]-B_{r}(f,x)p+B_{p}(f,rx)+B_{r}(f,xp),

(3.23)

\displaystyle[f,p,q](x)

\displaystyle=B_{pq}(f,x)-B_{q}(f\odot p,x)-B_{p}(f,qx)+f([p,q,x]).

(
{\mathrm{v}}
)

If
M
and
M^{\prime}
are two bimodules, then with the left or right multiplication defined by (
3.12
) and (
3.15
) we have

(3.24)

\displaystyle[q,f,p](x)

\displaystyle=A_{q}(x,f)p-A_{p}(x,q\odot f)+A_{p}(xq,f)-A_{q}(x,f\odot p).

Similarly, with the left or right multiplication defined by (
3.18
) and (
3.21
), we have

(3.25)

\displaystyle[q,f,p](x)

\displaystyle=qB_{p}(f,x)-B_{p}(q\odot f,x)+B_{q}(f,px)-B_{q}(f\odot p,x).

Proof.

The results follows by straightforward calculations. We prove assertion
({\mathrm{i}})
for example. By definition, we have

\displaystyle A_{p}(x,f\odot r)

\displaystyle=\left<px,f\odot r\right>-p\left<x,f\odot r\right>

\displaystyle=\left<px,f\right>r-A_{r}(px,f)-p\left(f(x)r-A_{r}(x,f)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(pf(x)+A_{p}(x,f)\right)r-A_{r}(px,f)-p\left(f(x)r-A_{r}(x,f)\right)

\displaystyle=[p,f(x),r]+A_{p}(x,f)r-A_{r}(px,f)+pA_{r}(x,f).

The rest proof runs in the same manner and is omitted.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Although we have defined the octonionic scalar multiplication on
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})
, the set
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})
is not an
\mathbb{O}
-module in general. Indeed, by definition (
3.12
),
it may hold

f\odot(r^{2})\neq(f\odot r)\odot r

for some
r\in\mathbb{O}
and
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})
.

The identities in Proposition
3.7
are all akin to identity (
2.3
).
Those identities about associators together with the real part structure of modules play a crucial role in the sequel.

3.3.
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear map

In this subsection, we shall generalize the notion of para-linear functions developed in our previous work
[
13
]
to arbitrary para-linear maps between
\mathbb{O}
-modules.

Convention:   The target module is always assumed to be an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule from now on, except where otherwise specified.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
M
be a left
\mathbb{O}
-module and
M^{\prime}
an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule. A map
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})
is called
(left)
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear
if

(3.26)

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,A_{p}(x,f)=0

for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
x\in M
.

Similarly, we can also define the notion of right
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear map with the left associator
A_{p}(x,f)
replaced by the right associator
B_{p}(f,x)
.

We denote by

\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})

the set of (left)
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear maps and denote

\text{End}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M):=\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M).

In order to avoid any confusion, we use systematically the subscripts
\mathcal{LO}
and
\mathcal{RO}
to indicate that the module under consideration is a left or a right module. But we may omit the subscripts when the module under consideration is understood well.

For
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
, we shall only use the notation
\left<x,f\right>
to represent the value
f(x)
. Similarly, only the notation
\left<f,x\right>
is used for
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{RO}}(M,M^{\prime})
. We shall state our results for left
\mathbb{O}
-modules, while the similar results for right
\mathbb{O}
-modules shall be collected in subsection
3.8
.

Let
M
be a left
\mathbb{O}
-module and
M^{\prime}
an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule.
For simplicity, we denote

f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)=\text{Re}\,f(x)

for any
x\in M
and
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})
.
In view of (
3.2
),

f
can be written as

f(x)=f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}f_{i}(x),

where
f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)\in\text{Re}\,M^{\prime}
and
f_{i}(x)\in\text{Re}\,{M^{\prime}}\text{ for any }i=1,\dots,7
.

We now give a characterization of
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear maps in terms of the real part operators. The proof runs in completely the same manner as in our previous work
[
13
]
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
M
be a left
\mathbb{O}
-module and
M^{\prime}
an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule. Then the following are equivalent:

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
;

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

f_{i}(x)=f_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{e_{i}}x)
for any
i=0,\dots,7
;

(
{\mathrm{iii}}
)

A_{p}(x,f)=\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{i},p,x])
.

Proof.

We first prove that assertion
({\mathrm{i}})
implies assertion
({\mathrm{ii}})
.
By definition, we have

\displaystyle f_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{e_{i}}x)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,f(\overline{e_{i}}x)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,(\overline{e_{i}}f(x))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{7}\text{Re}\,\left(\overline{e_{i}}e_{j}f_{j}(x)\right).

According to (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle f_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{e_{i}}x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{7}\text{Re}\,\left(\overline{e_{i}}e_{j}\right)f_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=f_{i}(x).

This proves that assertion
({\mathrm{i}})
implies assertion
({\mathrm{ii}})
.
We next come to prove that assertion
({\mathrm{ii}})
implies assertion
({\mathrm{iii}})
.
Without loss of generality we can assume
p=e_{i}
.
From above computation and assertion
({\mathrm{ii}})
, we get

\displaystyle A_{e_{i}}(x,f)

\displaystyle=f(e_{i}x)-e_{i}f(x)

\displaystyle=f_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{i}x)+\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}f_{j}(e_{i}x)-\left(-f_{i}(x)+e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{j,k=1}^{7}\epsilon_{ijk}e_{k}f_{j}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=f_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{i}x)+f_{i}(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{j}(e_{i}x))-e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)-\sum_{j,k=1}^{7}\epsilon_{ijk}e_{k}f_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}\left((e_{j}e_{i})x-[e_{j},e_{i},x]\right)\right)-e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{j,k=1}^{7}\epsilon_{ijk}e_{k}f_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{j}x)

\displaystyle=\Big(-\sum_{j,k=1}^{7}e_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}\left((\epsilon_{jik}e_{k}-\delta_{ij})x\right)\Big)+\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{j},e_{i},x])-e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{j,k=1}^{7}\epsilon_{ijk}e_{k}f_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{j}x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{j},e_{i},x]).

We used the fact that
\epsilon_{ijk}
is alternative in the last line.

Finally we prove that assertion
({\mathrm{iii}})
implies assertion
({\mathrm{i}})
. This is trivial in view of Theorem
3.1
. ∎

As an important consequence of
Theorem
3.10
, we find that the second associator vanishes for associative elements and an
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear map
f
is uniquely determined by
f_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Corollary 3.11
.

Assume that
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
.

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

For each associative element
x\in\mathscr{A}(M)
, we have

A_{p}(x,f)=0

and hence
f(px)=pf(x)
for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
.

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

f_{\mathbb{R}}=0\iff f=0
.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
M
be an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule and
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
.

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

If
f\lvert_{\text{Re}\,{M}}=0
, then
f=0
;

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,M^{\prime})
if and only if
f\lvert_{\text{Re}\,{M}}\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,{M},\text{Re}\,{M^{\prime}})
.

Proof.

We first prove assertion
({\mathrm{i}})
. Since
M
is an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule, each
x\in M
can be expressed as

x=x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}x_{i},

where
x_{j}\in\text{Re}\,M
for any
j=0,\dots,7.

According to assertion
({\mathrm{i}})
of Corollary
3.11
, we obtain

f(x)=f(x_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}f(x_{i}),

so that the condition
f\lvert_{\text{Re}\,{M}}=0
will yield
f=0
.

We come to prove assertion
({\mathrm{ii}})
.
If
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,M^{\prime})
, it follows from Lemma
3.3
that
f(\text{Re}\,M)\subseteq\text{Re}\,M^{\prime}.

Conversely, if
f(\text{Re}\,M)\subseteq\text{Re}\,M^{\prime}
, then by assertion
({\mathrm{i}})
of Corollary
3.11
again, for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
we have

\displaystyle f(px)

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}}f(px_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{7}f((pe_{i})x_{i})

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}}pf(x_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{7}(pe_{i})f(x_{i})

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}}pf(x_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{7}p(e_{i}f(x_{i}))

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}}pf(x).

∎

We now introduce two very useful bijections.

Lemma 3.13
.

If
M
is a left
\mathbb{O}
-module and
M^{\prime}

an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule.
Then we have a bijection of (left)
para-linear lift
map:

\displaystyle l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,:\qquad\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,\text{Re}\,M^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})

(3.27)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle(l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,g)(x):=g(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}g(e_{i}x).

Its inverse map is given by

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,_{*}:\qquad\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,\text{Re}\,M^{\prime})

(3.28)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\circ f=f_{\mathbb{R}}.

Proof.

We first show that the
l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,
map is well-defined.
In view of Theorem
3.10
,
l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,(g)
is a left para-linear map so that
l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,(g)\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
.
It is direct to check that

\text{Re}\,_{*}\circ l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,=\text{id},\quad l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,\circ\text{Re}\,_{*}=\text{id}.

∎

Lemma 3.14
.

If
M
and
M^{\prime}
are two
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules.
Then we have a bijection of (left)
para-linear extension
:

\displaystyle l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,:\qquad\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,M^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})

(3.29)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g,

which the
l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,
map defined by

(3.30)

\displaystyle(l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g)(px):=p(g(x))

for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
and all
x\in\text{Re}\,M.
Its inverse map is given by

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,^{*}:\qquad\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,M^{\prime})

(3.31)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle f\circ\text{Re}\,=f|_{\text{Re}\,M}.

Proof.

Based on Theorem
3.1
,
the para-linear extension is uniquely defined via
(
3.30
).

We need to prove that
l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
for any
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,M^{\prime})
. Let
p\in\mathbb{O}
and

x=x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}x_{i}\in M.

Then by definition, we have

\displaystyle A_{p}(x,l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g)

\displaystyle=(l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g)(px)-p(l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g)(x)

\displaystyle=(l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g)(px_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{7}(pe_{i})x_{i})-p(l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g)(x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}x_{i})

\displaystyle=pg(x_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{7}(pe_{i})g(x_{i})-pg(x_{0})-\sum_{i=1}^{7}p(e_{i}g(x_{i}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{7}[p,e_{i},g(x_{i})].

It follows that
\text{Re}\,A_{p}(x,l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g)=0
and thus
l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
.

By definition, one can check that

\text{Re}\,^{*}\circ l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,=\text{id},\quad l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,\circ\text{Re}\,^{*}=\text{id}.

This completes the proof. ∎

We shall denote

\mathrm{lif}\,=l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,,\quad\mathrm{ext}\,=l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,

for simple if no confusion arises.

3.4.
Second associator of para-linear maps

In subsection
3.2
, we have studied the second associator of
real linear maps over
\mathbb{O}
-modules.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
M
be a left
\mathbb{O}
-module and
M^{\prime}
an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule. If
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
, then for any
p,q\in\mathbb{O}
and
x\in M
we have

(3.32)

\displaystyle pA_{p}(x,f)

\displaystyle=A_{p}(x,f)\overline{p},

(3.33)

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\left(A_{p}(x,f)q\right)

\displaystyle=-\text{Re}\,\left(A_{q}(x,f)p\right).

Proof.

As usual, we write

f(x)=f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}f_{i}(x).

We first prove
(
3.32
). Without loss of generality we can assume
p\in\mathit{Im}(\mathbb{O})
. Let
p=\sum_{i=1}^{7}p_{i}e_{i}
with
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then by Theorem
3.10
, we have

pA_{p}(x,f)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{7}p_{i}e_{i}A_{p_{j}e_{j}}(x,f)=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{7}p_{i}p_{j}e_{i}e_{k}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{k},e_{j},x]),

and

A_{p}(x,f)\overline{p}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{7}A_{p_{j}e_{j}}(x,f)\overline{(p_{i}e_{i})}=-\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{7}p_{i}p_{j}e_{k}e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{k},e_{j},x]).

Since

e_{i}e_{k}+e_{k}e_{i}=-2\delta_{ik},

we conclude

pA_{p}(x,f)-A_{p}(x,f)\overline{p}=-2\sum_{i,j=1}^{7}p_{i}p_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{i},e_{j},x])=0.

The last step used the skew symmetricity of the associator
[e_{i},e_{j},x]
in
i,j
.

To prove (
3.33
), we first prove the following identity

(3.34)

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\left(f([p,q,x])\right)=\text{Re}\,\left(A_{p}(x,f){q}\right).

It suffices to show the case where
q=e_{i}
for each
i=1,\dots,7
.
By direct calculations, we have

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\left(A_{p}(x,f){q}\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,\left(\sum_{j=1}^{7}(e_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{j},p,x]))e_{i}\right)

by Theorem
3.10

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,\left(\sum_{j=1}^{7}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{j},p,x])e_{j}e_{i}\right)

by (
3.6
)

\displaystyle=-f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{i},p,x])

by (
3.5
)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,\left(f([p,q,x])\right).

Due to (
3.34
), we conclude that (
3.33
) holds.

∎

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
M
,
M^{\prime}
be two
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules and

.
Then for all

,

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\left(f([p,x,q])\right)

Without loss of generality we can assume that

It follows from Theorem

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\left(e_{i}A_{q}(x,f)\right)

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
M

\mathbb{O}
-module and

\left<x,f\odot r\right>:=\left<x,f\right>r-A_{r}(x,f)

for all
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
,
r\in\mathbb{O}

Proof.

First we claim that
f\odot r\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
for all

. Recall identity (

)

\displaystyle A_{p}(x,f\odot r)

\displaystyle=[p,f(x),r]+A_{p}(x,f)r-A_{r}(px,f)+pA_{r}(x,f).

In order to deduce
\text{Re}\,A_{p}(x,f\odot r)=0

we only need to show that

\text{Re}\,(A_{p}(x,f)r)=-\text{Re}\,(pA_{r}(x,f)).

This follows from identity (

Next we show
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})

\mathbb{O}
-module. It suffices to prove

(f\odot r)\odot r=f\odot r^{2}

for any

. By definition (
3.12
), we have

\displaystyle\left<x,(f\odot r)\odot r\right>

\displaystyle=\left<x,f\odot r\right>r-A_{r}(x,f\odot r)

\displaystyle=\left<x,f\right>r^{2}-A_{r}(x,f)r-A_{r}(x,f\odot r)

\displaystyle\left<x,f\odot r^{2}\right>

Since

Combining (
3.36
) and (

), we get

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\left<x,(f\odot r)\odot r-f\odot r^{2}\right>

\displaystyle=-\text{Re}\,(A_{r}(x,f)r)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,(A_{\overline{r}}(x,f)r)

assertion
({\mathrm{i}})
of Proposition
3.6

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,(\overline{r}A_{\overline{r}}(x,f))

by identity (
3.32
)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,A_{\overline{r}}(rx,f)

assertion
({\mathrm{iii}})
of Proposition
3.6

\displaystyle=0.

Note that

It follows from assertion

of Corollary

. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.18
.

Let
M
,
M^{\prime}
be two
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules. Then
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
admits a left
\mathbb{O}
-module structure with the left scalar multiplication defined by (
3.15
), i.e.,

\left<x,r\odot f\right>:=\left<xr,f\right>+A_{r}(x,f)

for all
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
,
r\in\mathbb{O}
, and
x\in M
.

Proof.

First we claim that

r\odot f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})

for all
r\in\mathbb{O}
and
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
. Indeed, using identities (
3.35
) and (
3.16
), we obtain

\text{Re}\,\left(A_{p}(x,r\odot f)\right)=\text{Re}\,(f([p,x,r]))-\text{Re}\,(pA_{r}(x,f))=0.

Next we show that
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
is a left
\mathbb{O}
-module. It suffices to prove
r\odot(r\odot f)=r^{2}\odot f
for all
r\in\mathbb{O}
.
For any
x\in M
, by definition (
3.15
) we have

(3.38)

\displaystyle\left<x,r\odot(r\odot f)\right>=\left<xr,r\odot f\right>+A_{r}(x,r\odot f)=\left<xr^{2},f\right>+A_{r}(xr,f)+A_{r}(x,r\odot f).

Since
f,r\odot f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\left<x,r\odot(r\odot f)-r^{2}\odot f\right>

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,\left(\left<xr^{2},f\right>+A_{r}(xr,f)+A_{r}(x,r\odot f)\right)-\text{Re}\,\left(\left<xr^{2},f\right>+A_{r^{2}}(x,f)\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,(A_{r}(xr,f)+A_{r}(x,r\odot f)-A_{r^{2}}(x,f))

\displaystyle=0.

It follows from Corollary
3.11
that
r\odot(r\odot f)=r^{2}\odot f
. This finishes the proof. ∎

As a consequence, we have the following property about the second associators.

Proposition 3.19
.

Let
M
,
M^{\prime}
be two
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules and
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
. Then for all ,
x\in M
and

\displaystyle A_{r}(xr,f)

\displaystyle=rA_{r}(x,f);

\displaystyle A_{r}(x,f)\overline{r}

\displaystyle=A_{r}(x{r},f).

Proof.

Let
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
,
x\in M
and

.
Combining (

) with (

In view of assertion
({\mathrm{ii}})
of Proposition

\displaystyle\left<x,r^{2}\odot f\right>

\displaystyle=\left<xr^{2},f\right>+A_{r^{2}}(x,f)

\displaystyle=\left<xr^{2},f\right>+rA_{r}(x,f)+A_{r}(rx,f).

From Theorem

3.7.
Regular composition of
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear maps

Since the para-linearity does not preserve under the ordinary composition of two para-linear maps, we need to introduce a new composition.

In this subsection, we always assume that
M,\ M^{\prime}
are two left
\mathbb{O}
-modules and
\ M^{\prime\prime}
is an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule.

Definition 3.26
.

Let
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M^{\prime},M^{\prime\prime})
and
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})
.
The
(left) regular composition
of
f
and
g
is defined as

f\circledcirc_{l}g(x):=f\circ g(x)+[f,g,x],

where

(3.46)

\displaystyle[f,g,x]:=-\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}\text{Re}\,\big(f(A_{e_{j}}(x,g))\big).

Remark 3.27
.

We shall just denote
f\circledcirc_{l}g=f\circledcirc g
if there is no confusion.
By direct calculation, we have an identity about associators concerning five terms:

(3.47)

A_{p}(x,f\circledcirc g)=A_{p}(g(x),f)+f(A_{p}(x,g))+[f,g,px]-p[f,g,x].

We shall show in Proposition
3.29
that the para-linearity preserves under the regular compositions. This fact justifies our Definition
3.26
. The point is that the regular composition is non-associative in contrast to the classical composition. This new composition plays a central role in the theory of non-associative categories.

We give a sufficient condition for the regular composition becoming the ordinary composition.

Lemma 3.28
.

Let
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M^{\prime},M^{\prime\prime})
and
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})
. Suppose one of the following conditions holds

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,M^{\prime})
;

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

M^{\prime}
is an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule,
x\in\text{Re}\,{M}
and
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
;

(
{\mathrm{iii}}
)

M^{\prime}
is an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule,
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M^{\prime},M^{\prime\prime})
and
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
.

We then have

[f,g,x]=0

for all
x\in M
and

f\circledcirc g=f\circ g.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
[f,g,x]=0
for all
x\in M
in each case.

If
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,M^{\prime})
then
[f,g,x]=0
due to the fact that
A_{e_{j}}(x,g)=0
.

Let
M^{\prime}
be an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule. If
x\in\text{Re}\,{M}
and
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
, by Corollary
3.11
we conclude

A_{e_{j}}(x,g)=0.

This yields
[f,g,x]=0
.

If
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M^{\prime},M^{\prime\prime})
and
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
, we then conclude from Lemma
3.3
that

\text{Re}\,f(A_{e_{j}}(x,g))=f(\text{Re}\,A_{e_{j}}(x,g))

\text{Re}\,f(A_{e_{j}}(x,g))=f(\text{Re}\,A_{e_{j}}(x,g))=0.

Proposition 3.29
.

If

and

We need to show that

for all

and
x\in M

In view of identity (

), it is sufficient to prove that

Without loss of generality we can assume

In view of (

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\big(f(A_{e_{k}}(x,g))-e_{k}[f,g,x]\big)=\text{Re}\,f(A_{e_{k}}(x,g))+\text{Re}\,\Bigl(e_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}\text{Re}\,\big(f(A_{e_{j}}(x,g))\big)\Bigl)=0.

This completes the proof.

3.8.
Right para-linear maps

The results of right para-linear maps are all parallel with the left cases. Given a right
\mathbb{O}
-module, we can endow it with a natural left
\mathbb{O}
-module structure so that we can interpret a right para-linear map as a left para-linear map. Thus we can establish these results via duality.

Let
M
be a right
\mathbb{O}
-module.
It induces a left scalar multiplication on
M
as follows:

p\cdot x:=x\overline{p},\quad\text{for all $x\in M$, $p\in\mathbb{O}$}.

One can check that this is indeed a left
\mathbb{O}
-module structure on
M
.
Similarly, a left
\mathbb{O}
-module also can induce a right
\mathbb{O}
-module structure. And an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule can induce a new
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule structure.

The induced module is denoted by
M^{C}
for all cases.

Let
M
and
N
be two right
\mathbb{O}
-modules. For any

f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,N),

we can regard
f
as a map between the left
\mathbb{O}
-modules
M^{C}
and
N^{C}
, denoted by

f^{C}\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M^{C},N^{C}).

Moreover, their associators have the following relation

B_{p}(f,x)=A_{p}(x,f^{C})

for any
x\in M
and
p\in\mathbb{O}
. This can be checked by direct calculation

\displaystyle f^{C}(p\cdot x)=f(x\overline{p})=f(x)\overline{p}-B_{\overline{p}}(f,x)=p\cdot f^{C}(x)+B_{p}(f,x).

Now we can state the dual version of Theorem
3.10
.

Theorem 3.33
.

Let
M
be a right
\mathbb{O}
-module and
M^{\prime}
be an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule.
Suppose that
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})
and write

f(x)=f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{7}f_{i}(x)e_{i}.

Then the following are equivalent:

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{RO}}(M,M^{\prime})
;

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

f_{i}(x)=-f_{\mathbb{R}}(xe_{i}),\quad i=1,\dots,7
;

(
{\mathrm{iii}}
)

B_{p}(f,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{7}f_{\mathbb{R}}([x,p,e_{i}])e_{i}
.

Similarly, we can also define the related bijections of right para-linear lift and right para-linear extension.
For a real linear map

, which can be viewed as

as well, it follows from definitions (

) and (

\left<x,f^{C}\odot r\right>=f^{C}(x)r-A_{r}(x,f^{C})=\overline{r}\cdot f(x)+B_{\overline{r}}(f,x)=\left<\overline{r}\odot f,x\right>.

Hence it holds

f^{C}\odot r=\overline{r}\odot f.

Similarly, we also have

Therefore, we have the following dual versions of Theorems
3.17
,
3.22
and
3.23
.

Theorem 3.34
.

Let
M
be a right
\mathbb{O}
-module and
M^{\prime}
an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule. Then
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{RO}}(M,M^{\prime})
admits a left

Theorem 3.35
.

Let
M,

Theorem 3.36
.

Let

(3.52)

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,{\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{RO}}(M,M^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,M^{\prime})

Let

Theorem 3.41
.

Let
M,M^{\prime}
be two
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules. Then

\displaystyle\varphi:\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{RO}}(M,M^{\prime})

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\hat{f}

is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\hat{\hat{f}}=f
. It remains to show that
\varphi
is an
\mathbb{O}
-homomorphism.
Let
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,M^{\prime})
be a left para-linear map. For any

, it follows from Theorem

that

\displaystyle\left<\varphi(e_{i}\odot f),x\right>

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,\left<x,e_{i}\odot f\right>-\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}\text{Re}\,\left<xe_{j},e_{i}\odot f\right>

\displaystyle=f_{\mathbb{R}}(xe_{i})-\sum_{j,k=1}^{7}e_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}(xe_{k}\epsilon_{jik}-\delta_{ij}x)

\displaystyle=f_{\mathbb{R}}(xe_{i})+e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)-\sum_{j,k=1}^{7}\epsilon_{jik}e_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}(xe_{k}),

\displaystyle\left<e_{i}\odot\varphi(f),x\right>

\displaystyle=e_{i}\big(f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}(xe_{j})\big)

\displaystyle=e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)-\sum_{j,k=1}^{7}(\epsilon_{ijk}e_{k}-\delta_{ij})f_{\mathbb{R}}(xe_{j})

\displaystyle=f_{\mathbb{R}}(xe_{i})+e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)-\sum_{j,k=1}^{7}\epsilon_{ijk}e_{k}f_{\mathbb{R}}(xe_{j}).

for

\mathbb{O}
-homomorphism by (

4.
Non-associative categories of para-linear maps

In this section, we aim to adopt the view of non-associative categories to study para-linear maps on
\mathbb{O}
-modules.
The category
\mathcal{LO}
, which will be introduced later, is a typical example of non-associative categories, whose objects in this category are
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules, morphisms are left para-linear maps, and compositions are left regular compositions.
This motivates us to introduce the notion of non-associative categories and functors between non-associative categories and establish some results on
\mathcal{LO}
similar to the classical case.

4.1.
Non-associative categories and weak functors

For the sake of completeness,
we give the definition of non-associative categories and functors in this subsection.

Definition 4.1
.

A
non-associative category

\mathcal{C}
consists of three ingredients: a class
\text{obj}\,(\mathcal{C})
of
objects, a set of morphisms
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
for every ordered pair
(X,Y)
of objects, and composition

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\times\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,Z)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Z)

\displaystyle(f,g)

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle gf,

for every ordered triple
X,Y,Z
of objects. These ingredients are subject to the
following axioms:

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

Each
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
has a
unique domain
X
and a unique target
Y
;

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

For each object
X
, there is an identity morphism
1_{X}\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,X)
such
that

f\,1_{X}=f,\qquad 1_{Y}\,f=f

for all
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-associative category. A morphism
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
is called an
associative morphism
if it satisfies:

\displaystyle(fg)h=f(gh),

\forall X^{\prime\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\to}}{X^{\prime}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\to}}{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}{Y}
;

\displaystyle(gf)h=g(fh),

\forall X^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\to}}{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}{Y}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\to}}{X^{\prime\prime}}
;

\displaystyle(gh)f=g(hf),

\forall X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}{Y}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\to}}{X^{\prime}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\to}}{X^{\prime\prime}}
.

Associated to the non-associative category
\mathcal{C}
is
an associative category
\mathscr{A}(\mathcal{C})
.
In
\mathscr{A}(\mathcal{C})
, the objects coincide with
\mathcal{C}
, the morphisms are associative morphisms from category
\mathcal{C}
, and composition is inherited from
\mathcal{C}
. We call
\mathscr{A}(\mathcal{C})
the
nucleus subcategory
of

Remark 4.6
.

By definition, we know that every functor is automatically a weak functor and the two notions of weak functor and functor coincide in the case of associative categories.
Generally, the composition of two weak functors may be not a weak functor any more. However, it is easy to see that the composition of a weak functor and a functor continues to be a weak functor.

We denote by

Definition 4.11
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{D}

Suppose

is a one-parameter family of morphisms in
\mathcal{{D}}
,

\tau=\left(\tau_{X}:S(X)\rightarrow T(X)\right)_{X\in\text{obj}\,(\mathcal{\mathcal{C}})}

making the following diagram
commute in
\mathcal{{D}}

\begin{array}[c]{ccc}S(X)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{X}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&T(X)\\
\,\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{S(f)}&&\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{T(f)}\\
S(Y)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{Y}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&T(Y).\end{array}

for all

in
{\mathcal{C}}
.

A

is a natural transformation
\tau
for
which each

is an isomorphism.

Remark 4.12
.

We point out that the usual composition of two natural transformations may be not a natural transformation generally.

We make the following notation. If
F,G:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{{D}}
are weak functors of the same variance, then we denote

\text{Nat}(F,G):=\{\text{natural transformations }F\to G\}.

Theorem 4.13

(
Yoneda lemma
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category, let
A\in\text{obj}\,(\mathcal{\mathcal{C}})
, and
let
G:\mathcal{C}\to\text{\bf{Sets}}
be a covariant functor. Then there is an bijection

\displaystyle y:\text{Nat}(w-\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,-),G)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle G(A)

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\tau_{A}(1_{A}).

Proof.

If
\tau:w-\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,-)\to G
is a natural transformation, then
\tau_{A}(1_{A})
lies in the set
G(A)
, for
\tau_{A}:\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,A)\to G(A).
Thus,
y
is
well-defined.

We prove that
y
is an injection.
For each
B\in\text{obj}\,(\mathcal{\mathcal{C}})
and
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,B)
, there is a commutative diagram

\begin{array}[c]{ccc}\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,A)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{A}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&G(A)\\
\hskip 10.00002pt\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{w-\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,-)(f)}&&\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{G(f)}\\
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,B)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{B}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&G(B),\end{array}

so that

(G(f))\tau_{A}(1_{A})=\tau_{B}(w-\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,-)(f)(1_{A}))=\tau_{B}(f1_{A})=\tau_{B}(f).

If
\sigma:w-\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,-)\to G
is another natural transformation, then

\sigma_{B}(f)=(G(f))\sigma_{A}(1_{A}).

Hence, if
\sigma_{A}(1_{A})=\tau_{A}(1_{A})
, then
\sigma_{B}=\tau_{B}
for all
B\in\text{obj}\,(\mathcal{\mathcal{C}})

and, hence,
\sigma=\tau
. Therefore,
y
is an injection.

To see that
y
is a surjection, take
x\in G(A)
. For
B\in\text{obj}\,(\mathcal{\mathcal{C}})
and
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,B)
, define

\tau_{B}(g)=(G(g))(x).

We claim that
\tau
is
a natural transformation; that is, if
h:B\to C
is a morphism in
\mathcal{C}
, then we have

(G(h)\circ\tau_{B})(g)=G(h)\circ G(g)(x)

and

\tau_{C}(w-\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,-)(h)(g))=\tau_{C}(hg)=G(hg)(x).

Since
G
is a covariant functor, it follows that

G(h)\circ G(g)=G(hg).

This implies that
\tau
is a natural transformation and

y(\tau)=\tau_{A}(1_{A})=G(1_{A})(x)=x.

Hence
y
is a surjection. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

If
G
is a weak functor, then we shall only get an injection in the Yoneda lemma.

These discussions show that the notion of weak functors in the setting of non-associative categories is natural. However, to study functors between non-associative categories is difficult and important. We shall discuss functors on the specific non-associative category

in the following subsections.

Theorem 4.15
.

Proof.

We need to verify two assertions.

\text{For any }f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,N),\quad\mbox{ we have }C(f)\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{RO}}(M^{C},N^{C}).

\text{Re}\,B_{p}(C(f),x)=\text{Re}\,A_{p}(x,f)=0,

\displaystyle C(f\circledcirc_{l}g)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{C}\circ g^{C}(x)+[x,f^{C},g^{C}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(C(f)\circledcirc_{r}C(g))(x).

This proves that

4.3.

We introduce

between the non-associative categories
\mathcal{LO}
and

Theorem 4.16
.

For any
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule
M
, we have a covariant functor

\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-):\mathcal{LO}\to\mathcal{LO}

and a contravariant functor

\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(-,M):\mathcal{LO}\to\mathcal{RO}.

Moreover, both functors are
\mathbb{O}
-linear.

Proof.

For short,
we denote the two functors above as

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-),

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(-,M).

The definition of
T
and
S
is canonical. More precisely,

\displaystyle T(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X),

\displaystyle S(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X,M)

for any
X\in\text{obj}\,(\mathcal{LO})
. For each morphism
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X,Y),
we define for every
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X)

\displaystyle\left<g,T(f)\right>

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle f\circledcirc_{l}g,

and for every
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(Y,M)

\displaystyle\left<S(f),g\right>

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle g\circledcirc_{l}f.

For simplicity, we omit the subscript ‘l’ since the regular composition is understood well.

We show that
T
is a covariant functor.
For any
p\in\mathbb{O}
,
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X,Y)
, and
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X)
, it follows from Lemma
3.32
that

\displaystyle A_{p}(g,T(f))

\displaystyle=f\circledcirc(p\odot g)-p\odot(f\circledcirc g)

\displaystyle=f\circledcirc(g\circledcirc R_{p})-(f\circledcirc g)\circledcirc R_{p}

\displaystyle=-[f,g,R_{p}].

In view of Lemma
3.30
, we have

\text{Re}\,A_{p}(g,T(f))=0,

which means

T(f)\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(T(X),T(Y)).

For a pair of morphisms

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{X^{\prime}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{X^{\prime\prime}}

in
\mathcal{LO}
, we need to verify

(4.3)

\displaystyle T(f_{1}\circledcirc f_{2})=T(f_{1})\circledcirc T(f_{2}).

For any

g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,X),

we have

\displaystyle{\ \ \ \,}\left<g,T(f_{1}\circledcirc f_{2})-T(f_{1})\circledcirc T(f_{2})\right>

\displaystyle=(f_{1}\circledcirc f_{2})\circledcirc g-\big(T(f_{1})\circ T(f_{2})\big)(g)-[T(f_{1}),T(f_{2}),g]

using Lemma
3.28

\displaystyle=(f_{1}\circledcirc f_{2})\circledcirc g-T(f_{1})(f_{2}\circledcirc g)

\displaystyle=(f_{1}\circledcirc f_{2})\circledcirc g-f_{1}\circledcirc(f_{2}\circledcirc g)

\displaystyle=[f_{1},f_{2},g]

using Lemma
3.30

\displaystyle=0.

Notice that

\text{Re}\,\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X)=\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,X)

due to Theorem
3.23
.
Since both sides of (
4.3
) are left para-linear maps, it follows from Corollary
3.11
that (
4.3
) holds.

Next we come to show that
T
is an
\mathbb{O}
-linear functor, i.e., for any
p\in\mathbb{O}
and

T(p\odot f)=p\odot T(f).

If
g\in\text{Re}\,\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X)=\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,X)
(by Theorem
3.23
), then again from Lemma
3.30
we have

\displaystyle\left<g,T(p\odot f)-p\odot T(f)\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p\odot f)\circledcirc g-f\circledcirc(g\odot p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f\circledcirc R_{p})\circledcirc g-f\circledcirc(R_{p}\circledcirc g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f,R_{p},g]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

It follows from Corollary
3.11
that
T
is
\mathbb{O}
-linear.

Secondly, we shall prove that
S
is a contravariant functor.
For any
p\in\mathbb{O}
,
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X,Y)
, and
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(Y,M)
, it follows from Lemma
3.31
that

\displaystyle B_{p}(S(f),g)

\displaystyle=(g\circledcirc f)\odot p-(g\odot p)\circledcirc f

\displaystyle=R_{p}\circledcirc(g\circledcirc f)-(R_{p}\circledcirc g)\circledcirc f

\displaystyle=-[R_{p},g,f].

Hence by Lemma
3.30
, we have

\text{Re}\,B_{p}(S(f),g)=0,

which means

For a pair of morphisms

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{X^{\prime}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{X^{\prime\prime}}

in
\mathcal{LO}
, we need to show

S(f_{1}\circledcirc f_{2})=S(f_{2})\circledcirc S(f_{1}).

In fact, for any

g\in\text{Re}\,\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(Y,M)=\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(Y,M),

we have

\displaystyle\left<S(f_{1}\circledcirc f_{2})-S(f_{2})\circledcirc S(f_{1}),g\right>

\displaystyle=g\circledcirc(f_{1}\circledcirc f_{2})-(g\circledcirc f_{1})\circledcirc f_{2}

\displaystyle=-[g,f_{1},f_{2}]

\displaystyle=0.

Then it follows from Corollary
3.11
that
S
is a contravariant functor.

Finally, it remains
to show that
S
is an
\mathbb{O}
-linear functor, i.e., for any
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X,Y)
,

S(p\odot f)=p\odot S(f).

Let
g\in\text{Re}\,\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,Y)
. Then

\displaystyle\left<S(p\odot f)-p\odot S(f),g\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\circledcirc(p\odot f)-p\odot(g\circledcirc f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\circledcirc(f\circledcirc R_{p})-(g\circledcirc f)\circledcirc R_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[g,f,R_{p}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

It follows from Corollary
3.11
again that
S
is
\mathbb{O}
-linear.
This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.17
.

Similarly, we also have a covariant functor

\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{RO}}(M,-):\mathcal{RO}\to\mathcal{RO}

and a contravariant functor

\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{RO}}(-,M):\mathcal{RO}\to\mathcal{LO}.

In contrast to the classical associative case where the target category of a Hom functor is always the
Sets
category, in the non-associative case the Hom functors admit a non-associative target category. Moreover, the weak Hom functors introduced in Example
4.7
have a close relation with Hom functors. For example, we have

w-\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-)=U\circ\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-),

where
U
is the forgetful functor introduced in Example
4.9
. This provides an alternative viewpoint to see that
w-\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-)
is a weak functor.

We shall always denote

f_{*_{l}}:=\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-)(f):\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X)\to\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,Y),

f^{*_{l}}:=\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(-,M)(f):\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(Y,M)\to\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X,M)

for any
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}{Y}
in
\mathcal{LO}
.
Similar notations can be defined for
\mathcal{RO}
.

Theorem
4.16
provides a fundamental fact in the study of octonionic functional analysis. The Hom functors can be used to define the Banach dual operator and Hilbert dual operator. This answers that why the seemly right method to define the Hilbert dual of an operator
T
as

\left<Tx,y\right>=\left<x,Ty\right>

does not work in an octonionic Hilbert space
[
8
]
. As a direct consequence of Theorem
4.16
, we have the basic properties of dual operators:

\displaystyle(p\odot f)^{*_{l}}=p\odot f^{*_{l}},

\displaystyle(f\odot p)^{*_{l}}=f^{*_{l}}\odot p,

\displaystyle(f\circledcirc g)^{*_{l}}=g^{*_{l}}\circledcirc f^{*_{l}}

for any
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\longrightarrow}}{Y}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}{Z}
.

As a consequence, we have a covariant functor

\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{RO}}(\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(-,\mathbb{O}),\mathbb{O}):\mathcal{LO}\to\mathcal{RO}\to\mathcal{LO}.

We denote by
1_{\mathcal{LO}}
the identity functor. Then the embedding
\mathbb{O}
-homomorphism

\tau_{X}:X\to X^{**}

introduced in Subsection
3.9
is a natural transformation of functors. This can be seen from the following lemma.

Lemma 4.18
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules. Then for any
\phi\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X,Y)
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\phi^{*_{l}*_{r}}\circledcirc_{l}\tau_{X}=\tau_{Y}\circledcirc_{l}\phi.

Proof.

Note that we have shown in Subsection
3.9
that
\tau_{X}

is an
\mathbb{O}
-linear map. Hence (
4.4
) is equivalent to

\phi^{*_{l}*_{r}}\circ\tau_{X}=\tau_{Y}\circ\phi.

For any
x\in X
and any
f\in Y^{*_{l}}
, we denote
x^{\prime\prime}=\tau_{X}(x)
as usual, then

\displaystyle\left<\phi^{*_{l}*_{r}}(x^{\prime\prime}),f\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x^{\prime\prime}\circledcirc_{r}\phi^{*_{l}})(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{\prime\prime}(\phi^{*_{l}}(f))+[f,x^{\prime\prime},\phi^{*_{l}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<x,\phi^{*_{l}}(f)\right>+[f,x^{\prime\prime},\phi^{*_{l}}].

By Definition
3.37
, we get

\displaystyle[f,x^{\prime\prime},\phi^{*_{l}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{7}\big(\text{Re}\,\left<x^{\prime\prime},B_{e_{j}}(\phi^{*_{l}},f)\right>\big)e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{7}\big(\text{Re}\,\left<x,B_{e_{j}}(\phi^{*_{l}},f)\right>\big)e_{j}.

It follows from Lemma
3.31
that for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
,

\displaystyle B_{p}(\phi^{*_{l}},f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{*_{l}}(f)\odot p-\phi^{*_{l}}(f\odot p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f\circledcirc\phi)\odot p-(f\odot p)\circledcirc\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{p}\circledcirc(f\circledcirc\phi)-(R_{p}\circledcirc f)\circledcirc\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[R_{p},f,\phi].

In view of
(
3.48
), we thus obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{7}\big(\text{Re}\,\left<x,B_{e_{j}}(\phi^{*_{l}},f)\right>\big)e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{7}\big(\text{Re}\,\left<x,[R_{e_{j}},f,\phi]\right>\big)e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{7}\big(\text{Re}\,R_{e_{j}}([f,\phi,x])\big)e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[f,\phi,x].

We used the fact that
[f,\phi,x]\in\mathit{Im}(\mathbb{O})
in the last equality.
Therefore
\phi^{*_{l}*_{r}}(x^{\prime\prime})=(\phi(x))^{\prime\prime}
for all
x\in X
. That is,
\phi^{*_{l}*_{r}}\circ\tau_{X}=\tau_{Y}\circ\phi
.
∎

4.4.
Tensor functors

In this subsection, we introduce the tensor functors between non-associative categories
\mathcal{LO}
and
\mathcal{RO}
.

We define
the tensor product of two
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules
M
and
M^{\prime}
as

(4.5)

\displaystyle M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}M^{\prime}:=(\text{Re}\,M\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\text{Re}\,M^{\prime})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{O}.

Then
M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}M^{\prime}
is an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule and

\text{Re}\,(M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}M^{\prime})=\text{Re}\,M\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\text{Re}\,M^{\prime}.

For
m\in M
and
m^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
, we write

m=\sum_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}m_{i},\qquad m^{\prime}=\sum_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}m^{\prime}_{i},

where
m_{i}\in\text{Re}\,M
and
m^{\prime}_{i}\in\text{Re}\,M^{\prime}
. We define

\displaystyle m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}m^{\prime}:=\sum_{i,j=0}^{7}(m_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}m^{\prime}_{j})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{i}e_{j}).

We remark that in general for any
p\in\mathbb{O}
,

mp\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}m^{\prime}\neq m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}pm^{\prime}.

Instead, by direct calculation, we have the following identity

(4.6)

\displaystyle mp\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}m^{\prime}-m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}pm^{\prime}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{7}(m_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}m^{\prime}_{j})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}[e_{i},p,e_{j}].

We shall use the specific case of (
4.6
) and its variants as follows.

Lemma 4.19
.

Let
M
,
M^{\prime}
be two
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules and
p\in\mathbb{O}
,
m\in M
,
m^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
.
If one of the three elements
p
,
m
and
m^{\prime}
is an associative element, then we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle p(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}m^{\prime})=pm\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}m^{\prime},

(4.8)

\displaystyle mp\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}m^{\prime}=m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}pm^{\prime},

(4.9)

\displaystyle(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}m^{\prime})p=m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}m^{\prime}p.

Proof.

Assertion (
4.8
) follows from (
4.6
) directly. The proof of (
4.7
) and (
4.9
) is similar.
∎

Now we come to define tensor functors.
Fix an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule
M
.
For any morphism
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{\prime}
, we introduce a map

\displaystyle f_{M}:\text{Re}\,M\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\text{Re}\,X

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X^{\prime}

\displaystyle m\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}x

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}f(x).

By the left para-linear extension defined in Lemma
3.14
,
f_{M}
induces a left para-linear map:

l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,f_{M}\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X,M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X^{\prime}).

In view of identities (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), by direct calculations we obtain that

(4.10)

\displaystyle l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,f_{M}(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x)=m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}f(x)

for all
m\in\text{Re}\,M
and all
x\in X
.
We thus have a natural tensor functor

\displaystyle M\otimes^{ll}_{\mathbb{O}}-:\qquad\mathcal{LO}\qquad

\displaystyle\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad\mathcal{LO}

\displaystyle X\qquad

\displaystyle\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X

\displaystyle X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}X^{\prime}\qquad

\displaystyle\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,f_{M}.

Theorem 4.20
.

For any
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule
M
, the tensor functor

M\otimes^{ll}_{\mathbb{O}}-:\mathcal{LO}\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\mathcal{LO}

is a covariant functor.

Proof.

To show
M\otimes^{ll}_{\mathbb{O}}-
is a covariant functor, it only needs to verify that

(4.11)

\displaystyle l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,f_{M}\circledcirc l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g_{M}=l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,(f\circledcirc g)_{M}

for any morphisms

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{\prime\prime}

in
\mathcal{LO}
.

Let
m\in\text{Re}\,M
and
x\in\text{Re}\,X
.
It follows that
m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x\in\text{Re}\,(M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X)
.
Then by Lemma
3.28
, we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle(l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,f_{M}\circledcirc l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g_{M})(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,f_{M}\circ l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g_{M})(m\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,f_{M}\,(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}g(x)).

Combining (
4.12
) with (
4.10
), we obtain

(4.13)

\displaystyle(l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,f_{M}\circledcirc l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g_{M})(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}f\circ g(x).

Using (
4.10
) again, we get

(4.14)

\displaystyle l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,(f\circledcirc g)_{M}(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x)=m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}(f\circledcirc g)(x)=m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}f\circ g(x).

It follows from (
4.13
)and (
4.14
) that

(l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,f_{M}\circledcirc l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g_{M})(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x)=l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,(f\circledcirc g)_{M}(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x).

Note that

\text{Re}\,(M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X)=\text{Re}\,M\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\text{Re}\,X.

Hence (
4.11
) follows from Corollary
3.12
.
∎

Similarly, we can also define three tensor functors
M\otimes^{lr}_{\mathbb{O}}-
,
M\otimes^{rr}_{\mathbb{O}}-
and
M\otimes^{rl}_{\mathbb{O}}-
. They are defined as follows

\displaystyle M\otimes^{lr}_{\mathbb{O}}-:\qquad\mathcal{LO}\qquad

\displaystyle\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad\mathcal{RO}

\displaystyle X\qquad

\displaystyle\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X

\displaystyle X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}X^{\prime}\qquad

\displaystyle\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad r\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,f_{M};

\displaystyle M\otimes^{rr}_{\mathbb{O}}-:\qquad\mathcal{RO}\qquad

\displaystyle\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad\mathcal{RO}

\displaystyle X\qquad

\displaystyle\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X

\displaystyle X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}X^{\prime}\qquad

\displaystyle\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad r\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,f_{M};

\displaystyle M\otimes^{rl}_{\mathbb{O}}-:\qquad\mathcal{RO}\qquad

\displaystyle\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad\mathcal{LO}

\displaystyle X\qquad

\displaystyle\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X

\displaystyle X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}X^{\prime}\qquad

\displaystyle\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,f_{M}.

We shall denote

(M\otimes^{ll}_{\mathbb{O}}-)(f):=1_{M}\otimes^{ll}f:\quad M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X\to M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X^{\prime}

for
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{\prime}
in category
\mathcal{LO}
and denote the similar notations for other tensor functors.

4.5.
Adjoint functor theorem

It turns out that the two functors

(M\otimes^{ll}_{\mathbb{O}}-,\quad\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-))

constitute an
adjoint pair
.

Definition 4.21
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be two non-associative categories.
Let
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
and
G:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}
be covariant functors. The
ordered pair
(F,G)
is an
adjoint pair
if, for each
C\in\text{obj}\,(\mathcal{C})
and
D\in\text{obj}\,(\mathcal{D})
,
there are bijections

\tau_{C,D}:w-\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(FC,D)\to w-\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(C,GD)

that are natural transformations in
{C}
and in
{D}
.

We get the adjoint functor theorem for
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules as follows:

Theorem 4.22
.

Let
M
be an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule. Then

(M\otimes^{ll}_{\mathbb{O}}-,\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-))
is an adjoint pair.

More precisely, we have an isomorphism of
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules for any
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules
X,\,Y
:

\tau_{X,Y}:\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X,Y)\to\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X,\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,Y)).

Moreover, the following two diagrams commute
for all
f:X^{\prime}\to X
in
\mathcal{LO}
and
g:Y\to Y^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{LO}
:

\begin{array}[c]{ccc}\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X,Y)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{X,Y}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X,\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,Y))\\
\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{(1_{M}\otimes^{ll}f)^{*_{l}}}&&\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{f^{*_{l}}}\\
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X^{\prime},Y)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{X^{\prime},Y}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X^{\prime},\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,Y));\end{array}

\begin{array}[c]{ccc}\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X,Y)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{X,Y}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X,\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,Y))\\
\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{g_{*_{l}}}&&\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{(\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,g)_{*_{l}}}\\
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X,Y^{\prime})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{X,Y^{\prime}}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X,\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,Y^{\prime})).\end{array}

Proof.

We first define the map
\tau_{X,Y}
by extension.
For any
\alpha\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X,Y)
, and
x\in\text{Re}\,X
, we define

\left<m,\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha)(x)\right>:=\alpha(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x)

for all
m\in\text{Re}\,M
.
Then by the left para-extension defined in Lemma
3.14
, we get

\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha)(x)\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,Y)

for all
x\in\text{Re}\,X
. Then by the left para-extension again, we obtain

\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha)\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X,\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,Y)).

By construction, if
m\in\text{Re}\,M
, then for all
x\in X
we have

(4.15)

\displaystyle\left<m,\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha)(x)\right>=\alpha(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x).

Indeed, if we write
x=\sum_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}x_{i}
as usual, then by left para-extension in construction, for any
m\in\text{Re}\,M
we have

\displaystyle\left<m,\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha)(x)\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{7}\left<m,e_{i}\odot(\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha)(x_{i}))\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{7}\left<e_{i}m,\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha)(x_{i})\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}\left<m,\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha)(x_{i})\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}\alpha(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x_{i}).

And it follows from Lemma
4.19
that

\alpha(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x)=\sum_{i=0}^{7}\alpha(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}e_{i}x_{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}\alpha(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x_{i}).

This proves (
4.15
).

We come to show that
\tau_{X,Y}
is
\mathbb{O}
-linear. Bearing Corollary
3.12
in mind, for all
r\in\mathbb{O}
and
\alpha\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X,Y)
we get

\displaystyle\tau_{X,Y}(r\odot\alpha)=r\odot\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\iff\left<x,\tau_{X,Y}(r\odot\alpha)\right>=\left<x,r\odot\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha)\right>,\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\text{Re}\,X;

\displaystyle\iff\tau_{X,Y}(r\odot\alpha)(x)=\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha)(xr),\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\text{Re}\,X;

\displaystyle\iff\left<m,\tau_{X,Y}(r\odot\alpha)(x)\right>=\left<m,\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha)(xr)\right>,\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\text{Re}\,X,\text{for all }m\in\text{Re}\,M;

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eqpf:tau}}}{{\iff}}\left<m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x,r\odot\alpha\right>=\left<m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}xr,\alpha\right>,\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\text{Re}\,X,\text{for all }m\in\text{Re}\,M;

\displaystyle\iff\left<(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x)r,\alpha\right>=\left<m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}xr,\alpha\right>,\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all }x\in\text{Re}\,X,\text{for all }m\in\text{Re}\,M.

Then (
4.8
) implies that
\tau_{X,Y}
is
\mathbb{O}
-linear. It is trivial to see that
\tau_{X,Y}
is a bijection. Hence we conclude that
\tau_{X,Y}
is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules as desired.

Similarly, to show

f^{*_{l}}\circ\tau_{X,Y}=\tau_{X^{\prime},Y}\circ(1_{M}\otimes^{ll}f)^{*_{l}},

it suffices to show

(4.16)

\displaystyle\left<m,\left<x^{\prime},f^{*_{l}}(\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha))\right>\right>=\left<m,\left<x^{\prime},\tau_{X^{\prime},Y}((1_{M}\otimes^{ll}f)^{*_{l}}(\alpha))\right>\right>

for all
\alpha\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X,Y)
and all
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
,
m\in\text{Re}\,M
.
By direct calculations, we have

\displaystyle\left<m,\left<x^{\prime},f^{*_{l}}(\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha))\right>\right>

\displaystyle=\left<m,\left<x^{\prime},\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha)\circledcirc f\right>\right>

\displaystyle=\left<m,(\tau_{X,Y}(\alpha)(f(x^{\prime})\right>

since
x^{\prime}\in\text{Re}\,X^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\alpha(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}f(x^{\prime}))

by (
4.15
).

Since
m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x^{\prime}\in\text{Re}\,(M\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}X^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle\left<m,\left<x^{\prime},\tau_{X^{\prime},Y}((1_{M}\otimes^{ll}f)^{*_{l}}(\alpha))\right>\right>

\displaystyle=\left<m,\left<x^{\prime},\tau_{X^{\prime},Y}(\alpha\circledcirc(1_{M}\otimes^{ll}f))\right>\right>

\displaystyle=(\alpha\circledcirc(1_{M}\otimes^{ll}f))(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\alpha(m\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}f(x^{\prime})).

This proves (
4.16
). ∎

4.6.
Enveloping categories

Now we introduce a new notion called
the enveloping category. The point is that it becomes a bridge between
the non-associative category and the associative category.
To do this, we have to focus our attention on additive categories.

Definition 4.23
.

A non-associative additive category is a non-associative category
\mathcal{C}
satisfying the following
axioms:

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

Every set
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
is equipped with a structure of an abelian group (written additively) such that composition of morphisms is biadditive with respect to this structure.

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

There exists a zero object
0\in\text{obj}\,(\mathcal{\mathcal{C}})
such that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(0,0)=0
.

(
{\mathrm{iii}}
)

(Existence of direct sums.) For any objects
X_{1},X_{2}\in\text{obj}\,(\mathcal{\mathcal{C}})
there exists an object
Y\in\text{obj}\,(\mathcal{\mathcal{C}})
and morphisms
p_{1}:Y\rightarrow X_{1}
,
p_{2}:Y\rightarrow X_{2}
,
i_{1}:X_{1}\rightarrow Y
,

i_{2}:X_{2}\rightarrow Y
such that
p_{1}i_{1}=1_{X_{1}}
,
p_{2}i_{2}=1_{X_{2}}
, and
i_{1}p_{1}+i_{2}p_{2}=1_{Y}
.

Definition 4.24
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-associative additive category.

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

(\mathcal{D},\iota)
is called an
associative cover category
of
\mathcal{C}
if
\mathcal{D}
is an associative additive category and
\iota:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is a faithful weak additive functor.

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

An associative cover category
(\widetilde{\mathcal{C}},\iota)
is called the
enveloping category
of
\mathcal{C}
if for any associative cover category
({\mathcal{D}},\eta)
, there exists a unique additive functor
T:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathcal{D}
such that the following diagram
commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}}

Example 4.25
.

We now provide some typical examples of enveloping categories.

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

If
\mathcal{C}
is an associative additive category, then one can check that (
\mathcal{C}
,
1_{\mathcal{C}}
) is the enveloping category of
\mathcal{C}
.

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

The non-associative category
\mathcal{LO}
takes

(\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}},\iota)
as its enveloping category.
Here we recall that the associative category
\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}
and the weak functor
\iota:\mathcal{LO}\to\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}
are defined in Example
4.8
. We delay its proof to Theorem
4.26
.

(
{\mathrm{iii}}
)

If
\mathcal{U}
be a unital non-associative algebra, then it takes
(\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{U}),\iota)
as its enveloping category, where

\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{U})
is the associative subalgebra generated by
left multiplications of
\mathcal{U}
and
\iota
is defined as

\iota:\mathcal{U}\to\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{U}),\quad x\mapsto L_{x}.

Here
\mathcal{U}
has been viewed as a specific sort of non-associative additive category with a single object, whose morphisms are

\mathcal{U}

and whose composition is the multiplication of
\mathcal{U}
. Similarly,

\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{U})
is interpreted as an associative category.

Theorem 4.26
.

The enveloping category of
\mathcal{LO}
is
(\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}},\iota)
and so is
\mathcal{RO}
.

To prove this theorem, we need some lemmas.
Let
M
be an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule and
\alpha\in\text{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{O})
. Then there associates a map
\alpha_{M}\in\text{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(M)
defined by

\alpha_{M}(px)=\alpha(p)x

for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
x\in\text{Re}\,M
.

Lemma 4.27
.

Let
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,M^{\prime})
and
\alpha\in\text{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{O})
. Then

(4.17)

\displaystyle f\circ\alpha_{M}=\alpha_{M^{\prime}}\circ f.

Proof.

This follows from direct calculations.
For any
x=\sum_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}x_{i}\in M
,

\displaystyle(f\circ\alpha_{M})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{7}f(\alpha(e_{i})x_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{7}\alpha(e_{i})f(x_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{7}\alpha_{M^{\prime}}(e_{i}f(x_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{7}\alpha_{M^{\prime}}(f(e_{i}x_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha_{M^{\prime}}\circ f)(x).

This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.28
.

For any
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})
, there is a unique decomposition

(4.18)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i,j=0}^{7}f_{ij}\circ{\alpha^{ij}}_{M}

for which
f_{ij}\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,M^{\prime})
,
\alpha^{ij}\in\text{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{O})
, and

\alpha^{ij}(e_{k})=\delta_{jk}e_{i}.

Proof.

Let
f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}f_{i}(x)
, where
f_{i}(x)\in\text{Re}\,(M^{\prime})
.
We define

f_{ij}(x)=f_{i}(e_{j}x)

for all
x\in\text{Re}\,M
and then we obtain
f_{ij}\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,M^{\prime})
by para-linear extension. One can check that for all
x=\sum_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}x_{i}\in M
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{7}(f_{ij}\circ{\alpha^{ij}}_{M})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{7}f_{ij}({\alpha^{ij}}_{M}(e_{k}x_{k}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k=0}^{7}f_{ij}({\alpha^{ij}}(e_{k})x_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{7}f_{ij}(e_{i}x_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{7}e_{i}f_{ij}(x_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{7}e_{i}f_{i}(e_{j}x_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}f_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x).

The uniqueness is trivial.
∎

Now we come to prove Theorem
4.26
.

Proof of Theorem
4.26
.

Let
(\mathcal{D},\eta)
be an associative cover category of
\mathcal{LO}
. We need to define a functor
T:\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathcal{D}
.
Note that
\text{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{O})
can be generated by
\{R_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{O}\}
algebraically. We define

T(R_{p})=\eta(R_{p})

for any
R_{p}\in\text{End}_{\mathcal{LO}}(\mathbb{O})
and extend it to
\text{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{O})
by preserving the composition and linear combination. This induces the definition of
T(\alpha_{M})
for any
\alpha\in\text{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{O})
and
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule
M
. We thus define

\displaystyle T:\qquad\qquad\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}\qquad

\displaystyle\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle M\qquad

\displaystyle\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad\eta(M)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i,j=0}^{7}f_{ij}\circ{\alpha^{ij}}_{M}\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,M^{\prime})\qquad

\displaystyle\shortmid\!\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\qquad T(f):=\sum_{i,j=0}^{7}\eta({f_{ij}})T({\alpha^{ij}}_{M}).

We first claim that

T(f)T(\alpha_{M})=T(\alpha_{M^{\prime}})T(f)

for all
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}}(M,M^{\prime})
and
\alpha\in\text{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{O})
.
Without loss of generality we can assume
\alpha_{M}=R_{p}
for some
p\in\mathbb{O}
. Since
\eta
is a weak functor, it follows that

T(f)T(\alpha_{M})=\eta(f)\eta(R_{p})=\eta(f\circ R_{p})=\eta(R_{p}\circ f)=\eta(R_{p})\eta(f).

This proves the claim.
For any

M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{\prime\prime}

in
\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}
, it follows from Lemma
4.27
that

\displaystyle T(g\circ f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(g_{mn}\circ{\alpha^{mn}}_{M^{\prime}}\circ f_{ij}\circ{\alpha^{ij}}_{M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(g_{mn}\circ f_{ij}\circ{\alpha^{mn}}_{M}\circ{\alpha^{ij}}_{M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta(g_{mn}\circ f_{ij})T({\alpha^{mn}}_{M}\circ{\alpha^{ij}}_{M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(g_{mn})T(f_{ij})T({\alpha^{mn}}_{M})T({\alpha^{ij}}_{M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(g_{mn})T({\alpha^{mn}}_{M^{\prime}})T(f_{ij})T({\alpha^{ij}}_{M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(g)T(f).

The uniqueness is obvious.
∎

4.7.
Natural associated functor

For a given functor between non-associative categories, it is natural to ask what is its natural counterpart functor in associative categories.

Definition 4.29
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be two non-associative additive categories. Assume
(\mathcal{\widetilde{C}},\iota_{1})
and
(\mathcal{\widetilde{D}},\iota_{2})
are their enveloping categories, respectively.
Suppose
S:\mathcal{\widetilde{C}}\rightarrow\mathcal{\widetilde{D}}
,
T:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
are covariant functors. A
natural lift transformation

\tau:S\rightarrow T
is a one-parameter family of morphisms in
\mathcal{\widetilde{D}}
,

\tau=\left(\tau_{X}:S(\iota_{1}(X))\rightarrow\iota_{2}(T(X))\right)_{X\in\text{obj}\,(\mathcal{\mathcal{C}})}

making the following diagram
commute in
\mathcal{\widetilde{D}}
:

\begin{array}[c]{ccc}S(\iota_{1}(X))&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{X}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&\iota_{2}(T(X))\\
\,\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{S(\iota_{1}(f))}&&\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{\iota_{2}(T(f))}\\
S(\iota_{1}(Y))&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{Y}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&\iota_{2}(T(Y)).\end{array}

for all
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y
in
\mathcal{C}
. We call
S
a natural lift of
T
in this case.

A
natural extension transformation

\eta:T\rightarrow S
is a one-parameter family of morphisms in
\mathcal{\widetilde{D}}
,

\eta=\left(\eta_{X}:\iota_{2}(T(X))\rightarrow S(\iota_{1}(X))\right)_{X\in\text{obj}\,(\mathcal{\mathcal{C}})}

making the following diagram
commute in
\mathcal{\widetilde{D}}
:

\begin{array}[c]{ccc}\iota_{2}(T(X))&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta_{X}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&S(\iota_{1}(X))\\
\,\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{\iota_{2}(T(f))}&&\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{S(\iota_{1}(f))}\\
\iota_{2}(T(Y))&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta_{Y}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&S(\iota_{1}(Y)).\end{array}

for all
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y
in
\mathcal{C}
. We call
S
a natural extension of
T
in this case.

Let
\tau
be a natural lift transformation. If each
\tau_{X}
is an isomorphism then
\tau
is called a
natural lift isomorphism
.
We can define
natural extension isomorphism
, similarly. In these two cases,
S
is called a
natural associated functor
of
T
.

Natural lift or extended transformation between contravariant functors can be defined similarly.

Remark 4.30
.

This definition is also a generalization of the notion of natural transformations in the associative case. It can be seen that the notion of natural extension transformation is the dual concept of the notion of natural lift transformation. The typical examples are given in Theorems
4.31

and
4.32
.

Recall that we have a
Hom
functor between non-associative categories

\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(-,M):\mathcal{LO}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}\mathcal{RO}

for any
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule
M
.
Note that their enveloping categories are both the associative category
\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}
. We have another
Hom
functor
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}}(-,\text{Re}\,M)
, and abbreviated by

\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(-,\text{Re}\,M):\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}},

for any given
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule
M
.
We remark that
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(X,\text{Re}\,M)
is an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule via the multiplication

(pf)(x):=f(px),\quad(fp)(x):=f(xp)

for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
x\in X
.
Similar discussion is also valid for Hom functor

\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,-):\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}.

It turns out that the bijection
l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,
defined in subsection
3.3
is a natural associated isomorphism:

l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,:\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(-,\text{Re}\,M)\simeq\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(-,M).

Theorem 4.31
.

For any
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule
M
, the left para-linear lift map is a natural lift isomorphism of contravariant functors

l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,:\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(-,\text{Re}\,M)\simeq\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(-,M).

Proof.

We need to verify that the following diagram commutes in
\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}
for all
f:Y\rightarrow X
in
\mathcal{LO}
:

\begin{array}[c]{ccc}\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(X,\text{Re}\,M)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,\!_{X}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(X,M)\\
\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{f^{*_{\mathbb{R}}}}&&\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{f^{*_{l}}}\\
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(Y,\text{Re}\,M)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,\!_{Y}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(Y,M).\end{array}

It suffices to show that

(4.19)

\displaystyle l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,(f^{*_{\mathbb{R}}}(g))=f^{*_{l}}(l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,g)

for any
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(X,\text{Re}\,M)
.
In fact, for all
x\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\Big(\big(l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,f^{*_{\mathbb{R}}}(g)\big)\ (x)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\big(l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,(g\circ f)\ (x)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\circ f(x)

and

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\Big(\big(f^{*_{l}}(l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,g)\big)\ (x)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\Big(\big((l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,g)\circledcirc f\big)\ (x)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\Big(\big((l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,g)\circ f\big)(x)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\Big((l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,g)\ \big(f(x)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\circ f(x).

Hence (
4.19
) follows from Corollary
3.11
.
Recall that
l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,
is a real linear bijection, i.e., an isomorphism in
\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Hence the left para-linear lift
l\text{-}\mathrm{lif}\,

is a natural isomorphism.
∎

For the para-linear extension map, we have a similar result.

Theorem 4.32
.

For any
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule
M
, the left para-linear extension
is a natural extension isomorphism of functors

l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,:\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,-)\simeq\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-).

Proof.

We need to verify that the following diagram commutes in
\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}
for all
f:X\rightarrow Y
in
\mathcal{LO}
:

\begin{array}[c]{ccc}\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,X)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,\!_{X}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X)\\
\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{f_{*_{\mathbb{R}}}}&&\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{f_{*_{l}}}\\
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,Y)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,\!_{Y}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,Y).\end{array}

It suffices to show that

(4.20)

\displaystyle l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,(f_{*_{\mathbb{R}}}(g))=f_{*_{l}}(l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g)

for any
g\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,X)
.
In fact, let
x\in\text{Re}\,M
, by the definition of left para-linear extension, we have

\displaystyle l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,(f_{*_{\mathbb{R}}}(g))(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,(f\circ g)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\circ g(x)

and

\displaystyle f_{*_{l}}(l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\circledcirc(l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g)\,(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\circ(l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,g)\,(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\circ g(x).

Hence (
4.19
) follows from Corollary
3.12
.
Since
l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,
is a real linear bijection, i.e., an isomorphism in
\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}
, it follows that the left para-linear extension
l\text{-}\mathrm{ext}\,

is a natural isomorphism.
∎

Remark 4.33
.

In view of Theorems
4.31
and
4.32
, we can say that the counterpart functor of
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(-,M)
in associative case is the usual Hom functor

\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(-,\text{Re}\,M):\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}

and the counterpart functor of
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-)
in associative case is the usual Hom functor

\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,-):\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}.

Similar results hold for Hom functor
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{RO}}(M,-)
and
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{RO}}(-,M)
. Hence many properties of
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(-,\text{Re}\,M)
can be translated into properties of
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(-,M)
.

Similarly, we can show that the natural associated functor of

is the usual tensor functor

\text{Re}\,M\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}-:\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}.

The proof is straight forward
and is omitted.

4.8.
Exactness of functors

In this subsection, we discuss the exactness of functors.
We attribute the issue of exactness to the associative situation
via the notion of the enveloping category.

Definition 4.34
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-associative additive categories and
(\mathcal{\widetilde{C}},\iota)
be its enveloping category. A sequence in
\mathcal{C}
is said to be exact if its image under
\iota
in
\mathcal{\widetilde{C}}
is exact. Similar notions can be also defined for short exact sequence, left (right) exact sequence and complex. We can also define left (right) exact functors as usual.

Theorem 4.35
.

Let
M
be an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule. The Hom functor
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,-)
is a left exact functor.

Proof.

Let
0\longrightarrow X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{\prime\prime}
be an exact sequence in
\mathcal{LO}
. We need to verify that there is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{*_{l}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{*_{l}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X^{\prime\prime}).

We conclude from Theorem
4.32
that there is an natural associated isomorphism
\tau
such that the following diagram commutes in
\mathbb{O}\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\mathbb{R}}
:

\begin{array}[c]{ccc}0&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&0\\
\Biggm\downarrow&&\Biggm\downarrow\\
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,X)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{X}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X)\\
\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{f_{*_{\mathbb{R}}}}&&\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{f_{*_{l}}}\\
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,X^{\prime})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{X^{\prime}}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X^{\prime})\\
\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{g_{*_{\mathbb{R}}}}&&\Biggm\downarrow\scriptstyle{g_{*_{l}}}\\
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,X^{\prime\prime})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau_{X^{\prime\prime}}}}{{\xlongrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}&\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X^{\prime\prime}).\end{array}

By the classical theory, we have an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{*_{\mathbb{R}}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,X^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{*_{\mathbb{R}}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\text{Re}\,M,X^{\prime\prime}).

The means that the sequence

0\longrightarrow\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{*_{l}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{*_{l}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(M,X^{\prime\prime})

is exact.
∎

An analogous result also holds for contravariant Hom functor
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{LO}}(-,M)
.
By the similar argument, we can show that the tensor functor
M\otimes^{ll}_{\mathbb{O}}-
is a right exact functor.

5.
Concluding remarks

By taking octonionic multiplications as a model, we have introduced a new notion of para-linearity in the non-associative setting.
This notion inspires us to bring many results from the non-associative case to the associative case. For example, we obtain two typical
non-associative categories of
\mathcal{LO}
and
\mathcal{RO}
and start the study of non-associative categories. This breaks the restrictions on the classical associative compounds. Some new notions are proposed, which play important roles in the study of non-associative categories. For example, we introduce the notions of weak functors and enveloping categories. This establishes a close relation between associative category and non-associative category. These make us to establish the Yoneda lemma in the setting of non-associative category and introduce exactness for non-associative category.
We introduce the notion of natural associated functors so that we can understand better functors between non-associative categories.

Although the theory of homology algebra is very mature in its associative case
[
25
]
, it remains untouched in the non-associative case.

It deserves to study further the theory of para-linear operators
in
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert spaces and
\mathbb{O}
-Banach spaces. Based on para-linear functionals, we can introduce the octonionic weak topology of an
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space. The Hom functor will enable us to study dual operators and reflexive spaces in octonionic functional analysis. By considering a para-bilinear multiplication in an
\mathbb{O}
-bimodule, we can introduce the notion of
\mathbb{O}
-algebras. This provides foundations for the study of octonionic
C^{*}
-algebras. The Gelfand-Naimark theorem for the octonionic
C^{*}
-algebras may open new horizons in the study of non-associative geometry.

The
\mathbb{O}
-para-linearity over
\mathbb{O}
-bimodules may also provide a tool in the study of Riemannian geometry. The theory of octonion bundles
[
9
]
as well as loop bundles
[
10
]
have been developed by Grigorian recently for the study of
G_{2}
-manifold. It is quite natural to study para-linear
connections instead of
linear connections in
G_{2}
-manifold.
[
15
]
presents a nice review of various geometric structures on Riemannian manifolds and discusses a unified description using normed division algebras. And we remark that the notion of para-linearity is a unified description for the classical linearity on normed division algebras. We believe that this notion will play an important role in the future.

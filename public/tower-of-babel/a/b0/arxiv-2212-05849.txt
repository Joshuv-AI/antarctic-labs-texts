Isomorphism between the Białynicki-Birula and the Landau-Peierls Fock space quantization of the electromagnetic field in position representation
M. Federico, H. R. Jauslin

Abstract
We first present a summary of the quantization of the electromagnetic field in position space representation, using two main approaches: the Landau-Peierls approach in the Coulomb gauge and the Białynicki-Birula approach, based on the Riemann-Silberstein vector. We describe both in a framework that starts with a classical Hamiltonian structure and builds the quantum model in a bosonic Fock space by a precisely defined principle of correspondence. We show that the two approches are completly equivalent. This is formulated by showing that there is a unitary map between the Fock spaces that makes them isomorphic. Since all the physically measurable quantities can be expressed in terms of scalar products, this implies that the two quantizations lead to exactly the same physical properties. We show furthemore that the isomorphism is preserved in the time evolutions. To show the equivalence, we use the concepts of helicity and frequency operators. The combination of these two operators provides a formulation that allows one to make the link between these two methods of quantization in a precise way. We also show that the construction in the Białynicki-Birula quantization that avoids the presence of negative eigenvalues in the Hamiltonian, in analogy with the one for the Dirac equation for electrons and positrons, can be performed through an alternative choice of the canonical variables for Maxwell’s equations.

Canonical quantization in the Coulomb gauge

Maxwell’s equations in free space can be written in terms of the vector potential
\vec{A}
in the Coulomb gauge as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\vec{A}}{\partial t^{2}}

\displaystyle=c^{2}\Delta\vec{A},

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\vec{A}

\displaystyle=0,

(2wb)

\displaystyle\vec{B}

\displaystyle=\nabla\times\vec{A},

(2wc)

\displaystyle\vec{E}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial\vec{A}}{\partial t},

(2wd)

By choosing as canonical variables

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\vec{A}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta H}{\delta\vec{\Pi}}=\frac{\vec{\Pi}}{\varepsilon_{0}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\vec{\Pi}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta H}{\delta\vec{A}}=\varepsilon_{0}c^{2}\Delta\vec{A}=-\varepsilon_{0}\Omega^{2}\vec{A},

where the Hamilton function

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}x\left(\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{0}}\vec{\Pi}\cdot\vec{\Pi}+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\vec{A}\cdot\Omega^{2}\vec{A}\right).

(2wxy)

The Hamilton equations (

\displaystyle\vec{B}(\vec{x})

2.5
Equivalence between the LP and momentum quantizations

In this section, we show that the isomorphism
\mathcal{M}
given by equations (

), (

) and (

If one considers e.g. a single-photon state in the position space LP representation which reads as

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{\vec{\psi}}^{\dagger}|\varnothing\rangle_{LP}=|\vec{\psi}\rangle_{LP},

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawax)

\displaystyle|\vec{\psi}\rangle_{LP}

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{M}^{-1}z\rangle_{LP}

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawaya)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}^{-1}|z\rangle_{m}

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayb)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}^{-1}\hat{B}_{z}^{\dagger}|\varnothing\rangle_{m}

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayc)

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayaza)

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\varepsilon_{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}k\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\omega_{\vec{k}}^{1/2}\left(\vec{\phi}_{\vec{k},\sigma}(\vec{x})\hat{B}_{\vec{\phi}_{\vec{k},\sigma}}-\vec{\phi}^{\star}_{\vec{k},\sigma}(\vec{x})\hat{B}^{\dagger}_{\vec{\phi}_{\vec{k},\sigma}}\right),

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbad)

where we have used in the last equality the usual notation

. Furthemore, if one plugs in the expression of the circular plane wave eigenfunctions (

\displaystyle\vec{\hat{E}}_{m}(\vec{x})

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\varepsilon_{0}(2\pi)^{3}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}k\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\omega_{\vec{k}}^{1/2}\left(\vec{\epsilon}_{\sigma}(\vec{k})e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\hat{a}_{\vec{k},\sigma}-\vec{\epsilon}_{\sigma}(\vec{k})^{\star}e^{-i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\vec{k},\sigma}\right).

With this example, we have shown how one can pass from one representation to the other using directly the classical isomorphism

in a standard way, i.e. directly applied to the states and through a similarity relation for creation-anihilation operators or observables. This simple relation guarantees that the predictions one can make with one theory is consistent with what is obtained with the other.

3
Białynicki-Birula’s complex formulation of Maxwell’s equations — Choice of the canonical variables

We consider now another approach for the quantization which has been developped mainly by Białynicki-Birula
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
. One of the advantages of this construction is that it does not require to choose any particular gauge since it starts by defining the canonical variables to be directly proportional to the electric and magnetic fields as
[
24
, p.25, Section 10]

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\vec{B}}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}}},

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabca)

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{0}}\vec{E}.

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcb)

The Hamilton function is defined by

\displaystyle K_{RS}

\displaystyle:=\frac{c}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}x\left(\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}\cdot\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}+\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}\cdot\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}\right)

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbda)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}x\left(\varepsilon_{0}\vec{E}\cdot\nabla\times\vec{E}+\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\vec{B}\cdot\nabla\times\vec{B}\right),

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdb)

yielding the following Hamilton equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta K_{RS}}{\delta\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}}=-c\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS},

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbea)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta K_{RS}}{\delta\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}}=c\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS},

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbeb)

that are equivalent to Maxwell’s equations. We denote the Hamilton function by
K_{RS}
and not by
H
, to emphasize that it is not the total electromagnetic energy
[
16
,
33
]
, which reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{tot}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}x\left(\varepsilon_{0}\vec{E}\cdot\vec{E}+\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\vec{B}\cdot\vec{B}\right)

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfa)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}x\left(\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}\cdot\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}+\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}\cdot\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}\right).

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfb)

3.1
Complex representation of Maxwell’s equations in position space — the Riemann-Silberstein vector

From the canonical variables defined above one can construct a complex representation of the field called the Riemann-Silberstein (RS) vector
[
24
,
25
]

\displaystyle\vec{F}_{RS}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}+i\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}}\left(\vec{E}+ic\vec{B}\right).

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfbg)

Maxwell’s equations can be written in the RS complex representation as

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\vec{F}_{RS}}{\partial t}=-\frac{\delta K_{RS}}{\delta\vec{F}_{RS}^{\star}}=c\nabla\times\vec{F}_{RS},

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfbh)

with

\displaystyle K_{RS}=c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}x\ \vec{F}_{RS}^{\star}\cdot\nabla\times\vec{F}_{RS}.

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfbi)

In analogy with what we did for the Coulomb gauge, we define the symplectic unitary map

\displaystyle\vec{F}_{RS}\mapsto z_{RS}(\vec{k},\sigma)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}x\ \vec{\phi}_{\vec{k},\sigma}^{\star}(\vec{x})\cdot\vec{F}_{RS}(\vec{x}),

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfbj)

from which we deduce the Hamilton function in terms of the RS momentum variable
z_{RS}

\displaystyle K_{RS}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}k\left[\omega_{\vec{k}}\ z_{RS}^{\star}(\vec{k},+)z_{RS}(\vec{k},+)-\omega_{\vec{k}}\ z_{RS}^{\star}(\vec{k},-)z_{RS}(\vec{k},-)\right].

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfbk)

Written in terms of the real variables defined as

\displaystyle p^{RS}_{\vec{k},\sigma}

\displaystyle:=-i\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{0}\omega_{\vec{k}}}{2}}(z_{RS}-z_{RS}^{\star}),

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfbla)

\displaystyle q^{RS}_{\vec{k},\sigma}

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{0}\omega_{\vec{k}}}}(z_{RS}+z_{RS}^{\star}),

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblb)

\displaystyle z_{RS}(\vec{k},\sigma)

\displaystyle=:\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left((\varepsilon_{0}\omega_{\vec{k}})^{1/2}q^{RS}_{\vec{k},\sigma}+i(\varepsilon_{0}\omega_{\vec{k}})^{-1/2}p^{RS}_{\vec{k},\sigma}\right),

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblc)

the Hamilton function becomes

\displaystyle K_{RS}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}k\left[\left(\frac{(p_{\vec{k},+}^{RS})^{2}}{2\varepsilon_{0}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\omega^{2}_{\vec{k}}(q^{RS}_{\vec{k},+})^{2}\right)-\left(\frac{(p^{RS}_{\vec{k},-})^{2}}{2\varepsilon_{0}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\omega^{2}_{\vec{k}}(q^{RS}_{\vec{k},-})^{2}\right)\right].

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbm)

We see here that it looks like two infinite collections of independent harmonic oscillators but the second one appears with a minus sign. This situation is similar to the one encountered with the Dirac equation, in which the electrons could have positive and negative energies. It poses a difficulty for the construction of the quantized model. However, this difficulty can be avoided by making a different choice for the canonical variables, as we describe in the next section. The construction we are going to introduce is strongly inspired by the work of Białynicki-Birula (BB)
[
22
]
which is the reason why we will call it the BB complex representation, although we formulate it in a slightly different form.

3.2
Alternative choice of canonical variables that avoid the negative eigenvalues in the Hamiltonian

To construct canonical variables that avoid the difficulty of the negative eigenvalues describe above, we have to locate the variables that lead to negative contributions. This can be done using the notion of helicity which allows us to decompose the RS canonical variables into their positive and negative helicity parts
\vec{F}_{RS}=\vec{F}_{RS}^{(h+)}+\vec{F}_{RS}^{(h-)}
or equivalently in terms of the real variables
\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}=\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}^{(h+)}+\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}^{(h-)}
and
\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}=\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}^{(h+)}+\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}^{(h-)}
, in terms of which the Hamilton function takes the form

\displaystyle K_{RS}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}x\left[\left(\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}^{(h+)}\cdot\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}^{(h+)}+\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}^{(h+)}\cdot\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}^{(h+)}\right)\right.

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbna)

\displaystyle\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\left.+\left(\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}^{(h-)}\cdot\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{P}}^{(h-)}+\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}^{(h-)}\cdot\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}^{(h-)}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}x\left[\left(\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}^{(h+)}\cdot\Omega\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}^{(h+)}+\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}^{(h+)}\cdot\Omega\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}^{(h+)}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\left.-\left(\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}^{(h-)}\cdot\Omega\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS}^{(h-)}+\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}^{(h-)}\cdot\Omega\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS}^{(h-)}\right)\right],

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbnb)

where we have used
c\nabla\times=\Omega\Lambda
to obtain the second expression and the fact that
\Lambda\vec{v}^{(h\pm)}=\pm\vec{v}^{(h\pm)}
. It shows that the negative contributions identified in the momentum representation come from the negative helicity part of the field. To circumvent this problem, one can define new canonical variables that we call the BB variables

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{BB}

\displaystyle:=\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{RS},\hskip 56.9055pt\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{BB}

\displaystyle:=\Lambda\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{RS},

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbnbo)

and a new classical Hamilton function

\displaystyle K_{BB}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}x\left[\left(\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{BB}^{(h+)}\cdot\Omega\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{BB}^{(h+)}+\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{BB}^{(h+)}\cdot\Omega\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{BB}^{(h+)}\right)\right.

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbnbp)

\displaystyle\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\left.+\left(\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{BB}^{(h-)}\cdot\Omega\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{BB}^{(h-)}+\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{BB}^{(h-)}\cdot\Omega\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{BB}^{(h-)}\right)\right],

which is positive since
\Omega
is a positive operator, as opposed to
\nabla\times
. This alternative choice of the canonical variables thus avoids the problem of negative eigenvalues.
We emphasize that
K_{BB}
is not the Hamilton function
K_{RS}
expressed in the new variables since the relation (
2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbnbo
) is not a canonical transformation.

3.3
The Białynicki-Birula complex representation

We choose a different Hamiltonian structure that gives the same classical Maxwell equations.
For the complex representation, we define a modification of the RS vector adapted to the new canonical variables which we call the Białynicki-Birula vector

\displaystyle\vec{F}_{BB}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\vec{\mathcal{Q}}_{BB}+i\vec{\mathcal{P}}_{BB}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}}\left(\vec{E}+ic\Lambda\vec{B}\right),

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbnbq)

which can be decomposed in the six-component field (bispinor) notation used originally in
[
22

\Psi_{BB}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\vec{F}_{BB}^{(h+)}\\
\vec{F}_{BB}^{(h-)}\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\vec{F}_{RS}^{(h+)}\\
\vec{F}_{RS}^{(h-)\star}\\
\end{array}\right).

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbnbr)

Remarks:

•

The BB vector
\vec{F}_{BB}
that we introduced here was not mentionned explicitely in
[
22
,
24
]
but is a completely equivalent formulation of the original formulation using the six-component field

. We prefer, however, to use

since it is a more compact notation which allows us to write simpler formulas especially when we will introduce the isomorphism with the LP field.

•

In the original works of BB
[

]
, the six-component field

was defined using the positive and negative frequency parts of the RS field which we denote by
\vec{F}_{RS}^{(f\pm)}
, instead of the positive and negative helicity parts as we have done here. This difference does not change anything for the definition of

since
\vec{F}_{RS}^{(h\pm)}=\vec{F}_{RS}^{(f\pm)}
. However, we stress out that this property is in general not true, e.g.
\vec{\psi}^{(h\pm)}\neq\vec{\psi}^{(f\pm)}
since
\vec{\psi}^{(f-)}=0
for any
\vec{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}_{LP}
while
\vec{\psi}^{(h-)}\neq 0
in general. Other examples are the electric and the magnetic fields for which
\vec{E}^{(h\pm)}\neq\vec{E}^{(f\pm)}
and
\vec{B}^{(h\pm)}\neq\vec{B}^{(f\pm)}
.

•

The structure of the Białynicki-Birula six-component spinor can be illustrated by the description of the general elliptically polarized plane waves propagating in the

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{0}(2\pi)^{3}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\cos(kz-\omega t)\\
\mp\sin(kz-\omega t)\\
0\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\vec{B}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c\sqrt{\varepsilon_{0}(2\pi)^{3}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\pm\sin(kz-\omega t)\\
\cos(kz-\omega t)\\

We remark that the left/right circularly polarized plane waves have respectively

The plane wave with left circular polarization is represented by the Białynicki-Birula six-component spinor

\displaystyle\Psi_{BB}^{\rm(left~circular)}=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\vec{E}^{+}+ic\vec{B}^{+}\\

The single-photon plane wave with general elliptic polarization is represented by

\displaystyle=

The Hamilton function

\displaystyle K_{BB}

and the corresponding Hamilton equations can be written as

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbnci)

Using the six-component notation

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncj)

Remark:

is not the energy of the field. We remove them because it is more convenient for the quantization as we will see later, and it allows to make the link with the quantization in the Coulomb gauge explicit.

3.4
Quantization in the Białynicki-Birula representation

We associate to the BB vector, a classical Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{BB}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{BB}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\vec{F}_{BB}(\vec{x})\Big|\langle\vec{F}_{BB}|\vec{F}_{BB}\rangle_{BB}<\infty\right\}

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncl)

with the following weighted scalar product
[

,

,

\displaystyle\langle\vec{F}_{BB}|\vec{F}^{\prime}_{BB}\rangle_{BB}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}x\ \vec{F}_{BB}(\vec{x})\cdot\Omega^{-1}\vec{F}^{\prime}_{BB}(\vec{x}).

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncm)

The main motivation for the choice of this weighted scalar product is that it is Lorentz invariant
[

,
7
,
8
,
27
]
. We will see that it is also an essential ingredient for the isomorphism with the standard quantization in the Coulomb gauge.

Remark:
Fields with different helicities are orthogonal to each other with respect to both scalar products

and
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle_{BB}
.

Classical electromagnetic fields can be expressed in terms of the BB vector by inverting equation (

\displaystyle\vec{E}(\vec{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{-i}{\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{0}c^{2}}}\Lambda\left(\vec{F}_{BB}(\vec{x})-\vec{F}_{BB}^{\star}(\vec{x})\right).

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncnb)

by the general procedure (

)-(

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}k\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\vec{g}_{\vec{k},\sigma}(\vec{x})z_{BB}(\vec{k},\sigma),\;\;\;\;z_{BB}(\vec{k},\sigma)=\langle\vec{g}_{\vec{k},\sigma}|\vec{F}_{BB}\rangle_{BB},

\displaystyle z_{BB}

\displaystyle\mapsto\hat{C}_{\vec{g}_{\vec{k},\sigma}},

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncncocpa)

\displaystyle\mapsto\hat{C}^{\dagger}_{\vec{g}_{\vec{k},\sigma}},

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncncocpb)

where
\hat{C}_{\vec{g}_{\vec{k},\sigma}}
and

are creation-anihilation operators in the BB Fock space

\displaystyle\vec{\hat{F}}_{BB}(\vec{x})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}k\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\vec{g}_{\vec{k},\sigma}(\vec{x})\hat{C}_{\vec{g}_{\vec{k},\sigma}},

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncncocpcqa)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}k\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\vec{g}^{\star}_{\vec{k},\sigma}(\vec{x})\hat{C}^{\dagger}_{\vec{g}_{\vec{k},\sigma}}.

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncncocpcqb)

Quantized electromagnetic observables are given directly by the operators

\displaystyle\vec{\hat{E}}(\vec{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{0}}}\left(\vec{\hat{F}}_{BB}(\vec{x})+\vec{\hat{F}}^{\dagger}_{BB}(\vec{x})\right),

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncncocpcqcra)

\displaystyle\vec{\hat{B}}(\vec{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{-i}{\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{0}c^{2}}}\Lambda\left(\vec{\hat{F}}_{BB}(\vec{x})-\vec{\hat{F}}^{\dagger}_{BB}(\vec{x})\right).

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncncocpcqcrb)

One can check that it corresponds to the quantization proposed by BB, as written e.g. in

]

5
Dynamics and BB quantum Hamiltonian

The Hamiltonian in a quantum theory plays two roles: it is the operator associated to the total energy observable of the system and it is the generator of the dynamics of the states in the Schrödinger representation. The construction of the dynamics within the Fock space formulation is done as follows: one starts with the one-quantum subspace, which coincides with the classical Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and whose time evolution is given by the classical one. From this, a one-quantum operator is identified as the generator of the dynamics. For Maxwell’s equations, the generator of the dynamics is
\hbar\Omega
for all representations we discussed before (
\hbar
has been added here in relation to (
2wxzae
), (
2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbnci
) or (
2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbnck
) in order to match the dimension required by a Schrödinger equation in the quantized model). The next step is to extend this operator
\hbar\Omega
, which is defined in the single-photon subspace, to the entire Fock space
\mathbb{F}^{\mathfrak{B}}(\mathcal{H})
. This can be done in general with the map denoted
\rmd\Gamma(\hbar\Omega)

[
14
]
and defined by its action on the monomials
|\eta_{1}\otimes_{S}\eta_{2}\otimes_{S}\ldots\otimes_{S}\eta_{n}\rangle
:

\displaystyle\rmd\Gamma(\hbar\Omega)~|\eta_{1}\otimes_{S}\eta_{2}\otimes_{S}\ldots\otimes_{S}\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle:=|\hbar\Omega\eta_{1}\otimes_{S}\eta_{2}\otimes_{S}\ldots\otimes_{S}\eta_{n}\rangle

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncncocpcqcrcscvcwcydbdcdd)

\displaystyle\>\>+|\eta_{1}\otimes_{S}\hbar\Omega\eta_{2}\otimes_{S}\ldots\otimes_{S}\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\>\>+\dots+

\displaystyle\>\>+|\eta_{1}\otimes_{S}\ \eta_{2}\otimes_{S}\ldots\otimes_{S}\hbar\Omega\eta_{n}\rangle.

If
\phi_{\kappa}
is an orthonormal basis, with respect to
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
, of eigenfunctions of

, the map

can be expressed as
[
14

\rmd\Gamma(\hbar\Omega)=\sum_{\kappa}\hbar\omega_{\kappa}\hat{B}^{\dagger}_{\phi_{\kappa}}\hat{B}_{\phi_{\kappa}}.

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncncocpcqcrcscvcwcydbdcde)

We apply this procedure to the different Hilbert spaces defined in the preceding sections starting with the LP representation

\displaystyle\rmd\Gamma_{LP}(\hbar\Omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}k\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\hbar\omega_{\vec{k}}\hat{B}^{\dagger}_{\vec{\phi}_{\vec{k},\sigma}}\hat{B}_{\vec{\phi}_{\vec{k},\sigma}}.

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncncocpcqcrcscvcwcydbdcdfa)

We see that it is equal to the quantized total energy operator which can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{LP}

\displaystyle=\hbar\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}x\ \vec{\hat{\Psi}}^{{\dagger}}\cdot\Omega\vec{\hat{\Psi}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}k\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\hbar\omega_{\vec{k}}\hat{B}^{\dagger}_{\vec{\phi}_{\vec{k},\sigma}}\hat{B}_{\vec{\phi}_{\vec{k},\sigma}},

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncncocpcqcrcscvcwcydbdcdfdg)

showing that the total energy is the generator of the dynamics in the Coulomb gauge formulation of the quantized electromagnetic field.

One can apply the same procedure to the BB formulation using the basis

and one obtains

\displaystyle\rmd\Gamma_{BB}(\hbar\Omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}k\sum_{\sigma=\pm}\hbar\omega_{\vec{k}}\hat{C}^{\dagger}_{\vec{g}_{\vec{k},\sigma}}\hat{C}_{\vec{g}_{\vec{k},\sigma}},

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncncocpcqcrcscvcwcydbdcdfdha)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}x\ \vec{\hat{F}}_{BB}^{{\dagger}}\cdot\vec{\hat{F}}_{BB}

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncncocpcqcrcscvcwcydbdcdfdhdia)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}d^{3}x\ \vec{g}^{\star}_{\kappa}(\vec{x})\cdot\vec{g}_{\kappa^{\prime}}(\vec{x})\right)\hat{C}^{\dagger}_{\vec{g}_{\kappa}}\hat{C}_{\vec{g}_{\kappa^{\prime}}}

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncncocpcqcrcscvcwcydbdcdfdhdic)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\kappa}\hbar\omega_{\kappa}\ \hat{C}^{\dagger}_{\vec{g}_{\kappa}}\hat{C}_{\vec{g}_{\kappa}}\equiv\hat{H}_{BB},

(2wxzafaoarasatauavawayazbabcbdbebfblbncncocpcqcrcscvcwcydbdcdfdhdid)

where we have introduced the more compact notations

The isomorphism between the LP and the BB representations can also be interpreted in a reversed way. If we start with the LP representation it has two weak features: it relays on the choice of a particular gauge and it is not manifestly Lorentz invariant. The isomorphism
\mathcal{I}
provides a simple way to modify the formulation to obtain a gauge independent and manifestly Lorentz invariant representation.

7
Conclusion

In this article, we have explored different approaches of the quantization of the free electromagnetic field in position space that use the LP field and the BB vector. The key result was to show that they are linked by an isomorphism of their respective Hilbert spaces that we explicitely wrote using the frequency and the helicity operators. With this relation we were able to show that the quantum theories built from them through a Fock space quantization are equivalent, meaning that they predict the same physical results. This equivalence is preserved during the time evolution. In the literature, both the LP field and BB vectors were used for different practical problems and using the results of the present work, any calculation made from one representation can be easily translated into the other using the isomorphism
\mathcal{I}
.

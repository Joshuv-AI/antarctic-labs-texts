Josephson Junction through a Disordered Topological Insulator with Helical Magnetization
Alexander Zyuzin, Mohammad Alidoust, Daniel Loss

Abstract
We study supercurrent and proximity vortices in a Josephson junction made of disordered surface states of a three-dimensional topological insulator with a proximity induced in-plane helical magnetization. In a regime where the rotation period of helical magnetization is larger than the junction width, we find supercurrent 0 0 - π \pi crossovers as a function of junction thickness, magnetization strength, and parameters inherent to the helical modulation and surface states. The supercurrent reversals are associated with proximity induced vortices, nucleated along the junction width, where the number of vortices and their locations can be manipulated by means of the superconducting phase difference and the parameters mentioned above.

It was shown that the critical supercurrent shows no reversal in Josephson junctions made of ballistic surface states of a TI (S-TI-S) with proximity induced ‘uniform’ magnetization

Here we generalize a quasiclassical approach for disordered
surface states of three-dimensional TIs in the presence of
s

We derive the Eilenberger equation

We first employ the Usadel approach for a S-TI-S junction with uniform magnetization and show that similarly to the ballistic limit the critical supercurrent is featureless and an exchange field parallel to the S-TI interfaces transfers into the superconducting phase difference across the junction.
We then study the supercurrent in a diffusive 2D S-TI-S with an in-plane helical magnetization, illustrated in Fig.
1

Interestingly, we find that multiple supercurrent reversals and proximity vortices can appear by simply manipulating the helical magnetization parameters and varying the junction thickness.

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-i\omega_{n}+\hat{H}(\mathbf{r})&-\epsilon_{g}\\
\epsilon_{g}^{*}&i\omega_{n}+\hat{\sigma}^{y}\hat{H}^{*}(\mathbf{r})\hat{\sigma}^{y}\end{array}\right)\check{G}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})+\frac{1}{\pi\nu\tau}\check{G}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r})\check{G}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}),

(23)

the second term on the right hand side of Eq. (
III.1
) is defined by the self-energy part of the averaged Green function. Here we allow for the proximity induced minigap
\epsilon_{g}

in the surface states of the TI.

We first subtract from Eq. (
III.1
) its conjugated equation and perform a Fourier transformation with respect to the relative space arguments:

\check{G}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{R}+\frac{\delta\mathbf{r}}{2},\mathbf{R}-\frac{\delta\mathbf{r}}{2})=\int\frac{d\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\check{G}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{R},\mathbf{p})e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot\delta\mathbf{r}},

(24)

in which we have defined
\mathbf{R}=(\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{r}^{\prime})/2
,
\delta\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
, and denoted the momentum vector of the quasiparticles by
\mathbf{p}
. We also define a new parameter
\xi_{p}=\alpha p-\mu
and by making use of the fact that the Green function peaks at the Fermi surface, we obtain the quasiclassical Green function

\displaystyle\check{g}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{R},\mathbf{n}_{F})=\int\frac{d\xi_{p}}{\pi i}\check{G}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{R},\mathbf{p}).

(25)

Using this assumption, we obtain the Eilenberger equation,
eiln
:

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}\{\hat{{\bm{\eta}}},{\bm{\nabla}}\check{g}\}=\bigg[\check{g},\omega_{n}\hat{\tau}^{z}+i\mathbf{h}\cdot\hat{{\bm{\sigma}}}\hat{\tau}^{z}+i\mu\hat{{\bm{\eta}}}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{F}+i\check{\epsilon}_{g}+\frac{\langle\check{g}\rangle}{\tau}\bigg],

where
\hat{\bm{{\bm{\eta}}}}=(-\hat{\sigma}^{y},\hat{\sigma}^{x})
,
\check{\epsilon}_{g}=\hat{\sigma}^{0}(-\epsilon_{g}\hat{\tau}^{+}+\epsilon_{g}^{*}\hat{\tau}^{-})/2
, and
\hat{\tau}^{\pm}=\hat{\tau}^{x}\pm i\hat{\tau}^{y}
. This equation governs moving quasiparticles in ballistic (where
1/\tau\rightarrow 0
) systems and those with weak nonmagnetic impurities with a finite
\tau
.

III.2
Usadel Equation

The Eilenberger equation can be simplified in systems with strong disorders such that
\mu>1/\tau>|\omega_{n}|,|h|,|\epsilon_{g}|
. In this case, the quasiparticles follow diffusive trajectories which is the so-called diffusive regime
usadel
.
In the diffusive regime of the surface states, one can integrate the quasiclassical Green function, Eq. (
25
), over all possible directions of quasiparticles’ momentum:

\displaystyle\langle\check{g}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{R},\mathbf{n}_{F})\rangle\equiv\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\Omega_{\mathbf{n}_{F}}}{2\pi}\check{g}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{R},\mathbf{n}_{F}),\;\mathbf{n}_{F}=\frac{\mathbf{p}_{F}}{|\mathbf{p}_{F}|},~~~~~\;\;

(27)

where
\Omega_{\mathbf{n}_{F}}
is the polar angle of the vector
\mathbf{n}_{F}
in the plane of TI surface.
We note that Eq. (
III.1
) is similar to the kinetic equation for the Keldysh component of the Green function of TI in the normal state
Schwab
.

To find a solution to Eq. (
III.1
), we expand the Green function through the Pauli matrices:

\check{g}=\frac{\hat{g}^{\prime}\hat{1}+\hat{\mathbf{g}}^{\prime}\cdot\hat{{\bm{\eta}}}+\hat{g}^{\prime}_{z}\hat{\sigma}^{z}}{2},

(28)

where
\hat{\mathbf{g}}^{\prime}=(\hat{g}^{\prime}_{x},\hat{g}^{\prime}_{y},0)
.
We assume that the Fermi energy in the conduction band of the TI is much larger than all other energy scales available in the system. We immediately find that leading contributions to
\check{g}
commute with
\hat{\eta}\mathbf{n}_{F}
. Thus,
\hat{g}^{\prime}_{z}
component is smaller than

\hat{g}^{\prime}
and
\hat{g}^{\prime}_{x,y}
by a factor of
\sim\mathrm{max}(\frac{1}{\tau},|\mathbf{h}|)/\mu\ll 1
and can be neglected. Also, the spin structure of
\check{g}
is proportional to the conduction band projector
(1+\hat{{\bm{\eta}}}\mathbf{n}_{F})/2
in the limit of
|\mathbf{h}|\ll\mu
. We then set
\hat{\mathbf{g}}^{\prime}=\mathbf{n}_{F}\hat{g}^{\prime}
and propose a solution to Eq. (
III.1
) in the form of a direct product of two
2\times 2
matrices:

\displaystyle\check{g}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{R},\mathbf{n}_{F})=\hat{g}^{\prime}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{R},\mathbf{n}_{F})\frac{1+\hat{{\bm{\eta}}}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{F}}{2},

(29)

where we define

\displaystyle\hat{g}^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-g&-if\\
if^{*}&\bar{g}\end{array}\right).

Notice that the conduction band projector satisfies:
(1+\hat{{\bm{\eta}}}\mathbf{n}_{F})^{2}/4=(1+\hat{{\bm{\eta}}}\mathbf{n}_{F})/2
and
serves as the source of superconducting triplet correlations in the system. Substituting
expression (
29
) into Eq. (
III.1
), we arrive at the following equation:

\displaystyle\alpha(\hat{{\bm{\eta}}}+\mathbf{n}_{F})\cdot{\bm{\nabla}}\hat{g}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg[\hat{g}^{\prime}(1+\hat{{\bm{\eta}}}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{F}),\omega_{n}\hat{\tau}^{z}+i\mathbf{h}\cdot\hat{{\bm{\sigma}}}\hat{\tau}^{z}

(33)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle i\check{\Delta}+\frac{\langle\hat{g}^{\prime}(1+\hat{{\bm{\eta}}}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{F})\rangle}{2\tau}\bigg].~~~~

In the limit of diffusive motion of quasiparticles, the Green function can be expanded through the first two terms of 2D harmonics, namely:

\hat{g}^{\prime}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{R},\mathbf{n}_{F})=\hat{g}_{s}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{R})+\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{a}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{R}),

(34)

where the zero harmonic in the expansion is isotropic and its amplitude is much larger than the first harmonic:
\hat{g}_{s}\gg\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{a}
.

We now substitute the expanded Green function, Eq. (
34
), into Eq. (
33
) and perform an integration over momentum directions. By taking a spin trace we finally arrive at:

\displaystyle\alpha\hat{{\bm{\nabla}}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{a}=2[\hat{g}_{s},\omega_{n}\hat{\tau}^{z}+i\check{\epsilon}_{g}].

(35)

Here, we have defined a new operator
\hat{{\bm{\nabla}}}
so that

\hat{{\bm{\nabla}}}X={\bm{\nabla}}X+\frac{i}{\alpha}(h_{x}\mathbf{e}_{y}-h_{y}\mathbf{e}_{x})[\hat{\tau}^{z},X],

(36)

where
\mathbf{e}_{x,y}
are unit vectors in the
x,y
directions.
Multiplying Eq. (
33
) by
\mathbf{n}_{F}
and integrating it over momentum directions, we find the following expression for the first harmonic term of the Green function expansion
\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{a}
:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{a}=-2\alpha\tau\hat{g}_{s}\hat{{\bm{\nabla}}}\hat{g}_{s}.

(37)

To obtain the Usadel equation, it suffices we substitute Eq. (
37
) into Eq. (
35
) which yields:

\displaystyle D\hat{{\bm{\nabla}}}\cdot(\hat{g}_{s}\hat{{\bm{\nabla}}}\hat{g}_{s})=[\omega_{n}\hat{\tau}^{z}+i\check{\epsilon}_{g},\hat{g}_{s}],

(38)

where the diffusion coefficient is denoted by
D=\alpha^{2}\tau
. In the case
\check{\epsilon}_{g}=0
, Eq.
38
leads to Eq.
8
.
The singlet part of the Green function,
\hat{g}_{s}
, satisfies the Usadel equation Eq. (
38
). In contrast, due to fast spin relaxation time which is given by the mean free time in the TI, the motion of the spin part,
\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{a}
, is not diffusive, and satisfies Eq. (
37
).
To obtain the Eilenberger and Usadel equations for surface states with the Dresselhaus type of spin-orbit coupling, one simply should use
{\bm{\eta}}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y})
in the Eilenberger equation, Eq. (
III.1
), and change
\alpha
to
\beta_{D}
. In the Usadel equation, Eq. (
38
), however one can perform the replacement below:

\hat{{\bm{\nabla}}}X={\bm{\nabla}}X+\frac{i}{\beta_{D}}(h_{x}\mathbf{e}_{x}+h_{y}\mathbf{e}_{y})[\hat{\tau}^{z},X].

(39)

III.3
Boundary Conditions and the Current Density

We consider low transparency limit of the interface (tunneling barrier) between the TI and superconductor
boundary_c1
;
boundary_c2
.
We neglect the inverse proximity effect so that the Green function in the superconductor at the interface is given by its bulk solution. Assuming that the electron tunneling across the TI-S interface is spin-conserving, while it is not momentum conserving, we eventually arrive at the following expression

2\gamma\hat{g}_{s}\mathbf{n}\cdot\hat{{\bm{\nabla}}}\hat{g}_{s}=[\hat{g}_{s},\hat{g}_{\text{SC}}],

(40)

where
\mathbf{n}
is the unit vector normal to the boundary and

\hat{g}_{\text{SC}}(\omega_{n})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega_{n}^{2}+|\Delta|^{2}}}[\omega_{n}\hat{\tau}^{z}+\frac{i}{2}(-\Delta\hat{\tau}^{+}+\Delta^{*}\hat{\tau}^{-})],

(41)

is the Green function in the superconductor, in which
|\Delta|
is the superconducting gap. The low transparency of the interface leads to large parameter
\gamma\gg 1
, which is the ratio of resistance per unit area of the surface of the tunneling barrier to the resistivity of the TI.

Let us now present the current density in the TI.
The current density flowing across the surface of TI is given by:

\mathbf{J}=\frac{e\alpha}{2}T\sum_{n}\lim_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbf{r}}\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{\tau}^{z}\hat{{\bm{\eta}}}\check{G}(\omega_{n},\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})],

(42)

where
e>0
is the absolute value of electron charge. Rewriting the current density through the quasiclassical Green function, we find:

\mathbf{J}=i\pi\frac{e\alpha}{4}T\sum_{n}\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{\tau}^{z}\hat{\mathbf{g}}_{a}].

(43)

Substituting expression (
37
) into (
42
), we obtain the current density in the diffusive limit, Eq. (
9
).

III.4
Derivation of Supercurrent through Topological Insulator with Uniform and Helical Magnetizations

In the low proximity limit, we can expand the Green function around the bulk solution which yields:

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{s}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{R})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathrm{sign}(\omega_{n})&-i{f_{+}}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{R})\\
i{f_{-}}(\omega_{n};\mathbf{R})&-\mathrm{sign}(\omega_{n})\end{array}\right).

The low proximity limit is experimentally relevant and can be easily achieved in temperatures near the superconducting critical temperature or low transparent SC-TI contacts, for instance.
To derive the current density, and consequently the total supercurrent
through a helical magnetization, we use the Usadel equation,
(

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}=(h_{x},h_{y},0),

(47a)

(47b)

To avoid complication, we assume that the inverse proximity effect is neglegible and the magnetization is restricted within

\displaystyle\gamma[\partial_{x}\mp 2ih_{y}(y)/\alpha]f_{\mp}|_{x=-d_{F}/2}=\frac{|\Delta|e^{\mp i\phi/2}}{\sqrt{\omega_{n}^{2}+|\Delta|^{2}}},~~~~~~

The Usadel equation and associated boundary conditions yeild the following solutions:

III.5
Proximity Vortices

We have full numerically solved the Usadel equation in the presence of the helical magnetization given by Eq. (
47
). Substituting the solutions into the current density expression, Eq. (

), and the absolute value of the Cooper pair wave function below

Let us first investigate the slow rotating magnetization case which results in clearer conclusions through the analytics and then discuss the helical magnetization which demands numerics. Considering a slow rotating magnetization
q<1
and setting
x=0
in the solutions (
50

\displaystyle J_{x}(y)=\frac{\pi}{2e}\frac{d_{F}^{2}}{\gamma^{2}R_{N}}{\cal N}_{0}\sin\bigg(\phi+\frac{2h_{0}d_{F}}{\alpha}Qy\bigg),~~~

(55)

and

\displaystyle U(y)=\frac{T}{4\gamma}\sum_{n}\frac{|\Delta|\text{csch}(k_{n}d_{F}/2)}{\sqrt{\omega_{n}^{2}+|\Delta|^{2}}}\cos\bigg(\frac{\phi}{2}+\frac{h_{0}d_{F}}{\alpha}Qy\bigg).~~~~~~~

n=\pm 1,\pm 3,...
provided that

IV
Conclusions

In conclusion, we have derived the Eilenberger and Usadel equations which describe the quasiparticles in the disordered surface states of a TI in the presence of superconductivity and magnetism. We employed this approach to study the supercurrent flow through a TI with proximity induced in-plane helical magnetization. In contrast to the case of S-TI-S junction with uniform magnetization, our results reveal that the helical magnetization can induce multiple supercurrent reversals and proximity vortices upon varying the junction thickness, magnetization strength, and helical magnetization parameters.

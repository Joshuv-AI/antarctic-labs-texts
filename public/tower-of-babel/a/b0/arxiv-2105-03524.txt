Anton Enste

Abstract

In this paper, a simple proof that shows that the Somos-5 sequence
produces only natural numbers is given. We also give simple proof that each

a_{n}
in the Somos-5 sequence is relatively prime to its four
predecessors. The manner in which the integrality is proven is taken
from the 1992 proof for the integrality of the Somos-4 sequence by
Janice. L. Malouf.

Lemma 2
.

For
a,x,y\in\mathbb{N}
we have:

\text{gcd}(a,x)=\text{gcd}(a,y)=1\Leftrightarrow\text{gcd}(a,xy)=1

Proof.

First we prove that:

\text{gcd}(a,x)=\text{gcd}(a,y)=1\Rightarrow\text{gcd}(a,xy)=1.

We assume that there is a number
d
, for which we have
d|a
and

d|xy
.
Now we have to prove that
d=1
. We want to use Euclid’s Lemma, so
we have to show that
\text{gcd}(d,x)=1
. We can do this with a
proof by contradiction. So we assume that:

\text{gcd}(d,x)=e>1.\\

We know:

\begin{split}e|d,e|x\text{ and }e|a\;(\text{because }e|d\text{ and }d|a),\\
\text{we conclude: }e|\text{gcd}(a,x)=1,\end{split}

so
e=1
. Now we can use Euclid’s Lemma. We have:

d|xy\text{ and }\text{gcd}(d,x)=1,

so we know that
d|y
. We now know the following:

\begin{split}d|a,\;(\text{because of the definition of $d$})\text{ and }\;d|y,\\
\text{ so we conclude: }d=1,\text{ because }\text{gcd}(a,y)=1.\end{split}

Now we prove the other direction:

\text{gcd}(a,xy)=1\Rightarrow\text{gcd}(a,x)=\text{gcd}(a,y)=1

This is easier to prove. If there was a divisor
d>1
, which divided

a
and
x
or
a
and
y
, we would have:

\text{gcd}(a,xy)\neq 1

∎

Lemma 5
.

Given an equation
y=a\pmod{z}
and another equation

y\cdot b=c\pmod{z}
and
\text{gcd}(b,z)=1
. We have:

\begin{split}\text{If }y=0\pmod{z}\Leftrightarrow y\cdot b=0\pmod{z}\\
\text{ and if }y\neq 0\pmod{z}\Leftrightarrow y\cdot b\neq 0\pmod{z}\end{split}

Proof.

Assume
a\neq 0
then
y\neq 0\pmod{z}
and because
b
and
z
are
relatively prime
c\neq 0
, because none of the divisors of
z
is
present in
b
.

If
a=0
and
y\pmod{z}=0
, then it doesn’t matter which natural
number we multiply
y
with the solution
c
will always be 0. This
simply follows from Euclid’s Lemma:

z|yb\text{ and }\text{gcd}(z,b)=1\Rightarrow z|y

∎

Theorem 2
.

For every
a_{n}
of the Somos-5 sequence, we have

{a_{n-1}a_{n-4}+a_{n-2}a_{n-3}=0\pmod{a_{n-5}}}
and because of that
one finds that every
a_{n}
is a natural number.

Proof.

This proof is also via induction. One also has to know a few
index shifts of Definition
1
:

\begin{split}a_{n-5}a_{n-10}=a_{n-6}a_{n-9}+a_{n-7}a_{n-8}\\
a_{n-4}a_{n-9}=a_{n-5}a_{n-8}+a_{n-6}a_{n-7}\\
a_{n-3}a_{n-8}=a_{n-4}a_{n-7}+a_{n-5}a_{n-6}\\
a_{n-2}a_{n-7}=a_{n-3}a_{n-6}+a_{n-4}a_{n-5}\\
a_{n-1}a_{n-6}=a_{n-2}a_{n-5}+a_{n-3}a_{n-4}\\
\end{split}

It is easy to prove that the first ten elements of the Somos-5
sequence are integers just by looking at them.

Assume that there is an
n>9
for which all
a_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
with

k\leq n-1
.

We want to prove that
a_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
.
So we want to know if

a_{n-1}a_{n-4}+a_{n-2}a_{n-3}=0\pmod{a_{n-5}}.

According to Lemma
5
:

a_{n-1}a_{n-4}+a_{n-2}a_{n-3}\pmod{a_{n-5}}=a_{n-8}a_{n-9}(a_{n-1}a_{n-4}+a_{n-2}a_{n-3})\pmod{a_{n-5}}

because

\text{gcd}(a_{n-5},a_{n-8})=\text{gcd}(a_{n-5},a_{n-9})=1\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Lem.\ref{lem;zwei}}}{{=}}\text{gcd}(a_{n-5},a_{n-8}a_{n-9}).

So now we have:

\begin{split}&a_{n-8}a_{n-9}(a_{n-1}a_{n-4}+a_{n-2}a_{n-3})\\
&=a_{n-8}a_{n-9}a_{n-1}a_{n-4}+a_{n-8}a_{n-9}a_{n-2}a_{n-3}\\
&=a_{n-8}a_{n-1}(a_{n-5}a_{n-8}+a_{n-6}a_{n-7})+a_{n-9}a_{n-2}(a_{n-4}a_{n-7}+a_{n-5}a_{n-6})\\
&=a_{n-8}a_{n-1}a_{n-6}a_{n-7}+0+a_{n-9}a_{n-2}a_{n-4}a_{n-7}+0\\
&=a_{n-8}a_{n-7}(a_{n-2}a_{n-5}+a_{n-3}a_{n-4})+a_{n-9}a_{n-4}(a_{n-3}a_{n-6}+a_{n-4}a_{n-5})\\
&=a_{n-8}a_{n-7}a_{n-3}a_{n-4}+0+a_{n-9}a_{n-4}a_{n-3}a_{n-6}+0\\
&=a_{n-3}a_{n-4}(a_{n-8}a_{n-7}+a_{n-9}a_{n-6})\\
&=a_{n-3}a_{n-4}a_{n-5}a_{n-10}=0\pmod{a_{n-5}}\\
\end{split}

(10)

So we have proven that in the Somos-5 sequence, every
a_{n}\in\mathbb{N}

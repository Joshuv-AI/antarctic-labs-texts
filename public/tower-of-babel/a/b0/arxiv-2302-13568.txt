, and the well
defined kinematics, provide ideal conditions to study the decay

\Sigma^{+}\to p\gamma
. A double-tagged technique is applied to determine the
absolute BF
Baltrusaitis et al. 1986
. This approach was
applied in a recent work on the
\Lambda\rightarrow n\gamma
decay
investigated in the
J/\psi\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
process
by
BESIII

Ablikim et al. 2022a
, which achieved a better
precision than fixed target experiments. Moreover, the
decay
\Sigma^{+}\to p\gamma
and the corresponding charge conjugate one provide a good
opportunity to search for charge-parity (
CP
)
violation
Donoghue et al. 1986
;
*Tandean:2002vy
, whose experimental
information is absent for

\Sigma^{+}\to p\gamma

Li et al. 1993
;
*PhysRevD.51.2271
currently.

In this Letter, using
J/\psi\to\Sigma^{+}\bar{\Sigma}^{-}
decays from
10\,087\pm\pm 44\text{\times}{10}^{6}

J/\psi
events
Ablikim et al. 2022b
collected at
BESIII
,
we report the measurements of the absolute BF and decay asymmetry parameter of

\Sigma^{+}\to p\gamma
, as well as a test of
CP
violation in hyperon decays.
Throughout this Letter, charge conjugation is always
implied unless noted otherwise.

A detailed description of the design and performance of the

BESIII
detector can be found in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2010
;
*HUANG2022142
,
while the simulation and analysis software framework for
BESIII

are described in Refs.
Li et al. 2006
;
*Asner:2009zza
;
*zhang2018low
.
First, events of
J/\psi\to\Sigma^{+}\bar{\Sigma}^{-}
are selected with a single-tag (ST)
approach,
i.e.
reconstructing a
\bar{\Sigma}^{-}
candidate in one of
its dominant decay modes
\bar{\Sigma}^{-}\to\bar{p}\pi^{0}

(
\mathrm{BF}=$51.57\pm\pm 0.30\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
)
Workman et al. 2022
.
Then, the double-tag (DT) decay
\Sigma^{+}\to p\gamma
is searched for in the
system recoiling against the ST
\bar{\Sigma}^{-}
hyperon. The
corresponding absolute BF is calculated by

\mathrm{BF}(\Sigma^{+}\to p\gamma)=\frac{N^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{DT}}~\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ST}}}{N^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{ST}}~\varepsilon_{\mathrm{DT}}},

(1)

where
N^{\mathrm{obs}}_{\mathrm{ST(DT)}}
and
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ST(DT)}}
are the ST (DT) yields and the corresponding detection efficiencies,
respectively.

The helicity decay amplitudes of the
processes
Fäldt and Kupsc 2017
;
*PhysRevD.99.056008
, which will be used
for the measurement of
\alpha_{\gamma}
, are functions of the five observables

\xi=(\theta_{\Sigma^{+}},\theta_{p},\phi_{p},\theta_{\bar{p}},\phi_{\bar{p}})
. Here,

\theta_{\Sigma^{+}}
is the angle between the
\Sigma^{+}
hyperon and the
electron beam in the
J/\psi
rest frame,
\theta_{p}
(
\theta_{\bar{p}}
) and

\phi_{p}
(
\phi_{\bar{p}}
) are the polar and azimuthal angles of the proton
(anti-proton) with respect to the
\Sigma^{+}
helicity frame,
respectively. The differential cross-section is given as

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma\propto\mathcal{W}(\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{\xi})=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{0}(\xi)+\alpha_{\psi}\mathcal{F}_{5}(\xi)+\alpha_{\gamma}\bar{\alpha}_{0}

(2)

\displaystyle\times\left(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\xi)+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}_{\psi}}\cos(\Delta\Phi)\mathcal{F}_{2}(\xi)+\alpha_{\psi}\mathcal{F}_{6}(\xi)\right)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{\psi}^{2}}\sin(\Delta\Phi)(\alpha_{\gamma}\mathcal{F}_{3}(\xi)+\bar{\alpha}_{0}\mathcal{F}_{4}(\xi)),

where
\alpha_{\psi}
and
\Delta\Phi
are the hyperon production
parameters of the process
J/\psi\to\Sigma^{+}\bar{\Sigma}^{-}
,
\bar{\alpha}_{0}
is the decay asymmetry parameter for the
\bar{\Sigma}^{-}\to\bar{p}\pi^{0}
decays, and

\mathcal{F}_{i}(\xi)
(
i=0,1,...,6
) are the angular functions as described in detail in Ref.
Fäldt and Kupsc 2017
. The parameters used here are fixed to the values in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2020b
, except for
\alpha_{\gamma}
to be determined in this
analysis.

A sample of Monte Carlo (MC) simulated events of generic
J/\psi
decays corresponding to the
luminosity of data is used to study possible background reactions. On
the ST side, the signal MC sample of
J/\psi\rightarrow\Sigma^{+}\left(\rightarrow\mathrm{anything}\right)\bar{\Sigma}^{-}\left(\rightarrow\bar{p}\pi^{0}\right)
is generated with its helicity decay
amplitude. A phase space (PHSP) MC sample of
J/\psi\rightarrow\Delta(1232)^{+}(\rightarrow{\mathrm{anything}})\bar{\Delta}(1232)^{-}(\rightarrow\bar{p}\pi^{0})
is generated to study ST background. On the DT side, MC
samples for the
J/\psi\to\Sigma^{+}\left(\rightarrow p\gamma\right)\bar{\Sigma}^{-}\left(\rightarrow\bar{p}\pi^{0}\right)
signal and the dominant background

J/\psi\to\Sigma^{+}\left(\rightarrow p\pi^{0}\right)\bar{\Sigma}^{-}\left(\rightarrow\bar{p}\pi^{0}\right)
are generated according to their helicity decay
amplitudes.

Charged tracks and photon candidates are detected in the main drift
chamber and electromagnetic calorimeter (EMC), respectively.
Requirements on the polar angle, distance of closest approach to the
interaction point, particle identification probability of charged
tracks, the polar angle, energy, EMC time and distance to the nearest
charged track of photon candidates are
applied as in Ref.
Ablikim et al. 2020b
. The
\pi^{0}
candidates are
reconstructed from pairs of photon candidates with an invariant mass

M_{\gamma\gamma}
satisfying
$116\text{\,}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{\text{$c$}}^{2}$<M_{\gamma\gamma}<$148\text{\,}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{\text{$c$}}^{2}$
. A kinematic fit
constraining their invariant mass to the
\pi^{0}

mass
Workman et al. 2022
is performed on these photon pairs,
and the updated four-momentum is used in further analysis.

The candidate ST
\bar{\Sigma}^{-}
events are required to have at least
one
\bar{p}
and one
\pi^{0}
, and the
\bar{p}\pi^{0}
combination is
required to have an invariant mass of

\left|M_{\bar{p}\pi^{0}}-M_{\bar{\Sigma}^{-}}\right|<$13.5\text{\,}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{\text{$c$}}^{2}$
, where
M_{\bar{\Sigma}^{-}}
is the
nominal
\bar{\Sigma}^{-}
mass
Workman et al. 2022
. If there is
more than one combination satisfying this requirement, the one with
the minimum
\left|M_{\bar{p}\pi^{0}}-M_{\bar{\Sigma}^{-}}\right|
is kept
for further analysis. Finally, the ST yield is obtained from the mass
distribution recoiling against the reconstructed
\bar{\Sigma}^{-}

candidate, which is defined as

M_{\mathrm{rec}}=\sqrt{\left(E_{\mathrm{cms}}-E_{\bar{p}}-E_{\pi^{0}}\right)^{2}/c^{4}-\left(\mathbf{P}_{\bar{p}}+\mathbf{P}_{\pi^{0}}\right)^{2}/c^{2}}.

(3)

Here,
E_{\mathrm{cms}}
is the center-of-mass energy,
E_{\bar{p}}

(
\mathbf{P}_{\bar{p}}
) and
E_{\pi^{0}}
(
\mathbf{P}_{\pi^{0}}
) are the
energies (momenta) of the anti-proton and
\pi^{0}
in the
J/\psi
rest frame, respectively.

With the above selection criteria, the distribution of
M_{\rm{rec}}

of the ST candidates is shown in Fig.

, where there
is a clear peak representing the signal of
J/\psi\to\Sigma^{+}\bar{\Sigma}^{-}
. For ST
candidates, the background events are dominated by mis-combinations
and

. In the fit, the signal and

background are described by their MC simulated shapes
convolved with a Gaussian function, which represents the resolution
difference between data and MC simulation. Other non-peaking

.

\Sigma^{+}
and
\Delta(1232)^{+}

and

, where

\sigma_{L}
is the decay length resolution. By vetoing these events, the
\Delta(1232)^{+}\rightarrow p\pi^{0}
background is reduced by
93\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
while

78\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
of the signal is preserved. The remaining
\Sigma^{+}\rightarrow p\pi^{0}
background usually includes a high energy photon

\pi^{0}

Multi-impurity chiral Kondo model: correlation functions and anyon fusion rules
Dor Gabay, Cheolhee Han, Pedro L. S. Lopes, Ian Affleck, Eran Sela

Abstract
The multichannel Kondo model supports effective anyons on the partially screened impurity, as suggested by its fractional impurity entropy. It was recently demonstrated for the multi-impurity chiral Kondo model, that scattering of an electron through the impurities depends on the anyon’s total fusion channel. Here we study the correlation between impurity-spins. We argue, based on a combination of conformal field theory, a perturbative limit with a large number of channels k k , and the exactly solvable two-channel case, that the inter-impurity spin correlation probes the anyon fusion of the pair of correlated impurities. This may allow, using measurement-only topological quantum computing protocols, to braid the multichannel Kondo anyons via consecutive measurements.

Boundary condition dependent correlation functions

Before diving into the calculations, we outline the main result of this section.

The picture we demonstrate is that in the chiral multi-impurity Kondo model, the expression
\vec{S}\sim\vec{\Phi}(x=0)
for a single impurity spin gets generalized to include an operator
\Gamma_{\ell}
acting on the degenerate Hilbert space spanned by the anyons,

\vec{S}_{\ell}\sim\Gamma_{\ell}\vec{\Phi}(x_{\ell}),~~~({\ell}=1,\dots M).

(29)

As the main result, we show that, unlike asymptotic correlations which probe the total fusion sector
j_{tot}
(Eq. (
10
)), impurity-spin correlations probe the fusion outcome of the individual pair of anyons associated with the correlated impurities. We conjecture a general form of the leading order contribution to the impurity-spin correlation function,

\displaystyle\braket{S^{a}_{n}(z_{n})S^{b}_{m}(z_{m})}_{j_{nm}}\approx\delta^{ab}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{k}(j_{nm})}{(z_{n}-z_{m})^{\frac{4}{2+k}}},\;\;\;(k\geq 2).

(30)

Here,
j_{nm}=0,1
spans the total angular momentum sectors of impurity spins
\vec{S}_{m}
and
\vec{S}_{n}
. This expression, in accordance with Eq. (
29
), shows on the one hand the power law dependence on the coordinates
(z_{n}-z_{m})
dictated by the primary field
\vec{\Phi}
, and on the other hand carries a dependence on the fusion state. Our large
k
results below are consistent with

\mathcal{F}_{k\to\infty}(j_{nm})=\frac{1}{6}j_{nm}(j_{nm}+1)-\frac{1}{4}.

(31)

From this equation, the impurity spin correlator probes the fusion state of the specific pair of anyons, independently of the impurity spins between them. In this section we confirm this conjecture in the large
k
-limit.

IV.2
Single Impurity

Next we dive into the technical calculations that lead to Eq. (
30
). First, we develop the large-
k
techniques for the single impurity case and only then discuss the multi-impurity case in Sec.
IV.3
. The impatient reader may skip to the main result in Eq. (
41
).

We begin with the preparatory step of studying the single impurity-spin correlation using perturbation theory in the large-
k
limit. The intra-impurity-spin correlation can be derived perturbatively to second-order in
\lambda
by expanding the exponential of the Hamiltonian in path integral form,

\displaystyle\braket{S^{a}(\tau_{n})S^{b}(\tau_{m})}=\braket{S^{a}(\tau_{n})S^{b}(\tau_{m})}_{(0)}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\sum_{cd}\int d\tau^{\prime}d\tau^{\prime\prime}\Bigl\langle{S^{a}(\tau_{n})S^{b}(\tau_{m})\Bigl(J^{c}S^{c}\Bigr)(\tau^{\prime})\Bigl(J^{d}S^{d}\Bigr)(\tau^{\prime\prime})\Bigr\rangle}.

Here,
J^{a}=\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\dagger}\sigma^{a}\psi
is the spin density field. The zeroth-order contribution is quite trivial, giving

\displaystyle\braket{S^{a}(\tau_{n})S^{b}(\tau_{m})}_{(0)}=\frac{\delta^{ab}}{4}.

(32)

The first-order contribution vanishes by rotational invariance, since
\braket{\vec{J}}=0
. This leaves us with the second order contribution, containing a nontrivial 4-spin correlator

\displaystyle\braket{S^{a}(\tau_{n})S^{b}(\tau_{m})}_{(2)}=\frac{k}{4}\lambda^{2}\int d\tau^{\prime}d\tau^{\prime\prime}\frac{\delta^{ab}}{(\tau^{\prime}-\tau^{\prime\prime}-i\epsilon)^{2}}\sum_{c}\Bigl\langle{S^{a}(\tau_{n})S^{a}(\tau_{m})S^{c}(\tau^{\prime})S^{c}(\tau^{\prime\prime})\Bigr\rangle},

(33)

where we utilize the free spin density field operator product expansion (OPE)

\displaystyle[J^{a}(z),J^{b}(w)]=\frac{k}{2}\frac{\delta^{ab}}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{i\epsilon^{abc}J^{c}}{z-w}.

(34)

The second term of the OPE does not contribute to Eq. (
33
). The infinitesimal value
\epsilon
is fictitiously prescribed to capture the logarithmic divergence from the integral evaluation done in Appendix
D
. It is now a matter of recognizing the various time-orderings resulting from the spin correlator to determine whether a nonzero second-order contribution exists. More generally, the 4-spin correlator can be re-expressed as

\displaystyle\Bigl\langle{S^{a}S^{b}S^{c}S^{d}\Bigr\rangle}=\frac{1}{16}\bigl(\delta^{ab}\delta^{cd}-\delta^{ac}\delta^{bd}+\delta^{ad}\delta^{bc}\bigr).

(35)

For
\tau^{\prime}>\tau^{\prime\prime}
, the spin indices in Eq. (
33
) for the six possible time orderings [numbers
(1)
-
(6)
depicted in Fig.
5
(a)] can then be expressed as

\displaystyle(1)=\sum_{c}\Bigl\langle{S^{a}(\tau_{n})S^{a}(\tau_{m})S^{c}(\tau^{\prime})S^{c}(\tau^{\prime\prime})\Bigr\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{16},

\displaystyle(2)=\sum_{c}\Bigl\langle{S^{a}(\tau_{n})S^{c}(\tau^{\prime})S^{a}(\tau_{m})S^{c}(\tau^{\prime\prime})\Bigr\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{16},

\displaystyle(3)=\sum_{c}\Bigl\langle{S^{a}(\tau_{n})S^{c}(\tau^{\prime})S^{c}(\tau^{\prime\prime})S^{a}(\tau_{m})\Bigr\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{16},

\displaystyle(4)=\sum_{c}\Bigl\langle{S^{c}(\tau^{\prime})S^{a}(\tau_{n})S^{c}(\tau^{\prime\prime})S^{a}(\tau_{m})\Bigr\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{16},

\displaystyle(5)=\sum_{c}\Bigl\langle{S^{c}(\tau^{\prime})S^{a}(\tau_{i})S^{a}(\tau_{m})S^{c}(\tau^{\prime\prime})\Bigr\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{16},

\displaystyle(6)=\sum_{c}\Bigl\langle{S^{c}(\tau^{\prime})S^{c}(\tau^{\prime\prime})S^{a}(\tau_{n})S^{a}(\tau_{m})\Bigr\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{16}.

(36)

Inserting this result back into Eq. (
33
) and evaluating the integral, as is done in Appendix
D
, we obtain a logarithmic
k
-dependent correction to the impurity-spin correlator

\displaystyle\braket{S^{a}(\tau_{n})S^{b}(\tau_{m})}=\frac{\delta^{ab}}{4}\Biggl[1-\frac{4}{k}\log(\tau_{n}-\tau_{m})+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\Bigr)\Biggr].

(37)

Interestingly, using
\vec{S}\sim\mathcal{C}\vec{\Phi}(x=0)
with some nonuniversal constant
\mathcal{C}
, as in Ref.
Ludwig and Affleck 1994
, we have

\displaystyle\langle S^{a}(\tau_{n})S^{b}(\tau_{m})\rangle\propto\delta^{ab}\frac{|\mathcal{C}|^{2}}{(\tau_{n}-\tau_{m})^{\frac{4}{2+k}}}.

(38)

Equation (
37
) contains the first two terms in the large-
k
expansion of this CFT result. In what follows, we show that a similar expression is attained in the multi-impurity scenario, up to a nontrivial factor associated to the total spin sectors of the correlated impurities.

IV.3
Multiple Impurities

In the multi-impurity scenario, the Kondo Hamiltonian contains a sum over
M
impurity spins. This sum is carried over to the first and second order perturbation terms.

As before, the first-order contribution is null due to the expectation value of the spin density. The zeroth- and second-order contributions of the inter-impurity-spin correlator for impurity spins at holomorphic coordinates
z_{n}=\tau_{n}-ix_{n}
and
z_{m}=\tau_{m}-ix_{m}
are

\displaystyle\braket{S^{a}_{n}(z_{n})S^{b}_{m}(z_{m})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\braket{S^{a}_{n}(\tau_{n})S^{b}_{m}(\tau_{m})}_{(0)}

(39)

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\sum_{\ell\ell^{\prime}=1}^{M}\sum_{cd}\int d\tau^{\prime}d\tau^{\prime\prime}\Bigl\langle{S^{a}_{n}(\tau_{n})S^{b}_{m}(\tau_{m})J^{c}(z^{\prime})S^{c}_{\ell}(\tau^{\prime})J^{d}(z^{\prime\prime})S^{d}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime\prime})\Bigr\rangle}.

The zeroth-order contribution can be evaluated by adopting the result of Eq. (
22
), where
j_{nm}=0,1
represents the different total angular momentum sectors generated by the pair of spins. This procedure is different from tracing over the total angular momentum sectors, as emphasized in the previous section.

Using the OPE of Eq. (
34
), the second-order contribution to the inter-impurity-spin correlator is

\displaystyle\braket{S^{a}_{n}(z_{n})S^{b}_{m}(z_{m})}_{(2)}=\frac{k}{4}\lambda^{2}\sum_{\ell\ell^{\prime}=1}^{M}\int d\tau^{\prime}d\tau^{\prime\prime}\frac{\delta^{ab}}{(\tau^{\prime}-\tau^{\prime\prime}-i\Delta{x})^{2}}\sum_{c}\Bigl\langle{S^{a}_{n}(\tau_{n})S^{a}_{m}(\tau_{m})S^{c}_{\ell}(\tau^{\prime})S^{c}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime\prime})\Bigr\rangle},

(40)

where
\Delta{x}=x_{\ell}-x_{\ell^{\prime}}
.

Evaluating this integral is more cumbersome than the one-impurity case. One has to consider all of the possible impurity configurations within the 4-spin correlator, as is done in Appendix
E
. After evaluating this integral and letting
\lambda
trend as
2/k
, the zeroth- and second-order contributions to the inter-impurity correlator in the large-
k
limit are

\displaystyle\braket{S^{a}_{n}(\tau_{n})S^{b}_{m}(\tau_{m})}_{j_{nm}}=\delta^{ab}\Bigl(\frac{1}{6}j_{nm}(j_{nm}+1)-\frac{1}{4}\Bigr)\Biggl[1-\frac{4}{k}\log(\tau_{n}-\tau_{m})+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\Bigr)\Biggr].

(41)

This is quite remarkable. We obtain the same expression as the large-
k
limit of the single impurity scenario, i.e. Eq. (
37
), up to a factor
\frac{1}{6}j_{nm}(j_{nm}+1)-\frac{1}{4}
dictating the total angular momentum sectors of the correlated impurity spins (i.e., either a singlet or triplet corresponding to values of
-1/4
and
1/12
, respectively). To leading order, Eq. (
41
) can be understood within the framework of CFT by expanding the
\ell
-th impurity-spin
\vec{S}_{\ell}
as
Ludwig and Affleck 1994

\displaystyle\vec{S}_{\ell}\sim\Gamma_{\ell}\vec{\Phi}.

(42)

In the large-
k
limit, inter-impurity-spin correlations, following this expansion, would result in Eq. (
30
). The additional dependence on
j_{nm}
implies that, instead of Eq. (
38
), the impurity-spin effectively has an operator
\Gamma_{\ell}
acting nontrivially on the anyon sector.

The prescribed impurity-spins allow us to probe their internal fusion-tree states independently of the impurity-spins residing between them. In the following section, this outcome will be demonstrated for two-channel Kondo systems.

Summary

In this paper we studied impurity spin correlation functions of the chiral multi-impurity Kondo model. Substantial evidence has been provided, both from the large-
k
limit and two-channel case, that, when computed in pure states within specific quantum sectors, impurity spin correlations display a dependence on the internal state nonlocally shared by the effectively fractionalized spins. An interpretation of this dependence has been given in terms of anyon fusion rules. Different than asymptotic correlators like the Green’s function that depend only on the total fusion channel
Lopes et al. 2020
,

In contrast to paradigmatic gapped topological phases as in the fractional quantum Hall effect, our proposed multi-impurity Kondo system is gapless. Nevertheless, the anyons do not couple to unfractionalized degrees of freedom, demonstrating a source of protection.

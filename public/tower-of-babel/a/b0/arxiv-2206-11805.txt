Monogamy of entanglement between cones
Guillaume Aubrun, Alexander Müller-Hermes, Martin Plávala

Abstract
. A separable quantum state shared between parties A A and B B can be symmetrically extended to a quantum state shared between party A A and parties B 1 , … , B k B_{1},\ldots,B_{k} for every k ∈ 𝐍 k\in\mathbf{N} . Quantum states that are not separable, i.e., entangled, do not have this property. This phenomenon is known as “monogamy of entanglement”. We show that monogamy is not only a feature of quantum theory, but that it characterizes the minimal tensor product of general pairs of convex cones 𝖢 A \mathsf{C}_{A} and 𝖢 B \mathsf{C}_{B} : The elements of the minimal tensor product 𝖢 A ⊗ min 𝖢 B \mathsf{C}_{A}\otimes_{\min}\mathsf{C}_{B} are precisely the tensors that can be symmetrically extended to elements in the maximal tensor product 𝖢 A ⊗ max 𝖢 ⊗ max k B \mathsf{C}_{A}\otimes_{\max}\mathsf{C}^{\otimes_{\max}k}_{B} for every k ∈ 𝐍 k\in\mathbf{N} . Equivalently, the minimal tensor product of two cones is the intersection of the nested sets of k k -extendible tensors. It is a natural question when the minimal tensor product 𝖢 A ⊗ min 𝖢 B \mathsf{C}_{A}\otimes_{\min}\mathsf{C}_{B} coincides with the set of k k -extendible tensors for some finite k k . We show that this is universally the case for every cone 𝖢 A \mathsf{C}_{A} if and only if 𝖢 B \mathsf{C}_{B} is a polyhedral cone with a base given by a product of simplices. Our proof makes use of a new characterization of products of simplices up to affine equivalence that we believe is of independent interest.



1.
Introduction

Let
V
denote a finite-dimensional vector space over the reals and
\mathsf{C}\subseteq V
a convex cone. We say that the cone
\mathsf{C}
is
proper
if it is closed, does not contain any line, and is not contained in any hyperplane. Any proper cone
\mathsf{C}\subseteq V
can be described in terms of its dual
\mathsf{C}^{*}\subseteq V^{*}
, given by

\mathsf{C}^{*}=\{f\in V^{*}~:~f(x)\geqslant 0\text{ for any }x\in\mathsf{C}\},

since by the bipolar theorem we have
(\mathsf{C}^{*})^{*}=\mathsf{C}
. While any proper cone can be described from the inside by its extremal rays, the bipolar theorem describes the cone from the outside as an intersection of half-spaces. This duality is fundamental in the study of convex cones and it is a basic problem in convex analysis to switch between these two descriptions.

We will be interested in the two canonical tensor products of any pair of proper cones. Given proper cones
\mathsf{C}_{A}\subseteq V_{A}
,
\mathsf{C}_{B}\subseteq V_{B}
, we may form the
minimal tensor product

\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\min}}\mathsf{C}_{B}=\conv\{x\otimes y~:~x\in\mathsf{C}_{A},\ y\in\mathsf{C}_{B}\}\subseteq V_{A}\otimes V_{B},

and the
maximal tensor product

\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{C}_{B}=(\mathsf{C}^{*}_{A}{\otimes_{\min}}\mathsf{C}^{*}_{B})^{*}=\{z\in V_{A}\otimes V_{B}~:~(f\otimes g)(z)\geqslant 0,\ f\in\mathsf{C}_{A}^{*},\ g\in\mathsf{C}_{B}^{*}\}.

For proper cones
\mathsf{C}_{A}
and
\mathsf{C}_{B}
both tensor products are again proper cones. Moreover, the tensor products may be iterated and since they are associative we may define the
k
-fold versions
\mathsf{C}^{\otimes_{\min}k}
and
\mathsf{C}^{\otimes_{\max}k}
for any proper cone
\mathsf{C}
. The two tensor products reflect the aforementioned descriptions of convex cones: The minimal tensor product is described by specifying generating elements and the maximal tensor product by an intersection of half-spaces. Moreover, their discrepancy can be seen as a general form of quantum entanglement
[
1
]
.

A central feature of quantum physics is known as
monogamy of entanglement
. In our language, it can be described using the cones of positive semidefinite
d\times d
matrices
\mathsf{M}^{+}_{d}
with complex entries, which is a proper cone inside the real vector space
\mathsf{M}_{d}^{\mathrm{sa}}
of Hermitian
d\times d
matrices. A bipartite quantum state is given by a matrix
\rho_{AB}\in\mathsf{M}_{d_{A}d_{B}}^{+}\simeq(\mathsf{M}_{d_{A}}\otimes\mathsf{M}_{d_{B}})^{+}\subsetneq\mathsf{M}_{d_{A}}^{+}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{M}_{d_{B}}^{+}
for some integers
{d_{A},d_{B}\geqslant 2}
and it is called
entangled
if it does not belong to
\mathsf{M}^{+}_{d_{A}}\otimes_{\min}\mathsf{M}^{+}_{d_{B}}
. The
monogamy theorem

[
6
,
16
]
asserts that a quantum state
\rho_{AB}\in(\mathsf{M}_{d_{A}}\otimes\mathsf{M}_{d_{B}})^{+}
is entangled if and only if there exists an integer
k\geqslant 2
for which no quantum state
\sigma_{AB_{1}\cdots B_{k}}\in(\mathsf{M}_{d_{A}}\otimes\mathsf{M}^{\otimes k}_{d_{B}})^{+}
satisfies the equation

\rho_{AB}=\left(\mathrm{Id}_{\displaystyle\mathsf{M}^{\mathrm{sa}}_{d_{A}}}\otimes\gamma^{\Tr}_{k}\right)\left(\sigma_{AB_{1}\cdots B_{k}}\right),

where

\gamma^{\Tr}_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Tr^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\displaystyle\mathsf{M}^{\mathrm{sa}}_{d_{B}}}\otimes\Tr^{\otimes(k-j)}

corresponds to (symmetrically) discarding all but one out of the
k
tensor factors labelled by
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}
. Physically, this means that a bipartite quantum state is entangled if and only if it cannot be partially shared with arbitrarily many parties. Our first main result establishes a similar property for general minimal and maximal tensor products.

Theorem 1
.

Consider proper cones
\mathsf{C}_{A}\subseteq V_{A}
and
\mathsf{C}_{B}\subseteq V_{B}
and a linear form
\phi
in the interior of
\mathsf{C}_{B}^{*}
. Then, we have

(1)

\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\min}}\mathsf{C}_{B}=\bigcap_{k\geqslant 1}\left(\mathrm{Id}_{V_{A}}\otimes\gamma_{k}^{\phi}\right)\left(\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{C}_{B}^{{\otimes_{\max}}k}\right),

where, for an integer
k\geqslant 1
, the
k
th reduction map

\gamma_{k}^{\phi}:V_{B}^{\otimes k}\to V_{B}
is defined as

\gamma_{k}^{\phi}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\phi^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{V_{B}}\otimes\phi^{\otimes(k-j)}.

Our Theorem
1
shows that the monogamy property of quantum entanglement is actually a common feature of the most general forms of entanglement for any pair of cones. The question whether Theorem
1
holds has been asked in
[
11
, §2.4.1]
. Even for cones of positive semidefinite matrices our results are new: They show that entanglement cannot be partially shared with arbitrarily many parties even if we allow for unphysical quantum states represented in the maximal tensor product.

Equation (
1
) produces a decreasing sequence of outer approximations to the minimal tensor product, called the
extendability hierarchy
. A natural question is whether this sequence stops after finitely many steps. We answer this question in terms of combinatorial properties of the base
K_{\phi}=\mathsf{C}_{B}\cap\phi^{-1}(1)
. We have the following theorem:

Theorem 2
.

Consider a proper cone
\mathsf{C}_{B}\subseteq V_{B}
and a linear form
\phi
in the interior of
\mathsf{C}_{B}^{*}
. Then, for every integer
k\geqslant 1
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

For every proper cone
\mathsf{C}_{A}\subseteq V_{A}
, we have the identity

\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\min}}\mathsf{C}_{B}=\left(\mathrm{Id}_{V_{A}}\otimes\gamma_{k}^{\phi}\right)\left(\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{C}_{B}^{{\otimes_{\max}}k}\right);

(ii)

The base
K_{\phi}
is affinely equivalent to a Cartesian product of
\leqslant k
simplices.

As a byproduct of our proof, we obtain a characterization of products of simplices as the only polytopes (up to affine equivalence) for which the operations “intersection” and “affine hull” commute when applied to the face lattice (see Figure
1
for an illustration). We denote by
\aff(X)
the affine hull of a subset
X
of a vector space, using the convention that
\aff(\emptyset)=\emptyset
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
P
be a polytope. The following are equivalent:

(i)

The polytope
P
is affinely equivalent to a Cartesian product of simplices;

(ii)

For every faces
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
of
P
, we have

(2)

\aff\left(\bigcap_{i\in I}F_{i}\right)=\bigcap_{i\in I}\aff(F_{i}).

In the case of dimension
3
, Theorem
3
means that the only polyhedra without stellation are the simplex, the cube and the triangular prism, extending an observation by Wenninger
[
15
, p.35]
.

Figure 1.
The planar case of Theorem
3
: if a polygon is neither a triangle nor a parallelogram, it has disjoint edges whose affine hulls intersect.

Conventions and preliminaries

Given an integer
n\geqslant 1
, we denote by
[n]
the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
. All the vector spaces we consider are implicitly assumed to be finite-dimensional. If
V
is a vector space, the action of
f\in V^{*}
on
x\in V
is denoted as
f(x)
,
\langle f,x\rangle
or
\langle x,f\rangle
. We always identify the double dual
V^{**}
with
V
itself. Similarly, for an integer
k\geqslant 2
we always identify
(V^{\otimes k})^{*}
with
(V^{*})^{\otimes k}
.

Given vector spaces
V_{1},V_{2}
, the adjoint of a linear map
\Phi:V_{1}\to V_{2}
is denoted as
\Phi^{*}:V_{2}^{*}\to V_{1}^{*}
. If
X\subseteq V
, the affine hull
\aff(X)
is the set of elements of the form
\lambda_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}x_{n}
for an integer
n\geqslant 1
, elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
in
X
and real numbers
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
such that
\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}=1
. An element
y\in V
belongs to
\aff(X)
if and only if every affine function
f:V\to\mathbf{R}
which vanishes on
X
vanishes at
y
.

2.
Basic properties of the extendibility hierarchy and examples

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional real vector space and
k\geqslant 1
an integer. The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
acts naturally on
V^{\otimes k}
: if
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}
, we define
U_{\sigma}:V^{\otimes k}\to V^{\otimes k}
as the linear map such that

U_{\sigma}(x_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{k})=x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}

for every
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in V
.
We define the
symmetric subspace

\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V)
as the subspace of
V^{\otimes k}
which is invariant for this action

\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V)=\{x\in V^{\otimes k}\ :\ U_{\sigma}x=x\textnormal{ for every }\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}\}.

The
symmetric projection
is the operator
P_{\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V)}:V^{\otimes k}\to V^{\otimes k}
defined as

P_{\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V)}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}U_{\sigma}.

It is a projection whose range equals
\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V)
. If we identify
(V^{\otimes k})^{*}
with
(V^{*})^{\otimes k}
, we have the relation

P_{\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V)}^{*}=P_{\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V^{*})}.

Moreover, if
\mathsf{C}\subseteq V
is a proper cone, then

P_{\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V)}(\mathsf{C}^{{\otimes_{\min}}k})=\mathsf{C}^{{\otimes_{\min}}k}\cap{\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V)},

P_{\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V)}(\mathsf{C}^{{\otimes_{\max}}k})=\mathsf{C}^{{\otimes_{\max}}k}\cap{\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V)}.

2.1.
The extendibility hierarchy

Consider proper cones
\mathsf{C}_{A}\subseteq V_{A}
,
\mathsf{C}_{B}\subseteq V_{B}
and
\phi\in\inter(\mathsf{C}_{B}^{*})
.
For an integer
k\geqslant 1
, denote by

(3)

\mathsf{Ext}_{k}(\mathsf{C}_{A},\mathsf{C}_{B},\phi)=(\mathrm{Id}_{V_{A}}\otimes\gamma_{k}^{\phi})\left(\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{C}_{B}^{{\otimes_{\max}}k}\right)

the cone appearing in Theorem
1
. Since
\gamma_{k}^{\phi}=(\mathrm{Id}_{V_{B}}\otimes\phi^{\otimes(k-1)})\circ P_{\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V_{B})}
, an element
x\in V_{A}\otimes V_{B}
belongs to
\mathsf{Ext}_{k}(\mathsf{C}_{A},\mathsf{C}_{B},\phi)
if and only if

\exists y_{k}\in\left(\mathrm{Id}_{V_{A}}\otimes P_{\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V_{B})}\right)\left(\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{C}_{B}^{{\otimes_{\max}}k}\right)\ :\ x=\left(\mathrm{Id}_{V_{A}}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{V_{B}}\otimes\phi^{\otimes(k-1)}\right)(y_{k}).

Such an element
y_{k}
is called a
k
-extension
of
x
and
\mathsf{Ext}_{k}(\mathsf{C}_{A},\mathsf{C}_{B},\phi)
is called the cone of
k
-extendible elements
. Observe that

\left(\mathrm{Id}_{V_{A}}\otimes P_{\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V_{B})}\right)\left(\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{C}_{B}^{{\otimes_{\max}}k}\right)=\left(V_{A}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V_{B})\right)\cap\left(\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{C}_{B}^{{\otimes_{\max}}k}\right)

and therefore, whenever
y_{k}
is a
k
-extension of
x
, then
(\mathrm{Id}_{V_{A}}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{V_{B}}^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes\phi)(y_{k})
is a
(k-1)
-extension of
x
. It follows that the sequence
\mathsf{Ext}_{k}(\mathsf{C}_{A},\mathsf{C}_{B},\phi)
is a decreasing sequence of cones.
We also define

\mathsf{Ext}_{\infty}(\mathsf{C}_{A},\mathsf{C}_{B},\phi)=\bigcap_{k\geqslant 1}\mathsf{Ext}_{k}(\mathsf{C}_{A},\mathsf{C}_{B},\phi).

The first level of this hierarchy is the maximal tensor product itself, i.e., we have
\mathsf{Ext}_{1}(\mathsf{C}_{A},\mathsf{C}_{B},\phi)=\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{C}_{B}
, and Theorem
1
asserts that

\mathsf{Ext}_{\infty}(\mathsf{C}_{A},\mathsf{C}_{B},\phi)=\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\min}}\mathsf{C}_{B}
.

2.2.
The dual of the extendibility hierarchy

The adjoint of the
k
th reduction map
\gamma_{k}^{\phi}
is the map
(\gamma_{k}^{\phi})^{*}:V_{B}^{*}\to(V_{B}^{*})^{\otimes k}
which for
\psi\in V_{B}^{*}
reads as

(\gamma_{k}^{\phi})^{*}(\psi)=P_{\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V_{B}^{*})}(\psi\otimes\phi^{\otimes(k-1)}).

Let
\zeta\in V_{A}^{*}\otimes V_{B}^{*}
. It follows immediately from (
3
) that

(4)

\zeta\in\mathsf{Ext}_{k}(\mathsf{C}_{A},\mathsf{C}_{B},\phi)^{*}\iff(\mathrm{Id}_{V_{A}}\otimes(\gamma_{k}^{\phi})^{*})(\zeta)\in\mathsf{C}_{A}^{*}{\otimes_{\min}}(\mathsf{C}_{B}^{*})^{{\otimes_{\min}}k}.

In particular, we can write down a statement which is dual to Theorem
1
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathsf{C}_{A}\subseteq V_{A}
and
\mathsf{C}_{B}\subseteq V_{B}
be proper cones. For every
x\in\inter(\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{C}_{B})
and
y\in\inter(\mathsf{C}_{B})
, there is an integer
k\geqslant 1
such that

\left(\mathrm{Id}_{V_{A}}\otimes P_{\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V_{B})}\right)(x\otimes y^{\otimes(k-1)})\in\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\min}}\mathsf{C}_{B}^{{\otimes_{\min}}k}.

Proof.

By Theorem
1
applied to
\mathsf{C}_{A}^{*}
and
\mathsf{C}_{B}^{*}
, we have

\mathsf{C}_{A}^{*}{\otimes_{\min}}\mathsf{C}_{B}^{*}=\mathsf{Ext}_{\infty}(\mathsf{C}_{A}^{*},\mathsf{C}_{B}^{*},y)=\bigcap_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\mathsf{Ext}_{k}(\mathsf{C}_{A}^{*},\mathsf{C}_{B}^{*},y).

Using the bipolar theorem in the form
(\mathsf{C}^{*})^{*}=\overline{\mathsf{C}}
whenever
\mathsf{C}
is a not-necessarily-closed convex cone, this is equivalent to the dual equation

\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{C}_{B}=\left(\bigcap_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\mathsf{Ext}_{k}(\mathsf{C}_{A}^{*},\mathsf{C}_{B}^{*},y)\right)^{*}=\overline{\bigcup_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\mathsf{Ext}_{k}(\mathsf{C}_{A}^{*},\mathsf{C}_{B}^{*},y)^{*}}.

Since
x\in\inter(\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{C}_{B})
, there is an integer
k\geqslant 1
such that
x\in\mathsf{Ext}_{k}(\mathsf{C}_{A}^{*},\mathsf{C}_{B}^{*},y)^{*}
. By (
4
), this means that
(\mathrm{Id}_{V_{A}}\otimes(\gamma_{k}^{y})^{*})(x)\in\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\min}}\mathsf{C}_{B}^{{\otimes_{\min}}k}
, which is the desired conclusion.
∎

2.3.
Examples

Simplicial cones

We say that a cone
\mathsf{C}\subseteq V
is
simplicial
(or
classical
) if any of its bases is a simplex. Since
\gamma_{1}^{\phi}
is the identity operator for any nonzero linear form
\phi
, the case
k=1
of Theorem
2
says that a cone
\mathsf{C}_{B}
is simplicial if and only if
\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\min}}\mathsf{C}_{B}=\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{C}_{B}
for every proper cone
\mathsf{C}_{A}
. This result can be traced back to
[
12
]
and is much simpler to prove than the case
k>1
of Theorem
2
. A strengthening of the

[

]
: given two proper cones
\mathsf{C}_{A}
,
\mathsf{C}_{B}
, we have
\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\min}}\mathsf{C}_{B}=\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{C}_{B}
if and only if either
\mathsf{C}_{A}
or
\mathsf{C}_{B}
is simplicial.

The quantum case

Consider an operator
\rho_{AB}\in(\mathsf{M}_{d_{1}}\otimes\mathsf{M}_{d_{2}})^{+}
and an integer
k\geqslant 1
. We say that
\rho_{AB}
is
k
-max-extendible if there exists an operator
\sigma_{AB_{1}\cdots B_{k}}\in\mathsf{M}_{d_{1}}^{+}{\otimes_{\max}}\left(\mathsf{M}_{d_{2}}^{+}\right)^{{\otimes_{\max}}k}
such that

\rho_{AB}=\left(\mathrm{Id}_{\displaystyle\mathsf{M}^{\mathrm{sa}}_{d_{1}}}\otimes\gamma^{\Tr}_{k}\right)\left(\sigma_{AB_{1}\cdots B_{k}}\right).

If moreover the operator
\sigma_{AB_{1}\cdots B_{k}}
can be chosen to be positive semidefinite, we say that
\rho_{AB}
is
k
-PSD-extendible. An operator which is
k
-PSD-extendible is also
k
-max-extendible, and the following proposition shows that the converse is false.

Proposition 5

(Proved in Appendix
5
)
.

There is an operator
\rho_{AB}\in(\mathsf{M}_{3}\otimes\mathsf{M}_{3})^{+}
which is
2
-max-extendible but not
2
-PSD-extendible.

The usual monogamy theorem
[
6
,
16
]
states that an operator which is
k
-PSD-extendible for every integer
k\geqslant 1
is separable; the stronger version given by Theorem
1
is that an operator which is
k
-max-extendible for every
k\geqslant 1
is separable.

Finally, let us mention the following consequence of Theorem
4
. To our knowledge, this result does not appear in the quantum information literature.

Corollary 6
.

Let
\rho_{AB}\in(\mathsf{M}_{d_{1}}\otimes\mathsf{M}_{d_{2}})^{+}
be of full rank. Then there exists an integer
k\geqslant 1
such that the operator on
\mathsf{M}_{d_{1}}\otimes\mathsf{M}_{d_{2}}^{\otimes k}
defined as

\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{AB_{i}}=\left(\mathrm{Id}_{\displaystyle\mathsf{M}_{d_{1}}^{\mathrm{sa}}}\otimes P_{\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(\mathsf{M}_{d_{2}}^{\mathrm{sa}})}\right)\left(\rho_{AB}\otimes\mathbf{1}^{\otimes(k-1)}_{d_{2}}\right)

is fully separable, i.e., belongs to
\mathsf{M}_{d_{1}}^{+}{\otimes_{\min}}\left(\mathsf{M}_{d_{2}}^{+}\right)^{{\otimes_{\min}}k}
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1

One direction is easy: if
x\in\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\min}}\mathsf{C}_{B}
, we may write
x=\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}
with
a_{i}\in\mathsf{C}_{A}
and
b_{i}\in\mathsf{C}_{B}
. We may assume by rescaling
a_{i}
that

\phi(b_{i})=1
. The formula
y_{k}=\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}^{\otimes k}
shows that
x
is
k
-extendible for every integer
k
.

The other direction of the proof relies on a specific choice of extensions. We say that
(y_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
is a
compatible sequence
(for
(\mathsf{C}_{A},\mathsf{C}_{B},\phi)
) if the following conditions hold:

(i)

We have
y_{0}\in\mathsf{C}_{A}\setminus\{0\}
;

(ii)

For every
k\geqslant 1
, we have
y_{k}\in\Big(V_{A}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}_{k}(V_{B})\Big)\cap\left(\mathsf{C}_{A}{\otimes_{\max}}\mathsf{C}_{B}^{{\otimes_{\max}}k}\right)
;

(iii)

For every
k\geqslant 1
, we have

y_{k-1}=\left(\mathrm{Id}_{V_{A}}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{V_{B}}^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes\phi\right)(y_{k}).

Iterating the last property shows that whenever
(y_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
is a compatible sequence, then
y_{k}
is a
k
-extension of
y_{1}
for every
k\geqslant 1
.

Our proof strategy is to first show in Lemma
7
that for every
x\in\mathsf{Ext}_{\infty}(\mathsf{C}_{A},\mathsf{C}_{B},\phi)
the extensions can be chosen such that they form a compatible sequence. Then we use a representation of compatible sequences given by Proposition
8
to finish the proof.

Lemma 7
.

Let
x\in\mathsf{Ext}_{\infty}(\mathsf{C}_{A},\mathsf{C}_{B},\phi)
. Then there exists a compatible sequence
(y_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
such that
y_{1}=x
.

Proof.

We necessarily have
y_{1}=x
and
y_{0}=(\mathrm{Id}_{V_{A}}\otimes\phi)(x)
. For every
k\geqslant 2
, let
x_{k}
be an arbitrary
k
-extension of
x
. The compatibility can be enforced by a compactness argument. For
k\leqslant n

Proof of Theorem

Before proving Theorem
3
, we recall some facts about polytopes. Given a polytope
P
and a vertex

, we denote by
P/v
the
vertex figure
of
P
at
v
(see
[
8
, Chapter 2.1]
for definition). A
d
-polytope
P
is said to be
simple
if any vertex is contained in exactly
d
facets, or equivalently if all its vertex figures are
(d-1)
-simplices. A polytope
P
is said to be
2
-
level
if for every facet
F\in\mathcal{F}(P)
, all vertices not in
F
lie in the same translate of
\aff(F)
. Since the complement of any facet in the graph of
P
is connected
[
14
, p. 475]
,
P
is
2

F\in\mathcal{F}(P)
lies in a translate of
\aff(F)
.

We use the following characterization of products of simplices. For characterizations of products of simplices up to combinatorial equivalence, see
[
17
]
.

Theorem 13

(Kaibel–Wolff,
[
10
]
)
.

A polytope is affinely equivalent to a product of simplices if and only if it is simple and
2
-level.

A polytope
P\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}
is said to be a
0/1-polytope
if its vertices are a subset of
\{0,1\}^{n}
. Kaibel and Wolff proved in
[
10
]
that a simple 0/1-polytope is a product of simplices. Since any
2
-level polytope is affinely equivalent to a 0/1-polytope (see
[
7
]
), this implies Theorem
13
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

The fact that a simplex satisfies (ii) is a consequence of the following observation: if
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
are affinely independent, then for every
J_{1}
,
J_{2}\subseteq I
, we have

\aff(\{x_{i}\ :\ i\in J_{1}\cap J_{2}\})=\aff(\{x_{i}\ :\ i\in J_{1}\})\cap\aff(\{x_{i}\ :\ i\in J_{2}\}).

By induction, to prove (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii), it suffices to prove that the class of polytopes satisfying (ii) is closed under products. Let
P
and
Q
be polytopes satisfying (ii) and consider a family
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
of faces of
P\times Q
. There are faces
(G_{i})_{i\in I}
of
P
and
(H_{i})_{i\in I}
of
Q
such that
F_{i}=G_{i}\times H_{i}
for every
i\in I
. Using the relation
\aff(A\times B)=\aff(A)\times\aff(B)
whenever
A
,
B
are subsets of vector spaces, we obtain

\aff\left(\bigcap_{i\in I}F_{i}\right)=\aff\left(\bigcap_{i\in I}G_{i}\times\bigcap_{i\in I}H_{i}\right)=\aff\left(\bigcap_{i\in I}G_{i}\right)\times\left(\bigcap_{i\in I}H_{i}\right)\\
=\left(\bigcap_{i\in I}\aff(G_{i})\right)\times\left(\bigcap_{i\in I}\aff(H_{i})\right)=\bigcap_{i\in I}\aff(G_{i})\times\aff(H_{i})=\bigcap_{i\in I}\aff(F_{i})

and the implication (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii) follows.

Conversely, let
P
be an
n
-polytope satisfying (ii). We first show that
P
is simple. Consider a vertex
x\in\mathcal{V}(P)
and facets
F_{1},\dots,F_{n-1}\in\mathcal{F}(P)
containing
x
. The set
\bigcap\aff(F_{i})
is the nonempty intersection of
n-1
affine hyperplanes, hence has dimension
\geqslant 1
. By (
2
), the face
\bigcap F_{i}
has dimension
\geqslant 1
. By the 1-1 correspondence between faces of
P
containing
x
and faces of
P/x
(see
[
8
, Proposition 2.4]
), the
(n-1)
-polytope
P/x
is dual-
(n-1)
-neighbourly, i.e., any
n-1
facets have a common point. Since a
d
-polytope which is dual-
k
-neighbourly for
k>\lfloor d/2\rfloor
is a simplex (see
[
8
, p. 123]
), it follows that
P/x
is a simplex for every
x\in\mathcal{V}(P)
and therefore
P
is simple.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}(P)
and
G
be a face of
P
such that
F\cap G=\emptyset
. By (
2
), we have
\aff(F)\cap\aff(G)=\emptyset
and therefore
G
lies in a translate of
\aff(F)
. This means that
P
is
2
-level. By Theorem
13
, it follows that
P
is affinely equivalent to a product of simplices.
∎

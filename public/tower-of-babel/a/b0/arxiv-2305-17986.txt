On the real spectrum of differential operators with PT-symmetric periodic matrix coefficients
O. A. Veliev

Abstract
We study the spectrum of the differential operator T T generated by the differential expression of order n > 2 n>2 with the m × m m\times m PT-symmetric periodic matrix coefficients. The case when m m and n n are the odd numbers was investigated in [8]. In this paper, we consider the all remained cases: (a) n n is an odd number and m m is an even number, (b) n n is an even number and m m is an arbitrary positive integer. We find conditions on the coefficients under which in the cases (a) and (b) the spectrum of T T contains the sets ( − ∞ , H ] (-\infty,H] ∪ [ H , ∞ ) \cup[H,\infty) and [ H , ∞ ) [H,\infty) respectively for some H > 0 . H>0. Key Words: Non-self-adjoint differential operator, PT-symmetric periodic matrix coefficients. Real spectrum. AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 34L05, 34L20.

Introduction and Preliminary Facts

In this paper, we consider the spectrum
\sigma(T)
of the differential
operator
T
generated in the space
L_{2}^{m}(-\infty,\infty)
by the
differential expression

(-i)^{n}y^{(n)}+(-i)^{n-2}P_{2}y^{(n-2)}+(-i)^{n-3}P_{3}y^{(n-3)}+...+P_{n}y,

(1)

where
n>2,

P_{k}=(p_{k,i,j})
for
k=2,3,...,n
are the
m\times m

matrices with the complex-valued PT-symmetric periodic entries

p_{k,i,j}\left(x+1\right)=p_{k,i,j}\left(x\right),\text{ }p_{k,i,j}\left(-x\right)=\overline{p_{k,i,j}\left(x\right)},\text{
}p_{k,i,j}\in L_{2}[0,1]

(2)

and
y=(y_{1},y_{2},...,y_{m})^{T}
is a vector-valued function. Here

L_{2}^{m}(a,b)
for
-\infty\leq a<b\leq\infty
is the space of the
vector-valued functions
f=\left(f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{m}\right)^{T}
with
the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{(a,b)}
and inner product

(\cdot,\cdot)_{(a,b)}
defined by

\left\|f\right\|_{(a,b)}^{2}=\int_{a}^{b}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{2}dx,\text{ }(f,g)_{(a,b)}=\int_{a}^{b}\left\langle f\left(x\right),g\left(x\right)\right\rangle dx,

where
\left|\cdot\right|
and
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
are the norm and inner product in
\mathbb{C}^{m}.

It is well known that (see for example [3, 5]) the spectrum
\sigma(T)
of
T

is the union of the spectra
\sigma(T_{t})
of the operators
T_{t}
for

t\in[0,2\pi)
generated in
L_{2}^{m}[0,1]
by (1) and the boundary
conditions

y^{(\mathbb{\nu})}\left(1\right)=e^{it}y^{(\mathbb{\nu})}\left(0\right),\text{ }\mathbb{\nu}=0,1,...,(n-1).

(3)

The spectrum of
T_{t}
consists of the eigenvalues. These eigenvalues are
known as Bloch eigenvalues of
T
and are the roots of the characteristic
equation

\Delta(\lambda,t):=\det(Y_{j}^{(\nu-1)}(1,\lambda)-e^{it}Y_{j}^{(\nu-1)}(0,\lambda))_{j,\nu=1}^{n}=

(4)

e^{inmt}+f_{1}(\lambda)e^{i(nm-1)t}+f_{2}(\lambda)e^{i(nm-2)t}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+f_{nm-1}(\lambda)e^{it}+1,

where
f_{1}(\lambda),f_{2}(\lambda),...
are the entire functions,

Y_{1}(x,\lambda),Y_{2}(x,\lambda),\ldots,Y_{n}(x,\lambda)
are the solutions
of the matrix equation

(-i)^{n}Y^{(n)}+(-i)^{n-2}P_{2}Y^{(n-2)}+(-i)^{n-3}P_{3}Y^{(n-3)}+...+P_{n}Y=\lambda Y

satisfying
Y_{k}^{(j)}(0,\lambda)=O_{m}
for
j\neq k-1
,
Y_{k}^{(k-1)}(0,\lambda)=I_{m}
. Here
O_{m}
and
I_{m}
are the
m\times m
zero
and identity matrices (see [4, Chapter 3]).

Note that there are a large number of papers for the scalar case
m=1
and

n=2
, namely for the Schrödinger operator (see the monographs [1,
Chapters 4 and 6] and [6, Chapters 3 and 5] and the papers they refer to). The
results and the method used in this paper are completely different from the
results and methods of those papers. Therefore we do not discuss the scalar

As far as I know, only the papers [7, 8] were devoted to the differential
operator with the periodic PT-symmetric matrix coefficients. In [7] the
Schrödinger operator with a PT-symmetric periodic matrix potential was
investigated, where
n=2
. In [8] we considered the following case.

Case 1

m
and
n
are the odd numbers.

We proved that in Case 1,
\sigma(T)
contains all real line
\mathbb{R}.
In

Case 2

n
is an odd number and
m
is an even number.

Case 3

n

m
is an arbitrary positive integer.

Therefore, this paper can be considered as a continuation and completion of

is
an odd number. That is why, the methods used in [8] and in this paper are

The Waring Rank of Ternary Cubic Forms
Gary Brookfield

Abstract
. We provide conditions on the coefficients of a ternary cubic form that determine its Waring rank.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 4A25, 12D99, 13B25

1.
Introduction

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
be a degree
d
form in variables
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
over the complex numbers. Then the
rank
of
f
, written
\operatorname{rank}[f]
, is the smallest natural number
r
such that
f
can be written as a sum of
r

d^{\rm th}
powers of linear forms in
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
. To distinguish this definition of rank from others in the literature,
\operatorname{rank}[f]
is often called the
Waring rank
of the form
f
.

For example, the cubic form
f_{xxx}=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}
can be written as a sum of four cubes:

(1.1)

\displaystyle f_{xxx}=\frac{1}{24}\left((x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})^{3}+(x_{1}-x_{2}-x_{3})^{3}+(x_{2}-x_{3}-x_{1})^{3}+(x_{3}-x_{1}-x_{2})^{3}\right)

There are many other ways of expressing
f_{xxx}
as a sum of four cubes, but it is not possible to express
f_{xxx}
as a sum of three cubes. Hence the rank of
f_{xxx}=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}
is four. See Section
6
for details.

The coefficient
1/24
on the cubes in the above expression for
f_{xxx}
is immaterial. Because we are working over the complex numbers, any such coefficients can be absorbed into the linear forms being cubed.

The Waring rank of forms has been studied in many special cases over the past 150 years. For example, much is known about the rank of binary forms, quadratic forms in any number of variables, completely reducible forms, monomials and binary binomials
[
4
,
9
]
. An introduction to the rank problem and its history can be found in
[
7
]
. Another general reference with an extensive bibliography is
[
2
]
.

For ternary cubic forms, the state of the art is represented by the table of the ranks of ternary cubic forms having particular normal forms found in
[
8
, Table 1]
. See also
[
3
]
. But this situation is not very satisfactory in the sense that it is saying that if a cubic ternary form can be written in a certain way - one of eleven different normal forms - then it can also be written in another way as a sum of cubes. The purpose of this article, is to show how the rank of a ternary cubic form can be determined directly from its coefficients.

It is fairly clear that the rank of a form is unchanged by a linear change of the variables. For example, if
f_{xxx}=a_{x}b_{x}c_{x}
with
\{a_{x},b_{x},c_{x}\}
linearly independent, then the linear transformation
x_{1}\mapsto a_{x}
,
x_{2}\mapsto b_{x}
,
x_{3}\mapsto c_{x}
sends
x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}
to
f_{xxx}
. Applying this transformation to both sides of (
1.1
) we see that
f_{xxx}
is a sum of four cubes. See (
6.2
). Since the linear transformation is invertible we get
\operatorname{rank}[f_{xxx}]=\operatorname{rank}[x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}]=4
.

So, in general, the rank of a form is an invariant of the form—it is unchanged by invertible linear transformations of its variables. Then it should be no surprise that the rank of a form depends on its concomitants (invariants, covariants, contravariants).

The simplest example of this is the generic case: If the Aronhold invariant
S
is nonzero for a ternary cubic form
f_{xxx}
, then
\operatorname{rank}[f_{xxx}]=4
(Lemma
5.18
).
This, and the other main results of this article, are summarized in the table in Section
5
.

2.
Preliminaries

All of the definitions and notation used in this article come from
Completely Reducible Cubic Ternary Forms

[
1
]
. All forms have coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
, the field of complex numbers, and all are ternary, that is, they are homogeneous polynomials in three variables
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
. We write
f_{x}
,
f_{xx}
and
f_{xxx}
for linear, quadratic and cubic forms in these variables. For a form of unspecified degree, we drop the subscripts. The
n^{\text{th}}
transvectant of forms
f
,
g
and
h
is defined in
[
1
, Section 4]
and is written
\mathcal{J}_{n\,}[f,g,h]
. When nonzero,
\mathcal{J}_{n\,}[f,g,h]
is a form of degree
\deg f+\deg g+\deg h-3n
in
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
. The first transvectant is just the Jacobian, which we write as
\mathcal{J}_{\,}[f,g,h]
. Frequently we will use “contravariant” variables
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}
, which geometrically, are the variable coordinates of the line
u_{x}=u_{1}x_{1}+u_{2}x_{2}+u_{3}x_{3}
.

For forms
a_{x}=a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+a_{3}x_{3}
,
b_{x}=b_{1}x_{1}+b_{2}x_{2}+b_{3}x_{3}
and
c_{x}=c_{1}x_{1}+c_{2}x_{2}+c_{3}x_{3}
,
\mathcal{J}_{\,}[a_{x},b_{x},c_{x}]
is just the determinant of the coefficients of these forms. For compactness, we abbreviate this as
[abc]
:

[abc]=\mathcal{J}_{\,}[a_{x},b_{x},c_{x}]=\begin{vmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&a_{3}\\
b_{1}&b_{2}&b_{3}\\
c_{1}&c_{2}&c_{3}\end{vmatrix}

So
\{a_{x},b_{x},c_{x}\}
is linearly dependent if and only if
[abc]=0
. A pair of forms
\{a_{x},b_{x}\}
is linearly dependent if and only if
[abu]=0
. Since
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}
are variables,
[abu]
is a linear form in
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}
with three coefficients that are functions of the coefficients of
a_{x}
and
b_{x}
. Thus
\{a_{x},b_{x}\}
is linearly dependent if and only if all three of these coefficients are zero.

We also need the contraction operator
\,\mathcal{C}_{ux}
defined by

\,\mathcal{C}_{ux}[f]=\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{1}\partial u_{1}}+\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{2}\partial u_{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{3}\partial u_{3}}

for all forms
f
in
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
and
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}
. We use the notation
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\sigma}
for substitutions as explained in
[
1
]
. For example, we write
S
for the Aronhold invariant of the cubic form
f_{xxx}
. The Aronhold invariant of another cubic form
g_{xxx}
could be written as
\|S\|_{f\mapsto g}
where
f\mapsto g
denotes the substitution of the coefficients of
f_{xxx}
by the coefficents of
g_{xxx}
.

Somewhat imprecisely, we will say that the forms that can be constructed from a given form
f
and
u_{x}
using transvectants, contractions and substitutions are
concomitants
of
f
. For the precise definition and for its importance to the theory of invariants, see Grace and Young,
The Algebra of Invariants

[
6
]
.

3.
The Rank of Quadratic Forms

The solution of rank problem for ternary quadratic forms is well known, but it is nonetheless worthwhile recording how the rank of such a form
f_{xx}
is determined by the two concomitants
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},u_{x}^{2}]=0
and
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},f_{xx}]=0
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f_{xx}
be a quadratic form.

(1)

\operatorname{rank}[f_{xx}]=1
if and only if
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},u_{x}^{2}]=0
and
f_{xx}\neq 0
.

(2)

\operatorname{rank}[f_{xx}]=2
if and only if
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},f_{xx}]=0
and
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},u_{x}^{2}]\neq 0
.

(3)

\operatorname{rank}[f_{xx}]=3
if and only if
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},f_{xx}]\neq 0
.

Proof.

This is just Lemma 6.1 of
[
1
]
expressed in terms of rank.
∎

If
\operatorname{rank}[f_{xx}]\leq 2
, then
f_{xx}
is reducible because

f_{xx}=a_{x}^{2}+b_{x}^{2}=(a_{x}+ib_{x})(a_{x}-ib_{x}).

The converse is true since, if
f_{xx}=a_{x}b_{x}
, then
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},f_{xx}]=0

[
1
, Lemma 6.2]
, and, in fact,
f_{xx}
can be written as a sum of two cubes in many ways:

f_{xx}=\frac{1}{4a_{0}b_{0}}\left((a_{0}a_{x}+b_{0}b_{x})^{2}-(a_{0}a_{x}-b_{0}b_{x})^{2}\right)

where
a_{0}b_{0}\neq 0
. Therefore we have the following lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

For a quadratic form
f_{xx}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},f_{xx}]=0

(2)

\operatorname{rank}[f_{xx}]\leq 2

(3)

f_{xx}
is reducible

We also note some properties of the joint concomitants of
f_{xx}
and a linear form
a_{x}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f_{xx}
and
a_{x}
be nonzero forms.

(1)

\mathcal{J}_{\,}[f_{xx},a_{x},u_{x}]=0
if and only if
a_{x}^{2}
divides
f_{xx}
.

(2)

\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},a_{x}^{2},u_{x}^{2}]=0
if and only if
a_{x}
divides
f_{xx}
.

Proof.

See Lemmas 5.2(B1) and 5.2(B2) of
[
1
]
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
f_{xx}
is irreducible and
a_{x}
is nonzero.

(1)

\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},a_{x}^{2}]=0
if and only if
f_{xx}=a_{x}c_{x}+b_{x}^{2}
for some forms
b_{x}
and
c_{x}
such that
\{a_{x},b_{x},c_{x}\}
is linearly independent.

(2)

\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},a_{x}^{2}]\neq 0
if and only if
f_{xx}=a_{0}a_{x}^{2}+b_{x}c_{x}
for some forms
b_{x}
and
c_{x}
such that
\{a_{x},b_{x},c_{x}\}
is linearly independent, and some nonzero
a_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

(1)

If
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},a_{x}^{2}]=0
, then, from
[
1
, Lemma 6.4]
,
f_{xx}=a_{x}c_{x}+b_{x}^{2}
for some forms
b_{x}
and
c_{x}
. In this circumstance,
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},f_{xx}]=-12[abc]^{2}
, so
f_{xx}
is irreducible if and only if
[abc]\neq 0
, if and only if
\{a_{x},b_{x},c_{x}\}
is linearly dependent.

(2)

Let
g_{xx}=f_{xx}-a_{0}a_{x}^{2}
with
a_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
to be determined. From the identity

\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[g_{xx},g_{xx},g_{xx}]=\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},f_{xx}]-3a_{0}\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},a_{x}^{2}],

we see that
a_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
can be chosen so that
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[g_{xx},g_{xx},g_{xx}]=0
. By Lemma
3.2
,
g_{xx}=b_{x}c_{x}
for some forms
b_{x}
and
c_{x}
and so
f_{xx}=a_{0}a_{x}^{2}+b_{x}c_{x}
. In this circumstance,
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},f_{xx}]=-12a_{0}[abc]^{2}
, so
f_{xx}
is irreducible if and only if
a_{0}[abc]\neq 0
, if and only if
\{a_{x},b_{x},c_{x}\}
is linearly dependent and
a_{0}\neq 0
.
∎

The two conditions in this lemma have an important geometric interpretation. When
\{a_{x},b_{x},c_{x}\}
is independent, the lines
a_{x}=0
,
b_{x}=0
and
c_{x}=0
form a triangle with three distinct vertices. If
f_{xx}=a_{x}c_{x}+b_{x}^{2}
, then the line
a_{x}=0
and the conic
f_{xx}=0
intersect at a single point, namely, the intersection of
a_{x}
and
b_{x}
. Hence
a_{x}=0
is tangent to
f_{xx}=0
at that point.

In the other case,
f_{xx}=a_{0}a_{x}^{2}+b_{x}c_{x}
, the line
a_{x}=0
and the conic
f_{xx}=0
intersect at two distinct points, namely, the intersection of
a_{x}
and
b_{x}
, and the intersection of
a_{x}
and
c_{x}
. Hence
a_{x}=0
is a secant line of
f_{xx}=0
.

Thus we have the following geometric lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
f_{xx}
is irreducible and
a_{x}
is nonzero.

(1)

\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},a_{x}^{2}]=0
if and only if
a_{x}=0
is a tangent line of
f_{xx}=0
.

(2)

\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},a_{x}^{2}]\neq 0
if and only if
a_{x}=0
is a secant line of
f_{xx}=0
.

Note the difference between
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},u_{x}^{2}]=0
and
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},a_{x}^{2}]=0
in Lemmas
3.1
(1) and
3.4
(1). In the first case, the coefficients of
u_{x}
are variables, so
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},u_{x}^{2}]
is a quadratic form in
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}
with six coefficients. The assumption that
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},u_{x}^{2}]=0
means that all six coefficients are zero. In contrast, the coefficients of
a_{x}
are fixed, so
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},a_{x}^{2}]
is a single complex number. Hence
\mathcal{J}_{2\,}[f_{xx},f_{xx},a_{x}^{2}]=0
is much weaker condition.

7.
Conic and Secant Line

Suppose that

is reducible but not completely reducible, that is,
f_{xxx}=a_{x}b_{xx}
where
b_{xx}
is an irreducible quadratic form and
a_{x}
is a linear form. Geometrically, the curve

b_{xx}=0
and the line

. We know already that, if the line
a_{x}=0
is tangent to the conic

\operatorname{rank}[f_{xxx}]=5
.

In this section, we consider the remaining case where
a_{x}=0
is a secant line of the conic

3.4

. Because

S
is nonzero and by Lemma
5.18
,
\operatorname{rank}[f_{xxx}]=4
.

To express

as a sum of four cubes, following Method

, we look for a form

and

such that

. Since

we get

S_{uuu}=a_{0}b_{0}c_{0}[abc]^{4}\qquad{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\hbox to0.0pt{$F$\hss}\mbox{$F$}}_{6u}=b_{0}^{2}c_{0}^{2}(a_{0}^{2}-12b_{0}c_{0})[abc]^{14}

and

a_{0}
,
b_{0}
,
c_{0}
and

are all nonzero. We set
u_{0}=1/(24a_{0}b_{0}c_{0})
so that

, and then

g_{xxx}=f_{xxx}-u_{0}u_{x}^{3}=f_{xxx}-\frac{1}{24a_{0}b_{0}c_{0}}(a_{0}a_{x}+b_{0}b_{x}+c_{0}c_{x})^{3}

Then

and
8a_{0}^{2}\|T\|_{f\mapsto g}=-[abc]^{6}(a_{0}^{2}-12b_{0}c_{0})

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{xxx}\|_{f\mapsto g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8a_{0}b_{0}c_{0}}[abc]^{2}(a_{0}a_{x}-b_{0}b_{x}-c_{0}c_{x})\left((a_{0}^{2}-12b_{0}c_{0})a_{x}^{2}-(b_{0}b_{x}-c_{0}c_{x})^{2}\right)

is a linear combination of the cubes of the three linear factors of

. By matching coefficients we can express

as a sum of three cubes and then
f_{xxx}
as a sum of four cubes. The final result for

is

\displaystyle 24a_{0}b_{0}c_{0}\sigma f_{xxx}

\displaystyle=\sigma(a_{0}a_{x}+b_{0}b_{x}+c_{0}c_{x})^{3}+\sigma(a_{0}a_{x}-b_{0}b_{x}-c_{0}c_{x})^{3}

\displaystyle\qquad-a_{0}(\sigma a_{x}-(b_{0}b_{x}-c_{0}c_{x}))^{3}-a_{0}(\sigma a_{x}+(b_{0}b_{x}-c_{0}c_{x}))^{3}

This formula gives infinitely many ways of expressing

as a sum of four cubes. It holds so long as

and
\sigma^{2}=a_{0}^{2}-12b_{0}c_{0}
. If only one way is needed, we can avoid square roots by setting

,
b_{0}=c_{0}=1
and

to get

96f_{xxx}=(4a_{x}+b_{x}+c_{x})^{3}+(4a_{x}-b_{x}-c_{x})^{3}-2(2a_{x}-b_{x}+c_{x})^{3}-2(2a_{x}+b_{x}-c_{x})^{3}

8.
Hesse Normal Form

Suppose that

for some linearly independent forms
\{a_{x},b_{x},c_{x}\}
and

Then

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=t(t^{3}-216s^{3})\,[abc]^{4}=t(t-6s)(t^{2}+6st+36s^{2})[abc]^{4}

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=(t^{6}+54_{0}s^{3}t^{3}-5832s^{6})\,[abc]^{6}

Since
[abc]\neq 0

With (

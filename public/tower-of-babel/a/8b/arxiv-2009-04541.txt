Variational truncations of singular integrals on spaces of homogeneous type
Pavel Zorin-Kranich

Abstract
. We prove sharp weighted estimates for r r -variations of averages and truncated singular integrals on suitable spaces of homogeneous type, including homogeneous nilpotent Lie groups.



1.
Introduction

Throughout the article
(X,\rho,\mu)
denotes an (Ahlfors–David)
D
-regular space of homogeneous type (definitions of these and other terms are given in Section

Variational estimates for the averaging operators

A comprehensive theory covering the full range of

Some of their estimates have been extended to weighted

Sparse domination has been developed in

In a short period of time since 2015 this idea has been applied in many settings which go beyond CZ theory, and we are not going to survey these developments.
In the CZ setting it is by now well understood that sparse domination follows from suitable localized non-tangentional endpoint estimates; several abstract results formalizing this principle appeared in

In this article we extend these

In this setting we also obtain some sharp weighted inequalities for square functions and

2.
Notation and tools

2.1.
Spaces of homogeneous type

Definition 2.1
.

A
quasi-metric
on a set
X
is a function
\rho:X\times X\to[0,\infty)
such that
\rho(x,y)=0\iff x=y
that is symmetric and satisfies the quasi-triangle inequality

(2.2)

\rho(x,y)\leq A_{0}(\rho(x,z)+\rho(z,y))\quad\text{for all}\quad x,y,z\in X

with some
A_{0}<\infty
independent of
x,y,z
.

A measure
\mu
on a quasi-metric space
(X,\rho)
is called
doubling
if there exists
A_{1}<\infty
such that

(2.3)

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq A_{1}\mu(B(x,r))\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in X,r>0,

A tuple
(X,\rho,\mu)
consisting of a set
X
, a quasi-metric
\rho
, and a doubling measure
\mu
is called a
space of homogeneous type
.

A space of homogeneous type
(X,\rho,\mu)
is called (Ahlfors–David)
D
-regular
,
D>0
, if there exist
0<c,C<\infty
such that for all
x\in X
and
r>0
we have

cr^{D}\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{D}.

A
D
-regular space necessarily has no atoms.
We say that a family
\mathcal{D}
of subsets of
X
has the
small boundary property
if there exist
\eta>0
and
C_{3}<\infty
such that for every
Q\in\mathcal{D}
and every
0<\tau\leq 1

(2.4)

\mu(\partial_{\tau\mathrm{diam}(Q)}Q)\leq C_{3}\tau^{\eta}\mu(Q),

where

(2.5)

\partial_{\tau}(Q)=\{x\in Q:\mathrm{dist}(x,X\setminus Q)\leq\tau\}\cup\{x\in X\setminus Q:\mathrm{dist}(x,Q)\leq\tau\}.

We say that
(X,\rho,\mu)
has the small boundary property if the collection of all metric balls has the small boundary property.

We denote the measure of a set
Q
by
\lvert Q\rvert=\mu(Q)
and the average of a function
f
over
Q
by
\left\langle f\right\rangle_{Q}=\lvert Q\rvert^{-1}\int_{Q}f\mathrm{d}\mu
.

2.2.
Dyadic cubes

Filtrations on spaces of homogeneous type that closely resemble dyadic filtrations on
\mathbb{R}^{D}
have been first constructed by Christ
[
Chr90
]
.
We recall their properties.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a space of homogeneous type.
A
system of dyadic cubes

\mathcal{D}
with constants
\kappa>1
,
a_{0}>0
,
C_{1}<\infty
consists of collections
\mathcal{D}_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, of open subsets of
X
such that
and constants
\kappa>1
,
a_{0},\eta>0
,
C_{1},C_{2}<\infty
with the following properties.

(1)

\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}\quad\mu(X\setminus\cup_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{k}}Q)=0
,

(2)

If
l\geq k
,
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{l}
,
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{k}
, then either
Q^{\prime}\subseteq Q
or
Q^{\prime}\cap Q=\emptyset
,

(3)

For every
l\geq k
and
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{k}
there exists a unique
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{l}
such that
Q\supseteq Q^{\prime}
,

(4)

\forall k\in\mathbb{Z},Q\in\mathcal{D}_{k}\quad\exists c_{Q}\in X:B(c_{Q},a_{0}\kappa^{k})\subseteq Q\subseteq B(c_{Q},C_{1}\kappa^{k})
.

Abusing the notation we write
k(Q)=k
if
Q\in\mathcal{D}_{k}
, so that each cube remembers its scale although the same cube (as a set) may appear in other
\mathcal{D}_{l}
.
We use
\mathcal{D}
to denote the disjoint union of
\mathcal{D}_{k}
.

If in addition the collection
\mathcal{D}
has the small boundary property (
2.4
), then we call
\mathcal{D}
a
Christ
system of dyadic cubes.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
Chr90
]
)
.

Every space of homogeneous type admits a system of Christ dyadic cubes.

Remark 2.8
.

It is known that for every quasimetric
\rho
there exists
0<\alpha\leq 1
and a metric
d
such that
d(x,y)\sim\rho(x,y)^{\alpha}
, see
[
MS79
]
for the first proof and
[
PS09
]
for the fact that one can choose
(2C_{\eqref{qm}})^{\alpha}=2
(a much more verbose statement of the latter result also appears in
[
Mit+13
]
).
It is easy to see that a system of (Christ) dyadic cubes with respest to
d
is also a system of (Christ) dyadic cubes with respect to
\rho
(with different constants).
However, the small boundary property of dyadic cubes seems to be substantially easier to achieve in the metric setting using the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function on
(0,\infty)
as on
[
DM00
, p. 146]
.

Throughout the article we fix a system of Christ dyadic cubes
\mathcal{D}
on
(X,\rho,\mu)
.
We denote by
\mathbb{E}_{k}
the conditional expectation operator onto the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{D}_{k}
.
The martingale difference operator is denoted by
\mathbb{D}_{k}=\mathbb{E}_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k+1}
.
The function
\mathbb{D}_{k}f
is constant on each cube of scale
k
and has integral
0
on each cube of scale
k+1
.

Definition 2.9
.

A finite family
(\mathcal{D}^{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of systems of dyadic cubes is called
adjacent
if all constants in their definitions coincide and there exists a constant
C_{3}<\infty
such that for every ball
B(x,r)\subset X
there exists
\alpha
and a cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}
such that
B(x,r)\subseteq Q\subseteq B(x,C_{3}r)
.

Theorem 2.10

(
[
HK13
]
)
.

Every space of homogeneous type admits a finite collection of adjacent systems of dyadic cubes.

Remark 2.11
.

It is also possible to construct adjacent systems of Christ dyadic cubes.

Example 2.12
.

Let
X=\mathbb{R}^{D}
with the Euclidean distance and the Lebesgue measure.
For each
\alpha\in\{0,1,2\}^{D}
the corresponding
shifted system of dyadic cubes
is given by

\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}=\{2^{-k}([0,1)^{D}+m+(-1)^{k}\frac{1}{3}\alpha),k\in\mathbb{Z},m\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\}.

Then the systems
\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\{0,1,2\}^{D}
, are adjacent.
In fact, on
\mathbb{R}^{D}
one can construct
D+1
shifted systems of dyadic cubes that are adjacent
[
Mei03
]
.

2.3.
Sparse collections and operators

Definition 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a system of dyadic cubes.
A collection
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{D}
is called

(1)

\eta
-sparse
if there exist pairwise disjoint subsets
E(Q)\subset Q\in\mathcal{S}
with
\lvert E(Q)\rvert\geq\eta\lvert Q\rvert
and

(2)

\Lambda
-Carleson
if one has
\sum_{Q^{\prime}\subset Q,Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mu(Q^{\prime})\leq\Lambda\mu(Q)
for all
Q\in\mathcal{D}
.

It is known that a collection is
\eta
-sparse if and only if it is
1/\eta
-Carleson
[
LN15
, §6.1]
.

The corresponding
sparse operator
is given by

(2.14)

A_{\mathcal{S}}f=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}\left\langle\lvert f\rvert\right\rangle_{CQ}

and
sparse square function
by

(2.15)

A_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}f=\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}\left\langle\lvert f\rvert\right\rangle_{CQ}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

The sparse operators (
2.14
) and square functions (
2.15
) can be dominated by finite linear combinations of similar sparse operators/square functions with respect to adjacent dyadic grids in which the averages of
f
are taken over
Q
instead of
CQ
, cf.
[
Ler16
, Remark 4.3]
.
Hence the usual estimates for sparse operators
[
HL18
,
DLR16
]
apply to (
2.14
) and (
2.15
).

We say that an operator
T
is pointwise controlled by a sparse operator (or square function) with constant
C<\infty
if for every function
f
there exist
1/2
-sparse collections
\mathcal{S}^{n}\subset\mathcal{D}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\lvert Tf\rvert\leq C\liminf_{n\to\infty}A_{\mathcal{S}^{n}}f\quad\text{or}\quad\lvert Tf\rvert\leq C\liminf_{n\to\infty}A_{\mathcal{S}^{n}}^{2}f,

respectively, holds pointwise almost everywhere.

We use a version of the sparse domination principle in
[
Ler16
]
.
Given a dyadic grid
\mathcal{D}
denote by
\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\subseteq}
the set of pairs
(Q^{\prime},Q)\in\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}
with
Q^{\prime}\subseteq Q
.
For a function
F:\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\subseteq}\to[0,\infty]
let

(2.16)

\mathcal{N}_{Q}F(x):=\sup_{x\in Q^{\prime}\subseteq Q}F(Q^{\prime},Q),\quad\mathcal{N}F(x):=\sup_{x\in Q^{\prime}\subseteq Q}F(Q^{\prime},Q).

In the first definition above the supremum is taken over all
Q^{\prime}
and in the second over all
Q^{\prime},Q
.
We use the convention that the supremum of an empty subset of
[0,\infty]
is
0
.

A simple stopping time argument using the doubling condition and starting at a scale
k_{0}
gives the following result.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
F:\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\subseteq}\to[0,\infty]
be a function that is monotonic in the sense that

Q^{\prime\prime\prime}\subseteq Q^{\prime\prime}\subseteq Q^{\prime}\subseteq Q\implies F(Q^{\prime\prime},Q^{\prime})\leq F(Q^{\prime\prime\prime},Q)

and
\ell^{r}
-subadditive for some
0<r<\infty
in the sense that

Q^{\prime\prime}\subseteq Q^{\prime}\subseteq Q\implies F(Q^{\prime\prime},Q)^{r}\leq F(Q^{\prime\prime},Q^{\prime})^{r}+F(Q^{\prime},Q)^{r}.

Suppose that for every dyadic cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}
we have

(2.18)

\lVert\mathcal{N}_{Q}F\rVert_{1,\infty}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}(CQ)}.

Then there exist
1/2
-sparse collections
\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}\subset\mathcal{D}
of cubes such that

(2.19)

\mathcal{N}F\lesssim C\liminf_{k_{0}\to\infty}\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}\left\langle\lvert f\rvert\right\rangle_{CQ}^{r}\Big)^{1/r}

holds pointwise almost everywhere.

2.4.
Bounded
r
-variation and jump counting

Definition 2.20
.

Let
I
be an ordered set and
(a_{t})_{t\in I}
a family of complex numbers.
The
homogeneous
r
-variation seminorm
is denoted by

\dot{V}^{r}(a_{t}:t\in I):=\sup_{t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{J}\in I}\big(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lvert a_{t_{j}}-a_{t_{j-1}}\rvert^{r}\big)^{1/r}

and the
inhomogeneous
r
-variation norm
by

V^{r}(a_{t}:t\in I):=\sup_{t\in I}\lvert a_{t}\rvert+\dot{V}^{r}(a_{t}:t\in I).

The
\lambda
-jump counting function

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(a_{t}:t\in I)
is the supremum over all
J
such that there exist
t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{J}
with
\lvert a_{t_{j}}-a_{t_{j-1}}\rvert>\lambda
for all
j=1,\dots,J
.

We refer to
[
JSW08
]
for the basic properties of the jump counting function and its relation to
r
-variations.

3.
Short variations

We will consider two types of short variation operators, both involving a non-tangentional supremum component introduced in
[
Kra13
]
.
Ergodic averages will be compared with a dyadic martingale using the short variations

(3.1)

S_{k}f(x):=\sup_{\rho(x,x^{\prime})\leq C\kappa^{k}}V^{2}(A_{t}f(x^{\prime})-\mathbb{E}_{k}f(x^{\prime}):\kappa^{k}/C\leq t\leq C\kappa^{k}).

For a CZ kernel
K
as in Theorem
1.8
(in fact we only need the cancellation condition in
y
and the size estimate, but not the smoothness condition) we define

(3.2)

S_{k}f(x):=\sup_{\rho(x,x^{\prime})\leq C\kappa^{k}}V^{2}(\int_{\kappa^{k}/C<\rho(x^{\prime},y)<t}K(x^{\prime},y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y:\kappa^{k}/C\leq t\leq C\kappa^{k}).

The non-tangentional short variation square function is defined by

(3.3)

Sf:=(\sum_{k}(S_{k}f)^{2})^{1/2}.

In the short variation we can in fact replace
2
-variation by
r
-variation for any

, see
[
arxiv:1409.7120
]
.
The following result has been essentially proved in
[
arxiv:1409.7120
]
following the arguments in
[
Jon+98
,
JRW03
]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
S_{k}
be given either by (
3.1
) or by (
3.2
).
Then the operator
S
given by (
3.3
) has weak type
(1,1)
.

As in
[
Zor17
]
, Theorem
3.4
immediately implies that
S
is pointwise controlled by a sparse square function.
Using the results from
[

]
this implies a sharp weighted refinement of the case

of
[

, Theorem 1.4]
.
Similarly as in
[

]
one can also show
\lVert Sf\rVert_{L^{2}(w)}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(Mw)}
and
\lVert Sf\rVert_{L^{1,\infty}(w)}\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}(Mw)}
.

3.1.
A reverse Hölder inequality

Lemma 3.5

(cf.
[
JRW03
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{D}
be a collection of disjoint dyadic cubes of scale
\leq k
.
For each
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
let
b^{Q}
be a scalar-valued function supported on
Q
with
\int b^{Q}=0
.
Then for every
\alpha>\frac{D-\eta}{2}
we have

(3.6)

S_{k}(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}b^{Q}(x))^{2}\lesssim(\kappa^{k})^{-2D}\sum_{Q:\mathrm{dist}(x,Q)\lesssim\kappa^{k}}(\kappa^{k}/\mathrm{diam}(Q))^{2\alpha}\lVert b^{Q}\rVert_{1}^{2}.

Proof.

Clearly only cubes with
\mathrm{dist}(x,Q)\lesssim\kappa^{k}
contribute to the left-hand side, so we may remove all other cubes from
\mathcal{Q}
.
Now the right-hand side of the conclusion becomes independent of
x
, so it suffices to estimate the variation at an arbitrary point, which we again call
x
.

We consider only the homogeneous variation, in order to get the inhomogeneous variation it suffices to additionally consider an arbitrary (but fixed)
t
, which is similar but easier.
For a suitable sequence
\kappa^{k}/C\leq t_{1}<\dots<t_{J}\leq C\kappa^{k}
we have

S_{k}(\sum_{Q}b^{Q}(x))^{2}\lesssim\sum_{j}\lvert\sum_{Q}\mathcal{B}b^{Q}(x,t_{j},t_{j+1})\rvert^{2},

where
\mathcal{B}
is either a difference between two averages or an integral over an annulus.
We decompose
\mathcal{Q}=\cup_{i\leq 0}\mathcal{Q}_{i}
according to scale:
\mathcal{Q}_{i}=\mathcal{Q}\cap\mathcal{D}_{k+i}
.
Each cube
Q
can only contribute to the
j
-th summand non-trivially in two cases:

(1)

if
Q\cap(\partial B(x,t))\neq\emptyset
for
t=t_{j}
or
t=t_{j+1}

(2)

or
Q\subset B(x,t_{j+1})\setminus B(x,t_{j})
in the case (
3.2
).

The second case can only occur for one index
j
, and the contribution of this term can be estimated even without the growing factor in (
3.6
).
In the first case, for a fixed scale
k+i
,
i\leq 0
, the small boundary property implies that there are at most
O(\kappa^{kD}\kappa^{i\eta}/\kappa^{(k+i)D})=O(\kappa^{i(\eta-D)})
cubes of this kind in
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
, call this collection
\mathcal{Q}_{i,j}
.
Splitting the collection
\mathcal{Q}
into scales and applying Hölder’s inequality we estimate

(3.7)

\sum_{j}\lvert\sum_{i\leq 0}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{i}}\mathcal{B}b^{Q}(x,t_{j},t_{j+1})\rvert^{2}\\

Lemma 3.8

4.
Averages

The estimate for the jump counting function in Theorem
1.3
will follow from Lemma
2.17
and the next result.

Proposition 4.1
.

For every
f\in L^{1}(X)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
, every
\lambda>0
and
k_{\max}<\infty
there exists a subadditive function
F_{\lambda}
on
\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\subseteq}
such that

\lambda\sqrt{\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(A_{t}f(x):\kappa^{k(Q^{\prime})}\leq t\leq\kappa^{k(Q)})}\leq F_{\lambda}(Q^{\prime},Q),\quad x\in Q^{\prime}\subseteq Q,k(Q)\leq k_{\max},

and

\mu\{\mathcal{N}_{Q}F_{\lambda}>\nu\}\leq C\nu^{-1}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}(CQ)}\quad\text{for all}\quad Q\in\mathcal{D},\nu>0.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>0
.
Note first that, as in
[
JSW08
, Lemma 1.3]
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda\sqrt{\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(A_{t}f(x):\kappa^{k(Q^{\prime})}\leq t\leq\kappa^{k(Q)})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{k=k(Q^{\prime})}^{k(Q)-1}\sup_{x^{\prime}\in Q_{k}(x)}S_{k}f(x^{\prime})^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+\lambda\sqrt{\mathcal{J}_{\lambda/2}(\mathbb{E}_{k}f(x):k(Q^{\prime})\leq k\leq k(Q))}.

The first term on the right-hand side is a subadditive function
F
of the order interval
[Q^{\prime},Q]
, and its contribution is estimated by Theorem
3.4
.

In order to estimate the second term we follow the proof of Lépingle’s inequality for martingales.
We construct the greedy stopping times with jump size
\lambda/8
starting with
l_{0}(x)\equiv k_{\max}
: given
l_{j}(x)
let
l_{j+1}(x)<l_{j}(x)
be the largest number with
\lvert(\mathbb{E}_{l_{j+1}}-\mathbb{E}_{l_{j}})f(x)\rvert>\lambda/8
(or
-\infty
if no such number exists).
Then

\lambda\sqrt{\mathcal{J}_{\lambda/2}(\mathbb{E}_{k}f(x):k_{0}\leq k\leq k_{1})}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{j}\lvert(\mathbb{E}_{t_{j+1}(x)}-\mathbb{E}_{t_{j}(x)})(\mathbb{E}_{k_{0}}-\mathbb{E}_{k_{1}})f(x)\rvert^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Let
F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(Q^{\prime},Q)
denote the right-hand side when
x\in Q^{\prime}\subset Q
,
k(Q^{\prime})=k_{0}
, and
k(Q)=k_{1}
.
Then
F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(Q^{\prime},Q)
is clearly a subadditive function of the order interval
[Q^{\prime},Q]
.
Moreover,

\mathcal{N}F\lesssim\Big(\sum_{j}\lvert M_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbb{E}_{t_{j+1}}-\mathbb{E}_{t_{j}})f\rvert^{2}\Big)^{1/2},

where
M_{\mathcal{D}}
is the dyadic maximal operator.
Hence the operator
f\mapsto\mathcal{N}F_{\lambda}^{\prime}
has strong type
(2,2)
by orthogonality of the operators
\mathbb{E}_{t_{j+1}}-\mathbb{E}_{t_{j}}
and the martingale maximal inequality.
Standard CZ theory can be applied to show that it also has weak type
(1,1)
because the filtration
\mathcal{D}
is doubling.
We obtain the claim with
F_{\lambda}=F+F_{\lambda}^{\prime}
.
∎

In order to pass to
r
-variations we need the fact that the
p
-th power of the

quasimetric is equivalent to a metric for

.

Lemma 4.2

(cf.
[
SW69
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Let
0<p<1
.
Then

\lVert\sum_{j}g_{j}\rVert_{p,\infty}^{p}\leq 2^{p}\Big(1+\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)\sum_{j}\lVert g_{j}\rVert_{p,\infty}^{p}.

Proof.

It suffices to consider
g_{j}\geq 0
.
Moreover, by homogeneity we may assume

\sum_{j}c_{j}=1,\quad c_{j}=\lVert g_{j}\rVert_{p,\infty}^{p}.

With this normalization we have to show

\lvert\{\sum_{j}g_{j}>\lambda\}\rvert\leq 2^{p}\Big(1+\frac{1}{1-p}\Big)\lambda^{-p}.

To this end decompose
g_{j}=l_{j}+m_{j}+u_{j}
, where
u_{j}=g_{j}\mathbf{1}_{g_{j}>\lambda/2}
and
l_{j}=\mathbf{1}_{g_{j}\leq\lambda/2}\min(g_{j},c_{j}\lambda/2)
.
Then

\sum_{j}\lvert\mathrm{supp}u_{j}\rvert=\sum_{j}\lvert\{g_{j}>\lambda/2\}\rvert\leq\sum_{j}c_{j}(\lambda/2)^{-p}=(\lambda/2)^{-p}

and

\sum_{j}l_{j}\leq\sum_{j}c_{j}\lambda/2\leq\lambda/2.

Hence it remains to estimate

\lvert\{\sum_{j}m_{j}>\lambda/2\}\rvert\leq(\lambda/2)^{-1}\int\sum_{j}m_{j}\leq(\lambda/2)^{-1}\sum_{j}\int(g_{j}-c_{j}\lambda/2)\mathbf{1}_{c_{j}\lambda/2\leq g_{j}\leq\lambda/2}\\
=(\lambda/2)^{-1}\sum_{j}\int_{t=c_{j}\lambda/2}^{\lambda/2}\lvert\{g_{j}>t\}\rvert\mathrm{d}t\leq(\lambda/2)^{-1}\sum_{j}\int_{t=c_{j}\lambda/2}^{\lambda/2}c_{j}t^{-p}\mathrm{d}t\\
\leq(1-p)^{-1}(\lambda/2)^{-1}\sum_{j}c_{j}(\lambda/2)^{1-p}\leq\frac{2^{p}}{1-p}\lambda^{-p}.\qed

Corollary 4.3
.

There exists
C<\infty
such that for all

we have

\mu\{\mathcal{N}_{Q}F>\nu\}\leq C\frac{r}{r-2}\nu^{-1}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}(CQ)}\quad\text{for all}\quad Q\in\mathcal{D},

where

F(Q^{\prime},Q)=\sup_{x^{\prime}\in Q^{\prime}}\dot{V}^{r}(A_{t}f(x^{\prime}):\kappa^{k(Q^{\prime})}\leq t\leq\kappa^{k(Q)}).

Corollary
4.3
and Lemma
2.17
imply the estimate for

-variations in Theorem
1.3
.

Proof.

By homogeneity we may normalize
\nu=1
.
Let
F_{\lambda}
be the functions from Proposition
4.1
.

Suppose
F(Q,Q^{\prime})>1
.
Then either
F_{1}(Q,Q^{\prime})>1
or all jumps involved in the definition of

are bounded by

In the latter case

so that in either case we obtain

Hence, using Lemma

\displaystyle\mu\{\mathcal{N}_{Q}F>1\}

\displaystyle\leq\mu\{\mathcal{N}_{Q}\big(\sum_{l\geq 0}2^{-l(r-2)}F_{2^{-l}}^{2}\big)\gtrsim 1\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\sum_{l\geq 0}2^{-l(r-2)}(\mathcal{N}_{Q}F_{2^{-l}})^{2}\rVert_{1/2,\infty}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{l\geq 0}\lVert 2^{-l(r-2)}(\mathcal{N}_{Q}F_{2^{-l}})^{2}\rVert_{1/2,\infty}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}(CQ)}\sum_{l\geq 0}2^{-l(r-2)/2}

5.
Singular integrals

In this section we prove Theorem
1.8
.
Let
\psi
be a smooth function supported on the interval
[1/\kappa,\kappa]
with
\sum_{k}\psi(\kappa^{-k}x)=1
for all
x\in(0,\infty)
.
Let

K_{k}(x,y)=K(x,y)\psi(\kappa^{-k}\rho(x,y))

be the smoothly truncated dyadic pieces of
K
of scale
k
and let
T_{k}
be the corresponding integral operators

T_{k}f(x)=\int K_{k}(x,y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y.

This coincides with the notation for truncated singular integrals (
1.2
), but there should be no confusion between integer parameters
k
and positive real parameters
t
.
The Hölder continuity hypothesis (
1.9
) implies that
K_{k}
satisfies the same smoothness condition (
1.11
) as

(up to a constant factor).
Standard calculations using the cancellation and smoothness conditions show that

(5.1)

\lVert T_{k^{\prime}}^{*}T_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(X)}+\lVert T_{k^{\prime}}T_{k}^{*}\rVert_{L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(X)}\lesssim 2^{-\epsilon\lvert k-k^{\prime}\rvert}.

Hence by the Cotlar–Stein lemma
[
Ste93
, p. 280]
the operator
T=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}T_{k}
is bounded on
L^{2}(X)
.

5.1.
Short variations

Fix

and define

F(Q^{\prime},Q):=\sup_{x\in Q^{\prime}}\dot{V}^{r}(\int_{\rho(x,y)>t}K(x,y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y:\kappa^{k(Q^{\prime})}\leq t\leq\kappa^{k(Q)}).

The function
F
is clearly monotonic and subadditive in the order interval
[Q^{\prime},Q]
and sublinear in
f
.
Moreover,
F(Q^{\prime},Q)
depends only on the values of
f
on
CQ
.
Hence, in view of Lemma
2.17
, Theorem
1.8
will follow if we show that the map
f\mapsto\mathcal{N}F
has weak type
(1,1)
globally.

We split

(5.2)

\displaystyle\int_{r<\rho(x,y)}K(x,y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=k_{0}(r)}^{\infty}\int K_{k}(x,y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y

(5.3)

\displaystyle-\int_{\kappa^{k_{0}(r)-1}<\rho(x,y)<\kappa^{k_{0}(r)}}K_{k_{0}(r)}(x,y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y

(5.4)

\displaystyle+\int_{r<\rho(x,y)<\kappa^{k_{0}(r)}}K(x,y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y,

where

.
The contributions of (

) and (

3.2
), so it remains to estimate the contribution of (

).

5.2.
Strong type
(2,2)

Let

F^{\prime}(Q^{\prime},Q):=\sup_{x\in Q^{\prime}}\dot{V}^{r}(\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}T_{k}f(x):k(Q^{\prime})\leq k_{0}\leq k(Q)).

First we show that the operator
f\mapsto\mathcal{N}F^{\prime}
has strong type
(2,2)
.
Following
[
DR86
, p. 548]
we decompose

\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}T_{k}=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z},k\geq k_{0}}\mathbb{D}_{l}T_{k}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},l\geq k_{0}}\mathbb{D}_{l}T_{k}-\sum_{l\geq k_{0},k<k_{0}}\mathbb{D}_{l}T_{k}+\sum_{l<k_{0},k\geq k_{0}}\mathbb{D}_{l}T_{k}=:I+II+III.

The contribution of
I
to
\mathcal{N}F^{\prime}(x)
is bounded by

\dot{V}^{r}(\sum_{l\geq k_{0}}\mathbb{D}_{l}Tf(x):k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}).

Notice that the supremum over
x\in Q^{\prime}
disappeared because functions in the image of
\mathbb{D}_{l}
are constant on dyadic cubes of scale
\leq l
.
This operator is bounded on
L^{2}(X)
with norm
\lesssim r/(r-2)
since
T
is bounded on
L^{2}(X)
and by Lépingle’s inequality for martingales in the form
[
Bou89
, Lemma 3.3]
.

The remaining two terms will be estimated by square functions.

Estimate for
II

As in the estimate for
I
we remove the supremum over
x\in Q^{\prime}
.
Then we estimate the
r
-variation by the
\ell^{2}
norm.
Using Minkowski’s inequality for the sum over
m
and Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the sum over
k
we estimate

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert\sum_{l\geq k_{0},k<k_{0}}\mathbb{D}_{l}T_{k}f\rvert^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{k<k_{0}\leq k+m}\mathbb{D}_{k+m}T_{k}f\rvert^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\geq 1}\Big(\sum_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}m\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert\mathbf{1}_{k<k_{0}\leq k+m}\mathbb{D}_{k+m}T_{k}f\rvert^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 1}m\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert\mathbb{D}_{k+m}T_{k}f\rvert^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Hence it suffices to find an estimate for the square function on the right-hand side that decays sufficiently quickly with
m
.
By orthogonality

\lVert\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert\mathbb{D}_{k+m}T_{k}f\rvert^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\rVert_{2}=\lVert\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{D}_{k+m}T_{k}f\rVert_{2}.

For each

\lVert\mathbb{D}_{k+m}T_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(X)}=\lVert T_{k}^{*}\mathbb{D}_{k+m}\rVert_{L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(X)}\lesssim 2^{-\epsilon m}

since

is bounded by a universal constant and supported on
\partial_{C\kappa^{k}}Q
for every

in view of the cancellation condition.

Also, for all

we have

(\mathbb{D}_{k+m}T_{k})^{*}\mathbb{D}_{k^{\prime}+m}T_{k^{\prime}}=0

and

\lVert\mathbb{D}_{k^{\prime}+m}T_{k^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}_{k+m}T_{k})^{*}\rVert_{L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(X)}\leq\lVert T_{k^{\prime}}T_{k}^{*}\rVert_{L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(X)}\lesssim 2^{-\epsilon\lvert k-k^{\prime}\rvert}

by (

).
By the Cotlar–Stein lemma
[
Ste93
, p. 280]
it follows that
\lVert\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{D}_{k+m}T_{k}\rVert_{L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(X)}\lesssim 2^{-\epsilon m}
.

Estimate for

This estimate is similar to

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{k_{0}}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\sup_{x^{\prime}\in Q}\lvert\sum_{l<k_{0}\leq k}\mathbb{D}_{l}T_{k}f(x^{\prime})\rvert^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{\rho(x,x^{\prime})\leq C\kappa^{k_{0}}}\lvert\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{k-m<k_{0}\leq k}\mathbb{D}_{k-m}T_{k}f(x^{\prime})\rvert^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{1}_{k-m<k_{0}\leq k}\sup_{\rho(x,x^{\prime})\leq C\kappa^{k}}\lvert\mathbb{D}_{k-m}T_{k}f(x^{\prime})\rvert\Big)^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\geq 1}\Big(\sum_{k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}}m\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\mathbf{1}_{k-m<k_{0}\leq k}\sup_{\rho(x,x^{\prime})\leq C\kappa^{k}}\lvert\mathbb{D}_{k-m}T_{k}f(x^{\prime})\rvert\Big)^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 1}m\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{\rho(x,x^{\prime})\leq C\kappa^{k}}\lvert\mathbb{D}_{k-m}T_{k}f(x^{\prime})\rvert^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

We view the square sum on the right-hand side as an intrinsic square function.
Indeed, the condition (
1.9
) implies that

also has Hölder type regularity.
Notice that
\mathbb{D}_{k-m}g(x^{\prime})=\int gh_{k-m,x^{\prime}}
, where

and bounded

.
It follows that for each

the function

Moreover, it is Hölder continuous since it is an average of Hölder continuous functions.
The required decay in

now comes from the estimate for the intrinsic square function,
[

, Theorem 1.3]
, with
\Phi=\kappa^{-\epsilon m}
.

5.3.
Weak type
(1,1)

By homogeneity it suffices to show

\lvert\{\mathcal{N}F^{\prime}>1\}\rvert\lesssim\lVert f\rVert_{1}.

We make a CZ decomposition

.
The good part

Let

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{X\setminus\tilde{Q}}\sum_{k>k(Q)}\sup_{\rho(x,x^{\prime})\lesssim\kappa^{k}}\lvert T_{k}b_{Q}(x^{\prime})\rvert\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k>k(Q)}\kappa^{kD}\lVert T_{k}b_{Q}\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k>k(Q)}\kappa^{-\epsilon(k-k(Q))}\lVert b_{Q}\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert b_{Q}\rVert_{1},

where we have used that
T_{k}b_{Q}
is supported in a ball or radius
O(\kappa^{k})
, the mean zero property of
b_{Q}
, and Hölder continuity of
K_{k}
.
Summing over all bad cubes we obtain the claim.

Remark 5.5
.

The above proof also yields
L^{p}
and weak type estimates for the jump counting function
\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(T_{t}f(x):t>0)
, and even its localized non-tangentional maximal version.
However, the jump counting function is not subadditive, and unlike in the case of averages we have been unable to construct a subadditive majorant that still satisfies the localized non-tangentional weak type
(1,1)
estimate.

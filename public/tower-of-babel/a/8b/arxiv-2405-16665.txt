Abstract
. We characterize when a finite group G G possesses a Sylow 3 3 -subgroup P P with abelianization of order 9 9 in terms of the number of height zero characters lying in the principal 3 3 -block of G G , settling a conjecture put forward by Navarro, Sambale, and Tiep in 2018. Along the way, we show that a recent result by Laradji on the number of character of height zero in a block that lie above a given character of some normal subgroup holds, without any hypothesis on the group for blocks of maximal defect.

1.
Introduction

A key role in the theory of finite groups is played by dihedral, semidihedral and generalized quaternion
2
-groups. These can be characterized, for example, as the non-abelian
2
-groups
P
whose commutator subgroup
P^{\prime}
has index
4
in
P
, by work of O. Taussky
[
Tau37
]
. Since the beginning of the
20
th Century, the representation theory of finite groups having a Sylow
2
-subgroup
P
with
[P:P^{\prime}]=4
(and more generally of blocks having a defect group
P
with
[P:P^{\prime}]=4
) has been largely studied. Partly relying on this extensive study, G. Navarro, B. Sambale, and P. H. Tiep showed in
[
NST18
, Thm. A]
that a finite group
G
possesses a Sylow
2
-subgroup
P
whose commutator subgroup has index
4
if, and only if, the principal
2
-block of
G
contains exactly
4
irreducible characters of odd degree.

Contrasting this situation, the representation theory of groups having a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
with
[P:P^{\prime}]=p^{2}
for an odd prime
p
is far from being understood.
It is perhaps not so surprising, given the fact that the structure of these
p
-groups for a general prime can be exceedingly complicated. For instance, write
f_{p}(n)
to denote the number of isomorphism classes of groups
P
of order
p^{n}
with
[P:P^{\prime}]=p^{2}
. For
p=2
, as mentioned above, we have that
f_{2}(n)=3
if
n\geq 4
, but already for
p=3
the function
f_{3}(n)
tends to infinity with
n
(see for instance
[
Neb89
]
).
In fact, proving that a finite group has a Sylow
3
-subgroup
P
with
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
if, and only if, its
principal
3
-block contains exactly
6
or
9
irreducible characters of degree coprime to
3
has remained a challenge since this statement was put forward in
[
NST18
, Conj. B]
. The main result of this article is to prove the above mentioned conjecture.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
P\in{\rm Syl}_{3}(G)
.
Then
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
if, and only if, there are exactly
6
or
9
irreducible characters of degree coprime to
3
lying in the principal
3
-block of
G
.

For a group
G
, we write
k_{0}(B_{0}(G))
to denote the number of irreducible characters of degree coprime to
3
lying in the principal
3
-block
B_{0}(G)
of
G
(as these are exactly those of
3
-height zero in
B_{0}(G)
). Theorem A above, together with
[
NST18
, Thm. A]
, imply that the conditions
[P:P^{\prime}]\leq p^{2}
and
k_{0}(B_{0}(G))\leq p^{2}
are equivalent for any finite group
G
and
p\in\{2,3\}
. This statement does not hold for arbitrary primes as the group
H=\verb|SmallGroup(1000,163)|
satisfies
k(H)=k_{0}(B_{0}(H))=25
with respect to the prime
p=5
and its Sylow
5
-subgroups are abelian of order
125
. We care to mention that, for a general prime
p
, it has been proven very recently in
[
NS23
]
that the local condition
[P:P^{\prime}]=p^{2}
can be detected in the character table of the group
G
. However, it is still not known whether there exists a simple
algorithm to do that for primes
p\geq 5
.

We also mention that Theorem A is an essential step toward proving the
p=3
analogue of the main result of
[
NRSV21
]
, which would give a way to determine from the character table whether a Sylow
3
-subgroup is
2
-generated.

Our proof of Theorem A eventually reduces to showing that the statement of Theorem A holds for almost simple groups
A
with
[A:{\rm Soc}(A)]_{3}\leq 3
, where
n_{3}
is the largest power of
3
dividing an integer
n
and
{\rm Soc}(A)
is the socle of
A
. On the way to proving Theorem A for these almost simple groups, we classify when
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
for those groups and prove some additional statements that could be of independent interest.
In order to perform the reduction, we have also proven results of independent interest regarding the behavior of characters in principal blocks with respect to normal subgroups and the character theory of simple non-abelian groups with respect to the action of group automorphisms on them. The former appear in Section
2
and the latter in Section
3
of this work.
Among the results briefly mentioned above, we would like to highlight the following generalization of the main theorem of
[
Lar23
]
.

Theorem B
.

Let
p
be a prime. Let
G
be a finite group,
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
and
B
a
p
-block of
G
with defect group
P
.
Let
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
such that
PN/N>1
. If

, then the number

of irreducible characters of degree coprime to

,

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we state some results (both old and new) on principal blocks (and more generally, blocks of maximal defect). In Section 3, we state our results on almost simple groups, which will be proven in Sections 4 and 5. We address the alternating groups in Section 4 and the groups of Lie type in Section 5. Finally, we complete the proof of Theorem A in Section 6.

2.
General statements on principal blocks

2.1.
Known results on principal blocks

For a finite group
G
and a prime
p
, we denote by
B_{0}(G)
the principal
p
-block of
G
.
Recall that

{1}_{G}\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))=\{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)\ |\ \sum_{{x\in G}\atop{(p,o(x))=1}}\chi(x)\neq 0\}\,.

In particular, if
G=H_{1}\times\dots\times H_{t}
, then

\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))=\{\theta_{1}\times\dots\times\theta_{t}\mid\theta_{i}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(H_{i}))\}.

Given a normal subgroup
N
of
G
, we can view the irreducible characters of
G/N
as irreducible characters of
G
containing
N
in their kernel, and under this identification

{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G/N))\subseteq{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G)),

with equality in the case where the order of
N
is coprime to
p
by
[
Nav98
, Thm. 9.9(c)]
.

For any
\theta\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(N))
, there is some
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))
lying over
\theta
(that is, with
[\chi,\theta^{G}]\neq 0
), and for any
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))
, every constituent of
\chi_{N}
lies in
B_{0}(N)
by
[
Nav98
, Thm. 9.2, Cor. 9.3, and Thm. 9.4]
. If moreover
B_{0}(G)
is the only block covering
B_{0}(N)
, then for any
\theta\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(N))
every constituent of
\theta^{G}
lies in
B_{0}(G)
. This happens, for instance, in the following case.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
p
be a prime. Suppose that
M
is a normal subgroup of
G
containing
{\bf C}_{G}(Q)
, where
Q\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(M)
. Then the principal
p
-block
B_{0}(G)
of
G
is the only block covering the principal
p
-block
B_{0}(M)
of
M
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a
p
-block of
G
covering
B_{0}(M)
. By
[
Nav98
, Thm. 9.26]
,
Q
is contained in some defect group
P
of
B
. Since
{\bf C}_{G}(P)\leq{\bf C}_{G}(Q)\leq M
, by
[
Nav98
, Lem. 9.20]

B
is regular with respect to
M
.
By
[
Nav98
, Thm. 9.19]
,
B_{0}(M)^{G}
is defined and hence

B=B_{0}(M)^{G}=B_{0}(G)

by Brauer’s Third Main Theorem
[
Nav98
, Thm. 6.7]
.
∎

Next is a well-known result of J. Alperin and E. C. Dade. We will use both the notation
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
and
{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))
to denote the set of irreducible characters of height zero (

-degree) in the principal

-block of

. Moreover we let

Theorem 2.2

(Alperin-Dade)
.

Suppose that
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
, with
G/N
a
p^{\prime}
-group.
Let

and assume that
G=N{\bf C}_{G}(P)
. Then restriction of characters defines
a natural bijection between the irreducible characters of the principal blocks
of
G
and
N
. In particular,
k_{0}(B_{0}(G))=k_{0}(B_{0}(N))
.

Proof.

The case where
G/N
is solvable was proved in
[
Alp76
]
and the general case
in
[
Dad77
]
. The last part of the statement follows from the first.
∎

The following is contained in
[
Mur94
, Lem. 4.3]
.

Lemma 2.3

(Murai)
.

Let

be a prime. Let

be a normal subgroup of

. If

.

For a normal subgroup

of

and given

, we will sometimes write
{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)=\{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))\ |\ [\chi_{N},\theta]\neq 0\}
and

2.2.
New results on principal blocks

Here we collect some new properties of principal blocks that are key to our reduction process and that we believe to be of independent interest.

If
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))
and a normal subgroup
N
is contained in the kernel of
\chi
, then in general it is not true that
\chi
, seen as a character of
G/N
, lies in
B_{0}(G/N)
.
The following result was actually proven within the proof of
[
NRSV21
, Thm. 3.5]
. We include it here for the reader’s convenience. We recall that
\Phi(P)
denotes the Frattini subgroup of a
p
-group
P
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
N
be a normal subgroup of a finite group
G
. Suppose that
N\subseteq\Phi(P)
, where
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
. Write
B_{0}=B_{0}(G)
to denote the principal
p
-block of
G
. If
\chi
is a height zero irreducible character in
B_{0}
and
N\subseteq{\rm Ker}(\chi)
, then
\chi
seen as a character of
G/N
belongs to the principal
p
-block of
G/N
.

Proof.

Write
\bar{\chi}
to denote the character
\chi
seen as a character of
G/N
. Since
\bar{\chi}

has degree not divisible by
p
, it follows that
\bar{\chi}
lies in a block of
G/N
of maximal defect
P/N
.
By
[
Nav98
, Prob. 4.5]
, we only need to prove that if
Nx\in G/N
, with
x\in G
, is
p
-regular
with
P/N\subseteq{\bf C}_{G/N}(Nx)
, then

\left({[G/N:{\bf C}_{G/N}(Nx)]\bar{\chi}(Nx)\over\bar{\chi}(1)}\right)\equiv[G/N:{\bf C}_{G/N}(Nx)]

modulo any maximal ideal
\mathcal{P}
of the ring
\bf R
of algebraic integers in
\mathbb{C}
containing
p
.
First notice that
Nx\in{\bf N}_{G/N}(P/N)={\bf N}_{G}(P)/N
. Also, we may assume that
x
is
a
p
-regular element, using that
Nx=(Nx)_{p^{\prime}}=Nx_{p^{\prime}}
. Notice that

{\bf C}_{G/N}(Nx)={\bf C}_{G}(x)N/N\,,

using that
(|N|,o(x))=1
(and
[
Isa08
, Cor. 3.28]
).
We have shown that
x
is a
p
-regular element of

{\bf N}_{G}(P)
centralizing
P/N
. Therefore
x
centralizes
P/\Phi(P)
,
and by

[
Isa08
, Cor. 3.29]

we have that
x
centralizes
P
.
In particular,
N\subseteq{\bf C}_{G}(x)
. Then
[G/N:{\bf C}_{G/N}(Nx)]=[G:{\bf C}_{G}(x)]
.
Since
\chi
is in the principal block of
G
, we have that

\left({[G/N:{\bf C}_{G/N}(Nx)]\bar{\chi}(Nx)\over\bar{\chi}(1)}\right)=\left({[G:{\bf C}_{G}(x)]\chi(x)\over\chi(1)}\right)\equiv[G:{\bf C}_{G}(x)]\,,

modulo any maximal ideal
\mathcal{P}
of the ring of algebraic integers
\bf R
in
\mathbb{C}
containing
p
, as desired.
∎

If
N\triangleleft G
and
\theta
is a character in the principal block of
N
that extends to

, in general it is not true that the principal block of
G
must contain some extension of
\theta
.
The following guarantees the existence of an extension in the principal block. The main idea of the proof is taken from the proof of
[
Isa76
, Thm. 6.26]
. We denote by
{\rm Lin}(G)
the subset of
{\rm Irr}(G)
of characters of

of degree 1, often called linear characters.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
N
be a normal
p
-subgroup of
G
. Suppose that
\lambda\in{\rm Lin}(N)
is
G
-invariant and extends to
P
, where
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
.
Then
\lambda
extends to some character in the principal block of
G
.

Proof.

Write
|G|=p^{a}m
with
(m,p)=1
. Let
\delta\in{\rm Lin}(G)
be an extension of
\lambda
(such an extension exists by
[
Isa76
, Theorem 6.26]
).
Choose a positive integer
b
such that
m
divides
b
and
b\equiv 1\bmod\,o(\lambda)
.
Notice then that
\delta^{b}\in{\rm Lin}(G)
. Write
G^{0}=\{g\in G\ |\ (o(g),p)=1\}
to denote the set of
p
-regular elements of
G
.
Since every
g\in G^{0}
satisfies
o(g)
divides
m
, we have that
\delta(g)
is an
m

\delta^{b}(g)=1
for every
g\in G^{0}
.
Since then

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in G^{0}}\delta^{b}(x)=|G^{0}|\neq 0\,,

we have that
\delta^{b}
lies in the principal

-block of

by
[
Nav98
, Thm. 3.19]
.
∎

Below

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
be a prime and
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
. Let
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
. Suppose that
PN/N>1
. Let
B
be a
p
-block of
G
with defect group
P
covering a
p
-block
b
of
N
with defect group
N\cap P
.
Let
\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{0}(b)
. Suppose that

\theta
extends to
PN
. Then
k_{0}(B|\theta)\geq 2
. If moreover
p\leq 3
, then

p
divides
k_{0}(B|\theta)
.

Proof.

First notice that, by induction on
[G:N]
, and using the Clifford correspondence together with the Fong–Reynolds correspondence
[
Nav98
, Thm. 9.14]
, we may assume that
\theta
is
G
-invariant.

Define

\psi=\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)}\chi(1)\chi\,.

Notice that
\psi(x)=0
if
x\notin N
and
\psi(x)=|G/N|\theta(1)\theta(x)
if
x\in N
by a theorem of Knörr (see
[
Nav18
, Thm. 5.21]
). In particular,
\psi^{*}\equiv 0
, where
{}^{*}\colon{\bf R}\to\mathbb{F}
is the ring homomorphism defined in
[
Nav98
, Ch. 2]
, naturally extended to characters of
G
(recall that
\bf R
is the ring of algebraic integers in
{\mathbb{C}}
and we choose a maximal ideal
\mathcal{P}
containing
p
so that
\mathbb{F}={\bf R}/\mathcal{P}
is algebraically closed of characteristic
p
and
∗
is the natural epimorphism).

We now follow the proof of
[
Nav98
, Thm. 5.10]
to show that
(\psi_{C})^{*}
vanishes on the set
G^{0}
of
p
-regular elements of
G
for every
p
-block
C
of
G
, where
\displaystyle\psi_{C}=\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(C|\theta)}\chi(1)\chi
.
Given
y\in G^{0}
(so
y\in G
such that
(o(y),p)=1
), we have that

\psi_{C}(y)=\sum_{\xi\in{\rm Irr}(C)}[\psi,\xi]\xi(y)=\sum_{\xi\in{\rm Irr}(C)}[\psi,\xi]\left(\sum_{\varphi\in{\rm IBr(G)}}d_{\xi,\varphi}\varphi(y)\right)\,.

Here, following the notation of
[
Nav98
, Chapter 2]
we used the symbol
d_{\xi,\varphi}
to denote the corresponding decomposition number.
In particular,

\psi_{C}(y)=\sum_{\varphi\in{\rm IBr}(G)}\left(\sum_{\xi\in{\rm Irr}(C)}[\psi,\xi]d_{\xi,\varphi}\right)\varphi(y)\,.

As
\psi=\sum_{C\in{\rm Bl}(G)}\psi_{C}
, we then have that

0=\psi(y)^{*}=\sum_{\varphi\in{\rm IBr}(G)}\left(\sum_{\xi\in{\rm Irr}(G)}[\psi,\xi]d_{\xi,\varphi}\right)^{*}\varphi(y)^{*}\,.

As the trace functions
\{\varphi^{*}\ |\ \varphi\in{\rm IBr}(G)\}
are

-linearly independent, by
[
Nav98
, Thm. 1.19]
,
we conclude that for any
\varphi\in{\rm IBr}(G)
we have that

\left(\sum_{\xi\in{\rm Irr}(G)}[\psi,\xi]d_{\xi,\varphi}\right)^{*}=0\,.

Now if
\varphi
does not lie in

then

\sum_{\xi\in{\rm Irr}(C)}[\psi,\xi]d_{\xi,\varphi}=0\,.

If
\varphi
lies in
C
then

0=\left(\sum_{\xi\in{\rm Irr}(G)}[\psi,\xi]d_{\xi,\varphi}\right)^{*}=\left(\sum_{\xi\in{\rm Irr}(C)}[\psi,\xi]d_{\xi,\varphi}\right)^{*}\,.

In particular, we have shown that
(\psi_{C}(1))^{*}=0
. In other words, the positive integer

\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(C|\theta)}\chi(1)^{2}

is divisible by
p
, so is the positive integer

\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{0}(C|\theta)}\chi(1)^{2}\,,

where we are using Lemma
2.3
to see that it is positive. This implies the first part of the statement. If
p\leq 3
, then
m^{2}\equiv 1\bmod\,p
for every integer

coprime to
p
, so the second part of the statement also follows.
∎

Using

Theorem 3.5

Suppose that

Proof.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
p=3
, let
S
be a nonabelian simple group of order divisible by
p
, and let
S\leq A\leq{\rm Aut}(S)
with
[A:S]_{3}=1
. Suppose that there exists an
A
-invariant nontrivial
\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))
and that there are at least 5
A
-orbits of characters in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))
. Suppose that
k_{0}(B_{0}(A))\in\{6,9\}
. Then
k_{0}(B_{0}(S))\leq 18
.

Proof.

In this case
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(S)
and
A=S{\bf N}_{A}(P)
by the Frattini argument, so
M=S{\bf C}_{A}(P)
is normal in
A
. By Lemma
2.1
,
B_{0}(A)
is the only
p
-block of
A
covering
B_{0}(M)
, so in particular
k(A/M)\leq k_{0}(B_{0}(A))\leq 9
, but since there are at least 5 orbits of characters in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))
and
[A:M]_{3}=1
, then
k(A/M)\in\{1,2,4,5\}
. If
A=M
, by Theorem
2.2
we have that
k_{0}(B_{0}(S))=k_{0}(B_{0}(A))
and we are done. So we may assume
A>M
. Let
\tilde{\theta}\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(M))
be the extension of
\theta
to
M
given by Theorem
2.2
. We claim that
\tilde{\theta}
is
A
-invariant. Indeed, let
a\in A
and notice that
\tilde{\theta}^{a}\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(M))
is also an extension of
\theta^{a}=\theta
. Since restriction is a bijection in Theorem
2.2
, we have that
\tilde{\theta}^{a}=\tilde{\theta}
and the claim is proved.

Suppose
k(A/M)=2
. In this case
A/M\cong C_{2}
. Since
1_{M}
and
\tilde{\theta}
extend to
A
, we have at most 5 other
A
-orbits in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(M))
of size at most 2, so
k_{0}(B_{0}(S))=k_{0}(B_{0}(M))\leq 12
.

Next, suppose that
k(A/M)=4
. Since
|A:M|_{3}=1
, then
A/M\in\{C_{4},C_{2}\times C_{2},D_{10}\}
. Since
\tilde{\theta}
is
A
-invariant, if
A/M=D_{10}
, or
A/M=C_{4}
, we would have that
\tilde{\theta}
extends to

, and we have too many characters in

. So necessarily

and

, then there are at most 4 other orbits of size at most 4, and then

, as wanted.

Finally, suppose that

, so

(again we are using that

is not divisible by 3 to exclude some groups having 5 conjugacy classes). In this case, every nontrivial

has a unique

over it, so in particular

is fully ramified in

needs to be a square, a contradiction.
∎

4.
Alternating and Symmetric Groups

Here we first show that Theorem
3.1
holds for simple alternating groups. Then, we show that Theorem
3.3
holds for symmetric and alternating groups.
Given a partition
\lambda
of a natural number
n
, we let
\chi^{\lambda}
be the corresponding irreducible character of the symmetric group
S_{n}
. Moreover, we let
C_{3}(\lambda)
and
\lambda^{\prime}
denote the
3
-core and the conjugate partition of
\lambda
, respectively. We refer the reader to
[
Ols93
]
for a complete account of the basic facts concerning the combinatorics of partitions.

Let
n\geq 5
be a natural number and let
A_{n}
denote the alternating group of rank
n
. We recall that
\mathrm{Aut}(A_{n})=S_{n}
for all
n\neq 6
. On the other hand,
\mathrm{Aut}(A_{6})/A_{6}\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}
. In particular, if
X\in\mathrm{Syl}_{3}(\mathrm{Aut}(A_{n}))
then
X\leq A_{n}
and every irreducible character of
A_{n}
is obviously
X
-invariant.

Let
a\in\{0,1,2\}
be such that
n=3w+a
, for some
w\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
be the partitions of
n
defined as follows:

\lambda_{1}=\begin{cases}(n-1,1)&\text{if }a=0,\\
(n-2,2)&\text{if }a=1,\\
(2,2,1^{n-4})&\text{if }a=2.\end{cases}\ \ \lambda_{2}=\begin{cases}(n-2,1^{2})&\text{if }a=0,\\
(n-4,2,1^{2})&\text{if }a=1,\\
(2,1^{n-2})&\text{if }a=2.\end{cases}

For any
i\in\{1,2\}
, it is easy to verify that
C_{3}(\lambda_{i})=(a)
and that
\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{i}^{\prime}
. It follows that
\chi^{\lambda_{i}}\in\mathrm{Irr}(B_{0}(S_{n}))
and therefore that
\theta_{i}:=(\chi^{\lambda_{i}})_{A_{n}}\in\mathrm{Irr}(B_{0}(A_{n}))
.
An easy application of the hook length formula
[
J78
, 20.1]
shows that:

\theta_{1}(1)=\begin{cases}n-1&\text{if }a=0,\\
n(n-3)/2&\text{if }a=1,\\
n(n-3)/2&\text{if }a=2.\end{cases}\ \ \theta_{2}(1)=\begin{cases}(n-1)(n-2)/2&\text{if }a=0,\\
(n^{2}-2n)(n-3)(n-5)/8&\text{if }a=1,\\
n-1&\text{if }a=2.\end{cases}

It follows that
\theta_{1}(1)
and
\theta_{2}(1)
are greater than
1
, coprime to
3
, and distinct integers.
This in turn implies that conditions (
a_{1}
) and (b) of Theorem
3.1
hold for simple alternating groups.

In order to prove that condition (c) holds as well, we can assume that
n\geq 6
as
9
should divide the order of
A_{n}
. We further assume that
n\geq 7
, as the case
n=6
can be easily treated by hand (it is easy to see that
\mathrm{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(A_{6}))
contains
3
characters of degrees
5
,
8
and
10
respectively).
We recall that Olsson’s theory of
3
-core towers
[
Ols93
, Cor. 11.8]
allows us to give a precise formula for
k_{0}(B_{0}(S_{n})))
. In particular, if
n=\sum_{k=0}^{t}a_{k}3^{k}
is the
3
-adic expansion of
n
then we have that

k_{0}(B_{0}(S_{n}))=\prod_{k=1}^{t}c(3^{k},a_{k}).

Here
c(M,a)
denotes the number of sequences of length
M
of the form
(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{M})
where
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{M}
are
3
-core partitions whose sizes sum to
a
.
In particular, we deduce that
k_{0}(B_{0}(S_{n})))\geq c(3^{t},a_{t})\geq c(3^{t},1)=3^{t}
, this in turn implies that
k_{0}(B_{0}(S_{n})))\geq 9
, whenever
n\geq 9
. Hence, for
n\geq 9
there are at least
5
non
\mathrm{Aut}(A_{n})
-conjugate irreducible characters of degree coprime to
3
in the principal block of
A_{n}
. For a slightly different reason the same argument applies to the cases
n\in\{7,8\}
. Here we have that
n=2\cdot 3+a
for some
a\in\{1,2\}
. It is easy to check that
c(3,2)=9
. Exactly as before we deduce that there are at least
5
non
\mathrm{Aut}(A_{n})
-conjugate irreducible characters of degree coprime to
3
in the principal block of
A_{n}
. We conclude that condition (c) of Theorem
3.1
holds for
A_{n}
.

We end this section with the verification of Theorem
3.3
for symmetric and alternating groups.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
let
X_{k}
denote a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
S_{3^{k}}
. We know that
X_{k}\cong C_{3}\wr C_{3}\wr\cdots\wr C_{3}
the iterated wreath product of
k
copies of the cyclic group of order
3
.
It is not difficult to show that
[X_{k}:X_{k}^{\prime}]=3^{k}
(it is very easy to show that
X_{k}
has exactly
3^{k}
linear characters. A slightly more involved exercise is to show that
X_{k}/X_{k}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the direct product of
k
copies of
C_{3}
).
Let
n=\sum_{k=0}^{t}a_{k}3^{k}
be the
3
-adic expansion of
n
as above. Given
X\in\mathrm{Syl}_{3}(S_{n})=\mathrm{Syl}_{3}(A_{n})
, we know from
[
JK81
, 4.1.22]
that

X=\prod_{k=1}^{t}(X_{k})^{\times a_{k}}.

Hence, we have that
[X:X^{\prime}]=\prod_{k=1}^{t}3^{k\cdot a_{k}}
. If we now assume that
[X:X^{\prime}]=9
we immediately deduce that
6\leq n\leq 11
. Using
[
Ols93
, Cor. 11.8]
and
[
Ols90
, Prop. 4.4]
we obtain that

\{k_{0}(B_{0}(S_{n}))\ |\ 6\leq n\leq 11\}=\{9\}
and that
\{k_{0}(B_{0}(A_{n}))\ |\ 6\leq n\leq 11\}=\{6,9\}
. This proves one implication of Theorem
3.3
.
The converse is similar. The formulas provided in
[
Ols93
, Cor. 11.8]
and
[
Ols90
, Prop. 4.4]
imply that if
k_{0}(B_{0}(A_{n})),k_{0}(B_{0}(S_{n}))\in\{6,9\}
then
n\in\{6,7,\ldots,11\}
. As discussed above we know that in these cases we have that
[X:X^{\prime}]=9
.

5.
Groups of Lie type

Let
p
be a prime and let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a connected reductive algebraic group over
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Let
F\colon{\mathbf{G}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{G}}
be a Steinberg endomorphism. Then the group of fixed points
G={\mathbf{G}}^{F}
is a group of Lie type. In this situation, we will write
G^{\ast}
for the group
({\mathbf{G}}^{\ast})^{F}
with
({\mathbf{G}}^{\ast},F)
in duality with
({\mathbf{G}},F)
as in
[
GM20
, Def. 1.5.17]
.

We summarize some relevant Deligne–Lusztig theory, for which we refer the reader to, e.g.
[
GM20
, Sec. 2.6]
. The set
{\rm Irr}(G)
is partitioned into rational Lusztig series
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
where
s
ranges over the semisimple elements of
G^{\ast}
, up to
G^{\ast}
-conjugacy. When
s=1
, the characters in the set
\mathcal{E}(G,1)
are known as unipotent characters. The set
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
is in bijection with the set of unipotent characters of
{\bf C}_{G^{\ast}}(s)
, and we refer to those corresponding to unipotent characters of
{\bf C}_{G^{\ast}}(s)
lying above the trivial character of
({\bf C}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}(s)^{\circ})^{F}
as semisimple characters. In particular, we assume that a choice of this Jordan decomposition has been made and will denote by
\chi_{s}\in\mathcal{E}(G,s)
the semsisimple character corresponding to
1_{{\bf C}_{G^{\ast}}(s)}
. (Although, when
{\bf C}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}(s)
is connected, no choice is necessary.) The degree of
\chi\in\mathcal{E}(G,s)
corresponding to
\psi\in\mathcal{E}({\bf C}_{G^{\ast}}(s),1)
is
[G^{\ast}:{\bf C}_{G^{\ast}}(s)]_{p^{\prime}}\psi(1)
, so that
\chi_{s}(1)=[G^{\ast}:{\bf C}_{G^{\ast}}(s)]_{p^{\prime}}
.

By

[
CE04
, Prop. 8.26 and (8.19)]
, we have a bijection
{\bf Z}(G^{\ast})\rightarrow{\rm Irr}(G/\textbf{O}^{p^{\prime}}(G))
, where if
z\mapsto\hat{z}
, then
\mathcal{E}(G,s)\otimes\hat{z}=\mathcal{E}(G,sz)
.

5.1.
Our Setting

We will work primarily in the following setting. Let
S
be a simple group of Lie type defined in characteristic
p
such that there is a simple, simply connected algebraic group
{\mathbf{G}}
over
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and a Steinberg endomorphism
F\colon{\mathbf{G}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{G}}
such that
S=G/{\bf Z}(G)
with
G={\mathbf{G}}^{F}
. (Recall that the Tits group
{}^{2}\!\operatorname{F}_{4}(2)^{\prime}
was considered in Proposition
3.4
, so we may omit this in our setting.)
We can write
\operatorname{Aut}(S)=\widetilde{S}\rtimes\mathcal{D}
, where
\widetilde{S}
is the group of inner-diagonal automorphisms of
S
and
\mathcal{D}
is an appropriate group generated by graph and field automorphisms. (See, e.g.
[
MT11
, Thm. 24.24]
.) In particular, we will write
F_{p}
for a generating field automorphism induced by the map
x\mapsto x^{p}
on
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and
F_{p^{i}}=F_{p}^{i}
for a positive integer
i
. Further, it will sometimes be useful to identify
\widetilde{S}
with the fixed points
{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{F}
of the adjoint group of the same type as
{\mathbf{G}}
. (See
[
MT11
, Prop. 24.21]
and the subsequent discussion.) If
\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(S)
, then a series
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
is mapped to a series
\mathcal{E}(G,s^{\varphi^{\ast}})
for an appropriate dual automorphism
\varphi^{\ast}
of
G^{\ast}
, by
[
Tay18
, Prop. 7.2]
.

We will also often use that, from
[
NT13
, Lem. 4.4 and Rem. 4.6]
, we have the characters in
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
for
s\in[G^{\ast},G^{\ast}]
are trivial on
{\bf Z}(G)
.

For the moment, let
\ell
be any prime and write
B_{0}(X)
for the principal
\ell
-block of a group
X
.
Now, when
\ell\neq p
, that is
\ell
is not the defining characteristic of
G
, a unipotent block is one containing unipotent characters, and the set of characters lying in unipotent blocks is exactly the union of the series
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
with
s\in G^{\ast}
an
\ell
-element, by
[
CE04
, Thm. 9.12]
. In particular,
{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))
is contained in this union. By
[
Hi90
, Cor. 3.4]
and
[
CE04
, Thm. 21.13]
, the character
\chi_{s}
is contained in
{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(S))
whenever
s
is a semisimple
\ell
-element and either
{\bf Z}({\mathbf{G}}^{\ast})
is connected or
\ell
is good for
{\mathbf{G}}
and
{\bf C}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}(s)
is connected.

On the other hand, when
\ell=p
is the defining characteristic, we have
{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(S))={\rm Irr}(S)\setminus\{\mathrm{St}_{S}\}
by
[
CE04
, Thm. 6.18]
.

The following observations regarding extensions will be useful.

Lemma 5.1
.

Keep the setting above. Let
\ell\neq p
be a non-defining prime dividing
|S|
but not dividing
|\widetilde{S}/S|
. Then restriction provides a bijection between
{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(\widetilde{S}))
and
{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(S))
.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(S))
and let
\widetilde{\chi}\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(\widetilde{S})\mid\chi)
, which exists by
[
Nav98
, Thm. 9.2]
. Since
\widetilde{S}/S
is abelian and restrictions from
\widetilde{S}
to
S
are multiplicity-free (see e.g.
[
CE04
, Thm. 15.11]
), we have by Clifford theory that the number of constituents of
\widetilde{\chi}|_{S}
is the number of
\hat{z}\in{\rm Irr}(\widetilde{S}/S)
such that
\widetilde{\chi}\hat{z}=\widetilde{\chi}
(see e.g.
[
RSV21
, Lem. 1.4]
). By
[
CE04
, Thm. 9.12]
, the characters in
B_{0}(\widetilde{S})
lie in Lusztig series indexed by semisimple
\ell
-classes of
({\mathbf{G}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{\ast})^{F}
and as discussed above,
the irreducible characters of
\widetilde{S}/S={\mathbf{G}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{F}/\textbf{O}^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{G}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{F})
are in bijection with the elements
z
of
{\bf Z}({\mathbf{G}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{\ast})^{F}
, and tensoring with
\hat{z}
sends the series
\mathcal{E}(\widetilde{S},s)
to
\mathcal{E}(\widetilde{S},sz)
. Since
\ell
does not divide the order of any such
z
, it follows that
\widetilde{\chi}|_{S}=\chi
. Now, since
\widetilde{S}/S
is abelian, every character of
\widetilde{S}
above
\chi
is an extension, and hence of the form
\widetilde{\chi}\hat{z}
for some
z\in{\bf Z}({\mathbf{G}}_{\mathrm{ad}}^{\ast})^{F}
. Again, since
\ell
does not divide the order of any such
z\neq 1
, we therefore see that
B_{0}(\widetilde{S})
contains only one extension of
\chi
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

5.4.
Sylow Subgroups with
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
in Non-Defining Characteristic

Here we discuss when the condition
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
holds for groups of Lie type defined in characteristic
p\neq 3
. It is worth noting that when
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
, we necessarily have
[P:\Phi(P)]\leq 9
, and
P
is either cyclic or
2
-generated. It will also be useful to note that when
G=H\wr K
is a standard wreath product, we have
G/G^{\prime}\cong H/H^{\prime}\times K/K^{\prime}
, for example from
[
Ro96
, Prob. 1.6.20]
.

Throughout, for an integer
m
with
3\nmid m
, we will write
d_{3}(m)
for the order of
m
modulo
3
. If
3\mid m
, we write
m_{3}
for the highest power of
3
dividing
m
.

5.4.1.
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)
and
\operatorname{PSL}_{3}^{\epsilon}(q)

We begin with the case of
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)
and
\operatorname{PSL}_{3}^{\epsilon}(q)
.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
q
be a power of a prime
p\neq 3
and let
S=\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)
or let
3\mid(q+\epsilon)
and
S=\operatorname{PSL}^{\epsilon}_{3}(q)
. Let
A
be an almost simple group
S\lhd A\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)
with
|A/S|_{3}\leq 3
and let
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(A)
. Then
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
if and only if
(q^{2}-1)_{3}=9
. Further, if
3\nmid|A/S|
, then
A=S{\bf C}_{A}(P)
.

Proof.

Note that in this case
3\nmid|\widetilde{S}/S|
. First suppose that
3\nmid|A/S|
, so we may take
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(S)
. Let
q=p^{f}
with
f=3^{a}\cdot m
and
3\nmid m
. Then
A\leq\widetilde{S}\rtimes\langle\tau,F^{\prime}\rangle
with
F^{\prime}=F_{p}^{3^{a}}
and
\tau
a graph automorphism in the case of
\operatorname{PSL}_{3}(q)
and trivial otherwise. Here
P
is cyclic of order
(q^{2}-1)_{3}
, and we can identify
P
with a Sylow
3
-subgroup of a cyclic Sylow
d
-torus of
G={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(q)
resp.
{\mathrm{SL}}_{3}^{\epsilon}(q)
, where
d=d_{3}(q)
, resp
d_{3}(\epsilon q)=2
. The automorphism
F^{\prime}
acts on this cyclic torus by
p^{d\cdot 3^{a}}
powers, so we see
F^{\prime}
acts trivially on
P
or by inversion, since we may write
(q^{2}-1)=\prod_{k\mid m}\Phi_{k}(p^{2\cdot 3^{a}})
and since
3\mid(p^{2\cdot 3^{a}}-1)
, we have
3\nmid\Phi_{k}(p^{2\cdot 3^{a}})
for any
1\neq k\mid m
.
In either case, we may find
g\in S
such that
F^{\prime}\in g{\bf C}_{A}(P)
. Similarly,
\tau
acts by inversion on
P
and
\widetilde{S}/S
is either trivial or order
2
acting trivially on the Sylow
d
torus containing
P
, and we see
A=S{\bf C}_{A}(P)
as claimed. Further, in this case
P
is abelian, so
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
if and only if
(q^{2}-1)_{3}=9
.

Now let
|A/S|_{3}=3
. Then
P\cong P_{1}\rtimes C_{3}
, where
C_{3}=\langle F_{q_{0}}\rangle
with
q=q_{0}^{3}
and
P_{1}\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(S)
. Since
P_{1}
is abelian, note then that
P/P^{\prime}\cong P_{1}/[P_{1},F_{q_{0}}]\times\langle F_{q_{0}}\rangle
.
Let
(q^{2}-1)_{3}=3^{b}
. Then
(q^{2}-1)=(q_{0}^{2}-1)(q_{0}^{4}+q_{0}^{2}+1)
and the second factor is divisible by
3
exactly once, so
(q_{0}^{2}-1)_{3}=3^{b-1}
. Then we can see directly that
P_{1}/[P_{1},F_{q_{0}}]
has size
3^{b-1}
, so that
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
exactly when
b=2
.
∎

We next complete the picture for
S=\operatorname{PSL}_{3}^{\epsilon}(q)
.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
q
be a power of a prime and let
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
with
3\mid(q-\epsilon)
. Let
G={\mathrm{SL}}_{3}^{\epsilon}(q)
,
S=\operatorname{PSL}_{3}^{\epsilon}(q)
, and
\widetilde{S}={\mathrm{PGL}}_{3}^{\epsilon}(q)
.

(i)

Let
R\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(X)
, where
X\in\{S,\widetilde{S},G,G\langle\delta\rangle\}
, where
\delta
is a diagonal automorphism of order
3
. Then

[R:R^{\prime}]=9
.

(ii)

If
S\leq A\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)
with
|A/S|_{3}=3
, then
R\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(A)
satisfies

if and only if the Sylow
3
-subgroup of

.

(iii)

If
S\leq A\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)

3\nmid|A/S|
and

[A:M]\leq 2
, where

Proof.

Throughout, we let
P
denote a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
G
and
\widetilde{P}
a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\widetilde{G}:={\mathrm{GL}}_{3}^{\epsilon}(q)
with
P=\widetilde{P}\cap G
. Let

, so
|Z|=3
. Then
Q:=P/Z=PZ/Z\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(S)
and
\widetilde{Q}:=\widetilde{P}{\bf Z}(\widetilde{G})/{\bf Z}(\widetilde{G})\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(\widetilde{S})
.

(i)
Let
(q-\epsilon)_{3}=3^{b}
. Then
\widetilde{P}\cong C_{3^{b}}\wr C_{3}
and
\widetilde{P}^{\prime}=\{(x,y,z)\in C_{3^{b}}^{3}\mid xyz=1\}

P

\widetilde{P}^{\prime}\neq P^{\prime}
. We claim that there is a subgroup

Let
a

and

in the base group
C_{3^{b}}^{3}
. The elements

and
g_{2}:=(a,a,a^{-2})
lie in
\widetilde{P}^{\prime}

contains

.

Now, we have
[Q:Q^{\prime}]=[P:P^{\prime}Z]

Now write

(ii) Now suppose that
|A/S|_{3}=3
. If
A
contains
\widetilde{S}
, then we are done by (i). So, we may assume

[
NS23
, Lem. 5.1]
. Alternatively, we may argue as follows. A Sylow

(iii) Let
q=p^{f}
with
f={3^{a}\cdot m}

(m,3)=1

F_{0}:=F_{p^{3^{a}}}
. In this case,

\tau
denotes a graph automorphism if
\epsilon=1

centralizes

if

but acts by inversion on

∎

5.4.2.
Other Linear and Unitary Groups

Throughout this subsection, we let
G={\mathrm{SL}}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
,
\widetilde{G}:={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
,
S=\operatorname{PSL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
, and
\widetilde{S}:={\mathrm{PGL}}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
. We will write
\widetilde{P}\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(\widetilde{G})
,
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(G)
with
P=\widetilde{P}\cap G
,
\widetilde{Q}\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(\widetilde{S})
with
\widetilde{Q}=\widetilde{P}{\bf Z}(\widetilde{G})/{\bf Z}(\widetilde{G})

The situation for

will be useful for the remaining classical groups, so we next discuss this case.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
\widetilde{P}
be a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
.

If
3\mid(q-\epsilon)
, then
[\widetilde{P}:\widetilde{P}^{\prime}]=9
if and only if one of the following holds:

•

n=1

(q-\epsilon)_{3}=9
; or

•

n\in\{2,3\}
and
(q-\epsilon)_{3}=3
.

3\mid(q+\epsilon)

[\widetilde{P}:\widetilde{P}^{\prime}]=9
if and only if one of the following holds:

and
(q+\epsilon)_{3}=9

4\leq n\leq 7
and
(q+\epsilon)_{3}=3

First, suppose

. Let

3
-adic decomposition of

. Then by
[
We55
]

is at least

\widetilde{P}_{0}\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(C_{q-\epsilon})

Again by
[

in this iterated wreath product.

is generated by at most two elements forces that each

with

Now suppose

. Then letting

again shows

Proposition 5.13
.

Let

and let

be a Sylow

-subgroup of

From the discussion in the proof of Proposition

Lemma 5.14

and let

First, assume

, so that

, and hence

since it has size

. We now induct on

, where

\widetilde{P}_{i}^{\prime}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\widetilde{P}_{i-1}^{3}\mid x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\in\widetilde{P}_{i-1}^{\prime}\}

Further, we can write
Z=\langle z\rangle
where

with

and

such that

by induction and we conclude

.

Now, suppose that
n=a_{0}+a_{1}3+a_{2}3^{2}+\cdots+a_{t}3^{t}=\sum a_{i}3^{i}
is the
3
-adic expansion of
n
, and let
j
be the smallest index such that

. Then

is generated by some

with

for each

such that

. That is,
\zeta I_{n}\leq\prod_{i=0}^{t}Z_{3^{i}}^{a_{i}}
, where
Z_{3^{i}}
is a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
{\bf Z}({\mathrm{SL}}_{3^{i}}^{\epsilon}(q))
. By above, we have
Z_{3^{i}}\leq\widetilde{P}_{i}^{\prime}
, so we have
Z\leq\prod(\widetilde{P}_{i}^{\prime})^{a_{i}}=\widetilde{P}^{\prime}
as desired.
∎

Corollary 5.15
.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
Q
be a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
S=\operatorname{PSL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
with
3\mid(q-\epsilon)
. Then
[Q:Q^{\prime}]=9
if and only if
n=3
or if
n=4
and
(q-\epsilon)_{3}=3
.

Proof.

Let
G={\mathrm{SL}}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
and write
Z:={\bf Z}(G)
. Let
Z_{3}\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(Z)
. Then
Q=PZ/Z\cong P/Z_{3}
with
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(G)
. We have
[Q:Q^{\prime}]=[P/Z_{3}:P^{\prime}Z_{3}/Z_{3}]=[P:P^{\prime}Z_{3}]
. If
Z_{3}\leq P^{\prime}
, we are done by Proposition
5.13
. Write
n=\sum a_{i}3^{i}
and
(q-\epsilon)_{3}=3^{b}
as before. If
n
is not a power of
3
, then
\sum a_{i}\geq 2
and we have
[Q:Q^{\prime}]\geq 3^{m-b}\geq 3^{\sum ia_{i}}
, where
m
is as in the proof of Proposition
5.13
. For
n>9
, we have
\sum ia_{i}\geq 3
. Hence we are left to consider the cases
n=6
and
n=3^{i}
.
If
n=3
, we saw in the proof of Lemma
5.14
that
Z_{3}\leq P^{\prime}
. If
n=6
, then we again have
Z_{3}\leq P^{\prime}
, since
P\cong P_{3}\times P_{3}
, and we have
Z_{3}\leq P_{3}^{\prime}\times P_{3}^{\prime}\leq P^{\prime}
.

So let
n=3^{i}\geq 9
. By Lemma
5.14
we have
P^{\prime}Z_{3}\leq\widetilde{P}^{\prime}
. Then exactly as in the proof of Proposition
5.13
, we see
[P:P^{\prime}Z_{3}]
is divisible by
3^{m}
, and we reduce to the same cases:
n=3
or
n=9
.
Since
Z_{3}\leq P^{\prime}
when
n=3
, this case follows from Proposition
5.13
. When
n=9
, we see again that
P/Z_{3}
has a minimal generating set of size three, so
[Q:Q^{\prime}]\neq 9
.
∎

Corollary 5.16
.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
S=\operatorname{PSL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
with
3\mid(n,q-\epsilon)
. Let
S\leq A\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)
such that
|A/S|_{3}=3
and
A
contains a diagonal automorphism of order
3
. Let
R\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(A)
with
[R:R^{\prime}]=9
. Then
n=3
.

Proof.

We may write
R=(P\langle\delta\rangle)/Z\leq\widetilde{P}/Z
, where
P
and
\widetilde{P}
are Sylow
3
-subgroups of
G
and
\widetilde{G}
as before,
\delta
is a diagonal automorphism of order
3
, and
Z\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}({\bf Z}(G))
. Recall from Lemma
5.14
that
Z\leq\widetilde{P}^{\prime}
. Further, notice that
(P\langle\delta\rangle)^{\prime}\leq(P\langle\delta\rangle)^{\prime}Z\leq\widetilde{P}^{\prime}\leq P\leq P\langle\delta\rangle\leq\widetilde{P}
, so that
[R:R^{\prime}]=[P\langle\delta\rangle:(P\langle\delta\rangle)^{\prime}Z]\geq[P\langle\delta\rangle:\widetilde{P}^{\prime}]=3^{1-b}[\widetilde{P}:\widetilde{P}^{\prime}]\geq 3\prod_{i=0}^{t}3^{ia_{i}}
, where the last equality is from the calculations in the proof of Proposition
5.12
. Then if
[R:R^{\prime}]=9
, we must have
\sum_{i}ia_{i}=1
, and hence
n\in\{3,4,5\}
. Then since
3\mid n
, we must have
n=3
as claimed.
∎

5.4.3.
The Remaining Cases

We now summarize (when combined with Propositions
5.10
and
5.11
) when
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
holds for simple groups of Lie type in non-defining characteristic.

Proposition 5.17
.

Let
S
be a simple group of Lie type defined in characteristic

and let

. Assume that
S\not\in\{\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q),\operatorname{PSL}_{3}^{\epsilon}(q)\}
. Then
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
if and only if

is one of:

(i)

\operatorname{PSL}_{4}^{\epsilon}(q)
when

;

(ii)

\operatorname{PSL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
with

and

(iii)

{\mathrm{PSp}}_{2n}(q)
or
{\mathrm{P\Omega}}_{2n+1}(q)

and

;

(iv)

{\mathrm{P\Omega}}_{8}^{-}(q)

;

(v)

\operatorname{G}_{2}(q)
,
{}^{3}\!\operatorname{D}_{4}(q)
, or
{}^{2}\!\operatorname{F}_{4}(q^{2})^{\prime}
.

In (ii)-(v), we further have
\widetilde{S}A=\widetilde{S}{\bf C}_{A}(P)
if

with

.

Proof.

First let
S=\operatorname{PSL}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
. If

, we are done by Proposition
5.15
. So assume

. Then

can be identified with a Sylow
3
-subgroup of

and the statement about

is Proposition
5.12
.

Now suppose that

is of one of the remaining classical types, so that

with

,
{\mathrm{P\Omega}}_{2n+1}(q)
with

, or
{\mathrm{P\Omega}}_{2n}^{\pm}(q)
with

. In these cases,

can be identified with a Sylow
3
-subgroup of the corresponding symplectic or orthogonal group
{\mathrm{Sp}}_{2n}(q)
,
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n+1}(q)
, or
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}^{\pm}(q)
. Weir
[
We55
]
has again described the Sylow subgroups in these cases. In the first two cases,

can be identified with a Sylow
3
-subgroup of

if

and of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2n}(q)
if

. The Sylow

-subgroups of

{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n+1}(q)
or
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n-1}(q)
. Note that in the latter situation when

, the order polynomials show that

is in
{\mathrm{SO}}_{7}(q)
in the case
-
and
{\mathrm{SO}}_{9}(q)
in the case

. Together with Proposition
5.12
, this now gives the first statement for the classical groups. Arguments just like in Proposition
5.10
and using the explicit structure of

yield that
\widetilde{S}A=\widetilde{S}{\bf C}_{A}(P)
in cases (ii)-(iv).

Now let
S
be an exceptional group of Lie type, including
{}^{3}\!\operatorname{D}_{4}(q)
and the Ree groups
{}^{2}\!\operatorname{F}_{4}(q^{2})^{\prime}
. (Recall that the orders of the Suzuki groups
{}^{2}\!\operatorname{B}_{2}(q^{2})
are not divisible by

and that the Ree groups
{}^{2}\!\operatorname{G}_{2}(q^{2})
are defined in characteristic
3
.) If
S=\operatorname{G}_{2}(q)
and
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
such that
3\mid(q-\epsilon)
, then we have
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(S)
is isomorphic to a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{3}^{\epsilon}(q)
. Similarly,
when
S={}^{3}\!\operatorname{D}_{4}(q)
, we see from
[
DZ87
, Prop. 2.2]
that
P
is an extension of a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{3}^{\epsilon}(q)
by a diagonal automorphism of order three, where
3\mid(q-\epsilon)
.
Next, let
S={}^{2}\!\operatorname{F}_{4}(q^{2})^{\prime}
, with
q^{2}=2^{2a+1}
. Note that

. Here by
[
Mal91
, Main Thm.]
,
S
has a maximal subgroup
{\mathrm{SU}}_{3}(q^{2}):2
and
P
is isomorphic to a Sylow
3
-subgroup of

. Then in all of these cases,

by Proposition
5.11
. Now, note that in these cases

is generated by field automorphisms. Further, recalling from the proof of Proposition
5.11
that either
F_{0}
or
\tau F_{0}
centralizes

, we obtain that

in these cases.

Now consider the remaining cases

,
\operatorname{E}_{6}^{\epsilon}(q)
,
\operatorname{E}_{7}(q)
, and
\operatorname{E}_{8}(q)
and let

be as in our setting

. If

with

we may identify

with a Sylow
3
-subgroup
\hat{P}
of

. If

with

, we have

, where

and

, Thm. 4.10.2]

-torus

In these cases, the relative Weyl groups are listed in

∎

Finally, we complete the picture when

.

Proposition 5.18
.

Let
S
be a simple group of Lie type defined in characteristic distinct from
3
such that

is not
\operatorname{PSL}_{3}^{\epsilon}(q)
nor
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)
. Let

be an almost simple group

. Let

. Then

.

Proof.

First, note that

has outer automorphism of order

, so is not being considered here. Then we may let

where

with

simple of simply connected type, and let

if

is a Frobenius map. If

(with

), we make the slight alteration on

according to
[
GM20
, 3.5.3]
. Let

and
\hat{P}_{1}\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(G)

with

. Then as before,
\hat{P}_{1}
is of the form

[

, Thm. 4.10.2]
, where

-torus

First, assume that

, where

is a field automorphism of order
3
(in this case

) or

and

and normalizes

.
Then we calculate that

is cyclic of size

. However, since

Then we are left with the case that

or
\operatorname{E}_{6}^{\epsilon}(q)
with

.

. If

and

for some prime power

, then
[

Finally, suppose that

with

. If

is not of the form

is either a Sylow
3
-subgroup

of the adjoint version

is a diagonal automorphism of order

induced by
\widetilde{S}/S

is an index-3 subgroup of

[
GLS98

NS23
, 5.2]
.) Now let

has index 9 in
\widetilde{P}\langle F_{q_{0}}\rangle

contains

∎

5.5.

In this section, we let

be a power of

Corollary 5.20

p=3
.
If
P
is abelian then
P\cong C_{9},C_{3}\times C_{3}
. In either case
k_{0}(B_{0})=k(B_{0})\in\{6,9\}
by work of Dade and of Sambale (see
[
Sam14
, Thm. 8.6 and Cor. 8.11]
, for instance).
We may thus assume that
P
is not abelian. In particular
P^{\prime}=\Phi(P)
.

Arguing by induction on
|G|
we may assume that
{\bf O}_{3^{\prime}}(G)=1
by
[
Nav98
, Thm. 9.9.(c)]
. We can also assume that
{\bf N}_{G}(P)<G
, as otherwise the group
G
would be
p
-solvable and by Fong’s Theorem (
[
Nav98
, Thm. 10.20]
)
k_{0}(B_{0})=k_{0}(B_{0}(G/P^{\prime}))=k(G/P^{\prime})
. Note that the group
G/P^{\prime}
has abelian Sylow
3
-subgroup.

We want to show that
also
{\bf O}_{3}(G)
may be assumed to be trivial.
Otherwise, let
N
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
contained in
{\bf O}_{3}(G)
. Then
N<P
.
Notice that
(P/N)^{\prime}=P^{\prime}N/N=\Phi(P)N/N=\Phi(P/N)
. As
[P/N:P^{\prime}N/N]
divides
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
and
P/N>1
we conclude that either
P^{\prime}\leq N
and
|P/N|=3
or
N\leq P^{\prime}=\Phi(P)
.

In the case where
|P/N|=3
then
N={\bf O}_{3}(G)
and
P\cap P^{g}=N
whenever
g\in G\setminus{\bf N}_{G}(P)
. By An–Eaton (see
[
AE05
, Cor. 1.6]
or
[
Sam14
, Thm. 7.17]
)
k_{0}(B_{0}({\bf N}_{G}(P)))=k_{0}(B_{0})
and we are done by induction.
In the case where
N\leq P^{\prime}
, by induction
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))\in\{6,9\}
. We claim that
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))=k_{0}(B_{0})
. Let
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0})
. Then
\chi_{P}
contains some linear character
\lambda
of
P
. Since
\lambda_{N}=1_{N}
, we conclude that
\chi
lies over
1_{N}
and
N\subseteq{\rm ker}(\chi)
. By Theorem
2.4
,
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))
.

We have shown that if
N
is a minimal normal subgroup of
G
, then
N
is semisimple of order divisible by
p
. If
G
is almost simple, write
S={\rm Soc}(G)
. Recall that
P^{\prime}=\Phi(P)
. By
[
NRSV21
, Lem. 2.2]
we have that either
[G:S]_{3}=1
or
[G:S]_{3}=3
and we are done by Theorem
3.3
. So we may assume that
G
is not almost simple. In this case,
[
NS23
, Thm. 3.2 and 3.3]
yields
k_{0}(B_{0})\leq 9
. As we know that
3
divides
k_{0}(B_{0})
(by
[
NST18
, Lem. 2.2]
, for instance) and
k_{0}(B_{0})>3
by
[
NST18
, Thm. C]
, we are done in this case.
∎

We continue by analyzing the structure of
P/P^{\prime}
for groups
G
satisfying
k_{0}(B_{0}(G))=6
and
P\in{\rm Syl}_{3}(G)
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group with
k_{0}(B_{0}(G))=6
, where

B_{0}
is the principal
3
-block. Then
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
where
P\in{\rm Syl}_{3}(G)
.

Proof.

Write
p=3
and
B_{0}=B_{0}(G)
. We proceed by induction on
|G|
.

Working by induction, we may assume that
{\rm\textbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
by
[
Nav98
, Thm. 9.9.(c)]
. We may also assume that
P
is not normal in
G
. Indeed, if
P\triangleleft G
, then
G
is
p
-solvable and since
{\rm\textbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
we have that
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))={\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)={\rm Irr}(G/P^{\prime})
. Analysing the structure of the finite groups with an abelian normal Sylow 3-subgroup and 6 conjugacy classes, we would arrive at
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
.

Let
N
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
. Then
p
divides the order of
N
.

(1) Suppose first that
N
is an elementary abelian 3-group. Notice that
P/N>1
as
{\bf N}_{G}(P)<G
, so
3\mid k_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))\leq 6
by Theorem
2.6
. Write
\bar{B}_{0}=B_{0}(G/N)
. The only options are then
k_{0}(\bar{B}_{0})\in\{3,6\}
.

(1.a) Suppose that
k_{0}(\bar{B}_{0})=3
, then
|P/N|=3
by
[
NST18
, Thm. C]
. Notice that
N={\bf O}_{p}(G)
and that if
g\in G\setminus{\bf N}_{G}(P)
then
P\cap P^{g}=N
. By
[
AE05
, Cor. 1.5]
(see also
[
Sam14
, Thm. 7.17]
), the block
B_{0}
satisfies the Alperin–McKay conjecture. In particular,
k_{0}(B_{0}({\bf N}_{G}(P)))=6
and we are done by induction (as
P
is not normal in
G
).

(1.b) Suppose that
k_{0}(\bar{B}_{0})=6
. Then
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))={\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G/N))
, so every height zero character in
B_{0}(G)
lies over
1_{N}
. By induction
[P/N:(P/N)^{\prime}]=9
. Notice that
(P/N)^{\prime}=P^{\prime}N/N
. We want to show that actually
N\subseteq P^{\prime}
and hence
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
.
Given any linear character
\lambda\in{\rm Irr}(P)
, we have that
\tau=\lambda_{N}\in{\rm Irr}(N)
. By Lemma
2.3
there is some
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0})
of height zero lying over
\tau
. Then
\tau=1_{N}
. Therefore
N
is contained in the kernel of every linear character of
P
, giving us

N\subseteq\bigcap_{\lambda\in{\rm Lin}(P)}{\rm ker}(\lambda)=P^{\prime},

as wanted.

(2) Suppose now that
N
is semisimple, and write
N=S_{1}\times S_{2}\times\cdots\times S_{t}
, where
S_{i}\cong S
are nonabelian simple of order divisible by 3. Since
p=3
, we have that
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))\in\{1,3,6\}
again by Theorem
2.6
.

(2.a) Suppose first that
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))=1
. Then 3 does not divide
[G:N]
and
P\leq N
.
Also, since
{\rm\textbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
, we have that
{\bf C}_{G}(N)=1
.
By the Frattini argument, we have that
G=N{\bf N}_{G}(P)
, so
M=N{\bf C}_{G}(P)
is normal in
G
. By Lemma
2.1
we have that
B_{0}(G)
is the only block covering
B_{0}(M)
. In particular, since
3
does not divide
[G:M]
, we have that
{\rm Irr}(G/M)\subseteq{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))
. More generally
{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)\subseteq{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0})
for every
\theta
of height zero in the principal block of
M
.
If
t=1
then
G
is almost simple and we are done by Theorem
3.3
. Hence we may assume that
t\geq 2
.
By Theorem
3.1
(b), the set of degrees of
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(N))
, has size at least 6.
Notice that for every
\psi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(M))
, there is
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
lying over it and hence the set of character degrees of characters in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(M))\setminus\{1_{M}\}
has size at most
6-k(G/M)
. By Theorem
2.2
so has the set of character degrees of characters in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(N))\setminus\{1_{N}\}
. These facts force
M=G
. Then again by Theorem
2.2
we have that
6=k_{0}(B_{0}(N))=k_{0}(B_{0}(S))^{t}
, absurd as
t\geq 2
.

(2.b) Suppose now that
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))=3
. Then
|PN/N|=3
. We first claim that
{\bf C}_{G}(N)=1
. Indeed, consider
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/{\bf C}_{G}(N)))
. As
[G:{\bf C}_{G}(N)]
is divisible by 3, we have that
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/{\bf C}_{G}(N)))\in\{3,6\}
by Theorem
2.6
. If
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/{\bf C}_{G}(N)))=3
then
[G:{\bf C}_{G}(N)]_{3}=3
by
[
NST18
, Thm. C]
and hence
|N|_{3}=3
, which implies
|G|_{3}=9
, and the result follows. Finally, suppose that
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/{\bf C}_{G}(N)))=6
, so all the characters in
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))
lie over
1_{{\bf C}_{G}(N)}
. Since
[G:N]_{3}=3
and 3 divides
|{\bf C}_{G}(N)|
, we necessarily have that 3 does not divide
[G:N{\bf C}_{G}(N)]
. Now take
\varphi\in{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}({\bf C}_{G}(N)))
nontrivial and consider
1_{N}\times\varphi\in{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(N\times{\bf C}_{G}(N)))
, then there is
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))
lying over
\varphi
, a contradiction.

We now claim that
P
(and hence
PN/N
) acts transitively on
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{t}\}
. In particular,
t\in\{1,3\}
and by Theorem
3.3
, that implies
t=3
.
Suppose that the action of
P
on
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{t}\}
is not transitive and write
N=R\times T
, where
R
is the product of one
P
-orbit.
Since
p
divides
k_{0}(B_{0}(R))
, then necessarily
p
divides the size of the set
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime},P}(B_{0}(R))
of
P
-invariant characters of height zero in
{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(R))
.
As
1_{R}\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime},P}(B_{0}(R))
, there exists
1_{R}\neq\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(R))
that is
P
-invariant. Analogously, let
1_{T}\neq\xi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(T))
be
P
-invariant. We have that
1_{R}\times\xi
and
\theta\times\xi
are
P
-invariant, so they extend to
PN
using
[
Isa76
, Cor. 8.16]
. By Theorem
2.7
, there are at least 3 characters in
{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))
of height zero above them. As
1_{R}\times\xi
and
\theta\times\xi
are not
G
-conjugate, this implies that
k_{0}(B_{0}(G))\geq 9
, a contradiction.

Therefore
N=S_{1}\times S_{2}\times S_{3}
,
|PN/N|=3
and
P
acts transitively on
\{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}\}
.
We claim that from every character
\varphi
in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(S))
we can construct a
P
-invariant character
\theta
in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(N))
with
\theta(1)=\varphi(1)^{3}
. Indeed, if
xN
generates
PN/N
, then
xN
seen as an element of
{\rm Out}(N)
acts on characters of
N
like
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\sigma
with
\alpha_{i}\in{\rm Out}(S)
and
\sigma\in{S}_{3}
of order
3
, satisfying
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{\sigma(1)}\alpha_{\sigma(2)}=1
. The character
\theta=\varphi\times\varphi^{\alpha_{1}}\times\varphi^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{\sigma(1)}}
is
P
-invariant whenever
\varphi
is a character of
S
, and hence it does extend to
PN
using
[
Isa76
, Cor. 8.16]
.
Now, by Theorem
2.7
, there are at least
3
characters in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)
.
With this method, and using Theorem
3.1
(b), we can construct at least
2
non-trivial
P
-invariant characters
\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(N))
with different degrees, but again that is not possible.

(2.c) Finally suppose that
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))=6
, thus every irreducible character in
{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))
lies over
1_{N}
. Since 3 divides
k_{0}(B_{0}(N))
, we have that there are at least 3 irreducible characters in
{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(N))
fixed by
P
. Let
\varphi
one of them, so
\varphi
extends to
PN
, using
[
Isa76
, Cor. 8.16]
. Then by Lemma
2.3
there exists
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))
lying over
\varphi
, a contradiction.
∎

We now finish the proof of Theorem A.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p=3
and
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
. Suppose that
k_{0}(B_{0}(G))=9
, then
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
|G|
.
Working as in the proof of Theorem
6.2
, we may assume that
{\rm\textbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
and
{\bf N}_{G}(P)<G
. Write
B_{0}=B_{0}(G)
.

(1) We next want to prove that we may also assume that
{\rm\textbf{O}}_{p}(G)=1
. Otherwise, let

N
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
contained in
{\rm\textbf{O}}_{p}(G)
.
Notice that
p
divides
|G/N|
as otherwise
N=P\triangleleft G
. By Theorem
2.6
,
3
divides
k_{0}(\bar{B_{0}})=k_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))\leq k_{0}(B_{0})=9
. The only possibilities are then
k_{0}(\bar{B_{0}})\in\{3,6,9\}
.

(1.a) Suppose that
k_{0}(\bar{B_{0}})=9
. In this case,
{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))={\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))
, so every character of height zero in
B_{0}(G)
lies over
1_{N}
.
Moreover, by induction,
[P:P^{\prime}N]=9
. We claim that
N\subseteq P^{\prime}
. Let
\lambda\in{\rm Lin}(P)
. Then
\theta=\lambda_{N}\in{\rm Irr}(N)
extends to
\lambda
.
By Lemma
2.3
we have that there exists
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))
over
\theta
. Hence
\theta=1_{N}
. Then

N\subseteq\bigcap_{\lambda\in{\rm Lin}(P)}{\rm ker}(\lambda)=P^{\prime},

as wanted. Therefore
[P:P^{\prime}]=[P:P^{\prime}N]=9
and we are done.

(1.b) Suppose that
k_{0}(\bar{B_{0}})=6
. In this case, by Theorem
6.2
,
[P:P^{\prime}N]=9
. We work to prove that
N\subseteq P^{\prime}
.
Let
\lambda\in{\rm Lin}(P)
. If
\theta=\lambda_{N}\neq 1_{N}
, then by Lemma
2.3
some
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0})
lies over
\theta
.
By Theorem
2.7
, we have that
3
divides
k_{0}(B_{0}|\theta)
. In fact,
k_{0}(B_{0}|\theta)=3
as
k_{0}(B_{0})=9
and
k_{0}(\bar{B}_{0})=6
. Moreover
k_{0}(B_{0}|\theta)=k_{0}(B_{0}(G_{\theta})|\theta)
, by the Clifford correspondence and the Third Main Theorem (
[
Nav98
, Thm. 6.7]
).
By Lemma
2.5
, we have that
\theta
extends to some
\psi\in{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G_{\theta}))
. Then, the equality
k_{0}(B_{0}(G_{\theta})|\theta)=k_{0}(B_{0}(G_{\theta}/N))
follows from Gallagher’s Theorem and
[
NV17
, Lem. 5.1]
. But

k_{0}(B_{0}(G_{\theta}/N))>3
by
[
NST18
, Thm. C]
because
[P/N:(P/N)^{\prime}]=9
, a contradiction.
Hence
\theta=1_{N}
, and we conclude
N\subseteq P^{\prime}
, as in (1.a). Then
[P:P^{\prime}]=[P:P^{\prime}N]=9
and we are done.

(1.c) Suppose that
k_{0}(\bar{B_{0}})=3
. Then
|P/N|=3
. Recall that
P
is not normal in
G
, so
N={\rm\textbf{O}}_{p}(G)
. Let
g\in G\setminus{\bf N}_{G}(P)
. Then
P\cap P^{g}=N
. Again, by Corollary 1.5 of
[
AE05
]
, the Alperin–McKay conjecture holds for
B_{0}(G)
. Hence
k_{0}(B_{0}({\bf N}_{G}(P)))=9
. Since
{\bf N}_{G}(P)<G
, we are done by induction.

(2) Now let
N
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
. By (1),
N
is a semisimple group of order divisible by
p
. Then
N=S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{t}
, where
S_{i}\cong S
are transitively permuted by the action of
G
, and
S
is a simple non-abelian group of order divisible by
p
. We now analyse the different possibilities for
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))\in\{1,3,6,9\}
.

(2.a)
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))=1
. In this case
(|G/N|,p)=1
and
P\subseteq N
. Since
{\rm\textbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
, we have that
{\bf C}_{G}(N)=1
, so we may assume that
t>1
by Theorem
3.3
. Consider
M=N{\bf C}_{G}(P)
. Then
M
is normal in
G
, by the Frattini argument, and
B_{0}
is the only block covering
B_{0}(M)
by Lemma
2.1
. In particular
k(G/M)\leq 8
. Notice that by Theorem
3.1
(b) and using that
t\geq 2
we obtain that there are at least 6 different character degrees of irreducible characters in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(N))
.
Using Theorem
2.2
, there are at least 6 different character degrees of irreducible characters in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(M))
too. Since every irreducible character in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(M))
lies under an irreducible character in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
we have that the set of character degrees of irreducible characters in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(M))\setminus\{1_{M}\}
is at most
9-k(G/M)
. This, together with the fact that
3
does not divide
[G:M]
, forces
k(G/M)\in\{1,2,4\}
.

(2.a.i) If
k(G/M)=1
then
G=M
, then by Theorem
2.2
,
9=k_{0}(B_{0}(N))=k_{0}(B_{0}(S))^{t}
. Hence either
t=1
and we are done by Theorem
3.3
or
t=2
and
k_{0}(B_{0}(S))=3
. By
[
NST18
, Thm. C]
, in the latter case,
|S|_{3}=3
and then
|P|=9
as wanted.

(2.a.ii) If
k(G/M)=2
then
[G:M]=2
, and then
k_{0}(B_{0}(S))^{t}=k_{0}(B_{0}(N))=k_{0}(B_{0}(M))\leq 1+2\cdot 7=15
. The above inequalities force
t=2
and
k_{0}(B_{0}(S))=3
. Then
|P|=9
by
[
NST18
, Thm. C]
.

(2.a.iii) Finally suppose that
k(G/M)=4
. In this case, since there are at least 5
G
-orbits in
{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(M))\setminus\{1_{M}\}
, we have that every element of
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(M))\setminus\{1_{M}\}
has exactly one element of
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
above it (and there are exactly 5
G
-orbits in
{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(M))\setminus\{1_{M}\}
). Since
{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))
is the unique block covering
{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(M))
this means that all the nontrivial elements in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(M))
are fully ramified in their stabilizers. This means that, if
\varphi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(M))
then
[G_{\varphi}:M]
is a square. By analyzing the structure of groups with 4 conjugacy classes we conclude that either
[G:M]=4
or
[G:M]=10
and
G_{\varphi}=M
for all
\varphi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(M))
. If
[G:M]=4
, then
k_{0}(B_{0}(S))^{t}=k_{0}(B_{0}(N))=k_{0}(B_{0}(M))\leq 1+4\cdot 5=21
and then
t=2
and
k_{0}(B_{0}(S))=3
, as wanted. If
[G:M]=10
, we have that
k_{0}(B_{0}(S))^{t}=k_{0}(B_{0}(N))=k_{0}(B_{0}(M))=1+10\cdot 5=51
which forces
t=1
, contradiction.

(2.b)
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))=9
. In this case every height zero character in the principal block of
G
contains
N
in its kernel. But that is absurd. Indeed, the set
{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(N))
has size divisible by
3
. In particular, also the set of
P
-invariant characters in
{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(N))
has size divisible by 3. Thus, we can choose a
P
-invariant
1_{N}\neq\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(N))
. By
[
Isa76
, Cor. 8.16]
,
\theta
extends to
PN
. By Lemma
2.3
, there would be some
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{0}(B_{0})
lying over
\theta
, so not containing
N
in its kernel.

We have shown that if
N
is a minimal normal subgroup of
G
then,
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))\in\{3,6\}
.

(2.c) If
k_{0}(B_{0}(G/N))=3
, then
[PN:N]=3
by
[
NST18
, Thm. C]
.
We claim that
N
is the only minimal normal subgroup of
G
. Once this statement is proven, we can assume that
{\bf C}_{G}(N)=1
. Suppose that
M
is another minimal normal subgroup of
G
. Since
3
divides
|M|
we have that
3
divides
k_{0}(B_{0}(M))
. Write
K=NM\cong N\times M
. Since
[G:N]_{3}=3
and
3
divides
|M|
we have that
[G:K]
is not divisible by 3 and
|M|_{3}=3
. Hence
P\leq K
.
Let
L=K{\bf C}_{G}(P)
which is normal in
G
by the Frattini argument. By the Alperin–Dade correspondence (Theorem
2.2
), there are the same number of
G
-orbits in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(L))

as in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(K))={\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(N))\times{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(M))
. If
L=G
, then by Theorem
2.2
, we have that

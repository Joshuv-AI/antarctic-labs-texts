Abstract
. We study a mean field game system introduced by Chan and Sircar ( AMO , 2015) to model production of an exhaustible resource. In particular, we study the sensitivity of the solution with respect to a parameter ε \varepsilon , which measures the degree to which producers are interchangeable. We prove that on some interval [ 0 , ε 0 ] [0,\varepsilon_{0}] , where ε 0 > 0 \varepsilon_{0}>0 , the solution is infinitely differentiable with respect to ε \varepsilon . The result is based on a set of new a priori estimates for forward-backward systems of linear partial differential equations.

1.
Introduction

Consider the following system of nonlinear partial differential equations:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{lll}(i)&\displaystyle u_{t}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}u_{xx}-ru+F(t,u_{x},m;\varepsilon)^{2}=0,\text{\hskip 56.9055pt}&0\leq x<\infty,\,\,0\leq t\leq T\\
(ii)&\displaystyle m_{t}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}m_{xx}-\left[F(t,u_{x},m;\varepsilon)m\right]_{x}=0,&0\leq x<\infty,\,\,0\leq t\leq T\\
(iii)&\displaystyle m(x,0)=m_{0}(x),\text{\hskip 5.69046pt}u(x,T)=u_{T}(x),&0\leq x<\infty\\
(iv)&\displaystyle u(0,t)=m(0,t)=0,&0\leq t\leq T\end{array}\end{cases}

where the data consist of a parameter
\varepsilon\geq 0
, a time horizon
T>0
, a smooth probability density
m_{0}(x)
, a smooth function
u_{T}(x)
, and two positive constants
\sigma
and
r
, and where

F(t,u_{x},m;\varepsilon)
is given by

(1.2)

F(t,u_{x},m;\varepsilon)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{2+\varepsilon\xi(t)}+\frac{\varepsilon\xi(t)}{2+\varepsilon\xi(t)}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{x}(x,t)m(x,t)\dif x-u_{x}(x,t)\right)

for some fixed smooth function
\xi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}[0,T]\to[0,1]
such that
\xi(T)=0
.
We assume that
m_{0}
and
u_{T}
satisfy zero- and first-order compatibility conditions so that solutions of (
1.1
) are classical (see
[
17
]
).

System (
1.1
) models a particular kind of
mean field game
for the production of exhaustible resources, as proposed by Chan and Sircar in
[
8
]
, cf.
[
9
,
22
]
.
In this game, players control the rate at which they will sell from their current stock, and they must leave the game once that stock goes to zero.
The optimal strategies are determined by the market price.
In equilibrium, the market price is determined by the average of all the players’ strategies.
When the demand depends linearly on the production rate (or on the price offered), one can determine the equilibrium by solving System (
1.1
); in particular, if
(u,m)
is the solution, then
F(t,u_{x},m;\varepsilon)
gives the equilibrium rate of production and
m(x,t)
gives the density of players whose remaining stock is
x
at time
t
.
The parameter
\varepsilon
measures the degree to which firms compete.
If
\varepsilon=0
, every firm is a monopolist, while if
\varepsilon
is very large, all firms are nearly interchangeable in the eyes of consumers.

Existence and uniqueness of solutions to System (
1.1
) has been established under the assumptions we make in this article, thanks to the results found in
[
16
,
19
,
17
]
, cf.
[
18
,
15
]
.
In
[
8
]
, Chan and Sircar proposed a numerical method to solving (
1.1
) that consists of assuming the following Taylor expansion:

(1.3)

\begin{split}u(x,t)&=u^{(0)}(x,t)+\varepsilon u^{(1)}(x,t)+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}u^{(2)}(x,t)+\cdots,\\
m(x,t)&=m^{(0)}(x,t)+\varepsilon m^{(1)}(x,t)+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}m^{(2)}(x,t)+\cdots.\end{split}

Formally,
u^{(k)}
and
m^{(k)}
can be derived by differentiating System (
1.1
)
k
times with respect to
\varepsilon
, letting
\varepsilon=0
, and then solving.
Notice that setting
\varepsilon=0
decouples the system.
Thus
u^{(k)}
and
m^{(k)}
are computed by solving two equations separately, avoiding the computational difficulties arising from the forward/backward-in-time coupling.

To establish rigorously the accuracy of this numerical method, one must answer the question: is the solution to System (
1.1
) differentiable with respect to
\varepsilon
?
In the present article, we provide an affirmative answer to this question under generic assumptions on the data.
In particular, we establish that there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
small enough such that for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the solution to System (
1.1
) is
k
times differentiable with respect to
\varepsilon
for
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
.
See Theorem
1.1
below.
More precisely, we establish that the difference between the solution and its
k
th order Taylor expansion is bounded by
C_{k}\varepsilon^{k+1}
for some constant
C_{k}
.
On the other hand, the constants
C_{k}
that appear in our analysis blow up very fast as
k\to\infty
, and for this reason we conjecture that the infinite series expansion does not in fact converge (see Remark
4.1
).

While the numerical method of Chan and Sircar gives a practical application, our work is also motivated by the theory of forward-backward linear systems of equations, which turn out to be of fundamental importance in mean field game theory.
In their seminal work
[
3
]
, Cardaliaguet et al. show that one can established well-posedness of the
master equation
by the following steps: (1) solve the mean field game system, (2) linearize the system by formally differentiating with respect to the measure variable
m
, and, most crucially, (3) proving
estimates on solutions to linearized systems
, which have as a corollary that the solution from step 1 is differentiable with respect to the initial measure.
We do not study the master equation in this article.
However, our sensitivity analysis follows exactly the same steps, where instead of linearizing with respect to an infinite-dimensional measure variable, we differentiate with respect to
\varepsilon
.
At an abstract level, the system of linear equations we study has the form

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{lll}(i)&\displaystyle w_{t}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}w_{xx}-rw+\Psi+F^{(0)}(\varepsilon)\big(G(t,w_{x},\mu;\varepsilon)-w_{x}\big)=0,\text{\hskip 14.22636pt}&0\leq x<\infty,\,\,0\leq t\leq T\\
(ii)&\displaystyle\mu_{t}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\mu_{xx}-\big(F^{(0)}\mu\big)_{x}=\Big[\Phi+\frac{1}{2}\big(G(t,w_{x},\mu;\varepsilon)-w_{x}\big)m^{(0)}\Big]_{x},&0\leq x<\infty,\,\,0\leq t\leq T\\
(iii)&\displaystyle\mu(x,0)=0,\text{\hskip 5.69046pt}w(x,T)=0,&0\leq x<\infty\\
(iv)&\displaystyle w(0,t)=\mu(0,t)=0,&0\leq t\leq T,\end{array}\end{cases}

where
F^{(0)},u^{(0)},m^{(0)},\Psi,
and
\Phi
are given functions, and

(1.5)

G(w_{x},\mu;\varepsilon)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\beta\big(\varepsilon\xi(t)\big)\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(u_{x}^{(0)}\mu+m^{(0)}w_{x}\Big)\dif x.

with
\beta
a known function such that
\beta(\varepsilon)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Our
main mathematical contribution
in this article is a set of a priori estimates on solutions to System (
1.4
).
(See Section
2
.)
The estimates share three types in common with
[
3
, Section 3.3]
:
(1) “energy estimates,” derived by expanding
\tfrac{\dif{}}{\dif{t}}\left\langle w,\mu\right\rangle
and using the duality between equations (i) and (ii);
(2) standard Schauder estimates on parabolic equations;
and (3) estimates on
\mu
in the space dual to a certain Hölder space.
Since the coupling depends on integral terms involving the unknowns, the energy estimates are considerably more technical than for standard mean field games.
In particular, they require a fourth type of estimate, namely (4) estimates on
\int_{0}^{T}\mathinner{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(x,t)\dif x\right)}^{2}\dif t
; see Section
2.1
.
Additionally, because the boundary conditions in our model are of Dirichlet type, the estimates on
\mu
in the dual of a Hölder space require additional care compared to similar estimates on a torus; see Section
2.3
.
All of our results hold in one space dimension, on which the problem was originally posed by Chan and Sircar (cf.
[
22
]
).
In higher space dimensions, the Dirichlet boundary conditions pose an additional difficulty for the purposes of regularity, since there would be a corner in the domain, although the interior estimates are expected to hold as in the one-dimensional case.
This is a technical aspect we do not address in the present work.

Mean field games were introduced circa 2006 by Lasry and Lions
[
27
]
and by Caines, Huang, and Malhamé
[
23
]
.
Since then, both theory and applications have been well-studied.
For a general exposition, we refer the reader to the texts
[
2
,
5
,
6
]
.
Economic models are a common application of mean field game theory.
See e.g. the overviews in
[
1
,
28
,
13
]
, and for the particular example of exhaustible resource production see
[
8
,
9
,
22
]
.
When the equilibrium strategy depends on the distribution of controls, as is often the case for economics applications, we give the name
mean field game of the controls

[
4
]
(or
extended mean field games

[
12
]
) to the resulting mathematical model.
The theory of partial differential equations for mean field games of controls has been developed in
[
4
,
25
,
24
,
14
,
12
,
20
]
.
The present work is, to the best of our knowledge, the first study of parameter sensitivity for a mean field game of controls.
Some of the estimates presented here will be useful in a forthcoming study of the master equation for a mean field game of controls with absorbing boundary conditions
[
21
]
.

).
In Section

Notation

) will denote the usual Lebesgue space on a domain
D

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{L^{p}(D)}

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{p}

We will often consider the space-time domain

such that the map

The norm is given by

(1.6)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert t\mapsto\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f(\cdot,t)\right\rVert}_{q}\right\rVert}_{p}.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
the space
{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}={\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\right)}
denotes the space of Hölder continuous functions

(1.7)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{\infty}+\sup\mathinner{\left\{\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u(x)-u(y)\right\rvert}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}^{\alpha}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}x,y\geq 0,\ x\neq y\right\}}.

If
k
is an integer,
{\mathcal{C}}^{k+\alpha}={\mathcal{C}}^{k+\alpha}\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\right)}
denotes the space of

\tfrac{\dif{{}^{k}}u}{\dif{x^{k}}}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}
.
It is a Banach space with the norm

(1.8)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{k+\alpha}}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\dif{{}^{k}}u}{\dif{x^{k}}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{\infty},

which by standard interpolation results is equivalent to the norm
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\tfrac{\dif{{}^{k}}u}{\dif{x^{k}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\tfrac{\dif{{}^{j}}u}{\dif{x^{j}}}\right\rVert}_{\infty}
.

For
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
the space
{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\beta}={\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\beta}\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)},[0,T]\right)}
denotes the space of all Hölder continuous functions
u
such that the following norm is finite:

(1.9)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\beta}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{\infty}+\sup\mathinner{\left\{\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert u(x,t)-u(y,s)\right\rvert}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}^{\alpha}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert}^{\beta}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}x,y\geq 0,\ t,s\in[0,T],\ x\neq y,\ t\neq s\right\}}.

If

are integers, we can also define
{\mathcal{C}}^{j+\alpha,k+\beta}

In particular, the space
{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}
denotes the standard Hölder space for parabolic equations (cf.
[
26
]
);
its norm can be written

(1.10)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{\infty}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{t}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{{}^{2}}u}{\partial{x^{2}}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}.

(1.11)

\displaystyle f^{(k)}=f^{(k)}(x,t;\varepsilon)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial\varepsilon^{k}}\left[f(x,t;\varepsilon)\right],

with the convention that

.

In studying System (
1.4
),

1.2.
Statement of the main result

Throughout this paper, we assume that conditions hold on the data so that System (
1.1
) has a unique solution
(u,m)
satisfying
u,m\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Sufficient conditions are provided in
[
17
]
, cf.
[
16
]
.
We will denote this solution by
(u^{(0)},m^{(0)})
.
In addition to smoothness, we will also need to assume the initial density
m_{0}
has finite first moment
\int_{0}^{\infty}xm_{0}(x)\dif x<\infty
.

Formally, if we differentiate (
1.1
)
k
times with respect to
\varepsilon
, we obtain the system

(1.12)

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{lll}(i)&\displaystyle u_{t}^{(k)}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}u_{xx}^{(k)}-ru^{(k)}+J_{k}\big(x,t,u_{x}^{(k)},m^{(k)};\varepsilon\big)=0,&0\leq x<\infty,\text{\hskip 2.84544pt}0\leq t\leq T\\
(ii)&\displaystyle m_{t}^{(k)}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}m_{xx}^{(k)}-(K_{k})_{x}\big(x,t,u_{x}^{(k)},m^{(k)};\varepsilon\big)=0,&0\leq x<\infty,\text{\hskip 2.84544pt}0\leq t\leq T\\
(iii)&\displaystyle m^{(k)}(x,0)=0,\hskip 5.69046ptu^{(k)}(x,T)=0,&0\leq x<\infty\\
(iv)&\displaystyle u^{(k)}(0,t)=m^{(k)}(0,t)=0,&0\leq t\leq T,\end{array}\end{cases}

where

(1.13)

J_{k}\big(x,t,u_{x}^{(k)},m^{(k)};\varepsilon\big)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}F^{(j)}\big(x,t,u_{x}^{(j)},m^{(j)};\varepsilon\big)F^{(k-j)}\big(x,t,u_{x}^{(k-j)},m^{(k-j)};\varepsilon\big)

and

(1.14)

K_{k}\big(x,t,u_{x}^{(k)},m^{(k)};\varepsilon\big)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}F^{(j)}\big(x,t,u_{x}^{(j)},m^{(j)};\varepsilon\big)m^{(k-j)}(x,t).

For clarity, we note that the full expression for
F^{(k)}
is given by

(1.15)

F^{(k)}\big(x,t,u_{x}^{(k)},m^{(k)};\varepsilon\big)=\frac{1}{2}\xi(t)^{k}\alpha^{(k)}(\varepsilon\xi(t))-\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{(k)}(x,t)\\[4.0pt]
+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-i}\binom{k}{i}\binom{k-i}{j}\xi(t)^{i}\beta^{(i)}\big(\varepsilon\xi(t)\big)\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{x}^{(j)}(x,t)m^{(k-i-j)}(x,t)\dif x,

where

(1.16)

\alpha(\varepsilon)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{2}{2+\varepsilon}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\beta(\varepsilon)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2+\varepsilon},

so that for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
k\geq 1
,

(1.17)

\alpha^{(k)}(\varepsilon)=\frac{2(-1)^{k}k!}{(2+\varepsilon)^{k+1}}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\beta^{(k)}=-\alpha^{(k)}.

The reader may wonder why, in writing
F^{(k)}
,
J_{k}
, and
K_{k}
, we have suppressed the arguments
u^{(j)}
and
m^{(j)}
for
j<k
.
This is because we will be arguing inductively as follows: to prove that
u^{(k)}
and
m^{(k)}
exist, we may assume that
u^{(j)}
and
m^{(j)}
are known functions for

.

We now state our main result.

.

)) such that for each

and all

and

such that for

(1.19)

2.
A priori estimates

In this section we present our main mathematical contribution by proving new a priori estimates for a coupled forward-backward system of linear partial differential equations (
1.4
).
It turns out that a useful first step for such systems is to prove “energy estimates,” which are derived by expanding
\tfrac{\dif{}}{\dif{t}}\left\langle w,\mu\right\rangle
and using the duality between equations (i) and (ii).
However, because of the integral term
G
appearing in (
1.4
), the energy estimates are not useful without certain a priori estimates on the quantity
\int_{0}^{T}\mathinner{\left(\mu(x,t)\dif x\right)}^{2}\dif t
, where
\mu
is the solution to the Fokker-Planck type equation (
1.4
)(ii).
We derive these estimates first in Section
2.1
, which may have independent interest to the reader interested in Fokker-Planck equations with a source.
Section
2.2
then provides the desired energy estimates, which we apply throughout the rest of the section.
In section
2.3
we arrive at further estimates for the Fokker-Planck equation in the dual to a Hölder space.
Finally, in Section
2.4
we give a priori estimates that establish full parabolic regularity.

2.1.
A priori estimates on Fokker-Planck equations with a source

In this subsection, we collect some estimates on the Fokker-Planck equation (
1.4
)(ii), in particular with respect to the
L^{1}
norm and first moment in the space variable.
These results have very little to do with the particular structure of the coupled system (
1.4

The proofs of these results are given in Appendix

Proposition 2.3

C_{1}\in(0,\infty)

C_{2}\in(0,\infty)

\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}

\sigma

r

T

(2.7)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}|w_{x}|^{2}m^{(0)}\dif x\dif t\leq C_{1}^{\prime}\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L_{x}^{1})}\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert w_{x}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}+C_{2}^{\prime}\Big(\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L_{x}^{1})}^{2}+\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Psi\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}\Big),

Proof.

We begin by observing

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\dif}{\dif t}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rt}w\mu\dif x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rt}\Big(\big(w_{t}-rw\big)\mu-w\mu_{t}\Big)\dif x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rt}\Bigg\{\mu\Big(\frac{-\sigma^{2}}{2}w_{xx}-F^{(0)}(G-w_{x})-\Psi\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+w\Big(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\mu_{xx}+\big[F^{(0)}\mu\big]_{x}+\Big[\Phi+\frac{1}{2}(G-w_{x})m^{(0)}\Big]_{x}\Big)\Bigg\}\dif x

\displaystyle=-e^{-rt}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(F^{(0)}G\mu+\Psi\mu+\Phi w_{x}+\frac{1}{2}w_{x}(G-w_{x})m^{(0)}\Big)\dif x.

The first equality follows via differentiation under the integral sign, the second equality follows from substituting in the equations for
w_{t}-rw
and
\mu_{t}
given by System (
1.4
), and the last equality follows via integration by parts.
Next, unpacking
G
, (
2.8
) implies

(2.9)

\displaystyle-\frac{\dif}{\dif t}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rt}w\mu\dif x

\displaystyle=e^{-rt}\beta(\varepsilon\xi)\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}F^{(0)}\mu\dif x\Bigg)\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(w_{x}m^{(0)}+u_{x}^{(0)}\mu\big)\dif x\Bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+e^{-rt}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\Psi\mu+\Phi w_{x}\big)\dif x-\frac{1}{2}e^{-rt}\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}^{2}m^{(0)}\dif x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}e^{-rt}\beta(\varepsilon\xi)\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}m^{(0)}\dif x\Bigg)\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(w_{x}m^{(0)}+u_{x}^{(0)}\mu\big)\dif x\Bigg).

We then have the following three estimates. First,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}F^{(0)}\mu

\displaystyle\dif x\Bigg)\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(w_{x}m^{(0)}+u_{x}^{(0)}\mu\big)\dif x\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}F^{(0)}\mu\dif x\Bigg)\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}m^{(0)}\dif x\Bigg)+\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}F^{(0)}\mu\dif x\Bigg)\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{x}^{(0)}\mu\dif x\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}F^{(0)}\mu\dif x\Bigg)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}m^{(0)}\dif x\Bigg)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{x}^{(0)}\mu\dif x\Bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert F^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}\Big)\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mu|\dif x\Bigg)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}^{2}m^{(0)}\dif x,

where the first inequality follows by two applications of Young’s inequality, and the last inequality via the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
Second, let
\delta>0

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\Psi\mu+\Phi w_{x}\big)\dif x

\displaystyle\leq\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Psi\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mu|\dif x+\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{x}^{1}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert w_{x}\right\rVert}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\delta}+\frac{\delta}{2}\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Psi\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mu|\dif x\Bigg)^{2}+\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{x}^{1}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert w_{x}\right\rVert}_{\infty}.

The last inequality above follows from a generalized Young’s inequality.
Third and last,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}m^{(0)}

\displaystyle\dif x\Bigg)\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(w_{x}m^{(0)}+u_{x}^{(0)}\mu\big)\dif x\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}m^{(0)}\dif x\Bigg)^{2}+\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}m^{(0)}\dif x\Bigg)\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{x}^{(0)}\mu\dif x\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}^{2}m^{(0)}\dif x+\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}m^{(0)}\dif x\Bigg)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{x}^{(0)}\mu\dif x\Bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\dif}{\dif t}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rt}w\mu\dif x

To make future calculations less cluttered define

Now, because

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big(1-\frac{5}{2}\beta(\varepsilon\xi)\Big)e^{-rt}\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}^{2}m^{(0)}\dif x\dif t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}f(t)e^{-rt}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mu|\dif x\Bigg)^{2}\dif t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}e^{-rt}\Big(\frac{1}{\delta}+2\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{x}^{1}}\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert w_{x}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}\Big)\dif t,

\displaystyle w_{x}^{2}m^{(0)}\dif x\dif t

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{1}C_{1}\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L_{x}^{1})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}e^{-rt}\Big(\frac{1}{\delta}+2\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{x}^{1}}\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert w_{x}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}\Big)\dif t,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{-rs}f(s)\dif s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}|u_{x}^{(0)}\mu|\dif x+2\int_{0}^{\infty}|w_{x}|m^{(0)}\dif x\Bigg)^{2}

3.

For completeness, we provide a statement of the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem, taken from
[

Theorem 3.1

(The Leray Schauder Fixed Point Theorem)
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be a Banach space, and let
{\mathcal{T}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\mathcal{X}}\times[0,1]\to{\mathcal{X}}
be a compact mapping satisfying
{\mathcal{T}}(x,0)=0
for all
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
.
Suppose there exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

(3.1)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{X}}}\leq C\,\,\text{ for all }\,\,(x,\lambda)\in{\mathcal{X}}\times[0,1]\,\text{ satisfying }{\mathcal{T}}(x,\lambda)=x.

Then the mapping
{\mathcal{T}}_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
, given by
{\mathcal{T}}_{1}(x)={\mathcal{T}}(x,1)
, has a fixed point.

We now aim to construct a suitable Banach space along with a compact operator to satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1/6]
such that
u^{(0)},m^{(0)}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2+2\alpha,1+\alpha}

Define the Banach space
{\mathcal{X}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}={\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}\cap L_{t}^{\infty}(L_{x}^{1})\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha/2}\mathinner{\left([0,T];({\mathcal{C}}^{1+2\alpha})^{*}\right)}
with norm

(3.2)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{X}}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L_{x}^{1})}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha/2}\mathinner{\left([0,T];({\mathcal{C}}^{1+2\alpha})^{*}\right)}}.

Let
\Psi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}
and
\Phi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\alpha,\alpha/2}\cap L_{t}^{\infty}(L_{x}^{1})
be given functions with
x\Phi\in L_{t}^{\infty}(L_{x}^{1})
and
\Psi(0,T)=0
.
For any

and
\lambda\in[0,1]
, let
\mu={\mathcal{T}}(m,\lambda)
be determined by solving the system

and
\nu\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\Phi+\frac{1}{2}\big(G(\varepsilon)-w_{x}\big)m^{(0)}
.
We start with the following formula: for every
t_{1}\geq t_{0}\geq 0
,

(A.30)

\begin{split}&\mu(x,t_{1})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\left(S(x-y,t_{1}-t_{0})-S(x+y,t_{1}-t_{0})\right)}\mu(y,t_{0})\dif y\\[4.0pt]
&\quad+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}S}{\partial{x}}}(x-y,t_{1}-s)+\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}S}{\partial{x}}}(x+y,t_{1}-s)\right)}\mathinner{\left(b(y,s)\mu(y,s)+\nu(y,s)\right)}\dif y\dif s,\end{split}

Using the same calculations as in (
A.6
), we get

(A.31)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mu(x,t_{1})\right\rvert}\dif x\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mu(y,t_{0})\right\rvert}\dif y+c^{\prime}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(t_{1}-s)^{-1/2}\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert b\right\rVert}_{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mu(y,s)\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\nu(y,s)\right\rvert}\right)}\dif y\dif s,

where
c^{\prime}
is defined in (
A.5
).
Let

f(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathinner{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mu(x,t)\right\rvert}\dif x\right)}^{2}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,g(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathinner{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\nu(x,t)\right\rvert}\dif x\right)}^{2},

so we can write

(A.32)

f(t_{1})^{1/2}\leq f(t_{0})^{1/2}+c\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}(t_{1}-s)^{-1/2}\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert b\right\rVert}_{\infty}f(s)^{1/2}+g(s)^{1/2}\right)}\dif s.

By Hölder’s inequality, we get

(A.33)

\displaystyle f(t_{1})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq f(t_{0})^{1/2}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert b\right\rVert}_{\infty}c\mathinner{\left(2(t_{1}-t_{0})^{1/2}\right)}^{1/2}\mathinner{\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}(t_{1}-s)^{-1/2}f(s)\dif s\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+c\mathinner{\left(2(t_{1}-t_{0})^{1/2}\right)}^{1/2}\mathinner{\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}(t_{1}-s)^{-1/2}g(s)\dif s\right)}^{1/2}.

Next, we square both sides and use the inequality
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
to get

(A.34)

f(t_{1})\leq 2f(t_{0})+4c(1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert b\right\rVert}_{\infty})(t_{1}-t_{0})^{1/2}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}(t_{1}-s)^{-1/2}\mathinner{\left(f(s)+g(s)\right)}\dif s.

We now estimate

(A.35)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\Bigl\lvert\Phi+\frac{1}{2}\big(G(\varepsilon)-w_{x}\big)m^{(0)}\Bigr\rvert}\dif x\leq\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{x}^{1}}+2\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\bigl\lvert w_{x}m^{(0)}\bigr\rvert}\dif x+\int_{0}^{\infty}|u_{x}^{(0)}\mu|\dif x,

so that

(A.36)

\displaystyle g(t)

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{x}^{1}}^{2}+12\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}|w_{x}m^{(0)}|\dif x\Bigg)^{2}+3\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}|u_{x}^{(0)}\mu|\dif x\Bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{x}^{1}}^{2}+12\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}^{2}m^{(0)}\dif x+3\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}f(t).

The first inequality follows from the fact that
\big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\big)^{2}\leq n\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}^{2}
.
The second inequality follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the fact that
\int_{0}^{\infty}m^{(0)}\dif x\leq 1
.
Now, define

(A.37)

h(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{1}{1+3\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}}\Big(3\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L_{x}^{1})}+12\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}^{2}m^{(0)}\dif x\Big).

Since

1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert b\right\rVert}_{\infty}=1+\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert F^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}\leq 2\big(1+\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}\big),

we see that (
A.36
) and (
A.34
) yield

(A.38)

f(t_{1})\leq 2f(t_{0})+24c\big(1+\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}\big)^{3}(t_{1}-t_{0})^{1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}(t_{1}-s)^{1/2}\big(f(s)+h(s)\big),

upon factoring out
\big(1+3\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}\big)
, and estimating
\big(1+3\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}\big)\leq 3\big(1+\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}\big)^{2}
.
From (
A.38
), we see that Proposition
A.1
applies with
A\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=2
, and
B\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=24c\big(1+\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}\big)^{3}\delta^{1/2}
.

Now, choose
\delta>0
small enough such that

(A.39)

2\delta^{1/2}B<\frac{1}{4}\iff\delta<\frac{1}{192c\big(1+\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}\big)^{3}}.

Then choose
\lambda>\frac{1}{\delta}\ln(2)
large enough such that

(A.40)

1-Ae^{-\lambda\delta}>\frac{1}{2}\iff\lambda>\frac{2}{\delta}\ln(2)\iff\lambda>C_{0}.

As a result,

(A.41)

\frac{2\delta^{1/2}B}{1-Ae^{-\lambda\delta}}<\frac{1}{2},

and by Proposition
A.1
we obtain

(A.42)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda t}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mu|\dif x\Bigg)^{2}\dif t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{1+3\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda t}\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{x}^{1}}^{2}\dif t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{12}{1+3\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda t}\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}^{2}m^{(0)}\dif x\dif t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L_{x}^{1})}^{2}}{C_{0}\big(1+3\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}\big)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{12}{1+3\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert u_{x}^{(0)}\bigr\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda t}\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}^{2}m^{(0)}\dif x\dif t.

The second inequality is obtained by the simple estimate

(A.43)

\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda t}\dif t=\frac{1}{\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda T})<\frac{1}{\lambda}<\frac{1}{C_{0}}.

Thus, (
2.3
) holds.

Now, in the case that
T
is finite, we can estimate for all
\lambda\in(0,C_{0}]
,

(A.44)

e^{-\lambda t}=e^{(2C_{0}-\lambda)t}e^{-2C_{0}t}\leq e^{(2C_{0}-\lambda)T}e^{-2C_{0}t}.

Consequently, using (
2.3
) with
\lambda=2C_{0}
, we obtain

(A.45)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda t}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mu|\dif x\Bigg)^{2}\dif t

\displaystyle\leq e^{(2C_{0}-\lambda)T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2C_{0}t}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mu|\dif x\Bigg)^{2}\dif t

\displaystyle\leq e^{(2C_{0}-\lambda)T}\Bigg(\frac{C_{1}\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L_{x}^{1})}^{2}}{C_{0}}+C_{2}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2C_{0}t}\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}^{2}m^{(0)}\dif x\dif t\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\Phi\bigr\rVert}_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L_{x}^{1})}^{2}e^{2C_{0}T}}{C_{0}}+C_{2}e^{2C_{0}T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda t}\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{x}^{2}m^{(0)}\dif x\dif t,

whereby the last inequality follows, because
e^{-2C_{0}t}\leq e^{-\lambda t}
for all
t\in[0,T]
. This completes the proof.
∎

A.2.
An abstract Hölder estimate

In this subsection we will give an abstract result on Hölder regularity for a parabolic equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions and bounded coefficients.
Our argument is in the spirit of
[
3
, Lemma 3.2.2.]
, but we cover the case of an unbounded domain.
The result stated below is also meant to allow for a possibly infinite time horizon, though in the present work we will not exploit this.

Lemma A.2
.

Fix
0<\alpha<1
.
Let
u
be a solution of

(A.46)

\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{t}}}+\lambda u-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{{}^{2}}u}{\partial{x^{2}}}}+V(x,t)\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}}=F,\quad u(0,t)=0,\quad u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)

where
\lambda
is any positive constant,
V
and
F
are a bounded continuous functions, and
u_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\alpha}_{\diamond}({\mathcal{D}})
(i.e.
u_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\alpha}(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}})
and
u_{0}(0)=0
).
Then

(A.47)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}\mathinner{\left(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}}\times\mathinner{\left[0,T\right]}\right)}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}\mathinner{\left(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}}\times\mathinner{\left[0,T\right]}\right)}}\leq C\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty},\alpha,\lambda\right)}\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{\infty}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\alpha}}\right)},

where
C\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty},\alpha,\lambda\right)}
is independent of
T
.

As a corollary, we have

(A.48)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha/4}\mathinner{\left([0,T];{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\alpha/2}(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}})\right)}}\leq C\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty},\alpha,\lambda\right)}\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{\infty}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\alpha}}\right)}.

Proof.

We will start by assuming
u_{0}=0
.
By a standard application of the maximum principle we have that
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{0}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}
.
Let
\phi(x)
be a smooth function, and observe that

(A.49)

\dfrac{\partial{}(\phi u)}{\partial{t}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\dfrac{\partial{{}^{2}}(\phi u)}{\partial{x^{2}}}+\mathinner{\left(V+\frac{\sigma^{2}\phi^{\prime}}{\phi}\right)}\dfrac{\partial{}(\phi u)}{\partial{x}}+\lambda(\phi u)=g(x,t),

where

(A.50)

g(x,t)=\phi(x)F(x,t)+\mathinner{\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}\mathinner{\left(\phi^{\prime}(x)\right)}^{2}}{\phi(x)}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)+V(x,t)\phi^{\prime}(x)\right)}u(x,t).

Fix a time
\tau>0
, and set
v(x,t)=e^{\lambda(t-\tau)}\phi(x)u(x,t)
.
Then (
A.49
) becomes

(A.51)

\dfrac{\partial{}v}{\partial{t}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\dfrac{\partial{{}^{2}}v}{\partial{x^{2}}}+\mathinner{\left(V+\frac{\sigma^{2}\phi^{\prime}}{\phi}\right)}\dfrac{\partial{}v}{\partial{x}}=\tilde{g},

where

(A.52)

\tilde{g}(x,t)=e^{\lambda(t-\tau)}g(x,t).

Fix any
a>0
and let
\phi(x)=\mathinner{\left(1+(x-a)^{2}\right)}^{-1/2}
.
Note that
\phi
satisfies the following properties:

(A.53)

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{\phi}\right\rvert}\leq 1,\ \mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}\right\rvert}\leq 2,\quad\mathinner{\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi(x)\right\rvert}^{p}\dif x\right)}^{1/p}=\mathinner{\left(\frac{2p}{p-1}\right)}^{1/p}\ \forall p>1,

and also
\mathinner{\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{p\lambda(t-\tau)}\dif t\right)}^{1/p}\leq(\lambda p)^{-1/p}
.
It follows that

(A.54)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\tilde{g}\right\rVert}_{L^{p}({\mathcal{D}}\times(0,\tau))}\leq C(p)\lambda^{-1/p}\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}+\mathinner{\left(1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty}\right)}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{0}\right)}\leq C(p)\lambda^{-1/p}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}\mathinner{\left(1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty}\right)},

where
C(p)
remains bounded as
p\to\infty
.

Take
p
arbitrarily large.
By the potential estimates in
[
26
, Section IV.3]
we have an estimate of the form

(A.55)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}v}{\partial{t}}}\right\rVert}_{L^{p}({\mathcal{D}}\times(0,\tau))}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{{}^{2}}v}{\partial{x^{2}}}}\right\rVert}_{L^{p}({\mathcal{D}}\times(0,\tau))}\leq C\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\tilde{g}-\mathinner{\left(V+\frac{2\phi^{\prime}}{\phi}\right)}\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}v}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{L^{p}({\mathcal{D}}\times(0,\tau))}\\
\leq C(p)\lambda^{-1/p}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}\mathinner{\left(1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{0}\right)}+C\mathinner{\left(1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{0}\right)}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}v}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{L^{p}({\mathcal{D}}\times(0,\tau))},

where the constants do not depend on
\tau
.
On the other hand we have

(A.56)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}_{L^{p}({\mathcal{D}}\times(0,\tau))}\leq C(p)\lambda^{-1/p}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{0}\leq C(p)\lambda^{-1-1/p}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}.

By interpolation (see e.g.
[
26
, Lemma II.3.3]
) we have

(A.57)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}v}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{L^{p}({\mathcal{D}}\times(0,\tau))}\leq\delta\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}v}{\partial{t}}}\right\rVert}_{L^{p}({\mathcal{D}}\times(0,\tau))}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{{}^{2}}v}{\partial{x^{2}}}}\right\rVert}_{L^{p}({\mathcal{D}}\times(0,\tau))}\right)}+\frac{C}{\delta}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}_{L^{p}({\mathcal{D}}\times(0,\tau))}

for some constant
C
, where
\delta>0
is sufficiently small.
Choosing
\delta>0
small enough, we deduce

(A.58)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}v}{\partial{t}}}\right\rVert}_{L^{p}({\mathcal{D}}\times(0,\tau))}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{{}^{2}}v}{\partial{x^{2}}}}\right\rVert}_{L^{p}({\mathcal{D}}\times(0,\tau))}\leq C(p)\lambda^{-1/p}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}\mathinner{\left(1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty}\right)}+C\mathinner{\left(1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}\right)}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}_{L^{p}({\mathcal{D}}\times(0,\tau))}\\
\leq C(p)\lambda^{-1/p}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}\mathinner{\left(1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty}\right)}+C\mathinner{\left(1+C(p)\lambda^{-1-1/p}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}\right)}

Then by a Sobolev type embedding theorem
[
26
, Lemma II.3.3]
we have

(A.59)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}v}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}\leq C(p,\lambda)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}\mathinner{\left(1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}\right)}+C

for
\alpha=1-\frac{3}{p}
(assuming
p>3
).
We can rewrite this as

(A.60)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert e^{\lambda(\cdot-\tau)}\phi u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert e^{\lambda(\cdot-\tau)}\mathinner{\left(\phi^{\prime}u+\phi\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}}\right)}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}\leq C(p,\lambda)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}\mathinner{\left(1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}\right)}+C.

Since
t\mapsto e^{\lambda t}
is locally Lipschitz with constant depending on
\lambda
, we can write this as

(A.61)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}}\times[\tau-1,\tau])}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi^{\prime}u+\phi\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}}\times[\tau-1,\tau])}\leq C(p,\lambda)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}\mathinner{\left(1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}\right)}+C(\lambda).

Using the fact that
\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{\phi}
is bounded by 1 and is globally Lipschitz with
\Lip\mathinner{\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{\phi}\right)}=1
, we also have

(A.62)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi^{\prime}u+\phi\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi^{\prime}u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi^{\prime}u+\phi\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}},

hence

(A.63)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}}\times[\tau-1,\tau])}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}}\times[\tau-1,\tau])}\leq C(p,\lambda)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}\mathinner{\left(1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}\right)}+C(\lambda).

Now, since
\frac{1}{\phi}
is globally Lipschitz with
\Lip(1/\phi)=1
, and bounded on
[a-1,a+1]
with an upper bound of 2, we deduce that

(A.64)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}\mathinner{\left([a-1,a+1]\times[\tau-1,\tau]\right)}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}\mathinner{\left([a-1,a+1]\times[\tau-1,\tau]\right)}}\leq C(p,\lambda)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}\mathinner{\left(1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}\right)}+C(\lambda).

This estimate is independent of
\tau
and
a
.
Letting
\tau
and
a
vary through all the positive integers, and since
p
can be determined through
\alpha
, it follows that

(A.65)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}\mathinner{\left(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}}\times\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\right)}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}\mathinner{\left(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}}\times\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\right)}}\leq C(\alpha,\lambda)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}\mathinner{\left(1+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty}^{2}\right)}+C(\lambda).

We now remove the assumption that
u_{0}=0
.
Let
w
be the solution of

(A.66)

\dfrac{\partial{}w}{\partial{t}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\dfrac{\partial{{}^{2}}w}{\partial{x^{2}}}+\lambda w=0,\quad w(x,0)=u_{0}(x).

As
\lambda>0
, by the maximum principle, we have
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert w\right\rVert}_{0}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert}_{0}
.
Then
\mathinner{\left[w\right]}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}\leq C\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}}
by
[
26
, Theorem 10.1]
; this estimate does not depend on time because of the global in time
L^{\infty}
bound.
This establishes
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert w\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}\leq C\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}}
.
We then take the derivative in
x
of Equation (
A.66
) and apply the same argument as above to
\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}w}{\partial{x}}}
to establish
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}w}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}\leq C\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\alpha}}.

Then let
\hat{u}
be the solution of

(A.67)

\dfrac{\partial{}\hat{u}}{\partial{t}}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\dfrac{\partial{{}^{2}}\hat{u}}{\partial{x^{2}}}+V(x,t)\dfrac{\partial{}\hat{u}}{\partial{x}}+\lambda\hat{u}=F(x,t)-V(x,t)\dfrac{\partial{}w}{\partial{x}}

with zero initial conditions.
Then by (
A.65
) we have

(A.68)

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\hat{u}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}\mathinner{\left(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}}\times\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\right)}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}\hat{u}}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}\mathinner{\left(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}}\times\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha,\lambda,\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty})\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F+V\mathinner{\dfrac{\partial{}w}{\partial{x}}}\right\rVert}_{0}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty},\alpha,\lambda\right)}\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{0}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\alpha}}\right)}.

As
u=\hat{u}+w
is the solution to (
A.46
), the claim is proved.

Finally, to prove (
A.48
), note that (
A.47
) immediately implies

(A.69)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u(\cdot,t)\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\alpha/2}(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}})}\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u(\cdot,t)\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\alpha}(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}})}\leq C\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty},\alpha,\lambda\right)}\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{\infty}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\alpha}}\right)},

and we also have

(A.70)

\begin{split}\sup_{t\neq s}\sup_{x\neq y}&\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}(x,t)-\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}(x,s)-\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}(y,t)+\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}(y,s)\right\rvert}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert}^{\alpha/4}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}^{\alpha/2}}\\
&\leq\sup_{t\neq s}\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{2\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\dfrac{\partial{}u}{\partial{x}}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}\min\mathinner{\left\{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert}^{\alpha/2},\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}^{\alpha}\right\}}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert}^{\alpha/4}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}^{\alpha/2}}\\
&\leq C\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert V\right\rVert}_{\infty},\alpha,\lambda\right)}\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{\infty}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\alpha}}\right)}.\end{split}

Combine (
A.69
) and (
A.70
) to get (
A.48
).
∎

Appendix B
Nonlocal Existence Lemma

For this lemma, recall the definition
{\mathcal{X}}={\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}\cap L_{t}^{\infty}(L_{x}^{1})\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha/2}\mathinner{\left([0,T];({\mathcal{C}}^{1+2\alpha})^{*}\right)}

with norm

(B.1)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{X}}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L_{x}^{1})}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha/2}\mathinner{\left([0,T];({\mathcal{C}}^{1+2\alpha})^{*}\right)}}.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
be such that
u^{(0)},m^{(0)}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2+2\alpha,1+\alpha}
.
Let
m\in{\mathcal{X}}
,
u_{T}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha}(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)})
, and
\Psi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\times[0,T])
be given.
Consider the backward parabolic equation

(B.2)

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{lll}(i)&\displaystyle u_{t}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}u_{xx}-ru+\lambda\Psi+\lambda F^{(0)}(\varepsilon)\big(G(u_{x},m;\varepsilon)-u_{x}\big)=0,\text{\hskip 14.22636pt}&0\leq x<\infty,\,\,0\leq t\leq T\\
(ii)&\displaystyle u(x,T)=u_{T}(x),&0\leq x<\infty\\
(iii)&\displaystyle u(0,t)=0,&0\leq t\leq T,\end{array}\end{cases}

where
G
is defined as in (
1.5
).
We assume the usual compatibility conditions of first order:

(B.3)

u_{T}(0)=0,\quad\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}u_{T}^{\prime\prime}(0)+\lambda\Psi(0,T)-\lambda F^{(0)}(\epsilon)(0,0)u_{T}^{\prime}(0)=0.

Then there exists a unique classical solution
u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\times[0,T])
to the boundary value problem (
B.2
).
Moreover, the following estimate holds:

(B.4)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}}\leq C\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{X}}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Psi\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha/2,\alpha}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{T}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha}}\right)}

where
C
depends only on the data.

Proof.

Let
u\in C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\times[0,T])
be given.
Note that

(B.5)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert G(u_{x},m;\varepsilon)\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}\leq C\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert m\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha/2}\mathinner{\left([0,T];({\mathcal{C}}^{1+2\alpha})^{*}\right)}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}}\right)},

where
C
depends on the
{\mathcal{C}}^{2+2\alpha,1+\alpha}
norms of
u^{(0)}
and
m^{(0)}
.
By
[
26
, Theorem IV.5.2]
, there exists a unique solution
w\in C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\times[0,T])
the boundary value problem

(B.6)

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{lll}(i)&\displaystyle w_{t}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}w_{xx}-rw+\lambda\Psi+\lambda F^{(0)}(\varepsilon)\big(G(u_{x},m;\varepsilon)-w_{x}\big)=0,\text{\hskip 14.22636pt}&0\leq x<\infty,\,\,0\leq t\leq T\\
(ii)&\displaystyle w(x,T)=u_{T}(x),&0\leq x<\infty\\
(iii)&\displaystyle w(0,t)=0.&0\leq t\leq T,\end{array}\end{cases}

and it satisfies the estimate

(B.7)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert w\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}}\leq C\mathinner{\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Psi\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert G(u_{x},m;\varepsilon)\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{T}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha}}\right)}.

We will denote
w=F(u)
.
Our goal it to show that
F
is a contraction on a suitably defined metric space, and use this to prove (
B.2
) has a solution.

Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\times[0,T])
, then define
u=u_{1}-u_{2}
and
w=F(u_{1})-F(u_{2})
.
Note that
w
satisfies

(B.8)

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{lll}(i)&\displaystyle w_{t}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}w_{xx}-rw+\lambda F^{(0)}(\varepsilon)\big(G(u_{x},0;\varepsilon)-w_{x}\big)=0,\text{\hskip 14.22636pt}&0\leq x<\infty,\,\,0\leq t\leq T\\
(ii)&\displaystyle w(x,T)=0,&0\leq x<\infty\\
(iii)&\displaystyle w(0,t)=0.&0\leq t\leq T.\end{array}\end{cases}

Recalling the definition of
G
in (
1.5
), we see that there is a constant
C_{1}
, depending only on the data, such that

(B.9)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert G(u_{x},0;\varepsilon)\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}\leq C_{1}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{x}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}.

Combining this with classical estimates from
[
26
, Theorem IV.5.2]
, we see that there is some constant
C_{2}
, depending only on the data, such that

(B.10)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert w\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}}\leq C_{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{x}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}.

Using interpolation on Hölder spaces, we deduce that there exists a constant
C_{3}
, depending only on
C_{2}
, such that

(B.11)

C_{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v_{x}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}}+C_{3}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert v\right\rVert}_{\infty}\quad\forall v\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}.

Hence

(B.12)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert w\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}}+C_{3}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{\infty}.

Next, we define
\tilde{w}(x,t)=e^{r(T-t)}w\pm r^{-1}(e^{r(T-t)}-1)C_{1}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{x}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}
, which satisfies

(B.13)

\pm(-\tilde{w}_{t}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\tilde{w}_{xx}-\lambda F^{(0)}(\varepsilon)\tilde{w}_{x})\leq 0.

Apply the maximum principle to
\tilde{w}
to deduce that

(B.14)

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert w(x,t)\right\rvert}\leq r^{-1}(e^{r(T-t)}-1)C_{1}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{x}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}\leq(T-t)e^{rT}C_{1}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}}.

We now apply all of these estimates only on the time interval
[T-\tau,T]
for some
\tau>0
.
We deduce that

(B.15)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert w\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\times[T-\tau,T])}+2C_{3}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert w\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\times[T-\tau,T])}\\
\leq\mathinner{\left(\frac{1}{4}+2C_{3}\tau e^{rT}C_{1}\right)}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\times[T-\tau,T])}+C_{3}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\times[T-\tau,T])}.

We now set
\tau=\dfrac{1}{8C_{3}e^{rT}C_{1}}
.
Define
{\mathcal{Y}}_{\tau}
to be the space
{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\times[T-\tau,T])
endowed with the norm

(B.16)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert w\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{Y}}_{\tau}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert w\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\times[T-\tau,T])}+2C_{3}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert w\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}(\mathinner{\left[0,\infty\right)}\times[T-\tau,T])}.

Observe that
{\mathcal{Y}}_{\tau}
is a Banach space.
Moreover, by the above estimates,
F\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\tau}\to{\mathcal{Y}}_{\tau}
is a contraction, since
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert F(u_{1})-F(u_{2})\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{Y}}_{\tau}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert u_{1}-u_{2}\right\rVert}_{{\mathcal{Y}}_{\tau}}
.
Hence
F
has a unique fixed point
u
, which is a solution to (
B.2
) and satisfies the estimate (
B.4
) on the time interval
[T-\tau,T]
.
However,
T
is arbitrary.
We can now partition the interval
[0,T]
into subintervals that are each at most
\tau
in length, i.e.
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}=T
where
t_{j+1}-t_{j}\leq\tau
.
Apply the same argument on each subinterval
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, replacing the final condition
u_{T}(x)
with
w(x,t_{j})
, for each
j
starting with
N
and going down to 1.
(Cf. the proof of
[
10
, Proposition 3.11]
.)
In this way we obtain a solution
u
to Equation (
B.2
), which indeed satisfies (
B.4
).
Uniqueness of this solution follows from uniqueness on each subinterval.

∎

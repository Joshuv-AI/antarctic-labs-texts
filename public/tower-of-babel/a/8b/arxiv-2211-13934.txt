Spectral Invariance of Quasi-Banach Algebras of Matrices and Pseudodifferential Operators
Karlheinz Gröchenig, Christine Pfeuffer, Joachim Toft

Abstract
. We extend the stability and spectral invariance of convolution-dominated matrices to the case of quasi-Banach algebras p < 1 p<1 . As an application we construct a spectrally invariant quasi-Banach algebra of pseudodifferential operators with non-smooth symbols that generalize Sjöstrand’s results.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35P05, 35S05, 47G30

1.
Introduction

Spectral invariance is an important phenomenon
for applications in the field of partial differential equations and in the theory of pseudodifferential operators. The first result due
to Beals
[
6
]
asserts that the inverse of a pseudodifferential operator
T
that is (i) invertible on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and (ii) with a symbol in the Hörmander class
S^{0}_{0,0}
is again a pseudodifferential operator with
a symbol in the same class. In other words, this class of pseudodifferential operators is
inverse-closed (closed under inversion)
in the algebra
\mathcal{A}
of
pseudodifferential operators with
S^{0}_{0,0}
-symbols.
As a consequence
the spectrum of
T

is independent of
the weighted
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
space or of the choice of
B^{s,a}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, see
[
32
,
36
]
.
This phenomenon is often referred to as spectral
invariance, and the resemblance to Wiener’s lemma for absolutely
convergent Fourier series has also motivated the terminology of
\mathcal{A}

being a Wiener algebra.

The next important step in the theory of spectral invariance of pseudodifferential operators was made by Sjöstrand
[
38
]
who introduced a class of non-smooth
symbols for which the associated algebra of pseudodifferential operators is spectrally
invariant in
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. This class, nowadays called the
Sjöstrand class, turned out to be an already known function space
that is paramount in time-frequency analysis, namely the modulation space
M^{\infty,1}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
. This connection spawned an intensive investigation of pseudodifferential operators with
time-frequency methods
[
19
,
24
,
26
,
21
,
14
,
40
,
39
,
42
,
43
,
38
,
35
]
. Among the new
results obtained by time-frequency methods was, firstly, a characterization of the
Hörmander class and the Sjöstrand class by means of the matrix
associated to a pseudodifferential operator with respect to a Gabor frame. Secondly, time-frequency analysis established a firm connection between the off-diagonal decay of these
matrices and the corresponding properties (boundedness, algebra,
inverse-closedness) of pseudodifferential operators. In this way, every (solid) inverse-closed
subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}))
can be mapped to an algebra of
pseudodifferential operators that is inverse-closed in
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

[
24
]
. In
contrast to the classical hard analysis methods the time-frequency approach is
so flexible that the theory can even be formulated for pseudodifferential operators on
locally compact abelian groups
[
26
]
.

The goal of this paper is the extension of the theory of spectrally
invariant algebras of pseudodifferential operators to the realm of
quasi-Banach algebras. Quasi-Banach algebras are interesting in their
own right, but quasi-Banach spaces and associated operators occur naturally in approximation theory and data
compression problems, see e.g.
[
11
,
25
]
.
Additionally in time-frequency analysis they occur in the
formulation of uncertainty principles
[
16
]
.

To formulate our main results, we briefly recall the definition of
modulation spaces and the Weyl form of pseudodifferential operators.
For a fixed non-zero Schwartz function
g\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and a
tempered distribution
f
the

short-time Fourier transform

V_{g}f
is the function on

\mathbb{R}^{2d}
defined by the formula

\displaystyle V_{g}f(x,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-2\pi it\cdot\xi}dt\,

(1.1)

(with suitable interpretation of the integral). For

0<p,q\leq\infty
the (unweighted)
modulation space

M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by the quasi-norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V_{g}f(x,\xi)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{q/p}d\xi\right)^{1/q}

(1.2)

(with usual modifications in case
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
)
and consists of all tempered distributions
f
with finite
quasi-norm. Modulation spaces on
{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
serve as symbol classes for pseudodifferential operators.
Our focus will be on the symbol class
M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})

for
p_{0}<1
. This is only a quasi-Banach space.

Given a symbol
\mathfrak{a}
on
{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
, the corresponding
pseudodifferential operator in the Weyl calculus is defined formally as

\mathfrak{a}^{w}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathfrak{a}\big(\frac{x+y}{2},\xi)f(y)e^{2\pi i(x-y)\cdot\xi}\,dyd\xi\,,

(again with a suitable interpretation of the integral and for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
).

With these definitions our main results can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(Spectral invariance)
.

Let
p_{0}\in(0,1]
and
\mathfrak{a}\in M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})

be such that
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is invertible on

M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
p\in[p_{0},\infty]
.
Then
(\mathfrak{a}^{w})^{-1}=\mathfrak{b}^{w}
for some

\mathfrak{b}\in M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
.

As a consequence we obtain that the spectrum of a pseudodifferential operator with a
symbol in
M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
is independent of the space on
which it acts.

Theorem 1.2

(Spectral invariance on Modulation Spaces)
.

If
\mathfrak{a}\in M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
for
p_{0}\in(0,1]
and
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is invertible on
M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

for some
p\in[p_{0},\infty]
, then
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is also
invertible on
M^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
q\in[p_{0},\infty)
.

More generally, we show in Theorem
4.10
the invertibility of
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
on the more broad class of modulation spaces
M^{r,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
r,q\in[p_{0},\infty)
under the assumptions of the previous theorem.

Methods.
We follow the proof outline of
[
24
]
. The
first step is to study the matrix representation of a pseudodifferential operator with respect
to a Gabor frame and then derive a characterization of the symbol
class in terms of the off-diagonal decay of the associated
matrix.

In the second step this leads to the study of spectrally invariant matrix
algebras. The appropriate class in our context are
convolution-dominated matrices, i.e., matrices
A=(a_{\lambda,\rho})_{\lambda,\rho\in\Lambda}
with an off-diagonal
decay of the form

|a_{\lambda,\rho}|\leq H(\lambda-\rho)

for a (smooth) function
H
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It turns out that such matrix
classes are spectrally invariant in
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(\Lambda))
.
To offer a glimpse of this aspect, we formulate a very special case of
our main result on matrices that does not require technical details.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A=(a_{kl})_{k,l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
be a matrix over the index set
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Assume that there exists a sequence
h\in\ell^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
for

0<p_{0}\leq 1
, such that

|a_{kl}|\leq h(k-l)\qquad\text{ for all }k,l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,.

(i) Spectral invariance: If
A
is invertible on some
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
for
p\in[p_{0},\infty]
, then
A
is invertible on all
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
for
q\in[p_{0},\infty]
.

(ii) Spectral Stability: If for some
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
with
p\in[p_{0},\infty]
,
A
satisfies the stability condition

\|Ac\|_{p}\geq C\|c\|_{p}\text{ for all }c\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\,,

then
A
satisfies
\|Ac\|_{q}\geq C_{q}\|c\|_{q}\text{ for all }c\in\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
with
q\in[p_{0},\infty]
.

In our approach we follow Sjöstrand ingenious proof of
Wiener’s lemma for absolutely convergent Fourier series
[
38
]
and built on
the presentation in
[
22
]
. Our ultimate results on spectral
invariance and stability (Theorems
3.5
and

3.14
) are a significant extension
of the above preliminary statement and provide several new
facts of spectral invariance:

(i)

they yield both spectral stability and
spectral invariance,

(ii)

they are formulated with respect to arbitrary operator algebras
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p})
(not just
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2})
as is usually done) and

(iii)

they cover the general case of
quasi-Banach algebras, and

(iv)

in addition we treat arbitrary index sets and not just
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
or a discrete abelian group as in most references.

Technically, the study of quasi-Banach algebras of
convolution-dominated matrices is the main part of our paper, its
application to pseudodifferential operators is then based on our
analysis in
[
24
]
. Our proof contains some new features and
avoids the functional
calculus associated with the pseudo-inverse. These arguments may be
useful in other contexts as well.

Related results.

There
are numerous results on the spectral invariance of matrices, we mention
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
41
,
40
,
39
,
37
,
38
,
30
]
for a small
sample and
[
20
]
for a survey. As long as the index set is a
discrete abelian group, one can use
methods from harmonic analysis to
establish spectral invariance.
This line of thought goes back to
Bochner and Philipps and is used
in
[
3
,
4
,
10
,
8
]
and many others. All these
proofs break down, however, when
unstructured index sets are considered.

The extension of spectral invariance to
quasi-Banach algebras of matrices and operators over
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is the subject of
the recent papers
[
8
,
10
]
. While there is some
thematic overlap, not all results are directly comparable. On the one
hand, we
restrict our attention to unweighted
\ell^{p}
-spaces, whereas

[
8
,
10
]
include weights. On the other hand, these papers prove that
invertibility of convolution-dominated matrices on the Hilbert space

\ell^{2}
implies invertibility on all
\ell^{p}
for
p>p_{0}
. Our
results also provide a converse, namely that invertilibity on some

\ell^{p}
implies invertibility on
\ell^{2}
.

As for the application to pseudodifferential operators,
the Banach algebra case
p_{0}=1
for the symbols with invertibility on
M^{2,2}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
was already proved by
Sjöstrand
[
38
]
. The quasi-Banach algebra case
p_{0}<1
with
invertibility on
M^{2,2}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
was established in

[
8
]
based on the time-frequency methods developed in
[
24
]
. We
want to emphasize that the general case where we start with
invertibility on
M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),p\neq 2
, is much more involved, as there are no
Hilbert space techniques available. For self-adjoint pseudodifferential operators

(\mathfrak{a}^{w})^{*}=\mathfrak{a}^{w}
one could argue with duality and
interpolation to reduce to the case of invertibility on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, but there is
no cheap trick for non-self-adjoint pseudodifferential operators. When we start with
invertibility on a quasi-Banach space
M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),p<1
, even duality
is no longer useful. For this reason we disregarded the methods of
Bochner-Philipps as used in
[
10
,
24
,
8
]
for the construction
of spectrally invariant matrix algebras and instead exploit
Sjöstrand’s original proof of Wiener’s lemma in
[
38
]
.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2 we collect the
relevant definitions about sequence spaces and amalgam spaces. In
Section 3 we treat the spectral invariance of convolution-dominated
matrices. We treat both the stability of such matrices on the
quasi-Banach spaces
\ell^{p},p<1
and the spectral invariance of the
algebra of convolution-dominated matrices. The main results are
Theorems
3.5
and
3.14
. In
Section 4 we first recapitulate the definitions of modulation spaces
and Gabor frames and various calculi of pseudodifferential operators
and then prove our main theorems that are already stated in the
introduction. For completeness we have postponed some easy and known
proofs to the appendix.

2.
Preliminaries

For the convenience of the reader we now list up
the definitions of some function spaces and their
properties needed during this paper. We start with
the sequence spaces.

2.1.
Sequence space
\ell^{p}

For each
0<p\leq\infty
and discrete
set
J
, we recall that
the set
\ell^{p}(J)
consists of all
complex-valued sequences
a

such that

\displaystyle\|a\|_{p}:=\left(\sum_{j\in J}|a_{j}|^{p}\right)^{1/p},\qquad a=(a_{j})_{j\in J}

is finite (with usual modifications for

p=\infty
). Then
\ell^{p}(J)
is a quasi-Banach space with quasi-norm

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{p}
, which is even a norm
if
p\geq 1
.

We recall the following properties for

\ell^{p}(J)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
0<p\leq 1
,
J
is a discrete set,

\Lambda
is countable.
Let
a=(a_{j})_{j\in J}\in\ell^{p}(J)

and
b,b_{\lambda}\in\ell^{p}(J)
,

\lambda\in\Lambda
.
Then the following is true:

(i)

\|a\|_{1}^{p}\leq\|a\|_{p}^{p}
, or

|\sum_{j\in J}a_{j}|^{p}\leq\sum_{j\in J}|a_{j}|^{p}
;

(ii)

if
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|b_{\lambda}\|^{p}_{p}<\infty
, then

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}b_{\lambda}

is uniquely defined as an element in

\ell^{p}(J)
, and

\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}b_{\lambda}\|_{p}^{p}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|b_{\lambda}\|_{p}^{p}
;

(iii)

If
J=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, then also
\|a\ast b\|_{p}\leq\|a\|_{p}\,\|b\|_{p}
.

2.2.
Wiener Amalgam Space

Let
X=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
or
X=C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be
convex and compact with positive volume.
The Wiener Amalgam space

W(X,L^{p_{0}})
for
0<p_{0}\leq\infty

consists of all
f\in X
such that

\displaystyle\bigl\lVert f\bigr\rVert_{K,W(X,L^{p_{0}})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|f\|^{p_{0}}_{L^{\infty}(x+K)}\,dx\right)^{1/p_{0}}<\infty\,.

This is always a quasi-norm, and a norm, if
p_{0}\geq 1
(see e.g.

[
28
]
). For
p_{0}=\infty
we have
W(X,L^{\infty})=X
.
By compactness it follows that
W(X,L^{p_{0}})
is independent
of the choice of
K
, and different
K
yield equivalent
quasi-norms. For convenience we set

\bigl\lVert\,\cdot\,\bigr\rVert_{W(X,L^{p_{0}})}=\bigl\lVert\,\cdot\,\bigr\rVert_{B_{1}(0),W(X,L^{p_{0}})}
.

Remark 2.1
.

We observe that every continuous function with compact support is
contained in
W(C_{b},L^{p})
for all
p>0
and that
W(C_{b},L^{p})

is translation invariant
(see e. g.
[
7
]
and the
references therein).

The Wiener Amalgam space

W(C_{b},L^{p_{0}})
,
0<p_{0}<\infty
arises naturally in the formulation
of sampling inequalities. We first recall that a set
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}

is
relatively separated
, if

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{rel}}(\Lambda):=\sup\{\#(\Lambda\cap B_{1}(x)):x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}<\infty.

(2.1)

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0<p_{0}<\infty
,
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}

be relatively separated and let

H\in W(C_{b},L^{p_{0}})
.
Then
(H(\lambda))_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell^{p_{0}}(\Lambda)
.

Lemma
2.2

follows by straight-forward estimates. In order
to be self-contained, a proof of the result
is given in Appendix
A
.

3.
Spectral Invariance of Convolution-Dominated
Matrices

In this section we prove a spectral invariance result for infinite
dimensional convolution-dominated matrices. For
this we first list some needed auxiliary tools.

We always denote the conjugate
exponent of
p\in[1,\infty]

by
p^{\prime}=\tfrac{p}{p-1}
, so that
p^{\prime}\in[1,\infty]

and

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1.

We identify a matrix
A=(a_{\lambda,\rho})_{\lambda\in\Lambda,\rho\in\Pi}
indexed by
\Lambda
and
\Pi
with a linear operator

\displaystyle(Ab)_{\lambda}:=\sum_{\rho\in\Pi}a_{\lambda\rho}b_{\rho}\qquad\text{ for }b=(b_{\rho})_{\rho\in\Pi}\,.

Then
A
is always well-defined on finite sequences and
A
maps
finite sequences on
\Pi
to arbitrary sequences on
\Lambda
. Some boundedness properties of matrices on
\ell^{p}
-spaces are
given by Schur’s test.

Proposition 3.1

(Schur test for
p\geq 1
)
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
\Lambda

and
\Pi
be countable sets,

A=(a_{\lambda,\rho})_{\lambda\in\Lambda,\rho\in\Pi}\in\mathbb{C}^{\Lambda\times\Pi}
be a matrix satisfying

\sup_{\rho\in\Pi}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|a_{\lambda,\rho}|\leq K_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\sum_{\rho\in\Pi}|a_{\lambda,\rho}|\leq K_{2}\,.

(3.1)

Then
A
is a bounded operator from
\ell^{p}(\Pi)

to

\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
, and

\lVert A\rVert_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(\Pi),\ell^{p}(\Lambda))}\leq K_{1}^{1/p^{\prime}}K_{2}^{1/p}.

For a proof of Proposition

3.1
,
see e. g.
[
18
, Lemma 6.1.2]
.

Because of (
3.1
) we let

\|A\|_{\operatorname{Schur}}=\sup_{\rho\in\Pi}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|a_{\lambda,\rho}|+\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\sum_{\rho\in\Pi}|a_{\lambda,\rho}|,

(3.2)

for every
A\in\mathbb{C}^{\Lambda\times\Pi}
.

The following quasi-Banach space version of the Schur test just follows by using the triangle inequality.

Proposition 3.2

(Schur test for
p\leq 1
)
.

Fix
0<p\leq 1
. Let

A=(a_{\lambda,\rho})_{\lambda\in\Lambda,\rho\in\Pi}\in\mathbb{C}^{\Lambda\times\Pi}
be a
matrix satisfying

\|A\|_{S-p}:=\sup_{\rho\in\Pi}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|a_{\lambda,\rho}|^{p}<\infty\,.

(3.3)

Then
A
defines a bounded operator from
\ell^{p}(\Pi)
to
\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
. The operator norm is bounded by

\|A\|_{S-p}^{1/p}
.
If
0<q\leq p\leq 1
, then

\|A\|_{S-p}^{q/p}=\Big(\sup_{\rho\in\Pi}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|a_{\lambda,\rho}|^{p}\Big)^{q/p}\leq\sup_{\rho\in\Pi}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|a_{\lambda,\rho}|^{q}=\|A\|_{S-q}\,.

(3.4)

Our treatment of the spectral invariance of pseudodifferential
operators relies on
spectral invariance properties of associated convolution-dominated
matrices. Roughly speaking, a matrix

A\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda))
is
called
convolution-dominated
,
if there exists a function
H
,
such that

|a_{\lambda,\rho}|\leq H(\lambda-\rho)\qquad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,\rho\in\Pi\,.

(3.5)

The function
H
is then called
an
envelope
of
A
. By specifying a norm on envelopes, we
define a particular class of convolution-dominated
matrices as follows.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\Lambda,\Pi\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be
relatively separated and
0<p_{0}\leq 1
. The set

\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}=\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}(\Lambda,\Pi)

consists of all convolution-dominated matrices
A
such that
(
3.5
) holds for an envelope

H\in W(C_{b},L^{p_{0}})
.
For
A=(a_{\lambda,\rho})_{\lambda\in\Lambda,\rho\in\Pi}
we set

\displaystyle\|A\|_{\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}}=\inf\{\|H\|_{W(C_{b},L^{p_{0}})}:|a_{\lambda,\rho}|\leq H(\lambda-\rho),\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,\rho\in\Pi\}.

We set
\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}(\Lambda,\Lambda)
.
We observe that if
\Lambda=\Pi\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a lattice,
then the restriction of
H\in W(C_{b},L^{p_{0}})
to
\Lambda
belongs to

\ell^{p_{0}}(\Lambda)
due to Lemma

2.2
. In this case,
A\in\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}(\Lambda)
, if and only if there is a sequence
H\in\ell^{p_{0}}(\Lambda)
, such that
|A_{\lambda,\rho}|\leq H(\lambda-\rho)
for
all
\lambda,\rho\in\Lambda
.

We note that
\|A\|_{\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}}
is a quasi-norm for
p_{0}<1
, and

\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}
is a quasi-Banach
*
-algebra (sometimes called a

p
-algebra) with respect to addition and multiplication of
matrices. For
p_{0}=1
,
\mathcal{C}^{1}
is a Banach
*
-algebra with norm

\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}}
.

For convolution-dominated operators
in Definition
3.3

we state the following boundedness result.
Here we set
q^{\prime}=\infty
when
q\leq 1

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
0<p_{0}\leq 1
,
\Lambda
and
\Pi
be
as in Definition
3.3

and let

A\in\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}(\Lambda,\Pi)
.
Then
A
is bounded from
\ell^{q}(\Pi)
to

\ell^{q}(\Lambda)
for every

q\in[p_{0},\infty]
, and

\displaystyle\|A\|_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{q}(\Pi),\ell^{q}(\Lambda))}\leq C\operatorname{rel}(\Lambda)^{\frac{1}{q}}\operatorname{rel}(\Pi)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\|A\|_{\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}(\Lambda,\Pi)},

(3.6)

where the constant
C>0
only
depends on
d
.

The result follows by
suitable combinations of Hölder’s
and Young’s inequalities. In
order to be self-contained we present a
proof in Appendix
A
.

3.1.
Invariance of the lower bound property
on
\ell^{p}
of convolution-dominated matrices

Our main technical contribution is the so-called stability of
convolution-dominated matrices. By this is meant the invariance of the
lower bound property of such matrices on
\ell^{p}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
p_{0}\leq 1
,
q\in[p_{0},\infty]
,

\Lambda,\Pi\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be
relatively separated,
and
A\in\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}(\Lambda,\Pi)
.
Assume that there exists

p\in[p_{0},\infty]
and
C_{0}>0
such that

\lVert Ac\rVert_{p}\geq C_{0}\lVert c\rVert_{p},\qquad c\in\ell^{p}(\Pi)\,.

(3.7)

Then there exists a constant
C>0

which is independent of
q
such that

\lVert Ac\rVert_{q}\geq C\lVert c\rVert_{q}\qquad\text{ for all }c\in\ell^{q}(\Pi)\,.

(3.8)

In other words, if
A
in Theorem

3.5
is bounded from below on
some
\ell^{p}
with
p\geq p_{0}
, then
A

is bounded from below on
\ell^{q}
for all
q\in[p_{0},\infty]
. Note that (
3.7
) is equivalent to saying that

A
is one-to-one on
\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
with closed range in
\ell^{p}(\Pi)
. Thus if
A
is one-to-one with closed range for

some

p\in[p_{0},\infty]
, then it is one-to-one with closed range for

all

p\in[p_{0},\infty]
.

Note that by (
3.6
)
A
in Theorem
3.5

is bounded from
\ell^{p}(\Pi)

to
\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
for all
p\geq p_{0}
, hence the estimates (
3.7
)
and (
3.8
) really make sense.

The proof of Theorem
3.5
is

In the case, when

\ell^{q},q<p
, we need to refine our
arguments. We will show that
the inverse of
A
has an envelope belonging to the same Wiener amalgam space than the envelope of
A
. The main idea of the proof of the following Theorem is taken from
[
38
]
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
0<p_{0}\leq 1
,
\Lambda,\Pi\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be relatively
separated.
Suppose
A\in\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}(\Lambda,\Pi)
is invertible
from
\ell^{p}(\Pi)
to
\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
for some
p\in[p_{0},\infty]
.
Then
A^{-1}\in\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}(\Pi,\Lambda)
.

Proof.

By assumption the matrix

A=(a_{\lambda,\rho})_{\lambda\in\Lambda,\rho\in\Pi}

has an envelope
H_{1}\in W(C_{b},L^{p_{0}})
so that
|a_{\lambda,\rho}|\leq H_{1}(\lambda-\rho)
.
We need to prove that the matrix
A^{-1}
also has an envelope
H_{2}\in W(C_{b},L^{p_{0}})
.
For this we recall the notation used in the previous lemmas:
K=\max_{x}\Phi^{\varepsilon}(x)^{-\min(1,p)}
from Lemma
3.8
, and

\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon}=\begin{cases}\big((KV^{\varepsilon,p}_{j,k})^{p_{0}/p}\big)_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}},&p\leq 1,\\[4.30554pt]
\big((KV_{j,k}^{\varepsilon})^{p_{0}}\big)_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}},&p>1,\end{cases}

where
V^{\varepsilon,p}_{j,k}
and

V^{\varepsilon}_{j,k}
are defined as in
(
3.14
) and (
3.15
).

Given
c\in\ell^{p}(\Pi)
, the
sequences
a
and
a_{A}
are defined by

a_{k}=\|\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{k}c\|_{p}^{p_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad a_{A,k}=\|\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{k}Ac\|_{p}^{p_{0}},\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

With this notation Lemma
3.8
(and
additionally Lemma
2.1
if
p>1
) says that

a\leq a_{A}+\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon}a\,.

(3.29)

Furthermore, Lemma
3.11
can be reformulated
as saying that
\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon}
is convolution-dominated.
In fact,
recall from (
3.19
) that

\displaystyle\Delta^{\varepsilon,p_{0}}(s):=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|\varepsilon t-s|_{\infty}\leq 5}\sup_{z\in[0,1]^{d}+t}|H_{1}(z)|^{p_{0}}\,,

and define
\Psi^{\varepsilon}

by
\Psi^{\varepsilon}(s):=\Delta^{\varepsilon,p_{0}}(s)
for all
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
|s|>6\sqrt{d}

and for all
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
|s|\leq 6\sqrt{d}

by
\Psi^{\varepsilon}(s):=\left(\sup_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|V_{j,k}^{\varepsilon,p}|\right)^{p_{0}/p}

if
p\leq 1
and
by
\Psi^{\varepsilon}(s):=\left(\sup_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|V_{j,k}^{\varepsilon}|\right)^{p_{0}}
if
p\geq 1
. In view of the normalization
K
,

\tilde{V}^{\varepsilon}
possesses the
envelope
K^{p_{0}/\min(1,p)}\Psi^{\varepsilon}
.
This means that

\displaystyle(V^{\varepsilon,p}_{j,k})^{p_{0}/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Psi^{\varepsilon}(k-j),

\displaystyle\qquad p

\displaystyle\leq 1,\ j,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,,

(3.30)

and

\displaystyle V^{\varepsilon}_{j,k}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Psi^{\varepsilon}(k-j),

\displaystyle\qquad p

\displaystyle>1,\ j,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,.

(3.31)

Our next goal is to represent

(I-\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon})^{-1}

as a Neumann series.
For this we choose
\varepsilon>0
such that

\|\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon}\|_{S-1}\leq K^{p_{0}/\min(1,p)}\|\Psi^{\varepsilon}\|_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}.\,

(3.32)

This is possible due to Lemma
3.10
and Lemma

3.9
, since
\sharp\{s\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|s|\leq 6\sqrt{d}\}
is finite and depends only on the dimension
d
.

As a consequence the geometric series
\widetilde{W}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon}\right)^{k}
converges
in the
\|.\|_{S-1}
-norm and we obtain

(\mathrm{I}-\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon})^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon}\right)^{k}=\mathrm{I}+\widetilde{W}\,.

Since all entries of
\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon}
are non-negative by definition,

\widetilde{W}
also has only non-negative entries and preserves
(pointwise) inequalities. Moreover, since
\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon}
is
convolution-dominated, so is
\widetilde{W}
, and by (
3.32
) there
exists an envelope
W\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
such that

\widetilde{W}_{jk}\leq W(k-j)\qquad j,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,.

Now (
3.29
) yields

a=(\mathrm{I}+\widetilde{W})(\mathrm{I}-\widetilde{V}^{\varepsilon})a\leq(\mathrm{I}+\widetilde{W})a_{A}\,,

(3.33)

or entrywise

\|\varphi_{k}^{\varepsilon}c\|_{p}^{p_{0}}\leq\|\varphi_{k}^{\varepsilon}Ac\|_{p}^{p_{0}}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}W(k-j)\|\varphi_{j}^{\varepsilon}Ac\|_{p}^{p_{0}}\,.

(3.34)

Since
A
is assumed to be invertible as a map from

\ell^{p}(\Pi)
to
\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
, there
exist
b_{\lambda}\in\ell^{p}(\Pi)
such that
Ab_{\lambda}=\delta_{\lambda}
, whence the matrix
B
with entries
b_{\rho\lambda}=(b_{\lambda})_{\rho}
is the inverse of
A
. Using
b_{\lambda}
in
(
3.34
) we obtain

\|\varphi_{k}^{\varepsilon}b_{\lambda}\|_{p}^{p_{0}}\leq\|\varphi_{k}^{\varepsilon}\delta_{\lambda}\|_{p}^{p_{0}}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}W(k-j)\|\varphi_{j}^{\varepsilon}\delta_{\lambda}\|_{p}^{p_{0}}\,.

(3.35)

Note that

\|\varphi_{k}^{\varepsilon}\delta_{\lambda}\|_{p}^{p_{0}}=\big(\sum_{\rho\in\Lambda}\varphi(\varepsilon\rho-k)^{p}\delta_{\lambda}(\rho)\big)^{p_{0}/p}=\varphi(\varepsilon\lambda-k)^{p_{0}}\,.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
\rho\in\Pi
. For the off-diagonal decay it
suffices to consider only indices satisfying
\varepsilon|\lambda-\rho|>4
. Choose
k_{\rho}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that

|\varepsilon\rho-k_{\rho}|<2\quad\text{ and }\quad\varphi(\varepsilon\rho-k_{\rho})^{p_{0}}\geq c

for some constant
c
(in fact, by (
A.2
) in the proof of Lemma
3.6

c=\eta^{-1}
).
Then

|\varepsilon\lambda-k_{\rho}|\geq\varepsilon|\lambda-\rho|-|\varepsilon\rho-k_{\rho}|>2\,,

and consequently
\varphi(\varepsilon\lambda-k_{\rho})=0
in
(
3.35
) since
\text{supp}(\varphi)\subseteq B_{2}(0)
. Now (
3.35
) simplifies
to

c|(b_{\lambda})_{\rho}|^{p_{0}}\leq\|\varphi_{k_{\rho}}^{\varepsilon}b_{\lambda}\|_{p}^{p_{0}}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}W(k_{\rho}-j)\varphi(\varepsilon\lambda-j)^{p_{0}}\leq\sum_{j:|j-\varepsilon\lambda|<2}W(k_{\rho}-j)\,.

(3.36)

This inequality suggests the following envelope for
B=A^{-1}
. Let

H(x)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|l-\varepsilon x|<4}W(l).

(3.37)

To obtain a continuous envelope, we use a cut-off function
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying
0\leq\psi\leq 1
and
\psi(x)=1
for

|x|\leq 4
and set

\displaystyle\tilde{H}(x)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}W(l)\psi(\varepsilon x-l)\,.

Since
\psi
has compact support and
W(C_{b},\ell^{1})
is translation
invariant,
we have

\displaystyle\|\tilde{H}\|_{W(C_{b},\ell^{1})}\leq\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|W(l)|\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\psi(\varepsilon\cdot-l)\|_{W(C_{b},\ell^{1})}\lesssim\|W\|_{\ell^{1}}\,,

therefore
\tilde{H}\in W(C_{b},\ell^{1})
.

Furthermore, since
|k_{\rho}-j-\varepsilon(\lambda-\rho)|\leq|k_{\rho}-\varepsilon\rho|+|\varepsilon\lambda-j|<4
, (
3.37
) says that

|b_{\rho\lambda}|^{p_{0}}\leq H(\rho-\lambda)\leq\tilde{H}(\rho-\lambda)\,,

(3.38)

or

|b_{\rho\lambda}|=|(b_{\lambda})_{\rho}|\leq\tilde{H}(\rho-\lambda)^{1/p_{0}}\,,

and
\tilde{H}^{1/p_{0}}\in W(C_{b},L^{p_{0}})
, as claimed.

This theorem enables us to extend the spectral invariance
result of Corollary
3.13
to the case

p_{0}\leq q\leq p
as follows.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
p_{0}\in(0,1]
,
\Lambda,\Pi\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be relatively separated and

A\in C^{p_{0}}(\Lambda,\Pi)
be
invertible from
\ell^{p}(\Pi)
to
\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
for some
p\in[p_{0},\infty]
. Then
A
is invertible from
\ell^{q}(\Pi)
to
\ell^{q}(\Lambda)
for all
q\in[p_{0},\infty)
.

Proof.

According to Corollary

3.13
(ii)
A
is invertible on
\ell^{q}

for
q\geq p
and
A
is one-to-one on all

\ell^{q}
. So
it remains to prove that
A
is surjective for

p_{0}\leq q<p
. We assume that
A
is invertible
on
\ell^{p}
with inverse
A^{-1}
.
Hence for
u\in\ell^{q}(\Lambda)\subseteq\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
there is some
c\in\ell^{p}(\Pi)

with
c=A^{-1}u
.
By Theorem
3.14

A^{-1}
is bounded on

\ell^{q}
, consequently, since
u\in\ell^{q}
we have
c=A^{-1}u\in\ell^{q}
. Thus
A
is onto
\ell^{q}(\Lambda)
.

The following consequence explains why the statement of
Theorem
3.15
is referred to as the
spectral invariance property.

Corollary 3.16
.

Let
0<p_{0}\leq 1
be arbitrary. If
A\in\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}(\Lambda)
and
A
is invertible on

\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
for some
p\in[p_{0},\infty]
, then
A^{-1}\in\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}(\Lambda)
and

\displaystyle Sp_{\mathcal{B}(\ell^{q})}(A)=Sp_{\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}}(A)\qquad\text{ for all }q\in[p_{0},\infty),

where
Sp_{\mathcal{A}}(A)
denotes the spectrum of
A
in the algebra
\mathcal{A}
.

In the literature many variations of this spectral invariance result exist. For an overview of those variations we refer to
[
20
]
. Here we just want to mention the following ones:
In case
p_{0}=1
and
p=q=2
the previous theorem also holds in the weighted case. It was proved by Baskakov e.g. in
[
3
,
4
]
and by Sjöstrand
[
38
]
in the unweighted case.

4.
Spectral Invariance of Pseudodifferential Operators

The aim of this section is to transfer the results on the spectral
invariance of matrices to
pseudodifferential operators on unweighted modulation spaces.
In the proofs we mainly follow
[
19
]
and

[
24
]
and replace an arbitrary spectrally invariant Banach
algebra of matrices by the quasi-Banach algebra
\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}
.

First we
list up all needed definitions and properties to reach that aim. We
start with recalling the modulation spaces.

4.1.
Modulation Spaces
M^{p,q}
.

For the definition of those spaces we need the short-time Fourier
transform, which we recall now.

Let
g\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\setminus\{0\}
be fixed. For every

f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the

short-time Fourier transform

V_{g}f
is
the function on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
defined by the formula

\displaystyle V_{g}f(x,\xi):=\langle f,g(\cdot-x)e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot\,\cdot}\rangle.

(4.1)

Here
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
is the unique extension of the

L^{2}
scalar product on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

into
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We observe that
if
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
p\in[1,\infty]
,
then
V_{g}f
is given by (
1.1
).
If
g
and
f
are both defined on

\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, then
V_{g}f
is a function on

\mathbb{R}^{4d}
.

We recall that for

0<p,q\leq\infty
and fixed
g\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\setminus\{0\}
,
the
modulation space

M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
consists of all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

such that (
1.2
) is finite. If
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
then
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Banach space with norm

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{M^{p,q}}
.
Otherwise
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a quasi-Banach space with
quasi-norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{M^{p,q}}
. We write
M^{p}=M^{p,p}
.

It is well-known that the definition of
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is independent
of the choice of the window function
g\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})

[
18
]
. For
p<1
or

q<1
the proof of this fact can be found in
[
17
]
.
Additionally,
the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
is dense in
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the case

p,q<\infty
, cf.
[
17
, Remark 14]
.

Due to
[
33
, Theorem 3.6]
and
[
17
, Theorem 3.4.]
the
following continuous embeddings of modulation spaces hold.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
0<p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty

and
0<q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq\infty
. Then

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow M^{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow M^{p_{2},q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

4.2.
Gabor frames

For the definition of Gabor frames
it is convenient to use
time-frequency shifts
\pi(z)f

of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

given by

\displaystyle(\pi(z)f)(t):=e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot t}f(t-x),\qquad z=(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d},\ t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The
Gabor system
with
respect to the
(Gabor) atom

g\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\backslash\{0\}

and lattice

\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2d}

is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)=\{\pi(\lambda)g:\lambda\in\Lambda\}.

Then the
analysis operator
and

synthesis operator

C_{g}\,:\,M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)\quad\text{and}\quad D_{g}\,:\,\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)\to M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

respectively, with respect to
g
and

\Lambda
, are given by

C_{g}f=\big(\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle\big)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\quad\text{and}\quad D_{g}c=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}c_{\lambda}\pi(\lambda)g,

when
f\in M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

c=(c_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda)
. Here the series converges
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The (Gabor) frame operator

S=S_{g_{1},g_{2},\Lambda}:M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by

\displaystyle S_{g_{1},g_{2},\Lambda}f:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g_{1}\rangle\pi(\lambda)g_{2}.

(4.2)

If
g_{1}=g_{2}=g
we write
S_{g,\Lambda}
instead of
S_{g_{1},g_{2},\Lambda}
. It follows that
C_{g},D_{g}
and
S_{g,\Lambda}

are well-defined and continuous
(see e. g. Chapters 11-14
in
[
18
]
).
Let
g\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\backslash\{0\}
.
If
S_{g,\Lambda}
is bounded and invertible on

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then we call
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)

Gabor frame
. For rectangular lattices

\Lambda=\alpha\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\beta\mathbb{Z}^{d}
the
existence of Gabor frames is well understood, see

[
9
,
18
,
31
,
45
]
.

For every Gabor frame
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
over a lattice,
there exists a
dual window
\gamma=S^{-1}g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, so that every
f
can be
expanded into a Gabor expansion

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=D_{g}C_{\gamma}f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left<f,\pi(\lambda)\gamma\right>\pi(\lambda)g,

(4.3)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=D_{\gamma}C_{g}f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left<f,\pi(\lambda)g\right>\pi(\lambda)\gamma.

(4.4)

For
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
a fundamental result of
Janssen
[
31
]
asserts that also

\gamma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Then the expansion formulas (
4.3
) and
(
4.4
) hold for every
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

with weak-
*
-convergence.

A Gabor frame
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is called tight if

S_{g,\Lambda}=C\,\mathrm{Id}
for some
C>0
. In this case

\gamma=S_{g,\Lambda}^{-1}g=C^{-1}g
, and the
Gabor expansion

f=C^{-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left<f,\pi(\lambda)\gamma\right>\pi(\lambda)g

looks like an orthonormal expansion.

Tight Gabor frames with the constant
C=1
can be constructed as
follows. Let
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
be a Gabor frame with frame
operator
S_{g,\Lambda}
. Due to
[
19
, Lemma 5.16]

S^{-1/2}_{g,\Lambda}\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is a tight Gabor frame
with constant
C=1
. By applying this procedure to a Gaussian
window, one sees that there exist tight Gabor frames
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
with
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

For future references we remark that if

p\in(0,\infty]
and
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
then

\displaystyle f\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad C_{g}f\in\ell^{p}(\Lambda)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad C_{\gamma}f\in\ell^{p}(\Lambda),

(4.5)

with norm equivalence

\|f\|_{M^{p}}\asymp\|C_{g}f\|_{\ell^{p}}\asymp\|C_{\gamma}f\|_{\ell^{p}}.

4.3.
Pseudodifferential Operators

For a real-valued
d\times d
-matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
and a symbol
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
the
pseudodifferential operator
Op_{A}(\mathfrak{a})
is defined
by

\displaystyle Op_{A}(\mathfrak{a})u(x)=\int\hskip-5.69054pt\int\mathfrak{a}(x-A(x-y),\xi)f(y)e^{2\pi i(x-y)\cdot\xi}\,dyd\xi,\qquad u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where the integrals should be interpreted in
distribution sense, if necessary.
If
A=0
, then
Op_{A}(\mathfrak{a})
agrees with the
Kohn-Nirenberg or normal representation
a(x,D)
.
If instead
A=\frac{1}{2}I
, where
I
is the

d\times d
identity matrix, then
Op_{A}(\mathfrak{a})

is the Weyl quantization
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
of
\mathfrak{a}
.

By
[
29
,
44
]
for each symbol

\mathfrak{a}_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})

and each
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}

there is a
unique
\mathfrak{a}_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
such that
Op_{A_{1}}(\mathfrak{a}_{1})=Op_{A_{2}}(\mathfrak{a}_{2})
, and that

\displaystyle Op_{A_{1}}(\mathfrak{a}_{1})=Op_{A_{2}}(\mathfrak{a}_{2})\Leftrightarrow\mathfrak{a}_{2}(x,\xi)=e^{2\pi it((A_{1}-A_{2})D_{\xi})\cdot D_{x}}\mathfrak{a}_{1}(x,\xi).

(4.6)

We refer to
[
44
]
for the proof of
the following result.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
A,A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and

p,q\in(0,\infty]
. Then the following is true:

(1)

e^{2\pi it(AD_{\xi})\cdot D_{x}}

is an isomorphism on
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
;

(2)

Op_{A_{1}}(M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}))=Op_{A_{2}}(M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}))
.

We can write a Weyl operator by means of the Wigner distribution
W
of
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which is defined by

\displaystyle W(f,g)(x,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x+t/2)\overline{g(x-t/2)}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot t}\,dt,\qquad x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(4.7)

Denote the inversion
ğ
of
g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\text{\u{g}}(x):=g(-x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

\displaystyle W(f,g)(x,\xi)=2^{d}e^{4\pi ix\cdot\xi}V_{\text{\u{g}}}f(2x,2\xi)\,,

and the Wigner
distribution is just a slight modification of the short-time Fourier
transform.
Since the short-time Fourier transform
V_{.}:\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\times\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
we immediately get
W(f,g)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for all
f,g\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
.

By means of the Wigner distribution the Weyl operator of a symbol
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
is given by the formula

\displaystyle\left<\mathfrak{a}^{w}f,g\right>=\left<\mathfrak{a},W(g,f)\right>\qquad\text{ for all }f,g\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}).

Pseudodifferential operators of Weyl form are continuous maps from
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, see

[
42
]
and
[
29
]
. Moreover they are continuous as maps
between certain modulation spaces, cf.
[
18
]
and

[
21
]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
0<p_{0}\leq 1
,
p,q\in[p_{0},\infty]

and
\mathfrak{a}\in M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
. Then

\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is bounded on
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

As proved in
[
43
, Theorem 3.1]
, this theorem also holds for
more general weighted modulation spaces.

Remark 4.1
.

By
[
42
]
it follows that
M^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})

is invariant under actions with chirps

e^{i(AD_{\xi})\cdot D_{x}}
with
A\in M(d\times d;\mathbb{C})
for all
p,q\in(0,\infty]
. Hence all results concerning
Weyl operators of this paper also
hold for operators of the form
Op_{A}(a)
.

Next we show that Gabor frame operators
with windows in
M^{p_{0}}

are pseudo-differential operators with
symbols in
M^{\infty,p_{0}}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
p_{0}\in(0,1]
,
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

A\in M(d\times d;\mathbb{R})
be a
d\times d
-matrix,
\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
a lattice,
and let
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})

be such that
S_{g_{1},g_{2},\Lambda}=Op_{A}(\mathfrak{a})
.
Then
\mathfrak{a}\in M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
.

Proof.

In view of Remark
4.1
we may assume that
A=0
.
The Weyl symbol of

S_{g_{1},g_{2},\Lambda}
with
g_{1}=g_{2}
was calculated in

[
2
, p.12]
, and by
similar arguments it follows that
the Kohn-Nirenberg symbol is

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(l,\lambda)\in\Lambda}g_{1}(x-l)\overline{\widehat{g}_{2}(\xi-\lambda)}e^{2\pi i(x-l)\cdot(\lambda-\xi)}.

(4.8)

In order to estimate the
M^{\infty,p_{0}}

norm of
\mathfrak{a}
we choose the window

\Psi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})\setminus\{0\}
as

\Psi(x,\xi):=\psi(x)\overline{\widehat{\psi}(\xi)}e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}.

By straightforward applications of
Fourier inversion formula, it follows that

\displaystyle(V_{\Psi}\mathfrak{a})(z,w)=\sum_{l,\lambda\in\Lambda}e^{-2\pi i(y\cdot\xi+l\cdot\eta)}(V_{\psi}g_{1})(x-l,\xi+\eta-\lambda)\cdot\overline{(V_{\psi}g_{2})(x+y-l,\xi-\lambda)},

(4.9)

(see e.g.
[
26
]
) with
z=(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

w=(\eta,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Now let
\Lambda^{2}=\Lambda\times\Lambda
,
and
\Lambda^{4}=\Lambda^{2}\times\Lambda^{2}
and choose

\varepsilon=\frac{1}{N}
with integer
N\geq 1
large enough,
such that

\{\psi(x-m)e^{2\pi ix\cdot\mu}\}_{m,\mu\in\varepsilon\Lambda}\quad\text{and}\quad\{\Psi(z-w_{1})e^{2\pi iz\cdot w_{2}}\}_{w_{1},w_{2}\in\varepsilon\Lambda^{2}},

are Gabor frames of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
,
respectively.
Then

\|g_{j}\|_{M^{p_{0}}}\asymp\|V_{\psi}g_{j}\|_{\ell^{p_{0}}(\varepsilon\Lambda^{2})}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mathfrak{a}\|_{M^{\infty,p_{0}}}\asymp\|V_{\Psi}\mathfrak{a}\|_{\ell^{\infty,p_{0}}(\varepsilon\Lambda^{4})},

in view of a result in
[
17
]
.

Let
F_{j}=|V_{\psi}g_{j}|
.
If
w_{1}=(m,\mu)\in\varepsilon\Lambda^{2}

and
w_{2}=(\nu,n)\in\varepsilon\Lambda^{2}
, then
(
4.9
)
yields

\displaystyle|(V_{\Psi}\mathfrak{a})(w_{1},w_{2})|^{p_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l,\lambda\in\Lambda}F_{1}(m-l,\mu+\nu-\lambda)^{p_{0}}F_{2}(m+n-l,\mu-\lambda)^{p_{0}}

\displaystyle=(|\check{F}_{1}|^{p_{0}}*|F_{2}|^{p_{0}})(Tw_{2}),\qquad Tw_{2}=(n,-\nu).

The right-hand side does not depend on
w_{1}
, and therefore

\|\mathfrak{a}\|_{M^{\infty,p_{0}}}\asymp\|V_{\Psi}\mathfrak{a}\|_{\ell^{\infty,p_{0}}(\varepsilon\Lambda^{4})}\lesssim\|{\check{F}}_{1}^{p_{0}}*F_{2}^{p_{0}}\|_{\ell^{1}(\varepsilon\Lambda^{2})}^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\\[4.30554pt]
\leq\big(\|F_{1}^{p_{0}}\|_{\ell^{1}(\varepsilon\Lambda^{2})}\|F_{2}^{p_{0}}\|_{\ell^{1}(\varepsilon\Lambda^{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}=\|F_{1}\|_{\ell^{p_{0}}(\varepsilon\Lambda^{2})}\|F_{2}\|_{\ell^{p_{0}}(\varepsilon\Lambda^{2})}\asymp\|g_{1}\|_{M^{p_{0}}}\|g_{2}\|_{M^{p_{0}}}\,.

Thus
\mathfrak{a}\in M^{\infty,p_{0}}
.

4.4.
Almost Diagonalization of Pseudodifferential Operators

In this section we list several characterizations of symbols in

\mathfrak{a}
of
M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
,
p_{0}\in(0,\infty]
. The
following characterization of a symbol class by means of the almost
diagonalization of the associated pseudodifferential operator was found in
[
19
, Theorem
3.2]
for
p_{0}=1
and subsequently generalized to the full range
of
p_{0}
in
[
5
, Thm. 3.2]
(with almost the same proof).
This characterization helps to deduce spectral properties of pseudodifferential operators
from spectral properties of infinite matrices.
We only formulate the unweighted case of
[
5
, Thm. 3.2]
, which is sufficient for this paper.

Theorem 4.5
.

(Almost Diagonalization).
We fix a non-zero
g\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\backslash\{0\}
and a lattice
\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
such that
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is a Gabor frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then for any
p_{0}\in(0,\infty]
the following properties are equivalent:

i)

\mathfrak{a}\in M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
.

ii)

\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
and
there exists a function
H\in L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
, in
fact
H\in W(C_{b},L^{p_{0}})
, such
that

\displaystyle|\langle\mathfrak{a}^{w}\pi(z)g,\pi(w)g\rangle|\leq H(w-z)\qquad\forall w,z\in{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}.

(4.10)

iii)

\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
and there exists a sequence
h\in\ell^{p_{0}}(\Lambda)
such that

\displaystyle|\langle\mathfrak{a}^{w}\pi(\rho)g,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle|\leq h(\lambda-\rho)\qquad\forall\lambda,\rho\in\Lambda.

(4.11)

As an immediate application we obtain the following result.

Corollary 4.6
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
4.5
we assume that
T:\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is continuous and satisfies

\displaystyle|\langle T\pi(\rho)g,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle|\leq h(\lambda-\rho)\qquad\forall\lambda,\rho\in{\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}.

for some
h\in\ell^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{Z}^{2d}})
. Then
T=\mathfrak{a}^{w}
for some
\mathfrak{a}\in M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
.

Proof.

Because of the continuity of
T:\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the Schwartz’s kernel theorem asserts the existence of a symbol
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
with
T=\mathfrak{a}^{w}
. An application of Theorem
4.5
yields the claim.

4.5.
Matrix Formulation

We fix a lattice
\Lambda
and a window
g\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\backslash\{0\}
such that
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is a frame
with dual window
\gamma
and associated Gabor expansion (
4.3
) and
(
4.4
).

For the manipulations to be meaningful,
we assume of a symbol
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
that the
Weyl operator
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is bounded on
M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
p\in(0,\infty]
. Just keep in mind, that due to
Proposition
4.1

M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. On account of the Gabor expansion
(
4.3
) we have
D_{g}C_{\gamma}f=f

for all
f\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Together
with the continuity of
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
on
M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we obtain for
all
f\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle C_{g}(\mathfrak{a}^{w}f)(\lambda)=\langle\mathfrak{a}^{w}f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda}\langle f,\pi(\mu)\gamma\rangle\langle\mathfrak{a}^{w}\pi(\mu)g,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle\quad\text{for all }\lambda\in\Lambda.

(4.12)

We define the matrix
M(\mathfrak{a})
of
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
with
respect to the frame
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
by its entries

\displaystyle M(\mathfrak{a})_{\lambda,\mu}=\langle\mathfrak{a}^{w}\pi(\mu)g,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle\qquad\text{for all }\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda\,.

We can then recast (
4.12
) as

\displaystyle C_{g}(\mathfrak{a}^{w}f)=M(\mathfrak{a})C_{\gamma}f.

(4.13)

Likewise, Theorem
4.5
now reads as follows.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
. Then
\mathfrak{a}\in M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
, if and only if
M(\mathfrak{a})\in\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}
.

The matrix
P
associated to the identity operator has the entries

\displaystyle P_{\lambda,\mu}=\langle\pi(\mu)g,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle,\qquad\text{for all }\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda.

(4.14)

To simplify the analysis of
P
, we assume from now on that
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is a tight frame with
S_{g,\Lambda}=\mathrm{I}
. As
mentioned already, tight frames with a window in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

always exist.
The matrix
P
has the following properties.

Lemma 4.8
.

Assume that
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is a tight frame with
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
0<p_{0}\leq p\leq\infty
and
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
be such that the associated pseudodifferential operator
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is bounded on
M^{p}
. Then

(i)

P
is a projection from
\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
to range

C_{g}(M^{p})
, i.e.,
Pc=c\in\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
if and only if there exists
f\in M^{p}

such that
c_{\lambda}=\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle=(C_{g}f)_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

(ii)

PM(\mathfrak{a})=M(\mathfrak{a})
and
M(\mathfrak{a})P=M(\mathfrak{a})
.

(iii)

P\in\mathcal{C}^{p}(\Lambda)
.

(iv)

For
\mathfrak{a}\in M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
we have
M(\mathfrak{a})+I-P\in\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

(i) For all
\lambda,\nu\in\Lambda
the assumption that

S_{g,\Lambda}=\mathrm{Id}
and
(
4.2
) imply that

\displaystyle(P^{2})_{\lambda,\nu}

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda}\langle\pi(\nu)g,\pi(\mu)g\rangle\pi(\mu)g,\pi(\lambda)g\Big\rangle=\langle S_{g,\Lambda}\pi(\nu)g,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle=\langle\pi(\nu)g,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle

\displaystyle=P_{\lambda,\nu}.

Consequently
P^{2}=P
and
P
is a projection.
Next let
c\in\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
with
Pc=c
. With

f=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda}c_{\mu}\pi(\mu)g
we have for
\lambda\in\Lambda
,

\displaystyle c_{\lambda}=(Pc)_{\lambda}=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda}\langle\pi(\mu)g,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle c_{\mu}=\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle=(C_{g}f)_{\lambda}\,,

whence
c=C_{g}f
. Then the norm-equivalence
(
4.5
) yields

f\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Conversely, assume that
c=C_{g}f
for some
f\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then we get due to (
4.3
)

\displaystyle(Pc)_{\lambda}=\langle\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda}\langle f,\pi(\mu)g\rangle\pi(\mu)g,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle=\langle S_{g,\Lambda}f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle=\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle=c_{\lambda},\quad\lambda\in\Lambda\,,

and (i) holds.

(ii) is proved similarly by straight forward calculations using
(
4.2
).

(iii) If
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then also
V_{g}g\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
, e.g., by

[
18
, Thm. 11.2.5]
. In particular, this implies that for every

N\geq 0

|V_{g}g(z)|\lesssim(1+|z|)^{-N}\,,

and thus

|P_{\lambda,\mu}|=|\langle\pi(\mu)g,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle|=|V_{g}g(\lambda-\mu)|\lesssim(1+|\lambda-\mu|)^{-N}\,.

By choosing
N
large enough, we see that
H(z)=(1+|z|)^{-N}
is in

W(C_{b},L^{p_{0}})
and consequently
P\in\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}(\Lambda)
.

(iv) Since all matrices
P,\mathrm{I}
and
M(\mathfrak{a})
are in
\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}
, their sum

M(\mathfrak{a})+\mathrm{Id}+P
is also in
\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}
.

4.6.
Spectral invariance.

We have already seen before that it is possible to relate a pseudodifferential operator

\mathfrak{a}^{w}
to an infinite matrix
M(\mathfrak{a})
.
It turns out that there is a connection between the invertibility of
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
and
M(\mathfrak{a})
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Assume that
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is a tight frame with
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
0<p\leq\infty
and
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
be such that the associated Weyl operator

\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is bounded on
M^{p}
. Then

\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is invertible on
M^{p}
,
if and only if

i)

\|M(\mathfrak{a})Pc\|_{p}\gtrsim\|Pc\|_{p}\quad\text{ for all }c\in\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
and

ii)

for every
c_{0}\in P\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
, there is a
c\in P\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
such that

M(\mathfrak{a})Pc=Pc_{0}
.

Here the projection
P
is defined as in (
4.14
).

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
be invertible on
M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Using (
4.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|M(\mathfrak{a})C_{g}f\|_{p}=\|C_{g}(\mathfrak{a}^{w}f)\|_{p}\asymp\|\mathfrak{a}^{w}f\|_{M^{p}}\gtrsim\|f\|_{M^{p}}\asymp\|C_{g}f\|_{p}\,,

which is (i).
To prove (ii), let
c_{0}\in P\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
be arbitrary.
Then there exists
h\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
C_{g}h=c_{0}=Pc_{0}
by Lemma
4.8
.
Since
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is bijective on
M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there is a
f\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}^{w}f=h.

Then we obtain for
c=C_{g}f
due to Lemma
4.8
that
Pc=c
and that

\displaystyle M(\mathfrak{a})Pc=M(\mathfrak{a})c=M(\mathfrak{a})C_{g}f=C_{g}(\mathfrak{a}^{w}f)=C_{g}h=c_{0}=Pc_{0}.

This implies (ii).

Conversely assume that (i) and (ii) hold. Using
(
4.13
) (with
\gamma=g
) and (i), we obtain for all
f\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{a}^{w}f\|_{M^{p}}\asymp\|C_{g}(\mathfrak{a}^{w}f)\|_{p}=\|M(\mathfrak{a})C_{g}f\|_{p}\gtrsim\|C_{g}f\|_{p}\asymp\|f\|_{M^{p}}.

Hence
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is one-to-one on
M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
To prove that
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is surjective we choose an arbitrary

h\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
c_{0}:=C_{g}h\in\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
. Then

c_{0}=Pc_{0}\in P\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
.

By assumption (ii) there is a
c\in P\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
such that

M(\mathfrak{a})Pc=Pc_{0}=c_{0}=C_{g}h.

(4.15)

Since the image of
P
is
C_{g}(M^{p})
, there is a
f\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

\displaystyle Pc=C_{g}f.

A combination of (
4.3
),
(
4.13
)
and (
4.15
) yields

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}^{w}f=D_{g}C_{g}(\mathfrak{a}^{w}f)=D_{g}M(\mathfrak{a})Pc=D_{g}C_{g}h=h.

This implies that
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
maps onto
M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and hence invertible on
M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

In the previous lemma we proved the equivalence of the invertibility of
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
on

M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and of
M(\mathfrak{a})
on

P\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
. Since
\mathrm{ker}\,P\neq\{0\}
and

M(\mathfrak{a})=M(\mathfrak{a})P
,
M(\mathfrak{a})
cannot be
invertible on the whole space
\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
.
In the literature this problem is usually overcome by using the
pseudo-inverse of
M(\mathfrak{a})
and holomorphic functional
calculus. Here we use a new trick, which may be of independent
interest. Consider the matrix
A=M(\mathfrak{a})+\mathrm{Id}-P
.
We can then use the spectral invariance result for infinite
convolution-dominated matrices of Theorem
3.15

to derive a
spectral invariance result for pseudodifferential operators
on modulation spaces, cf. Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
p\in[p_{0},\infty]
be the index for which
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is invertible on
M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
A=M(\mathfrak{a})+\mathrm{Id}-P
, where
P
is the projection defined in
(
4.14
).
First we check the assumptions of Theorem

3.15
and prove that

\displaystyle A=M(\mathfrak{a})+I-P\qquad\text{ is invertible on }\ell^{p}(\Lambda).

(4.16)

Assume that
Ac=0
for some
c\in\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
. Then by i) and ii) of Lemma
4.8

\displaystyle 0=Ac=\left(M(\mathfrak{a})+I-P\right)\left(Pc+(I-P)c\right)=M(\mathfrak{a})Pc+(I-P)c.

If we apply
P
respectively
I-P
to the previous equality and use Lemma
4.8
again, we obtain

\displaystyle M(\mathfrak{a})Pc=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad(I-P)c=0.

Since
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is invertible on
M^{p}
we obtain by Lemma
4.9
and the previous estimate

\displaystyle\|Pc\|_{p}\lesssim\|M(\mathfrak{a})Pc\|_{p}=0,

and consequently
Pc=0
. Hence
c=Pc+(I-P)c=0
which shows that
A

is one-to-one.

To show the surjectivity of
A
, we let
c_{0}\in\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
be arbitrary.
Since
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is invertible on
M^{p}
, Lemma

4.9
yields the existence of
c\in P\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
with

\displaystyle M(\mathfrak{a})Pc=Pc_{0}.

(4.17)

Then

\widetilde{c}=Pc+(I-P)c_{0}\in\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
and by Lemma
4.8
and (
4.17
)

\displaystyle A\widetilde{c}=M(\mathfrak{a})\widetilde{c}+(I-P)\widetilde{c}=M(\mathfrak{a})Pc+(I-P)c_{0}=Pc_{0}+(I-P)c_{0}=c_{0}.

Thus
A
is onto on
\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
and therefore invertible on

\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
. Due to Lemma
4.8
we have
A\in\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}(\Lambda)
. Since (
4.16
) also holds we can apply Theorem

3.15
and get the invertibility of
A
on
\ell^{q}(\Lambda)
for all
q\in[p_{0},\infty)
.

Next we show, that

\displaystyle M(\mathfrak{a})\text{ is invertible on }P\ell^{q}(\Lambda)\text{ for all }q\in[p_{0},\infty).

(4.18)

Let
q\in[p_{0},\infty)
be arbitrary. Since
I-P\equiv 0
on
P\ell^{q}(\Lambda)
,
the injectivity of
A
implies that
M(\mathfrak{a})
is one-to-one on
P\ell^{q}(\Lambda)
.
For an arbitrary
c_{0}\in P\ell^{q}(\Lambda)\subseteq\ell^{q}(\Lambda)
,
there is some
c\in\ell^{q}(\Lambda)
with
Ac=c_{0}
,
since
A
is onto on
\ell^{q}(\Lambda)
. Since
(I-P)M(\mathfrak{a})=0
by Lemma
4.8
, we obtain, after
applying
I-P
to
Ac=c_{0}
, that
0=(I-P)c_{0}=(I-P)Ac=(I-P)c
. Then
c\in P\ell^{q}(\Lambda)
and

\displaystyle M(\mathfrak{a})c=M(\mathfrak{a})c+(I-P)c=Ac=c_{0}.

Hence
M(\mathfrak{a})
is onto
P\ell^{q}(\Lambda)
and (
4.18
) holds.
By Lemma
4.9

\mathfrak{a}
is
invertible on
M^{q}
.

We now prove Theorem
1.1
and obtain more
refined information about the inverse
(\mathfrak{a}^{w})^{-1}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Theorem
1.2
we get the invertibility of
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
on
M^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
By
[
19
, Theorem 4.6]
and the embedding

M^{\infty,p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\subseteq M^{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, there is a symbol
\mathfrak{b}\in M^{\infty,1}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
with

\mathfrak{b}^{w}=(\mathfrak{a}^{w})^{-1}
.
We consider the associated matrices
M(\mathfrak{a})
and

M(\mathfrak{b})
with respect to a tight Gabor frame
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
with
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and again denote by
P
the projection with
entries
P_{\lambda\mu}=\langle\pi(\mu)g,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle
.
On account of Lemma
4.8
we get for all
c\in\text{ran }C_{g}
the existence of an
f\in M^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

c=C_{g}f=Pc
.
Then for
all
c=C_{g}f=Pc\in\text{ran }C_{g}
we obtain using (
4.13
)

\displaystyle M(\mathfrak{b})M(\mathfrak{a})c=M(\mathfrak{b})M(\mathfrak{a})C_{g}f=M(\mathfrak{b})C_{g}(\mathfrak{a}^{w}f)=C_{g}(\mathfrak{b}^{w}\mathfrak{a}^{w}f)=C_{g}f=c\,.

If
Pc=0
, then
M(\mathfrak{a})c=M(\mathfrak{a})Pc=0
,
consequently on
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
we have

M(\mathfrak{b})M(\mathfrak{a})=P\,.

It follows that

\big(M(\mathfrak{b})+\mathrm{Id}-P\big)\big(M(\mathfrak{a})+\mathrm{Id}-P\big)=\big(M(\mathfrak{b})+\mathrm{Id}-P\big)\,A=\mathrm{Id}\,.

This means that
B=M(\mathfrak{b})+\mathrm{Id}-P
is the inverse
of the invertible matrix
A
(since
the inverse is unique).
Since
A\in\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}
by Lemma
4.8
,
Theorem
3.14
implies that also
B\in\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}
.

Consequently
M(\mathfrak{b})\in\mathcal{C}^{p_{0}}
. Now the characterization
of Corollary
4.7
implies that
\mathfrak{b}\in M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
, as claimed.

By using Theorem
1.1
we can now deduce the
invertibility of
\mathfrak{a}
on more general modulation spaces which generalizes Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 4.10

(Spectral invariance on Modulation Spaces)
.

If
\mathfrak{a}\in M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
for
p_{0}\in(0,1]
and
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is invertible on

M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
p\in[p_{0},\infty]
,
then
\mathfrak{a}^{w}
is also invertible on
M^{r,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

for all
r,q\in[p_{0},\infty)
.

Proof.

On account of Theorem
1.1
there is
a
\mathfrak{b}\in M^{\infty,p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
with

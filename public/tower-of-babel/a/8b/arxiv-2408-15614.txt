Uniform rank-metric stability of Lie algebras and groups
Benjamin Bachner

Abstract
. We study uniform stability of discrete groups, Lie groups and Lie algebras in the rank metric, and the connections between uniform stability of these objects. We prove that semisimple Lie algebras are far from being flexibly ℂ \mathbb{C} -stable, and that semisimple Lie groups and lattices in semisimple Lie groups of higher rank are not strictly ℂ \mathbb{C} -stable. Furthermore, we prove that free groups are not uniformly flexibly F F -stable over any field F F .

The notion of homomorphism stability is the general problem of whether
almost homomorphisms are close to genuine homomorphisms, and is usually
referred to as Ulam stability
[
18

on the objects in question (usually groups or algebras), and on the
precise meaning of almost homomorphisms and distances between them,
as well as the chosen metric. A theorem of Kazhdan gives such a result
for unitary almost representations in the operator norm:

\left\|\varphi\left(\gamma\right)-\psi\left(\gamma\right)\right\|_{op}\leq 2\delta

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

In general, we say that the group

there exists a genuine representation

\left\|\varphi\left(\gamma\right)-\psi\left(\gamma\right)\right\|_{op}\leq\varepsilon

2.
Preliminaries and first observations

2.1.
Uniform Stability

Let
\Gamma
be a group and let
\mathcal{G}=\left\{H_{i}\right\}_{i\in I}

be a family of groups. Let
\mathcal{F}=\left\{\varphi:\Gamma\to H\;|\;H\in\mathcal{G}\right\}

be the set of functions from
\Gamma
to groups in
\mathcal{G}
,
a
defect function
def:\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

and a
distance
function

d:\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}\to\left[0,\infty\right]
.
We say that
\Gamma
is
\left(\mathcal{G},def,d\right)
-stable

if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
\delta>0
such that for
any function
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}
with
def\left(\varphi\right)\leq\delta

there exists a homomorphism
\psi\in\mathcal{F}
such that
d\left(\varphi,\psi\right)\leq\varepsilon
.

In our context, the family
\mathcal{G}
will be all invertible matrices
over
F
,
\mathcal{GL}\left(F\right)=\left\{GL_{n}\left(F\right)\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
and the the (uniform) defect function will be

def\left(\varphi\right)=sup\,\left\{rk\left(\varphi\left(\gamma_{1}\right)\varphi\left(\gamma_{2}\right)-\varphi\left(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\right)\right)\;|\;\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma\right\}.

The distance
d
with respect to
strict
uniform stability
will be

d^{strict}\left(\varphi,\psi\right)=\begin{cases}\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma}{sup}\;rk\left(\varphi\left(\gamma\right)-\psi\left(\gamma\right)\right)&\varphi,\psi:\Gamma\to GL_{m}\left(F\right)\\
\infty&else\end{cases},

so that the codomains of
\varphi
and
\psi
must coincide. In the
context of
flexible stability,
we allow taking the distance
between maps with different codomains. We use the notation used in

[
8
]
and
[
3
]
: For
A\in M_{n}\left(F\right)
,
let
\widehat{A}
be the operator on
\mathbb{C}^{\oplus\mathbb{N}}
which acts
as
A
on the first
n
basis vectors and
0
otherwise. Thus we
may think of
\widehat{A}
as an infinite matrix that vanishes everywhere
except for the submatrix
\widehat{A}_{\left[n\right],\left[n\right]}

where it equals
A
. For
A\in GL_{n}\left(F\right)
and
B\in GL_{m}\left(F\right)
,
define

rk\left(A-B\right)\vcentcolon=\frac{dim\left(im\left(\widehat{A}-\widehat{B}\right)\right)}{min\left(n,m\right)},

And define the flexible distance of
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{F}
by

d^{flex}\left(\varphi,\psi\right)=\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma}{sup}\,rk\left(\varphi\left(\gamma\right)-\psi\left(\gamma\right)\right).

We note that since we require the target matrices to be nonsingular,
we have

dim\left(im\left(\widehat{A}-\widehat{B}\right)\right)\geq\left|dim\left(im\left(\widehat{A}\right)\right)-dim\left(im\left(\widehat{B}\right)\right)\right|=\left|n-m\right|,

so that
rk\left(A-B\right)
can be small only if
\frac{\left|n-m\right|}{min\left(n,m\right)}

is small. Thus, in flexible stability we allow an increase of the
target dimension, but this increase must be small relative to the
dimension.

The group
\Gamma
is strictly uniformly
F
-stable if it is
\left(\mathcal{GL}\left(F\right),def,d^{strict}\right)
-stable
and flexibly uniformly
F
-stable if it is
\left(\mathcal{GL}\left(F\right),def,d^{flex}\right)
-stable.

When working with Lie groups, we would like the relevant functions
to be in the associated category, which in this case is smooth maps.
So if

\mathcal{G}

The defect of

def\left(\phi\right)=sup\,\left\{rk\left(\left[\phi\left(x\right),\phi\left(y\right)\right]-\phi\left(\left[x,y\right]\right)\right)\;|\;x,y\in\mathfrak{g}\right\}.

Strict/flexible uniform

is then taken

\mathcal{G}=\left\{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\left(F\right)\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

Stability of finitely presented algebras was considered in

3
]

including Lie algebras, with respect to pointwise stability. For finite

which are the defining relations of

times the pointwise defect

and the distance
\underset{x\in\mathfrak{g}}{sup}\;rk\left(\phi\left(x\right)-\theta\left(x\right)\right)

again by linearity, triangle inequality and invariance to scalar multiples,

2.2.
Compressions and strict vs. flexible stability

Fix a subspace
U\leq F^{n}
of dimension
k
, a basis
B=\left\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\right\}

for
U
,
\iota:U\to F^{n}
the inclusion and

and

.
For any
M\in M_{n}\left(F\right)
, we denote by

\left[M\right]_{U,B,p}\vcentcolon=p\circ M\circ\iota\in M_{k}\left(F\right)

the matrix representing the linear map
p\circ M\circ\iota\in End\left(U\right)

in the basis
B
, and call it the
compression of
M
associated
to
\left(U,B,p\right)
.

We will often omit
B
and
p

from the notation writing
\left[M\right]_{U}
, where a choice of
a basis and a projection are implicit.

Lemma 2.1
.

(1)

Let
U\leq F^{n}
be a subspace of dimension

k
, and
\left[M\right]_{U,B,p}
the compression associated to

U
with a basis
B
and a projection
p
. Then we have

rank\left(\left[M\right]_{U,B,p}\right)\geq rank\left(M\right)-2\left(n-k\right)\quad\forall M\in M_{n}\left(F\right),

and for any
M_{1},M_{2}\in M_{n}\left(F\right)

rank\left(\left[M_{1}M_{2}\right]_{U,B,p}-\left[M_{1}\right]_{U,B,p}\left[M_{2}\right]_{U,B,p}\right)\leq n-k.

(2)

Let
U_{1},U_{2}\leq F^{n}
be subspaces
of dimension
k
,
\iota_{i}:U_{i}\hookrightarrow F^{n}
,
p_{i}:F^{n}\twoheadrightarrow U_{i}

inclusions and projections, and
B_{i}
bases for
U_{i}
, respectively.
Then for some
A\in GL_{k}\left(F\right)
,

rank\left(\left[M\right]_{U_{1},B_{1},p_{1}}-A\left[M\right]_{U_{2},B_{2},p_{2}}A^{-1}\right)\leq 4\left(n-k\right),\quad\forall M\in M_{n}\left(F\right).

Proof.

We have by the triangle inequality

\displaystyle rank\left(\left[M\right]_{U}\right)

\displaystyle=rank\left(p\circ M\circ\iota\right)=rank\left(\iota\circ p\circ M\circ\iota\circ p\right)

\displaystyle=rank\left(M-M\left(I_{n}-\iota\circ p\right)-\left(I_{n}-\iota\circ p\right)M\circ\iota\circ p\right)

\displaystyle\geq rank\left(M\right)-2rank\left(I_{n}-\iota\circ p\right)=rank\left(M\right)-2\left(n-k\right).

For
M_{1},M_{2}\in M_{n}\left(F\right)
,

\displaystyle rank\left(\left[M_{1}M_{2}\right]_{U}-\left[M_{2}\right]_{U}\left[M_{2}\right]_{U}\right)

\displaystyle=rank\left(p\circ\left(M_{1}M_{2}-M_{1}\left(\iota\circ p\right)M_{2}\right)\circ\iota\right)

\displaystyle\leq rank\left(M_{1}M_{2}-M_{1}\left(\iota\circ p\right)M_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=rank\left(M_{1}\left(I_{n}-\iota\circ p\right)M_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq rank\left(I_{n}-\iota\circ p\right)=n-k,

proving
1
. For
2
, let

W=U_{1}\cap U_{2}
, and let
B_{W}=\left\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}\right\}

be a basis for
W
. By conjugating the whole expression

\left[M\right]_{U_{1},B_{1},p_{1}}-A\left[M\right]_{U_{2},B_{2},p_{2}}A^{-1}

by some matrix in
GL_{k}\left(F\right)
, we may assume that the
basis
B_{1}
is some completion of
B_{W}
to a basis of
U_{1}
.
Now by choosing
A\in GL_{k}\left(F\right)
we may further assume
that the basis
B_{2}
is given by some completion of
B_{W}
to
a basis of
U_{2}
and that the expression is given by

\left[M\right]_{U_{1},B_{1},p_{1}}-\left[M\right]_{U_{2},B_{2},p_{2}}.

Now for
w_{i}\in W
,

\left(p_{1}\circ M\circ\iota_{1}-p_{2}\circ M\circ\iota_{2}\right)w_{i}=\left(p_{1}-p_{2}\right)Mw_{i},

thus the restriction of
\left[M\right]_{U_{1},B_{1},p_{1}}-\left[M\right]_{U_{2},B_{2},p_{2}}

to the subspace

rank\left(p_{1}-p_{2}\right)\leq n-dimW\leq 2\left(n-k\right)
.
It follows that

rank\left(\left[M\right]_{U_{1},B_{1},p_{1}}-\left[M\right]_{U_{2},B_{2},p_{2}}\right)\leq 2\left(n-k\right)+n-\left(2k-n\right)=4\left(n-k\right).

∎

Lemma
2.1
implies that a compression of an almost
representation (and in particular, of a representation) onto a large
subspace is again an almost representation (and that compressions
of nonsingular matrices have almost full rank), and that up to low
rank perturbation and conjugation, all
k
-dimensional compressions
of almost representations are equivalent. In particular, if all representations
have enough subrepresentations, this implies that flexible stability
is equivalent to strict stability.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
F
be an algebraically closed field of
characteristic zero. Then a solvable Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over
K\leq F

is flexibly uniformly
F
-stable if and only if it is strictly uniformly

F
-stable.

Proof.

Strict stability clearly implies flexible stability. Assume
\mathfrak{g}

is flexibly stable. Then for any
\delta>0
there is an
\varepsilon>0

such that for any
\delta
-representation
\varphi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\left(F\right)

there is a representation
\psi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{N}\left(F\right)

such that

\underset{x\in\mathfrak{g}}{sup}\,rk\left(\widehat{\varphi\left(x\right)}-\widehat{\psi\left(x\right)}\right)\leq\varepsilon,

and we may assume
N\leq\left(1+\varepsilon d\right)n
, where
d

is the number of generators of
\mathfrak{g}
. By Lie’s theorem, there is
a complete flag

of invariant subspaces of
\psi
with

. Then for any

k
(and choice of projection and basis

), the compression

\left[\psi\right]_{V_{k}}=p_{V_{k}}\circ\psi\circ\iota_{V_{k}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\left(F\right)

is a representation, and

\underset{x\in\mathfrak{g}}{sup}\;rk\left(p_{V_{k}}\circ\widehat{\varphi\left(x\right)}\circ\iota_{V_{k}}-\widehat{\left[\psi\right]_{V_{k}}\left(x\right)}\right)\leq\underset{x\in\mathfrak{g}}{sup}\;rk\left(\widehat{\varphi\left(x\right)}-\widehat{\psi\left(x\right)}\right)\leq\varepsilon.

Thinking of the matrices as living in
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}\left(F\right)
,
by definition of
\widehat{\varphi\left(x\right)}
it has an
n
-dimensional
compression that equals
\varphi
, so by Lemma
2.1
(
enu:compression2)
there is a

such that

rank\left(\varphi\left(x\right)-A\left(p_{V_{n}}\circ\widehat{\varphi\left(x\right)}\circ\iota_{V_{n}}\right)A^{-1}\right)\leq 4\left(N-n\right)\leq 4\varepsilon dn\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\implies\underset{x\in\mathfrak{g}}{sup}\;rk\left(\varphi\left(x\right)-A\left[\psi\right]_{V_{n}}A^{-1}\left(x\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{x\in\mathfrak{g}}{sup}\;rk\left(\varphi\left(x\right)-A\left(p_{V_{n}}\circ\widehat{\varphi\left(x\right)}\circ\iota_{V_{n}}\right)A^{-1}\right)+rk\left(A\left(p_{V_{n}}\circ\widehat{\varphi\left(x\right)}\circ\iota_{V_{n}}-\widehat{\left[\psi\right]_{V_{n}}\left(x\right)}\right)A^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(4d+1\right)\varepsilon,

where
A\left[\psi\right]_{V_{n}}A^{-1}

Remark 2.3
.

It is interesting to note a similarity between Corollary
2.2

and strict vs. flexible pointwise stability of groups. In
[
7
, Proposition 6.2]

it was proven that for amenable groups (and in particular solvable
groups), strict stability is equivalent to flexible stability in the
Hilbert-Schmidt norm. A similar result holds for permutation stability
as proved in
[
12
, Lemma 3.2]
.

In the case of finite dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
, Corollary
2.2

can be helpful only in the abelian case, as we know from
[
3
]

that nonabelian solvable Lie algebras are not flexibly stable. We
note that stability of abelian Lie algebras are equivalent to
pointwise

stability of free abelian groups, since from bi-invariance

rk\left(ABA^{-1}B^{-1}-I_{n}\right)=rk\left(AB-BA\right),

so the same corollary holds for free abelian groups. The proof of
Corollary
2.2
is easily seen to hold also for
uniform

stability of free abelian groups. However, currently it is not even
known whether
\mathbb{Z}
is uniformly
F
-stable for any field
F
.
By contrast, finite groups were proven to be
F
-stable in
[
14
, Section 6]
,
for any field
F
. Pointwise and uniform stability for finite groups
are easily seen to be equivalent, similar to the case of finite dimensional
Lie algebras.

Remark 2.4
.

We have defined uniform stability only for individual groups/algebras.
However, it is also common to consider uniform stability for families
of groups, such as amenable groups in Kazhdan’s theorem, where the
dependence of
\delta
on
\varepsilon
is uniform for the entire
family. The uniform rank metric stability of finite groups in
[
14
, Section 6]

depends on the size of the group, and so it does not prove uniform
stability for the
class
of finite groups.

2.3.
Verma modules and central characters

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
, with a Cartan
subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of dimension
\ell
, the rank of
\mathfrak{g}
. Denote
by
\Phi\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
the root system of
\mathfrak{g}
relative to

\mathfrak{h}
. To each root
\alpha
denote by
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
the corresponding
root space, and fix a set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}\subset\Phi

and a set of simple roots
\Delta\subset\Phi^{+}
having
\ell

elements. This defines a Cartan decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+},

where

\mathfrak{n}^{+}\vcentcolon=\bigoplus_{\alpha>0}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\;,\;\mathfrak{n}^{-}\vcentcolon=\bigoplus_{\alpha<0}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},

with corresponding Borel subalgebras

\mathfrak{b}^{+}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+}\;,\;\mathfrak{b}^{-}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{-}

Let
\Lambda_{r}
be the root lattice and
\Lambda
be the integral
weight lattice in
\Phi
, and
\Lambda^{+}\subseteq\Lambda
the dominant
integral weights. Let
\rho
be the half sum of the positive roots,
also called the Weyl vector. Denote by
W
the (finite) Weyl group
of
\Phi
, generated by the reflections
s_{\alpha}
.

There is a standard basis for
\mathfrak{g}
consisting of
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\in\mathfrak{n}^{-}
,

h_{1},\ldots,h_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{h}
and
x_{1}\ldots,x_{m}\in\mathfrak{n}^{+}
,
such that
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{i}}
for positive roots
\alpha_{i}\in\Phi^{+}
,

y_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{i}}
, and
h_{1}=h_{\alpha_{i}}
for simple
roots
\alpha_{i}\in\Delta
. According to the Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt
(PBW) theorem, there is a corresponding basis for the universal enveloping
algebra
U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
consisting of monomials

y_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots y_{m}^{r_{m}}h_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots h_{\ell}^{s_{\ell}}x_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{t_{m}}

A
U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
-module is a highest weight module of weight

\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
if there is a vector
v_{0}\in M
of weight

\lambda
such that
\mathfrak{n}^{+}\cdot v_{0}=0
and
M=U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\cdot v_{0}
.
By the PBW theorem, such a module satisfies
M=U\left(\mathfrak{n}^{-}\right)\cdot v_{0}
.
A universal highest weight module of weight
\lambda
is called a
Verma module and is denoted by
M\left(\lambda\right)
. One may construct
it by induction from
\mathfrak{b}
: Since
\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{n}
is isomorphic
to
\mathfrak{h}
, there is a
1
-dimensional representation
\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}

of
\mathfrak{b}
with trivial
\mathfrak{n}
-action and
\mathfrak{h}
acting as
\lambda
.
By PBW,
U\left(\mathfrak{b}\right)\leq U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
is a subalgebra
and thus acts on
U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
by left multiplication.

Set

M\left(\lambda\right)\vcentcolon=Ind_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}=U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\otimes_{U\left(\mathfrak{b}\right)}\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}

Which has a structure of a left
U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
-module with
highest weight vector
1\otimes 1
. Again by the PBW theorem,
U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\cong U\left(\mathfrak{n}^{-}\right)\otimes U\left(\mathfrak{b}\right)

and thus
M\left(\lambda\right)\cong U\left(\mathfrak{n}^{-}\right)\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}

as a left
U\left(\mathfrak{n}^{-}\right)
-module, and is therefore a free

U\left(\mathfrak{n}^{-}\right)
-module of rank
1
. Every highest weight
module for
\lambda
is a quotient of
M\left(\lambda\right)
, and
each
M\left(\lambda\right)
has a unique simple quotient
L\left(\lambda\right)

which is finite-dimensional if and only if
\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}
.

We define the shifted action of
W
on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
by
w\cdot\lambda=w\left(\lambda+\rho\right)-\rho
,
and we say that
\lambda,\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
are
W
-linked
if

\mu=w\cdot\lambda
for some
w\in W
.

The center
Z\left(U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\right)
acts on a highest
weight module
M
of weight
\lambda
via scalars:

z\cdot v=\chi_{\lambda}\left(z\right)v\quad\forall v\in M

Thus
\chi_{\lambda}:Z\left(U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\right)\to\mathbb{C}
is
an algebra homomorphism that we call the
central character

associated with
\lambda
. A fundemental theorem on central characters
is due to Harish-Chandra
[
11
, Theorem 1.10]
:

Theorem
.

(Harish-Chandra isomorphism)

(1)

There is an isomorphism of algebras
Z\left(U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\right)\cong S\left(\mathfrak{h}\right)^{W}

where
S\left(\mathfrak{h}\right)^{W}
is the algebra of
W
-invariant
elements of the symmetric algebra of
\mathfrak{h}
.

(2)

For
\lambda,\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
,
\chi_{\lambda}=\chi_{\mu}
if and
only if
\mu
and
\lambda
are
W
-linked.

(3)

Every central character
\chi:Z\left(U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\right)\to\mathbb{C}

is of the form
\chi_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
.

The algebra
S\left(\mathfrak{h}\right)^{W}
of invariants is isomorphic
to a polynomial algebra in
\ell>0
variables, and therefore
Z\left(U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\right)

is (freely) generated as an algebra by
\ell
elements.

2.4.
Lattices in semisimple Lie groups

Let
G
be a semisimple Lie group. A discrete subgroup
\Gamma\leq G

is a
lattice
if
G/\Gamma
has finite volume (i.e. there is
a
\sigma
-finite,
G
-invariant Borel measure
\nu
on
G/\Gamma
).
Lattices possess various rigidity properties, one of the most fundemental
properties is the Borel Density Theorem. The following formulation
is taken from
[
16
, Corollary 4.5.6]
.

Theorem

(Borel Density)
.

Let
G\leq SL_{r}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
be a connected linear semisimple
Lie group and
\Gamma\leq G
a lattice. If
\Gamma
projects densely
onto the maximal compact factor of
G
, then
\Gamma
is Zariski
dense in
G
. That is, if
Q\in\mathbb{R}\left[x_{1,1},\ldots,x_{r,r}\right]

is a polynomial function on
M_{r}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
such that
Q\left(\Gamma\right)=0
,
then
Q\left(G\right)=0
.

A remarkable theorem on lattices in higher rank is Margulis Superrigidity,
which states that in higher rank
G
and
\Gamma\leq G
an irreducible
lattice, essentially all finite dimensional representations of
\Gamma

can be extended to rational representations of
G
up to some bounded
error. For noncocompact
\Gamma
, any representation can be extended
to a rational representation of
G
on some finite index subgroup.
For cocompact lattices this is not necessarily true - there may be
unitary representations that do not factor through a finite quotient.
But we know from Margulis arithmeticity that
\Gamma
is an arithmetic
subgroup of
G
, and therefore we may add compact factors to
G

so that
\Gamma
is commensurable to the integral points of
G^{\prime}=G\times K

(these compact factors are in fact Galois twistings corresponding
to the number field defining the arithmetic group
\Gamma
, see
[
16
, Section 5.5]
).
Then it is true that finite dimensional representations of
\Gamma

extend on some finite index subgroup to a representation of
G^{\prime}

(see
[
16
, Section 16.4]
).

Theorem

(Margulis Superrigidity)
.

Let
G
be a connected, algebraically simply connected semisimple
Lie group of rank
\geq 2
without compact factors that is defined
over
\mathbb{Q}
and let
\Gamma\leq G
be an irreducible lattice.
Then there is a compact semisimple group
K
,
G^{\prime}=G\times K
where

\Gamma\leq G^{\prime}
is an irreducible lattice projecting densely onto

K
, such that for any representation
\varphi:\Gamma\to GL_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

there is a finite index subgroup
\tilde{\Gamma}\leq\Gamma
and a
rational representation
\Phi:G^{\prime}\to GL_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
such
that
\Phi\mid_{\tilde{\Gamma}}=\varphi
.

3.
Semisimple Lie algebras and Lie groups

3.1.
Non-stability of semisimple Lie algebras

For any finitely generated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
\varepsilon
-representation

\varphi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
, we may extend
\varphi
to
all of
U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
and this will be a
pointwise

asymptotic representation of the associative algebra
U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
.
This is because pointwise asymptotic representations are determined
by the action of the generators, and the generators of
\mathfrak{g}
as
a Lie algebra generate
U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
as an associative algebra.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra.
The following theorem shows that although
\mathfrak{g}
has only finitely
many
n
-dimensional representations, it has uncountably many
\varepsilon_{n}
-representations
(for some
\varepsilon_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
) that are nonequivalent:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra
of rank
\ell
and
m=\left|\Phi^{+}\right|
the number of positive
roots. Then there is a family

\left\{\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{k_{n}}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)\;|\;\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\right\}

of
\varepsilon_{n}
-representations, where
\varepsilon_{n}=\frac{2m^{2}}{n}

and
k_{n}=\binom{n+m}{m}-1
, satisfying the following properties:

(1)

The vector space
\mathbb{C}^{k_{n}}=V=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}}V_{\mu}

is a sum of weight spaces, and there is a vector
v_{0}
of weight

\lambda
such that
\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}\left(\mathfrak{n}^{+}\right)\cdot v_{0}=0

and
\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}\left(\mathfrak{n}^{-}\right)=V
.

(2)

Let
z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell}
be generators of
Z\left(U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\right)

as an algebra and
R=\underset{i}{max}\,deg\left(z_{i}\right)
. Denote
by
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}^{\lambda}
its extension to
U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
.
Then

rk\left(\tilde{\varphi}_{n}^{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)-\chi_{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)I_{k_{n}}\right)\leq\left(R+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}

(3)

For any
\lambda,\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
that are not
W
-linked,

\displaystyle d^{strict}\left(\varphi_{n}^{\lambda},A\varphi_{n}^{\mu}A^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\right)^{-1}\left(1-2\left(R+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\left(\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\right)^{-1}

For any
A\in GL_{k_{n}}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
.

(4)

For any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and any representation

\psi
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\lambda
is not
W
-linked to any

\mu
appearing as a highest weight of a subrepresentation of
\psi
,

\displaystyle d^{flex}\left(\varphi_{n}^{\lambda},\psi\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\ell\right)^{-1}\left(1-\ell\left(R+m+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\left(\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\ell\right)^{-1}

In particular, if
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is not
W
-linked to any

\mu\in\Lambda^{+}
, then
\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}
is bounded from representations
of
\mathfrak{g}
, and thus
\mathfrak{g}
is not flexibly
\mathbb{C}
-stable.

The maps
\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}
are constructed via finite dimensional
compressions of infinite dimensional weight representations of
\mathfrak{g}

onto subspaces that are “almost invariant”. Looking at weight
diagrams of such infinite dimensional representations, it is apparent
that
\mathfrak{g}
acts “locally” on the weight spaces, so that if
one takes a large ball in
\mathfrak{h^{*}}
, the relations of
\mathfrak{g}

are satisfied on the interior and not on the boundary, which can be
shown to be small with respect to the whole subspace. One can instead
note that
U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
has polynomial growth and use this
to construct the almost invariant subspaces taking polynomials up
to a given degree in quotients of the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
.

Proof.

We construct
\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}
by compressing the Verma module

M\left(\lambda\right)
to a finite dimensional subspace. Recall
that
M\left(\lambda\right)
is free as a
U\left(\mathfrak{n}^{-}\right)
-module,
and therefore has a basis of the form

M\left(\lambda\right)=span\left\{y_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots y_{m}^{r_{m}}\cdot v_{0}\;|\;r_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\right\},

where
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}
are the negative root spaces and
v_{0}

the highest weight vector. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, Denote by

D_{n}=span\left\{y_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots y_{m}^{r_{m}}\cdot v_{0}\;|\;\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}\leq n\right\}

the subspace spanned by all monomials in
U\left(\mathfrak{n}^{-}\right)

of degree
\leq n
acting on
v_{0}
. A standard partition argument
shows that there are
\binom{n+m-1}{m-1}
such combinations for fixed

a
, and

dim\left(D_{n}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{j+m-1}{m-1}=\binom{n+m}{m}-1.

Define the action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
D_{n}
by

\left(\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}\left(x\right)\right)\left(y_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots y_{m}^{r_{m}}\cdot v_{0}\right)=\begin{cases}xy_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots y_{m}^{r_{m}}\cdot v_{0}&xy_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots y_{m}^{r_{m}}\cdot v_{0}\in D_{n}\\
0&else\end{cases}.

We claim that the image of this action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
D_{n-1}
is
contained in
D_{n}
; For
\mathfrak{n}^{-}
, after reordering using a
PBW basis for
\mathfrak{n}^{-}
we have a sum of monomials of degree at
most
n
. The action of
\mathfrak{h}
is by scalars and therefore any

D_{k}
is
\mathfrak{h}
-invariant. We prove by induction on
n
that

\mathfrak{n}^{+}\cdot D_{n}\subseteq D_{n}
: When
n=0
this is obvious. Assume
it holds for
n-1
, then for
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}
(
\beta>0
) we
have

xy_{1}^{r_{1}}y_{2}^{r_{2}}\cdots y_{m}^{r_{m}}\cdot v_{0}=y_{1}xy_{1}^{r_{1}-1}y_{2}^{r_{2}}\cdots y_{m}^{r_{m}}\cdot v_{0}+zy_{1}^{r_{1}-1}y_{2}^{r_{2}}\cdots y_{m}^{r_{m}}\cdot v_{0}

for some
z\in\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{1}+\beta}
. By the induction hypothesis,

xy_{1}^{r_{1}-1}y_{2}^{r_{2}}\cdots y_{m}^{r_{m}}\cdot v_{0}\in D_{n-1}\implies y_{1}xy_{1}^{r_{1}-1}y_{2}^{r_{2}}\cdots y_{m}^{r_{m}}\cdot v_{0}\in D_{n}
.
Moreover, if
\beta-\alpha_{1}>0
then again use the induction hypothesis
for
zy_{1}^{r_{1}-1}y_{2}^{r_{2}}\cdots y_{m}^{r_{m}}\cdot v_{0}
,
and if
\beta-\alpha_{1}\leq 0
then
z\in\mathfrak{n}^{-}
or
z\in\mathfrak{h}

and in any case
zy_{1}^{r_{1}-1}y_{2}^{r_{2}}\cdots y_{m}^{r_{m}}\cdot v_{0}\in D_{n}
.

Now since
\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\cdot D_{n-1}\subseteq D_{n}
,
we have
\left[\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}\left(x\right),\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}\left(y\right)\right]\cdot D_{n-2}\subseteq D_{n}

for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}
acts as
M\left(\lambda\right)

when restriced to
D_{n-2}
, we have
\left(\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}\left(\left[x,y\right]\right)-\left[\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}\left(x\right),\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}\left(y\right)\right]\right)\cdot D_{n-2}=0
.
Thus

\displaystyle rank\left(\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}\left(\left[x,y\right]\right)-\left[\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}\left(x\right),\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}\left(y\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq dim\,D_{n}-dim\,D_{n-2}=\binom{n+m-1}{n}+\binom{n+m-2}{n}

\displaystyle\implies\varepsilon_{n}\leq\frac{\binom{n+m-1}{n}+\binom{n+m-2}{m-1}}{\binom{n+m}{m}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{n+m}\left(1+\frac{mn}{\left(n+m-1\right)}\right)\leq\frac{m}{n+m}\left(1+m\right)<\frac{2m^{2}}{n}.

All of the generators
z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell}
of
Z\left(U\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\right)

have degree at most
R
, so when restricted to
D_{n-R}
they act
as the scalars
\chi_{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)
. Thus

\displaystyle rank\left(\tilde{\varphi}_{n}^{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)-\chi_{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)I_{k_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq dimD_{n}-dimD_{n-R}

\displaystyle=\binom{n+m}{m}-\binom{n-R+m}{m}

\displaystyle\implies rk\left(\tilde{\varphi}_{n}^{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)-\chi_{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)I_{k_{n}}\right)\leq 1-\frac{\binom{n-R+m}{m}}{\binom{n+m}{m}}\leq 1-\frac{\left(n-R\right)^{m}}{\left(n+m\right)^{m}}.

Using the inequality
log\left(1+x\right)\geq\frac{x}{1+x}
,

log\left(\frac{\left(n-R\right)^{m}}{\left(n+m\right)^{m}}\right)=mlog\left(1-\frac{m+R}{n+m}\right)\geq-m\frac{m+R}{n-R},

so that

\displaystyle rk\left(\tilde{\varphi}_{n}^{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)-\chi_{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)I_{k_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1-exp\left(-m\frac{m+R}{n-R}\right)\leq m\frac{m+R}{n-R}

\displaystyle<m\frac{m+2R}{n}<\left(R+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}.

This proves
2
.

For
3
, by Harish-Chandra’s theorem
\chi_{\lambda}\neq\chi_{\mu}

so that they differ on at least one of the generators, say
z_{j}
.
Therefore, for any
A\in GL_{k_{n}}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
,

\displaystyle rk\left(\tilde{\varphi}_{n}^{\lambda}\left(z_{j}\right)-A\tilde{\varphi}_{n}^{\mu}\left(z_{j}\right)A^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq rk\left(\chi_{\lambda}\left(z_{j}\right)-A\chi_{\mu}\left(z_{j}\right)A^{-1}\right)-2\left(R+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle=1-2\left(R+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}.

Let
\eta=\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}-A\varphi_{n}^{\mu}A^{-1}
and
\delta=\underset{x\in\mathfrak{g}}{max}\;rk\left(\eta\left(x\right)\right)
.
Every
z_{i}
is a polynomial of degree
\leq R
in the PBW basis,
so it is a sum of at most
\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}-1
monomials of degree
at most
R
. By
[
3
, Lemma 3.3]
we therefore
have

rk\left(\tilde{\varphi}_{n}^{\lambda}\left(z_{j}\right)-A\tilde{\varphi}_{n}^{\mu}\left(z_{j}\right)A^{-1}\right)\leq\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\delta.

Combining the above we get

1-2\left(R+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}\leq\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\delta

\implies\delta\geq\left(\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\right)^{-1}\left(1-2\left(R+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}\right),

proving
3
.

Let
\psi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{N}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
be a representation,
where
N\leq\left(1+\varepsilon_{n}m\right)k_{n}
, and suppose that

\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is not
W
-linked to any
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}

appearing as a highest weight of a subrepresentation of
\psi
. By
complete reducibility, it is a (conjugation of) a direct sum of isotypic
components

\psi=A\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{q}\psi_{\mu_{i}}\right)A^{-1},

where
\psi_{\mu_{i}}
is the direct sum of copies of the irreducible
representation with highest weight
\mu_{i}
(which must in particular
be dominant integral weights). The central character of
\psi
is
therefore

\chi_{\psi}=A\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{q}\chi_{\mu_{i}}I_{d_{i}}\right)A^{-1}.

Let
\eta=\widehat{\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}}-\widehat{\psi}
and
\delta=\underset{x\in\mathfrak{g}}{max}\;rk\left(\eta\left(x\right)\right)
.
Similar to the calculation above, for each
1\leq i\leq\ell
we have

\displaystyle rk\left(\tilde{\psi}\left(z_{i}\right)-\chi_{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)I_{k_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq rk\left(\tilde{\psi}\left(z_{i}\right)-A\tilde{\varphi}_{n}^{\lambda}\left(z_{j}\right)A^{-1}\right)+rk\left(A\tilde{\varphi}_{n}^{\lambda}\left(z_{j}\right)A^{-1}-\chi_{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)I_{k_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\delta+\left(R+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}.

Since
N-k_{n}\leq\varepsilon_{n}m
, we have

\displaystyle rk\left(\tilde{\psi}\left(z_{i}\right)-\chi_{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)I_{N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\delta+\left(R+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}+\left(N-k_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\delta+\left(R+m+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}.

Thinking of
\widehat{\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}},\psi
as embedded in
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
,
the subspaces
W_{i}=ker\left(\tilde{\psi}\left(z_{i}\right)-\chi_{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)I_{N}\right)\leq\mathbb{C}^{N}

have dimension at least
k_{n}\left(1-\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\delta-\left(R+m+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}\right)

and for any
v\in W
,

\tilde{\psi}\left(z_{i}\right)v=\chi_{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)v,

hence
\tilde{\psi}\left(z_{i}\right)
has an eigenspace of dimension
at least
k_{n}\left(1-\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\delta-\left(R+m+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}\right)

corresponding to the eigenvalue
\chi_{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)
.
Suppose the intersection

W=\bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell}W_{i}

contains a nonzero vector
0\neq w\in W
, then
w
satisfies for
any
1\leq i\leq\ell

\tilde{\psi}\left(z_{i}\right)w=\chi_{\lambda}\left(z_{i}\right)w,

and in particular it follows that
w
must be in some isotypic component
of
\psi
. This is a contradiction because
\chi_{\lambda}\neq\chi_{\mu_{i}}

by assumption and Harish-Chandra’s theorem, so
W=0
. But since all

W_{i}
are subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
,

\displaystyle 0=dim\,W

\displaystyle\geq N-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(N-dim\,W_{i}\right)\geq N-\ell k_{n}\left(\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\delta+\left(R+m+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\geq N\left(1-\ell\left(\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\delta+\left(R+m+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\implies 1\leq\ell\left(\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\delta\right)+\ell\left(R+m+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}

\displaystyle\delta\geq\left(\binom{R+\ell}{\ell}R\ell\right)^{-1}\left(1-\ell\left(R+m+1\right)\varepsilon_{n}\right)

∎

By complexifying real Lie algebras, we can prove similar results for
real Lie algebras.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite dimensional real Lie algebra
and
\varphi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
an
\varepsilon
-representation.

Then there is a
4\varepsilon
-representation
\tilde{\varphi}:\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

extending
\varphi
. Moreover, if
d^{strict}\left(\varphi,\psi\right)=\delta

then
d^{strict}\left(\tilde{\varphi},\tilde{\psi}\right)\leq 2\delta

(and similarly for

).

Proof.

Define
\tilde{\varphi}
in the obvious way by
\tilde{\varphi}\left(x+iy\right)=\varphi\left(x\right)+i\varphi\left(y\right)
.
Then

\left[\tilde{\varphi}\left(x+iy\right),\tilde{\varphi}\left(w+iv\right)\right]=\left[\varphi\left(x\right),\varphi\left(w\right)\right]-\left[\varphi\left(y\right),\varphi\left(v\right)\right]+i\left(\left[\varphi\left(x\right),\varphi\left(v\right)\right]+\left[\varphi\left(y\right),\varphi\left(w\right)\right]\right)

\tilde{\varphi}\left(\left[x+iy,w+iv\right]\right)=\varphi\left(\left[x,w\right]\right)-\varphi\left(\left[y,v\right]\right)+i\left(\varphi\left(\left[x,v\right]\right)+\varphi\left(\left[y,w\right]\right)\right),

and it follows that
\tilde{\varphi}
is a
4\varepsilon
-representation.

If
\psi
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
d^{strict}\left(\varphi,\psi\right)=\delta

(respectively
d^{flex}\left(\varphi,\psi\right)=\delta
) then for any

x+iy\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}

rk\left(\tilde{\varphi}\left(x+iy\right)-\tilde{\psi}\left(x+iy\right)\right)\leq rk\left(\varphi\left(x\right)-\psi\left(x\right)\right)+rk\left(i\left(\varphi\left(y\right)-\psi\left(y\right)\right)\right)\leq 2\delta

∎

\mathbb{C}

\mathbb{C}

If

\tilde{\varphi}

\varphi
of

∎

Notice that the proof of Theorem

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\psi_{1}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

\lambda
, and let
\psi_{2}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

Suppose

3.2.
Semisimple Lie groups and lattices

One of the motivations to consider stability of Lie algebras is that
almost representations arise naturally as differentials of (smooth)
almost representations of Lie groups. Unlike matrix norms, the rank
metric is in general compatible with differentiation:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
B_{\delta}\left(M\right)\subseteq M_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

be the
\delta
-ball around
M
in
M_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
,

B_{\delta}\left(M\right)=\left\{A\in M_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)\;|\;rk\left(A-M\right)\leq\delta\right\},

and let
\gamma:\left(-1,1\right)\to B_{\delta}\left(M\right)
be
a smooth curve. Then
\frac{d\gamma}{dt}:\left(-1,1\right)\to B_{2\delta}\left(0\right)
,
so that
rk\left(\frac{d\gamma}{dt}\mid_{t_{0}}\right)\leq 2\delta
.

Proof.

Being contained in
B_{\delta}\left(M\right)
is equivalent to the
vanishing of all the
\left(k+1\right)
-minors of
\gamma\left(t\right)-M

for
k=\left\lfloor\delta n\right\rfloor
, and is therefore a closed
condition - thus
rank\left(\bullet\right)
is a lower semicontinuous
function. We therefore have

\displaystyle rank\left(\underset{h\to 0}{lim}\,\frac{\gamma\left(t_{0}+h\right)-\gamma\left(t_{0}\right)}{h}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{h\to 0}{liminf}\,rank\left(\frac{\gamma\left(t_{0}+h\right)-\gamma\left(t_{0}\right)}{h}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{h}{sup}\;rank\left(\gamma\left(t_{0}+h\right)-M\right)+rank\left(M-\gamma\left(t_{0}\right)\right)\leq 2\delta n

∎

Proposition 3.6
.

•

Let
G
be a Lie group and
\Phi:G\to GL_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
a
smooth uniform
\varepsilon
-representation. Then
\varphi\vcentcolon=d\Phi\mid_{id}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

is an
8\varepsilon
-representation.

•

If
\Psi:G\to GL_{N}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
is another smooth map,
\psi=d\Psi

and
d^{strict}\left(\Phi,\Psi\right)\leq\delta
(respectively
d^{flex}\left(\Phi,\Psi\right)\leq\delta
),
then
d^{strict}\left(\varphi,\psi\right)\leq 2\delta
(
d^{flex}\left(\varphi,\psi\right)\leq 2\delta
).

Proof.

For any
g,h\in G
we have by the triangle inequality

rk\left(\Phi\left(ghg^{-1}\right)-\Phi\left(g\right)\Phi\left(h\right)\Phi\left(g\right)^{-1}\right)\leq 2\varepsilon.

Written differently setting
\Psi_{g}\left(h\right)=ghg^{-1}
,

rk\left(\Phi\left(\Psi_{g}\left(h\right)\right)-\Psi_{\Phi\left(g\right)}\left(\Phi\left(h\right)\right)\right)\leq 2\varepsilon.

Taking the differential at
h=id
we get by Lemma
3.5

that

(3.1)

rk\left(\varphi\left(Ad_{g}\left(x\right)\right)-Ad_{\Phi\left(g\right)}\left(\varphi\left(x\right)\right)\right)\leq 4\varepsilon,

for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. Taking again the differential at
g=id
,

rk\left(\varphi\left(ad\left(y\right)\left(x\right)\right)-ad\left(\varphi\left(y\right),\varphi\left(x\right)\right)\right)\leq 8\varepsilon.

Thus,

rk\left(\varphi\left(\left[y,x\right]\right)-\left[\varphi\left(y\right),\varphi\left(x\right)\right]\right)\leq 8\varepsilon.

The second assertion follows by applying again Lemma
3.5

and differentiating
\Phi\left(g\right)-\Psi\left(g\right)
.
∎

A natural way to construct almost representations of a Lie group
G

is by compressing representations as in Lemma
2.1
.
It is straightforward to check that taking differentials commutes
with compressing, so that the differential of a compression of a representation

\Phi
of
G
at
id
will be a compression of the differential

d\Phi
on
\mathfrak{g}
.

It therefore follows from Corollary
3.3
and Proposition

3.6
, together with Proposition
3.4
,

Corollary 3.7
.

Semisimple Lie groups are not strictly
\mathbb{C}
-stable.

Proof.

Assume
G
is a complex Lie group, the real case follows from Corollary

3.3
. A codimension
1
compression of a highest weight
representation
\Phi_{n}:G\to GL_{k_{n}}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
is a
\frac{2}{k_{n}-1}
-representation
by Lemma
2.1
. Take a sequence
\Phi_{n}
of such
representations with
k_{n}\to\infty
, and let
\check{\Phi}_{n}

be the corresponding compression. If
G
were strictly stable, there
would be a sequence
\Psi_{n}:G\to GL_{k_{n}}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
of
representations with
d^{strict}\left(\check{\Phi}_{n},\Psi_{n}\right)\to 0
.
Taking differentials, it follows from Proposition
3.6

that
d^{strict}\left(d\check{\Phi}_{n},d\Psi_{n}\right)\to 0
. But
this is a contradiction to Proposition
3.4
, whereby

d\check{\Phi}_{n}
is bounded in rank (in the strict sense) from
any representation of
\mathfrak{g}
.
∎

We would like to apply this result to lattices in higher rank. Since

rk\left(\bullet\right)
is a polynomial function on the entries
of a matrix, two maps that are close on a Zariski dense subgroup are
close on the entire group:

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
G\leq SL_{r}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
be a linear
Lie group and
H\leq G
a Zariski dense subgroup. Suppose
\Phi,\Psi:G\to GL_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

are rational maps satisfying

\underset{h\in H}{sup}\,rk\left(\Phi\left(h\right)-\Psi\left(h\right)\right)\leq\varepsilon.

Then

\underset{g\in G}{sup}\,rk\left(\Phi\left(g\right)-\Psi\left(g\right)\right)\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

The condition
rk\left(\Phi\left(g\right)-\Psi\left(g\right)\right)\leq\varepsilon

is polynomial in the matrix entries, and is equivalent to the vanishing
of all
\left(k+1\right)
-minors for
k=\left\lfloor\varepsilon n\right\rfloor
.
Since
\Phi,\Psi
are rational, it follows from Zariski density of

H
that
\underset{g\in G}{sup}\,rk\left(\Phi\left(g\right)-\Psi\left(g\right)\right)\leq\varepsilon
.
∎

We may now use Borel Density and apply Lemma
3.8
to
lattices in semisimple Lie groups.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
G\leq SL_{r}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
be a
connected linear semisimple Lie group and
\Gamma\leq G
a lattice
that projects densely into the maximal compact factor of
G
. Suppose

\Phi,\Psi:G\to GL_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
are rational maps satisfying

\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma}{sup}\,rk\left(\Phi\left(\gamma\right)-\Psi\left(\gamma\right)\right)\leq\varepsilon.

Then

\underset{g\in G}{sup}\,rk\left(\Phi\left(g\right)-\Psi\left(g\right)\right)\leq\varepsilon.

Margulis Superrigidity then implies that lattices in higher rank are
not strictly stable.

proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By adding compact factors to
G
, we may assume WLOG that
\Gamma

is commensurable to
G_{\mathbb{Z}}
, and that it projects densely onto
the maximal compact factor of
G
.

A codimension
1
compression of a highest weight representation

\Phi:G\to GL_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
is a
\frac{2}{n-1}
-representation
by Lemma
2.1
. Let
\varphi:\Gamma\to GL_{n-1}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

be the restriction of
\Phi
to the lattice
\Gamma
. Suppose
\psi:\Gamma\to GL_{n-1}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

is a representation such that
d^{strict}\left(\varphi,\psi\right)=\delta
.
Then by Margulis Superrigidity, restricting to a finite index subgroup

\bar{\Gamma}\leq\Gamma
,
\psi
is the restriction of a representation

\Psi
of
G
. By Corollary
3.9
, since
\Phi,\Psi

are rational and since
\bar{\Gamma}
is also a lattice that projects
densely into the maximal compact factor, we have

d^{strict}\left(\Phi,\Psi\right)\leq\delta,

contradicting Corollary
3.7
.
∎

3.3.
Remarks on lifting almost representations of Lie algebras

It is natural to ask whether the converse of Proposition
3.6

holds, i.e. whether almost representations of real Lie algebras always
arise as differentials of almost representations of the corresponding
simply connected Lie group. In particular, whether the
\varepsilon_{n}
-representations

\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}
in Theorem
3.1
, restricted to some
real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, arise as differentials of almost representations
of the corresponding Lie group
G
. Indeed, a positive answer for
any
\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}
for
\lambda
such that
\chi_{\lambda}

is not the central character of any finite dimensional representation,
would imply that Lie groups and lattices in higher rank Lie groups
are not
flexibly

\mathbb{C}
-stable, using the same reasoning as
in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We do not know the answer
to this question.

However, we do note that there seem to be some difficulties in lifting
such almost representations of
\mathfrak{g}
to those of
G
; Recall that
the Weyl group of
G
is
W=N_{G}\left(\mathfrak{h}\right)/C_{G}\left(\mathfrak{h}\right)
,
so by equation
3.1
in the proof of Proposition
3.6
,
we have

rk\left(\varphi\left(wxw^{-1}\right)-\Phi\left(w\right)\varphi\left(x\right)\Phi\left(w\right)^{-1}\right)\leq 4\varepsilon,

for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
w\in W
. We can interpret this by saying
that
\varphi
has to be almost invariant under the Weyl group action.

Consider the simplest case of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

(whose representations can be seen as the building blocks of representations
of all semisimple Lie algebras). Let

\mathbf{e}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{f}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{h}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}

be the standard basis of
\mathfrak{g}
, then the nontrivial element
w\in W

acts on
\mathfrak{g}
by

\mathbf{h}\mapsto-\mathbf{h},\;\mathbf{e}\mapsto\mathbf{-\mathbf{f}},\;\mathbf{f}\mapsto-\mathbf{e},

which when applied to
L\left(\lambda\right)
results in the
lowest
weight module
\bar{L}\left(-\lambda\right)
with lowest weight

-\lambda
(see
[
15
, 3]
). When
\lambda
is dominant
integral (in this case
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
), these modules are
indeed isomorphic. But if
\lambda\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, this is not
true, but they do have the same central character. If
\varphi_{n}^{\lambda}

can be lifted to some
\Phi_{n}^{\lambda}
, this means that finite
dimensional compressions of
L\left(\lambda\right)
are arbitrarily
close in rank to compressions of (a conjugation of)
\bar{L}\left(-\lambda\right)
.
The technique of central characters only helps in separating weight
modules with distinct central characters, but in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)

for every central character
\chi_{\lambda}
there is a family of
weight modules

\left\{V\left(\xi,\chi_{\lambda}\right)\;|\;\xi\in\mathbb{C}/2\mathbb{Z}\right\}

having central character
\chi_{\lambda}
and spectrum
\xi
(see

[
15
, 3.3]
). We do not know whether modules with the same
central character but different spectrum can be close to one another,
in the sense that their respective finite dimensional compressions
are arbitrarily close in the rank metric.

4.
Free groups

In
[
17
]
, Rolli constructed uniform almost representations
of nonabelian free groups in the operator norm that are bounded from
true representations (see also
[
5
, Section 3]
).
This inspired a construction by Becker and Chapman
[
4
, Section 5]

of uniform almost homomorphisms to permutation groups
S_{n}
, that
are bounded from true representations.

We construct similar almost representations in the rank metric inspired
by Rolli’s construction. It is interesting to note that it is in some
sense opposite to Rolli’s construction; There, the maps
\varphi
behave
locally like representations that are contained in some
\delta
-ball
around the identity, but since
GL_{n}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
has no small
subgroups, it is bounded from true representations where high powers
of the generators must be bounded away from the identity. In the case
of the rank metric, a finite set of elements close to the identity
will generate an algebra that is contained in a small ball around
the identity (so in a sense there are “many” small subgroups),
but the almost representations that we construct have elements as
far as possible from the identity.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{2}=\left\langle a,b\right\rangle
and

n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there is a
\frac{3}{n}
-representation
\varphi:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to GL_{n}\left(F\right)

such that

rk\left(\varphi\left(a\right)-I_{n}\right),rk\left(\varphi\left(b\right)-I_{n}\right)\leq\frac{1}{n},

and such that there is an element
w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
satisfying

rk\left(\varphi\left(w\right)-I_{n}\right)=1.

Moreover, for any representation
\psi:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to GL_{N}\left(F\right)
,

d^{flex}\left(\varphi,\psi\right)\geq\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{6n}
.

Proof.

Let
\delta=\frac{1}{n}
and let

B_{\delta}=\left\{A\in GL_{n}\left(F\right)\;|\;rk\left(A-I\right)\leq\delta\right\}

be the
\delta
-ball around
I
. Let
\tau:\mathbb{Z}\to B_{\delta}

be a symmetric map so that
\tau\left(k\right)^{-1}=\tau\left(k^{-1}\right)
,

\tau\left(0\right)=I
, and define
\varphi:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to GL_{n}\left(F\right)

by acting on a reduced word of the form

w=a^{n_{1}}b^{m_{1}}\cdots a^{n_{k}}b^{m_{k}}

by

\varphi\left(w\right)=\tau\left(m_{1}\right)\cdots\tau\left(m_{k}\right).

Allowing
n_{1}=0
and
m_{k}=0
defines
\varphi
on all reduced words.
Now for words
w_{1}=a^{n_{1}}b^{m_{1}}\cdots a^{n_{k}}b^{m_{k}}
,

w_{2}=a^{\ell_{1}}b^{q_{1}}\cdots a^{\ell_{d}}b^{q_{d}}
, if
m_{k}\neq 0

and
\ell_{1}\neq 0
, there is no cancellation and
\varphi\left(w_{1}w_{2}\right)=\varphi\left(w_{1}\right)\varphi\left(w_{2}\right)
,
and the same occurs if
m_{k}=\ell_{1}=0
. If exactly one of
m_{k},\ell_{1}

equals
0
, assume WLOG that
m_{k}=0
and
\ell_{1}\neq 0
, and
the other case is proven similarly. We have

w_{1}w_{2}=a^{n_{1}}b^{m_{1}}\cdots a^{n_{k-r}}b^{m_{k-r}}ca^{\ell_{r}}b^{q_{r}}\cdots a^{\ell_{d}}b^{q_{d}}

for
c
of the form
c=a^{\alpha}b^{\beta}
, which occurs if
n_{k-i+1}=-\ell_{i}

and
m_{k-i}=-q_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r-2
. Symmetry of
\tau
ensures
that the corresponding powers cancel so that

\displaystyle\varphi\left(w_{1}\right)\varphi\left(w_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\tau\left(m_{1}\right)\cdots\tau\left(m_{k}\right)\tau\left(q_{1}\right)\cdots\tau\left(q_{d}\right)

\displaystyle=\tau\left(m_{1}\right)\cdots\tau\left(m_{k-r}\right)\tau\left(m_{k-\left(r-1\right)}\right)\tau\left(q_{r-1}\right)\tau\left(q_{r}\right)\cdots\tau\left(q_{d}\right),

and

\varphi\left(w_{1}w_{2}\right)=\tau\left(m_{1}\right)\cdots\tau\left(m_{k-r}\right)\tau\left(\beta\right)\tau\left(q_{r}\right)\cdots\tau\left(q_{d}\right)

\implies rk\left(\varphi\left(w_{1}w_{2}\right)-\varphi\left(w_{1}\right)\varphi\left(w_{2}\right)\right)=rk\left(\tau\left(m_{k-\left(r-1\right)}\right)\tau\left(q_{r-1}\right)-\tau\left(\beta\right)\right)\leq 3\delta.

It is thus a uniform
3\delta
-representation. Assume further that

\left\{\tau\left(k\right)\right\}_{k=1}^{n}
generate a matrix

M
with
rank\left(M-I_{n}\right)=n
, thus

\varphi\left(abab^{2}\cdots ab^{t}\right)=\tau\left(1\right)\cdots\tau\left(t\right)=M,

for some
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
\psi:\Gamma\to GL_{N}\left(F\right)
be a representation and

\varepsilon_{0}=d^{flex}\left(\varphi,\psi\right)
. Since
rk\left(\varphi\left(a\right)-I_{n}\right)=0

and
rk\left(\varphi\left(b\right)-I_{n}\right)\leq\delta
, we have

\displaystyle rk\left(\psi\left(a\right)-I_{N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq rk\left(\psi\left(a\right)-\varphi\left(a\right)\right)+rk\left(\varphi\left(a\right)-I_{n}\right)+rk\left(I_{n}-I_{N}\right)\leq 2\varepsilon_{0},

\displaystyle rk\left(\psi\left(b\right)-I_{N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq rk\left(\psi\left(b\right)-\varphi\left(b\right)\right)+rk\left(\varphi\left(b\right)-I_{n}\right)+rk\left(I_{n}-I_{N}\right)\leq 2\varepsilon_{0}+\delta.

Since
\psi
is a representation, any
v\in W\vcentcolon=ker\left(\psi\left(a\right)-I_{N}\right)\cap ker\left(\psi\left(b\right)-I_{N}\right)

is fixed by any word in
\psi\left(a\right),\psi\left(b\right)
.
We have

\displaystyle dim\left(W\right)

\displaystyle\geq N-rank\left(\psi\left(a\right)-I_{N}\right)-rank\left(\psi\left(b\right)-I_{N}\right)

\displaystyle\geq N-\left(4\varepsilon_{0}+\delta\right)n.

Thus for any
w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
,

\displaystyle rank\left(\psi\left(w\right)-I_{N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N-dim\left(W\right)\leq\left(4\varepsilon_{0}+\delta\right)n

\implies rk\left(\psi\left(abab^{2}\cdots ab^{t}\right)-I_{N}\right)\leq 4\varepsilon_{0}+\delta.

But by construction we also have

rk\left(\varphi\left(abab^{2}\cdots ab^{t}\right)-I_{n}\right)=1

\displaystyle\implies rk\left(\psi\left(abab^{2}\cdots ab^{t}\right)-I_{N}\right)\geq

\displaystyle 1-rk\left(\psi\left(abab^{2}\cdots ab^{t}\right)-\varphi\left(abab^{2}\cdots ab^{t}\right)\right)-rk\left(I_{N}-I_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-2\varepsilon_{0},

and hence

1-2\varepsilon_{0}\leq 4\varepsilon_{0}+\delta

\implies\varepsilon_{0}\geq\frac{1}{6}-\frac{\delta}{6}=\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{6n}.

∎

proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\pi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
be a surjection, and choose
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma

such that
\pi\left(\gamma_{1}\right)=a
and
\pi\left(\gamma_{2}\right)=b
.
Define
\tilde{\varphi}\vcentcolon=\varphi\circ\pi:\Gamma\to GL_{n}\left(F\right)

where
\varphi
is as in Proposition
4.1
. Then

\displaystyle rk\left(\tilde{\varphi}\left(\gamma_{1}\right)-I_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle rk\left(\tilde{\varphi}\left(\gamma_{2}\right)-I_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n},

and there is a word
w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
such that

rk\left(\tilde{\varphi}\left(w\left(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\right)-I_{n}\right)\right)=1,

where
w\left(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\right)
is the word substitution
in
\Gamma
. Arguing similarly to the proof of Proposition
4.1
,
we have for any representation
\psi:\Gamma\to GL_{N}\left(F\right)
,

1-2d^{flex}\left(\tilde{\varphi},\psi\right)\leq rk\left(w\left(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\right)-I_{N}\right)\leq 4d^{flex}\left(\tilde{\varphi},\psi\right)+\frac{1}{n}

\implies d^{flex}\left(\tilde{\varphi},\psi\right)\geq\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{6n}.

∎

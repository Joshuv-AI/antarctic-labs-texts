Abstract
. In this article we derive an explicit diameter bound for graphs satisfying the so-called curvature dimension conditions C ​ D ​ ( K , n ) CD(K,n) . This refines a recent result due to Liu, Münch and Peyerimhoff when the dimension n n is finite.

1.
Introduction

First, we prepare some notions about discrete geometric analysis (for further materials in this rapidly growing area, one can refer to the monographs by Bakry-Gentil-Ledoux
[
1
]
and Keller-Lenz-Wojciechowski
[
4
]
).

Definition 1.1

(Weighted graphs and nondegeneracy)
.

A triple
G=(V,w,m)
is called a
weighted (and measured) graph
if
V
is a countable set, if
w:V^{2}\to[0,\infty)
is symmetric, i.e.,

w(x,y)=w(y,x),\quad\forall\,x,y\in V,

and zero on the diagonal, i.e.,

w(x,x)=0,\quad\forall\,x\in V,

and if
m:V\to(0,\infty)
. We call
V
the
vertex set
,
w
the
edge weight
and
m
the
vertex measure
. If
w(x,y)>0
, we write
x\sim y.
The measure
m
on
V
is called
non-degenerate
, if
\inf_{x\in V}m(x)>0
.

Definition 1.2

(Locally finite graphs, vertex degree, and connectedness)
.

A triple
G=(V,w,m)
is called a
locally finite graph
if

\deg(x):=\sum_{y\in V}w(x,y)<\infty,\quad\forall\,x\in V.

Moreover, it is called a
connected graph
if for all
x,y\in V
there exists a sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}\subset V
such that
x=x_{0}\sim x_{1}\sim\cdots\sim x_{n}=y
.

Definition 1.3

(Laplacians on graphs)
.

We define the
graph Laplacian

\Delta
as

\Delta f(x):=\frac{1}{m(x)}\sum_{y\in V}w(x,y)(f(y)-f(x)),\quad x\in V.

Here,
G=(V,w,m)
is a locally finite graph and
f:V\to\mathbb{R}.

Definition 1.4

(Bakry-Émery curvature dimension conditions)
.

The
Bakry-Émery operators

\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{2}
are defined via

2\Gamma(f,g):=\Delta(fg)-f\Delta g-g\Delta f

and

2\Gamma_{2}(f,g):=\Delta\Gamma(f,g)-\Gamma(f,\Delta g)-\Gamma(g,\Delta f).

We write for simplicity
\Gamma f=\Gamma(f,f)
and
\Gamma_{2}(f)=\Gamma_{2}(f,f)
.

A graph
G
is said to satisfy the
curvature dimension condition

CD(K,n)
for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in(0,\infty]
if for all
f
,

\Gamma_{2}(f)\geq\frac{1}{n}(\Delta f)^{2}+K\Gamma f.

Definition 1.5

(Combinatorial distance)
.

Let
G=(V,w,m)
be a connected graph.
We define the
combinatorial distance

d:V^{2}\to[0,\infty)
via

\displaystyle d(x,y):=\min\{n:\mbox{ there exist }x=x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}=y\mbox{ s.t. }x_{i}\sim x_{i-1}\,\,\forall\,i=1\ldots n\}

and the
combinatorial diameter
as

\operatorname{diam}_{d}(G):=\sup_{x,y\in V}d(x,y)
.

Definition 1.6

(Resistance distance)
.

Let
G=(V,w,m)
be a locally finite graph.
We define the
resistance distance

\rho:V^{2}\to[0,\infty)
via

\rho(x,y):=\sup\{|f(y)-f(x)|:\left\|\Gamma f\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}

where
\left\|\Gamma f\right\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in V}(\Gamma f)(x)
, and the
resistance diameter
as

\operatorname{diam}_{\rho}(G):=\sup_{x,y\in V}\rho(x,y).

By
[
5
, Lemma 1.4]
, we have the following relationship between the combinatorial and resistance distances. We also define

\operatorname{Deg}(x):=\frac{\deg(x)}{m(x)}\text{ \,\,and \,\,}\operatorname{Deg}_{\max}:=\sup_{x\in V}\operatorname{Deg}(x).

Lemma 1.7

(Liu, Münch and Peyerimhoff)
.

Let
G=(V,w,m)
be a locally finite connected graph with
\operatorname{Deg}_{\max}<\infty
. Then for all
x_{0},y_{0}\in V
,

d(x_{0},y_{0})\leq\sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{Deg}_{\max}}{2}}\rho(x_{0},y_{0}).

The following explicit resistance distance bound is our main result.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
G=(V,w,m)
be a locally finite, connected, complete graph with non-degenerate vertex measure satisfying
CD(K,n)
for
K>0
,
n<\infty
and
\operatorname{Deg}_{\max}<\infty
.
Then for all
x_{0},y_{0}\in V
,

(1.1)

\rho(x_{0},y_{0})\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{K}}\left(\arcsin\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{Kn}{2\operatorname{Deg}\left(x_{0}\right)}+1}}+\arcsin\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{Kn}{2\operatorname{Deg}\left(y_{0}\right)}+1}}\right).

Remark 1.9
.

The completeness assumption is needed when using certain functional consequences of the curvature dimension conditions, see Lemma
2.1
below. For the definition of completeness of graphs, see
[
3
, Sections 1 and 2.1]
or
[
2
, Definition 2.9, Definition 2.13]
.
Our resistance distance bound refines a recent result due to Liu, Münch and Peyerimhoff in the sense the two
arcsin
-s in (
1.1
) sum to
\pi
as
\min\{\operatorname{Deg}\left(x_{0}\right),\operatorname{Deg}\left(y_{0}\right)\}\rightarrow\infty
. (
1.1
) also holds when
n=\infty
, see
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Remark 1.10
.

For diameter bounds via a different curvature, see Steinerberger
[
6
]
.

Corollary 1.11

(Diameter bounds under
CD(K,n)
)
.

Let
G=(V,w,m)
be a locally finite, connected, complete graph with non-degenerate vertex measure satisfying
CD(K,n)
for
n<\infty
and
\operatorname{Deg}_{\max}<\infty
.
Then

\operatorname{diam}_{d}(G)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2n\operatorname{Deg}_{\max}}{K}}\arcsin\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{Kn}{2\operatorname{Deg}_{\max}}+1}}.

2.
Proofs of Theorem
1.8
and Corollary
1.11

By
[
5
, Lemma 2.3]
, we have the following consequence under
CD(K,n)
.

Lemma 2.1

(Liu, Münch and Peyerimhoff)
.

Let
G=(V,w,m)
be a complete graph with non-degenerate vertex measure.
Suppose
G
satisfies
CD(K,n)
. Then for all bounded
f:V\to{\mathbb{R}}
with bounded
\Gamma f
,

\Gamma P_{t}f\leq e^{-2Kt}P_{t}\Gamma f-\frac{1-e^{-2Kt}}{Kn}(\Delta P_{t}f)^{2}.

Here,
P_{t}
denotes the heat semigroup associated to
\Delta
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

We follow the streamlined arguments for
[
5
, Theorem 2.4]
. By Lemma
2.1
, we see that
CD(K,n)
is equivalent to

\Gamma P_{t}f\leq e^{-2Kt}P_{t}\Gamma f-\frac{1-e^{-2Kt}}{Kn}(\Delta P_{t}f)^{2}

for all bounded function
f:V\to\mathbb{R}
. We fix
x_{0},y_{0}\in V
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then by the definition of
\rho
, there is a function
f:V\to\mathbb{R}
s.t.

f(y_{0})-f(x_{0})>\rho(x_{0},y_{0})-\varepsilon
and
\Gamma f\leq 1
. W.l.o.g., we assume that
f
is bounded. When
\operatorname{Deg}_{\max}<\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma P_{t}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{y\in V}{w(x,y)(P_{t}f(y)-P_{t}f(x))^{2}}}{2m(x)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left(\sum_{y\in V}{\frac{w(x,y)(P_{t}f(y)-P_{t}f(x))}{m(x)}}\right)^{2}}{2\frac{\sum_{y\in V}{w(x,y)}}{m(x)}}=\frac{\left(\Delta P_{t}f(x)\right)^{2}}{2\operatorname{Deg}(x)},\quad x\in V.

Combined with
CD(K,n)
condition, we obtain

\left(\frac{1}{2\operatorname{Deg}(x)}+\frac{1-e^{-2Kt}}{Kn}\right)(\Delta P_{t}f(x))^{2}\leq e^{-2Kt}P_{t}(\Gamma f)(x),\quad x\in V.

Taking square root and applying
\Gamma f\leqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}P_{t}f(x)|

\displaystyle=|\Delta P_{t}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{-Kt}}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\operatorname{Deg}(x)}+\frac{1-e^{-2Kt}}{Kn}}}=\sqrt{Kn}\frac{e^{-Kt}}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{Kn}{2\operatorname{Deg}(x)}+1\right)-e^{-2Kt}}},\quad x\in V.

When
T>0
, we have

\displaystyle|P_{T}f(x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}{\left|\partial_{t}P_{t}f(x)\right|dt}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}{\left|\partial_{t}P_{t}f(x)\right|dt}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}{\sqrt{Kn}\frac{e^{-Kt}}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{Kn}{2\operatorname{Deg}(x)}+1\right)-e^{-2Kt}}}dt}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{n}{K}}\arcsin\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{Kn}{2\operatorname{Deg}(x)}+1}},\quad x\in V.

Hence combined with the triangle inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\rho(x_{0},y_{0})-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq|f(x_{0})-f(y_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq\left|P_{T}f(x_{0})-f(x_{0})\right|+\left|P_{T}f(x_{0})-P_{T}f(y_{0})\right|+\left|P_{T}f(y_{0})-f(y_{0})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{K}}\left(\arcsin\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{Kn}{2\operatorname{Deg}\left(x_{0}\right)}+1}}+\arcsin\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{Kn}{2\operatorname{Deg}\left(y_{0}\right)}+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left|P_{T}f(x_{0})-P_{T}f(y_{0})\right|

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{\frac{n}{K}}\left(\arcsin\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{Kn}{2\operatorname{Deg}\left(x_{0}\right)}+1}}+\arcsin\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{Kn}{2\operatorname{Deg}\left(y_{0}\right)}+1}}\right).

Due to
CD(K,n)
which implies

\|\Gamma P_{T}f\|_{\infty}{\rightarrow}0
as
T\to\infty
, and since
G
is connected,

\left|P_{T}f(x_{0})-P_{T}f(y_{0})\right|{\rightarrow}0\text{\,\, as \,\,}T\to\infty.

Taking the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.11
.

By Lemma
1.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle d(x_{0},y_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{Deg}_{\max}}{2}}\rho(x_{0},y_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{n\operatorname{Deg}_{\max}}{2K}}\left(\arcsin\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{Kn}{2\operatorname{Deg}\left(x_{0}\right)}+1}}+\arcsin\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{Kn}{2\operatorname{Deg}\left(y_{0}\right)}+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\mathrm{\operatorname{Deg}_{\max}}n}{K}}\arcsin\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{Kn}{2\operatorname{Deg}_{\max}}+1}}.

Taking the supremum over
x_{0},y_{0}\in V
finishes the proof.
∎

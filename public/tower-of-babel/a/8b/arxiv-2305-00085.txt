Restricted partition functions and the r r -log-concavity of quasi-polynomial-like functions
Krystian Gajdzica

Abstract
. Let 𝒜 = ( a i ) i = 1 ∞ \mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty} be a weakly increasing sequence of positive integers and let k k be a fixed positive integer. For an arbitrary integer n n , the restricted partition p 𝒜 ​ ( n , k ) p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k) enumerates all the partitions of n n whose parts belong to the multiset { a 1 , a 2 , … , a k } \{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\} . In this paper we investigate some generalizations of the log-concavity of p 𝒜 ​ ( n , k ) p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k) . We deal with both some basic extensions like, for instance, the strong log-concavity and a more intriguing challenge that is the r r -log-concavity of both quasi-polynomial-like functions in general, and the restricted partition function in particular. For each of the problems, we present an efficient solution.

1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a fixed positive integer and let
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a non-decreasing sequence of positive integers (in fact, it is enough to assume that
\mathcal{A}
is a
k
-tuple).A restricted partition
\lambda
of a non-negative integer
n
is a sequence of positive integers
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{j}
such that

n=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{j},

and all of these numbers
\lambda_{i}
belong to the multiset
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
. These elements
\lambda_{i}
are further called parts of the partition
\lambda
. Moreover, we also assume that two restricted partitions are considered the same if there is only a difference in the order of their parts. The restricted partition function
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
counts all restricted partitions of
n
. For example, let
\mathcal{A}=(2,3,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}3},5,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}5},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}5},7,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}7}.\ldots)
be a sequence of consecutive prime numbers such that the
i
-th prime number appears in
i
distinct colors. If
k=6
and
n=7
, then we only take into account those restricted partitions of
7
whose parts belong to
\{2,3,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}3},5,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}5},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}5}\}
, that are:
({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}5},2)
,
({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}5},2)
,
(5,2)
,
({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}3},2,2)
, and
(3,2,2)
. Thus,
p_{\mathcal{A}}(7,6)=5
. We may extend the definition of
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
to all integers simply by setting
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)=0
if
n
is negative. It is also worth noting that
p_{\mathcal{A}}(0,k)=1
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, because of the empty sequence
\lambda=()
. Moreover, if we consider the sequence of consecutive positive integers
\mathcal{A}_{1}=(1,2,3,4,\ldots)
and allow ‘
k=\infty
’, then we obtain the well-known partition function
p(n)=p_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n,\infty)
. In
1748
Euler discovered the generating function for
p(n)
, that is

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)x^{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-x^{i}}.

For the function
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
, the corresponding generating function takes the form

(1.1)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)x^{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{1-x^{a_{i}}}.

There is an abundance of literature devoted to the restricted partition function. For arithmetic properties of
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
, we refer the reader to
[
15
,
31
,
38
]
. On the other hand, for some information about the asymptotic behavior we encourage to see
[
1
,
8
,
12
]
.

The main goal of this manuscript is to generalize some results recently obtained by the author in
[
16
]
. There is an efficient criterion for the log-concavity of the restricted partition function. Let us recall that a sequence
(w_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
of real numbers is log-concave, if the inequality

\displaystyle w_{n}^{2}>w_{n+1}w_{n-1}

holds for all
n\geqslant 1
. The main result of the aforementioned paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a weakly increasing sequence of positive integers, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 4}
be fixed. Suppose further that
\gcd A=1
for all
(k-2)
-multisubsets
A
of
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
. If we have that
k>4
, then both

(1.2)

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(n,k)>\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+1,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-1,k)

and

(1.3)

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(n,k)>p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+1,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-1,k)

hold for all
n\geqslant\frac{2}{k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+iDk)
, where
D=\text{lcm}{(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})}
. For
k=4
, (
1.3
) remains valid for all such
n
while (
1.2
) is true for each
n\geqslant\frac{3}{k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+iDk)
. Additionally, for the constant sequence
\mathcal{A}=(1,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}1},\ldots)
, we have that (
1.3
) is satisfied for all positive integers
n
and
k\geqslant 2
; and (
1.2
) is true for any integers
k\geqslant 3
and
n\geqslant\frac{k}{k-2}
.

The above criterion is optimal in a sense that it can not be extended for any other sequence
\mathcal{A}
and parameter
k
. That is a consequence of
[
16
, Corollary 5.8]
:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an arbitrary non-decreasing sequence of positive integers. The sequence
\left(p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is eventually log-concave if and only if we have
k\geqslant 2
and
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{k}=1
or
k\geqslant 4
and
\gcd A=1
for all
(k-2)
-multisubsets
A
of
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
.

Now, we investigate some generalizations of Theorem
1.1
. Similar to DeSalvo and Pak
[
11
]
(in the case of

), we discuss the so-called

of

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(n,k)>p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+m,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-m,k),

for all fixed positive integers

. The authors prove the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

For all
n>m>1
, we have

\displaystyle p^{2}(n)>p(n+m)p(n-m).

It is worth noting that the above statement was firstly conjectured by Chen
[
6
]
as well as the next one.

Conjecture 1.4
.

For all positive integers
a
and

such that

, we have

\displaystyle p^{2}(an)>p(an+bn)p(an-bn).

Therefore, we also examine under what conditions on a sequence
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
k>0
, the inequality

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(an,k)

\displaystyle>p_{\mathcal{A}}(an+bn,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(an-bn,k)

is valid for all sufficiently large values of
n
, where
a
and
b
are fixed positive integers such that
a>b
.

Furthermore, DeSalvo and Pak
[
11
]
resolve Sun’s conjecture
[

]
, namely, they prove that the sequence

is log-concave for

On the other hand, we deal with the log-concavity of

, where
\alpha
is an arbitrary positive real number:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)}{n^{\alpha}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle>\left(\frac{p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+1,k)}{(n+1)^{\alpha}}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-1,k)}{(n-1)^{\alpha}}\right).

It should be pointed out that research related to log-behavior is not only art for art’s sake, but it plays a crucial role in many subjects, and there is a wealth of literature devoted to its applications, see

. For example, log-concave sequences appear in mathematical biology, where they enumerate secondary structures of some biopolymers

.
Also, the log-concavity plays an important role in signal processing in the so-called white noise theory, see

.

We also discuss a bit more involved challenge related to the restricted partition function — that is the
r
-log-concavity of
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
. A sequence
\omega=(w_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
is said to be (asymptotically)
r
-log-concave, if there is a positive integer
N
such that

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\omega\right)_{i},\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{2}\omega\right)_{i},\ldots,\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{r}\omega\right)_{i}

are positive for each
i\geqslant N
, where

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\omega=\left(w_{i+1}^{2}-w_{i}w_{i+2}\right)_{i=0}^{\infty}\text{ and }\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{k}\omega=\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{k-1}\omega\right)

for
k\in\{2,3,\ldots,r\}
.
It is clear that
1
-log-concavity of
(w_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
corresponds to
w_{n+1}^{2}-w_{n}w_{n+2}>0
; the
2
-log-concavity additionally requires that the inequality

w_{n+2}^{4}-2w_{n+1}w_{n+2}^{2}w_{n+3}+w_{n+1}^{2}w_{n+2}w_{n+4}+w_{n}w_{n+2}w_{n+3}^{2}-w_{n}w_{n+2}^{2}w_{n+4}>0

is satisfied for all
n\geqslant N
. Clearly, as
r
grows, the required conditions become more and more intricate.

Despite the fact that the
r
-log-concavity problem for a given sequence isa highly non-trivial task in general, Hou and Zhang
[
21
]
discovered an effective criterion for its solution (see, Theorem
4.3
below). Moreover, these authors used that result and proved that the partition function
p(n)
is (asymptotically)

-log-concave for each
r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, for more details see
[
22
]
. Afterwards, Mukherjee, Zhang and Zhong
[

]
applied the aforementioned criterion and showed that the overpartition function is (asymptotically)
r
-log-concave for any
r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Recall that an overpartition
[
9
]
of an integer
n
is a partition of
n
where the first occurrence of every distinct part may be overlined. The overpartition function
\overline{p}(n)
denotes the number of all overpartitions of
n
. There is a rich and vast literature dealing with the analogous problems for other sequences, we encourage the reader to see, for instance,
[

,

We present a similar result to those mentioned above in the case of the restricted partition function
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
. In fact, we show a bit more general criterion for
r
-log-concavity of quasi-polynomial-like functions. By a quasi-polynomial-like function we mean any expression of the form

\displaystyle f(n)=a_{l}(n)n^{l}+a_{l-1}(n)n^{l-1}+\cdots+a_{l-s}(n)n^{l-s}+o\left(n^{l-s}\right),

where
l
and
s
are fixed positive integers such that
l\geqslant s
,
a_{l-s}(n),\ldots,a_{l}(n)
are real coefficients depending on the residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0muM)
for some
M\geqslant 2
, and
o
denotes the standard little-
o
notation. The main result of this paper presents, in some sense, optimal requirements on the function
f(n)
to prove that it is
r
-log-concave for any
r\geqslant 1
.

This manuscript is organized as follows. Section 2 contains necessary notations, properties and tools that are used throughout the article. Section 3 is devoted to some basic generalizations of the log-concavity criterion for the restricted partition function. We investigate the analogues of both Theorem
1.3
and Conjecture
1.4

r
-log-concavity of both quasi-polynomial-like functions and the restricted partition function
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
. To deal with these problems, we use methods from Hou and Zhang’s paper
[
21
]
. Actually, we also present an alternative more direct approach which is effective in the case of quasi-polynomial-like functions.

2.
Preliminaries

At the beginning, let us fix some notations. The set of non-negative integers is denoted by
\mathbb{N}
, while
\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}
correspond to the sets of positive integers and positive real numbers, respectively. Additionally, we put
\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant k}=\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0,1,\ldots,k-1\}
.

Henceforth,
\mathcal{A}=(a_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
denotes a weakly increasing sequence of positive integers. Let
k
and
n
be fixed positive integers. The restricted partition function
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
enumerates all partitions of
n
with parts in the multiset
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
. We additionally set
p_{\mathcal{A}}(0,k)=1
and
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)=0
if
n<0
. From the equality (
1.1
), one can easily derive a recurrence relation for the restricted partition function of the form

p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)=p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-a_{k},k)+p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k-1),

where
k\geqslant 2
. For
k=1
, we have

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,1)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }a_{1}\nmid n,\\
1,&\text{if }a_{1}\mid n.\end{cases}

It turns out that restricted partition function
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
behaves ‘almost like’ a polynomial of degree
k-1
. More precisely, it is a
quasi
-polynomial of the form

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)=c_{k-1}(r)n^{k-1}+c_{k-2}(r)n^{k-2}+\cdots+c_{0}(r),

where every
c_{j}(r)
depends on the residue class
r
of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu\text{lcm}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}))
for
0\leqslant j\leqslant k-1
(for additional information about quasi-polynomials, we refer the reader to Stanley’s book
[
35
, Section 4.4]
). This result goes back to Bell
[
3
]
, who showed it via partial fraction decomposition of the related rational generating function. However, there are more concrete estimates for the restricted partition function. For instance, Almkvist
[
1
]
obtained a very elegant result related to the asymptotic behavior of
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
. Let us define symmetric polynomials
\sigma_{i}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})
by the power series expansion

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{x_{i}t/2}{\sinh(x_{i}t/2)}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sigma_{m}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})t^{m}.

Then Almkvist’s characterization of
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
is the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 2}
be fixed. For a given integer
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
, if
\gcd A=1
for every
j
-element multisubset (
j
-multisubset)
A
of
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
and
\sigma=a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}
, then

p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)=\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}}\sum_{i=0}^{k-j}\sigma_{i}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})\frac{(n+\sigma/2)^{k-1-i}}{(k-1-i)!}+O(n^{j-2})

as
n\to\infty
.

One can verify that
\sigma_{0}=1
, and
\sigma_{i}=0
if
i
is odd. Moreover, if we put
s_{i}=a_{1}^{i}+a_{2}^{i}+\cdots+a_{k}^{i}
, then

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}=-\frac{s_{2}}{24}\text{,}\hskip 5.69046pt\sigma_{4}=\frac{5s_{2}^{2}+2s_{4}}{5760}\text{,}\hskip 5.69046pt\sigma_{6}=-\frac{35s_{2}^{3}+42s_{2}s_{4}+16s_{6}}{2903040}.

Further, it turns out that converse implication in Theorem
2.1
is also true, because of the following.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
be fixed. If

p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)=c_{k-1}n^{k-1}+c_{k-2}n^{k-2}+\cdots+c_{j-1}n^{j-1}+c_{j-2}(n)n^{j-2}+\cdots+c_{0}(n),

where
c_{k-1},\ldots,c_{j-1}
are independent of a residue class
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu\text{lcm}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}))
, then
\gcd A=1
for all
j
-multisubsets
A
of
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
.

For more details about the aforementioned result, we refer the reader to
[
16
]
. It is also worth noting that we have an explicit formula for
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
— that is a result due to Cimpoeaş and Nicolae
[
8
]
with a small modification. Before we present their theorem, let us recall that the unsigned Stirling number of the first kind
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}
is the number of permutations of
n
elements with exactly
m
cycles. It might be defined equivalently as the coefficient of the rising factorial (Pochhammer function), namely

\displaystyle x^{\overline{n}}:=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}x^{i}.

We encourage the reader to see
[
17
, Chapter 6]
for more information about Stirling numbers.

Theorem 2.3
.

For any
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
k\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant j_{1}\leqslant\frac{D}{a_{1}}-1\\
\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}\hbox{.}\kern-0.79997pt}\\
0\leqslant j_{k}\leqslant\frac{D}{a_{k}}-1\\
S\equiv n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0muD)\end{subarray}}\sum_{i=m}^{k-1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k}{i+1}(-1)^{i-m}\binom{i}{m}D^{-i}S^{i-m}n^{m},

where
D=\text{lcm}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
and
S=a_{1}j_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}j_{k}
.

Remark 2.4
.

One can notice that there is a difference between the statement in Theorem
2.3
and the original formula from Cimpoeaş and Nicolae’s paper
[
8
, Theorem 2.8]
which states that

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant j_{1}\leqslant\frac{D}{a_{1}}-1\\
\vbox{\hbox{.}\hbox{.}\hbox{.}\kern-0.79997pt}\\
0\leqslant j_{k}\leqslant\frac{D}{a_{k}}-1\\
S\equiv n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0muD)\end{subarray}}\sum_{i=m}^{k-1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k}{i}(-1)^{i-m}\binom{i}{m}D^{-i}S^{i-m}n^{m}

with the same notation as before. At first, there is a typographical error over the first sum, namely, there has to be
k-1
instead of
n-1
. Furthermore, we need to replace
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k}{i}
by
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k}{i+1}
— that is a consequence of the equality

\displaystyle\binom{n+k-1}{k-1}=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k}{i+1}n^{i},

which is accidentally misspelled at the beginning of Section 2 in
[
8
]
.

For the sake of clarity, it should be also pointed out that in Theorem
2.3
, we may have that
S=0
. If this is the case, we assume that
0^{0}=1
.

From the above-mentioned properties, the author
[
16
]
deduced the following lower and upper bounds for the restricted partition function.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 2}
be fixed. For a given integer
j\in\{2,3,\ldots,k\}
, if
\gcd A=1
for all
j
-multisubsets
A
of
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
, then

c_{k-1}n^{k-1}+\cdots+c_{j-1}n^{j-1}-Fn^{j-2}<p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)<c_{k-1}n^{k-1}+\cdots+c_{j-1}n^{j-1}+Fn^{j-2}

holds for every
n>0
, where all the coefficients
c_{i}
are uniquely determined by Theorem
2.1
, and
F=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+iDk)}{k!\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}}
with
D=\text{lcm}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
.

It is worth mentioning that the constant
F
appearing in Proposition
2.5
is not optimal, nevertheless we will soon observe the usefulness of the result.

3.
Some basic extensions of the log-concavity criterion for
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)

Our first goal is to resolve a few of the problems presented in Introduction, that are

(3.1)

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(n,k)

\displaystyle>p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+m,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-m,k),

(3.2)

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(an,k)

\displaystyle>p_{\mathcal{A}}(an+bn,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(an-bn,k),

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)}{n^{\alpha}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle>\left(\frac{p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+1,k)}{(n+1)^{\alpha}}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-1,k)}{(n-1)^{\alpha}}\right),

where
a,b,m
are fixed positive integers such that
a>b
while
\alpha
is an arbitrary positive real number. Essentially, all of them are very similar to Theorem
1.1
in the proof. Let us present a heuristic reasoning for the inequality (
3.1
). The idea is quite easy, we apply Proposition
2.5
in order to bound both
p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(n,k)
from below and
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+m,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-m,k)
from above. Subsequently, we just compare these bounds and examine when the relevant inequality between them holds.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 2}
be fixed. Suppose farther that
k\geqslant 4
and
\gcd A=1
for every
(k-2)
-multisubset
A\subset\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
. If
2\hat{d}\geqslant 3m^{3}
, then the inequality

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(n,k)>p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+m,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-m,k)

holds for all
n\geqslant\hat{d}
, where
\hat{d}=k^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+iDk)
with
D=\text{lcm}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
; otherwise it is valid for any
n\geqslant 3m
. Additionally, for the constant sequence
\mathcal{A}=(1,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}1},\ldots)
, the above inequality is satisfied for all positive integers
n
and
k\geqslant 2
.

Proof.

First, let
\mathcal{A}=(1,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}1},\ldots)
be a constant sequence. Observe that Theorem
2.3
maintains that

(3.4)

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)=\binom{n+k-1}{k-1}.

Thus, the second part of the statement can be directly verified. For the first one, let
k
,
m
and
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
satisfy the assumptions in the theorem. It follows from Proposition
2.5
that the inequalities

\displaystyle an^{k-1}+bn^{k-2}+cn^{k-3}-dn^{k-4}<p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)<an^{k-1}+bn^{k-2}+cn^{k-3}+dn^{k-4}

hold for all
n
, where the coefficients
a,b,c
and
d
are given by

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1)!\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}},

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma}{2(k-2)!\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}},

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\frac{3\sigma^{2}-s_{2}}{24(k-3)!\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}},

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+iDk)}{k!\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}}

with
\sigma=a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}
,
s_{2}=a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}+\cdots+a_{k}^{2}
and
D=\text{lcm}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
. Now, it is enough to check when the following inequality

\displaystyle\left[an^{k-1}+bn^{k-2}+cn^{k-3}-dn^{k-4}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\left[a(n+m)^{k-1}+b(n+m)^{k-2}+c(n+m)^{k-3}+d(n+m)^{k-4}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left[a(n-m)^{k-1}+b(n-m)^{k-2}+c(n-m)^{k-3}+d(n-m)^{k-4}\right]

is true. The above might be simplified to

\displaystyle 3m^{2}n^{4}+4(\hat{b}m^{2}-\hat{d})n^{3}

\displaystyle+(2\hat{b}^{2}m^{2}-3m^{4}-4\hat{b}\hat{d})n^{2}+2(\hat{b}\hat{c}m^{2}-\hat{b}m^{4}-3\hat{d}m^{2}-2\hat{c}\hat{d})n

\displaystyle+\hat{c}^{2}m^{2}-2\hat{b}\hat{d}m^{2}-\hat{b}^{2}m^{4}+2\hat{c}m^{4}+m^{6}>0,

where
\hat{b}=b/a
,
\hat{c}=c/a
and
\hat{d}=d/a
. Next, let
f(n)
denote the polynomial on the left hand side. Both real roots of the second derivative of
f(n)
are given by

\displaystyle n_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\hat{d}-2\hat{b}m^{2}-\sqrt{2}\sqrt{2\hat{d}^{2}+3m^{6}}}{6m^{2}},

\displaystyle n_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\hat{d}-2\hat{b}m^{2}+\sqrt{2}\sqrt{2\hat{d}^{2}+3m^{6}}}{6m^{2}}.

Now, it is convenient to investigate two separately cases. Either we have that
2\hat{d}^{2}\geqslant 3m^{6}
or
2\hat{d}^{2}<3m^{6}
.

Case 1
:

If the inequality
2\hat{d}^{2}\geqslant 3m^{6}
holds, then
n_{2}\leqslant\hat{d}
. But, this implies that
f^{\prime}(n)
is increasing for all
n\geqslant\hat{d}
. One can also easy verify that
f^{\prime}(\hat{d})>0
and
f(\hat{d})>0
, so we get that both
f^{\prime}(n)
and
f(n)
are positive for every
n\geqslant\hat{d}
.

Case 2
:

Since we assume that
2\hat{d}^{2}<3m^{6}
, it is straightforward to see that
n_{2}\leqslant m
. Thus
f^{\prime}(n)
grows for
n\geqslant m
. Furthermore, one may check that
f^{\prime}(3m)>0
as well as
f(3m)>0
. Hence,
f(n)
is positive for all
n\geqslant 3m
, which finishes the proof.

∎

Let us note that the case of
m=1
is omitted in the above, because it is contained in Theorem
1.1
. It is also worth pointing out that the demands for parameter
n
in Theorem
3.1
are not optimal. Nevertheless, the result may be applied for a wide class of integer sequences. Furthermore, by virtue of Corollary
1.2
and Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the following characterization.

Corollary 3.2
.

The sequence
\left(p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is eventually strong log-concave (the inequality (
3.1
) is satisfied for any positive integer
m
and sufficiently large values of
n
) if and only if we have
k\geqslant 2
and
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{k}=1
or
k\geqslant 4
and
\gcd A=1
for all
(k-2)
-multisubsets
A
of
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
.

Proof.

It follows directly from Corollary
1.2
and Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Now, it is time to deal with the next task of this section. Surprisingly, the following is true.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
u,v\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
be fixed such that
u>v
. If
k\geqslant 2
and
\gcd(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})=1
, then the inequality

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(un,k)>p_{\mathcal{A}}(un+vn,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(un-vn,k)

is true for every
n>\frac{4u}{kv^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+iDk)
with
D=\text{lcm}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
whenever
un,un+vn
and
un-vn
are integers.

Since the proof of Corollary
3.3
is very easy and analogous to that one of Theorem
3.1
, we leave it as an exercise for the reader. It is worth pointing out that even though the inequalities (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) look very similar, the criteria for their solutions are significantly different. In addition, even if we assume that
\gcd(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})>1
, then (
3.2
) might be still valid for all sufficiently large values of
n
, as it is shown in the following instance.

Example 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(2,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}2},\ldots)
be a constant sequence and let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
be fixed. Suppose further that
u=4
and
v=2
. Thus we deal with the inequality of the form

(3.5)

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(4n,k)>p_{\mathcal{A}}(6n,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(2n,k).

Now, observe that the equality
p_{\mathcal{A}}(2n,k)=p_{\mathcal{B}}(n,k)
holds for every integer
n
, where
\mathcal{B}=(1,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}1},\ldots)
. Therefore, as a consequence of the equality (
3.4
), we may reduce the problem to

\displaystyle\binom{2n+k-1}{k-1}^{2}>\binom{3n+k-1}{k-1}\binom{n+k-1}{k-1}.

Finally, it might be easily checked that the leading coefficient on the left hand side is
4^{k-1}/\left((k-1)!\right)^{2}
, while on the right hand side it is
3^{k-1}/\left((k-1)!\right)^{2}
, and both of them stand next to
n^{2k-2}
. Hence, for each
k>1
, we get that the inequality (
3.5
) is satisfied for all but finitely many positive integers
n
.

The above example shows that the inequality (
3.2
) is not a really interesting problem for the restricted partition function, despite the fact that its analogue for
p(n)
is (see,
[
6
,
11
]
).

In the last part of this section, we present a complete solution of the inequality (
3.3
). It turns out to be a more fascinating challenge than the previous ones, as the following result suggests.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
be fixed. The sequence
\left(\frac{p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)}{n^{\alpha}}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is eventually log-concave if and only if we have
k>\alpha+1
and either
2\leqslant k\leqslant 3
and
a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}=1
or
k\geqslant 4
and
\gcd A=1
for all
(k-2)
-multisubsets
A
of
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
\alpha
be as in the statement. At the beginning, we prove the implication to the left hand side. We just need to solve the inequality

\displaystyle(n^{2}-1)^{\alpha}p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(n,k)-n^{2\alpha}p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+1,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-1,k)>0.

Our first goal is to bound the left hand side from below. In order to do that we apply the generalized binomial theorem which maintains that

\displaystyle(n^{2}-1)^{\alpha}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\binom{\alpha}{j}n^{2(\alpha-j)},

where

\displaystyle\binom{\alpha}{j}=\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-j+1)}{j!}.

Since we must have
n\geqslant 2
, it is clear that the following inequalities

\displaystyle(n^{2}-1)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle>\sum_{j=0}^{\left\lfloor\alpha\right\rfloor+1}(-1)^{j}\binom{\alpha}{j}n^{2(\alpha-j)}-\binom{\alpha}{\left\lfloor\alpha\right\rfloor}n^{2(\alpha-\left\lfloor\alpha\right\rfloor-2)}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}n^{-2j}

\displaystyle>\sum_{j=0}^{\left\lfloor\alpha\right\rfloor+1}(-1)^{j}\binom{\alpha}{j}n^{2(\alpha-j)}-2\binom{\alpha}{\left\lfloor\alpha\right\rfloor}n^{2(\alpha-\left\lfloor\alpha\right\rfloor-2)}=f(n)

are valid. We consider two cases depending on both the value of
k>\alpha+1
and the multiset
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
.

Case 1
:

First, we assume that
k\geqslant 4
and
\gcd A=1
for all
(k-2)
-multisubsets
A
of
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
. It is enough to deal with

\displaystyle f(n)p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(n,k)-n^{2\alpha}p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+1,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-1,k)>0.

From the assumptions on the multiset
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}
and Theorem
2.1
, we get that

\displaystyle f(n)p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(n,k)-n^{2\alpha}p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+1,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-1,k)=(k-1-\alpha)a^{2}n^{2k+2\alpha-4}+\widetilde{f}(n),

where

a=\frac{1}{(k-1)!\prod_{i=1}^{k}{a_{i}}}

and
\widetilde{f}(n)
is a generalized quasi-polynomial of degree
2k+2\alpha-5
, that is an expression of the form

\widetilde{f}(n)=n^{2\left(\alpha-\left\lfloor\alpha\right\rfloor-2\right)}\sum_{j=0}^{2k+2\left\lfloor\alpha\right\rfloor-1}t_{j}(n)n^{j},

where each
t_{m}(n)
is some real number depending on the residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu\text{lcm}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}))
. Since
k>\alpha+1
, the leading coefficient
(k-1-\alpha)a^{2}
is positive. Therefore, we obtain that the sequence
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)/n^{\alpha}
is log-concave for all but finitely many positive integers
n
.

Case 2
:

Now, let us assume that
2\leqslant k\leqslant 3
and
a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}=1
. These two alternatives might be investigated separately by repeating the reasoning from Case
1
and employing the formula (
3.4
). We encourage the reader to check all the details on their own.

To prove the implication to the right hand side let us fix
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. It is clear that the sequence
\left(p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,1)/n^{\alpha}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
can not be log-concave. Therefore, let
k\geqslant 2
and suppose, for contradiction, that the assumptions on the numbers
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}
and
k
do not hold. Before we go into the main part of the proof, let us observe that we may also determine an upper bound for
(n^{2}-1)^{\alpha}
. Analogously to the computations from the beginning, one can easily get that

\displaystyle(n^{2}-1)^{\alpha}<\sum_{j=0}^{\left\lfloor\alpha\right\rfloor+1}(-1)^{j}\binom{\alpha}{j}n^{2(\alpha-j)}+2\binom{\alpha}{\left\lfloor\alpha\right\rfloor}n^{2(\alpha-\left\lfloor\alpha\right\rfloor-2)}=g(n).

Once again we have a few possibilities to examine. In order to make the text more transparent, we label each of the cases by its general assumptions.

Case 1
(
4\leqslant k<\alpha+1
):

At first, we also assume that
\gcd A=1
for all
(k-2)
-multisubsets
A
of
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
. In order to show that
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)/n^{\alpha}
can not be log-concave, it suffices to note that the leading coefficient of

\displaystyle g(n)p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(n,k)-n^{2\alpha}p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+1,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-1,k)=(k-1-\alpha)a^{2}n^{2k+2\alpha-4}+\widetilde{g}(n),

where
a
is as before and
\widetilde{g}(n)
is some generalized quasi-polynomial of degree
2k-2\alpha-5
, is negative.

On the other hand, if the assumptions on the multiset
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
do not hold, then Proposition
2.2
asserts that

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)=c_{k-1}(n)n^{k-1}+c_{k-2}(n)n^{k-2}+c_{k-3}(n)n^{k-3}+\cdots+c_{0}(n),

where at least one of
c_{k-1}(n)
,
c_{k-2}(n)
, or
c_{k-3}(n)
depends on a residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu\text{lcm}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}))
. Let
t\in\{k-3,k-2,k-1\}
be the largest index with this property. Now, it is enough to take any
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu\text{lcm}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}))
such that
c_{t}(n)
is the smallest, and at least one of
c_{t}(n+1)
or
c_{t}(n-1)
is strictly larger than
c_{t}(n)
. If we do so, then the leading coefficient of the generalized quasi-polynomial

\displaystyle g(n)p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(n,k)-n^{2\alpha}p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+1,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-1,k)

is negative, and, in particular, the sequence
\left(p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)/n^{\alpha}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
can not be log-concave for infinitely many values of
n
.

Case 2
(
4\leqslant k=\alpha+1
):

In contrast to Case
1
, if we assume that
\gcd A=1
for all
(k-2)
-multisubsets
A
of
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
, then we obtain that

\displaystyle g(n)p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(n,k)-n^{2\alpha}p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+1,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n-1,k)=\widetilde{g}(n),

where
\widetilde{g}(n)
is as before. Hence, we need to say something about the leading coefficient of
\widetilde{g}(n)
. There are two possibilities: either the coefficient
c_{k-4}(n)
of

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)=c_{k-1}(n)n^{k-1}+c_{k-2}(n)n^{k-2}+c_{k-3}(n)n^{k-3}+\cdots+c_{0}(n)

is independent of the residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu\text{lcm}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}))
or not. If
c_{k-4}(n)
does not depend on the residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu\text{lcm}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}))
, then Proposition
2.2
points out that
\gcd A=1
for any
(k-3)
-multisubset
A
of
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
, and we get that

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}(n)=-2abn^{2k+2\alpha-5}+O(n^{2k+2\alpha-6}),

where
a
is as before and

b=\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}}{2(k-2)!\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}}.

Thus, the leading coefficient of
\widetilde{g}(n)
is negative, as required. Let us note here that the case of
k=4
is also included above. In that situation we must have
a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}=a_{4}=1
(
\gcd(m)=m
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
), and
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
is given by (
3.4
).

On the other hand, if at least one of the coefficients
c_{i}(n)
for
i\in\{k-4,k-3,k-2,k-1\}
depends on the residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu\text{lcm}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}))
, then we present analogous reasoning to that one from the second part of Case
1
, and obtain that the sequence
\left(p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)/n^{\alpha}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
can not be log-concave for infinitely many values of
n
.

Case 3
(
2\leqslant k\leqslant 3\text{ }\&\text{ }k\leqslant\alpha+1
):

Applying preceding methods, it is not difficult to deduce the required result. We leave this case as an exercise for the reader.

∎

4.
The
r
-log-concavity of quasi-polynomials like functions

In this section, we explore a bit more complex problem — the so-called
r
-log-concavity of
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
. Let us recall that a sequence of real numbers
\omega=\left(w_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
is said to be (asymptotically)
r
-log-concave for
r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, if for some
N
all terms of the sequences

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\omega\right)_{i},\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{2}\omega\right)_{i},\ldots,\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{r}\omega\right)_{i}

are positive for every
i\geqslant N
, where

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\omega=\left(w_{i+1}^{2}-w_{i}w_{i+2}\right)_{i=0}^{\infty}\text{ and }\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{k}\omega=\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{k-1}\omega\right)

for
k\in\{2,3,\ldots,r\}
.
As it was mentioned in Introduction, the
1
-log-concavity of
(w_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
corresponds to the inequality
w_{n+1}^{2}-w_{n}w_{n+2}>0
; the
2
-log-concavity in addition demands that the following inequality

w_{n+2}^{4}-2w_{n+1}w_{n+2}^{2}w_{n+3}+w_{n+1}^{2}w_{n+2}w_{n+4}+w_{n}w_{n+2}w_{n+3}^{2}-w_{n}w_{n+2}^{2}w_{n+4}>0

is satisfied for all
n\geqslant N
. We resign from demonstrating other inequalities for
r\geqslant 3
.

The question arises whether we can successfully apply the methods from Section 2 to find out a convenient criterion for the
r
-log-concavity of the restricted partition function
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
. In fact, it is not the best idea to bound the left hand side of each of the inequalities from above and below. It is sill possible to do so for the
2
-log-concavity problem, but what about the
100
-log-concavity of
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
? Nota bene, if we take advantage of the aforementioned approach to resolve the
2
-log-concave case, then we immediately get the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
k\geqslant 7
be fixed. If
\gcd A=1
for every
(k-6)
-multisubset
A\subset\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
, then
\left(p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
is asymptotically
2
-log-concave.

Proof.

The assumptions from the statement together with Proposition
2.5
assert that
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
might be bounded from above and below by

\displaystyle f(n)<p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)<g(n),

where
f(n)=c_{k-1}n^{k-1}+\cdots+c_{k-7}n^{k-7}-Fn^{k-8}
and
g(n)=c_{k-1}n^{k-1}+\cdots+c_{k-7}n^{k-7}+Fn^{k-8}
, where all the coefficients
c_{i}
are uniquely determined by Theorem
2.1
, while

F=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+iDk)}{k!\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}}

with
D=\text{lcm}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
. Next, it is enough to examine the inequality of the form

\displaystyle f^{4}(n+2)

\displaystyle-2g(n+1)g^{2}(n+2)g(n+3)+f^{2}(n+1)f(n+2)f(n+4)

\displaystyle+f(n)f(n+2)f^{2}(n+3)-g(n)g^{2}(n+2)g(n+4)>0.

After some tiresome calculations one can determine that the leading coefficient of the left hand side is equal to
588c_{k-1}
, where

c_{k-1}=\frac{1}{(k-1)!\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}}.

Hence, the sequence
\left(p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
must be
2
-log-concave for all but finitely many values of
n
, as required.
∎

Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
4.1
can mislead us to believe that in order to receive the asymptotic
r
-log-concavity of
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
(for some
r
), we need to require that
\gcd A=1
for every
(k-(r+1)!)
-multisubset
A\subset\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
. But, it is not true as we see in the following.

. On the other hand, Figure 11 as well as Figure 12 suggest that the sequence
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{A}_{1},k}^{2}(n)

Indeed, there is a bit more efficient criterion for the
r
-log concavity than Proposition
4.1
. In order to find it, we apply a small modification of a very useful theorem obtained by Hou and Zhang
[
21
]
. To state their result, we need a little preparation.

Let
\omega=\left(w_{i}\right)_{i=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of positive real numbers. We set

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}\omega\right)_{i}=\frac{w_{i+1}}{w_{i}}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pt\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}\omega\right)_{i}=\frac{\left(\mathcal{R}\omega\right)_{i+1}}{\left(\mathcal{R}\omega\right)_{i}}=\frac{w_{i}w_{i+2}}{w_{i+1}^{2}}.

In particular, observe that the
1
-log-concavity of
\left(w_{i}\right)_{i=0}^{\infty}
is equivalent to the statement
\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}w\right)_{i}<1
. Actually, it is also true for such a sequence of real numbers
\left(w_{i}\right)_{i=0}^{\infty}
that both
\left(w_{i}w_{i+2}\right)_{i=0}^{\infty}
and
\left(w_{i+1}^{2}\right)_{i=0}^{\infty}
are positive.

Next, we assume that
\left(x_{i}\right)_{i=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of real numbers. Further, suppose that there are numbers
d_{j}
and a
(m+1)
-tuple
\alpha=(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
such that

\alpha_{0}<\alpha_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{m}

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{\alpha_{m}}\left(x_{n}-\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{d_{j}}{n^{\alpha_{j}}}\right)=0.

The value

y_{n,\alpha}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{d_{j}}{n^{\alpha_{j}}}

is called the
Puiseux-type
approximation of
x_{n}
(of degree
\alpha_{m}
) and is denoted by
x_{n}\approx y_{n,\alpha}
. When it is clear from the context, we will write
y_{n}
instead of
y_{n,\alpha}
. It turns out that only the first few summands of
y_{n}
and
\alpha_{m}
are important from our perspective. Therefore, we use the standard little-
o
notation to write

x_{n}=y_{n}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{\alpha_{m}}}\right)=\frac{d_{0}}{n^{\alpha_{0}}}+\frac{d_{1}}{n^{\alpha_{1}}}+\cdots+\frac{d_{m}}{n^{\alpha_{m}}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{\alpha_{m}}}\right).

Now, we are able to recall Hou and Zhang’s criterion
[
21
]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\omega=\left(w_{i}\right)_{i=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of positive real numbers such that
\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}\omega\right)_{n}
has a Puiseux-type approximation
y_{n}
of the form

y_{n}=1+\frac{d_{1}}{n^{\alpha_{1}}}+\cdots+\frac{d_{m}}{n^{\alpha_{m}}},

where
m\geqslant 1
,
0<\alpha_{1}\leqslant\alpha_{m}
. If
d_{1}<0
and
\alpha_{1}<2
, then
\left(w_{i}\right)_{i=0}^{\infty}
is asymptotically
\left\lfloor\alpha_{m}/\alpha_{1}\right\rfloor
-log-concave.

For the sake of clarity, let us further write
p_{\mathcal{A},k}(n)=p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
whenever we apply an operator (
\mathcal{R}
or
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}
) to
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
in order to emphasize that we consider it asa sequence in
n
. Unfortunately, we can not explicitly employ Theorem
4.3
to
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
, because for the restricted partition function we usually have that
\alpha_{1}=2
. More precisely, if
k\geqslant 4
and
\gcd A=1
for all
(k-2)
-multisubsets
A
of
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
, then Theorem
2.1
implies that

p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)=an^{k-1}+bn^{k-2}+cn^{k-3}+c_{k-4}(n)n^{k-4}+\cdots+c_{0}(n),

where
c_{0}(n),\ldots,c_{k-4}(n)
depend on the residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu\text{lcm}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}))
and

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1)!\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}},

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma}{2(k-2)!\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}},

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\frac{3\sigma^{2}-s_{2}}{24(k-3)!\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}}

with
\sigma=a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}
and
s_{2}=a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}+\cdots+a_{k}^{2}
. Now, it is straightforward to compute that

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+2,k)=\beta_{2k-2}n^{2k-2}+\beta_{2k-3}n^{2k-3}+\beta_{2k-4}n^{2k-4}+\beta(n)

and

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(n+1,k)=\gamma_{2k-2}n^{2k-2}+\gamma_{2k-3}n^{2k-3}+\gamma_{2k-4}n^{2k-4}+\gamma(n),

where

\displaystyle\beta_{2k-2}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{2k-2}=a^{2},

\displaystyle\beta_{2k-3}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{2k-3}=2a(a(k-1)+b),

\displaystyle\beta_{2k-4}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{2k-4}-a^{2}(k-1)=2a^{2}(k-1)(k-2)+2ab(2k-3)+2ac+b^{2},

and
\beta(n)
, together with
\gamma(n)
, is some quasi-polynomial of degree
2k-5
. Hence, the Puiseux-type approximation of

\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}p_{\mathcal{A},k}\right)(n)
of degree
2
might be calculated as follows:

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}p_{\mathcal{A},k}\right)(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{\mathcal{A}}(n+2,k)p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)}{p_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}(n+1,k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta_{2k-2}n^{2k-2}+\beta_{2k-3}n^{2k-3}+\beta_{2k-4}n^{2k-4}+\beta(n)}{\gamma_{2k-2}n^{2k-2}+\gamma_{2k-3}n^{2k-3}+\gamma_{2k-4}n^{2k-4}+\gamma(n)}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{-a^{2}(k-1)n^{2k-4}+\beta(n)-\gamma(n)}{\gamma_{2k-2}n^{2k-2}+\gamma_{2k-3}n^{2k-3}+\gamma_{2k-4}n^{2k-4}+\gamma(n)}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{1-k}{n^{2}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right),

where the last equality is a consequence of the following equality

\displaystyle\frac{-a^{2}(k-1)n^{2k-4}+\beta(n)-\gamma(n)}{\gamma_{2k-2}n^{2k-2}+\gamma_{2k-3}n^{2k-3}+\gamma_{2k-4}n^{2k-4}+\gamma(n)}-\frac{1-k}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}(\beta(n)-\gamma(n))+(k-1)(\gamma_{2k-3}n^{2k-3}+\gamma_{2k-4}n^{2k-4}+\gamma(n))}{n^{2}(\gamma_{2k-2}n^{2k-2}+\gamma_{2k-3}n^{2k-3}+\gamma_{2k-4}n^{2k-4}+\gamma(n))}.

It should be pointed out that without any additional assumptions on the multiset
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
, we can not simply present a bit more precise Puiseux-type approximation of
\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}p_{\mathcal{A},k}\right)(n)
— mainly because if we try to do so, then we obtain that the nominator over
n^{3}
depends on the residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu\text{lcm}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}))
.

Let us now slightly modify Theorem
4.3
to get a relevant criterion in the case of the restricted partition function
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
. To achieve this goal we use two auxiliary lemmas. The first of them is due to Hou and Zhang
[
21
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\omega=\left(w_{i}\right)_{i=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of real numbers. We have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{2}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\omega=\left(t_{n+1}^{2}\frac{(t_{n}-1)(t_{n+2}-1)}{(t_{n+1}-1)^{2}}\right)_{n=0}^{\infty},

where
\left(t_{i}\right)_{i=0}^{\infty}=\mathcal{R}^{2}\omega
.

On the other hand, the second one gives us information about the Puiseux-type approximation of those functions which grow as fast as polynomials.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 2}
and
a_{l},a_{l-1},\ldots,a_{l-s}\in\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary. Suppose further that there is a function
f(n)=a_{l}n^{l}+a_{l-1}n^{l-1}+\cdots+a_{l-s}n^{l-s}+o\left(n^{l-s}\right)
. Moreover, let us put
f(n)f(n+2)-f^{2}(n+1)=d_{2l-2,1}n^{2l-2}+\cdots+d_{2l-s,1}n^{2l-s}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)
(in fact,
d_{2l,1}=d_{2l-1,1}=0
) and
f^{2}(n+1)=b_{2l}n^{2l}+\cdots+b_{2l-s}n^{2l-s}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)
. Then the equality

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}f\right)(n)=1+\frac{d_{2}}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle+\cdots+\frac{d_{j}}{n^{j}}+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{s-j}d_{2l-j-i,j}n^{2l-j-i}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)}{b_{2l}n^{2l}+\cdots+b_{2l-s}n^{2l-s}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)}

holds for every
2\leqslant j\leqslant s
with
d_{i}=d_{2l-i,i-1}/b_{2l}
, where

\displaystyle d_{u,v}=d_{u,v-1}-d_{2l-v,v-1}b_{u+v}/b_{2l}

for
2\leqslant v\leqslant s-1
and
2l-s\leqslant u\leqslant 2l-v-1
.
In particular, the Puiseux-type approximation
y_{n}
of
f(n)f(n+2)/f^{2}(n+1)
takes the form

\displaystyle y_{n}=1+\frac{-l}{n^{2}}+\frac{d_{3}}{n^{3}}+\cdots+\frac{d_{s}}{n^{s}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{s}}\right).

Proof.

Let
s\geqslant 2
and
f(n)
be fixed. Employing the same method as in the discussion before Lemma
4.4
, we get that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}f\right)(n)=\frac{c_{2l}n^{2l}+c_{2l-1}n^{2l-1}+\cdots+c_{2l-s}n^{2l-s}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)}{b_{2l}n^{2l}+b_{2l-1}n^{2l-1}+\cdots+b_{2l-s}n^{2l-s}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)},

where

\displaystyle c_{2l}=b_{2l}

\displaystyle=a_{l}^{2}

\displaystyle c_{2l-1}=b_{2l-1}

\displaystyle=2a_{l}(la_{l}+a_{l-1})

\displaystyle c_{2l-2}=b_{2l-2}-la_{l}^{2}

\displaystyle=2l(l-1)a_{l}^{2}+2(2l-1)a_{l}a_{l-1}+2a_{l}a_{l-2}+a_{l-1}^{2},

and all the remaining terms
b_{i}
and
c_{j}
can be explicitly determined. Hence, it is clear that

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}f\right)(n)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{d_{2l-2,1}n^{2l-2}+\cdots+d_{2l-s,1}n^{2l-s}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)}{b_{2l}n^{2l}+b_{2l-1}n^{2l-1}+\cdots+b_{2l-2r}n^{2l-2r}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{d_{2}}{n^{2}}+\frac{d_{2l-3,2}n^{2l-3}+\cdots+d_{2l-s,2}n^{2l-s}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)}{b_{2l}n^{2l}+b_{2l-1}n^{2l-1}+\cdots+b_{2l-2r}n^{2l-2r}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{-l}{n^{2}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right),

as required. In particular, we proved the lemma for
j=2
. Now, we assume that for a fixed
s
the claim holds for every
2\leqslant m\leqslant j-1
, and it suffices to verify its correctness for
m=j
. The induction hypothesis maintains that

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}f\right)(n)=1+\frac{d_{2}}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle+\cdots+\frac{d_{j-1}}{n^{j-1}}+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{s-j+1}d_{2l-j+1-i,j-1}n^{2l-j+1-i}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)}{b_{2l}n^{2l}+\cdots+b_{2l-s}n^{2l-s}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)}.

Since
j\leqslant s
, we may also write

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}f\right)(n)=1

\displaystyle+\frac{d_{2}}{n^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{d_{j-1}}{n^{j-1}}+\frac{d_{2l-j,j-1}/b_{2l}}{n^{j}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sum_{i=j+1}^{s}\left(d_{2l-i,j-1}-d_{2l-j,j-1}b_{2l-i+j}/b_{2l}\right)n^{2l-i}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)}{b_{2l}n^{2l}+\cdots+b_{2l-s}n^{2l-s}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle+\frac{d_{2}}{n^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{d_{j-1}}{n^{j-1}}+\frac{d_{j}}{n^{j}}+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{s-j}d_{2l-j-i,j}n^{2l-j-i}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)}{b_{2l}n^{2l}+\cdots+b_{2l-s}n^{2l-s}+o\left(n^{2l-s}\right)}.

This completes the proof by the law of induction.
∎

The upcoming result is a key to resolve the
r
-log-concavity problem of both some functions ‘similar’ to polynomials in general, and the restricted partition function in particular.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
l
and
r
be arbitrary positive integers such that
l\geqslant 2r
. Suppose further that
f(n)=a_{l}n^{l}+a_{l-1}n^{l-1}+\cdots+a_{l-2r}n^{l-2r}+o\left(n^{l-2r}\right)
. Then

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{j-1}f\right)(n)=1+\frac{2^{j}-2^{j-1}l-2}{n^{2}}+q_{j}(n)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}\right)

for any
1\leqslant j\leqslant r
, where
q_{j}(n)
is an expression of the form

q_{j}(n)=\frac{z_{3,j}}{n^{3}}+\cdots+\frac{z_{2(r-j+1),j}}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}

for some real numbers
z_{3,j},z_{4,j},\ldots,z_{2(r-j+1),j}
.

Proof.

Let us fix parameters
l
,
r
and a function
f(n)
. Applying Lemma
4.5
with
s=2r
maintains that

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}f\right)(n)=\frac{f(n)f(n+2)}{f^{2}(n+1)}=1+\frac{-l}{n^{2}}+\frac{d_{3}}{n^{3}}+\cdots+\frac{d_{2r}}{n^{2r}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2r}}\right)

with some real numbers
d_{3},d_{4},\ldots,d_{2r}
. This completes the proof in the case of
j=1
. Now, let us assume that the statement is valid for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant j-1
, and let us verify its correctness for
i=j
. It follows from the induction hypothesis that

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{j-2}f\right)(n)=1+\frac{2^{j-1}-2^{j-2}l-2}{n^{2}}+q_{j-1}(n)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+2)}}\right),

where
q_{j-1}(n)
is an expression of the form

\displaystyle q_{j}(n)=\frac{z_{3,j-1}}{n^{3}}+\cdots+\frac{z_{2(r-j+2),j-1}}{n^{2(r-j+2)}}

for some real numbers
z_{3,j-1},z_{4,j-1},\ldots,z_{2(r-j+2),j-1}
. To simplify the notation put

\displaystyle t_{n}=1+\frac{m}{n^{2}}+q_{j-1}(n)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+2)}}\right)

with
m=2^{j-1}-2^{j-2}l-2
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{j-2}f\right)(n)=t_{n}.

Our goal is to determine Puiseux-type approximation of
\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{j-1}f\right)(n)
(of degree
2(r-j+1)
). From the generalized binomial theorem, we get that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n+u)^{v}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\binom{-v}{i}n^{-v-i}u^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}\binom{v+i-1}{i}\frac{u^{i}}{n^{v+i}}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{2(r-j+2)-v}(-1)^{i}\binom{v+i-1}{i}\frac{u^{i}}{n^{v+i}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+2)}}\right)

holds for all positive integers
u
and
v
such that
v\leqslant 2(r-j+2)
. Therefore, it is straightforward to see that

\displaystyle t_{n+1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{m}{(n+1)^{2}}+q_{j-1}(n+1)+o\left(\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2(r-j+2)}}\right)\right)^{2}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{2m}{n^{2}}+\widetilde{q}_{j-1}(n)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+2)}}\right),

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{q}_{j-1}(n)=\frac{\widetilde{z}_{3,j-1}}{n^{3}}+\cdots+\frac{\widetilde{z}_{2(r-j+2),j-1}}{n^{2(r-j+2)}}

for some real numbers
\widetilde{z}_{3,j-1},\widetilde{z}_{4,j-1},\ldots,\widetilde{z}_{2(r-j+2),j-1}
. For the sake of brevity, we put

(4.4)

\displaystyle w_{n}=\frac{n^{2}}{m}(t_{n}-1).

Thus, it is clear that

\displaystyle w_{n}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{n^{2}}{m}\cdot q_{j-1}(n)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{z_{3,j-1}/m}{n}+\cdots+\frac{z_{2(r-j+2),j-1}/m}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\widehat{z}_{1}}{n}+\cdots+\frac{\widehat{z}_{2(r-j+1)}}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}\right),

where
\widehat{z}_{i}=z_{i+2,j-1}/m
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant 2(r-j+1)
. We apply Taylor expansion of the logarithm function in order to estimate
w_{n}w_{n+2}/w_{n+1}^{2}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\log{w_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{i+1}}{i}(w_{n}-1)^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{1}}{n}+\frac{e_{2}}{n^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{e_{2(r-j+1)}}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}\right),

with
e_{1}=\widehat{z}_{1}
,
e_{2}=\widehat{z}_{2}-\widehat{z}_{1}^{2}/2
and all the remaining values
e_{3},e_{4},\ldots,e_{2(r-j+1)}
are uniquely determined in terms of
\widehat{z}_{1},\widehat{z}_{2},\ldots,\widehat{z}_{2(r-j+1)}
. After some tiresome but elementary calculations, we obtain that

\displaystyle\log{\frac{w_{n}w_{n+2}}{w_{n+1}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\log{w_{n}}+\log{w_{n+2}}-2\log{w_{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{1}}{n}+\cdots+\frac{e_{2(r-j+1)}}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}+\frac{e_{1}}{n+2}+\cdots+\frac{e_{2(r-j+1)}}{(n+2)^{2(r-j+1)}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-2\left(\frac{e_{1}}{n+1}+\cdots+\frac{e_{2(r-j+1)}}{(n+1)^{2(r-j+1)}}\right)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\widehat{e}_{3}}{n^{3}}+\cdots+\frac{\widehat{e}_{2(r-j+1)}}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}\right),

where
\widehat{e}_{3}=2e_{1}
,
\widehat{e}_{4}=6(e_{2}-e_{1})
, and each of the numbers
\widehat{e}_{5},\widehat{e}_{6},\ldots,\widehat{e}_{2(r-j+1)}
is uniquely determined in terms of
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{2(r-j+1)}
. In particular, it means that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}w_{n+2}}{w_{n+1}^{2}}=1+\frac{\check{e}_{3}}{n^{3}}+\frac{\check{e}_{4}}{n^{4}}+\cdots+\frac{\check{e}_{2(r-j+1)}}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}\right),

where all the numbers
\check{e}_{3},\check{e}_{4},\ldots,\check{e}_{2(r-j+1)}
can be explicitly presented in terms of
\widehat{e}_{3},\widehat{e}_{4},\ldots,\widehat{e}_{2(r-j+1)}
, for example, we have
\check{e}_{3}=\widehat{e}_{3}
and
\check{e}_{4}=\widehat{e}_{4}
. Hence, by (
4.2
), (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) we have that

\displaystyle\frac{(t_{n}-1)(t_{n+2}-1)}{(t_{n+1}-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)^{4}}{n^{2}(n+2)^{2}}\cdot\frac{w_{n}w_{n+2}}{w_{n+1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)^{4}}{n^{2}}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=0}^{2(r-j+1)}(-1)^{i}\binom{i+1}{i}\frac{2^{i}}{n^{i+2}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+2)}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=\frac{(n+1)^{4}}{n^{2}}}\times\left(1+\frac{\check{e}_{3}}{n^{3}}+\cdots+\frac{\check{e}_{2(r-j+1)}}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{2}{n^{2}}+\frac{\widetilde{e}_{3}}{n^{3}}+\cdots+\frac{\widetilde{e}_{2(r-j+1)}}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}\right),

where all the values
\widetilde{e}_{3},\widetilde{e}_{4},\ldots,\widetilde{e}_{2(r-j+1)}
can be systematically derived, e.g.
\widetilde{e}_{3}=\check{e}_{3}-4
. Next, Lemma
4.4
and the equality (
4.3
) assert that

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{j-1}p_{\mathcal{A},k}\right)\right)(n)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{2m}{n^{2}}+\widetilde{q}_{j-1}(n)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+2)}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(1+\frac{2}{n^{2}}+\frac{\widetilde{e}_{3}}{n^{3}}+\cdots+\frac{\widetilde{e}_{2(r-j+1)}}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{2m+2}{n^{2}}+q_{j}(n)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{2^{j}-2^{j-1}l-2}{n^{2}}+q_{j}(n)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}\right),

where

q_{j}(n)=\frac{z_{3,j}}{n^{3}}+\cdots+\frac{z_{2(r-j+1),j}}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}

for some real numbers
z_{3,j},z_{4,j},\ldots,z_{2(r-j+1),j}
. This completes the inductive step and thereby ends the proof.
∎

We are ready to present the main result of the paper. Surprisingly, we prove that in two different ways.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
l
and
r
be arbitrary positive integers such that
l\geqslant 2r
. If
f(n)=a_{l}n^{l}+a_{l-1}n^{l-1}+\cdots+a_{l-2r}n^{l-2r}+o\left(n^{l-2r}\right)
, then the sequence
\left(f(n)\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
is asymptotically
r
-log-concave.

Proof.

For fixed parameters
l
and
r
and a function
f(n)
, Theorem
4.6
points out that

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{j-1}f\right)(n)=1+\frac{2^{j}-2^{j-1}l-2}{n^{2}}+q_{j}(n)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}\right)

for any
1\leqslant j\leqslant r
, where
q_{j}(n)
is an expression of the form

q_{j}(n)=\frac{z_{3,j}}{n^{3}}+\cdots+\frac{z_{2(r-j+1),j}}{n^{2(r-j+1)}}

for some real numbers
z_{3,j},z_{4,j},\ldots,z_{2(r-j+1),j}
.
Since we have that
l\geqslant 2r
, one can easily deduce that the inequalities

\displaystyle 2^{j}-2^{j-1}l-2\leqslant 2^{j}-2^{j}r-2<0

are true for every
1\leqslant j\leqslant r
. But in particular it means that the sequence
\left(f(n)\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
is asymptotically
j
-log-concave for any such a
j
, as required.
∎

Let us also present an alternative, more direct, approach to the problem.

Second proof of Theorem
4.7
.

Under the assumptions from the statement, we prove by induction that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{s}f\right)(n)

\displaystyle=a_{l}^{2^{s}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{s}m_{j}^{2^{s-j}}\right)n^{m_{s+1}}+q_{s}(n)+o\left(n^{m_{s+1}-2(r-s)}\right),

holds for each
1\leqslant s\leqslant r
, where
m_{i}=2^{i-1}l+2-2^{i}
and

\displaystyle q_{s}(n)=\delta_{m_{s+1}-1}n^{m_{s+1}-1}+\cdots+\delta_{m_{s+1}-2(r-s)}n^{m_{s+1}-2(r-s)}

with some real numbers
\delta_{i}
. For
s=1
, we have that

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}f\right)(n)

\displaystyle=f^{2}(n+1)-f(n)f(n+2)

\displaystyle=a_{l}^{2}ln^{2l-2}+d_{2l-3}n^{2l-3}+\cdots+d_{2l-2r}n^{2l-2r}+o\left(n^{2l-2r}\right),

where
d_{2l-2},d_{2l-3},\ldots,d_{2l-2r}
are some real numbers, as required. Therefore, let us assume that the claim is true for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant s-1<r
, and verify its correctness for
i=s
. It follows from the induction hypothesis that

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{s-1}f\right)(n)

\displaystyle=a_{l}^{2^{(s-1)}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}m_{j}^{2^{s-1-j}}\right)n^{m_{s}}+q_{s-1}(n)+o\left(n^{m_{s}-2(r-s+1)}\right),

where
q_{s-1}
is of the required form. Now, we can just write

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{s}f\right)(n)

\displaystyle=\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{s-1}f\right)^{2}(n+1)-\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{s-1}f\right)(n)\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{s-1}f\right)(n+2)

\displaystyle=\left(a_{l}^{2^{(s-1)}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}m_{j}^{2^{s-1-j}}\right)\right)^{2}m_{s}n^{2m_{s}-2}+q_{s}(n)+o\left(n^{2m_{s}-2(r-s)-2}\right)

\displaystyle=a^{2^{s}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{s}m_{j}^{2^{s-j}}\right)n^{m_{s+1}}+q_{s}(n)+o\left(n^{m_{s+1}-2(r-s)}\right),

where

\displaystyle q_{s}(n)=\delta_{m_{s+1}-1}n^{m_{s+1}-1}+\cdots+\delta_{m_{s+1}-2(r-s)}n^{m_{s+1}-2(r-s)}

with some real numbers
\delta_{i}
. This ends the proof of (
4.6
) by the law of induction.

Now, it is enough to see that the leading coefficient of (
4.6
) is positive for any
1\leqslant s\leqslant r
. Thus the sequence
\left(f(n)\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
is asymptotically
r
-log-concave, as required.
∎

It is worth noting that despite the fact that the former proof requires more sophisticated preparation than the latter one, the method which we use there might be also effectively applied for more complicated functions like the partition function
[
22
]
or the overpartition function
[
28
]
.

In particular, Theorem
4.7
delivers us the following efficient criterion for the
r
-log-concavity of the restricted partition function.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
,
r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
k>2r
be fixed. Suppose further that
\gcd A=1
for all
(k-2r)
-multisubsets
A
of
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
. Then the sequence
\left(p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
is asymptotically
r
-log-concave.

Proof.

The property is a direct consequence of Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
4.7
.
∎

There appears a natural question whether Theorem
4.8
is optimal in a sense that it can not be further generalized for any other sequences
\mathcal{A}
. In fact, it is true, which directly follows from the following more general result.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
l
and
r
be arbitrary positive integers such that
l\geqslant 2r
. Suppose further that we have

\displaystyle f(n)=a_{l}(n)n^{l}+a_{l-1}(n)n^{l-1}+\cdots+a_{l-2r}(n)n^{l-2r}+o\left(n^{l-2r}\right),

where the coefficients
a_{l-2r}(n),\ldots,a_{l}(n)
might depend on the residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0muM)
for some positive integer
M\geqslant 2
. Then the sequence
\left(f(n)\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
is asymptotically
r
-log-concave if and only if all the numbers
a_{l-2r}(n),\ldots,a_{l}(n)
are independent of the residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0muM)
.

Proof.

The implication to the left hand side is clear by Theorem
4.7
. To deal with the implication to the right hand side let us present the reasoning by induction on
r
. First, let us assume that
r=1
and suppose, for contradiction, that at least one of the coefficients
a_{l}(n)
,
a_{l-1}(n)
or
a_{l-2}(n)
depend on the residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0muM)
. Let us set
t=\max\{j\in\{l-2,l-1,l\}:a_{j}(n)\text{ is not constant}\}
. It is enough to consider such residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0muM)
that
a_{t}(n+1)
is the smallest and at least one of
a_{t}(n)
or
a_{t}(n+2)
is strictly bigger than
a_{t}(n+1)
, and compute
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}f(n)
. In that case, we get that the leading coefficient is negative. Hence, the sequence
\left(f(n)\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
can not be asymptotically
1
-log-concave, as required.

Now, let us assume that the statement is valid for each
1\leqslant\widetilde{r}<r
and let us examine whether it is also true for
\widetilde{r}=r
. The induction hypothesis asserts that

\displaystyle f(n)=\sum_{i=l-2r+2}^{l}a_{i}n^{i}+a_{l-2r+1}(n)n^{l-2r+1}+a_{l-2r}(n)n^{l-2r}+o\left(n^{l-2r}\right),

otherwise
f(n)
could not be even asymptotically
(r-1)
-log-concave. Suppose for contradiction that at least one of the coefficients
a_{l-2r}(n)
or
a_{l-2r+1}(n)
depend on the residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0muM)
. Let us assume that
a_{l-2r+1}(n)
is not independent of the residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0muM)
— the alternative reasoning for
a_{l-2r}(n)
is analogous, and we leave it as an exercise for the reader. The coefficient
b_{2l-2r+1}(n)
of

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{L}}f(n)=\left(\sum_{i=2l-2r+2}^{2l-2}b_{i}n^{i}\right)+b_{2l-2r+1}(n)n^{2l-2r+1}+b_{2l-2r}(n)n^{2l-2r}+o\left(n^{2l-2r}\right)

is of the form

\displaystyle b_{2l-2r+1}(n)=\left(2a_{l-2r+1}(n+1)-a_{l-2r+1}(n)-a_{l-2r+1}(n+2)\right)a_{l}-q,

where
q
is a constant depending on
a_{l-2r+2},\ldots,a_{l}
. Therefore, if we take sucha residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0muM)
that
a_{l-2r+1}(n+1)
is the smallest and at least one of
a_{l-2r+1}(n)
or
a_{l-2r+1}(n+2)
is strictly bigger than
a_{l-2r+1}(n+1)
, and we do the opposite (considering the largest possible coefficient
a_{l-2r+1}(n+1)
and at least one of
a_{l-2r+1}(n)
or
a_{l-2r+1}(n+2)
strictly smaller than
a_{l-2r+1}(n+1)
), then we deduce that the coefficient
b_{2l-2r+1}(n)
of
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}f(n)
depends on the residue class of
n\mkern 4.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0muM)
. Thus the induction hypothesis points out that
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}f(n)
can not be asymptotically
(r-1)
-log-concave which implies that
f(n)
is not
r
-log-concave, as required.
∎

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
,
r\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
k>2r
be fixed. Then the sequence
\left(p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
is asymptotically
r
-log-concave if and only if we have that
\gcd A=1
for all
(k-2r)
-multisubsets
A
of
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}
.

Proof.

The implication to the left hand side follows from Theorem
4.8
. On the other hand, the implication to the right hand side is a direct consequence of both Theorem
4.9
and Proposition
2.2
.
∎

It is worth noting that one can use the above criterion to explicitly calculate when the
r
-log-concavity of
p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)
holds for given parameters
\mathcal{A}
,
k
and
r
.

Until now, we have not discussed the easiest case of the quasi-polynomial-like function. Therefore, for the sake of completeness, let us present a criterion for the
r
-log-concavity of a polynomial.

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
f(n)=a_{k}n^{k}+a_{k-1}n^{k-1}+\cdots+a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}[n]
be a polynomial of
\deg(f)=k
. Then the sequence
\left(f(n)\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
is

(1)

not asymptotically
1
-log-concave if and only if
k=0
;

(2)

at most asymptotically
1
-log-concave if and only if
k=1
;

(3)

asymptotically
r
-log-concave for any
r\geqslant 1
if and only if
k\geqslant 2
.

Proof.

The statement can be easily verified for
k\leqslant 1
. If
k\geqslant 2
, then Theorem
4.7
asserts that the sequence
\left(f(n)\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
is asymptotically
1
-log-concave. Moreover, we know that

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}f(n)=b_{2k-2}n^{2k-2}+b_{2k-3}n^{2k-3}+\cdots+b_{0}

for some real numbers
b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{2k-2}
with
b_{2k-2}>0
. Since
2k-2\geqslant 2
, we obtain that the sequence
\left(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}f(n)\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
is asymptotically
1
-log-concave (in particular,
\left(f(n)\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
is asymptotically
2
-log-concave). By repeating the above procedure, we deduce the required property.
∎

Corollary 4.12
.

If
\mathcal{A}=(1,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}1},\ldots)
, then the sequence
\left(p_{\mathcal{A}}(n,k)\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}
is

(1)

not asymptotically
1
-log-concave if and only if
k=1
;

(2)

at most asymptotically
1
-log-concave if and only if
k=2
;

(3)

asymptotically
r
-log-concave for any
r\geqslant 1
if and only if
k\geqslant 3
.

Proof.

That is a direct consequence of the formula (

) and Corollary
4.11
.
∎

Abstract
. This article investigates the properties of order-divisor graphs associated with finite groups. An order-divisor graph of a finite group is an undirected graph in which the set of vertices includes all elements of the group, and two distinct vertices with different orders are adjacent if the order of one vertex divides the order of the other. We prove some beautiful results in order-divisor graphs of finite groups. The primary focus is on examining the girth, degree of vertices, and size of the order-divisor graph. In particular, we provide a comprehensive description of these parameters for the order-divisor graphs of finite cyclic groups and dihedral groups.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda m\rvert n}\phi(\lambda m)-\phi(m)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m)-\phi(m)

\displaystyle m-\phi(m)+\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m)-\phi(m)

\displaystyle deg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m-2\phi(m)+\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{i}-2\phi(p^{i})+\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{p^{k}}{p^{i}}}\phi(p^{i}\lambda)

When
i\geq 1
,

\displaystyle deg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p^{i-1}-p^{i}+\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert{p^{k-i}}}\phi(p^{i}\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p^{i-1}-p^{i}+\phi(p^{i})+\phi(p^{i+1})+\phi(p^{i+2})+...+\phi(p^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p^{i-1}-p^{i}+(p^{i}-p^{i-1})+(p^{i+1}-p^{i})+(p^{i+2}-p^{i}+1)+\cdots+(p^{k}-p^{k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{k}+p^{i-1}-p^{i}.

When
i=0
,

\displaystyle deg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1+1+\phi(p)+\phi(p^{2})+\cdots+\phi(p^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{k}-1.

∎

Next, we explore intriguing findings regarding the sum of degrees in the order-divisor graph and the sum
of the orders of each element in the associated group.

Corollary 23
.

For every prime
p
and each positive integer
k
,

\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}}deg(x)

=

\frac{2p^{2k}-2}{p+1}
.

Proof.

We know that, for each divisor
d
of
n
,
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
contains exactly
\phi(d)
elements that have order
d
. Therefore, by Corollary
22
,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}}deg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p^{k}-1)+(p^{k}+1-p)\phi(p)+(p^{k}+p-p^{2})\phi(p^{2})+\cdots+(p^{k}+p^{k-1}-p^{k})\phi(p^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p^{k}-1)+(p^{k}+1-p)(p-1)+(p^{k}+p-p^{2})(p^{2}-p)+\cdots+(p^{k}+p^{k-1}-p^{k})(p^{k}-p^{k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p^{k}-1)+(p^{k}-(p-1))(p-1)+(p^{k}-(p^{2}-p))(p^{2}-p)+\cdots+(p^{k}-(p^{k}-p^{k-1}))(p^{k}-p^{k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p^{k}-1)+p^{k}(p^{k}-1)-\left((p-1)^{2}+p^{2}(p-1)^{2}+p^{4}(p-1)^{2}+\cdots+p^{2(k-1)}(p-1)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{2k}-1-(p-1)^{2}\left(\frac{p^{2k}-1}{p^{2}-1}\right)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}}deg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2p^{2k}-2}{p+1}.

∎

Remark 32
.

It is quite difficult to compute directly the degree of each vertex in
OD(D_{n})
for the large
values of
n
. Hence there is a need to find some explicit formula to determine the degree without actually drawing
O(D_{n})
. In this section,
we introduce a general formula for calculating the degree of each vertex in
O(D_{n})
.

Theorem 33
.

Let
n
be an odd positive integer with
n\geq 3
and let
x\in D_{n}
has order
m
. Then

deg(x)=\begin{cases}m-2\phi(m)+\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m),&\text{if $m\textgreater 2$}\\
2n-1,&\text{if $m=1$}\\
1,&\text{if $m=2$},\end{cases}

where
\phi
represents the Euler’s totient function.

Proof.

When
m

>2
, then
x\in<a>
. Hence,

\displaystyle Ldeg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{d\rvert m}\phi(d)-\phi(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m-\phi(m).

and

\displaystyle Udeg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda m\rvert n}\phi(\lambda m)-\phi(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m)-\phi(m).

Hence,

\displaystyle deg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ldeg(x)+Udeg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m-\phi(m)+\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m)-\phi(m)

\displaystyle deg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m-2\phi(m)+\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m).

When
m=1
, then
x
is the identity element and so
deg(x)=2n-1
. Also when
m=2
, then since
n
is odd, the identity is the only vertex adjacent with
x
.
Hence, in this case,

deg(x)=1
.
∎

Theorem 34
.

Let
n
be an even positive integer with
n\geq 3
and let
x\in D_{n}
has order
m
. Then

deg(x)=\begin{cases}m-2\phi(m)+\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m),&\text{if $m$ is odd};\\
\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{2}}\phi(2\lambda),&\text{if $m=2$};\\
n+m-2\phi(m)+\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m),&\text{if $m$ is even, $m\textgreater 2$};\\
2n-1,&\text{if $m=1$},\end{cases}

where
\phi
represents the Euler’s totient function.

Proof.

Suppose
m
is odd. Then
x\in<a>=\{e,a,a^{2},...,a^{n-1}\}
and
x
cannot be adjacent to any vertex in
\{b,ab,a^{2}b,...,a^{n-1}b\}
.
Hence

\displaystyle Ldeg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{d\rvert m}\phi(d)-\phi(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m-\phi(m).

and

\displaystyle Udeg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda m\rvert n}\phi(\lambda m)-\phi(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m)-\phi(m).

Hence,

\displaystyle deg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ldeg(x)+Udeg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m-\phi(m)+\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m)-\phi(m)

\displaystyle deg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m-2\phi(m)+\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m).

Suppose
m=2
. Then
x
cannot be adjacent to any vertx in
\{b,ab,ab^{2},...,ab^{n-1}\}
. Hence
Ldeg(x)=1
. Thus

\displaystyle deg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ldeg(x)+Udeg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m)-1=\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m).

Suppose
m>2
is even. Then
x\in<a>=\{e,a,a^{2},..,a^{n-1}\}
and all the vertices in
\{b,ab,a^{2}b,..,a^{n-1}b\}
are lower vertices adjacent to
x
.

Hence

\displaystyle Ldeg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n+\sum\limits_{d\rvert m}\phi(d)-\phi(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n+m-\phi(m).

and

\displaystyle Udeg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda m\rvert n}\phi(\lambda m)-\phi(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m)-\phi(m).

Hence,

\displaystyle deg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ldeg(x)+Udeg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n+m-\phi(m)+\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m)-\phi(m)

\displaystyle deg(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n+m-2\phi(m)+\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{m}}\phi(\lambda m).

When
m=1
, then
x
is the identity element and so
deg(x)=2n-1
.
∎

Theorem 35
.

Let
n
be an odd integer with
n\geq 3
. Then
the size of
OD\big(D_{n}\big)
is given by

|E(OD(D_{n}))|=\frac{1}{2}\left(3n-1+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\mid n\\
m>1\end{subarray}}\bigg(m-2\phi\big(m\big)+\sum\limits_{\lambda|\frac{n}{m}}\phi\big(\lambda m\big)\bigg)\phi\big(m\big)\right)
, where
\phi
represents the Euler’s totient function.

Proof.

Since,
n
is odd, the elements with order
2
are
\{b,ab,a^{2}b,...,a^{n-1}b\}
.
Moreover in the cyclic part
<a>
of
D_{n}
, for each divisor
m
of
n
,
there are exactly
\phi(m)
elements of order
m
.
Then apply Theorem
33
and the well known Handshaking Lemma.
∎

Theorem 36
.

Let
n
be an even integer with
\geq 3
. Then
the size of
OD\big(D_{n}\big)
is given by

\displaystyle|E(OD(D_{n}))|=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{aligned} &2n-1+\big(n+1\big)\sum\limits_{\lambda\rvert\frac{n}{2}}\phi(2\lambda)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m>1,m\mid n\\

&+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m>2,m\mid n\\
m~is~even\end{subarray}}\bigg(n+m-2\phi\big(m\big)+\sum\limits_{\lambda|\frac{n}{m}}\phi\big(\lambda m\big)\bigg)\phi\big(m\big)\end{aligned}\right),

\phi
represents the Euler’s totient function.

Proof.

Since,
n
is even, so the elements with order
2
are
\{a^{n/2},b,ab,a^{2}b,...,a^{n-1}b\}
.
Moreover in the cyclic part
<a>
of
D_{n}
, for each divisor
m
of
n
,
there are exactly
\phi(m)
elements of order
m
.
Then apply Theorem
34
and the well known Handshaking Lemma.
∎

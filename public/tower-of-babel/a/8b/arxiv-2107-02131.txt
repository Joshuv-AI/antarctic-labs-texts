Local Statistics for Zeros of Artin-Schreier L L -functions
Alexei Entin, Noam Pirani

Abstract
We study the local statistics of zeros of L L -functions attached to Artin-Scheier curves over finite fields. We consider three families of Artin-Schreier L L -functions: the ordinary, polynomial (the p p -rank 0 stratum) and odd-polynomial families. We compute the 1-level zero-density of the first and third families and the 2-level density of the second family for test functions with Fourier transform supported in a suitable interval. In each case we obtain agreement with a unitary or symplectic random matrix model.

The zeta function of

The

\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{0}=\left\{f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]:f=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}x^{i},a_{d}\neq 0,a_{i}=0\mbox{ if }i>0,p|i\right\}.

3.

The

\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{0,\mathrm{odd}}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{0}:f(x)=-f(-x)\right\}.

The curves in this subfamily of the polynomial A-S family have the property that

The distribution of

Next we define the 1-level and 2-level densities for the zeros of

W_{1}(f,\psi,\Phi)=\sum_{i=1}^{2\mathfrak{g}/(p-1)}\phi_{2\mathfrak{g}/(p-1)}(\theta_{i}(f,\psi)),

\phi_{2\mathfrak{g}/(p-1)}(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi\left(\frac{2\mathfrak{g}}{p-1}(t+n)\right),\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}(C_{f}),

(1.4)

Similarly for a bivariate test function

Reformulation in terms of Dirichlet
L
-functions

Throughout the rest of the paper
p>2
is a prime,
q
is a power of
p
and we fix a non-trivial additive character
\psi:\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. In the present section we explain how to associate a primitive Dirichlet character
\chi_{f}
with each A-S
L
-function
L(u,f,\psi)
in each of the families we consider, so that
L(u,\chi_{f})=(1-u)L(u,f,\psi)
(the left hand side is a Dirichlet
L
-function). That a suitable (generalized) Dirichlet character exists for any A-S
L
-function follows from class field theory, but we will need to compute these characters explicitly and will only do so for polynomial and ordinary A-S
L
-functions.

2.1
Dirichlet characters and L-functions

We recall some basic facts about Dirichlet characters over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and their associated
L
-functions. See
[
26
]
*Chapter 4 for a thorough introduction.

Denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of monic polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. Let
Q(x)\in\mathcal{M}
be a monic polynomial of degree
m\geq 1
, and let
\chi
be a character of the multiplicative group
(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/Q)^{\times}
. We may extend
\chi
to a strongly multiplicative function
\chi:\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

\chi(g)=\begin{cases}0,&(g,Q)\neq 1,\\
\chi(g\text{ mod }Q),&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The extended function is called a (classical) Dirichlet character. A Dirichlet character
\chi
modulo
Q
is called
primitive
if there is no proper divisor
Q_{1}|Q
such that for
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
coprime with
Q
the value
\chi(g)
depends only on
g\text{ mod }Q_{1}
. It is called
trivial
if it takes
1
on every polynomial coprime with
Q
. It is called
even
if it takes the value
1
on the constants
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, and
odd
otherwise.
We denote

e(\chi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\chi\mbox{ is even},\\
0,&\chi\mbox{ is odd}.\end{array}\right.

If
Q\in\mathcal{M}
is a modulus we denote by
(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/Q)^{\times*}
the group of Dirichlet characters modulo
Q
(which we identify with the dual group of
(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/Q)^{\times}
). If
H\subset(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/Q)^{\times*}
is a subset, we denote by
H^{\mathrm{pr}}
the set of primitive characters in
H
.

To a Dirichlet character
\chi
one attaches its Dirichlet
L
-function

L(u,\chi)=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{M}}\chi(F)u^{\deg{F}}=\prod_{P\in\mathcal{P}}(1-\chi(P)u^{\deg{P}})^{-1}=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}A_{r}(\chi)u^{r},

(2.1)

where
A_{r}(\chi)=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{M}\atop{\deg F=r}}\chi(F)
and
\mathcal{P}
is the set of prime polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, where by a prime polynomial we always mean a monic irreducible polynomial.
If
\chi
is non-trivial then
A_{r}(\chi)=0
for
r\geq m=\deg Q
and thus
L(u,\chi)
is a polynomial of degree
\leq m-1
in the variable
u
.

If
\chi
is a primitive character,
L(u,\chi)
factors as follows:

L(u,\chi)=(1-u)^{e(\chi)}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1-e(\chi)}(1-\rho_{i}q^{1/2}u).

By the Riemann Hypothesis for curves over finite fields we also have
|\rho_{i}|=1
, and
\rho_{i}
are called the normalized (inverse) roots or zeros of
L(u,\chi)
.

Recall that we fix a non-trivial additive character

Lemma 2.1

Let

2.4
Associating a Dirichlet character with an ordinary Artin-Schreier curve

Let
d
be a natural number,

a monic squarefree polynomial of degree
d
or
d-1
and
f=h/g\in\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{\mathrm{ord}}
. Recall that this condition means that
(h,g)=1
and additionally
\deg h\leq d
if
\deg g=d
and
\deg h=d
if
\deg g=d-1
(equivalently the polar divisor of
f=h/g
has degree
d
, and no non-simple poles).
For
f=h/g\in\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{\mathrm{ord}}

and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
define

\chi_{f}(c)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}\psi\left(\mathrm{tr}_{q/p}\left(\sum_{c(\alpha)=0}f(\alpha)\right)\right),&(c,g)=1,\\
0,&(c,g)\neq 1,\end{array}\right.

(2.7)

the summation being over roots in
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
counted with multiplicity.
Note that for
(c,g)=1
,
\sum_{c(\alpha)=0}{f(\alpha)}
is in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, since it is a symmetric function in the roots of
c
. Moreover,
\chi_{f}
is clearly a strongly multiplicative function. It is not generally a Dirichlet character, but we will see below that it is a Dirichlet character modulo
g^{2}
provided
\deg h<\deg g
. The
L
-function of
\chi_{f}
is defined as in (

).

Remark 2.7
.

Note that this definition of
\chi_{f}
differs from (
2.4
). We will make it clear in the beginning of each section which of the two notions of
\chi_{f}
we use, but there should be little confusion because we will only use (
2.4
) when considering the polynomial families and (

) when considering the ordinary family.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
f=h/g\in\mathcal{AS}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{d}
. Then
L(u,\chi_{f})=(1-\delta(f)u)L(u,f,\psi)
, where

\delta(f)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}\psi\left(\mathrm{tr}_{q/p}f(\infty)\right),&\deg h=\deg g=d,\\
1,&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(2.8)

Proof.

Let
r
be a natural number and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
an element with (monic) minimal polynomial
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. Since
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
we have
\deg c|r
. Note that

\psi\left(\mathrm{tr}_{q^{r}/p}f(\alpha)\right)=\psi\left(\frac{r}{\deg c}\mathrm{tr}_{q^{\deg c}/p}f(\alpha)\right)=\chi_{f}(c)^{r/\deg c}

(2.9)

by (
2.7
).
By (
1.3
) and (
2.8
) we have

L(u,f,\psi)=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{u^{r}}{r}\left(\delta(f)^{r}+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}},g(\alpha)\neq 0}\psi\left(\mathrm{tr}_{q^{r}/p}f(\alpha)\right)\right)\right]\right).

Using the multiplicativity of the function
\chi_{f}(c)
and (
2.9
),

u\frac{\mathrm{d}\log L(u,f,\psi)}{\mathrm{d}u}=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}u^{r}\left(\delta(f)^{r}+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}},g(x)\neq 0}\psi\left(\mathrm{tr}_{q^{r}/p}f(\alpha)\right)\right)\\
=\frac{\delta(f)u}{1-\delta(f)u}+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}u^{r}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{P}\atop{\deg c|r}}(\deg c)\chi_{f}(c)^{r/\deg c}=\frac{\delta(f)u}{1-\delta(f)u}+\sum_{c\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\deg c)\chi_{f}(c)^{k}u^{k(\deg c)}\\
=\frac{\delta(f)u}{1-\delta(f)u}+\sum_{c\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{(\deg c)\chi_{f}(c)u^{\deg c}}{1-\chi_{f}(c)u^{\deg c}}=\frac{\delta(f)u}{1-\delta(f)u}+\sum_{c\in\mathcal{P}}u\frac{\mathrm{d}\log(1-\chi_{f}(c)u^{\deg c})^{-1}}{\mathrm{d}u}\\
=u\frac{\mathrm{d}\log\left((1-\delta(f)u)^{-1}L(u,\chi_{f})\right)}{\mathrm{d}u}.

Since
L(0,f,\psi)=L(0,\chi_{f})=1
we have
L(u,f,\psi)=(1-\delta(f)u)^{-1}L(u,\chi_{f})
.
∎

It turns out that for
f=h/g\in\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{\mathrm{ord}}
the function
\chi_{f}
is a Hecke character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
(see
[
26
]
*§9 for this notion) with modulus
g^{2}
if
\deg h\leq\deg g=d
and
g^{2}\cdot\infty^{2}
if
\deg h>\deg g=d-1
. We will only need this fact for the case
\deg h<\deg g
, in which case
\chi_{f}
is in fact a Dirichlet character modulo
g^{2}
. For monic squarefree
g
of degree
d
we denote

\mathcal{H}_{g}=\{f=h/g:h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x],(h,g)=1,\deg h<d\}\subset\mathcal{AS}_{d,g}^{\mathrm{ord}}.

(2.10)

Proposition 2.9
.

For
f=h/g\in\mathcal{H}_{g}
, the function
\chi_{f}
is a Dirichlet character modulo
g^{2}
.

Proof.

The (strong) multiplicativity of
\chi_{f}
is immediate from (
2.7
), so we need to show that
\chi_{h/g}(c)
depends only on
c
mod
g^{2}
. We prove the stronger claim that

\sum_{c(\alpha)=0}{h(\alpha)}/{g(\alpha)}

depends only on
c
(mod
g^{2}
). We factor
g
over
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, writing

g=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x-\beta_{i})
. Note that all the
\beta_{i}
are distinct since we assumed
g
is squarefree in the definition of
\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{\mathrm{ord}}
. Since
\deg h<d=\deg g
we can write a partial fraction decomposition

\frac{h(x)}{g(x)}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{c_{i}}{x-\beta_{i}},

for some
c_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. This allows us to write

\sum_{c(\alpha)=0}\frac{h(\alpha)}{g(\alpha)}=\sum_{c(\alpha)=0}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{c_{i}}{\alpha-\beta_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\sum_{c(\alpha)=0}\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta_{i}}.

We claim that for every
i
,
\sum_{c(\alpha)=0}\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta_{i}}

depends only on the residue class
c\bmod{(x-\beta_{i})^{2}}
. Then the whole sum will be determined by
c
mod
g^{2}=(x-\beta_{1})^{2}\cdots(x-\beta_{d})^{2}
. Fix some
1\leq i\leq d
and write

c=a_{0}+a_{1}(x-\beta_{i})+\cdots+a_{r}(x-\beta_{i})^{r},a_{j}\in{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}.

By Vieta’s formula, we have
\sum_{c(\alpha)=0}\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta_{i}}=-a_{1}/a_{0}
. Thus it depends only on
c\bmod(x-\beta_{i})^{2}
and therefore the sum
\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\sum_{c(\alpha)=0}\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta_{i}}
depends only on
c\bmod g^{2}
, as required.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

The map
f\mapsto\chi_{f}
gives a bijection between
\mathcal{H}_{g}
and the set of primitive Dirichlet characters modulo
g^{2}
of order
p
. In particular for
f\in\mathcal{H}_{g}
the character
\chi_{f}
is even.

Proof.

First we show that
f\mapsto\chi_{f}
is injective on
\mathcal{H}_{g}
.

Assume that
\chi_{f_{1}}=\chi_{f_{2}}
for some
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{g}
. Denote
f=f_{1}-f_{2}
and
f=h/g,\deg h<d=\deg g
. For every
r\geq 1
, and every
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
such that
g(\alpha)\neq 0
, we have
\mathrm{tr}_{q^{r}/p}(f(\alpha))=0
by plugging the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

into (
2.7
). By the additive version of Hilbert’s Theorem 90 we have
f(\alpha)=b^{p}-b

for some
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
. Fixing this
b
we note that it is also true that
f(\alpha)=(b+i)^{p}-(b+i)
for
i\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Thus there are at least
p
points defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
on the curve
y^{p}-y=f(x)
with
abscissa
x=\alpha
. This is true for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
with
g(\alpha)\neq 0
, so the
number of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
-rational points on the curve
y^{p}-y=f(x)
(and hence on
C_{f}
) is at least
pq^{r}-(p-1)\deg g
.
Note that this is true for every
r\geq 1
. This is impossible since the curve

C_{f}
is absolutely irreducible and therefore
\#C_{f}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})\sim q^{r}
as
r\to\infty
.

Hence it remains to show that the number of order
p
primitive characters modulo
g^{2}
is exactly
\#\mathcal{H}_{g}
. It is immediate from the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{g}
that
\#\mathcal{H}_{g}=\varphi(g)
, where
\varphi(g)=\#(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/g)^{\times}
denotes the Euler totient function.

To count the order
p
primitive characters modulo
g^{2}
we apply inclusion-exclusion (
\mu
denotes the Möbius function):

\displaystyle\#

\displaystyle\{\text{order }p\text{ primitive characters modulo }g^{2}\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q|g^{2}}\mu(Q)\cdot\#\{\text{order }p\text{ characters with modulus }g^{2}/Q\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q|g}\mu(Q)\cdot\#\{\text{order }p\text{ characters modulo }g^{2}/Q\}.

Factor
g=Q_{1}\cdots Q_{l}
into distinct primes. For
Q=Q_{1}\cdots Q_{s}
we have
g^{2}/Q=Q_{1}\cdots Q_{s}\cdot Q_{s+1}^{2}\cdots Q_{l}^{2}
and therefore

(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(g^{2}/Q))^{\times}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}\mathbb{Z}/(q^{\deg Q_{i}}-1)\oplus\bigoplus_{i=s+1}^{l}(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\log_{p}q\cdot\deg Q_{i}}.

The
p
-torsion subgroup of
(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(g^{2}/Q))^{\times*}
is isomorphic to the
p
-torsion subgroup of

(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(g^{2}/Q))^{\times}
which has order
\prod_{i=s+1}^{l}p^{\log_{p}q\cdot\deg Q_{i}}=q^{\deg(g/Q)}
.

Hence

\#\{\text{order }p\text{ primitive character modulo }g^{2}\}=\sum_{Q|g}\mu(Q)q^{\deg(g/Q)}=\varphi(g)=\#\mathcal{H}_{g},

which completes the proof.
∎

2.5
Averaging traces and trace products over primitive Dirichlet characters

For an even primitive Dirichlet character
\chi
modulo
Q
with
\deg Q=m
we recall that its
L
-function factors as follows:

L(u,\chi)=(1-u)\prod_{i=1}^{m-2}(1-q^{1/2}\rho_{i}u),

(2.11)

with
|\rho_{i}|=1
. For any integer
r
define the
r
-th trace of
\chi
by

T_{\chi}^{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{m-2}\rho_{i}^{r}.

(2.12)

This quantity can be interpreted as the (normalized) trace of the
r
-th power of the Frobenius acting on a certain irreducible piece of the étale cohomology of the Carlitz curve of
Q
, but we will not need this fact and will only use the elementary expression (
2.12
). The
r
-th traces are instrumental in studying
L
-zero densities (and other
L
-zero statistics).

Applying the operator
u\frac{\mathrm{d}\log}{\mathrm{d}u}
to (
2.11
), using the Euler product (
2.1
) and comparing coefficients we get for any integer
r\geq 1
:

T^{r}_{\chi}=\sum_{i=1}^{m-2}\rho_{i}^{r}=-q^{-r/2}-q^{-r/2}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}\atop{\deg P|r}}(\deg P)\chi(P)^{r/\deg P}=-q^{-r/2}-q^{-r/2}\sum_{\deg c=r\atop{\mathrm{monic}}}\Lambda(c)\chi(c),

(2.13)

where

\Lambda(c)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\deg P,&c=P^{k},P\mbox{ prime},k\geq 1,\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

is the von Mangoldt function.
Note that
T^{-r}_{\chi}=\overline{T^{r}_{\chi}}
.

Let
Q\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a (monic) modulus of degree
m
, and let

G=\{\chi\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/Q\right)^{\times*}:\chi(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})=1\}

be the group of even Dirichlet characters modulo
Q
, which we view as a subgroup of the dual group of
(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/Q)^{\times}
. For a monic divisor
Q^{\prime}|Q
, we denote by
G_{Q^{\prime}}
the subgroup of even Dirichlet characters with period
Q^{\prime}
(i.e. characters
\chi
modulo
Q
such that for
(g,Q)=1
the value
\chi(g)
depends only on
g\bmod Q^{\prime}
). Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. We denote by
{H^{\mathrm{pr}}}
the set of primitive characters inside
H
. If
X:H\to\mathbb{C}
is a function, we can express the sum
\sum_{\chi\in H^{\mathrm{pr}}}X(\chi)
in terms of sums over subgroups of
H
. Denote by
H_{Q^{\prime}}=H\cap G_{Q^{\prime}}
the subgroup of characters in
H
of period
Q^{\prime}
. By inclusion-exclusion we have

\sum_{\chi\in H^{\mathrm{pr}}}X(\chi)=\sum_{Q^{\prime}|Q}\mu(Q/Q^{\prime})\sum_{\chi\in H_{Q^{\prime}}}X(\chi),

where
\mu
is the Möbius function, and the sum is over monic divisors
Q^{\prime}
of
Q
. The last equation allows us to express averages over primitive characters in
H
in terms of averages over subgroups of
H
:

\left\langle X(\chi)\right\rangle_{\chi\in H^{\mathrm{pr}}}=\sum_{Q^{\prime}|Q}\mu(Q/Q^{\prime})\frac{\#H_{Q^{\prime}}}{\#H^{\mathrm{pr}}}\left\langle X(\chi)\right\rangle_{\chi\in H_{Q^{\prime}}}.

(2.14)

For integers
r,s
we denote the means of traces and trace products over
{H^{\mathrm{pr}}}
by

M_{H}^{r}=\left\langle T_{\chi}^{r}\right\rangle_{\chi\in{H^{\mathrm{pr}}}},

(2.15)

M_{H}^{r,s}=\left\langle T_{\chi}^{r}T_{\chi}^{s}\right\rangle_{\chi\in{H^{\mathrm{pr}}}}.

(2.16)

For a subgroup
N\subset(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/Q)^{\times}
, we let
\psi(s;N)
denote the following sum

\psi(s;N)=\sum_{\deg c=r\atop{\mathrm{monic}\atop{c\bmod Q\in N}}}\Lambda(c)

(2.17)

(this is the Chebychev
\psi

).

The following lemma is a slight reformulation of

]

\displaystyle q^{\frac{r+s}{2}}M_{H}^{r,s}

\displaystyle=\left\langle q^{r/2}T_{\chi}^{r}\cdot q^{s/2}T_{\chi}^{s}\right\rangle_{\chi\in{H^{\mathrm{pr}}}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\sum_{\deg h=r}\Lambda(h)\chi(h)+1\right)\left(\sum_{\deg g=s}\Lambda(g)\chi(g)+1\right)\right\rangle_{\chi\in{H^{\mathrm{pr}}}}

\displaystyle=-1-q^{r/2}M_{H}^{r}-q^{s/2}M_{H}^{s}+\sum_{\deg h=r,\deg g=s}\Lambda(h)\Lambda(g)\left\langle\chi(hg)\right\rangle_{\chi\in{H^{\mathrm{pr}}}}.

By (
2.14
) we have

\left\langle\chi(hg)\right\rangle_{\chi\in{H^{\mathrm{pr}}}}=\sum_{Q^{\prime}|Q}\mu(Q/Q^{\prime})\frac{\#H_{Q^{\prime}}}{\#{H^{\mathrm{pr}}}}\left\langle\chi(hg)\right\rangle_{\chi\in H_{Q^{\prime}}}

and by the orthogonality relation (
2.6
) we have
\left\langle\chi(hg)\right\rangle_{\chi\in H_{Q^{\prime}}}=\mathds{1}_{hg\bmod Q\in H_{Q^{\prime}}^{\perp}}
(for any relation
\mathcal{R}
we denote by
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{R}}
its indicator, which takes the value 1 if
\mathcal{R}
holds and 0 otherwise). Plugging this back in and exchanging the order of summation we get

q^{\frac{r+s}{2}}M_{H}^{r,s}=-1-q^{r/2}M_{H}^{r}-q^{s/2}M_{H}^{s}+\sum_{Q^{\prime}|Q}\mu(Q/Q^{\prime})\frac{\#H_{Q^{\prime}}}{\#{H^{\mathrm{pr}}}}\tau(r,s;H_{Q^{\prime}}^{\perp}).

Dividing by
q^{\frac{r+s}{2}}
we get the desired result. Note that the right hand side in the last equation is real. The case of
r\leq 0,s\leq 0
follows because
M_{H}^{r,s}=\overline{M_{H}^{-r,-s}}=M_{H}^{-r,-s}
.
The case
r\geq 0,s<0
is treated similarly, only the expression
\left\langle\chi(hg)\right\rangle_{\chi\in H_{Q^{\prime}}}
is replaced with
\left\langle\chi(h)\overline{\chi(g)}\right\rangle=\left\langle\chi(h/g)\right\rangle
resulting in the stated expression.
∎

3
Counting short vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-lattices

A fundamental tool in all of our estimates of the trace means
M_{H}^{r},M_{H}^{r,s}
(defined in section
2.5
) for the specific subgroups
H
we encounter, will be bounding the number of short vectors in certain
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-lattices. In the present section we summarize the background we will need, mainly the basic theory of reduced bases for
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-lattices developed by Lenstra
[
18
]
, and derive some useful bounds on the number of short vectors in a lattice. Analogous (and even more general) bounds are also known over the integers, see e.g.
[
17
]
*§2,
[
28
]
*§5.

In what follows an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-lattice
\Gamma
of rank
n
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-submodule
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{n}
of finite index. The volume of
\Gamma
is defined to be the index
\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma)=[\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{n}:\Gamma]
.
For a vector
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{n}
,
the degree of
v
is defined by
\deg v=\max(\deg v_{1},\ldots,\deg v_{n})
. We say that
v
is
primitive in
\Gamma
if one cannot write
v=aw,w\in\Gamma,a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with
\deg a\geq 1
.

Theorem 3.1

(Lenstra)
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{n}
be a lattice of rank
n
and volume
\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma)=q^{m}
. Then there exists a basis
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
for
\Gamma
(as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-module) such that
\deg b_{1}+\ldots+\deg b_{n}=m
. For such a basis and any
v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{c_{i}b_{i}},c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
we have
\deg v=\max{\deg{c_{i}b_{i}}}
.

Proof.

The first assertion is proved in
[
18
, Proposition 1.14]
. The second assertion is
[
18
, Proposition 1.1]
.
∎

The next lemma allows us to bound the number of short primitive vectors in a rank 2 lattice.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{2}
be a lattice of rank
2
,
\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma)=q^{m}
,
r\geq 0
a nonnegative integer.
Then the number of primitive
v\in\Gamma
with
\deg v\leq r
is
at most
\max(q,q^{2r-m+2})
.

Proof.

Let
b_{1},b_{2}
be a basis of
\Gamma
as given by Theorem
3.1
and denote
\deg b_{i}=m_{i}
with
m_{1}+m_{2}=m
. Let
v=c_{1}b_{1}+c_{2}b_{2}
be a vector with
\deg v\leq r
. If

r<m_{2}
then since
r\geq\deg(v)=\max(\deg(c_{1}b_{1}),\deg(c_{2}b_{2}))
,
we must have
c_{2}=0
and thus
v=c_{1}b_{1}
. If
\deg c_{1}\geq 1
,

v
is not primitive, so there are at most
q
possible choices of
v
. The same argument applies if
r<m_{1}
. If

r\geq m_{1},m_{2}
then by Theorem
3.1
a vector
v=c_{1}b_{1}+c_{2}b_{2}\in\Gamma
(not necessarily
primitive) of degree at most
r
satisfies
\deg c_{i}\leq r-m_{i}
. Thus the
number of vectors
v\in\Gamma
of degree at most
r
is bounded by
q^{2r-m_{1}-m_{2}+2}=q^{2r-m+2}
.
∎

We will also need a slight generalization:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{2}
be a lattice of rank
2
,
\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma)=q^{m}
,
r,s\geq 0
nonnegative integers.
Then the number of primitive
v=(g,h)\in\Gamma,g,h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with
\deg g\leq r
,
\deg h\leq s
is
at most
\max(q,q^{r+s-m+2})
.

Proof.

Assume by way of symmetry that
r\geq s
. Consider the modified lattice

\Gamma^{\prime}=\{(g,hx^{r-s}):(g,h)\in\Gamma\}.

We have
\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma)=q^{m+r-s}
and applying Lemma
3.2
to
\Gamma^{\prime}
we see that there are at most
\max(q,q^{r+s-m+2})
primitive vectors
(g,h)
in
\Gamma
with
\deg((g,hx^{r-s}))\leq r
, which is equivalent to the assertion.∎

Lemma 3.4
.

(i)

Let
0\neq Q\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a polynomial and let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a polynomial coprime with
Q
. Then

\Gamma=\{(g,h)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{2}:h\equiv ag\pmod{Q}\}

is a lattice of volume
q^{\deg Q}
.

(ii)

Let
r,s\geq 0
be integers such that
r+s\geq\deg Q-1
. Then there exists a solution
(g,h)
to the congruence
h\equiv ag\pmod{Q}
with
(h,g)\not\equiv(0,0)\pmod{Q}
and
\deg g\leq r,\deg h\leq s
.

Proof.

(i)
It is easy to see that
\Gamma
is spanned by the vectors
(1,a),(0,Q)
. It is shown in
[
18
, §1]
that the volume the lattice in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{n}
spanned by the rows of a matrix
A\in\mathrm{M}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x])
is given by
q^{\deg(\det A)}
(provided
\det A\neq 0
), which equals
q^{\deg Q}
in our case.

(ii)
Denote
V=\{(g,h)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x],\deg g\leq r,\deg h\leq s\}
. This is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space of dimension
r+s+2
. The map
V\to\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/Q:(g,h)\mapsto h-ag\bmod Q
cannot be injective (since its range has smaller dimension than its domain) and so has a nonzero vector in its kernel, giving the desired solution.
∎

The following bound will be used in section
6
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{n}
be a lattice with volume
\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma)=q^{m}
and denote
\mu=\min_{0\neq v\in\Gamma}\deg v
. Then for any real
s\geq 0
,

\#\{v\in\Gamma:\deg v\leq s\}\leq q^{\max(0,n(s+1)-m,(n-1)(s+1-\mu))}.

Proof.

Let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
be a basis for
\Gamma
as in Theorem
3.1
, so each
v\in\Gamma
can be written uniquely as
v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}b_{i}
with
\deg v=\max\deg c_{i}b_{i}
. First assume that
\max\deg b_{i}\leq s
. Then for
v
as above with
\deg v\leq s
we must have
\deg c_{i}\leq s-\deg b_{i}
and we have
q^{s-\deg b_{i}+1}
possible values for
c_{i}
and
q^{n(s+1)-\sum b_{i}}=q^{n(s+1)-m}
possible values of
v
in total.

On the other hand if
\deg b_{i}>s
for some
i
, say
\deg b_{n}>s
, then for
v
as above with
\deg v\leq s
we must have
c_{n}=0
and
\deg c_{i}\leq s-\deg b_{i}\leq s-\mu
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. It follows that there are
\max(1,q^{(n-1)(s+1-\mu)})
possible values of
v
.
∎

4
2-level density for the polynomial A-S family

In the present section we prove Theorem
1.1
.
Throughout the present section
p>2
is prime,
q
is a power of
p
,
\psi:\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
a nontrivial additive character. Let
d
be a natural number with
(d,p)=1
. Until section
4.3
the asymptotic
O(\cdot)
notation will always have an absolute implicit constant (independent of
q,d
).

Let
L(u,f,\psi)
be an A-S
L
-function (not necessarily polynomial) with factorization

L(u,f,\psi)=\prod_{j=1}^{2\mathfrak{g}/(p-1)}(1-q^{-1/2}\rho_{j}u),\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}(C_{f}).

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}
define the
r
-th trace
of
L(u,f,\psi)
to be

T_{f}^{r}=\sum_{j=1}^{2\mathfrak{g}/(p-1)}\rho_{j}^{r}

(4.1)

(
\psi
is implicit in the notation).
Let
\mathcal{F}_{d}\subset\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{0}
be the subfamily defined by (
2.2
). We will prove Theorem
1.1
through estimating the trace-product averages

M_{d}^{r,s}=\left\langle T_{f}^{r}T_{f}^{s}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{d}}.

An estimate of
M_{d}^{r,s}
was given in
[
13
]
*Proposition 6.3, but only in the range
|r|+|s|<d
. In the present section we give an estimate which is nontrivial in the wider range
|r|+|s|<(2-2/p)d
. This will allow us to obtain the improved range in Theorem
1.1
.

By Proposition
2.2
we have
M_{d}^{r,s}=M_{H}^{r,s}
, where

H=\left\{\chi\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/x^{d+1}\right)^{\times*}:\chi^{p}=1\right\}

and
M_{H}^{r,s}
is given by (
2.16
).
By Lemma
2.12
and the fact that
\#H_{x^{d+1}}/\#{H^{\mathrm{pr}}}=\frac{q}{q-1}
,
\#H_{x^{d}}/\#{H^{\mathrm{pr}}}=\frac{1}{q-1}
(here we use
(d,p)=1
) we have

M_{H}^{r,s}=-q^{-\frac{|r|+|s|}{2}}-q^{-|s|/2}M_{H}^{r}-q^{-|r|/2}M_{H}^{s}+q^{-\frac{|r|+|s|}{2}}\bigg[\frac{q}{q-1}\tau(r,s;H_{x^{d+1}}^{\perp})-\frac{1}{q-1}\tau(r,s;H_{x^{d}}^{\perp})\bigg].

(4.2)

For the rest of this section we let
Q
denote
x^{d}
or
x^{d+1}
. We need to describe the orthogonal group to
H_{Q}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

A polynomial
u(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
,
u(0)\neq 0
satisfies
u\bmod Q\in H_{Q}^{\perp}

if and only if
u\equiv\varphi(x^{p})\pmod{Q}
for some
\varphi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with
\varphi(0)\neq 0,\deg\varphi\leq d/p
.

Proof.

The group
H_{Q}
consists of all degree
p
characters of
\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/Q\right)^{\times}
and therefore
H_{Q}^{\perp}=\left(\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/Q\right)^{\times}\right)^{p}
. Since
Q
is
x^{d}
or
x^{d+1}
and the
p
-th powers in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
are exactly
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x^{p}]
. Noting that
(d,p)=1
the assertion follows.∎

We will need the estimate of

M_{d}^{r}=\langle T_{f}^{r}\rangle_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{d}}=M_{H}^{r}

given in
[
13
]
*Theorem 1:

Proposition 4.2
.

M_{d}^{r}=O\left(q^{(1/p-1/2)r}+rq^{r/2-(1-1/p)d}\right)
(for
r>0
).

As a corollary we obtain for
r,s\neq 0
(by (
4.2
) and the fact that
M_{H}^{r}=M_{H}^{-r}
which follows from Lemma
2.11
):

M_{H}^{r,s}=O\left(q^{\frac{\max(|r|,|s|)}{p}-\frac{|r|+|s|}{2}}+\max(|r|,|s|)q^{\left||r|-|s|\right|/2-d(1-1/p)}\right)+\\
+q^{-\frac{|r|+|s|}{2}}\bigg[\frac{q}{q-1}\tau(r,s;H_{x^{d+1}}^{\perp})-\frac{1}{q-1}\tau(r,s;H_{x^{d}}^{\perp})\bigg].

(4.3)

Thus, to estimate
M_{H}^{r,s}
we must estimate the sums
\tau(r,s;H_{Q}^{\perp})
. There are two fundamentally different cases: the
diagonal case

r=-s
and the
off-diagonal case

r\neq-s
.

4.1
Estimating the diagonal terms
M_{d}^{r,-r}

In the present subsection we prove the following estimate for
r>0
:

M_{d}^{r,-r}=M_{H}^{r,-r}=r+O(r^{2}q^{r-(1-1/p)d+3}+rq^{-r/2}).

(4.4)

Note that for any fixed
\epsilon>0
and
\epsilon d<r<(1-1/p-\epsilon)d
the error term is small compared with the main term.

Recall that

\tau(r,-r;H_{Q}^{\perp})=\sum_{\deg g=\deg h=r\atop{(gh,x)=1\atop{gh^{-1}\bmod Q\in H_{Q}^{\perp}}}}\Lambda(g)\Lambda(h),

the sum being over monic
g,h
.
We separate the pairs
(g,h)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{2}
for which the residue
gh^{-1}\pmod{Q}
is in
H_{Q}^{\perp}
into two types: first there are pairs for which
g=h
(diagonal pairs), and then there are pairs with
g\neq h
(off-diagonal pairs). The contribution of diagonal pairs is easy to estimate using the Prime Polynomial Theorem (see below).

The key observation for bounding the contribution of the off-diagonal pairs
g\neq h
is that such pairs
(g,h)
are primitive vectors of degree
r
in a lattice of the form

\Gamma_{\varphi}=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{2}:b(x)\equiv\varphi(x^{p})a(x)\pmod{Q}\},

(4.5)

with
\varphi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x],\deg\varphi\leq d/p
.
Indeed, since we have
hg^{-1}\bmod{Q}\in H_{Q}^{\perp}
, by Lemma
4.1
we have a congruence
hg^{-1}\equiv\varphi(x^{p})\pmod{Q}
for some
\varphi
coprime with
x
with
\deg\varphi\leq d/p
. Since
g,h
are prime powers of the same degree,
g\neq h
implies
(g,h)=1
, i.e. the vector
(g,h)
is primitive in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]^{2}
and
a fortiori
in
\Gamma_{\varphi}
.

Denoting the contribution of off-diagonal pairs by

E(r)=\sum_{\deg g=\deg h=r\atop{(gh,x)=1\atop{gh^{-1}\bmod Q\in H_{Q}^{\perp}}}}\Lambda(g)\Lambda(h)-\sum_{\deg g=r\atop{g\neq x^{r}}}\Lambda(g)^{2}.

We see that

E(r)=O(r^{2})\cdot\#\{\text{Primitive vectors }(u,v)\text{ in }\Gamma_{\varphi}\text{ of degree }r\text{, for some }\varphi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x],\deg\varphi\leq d/p\}.

Fix
\varphi
with
(\varphi,x)=1,\deg\varphi\leq d/p
. By Lemma
3.4
, the volume of the lattice
\Gamma_{\varphi}
is
q^{\deg Q}
, which is
q^{d}
or
q^{d+1}
. By Lemma
3.2
the number of primitive vectors in
\Gamma_{\varphi}
of degree
r
is at most
O(q^{2r-d+2})
. Since there are
O(q^{d/p+1})
possible values of
\varphi
, we have
E(r)=O(r^{2}q^{2r-d(1-1/p)+3})
. Thus,

\tau(r,-r;H_{Q}^{\perp})=\sum_{\deg g=r}\Lambda(g)^{2}+O(r^{2}q^{2r-d(1-1/p)+3}).

(4.6)

By the Prime Polynomial Theorem (see e.g.
[
26
]
*Theorem 2.2) we have

\sum_{\deg g=r}\Lambda(g)^{2}=rq^{r}+O(rq^{r/2}),

and therefore

\tau(r,-r;H_{Q}^{\perp})=rq^{r}+O(rq^{r/2}+r^{2}q^{2r-(1-1/p)d+3}).

Recalling (
4.3
) we finally obtain

\displaystyle M_{H}^{r,-r}

\displaystyle=O(q^{-(1-1/p)r}+rq^{-(1-1/p)d})+

\displaystyle+q^{-r}\bigg[\frac{q}{q-1}rq^{r}-\frac{1}{q-1}rq^{r}+O(rq^{r/2}+r^{2}q^{2r-(1-1/p)d+3})\bigg]

\displaystyle=r+O(rq^{-r/2}+r^{2}q^{r-(1-1/p)d+3}),

which is exactly (
4.4
).

4.2
Bounding the off-diagonal terms
M_{d}^{r,s},r\neq-s

In the present subsection we prove the following estimates, valid for
r,s\neq 0,r\neq-s
:

M_{d}^{r,s}=M_{H}^{r,s}=O\left(|rs|q^{(1/p-1/2)(|r|+|s|)}+|rs|q^{\frac{|r|+|s|}{2}-d(1-1/p)+3}\right).

(4.7)

Since
M_{d}^{r,s}=M_{d}^{s,r}=M_{d}^{-r,-s}
we may assume without loss of generality that
r>0,|r|\geq|s|
.

The case of
M_{d}^{r,-s}
,
r>s>0,r\neq s
.
Lemma
2.12
gives:

M_{H}^{r,-s}=-q^{-\frac{r+s}{2}}-q^{-s/2}M_{H}^{r}-q^{-r/2}M_{H}^{s}+q^{-\frac{r+s}{2}}\bigg[\frac{q}{q-1}\tau(r,-s;H_{x^{d+1}}^{\perp})-\frac{1}{q-1}\tau(r,-s;H_{x^{d}}^{\perp})\bigg].

(4.8)

By Proposition
4.2
we have

M_{H}^{r}=M_{d}^{r}=O(q^{(1/p-1/2)r}+rq^{r/2-d(1-1/p)}),

(4.9)

so it remains to bound the contribution of the sums
\tau(r,-s;H_{Q}^{\perp})
for
Q=x^{d},x^{d+1}
.
By (
2.18
) and since
Q
is a power of
x
we have

\tau(r,-s;H_{Q}^{\perp})=\sum_{\deg h=r,\deg g=s\atop{(gh,x)=1\atop hg^{-1}\bmod Q\in H_{Q}^{\perp}}}\Lambda(h)\Lambda(g).

We treat coprime pairs with
\gcd(h,g)=1
and non-coprime pairs
\gcd(h,g)\neq 1
separately.

Coprime pairs
\gcd(g,h)=1
.
Let
g,h
be a coprime pair as above, that is
\deg g=r,\deg h=s,gh^{-1}\bmod Q\in H_{Q}^{\perp},\gcd(g,h)=1
. By Lemma
4.1
, for every such pair there is a polynomial
\varphi
with
(\varphi,x)=1,\deg\varphi\leq d/p
such that

h(x)\equiv g(x)\varphi(x^{p})\pmod{Q},

(4.10)

and therefore
(g,h)
is a primitive vector in the lattice
\Gamma_{\varphi}
(defined in (
4.5
)) with
\deg g=r,\deg h=s
.
There are at most
q^{d/p+1}
possible values of
\varphi
and by Lemma
3.3
and the fact that
\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma_{\varphi})=q^{\deg Q}\geq q^{d}
, for a given
\varphi
there are at most
O(q^{r+s-d+2})
possibilities for
(g,h)
.
In total there are
O\left(q^{s+r-d+d/p+3}\right)
possibilities for the triple
\varphi,g,h
and
hence also for the number of coprime pairs
g,h
as above. The total contribution of such pairs to

\tau(r,-s,H_{Q}^{\perp})
is therefore

O\left(rsq^{{s+r}-(1-1/p)d+3}\right).

Non-coprime pairs
\gcd(g,h)\neq 1
.
It is left to bound the contribution of the non-coprime pairs
(g,h)
to
\tau(r,-s;H_{Q}^{\perp})
. Such pairs are of the form
g,h
which are a power of a common prime
x\neq P\in\mathcal{P}
. Write
g=P^{r/\deg P},h=P^{s/\deg P}
. The condition
gh^{-1}\bmod{Q}\in H_{Q}^{\perp}
is equivalent to

P^{(r-s)/\deg P}\bmod{Q}\in H_{Q}^{\perp}
. Since
H_{Q}^{\perp}=\left((\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/Q)^{\times}\right)^{p}
this condition is automatic if
p|(r-s)/\deg P
and is equivalent to
P\bmod Q
being a
p
-th power if
p\nmid(r-s)/\deg P

If
p|(r-s)/\deg P
then
\deg P\leq(r-s)/p
and there are
O(q^{\frac{r-s}{p}})
suitable
P
, contributing a total of
O(s^{2}q^{\frac{r-s}{p}})
to the sum
\tau(r,-s,H_{Q}^{\perp})
.
On the other hand if
p\nmid(r-s)/\deg P
we must have
P\equiv\varphi(x)^{p}\pmod{Q}
, for some
\varphi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
of degree
\leq d/p
(since
P
is prime this is only possible if
\deg P\geq d
). There are
O(q^{d/p+1})
possible values of
\varphi
and having
\varphi
fixed,
P
has at most

q^{s-d}
possible values (note that
\deg P\leq s
since
h
is a power of
P
). Overall, the number of
possible
P
is at most
O(q^{s-(1-1/p)d+1})
and their contribution to
\tau(s,-r,H_{Q}^{\perp})
is
O(s^{2}q^{s-(1-1/p)d+1})
.

We conclude that

\tau(r,-s;H_{Q}^{\perp})=O\left(rsq^{s+r-d(1-1/p)+3}+s^{2}q^{(r-s)/p}\right).

Overall we get, using (
4.8
) and (
4.9
),

\displaystyle M_{H}^{r,-s}=O\left(q^{\frac{r}{p}-\frac{r+s}{2}}+rsq^{\frac{s+r}{2}-(1-\frac{1}{p})d+3}+s^{2}q^{\frac{r-s}{p}-\frac{r+s}{2}}\right),

confirming (
4.7
) in this case.

The case
M_{d}^{r,s}
,
r\geq s>0
.
Recall that

\tau(r,s;H_{Q}^{\perp})=\sum_{\deg g=r,\deg h=s\atop{gh\bmod Q\in H_{Q}^{\perp}}}\Lambda(g)\Lambda(h).

Once again we separate the contribution of coprime pairs (that is, pairs with

Coprime pairs

We start with the case of coprime pairs. By Lemma

\varphi(0)\neq 0

There are at most

is a

\tau(r,s;H_{Q}^{\perp})
.

Non-coprime pairs
\gcd(g,h)\neq 1
.
If
(g,h)
is a non-coprime pair then
g,h
are powers of a common prime

and are uniquely determined by the product

then

with
\varphi
monic and there are
O(q^{(r+s)/p})

O(q^{d/p+1})

Overall we obtain

and therefore using (

) and (

h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]

\displaystyle\#\{c

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x^{2}]\text{ is irreducible},\deg c=r\}=\#\{c(x^{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x^{2}]:c\text{ is inert,}\deg c=r/2\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{q^{r/2}}{r/2}+O(q^{r/4}/r)=\frac{q^{r/2}}{r}+O(q^{r/4}/r),

and the proposition follows (the contribution of prime powers to
\psi(r;A)
is also
O(q^{r/4})
).
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Combine Proposition
5.3
and Proposition
5.4
(and recall that
p\geq 3
).
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Combine (
5.1
) with Proposition
5.2
.

∎

5.2
1-level density for the odd polynomial family

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
be a fixed (Schwartz) test function with
\mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\subset(-1+1/p,1-1/p)
. For the remainder of the present section, the constants implicit in the asymptotic
O
-notation may depend on the test function
\Phi
, but no other parameter. In other words
O(\cdot)=O_{\Phi}(\cdot)
. We keep the notation
M_{d}^{r}=\langle T_{f}^{r}\rangle_{f\in\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{0,\mathrm{odd}}}
used throughout the present section. Recall that for
f\in\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{0,\mathrm{odd}}
we have
\mathfrak{g}(C_{f})=(p-1)(d-1)/2
and

has
d-1
zeros. We denote by
\phi_{d-1}
the periodic sampling function associated with

, given by (
1.4
).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

First we show that

\left\langle W_{1}(f,\psi,\Phi)\right\rangle_{f\in\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{0,\mathrm{odd}}}=\hat{\Phi}(0)+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\left(\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{r}{d-1}\right)+\hat{\Phi}\left(-\frac{r}{d-1}\right)\right)\frac{M_{d}^{r}}{d-1}.

(5.12)

Let
f\in\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{0,\mathrm{odd}}
and
\rho_{j}=e^{2\pi i\theta_{j}}
the normalized inverse zeros of
L(u,f,\psi)
. By Fourier expansion we have

\phi_{d-1}(\theta_{j})=\sum_{r=-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{\phi}_{d-1}(r)e^{2\pi ir\theta_{j}}=\sum_{r=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{d-1}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{r}{d-1}\right)e^{2\pi ir\theta_{j}},

since
\hat{\phi}_{d-1}(r)=\frac{1}{d-1}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{r}{d-1}\right)
.
Hence

W_{1}(f,\psi,\Phi)=\sum_{r=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{d-1}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{r}{d-1}\right)T_{f}^{r}.

Averaging over
\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{0,\mathrm{odd}}
and noting that (by Lemma
2.4
)
M_{d}^{r}=\overline{M_{d}^{r}}=M_{d}^{-r}

we get (
5.12
). Let
\delta>0
be such that
\mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\subset(-1+1/p+\delta,1-1/p-\delta)
. Then by (
5.12
) and Proposition
5.1
we have for
d\geq d_{0}(\delta)
large enough

\left\langle W_{1}(f,\psi,\Phi)\right\rangle_{f\in\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{0,\mathrm{odd}}}=\hat{\Phi}(0)+\sum_{r=1}^{(1-1/p-\delta)d}\left(\hat{\Phi}\left(-\frac{r}{d-1}\right)+\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{r}{d-1}\right)\right)\frac{M_{d}^{r}}{d-1}\\
=\hat{\Phi}(0)+\frac{1}{d-1}\cdot\sum_{r=1}^{(1-1/p-\delta)d}\left(\hat{\Phi}\left(-\frac{r}{d-1}\right)+\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{r}{d-1}\right)\right)\left(-\mathds{1}_{2|r}+O\left(rq^{-r/6+3\cdot\mathds{1}_{r>d/4-1}}+rq^{-\delta d/2+3}\right)\right)\\
=\hat{\Phi}(0)-\sum_{r=1}^{(1-1/p-\delta)d}\frac{\mathds{1}_{2|r}}{d-1}\left(\hat{\Phi}\left(-\frac{r}{d-1}\right)+\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{r}{d-1}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{d}\right).

Note that on the other hand for
1\leq r\leq d-1
,

\int_{U\in\mathrm{USp}(d-1)}\mathrm{tr}(U^{r})\mathrm{d}U=-\mathds{1}_{2|r}

(see
[
10
]
*Theorem 6) and a similar calculation to (
5.12
) shows

\left\langle W_{1}(U,\Phi)\right\rangle_{U\in\mathrm{USp}(d-1)}=\sum_{r=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{d-1}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{r}{d}\right)\int_{\mathrm{USp}(d-1)}\mathrm{tr}(U^{r})\mathrm{d}U\\
=\hat{\Phi}(0)-\sum_{r=1}^{(1-1/p-\delta)d}\frac{\mathds{1}_{2|r}}{d-1}\left(\hat{\Phi}\left(-\frac{r}{d-1}\right)+\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{r}{d-1}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{d}\right)=\left\langle W_{1}(f,\psi,\Phi)\right\rangle_{f\in\mathcal{AS}_{d}^{0,\mathrm{odd}}}+O\left(\frac{1}{d}\right).$$

Taking

and using (
1.7
) we obtain Theorem
1.2
.
∎

6
The ordinary A-S family

In the present section we prove Theorem
1.3
. Throughout the section we use the notation from sections
2.4
and
2.5
.

Let
d
be a natural number,
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
a monic squarefree polynomial of degree
d
or
d-1
. For each of the families
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{AS}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{d,g},\mathcal{H}_{g}
(
\mathcal{H}_{g}
is defined by (
2.10
)) and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
we denote the trace average

M^{r}_{\mathcal{F}}=\left\langle T^{r}_{f}\right\rangle_{f\in\mathcal{F}},

where
T^{r}_{f}=\sum_{i=1}^{2d-2}\rho_{i}(f,\psi)^{r}
,
\rho_{i}(f,\psi)
being the normalized inverse zeros of
L(u,f,\psi)
. Our strategy for estimating
M^{r}_{\mathcal{AS}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{d,g}}
will be by reducing the problem to estimating
M^{r}_{\mathcal{H}_{g}}
(this will only work directly for

and slight modifications to our arguments will be necessary for the case

). Theorem
1.3
will be derived from these estimates.

6.1
Estimating means of traces for the family
\mathcal{H}_{g}

Assume
g
is squarefree with
\deg g=d
and denote by

H=\left\{\chi\in(F_{q}[x]/g^{2})^{\times*}:\chi^{p}=1\right\}

the group of order
p
Dirichlet characters modulo
g^{2}
.
By Proposition
2.8
combined with Proposition
2.10
we have (using the notation (
2.15
))
M^{r}_{\mathcal{H}_{g}}=M^{r}_{H}
.

We recall from section
2.5
that for a group of Dirichlet characters
H
modulo
g^{2}
, and
Q|g^{2}
a monic divisor, we denote by
H_{Q}\subset H
the subgroup of Dirichlet characters in
H
with period
Q
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
r
be a positive integer. Then for
r>0
,

M^{r}_{\mathcal{H}_{g}}=M_{H}^{r}=-q^{-r/2}\left(1+\sum_{Q|g}\mu(g/Q)\frac{q^{\deg Q}}{\varphi(g)}\psi\left(r;(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/gQ)^{\times p}\right)\right),

where
\varphi(g)=\#(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/g)^{\times}
is the Euler totient function and
\psi(r;*)
is defined by (
2.17
).

Proof.

Since
g
is squarefree, by Lemma
2.11
applied to
H
we have (the terms with
\mu(g^{2}/Q^{\prime})\neq 0
have the form
Q^{\prime}=gQ
with
Q|g
)

M_{H}^{r}=-q^{-r/2}\left(1+\sum_{Q^{\prime}|g^{2}}\mu(g^{2}/Q^{\prime})\frac{\#H_{Q^{\prime}}}{\#{H^{\mathrm{pr}}}}\psi(r;H_{Q^{\prime}}^{\perp})\right)=-q^{-r/2}\left(1+\sum_{Q|g}\mu(g/Q)\frac{\#H_{gQ}}{\#{H^{\mathrm{pr}}}}\psi\left(r;H_{gQ}^{\perp}\right)\right),

Now the proposition follows from the elementary facts (see proof of Proposition
2.10
)
\#H^{\mathrm{pr}}=\varphi(g),\#H_{gQ}=q^{\deg Q}
and

H_{gQ}^{\perp}=\left\{F\bmod g^{2}:F\bmod gQ\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/gQ)^{\times p}\right\}

(note that
(F,g^{2})=1
iff
(F,gQ)=1
).
∎

Hence we turn our attention to bounding
\psi\left(r;(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/gQ)^{\times p}\right)
for
Q|g
. The key observation is that if
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is a
p
-th power modulo
gQ
then writing
c=u^{p}+UQg
and taking derivatives we get that
c^{\prime}=U^{\prime}Qg+UQ^{\prime}g+UQg^{\prime}
is divisible by
Q
(since
Q|g
). This will allow us to give a good bound on the number of suitable
c
of given degree. Our method crucially relies on this special property of the group
(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/g^{2})^{\times p}
and we would not be able to obtain a comparable result for a general group of Dirichlet characters of similar size in terms of their (common) modulus. The next lemma contains the key estimate:

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
Q
be a squarefree polynomial. We have

\#\{c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]:\deg c\leq r,Q|c^{\prime}\}\leq\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}q^{\frac{r}{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)(r-2\deg Q)+2p},&\deg Q<r<2\deg Q,\\
q^{\frac{r}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(r-2\deg Q)+2p},&r\geq 2\deg Q.\end{array}\right.

The proof of the lemma (to be given below) is based on viewing the set
\{c^{\prime}:c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\}\cap Q\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
as a lattice over the ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x^{p}]
and bounding the number of short vectors in this lattice. Denote
R=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x^{p}]
. Consider the (free)
R
-module

V=\{c^{\prime}:c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\}=R\oplus xR\oplus x^{2}R\oplus\ldots\oplus x^{p-2}R\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}[x].

For a vector
v=\sum_{i=0}^{p-2}a_{i}(x^{p})x^{i}\in V
we define its degree to be
\deg_{R}{v}=\max(\deg a_{i})
. We will denote by
\deg v=\deg_{x}v
the degree of
v
as a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. Note that we always have the inequalities

\frac{\deg_{x}v}{p}-1<\deg_{R}(v)\leq\frac{\deg_{x}v}{p}.

(6.1)

For a squarefree polynomial
Q
consider the
R
-lattice
\Lambda_{Q}=Q\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\cap V
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Assume
Q
is a squarefree polynomial. As an
R
-lattice in
V
,
\Lambda_{Q}
has volume
\mathrm{vol}(\Lambda_{Q})=q^{\deg{Q}}
.

Proof.

We want to calculate
[V:\Lambda_{Q}]
. By the second isomorphism theorem for
R
-modules,

V/\Lambda_{Q}\cong(V+Q\mathbb{F}_{q}[x])/Q\mathbb{F}_{q}[x].

We show that
V+Q\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and it would follow that
[V:\Lambda_{Q}]=q^{\deg{Q}}
.

Write

Q=a_{0}(x^{p})+xa_{1}(x^{p})+\ldots+x^{p-1}a_{p-1}(x^{p}),a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x].

(6.2)

Fix
0\leq i\leq p-1
. Multiplying (
6.2
) by
x^{p-1-i}
and subtracting polynomials from
V
we get that the polynomial
a_{i}(x^{p})x^{p-1}
is in
V+Q\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. Now we note that
\gcd(a_{0},\ldots,a_{p-1})=1
, since otherwise
Q
is not squarefree. Consequently there are
c_{0},\ldots,c_{p-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
such that
\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}c_{i}a_{i}=1
. Hence

x^{p-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}c_{i}(x^{p})a_{i}(x^{p})x^{p-1}\in V+Q\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]

(since the latter is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x^{p}]
-module).
Now since
1,x,\ldots,x^{p-2}\in V
, we have that
1,x,\ldots,x^{p-1}\in V+Q\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. But this is a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
as an
R
-module and we have
V+Q\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
as needed.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

For
Q
squarefree,

\#\{v\in\Lambda_{Q}:\deg_{x}v\leq r-1\}\leq\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}q^{\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)r-\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)(\deg Q)+2p-1},&\deg Q<r<2\deg Q,\\
q^{\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)r-\deg Q+2p-1},&r\geq 2\deg Q.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

First note that for any
0\neq v\in\Lambda_{Q}
we have
\deg_{R}(v)>\frac{\deg v}{p}-1\geq\frac{\deg Q}{p}-1
(from (
6.1
)). Now
applying Lemma
3.5
to
\Lambda_{Q}
(with
n=p-1
,
s=(r-1)/p
,
\mu=\min_{0\neq v\in\Lambda_{Q}}\deg_{R}v>\frac{\deg Q}{p}-1
) we obtain (once again using (
6.1
))

\#\{v\in\Lambda_{Q}:\deg_{x}v\leq r-1\}\leq\#\{v\in\Lambda_{Q}:\deg_{R}v\leq(r-1)/p\}\leq\\
\leq q^{\max\left(0,(p-1)(\frac{r}{p}+1)-\deg Q,(p-2)(\frac{r}{p}+2-\frac{\deg Q}{p})\right)}\leq q^{\max\left(0,\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)r-\deg Q+2p-1,\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)r-\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)(\deg Q)+2p-1\right)},

which implies the assertion.
∎

Proof of Lemma
6.2
.

We note that the conditions
\deg c\leq r,Q|c^{\prime}
are equivalent to
c^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{Q}
. Since the kernel of the map
c\mapsto c^{\prime}
consists of the polynomials lying in
R=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x^{p}]
, any fixed value of
c^{\prime}
with
\deg c^{\prime}\leq r-1
corresponds to at most
q^{r/p+1}
values of
c
. Combining this observation with Lemma
6.4
gives Lemma
6.2
.∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
g
be squarefree,
Q|g
. Then

\psi\left(r;(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/gQ)^{\times p}\right)\leq\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}q^{r/p},&r\leq\deg Q,\\
rq^{\frac{r}{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)(r-2\deg Q)+\min(2p,r/p)},&\deg Q<r<2\deg Q,\\
rq^{\frac{r}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(r-2\deg Q)+\min(2p,r/p)},&r\geq 2\deg Q.\end{array}\right.

(6.3)

Proof.

Since
Q|g
, we have
\psi\left(r;(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/gQ)^{\times p}\right)\leq\psi\left(r;(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/Q^{2})^{\times p}\right)
, so it is enough to bound the latter by the right hand side of (
6.3
). Let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a prime power with
\deg c=r
such that
c\bmod Q^{2}
is a
p
-th power, i.e.
c\equiv u^{p}\pmod{Q^{2}}
. Writing explicitly
c-u^{p}=UQ^{2}
for some
U\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and taking derivatives, we get

c^{\prime}=U^{\prime}Q^{2}+2UQQ^{\prime}=Q(QU^{\prime}+2Q^{\prime}U)
, hence
Q|c^{\prime}
.

First we assume
r>\deg Q
. If
2p<r/p
we need to obtain the bounds with
2p
in place of the minima in the exponents. Applying Lemma
6.2
and using
\Lambda(c)\leq r
we obtain the required bound. If
2p>r/p
then we can replace Lemma
6.2
with the easier bound

\#\{c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]:c\mbox{ monic},\deg c=r,Q|c^{\prime}\}\leq q^{r-\deg Q+r/p}

which follows by noting that there are at most
q^{r-\deg Q}
possible values of
c^{\prime}
(since
\deg c^{\prime}\leq r-1,Q|c^{\prime}
and we are assuming
\deg Q>r
) and for each of them there are at most
q^{r/p+1}
possible values of
c
. The requirement that
c
is monic reduces the total exponent by 1. From this point we argue as in the case
2p<r/p
.

Finally if
r\leq\deg Q
, since
Q|c^{\prime}
and
\deg c^{\prime}<r=\deg Q
we must have
c^{\prime}=0
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x^{p}]
can be written as
c=b^{p}
with
\Lambda(c)=\Lambda(b)
. In this case we have (by the Prime Polynomial Theorem)

\psi\left(r;(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/Q^{2})^{\times p}\right)\leq\sum_{\deg b=r/p}\Lambda(b)=\mathds{1}_{p|r}\cdot\psi(r/p)\leq q^{r/p}

as required.

∎

We are now ready to derive our main bound on
M_{\mathcal{H}_{g}}^{r}
, which applies in the range
r\leq 2d
.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
g
be a squarefree polynomial of degree
d
and
1\leq r\leq 2d
. Then

M_{\mathcal{H}_{g}}^{r}=O\left(\frac{q^{2d}}{\varphi(g)^{2}}q^{(1/p-1/2)r}+\frac{r\tau(g)q^{d}}{\varphi(g)}q^{(1-1/p)(r-2d)+\min(2p,r/p)}\right),

where
\varphi(g)=\#(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/g)^{\times}
is the Euler totient function and
\tau(g)
is the number of monic divisors of
g
.

Remark 6.7
.

We will see below that
\frac{q^{d}}{\varphi(g)}=O_{\epsilon}(d^{\epsilon})
and
\tau(g)=O_{\epsilon}(q^{\epsilon d})
for any
\epsilon>0
, so these factors will not affect our calculation of (the limit of) 1-level density.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.1
we have

|M_{\mathcal{H}_{g}}^{r}|\leq q^{-r/2}\sum_{Q|g}\frac{q^{\deg Q}}{\varphi(g)}\psi\left(r;(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/gQ)^{\times p}\right)+O(q^{-r/2}).

We can now separately bound the contribution from the different ranges appearing in Lemma

6.5
. We factor
g=P_{1}\ldots P_{l}
, where
P_{i}
are distinct primes.

The contribution of
the range
r\leq\deg Q
is bounded by (Lemma
6.5
)

\frac{q^{d}}{\varphi(g)}q^{(1/p-1/2)r}\sum_{Q|g}q^{-\deg(g/Q)}=\frac{q^{d}}{\phi(g)}q^{(1/p-1/2)r}\sum_{Q|g}q^{-\deg Q}=\frac{q^{d}}{\varphi(g)}q^{(1/p-1/2)r}\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left(1+q^{-\deg P_{i}}\right)\\
\leq\frac{q^{2d}}{\varphi(g)^{2}}q^{(1/p-1/2)r},

since

\varphi(g)\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left(1+q^{-\deg P_{i}}\right)=q^{d}\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left(1-q^{-\deg P_{i}}\right)\left(1+q^{\deg P_{i}}\right)\leq q^{d}.

The contribution of the range
\deg Q<r<2\deg Q
is bounded by (Lemma
6.5
and
\deg Q\leq d
)

\frac{q^{d}}{\varphi(g)}\sum_{Q|g}q^{\deg Q-d}rq^{(1/2-1/p)(r-2\deg Q)+\min(2p,r/p)}\\
\leq\frac{rq^{d}}{\varphi(g)}\sum_{Q|g}q^{(1/2-1/p)(r-2d)+\min(2p,r/p)}\leq\frac{r\tau(g)q^{d}}{\varphi(g)}q^{(1/2-1/p)(r-2d)+\min(2p,r/p)}.

Similarly, the contribution of
r\geq 2\deg Q
is bounded by (Lemma
6.5
and recall that
r\leq 2d
)

\frac{r\tau(g)q^{d}}{\varphi(g)}q^{\frac{1}{2}(r-2d)+\min(2p,r/p)}\leq\frac{r\tau(g)q^{d}}{\varphi(g)}q^{(1/2-1/p)(r-2d)+\min(2p,r/p)}.

Combining the above three bounds we obtain the stated bound.
∎

6.2
Means of traces for the family
\mathcal{AS}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{d,g},\deg g=d

Throughout the present subsection
d
is a natural number and
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is a monic squarefree polynomial with
\deg g=d
. Recall that

\mathcal{AS}_{d,g}^{\mathrm{ord}}=\{f=h/g:h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x],\deg h=d,(h,g)=1\},

\mathcal{H}_{g}=\{f=h/g:h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x],\deg h<d,(h,g)=1\}.

The family
\mathcal{AS}_{d,g}^{\mathrm{ord}}
decomposes as follows:

\mathcal{AS}_{d,g}^{\mathrm{ord}}=\bigsqcup_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\{f+b:f\in\mathcal{H}_{g}\}

(6.4)

(disjoint union).

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
g
be a squarefree polynomial of degree
d
and
1\leq r<2d
.

M^{r}_{\mathcal{AS}_{d,g}^{\mathrm{ord}}}=O\left(\frac{q^{2d}}{\varphi(g)^{2}}q^{(1/p-1/2)r}+\frac{r\tau(g)q^{d}}{\varphi(g)}q^{(1-1/p)(r-2d)+\min(2p,r/p)}\right),

with
\varphi,\tau
as in Proposition
6.6
.

Proof.

By (
6.4
) and Lemma
2.1
,

M^{r}_{\mathcal{AS}_{d,g}^{\mathrm{ord}}}=\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{AS}_{d,g}^{\mathrm{ord}}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{AS}_{d,g}^{\mathrm{ord}}}T^{r}_{f}=\frac{1}{q\cdot\#\mathcal{H}_{g}}\sum_{f_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{g},b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi(b)^{r}T^{r}_{f_{1}}=\langle\psi(\mathrm{tr}_{q/p}b)^{r}\rangle_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\cdot M^{r}_{\mathcal{H}_{g}}=\mathds{1}_{p|r}\cdot M^{r}_{\mathcal{H}_{g}},

and the assertion follows from Proposition
6.6
.∎

6.3
Means of traces for the family
\mathcal{AS}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{d,g},\deg g=d-1

For the present subsection assume that
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is monic, squarefree with
\deg g=d-1
. In this case we cannot directly derive a bound on
M^{r}_{\mathcal{AS}_{d,g}^{\mathrm{ord}}}
from our bound on
\mathcal{H}_{g}
. In the present subsection we explain how the arguments in section
6.1
need to be modified to produce a similar bound on
M^{r}_{\mathcal{AS}_{d,g}^{\mathrm{ord}}}
in the present case.

Remark 6.9
.

If there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
g(\alpha)\neq 0
we could apply a Möbius transformation (with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
) to the variable
x
that moves
\alpha
to
\infty
and reduce the problem back to the case
\deg g=d
. However it can happen that no such
\alpha
exists.

Recall that in section
2.4
we defined the functions
\chi_{f}:\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\to\mathbb{C}
for
f=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
by (
2.7
). It is evident from the definition that
\chi_{f_{1}+f_{2}}=\chi_{f_{1}}\chi_{f_{2}}
. For
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
we introduce the following definitions.

\Lambda^{a}(c)=\chi_{ax}(c)\Lambda(c),\>c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\mbox{ monic}

(
\Lambda
is the von Mangoldt function),

\psi^{a}(r;N)=\sum_{\deg c=r}\Lambda^{a}(c),

where
N\subset(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/Q)^{\times}
for some modulus
Q
. Define also

\mathcal{H}_{g}^{a}=\{f+ax:f\in\mathcal{H}_{g}\}.

Note that
\mathcal{H}_{g}^{0}=\mathcal{H}_{g}
and (recall
\deg g=d-1
)

\mathcal{AS}_{d,g}^{\mathrm{ord}}=\bigsqcup_{0\neq a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\atop{b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}}\{f+b:f\in\mathcal{H}_{g}^{a}\}

(6.5)

(disjoint union).

By slightly modifying the proof of Proposition

M^{r}_{\mathcal{H}_{g}^{a}}=-q^{-r/2}\left(1+\sum_{Q|g}\mu(g/Q)\frac{q^{\deg Q}}{\varphi(g)}\psi^{a}\left(r;(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/gQ)^{\times p}\right)\right).

From here one can argue essentially verbatim as in section

(note that

Applying the arguments in the proof of Proposition

Let

6.4
1-level density for
\mathcal{AS}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{d,g}

In the present subsection we estimate the 1-level density for the family
\mathcal{AS}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{d,g}
, completing the proof of Theorem
1.3
. Throughout the rest of the section
d
is a natural number and
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is monic squarefree with
\deg g\in\{d,d-1\}
. We also fix a test function
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\subset(-1+\delta,1-\delta)
, for a fixed
\delta>0
. In the present section the asymptotic
O
-notation may depend on
\Phi,\delta
, i.e
O(\cdot)=O_{\Phi,\delta}(\cdot)
, but not on any other parameters.

To shorten notation we denote

M^{r}=M_{\mathcal{AS}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{d,g}}^{r}.
Note that
M^{-r}=\overline{M^{r}}
. For
f\in\mathcal{AS}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{d,g}
we have
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}(C_{f})=(d-1)(p-1)
and each
L(u,f,\psi)
has
2d-2
zeros.
First we write as in (
5.12
):

\left\langle W_{1}(f,\psi,\Phi)\right\rangle_{f\in\mathcal{AS}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{d,g}}=\hat{\Phi}(0)+\frac{1}{2d-2}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\left(\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{r}{2d-2}\right)M^{r}+\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{-r}{2d-2}\right)\overline{M^{r}}\right)\\
=\hat{\Phi}(0)+\frac{1}{2d-2}\sum_{r=1}^{(1-\delta)(2d-2)}\left(\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{r}{2d-2}\right)M^{r}+\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{-r}{2d-2}\right)\overline{M^{r}}\right).

(6.6)

We want to apply the bound in Proposition
6.10
, but we first need to bound the quantities
q^{\deg g}/\varphi(g)
and
\tau(g)
(
\varphi,\tau
are the Euler totient and divisor functions respectively).

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be squarefree of degree
d
and
\epsilon>0
a fixed constant. Then

(i)

\frac{q^{d}}{\varphi(g)}=O_{\epsilon}(d^{\epsilon}).

(ii)

\tau(g)=O_{\epsilon}(q^{\epsilon d}).

Here the implicit constants depend only on
\epsilon
(not on
q,d
).

Proof.

Over
\mathbb{Z}
the analogous bounds
\frac{n}{\varphi(n)}=O_{\epsilon}(\log^{\epsilon}n),\tau(n)=n^{\epsilon}
are well-known consequences of the Prime Number Theorem. See
[
21
]
*Theorems 2.9,2.11 for even stronger statements. The proofs can be easily adapted to
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
(we omit the details for brevity).∎

Now from (
6.6
), Proposition
6.10
and Lemma
6.11
we have (recall that
\Phi,\delta>0
are fixed and we also use
\min(r/p,2p)\leq\sqrt{2r}\leq\sqrt{d}
)

\left\langle W_{1}(f,\psi,\Phi)\right\rangle_{f\in\mathcal{AS}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{d,g}}-\hat{\Phi}(0)\ll\frac{1}{d}\sum_{r=1}^{(1-\delta)(2d-2)}|M^{r}|\\
\leq\frac{1}{d}\left(\frac{q^{\deg g}}{\phi(g)}\right)^{2}\cdot\sum_{r=1}^{(1-\delta)(2d-2)}\left(q^{(1/p-1/2)r}+d\tau(d)q^{(1-1/p)(r-2d+2)+\min(2p,r/p)}\right)\\
\ll_{\epsilon}d^{\epsilon-1}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{(1-\delta)(2d-2)}q^{(1/p-1/2)r}\right)+d^{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon d}q^{(2/p-2)\delta d+2\sqrt{d}}\to 0\ll_{\epsilon}d^{\epsilon-1}+d^{\epsilon}q^{(\epsilon+(2/p-2)\delta)d+2\sqrt{d}}\to 0

for
d\to\infty
as long as
\epsilon<\delta
, uniformly in
q,g
. This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

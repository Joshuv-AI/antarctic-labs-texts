Toric surfaces with symmetries by reflections
Jongbaek Song

Abstract
. Let W W be a reflection group in a plane and P P a rational polygon that is invariant under the W W -action. The action of W W on P P induces a W W -action on the toric variety X P X_{P} associated with P P . In this paper, we study the W W -representation on the cohomology H ∗ ​ ( X P ) H^{\ast}(X_{P}) and show that the invariant subring H ∗ ​ ( X P ) W H^{\ast}(X_{P})^{W} is isomorphic to the cohomology ring of the toric variety associated with the fundamental region P / W P/W . As an example, we provide an explicit description of the main result for the case of the toric variety associated with the fan of Weyl chambers of type G 2 G_{2} .

1.
Introduction

A toric variety is a complex algebraic variety on which an algebraic torus acts with an open dense orbit. Due to the nice torus symmetry of a toric variety,
one can construct a toric variety
X
from a certain combinatorial object called a
fan

\Sigma
, and the combinatorics of
\Sigma
can be recovered using the orbit structure of the torus action on
X
. For the case of a projective toric variety, we often begin with a lattice
M
and a rational polytope
P\subset M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
to define a toric variety
X_{P}
by considering the normal fan
\Sigma_{P}
of
P
(see
[
CLS11
, Section 2.3]
for instance). In general, two different polytopes may define the same normal fan. In this case, they define the same toric variety with different projective embeddings.

Recall that the normal fan of
P
is determined by the face structure of
P
together with primitive vectors
\lambda_{i}\in M^{\ast}
defining the hyperplane supporting
each codimension-1 face
F_{i}
of
P
. Therefore, most of the topological information of
X_{P}
is encoded in the face structure of
P
and
\lambda_{i}
’s. For instance, when
P
is a simple polytope, the associated toric variety
X_{P}
is an orbifold. In this case, the cohomology ring
H^{\ast}(X_{P};\mathbb{Q})
is given by

(1.1)

H^{\ast}(X_{P};\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]/\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J},

where
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
are ideals

(1)

\mathcal{I}=\left<\prod_{k=1}^{r}x_{i_{k}}\mid\bigcap_{k=1}^{r}F_{i_{k}}=\emptyset\right>
;

(2)

\mathcal{J}=\left<\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left<m,\lambda_{i}\right>x_{i}\mid m\in M\right>
.

This result is due to Jurkiewicz
[
Jur80
]
for projective nonsingular toric varieties and to Danilov
[
Dan78
]
for arbitrary toric orbifolds.

When a toric variety
X_{P}
arises from a polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
that is preserved by an action of a finite group
W\leq GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, the action of
W
on
P
determines a
W
-action on
X_{P}
. Hence, the cohomology
H^{\ast}(X_{P})
is equipped with the induced
W
-module structure. A typical example is given by the toric variety associated with the permutohedron
P_{n}
, namely the convex hull of the orbit of
(1,2,\dots,n+1)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by the action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n+1}
defined by permuting coordinates. Instead of
\mathfrak{S}_{n+1}
and
(1,\dots,n+1)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, one can consider the Weyl group for each of other Lie types and a point having distinct coordinates in the corresponding root lattice respectively, which yields a lattice polytope known as a
weight polytope
in the literature. Accordingly, we have the associated toric variety.
We refer the readers to
[
Pro90
]
, where the author considered the fan consisting of Weyl chambers, that is the normal fan of the permutohedron. We also refer to
[
Abe15
,
BB11
,
Huh14
,
Kly85
,
DMPS92
,
DL94
,
Ste94
]
for relevant studies of these varieties.

In this paper, we consider arbitrary 2-dimensional polytopes
P
that are preserved by actions of groups
W
generated by reflections. Note that the reflection group of a plane is the dihedral group

(1.2)

D_{2\ell}=\left<s_{1},s_{2}\mid s_{1}^{2}=s_{2}^{2}=1,~(s_{1}s_{2})^{\ell}=(s_{2}s_{1})^{\ell}\right>

generated by two reflections
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
corresponding to two lines with angle
\frac{2\pi}{\ell}
for some
\ell\geq 3
. Therefore, we consider the cases where the group
W
is either the group generated by a single reflection or the dihedral group
D_{2\ell}
. In either cases, one can take a region
R\subset P
such that each point
\mathbf{x}\in R
represents the
W
-orbit of
\mathbf{x}
. We denote by
P/W
the closure
\overline{R}\subset P
and call it the
fundamental region
. Notice that
P/W
is again a convex rational polytope, hence we may associate
P/W
with a toric variety
X_{P/W}
. Then, we study the
W
-action on the cohomology
H^{\ast}(X_{P})
and show the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
P
be a 2-dimensional rational polytope with an action of a reflection group
W
. Let
X_{P}
and
X_{P/W}
be toric varieties associated with
P
and the fundamental region
P/W
, respectively. Then, there is a ring isomorphism

H^{\ast}(X_{P/W})\cong H^{\ast}(X_{P})^{W}.

Using the following classical result

H^{\ast}(X/W)\cong H^{\ast}(X)^{W}

for any locally compact Hausdorff
W
-space for some finite group
W
(see for instance
[
Bor60
, III-2]
), the isomorphism of Theorem
1.1
also implies

(1.3)

H^{\ast}(X_{P/W})\cong H^{\ast}(X_{P}/W).

One of the motivations of Theorem
1.1
is the following question posed in
[
HMSS21
]
.

Question 1.2
.

[
HMSS21
, Question 8.1]

If
W
is generated by reflections in
GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and acts on
P
, must
X_{P}/W
be isomorphic with
X_{P/W}
?

Several classes of toric varieties supporting Question
1.2
are provided in
[
HMSS21
, Section 8]
. The result of Theorem
1.1
gives a cohomological evidence of this question for toric surfaces.

This paper is organized as follows.
We begin in Section
2
with a brief background about toric surfaces, where we mostly focus on their rational cohomology rings. For simplicity, we write
H^{\ast}(-)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}H^{\ast}(-;\mathbb{Q})
otherwise stated.

In Sections

is symmetric by a single reflection and by a dihedral group respectively, and give a proof of Theorem
1.1
for each case. Although the main ideas are similar, we separate them to give a more detailed explanation for each case.

Section

as a particular case of Theorem

. Instead of working with a fan, we consider a polytope whose normal fan is the fan of Weyl chambers of type

2.
Toric surface

Let
T(\cong(S^{1})^{n})
be an
n
-dimensional torus. We denote by
M
and
N
the character lattice of
T
and the lattice of 1-parameter subgroups, respectively. Given a character
\chi^{m}\in\Hom(S^{1},T)
for
m\in M
and a
1

\lambda^{n}\in\Hom(T,S^{1})
for
n\in N
, we have
\chi^{m}\circ\lambda^{n}\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}
given by
t\mapsto t^{r}
for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, there is a natural bilinear pairing

\left<~,~\right>\colon M\times N\to\mathbb{Z}

defined by
\left<m,n\right>=r

Let
P
be an
n
-dimensional rational polytope in
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
with
\ell
-facets, say
F_{1},\dots,F_{\ell}
. We denote by
\lambda_{i}\in N
the primitive (outward) vector defining a facet
F_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq\ell
, namely,

(2.1)

P=\bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell}\left\{\mathbf{x}\in M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\mid\left<\mathbf{x},\lambda_{i}\right>+a_{i}\leq 0\text{ for }\lambda_{i}\in N,~a_{i}<0\right\}

and
F_{i}=\{\mathbf{x}\in P\mid\left<\mathbf{x},\lambda_{i}\right>+a_{i}=0\}
. We assume that no redundant inequality exists in (
2.1
) without loss of generality.
Then, one can associate a toric variety
X_{P}
of complex dimension
n
. We refer to
[
CLS11
]
for explicit definitions of
X_{P}
. Also, we refer to
[
BP15
, Chapter 7]
for more topological viewpoint. When
X_{P}
is of complex dimension
2
, namely when it is associated to a rational polygon, we call
X_{P}
a toric surface.

In this manuscript, we focus on the rational cohomology ring
H^{\ast}(X_{P})
of a toric surface
X_{P}
. So, the ideal
\mathcal{I}
of (
1.1
) can be written by

(2.2)

\mathcal{I}=\left<x_{i}x_{j}\mid E_{i}\cap E_{j}=\emptyset\right>,

where
E_{i}
denotes a facet, namely an edge of
P
.

We notice that
H^{\ast}(X_{P})
is a Poincaré duality algebra as
X_{P}
is an orbifold, see for instance
[
CLS11
, Theorem 11.4.8]
. The following proposition about Poincaré duality algebras will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, which will be discussed in the following two sections.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\phi=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{n}\colon\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}\to\mathcal{B}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}B_{i}
be a morphism of graded algebras. If
\mathcal{A}
is a Poincaré duality algebra and
\phi_{n}\colon A_{n}\to B_{n}
is an isomorphism, then
\phi
is an isomorphism.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
for Case (1-1)

Recall from (
2.1

\lambda_{i}\in N

\eta\in N
are vectors defining the edge

\sigma

\lambda_{i}

\eta
satisfy the following three conditions:

(i)

\lambda_{n+i}-\lambda_{i}=c_{i}\eta
for some
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
and for each
i=1,\dots,n
;

(ii)

\lambda_{2n+1}\perp\eta
;

(iii)

\lambda_{2n+1}=-\lambda_{2n+2}
.

Applying (
1.1
), we have

H^{\ast}(X_{P/\sigma})=\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x_{2n+1},x_{2n+2},x_{\sigma}]/\mathcal{I}_{\text{(1-1)}}+\mathcal{J}_{\text{(1-1)}}

where
\mathcal{I}_{\text{(1-1)}}
is the ideal generated by

\left\{x_{j}x_{k}\mid E_{j}\cap E_{k}=\emptyset\text{ for }j,k\in\{1,\dots,n,2n+1,2n+2\}\right\}\cup\{x_{j}x_{\sigma}\mid 1\leq j\leq n\}

and
\mathcal{J}_{\text{(1-1)}}
is the ideal generated by

\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left<m,\lambda_{i}\right>x_{i}+\left<m,\lambda_{2n+1}\right>x_{2n+1}+\left<m,\lambda_{2n+2}\right>x_{2n+2}+\left<m,\eta\right>x_{\sigma}~\Big|~m\in M\right\}.

Now, we consider the map

(3.2)

\phi\colon H^{\ast}(X_{P/\sigma})\to H^{\ast}(X_{P})

defined by

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}x_{i}+x_{n+i}&i=1,\dots,n;\\
x_{i}&i=2n+1,~2n+2,\end{cases}

\displaystyle x_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}x_{n+i},

where
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
is defined in
(i)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The image of
\phi
is the invariant ring
H^{\ast}(X_{P})^{\sigma}
.

Proof.

Obviously,
\phi(x_{i})
is
\sigma
-invariant for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n,2n+1,2n+2\}
. Below, we prove that
\phi(x_{\sigma})
is
\sigma
-invariant. Since
\lambda_{2n+1}\perp\eta
, we take
\{\eta_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\eta,\eta_{2}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\lambda_{2n+1}\}
as a basis of
N
. Let
\{\eta_{1}^{\ast},\eta_{2}^{\ast}\}\subset M
be the dual basis of
\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}
, i.e.,
\left<\eta_{i}^{\ast},\eta_{j}\right>=\delta_{ij}
for
1\leq i,j\leq 2
.

Recall from (
1.1
) that

\sum_{i=1}^{2n+2}\left<m,\lambda_{i}\right>x_{i}=0\in H^{\ast}(X_{P}).

Applying
m=\eta^{\ast}
and
\lambda_{n+i}=\lambda_{i}+c_{i}\eta
for
i=1,\dots,n
, we have

(3.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left<\eta^{\ast},\lambda_{i}\right>x_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left<\eta^{\ast},\lambda_{i}\right>x_{n+i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}x_{n+i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left<\eta^{\ast},\lambda_{i}\right>(x_{i}+x_{n+i})+\phi(x_{\sigma})

which is
0
in
H^{\ast}(X_{P})
. Since the first term of the right-hand side of (
3.3
) is
\sigma
-invariant, we conclude that so is
\phi(x_{\sigma})
. Therefore, we have
{\rm im}\phi\subset H^{\ast}(X_{P})^{\sigma}
.

To show
H^{\ast}(X_{P})^{\sigma}\subset{\rm im}\phi
, recall that the torus equivariant cohomology of
X_{P}
is

H^{\ast}_{T}(X_{P})=\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{2n+2}]/\mathcal{I}
, where
\mathcal{I}
is the ideal of (
2.2
) (for instance, see
[
CLS11
, Section 12.4]
). It is straightforward to see that the invariant ring
H^{\ast}_{T}(X_{P})^{\sigma}
is generated by
\{x_{i}+x_{n+i}\mid i=1,\dots,n\}\cup\{x_{2n+1},x_{2n+2}\}
. Since the natural surjective homomorphism
H^{\ast}_{T}(X_{P})\to H^{\ast}(X_{P})
is
\sigma
-equivariant, its restriction
H^{\ast}_{T}(X_{P})^{\sigma}\to H^{\ast}(X_{P})^{\sigma}
is also surjective.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The map
\phi
is well-defined.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\ker\phi
contains both
\mathcal{I}_{\text{(1-1)}}
and
\mathcal{J}_{\text{(1-1)}}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\phi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left<m,\lambda_{i}\right>x_{i}+\left<m,\lambda_{2n+1}\right>x_{2n+1}+\left<m,\lambda_{2n+2}\right>x_{2n+2}+\left<m,\eta\right>x_{\sigma}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left<m,\lambda_{i}\right>(x_{i}+x_{n+i})+\left<m,\lambda_{2n+1}\right>x_{2n+1}+\left<m,\lambda_{2n+2}\right>x_{2n+2}+\left<m,\eta\right>\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}x_{n+i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\left<m,\lambda_{i}\right>x_{i}+\left<m,\lambda_{i}+c_{i}\eta\right>x_{n+i}\big)+\left<m,\lambda_{2n+1}\right>x_{2n+1}+\left<m,\lambda_{2n+2}\right>x_{2n+2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2n+2}\left<m,\lambda_{i}\right>x_{i}\in\mathcal{J}.

For
x_{j}x_{k}\in\mathcal{J}_{\text{(1-1)}}
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi(x_{j}x_{k})=\begin{cases}(x_{j}+x_{n+j})(x_{k}+x_{n+k})&\text{if }1\leq j,k\leq n;\\
(x_{j}+x_{n+j})x_{k}&\text{if }1\leq j\leq n\text{ and }2n+1\leq k\leq 2n+2;\\
x_{j}(x_{k}+x_{n+k})&\text{if }2n+1\leq j\leq 2n+2\text{ and }1\leq k\leq n;\\
x_{j}x_{k}&\text{if }2n+1\leq j\neq k\leq 2n+2.\end{cases}\end{split}

In any of above cases,
\phi(x_{j}x_{k})\in\mathcal{J}
. Also, for
x_{j}x_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{J}_{\text{(1-1)}}
with
1\leq j\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(x_{j}x_{\sigma})

\displaystyle=x_{j}\phi(x_{\sigma})+x_{n+j}\phi(x_{\sigma})

\displaystyle=x_{j}\phi(x_{\sigma})+x_{n+j}\sigma\left(\phi(x_{\sigma})\right)

\displaystyle=x_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}x_{n+i}+x_{n+j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}x_{i}=0,

where the second equality follows from Lemma
3.1
and the last equality follows from (
2.2
).
Hence, we have established the claim.
∎

Since the automorphism

Therefore, the map

1.3.
Twists of Galois modules

From now on, we denote the

-adic cyclotomic character

Example I.3.1
.

Given a number field
F

Remark I.3.2
.

It is a well-known result that the cyclotomic character factors through

\mathrm{Gal}(F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})|F)\simeq\Delta\times\Gamma

The “twist” provided by the cyclotomic character is the most commonly known, although from the definition one can tell that this can be extended to more general characters. For that reason, we provide the following definition.

Definition I.3.3
.

Let
\chi:G_{F}\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}

1.4.
Pontryagin Duality

Recall that one can define an endofunctor on the category of locally compact abelian groups, which is usually denoted by

Remark I.4.1

Note that if

Remark I.4.2

If

(g\cdot f)(a)=f(g^{-1}\cdot a),\qquad\forall g\in G,\forall a\in A,\forall f\in A^{\vee}.

Example I.4.3

Clearly, one has that

Finally, we introduce Euler characteristics of Iwasawa modules.

The following section will serve the purpose of illustrating the subject of stable homotopy theory using the language of

2.1.
The language of
\infty
-categories

For our purposes in this paper, we can think of an
\infty
-category
as a category that is weakly enriched over spaces, in the sense that there is a
space
of maps between every pair of objects, and the composition law is defined only up to
coherent
homotopy. Homotopy coherence is the problem one faces when trying to lift diagrams in the homotopy category of spaces to the ordinary category of spaces. It is easy to see that the existence of such a lift is equivalent to the existence of certain higher-degree homotopies, and this is precisely the problem that
\infty
-categories tackle, as we are asking their sets of maps to be spaces, and therefore contain homotopical information in all degrees. Although we are asking for the composition law to be only unique up to coherent homotopy, which is a much stronger condition than being unique up to homotopy, this is still sufficient to provide a robust theory of
\infty
-categories, in which all constructions for
1
-categories (functors, limits, colimits…) can also be performed.

Remark II.1.1
.

One can also look at the weaker concept of an
\infty
-groupoid
, whose main difference with
\infty
-categories is that all maps admit an inverse. As explained in
[
Lur09
, Example 1.1.1.4]
, one can think of
\infty
-groupoids as categories behaving as spaces, and from it one defines the
\infty
-category of spaces
\mathcal{S}
, which is the
\infty
-categorical analogue of the classical category of spaces.

\pi_{0}:\mathrm{Sp}\to\mathcal{A}b
comes from the fact that
\mathrm{Sp}
admits a canonical
t

\mathrm{Sp}
gives rise to a long exact sequence of abelian groups just as in classical homotopy theory. However, note that in this case one can take negative homotopy groups.

An alternative way to visualize

is given by Brown’s representability theorem
[

, which roughly states that any generalized cohomology theory is representable by a spectrum. Thus,
\mathrm{Sp}
can be seen as the
\infty

, and check that the assignment
X\mapsto E^{n}(X)
gives a generalized cohomology theory. In fact, if
X
is also a spectrum, one can define
E^{n}(X)=[\Omega^{n}E,X]
.

2.4.
Bousfield localization of spectra and Iwasawa theory

Let us fix a spectrum
E\in\mathrm{Sp}
. Another spectrum
X
is said to be
E
-acyclic
if
X\otimes E\simeq 0
. On the other hand,
X
is said to be
E
-local
if
Y\to X
is null-homotopic (i.e, factors through a zero object) whenever
Y
is acyclic. Then, there exists a functor
L_{E}
from
\mathrm{Sp}
called
Bousfield localization
, which is characterized up to equivalence by the following two properties, namely that
L_{E}X
is
E
-local and that there is a natural map
X\to L_{E}X
and its fiber is
E
-acyclic. We then define the
p
-adic completion

X_{p}^{\wedge}
of a spectrum
X
to be the Bousfield localization of
X
with respect to the Moore spectrum
S\mathbf{Z}/p
.

Now, let
X
be a space, and let
\mathrm{KU}^{\ast}(X)
be the ring of (periodic) complex
K
-theory. Since
\mathrm{KU}^{\ast}
defines a generalized cohomology theory, it corresponds to a spectrum
\mathrm{KU}
by Brown’s theorem. We refer to
\mathrm{KU}
as the
complex
K
-theory spectrum
. Finally, we define the
K(1)
-local
K
-theory spectrum
as
L_{K(1)}K=\mathrm{KU}_{p}^{\wedge}
.

Remark II.4.1
.

In fact, the
K(1)
-local adjective comes from the first Morava
K
-theory spectrum, which satisfies that
L_{K(1)}K(X)\simeq\mathrm{KU}(X)_{p}^{\wedge}
. In general, one can construct the
n
-th Morava
K
-theory spectrum

, which is a central tool in stable homotopy theory.

As explained in
[
Mit05
]
,
K(1)
-local

-theory already has a direct relation with Iwasawa theory: One can identify the group of Adams operations on
K(1)
-local
K
-theory with
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty}|\mathbf{Q})
via the cyclotomic character and obtain
(L_{K(1)}K)^{\ast}(L_{K(1)}K)\simeq\Lambda(\Gamma)[\Delta]
.

containing all primes dividing

denote the maximal extension of

which is unramified outside

and the infinite places of

-extension of

Definition III.0.1

A Galois extension

is said to be a

-adic Lie extension
if

(1)

(2)

(3)

-extension of

Finally, and following

is an abelian

3.1.
The main conjecture

Let us start by noting that if we denote
\Gamma=\mathrm{Gal}(F^{\mathrm{cyc}}|F)
,

can be written as

. If we denote by

the fixed field of
\Gamma
, which satisfies that

, then

can be written as the composite field

. Moreover, we will refer to the
p
-adic cyclotomic character
\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}:\mathrm{Gal}(F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})|F)\to\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}
in a slightly unconventional way, namely as a character of
G
that is trivial on

, given that
\Gamma
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{Gal}(F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})|F)
and thus we can look at the restriction of
\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}
to
\Gamma
, as it was commented in
I.3.2
.

Following the notations from
1.5
, let
\psi\in\hat{H}\setminus\{\mathbf{1}\}
be of
type S
, that is, so that
F_{\psi}
, the field cut out by
\ker(\psi)
, or perhaps more precisely, the minimal extension of
F
through which
\psi
factors, is totally real and
F_{\psi}\cap F^{\mathrm{cyc}}=F
. For simplicity, we will also assume that the order of
\psi
is coprime to
p
. Furthermore, fix a topological generator
\gamma\in\Gamma
, whose image through the cyclotomic character
\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\gamma)
we denote by
u
,

Now, let
L^{\Sigma}(\psi,s)
denote the
L
-function with Euler factors at
\Sigma
in the Euler product expression removed, whose rationality is justified in
[
Wil90
, (1.2)]
by using a result by Klingen-Siegel. We then have the following theorem.

Theorem III.1.1
.

(Deligne-Ribet
[
DR80
]
, Cassou-Noguès
[
CN79
]
, Barsky
[
Bar77
]
)
Let
n\geq 1
be an integer with
n\equiv 0\mod[F(\mu_{p}):F]
. There exists a unique formal power series
L_{p}(\psi)\in\mathcal{O}_{\psi}\llbracket T\rrbracket
such that

L_{p}(\psi,u^{n}-1)=L^{\Sigma}(\psi,1-n).

Remark III.1.2
.

As explained in
[
Wil90
]
, the theorem can also be stated for
\mathbf{1}
and characters of type
W
, that is, characters in which
F_{\psi}\subseteq F^{\mathrm{cyc}}
, multiplying the left hand side by
(\psi(\gamma)u^{n}-1)^{-1}
but for simplicity we only focus on the type
S

Remark III.1.3
.

As explained in
[
Coa+12
, p. 7]
the theorem can also be stated regarding

as a

on
G
, so that if
n>0
is an integer with
n\equiv 0\mod[F(\mu_{p}):F]
,

L_{p}(\psi,\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{n}):=\int_{G}\left(\psi\otimes\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{n}\right)dL_{p}(\psi)=L^{\Sigma}(\psi,1-n).

This is why we will denote
L_{p}(\psi,\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{n})
and
L_{p}(\psi,u^{n}-1)
indistinctly.

For the rest of this section, let us fix
\psi
of type
S
; denote
X=X(F_{\infty})
, and let
X_{\psi}=e_{\psi}(X\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\mathcal{O}_{\psi})
. By a classical theorem of Iwasawa,
X_{\psi}
is finitely generated and torsion as an
\mathcal{O}_{\psi}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
-module, and therefore we can look at a characteristic power series
C_{\psi}:=\mathrm{ch}(X_{\psi})\in\mathcal{O}_{\psi}\llbracket T\rrbracket
for

. It is easy to check that

(3.1)

\displaystyle C_{\psi}(T)\mathcal{O}_{\psi}\llbracket T\rrbracket=\varpi_{\psi}^{\mu_{\psi}}\det\left(T-(\gamma-1)|X_{\psi}\otimes F_{\psi}\right)\mathcal{O}_{\psi}\llbracket T\rrbracket,

where
\varpi_{\psi}
is any fixed local parameter for
\mathcal{O}_{\psi}
, and
\mu_{\psi}
is the algebraic
\mu
-invariant, which by the work of Wiles agrees with the analytic invariant obtained after applying the Weierstrass preparation theorem to
L_{p}(\psi,T)
above. With this, one can state the algebraic part of the IMC as follows.

Theorem III.1.4
.

(IMC for totally real fields)
For all
\psi\in\hat{H}
of order prime to
p
,

L_{p}(\psi,T)\mathcal{O}_{\psi}\llbracket T\rrbracket=C_{\psi}(T)\mathcal{O}_{\psi}\llbracket T\rrbracket.

3.2.
The group-cohomological Euler characteristic formula

Now, let us note that
G
acts on
\mathbf{Z}_{p}(n)
as long as
n\equiv 0\mod[F(\mu_{p}):F]
, so let
n
be an integer – positive or negative – of that form. The determinant part in equation (
3.1
) admits a cohomological interpretation by the work of Bayer-Neukirch
[
BN78
, Theorem 6.1]
and Schneider
[
Sch83
, Theorem §4.4]
. Namely, the determinant does not vanish at
T=0
if and only if the
\Gamma
-invariants (or equivalently, the
\Gamma
-coinvariants) of
X_{\psi}
are finite dimensional. When one of these three equivalent conditions holds, one has

(3.2)

\displaystyle|C_{\psi}(u^{n}-1)|_{p}=\frac{\sharp\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(\Gamma,X_{\psi}(-n)\right)}{\sharp\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(\Gamma,X_{\psi}(-n)\right)},

where the
(-n)
-th Tate twist of
X_{\psi}
appearing in the right hand side follows from
I.6.2
.

Now, combining
III.1.1
,
III.1.4
and (
3.2
), we obtain the following well-known result.

Proposition III.2.1
.

Let
n
be an integer with
n\equiv 0\mod[F(\mu_{p}):F]
. Then,

|L_{p}(\psi,\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{n})|_{p}=\frac{\sharp\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(\Gamma,X_{\psi}(-n)\right)}{\sharp\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(\Gamma,X_{\psi}(-n)\right)}.

Remark III.2.2
.

In particular, note that when
n>0
is an integer such that
n\equiv 0\mod[F(\mu_{p}):F]
, we have a group-cohomological interpretation of the
p
-adic valuation of the
L
-function, that is,

\left|L^{\Sigma}(\psi,1-n)\right|_{p}=\frac{\sharp\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(\Gamma,X_{\psi}(-n)\right)}{\sharp\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(\Gamma,X_{\psi}(-n)\right)}.

3.3.
The étale-cohomological Euler characteristic formula

Let
n
be an integer as in
III.2.1
. We can now use Shapiro’s lemma to extend the formula above as follows

(3.3)

\displaystyle|L_{p}(\psi,\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{n})|_{p}=\frac{\#\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(\Gamma,X_{\psi}(-n)\right)}{\#\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(\Gamma,X_{\psi}(-n)\right)}=\frac{\#\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(G,X(-n)\right)}{\#\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(G,X(-n)\right)}.

Let us now recall that we can identify
X
with
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{\infty}}[1/p];\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p})^{\vee}
. This is due to the fact that since
X
is the Galois group of the maximal abelian
p
-extension of
F^{\mathrm{cyc}}
that is unramified outside
p
, it can be identified with the maximal abelian pro-
p
factor group of the algebraic fundamental group of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{\infty}}[1/p])
. Taking this into account,

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{\nu}(G,X(-n))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{H}^{\nu}\left(G,\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{\infty}}[1/p];\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}(n))^{\vee}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{H}^{1-\nu}\left(G,\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{\infty}}[1/p];\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}(n))\right)^{\vee},\qquad\nu=0,1.

where the first equality follows from the fact that the Pontryagin dual inverts the action of the cyclotomic character, and the second equality follows from the proof of
[
Sch83
, Lemma §I.2.4]
. Combining these equalities with
3.3
, we obtain

|L_{p}(\psi,\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{n})|_{p}=\frac{\#\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(G,\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{F_{\infty}}[1/p],\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}(n)\right)\right)}{\#\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(G,\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{F_{\infty}}[1/p],\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}(n)\right)\right)}.

Following
[
Sch83
, Lemma §II.4.1]
we can use the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence

E^{i,j}_{2}=\mathrm{H}^{i}\left(G,\mathrm{H}^{j}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{F_{\infty}}[1/p],\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}(n)\right)\right)\Rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{i+j}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{F}[1/p],\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}(n)\right).

Schneider studies the groups appearing in this spectral sequence and observes that it degenerates in the second page, and from there he deduces that, since the weak Leopoldt conjecture is proven in the case of totally real number fields, and that is equivalent to
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{\psi}}[1/p],\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p})=0
, one has the following classical result.

Theorem III.3.1
.

(
[
BN78
, Theorem 6.1]
,
[
Sch83
, Theorem §4.4]
)
Let
n
be an integer with
n\equiv 0\mod[F(\mu_{p}):F]
. Then, the following equality holds

\left|L_{p}(\psi,\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{n})\right|_{p}=\frac{\#\mathrm{H}^{0}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{F}[1/p],\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}(n)\right)}{\#\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{F}[1/p],\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}(n)\right)}.

Corollary III.5.1

From now on, we let

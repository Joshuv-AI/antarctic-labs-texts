Quotients of permutation groups by nonabelian minimal normal subgroups
Derek F. Holt

Abstract
We prove that a quotient G / N G/N of a subgroup G G of Sym ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{Sym}(n) by a nonabelian minimal normal subgroup N N of G G embeds into Sym ⁡ ( m ) \mathrm{Sym}(m) for some m < n m<n . This result was proved previously in [ 4 ] , and we also prove that, if G G is transitive then we can take m ≤ 2 ​ n / 5 m\leq 2n/5 .



1
Introduction

For a finite group
G
we define
P(G)
to be the smallest degree of a
faithful permutation representation of
G
; that is the smallest
n
for which
there is an embedding
G\to\mathrm{Sym}(n)
. It was shown by Neumann in
[
11
]

that
P(G/N)
can be exponentially larger than
P(G)
for normal
subgroups
N
of
G
. In
[
7
]
, Easdown and Praeger called
a quotient
exceptional
if
P(G/N)>P(G)
and proved that
various type of quotients of finite groups
G
, such as
G/O_{p}(G)
for
a prime
p
, and
G/S(G)
with
S(G)
the solvable radical of
G
,
are not exceptional.

It was proved in
[
4
]
that
G/N
is not exceptional when
N
is
a nonabelian minimal normal subgroup of
G
, and we prove a stronger result for
transitive subgroups
G
of
\mathrm{Sym}(n)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{Sym}(n)
, and let
N
be a nonabelian minimal normal subgroup of
G
.
Then
G/N
embeds into
\mathrm{Sym}(m)
for some
m<n
.
Furthermore, if
G
is a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Sym}(n)
, then
we can choose
m\leq 2n/5
.

The examples
G=\mathrm{Sym}(5)
,
N=\mathrm{Alt}(5)
with
n=5
, and
G=\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(2,9)
,

N=\mathrm{PSL}(2,9)
with
n=10
, together with
wreath products of these groups by various other transitive groups, show that
the bound
m\leq 2n/5
for transitive subgroups of
\mathrm{Sym}(n)
is best possible.

By repeated applications of this result, we get the immediate corollary:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{Sym}(n)
, and let
N
be a nontrivial normal subgroup of
G
with
no abelian composition factors. Then
G/N
embeds into
\mathrm{Sym}(m)
for some
m<n
.
Furthermore, if
G
is a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Sym}(n)
, then
we can choose
m\leq 2n/5
.

One motivation for investigating this particular problem was the algorithm
described in
[
10
]
for finding a smallest sized generating set of a finite
group
G
, and the probabilistic variation and Magma implementation described
in
[
9
]
. That algorithm starts by computing a chief series of a finite
group
G
. The chief series computed by the Magma intrinsic has the
chief factors in
S(G)
at the bottom of the series, but we observed that
the algorithm often runs faster when using a chief series with as many
nonabelian chief factors at the bottom as possible. Such a chief series can be
found more easily in a permutation group if we have an efficient method
of representing quotients
G/N
for which the composition factors of
N
are
nonabelian.

2
Preliminary lemmas

We assume throughout the paper that
G
is a finite group,
although the first two lemmas are valid for arbitrary groups.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N\unlhd G
with
N
a direct product of nonabelian
simple groups,
and let
K
be a normal subgroup of
G
with
K\cap N=1
. Let
L
be a normal
subgroup of
G
with
L\leq KN\cong K\times N
. Then
L
contains all simple
direct factors of
N
onto which elements of
L
project non-trivially.

Proof.

Identify
KN
with
K\times N
, and suppose that
there exists
(k,n)\in L
such that
n
has non-trivial projection
n_{s}
onto
a simple direct factor
S
of
N
. Then, since
Z(S)=1
, there exists

s\in S
with
s^{-1}n_{s}s\neq n_{s}
and then the commutator
[(k,n),(1,s)]=(1,[n_{s},s])\in L\cap S
, so
L\cap S\neq 1
and the simplicity of
S

implies that
S\leq L
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
N=S^{k}
for a nonabelian simple group
S
, and let

H\leq N
be a subdirect product (i.e.
H
projects onto each of the

k
simple factors of
N
). Then
N_{N}(H)=H
.

Proof.

We use induction on
k
. When
k=1
we have
H=N
and the result
holds. Suppose
k>1
, write
N=S\times T
with
T=S^{k-1}
, and let

g\in N_{N}(H)
. Then by the inductive hypothesis, the projection of
N_{N}(H)

onto
T
lies in the projection of
H
onto
T
so, by replacing
g
by a
product with an element in
H
, we can assume that
g\in S
. If
g\neq 1
,
then there exists
s\in S
with
[g,s]\neq 1
and, since
H
projects onto
S
,
we have
[g,s]\in H
. But then, since
S\cap H\unlhd S
,
we get
S\leq H
and so
g\in H
, which proves the result.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
H\leq G
with
|G:H|=k
and suppose that there is an embedding

H\to\mathrm{Sym}(n)
for some
n
. Then there is an embedding
G\to\mathrm{Sym}(kn)
.

Proof.

Suppose that the image of the embedding
H\to\mathrm{Sym}(n)
has
t
orbits with
point stabilizers
H_{1},\ldots,H_{t}
. Then we can define an embedding

G\to\mathrm{Sym}(kn)
such that the image also has
t
orbits, with the same
point stabilizers
H_{1},\ldots,H_{t}
.
∎

The wreath product
W:=U\wr_{\Gamma}V
of groups

U\leq\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta)
and
V\leq\mathrm{Sym}(\Gamma)
with product action is defined
in
[
6
, Section 2.1]
. This (right) action is on the set

\Omega:=\mathrm{Fun}(\Gamma,\Delta)
with
W=\mathrm{Fun}(\Gamma,U)\rtimes V
and

\phi^{(f,v)}(\gamma):=\phi(\gamma^{v^{-1}})^{f(\gamma^{v^{-1}})}\ \ \ \mathrm{for}\ \phi\in\Omega,\,f\in\mathrm{Fun}(\Gamma,U),\,v\in V,\,\gamma\in\Gamma.

In particular, taking
f=1
, the identity element of
\mathrm{Fun}(\Gamma,U)
that
maps all elements of
\Gamma
to the identity element of
U
, and
identifying
V
with the subgroup
\{(1,v):v\in V\}
, the action of
V

on
\Omega
is given by
\phi^{v}(\gamma)=\phi(\gamma^{v^{-1}})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
W:=U\wr_{\Gamma}V
be the wreath product with product
action on
\Omega
as just defined, with
|\Delta|>1
, and let

\alpha,\beta\in\Delta
with
\alpha\neq\beta
. Then there is subset

\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}
of
\Omega
that is fixed by the subgroup
V
of
W
,
and for which the action of
V
on
\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}
is equivalent to its
action on
\Gamma
.

Proof.

For
\gamma\in\Gamma
define
\phi_{\gamma}\in\Omega

by
\phi_{\gamma}(\gamma)=\alpha
and
\phi_{\gamma}(\delta)=\beta
for

\delta\in\Gamma\setminus\{\gamma\}
, and let

\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}:=\{\phi_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
.
It is straightforward to check that
\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}
has the required
property with the equivalence
\gamma\mapsto\phi_{\gamma}
between

\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}
.
∎

The O’Nan-Scott Theorem for a finite primitive permutation group
G
of degree

n
on the set
\Omega
with socle
H
is stated (and later proved)
in
[
6
, Theorem 4.1A]
(see also
[
6
, Theorem 4.6A]
).
The four possibilities when
H=S^{k}
is nonabelian with
S
simple are described
in Case (b) of the theorem. In Case (b) (i)
k=1
and
G
is almost simple.
In Case (b) (ii)
G
is of diagonal type with
n=|S|^{k-1}
and the point
stabiliser
H_{\alpha}
is a diagonal subgroup of
S
.
In Case (b) (iv),
H
acts regularly on
\Omega
, so
n=|S|^{k}
. Finally, in
Case (b) (iii), we have
k>1
, and
G
is contained in a wreath product

U\wr\mathrm{Sym}(k/d)
with product action, where
U
is a primitive permutation
group of almost simple or diagonal type with socle
S^{d}
and degree
\ell
,
and
n=\ell^{k/d}
.
The group
U
is almost simple if and only if
d=1
, in which case
n=\ell^{k}
,
and we shall say that
G
is primitive of
almost simple product type
.
Note also that
\ell=|S|^{d-1}
when
U
has diagonal type, so

n\geq|S|^{k/2}
in this case.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that
G\leq\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
has a unique minimal normal
N
, where

N=S^{k}
for some nonabelian simple group
S
, and suppose that
N^{\Omega}

is transitive. Let
S
be one of the simple factors of
N
, and let
T
be
the almost simple group
N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S)
. Then
|\Omega|\geq P(T)^{k}
.
Furthermore, there are embeddings
G\to T\wr\mathrm{Sym}(k)\to\mathrm{Sym}(P(T)^{k})
for
which the image of
N
in
\mathrm{Sym}(P(T)^{k})
acts transitively.

Proof.

If
k=1
then
G=T
and the result is clear, so suppose that

k>1
. Since
N^{\Omega}
is transitive, it acts non-trivially on any
block system preserved by
G
on
\Omega
so, by replacing
G
by its action on any such block system, we may assume that
G^{\Omega}
is primitive.
As we saw above, if
G
is of almost simple product type then
n=\ell^{k}
,
where
\ell
is the degree of a primitive permutation representation of an
almost simple group
U
with socle
S
, and otherwise
n=|S|^{t}
for some

t\geq k/2
.
On the other hand, if
T=N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S)
as in the statement of the lemma,
then by
[
2
, Section 3.2]
there is an embedding
G\to T\wr\mathrm{Sym}(k)
,
and the final assertion of the lemma follows by considering the product
action of
T\wr\mathrm{Sym}(k)
using a (necessarily primitive) permutation
representation of
T
of degree
P(T)
.

It is straightforward to check from
[
8
, Table 4]
for simple groups
of Lie type,
[
5
]
for the sporadic groups,
and
[
3
, Proposition 2.2]
for almost simple groups that, for any almost
simple group
U
with socle
S
, we have
P(U)^{2}<|S|
. So a faithful
permutation representation of
G
of smallest possible degree such that
N

acts transitively is primitive of almost simple product type. Furthermore,
given such a representation with
G
embedding into
U\wr\mathrm{Sym}(k)
, the outer
automorphisms of
S
that are induced by elements in
N_{G}(S)/S
must be induced
by elements of
U
, and so we have
T\leq U
, and the result follows.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
T
be an almost simple group with socle
S
and suppose that

G\leq T\wr\mathrm{Sym}(k)
, where
N=S^{k}\leq G
.
Then
P(G/N)\leq k|T/S|\leq 2kP(T)/5
.

Proof.

Since
G/N\leq(T/S)\wr\mathrm{Sym}(k)
, we have
P(G/N)\leq k|T/S|
.
By
[
9
, Lemma 4]
we have
P(S)>3|\mathrm{Out}(S)|
except in the four
cases
(S,P(S),|\mathrm{Out}(S)|)=(A_{6},6,4)
,
(\mathrm{PSL}(3,4),21,12)
,
(A_{5},5,2)
, and

(\mathrm{PSL}(2,8),9,3)
. So, except in those four cases have

k|T/S|\leq k|\mathrm{Out}(S)|<kP(S)/3\leq kP(T)/3
, which proves the theeorem.
In fact we still have
k|T/S|\leq 2kP(T)/5
when
(S,P(S),|\mathrm{Out}(S)|)=(A_{5},5,2)
or
(\mathrm{PSL}(2,8),9,3)
.
When
(S,P(S),|\mathrm{Out}(S)|)=(A_{6},6,4)
we have
\deg(T)\geq 10
when
|T/S|=4

and, when
(S,P(S),|\mathrm{Out}(S)|)=(\mathrm{PSL}(3,4),21,12)
, we have

P(T)\geq 42
when
|T/S|=12
, so the result also holds in these cases.
∎

3
Proof of theorem

We turn now to the proof of the theorem. Assume that the group
G
is acting
(faithfully) on the set
\Omega
with
|\Omega|=n
. We prove the theorem by
induction on
n
, and observe that, when proving the theorem for some
particular value of
n
, we can assume that the corollary holds for all
m<n
.
Let
G
with normal subgroup
N
be a counterexample of minimal degree.

Lemma 3.1
.

G
is transitive on
\Omega
.

Proof.

Since
N
is a minimal normal subgroup of
G
, it acts either
trivially or faithfully on each orbit of
G
.
Suppose that
G
is intransitive on
\Omega
, and let
\Delta
be an
orbit of
G
on
\Omega
on which
N
acts nontrivially, and hence faithfully.
Let
\Gamma=\Omega\setminus\Delta
. By induction there are homomorphisms

\rho_{\Delta}:G^{\Delta}\to\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta^{\prime})
with
\ker\rho_{\Delta}=N^{\Delta}
and

\rho_{\Gamma}:G^{\Gamma}\to\mathrm{Sym}(\Gamma)
with
\ker\rho_{\Gamma}=N^{\Gamma}
,
where
\Delta^{\prime}
is a set with
|\Delta^{\prime}|<|\Delta|
.
Define
\rho:G\to\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta^{\prime}\cup\Gamma)
by

\rho(g):=\rho_{1}(g^{\Delta})\rho_{2}(g^{\Gamma})
.
We claim that
\ker\rho=N
, which contradicts the assumption that
G
with
normal subgroup
N
is a counterexample to the theorem.

Clearly
N\leq\ker\rho
so suppose that
g\in\ker\rho
.
Then
g^{\Delta}\in\ker\rho_{1}=N^{\Delta}
, so there exists

n_{1}\in N
with
g^{\Delta}=n_{1}^{\Delta}
, and then
gn_{1}^{-1}

is in the kernel
K_{1}
of the action of
G
on
\Delta
.
Since
(gn_{1}^{-1})^{\Gamma}\in\ker\rho_{2}=N^{\Gamma}
, there exists

n_{2}\in N
with
gn_{1}^{-1}n_{2}^{-1}
in the kernel
K_{2}
of the action of

G
on
\Gamma
, and we also have

gn_{1}^{-1}n_{2}^{-1}\in K_{1}N=K_{1}\times N
, because
N
acts
faithfully on
\Delta
.
Now, by applying Lemma
2.1
to
K_{1}
,
N
, and

L:=K_{2}\cap K_{1}N
, we find that any simple direct factor of
N

onto which
n_{2}
projects non-trivially lies in
K_{2}
, and so

n_{2}\in K_{2}
and hence
gn_{1}^{-1}\in K_{1}\cap K_{2}=1
,
so
g=n_{1}\in N
. This proves the claim and the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

N
is transitive on
\Omega
.

Proof.

Suppose that
N
is intransitive on
\Omega
. Then
G
is imprimitive on

\Omega
and preserves the block system
\Gamma
, the set of orbits of
N
.
Let
\Delta\in\Gamma
. Then by
[
1
, Theorem 1.8]
there is an
embedding
\alpha:G\to G_{\Delta}^{\Delta}\wr G^{\Gamma}
. Since

N^{\Delta}\unlhd G_{\Delta}^{\Delta}
, there is a natural projection

\pi:G_{\Delta}^{\Delta}\wr G^{\Gamma}\to G_{\Delta}^{\Delta}/N^{\Delta}\wr G^{\Gamma}
.

We claim that the kernel of the composite map

\pi\alpha:G\to G_{\Delta}^{\Delta}/N^{\Delta}\wr G^{\Gamma}
is equal to
N
.
This kernel clearly contains
N
so, for
g\in\ker\pi\alpha
, we need to
show that
g\in N
. Certainly
g
is in the kernel
K
of the induced action
of
G
on
\Gamma
; that is,
g
maps to an element of the base group of the
wreath product. Let
\Gamma=\{\Delta=\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\ldots,\Delta_{t}\}
.
Then
g^{\Delta_{i}}\in N^{\Delta_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq t
.
To prove the claim, we will successively replace
g
by
gn
for elements

n\in N
for
1\leq i\leq t
such that, after the
i
-th such replacement,

g
acts trivially on
\Delta_{1},\cup\cdots\cup\Delta_{i}
.
We can do this for
i=1
because
g^{\Delta_{1}}\in N^{\Delta_{1}}
.

Suppose by induction that
g
acts trivially on

\Delta^{\prime}:=\Delta_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\Delta_{i-1}
for some
i
with

1<i\leq t
, and let
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
be the kernels of the actions of
K

on
\Delta^{\prime}
and
\Delta_{i}
, respectively. So
g\in K_{1}
.

Since
N
is a direct product of nonabelian simple groups that are permuted
under the action of conjugation by elements of
K
, we can write

N=N_{1}\times N_{2}
, where
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are normal in
K
, and the simple
direct factors of
N
in
N_{1}
lie in
K_{1}
, but those of
N_{2}
do not.
So
N_{1}\leq K_{1}
and
K_{1}\cap N_{2}=1
.
We have
g^{\Delta_{i}}=(n_{1}n_{2})^{\Delta_{i}}

for some
n_{1}\in N_{1}
and
n_{2}\in N_{2}
, and then

gn_{1}^{-1}n_{2}^{-1}\in K_{2}\cap K_{1}N_{2}
.
Now, by applying Lemma
2.1
to the normal subgroups
K_{1}
,
N_{2}
,
and
L:=K_{2}\cap K_{1}N_{2}
, we find that all of the factors of
N_{2}
onto which

n_{2}
projects nontrivially lie in
K_{2}
, so
n_{2}\in K_{2}
and hence

gn_{1}^{-1}\in K_{1}\cap K_{2}
,
which completes the induction and the proof of the claim.

Now, by the minimality of
G
and
N
,we can apply Corollary
1.2
to

N^{\Delta}\unlhd G_{\Delta}^{\Delta}
, to find an embedding

\rho:G_{\Delta}^{\Delta}/N^{\Delta}\to\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta^{\prime})

and
|\Delta^{\prime}|\leq 2|\Delta|/5
, which induces an embedding

\bar{\rho}:G_{\Delta}^{\Delta}/N^{\Delta}\wr G^{\Gamma}\to\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega^{\prime})

with
|\Omega^{\prime}|\leq 2|\Omega|/5
.
Then the composite map
\bar{\rho}\pi\alpha:G\to\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega^{\prime})
has
kernel
N
, which proves the lemma.
∎

We have
N\cong S^{k}
for some nonabelian simple group
S
and
k\geq 1
,
where the simple direct factors are permuted transitively under the conjugation
action of
G
. We have
\mathrm{Aut}(N)\cong\mathrm{Aut}(S)\wr\mathrm{Sym}(k)
, and

\mathrm{Out}(N)\cong\mathrm{Out}(S)\wr\mathrm{Sym}(k)
, which embeds into
\mathrm{Sym}(k|\mathrm{Out}(S)|)
.
Let
C=C_{G}(N)
.

Suppose first that
C=1
. Then
N
is the unique minimal normal subgroup
of
G
, and by Lemma
2.5
we have
n\geq|P(T)|^{k}
with

T=N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S)
. Furthermore we have
P(G/N)\leq 2kP(T)/5
by
Lemma
2.6
, and the result follows because
kP(T)\leq P(T)^{k}
.
So we may assume that
|C|>1
.

For
\alpha\in\Omega
let
H:=G_{\alpha}
be a point stabiliser,
so
n=|G:H|
and, since
N^{\Omega}
is transitive, we have
G=NH
.
We have
C_{\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)}(N)\cong N_{N}(H\cap N)/H\cap N

by
[
6
, Theorem 4.2A (i)]
. Since
G=NH
, the simple direct factors of
N

are all conjugate under the conjugation action of
H
, so
the projection of
H\cap N
onto each of the simple direct factors of
N

must be the same. If this projection is equal to
S
, then

N_{N}(H\cap N)=H\cap N
by Lemma
2.2
, so
C=1
and
the result follows as above. On the other hand, if
H\cap N=1
, then

N^{\Omega}
is regular so
n=|N|=|S|^{k}
and, by
[
6
, Theorem 4.2A (iii)]

we have
G/N\leq N_{\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)}(N)/N\cong\mathrm{Aut}(S)\wr\mathrm{Sym}(k)
, so

P(G/N)\leq kP(\mathrm{Aut}(S))<2|S|/5\leq 2n/5
.

So we can assume that the projection of
H\cap N
onto the simple direct
factors of
N
is a nontrivial proper subgroup
R
of
S
and hence

H\cap N\leq R^{k}
. Let
a:=|R^{k}:H\cap N|
,
Q:=N_{S}(R)
, and
b:=|Q:R|
.
Then
N_{N}(H\cap N)\leq Q^{k}
, so
|C|\leq|N_{N}(H\cap N)/H\cap N|\leq ab^{k}
.

Since
H
acting by conjugation permutes the factors of
S^{k}
and
H

normalises
H\cap N
, we have
H\leq N_{G}(R^{k})
and hence
H\leq N_{G}(Q^{k})
.
So
H\leq N_{G}(Q^{k}C)
, and we can choose a maximal subgroup
M
of
G

containing
HQ^{k}C
. Let
\Gamma
be the set of blocks of imprimitivity
of
G
containing the block
\alpha^{M}
. Then, since
N/C
is the unique
minimal normal subgroup of
G/C
, the kernel of the action of
G
on

\Gamma
is equal to
C
and, since the stabiliser of a block contains
Q^{k}
,
we see from Lemma
2.5
and the preceding discussion
of the cases of the O’Nan-Scott Theorem that the induced action
G^{\Gamma}
of

G
on
\Gamma
must be primitive of almost simple product type and contained
in
T\wr\mathrm{Sym}(k)
with product action, where
T\leq\mathrm{Aut}(S)
and

|\Gamma|=\deg(T)^{k}
.

Now, by Lemma
2.4
,
in this action of
T\wr\mathrm{Sym}(k)
, the subgroup
\mathrm{Sym}(k)
has orbits of length

k
on which it acts naturally. Let
D^{\Gamma}
(with
C\leq D\leq G)
be the
intersection of
G^{\Gamma}
with this subgroup. Then
D
acts faithfully
on a union of
k
blocks in
\Gamma
, a set of size
k|M:H|
, and
D

is isomorphic to a subgroup of index at most
|T/S|^{k}
in
G/N
.
So, by Lemma
2.3
, there is a faithful action of
G/N
on a
set of size at most
k|T/S|^{k}|M:H|
.

Since
n=|\Gamma||M:H|
with
|\Gamma|=\deg(T)^{k}\geq P(T)^{k}

and
|T/S|\leq 2P(T)/5
by Lemma

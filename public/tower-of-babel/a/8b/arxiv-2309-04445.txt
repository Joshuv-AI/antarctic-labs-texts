Wold decomposition for isometries with equal range
Satyabrata Majee, Amit Maji

Abstract
. Let n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , and let V = ( V 1 , … , V n ) V=(V_{1},\dots,V_{n}) be an n n -tuple of isometries acting on a Hilbert space ℋ \mathcal{H} . We say that V V is an n n -tuple of isometries with equal range if V i m i ​ V j m j ​ ℋ = V j m j ​ V i m i ​ ℋ V_{i}^{m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}}\mathcal{H}=V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{m_{i}}\mathcal{H} and V i ∗ m i ​ V j m j ​ ℋ = V j m j ​ V i ∗ m i ​ ℋ V_{i}^{*m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}}\mathcal{H}=V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{*m_{i}}\mathcal{H} for m i , m j ∈ ℤ + m_{i},m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+} , where 1 ≤ i < j ≤ n 1\leq i<j\leq n . We prove that each n n -tuple of isometries with equal range admits a unique Wold decomposition . We obtain analytic models of the above class, and as a consequence, we show that the wandering data are complete unitary invariants for n n -tuples of isometries with equal range . Our results unify all prior findings on the decomposition for tuples of isometries in the existing literature.

1.
Introduction

One of the key problems in operator theory, operator algebras, and analytic function theory is the classification and representation of
n
-tuples (
n\geq 2
) of commuting isometries acting on Hilbert spaces. The foundation of this study is the complete and explicit structure theory of a single isometry. It says that each isometry is the direct sum of a unitary operator and copies of the unilateral shift, and we shall refer to this classical decomposition as the
Wold decomposition or Wold-von Neumann decomposition
(see details in Section 2). The aim of this paper is to obtain
Wold decomposition
for a large class of
n
-tuples of isometries (not necessarily commuting or twisted) on Hilbert spaces.

Wold decomposition
plays a crucial role in many areas of operator algebras and operator theory, namely, dilation theory, invariant subspace theory, operator model
[
13
]
; and also prediction theory
[
4
]
, time series analysis
[
8
]
, stochastic process
[
9
]
etc. Thus, it is a natural query to ask whether Wold’s model can be extended to several variables. Nevertheless, this generalization is not simple, and for example, in the case of commuting isometries, the structure is quite complex and mainly unknown. There has been a lot of research in this area over the past few decades, and a variety of significant and intriguing findings have been made. The structure of an
n
-tuple (
n\geq 2
) of commuting isometries on a Hilbert space is still not completely known. Many researchers investigated Wold-type decomposition for a pair or family of commuting isometries, like, Suciu
[
16
]
developed a structure theory for a semigroup of isometries. Later, Słoćinski
[
15
]
obtained a Wold-type decomposition for pairs of doubly commuting isometries from Suciu’s decomposition of the semigroup of isometries and Popovici
[
10
]
studied the Wold-type decomposition for pairs of commuting isometries. Recently, Jeu and Pinto
[
5
]
established
Wold decomposition
for
n
-tuple (
n\geq 2
) of doubly non-commuting isometries, that is, an
n
-tuple of isometries
(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
satisfying the following conditions:
For all
i,j
with
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
z_{ij}\in S^{1}:=\{z_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}:|z_{ij}|=1\}
are given such that
z_{ji}=\overline{z}_{ij}
for all
i\neq j
,
and

V_{i}V_{j}={z}_{ij}V_{j}V_{i}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{and}\hskip 28.45274ptV_{i}^{*}V_{j}=\overline{z}_{ij}V_{j}V_{i}^{*}

for all
i\neq j
. An
n
-tuple of isometries
(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}

becomes doubly commuting if
z_{ij}=1
for all
i,j
and a
\mathcal{U}_{n}
-twisted isometry if
z_{ij}
’s are replaced by unitaries
U_{ij}
, where each
V_{k}
is in the commutant of
U_{ij}
for
i\neq j
and
k=1,\ldots,n
.
Inspiring from the
Wold-von Neumann decomposition
for a single isometry, Rakshit, Sarkar, and Suryawanshi
[
11
]
formulated the following definition for
n
-tuples of isometries:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
V=(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
be any
n
-tuple of isometries on a Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}
. Then we call that the
n
-tuple
V
admits
Wold-von Neumann decomposition
(or
Wold decomposition
in short)
if there exist
2^{n}
-closed subspaces
\{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}
of

\mathcal{H}
(including trivial subspaces) such that

(a)

\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
reduces each isometry
V_{i}
for
i=1,2,\dots,n
and
\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
,

(b)

\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
,

(c)

for each
\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}
,
V_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}

is a shift for
i\in\Lambda
, and
V_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
is unitary for

j\in I_{n}\setminus\Lambda
.

The authors showed that each
\mathcal{U}_{n}
-twisted isometry admits
Wold decomposition
in the above sense, and also obtained analytic models of
\mathcal{U}_{n}
-twisted isometries.

Let
n\geq 2
and
V=(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that

V_{i}^{m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}}\mathcal{H}=V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{m_{i}}\mathcal{H}
and
V_{i}^{*m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}}\mathcal{H}=V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{*m_{i}}\mathcal{H}
for
m_{i},m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
1\leq i<j\leq n
.
We shall refer
V=(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
as an
n
-tuple of
isometries with equal range
. Clearly,
n
-tuples
(n\geq 2)
of doubly non-commuting isometries and
\mathcal{U}_{n}
-twisted isometries are
n
-tuples of
isometries with equal range
. One of the central results of this paper is that each
n
-tuple of
isometries with equal range
admits
Wold-von Neumann decomposition
. Indeed, we have the following result (see Section 5 below):

Theorem
.

Let
V=(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
be an
n
-tuple (
n\geq 2
) of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that
V_{i}^{m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}}\mathcal{H}=V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{m_{i}}\mathcal{H}
and
V_{i}^{*m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}}\mathcal{H}=V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{*m_{i}}\mathcal{H}
for
m_{i},m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Then there exist
2^{n}
joint
V
-reducing subspaces
\{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}:\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}\}
(counting the trivial subspace
\{0\}
) such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda},

where, for
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\neq\{0\}
,
V_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
is unitary if

j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
and
V_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
is a shift if
i\in\Lambda
. Moreover, for each

\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\bigoplus_{{\bm{p}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{|\Lambda|}}V_{{\Lambda}}^{\bm{p}}\bigg(\bigcap_{{\bm{q}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{{n-|\Lambda|}}}V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}\ \mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\bigg),

and the above decomposition is unique.

It is important to note that our results, restricted to the tuples of doubly commuting or
\mathcal{U}_{n}
-twisted isometries, recover the classification results. Also, orthogonal decomposition for the non-commuting isometries will be fruitful as applications to the
C^{*}
-algebra context, see
[
6
]
,
[
18
]
.

The outline of this article is organized as follows: In Section 2, we discuss some basic definitions and the classical
Wold-von Neumann decomposition
for a single isometry. We also supplement some fundamental results that, although simple, are essential to determining the
Wold decomposition
for tuples of isometries.
In Section 3, we present some important auxiliary results and the general structure of
n
-tuples of isometries. Section 4 is concerned with the
Wold decomposition
for pairs of
isometries with equal range
. Section 5 is devoted to the
Wold decomposition
for
n
-tuple of
isometries with equal range
. A concrete analytic model for an
n
-tuple of
isometries with equal range
has been presented in Section 6. Finally, in Section 7 we obtain that the wandering data are complete unitary invariants for
n
-tuples of
isometries with equal range
. Our findings are more broadly applicable as they cover a large class of tuples of isometries, and on the other hand, our method is new and notably distinct from earlier research.

2.
Notations and Preparatory results

In this section, we recall some basic definitions and some fundamental results that will be used frequently in the sequel.

Throughout this article,
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
denotes the set of all non-negative integers,
\mathcal{H}
denotes a separable Hilbert space over complex field,
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is the
C^{*}
-algebra of all bounded linear operators (operators in short) on
\mathcal{H}
, and
I_{\mathcal{H}}
stands for an identity operator on
\mathcal{H}
. A subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathcal{H}
is invariant under

V\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
if
V(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{M}
and subspace

\mathcal{M}
reduces
V
if
V(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{M}
and

V(\mathcal{M}^{\perp})\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{\perp}
. We denote
\mathcal{N}(V)

and
\mathcal{R}(V)
as the kernel of
V
and range of
V
, respectively. We shall frequently use the basic properties of an isometry
V
(that is,
V^{*}V=I_{\mathcal{H}}
) on
\mathcal{H}
namely,
V^{*}\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{R}(V)=\mathcal{N}(V^{*})^{\perp}
. A
wandering subspace
of an isometry
V
is a closed subspace
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
such that

V^{k}\mathcal{W}\perp V^{l}\mathcal{W}~~\ \text{for all}\ \ {k,l}\ \in\ \mathbb{Z}_{+}\ \text{with}\ k\neq l.

An isometry
V
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to be a
unilateral shift
or shift
if
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}V^{m}\mathcal{W}
for
some wandering subspace
\mathcal{W}
of
V
; equivalently, an isometry

V
is called a shift if
V^{*m}\rightarrow 0
as
m\rightarrow\infty

in the strong operator topology (see Halmos
[
3
]
). If
\mathcal{W}

is a
wandering subspace
of a shift
V
on
\mathcal{H}
, then

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{H}\ominus V\mathcal{H}

and the multiplicity of the shift
V
is defined as the dimension of
\mathcal{W}

(see
[
13
]
).

One of the fundamental results in classification theory is the
Wold-von Neumann decomposition
of an isometry on a Hilbert space due to H. Wold
[
19
]
and J. von-Neumann
[
17
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
V
be an isometry acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{H}\ominus V\mathcal{H}
.
Then
\mathcal{H}
decomposes uniquely as a direct sum of two
V
-reducing subspaces

\mathcal{H}_{s}=\displaystyle{\mathop{\oplus}_{m=0}^{\infty}}V^{m}\mathcal{W}
and

\mathcal{H}_{u}=\mathcal{H}\ominus\mathcal{H}_{s}=\displaystyle\cap_{m=0}^{\infty}V^{m}\mathcal{H}
and

V=\begin{bmatrix}V_{s}&O\\
O&V_{u}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{s}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{u}),

where
V_{s}=V|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}
is a shift and
V_{u}=V|_{\mathcal{H}_{u}}
is unitary.

Before moving on to the main context, we need the following simple and fundamental results:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(V,W)
be a pair of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{R}(V)=\mathcal{R}(W)
. Then
VV^{*}=WW^{*}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
(V,W)
is a pair of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that

\mathcal{R}(V)=\mathcal{R}(W)
. Now
[\mathcal{R}(V)]^{\perp}=[\mathcal{R}(W)]^{\perp}
implies

\mathcal{N}(V^{*})=\mathcal{N}(W^{*})
. Also
\mathcal{R}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-VV^{*})=\mathcal{N}(V^{*})
gives

I_{\mathcal{H}}-VV^{*}=P_{\mathcal{N}(V^{*})}
. Therefore,

VV^{*}=I_{\mathcal{H}}-P_{\mathcal{N}(V^{*})}=I_{\mathcal{H}}-P_{\mathcal{N}(W^{*})}=WW^{*}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(V,W)
be a pair of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{R}(V)=\mathcal{R}(W)
. Then
there exists a unique unitary operator
U\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that
V=WU
.

Proof.

Suppose
(V,W)
is a pair of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that
V\mathcal{H}=W\mathcal{H}
.
Then for each
x\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists a unique
y\in\mathcal{H}
such that
Vx=Wy
.
Now define a map
U:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
as
Ux=y
. Clearly,
U
is well-defined and linear on
\mathcal{H}
. Thus

Vx=WUx\quad(x\in\mathcal{H}).

Since
V
and
W
are isometries on
\mathcal{H}
, for each
x\in\mathcal{H}

\|Ux\|=\|WUx\|=\|Vx\|=\|x\|.

Therefore,
U
is an isometry and it is also easy to see that
U
is onto. Thus
U

is a unitary operator on
\mathcal{H}
such that
Vx=WUx
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
. Hence
V=WU
.

Suppose that there exist two unitaries
U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that
V=WU_{1}
and
V=WU_{2}
. Then
U_{1}=W^{*}V=U_{2}
. This completes the proof.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(V,W)
be a pair of isometries acting on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{R}(V^{m}W^{n})=\mathcal{R}(W^{n}V^{m})
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. If
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
reduces both the isometries
V
and
W
, then
V^{m}W^{n}\mathcal{M}=W^{n}V^{m}\mathcal{M}
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
reduces both the isometries
V
and
W
. Then

V|_{\mathcal{M}}
and
W|_{\mathcal{M}}
are isometries on
\mathcal{M}
. Since
\mathcal{R}(V^{m}W^{n})=\mathcal{R}(W^{n}V^{m})
, using Lemma
2.2
we obtain

V^{m}W^{n}W^{*n}V^{*m}=W^{n}V^{m}V^{*m}W^{*n}~~\mbox{for all}~~m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Therefore,

V^{m}W^{n}\mathcal{M}=V^{m}W^{n}W^{*n}V^{*m}\mathcal{M}=W^{n}V^{m}V^{*m}W^{*n}\mathcal{M}=W^{n}V^{m}\mathcal{M}

for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(V,W)
be a pair of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
with
\mathcal{R}(V^{m}W)=\mathcal{R}(WV^{m})
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. If
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{u}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{s}
is the Wold decomposition for the isometry
V
, then
\mathcal{H}_{u}
is invariant under
W
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{u}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{s}

is the
Wold decomposition
for the isometry
V
, where
\mathcal{H}_{u}=\displaystyle\mathop{\cap}_{m=0}^{\infty}V^{m}\mathcal{H}
. Since
W
is an isometry
on
\mathcal{H}
and
V^{m}W\mathcal{H}=WV^{m}\mathcal{H}
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we have

W\mathcal{H}_{u}=W\displaystyle\big(\mathop{\cap}_{m=0}^{\infty}V^{m}\mathcal{H}\big)=\displaystyle\mathop{\cap}_{m=0}^{\infty}WV^{m}\mathcal{H}=\displaystyle\mathop{\cap}_{m=0}^{\infty}V^{m}W\mathcal{H}\subseteq\displaystyle\mathop{\cap}_{m=0}^{\infty}V^{m}\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{u}.

It follows that
\mathcal{H}_{u}
is
W
-invariant subspace.

The following is an immediate result of the aforementioned lemma:

Corollary 2.6
.

Suppose
(V,W)
is a pair of isometries acting on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{R}(WV^{m})=\mathcal{R}(V^{m}W)
and
\mathcal{R}(W^{*}V^{m})=\mathcal{R}(V^{m}W^{*})
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. If
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{u}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{s}
is the Wold decomposition for
V
, then both the subspaces

\mathcal{H}_{u}
and
\mathcal{H}_{s}
reduce
W
.

The following results will be useful to determine the
Wold decomposition

for tuples of isometries:

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(V,W)
be a pair of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{R}(V^{m}W^{n})=\mathcal{R}(W^{n}V^{m})
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Suppose
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
reduces both the isometries
V
and
W
. If either
V|_{\mathcal{M}}
or
W|_{\mathcal{M}}
is unitary, then

V^{*m}W^{n}\mathcal{M}=W^{n}V^{*m}\mathcal{M}
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Proof.

Assume that
(V,W)
is a pair of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such
that
\mathcal{R}(V^{m}W^{n})=\mathcal{R}(W^{n}V^{m})
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
Now using Lemma
2.2
, we have

V^{m}W^{n}W^{*n}V^{*m}=W^{n}V^{m}V^{*m}W^{*n}\mbox{~~for}~m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

If
V|_{\mathcal{M}}
is unitary, then for each
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we have

W^{n}V^{*m}\mathcal{M}=W^{n}W^{*n}V^{*m}\mathcal{M}=V^{*m}V^{m}W^{n}W^{*n}V^{*m}\mathcal{M}=V^{*m}W^{n}V^{m}V^{*m}W^{*n}\mathcal{M}=V^{*m}W^{n}\mathcal{M}.

On the other hand if
W|_{\mathcal{M}}
is unitary, then clearly

V^{*m}W^{n}\mathcal{M}=V^{*m}\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}=W^{n}\mathcal{M}=W^{n}V^{*m}\mathcal{M}\mbox{~~~for all}~m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

This completes the proof.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(V,W)
be a pair of isometries acting on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{R}(V^{m}W^{n})=\mathcal{R}(W^{n}V^{m})
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{R}(V^{*m}W^{n})=\mathcal{R}(W^{n}V^{*m})
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{R}(W^{*n}V^{m})=\mathcal{R}(V^{m}W^{*n})
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
:
Suppose that
V^{*m}W^{n}\mathcal{H}=W^{n}V^{*m}\mathcal{H}
for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Then

\mathcal{R}(V^{*m}W^{n})
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, and hence
\mathcal{R}(W^{*n}V^{m})
is
also closed. Therefore,

V^{m}W^{*n}\mathcal{H}=W^{*n}W^{n}V^{m}\mathcal{H}=W^{*n}V^{m}W^{n}V^{*m}\mathcal{H}=W^{*n}V^{m}V^{*m}W^{n}\mathcal{H}=W^{*n}V^{m}\mathcal{H}.

The converse part follows in the same line.

The next result is more inclusive than the last one.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(V,W)
be a pair of isometries acting on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{R}(V^{m}W^{n})=\mathcal{R}(W^{n}V^{m})
and
\mathcal{R}(V^{*m}W^{n})=\mathcal{R}(W^{n}V^{*m})
for all

m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. If
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
reduces both the
isometries
V
and
W
, then
V^{*m}W^{n}\mathcal{M}=W^{n}V^{*m}\mathcal{M}

for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{M}
is
V,W
reducing subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, then by Lemma
2.4
,
V^{m}W^{n}\mathcal{M}=W^{n}V^{m}\mathcal{M}
. Again
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{H}_{u}\cap\mathcal{M})\oplus(\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M})
, where
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{u}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{s}
is the Wold decomposition for
V
. Now Corollary
2.6
yields that
\mathcal{H}_{u}\cap\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M}
reduce both the operators
V
and
W
. Let
\eta\in\mathcal{M}
. Then
\eta=h_{u}\oplus h_{s}
, where
h_{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{u}\cap\mathcal{M}
and
h_{s}\in\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M}
. Since
V|_{\mathcal{H}_{u}\cap\mathcal{M}}
is unitary, it follows that

V^{*m}W^{n}(\mathcal{H}_{u}\cap\mathcal{M})=W^{n}V^{*m}(\mathcal{H}_{u}\cap\mathcal{M})
. On the other hand,

V|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M}}
is shift and hence
h_{s}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}V^{k}\eta_{k}
, where
\eta_{k}\in\mathcal{N}(V^{*}|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M}})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M}
. Consequently, for each fixed
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle V^{*m}W^{n}h_{s}=V^{*m}W^{n}\big(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}V^{k}\eta_{k}\big)

\displaystyle=V^{*m}W^{n}\big(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m-1}V^{k}\eta_{k}\big)+V^{*m}W^{n}\big(\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}V^{k}\eta_{k}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m-1}V^{*m}W^{n}V^{k}\eta_{k}+V^{*m}W^{n}\big(\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}V^{k}\eta_{k}\big).

Since
\mathcal{R}(V^{*m}W^{n})=\mathcal{R}(W^{n}V^{*m})
and
\mathcal{R}(W^{*n}V^{m})=\mathcal{R}(V^{m}W^{*n})
are equivalent by Lemma
2.8
, for each
h\in\mathcal{H}
there exists
h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
such that
W^{*n}V^{m}h=V^{m}W^{*n}h^{\prime}
. Now for each
h\in\mathcal{H}
and for
\eta_{k}\in\mathcal{N}(V^{*}|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M}})
, where
0\leq k\leq m-1
, we have

\displaystyle\langle V^{*m}W^{n}V^{k}\eta_{k},h\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle W^{n}V^{k}\eta_{k},V^{m}h\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle V^{k}\eta_{k},W^{*n}V^{m}h\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle V^{k}\eta_{k},V^{m}W^{*n}h^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\eta_{k},V^{m-k}W^{*n}h^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle V^{*(m-k)}\eta_{k},W^{*n}h^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

It follows that
V^{*m}W^{n}V^{k}\eta_{k}=0
for
0\leq k\leq m-1
. Hence

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m-1}V^{*m}W^{n}V^{k}\eta_{k}=0
.

Now
V^{m},W^{n}
are isometries on
\mathcal{H}
and
V^{m}W^{n}\mathcal{H}=W^{n}V^{m}\mathcal{H}
for each

m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Using Lemma
2.2
, we have

V^{m}W^{n}W^{*n}V^{*m}=W^{n}V^{m}V^{*m}W^{*n}
. Then

\displaystyle W^{*n}V^{*m}

\displaystyle=W^{*n}V^{*m}W^{n}V^{m}V^{*m}W^{*n}

\displaystyle\mbox{and~~}W^{n}V^{m}

\displaystyle=V^{m}W^{n}W^{*n}V^{*m}W^{n}V^{m}.

Now using this, we have

\displaystyle W^{n}V^{*m}(\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=W^{n}W^{*n}V^{*m}(\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=W^{n}W^{*n}V^{*m}W^{n}V^{m}V^{*m}W^{*n}(\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=W^{n}W^{*n}V^{*m}W^{n}V^{m}V^{*m}(\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M}).

Thus for each
h_{s}\in\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M}
, there exists some
h_{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M}
such that

W^{n}W^{*n}V^{*m}W^{n}V^{m}V^{*m}h_{s}=W^{n}V^{*m}h_{s}^{\prime}.

Now

\displaystyle V^{*m}W^{n}h_{s}=V^{*m}W^{n}\big(\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}V^{k}\eta_{k}\big)

\displaystyle=V^{*m}W^{n}V^{m}\big(\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}V^{k-m}\eta_{k}\big)

\displaystyle=V^{*m}V^{m}W^{n}W^{*n}V^{*m}W^{n}V^{m}\big(\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}V^{k-m}\eta_{k}\big)

\displaystyle=W^{n}W^{*n}V^{*m}W^{n}V^{m}V^{*m}\big(\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}V^{k}\eta_{k}\big)

\displaystyle=W^{n}W^{*n}V^{*m}W^{n}V^{m}V^{*m}(h_{s}-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m-1}V^{k}\eta_{k})

\displaystyle=W^{n}V^{*m}h_{s}^{\prime}

as
\eta_{k}\in\mathcal{N}(V^{*}|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M}})
. Therefore,

V^{*m}W^{n}\eta=V^{*m}W^{n}h_{u}+V^{*m}W^{n}h_{s}=W^{n}V^{*m}h_{u}^{\prime}+W^{n}V^{*m}h_{s}^{\prime}=W^{n}V^{*m}\eta^{\prime},

where
\eta^{\prime}=h_{u}^{\prime}\oplus h_{s}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}
. Hence
V^{*m}W^{n}\mathcal{M}=W^{n}V^{*m}\mathcal{M}

for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. This completes the proof.

Now we shall give some concrete examples of pairs of isometries

(V_{1},V_{2})
on Hilbert spaces satisfying certain properties.

Example 2.10
.

Let
(V_{1},V_{2})
be a pair of isometries on a Hilbert space

\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})
defined by

\displaystyle V_{1}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)

\displaystyle=(0,x_{0},x_{1},\dots),

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad V_{2}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)

\displaystyle=(0,\alpha_{0}{x_{0}},\alpha_{1}{x_{1}},\dots)

where
(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})
and
|\alpha_{i}|=1

for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Then for each
(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})

\displaystyle V_{1}V_{2}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)

\displaystyle=V_{1}(0,\alpha_{0}{x_{0}},\alpha_{1}{x_{1}},\dots)=(0,0,\alpha_{0}{x_{0}},\alpha_{1}{x_{1}},\dots),

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad V_{2}V_{1}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)

\displaystyle=V_{2}(0,x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)=(0,0,\alpha_{1}{x_{0}},\alpha_{2}{x_{1}},\dots).

Therefore, the pair of isometries
(V_{1},V_{2})
is neither commuting nor twisted.
Again

\displaystyle V_{1}V_{2}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)

\displaystyle=(0,0,\alpha_{0}{x_{0}},\alpha_{1}{x_{1}},\dots)

\displaystyle=V_{2}V_{1}(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\alpha_{1}}{x_{0}},\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}{x_{1}},\dots)

\displaystyle=V_{2}V_{1}(y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},\dots),

where
(y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},\dots)=(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\alpha_{1}}{x_{0}},\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}{x_{1}},\dots)\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})
, that means,
\mathcal{R}(V_{1}V_{2})=\mathcal{R}(V_{2}V_{1})
.
Also

\displaystyle V_{2}V_{1}^{*}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)

\displaystyle=(0,\alpha_{0}x_{1},\alpha_{1}x_{2},\dots),

\displaystyle\mbox{and~~}V_{1}^{*}V_{2}((y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},\dots)

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{0}y_{0},\alpha_{1}y_{1},\dots).

Hence the pair of isometries
(V_{1},V_{2})
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})
is neither
doubly commuting nor doubly twisted. However,
\mathcal{R}(V_{1}V_{2})=\mathcal{R}(V_{2}V_{1})
but

\mathcal{R}(V_{1}^{*}V_{2})\neq\mathcal{R}(V_{2}V_{1}^{*})
.

Example 2.11
.

Let
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
denotes the Hardy space over the open unit disc

\mathbb{D}
. The Hardy space over the bidisc
\mathbb{D}^{2}
, denoted by
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
, can be identified with
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\otimes H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
through the canonical unitary
z^{n_{1}}\otimes z^{n_{2}}\mapsto z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}
for
(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}
. The multiplication operator
M_{z_{i}}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
by the co-ordinate function
z_{i}
on
\mathbb{D}
is defined by
M_{z_{i}}f=z_{i}f
for
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
and
i=1,2
.

For each sequence
\bm{r}=(r_{n})\subset S^{1}
, define a map
\Delta[{\bm{r}}]
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
as

\Delta[{\bm{r}}]z^{n}={r_{n}}z^{n}\hskip 28.45274pt(n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}).

Then
\Delta[{\bm{r}}]
is a unitary diagonal operator. Now define
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
as

V_{1}=M_{z}\otimes I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\ \ \ \text{and}\quad V_{2}=\Delta[{\bm{r}}]\otimes M_{z}.

Clearly,
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a pair of isometries on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
.
Then for
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

V_{1}^{p}=M_{z}^{p}\otimes I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\ \ \ \text{and}\quad V_{2}^{q}=\Delta[{\bm{r}}]^{q}\otimes M_{z}^{q}.

Now

\displaystyle V_{1}^{p}V_{2}^{q}(z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}})

\displaystyle=(M_{z}^{p}\Delta[{\bm{r}}]^{q}\otimes M_{z}^{q})(z^{n_{1}}\otimes z^{n_{2}})

\displaystyle=r_{n_{1}}^{q}z^{n_{1}+p}\otimes z^{n_{2}+q}

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{n_{1}}^{q}}{r_{n_{1}+p}^{q}}(r_{n_{1}+p}^{q}z^{n_{1}+p}\otimes z^{n_{2}+q})

\displaystyle=(\Delta[{\bm{r}}]^{q}M_{z}^{p}\otimes M_{z}^{q})\big(\frac{r_{n_{1}}^{q}}{r_{n_{1}+p}^{q}}(z^{n_{1}}\otimes z^{n_{2}})\big)

\displaystyle=V_{2}^{q}V_{1}^{p}(\frac{r_{n_{1}}^{q}}{r_{n_{1}+p}^{q}}z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}).

Also

\displaystyle V_{1}^{*p}V_{2}^{q}(z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}})

\displaystyle=(M_{z}^{*p}\Delta[{\bm{r}}]^{q}\otimes M_{z}^{q})(z^{n_{1}}\otimes z^{n_{2}})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}{r}_{n_{1}}^{q}z^{n_{1}-p}\otimes z^{n_{2}+q}&\text{if $n_{1}\geq p$},\\
0&\text{if $n_{1}<p$}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{{r}_{n_{1}}^{q}}{{r}_{n_{1}-p}^{q}}({r}_{n_{1}-p}^{q}z^{n_{1}-p}\otimes z^{n_{2}+q})&\text{if $n_{1}\geq p$},\\
0&\text{if $n_{1}<p$}\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle=(\Delta[{\bm{r}}]^{q}M_{z}^{*p}\otimes M_{z}^{q})\big(\frac{{r}_{n_{1}}^{q}}{{r}_{n_{1}-p}^{q}}(z^{n_{1}}\otimes z^{n_{2}})\big)

\displaystyle=V_{2}^{q}V_{1}^{*p}(\frac{{r}_{n_{1}}^{q}}{{r}_{n_{1}-p}^{q}}z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}).

Therefore,
(V_{1},V_{2})
is neither commuting nor twisted, but

\mathcal{R}(V_{1}^{p}V_{2}^{q})=\mathcal{R}(V_{2}^{q}V_{1}^{p})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{1}^{*p}V_{2}^{q})=\mathcal{R}(V_{2}^{q}V_{1}^{*p})
for all
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, that is,
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a pair of
isometries with equal range
.

3.
Structure for
n
-tuple of isometries

In this section, we explore some important supplementary results for
n
-tuples of isometries satisfying certain conditions and also establish the structure of decomposition spaces for
n
-tuple of isometries
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
satisfying
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{m_{i}})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{*m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{*m_{i}})
for
m_{i},m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
1\leq i<j\leq n
.

In what follows, the notations below will be fixed for the several variables case.
We assume
n\geq 2
is a positive integer. For a given integer
k
with
1\leq k\leq n
, we denote the set
\{1,2,\dots,k\}
by
I_{k}
. Let
\Lambda=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{l}\}\subseteq I_{n}
for
1\leq l\leq n
,
I_{n}\backslash\Lambda=\{i_{l+1}<\dots<i_{n}\}
. The cardinality
of the set
\Lambda
is denoted by
|\Lambda|
. We denote by
V_{\Lambda}
the

|\Lambda|
-tuple of isometries
(V_{i_{1}},\dots,V_{i_{l}})
and
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{|\Lambda|}:=\{{\bf{m}}=(m_{i_{1}},\dots,m_{i_{l}}):m_{i_{j}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},1\leq j\leq l\}
. Also
V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{m_{i_{l}}}
is denoted by
V_{\Lambda}^{\bf m}
for
{\bf m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\Lambda}
, and
e_{j}
denotes the element in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
with
1
in the
j
th position and zero elsewhere. Before proceeding further, we introduce one more notation for the rest of the paper: if
\Lambda\subseteq I_{k}
and
q\notin I_{k}
, then
\widetilde{\Lambda}
is denoted by same
\Lambda
but we will treat
\widetilde{\Lambda}
as a subset of
I_{k}\cup\{q\}
.

We define
\mathcal{W}_{i}=\mathcal{N}(V_{i}^{*})
for each
1\leq i\leq n
and

\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}(V)=\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}:=\bigcap_{i\in\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{i},

where
\Lambda
is a non-empty subset of
I_{k}
for
1\leq k\leq n
,
and
\mathcal{W}_{\emptyset}=\mathcal{H}
.

Now we list some important results which will be used throughout the sequel.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of isometries on
\mathcal{H}

such that
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}V_{j})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}V_{i})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{*}V_{j})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}V_{i}^{*})

for
1\leq i<j\leq n
. If
\Lambda
is a subset of
I_{n}
, then
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

reduces
V_{j}
for each
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
.

Proof.

Since
V_{i}V_{j}\mathcal{H}=V_{j}V_{i}\mathcal{H}
and
V_{i}V_{j}^{*}\mathcal{H}=V_{j}^{*}V_{i}\mathcal{H}
, for every

h\in\mathcal{H}
there exist
h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
such that
V_{j}V_{i}h=V_{i}V_{j}h^{\prime}

and
V_{j}^{*}V_{i}h=V_{i}V_{j}^{*}h^{\prime\prime}
, respectively.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=\bigcap\limits_{i\in\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{i}
.
For each
h\in\mathcal{H}
and for
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
,
we have

\displaystyle\langle V_{j}^{*}\eta,V_{i}h\rangle=\langle\eta,V_{j}V_{i}h\rangle=\langle\eta,V_{i}V_{j}h^{\prime}\rangle=\langle V_{i}^{*}\eta,V_{j}h^{\prime}\rangle=0

and

\displaystyle\langle V_{j}\eta,V_{i}h\rangle=\langle\eta,V_{j}^{*}V_{i}h\rangle=\langle\eta,V_{i}V_{j}^{*}h^{\prime\prime}\rangle=\langle V_{i}^{*}\eta,V_{j}^{*}h^{\prime\prime}\rangle=0.

Therefore, for each fixed
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
, we obtain

V_{j}^{*}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda},V_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\subseteq(V_{i}\mathcal{H})^{\perp}=\mathcal{W}_{i}

for all
i\in\Lambda
. Hence
V_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

and
V_{j}^{*}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
for
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that

\mathcal{R}(V_{i}V_{j})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}V_{i})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{*}V_{j})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}V_{i}^{*})
for
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Then for each subset
\Lambda
of
I_{n}
and
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
,

\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\ominus V_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=\big(\bigcap_{i\in\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{i}\big)\cap\mathcal{W}_{j}.

Proof.

Suppose
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
. Following Proposition
3.1
, we have
V_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
and
V_{j}^{*}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
. Thus
V_{j}|_{\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}}
is an isometry on
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
and
\mathcal{N}(V_{j}^{*}|_{\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}})=\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\ominus V_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\cap[V_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}]^{\perp}
.
Suppose that
\eta\in\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\ominus V_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
; then
\eta\in\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
and
\eta\perp V_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
. It follows
V_{j}^{*}\eta\perp\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
. But
V_{j}^{*}\eta\in\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
as
\eta\in\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
and

V_{j}^{*}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
. Therefore,
V_{j}^{*}\eta=0
, and hence
\eta\in\mathcal{W}_{j}
. As a consequence
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\ominus V_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\cap\mathcal{W}_{j}
.

Now we shall prove the converse inclusion. To do that, let

\eta\in\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\cap\mathcal{W}_{j}
for
j\in I_{n}\setminus\Lambda
. Then,
for each
\eta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
, we get

\langle\eta,V_{j}\eta^{\prime}\rangle=\langle V_{j}^{*}\eta,\eta^{\prime}\rangle=0.

Therefore,
\eta\in\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\cap[V_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}]^{\perp}
, that means,

\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\cap\mathcal{W}_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\ominus V_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
.
Hence, for each
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
,

\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\ominus V_{j}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\cap\mathcal{W}_{j}=\big(\bigcap_{i\in\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{i}\big)\cap\mathcal{W}_{j}.

This finishes the proof.

The structure of the decomposition spaces for an
n
-tuple of isometries is obtained in the following result, which will be applied in the subsequent sections. It is also important to note that similar result was also observed by firstly Gaspar and Suciu
[
2
]
in the case of commuting tuples of isometries.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of isometries on
\mathcal{H}

such that
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{m_{i}})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{*m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{*m_{i}})
for
m_{i},m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Then there exist
2^{n}
joint
V
-reducing subspaces
\{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}:\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}\}
(counting the trivial subspace
\{0\}
) such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}

where for each
\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\big(\bigcap_{i\in\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{si}\big)\cap\big(\bigcap_{j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{uj}\big),

where
\mathcal{H}_{si}
and
\mathcal{H}_{ui}
are the shift part and unitary part in the Wold decomposition for each
V_{i}
. Also for
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\neq\{0\}
,
V_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
is a shift if
i\in\Lambda
and
V_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
is unitary if
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
. Moreover, the above decomposition is unique.

Proof.

Assume that
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
is an
n
-tuple of isometries on
\mathcal{H}

such that
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{m_{i}})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{*m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{*m_{i}})
for
m_{i},m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Suppose
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{ui}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{si}
is the
Wold decomposition
for each isometry
V_{i}
such that
V_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}_{ui}}
is unitary and
V_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}_{si}}
is shift for
i=1,2,\dots,n
. Now the subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{ui}
and
\mathcal{H}_{si}
reduce the isometries
V_{k}
for each
i,k=1,2,\dots,n
by Corollary
2.6
and Lemma
2.8
.

For each
\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}
, we define

\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\big(\bigcap_{i\in\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{si}\big)\cap\big(\bigcap_{j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{uj}\big).

Clearly,
\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
. Thus
it is enough to show that

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{u1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{s1}\subseteq\bigoplus_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{ui},\mathcal{H}_{si}
reduce
V_{k}
, the space
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
reduces
V_{k}
for
k=1,2,\dots,n
. Also
\mathcal{N}(V_{k}^{*}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}})=\mathcal{N}(V_{k}^{*})\cap\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
. Therefore, by Wold decomposition for the isometry
V_{k}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=[\bigcap_{m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{k}^{m_{k}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}]\oplus[\bigoplus_{m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{k}^{m_{k}}(\mathcal{N}(V_{k}^{*})\cap\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda})].

Let
\Lambda\subseteq I_{p}\subsetneq I_{n}
and
k\notin I_{p}
. It is easy to see that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\bigcap_{m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{k}^{m_{k}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\subseteq(\bigcap_{m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{k}^{m_{k}}\mathcal{H})\cap\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{H}_{uk}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\Lambda}},

where
\tilde{\Lambda}=\Lambda
treated as a subset of
I_{p}\cup\{k\}
. Again for each

m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

V_{k}^{m_{k}}(\mathcal{N}(V_{k}^{*})\cap\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda})\subseteq V_{k}^{m_{k}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}

and

\bigoplus_{m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{k}^{m_{k}}(\mathcal{N}(V_{k}^{*})\cap\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda})\subseteq\bigoplus_{m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{k}^{m_{k}}\mathcal{N}(V_{k}^{*})=\mathcal{H}_{sk}.

The above two inclusions give

(3.3)

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{k}^{m_{k}}(\mathcal{N}(V_{k}^{*})\cap\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\cap\mathcal{H}_{sk}=\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\Lambda}\cup\{k\}}.

Therefore, equations (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) imply that
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\Lambda}\cup\{k\}}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}
.

In the similar way, for
\Lambda\subseteq I_{p}\subsetneq I_{n}
with
k,l\notin I_{p}
, we have

\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\Lambda}\cup\{k\}\cup\{l\}}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\Lambda}\cup\{k\}}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\Lambda}\cup\{l\}}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\Lambda}},

where
\tilde{\Lambda}=\Lambda
but we consider it as a subset of
I_{p}\cup\{k,l\}
. Applying Wold decomposition for
V_{1}
on
\mathcal{H}
, we have

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{u1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{s1}=\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\{1\}},

where
\emptyset,\{1\}\subseteq I_{1}=\{1\}
. Using the above process repeatedly to

\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\{1\}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}\subseteq\bigoplus_{\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{H}_{\{1\}}\subseteq\bigoplus_{\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}_{\{\tilde{1}\}\cup\Lambda}

where
\Lambda^{\prime}=\{2,3,\dots,n\}
and
\{\tilde{1}\}=\{1\}
but treated as a subset of
I_{n}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\{1\}}\subseteq(\bigoplus_{\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda})\oplus(\bigoplus_{\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}_{\{\tilde{1}\}\cup\Lambda})=\bigoplus_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}.

Hence
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
; and
V_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
is unitary for
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
and
V_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
is shift for
i\in\Lambda
.

Finally, the uniqueness of this decomposition follows from the uniqueness of Wold decomposition for an isometry.

Remark 3.4
.

In the preceding result, the structure of decomposition spaces is motivated by the proof of Theorem 2.1 in [12], that characterizes any tuple of isometries admitting
Wold decomposition
. We recognize that the structure of the decomposition space in the intersection form presented above is appropriate for any given
n
-tuples of
isometries with equal range
which is a bigger class than the tuples of doubly non-commuting isometries and
\mathcal{U}_{n}
-twisted isometries.

4.
Decomposition for pairs of isometries

In this section, we focus on the representation of decomposition spaces for pairs of
isometries with equal range
. It is challenging to express the representation of the decomposition spaces described in Theorem
3.3
.

Let
(V_{1},V_{2})
be a pair of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{R}(V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n})=\mathcal{R}(V_{2}^{n}V_{1}^{m})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{1}^{*m}V_{2}^{n})=\mathcal{R}(V_{2}^{n}V_{1}^{*m})
for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Also recall that
\mathcal{W}_{i}=\mathcal{N}(V_{i}^{*})
for
i=1,2
. Then, a unique decomposition follows from Theorem
3.3
, and from the equation (
3.1
), the decomposition spaces become

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{u1}\cap\mathcal{H}_{u2}=\big(\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{H}\big)\cap\big(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}\big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\{1\}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{s1}\cap\mathcal{H}_{u2}=\big(\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}\big)\cap\big(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}\big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\{2\}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{u1}\cap\mathcal{H}_{s2}=\big(\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{H}\big)\cap\big(\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{2}\big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\{1,2\}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{s1}\cap\mathcal{H}_{s2}=\big(\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}\big)\cap\big(\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{2}\big).

Now our aim is to obtain the complete structure of the orthogonal spaces. Note that

\bigcap_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}\subseteq\big(\bigcap\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{H}\big)\cap\big(\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}\big).

For the converse part, let
h_{uu}\in\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}=\big(\bigcap\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{H}\big)\cap\big(\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}\big)
. Then, for each fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\displaystyle V_{2}^{*n}h_{uu}\in V_{2}^{*n}(\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{H})\subseteq\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{*n}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{H}=\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{*n}\mathcal{H}=\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{H}.

Again
V_{2}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}}
is unitary on
\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}
. It follows that
for every fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

h_{uu}=V_{2}^{n}V_{2}^{*n}h_{uu}\in\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{H}=\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}.

Hence

(4.1)

\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}=\big(\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{H}\big)\cap\big(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}\big)=\bigcap_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}.

Now
\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{1}
and

\bigoplus\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
implies that

\bigoplus\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}(\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{1})\subseteq\bigoplus\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}
and

\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}(\bigoplus\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1})\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}
. But

\bigoplus\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}(\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{1})=\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}(\bigoplus\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1})
and hence

\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{1})\subseteq\big(\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}\big)\cap\big(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}\big)=\mathcal{H}_{\{1\}}.

Conversely, let
h_{su}\in\mathcal{H}_{\{1\}}=\big(\bigoplus\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}\big)\cap\big(\bigcap\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}\big)
. Then

V_{2}^{*n}h_{su}\in V_{2}^{*n}(\bigoplus\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}\big).

Now using the conditions
V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}=V_{2}^{n}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{H}
and
V_{1}^{*m}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}=V_{2}^{n}V_{1}^{*m}\mathcal{H}
and Lemma
2.8
, for each fixed
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we have

V_{2}^{*n}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}=V_{2}^{*n}V_{1}^{m}(\mathcal{H}\ominus V_{1}\mathcal{H})=V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{*n}\mathcal{H}\ominus V_{1}^{m+1}V_{2}^{*n}\mathcal{H}=V_{1}^{m}(\mathcal{H}\ominus V_{1}\mathcal{H})=V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}.

Therefore,

V_{2}^{*n}h_{su}\in\bigoplus\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}.

Since
V_{2}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\{1\}}}
is unitary, for each fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we get

\displaystyle h_{su}=V_{2}^{n}V_{2}^{*n}h_{su}\in\bigoplus\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}=\bigoplus\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\{1\}}=\big(\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}\big)\cap\big(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}\big)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\big(\bigoplus\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{1}\big)

(4.2)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\big(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{1}\big).

Similarly,

(4.3)

\mathcal{H}_{\{2\}}=\big(\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{H}\big)\cap\big(\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{2}\big)=\bigoplus\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\big(\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{2}\big).

Finally, it is easy to see that

\bigoplus_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\mathcal{W}_{2})\subseteq\big(\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}\big)\cap\big(\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{2}\big)=\mathcal{H}_{\{1,2\}}.

To prove the reverse direction, let
h_{ss}\in\mathcal{H}_{\{1,2\}}=\displaystyle\big(\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}\big)\cap\big(\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{2}\big)
. Then

h_{ss}\in\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}.

Now, using the relation
(I_{\mathcal{H}}-V_{2}^{n}V_{2}^{*n})V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}=V_{1}^{m}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-V_{2}^{n}V_{2}^{*n})\mathcal{W}_{1}
for each fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(I_{\mathcal{H}}-V_{2}^{n}V_{2}^{*n})h_{ss}\in(I_{\mathcal{H}}-V_{2}^{n}V_{2}^{*n})\Big(\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-V_{2}^{n}V_{2}^{*n})V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-V_{2}^{n}V_{2}^{*n})\mathcal{W}_{1}.

Again
V_{2}|_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}
is an isometry, and thus for each fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

\displaystyle(I_{\mathcal{H}}-V_{2}^{n}V_{2}^{*n})\mathcal{W}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}_{1}\ominus V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{1}

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{W}_{1}\ominus V_{2}\mathcal{W}_{1}]\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{2}^{n-1}[\mathcal{W}_{1}\ominus V_{2}\mathcal{W}_{1}]

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\mathcal{W}_{2})\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{2}^{n-1}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\mathcal{W}_{2})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n-1}V_{2}^{k}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\mathcal{W}_{2}),

as Corollary
3.2
gives
\mathcal{W}_{1}\ominus V_{2}\mathcal{W}_{1}=\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\mathcal{W}_{2}
. Consequently,

(I_{\mathcal{H}}-V_{2}^{n}V_{2}^{*n})h_{ss}\in\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}V_{1}^{m}\Big(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n-1}V_{2}^{k}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\mathcal{W}_{2})\Big).

As
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}V_{2}^{*n}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\{1,2\}}}=0
in the strong operator topology, letting
n\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle h_{ss}\in\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}V_{1}^{m}\Big(\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}V_{2}^{n}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\mathcal{W}_{2})\Big)=\bigoplus_{m,n=0}^{\infty}V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\mathcal{W}_{2}).

Hence,

(4.4)

\mathcal{H}_{\{1,2\}}=\big(\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{1}\big)\cap\big(\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{2}\big)=\bigoplus_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\mathcal{W}_{2}).

To summarize the above, we have the following result:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\left(V_{1},V_{2}\right)
be a pair of isometries on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{R}(V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n})=\mathcal{R}(V_{2}^{n}V_{1}^{m})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{1}^{*m}V_{2}^{n})=\mathcal{R}(V_{2}^{n}V_{1}^{*m})
for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Then there is a unique decomposition

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{uu}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{us}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{su}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{ss},

where
\mathcal{H}_{uu},\mathcal{H}_{us},\mathcal{H}_{su},
and

\mathcal{H}_{ss}
are the subspaces reducing
V_{1},V_{2}
such that

•

V_{1}|_{\mathcal{H}_{uu}},V_{2}|_{\mathcal{H}_{uu}}
are unitary operators,

•

V_{1}|_{\mathcal{H}_{us}}
is unitary,
V_{2}|_{\mathcal{H}_{us}}
is a shift,

•

V_{1}|_{\mathcal{H}_{su}}
is shift,
V_{2}|_{\mathcal{H}_{su}}
is unitary, and

•

V_{1}|_{\mathcal{H}_{ss}}
,
V_{2}|_{\mathcal{H}_{ss}}
are shifts.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{uu}=\bigcap_{m,n=0}^{\infty}V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{us}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}V_{2}^{n}\Big(\bigcap_{m=0}^{\infty}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{N}(V_{2}^{*})\Big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{su}=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}V_{1}^{m}\Big(\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{N}(V_{1}^{*})\Big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{ss}=\bigoplus_{m,n=0}^{\infty}V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n}\Big(\mathcal{N}(V_{1}^{*})\cap\mathcal{N}(V_{2}^{*})\Big).

Remark 4.2
.

Note that the class of pairs of isometries
(V_{1},V_{2})
with equal range, i.e.,

\mathcal{R}(V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n})=\mathcal{R}(V_{2}^{n}V_{1}^{m})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{1}^{*m}V_{2}^{n})=\mathcal{R}(V_{2}^{n}V_{1}^{*m})
for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
contains the class of
\mathcal{U}_{2}
-twisted isometries (in particular, class of pairs of doubly commuting isometries). However, the orthogonal decomposition spaces for the pairs of
isometries with equal range
are the same as the orthogonal decomposition spaces for the pairs of doubly commuting isometries, which were obtained by Słoćinski in
[
15
]
. Moreover, the decomposition is unique in both cases.

5.
Decomposition for
n
-tuples of isometries

This section deals with
Wold decomposition
for
n
-tuples of
isometries with equal range
. Here, we find the explicit representation of the orthogonal decomposition spaces.

We begin with a few significant findings that will be helpful in the follow-up.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
V=(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
be an n-tuple of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{m_{i}})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{*m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{*m_{i}})
for
m_{i},m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Then

\displaystyle V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{m_{i_{n}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{m_{i_{n}}}V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}\mathcal{H}

and

\displaystyle V_{i_{1}}^{*m_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{m_{i_{n}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{m_{i_{n}}}V_{i_{1}}^{*m_{i_{1}}}\mathcal{H},

where
m_{i_{j}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
for
i_{j}\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.

Proof.

Consider
\Lambda=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{l}\}
for
2\leq l\leq n
and
i_{j}\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Then
(V_{i_{p}},V_{i_{q}})
is any pair of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that
V_{i_{p}}^{m_{i_{p}}}V_{i_{q}}^{m_{i_{q}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{q}}^{m_{i_{q}}}V_{i_{p}}^{m_{i_{p}}}\mathcal{H}
and

V_{i_{p}}^{*m_{i_{p}}}V_{i_{q}}^{m_{i_{q}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{q}}^{m_{i_{q}}}V_{i_{p}}^{*m_{i_{p}}}\mathcal{H}
,
where
m_{i_{p}},m_{i_{q}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
i_{p},i_{q}\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
for
1\leq p<q\leq n
. Thus, the statement is trivial for
n=2
.

We will prove the statement for
n\geq 3
by mathematical induction. Now, the given conditions
V_{i_{p}}^{m_{i_{p}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}V_{i_{p}}^{m_{i_{p}}}\mathcal{H}
and
V_{i_{p}}^{*m_{i_{p}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}V_{i_{p}}^{*m_{i_{p}}}\mathcal{H}
infer that
V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\mathcal{H}
reduces
V_{i_{p}}
for
p=1,2
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.9
, we have

V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}\mathcal{H}

and

V_{i_{1}}^{*m_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{1}}^{*m_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}V_{i_{1}}^{*m_{i_{1}}}\mathcal{H}

where
i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}
are distinct elements in
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, respectively.
Hence the statement is true for
n=3
.

Suppose the above statement is true for any
k
-tuple
(V_{i_{1}},\ldots,V_{i_{k}})
,
k<n
, of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that
V_{i_{p}}^{m_{i_{p}}}V_{i_{q}}^{m_{i_{q}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{q}}^{m_{i_{q}}}V_{i_{p}}^{m_{i_{p}}}\mathcal{H}
and

V_{i_{p}}^{*m_{i_{p}}}V_{i_{q}}^{m_{i_{q}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{q}}^{m_{i_{q}}}V_{i_{p}}^{*m_{i_{p}}}\mathcal{H}
,
where
m_{i_{p}},m_{i_{q}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
i_{p},i_{q}\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
for
1\leq p<q\leq n
. Then

\displaystyle V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}\mathcal{H}

and

\displaystyle V_{i_{1}}^{*m_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}V_{i_{1}}^{*m_{i_{1}}}\mathcal{H}

for
m_{i_{j}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
1\leq j\leq k<n
.
Since the statement is true for any
k
-tuple of isometries with
k<n
, replacing
V_{i_{1}}
by
V_{i_{k+1}}
we have

V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\dots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}\mathcal{H}

and

V_{i_{k+1}}^{*m_{i_{k+1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\dots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}V_{i_{k+1}}^{*m_{i_{k+1}}}\mathcal{H}.

Set
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}\mathcal{H}
. Then the above relations imply that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
reduces
V_{i_{1}},V_{i_{k+1}}
and employing Lemma
2.4
, we get
V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. In the same lines of argument the subspace
V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}\mathcal{H}
reduces the isometries
V_{i_{2}}
and
V_{i_{k+1}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}(V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle=V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}(V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}(V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{3}}^{m_{i_{3}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}V_{i_{1}}^{m_{i_{1}}}\mathcal{H}.

On the other hand, using Lemma
2.9
, we obtain

\displaystyle V_{i_{1}}^{*m_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=V_{i_{1}}^{*m_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}V_{i_{1}}^{*m_{i_{1}}}(V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle=V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}V_{i_{1}}^{*m_{i_{1}}}\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=V_{i_{2}}^{m_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{k}}^{m_{i_{k}}}V_{i_{k+1}}^{m_{i_{k+1}}}V_{i_{1}}^{*m_{i_{1}}}\mathcal{H}.

Hence the statement is true for
n=k+1
.

This completes the proof.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
V=(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that

\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{m_{i}})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{*m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{*m_{i}})
for
m_{i},m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
1\leq i<j\leq n
. If
\Lambda
is a subset of
I_{k}
for
k<n
, then

(1)

V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{k}\backslash\Lambda}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda\cup\{k+1\}}=V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{k}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda\cup\{k+1\}}
.

(2)

V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{k}\backslash\Lambda}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{k}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
.

(3)

V^{*\bm{q}}_{I_{k}\backslash\Lambda}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V^{*\bm{q}}_{I_{k}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
.

(4)

V^{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda\cup\{k+1\}}=V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}V^{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda\cup\{k+1\}}
.

Proof.

(1)

Let
\Lambda=\{1,\dots,l\}\subseteq I_{k}
for
1\leq l\leq k<n
,
and
I_{k}\backslash\Lambda=\{l+1,\dots,k\}
. Then, using Proposition
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{k}\backslash\Lambda}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda\cup\{k+1\}}

\displaystyle=V_{{l+1}}^{q_{l+1}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}[\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\ominus V_{k+1}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}]

\displaystyle=V_{{l+1}}^{q_{l+1}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}-V_{{l+1}}^{q_{l+1}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}+1}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}.

Applying Lemma
5.1
repeatedly, we obtain

V_{l+1}^{q_{l+1}}V_{l+2}^{q_{l+2}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}\mathcal{H}=V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}V_{l+1}^{q_{l+1}}V_{l+2}^{q_{l+2}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}\mathcal{H},

that means
V_{l+1}^{q_{l+1}}V_{l+2}^{q_{l+2}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}
and
V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}
are isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that

\mathcal{R}[(V_{l+1}^{q_{l+1}}V_{l+2}^{q_{l+2}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}})V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}]=\mathcal{R}[V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}(V_{l+1}^{q_{l+1}}V_{l+2}^{q_{l+2}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}})].

Now Proposition
3.1
infers that
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
is

V_{l+1}^{q_{l+1}},\dots,V_{k}^{q_{k}},V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}
-reducing subspace of

\mathcal{H}
and hence, by Lemma
2.4
we have

V_{l+1}^{q_{l+1}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}V_{l+1}^{q_{l+1}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda},

and also

\displaystyle V_{l+1}^{q_{l+1}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}+1}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}+1}V_{l+1}^{q_{l+1}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}V_{l+1}^{q_{l+1}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}V_{k+1}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}.

Hence,

\displaystyle V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{k}\backslash\Lambda}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda\cup\{k+1\}}

\displaystyle=V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}V_{l+1}^{q_{l+1}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}[\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\ominus V_{k+1}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}]

\displaystyle=V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{k}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda\cup\{k+1\}}.

(2) Note that
\mathcal{W}_{1}=\mathcal{H}\ominus V_{1}\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\mathcal{W}_{2}=\mathcal{W}_{1}\ominus V_{2}\mathcal{W}_{1}=\mathcal{H}-V_{1}\mathcal{H}-V_{2}\mathcal{H}+V_{1}V_{2}\mathcal{H}
. Let
\Lambda=\{1,\dots,l\}\subseteq I_{n}
. Then, using the mathematical induction, we can show that

\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{H}-\sum_{i}V_{i}\mathcal{H}+\sum_{i,j}V_{i}V_{j}\mathcal{H}-\dots+(-1)^{l}V_{1}V_{2}\cdots V_{l}\mathcal{H}.

Now using Lemma
5.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{k}\backslash\Lambda}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{k}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}.

(3) Again using Lemma
5.1
, we have in the same line

\displaystyle V^{*\bm{q}}_{I_{k}\backslash\Lambda}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V^{*\bm{q}}_{I_{k}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}.

(4) Since
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda\cup\{k+1\}}=\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\ominus V_{k+1}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
, relation (2) yields

\displaystyle V^{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda\cup\{k+1\}}

\displaystyle=V^{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\ominus V^{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}+1}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}V^{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\ominus V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}V^{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}V_{k+1}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}V^{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{{\Lambda}\cup\{k+1\}}.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
V=(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
be an n-tuple isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{m_{i}})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{*m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{*m_{i}})
for
m_{i},m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
where
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Suppose

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{uj}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{sj}
is the Wold decomposition for
V_{j}

such that
\mathcal{H}_{uj}=\bigcap\limits_{q_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{j}^{q_{j}}\mathcal{H}
for

j=1,\dots,n
. Then

\bigcap_{j\in I_{l}}\mathcal{H}_{uj}=\bigcap_{q_{1},\dots,q_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{q_{1}}\cdots V_{l}^{q_{l}}\mathcal{H},

where
1\leq j\leq l\leq n
.

Proof.

Since
(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
is an
n
-tuple of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
, for
1\leq j\leq l\leq n
, we obtain

\bigcap_{q_{1},\dots,q_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{q_{1}}\cdots V_{l}^{q_{l}}\mathcal{H}\subseteq\bigcap_{j\in I_{l}}\big(\bigcap_{q_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{j}^{q_{j}}\mathcal{H}\big)=\bigcap_{j\in I_{l}}\mathcal{H}_{uj}.

It suffices to prove that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\bigcap_{j\in I_{l}}\big(\bigcap_{q_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{j}^{q_{j}}\mathcal{H}\big)\subseteq\bigcap_{q_{1},\dots,q_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{q_{1}}\cdots V_{l}^{q_{l}}\mathcal{H}

for
1\leq j\leq l\leq n
, and we will prove this by mathematical induction.
If
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a pair of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that
V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}=V_{2}^{n}V_{1}^{m}\mathcal{H}
and
V_{1}^{*m}V_{2}^{n}\mathcal{H}=V_{2}^{n}V_{1}^{*m}\mathcal{H}
for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, the equation (
4.1
) implies that the above statement is true for
l=2
.

Now suppose the statement is true for any
k
-tuple,
k<l
, of isometries

(V_{1},\dots,V_{k})
on
\mathcal{H}
, that means for
1\leq j\leq k<l

\displaystyle\bigcap_{j\in I_{k}}\big(\bigcap_{q_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{j}^{q_{j}}\mathcal{H}\big)\subseteq\bigcap_{q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{q_{1}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}\mathcal{H}.

Let
h\in\bigcap\limits_{j\in I_{k+1}}\big(\bigcap\limits_{q_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{j}^{q_{j}}\mathcal{H})=\bigcap\limits_{j\in I_{k}}\big(\bigcap\limits_{q_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{j}^{q_{j}}\mathcal{H})\cap\big(\bigcap\limits_{q_{k+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{k+1}^{q_{k+1}}\mathcal{H})
. Then, for each
q_{k+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we have

V_{k+1}^{*q_{k+1}}h\in\bigcap\limits_{j\in I_{k}}\big(\bigcap\limits_{q_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{j}^{q_{j}}V_{k+1}^{*q_{k+1}}\mathcal{H})=\bigcap_{j\in I_{k}}\big(\bigcap_{q_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{j}^{q_{j}}\mathcal{H}\big)\subseteq\bigcap_{q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{q_{1}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}\mathcal{H}.

Therefore, using Lemma
5.1
, we get

h\in\bigcap_{q_{1},\dots,q_{k},q_{k+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{k+1}^{q_{k+1}}V_{1}^{q_{1}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}\mathcal{H}=\bigcap_{q_{1},\dots,q_{k},q_{k+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{q_{1}}\cdots V_{k}^{q_{k}}V_{k+1}^{q_{k+1}}\mathcal{H}.

Hence, (
5.1
) holds for
l=k+1
and this finishes the proof.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
V=(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that

\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{m_{i}})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{*m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{*m_{i}})
for
m_{i},m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Suppose that
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{ui}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{si}
is the Wold decomposition for
V_{i}
such that
\mathcal{H}_{si}=\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i}^{p_{i}}\mathcal{W}_{i}
for
\ i=1,2,\dots,n
. Then

\bigcap_{i\in I_{l}}\mathcal{H}_{si}=\bigoplus_{p_{1},\dots,p_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots V_{l}^{p_{l}}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathcal{W}_{l}),

where
1\leq i\leq l\leq n
.

Proof.

Suppose
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a pair of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{R}(V_{1}^{m}V_{2}^{n})=\mathcal{R}(V_{2}^{n}V_{1}^{m})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{1}^{*m}V_{2}^{n})=\mathcal{R}(V_{2}^{n}V_{1}^{*m})
for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Then the equation (
4.4
) implies that the above statement is true for
l=2
.

Assume that the statement is true for any
k
-tuple
(k<l)
of isometries

(V_{1},\dots,V_{k})
on
\mathcal{H}
. Then, for
1\leq i\leq k<l
, we have

\displaystyle\bigcap_{i\in I_{k}}\big(\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i}^{p_{i}}\mathcal{W}_{i}\big)=\bigoplus_{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots V_{k}^{p_{k}}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathcal{W}_{k}).

To show the above statement is true for
(k+1)
-tuple of isometries
(V_{1},\dots,V_{k+1})
on
\mathcal{H}
, firstly observe that for any
i\in I_{k+1}
,

\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{W}_{k+1}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{i}.

Now using the fact
V_{j}\mathcal{W}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{i}
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq k+1
and applying Lemma
5.2
, for each
i\in I_{k+1}
, we have

\bigoplus_{p_{1},\dots,p_{k+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathcal{W}_{k+1})\subseteq\bigoplus_{p_{1},\dots,p_{k+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}\mathcal{W}_{i}\subseteq\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i}^{p_{i}}\mathcal{W}_{i}.

It follows that

\bigoplus_{p_{1},\dots,p_{k+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathcal{W}_{k+1})\subseteq\bigcap_{i\in I_{k+1}}\big(\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i}^{p_{i}}\mathcal{W}_{i}\big).

For the reverse inclusion, let

(5.2)

h\in\bigcap_{i\in I_{k+1}}\big(\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i}^{p_{i}}\mathcal{W}_{i}\big)=\bigoplus_{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots V_{k}^{p_{k}}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathcal{W}_{k})\cap\big(\bigoplus\limits_{p_{k+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}\mathcal{W}_{i}\big).

Define
\mathcal{W}_{K}={{\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{k}}\mathcal{W}_{i}}
. Then, by Proposition
3.1
,
V_{k+1}|_{\mathcal{W}_{K}}
is an isometry. So, for each fixed
p_{k+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we get

\displaystyle(I_{\mathcal{H}}-V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}V_{k+1}^{*p_{k+1}})\mathcal{W}_{K}

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{W}_{K}\ominus V_{k+1}\mathcal{W}_{K}]\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}-1}[\mathcal{W}_{K}\ominus V_{k+1}\mathcal{W}_{K}]

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{W}_{K}\cap\mathcal{W}_{k+1})\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}-1}(\mathcal{W}_{K}\cap\mathcal{W}_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{r=0}^{p_{k+1}-1}V_{k+1}^{r}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathcal{W}_{k+1})

as Proposition
3.2
gives
\mathcal{W}_{K}\ominus V_{k+1}\mathcal{W}_{K}=\mathcal{W}_{K}\cap\mathcal{W}_{k+1}
. Now employing Lemma
5.2
, for each fixed
p_{k+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, relation (
5.2
) becomes

\displaystyle(I_{\mathcal{H}}-V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}V_{k+1}^{*p_{k+1}})h

\displaystyle\in\bigoplus_{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}V_{k+1}^{*p_{k+1}})V_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots V_{k}^{p_{k}}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{W}_{k})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots V_{k}^{p_{k}}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}V_{k+1}^{*p_{k+1}})(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{W}_{k})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{r=0}^{p_{k+1}-1}\bigoplus_{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots V_{k}^{p_{k}}V_{k+1}^{r}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{W}_{k+1}).

Now
\lim\limits_{p_{k+1}\rightarrow\infty}V_{k+1}^{*p_{k+1}}|_{\mathcal{H}_{s(k+1)}}=0

in the strong operator topology. Letting
p_{k+1}\rightarrow\infty
, we have

h\in\bigoplus_{p_{1},\dots,p_{k+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots V_{k}^{p_{k}}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\dots\cap\mathcal{W}_{k+1}).

Therefore,

\bigcap_{i\in I_{k+1}}\big(\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i}^{p_{i}}\mathcal{W}_{i}\big)\subseteq\bigoplus_{p_{1},\dots,p_{k+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots V_{k}^{p_{k}}V_{k+1}^{p_{k+1}}(\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathcal{W}_{k+1}).

Hence the statement is true for
l=k+1
. This completes the proof.

Now we are in a position to state and prove our main result as follows:

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
V=(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{m_{i}})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{*m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{*m_{i}})
for
m_{i},m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Then there exist
2^{n}
joint
V
-reducing subspaces
\{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}:\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}\}
(counting the trivial subspace
\{0\}
) such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda},

where, for
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\neq\{0\}
,
V_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
is unitary if
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
and
V_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
is a shift if
i\in\Lambda
. Moreover, for each
\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}
,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\bigoplus_{{\bm{p}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{|\Lambda|}}V_{{\Lambda}}^{\bm{p}}\bigg(\bigcap_{{\bm{q}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{{n-|\Lambda|}}}V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}\ \mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\bigg)

and the above decomposition is unique.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 2
be a fixed positive integer. Suppose
V=(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
is an
n
-tuple of isometries on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{m_{i}})
and
\mathcal{R}(V_{i}^{*m_{i}}V_{j}^{m_{j}})=\mathcal{R}(V_{j}^{m_{j}}V_{i}^{*m_{i}})
for
m_{i},m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
where
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Thus, by Theorem
3.3
, there exist
2^{n}
joint
V
-reducing subspaces
\{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}:\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}\}
(including the subspace {0}) such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}

and for each non-zero decomposition subspace
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
, we have
V_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
is unitary if
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
and
V_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
is a shift if
i\in\Lambda
. Also, from the equation (
3.1
), we have the orthogonal decomposition spaces as

\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\big(\bigcap_{j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{uj}\big)\cap\big(\bigcap_{i\in\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{si}\big).

Now consider
\Lambda=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{l}\}\subseteq I_{n}
for
1\leq l\leq n
, and
I_{n}\backslash\Lambda=\{i_{l+1}<\dots<i_{n}\}
. Then Lemma
5.3
gives

\displaystyle\bigcap_{j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{uj}

\displaystyle=\big(\bigcap_{q_{i_{l+1}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{l+1}}^{q_{i_{l+1}}}\mathcal{H}\big)\cap\cdots\cap\big(\bigcap_{{q_{i_{n}}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}\mathcal{H}\big)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{q_{i_{l+1},\dots,q_{i_{n}}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{l+1}}^{q_{i_{l+1}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}\mathcal{H},

and Lemma
5.4
yields

\displaystyle\bigcap_{i\in\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{si}

\displaystyle=\big(\bigoplus_{p_{i_{1}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}\mathcal{W}_{i_{1}}\big)\cap\cdots\cap\big(\bigoplus_{p_{i_{l}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}\mathcal{W}_{i_{l}}\big)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{l}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}(\mathcal{W}_{i_{1}}\cap\dots\cap\mathcal{W}_{i_{l}})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{l}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}.

Therefore,

\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\Big(\bigcap_{q_{i_{l+1},\dots,q_{i_{n}}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{l+1}}^{q_{i_{l+1}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}\mathcal{H}\Big)\cap\Big(\bigoplus_{p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{l}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\Big).

Let
h\in\Big(\bigcap\limits_{q_{i_{l+1},\dots,q_{i_{n}}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{l+1}}^{q_{i_{l+1}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}\mathcal{H}\Big)\cap\Big(\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{l}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\Big)
. Again, by Proposition
3.1
,
V_{j}|_{\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}}
is isometry on
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
for
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
. Using Lemma
5.2
, for each
q_{i_{l+1}},\dots,q_{i_{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we obtain

\displaystyle V_{i_{n}}^{*q_{i_{n}}}\cdots V_{i_{l+1}}^{*q_{i_{l+1}}}h

\displaystyle\in\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{l}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{n}}^{*q_{i_{n}}}\cdots V_{i_{l+1}}^{*q_{i_{l+1}}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{l}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}V_{i_{n}}^{*q_{i_{n}}}\cdots V_{i_{l+1}}^{*q_{i_{l+1}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{l}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}.

Now
V_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
is unitary for
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
, that is,
V_{i_{l+1}},\dots,V_{i_{n}}
are unitaries on
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
. Therefore, for each
q_{i_{l+1}},\dots,q_{i_{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

\displaystyle h=V_{i_{l+1}}^{q_{i_{l+1}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}V_{i_{n}}^{*q_{i_{n}}}\cdots V_{i_{l+1}}^{*q_{i_{l+1}}}h

\displaystyle\in\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{l}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{l+1}}^{q_{i_{l+1}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus\limits_{\bm{p}=(p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{l}})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{|\Lambda|}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V_{i_{l+1}}^{q_{i_{l+1}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

which implies

h\in\bigcap_{q_{i_{l+1}},\dots,q_{i_{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\bigoplus\limits_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{|\Lambda|}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V_{i_{l+1}}^{q_{i_{l+1}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=\bigoplus\limits_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{|\Lambda|}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\big(\bigcap_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\big).

It follows that

\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\big(\bigcap_{j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{uj}\big)\cap\big(\bigcap_{i\in\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{si}\big)\subseteq\bigoplus\limits_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{|\Lambda|}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\big(\bigcap_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\big).

To prove the reverse inclusion, we know that
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{i}
for all
i\in\Lambda
. Now, using Lemma
5.2
, we obtain

\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{l}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\subseteq\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{l}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}\mathcal{W}_{i}\subseteq\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i}^{p_{i}}\mathcal{W}_{i}.

Also

\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{l}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
. Therefore,

\bigoplus\limits_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{|\Lambda|}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\big(\bigcap_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\big)\subseteq\bigcap_{j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}\big(\bigcap_{q_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{j}^{q_{j}}\mathcal{H}\big)\cap\bigcap_{i\in\Lambda}\big(\bigoplus\limits_{p_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i}^{p_{i}}\mathcal{W}_{i}\big)=\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}.

Hence, from both the inclusions,

\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\bigoplus\limits_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{|\Lambda|}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\big(\bigcap_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V^{\bm{q}}_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\big).

This finishes the proof.

There are few remarks in order:

Remark 5.6
.

(1)

(2)

6.
Analytic Models

This section deals with the analytic model for
n
-tuples of
isometries with equal range
. Recall that the pairs
(M_{z},\Delta[{\bm{r}}])
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, and
(M_{z_{1}},M_{z_{2}}(\Delta[\bm{r}]\otimes I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}}))
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
given in Example
2.11
, are typical examples of pairs of
isometries with equal range
, and these are actually “building blocks” in the analytic models.

Before proceeding to the main results, we recall some standard notations and definitions:
For
l\geq 1
, let
\mathbb{D}^{l}=\mathbb{D}\times\dots\times\mathbb{D}
be the open unit polydisc in

\mathbb{C}^{l}
. We use the notation
\bm{z}
for the
l
-tuple
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{l})
in
\mathbb{C}^{l}
. Also, for any multi-index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{l})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{l}
and
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{l}
, we write
\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}=z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{l}^{k_{l}}
.
Two tuples
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})

on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
are said to be unitarily equivalent if there exists a unitary
U:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
such that
UV_{i}=T_{i}U
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a separable complex Hilbert space. Then
\mathcal{E}
-valued Hardy space over the unit polydisc

\mathbb{D}^{l}
, denoted by
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{l})
, is the Hilbert space of all holomorphic functions
f(\bm{z})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}_{+}}a_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}
, where
a_{\bm{k}}\in\mathcal{E}
, on
\mathbb{D}^{l}
such that

\|f\|_{H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{l})}:=\left(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}_{+}}\|a_{\bm{k}}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty.

We shall frequently identify the
\mathcal{E}
-valued Hardy space
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{l})

with
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{l})\otimes\mathcal{E}
by the canonical unitary map
\Pi:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{l})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{l})\otimes\mathcal{E}
defined by

\Pi(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta)=\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\otimes\eta\quad\quad(\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}_{+},\eta\in\mathcal{E}).

It is again easy to observe that the scalar valued Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{l})
,
l>1

over
\mathbb{D}^{l}
can be identified via the natural unitary map

z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{l}^{k_{l}}\mapsto z^{k_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes z^{k_{l}}

with the
l
-fold Hilbert space tensor product
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\otimes\cdots\otimes H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
of the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
over the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
. Let
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{l}})
denote the
l
-tuple of multiplication operators by the co-ordinate functions
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{l}
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{l})
, that is,

(M_{z_{j}}f)(\bm{w})=w_{j}f(\bm{w}),

for all
f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{l})
,
\bm{w}\in\mathbb{D}^{l}
and
j=1,\ldots,l
. It is also easy to check that
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{l}})
is an
l
-tuple of
doubly commuting
shifts on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{l})
. Clearly, the shift
M_{z_{j}}
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{l})
can be identified with
M_{z_{j}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{l})\otimes\mathcal{E}
for
j=1,\ldots,l
. This canonical identification will be used throughout the rest of the paper.

Returning back to the analytic representation of a single isometry, let
V
be an isometry on
\mathcal{H}
. Then the
Wold decomposition
for the isometry
V

gives
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\{1\}}
,
where
\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}=\cap_{m=0}^{\infty}V^{m}\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{H}_{\{1\}}=\oplus_{m=0}^{\infty}V^{m}\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{H}\ominus V\mathcal{H}
. Now define a map

\tau_{V}:\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\{1\}}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D})
by

\tau_{V}(h_{u}\oplus V^{m}\eta)=h_{u}\oplus z^{m}\eta\quad(m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},h_{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset},\eta\in\mathcal{W}).

Clearly,
\tau_{V}
is unitary, and also

\tau_{V}\begin{bmatrix}V|_{\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}}&0\\
0&V|_{\mathcal{H}_{\{1\}}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}V|_{\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}}&0\\
0&M_{z}\end{bmatrix}\tau_{V},

that means,
V=V|_{\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}}\oplus V|_{\mathcal{H}_{\{1\}}}
on
\mathcal{H}
is unitarily equivalent to
V|_{\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}}\oplus M_{z}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D})
. It follows that the shift part of the isometry
V
yields an analytic representation in terms of multiplication operator
M_{z}
on the
\mathcal{W}
-valued Hardy space
H^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D})
over the unit disc.

Inspiring from the above analytic structure of a single isometry as well as analytic models for certain tuples of isometries described in
[
5
]
&
[
11
]
, we intend to find the analytic models for tuples of
isometries with equal range
. Before moving on to the main results, we introduce some fundamental concepts from
[
5
]
&
[
11
]
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
j\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
. Let
U=(U^{(m)})
be a sequence of unitaries on

\mathcal{E}
. The
j
-th diagonal operator with symbol
U
, denoted by
\Delta_{j}[U]
, is a unitary operator on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{l})
defined as

\Delta_{j}[U](\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\eta)=\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}(U^{(k_{j})}\eta)\quad\quad(\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}_{+},\eta\in\mathcal{E}).

Definition 6.2
.

Let
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of isometries with equal range on
\mathcal{H}
.
Suppose
\Lambda=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{l}\}\subseteq I_{n}
for
1\leq l\leq n
, and
I_{n}\backslash\Lambda=\{i_{l+1}<\dots<i_{n}\}
. The
\Lambda
-wandering subspace of the tuple
V
is defined as

\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)=\bigcap_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda},

and the
\Lambda
-wandering data of
V
as the
(n-l+1)
-tuple

wd_{\Lambda}(V)=(I|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)},V_{i_{l+1}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)},\dots,V_{i_{n}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)})

on
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)
.

If the tuple
V
is obvious from the context, we will simply write
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)
as
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}
. The wandering data
wd_{\Lambda}(V)
defined above is guaranteed to be a well-defined
(n-l+1)
-tuple on
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}
by the following lemma:

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of isometries with equal range on
\mathcal{H}
. If
\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}
, then
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}
reduces
V_{j}
for
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
. Moreover,
V_{j}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}
is unitary for
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
and
V_{i}^{*}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}=0
for
i\in\Lambda
.

Proof.

Suppose
\Lambda=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{l}\}
for
1\leq l<n
and
I_{n}\backslash\Lambda=\{i_{l+1}<\dots<i_{n}\}
. Then
\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
is
V_{j}
-reducing subspace for
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
by Proposition
3.1
. Now Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.9
yield

V_{i_{t}}^{q_{i_{t}}}V_{i_{s}}^{q_{i_{s}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=V_{i_{s}}^{q_{i_{s}}}V_{i_{t}}^{q_{i_{t}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\quad\text{and}\quad V_{i_{t}}^{*q_{i_{t}}}V_{i_{s}}^{q_{i_{s}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=V_{i_{s}}^{q_{i_{s}}}V_{i_{t}}^{*q_{i_{t}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

where
i_{t},i_{s}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
and
q_{i_{t}},q_{i_{s}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Applying Lemma
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle V_{i_{l+1}}^{q_{i_{l+1}}}V_{i_{l+2}}^{q_{i_{l+2}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=V_{i_{l+2}}^{q_{i_{l+2}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}V_{i_{l+1}}^{q_{i_{l+1}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda},

and

\displaystyle V_{i_{l+1}}^{*q_{i_{l+1}}}V_{i_{l+2}}^{q_{i_{l+2}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=V_{i_{l+2}}^{q_{i_{l+2}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}V_{i_{l+1}}^{*q_{i_{l+1}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

where
q_{i_{l+1}},\dots,q_{i_{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Let
j=i_{r}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
for
r\in\{l+1,\dots,n\}
. Then using the above relations we get

\displaystyle V_{i_{r}}(\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda})

\displaystyle=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V_{i_{r}}(V_{i_{l+1}}^{q_{i_{l+1}}}\cdots V_{i_{r-1}}^{q_{i_{r-1}}}V_{i_{r}}^{q_{i_{r}}}V_{i_{r+1}}^{q_{i_{r+1}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda})

\displaystyle=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V_{i_{r}}^{q_{i_{r}}+1}V_{i_{r+1}}^{q_{i_{r+1}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}V_{i_{l+1}}^{q_{i_{l+1}}}\cdots V_{i_{r-1}}^{q_{i_{r-1}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V_{i_{r}}^{q_{i_{r}}}V_{i_{r+1}}^{q_{i_{r+1}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}V_{i_{l+1}}^{q_{i_{l+1}}}\cdots V_{i_{r-1}}^{q_{i_{r-1}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V_{i_{l+1}}^{q_{i_{l+1}}}\cdots V_{i_{r}}^{q_{i_{r}}}\cdots V_{i_{n}}^{q_{i_{n}}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}.

If
\Lambda=\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{W}_{\emptyset}=\mathcal{H}
. Now Theorem
5.5
asserts that
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}
reduces
V_{j}
for all
j\in I_{n}
. On the other hand, if
\Lambda=I_{n}
, the statement holds trivially.
Therefore,

\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

reduces each
V_{j}
for
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
.

Finally,
V_{j}(\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda})=\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}
implies that
V_{j}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}
is unitary for
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
. Clearly,
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
for all
\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}
, and hence
V_{i}^{*}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}=0
for all
i\in\Lambda
.

The next result on the
\Lambda
-wandering subspace is significant in the continuation.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of isometries with equal range on
\mathcal{H}
.
If
\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}
, then

(a)

V_{i_{j}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}=V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}+e_{j}}\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}
for
i_{j}\in\Lambda
.

(b)

V_{i_{k}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}=V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V_{i_{k}}\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}
for
i_{k}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{l}\}
for
1\leq l<n
and
I_{n}\backslash\Lambda=\{i_{l+1}<\dots<i_{n}\}
. Recall the proof of part (2) of Lemma
5.2
that

\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=\mathcal{H}-\sum V_{i_{p}}\mathcal{H}+\sum V_{i_{p}}V_{i_{q}}\mathcal{H}-\dots+(-1)^{l}V_{i_{1}}V_{i_{2}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}\mathcal{H}.

(a)
If
i_{j}\in\Lambda
, then, using Lemma
5.1
repeatedly on the second line below, we have

\displaystyle V_{i_{j}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V_{i_{j}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V_{i_{j}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}\cdots V_{i_{j}}^{p_{i_{j}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}[\mathcal{H}-\sum V_{i_{p}}\mathcal{H}+\dots+(-1)^{l}V_{i_{1}}V_{i_{2}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}\mathcal{H}]

\displaystyle=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}\cdots V_{i_{j}}^{p_{i_{j}}+1}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}+e_{j}}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}+e_{j}}\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}.

(b)
If
i_{k}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
, then, again using Lemma
5.1
, we obtain

V_{i_{k}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V_{i_{k}}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle V_{i_{k}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V_{i_{k}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V_{i_{k}}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V_{i_{k}}\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}.

Next, we note the observation that follows from the above lemma and Lemma
2.3
.

Let
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})

i_{j}\in\Lambda=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{l}\}\subseteq I_{n}

Let
i_{j}\neq i_{2}
. Then Lemma
5.1
yields

\displaystyle V_{i_{j}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{j}}V_{i_{2}}^{p_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}U_{i_{j}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})}\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{p_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{j}}V_{i_{3}}^{p_{i_{3}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}U_{i_{j}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})}\mathcal{H}.

Again Lemma
2.3
gives a unique unitary
U_{i_{j}i_{2}}^{(p_{i_{2}})}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that

V_{i_{j}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}=V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{p_{i_{2}}}V_{i_{j}}V_{i_{3}}^{p_{i_{3}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}U_{i_{j}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})}U_{i_{j}i_{2}}^{(p_{i_{2}})}.

Applying Lemma
2.3
repeatedly, we obtain
(j-1)
unitaries
U_{i_{j}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})},U_{i_{j}i_{2}}^{(p_{i_{2}})}\dots,U_{i_{j}i_{j-1}}^{(p_{i_{j-1}})}
on
\mathcal{H}
such that

\displaystyle V_{i_{j}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}

\displaystyle=V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{p_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{j}}^{p_{i_{j}}+1}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}U_{i_{j}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})}U_{i_{j}i_{2}}^{(p_{i_{2}})}\cdots U_{i_{j}i_{j-1}}^{(p_{i_{j-1}})}

\displaystyle=V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}+e_{j}}U_{i_{j}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})}U_{i_{j}i_{2}}^{(p_{i_{2}})}\cdots U_{i_{j}i_{j-1}}^{(p_{i_{j-1}})}

\displaystyle=V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}+e_{j}}U_{i_{j}},

where
U_{i_{j}}=U_{i_{j}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})}U_{i_{j}i_{2}}^{(p_{i_{2}})}\cdots U_{i_{j}i_{j-1}}^{(p_{i_{j-1}})}
is a unique unitary operator
\mathcal{H}
.

(2)

If
\Lambda=\emptyset
, then
U_{i_{k}}=I_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
i_{k}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
. Suppose
\Lambda\neq\emptyset
. Then Lemma
5.1
yields

V_{i_{k}}V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{p_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}\mathcal{H}=V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{k}}V_{i_{2}}^{p_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}\mathcal{H}.

Now Lemma
2.3
gives a unique unitary
{U}_{i_{k}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that

V_{i_{k}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}=V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{k}}V_{i_{2}}^{p_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}{U}_{i_{k}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})}.

Repeating this process
l

l
unitaries
{U}_{i_{k}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})},\dots,{U}_{i_{k}i_{l}}^{(p_{i_{l}})}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that

\displaystyle V_{i_{k}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}

\displaystyle=V_{i_{1}}^{p_{i_{1}}}V_{i_{2}}^{p_{i_{2}}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{i_{l}}}V_{i_{k}}{U}_{i_{k}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})}{U}_{i_{k}i_{2}}^{(p_{i_{2}})}\cdots{U}_{i_{k}i_{l}}^{(p_{i_{l}})}

\displaystyle=V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V_{i_{k}}U_{i_{k}},

where
U_{i_{k}}=U_{i_{k}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})}\cdots U_{i_{k}i_{l}}^{(p_{i_{l}})}
is a unique unitary operator on
\mathcal{H}
.

From the above proposition and Lemma
6.4
, we record the following observations which will be useful in finding the analytic models of tuples.

Remark 6.6
.

Let
(V_{1},\dots,V_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of
isometries with equal range
on

\mathcal{H}
. Suppose
\Lambda=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{l}\}\subseteq I_{n}
. Then

(i)

, and

for

infer that
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}
reduce the unitaries

, and
U_{i_{k}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})},\ldots,U_{i_{k}i_{l}}^{(p_{i_{l}})}
obtained in Proposition
6.5
. Thus
U_{i_{j}}=U_{i_{j}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})}\cdots U_{i_{j}i_{j-1}}^{(p_{i_{j-1}})}
and
U_{i_{k}}=U_{i_{k}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})}\cdots U_{i_{k}i_{l}}^{(p_{i_{l}})}
are unitaries on
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}
.

(ii)

Lemma
2.4
yields
V_{i_{j}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}+e_{j}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
for

and
V_{i_{k}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V_{i_{k}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
for
i_{k}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
. Thus,
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
reduce the unitaries
U_{i_{j}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})},\dots,U_{i_{j}i_{j-1}}^{(p_{i_{j-1}})}
and
U_{i_{k}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})},\dots,U_{i_{k}i_{l}}^{(p_{i_{l}})}
, which are obtained in Proposition
6.5
, and hence
U_{i_{j}}=U_{i_{j}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})}\cdots U_{i_{j}i_{j-1}}^{(p_{i_{j-1}})}
and
U_{i_{k}}=U_{i_{k}i_{1}}^{(p_{i_{1}})}\cdots U_{i_{k}i_{l}}^{(p_{i_{l}})}
are unitaries on
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
.

Recall that the non-zero shift part (if any) of a single isometry due to
Wold decomposition
yields the analytic structure of the isometry. Now Theorem
5.5
says that an
n
-tuple of isometries
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
with equal range on
\mathcal{H}
admits
Wold decomposition
. More precisely, there exist
2^{n}
joint
V
-reducing subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
such that
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
, where

\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\bigoplus\limits_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{|\Lambda|}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bf p}\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}
; and for
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\neq\{0\}
,
V_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
is unitary if
j\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
and
V_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
is a shift if
i\in\Lambda
. Thus, it is important to separate the shift parts (if any) from the tuple of isometries to describe the analytic structure of
n
-tuples of
isometries with equal range
. To do that we firstly find out the analytical construction of the shift part
V|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
for
\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}
. Since
V|_{\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}}
is unitary, we will discuss when
\Lambda\neq\emptyset
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\neq\{0\}
, where
\Lambda=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{l}\}\subseteq I_{n}
for some
1\leq l\leq n
and
|\Lambda|=l
. Now for each orthogonal decomposition space
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\bigoplus\limits_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{l}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}
, define the canonical unitary
\tau_{\Lambda}:\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}(\mathbb{D}^{l})
as

(6.1)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Lambda}(V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\xi)={\bm{z}}^{\bm{p}}\xi\quad(\ \bm{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{l})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{l},\ \xi\in\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}).

Therefore,

(\tau_{\Lambda}V_{i_{1}}\tau_{\Lambda}^{*})(\bm{z}^{\bm{p}}\xi)=\tau_{\Lambda}(V_{i_{1}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\xi)=\tau_{\Lambda}(V_{i_{1}}^{p_{1}+1}V_{i_{2}}^{p_{2}}\cdots V_{i_{l}}^{p_{l}}\xi)=z_{1}({\bm{z}}^{\bm{p}}\xi)=M_{z_{1}}({\bm{z}}^{\bm{p}}\xi),

that means
\tau_{\Lambda}V_{i_{1}}=M_{z_{1}}\tau_{\Lambda}
. Now if
i_{j}\in\Lambda
for
j\in\{2,3,\dots,l\}
, then, by Proposition
6.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\tau_{\Lambda}V_{i_{j}}\tau_{\Lambda}^{*})({\bm{z}}^{\bm{p}}\xi)

\displaystyle=\tau_{\Lambda}(V_{i_{j}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\xi)

\displaystyle=\tau_{\Lambda}(V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}+e_{j}}U_{i_{j}}\xi)

\displaystyle=\tau_{\Lambda}(V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}+e_{j}}U_{i_{j}i_{1}}^{(p_{1})}U_{i_{j}i_{2}}^{(p_{2})}\cdots U_{i_{j}i_{j-1}}^{(p_{{j-1}})}\xi)

\displaystyle=z_{j}({\bm{z}}^{\bm{p}}U_{i_{j}i_{1}}^{(p_{1})}U_{i_{j}i_{2}}^{(p_{2})}\cdots U_{i_{j}i_{j-1}}^{(p_{{j-1}})}\xi)

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}(\Delta_{1}[U_{i_{j}i_{1}}]\cdots\Delta_{j-1}[U_{i_{j}i_{j-1}}])({\bm{z}}^{\bf p}\xi).

Therefore,

\tau_{\Lambda}V_{i_{j}}\tau_{\Lambda}^{*}=M_{z_{j}}(\Delta_{1}[U_{i_{j}i_{1}}]\cdots\Delta_{j-1}[U_{i_{j}i_{j-1}}])\quad\text{for}~~2\leq j\leq l.

For the remaining part, suppose
i_{k}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
for
k\in\{l+1,\dots,n\}
. Again using Proposition
6.5
, we have

\displaystyle(\tau_{\Lambda}V_{i_{k}}\tau_{\Lambda}^{*})({\bm{z}}^{\bm{p}}\xi)

\displaystyle=\tau_{\Lambda}(V_{i_{k}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\xi)

\displaystyle=\tau_{\Lambda}(V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}V_{i_{k}}U_{i_{k}}\xi)

\displaystyle=\tau_{\Lambda}\left(V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}(V_{i_{k}}U_{i_{k}i_{1}}^{(p_{1})}\cdots U_{i_{k}i_{l}}^{(p_{{l}})}\xi)\right)

\displaystyle={\bm{z}}^{\bm{p}}(V_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}U_{i_{k}i_{1}}^{(p_{1})}\cdots U_{i_{k}i_{l}}^{(p_{{l}})}\xi)

\displaystyle=(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{l})}\otimes V_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}})(\Delta_{1}[U_{i_{k}i_{1}}]\cdots\Delta_{l}[U_{i_{k}i_{l}}])({\bm{z}}^{\bm{p}}\xi).

Thus, for
l+1\leq k\leq n
, we obtain

\tau_{\Lambda}V_{i_{k}}\tau_{\Lambda}^{*}=(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{l})}\otimes V_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}})(\Delta_{1}[U_{i_{k}i_{1}}]\cdots\Delta_{l}[U_{i_{k}i_{l}}]).

Consider now the
n
-tuple of operators
M_{\Lambda}=(M_{\Lambda,1},\dots,M_{\Lambda,n})
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}(\mathbb{D}^{l})
consisting of
l
operators

\{\tau_{\Lambda}V_{i_{j}}\tau_{\Lambda}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{l}
and
(n-l)
operators

\{\tau_{\Lambda}V_{i_{k}}\tau_{\Lambda}^{*}\}_{k=l+1}^{n}
, where

(6.2)

\displaystyle M_{\Lambda,s}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}M_{z_{1}}&\text{if $s=i_{1}$}\\
M_{z_{j}}(\Delta_{1}[U_{i_{j}i_{1}}]\cdots\Delta_{j-1}[U_{i_{j}i_{j-1}}])&\text{if $s=i_{j}$ and $2\leq j\leq l$}\\
(I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{l})}\otimes V_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}})(\Delta_{1}[U_{i_{k}i_{1}}]\cdots\Delta_{l}[U_{i_{k}i_{l}}])&\text{ if $s=i_{k}$ and $l+1\leq k\leq n$},\end{cases}

and
s\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Note that

\tau_{\Lambda}\mathcal{N}(V_{i_{j}}^{*})=\tau_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\ominus V_{i_{j}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda})=H^{2}_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}(\mathbb{D}^{l})\ominus M_{\Lambda,i_{j}}H^{2}_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}(\mathbb{D}^{l})=\mathcal{N}(M_{\Lambda,i_{j}}^{*})

for
1\leq j\leq l
. Therefore,
\tau_{\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=\bigcap\limits_{i_{j}\in\Lambda}\mathcal{N}(M_{\Lambda,i_{j}}^{*})
. Again for each
\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-l}
,

\tau_{\Lambda}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}=(\tau_{\Lambda}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\tau_{\Lambda}^{*})(\tau_{\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda})=M_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\big(\bigcap\limits_{i_{j}\in\Lambda}\mathcal{N}(M_{\Lambda,i_{j}}^{*})\big).

It follows that

(6.3)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V))=\tau_{\Lambda}\big(\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-l}}V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}\big)=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-l}}M_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\big(\bigcap\limits_{i_{j}\in\Lambda}\mathcal{N}(M_{\Lambda,i_{j}}^{*})\big)=\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(M_{\Lambda}).

From equation (
6.1
), we get
\tau_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V))=\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)
. Therefore,
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)=\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(M_{\Lambda})
. Now, for each
i_{k}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
and
\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}
, we have

M_{\Lambda,i_{k}}(\xi)=(\tau_{\Lambda}V_{i_{k}}\tau_{\Lambda}^{*})(\xi)=\tau_{\Lambda}(V_{i_{k}}\xi)=V_{i_{k}}\xi

as
\tau_{\Lambda}^{*}\xi=\xi
and
V_{i_{k}}\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}
. Hence

M_{\Lambda,i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}=V_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}
.

We summarize the previous deliberation as follows:

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}
, and
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
be the Wold decomposition for an
n
-tuple of isometries
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
with equal range on
\mathcal{H}
. If

M_{\Lambda}=(M_{\Lambda,1},\ldots,M_{{\Lambda},n})
on

, where

’s are defined by equation (
6.2
). If

is expressed as

and the other wandering data for

are zero tuples.

We refer
M_{\Lambda}
as the model operator corresponding to each
\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}
. It is easy to see that the model operator

is an
n
-tuple of
isometries with equal range
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}(\mathbb{D}^{|\Lambda|})
. Our goal is now to formulate an analytic model for an
n
-tuple of isometries

with equal range on
\mathcal{H}
. To do that, we will apply Proposition
6.7
to each
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
for
\Lambda(\neq\emptyset)\subseteq I_{n}
and then assemble all the pieces together. Indeed, we have the following result:

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of isometries with equal range on
\mathcal{H}
. Then
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
is unitarily equivalent to
(M(V_{1}),\ldots,M(V_{n}))
on
\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}H^{2}_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}(\mathbb{D}^{|\Lambda|})
.

Proof.

Suppose
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
is an
n
-tuple of
isometries with equal range
on
\mathcal{H}
. Then Theorem
5.5
yields that there exist
2^{n}
joint
V
-reducing orthogonal decomposition spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
such that
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
. Moreover,

V_{j}=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}V_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}\in\mathcal{B}(\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda})

for each
j\in I_{n}
. Now Proposition
6.7
asserts
that for each
\Lambda(\neq\emptyset)\subseteq I_{n}
,
V|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}=(V_{1}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}},\dots,V_{n}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}})
on
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
is unitarily equivalent to
M_{\Lambda}=(M_{\Lambda,1},\dots,M_{\Lambda,n})
on

via the unitary map
\tau_{\Lambda}:\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}(\mathbb{D}^{|\Lambda|})
, where
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}
is the
\Lambda
-wandering subspace for
V
.
Consider

M(V_{j})=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}M_{\Lambda,j}\in\mathcal{B}\big(\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}H^{2}_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}(\mathbb{D}^{|\Lambda|})\big)

for each

. Define
\tau=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\tau_{\Lambda}
. Clearly,
\tau
is a unitary operator from
\mathcal{H}
to

\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}H^{2}_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}}(\mathbb{D}^{|\Lambda|})
. Therefore, for each

, we have

\tau V_{j}\tau^{*}=\big(\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\tau_{\Lambda}\big)\big(\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}V_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}\big)\big(\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\tau_{\Lambda}^{*}\big)=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\tau_{\Lambda}{V_{j}}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}\tau_{\Lambda}^{*}=\bigoplus_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}M_{\Lambda,j}=M(V_{j}).

Hence

.

7.
Invariants

In this section we show that the wandering data are completely unitary invariants for

n
-tuples of
isometries with equal range
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
on
\mathcal{H}
and
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
on
\mathcal{K}
be two
n
-tuples of isometries with equal range. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
is unitarily equivalent to
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
.

(2)

For all
\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}
,
(I|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)},V_{i_{l+1}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)},\ldots,V_{i_{n}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)})
is unitarily equivalent
to

(I|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)},T_{i_{l+1}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)},\ldots,T_{i_{n}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)})
,
where
i_{k}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
for
k\in\{l+1,\dots,n\}
.

Proof.

(1)\implies(2):

Suppose that the tuples
V
and
T
are unitarily equivalent. Then there exists a unitary map
\Pi:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
such that
\Pi V_{i}=T_{i}\Pi
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Now for each
\Lambda=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{l}\}\subseteq I_{n}
, we have

\Pi(\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}(V))=\bigcap\limits_{i_{j}\in\Lambda}\Pi\mathcal{N}(V_{i_{j}}^{*})=\bigcap\limits_{i_{j}\in\Lambda}\mathcal{N}(T_{i_{j}}^{*})=\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}(T)

as
\Pi\mathcal{N}(V_{i_{j}}^{*})=\Pi\mathcal{H}\ominus\Pi V_{i_{j}}\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{K}\ominus T_{i_{j}}\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{N}(T_{i_{j}}^{*})
for
1\leq j\leq l
. Therefore,

\Pi\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}(\Pi V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\Pi^{*})\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}(T)=\bigcap\limits_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n-|\Lambda|}}T_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}(T)=\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)

where
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)
and
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)
are
\Lambda
-wandering subspaces for the tuples
V
and
T
, respectively. Thus
\Pi|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)}:\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)
is unitary. Again Lemma
6.3
says that
\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)
reduces
V_{i_{k}}
for
i_{k}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda=\{i_{1+1}<\dots<i_{n}\}
. Then, for
i_{k}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
,
\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-|\Lambda|}
and
\eta\in\mathcal{W}_{\Lambda}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big(\Pi|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)}V_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)}\big)V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\eta=(\Pi V_{i_{k}})V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\eta=(T_{i_{k}}\Pi)V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\eta

\displaystyle=\big(T_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)}\Pi|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)}\big)V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\eta

as
V_{I_{n}\backslash\Lambda}^{\bm{q}}\eta\in\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)
. Hence there
exists a unitary operator

\Pi|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)}:\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)
such that
\Pi|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)}V_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)}=T_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)}\Pi|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)}
for
{i_{k}}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
. Thus
(I|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)},V_{i_{l+1}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)},\ldots,V_{i_{n}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)})
is unitarily equivalent to
(I|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)},T_{i_{l+1}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)},\ldots,T_{i_{n}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)})
for all
\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}
.

(2)\implies(1):

Assume that for each
\Lambda=\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{l}\}\subseteq I_{n}
, there exists a unitary map
\pi_{\Lambda}:\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)
such that
\pi_{\Lambda}V_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)}=T_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)}\pi_{\Lambda}
where
i_{k}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
for
k\in\{l+1,\dots,n\}
. Now Theorem
5.5
asserts that
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}
and
\mathcal{K}=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\bigoplus\limits_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{l}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)
and
\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}=\bigoplus\limits_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{l}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(T)
.

For each
\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}
, define a unitary operator
\Phi_{\Lambda}:\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}
by

\Phi_{\Lambda}(V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}U_{i_{j}}\xi):=T_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}(\widetilde{U}_{i_{j}}\pi_{\Lambda}\xi)\quad(\bm{p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{l})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{l},\ \ \xi\in\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V))

where the unitaries
U_{i_{j}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),\widetilde{U}_{i_{j}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
are defined as (see Proposition
6.5
, and Remark
6.6
)

(7.1)

\displaystyle U_{i_{j}}=\begin{cases}V_{\Lambda}^{*(\bm{p}+e_{j})}V_{i_{j}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}&\text{if}\quad i_{j}\in\Lambda,\\
V_{\Lambda}^{*\bm{p}}V_{i_{j}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}&\text{if}\quad i_{j}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda,\end{cases}

and

(7.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{i_{j}}=\begin{cases}T_{\Lambda}^{*(\bm{p}+e_{j})}T_{i_{j}}T_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}&\text{if}\quad i_{j}\in\Lambda,\\
T_{\Lambda}^{*\bm{p}}T_{i_{j}}T_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}&\text{if}\quad i_{j}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda.\end{cases}

In particular, if
i_{j}=i_{1}\in\Lambda
, then
U_{i_{j}}=I_{\mathcal{H}}
and

\widetilde{U}_{i_{j}}=I_{\mathcal{K}}
. Thus
\Phi_{\Lambda}(V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\xi)=T_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}(\pi_{\Lambda}\xi)
for
\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{l},\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}(V)
. Now for each
i_{j}\in\Lambda
, using equation (
7.1
),
we get

\displaystyle(\Phi_{\Lambda}V_{i_{j}}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}})(V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\xi)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\Lambda}(V_{i_{j}}V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\xi)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\Lambda}(V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}+e_{j}}U_{i_{j}}\xi)

\displaystyle=T_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}+e_{j}}(\widetilde{U}_{i_{j}}\pi_{\Lambda}\xi)

\displaystyle=T_{i_{j}}|_{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}}T_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}(\pi_{\Lambda}\xi)

\displaystyle=T_{i_{j}}|_{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}}\Phi_{\Lambda}(V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\xi).

It follows that

\Phi_{\Lambda}V_{i_{j}}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}\Phi_{\Lambda}^{*}=T_{i_{j}}|_{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}}\quad\text{for}\quad i_{j}\in\Lambda.

For
i_{k}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda
, using equation (
7.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\Phi_{\Lambda}V_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}})(V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\xi)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\Lambda}(V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}U_{i_{k}}\xi)

\displaystyle=T_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}(\widetilde{U}_{i_{k}}\pi_{\Lambda}\xi)

\displaystyle=T_{i_{k}}T_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}(\pi_{\Lambda}\xi)

\displaystyle=T_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}}\Phi_{\Lambda}(V_{\Lambda}^{\bm{p}}\xi).

Therefore,

\Phi_{\Lambda}V_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}\Phi_{\Lambda}^{*}=T_{i_{k}}|_{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}}\quad\text{for}\quad i_{k}\in I_{n}\backslash\Lambda.

Again Theorem
5.5
yields that
V_{j}=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}V_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}
and
T_{j}=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}T_{j}|_{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}}
for each
j=1,2,\dots,n
. We now consider the unitary operator
\Phi=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\Phi_{\Lambda}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
. Then for each
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
,

\Phi V_{j}\Phi^{*}=\big(\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\Phi_{\Lambda}\big)\big(\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}V_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}\big)\big(\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\in I_{n}}\Phi_{\Lambda}^{*}\big)=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}\Phi_{\Lambda}V_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}}\Phi_{\Lambda}^{*}=\bigoplus\limits_{\Lambda\subseteq I_{n}}T_{j}|_{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}}=T_{j}.

This completes the proof.

On The Tree Structure of Natural Numbers, II
Vitalii V. Iudelevich

Abstract
. Each natural number can be associated with some tree graph. Namely, a natural number n n can be factorized as n = p 1 α 1 ​ … ​ p k α k , n=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots p_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}, where p i p_{i} are distinct prime numbers. Since α i \alpha_{i} are naturals, they can be factorized in such a manner as well. This process may be continued, building the ”factorization tree” until all the top numbers are 1 1 . Let H ⁡ ( n ) H(n) be the height of the tree corresponding to the number n n , and let the symbol ↑ ⁣ ↑ \uparrow\uparrow denote tetration. In this paper, we derive the asymptotic formulas for the sums ℳ ⁡ ( x ) = ∑ p ⩽ x H ⁡ ( p − 1 ) , ℋ ⁡ ( x ) = ∑ n ⩽ x 2 ↑ ↑ H ⁡ ( n ) , \mathcal{M}(x)=\sum_{p\leqslant x}H(p-1),\ \ \mathcal{H}(x)=\sum_{n\leqslant x}2\uparrow\uparrow H(n), and ℒ ⁡ ( x ) = ∑ n ⩽ x 2 ↑ ↑ H ⁡ ( n ) 2 ↑ ↑ H ⁡ ( n + 1 ) , \mathcal{L}(x)=\sum_{n\leqslant x}\dfrac{2\uparrow\uparrow H(n)}{2\uparrow\uparrow H(n+1)}, where the summation in the first sum is taken over primes.

Every natural number can be uniquely factorized into a product of prime powers by the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic. The prime powers themselves are integers and thus can be factorized too. We can continue this procedure until all the prime powers become prime. We call the obtained decomposition the

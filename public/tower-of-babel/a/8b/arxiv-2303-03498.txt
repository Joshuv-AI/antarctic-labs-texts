Properties of Marginal Sequential Monte Carlo Methods
Francesca R. Crucinio, Adam M. Johansen

Abstract
We provide a framework which admits a number of “marginal” sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) algorithms as particular cases — including the marginal particle filter [Klaas et al., 2005, in: Proceedings of Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence, pp. 308–315], the independent particle filter [Lin et al., 2005, Journal of the American Statistical Association 100, pp. 1412–1421] and linear-cost Approximate Bayesian Computation SMC [Sisson et al., 2007, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (USA) 104, pp. 1760–1765.]. We provide conditions under which such algorithms obey laws of large numbers and central limit theorems and provide some further asymptotic characterizations. Finally, it is shown that the asymptotic variance of a class of estimators associated with certain marginal SMC algorithms is never greater than that of the estimators provided by a standard SMC algorithm using the same proposal distributions.

Introduction

Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) methods are a class of Monte Carlo methods which approximate a sequence of distributions and integrals with respect to those distributions using a population of weighted samples (or particles) which evolve according to a combination of mutation and selection dynamics.
Such methods became popular in the context of filtering for state-space models in the engineering and statistics literature following the seminal works of
Gordon et al. 1993
, and have been extensively studied from a theoretical perspective as mean field approximations of a Feynman-Kac flow since
Del Moral 1996
. SMC methods have grown in popularity and have been successfully employed in numerous problems including posterior approximation
(
Chopin 2002
;
Del Moral et al. 2006
)
, approximate Bayesian computation
(
Sisson et al. 2007
;
Didelot et al. 2011
)
, Bayesian model comparison
(
Zhou et al. 2016
)
, parameter estimation
(
Poyiadjis et al. 2011
)
. A book-length introductory treatment of SMC is provided by
Chopin and Papaspiliopoulos 2020
.

SMC methods approximate a sequence of distributions
(\hat{\eta}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
defined on Polish spaces
(E^{n},\mathcal{E}^{n})
, where
\mathcal{E}
denotes the
\sigma
-field associated with
E
, of increasing dimension with the relationship

\hat{\eta}_{n}(\mathrm{d}x_{1:n})\propto U_{n}(x_{n-1},x_{n})K_{n}(x_{n-1},\mathrm{d}x_{n})\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{1:n-1})

for some Markov kernels
K_{n}:E\times\mathcal{E}\to[0,1]
and non-negative functions
U_{n}:E\times E\to\real
.

In this work we focus on a particular class of SMC methods, in which the past evolution of the process is marginalized out. Marginal sequential Monte Carlo (MSMC) methods have been introduced in
Klaas et al. 2005
;
Lin et al. 2005
where they were shown empirically to improve upon standard SMC in terms of the conditional variance of their unnormalized importance weights.

Despite being a popular class of algorithms which provides good results in practice (e.g.
Sisson et al. 2007
;
Didelot et al. 2011
;
Everitt et al. 2017
;
Poyiadjis et al. 2011
), the theoretical properties of MSMC are less studied. This paper aims to fill this gap.
We provide a comprehensive analysis of the limiting behaviour of MSMC and show that it shares many of the key properties of standard SMC methods (see, e.g.
Del Moral 2004
).
In particular, we establish that the estimates provided by MSMC methods obey laws of large numbers (
1
and
3
), a central limit theorem (
6
) and show that their
\mathbb{L}_{p}
errors decay at the usual
N^{-1/2}
rate (
2
) and that the bias decays at rate
N^{-1}
(
5
).
Finally, we derive explicit expressions for the asymptotic variance of some popular classes of MSMC methods and provide a comparison with their non-marginal counterparts, showing that the asymptotic variance of the estimates obtained with marginal SMC is never larger than that one would obtain with standard SMC using the same proposals.

Our proofs combine well-known techniques to obtain the analogous results for standard SMC with novel results which control the effect of marginalization.
Embedding this result within the inductive approach normally used to establish the results for standard SMC we obtain a full characterization of MSMC.

We introduce a framework for describing marginal sequential Monte Carlo methods in
Section

2
, where we also present some special cases of particular interest. Our main contributions are in
Section

3
in which we present the theoretical properties of these algorithms and show that their asymptotic variance is never greater than that of standard SMC.
Proofs of the results are postponed to
Appendices

C
,
D
,
F
,
E
and
G
.
We close the paper with a discussion in
Section

4
.

2
Marginal sequential Monte Carlo methods

Marginal sequential Monte Carlo methods (MSMC) are a class of sequential Monte Carlo algorithms introduced in
Klaas et al. 2005
to approximate a sequence of distributions
(\hat{\eta}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
defined on measurable spaces
(E,\mathcal{E})
, where
\mathcal{E}
denotes the
\sigma
-field associated with
E
.
The sequence of distributions satisfies, for some Markov kernels
K_{n}:E\times\mathcal{E}\to[0,1]
and non-negative functions
U_{n}:E\times E\to\real
, the recursion

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}_{n}(\mathrm{d}x_{n})\propto\int U_{n}(x_{n-1},x_{n})K_{n}(x_{n-1},\mathrm{d}x_{n})\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1})

(1)

with
\hat{\eta}_{0}(\mathrm{d}x_{0})\propto U_{0}(x_{0})K_{0}(\mathrm{d}x_{0})
.

Contrary to standard SMC algorithms, in which a cloud of particles is used to approximate a measure-valued recursion defined over
(E^{n},\mathcal{E}^{n})
with approximations of time marginals obtained by discarding part of the sampled paths, MSMC algorithms deal directly with the marginal recursion and the presence of the integral w.r.t.
\hat{\eta}_{n-1}
in (
1
) requires an additional approximation.
Hence, as described below, in MSMC a sample approximation of the integral w.r.t.
\hat{\eta}_{n-1}
is used to define an alternative sequence of targets.

Before describing MSMC algorithms, we describe one step of the idealized algorithms targeting (
1
) which MSMC approximates.
At time
n
, we start with a cloud of equally weighted particles
\{\widetilde{X}_{n-1}^{i},1/N\}_{i=1}^{N}
approximating
\hat{\eta}_{n-1}
.
In the idealized algorithm, the particles are propagated forward in time using proposals

\displaystyle\eta_{n}(\mathrm{d}x_{n})=\hat{\eta}_{n-1}M_{n}(\mathrm{d}x_{n}):=\int M_{n}(x_{n-1},\mathrm{d}x_{n})\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1})

(2)

for
n\geq 1
and some Markov kernels
M_{n}:E\times\mathcal{E}\to[0,1]
to obtain a new cloud of particles
\{X_{n}^{i},1/N\}_{i=1}^{N}
.
The new cloud of particles is used as proposal in an importance sampling step targeting
\hat{\eta}_{n}
, with importance weights proportional to the Radon-Nykodim derivative
\mathrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{n}/\mathrm{d}\eta_{n}

\displaystyle G_{n}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\textrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(U_{n}\cdot K_{n})}{\textrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{n-1}M_{n}}(x_{n})=\frac{\mathrm{d}\left(\int U_{n}(x_{n-1},\cdot)K_{n}(x_{n-1},\cdot)\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1})\right)}{\mathrm{d}\left(\int M_{n}(x_{n-1},\cdot)\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1})\right)}(x_{n}),

(3)

where
(U_{n}\cdot K_{n})(x_{n-1},\mathrm{d}x_{n}):=U_{n}(x_{n-1},x_{n})K_{n}(x_{n-1},\mathrm{d}x_{n})
, to obtain a weighted cloud of particles
\{X_{n}^{i},W_{n}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, with
W_{n}^{i}\propto G_{n}(X_{n}^{i})
, which approximates
\hat{\eta}_{n}
.
The equally weighted population approximating
\hat{\eta}_{n}
,
\{\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i},\frac{1}{N}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, is obtained by resampling from
\{X_{n}^{i},W_{n}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, see e.g.
Gerber et al. 2019
for a recent review of popular resampling schemes.

The target distribution (
1
) involves an intractable integral w.r.t.
\hat{\eta}_{n-1}
. For any probability measure,
\eta
, and non-negative integrable function
G
, we can define
\Psi_{G}(\eta)(\mathrm{d}x):=G(x)\eta(\mathrm{d}x)/\eta(G)
. Given a cloud of weighted particles
\{X_{n-1}^{i},W_{n-1}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
approximating
\hat{\eta}_{n-1}=\Psi_{G_{n-1}}(\eta_{n-1})
we approximate
\hat{\eta}_{n-1}
with

\displaystyle\Psi_{G^{N}_{n-1}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1}):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{n-1}^{i}\delta_{X_{n-1}^{i}}(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1})

(4)

and define the approximate targets

\Psi_{G^{N}_{n-i}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})(U_{n}\cdot K_{n})(\mathrm{d}x_{n})\propto\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{n-1}^{i}U_{n}(X_{n-1}^{i},x_{n})K_{n}(X_{n-1}^{i},\mathrm{d}x_{n}).

We then proceed as in the idealized algorithm, and, since the integrals w.r.t.
\hat{\eta}_{n-1}
are intractable, replace
\hat{\eta}_{n-1}
with its particle approximation obtained at time
n-1
.
Thus, the particles are propagated forward in time using an approximation of the proposals in (
2
),

\eta_{n}(\mathrm{d}x_{n})\approx\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{n-1}^{i}M_{n}(X_{n-1}^{i},\mathrm{d}x_{n})
,
and resampled using approximate weights

\displaystyle G_{n}^{N}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\textrm{d}\Psi_{G^{N}_{n-i}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})(U_{n}\cdot K_{n})}{\textrm{d}\Psi_{G^{N}_{n-i}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})M_{n}}(x_{n})=\frac{\mathrm{d}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{n-1}^{i}U_{n}(X_{n-1}^{i},\cdot)K_{n}(X_{n-1}^{i},\cdot)\right)}{\mathrm{d}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{n-1}^{i}M_{n}(X_{n-1}^{i},\cdot)\right)}(x_{n}).

(5)

The resulting MSMC method is summarized in
Algorithm

1
. For convenience, we identify the three fundamental steps of
Algorithm

1
as a
mutation
step (
7
),
reweighting
steps (
8
and
9
) and a
resampling
step (
10
). To each step, we associate a measure and its corresponding particle approximation: the mutated measure
\eta_{n}
in (
2
) is approximated by
\eta^{N}_{n}:=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{n}^{i}}
obtained after
7
,

and

provide a particle approximation (
4
) of the reweighted measure
\hat{\eta}_{n}=\Psi_{G_{n}}(\eta_{n})
,
after resampling we obtain another approximation of
\hat{\eta}_{n}
in (
1
),
\hat{\eta}_{n}^{N}:=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\tilde{X}_{n}^{i}}
.

2.1
Examples of Marginal SMC

In the previous section we introduced the general class of marginal sequential Monte Carlo methods. This class encompasses a number of well-known algorithms, we briefly discuss some examples below.

2.1.1
Marginal Particle Filters

Marginal particle filters (MPF;
Klaas et al. 2005
) are a class of algorithms to perform inference on state space models (SSM), a family of time series models consisting of two discrete-time processes: a latent process
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and conditionally independent observations
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. Such a SSM
(X_{n},Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is defined by the transition density
f_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1})
of the latent process, with the convention that
f_{0}(x_{0}|x_{-1})\equiv f_{0}(x_{0})
, and the observation likelihood
g_{n}(y_{n}|x_{n})
.

In this case, the target distribution is the filtering distribution

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}_{n}(\mathrm{d}x_{n})\equiv p(x_{n}|y_{1:n})\mathrm{d}x_{n}\propto g_{n}(y_{n}|x_{n})\int f_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1})\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1})\mathrm{d}x_{n},

(6)

with
\hat{\eta}_{0}(\mathrm{d}x_{0})=f_{0}(x_{0})\mathrm{d}x_{0}
,
K_{n}(x_{n-1},\mathrm{d}x_{n})=f_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1})\mathrm{d}x_{n}
and
U_{n}(x_{n-1},x_{n})\equiv U_{n}(x_{n})=g_{n}(y_{n}|x_{n})
, and proposal density which can incorporate the observation
y_{n}
,
M_{n}(x_{n-1},\mathrm{d}x_{n})=q_{n}(x_{n}|y_{n},x_{n-1})\mathrm{d}x_{n}
. The corresponding weights are given by

\displaystyle G_{n}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=g_{n}(y_{n}|x_{n})\frac{\int f_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1})\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1})}{\int q_{n}(x_{n}|y_{n},x_{n-1})\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1})};

\displaystyle G_{n}^{N}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=g_{n}(y_{n}|x_{n})\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{n-1}^{i}f_{n}(x_{n}|X_{n-1}^{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{n-1}^{i}q_{n}(x_{n}|y_{n},X_{n-1}^{i})}.

Similar algorithms have also been employed to approximate the score function when performing parameter estimation for SSM
(
Poyiadjis et al. 2011
)
.

2.1.2
Marginal Auxiliary Particle Filters

Marginal auxiliary particle filters (MAPFs) are a variant of MPF introduced in
Klaas et al. 2005
, as a marginalized version of standard auxiliary particle filters
(
Pitt and Shephard 1999
;
Carpenter et al. 1999
)
.
MAPF can be described as standard MPF applied to
\hat{\eta}_{0}(\mathrm{d}x_{0})\propto f_{0}(x_{0})\tilde{p}(y_{1}|x_{0})\mathrm{d}x_{0}
and

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}_{n}(\mathrm{d}x_{n})\propto\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x_{n})p(x_{n}|y_{1:n})\mathrm{d}x_{n}\propto

\displaystyle\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x_{n})g(y_{n}|x_{n})\left(\int f_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1})p(x_{n-1}|y_{1:n-1})\mathrm{d}x_{n-1}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{n}

(7)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\propto

\displaystyle\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x_{n})g(y_{n}|x_{n})\left(\int\frac{f_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1})}{\tilde{p}(y_{n}|x_{n-1})}\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1})\right)\mathrm{d}x_{n},

where
\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x_{n})
is an approximation of
p(y_{n+1}|x_{n}):=\int g_{n+1}(y_{n+1}|x_{n+1})f_{n+1}(x_{n+1}|x_{n})\mathrm{d}x_{n+1}
, to which an importance sampling step is added to guarantee that we are targeting the correct distribution
p(x_{n}|y_{1:n})

(
Johansen and Doucet 2008
)
.

Setting
K_{n}(x_{n-1},\mathrm{d}x_{n})=f_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1})\mathrm{d}x_{n}
and
U_{n}(x_{n-1},x_{n})=g_{n}(y_{n}|x_{n})\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x_{n})/\tilde{p}(y_{n}|x_{n-1})
,
one can apply
Algorithm

1
with proposal kernel
M_{n}(x_{n-1},\mathrm{d}x_{n})=q_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1},y_{n})\mathrm{d}x_{n}
which incorporates the current observation so that the weights are

\displaystyle G_{n}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=g_{n}(y_{n}|x_{n})\frac{\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x_{n})\int f_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1})/\tilde{p}(y_{n}|x_{n-1})\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1})}{\int q_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1},y_{n})\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1})},

\displaystyle G_{n}^{N}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=g_{n}(y_{n}|x_{n})\frac{\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x_{n})\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{n-1}^{i}f_{n}(x_{n}|X_{n-1}^{i})/\tilde{p}(y_{n}|X_{n-1}^{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{n-1}^{i}q_{n}(x_{n}|X_{n-1}^{i},y_{n})}.

To obtain an algorithm targeting
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{d}x_{n}):=p(x_{n}|y_{1:n})\mathrm{d}x_{n}
, one additional importance sampling step is applied, using as proposal the approximation of
\hat{\eta}_{n}
before resampling,
\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})
, and importance weights

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}_{n}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi_{n}}{\mathrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{n}}(x_{n})\propto\frac{1}{\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x_{n})}.

(8)

We point out that the description of MAPF given here is convenient to obtain the theoretical characterization below, but, in practice one would use
\eta^{N}_{n}
as a proposal in a importance sampling step with weights

\displaystyle G_{n}^{N}(x_{n})\cdot\widetilde{w}_{n}(x_{n})=g_{n}(y_{n}|x_{n})\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{n-1}^{i}f_{n}(x_{n}|X_{n-1}^{i})/\tilde{p}(y_{n}|X_{n-1}^{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{n-1}^{i}q_{n}(x_{n}|X_{n-1}^{i},y_{n})},

where
W_{n-1}^{i}
denote the
simulation
(or
auxiliary
) weights defined in
Klaas et al. 2005
and
W_{n-1}^{i}/\tilde{p}(y_{n}|X_{n-1}^{i})
the
inferential
weights (see also
Chopin and Papaspiliopoulos 2020
).

2.1.3
Independent Particle Filters

Independent particle filters (IPF) are a class of particle filters introduced in
Lin et al. 2005
built to deal with SSM for which the current observation provides significant information about the current state but the transition dynamics are weak.
In these scenarios, it is natural to consider proposal kernels
M_{n}
which result in draws of
x_{n}
which are conditionally independent of individual past particles and of each
other, i.e.
M_{n}(x_{n-1},\mathrm{d}x_{n})=q_{n}(x_{n}|y_{n})\mathrm{d}x_{n}
.
Taking
g_{n}
and
f_{n}
as in
Section

2.1.1
, one obtains the basic IPF as a special case of marginal SMC (see also
Lai et al. 2022
) in which
U_{n}(x_{n-1},x_{n})=g_{n}(y_{n}|x_{n})
,
K_{n}(x_{n-1},\mathrm{d}x_{n})=f_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1})\mathrm{d}x_{n}
.

2.1.4
SMC for Approximate Bayesian Computation

ABC-SMC is an instance of SMC samplers
(
Del Moral et al. 2006
)
studied in
Sisson et al. 2007
;
Didelot et al. 2011
which
approximates the posterior distribution of a parameter
\theta
when the likelihood function
p(y_{obs}|\theta)
is intractable but can be sampled from.
In this case, the sequence of distributions is defined over the space of parameter and data, i.e.
x_{n}=(\theta_{n},y_{n})
, and is given by
\hat{\eta}_{n}(\mathrm{d}(\theta_{n},y_{n}))\propto p(\mathrm{d}\theta_{n})p(\mathrm{d}y_{n}|\theta_{n})\pi_{\epsilon_{n}}(y_{obs}|y_{n})
, for some decreasing sequence
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, where
p(\mathrm{d}\theta)
is a prior on the parameter
\theta
,
p(\cdot|\theta)
denotes the intractable likelihood and
\pi_{\epsilon_{n}}
is the density of a normalized kernel with a degree of concentration determined by
\epsilon_{n}
which measures how close
y_{n}
is to the observed data
y_{obs}
.

The ABC-SMC algorithm of
Sisson et al. 2007
is an instance of marginal SMC with

\displaystyle K_{n}((\theta_{n-1},y_{n-1}),\mathrm{d}(\theta_{n},y_{n}))

\displaystyle=p(\mathrm{d}\theta_{n})p(\mathrm{d}y_{n}|\theta_{n})

\displaystyle U_{n}((\theta_{n-1},y_{n-1}),(\theta_{n},y_{n}))

\displaystyle=U_{n}((\theta_{n},y_{n}))=\pi_{\epsilon_{n}}(y_{obs}|y_{n})

\displaystyle M_{n}((\theta_{n-1},y_{n-1}),\mathrm{d}(\theta_{n},y_{n}))

\displaystyle=q_{n}(\mathrm{d}\theta_{n}|\theta_{n-1})p(\mathrm{d}y_{n}|\theta_{n}),

for some proposal
q_{n}
(we assume for brevity that the observations themselves are used, rather than some summary statistic but the use of such statistics does not present any substantial difficulties).
A similar marginal SMC algorithm has also been considered in
Everitt et al. 2017
for doubly intractable models.

3
Convergence Results

We now state our main results, which show that marginal SMC methods have qualitatively the same convergence properties as standard SMC methods.
We give an overview of the proofs for these results in
Section

3.1
, full details are given in
Appendices

C
,
E
,
D
and
F
.
For simplicity, we focus on the case of measurable bounded test functions
\varphi:E\to\real
with
\left|\left|\varphi\right|\right|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in E}|\varphi(x)|<\infty
, a set we denote by
\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
.
For any distribution
\eta
and any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
we denote
\eta(\varphi):=\int\varphi(x)\eta(\mathrm{d}x)
, similarly for all empirical distributions
\eta^{N}:=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X^{i}}
we denote the corresponding average by
\eta^{N}(\varphi):=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi(X^{i})
.

These results are presented under fairly strong assumptions, which are somewhat standard in this literature, in the interests of brevity. The techniques which allow these to be relaxed in the standard case would also apply here, but their use would substantially complicate the presentation. Similarly, in our arguments we only consider multinomial resampling
(
Gordon et al. 1993
)
. Lower variance resampling schemes can be employed but considerably complicate the theoretical analysis
(
Gerber et al. 2019
)
.

Assumption 1
.

The potentials
G_{n}
are positive everywhere,
G_{n}(x_{n})>0
for every
x_{n}\in E
.

Assumption 2
.

For all
n\geq 0
, the functions
U_{n}
are bounded above, i.e.

U_{n}(x_{n-1},x_{n})\leq\left|\left|U_{n}\right|\right|_{\infty}<\infty
, and the Radon-Nykodim derivative
\mathrm{d}K_{n}(x_{n-1},\cdot)/\mathrm{d}M_{n}(x_{n-1},\cdot)
is bounded above for all
x_{n-1}
, i.e. there exists some
\alpha>0
such that, for every
x_{n-1}\in E
,
\left|\left|\mathrm{d}K_{n}(x_{n-1},\cdot)/\mathrm{d}M_{n}(x_{n-1},\cdot)\right|\right|_{\infty}\leq\alpha<\infty
.

Assumption 3
.

For all
n\geq 0
, the functions
U_{n}
are bounded below, and the Radon-Nykodim derivative
\mathrm{d}K_{n}(x_{n-1},\cdot)/\mathrm{d}M_{n}(x_{n-1},\cdot)
is bounded below for all
x_{n-1}
, i.e. there exist
\beta>0
such that
0<\beta\leq U_{n}(x_{n-1},x_{n})
and
0<\beta\leq\mathrm{d}K_{n}(x_{n-1},\cdot)/\mathrm{d}M_{n}(x_{n-1},\cdot)
uniformly in
x_{n-1}
.

These assumptions are common in the SMC literature
(
Del Moral 2004
;
Del Moral 2013
)
, in particular
1
(and its stronger version
3
) ensure that the system does not become extinct (i.e. the weights have never all simultaneously taken the value zero), and can be relaxed in various ways, including introducing stopping times
(
Del Moral 2004
)
or considering local boundedness
(
Whiteley 2013
)
.

2
ensures that, uniformly in
x\in E
,
G_{n}(x)\leq m_{g},\ G_{n}^{N}(x)\leq m_{g}
, whereas
3
further guarantees

G_{n}(x)\geq m_{g}^{-1},\ G_{n}^{N}(x)\geq m_{g}^{-1}
, where

3.1
Method of proof

We emphasize that the proof techniques used are small extensions of those used in the standard SMC setting; the primary interest of these results is that they demonstrate that the marginal version of the algorithm inherits many of the good properties of the standard algorithm and allowing comparison between the two algorithms via their asymptotic variances.
The details of the proofs are postponed to
Appendices

C
,
D
,
F
,
E
and

where we give self-contained arguments incorporating the novel elements discussed in this section with well-known techniques used to obtain similar results for standard SMC.

The main difference between standard sequential Monte Carlo methods and marginal SMC methods is the presence of an additional approximations in the weights (
5
); if we could use the idealized algorithm in which
G_{n}
in (
3
) can be computed exactly, then we could apply the theoretical results for standard SMC (e.g.,
Del Moral 2013

Hence, to obtain convergence results for marginal SMC we need to control the behaviour of the non-standard weights
G_{n}^{N}
. We point out that since the weights
G_{n}^{N}
are biased approximations of
G_{n}
, we cannot use the arguments based on extensions of the state space (as in particle filters using unbiased estimates of the potentials;
Fearnhead et al. 2008
) to provide theoretical guarantees for MSMC.

To control the effect of
G_{n}^{N}
, we identify the conditional expectation of
\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)
in
7
extending the result of
Branchini and Elvira 2021
.
7

Appendix

For the proof of the

4
Discussion

In this work we established that a class of marginal sequential Monte Carlo (MSMC) algorithms, which encompasses marginal particle filters and other popular algorithms in the literature, satisfies many of the key properties that standard sequential Monte Carlo methods have.
The results in
Section

3
guarantee that the estimates provided by MSMC are consistent, asymptotically normal, with a bias decaying at rate
N^{-1}
and
\mathbb{L}_{p}
error decaying at rate
N^{-1/2}
.

Comparing the asymptotic variances in
6
with those for standard SMC obtained in, e.g.,
Chopin 2004
;
Del Moral 2004
, we find that marginal particle filters have no larger asymptotic variance than the corresponding non-marginal particle filter (
Corollaries

1
and
2
), a phenomenon already observed empirically (e.g.
Klaas et al. 2005
;
Xu and Jasra 2019
).

Corollary

2
complements
Klaas et al. 2005
showing that the importance sampling weights of AMPF have lower (conditional) variance.

Unbiasedness of the normalizing constant estimates has been shown in
Branchini and Elvira 2021
.
Combining
7
with the approach of
Branchini and Elvira 2021
one can further show that the unnormalized flow provides unbiased estimates.

Quantifying the variance reduction obtained by employing marginal particle filters instead of standard particle filter is a more challenging question, the answer to which is likely to be dependent on the specific state space model and proposals
q_{k}
. The variance reduction obtained by using MPF instead of PF should then be weighted against the additional computation cost required by MPF w.r.t. the
O(N)
cost of PF. In their most naive implementation MPF require an
O(N^{2})
cost, which can however be reduced to
O(N\log N)
using techniques from
N
-body learning (e.g.
Gray and Moore 2000
;
Lang et al. 2005
) as shown in
Klaas et al. 2005
or efficient implementations using GPUs
(
Charlier et al. 2021
)
as shown in
Clarté et al. 2022
for sums of the form of those in (
5
).

Finally,
we expect that similar ideas could be applied to more sophisticated SMC algorithms employing marginalization techniques (e.g.
Xu and Jasra 2019
;
Crucinio and Johansen 2023
).

As a preliminary we reproduce part of

, a Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund-type inequality of which we will make extensive use.

Lemma 1

(Del Moral, 2004)
.

Given a sequence of probability measures
(\mu_{i})_{i\geq 1}
on a given measurable space
(E,\mathcal{E})
and a collection of independent random variables, one distributed according to each of those measures,
(X_{i})_{i\geq 1}
, where
\forall i,X_{i}\sim\mu_{i}
, together with any sequence of measurable functions
(h_{i})_{i\geq 1}
such that
\mu_{i}(h_{i})=0
for all
i\geq 1
, we define for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

m_{N}(X)(h)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}(X_{i})\ \textrm{ and }\ \sigma_{N}^{2}(h)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\sup(h_{i})-\inf(h_{i})\right)^{2}.

If the
h_{i}
have finite oscillations (i.e.,
\sup(h_{i})-\inf(h_{i})<\infty\ \forall i\geq 1
) then we have:

\sqrt{N}\Exp\left[\left|m_{N}(X)(h)\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leq b(p)^{1/p}\sigma_{N}(h),

with, for any pair of integers
q,p
such that
q\geq p\geq 1
, denoting
(q)_{p}=q!/(q-p)!
:

\displaystyle b(2q)=(2q)_{q}2^{-q}\ \textrm{ and }\ b(2q-1)=\frac{(2q-1)_{q}}{\sqrt{q-\frac{1}{2}}}2^{-(q-\frac{1}{2})}.

(13)

We are now ready to prove
2
:

2
.

We proceed by induction, taking
n=0
as the base case.
At time

, the particles
(X_{0}^{i})_{i=1}^{N}

\Exp\left[\varphi(X_{0}^{i})\right]=\eta_{0}(\varphi)
for

for

\displaystyle\eta_{0}^{N}(\varphi)-\eta_{0}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_{0}^{i}(X_{0}^{i}),

and apply

Lemma 2

(Mutation)
.

Under the conditions of
2
, assume that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
, for some
p\geq 1
and some finite constant
C_{p,n-1}

\Exp\left[|\hat{\eta}^{N}_{n-1}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\varphi)|^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leq C_{p,n-1}\frac{\left|\left|\varphi\right|\right|_{\infty}}{N^{1/2}}

then, after the mutation step

\Exp\left[|\eta^{N}_{n}(\varphi)-\eta_{n}(\varphi)|^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leq\widetilde{C}_{p,n}\frac{\left|\left|\varphi\right|\right|_{\infty}}{N^{1/2}}

for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
and for some finite constant
\widetilde{C}_{p,n}

Proof.

The proof follows that of
Crisan and Doucet 2002
. Divide into two terms and apply Minkowski’s inequality

\displaystyle\Exp\left[|\eta^{N}_{n}(\varphi)-\eta_{n}(\varphi)|^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\Exp\left[|\eta^{N}_{n}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n-1}M_{n}(\varphi)|^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\Exp\left[|\eta^{N}_{n}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n-1}^{N}M_{n}(\varphi)|^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle+\Exp\left[|\hat{\eta}_{n-1}^{N}M_{n}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n-1}M_{n}(\varphi)|^{p}\right]^{1/p}.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{n-1}^{N}
denote the
\sigma
-field generated by the particle system up to (and including) time
n-1
before the mutation step at time
n
,
\mathcal{G}_{n-1}^{N}=\sigma\left(\widetilde{X}_{p}^{i}:i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\right)\vee\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}
and consider the sequence of functions
\Delta_{n}^{i}:E\mapsto\real
for

\Delta_{n}^{i}(x):=\varphi(x)-\Exp\left[\varphi(X_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{G}_{n-1}^{N}\right]=\varphi(x)-M_{n}\varphi(\widetilde{X}_{n-1}^{i}).

Conditionally on
\mathcal{G}_{n-1}^{N}
,
\Delta_{n}^{i}(X^{i}_{n}),\ i=1,\ldots,N
are independent and have expectation equal to 0, moreover

\displaystyle\eta^{N}_{n}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n-1}^{N}M_{n}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\varphi(X_{n}^{i})-M_{n}\varphi(\widetilde{X}_{n-1}^{i})\right]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_{n}^{i}(X_{n}^{i}).

Conditioning on
\mathcal{G}_{n-1}^{N}
and applying
Lemma

1
we have, for all
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}\Exp\left[|\eta^{N}_{n}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n-1}^{N}M_{n}(\varphi)|^{p}\mid\mathcal{G}_{n-1}^{N}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq b(p)^{1/p}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\sup(\Delta_{n}^{i})-\inf(\Delta_{n}^{i})\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(17)

\displaystyle\leq b(p)^{1/p}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}4\left(\sup|\Delta_{n}^{i}|\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq b(p)^{1/p}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}16\left|\left|\varphi\right|\right|_{\infty}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 4b(p)^{1/p}\left|\left|\varphi\right|\right|_{\infty}

with

as in (
13

Combining this result with the hypothesis yields

\displaystyle\Exp\left[|\eta^{N}_{n}(\varphi)-\eta_{n}(\varphi)|^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leq(4b(p)^{1/p}+C_{p,n-1})\frac{\left|\left|\varphi\right|\right|_{\infty}}{N^{1/2}},

Lemma 3

(Weight Comparison)

Under the conditions of

2

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}\Exp\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)-\Exp\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]|^{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]^{1/p}

Lemma 4

(Approximate reweighting)
.

Under the conditions of
2
, assume that for
p\geq 1
and some finite constants and

Apply the definition of
\Psi_{G_{n}}

\displaystyle|\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)-\Psi_{G_{n}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)|

\displaystyle\leq(4b(p)^{1/p}+\bar{C}_{p,n})\frac{\left|\left|\varphi\right|\right|_{\infty}}{N^{1/2}}.

Thus,

Lemma 6

(Mutation)
.

Assume that, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
,
\hat{\eta}^{N}_{n-1}(\varphi)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\varphi)
, then, after the mutation step,
\eta^{N}_{n}(\varphi)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\eta_{n}(\varphi)

for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
.

Proof.

Using (
17
) and Markov’s inequality, it follows that
\eta^{N}_{n}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n-1}^{N}M_{n}(\varphi)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}0
.
We can use the hypothesis to get

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}_{n-1}^{N}M_{n}(\varphi)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\hat{\eta}_{n-1}M_{n}(\varphi).

Combining the two results above and using Slutzky’s lemma we obtain

Lemma 7

(Weight Comparison)
.

Assume that, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
,
\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})(\varphi)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\Psi_{G_{n-1}}(\eta_{n-1})(\varphi)
,
then
\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)
, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
.

Proof.

Consider the decomposition

\displaystyle\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)-\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)

\displaystyle=\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)-\Exp\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]+\Exp\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]-\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi).

Using the hypothesis,
7
and (
12
), we have that
\Exp\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)
.
In addition, we have that, almost surely,

\displaystyle\Exp\left[|\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)-\Exp\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]|^{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]^{1/p}\leq 4b(p)^{1/p}m_{g}\frac{\left|\left|\varphi\right|\right|_{\infty}}{N^{1/2}},

as shown in
Lemma

3

Hence, by Markov’s inequality,
\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)-\Exp\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\overset{p}{\rightarrow}0
.
Therefore,
\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)
.
∎

Lemma 8

(Approximate reweighting)
.

Assume that, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
,
\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)
, then
\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\Psi_{G_{n}}(\eta_{n})(\varphi)

for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
.

Proof.

Take the definition of
\Psi_{G_{n}}
and
\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}

\displaystyle\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)-\Psi_{G_{n}}(\eta_{n})(\varphi)=\frac{\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)}{\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N})}-\frac{\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})}.

A simple application of the continuous mapping theorem to the hypothesis gives

\displaystyle\frac{\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)}{\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N})}\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\frac{\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})},

and the result follows.

Lemma 9

(Multinomial resampling)
.

Assume that, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
,
\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)
,
then after the resampling step performed through multinomial resampling
\hat{\eta}_{n}^{N}(\varphi)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)

for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
.

Proof.

Consider the decomposition

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}_{n}^{N}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)=\hat{\eta}_{n}^{N}(\varphi)-\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)+\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi).

Using (
18

\hat{\eta}_{n}^{N}(\varphi)-\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}0

Since
\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\equiv\Psi_{G_{n}}(\eta_{n})(\varphi)
, this result combined with the hypothesis yields

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}_{n}^{N}(\varphi)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi).

∎

\sqrt{N}\left[\hat{\eta}^{N}_{n-1}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\varphi)\right]\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N\left(0,V_{n-1}(\varphi)\right)

(23)

then, after the mutation step

\sqrt{N}\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(\varphi)-\eta_{n}(\varphi)\right]\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N\left(0,\widetilde{V}_{n}(\varphi)\right)

for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
, where
\widetilde{V}_{n}(\varphi)=\hat{\eta}_{n-1}\left({\rm{var}}_{M_{n}}(\varphi)\right)+V_{n-1}(M_{n}\varphi)
.

Proof.

As for the proof of convergence in mean of order
p
, consider the following decomposition

\displaystyle T_{N}:=\sqrt{N}\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(\varphi)-\eta_{n}(\varphi)\right]=\sqrt{N}\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n-1}^{N}M_{n}(\varphi)\right]+\sqrt{N}\left[\hat{\eta}_{n-1}^{N}M_{n}(\varphi)-\eta_{n}(\varphi)\right]=U_{N}+R_{N}

where
U_{N}:=\sqrt{N}\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n-1}^{N}M_{n}(\varphi)\right]
and
R_{N}:=\sqrt{N}\left[\hat{\eta}_{n-1}^{N}M_{n}(\varphi)-\eta_{n}(\varphi)\right]
.

Consider for
i=1,\ldots,N
the functions
\Delta_{n}^{i}:E\mapsto\mathbb{R}

\Delta_{n}^{i}(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left[\varphi(x)-\Exp\left[\varphi(X_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{G}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\right],

so that

\displaystyle U_{N}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{N}\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n-1}^{N}M_{n}(\varphi)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi(X_{n}^{i})-\Exp\left[\varphi(X_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{G}^{N}_{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_{n}^{i}(X^{i}_{n}).

Let us denote
\mathcal{H}_{n,i}^{N}:=\mathcal{G}_{n-1}^{N}\vee\sigma\left(\left(X_{n}^{j}\right)_{j=1}^{i}\right)
.
Conditional on
\mathcal{G}_{n-1}^{N}
, the
\Delta_{n}^{i}(X^{i}_{n}),\ i=1,\ldots,N
are independent; in addition, due to the boundedness of
\varphi
,
(\Delta_{n}^{i}(X_{n}^{i}),\mathcal{H}_{n,i}^{N})_{i=1}^{N}
is a square integrable martingale difference sequence which satisfies the Lindeberg condition, and for which

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\rm{var}}\left(\Delta_{n}^{i}(X_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{H}^{N}_{n,i-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Exp\left[\Delta_{n}^{i}(X_{n}^{i})^{2}\mid\mathcal{G}^{N}_{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Exp\left[\left(\varphi(X_{n}^{i})-\Exp\left[\varphi(X_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{G}^{N}_{n-1}\right]\right)^{2}\mid\mathcal{G}^{N}_{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\Exp\left[\varphi(X_{n}^{i})^{2}\mid\mathcal{G}^{N}_{n-1}\right]-\Exp\left[\varphi(X_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{G}^{N}_{n-1}\right]^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(M_{n}\varphi^{2}(\widetilde{X}_{n-1}^{i})-\left(M_{n}\varphi(\widetilde{X}_{n-1}^{i})\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{\eta}_{n-1}^{N}\left({\rm{var}}_{M_{n}}\left(\varphi\right)\right)\rightarrow\hat{\eta}_{n-1}\left({\rm{var}}_{M_{n}}\left(\varphi\right)\right)

in probability as
N\rightarrow\infty
(
1
).
Thus the conditions of a suitable CLT for triangular arrays, such as

Douc and Moulines 2008
, are satisfied and we have that

\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuU_{N}\right)\mid\mathcal{G}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\exp\left(-\hat{\eta}_{n-1}\left({\rm{var}}_{M_{n}}\left(\varphi\right)\right)u^{2}/2\right)
.
Straightforward application of the hypothesis leads to

\displaystyle R_{N}=\sqrt{N}\left[\hat{\eta}_{n-1}^{N}M_{n}(\varphi)-\eta_{n}(\varphi)\right]=\sqrt{N}\left[\hat{\eta}_{n-1}^{N}M_{n}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n-1}M_{n}(\varphi)\right]\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N\left(0,V_{n-1}(M_{n}\varphi)\right).

The characteristic function of
T_{N}
is

\Phi_{T_{N}}(u)=\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuT_{N}\right)\right]=\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuR_{N}\right)\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuU_{N}\right)\mid\mathcal{G}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\right].

Thus, by the continuous mapping Theorem and Slutzky’s Lemma,

\exp\left(iuR_{N}\right)\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuU_{N}\right)\mid\mathcal{G}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\exp\left(-\hat{\eta}_{n-1}\left({\rm{var}}_{M_{n}}\left(\varphi\right)\right)u^{2}/2+iuZ\right)

where
Z\sim\N(0,V_{n-1}(M_{n}\varphi))
.
Hence, by the dominated convergence theorem for any real
u
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{T_{N}}(u)=\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuR_{N}\right)\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuU_{N}\right)\mid\mathcal{G}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\right]\rightarrow\exp\left(-(\hat{\eta}_{n-1}\left({\rm{var}}_{M_{n}}\left(\varphi\right)\right)+V_{n-1}(M_{n}\varphi))u^{2}/2\right)

and Lévy’s continuity Theorem (e.g.
Shiryaev 1996
) gives that
T_{N}
follows a Normal distribution with mean 0 and variance
\widetilde{V}_{n}(\varphi)=\hat{\eta}_{n-1}\left({\rm{var}}_{M_{n}}(\varphi)\right)+V_{n-1}(M_{n}\varphi)
.
∎

We next show that the approximate weights also give rise to a random variable converging to a Normal distribution:

Lemma 12

(Weight Comparison)
.

Assume that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
and for some finite
V_{n-1}(\varphi)

\sqrt{N}\left[\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n-1})(\varphi)-\Psi_{G_{n-1}}(\eta_{n-1})(\varphi)\right]\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N\left(0,\bar{V}_{n-1}(\varphi)\right)

then,

\sqrt{N}\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)-\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)\right]\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N\left(0,\widehat{V}_{n}(\varphi)\right)

for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
, where
\widehat{V}_{n}(\varphi)={\rm{var}}_{\eta_{n}}(G_{n}\varphi)+\bar{V}_{n-1}(K_{n}(U_{n}\varphi))
.

Proof.

Consider the following decomposition

\displaystyle T_{N}

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{N}\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)-\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{N}\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)-\Exp\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\right]+\sqrt{N}\left[\Exp\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]-\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)\right]

\displaystyle=U_{N}+R_{N}

where
U_{N}:=\sqrt{N}\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)-\Exp\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\right]
and
R_{N}:=\sqrt{N}\left[\Exp\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]-\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)\right]
.

To control
U_{N}
let us consider the sequence of functions
\Delta_{n}^{i}:E\mapsto\mathbb{R}
for
i=1,\ldots,N

\Delta_{n}^{i}(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left[G_{n}^{N}(x)\varphi(x)-\Exp\left[G_{n}^{N}(X_{n}^{i})\varphi(X_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\right].

so that

\displaystyle U_{N}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{N}\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)-\Exp\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}G_{n}^{N}(X_{n}^{i})\varphi(X_{n}^{i})-\Exp\left[G_{n}^{N}(X_{n}^{i})\varphi(X_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_{n}^{i}(X^{i}_{n}).

Let us denote
\mathcal{H}_{n,i}^{N}:=\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\vee\sigma\left(\left(X_{n}^{j}\right)_{j=1}^{i}\right)
.
Conditional on
\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}
, the
\Delta_{n}^{i}(X^{i}_{n}),\ i=1,\ldots,N
are independent; in addition, due to the boundedness of
\varphi
and
G_{n}^{N}

(\Delta_{n}^{i}(X_{n}^{i}),\mathcal{H}_{n,i}^{N})_{i=1}^{N}
is a square integrable martingale sequence which satisfies the Lindeberg condition, and for which

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\rm{var}}\left(\Delta_{n}^{i}(X_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{H}^{N}_{n,i-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Exp\left[\Delta_{n}^{i}(X_{n}^{i})^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}^{N}_{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Exp\left[\left(G_{n}^{N}(X_{n}^{i})\varphi(X_{n}^{i})-\Exp\left[G_{n}^{N}(X_{n}^{i})\varphi(X_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{F}^{N}_{n-1}\right]\right)^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}^{N}_{n-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\Exp\left[\left(G_{n}^{N}(X_{n}^{i})\varphi(X_{n}^{i})\right)^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}^{N}_{n-1}\right]-\Exp\left[G_{n}^{N}(X_{n}^{i})\varphi(X_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{F}^{N}_{n-1}\right]^{2}\right)

where the last equality follows using (
11

\nu^{N}_{n-1}:=\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})M_{n}

Using

, we have

\displaystyle{\rm{var}}_{\nu^{N}_{n-1}}\left(G_{n}^{N}\varphi\right)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}{\rm{var}}_{\eta_{n}}\left(G_{n}\varphi\right).

Thus, we can apply a CLT for triangular arrays (see

\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuU_{N}\right)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\exp\left(-{\rm{var}}_{\eta_{n}}(\varphi)u^{2}/2\right)

As in the proof of

\displaystyle R_{N}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{N}\left[\Exp\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]-\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{N}\left[\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})(K_{n}(U_{n}\varphi))-\Psi_{G_{n-1}}(\eta_{n-1})(K_{n}(U_{n}\varphi))\right]\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N\left(0,\bar{V}_{n-1}(K_{n}(U_{n}\varphi))\right).

The characteristic function of
T_{N}
is

\Phi_{T_{N}}(u)=\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuT_{N}\right)\right]=\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuR_{N}\right)\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuU_{N}\right)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\right].

By the continuous mapping Theorem and Slutzky’s Lemma,

\displaystyle\exp\left(iuR_{N}\right)\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuU_{N}\right)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\exp\left(-{\rm{var}}_{\eta_{n}}(G_{n}\varphi)u^{2}/2+iuZ\right)

where
Z\sim\N(0,\bar{V}_{n-1}(K_{n}(U_{n}\varphi)))
.
Then, by the same argument used in

,

follows a Normal distribution with mean 0 and variance
\widehat{V}_{n}(\varphi)={\rm{var}}_{\eta_{n}}(G_{n}\varphi)+\bar{V}_{n-1}(K_{n}(U_{n}\varphi))
.
∎

Lemma 13

(Approximate reweighting)
.

Assume that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
and some finite
\widehat{V}_{n}(\varphi)

\sqrt{N}\left[\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)-\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)\right]\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N\left(0,\widehat{V}_{n}(\varphi)\right)

then

\sqrt{N}\left[\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)-\Psi_{G_{n}}(\eta_{n})(\varphi)\right]\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N\left(0,\bar{V}_{n}(\varphi)\right)

for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
, where
\bar{V}_{n}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(U_{n})^{2}}\widehat{V}_{n}\left(\varphi-\Psi_{G_{n}}(\eta_{n})(\varphi)\right)
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{\varphi}:=\varphi-\Psi_{G_{n}}(\eta_{n})(\varphi)
and consider the vector function

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}(x)\\
\psi_{2}(x)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{\varphi}(x)\\
1\end{pmatrix}

with bounded components
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
.

Combining the hypothesis with the Cramér-Wold Theorem (e.g.
Billingsley 1995
) yields

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}\begin{bmatrix}\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\bar{\varphi})-\eta_{n}(G_{n}\bar{\varphi})\\
\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N})-\eta_{n}(G_{n})\\
\end{bmatrix}\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N\left(0,\Sigma_{n}(\psi)\right),\qquad\qquad\Sigma_{n}(\psi):=\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{V}_{n}(\psi_{1})&\textrm{cov}_{n}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\\
\textrm{cov}_{n}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})&\widehat{V}_{n}(\psi_{2})\\
\end{pmatrix},

where
\textrm{cov}_{n}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
denotes the covariance operator induced by
\widehat{V}_{n}
.

Applying the
\delta
-method with function
s(u,v)=u/v
and observing that
\eta_{n}(G_{n}\bar{\varphi})=0
and
\eta_{n}(G_{n})>0
, gives

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}\left[\frac{\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\bar{\varphi})}{\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N})}-\frac{0}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})}\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{N}\left[\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)-\Psi_{G_{n}}(\eta_{n})(\varphi)\right]

\displaystyle\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N\left(0,\nabla^{T}s(0,\eta_{n}(G_{n}))\Sigma_{n}\left(\psi\right)\nabla s(0,\eta_{n}(G_{n}))\right).

The gradient of
s
evaluated at
(0,\eta_{n}(G_{n}))
is

\displaystyle\nabla^{T}s(u,v)\mid_{(u,v)=(0,\eta_{n}(G_{n}))}=\left(\frac{1}{v},-\frac{u}{v^{2}}\right)\big|_{(u,v)=(0,\eta_{n}(G_{n}))}=\left(\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})},0\right),

hence

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}\left[\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)-\Psi_{G_{n}}(\eta_{n})(\varphi)\right]\

\displaystyle\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N\left(0,\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})^{2}}\widehat{V}_{n}\left(\psi_{1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N\left(0,\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})^{2}}\widehat{V}_{n}\left(\varphi-\Psi_{G_{n}}(\eta_{n})(\varphi)\right)\right),

giving
\bar{V}_{n}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})^{2}}\widehat{V}_{n}\left(\varphi-\Psi_{G_{n}}(\eta_{n})(\varphi)\right)
.
∎

Finally, following
Chopin 2004
we establish convergence for the multinomial resampling step.
As shown in
Gerber et al. 2019
the asymptotic variance in the multinomial resampling case provides an upper bound to that obtained with more sophisticated resampling schemes.

Lemma 14

(Multinomial resampling)
.

Assume that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
and for some finite
\bar{V}_{n}(\varphi)

\sqrt{N}\left[\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\right]=\sqrt{N}\left[\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)-\Psi_{G_{n}}(\eta_{n})(\varphi)\right]\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N\left(0,\bar{V}_{n}(\varphi)\right)

(24)

then after the multinomial resampling step

\sqrt{N}\left[\hat{\eta}_{n}^{N}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\right]\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N(0,V_{n}(\varphi))

for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(E)
, where
V_{n}(\varphi)={\rm{var}}_{\hat{\eta}_{n}}(\varphi)+\bar{V}_{n}(\varphi)
.

Proof.

As for the proof of convergence in mean of order
p
, consider the following decomposition

\displaystyle T_{N}:=\sqrt{N}\left[\hat{\eta}_{n}^{N}(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\right]=\sqrt{N}\left[\hat{\eta}_{n}^{N}(\varphi)-\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)\right]+\sqrt{N}\left[\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\right]=U_{N}+R_{N}

where
U_{N}:=\sqrt{N}\left[\hat{\eta}_{n}^{N}(\varphi)-\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)\right]
and
R_{N}:=\sqrt{N}\left[\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\right]
.

Consider for
i=1,\ldots,N
the functions
\Delta_{n}^{i}(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i}):E\mapsto\mathbb{R}
defined for the resampled particles

\Delta_{n}^{i}(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i}):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left[\varphi(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i})-\Exp\left[\varphi(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{F}^{N}_{n}\right]\right]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left[\varphi(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i})-NW_{n}^{i}\varphi(X_{n}^{i})\right],

so that

\displaystyle U_{N}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{N}\left[\hat{\eta}_{n}^{N}(\varphi)-\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\varphi(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i})-NW_{n}^{i}\varphi(X_{n}^{i})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_{n}^{i}(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i}).

Let us denote
\mathcal{H}_{n,i}^{N}:=\mathcal{F}_{n}^{N}\vee\sigma\left(\left(X_{n}^{j}\right)_{j=1}^{i}\right)
.
Conditionally on
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{N}
, the
\varphi(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i})

i=1,\ldots,N
are independent draws from the categorical distribution with probability of outcome
\varphi(X_{n}^{i})
given by
W_{n}^{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,N
.
It follows that the
\Delta_{n}^{i}(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,N
are conditionally independent random variables with mean 0.
Due to the boundedness of
\varphi
,
(\Delta_{n}^{i}(X_{n}^{i}),\mathcal{H}_{n,i}^{N})_{i=1}^{N}
is a square integrable martingale difference sequence which satisfies the Lindeberg condition, and for which

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\rm{var}}\left(\Delta_{n}^{i}(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{H}^{N}_{n,i-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Exp\left[\Delta_{n}^{i}(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i})^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}^{N}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Exp\left[\left(\varphi(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i})-\Exp\left[\varphi(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{F}^{N}_{n}\right]\right)^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}^{N}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\Exp\left[\varphi(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i})^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}^{N}_{n}\right]-\Exp\left[\varphi(\widetilde{X}_{n}^{i})\mid\mathcal{F}^{N}_{n}\right]^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{n}^{i}\varphi^{2}(X_{n}^{i})-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{n}^{i}\varphi(X_{n}^{i})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle={\rm{var}}_{\nu^{N}_{n}}(\varphi),

where the second to last equality is the variance of the multinomial distribution and
\nu^{N}_{n}:=\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})
.
As a consequence of the WLLN (
1
), we have

\displaystyle\nu^{N}_{n}(\varphi)=\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\eta^{N}_{n})(\varphi)=\frac{\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)}{\eta^{N}_{n}(G_{n}^{N})}\longrightarrow\frac{\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})}=\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)

in probability.
Hence,

\displaystyle{\rm{var}}_{\nu_{n}^{N}}(\varphi)=\nu_{n}^{N}(\varphi^{2})-\nu_{n}^{N}(\varphi)^{2}\longrightarrow\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi^{2})-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)^{2}={\rm{var}}_{\hat{\eta}_{n}}(\varphi)\qquad\text{in probability as }N\rightarrow\infty.

We can apply a CLT for triangular arrays (
Douc and Moulines 2008
) to show
\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuU_{N}\right)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}^{N}\right]\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\exp\left(-{\rm{var}}_{\hat{\eta}_{n}}(\varphi)u^{2}/2\right)
.

By applying the hypothesis to

\displaystyle R_{N}=\sqrt{N}\left[\Psi_{G_{n}^{N}}(\hat{\eta}_{n}^{N})(\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\right]\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\N\left(0,\bar{V}_{n}(\varphi)\right).

The characteristic function of
T_{N}
is

\Phi_{T_{N}}(u)=\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuT_{N}\right)\right]=\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuR_{N}\right)\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuU_{N}\right)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}^{N}\right]\right],

and, by the continuous mapping Theorem and Slutzky’s Lemma,

\displaystyle\exp\left(iuR_{N}\right)\Exp\left[\exp\left(iuU_{N}\right)\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}^{N}\right]\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\exp\left(-{\rm{var}}_{\hat{\eta}_{n}}(\varphi)u^{2}/2+iuZ\right)

where
Z\sim\N(0,\bar{V}_{n}(\varphi))
.
Then, by the same argument used in
Lemma

11
,
T_{N}
follows a Normal distribution with mean 0 and variance
V_{n}(\varphi)={\rm{var}}_{\hat{\eta}_{n}}(\varphi)+\bar{V}_{n}(\varphi)
.
∎

\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})\left(M_{n}([G_{n}^{N}\varphi]^{2})\right)=\Exp\left[\eta_{n}^{N}([G_{n}^{N}\varphi]^{2})|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]

\displaystyle\Exp\left[\left\lvert\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})\left(M_{n}((G_{n}^{N}\varphi)^{2})\right)-\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})\left(M_{n}((G_{n}\varphi)^{2})\right)\right\rvert^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\Exp\left[\left\lvert\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Exp\left[\varphi(X_{n}^{i})^{2}[G_{n}^{N}(X^{i}_{n})^{2}-G_{n}(X^{i}_{n})^{2}]|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\right\rvert^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{2\left|\left|\varphi\right|\right|_{\infty}^{2}m_{g}}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Exp\left[\left\lvert\Exp\left[G_{n}^{N}(X^{i}_{n})-G_{n}(X^{i}_{n})|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\right\rvert^{p}\right]^{1/p}.

Computing the conditional expectation above we find

\displaystyle\Exp\left[G_{n}^{N}(X^{i}_{n})-G_{n}(X^{i}_{n})|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]

\displaystyle=\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})(K_{n}(U_{n}))-\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})(M_{n}(G_{n})),

and we obtain the following bound

\displaystyle\Exp\left[\left\lvert\Exp\left[G_{n}^{N}(X^{i}_{n})-G_{n}(X^{i}_{n})|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}^{N}\right]\right\rvert^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\Exp\left[\left\lvert\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})(K_{n}(U_{n}))-\Psi_{G_{n-1}}(\eta_{n-1})(M_{n}(G_{n}))\right\rvert^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle+\Exp\left[\left\lvert\Psi_{G_{n-1}}(\eta_{n-1})(M_{n}(G_{n}))-\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})(M_{n}(G_{n}))\right\rvert^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{C}_{p,n-1}\frac{\left|\left|U_{n}\right|\right|_{\infty}}{N^{1/2}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}+}\bar{C}_{p,n-1}\frac{m_{g}}{N^{1/2}}

where we used the fact that
G_{n}=\mathrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(U_{n}\cdot K_{n})/\mathrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{n-1}M_{n}
in the first expectation and
2
to obtain the bounds.
It follows that

\displaystyle\Exp\left[\left\lvert\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})\left(M_{n}((G_{n}^{N}\varphi)^{2})\right)-\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})\left(M_{n}((G_{n}\varphi)^{2})\right)\right\rvert^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leq\bar{C}_{p,n-1}\frac{2\left|\left|\varphi\right|\right|_{\infty}^{2}m_{g}}{N^{1/2}}(\left|\left|U_{n}\right|\right|_{\infty}+m_{g}),

and thus
\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})\left(M_{n}((G_{n}^{N}\varphi)^{2})\right)-\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})\left(M_{n}((G_{n}\varphi)^{2})\right)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}0
.
The WLLN in
1
then guarantees
\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})\left(M_{n}((G_{n}\varphi)^{2})\right)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\Psi_{G_{n-1}}(\eta_{n-1})\left(M_{n}((G_{n}\varphi)^{2})\right)
and, by the continuous mapping theorem, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})\left(M_{n}((G_{n}^{N}\varphi)^{2})\right)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\Psi_{G_{n-1}}(\eta_{n-1})\left(M_{n}((G_{n}\varphi)^{2})\right).

A further application of the continuous mapping theorem shows that

\displaystyle{\rm{var}}_{\nu^{N}_{n-1}}\left(G_{n}^{N}\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})\left(M_{n}((G_{n}^{N}\varphi)^{2})\right)-\Psi_{G_{n-1}^{N}}(\eta_{n-1}^{N})\left(M_{n}(G_{n}^{N}\varphi)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\Psi_{G_{n-1}}(\eta_{n-1})\left(M_{n}((G_{n}\varphi)^{2})\right)-\Psi_{G_{n-1}}(\eta_{n-1})\left(M_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\eta_{n}((G_{n}\varphi)^{2})-\eta_{n}(G_{n}\varphi)^{2}={\rm{var}}_{\eta_{n}}(G_{n}\varphi).

∎

G.1
Closed Form Expression

To obtain the variance expression in

it is useful to introduce the unnormalized measures

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}_{n}(\mathrm{d}x_{n})=\int U_{n}(x_{n-1},x_{n})K_{n}(x_{n-1},\mathrm{d}x_{n})\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1}),

so that
\hat{\eta}_{n}=\hat{\gamma}_{n}/\hat{\gamma}_{n}(1)
, where
1
denotes the unit function
x\in E\mapsto 1
.
We highlight that in the literature on standard SMC methods the unnormalized measure
\hat{\gamma}_{n}
normally has total mass such that
\hat{\gamma}_{n}(1)=\prod_{p=0}^{n}\eta_{p}(G_{p})
. In our case, the present definition of
\hat{\gamma}_{n}
is more convenient as we work more directly on the marginal space
E
.

With the notation introduced above we have

\displaystyle G_{n}(x_{n})=\frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(U_{n}\cdot K_{n})}{\mathrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{n-1}M_{n}}(x_{n})=\frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(U_{n}\cdot K_{n})}{\mathrm{d}\eta_{n}}(x_{n})=\frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{\gamma}_{n}}{\mathrm{d}\eta_{n}}(x_{n}),

from which it is easy to see that
\eta_{n}(G_{n})=\hat{\gamma}_{n}(1)
and

\displaystyle\frac{G_{n}(x_{n})}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{n}}{\mathrm{d}\eta_{n}}(x_{n})

(26)

for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof of
8
.

We prove the result by induction.
At
n=0
we have

\displaystyle\bar{V}_{0}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\eta_{0}\left[\left(\frac{G_{0}}{\eta_{0}(G_{0})}\left[\varphi-\hat{\eta}_{0}(\varphi)\right]\right)^{2}\right].

At
n=1
, we have from
6

\displaystyle\bar{V}_{1}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta_{1}(G_{1})^{2}}\hat{V}_{1}\left(\varphi-\hat{\eta}_{1}(\varphi)\right)=\frac{1}{\eta_{1}(G_{1})^{2}}{\rm{var}}_{\eta_{1}}(G_{1}\left[\varphi-\hat{\eta}_{1}(\varphi)\right])+\frac{1}{\eta_{1}(G_{1})^{2}}\bar{V}_{0}\left(K_{1}(U_{1}\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{1}(\varphi)K_{1}(U_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta_{1}(G_{1})^{2}}\eta_{1}\left[\left(G_{1}\left[\varphi-\hat{\eta}_{1}(\varphi)\right]\right)^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{\eta_{1}(G_{1})^{2}}\bar{V}_{0}\left(K_{1}(U_{1}\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{1}(\varphi)K_{1}(U_{1})\right),

where the last equality follows from using (
26
). For the second term we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta_{1}(G_{1})^{2}}\bar{V}_{0}\left(K_{1}(U_{1}\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{1}(\varphi)K_{1}(U_{1})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\eta_{1}(G_{1})^{2}}\frac{1}{\eta_{0}(G_{0})^{2}}\eta_{0}\left[G_{0}^{2}\left[K_{1}(U_{1}\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{1}(\varphi)K_{1}(U_{1})-\hat{\eta}_{0}(K_{1}(U_{1}\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{1}(\varphi)K_{1}(U_{1}))\right]^{2}\right]

It follows that

\displaystyle\bar{V}_{1}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta_{1}(G_{1})^{2}}\eta_{1}\left[\left(G_{1}\left[\varphi-\hat{\eta}_{1}(\varphi)\right]\right)^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{\eta_{1}(G_{1})^{2}}\frac{1}{\eta_{0}(G_{0})^{2}}\eta_{0}\left[G_{0}^{2}\left[K_{1}(U_{1}\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{1}(\varphi)K_{1}(U_{1})\right]^{2}\right],

as required.

Whe now establish the inductive step: assuming (
9
) holds for
n-1
, then, using (
26
),

\displaystyle\bar{V}_{n}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})^{2}}\widehat{V}_{n}\left(\varphi-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\right)

(27)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})^{2}}{\rm{var}}_{\eta_{n}}(G_{n}\left[\varphi-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\right])+\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})^{2}}\bar{V}_{n-1}\left(K_{n}(U_{n}\left[\varphi-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\right])\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})^{2}}{\rm{var}}_{\eta_{n}}(G_{n}\left[\varphi-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\right])+\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})^{2}}\bar{V}_{n-1}\left(K_{n}(U_{n}\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)K_{n}(U_{n})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})^{2}}\eta_{n}\left[\left(G_{n}\left[\varphi-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\right]\right)^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})^{2}}\bar{V}_{n-1}\left(K_{n}(U_{n}\varphi)-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)K_{n}(U_{n})\right).

Let us denote

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\eta_{j}(G_{j})^{2}}\eta_{k}\left[\left(G_{k}\left[\Gamma_{k:n}\left(\varphi\right)-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\Gamma_{k:n}(1)\right]\right)^{2}\right].

(29)

Plugging (

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})^{2}}\eta_{n}\left[\left(G_{n}\left[\varphi-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\right]\right)^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(G_{n})^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\prod_{j=k}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\eta_{j}(G_{j})^{2}}\eta_{k}\left[\left(G_{k}\left[\Gamma_{k:n}\left(\varphi\right)-\hat{\eta}_{n}(\varphi)\Gamma_{k:n}(1)\right]\right)^{2}\right]

The result follows by induction.

\displaystyle\qquad=\int\left(\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}\frac{g_{j}(y_{j}|x_{j})f_{j}(x_{j}|x_{j-1})}{\eta_{j}(G_{j})}\right)\frac{\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x_{n})}{\tilde{p}(y_{k+1}|x_{k})}\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi_{n}}{\mathrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{n}}(x_{n})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{k+1:n}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}\frac{p(y_{j}|y_{1:j-1})}{\eta_{j}(G_{j})}\right)\int\frac{p(x_{k:n}|y_{1:n})}{p(x_{k}|y_{1:k})}\frac{\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x_{n})}{\tilde{p}(y_{k+1}|x_{k})}\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi_{n}}{\mathrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{n}}(x_{n})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{k+1:n}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}\frac{p(y_{j}|y_{1:j-1})}{\eta_{j}(G_{j})}\right)\frac{p(x_{k}|y_{1:n})}{p(x_{k}|y_{1:k})}\int p(x_{k+1:n}|x_{k},y_{1:n})\frac{\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x_{n})}{\tilde{p}(y_{k+1}|x_{k})}\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi_{n}}{\mathrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{n}}(x_{n})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{k+1:n}.

Since
\hat{\eta}_{n}(\mathrm{d}x_{n})=\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x_{n})\pi_{n}(\mathrm{d}x_{n})/\int\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x_{n}^{\prime})\pi_{n}(\mathrm{d}x_{n}^{\prime})
and
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{d}x_{n})=p(x_{n}|y_{1:n})\mathrm{d}x_{n}
, we also have that

\displaystyle\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x_{n})\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi_{n}}{\mathrm{d}\hat{\eta}_{n}}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=\int\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|x^{\prime}_{n})p(x_{n}^{\prime}|y_{1:n})\mathrm{d}x_{n}^{\prime}:=\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|y_{1:n})

and

\displaystyle\eta_{j}(G_{j})=p(y_{j}|y_{1:j-1})\frac{\int p(x_{j}|y_{1:j})\tilde{p}(y_{j+1}|x_{j})\mathrm{d}x_{j}}{\int p(x_{j-1}|y_{1:j-1})\tilde{p}(y_{j}|x_{j-1})\mathrm{d}x_{j-1}}=p(y_{j}|y_{1:j-1})\frac{\tilde{p}(y_{j+1}|y_{1:j})}{\tilde{p}(y_{j}|y_{1:j-1})},

from which follows

\displaystyle\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}\frac{p(y_{j}|y_{1:j-1})}{\eta_{j}(G_{j})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{p}(y_{k+1}|y_{1:k})}{\tilde{p}(y_{n+1}|y_{1:n})}.

Using the above we can simplify (
32
) to

\displaystyle\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}\frac{1}{\eta_{j}(G_{j})}\Gamma_{k:n}\left(\tilde{w}_{n}\left[\varphi-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\right)(x_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{p(x_{k}|y_{1:n})}{p(x_{k}|y_{1:k})}\frac{\tilde{p}(y_{k+1}|y_{1:k})}{\tilde{p}(y_{k+1}|x_{k})}\int p(x_{n}|y_{k+1:n},x_{k})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n},

so that for
k\neq 0
we have

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\left[\frac{G_{k}^{2}}{\eta_{k}(G_{k})^{2}}\Gamma_{k:n}\left(\tilde{w}_{n}\left[\varphi-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\right)^{2}\right]\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}\frac{1}{\eta_{j}(G_{j})^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\int\frac{p(x_{k}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{\int q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1}^{\prime},y_{k})\hat{\eta}_{k-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}^{\prime})}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{k+1:n},x_{k})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{k},

and, for
k=0
,

\displaystyle\eta_{0}\left[\frac{G_{0}^{2}}{\eta_{0}(G_{0})^{2}}\Gamma_{0:n}\left(\tilde{w}_{n}\left[\varphi-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\right)^{2}\right]\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\eta_{j}(G_{j})^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\int\frac{p(x_{0}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{q_{0}(x_{0})}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{1:n},x_{0})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{0},

Combining the expressions above we obtain

\displaystyle V^{\textrm{MAPF}}_{n}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p(x_{0}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{q_{0}(x_{0})}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{1:n},x_{0})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{0}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{p(x_{k}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{\int q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1}^{\prime},y_{k})\hat{\eta}_{k-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}^{\prime})}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{k+1:n},x_{k})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{k}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{p(x_{n}|y_{1:n})^{2}\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]^{2}}{\int q_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1}^{\prime},y_{n})\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1}^{\prime})}\mathrm{d}x_{n}.

G.3.1
Variance of FA-MAPF

The variance expression for FA-MAPF follows by plugging into the above
M_{0}=q_{0}=f_{0}
,
\tilde{p}(y_{n}|x_{n-1})=p(y_{n}|x_{n-1})
and
q_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1},y_{n})=\frac{g_{n}(y_{n}|x_{n})f_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1})}{p(y_{n}|x_{n-1})}
.
Observing that

\displaystyle\int q_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1},y_{n})\hat{\eta}_{n-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{g_{n}(y_{n}|x_{n})f_{n}(x_{n-1},x_{n})}{p(y_{n}|x_{n-1})}\frac{p(x_{n-1}|y_{1:n-1})p(y_{n}|x_{n-1})}{\int p(x_{n-1}^{\prime}|y_{1:n-1})p(y_{n}|x_{n-1}^{\prime})\mathrm{d}x_{n-1}^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x_{n-1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{n}(y_{n}|x_{n})\int f_{n}(x_{n-1},x_{n})p(x_{n-1}|y_{1:n-1})\mathrm{d}x_{n-1}}{\int\int g_{n}(y_{n}|x_{n})f_{n}(x_{n-1}^{\prime},x_{n})\mathrm{d}x_{n}p(x_{n-1}^{\prime}|y_{1:n-1})\mathrm{d}x_{n-1}^{\prime}}=p(x_{n}|y_{1:n})

for all
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle V^{\textrm{FA-MAPF}}_{n}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p(x_{0}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{f_{0}(x_{0})}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{1:n},x_{0})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{0}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{p(x_{k}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{p(x_{k}|y_{1:k})}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{k+1:n},x_{k})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{k}

\displaystyle+\int p(x_{n}|y_{1:n})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{n},

which coincides with the variance expression in
Johansen and Doucet 2008
.

G.4
Variance Comparison

We now compare the variance expressions in
Corollaries

1
and
2
with those of the corresponding non-marginal algorithms obtained in
Johansen and Doucet 2008
.

G.4.1
Marginal Particle Filters

The MPF variance in
Corollary

1
can be decomposed as
V_{n}^{\textrm{MPF}}(\varphi)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{MPF}}(\varphi)
where

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,0}^{\textrm{MPF}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p(x_{0}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{q_{0}(x_{0})}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{1:n},x_{0})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{0},

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{MPF}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p(x_{k}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{\int q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{k+1:n},x_{k})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{k}

for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, and

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,n}^{\textrm{MPF}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p(x_{n}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{\int q_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1},y_{n})p(x_{n-1}|y_{1:n-1})\mathrm{d}x_{n-1}}(\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{n},

while the variance of a standard (i.e. not marginal) particle filter given in
Johansen and Doucet 2008
is given by
V_{n}^{\textrm{PF}}(\varphi)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{PF}}(\varphi)
where

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,0}^{\textrm{PF}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p(x_{0}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{q_{0}(x_{0})}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{1:n},x_{0})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{0},

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{PF}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p(x_{0:k}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})p(x_{0:k-1}|y_{1:k-1})}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{k+1:n},x_{k})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{0:k}

for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, and

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,n}^{\textrm{PF}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p(x_{0:n}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{q_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1},y_{n})p(x_{0:n-1}|y_{1:n-1})}(\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{0:n}.

From the above it is easy to see that
\sigma_{n,0}^{\textrm{MPF}}(\varphi)=\sigma_{n,0}^{\textrm{PF}}(\varphi)
.
Using (
30
) we have

\displaystyle p(x_{k}|y_{1:n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\int p(x_{0:n},y_{1:n})\mathrm{d}x_{0:k-1}\mathrm{d}x_{k+1:n}}{p(y_{1:n})}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{k}(y_{k}|x_{k})}{p(y_{k}|y_{1:k-1})}\int f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1})p(x_{0:k-1}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{0:k-1}\int\frac{p(x_{k:n}|y_{1:n})}{p(x_{k}|y_{1:k})}\mathrm{d}x_{k+1:n}

and

\displaystyle p(x_{0:k}|y_{1:n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\int p(x_{0:n},y_{1:n})\mathrm{d}x_{k+1:n}}{p(y_{1:n})}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{k}(y_{k}|x_{k})}{p(y_{k}|y_{1:k-1})}f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1})p(x_{0:k-1}|y_{1:k-1})\int\frac{p(x_{k:n}|y_{1:n})}{p(x_{k}|y_{1:k})}\mathrm{d}x_{k+1:n},

and we can write for all
k\geq 1

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{PF}}(\varphi)-\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{MPF}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int g_{k}(y_{k}|x_{k})^{2}\left(\int\frac{f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1})^{2}p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})}{q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})}\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}-\frac{\left(\int f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1})p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}\right)^{2}}{\int q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\psi_{k}(x_{k})\mathrm{d}x_{k}

where we defined

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x_{k}):=\frac{1}{p(y_{k}|y_{1:k-1})^{2}}\left(\int\frac{p(x_{k:n}|y_{1:n})}{p(x_{k}|y_{1:k})}\mathrm{d}x_{k+1:n}\right)^{2}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{k+1:n},x_{k})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}

for
1\leq k<n
and
\psi_{n}(x_{n}):=\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]^{2}/p(y_{n}|y_{1:n-1})^{2}
for
k=n
.
Since
\psi_{k}
is always positive, we have that
\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{PF}}(\varphi)\geq\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{MPF}}(\varphi)

if for all
x_{k}\in E

\displaystyle g_{k}(y_{k}|x_{k})^{2}\int\frac{f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1})^{2}p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})}{q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})}\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}\geq g_{k}(y_{k}|x_{k})^{2}\frac{\left(\int f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1})p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}\right)^{2}}{\int q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}}.

(33)

The condition in (
33
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\int\left[G_{k}^{\textrm{PF}}(x_{k-1},x_{k})\right]^{2}q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq\left[G_{k}^{\textrm{MPF}}(x_{k})\right]^{2}\int q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}

where

\displaystyle G_{k}^{\textrm{PF}}(x_{k-1},x_{k})=\frac{g_{k}(y_{k}|x_{k})f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1})}{q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})}

and
G_{k}^{\textrm{MPF}}(x_{k})
is given in
Section

2.1.1
. For any fixed
x_{k}\in E
, let us denote by
\nu_{k}(\mathrm{d}x_{k-1},x_{k})
the probability distribution obtained as

\displaystyle\nu_{k}(\mathrm{d}x_{k-1},x_{k})=\frac{q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}}{\int q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1}^{\prime},y_{k})p(x_{k-1}^{\prime}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}^{\prime}},

then the condition above is equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{k}(\cdot,x_{k})}\left[\left[G_{k}^{\textrm{PF}}(\cdot,x_{k})\right]^{2}\right]\geq\left[G_{k}^{\textrm{MPF}}(x_{k})\right]^{2}.

A simple application of Jensen’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{k}(\cdot,x_{k})}\left[\left[G_{k}^{\textrm{PF}}(\cdot,x_{k})\right]^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{k}(\cdot,x_{k})}\left[G_{k}^{\textrm{PF}}(\cdot,x_{k})\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int\frac{q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})}{\int q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1}^{\prime},y_{k})p(x_{k-1}^{\prime}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}^{\prime}}\frac{g_{k}(y_{k}|x_{k})f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1})}{q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})}\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=g_{k}(y_{k}|x_{k})^{2}\frac{\left(\int f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1}^{\prime})p(x_{k-1}^{\prime}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}}{\left(\int q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1}^{\prime},y_{k})p(x_{k-1}^{\prime}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left[G_{k}^{\textrm{MPF}}(x_{k})\right]^{2},

showing that (
33
) is satisfied and thus
\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{PF}}(\varphi)\geq\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{MPF}}(\varphi)
for all
1\leq k\leq n
. It follows straightforwardly that
V_{n}^{\textrm{MPF}}(\varphi)\leq V_{n}^{\textrm{PF}}(\varphi)
.

Since the function
t\mapsto t^{2}
is not affine, equality can only occur when
G_{k}^{\textrm{PF}}(\cdot,x_{k})
is almost surely constant w.r.t.
x_{k-1}
(see, e.g.
Walker 2014
), i.e.
G_{k}^{\textrm{PF}}(\cdot,x_{k})=G_{k}^{\textrm{PF}}(x_{k})
a.s.; this requires
q_{k}\equiv f_{k}
which corresponds to the bootstrap particle filter.
In all other cases, the variance reduction due to marginalization can be quantified using, for example,
Walker 2014
.

G.4.2
Marginal Auxiliary Particle Filters

The MAPF variance in
Corollary

2
can be decomposed as
V_{n}^{\textrm{MAPF}}(\varphi)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{MAPF}}(\varphi)
where

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,0}^{\textrm{MAPF}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p(x_{0}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{q_{0}(x_{0})}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{1:n},x_{0})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{0}

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{MAPF}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p(x_{k}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{\int q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})\tilde{p}(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{k+1:n},x_{k})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{k}

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,n}^{\textrm{MAPF}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p(x_{n}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{\int q_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1},y_{n})\tilde{p}(x_{n-1}|y_{1:n})\mathrm{d}x_{n-1}}(\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{n},

where we denoted
\hat{\eta}_{k-1}(\mathrm{d}x_{k-1})=\tilde{p}(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}\propto p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})\tilde{p}(y_{k}|x_{k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}
.
The variance of a standard (i.e. not marginal) auxiliary particle filter given in
Johansen and Doucet 2008
is given by
V_{n}^{\textrm{APF}}(\varphi)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{APF}}(\varphi)
where

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,0}^{\textrm{APF}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p(x_{0}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{q_{0}(x_{0})}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{1:n},x_{0})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{0}

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{APF}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p(x_{0:k}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})\tilde{p}(x_{0:k-1}|y_{1:k})}\left(\int p(x_{n}|y_{k+1:n},x_{k})\left[\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{n}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{0:k}

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,n}^{\textrm{APF}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{p(x_{0:n}|y_{1:n})^{2}}{q_{n}(x_{n}|x_{n-1},y_{n})\tilde{p}(x_{0:n-1}|y_{1:n})}(\varphi(x_{n})-\bar{\varphi}_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{0:n}.

From the above it is easy to see that
\sigma_{n,0}^{\textrm{MAPF}}(\varphi)=\sigma_{n,0}^{\textrm{APF}}(\varphi)
.
In addition, following the same steps as in
Section

G.4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{APF}}(\varphi)-\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{MAPF}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int g_{k}(y_{k}|x_{k})^{2}\left(\int\frac{f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1})^{2}p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})}{q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})\tilde{p}(y_{k}|x_{k-1})}\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}-\frac{\left(\int f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1})p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}\right)^{2}}{\int q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})\tilde{p}(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\tilde{p}(y_{k}|y_{1:k-1})\psi(x_{k})\mathrm{d}x_{k}

with
\psi_{k}
given in
Section

G.4.1
and

\tilde{p}(y_{k}|y_{1:k-1}):=\int\tilde{p}(y_{k}|x_{k-1})p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}
.
Proceeding as in
Section

G.4.1
, we have
\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{APF}}(\varphi)\geq\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{MAPF}}(\varphi)
if

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{k}(\cdot,x_{k})}\left[\left[G_{k}^{\textrm{APF}}(\cdot,x_{k})\right]^{2}\right]\geq\left[G_{k}^{\textrm{MAPF}}(x_{k})\right]^{2}.

where
\nu_{k}(\cdot,x_{k})
the probability distribution obtained for all
x_{k}\in E
as

\displaystyle\nu_{k}(\mathrm{d}x_{k-1},x_{k})=\frac{\tilde{p}(y_{k}|x_{k-1})q_{k}(x_{k}|\cdot,y_{k})p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}}{\int\tilde{p}(y_{k}|x_{k-1}^{\prime})q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1}^{\prime},y_{k})p(x_{k-1}^{\prime}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}^{\prime}},

the weights are

\displaystyle G_{k}^{\textrm{APF}}(x_{k-1},x_{k})=\frac{g_{k}(y_{k}|x_{k})f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1})}{\tilde{p}(y_{k}|x_{k-1})q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})}

and
G_{k}^{\textrm{MAPF}}(x_{k})
is given in
Section

2.1.2
.
A simple application of Jensen’s inequality then gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{k}(\cdot,x_{k})}\left[\left[G_{k}^{\textrm{APF}}(\cdot,x_{k})\right]^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{k}(\cdot,x_{k})}\left[G_{k}^{\textrm{APF}}(\cdot,x_{k})\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\int\frac{\tilde{p}(y_{k}|x_{k-1})q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})p(x_{k-1}|y_{1:k-1})}{\int\tilde{p}(y_{k}|x_{k-1}^{\prime})q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1}^{\prime},y_{k})p(x_{k-1}^{\prime}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}^{\prime}}\frac{g_{k}(y_{k}|x_{k})f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1})}{\tilde{p}(y_{k}|x_{k-1})q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})}\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=g_{k}(y_{k}|x_{k})^{2}\frac{\left(\int f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1}^{\prime})p(x_{k-1}^{\prime}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}}{\left(\int\tilde{p}(y_{k}|x_{k-1}^{\prime})q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1}^{\prime},y_{k})p(x_{k-1}^{\prime}|y_{1:k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k-1}^{\prime}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left[G_{k}^{\textrm{MAPF}}(x_{k})\right]^{2},

showing that
\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{APF}}(\varphi)\geq\sigma_{n,k}^{\textrm{MAPF}}(\varphi)
for all
1\leq k\leq n
. It follows straightforwardly that
V_{n}^{\textrm{MAPF}}(\varphi)\leq V_{n}^{\textrm{APF}}(\varphi)
.

Since the function
t\mapsto t^{2}
is not affine, equality can only occur when
G_{k}^{\textrm{APF}}(\cdot,x_{k})
is almost surely constant w.r.t.
x_{k-1}
(see, e.g.
Walker 2014
), i.e.
G_{k}^{\textrm{APF}}(\cdot,x_{k})=G_{k}^{\textrm{APF}}(x_{k})
a.s.; this occurs when

\displaystyle q_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1},y_{k})=\frac{C(x_{k})f(x_{k}|x_{k-1})}{\int C(x_{k}^{\prime})f(x_{k}^{\prime}|x_{k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k}^{\prime}}

and
\tilde{p}(y_{k}|x_{k-1})=\int C_{k}(x_{k})f_{k}(x_{k}|x_{k-1})\mathrm{d}x_{k}
, for some positive function
C_{k}
.
In this case, in fact, we have
G_{k}^{\textrm{APF}}(x_{k-1},x_{k})=g_{k}(y_{k}|x_{k})/C_{k}(x_{k})
and we see that the FA-APF satisfies this condition with
C_{k}(x_{k})=g_{k}(y_{k}|x_{k})
.
In all other cases, the variance reduction due to marginalization can be quantified using, for example,
Walker 2014
.

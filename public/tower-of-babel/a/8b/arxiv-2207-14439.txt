Treatment Effect Estimation with Unobserved and Heterogeneous Confounding Variables
Kevin Jiang Yang Ning

Abstract
The estimation of the treatment effect is often biased in the presence of unobserved confounding variables which are commonly referred to as hidden variables. Although a few methods have been recently proposed to handle the effect of hidden variables, these methods often overlook the possibility of any interaction between the observed treatment variable and the unobserved covariates. In this work, we address this shortcoming by studying a multivariate response regression problem with both unobserved and heterogeneous confounding variables of the form 𝒀 = 𝑨 T ​ 𝑿 + 𝑩 T ​ 𝒁 + ∑ j = 1 p 𝑪 j T ​ X j ​ 𝒁 + 𝑬 \bm{Y}=\bm{A}^{T}\bm{X}+\bm{B}^{T}\bm{Z}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bm{C}^{T}_{j}X_{j}\bm{Z}+\bm{E} , where 𝒀 ∈ ℝ m \bm{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m} are m m -dimensional response variables, 𝑿 ∈ ℝ p \bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{p} are observed covariates (including the treatment variable), 𝒁 ∈ ℝ K \bm{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{K} are K K -dimensional unobserved confounders, and 𝑬 ∈ ℝ m \bm{E}\in\mathbb{R}^{m} is the random noise. Allowing for the interaction between X j X_{j} and 𝒁 \bm{Z} induces the heterogeneous confounding effect. Our goal is to estimate the unknown matrix 𝑨 \bm{A} , the direct effect of the observed covariates or the treatment on the responses. To this end, we propose a new debiased estimation approach via SVD to remove the effect of unobserved confounding variables. The rate of convergence of the estimator is established under both the homoscedastic and heteroscedastic noises. We also present several simulation experiments and a real-world data application to substantiate our findings.



Keywords:

Hidden variables, interaction, multivariate response regression, treatment effect estimation, principal component analysis

1
Introduction

Treatment effect estimation in the presence of unobserved confounding variables is a very challenging problem, arising in many areas including statistics, biology, computer science and economics. With some additional domain knowledge, such as the existence of instrumental variables or negative controls, the effect of unobserved confounding variables can be removed, leading to consistent estimation of the treatment effect; see
Wooldridge 2015
;
Lipsitch et al. 2010
for a review. However, if such information is unavailable, how to correct for the bias due to the unobserved confounding variables is largely unexplored.

In this context, a class of methods known as surrogate variable analysis (SVA) has been proposed to account for the hidden variables (e.g., batch effect) in the analysis of genomics data (e.g.
Alter et al. 2000
,
Leek and Storey 2007
,
Sun et al. 2012
). These methods relax the assumptions commonly used in the literature on instrumental variables or negative controls, but still require substantial apriori knowledge of the data or impose a strict structure on the underlying model. For example,
Gagnon-Bartsch and Speed 2012
required knowing a null set of features in the exposure variables,
McKennan and Nicolae 2019
required row-wise sparsity of the non-null features, and
Wang et al. 2017
imposed a linear causal relationship of the observed variables and the hidden variables. More recently,
Bing et al. 2022
extended the methods to deal with the model with high-dimensional features. However, all of these methods ignore the possibility of any interaction between the observed treatment variable and the hidden variables, which appears frequently in the presence of heterogeneous treatment effect (e.g., the treatment effect may vary according to the value of the confounding variables). Failing to account for this structure may lead to model specification, so that the validity and interpretability of statistical findings may be severely limited.

In this paper, we consider the following model

\displaystyle\bm{Y}=\bm{A}^{T}\bm{X}+\bm{B}^{T}\bm{Z}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bm{C}^{T}_{j}X_{j}\bm{Z}+\bm{E},

(1.1)

where
\bm{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are the response variables,
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
are the observed covariates (including the treatment variable and observed confounders),
\bm{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
are the unobserved covariates or hidden variables, and
\bm{E}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are the random errors. The matrices
\bm{A},\bm{B},\bm{C}_{1},...,\bm{C}_{p}
are unknown parameters. The model (
1.1
) allows the hidden variables to interact through the
\bm{C}^{T}_{j}X_{j}\bm{Z}
terms for
j=1,\dots,p
in a multiplicative manner. As a special case of (
1.1
), consider
p=1
and take
X_{1}\in\{0,1\}
to be a binary treatment variable. Then the conditional average treatment effect (CATE) can be shown to be
\bm{A}^{T}+\bm{C}^{T}_{j}\bm{Z}
, which depends on the value of unobserved confounding variable
\bm{Z}
. Thus, model (
1.1
) provides a parsimonious way to account for the heterogeneous treatment effect.

Given
n
i.i.d samples
(\bm{Y}_{i},\bm{X}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, our goal is to estimate the unknown matrix
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m}
, the association between
\bm{X}
and
\bm{Y}
in the presence of hidden variables, which can be also interpreted as the direct effect of the treatment on the response in a causal inference framework
(
Bing et al. 2022
)
. However, in general,
\bm{A}
is not identifiable by only observing
(\bm{Y},\bm{X})
, as
\bm{Z}
can be correlated with
\bm{X}
in an arbitrary way. To address this problem, we construct a parameter
\bm{\Theta}
that approximates
\bm{A}
by teasing out the effect induced by the hidden variables. In particular, we propose a debiased estimator by projecting the response variables to an appropriate singular vector space. Theoretically, we characterize the stochastic error and approximation error of our debiased estimator under both the homoscedastic and the more general heteroscedastic and correlated noises.

The paper is organized as follows. We first give a detailed estimation algorithm in the homoscedastic setting in Section
2
. Section
3
presents our main theoretical result concerning the convergence rate of our debiased estimator. We then extend the method to the heteroscedastic setting in Section
4
by proposing a modified version of our algorithm and giving an adjusted convergence rate. Finally, Sections
5
and
6
give simulation results and a real world data application to high-throughput microarray data.

2
Debiased Estimator via SVD

Recall that in model (
1.1
),
\bm{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are the response variables,
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
are the observed covariates and
\bm{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
are the hidden variables, where the number of hidden variables
K
is unknown and is assumed to be much less than
m
. In addition, we assume the random noise
\bm{E}
is independent of
\bm{X}
and
\bm{Z}
. In this section, we focus on the setting with homoscedastic errors, i.e.,
\Cov(\bm{E})=\sigma^{2}\bm{I}_{m}
, where
\bm{I}_{m}
is an identity matrix. The extension to heterogeneous and correlated errors is studied in Section
4
.

To motivate the proposed method, write
\bm{W}=\bm{Z}-\bm{\psi}^{T}\bm{X}
, where
\bm{\psi}:=\{\mathbb{E}(\bm{X}\bm{X}^{T})\}^{-1}\mathbb{E}(\bm{X}\bm{Z}^{T})\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times K}
is obtained by the
\mathcal{L}_{2}
projection of
\bm{Z}
onto
\bm{X}
. Note that we do not require
\bm{Z}
and
\bm{X}
to be linearly dependent when defining
\bm{W}
. Using this decomposition of
\bm{Z}
, we may rewrite (
1.1
) as

\displaystyle\bm{Y}

\displaystyle=\bm{A}^{T}\bm{X}+(\bm{B}^{T}\bm{W}+\bm{B}^{T}\bm{\psi}^{T}\bm{X})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bm{C}_{j}^{T}X_{j}(\bm{W}+\bm{\psi}^{T}\bm{X})+\bm{E}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{p}(\bm{A}_{(\cdot,j)}^{T}+\bm{B}^{T}\bm{\psi}_{(\cdot,j)}^{T})X_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bm{C}_{j}^{T}X_{j}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{p}\bm{\psi}_{(\cdot,k)}^{T}X_{k}+\bm{B}^{T}\bm{W}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bm{C}_{j}^{T}X_{j}\bm{W}+\bm{E}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bm{L}_{1j}^{T}X_{j}+\sum_{1\leq j\leq k\leq p}\bm{L}_{2,jk}^{T}X_{j}X_{k}+\bm{\epsilon},

(2.1)

where
\bm{L}_{1j}^{T}=\bm{A}_{(\cdot,j)}^{T}+\bm{B}^{T}\bm{\psi}_{(\cdot,j)}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\bm{L}_{2,jk}^{T}=\bm{C}_{j}^{T}\bm{\psi}_{(\cdot,k)}^{T}+\bm{C}_{k}^{T}\bm{\psi}_{(\cdot,j)}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
for
j\neq k
and
\bm{L}_{2,jk}^{T}=\bm{C}_{j}^{T}\bm{\psi}_{(\cdot,k)}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
for
j=k
, and
\bm{\epsilon}=\bm{B}^{T}\bm{W}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bm{C}_{j}^{T}X_{j}\bm{W}+\bm{E}
. Thus, given
n
i.i.d copies of
(\bm{X},\bm{Y})
, we can estimate the coefficient matrices
\bm{L}_{1j},\bm{L}_{2,jk}
from a linear regression with all the linear and pairwise interactions among
\bm{X}
.

The second step is to estimate the covariance matrix of the residuals
\bm{\epsilon}
, which has the following structures

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\bm{\epsilon}\bm{\epsilon}^{T}|\bm{X})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[(\bm{B}^{T}\bm{W}+\bm{E})(\bm{B}^{T}\bm{W}+\bm{E})^{T}|\bm{X}]+\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq p}\mathbb{E}(\bm{C}_{j}^{T}\bm{W}\bm{W}^{T}\bm{C}_{k}|\bm{X})X_{j}X_{k}

\displaystyle~~~+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mathbb{E}(\bm{C}_{j}^{T}\bm{W}(\bm{B}^{T}\bm{W}+\bm{E})^{T}|\bm{X})X_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mathbb{E}((\bm{B}^{T}\bm{W}+\bm{E})\bm{W}^{T}\bm{C}_{j}|\bm{X})X_{j}

\displaystyle=\bm{\phi}_{\bm{B}}+\sum_{1\leq j\leq k\leq p}\bm{\phi}_{(\bm{C}_{j},\bm{C}_{k})}X_{j}X_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bm{\phi}_{(\bm{B},\bm{C}_{j})}X_{j}

(2.2)

where we use the fact that
\bm{E}
is independent of
\bm{X},\bm{Z}
, and
\bm{\phi}_{\bm{B}}=\bm{B}^{T}\Cov(\bm{W}|\bm{X})\bm{B}+\Cov(\bm{E})
,
\bm{\phi}_{(\bm{C}_{j},\bm{C}_{k})}=\bm{C}_{j}^{T}\Cov(\bm{W}|\bm{X})\bm{C}_{k}+\bm{C}_{k}^{T}\Cov(\bm{W}|\bm{X})\bm{C}_{j}
for
j\neq k
and
\bm{\phi}_{(\bm{C}_{j},\bm{C}_{k})}=\bm{C}_{j}^{T}\Cov(\bm{W}|\bm{X})\bm{C}_{k}
for
j=k
, and
\bm{\phi}_{(\bm{B},\bm{C}_{j})}=\bm{C}_{j}^{T}\Cov(\bm{W}|\bm{X})\bm{B}+\bm{B}^{T}\Cov(\bm{W}|\bm{X})\bm{C}_{j}
.

If
\bm{\Sigma}_{W}:=\Cov(\bm{W}|\bm{X})
does not depend on
\bm{X}
, we can regress the estimated covariance matrix of the residuals on
\bm{X}
to estimate
(\bm{\phi}_{\bm{B}},\bm{\phi}_{(\bm{C}_{j},\bm{C}_{k})},\bm{\phi}_{(\bm{B},\bm{C}_{j})})
for
1\leq j,k\leq p
. For notational simplicity, we write
\bm{\phi}_{(\bm{C}_{j},\bm{C}_{j})}
as
\bm{\phi}_{\bm{C}_{j}}
. Suppose
\bm{\phi}_{\bm{B}}
and
\bm{\phi}_{\bm{C}_{j}}
for
1\leq j\leq p
are known (or well estimated via least square estimation). Under some conditions detailed in Section
3
, we can recover the right singular space of
\bm{B}
and
\bm{C}_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq p
. With some simple algebra, this gives us the projection matrix
\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}=\bm{D}^{T}(\bm{D}\bm{D}^{T})^{-1}\bm{D}
, where
\bm{D}^{T}:=(\bm{B}^{T},\bm{C}_{1}^{T}...,\bm{C}_{p}^{T})\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(p+1)K}
. Define
\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}=\bm{I}_{m}-\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}
.

The key step in our method is to project the
m
-dimensional response
\bm{Y}
to the orthogonal complement of the right singular space of
\bm{D}
. Multiplying
\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}
on both sides of equation (
1.1
), we get

\displaystyle\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}\bm{Y}

\displaystyle=\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}\bm{A}^{T}\bm{X}+\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}\bm{B}^{T}\bm{Z}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}\bm{C}^{T}_{j}X_{j}\bm{Z}+\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}\bm{E}

\displaystyle=\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}\bm{A}^{T}\bm{X}+\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}\bm{E},

(2.3)

where we use the fact that
\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}\bm{B}^{T}=\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}\bm{C}^{T}_{j}=0
by the definition of the projection matrix. Thus, by leveraging the singular vector space of
\bm{B}
and
\bm{C}_{j}
, we eliminate the effect of hidden variables
\bm{Z}
from the linear regression as shown in (
2.3
). However, in this case, we can only recover the coefficient matrix
\bm{\Theta}^{T}:=\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}\bm{A}^{T}
, which differs from the parameter of interest
\bm{A}^{T}
in general. The difference between
\bm{\Theta}
and
\bm{A}
leads to an approximation error in our theoretical analysis. In particular, under the conditions in Section
3
, we show that the approximation error is asymptotically ignorable.

For clarity, we summarize our debiased estimation procedure in Algorithm
1
. Note that the algorithm requires the number of hidden variables
K
as the input. The discussion on selection of
K
is deferred to Section
5.3.3
.

Require:

n
i.i.d data
(\bm{Y}_{i},\bm{X}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, rank
K
;

1. Solve
(\widehat{\bm{L}}_{1j},\widehat{\bm{L}}_{2,jk})_{1\leq j,k\leq p}=\arg\min\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\bm{Y}_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bm{L}_{1j}^{T}X_{ji}-\sum_{1\leq j\leq k\leq p}\bm{L}_{2,jk}^{T}X_{ji}X_{ki}\|_{2}^{2}
.

2. Obtain
\widehat{\Sigma}_{\bm{\epsilon}_{i}}=\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i}\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
where
\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i}=\bm{Y}_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\widehat{\bm{L}}_{1j}^{T}X_{ji}-\sum_{1\leq j\leq k\leq p}\widehat{\bm{L}}_{2,jk}^{T}X_{ji}X_{ki}
.

3. Solve

\displaystyle(\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{\bm{B}},\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{(\bm{C}_{j},\bm{C}_{k})},\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{(\bm{B},\bm{C}_{j})})_{1\leq j,k\leq p}=\arg\min\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\widehat{\Sigma}_{\bm{\epsilon}_{i}}-\bm{\phi}_{\bm{B}}-\sum_{1\leq j\leq k\leq p}\bm{\phi}_{(\bm{C}_{j},\bm{C}_{k})}X_{ji}X_{ki}-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bm{\phi}_{(\bm{B},\bm{C}_{j})}X_{ji}\|_{F}^{2}.

4. Compute
\widehat{\bm{U}}_{\bm{B}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times K}
and
\widehat{\bm{U}}_{\bm{C}_{j}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times K}
, the first
K
eigenvectors of
\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{\bm{B}}
and
\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{\bm{C}_{j}}:=\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{(\bm{C}_{j},\bm{C}_{j})}
. Via SVD, obtain
\widehat{\bm{U}}_{\bm{D}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(p+1)K}
, the first
(p+1)K
left singular vectors of
(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{\bm{B}},\widehat{\bm{U}}_{\bm{C}_{1}},...,\widehat{\bm{U}}_{\bm{C}_{p}})
. Then estimate the projection matrix
\widehat{\bm{P}}_{\bm{D}}=\widehat{\bm{U}}_{\bm{D}}\widehat{\bm{U}}_{\bm{D}}^{T}
, and
\widehat{\bm{P}}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}=\bm{I}_{m}-\widehat{\bm{P}}_{\bm{D}}
.

5. Obtain the debiased estimator
\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}
by solving

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}=\arg\min_{\Theta^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\widehat{\bm{P}}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}\bm{Y}_{i}-\bm{\Theta}^{T}\bm{X}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}.

(2.4)

3
Theoretical Results

In this section, we establish the rate of convergence of the proposed debiased estimator. In particular, we focus on the asymptotic regime with
p
fixed and
K,m,n\rightarrow\infty
.

Assumption 1
.

Assume that
\bm{\Sigma}_{W}:=\Cov(\bm{W}|\bm{X})
does not depend on
\bm{X}
, the matrix
\bm{\Sigma}_{W}
has full rank and the noise is homoscedastic, i.e.,
\Cov(\bm{E})=\sigma^{2}\bm{I}_{m}
.

Assumption 2
.

Denote
\bar{\bm{X}}=(X_{1},...,X_{p},X_{1}^{2},X_{1}X_{2},...,X_{p}^{2})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{p(p+1)/2}
. Assume that the matrix
\mathbb{E}(\bar{\bm{X}}\bar{\bm{X}}^{T})
is positive definite. We further assume
\max_{1\leq k\leq m}\mathbb{E}(\bar{\bm{X}}^{T}\bar{\bm{X}}\epsilon^{2}_{k})
and
\max_{1\leq j,k\leq m}\mathbb{E}(\bar{\bm{X}}^{T}\bar{\bm{X}}\phi^{2}_{jk})
are bounded by a constant, where
\epsilon_{k}
is the
k
th entry of
\bm{\epsilon}=\bm{B}^{T}\bm{W}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bm{C}_{j}^{T}X_{j}\bm{W}+\bm{E}
, and
\phi_{jk}
is the
(j,k)
th entry of
\bm{\phi}=\bm{\epsilon}\bm{\epsilon}^{T}-\bm{\phi}_{\bm{B}}-\sum_{1\leq j\leq k\leq p}\bm{\phi}_{(\bm{C}_{j},\bm{C}_{k})}X_{j}X_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bm{\phi}_{(\bm{B},\bm{C}_{j})}X_{j}
. Finally, for
j=1,..,p
,
\mathbb{E}(X_{j}^{6})
is finite.

Assumption 3
.

Let
\lambda_{B}
and
\lambda_{C_{j}}
be the
K
th largest eigenvalues of
\bm{B}^{T}\bm{\Sigma}_{W}\bm{B}
and
\bm{C}_{j}^{T}\bm{\Sigma}_{W}\bm{C}_{j}
, respectively. Assume
\lambda_{B}\asymp\lambda_{C_{j}}\asymp m
for
j=1,..,p
. The matrix
\bm{D}^{T}:=(\bm{B}^{T},\bm{C}_{1}^{T}...,\bm{C}_{p}^{T})\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(p+1)K}
has rank
(p+1)K
, and the
(p+1)K
th largest eigenvalue of
\bm{D}^{T}\bm{D}
denoted by
\lambda_{D}
satisfies
\lambda_{D}\asymp m
.

Assumption
1
is already required in Section
2
in order to develop the proposed method. Assumption
2
is a standard moment condition that guarantees the desired rate for the least square type estimators in Algorithm
1
. Finally, Assumption
3
is known as the pervasiveness assumption in the factor model literature for identification and consistent estimation of the right singular space of
\bm{B}
and
\bm{C}_{j}
; see
Bai 2003
;
Fan et al. 2013
. In particular,
\lambda_{B}\asymp m
holds if the smallest and largest eigenvalues of
\bm{\Sigma}_{W}
are bounded away from 0 and infinity by some constants, and the columns of
\bm{B}
are i.i.d. copies of a
K
-dimensional sub-Gaussian random vector whose covariance matrix has bounded eigenvalues. In this assumption, we also require that
\bm{D}^{T}
has full column rank (and
(p+1)K\leq m
), which is used to construct the estimated singular vectors
\widehat{\bm{U}}_{\bm{D}}
in Algorithm
1
.

We are now ready to present the main result concerning the convergence rate of our estimator
\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}
to the true coefficient matrix
\bm{A}
.

Theorem 1
.

Under Assumptions
1
-
3
, the estimator
\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}
from Algorithm
1
satisfies:

\frac{1}{m}\|\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}-\bm{A}\|_{F}^{2}=\mathcal{O}_{p}\Big(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{K}{m}\eta\Big),

(3.1)

where
\eta=\frac{1}{Km}\|\bm{D}\bm{A}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}
.

Note that the term
\frac{1}{m}\|\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}-\bm{A}\|_{F}^{2}
can be interpreted as the squared error per response. This theorem shows that the error can be decomposed into two parts,
\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{1}{n})
the typical parametric rate for estimating a finite dimensional parameter and
\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{K}{m}\eta)
the approximation error due to the difference between
\bm{\Theta}
and
\bm{A}
. To further examine the approximation error, we can show that
\|\bm{D}\bm{A}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}=\mathcal{O}_{p}(Km)
, when each row of
\bm{A}
and
\bm{D}
are independently generated from
N(0,\bm{I}_{m})
(or more generally
N(0,\bm{\Sigma})
where
\bm{\Sigma}
has a bounded operator norm). This implies
\eta=\mathcal{O}_{p}(1)
. In addition, if
m\gg nK
, the approximation error is asymptotically ignorable compared to the parametric rate, leading to
\frac{1}{m}\|\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}-\bm{A}\|_{F}^{2}=\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{1}{n})
which is the best possible rate for estimating a finite dimensional parameter in regular parametric models. Even if
m\gg nK
does not hold, the estimator is still consistent in terms of the squared error per response, as long as
K/m\rightarrow 0
.

Finally, we note that when
\eta=\mathcal{O}_{p}(1)
, the error bound decreases with
m
, which implies that by collaborating more types of responses, the treatment effect estimation can be more accurate. This phenomenon can be viewed as the bless of dimensionality in our problem.

4
Extension to Heteroscedastic and Correlated Noise

In this section, we generalize our method to the setting with heteroscedastic and correlated errors, i.e.,
\Cov(\bm{E})
can be any positive definite matrix. Recall that
\bm{\phi}_{\bm{B}}=\bm{B}^{T}\Cov(\bm{W}|\bm{X})\bm{B}+\Cov(\bm{E})
. In view of Algorithm
1
, when the noise is heteroscedastic and correlated, the main challenge is that the eigenspace of
\bm{\phi}_{B}
corresponding to the first
K
eigenvalues no longer coincides with the right singular space of
\bm{B}
. Because of this, we may no longer be able to identify
\bm{P}_{D}
via the eigenspaces of the coefficient matrices in step 4 of Algorithm
1
. To address this problem, we turn to a recently developed procedure called HeteroPCA
(
Zhang et al. 2022
)
that allows us to recover the desired right singular space of
\bm{B}
by iteratively imputing the diagonal entries of
\bm{\phi}_{B}
via the diagonals of its low-rank approximations. For completeness, we restate their procedure in Algorithm
2
. The estimation of
\bm{A}
remains nearly identical to the homoscedastic setting, except we now estimate
\widetilde{\bm{U}}_{\bm{B}}
by performing HeteroPCA rather than PCA on the coefficient matrix
\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{\bm{B}}
obtained from step 3 in Algorithm
1
. The full procedure is detailed in Algorithm
3
. This algorithm requires to specify the number of iterations
T
in the HeteroPCA algorithm. Usually, a small
T
such as
T=5
yields satisfactory results in our simulations.

To establish the rate of convergence of the debaised estimator in Algorithm
3
, we further impose the following condition.

Assumption 4
.

Let
\bm{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times K}
be the first
K
left singular vectors of
\bm{B}^{T}
, and
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{B}
be the first and
K
th largest eigenvalues of
\bm{B}^{T}\Sigma_{\bm{W}}\bm{B}
, respectively. Let
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
denote the canonical basis vector of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then,
\exists\hskip 2.84526ptC_{U}>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{B}}\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\|e_{j}^{T}U\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{U}.

This condition is a variation of the incoherence condition in the matrix completion literature
(
Candès and Tao 2010
)
, which is mainly used to recover the singular vector space
\bm{U}

We note that, unlike the original HeteroPCA algorithm

Assume that
\bm{\Sigma}_{W}=\Cov(\bm{W}|\bm{X})

\bm{X}

\bm{\Sigma}_{W}

5.1
Data generating mechanism

The design matrix is generated as
\bm{X}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}_{p}(0,\bm{\Sigma})
independently for all
1\leq i\leq n
and for
\Sigma_{jk}=(-1)^{j+k}(0.5)^{|j-k|}
for all
1\leq j,k\leq p

\bm{Z}_{i}=\bm{\psi}^{T}\bm{X}_{i}+\bm{W}_{i}
with
\bm{\psi}_{jk}\sim\eta\cdot\mathcal{N}(0.5,0.1)
independently for all
1\leq j\leq p
and
1\leq k\leq K
, where
\eta
controls the level of dependence the observed and hidden variables. Set
\bm{A}_{\ell j}^{T}\sim\mathcal{N}(0.5,0.1)
, and
\bm{B}_{\ell k}^{T}
and
\bm{C}_{j,\ell k}^{T}

\sim\mathcal{N}(0.1,1)
independently for all
1\leq\ell\leq m
,
1\leq k\leq K
, and
1\leq j\leq p
. The stochastic error
\bm{W}_{i}
is sampled as
\bm{W}_{ik}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
independently for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq K
. In the homoscedastic setting,
\bm{E}_{i\ell}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
independently for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq\ell\leq m

The number of hidden variables

5.3.3
Selection of
K

In practice, the number of hidden variables
K
is often unknown. In this section, we propose a practical approach to estimate
K
via a variant of the ratio test proposed by
Bing et al. 2022
. In particular, we select
K
as the most common index of the largest eigenvalue gap across the coefficient matrices
\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{B}
and
\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{C_{j}}
obtained from step 3 of our algorithms. More formally, define

\displaystyle\widehat{K}=\arg\max_{i\in\{1,...,K^{*}\}}|S_{i}|,~~\textrm{where}~~S_{i}=\Big\{j\in\{1,...,p+1\}:\frac{\widehat{\lambda}_{j,i}}{\widehat{\lambda}_{j,i+1}}\geq\frac{\widehat{\lambda}_{j,k}}{\widehat{\lambda}_{j,k+1}}\forall k\neq i\Big\},

\widehat{\lambda}_{1,i}
and
\widehat{\lambda}_{j,i}
,
1\leq i\leq m
, are the ordered nonincreasing eigenvalues of
\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{B}
and
\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{C_{j}}
, respectively, and
K^{*}
is an upper bound, often
\frac{\lfloor(n\wedge m)\rfloor}{2}
, on the number of hidden variables. In other words, the set
S_{i}
includes all the indices
j
such that the eigenvalue ratio
\frac{\widehat{\lambda}_{j,i}}{\widehat{\lambda}_{j,i+1}}
is maximized at
i
. Then
\widehat{K}
corresponds to the one with the largest cardinality of
S_{i}
. This approach can be viewed as a generalization of the elbow method in clustering and PCA.

To evaluate the performance of this approach, we consider the following experiments. Recall that
\lambda_{K}(\bm{B}^{T}\bm{\Sigma}_{W}\bm{B})
is the
K
th largest eigenvalue of
\bm{B}^{T}\bm{\Sigma}_{W}\bm{B}
. We define the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\lambda_{K}(\bm{B}^{T}\bm{\Sigma}_{W}\bm{B}),

which quantifies how the
K
th largest eigenvalue is separated from 0. In our experiments, we generate
\bm{B}_{\ell k}^{T}\sim\mathcal{N}(0.1,1)
independently for all
1\leq\ell\leq m
and
1\leq k\leq K
, and vary the SNR by setting
\bm{\Sigma}_{W}=\sigma_{W}^{2}\bm{I}_{K}
across
\sigma_{W}\in\{0.1,0.3,...,1.3,1.5\}
while fixing
\alpha=0
and
\eta=0.5
. For comparison, we also consider the method for selecting
K
used in the Non-interaction approach
(
Bing et al. 2022
)
. We also consider two settings: (i) small
m
and large
n
(
m=25,n=1000
) and (ii) large
m
and large
n
(
m=500,n=1000
).

From Figures
5.3
and
5.4
, we see that the non-interaction-based method consistently overestimates the number of hidden variables even when the SNR is large. On the other hand, our method consistently selects the correct value of
K
(i.e.,
K=3

Proof of Theorem
1

Proof.

We prove the result under
p=1
since the proof can be easily generalized to the case when
p>1
. Throughout the proof, denote
\bm{C}_{1}=\bm{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times K}
,
\bm{L}_{2,11}^{T}=\bm{L}_{2}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\bm{L}^{T}=[\bm{L}_{1}^{T},\bm{L}_{2}^{T}]\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times 2}
and
\widehat{\bm{L}}^{T}=[\widehat{\bm{L}}_{1}^{T},\widehat{\bm{L}}_{2}^{T}]
.

For step 1, we may follow the standard results for ordinary least square estimators using Assumption
2
and show that
\|\widehat{\bm{L}}-\bm{L}\|_{F}^{2}=\mathcal{O}_{p}(m/n)
.

In step 3, for all
(r,s)\in[m]\times[m]
, we regress
\bm{Y}_{rs}:=((\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i}\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i}^{T})_{rs})_{i\in[n]}
on
\bm{X}:=([1,X_{i},X_{i}^{2}])_{i\in[n]}
to obtain
[(\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{\bm{B}})_{rs},\widehat{(\bm{\phi}}_{(\bm{B},\bm{C})})_{rs},(\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{\bm{C}})_{rs}]^{T}
which, for brevity, we denote as
\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{rs}
. Let
\widetilde{\bm{\beta}}_{rs}
be the least square estimator using the response
((\bm{\epsilon}_{i}\bm{\epsilon}_{i}^{T})_{rs})_{i\in[n]}
, and denote
\bm{\beta}_{rs}
as the true value of
\widetilde{\bm{\beta}}_{rs}
, that is the parameter value that minimizes
\mathbb{E}(\bm{\phi}_{s}\bm{\phi}_{r})
. Then,

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{rs}-\bm{\beta}_{rs})\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\sqrt{n}(\widetilde{\bm{\beta}}_{rs}-\bm{\beta}_{rs})\|_{2}^{2}+\|\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{rs}-\widetilde{\bm{\beta}}_{rs})\|_{2}^{2}\leq\mathcal{O}_{p}(1)+\mathcal{O}_{p}(1)

where the former term follows from the standard results on least square estimator and Assumption
2
. The latter term deserves some explanation which we now provide. For ease of notation, denote
((\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i}\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i}^{T})_{rs})_{i\in[n]}
as
\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}^{T}
.

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{n}(\widetilde{\bm{\beta}}_{rs}-\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{rs})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\sqrt{n}(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{X})^{-1}\bm{X}^{T}(\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}^{T}-\bm{\epsilon}\bm{\epsilon}^{T})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{X})^{-1}\|_{F}^{2}\cdot\|\sqrt{n}\bm{X}^{T}(\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}^{T}-\bm{\epsilon}\bm{\epsilon}^{T})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}_{p}(n^{-2})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{p}(n^{2})=\mathcal{O}_{p}(1).

Adding 0 and using the triangle inequality on the latter term yields:

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{n}\bm{X}^{T}(\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}^{T}-\bm{\epsilon}\bm{\epsilon}^{T})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\sqrt{n}\bm{X}^{T}(\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}^{T}-\bm{\epsilon}\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}^{T}+\bm{\epsilon}\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}^{T}-\bm{\epsilon}\bm{\epsilon}^{T})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\|\sqrt{n}\bm{X}^{T}(\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}-\bm{\epsilon})\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}^{T}\|_{2}^{2}+2\,\|\sqrt{n}\bm{X}^{T}\bm{\epsilon}(\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}^{T}-\bm{\epsilon}^{T})\|_{2}^{2},

where
(\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}-\bm{\epsilon})\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}^{T}
, for example, is understood to mean
((\widehat{\epsilon}_{i}-\epsilon_{i})\widehat{\epsilon}_{i}^{T})_{rs,1\leq i\leq n}
. Consider the first term,
\sqrt{n}\bm{X}^{T}(\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}-\bm{\epsilon})\widehat{\bm{\epsilon}}^{T}
. Its
j
th entry, for
j\in\{1,2,3\}
, is given by:

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{j-1}\left((L_{r1}-\widehat{L}_{r1})X_{i}+(L_{r2}-\widehat{L}_{r2})X_{i}^{2}\right)\cdot(Y_{is}-\widehat{L}_{s1}X_{i}-\widehat{L}_{s2}X_{i}^{2})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl((L_{r1}-\widehat{L}_{r1})X_{i}^{j}Y_{is}+(L_{r2}-\widehat{L}_{r2})X_{i}^{j+1}Y_{is}

\displaystyle~~~-\widehat{L}_{s1}(L_{r1}-\widehat{L}_{r1})X_{i}^{j+1}-\widehat{L}_{s1}(L_{r2}-\widehat{L}_{r2})X_{i}^{j+2}

\displaystyle~~~-\widehat{L}_{s2}(L_{r1}-\widehat{L}_{r1})X_{i}^{j+2}-\widehat{L}_{s2}(L_{r2}-\widehat{L}_{r2})X_{i}^{j+3}\bigr).

Squaring the above and noting the first and second terms dominate the convergence rate, we obtain:

\displaystyle n\cdot(L_{r1}-\widehat{L}_{r1})^{2}\cdot(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{j}Y_{ij})^{2}=\mathcal{O}_{p}(n)\cdot\mathcal{O}_{p}(n^{-1})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{p}(n^{2})=\mathcal{O}_{p}(n^{2}),

where we used the convergence rate from step 1 for the 2nd term and the law of large numbers for the 3rd term. The other terms are handled similarly, so we conclude that
\|\widehat{\beta}_{rs}-\beta_{rs}\|_{2}^{2}=\mathcal{O}_{p}(1/n)
. Following a similar analysis, we can show that

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{\bm{B}}-\bm{\phi}_{\bm{B}})\|_{F}^{2}=\mathcal{O}_{p}(m^{2});\quad\quad\|\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{\bm{C}}-\bm{\phi}_{\bm{C}})\|_{F}^{2}=\mathcal{O}_{p}(m^{2}).

For step 4, write the eigendecomposition of the coefficient matrices from step 3 as

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}_{B}=\bm{U}_{B}\bm{D}_{B}\bm{U}_{B}^{T};\quad\quad\bm{\phi}_{C}=\bm{U}_{C}\bm{D}_{C}\bm{U}_{C}^{T},

the eigendecomposition of the estimated coefficient matrices as

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{B}=\widehat{\bm{U}}_{B}\widehat{\bm{D}}_{B}\widehat{\bm{U}}_{B}^{T};\quad\quad\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{C}=\widehat{\bm{U}}_{C}\widehat{\bm{D}}_{C}\widehat{\bm{U}}_{C}^{T},

and the SVD of the concatenated matrices of the 2 sets of
K
leading eigenvectors as

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}_{B}&\bm{U}_{C}\end{bmatrix}=\bm{U}\bm{D}\bm{V}^{T};\quad\quad\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{\bm{U}}_{B}&\widehat{\bm{U}}_{C}\end{bmatrix}=\widehat{\bm{U}}\widehat{\bm{D}}\widehat{\bm{V}}^{T}.

Then,
\bm{U}
are the first
2K
eigenvectors of
\bm{\Sigma}:=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}_{B}&\bm{U}_{C}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}_{B}^{T}\\
\bm{U}_{C}^{T}\end{bmatrix}
. Similarly,
\widehat{\bm{U}}
are the first
2K
eigenvectors of
\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}:=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{\bm{U}}_{B}&\widehat{\bm{U}}_{C}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{\bm{U}}_{B}^{T}\\
\widehat{\bm{U}}_{C}^{T}\end{bmatrix}
. Denote
\lambda_{B}
and
\lambda_{C}
as the
K
th largest eigenvalue of
\bm{B}^{T}\bm{\Sigma}_{W}\bm{B}
and
\bm{C}^{T}\bm{\Sigma}_{W}\bm{C}
, respectively. Now, by a variant of the Davis-Kahan theorem (Theorem 2 in
[
Yu et al. 2015
]
), with
r=1,s=2K,d:=s-r+1=2K
, and the convergence rate obtained in step 3,
\exists
orthogonal matrices
\bm{O}_{B},\bm{O}_{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times K}
s.t.

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\bm{U}}_{B}\bm{O}_{B}-\bm{U}_{B}\|_{F}\leq 2^{5/2}\,\|\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{B}-\bm{\phi}_{B}\|_{F}\cdot\left(\lambda_{K}(\bm{B}^{T}\bm{\Sigma}_{W}\bm{B})\right)^{-1}=\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{m}{\lambda_{B}\sqrt{n}}),

(A.1)

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\bm{U}}_{C}\bm{O}_{C}-\bm{U}_{C}\|_{F}\leq 2^{5/2}\,\|\widehat{\bm{\phi}}_{C}-\bm{\phi}_{C}\|_{F}\cdot\left(\lambda_{K}(\bm{C}^{T}\bm{\Sigma}_{W}\bm{C})\right)^{-1}=\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{m}{\lambda_{C}\sqrt{n}}),

(A.2)

where we used the fact that
\bm{\Sigma}_{E}=\sigma^{2}\bm{I}_{m}
and
\text{rank}(\bm{\Sigma}_{W})=K
, so that
\lambda_{K}(\bm{\phi}_{B})-\lambda_{K+1}(\bm{\phi}_{B})=\lambda_{K}(\bm{B}^{T}\bm{\Sigma}_{W}\bm{B})
.

Since
\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}=\bm{U}\bm{U}^{T}
and
\widehat{\bm{P}}_{D}=\widehat{\bm{U}}\widehat{\bm{U}}^{T}
, it now follows that, by another application of the Davis-Kahan Theorem,
\exists
orthogonal matrix
\bm{O}\in\mathbb{R}^{2K\times 2K}
s.t.

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\bm{P}}_{D}-\bm{P}_{D}\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\|{\widehat{\bm{U}}}{\widehat{\bm{U}}}^{T}-{\bm{U}}{\bm{U}}^{T}\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\|{\widehat{\bm{U}}}{\widehat{\bm{U}}}^{T}-{\bm{U}}\bm{O}^{T}{\widehat{\bm{U}}}^{T}+{\bm{U}}\bm{O}^{T}{\widehat{\bm{U}}}^{T}-{\bm{U}}{\bm{U}}^{T}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\|({\widehat{\bm{U}}}\bm{O}-{\bm{U}})\bm{O}^{T}{\widehat{\bm{U}}}^{T}\|_{F}+\|{\bm{U}}({\widehat{\bm{U}}}\bm{O}-{\bm{U}})^{T}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\|{\widehat{\bm{U}}}\bm{O}-{\bm{U}}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot 2^{3/2}\,\|{\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}}-{\bm{\Sigma}}\|_{F}\cdot\left(\lambda_{2K}({\bm{\Sigma}})-\lambda_{2K+1}({\bm{\Sigma}})\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=2^{5/2}\,\|{\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}}-{\bm{\Sigma}}\|_{F}\cdot\left(\lambda_{2K}({\bm{\Sigma}})\right)^{-1}.

(A.3)

Since the matrix
\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}_{B}&\bm{U}_{C}\end{bmatrix}
has rank
2K
by Assumption
3
,
\bm{U}_{B}
and
\bm{U}_{C}
cannot be linearly dependent. Hence, for any column
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
of
\bm{U}_{B}
and
\bm{U}_{C}
(there are
2K
of them), we have
v^{T}\bm{\Sigma}v=v^{T}(\bm{U}_{B}\bm{U}_{B}^{T}+\bm{U}_{C}\bm{U}_{C}^{T})v\geq 1
, which implies
\lambda_{2K}({\bm{\Sigma}})\geq 1
.

Finally, it suffices to upper bound
\|{\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}}-{\bm{\Sigma}}\|_{F}
. Similar to derivation of (
A.3
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\widehat{\bm{\Sigma}}}-{\bm{\Sigma}}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{U}_{B}\bm{U}_{B}^{T}-\widehat{\bm{U}}_{B}\widehat{\bm{U}}_{B}^{T}\|_{F}+\|\bm{U}_{C}\bm{U}_{C}^{T}-\widehat{\bm{U}}_{C}\widehat{\bm{U}}_{C}^{T}\|_{F}=\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{m}{\lambda_{B}\sqrt{n}}+\frac{m}{\lambda_{C}\sqrt{n}}),

where we use (
A.1
) and (
A.2
) in the last step.

Thus, we see that

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\bm{P}}_{\bm{D}}-\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}\|_{F}=(\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{m}{\sqrt{n}})\cdot\lambda_{B}^{-1}+\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{m}{\sqrt{n}})\cdot\lambda_{C}^{-1})\cdot\left(\lambda_{2K}(\bm{\Sigma})\right)^{-1}.

(A.4)

Under Assumption
3
and the earlier remark showing
\lambda_{2K}(\Sigma)\geq 1
, the convergence rate in (
A.4
) can be further simplified to

\|\widehat{\bm{P}}_{\bm{D}}-\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}\|_{F}=\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}).

(A.5)

For step 5, recall that
\bm{\Theta}^{T}=\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}^{\perp}\bm{A}^{T}
and
\bm{X}
is the data matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
. Adding and subtracting the least squares estimator with
\bm{P}_{D}
known and using the triangle inequality, we obtain:

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}-\bm{\Theta}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}_{(j,\cdot)}-\bm{\Theta}_{(j,\cdot)}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{X})^{-1}\bm{X}^{T}(\widehat{\bm{P}}^{\perp}_{D}\bm{Y})_{(j,\cdot)}^{T}-(\bm{P}^{\perp}_{D}\bm{A}^{T})_{(j,\cdot)}^{T}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{X})^{-1}\bm{X}^{T}(\widehat{\bm{P}}^{\perp}_{D}\bm{Y})_{(j,\cdot)}^{T}-(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{X})^{-1}\bm{X}^{T}(\bm{P}^{\perp}_{D}\bm{Y})_{(j,\cdot)}^{T}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle~~+2\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{X})^{-1}\bm{X}^{T}(\bm{P}^{\perp}_{D}\bm{Y})_{(j,\cdot)}^{T}-(\bm{P}^{\perp}_{D}\bm{A}^{T})_{(j,\cdot)}^{T}\|_{2}^{2}.

We bound each of these terms in turn. The first is bounded above as:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{X})^{-1}\bm{X}^{T}(\widehat{\bm{P}}^{\perp}_{D}\bm{Y})_{(j,\cdot)}^{T}-(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{X})^{-1}\bm{X}^{T}(\bm{P}^{\perp}_{D}\bm{Y})_{(j,\cdot)}^{T}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{X})^{-1}\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Y}^{T}\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|(\widehat{\bm{P}}_{D}-\bm{P}_{D})_{(j,\cdot)}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{X})^{-1}\|^{2}_{2}\cdot\|\bm{X}^{T}\bm{Y}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}\cdot\|\widehat{\bm{P}}_{D}-\bm{P}_{D}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{1}{n^{2}})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{p}(mn^{2})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{1}{n})=\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{m}{n}).

For the second term, we note that
\bm{P}^{\perp}_{D}\bm{Y}=\bm{P}^{\perp}_{D}\bm{A}^{T}\bm{X}^{T}+\bm{P}^{\perp}_{D}\bm{E}
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{X})^{-1}\bm{X}^{T}(\bm{P}^{\perp}_{D}\bm{Y})_{(j,\cdot)}^{T}-(\bm{P}^{\perp}_{D}\bm{A}^{T})_{(j,\cdot)}^{T}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{X})^{-1}\bm{X}^{T}\bm{E}^{T}\bm{P}^{\perp}_{D}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{P}^{\perp}_{D}\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\|(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{X})^{-1}\bm{X}^{T}\bm{E}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}=\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{m}{n}).

Altogether, we obtain
\|\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}-\bm{\Theta}\|_{F}^{2}=\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{m}{n})
. Finally, we note that
\bm{A}^{T}-\bm{\Theta}^{T}=\bm{P}_{\bm{D}}\bm{A}^{T}
. By Lemma
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}-\bm{A}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\|\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}-\bm{\Theta}\|_{F}^{2}+2\,\|\bm{\Theta}-\bm{A}\|_{F}^{2}=\mathcal{O}_{p}(\frac{m}{n}+K\eta).

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 1
.

Under the same assumptions in Theorem
2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\|\bm{P}_{D}\bm{A}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}=\mathcal{O}(K\eta/m),

where
\eta=\frac{1}{Km}\|\bm{D}\bm{A}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}
.

Proof.

Recall
\bm{D}^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{B}^{T}&\bm{C}_{1}^{T}&...&\bm{C}^{T}_{p}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(p+1)K}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\bm{P}_{D}\bm{A}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\bm{D}^{T}(\bm{D}\bm{D}^{T})^{-1}\bm{D}\bm{A}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{D}^{T}(\bm{D}\bm{D}^{T})^{-1}\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\|\bm{D}\bm{A}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}=\left(\lambda_{\min}(\bm{D}\bm{D}^{T})\right)^{-1}\cdot\|\bm{D}\bm{A}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}.

Since
\bm{D}
has rank
(p+1)K
, the smallest eigenvalue of
\bm{D}\bm{D}^{T}
is equal to
\lambda_{D}
, the
(p+1)K
th eigenvalue of
\bm{D}^{T}\bm{D}=\bm{B}^{T}\bm{B}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bm{C}_{j}^{T}\bm{C}_{j}
. By Assumption
3
, we know
\lambda_{D}\asymp m
. Thus,
\frac{1}{m}\|\bm{P}_{D}\bm{A}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}=\mathcal{O}(K\eta/m)
, where
\eta=\frac{1}{Km}\|\bm{D}\bm{A}^{T}\|_{F}^{2}
.
∎

The Avoidance Principle for Noncompact Hypersurfaces Moving by Mean Curvature Flow
Brian White

Abstract
. Consider a pair of smooth, possibly noncompact, properly immersed hypersurfaces moving by mean curvature flow, or, more generally, a pair of weak set flows. We prove that if the ambient space is Euclidean space and if the distance between the two surfaces is initially nonzero, then the surfaces remain disjoint at all subsequent times. We prove the same result when the ambient space is a complete Riemannian manifold of nonzero injectivity radius, provided the curvature tensor (of the ambient space) and all its derivatives are bounded.

1.
introduction

The classical avoidance principle for mean curvature flow says that
two smooth, properly immersed, initially disjoint hypersurfaces moving by mean curvature flow in Euclidean space
remain disjoint as long as they are smooth, if at least one of them is compact.
That avoidance principle is an easy consequence
of the strong maximum principle. Ilmanen (
[
7
]
*§10,
[
6
]
)
generalized the avoidance principle
to arbitrary “set-theoretic subsolutions of mean curvature flow”
or (in the terminology of
[
8
]
and
[
4
]
)
“weak set flows”. A special case is that of surfaces moving by the
level set flow of
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
.
The support of a codimension-one, integral Brakke flow is a weak set flow, so Ilmanen’s avoidance principle
also applies to such Brakke flows.
In
[
4
]
, Ilmanen’s avoidance principle for weak set flows in Euclidean space was extended to weak set flows
in a complete Riemannian manifold, provided the Ricci curvature of the ambient manifold is bounded below.

Those papers left open, even in the case of smooth hypersurfaces in Euclidean space,
the question of whether there is an avoidance principle when neither surface is compact.
The correct hypothesis for such an avoidance principle
is not that the surfaces are initially disjoint. There is, for example,
a smooth curvature flow
t\in[0,T)\mapsto C(t)
in the plane such that at time
0
the curve is the union of the graphs

y=1/(1+x^{2})
and
y=-1/(1+x^{2})
and such that at times
t\in(0,T)
, the curve is a simple closed curve.
(See
[
5
]
*7.3.)
Of course the static flow
t\mapsto X(t):=\mathbf{R}\times\{0\}
is also a curvature flow.
Note that
C(t)
and
X(t)
are disjoint at time
0
but intersect for all
t\in(0,T)
.

The correct hypothesis is that the distance between the two surfaces is initially positive.
Note that if either surface is compact, this hypothesis is equivalent to disjointness.

This paper proves that the avoidance principle does indeed remain true for noncompact
weak set flows in Euclidean space, or, more generally, in a Riemannian manifold, provided the manifold
is well-behaved at infinity:

Theorem 1

(Avoidance Theorem)
.

Suppose that
N
is a complete, connected Riemannian manifold
with positive injectivity radius such that
|\nabla^{k}\operatorname{Riem}|
is bounded for each nonnegative
integer
k
.
Let
\Lambda
be a lower bound for the Ricci curvature of
N
.
Suppose that
t\in[0,\infty)\mapsto X(t),Y(t)
are weak set flows in
N
.
Then

e^{-\Lambda t}d(X(t),Y(t))

is an increasing function of
t
.

Here
d(P,Q)=\inf\{d(p,q):p\in P,\,q\in Q\}
, where
d(p,q)
is geodesic distance
from
p
to
q
. (Thus
d(p,q)=|p-q|
in Euclidean space.)

We now describe Ilmanen’s proof that the avoidance principle holds
in Euclidean space if at least one of the
flows is compact. By definition of weak set flow, a weak set flow
cannot bump into a smooth, compact mean curvature flow, provided the two flows are initially disjoint.
It follows easily (in Euclidean space)
that the distance between the two is an increasing
function of time. Suppose that
two weak set flows
X(\cdot)
and
Y(\cdot)
,
one compact, are initially disjoint.
Ilmanen proved a
C^{1,1}
interpolation theorem, according to which there is a compact
C^{1,1}
hypersurface
M

between
X(0)
and
Y(0)
such that

(1)

d(X(0),M)=R=d(Y(0),M),

where
R=\frac{1}{2}d(X(0),Y(0))
.
Now let
M
evolve by mean curvature flow. Then
M(t)
will be smooth for
t\in(0,\epsilon]

for some
\epsilon>0
. It follows that
d(X(t),M(t))\geq R
and
d(Y(t),M)\geq R

for all
t\in[0,\epsilon]
, and therefore that

d(X(t),Y(t))\geq d(X(t),M(t))+d(M(t),Y(t))\geq 2R

for all
t\in[0,\epsilon]
. The result follows rather directly.

The same idea was used in
[
4
]
for general ambient manifolds.
That paper replaced Ilmanen’s
C^{1,1}
interpolation theorem by an easier

C^{1}
interpolation theorem.

If one tries to adapt Ilmanen’s proof to noncompact
X(t)
and
Y(t)
, one can at least get started:
there is a locally
C^{1,1}
surface
M
between
X(0)
and
Y(0)
satisfying (
1
).
However, it is not clear that there is
uniformly
C^{1,1}
interpolating surface, and it seems that uniform
C^{1,1}
bounds
are needed in order to use it to prove avoidance.

Likewise, to adapt the proof in
[
4
]
to noncompact
X(t)
and
Y(t)
,
it would seem that uniform
C^{1}
bounds on the interpolating surface
M
are needed.
However, this paper shows that uniform
C^{1}
bounds are not required.

Let
K
be the set of points in
N
at distance
\geq R
from
X(0)\cup Y(0)
, where

R=(1/2)d(X(0),Y(0))
. Thus the interpolating surface
M
of
[
4
]

is contained in
K
. This paper does not prove uniform
C^{1}
bounds on
M
.
However, it does prove uniform
C^{1}
bounds on the points of
M
near
\partial K
,
and it shows that those bounds suffice for proving avoidance.

It is natural to wonder if there is a simpler proof in the case that the two flows are assumed to be
smooth. If one had any proof that only used the maximum principle, then
that proof should apply to any weak set flow (by definition of weak set flow). Thus, it seems
unlikely that assuming smoothness could result in any simplifications.

The organization of the paper is as follows. Sections
2

and
3

Section
4

The proof uses various facts about the distance function, about certain harmonic
functions, and about separating Brakke flows.
Those facts are proved in Sections

,
6
,
and
7
.

To understand the main ideas of the proof, it may be helpful to focus
on the case when the ambient space is Euclidean. In that case, it is not necessary to use the exponential map. Where the proof uses the exponential map
at a point

, in the Euclidean case one simply translates by
-p
(and, in some cases, also rotates
and dilates).

The definition and basic properties of weak set flows may be found in
[

Sections 6 and 7 of

2.
Conventions

If
t\in[0,\infty)\mapsto M(t)
is an integral
Brakke flow in the Riemannian manifold
N
, we let
\mathbf{M}
denote
its spacetime support:

\mathbf{M}:=\overline{\{(x,t):t\in[0,\infty),\,x\in\operatorname{spt}M(t)\}},

and we let

\mathbf{M}(t)=\{x:(x,t)\in\mathbf{M}\}.

Thus
t\mapsto\mathbf{M}(t)
is the weak set flow associated to the Brakke flow
M(\cdot)
.

If
p
and
q
are points in a connected Riemannian manifold
N
and if
X
and
Y
are subsets of
N
,
we let
d(p,q)
be the geodesic distance from
p
to
q
, and we let

\displaystyle d(p,X)=d(X,p)=\inf_{x\in X}d(p,x),

\displaystyle d(X,Y)=\inf_{x\in X,y\in Y}d(x,y).

Lemma 2

(Finite Speed Lemma)
.

For
r>0
, there is an
h=h(r,\Lambda,m)
with the following property.
If

The Finite Speed Lemma
2

implies control on how fast a weak set flow can move away from its initial set:

Corollary 3
.

For every
\epsilon>0
, there is a
\delta>0
with the following property.
If

is a weak set flow in
N

\cup_{t\in[0,\delta]}Z(t)\subset\{p:d(p,Z(0))\leq\epsilon\}.

The following corollary gives a bound on the rate at which two
weak set flows can approach each other.

Corollary 4
.

If
t\in[t_{0},\infty)\mapsto X(t),\,Y(t)
are weak set flows with

d(X(t_{0}),Y(t_{0}))>r>0,

then

d(X(t_{0}+t),Y(t_{0}+t))\geq d(X(t_{0}),Y(t_{0}))-2ht

for
t\leq(R-r)/h
, where
h
is as in Lemma
2
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove it for
t_{0}=0
. Let

R=\frac{1}{2}d(X(t_{0}),Y(t_{0})),

and let
\tilde{X}=\{p:d(p,X(0))\geq R\}
and
\tilde{Y}=\{p:d(p,Y(0))\geq R\}
.
Then

\displaystyle d(X(t),\tilde{X})\geq R-ht,

\displaystyle d(Y(t),\tilde{Y})\geq R-ht

for
t\in[0,(R-r)/h]
. Thus

d(X(t),Y(t))\geq d(X(t),\tilde{X})+d(\tilde{Y},Y(t))\geq 2R-2ht.

Proposition 5
.

Suppose that
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}
.
The following are equivalent.

(1)

If
t\in[t_{0},\infty)\mapsto X(t),Y(t)
are weak set flows in
N
,
then there is an
\epsilon>0
such that

e^{-\lambda(t-t_{0})}d(X(t),Y(t))\geq d(X(t_{0}),Y(t_{0}))

for all

.

(2)

If
t\in[t_{0},\infty)\mapsto X(t),Y(t)
are weak set flows that in
N
, then

e^{-\lambda(t-t_{0})}d(X(t),Y(t))\geq d(X(t_{0}),Y(t_{0}))

for all
t\in[t_{0},\infty)
.

(3)

If
t\in[0,\infty)\mapsto X(t),Y(t)
are weak set flows, then

t\in[0,\infty)\mapsto e^{-\lambda t}d(X(t),Y(t))

is an increasing function of
t
.

Furthermore, if

t\in[0,\infty)\mapsto e^{-\lambda t}d(X(t),Y(t))

is an increasing function of
t
for each
\lambda<\Lambda
, then it is also
an increasing function of
t
for
\lambda=\Lambda
.

Proof.

Suppose that (
1
) holds. In (
2
), let
\mathcal{T}
be the set of
T\in[t_{0},\infty)
such that the
inequality in (
2
)
holds for all
t\in[t_{0},T]
.
Then
\mathcal{T}
is closed (by Corollary
4
),
and it is nonempty since

.
By (

),
\mathcal{T}
is relatively open in

.
Thus
\mathcal{T}=[t_{0},\infty)
.
Hence (

) implies (

).

Trivially, (

) implies (

) and (
3
) implies (

).
The “furthermore” assertion is also trivially true.
∎

The Avoidance Theorem

Theorem 8

(Avoidance Theorem)
.

Suppose that
N
is a complete, connected Riemannian
(m+1)
-manifold (without boundary)
such that
\operatorname{inj}(N)>0
and such that

\sup_{N}|\nabla^{k}\operatorname{Riem}|<\infty

for each nonnegative integer
k
. Let
\Lambda
be a lower bound for the Ricci curvature of
N
.
Suppose that
t\in[0,\infty)\mapsto X(t),\,Y(t)
are weak set flows in
N
.
Then

e^{-\Lambda t}d(X(t),Y(t))

is an increasing function of
t\in[0,\infty)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5
, it suffices to prove that if
\lambda<\Lambda
, and if

R=\frac{1}{2}d(X(0),Y(0)),

then there is an
\epsilon>0
such that

(2)

\text{ $e^{-\lambda t}d(X(t),Y(t))\geq 2R$ for $t\in[0,\epsilon]$}.

We may assume that
R>0
, as (
2
) is trivially true for
R=0
.
We may also assume that
\Lambda\leq 0
, as otherwise
N
is compact,
and the avoidance principle is already known in that case. See
[
4
]
.

Case 1
.

d(X(0),Y(0))\leq r:=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{inj}(N)
.

Let
X=X(0)
and
Y=Y(0)
.
Let
K
be the set of points
p
such that
d(p,X\cup Y)\geq R
.
Let
S
be the set of points
p
such that
d(p,X)=d(p,Y)=R
.
Thus
S\subset\partial K
.

We will use the following interpolation theorem.

Theorem 9

(Interpolation Theorem)
.

There is a closed region
\Omega
in
N
with the following properties:

(1)

\Omega
is a
C^{1}
manifold-with-boundary.

(2)

\Omega
contains
\{p:d(p,X)\leq R\}
and is disjoint from
\{p:d(p,Y)<R\}
.

Furthermore,
\Sigma:=\partial\Omega
is uniformly
C^{1}
near
\partial K
in the following sense.
For every
\epsilon>0
, there is a
\delta>0
such that if
p,q\in\Sigma
, if
d(p,\partial K)<\delta
,
and if
d(p,q)<\delta
, then

d(\nu(p),\nu(q))\leq\epsilon.

Here,
\nu
is the unit normal to
\Sigma
that points out from
\Omega
.
The meaning of
d(\nu(p),\nu(q))
is given by:

Definition 10
.

If
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
are tangent vectors to
N
, we let
d(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})

be the supremum of

|\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w}|

among
C^{1}

\mathbf{w}
on
N
such that
|\mathbf{w}|\leq 1
and

|\nabla\mathbf{w}|\leq 1
at all points.

The Interpolation Theorem is proved in §
6
.

Let
t\in[0,\infty)\mapsto M(t)
be a separating Brakke flow such that
M(0)
is the Radon
measure associated to
\Sigma
. See §
7
for the definition of “separating flow”
and for a proof that the flow
M(\cdot)
exists.

If
(p,t)
is a regular point of
\mathbf{M}
, we let
a(\mathbf{M},p,t)
be the norm of the second fundamental
form of
\mathbf{M}(t)
at
p
. If
(p,t)
is a singular point of
\mathbf{M}
, we
let
a(\mathbf{M},p,t)=\infty
. We let

A(\mathbf{M},p,t)=\sup\{a(\mathbf{M},p^{\prime},t^{\prime}):d(p^{\prime},p)\leq|t|^{1/2},\,t/2\leq t^{\prime}\leq 2t\}.

Claim 1
.

There is an
\epsilon>0
with the following property.
If
0<T\leq\epsilon
, if
p\in\mathbf{M}(T)
, and if
d(p,K^{c})\leq T^{1/2}
, then

T^{1/2}A(\mathbf{M},p,T)<1.

Proof of Claim
1
.

Suppose not. Then there exist
t_{i}\to 0
and
p_{i}\in\mathbf{M}(t_{i})
such that

d(p_{i},K^{c})\leq t_{i}^{1/2}

and such that

t_{i}^{1/2}A(\mathbf{M},p_{i},t_{i})\geq 1.

Let
L_{i}:\mathbf{R}^{m+1}\to\operatorname{Tan}(N,p_{i})
be a linear isometry,
and let

\displaystyle\phi_{i}:B_{i}=B^{m+1}(0,r/\sqrt{t_{i}})\to N,

\displaystyle\phi_{i}(v)=\exp_{p_{i}}(L_{i}(t_{i}^{1/2}v)),

where

) to a curve of length

Let
\Sigma_{i}=\phi_{i}^{-1}(\Sigma)
.

Let

be the Brakke flow in

After passing to a subsequence,

\Sigma^{\prime}

\mathbf{R}^{m+1}

We claim that
\Sigma^{\prime}
is nonempty. For if it were empty, then (after passing to a further subsequence)
the flows
\mathbf{M}_{i}
would converge to a weak set flow

in
\mathbf{R}^{m+1}

with

Thus
\Sigma^{\prime}
is nonempty.
Thus if
\tilde{p}_{i}
is a point in

Consequently, if

It follows that

The contradiction proves Claim

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon,

Let

Suppose the claim is false. Then

\lim_{i}d(q_{i},K^{c})\leq\frac{1}{2}T^{1/2}.

Note that the
g_{i}
-harmonic functions
h\circ F_{i}:B_{i}\to[0,1]

converge, perhaps after passing to a further subsequence, to a harmonic
function
h^{\prime}:K^{\prime}\to[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(p)

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{if $p\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}=a$},

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(p)

\displaystyle=1\qquad\text{if $p\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}=b$},

and such that

(14)

h^{\prime}(0)=c\in[1/3,2/3].

If
b=\infty
, then
a=-1
, and thus
h^{\prime}\equiv 0
, contrary to (
14
).
Likewise, if
a=-\infty
, then
b=1
, and thus
h^{\prime}\equiv 1
, contrary to (
14
).
Consequently,
a
and
b
are both finite, so

h^{\prime}(p)=\frac{p\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}-a}{b-a}.

and therefore

\frac{\nabla h^{\prime}}{|\nabla h^{\prime}|}=\mathbf{e}_{1}.

The assertions of the theorem follow immediately.
∎

Corollary 21
.

For every
\epsilon>0
, there is a
\delta>0
with the following property.
If

\displaystyle R-\delta<\rho<R,

\displaystyle p,q\in K(\rho),

\displaystyle d(p,q)<\delta,

\displaystyle d(p,\partial K(\rho))<\delta,

\displaystyle h^{\rho}(p),h^{\rho}(q)\in[1/3,2/3],

Then

d(\mathbf{w}(p),\mathbf{w}(q))<\epsilon.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{p}\in\mathcal{X}(p)
and
\mathbf{u}_{q}\in\mathcal{X}(q)
.
By Lemma
20
, we can, by choosing
\delta
small, ensure that

d(\mathbf{w}(p),\mathbf{u}_{p})+d(\mathbf{w}(q),\mathbf{u}_{q})<\epsilon/2.

By Corollary
15
, we can choose
\delta
small enough that

d(\mathbf{u}_{p},\mathbf{u}_{q})<\epsilon/2.

Thus
d(\mathbf{w}(p),\mathbf{w}(q))<\epsilon
.
∎

Theorem 22

(Interpolation Theorem)
.

There is a closed region
\Omega
in
N
with the following properties:

(1)

\Omega
is a
C^{1}
manifold-with-boundary.

(2)

\Omega
contains
\{p:d(p,X)\leq R\}
and is disjoint from
\{p:d(p,Y)<R\}
.

Furthermore,
\Sigma:=\partial\Omega
is uniformly
C^{1}
near
\partial K
in the following sense.
For every
\epsilon>0
, there is a
\delta>0
such that if
p,q\in\Sigma
, if
d(p,\partial K)<\delta
,
and if
d(p,q)<\delta
, then

d(\nu(p),\nu(q))<\epsilon,

where
\nu(\cdot)
is the unit normal to
\Sigma
that points out from
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
0<\rho_{i}<R
with
\rho_{i}\to R
and let
h^{\rho_{i}}
be as the harmonic
function defined at the beginning of §
6
.
By passing to a subsequence, we can assume that
h^{\rho_{i}}
converges locally uniformly
in
K\setminus S
to a harmonic function such that

h(p)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $x(p)=R$ and $y(p)>R$},\\
1&\text{if $y(p)=R$ and $x(p)>R$}.\end{cases}

Let
c\in[1/3,2/3]
be a regular value of
h
and also of each
h^{\rho_{i}}
.
Thus

\Omega_{i}:=\{h^{\rho_{i}}\leq c\}\cup\{x(\cdot)\leq\rho_{i}\}

is a
C^{1}
manifold-with-boundary, the boundary being

\Sigma_{i}:=\{p:h^{\rho_{i}}(p)=c\}.

By passing to a further subsequence, we can assume that
\Omega_{i}
and
\Sigma_{i}

converge to closed sets
\Omega
and
\Sigma
. (Actually, convergence holds
without passing to a further subsequence, but we do not need that fact.)
Clearly,
\Sigma\setminus S=h^{-1}(c)
, and

(15)

h^{-1}(c)\subset\Sigma\subset h^{-1}(c)\cup S.

Note that each path from a point
p
with
x(p)<R
to a point

with
y(q)<R

must cross
\Sigma^{i}
for all sufficiently large

and thus must also cross
\Sigma
.
It follows that
S\subset\Sigma
and therefore (by (
15
)) that

\Sigma=h^{-1}(c)\cup S.

The convergence of
\Sigma_{i}\setminus S
to
\Sigma\setminus S

is smooth with multiplicity
1
since
c
is a regular value of
h
.
Note that

\nu_{i}=\nabla h^{\rho_{i}}/|\nabla h^{\rho_{i}}|

is the unit normal to
\Sigma_{i}
that points out of
\Omega_{i}
.

Claim 5
.

If
p_{i}\in\Sigma_{i}
converges to
p\in\Sigma
,
then
\nu_{i}(p)
converges to a limit
\nu(p)
.

Proof of Claim
5
.

If
p\in\Sigma\setminus S
, then this is true since
c
is a regular value
of
h
. (In this case,
\nu(p)=\nabla h(p)/|\nabla h(p)|
.)

Now suppose that
p\in S
.
By Corollary
16
, there is a unique
\mathbf{u}(p)
in
\mathcal{X}(p)
.
Let
\mathbf{u}_{i}\in\mathcal{X}(p_{i})
.
Then

by Lemma

,
and
\mathbf{u}_{i}\to\mathbf{u}(p)

,
so

converges to

.
Thus Claim

∎

Thus
\Sigma_{i}
converges to
\Sigma
in

with multiplicity

.
(To see that the multiplicity is

,
note that if the convergence were multi-sheeted at a point

7.
Separating Flows

Suppose
t\in[0,\infty)\mapsto M(t)
is an
m
-dimensional integral Brakke flow in
N
.
We say that
M(\cdot)
is a
separating flow
provided:

(1)

M(0)
is the Radon measure associated to an embedded,
C^{1}
manifold
\Sigma
.

(2)

\Sigma
is the boundary of a region
\Omega
.

(3)

if
p
and
q
are points in
N\setminus\Sigma
with
p\in\Omega
and
q\in\Omega^{c}
,
then any path in
N\times[0,\infty)
from
(p,0)
to
(q,0)
must intersect the spacetime
support
\mathbf{M}
of
M(\cdot)
.

(4)

The flow
M(\cdot)
has the local regularity property of
[
9
]
.

Theorem 23
.

Suppose that
t\in[0,\infty)\mapsto M_{i}(t)
is a sequence of separating Brakke flows
that converge to a Brakke flow
M(\cdot)
.
Suppose also that
\operatorname{spt}M(0)
is a
C^{1}
submanifold, and that
\operatorname{spt}M_{i}(0)

converges in
C^{1}
with multiplicity
1
to
\operatorname{spt}M(0)
.
Then

is a separating flow.

The theorem follows easily from the fact that
\mathbf{M}_{i}
converges to
\mathbf{M}
(which follows,
for example, from Brakke’s clearing out lemma), and from the local regularity theory
in
[
9
]
.

Theorem 24
.

Let
\Omega
be a region in

such that

is an
(m+1)
-dimensional

. Let

.

]

.
For completeness, we sketch the proof below

Proof.

By passing to the double cover, we can assume that

Consider first the case when

Let

For

-dimensional locally integral currents

Note that if

Lemma 25
.

Suppose

Existence, stability and regularity of periodic solutions for nonlinear Fokker-Planck equations
Eric Luçon, Christophe Poquet

Abstract
. We consider a class of nonlinear Fokker-Planck equations describing the dynamics of an infinite population of units within mean-field interaction. Relying on a slow-fast viewpoint and on the theory of approximately invariant manifolds we obtain the existence of a stable periodic solution for the PDE, consisting of probability measures. Moreover we establish the existence of a smooth isochron map in the neighborhood of this periodic solution.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The model

We are interested in this paper in the existence, stability and regularity of periodic solutions to the following nonlinear PDE on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(
d\;\geqslant\;1
):

\partial_{t}u_{t}=\nabla\cdot\left(\sigma^{2}u_{t}\right)+\nabla\cdot\left(K\left(x-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}yu_{t}({\rm d}y)\right)u_{t}\right)-\delta\nabla\cdot\left(F(x)u_{t}\right).

(1.1)

Here,
t\;\geqslant\;0\mapsto u_{t}
is a probability measure-valued process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
K
and
\sigma
and diagonal matrices with positive coefficients and
F:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a smooth bounded function with bounded derivatives. Equation (
1.1
) has a natural probabilistic interpretation: if
u_{0}
is a probability distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it is well known
[
30
,
37
]
that
u_{t}
is the law of the McKean-Vlasov process
X_{t}
where
X_{0}\sim u_{0}
and

dX_{t}=\delta F(X_{t})\,\text{\rm d}t-K\left(X_{t}-{\mathbb{E}}[X_{t}]\right){\rm d}t+\sqrt{2}\sigma\,\text{\rm d}B_{t},

(1.2)

The dynamics of the process
(X_{t})_{t\;\geqslant\;0}
is the superposition of a local part
\delta F(X_{t}){\rm d}t
, where
\delta>0
is a scaling parameter, a linear interaction term
K\left(X_{t}-{\mathbb{E}}[X_{t}]\right){\rm d}t
, modulated by the intensity matrix
K
, and an additive noise given by a standard Brownian motion
(B_{t})_{t\;\geqslant\;0}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The difficulty in the analysis of (
1.2
) lies in its nonlinear character:
X_{t}
interacts with its own law, more precisely its own expectation
\mathbb{E}\left[X_{t}\right]
. The long-time dynamics of (
1.2
) is a longstanding issue in the literature. In particular, the existence of stable equilibria for (
1.1
) (that is invariant measures for (
1.2
)) has been studied for various choices of dynamics, interaction and regimes of parameters
\delta,K,\sigma
, mostly in a context where the corresponding particle dynamics defined in (
1.3
) below is reversible (see e.g.
[
39
,
7
,
11
]
for further details and references).

The question we address in the present paper concerns the existence of periodic solutions to nonlinear equation such as (
1.1
). In this case, a major difficulty lies in the fact that the underlying microscopic dynamics is not reversible. From a practical perspective, the emergence of periodicity in such models relates in particular to chemical reactions (Brusselator model
[
35
]
), neurosciences
[
20
,
21
,
2
,
9
,
17
,
27
,
33
,
28
,
14
]
, and statistical physics (e.g. spin-flip models
[
13
,
16
]
, see also to
[
12
]
, where the model considered is in fact not mean-field, but the Ising model with dissipation). An example of particular interest concerns the FitzHugh-Nagumo model
[
34
,
2
]
(take
d=2
and
F(x,y)=\left(x-\frac{x^{3}}{3}-y,\frac{1}{c}\left(x+a-by\right)\right)
), commonly used as a prototype for excitability in neuronal models
[
26
]
or in physics
[
3
]
. Roughly speaking, excitability refers to the ability for a neuron to emit spikes (oscillations) in the presence of perturbations (such as noise and/or external input) whereas this neuron would be at rest (steady state) without perturbation. The long-time dynamics of (
1.1
) in the FizHugh-Nagumo case has been the subject of several previous works (existence of equilibria
[
31
,
33
]
or periodic solutions
[
27
,
28
]
) under various asymptotics of the parameters
(\delta,K,\sigma)
. A crucial feature in this context is the influence of noise and interaction in the emergence and stability of periodic solutions: generically, some balance has to be found in the quantity of noise and interaction that one needs to put in the system in order to observe oscillations (see
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
for further details).

Stability properties and regular isochron map

The purpose of the present paper is to complement the previous results concerning the existence of periodic orbits for (
1.1
) with accurate stability properties for this periodic solution and with the existence of a sufficiently regular isochron map, properties that are absent in the previous works cited above. We obtain these additional properties by applying a result concerning normally hyperbolic invariant manifolds in Banach spaces proved by Bates, Lu and Zeng
[
5
]
. The technical counterpart is that we require assumptions on
F
and
\sigma
that are somehow stricter than the ones used in
[
35
,
27
,
33
,
28
]
, in the sense that we are considering a dynamics
F
that is bounded together with all its derivatives (the Brusselator model and FitzHugh Nagumo model considered in the references given above have polynomial growth) as well as nondegenerate noise on all components (while in
[
33
,
28
]
the noise is only present in one of the two variables).

Large time asymptotics for the mean-field particle system

Standard propagation of chaos results
[
37
]
shows that (
1.2
) is the natural limit of the following mean-field particle system

dX_{i,t}=\delta F(X_{i,t})\,\text{\rm d}t-K\left(X_{i,t}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j,t}\right)\,\text{\rm d}t+\sqrt{2}\sigma\,\text{\rm d}B_{i,t},

(1.3)

in the sense that one can easily couple (
1.3
) and (
1.2
) by choosing the same realization of the noise, so that the resulting error is of order
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}
as
N\to\infty
, at least on any
[0,T]
with
T
that can be arbitrarily large but fixed independently from
N
. At the level of the whole particle system, this boils down to the convergence as
N\to\infty
of the empirical measure
u_{N,t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{i,t}}
to
u_{t}
, solution to (
1.1
). Hence, supposing that (
1.1
) has a periodic solution
\left(\Gamma^{\delta}_{t}\right)_{t\;\geqslant\;0}
, if the empirical measure
u_{N,0}
is initially close to
\Gamma^{\delta}_{\theta_{0}}
,
u_{N,t}
has, for
N
large, a behavior close to being periodic, since it stays close to
\Gamma^{\delta}_{\theta_{0}+t}
.

The companion paper
[
29
]
of the present work is concerned with the behavior of the empirical measure
u_{N,t}
on a time scale
T
that is no longer bounded, but of order
N
. We show in
[
29
]
that
u_{N,Nt}
is close to
\Gamma^{\delta}_{\theta_{0}+Nt+\beta^{N}_{t}}
, where
\beta^{N}_{t}
is a random process in
{\mathbb{R}}
whose weak limit as
N\to\infty
has constant drift and diffusion coefficient. This kind of result was already obtained in
[
15
,
8
]
in the case of the plane rotators model (mean-field noisy interacting oscillators defined on the circle), for which in the scale
Nt
the empirical measure has a diffusive behavior along the curve of stationary points. Our aim in
[
29
]
is to get similar results for models like (
1.1
) that are defined in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and are not reversible (while the plane rotators model is). As we will explain in more details later, the additional stability and regularity results concerning periodic solution to (
1.1
) obtained in the present paper are crucial for the study of long time behavior of the mean-field particle systems (
1.3
) made in
[
29
]
.

1.2.
Slow fast viewpoint and application to the FitzHugh-Nagumo model

We give in this paragraph informal intuition on the possibility of emergence of periodic solutions to (
1.1
). The point of view we adopt here is a slow-fast approach, based on the assumption that the parameter
\delta
in (
1.1
) is small, as it was already the case in
[
27
,
28
]
. More precisely, the linear character of the interaction term in (
1.1
) allows us to decompose the dynamics of (
1.1
) into its expectation
m_{t}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}d}xu_{t}(x)
and its centered version
p_{t}(x)=u_{t}(x-m_{t})
: (
1.1
) is equivalent to the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\partial_{t}p_{t}&=&{\mathcal{L}}p_{t}-\nabla\cdot(p_{t}(\delta F_{m_{t}}-\dot{m}_{t}))\\
\dot{m}_{t}&=&\delta\int F_{m_{t}}\,\text{\rm d}p_{t}\end{array}\right.,

(1.4)

where

{\mathcal{L}}u=\nabla\cdot(\sigma^{2}\nabla f)+\nabla\cdot\left(Kxf\right),

(1.5)

and
F_{m}(x)=F(x+m)
. Remark that
(p_{t},m_{t})
is the weak limit as
N\to\infty
of the process
\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{Y_{i,t}},m_{N,t}\right)
, where

m_{N,t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i,t},\quad\text{and}\quad Y_{i,t}=X_{i,t}-m_{N,t}.

(1.6)

In this set-up,
p_{t}
is here the fast variable, while
m_{t}
is the slow one. For
\delta=0
, this system reduces to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\partial_{t}p^{0}_{t}&=&{\mathcal{L}}p^{0}_{t}\\
m^{0}_{t}&=&m_{0}\end{array}\right.,

(1.7)

so
p^{0}_{t}=e^{t{\mathcal{L}}}p_{0}
is the distribution of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, and thus converges exponentially fast to
\rho
, the density of the Gaussian distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with mean
0
and variance
\sigma^{2}K^{-1}
(see Proposition
1.1
for more details on the contraction properties of
{\mathcal{L}}
). So heuristically, taking
\delta
small, at first approximation
p_{t}
stays close to
\rho
while
m_{t}
satisfies

\dot{m}_{t}\approx\delta\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}F_{m_{t}}(x)\rho(x)\,\text{\rm d}x=\delta\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}F(x)\rho(m_{t}-x)\,\text{\rm d}x=\delta(F*\rho)(m_{t}).

(1.8)

For the non-centered PDE (
1.1
) this approximation means that
u_{t}
is close to a Gaussian distribution with variance variance
\sigma^{2}K^{-1}
and mean
m_{t}
, where the dynamics of
m_{t}
is governed at first order by (
1.8
). Following this heuristic, we expect a periodic behavior for the system (
1.4
) if the approximate dynamics of
m_{t}
is itself periodic. In this spirit, the main hypothesis we will adopt below is that the solution
z_{t}
to

\dot{z}_{t}=\delta\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}F_{z_{t}}(x)\rho(x)\,\text{\rm d}x=\delta\left\langle F_{z_{t}},\rho\right\rangle

(1.9)

admits a stable periodic solution
(\alpha^{\delta}_{t})_{t\in[0,\frac{T_{\alpha}}{\delta}]}
(more details on this notion of stability, in terms of Floquet exponents, will be given in Section
1.4
). In Proposition
1.7
we will show that with these hypotheses, the manifold
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\delta}=(\rho,\alpha^{\delta}_{t})_{t\in[0,T_{\alpha}/\delta]}
is approximately invariant for (
1.4
).

Let us now describe a situation where the above heuristics is true: in
[
27
,
28
]
we considered the classical FitzHugh-Nagumo model
defined by
d=2
and

F(x,y)=\left(x-\frac{x^{3}}{3}-y,\frac{1}{c}\left(x+a-by\right)\right).

(1.10)

A direct calculation shows that in that case, with
K=\text{diag}(k_{1},k_{2})
and
\sigma=\text{diag}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}F_{z_{1},z_{2}}(x,y)\rho(x,y)\,\text{\rm d}x\,\,\text{\rm d}y=\left(\left(1-\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{k_{1}}\right)z_{1}-\frac{z_{1}^{3}}{3}-z_{2},\frac{1}{c}\left(z_{1}+a-bz_{2}\right)\right),

(1.11)

which defines again a FitzHugh-Nagumo model. The additional factor
\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{k_{1}}
in (
1.11
) reflects the influence of noise and interaction in the mean-field system (
1.2
). For an accurate choice of parameters (take e.g.
a=\frac{1}{3}
,
b=1
and
c=10
), it can be shown that the dynamics of the mean value (
1.9
) has a unique steady state when
\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{k_{1}}=0
whereas it admits a stable periodic solution for
\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{k_{1}}
not too small and not too large, for example
\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{k_{1}}=0.2
. We refer to
[
27
]
, § 3.4 for a more details of the corresponding bifurcations). The purpose of
[
27
,
28
]
was to show that the heuristics developed above is true, i.e. the periodicity of (
1.9
) propagates to (
1.4
). This emergence of periodic behavior induced by noise and interaction is a signature of excitability: the system (
1.1
) exhibits a periodic behavior induced by the combined effect of the noise and the interaction, which is not present in the isolated system
\dot{z}_{t}=F(z_{t})
. We refer to
[
27
]
for a discussion and references on this phenomenon.

As already said, the point of the present work is to go beyond the existence of oscillations for (
1.1
), that is to prove regularity for the dynamics around such a limit cycle. Unfortunately the FitzHugh Nagumo model does not satisfy the hypotheses of this present work, since it has polynomial growth at infinity.
However it is easy to see that if
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is a smooth non-increasing function that satisfies
\psi(t)=1
for
t\;\leqslant\;1
and
\psi(t)=0
for
t\;\geqslant\;2
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
the function
x\mapsto F(x)\psi(\varepsilon|x|)
satisfies our hypotheses, and that
z\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}F_{z}(x)\psi(\varepsilon|x+z|)\rho(x)dx
converges to
z\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}F_{z}(x)\rho(x)dx
in
C^{1}(\mathcal{B}(0,R),{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for any ball
\mathcal{B}(0,R)
centered at
0
with radius
R
. So, relying on classical results on normally hyperbolic manifolds
[
18
,
19
,
40
]
(a definition of this notion will be provided in Section
1.4
), if (
1.9
) admits a stable limit cycle, then it will also be the case replacing
F
with
x\mapsto F(x)\psi(\varepsilon|x|)
for
\varepsilon
small enough.

1.3.
Weighted Sobolev norms

We present in this section the Sobolev spaces that we will use in the paper. Let us denote by
\left|x\right|_{A}=\left(x\cdot Ax\right)^{1/2}
the Euclidean norm twisted by some positive matrix
A
, and, for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, let us define the weight
w_{\theta}
by

w_{\theta}(x)=\exp\left(-\frac{\theta}{2}\left|x\right|_{K\sigma^{-2}}^{2}\right).

(1.12)

We denote as
L^{2}_{\theta}
the
L^{2}
-space with weight
w_{\theta}
, that is with norm

\left\|h\right\|_{L^{2}_{\theta}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|h(x)\right|^{2}w_{\theta}(x){\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(1.13)

For any
\theta>0
we consider the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator

{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta}^{\ast}f=\nabla\cdot(\sigma^{2}\nabla f)-\theta Kx\cdot\nabla f.

(1.14)

It is well know (see for example
[
1
]
) that
\mathcal{L}_{\theta}^{\ast}
admits the following decomposition: for all
l\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
,

{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta}^{\ast}\psi_{l}=-\lambda_{l}\psi_{l},\quad\text{with}\quad\lambda_{l}=\theta\sum_{i=1}^{d}k_{i}l_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{l}(x):=\psi_{l,\theta}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}h_{l_{i}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\theta k_{i}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}}}x_{i}\right),

(1.15)

where
h_{n}
is the
n^{\text{th}}
renormalized Hermite polynomial:

h_{n}(x)=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{4}}}e^{\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\frac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}x^{n}}\left\{e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\right\}.

(1.16)

The family
(\psi_{l,\theta})_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}_{\theta}
. For
f,g
with decompositions
f=\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}f_{l}\psi_{l}
and
g=\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}g_{l}\psi_{l}
, we consider the scalar products

\langle f,g\rangle_{H^{r}_{\theta}}=\left\langle(a_{\theta}-{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta}^{\ast})^{r}f,\bar{g}\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\theta}}=\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{l})^{r}f_{l}\bar{g}_{l},

(1.17)

where
a_{\theta}=\theta\,\mathrm{Tr}K

and denote by
H^{r}_{\theta}
the completion of the space of smooth function
u
satisfying
\|u\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}<\infty
. The choice of the constant
a_{\theta}
is made to simplify some technical proofs given in the Appendix
A
(see the proof of Proposition
A.2
). Another choice of positive constant would produce an equivalent norm. From Lemma
A.1
it is clear that
\left\|\partial_{x_{i}}f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;\|f\|_{H^{r+1}_{\theta}}
, and that, if
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the norm
\|f\|_{H^{n}_{\theta}}
is in fact equivalent to

\sqrt{\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d},\,\sum_{i=1}^{d}l_{i}\;\leqslant\;n}\left\|\partial^{l_{1}}_{x_{1}}\ldots\partial^{l_{d}}_{x_{d}}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\theta}}}.

(1.18)

We denote by
H^{-r}_{\theta}
the dual of
H^{r}_{\theta}
. Relying on a “pivot” space structure (for more details, see Appendix
A
), the product
\langle u,f\rangle_{H^{-r}_{\theta},H^{r}_{\theta}}
can be identified with the flat
L^{2}
product
\langle u,f\rangle
:
L^{2}_{-\theta}
can be seen as a subset of
H^{-r}_{\theta}
, and for all
f\in H^{r}_{\theta}
and
u\in L^{2}_{-\theta}
we have

\langle u,f\rangle_{H^{-r}_{\theta},H^{r}_{\theta}}=\langle u,f\rangle.

(1.19)

This identification allows us to view the operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta}
defined by

{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta}u=\nabla\cdot(\sigma^{2}\nabla f)+\nabla\cdot\left(\theta Kxf\right),

(1.20)

seen as an operator in
H^{-r}_{\theta}
, as the adjoint of
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta}
, seen as an operator in
H^{r}_{\theta}
. This is in particular the case for
{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{L}}_{1}
, whose contraction properties will be crucial in the results given in this paper.

Our aim in this paper is to give the existence of a periodic solution for (
1.4
) viewing
p_{t}
as an element of
H^{-r}_{\theta}
. The necessity of considering
H^{-r}_{\theta}
instead of simply taking
H^{-r}_{1}
goes back to the companion paper
[
29
]
, in which we study the long time behavior of the empirical measure
u_{N,t}
in the same functional spaces. Since this empirical measure involves a sum of Dirac distributions, it can be seen as an element of
H^{-r}_{\theta}
for
r>d/2
, and we have
\|\delta_{x}\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;Cw_{\frac{\theta}{4-\eta}}(x)
for
\eta>0
(see Lemma 2.1 in
[
29
]
). Some moment estimates lead us in
[
29
]
to bound terms of the form
{\mathbb{E}}\left[w_{\frac{m\theta}{4-\eta}}(Y_{i,t})\right]
with
m
large and
Y_{i,t}
defined in (
1.6
). Since we consider cases where
Y_{i,t}
has a distribution close to
\rho
, for this expectation to be bounded we need to consider small values of
\theta
. We need therefore to work in
H_{\theta}^{-r}
for general
\theta
and not only for
\theta=1
.

It is well known that the semi-group
e^{t{\mathcal{L}}}
satisfies, for
\lambda<\min(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
and
u\in H^{-r}_{1}
with
\int u=0
, the contraction property

\left\|e^{t{\mathcal{L}}}u\right\|_{H^{-r}_{1}}\;\leqslant\;Ct^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}e^{-t\lambda}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{-(r+\alpha)}_{1}}.

(1.21)

By obtaining similar estimates (see the following Proposition, which is a particular case of the slightly more general Proposition
A.3
), we will be able to work in the space
H^{-r}_{\theta}
with any value of
\theta
smaller than
1
, but with the constraint of considering values of
r
larger than a
r_{0}>0
(independent from
\theta
).

Proposition 1.1
.

For all
0<\theta\;\leqslant\;1
the operator
{\mathcal{L}}
is sectorial and generates an analytical semi-group in
H^{-r}_{\theta}
. Moreover we have the following estimates: for any
\alpha\;\geqslant\;0
,
r\;\geqslant\;0
and
\lambda<\min(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
there exists a constant
C_{\mathcal{L}}>0
such that for all
u\in H_{\theta}^{-(r+\alpha)}
,

\left\|e^{t{\mathcal{L}}}u\right\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C_{\mathcal{L}}\left(1+t^{-\alpha/2}e^{-\lambda t}\right)\|u\|_{H^{-(r+\alpha)}_{\theta}},

(1.22)

and for
r\;\geqslant\;1
,

\left\|e^{t{\mathcal{L}}}\nabla u\right\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C_{\mathcal{L}}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\lambda t}\|u\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}\,.

(1.23)

Moreover for all
r\;\geqslant\;0
,
0<\varepsilon\;\leqslant\;1
and
s\;\geqslant\;0
,

\left\|\left(e^{(t+s)\mathcal{L}}-e^{t\mathcal{L}}\right)u\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-r}}\;\leqslant\;C_{\mathcal{L}}s^{\varepsilon}t^{-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}e^{-\lambda t}\left\|u\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-(r+1)}}.

(1.24)

Finally, there exist
r_{0}>0
such that for any
0<\theta\;\leqslant\;1
, for all
r>r_{0}
,
t>0
and all
u\in H^{-r}_{\theta}
satisfying
\int u=0
,

\left\|e^{t{\mathcal{L}}}u\right\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C_{\mathcal{L}}e^{-\lambda t}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}.

(1.25)

1.4.
Main results

With the notation
\mu_{t}:=(p_{t},m_{t})
the system (
1.4
) becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}p_{t}&={\mathcal{L}}p_{t}+\delta G_{1}(\mu_{t})\\
\dot{m}_{t}&=\delta G_{2}(\mu_{t})\end{array}\right.,

(1.26)

where

G(\mu)=G(p,m)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}G_{1}(p,m)\\
G_{2}(p,m)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\nabla\cdot\left(p\left(F_{m}-\int F_{m}p\right)\right)\\
\int F_{m}p\end{array}\right).

(1.27)

We place ourselves on the space

\mathbf{H}^{r}_{\theta}:=H_{\theta}^{r}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

endowed with the scalar product

\left\langle(f,m)\,,\,(g,m^{\prime})\right\rangle_{\mathbf{H}^{r}_{\theta}}:=\left\langle f\,,\,g\right\rangle_{H_{\theta}^{r}}+m\cdot m^{\prime}.

(1.28)

We will denote
\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}
the dual of
\mathbf{H}^{r}_{\theta}
. Clearly
\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}=H^{-r}_{\theta}\times{\mathbb{R}}
and, relying as above on a “pivot” space structure, the product
\langle(\nu,h),(\phi,\psi)\rangle_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta},\mathbf{H}^{r}_{\theta}}
can be identified with the flat scalar product

\mathopen{\hbox{${\big\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\big\langle}$}}(\nu,h);(\varphi,\psi)\mathclose{\hbox{${\big\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\big\rangle}$}}=\langle\nu,\varphi\rangle+h\cdot\psi.

(1.29)

The following theorem states the existence and uniqueness of mild solutions of (
1.26
). Its proof, given in Section
2
, relies on classical arguments, due to the fact that
G:\mathbf{H}^{-r+1}_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}
is locally Lispchitz and
{\mathcal{L}}
is sectorial (see
[
36
]
).

Theorem 1.2
.

For any initial condition
\mu=(p,m)\in\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}
with
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}p=1
there exists a unique maximal mild solution
\mu_{t}:=(p_{t},m_{t})=T^{t}(\mu)
to (
1.26
) on
[0,t_{c}]
for some
t_{c}>0
, which satisfies

t\mapsto T^{t}(\mu)\in\mathcal{C}\left(\left[0,t_{c}\right);\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}\right)
.

Moreover,
\mu\mapsto T^{t}(\mu)
is
C^{2}
, and for any
R>0
, there exists a
\delta(R)>0
such that for all
0\;\leqslant\;\delta\;\leqslant\;\delta(R)
and
\mu_{0}=(p_{0},m_{0})
satisfying
\|p_{0}-\rho\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;R
the solution
T^{t}(\mu_{0})
is well defined for all
t\;\geqslant\;0
and there exists a
C(R)>0
such that

\sup_{t\;\geqslant\;0}\|p_{t}\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C(R).

(1.30)

Remark 1.3
.

Since we are interested in the existence of a periodic solution made of probability distributions, we will only consider initial condition
(p_{0},m_{0})
satisfying
\int p_{0}=1
, and the conservation of mass will induce that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}p_{t}=1
for all
t
. In the same spirit, we will only apply the differential of the semi-group
DT^{t}(\mu)
to elements
\nu=(\eta,n)\in\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}
that satisfy
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\eta=0
.

As it was previously mentioned, we suppose in the following that the ordinary differential equation (
1.9
)
admits a stable periodic solution
(\alpha^{\delta}_{t})_{t\in[0,\frac{T_{\alpha}}{\delta}]}
. To state more precisely this hypothesis we rely on Floquet formalism (see for example
[
38
]
): let us denote by
\pi^{\delta}_{u+t,u}
the principal matrix solution associated to the periodic solution
\alpha
, that it the solution to

\partial_{t}\pi^{\delta}_{u+t,u}=\delta\langle DF_{\alpha^{\delta}_{u+t}},\rho\rangle\pi^{\delta}_{u+t,u},\ \pi^{\delta}_{u,u}=I.

(1.31)

The process
\pi^{\delta}_{u+t,u}
characterizes the linearized dynamics around
(\alpha^{\delta}_{t})_{t\in[0,\frac{T}{\delta}]}
. We will suppose that this linearized dynamics is a contraction on a supplementary space of the tangent space to
(\alpha^{\delta}_{t})_{t\in[0,\frac{T}{\delta}]}
. More precisely, we suppose that there exist projections
P^{\delta,c}_{u}
and
P^{\delta,s}_{u}
for all
u\in[0,\frac{T}{\delta}]
with
u\mapsto P^{\delta,c}_{u}
and
u\mapsto P^{\delta,s}_{u}
smooth, that satisfy
P^{\delta,s}_{u}+P^{\delta,c}_{u}=I
(
P^{\delta,c}_{u}
being a projection on
\text{vect}(\dot{\alpha}^{\delta}_{u})
) and that commute with
\pi^{\delta}
, i.e.

P^{\delta,s}_{u}+P^{\delta,c}_{u}=I,\quad P^{\delta,s}_{u+t}\pi^{\delta}_{u+t,u}=\pi^{\delta}_{u+t,u}P^{\delta,s}_{u}.

(1.32)

We emphasize here the fact that the factor
\delta
in (
1.9
) is only responsible for a time-scale of the dynamics (the smaller
\delta
, the slower the dynamics, the period being then
T_{\alpha}/\delta
): we have
\alpha^{\delta}_{t}=\alpha^{1}_{\delta t}
. Its effect on the projections is only a change of parametrization:
P^{\delta,s}_{u}
and
P^{\delta,c}_{u}
are defined on
[0,T/\delta)
, and
P^{\delta,s}_{u/\delta}=P^{1,s}_{u}
,
P^{\delta,c}_{u/\delta}=P^{1,c}_{u}
for
u\in[0,T_{\alpha})
.

The stability of the periodic solution
(\alpha^{\delta}_{t})_{t\in[0,\frac{T_{\alpha}}{\delta}]}
is expressed by the following hypothesis: there exist positive constants
c_{\alpha},C_{\alpha}
and
\lambda_{\alpha}
such that

\left|\pi^{\delta}_{u+t,u}P^{\delta,s}_{u}n\right|\;\leqslant\;C_{\alpha}e^{-\delta\lambda_{\alpha}t}|n|\quad\text{and}\quad c_{\alpha}|n|\;\leqslant\;\left|\pi^{\delta}_{u+t,u}P^{\delta,c}_{u}n\right|\;\leqslant\;C_{\alpha}|n|.

(1.33)

The factor
\delta
in the rate of contraction is again due to the fact that the factor
\delta
in (
1.9
) slows the dynamics.

With these hypotheses
(\alpha^{\delta}_{t})_{t\in[0,\frac{T}{\delta}]}
is in fact a simple example of Normally Hyperbolic Invariant Manifold (NHIM). We follow here the definition given in
[
4
]
for this concept: on a Banach space
\mathbf{X}
, a smooth compact connected manifold
\mathbf{M}
is said to be a normally hyperbolic invariant manifold for a continuous semi flow
\mathbf{T}
(such that
u\mapsto\mathbf{T}^{t}(\mu)
is
C^{1}
for all
t\;\geqslant\;0
) if

(1)

\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{M})\subset\mathbf{M}
for all
t\;\geqslant\;0
,

(2)

For each
m\in\mathbf{M}
there exists a decomposition
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{X}^{c}_{m}+\mathbf{X}^{u}_{m}+\mathbf{X}^{s}_{m}
of closed subspaces with
\mathbf{X}^{c}_{m}
the tangent space to
\mathbf{M}
at
m
,

(3)

For each
m\in\mathbf{M}
and
t\;\geqslant\;0
, denoting
m_{1}=\mathbf{T}^{t}(m)
, we have
D\mathbf{T}^{t}(m)_{|\mathbf{X}^{\iota}_{m}}:\mathbf{X}^{\iota}_{m}\rightarrow\mathbf{X}^{\iota}_{m_{1}}
for
\iota=c,u,s
, and
D\mathbf{T}^{t}(m)_{|\mathbf{X}^{u}_{m}}
is an isomorphism from
\mathbf{X}^{u}_{m}
to
\mathbf{X}^{u}_{m_{1}}
.

(4)

There exists a
t_{0}\;\geqslant\;0
and a
\lambda>0
such that, for all
t\;\geqslant\;t_{0}
,

\lambda\inf\{|D\mathbf{T}^{t}(m)[x^{u}]|:\,x^{u}\in\mathbf{X}^{u},\,|x^{u}|=1\}>\max\left\{1,\left\|D\mathbf{T}^{t}(m)_{|\mathbf{X}^{c}_{m}}\right\|\right\},

(1.34)

\lambda\min\{1,\inf|D\mathbf{T}^{t}(m)[x^{c}]|:\,x^{c}\in\mathbf{X}^{c}_{m},\,|x^{c}|=1\}\}>\left\|D\mathbf{T}^{t}(m)_{|\mathbf{X}^{s}_{m}}\right\|.

(1.35)

(
1.34
) implies that the semi flow
\mathbf{T}^{t}
is expansive at
m
in the direction
\mathbf{X}^{u}_{m}
at a rate strictly larger than on
\mathbf{M}
, while (
1.35
) shows implies that it is contractive at
m
in the direction
\mathbf{X}^{s}_{m}
at a rate greater than on
\mathbf{M}
.

This kind of structure is known to be robust under perturbation of the semi-flow: it has been shown in
[
18
,
19
]
for flows in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and then generalized in
[
24
]
in the case of Riemanian manifolds and in
[
4
,
36
]
in the infinite dimensional setting. An improvement of these classical results has been obtained in
[
5
]
by Bates, Lu and Zeng, who showed that if a system admits a manifold that is approximately invariant and approximately normally hyperbolic (a precise definition of these notions will be given in Section
1.5
), then the system possesses an actual normally hyperbolic invariant manifold in a neighborhood.

We will rely on this deep result in our work. Here, the slow fast viewpoint described in Section
1.2
suggests that for
\delta
small the manifold
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\delta}=\{(\rho,\alpha_{t}):\,t\in[0,T_{\alpha})\}
is an approximately invariant manifold which is approximately normally hyperbolic (without unstable direction). This statement will be written rigorously in Section
1.5
, and proved in Section
3
. This idea will allow us to prove for
\delta
small enough the existence of a stable periodic solution to (
1.4
), as an actual normally hyperbolic invariant manifold in a neighborhood of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\delta}
. For a stable periodic solution, conditions (
1.34
) and (
1.35
) reduce to the fact that
DT^{t}(m)
is bounded from above and below in the direction of the tangent space to the invariant manifold defined by the periodic solution, and is contractive on a stable direction.

1.5.

With this notation we have

\tau

We give in this section the existence, uniqueness and regularity result of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We first remark that
G:\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathbf{H}^{-(r+1)}_{\theta}
is locally Lispchitz. Indeed, for any
(p,m)\in\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}
and any
(\varphi,\psi)\in\mathbf{H}^{r+1}_{\theta}
,

\mathopen{\hbox{${\big\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\big\langle}$}}G(p,m),(\varphi,\psi)\mathclose{\hbox{${\big\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\big\rangle}$}}=-\left\langle p\left(F_{m}-\int F_{m}p\right),\nabla\varphi\right\rangle+\psi\cdot\int F_{m}p.

(2.1)

We have
\left|\int F_{m}p\right|\;\leqslant\;\|F_{m}\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\|p\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}
, and due to the fact that all derivatives of
F
are bounded,
\|F_{m}\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C_{F}
independently from
m
. Moreover, due to the same reason, we have
\|F_{m}\cdot\nabla\varphi\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C_{F}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}
independently from
m
. This means that

\left|\mathopen{\hbox{${\big\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\big\langle}$}}G(p,m),(\varphi,\psi)\mathclose{\hbox{${\big\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\big\rangle}$}}\right|\;\leqslant\;C\|p\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}\left(1+\|p\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}\right)\|\varphi\|_{H^{r+1}_{\theta}}+C\|p\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}|\psi|.

(2.2)

We deduce

We first prove the following lemma, which shows the link between the norm

Lemma A.1
.

For all
\theta>0

C_{1}
,
C_{2}

(A.1)

Proof.

For
u
with decomposition

\displaystyle=(\theta^{\prime}-\theta)\sum_{i=1}^{d}k_{i}\left(l_{i}\psi_{\theta,l}+\sqrt{l_{i}(l_{i}-1)}\psi_{\theta,l_{\downdownarrows_{i}}}\right),

(A.13)

where we used the following notations for the shifts w.r.t. the
i
th coordinate

\displaystyle l_{\downarrow_{i}}

\displaystyle=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{i-1},l_{i}-1,l_{i+1},\ldots,l_{d}),

(A.14)

\displaystyle l_{\uparrow_{i}}

\displaystyle=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{i-1},l_{i}+1,l_{i+1},\ldots,l_{d})

(A.15)

and the convention
\psi_{l}=0
if
l_{i}<0
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. In particular we have, recalling that
\lambda_{\theta,l}=\theta\sum_{i=1}^{d}k_{i}l_{i}
,

-{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\psi_{\theta,l}=\frac{\theta^{\prime}\lambda_{\theta,l}}{\theta}\psi_{\theta,l}+(\theta^{\prime}-\theta)\sum_{i=1}^{d}k_{i}\sqrt{l_{i}(l_{i}-1)}\psi_{\theta,l_{\downdownarrows_{i}}},

(A.16)

So, we deduce that for
f=\sum_{l}f_{l}\psi_{\theta,l}
, with
f_{l}\in{\mathbb{C}}
for all
l
,

\displaystyle\left\|\left({\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}\right)f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\sum_{l}(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})^{r}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}\sqrt{(l_{i}+1)(l_{i}+2)}f_{l_{\upuparrows_{i}}}\right|^{2}.

Setting

\displaystyle I(r,f)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{l}(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})^{r}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}\sqrt{(l_{i}+1)(l_{i}+2)}f_{l_{\upuparrows_{i}}}\right)^{2}.

(A.17)

Using Jensen’s inequality, we obtain (recalling that
a_{\theta}=\theta\mathrm{Tr}(K)
),

\displaystyle I(r,f)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\sum_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{d}=0}^{+\infty}\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)^{r}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}(l_{i}+1)\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}(l_{i}+2)\left|f_{l_{\upuparrows_{i}}}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{d}=0}^{+\infty}\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)^{r+1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}(l_{i}+2)\left|f_{l_{\upuparrows_{i}}}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{d}=2}^{+\infty}\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l-2}\right)^{r+1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}l_{i}\left|f_{l_{1}-2,\ldots,l_{i},l_{i+1}-2,l_{d}-2}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{d}=2}^{+\infty}\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l-2}\right)^{r+1}\theta k_{i}l_{i}\left|f_{l_{1}-2,\ldots,l_{i},l_{i+1}-2,l_{d}-2}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{l_{i}=2}^{+\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{p}=0\\
p\neq i\end{subarray}}^{+\infty}\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l_{\downdownarrows_{i}}}\right)^{r+1}\theta k_{i}l_{i}\left|f_{l}\right|^{2}.

Now we use that
\lambda_{\theta,l_{\downdownarrows_{i}}}\;\leqslant\;\lambda_{\theta,l}
for any
l
and
i
, so that

I(r,f)\;\leqslant\;\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{l_{i}=2}^{+\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{p}=0\\
p\neq i\end{subarray}}^{+\infty}\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)^{r+1}\theta k_{i}l_{i}\left|f_{l}\right|^{2},

(A.18)

This sum is anyway smaller than

\displaystyle I(r,f)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{l_{i}=0}^{+\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{p}=0\\
p\neq i\end{subarray}}^{+\infty}\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)^{r+1}\theta k_{i}l_{i}\left|f_{l}\right|^{2}=\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{+\infty}\ldots\sum_{l_{d}=0}^{+\infty}\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)^{r+1}\lambda_{\theta,l}\left|f_{l}\right|^{2},

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{+\infty}\ldots\sum_{l_{d}=0}^{+\infty}\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)^{r+2}\left|f_{l}\right|^{2}=\left\|(a_{\theta}-\mathcal{L}_{\theta}^{\ast})f\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{r}}^{2}.

(A.19)

Coming back to (
A.17
), we obtain

\left\|\left({\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;\left(1-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}\right)\left\|(a_{\theta}-\mathcal{L}_{\theta}^{\ast})f\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{r}},

(A.20)

which implies in particular that

\left\|\left(a_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)f\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{r}}\;\leqslant\;\left(2-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}\right)\left\|(a_{\theta}-{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta})f\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{r}}=\left(2-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}\right)\left\|f\right\|_{H^{r+2}_{\theta}}.

(A.21)

Let us now look for a lower bound. Using
(a+b)^{2}\;\geqslant\;\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}a^{2}-\varepsilon b^{2}
(
\varepsilon>0
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|\left(a_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})^{r}\Bigg((a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})f_{l}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(1-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}\sqrt{(l_{i}+1)(l_{i}+2)}f_{l_{\upuparrows_{i}}}\Bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\sum_{l}(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})^{r+2}f_{l}^{2}-\varepsilon\left(1-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}\right)^{2}I(r,f).

(A.22)

Hence, recalling (
A.19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\left(a_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}-\left(1-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)\left\|(a_{\theta}-\mathcal{L}_{\theta}^{\ast})f\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{r}}^{2}

(A.23)

So for
\varepsilon>0
small enough (depending only on
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
), there exists a constant
c_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}>0
such that we have
\left\|\left(a_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\;\geqslant\;c_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}\left\|(a_{\theta}-{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta})f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}
. This
means, together with (
A.21
), that

c_{\theta,\theta^{\prime}}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{r+2}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;\left\|\left(a_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;\left(2-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}\right)\left\|f\right\|_{H^{r+2}_{\theta}}.

(A.24)

In particular
0
is in the resolvent set of
a_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}
, and
a_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}
has a compact resolvent, since it is the case for
a_{\theta}-{\mathcal{L}}^{\ast}_{\theta}
. So
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}
has a discrete spectrum, composed of a sequence of eigenvalues with modulus going to infinity. But any eigenfunction
\psi
of
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}
in
H^{r}_{\theta}
is also an eigenfunction of
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}
in
H^{r}_{\theta^{\prime}}
, and thus the eigenvalues of
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}
in
H^{r}_{\theta}
are the
\lambda_{l,\theta^{\prime}}
’s, with associated eigenfunctions the
\psi_{l,\theta^{\prime}}
’s. So in particular
{\mathcal{L}}^{\ast}_{\theta^{\prime}}
is sectorial, and thus generates an analytic semigroup
e^{t\mathcal{L}^{\ast}_{\theta^{\prime}}}
in
H^{r}_{\theta}
.

Let us now prove that the interpolation spaces induced by
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}
and
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta}
in
H^{r}_{\theta}
are equivalent. Since the operator
\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}^{\ast}_{\theta^{\prime}}-\mathcal{L}^{\ast}_{\theta}\right)\left(a_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)^{-1}
(and thus
(1+(\theta\mathcal{L}^{\ast}-\mathcal{L}^{\ast}_{\theta})(a_{\theta}-\theta{\mathcal{L}}^{*})^{-1})^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\;\geqslant\;0
) is bounded in
H^{r}_{\theta}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{r+\alpha}_{\theta}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(a_{\theta}-{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta}\right)^{\alpha/2}f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(1+\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}-{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta}\right)\left(a_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)^{-1}\right)^{\alpha/2}\left(a_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)^{\alpha/2}f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}

(A.25)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C\left\|\left(a_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)^{\alpha/2}f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}.

The inverse bound
\left\|\left(a_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)^{\alpha/2}f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C\|f\|_{H^{r+\alpha}_{\theta}}
follows from similar arguments.

We are now in condition to prove (
A.5
), applying
[
23
]
, Th. 1.4.3. Indeed,
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}
has a real spectrum smaller than
-\theta^{\prime}\min(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
on the the subspace of
H^{r}_{\theta}
generated by the eigenfunctions
\psi_{l,\theta^{\prime}}
with
l\neq 0
, so applying this Theorem we get, denoting
{\mathcal{P}}_{\theta^{\prime}}f=f-\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}fw_{\theta^{\prime}}}{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)^{2}}
the projection on this subspace (which is an element of
\mathcal{B}(H^{r}_{\theta})
for
0<\theta\;\leqslant\;\theta^{\prime}
),

\left\|e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\theta^{\prime}}f\right\|_{H^{r+\alpha}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C_{1}\left\|\left(a_{\theta}-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)^{\alpha/2}e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\theta^{\prime}}f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C_{2}t^{-\alpha/2}e^{-\lambda t}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}.

(A.26)

This implies
(
A.5
)
, since
e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}\left(1-{\mathcal{P}}_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)f=\left(1-{\mathcal{P}}_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)f

The proof of (
A.6
) relies on the classical identity, valid for

,

e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}f={\mathbb{E}}\left[f\left(e^{-t\theta^{\prime}K}x+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t\theta^{\prime}K}}G_{\theta^{\prime}}\right)\right],

(A.27)

where

a gaussian variable on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with mean
0

(\theta^{\prime}K)^{-1}\sigma^{2}
.
This implies directly, for

with

,

\nabla e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{N}}f=e^{-t\theta^{\prime}K}\,e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{N}}\nabla f,

(A.28)

and thus, denoting
\underline{K}=\min\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\}

\left\|\nabla e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{N}}f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;e^{-\theta^{\prime}\underline{K}t}\left\|e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{N}}\nabla f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C\left(1+t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\lambda t}\right)e^{-\theta^{\prime}\underline{K}t}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{H^{r-1}_{\theta}}\\
\;\leqslant\;C^{\prime}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\lambda t}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}.

(A.29)

For the proof of the third assertion, since
[
23
]
, Th. 1.4.3. implies
that for

,

\left\|\left(e^{s\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}-1\right)f\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{r}}\;\leqslant\;C_{\varepsilon}s^{\varepsilon}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{r+2\varepsilon}},

(A.30)

\left\|\left(e^{(t+s)\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}-e^{t\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}\right)f\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{r+1}}=\left\|\left(e^{s\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}-1\right)e^{t\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\theta^{\prime}}f\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{r+1}}\;\leqslant\;C_{\varepsilon}s^{\varepsilon}\left\|e^{t\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\theta^{\prime}}f\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{r+1+2\varepsilon}}\\
\;\leqslant\;Cs^{\varepsilon}t^{-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}e^{-\lambda t}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{r}}.

(A.31)

The last assertion is not a direct consequence of the estimates obtained above, since the hypothesis
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}fw_{\theta}=0
is not well adapted to the eigenfunctions
\psi_{l,\theta^{\prime}}
of
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}
. In particular, having

does not imply
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}fw_{\theta}=0
, while it is the case when
\theta=\theta^{\prime}
. We will only be able to obtain this estimates for
r
large enough, via direct calculations. Remark first that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}f-\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}fw_{\theta}}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w_{\theta}}\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}^{2}\\
=\left\langle{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}f-\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}fw_{\theta}}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w_{\theta}},e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}f-\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}fw_{\theta}}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w_{\theta}}\right\rangle_{H^{r}_{\theta}}.

(A.32)

Recalling that
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}a=0
and remarking that
\left\langle a,e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}f-\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}fw_{\theta}}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w_{\theta}}\right\rangle_{H^{r}_{\theta}}=0
for any constant
a
, we get

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}f-\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}fw_{\theta}}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w_{\theta}}\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}^{2}\\
=\left\langle{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\left(e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}f-\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}fw_{\theta}}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w_{\theta}}\right),e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}f-\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}fw_{\theta}}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w_{\theta}}\right\rangle_{H^{r}_{\theta}},

(A.33)

so the proof of the last assertion reduces to the study of
\langle{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}f,f\rangle_{H^{r}_{\theta}}
with
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}fw_{\theta}=0
. Now for
f
satisfying
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}fw_{\theta}=0
, with decomposition

, we get

\displaystyle-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}\left\langle{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}f,f\right\rangle_{H^{r}_{\theta}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\neq 0}(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})^{r}\lambda_{\theta,l}|f_{l}|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(1-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}\right)\left(\sum_{l\neq 0}(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}\sqrt{(l_{i}+1)(l_{i}+2)}f_{l}\bar{f}_{l_{\upuparrows_{i}}}\right).

(A.34)

Now remark that for the second term, using Cauchy Schwarz inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{l\neq 0}(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}\sqrt{(l_{i}+1)(l_{i}+2)}f_{l}\bar{f}_{l_{\upuparrows_{i}}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{l\neq 0}\left\{(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})^{r/2}\sqrt{\lambda_{\theta,l}}f_{l}\right\}\left\{\frac{\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)^{r/2}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\theta,l}}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}\sqrt{(l_{i}+1)(l_{i}+2)}\bar{f}_{l_{\upuparrows_{i}}}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left|\sum_{l\neq 0}(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})^{r}\lambda_{\theta,l}|f_{l}|^{2}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\sum_{l\neq 0}\frac{\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)^{r}}{\lambda_{\theta,l}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}\sqrt{(l_{i}+1)(l_{i}+2)}\bar{f}_{l_{\upuparrows_{i}}}\right)^{2}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using Jensen’s inequality

\left|\sum_{l\neq 0}\frac{\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)^{r}}{\lambda_{\theta,l}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}\sqrt{(l_{i}+1)(l_{i}+2)}\bar{f}_{l_{\upuparrows_{i}}}\right)^{2}\right|\\
\;\leqslant\;\sum_{l\neq 0}\frac{\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)^{r}}{\lambda_{\theta,l}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}(l_{i}+2)\right)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}(l_{i}+2)\left|f_{l_{\upuparrows_{i}}}\right|^{2},\\
=\sum_{l\neq 0}\frac{\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)^{r}}{\lambda_{\theta,l}}\left(2a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}(l_{i}+2)\left|f_{l_{\upuparrows_{i}}}\right|^{2},

Denoting by
\mathcal{N}_{i}:=\upuparrows_{i}\left(\mathbb{N}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right)=\left\{l\in\mathbb{N}^{d},l_{i}\;\geqslant\;2,\sum_{j=1}^{d}l_{j}\;\geqslant\;3\right\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{l\neq 0}\frac{\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)^{r}}{\lambda_{\theta,l}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}\sqrt{(l_{i}+1)(l_{i}+2)}\bar{f}_{l_{\upuparrows_{i}}}\right)^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\frac{\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l_{\downdownarrows_{i}}}\right)^{r}}{\lambda_{\theta,l_{\downdownarrows_{i}}}}\left(2a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l_{\downdownarrows_{i}}}\right)\theta k_{i}l_{i}\left|f_{l}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\frac{\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}-2\theta k_{i}\right)^{r}}{\lambda_{\theta,l}-2\theta k_{i}}\left(2a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}-2\theta k_{i}\right)\theta k_{i}l_{i}\left|f_{l}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}b_{\theta,l,i}\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)^{r}\theta k_{i}l_{i}\left|f_{l}\right|^{2},

where

b_{\theta,l,i}:=\frac{\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}-2\theta k_{i}\right)^{r}\left(2a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}-2\theta k_{i}\right)}{\left(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\right)^{r}(\lambda_{\theta,l}-2\theta k_{i})}=\left(1-\frac{2\theta k_{i}}{a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}}\right)^{r}\left(1+\frac{2a_{\theta}}{\lambda_{\theta,l}-2\theta k_{i}}\right).

(A.35)

Now, for
l\in\mathcal{N}_{i}
, we have

a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\;\leqslant\;d\theta k_{max}+\lambda_{\theta,l}\;\leqslant\;(d+1)\frac{k_{max}}{k_{min}}\lambda_{\theta,l},

(A.36)

and

\lambda_{\theta,l}-2\theta k_{i}\;\geqslant\;\theta\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}l_{j}-2\right)k_{min}\;\geqslant\;\frac{k_{min}}{3k_{max}}\lambda_{\theta,l},

(A.37)

so that

b_{\theta,l,i}\;\leqslant\;\left(1-\frac{2k_{min}^{2}}{(d+1)k_{max}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}k_{j}l_{j}}\right)^{r}\left(1+\frac{6d\,k^{2}_{max}}{k_{min}}\frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}k_{j}l_{j}}\right).

(A.38)

Now, observe that for
c_{1},c_{2}>0
,
x\mapsto\left(1-\frac{c_{1}}{x}\right)^{r}\left(1+\frac{c_{2}}{x}\right)
is strictly increasing with limit
1
as
x\to\infty
, provided that
r>\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}
.
Hence, taking
r
large enough in (
A.38
) (
r
depending only on
K
and
d
, not on
l
,
i
nor
\theta
) we have
|b_{\theta,l,i}|\;\leqslant\;1
, which means that the second term of the right-hand side of (
A.34
) is bounded as follows:

\left|\sum_{l\neq 0}(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\theta k_{i}\sqrt{(l_{i}+1)(l_{i}+2)}f_{l}\bar{f}_{l_{\upuparrows_{i}}}\right|\;\leqslant\;\sum_{l\neq 0}(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})^{r}\lambda_{\theta,l}|f_{l}|^{2}.

(A.39)

We deduce from (
A.34
) and this estimate that

\left|-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}\left\langle\mathcal{P}^{\perp}_{\theta}{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}_{\theta}f,\bar{f}\right\rangle_{H^{r}_{\theta}}-\sum_{l\neq 0}(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})^{r}\lambda_{\theta,l}|f_{l}|^{2}\right|\;\leqslant\;\left(1-\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}}\right)\sum_{l\neq 0}(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})^{r}\lambda_{\theta,l}|f_{l}|^{2},

(A.40)

which means that

-\mathrm{Re}\left\langle{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}f,\bar{f}\right\rangle_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\;\geqslant\;\sum_{l\neq 0}(a_{\theta}+\lambda_{\theta,l})^{r}\lambda_{\theta,l}|f_{l}|^{2}\;\geqslant\;\theta k_{min}\|f\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}^{2}.

(A.41)

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{(L^{2}_{\theta})^{\prime}\times L^{2}_{\theta}}
with
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
). Now, since
H^{r}_{\theta}\rightarrow L^{2}_{\theta}
is dense, we have a dense injection
(L^{2}_{\theta})^{\prime}\rightarrow H^{-r}_{\theta}
. With the identification
(L^{2}_{\theta})^{\prime}\approx L^{2}_{-\theta}
, we obtain, for all
u\in L^{2}_{-\theta}\subset H^{-r}_{\theta}
and all
f\in H^{r}_{\theta}
,

\langle u,f\rangle_{H^{-r}_{\theta}\times H^{r}_{\theta}}=\langle u,f\rangle.

Remark in particular that if
u\in L^{2}_{-\theta}
, then for all
f\in H^{r+1}_{\theta}
we have

\left|\langle\partial_{x_{i}}u,f\rangle\right|=\left|-\langle u,\partial_{x_{i}}f\rangle\right|\;\leqslant\;C\|u\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}\|f\|_{H^{r+1}_{\theta}},

(A.42)

so that if
u\in H^{-r}_{\theta}
then
\nabla u\in H^{-(r+1)}_{\theta}
with

\|\nabla u\|_{H^{-(r+1)}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C\|u\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}.

(A.43)

With this structure, since
L^{2}_{\theta}
is reflexive the closure of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}
, seen as an operator on
(L^{2}_{\theta})^{\prime}
, is the adjoint of
{\mathcal{L}}^{\ast}_{\theta^{\prime}}
(
[
25
]
, Th. 5.29) and is thus sectorial and defines an analytical semi-group
e^{t{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}}
in
H^{-r}_{\theta}
. In the same way, since
H^{r}_{\theta}
is reflexive, the adjoint of
e^{t{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}^{\ast}}
seen as an operator on
H^{r}_{\theta}
is
e^{t{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}}
seen as an operator on
H^{-r}_{\theta}
(
[
32
]
, Cor. 10.6).

From Proposition
A.2
and the structure described above we deduce directly the following estimates for the semi-group induced by
{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}
(recall (
1.20
)) in
H^{-r}_{\theta}
and
t>0
.

Proposition A.3
.

For all
0<\theta\;\leqslant\;\theta^{\prime}
the operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}
is sectorial and generates an analytical semi-group in
H^{-r}_{\theta}
. Moreover we have the following estimates: for any
r\;\geqslant\;0
,
\alpha\;\geqslant\;0
and
\lambda<\theta\min(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
u\in H_{\theta}^{-(r+\alpha)}
,

\left\|e^{t{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}}u\right\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C\left(1+t^{-\alpha/2}e^{-\lambda t}\right)\|u\|_{H^{-(r+\alpha)}_{\theta}},

(A.44)

and for all
r\;\geqslant\;1
,

\left\|e^{t{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}}\nabla u\right\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\lambda t}\|u\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}\,.

(A.45)

Moreover for all
r\;\geqslant\;0
,
0<\varepsilon\;\leqslant\;1
and
s\;\geqslant\;0
,

\left\|\left(e^{(t+s)\mathcal{L}_{\theta^{\prime}}}-e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\theta^{\prime}}}\right)u\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-r}}\;\leqslant\;Cs^{\varepsilon}t^{-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}e^{-\lambda t}\left\|u\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-(r+1)}}.

(A.46)

Finally, there exist
r_{0}>0
,
C>0
such that for any
0<\theta\;\leqslant\;\theta^{\prime}
, for all
r>r_{0}
,
t>0
and all
u\in H^{-r}_{\theta}
satisfying
\int u=0
,

\left\|e^{t{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}}u\right\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;Ce^{-\lambda t}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}.

(A.47)

Proof of Proposition
A.3
.

The spectral structure of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}
follows directly from the one of
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}
. To prove the first estimate of the proposition it is now sufficient to remark that for all
f\in H^{r}_{\theta},u\in L^{2}_{-\theta}
,

\left|\langle e^{t{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}}u,f\rangle\right|=\left|\langle u,e^{t{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}^{\ast}}f\rangle\right|\;\leqslant\;C\left(1+t^{-\alpha/2}e^{-\lambda t}\right)\|f\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\|u\|_{H^{-(r+\alpha)}_{\theta}}.

For the second point,

\left|\langle e^{t{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}}\nabla u,f\rangle\right|=\left|\langle u,\nabla e^{t{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}^{\ast}}f\rangle\right|\;\leqslant\;Ct^{-\alpha/2}e^{-\lambda t}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\|u\|_{H^{-(r+\alpha)}_{\theta}}.

The third point follows from similar estimates. For the last point, remark that
if
\left\langle u\,,\,1\right\rangle=0
,

\left|\langle e^{t{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\prime}}}u,f\rangle\right|=\left|\left\langle u,e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}f\right\rangle\right|=\left|\left\langle u,e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}f-\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{t{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}}fw_{\theta}}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w_{\theta}}\right\rangle\right|\\
\;\leqslant\;Ct^{-\alpha/2}e^{-\lambda t}\left\|f-\int fw_{\theta}\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\|u\|_{H^{-(r+\alpha)}_{\theta}},

(A.48)

and
\left\|f-\int fw_{\theta}\right\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;2\|f\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}
.

∎

Appendix B
Grönwall Lemma

Lemma B.1
.

Let
t\mapsto y_{t}
be a nonnegative and continuous function on
[0,T)

satisfying, for all
t\in[0,T)
and some positive constants
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
,

y_{t}\;\leqslant\;c_{0}+c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-s}}\right)y_{s}\,\text{\rm d}s.

(B.1)

Then for all
t\in[0,T)
,
y_{t}\;\leqslant\;2c_{0}e^{\alpha t}
with
\alpha=2c_{1}+4c_{1}^{2}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)^{2}
, where
\Gamma
is the usual special function
\Gamma(r)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{r-1}e^{-x}dx
.

For the proof of this Lemma, see
[
20
]
, Lemma 5.2.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
a,b,\lambda>0
and
\phi
a nonnegative measurable function on
[0,+\infty)
such that
\phi
is integrable on
[0,+\infty)
. Suppose that
t\;\geqslant\;0\mapsto u_{t}
is a nonnegative function satisfying

u_{t}\;\leqslant\;a+b\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+\phi(t-s)\right)e^{-\lambda s}u_{s}{\rm d}s.

(B.2)

Then, there exists some constant
C(b,\phi)>0
such that

\sup_{t\;\geqslant\;0}u_{t}\;\leqslant\;2a\exp\left(\frac{C(b,\phi)}{\lambda}\right).

(B.3)

Proof of Lemma
B.2
.

Define
A=A(b,\phi)\;\geqslant\;0
such that
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\phi(u)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\phi(u)\;\geqslant\;A\right\}}{\rm d}u\;\leqslant\;\frac{1}{2b}
. Then, for all
v\;\leqslant\;t

\displaystyle u_{v}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;a+b\int_{0}^{v}e^{-\lambda s}u_{s}{\rm d}s+b\int_{0}^{v}\phi(v-s)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\phi(v-s)\;\geqslant\;A\right\}}e^{-\lambda s}u_{s}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\quad+b\int_{0}^{v}\phi(v-s)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\phi(v-s)\;\leqslant\;A\right\}}e^{-\lambda s}u_{s}{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;a+b\int_{0}^{v}e^{-\lambda s}u_{s}{\rm d}s+b\sup_{s\;\leqslant\;v}u_{s}\int_{0}^{v}\phi(v-s)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\phi(v-s)\;\geqslant\;A\right\}}{\rm d}s+bA\int_{0}^{v}e^{-\lambda s}u_{s}{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;a+b\left(1+A\right)\int_{0}^{v}e^{-\lambda s}u_{s}{\rm d}s+\frac{1}{2}u^{\ast}_{v}\;\leqslant\;a+b\left(1+A\right)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}u^{\ast}_{s}{\rm d}s+\frac{1}{2}u^{\ast}_{t},

where we have defined
u^{\ast}(s):=\sup_{r\;\leqslant\;s}u_{r}
. Since the last inequality is true for all
v\;\leqslant\;t
, we get

\displaystyle u^{\ast}_{t}\;\leqslant\;2a+2b\left(1+A\right)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}u^{\ast}_{s}{\rm d}s.

The usual Grönwall lemma applied to
t\mapsto u^{\ast}_{t}
gives the conclusion, for
C(b,\phi)=2b(1+A(b,\phi))
.
∎

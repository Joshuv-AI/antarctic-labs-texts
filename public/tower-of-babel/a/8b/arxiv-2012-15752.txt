Some solutions of functional equation I ⁡ ( I ⁡ ( y , x ) , I ⁡ ( x , y ) ) = I ⁡ ( x , y ) I(I(y,x),I(x,y))=I(x,y) involving fuzzy implications
Nageswara Rao Vemuri

Abstract
In this article, a functional equation(IE) involving fuzzy implications has been considered. Two different perspectives of this equation have been provided to realize its significance. As it is very difficult to find the solutions of (IE) in general, the investigation of solutions of (IE) is restricted to main families of fuzzy implications.



1
Introduction

Fuzzy implications are a generalization of classical implication
\rightarrow
from
\{0,1\}
setting to the multivalued(MV) setting
[0,1]
.
They are defined as follows.

Definition 1.1

(
[
1
]
, Definition 1.1.1 &
[
3
,
2
]
)
.

A function
I\colon[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]
is called a
fuzzy implication
if it satisfies, for all
x,x_{1},x_{2},y,y_{1},y_{2}\in[0,1]
, the following conditions:

\displaystyle\text{if }x_{1}\leq x_{2},\text{ then }I(x_{1},y)\geq I(x_{2},y)\;,\text{ i.e., }I(\>\cdot\>,y)\text{ is decreasing\;,}

(I1)

\displaystyle\text{if }y_{1}\leq y_{2},\text{ then }I(x,y_{1})\leq I(x,y_{2})\;,\text{ i.e., }I(x,\>\cdot\>)\text{ is increasing\;,}

(I2)

\displaystyle I(0,0)=1\;,I(1,1)=1\ ,I(1,0)=0\ .

(I3)

The set of fuzzy implications will be denoted by
\mathbb{I}
.

Fuzzy implications play a significant role in the areas like, artificial intelligence, machine learning, decison making etc. The suitability of a fuzzy implication for a particular application/ problem will be decided by the properties or functional equations that the particular fuzzy implication satisfies.

Recall that the properties or functional equations of fuzzy implications are a mere generalization of classical tautologies to MV-setting and they involve some other fuzzy logic connectives also. However, functional equations of fuzzy implications are not only a generalizations of classical tautologies but may have also some mathematical perspectives. Thus the investigations of solutions of functional equations of fuzzy implications not only give fuzzy implications suitable for a specific application but also lead to glean mathematical aspects of the set of fuzzy implications.

Unfortunately, only few works have done on the investigations of solutions of functional equations of fuzzy implications so far, please see,
[
5
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
6
]
.

In this article, we consider the following functional equation, for a fuzzy implication
I
,

I(I(y,x),I(x,y))=I(x,y)

(IE)

1.1
IE: Interpretation

The functional equation (
IE
) has the following perspectives.

(A). Logical Perspective:

In the classical logic, the formula

((q\rightarrow p)\rightarrow(p\rightarrow q))\equiv(p\rightarrow q)

where
\rightarrow
is a classical implication, is always a tautology. This tautology, when generalized to fuzzy logic, it can be represented by

I_{1}(I_{2}(y,x),I_{3}(x,y))=I_{4}(x,y)

(GFE)

where
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
and
I_{4}
are fuzzy implications defined on
[0,1]
and need not be the same always.
Note that it is never possible to find fuzzy implications
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
and
I_{4}
satisfying (
IE
) simultaneously. Due to this reason, in this article, we restrict ourselves to the case of
I_{1}=I_{2}=I_{3}=I_{4}
, which gives us (
GFE
).

(B). Algebraic Perspective:

In
[
7
]
, Vemuri and Jayaram proposed a generating method
\triangledown
of fuzzy implications as follows.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
I,J\in\mathbb{I}
. Define the function
(I\triangledown J)
as

(I\triangledown J)(x,y)=I(J(y,x),J(x,y)),\qquad x,y\in[0,1]\;.

Further, they have shown that
(I\triangledown J)
is always a fuzzy implication and also, the pair
(\mathbb{I},\triangledown)
is a semigroup. The set of idempotent elements of the semigroup
(\mathbb{I},\triangledown)
is exactly the collection of fuzzy implications satisfying the functional equation (
IE
). It is already known that the set of idempotent elements of a semigroup will glean more properties of that semigroup.

Due to the perspectives of (
IE
), it is always useful to investigate the solutions of (
IE
).

Definition 1.3

(cf.

.

1.

A fuzzy implication
I
is said to satisfy

(a)

the left neutrality property (

\displaystyle I(1,y)=y,\qquad y\in[0,1]\;.

(NP)

(b)

(IP)

(d)

\displaystyle I(x,I(y,z))=I(y,I(x,z)),\qquad x,y,z\in[0,1]\;.

(EP)

2.

2
Some solutions of (
IE
)

Due to the variety of fuzzy implications, it is very difficult to find the fuzzy implications that satisfy (
IE
). Hence, in this article, we restrict the solutions of (
IE
) to major families of fuzzy implications only. The more details about the t-norms, t-conorms and fuzzy negations can be found in
[
4
]
and
[
1
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
I
has (OP). Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

I
satisfies the equation (
IE
).

(ii)

I
has (NP) on its range.

Proof.

(i)

\Rightarrow(ii)
Let
I
has (OP) and satisfies the equation (
IE
). Let
\alpha\in range\ of\ I.
Then there exist
x_{0},y_{0}\in[0,1]
such that
I(x_{0},y_{0})=\alpha
.
If
\alpha=1,
then it is clear. Let
\alpha<1.
Since
I
has (OP) and
\alpha<1,
we have
x_{0}>y_{0}
and
I(y_{0},x_{0})=1
.
Now
I(1,\alpha)=I(I(y_{0},x_{0}),I(x_{0},y_{0}))=I(x_{0},y_{0})=\alpha.
Thus
I
has (NP) on the range.

(ii)

\Rightarrow(i)
Let
I
has (OP) and has (NP) on the range. If
x\leq y,
then
I(x,y)=1
and
I(I(y,x),I(x,y))=I(I(y,x),1)=1.
On the other hand let
x>y
. Then

I(I(y,x),I(x,y))=I(1,I(x,y))=I(x,y)

by our assumption. Thus
I
satisfies the equation (
IE
).

∎

2.1
R-implications and the equation (
IE
).

Definition 2.2

(
[
1
]
, Definition 2.5.1)
.

A function
I:[0,1]^{2}\longrightarrow[0,1]
is called an
R- implication
if there exists a t-norm
T
such that

I(x,y)=\sup\{t\in[0,1]|T(x,t)\leq y\},\qquad\ x,y\in[0,1].

If
I
is an
R
-implication generated from a t-norm
T
, then it is denoted by
I_{T}
and
I_{T}\in\mathbb{I}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
I
be an R-implication generated from a t-norm. Then
I
satisfies the equation (
IE
).

Proof.

Let
I
be an R-implication generated from a t-norm. Then
I
has both (IP) and (NP). Since
I
has (IP), for
x\leq y
, we have
I(x,y)=1
. So, on the one hand if
x\leq y
, we have
I(x,y)=1
and on the other hand
I(I(y,x),I(x,y))=I(I(y,x),1)=1.
When
x>y
, again we have
I(I(y,x),I(x,y))=I(1,I(x,y))=I(x,y)
by using (NP) of
I
. Thus it is shown that every R-implication satisfies the equation (
IE
).
∎

(S,N)-implications and the equation (

Definition 2.4

(
[
1
]
, Definition 2.4.1)

A function
I:[0,1]^{2}\longrightarrow[0,1]
is called an
(S,N)-implication
if there exist a t-conorm
S

N

I(x,y)=S(N(x),y),\qquad\ x,y\in[0,1].

(1)

If
I
is an
(S,N)
-implication then we will often denote it by

-implications will be denoted by

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
I
be an (S,N)-implication defined from a t-conorm
S
and a fuzzy negation
N
. Then in the following cases
I
satisfies the equation (
IE
).

(i)

S=\max

(ii)

S=S_{D}

(iii)

The pair
(S,N)
satisfies
S(N(x),x)=1
, for all
x\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Let
I
be an (S,N)-implication defined from a t-conorm
S
and a fuzzy negation
N
.

(i)

Let
S(x,y)=\max(x,y)
, i.e.,
S
is maximum t-conorm. Then
I
is given by
I(x,y)=\max(N(x),y)
, for all
x,y\in[0,1].

Let
x,y\in[0,1]
be fixed arbitrarily. Then we have two cases:
x\leq N(y),x\geq N(y).

(a)

Let
x\leq N(y).
Then
N(x)\geq N(N(y)).
Now,

\displaystyle I(I(y,x),I(x,y))

\displaystyle=I(\max(N(y),x),\max(N(x),y))

\displaystyle=I(N(y),\max(N(x),y))

\displaystyle=\max(N(N(y)),\max(N(x),y))

\displaystyle=\max(\max(N(x),N(N(y))),y)

\displaystyle=\max(N(x),y)=I(x,y)

Thus in this case
I
sayisfies the equation (
IE
).

(b)

Let
x\geq N(y).
Now,

\displaystyle I(I(y,x),I(x,y))

\displaystyle=I(\max(N(y),x),\max(N(x),y))

\displaystyle=I(x,\max(N(x),y))

\displaystyle=\max(N(x),\max(N(x),y))

\displaystyle=\max(N(x),y)=I(x,y)

Thus in this case also
I
satisfies the equation (
IE
).

(ii)

Let
S=S_{D}
, the drastic t-conorm. Then
I
can be given by

I(x,y)=\begin{cases}y,&\text{if $x=1$}\\
N(x),&\text{if $y=0$}\\
1,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Now, it is very easy to check that this (S,N)-implication
I
satisfies the equation (
IE
).

(iii)

Assume that the pair
(S,N)
satisfies
S(N(x),x)=1
, for all
x\in[0,1]
. Then the
(S,N)
-implication satisfies (
IP
). Since, every
(S,N)
-implication satisfies (
NP
), it follows easily that
(S,N)
-implication satisfies (
IE
).

∎

Theorem 2.10
.

No
f

I
satisfies the equation (
IE
).

Proof.

Let
I
be an f-implication generated from a strictly decreasing function
f:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,\infty]
s.t
f(1)=0
. Now the equation
I(I(y,x),I(x,y))=I(x,y)
has the following form

f^{-1}\left(f^{-1}(y\cdot f(x))\cdot x\cdot f(y)\right)=f^{-1}(x\cdot f(y)),\forall x,y\in[0,1].

Let
x,y\notin\{0,1\}.
Then we have

f^{-1}(y\cdot f(x))\cdot x\cdot f(y)=x\cdot f(y)

yf(x)=f(1)=0

i.e,either\ y=0\ or\ f(x)=0.

i.e,either\ y=0\ or\ x=1.

This is a contradiction.
∎

Definition 2.11

(
[
1
]
, Definition 3.2.1)
.

Let
g:[0,1]\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
be a strictly increasing and continuous function with
g(0)=0.
The function
I:[0,1]^{2}\rightarrow[0,1]
defined by

I(x,y)=g^{(-1)}\left(\frac{1}{x}\cdot g(y)\right),\qquad x,y\in[0,1]\ ,

with the understanding
\frac{1}{0}=\infty
and
\infty\cdot 0=\infty

g-generated implication
, where the function

is the pseudo inverse of

g^{(-1)}(x)=\begin{cases}g^{-1}(x),&\text{if $x\in[0,\ g(1)]$}\ ,\\
1,&\text{if $x\in[g(1),\ \infty]$}\ .\end{cases}

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
I
be a g-generated implication. If
I
satisfies the equation (
IE
) then
g(1)<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
I
be a g-generated implication. Let us assume that
I
satisfies the equation (
IE
). Suppose that
g(1)=\infty
. i.e.,
g^{(-1)}=g^{-1}
.

Let
x,y\in(0,1)
. Now the equation
I(I(y,x),I(x,y))=I(x,y)
will imply

g^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{g^{-1}(\frac{1}{y}\cdot g(x))}\cdot\frac{1}{x}\cdot g(y)\right)=g^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\cdot g(y)\right)

i.e.,\frac{1}{g^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{y}\cdot g(x)\right)}\cdot\frac{1}{x}\cdot g(y)=\frac{1}{x}\cdot g(y)

i.e.,g(1)=\infty=\frac{1}{y}\cdot g(x)

i.e.,either\ g(x)=\infty\ or\ y=0

i.e.,either\ x=1\ or\ y=0

which is a contradiction. Thus
g(1)<\infty
.
∎

3
Conclusion

The functional equation (IE) has both theoretical and applicational significance in many areas. keeping the significance of this equation, here in this work, we have taken the task of finding the solutions of (IE). Interestingly, while every R-implication satisfies (IE), it is found out that no f-implication satisfies the equation (IE). However, application of fuzzy implications satisfying (IE) are yet to be done.

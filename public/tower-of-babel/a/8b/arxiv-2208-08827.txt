Characteristic Polynomials of Orthogonal and Symplectic Random Matrices, Jacobi Ensembles & L-functions
MUSTAFA ALPER GUNES

Abstract
Starting from Montgomery’s conjecture, there has been a substantial interest on the connections of random matrix theory and the theory of L-functions. In particular, moments of characteristic polynomials of random matrices have been considered in various works to estimate the asymptotics of moments of L-function families. In this paper, we first consider joint moments of the characteristic polynomial of a symplectic random matrix and its second derivative. We obtain the asymptotics, along with a representation of the leading order coefficient in terms of the solution of a Painlevé equation. This gives us the conjectural asymptotics of the corresponding joint moments over families of Dirichlet L-functions. In doing so, we compute the asymptotics of a certain additive Jacobi statistic, which could be of independent interest in random matrix theory. Finally, we consider a slightly different type of joint moment that is the analogue of an average considered over U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) in various works before. We obtain the asymptotics and the leading order coefficient explicitly.

Motivation

Let
U
be a matrix in
SO(2N)
or
Sp(2N)
sampled according to the Haar measure with eigenvalues
e^{\pm i\theta_{1}},\ldots,e^{\pm i\theta_{N}}
and define the functions,

\psi_{U}(\theta)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-e^{-i\left(\theta-\theta_{j}\right)}\right)\left(1-e^{-i\left(\theta+\theta_{j}\right)}\right),

and

\mathcal{Z}_{U}(\theta)=e^{\frac{iN}{2}(\theta+\pi)-i\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-e^{-i\left(\theta-\theta_{j}\right)}\right).

Note that
\psi_{U}
is the characteristic polynomial of
U
, whereas
\mathcal{Z}_{U}
is the so-called
\mathcal{Z}
-function corresponding to the eigenvalues on the upper part of the unit circle. In this paper, we will compute the
N\to\infty
asymptotics of the corresponding quantities:

\mathfrak{R}_{N}^{G}(s,h)=\mathbb{E}_{N}\left[\left|\mathcal{Z}_{U}(0)\right|^{2s}\left|\frac{d}{d\theta}\log\mathcal{Z}_{U}(\theta)\bigg\rvert_{\theta=0}\right|^{h}\right],

(1)

and

\mathfrak{L}_{N}^{G}(s,h)=\mathbb{E}_{N}\left[\left|\psi_{U}(0)\right|^{2s-h}\left|\psi^{\prime\prime}_{U}(0)\right|^{h}\right],

(2)

where
G
is one of the matrix groups
SO(2N)
or
Sp(2N)
, and the averages are taken with respect to the Haar measures on each group.

While
\mathfrak{R}_{N}^{G}(s,h)
can be seen as an analogue (notwithstanding certain differences) of a quantity that has been considered as an average over
U(N)
in various works before due to its connections with the Riemann
\zeta
-function, (see
[
20
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
11
]
), results obtained in this paper regarding the asymptotics of
\mathfrak{L}_{N}^{G}(s,h)
give rise to conjectures about moments of families of L-functions.

1.1

Random Matrix Theory & L-functions

Starting from a conjecture due to Montgomery
[
16
]
, there has been a substantial amount of work done on the connections between random matrix theory and analytic number theory. Montgomery’s conjecture, which was based on both rigorous calculations and certain conjectures on twin primes, stated that the pair correlation function of the zeros of the Riemann Zeta function, high up the critical line, could be modeled via the pair correlation function of eigenangles of a random unitary matrix of large size. Later, it was proved by Keating and Snaith
[
15
]
, that the value distribution of
\zeta(s)
coincided with the distribution of the characteristic polynomial of a random unitary matrix in the same limit. In
[
13
]
, as an attempt to generalize this connection to other classes of L-functions, Katz and Sarnak considered various statistics related to Haar-distributed random matrices from classical compact groups,
U(N)
,
Sp(2N)
and
SO(2N)
. They showed that in the
N\to\infty
limit, certain statistics related to consecutive spacings of eigenangles all converge to the same limit, that of the Circular Unitary Ensemble (CUE). On the other hand, they realized that this universality does not hold if one considers the distribution of the
k
-th eigenvalue over these compact groups. Namely, let
G
be one of the groups,
U(N),Sp(2N)
or
O(N)
and let the eigenvalues of a matrix in these groups be ordered around the unit circle (starting from
1
and going anti-clockwise). It is proved in
[
13
]
that the averages

\nu_{k}(G)[\alpha,\eta]:=\mu_{\textbf{Haar}}\left\{A\in G:\alpha\leq\frac{\theta_{k}(A)N}{2\pi}\leq\eta\right\},

as
N\to\infty
, converge to different quantities corresponding to the symmetry type of the matrix group.

To translate this to the language of L-functions, consider a family of L-functions
\mathcal{L}
where the L-functions are ordered by their conductors
c(L)
(as we will see later on, they may be ordered by various other types of quantities, for instance cusp form L-functions are ordered by their weight).

If one assumes the Riemann Hypothesis for these L-functions, then all the zeros lie on the critical line (we will assume that the critical line is always
\Re(s)=\frac{1}{2}
) and the ones with non-negative imaginary part can be written as:

\left\{\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty},

where
\gamma_{n}
’s are in increasing order.
Katz and Sarnak provide strong evidence that analogously to the random matrix theory average, as
N\to\infty
, the quantity

\frac{\left|\left\{L\in\mathcal{L},c(L)\leq N:\alpha\leq\frac{\gamma_{L,j}\log c(L)}{2\pi}\leq\eta\right\}\right|}{\left|\left\{L\in\mathcal{L},c(L)\leq N\right\}\right|},

converges where
\gamma_{L,j}
denotes the
j
-th zero on the line
\Re(s)=\frac{1}{2}
. The factor
\log c(L)
appears here due to the density of the zeros. The limiting quantity, as they conjecture, depends on the
symmetry type
of the L-function family, which, in most cases, is difficult to determine.

Due to the strong evidence that the zero statistics near
s=\frac{1}{2}
over families of L-functions can be modeled by one of the compact random matrix groups, it becomes natural to wonder whether one can obtain conjectural asymptotics for a sum of the form

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{L}_{N}|}\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}_{N}}L\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{m},

as
N\to\infty
, where
\mathcal{L}
is an L-function family ordered by some quantity
N
, and
\mathcal{L}_{N}
is the subset of the family determined by
N
. Conrey and Farmer
[
8
]
provide evidence that asymptotically these averages are also determined by the symmetry type of the family. Hence, one would expect that these asymptotics agree with asymptotics of moments of characteristic polynomials of random matrices from the corresponding matrix group. Keating and Snaith give conjectures in this direction in
[
14
]
. Thus, we expect that the asymptotics we will compute give rise to conjectures regarding families of orthogonal and symplectic L-functions. Here note that we consider averages over
SO(2N)
since L-functions with orthogonal symmetry either exhibit odd or even symmetry and in the odd case, the function vanishes at
s=\frac{1}{2}
whereas for even symmetry we expect that the statistics related to the zeros near
s=\frac{1}{2}
exhibit behaviour similar to eigenangles of
SO(2N)
(see
[
13
]
for any details).

Main Results

For technical reasons that appear in the proofs, we are able to prove our main results for a restricted parameter range (which still covers most of the range for which these moments exist). Thus, for ease of notation, if

Let

where for
a\geq b
,
a,b>0

7

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tau_{a,b}(0)=0,&\text{for }\ a>0,\\
&\\
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\tau_{a,b}(t)\right|_{t=0}=-\frac{1}{4a},&\text{for}\ a>1.\end{cases}

(3)

Remark 1.2
.

We believe that the random variable

in fact only depends on

At the centre of our proofs will be the Jacobi ensemble, which we define now.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
a,b>-1
. Define the probability measure, supported on
[0,1]^{N}
:

\mu_{N}^{(a,b)}(d\mathbf{x})=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{\Gamma(a+b+N+j+1)}{\Gamma(a+j+1)\Gamma(b+j+1)\Gamma(j+2)}\prod_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{a}\left(1-x_{j}\right)^{b}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}\left(x_{k}-x_{j}\right)^{2}d\mathbf{x}.

(4)

This is called the Jacobi ensemble corresponding to parameters
a,b
. Similarly, for a measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, we denote

\mathbb{E}_{N}^{(a,b)}[f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(\mathbf{x})\mu_{N}^{(a,b)}(d\mathbf{x}).

In section 2, we will start by writing
\mathfrak{L}_{N}^{Sp}
in terms of the moments of a linear Jacobi statistic. We will show that all derivatives of the Laplace transform of this statistic converge to those of a limiting random variable. In essence, the transformation to the Jacobi ensemble should work for
SO(2N)
as well, but the Riemann-Hilbert methods used in
[
6

require an extra restriction on the parameters of the Jacobi ensemble. That is, it is crucial for the arguments in this paper that the Laplace transform of
\{M_{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
converges pointwise to some limiting function. However, for the Jacobi parameters that correspond to
SO(2N)
, this does not follow from the results of
[
6
]
. Finally, we also note that the analogous linear statistic corresponding to the Laguerre unitary ensemble has been studied in various works before (see
[
7
]
),
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
).

This result, following the discussion in the previous section, gives rise to the following conjecture about joint moments of Dirichlet L-functions. These type of joint moments have been studied in the past both in random matrix theory and number theory settings (see, for instance,
[
11
]
,
[
17
]
). Here we denote by
L(s,\chi_{d})
, the L-function corresponding to a Dirichlet character modulo
d

\chi_{d}
.

Conjecture 1.4
.

Let
s+\frac{1}{2}>h\geq 0

N\to\infty

\frac{1}{N^{*}}\sum\left|L\left(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{d}\right)\right|^{2s-h}\left|L^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{d}\right)\right|^{h}\sim\log^{s(s+1)/2+2h}(N^{1/2})\\

where for the sum is taken over all quadratic Dirichlet characters with modulus less than or equal to

Using a similar method, that is switching to the Jacobi ensemble, one can also obtain the asymptotics of

For

\frac{d^{2}\psi_{U}}{d\theta^{2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\{\psi^{\prime\prime}_{j}(\theta)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\

\mathfrak{L}_{N}^{Sp}(s,h)=\mathfrak{L}_{N}^{Sp}(s,0)\mathbb{E}_{N}^{\left(s+\frac{1}{2},+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2x_{j}}+N^{2}\right)^{h}\right].

(5)

\mathfrak{L}_{N}^{Sp}(s,0)\sim N^{s(s+1)/2}\frac{2^{\frac{s^{2}}{2}}G(1+s)\sqrt{\Gamma(1+s)}}{\sqrt{G(1+2s)}\Gamma(1+2s)}.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
a\geq b
,
a,b>0
. If
\left(x_{1}^{(N)},\ldots,x_{N}^{(N)}\right)
denote points sampled according to
\mu_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left(d\bm{x}\right)
, there exists a non-negative (and non-trivial) random variable
M(a,b)
such that

M_{N}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{N^{2}x_{j}^{(N)}}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{d}M(a,b),

(7)

and for all
h\in[0,\alpha\left(a+1)\right)

\mathbb{E}_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left[\left(\frac{M_{N}}{2}+1\right)^{h}\right]\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{M(a,b)}{2}+1\right)^{h}\right].

Furthermore, the ’tau’ function corresponding to the random variable
M(a,b)
,

\tau_{a,b}(t):=t\frac{d}{dt}\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-tM(a,b)}\right],

solves the following form of the
\sigma
-Painlevé-III equation:

\left(t\frac{d^{2}\tau_{a,b}(t)}{dt^{2}}\right)^{2}+4\left(\frac{d\tau_{a,b}}{dt}\right)^{2}\left(t\frac{d\tau_{a,b}}{dt}-\tau_{a,b}(t)\right)-\left(a\frac{d\tau_{a,b}}{dt}+1\right)^{2}=0.

To prove this result, we will first prove convergence in distribution, and then show that the
h
-th moments are uniformly integrable so that convergence of
L^{h}
norms also takes place. In order to do this, we first focus on
h\in\mathbb{N}
. Throughout the rest of this section, whenever we say
M_{N}

sampled according to
\mu_{N}^{(a,b)}
, we will mean
M_{N}
is defined as in (
7
) where
(x_{1}^{(N)},\ldots,x_{N}^{(N)})
has the law

\mu_{N}^{(a,b)}
. Now, notice that one may expand:

\mathbb{E}_{N}^{(a,b)}\left[M_{N}^{h}\right]=\frac{1}{N^{2h}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{l}=h\\
\lambda_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{l}>0\end{subarray}}\binom{h}{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{l}}\kappa_{N}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{l})\mathbb{E}_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{l}x_{j}^{-\lambda_{j}}\right],

(8)

where

\kappa_{N}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{l})=\frac{N!}{(N-l)!n_{1}!\ldots n_{m}!}.

Here,
m
is the number of distinct integers occurring in the partition
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{l})
and
n_{k}
is the number of times
k
-th integer occurs. Notice that to obtain this sum, we expanded the
h
-th power as a multinomial and used the symmetry of the Jacobi ensemble under permutations of indices.

Using standard arguments from random matrix theory (see, for instance,
[
9
]
), one can show that the
l
-point density of the Jacobi ensemble in (
4
) is given by:

\rho_{N}^{(a,b)}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l}\right)=\frac{(N-l)!}{N!}\det\left[K_{N}^{(a,b)}(x_{i},x_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{l}.

(9)

The so-called
correlation kernel
,
K_{N}(x,y)
, appearing in the determinant is given by:

K_{N}^{(a,b)}(x,y)=\sqrt{w^{(a,b)}(x)w^{(a,b)}(y)}\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j}^{(a,b)}(x)P_{j}^{(a,b)}(y),

where

w^{(a,b)}(x)=x^{a}(1-x)^{b},

and
\left\{P_{j}^{(a,b)}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is the sequence of orthonormal Jacobi polynomials corresponding to parameters
a,b
. To be more precise, these are polynomials indexed by their degree that satisfy the orthonormality condition:

\int_{0}^{1}P_{j}^{(a,b)}(x)P_{k}^{(a,b)}(x)w^{(a,b)}(x)dx=\delta_{jk}.

In particular, by studying the asymptotics of fixed-dimensional integrals of the correlation kernel, one should be able to obtain the asymptotics of moments of
M_{N}(a,b)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
a\geq b\geq-\frac{1}{2}
,
h\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<h<a+1
, and
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{l})
be a partition of
h
. Then;

\kappa_{N}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{l})\mathbb{E}_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{l}x_{j}^{-\lambda_{j}}\right]=O\left(N^{2h}\right).

Proof.

Using (
9
), (from here on the constant may change from line to line)

\kappa_{N}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{l})\mathbb{E}_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{l}x_{j}^{-\lambda_{j}}\right]\leq\text{const }\int_{[0,1]^{l}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}x_{j}^{-\lambda_{j}}\det\left[K_{N}^{(a,b)}(x_{i},x_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{l}dx_{1}\ldots dx_{l}.

(10)

Now exapnding the determinant as a finite sum over the
l
-th symmetric group, and using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to note

\left|K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x,y)\right|\leq\sqrt{K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x,x)}\sqrt{K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}(y,y)},

(11)

we can bound the integral in (
10
) by:

\text{const}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{l}\int_{0}^{1}x^{-\lambda_{j}}K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x,x)dx.

We claim, for
\lambda\geq 1
, that

\int_{0}^{1}x^{-\lambda}K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x,x)dx=O\left(N^{2\lambda}\right).

(12)

First, note that we may restrict the integration to
(0,\frac{1}{2})
since

\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}x^{-\lambda}K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x,x)dx\leq 2^{h}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}K_{N}^{(a,b)}(x,x)dx=2^{h}N\mathbb{P}\left(x_{1}\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)\right)=O(N^{2\lambda}).

Now, making the substitution
x=\frac{u}{N^{2}}
, the integral over
\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
becomes:

N^{2\lambda}\int_{0}^{N^{2}/2}u^{-\lambda}\frac{1}{N^{2}}K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left(\frac{u}{N^{2}},\frac{u}{N^{2}}\right)du.

On
(0,1)
, we use Szegö’s well-known bound (see
[
19
]
):

\sup_{x\in[0,1]}P_{j}^{2}(x)\leq\text{const}\cdot j^{2a+1},

to see that

\int_{0}^{1}u^{-\lambda}\frac{1}{N^{2}}K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left(\frac{u}{N^{2}},\frac{u}{N^{2}}\right)du\\
\leq\frac{\text{const}}{N^{2a+2}}\sum_{j=0}^{N}j^{2a+1}\int_{0}^{1}u^{a-\lambda}du\leq\text{const}\cdot\frac{1}{N^{2a+2}}\sum_{j=0}^{N}j^{2a+1}=O(1).

On
(1,\frac{N^{2}}{2})
, we use the bound (see
[
18
]
):

w^{\left(a,b\right)}(x)\left(P_{j}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x)\right)^{2}\leq\frac{\text{const}}{\sqrt{x(1-x)}},

to note that

\int_{1}^{N^{2}/2}u^{-\lambda}\frac{1}{N^{2}}K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left(\frac{u}{N^{2}},\frac{u}{N^{2}}\right)du\leq\text{const}\int_{1}^{N^{2}}u^{-\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}du=O(1),

which gives us the desired result.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
a\geq b\geq-\frac{1}{2}
,
a>0
. Then, the sequence
\left\{M_{N}\right\}_{N\geq 1}
sampled according to
\mu_{N}^{(a,b)}
is tight.

Proof.

Using Markov’s inequality:

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}_{N}^{(a,b)}\left(M_{N}\geq R\right)\leq\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{N}^{(a,b)}\left[M_{N}\right]}{R}.

We claim that

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}_{N}^{(a,b)}\left[M_{N}\right]<\infty.

Indeed, using symmetry:

\mathbb{E}_{N}^{(a,b)}\left[M_{N}\right]=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}_{N}^{(a,b)}\left[\frac{1}{x_{1}}\right]=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x,x)}{x}dx.

We first restrict the integral to
[0,\frac{1}{2}]
by using the boundedness of the random variable on
\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
. Then, arguing as in the proof of Proposition

, we see that this integral is bounded uniformly in
N
. Hence,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}_{N}^{(a,b)}\left(M_{N}\geq R\right)=0,

which proves the proposition.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

For
a\geq b
;
a,b>0

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove that (let

\mathbb{E}_{N}^{(a,b)}\left[M_{N}^{\alpha}\right]=\frac{1}{N^{2\alpha}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{l}=\alpha\\

(13)

Using the bounds obtained in Proposition

N

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
a\geq b\geq-\frac{1}{2}

a>0

\mathbb{E}_{N}^{(a,b)}\left[\left(\mathcal{Z}_{N}\right)^{h}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{N}^{(a,b)}\left[\left(\frac{1}{N\log N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\sqrt{1-x_{j}^{(N)}}}{\sqrt{x_{j}^{(N)}}}\right)^{h}\right]\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{h}.

\mathbb{E}_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left[\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\sqrt{1-x_{j}}}{\sqrt{x_{j}}}\right|^{h}\right]\\

2

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
a\geq b\geq-\frac{1}{2}
,
a\geq 0
. We have:

\mathcal{S}_{N}=\int_{0}^{1}x^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-x)^{1/2}K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x,x)dx\sim\frac{2}{\pi}N\log N.

Proof.

We start by fixing an
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and dividing the integral into two contributions:
[0,\epsilon]

[\epsilon,1]

\frac{1}{N\log N}\int_{\epsilon}^{1}x^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-x)^{1/2}K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x,x)dx=\frac{1}{\log N}\mathbb{E}_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left[\frac{\sqrt{1-x_{1}}}{\sqrt{x_{1}}}\mathbf{1}_{\{x_{1}\geq\epsilon\}}\right]\\
\leq\frac{1}{\log N}\frac{\sqrt{1-\epsilon}}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\xrightarrow{\text{as }N\to\infty}0.

(17)

To compute the asymptotics of the contribution on
[0,\epsilon]
, one first needs to consider the
l\to\infty

H(l):=\int_{0}^{\epsilon}x^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}w^{\left(s-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}\right)}(x)\left(P_{l}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x)\right)^{2}dx.

(18)

\textbf{Step 1}:
Let

F(l):=\int_{0}^{\epsilon}{h_{l}}\frac{\arccos\left(1-2x\right)}{x}\frac{J_{a}^{2}\left(\left(l+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\arccos(1-2x)\right)}{\left(l+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{2a}}dx.

We claim that there exists a constant
\mathfrak{C}>0
such that:

\sup_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left|H(l)-F(l)\right|\leq\mathfrak{C}.

To prove this claim, we will use an asymptotic expansion obtained in
[
10
]
:

\sqrt{w^{\left(a,b\right)}(x)}P_{l}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x)\\
=\sqrt{h_{l}}\left(\frac{\arccos\left(1-2x\right)}{\sqrt{x(1-x)}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{J_{a}\left(\left(l+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\arccos(1-2x)\right)}{\left(l+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{a}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\left(l+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{a+1}}\right)\right),

(19)

where

h_{l}=\frac{(2l+a+b+1)\Gamma\left(l+a+1\right)\Gamma(l+a+b+1)}{2\Gamma(l+1)\Gamma(l+b+1)},

denotes the Bessel function with parameter
\nu
. Here, error term is uniform on
x\in[0,\epsilon]

[0,\epsilon]

\left\{a_{l}(x)\right\}_{l=1}^{\infty}

\left\{b_{l}(x)\right\}_{l=1}^{\infty}

\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{x\in[0,\epsilon]}\left|a_{l}(x)-b_{l}(x)\right|=0,

\left|a_{l}^{2}(x)-b_{l}^{2}(x)\right|\leq\left|a_{l}(x)+b_{l}(x)\right|\leq 2(1+\left|b_{l}(x)\right|).

Thus, using (
19

\Gamma(z)

x^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\biggl|w^{\left(a,b\right)}(x)\left(P_{l}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x)\right)^{2}-{h_{l}}\frac{\arccos\left(1-2x\right)}{\sqrt{x(1-x)}}\frac{J_{a}^{2}\left(\left(l+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\arccos(1-2x)\right)}{\left(l+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{2a}}\biggl|\\
\leq\text{const}\cdot x^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1+\sqrt{l}\left(\frac{\arccos\left(1-2x\right)}{\sqrt{x(1-x)}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|J_{a}\left(\left(l+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\arccos(1-2x)\right)\right|\right)\\

where the constant is independent of
x

\left|J_{\alpha}(x)\right|\leq Cx^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Since

Now that our claim is established, we want to compute the asymptotics of
\sum_{l}H(l)
by comparing with
\sum_{l}F(l)
. In particular, note that:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N\log N}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left|H(l)-F(l)\right|\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N\log N}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\text{const}=0.

(20)

Step 2:
We compute the asymptotics of
\sum_{l}F(l)

F(l)=\frac{h_{l}}{\left(l+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{2a}}\int_{0}^{\arccos(1-2\epsilon)}\frac{\theta\sin\theta}{1-\cos\theta}J_{a}^{2}\left(\left(l+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\theta\right)d\theta.

Noting the fact that the function

g(\arccos(1-2\epsilon))\frac{h_{l}}{\left(l+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{2a+1}}\int_{0}^{\left(l+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\arccos(1-2\epsilon)}J_{a}^{2}(u)du\\

Now, on
[0,1]

J_{a}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\cos\left(x-\frac{\pi}{2}\left(a+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)+O(x^{-3/2}),

Here, for the last equality we used the fact that

\lim_{l\to\infty}\frac{h_{l}}{\left(l+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{2a+1}}=1,

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N\log N}\sum_{l=1}^{N}F(l)\leq\frac{2}{\pi}.

But then by combining (

\frac{g(\arccos(1-2\epsilon))}{\pi}\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathcal{S}_{N}\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathcal{S}_{N}\leq\frac{2}{\pi}.

Since this holds for any

Proposition 3.4

Let
a\geq b\geq-\frac{1}{2}

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2}\log^{2}N}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}x^{-1/2}y^{-1/2}\left(1-x\right)^{1/2}\left(1-y\right)^{1/2}\left(K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x,y)\right)^{2}dxdy=0.

\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right]\times\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right]

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{1/2}^{1}x^{-1/2}y^{-1/2}\left(1-x\right)^{1/2}\left(1-y\right)^{1/2}\left(K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x,y)\right)^{2}dxdy\\

\int_{0}^{1/2}\int_{0}^{1/2}x^{-1/2}y^{-1/2}\left(1-x\right)^{1/2}\left(1-y\right)^{1/2}\left(K_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}(x,y)\right)^{2}dxdy=\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\left({I}(j,k)\right)^{2},

\mathcal{T}_{N,\delta}+\mathcal{B}_{N,\delta}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=1\\

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{N,\delta}}{N^{2}\log^{2}N}=0.

\left\{a_{l}(x)\right\}_{l=1}^{\infty}

\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{x\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]}\left|a_{l}(x)-b_{l}(x)\right|=0,

\left|a_{j}(x)a_{k}(x)-b_{j}(x)b_{k}(x)\right|\leq\left|a_{j}(x)\right|\left|a_{k}(x)-b_{k}(x)\right|+\left|b_{k}(x)\right|\left|a_{j}(x)-b_{j}(x)\right|\\
\leq\text{const}\left(\left|a_{j}(x)\right|+\left|b_{k}(x)\right|\right)\leq M\left(1+\left|b_{j}(x)\right|+\left|b_{k}(x)\right|\right).

Thus, arguing as before, we obtain that there exists a constant

\sup_{j,k}\left|I(j,k)-L(j,k)\right|\leq K,

where we define:

L(j,k)=\int_{0}^{1/2}\sqrt{h_{j}h_{k}}\frac{\arccos\left(1-2x\right)}{x}\\
\frac{J_{a}\left(\left(j+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\arccos(1-2x)\right)J_{a}\left(\left(k+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\arccos(1-2x)\right)}{\left(j+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{a}\left(k+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{a}}dx.

Thus, for all
j,k
, using the trivial bound
|x|\leq x^{2}+1
:

I^{2}(j,k)\leq CL^{2}(j,k)+\text{const}.

(26)

Hence, it is enough to show the claim for
I(j,k)
replaced by
L(j,k)
. Note that making the substitution
\theta=\arccos(1-2x)
in the definition of
L(j,k)
gives:

L(j,k)=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\sqrt{h_{j}h_{k}}}{\left(j+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{a}\left(k+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{a}}\frac{\theta\sin\theta}{1-\cos\theta}\\
J_{a}\left(\left(j+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\theta\right)J_{a}\left(\left(k+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\theta\right)d\theta.

Now, we divide the integral into two contributions:
(0,\frac{1}{k})
and
(\frac{1}{k},\frac{\pi}{2})
. Note that since Bessel functions are bounded (by a constant depending on the parameter of the function), the contribution on
(0,\frac{1}{k})
is bounded by:

\text{const}\cdot\frac{1}{k}\frac{\sqrt{h_{j}h_{k}}}{\left(j+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{a}\left(k+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{a}}\leq\text{const}\sqrt{\frac{j}{k}},

Now, to get asymptotics on the other contribution, we use (
22
). To be more precise, since on
\theta\in\left(\frac{1}{k},\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
;
\left(j+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\theta
and
\left(k+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\theta
are bounded away from zero uniformly in
j,k
; we see that there exists a bounded function
f:[1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
such that:

J_{a}\left(\left(j+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\theta\right)\\
=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\left(j+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\theta}}\cos\left(\left(j+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\theta-\frac{\pi(a+\frac{1}{2})}{2}\right)+\frac{f\left(\left(j+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\theta\right)}{\left(j+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\theta^{\frac{3}{2}}},

and similarly with
j
replaced by
k
.
Using this, we obtain that the contribution on
(\frac{1}{k},\frac{\pi}{2})
is given as (since the terms coming from the errors are integrable):

M(j,k):=\int_{1/k}^{\pi/2}g(\theta)\frac{2\sqrt{h_{j}h_{k}}}{\pi\left(j+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{(a+\frac{1}{2})}\left(k+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)^{(a+\frac{1}{2})}}\\
\frac{\cos\left(\left(j+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\theta-\frac{\pi(a+\frac{1}{2})}{2}\right)\cos\left(\left(k+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\theta-\frac{\pi(a+\frac{1}{2})}{2}\right)}{\theta}d\theta+O(1)\\
=O(1)\int_{1/k}^{\pi/2}g(\theta)\frac{\cos\left(\left(j+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\theta-\frac{\pi(a+\frac{1}{2})}{2}\right)\cos\left(\left(k+\frac{a+b+1}{2}\right)\theta-\frac{\pi(a+\frac{1}{2})}{2}\right)}{\theta}d\theta+O(1).

where we recall that we denote:

g(\theta)=\frac{\theta\sin\theta}{1-\cos\theta}.

Now, we divide the integral into two integrals via the identity

\cos(a)\cos(b)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\cos(a-b)+\cos(a+b)\right).

(27)

Then, the first integral becomes (up to multiplication by a bounded quantity):

\int_{1/k}^{\pi/2}\frac{g(\theta)\cos\left((j-k)\theta\right)}{\theta}d\theta=\int_{1}^{\frac{\pi k}{2}}\frac{\cos\left(\left(\frac{j}{k}-1\right)u\right)g\left(\frac{u}{k}\right)}{u}du,

which can be seen to be bounded for all
j,k
such that
\frac{j}{k}-1\geq\delta>0
, using integration by parts. As for the second integral that comes from (
27
),

\int_{1/k}^{\pi/2}\frac{g(\theta)\cos\left((j+k+a+b+1)\theta-\pi(a+\frac{1}{2})\right)}{\theta}d\theta\\
=\int_{1}^{\frac{\pi k}{2}}\frac{g\left(\frac{u}{k}\right)\cos\left(\left(\frac{j+a+b+1}{k}+1\right)u-\pi(a+\frac{1}{2})\right)}{u}du=O(1),

which can be seen again via integrating by parts. In particular, using the trivial identity

(x+y)^{2}\leq 2(x^{2}+y^{2})
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
j,k
such that
\frac{j}{k}\geq 1+\delta
:

L^{2}(j,k)\leq C\frac{j}{k}.

Noting that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=1\\
\frac{j}{k}\geq 1+\delta\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{j}{k}\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{k}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}j\right)=O(N^{2}\log(N)),

the claim follows.

\textbf{Claim 2}:
There exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\delta
such that

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{B}_{N,\delta}}{N^{2}\log^{2}N}\leq C\delta.

Note that once again, it is sufficient to prove the claim for
I(j,k)
replaced by
L(j,k)
. To prove this bound, we start again by dividing the integral defining
L(j,k)
into two contributions:
\left(0,\frac{1}{k}\right)
and
\left(\frac{1}{k},\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
. Exactly as before, we get the bound,

C\sqrt{\frac{j}{k}}\leq C\sqrt{1+\delta}\leq 2C,

on the integral over
\left(0,\frac{1}{k}\right)
, where
C
only depends on the parameter of the Bessel function and hence does not depend on
\delta
. As for the other integral, we use the estimate,

\left|J_{a}(x)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{x}},

to show that the integral over
\left(\frac{1}{k},\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
is bounded by

\text{const}\int_{1/k}^{\pi/2}\frac{1}{u}du=O(\log k).

Thus, we get a constant
C>0
independent of
\delta
such that

\mathcal{B}_{N,\delta}\leq C\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=1\\
1<\frac{j}{k}\leq 1+\delta\end{subarray}}^{N}\log^{2}(k)+1\right).

Now, note that for each
k
,
\log^{2}(k)+1
appears as many times as the number of integers
j\leq N
such that
k<j\leq k(1+\delta)
. But this is at most
\delta k
, and hence,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=1\\
1<\frac{j}{k}\leq 1+\delta\end{subarray}}^{N}\log^{2}(k)+1\leq\delta\sum_{k=1}^{N}k(\log^{2}(k)+1)\leq\delta N^{2}\log^{2}N+\delta N^{2},

which proves the claim.
Combining the two claims, we see that:

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2}\log^{2}N}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}I^{2}(j,k)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left(\mathcal{T}_{N,\delta}+\mathcal{B}_{N,\delta}\right)\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathcal{T}_{N,\delta}+\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathcal{B}_{N,\delta}\leq C\delta.

Since
C
is independent of
\delta
and
\delta\in(0,1)
was arbitrary the result follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Combining Propositions
3.2
,
3.3
,
25
, one can see that for
h=1,2
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left[\left(\mathcal{Z}_{N}\right)^{h}\right]=\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{h}.

In particular:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left[\left(\mathcal{Z}_{N}-\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2}\right]=\lim_{N\to\infty}\left[\mathbb{E}_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left[\left(\mathcal{Z}_{N}\right)^{2}\right]-\frac{4}{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left[\mathcal{Z}_{N}\right]+\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\right]=0.

Hence, we have that
\mathcal{Z}_{N}
converges, in distribution, to
\frac{2}{\pi}
, and hence by Skorokhod’s Representation Theorem we may assume without loss of generality that the convergence is in almost sure sense. Moreover, expanding as in (
16
) and applying Propositions
\ref{prop1},\ref{prop2}
and
25
, one can infer,

\sup_{N\geq 1}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{N}^{\left(a,b\right)}\left[\left(\mathcal{Z}_{N}\right)^{\alpha(2a+2)}\right]\right\}<\infty,

which means the sequence of random variables

\left\{\left(\mathcal{Z}_{N}\right)^{h}\right\}_{N\geq 1},

sampled according to
\mu_{N}^{(a,b)}

are uniformly integrable. Combining the uniform integrability of this sequence with the assumed almost sure convergence, we get the desired result.

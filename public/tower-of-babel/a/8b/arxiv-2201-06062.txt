Affine subspace concentration conditions
Kuang-Yu Wu

Abstract




Abstract.
We define a new notion of affine subspace concentration conditions for lattice polytopes and prove that these conditions hold for smooth and reflexive polytopes with barycenter at the origin. Our proof involves considering the slope stability of the canonical extension of the tangent bundle by the trivial line bundle with the extension class
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X})
on Fano toric varieties.

Keywords.
Toric vector bundles, subspace concentration conditions, canonical extension, Fano toric varieties

1. Introduction

Subspace concentration conditions for polytopes and more generally for
convex bodies have been of interest to convex geometers recently. One
main reason is its close relation to the logarithmic Minkowski
problem, which asks what finite Borel measure on
S^{n-1}
is the
cone-volume measure of a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In terms of
polytopes, given
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\in S^{n-1}
and

V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}\in(0,\infty)
, the problem asks if there is a polytope

P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
that contains the origin and has exactly
m
facets

F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}
such that the normal vector of each facet
F_{k}
is
exactly
v_{k}
and the volume of the cone over each
F_{k}
with vertex
at the origin is exactly
V_{k}
. As it turns out, subspace
concentration conditions give a sufficient condition for a positive
answer to the logarithmic Minkowski problem. See, for example,

[
BLY+13
,
Zhu14
,
CLZ19
]
.

In this paper,
we introduce new conditions of the same type,
which we call the
affine subspace concentration conditions
,
and we prove that these conditions hold for smooth and reflexive lattice polytopes with barycenter at the origin.

Theorem A

(
=\,
Theorem
4.4
, Affine subspace concentration conditions)
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth and reflexive lattice polytope with barycenter at the origin, and denote its facets by
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
. For each facet
P_{k}
, let
v_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be its primitive inner normal vector, and let
\operatorname{vol}(P_{k})
be its lattice volume with respect to the intersection of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and the affine span of
P_{k}
.

Then, for every proper affine subspace
A\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the following inequality holds:

\frac{1}{\dim A+1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k\,:\,v_{k}\in A}\operatorname{vol}(P_{k})\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{m}\operatorname{vol}(P_{k}).

In addition, whenever equality holds for some
A
, equality also holds for some affine subspace
A^{\prime}
complementary to
A
.

This theorem is inspired by
[
HNS22
]
, in which the subspace concentration properties are shown for polytopes with the same assumptions.

Proposition 1.1

(
cf.

[
HNS22
, Corollary 1.7]
)
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth and reflexive lattice polytopes with barycenter at the origin. Then, for every proper linear subspace
F\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the following inequality holds:

\frac{1}{\dim F}\sumop\displaylimits_{k\,:\,v_{k}\in F}\operatorname{vol}(P_{k})\leq\frac{1}{n}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{m}\operatorname{vol}(P_{k}).

In addition, whenever equality holds for some
F
, equality also holds for some subspace
F^{\prime}
complementary to
F
.

In fact, our result is neither stronger nor weaker than the original subspace concentration conditions; specifically, our result gives something new whenever
A
is
not
a linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Similar subspace concentration conditions have also been studied in more general settings. The subspace concentration conditions are proved in
[
HL14
]
for centered polytopes and more generally in
[
BH16
]
for centered convex bodies.

Example 1.2
.

Let
P
be a reflexive lattice triangle.
The affine subspace concentration conditions hold for
P
if and only if the lattice length of each side of
P
does not exceed one third of the perimeter and the total lattice length of any two sides of
P
does not exceed two thirds of the perimeter. This is equivalent to all three sides of
P
having the same lattice length.

is the unique smooth and reflexive lattice triangle, up to change of basis. Its barycenter is clearly at the origin. The affine subspace concentration conditions are indeed satisfied since each of its sides has lattice length
3
.

Figure

is one example of polytopes for which the affine subspace concentration conditions fail. The lattice lengths of its sides are
1
,
1
, and
2
. Note that the triangle is reflexive but not smooth, and its barycenter is not at the origin.

We do not claim that the converse to our main theorem holds. In fact, Figure
1(c)
is a lattice triangle that has its barycenter at the origin and is reflexive but not smooth. However, it satisfies the affine subspace concentration conditions since all three sides has lattice length
1
.

1.1. Idea of the proof

As in
[
HNS22
]
, our proof of the main result relies on toric geometry as well as some Kähler geometry.

First, let
X
be the smooth Fano toric variety corresponding to the polytope
P
. We consider the canonical extension

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X}\longrightarrow 0

with the extension class
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X})\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X},\mathcal{O})
. The main work we will do is in Section
3
, where we show that this rank
n+1
extension
\mathcal{E}
can be made a toric vector bundle and compute its corresponding filtrations in the sense of Klyachko’s classification theorem of toric vector bundles.

Proposition B

(
=\,
Proposition
3.1
)
.

There is a
T
-action on the extension
\mathcal{E}
that makes it a toric vector bundle whose corresponding filtrations
E^{\rho}(i)
of the
(n+1)
-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
E\cong\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
are given by

E^{\rho_{k}}(i)=\begin{cases}E,&i\leq 0,\\
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{(v_{k},-1)\},&i=1,\\
0,&i>2.\end{cases}

Then we insert some Kähler geometry. Since
P
has its barycenter at the origin, it is known that
X
admits a Kähler–Einstein metric; see
[
WZ04
,
Mab87
]
. A theorem of Tian
[
Tia92
]
then implies that the canonical extension
\mathcal{E}
admits a Hermitian–Einstein metric, and by the easy direction of the Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau theorem, this implies the slope polystability of
\mathcal{E}
with respect to
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X})
.

Combining this with Proposition B and a proposition in
[
HNS22
]
which characterizes the stability of toric vector bundles, we prove the affine subspace concentration conditions in Section
4
.

2. Preliminaries

Here we list some definitions and facts regarding toric varieties and toric vector bundles that we will use in this article. One may refer to
[
CLS11
,
Ful93
]
for more details about toric varieties and to
[
Kly90
,
Per04
,
Pay08
]
for more details about toric vector bundles.

2.1. Toric varieties

We work throughout over
\mathbb{C}
. By a toric variety, we mean an irreducible and normal algebraic variety
X
containing a torus
T\cong(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
as a Zariski open subset such that the action of
T
on itself (by multiplication) extends to an algebraic action of
T
on
X
.

Let
M
be the group of characters of
T
and
N
the group of
1
-parameter subgroups of
T
. Both
M
and
N
are lattices of rank
n
(equal to the dimension of
T
),
i.e.
, isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and they are dual to each other. Explicitly, the character corresponding to
u=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is given by

\chi^{u}\colon T\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*},\quad(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\longmapsto t_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{a_{n}},

and the
1
-parameter subgroup corresponding to
v=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is given by

\lambda^{v}\colon\mathbb{C}^{*}\longrightarrow T,\quad t\longmapsto(t^{b_{1}},\ldots,t^{b_{n}}).

The pairing of
M
and
N
is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon M\times N\to\mathbb{Z}
. We have
\chi^{u}(\lambda^{v}(t))=t^{\langle u,v\rangle}
for all
u\in M
and
v\in N
.

Every toric variety
X
is associated to a fan
\Delta
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}:=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\,(\cong\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Each cone
\sigma
corresponds to a
T
-invariant open affine subset of
X
, denoted by
U_{\sigma}
. One has
U_{\sigma}\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
, where
\sigma^{\vee}
is the dual cone of
\sigma
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}:=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}

A fan
\Delta
is said to be
complete
if it supports on the whole
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and is said to be
smooth
if every cone in
\Delta
is generated by a subset of a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
N
. A toric variety
X
is complete if and only if its associated fan
\Delta
is complete, and
X
is smooth if and only if
\Delta
is smooth.

There is an inclusion-reversing bijection between the cones
\sigma\in\Delta
and the
T
-orbits in
X
. Let
O_{\sigma}
be the orbit corresponding to
\sigma
. The dimension of
\sigma
is equal to the codimension of
O_{\sigma}
in
X
.

Fix a cone
\sigma\in\Delta
, and let
v
be a lattice point in the relative interior of
\sigma
. The
1
-parameter subgroup
\lambda^{v}
approaches a point
p_{\sigma}
in the orbit
O_{\sigma}
as
t\to 0
. This point
p_{\sigma}
is called the
distinguished point
corresponding to
\sigma
.

Given a
1
-dimensional cone
\rho\in\Delta
, the closure of
O_{\rho}\subseteq X
is a Weil divisor, which we denote by
D_{\rho}
. Suppose
X
is smooth. The canonical divisor
K_{X}
of
X
is equal to
-\sumop\displaylimits_{\rho}D_{\rho}
, where the sum is taken over all
1
-dimensional cones
\rho\in\Delta
.

2.2. Polytopes and toric varieties

Let
M_{\mathbb{R}}:=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A lattice polytope
P
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
is the convex hull in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
of finitely many points in
M
. The dimension of
P
is the dimension of the affine span of
P
. When
\dim P=\dim M_{\mathbb{R}}
, we say that
P
is full-dimensional.

Let
P\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}
be a full-dimensional lattice polytope, and let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
be the
facets
of
P
,
i.e.
, codimension
1
faces of
P
. For each facet
P_{k}
, there exist a unique primitive lattice point
v_{k}\in N
and a unique integer
c_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

P_{k}=\{u\in P\,|\,\langle u,v_{k}\rangle=-c_{k}\}

and
\langle u,v_{k}\rangle\geq-c_{k}
for all
u\in P
.

Denote the (inner) normal fan of
P
by
\Delta_{P}
. The
1
-dimensional cones in
\Delta_{P}
are exactly the rays generated by
v_{k}
. The toric variety
X_{\Delta_{P}}
associated to
\Delta_{P}
is called the toric variety of
P
and denoted by
X_{P}
. Let
D_{k}
be the divisor corresponding to the
1
-dimensional cone generated by
v_{k}
. Then we may define a divisor on
X
by
D_{P}:=\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}D_{k}
. Such a divisor
D_{P}
is necessarily ample.

This process is reversible, and there is a 1-to-1 correspondence between full-dimensional lattice polytopes
P\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}
and pairs
(X,D)
of a complete toric variety
X
together with an ample
T
-invariant divisor
D
on
X
.

Fix a vertex
u
of
P
. Let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
be the next lattice points on the
n
edges (
i.e.
,
1
-dimensional faces of
P
) containing
u
. We say that
P
is
smooth
if
\{u_{1}-u,\ldots,u_{n}-u\}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
M
for all vertices
u
of
P
. The toric variety
X_{P}
of
P
is smooth if and only if
P
is smooth.

A polytope
P
is called
reflexive
if the integers
c_{k}
(
k=1,\ldots,m
) defined above are all equal to
1
, and a smooth variety
X
is said to be
Fano
if its anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
is ample. A reflexive and smooth polytope
P
corresponds to a smooth Fano toric variety
X=X_{P}
together with its anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
.

2.3. Toric vector bundles

A vector bundle
\pi\colon\mathcal{E}\to X
over a toric variety
X=X_{\Delta}
is said to be toric (or equivariant) if there is a
T
-action on
\mathcal{E}
such that
t\circ\pi=\pi\circ t
for all
t\in T
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\to X
be a toric vector bundle. Fix a cone
\sigma\in\Delta
, and let
U_{\sigma}\subseteq X
be the corresponding open affine subset. The
T
-action on
\mathcal{E}
induces a
T
-action on sections given by

(t\cdot s)(p):=t\cdot(s(t^{-1}\cdot p)),

where
t\in T
,
p\in U_{\sigma}
, and
s\in\Gamma(U_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})
. A section
s\in\Gamma(U_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})
is called a
T
-
eigensection
if there exists a
u\in M
such that
t\cdot s=\chi^{u}(t)\cdot s
for all
t\in T
. For each
u\in M
, let
\Gamma(U_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})_{u}
be the set of
T
-eigensections
s
with
t\cdot s=\chi^{u}(t)\cdot s
.

Let
t_{0}\in T
be the identity and
E=\mathcal{E}_{t_{0}}
the fiber over
t_{0}
. The evaluation at
t_{0}
defines an injection
\Gamma(U_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})_{u}\hookrightarrow E
for each
u\in M
. Define
E^{\sigma}_{u}\subseteq E
to be the image of
\Gamma(U_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})_{u}
under this injection.

Given a
u^{\prime}\in M
that lies in the dual of
\sigma
, defined by
\sigma^{\vee}:=\{u\in M\,|\,\langle u,v\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }v\in\sigma\}
, the character
\chi^{u^{\prime}}
of
T
extends to a regular function on
U_{\sigma}
. Then, the multiplication by
\chi^{u^{\prime}}
gives a map
\Gamma(U_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})_{u}\to\Gamma(U_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})_{u-u^{\prime}}
and in turn induces an inclusion
E^{\sigma}_{u}\subseteq E^{\sigma}_{u-u^{\prime}}
.
If
u^{\prime}
further satisfies
\langle u,v\rangle=0
for all
v\in\sigma
(
i.e.
,
u^{\prime}\in\sigma^{\perp}
), then
-u^{\prime}
also lies in
\sigma^{\vee}
, and we obtain
E^{\sigma}_{u}=E^{\sigma}_{u-u^{\prime}}
. Therefore,
E^{\sigma}_{u}
depends only on the class of
u
in
M_{\sigma}:=M/(M\cap\sigma^{\perp})
.

Let
\rho
be a
1
-dimensional cone in
\Delta
. In this case,
M_{\rho}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
. Let
v_{\rho}\in N
be the primitive generator of
\rho
. For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, take one
u_{i}\in M
such that
\langle u_{i},v_{\rho}\rangle=i
, and define
E^{\rho}(i):=E^{\rho}_{u_{i}}
. Then, we get a decreasing filtration of
E

\cdots\supseteq E^{\rho}(i)\supseteq E^{\rho}(i-1)\supseteq\cdots.

These filtrations corresponding to the
1
-dimensional cones in fact contain all information about a toric vector bundle according to the following classification theorem of Klyachko.

Klyachko’s classification theorem

(
cf.

[
Kly90
, Theorem 2.2.1]
)
.

The category of toric vector bundles on a toric variety
X=X(\Delta)
is naturally equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional vector spaces
E
with collections of decreasing filtrations
\{E^{\rho}(i)\}
indexed by the
1
-dimensional cones in
\Delta
, satisfying the following compatibility condition: for each cone
\sigma\in\Delta
, there is a decomposition
E=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{[u]\in M_{\sigma}}E_{[u]}
such that

E^{\rho}(i)=\sumop\displaylimits_{[u]:\langle u,v_{p}\rangle\geq i}E_{[u]}

for all
\rho\preceq\sigma
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Another way to consider a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
is to restrict it to open affine sets. To discuss this, let
\sigma
be a cone in
\Delta
and
U_{\sigma}\subseteq X
the corresponding open affine set. On
U_{\sigma}
, the toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
splits equivariantly into a direct sum of toric line bundles whose underlying line bundles are trivial; see
[
Pay08
, Proposition 2.2]
. Collecting non-vanishing sections of these toric line bundles, one can get a local frame consisting of
T
-eigensections.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\rho
be a
1
-dimensional cone contained in
\sigma
and
v_{\rho}\in\rho\cap N
the primitive generator of
\rho
. Suppose that
\mathcal{E}
has a local frame
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\}
on
U_{\sigma}
such that
t\cdot s_{k}=\chi^{u_{k}}(t)\cdot s_{k}
for some
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\in M
, and define
L_{k}:=\operatorname{span}\{s_{k}(t_{0})\}\subseteq E
for each
k=1,\ldots,r
. Then, we have

E^{\rho}(i)=\sumop\displaylimits_{k:\langle u_{k},v_{\rho}\rangle\geq i}L_{k}.

Proof.

By assumption, we have

\mathcal{E}|_{U_{\sigma}}\cong\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{r}\mathcal{L}_{k},

where
\mathcal{L}_{k}
is the trivial line bundle
U_{\sigma}\times\mathbb{C}
spanned by
s_{k}
equipped with the
T
-action defined by

t\cdot(p,s_{k}(p))=(t\cdot p,\chi^{u}(t)\cdot s_{k}(p)).

By Klyachko’s classification theorem, each toric line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{k}
on
U_{\sigma}
also corresponds to a collection of filtrations of
L_{k}
indexed by the
1
-dimensional cones contained in
\sigma
. The filtration corresponding to
\rho
is given by

(L_{k})^{\rho}(i)=\begin{cases}L_{k},&i\leq\langle u_{k},v_{\rho}\rangle,\\
0,&i>\langle u_{k},v_{\rho}\rangle.\end{cases}

Then, adding these filtrations together, we obtain the desired filtration of
E
.
∎

3. The canonical extension

Let
X
be a smooth Fano variety of dimension
n
. The first Chern class
c_{1}(X)
of
X
lies in the
(1,1)
-Dolbeault cohomology
H^{1,1}(X)
, which is naturally isomorphic to the sheaf cohomology
H^{1}(X,{}^{1}_{X})
by Dolbeault’s theorem. The cotangent bundle
{}^{1}_{X}
is the dual of the tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{X}
, so we have

H^{1}(X,{}^{1}_{X})=H^{1}(X,\mathcal{T}_{X}^{*})\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{T}_{X}^{*})\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X})

by for example
[
Har77
, Propositions 6.3 and 6.7]
. Thus,
c_{1}(X)
can be viewed as an extension class in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X})
and in turn corresponds to an extension
\mathcal{E}
of the tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{X}
by the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}
given by

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X}\longrightarrow 0.

In this section, we will show that if
X
is toric, then
\mathcal{E}
can be made a toric vector bundle, and we will compute its corresponding filtrations in the sense of Klyachko’s classification theorem of toric vector bundles.

Let
\Delta_{X}
be the corresponding fan of
X
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Denote the
1
-dimensional cones in
\Delta_{X}
by
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m}
, and let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\in N
be the primitive generators of these
1
-dimensional cones, respectively.

Proposition 3.1
.

There is a
T
-action on the extension
\mathcal{E}
that makes it a toric vector bundle whose corresponding filtrations
E^{\rho}(i)
of the
(n+1)
-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
E:=N_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
are given by

E^{\rho_{k}}(i)=\begin{cases}E,&i\leq 0,\\
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{(v_{k},-1)\},&i=1,\\
0,&i>2.\end{cases}

To prove this, we will use the following construction of the extension
\mathcal{E}
. First, embed
X
into a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{N}
with the very ample divisor
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X})=-K_{X}
. Next, further embed
\mathbb{P}^{N}
into
\mathbb{P}^{N+1}
. Fix a point
\tilde{p}_{0}\in\mathbb{P}^{N+1}\backslash\mathbb{P}^{N}
, and let
Y\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N+1}
be the cone over
X
with vertex
\tilde{p}_{0}
. Then
\mathcal{E}
will be exactly the restriction of the logarithmic tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{Y}(-\log X)
of
Y
to
X
. A proof of this can be found in
[
Wah76
, Proposition 3.3]
.

In the following, we will first construct
Y
as a toric variety and get an induced toric action on the logarithmic tangent sheaf. Then, we will make
\mathcal{E}
a toric vector bundle.

3.1. The logarithmic tangent sheaf

Since
X
is a smooth and Fano toric variety, its anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
is very ample. Let
P
be the polytope corresponding to
(X,-K_{X})
. Then the vector space
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}))
of the global sections of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X})
is spanned by the characters corresponding to the lattice points in
P
,
i.e.
,
\Gamma(X,-K_{X})=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{u\in P\cap M}\mathbb{C}\cdot\chi^{u}
.

Let
N=\dim\Gamma(X,-K_{X})-1
. Then the embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
can be explicitly given by
x\mapsto[\chi^{u}(x)]_{u\in P\cap M}
. In particular, the restriction of the embedding to the torus
T\subseteq X
is given by
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\mapsto[\chi^{u}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})]_{u\in P\cap M}
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that the embedding of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
in
\mathbb{P}^{N+1}
is given by

[Z_{0}:\cdots:Z_{N}]\longmapsto[Z_{0}:\cdots:Z_{N}:0]

so that
\mathbb{P}^{N}
is identified with the hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}^{N+1}
where the last coordinate vanishes. Also, we may set
\tilde{p}_{0}=[0:\cdots:0:1]
. Then the cone over
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
with vertex
\tilde{p}_{0}
is given by

Y=\left\{[x:Z_{N+1}]\in\mathbb{P}^{N+1}\,\big|\,x\in X,Z_{N+1}\in\mathbb{C}\right\}\cup\{\tilde{p}_{0}\}.

There is a dense
(n+1)
-dimensional torus
\tilde{T}:=T\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
in
Y
, and we may let it act on
Y
by

(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},t_{n+1})\cdot[x:Z_{N+1}]:=[(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\cdot x:t_{n+1}Z_{N+1}].

We have now made
Y
a toric variety.

We will denote the character lattice of
\tilde{T}
by
\tilde{M}\cong M\oplus\mathbb{Z}
. We also let

The distinguished points on

[1:\cdots:1:0]

Now, consider the limits of the

\tilde{T}
corresponding to the lattice points
(0,\ldots,0,1)

(k=1,\ldots,m)

Next, since

Then, we obtain

4. Affine subspace concentration conditions

Let
M\,(\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n})

n

M_{\mathbb{R}}:=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}

be a smooth and reflexive lattice polytope with barycenter at the origin. By replacing

with its subspace spanned by

is full-dimensional,
i.e.
,
\dim P=n
.

Such a polytope corresponds to an
n
-dimensional smooth Fano toric variety
X=X_{P}
, together with its anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
. The fan
\Delta_{X}
of
X

Denote the

Proposition 4.1

(

[

.

Let
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
be a polarized smooth toric variety and
Q\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}
the corresponding polytope. For each
1

\rho
in the fan of
X
, let
Q^{\rho}
be the facet of
Q
corresponding to
\rho
and
\operatorname{vol}(Q^{\rho})
the lattice volume of the facet
Q^{\rho}
with respect to the intersection of
M
with the affine span of
Q^{\rho}
. A toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
corresponding to filtrations
E^{\rho}(i)
is
(
semi-
)
stable with respect to
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
if and only if the following inequality holds for every proper
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace
F\subsetneq E
and
F^{\rho}(i)=E^{\rho}(i)\cap F
:

(
*
)

\frac{1}{\dim F}\sumop\displaylimits_{i,\rho}i\cdot f^{[\rho]}(i)\cdot\operatorname{vol}(Q^{\rho})\overset{(\leq)}{<}\frac{1}{\dim E}\sumop\displaylimits_{i,\rho}i\cdot e^{[\rho]}(i)\cdot\operatorname{vol}(Q^{\rho}),

where
f^{[\rho]}(i):=\dim F^{\rho}(i)-\dim F^{\rho}(i+1)
and
e^{[\rho]}(i):=\dim E^{\rho}(i)-\dim E^{\rho}(i+1)

It turns out that to check the stability of toric vector bundle, it suffices to consider equivariant reflexive subsheaves
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{E}
corresponding to a
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace
F
of

together with the filtrations
\{F^{\rho}(i):=F\cap E^{\rho}(i)\}
. Then the equation (

∗

‣
4.1
) is equivalent to the statement that
\mu(\mathcal{F})\leq\mu(\mathcal{E})
.

In our setting, we consider the anticanonical polarization
D=-K_{X}
and the canonical extension
\mathcal{E}
of
\mathcal{T}_{X}
by
\mathcal{O}_{X}
with
the extension class
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X})\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{T}_{X})
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
E:=N_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
. For every proper
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace
F\subsetneq E
, we have

\frac{1}{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}F}\sumop\displaylimits_{k\,:\,(v_{k},-1)\in F}\operatorname{vol}(P_{k})\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{m}\operatorname{vol}(P_{k}).

In addition, whenever equality holds for some
F
, equality also holds for some subspace
F^{\prime}\subsetneq E
complementary to
F
.

Proof.

First, since the barycenter of
P
is at the origin, by
[
WZ04
, Corollary 1.3]
and
[
Mab87
, Corollary 5.5]
, we have that
X
admits a Kähler–Einstein metric. Then, applying
[
Tia92
, Theorem 1.1]
and the easy direction of the Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau theorem, we obtain that
\mathcal{E}
is polystable with respect to
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X})
. In particular,
\mathcal{E}
is semistable with respect to
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X})
.

Define
F^{\rho_{k}}(i):=F\cap E^{\rho_{k}}(i)
. Then, by Proposition
3.1
, for all
k=1,\ldots,m
, we have
F^{\rho_{k}}(i)=F
for
i\leq 0
,
F^{\rho_{k}}(i)=0
for
i\geq 2
, and

F^{\rho_{k}}(1)=\begin{cases}\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{(v_{k},-1)\}&\text{if }(v_{k},-1)\in F,\\
0&\text{if }(v_{k},-1)\notin F.\end{cases}

Thus, using the notation in Proposition
4.1
, we have
f^{[\rho_{k}]}(i)=0
for all
i\neq 0,1
and

f^{[\rho_{k}]}(1)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }(v_{k},-1)\in F,\\
0&\text{if }(v_{k},-1)\notin F.\end{cases}

The inequality then follows from Proposition
4.1
.

To see the second part, note that a proper
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace
F\subsetneq E
such that equality holds corresponds to a proper subbundle
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{E}
that has the same slope as
\mathcal{E}
. Since
\mathcal{E}
is polystable,
\mathcal{E}
has another (proper) subbundle
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
with the same slope such that
\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Then the subspace
F^{\prime}
corresponding to
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is exactly the desired subspace.
∎

Next, we replace the complex vector space
E
with a real one.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
V:=N_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. For every proper
\mathbb{R}
-linear subspace
W\subsetneq V
, we have

\frac{1}{\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W}\sumop\displaylimits_{k\,:\,(v_{k},-1)\in W}\operatorname{vol}(P_{k})\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{m}\operatorname{vol}(P_{k}).

In addition, whenever equality holds for some
W
, equality also holds for some subspace
W^{\prime}\subsetneq V
complementary to
W
.

Proof.

First, view the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
E
defined in the previous proposition as
V\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
. Embed
V
into
E
by
w\mapsto w\otimes 1
. By abuse of notation, we will also use
V
to denote its image in
E
, and similarly for (real) subspaces of
V
.

An important observation is that the vectors
(v_{k},-1)
are all in
V
. Thus, given a
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
, we have
(v_{k},-1)\in E^{\prime}
if and only if
(v_{k},-1)\in(E^{\prime}\cap V)
.

Now, to prove the inequality, consider
F:=W\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
, which is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace of
E
. We have
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}F=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W
. Also, by the observation above, we have
(v_{k},-1)\in F
if and only if
(v_{k},-1)\in W
. Then, the inequality simply follows from Proposition
4.2
.

Next, to show the second part of the corollary, suppose the equality holds for some real subspace
W\subsetneq V
. Similarly to above, consider
F:=W\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
. Note that
the equality in Proposition
4.2
holds for
F
. Then, the second part of Proposition
4.2
gives a
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace
F^{\prime}
of
E
which is complementary to
F
and for which the equality in Proposition
4.2
holds.

Consider
W^{\prime}:=F^{\prime}\cap V
. Note that
W^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\subseteq F^{\prime}
, which implies
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W^{\prime}\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}F^{\prime}
. By the observation above,
\{(v_{k},-1)\}\cap F^{\prime}=\{(v_{k},-1)\}\cap W^{\prime}
. Therefore, we obtain

\frac{1}{\dim W^{\prime}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k\,:\,(v_{k},-1)\in W^{\prime}}\operatorname{vol}(P_{k})\geq\frac{1}{\dim F^{\prime}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k\,:\,(v_{k},-1)\in F^{\prime}}\operatorname{vol}(P_{k})=\frac{1}{n+1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{m}\operatorname{vol}(P_{k}).

But we have proved the opposite inequality in the first part. Hence, equality must hold here. Moreover,
W^{\prime}
is complementary to
W
since we have
W\cap W^{\prime}\subseteq F\cap F^{\prime}=0
and

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W^{\prime}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}F^{\prime}=n-\dim_{\mathbb{C}}F=n-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W.\qed

Finally, we deduce the affine subspace concentration conditions.

Theorem 4.4

(Affine subspace concentration conditions)
.

For every proper affine subspace
A\subsetneq N_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the following inequality holds:

\frac{1}{\dim_{\mathbb{R}}A+1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k\,:\,v_{k}\in A}\operatorname{vol}(P_{k})\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{m}\operatorname{vol}(P_{k}).

In addition, whenever equality holds for some
A
, equality also holds for some affine subspace
A^{\prime}
complementary to
A
.

Proof.

Let
x_{n+1}
be the last coordinate of
V=N_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathbb{R}\,(\cong\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
, and let
W_{0}
be the hyperplane
\{x_{n+1}=0\}
in
V
. Embed
N_{\mathbb{R}}
into
V
via
v\mapsto(v,-1)
. By abuse of notation, we also use
N_{\mathbb{R}}
to denote the image of this embedding,
i.e.
, the hyperplane
\{x_{n+1}=-1\}
in
V
one unit below
W_{0}
. Note that this embedding maps each
v_{k}\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
to
(v_{k},-1)\in V
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the affine subspaces of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and the linear subspaces of
V
that are not contained in
W_{0}
. Given a linear subspace
W\subseteq V
that is not contained in
W_{0}
, we can get an affine subspace in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
by taking the intersection
A:=W\cap N_{\mathbb{R}}
. On the other hand, given an affine subspace
A\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
, we can take the linear span of
A
(and the origin
0\in V
) to recover the linear subspace
W
.

Note that if a linear subspace
W\subseteq V
corresponds to an affine subspace
A\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
, then
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}W=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}A+1
. Then, it is easy to see that the inequality here follows from Corollary
4.3
.

The second part also almost follows directly from Corollary
4.3
, but we need to show that the complementary linear subspace
W^{\prime}\subseteq V
obtained from the corollary cannot be contained in
W_{0}
. Indeed, since
W_{0}
does not contain any of the vectors
(v_{k},-1)
, every linear subspace of
W_{0}
will give us
0
on the left-hand side of the inequality in Corollary
4.3
. However, the right-hand side of the inequality is positive, so equality cannot hold for any linear subspace of
W_{0}
. Therefore,
W^{\prime}
is not contained in
W_{0}
, and its intersection with
N_{\mathbb{R}}
gives the desired complementary affine subspace
A^{\prime}
.
∎

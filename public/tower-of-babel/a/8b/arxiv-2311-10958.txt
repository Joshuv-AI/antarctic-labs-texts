Strong law of large numbers for the generalized Fréchet means with random minimizing domains
Jaesung Park, Sungkyu Jung

Abstract
This paper introduces a novel extension of Fréchet means, called generalized Fréchet means as a comprehensive framework for characterizing features in probability distributions in general topological spaces. The generalized Fréchet means are defined as minimizers of a suitably defined cost function. The framework encompasses various extensions of Fréchet means in the literature. The most distinctive difference of the new framework from the previous works is that we allow the domain of minimization of the empirical means be random and different from that of the population means. This expands the applicability of the Fréchet mean framework to diverse statistical scenarios, including dimension reduction for manifold-valued data.



1
Introduction

This paper introduces
generalized Fréchet means
as a general framework for characterizing probability distributions on a topological space, and conditions inducing the consistency of empirical means to the population counterparts. This framework includes the settings of the Fréchet mean
(
Fréchet 1948
)
, Fréchet median
(
Arnaudon et al. 2012
)
, the
L^{p}
center of mass (for
p\in[1,\infty)
)
(
Afsari 2011
)
, and a large class of
M
-estimators, the
H
-Fréchet mean and
\mathfrak{c}
-Fréchet mean defined in
Schötz 2022
, as well as the Fréchet
\rho
-mean of
Huckemann et al. 2011
. While each of the above extensions of Fréchet means is defined as the minimizers of an appropriately defined objective function on a fixed domain, we allow the empirical version of generalized Fréchet means to be the minimizers on a (possibly)
random domain
. This is the key difference between the previous extensions of Fréchet means and our generalized Fréchet means.

By allowing the random minimizing domains in the empirical case, we believe that the proposed general framework can be applied to a wide range of statistical applications, including various extensions of PCAs such as those discussed in
Jung et al. 2012
,
Huckemann and Eltzner 2018
, and
Pennec 2018
.

The subsequent sections are structured as follows. In Section
2
, we establish a novel concept generalized Fréchet mean. In Section
3
, we see that generalized Fréchet mean includes previous various extensions of Fréchet means. In Section
4
, we introduce the concept of Ziezold strong consistency
(
Ziezold 1977
)
and Bhattacharya-Patrangenaru strong consistency
(
Bhattacharya et al. 2003
)
, consistency of random closed sets. In Section
5
, we establish our main result (Theorem
1
), Bhattacharya-Patrangenaru strong consistency of empirical generalized Fréchet means with appropriate conditions. In Sec
6
, we suggest corollaries of Theorem
1
in various settings for a more wide applicability.

2
Generalized Fréchet means

In this section, we formally introduce the framework of generalized Fréchet means. Let
T
be a topological space in which data lie. Let
X,X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
be i.i.d. random elements from an abstract probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P})
to
T
. We denote
P
for the distribution of
X
.
We are concerned with the situation that certain characteristics of
P
are described by an element in a metric space
(M,d)
.
We call
T
the
data space
, and
M
the
descriptor space
, as previously used in
Schötz 2022
. Note that
M
may coincide with
T
, but
it is generally understood that
M\neq T
.
A
cost function

\mathfrak{c}:T\times M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
prescribes the cost
\mathfrak{c}(t,m)
of using a descriptor
m\in M
in describing a data point
t\in T
.
We assume for
P
that for any
m\in M
,
\mathbb{E}\lvert\mathfrak{c}(X,m)\rvert<\infty
.

We denote
\mathcal{C}(M)
for the collection of all nonempty closed subsets of
M
.
Suppose that
M_{0}\in\mathcal{C}(M)
, and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
M_{n}:(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(M)
be a random closed subset of
M
.
Here, the two random sequences
\{M_{1},M_{2},\ldots\}
and
\{X,X_{1},X_{2},\ldots\}
may or may not be independent.
Moreover, for any
f:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
M^{\prime}\subset M
and
\varepsilon>0
, we define

\varepsilon\text{-}\operatorname{\mathop{\rm argmin}}_{m\in M^{\prime}}f:=\{m\in M^{\prime}|f(m)\leq\varepsilon+\inf_{m\in M^{\prime}}f(m)\}.

Definition 1
.

(a)

The set of
population generalized Fréchet means
of
X
is

E_{0}:=\underset{m\in M_{0}}{\arg\min}~F(m),

where
F(m)=\mathbb{E}\mathfrak{c}(X,m)
for
m\in M
. We denote
l_{0}:=\inf_{m\in M_{0}}F(m).

(b)

Let
\varepsilon_{n}\geq 0
be a nonnegative sequence satisfying
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}=0
. For each
n
, the set of
empirical generalized Fréchet means
of
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
is

\hat{E}_{n}(\omega):=\varepsilon_{n}\text{-}\underset{m\in M_{n}(\omega)}{\arg\min}~F_{n}(\omega)(m),

where
F_{n}(\omega)(m)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathfrak{c}(X_{j}(\omega),m)
for
m\in M
and
\omega\in\Omega
. We denote
\hat{l}_{n}(\omega):=\inf_{m\in M_{n}(\omega)}F_{n}(\omega)(m)\text{ for }\omega\in\Omega.

In Definition
1
, the population and empirical means may have different domains for minimization. This setting reflects the situation where
M_{n}
is an estimator of
M_{0}
. Moreover,

\varepsilon\text{-}\mathop{\rm argmin}
in the definition of empirical means accommodates the use of numerical approximations.

3
Relations to previous extensions of Fréchet means

We show that the framework of the generalized Fréchet means includes various extensions of Fréchet means as its special cases, as we elaborate below.

The Fréchet means are defined for the case
T=M
, i.e. the data space is the metric space
(M,d)
, as a generalization of arithmetic mean in the vector space to the case where the random element
X
lies in the metric space
M
. The population and empirical Fréchet means are defined as an element of

{\operatorname{argmin}_{m\in M}}~\mathbb{E}d(X,m)^{2}\text{ and }{\operatorname{argmin}_{m\in M}}~\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d(X_{i},m)^{2},

respectively. These are generalized to the
L^{p}
center of mass
(
Afsari 2011
)
for
p\geq 1
, defined by an element of

\underset{m\in M}{\operatorname{argmin}}~\mathbb{E}d(X,m)^{p}\quad\text{ and }\quad\underset{m\in M}{\operatorname{argmin}}~\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d(X_{i},m)^{p},

(1)

representing the population and empirical
L^{p}
centers, respectively. Note that the Fréchet median and Fréchet mean are special cases of the
L^{p}
center of mass for
p=1
and
p=2
, respectively.

The
L^{p}
loss in (
1
) may be replaced by
H\{d(X,m)\}
, where
H:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
,
H(t)=\int_{0}^{t}h(u)du
, is given by any non-decreasing function
h:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
. Such an extension was first introduced by
Schötz 2022
and is called
H
-Fréchet means.

Huckemann et al. 2011
defined the Fréchet
\rho
-mean, a generalization of the Fréchet means for the case where the data space
T
is different from the descriptor space
M
. The Fréchet
\rho
-mean is defined by replacing the squared loss
d(X,m)^{2}
of the Fréchet mean by
\rho(X,m)^{2}
, where the function
\rho:T\times M\rightarrow[0,\infty)
plays the role of a “distance” function between a data point
X
and a descriptor
m
. Finally, the
\mathfrak{c}
-Fréchet mean of
Schötz 2022
replaces the squared loss used in
Huckemann et al. 2011
with a much more flexible cost function
\mathfrak{c}
, which may have negative values. Both Fréchet
\rho
-means and
\mathfrak{c}
-Fréchet means
are applicable to the case
T=M
, but
\rho
(or
\mathfrak{c}
) needs not to be a function of the metric.

All of the aforementioned generalizations of Fréchet means are special cases of the generalized Fréchet mean; see Table
1
and Diagram (

). The key differences between generalized Fréchet mean and previous extensions are possibly different domains of minimization for population and empirical definitions, and the introduction of
\epsilon\text{-}\mathop{\rm argmin}
.

4
Convergence of random closed sets

Since both of the empirical and population generalized Fréchet mean sets,
\hat{E}_{n}
and
E_{0}
, are generally non-singleton sets, we use appropriate definitions for the convergence of a sequence of sets to a set, as introduced in
Huckemann et al. 2011
and further discussed in
Schötz 2022
.

Definition 2
.

Let
E_{0}\in\mathcal{C}(M)
, and
E_{n}:(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(M)
be a sequence of random closed sets in
M
.

(a)

E_{n}
is a strongly consistent estimator of
E_{0}
in the sense of Ziezold (Z-SC) if for almost all
\omega\in\Omega
, every accumulation point of the arbitrary sequence
m_{n}\in E_{n}(\omega)
is in
E_{0}
. In other words,
E_{n}
is a Z-SC estimator of
E_{0}
if
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\bigcup_{k=n}^{\infty}{E}_{n}}\subset E_{0}
almost surely.

(b)

E_{n}
is a strongly consistent estimator of
E_{0}
in the sense of Bhattacharya-Patrangenaru (BP-SC) if

\mathcal{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega|\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sup_{m\in E_{n}(\omega)}d(m,E_{0})\right)=0\right\}\right)=1.

(c)

E_{n}
is a weakly consistent estimator of
E_{0}
(WC) if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega|\sup_{m\in E_{n}(\omega)}d(m,E_{0})\leq\varepsilon\right\}\right)=1\text{ for all }\varepsilon>0.

When the population parameter
E_{0}
is clear from the context, we also say
E_{n}
is Ziezold-consistent (or BP-consistent, respectively).
Huckemann et al. 2011
coined these terms, generalizing the notions of strong consistency introduced in
Ziezold 1977
and
Bhattacharya et al. 2003
, respectively. The notions of convergences used in the Ziezold and BP consistency (in (a) and (b) above) are sometimes referred to as the convergence in outer limits and the one-sided Hausdorff convergence, respectively
(
Schötz 2022
)
. To discuss the relation between the various notions of consistency, the following definition for the sequence of sets is needed:

Definition 3
.

A sequence
B_{n}\subset M
is eventually precompact if
\overline{\cup_{n\geq N}B_{n}}
is compact for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Relations between the three notions of consistency are well-known
(
Ginestet 2012
;
Schötz 2022
)
.

Lemma 1
.

(a)

If
E_{n}
is BP-consistent with
E_{0}
, then it is Ziezold-consistent.

(b)

If
E_{n}
is Ziezold-consistent with
E_{0}
, and with probability 1,
E_{n}
is eventually precompact, then
E_{n}
is BP-consistent.

(c)

If
{E}_{n}
is BP-consistent, then
{E}_{n}
is weakly consistent.

Proof of Lemma
1
.

(a) It is known that
{\lim}_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{m\in E_{n}(\omega)}d(m,E_{0})=0
implies
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\bigcup_{k=n}^{\infty}{E}_{n}(\omega)}\subset E_{0}
for a fixed
\omega

(
Schötz 2022
, Theorem 2.4.(a))
. Thus a BP-SC estimator is also a Z-SC estimator.

(b) It is known that if
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\bigcup_{k=n}^{\infty}{E}_{n}(\omega)}\subset E_{0}
and
E_{n}(\omega)
is eventually precompact, then
{\lim}_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{m\in E_{n}(\omega)}d(m,E_{0})=0
for a fixed
\omega

(
Schötz 2022
, Theorem 2.4.(b))
. Thus Z-SC estimator is also a BP-SC estimator when it is eventually precompact almost surely.

Item (c) is easily checked by Definition
2
.
∎

In general, Ziezold-consistent
E_{n}
may not be BP-consistent
(
Huckemann et al. 2011
)
. Moreover, while the BP-consistency leads to weak consistency, the converse does not hold in general
(
Ginestet 2012
)
.

5
Consistency of Generalized Fréchet means

In this section, we list general conditions for the empirical generalized Fréchet mean set
\hat{E}_{n}
to be BP-consistent with
E_{0}(P)
. Conditions will be imposed on the cost function
\mathfrak{c}
and the domains
M_{0}
,
M_{n}
for minimization, as well as on the distribution
X\sim P
. As seen in Lemma
1
(b), Ziezold-consistency is guaranteed under weaker assumptions than for BP-consistency.

We first discuss conditions implying Ziezold-consistency. Intuitively, the convergence of
M_{n}
to
M_{0}
in some sense is necessary to imply Ziezold-consistency. We use Kuratowski convergence for such a purpose.

Definition 4
.

(
Beer 1993
)
For
P_{0}\in\mathcal{C}(M)
and
P_{n}\in\mathcal{C}(M)
, we say that
P_{n}
converges to
P_{0}
in the sense of Kuratowski if
P_{n}
and
P_{0}
satisfies the followings.

(i)

If an arbitrary sequence
m_{n}\in P_{n}
has an accumulation point, then that point is in
P_{0}
.

(ii)

For an arbitrary
m_{0}\in P_{0}
, there exist a sequence
m_{n}\in P_{n}
that converges to
m_{0}
.

Condition 1
.

For almost all
\omega\in\Omega
,
M_{n}(\omega)
converges to
M_{0}
in the sense of Kuratowski.

Of course, Condition
1
is satisfied if
M_{0}=M_{n}
for all
n
, as we check in the following.

Lemma 2
.

If
M_{n}=M_{0}\in\mathcal{C}(M)
for all
n\geq 1
,
M_{n}
converges to
M_{0}
in the sense of Kuratowski.

Proof of Lemma
2
.

Since
M_{0}
is closed, for every
m_{n}\in M_{n}=M_{0}
, accumulation points of
m_{n}
is in
M_{0}
. Moreover, for every
m_{0}\in M_{0}=M
, sequence
m_{n}=m_{0}\in M_{n}=M
converges to
m_{0}
. Thus
M_{n}
converges to
M_{0}
in the sense of Kuratowski.
∎

Next result is a key lemma that characterizes conditions guaranteeing Ziezold consistency of
\hat{E}_{n}
.

Lemma 3
.

Suppose Condition
1
holds, and for almost all
\omega\in\Omega
,

\begin{split}&\text{for any }m_{0}\in M_{0}\text{ and }m_{n}\in M_{n}(\omega)\text{ converging to }m_{0},F_{n}(\omega)(m_{n})\rightarrow F(m_{0}).\end{split}

(3)

Then the following holds.

(a)

\hat{E}_{n}
is a Z-SC estimator of
E_{0}
and
\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\limsup}~\hat{l}_{n}(\omega)\leq l_{0}
almost surely.

(b)

Moreover, for any
\omega\in\Omega
satisfying (
3
), if
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\bigcup_{k=n}^{\infty}\hat{{E}}_{k}(\omega)}\neq\emptyset
, then
\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\limsup}~\hat{l}_{n}(\omega)=l_{0}
.

Proof of Lemma
3
.

We fix
\omega\in\Omega
that satisfies (
3
).

We first show that
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\hat{l}_{n}(\omega)\leq l_{0}
. For arbitrary
m_{0}\in M_{0}
, there exist a sequence
{m_{n}}\in{M_{n}(\omega)}
converges to
m_{0}
by ii) in Definition
4
. By (
3
),
F_{n}(\omega)({m_{n}})\rightarrow F(m_{0})
. Since
\hat{l}_{n}(\omega)\leq F_{n}(\omega)({m_{n}})
by definition of
\hat{l}_{n}(\omega)
,
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\hat{l}_{n}(\omega)\leq F(m_{0})
. Since
m_{0}\in M_{0}
was arbitrary, we get
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{l}_{n}(\omega)\leq l_{0}
.

Secondly, we show that
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\bigcup_{k=n}^{\infty}\hat{E}_{k}(\omega)}\subset E_{0}
. It is sufficient to show that
b\in E_{0}
for arbitrary
b\in\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\bigcup_{k=n}^{\infty}\hat{E}_{k}(\omega)}
. We assume that
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\bigcup_{k=n}^{\infty}\hat{E}_{k}(\omega)}
is not empty. (If it is empty, Z-SC is trivial.) To show
b\in E_{0}
, it is enough to check
F({b})=l_{0}
and
{b}\in M_{0}
.

We show that
b\in M_{0}
. Since
b\in\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\bigcup_{k=n}^{\infty}\hat{E}_{k}(\omega)}
, there exist a subsequence
m_{n_{k}}\in\hat{E}_{n_{k}}(\omega)
converges to
b
. By i) in Definition
4
,
{b}\in M_{0}
.
It is trivial that
l_{0}\leq F({b})
since
{b}\in M_{0}
. Moreover,
\hat{l}_{n_{k}}+\varepsilon_{n_{k}}(\omega)\geq F_{n_{k}}(\omega)(m_{n_{k}})\rightarrow F({b})
by (
3
). Thus
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\hat{l}_{n}(\omega)\geq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\hat{l}_{n_{k}}(\omega)=F(b)\geq l_{0}
.

Finally, we get

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\hat{l}_{n}(\omega)\leq l_{0}\leq F(b)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\hat{l}_{n_{k}}(\omega)\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\hat{l}_{n}(\omega)\text{ and }{b}\in M_{0}.

Thus
F({b})=l_{0}=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\hat{l}_{n}(\omega)
and
{b}\in M_{0}
.
∎

The convergence of the objective function
F_{n}
in (
3
) can be ensured by Conditions
2
—
4
, discussed below.

Condition 2
.

(M,d)
is separable and complete. In other words,
M
is a Polish space.

Condition 3
.

For each
t\in T
,
\mathfrak{c}(t,\cdot):M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous.

Condition
2
is a common and essential assumption in the study of the Strong Law of Large Numbers of minimizers of random functions
(
Schötz 2022
)
, and is satisfied when
M
is compact.
Under Conditions
2
and
3
, the behavior of the cost function
\mathfrak{c}
can be further characterized by a countable collection of local infimum and supremum of cost functions, defined by

\pi_{m,r}(t):=\inf_{m^{\prime}\in B(m,r)}(\mathfrak{c}(t,m^{\prime})),\quad\Pi_{m,r}(t):=\sup_{m^{\prime}\in B(m,r)}(\mathfrak{c}(t,m^{\prime}))

for
m\in M,r>0,t\in T,

where
B(m,r)
is the open ball in
M
with center
m
and radius
r>0
. This technique, sometimes called a scalarization of
\mathfrak{c}
, was used in
Korf and Wets 2001
.
Under Conditions
2
and
3
,

\pi_{m,r}(\cdot)
and
\Pi_{m,r}(\cdot)
are measurable functions for all
m\in M
and
r>0

(
Korf and Wets 2001
, Theorem 3.4.(b))
.

We additionally assume that
\pi_{m,r}(X)
and
\Pi_{m,r}(X)
are integrable for some
r_{m}>0
.

Condition 4
.

For every
m\in M
, there exist
r=r_{m}>0
such that
\pi_{m,r}(X)
and
\Pi_{m,r}(X)
are integrable.

Lemma 4
.

Assume Conditions
2
,
3
, and
4
. Then (
3
) holds almost surely.

Before we show Lemma
4
, we list some properties of
\pi_{m,r}(t)
and
\Pi_{m,r}(t)
in Lemma
5
below, which are immediately obtained by the definitions of
\pi_{m,r}(t)
and
\Pi_{m,r}(t)
.

Lemma 5
.

(a)

\pi_{m,r}(t)
increases (and
\Pi_{m,r}(t)
decreases) as
r
decreases.

(b)

Since
\pi_{m,r}(t)
increases (and
\Pi_{m,r}(t)
decreases) as
r
decreases, Condition
4
implies that for all
0<r_{0}\leq r_{m}
,
\pi_{m,r_{0}}(X)
and
\Pi_{m,r_{0}}(X)
are integrable.

(c)

Under Condition
3
,
\pi_{m_{i},r_{i}}(t)\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}(t,m)
and
\Pi_{m_{i},r_{i}}(t)\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}(t,m)
as
m_{i}\rightarrow m
and
r_{i}\rightarrow 0
.

Proof of Lemma
4
.

Since
M
is a separable metric space (Condition
2
), its subset
M_{0}
is also separable
(
Roy 1970
)
and thus there exist a countable dense subset
\tilde{M}_{0}=\{m_{01},m_{02}.\cdots\}
of
M_{0}
.

For each
m\in\tilde{M}_{0}
, let
r_{m}>0
defined as Condition
4
. Note that
\pi_{m,r}(X)
and
\Pi_{m,r}(X)
are integrable for
0<r\leq r_{m}
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{m,r}
be the subset of
\Omega
, defined by

\mathcal{A}_{m,r}:=\{\omega\in\Omega~|~\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{m,r}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}\pi_{m,r}\text{ and }\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Pi_{m,r}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}\Pi_{m,r}\}

for
m\in\tilde{M}_{0}
and
0<r\leq r_{m}
. By strong law of large number
(
Durrett 2019
, Theorem 2.4.1)
,
P(\mathcal{A}_{m,r})=1
.

We will show that for all
\omega\in\mathcal{A}
, (
3
) holds where
\mathcal{A}:=\cup_{m\in\tilde{M}_{0}}\cup_{r\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,r_{m}]}\mathcal{A}_{m_{m,r}}
, which is a countable union of probability one subset of
\Omega
, thus also a probability one subset of
\Omega
. We fix an arbitrary
\omega\in\mathcal{A}
and
m_{0}\in M_{0}
. We also choose a sequence
m_{n}\in M_{n}(\omega)
such that
m_{n}\rightarrow m_{0}
. Such
m_{n}
exists by Condition
1
.

Note that we can choose a sequence
m^{l}\in\tilde{M}_{0}
,
r^{l}\searrow 0
such that
m_{0}\in B(m^{l},r^{l})
,
r^{l}\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,r_{m^{l}}]
,
B(m^{l+1},r^{l+1})\subset B(m^{l},r^{l})
. Since
m_{n}
conveges to
m_{0}
, for all
l\geq 1
, there exists
N\geq 1
and so that
m_{n}\in B(m^{l},r^{l})
for
n\geq N
. Thus, the following holds.

\mathbb{E}\pi_{m^{l},r^{l}}(X)=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{m^{l},r^{l}}(X_{i}(\omega))\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}F_{n}(\omega)(m_{n})

Similarly,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}F_{n}(\omega)(m_{n})\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Pi_{m^{l},r^{l}}(X_{i}(\omega))=\mathbb{E}\Pi_{m^{l},r^{l}}(X)

holds.

Since
m\mapsto\mathfrak{c}(t,m)
is continuous for each
t\in T
(Condition
3
),
\pi_{m^{l},r^{l}}(t)\nearrow\mathfrak{c}(t,m_{0})
as
l\rightarrow\infty
for each
t\in T
. Then
\mathbb{E}\pi_{m^{l},r^{l}}(X)\nearrow\mathbb{E}\mathfrak{c}(X,m_{0})
by Monotone Convergence Theorem
(
Durrett 2019
, Theorem 1.6.6)
. Similarly,
\Pi_{m^{l},r^{l}}(t)\searrow\mathfrak{c}(t,m_{0})
as
l\rightarrow\infty
for each
t\in T
. Since
\Pi_{m^{l},r^{l}}(X)
is dominated by
\Pi_{m^{1},r^{1}}(X)
,
\mathbb{E}\Pi_{m^{l},r^{l}}(X)\searrow\mathbb{E}\mathfrak{c}(X,m_{0})
by Dominated Convergence Theorem
(
Durrett 2019
, Theorem 1.6.7)
.

Finally, we get
{\lim}_{n\rightarrow\infty}F_{n}(\omega)(m_{n})=\mathbb{E}\mathfrak{c}(X,m_{0})
since

\mathbb{E}\mathfrak{c}(X,m_{0})\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}F_{n}(\omega)(m_{n})\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}F_{n}(\omega)(m_{n})\leq\mathbb{E}\mathfrak{c}(X,m_{0}).\qed

,
3
and

Condition 5
.

\hat{E}_{n}
is eventually precompact almost surely.

.

If Conditions

,
2
,

, and

Definition 5

We say that

Assume that

We mention that

Secondly, we show Condition

\mathfrak{c}(X,m^{\prime})=G(\rho(X,m^{\prime}))\leq G(\rho(X,m)+\lvert\rho(X,m)-\rho(X,m^{\prime})\rvert)\leq bG(\rho(X,m))+bG(\lvert\rho(X,m)-\rho(X,m^{\prime})\rvert)\leq bG(\rho(X,m))+bG(1)

Corollary A.5

(Z-SC in
Huckemann et al. 2011
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{c}=\rho^{2}
for
\rho:T\times M\rightarrow[0,\infty)
,
M=M_{0}=M_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
and for all
\omega\in\Omega
,
\varepsilon_{n}=0
for all
n\geq 1
.
Suppose
\rho:T\times M\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a continuous function with respect to the product topology of
T\times M
. Assume that
(M,d)
is separable and
\mathbb{E}\left(\rho(X,p)^{2}\right)<\infty
for all
p\in P
and
\rho
is equicontinuous in the sense of Definition
5
. If additionally 1)
E_{0}\neq\emptyset
, 2)
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}{E_{n}(\omega)}
enjoys the Heine-Borel property almost surely, and 3) the coercivity condition in the Defitnition
A.1
is satisfied, then
\hat{E}_{n}
is a strong consistent estimator of
E_{0}
in the sense of Bhattacharya-Patrangenaru.

We compare Corollary
A.5
and Corollary
3
from two perspectives, conditions and conclusion.

We first compare conditions of Corollary
3
and Corollary
A.5
. Corollary
3
assumes
(M,d)
be separable,
\mathfrak{c}=\rho^{2}
for equicontinuous and coercieve
\rho
,
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}{E_{n}(\omega)}
enjoys the Heine-Borel property almost surely.

In Corollary
3
, almost sure Kuratowski convergence is assumed,
(M,d)
is assumed to be separable and complete,
\mathfrak{c}=H\{\rho\}
for equicontinuous, coercieve
\rho
and continuous, non-
decreasing, additive
G
, and
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}{E_{n}(\omega)}
enjoys the Heine-Borel property almost surely.

Since
M_{0}=M_{n}=M
for all
n\geq 1
, Condition
1
trivially holds by Lemma
2
. Thus it needs a weaker condition than Condition
2
. Condition
3
is also assumed in Theorem
A.3
, with the stronger assumption that
\rho:T\times M\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is continuous with respect to the product topology of
T\times M
. Moreover, Lemma
8
show that
\rho^{2}
satisfies Condition
4
when
\rho
is equicontinuous. Moreover,
\mathfrak{c}=G\circ\rho
includes
\rho^{2}
, thus our theorem handles more wider class of minimizing functions.

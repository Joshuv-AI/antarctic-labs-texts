Abstract
. A complete characterization of parabolic self-maps of finite shift is given in terms of their Herglotz’s representation. This improves a previous result due to Contreras, Díaz-Madrigal, and Pommerenke. We also derive some consequences for the rate of convergence of these functions to their Denjoy-Wolff point, improving a related result of Kourou, Theodosiadis, and Zarvalis for the continuous setting.

1.
Introduction

In the field of Discrete Complex Dynamics of the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}=\{z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:y>0\}
, the main object of study is the behaviour of the sequence of iterates of holomorphic self-maps
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
, that is, the sequence of functions given by
f^{n+1}=f\circ f^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where we define
f^{0}=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{H}}
.

In this paper we focus on non-elliptic self-maps
f
, that is, those with no fixed points on
\mathbb{H}
. The dynamical behaviour of non-elliptic functions is described in the following seminal result:

Theorem 1.1

(Denjoy-Wolff)
.

[
2
, Theorem 3.2.1]

Let
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
be a non-elliptic holomorphic map. Then, there exists
\tau\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
such that
f^{n}\to\tau
uniformly on compact sets of
\mathbb{H}
.

The point
\tau
is commonly known as the Denjoy-Wolff point of
f
, and it acts as a global attractor of its dynamic.

Up to a conjugation with an isomorphism of
\mathbb{H}
, one can always suppose that the Denjoy-Wolff point of a non-elliptic self-map is at infinity. Indeed, this might be useful to find different non-elliptic functions whose behaviour varies. To this extent, we note that under this assumption one has
[
2
, Corollary 2.5.5]

\angle\lim_{z\to\infty}f(z)=\infty,\quad\angle\lim_{z\to\infty}\dfrac{f(z)}{z}=\lambda\in[1,+\infty).

If
\lambda>1
,
f
is known as hyperbolic. This work deals with parabolic functions, that is, those for which
\lambda=1
.

The study of parabolic functions will be done through a representation result which is due to Herglotz
[
1
, Theorem 6.2.1]
: every holomorphic function
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
can uniquely be written as

f(z)=\alpha z+\beta+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1+tz}{t-z}d\mu(t),\quad z\in\mathbb{H},

where
\alpha\geq 0
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu
is a positive finite measure on
\mathbb{R}
.

Using
[
11
, Chapter 5, Lemma 2]
, one can deduce the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

A holomorphic self-map
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
is parabolic with Denjoy-Wolff point at infinity if and only if it can be written as

(1)

f(z)=z+\beta+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1+tz}{t-z}d\mu(t),\quad z\in\mathbb{H},

where
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu
is a positive finite measure on
\mathbb{R}
, both of them not simultaneously null.

Following the latter result, every property of
f
should be mirrored on the corresponding representing parameters
\beta
and
\mu
: this is the idea behind this paper. In particular, we are interested in characterizing the following property:

Definition 1.3
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
be a holomorphic self-map which is parabolic and whose Denjoy-Wolff point is at infinity. The self-map
f
is said of finite (respectively, infinite) shift if there exists an initial point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
for which the orbit
z_{n}=x_{n}+iy_{n}=f^{n}(z_{0})
is such that
\sup_{n}y_{n}<+\infty
(respectively,
\sup_{n}y_{n}=+\infty
).

Remark 1.4
.

[
9
, Proposition 3.2]

The latter definition does not depend on the initial point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
.

Functions of finite shift have attracted much attention in the area. For example, a characterization of this property in terms of angular regularity of the functions is given in
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
. Poggi-Corradini also used a related property to work with backward orbits; see
[
14
]
. There are some references in the continuous version of Complex Dynamics (that is, the theory of holomorphic semigroups) as well; see
[
7
]
for an introduction to this topic. For example, in
[
3
, Theorem 3]
,
[
12
]
, and
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
, the authors characterize parabolic semigroups of finite shift in terms of different geometric quantities.

Beyond functions of finite shift, in
[
8
]
, Theorem
1.2
has also proved to be useful to characterize the hyperbolic step of parabolic functions (see Section
2
for a definition). Indeed, many of the tools of this work have been previously derived there.

The main result of this work is the following, which completely characterizes self-maps of finite shift:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
be a parabolic map with its Denjoy-Wolff point at infinity. If
f
is of finite shift, then
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|t\right|d\mu(t)<+\infty
.

Moreover,
f
is of finite shift if and only if one of the following holds:

(i)

\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left|t\right|d\mu(t)<+\infty
,
\int_{(0,+\infty)}t^{2}d\mu(t)<+\infty
, and
\beta>\int_{\mathbb{R}}td\mu(t)
.

(ii)

\int_{(-\infty,0)}t^{2}d\mu(t)<+\infty
,
\int_{(0,+\infty)}\left|t\right|d\mu(t)<+\infty
, and
\beta<\int_{\mathbb{R}}td\mu(t)
.

The proof of the first part of this result is given in Subsection
3.1
. Then, in Subsection
3.2
, the second part is deduced as a consequence of different results in
[
9
,
8
]
. Notice that this result improves the description of functions of finite shift given in
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
, as noted in Subsection
3.3
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.5
, we obtain the following result:

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
be a parabolic map of finite shift with its Denjoy-Wolff point at infinity. Then

\dfrac{f^{n}}{n}\to\beta-\int_{\mathbb{R}}td\mu(t),\quad n\to+\infty,

uniformly on compact sets of
\mathbb{H}
.

We end this paper with a discussion on the rate of convergence of parabolic functions of finite shift to its Denjoy-Wolff point. This issue has been extensively discussed in
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
under the general setting of continuous dynamics. In the specific setting of parabolic semigroups of finite shift, better estimates for the rate of convergence have been obtained in
[
13
]
.

To introduce this topic in detail, we should move from the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
into the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. Using a Möbius map
S\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{H}
, this can be done by relating any non-elliptic self-map
g\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
with a self-map
f=S\circ g\circ S^{-1}\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
whose Denjoy-Wolff point is infinity.

Definition
1.3
can be translated into the setting a self-map
g
of the unit disk, meaning that
g
is of finite shift if there exists some orbit of
g
and a horocycle (see
[
2
, Definition 2.1.1]
) of center
\tau
in which the orbit does not enter. If this is not the case,
g
is said of infinite shift. Moreover, Theorem
1.1
also remains valid. The point
\tau=S^{-1}(\infty)\in\partial\mathbb{D}
is called the Denjoy-Wolff of
g
and it follows that
g^{n}\to\tau
as
n\to+\infty
uniformly on compact sets of
\mathbb{D}
. By discussing the rate of convergence to the Denjoy-Wolff point we mean to estimate how fast the latter convergence is. To this extent, we prove the next result in Section
4
:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
g\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a parabolic map of finite shift whose Denjoy-Wolff point is
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then, there exists
C=C(g)>0
such that

n\left|g^{n}-\tau\right|\to C,\quad n\to+\infty,

uniformly on compact sets of
\mathbb{D}
.

As we will see at the end, this result can be used to improve the rate of convergence given in
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
.

2.
Preliminaries: parabolic dynamics.

Parabolic self-maps are further classified in terms of their hyperbolic step. To define it, let
\rho
be the pseudo-hyperbolic distance on
\mathbb{H}
. For any initial point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
, the Scharwz-Pick Lemma assures that the sequence
\rho(f^{n+1}(z_{0}),f^{n}(z_{0}))
is non-increasing with respect to
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, it must converge. Indeed, if it converges to zero, then it does so for every initial point
[
2
, Corollary 4.6.9.(i)]
. Thus, the following definition is given:

Definition 2.1
.

A parabolic self-map
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
is said of zero hyperbolic step if
\rho(f^{n+1}(z_{0}),f^{n}(z_{0}))\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
for some (every)
z_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
. In another case, it is said of positive hyperbolic step.

Determining the hyperbolic step of a given function can be a subtle task. However, some theoretical results are known:

Theorem 2.2
.

[
15
]

Let
z_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
be an initial point, and consider its orbit
z_{n}=x_{n}+iy_{n}=f^{n}(z_{0})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The following limit

b=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{x_{n+1}-x_{n}}{y_{n}}\in\mathbb{R}

exists. Furthermore,
b=0
if and only if
f
is of zero hyperbolic step. Also,
z_{n+1}/z_{n}\to 1
(see
[
15
, Eq. (3.16)]
), and
y_{n+1}/y_{n}\to 1
(see
[
15
, Eq. (3.17)]
).

Theorem 2.3
.

[
9
, Proposition 3.3]

Parabolic self-maps of finite shift are also of positive hyperbolic step.

Remark 2.4
.

There are also self-maps of infinite shift and positive hyperbolic step. For examples of this with the aid of the representation of Theorem
1.2
, we refer to
[
8
, Theorem 1.6]
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.5
and Corollary
1.6

In this section, from now on, the function
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
will always be a holomorphic self-map which is parabolic and whose Denjoy-Wolff point is at infinity. Indeed, with the aid of Theorem
1.2
, we will identify
f
with
(\beta,\mu)
.

3.1.
Proof of the first part of Theorem
1.5
.

Assume that
f
is of finite shift and let us show that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|t\right|d\mu(t)<+\infty
. Notice that, by Theorem
2.3
,
f
is of positive hyperbolic step. In particular, Theorem
2.2
assures that for a given initial point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
, the orbit
z_{n}=x_{n}+iy_{n}=f^{n}(z_{0})
is such that

(2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{x_{n+1}-x_{n}}{y_{n}}=L\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

But, as
f
is of finite shift, there must exists
Y>0
such that

(3)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}y_{n}=Y.

Thus,
x_{n+1}-x_{n}\to LY=:\Delta\neq 0
. In the following, we will suppose that
\Delta>0
(otherwise, the proof will apply with slight modifications). In that case, Stolz’s lemma assures that

(4)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\dfrac{x_{n}}{n\Delta}=1.

Under this setting, we can show the following:

Lemma 3.1
.

\int_{(0,+\infty)}t^{2}d\mu(t)<+\infty
.

Proof.

First of all, notice that

y_{n+1}=y_{n}\left(1+\dfrac{y_{n+1}-y_{n}}{y_{n}}\right)=y_{0}\prod_{k=0}^{n}\left(1+\dfrac{y_{k+1}-y_{k}}{y_{k}}\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Thus, the following are equivalent:

(i)

y_{n}
is bounded,

(ii)

\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{y_{n+1}-y_{n}}{y_{n}}\right)<+\infty,

(iii)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{y_{n+1}-y_{n}}{y_{n}}<+\infty.

But, from (
1
),

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{y_{n+1}-y_{n}}{y_{n}}=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\mathrm{Im}(f(z_{n})-z_{n})}{y_{n}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{1+t^{2}}{(t-x_{n})^{2}+y_{n}^{2}}\right)d\mu(t).

Using (
4
), let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

\dfrac{\Delta}{2}\leq x_{n+1}-x_{n}\leq\dfrac{3\Delta}{2},\quad n\geq N.

Thus, for every
t>x_{N}
one can find
k=k(t)\geq N
such that
\left|t-x_{k}\right|\leq\Delta
. In that case, using also (
3
),

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\dfrac{1+t^{2}}{(t-x_{n})^{2}+y_{n}^{2}}\geq\dfrac{1+t^{2}}{\Delta^{2}+y_{k}^{2}}\geq\dfrac{1+t^{2}}{\Delta^{2}+Y^{2}},\quad t>x_{N}.

It follows that,

+\infty>\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{1+t^{2}}{(t-x_{n})^{2}+y_{n}^{2}}\right)d\mu(t)\geq\int_{(x_{N},+\infty)}\dfrac{1+t^{2}}{\Delta^{2}+Y^{2}}d\mu(t).

Since
\mu
is a positive finite measure, the result follows.
∎

To continue with the proof of the first part of Theorem
1.5
, let us proceed by supposing that

(5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|t\right|d\mu(t)=+\infty.

In that case, by Lemma
3.1
, it has to be that

\int_{(-\infty,0)}\left|t\right|d\mu(t)=+\infty.

Under this hypothesis, we will prove that
x_{n+1}-x_{n}\to+\infty
, which contradicts (
4
), therefore showing that (
5
) cannot hold. Thus, the result will follow.

To do so, write
f
as

f(z)=z+\tilde{\beta}+p_{1}(z)+p_{0}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{H},

where

\omega(A)=\mu(A\cap(-\infty,0]),\quad\dfrac{d\nu}{d\mu}(t)=(1+t^{2})\chi_{(0,+\infty)}(t),

and

\tilde{\beta}=\beta-\int_{(0,+\infty)}td\mu(t)\in\mathbb{R},\quad p_{1}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1+tz}{t-z}d\omega(t),\quad p_{0}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{d\nu(t)}{t-z},\quad z\in\mathbb{H}.

Note that, by Lemma
3.1
,
\tilde{\beta}
is well-defined and the measure
\nu
is finite. Indeed, if
y_{0}\geq 1
, notice that

\left|\text{Re}(p_{0}(z_{n}))\right|\leq\left|p_{0}(z_{n})\right|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{d\nu(t)}{\left|t-z_{n}\right|}\leq\dfrac{\nu(\mathbb{R})}{y_{n}}\leq\nu(\mathbb{R}),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, it suffices to prove that
\mathrm{Re}(p_{1}(z_{n}))\to+\infty
. To see this, notice that
p_{1}
is a well-defined function on
\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
. Indeed, write

\text{Re}(p_{1}(x+iy))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{t-x+t^{2}x-t(x^{2}+y^{2})}{(t-x)^{2}+y^{2}}d\omega(t),

and notice that

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}\left(\dfrac{t-x+t^{2}x-t(x^{2}+y^{2})}{(t-x)^{2}+y^{2}}\right)=-\dfrac{2y(1+t^{2})(t-x)}{((t-x)^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}\geq 0,

whenever
t\leq 0<x
,
y\geq 0
. Thus, by (
4
), find
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x_{n}>1
for all
n\geq N
and notice that

\mathrm{Re}(p_{1}(z_{n}))\geq\mathrm{Re}(p_{1}(x_{n}))=\int_{(-\infty,0]}\dfrac{1+tx_{n}}{t-x_{n}}d\omega(t),

which is well-defined. Since
x_{n}\to+\infty
, the proof follows from the fact that

(6)

\lim_{x\to+\infty}\int_{(-\infty,0]}\dfrac{1+tx}{t-x}d\omega(t)=+\infty.

To prove (
6
), consider
x>0
and notice that the integrand is positive if and only if
t<-1/x
. By Fatou’s lemma, one has

\liminf_{x\to+\infty}\int_{(-\infty,-1/x)}\dfrac{1+tx}{t-x}d\omega(t)\geq\int_{(-\infty,0)}(-t)d\omega(t)=+\infty,

where we have used (
5
). On the other hand,

\int_{[-1/x,0]}\left|\dfrac{1+tx}{t-x}\right|d\omega(t)\leq\dfrac{1}{x}\omega([-1/x,0])\to 0,\quad x\to+\infty.

Thus, (
6
) holds.

3.2.
Proof of the second part of Theorem
1.5
.

Recall the following result:

Proposition 3.2
.

[
8
, Proposition 3.4]

Let
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
be a parabolic self-map with its Denjoy-Wolff point at infinity such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|t\right|d\mu(t)<+\infty
. Then,
f
is of positive hyperbolic step if and only if it is of finite shift.

Thus, the second part of Theorem
1.5
follows from Subsection
3.1
and
[
8

.

3.3.
Further remarks.

Let us recall that functions of finite shift are also in connection with self-maps enjoying some regularity in an angular sense. To introduce these ideas, the following definition is given in
[
9
]
:

Definition 3.3
.

Consider a holomorphic map
\phi\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
with a boundary fixed point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, that is,

\angle\lim_{z\to\tau}\phi(z)=\tau.

Then,
\phi
is said to be of angular-class of order
p\in\mathbb{N}
at
\tau
, denoted as
\phi\in C_{A}^{p}(\tau)
, if there exist
c_{1},\ldots,c_{p}
and a holomorphic function
\gamma\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\phi(z)=\tau+\sum_{k=1}^{p}\dfrac{c_{k}}{k!}(z-\tau)^{k}+\gamma(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},\quad\angle\lim_{z\to\tau}\dfrac{\gamma(z)}{(z-\tau)^{p}}=0.

In particular, as a consequence of Julia-Wolff-Carathéodory Theorem
[
2
, Corollary 2.5.5]
, every non-elliptic function is of angular-class of first order at its Denjoy-Wolff point.

These angular-classes are intimately linked to functions of finite shift, as the following result suggests:

Theorem 3.4
.

[
9
, Theorem 4.1]

Let
\phi\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a parabolic self-map of positive hyperbolic step with Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then,
\phi
is of finite shift if and only if
\phi\in C_{A}^{2}(\tau)
.

Theorem
1.5
can be seen as a refinement of the latter result. To see this, one should link
[
9
, Propositions 2.1]
and
[
8
, Lemma 3.3]
to state the following:

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
be a parabolic self-map with its Denjoy-Wolff point at infinity such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|t\right|d\mu(t)<+\infty
. Consider the self-map
\phi\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
which is conjugated to
f
and has Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then,
\phi\in C_{A}^{2}(\tau)
.

However, the converse is not true. These remarks have already been noticed in
[
8
, p. 15]
.

3.4.
Proof of Corollary
1.6
.

We will prove that there exists a non-empty open set
U\subset\mathbb{H}
such that

\dfrac{f^{n}(z)}{n}\to\beta-\int_{\mathbb{R}}td\mu(t),\quad n\to+\infty,

for all
z\in U
. Then, the result will follow from the identity principle and the fact that the sequence
\{f^{n}/n\}
is a normal family.

To do so, as
f
is of finite shift, let us suppose that
f
is as in Theorem
1.5
.(i) (if it is as in Theorem
1.5
.(ii), the proof will also work with slight modifications). Write
f
as

f(z)=z+\beta-\int_{\mathbb{R}}td\mu(t)+p(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{H}.

By
[
8
, Lemma 3.9]
, there exists
a,b>0
such that
p(f^{n}(z))\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
if

z\in\Omega:=\{z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:x>a,\,y>b\}.

In particular, if
z\in\Omega
, it follows that

\mathrm{Re}(f^{n+1}(z))-\mathrm{Re}(f^{n}(z))\to\beta-\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\mu(t),\quad n\to+\infty.

Applying Stolz’s lemma, it follows that

\dfrac{\mathrm{Re}(f^{n}(z))}{n}\to\beta-\int_{\mathbb{R}}td\mu(t),\quad n\to+\infty.

Moreover, since
f
is of finite shift,
\mathrm{Im}(f^{n}(z))
is bounded, and so
\mathrm{Im}(f^{n})/n\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
. Then, the result follows.

4.
Application: rate of convergence on the unit disk

This section aims to develop a proof of Theorem
1.7
and to discuss its implications on the theory of continuous dynamics. To do so, recall that any self-map
g\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
whose Denjoy-Wolff point is
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
can be conjugated to a self-map
f=S\circ g\circ S^{-1}\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
whose Denjoy-Wolff point is infinity, where
S\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{H}
is the Möbius map given by

S(z)=i\dfrac{\tau+z}{\tau-z},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},\quad S^{-1}(w)=\tau\dfrac{w-i}{w+i},\quad w\in\mathbb{H}.

From this, a computation shows:

Lemma 4.1
.

With the above notations,

g^{n}(z)-\tau=\dfrac{-2i\tau}{f^{n}(S(z))+i},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

It follows from Lemma
4.1
and Corollary
1.6
, having in mind that
f^{n}(S(z))\to\infty
as
n\to+\infty
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

4.2.
Continuous dynamics.

The uniform convergence on compact sets given in Corollary
1.6
lets us relate the “discrete” rate of convergence in Theorem
1.7
with its analogous “continuous” one. That is, the next result (which improves
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
and complements
[
5
, Theorem 1.(b)]
) can also be derived:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\{\phi_{t}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}\}
be a parabolic semigroup of self-maps of finite shift whose Denjoy-Wolff point is
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. There exists
C=C(\phi_{1})>0
such that

t\left|\phi_{t}-\tau\right|\to C,\quad t\to+\infty,

uniformly on compact sets of
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

Consider
g=\phi_{1}
. Notice that
g
is a parabolic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
which is of finite shift and has Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, for every
t>0
, consider the decomposition
t=n+s
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in[0,1)
. Notice that, given
z\in\mathbb{D}
, one has

\min_{z\in K}\left|g^{n}(K)-\tau\right|\leq\left|\phi_{t}(z)-\tau\right|\leq\max_{z\in K}\left|g^{n}(K)-\tau\right|,

where
K=\{\phi_{s}(z):s\in[0,1]\}\subset\mathbb{D}
is a compact set. Since
n=n(t)
and
t/n\to 1
as
t\to+\infty
, the result follows from Theorem
1.7
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The constant in Theorem
1.7
(respectively, in Theorem
4.2
) is

C=\dfrac{2}{\left|\beta-\int_{\mathbb{R}}td\mu(t)\right|},

where
\beta
and
\mu
are the representing parameters in terms of Theorem

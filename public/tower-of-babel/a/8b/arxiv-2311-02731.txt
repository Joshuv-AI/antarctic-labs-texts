Abstract
We define two types of Witten’s zeta functions according to Cartan’s classification of compact symmetric spaces. The type II is the original Witten zeta function constructed by means of irreducible representations of the simple compact Lie group U. The type I Witten zeta functions, we introduce here, are related to the irreducible spherical representations of U. They arise in the harmonic analysis on compact symmetric spaces of the form U/K, where K is the maximal subgroup of U. To construct the type I zeta function we calculate the partition functions of 2d YM theory with broken gauge symmetry using the Migdal-Witten approach. We prove that for the rank one symmetric spaces the generating series for the values of the type I functions with integer arguments can be defined in terms of the generating series of the Riemann zeta-function.

Introduction

Let
U
be a compact simple Lie group and
\Sigma_{g}
is a curve of genus
g
.
Witten in
[
18
]
calculated the volume of the moduli space of flat
U
-bundles over

\Sigma_{g}
. To do this he constructed the partition function of 2d Yang-Mills theory.
The calculations in the 2d Yang-Mills theory had been done previously
[
4
,
10
,
13
]
.
A crucial point in Witten’s calculations is the A. Migdal’s lattice approximation
of the functional integral. As a result
the partition function takes the form of series over dominant weights

\Gamma^{+}(U)
of irreducible representations of
U
.
As the coupling constant of YM theory tends to zero this series becomes what Zagier
called the Witten zeta function (WZF)
[
19
]
.
It can be regarded as a group theoretical generalisation of the
Riemann zeta function, since
for
U=SU(2)
the WZF coincides with it.
Note that for
U=
SU
(3)
it is related to the Mordell-Tornheim zeta function.
In recent years, the Witten zeta function has been the subject of muny mathematical works

[
11
]
.

Simple compact groups are a special class of compact symmetric spaces.
According to the Cartan classification of symmetric spaces
[
7
]
they are
called the type II symmetric spaces. Therefore, we call these zeta functions type II.
Another class of compact symmetric spaces are the coset spaces
X=U/K
,
where
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
U
. These are called the type I symmetric spaces.
The Witten zeta function of type I is the main object of our investigation.
It is defined by series over the dominant class-one representations
\Gamma^{+}(U/K)
associated with
the pair
(U,K)
.
With this construction we
bring the type I WZF into correspondence with some symmetric spaces such as
spheres, projective spaces, Grassmannians and so on (see Tables 1 and 2 below). In particular, the type I
WZF of the sphere
S^{2}
is the Hurwitz zeta function
\zeta(s,\frac{1}{2})
.
To construct the type I WZF we calculate partition functions of 2d YM theory with broken gauge symmetry using the Migdal-Witten approach. Finally, we prove that for the rank one
symmetric spaces the type I the generating series for
the values of WZF with integer arguments can be defined in terms of
the generating series of the Riemann zeta-function.

It should be noted that there are closed objects to the Witten zeta functions.
The Witten zeta functions are related to the degeneracy of the spectrum of the
Laplace-Beltrami operators on a compact symmetric spaces.
Another zeta functions related to the spectrum of these operators are the Minakshisundaram-Pleijel zeta-functions
[
12
]
.
The properties of these functions have been studied in
[
1
,
2
,
9
,
14
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In next section we explain briefly Migdal-Witten construction. In Section 3 we define the type I
models. In Section 4 we consider the class one representations. They determine the type I zeta functions.
Finally, in Section 5 we investigate in details the rank one symmetric spaces and construct
the generating functions for the values of the type I zeta functions at integer arguments.

2d Yang-Mills theory and Witten zeta functions

In this section we briefly recall Witten’s construction of zeta functions associated with simple compact groups.
Let
U
be a compact simple Lie group and
\mathcal{P}
is a a principal
U
-bundle over
\Sigma_{g}
.
Consider the 2d Yang-Mills
U
theory on the curve
\Sigma_{g}

[
4
,
10
,
13
,
18
]

It means that we
consider an adjoint-bundle
{\rm ad}(\mathcal{P})
over
\Sigma_{g}
equipped with connection

d+A

The 2d YM action has the form

I(e)=-\frac{1}{2e^{2}}\int d\mu\,{\rm tr}(F,F)\,,

(2.1)

where
d\mu
is a measure on
\Sigma_{g}
and
F
is the curvature divided on the volume two-form
on
\Sigma_{g}
.

The partition function is defined by the functional integral

Z(U,\Sigma_{g},e)=\int DA\exp-I(e)\,.

(2.2)

This functional integral was defined
by a lattice approximation by Migdal
[
10
]
for
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
by Witten
[
18
]
for general Riemann surface. It takes the following form.
Let
R=\{\alpha\}
be a system of roots corresponding to the group
U
and
\Gamma(U)=\{\nu\}
is the weight lattice of irreducible unitary representations of
U
in the finite-dimensional spaces
V_{\nu}

\Gamma(U)=\{\nu\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\,|\,2(\nu,\alpha)/(\alpha,\alpha)\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}

(2.3)

and
\Gamma^{+}(U)
is the subset of the dominate weights

\Gamma^{+}(U)=\{\nu\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\,|\,2(\nu,\alpha)/(\alpha,\alpha)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}\}\,.

(2.4)

The partition function of theory takes the form

Z^{II}(U,\Sigma_{g},q)=\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma^{+}(U)}\frac{q^{c_{2}(\nu)}}{(d^{II}_{\nu})^{2g-2}}\

(2.5)

Here
c_{2}(\nu)
is the eigenvalue of the second Casimir operator,
q=\exp(-\varrho)
,
\varrho=Vol\,(\Sigma_{g})
, and
d_{\nu}=\dim\,V_{\nu}

d_{\nu}=\prod_{\alpha\in R^{+}}d_{\alpha}(\nu)\,,~~d_{\alpha}(\nu)=\frac{(\nu+\rho,\alpha)}{(\rho,\alpha)}

For
q=1

(\varrho\to 0)

Z^{II}(U,\Sigma_{g},1)=\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma^{+}(U)}\frac{1}{(d_{\nu})^{2g-2}}

(2.6)

is the volume of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(U,\Sigma_{g})
of flat
U
-bundles
over
\Sigma_{g}

[
18
]
.
The generalization of (
2.6
)

\zeta^{II}_{U}(s)=\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma^{+}(U)}\frac{1}{(d_{\nu})^{s}}\,,~~s\in{\mathbb{C}}\,.

(2.7)

is called by Zagier the
Witten zeta-functions

[
19
]
. In particular,

\zeta^{II}_{SU_{2}}(s)
is the Riemann zeta-function. We introduce the superscript II to distinguish it from the defined below type I zeta-functions (
3.15
).

2.0.1
Brief explanation of (
2.5
)

To define (
2.5
), we cover
\Sigma_{g}
with polygons. In the limit as the
lattice becomes finer, the desired continuum theory can be recovered.

Let
\gamma
enumerates the edges of polygons and
i
run over all plaquettes (the interior part of the polygon). Fix a plaquette
w
and let
u_{\gamma_{i}}=u_{i}
be a map of the edge
\gamma_{i}
of
the plaquette
w
to the group
U
.

Consider the holonomy

\mathcal{U}_{w}=u_{1}\ldots u_{k}

around the plaquette
w
. The lattice gauge transformations acts on
\mathcal{U}_{w}

by the conjugation. In particular,
the trace
{\rm tr}(\mathcal{U}_{w})
is gauge invariant. It is a function on the group
U

and
it can be expanded in the basis of
characters
\chi_{\nu}(u)
of irreducible representations. According with
[
10
,
18
]

define the function on the plaquette
w

\mathcal{G}^{II}_{w}(\mathcal{U}_{w},\varrho_{w})=\mathcal{G}^{II}(u_{i},\varrho_{i})=\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma^{+}(U)}d_{\nu}\chi_{\nu}(\mathcal{U}_{w})\exp(-\varrho_{w}c_{2}(\nu)/2)\,,

(2.8)

The functional integral (
2.2
) is approximated by the sum

Z^{II}(U,\Sigma_{g})=\int\prod_{\gamma}du_{\gamma}\prod_{i}\mathcal{G}^{II}(u_{i},\varrho_{i})\,.

(2.9)

This formula can be justified by two arguments.
First, consider its behavior for a small plaquette as
\varrho_{w}\to 0
.
Since the delta functional

\int_{U}du\delta(u)f(u)=f(Id)
has the expansion

\delta(u)=\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma^{+}(U)}d_{\nu}\chi_{\nu}(u)

(2.10)

the factor
\mathcal{G}_{w}(\mathcal{U}_{w},\varrho_{w})
provides the correct continuum limit

\lim_{\varrho_{w}\to 0}\mathcal{G}^{II}_{w}(\mathcal{U}_{w},\varrho_{w})=\delta(\mathcal{U}_{w}-Id)\,.

Moreover, the factor
\mathcal{G}_{w}(\mathcal{U}_{w},\varrho_{w})
defines a theory invariant
under subdivision. The proof of this fact in
[
18
]
is based on the orthogonality relation for the characters

\int_{U}\chi_{\nu}(u)\overline{\chi}_{\mu}(uv^{-1})du=\delta_{\mu\nu}\frac{1}{d_{\nu}}\chi_{\nu}(v)\,.

(2.11)

We also need another important properties of characters (see section 3.3.1)

\int_{U}\chi_{\nu}(uAu^{-1}B)du=\frac{1}{d_{\nu}}\chi_{\nu}(A)\chi_{\nu}(B)\,.

(2.12)

\int_{U}\chi_{\mu}(Au)\chi_{\nu}(u^{-1}B)du=\delta_{\mu\nu}\frac{1}{d_{\nu}}\chi_{\nu}(AB)\,.

(2.13)

From (
2.8
) we obtain

Z^{II}(U,\Sigma_{g})=\sum_{\nu\in\Gamma^{+}(U)}d_{\nu}e^{-\rho c_{2}(\nu)/2}\int du_{i}dv_{j}\chi_{\nu}(u_{1}v_{1}u_{1}^{-1}v_{1}^{-1}\cdots u_{g}v_{g}u_{g}^{-1}v_{g}^{-1})\,.

(2.14)

It follows from (
2.12
) that every time one integrates over
u_{i}
or
v_{j}
one gets a factor of
1/d_{\nu}
.
On the last step we come to the integral

\int_{U}du_{g}\chi_{\nu}(u_{g})\chi_{\nu}(u_{g}^{-1})=\chi_{\nu}(1)=d_{\nu}\,.

Putting it all together we come to (
2.5
).

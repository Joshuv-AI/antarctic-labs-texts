Partial Regularity for the three-dimensional Stochastic Ericksen–Leslie equations
Hengrong Du, Chuntian Wang

Abstract
. In this article, we investigate the existence of martingale suitable weak solutions to 3D stochastic Ericksen–Leslie equations driven by an external random force. Despite the inherent white noise, we prove that paths of martingale suitable weak solutions exhibit singular points of one-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure zero. Our approach is mainly based on two ideas: a local energy-preserving approximation which serves to establish the existence of suitable weak solutions, and a blow-up argument employed to achieve the partial regularity.



1.
Introduction

Liquid crystal, an anisotropic phase situated between the isotropic liquid phase and solid phase, has long intrigued scientists from various disciplines. Among many types of liquid crystal states, the nematic phase is particularly notable for its characteristic that in this kind of material, the rod-shaped molecules display the orientational order (tending to point in the same direction) without positional order.
The hydrodynamics of nematic liquid crystals has been a subject of extensive research, see for instance
[
25
,
38
,
49
,
50
]
.
A fundamental mathematical model
for describing hydrodynamics in nematic liquid
crystal flows is
provided by
the Ericksen–Leslie equations

[
16
,
17
,
33
]
.

The Ericksen–Leslie equations combine momentum balance, typically captured by the Navier–Stokes equation
[
21
,
45
,
46
]
, with the evolution of molecular orientation, characterized by the heat flow of harmonic maps
[
36
]
.
In this article, we focus on the
simplified
version of the full Ericksen–Leslie equations,
which was initially studied by Lin and Liu

[
34
,
35
]
, driven by a random force:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\,\mathrm{d}{{\bf{u}}}-\Delta{{\bf{u}}}\,\mathrm{d}t+{{\bf{u}}}\cdot\nabla{{\bf{u}}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\nabla P\,\mathrm{d}t=-\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\nabla{{\bf{d}}}\odot\nabla{{\bf{d}}})\,\mathrm{d}t+\,\mathrm{d}W,\\
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits{{\bf{u}}}=0,\\
\,\mathrm{d}{{\bf{d}}}-\Delta{{\bf{d}}}\,\mathrm{d}t+{{\bf{u}}}\cdot\nabla{{\bf{d}}}\,\mathrm{d}t=-f_{\epsilon}({{\bf{d}}})\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{array}\right.

Here the unknown triple

\displaystyle({{\bf{u}}},P,{{\bf{d}}})
represents the fluid velocity field, the pressure function, and the director field which characterizes the mean orientation of molecules, and
\displaystyle f_{\epsilon}({{\bf{d}}})=\nabla_{{\bf{d}}}F_{\epsilon}({{\bf{d}}})
, where

F_{\epsilon}({{\bf{d}}})=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}(|{{\bf{d}}}|^{2}-1)^{2}.

It is evident that (
1.1
)
1,2
is a forced Stokes system with the so-called Ericksen stress tensor
\displaystyle(\nabla{{\bf{d}}}\odot\nabla{{\bf{d}}})_{ij}=\partial_{x_{i}}{{\bf{d}}}\cdot\partial_{x_{j}}{{\bf{d}}}
and a random force
\displaystyle W
which
will
be specified later, while (
1.1
)
3
is a transported semi-linear heat flow associated to the Ginzburg–Landau potential function
\displaystyle F_{\epsilon}({{\bf{d}}})
.
The ultimate goal is to study the singular limit as
\displaystyle\epsilon\to 0
in (
1.1
)
3
, which leads to the following transported heat flow of harmonic maps for the director field
\displaystyle{\bf{d}}
:

\,\mathrm{d}{{\bf{d}}}-\Delta{{\bf{d}}}\,\mathrm{d}t+{{\bf{u}}}\cdot\nabla{{\bf{d}}}\,\mathrm{d}t=|\nabla{{\bf{d}}}|^{2}{{\bf{d}}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

It is well-established that the critical dimension
for heat flow of
harmonic maps
is two

[
8
,
9
]
. Consequently, in 2D, it has been
confirmed that solutions to the deterministic (limited) Ericksen–Leslie
equation are partially regular
[
37
]
, and singularities
could occur

,
30
]
. Moreover, the singular limit as

\displaystyle\epsilon\to 0
has been justified through concentration-cancellation

[

]
, as also discussed in
[
14
]
. However,
in 3D, the well-posedness for the deterministic (limited) Ericksen–Leslie equation
remains an open problem with only a few partial results available,
such as those presented in
[
39
,
47
]
.

Despite vast success in studies of the deterministic Ericksen–Leslie equations, it is worth noting that real-world scenarios introduce complexities that a deterministic framework
could
possibly
overlook, including environmental disturbances
[
10
]
, measurement uncertainties, and inherent thermal fluctuations
[
3
,
31
]
. To address these intricacies, a typical way is to introduce a stochastic component into the Ericksen–Leslie equations,
which leads
to the formulation of the
stochastic Ericksen–Leslie
(SEL) equations.
A probable consequence of the stochastic force in the nematic liquid crystal flows is its potential impact on the emergence of singularities, which can manifest as either turbulence in fluid flow or defects in molecular alignments.
These phenomena
carry
substantial implications, impacting our understanding of theory and the practical applications of liquid crystal materials.

To this end,
in this article, we study the SEL equations on the three-dimensional tori
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{3}
driven by
additive white noise. Despite a recent growing interest in the SEL equations, to our best knowledge, there have been no reported results regarding the regularity of
the SEL equations in three dimensions. This research gap persists
even when
\displaystyle\epsilon
in (
1
) is held fixed rather than tending towards zero, as mentioned in

[
4
,
5
]
.
It’s worth mentioning that well-posedness results concerning the (limited) SEL equations
in two dimensions can be found in
[
12
]
(with additive noise), and in
[
48
]
(with multiplicative noise); for further details,
one may refer to
[
6
,
16
,
44
]
.
Here
our main objectives are two-fold.
Firstly, we introduce the concept and establish the existence of martingale
suitable
weak solutions to (
1.1
). Since
\displaystyle\epsilon
plays no role in our analysis, from now on we fix
\displaystyle\epsilon=1
and omit it from
\displaystyle F_{\epsilon}
and
\displaystyle f_{\epsilon}
. It is worth noting that the concept of martingale solutions is equivalent to that of statistical solutions, a topic extensively studied in statistical fluid dynamics (see for instance
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
).
Secondly, we demonstrate that, with
certain assumptions on the white noise, the set of singular points, where solutions became unbounded, cannot have a positive one-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure.

The article is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we specify notations and stochastic framework to set up the SEL equations. Then in Section
3
we
introduce a notion of suitable weak solutions,
drawing inspiration
from

[
20
,
42
]

in the context of the

This approach involves splitting
solutions to the SEL equations
into two parts. The first part is
governed by

Then in Section
4

Finally
in Section
5
we establish the partial regularity of martingale suitable weak solutions.

2.1.
Functional spaces

We introduce two fundamental spaces that take into account the incompressibility condition (

H:=\{{\bf{u}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})|\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits{\bf{u}}=0\},

The operator

\displaystyle Au=-P_{L}\Delta{\bf{u}}
, where

\displaystyle 0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{m}\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\lambda_{m}\to\infty

\displaystyle m\to\infty

The fractional power
\displaystyle A^{\alpha}
of
\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\alpha\geq 0

A^{\alpha}{\bf{u}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}e_{i}({\bf{u}},e_{i})_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})},

with domain

Also we note that
\displaystyle D(A^{\alpha})\subset H^{2\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})

\displaystyle H^{2\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})

5.
Partial regularity

5.1.
Blowing up analysis

First, we recall the maximum principle for the
\displaystyle L^{\infty}
of
\displaystyle{\bf{d}}
from the transported Ginzburg–Landau heat flow:

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}{{\bf{d}}}+{{\bf{u}}}\cdot\nabla{{\bf{d}}}=\Delta{{\bf{d}}}-f({{\bf{d}}}),\\
\nabla\cdot{\bf{u}}=0,\\
{{\bf{d}}}(0)={{\bf{d}}}_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
39
]
)
.

For
\displaystyle T>0
, assume that
\displaystyle{{\bf{u}}}\in L^{2}(0,T;V)
and
\displaystyle{{\bf{d}}}_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{R}^{3})
with

\displaystyle|{{\bf{d}}}_{0}(x)|\leq 1
a.e.
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}
. Suppose
\displaystyle{{\bf{d}}}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3}))
is a weak solution to (
5.1
). Then
\displaystyle|{{\bf{d}}}(x,t)|\leq 1
for a.e.
\displaystyle(x,t)\in Q_{T}
.

The following lemma will play an essential role in the partial regularity of suitable weak solutions.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exist
\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
\displaystyle 0<\tau_{0}<\frac{1}{2}

and
\displaystyle C_{0}>0
such that if
\displaystyle({{\bf{v}}},{{\bf{d}}},{\pi})
is a suitable weak solution in
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{3}\times(0,\infty)
, then for
\displaystyle z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times(r^{2},\infty)
, and
\displaystyle r>0
, if it holds that

|{{\bf{z}}}|\leq M,\quad|{{\bf{d}}}|\leq M\text{ in }\mathcal{Q}_{r}(z_{0}),

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{r}(z_{0})=B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-r^{2},t)
,
and

\Theta_{z_{0}}(r):=r^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{r}(z_{0})}(|{{\bf{v}}}|^{3}+|\nabla{{\bf{d}}}|^{3})+\left(r^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{r}(z_{0})}|\pi|^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)^{2}\leq\varepsilon_{0}^{3},

then

\Theta_{z_{0}}(\tau_{0}r)=(\tau_{0}r)^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\tau_{0}r}(z_{0})}(|{{\bf{v}}}|^{3}+|\nabla{{\bf{d}}}|^{3})+\left((\tau_{0}r)^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\tau_{0}r}(z_{0})}|\pi|^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\{\Theta_{z_{0}}(r),C_{0}r^{3}\}.

Proof.

Proof by contradiction. Suppose that
it is false. Then there exists an
\displaystyle M_{0}>0
such that for any
\displaystyle\tau\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, there exist
sequences

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}\to 0
,
\displaystyle C_{i}\to\infty
,
\displaystyle r_{i}>0
, and
\displaystyle z_{i}=(x_{i},t_{i})\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times(r_{i}^{2},\infty)
such that

(5.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}|{{\bf{z}}}|\leq M_{0},,\quad|{{\bf{d}}}|\leq M_{0}\text{ in }\mathcal{Q}_{r_{i}}(z_{i}),\\
\Theta_{z_{i}}(r_{i})=\varepsilon_{i}^{3},\\
\Theta_{z_{i}}(\tau r_{i})>\frac{1}{2}\max\{\varepsilon_{i}^{3},C_{i}r_{i}^{3}\}.\end{array}\right.

First we notice that
\displaystyle C_{i}r_{i}^{3}\leq 2\Theta_{z_{i}}(\tau r_{i})\leq 2\tau^{-4}\Theta_{z_{i}}(r_{i})=2\tau^{-4}\varepsilon_{i}^{3}
. This implies that

r_{i}\leq\left(\frac{2\varepsilon_{i}^{3}}{C_{i}\tau^{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\to 0.

Next, we define the rescaling sequence

({{{\bf{z}}}}^{i},{{{\bf{v}}}}^{i},{{{\bf{d}}}}^{i},\pi^{i})(x,t)=(r_{i}{{\bf{z}}},r_{i}{{\bf{v}}},{{\bf{d}}},r_{i}^{2}{\pi})(x_{i}+r_{i}x,t_{i}+r_{i}^{2}t).

It is straightforward to verify that
\displaystyle({{\bf{z}}}^{i},{{\bf{v}}}^{i},{{\bf{d}}}^{i},{\pi}^{i})
satisfies the re-scaled equation

(5.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}{{\bf{v}}}^{i}+({{\bf{z}}}^{i}+{{\bf{v}}}^{i})\cdot\nabla({{\bf{z}}}^{i}+{{\bf{v}}}^{i})+\nabla\pi^{i}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\Delta{\bf{v}}^{i}-\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\left(\nabla{{\bf{d}}}_{i}\odot\nabla{{\bf{d}}}^{i}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla{{\bf{d}}}^{i}|^{2}{\rm I}_{3}-r_{i}^{2}F({{\bf{d}}}^{i}){\rm I}_{3}\right),\\
\nabla\cdot{{\bf{v}}}^{i}=0,\\
\partial_{t}{{\bf{d}}}^{i}+({{\bf{z}}}^{i}+{{\bf{v}}}^{i})\cdot\nabla{{\bf{d}}}^{i}=\Delta{{\bf{d}}}^{i}-r^{2}_{i}f({{\bf{d}}}^{i}).\end{array}\right.

And (
5.2
) becomes

(5.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{1}(0)}(|{{\bf{v}}}^{i}|^{3}+|\nabla{{\bf{d}}}^{i}|^{3})+\left(\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{1}(0)}|\pi^{i}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)^{2}=\varepsilon_{i}^{3},\\
\displaystyle\tau^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\tau}(0)}(|{{\bf{v}}}^{i}|^{3}+|\nabla{{\bf{d}}}^{i}|^{3})+\left(\tau^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\tau}(0)}|\pi^{i}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)^{3}>\frac{1}{2}\max\{\varepsilon_{i}^{3},C_{i}r_{i}^{3}\}.\end{array}\right.

Then we define the blowing-up sequence

(\widehat{{\bf{z}}}^{i},\widehat{{\bf{v}}}^{i},\widehat{{\bf{d}}}^{i},\widehat{\pi}^{i})=\left(\frac{{{\bf{z}}}^{i}}{\varepsilon_{i}},\frac{{{\bf{v}}}^{i}}{\varepsilon_{i}},\frac{{{\bf{d}}}^{i}-\overline{{{\bf{d}}}^{i}}}{\varepsilon_{i}},\frac{{\pi}^{i}}{\varepsilon_{i}}\right),

where
\displaystyle\overline{{{\bf{d}}}^{i}}=\fint_{\mathcal{Q}_{1}(0)}{{\bf{d}}}^{i}
, which denotes the average of
\displaystyle{{\bf{d}}}^{i}
over
\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{1}(0)
. Then it turns out that
\displaystyle(\widehat{{\bf{z}}}^{i},\widehat{{\bf{v}}}^{i},\widehat{{\bf{d}}}^{i},\widehat{\pi}^{i})
solves the following equation:

(5.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\widehat{{\bf{v}}}^{i}+\varepsilon_{i}(\widehat{{\bf{z}}}^{i}+\widehat{{\bf{v}}}^{i})\cdot\nabla(\widehat{{\bf{z}}}^{i}+\widehat{{\bf{v}}}^{i})+\nabla\widehat{\pi}^{i}=\Delta\widehat{{\bf{v}}}^{i}\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\left(\varepsilon_{i}\nabla\widehat{{\bf{d}}}^{i}\odot\nabla\widehat{{\bf{d}}}^{i}-\frac{\varepsilon_{i}}{2}|\nabla\widehat{{\bf{d}}}^{i}|^{2}{\rm I}_{3}-\frac{r_{i}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{i}}F({{\bf{d}}}^{i}){\rm I}_{3}\right),\\

\partial_{t}\widehat{{\bf{d}}}^{i}+\varepsilon_{i}(\widehat{{\bf{z}}}^{i}+\widehat{\bf{v}}^{i})\cdot\nabla\widehat{{\bf{d}}}^{i}=\Delta\widehat{{\bf{d}}}^{i}-\frac{r_{i}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{i}}f({\bf{d}}^{i}).\end{array}\right.

Furthermore, we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{1}(0)}\widehat{{\bf{d}}}^{i}=0,\\

\tau^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\tau}(0)}\left(|\widehat{{\bf{v}}}^{i}|^{3}+|\nabla\widehat{{\bf{d}}}^{i}|^{3}\right)+\left(\tau^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\tau}(0)}|\widehat{\pi}^{i}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)^{2}>\frac{1}{2}\max\{1,C_{i}\left(\frac{r_{i}}{\varepsilon_{i}}\right)^{3}\}.\end{array}\right.

From (
5.6
), by weak compactness, we can show there exists

such that, after passing to a subsequence,

We claim that

\sup_{i}\left(\|\widehat{{\bf{v}}}^{i}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{t}^{2}H_{x}^{1}(\mathcal{Q}_{1}(0))}+\|\widehat{{\bf{d}}}^{i}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}\cap L_{t}^{2}H_{x}^{2}(\mathcal{Q}_{1}(0))}\right)<\infty.

We choose a cut-off function

such that

Replacing
\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{t_{i}-\frac{r_{i}^{2}}{4}\leq t\leq t_{i}}\int_{B_{r_{i}}(x_{i})}\left(|{{\bf{v}}}|^{2}+|\nabla{{\bf{d}}}|^{2}+F({{\bf{d}}})\right)\varphi_{i}^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}x}+\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{r_{i}/2}(z_{i})}\left(|\nabla{{\bf{v}}}|^{2}+|\Delta{{\bf{d}}}|^{2}+|f({{\bf{d}}})|^{2}\right)\varphi_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{r_{i}}(z_{i})}\left(|{{\bf{v}}}|^{2}+|\nabla{{\bf{d}}}|^{2}+F({{\bf{d}}})\right)|\partial_{t}\left(\varphi_{i}^{2}\right)|

\displaystyle\displaystyle+C\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{r_{i}}(z_{i})}|({{\bf{z}}}+{{\bf{v}}})||{{\bf{z}}}||\nabla{{\bf{v}}}||\nabla\left(\varphi_{i}^{2}\right)|+|{\pi}||{{\bf{v}}}||\nabla\left(\varphi_{i}^{2}\right)|+\left(|{{\bf{v}}}|^{2}+|\nabla{{\bf{d}}}|^{2}\right)\Delta\left(\varphi_{i}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{-\frac{1}{4}\leq t\leq 0}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(0)}}(|\widehat{{\bf{v}}}^{i}|^{2}+|\nabla\widehat{{\bf{d}}}_{i}^{2}|^{2})+\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)}(|\nabla\widehat{{\bf{v}}}^{i}|+|\nabla^{2}\widehat{{\bf{d}}}^{i}|^{2})\leq C,\qquad\forall i.

We observe that
\displaystyle(\widehat{{\bf{v}}},\widehat{{\bf{d}}},\widehat{\pi})

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\widehat{{\bf{v}}}+\nabla\widehat{\pi}=\Delta\widehat{{\bf{v}}},\\

By the standard interior regularity estimate we have that

\displaystyle(\widehat{\textbf{u}},\widehat{\textbf{d}})\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{4}}(0))
,
\displaystyle\widehat{\pi}\in L^{\infty}([-\frac{1}{16},0],C^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{4}}(0)))
with the following estimate:

(5.8)

\displaystyle\tau^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\tau}(0)}(|\widehat{\textbf{u}}|^{3}+|\nabla\widehat{\textbf{d}}|^{3})+\left(\tau^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\tau}(0)}|\widehat{\pi}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{3}\left[\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)}(|\widehat{\textbf{u}}|^{3}+|\nabla\widehat{\textbf{d}}|^{3})+\left(\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)}|\widehat{\pi}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)^{2}\right]

From the strong convergence of

By iterating Lemma

Lemma 5.3

For any
\displaystyle M>0

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}>0

\displaystyle M

For any open set

\displaystyle 1\leq p<\infty

\displaystyle 0\leq\lambda\leq 5

5.16

\displaystyle\eta

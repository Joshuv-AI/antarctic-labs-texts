An equational approach to enriched distributivity
Adriana Balan, Alexander Kurz

Abstract
. The familiar adjunction between ordered sets and completely distributive lattices can be extended to generalised metric spaces, that is, categories enriched over a quantale (a lattice of “truth values”), via an appropriate distributive law between the “down-set” monad and the “up-set” monad on the category of quantale-enriched categories. If the underlying lattice of the quantale is completely distributive, and if powers distribute over non-empty joins in the quantale, then this distributive law can be concretely formulated in terms of operations, equations and choice functions, similar to the familiar distributive law of lattices. AMS 2010 Subject Classification: 18B35,18C05,06D10,18D20,06F07,08A65. Keywords: quantale, enriched category, weighted (co)limit, (co)complete enriched category, completely distributive quantale-enriched category.

1.2.
Quantales.

Historically, quantales were introduced in the 1980s

They can be perceived as lattices of “truth-values” or “distances”, equipped with an extra operation expressing conjunction (logical interpretation) or addition of distances (metric interpretation).
Alternatively, quantales are also complete idempotent semirings, hence appearing in tropical and idempotent analysis.

\displaystyle\bigvee_{f}\bigwedge_{k}\left(\vphantom{\bigvee_{i}}\psi(k)\rhd(G(k)(fk)\ast fk)\right)

for each functions
\psi:K\to\Omega
,
G:K\to\Omega^{A}
, hence we recover the complete distributivity law with obtained in Theorem
3.7
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Earlier we mentioned that the equational theory of
\Omega\hskip 0.0pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt\mathsf{CD}
is generated by
\Omega
, regardless of
Assumption
. Hence there is no hope for obtaining various variants of distributivity laws (like the one discussed below) unless these already hold in
\Omega
.

For example, observe that the parentheses in expressions involving tensors and cotensors, e.g.
v\rhd(w\ast a)
, do not change in the above law (
13
) of complete
\Omega
-distributivity.
One may then ask whether the distributivity-like property

v\rhd(w\ast a)=[v,w]\ast a

could be of interest for
\Omega\hskip 0.0pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt\mathsf{CD}
.
But the corresponding relation for
\Omega
itself, namely
[v,w{\otimes}u]=[v,w]{\otimes}u
, implies that the quantale is
trivial
.
To see this, observe that
e\leq[v,v]=[v,e\otimes v]=[v,e]\otimes v\leq e
. Therefore
\Omega
is an ordered abelian group
[
19
]
, hence it cannot be bounded as a lattice unless it is trivial
[
1
]
.

Consequently, completely distributive
\Omega
-categories are not so distributive after all. ∎

Before ending the paper, we point out one more issue: comparing the constructive versus the non-constructive equations of
\Omega
-completely distributivity, namely (
16
) and (
13
), as recalled below, we see that the left hand sides coincide.

(19)

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{k\in K}\psi(k)\rhd\left(\bigvee_{a\in A}G(k)(a)\ast a\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bigvee_{a\in A}\left(\bigwedge_{k\in K}[\psi(k),G(k)^{\downarrow}(a)]\right)\ast a}

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{k\in K}\psi(k)\rhd\left(\bigvee_{a\in A}G(k)(a)\ast a\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bigvee_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigwedge_{k\in K}\psi(k)\rhd(G(k)(fk)\ast fk)}\ .

A careful inspection of the proof of Theorem
3.7
reveals that the inequality

\bigvee_{a\in A}\left(\bigwedge_{k\in K}[\psi(k),G(k)^{\downarrow}(a)]\right)\ast a\leq\bigvee_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\bigwedge_{k\in K}\psi(k)\rhd(G(k)(fk)\ast fk)

holds in
\Omega\hskip 0.0pt\mbox{-}\hskip 0.5pt\mathsf{CD}
if
Assumption
holds. We shall now see an example where the inequality is not true.

Example 3.11
.

Take one of the two non-distributive quantales
M_{3}
and
N_{5}
from Example
2.1
4
.
Then
\Omega
-distributivity (

) always holds in
\Omega

13
), which implies lattice-distributivity, does not hold in the non-distributive lattices
M_{3}
and
N_{5}
.
Let us consider for example
\Omega=M_{3}
.
Put
K=\{0,1\}
and take the constant trivial weight
\psi(0)=\psi(1)=e
. Also, consider the following
K
-indexed family of
\Omega
-subsets of
\Omega
itself, seen as an
\Omega
-category:

G(k)(x)=\begin{cases}e&k=0,x=e\mbox{ or }k=1,x\in\{a,b\}\\

Equation (

) then becomes

During the evaluation the right-hand side of (
13

G(0)^{\downarrow}
and
G(1)^{\downarrow}
are, respectively,
\{\bot,e\}
and
\{\bot,a,b\}

But to compute the “enriched meet”, we need to go back to the formula of the “weighted downsets” (
3

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{|c||c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr[-,-]&\bot&a&e&b&\top\\
\hline\cr\hline\cr\bot&\top&\top&\top&\top&\top\\
a&b&\top&b&b&\top\\
e&\bot&a&e&b&\top\\
b&a&a&a&\top&\top\\

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{|c||c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr x&\bot&a&e&b&\top\\
\hline\cr\hline\cr G(0)^{\downarrow}(x)&\top&b&e&a&\bot\\
\hline\cr G(1)^{\downarrow}(x)&\top&\top&\top&\top&\top\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

The table above on the left exhibits the internal hom for the quantale structure of
M_{3}
of Example
2.1
4
.
Therefore
G(0)^{\downarrow}(x)=[x,e]
and
G(1)^{\downarrow}(x)=[x,a]\vee[x,b]
.
We see that the enrichment does change
G(1)^{\downarrow}
significantly.
One reason is
–
as for example the calculation
G(1)^{\downarrow}(e)=\left(G(1)(a)\otimes[e,a]\right)\vee\left(G(1)(b)\otimes[e,b]\right)=[e,a]\vee[e,b]=a\vee b=\top

–
that due to the tensor products
e\otimes a=a

e
is not below

.
Another reason, is that while

evaluates to zero over
\mathbbm{2}

[\top,a]=a

In fact, the only difference to the right-hand side of (

\Omega
-down-closure in the above calculation, but these, read in the appropriate enriched way, do enlarge the downsets just enough to make the equality work again.

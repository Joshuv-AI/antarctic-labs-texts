A reprise of the NTV conjecture for the Hilbert transform
Eric T. Sawyer

Abstract
. We give a somewhat different organization of the proof of the NTV conjecture for the Hilbert transform that was proved by T. Hytönen, M. Lacey, E.T. Sawyer, C.-Y. Shen and I. Uriartre-Tuero in [ LaSaShUr3 ] , [ Lac ] and [ Hyt2 ] , building on previous work of F. Nazarov, S. Treil and A. Volberg in [ NTV3 ] . After modifying the decomposition of the main bilinear form, the proof alters the bottom-up corona construction, the size functional, the straddling lemmas, and the use of recursion of admissible collections of pairs of intervals, from M. Lacey [ Lac ] . However, the essence of control of the stopping form remains as in the fundamental work of Lacey. The alternate approach of Sawyer and Wick [ SaWi ] to controlling functional energy is also described, which differs from that previously in the literature, [ LaSaShUr3 ] and [ Hyt2 ] .



1.
Introduction

F. Nazarov, S. Treil and A. Volberg formulated the two weight question for
the Hilbert transform
[
Vol
]
, that in turn led to the famous NTV
conjecture:

Conjecture 1
.

[
Vol
]
Given two positive locally finite Borel measures
\sigma
and
\omega
on the real line
\mathbb{R}
, the Hilbert transform
Hf\left(x\right)=\mathop{\mathrm{p}v}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f\left(y\right)}{y-x}dy
is
bounded from
L^{2}\left(\sigma\right)
to
L^{2}\left(\omega\right)
,
i.e. the operator norm

(1.1)

\mathfrak{N}_{H}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\equiv\sup_{f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R};\sigma\right)}\frac{1}{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\sigma\right)}}\left\|H\left(f\sigma\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}\ ,

is finite uniformly over appropriate truncations of
H
, if the two tailed
Muckenhoupt characteristic,

\mathcal{A}_{2}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\equiv\sup_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\left(\frac{1}{\left|I\right|}\int_{I}\mathbf{s}_{I}\left(x\right)^{2}d\omega\left(x\right)\right)\left(\frac{1}{\left|I\right|}\int_{I}\mathbf{s}_{I}\left(y\right)^{2}d\sigma\left(y\right)\right)\ ,

is finite, where
\mathbf{s}_{I}\left(x\right)=\frac{\ell\left(I\right)}{\ell\left(I\right)+\left|x-c_{I}\right|}
, and if the testing
characteristic

(1.2)

\mathfrak{T}_{H}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\equiv\sup_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|I\right|_{\sigma}}}\left\|H\mathbf{1}_{I}\sigma\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}\ ,

is finite as well as its dual
\mathfrak{T}_{H}\left(\omega,\sigma\right)
.

In a groundbreaking series of papers including
[
NTV1
]
,
[
NTV2
]
and

[
NTV3
]
, Nazarov, Treil and Volberg used weighted Haar decompositions
with random grids of
\left(r,\varepsilon\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}
grids,
introduced their ‘pivotal condition’, and proved the above conjecture under
the side assumption that this pivotal condition held. Subsequently in
[
LaSaUr2
]
, it was shown that the pivotal condition was not necessary for the
norm inequality to hold in general, a necessary ‘energy condition’ was
introduced as a substitute, and a hybrid merging of these two conditions was
shown to be sufficient for use as a side condition. Eventually, these three
authors with C.-Y. Shen established the NTV conjecture in the absence of
common point masses in the measures
\sigma
and
\omega
in a two part
paper; M. Lacey, E. T. Sawyer, C.-Y. Shen and I. Uriarte-Tuero
[
LaSaShUr3
]
and M. Lacey
[
Lac
]
.

The final assumption of no common point masses was removed shortly after by
T. Hytönen
[
Hyt2
]
who replaced the two tailed Muckenhoupt
characteristic with a variant involving holes,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}^{\mathop{\mathrm{h}ole}}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sup_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\left\{\mathrm{P}\left(I,\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}\setminus I}\omega\right)\left(\frac{\left|I\right|_{\sigma}}{\left|I\right|}\right)+\left(\frac{\left|I\right|_{\omega}}{\left|I\right|}\right)\mathrm{P}\left(I,\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}\setminus I}\sigma\right)\right\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(I,\mu\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\ell\left(I\right)}{\left(\ell\left(I\right)+\left|y-c_{I}\right|\right)^{2}}d\mu\left(y\right),\ \ \ \ \ c_{I}\equiv\mathop{\mathrm{c}entre}\text{ of }I,

and who also simplified some aspects of the proof. The reason for
introducing the holes is that if
\sigma
and
\omega
share a common
point mass, then the classical Muckenhoupt characteristic

A_{2}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\equiv\sup_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\frac{\left|I\right|_{\sigma}}{\left|I\right|}\frac{\left|I\right|_{\omega}}{\left|I\right|},

fails to be finite - simply let
I
in the
\sup
above shrink to a common
point mass. The
\mathop{\mathrm{h}oled}
Muckenhoupt condition trivially implies the
more elementary
\mathop{\mathrm{o}ffset}
Muckenhoupt condition

A_{2}^{\mathop{\mathrm{o}ffset}}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\equiv\sup_{\left(Q,Q^{\prime}\right)\in\mathcal{N}}\left(\frac{1}{\left|Q^{\prime}\right|}\int_{Q^{\prime}}d\omega\right)\left(\frac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}d\sigma\right),

where
\mathcal{N}
is the set of neighbouring pairs
\left(Q,Q^{\prime}\right)
of intervals of comparable side length, i.e.
\left(Q,Q^{\prime}\right)\in\mathcal{D}
for some dyadic grid
\mathcal{D}
where
2^{-r}\ell\left(Q^{\prime}\right)\leq\ell\left(Q\right)\leq 2^{r}\ell\left(Q^{\prime}\right)
,
Q\cap Q^{\prime}=\emptyset
, and
\overline{Q}\cap\overline{Q^{\prime}}\not=\emptyset
. Here
r
is the
goodness parameter in
[
NTV3
]
and
[
LaSaShUr3
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to give a modified proof of the above results
for the Hilbert transform, and to highlight where the various
characteristics enter into the proof. Our proof is somewhat simplified in
that it avoids the recursion of admissible collections of pairs of intervals
that was used in Part II
[
Lac
]
, and the recent approach to controlling
functional energy due to Sawyer and Wick
[
SaWi
]
, is discussed in the
setting
p=2
. However, apart from some reorganization using nonlinear
bounds, the essence of control of the stopping form remains as in the
fundamental work of M. Lacey
[
Lac
]
, and the control of functional
energy is similar in spirit to that in
[
LaSaShUr3
]
, and can in fact be
imported as a black box from
[
LaSaShUr3
]
if one wishes.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\sigma
and
\omega
be positive locally finite Borel
measures on
\mathbb{R}
. The Hilbert transform
H_{\sigma}
is bounded
from
L^{2}\left(\sigma\right)
to
L^{2}\left(\omega\right)

if
and only if
the two tailed Muckenhoupt characteristic with holes, and both
of the testing characteristics, are finite. More precisely, there are
universal positive constants
c,C
such that for all pairs
\left(\sigma,\omega\right)
of positive locally finite Borel measures,

c\leq\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{A}_{2}^{\mathop{\mathrm{h}ole}}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)}+\mathfrak{T}_{H}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)+\mathfrak{T}_{H}\left(\omega,\sigma\right)}{\mathfrak{N}_{H}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)}\leq C.

2.
Reprise of the proof of the
NTV
conjecture

We assume the Haar supports of all functions
f\in L^{2}\left(\sigma\right)
and
g\in L^{2}\left(\omega\right)
are contained, along with
their two children, in the
\left(r,\varepsilon\right)
-
\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}

grid
\mathcal{D}_{\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}}
, where
J\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}}
if
\mathop{\mathrm{d}ist}\left(J,\partial K\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\ell\left(J\right)^{\varepsilon}\ell\left(K\right)^{1-\varepsilon}
for all
K\supset J
with
\ell\left(K\right)\geq 2^{r}\ell\left(J\right)
. See

[
NTV3
]
and
[
LaSaShUr3
]
for the reduction of the norm inequality to
good functions with goodness parameters
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
.

We will make use of the following bound for sums of Haar coefficients, which
is proved e.g. in
[
LaSaUr2
]
and
[
LaSaShUr3
]
. For any interval
J

with center
c_{J}
, and any finite measure
\nu
, define the Poisson
integral,

\mathrm{P}\left(J,\nu\right)\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\ell\left(J\right)}{\left(\ell\left(J\right)+\left|y-c_{J}\right|\right)^{2}}d\nu\left(y\right).

Lemma 4

(Monotonicity and Energy Lemma)
.

Suppose that
J\subset I^{\prime}\subset 2I^{\prime}\subset F
are dyadic intervals. Then

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle H_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{F\setminus I^{\prime}},\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{P}\left(J,\mathbf{1}_{F\setminus I^{\prime}}\sigma\right)}{\ell\left(J\right)}\right)^{2}\left\|\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}x\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}^{2}\left\|\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}^{2}\ ,

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle H_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{F\setminus I^{\prime}},\sum_{J^{\prime}\subset J}a_{J^{\prime}}h_{J^{\prime}}^{\omega}\right\rangle_{\omega}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left(J,\mathbf{1}_{F\setminus I^{\prime}}\sigma\right)^{2}\left|J\right|_{\omega}\left\|\sum_{J^{\prime}\subset J}a_{J^{\prime}}h_{J^{\prime}}^{\omega}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}^{2}.

We will also need the following critical Poisson Decay Lemma from
[
Vol
]
.

Lemma 5

(Poisson Decay Lemma)
.

Suppose
J\subset I\subset K
are dyadic intervals
and that
d\left(J,\partial I\right)>2\ell\left(J\right)^{\varepsilon}\ell\left(I\right)^{1-\varepsilon}
for some
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
. Then for a positive Borel measure
\mu
we have

(2.1)

\mathrm{P}(J,\mu\mathbf{1}_{K\setminus I})\lesssim\left(\frac{\ell\left(J\right)}{\ell\left(I\right)}\right)^{1-2\varepsilon}\mathrm{P}(I,\mu\mathbf{1}_{K\setminus I}).

2.1.
First steps and outline of the decomposition of forms

Now we can begin the proof of Theorem
2
. Following
[
NTV3
]
and

[
LaSaShUr3
]
, we expand the Hilbert transform bilinear form
\left\langle H_{\sigma}f,g\right\rangle_{\omega}
in terms of the Haar
decompositions of
f
and
g
,

\left\langle H_{\sigma}f,g\right\rangle_{\omega}=\sum_{I,J\in\mathcal{D}}\left\langle H_{\sigma}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f,\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega},

and then assuming the Haar supports of
f
and
g
lie in an
\left(r,\varepsilon\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}
grid
D_{\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}}
(see
[
NTV3
]

for the origin of goodness, and also
[
LaSaShUr3
]
for its use here), we
decompose the double sum above as follows,

\displaystyle\left\langle H_{\sigma}f,g\right\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{D}\\
J\subset_{\tau}I\end{subarray}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{D}\\
I\subset_{\tau}J\end{subarray}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{D}\\
I\cap J=\emptyset\end{subarray}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{D}\\
J\subset I\text{ and }\ell\left(J\right)\geq 2^{-\tau}\ell\left(I\right)\end{subarray}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{D}\\
I\subset J\text{ and }\ell\left(I\right)\geq 2^{-\tau}\ell\left(J\right)\end{subarray}}\right\}\left\langle H_{\sigma}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f,\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{b}elow}}\left(f,g\right)+\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{a}bove}}\left(f,g\right)+\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{d}isjoint}}\left(f,g\right)+\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{c}omparable}}\left(f,g\right)+\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{c}omparable}}^{\ast}\left(f,g\right),

where
\tau\in\mathbb{N}
is fixed below, larger than the goodness
parameter
r
, and
J\subset_{\tau}I
means that
J\subset I
and
\ell\left(J\right)\leq 2^{-\tau}\ell\left(I\right)
. The first two forms
are symmetric, and using a CZ-energy corona decomposition with parameter
\Gamma>1
, we subsequently decompose the below form
\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{b}elow}}\left(f,g\right)
into another four forms,

\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{b}elow}}\left(f,g\right)=\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{n}eigh}}\left(f,g\right)+\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{f}ar}}\left(f,g\right)+\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{p}ara}}\left(f,g\right)+\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{s}top}}\left(f,g\right),

in which there is control of both averages of
f
and a Poisson-Energy
characteristic
\Gamma\left(\sigma,\omega\right)
in each corona.
Altogether, we will eventually have eleven forms in our decomposition of the
inner product
\left\langle H_{\sigma}f,g\right\rangle_{\omega}
. It
turns out that all of the forms, save for the far, paraproduct, and stopping
forms, are controlled directly by the parameter
\Gamma
and the testing
and holed Muckenhoupt characteristics. Control of the far, paraproduct, and
stopping forms will require finiteness of the energy characteristics
introduced in
[
LaSaUr2
]
,

(2.2)

\mathcal{E}_{2}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)^{2}\equiv\sup_{\overset{\cdot}{\bigcup}_{r=1}^{\infty}I_{r}\subset I}\left(\frac{\mathrm{P}\left(I_{r},\mathbf{1}_{I\setminus I_{r}}\sigma\right)}{\ell\left(I_{r}\right)}\right)^{2}\mathsf{E}\left(I_{r},\omega\right)^{2}\frac{\left|I_{r}\right|_{\omega}}{\left|I\right|_{\sigma}}\text{ and
its dual }\mathcal{E}_{2}\left(\omega,\sigma\right),

where the supremum is taken over all pairwise disjoint subdecompositions of
an interval
I
into dyadic subintervals
I_{r}\in\mathcal{D}\left[I\right]
. The holed Muckenhoupt characteristic is needed only to control the far
form, while the smaller offset Muckenhoupt characteristic suffices elsewhere.

It may help to make a couple of comments on the broad structure of the
proof. The testing characteristics are used to control forms in which the
measures
\sigma
and
\omega
‘see each other’ in inner products
\left\langle H_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{K}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f,\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}
with
J\subset K

(such as comparable and paraproduct forms), while the holed and offset
Muckenhoupt characteristics are used to control forms in which the measures
\sigma
and
\omega
‘do
not
see each other’ in inner products
\left\langle H_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{K}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f,\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}
with
K\cap J=\emptyset
(such as disjoint, neighbour, far and stopping forms).

In some of these cases, there arise parts of the form in which the coronas
associated to the measures are far apart, and it is then necessary to derive
additional
geometric decay
in the distance between coronas, much as
in the well-known Colar-Stein Lemma.

A main tool for this is the Poisson Decay Lemma, which extracts geometric
decay from goodness of intervals in the Haar supports of the functions, as
pioneered in
[
NTV3
]
. This decay is used in controlling the disjoint,
outer and stopping forms. Additional sources of needed geometric decay arise
from the coronas themselves as follows. There are three coronas at play in
the proof:

(1)

a top-down Calderón-Zygmund corona that derives geometric decay
from a
\sigma
-Carleson condition, that holds automatically from the
definition of the stopping times,

(2)

a top-down Poisson-Energy corona that also derives geometric decay
from a
\sigma
-Carleson condition, but that is no longer automatic from
the definition, and instead requires the energy condition, as pioneered in

[
LaSaUr2
]
,

(3)

and a bottom-up Energy corona that derives geometric decay from an ‘
\omega
-energy Carleson condition’, that again holds automatically from the
definition of the stopping times, as pioneered in
[
Lac
]
.

Since the first two corona constructions both yield a
\sigma
-Carleson
condition, it is convenient to bundle them together in a single CZ-PE corona
construction, see (
3.1
) for the associated stopping
times. This corona construction has two additional important properties,
control of averages of
f
that is crucial for controlling the paraproduct
form, and control of a Poisson-Energy characteristic that is crucial for
controlling the stopping form. The
\sigma
-Carleson condition is used in
the far, paraproduct and stopping forms while the ‘
\omega
-energy Carleson
condition’ is used in the stopping form.

2.3.
Control of the neighbour form by the offset Muckenhoupt
characteristic

The neighbour form is easily controlled by the

\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\equiv\left\{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}:\pi_{\mathcal{F}}^{1}F^{\prime}=F\right\}
for the collection of
\mathcal{F}
-children of

F
.

Proposition 9

(The Intertwining Proposition)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is
\sigma
-Carleson.
Then

\left|\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\ \sum_{I:\ I\supsetneqq F}\ \left\langle H_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{1}_{I_{F}}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f\right),\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}\right|\lesssim\left(\mathfrak{F}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)+\mathfrak{T}_{H}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\right)\ \left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\sigma\right)}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}.

Proof.

We write the left hand side of the display above as

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\ \sum_{I:\ I\supsetneqq F}\ \left\langle H_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{1}_{I_{F}}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f\right),g_{F}\right\rangle_{\omega}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\ \left\langle H_{\sigma}\left(\sum_{I:\ I\supsetneqq F}\mathbf{1}_{I_{F}}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f\right),g_{F}\right\rangle_{\omega}\equiv\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\ \left\langle H_{\sigma}f_{F},g_{F}\right\rangle_{\omega}\ ,

where

g_{F}=\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}^{\omega}g=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\text{ and }f_{F}\equiv\sum_{I:\ I\supsetneqq F}\mathbf{1}_{I_{F}}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f\ .

Note that
g_{F}
is supported in
F
, and that
f_{F}
is constant on
F
.
The intervals
I
occurring in this sum are linearly and consecutively
ordered by inclusion, along with the intervals
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}

that contain
F
. More precisely, we can write

F\equiv F_{0}\subsetneqq F_{1}\subsetneqq F_{2}\subsetneqq...\subsetneqq F_{n}\subsetneqq F_{n+1}\subsetneqq...F_{N}

where
F_{m}=\pi_{\mathcal{F}}^{m}F
for all
m\geq 1
. We can also write

F=F_{0}\subsetneqq I_{1}\subsetneqq I_{2}\subsetneqq...\subsetneqq I_{k}\subsetneqq I_{k+1}\subsetneqq...\subsetneqq I_{K}=F_{N}

where
I_{k}=\pi_{\mathcal{D}}^{k}F
for all
k\geq 1
, and by convention
we set
I_{0}=F
. There is a (unique) subsequence
\left\{k_{m}\right\}_{m=1}^{N}
such that

F_{m}=I_{k_{m}},\ \ \ \ \ 1\leq m\leq N.

Recall that

f_{F}\left(x\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\left(I_{k}\right)_{F}}\left(x\right)\bigtriangleup_{I_{k}}^{\sigma}f\left(x\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{I_{k}\setminus I_{k-1}}\left(x\right)\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{\infty}\bigtriangleup_{I_{\ell}}^{\sigma}f\left(x\right).

Assume now that
k_{m}\leq k<k_{m+1}
. Using a telescoping sum, we compute
that for

x\in I_{k+1}\setminus I_{k}\subset F_{m+1}\setminus F_{m},

we have

\left|f_{F}\left(x\right)\right|=\left|\sum_{\ell=k+2}^{\infty}\bigtriangleup_{I_{\ell}}^{\sigma}f\left(x\right)\right|=\left|\mathbb{E}_{\theta I_{k+2}}^{\sigma}f-\mathbb{E}_{I_{K}}^{\sigma}f\right|\lesssim\mathbb{E}_{F_{m+1}}^{\sigma}\left|f\right|\ .

Now
f_{F}
is constant on
F
and

\displaystyle\left|f_{F}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{N}\left(\mathbb{E}_{F_{m+1}}^{\sigma}\left|f\right|\right)\ \mathbf{1}_{F_{m+1}\setminus F_{m}}=\left(\mathbb{E}_{F}^{\sigma}\left|f\right|\right)\ \mathbf{1}_{F}+\sum_{m=0}^{N}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathcal{F}}^{m+1}F}^{\sigma}\left|f\right|\right)\ \mathbf{1}_{\pi_{\mathcal{F}}^{m+1}F\setminus\pi_{\mathcal{F}}^{m}F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}_{F}^{\sigma}\left|f\right|\right)\ \mathbf{1}_{F}+\sum_{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}:\ F\subset F^{\prime}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathcal{F}}F^{\prime}}^{\sigma}\left|f\right|\right)\ \mathbf{1}_{\pi_{\mathcal{F}}F^{\prime}\setminus F^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\ \mathbf{1}_{F}+\sum_{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}:\ F\subset F^{\prime}}\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\pi_{\mathcal{F}}F^{\prime}\right)\ \mathbf{1}_{\pi_{\mathcal{F}}F^{\prime}\setminus F^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\ \mathbf{1}_{F}+\sum_{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}:\ F\subset F^{\prime}}\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\pi_{\mathcal{F}}F^{\prime}\right)\ \mathbf{1}_{\pi_{\mathcal{F}}F^{\prime}}\ \mathbf{1}_{F^{c}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\ \mathbf{1}_{F}+\Phi\ \mathbf{1}_{F^{c}}\ ,\ \ \ \ \ \text{\ for all }F\in\mathcal{F},

where

\Phi\equiv\sum_{F^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{F}}\ \alpha_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F^{\prime\prime}\right)\ \mathbf{1}_{F^{\prime\prime}}\ .

Now we write

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\ \left\langle H_{\sigma}f_{F},g_{F}\right\rangle_{\omega}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\ \left\langle H_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{1}_{F}f_{F}\right),g_{F}\right\rangle_{\omega}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\ \left\langle H_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{1}_{F^{c}}f_{F}\right),g_{F}\right\rangle_{\omega}\equiv I+II.

Then local interval testing and quasi-orthogonality, together with the fact
that
f_{F}
is a constant on
F
bounded by
\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)
, give

\displaystyle\left|I\right|=\left|\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{1}_{F}\left(x\right)H_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{1}_{F}f_{F}\right)\left(x\right)\ g_{F}\left(x\right)\ d\omega\left(x\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\mathbf{1}_{F}\left(x\right)H_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{1}_{F}\right)\left(x\right)\right|^{2}d\omega\left(x\right)}\ \sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|g_{F}\left(x\right)\right|^{2}d\omega\left(x\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{H}^{\mathop{\mathrm{l}oc}}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\right|^{2}\left|F\right|_{\sigma}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}\lesssim\mathfrak{T}_{H}^{\mathop{\mathrm{l}oc}}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\sigma\right)}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}.

Now
\mathbf{1}_{F^{c}}f_{F}
is supported outside
F
, and each
J
in the
Haar support of
g_{F}=\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}^{\omega}g
is either in
\mathcal{N}_{r}\left(F\right)=\left\{J\in\mathcal{D}\left[F\right]:\ell\left(J\right)\geq 2^{-r}\ell\left(F\right)\right\}
, in which case the desired bound for term
I
is
straightforward, or
J
is
\left(r,\varepsilon\right)
-deeply embedded
in
F
, i.e.
J\subset_{r,\varepsilon}F
, and so
J\subset_{r,\varepsilon}W
for some
W\in\mathcal{M}_{\left(r,\varepsilon\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{d}eep}}\left(F\right)
. Thus with
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\flat}\left(F\right)\equiv\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\setminus\mathcal{N}_{r}\left(F\right)
we have

\displaystyle\left|II\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}H_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{1}_{F^{c}}f_{F}\right)\left(x\right)\ \mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\flat}\left(F\right)}^{\omega}g\left(x\right)\ d\omega\left(x\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{W\in\mathcal{M}_{\left(r,\varepsilon\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{d}eep}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\flat}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{D}\left[W\right]}^{\omega}H_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{1}_{F^{c}}f_{F}\right)\left(x\right)\ \mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\flat}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{D}\left[W\right]}^{\omega}g\left(x\right)\ d\omega\left(x\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{W\in\mathcal{M}_{\left(r,\varepsilon\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{d}eep}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\flat}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{D}\left[W\right]}^{\omega}H_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{1}_{F^{c}}f_{F}\right)\left(x\right)\right|^{2}d\omega\left(x\right)}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \times\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{W\in\mathcal{M}_{\left(r,\varepsilon\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{d}eep}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\flat}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{D}\left[W\right]}^{\omega}g\left(x\right)\right|^{2}d\omega\left(x\right)}.

The second factor is bounded by
C\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}
, and we use the second line in the Energy Lemma
4
on the first factor to bound it by,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{W\in\mathcal{M}_{\left(r,\varepsilon\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{d}eep}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}\ \left(\frac{\mathrm{P}\left(W,\mathbf{1}_{F^{c}}f_{F}\sigma\right)}{\ell\left(W\right)}\right)^{2}\int_{W}\left|\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{D}\left[W\right]}^{\omega}x\right|^{2}d\omega}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\lVert\Phi\rVert_{L^{p}\left(\sigma\right)}\lesssim\mathfrak{F}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\sigma\right)},

using the definition of the functional energy characteristic
\mathfrak{F}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)
, and the dyadic
\sigma
-maximal function
inequality
\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\sigma\right)}\lesssim\left\|M_{\sigma}f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\sigma\right)}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\sigma\right)}
. This
completes the proof of the Intertwining Proposition
9
.

Thus we have the following control of the
\mathop{\mathrm{f}ar}
form,

\left|\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{f}ar}}\left(f,g\right)\right|\lesssim\left(\mathfrak{F}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)+\mathfrak{T}_{H}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)+\mathcal{A}_{2}^{\mathop{\mathrm{h}ole}}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\right)\ \left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\sigma\right)}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}\ .

Finally then, to control the quadratic functional energy characteristic
\mathfrak{F}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)
, we can of course appeal to the
bound in
[
LaSaShUr3
]
as a black box, to conclude that,

\left|\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{f}ar}}\left(f,g\right)\right|\lesssim\left(\mathfrak{T}_{H,p}^{\ell^{2}}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)+\mathcal{A}_{2}^{\mathop{\mathrm{h}ole}}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\right)\ \left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\sigma\right)}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}\ ,

thus completing our treatment of the
\mathop{\mathrm{f}ar}
form.

Alternatively, we can use the following somewhat simpler argument introduced
in
[
SaWi
]
. Consider the
refined
functional energy inequality,

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{W\in\mathcal{M}_{\left(r,\varepsilon\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{d}eep}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}\left(\frac{\mathrm{P}\left(W,h\mathbf{1}_{F^{c}}\sigma\right)}{\ell\left(W\right)}\right)^{2}\int_{W}\left|\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{D}\left[W\right]}^{\omega}x\right|^{2}d\omega\leq\mathfrak{F}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\lVert h\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\sigma\right)}\ ,

in which the sum in
W\in\mathcal{M}_{\left(r,\varepsilon\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{d}eep}}\left(F\right)
is restricted to the corona
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)
. This smaller functional energy also serves to
control the Intertwining Proposition by an inspection of (
3.8
)
above. Using

\Lambda\equiv\overset{\cdot}{\bigcup}_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{M}_{\left(r,\varepsilon\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{d}eep}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right),

where
\overset{\cdot}{\bigcup}
denotes that the sets
\mathcal{M}_{\left(r,\varepsilon\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{d}eep}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)
are pairwise disjoint, together with the
orthogonality of the projections
\left\{\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{D}\left[W\right]}^{\omega}x\right\}_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in\mathcal{F}\\
W\in\mathcal{M}_{\left(r,\varepsilon\right)-\mathop{\mathrm{d}eep}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\end{subarray}}
in
L^{2}\left(\omega\right)
, this refined functional
energy inequality is equivalent to the boundedness from
L^{2}\left(\sigma\right)
to
L^{2}\left(\omega\right)
of the operator
Th\left(x\right)\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}}K\left(x,y\right)h\left(y\right)d\sigma\left(y\right)
with kernel,

(3.9)

K\left(x,y\right)\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{W\in\Lambda}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{R\setminus W^{\ast}}\left(y\right)}{\left(\ell\left(W\right)+\left|y-c_{W}\right|\right)^{2}}\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{D}\left[W\right]}^{\omega}Z\left(x\right),

where
Z
denotes the identity map
Z\left(x\right)=x
on
\mathbb{R}
.

Now we enlarge the kernel
K\left(x,y\right)
by removing the indicator
\mathbf{1}_{R\setminus W^{\ast}}\left(y\right)
from the right hand side
and replacing the projection
\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{D}\left[W\right]}^{\omega}Z\left(x\right)=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{D}\left[W\right]}\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}Z\left(x\right)
by the ‘absolute’
projection,

\left|\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{D}\left[W\right]}^{\omega}\right|Z\left(x\right)\equiv\sqrt{\sum_{J\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{D}\left[W\right]}\left|\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}Z\left(x\right)\right|^{2}}.

Then boundedness of the positive linear operator
\widehat{T}h\left(x\right)\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{K}\left(x,y\right)h\left(y\right)d\sigma\left(y\right)
, where

K\left(x,y\right)\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{W\in\Lambda}\frac{1}{\left(\ell\left(W\right)+\left|y-c_{W}\right|\right)^{2}}\left|\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\cap\mathcal{D}\left[W\right]}^{\omega}\right|Z\left(x\right),

is sufficient for the refined functional energy inequality.

Now the kernel
K
is

(1)

essentially decreasing in the variable
y
away from
x
, i.e.

\widehat{K}\left(x,y_{1}\right)\leq C\widehat{K}\left(x,y_{2}\right),\ \ \ \ \ \left|y_{1}-x\right|\geq\left|y_{2}-x\right|,

(2)

roughly constant in the variable
y
on intervals away from
x
, i.e.

\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{\widehat{K}\left(x,y_{1}\right)}{\widehat{K}\left(x,y_{2}\right)}\leq C,\ \ \ \ \ y_{1},y_{2}\in I\text{ with }x\notin 2I,

(3)

and reverse Hölder in the variable
x
on intervals away from
y
,
i.e. for every
1<r<\infty
, there is
C_{r}<\infty
such that,

\left(\frac{1}{\left|I\right|_{\sigma}}\int_{I}\left|\widehat{K}\left(x,y\right)\right|^{r^{\prime}}d\omega\left(x\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\leq C_{r}\frac{1}{\left|I\right|_{\sigma}}\int_{I}\left|\widehat{K}\left(x,y\right)\right|d\omega\left(x\right),\ \ \ \ \ y\notin 2I.

Properties (1) and (2) are easy consequences of the formula for the Poisson
kernel, see
[
SaWi
, (7.10) and (7.11)]
for details, and see
[
SaWi
, implicit in the proof of Theorem 51]
for property (3). Now as shown in

[
SaWi
, Section 11, the appendix]
, this reduces boundedness of
\widehat{T}
to two dual testing conditions, which can then be controlled as in
[
SaWi
, Subsections 7.3 and 7.4]
, and we leave these details to the reader
(they simplify considerably in the case
p=2
).

4.
Reduction of the diagonal form by the NTV reach

We first apply the clever ‘NTV reach’ of
[
NTV3
]
, which splits the
diagonal form

\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{d}iag}}\left(f,g\right)=\sum_{\left(I,J\right)\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathop{\mathrm{d}iag}}}\left\langle H_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{1}_{I_{J}}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f\right),\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(I,J\right)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\\
J\subset_{\tau}I\end{subarray}}\left(E_{I_{J}}^{\sigma}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f\right)\left\langle H_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{I_{J}}f,\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega},

into a paraproduct and stopping form,

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{d}iag}}\left(f,g\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(I,J\right)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\\
J\subset_{\tau}I\end{subarray}}\left(E_{I_{J}}^{\sigma}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f\right)\left\langle H_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{F},\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(I,J\right)\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\\
J\subset_{\tau}I\end{subarray}}\left(E_{I_{J}}^{\sigma}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f\right)\left\langle H_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{F\setminus I_{J}},\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{p}ara}}^{F}\left(f,g\right)+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{s}top}}^{F}\left(f,g\right)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{p}ara}}\left(f,g\right)+\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{s}top}}\left(f,g\right).

4.1.
Control of the paraproduct form by the testing characteristic

We control the local paraproduct form
\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{p}ara}}^{F}\left(f,g\right)
by the testing condition for
H
. Indeed, from the telescoping
identity for Haar projections, see e.g.
[
LaSaUr2
]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{p}ara}}^{F}\left(f,g\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\text{ and }J\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\\
J\subset I\text{ and }\ell\left(J\right)\leq 2^{-\mathbf{r}}\ell\left(I\right)\end{subarray}}\left(E_{I_{J}}^{\sigma}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f\right)\left\langle H_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{F},\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{J\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}\left\langle H_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{F},\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}\left\{\sum_{I\in\mathcal{C}_{F}\text{:\ }J\subset I\text{ and }\ell\left(J\right)\leq 2^{-r}\ell\left(I\right)}E_{I_{J}}^{\sigma}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{J\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}\left\langle H_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{F},\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}\left\{E_{I^{\natural}\left(J\right)_{J}}^{\sigma}f-E_{F}^{\sigma}f\right\}=\left\langle H_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{F},\sum_{J\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}\left\{E_{I^{\natural}\left(J\right)_{J}}^{\sigma}f-E_{F}^{\sigma}f\right\}\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}\ ,

where
I^{\natural}\left(J\right)
is the smallest
I\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\cap\Lambda_{f}^{\sigma}
(where
\Lambda_{f}^{\sigma}
is the Haar support of
f)
, that contains
J
and satisfies

J\subset_{r}I
.

Thus from Cauchy-Schwarz and the bound on the coefficients
\lambda_{J}\equiv E_{I^{\natural}\left(J\right)_{J}}^{\sigma}f-E_{F}^{\sigma}f

given by
\left|\lambda_{J}\right|\lesssim\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left|\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{p}ara}}^{F}\left(f,g\right)\right|=\left|\left\langle H_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{F},\sum_{J\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}\left\{E_{I^{\natural}\left(J\right)_{J}}^{\sigma}f-E_{F}^{\sigma}f\right\}\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{1}_{F}H_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{F}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}\left\|\sum_{J\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}\lambda_{J}\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}\lesssim\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\ \left\|\mathbf{1}_{F}H_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{F}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}\ \left\|\sum_{J\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{H}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\ \alpha_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\ \sqrt{\left|F\right|_{\sigma}}\ \left\|\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}^{\omega}g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}.

Then summing in
F
we obtain the following bound for the paraproduct form
\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{p}ara}}\left(f,g\right)\equiv\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{p}ara}}^{F}\left(f,g\right)
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{p}ara}}\left(f,g\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\left|\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{p}ara}}^{F}\left(f,g\right)\right|\leq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{T}_{H}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\ \alpha_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\ \sqrt{\left|F\right|_{\sigma}}\ \left\|\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}^{\omega}g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{H}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)^{2}\ \left|F\right|_{\sigma}}\sqrt{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\left\|\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}^{\omega}g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{H}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\sigma)}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

by quasi-orthogonality in (
3.4
) and orthogonality in Haar
projections
\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)}^{\omega}g
.

4.2.
Control of the stopping form by the Poisson-Energy characteristic

To control the stopping form, it suffices to assume the Haar supports of
f

and
g
are contained in a fixed large, but finite subset
\digamma
of the
grid
\mathcal{D}
, and to uniformly control over these subsets, each local
stopping form

\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{s}top}}^{F}\left(f,g\right)\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{C}_{F}\text{ and }J\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)\\
J\subset I\text{ and }\ell\left(J\right)\leq 2^{-\tau}\ell\left(I\right)\end{subarray}}\left(E_{I_{J}}^{\sigma}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f\right)\left\langle H_{\sigma}\left(\mathbf{1}_{F\setminus I_{J}}\right),\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}.

Indeed, then quasi-orthogonality in (

) and orthogonality in Haar
projections will be used to finish control of the stopping form. In fact we

\left|\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{s}top}}^{F}\left(f,g\right)\right|\leq C\mathrm{P}\mathsf{E}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\sigma)}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\ ,\ \ \ \ \ \text{for all }F\in\mathcal{F},

where
\mathrm{P}\mathsf{E}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\sigma,\omega\right)
is
defined in (

). The key technical estimate needed is the
following Stopping Child Lemma, which is a reformulation of the ‘straddling’
lemmas in M. Lacey
[
Lac

Lemma 10

(Stopping Child Lemma)
.

(a reformulation of
[
Lac
, Lemmas 3.19 and 3.26]
) Let
f\in L^{2}\left(\sigma\right)
,
g\in L^{2}\left(\omega\right)

\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}

\mathcal{F}
be
a collection of
\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}

\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)

\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}

stopping times with top

\alpha_{A}\left(S\right)\equiv\sup_{I\in\left(\Lambda_{f}^{\sigma}\left[S\right],A\right]\cap\mathcal{D}_{\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}}}\left|E_{I}^{\sigma}f\right|,\ \ \ \ \ \text{for }S\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(A\right),\ A\in\mathcal{A}.

\left|\mathsf{B}_{\mathcal{A}}^{A}\left(f,g\right)\right|\leq C\mathrm{P}\mathsf{E}_{\mathcal{A}}^{A,\mathop{\mathrm{t}rip}}\left(\Lambda_{g}^{\omega};\sigma,\omega\right)\sqrt{\sum_{S\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(A\right)}\left|S\right|_{\sigma}\alpha_{A}\left(S\right)^{2}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}.

Proof.

By the telescoping property of martingale differences, together with the
bound
\alpha_{A}\left(S\right)
on the averages of
\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(A\right)}^{\sigma}f
in the
\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}
intervals
in the tower
\left(\Lambda_{f}^{\sigma}\left[S\right],A\right]

the goodness assumption on
f
, we have

(4.5)

\left|\varphi_{J}^{S}\right|=\left|\sum_{I\in\left(\Lambda_{f}^{\sigma}\left[S\right],A\right]:\ J\subset_{\tau}I}\left(E_{I_{J}}^{\sigma}\bigtriangleup_{I}^{\sigma}f\right)\mathbf{1}_{A_{0}\setminus I_{J}}\right|\lesssim\alpha_{A}\left(S\right)\mathbf{1}_{F\setminus S}\ .

From the Monotonicity Lemma and the fact that

\Lambda_{g}^{\omega}\cap\mathcal{D}\left[S\right]\subset\left(\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}}^{F}\left(S\right)}K\right)\cup\mathcal{N}_{\tau}\left(S\right)

\tau

S

\displaystyle\left|\mathsf{B}_{\mathcal{A}}^{A}\left(f,g\right)\right|\leq\left|\sum_{S\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(A\right)}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{D}\left[S\right]:\ J\subset_{\tau}\Lambda_{f}^{\sigma}\left[S\right]}\left\langle H_{\sigma}\varphi_{J}^{S},\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\rangle_{\omega}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(A\right)}\sum_{J\in\Lambda_{g}^{\omega}:\ J\subset_{\tau}\Lambda_{f}^{\sigma}\left[S\right]}\frac{\mathrm{P}\left(J,\left|\varphi_{J}^{S}\right|\sigma\right)}{\ell\left(J\right)}\left\|\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}x\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}\left\|\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(A\right)}\alpha_{A}\left(S\right)\sum_{K\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}}^{F,\mathop{\mathrm{t}rip}}\left(S\right)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in\Lambda_{g}^{\omega}:\ J\subset_{\tau}\Lambda_{f}^{\sigma}\left[S\right]\\

\displaystyle+\sum_{S\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(A\right)}\alpha_{A}\left(S\right)\sum_{J\in\Lambda_{g}^{\omega}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\tau}\left(S\right):\ J\subset_{\tau}\Lambda_{f}^{\sigma}\left[S\right]}\frac{\mathrm{P}\left(J,\mathbf{1}_{F\setminus S}\sigma\right)}{\ell\left(J\right)}\left\|\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}x\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}\left\|\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}

\displaystyle\left|\mathsf{B}_{\mathcal{A}}^{A}\right|_{\mathop{\mathrm{s}traddle}}^{\mathop{\mathrm{t}rip}}\left(f,g\right)+\left|\mathsf{B}_{\mathcal{A}}^{A}\right|_{\mathop{\mathrm{s}traddle}}^{\mathop{\mathrm{n}ear}}\left(f,g\right).

Since

\frac{\mathrm{P}\left(J,\mathbf{1}_{F\setminus S}\sigma\right)}{\ell\left(J\right)}\lesssim\frac{\mathrm{P}\left(K,\mathbf{1}_{F\setminus S}\sigma\right)}{\ell\left(K\right)},

for
K\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}}^{F,\mathop{\mathrm{t}rip}}\left(S\right)\cup\mathcal{N}_{\tau}\left(S\right)
, we have with
\widehat{\mathsf{P}}_{S;K}^{\omega}\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in\Lambda_{g}^{\omega}:\ J\subset_{\tau}\Lambda_{f}^{\sigma}\left[S\right]\\
J\subset W\end{subarray}}\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathsf{B}_{\mathcal{A}}^{A}\right|_{\mathop{\mathrm{s}traddle}}^{\mathop{\mathrm{t}rip}}\left(f,g\right)\leq\sum_{S\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(A\right)}\alpha_{A}\left(S\right)\sum_{K\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}}^{F}\left(S\right)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in\Lambda_{g}^{\omega}:\ J\subset_{\tau}\Lambda_{f}^{\sigma}\left[S\right]\\
J\subset K\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{P}\left(K,\mathbf{1}_{F\setminus S}\sigma\right)}{\ell\left(K\right)}\left\|\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}x\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}\left\|\bigtriangleup_{J}^{\omega}g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(A\right)}\alpha_{A}\left(S\right)\sum_{K\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}}^{F}\left(S\right)}\frac{\mathrm{P}\left(K,\mathbf{1}_{F\setminus S}\sigma\right)}{\ell\left(K\right)}\left\|\widehat{\mathsf{P}}_{S;K}^{\omega}x\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}\left\|\widehat{\mathsf{P}}_{S;K}^{\omega}g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathrm{P}\mathsf{E}_{\mathcal{A}}^{A,\mathop{\mathrm{t}rip}}\left(\Lambda_{g}^{\omega};\sigma,\omega\right)\sum_{S\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(A\right)}\alpha_{A}\left(S\right)\sum_{K\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}}^{F}\left(S\right)}\sqrt{\left|K\right|_{\sigma}}\left\|\widehat{\mathsf{P}}_{S;K}^{\omega}g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathrm{P}\mathsf{E}_{\mathcal{A}}^{A,\mathop{\mathrm{t}rip}}\left(\Lambda_{g}^{\omega};\sigma,\omega\right)\sum_{S\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(A\right)}\alpha_{A}\left(S\right)\sqrt{\sum_{K\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}}^{F}\left(S\right)}\left|K\right|_{\sigma}}\sqrt{\sum_{K\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathop{\mathrm{g}ood}}^{F}\left(S\right)}\left\|\widehat{\mathsf{P}}_{S;K}^{\omega}g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathrm{P}\mathsf{E}_{\mathcal{A}}^{A,\mathop{\mathrm{t}rip}}\left(\Lambda_{g}^{\omega};\sigma,\omega\right)\sqrt{\sum_{S\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(A\right)}\left|S\right|_{\sigma}\alpha_{A}\left(S\right)^{2}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}.

The corresponding bound for the form
\left|\mathsf{B}_{\mathcal{A}}^{A}\right|_{\mathop{\mathrm{s}traddle}}^{\mathop{\mathrm{n}ear}}\left(f,g\right)
is
similar, but easier since there are at most

4.2.2.
Dual tree decomposition

To control the local stopping forms
\mathsf{B}_{\mathop{\mathrm{s}top}}^{F}\left(f,g\right)
, we need to introduce further corona decompositions within each
corona
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)
. These coronas will be
associated to stopping intervals
\mathcal{U}\left[F\right]\subset\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(F\right)
, whose construction uses a dual
tree decomposition originating with M. Lacey in
[
Lac
]
. For the sake of
generality, we present the decomposition in the setting of trees that are
not necessarily dyadic.

Definition 11
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a tree with root
o
.

(1)

Let
P\left(\alpha\right)\equiv\left\{\beta\in\mathcal{T}:\beta\succeq\alpha\right\}
and
S\left(\alpha\right)\equiv\left\{\beta\in\mathcal{T}:\beta\preceq\alpha\right\}
denote the predessor and
successor sets of
\alpha\in\mathcal{T}
.

(2)

A
geodesic

\mathfrak{g}
is a maximal linearly ordered subset
of
\mathcal{T}
. A finite geodesic
\mathfrak{g}
is an interval
\mathfrak{g}=\left[\alpha,\beta\right]=P\left(\beta\right)\setminus S\left(\alpha\right)
, and an infinite geodesic is an interval
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}\setminus P\left(\alpha\right)
for some
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}
.

(3)

A
stopping time
3
3

3

This definition of stopping time used in the theory of trees is a slight
variant of what has been used above, but should cause no confusion.

T
for
a tree
\mathcal{T}
is a subset
T\subset\mathcal{T}
such that

S\left(\beta\right)\cap S\left(\beta^{\prime}\right)=\emptyset\text{
for all }\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in T\text{ with }\beta\neq\beta^{\prime}.

(4)

A sequence
\left\{T_{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{N}
of stopping times
T_{n}

is
decreasing
if, for every
\beta\in T_{n+1}
with
0\leq n<N
,
there is
\beta^{\prime}\in T_{n}
such that
S\left(\beta\right)\subset S\left(\beta^{\prime}\right)
. We think of such a sequence as
getting further from the root as
n
increases.

(5)

For
T
a stopping time in
\mathcal{T}
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{T}
,
we define

\left[T,\alpha\right)\equiv\bigcup_{\beta\in T}\left[\beta,\alpha\right),

where the interval
\left[\beta,\alpha\right)=\emptyset
unless
\beta\prec\alpha
. In the case
\left[T,\alpha\right)=\emptyset
, we write
\alpha\preceq T
, and in the case
\left[T,\alpha\right)\not=\emptyset
, we write
\alpha\succ T
. The set
\left[T,\alpha\right)
can be
thought of as the set of points in the tree
\mathcal{T}
that ‘lie between’

T
and
\alpha
but are strictly less than
\alpha
.

(6)

For any
\alpha\in\mathcal{T}
, we define the set of its
children

\mathfrak{C}_{T}\left(\alpha\right)
to consist of the
maximal
elements
\beta\in\mathcal{T}
such that
\beta\prec\alpha
.

We define the dual integration operator
I^{\ast}
on
\mathcal{T}
by
I^{\ast}\mu\left(\alpha\right)\equiv\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{T}:\ \beta\preccurlyeq\alpha}\mu\left(\beta\right)
. Here is the dual
stopping time lemma that abstracts that of M. Lacey in
[
Lac
]
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a tree with root
o
, and suppose there
is a uniform bound on the number of children of any tree element. Suppose
further that
\mu:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\left[0,\infty\right)
is
nontrivial with finite support, and let
T_{0}
be the stopping time
consisting of the minimal tree elements in the support of
\mu
. Fix
\Gamma>1
. If there is no element
\alpha\in\mathcal{T}
with
I^{\ast}\mu\left(\alpha\right)>\Gamma\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{T}:\ \beta\prec\alpha}I^{\ast}\mu\left(\beta\right)
, we say that
\mu
is
\Gamma
-irreducible. Otherwise, there is a unique increasing sequence
\left\{T_{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{N+1}
, with
T_{N+1}=\left\{o\right\}
, of stopping
times
T_{n}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\leq N
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle I^{\ast}\mu\left(\alpha\right)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\Gamma\sum_{\beta\in T_{n-1}:\ \beta\prec\alpha}I^{\ast}\mu\left(\beta\right),\ \ \ \ \ \text{for
all }\alpha\in T_{n}\ ;

\displaystyle I^{\ast}\mu\left(\gamma\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Gamma\sum_{\beta\in T_{n-1}:\ \beta\prec\gamma}I^{\ast}\mu\left(\beta\right),\text{ \ \ \ \ for
all }\gamma\in\left[\alpha,T_{n-1}\right)\text{ with }\alpha\in T_{n}\ ;

\displaystyle I^{\ast}\mu\left(o\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Gamma\sum_{\beta\in T_{N}:\ \beta\prec\gamma}I^{\ast}\mu\left(\beta\right)\ .

Moreover, for
1\leq n\leq N+1
, this unique sequence
\left\{T_{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{N+1}
satisfies

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left|\left(T_{n-1},\alpha\right)\right|_{\mu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\Gamma-1\right)\sum_{\beta\in T_{n-1}:\ \beta\prec\alpha}I^{\ast}\mu\left(\beta\right),\ \ \ \ \ \text{for all }\alpha\in T_{n}\ ,

\displaystyle\left|\left(T_{n-1},\gamma\right]\right|_{\mu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\Gamma-1\right)\sum_{\beta\in T_{n-1}:\ \beta\prec\gamma}I^{\ast}\mu\left(\beta\right),\ \ \ \ \ \text{for all }\gamma\in S\left(\alpha\right)\setminus\left\{\alpha\right\}\setminus\bigcup_{\delta\in T_{n-1}}S\left(\delta\right)\ .

Proof.

If
T_{n}
is already defined, let
T_{n+1}
consist of all minimal points
\alpha\in\mathcal{T}
satisfying
I^{\ast}\mu\left(\alpha\right)>\Gamma\sum_{\beta\in T_{n}:\ \beta\prec\alpha}I^{\ast}\mu\left(\beta\right)
, provided at least one such point

exists. If not
then set

and define
T_{N+1}\equiv\left\{o\right\}
. It is easy to
see that
\left\{T_{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{N+1}
is an increasing sequence of
stopping times that satisfies (
4.6
), and is unique with these
properties. Moreover (
4.7
) also holds since for
\alpha\in T_{n}
we have

\displaystyle\left|\left(T_{n-1},\alpha\right)\right|_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{T}}\left(\alpha\right)}\left|\left(T_{n-1},\gamma\right)\right|_{\mu}=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{T}}\left(\alpha\right)}\left(I^{\ast}\mu\left(\gamma\right)-\sum_{\beta\in T_{n-1}:\ \beta\preccurlyeq\gamma}I^{\ast}\mu\left(\beta\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{T}}\left(\alpha\right)}\left(\Gamma\sum_{\beta\in T_{n-1}:\ \beta\prec\gamma}I^{\ast}\mu\left(\beta\right)-\sum_{\beta\in T_{n-1}:\ \beta\preccurlyeq\gamma}I^{\ast}\mu\left(\beta\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\Gamma-1\right)\sum_{\gamma\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathcal{T}}\left(\alpha\right)}\sum_{\beta\in T_{n-1}:\ \beta\prec\gamma}I^{\ast}\mu\left(\beta\right)=\left(\Gamma-1\right)\sum_{\beta\in T_{n-1}:\ \beta\prec\alpha}I^{\ast}\mu\left(\beta\right),

and the same argument proves the second line in (

),
since
\gamma\in S\left(\alpha\right)\setminus\left\{\alpha\right\}\setminus\bigcup_{\delta\in T_{n-1}}S\left(\delta\right)
was
not

chosen by the stopping criterion in the first line of (
4.6
),
and hence

I^{\ast}\mu\left(\gamma\right)\leq\Gamma\sum_{\beta\in T_{n-1}:\ \beta\prec\gamma}I^{\ast}\mu\left(\beta\right).

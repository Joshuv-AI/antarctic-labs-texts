On Presburger arithmetic extended with non-unary counting quantifiers \rsuper * Thanks: The authors thank Christian Schwarz from TU Ilmenau for proofreading the paper and for correcting two mistakes. They also thank the reviewers of this paper for pointing to some inaccuracies.
Peter Habermehl ORCID:0000-0002-7982-0946, Dietrich Kuske

Abstract
. We consider a first-order logic for the integers with addition. This logic extends classical first-order logic by modulo-counting, threshold-counting and exact-counting quantifiers, all applied to tuples of variables (here, residues are given as terms while moduli and thresholds are given explicitly). Our main result shows that satisfaction for this logic is decidable in two-fold exponential space. If only threshold- and exact-counting quantifiers are allowed, we prove an upper bound of alternating two-fold exponential time with linearly many alternations. This latter result almost matches Berman’s exact complexity of first-order logic without counting quantifiers. To obtain these results, we first translate threshold- and exact-counting quantifiers into classical first-order logic in polynomial time (which already proves the second result). To handle the remaining modulo-counting quantifiers for tuples, we first reduce them in doubly exponential time to modulo-counting quantifiers for single elements. For these quantifiers, we provide a quantifier elimination procedure similar to Reddy and Loveland’s procedure for first-order logic and analyse the growth of coefficients, constants, and moduli appearing in this process. The bounds obtained this way allow to restrict quantification in the original formula to integers of bounded size which then implies the first result mentioned above. Our logic is incomparable with the logic considered by Chistikov et al. in 2022. They allow more general counting operations in quantifiers, but only unary quantifiers. The move from unary to non-unary quantifiers is non-trivial, since, e.g., the non-unary version of the Härtig quantifier results in an undecidable theory.

(1)

]

(2)

We provide a quantifier elimination procedure for the logic

\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x},\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\bigr]

(3)

Analysing the size of constants, coefficients, and moduli

\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x},\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\bigr]

from

2.1.
An excursion into Presburger arithmetic

Berman proved in
[
Ber80
]
that Presburger arithmetic is complete
for the class STA
(*,2^{2^{O(n)}},n)
of all problems that can be
solved by an alternating Turing machine in doubly exponential time
with
n
alternations. Here, we are mainly interested in the proof of
the upper bound. He presents this proof in a very sketchy way
essentially saying that Ferrante and Rackoff have shown in

[
FR79
]
that quantification can be reduced to integers of at
most triply exponential size (which can be represented in doubly
exponential space). It should be noted that this latter result holds
for any formula, no matter whether it is in prenex normal form or it
contains the Boolean connective
\leftrightarrow
. Berman’s result
actually means that the algorithm by Ferrante and Rackoff can be
implemented on an alternating Turing machine with the above time and
alternation bound. Looking into the algorithm from
[
FR79
]
, one
sees that the formula is first transformed into prenex normal form and
that then, the alternation of the Turing machine equals the quantifier
alternation depth of the resulting formula. Note that turning a
formula into prenex normal form is possible in polynomial time
whenever the Boolean connectives are restricted to
\lnot
,
\lor
,

\land
, and
\to
. Differently here, we also allow the connective

\leftrightarrow
which gives a convenient way to write certain
formulas succinctly. But in the presence of this connective, we do not
know how to compute equivalent formulas in prenex normal form in
polynomial time.

For later reference, we now sketch a proof that, also in the presence
of
\leftrightarrow
, Berman’s upper bound holds. Since the
computation of prenex normal forms is too costly, we need another
bound for the alternation. To this aim, we define the
block
depth
of a formula. Intuitively, the block depth
\mathrm{bd}^{\mathrm{FO}}(\alpha)
of
the formula
\alpha\in\mathrm{FO}
bounds the number of blocks of
existential quantifiers along any path in the syntax tree of
\alpha
.

{defi}

•

\mathrm{BD}^{\mathrm{FO}}_{0}
is the set of atomic formulas.

•

For
n\geqslant 1
, the set
\mathrm{BD}^{\mathrm{FO}}_{n}
contains the formulas of the form

\exists x_{1}\,\exists x_{2}\,\dots\,\exists x_{m}\colon\beta
where

m\geqslant 0
and
\beta
is a Boolean combination (possibly using

\lnot
,
\land
,
\lor
,
\to
, and
\leftrightarrow
) of
formulas from
\mathrm{BD}^{\mathrm{FO}}_{n-1}
.

•

The
block depth

\mathrm{bd}^{\mathrm{FO}}(\alpha)
of a formula
\alpha\in\mathrm{FO}
is
the minimal natural number
n
with
\alpha\in\mathrm{BD}^{\mathrm{FO}}_{n}
.

Note that the block depth of any formula is at most half of its depth
(which is the maximal length of a branch in the syntax tree) and
therefore half of its length.

With this definition in place, we can now formulate Berman’s upper
bound for first-order logic in presence of the Boolean connective

\leftrightarrow
.

Theorem 2
.

There is an alternating Turing machine that, on input of a closed
formula
\varphi\in\mathrm{FO}
, decides in time doubly exponential in

|\varphi|
with
2\,\mathrm{bd}^{\mathrm{FO}}(\varphi)\leqslant|\varphi|
alternations whether

\varphi
holds or not.

Proof 2.1

(Proof sketch)
.

The alternating algorithm runs as follows:

•

If
\varphi
is atomic, then validity of the closed formula

\varphi
is checked deterministically.

•

Now let
\varphi=\exists x_{1}\dots\exists x_{m}\colon\psi
where

\psi
is a Boolean combination of formulas

\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{\ell}
of block depth at most
n
. Then the
alternating algorithm first guesses
m
integers
k_{1},\dots,k_{m}

of bounded size (which suffices by
[
FR79
]
) as well as a set

X\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,\ell\}
. Then, it branches universally
checking that

(1)

the Boolean combination
\psi
holds while assuming
X
is the set
of indices
i
such that
\sigma_{i}(k_{1},\dots,k_{m})
holds,

(2)

for all
i\in X
, the closed formula

\sigma_{i}(k_{1},\dots,k_{m})
holds, and

(3)

for all
j\in\{1,2,\dots,\ell\}\setminus X
, the closed formula

\sigma_{j}(k_{1},\dots,k_{m})
does not hold.

Thus, the algorithm first branches existentially and then
universally before checking whether the corresponding formulas

\sigma_{i}
of block depth
\leqslant n
hold or not.

3.
Existential and unary modulo-counting quantifiers suffice

In this section, we will transform a formula from
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x},\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\bigr]
into an
equivalent one from
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]
. Note that the logic
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]
is an
intermediate logic between these two logics:

•

In the logic
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x},\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\bigr]
, we can use the non-unary
threshold- and exact-counting quantifiers

\exists^{\geqslant c}(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})
and

\exists^{=c}(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})
while
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]
does not
allow threshold- and exact-counting quantification.

•

\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]
allows non-unary modulo-counting
quantifiers
\exists^{(t,p)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})
with
t
an
arbitrary term while
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]
allows only unary
modulo-counting quantification of the form
\exists^{(q,p)}x
with

q\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

We will transform a formula from
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x},\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\bigr]
first into an equivalent
formula from
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]
, i.e., we will eliminate threshold counting
quantifiers. In a second step, the resulting formula from
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]

will be translated into an equivalent one from
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]
, i.e., we will
eliminate non-unary modulo-counting quantifiers as well as terms as
residue. Both these transformations will leave the sets of
coefficients, constants, and moduli unchanged; the first
transformation will be done in polynomial time while the second one
uses doubly exponential time.

3.1.
Elimination of threshold- and exact-counting quantifiers

Here, we give the transformation from
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x},\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\bigr]
to
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]
.
We will provide a polynomial-time transformation that does not change
the sets
Coeff
,
Const
, and
Mod
. In addition, this
transformation will not introduce new modulo-counting quantifiers so
that formulas from
\mathrm{FO}[\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}]
get translated into equivalent formulas

\varphi
from first-order logic
2
2

2

Stefan Göller (private
communication) explained to us a polynomial translation of formulas
from
\mathrm{FO}[\exists^{\geqslant c}x]
to
\mathrm{FO}
. The work in this section is
an extension and elaboration of his idea.
whose validity can then
be checked using Theorem
2

We now come to the translation, i.e., to the elimination of threshold-
and exact-counting quantifiers for tuples. First note that the
formulas
\exists^{=c}\overline{y}\colon\varphi
and

\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{y}\colon\varphi\land\lnot\exists^{\geqslant c+1}\overline{y}\colon\varphi
are clearly equivalent, i.e., semantically, there is
no need for the exact-counting quantifier
\exists^{=c}
. But applying
this replacement to all exact-counting quantifiers in a formula
increases the size of the formula exponentially. Similarly,

\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{y}\colon\varphi
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\exists\overline{y_{1}}\,\exists\overline{y_{2}}\,\dots\,\exists\overline{y_{c}}\colon

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant c}\lnot\overline{y_{i}}=\overline{y_{j}}

\displaystyle\land\forall\overline{y}\colon\biggl(\Bigl(\bigvee_{1\leqslant i\leqslant c}\overline{y}=\overline{y_{i}}\Bigr)\to\varphi\biggr)

(where
(y^{1},y^{2},\dots,y^{\ell})=(y_{i}^{1},\dots,y_{i}^{\ell})
abbreviates

\bigwedge_{1\leqslant j\leqslant\ell}y^{j}=y^{j}_{i}
). Since the constant
c
is
written in binary, already the prefix of existential quantifiers is of
exponential length, i.e., also this transformation incurs an
exponential blow-up in formula size. Finally note that the non-unary
quantifiers
\exists\overline{y}
and
\forall\overline{y}
are equivalent to

\exists y^{1}\,\exists y^{2}\cdots\exists y^{\ell}
and

\forall y^{1}\,\forall y^{2}\cdots\forall y^{\ell}
, respectively.

Thus, we saw that any formula from
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x},\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\bigr]
can be transformed
into an equivalent one from
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]
(and similarly for
\mathrm{FO}[\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}]
and

\mathrm{FO}
), but at the cost of an exponential size increase. Our first
result shows that this size increase can be avoided.

The crucial part in this construction is the elimination of a
threshold- or exact-counting quantifier in front of a formula from

\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]
or from
\mathrm{FO}
, respectively. This construction adapts a
binary search strategy. For instance, the formula

\exists^{=2c}y\colon y_{0}\leqslant y<y_{1}\land\varphi(y)
expresses
that the interval
3
3

3

All intervals in this paper are
considered as sets of integers or of tuples of integers.

[y_{0},y_{1})
contains precisely
2c
many numbers
y
satisfying

\varphi
. This is equivalent to saying that there exists some number

y_{\frac{1}{2}}
in the said interval such that both intervals

[y_{0},y_{\frac{1}{2}})
and
[y_{\frac{1}{2}},y_{1})
contain precisely
c

numbers satisfying
\varphi
. The constructed formula then contains
the conjunction of the two formulas

\exists^{=c}y\colon\bigl(y_{0}\leqslant y<y_{\frac{1}{2}}\land\varphi(y)\bigr)
and

\exists^{=c}y\colon\bigl(y_{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant y<y_{1}\land\varphi(y)\bigr)
. Therefore, using this binary-search idea alone does
not prevent an exponential blow-up. The solution is to replace the
conjunction of these two formulas by an expression of the form

\forall a,b\colon\Bigl((a,b)\in\{(y_{0},y_{\frac{1}{2}}),(y_{\frac{1}{2}},y_{1})\}\to\exists^{=c}y\colon\bigl(a\leqslant y<b\land\varphi(y)\bigr)\Bigr)\,.

This idea (known as Fischer-Rabin-trick) goes back to
[
FR74
]

where it is attributed to earlier work by Fischer and Meyer as well
as by Strassen without specifying concrete publications.

A similar idea transforms the formula

\exists^{=2c+1}y\colon y_{0}\leqslant y<y_{1}\land\varphi(y)
into

\exists y_{\frac{1}{2}}\colon\begin{array}[t]{cl}&y_{0}<y_{\frac{1}{2}}<y_{1}\\
\land&\varphi(y_{\frac{1}{2}})\\
\land&\forall a,b\colon\Bigl((a,b)\in\{(y_{0},y_{\frac{1}{2}}),(y_{\frac{1}{2}}+1,y_{1})\}\to\exists^{=c}y\colon\bigl(a\leqslant y<b\land\varphi(y)\bigr)\Bigr)\,.\end{array}

Note that this results in an exponential increase in formula size
since the formula
\varphi
is mentioned twice. To avoid this size
increase, we “postpone” the evaluation of the formula

\varphi(y_{\frac{1}{2}})
. Slightly more precisely, the above
construction proceeds recursively since in both cases, we have the
subformula

\exists^{=c}y\colon\bigl(a\leqslant y<b\land\varphi(y)\bigr)
. Along
this recursion, we collect in some set
V
all the variables

y_{\frac{1}{2}}
seen in between that are required to satisfy

\varphi
. At the very end of the recursion, we write down the formula

\forall y\colon\bigl((\bigvee_{x\in V}x=y)\to\varphi(y)\bigr)

expressing all the “postponed” requirements at once.

The above idea is based on the linear order on the integers. If we
consider the non-unary quantifier
\exists^{=c}\overline{y}
, the role
of this linear order
\leqslant
is played by the lexicographic order on
tuples
\overline{y}
.

The proof of the following lemma formalises the above ideas. The
crucial requirement is that the formula and its block depth shall grow
only by a small summand (the latter makes sense only in case the
formula
\varphi
does not contain any modulo-counting quantifiers,

\mathrm{FO}
).

\alpha=\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{y}\colon\varphi

\varphi\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]

I_{1}=\bigl[f(\overline{z_{1}}),f(\overline{z_{2}}^{\prime})\bigr)
and
the open interval
I_{2}=\bigl(f(\overline{z_{2}}^{\prime}),f(\overline{z_{3}})\bigr)
and to verify
that both these intervals satisfy the

\psi_{n,V\cup\{\overline{z_{2}}^{\prime}\}}
, i.e., contain in particular
precisely
n
witnesses for
\varphi
, and that
f(\overline{z_{2}}^{\prime})
satisfies

\varphi
. Since

is the disjoint union of the intervals

,

\{f(\overline{z_{2}}^{\prime})\}
, and

, this ensures that the interval

contains precisely

\varphi
.

Then the formula

\exists\overline{z}_{\mathrm{left}},\overline{z}_{\mathrm{right}}\colon\bigl((\overline{z}_{\mathrm{left}}<^{\ell}_{\mathrm{lex}}\overline{z}_{\mathrm{right}}\land\psi_{c,\emptyset}\bigr)

is equivalent to
\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x}\colon\varphi
since it
expresses that some interval contains precisely

witnesses for

\varphi
. Furthermore, the formula

\exists\overline{z_{1}},\overline{z_{2}}\,\forall\overline{z}_{\mathrm{left}},\overline{z}_{\mathrm{right}}\colon\bigl((\overline{z}_{\mathrm{left}}\leqslant^{\ell}_{\mathrm{lex}}\overline{z_{1}}\land\overline{z_{2}}\leqslant^{\ell}_{\mathrm{lex}}\overline{z}_{\mathrm{right}})\to\psi_{c,\emptyset}\bigr)

is equivalent to
\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\colon\varphi
since it
expresses that for some interval, any superinterval contains
precisely
c
witnesses for
\varphi
.

It remains to analyse the size of the resulting formula as well as
the block depth in case
\varphi\in\mathrm{FO}
.

To estimate the size of
\psi_{c,\emptyset}
, note the following:

•

The size of the formulas
\psi_{0,V}
and
\psi_{1,V}
is of
the form
|\varphi|+O\bigl(\ell\cdot\log(c)\bigr)
since we allow
the Boolean connective
\leftrightarrow
in our formulas and since
the size of

is bounded by
\lceil\log(c)\rceil
(the formula
size doubles if we consider
\leftrightarrow
as abbreviation).

•

The size increase when moving from
\psi_{n,V}
to

\psi_{2n,V}
is bounded by a summand of size
O(\ell)
and the
same applies to the construction of
\psi_{2n+1,V}
from

\psi_{n,V\cup\{\overline{z_{2}}^{\prime}\}}
.

It follows that

|\psi_{c,\emptyset}|\leqslant|\varphi|+\varkappa\cdot\ell\cdot\log(c)

for some constant
\varkappa
. One sees easily that the same holds
for the formula
\psi
and that it can be constructed in time

|\varphi|+O\bigl(\ell\cdot\log(c)\bigr)
.

Now suppose
\varphi\in\mathrm{FO}
. Since in the construction, we only
introduce classical existential quantifiers, we obtain

\psi_{c,\emptyset}\in\mathrm{FO}
. We want to analyse the block depth of

\psi_{c,\emptyset}
. First note that

\displaystyle\mathrm{bd}^{\mathrm{FO}}(\psi_{0,V}),\mathrm{bd}^{\mathrm{FO}}(\psi_{1,V})

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{bd}^{\mathrm{FO}}(\varphi)+2\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{bd}^{\mathrm{FO}}(\psi_{2n,V})

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{bd}^{\mathrm{FO}}(\psi_{n,V})+2\,,\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathrm{bd}^{\mathrm{FO}}(\psi_{2n+1,V})

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{bd}^{\mathrm{FO}}(\psi_{n,V\cup\{\overline{z_{2}}^{\prime}\}})+2\,.

It follows that

\mathrm{bd}^{\mathrm{FO}}(\psi_{c,V})\leqslant\mathrm{bd}^{\mathrm{FO}}(\varphi)+2\cdot\lceil\log(c)\rceil
. In
the final step, the block depth increases by at most
2
. Hence we
obtain
\mathrm{bd}^{\mathrm{FO}}(\psi)\leqslant\mathrm{bd}^{\mathrm{FO}}(\varphi)+2\lceil\log(c)\rceil+2
.

The above lemma can be applied iteratively to all threshold- and
exact-counting quantifiers. Hence, from a formula from
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x},\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\bigr]
,
we obtain an equivalent formula in
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]
, and from a formula
from
\mathrm{FO}[\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}]
, we obtain a formula from
\mathrm{FO}
. In order to bound the block
depth of this formula from
\mathrm{FO}
, we extend its definition to formulas
from
\mathrm{FO}[\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}]
as follows:

{defi}

•

\mathrm{BD}_{0}
is the set of atomic formulas.

•

For
n\geqslant 1
, the set
\mathrm{BD}_{n}
contains the formulas of the
following forms:

–

\exists x_{1}\,\exists x_{2}\,\dots\,\exists x_{m}\colon\beta

where

and
\beta
is a Boolean combination (possibly
using

,
\land
,
\lor
,
\to
, and

\mathrm{BD}_{n-1}

–

\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x}\colon\beta
or

\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\colon\beta

is a Boolean

\mathrm{BD}_{n-2\lceil\log_{2}c\rceil-2}

•

The

\mathrm{bd}(\alpha)
of a formula

is
the minimal natural number

.

Note that the block depth of a formula from

\mathrm{FO}[\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}]
is at most twice the length of the formula (since the constants

in
\exists^{\geqslant c}
and
\exists^{=c}
are written in

\alpha\in\mathrm{FO}

.

Proposition 4
.

From a formula
\varphi\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x},\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\bigr]
, one can construct in time
polynomial in
|\varphi|
an equivalent formula

\psi\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]
.

In addition, we have
\textsc{Coeff}(\psi)\subseteq\textsc{Coeff}(\varphi)
,

\textsc{Const}(\psi)\subseteq\textsc{Const}(\varphi)
, and

\textsc{Mod}(\psi)\subseteq\textsc{Mod}(\varphi)
.

Furthermore, if
\varphi\in\mathrm{FO}[\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}]
, then the resulting formula
\psi

belongs to
\mathrm{FO}
and the block depth
\mathrm{bd}(\psi)
of
\psi
equals
that of
\varphi
.

Proof 3.2
.

Let
\varphi_{0}=\varphi
contain
n
threshold- or exact-counting
quantifiers. We construct, inductively, formulas
\varphi_{i+1}

from
\varphi_{i}
using Lemma

that contain one

, suppose we eliminate a quantifier of the form

\exists^{\geqslant c_{i}}(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell_{i}})
or

\exists^{=c_{i}}(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell_{i}})
. Then
\varphi_{i+1}
can be

\varphi_{i}
in time

. Since

and

\sum_{0\leqslant i<n}\log(c_{i})\leqslant|\varphi|
, the construction of

.

Now suppose

\mathrm{FO}
. Furthermore, when moving from

From Berman’s upper bound for Presburger arithmetic, we get
immediately the following for the logic

Corollary 5
.

Satisfaction of a closed formula

Proof 3.3
.

The transformation of
\varphi
into an equivalent closed formula

\psi
from

increases the size of the formula only

. Hence the claim follows from Berman’s

.

Somewhat surprisingly, the above result says that adding the

\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x}
and

does

[

]
.

3.2.
Elimination of non-unary modulo-counting quantifiers

Here, we give the transformation from
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]
to
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]
.
We will provide a transformation that can be computed in doubly
exponential time and does not change the sets
Coeff
,
Const
, and

Mod
, nor the quantifier depth.

The crucial task in this section is to express a non-unary
quantification
\exists^{(t,p)}(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})
(where the
remainder is given as a term
t
) using only unary modulo-counting
quantifications where the remainder is given as a constant. The first
step is obvious: Using a case distinction, we replace

\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{y}\colon\varphi
by the disjunction of all
formulas
t\equiv_{p}r\land\exists^{(r,p)}\overline{y}\colon\varphi
for

0\leqslant r<p
. As a second step, one has to eliminate the quantification
over a tuple
\overline{y}
. We explain the basic idea using the formula

\exists^{(0,2)}(y_{1},y_{2})\colon\rho(y_{1},y_{2})
where
\rho
is some
formula and
R
is the set of pairs of integers satisfying
\rho
. We
have to express that
R
is finite and its number of elements is even.

Assuming
R
to be finite, its size is even iff the number of elements

y_{1}
with

\Bigl|\bigl\{y_{2}\bigm|(y_{1},y_{2})\in R\bigr\}\Bigr|\text{ odd}

is even. This can be expressed by the formula

\exists^{(0,2)}y_{1}\,\exists^{(1,2)}y_{2}\colon\rho(y_{1},y_{2})\,.

Further,
R
is finite iff its number of elements is even or odd. But
this would not eliminate the non-unary quantification. Alternatively,

R
is finite iff it is bounded, i.e., if

\exists z\,\forall y_{1}\,\forall y_{2}\,\bigl(\rho(y_{1},y_{2})\to|y_{1}|,|y_{2}|\leqslant z\bigr)

holds. Although being a simple formula, its quantifier rank is larger
than that of the formula we started with. Yet another
characterisation of finiteness of
R
is “only finitely many elements
can be extended to a tuple from
R
and no element can be extended in
infinitely many ways”. The following formula expresses precisely
this:

\displaystyle\exists^{(0,2)}y_{1}\,\exists y_{2}\colon\rho(y_{1},y_{2})\lor\exists^{(1,2)}y_{1}\,\exists y_{2}\colon\rho(y_{1},y_{2})

\displaystyle\land

\displaystyle\forall y_{1}\,\Bigl(\exists^{(0,2)}y_{2}\colon\rho(y_{1},y_{2})\lor\exists^{(1,2)}y_{2}\colon\rho(y_{1},y_{2})\Bigr)

The proof of the following lemma formalises this idea (and extends it
to other moduli and remainder given as terms). In other words, it
shows how to eliminate a single non-unary modulo-counting quantifier.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\alpha=\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{y}\colon\varphi
with

\varphi\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]
. There exists a formula
\psi\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]

with
\psi\iff\alpha
,
\textsc{Const}(\psi)=\textsc{Const}(\alpha)
,

\textsc{Coeff}(\psi)=\textsc{Coeff}(\alpha)
,
\textsc{Mod}(\psi)=\textsc{Mod}(\alpha)
, and

\mathrm{qd}(\psi)=\mathrm{qd}(\alpha)
.

Furthermore,
\psi
can be constructed from
\alpha
in time

O\bigl(p^{p\cdot\ell}\cdot|\alpha|\bigr)

where
\ell
is the length
of the tuple
\overline{y}
.

Note that the modulus
p
is given in binary. Hence the time bound
is doubly exponential in the size of the formula

\alpha
.

Proof 3.4
.

First suppose
\ell=1
and consider the formula

\psi:=\bigvee_{0\leqslant r<p}(r\equiv_{p}t\land\exists^{(r,p)}y_{1}\colon\varphi)

which is clearly equivalent to
\alpha
and has all the properties
required by the claim of the lemma.

So suppose
\ell>1
. First, we construct inductively a formula from

\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]
expressing that there are only finitely many tuples

\overline{y}
satisfying
\varphi
(for
0\leqslant n<\ell-1
):

\displaystyle\eta_{\ell-1}(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell-1})=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{0\leqslant i<p}\exists^{(i,p)}y_{\ell}\colon\varphi

\displaystyle\eta_{n}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})=

\displaystyle\bigvee_{0\leqslant i<p}\exists^{(i,p)}y_{n+1}\,\exists(y_{n+2},\dots,y_{\ell})\colon\varphi

\displaystyle\land\forall y_{n+1}\colon\eta_{n+1}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n+1})

Let
(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell-1})
be any tuple of integers. The formula

\eta_{\ell-1}
expresses that the tuple
(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell-1})

can be extended to a tuple satisfying
\varphi
in only finitely
many ways.

Now let
(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell-2})
be any tuple of integers. The first
line of the formula
\eta_{\ell-2}
expresses that the tuple

(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell-2})
can be extended to a tuple

(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell-1})
that allows a further extension to a tuple
satisfying
\varphi
in only finitely many ways. The second line
expresses that, for any integer
y_{\ell-1}
, there are only
finitely many extensions of the tuple
(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell-1})
to a
tuple
(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})
satisfying
\varphi
. Hence,

\eta_{\ell-2}
expresses that there are only finitely many
extensions of
(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell-2})
satisfying
\varphi
.

Arguing inductively, we obtain that
\eta_{0}
expresses that there
are only finitely many tuples
(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})
satisfying

\varphi
.

Now consider the formula

\beta=\bigvee_{0\leqslant r<p}\bigl(r\equiv_{p}t\land\eta_{0}\land\exists^{(r,p)}\overline{y}\colon\varphi\bigr)

that is equivalent with
\alpha
. It remains to rewrite

\exists^{(r,p)}\overline{y}\colon\varphi
into a formula from

\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]
. In this construction, we can assume that
\eta_{0}

holds, i.e., that there are only finitely many tuples

(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})
satisfying
\varphi
. To this aim, consider the

\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]
-formulas (for

and

)

\displaystyle\delta_{\ell-1}^{d}(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell-1})

\displaystyle=\exists^{(d,p)}y_{\ell}\colon\varphi\text{ and}

\displaystyle\delta_{n}^{d}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{(*)}\bigwedge_{0<i<p}\exists^{(d_{i},p)}y_{n+1}\colon\delta_{n+1}^{i}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n+1})

where the disjunction
(*)
extends over all tuples

(d_{1},\dots,d_{p-1})
over

such that

\sum_{0<i<p}d_{i}\cdot i\equiv_{p}d\,.

(1)

Let
(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell-1})
be a tuple of integers. Then the

expresses that there are

to extend the tuple
(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell-1})
to a tuple satisfying

\varphi
(all counts in this paragraph are understood modulo

).
Next let
(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell-2})
be a tuple of integers. Then the

\delta_{\ell-2}^{d}
expresses that, for

, there are

that satisfy

many ways to a tuple satisfying
\varphi
. Thus, the
formula
\delta_{\ell-2}^{d}
expresses that the tuple

(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell-2})
can be extended in
d
many ways to a tuple
satisfying
\varphi
.
Arguing inductively, the formula
\delta_{0}^{d}
expresses that there
are
d
many tuples satisfying
\varphi
.

Setting

\psi:=\eta_{0}\land\bigvee_{0\leqslant r<p}\bigl(r\equiv_{p}t\land\delta_{0}^{r}\bigr)

we consequently get
\alpha\Leftrightarrow\beta\Leftrightarrow\psi\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]
.

Note that the construction of
\eta_{0}
and
\delta_{0}
leaves the
sets
\textsc{Coeff}(.)
,
\textsc{Mod}(.)
, and
\textsc{Const}(.)
and the
quantifier-depth unchanged.

It remains to bound the time needed to construct the formula
\psi
.
First,
\eta_{0}
can be constructed in time
O(\ell\cdot p\cdot|\alpha|)
since
the formula
\eta_{n+1}
appears only once in
\eta_{n}
. Next, any of
the formulas
\delta_{\ell-1}^{d}
can be constructed in time

. We now consider the construction of
\delta_{n}^{d}

from the formulas
\delta_{n+1}^{i}
. Note that the tuple

(d_{1},\dots,d_{p-2})
together with equation (

)
completely determines the value of

Proposition 7
.

From a formula
\varphi\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]

.

\textsc{Coeff}(\gamma)\subseteq\textsc{Coeff}(\varphi)
,

\textsc{Const}(\gamma)\subseteq\textsc{Const}(\varphi)
,

\textsc{Mod}(\gamma)\subseteq\textsc{Mod}(\varphi)
, and
\mathrm{qd}(\gamma)\leqslant\mathrm{qd}(\varphi)
.

Proof 3.5
.

Let
P
be the maximal value such that some modulo-counting

\exists^{(t,P)}
appears in the formula
\varphi
and
let

be the maximal arity of any modulo-counting quantifier in

\varphi
. Finally, let

be the number of non-unary

\varphi
.

Let
\varphi_{0}=\varphi
. To inductively construct
\varphi_{i+1}

\varphi_{i}
, we chose some subformula

with
\ell>1
and

. This subformula is replaced by an equivalent

. This reduces the number of non-unary

From Lemma

, we get

,

\textsc{Const}(\gamma)=\textsc{Const}(\varphi)
,

,

\textsc{Mod}(\gamma)=\textsc{Mod}(\varphi)
, and

.

, we get that

in time

can be

in time

. Furthermore,

. Consequently, the

.

an equivalent formula

.

\textsc{Coeff}(\gamma)\subseteq\textsc{Coeff}(\varphi)

, and

\textsc{Mod}(\gamma)\subseteq\textsc{Mod}(\varphi)
.

.

.

, one first constructs in

from

. This

,

from

4.
Quantifier elimination

This section provides a quantifier elimination procedure for the logic
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]

where, differently from the full logic
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x},\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\bigr]
, only unary
quantifications
\exists y
and
\exists^{(q,p)}y
with
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
are
allowed.

As usual with quantifier elimination procedures, we first demonstrate
how to eliminate a single quantifier in front of a Boolean combination
of atomic formulas. Since the classical existential quantifier and the
modulo-counting quantifier behave rather differently, we handle them
in separate Lemmas
10
and
11
. The main point in both
these lemmas is

(1)

properties of the form
\exists/\exists^{(q,p)}x\colon\beta

where
\beta
is quantifier-free can be expressed without
quantification and

(2)

the sets of coefficients, constants, and moduli vary in this
process, but these sets can be controlled.

Our quantifier elimination is effective, but we do not concentrate on
this fact. We do, in particular, not aim at a fast elimination
algorithm nor at small resulting formulas. All we need for our later
decision procedure is a bound on the size of the coefficients,
constants, and moduli appearing in the resulting formula.

For this bound, suppose
\beta
is a quantifier-free formula and
E

is a quantifier
\exists
or
\exists^{(q,p)}
. We will prove that

Ex\colon\beta
is equivalent to some quantifier-free formula
\gamma

whose sets of coefficients etc. are contained in the following sets
(with
p=1
in case
E=\exists
):

\displaystyle\textsc{Coeff}_{p}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\bigl\{a_{1}a_{2}-a_{3}a_{4}\bigm|a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\in\textsc{Coeff}(\beta)\bigr\}

\kappa\geqslant 2
with the following property. Let

and consider a formula
\varphi(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})

|n_{i}|\leqslant N
. Then the closed formula

\exists x\colon\varphi(x,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
holds if and only if
there exists

such that
\varphi(n,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
holds
with

\displaystyle|n|\leqslant 2^{\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)^{\kappa^{d}}}\cdot\max\textsc{Const}(\varphi)\cdot N\cdot\max\{1,\ell\}\,.

Proof 5.2
.

The implication “
\Leftarrow
” is trivial since, if there is a
small
n
satisfying
\varphi(x,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
, then
\exists x\colon\varphi(x,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
holds.

Conversely suppose
\exists x\colon\varphi(x,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})

holds. Let
\varphi_{\overline{n}}=\varphi_{\overline{n}}(x)
be the formula
obtained from
\varphi
by substituting
n_{i}
for
y_{i}
. For any
inequality
s<t
in
\varphi
, the term
s-t
contains at most

\ell
of the variables

, each with a coefficient from

\textsc{Coeff}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathbf{P}(\varphi)
. Hence these substitutions
at most eliminate coefficients, do not change moduli, but can
increase constants by
N\cdot\ell\cdot\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)
. Hence we
get

\displaystyle\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi_{\overline{n}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)\ \text{and}

\displaystyle\max\textsc{Const}(\varphi_{\overline{n}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\textsc{Const}(\varphi)+N\cdot\ell\cdot\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)\,.

By Theorem
13
, there exists an equivalent Boolean
combination
\gamma_{\overline{n}}
of atomic formulas with

\displaystyle\max\mathbf{P}(\gamma_{\overline{n}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)^{4^{d}}=:A\ \text{and }

\displaystyle\max\textsc{Const}(\gamma_{\overline{n}})

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)^{4^{d}}}\cdot(\max\textsc{Const}(\varphi)+N\cdot\ell\cdot\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi))

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)^{5^{d}}}\cdot\max\textsc{Const}(\varphi)\cdot N\cdot\max\{1,\ell\}=:B\,.

From Lemma
16
, we obtain that there is some
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with

|n|\leqslant A^{A^{5}}\cdot B
such that
\gamma_{\overline{n}}(n)
holds. Hence,
for this
n
, also
\varphi(n,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
holds. Note that
we have (with
p=\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)
)

A^{A^{5}}\leqslant\left(p^{4^{d}}\right)^{\left(p^{4^{d}}\right)^{5}}=p^{4^{d}\cdot p^{5\cdot 4^{d}}}\leqslant p^{p^{5\cdot 4^{d}+2d}}\leqslant 2^{p^{c^{d}}}=2^{\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)^{c^{d}}}

for some
c\geqslant 1
and therefore

\displaystyle|n|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)^{c^{d}}}\cdot 2^{\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)^{5^{d}}}\cdot\max\textsc{Const}(\varphi)\cdot N\cdot\max\{1,\ell\}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)^{\kappa^{d}}}\cdot\max\textsc{Const}(\varphi)\cdot N\cdot\max\{1,\ell\}

for some
\kappa\geqslant 2
.

In the following, we want to prove a similar result for the modulo-counting
quantifier. Recall that
\exists^{(q,p)}x\colon\varphi(x)
can only
be true if
\varphi
has only finitely many witnesses, i.e., if the
formula
\exists y\forall x\colon\bigl(\varphi(x)\to|x|\leqslant y\bigr)

is true. Applying the above corollary, one finds a finite interval
such that
\varphi
has infinitely many witnesses iff it has at least
one witness in this interval. In case
\varphi
has only finitely many
witnesses, then all of them are of bounded absolute value. More
precisely, we get the following.

Lemma 18
.

Let
d\geqslant 1
and
\kappa\geqslant 2
be the constant from
Corollary
17
. Furthermore, let

\varphi=\varphi(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]
be a formula of
quantifier-depth at most
d
, let
n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with

|n_{i}|\leqslant N
. Suppose there exist only finitely many
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such
that
\varphi(n,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
holds. Then all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such
that
\varphi(n,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
holds satisfy

|n|\leqslant 2^{\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)^{\kappa^{d+1}}}\cdot\max\textsc{Const}(\varphi)\cdot N\cdot\max\{1,\ell\}\,.

Proof 5.3
.

Since there are only finitely many
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\varphi(n,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
holds, the closed formula

\exists y\forall x\colon\bigl(\varphi(x,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})\to|x|\leqslant y\bigr)

(3)

holds. Let
\varphi^{\prime}
denote the subformula starting with
\forall x
. Note that its quantifier-depth equals
d+1
,

\mathbf{P}(\varphi)=\mathbf{P}(\varphi^{\prime})
, and

\textsc{Const}(\varphi)=\textsc{Const}(\varphi^{\prime})
. Hence, by Corollary
17
,
the above formula (
3

\exists y\colon\bigl(|y|\leqslant 2^{\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)^{\kappa^{d+1}}}\cdot\max\textsc{Const}(\varphi)\cdot N\cdot\max\{1,\ell\}\land\varphi^{\prime}\bigr)

and therefore to

\forall x\colon\bigl(\varphi(x,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})\to|x|\leqslant 2^{\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)^{\kappa^{d+1}}}\cdot\max\textsc{Const}(\varphi)\cdot N\cdot\max\{1,\ell\}\bigr)\,.

Now the claim follows since the formula (
3
) and
therefore this formula holds.

Corollary 19
.

Let
d\geqslant 1
and
\kappa
be the constant from Corollary
17
and

C=2^{\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)^{\kappa^{d+1}}}\cdot\max\textsc{Const}(\varphi)\cdot N\cdot\max\{1,\ell\}\,.

Let
\varphi=\varphi(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]
be a formula of
quantifier-depth at most
d
, let
n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with

|n_{i}|\leqslant N
. Then

\exists^{(q,p)}x\colon\varphi(x,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
is true if and
only if the following hold:

(1)

no integer
n
with
C<|n|\leqslant C^{2}
makes

\varphi(n,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
true and

(2)

\bigl|\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\colon|n|\leqslant C\text{ and
}\varphi(n,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})\text{ is true}\}\bigr|\equiv_{p}q
.

Proof 5.4
.

We first show that

\exists^{(q,p)}x\colon\varphi(x,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
is true if and
only if

(a
′
)

\forall x\colon\bigl(\varphi(x,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})\to|x|\leqslant C\bigr)
is true and

(b)

\bigl|\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\colon|n|\leqslant C\text{ and
}\varphi(n,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})\text{ is true}\}\bigr|\equiv_{p}q
.

Suppose there are infinitely many integers
n
such that

\varphi(n,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
holds. Then the formula

\exists^{(q,p)}x\colon\varphi
does not hold. Furthermore,
statement (a
′
) is false since there are only finitely many
integers
x
with
|x|\leqslant C
. Hence, in this case, the equivalence
holds.

So it remains to consider the case that there are only finitely many
integers
n
such that
\varphi(n,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})
holds. Then, by
Lemma
18
, all these integers satisfy
|n|\leqslant C
. Consequently,
statement (a

) is true and

\bigl\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\bigm|\varphi(n,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})\text{ holds}\bigr\}=\bigl\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\colon|n|\leqslant C\text{ and }\varphi(n,n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})\text{ holds}\bigr\}\,.

Hence, in this case,
\exists^{(q,p)}x\colon\varphi
is equivalent
to statement (b). Since (a
′

We complete the proof of this corollary by showing that (a)
and (a

\varphi^{\prime}=\bigl(\varphi(x,x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})\land|x|>C\bigr)\,.

Then

\displaystyle|n|

\displaystyle=2^{\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)^{\kappa^{d}}}\cdot C\cdot N\cdot\max\{1,\ell\}

Hence, statement (a

Corollaries
17

\varphi

\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]

\exists^{(q,p)}x\colon\psi(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\left(2^{\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)^{\kappa^{d+2}}}\cdot\max\textsc{Const}(\varphi)\right)^{4^{d}}

Proposition 20

Satisfaction of a closed formula
\varphi\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]

Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]

is at most

. Since coefficients etc. are written in binary,

\max\mathbf{P}(\varphi)

. Consequently, the proposition shows that satisfaction

\varphi\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]

Recall that for formulas from
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]
we require modulo-counting
quantifiers of the form
\exists^{(t,p)}(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})
to satisfy

. We now show that also without this
restriction, the doubly exponential space bound remains true.

Theorem 21
.

Satisfaction of a closed formula
\varphi\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]
can be decided
in space doubly exponential in
|\varphi|
.

Proof 5.5
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]
be a closed formula. By
Proposition
7
, we can compute in doubly exponential
time an equivalent closed formula
\gamma\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(q,p)}x]
without changing the
sets of coefficients, moduli, or constants and without increasing
the quantifier depth. Because of the time bound, this construction
requires at most doubly exponential space and
|\gamma|
is at most
doubly exponential in
|\varphi|
.

By Proposition
20
, validity of
\gamma
can be decided
using space
O(4^{\mathrm{qd}(\gamma)}\cdot\log D)
with
D
given by
Equation (
4
). Since
\gamma
and
\varphi
agree
on the sets of coefficients etc. and on the quantifier depth, this
value is doubly exponential in the size of
\varphi
.

Since Proposition
4
allows to translate formulas
from
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x},\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\bigr]
into equivalent formulas from
\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x}]
in
polynomial time, we also get the corresponding result for the logic

\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x},\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\bigr]

Corollary 22
.

Satisfaction of a closed formula
\varphi\in\mathrm{FO}\bigl[\exists^{(t,p)}\overline{x},\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}\bigr]
can be
decided in space doubly exponential in
|\varphi|
.

Note that this complexity matches the best known upper space bound for
Presburger arithmetic without modulo-counting quantifiers
from
[
FR79
]
. From
[
Ber80
]
, we know that Presburger
arithmetic can be decided in alternating doubly exponential time with
linearly many alternations (and our Corollary
5

extends this to the logic
\mathrm{FO}[\exists^{\geqslant c}\overline{x},\exists^{=c}\overline{x}]
). Our handling of the modulo-counting
quantifier requires us to count witnesses of bounded size. As the
number of potential witnesses is triply exponential, we do not see how
to do this using an alternating Turing machine in only doubly
exponential time.

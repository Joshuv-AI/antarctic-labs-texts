Ultrafast scattering dynamics of coherent phonons in Bi 1-x Sb x in the Weyl semimetal phase
Yuta Komori, Yuta Saito, Paul Fons, Muneaki Hase

Abstract
We investigate ultrafast phonon dynamics in the Bi 1-x Sb x alloy system for various compositions x x using a reflective femtosecond pump-probe technique. The coherent optical phonons corresponding to the A 1g local vibrational modes of Bi-Bi, Bi-Sb, and Sb-Sb are generated and observed in the time domain with a few picoseconds dephasing time. The frequencies of the coherent optical phonons were found to change as the Sb composition x x was varied, and more importantly, the relaxation time of those phonon modes was dramatically reduced for x x values in the range 0.5–0.8. We argue that the phonon relaxation dynamics are not simply governed by alloy scattering, but are significantly modified by anharmonic phonon-phonon scattering with implied minor contributions from electron-phonon scattering in a Weyl-semimetal phase.



Keywords
: coherent phonon, phonon scattering, ultrafast spectroscopy, topological materials.

For our samples, the

The two samples

2.2
Experimental methods

Time-resolved measurements using a reflection-type pump-probe setup were carried out to observe the coherent phonon signal
[
37

38

The light source was a mode-locked Ti:sapphire laser oscillator with a center wavelength of 830 nm, providing ultrashort pulses

The pump and probe beams were focused by an off-axis parabolic mirror on the samples to a diameter of

\approx

40

41

The fluence of the pump beam was fixed at

\approx
43

The penetration depth of the laser beam estimated from the absorption coefficients was about 17–20 nm

Thus, in the present study the optical excitation was homogeneous over the entire sample thickness and the effects of the penetration depth did not play a role on the

The time zero was determined as the position of the initial drop, which was confirmed with a Bi semimetal sample

The measurements were performed in air at room temperature.

Figure

In order to estimate the relaxation time of the coherent phonon mode for various Sb concentrations

Thus, the fitting function used was

\frac{\Delta R}{R}=H(t)\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{3}A_{i}\exp(-t/\tau_{i})\cos(\omega_{i}t+\phi_{i})+A_{c}\exp(-t/\tau_{c})\Bigr],

(1)

where

Figure
3

oscillations as a function of the Sb concentration (

Interestingly, the phonon scattering rate is largest for Sb concentrations of about

The scattering rate 1/

1/\tau_{\rm{Anharmonic}}

The

\gamma_{0}[1+n(\omega_{\rm{LA,TA}})+n(\omega_{\rm{A}_{1g}})]

[

\gamma_{0}
is the effective anharmonic constant,

Note that the anharmonic phonon–phonon scattering term is assumed to be a constant because the anharmonic decay channel depends mainly on the distribution of lower lying TA and LA phonons and thus on the lattice temperature.
For the general case with a constant crystal structure, changes in

We have estimated the lattice temperature rise for Bi (

In the latter sections, however, we will show

varies with the composition

The scattering rate due to the alloy scattering can be expressed using the phonon frequency
\omega

[
53
,
54
]
as,

\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm{Alloy}}(\omega)}=\frac{\pi}{6}V_{0}\Gamma_{\rm{Alloy}}{\omega}^{2}D(\omega),

(2)

where
V_{0}
is the volume per atom,
\Gamma_{\rm{Alloy}}
is an alloy constant, and
D(\omega)
is the vibrational density of states per unit volume.
Here, the total vibrational density of states is given by the sum over phonon branch

,

D(\omega)=\sum_{\mathrm{\it b}}{}\int\frac{d\vec{q}}{{(2\pi)}^{3}}\delta[\omega-\omega_{b}(\vec{q})],

(3)

where
\vec{q}
is the wavevector. The scattering-rate constant is given by three different contributions,

\Gamma_{\rm{Alloy}}(x)=\Gamma_{\rm{Mass}}(x)+\Gamma_{\rm{Iso}}(x)+\Gamma_{\rm{Strain}}(x),

(4)

where the first contribution is due to the mass difference, the second due the isotope effect, and third strain effects.
Here we focus on the mass difference, which plays the dominant role in scattering in the alloy Bi
1-x
Sb
x

[
52
]
. The mass difference constant is given by,

\Gamma_{\rm{Mass}}=\sum_{i}f_{i}{\left(1-\frac{M_{i}}{\overline{M}}\right)}^{2},

(5)

where
f_{i}
is the mass ratio of atoms with mass
M_{i}
, and
\overline{M}
is the average mass given by
\overline{M}=\sum_{i}f_{i}M_{i}
. The
\Gamma_{\rm{Mass}}(x)
term depends on the atomic concentration
x
, and therefore is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm{Mass}}(x)

\displaystyle=x(1-x)\frac{{(M_{\rm{Sb}}-M_{\rm{Bi}})}^{2}}{{[xM_{\rm{Sb}}+(1-x)M_{\rm{Bi}}]}^{2}}

(6)

\displaystyle=x(1-x)\frac{{(121.76u-208.98u)}^{2}}{{[x\cdot 121.76u+(1-x)\cdot 208.98u]}^{2}},

where the atomic mass of Bi is 208.98
u
, and that of Sb is 121.76
u
, with

being given in unified atomic mass units.
Thus, the
1/\tau_{\rm{Alloy}}(\omega)
term shown in equation (

) can be written as,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm{Alloy}}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{6}V_{0}{\omega}^{2}D(\omega)\cdot x(1-x)

(7)

\displaystyle\times\frac{{(121.76u-208.98u)}^{2}}{{[x\cdot 121.76u+(1-x)\cdot 208.98u]}^{2}}.

In addition, here, we apply the Einstein model for the

term shown in equation (
3

According to the Einstein model, equation (

\displaystyle D(\omega)=N\delta(\omega-\omega_{0})=\rm{const.},

(8)

where

is the number of unit cells. Thus,

is independent of the Sb concentration

can be finally written as,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm{Alloy}}}=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}{\omega}^{2}\cdot x(1-x)

(9)

\displaystyle\times\frac{{(121.76u-208.98u)}^{2}}{{[x\cdot 121.76u+(1-x)\cdot 208.98u]}^{2}},

where
\Gamma_{0}=\pi V_{0}D(\omega)/6
is a proportional constant.
Since the exact values of both

and

Sb

system, we held

We now discuss why the relaxation rate of the Sb-Sb vibrational mode around
x=0.5
is smaller than the corresponding theoretical value. One plausible explanation is that the anharmonic phonon-phonon scattering rate is accentuated by mass disorder when the phonon frequency decreases, as seen in Si

In the present study, around
x=0.5

In this case, the relaxation rate due to the
1/\tau_{\rm{Anharmonic}}
term should exist for the two modes (Bi-Bi and Bi-Sb vibrations) around
x=0.5
, while the
1/\tau_{\rm{Anharmonic}}
term for the Sb-Sb vibrational mode should decrease.

To test for the above possible scenario based on anharmonic phonon-phonon decay channels in Bi
1-x
Sb
x
around
x=0.5
,
we extracted the phonon dispersion from the literature as schematically shown in figure
5

[
17
]

Although there are some discrepancies between the frequencies of the optical phonon modes at the
\Gamma

5
and our observations [figure

<\omega<
2.8 THz.
Therefore, the decay channel of the Sb-Sb A
1g
mode (4.0 THz) is forbidden (red dashed arrows) while the other two optical phonon modes of the Bi-Bi and Bi-Sb A

Thus, phonon dispersion considerations can support the idea that the
1/\tau_{\rm{Anharmonic}}
term significantly decreases for the A

CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, we have investigated the dynamics of coherent optical phonons in Bi
1-x
Sb
x
for various Sb compositions
x
by using a femtosecond pump-probe technique. The coherent optical phonons corresponding to the A
1g
modes of Bi-Bi, Bi-Sb, and Sb-Sb local vibrations were generated and observed in the time domain.
The frequencies of the coherent optical phonons were found to change with the Sb composition
x
, and more importantly, the relaxation times of these phonons were strongly attenuated for
x
values in the range 0.5–0.8. We argue that the phonon relaxation dynamics are not simply governed by alloy scattering, e.g., scattering due to mass differences, but are significantly modified by alloy-induced anharmonic phonon-phonon decay channels with minor contributions from electron-phonon scattering in the Weyl semimetal phase, which is expected to appear at
x
= 0.5.

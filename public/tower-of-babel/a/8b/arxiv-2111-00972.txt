Nonparametric Cointegrating Regression Functions with Endogeneity and Semi-Long Memory
Sepideh Mosaferi, Mark S. Kaiser

Abstract
This article develops nonparametric cointegrating regression models with endogeneity and semi-long memory. We assume semi-long memory is produced in the regressor process by tempering of random shock coefficients. The fundamental properties of long memory processes are thus retained in the regressor process. Nonparametric nonlinear cointegrating regressions with serially dependent errors and endogenous regressors that are driven by long memory innovations have been considered in Wang and Phillips 2016 . That work also implemented a statistical specification test for testing whether the regression function follows a parametric form. The convergence rate of the proposed test is parameter dependent, and its limit theory involves the local time of fractional Brownian motion. The present paper modifies the test statistic proposed for the long memory case by Wang and Phillips 2016 to be suitable for the semi-long memory case. With this modification, the limit theory for the test involves the local time of standard Brownian motion. Through simulation studies, we investigate properties of nonparametric regression function estimation with semi-long memory regressors as well as long memory regressors. Keywords: Fractional differencing parameter; test statistic; standard Brownian motion; simulation studies; tempered process.



Introduction

Regression models that relate two time series have long been of interest in economics, and any number of quantities of interest in such models are nonstationary. If two variables in a linear regression model are integrated (in the time series sense) of the same order, but some linear combinations of them are stationary then the model is said to be a cointegrated regression model. That label is also used to refer to models in which the covariate or regressor process is nonstationary and the response process is related to it through a nonlinear function (see
Tjøstheim 2020
for a recent review). Nonlinear cointegrated regression models have been applied to, among others, problems in financial markets, stock prices, heavy traffic, and energy markets (e.g.,
Fasen 2013
and references therein).
In particular, let

y_{k}=f(x_{k})+u_{k},\qquad k=1,\ldots,N,

(1)

be a nonlinear cointegrating regression model, where
x_{k}
is a nonstationary regressor,
u_{k}
is an error process, and
f(\cdot)
is an unknown real function.

Two problems that have been the focus of much research are estimation of the unknown regression function
f(\cdot)
nonparametrically, and testing that
f(\cdot)
has a particular parametric form. Many of the theoretical results related to these problems
have assumed strict exogeneity in which the regressor
x_{k}
is assumed to be uncorrelated with the regression error
u_{k}
(e.g.,
Karlsen et al. 2007
,
Cai et al. 2009
,
Wang and Phillips 2009a
, and
Wang 2014
). Often, the regressor
x_{k}
is assumed to be a short memory process.
Extending this framework by allowing the regressor
x_{k}
to be driven by long memory innovations and permitting correlation with
u_{k}
(so-called endogeneity) has received less attention in the literature and leads to some technical problems that have not been completely resolved.

Recently,
Wang and Phillips 2016
established a limit theory for nonparametric estimation of regression models that allow for both endogeneity and long memory in the regressor process. They also developed a test statistic for parametric forms of regression functions of the type
f(x)=g(x,\theta_{0})
where
g(\cdot,\theta_{0})
represents a parametric family of functions with unknown parameter
\theta_{0}\in\Theta
. The authors assumed that
\Theta
is a compact subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
for some finite
m
.
The test of
Wang and Phillips 2016
is a modification of a test statistic given by
Hardle and Mammen 1993
for the random sample case. The test was also used in
Gao et al. 2012
for a nonlinear cointegrating model with a martingale error structure and no endogeneity.

A property of the test under long memory and endogeneity is that the limit distribution depends on the value of the fractional differencing parameter
d
in the nonstationary regressor
x_{k}
.
The motivation for the work reported here was that if one employs the idea of tempering and assumes that there are semi-long memory input shocks to the regressor process
x_{k}
, the limit distribution of the test statistic given by
Wang and Phillips 2016
no longer depends on the unknown parameter
d
.
We describe this idea in detail and list the consequences, which are related to the asymptotic theory for nonparametric cointegrating regression functions.

We now summarize the main contributions of this paper. First, we demonstrate asymptotic properties of kernel estimators of the unknown regression function
f(x)
in (
1
). Then, we consider limit theory for the test statistic of
Wang and Phillips 2016
under the assumption that the regressor process involves semi-long memory input shocks. We show that the limit theory of this test statistic under semi-long memory involves the local time of standard Brownian motion, so that the limit distribution in this case does not involve the fractional differencing parameter
d
. These findings extend the theory developed in
Wang and Phillips 2009a
;
Wang and Phillips 2009b
;
Wang and Phillips 2016
. In principal, we investigate the properties of regression function estimators through simulation studies.

The remainder of the article is organized as follows. The regressor process
x_{k}
is described in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we provide some initial results, which are suitable for developing the limit theory presented in the following sections. In Section
4
, we introduce nonlinear cointegrating regression models under the assumption that the regressor process involves strongly tempered shocks.
We establish the limit distribution of the estimator of the regression function.
In Section
5
, we present a test for parametric forms of the regression function, show that its asymptotic distribution is free of the fractional differencing parameter, and consider its power under local alternatives. Section
6
contains the results of simulation studies for estimation of the regression function.
Section
7
discusses several issues related to the use of long memory and semi-long memory processes, and Section
8
contains concluding remarks. The proofs of all technical results are contained in the Appendix.

C,C_{1},C_{2},...
as generic constants which may differ at each appearance. We use
\rightarrow_{P}
for convergence in probability,
\Rightarrow
for weak convergence of the associated probability measures,
\rightarrow_{D}
for convergence in distribution, and

f

g

f\asymp g
means
C_{1}\leq f/g\leq C_{2}
.
We use
||x||=\max_{i}|x_{i}|
for vector

,

The regressor process

The models we consider throughout this article are of the general form (
1
) in which the regressor process
x_{k}
is the sum of input shocks that result in either a long memory process or a semi-long memory process. In the long memory process, we take for
k=1,\ldots,N

\displaystyle x_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{k}X_{d}(s),

\displaystyle X_{d}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b_{d}(j)\zeta(s-j),

(2)

where
\zeta(s)
is an i.i.d. noise with
\mathbb{E}(\zeta(0))=0
and
\mathbb{E}(\zeta^{2}(0))=1
. The coefficient
b_{d}(j)
regularly varies at infinity as
j^{d-1}
. In this article, we use

b_{d}(j)\ \sim\ \frac{c_{d}}{\Gamma(d)}\,j^{d-1},\quad j\to\infty,\quad c_{d}\neq 0,\quad d\neq 0,

where
\Gamma(d)
is the gamma function defined as
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}x^{d-1}dx
, and
0<d<1/2
is the fractional differencing parameter.

In contrast to the long memory process, a semi-long memory process contains strongly tempered shocks as

\displaystyle x_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{k}X_{d,\lambda}(s),

\displaystyle X_{d,\lambda}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda j}b_{d}(j)\zeta(s-j),

(3)

where
\zeta(s)
and
b_{d}(j)
are the same as for the long memory case. In (
2
),

\lambda\equiv\lambda_{N}>0
is a sample size dependent parameter and satisfies the following main assumption:

•

Semi-Long Memory Assumption
: The tempering parameter
\lambda\to 0
and
N\lambda\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
.

The tempering parameter
\lambda
in expression (
2
) allows the range of fractional differencing parameter
d
to be extended from
(0,1/2)
to
(0,\infty)
.

Initial results

The local time process of a stochastic process
G(x)
is defined as

L_{G}(t,s)=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}I\{|G(r)-s|\leq\epsilon\}dr,

where
(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
. Let
d_{N}:=[\mathbb{E}(x_{N}^{2})]^{1/2}
and consider
x_{k,N}:=x_{k}/d_{N}
for
1\leq k\leq N
to be a triangular array. A function of
x_{k,N}
that will be used in the sequel takes the form of a sample average of functions of
x_{k,N}
as

S_{N}:=\frac{c_{N}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}g(c_{N}x_{k,N}),

where
g(x)
is a bounded function such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|g(x)|dx<\infty
and
c_{N}:=d_{N}/h
where
c_{N}\rightarrow\infty
and
c_{N}/N\rightarrow 0
.
The bandwidth parameter is denoted by
h
and satisfies
h\equiv h_{N}\rightarrow 0
as
N\rightarrow\infty
.
Functions in the form of
S_{N}
commonly arise in nonlinear cointegrating regressions, which could be the kernel function
K(.)
or its squared
K^{2}(.)
; see
Karlsen and Tjøstheim 2001
,
Karlsen et al. 2007
, and
Wang and Phillips 2009a
.

The limit behavior of
S_{N}
will be important in order to establish the limit behavior of kernel estimators of
f(x)
in the nonparametric regression context. The limit distribution of
S_{N}
is as follows

S_{N}\rightarrow_{D}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)dxL_{B}(1,0),

(4)

where
L_{B}(t,s)
is the local time of standard Brownian motion
B(t)
at the spatial point
s
; see Proposition
A.9
part (i) to follow. When the function
g
is a kernel density, the integral in expression (
4
) is unity, and the limit is then the local time of
B
at the origin; see
Jeganathan 2004
and
Wang and Phillips 2009a
for some of the related results.
Under long memory case, the local time given in expression (
4
) is in the form of
L_{B_{d+1/2}}(t,s)
, and therefore depends on the unknown parameter
d
. This complicates the limit theory of the kernel estimator of regression function as well as the associated test statistic.

Nonparametric regression function estimation

Assume model (
1
) where
f(\cdot)
is an unknown real regression function. To induce endogeneity, let
\eta_{k}=(\zeta(k),\epsilon(k))^{\prime}
be a sequence of random vectors with
\mathbb{E}(\eta_{0})=0
and
\mathbb{E}(\eta_{0}\eta_{0}^{\prime})=\Sigma
where

\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathbb{E}(\zeta(0)\epsilon(0))\\
\mathbb{E}(\epsilon(0)\zeta(0))&\mathbb{E}(\epsilon(0)^{2})\end{pmatrix},

(5)

and take
u_{k}
in (
1
) to be
u_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_{j}\eta_{k-j}
for the coefficient vector
\psi_{j}=(\psi_{j1},\psi_{j2})
. Therefore,
\mathbb{E}(u_{0}^{2})=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_{j}\Sigma\psi_{j}^{\prime}
and
cov(u_{k},x_{k})\neq 0
by recalling expression (
2
).
Now, assume
\eta_{0}
and
\psi_{j}
satisfy the following assumption.

Assumption 4.1
.

Let
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_{j}\neq 0
and
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}j^{1/4}(|\psi_{j1}|+|\psi_{j2}|)<\infty
. Also, let
\mathbb{E}||\eta_{0}||^{\alpha}<\infty
for
\alpha>2
.

Additionally, assume the characteristic function
\varphi(t)
of
\zeta(0)
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|t|)|\varphi(t)|dt<\infty,

which ensures the smoothness in the corresponding kernel density (see,
Wang and Phillips 2016
).

The Nadaraya-Watson regression estimator of
f(x)
is

\hat{f}(x)=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{N}y_{k}K_{h}(x_{k}-x)}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}K_{h}(x_{k}-x)},

(6)

where
K_{h}(s)=h^{-1}K(s/h)
, and
K(\cdot)
is a non-negative bounded continuous function.
In order to establish the asymptotic behavior for the kernel estimate
f(x)
,
\{x_{k,N}\}_{k\geq 1,N\geq 1}
needs to be a strong smooth array (see Definition
A.1
as well as Proposition
A.1
).
Now, we impose the following two assumptions on the kernel
K(.)
:

Assumption 4.2
.

K(x)
is a non-negative bounded continuous function satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(x)dx=1
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\hat{K}(x)|dx<\infty
, where
\hat{K}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ixt}K(t)dt
.

Assumption 4.3
.

For a given
x
, there exists a real positive function
f_{1}(s,x)
and
\gamma\in(0,1]
such that when
h
is sufficiently small,

|f(hy+x)-f(x)|\leq h^{\gamma}f_{1}(y,x)\,\forall y\in\mathbb{R}

and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(s)[f_{1}(s,x)+f_{1}^{2}(s,x)]ds<\infty
hold.

The following theorem is the main result on the Nadaraya-Watson kernel estimator of (
6
) and recall that
\lambda
is a function of
N
such that
\lambda\rightarrow 0
and
N\lambda\rightarrow\infty
as
N\rightarrow\infty
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Under Assumptions
4.1
–
4.3
and for any
h
satisfying
\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\rightarrow\infty
and
\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h^{1+2\gamma}\rightarrow 0
where
\gamma\in(0,1]
, we have

\Big\{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\Big\}^{1/2}\Big(\hat{f}(x)-f(x)\Big)\rightarrow_{D}\frac{d_{0}}{\sqrt{d_{1}}}N(0,1)\,L_{B}^{-1/2}(1,0),

(7)

where
d_{0}^{2}=\mathbb{E}(u^{2}_{0})\int_{\mathbb{R}}K^{2}(s)ds
,
d_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(s)ds
, and
N(0,1)
denotes a standard normal which is independent from the local time of Brownian motion
L_{B}(1,0)
.
If we normalize the limit form (
7
), we have:

\Big\{h\sum_{k=1}^{N}K_{h}(x_{k}-x)\Big\}^{1/2}\Big(\hat{f}(x)-f(x)\Big)\rightarrow_{D}N(0,\sigma^{2}),

(8)

where
\sigma^{2}=d_{0}^{2}/d_{1}

For a fixed value of

has a continuous

derivative in a small neighborhood of

\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\rightarrow\infty

Remark 4.1
.

The local time given in Theorem

is the local time of standard Brownian motion, which is independent of the unknown parameter

is different from Theorem 2.1 in

Remark 4.2
.

Based on Theorem

has to satisfy certain rate conditions to ensure that the asymptotic distribution on the right side of expression (

\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\rightarrow\infty

\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h^{1+2\gamma}\rightarrow 0

The term

in

can be estimated by

\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{N}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{N}[y_{k}-\hat{f}(x_{k})]^{2}K_{h}(x_{k}-x)}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}K_{h}(x_{k}-x)}.

(9)

Expression (

) is appropriate for use in the interval estimation and other inferential procedures.
When
\mathbb{E}(u_{0}^{8})<\infty
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(s)f_{1}^{2}(s,x)ds<\infty

satisfying

\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{N}\rightarrow_{P}\mathbb{E}(u_{0}^{2})
. The consistency of

is stated in Theorem 3.2 in

Specification test for the regression function

When there is no reason to believe that
f(x)
in (
1
) follows a particular parametric form, the use of a nonparametric estimator is attractive, but nonparametric estimators generally have a slow convergence rate in comparison with the parametric estimators. It is often possible to determine a plausible parametric regression function, and then conduct a test of the posited parametric specification, formulated as a hypothesis as

H_{0}:f(x)=g(x,\theta_{0}),

(10)

where
\theta_{0}
in (
10
) is a vector of unknown parameters that belongs to a compact and convex space
\Theta
.
Tests of (
10
) have been previously considered by
Hardle and Mammen 1993
,
Horowitz and Spokoiny 2001
,
Gao et al. 2009
,
Wang and Phillips 2012
, and
Wang and Phillips 2016
under different assumptions on the data generating mechanism.
Indeed,
Gao et al. 2009
and
Wang and Phillips 2012
considered a kernel-smoothed U statistic of the form
\sum_{j,k=1,j\neq k}^{N}\hat{u}_{k}\hat{u}_{j}K[(x_{k}-x_{j})/h]
with
\hat{u}_{k}=y_{k}-g(x_{k},\hat{\theta}_{N})
.

The asymptotic for the U statistic is hard to be extended to the case of endogenous regressors. Thus,
Wang and Phillips 2016
modified a test statistic previously suggested by
Hardle and Mammen 1993
for the consideration of endogenous regressors with long memory. In that case, both the limit theory and convergence rate of the test statistic depend on the fractional differencing parameter
d
through the local time of fractional Brownian motion
L_{B_{d+1/2}}(t,s)
, which complicates its use in actual problems. In this section, we consider the statistic under an assumption of semi-long memory input shocks to the regressors
x_{k}
. Both the limit distribution of
Wang and Phillips 2016
under long memory, and our limit distribution for use with semi-long memory regressors are based on the following statistic,

T_{N}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\{\sum_{k=1}^{N}K\Big[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}\Big][y_{k}-g(x_{k},\hat{\theta}_{N})]\Big\}^{2}\pi(x)dx.

(11)

For estimating
\hat{\theta}_{N}
, we use a non-linear least squares method by setting
Q_{N}(\theta)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}(y_{k}-g(x_{k},\theta))^{2}
and minimizing it over
\theta\in\Theta
as follows

\hat{\theta}_{N}=\text{argmin}_{\theta\in\Theta}Q_{N}(\theta).

Additionally,
\pi(x)
in (
11
) is a positive integrable weight function with a compact support. To develop the asymptotic theory of
T_{N}
in the context of semi-long memory regressors and endogeneity, we provide three assumptions as follows.

Assumption 5.1
.

K(x)\pi(x)
has a compact support such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(x)dx=1
and
|K(x)-K(y)|\leq C|x-y|
whenever
|x-y|
is sufficiently small.

Assumption 5.2
.

There exist
g_{1}(x)
and
g_{2}(x)
such that for each
\theta,\theta_{0}\in\Theta
,
|g(x,\theta)-g(x,\theta_{0})|\leq C||\theta-\theta_{0}||g_{1}(x)
holds, and for some
0<\beta\leq 1
,
|g_{1}(x+y)-g_{1}(x)|\leq C|y|^{\beta}g_{2}(x)
holds, whenever
y
is sufficiently small. Additionally,
\int_{\mathbb{R}}[1+g_{1}^{2}(x)+g^{2}_{2}(x)]\pi(x)dx<\infty
holds.

Assumption 5.3
.

Under
H_{0}
,
||\hat{\theta}_{N}-\theta_{0}||=o_{P}\Big(\{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\}^{-1/2}\Big)
.

As noted in
Wang and Phillips 2016
, Assumption
5.2
covers a wide range of functions
g(x,\theta)
and
\pi(x)
. Typical examples of
g(x,\theta)
include
\theta e^{x}/(1+e^{x})
,
e^{-\theta|x|}
,
e^{-\theta x^{2}}
,
e^{\theta|x|}/(1+e^{\theta|x|})
,
\theta\log|x|
,
(x+\theta)^{2}
, etc.
Note that Assumptions
5.1
–
5.3
may be compared with those imposed by
Wang and Phillips 2016
.
We now consider the asymptotic behavior of
T_{N}
in (
11
) and its convergence rate in the semi-long memory setting.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let Assumptions
4.1
, and
5.1
–
5.3
hold. Then, under
H_{0}
, we have

T_{\lambda,d}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h}T_{N}\rightarrow_{D}d_{(0)}^{2}L_{B}(1,0),

(12)

where
d_{(0)}^{2}=\mathbb{E}(u^{2}_{0})\int_{\mathbb{R}}K^{2}(s)ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\pi(x)dx
,
N^{1/2-\delta_{0}}\lambda^{d}h\rightarrow\infty
, and
\delta_{0}
is as small as required.

Remark 5.1
.

(i) Under semi-long memory setting for
d\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the limit distribution of (
12
) is free of the unknown fractional differencing parameter
d
; however, the convergence rate depends on the unknown parameter
d
even when
\lambda\rightarrow 0
and
N\lambda\rightarrow\infty
.
(ii) Under long memory setting for
d\in(0,1/2)
, the test statistic and limit distribution given by
Wang and Phillips 2016
are

T_{N,d}:=(d_{N}/Nh)T_{N}\rightarrow_{D}d^{2}_{(0)}L_{B_{d+1/2}}(1,0)
,
where
d_{N}\sim N^{d+1/2}
as
N\rightarrow\infty
, and
T_{N}
is as given in (
11
). The limit distribution of this version of the test statistic relies on
d
through the fractional Brownian motion process
B_{d+1/2}
.
(iii) Under short memory setting for
d=0
and
d_{N}\sim N^{1/2}
, the test statistic and limit distribution are

T_{N,0}=(1/\sqrt{N}h)T_{N}\rightarrow_{D}d^{2}_{(0)}L_{B}(1,0)
.
(iv) Note that the limit distribution of test statistic (
12
) formulated under the semi-long memory case is similar to the short memory case. In neither case does the limit distribution depend on
d
.

To verify that the test has nontrivial power, one can assess the null hypothesis (
10
) against a local alternative

H_{A}:f(x)=g(x,\theta)+\rho_{N}m(x),\quad\text{for}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

(13)

Here,
\rho_{N}
is a sequence of numerical constants which measures the local deviation from the null hypothesis. Also in (

is a real function free of
\theta

and its derivative functions.
To make
m(x)
smooth enough under
H_{A}
for the sake of asymptotic power development, we give the following two assumptions.

Assumption 5.4
.

(i) There exist
m_{1}(x)
and
\gamma\in(0,1]
such that for any
y
sufficiently small,
|m(x+y)-m(x)|\leq C|y|^{\gamma}m_{1}(x)
holds. (ii) Let
\int_{\mathbb{R}}[1+m^{2}(x)+m^{2}_{1}(x)]\pi(x)dx<\infty
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}m^{2}(x)\pi(x)dx>0
hold. (iii) The function
m(x)
is not an element of the space spanned by
g(x,\theta_{0})
and its derivative functions.

Assumption 5.5
.

Under
H_{A}
,
||\hat{\theta}_{N}-\theta_{0}||=o_{P}\Big(\{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\}^{-1/2}\Big)
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let Assumptions
4.1
,
5.1
–
5.2
, and
5.4
–
5.5
hold. Then, under
H_{A}
, we have

\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}P\Big(T_{\lambda,d}\geq T_{0}\Big)=1,

where
T_{0}
is positive, and
h\rightarrow 0
satisfies
N^{1/2-\delta_{0}}\lambda^{d}h\rightarrow\infty
for a small enough
\delta_{0}
and any
\rho_{N}
satisfying
N^{1/2}\lambda^{d}h\rho_{N}^{2}\rightarrow\infty
.

Remark 5.2
.

Based on Theorem
5.2
, the test statistic
T_{\lambda,d}
has nontrivial power against the local alternatives in the form of (
13

\rho_{N}\rightarrow 0
at a rate that is slower than
\{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\}^{-1/2}
as
\{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\}^{-1}\rightarrow 0

in (

ensures the divergence of normalized test statistic

and test consistency under

; see Remarks 3.2 and 3.3 in

Discussion on the application of LM and SLM processes

The strongly tempered process
(
2
)
belongs to the general class of stochastic processes called tempered linear processes, see
Sabzikar and Surgailis 2018
. These processes have a semi-long memory property in the sense that their autocovariance functions initially resemble that of a long memory process but eventually decay fast at an exponential rate. One special case of such processes is the autoregressive tempered fractionally integrated moving average ARTFIMA
(p,d,\lambda,q)
process with
p
as the order of autoregressive polynomial and
q
as the order of moving average polynomial, which has been studied by
Meerschaert et al. 2014
and
Sabzikar et al. 2019
.

The class of ARTFIMA
(0,d,\lambda,0)
which does not have autoregressive and moving average components is highly applicable. Some empirical examples include modeling of the log returns for AMZN stock prices, modeling of geophysical turbulence in water velocity data, and modeling of climate data sets given in
Sabzikar et al. 2019
. In all cases, the authors have found that by using an ARTFIMA model, we are able to capture aspects of the low frequency activity of time series better than an ARFIMA model without the consideration of
\lambda
in the model.

In practice when analyzing a single data set with size
N
, the tempering parameter
\lambda
is fixed, and one can use Whittle estimation or maximum likelihood estimation to estimate the unknown parameters
d
and
\lambda
. These estimators are strongly consistent under general conditions (see
Sabzikar et al. 2019
). On the other hand, if one wishes to estimate
\lambda
as a function of
N
from a sequence of increasing time series, the problem becomes challenging.
This problem could be pursued by developing confidence intervals such that we impose an assumption of
\zeta(s)\sim N(0,1)
in expression (
2
) or assume
X_{d,\lambda}(s)=e^{-\lambda}X_{d,\lambda}(s-1)+\xi(s)
after some algebra, where
\xi(s)
is a function of
\zeta(s)
’s. This situation is complex and the simplest case is AR(1) setting. Some approaches in this regard are given by
Mikusheva 2007
and
Phillips 2014
. These extensions require complex further calculations which are beyond the scope of the current manuscript (see Remark 3.7 in
Sabzikar et al. 2020
for a related argument as well as Section 5 in
Sabzikar and Surgailis 2018
). For the simulation studies in this manuscript, we fixed
\lambda
at multiple points.

The kernel estimator of the unknown regression function
f(x)
given in (
6
) depends on the bandwidth
h
.
In order to select the bandwidth, note that in the nonstationary case, the amount of time spent by the process around any particular spatial point is of order
\sqrt{N}
rather than
N
(see also
Wang and Phillips 2009a
). For the practical interest and the nonstationary regressor
x_{k}
with semi-long memory, the corresponding rate in such regressions is
\{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\}^{1/2}
where we require that
\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\rightarrow\infty
. Note that the choice of bandwidth
h
is related to the nature of the nonstationary regressor
x_{k}
. In our simulations, we found that the rate
h=o(N^{-1/6})
is sufficient for undersmoothing to remove the asymptotic bias of
\hat{f}(x)
.

Finally, the test statistic
T_{\lambda,d}
which is discussed in this manuscript, has an asymptotic reference distribution given in Theorem
5.1
, which is of complex form and is not easy to be used in practice.
The limit distribution of the statistic involves the term
d^{2}_{(0)}
, which does not have a simple form and depends on
x
through
\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{N}
.
Under appropriate conditions, one can approximate the sampling distribution of the test statistic through the use of the subsampling technique given by
Politis and Romano 1994
, which allows us to be able to study the properties of test statistic under both long memory and semi-long memory input shocks to the regressors.
In order to establish the validity of subsampling, one can use the between-block mixing coefficient, which is weaker than the regular
\alpha
-mixing coefficient and is sufficient for strong dependency; see for instance
Bai and Taqqu 2017
.
The extension of subsampling is beyond the scope of this paper, and we leave it for future work.

Conclusion

In this article we have considered the use of tempered or semi-long memory regressor processes within the context of cointegrating regression models for time series analysis. We have developed limit theory that allows the construction of pointwise confidence intervals for a nonlinear regression function when estimation is accomplished through the use of a kernel smoother. We have also demonstrated that the limit distribution of a test statistic for specification of the regression function does not depend on the differencing parameter
d
under semi-long memory. Through simulation studies we have shown that performance of interval estimators of a regression function is more stable and has generally better performance for the semi-long memory case than for long-memory. It is thus beneficial to use methods based on this theory if it can provide a reasonable framework within which to consider a particular problem.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
x_{k,N}=x_{k}/d_{N}
. A random array
\{x_{k,N}\}_{k\geq 1,N\geq 1}
is called strong smooth.

Proof.

We verify this proposition from the Definition
A.1
. Assume
\{x_{k,N}\}_{k\geq 1,N\geq 1}
is a random triangular array such that
x_{0,N}\equiv 0
. Let
d_{l,k}\equiv\lambda_{l-k}^{-d}\,(l-k)^{1/2}
, therefore

\displaystyle d_{l,k,N}\equiv\frac{d_{l,k}}{d_{N}}\propto\Big(\frac{\lambda_{l-k}}{\lambda_{N}}\Big)^{-d}\,\sqrt{\frac{l-k}{N}},\qquad N\lambda_{N}\rightarrow\infty.

Recall that
\lambda_{N}\equiv\lambda>0
is a sample size dependent parameter.

(a)

\inf_{(l,k)\in\Omega_{N}(\eta)}d_{l,k,N}\equiv\inf_{(l,k)\in\Omega_{N}(\eta)}(\lambda_{l-k}/\lambda_{N})^{-d}\sqrt{(l-k)/N}\geq\sqrt{\frac{\eta N}{N}}\lambda_{\eta N/N}^{d}>0
.

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l=(1-\eta)N}^{N}\Big\{\Big(\frac{\lambda_{l}}{\lambda_{N}}\Big)^{-d}\sqrt{l/N}\Big\}^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\lambda_{N}^{d}}\int_{(1-\eta)N}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{l}^{d}}{\sqrt{l}}dl

\displaystyle\quad\leq\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\lambda_{N}^{d}}\Big\{\sqrt{N}\lambda_{N}^{d}-\sqrt{(1-\eta)N}\lambda_{(1-\eta)N}^{d}\Big\}=0.

(ii)

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\max_{0\leq k\leq(1-\eta)N}\sum_{l=k+1}^{k+\eta N}\Big\{\Big(\frac{\lambda_{l-k}}{\lambda_{N}}\Big)^{-d}\sqrt{(l-k)/N}\Big\}^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\lambda_{N}^{d}}\max\limits_{0\leq k\leq(1-\eta)N}\int_{k+1}^{k+\eta N}\frac{\lambda_{l-k}^{d}}{\sqrt{l-k}}dl

\displaystyle=\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\lambda_{N}^{d}}\Big\{\sqrt{\eta N}\lambda_{\eta N}^{d}-1\Big\}=0.

(iii)

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\max\limits_{0\leq k\leq N-1}\sum\limits_{l=k+1}^{N}\Big\{\Big(\frac{\lambda_{l-k}}{\lambda_{N}}\Big)^{-d}\sqrt{(l-k)/N}\Big\}^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\lambda_{N}^{d}}\max\limits_{0\leq k\leq N-1}\int_{k+1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{l-k}^{d}}{\sqrt{l-k}}dl

\displaystyle\quad\leq\limsup\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\lambda_{N}^{d}}\max\limits_{0\leq k\leq N-1}\Big\{\sqrt{N-k}\lambda_{N-k}^{d}-1\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad=\limsup\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\Big\{1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\lambda_{N}^{d}}\Big\}=1<\infty.

(b)

If we define
f_{l,k,N}(t)=\mathbb{E}\Big(e^{it[x_{l,N}-x_{k,N}]/d_{l,k,N}}\Big)
, then

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|h_{l,k,N}(x)-n(x)\Big|\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big|f_{l,k,N}(t)-e^{-t^{2}/2}\Big|dt\rightarrow 0,

where
n(x)=e^{-x^{2}/2}\big/\sqrt{2\pi}
is the density of standard normal (see proof of Corollary 2.2 in
Wang and Phillips 2009a
for a similar argument). Thus, all the conditions given in Definition
A.1
hold, and
{x_{k,N}}
is a strong smooth array.
The alternative way to prove this proposition is to use expression (
A.1
), where
\{\zeta(k)\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with
\mathbb{E}(\zeta(0))=0
and
\mathbb{E}(\zeta^{2}(0))=1
; see expression (A.7) in
Sabzikar et al. 2020
. Secondly, the triangular array
N^{-1/2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\zeta(k)
satisfies the strong smooth conditions; see
Wang and Phillips 2009a
. This together with (
A.1
) can complete the proof of Proposition
A.1
.
∎

as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\psi^{\prime\prime}_{N}(t)]^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}h}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor Nt\rfloor}K^{2}\Big[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}\Big]\mathbb{E}(u_{k}^{2})\Big\}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{Nh^{2}}\Big\{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Big\{K^{4}\Big[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}\Big]\Big\}+2\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq N}\mathbb{E}\Big\{K^{2}\Big[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}\Big]K^{2}\Big[\frac{x_{l}-x}{h}\Big]\Big\}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{Nh^{2}}\Big\{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sup_{y}\mathbb{E}r^{2}_{y}\Big(\frac{x^{\prime}_{0,k}}{h}\Big)+2\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq N}\sup_{y}\mathbb{E}r_{y}\Big(\frac{x^{\prime}_{0,k}}{h}\Big)\sup_{y}\mathbb{E}r_{y}\Big(\frac{x^{\prime}_{k,j}}{h}\Big)\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{Nh^{2}}\Big\{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{h}{\sqrt{k}}+2\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq N}\frac{h^{2}}{\sqrt{k}\sqrt{l-k}}\Big\}<\infty,

uniformly on
N
. The last line could be followed by a similar path from the proof of Lemma 7.1 part (a) in
Wang and Phillips 2009b
. Note that
\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\mathbb{E}|\psi_{N}(t)-\psi_{N}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}=o(1)
, which can be verified by a similar argument given in Proposition
A.6
. This implies
\sup_{N}\mathbb{E}[\psi_{N}(t)]^{2}<\infty
, and therefore
\psi_{N}(t)
is uniformly integrable. Furthermore, we can write

\displaystyle|\psi_{N}(t)-S^{2}_{N}(t)|=\frac{2}{\sqrt{N}h}\sum\limits_{1\leq k<l\leq\lfloor Nt\rfloor}u_{k}u_{l}K\Big[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}\Big]K\Big[\frac{x_{l}-x}{h}\Big],

which implies
\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\mathbb{E}|\psi_{N}(t)-S^{2}_{N}(t)|=o(1)
and can be verified by a similar argument given in Proposition
A.6
. Therefore,
S^{2}_{N}(t)
is uniformly integrable, which implies
S_{N}(t)
is uniformly integrable.
∎

Proposition A.3
.

Let
\Theta_{1N}:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}K[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}]
. Then,
\Theta_{1N}=O_{P}(\{\sqrt{N}h\}^{1/2})
.

Proof.

Based on the Proposition
A.2
, uniformly in
N
, we have
\mathbb{E}S^{2}_{N}(1)\leq C
, where
C
is a generic constant. Therefore, based on the Markov’s inequality

\displaystyle P(\Theta_{1N}\geq\{\sqrt{N}h\}^{1/2})\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}(\Theta^{2}_{1N})}{\sqrt{N}h}=\mathbb{E}S^{2}_{N}(1)\leq C.

As a result,
\Theta_{1N}=O_{P}(\{\sqrt{N}h\}^{1/2})
.
∎

Proposition A.4
.

Let
\Theta_{2N}:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}[f(x_{k})-f(x)]K[(x_{k}-x)/h]
. Then,

\Theta_{2N}=O_{P}(Nh^{\gamma+1}/d_{N})
where
d_{N}=\sqrt{N}/\lambda^{d}
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\Theta_{2N}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Big\{|f(x_{k,N}d_{N})-f(x)|K\Big[\frac{x_{k,N}d_{N}-x}{h}\Big]\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\{|f(d_{N}d_{k,0,N}y)-f(x)|\times K\Big(\frac{d_{N}d_{k,0,N}y}{h}-\frac{x}{h}\Big)\Big\}h_{k,0,N}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{h}{d_{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{d_{k,0,N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\{|f(hy+x)-f(x)|K(y)\}dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Nh^{\gamma+1}}{d_{N}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{d_{k,0,N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(s)f_{1}(s,x)ds\leq A\frac{Nh^{\gamma+1}}{d_{N}},

where
A
is a constant and
\gamma\in(0,1]
. The last line of proof follows from Assumption
4.3
and note that we have considered
x_{k,N}=d_{k,0,N}y
and
d_{N}d_{k,0,N}y=hy+x
. Therefore,
\Theta_{2N}=O_{P}(Nh^{\gamma+1}/d_{N})
.
∎

Proposition A.6
.

Assume
h\rightarrow 0
and
\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\rightarrow\infty
. Let

\displaystyle T_{iN}(t):=\{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\}^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor Nt\rfloor}K^{i}\Big[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}\Big],\quad\text{and}\quad T^{\prime}_{iN}(t):=\{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\}^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor Nt\rfloor}K^{2}\Big[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}\Big]u^{2}_{k}.

Then,
T_{iN}(t)\rightarrow_{D}d_{i}L_{B}(t,0)
and
T^{\prime}_{iN}(t)\rightarrow_{D}d_{0}^{2}L_{B}(t,0)
for
i=1,2
,
where
d_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}K^{i}(s)ds
and
d_{0}^{2}=\mathbb{E}(u^{2}_{0})\int_{\mathbb{R}}K^{2}(s)ds
.

Proof.

Let’s define
x_{k,N}:=\hat{\xi}_{N}(k/N)
for
1\leq k\leq N
such that

\hat{\xi}_{N}(t)\Rightarrow B(t)
on
D[0,1]
following Theorem 4.3 in
Sabzikar and Surgailis 2018
. There exists an equivalent process
\hat{\xi}_{N}(k/N)
of
\xi_{N}(k/N)
i.e.
\hat{\xi}_{N}(k/N)=_{D}\xi_{N}(k/N)

such that
\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|\hat{\xi}_{N}(t)-B(t)|=o_{P}(1)
.
Now, apply part (i) of Proposition
A.9
by setting
c_{N}=\frac{d_{N}}{h}
and
g(t)=K^{i}[t-\frac{x}{h}]
for
i=1,2
; then we have

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq 1}\Big|\{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\}^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor Nt\rfloor}K^{i}\Big[\frac{d_{N}\hat{\xi}_{N}(k/N)-x}{h}\Big]-L_{B}(t,0)\int_{\mathbb{R}}K^{i}(s)ds\Big|\rightarrow_{P}0,

(A.13)

as
c_{N}\rightarrow\infty
,
\frac{c_{N}}{N}\rightarrow 0
, and
N\rightarrow\infty
.
This together with the fact that
\hat{\xi}_{N}(\frac{k}{N})=_{D}\xi_{N}(\frac{k}{N})=\frac{x_{k}}{d_{N}}
for
1\leq k\leq N
imply for
N\geq 1
,
T_{iN}(t)
is f.d.d. for
d_{i}L_{B}(t,0)
.
Additionally,
T_{iN}(t)
is tight for
N\geq 1
following Proposition
A.7
, i.e.

\displaystyle\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N}\Big|K^{i}\Big[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}\Big]\Big|=o_{P}\Big(\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\Big),

under the assumption of
\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|x_{\lfloor Nt\rfloor,N}-B(t)|=o_{P}(1)
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle T_{iN}(t)\rightarrow_{D}d_{i}\,L_{B}(t,0)\quad\text{for}\quad i=1,2.

Now, in order to prove

T^{\prime}_{iN}(t):=\{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\}^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor Nt\rfloor}K^{2}[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}]u^{2}_{k}\rightarrow_{D}d_{0}^{2}\,L_{B}(t,0)
, we define

\displaystyle T_{iN}^{\prime\prime}(t):=\{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\}^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor Nt\rfloor}K^{2}\Big[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}\Big]\mathbb{E}u^{2}_{k},

and need to show
\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\mathbb{E}|T^{\prime}_{iN}(t)-T^{\prime\prime}_{iN}(t)|^{2}=o(1)
.
Based on the Preliminary given in Section A.1,
x_{k}=x^{*}_{0,k}+x^{\prime}_{0,k}
for
s=0
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|T^{\prime}_{iN}(t)-T^{\prime\prime}_{iN}(t)\Big|^{2}\Big|\zeta(0),\zeta(-1),...\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\Big\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h}\Big\}^{2}\sup\limits_{y,1\leq M\leq N}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{k=1}^{M}K^{2}\Big[\frac{y+x^{\prime}_{0,k}}{h}\Big](u^{2}_{k}-\mathbb{E}(u^{2}_{k}))^{2}\Big]^{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\Big\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h}\Big\}^{2}\sup\limits_{y}\Big[\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}r^{2}\Big(\frac{x^{\prime}_{0,k}}{h}\Big)g^{2}(u_{k})+2\sum\limits_{1\leq k<l\leq N}\Big|\mathbb{E}r\Big(\frac{x^{\prime}_{0,k}}{h}\Big)r\Big(\frac{x^{\prime}_{0,l}}{h}\Big)g(u_{k})g_{1}(u_{l})\Big|\Big]

as
g(t)=t^{2}-\mathbb{E}u^{2}_{k}
,
g_{1}(t)=t^{2}-\mathbb{E}u^{2}_{l}
, and
r(t)=K^{2}(\frac{y}{h}+t)
. Now based on the last part of proof of Proposition 7.2 in
Wang and Phillips 2009b
and as
\{\sqrt{N}\lambda^{d}h\}^{-1}\rightarrow 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|T^{\prime}_{iN}(t)-T^{\prime\prime}_{iN}(t)\Big|^{2}|\zeta(0),\zeta(-1),\cdots\Big]\rightarrow 0\qquad\text{a.s.}

Therefore,
T^{\prime}_{iN}(t)
and
T^{\prime\prime}_{iN}(t)
have the same f.d.d. Since
\mathbb{E}u^{2}_{k}=\mathbb{E}u^{2}_{m_{0}}
for
k\geq m_{0}
and
T^{\prime\prime}_{iN}(t)\rightarrow_{D}d_{0}^{2}L_{B}(t,0)
from the modification of expression (
A.12
), we can easily show that
T^{\prime}_{iN}(t)
is tight from Proposition
A.7
.
Therefore,
T^{\prime}_{iN}(t)\rightarrow_{D}d_{0}^{2}L_{B}(t,0)
on

∎

Proposition A.7
.

S_{N}(t)=\{\sqrt{N}h\}^{-1/2}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor Nt\rfloor}u_{k}K[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}]
is tight on
D[0,1]
.

Proof.

According to Theorem 4 of
Billingsley 1974
, we need to show

\displaystyle\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N}\Big|u_{k}K\Big[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}\Big]\Big|=o_{P}\Big(\{\sqrt{N}h\}^{1/2}\Big).

(A.14)

The proof follows from Proposition 7.4 in
Wang and Phillips 2009b
, and therefore we give an outline. There exists a sequence of
\alpha_{N}(\nu,\kappa)
such that
\lim_{\kappa\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{N}(\nu,\kappa)=0
for each
\nu>0
and
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\cdots\leq t_{m}\leq t\leq 1
where
t-t_{m}\leq\kappa
. We have

\displaystyle P\Big[\Big|S_{N}(t)-S_{N}(t_{m})\Big|\geq\nu\Big|S_{N}(t_{1})...S_{N}(t_{m})\Big]\leq\alpha_{N}(\nu,\kappa)\quad\text{a.s.}

Therefore, we need to show

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{|t-t_{m}|\leq\kappa}P\Big(\Big|\sum_{k=\lfloor Nt_{m}\rfloor+1}^{\lfloor Nt\rfloor}u_{k}K\Big[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}\Big]\Big|\geq\nu\,N^{1/2}\lambda^{-d}\Big|\zeta(\lfloor Nt_{m}\rfloor),\zeta({\lfloor Nt_{m}\rfloor}-1),\cdots,\zeta(1)\Big)\leq\alpha_{N}(\nu,\kappa).

We may choose
\alpha_{N}(\nu,\kappa)
such that

\displaystyle\alpha_{N}(\nu,\kappa)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{Nh^{2}}}\sup\limits_{y,0\leq t\leq\kappa}\mathbb{E}\Big\{\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor Nt\rfloor}u_{k}K\Big[\frac{y+x^{\prime}_{0,k}}{h}\Big]\Big\}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{Nh^{2}}}\sup\limits_{y}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor N\kappa\rfloor}\mathbb{E}\Big\{u^{2}_{k}K^{2}\Big[\frac{y+x^{\prime}_{0,k}}{h}\Big]\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{Nh^{2}}}\sup\limits_{y}2\sum\limits_{1\leq k<l\leq\lfloor N\kappa\rfloor}\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big\{u_{k}u_{l}K\Big[\frac{y+x^{\prime}_{0,k}}{h}\Big]K\Big[\frac{y+x^{\prime}_{0,l}}{h}\Big]\Big\}\Big|.

Following expression (
A.3
) in Lemma
A.1
, we can show

\displaystyle\alpha_{N}(\nu,\kappa)\leq\frac{C}{\nu^{2}\sqrt{Nh^{2}}}\times\frac{\lfloor N\kappa\rfloor h\lambda^{d}}{(\lfloor N\kappa\rfloor)^{1/2}}\Big[1+h\sqrt{j}\Big]=\frac{C}{\nu^{2}\sqrt{N}}\times(\lfloor N\kappa\rfloor)^{1/2}\lambda^{d}\Big[1+h\sqrt{j}\Big],

which yields
\lim_{\kappa\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{N}(\nu,\kappa)=0
.
Note that

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq N}\Big|u_{k}K\Big[\frac{x_{k}-x}{h}\Big]\Big|\leq\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}^{4}K^{4}\Big[\frac{x_{j}-x}{h}\Big]\Big\}^{1/4}.

Following Lemma
A.1
, one can show that
\mathbb{E}u_{j}^{4}K^{4}[\frac{x_{j}-x}{h}]\leq Ch/\sqrt{j}
, which concludes (
A.14
). As a result,
S_{N}(t)
is tight.
∎

A Lagrangian method for indefinite q q -integrals
Gamela E. Heragy a , Zeinab S.I. Mansour, b and Karima M. Oraby a

Abstract
A Lagrangian method is introduced recently for deriving indefinite integrals of special functions that satisfy homogeneous (nonhomogeneous) second-order linear differential equations. This paper extends this method to include indefinite Jackson q q -integrals of special functions satisfying homogeneous (nonhomogeneous) second-order linear q q -difference equations. Many q q -integrals, both previously known and completely new, are derived using the method. We introduce samples of indefinite and definite q q -integrals for Jackson’s q q -Bessel functions, q q -hypergeometric functions, and some orthogonal polynomials.

Conway in

where
f(x)
satisfies the first-order differential equation

\displaystyle f(x)=\exp(\int p(x)dx).

Here
p(x)
and

q(x)
are arbitrary complex-valued differentiable functions of
x
in
\mathbb{R}
, with
h(x)
being at least twice differentiable. In a series of papers, see
[
6

4
,
3
,
7
,
10
,
5
,
8
,

]
, Conway developed this method to obtain more indefinite integrals. In this paper, we extend Conway’s results to include special functions satisfying second-order

\displaystyle J_{\nu}^{(2)}(z;q)=\frac{(q^{v+1};q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{n}q^{n(n+\nu)}}{(q,q^{v+1};q)_{n}}(z/2)^{2n+\nu},\quad z\in\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle J_{\nu}^{(3)}(z;q)=\frac{(q^{v+1};q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}}{(q,q^{v+1};q)_{n}}(z)^{2n+\nu},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

Let

\displaystyle=u(x/q)v(x/q)\Big|_{a}^{b}-\frac{1}{q}\int_{a}^{b}u(x/q)D_{q^{-1}}v(x)d_{q}x.

(2)

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
p(x)
and
r(x)
be continuous functions at zero. Let
y(x)
be a solution of the second-order
q
-difference equation

\frac{1}{q}D_{q^{-1}}D_{q}y(x)+p(x)D_{q^{-1}}y(x)+r(x)y(x)=0.

(4)

Then

\displaystyle\int f(x)\Big(\frac{1}{q}D_{q^{-1}}D_{q}h(x)+p(x)D_{q^{-1}}h(x)+r(x)h(x)\Big)y(x)d_{q}x

\displaystyle=f(x/q)\Big(y(x/q)D_{q^{-1}}h(x)-h(x/q)D_{q^{-1}}y(x)\Big),

(5)

where
h(x)
is an arbitrary function, and
f(x)
is a solution of the first order
q
-difference equation

\frac{1}{q}D_{q^{-1}}f(x)=p(x)f(x).

(6)

Proof.

Applying the
q
-integration by parts rule (
1.1
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\int f(x)y(x)D_{q^{-1}}D_{q}h(x)d_{q}x=(D_{q}h)(x/q)f(x/q)y(x/q)-\frac{1}{q}\int(D_{q}h)(x/q)D_{q^{-1}}(f(x)y(x))d_{q}x.

Therefore, by the
q
-product rule, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\int f(x)y(x)D_{q^{-1}}D_{q}h(x)d_{q}x

\displaystyle=D_{q^{-1}}h(x)f(x/q)y(x/q)-\frac{1}{q}\int(D_{q}h)(x/q)f(x/q)D_{q^{-1}}y(x)d_{q}x

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{q}\int(D_{q}h)(x/q)D_{q^{-1}}(f(x))y(x)d_{q}x.

Applying (
1.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{q}\int(D_{q}h)(x/q)f(x/q)

\displaystyle D_{q^{-1}}y(x)d_{q}x=-h(x/q)f(x/q)D_{q^{-1}}y(x)

\displaystyle+\int h(x)f(x)D_{q}D_{q^{-1}}y(x)d_{q}x+\int h(x)D_{q^{-1}}y(x)D_{q}f(x/q)d_{q}x.

Using Equations (
4
) and (
6
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\int f(x)y(x)D_{q^{-1}}D_{q}h(x)d_{q}x

\displaystyle=D_{q^{-1}}h(x)f(x/q)y(x/q)-h(x/q)f(x/q)D_{q^{-1}}y(x)

\displaystyle-\int h(x)f(x)r(x)y(x)d_{q}x-\int D_{q^{-1}}h(x)f(x)p(x)y(x)d_{q}x,

and we get the desired result.
∎

Theorem 2.3

Uniform K-stability of polarized spherical varieties
Thibaut Delcroix

Abstract




Abstract.
We express notions of K-stability of polarized spherical varieties in terms of combinatorial data, vastly generalizing the case of toric varieties. We then provide a combinatorial sufficient condition for
G
-uniform K-stability by studying the corresponding convex geometric problem.
Thanks to recent work of Chi Li and a remark by Yuji Odaka, this provides an explicitly checkable sufficient condition for the existence of constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics. As a side effect, we show that, on several families of spherical varieties,
G
-uniform K-stability is equivalent to K-polystability with respect to
G
-equivariant special test configurations for polarizations close to the anticanonical bundle.

Keywords.
K-stability, spherical varieties, cscK metrics, moment polytope, Kähler–Einstein metrics

1. Introduction

In the seminal article
[
Don02
]
, Donaldson initiated a study of the existence of constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics on polarized toric manifolds. There, he notably introduced the general condition of K-stability, thus formulating a precise version of the Yau–Tian–Donaldson conjecture whose aim is to give an algebro-geometric characterization of the existence of constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics on general polarized varieties. Focusing on toric varieties, he further translated the condition of K-stability into a convex geometric problem, and with additional work concluded in
[
Don09
]
, he was able to prove the Yau–Tian–Donaldson conjecture for non-singular toric surfaces. Together with the case of Kähler–Einstein metrics, it is still today some of the most convincing evidence for the conjecture.

The conjecture is still open in general, and examples (see
[
ACGT08
]
) indicate that the condition of K-stability should be modified slightly to a condition of
uniform
K-stability which has been introduced and refined by several authors
[
Sz15
,
Der16
,
BHJ17
,
His1
]
. However, the work of Donaldson combined with some more recent advances (notably
[
CC2
]
) shows that the uniform Yau–Tian–Donaldson conjecture is true for toric manifolds. A different proof of this fact has been provided very recently by Li
[
Li
]
. Odaka noticed that the recent work of Li essentially proves the uniform Yau–Tian–Donaldson conjecture for polarized spherical manifolds as well (this is explained in more details in his appendix to the present article).

Motivated by Odaka’s remark, we translate the uniform K-stability condition into a convex geometric problem for polarized spherical varieties, in terms of the combinatorial data encoding these. This translation provides a much wider playground than the toric case where one can try to show the (non-uniform) Yau--Tian--Donaldson conjecture for different classes of varieties, or derive explicit combinatorial conditions for the existence of constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics. We will concentrate here on the second goal and provide a combinatorial sufficient criterion for uniform K-stability which applies to a wide range of polarized spherical varieties. We intend to present progress on the first goal in another article.

To provide the reader with a better flavor of the convex geometric problem associated to K-stability of spherical varieties, let us first recall the case of toric varieties. A polarized toric variety
(X,L)
is an ordered pair formed by a complex normal
n
-dimensional projective variety
X
equipped with an effective action of
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
and a
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
-linearized ample line bundle
L
on
X
. Such data is fully encoded by a convex polytope
\Delta
with integral vertices in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the correspondence is very explicit: the integral points of
\Delta
coincide with the different
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
-weights of the action of
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
on the space of global holomorphic sections
H^{0}(X,L)
. Let
\mathop{d\mu}
denote the standard Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
\mathop{d\sigma}
denote the measure on
\partial\Delta
which coincides on each facet of
\Delta
with the Lebesgue measure on its affine span
V
, normalized to give unit
mass to a fundamental region of the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap V
. Donaldson shows that the polarized toric variety
(X,L)
is K-stable if and only if the following functional is positive for any rational piecewise linear concave function
g
on
\Delta
:

\LFunctional(g)=2a\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}g\mathop{d\mu}-\intop\nolimits_{\partial\Delta}g\mathop{d\sigma},

where
a
is the real number such that
\LFunctional
vanishes on constants.

A polarized spherical variety
(X,L)
is an ordered pair formed by a normal projective variety
X
equipped with an action of a connected complex reductive group
G
and a
G
-linearized ample line bundle
L
on
X
, such that a Borel subgroup of
G
acts with an open orbit on
X
. Such a variety is encoded as well by combinatorial data, including the data of a convex polytope in a real vector space
\mathbb{R}^{r}
(of dimension smaller than
X
in general), but the definition of these is more involved. Let us just mention the nature of the problem here; we refer to the body of the paper (mainly Section
3
) for precise definitions. The functional
\LFunctional
from the toric case is modified as

(1.1)

\LFunctional(g)=\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}2g(aP-Q)\mathop{d\mu}-\intop\nolimits_{\partial\Delta}gP\mathop{d\sigma},

where
\Delta
is some convex polytope with rational vertices in
\mathbb{R}^{r}
, the measure
\mathop{d\mu}
and
\mathop{d\sigma}
are defined as in the toric case,
P
and
Q
are polynomials, and the scalar
a
is still such that
\LFunctional
vanishes on constants. Let us consider as well the functional

\JFunctional(g)=\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}\left(\max_{\Delta}g-g\right)P\mathop{d\mu}.

We add that the polynomial
P
is positive on the interior of
\Delta
, so that the functional
\JFunctional
plays a role of (semi)norm: it is non-negative and vanishes only on constant concave functions. Our first main statement translates conditions of K-stability into conditions on the functionals above (see Section
2
for a recall on these notions). Before stating these, let us note that there is additional combinatorial data associated to a polarized spherical variety: its valuation cone, which may for now simply be interpreted as the data of some full-dimensional convex cone
\mathcal{V}
in
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
centered at
0
. We denote by
\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V})
the linear part of
\mathcal{V}
, that is, the largest linear subspace contained in
\mathcal{V}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized
G
-spherical variety.
Then

(1)

it is
G
-equivariantly K-polystable if and only if
\LFunctional(g)\geq 0
for any rational piecewise linear concave function
g\colon\Delta\to\mathbb{R}
with slopes in
\mathcal{V}
, and equality holds only if
g\in\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V})
;

(2)

and it is
G
-uniformly K-stable if and only if there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that

\LFunctional(g)\geq\varepsilon\inf_{l\in\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V})}\JFunctional(g+l)

for all rational piecewise linear concave functions
g\colon\Delta\to\mathbb{R}
with slopes in
\mathcal{V}
.

We will further give another formulation of the condition of uniform K-stability and use this new formulation to obtain a sufficient criterion. We now assume that
\Delta
contains the origin in its interior. Let
\mathcal{W}
be a linear complement to
\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V})
. Let
\mathcal{N}_{0}^{\infty}
be the set of all continuous concave functions
f
on
\Delta
, smooth on the interior with differentials in
\mathcal{V}
, such that
\max f=0
and
d_{0}f\in\mathcal{W}
. There exist a non-positive integrable function
J
on
\Delta
with negative integral and an integrable function
K
with integral zero on
\Delta
such that the functional
\LFunctional_{s}
defined on
\mathcal{N}_{0}^{\infty}
by

(1.2)

\LFunctional_{s}(f)=\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}(f(x)K(x)+d_{x}f(x)J(x))\mathop{d\mu}(x)

coincides with
\LFunctional
on
\mathcal{N}_{0}^{\infty}
. Here,
d_{x}f
denotes the differential of a function
f
at
x
, so that
d_{x}f(x)
is equal to
\nabla f(x)\cdot x
if one chooses a Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We refer to Section
6
for the details on the definition of
K
and
J
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
K+J\leq 0
.
Let
b
be the element of
\Delta
defined by

\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}(x-b)(K(x)+J(x))\mathop{d\mu}(x)=0.

If
b
is in the relative interior of
-\mathcal{V}^{\vee}
, then
(X,L)
is
G
-uniformly K-stable.

Despite its simplicity, the above theorem actually provides an explicit and tractable condition to check for a huge family of polarized spherical varieties. The main evidence for this follows from two facts. The first one is that, when specialized to the situation coming from an anticanonically polarized spherical variety, all assumptions but the condition on
b
are automatically satisfied, and that condition translates to the criterion for K-polystability with respect to
G
-equivariant special test configurations obtained in
[
DelKSSV
]
. In fact, as we will show in Section
8
, the condition on
b
translates to
G
-equivariant K-polystability with respect to special test configurations in all cases.

The second evidence is that we can prove in many cases that if we fix the variety
X
but vary the polarization near the anticanonical polarization, the condition on
K+J
is open. The condition
K+J\leq 0
obviously does not look open, and an easier way to show openness is to rely on a stronger condition
K+J\leq c<0
, but it is not always possible for spherical varieties; instead we must understand well how
K+J
vanishes or converges to zero. These two facts together show that one obtains an explicit condition to check for the
existence of constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics on smooth Fano varieties, for polarizations close to the anticanonical one (again, for an explicit range). We will prove this for toroidal horospherical varieties, as well as for (non-Hermitian) symmetric varieties, but we expect this to hold much more generally, and our proof easily adapts to different situations.

Finally, we note that the condition on
b
consists of
\dim(\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V}))
closed conditions and
\dim(\mathcal{V})-\dim(\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V}))
open conditions. In particular, on a Kähler–Einstein Fano symmetric (non-Hermitian) manifold such that
\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V})=\{0\}
, our theorem shows that there exist cscK metrics in an explicit neighborhood of the anticanonical line bundle.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is devoted to the recollection of notions on K-stability. Section
3
summarizes key combinatorial properties of spherical varieties. In Section
4
, we associate to a spherical test configuration a piecewise linear concave function and translate the effect of twisting a test configuration in terms of this function. We then express, in Section
5
, the non-Archimedean functionals for spherical test configurations as functionals on the associated concave functions. We will show how Theorem
1.2
applies to K-stability in Section
6
; we then prove this theorem in Section
7
. We provide a full statement of the sufficient condition for
G
-uniform K-stability thus obtained in Section
8
. The remaining three sections are devoted to illustrating the applicability of the sufficient condition. In
Section
9
, we apply our criterion to the blowup of the three-dimensional quadric at a one-dimensional subquadric and obtain from this the existence of cscK metrics in an explicit neighborhood of the anticanonical line bundle. We show that the criterion always applies for the anticanonical line bundle on Fano manifolds in Section
10
. In the final Section
11
, we provide examples of statements to the effect that in a wide range of situations, our criterion shows that
G
-uniform K-stability is equivalent to
G
-equivariant K-polystability with respect to special test configurations for polarizations close to the anticanonical line bundle. The appendix by Yuji Odaka shows that for non-singular spherical varieties,
G
-uniform K-stability is equivalent to existence of cscK metrics.

2. Background on K-stability

Our references for this section are
[
BHJ17
,
His1
]
. We recall the main notions for the reader’s convenience.

Let
G
be a complex reductive group. Let
(X,L)
be a
G
-polarized variety. A (normal, ample)
G
-equivariant test configuration
for
(X,L)
consists of the data of a normal
(G\times\mathbb{C}^{*})
-variety
\mathcal{X}
, a
(G\times\mathbb{C}^{*})
-linearized ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{X}
, and a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant flat morphism
\pi\colon(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})\to\mathbb{C}
whose fiber
(X_{1},\mathcal{L}_{1})
over
1
is
G
-equivariantly isomorphic to
(X,L^{r})
for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. If the (scheme-theoretic) central fiber is normal, then the test configuration is called
special
. If the total space of the test configuration is (
G
-equivariantly) isomorphic to
X\times\mathbb{C}
, then the test configuration is called a
product test configuration
.

The numerical invariants associated to a test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
may be defined in terms of the central fiber
(X_{0},\mathcal{L}_{0})
as follows. First note that it is equipped with a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action induced by the action on the test configuration. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
d_{k}
denote the dimension
\dim H^{0}(X_{0},\mathcal{L}_{0}^{k})
, let
\lambda_{1,k},\ldots,\lambda_{d_{k},k}
denote the weights of the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
H^{0}(X_{0},\mathcal{L}_{0}^{k})
, and let
w_{k}
denote the sum of the
\lambda_{i,k}
. The quotient
\frac{w_{k}}{kd_{k}}
admits an expansion in powers of
k
at infinity; we will be interested in the first two terms:

\frac{w_{k}}{kd_{k}}=F_{0}+F_{1}k^{-1}+o\left(k^{-1}\right).

The
non-Archimedean
J
-functional
of a test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
is

\JNA(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})=\sup\{\lambda_{i,k}/k\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},1\leq i\leq d_{k}\}-F_{0},

and the
Donaldson–Futaki invariant
is

\DF(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})=-F_{1}.

It is often more convenient to work with the
non-Archimedean Mabuchi functional

\MNA
instead of the Donaldson–Futaki invariant. Indeed, the latter does not vary linearly with base changes of the form
z\mapsto z^{m}
on the test configurations, while the former does. Their values coincide when the central fiber is reduced, and the Donaldson–Futaki invariant is always greater than or equal to the non-Archimedean Mabuchi functional. Furthermore, given any test configuration, there exists a base change such that the resulting test configuration has reduced central fiber, and base change preserves
G
-equivariance.

The
G
-polarized variety
(X,L)
is called
G
-equivariantly K-semistable
if
\MNA(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})\geq 0
for all
G
-equivariant test configurations, and
G
-equivariantly K-polystable
if furthermore
\MNA(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})=0
if and only if the test configuration is a product test configuration. We also use the self-explaining
terminology
G
-equivariantly K-polystable with respect to special test configurations
and abbreviate it to
G
-stc K-polystable
.

The total space of a given
G
-equivariant test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
may actually be the underlying total space of several different
G
-equivariant test configurations. Let
F
be the group of
(G\times\mathbb{C}^{*})
-equivariant automorphisms of
\mathcal{X}
. It contains the factor
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Let
\mathfrak{Y}(F)
denote the set of one-parameter subgroups of
F
. Let
\beta
denote the character of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
of weight
1
, which is identified with a character of
F
. Any one-parameter subgroup
\eta\in\mathfrak{Y}(F)
such that
\beta(\eta)=1
defines a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
, a projection to
\mathbb{C}
, and hence a
G
-equivariant test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})_{\eta}
, in general different from the initial
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
. The test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})_{\eta}
is called the
twist by
\eta
of the test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
. Since we can work up to base change, the same notion makes sense for any
element of
\eta\in\mathfrak{Y}(F)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
with
\beta(\eta)>0
. One can actually extend the definition to irrational
\eta\in\mathfrak{Y}(F)\otimes\mathbb{R}
though it is not needed for our paper.

Without involving a group
G
,

(
J-
)
uniform K-stability
is defined as the existence of a positive constant
\varepsilon>0
such that for all test configurations,

\MNA(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})\geq\varepsilon\JNA(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}).

The polarized variety
(X,L)
is called
G
-uniformly K-stable
if there exists a positive constant
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
G
-equivariant test configurations,

\MNA(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})\geq\varepsilon\inf_{\eta\in\mathfrak{Y}(F)\otimes\mathbb{Q},\penalty\ \beta(\eta)=1}\JNA(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})_{\eta}.

In other words and up to base changes, on the right-hand side, instead of the
\JNA
of the test configuration, we consider the infimum of
\JNA
over all test configurations with
(G\times\mathbb{C}^{*})
-isomorphic polarized total space but different projections to
\mathbb{C}
.

3. Background on polarized spherical varieties

3.1. Main notions

Our references for this section are
[
Kno91
,
Bri89
]
. We recall the main notions for the reader’s convenience.

Let
G
be a complex connected reductive group. We fix a choice of a Borel subgroup
B\subset G
and a choice of a maximal torus
T\subset B
. Let
G/H
be a
spherical
G
-homogeneous space
, that is, such that
B
acts on
G/H
with an open orbit. The
spherical lattice

M
of
G/H
is the subgroup of the group of characters of
B
consisting of the weights of
B
-eigenfunctions in the field
\mathbb{C}(G/H)
. We denote by
N
the dual lattice:
N=\operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. Since
B
has an open orbit, the value of a
B
-invariant valuation on a
B
-eigenfunction depends only on the eigenvalue, which is an element of
M
. We denote by
\varrho
the map from the set of
B
-invariant valuations of
\mathbb{C}(G/H)
to
N\otimes\mathbb{R}
. The image of the subset of
G
-invariant
valuations generates a cosimplicial convex cone
\mathcal{V}
called the
valuation cone
of
G/H
.

The
G
-equivariant embeddings of
G/H
are in one-to-one correspondence with
colored fans
(see
[
Kno91
]
for a detailed exposition of this correspondence).
Let
X
be a complete
G
-equivariant embedding of
G/H
, with colored fan
\mathcal{F}_{X}
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{X}
be the set of
B
-stable prime divisors in
X
.
It is a finite set comprised of closures of codimension one
B
-orbits in
G/H
and of closures of codimension one
G
-orbits, the latter corresponding to colorless rays in
\mathcal{F}_{X}
.
We identify such a divisor
D
with the induced valuation and thus get an associated element
\varrho(D)
of
N\otimes\mathbb{R}
via the map
\varrho
.

Let
L
be an ample
G
-linearized line bundle on
X
.
Then
G
acts on the space of holomorphic sections of
L
.
Fix a
B
-eigenvalue
s
for this action, and denote its
B
-weight by
\chi
.
The
B
-invariant Cartier divisor on
X
defined by
s
is of the form

(3.1)

\sumop\displaylimits_{D\in\mathcal{P}_{X}}n_{D}D,

and there exists an integral piecewise linear function
f
defined on
N\otimes\mathbb{R}
such that
(f\circ\varrho)(D)=n_{D}
whenever
D
contains a
G
-orbit (in other words, when either
D
is
G
-invariant, or
D
is a
color
of
X
).
Conversely, a divisor as in (
3.1
) with the same property defines a Cartier divisor.

The condition that
L
is ample is equivalent to the condition that the minimal function
f
which satisfies the above condition further satisfies the following:

i)

f
is a convex function,

ii)

(f\circ\varrho)(D)<n_{D}
for each
D\in\mathcal{P}_{X}
which does not contain
G
-orbits,

iii)

the slopes of
f
on two distinct maximal cones of
\mathcal{F}_{X}
are distinct.

Brion defines a polytope
\Delta=\Delta(X,L)
associated to
s
as the convex polytope in
M\otimes\mathbb{R}
defined by the equations
\varrho(D)(m)+n_{D}\geq 0
for all
D\in\mathcal{P}_{X}
.
Integral points of
\Delta
give the decomposition of
H^{0}(X,L)
as a
G
-representation.
More precisely, if
V_{\lambda}
denotes an irreducible representation of
G
with highest weight
\lambda
, then
H^{0}(X,L)
is
G
-isomorphic to

\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{m\in M\cap\Delta}V_{\chi+m},

where we recall that
\chi
is the
B
-weight of
s
.

The degree
L^{n}
is obtained from this polytope by Brion as follows. Let
R^{+}
denote the positive root system of
(G,B,T)
, and let
R_{X}^{+}
denote the set of positive roots that are
not
orthogonal to
\chi+\Delta
. Then

(3.2)

L^{n}=n!\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}P\mathop{d\mu},

where
\mathop{d\mu}
is the Lebesgue measure on
M\otimes\mathbb{R}
normalized by
M
, and
P
is the Duistermaat–Heckman polynomial defined by

(3.3)

P(x)=\prodop\displaylimits_{\alpha\in R_{X}^{+}}\frac{\langle x+\chi,\alpha\rangle}{\langle\varpi,\alpha\rangle},

where
\varpi
is the half sum of positive roots of
G
. The above result is proved by considering the first-order asymptotic of the dimensions of the spaces of pluri-sections
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
and Weyl’s dimension formula

\dim(V_{\lambda})=\prodop\displaylimits_{\alpha\in R^{+}}\frac{\langle\alpha,\lambda+\varpi\rangle}{\langle\alpha,\varpi\rangle}.

Indeed, up to the
n!
factor, the highest-order (in
k
) coefficient of
\sumop\displaylimits_{m\in M\cap k\Delta}\dim(V_{k\chi+m})
gives the integral in (
3.2
), and the polynomial appearing in this volume is the highest-order summand of the polynomial giving the dimension formula, restricted to the affine space
\chi+M\otimes\mathbb{R}
.

We will actually need the following refinement, which is a consequence of a general result of Pukhlikov and Khovanskiĭ
[
PK92a
]
. The dimension
\dim H^{0}(X,L^{k})
admits an expansion in powers of
k
of the form

(3.4)

\dim H^{0}(X,L^{k})=k^{n}\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}P\mathop{d\mu}+k^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{\partial\Delta}P\mathop{d\sigma}+\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}Q\mathop{d\mu}\right)+o(k^{n-1}),

where

(3.5)

Q(x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha\in R_{X}^{+}}\frac{\langle\alpha,\varpi\rangle}{\langle\alpha,x+\chi\rangle}P(x)

and
\mathop{d\sigma}
denotes the measure on
\partial\Delta
which coincides on each facet with the Lebesgue measure normalized by the intersection of
M
with the affine space spanned by the face. We explain how this result follows from
[
PK92a
]
in the next subsection.

3.2. Second coefficient in the expansion

The proof of expansion (
3.4
) arises from a generalization of Ehrhart’s multiplicity theorem, as follows from work of McMullen
[
McM77
]
and Khovanskiĭ–Pukhlikov
[
PK92a
]
.

Let
V
be a real vector space and
\Lambda
a lattice in
V
.

Khovanskiĭ and Pukhlikov’s general results (in particular,
[
PK92a
, Corollary 2.5]
) show that, on the group of virtual polytopes, which consists essentially of formal finite real linear combinations of polytopes equipped with Minkowki addition, the evaluation of a given fixed homogeneous polynomial at integral points of a polytope with vertices in
\Lambda
extends to a polynomial function (valuation in the terminology of
[
PK92a
]
) from the group of virtual polytopes to
\mathbb{R}
.
In particular, its restriction to the subgroup generated by a single polytope is a polynomial function.

Assume that
f\colon V\to\mathbb{R}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
and
\hat{\Delta}
is an
r
-dimensional polytope in
V
with vertices in
\Lambda
; then it follows from the results quickly summarized above that the function

k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\longmapsto\sumop\displaylimits_{x\in\Lambda\cap k\hat{\Delta}}f(x)

is the restriction of a polynomial function
\mathcal{F}
from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
.
We note that

\mathcal{F}(k)=k^{d}\sumop\displaylimits_{x\in\frac{1}{k}\Lambda\cap\hat{\Delta}}f(x)=k^{d+r}\sumop\displaylimits_{x\in\frac{1}{k}\Lambda\cap\hat{\Delta}}\frac{f(x)}{k^{r}},

and the second sum converges to
\intop\nolimits_{\hat{\Delta}}f\mathop{d\mu}
for the Lebesgue measure
\mathop{d\mu}
on the affine span of
\hat{\Delta}
, normalized by the lattice; hence the polynomial
\mathcal{F}
is a degree
d+r
polynomial whose degree
d+r
coefficient is
\intop\nolimits_{\hat{\Delta}}f\mathop{d\mu}
.

The formula for the second coefficient of the polynomial
\mathcal{F}
follows from Minkowski’s inversion in the group of virtual polytopes
[
PK92a
, Theorem 2.2]
: the inverse of the polytope
\hat{\Delta}
in this group is
(-1)^{r}\Int(-\hat{\Delta})
(which can be interpreted as the formal sum
\sumop\displaylimits_{F}(-1)^{\dim(F)}(-F)
, where
F
runs over all faces of
\hat{\Delta}
and
-F
denotes the polytope symmetric to
F
with respect to the origin in
V
). It thus follows from the polynomiality theorem that the polynomial function
\mathcal{F}
admits the following expression at negative integers:

\mathcal{F}(-k)=(-1)^{r}\sumop\displaylimits_{x\in\Lambda\cap\Int(-k\hat{\Delta})}f(x)=(-k)^{r+d}\sumop\displaylimits_{x\in\frac{1}{k}\Lambda\cap\Int(\hat{\Delta})}\frac{f(x)}{k^{r}}.

Write
\mathcal{F}(k)=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{r+d}a_{j}k^{j}
. We already know that
a_{r+d}=\intop\nolimits_{\hat{\Delta}}f\mathop{d\mu}
, and we want to find the second coefficient. Consider the sum

\mathcal{F}(k)-(-1)^{r+d}\mathcal{F}(-k)=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{r+d}\left(a_{j}-(-1)^{r+d+j}a_{j}\right)k^{j};

then of course its highest possibly non-zero coefficient is the coefficient of
k^{r+d-1}
.
On the other hand, in view of previous formulas, the left-hand side may be interpreted as

\mathcal{F}(k)-(-1)^{r+d}\mathcal{F}(-k)=k^{r+d-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{x\in\frac{1}{k}\Lambda\cap\partial\hat{\Delta}}\frac{f(x)}{k^{r-1}}.

We deduce from the two equalities that

2a_{r+d-1}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{F}(k)-(-1)^{r+d}\mathcal{F}(-k)}{k^{r+d-1}}=\intop\nolimits_{\partial\hat{\Delta}}f\mathop{d\sigma},

where
\mathop{d\sigma}
is the measure on the boundary which, restricted to a facet, coincides with the translate of the Lebesgue measure on the linear space spanned by the facet, normalized by the induced lattice.

To get the expansion (
3.4
), we apply this to the two highest-degree homogeneous components in the Weyl dimension formula. One should however be careful in choosing the right space to apply this. Consider the vector space
V:=(M\otimes\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathbb{R}
, equipped with the lattice
\Lambda:=\{(m,k)\mid m-k\chi\in M\}
, where
\chi
is the
B
-weight of a fixed
B
-eigensection
s
of
L
. Consider the
r
-dimensional polytope

\hat{\Delta}=\{(x,1)\mid x\in\Delta\},

where
\Delta
is the polytope associated to
(X,L)
as in Section
3.1

Consider the degree
d
polynomial
f
extending the Weyl dimension formula applied to the weights
x+t\chi
for
x\in M
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

f\colon V\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad(x,t)\longmapsto\prodop\displaylimits_{\alpha\in R}\frac{\langle\alpha,x+t\chi+\varpi\rangle}{\langle\alpha,\varpi\rangle},

and let
f_{d}
and
f_{d-1}
denote its highest-degree homogeneous components.
Note that
P(x)=f_{d}(x,1)
and
Q(x)=f_{d-1}(x,1)
.
Applying the results of
[
PK92a
]
and the argument to compute the second coefficient described above yields the expansion (
3.4
).

4. Test configurations for polarized spherical varieties

4.1. Statement

In this section, we encode equivariant test configurations for polarized spherical varieties by certain concave piecewise linear functions. In addition to Donaldson’s work on toric varieties
[
Don02
]
, this task has already been accomplished in different special cases; see
[
AB04
,
Nyb
,
DelKSSV
]
. We freely use notation from Section
3
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For a polarized spherical variety
(X,L)
,
G
-equivariant test configurations are in one-to-one correspondence with positive rational piecewise linear concave functions on
\Delta(X,L)
, with slopes in the valuation cone of
X
.

The test configuration is furthermore special if the associated function is integral linear, and it is a product test configuration if it is integral linear, with slope in
\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V})
.

Furthermore, rational twists of a given test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
are in one-to-one correspondence with elements of
\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V})\cap N\otimes\mathbb{Q}
, and if
g
is the function associated to
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
, then the set of functions corresponding to the twists is
\{g+l\mid l\in\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V})\cap N\otimes\mathbb{Q}\}
.

The correspondence is explicitly described in the proof below.
The key picture to keep in mind is that the polytope associated (as in
[
Bri89
]
) to a trivially compactified test configuration can be described as the set of point below the graph of a concave integral piecewise linear function on some multiple of the polytope associated to the initial polarized variety.

4.2. From a test configuration to a concave function…

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized spherical variety. Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
be a
G
-equivariant test configuration for
(X,L)
. We still denote by
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
the
trivially compactified

G
-equivariant test configuration for
(X,L)
. That is, we glue the trivial family over
\mathbb{C}
to
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
along
\mathbb{C}^{*}
to obtain a family over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We denote the point added to
\mathbb{C}
by
\infty
and keep the notation
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
for the family over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Note that
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
is a polarized spherical variety under the action of
G\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
(this remark will also be used in Odaka’s appendix). Its open orbit is
G/H\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
, and the combinatorial data is easily derived from that of
X
.

Let
\hat{s}
be the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariant meromorphic section of
\mathcal{L}
whose restriction to
(X,L^{r})=(X_{1},\mathcal{L}_{1})
coincides with
s^{\otimes r}
. The divisor associated to
\hat{s}
is
(B\times\mathbb{C}^{*})
-stable, hence an integral linear combination of the form

(4.1)

\sumop\displaylimits_{\hat{D}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}}n_{\hat{D}}\hat{D},

where
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
is the set of prime
(B\times\mathbb{C}^{*})
-stable divisors on
\mathcal{X}
.

There are three types of such divisors:

•

Each divisor
\hat{D}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
with
\hat{\varrho}(\hat{D})\in(N\otimes\mathbb{R}\times\{0\})
must be of the form
\hat{D}=\overline{D\times\mathbb{C}^{*}}
for some
D\in\mathcal{P}_{X}
. Then by our choice of section,
n_{\hat{D}}=rn_{D}
. Note that since the Borel subgroup of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
itself, all other elements of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
must be
(G\times\mathbb{C}^{*})
-stable.

•

There is only one divisor
\hat{D}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
such that
\hat{\varrho}(\hat{D})\in(\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0})
; this is the fiber
X_{\infty}
because
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
is trivial at
\infty
.
The choice of section, on the other hand, implies that for this divisor,
n_{\hat{D}}=0
.

•

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the set
\hat{\varrho}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}})\cap(\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{R}_{<0})
, and write each element in
\mathcal{A}
as an ordered pair
(u,s)
with
u\in\mathcal{V}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}_{<0}
.
Let us also write
n_{u,s}
for the corresponding coefficient in (
4.1
).

Let
\hat{\Delta}
denote the polytope in
\hat{M}\otimes\mathbb{R}
associated to the divisor (
4.1
).
In view of the previous description of the divisor, the polytope can be described as

\hat{\Delta}=\{(rx,t)\mid x\in\Delta,0\leq t\leq g(x)\},

where
g
is a (positive) rational concave piecewise linear function on
\Delta
, expressed as

g(x)=\inf_{(u,s)\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\frac{ru(x)+n_{u,s}}{-s}\right).

Note that since each
s
is negative, the slopes
(-s)^{-1}ru
are rational points of the valuation cone
\mathcal{V}
.

Remark 4.2
.

It may not seem natural that the positive direction corresponds to
\infty
for the reader accustomed to a certain point of view on toric varieties.
It stems from the fact that, under the action
w\cdot f(z)=f(w^{-1}z)
, the function
f\colon z\mapsto z^{k}
is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-eigenvector with eigenvalue the one-parameter subgroup
\chi\colon w\mapsto w^{-k}
rather than
f
itself.

4.3. …and back

We now explain how to reconstruct a test configuration from a concave function.
Let
g
be a positive rational piecewise linear concave function on
\Delta(X,L)
, with slopes in the valuation cone of
X
.
We can find a positive integer
r
, a subset
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{Z}_{<0}
, and integers
n_{u,s}
for all
(u,s)\in\mathcal{A}
such that

g(x)=\inf_{(u,s)\in\mathcal{A}}\left(\frac{ru(x)+n_{u,s}}{-s}\right)

and
u
is a primitive element of
N
for all
(u,s)\in\mathcal{A}
.

Consider the polytope

\hat{\Delta}=\{(rx,t)\mid x\in\Delta,0\leq t\leq g(p)\}.

We build a colored fan
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{X}}
for the
(G\times\mathbb{C}^{*})
-homogeneous space
G/H\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
from
\hat{\Delta}
as follows.
Recall that we are given
(X,L)
and the corresponding divisor (
3.1
), and that colors of
G/H\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
may be identified with colors of
G/H
.
We first include in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{X}}
the colored cones
(\sigma\times\{0\},S)
and
(\sigma\times\mathbb{R}_{>0},S)
, where
(\sigma,S)
is a colored cone of
\mathcal{F}_{X}
.
These account for the trivial family over
\mathbb{C}^{*}\cup\{\infty\}
.
To complete the fan, we add, for each cone
\sigma
in the opposite of the normal fan to
\hat{\Delta}
which has not been considered yet and whose intersection with the interior of
\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{R}
is non-empty, a colored cone
(\sigma,S)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{X}}
, where
S
is defined as follows.
It suffices to define it for maximal colored cones. For such a cone
\sigma
, let
m_{\sigma}
denote the corresponding vertex of
\hat{\Delta}
.
Then
S
is the set of all colors
D
of
G/H\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
in
\sigma
such that
-\varrho(\overline{D})(m_{\sigma})+n_{D}>0
.

We have thus defined a colored fan, hence an embedding
\mathcal{X}
of
G/H\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
As follows from the description of equivariant morphisms between spherical varieties, see
[
Kno91
, Theorem 4.1]
,
\mathcal{X}
admits a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant surjective morphism to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, which induces a trivial family with fiber
X
over the affine chart
\mathbb{C}^{*}\cup\{\infty\}
.
We identify this subvariety with
X\times(\mathbb{C}^{*}\cup\{\infty\})
.

The polytope
\hat{\Delta}
is the polytope associated to the
(B\times\mathbb{C}^{*})
-stable Cartier divisor

d=\sumop\displaylimits_{\hat{D}\in\mathcal{P}_{X}}n_{\hat{D}}\hat{D},

where

•

n_{\hat{D}}=rn_{D}
for each divisor
\hat{D}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
with
\hat{\varrho}(\hat{D})\in(N\otimes\mathbb{R}\times\{0\})
(equivalently
\hat{D}=\overline{D\times\mathbb{C}^{*}}
for some
D\in\mathcal{P}_{X}
);

•

n_{\hat{D}}=0
for the only divisor
\hat{D}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}
such that
\hat{\varrho}(\hat{D})\in(\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0})
, which is the fiber
X_{\infty}
;

•

n_{\hat{D}}=n_{u,s}
for
(u,s)\in\mathcal{A}
and
\hat{D}
the
G
-stable divisor which is the closure of the codimension one
G
-orbit associated to the colorless ray generated by
(u,s)
.

In particular, the restriction of this divisor to
X\times(\mathbb{C}^{*}\cup\{\infty\})
is the product of the divisor (
3.1
) with
\mathbb{C}^{*}\cup\{\infty\}
.
Furthermore, this divisor satisfies the ampleness assumption.
This is not obvious since the colors of
\mathcal{X}
are different from the colors of
X
in general, but our choices of colors for each colored cone were tailored to ensure ampleness.
The associated line bundle
\mathcal{O}(d)
is
(G\times\mathbb{C}^{*})
-linearizable (maybe up to passing to a suitable finite tensor power, which does not seriously affect our statement).
Choosing the linearization such that the natural section
s
of
\mathcal{O}(d)
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariant and has
B
-weight
\chi

yields the final identification of
\mathcal{O}(d)
with the pullback of
L
by the first projection on
X\times(\mathbb{C}^{*}\cup\{\infty\})
.
This concludes the construction of the test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
.

4.4. Effect of twisting

We now elucidate the different possible twists of a given test configuration, as involved in the definition of
G
-uniform K-stability.
For a spherical homogeneous space
G/H\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
, one can easily identify
\Aut_{G\times\mathbb{C}^{*}}(G/H\times\mathbb{C}^{*})
: it is the group
N_{G\times\mathbb{C}^{*}}(H\times\{1\})/(H\times\{1\})
, acting on the right on
G/H\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
Furthermore, this group is diagonalizable, and the action of its neutral component
F
extends to any embedding.
Finally,
\mathfrak{Y}(F)\times\mathbb{R}
may be identified with the linear part
\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V})
of the valuation cone of
G/H\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

The above is not actually necessary since we can identify the possible twists directly by the theory of spherical embeddings.
Indeed, the colored fans of the twist of a test configuration and of the initial test configurations are the same, as is the combinatorial data identifying the line bundle.
Note that what we just wrote is true for the total space of the test configuration itself but not for the compactification, which depends on the twist.
The only difference is thus that the privileged direction coming from the factor
\mathbb{C}^{*}
can be chosen differently, and that will affect the final expression of
g
.

More precisely, the direction can be chosen arbitrarily among those directions in
(\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V})\cap N)\times\{1\}
, or
(\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V})\cap(N\otimes\mathbb{Q}))\times\{1\}
to allow for rational twists.
The effect on
g
is by adding the function
l
for some element
l
of
\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V})\cap N\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.
This concludes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

5. Non-Archimedean functionals for spherical test configurations

In this section, we compute the non-Archimedean Mabuchi functional and the non-Archimedean
J
-functional of the test configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
associated to the concave function
g
.
The computation follows the method of
[
Don02
]
and was previously used to obtain sub-cases of our result in
[
Nyb
,
AK05
]
.

We use the notation of Section
3
, and we set

V:=\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}P\mathop{d\mu}\quad\text{and}\quad a:=\frac{1}{2V}\left(\intop\nolimits_{\partial\Delta}P\mathop{d\sigma}+2\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}Q\mathop{d\mu}\right).

Theorem 5.1
.

We have

\MNA(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})=\frac{1}{V}\left(a\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}gP\mathop{d\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{\partial\Delta}gP\mathop{d\sigma}-\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}gQ\mathop{d\mu}\right)

and

\JNA(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})=\frac{1}{V}\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}\left(\max_{\Delta}g-g\right)P\mathop{d\mu}.

With the notation of the introduction, we thus have
2V\MNA(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})=\LFunctional(g)
and
V\JNA(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})=\JFunctional(g)
.
We provide an expression for
\MNA
rather than for the Donaldson–Futaki invariant.
This is because it is linear with respect to base change, so it is enough to compute it up to base change.
We can thus in particular assume that the test configuration is reduced and use the definition of the Donaldson–Futaki invariant recalled in Section
2
.
This reduction will appear during the proof.

Proof.

Let
X_{x}
denote the fiber of the test configuration for
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
.
Donaldson uses the following exact (for large enough
k
) sequences of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-representations obtained by restriction of sections:

0\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}^{k}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-X_{0}))\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}^{k})\longrightarrow H^{0}(X_{0},\mathcal{L}^{k}|_{X_{0}})\longrightarrow 0,

0\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}^{k}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-X_{\infty}))\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}^{k})\longrightarrow H^{0}(X_{\infty},\mathcal{L}^{k}|_{X_{\infty}})\longrightarrow 0.

Donaldson further notes that the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
H^{0}(X_{\infty},\mathcal{L}^{k}|_{X_{\infty}})
is trivial and that the family of weights of the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-representation
H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}^{k}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-X_{\infty}))
is
(\lambda_{i}+1)_{i\in I}
if
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in I}
is the family of weights of the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-representation
H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}^{k}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-X_{0}))
.

This allows us to express the quantities involved in the definitions of the Donaldson–Futaki invariant and the non-Archimedean
J
-functional as follows.
The sum
w_{k}
of weights of
H^{0}(X_{0},\mathcal{L}^{k}|_{X_{0}})
is given by

w_{k}=\dim H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}^{k})-\dim H^{0}(X_{0},\mathcal{L}^{k}|_{X_{0}}).

Furthermore, if
\lambda_{k}
denotes the maximum of all weights of
H^{0}(X_{0},\mathcal{L}^{k}|_{X_{0}})
, then

\sup_{k}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{k}=\max_{\Delta}g.

In order to use the expansion (
3.4
) applied to
\hat{\Delta}
, we may remark that for any (say continuous) function
f
on
\Delta
,

\intop\nolimits_{\hat{\Delta}}f(x)\mathop{d\mu}(x,t)=\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}f(x)g(x)\mathop{d\mu}(p)

and

\intop\nolimits_{\partial\hat{\Delta}}f(x)\mathop{d\sigma}(x,t)=2\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}f(x)\mathop{d\mu}(x)+\intop\nolimits_{\partial\Delta}g(x)f(x)\mathop{d\sigma}(x).

The latter equality follows from the decomposition of the boundary as the slice
\hat{\Delta}\cap(M\otimes\mathbb{R}\times\{0\})
, the graph
\{(x,g(x))\mid p\in\Delta\}
of
g
(giving each one half of the first summand), and the vertical part
\{(x,t)\mid x\in\partial\Delta,

0\leq t\leq g(x)\}
giving the second summand.
For the graph of
g
to give the right contribution, given the definition of
\mathop{d\sigma}
, it is actually
necessary to assume
that
g
is defined by
integral
linear forms. We can restrict to this case by base change since we are interested in the non-Archimedean Mabuchi functional rather than the Donaldson–Futaki invariant.

By the expansion (
3.4
) applied to both
\Delta
and
\hat{\Delta}
, we obtain the following expansions:

kd_{k}=k^{n+1}\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}P\mathop{d\mu}+k^{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{\partial\Delta}P\mathop{d\sigma}+\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}Q\mathop{d\mu}\right)+o(k^{n}),

w_{k}=k^{n+1}\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}gP\mathop{d\mu}+k^{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{\partial\Delta}gP\mathop{d\sigma}+\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}gQ\mathop{d\mu}\right)+o(k^{n}).

Writing
w_{k}=Ak^{n+1}+Bk^{n}+o(k^{n})
and
kd_{k}=Ck^{n+1}+Dk^{n}+o(k^{n})
, we have

\frac{w_{k}}{kd_{k}}=\frac{A}{C}+\frac{1}{C}\left(B-\frac{AD}{C}\right)\frac{1}{k}+o\left(\frac{1}{k}\right).

Substituting the expressions above proves Theorem
5.1
.
∎

Combining the results of Sections
4
and
5
proves Theorem
1.1
.

6. Restating the problem

In this section, we will show how Theorem
1.2
applies to the uniform K-stability problem.
For this, we will obtain a new expression of
\LFunctional
when applied to smooth functions and show how to derive uniform K-stability in these terms.
To simplify the notation, we assume (by choosing the global section appropriately) that the origin
0\in M\otimes\mathbb{R}
is in the interior of the polytope
\Delta
.

6.1. A new expression of
\boldsymbol{\LFunctional}
on smooth functions

Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{k}
denote the facets of
\Delta
, and let
T_{i}
denote the pyramid with vertex the origin and base
E_{i}
.
This provides in particular a decomposition
\Delta=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{i}T_{i}
.
The author learned the idea of using such a decomposition in
[
ZZ08
,
LZZ18
]
.

We will need notation for the set of equations defining
\Delta
: for each facet, let
u_{i}
denote the outward-pointing primitive normal in
N
,
and let
n_{i}
be the positive number such that

\Delta=\{x\in M\otimes\mathbb{R}\mid\forall i,u_{i}(x)\leq n_{i}\}.

Let
J
and
K
be the functions on
\Delta
defined (almost-everywhere) by

(6.1)

J(x)=\frac{-P(x)}{n_{i}}

and

(6.2)

K(x)=2aP(x)-2Q(x)-\frac{1}{n_{i}}d_{x}P(x)-\frac{1}{n_{i}}rP(x)

for
x\in\Int(T_{i})
, where
r
denotes the dimension of
M\otimes\mathbb{R}
, also called the
rank
of
X
.

Note that these functions are not continuous in general but piecewise polynomial with respect to the decomposition of the polytope, hence integrable. Furthermore,
J
is negative on the interior of
\Delta
.

Proposition 6.1
.

For any continuous function
f
on
\Delta
, smooth on the interior, we have

\LFunctional(f)=\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}(f(x)K(x)+d_{x}f(x)J(x))\mathop{d\mu}(x).

Proof.

We identify
M\otimes\mathbb{R}
with the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{r}
by choosing a basis of
M
.
Let
\nu
denote the unit outward-pointing normal vector to
\partial\Delta
, and let
\mathop{d\sigma_{e}}
denote the area measure on
\partial\Delta
. Also, let
(x\cdot\nu)
denote the scalar product of
x\in M\otimes\mathbb{R}
with
\nu
induced by the identification with
\mathbb{R}^{r}
.

For
f
smooth, the divergence theorem yields, for all
i
,

\intop\nolimits_{E_{i}}f(x)P(x)(x\cdot\nu)\mathop{d\sigma_{e}}(x)=\intop\nolimits_{T_{i}}\left(f(x)d_{x}P(x)+d_{x}f(x)P(x)+rf(x)P(x)\right)\mathop{d\mu}(x).

Note that the considered vector field is radial; hence there are no contributions from the other facets of
T_{i}
.

Let
c_{i}
denote the constant such that
u_{i}(x)=c_{i}(x\cdot\nu|_{E_{i}})

As in the introduction, we choose a complement

7. A combinatorial sufficient condition

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.2
.
The proof is rather elementary and follows from a well-chosen decomposition of
\LFunctional
as a sum of terms which are each non-negative under the assumptions.
We will begin with a simpler analogue of Theorem
1.2
that uses this decomposition and then proceed to the proof.

7.1. A condition for semistability

For now, let
b
be any point in
\Delta
.
Write the integrand of the functional
\LFunctional_{s}
as

(7.1)

\displaystyle f(x)K(x)+d_{x}f(x)J(x)=

\displaystyle\left(d_{x}f(x-b)-f(x)+f(b)\right)J(x)

(7.2)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(f(x)-f(b)-d_{b}f(x-b)\right)(K+J)(x)

(7.3)

\displaystyle\quad+d_{x}f(b)J(x)

(7.4)

\displaystyle\quad+f(b)K(x)

(7.5)

\displaystyle\quad+d_{b}f(x-b)(K+J)(x).

We thus have a decomposition of
\LFunctional_{s}
as a sum of each corresponding integral.

Proposition 7.1
.

Assume that
K+J\leq 0
.
Let
b
be the element of
\Delta
defined by

\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}(x-b)(K(x)+J(x))\mathop{d\mu}(x)=0.

If
b
is in
-\mathcal{V}^{\vee}
, then

\LFunctional_{s}(f)\geq 0

for any continuous concave function
f
on
\Delta
, smooth in the interior, with differentials in
\mathcal{V}
.

Proof.

Using
b
as defined in the statement, we consider the decomposition of
\LFunctional_{s}
as above. Then

•

the contribution from summands (
7.1
) and (
7.2
) is non-negative by the concavity and non-positivity of

and

.

•

the contribution (
7.3
) is non-negative by the assumption on the barycenter

and the fact that the differentials of

are in
\mathcal{V}
,

•

the contribution of the summand (
7.4
) is zero since the integral of

is zero,

•

and the contribution (
7.5
) is zero by the definition of

. ∎

7.2. A preparatory pre-compactness result

For the full proof of Theorem
1.2
and for future reference, we will use the following pre-compactness result, which is a generalization of one used by Donaldson
[
Don02
, Corollary 5.2.5]
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
C
be a positive real number.
Any sequence of non-positive concave functions
(f_{m})
on
\Delta
with
\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}(-f)P\mathop{d\mu}\leq C
has a sub-sequence which converges to a concave function
f_{\infty}
on the interior of
\Delta
, and the convergence is uniform over strict compact subsets of
\Delta
.

For the proof, we use the standard Euclidean structure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For any
x\in\Delta
, let
d_{x}
denote the distance from
x
to the boundary
\partial\Delta
.
For any positive number
d
, set

\Delta_{d}:=\{x\in\Delta\mid d_{x}\geq d\}.

Note that by the continuity of
P
, for any positive
d
, there exists a positive constant
\delta_{d}
such that
P\geq\delta_{d}
on
\Delta_{d}
.
Recall that if
f
is a concave function on
\Delta
, a linear function

is a
superdifferential
of
f
at
x
if for all
y
,
f(y)\leq f(x)+l(y-x)
.

Lemma 7.3
.

For any
(
small enough
)
positive
d
, there exists a positive constant
\kappa=\kappa(d)
such that for any point
x\in\Delta_{d}
, for any non-positive concave function
f
on
\Delta
with finite
\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}fP\mathop{d\mu}
, and for any superdifferential
l
of
f
at
x
,

\lVert\,l\,\rVert\leq\kappa\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}(-f)P\mathop{d\mu}.

Proof.

Consider the ball
B
of center
x
and radius
d/2
, which is contained in
\Delta_{d/2}
, and the half-ball
B^{-}\subset B
where the affine function
y\mapsto l(y-x)
is negative.
Then

\intop\nolimits_{B^{-}}l(y-x)P(y)\mathop{d\mu}(y)\leq-\lVert\,l\,\rVert\delta_{d/2}\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^{r+1}C,

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
x
,
d
,
f
.
We have furthermore

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{B^{-}}l(y-x)P(y)\mathop{d\mu}(y)

\displaystyle\geq\intop\nolimits_{B^{-}}(l(y-x)+f(x))P(y)\mathop{d\mu}(y)\quad\text{since }f\text{ is non-positive}

\displaystyle\geq\intop\nolimits_{B^{-}}f(y)P(y)\mathop{d\mu}(y)\quad\text{by the definition of a superdifferential}

\displaystyle\geq\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}f(y)P(y)\mathop{d\mu}(y)\quad\text{by the non-positivity again.}

This concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
7.2
.

By concavity, we have

0\geq\max_{\Delta_{d}}f\geq\frac{1}{\intop\nolimits_{\Delta_{d}}P\mathop{d\mu}}\intop\nolimits_{\Delta_{d}}fP\mathop{d\mu}\geq\frac{1}{\intop\nolimits_{\Delta_{d}}P\mathop{d\mu}}\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}fP\mathop{d\mu},

and we deduce from Lemma
7.3

\min_{\Delta_{d}}f\geq\max_{\Delta_{d}}f+\mathrm{diam}(\Delta)\kappa(d)\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}fP\mathop{d\mu}.

As a consequence, a bound on
\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}fP\mathop{d\mu}
provides a uniform bound and a Lipschitz bound on any strict compact subset of
\Delta
.
The proposition then follows by applying the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem.
∎

7.3. End of the proof of Theorem
1.2

By Proposition
6.1
, we have
\LFunctional_{s}=\LFunctional
for continuous functions that are smooth on the interior of
\Delta
.
In particular, this is true for any linear function
l\in N\otimes\mathbb{R}
.
Furthermore, for
l\in N\otimes\mathbb{R}
, we have

\LFunctional(l)=\LFunctional_{s}(l)=\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}l(K+J)\mathop{d\mu}=-C_{3}b

for some positive constant
C_{3}
.

As a consequence, the condition that
b
is in the relative interior of
-\mathcal{V}^{\vee}
is equivalent to
\LFunctional(l)\geq 0
for all
l\in\mathcal{V}
, with equality if and only if
l\in\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V})
.
In particular, it implies that
\LFunctional
vanishes on
\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal{V})
.

We prove the result by contradiction using Proposition
6.4
. Let
(f_{m})
be a sequence of functions in
\mathcal{N}_{0}^{\infty}
. Assume for the sake of a contradiction that
\LFunctional(f_{m})\to 0
while
\LFunctional_{+}(f_{m})=1
for all
m
.
The second condition implies, by Lemma
6.3
and Proposition
7.2
, the existence of a limit
f_{\infty}
defined on the interior of the polytope, such that
(f_{m})
converges to
f_{\infty}
uniformly on every compact subset of the interior of
\Delta
.

Furthermore, by using the expression of
\LFunctional(f_{m})
as a sum of non-negative terms as in Section
7.1
, we obtain that each individual term converges to zero.
In particular, that

\lim_{m\to\infty}f_{m}(x)-f_{m}(b)-d_{b}f_{m}(x-b)=0

almost-everywhere implies that
f_{\infty}
is affine.
The slope of
f_{\infty}
is necessarily in
\mathcal{W}\cap\mathcal{V}
by the normalization.
Finally, the assumption on the barycenter

and the term (

) in Section

imply that the slope must be zero.
Hence

.
Back to our assumptions, we have
\lim_{m\to\infty}\LFunctional(f_{m})-\LFunctional_{+}(f_{m})=-1
, but on the other hand,

\LFunctional(f_{m})-\LFunctional_{+}(f_{m})=\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}\inf(2aP(x)-Q(x),0)f_{m}(x)\mathop{d\mu}(x)
converges to zero by the local uniform convergence of

We have reached a contradiction.
∎

8. Full statement

Let us wrap up in this section the statement of the sufficient condition for uniform K-stability of polarized spherical varieties we proved.

Let us note that
\Delta
is not the most direct choice of polytope associated to the polarized spherical variety
(X,L)
.
The
moment polytope

\Delta_{+}
is in some sense more natural to consider as it depends on fewer choices.
Recall that the moment polytope of
(X,L)
is the convex polytope obtained by taking the closure of the set of all (normalized)
B
-weights of plurisections of
L
.
It does not lie in the same space as
\Delta
in general.
More precisely, the relation between the two is a simple translation:
\Delta_{+}=\chi+\Delta
, and
\Delta_{+}
lies in the affine space
\chi+M\otimes\mathbb{R}
.
One easily sees from the previous sections that it is not important in our results for
\chi
to be in
M
.
Hence, the data of
\Delta_{+}
alone allows one to recover both
\Delta
and one choice (or several choices) of
\chi
to apply our sufficient criterion.
On the other hand, the data of
M
is not readily read off from
\Delta_{+}
alone.
The importance of
M
in the statement is seen through the integers
n_{i}
.

The full statement for our sufficient condition for
G
-uniform K-stability is as follows.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized
G
-spherical variety with spherical lattice
M
of rank
r
, valuation cone
\mathcal{V}
, and moment polytope
\Delta_{+}
. Let
R_{X}^{+}
denote the positive roots of
G
not orthogonal to
\Delta_{+}
.
Choose an element
\chi
in the interior of
\Delta_{+}
, number the facets of the translated polytope
\Delta:=-\chi+\Delta_{+}
by
E_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq s
, and let
n_{i}
be the positive numbers such that

\Delta=\{x\in M\otimes\mathbb{R}\mid u_{i}(x)\leq n_{i}\},

where
u_{i}\in N=\mathrm{Hom}(M,\mathbb{Z})
denotes the outward-pointing primitive normal to
E_{i}
.
For
m=r
or
r+1
, let
L_{m}
be the almost-everywhere defined function on
\Delta
such that for
x
in the interior of the convex hull of
E_{i}\cup\{0\}
,

L_{m}(x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha\in R_{X}^{+}}\left\langle\alpha,\left(mn_{i}^{-1}-2a\right)(x+\chi)+\mathrm{Card}\left(R_{X}^{+}\right)\left(n_{i}^{-1}x+2\varpi\right)\right\rangle\prodop\displaylimits_{\beta\in R_{X}^{+}\setminus\{\alpha\}}\langle\beta,x+\chi\rangle,

where the constant

is defined by
\intop\nolimits_{\Delta}L_{r}\mathop{d\mu}=0
for some Lebesgue measure

on

.

Assume that

is non-negative on
\Delta
, and let

denote the barycenter of
\Delta
with respect to the measure

.
Assume furthermore that

is

-uniformly K-stable.

If

is non-negative on

is

of

Note that

and

and

In particular, the barycenters

Theorem

.
∎

Remark 8.5

The natural question regarding this example is whether it is a Calabi dream space in the sense of Chen and Cheng, that is, if all classes admit cscK metrics.
It is unlikely that our main theorem is enough for this, but we intend to answer this question in a later work by studying optimal degenerations for rank two spherical threefolds.

\mathbb{Q}
-Fano and that

(

Assume for simplicity that

The proposition will follow from the judicious choice

and two steps:

(1)

Using Remark

(2)

We can replace

Let us begin with the simple case of toric varieties.
We obviously have

.

For the general case, we will use the description of the anticanonical divisor of spherical varieties by Brion in

Namely, there exists a section of the anticanonical line bundle, with

Now consider the polytope

Recall that the equations defining

To put these in the form

11. Polarizations close to the anticanonical line bundle

In this final section, we illustrate how our main theorem applies to different situations to give equivalence of
G
-uniform K-stability and
G
-stc K-polystability for polarizations close to the anticanonical line bundle.
We however believe that the criterion better shows its strength when applied to a concrete situation as in Section
9
.

Proposition 11.1
.

Let
(X,L)
be a
G
-spherical variety, and assume that there exist a choice of
\chi\in\Delta_{+}
and a positive number
\delta
such that
L_{r+1}>\delta
on
\Delta
.
Then for polarizations close to
L
,
G
-uniform K-stability is equivalent to
G
-stc K-polystability, which is equivalent to the barycenter condition.

Proof.

The result follows from the simple remark that all the combinatorial
data associated to the polarization vary continuously. One can consequently
choose a continuous family of elements of the varying moment
polytopes such that it coincides with
\chi
on the given

L
. Then the corresponding function
L_{r+1}
varies
continuously as well, and the condition
\min L_{r+1}>\delta
is
an open condition.
∎

Corollary 11.2
.

Let
(X,L)
be a Gorenstein Fano toroidal horospherical variety.
Then there exists a neighborhood of the anticanonical line bundle where
G
-uniform K-stability is equivalent to vanishing of the Futaki invariant.

Proof.

For any polarized toroidal horospherical variety, the moment polytope does not touch the walls of the positive Weyl chamber of
G
defined by roots not in
R_{X}^{+}
.
Hence the Duistermaat–Heckman polynomial is positive on each moment polytope.
As a consequence of the proof of Theorem
10.1
, with the choice
\chi=2\varpi_{X}
,
L_{r+1}
is positive on
\Delta
for the anticanonical line bundle.
As a consequence, we can apply Proposition
11.1
to obtain that, in a neighborhood of the anticanonical line bundle,
G
-uniform K-stability is equivalent to
G
-stc K-polystability.
Finally, all special test configurations for horospherical manifolds are product test configurations since the valuation cone is
N\otimes\mathbb{R}
in this case.
Hence
G
-uniform K-stability with respect to special test configurations is equivalent to vanishing of the Futaki invariant.
∎

Proposition 11.3
.

Let
X
be a Gorenstein Fano
G
-spherical polarized variety such that the open orbit
G/H
is a non-Hermitian symmetric variety.
Then on a neighborhood of the anticanonical line bundle,
G
-uniform K-stability is equivalent to
G
-stc K-polystability.

Proof.

For a non-Hermitian symmetric space
G/H
, the valuation cone is the negative Weyl chamber defined by a root system in (a subspace of)
M\otimes\mathbb{R}
(the restricted root system of the symmetric space), and the images of colors in
N\otimes\mathbb{R}
are exactly one positive multiple of each simple coroot of this root system (restricted coroots).
As a consequence, the outward-pointing normals to facets of moment polytopes (which are always in
M\otimes\mathbb{R}
if the symmetric space is
not
Hermitian) are either negative restricted coroots or elements of the positive restricted Weyl chamber.
Furthermore, the name
restricted
is appropriate in the sense that restricted roots are exactly (doubles of) restrictions of roots in
R_{X}^{+}
to
N\otimes\mathbb{R}
.
Finally, the restriction of the Duistermaat–Heckman polynomial to a facet vanishes exactly when the facet is defined by a restricted coroot.

Consider the anticanonical line bundle on
X
, and instead of
\chi=2\varpi_{X}
as in Section
10
, consider the element
\chi=2t\varpi_{X}
.
It is still in
\Delta
for
t
close to
1
since in our symmetric situation,
2\varpi_{X}
is the half sum of positive restricted roots and is an element of
M\otimes\mathbb{R}
.

We then write, still for the anticanonical line bundle,

\displaystyle L_{r+1}(x)

\displaystyle=\left((r+1)n_{i}^{-1}-2a+\mathrm{Card}\left(R_{X}^{+}\right)n_{i}^{-1}\right)\prodop\displaylimits_{\alpha\in R_{X}^{+}}\langle\alpha,x+\chi\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha\in R_{X}^{+}}\left\langle\alpha,\mathrm{Card}\left(R_{X}^{+}\right)\left(2\varpi_{X}-n_{i}^{-1}\chi\right)\right\rangle\prodop\displaylimits_{\beta\in R_{X}^{+}\setminus\{\alpha\}}\langle\beta,x+\chi\rangle.

We know from Section
10
that for every

,

\left((r+1)n_{i}^{-1}-2a+\mathrm{Card}\left(R_{X}^{+}\right)n_{i}^{-1}\right)

is strictly positive if
t
is close to
1
since these numbers vary continuously with

and are equal to
1
for
t=1
.
For the other term, we have

2\varpi_{X}-n_{i}^{-1}\chi=\left(1-tn_{i}^{-1}\right)2\varpi_{X}.

The values of
n_{i}
depend on

, but

•

if the Duistermaat–Heckman polynomial vanishes on the facet
E_{i}
(
i.e.
, it is defined by a restricted coroot), then we can
choose
the value of
n_{i}
to ensure that

is positive for any

;

•

else, the outward-pointing normal
u_{i}
to the facet

is in the positive restricted Weyl chamber, and

, so that

We can then fix a choice of

.
Applying the same arguments as for Proposition

∎

Remark 11.4
.

Let us stress again that it is very likely that the statements proved above hold more generally for spherical varieties.
It would for example be rather straightforward to push further the last proposition so that it applies to all
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein weak Fano spherical varieties whose open orbit is affine.
We leave to further research the exploration of different special cases or the question of finding an argument applying to general spherical varieties.

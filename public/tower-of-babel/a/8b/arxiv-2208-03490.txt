Left seminear-rings, groups semidirect products and left cancellative left semi-braces
M. CASTELLI

Abstract
We study some relations between left cancellative left semi-braces and other existing algebraic structures. In particular, we show that every left semi-brace arises from a left seminear-ring, extending the correspondence given by Rump between skew left braces and left near-rings in [ 29 ] . Moreover, we show a correspondence between certain groups semidirect products and left cancellative left semi-braces satisfying an additional hypothesis on the set of idempotents. As an application, we classify left cancellative left semi-braces of size p ​ q pq and 2 ​ p 2 2p^{2} such that the set of idempotents E E is a Sylow subgroup of the multiplicative group. Finally, we study various type of nilpotency, recently introduced in [ 9 ] , of these left semi-braces.

Introduction

The Yang-Baxter equation, that comes from the papers due by Yang
[
36
]
and Baxter
[
4
]
, is a basic equation of statistical mechanics. Classifying the solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation seems to be hard and it is still an open problem. In 1992 Drinfel’d
[
19
]
moved the interest of several researchers for finding and classifying a particular class of solutions of this equation, the so-called set-theoretic solutions. Namely, a
set-theoretic solution of the Yang-Baxter equation
, or simply a
solution
, on a non-empty set
X
is a pair
\left(X,r\right)
, where

r:X\times X\to X\times X
is a map such that the relation

\displaystyle\left(r\times\id_{X}\right)\left(\id_{X}\times r\right)\left(r\times\id_{X}\right)=\left(\id_{X}\times r\right)\left(r\times\id_{X}\right)\left(\id_{X}\times r\right)

is satisfied.
Writing a solution
(X,r)
as
r\left(x,y\right)=\left(\lambda_{x}\left(y\right)\rho_{y}\left(x\right)\right)
, with

\lambda_{x},\rho_{x}
maps from
X
into itself, for every

, we say that
(X,r)
is
left non-degenerate
if
\lambda_{x}\in\Sym_{X}
for every
x\in X
,
non-degenerate
if
\lambda_{x},\rho_{x}\in\Sym_{X}
for every
x\in X
, and
involutive
if

.
After the seminal papers of Gateva-Ivanova and Van den Bergh
[
22
]
, Gateva-Ivanova and Majid
[
21
]
, and Etingov, Schedler, and Soloviev
[
20
]
, the involutive non-degenerate solutions have been widely studied.

In order to find and classify these solutions, several algebraic structures were provided. In 2007 Rump
[
25
]
introduced
left braces
, algebraic structures that include Jacobson radical rings. Left braces determine set-theoretic solutions, and conversely, given a set-theoretic solution
(X,r)
, one can associate a left brace
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
. As showed in several recent papers, left braces can be used to take informations on set-theoretic solutions such as retractability, indecomposability and simplicity (see for example
[
8
,
14
,
16
,
30
]
and related references).

Recently, various generalizations of left braces were introduced to study set-theoretic solutions that are not necessarily involutive. In
[
23
]
, Guarnieri and Vendramin introduced skew left braces to study bijective non-degenerate solutions. As one can expect, skew left braces give structural informations on bijective non-degenerate solutions (see, for example,
[
2
,
15
,
23
,
33
]
). Skew left braces were furtherly generalized by Catino, Colazzo and Stefanelli in
[
10
]
to deal with left non-degenerate solutions, not necessarily bijective. Technically, they introduced the notion of
left cancellative left semi-brace
, that is a triple
(B,+,\circ)
such that

is a left cancellative semigroup,

is a group and the equality

(1)

is the inverse of

these structures are simply called left semi-braces: here, we will use the prefix

to distinguish them from the left semi-braces studied in

,
11
,
12
,
13
,

By
[
10

gives a left non-degenerate solution by
\lambda_{a}(b):=a\circ(a^{-}+b)
and
\rho_{b}(a):=(a^{-}+b)^{-}\circ b
for all
a,b\in B
. On the other hand, skew left braces and their generalizations have many connections with other topics such as Hopf-Galois extensions, regular subgroups, rings and near rings, left-orderable groups: for this reason, they increased of interest indipendently of the Yang-Baxter equation.

Several authors studied and classified large classes of skew left braces and investigated the ones having specific properties (see for example
[
1
,
3
,
18

28
,
32
,
35
]

At the same time, some other papers studied general effects of the "distributive law" to left braces and their generalizations. To this end, several unifying theories were developed using ring and group theoretical tools. In this context, some remarkable links between skew left braces and other existing algebraic structures, such as rings, heaps and near-rings, were recently discovered (see for example
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
29

In this paper, we follow these two tracks to investigate left cancellative left semi-braces, that represent, up to now, a quite unexplored field. Indeed, after the seminal papers of Catino, Colazzo and Stefanelli

The first aim to this paper is to extend the link between skew left braces and left near-rings given by Rump in

In the final section, we study left and right nilpotency, recently introduced in

Basic definitions and results

In this section, we introduce the basic definitions and results involving left semi-braces mainly contained in
[
9
,
10
,
24
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A set
B
with two binary operation
+
and
\circ
is said to be a
left semi-brace
is
(B,+)
is a semigroup,
(B,\circ)
is a group and the equality

a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b+a\circ(a^{-}+c)

(2)

holds for all
a,b,c\in B
, where
a^{-}
is the inverse of
a
in
(B,\circ)
(here, we assume that the operation
\circ
has higher precedence than the operation
+
). A left semi-brace is said to be
left cancellative
if
(B,+)
is a left cancellative semigroup.

The neutral element of
\circ
will be denoted by
0
. The simpler example of left semi-brace is the one given by an arbitrary group
(E,\circ)
together with the
+
operation given by
a+b:=b
for all
a,b\in E
. Following the terminology of
[
10
]
, we refer to this left semi-brace as the
trivial left semi-brace
on the group
(E,\circ)
. We remark that trivial left semi-braces are different from the
trivial skew left braces
: indeed, a trivial skew left brace on a group
(B,\circ)
will be given by
a+b:=a\circ b
for all
a,b\in B
.

Trivial left semi-braces clearly are left cancellative: through this paper, all the left semi-braces will be left cancellative. In this case, it is well-known that
0
is an idempotent respect to the operation
+
and hence it is a left identity for
(B,+)
. Moreover, the additive structure has been completely determined, as we can see in the following result contained in
[
10
]
.

Proposition 1.2

(Section
2
and Proposition
5
,
[
10
]
)
.

Let
B
be a left cancellative left semi-brace. Then, the additive semigroup
(B,+)
is the direct sum
G+E
, where
G:=B+0
is a subgroup of
(B,\circ)
and
E
is the set of idempotents of
(B,+)
, which also is a subgroup of
(B,\circ)
. Moreover,
(G,+,\circ)
is a skew left brace and
(E,+,\circ)
is a trivial left semi-brace.

Convention 1
.

From now on, given a left cancellative left semi-brace
(B,+,\circ)
, we will indicate by
G
the skew left brace contained in
B
and by
E
the trivial left semi-brace given by the additive idempotents.

For our scopes, the map

, introduced in
[
14
]
for left braces and generalized in
[
10
,
24
]
for left semi-braces, is of crucial importance.

Proposition 1.3

(Propositions 3-7,
[
10
]
)
.

Let

be a left cancellative left semi-brace. Then, the map
\lambda_{a}
given by
\lambda_{a}(b):=a\circ(a^{-}+b)
is an automorphism of
(B,+)
and give rise to a homomorphism

(B,\circ)
to

. Moreover, the following properties hold:

-

\lambda_{a}(E)=E

;

-

\lambda_{a}(0)=0

.

We remark that the previous proposition give us an important information: the

on

(B,\circ)

As for left braces and skew left braces, the notion of ideal of a left cancellative left semi-brace, which we recall below, is strictly related to the map

Definition 1.4

(Definition

Let

By the previous definition, the set of idempotents

In order to construct new examples of left cancellative left semi-braces, in

a suitable version of the semidirect product for left cancellative left semi-braces was provided.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let

Let

As a simple consequences of the previous proposition, we obtain the following results.

Corollary 1.7

Let

It follows by

Corollary 1.8

Let

As we said before, the additive structure of a left cancellative left semi-brace has been completely determined. This fact has an important consequence on the multiplicative structure.

Proposition 1.9

Let

Let

∎

2
Left seminear-rings and left semi-braces

In this section, we show the correspondence between left cancellative left semi-braces and left seminear-rings, which extends the one given by Rump in
[
29
]
between skew left braces and left near-rings.

We start recalling basic definitions and results on left seminear-rings.

Definition 2.1
.

A triple
(S,+,\cdotp)
is said to be a
left seminear-ring
if
(S,+)
and
(S,\cdotp)
are semigroups and
a\cdotp(b+c)=a\cdotp b+a\cdotp c
for all
a,b,c\in S
. Moreover, a seminear ring is said to be
unital
if
(S,\cdotp)
is a monoid: in this case, the neutral element will be indicated by
1
.

An element
o
of a left seminear-ring
(S,+,\cdotp)
is said to be a
right zero
if
s\cdotp o=o
for all
s\in S
.

Examples 1
.

1)

Every left near-ring is a left seminear-ring.

2)

Let
(S,+)
be a semigroup and
M(S)
be the set of maps from
S
to itself. Moreover, let
+
and
\cdotp
the binary operations on
M(S)
given by
(f+g)(x):=f(x)+g(x)
and
(f\cdotp g)(x):=g(f(x))
for all
x\in S,
and
f,g\in M(S).
Then, the triple
(M(S),+,\cdotp)
is a left seminear-ring having the identity function as unit. Moreover, any constant function is a right zero.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(S,+,\cdotp)
be a left seminear-ring. A subset
T
of
S
is said to be a
left subseminear-ring
if it is closed under the operations
+
and
\cdotp
.

The left subseminear-ring provided in the following proposition is of crucial importance for the goal of this section.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
S
be a left seminear ring and
o
a right zero of
(S,+)
. Then, the set
S_{o}:=\{o\cdotp a|a\in S\}
is a left subseminear-ring of
S
containing
o

Proof.

Straightforward.
∎

Definition 2.4

Let

where

Now we are able to show the main results of the section. At first, we show that left seminear-rings provide left semi-braces.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(S,+,\cdotp)
be a unital left seminear-ring and
(o,e)
be an exponential of

. Moreover, suppose that
(S,+)
is a left cancellative semigroup. Then, the triple

Since

, there exists an element that we indicate by

such that
1+e(s\circ^{\prime}r)=(1+e(s))\cdotp(1+e(r))=1+e(s)+(1+e(s))\cdotp e(r)
. Moreover, since
(S,+)
is left cancellative we have that
e(s\circ^{\prime}r)=e(s)+(1+e(s))\cdotp e(r)

\displaystyle s\circ^{\prime}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o\cdotp e(s\circ^{\prime}r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o\cdotp(e(s)+(1+e(s))\cdotp e(r))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o\cdotp e(s)+(o\cdotp 1+o\cdotp e(s))\cdotp e(r))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s+s\cdotp e(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\cdotp(1+e(r))

where the fourth equality follows because

\displaystyle(s\circ r)\circ t=(s\cdotp(1+e(r)))\cdotp(1+e(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(o\cdotp(1+e(s))\cdotp(1+e(r)))\cdotp(1+e(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o\cdotp(1+e(s))\cdotp(((1+e(r))\cdotp(1+e(t)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o\cdotp(1+e(s))\cdotp(1+e(r\circ t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\circ(r\circ t)

Therefore, the previous propositions showed the following correspondence theorem.

Theorem 2.7

Let
(S,+,\circ)

is given by
s\circ t:=s\cdotp(1+e(t))
for all
s,t\in S_{o}
, is a left cancellative left semi-brace. Conversely, every left cancellative left semi-brace arises in this way.

As a final remark of the section, we highlight that the previous theorem, replacing left seminear-ring by left near-ring and left cancellative left semi-brace by skew left brace, allow to recover Rump’s correspondence given in
[
29
, Theorem 1]
.

3
Left semi-braces with
G=Ker(\lambda_{|_{E}})
and groups semidirect products

In this section, we study the structure of left cancellative left semi-braces where the group
G
acts trivially on the additive idempotents by the map
\lambda
. As a main result, we show such a left semi-braces are completely determined by a skew left brace and a groups semidirect product.

By
1.3
,
\lambda
induces an action of
(B,\circ)
on
E
. From now on, we indicate this action by
\lambda_{|_{E}}
. We start showing that the kernel of
\lambda_{|_{E}}
is contained in
G
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
B
be a left cancellative left semi-braces. Then, the kernel of
\lambda_{|_{E}}
is contained in
G
.

Proof.

Let
b
an element of
Ker(\lambda_{|_{E}})
. By
1.9
, there exist
g\in G,e\in E
such that
b=g\circ e
. Then
\lambda_{g\circ e}(f)=\lambda_{g}\lambda_{e}(f)=f
for all
f\in E
, and this implies that
0=\lambda_{g}^{-1}(0)=\lambda_{e}(0)=e
, hence
Ker(\lambda_{|_{E}})
is contained in
G
.
∎

Now, we want to provide a structure theorem for the left cancellative left semi-braces having
G
equal to
Ker(\lambda_{|_{E}})
. At first, we need a preliminary lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
B
be a left cancellative left semi-braces,
e
an element of
E
and suppose that
G=Ker(\lambda_{|_{E}})
. Then, the map
\alpha_{e}:G\longrightarrow G
,
g\mapsto e\circ g\circ e^{-}
is an element of
Aut(G,+,\circ)
.

Proof.

Since
G
is equal to the kernel of the groups homomorphism
\lambda_{|_{E}}:(B,\circ)\longrightarrow Aut(E,+)
, it is a normal subgroup of
(B,\circ)
and clearly
\alpha_{e}\in Aut(G,\circ)
. Therefore it is sufficient to show that
\alpha_{e}(g_{1}+g_{2})=\alpha_{e}(g_{1})+\alpha_{e}(g_{2})
for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
. Indeed

\displaystyle e\circ(g_{1}+g_{2})\circ e^{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\circ(g_{1}+g_{2}+\lambda_{g_{1}+g_{2}}(e^{-}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\circ(g_{1}+g_{2}+e^{-})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\circ(g_{1}+e^{-}+g_{2}+e^{-})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\circ(g_{1}\circ\lambda_{g_{1}^{-}}(e^{-})+g_{2}\circ\lambda_{g_{2}^{-}}(e^{-}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\circ(g_{1}\circ e^{-}+g_{2}\circ e^{-})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\circ g_{1}\circ e^{-}+e\circ(e^{-}+g_{2}\circ e^{-})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\circ g_{1}\circ e^{-}+e\circ g_{2}\circ e^{-}

for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
, hence the thesis.
∎

The previous lemma states that, given a left cancellative semi-brace
B
such that
G=Ker(\lambda_{|_{E}})
, one can define a standard semidirect product of left semi-brace of
G
by
E
using the map
\alpha:E\longrightarrow Aut(G,+,\circ)
,
e\mapsto\alpha_{e}
. The next theorem shows that this semidirect product completely determines the structure of
B
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
B
be a left cancellative left semi-braces. Then,
G
is equal to
Ker(\lambda_{|_{E}})
if and only if
B
is isomorphic to a semidirect product of left semi-braces
A\rtimes T
of a skew left brace
A
by a trivial left semi-brace
T
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a left cancellative left semi-brace such that
G=Ker(\lambda_{|_{E}})
. Let
f
be the map from
B
to
G\rtimes_{\alpha}E
given by
f(g+e):=(g,e)
for all
g\in G
,
e\in E
. Clearly,
f
is bijective and

f((g_{1}+e_{1})+(g_{2}+e_{2}))=f(g_{1}+g_{2}+e_{2})=(g_{1}+g_{2},e_{2})

f(g_{1}+e_{1})+f(g_{2}+e_{2})=(g_{1},e_{1})+(g_{2},e_{2})=(g_{1}+g_{2},e_{2})

for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
,
e_{1},e_{2}\in E
, hence
f
is an isomorphism between the additive semigroups. Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle(g_{1}+e_{1})\circ(g_{2}+e_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{1}\circ\lambda_{g_{1}^{-}}(e_{1})\circ g_{2}\circ\lambda_{g_{2}^{-}}(e_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{1}\circ e_{1}\circ g_{2}\circ e_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{1}\circ e_{1}\circ g_{2}\circ e_{1}^{-}\circ e_{1}\circ e_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g_{1}\circ e_{1}\circ g_{2}\circ e_{1}^{-})+\lambda_{(g_{1}\circ e_{1}\circ g_{2}\circ e_{1}^{-})^{-}}(e_{1}\circ e_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g_{1}\circ\alpha_{e_{1}}(g_{2}))+(e_{1}\circ e_{2})

for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
,
e_{1},e_{2}\in E
, and this clearly implies that
f
is an isomorphism from
(B,\circ)
to the multiplicative group of
G\rtimes_{\alpha}E
, therefore the necessary condition follows.

Conversely, if
B\cong A\rtimes T
for some skew left brace
A
and a trivial left semi-brace
T
, by a standard calculation one can show that
\{0\}\times T
is the set of idempotents
E
and
G
is equal to
A\times\{0\}
. Moreover, we have that
\lambda_{(g_{1},e_{1})}(0,e_{2})=(0,e_{1}\circ e_{2})
for all
g_{1}\in A,

e_{1},e_{2}\in E
, therefore it follows that
Ker(\lambda_{|_{E}})=A\times\{0\}
.
∎

Now, we conclude the section by a further characterization of left cancellative left semi-braces having
G=Ker(\lambda_{|_{E}})
. Indeed, we show that all these left semi-braces come from a particular semidirect product of groups.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
B
be a left cancellative left semi-braces such that
G=Ker(\lambda_{|_{E}})
. Then,
G
is a skew left brace such that
(G,\circ)
is a normal subgroup of
(B,\circ)
, the intersection
G\cap E
is equal to
\{0\}
, the automorphism of
(G,\circ)
given by
g\mapsto e\circ g\circ e^{-}
is also an automorphism of
(G,+)
for all
e\in E
and
(B,\circ)
is equal to the semidirect product of
(G,\circ)
and
(E,\circ)
.

Conversely, every groups semidirect product
A=H\rtimes_{\beta}F
together with a skew left brace structure on
H
such that
\beta_{f}\in Aut(H,+,\circ)
for all
f\in F
give rise to a left cancellative left semi-brace on
A
such that
H\times\{0\}
is equal to
Ker(\lambda_{|_{F}})
and
\{0\}\times F
is the set of idempotents.

Proof.

The first part follows by
3.3
. Conversely, suppose that
A
is equal to
H\rtimes_{\beta}F
, where
H
,
F
and
\beta
are as in the hypothesis. Let
\oplus
the binary operation on
H\times F
given by

(h_{1},f_{1})\oplus(h_{2},f_{2}):=(h_{1}+h_{2},f_{2})

for all
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
,
f_{1},f_{2}\in F
. Since
(A,\oplus,\circ)
coincides with the semidirect product of the skew left brace
(H,+,\circ)
and the trivial left semi-brace on
(F,\circ)
, it is a left cancellative left semi-brace. By an easy calculation, one can show that
H\times\{0\}
is equal to
Ker(\lambda_{|_{F}})
and
\{0\}\times F
is the set of idempotents.
∎

6
Left and right nilpotency of left cancellative left semi-braces

The goal of this section is to study various types of nilpotency, recently introduced in
[
9
]
, for the left semi-braces characterized in Section
3
. In particular, we characterize the ones that are right nilpotent and we find sufficient conditions to be right nil or left nil. These results will be applied to partially understand left and right nilpotency of the left semi-braces of size
pq
and
2p^{2}
obtained in Sections
4
and
5
, many of which will allow us to answer in negative to
[
9
, Question 1]
.

Let
B
be a left cancellative left semi-brace and
\cdotp
the binary operation on
B
given by

a\cdotp b:=\lambda_{a}(a^{-})+a\circ b+\lambda_{b}(b^{-})

for all
a,b\in B
. By
[
9
, Section 3]
, we have that
a\cdotp b\in G
, for all
a,b\in B
. Therefore, if
X
and
Y
are subsets of
B
, we denote by
X\cdotp Y
the additive subgroup of
(G,+)
generated by the elements of the form
a\cdotp b
, where
a\in X
and
b\in Y
. In the case
X=Y:=B
one can give two further notions which will be important in this section.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
B
be a left cancellative left semi-brace. We define the subset
B^{(n)}
and
B^{n}
by
B^{(1)}=B^{1}:=B
and

B^{(n+1)}:=B^{(n)}\cdotp B+E\qquad B^{n+1}:=B\cdotp B^{n}+E

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In analogy to skew left braces, we say that a left cancellative left semi-brace
B
is
right
(resp.
left
)
nilpotent
if there exist a natural number
n
such that
B^{(n)}
(resp.
B^{n}
) is equal to the set
E
. As a weaker version of the right nilpotency, we call a left cancellative left semi-brace
right nil
if for every
b\in B
there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
b^{(n)}=0
, where
b^{(1)}:=b
and
b^{(n+1)}:=b^{(n)}\cdotp b
for all
b\in B
. As one can expect, the concept of
left nil
left semi-brace is defined in a similar way.

As a first result of the section, we characterize left semi-braces of Section
2
that are right nilpotent.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
B:=G\rtimes_{\alpha}E
be a left cancellative left semi-brace as in
3.3
. Then,
B
is right nilpotent if and only if
\alpha_{e}=id_{G}
for all
e\in E
and
G
is right nilpotent.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha_{e}=id_{G}
for all
e\in E
and
G
is right nilpotent. Then,
E
is an ideal of
B
and therefore by
[
9
, Corollary 31]

B^{(n)}=G^{(n)}+E
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, if
G
is right nilpotent and
m
is a natural number such that
G^{(m)}=0
, we have that
B^{(m)}=E
.

Conversely, if
B
is right nilpotent we have that
E
is an ideal of
B
, hence a normal subgroup of
(B,\circ)
. This implies that
\alpha_{e}=id_{G}
for all
e\in E
. Using again the equality
B^{(n)}=G^{(n)}+E
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the right nilpotency of
G
also follows.
∎

By the previous result, examples of left cancellative left semi-braces that are not right nilpotent occur in abundance among the ones considered in Section
3
. However, a large family of these left semi-braces are right nil, as we can see in the next proposition. At first, we need a technical lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
B:=G\rtimes_{\alpha}E
be a left cancellative left semi-brace as in
3.3
. Then

(g_{1},e_{1})\cdotp(g_{2},e_{2})=(-g_{1}+g_{1}\circ\alpha_{e_{1}}(g_{2})-g_{2},0)

for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
,
e_{1},e_{2}\in E
.

Proof.

It follows by an easy calculation.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
B:=G\rtimes_{\alpha}E
be a left cancellative left semi-brace as in
3.3
. Moreover, suppose that
G
is a trivial skew left brace. Then,
b^{(3)}=0
for all

.

Proof.

By
Lemma

6.3
,
(g_{1},e_{1})^{(2)}=(\alpha_{e_{1}}(g_{1})-g_{1},0)
and

(g_{1},e_{1})\cdotp(g_{2},e_{2})=([(1+dg_{1})(1+k_{e_{1}}s)-1]g_{2},0)=((k_{e_{1}}s+dg_{1}+dk_{e_{1}}g_{1}s)g_{2},0)

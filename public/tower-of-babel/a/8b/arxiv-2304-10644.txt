Splitting the cohomology of Hessenberg varieties and e e -positivity of chromatic symmetric functions
Alex Abreu and Antonio Nigro

Abstract
. For each indifference graph, there is an associated regular semisimple Hessenberg variety, whose cohomology recovers the chromatic symmetric function of the graph. The decomposition theorem applied to the forgetful map from the regular semisimple Hessenberg variety to the projective space describes the cohomology of the Hessenberg variety as a sum of smaller pieces. We give a combinatorial description of the Frobenius character of each piece. This provides a generalization of the symmetric functions attached to Stanley’s local h -polynomials of the permutahedral variety to any Hessenberg variety. As a consequence, we can prove that the coefficient of e λ e_{\lambda} , where λ \lambda is any partition of length 2 2 , in the e e -expansion of the chromatic symmetric function of any indifference graph is non-negative.

Given a simplicial subdivision of the simplicial complex

These local

Stanley’s chromatic symmetric function of a graph
G
is defined as

\csf(G;x):=\sum_{\kappa\colon V(G)\to\mathbb{N}}\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma)}x_{\kappa(v)}

where the sum runs through all proper colorings
\kappa\colon V(G)\to\mathbb{N}
, that is,
\kappa(v)\neq\kappa(w)
, whenever
v
and
w
are neighbors in
G
. This symmetric function is a generalization the chromatic polynomial of
G
.

In
[
SW16
]
, Shareshian–Wachs introduced a
q

\csf(G;x)
defined for graphs with vertex set
[n]

\csf_{q}(G;x,q):=\sum_{\kappa\colon[n]\to\mathbb{N}}q^{\asc_{G}(\kappa)}\prod_{j\in[n]}x_{\kappa(j)}

where
\asc_{G}(\kappa):=|\{(i,j);i<j\leq\mathbf{m}(i),\kappa(i)<\kappa(j)\}
is the number of
G
-ascents of the coloring
\kappa
. The function
\csf_{q}(G;x;q)
is usually just a quasisymmetric function, but it is symmetric for certain classes of graphs.

An indifference graph
G
is a graph that has vertex set
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and there exists a non-decreasing function
\mathbf{m}\colon[n]\to[n]
satisfying
\mathbf{m}(i)\geq i
for every
i\in[n]
(these functions are called Hessenberg functions) such that
E(G)=\{\{i,j\};i<j\leq\mathbf{m}(i)\}
. We write
G_{\mathbf{m}}
for the indifference graph induced by
\mathbf{m}
. If
G
is an indifference graph, then
\csf_{q}(G;x,q)
is a symmetric function. The path graph
P_{n}
on
n
vertices is the indifference graph associated to the Hessenberg function
\mathbf{m}(i)=\min(i+1,n)
for
i\in[n]
.

Recall that the ring of symmetric functions has a natural involution
\omega
that takes the complete homogeneous symmetric functions
h_{j}
to the elementary symmetric functions
e_{j}
. In their work, Shareshian–Wachs noticed that the omega dual of

is also the generating function of the chromatic quasisymmetric function of the path graph on
n

]
), that is

\sum\csf_{q}(P_{n};x,q)z^{n}=\frac{\sum_{n\geq 0}e_{n}z^{n}}{1-\sum_{n\geq 2}q[n-1]_{q}e_{n}z^{n}}.

In particular the chromatic quasisymmetric function of the path graph on
n
vertices is the omega dual of the Frobenius character of the cohomology of the permutahedral variety associated to
S_{n}
.
The permutahedral variety is a special case of a Hessenberg variety, which is defined as follows. Given a Hessenberg function
\mathbf{m}
and a

\mathbf{m}
and

\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbf{m}}(X):=\{V_{\bullet};XV_{i}\subset V_{\mathbf{m}(i)}\text{ for }i\in[n]\}\subset\mathcal{B},

where
\mathcal{B}
is the flag variety of

is regular semisimple, we say that
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbf{m}}(X)
is a

The cohomology of a regular semisimple Hessenberg variety has a natural structure of

-module given by Tymozcko

(
[

Hessenberg varieties are usually not toric varieties, so they do not have an associated fan, but still, in analogy with the case of the permutahedral variety, one could think of the Frobenius character of its cohomology as the analogue of the symmetric function generalization of its

Question 1.1

What is the analogue for Hessenberg varieties of the local

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbf{m}}(X)

\displaystyle\to\Gr(1,n)=\mathbb{P}^{n-1}

\displaystyle V_{\bullet}

\displaystyle\mapsto V_{1}.

Proposition 2.1

Let

Remark 2.2
.

Recall the definition of the Schubert cell. For a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
and a full flag
F_{\bullet}
the Schubert cell is defined as

\Omega_{\sigma}^{\circ}(F_{\bullet}):=\{V_{\bullet};\dim F_{i}\cap V_{j}=r_{ij}(\sigma)\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,n\text{ and }j=1,\ldots,n\}.

For a partial flag variety
V^{\prime}_{\bullet}=V_{0}^{\prime}\subset V_{1}^{\prime}\subset\ldots V^{\prime}_{n-k}
and a permutation
\sigma\in(S_{n})^{J_{k}}
, we define the cell

\Omega_{\sigma,n-k}^{\circ}(V^{\prime}_{\bullet})=\{V_{\bullet};\dim V_{i}^{\prime}\cap V_{j}:=r_{ij}(\sigma)\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,n-k\text{ and }j=1,\ldots,n\},

which is the union of Schubert cells

\Omega_{\sigma,n-k}^{\circ}(V^{\prime}_{\bullet})=\bigsqcup_{\sigma^{\prime}\in(S_{1}^{n-k}\times S_{k})\sigma}\Omega_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\circ}(\overline{V}^{\prime}_{\bullet})

where
\overline{V}^{\prime}_{\bullet}
is any completion of
V^{\prime}_{\bullet}
to a full flag. So the cell
(\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbf{m},k,\sigma}^{\circ})^{rs}
is the intersection of
(\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbf{m}}^{\circ})^{rs}
with the cell
\Omega_{\sigma}(V_{\bullet}^{\prime})
.

Moreover, for a full flag
F_{\bullet}=(F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\ldots\subset F_{n})
, we define

\Omega_{\sigma,n-k}^{\circ}(F_{\bullet}):=\Omega_{\sigma,n-k}^{\circ}(F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\ldots\subset F_{n-k}).

Each permutation
\sigma\in(S_{n})^{J_{k}}
is determined by its restriction
\sigma|_{[n-k]}\colon[n-k]\to[n]
. Indeed, we have that
\sigma(i+1)>\sigma(i)
for every
i=n-k+1,\ldots,n-1
. It is sometimes more intuitive to consider only the function
\tau\colon[n-k]\to[n]
associated to
\sigma
, in this case we write
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbf{m},k,\tau}
instead of
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbf{m},k,\sigma}
. Also note that

r_{ij}(\sigma)=|\{k;k\leq i,\tau(k)\leq j\}|=:r_{ij}(\tau)

whenever
i\leq n-k
.

We have a characterization of the injective functions
\tau\colon[n-k]\to[n]
satisfying
\tau(1)=1
and
\tau(i+1)<\mathbf{m}(\tau(i))
in terms of increasing subtrees of the graph
G_{\mathbf{m}}
. We denote by
\IncTree_{n-k}(G_{\mathbf{m}})
the set of increasing trees with
n-k
vertices contained in
G_{\mathbf{m}}
and with root at vertex
1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
k
be a nonnegative integer, then there is a bijection

f\colon\left\{\sigma\in(S_{n})^{J_{k}};\begin{array}[]{c}\sigma(i+1)\leq\mathbf{m}(\sigma(i))\text{ for every }i=1,\ldots,n-k-1\\
\sigma(1)=1\end{array}\right\}\leftrightarrow\IncTree_{n-k}(G_{\mathbf{m}}).

{proof}

We use Stanley’s bijection. We let
T
be the tree whose vertices are
\sigma(1),\sigma(2),\ldots,\sigma(n-k)
and there is an edge connecting vertices
\sigma(j)
and
\sigma(i)
, with
\sigma(j)>\sigma(i)
if
i
is the largest index smaller than
j
such that
\sigma(i)<\sigma(j)
(such an
i
always exists because
\sigma(1)=1<\sigma(j)
for every
j>1
). In particular the tree
T
is increasing as for every vertex
\sigma(j)
there exists only one edge connecting
\sigma(j)
with a vertex
\sigma(i)
smaller than
\sigma(j)
.

To prove that
T
is a subtree of
G_{\mathbf{m}}
, it is enough to check that every
i
as above satisfies
\mathbf{m}(\sigma(i))\geq\sigma(j)
. However, we know that
\sigma(i+1)\leq\mathbf{m}(\sigma(i))
and by construction either
\sigma(i+1)=\sigma(j)
or
\sigma(i+1)>\sigma(j)
, in either case we have that
\sigma(j)<\mathbf{m}(\sigma(i))
.

Every increasing tree
T
on
n-k
vertices comes from a permutation
\sigma\in(S_{n})^{J_{k}}
, we now prove that if
T
is a subtree of
G_{\mathbf{m}}
, then
\sigma(i+1)<\mathbf{m}(\sigma(i))
for every
i=1,\ldots,n-k-1
. If
\sigma(i)>\sigma(i+1)
there is nothing to do. Otherwise, if
\sigma(i)<\sigma(i+1)
then there is an edge connecting
\sigma(i)
and
\sigma(i+1)
which must be an edge of
G_{\mathbf{m}}
, which implies that
\sigma(i+1)<\mathbf{m}(\sigma(i))
.

We will abuse notation and write
\IncTree_{n-k}(G_{\mathbf{m}})
to mean:

•

the set of increasing subtrees of
G_{\mathbf{m}}
with
n-k
vertices;

•

the set of injective functions
\tau\colon[n-k]\to[n]
such that
\tau(1)=1
and
\tau(i+1)\leq\mathbf{m}(\tau(i))
for every
i=1,\ldots,n-k-1
;

•

the set of permutations
\sigma\in(S_{n})^{J_{k}}
such that
\sigma(1)=1
and
\sigma(i+1)\leq\mathbf{m}(\sigma(i))
for every
i=1,\ldots,n-k-1
.

Moreover, we write
\wt_{\mathbf{m}}(\sigma)
to denote the number of
\mathbf{m}
-inversions of
\sigma
, that is pairs
i<j
such that
\sigma(j)<\sigma(i)\leq\mathbf{m}(\sigma(j))
. We will also write
\wt_{\mathbf{m}}(\tau)
and
\wt_{\mathbf{m}}(T)
to mean the number of
\mathbf{m}
-inversions of the permutation
\sigma
associated to
\tau
or
T
.

If
\tau(1)=1
(which will always be the case going forward), we let
j_{1}<j_{2}<\ldots<j_{k}
be integers such that

(2a)

\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}=[n]\setminus\Ima(\tau),

and define
\mathbf{m}\setminus\tau
as the Hessenberg function

defined as

(\mathbf{m}\setminus\tau)(i)=\max\{i_{0};j_{i_{0}}\leq\mathbf{m}(j_{i})\}.

In particular the graph

is obtained from the graph

by removing the vertices in

. We have the following characterization of the cell

Proposition 2.4
.

The following properties hold.

(1)

The cell
(\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbf{m},k,\tau}^{\circ})^{rs}
is nonempty only if
\tau(i+1)\leq\mathbf{m}(\tau(i))
for every
i=1,\ldots,n-k-1
and
\tau(1)=1
.

(2)

We have a stratification

\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbf{m}}^{rs}=\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0,\ldots,n-1\\
\tau\colon[n-k]\hookrightarrow[n]\end{subarray}}(\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbf{m},k,\tau}^{\circ})^{rs}.

(3)

If
\tau
satisfies that
\tau(1)=1
and
\tau(i+1)<\mathbf{m}(\tau(i))
for every
i=1,\ldots,n-k-1
, then the map
(\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbf{m},k,\tau}^{\circ})^{rs}\to\mathcal{H}_{k}^{\circ}
factors through a map

(\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbf{m},k,\tau}^{\circ})^{rs}\to\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbf{m}\setminus\tau,\mathcal{H}_{k}^{\circ}}^{rs}

whose fibers are isomorphic to the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{\wt_{\mathbf{m}}(\tau)}
.

{proof}

Let us prove item (1). First, note that since
V_{1}=V_{1}^{\prime}
we must have that
\dim V_{1}^{\prime}\cap V_{1}=1
and hence
(\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbf{m},k,\tau}^{\circ})^{rs}
is empty if
\tau(1)\neq 1
.
Assume by contradiction that there exist an integer
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-k-1\}
such that
\tau(i+1)>\mathbf{m}(\tau(i))
(in particular
\tau(i+1)>\tau(i)
), and a pair
(X,V_{\bullet})\in(Y_{\mathbf{m},k,\tau}^{\circ})^{rs}
. Note that

r_{i+1,j}(\tau)=\begin{cases}r_{ij}(\tau)&\text{ if }\tau(i+1)>j\\
r_{ij}(\tau)+1&\text{ if }\tau(i+1)\leq j,\end{cases}

so we have the following equalities

\displaystyle r_{i-1,\tau(i)}(\tau)+1

\displaystyle=r_{i,\tau(i)}(\tau)=r_{i+1,\tau(i)}(\tau),

\displaystyle r_{i,\tau(i+1)-1}(\tau)

\displaystyle=r_{i+1,\tau(i+1)-1}(\tau),

which implies

\displaystyle\dim V^{\prime}_{i-1}\cap V_{\tau(i)}+1

\displaystyle=\dim V^{\prime}_{i}\cap V_{\tau(i)}=\dim V^{\prime}_{i+1}\cap V_{\tau(i)},

\displaystyle\dim V^{\prime}_{i}\cap V_{\tau(i+1)-1}

\displaystyle=\dim V^{\prime}_{i+1}\cap V_{\tau(i+1)-1}.

However, we have that
XV_{\tau(i)}\subset V_{\mathbf{m}(\tau(i))}\subset V_{\tau(i+1)-1}
and
XV^{\prime}_{i}\subset V^{\prime}_{i+1}
. Hence
X(V^{\prime}_{i}\cap V_{\tau(i)})\subset V^{\prime}_{i+1}\cap V_{\tau(i+1)-1}
. We now claim that

. Indeed, if that were that case, we would have that
V_{i}^{\prime}\cap V_{\tau(i)}\subset V^{\prime}_{i}\cap X^{-1}V^{\prime}_{i}=V^{\prime}_{i-1}
and hence
\dim V^{\prime}_{i}\cap V_{\tau(i)}=\dim V^{\prime}_{i-1}\cap V_{\tau(i)}
, a contradiction. This means that
X(V^{\prime}_{i}\cap V_{\tau(i)})\not\subset V^{\prime}_{i}
, and since
X(V^{\prime}_{i}\cap V_{\tau(i)})\subset V^{\prime}_{i+1}\cap V_{\tau(i+1)-1}
, we have that
V_{\tau(i+1)-1}\cap V^{\prime}_{i+1}\neq V_{\tau(i+1)-1}\cap V^{\prime}_{i}
, a contradiction. This finishes the proof of item (1).

Item

Lemma 2.6

Then, for every

\ch(C_{\Sigma_{1}})=\omega(\LLT(P_{n};x,q+1)).

{proof}

We have the following characterization of

\LLT(P_{n};x,q+1)=\sum_{\mu\vDash n}q^{n-\ell(\mu)}e[\mu].

Analogously, for each sequence
\underline{S}:=(S_{1}\subset S_{2}\subset\ldots S_{d}\subsetneqq[n])
we have an associated cone
\delta_{\underline{S}}
of dimension
d
. The composition of
\underline{S}
is simply
\mu(\underline{S})=|S_{1}|+|S_{2}\setminus S_{1}|+\ldots|[n]\setminus S_{d}|
which has length
d+1
, in particular, the codimension of
\delta_{\underline{S}}
is equal to
n-1-d=n-\ell(\mu(\underline{S}))
. Clearly, the
S_{n}
-module generated by all the cones with fixed composition
\mu
is isomorphic to the induced representation
i_{S_{\mu}}^{S_{n}}
, hence we have that

\ch(C_{\Sigma_{1}})=\sum_{\mu\vDash n}q^{n-\ell(\mu)}h[\mu].

The result follows.

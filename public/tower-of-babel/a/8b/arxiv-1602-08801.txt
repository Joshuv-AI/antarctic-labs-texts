Introduction

Given
H\in(0,1)
, a fractional Brownian motion (fBm)
B^{H}=\{B_{t}^{H},0\leq t\leq T\}
with Hurst index
H

E\left[B_{t}^{H}B_{s}^{H}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left[t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\right]

For
H=1/2

B^{H}
coincides with the
standard Brownian motion
B

B^{H}
is neither a semimartingale nor
a Markov process unless
H=1/2
, so many of the powerful techniques
from stochastic analysis are not available when dealing with
B^{H}
.
As a Gaussian process, one can construct the stochastic calculus of
variations with respect to
B^{H}
. Some surveys and complete
literatures for fBm could be found in Biagini

Let now

\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{s}-a)\delta B^{H}_{s}

F^{\prime\prime}=f
exists in the sense of Schwartz’s distribution. Then the process

(1.1)

{\mathcal{K}}^{H}_{t}(a):=2\left(F(B^{H}_{t}-a)-F(-a)-\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{s}-a)\delta B^{H}_{s}\right),

exists for all

. Denote

(1.2)

{\mathcal{X}}^{H}_{t}(a):=\int_{0}^{t}f(B^{H}_{s}-a)ds^{2H}

for all

•

if the Lebesgue integral (
1.2

{\mathcal{K}}^{H}_{t}(a)={\mathcal{X}}^{H}_{t}(a)\;?

•

how to characterize the process
{\mathcal{K}}^{H}_{t}(a)

1.2

Clearly, the first question is positive by approximating. However, the second question is not obvious even if
H=\frac{1}{2}

•

for which functions does the Lebesgue integral (
1.2

When

When

the second and third questions above are not trivial. The main difficulty consists in the fact that the stochastic integral

(1.4)

\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{s}-a)\delta B^{H}_{s}

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Cauchy principal value

It is known that the Cauchy principal value, named after Augustin Louis Cauchy, is a method for assigning values to certain improper integrals which would otherwise be undefined. Depending on the type of singularity in the integrand
f
, the Cauchy principal value is defined as one of the following:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\left(\int_{a}^{c-\varepsilon}f(x)dx+\int_{c+\varepsilon}^{b}f(x)dx\right)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{a}^{b}1_{\{|c-x|\geq\varepsilon\}}f(x),

where
c\in(a,b)
is a unique point such that

\int_{a}^{b}f(x)dx=\infty.

The limiting operation given in (
2.1
) is called the (Cauchy) principal value of the divergent integral
\int_{a}^{b}f(x)dx
and the limiting process displayed in (
2.1
) is denoted as

{\rm v.p.}\int f(x)dx.

The notation v.p. (valeur principale) is seen in European writings. We have, as an example,

{\rm v.p.}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{dx}{c-x}=\log\frac{c-a}{b-c}

for all
a<c<b
. Moreover, for a Borel function
\varphi\,:\,{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with

\int_{a}^{a+1}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(a)|}{x-a}dx+\int_{a+1}^{\infty}\frac{|\varphi(x)|}{x-a}dx<\infty,

we can define the Cauchy’s principal value

\displaystyle{\rm v.p.}\int_{a}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi(x)}{x-a}dx:

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{a+1}\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(a)}{x-a}dx+\int_{a+1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi(x)}{x-a}dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\left(\int_{a+\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi(x)}{x-a}dx+\varphi(a)\log{\varepsilon}\right).

Recall that the Hilbert transform
{\mathscr{H}}f
of
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
is defined as follows

(2.2)

{\mathscr{H}}f(a):=\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}1_{\{|x-a|\geq\varepsilon\}}\frac{f(x)dx}{x-a}=\frac{1}{\pi}{\rm v.p.}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(x)dx}{x-a}\equiv\frac{1}{\pi}\;{\rm v.p.}\frac{1}{x}\ast f(x),

where
\ast
denotes the convolution in the theory of distributions, which plays an important role in real and complex analysis. It is also important to note that
{\mathcal{H}}f
belongs to
L^{2}
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}({\mathscr{H}}f(x))^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{2}(x)dx

holds, and moreover, if
f
is a Hölder continuous function with compact support, then the limit in (
2.2
) exists for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. For more aspects on these material we refer to King
[
22
]
.

Fractional Brownian motion

In this subsection, we briefly recall some basic definitions and
results of fBm. For more aspects on these material we refer to Alós et al.
[

]

[

,

[

, Nourdin
[

, Nualart
[
26
]
and the references therein. Throughout this paper we assume that
0<H<1
is arbitrary but fixed and we let
B^{H}=\{B_{t}^{H},0\leq t\leq T\}
be a one-dimensional
fBm with Hurst index
H
defined on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F}^{H},P)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the completion of the linear space
{\mathcal{E}}
generated by the indicator functions
{1}_{[0,t]},t\in[0,T]
with respect to the inner product

\langle{1}_{[0,s]},{1}_{[0,t]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\frac{1}{2}\left[t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\right].

The application
\varphi\in{\mathcal{E}}\to B^{H}(\varphi)
is an
isometry from
{\mathcal{E}}
to the Gaussian space generated by

B^{H}
and it can be extended to
{\mathcal{H}}
. When
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
can be written as

{\mathcal{H}}=\left\{\varphi:[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}\;\;|\;\;\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}}<\infty\right\},

where

\|\varphi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}:=\int^{T}_{0}\int^{T}_{0}\varphi(s)\varphi(r)\phi(s,r)dsdr

with
\phi(s,r)=H(2H-1)|s-r|^{2H-2}
. Notice that the elements of the
Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
may not be functions but distributions
of negative order (see, for instance, Pipiras-Taqqu
[
27
]
). Denote by
\mathcal{S}
the set of smooth functionals of the form

(2.3)

F=f(B^{H}(\varphi_{1}),B^{H}(\varphi_{2}),\ldots,B^{H}(\varphi_{n})),

where
f\in C^{\infty}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
(
f
and all its
derivatives are bounded) and
\varphi_{i}\in{\mathcal{H}}
. The
derivative operator

D^{H}
(the Malliavin derivative) of a
functional
F
of the form (
2.3
) is defined as

D^{H}F=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}(B^{H}(\varphi_{1}),B^{H}(\varphi_{2}),\ldots,B^{H}(\varphi_{n}))\varphi_{j}.

The derivative operator
D^{H}
is then a closable operator from

L^{2}(\Omega)
into
L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathcal{H}})
. We denote by

{\mathbb{D}}^{1,2}
the closure of
{\mathcal{S}}
with respect to
the norm

\|F\|_{1,2}:=\sqrt{E|F|^{2}+E\|D^{H}F\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}}}.

The
divergence integral

\delta^{H}
is the adjoint of
derivative operator
D^{H}
. That is, we say that a random variable

u
in
L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathcal{H}})
belongs to the domain of the
divergence operator
\delta^{H}
, denoted by
{\rm{Dom}}(\delta^{H})
, if

E\left|\langle D^{H}F,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right|\leq c\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for every
F\in\mathcal{S}
. In this case
\delta^{H}(u)
is defined
by the duality relationship

(2.4)

E\left[F\delta^{H}(u)\right]=E\langle D^{H}F,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

for any
u\in{\mathbb{D}}^{1,2}
. We have
{\mathbb{D}}^{1,2}\subset{\rm{Dom}}(\delta^{H})
, and when
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle E\left[\delta^{H}(u)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=E\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+E\int_{[0,T]^{4}}D^{H}_{\xi}u_{r}D^{H}_{\eta}u_{s}\phi(\eta,r)\phi(\xi,s)dsdrd\xi d\eta

for any
u\in{\mathbb{D}}^{1,2}
. By the duality between
D^{H}
and
\delta^{H}
one have that the following result for the convergence of divergence integrals which is given in Naulart
[
26
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\{u_{n},n=1,2,\ldots\}\subset{\rm Dom}(\delta^{H})
such that
u_{n}\to u

in
L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}})
for some
u\in L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}})
. If that there exists
U\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\delta^{H}(u_{n})\longrightarrow U

in
L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}})
, as
n\to\infty
. Then,
u
belongs to
{\rm Dom}(\delta^{H})
and
\delta^{H}(u)=U
.

We will use the notation

\delta^{H}(u)=\int_{0}^{T}u_{s}\delta B^{H}_{s}

to express the Skorohod integral of a process
u
, and the
indefinite Skorohod integral is defined as

\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}\delta B^{H}_{s}=\delta^{H}(u{1}_{[0,t]})
. Recall the Itô type formula for fBm
B^{H}
,

\displaystyle f(B_{t}^{H})=f(0)+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime}(B_{s}^{H})\delta B_{s}^{H}+H\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime\prime}(B_{s}^{H})s^{2H-1}ds

for any
f\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Also recall
that
B^{H}
has a local time
{\mathcal{L}}^{H}(x,t)
continuous in

(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times[0,\infty)
which satisfies the
occupation formula (see Geman-Horowitz
[
16
]
)

(2.6)

\int_{0}^{t}\Phi(B_{s}^{H})ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(x){\mathcal{L}}^{H}(x,t)dx

for every nonnegative bounded function
\Phi
on
{\mathbb{R}}
, and such that

{\mathcal{L}}^{H}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\delta(B_{s}^{H}-x)ds=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\lambda\big(s\in[0,t],|B_{s}^{H}-x|<\epsilon\big),

where
\lambda
denotes Lebesgue measure and
\delta(x)
is the
Dirac delta function. It is well-known that the local time
{\mathcal{L}}^{H}(x,t)
has Hölder continuous paths of order
\gamma\in(0,1-H)
in time, and of order
\kappa\in(0,\frac{1-H}{2H})
in the space variable, provided
H\geq\frac{1}{3}
. Define the so-called weighted local time

{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(x,t)
of
B^{H}
at
x
as follows

{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(x,t)=2H\int_{0}^{t}s^{2H-1}{\mathcal{L}}^{H}(x,ds)\equiv 2H\int_{0}^{t}\delta(B_{s}^{H}-x)s^{2H-1}ds.

The Hölder continuity properties of
{\mathcal{L}}^{H}(x,t)
can be transferred to the weighted local time
{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(x,t)
, and then
{\mathscr{L}}^{H}
has a compact support in
x
, and the
following Tanaka formula holds (see Coutin
et al

[
8
]
and Hu

[

]

(2.7)

\displaystyle(B_{t}^{H}-x)^{-}

\displaystyle=(-x)^{-}-\int_{0}^{t}{1}_{\{B^{H}_{s}<x\}}\delta B^{H}_{s}+\frac{1}{2}{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(x,t).

c
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
r,s\in[0,T],\;s\geq r
and
0<H<1

(2.8)

\frac{1}{2}(2-2^{H})r^{2H}(s-r)^{2H}\leq s^{2H}r^{2H}-\mu^{2}_{s,r}\leq 2r^{2H}(s-r)^{2H},

where
\mu_{s,r}=E(B^{H}_{s}B^{H}_{r})
.

By the local nondeterminacy of fBm we can prove the lemma (Yan
et al

[
34
]
), and Yan
et al

[

]
gave an elementary proof by using the inequality

(2.9)

\displaystyle(1+x)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq 1+(2^{\alpha}-1)x^{\alpha}

with
0\leq x,\alpha\leq 1
. It is important to note that inequality (
2.9
) is stronger than the well known
(Bernoulli) inequality

(1+x)^{\alpha}\leq 1+\alpha x^{\alpha}\leq 1+x^{\alpha},

because
2^{\alpha}-1\leq\alpha
for all
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
s>r>0
and
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
we have

(2.10)

c_{H}(s-r)rs^{2H-2}\leq\mu-r^{2H}\leq C_{H}(s-r)rs^{2H-2}

and

(2.11)

c_{H}(s-r)s^{2H-1}\leq s^{2H}-\mu\leq C_{H}(s-r)s^{2H-1}

where
\mu_{s,r}=E(B^{H}_{s}B^{H}_{r})
.

Proof.

For the inequalities (
2.11
) we have

\mu-r^{2H}=\frac{1}{2}\left(s^{2H}-r^{2H}-(s-r)^{2H}\right)=\frac{1}{2}s^{2H}\left(1-x^{2H}-(1-x)^{2H}\right)

with
x=\frac{r}{s}
. By the continuity of the functions

f_{1}(x)=\frac{1-x^{2H}-(1-x)^{2H}}{x(1-x)},\qquad f_{2}(x)=\frac{x(1-x)}{1-x^{2H}-(1-x)^{2H}}

for
x\in(0,1)
and
\lim_{x\to 0}f_{i}(x)=\lim_{x\to 1}f_{i}(x)=2H
for
i=1,2
, we see that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\frac{1}{C}x(1-x)\leq 1-x^{2H}-(1-x)^{2H}\leq Cx(1-x)

for all
x\in[0,1]
, which gives the inequalities (
2.10
). The inequalities (
2.11
) is clear.
∎

3.
The existence of
{\mathcal{C}}^{H}(a)

Beside on the smooth approximation one can prove the existence of
{\mathcal{C}}^{H}
. In order to use the smooth approximation, we define the function
(x,y)\mapsto\Psi_{s,r,a,b}(x,y)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by

\displaystyle\Psi_{s,r,a,b}(x,y):=\varphi_{s,r}

\displaystyle(x,y)-\varphi_{s,r}(x,b)\theta(1+b-y)

\displaystyle-\varphi_{s,r}(a,y)\theta(1+a-x)+\varphi_{s,r}(a,b)\theta(1+a-x)\theta(1+b-y)

with
s,r>0
and
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
, where
\theta(x)=1_{\{x>0\}}
and
\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)
denotes the density function of
(B^{H}_{s},B^{H}_{r})
. That is,

(3.1)

\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi\rho_{s,r}}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2\rho^{2}_{s,r}}\left(r^{2H}x^{2}-2\mu_{s,r}xy+s^{2H}y^{2}\right)\right\},

where
\mu_{s,r}=E(B^{H}_{s}B^{H}_{r})
and
\rho^{2}_{s,r}=(rs)^{2H}-\mu^{2}_{s,r}
.
Denote the density function of
B^{H}_{s}
by
\varphi_{s}(x)
. The following Lemmas give some properties and estimates of
\Psi_{s,r,a,b}(x,y)
. The first lemma is a simple calculus exercise.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G_{i}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
have compact supports for
i=1,2
. Then we have

(3.2)

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}&G^{\prime}_{1}(x-a)G^{\prime}_{2}(y-b)\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)dxdy\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}G^{\prime}_{1}(x-a)G^{\prime}_{2}(y-b)\Psi_{s,r,a,b}(x,y)dxdy\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt-G_{2}(1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}G_{1}(x-a)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\varphi_{s,r}(x,b)dx\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt-G_{1}(1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}G_{2}(y-b)\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\varphi_{s,r}(a,y)dy-\varphi_{s,r}(a,b)G_{1}(1)G_{2}(1)\end{split}

for all
r,s>0
and
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and moreover, if
G_{i}(1)=0
for
i=1,2
, we then have

(3.3)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}G^{\prime}_{1}(x-a)G^{\prime}_{2}(y-b)\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)dxdy=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}G^{\prime}_{1}(x-a)G^{\prime}_{2}(y-b)\Psi_{s,r,a,b}(x,y)dxdy

for all
r,s>0
and
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\beta\in[0,1]
we have

(3.4)

|\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)-\varphi_{s,r}(z,y)|\leq\frac{r^{\beta H}}{\rho_{s,r}^{1+\beta}}|x-z|^{\beta}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2r^{2H}}y^{2}}

and

(3.5)

|\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)-\varphi_{s,r}(x,z)|\leq\frac{s^{\beta H}}{\rho_{s,r}^{1+\beta}}|y-z|^{\beta}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2s^{2H}}x^{2}}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\Bigl|

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{1}{2\rho^{2}_{s,r}}\left(r^{2H}x^{2}-2\mu_{s,r}xy+s^{2H}y^{2}\right)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2\rho^{2}_{s,r}}\left(r^{2H}z^{2}-2\mu_{s,r}zy+s^{2H}y^{2}\right)}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\Bigl|e^{-\frac{1}{2\rho^{2}_{s,r}}\left(r^{2H}x^{2}-2\mu_{s,r}xy+s^{2H}y^{2}\right)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2\rho^{2}_{s,r}}\left(r^{2H}z^{2}-2\mu_{s,r}zy+s^{2H}y^{2}\right)}\Bigr|^{\beta}

for all
\beta\in[0,1]
. It follows from Mean Value Theorem that

\displaystyle|\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)

\displaystyle-\varphi_{s,r}(z,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi\rho_{s,r}}\Bigl|e^{-\frac{1}{2\rho^{2}_{s,r}}\left(r^{2H}x^{2}-2\mu_{s,r}xy+s^{2H}y^{2}\right)}-e^{-\frac{1}{2\rho^{2}_{s,r}}\left(r^{2H}z^{2}-2\mu_{s,r}zy+s^{2H}y^{2}\right)}\Bigr|^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\rho_{s,r}}\Bigl|(x-z)(-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}_{s,r}})(r^{2H}\xi-\mu_{s,r}y)e^{-\frac{1}{2\rho^{2}_{s,r}}\left(r^{2H}\xi^{2}-2\mu_{s,r}\xi y+s^{2H}y^{2}\right)}\Bigr|^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\rho_{s,r}}\Bigl|(x-z)\frac{r^{2H}}{\rho^{2}_{s,r}}(\xi-\frac{\mu_{s,r}}{r^{2H}}y)e^{-\frac{r^{2H}}{2\rho^{2}_{s,r}}(\xi-\frac{\mu_{s,r}}{r^{2H}}y)^{2}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2r^{2H}}}\Bigr|^{\beta}

for some
\xi
between
z
and
x
. Combining this with the fact
|x|e^{-x^{2}}\leq 1
, we get

\displaystyle|\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)

\displaystyle-\varphi_{s,r}(z,y)|\leq\frac{r^{\beta H}}{\rho_{s,r}^{1+\beta}}|x-z|^{\beta}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2r^{2H}}y^{2}}

for all
\beta\in[0,1]
. Similarly, one can obtain the estimate (
3.5
).
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

The estimate

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}(s,r,a,b):=\int_{a}^{\infty}\int_{b}^{\infty}\frac{|\Psi_{s,r,a,b}(x,y)|}{(x-a)(y-b)}dxdy\leq\frac{C_{H,T,\beta}s^{\beta H/2}}{r^{(1+\beta)H}(s-r)^{(1+\beta)H}}

holds for all
\beta\in(0,1),0<r<s\leq T
and
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}(s,r,a,b)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\leq\int_{a}^{a+1}dx\int_{b}^{b+1}\frac{1}{(x-a)(y-b)}|\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)-\varphi_{s,r}(x,b)-\varphi_{s,r}(a,y)+\varphi_{s,r}(a,b)|dy

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{a+1}^{\infty}dx\int_{b}^{b+1}\frac{1}{(x-a)(y-b)}|\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)-\varphi_{s,r}(x,b)|dy

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{a}^{a+1}dx\int_{b+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(x-a)(y-b)}|\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)-\varphi_{s,r}(a,y)|dy

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{a+1}^{\infty}dx\int_{b+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(x-a)(y-b)}\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)dy

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\equiv\Lambda_{11}(s,r,a,b)+\Lambda_{12}(s,r,a,b)+\Lambda_{13}(s,r,a,b)+\Lambda_{14}(s,r,a,b).

Clearly,
\Lambda_{14}(s,r,a,b)\leq 1
and we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{12}(s,r,a,b)+\Lambda_{13}(s,r,a,b)

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{s^{\beta H}}{\rho^{1+\beta}_{s,r}}\int_{a+1}^{\infty}dx\int_{b}^{b+1}\frac{1}{(x-a)(y-b)^{1-\beta}}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2s^{2H}}x^{2}}dy

\displaystyle\qquad+2\frac{r^{\beta H}}{\rho^{1+\beta}_{s,r}}\int_{a}^{a+1}dx\int_{b+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(x-a)^{1-\beta}(y-b)}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2r^{2H}}y^{2}}dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{H,\beta}s^{(1+\beta)H}}{r^{(1+\beta)H}(s-r)^{(1+\beta)H}}

and
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
by Lemma
3.2
with
\beta\in(0,1)
and Lemma
2.1
. In order to estimate
\Lambda_{11}(s,r,a,b)

3.2
we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle|\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)

\displaystyle-\varphi_{s,r}(x,b)-\varphi_{s,r}(a,y)+\varphi_{s,r}(a,b)|\leq 2\frac{s^{\beta H}}{\rho_{s,r}^{1+\beta}}|y-b|^{\beta}

and

(3.8)

\begin{split}|\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)&-\varphi_{s,r}(x,b)-\varphi_{s,r}(a,y)+\varphi_{s,r}(a,b)|\leq 2\frac{r^{\beta H}}{\rho_{s,r}^{1+\beta}}|x-a|^{\beta}\end{split}

for all

(3.9)

|\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)-\varphi_{s,r}(x,b)-\varphi_{s,r}(a,y)+\varphi_{s,r}(a,b)|\leq 2\frac{(sr)^{\beta H/2}}{\rho_{s,r}^{1+\beta}}|(x-a)(y-b)|^{\beta/2}.

It follows from Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{11}(s,r,a,b)

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{a+1}\!\!dx\int_{b}^{b+1}\!\!\frac{dy}{(x-a)(y-b)}|\varphi_{s,r}(x,y)-\varphi_{s,r}(x,b)-\varphi_{s,r}(a,y)+\varphi_{s,r}(a,b)|

∎

The next proposition shows the process

(3.10)

{\mathcal{C}}^{+,H}_{t}(a):=2\left(F_{+}(B^{H}_{t}-a)-F_{+}(-a)-\int_{0}^{t}F_{+}^{\prime}(B^{H}_{s}-a)\delta B^{H}_{s}\right)

exists in

(3.11)

F_{+}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&{\text{if $x\leq 0$}},\\
x\log{x}-x,&{\text{if $x>0$}}.\end{cases}

Proposition 3.1
.

Let the function
F_{+}
be given as above. Then the random variable

(3.12)

\int_{0}^{t}F_{+}^{\prime}(B^{H}_{s}-a)\delta B^{H}_{s}

exists and

(3.13)

\begin{split}E\left|\int_{0}^{t}F_{+}^{\prime}(B^{H}_{s}-a)\delta B^{H}_{s}\right|^{2}&=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}E[F^{\prime}_{+}(B^{H}_{s}-a)F^{\prime}_{+}(B^{H}_{r}-a)]\phi(s,r)dsdr\\
&\hskip-71.13188pt+\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{0}^{s}d\xi\int_{0}^{t}dr\int_{0}^{r}d\eta\phi(s,\eta)\phi(r,\xi)\int_{a}^{\infty}\int_{a}^{\infty}\frac{\Psi_{s,r,a,a}(x,y)dxdy}{(x-a)(y-a)}\end{split}

for all
t\geq 0
and
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

By smooth approximation we can obtain the statement. Define the function
\zeta
on
{\mathbb{R}}
by

(3.14)

\zeta(x):=\begin{cases}ce^{\frac{1}{(x-1)^{2}-1}},&{\text{$x\in(0,2)$}},\\
0,&{\text{otherwise}},\end{cases}

where
c
is a normalizing constant such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\zeta(x)dx=1
. Define the mollifiers

(3.15)

\zeta_{n}(x):=n\zeta(nx),\qquad n=2,\ldots

and the sequence of smooth functions

(3.16)

\displaystyle G_{n}(x):

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}F^{\prime}_{+}(y)\zeta_{n}(x-y)dy=n\int_{x-\frac{2}{n}}^{x}F^{\prime}_{+}(y)\zeta(n(x-y))dy

(3.17)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2}F^{\prime}_{+}(x-\frac{y}{n})\zeta(y)dy,\qquad n=2,\ldots.

Then
G_{n}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with compact support for all

Lemma 3.4
.

Let the functions
G_{n},n\geq 2
be defined as above. Then we have

|G_{n}(x)|\leq\psi_{1}(x):=\begin{cases}0,&{\text{ if $x\leq 0$}},\\
C(1+|\log x|),&{\text{ if $x>0$}}\end{cases}

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and

(3.18)

G_{n}(x)\longrightarrow F^{\prime}_{+}(x)

for all
x\neq 0
, as
n
tends to infinity.

Proof.

Clearly,
G_{n}(x)=0
for
x\leq 0
and

(3.19)

G_{n}(x)=n\int_{(x-\frac{2}{n})\vee 0}^{x}\zeta(n(x-y))\log ydy

for
x>0
.

When
0<x\leq\frac{2}{n}
, we have

\displaystyle|G_{n}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq n\int_{0}^{x}\zeta(n(x-y))|\log y|dy\leq-n\int_{0}^{x}\log ydy

\displaystyle=nx(1-\log x)\leq 2(1-\log x).

On the other hand, by (
3.17
) we get

\displaystyle G_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=n\int_{0}^{2}\zeta(z)\log(x-\frac{z}{n})dy=\int_{0}^{2}\zeta(z)\log[x(1-\frac{z}{nx})]dz

\displaystyle=\log x\int_{0}^{2}\zeta(z)dz+\int_{0}^{2}\zeta(z)\log(1-\frac{z}{nx})dz

\displaystyle=\log x+\int_{0}^{2}\zeta(z)\log(1-\frac{z}{nx})dz

for
x>\frac{2}{n}
, which gives

\displaystyle|G_{n}(x)|\leq C\left(1+|\log x|\right)

for
x>\frac{2}{n}
since

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}|\log(1-\frac{z}{u})|e^{-\frac{1}{1-(1-z)^{2}}}dz<\infty

with
u>2
.

Finally, the convergence (
3.18
) follows from
G_{n}(x)=F^{\prime}_{\alpha}(x)=0
for
x<0
and the next estimate:

\displaystyle|G_{n}(x)-F^{\prime}_{+}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{-1}\int_{0}^{2}\zeta(y)\left|F^{\prime}_{+}(x-\frac{y}{n})-F^{\prime}_{+}(x)\right|dy

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-1}\int_{0}^{2}\left|\log(x-\frac{y}{n})-\log x\right|\zeta(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{-1}\int_{0}^{2}\log\left(1+\frac{y}{nx-y}\right)\zeta(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(1+\frac{2}{nx-2}\right)\int_{0}^{2}\zeta(y)dy=\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(1+\frac{2}{nx-2}\right)

for all
x>\frac{2}{n}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let the functions
G_{n},n\geq 2
be defined as above. Then we have

|G^{\prime}_{n}(x)|\leq\psi_{2}(x):=\begin{cases}0,&{\text{ if $x\leq 0$}},\\
Cx^{-1}(1+|\log x|),&{\text{ if $x>0$}}\end{cases}

for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and

for all

Proof.

Clearly,

for
x\leq 0
, and we have for

\begin{split}G^{\prime}_{n}(x)&=\int_{0}^{2}F^{\prime\prime}_{+}(x-\frac{y}{n})\zeta(y)dy=\int_{0}^{2}\zeta(y)\frac{1}{x-\frac{y}{n}}dy\\
&=\int_{0}^{2}\zeta(y)\frac{n}{nx-y}dy=\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{2}\zeta(y)\left(1+\frac{y}{nx-y}\right)dy\end{split}

by (
3.16

\displaystyle|G^{\prime}_{n}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{2}\zeta(y)\left(1+\frac{y}{2-y}\right)dy\leq\frac{C}{x}

for
x>\frac{2}{n}
. On the other hand, for

\displaystyle G^{\prime}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle|G^{\prime}_{n}(x)|

for

, which gives the estimates of

∎

Denote by

the process concerning the right hand in (
5.2
) and let

u_{t}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{t}-x)g(x)dx

for
t\geq 0
. Then, the process
X
and
u
are measurable. Thus, it is enough to show that the following duality relationship holds:

(5.3)

E\left[UX_{T}\right]=E\left[\langle D^{H}U,u\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\right]

for all
U\in{\mathbb{D}}^{1,2}
by Lemma
5.1
. This is clear. In fact, noting that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}(D^{H}_{s}U)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{r}-x)\phi(s,r)dsdr\Bigr)g(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}(D^{H}_{s}U)\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}}F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{r}-x)g(x)dx\Bigr)\phi(s,r)dsdr,\qquad{\rm a.s.}

for all
U\in{\mathbb{D}}^{1,2}
, we have

\displaystyle E[UX_{T}]

\displaystyle=E\left[U\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{s}-x)\delta B^{H}_{s}\right)g(x)dx\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}E\left[U\int_{0}^{T}F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{s}-x)\delta B^{H}_{s}\right]g(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(E\langle D^{H}U,F^{\prime}(B^{H}-x)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right)g(x)dx

\displaystyle=E\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}(D^{H}_{s}U)F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{r}-x)\phi(s,r)dsdr\Bigr)g(x)dx

\displaystyle=E\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}(D^{H}_{s}U)\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}}F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{r}-x)g(x)dx\Bigr)\phi(s,r)dsdr=E\left[\langle D^{H}U,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right]

for all
U\in{\mathbb{D}}^{1,2}
, and the lemma follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Let
F(x)=x\log|x|-x
. Then second derivative
(F\ast g)^{\prime\prime}=F^{\prime\prime}\ast g
exists in the sense of Schwartz’s distribution, and similar to Theorem
4.1
we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{X}}^{g}_{t}

\displaystyle=H\int_{0}^{t}{\rm v.p.}\frac{1}{x}\ast g(B^{H}_{s})s^{2H-1}ds

for all
t\geq 0
, where
{\mathcal{C}}^{g}
is defined in Lemma
5.1
. It follows from Lemma
5.2
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{C}}^{H}_{t}(x)g(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(F(B^{H}_{s}-x)-F(-x)-\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{s}-x)\delta B^{H}_{s}\right)g(x)dx

\displaystyle=F\ast g(B^{H}_{t})-F\ast g(0)-\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime}\ast g(B^{H}_{s})\delta B^{H}_{s}

\displaystyle=F\ast g(B^{H}_{t})-F\ast g(0)-\int_{0}^{t}(F\ast g)^{\prime}(B^{H}_{s})\delta B^{H}_{s}

\displaystyle=H\int_{0}^{t}{\rm v.p.}\frac{1}{x}\ast g(B^{H}_{s})s^{2H-1}ds

\displaystyle=H\pi\int_{0}^{t}{\mathscr{H}}g(B^{H}_{s})s^{2H-1}ds

for all
t\geq 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
and let
g,g_{n}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
be continuous with compact supports. If
g_{n}\to g
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, as
n
tends to infinity, we then have

(5.4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{C}}^{H}_{t}(x)g_{n}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{C}}^{H}_{t}(x)g(x)dx

for all
t\geq 0
, in the
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The convergence follows from the identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(g_{n}(x)-g(x)\right)^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left({\mathscr{H}}g_{n}(x)-{\mathscr{H}}g(x)\right)^{2}dx.

and Theorem
5.1
.
∎

6.
The case
0<H<\frac{1}{2}

In the final section we consider the process
{\mathcal{C}}^{H}
with
0<H<\frac{1}{2}
. Recall that for
0<H<\frac{1}{2}
, Yan
et al

[
33
]
obtained the
generalized quadratic covariation
of
f(B^{H})
and
B^{H}
defined by

[f(B^{H}),B^{H}]^{(H)}_{t}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2H}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{f(B^{H}_{s+\varepsilon})-f(B^{H}_{s})\right\}(B^{H}_{s+\varepsilon}-B^{H}_{s})ds^{2H}

in probability, where
f
is a Borel function. In Yan
et al

[
33
]
one constructed the Banach space
{\mathscr{H}}=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},\mu(dx))
with

\mu(dx)=\left(\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2s^{2H}}}\frac{2Hds}{\sqrt{2\pi}s^{1-H}}\right)dx

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2s^{2H}}}\frac{2Hdxds}{\sqrt{2\pi}s^{1-H}}=E\left(\int_{0}^{T}|f(B^{H}_{s})|^{2}ds^{2H}\right),

such that the generalized quadratic covariation
[f(B^{H}),B^{H}]^{(H)}
exists in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and

(6.1)

E\left|[f(B^{H}),B^{H}]_{t}^{(H)}\right|^{2}\leq C\|f\|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}},

provided
f\in{\mathscr{H}}
. Moreover, the Bouleau-Yor identity takes the form

[f(B^{H}),B^{H}]_{t}^{(H)}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x){\mathscr{L}}^{H}(dx,t)

for all
f\in{\mathscr{H}}
. By using the generalized quadratic covariation Yan
et al

[
33
]
obtained the next Itô formula:

(6.2)

F(B^{H}_{t})=F(0)+\int_{0}^{t}f(B^{H}_{s})\delta B^{H}_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\left[f(B^{H}),B^{H}\right]^{(H)}_{t}

for all
0<H<\frac{1}{2}
, where
F
is an absolutely continuous function such that
F^{\prime}=f\in{\mathscr{H}}
is left (right) continuous. It is important to note that the method used in Yan
et al

[
33
]
is inefficacy for
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
in general and the similar results for
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
is unknown so far.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
0<H<\frac{1}{2}
and let
F(x)=x\log|x|-x
. Then
F^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{H}}
and the Itô type formula

(6.3)

F(B^{H}_{t}-a)=F(-a)+\int_{0}^{t}\log|B^{H}_{s}-a|\delta B^{H}_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\log(B^{H}-a),B^{H}\right]^{(H)}_{t}

holds and

(6.4)

{\mathcal{C}}^{H}_{t}(a)=\left[\log|B^{H}-a|,B^{H}\right]^{(H)}_{t}

for all
t\geq 0
and
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

Let
F_{+}
and
F_{-}
be defined in Section
3
. Then
F^{\prime}_{+}\in{\mathscr{H}}
is left continuous, and

(6.5)

F_{+}(B^{H}_{t}-a)=F_{+}(-a)+\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime}_{+}(B^{H}_{s}-a)\delta B^{H}_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\left[F^{\prime}_{+}(B^{H}-a),B^{H}\right]^{(H)}_{t}

by Itô’s formula (
6.2
). Similarly, we have

(6.6)

F_{-}(B^{H}_{t}-a)=F_{-}(-a)+\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime}_{-}(B^{H}_{s}-a)\delta B^{H}_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\left[F^{\prime}_{-}(B^{H}-a),B^{H}\right]^{(H)}_{t}

since
F^{\prime}_{-}\in{\mathscr{H}}
is right continuous. Thus, the corollary follows from
F=F^{\prime}_{+}+F^{\prime}_{-}
.
∎

Denote

\displaystyle\Theta_{\varepsilon}(t,a):

\displaystyle={\mathscr{L}}^{H}(a-\varepsilon,t)F^{\prime}(-\varepsilon)-{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(a+\varepsilon,t)F^{\prime}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\left[{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(a-\varepsilon,t)-{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(a+\varepsilon,t)\right]\log\varepsilon.

By integration by parts we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{H}_{t}(a)

\displaystyle=\left[\log|B^{H}-a|,B^{H}\right]^{(H)}_{t}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log|x-a|{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(dx,t)

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\left(\int_{a+\varepsilon}^{\infty}\log|x-a|{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(dx,t)+\int_{-\infty}^{a-\varepsilon}\log|x-a|{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(dx,t)\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\left(\int_{a+\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(x,t)}{x-a}dx+\int_{-\infty}^{a-\varepsilon}\frac{{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(x,t)}{x-a}dx\right)+\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\Theta_{\varepsilon}(t,a)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}1_{\{|x-a|\geq\varepsilon\}}\frac{{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(x,t)}{x-a}dx

\displaystyle={\rm v.p.}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(x,t)}{x-a}dx=\pi{\mathscr{H}}{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(\cdot,t)(x)

almost surely and in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, for all
t\geq 0
and
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
since
x\mapsto{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(x,\cdot)
is Hölder continuous and has the compact support.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
F
be given by Corollary
6.1
and let
g
be a continuous function with compact support. Then the integral
F^{\prime}\ast g\in{\mathscr{H}}
, and for all
0<H<\frac{1}{2}
, the process

2\left((F\ast g)(B^{H}_{t})-(F\ast g)(0)-\int_{0}^{t}(F^{\prime}\ast g)(B^{H}_{s})\delta B^{H}_{s}\right)

is well-defined in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and is equal to

[(F^{\prime}\ast g)(B^{H}),B^{H}]^{(H)}_{t}

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Thus, similar to proof of Theorem
5.1
we can obtain the following occupation formula.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
0<H<\frac{1}{2}
and let
g
be a continuous function with compact support. Then we have, almost surely,

(6.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{C}}^{H}_{t}(x)g(x)dx=2H\pi\int_{0}^{t}({\mathscr{H}}g)(B^{H}_{s})s^{2H-1}ds

and

2H\pi\int_{0}^{t}g(B^{H}_{s})s^{2H-1}ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{C}}^{H}_{t}(x)({\mathscr{H}}^{-1}g)(x)dx

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

Let
F(x)=x\log|x|-x
. By Corollary
6.1
and (
6.1
) we have

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{s}-x)\delta B^{H}_{s}\right)g(x)dx\right|^{2}<\infty,

since
g
admits a compact support. Thus, similar to Lemma
5.2
we can show that the Fubini theorem

(6.8)

\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{s}-x)g(x)dx\right)\delta B^{H}_{s}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{s}-x)\delta B^{H}_{s}\right)g(x)dx

holds for all
0\leq t\leq T
. It follows from Corollary
6.1
and Corollary
6.2
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{C}}^{H}_{t}(x)g(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(F(B^{H}_{s}-x)-F(-x)-\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime}(B^{H}_{s}-x)\delta B^{H}_{s}\right)g(x)dx

\displaystyle=F\ast g(B^{H}_{t})-F\ast g(0)-\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime}\ast g(B^{H}_{s})\delta B^{H}_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[(F^{\prime}\ast g)(B^{H}),B^{H}\right]^{(H)}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(F^{\prime}\ast g)(x){\mathscr{L}}^{H}(dx,t)

On the other hand, by the Hölder continuity of
(x,t)\mapsto{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(x,t)
and Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(a)da

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}F^{\prime}(x-a){\mathscr{L}}^{H}(dx,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(a)\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\left(\int_{a+\varepsilon}^{\infty}F^{\prime}(x-a){\mathscr{L}}^{H}(dx,t)+\int_{-\infty}^{a-\varepsilon}F^{\prime}(x-a){\mathscr{L}}^{H}(dx,t)\right)da

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(a)\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\left(\Theta_{\varepsilon}(t,a)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}1_{\{|x-a|>\varepsilon\}}F^{\prime\prime}(x-a){\mathscr{L}}^{H}(x,t)dx\right)da

\displaystyle=-2H\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(a)\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\left(\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{|B^{H}_{s}-a|>\varepsilon\}}F^{\prime\prime}(B^{H}_{s}-a)s^{2H-1}ds\right)da

\displaystyle=-2H\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}s^{2H-1}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}1_{\{|B^{H}_{s}-a|>\varepsilon\}}\frac{g(a)}{B^{H}_{s}-a}da

\displaystyle=-2H\pi\int_{0}^{t}{\mathscr{H}}g(B^{H}_{s})s^{2H-1}ds

almost surely and in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, for all
t\in[0,T]
. This shows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{H}_{t}(x)g(x)dx=H\pi\int_{0}^{t}{\mathscr{H}}g(B^{H}_{s})s^{2H-1}ds

and the theorem follows.
∎

Remark 6.1
.

When
0<H<\frac{1}{2}
, from the discussion in this section, we have fund that for all non-locally integrable Borel functions
f\in{\mathscr{H}}
, the identities

{\mathcal{K}}_{t}^{H}(f,a):=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{|B^{H}_{s}-a|>\varepsilon\}}f(B^{H}_{s})ds^{2H}=[f(B^{H}),B^{H}]^{(H)}_{t}

in

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{K}}_{t}^{H}(f,x)g(x)dx=2H\int_{0}^{t}{\rm v.p}(f^{\prime}\ast g)(B^{H}_{s})s^{2H-1}ds

hold for all continuous functions
g
with compact supports, provided

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(a-\varepsilon,t)f(-\varepsilon)-{\mathscr{L}}^{H}(a+\varepsilon,t)f(\varepsilon)\longrightarrow 0,

in
L^{2}(\Omega)
(almost surely), as
\varepsilon
tends to zero.

Numerical integration of quantum time evolution in a curved manifold
Jessica F. K. Halliday, Emilio Artacho

Abstract
The numerical integration of the Schrödinger equation by discretization of time is explored for the curved manifolds arising from finite representations based on evolving basis states. In particular, the unitarity of the evolution is assessed, in the sense of the conservation of mutual scalar products in a set of evolving states, and with them the conservation of orthonormality and particle number. Although the adequately represented equation is known to give rise to unitary evolution in spite of curvature, discretized integrators easily break that conservation, thereby deteriorating their stability. The Crank Nicolson algorithm, which offers unitary evolution in Euclidian spaces independent of time-step size d ​ t \mathrm{d}t , can be generalised to curved manifolds in different ways. Here we compare a previously proposed algorithm that is unitary by construction, albeit integrating the wrong equation, with a faithful generalisation of the algorithm, which is, however, not strictly unitary for finite d ​ t \mathrm{d}t .



I
Introduction

Algorithms for the efficient numerical integration of the time-dependent
Schrödinger equation in discretised real time for finite representations
have been discussed at some length in the literature (see e.g.
Refs
Kosloff1991
;
Castro2004
;
Correa
;
Varga2017
), normally
in the context of the single-particle states in time-dependent Hartree-Fock
or time-dependent density-functional theory (TD-DFT)
RungeGross
.
The consideration of evolving basis sets complicates matters, and
there is less knowledge accumulated on good integrators for them and for
arbitrarily quick basis evolution
Sankey
;
Andermatt2016
;
Kaxiras2015
;
Artacho2017
;
Hekele2020
.
Evolving basis sets are routinely encountered in electronic structure
calculations for which
atom-centered basis functions are used, and where atoms move, that is,
any first-principles dynamical calculation method in quantum chemistry or
condensed matter and materials physics using atomic orbitals as
basis sets.
There are many such software packages that are widely used in
either or both communities.
For a brief review and links to codes used in
quantum chemistry see, e.g., Ref.
Handbook2017
; for
methods and programs using atomic orbitals in condensed matter
see, e.g., Refs.
Garcia2020
;
Kuhne2020
;
Oliveira2020
;
Dovesi2020
;
QuantumATK2019
;
Blum2009
;
Delley2000
.

The equation for the evolution of quantum states for a moving basis is
easily obtained.
For a basis set
\{|e_{\mu},t\rangle,\,\mu=1\dots\mathcal{N}\}
,

H|\psi_{n}\rangle=i\hbar\partial_{t}|\psi_{n}\rangle
straightforwardly becomes

\sum_{\nu}^{\mathcal{N}}H_{\mu\nu}\psi^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}n}=i\hbar\sum_{\nu}^{\mathcal{N}}(S_{\mu\nu}\partial_{t}\psi^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}n}+D_{\mu\nu t}\psi^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}n})\,,

(1)

with
H_{\mu\nu}=\langle e_{\mu}|H|e_{\nu}\rangle
,

S_{\mu\nu}=\langle e_{\mu}|e_{\nu}\rangle
,

D_{\mu\nu t}=\langle e_{\mu}|\partial_{t}|e_{\nu}\rangle
, and

\psi^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}n}
the coefficients in the expansion

|\psi_{n}\rangle=\sum_{\nu}^{\mathcal{N}}|e_{\nu}\rangle\psi^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}n}\,.

(2)

It is known that the evolution of a set of states following this equation
is unitary in the sense that it
preserves their mutual scalar products (see e.g.
Todorov2001
).
Therefore, if the evolving states are, for instance, the occupied Kohn-Sham
states in TD-DFT evolution, they preserve their orthonormality, and the
number of particles is conserved.

It is not obvious, however, how to guarantee such unitarity for
approximate algorithms based on time discretization.
Notice that the
D_{\mu\nu t}
matrix does not need to be
anti-hermitian if the evolution of basis vectors
|e_{\mu}\rangle

and
|e_{\nu}\rangle
is arbitrary (think e.g. of one of them not evolving
while the other does), and therefore the usual thinking in terms of
unitary matrices from the exponential of hermitian matrices does
not apply, at least directly.
In this work we focus on how the unitarity of the time evolution
is affected by the discretization of time for numerical integration.

The semiclassical description of atomic collisions has made use of
travelling orbitals
Errea1979
, defined as

\langle\mathbf{r}|e_{\mu},t\rangle=e^{im_{e}\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{r}/\hbar}f_{\mu}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{v}t)\,,

where
f_{\mu}(\mathbf{r})=R(r)Y_{l}^{m}(\theta,\phi)
,
is a time-independent atomic-like orbital, and
\mathbf{v}
is the velocity
at which it is travelling, normally attached to a nucleus.
They are very well adapted to the situation in which the electrons
themselves travel with the atoms and, therefore, with the basis
states, which is frequently the case in atomic collisions
Errea1979
.
They are not so useful beyond that realm, as in e.g. atoms
moving in metals, where the electrons are pushed around by a
moving nucleus, but do not necessarily accompany it.

A more general procedure based on a Löwdin orthonormalization
was proposed by Tomfohr and Sankey (TS hernceforth) in
Ref.
Sankey
, which was built strictly to preserve unitarity of
evolution for finite time steps for any evolving basis.
It has been quite successfully used for projectiles traversing solids at
non-adiabatic but relatively low velocity
Zeb2012
;
Zeb2013
;
Ullah2015
;
Halliday2019
;
Gu2020
.
The method was further discussed in Ref.
Artacho2017
, where it was
shown to affect the equation being integrated, with the potential to
lead to inaccurate propagation for high velocity, even in the converged
low time-step limit.

Finally, a different way of approaching the integration of Eq. (
1
)
is by rewriting it as

\sum_{\nu}^{\mathcal{N}}(H_{\mu\nu}-i\hbar D_{\mu\nu t})\psi^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}n}=i\hbar\sum_{\nu}^{\mathcal{N}}S_{\mu\nu}\partial_{t}\psi^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}n}\,,

and taking
H_{\mu\nu}-i\hbar D_{\mu\nu t}
as
a modified Hamiltonian matrix (see e.g. Ref.
Andermatt2016
).
The behavior for finite time step of this pragmatic approach is not
easy to discern from general considerations, since the
D_{\mu\nu t}
matrix
is not expected to be anti-hermitian, as mentioned above.
It does work reasonably well, however
Andermatt2016
.
Here we explore it further, both formally, and by explicitly comparing its
time-step convergence with the very stable TS integrator
Sankey
,
while the accuracy of the latter is
further scrutinized.

For a better understanding of the evolution we use in the following the
recent geometric interpretation
Artacho2017
of Eq. (
1
).
The same paper proposed a strictly unitary integrator for an evolving basis,
as long as the spanned Hilbert space
\Omega
were invariant at all times,
which is approximately the case for a well converged basis set.
Here we explore the situation for an evolving Hilbert space
\Omega(t)
,
defining a curved fibre bundle
Artacho2017
,
for the general situation in which its curvature cannot be neglected.

Ref.
Artacho2017
identifies the
D_{\mu\nu t}
matrix as a
connection in differential geometry.
The integration procedure of Ref.
Andermatt2016
can then be
interpreted in this context as using the connection as a gauge potential
in the Hamiltonian
Artacho2017
.
We will hence refer to this procedure as the gauge-potential (GP) integrator.

II
Unitarity in the equations

Following Ref.
Artacho2017
, the expansion in Eq. (
2
) of any
quantum state in a non-orthogonal and evolving basis set,

\{|e_{\mu},t\rangle,\,\mu=1\dots\mathcal{N}\}
, defines an
evolving
\mathcal{N}
-dimensional Hilbert space
\Omega(t)
, which, in turn,
defines a
(\mathcal{N}+1)
-dimensional fibre bundle
\Xi
, which
represents a non-Euclidian manifold.
In its natural representation
Artacho1991
, and summing over repeated indices,
Eq. (
1
) becomes
Artacho2017

H^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}\nu}\psi^{\nu}_{\phantom{i}n}=i\hbar\,\text{\dh}_{t}\psi^{\mu}_{\phantom{i}n}\;,

(3)

with

\psi^{\mu}_{\phantom{i}n}=\langle e^{\mu}|\psi_{n}\rangle\quad\mathrm{and}\quad H^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}\nu}=\langle e^{\mu}|H|e_{\nu}\rangle\,.

The set
\{|e^{\mu},t\rangle,\,\mu=1\dots\mathcal{N}\}
is the
dual basis of
\{|e_{\mu},t\rangle\}
, also a basis of
\Omega(t)
,
satisfying

\langle e^{\mu},t|e_{\nu},t\rangle=\delta^{\mu}_{\phantom{i}\nu},\,\forall\mu,\nu

at any time
t
.

\text{\dh}_{t}
represents the covariant time derivative
Artacho2017
defined as

\text{\dh}_{t}\psi^{\mu}_{\phantom{i}n}=\partial_{t}\psi^{\mu}_{\phantom{i}n}+D^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}\nu t}\psi^{\nu}_{\phantom{i}n}\;,

where
D^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}\nu t}=\langle e^{\mu}|\partial_{t}e_{\nu}\rangle

gives the connection in the manifold (note the convention in the order of indices).

Similarly, a bra evolves following

\langle\psi_{m}|H=-i\hbar\partial_{t}\langle\psi_{m}|\,,

where we have made use of the hermiticity of the Hamiltonian operator.
It is represented by
Artacho2017

\psi_{m\nu}H^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}\mu}=-i\hbar\,\text{\dh}_{t}\psi_{m\mu}=-i\hbar(\partial_{t}\psi_{m\mu}+\psi_{m\nu}D^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}t\mu})\;,

(4)

with
\psi_{m\nu}=\langle\psi_{m}|e_{\nu}\rangle
, and

D^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}t\mu}=\langle\partial_{t}e^{\nu}|e_{\mu}\rangle=-D^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}\mu t}\;,

(5)

the latter equality being due to

\partial_{t}\langle e^{\nu}|e_{\mu}\rangle=0
.
Eq. (
5
) is the key for the unitarity of
the propagation, replacing the conventional expectation of
hermiticity of
iD_{\mu\nu t}
.

II.1
Conservation of scalar products

We start by showing the expected
Todorov2001
unitarity of the
evolution in the manifold, defined here as conservation of scalar products

\partial_{t}\langle\psi_{n}|\psi_{m}\rangle=0\,,\quad\forall\,m,n

(6)

among the propagating
states
\{|\psi_{m}\rangle,m=1\dots N_{e}\}
at any time.
The evolution of the coefficients for the ket and bra then is determined by
Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
), as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}m}=-(\frac{i}{\hbar}\,H^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}\nu}+D^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}\nu t})\,\psi^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}m}

(7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{m\mu}=\psi_{m\nu}(\frac{i}{\hbar}\,H^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}\mu}+D^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}\mu t})\;.

(8)

It is easy to check that the scalar products

\langle\psi_{m}|\psi_{n}\rangle=\psi_{m\mu}\psi^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}n}

are preserved in time:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle\psi_{m}|\psi_{n}\rangle=\partial_{t}(\psi_{m\mu}\,\psi^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}n})=(\partial_{t}\psi_{m\mu})\psi^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}n}+\psi_{m\mu}(\partial_{t}\psi^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}n})

\displaystyle=\psi_{m\nu}(\frac{i}{\hbar}H^{\nu}_{\phantom{i}\mu}+D^{\nu}_{\phantom{i}\mu t})\psi^{\mu}_{\phantom{i}n}-\psi_{m\mu}(\frac{i}{\hbar}H^{\mu}_{\phantom{i}\nu}+D^{\mu}_{\phantom{i}\nu t})\psi^{\nu}_{\phantom{i}n}=0\,.

In addition to the Hamiltonian operator being hermitian, the unitarity of the
propagation is therefore direct consequence of Eq. (
5
).
The natural representation does not recover the usual self-adjoint matrix shape,
but offers quite transparent relations and derivations.
If the dealings above seem a bit of a sleight of hand, Appendix
A

reproduces the result in the matrix representation.

III
Finite time step
\mathrm{d}t

After time discretisation for numerical integration, we are interested
in propagating the set of coefficients

\psi^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}m}(t)\rightarrow\psi^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}m}(t+\mathrm{d}t)

for finite d
t
, trying to maximise both the quality and the stability of
whatever the algorithm we use.
Preservation of the orthonormality of the propagating states
is key for that purpose.
Here we will explore the behaviour of the Crank Nicolson algorithm
in the
\Xi
curved manifold.

For the finite time-step d
t
we will neglect henceforth the
time evolution of both the hamiltonian and the connection between

t
and
t+\mathrm{d}t
.
This is compatible with various integration algorithms
such as extrapolating the Hamiltonian to
t+\mathrm{d}t/2
.
Other algorithms, such as the self-consistent Crank Nicolson
Kaxiras2008

or self-consistent predictor-corrector schemes
Bao2015

may need further consideration.

Unitary propagation for static basis

Let us start with a reminder of unitary propagation when

and, consequently, the state manifold is a regular Hilbert space

.
It means that

,

(9)

Scalar products among

\displaystyle|\psi_{n}(t+\mathrm{d}t)\rangle=

III.2
Constant connection

For a situation in which both the basis and the (tangent)
Hilbert space
\Omega(t)
do change with time, we
consider now the case in which
\mathrm{d}t
is finite but small
enough so that the connection
D^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}\nu t}

can be taken as constant (the situation for varying basis set
but within an invariant or converged

in Ref.

).

III.2.1
Metric tensor evolution under constant connection

First, let us see how the metric tensors evolve between

t
and
t+\mathrm{d}t
.
In general, and still exact,

\partial_{t}S_{\mu\nu}=D_{\mu t\nu}+D_{\mu\nu t}=D_{\mu t}^{\phantom{m}\sigma}S_{\sigma\nu}+S_{\mu\lambda}D^{\lambda}_{\phantom{e}\nu t}\,.

(12)

If both
D^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}\nu t}
and
D_{\mu t}^{\phantom{m}\nu}

are taken as constant, given the premise of this Section, and
given the fact that
D_{\mu t}^{\phantom{m}\nu}=D^{\nu\phantom{m}*}_{\phantom{e}\mu t}
,
the solution of Eq. (
12
) is

S_{\mu\nu}(t+\mathrm{d}t)=e^{\mathrm{d}tD_{\mu t}^{\phantom{m}\sigma}}S_{\sigma\lambda}(t)e^{\mathrm{d}tD^{\lambda}_{\phantom{e}\nu t}}\;,

(13)

which will be exact as long as those connections
are strictly constant, but will represent an approximate
solution for small
\mathrm{d}t
but varying connections.
Analogously,

\partial_{t}S^{\mu\nu}=D^{\mu\phantom{i}\nu}_{\phantom{e}t}+D^{\mu\nu}_{\phantom{m}t}=D^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}t\sigma}S^{\sigma\nu}+S^{\mu\lambda}D_{\lambda\phantom{e}t}^{\phantom{e}\nu}\,,

gives

S^{\mu\nu}(t+\mathrm{d}t)=e^{\mathrm{d}tD^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}t\sigma}}\,S^{\sigma\lambda}(t)\,e^{\mathrm{d}tD_{\lambda\phantom{e}t}^{\phantom{e}\nu}}\;,

(14)

since both
D^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}t\sigma}
and
D_{\lambda\phantom{e}t}^{\phantom{e}\nu}

are constant again.

III.2.2
Calculation of the connection

The results in Eq. (
13
) and
Eq. (
14
) are important, not only
for further algebraic manipulations, but because they represent
consistency conditions for the evolution, and, to some extent,
they define the connection.
In explicit calculations, the overlap matrix is defined extrinsically
at any time step, i.e., it does not arise from evolution, but
given the positions of atoms at a given time step and given
the basis set definition in the larger ambient Hilbert space, as

S_{\mu\nu}(t)=\int\!\phi^{*}_{\mu}(\mathbf{r},t)\phi_{\nu}(\mathbf{r},t)\,\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}

(15)

where, typically,

\phi_{\mu}(\mathbf{r},t)=\langle\mathbf{r}|e_{\mu},t\rangle=\phi_{\mu}[\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}_{\mu}(t)]
, and where
\mathbf{R}_{\mu}(t)

represents the position of the centre of the orbital at that time.

The connection itself can be computed extrinsically, normally as

D^{\mu}_{\phantom{i}\nu t}=S^{\mu\sigma}D_{\sigma\nu t}

and

D_{\sigma\nu t}=\int\!\phi^{*}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r},t)\,\partial_{t}\phi_{\nu}(\mathbf{r},t)\,\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}\;.

(16)

There are therefore two possibilities for an approximate evolution for
finite
\mathrm{d}t
. (
i
) The connection can be calculated as in Eq. (
16
)
neglecting the small discrepancy in the evolved overlap
S_{\mu\nu}(t+\mathrm{d}t)

between the actual one, as in Eq. (
15
), and the one
that would result from evolving under the calculated connection, as in
Eq. (
13
).
Alternatively, (
ii
) a connection can be proposed, instead of via
Eq. (
16
), by construction to satisfy Eq. (
13
)
from the overlaps calculated using Eq. (
15
) both
at
t
and
t+\mathrm{d}t
.

In this work we test option
(i)
, since it is the most attractive numerically.
The calculation of the connection via Eq. (
16
) can be
done in linear-scaling operations.
We have implemented it in the
Siesta
program

Soler2002
;
Artacho2008
;
Garcia2020
,
which uses finite-support atomic orbitals as basis sets, and
contains a real-time TD-DFT implementation
Tsolakidis2002
.
The integrals in Eq. (
16
) represent two-centre integrals
which are evaluated by a trivial extension of the method explained in
Section 5 of Ref.
Soler2002
.
The implemented connection is tested below.
Option (
ii
) is further explored in Appendix
D
, where a possible
alternative direction towards better orthonormality preservation is outlined,
albeit with worse scaling in computational expense as far as we can see.

III.3.4
Orthonormalisation correction

Of course, the Crank-Nicolson propagation step of GP, in
Eq. (
19
), can be made strictly unitary by force
if adding a Löwdin orthonormalisation step using

\mathcal{S}^{-1/2}(t+\mathrm{d}t)
as obtained from the
diagonalization of

\mathcal{S}_{mn}(t+\mathrm{d}t)=\langle\psi_{m}(t+\mathrm{d}t)|\psi_{n}(t+\mathrm{d}t)\rangle\,.

Notice that the
\mathcal{S}
matrix is of
N\times N
dimensions,

N
being the number of occupied propagating states, much
smaller than the number of basis states.

In practical terms, and given the satisfactory unitarity
achieved directly by Eq. (
19
) for small
\mathrm{d}t
,
one can choose to evolve using the uncorrected algorithm for
some time, then evaluating the
\mathcal{S}
matrix once
every number steps
n_{s}
, and, whenever

\max_{mn}\{|\mathcal{S}_{mn}-\delta_{mn}|\}
exceeds
some tolerance
\epsilon
, an orthonormalisation
step would be performed as described.
The algorithm will be then optimised by choosing the best
combination of
\mathrm{d}t
,
n_{s}
and
\epsilon
, which will
depend on the system under study.

We have shown above how different problems have different
demands for convergence, depending, for instance, on
their evolution being dominated by the basis motion and
the connection, or by the Hamiltonian itself, either the
evolution of the external potential, or the Hamiltonian
effective spectral width for the evolving states.
The interplay of those parameters can therefore vary
quite substantially.
The method would be nicely completed with a learning
algorithm to adjust those parameters dynamically.

IV
Conclusions

The effect of the time evolution of the basis set and of the Hilbert space
it spans is explored for the description of the time evolution of
quantum states.
The exploration is both formal and numerical, assessing the deterioration
of the conservation of scalar products of evolving states (unitarity of
the evolution) and its implication for convergence with time step in the
integration of the dynamical equation by time discretisation.

Formally, a Crank-Nicolson algorithm using the connection of
the manifold as gauge potential (GP) is shown to keep unitarity only
approximately, unlike the integrator proposed in Ref.
Artacho2017

for a moving basis within a static Hilbert space, and unlike the TS
algorithm
Sankey
, which is perfectly unitary regardless of
\mathrm{d}t

size by construction and works well at low velocities

Zeb2012
;
Zeb2013
;
Ullah2015
;
Halliday2019
;
Gu2020
, but was
suggested to describe unphysical evolution at higher velocities.

The most numerically convenient GP integrator
[that of Eq. (
19
)]
is tried on two systems, a collision between two He atoms and
the passing of a constant-velocity H projectile across graphite.
The unitarity and
\mathrm{d}t
convergence for that algorithm is compared
with the TS one, the latter displaying the expected better
convergence with
\mathrm{d}t
, requiring from two to ten times less time steps
for a given simulation time.
That advantage is however offset by the more efficient (and better
basis-size scaling) GP algorithm of Eq. (
19
),
which only demands, per time step, the calculation of a sparse matrix of
two-centre integrals (in linear-scaling operations) instead of the overlap matrix
diagonalization of TS.

The deviation from the physical evolution of the TS
integration is confirmed for nuclear (and basis function) velocities
comparable to or larger than the valence electron velocities.
A very significant overestimation of the Bragg peak (a factor of two
in both height and position) is observed for the electronic stopping power of
the proton shooting through graphite along the centre of a (0001)
channel.
And a very clear modification of the expected physics is observed for
the same integrator when describing the two-atom collision.

Although other routes for unitary integrators are proposed in this
work, only the approximately unitary GP algorithm of
Eq. (
19
) is found to be satisfactory.
The work also provides a better perspective in the understanding
of unitary evolution of quantum states with evolving basis sets, in the
context of curved manifolds.

Appendix C
Parallel transport

C.1
Evolution

The dynamics expressed in Eqs. (
7
) and (
8
)
is in some sense counterintuitive, given the fact that neither

D^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}\nu t}

D_{\mu\nu t}
are

give unitary propagation.
The parallel transport case can be illustrative,
i.e., the evolution of states for zero covariant derivative.
In an Euclidean space the states would not change in time, but
they do in a curved manifold.
Because of the Schrödinger equation, zero covariant derivative
implies
H^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}\nu}(t)=0
.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}m}

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}\psi_{m\nu}D^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}\mu t}\;.

(24)

For constant
D^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}\nu t}

the coefficients for the ket and the bra evolve as given by
the solution of Eq.
C.1
, namely,

\displaystyle\psi^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}m}(t+\mathrm{d}t)

\displaystyle=e^{-\mathrm{d}tD^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}\nu t}}\,\psi^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}m}(t)

\displaystyle=\psi_{m\nu}(t)\,e^{\mathrm{d}tD^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}\mu t}}\;.

In the

Appendix E
Evolved bra for constant connection

A valid question after Section
III.3.1

is whether the evolving bra in Eq. (
18
)
corresponds to the instantaneous bra of the evolving ket in
Eq. (
17
).
The instantaneous bra is, turning Eq. (
17
) around,

\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\mu}(t+\mathrm{d}t)=\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\nu}(t)\,e^{\mathrm{d}t(\frac{i}{\hbar}\,H_{\nu}^{\phantom{e}\mu}-D_{\nu t}^{\phantom{m}\mu})}\;,

(40)

which is just the complex conjugate of the coefficients for the ket in
Eq. (
17
).
It is also the solution of the equation of motion given by

\partial_{t}\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\mu}=\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\nu}(\frac{i}{\hbar}\,H_{\nu}^{\phantom{e}\mu}-D_{\nu t}^{\phantom{m}\mu})\;,

(41)

given that both
H_{\nu}^{\phantom{e}\mu}
and
D_{\nu t}^{\phantom{m}\mu}

are time-independent if
H^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}\nu}
and
D^{\mu}_{\phantom{e}\nu t}

are, as assumed before.
This equation is derived from
\langle\psi_{m}|H=-i\hbar\partial_{t}\langle\psi_{m}|
,

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}\langle\psi_{m}|\right)|e^{\mu}\rangle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle\psi_{m}|H|e^{\mu}\rangle

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\mu}-\langle\psi_{m}|\partial_{t}e^{\mu}\rangle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\nu}H_{\nu}^{\phantom{e}\mu}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\mu}-\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\nu}D_{\nu\phantom{e}t}^{\phantom{e}\mu}=\frac{i}{\hbar}\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\nu}H_{\nu}^{\phantom{e}\mu}\;,

which, remembering that
D_{\nu\phantom{e}t}^{\phantom{e}\mu}=-D_{\nu t}^{\phantom{m}\mu}
,
is nothing but Eq. (
41
), or, canonically,

-i\hbar\,\text{\dh}_{t}\psi_{m}^{\phantom{e}\mu}=\psi_{m}^{\phantom{e}\nu}H_{\nu}^{\phantom{e}\mu}

Similarly, Eqs. (
8
) and (
41
)
can be easily shown to be equivalent:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{m\mu}

\displaystyle=\psi_{m\nu}(\frac{i}{\hbar}\,H^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}\mu}+D^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}\mu t})

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\sigma}S_{\sigma\mu})

\displaystyle=\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\lambda}S_{\lambda\nu}(\frac{i}{\hbar}\,H^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}\mu}+D^{\nu}_{\phantom{e}\mu t})

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\sigma})S_{\sigma\mu}

\displaystyle=\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\nu}(\frac{i}{\hbar}\,H_{\nu\mu}+D_{\nu\mu t})-\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\sigma}\partial_{t}S_{\sigma\mu}

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\sigma})S_{\sigma\mu}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{m}^{\phantom{n}\mu}

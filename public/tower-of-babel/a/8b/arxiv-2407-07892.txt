Quasinormal modes and gray-body factors of regular black holes in asymptotically safe gravity
Oleksandr Stashko

Abstract
Recently, Bonanno et al. (2024) presented an explicit metric describing the exterior of a collapsing dust ball within the framework of asymptotically safe gravity. Based on this metric, we investigate in detail the properties of the quasinormal mode (QNM) spectra for test massless scalar, vector, and Dirac fields. We find accurate values for the fundamental QNM frequency and the first overtones, demonstrating the appearance of a peculiar spiral-like behavior of the overtones compared to the fundamental modes. Additionally, we study black hole shadows and show that they align with EHT observations. Finally, we study the grey-body factors and provide explicit lower bound estimates.

We are going to study the behavior of massless test scalar (
\Phi

A_{\mu}

5

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\Phi\right)

\displaystyle=0,

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}\left(F_{\rho\sigma}g^{\rho\nu}g^{\sigma\mu}\sqrt{-g}\right)

\displaystyle=0\,,

(10)

\displaystyle\gamma^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\alpha}}-\Gamma_{\alpha}\right)\Upsilon

\displaystyle=0,

(11)

where
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
is the vector field tensor;
\gamma^{\alpha}
and
\Gamma_{\alpha}
are noncommutative gamma matrices and spin connections in the tetrad formalism
[
75
]

Equations (
9
), (
10

\Psi

[
76
,
77
]

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Psi}{\partial t^{2}}-\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Psi}{\partial r^{*}{}^{2}}+V^{(i)}_{\rm eff}(r)\Psi=0,

(12)

where
r=r(r^{*})
, is the “tortoise coordinate” defined as

dr^{*}={dr}/f(r)
.

In the case of the massless scalar field (
i=s
) the effective potential is

\displaystyle V^{(s)}_{\rm eff}(r)=f(r)\left(\frac{l(l+1)}{r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r}\frac{df(r)}{dr}\right),

(13)

For the massless vector field (
i=v
), the effective potential is

\displaystyle V^{(v)}_{\rm eff}(r)=f(r)\left(\frac{l(l+1)}{r^{2}}\right),

(14)

And for the massless Dirac field (

\displaystyle V^{(d,\pm)}_{\rm eff}(r)=f(r)\frac{l+1}{r}\left(\frac{l+1}{r}\mp\sqrt{f(r)}\pm\frac{d}{dr}\sqrt{f(r)}\right)\,,

(15)

IV
Quasinormal modes

IV.1
Chebyshev pseudo-spectral method

Quasinormal frequencies are the eigenvalues
\omega
of the master equation (
12
), which correspond to in-going waves at the black hole horizon and outgoing waves at spatial infinity. They satisfy the following boundary conditions

\displaystyle\Psi\sim e^{-i\omega(r^{*}+t)},\penalty\ r\to r_{\rm h},\,\,\,\Psi\sim e^{i\omega(r^{*}-t)},\penalty\ r\to\infty.

(16)

In order to solve the corresponding eigenproblem, we use the Chebyshev pseudo-spectral method
[
79
]
.
For numerical purposes, it is convenient to rewrite (
12
) using the ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates
(v=t+r^{*},r)

[
80
]
.
After separation of variable
v
in the form
\Psi\sim\exp(-i\omega v)\Psi(r)
, the master equation (
12
) in these new coordinates takes the form

\displaystyle f(r)\frac{d^{2}\Psi}{dr^{2}}+\left(\frac{df(r)}{dr}-2i\omega\right)\frac{d\Psi}{dr}-\frac{V^{(i)}_{\rm eff}(r)}{f(r)}\Psi=0,

(17)

with boundary conditions

\displaystyle\Psi\sim const,\penalty\ r\to r_{\rm h},\quad\Psi\sim e^{2i\omega r^{*}},\penalty\ r\to\infty\,.

(18)

correspondingly at the black hole horizon and at spatial infinity.

Pseudospectral methods usually work on a finite interval; that is why we compactify our semi-interval
[r_{h},\infty)
to
[0,1]
by introducing a new variable
3
3

3

In the case of the Dirac field, it is more convenient to use the following compactification:
r=r_{h}/(1-u^{2})
.

\displaystyle r=\frac{r_{h}}{1-u},

(19)

As we use the Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates, we have
\Psi\sim const
at the horizon, and we only need to separate the correct asymptotic solution at spatial infinity. We substitute

\displaystyle\Psi(r)=(1-u)^{-4i\omega M}e^{\frac{2ir_{h}\omega}{1-u}}\Phi(r),

(20)

which gives

\displaystyle\Phi\sim const,\penalty\ u\to{0},\quad\Phi\sim const,\penalty\ u\to{1}.

(21)

The final equation might suffer from numerical singularities at the boundaries
u=0
and
u=1
. To avoid them, we can make an additional substitution
\Phi(u)=u(1-u)\psi(u)
, and our boundary conditions can now be written as follows

\displaystyle\psi(0)=0,\quad\psi(1)=0.

(22)

All previous substitutions lead to an equation of the form

\displaystyle A_{2}(u)\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)+A_{1}(u)\psi^{\prime}(u)+A_{0}(u)\psi(u)=0,

(23)

where
A_{i}(u)=A_{i}(u,\omega,\omega^{2})
,
i=0,1,2
. At the next step, we discretize Eq. (
23
) on the Chebyshev-Lobatto grid, which is defined as

\displaystyle u_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\cos\left[\frac{\pi j}{N}\right]\right),\penalty\ \penalty\ j=0,1...N.

(24)

This results in the matrix equation

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{M}_{0}+\tilde{M}_{1}\omega+\tilde{M}_{2}\,\omega^{2}\right)\tilde{\psi}=0,

(25)

where
\tilde{\psi}=[\psi(u_{0}),...\psi(u_{N})]^{T}
is the vector of function values at grid points and
\tilde{M}_{i}
are matrices of discretized coefficients.
Following
[
80
]
we rewrite (
25
) to obtain a linear form of the eigenproblem

\displaystyle\left(M_{0}+M_{1}\omega\right)\tilde{\psi}_{1}=\mathbb{0},

(26)

where

M_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{M}_{0}&\tilde{M}_{1}\\
\mathbb{0}&\mathbb{1}\end{pmatrix},\,M_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{0}&\tilde{M}_{2}\\
-\mathbb{1}&\mathbb{0}\end{pmatrix},\,\tilde{\psi}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\psi}\\
\omega\tilde{\psi}\end{pmatrix}.

(27)

Then corresponding QNM frequencies can be
determined by solving generalized eigenvalue problem (
26
). The resulting set of the eigenvalues also contains spurious eigenvalues
[
79
]
. To eliminate them we perform the calculations on two grids of different sizes and select only overlapping values. The typical grid sizes are
N=100-500
, which is sufficient to obtain good accuracy and the first few overtones. The presence of square roots in the effective potential for the Dirac field case might trigger some numerical instabilities, which affect the convergence of the pseudospectral methods and require higher precision and grid resolution to obtain high overtone values.

Black hole shadows and eikonal approximation

In this section, we investigate the properties of null geodesics and the eikonal approximation for quasinormal modes. We begin with the Lagrangian for geodesics, given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\equiv\dfrac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu},

(28)

where
\dot{x}^{\mu}=dx^{\mu}/d\tau
and
\tau

Without loss of generality, we can restrict our consideration to geodesic motion in the equatorial plane. The metric (
5

\displaystyle f(r)\dot{t}=E,\quad r^{2}\dot{\phi}=L,

(29)

g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu}=0

\displaystyle(\dot{r})^{2}=E^{2}-U_{\rm eff}(r),\quad U_{\rm eff}(r)=f(r)\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}},

(30)

denotes the effective radial potential of the geodesic motion. We are interested in photon circular orbits, which satisfy the conditions

\displaystyle U_{\rm eff}(r)=E^{2},\quad U_{\rm eff}^{\prime}(r)=0,

(31)

\displaystyle rf^{\prime}(r)-2f(r)=0.

Substituting the metric function (

\displaystyle r_{\rm ph}/M=1+2\sin\left[\frac{1}{3}\arccos\left(\frac{3\xi}{M^{2}}-1\right)+\frac{\pi}{6}\right],

VI
Scattering problem and grey-body factors

The black hole grey-body factors modify the spectrum of Hawking radiation observed at spatial infinity by a distant observer, determining the proportion of initial quantum radiation reflected back to the event horizon by the potential barrier.

The scattering boundary conditions have the following form

\displaystyle\Psi\sim e^{-i\omega r_{*}}+Re^{i\omega r_{*}},\,r\to{r_{\rm h}},\,\,\Psi\sim Te^{-i\omega r_{*}},\,r\to{\infty}

(42)

where
\omega
is real, and
R
and
T
correspond to the reflection and transmission coefficients, respectively. They satisfy

\left|T\right|^{2}+\left|R\right|^{2}=1.

(43)

The black hole grey-body factor
A_{l}
is defined as the transmission coefficient

A_{l}\equiv\left|T\right|^{2}=1-\left|R\right|^{2}.

(44)

The transmission for each multipole
l
can be calculated using the WKB approach

|R|^{2}=\frac{1}{1+e^{-2i\pi K}},\quad|T|^{2}=\frac{1}{1+e^{2i\pi K}}.

(45)

The phase factor
K
is determined using equation
[
94
]

\displaystyle K=\frac{i\left(\omega^{2}-V^{(i)}_{\rm eff}(r_{m})\right)}{\sqrt{-2\partial^{2}_{(r^{*})^{2}}V^{(i)}_{\rm eff}(r_{m})}}+\sum_{i=2}^{6}\Lambda_{i}(K),

(46)

where
r_{m}
is the point of the maximum of the effective potential
V^{(i)}_{\rm eff}
and
\Lambda_{i}(K)
corresponds to higher-order WKB corrections
[
94
,
95
]
at
r=r_{m}
.

Typical examples of grey-body factors as a function of
\omega
are shown in Fig.
7
. One can observe that grey-body factors decrease with increasing
\xi
, which means that a larger fraction of particles is reflected by the effective potential.

\Xi(r)={\sqrt{(h^{\prime})^{2}+[\omega^{2}-V^{(i)}_{\rm eff}(r)-h^{2}]^{2}}\over 2h}.

z(\xi)
has the following form

\begin{split}&z(\xi)=\frac{\sqrt{3}M\pi}{a^{2}}+\frac{3}{2r_{h}}-\frac{6M}{r_{h}^{2}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{2}{3},1,\frac{5}{3},-\frac{6M\xi}{r_{h}^{3}}\right)\\
&-\frac{2M\sqrt{3}}{a^{2}}\arctan\left(\frac{2r_{h}-a}{\sqrt{3}a}\right)-\frac{M}{a^{2}}\log\left(\frac{(a+r_{h})^{2}}{(a-r_{h})^{2}+ar_{h}}\right),\end{split}

(51)

where
a=(6M\xi)^{1/3}
and
r_{h}=r_{h}(\xi)
. For small
\xi
, we have

\displaystyle z(\xi)=-\frac{\sqrt{3}\pi a}{128M^{2}}-\frac{3\sqrt{3}\pi a^{2}}{2048M^{3}}+O\left(a^{3}\right),

(52)

In the case of vector and Dirac fields, the corresponding expressions have a more compact form

\displaystyle A_{l}^{(v)}(\xi)\geq\sech^{2}\left[\frac{l(l+1)}{2\omega r_{h}(\xi)}\right],

(53)

\displaystyle A_{l}^{(d)}(\xi)\geq\sech^{2}\left[\frac{(l+1)^{2}}{2\omega r_{h}(\xi)}\right],

(54)

One can see that
r_{h}(\xi)
and
z(\xi)
are monotonically decreasing functions with
r_{h}(0)=2M
,
r_{h}(\xi_{\rm cr})=r_{h}^{\rm ext}<2M
and
z(0)=0
,
z(\xi_{\rm cr})<0
. Consequently,
A_{l}^{(s,v,d)}(\xi)\leq A_{l}^{(s,v,d)}(0)
and the lower bounds for grey-body factors are always smaller compared to those for the Schwarzschild black hole.

VII
Conclusions

In this paper, we provide a detailed consideration of the QNM spectra of massless scalar, vector, and Dirac fields for the regular black hole spacetime described by (
6
) which corresponds to the static exterior of a collapsing dust ball within the framework of asymptotically safe gravity
[
35
]
. We obtain accurate values for the fundamental mode and the first few overtones for each test field.

We find that the fundamental mode is weakly sensitive to the scale parameter
\xi
, but for the overtones we have the peculiar spiral-like behavior in the QNM trajectories in the complex
\omega
plane as a function of
\xi
.

Additionally, we study black hole shadow and demonstrate that shadow radius decreases with increasing
\xi
, and for
\xi\in(0,\xi_{\rm cr}]
it aligns with current EHT results. In the context of the eikonal approximation related to black hole shadows, we find that the real and imaginary parts of
\omega
are always larger and smaller, respectively, than in the Schwarzschild black hole case.

Furthermore, we study the grey-body factors and derive explicit lower bound estimations. We find that they decrease with increasing
\xi
and they are always smaller compared to those in the case of the Schwarzschild black hole.

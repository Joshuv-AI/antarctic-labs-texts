A note on subgroups whose quotients are quasi-lines
Pénélope Azuelos

Abstract
We study finitely generated pairs of groups H ≤ G H\leq G such that the Schreier graph of H H is quasi-isometric to a line. Under this hypothesis, we show that H H is a virtual fiber subgroup if and only if G G contains infinitely many double cosets of H H . Along the way, we prove that if a group acts essentially on a finite dimensional CAT(0) cube complex with no facing triples then it virtually surjects onto the integers with kernel commensurable to a hyperplane stabiliser.

Keywords:
Schreier graph, quasi-line, virtual fiber subgroup, separable subgroup, bounded packing, Houghton’s groups

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:

A well-known result of Hopf
[
Hop44
]
states that any finitely generated two-ended group is virtually cyclic. Analogous results exist for pairs of finitely generated groups
H\leq G
which are two-ended in appropriate ways, subject to
H
being “sufficiently normal”. The first such result is a theorem of Houghton stating that, if
H
has two relative ends and infinite index in its normaliser, then there exists a finite index subgroup
G^{\prime}\leq G
such that
H
is a normal subgroup of
G^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime}/H
is infinite cyclic
[
Hou74
, Theorem 3.7]
.

The number of relative ends of a subgroup
H\leq G
was introduced by Houghton in the same paper. It can be defined as follows. Fix a finite generating set
S
for
G
and let
\Cay(G,S)
be the Cayley graph of
G
with respect to
S
. Given a subspace
K\subseteq\Cay(G,S)
, we say that a connected component of
\Cay(G,S)-K
is
deep
if it contains points arbitrarily far from
K
. Given
R\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{N}_{R}(H)
denote the
R
-neighbourhood of
H
. The number of relative ends of
H
is the maximum over
R\geq 0
of the number of
H
-orbits of deep components of
\Cay(G,S)-\mathcal{N}_{R}(H)
. In contrast, the number of
filtered ends
of
H
is the maximum over
R\geq 0
of the number of deep components of
\Cay(G,S)-\mathcal{N}_{R}(H)
. This invariant was introduced by Kropholler and Roller in
[
KR89
]
; a more geometric perspective can be found in
[
Geo08
, Chapter 14]
.

If
H
has two filtered ends it might not have two relative ends but there is a subgroup of
H
of index at most 2 which does. This stronger assumption allows for the condition that
H
has infinite index in its normaliser to be weakened as follows. Two subgroups
H,L\leq G
are
commensurable
if
H\cap K
has finite index in both
H
and
K
. The
commensurator
of
H
is the subgroup

\Comm_{G}(H)\coloneqq\{g\in G:H\text{ and }gHg^{-1}\text{ are commensurable}\}\leq G.

Clearly
N(H)\leq\Comm_{G}(H)
, where
N(H)
denotes the normaliser of
H
. Kropholler and Roller showed that, if
H
has two filtered ends and infinite index in its commensurator, then there are finite index subgroup
G^{\prime}\leq G
and
H^{\prime}\leq H
such that
H^{\prime}
is a normal subgroup of
G^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime}/H^{\prime}
is infinite cyclic
[
KR89
, Theorem 1.3]
.

We strengthen the condition that
H
has two relative ends in a different way, assuming instead that the quotient
H\bs\Cay(G,S)
is quasi-isometric to a line. This allows us to weaken the condition on the normaliser to the assumption that there are infinitely many distinct double cosets of
H
.

We say that
H\leq G
:

– is a
virtual fiber subgroup
if it is finitely generated and there are finite index subgroups
G^{\prime}\leq G
and
H^{\prime}\leq H
such that
H^{\prime}\unlhd G^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime}/H^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}
;

– is
separable
if for every
g\in G-H
there is a finite index subgroup
K\leq G
such that
H\leq K
and
g\notin K
;

– has
bounded packing
if, having fixed a proper word metric
d_{S}
on
G
, for every
D\geq 0
there is a constant
R_{D}\geq 0
such that in every set
\mathcal{A}
of
R_{D}+1
left cosets of
H
there are
gH,g^{\prime}H\in\mathcal{A}
such that
d_{S}(gH,g^{\prime}H)>D
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group. Suppose that
H\leq G
is a finitely generated subgroup such that
H\bs G
is quasi-isometric to a line. Then the following are equivalent:

1.

H
is a virtual fiber subgroup.

2.

H
is separable.

3.

H
has bounded packing.

4.

There are infinitely many distinct double cosets of
H
.

Remark 1.2
.

The descending implications of the theorem all hold without the assumption that
H\bs G
is quasi-isometric to a line. The implications (1)
\Rightarrow
(2) and (3)
\Rightarrow
(4) are straightforward while (2)
\Rightarrow
(3) is due to Yang
[
Yan11
]
. In contrast, none of the ascending implications hold without some extra condition on
H
or on
G
(see Examples
1.3
,
1.4
,
1.5
below). Moreover, none of the properties (1)-(4) follow directly from the fact that
H\bs G
is quasi-isometric to a line. This is illustrated in Section
5
using a class of groups introduced by Houghton
[
Hou78
]
.

Example 1.3
.

If
G
is a hyperbolic surface group and
H\leq G
is cyclic then
H
is separable by
[
Sco78
]
but
H
is not a virtual fiber subgroup. Note that
H
also has two relative ends and two filtered ends.

Example 1.4
.

Burger and Mozes constructed in
[
BM00
]
a family of finitely presented simple groups which split as an amalgamated free product
F\ast_{E}F
, where
F
is a free group of finite rank and
E
is a finite index subgroup of both factors. Let
G=F\ast_{E}F
be such a group. Then, since
G
acts on a locally finite tree with
E
an edge stabiliser,
[
HW09
, Theorem 3.2]
implies that
E
has bounded packing in
G
. However, the fact that
G
is simple prevents it from having any proper finite index subgroups, so
E
cannot be separable.

Example 1.5
.

Let
G,H
be finitely generated groups such that
H\leq G
and
H
does not have bounded packing in
G
. Then
H\times\{0\}
does not have bounded packing in
G\times\mathbb{Z}
but
\{H\times\{n\}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
are pairwise distinct double cosets of
H
.

Theorem
1.1
is proved in Section
3
. We in fact prove a slightly more general statement (Theorem
3.1
) where we allow
H\bs G
to be quasi-isometric to a finitely ended tree with at least two ends.

Sardar showed that, if
G
is a nilpotent-by-polycyclic group, then every finitely generated subgroup of
G
has bounded packing
[
Sar15
, Theorem 3.7]
. We therefore have the following corollary of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated nilpotent-by-polycyclic group. If
H\leq G
is finitely generated then
H\bs G
is quasi-isometric to a line if and only if
H
is a virtual fiber subgroup.

Subgroups with quotient quasi-isometric to a line.

Part of the motivation for studying subgroups whose quotients are quasi-lines comes from previous work of the author with Damien Gaboriau
[
AG23
, Theorem 3.10]
where we showed that, if
H\leq G
is finitely generated and
H\bs G
is quasi-isometric to a line, then there are at most countably many intermediate subgroups
H\leq K\leq G
. In fact the result is more precise: the subgroup
H
vanishes by the third Cantor-Bendixson derivative of the space of subgroups of
G
. Even in the case where
H
is known to be a virtual fiber, it is the quasi-isometry to
\mathbb{Z}
which reveals this property of
H
.

Bounded packing.

The bounded packing property was introduced by Hruska and Wise in
[
HW09
]
. Examples of subgroups which satisfy this property include, on the one hand, subgroups which are normal (this was shown by Hruska and Wise) or commensurated
[
CM13
]
, and, on the other, quasiconvex subgroups of hyperbolic groups
[
Sag97
]
(see also
[
CDV17
, Theorem 2]
) and many of their generalisations (e.g. relatively quasiconvex subgroups of suitable relatively hyperbolic groups
[
HW09
]
, strongly quasiconvex subgroups of any finitely generated group
[
Tra19
]
, hierarchically quasiconvex subgroups of hierarchically hyperbolic groups
[
HHP23
]
). This list is highly non-exhaustive.

CAT(0) cube complexes.

The main content of Theorem
1.1
is the implication
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
and this is proved using an action of
G
on a finite dimensional CAT(0) cube complex. This cube complex is obtained using Sageev’s construction
[
Sag95
]
applied to the codimension one subgroup
H
and the fact that it is finite dimensional follows from the fact that
H
has bounded packing
[
HW09
]
. This will allow us to use techniques developed by Caprace and Sageev in
[
CS11
]
, some of which we recall in the next section.
We will also need the following proposition, which we prove in Section
4
.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group and suppose that
\Gamma
acts essentially on a finite dimensional CAT(0) cube complex
Y
which has no facing triples. Then for any hyperplane
\h
in
Y
, there are finite index subgroups
K\leq\Stab_{\Gamma}(\h)
and
\Lambda\leq\Gamma
such that
K\unlhd\Lambda
and
\Lambda\bs K=\mathbb{Z}
.

The fact that such a group virtually surjects onto
\mathbb{Z}
is not entirely new but the control we have over the kernel appears to be (see Remark
4.4
).

Cube complexes and facing triples

We assume some familiarity with actions on CAT(0) cube complexes; introductory material can be found in
[
CN05
,
HW14
,
Nic04
]
. Recall that in
[
Sag95
]
Sageev gave a construction which, given a pair of groups
H\leq G
such that
H
has at least two relative ends, produces a CAT(0) cube complex on which
G
acts with a single orbit of hyperplanes such that the stabiliser of some hyperplane is commensurable with
H
. As was pointed out by Haglund and Wise in
[
HW08
, Corollary 3.1]
, it was observed in
[
Sag97
]
that if
H
is finitely generated and has bounded packing then this construction yields a finite dimensional cube complex (see also
[
HW14
, Corollary 3.31]
).

We will work with the piecewise
\ell^{1}
metric on CAT(0) cube complexes (see
[
Mie14
]
) which we denote by
d
. Let
g
be a cubical automorphism of a CAT(0) cube complex
C
and recall that
g
is an isometry with respect to
d
and the CAT(0) metric (i.e. the piecewise
\ell^{2}
metric). A
combinatorial geodesic
in
C
is a geodesic path
\ell:[0,L]\rightarrow C^{(1)}
where
L\in\mathbb{N}
and
\ell(i)\in C^{(0)}
for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,L]
.
Haglund showed in
[
Hag23
, Theorem 6.3]
that
g
either fixes a point in
C
or admits a combinatorial geodesic axis in the
cubical subdivision

C^{\prime}
of
C
(see
[
Hag23
, Definition 2.3, Lemma 4.2]
).
In the later case,
g
is called
hyperbolic
and admits a (not necessarily unique or combinatorial)
geodesic axis in
C
(i.e. a bi-infinite geodesic on which
\la g\ra
acts by translation).

Let us recall some notions and results from
[
CS11
]
.
Let
C
be a finite dimensional cube complex and
G
be a group acting by cubical automorphisms on
C
.
The action is said to be
essential
if one (equivalently every)
G
-orbit contains points arbitrarily far from any halfspace of
G
. If
\h
is a hyperplane of
C
then we denote by
\h^{+},\h^{-}
the halfspaces bounded by
\h
. For any
g\in G
we write
g\h^{+}
(respectively
g\h^{-}
) to denote the image of
\h^{+}
(respectively
\h^{-}
) under
g
and
(g\h)^{+}
to denote an independent choice of halfspace bounded by
g\h
. An element
g\in G
is said to
skewer

\h
if there is some
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that either
g^{n}\h^{+}\subsetneq\h^{+}
or
g^{n}\h^{-}\subsetneq\h^{-}
. Caprace and Sageev show in
[
CS11
, Lemma 2.3]
that a element
g\in G
skewers a hyperplane
\h
if and only if
g
is hyperbolic and one (equivalently any) axis of
g
intersects
\h
at a single point. This statement refers to geodesic axes of
g
with respect to the CAT(0) metric but the same proof shows that the statement holds for
\ell^{1}
geodesic axes of
g
, including combinatorial axes when they exist. The following characterisation of essentiality will be useful.

Proposition 2.1

(Caprace-Sageev,
[
CS11
, Lemma 2.4, Proposition 3.2]
)
.

Let
C
be a finite dimensional CAT(0) cube complex and
G
be a group acting on
C
with a single orbit of hyperplanes. Then the following are equivalent.

•

C
is unbounded.

•

A hyperplane of
C
is skewered by some element of
G
.

•

Every hyperplane of
C
is skewered by some element of
G
.

•

The action of
G
on
C
is essential.

Suppose that
\h\subseteq C
is a hyperplane and
g\in G
is a hyperbolic isometry which does not skewer
\h
. If some (equivalently every) axis of
g
lies in a neighbourhood of
\h
then
g
is said to be
parallel
to
\h
. Otherwise
g
is
peripheral
to
\h
.

Lemma 2.2

(Caprace-Sageev,
[
CS11
, Lemma 4.4]
)
.

If
C
is a finite dimensional CAT(0) cube complex,
\h\subseteq C
is a hyperplane and
g
is a hyperbolic isometry of
C
which is peripheral to
\h
then there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
g^{n}\h\cap\h=\emptyset
.

A
facing triple
is a disjoint triple of hyperplanes
\{\h_{0},\h_{1},\h_{2}\}
such that, for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
,
\h_{i}
does not separate
\h_{i+1}
from
\h_{i+2}
. The classification of hyperbolic isometries is greatly simplified when
C
has no facing triples:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
C
be a finite dimensional CAT(0) cube complex and suppose that
C
has no facing triples. Let
g
be a hyperbolic isometry of
C
and
\h
be a hyperplane. Then either
g
skewers
\h
or there is some
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
g^{n}\h=\h
.

Proof.

First note that
C
is proper by
[
Hag22
, Corollary 3.9]
. In particular, by
[
CS11
, Lemma 4.3]
, the isometry
g
is parallel to
\h
if and only if there is a power of
g
which stabilises
\h
.
Moreover, it follows from Lemma
2.2
that, if
g
is peripheral to
\h
, then there is some
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\{\h,g^{n}\h,g^{2n}\h\}
is a facing triple. Thus if
g
does not skewer
\h
then it must be parallel to it.
∎

The following is an immediate consequence.

.

Let

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

Let

Let us fix some notation for the rest of this section. If
G
is a group with a finite generating set
S
, we denote the corresponding word metric on

by

and let
X\coloneqq\Cay(G,S)
be the Cayley graph of

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a group with finite generating set
S
and
H\leq G
be a finitely generated subgroup such that
X_{H}
is quasi-isometric to a tree with
2\leq e<\infty
ends.
Suppose that
K\leq G
is finitely generated. Then there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for any
g_{1},\dots,g_{N+1}\in G
, either

•

\rho_{H}(g_{i}K)=\rho_{H}(g_{j}K)
for some
i\neq j
or

•

\rho_{H}(g_{i}K)
is bounded for some
i
.

Proof.

First note that, for any
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
, if
\rho_{H}(g_{1}K)\cap\rho_{H}(g_{2}K)\neq\emptyset
then
\rho_{H}(g_{1}K)=\rho_{H}(g_{2}K)
.

Let
D\geq 0
be such that any ball of radius
D
separates

into at least two unbounded connected components. Fix a finite generating set
T
for
K
and suppose in addition that
D>|t|_{S}
for each
t\in T
, where
|.|_{S}
denotes the word length of an element with respect to

. Note that
\rho_{H}(gK)
is
D

g\in G
. Let
R\geq 1
be such that any ball of radius
D
in
X_{H}
contains at most
R

N\coloneqq eR
. Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{e}
denote the ends of
X_{H}
. Choose
g_{1},\dots,g_{N+1}\in G
such that each
\rho_{H}(g_{i}K)
is infinite and suppose that these elements are ordered such that the following holds. There exists
1=N_{0}\leq N_{1}\leq\dots\leq N_{e-1}\leq N_{e}=N+2
such that, for each
i\in\{1,\dots,e\}
, if
j\in[N_{i-1},N_{i})
, then there is a subset of
\rho_{H}(g_{j}K)
which converges towards

.
It follows that, for each
i\in\{1,\dots,e\}
, there is a ball

of radius

which intersects
\rho_{H}(g_{j}K)
for all

Since the number of the vertices in

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
G
be a group with finite generating set
S
and
H\leq G
be a finitely generated subgroup such that
H\bs G
is quasi-isometric to a tree with
2\leq e<\infty
ends.
Then either the set of double cosets
\{HgH:g\in G\}
is finite or
H
has bounded packing in
G
.

Proof.

By
[
CM13
, Corollary 2.4]
, an element
g\in G
is in the commensurator
\Comm_{G}(H)
if and only if the cosets
H
and
gH
are within finite Hausdorff distance from each other. Thus
g\in\Comm_{G}(H)
if and only if
\rho_{H}(gH)
is bounded.
By Lemma

, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
and

such that, for all

, there exists

such that

.
Therefore if the set

is infinite, then there is an infinite family of double cosets

such that each

. Thus

has infinite index in
\Comm_{G}(H)
. This implies that

has finite index in

. By
[

,

has bounded packing in

and by
[

, Proposition 2.5]
this implies that
H
has bounded packing in
G
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
G
be a group with finite generating set
S
and
H\leq G
be a finitely generated subgroup such that
H\bs G
is quasi-isometric to a tree with
2\leq e<\infty
ends.
If
H
has bounded packing then
H
is a virtual fiber subgroup.

Proof.

Since
H
has at least two relative ends and bounded packing, there is an action of
G
on an unbounded finite dimensional CAT(0) cube complex
C
with a single orbit of hyperplanes
G\cdot\h
such that the
G
-stabiliser of
\h
, denoted
\Stab_{G}(\h)
, is commensurable with
H
(see Section
2
). We assume without loss of generality that
H=\Stab_{G}(\h)
. By Proposition
2.1
, the action of
G
on
C
is essential and there is a hyperbolic element
\gamma\in G
which skewers

. Label the halfspaces of
\h
and replace
\gamma
with a proper power if necessary so that
\gamma\h^{+}\subsetneq\h^{+}
.
No power of
\gamma
is contained in
H
so
\rho_{H}(\la\gamma\ra)
is infinite. By Lemma
3.4
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
k_{1},\dots,k_{N}\in G
such that the
\rho_{H}
-images of
\{k_{i}\la\gamma\ra\}
are infinite and pairwise distinct and, for all
k\in G
, if
\rho_{H}(k\la\gamma\ra)
is infinite then
\rho_{H}(k\la\gamma\ra)=\rho_{H}(k_{i}\la\gamma\ra)
for some
i
.

Claim 1
.

If
k\in G
is such that
\rho_{H}(k\la\gamma\ra)
is finite then
k\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{h}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

If
\rho_{H}(k\la\gamma\ra)
is finite then
Hk\gamma^{n}=Hk
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, so
k\gamma^{n}k^{-1}\in H
. Therefore

d(\h,k\gamma^{n}\h)=d(\h,k\h)=d(\h,k\gamma^{-n}\h).

If
k\h\cap\h=\emptyset
then either
k\h\subseteq\h^{+}
or
k\h\subseteq\h^{-}
. In the former case, either
k\gamma^{n}\h^{+}\subsetneq k\h^{+}\subsetneq\h^{+}
or
k\gamma^{-n}\h^{-}\subsetneq k\h^{-}\subsetneq\h^{+}
, and in the latter, either
k\gamma^{n}\h^{+}\subsetneq k\h^{+}\subsetneq\h^{-}
or
k\gamma^{-n}\h^{-}\subsetneq k\h^{-}\subsetneq\h^{-}
. In any case
k\h
separates
\h
from either
k\gamma^{n}\h
or
k\gamma^{-n}\h
. This implies that either
d(\h,k\h)<d(\h,k\gamma^{n}\h)
or
d(\h,k\h)<d(\h,k\gamma^{-n}\h)
, either of which is a contradiction.
∎

Let
k\in G
be such that
k\h\cap\h=\emptyset
. Claim
1
implies that
\rho_{H}(k\la\gamma\ra)
is infinite, so there is
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
such that
Hk\la\gamma\ra=Hk_{i}\la\gamma\ra
. In particular, there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
h\in H
such that
k=hk_{i}\gamma^{n}
. Thus if
\ell
is an axis for
\gamma
then
d(\h,k\ell)=d(\h,hk_{i}\gamma^{n}\ell)=d(\h,k_{i}\ell)
. Let
M\coloneqq\max\{d(\h,k_{i}\ell):1\leq i\leq N\}
.

Claim 2
.

There is no facing triple of hyperplanes in
C
.

Proof.

Let
g,g^{\prime}\in G
be such that
\{\h,g\h,g^{\prime}\h\}
is a facing triple. Then
\gamma
cannot skewer both
g\h
and
g^{\prime}\h
so, up to relabelling, we can assume that
\ell\cap g\h=\emptyset
. This implies that
\h
and
\ell
are both contained in a halfspace bounded by
g\h
, which we denote by
(g\h)^{+}
. Let
\psi=g\gamma g^{-1}
and note that
\psi
skewers
g\h
. Let
N\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
\psi^{N}(g\h)^{-}\subsetneq(g\h)^{-}
. Then for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

d(\h,g^{-1}\psi^{-iN}\ell)=d(\psi^{iN}g\h,\ell)\geq i.

Moreover
g^{-1}\psi^{-iN}\h\cap\h=g^{-1}\psi^{-iN}(\h\cap\psi^{iN}g\h)=\emptyset
since
g\h
separates
\psi^{iN}g\h
from
\h
. But then
d(\h,g^{-1}\psi^{-iN}\ell)\leq M
for all
i
, which is a contradiction.
∎

The result then follows from Proposition
1.7
.
∎

4
Reducing a Euclidean cube complex to a line

The aim of this section is to prove Proposition
1.7
.

Let
Y
be a finite dimensional CAT(0) cube complex. For any subspace
A\subseteq Y
let
\mathcal{H}(A)
denote the set of hyperplanes of
Y
which separate points in
A
. Let
d_{Haus}
denote the Hausdorff distance in
Y
with respect to the
\ell^{1}
metric
d
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
and
Y
are as in Proposition
1.7
.
Let
\h\subseteq Y
be a hyperplane and
\gamma\in\Gamma
be such that
\gamma\h^{+}\subsetneq\h^{+}
. Then every hyperplane in the symmetric difference
\mathcal{H}(\h)\Delta\mathcal{H}(\gamma\h)
is skewered by
\gamma
. It follows that
|\mathcal{H}(\h)\Delta\mathcal{H}(\gamma\h)|<\infty
and
d_{Haus}(\h,\gamma\h)<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\kk
be a hyperplane which intersects
\h
but not
\gamma\h
. If
\kk
separates
\gamma\h
from
\gamma^{-n}\h
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
then
\gamma
skewers
\kk
. So suppose that
\kk
does not separate
\gamma\h
from any
\gamma^{-n}\h
. Since there are no facing triples,
\kk
must intersect
\gamma^{-n}\h
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore for all
n\geq 1
,
\gamma^{n}\kk
intersects both
\h
and
\gamma\h
while
\gamma^{-n}\kk
intersects neither
\h
nor
\gamma\h
. Similarly, for any
\kk\in\mathcal{H}(\h)\Delta\mathcal{H}(\gamma\h)
and
n\neq 0
,
\gamma^{n}\kk\notin\mathcal{H}(\h)\Delta\mathcal{H}(\gamma\h)
.
This implies in particular that any
\kk\in\mathcal{H}(\h)\Delta\mathcal{H}(\gamma\h)
is not stabilised by any power of
\gamma
and, by Lemma
2.3
, must therefore be skewered by
\gamma
.

Let
\ell
be a geodesic axis of
\gamma
and
\sigma\subseteq\ell
be a fundamental domain for the action of
\la\gamma\ra
on
\ell
. Every hyperplane which is skewered by
\gamma
intersects
\ell
and is therefore of the form
\gamma^{n}\kk
, where
\kk
intersects
\sigma
. At most one hyperplane in each
\la\gamma\ra
-orbit is in
\mathcal{H}(\h)\Delta\mathcal{H}(\gamma\h)
so this implies that
|\mathcal{H}(\h)\Delta\mathcal{H}(\gamma\h)|\leq|\sigma|<\infty
, where
|\sigma|
is the length of
\sigma
.

It follows from
[
CFI16
, Lemma 2.24]
that
d_{Haus}(\h,\gamma\h)\leq|\mathcal{H}(\h)\Delta\mathcal{H}(\gamma\h)|+d(\h,\gamma\h)
therefore
d_{Haus}(\h,\gamma\h)<\infty
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
and
Y
are as in Proposition
1.7
.
Then there is a hyperbolic element
\gamma\in\Gamma
which skewers every hyperplane of
Y
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a (necessarily finite) set of pairwise intersecting hyperplanes of
Y
and
g\in\Gamma
be an isometry which skewers each
\h\in\mathcal{H}
. Label the halfspaces and replace
g
with a proper power if necessary so that
g\h^{+}\subsetneq\h^{+}
for each
\h\in\mathcal{H}
.
Suppose that there exists a hyperplane
\kk
of
Y
which is not skewered by
g
and let
k\in\Gamma
be such that
k\kk^{+}\subsetneq\kk^{+}
. By Lemma
2.3
, there exists
n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
g^{n_{1}}\kk^{+}=\kk^{+}
.
Moreover, there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for each
\h\in\mathcal{H}
, either
k^{m}\h^{+}=\h^{+}
,
k^{m}\h^{+}\subsetneq\h^{+}
or
k^{m}\h^{-}\subsetneq\h^{-}
. In the first two cases,
g^{n_{1}n}k^{m}\h^{+}\subseteq g^{n_{1}n}\h^{+}\subsetneq\h^{+}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In the latter case, note that
d_{Haus}(\h,k^{m}\h)<\infty
by Lemma
4.1
and, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
d(g^{nn_{1}}\h,\h)>d_{Haus}(\h,k^{m}\h)
, we have that
g^{n_{1}n}k^{m}\h^{+}\subsetneq\h^{+}
. Since there are finitely many hyperplanes in
\mathcal{H}
, we can choose
n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large so that
g^{n_{1}n_{2}}k^{m}\h^{+}\subsetneq\h^{+}
for all
\h\in\mathcal{H}
.
We also have that
g^{n_{1}n_{2}}k^{m}\kk^{+}\subsetneq g^{n_{1}n_{2}}\kk^{+}=\kk^{+}
so the element
g^{\prime}\coloneqq g^{n_{1}n_{2}}k^{m}
skewers every hyperplane in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}\cup\{\kk\}
. Moreover, for each
\h\in\mathcal{H}
,
\kk
cannot separate hyperplanes in
\la g\ra\cdot\h
without being skewered by
g
. By the lack of facing triples, this implies that
\kk
must intersect
g^{n}\h
for some
n
. It then follows from Lemma
4.1
that
\kk
intersects
g^{n}\h
for all
n
. In particular,
\kk
intersects every hyperplane in
\mathcal{H}
, so
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is pairwise intersecting and
|\mathcal{H}^{\prime}|\leq\dim(Y)
. If there exists a hyperplane
\kk^{\prime}
which is not skewered by
g^{\prime}
, then the above argument applied to
\mathcal{H}^{\prime},g^{\prime}
and
\kk^{\prime}
implies that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\cup\{\kk^{\prime}\}
is pairwise intersecting and there exists
g^{\prime\prime}\in\Gamma
which skewers every hyperplane in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
. We can only iterate this process at most
\dim(Y)-1
times until we obtain the desired element
\gamma
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.7
.

If
K
is a hyperplane stabiliser in the cubical subdivision of
Y
then there is a hyperplane
\h
in
H
such that
[\Stab_{\Gamma}(\h):K]\leq 2
. Therefore, by replacing
Y
with its cubical subdivision if necessary, we can assume that
\Gamma
acts on
Y
without hyperplane inversions.

By Lemma
4.2
there is a hyperbolic isometry
\gamma\in\Gamma
which skewers every hyperplane of
Y
. There are finitely many
\la\gamma\ra
-orbits of hyperplanes in
Y
so we can replace
\gamma
with a proper power so that
\gamma\h^{+}\subsetneq\h^{+}
for each hyperplane
\h\subseteq Y
.
Fix a hyperplane
\h\subseteq Y
, let
K\coloneqq\Stab_{\Gamma}(\h)=\Stab_{\Gamma}(\h^{+})
and let
\Gamma^{\prime}\coloneqq\la K,\gamma\ra\leq\Gamma
.

Let
M^{+}
(respectively
M^{-}
) be the set of hyperplanes of
Y
which lie in
\h^{+}
(respectively
\h^{-}
). Then
kM^{+}=M^{+}
for any
k\in K
. Moreover,
\gamma M^{+}\subseteq M^{+}
and, if
\kk\in M^{+}-\gamma M^{+}
, then
\kk
either separates
\gamma\h
from
\h
or
\kk
intersects
\gamma\h
but not
\h
, so
|M^{+}-\gamma M^{+}|<\infty
by Lemma
4.1
and thus
|M^{+}\Delta\gamma M^{+}|<\infty
. Similarly,
|M^{+}\Delta\gamma^{-1}M^{+}|=|\gamma^{-1}M^{+}-M^{+}|<\infty
. If
N
is a set of hyperplanes such that
|M^{+}\Delta N|<\infty
then
|M^{+}\Delta\gamma^{\pm 1}N|\leq|M^{+}\Delta\gamma^{\pm 1}M^{+}|+|\gamma^{\pm 1}(M^{+}\Delta N)|<\infty
and, if
k\in K
, then
|M^{+}\Delta kN|\leq|M^{+}\Delta kM^{+}|+|k(M^{+}\Delta N)|<\infty
. It follows that
|M^{+}\Delta gM^{+}|<\infty
for all
g\in\Gamma^{\prime}
, so
M^{+}
is
commensurated by the action of
\Gamma^{\prime}
. A symmetric argument shows that
M^{-}
is also commensurated by the action of
\Gamma^{\prime}
. This implies that the map
tr_{M^{+}}:\Gamma^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
given by

tr_{M^{+}}(g)=|M^{+}-g^{-1}M^{+}|-|g^{-1}M^{+}-M^{+}|

is well defined and, by
[
Cor16
, Proposition 4.H.1]
, it is a homomorphism.

Clearly
K\leq\ker(tr_{M^{+}})
. Let us show that
K
has finite index in
\ker(tr_{M^{+}})
. Given a hyperplane
\kk
of
Y
, define
L_{\kk}
as follows. If
\kk
does not separate
\gamma^{n_{1}}\h
from
\gamma^{n_{2}}\h
for any
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
then
L_{\kk}\coloneqq 0
. Otherwise

L_{\kk}\coloneqq\min\{|n_{1}-n_{2}|:n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ and }\kk\text{ separates }\gamma^{n_{1}}\h\text{ from }\gamma^{n_{2}}\h\}.

It is clear from the definition that
L_{\gamma^{n}\kk}=L_{\kk}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore, since there are finitely many
\la\gamma\ra
-orbits of hyperplanes in
Y
, there exists
L\geq 0
such that
L_{\kk}\leq L
for all
\kk
.
Let
g\in\ker(tr_{M^{+}})
.
Then
g\h
intersects
\h
since otherwise either
|{M^{+}}-g^{-1}{M^{+}}|>0=|g^{-1}{M^{+}}-{M^{+}}|
or
|g^{-1}{M^{+}}-{M^{+}}|=0<|{M^{+}}-g^{-1}{M^{+}}|
. Moreover, any hyperplane which intersects
g\h
but not
\h
is contained in
(M^{+}-gM^{+})\cup(M^{-}-gM^{-})
. Both
M^{+}
and
M^{-}
are commensurated by
g
, so these sets are finite which implies that there exist
n_{1}<0<n_{2}
such that
g\h
does not intersect
\gamma^{n_{1}}\h
or
\gamma^{n_{2}}\h
. Since
\{\gamma^{n_{1}}\h,g\h,\gamma^{n_{2}}\h\}
is not a facing triple, this implies that
g\h
separates
\gamma^{n_{1}}\h
from
\gamma^{n_{2}}\h
. Hence
0<L_{g\h}\leq L
so the
\ker(tr_{M^{+}})
-orbit of
\h
is contained in the finite set of hyperplanes which separate
\gamma^{1-L}\h
from
\gamma^{L-1}\h
. Thus
K
has finite index in
\ker(tr_{M^{+}})
. It follows that
K
is normalised by a finite index subgroup
\Lambda
of
\Gamma^{\prime}
and
\Lambda/K=\mathbb{Z}
.

It remains to show that
\Gamma^{\prime}
has finite index in
\Gamma
.
Let
R\coloneqq d_{Haus}(\h,\gamma\h)<\infty
, let
\ell
be a combinatorial geodesic axis for
\gamma
and let
\sigma\subseteq\ell
be a segment of length
R
. Let
1\leq R^{\prime}\leq R
be such that exactly
R^{\prime}
of the edges of
\sigma
are dual to hyperplanes in the
\Gamma
-orbit of
\h
and let
g_{1},\dots,g_{R^{\prime}}\in\Gamma
be such that each hyperplane in
\Gamma\cdot\h
which intersects
\sigma
is of the form
g_{i}\h
for some
i
. Then, for any
g\in\Gamma
, there is some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
i\in\{1,\dots,R\}
such that
g\gamma^{n}\h=g_{i}\h
. Therefore
g_{i}^{-1}g\gamma^{n}\in K
and
g_{i}^{-1}g\in K\gamma^{-n}\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}
. Hence
\Gamma^{\prime}
has index at most
R
in
\Gamma
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

If we assume, as in the proof of Proposition
3.6
, that
\Gamma
and
\Stab_{\Gamma}(\h)
are finitely generated and
\Stab_{\Gamma}(\h)\bs\Gamma
is quasi-isometric to a tree with
2\leq e<\infty
ends, then there is a much shorter proof:

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mathcal{H}_{n}
denote the set of hyperplanes whose distance from
\h
is exactly
n
. Then the hyperplanes of
\mathcal{H}_{n}
contained in
\h^{-}
are pairwise intersecting, as are those contained in
\h^{+}
. Therefore
|\mathcal{H}_{n}|\leq 2\dim(Y)
and, since
\Stab_{\Gamma}(\h)
acts on
\mathcal{H}_{n}
, there is a homomorphism
f_{n}:\Stab_{\Gamma}(\h)\rightarrow S(2\dim(C))
, where
S(2\dim(C))
is the symmetric group on
2\dim(C)
elements and
\ker f_{n}
acts trivially on
\mathcal{H}_{n}
. The kernel
\ker f_{n}
has index at most
(2\dim(C))!
in
\Stab_{\Gamma}(\h)
and, since
\Stab_{\Gamma}(\h)
is finitely generated, there are finitely many such subgroups. Thus the intersection
K\coloneqq\cap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\ker f_{n}
is a finite intersection of finite index subgroups of
\Stab_{\Gamma}(\h)
and is itself a subgroup of
\Stab_{\Gamma}(\h)
with finite index. Let
g\in\Gamma
be such that
g\h^{+}\subsetneq\h^{+}
. Then
K
fixes
g^{n}\h
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, so
K
has infinite index in its normaliser, which implies that
[\Gamma:N(K)]<\infty
by Proposition
3.3
. The quotient
N(K)/K
has
e
ends but, since it is a group,
e
must equal
2
so
N(K)/K
is virtually cyclic
[
Hop44
]
.

Remark 4.4
.

While there are statements in the literature of a similar nature to Proposition
1.7
, these do not, to the author’s knowledge, directly imply this result.

If, in addition to the conditions of the proposition, one assumes for example that
\Gamma
acts on
Y
without a fixed point in the visual boundary then
[
CS11
, Theorem E]
implies that
\Gamma
fixes a Euclidean flat in
Y
. The task of finding an appropriate map to
\mathbb{Z}
then requires some work (and probably some CAT(0) geometry). It is moreover possible to reduce to the case where either there is no fixed point in the visual boundary or there is a fixed point in the Roller boundary
[
CFI16
, Proposition 2.26]
. In the later case, Caprace’s appendix in
[
CFI16
]
provides several epimorphisms from a finite index subgroup of
\Gamma
to
\mathbb{Z}
(in fact, in either case one can show that
\Gamma
virtually fixes the simplicial boundary of
Y
pointwise which suffices to apply Caprace’s argument). The fact that one of these has a kernel with the required properties is not immediate, but it can be shown following much the same argument as the proof of Proposition
1.7
.

5
A family of examples

A family of groups introduced by Houghton
[
Hou78
]
provides an example, for each
n\geq 2
, of a finitely generated pair of groups
H_{n}\leq G_{n}
such that
H_{n}\bs G_{n}
is an
n
-ended quasi-tree but
H_{n}
is not a virtual fiber subgroup. These groups are known to provide examples of pairs of groups with an arbitrary number of relative ends
[
Sco77
, Example 2.1]
and, as will become apparent, the fact that
H_{n}\bs G_{n}
is not only
n
-ended but a quasi-tree is obvious from the construction.

Definition 5.1

(Houghton’s groups)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
X_{n}
be a disjoint union of
n

\mathbb{N}

and constants

f(i,m)=(i,m+a_{i})
for all
(i,m)\in X_{n}-K

Houghton’s group
\mathcal{G}_{n}
is the group of all translations at infinity of
X_{n}
.

When
n=1
,

is the group of finitely supported permutations on
\mathbb{N}
, which is not finitely generated. However
\mathcal{G}_{n}
is finitely generated for all
n\geq 2
. For
n\geq 3

defined below. The equivalence of the two definitions essentially follows from the observation of Wiegold

Lee12

[

]

Let

be the increasing union

is a subgroup of the permutation group of

M\coloneqq\Stab_{L}((1,1))
. Then
\alpha\in M
so
M=H_{n}\rtimes\la\alpha\ra\cong\mathcal{G}_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}=L
. Moreover, the Schreier graph of
M
with respect to
S_{n}\cup\{\alpha\}
is a quasi-tree whose number of ends is the number of orbits of the action of
\la\sigma\ra
on
\{1,\dots,n\}
and
L=M\cup Mg_{1}M
.

Using cyclic notation, if we take for instance
\sigma=(2,\dots,n)
then this provides an example of a pair
H\leq G
where
H\bs G
is a quasi-line,
H
is not a virtual fiber subgroup and both
H
and
G
are of type
F_{n-1}
.

Suppose that
G,N,Q
are finitely generated groups which fit into a short exact sequence:

Fix a (set-theoretic) section
\varphi:Q\rightarrow G
of
\pi
. If
K\leq Q
is a finitely generated subgroup such that
K\bs Q
is an
n
-ended quasi-tree for some
2\leq n<\infty
then the pull-back
H\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(K)
is a finitely generated subgroup of
G
and
H\bs G
is an
n
-ended quasi-tree. Moreover, if there is a finite subset
F\subseteq Q
such that
Q=KFK
then
G=H\varphi(F)H
. Therefore if
K\leq Q
is not a virtual fiber then neither is
H\leq G
. As demonstrated by Example
5.2
, there exists, for each
2\leq n<\infty
, such a pair
K\leq Q
where
Q
is finitely presented. Therefore one can use the Rips construction
[
Rip82
]
or any of its variants (see e.g.

]
and the references therein), to produce examples of finitely generated pairs
H\leq G
where
H\bs G
is an
n
-ended quasi-tree,
H
is not a virtual fiber and
G
is, in some precise way, “well-behaved”. For example, Arenas’ construction
[

]
yields a group
G
which is torsion-free hyperbolic (in particular, of type

1.
Introduction

The study of commutators of singular intergral operator
T
can be traced back to the celebrated works of Coifman, Rochberg and Weiss
[
12
]
. Recall that, for a suitable smooth function
f
, the
commutator of
T
is defined as

[b,T]f=T(bf)-bT(f)
. In
[
12
]
, it was shown
that if
b
belongs to BMO(
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
), then
[b,T]
is
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})

(1<p<\infty)
bounded. Conversely,
if all commutators of Riesz transform
[R_{j},b]
are
L^{p}
bounded for
1\leq j\leq n
, then
b\in
BMO(
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
).

In 1978, Uchiyama
[
27
]

first studied the compactness of commutators of a singular integral operator
with the kernel
\Omega\in{\rm Lip}_{1}({\rm S}^{n-1})
defined by

T_{\Omega}f(x)=\textrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Omega(y/|y|)}{|y|^{n}}f(x-y)dy.

He showed that the commutator
[b,T_{\Omega}]

is compact on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<p<\infty
if and only if

b\in{\rm CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
{\rm CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

is the closure of
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the
{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

topology.

Since then, the study on the compactness of commutators of different operators
has attracted much more attention. For examples, the compactness of commutators of the linear Fourier multipliers and pseudodifferential
operators was considered by Cordes
[
13
]
. Beatrous and Li
[
1
]
studied the boundedness and compactness of the commutators of Hankel type operators. Krantz and Li
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
applied the compactness characterization
of the commutator
[b,T_{\Omega}]
to study Hankel type operators on Bergman space. Wang
[
28
]
showed that the commutators
of fractional integral operator are compact form
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
to
L^{q}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. In 2009, Chen and Ding

[
8
]
proved thar the commutator of singular integrals with variable kernels is compact
on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
if and only if
b\in\rm CMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and they also establised the compactness of
Littlewood-Paley square functions in
[
9
]
. Later on, Chen, Ding and Wang
[
10
]

obtained the compactness of commutators for Marcinkiewicz Integral in Morrey Spaces.
Recently, Liu, Wang and Xue
[
23
]
showed the compactness of the commutator of oscillatory singular integrals with non-convolutional type kernels.

In order to state more results, we need to give some definitions and notions. Let
K(x,y,z)
be a locally integrable function defined
away from the diagonal
x=y=z
in
\mathbb{R}^{3n}
and satisfy the conditions as follows:

(i)

The size condition
:
|K(x,y,z)|\leq{A}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{-2n}};

(ii)

The regularity conditions
:
for
\gamma>0
,

\indent\quad|K(x,y,z)-K_{(}x^{\prime},y,z)|\leq\frac{A|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}},\hbox{\ whenever \ }|x-x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\{|x-y|,|x-z|\};

\indent\quad|K(x,y,z)-K_{(}x,y^{\prime},z)|\leq\frac{A|y-y^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}},\hbox{\ whenever \ }|y-y^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\{|x-y|,|x-z|\};

\indent\quad|K(x,y,z)-K_{(}x,y,z^{\prime})|\leq\frac{A|z-z^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}},\hbox{\ whenever \ }|z-z^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\{|x-y|,|x-z|\}.

Let
T
be the following bilinear singular integral operator

T(f,g)(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}}K(x,y,z)f(y)g(z)dydz,\quad\hbox{for \ all\ }x\notin\operatorname{supp}(f)\cap\operatorname{supp}(g),

and define the corresponding truncated maximal bilinear singular integral operator
T^{*}
by

T^{*}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta}K(x,y,z)f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|.

Given
\vec{b}=(b_{1},b_{2})\in\rm BMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\times\rm BMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
b\in{\rm BMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
, the iterated commutator of
T
is defined by

T_{\vec{b}}(f,g)=[b_{2},[b_{1},T]_{1}]_{2}(f,g),

and the commutators in the
j
-th entry are defined by

[b,T]_{1}(f,g)=bT(f,g)-T(bf,g)\quad\hbox{ and \ \ }[b,T]_{2}(f,g)=bT(f,g)-T(f,bg).

Similarly, the commutators of
T^{*}
in the
j
-th entry are defined by

(1.1)

T^{*}_{b,1}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta}K(x,y,z)(b(x)-b(y))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|,

(1.2)

T^{*}_{b,2}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta}K(x,y,z)(b(x)-b(z))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|.

The iterated commutators of
T^{*}
is defined by

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&T^{*}_{\vec{b}}(f,g)(x)\\
&=\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta}K(x,y,z)(b_{1}(x)-b_{1}(y))(b_{2}(x)-b_{2}(z))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|.\end{split}

It is known that the commutators of bilinear singular integral operator were first defined and studied by Pérez and Torres
[
22
]
. In 2009, Lerner et. al.
[
21
]
established the weighted estimates for multilinear operators
T
and its corresponding iterated commutators
T_{\vec{b}}
with weight
\vec{w}\in A_{\vec{p}}
. As for
T^{*}
, in
[
7
]
, Chen gave the weighed
L^{p}
-estimate for
T^{*}
with
A_{\vec{p}}
weights (see definition
2.1
). In
[
29
]
, Xue proved the following weighted strong type estimate for
T^{*}_{(b_{1},b_{2})}
with
A_{\vec{p}}
weights.

Theorem A
(
[
29
]
).
Let
\vec{w}:=(w_{1},w_{2})\in A_{\vec{p}}
,
w:=w_{1}^{p/p_{1}}w_{2}^{p/p_{2}}
,
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
with
1<p_{j}<\infty
, and
b_{j}\in\rm BMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}}),\ j=1,2
. Then there is a constant
C>0
such that

\|T^{*}_{(b_{1},b_{2})}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq C\prod_{j=1}^{2}\|b_{j}\|_{\rm BMO}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}.

Remark 1.1
.

Using the method of
[
29
]
, we can also get the weighted boundedness of
T^{*}_{b,j},\ j=1,2
.

\|T^{*}_{b,j}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C\|b\|_{\rm BMO}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})},

with
\vec{w}\in A_{\vec{p}}
,
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
,
1<p_{j}<\infty,\ j=1,2
.

It is quite natural to consider the compactness of commutators of bilinear operators. Bényi and Torres
[
2
]
first showed that commutators of bilinear Calderón-Zygmund operators with multiplication by
\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
functions are compact operators from
L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}
to
L^{p}
for
1<p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
and
1/p=1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}
. Later on, Bényi et. al.
[
4
]
gave the following weigthed compactness:

Theorem B
(
[
4
]
).
Suppose
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty,1<p<\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
,
b\in\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
,
\vec{b}\in\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\times\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, and
\vec{w}=(w_{1},w_{2})\in A_{p}\times A_{p}
,
v_{\vec{w}}=w_{1}^{p/p_{1}}w_{2}^{p/p_{2}}
. Then
[b,T]_{1}
,
[b,T]_{2}
and
T_{\vec{b}}
are compact operators from
L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})\times L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})
to
L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})
.

The compactness for commutators of bilinear fractional integrals were also given in
[
3
]
.
Since then, this subject has attracted much attention. We refer the reader to
[
14
,
31
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
for the compactness of commutators of multilinear operators. Very recently, extrapolation for linear and multilinear compact operators with applications were given by Cao et. al in
[

, and Hytönen et. al. in
[

. It should be pointed out that all the above results hold for linear not sublinear opertors.

In
[
4
]
, it is pointed out that
A_{p}\times A_{p}\subsetneq A_{\vec{p}}
. Hence, a natural question arises:

Question 1.2
.

Does the compactness result in Theorem B still hold for multiple weights class
A_{\vec{p}}
?

Recently, Bu and Chen
[
24
]
studied this question and they showed that the commutators of bilinear singular integral operators are compact from
L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})\times L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})
to
L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})
when
\vec{w}=(w_{1},w_{2})\in A_{\vec{p}}
,

for

,
1<p_{1},\ p_{2}<\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
. However, they need to assume

, which leaves this question still open. Moreover, it seems that the condition

is not natural, since the strong boundedness holds for

Now we turn to the the commutators of bilinear maximal Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operators. In
[
14
]
, Ding, Mei and Xue obtained the following compactness result:

Theorem C
(
[
14
]
).
Let
1<p<\infty
,
1<p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
. If
b\in\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
,
\vec{b}\in\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\times\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.
Then the operators
T^{*}_{b,1}
,

defined by (

Inspired by the above results, one may further ask the following questions:

Question 1.3
.

Are the operators
T^{*}_{b,1}
,
T^{*}_{b,2}
and
T^{*}_{\vec{b}}
compact from
L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})\times L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})
to
L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})
when
\vec{w}
belongs to
A_{\vec{p}}
for
1/2<p<\infty.
?

For
T^{*}
, the difficulty lies in that,
T^{*}
is a sublinear opeator, and the proof in Theorem C relies heavily on the properties of translation invariat operators. However, when it comes to the case of weighted compactness, the weights are not translation invariat. Therefore, these questions become
more difficult and subtle to deal with.

The main purpose of this paper is to give a firm answer to these questions. Smooth truncated techniques will play an essential role in solving these questions.

We summarize our results as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}<p<\infty
,
1<p_{1},\ p_{2}<\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
and
\vec{w}=(w_{1},w_{2})\in A_{\vec{p}}
,
v_{\vec{w}}=w_{1}^{p/p_{1}}w_{2}^{p/p_{2}}
. If

,
then the operators
T^{*}_{b,1}
and
T^{*}_{b,2}
defined by (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are compact operators from
L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})\times L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})
to
L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}<p<\infty
,
1<p_{1},\ p_{2}<\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
and
\vec{w}=(w_{1},w_{2})\in A_{\vec{p}}
,

. If
\vec{b}\in\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\times\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
,
then the operator

defined by (

) is a compact operator from

Using the general weighted version of Frechet-Kolmogorov theorems (Lemma

Corollary 1.1

Let

\vec{w}=(w_{1},w_{2})\in A_{\vec{p}}

[b,T]_{1}
,
[b,T]_{2}

are compact operators from

2.
Definitions and some lemmas

We first recall some notation and definitions. Given
a Lebesgue measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
|E|
will denote the Lebesgue measure of
E
. Let
B=B(x,r)
be a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered at
x
with radius
r
and
Q(x,r)
be a cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered at
x
with the side length
2r
. A weight
w
is a non-negative measurable and local integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The measure associated with
w
is the set function given by
w(E)=\int_{E}wdx
. For
0<p<\infty
, we denote by
L^{p}(w)
the space of all Lebesgue measurable function
f(x)
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx|\Big)^{1/p}.

Recall that a weight
w
belongs to the classical Muckenhopt class
A_{p}
(1<p<\infty)
, if

\sup_{Q}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(y)dy\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(y)^{1-p^{\prime}}dy\Big)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}<\infty.

w\in A_{1}
, if there is a constants
C
such that

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(y)dy\leq C\inf_{x\in Q}w(x),\ \ \ \ for\ a.\ e.\ x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

The following multiple weights calss
A_{\vec{p}}
was first introduced in
[
21
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

(Multiple
A_{\vec{p}}
weights class,
[
21
]
)
.
Let
1\leq p_{1},\ p_{2}<\infty
,
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
,
\frac{1}{p_{j}}+\frac{1}{p_{j}^{\prime}}=1,\ j=1,2
. Given
\vec{w}=(w_{1},w_{2})
, set

v_{\vec{w}}=w_{1}^{p/p_{1}}w_{2}^{p/p_{2}}.

Then, we say
\vec{w}\in A_{\vec{p}}
if

[\vec{w}]_{A_{\vec{p}}}=:\sup_{Q}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}v_{\vec{w}}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w_{1}^{1-p_{1}^{\prime}}\Big)^{\frac{p}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w_{2}^{1-p_{2}^{\prime}}\Big)^{\frac{p}{p_{2}^{\prime}}}<\infty.

When
p_{j}=1,\ j=1,2
,
\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w_{j}^{1-p_{j}^{\prime}}\Big)^{\frac{p}{p_{j}^{\prime}}}
is understood as
(\inf_{Q}w_{j})^{-1}
.

It was shown in
[
21
]
that
\vec{w}\in A_{\vec{p}}
if and only if
v_{\vec{w}}\in A_{2p}
,
w_{1}^{1-p_{1}^{\prime}}\in A_{2p_{1}^{\prime}}
and
w_{2}^{1-p_{2}^{\prime}}\in A_{2p_{2}^{\prime}}
.

For the classical Muckenhopt weights class
A_{p}
, it enjoys the following properties:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then

(i)

If
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a constant

\theta\in(0,1)
such that
w^{1+\theta}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Both

\theta
and the
A_{p}
constant of
w^{1+\theta}
depend only on

n,\,p
and the
A_{p}
constant of
w
.

(ii)

If
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{|x|>N}\frac{w(x)}{|x|^{np}}dx=0,\ \ \ \lim\limits_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{|x|>N}\frac{w^{1-p^{\prime}}(x)}{|x|^{np^{\prime}}}dx=0.

(iii)

if
w\in A_{\infty}=\displaystyle\bigcup_{1\leq p<\infty}A_{p}
, then there exists a constant
\theta\in(0,1)
such that for all cubes
Q
and any set
E\subset Q
,

\frac{w(E)}{w(Q)}\leq C\Big(\frac{|E|}{|Q|}\Big)^{\theta}.

It should be pointed out that (i) of Lemma
2.1
follows
from
[
11
]
and (ii) of Lemma
2.1
follows from
[
15
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

(Bilinear Hardy-Littiewood maximal operator,
[
21
]
)
.
For any
f,\ g\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, the bilinear Hardy-Littiewood maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
is defined by

\mathcal{M}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\Big(\frac{1}{Q}\int_{Q}|f(y)|dy\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{Q}\int_{Q}|g(z)|dz\Big),

where the supremum is taken over all the cubes
Q
of
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
containing

.

Then, the following characterization of the strong-type inequality holds for
\mathcal{M}
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
21
]
)
.

Let
1<p_{j}<\infty,\ j=1,2
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
. Let
\vec{w}=(w_{1},w_{2})\in A_{\vec{p}}
and
v_{\vec{w}}=w_{1}^{p/p_{1}}w_{2}^{p/p_{2}}
. Then the inequality

(2.1)

\|\mathcal{M}\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}

holds for every

In order to prove Theorem

Lemma 2.3

(
[

)
.

For any

,

\iint_{\frac{\delta}{1+2\epsilon}\leq|y|+|z|\leq\delta}\frac{dydz}{(|y|+|z|)^{2n}}\leq C[1-(1+2\epsilon)^{-n}],

We end this section by introducing the general weighted version of Frechet-Kolmogorov
theorems, which was proved by Xue, Yabuta and Yan in

.

Lemma 2.4
.

(
[
31
]
)
. Let
w
be a weight on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that
w^{-1/(p_{0}-1)}
is also a weight on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
p_{0}>1
. Let
0<p<\infty
and
\mathcal{F}
be a subset in
L^{p}(w)
, then
\mathcal{F}
is sequentially compact in
L^{p}(w)
if the following three conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\mathcal{F}
is bounded, i.e.,

\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}<\infty
;

(ii)

\mathcal{F}
uniformly vanishes at infinity, i.e.,

\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{|x|>N}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx=0;

(iii)

\mathcal{F}
is uniformly equicontinuous, i.e.,

\lim\limits_{|h|\rightarrow 0}\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(\cdot+h)-f(\cdot)|^{p}w(x)dx=0.

3.
Proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

In this section, we shall adapt the methods used in
[
14
]
and
[
26
]
to prove Theorem
1.1
-
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We only prove
T^{*}_{b,1}
is compact, and the proof of the commutator
T^{*}_{b,2}
can be get similarly. We shall prove Theorem
1.1
via smooth truncated techniques.

First, we introduce the following smooth truncated function. Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
satisfy

(3.1)

\displaystyle 0\leq\varphi\leq 1\ \ \ and\ \ \ \varphi(x)=\begin{cases}1,&x\in[0,1],\\
0,&x\in[2,\infty).\end{cases}

For any
\eta>0
, let

(3.2)

K_{\eta}(x,y,z)=K(x,y,z)\Big[1-\varphi\Big(\frac{2}{\eta}(|x-y|+|x-z|)\Big)\Big].

Define

(3.3)

T_{\eta}^{*}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta}K_{\eta}(x,y,z)f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|

and

(3.4)

T_{\eta,b,1}^{*}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta}K_{\eta}(x,y,z)(b(x)-b(y))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|.

For any
b\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
\eta>0
, by (
3.2
), (
3.4
), one has

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|T_{b,1}^{*}(f,g)(x)-T_{\eta,b,1}^{*}(f,g)(x)|\\
&\leq\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{\delta<|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\eta}K(x,y,z)\varphi\Big(\frac{2}{\eta}(|x-y|+|x-z|)\Big)(b(x)-b(y))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&\leq\|\nabla b\|_{\infty}\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\iint_{\delta<|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\eta}\frac{|x-y||f(y)g(z)|}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n}}dydz\\
&\leq C\eta\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-j}}{(2^{-j-1}\eta)^{2n}}\iint_{2^{-j-1}\eta<|x-y|+|x-z|\leq 2^{-j}\eta}|f(y)g(z)|dydz\\
&\leq C\eta\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-j}}{(2^{-j}\eta)^{2n}}\int_{Q(x,2^{-j}\eta)}|f(y)|dy\int_{Q(x,2^{-j}\eta)}|g(z)|dz\\
&\leq C\eta\mathcal{M}(f,g)(x).\end{split}

Then, using the boundedness of
\mathcal{M}
from
L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})\times L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})
to
L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})
(Lemma
2.2
), we can obtain

\|T_{b,1}^{*}(f,g)-T_{\eta,b,1}^{*}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C\eta\|f\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})},

which implies that

(3.5)

\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}\|T_{b,1}^{*}(f,g)-T_{\eta,b,1}^{*}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}=0.

On the other hand, if
b\in\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
b_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that
\|b-b_{\epsilon}\|_{\rm BMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}<\epsilon
, so that

\displaystyle\|T_{b,1}^{*}(f,g)-T_{b_{\epsilon},1}^{*}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{b-b_{\epsilon},1}^{*}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|b-b_{\epsilon}\|_{\rm BMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon.

Thus, to prove
T_{b,1}^{*}
is compact on

L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})
for any
b\in\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, it suffices to prove that
T_{b,1}^{*}
is compact on
L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})
for any
b\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By (
3.5
) and
[
30
]
,
it suffices to show that
T^{*}_{\eta,b,1}
is compact for any
b\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
\eta>0
is small enough. For arbitrary bounded sets
F\subset L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})
and
G\subset L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})
, let

\mathcal{F}=\{T_{\eta,b,1}^{*}(f,g):f\in F,\ g\in G\}.

Then, we need to show that for
b\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the conditions
\rm(i)
-
\rm(iii)
of Lemma
2.4
. We divide the proof in three steps.

Step I.

\mathcal{F}
satisfies condition

\rm(i)
.

First, we verify that
K_{\eta}(x,y,z)
satisfies the size and regularity conditions in the introduction section with
\tilde{A}=\max\{2A,2^{2n+2+\gamma}A\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}\}.

From the definition of
K_{\eta}(x,y,z)
, it is easy to see that

(3.6)

|K_{\eta}(x,y,z)|\leq|K(x,y,z)|\leq\frac{\tilde{A}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n}}.

Next, we will show, when
|x-x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\{|x-y|,|x-z|\}
, it holds that

(3.7)

|K_{\eta}(x,y,z)-K_{\eta}(x^{\prime},y,z)|\leq\frac{\tilde{A}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}}

We consider the following four cases:

(a)
|x-y|+|x-z|\geq\eta
and
|x^{\prime}-y|+|x^{\prime}-z|\geq\eta
. In this case, we have

K_{\eta}(x,y,z)=K(x,y,z)
and
K_{\eta}(x^{\prime},y,z)=K(x^{\prime},y,z)
, which together with the size condition yields (
3.7
).

(b)
|x-y|+|x-z|\geq\eta
and
|x^{\prime}-y|+|x^{\prime}-z|<\eta
. Note that
|x-x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\{|x-y|,|x-z|\}
. In this case, it holds that
K_{\eta}(x,y)=K(x,y)
and
\eta>|x^{\prime}-y|+|x^{\prime}-z|\geq\frac{1}{2}(|x-y|+|x-z|)
. These together with regularity conditions and
|x-x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}(|x-y|+|x-z|)
imply that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|K_{\eta}(x,y,z)-K_{\eta}(x^{\prime},y,z)|&\leq|K(x,y,z)-K(x^{\prime},y,z)|+|K(x^{\prime},y,z)|\varphi\Big(\frac{2}{\eta}|x^{\prime}-y|+|x^{\prime}-z|\Big)\\
&=|K(x,y,z)-K(x^{\prime},y,z)|+|K(x^{\prime},y,z)|\\
&\quad\times\Big|\varphi\Big(\frac{2}{\eta}(|x^{\prime}-y|+|x^{\prime}-z|)\Big)-\varphi\Big(\frac{2}{\eta}(|x-y|+|x-z|)\Big)\Big|\\
&\leq\displaystyle\frac{A|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}}+\frac{A|x-x^{\prime}|}{(|x^{\prime}-y|+|x^{\prime}-z|)^{2n}}\frac{4}{\eta}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\frac{A|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}}+\frac{4A\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}|x-x^{\prime}|}{(|x^{\prime}-y|+|x^{\prime}-z|)^{2n+1}}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\frac{A|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}}+\frac{2^{2n+3}A\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}}\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{1-\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{1-\gamma}}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\frac{A|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}}+\frac{2^{2n+3}A\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}}(\frac{1}{2})^{1-\gamma}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{A}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}}.\end{split}

which proves (
3.7
).

(c)
|x-y|+|x-z|<\eta
and
|x^{\prime}-y|+|x^{\prime}-z|\geq\eta
. This case is similar to
case (b).

(d)
|x-y|+|x-z|<\eta
and
|x^{\prime}-y|+|x^{\prime}-z|<\eta
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}|K_{\eta}(x,y,z)-K_{\eta}(x^{\prime},y,z)|&\leq|K(x,y,z)-K(x^{\prime},y,z)|+|K(x,y,z)-K(x^{\prime},y,z)|\varphi\Big(\frac{2}{\eta}(|x-y|+|x-z|)\Big)\\
&\quad+|K(x^{\prime},y,z)|\Big|\varphi\Big(\frac{2}{\eta}(|x^{\prime}-y|+|x^{\prime}-z|)\Big)-\varphi\Big(\frac{2}{\eta}(|x-y|+|x-z|)\Big)\Big|\\
&\leq\displaystyle\frac{2A|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}}+\frac{4A\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}}(\frac{1}{2})^{1-\gamma}\\
&\leq\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{A}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}}.\end{split}

Similar arguments to those in deriving (
3.7
) may give that

(3.8)

|K(x,y,z)-K(x,y^{\prime},z)|\leq\frac{\tilde{A}|y-y^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{n+\gamma}},

whenever
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\{|x-y|,|x-z|\}
, and

(3.9)

|K_{\eta}(x,y,z)-K_{\eta}(x,y,z^{\prime})|\leq\frac{\tilde{A}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{n+\gamma}},

whenever
|z-z^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\{|x-y|,|x-z|\}
.

Hence, the weighted strong type estimate also hold for
T_{\eta}^{*}
,
T^{*}_{\eta,{b,j}},\ j=1,2
. Thus, we have

\sup\limits_{f\in F,g\in G}\|T^{*}_{\eta,b,1}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C\sup\limits_{f\in F,g\in G}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}\leq C<\infty,

which yields the fact that the set
\mathcal{F}
is bounded.

Step II.

\mathcal{F}
satisfies condition

\rm(ii)
.

We adapt the method using in
[
24
]
to verify the condition (ii) of Lemma
2.4
. Assume
b\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\operatorname{supp}(b)\subset B(0,R)
, where
B(0,R)
is the ball of radius
R>1
center at origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any
|x|>N>2R
and
l>0
,
denote

T_{0}(f,g)(x)=\int_{|z|<|x|}\int_{|y|<R}|k_{\eta}(x,y,z)||f(y)||g(z)|dydz,

and for
l\geq 1
,

T_{l}(f,g)(x)=\int_{2^{l-1}|x|<|z|<2^{l}|x|}\int_{|y|<R}|k_{\eta}(x,y,z)||f(y)||g(z)|dydz.

Since
\vec{w}\in A_{\vec{p}}
, we have
w_{1}^{-p_{1}^{\prime}/p_{1}}=w_{1}^{1-p_{1}^{\prime}}\in A_{2p_{1}^{\prime}}\subset A_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, then, by Lemma
2.1
(iii), there exists a constant
\theta\in(0,1)
such that

\int_{B(0,R)}w_{1}^{-p_{1}^{\prime}/p_{1}}(y)dy\leq C(2^{-(j+l)}R/N)^{n\theta}\int_{B(0,2^{l+j}N)}w_{1}^{-p_{1}^{\prime}/p_{1}}(y)dy

holds for any integers
l\geq 0,j\geq 1
. This gives that

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{l}(f,g)(x)&\leq C\int_{2^{l-1}|x|<|z|<2^{l}|x|}\int_{|y|<R}\frac{|f(y)||g(z)|}{(|x|+|x-z|)^{2n}}dydz\\
&\leq C(2^{l-1}|x|)^{-2n}\int_{2^{l-1}|x|<|z|<2^{l}|x|}\int_{|y|<R}|f(y)||g(z)|dydz\\
&\leq C(2^{l-1}|x|)^{-2n}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}^{p_{1}}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}^{p_{2}}\Big(\int_{B(0,R)}w_{1}^{-p_{1}^{\prime}/p_{1}}(y)dy\Big)^{1/p_{1}^{\prime}}\\
&\quad\times\Big(\int_{B(0,2^{l}|x|)}w_{1}^{-p_{2}^{\prime}/p_{2}}(y)dy\Big)^{1/p_{2}^{\prime}}\\
&\leq C(2^{l-1}|x|)^{-2n}(2^{-(j+l)}R/N)^{n\theta/p_{1}^{\prime}}\Big(\int_{B(0,2^{l+j}N)}w_{1}^{-p_{1}^{\prime}/p_{1}}(y)dy\Big)^{1/p_{1}^{\prime}}\\
&\quad\times\Big(\int_{B(0,2^{l}|x|)}w_{1}^{-p_{2}^{\prime}/p_{2}}(y)dy\Big)^{1/p_{2}^{\prime}}.\end{split}

In the same way, we can get

T_{0}(f,g)(x)\leq C|x|^{-2n}(2^{-j}R/N)^{n\theta/p_{1}^{\prime}}\Big(\int_{B(0,2^{j}N)}w_{1}^{-p_{1}^{\prime}/p_{1}}(y)dy\Big)^{1/p_{1}^{\prime}}\Big(\int_{B(0,|x|)}w_{1}^{-p_{2}^{\prime}/p_{2}}(y)dy\Big)^{1/p_{2}^{\prime}}.

Therefore, for
p\geq 1
, by Minkowski’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big(\int_{|x|>N}|T^{*}_{\eta,b,1}(f,g)(x)|^{p}v_{\vec{w}}(x)dx\Big)^{1/p}\\
&\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{2^{j-1}N<|x|<2^{j}N}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\int_{|y|<R}|k_{\eta}(x,y,z)||f(y)||g(z)|dydz\Big)^{p}v_{\vec{w}}(x)dx\Big)^{1/p}\\
&\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{2^{j-i}N<|x|<2^{j}N}|T_{l}(f,g)(x)|^{p}v_{\vec{w}}(x)dx\Big)^{1/p}\\
&\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2^{l+j-2}N)^{-2n}(2^{-(j+l)}R/N)^{n\theta/p_{1}^{\prime}}\Big(\int_{B(0,2^{j}N)}v_{\vec{w}}(x)dx\Big)^{1/p}\\
&\quad\times\Big(\int_{B(0,2^{j+l}N)}w_{1}^{-p_{1}^{\prime}/p_{1}}(y)dy\Big)^{1/p_{1}^{\prime}}\Big(\int_{B(0,2^{j+l}N)}w_{1}^{-p_{2}^{\prime}/p_{2}}(y)dy\Big)^{1/p_{2}^{\prime}}\\
&\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-jn\theta/p_{1}^{\prime}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}2^{-ln\theta/p_{1}^{\prime}}(R/N)^{n\theta/p_{1}^{\prime}}\leq C(R/N)^{n\theta/p_{1}^{\prime}}.\end{split}

When
p<1
, since
(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}a_{l})^{p}\leq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}a_{l}^{p}
, similar to the above estimate, we can obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{|x|>N}|T^{*}_{\eta,b,1}(f,g)(x)|^{p}v_{\vec{w}}(x)dx&\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j-1}N<|x|<2^{j}N}\Big|\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}T_{l}(f,g)(x)\Big|^{p}v_{\vec{w}}(x)dx\\
&\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j-i}N<|x|<2^{j}N}|T_{l}(f,g)(x)|^{p}v_{\vec{w}}(x)dx\\
&\leq C(R/N)^{n\theta p/p_{1}^{\prime}}.\end{split}

Then, for any
1/2<p<\infty
, we have

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\int_{|x|>N}|T^{*}_{\eta,b,1}(f,g)(x)|^{p}v_{\vec{w}}(x)dx=0

holds whenever
f\in F,g\in G
.

Step III.

\mathcal{F}
satisfies condition

\rm(iii)
.

It remains to show that the set
\mathcal{F}
is uniformly equicontinuous.
It suffices to verify that for any
0<\epsilon<1/4
, if
|h|
is sufficiently small and dependents only on
\epsilon
, then

(3.10)

\|T^{*}_{\eta,b,1}(f,g)(h+\cdot)-T^{*}_{\eta,b,1}(f,g)(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C\epsilon,

holds uniformly for
f\in F,g\in G
.

In what follows, we fix
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{16})
and
|h|<\frac{\epsilon\eta}{4}
. Denote
K_{\eta,\delta}(x,y,z)=k_{\eta}(x,y,z)\chi_{\{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta\}}
. Then, we have the following decomposition

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|T^{*}_{\eta,b,1}(f,g)(x+h)-T^{*}_{\eta,b,1}(f,g)(x)|\\
&\leq\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}}K_{\eta,\delta}(x+h,y,z)(b(x+h)-b(y))f(y)g(z)dydz\\
&\quad-\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}}K_{\eta,\delta}(x,y,z)(b(x)-b(y))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&\leq\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}}K_{\eta,\delta}(x,y,z)(b(x+h)-b(x))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&\quad+\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}}(K_{\eta,\delta}(x+h,y,z)-K_{\eta,\delta}(x,y,z))(b(x+h)-b(y))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&=:I_{1}(x)+I_{2}(x).\end{split}

For
I_{1}(x)
, it holds that

\begin{array}[]{ll}&I_{1}(x)=\displaystyle|b(x+h)-b(x)|\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta}K_{\eta}(x,y,z)f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&\qquad\leq C\displaystyle|h|T^{*}_{\eta}(f,g)(x)\end{array}

Then, by the boundedness of
T^{*}_{\eta}(f,g)
, we have

(3.12)

\|I_{1}\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C|h|\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}\leq C|h|\leq C\epsilon.

Next we will estimate
I_{2}(x)
. When
|x-y|+|x-z|<\frac{\eta}{4}
and
|h|<\frac{\epsilon\eta}{4}<\frac{\eta}{16}
, then
|x+h-y|+|x+h-z|<\frac{3\eta}{8}
.
Hence

\varphi(\frac{2}{\eta}(|x+h-y|+|x+h-z|))=1=\varphi(\frac{2}{\eta}(|x-y|+|x-z|)).

This implies

K_{\eta}(x+h,y,z)=0=K_{\eta}(x,y,z).

Then, for
I_{2}(x)
, we decompose it as follows:

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2}(x)&\leq\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\frac{\eta}{4}}(K_{\eta}(x+h,y,z)-K_{\eta}(x,y,z))\chi_{\{|x+h-y|+|x+h-z|>\delta\}}\\
&\quad\times(b(x+h)-b(y))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&\quad+\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\frac{\eta}{4}}K_{\eta}(x,y,z)(\chi_{\{|x+h-y|+|x+h-z|>\delta\}}-\chi_{\{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta\}})\\
&\quad\times(b(x+h)-b(y))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&=:I_{21}(x)+I_{22}(x).\end{split}

For
I_{21}(x)
, When
|x-y|+|x-z|\geq\frac{\eta}{4}
and
|h|<\frac{\epsilon\eta}{4}<\frac{\eta}{16}
, we have
|h|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\{|x-y|,|x-z|\}
. then, using (
3.7
) and splitting into annuli, we obtain

(3.14)

\begin{split}I_{21}(x)&\leq C\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\frac{\eta}{4}}\frac{|h|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}}|f(y)g(z)|dydz\\
&\leq C|h|^{\gamma}\eta^{-\gamma}\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\frac{2^{-j\gamma}}{(\frac{\eta}{4}2^{j})^{2n}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|\sim\frac{\eta}{4}2^{j}}|f(y)g(z)|dydz\\
&\leq C|h|^{\gamma}\eta^{-\gamma}\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\frac{2^{-j\gamma}}{(\frac{\eta}{4}2^{j})^{2n}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{Q(x,\frac{\eta}{4}2^{j})}|f(y)|dy\int_{Q(x,\frac{\eta}{4}2^{j})}|g(z)|dz\\
&\leq C|h|^{\gamma}\eta^{-\gamma}\mathcal{M}(f,g)(x).\end{split}

Then, for any
(f,g)\in{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\times{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}
, by the boundedness of
\mathcal{M}
, we have

\|I_{21}\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C|h|^{\gamma}\eta^{-\gamma}\|\mathcal{M}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C|h|^{\gamma}\eta^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}.

Taking
\gamma=|h|^{1/2}
, we get

(3.15)

\|I_{21}\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C|h|^{\gamma/2}\leq C\epsilon.

For
I_{22}(x)
, we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{22}(x)&\leq\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{{|x-y|+|x-z|>\frac{\eta}{4}\atop|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\delta}\atop|x+h-y|+|x+h-z|>\delta}K_{\eta}(x,y,z)(b(x+h)-b(y))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&\quad+\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{{|x-y|+|x-z|>\frac{\eta}{4}\atop|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta}\atop|x+h-y|+|x+h-z|\leq\delta}K_{\eta}(x,y,z)(b(x+h)-b(y))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&=:I_{22}^{1}(x)+I_{22}^{2}(x).\end{split}

For
I_{22}^{1}(x)
, since
|h|<\frac{\epsilon\eta}{4},0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{4}
and
|x-y|+|x-z|>\frac{\eta}{4},\ |x-y|+|x-z|\leq\delta,\ |x+h-y|+|x+h-z|>\delta
, then
|x-y|+|x-z|>\epsilon^{-1}|h|
and
|x-y|+|x-z|\geq\frac{\delta}{1+2\epsilon}
. Therefore, for any
1<r<\min\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
, by (
3.7
), lemma
2.3
and Hölder inequality, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{22}^{1}(x)&\leq C\|b\|_{\infty}\sup_{\delta>0}\iint_{\frac{\delta}{1+2\epsilon}\leq|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\delta}\frac{|f(y)g(z)|}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n}}dydz\\
&\leq C\sup_{\delta>0}\Big(\iint_{\frac{\delta}{1+2\epsilon}\leq|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\delta}\frac{|f(y)g(z)|^{r}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n}}dydz\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
&\quad\times\Big(\iint_{\frac{\delta}{1+2\epsilon}\leq|y|+|z|\leq\delta}\frac{dydz}{(|y|+|z|)^{2n}}dydz\Big)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq C\sup_{\delta>0}\Big((1+2\epsilon)^{2n}\delta^{-2n}\iint_{\frac{\delta}{1+2\epsilon}\leq|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\delta}|f(y)g(z)|^{r}dydz\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}[1-(1+2\epsilon)^{-n}]^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq C(1+2\epsilon)^{2n}[1-(1+2\epsilon)^{-n}]^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\sup_{\delta>0}\Big(\delta^{-2n}\int_{Q(x,\delta)}|f(y)|^{r}dy\int_{Q(x,\delta)}|g(z)|^{r}dz\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
&\leq C\epsilon^{1/r^{\prime}}[\mathcal{M}(|f|^{r},|g|^{r})(x)]^{\frac{1}{r}}.\end{split}

Notice that
(f,g)\in L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})\times L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})
,
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
, then
(|f|^{r},|g|^{r})\in L^{p_{1}/r}(w_{1})\times L^{p_{2}/r}(w_{2})
,
\frac{r}{p}=\frac{r}{p_{1}}+\frac{r}{p_{2}}
. Thus, the boundedness of
\mathcal{M}
gives that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|I_{22}^{1}\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}&\leq C\epsilon^{1/r^{\prime}}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|\mathcal{M}(|f|^{r},|g|^{r})(x)|^{\frac{p}{r}}v_{\vec{w}}(x)dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&=C\epsilon^{1/r^{\prime}}\|\mathcal{M}(|f|^{r},|g|^{r})\|_{L^{p/r}(v_{\vec{w}})}^{1/r}\\
&\leq C\epsilon^{1/r^{\prime}}\||f|^{r}\|_{L^{p_{1}/r}(w_{1})}^{1/r}\||g|^{r}\|_{L^{p_{2}/r}(w_{2})}^{1/r}\\
&=C\epsilon^{1/r^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}\\
&\leq C\epsilon.\end{split}

For
I_{22}^{2}(x)
, since
|h|<\frac{\epsilon\eta}{4},0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{4}
and
|x-y|+|x-z|>\frac{\eta}{4},\ |x-y|+|x-z|>\delta,\ |x+h-y|+|x+h-z|\leq\delta
, then
|x-y|+|x-z|>\epsilon^{-1}|h|
and
|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\frac{\delta}{1-2\epsilon}
. Then,for any
1<r<\min\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
, by Hölder inequality, (
3.7
) and lemma
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{22}^{2}(x)&\leq C\|b\|_{\infty}\sup_{\delta>0}\iint_{\delta\leq|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\frac{\delta}{1-2\epsilon}}\frac{|f(y)g(z)|}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n}}dydz\\
&\leq C\sup_{\delta>0}\Big(\iint_{\delta\leq|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\frac{\delta}{1-2\epsilon}}\frac{|f(y)g(z)|^{r}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n}}dydz\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
&\quad\times\Big(\iint_{\delta\leq|y|+|z|\leq\frac{\delta}{1-2\epsilon}}\frac{dydz}{(|y|+|z|)^{2n}}dydz\Big)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq C\sup_{\delta>0}\Big(\delta^{-2n}\iint_{\delta\leq|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\frac{\delta}{1-2\epsilon}}|f(y)g(z)|^{r}dydz\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}[(1-2\epsilon)^{-n}-1]^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq C\epsilon^{1/r^{\prime}}[\mathcal{M}(|f|^{r},|g|^{r})(x)]^{\frac{1}{r}}.\end{split}

Similar to (
3.17
), it holds that

(3.18)

\|I_{22}^{2}\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C\epsilon,

which together with (
3.12
), (
3.15
) and (
3.17
) yields (
3.10
). This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We still use smooth truncated techniques to prove Theorem
1.2
. Similarly as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we define

T_{\eta,\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta}K_{\eta}(x,y,z)(b_{1}(x)-b_{1}(y))(b_{2}(x)-b_{2}(y))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|.

Then, for any
\vec{b}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\times\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, it is easy to get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|T_{\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)(x)-T_{\eta,\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)(x)|\\
&\leq\|\nabla b_{1}\|_{\infty}\|\nabla b_{2}\|_{\infty}\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\iint_{\delta<|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\eta}\frac{|x-y||x-z||f(y)g(z)|}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n}}dydz\\
&\leq C\eta^{2}\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-2j}}{(2^{-j-1}\eta)^{2n}}\iint_{2^{-j-1}\eta<|x-y|+|x-z|\leq 2^{-j}\eta}|f(y)g(z)|dydz\\
&\leq C\eta^{2}\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-2j}}{(2^{-j}\eta)^{2n}}\int_{Q(x,2^{-j}\eta)}|f(y)|dy\int_{Q(x,2^{-j}\eta)}|g(z)|dz\\
&\leq C\eta^{2}\mathcal{M}(f,g)(x).\end{split}

Therefore, using the boundedness of
\mathcal{M}
from
L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})\times L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})
to
L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})
, we can obtain

\|T_{\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)-T_{\eta,\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C\eta^{2}\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)},

which implies that

\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}\|T_{\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)-T_{\eta,\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(w)}=0.

On the other hand, if
\vec{b}\in\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\times\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\vec{b}^{\epsilon}=(b_{1}^{\epsilon},b_{2}^{\epsilon})\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\times\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that
\|b_{j}-b_{j}^{\epsilon}\|_{\rm BMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}<\epsilon,\ j=1,2
, so that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|T_{\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)-T_{\vec{b}^{\epsilon}}^{*}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}&\leq\|T_{(b_{1}-b_{1}^{\epsilon},b_{2})}^{*}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}+\|T_{(b_{1},b_{2}-b_{2}^{\epsilon})}^{*}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\\
&\leq C\|b_{2}\|_{\rm BMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\|b_{1}-b_{1}^{\epsilon}\|_{\rm BMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}\\
&\quad+C\|b_{1}\|_{\rm BMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\|b_{2}-b_{2}^{\epsilon}\|_{\rm BMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}\\
&\leq C\epsilon.\end{split}

Thus, to prove
T_{\vec{b}}^{*}
is compact on

L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})
for any
\vec{b}\in\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\times\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, we only need to show that
T_{\eta,\vec{b}}^{*}
is compact for any
\vec{b}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
\eta>0
is small enough. For arbitrary bounded sets
F\subset L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})
and
G\subset L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})
, let

\mathcal{G}=\{T_{\eta,\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g):f\in F,\ g\in G\}.

Then, we shall prove that for any
\vec{b}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\mathcal{G}
satisfies the conditions
\rm(i)
-
\rm(iii)
of Lemma
2.4
.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we can get (i) holds easily.

Assume
b_{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\operatorname{supp}(b_{j})\subset B(0,R)
,
j=1,2
, where
B(0,R)
is the ball of radius
R>1
center at origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any
|x|>N>2R
,
\vec{w}\in A_{\vec{p}}
, by Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{\eta,\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)(x)&\leq C\int_{B(0,R)}\int_{B(0,R)}\frac{|b_{1}(y)|b_{2}(z)|}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n}}|f(y)||g(z)|dydz\\
&\leq C\|b_{1}\|_{\infty}\|b_{2}\|_{\infty}|x|^{-2n}\int_{B(0,R)}\int_{B(0,R)}|f(y)||g(z)|dydz\\
&\leq C|x|^{-2n}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}\Big(\int_{B(0,R)}w_{1}^{-p_{1}^{\prime}/p_{1}}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}\Big(\int_{B(0,R)}w_{1}^{-p_{2}^{\prime}/p_{2}}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

Thus, it follows that

\int_{|x|>N}|T_{\eta,\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)(x)|^{p}v_{\vec{w}}(x)dx\leq C\int_{|x|>N}\frac{v_{\vec{w}}(x)}{|x|^{2np}}dx,

Since
\vec{w}\in A_{\vec{p}}
,
\frac{1}{2}<p<\infty
, then
1<2p<\infty
and
v_{\vec{w}}\in A_{2p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
(see
[
21
]
), which together with Lemma
2.1
(ii) yields that

\lim\limits_{A\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{|x|>A}|T_{\eta,\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)(x)|^{p}v_{\vec{w}}(x)dx=0,

whenever
f\in F
and
g\in G
.

It remains to show that the set
\mathcal{F}
is uniformly equicontinuous.
It suffices to verify that for any
0<\epsilon<1/4
, if
|h|
is sufficiently small and dependent only on
\epsilon
, then

(3.19)

\|T_{\eta,\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)(h+\cdot)-T_{\eta,\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C\epsilon,

holds uniformly for
f\in F,g\in G
.

Fix
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{16})
and
|h|<\frac{\epsilon\eta}{4}
. Denote
K_{\eta,\delta}(x,y,z)=K_{\eta}(x,y,z)\chi_{\{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta\}}
and
a(x,y,z)=(b_{1}(x)-b_{1}(y))(b_{2}(x)-b_{2}(y))
,
a_{h}(x,y,z)=a(x+h,y,z)-a(x,y,z)
. Then

(3.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|T_{\eta,\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)(x+h)-T_{\eta,\vec{b}}^{*}(f,g)(x)|\\
&\leq\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}}K_{\eta,\delta}(x+h,y,z)a(x+h,y,z)f(y)g(z)dydz\\
&\quad-\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}}K_{\eta,\delta}(x,y,z)a(x,y,z)f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&\leq\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}}K_{\eta,\delta}(x,y,z)a_{h}(x,y,z)f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&\quad+\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}}(K_{\eta,\delta}(x+h,y,z)-K_{\eta,\delta}(x,y,z))a(x+h,y,z)f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&=:J_{1}(x)+J_{2}(x).\end{split}

For
J_{1}(x)
, notice that

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{h}(x,y,z)=&(b_{1}(x+h)-b_{1}(x))(b_{2}(x+h)-b_{2}(x))+(b_{1}(x+h)-b_{1}(x))(b_{2}(x)-b_{2}(z))\\
&+(b_{1}(x)-b_{1}(y))(b_{2}(x+h)-b_{2}(x)).\end{split}

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}J_{1}(x)&\leq\displaystyle|h|^{2}\|\nabla b_{1}\|_{\infty}\|\nabla b_{2}\|_{\infty}\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta}K_{\eta}(x,y,z)f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&\quad+|h|\|\nabla b_{1}\|_{\infty}\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta}K_{\eta}(x,y,z)(b_{2}(x)-b_{2}(z))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&\quad+|h|\|\nabla b_{2}\|_{\infty}\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta}K_{\eta}(x,y,z)(b_{1}(x)-b_{1}(y))f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&\leq C|h|^{2}T_{\eta}^{*}(f,g)(x)+C|h|T_{\eta,b_{2},2}^{*}(f,g)(x)+C|h|T_{\eta,b_{1},1}^{*}(f,g)(x).\end{split}

By the weighted boundedness of
T^{*}_{\eta}(f,g)
and
T_{\eta,b_{j},j}^{*},\ j=1,2
, we have

(3.21)

\|J_{1}\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C(|h|^{2}+|h|)\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}.

For
J_{2}(x)
. When
|x-y|+|x-z|<\frac{\eta}{4}
and
|h|<\frac{\epsilon\eta}{4}<\frac{\eta}{16}
, then
|x+h-y|+|x+h-z|<\frac{3\eta}{8}
.
Hence

\varphi(\frac{2}{\eta}(|x+h-y|+|x+h-z|))=1=\varphi(\frac{2}{\eta}(|x-y|+|x-z|)).

This implies

K_{\eta}(x+h,y,z)=K_{\eta}(x,y,z)=0.

Then, we decompose it as follows:

(3.22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}J_{2}(x)&\leq\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\frac{\eta}{4}}(K_{\eta}(x+h,y,z)-K_{\eta}(x,y,z))\chi_{\{|x+h-y|+|x+h-z|>\delta\}}\\
&\quad\times a(x+h,y,z)f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&\quad+\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\frac{\eta}{4}}K_{\eta}(x,y,z)(\chi_{\{|x+h-y|+|x+h-z|>\delta\}}-\chi_{\{|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta\}})\\
&\quad\times a(x+h,y,z)f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&=:J_{21}(x)+J_{22}(x).\end{split}

For
J_{21}(x)
, when
|x-y|+|x-z|\geq\frac{\eta}{4}
and
|h|<\frac{\epsilon\eta}{4}<\frac{\eta}{16}
, we have
|h|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\{|x-y|,|x-z|\}
. Then, using (
3.7
) and splitting into annuli, we obtain

\begin{split}J_{21}(x)&\leq C\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|>\frac{\eta}{4}}\frac{|h|^{\gamma}}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n+\gamma}}|f(y)g(z)|dydz\\
&\leq C|h|^{\gamma}\eta^{-\gamma}\displaystyle\sup_{\delta>0}\frac{2^{-j\gamma}}{(\frac{\eta}{4}2^{j})^{2n}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\iint_{|x-y|+|x-z|\sim\frac{\eta}{4}2^{j}}|f(y)g(z)|dydz\\
&\leq C|h|^{\gamma}\eta^{-\gamma}\mathcal{M}(f,g)(x).\end{split}

Then, for any
(f,g)\in{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\times{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}
, by the boundedness of
\mathcal{M}
, we have

\|J_{21}\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C|h|^{\gamma}\eta^{-\gamma}\|\mathcal{M}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C|h|^{\gamma}\eta^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(w_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(w_{2})}.

Taking
\gamma=|h|^{1/2}
, we get

(3.23)

\|J_{21}\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C|h|^{\gamma/2}\leq C\epsilon.

For
J_{22}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{22}(x)&\leq\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{{|x-y|+|x-z|>\frac{\eta}{4}\atop|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\delta}\atop|x+h-y|+|x+h-z|>\delta}K_{\eta}(x,y,z)a(x+h,y,z)f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&\quad+\sup_{\delta>0}\Big|\iint_{{|x-y|+|x-z|>\frac{\eta}{4}\atop|x-y|+|x-z|>\delta}\atop|x+h-y|+|x+h-z|\leq\delta}K_{\eta}(x,y,z)a(x+h,y,z)f(y)g(z)dydz\Big|\\
&=:J_{22}^{1}(x)+J_{22}^{2}(x).\end{split}

Analogous to the estimates of
I_{22}^{1}(x)
and
I_{22}^{2}(x)
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}J_{22}^{1}(x)&\leq C\|b_{1}\|_{\infty}\|b_{2}\|_{\infty}\sup_{\delta>0}\iint_{\frac{\delta}{1+2\epsilon}\leq|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\delta}\frac{|f(y)g(z)|}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n}}dydz\\
&\leq C\sup_{\delta>0}\Big((1+2\epsilon)^{2n}\delta^{-2n}\iint_{\frac{\delta}{1+2\epsilon}\leq|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\delta}|f(y)g(z)|^{r}dydz\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}[1-(1+2\epsilon)^{-n}]^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq C\epsilon^{1/r^{\prime}}[\mathcal{M}(|f|^{r},|g|^{r})(x)]^{\frac{1}{r}}.\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}J_{22}^{2}(x)&\leq C\|b_{1}\|_{\infty}\|b_{2}\|_{\infty}\sup_{\delta>0}\iint_{\delta\leq|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\frac{\delta}{1-2\epsilon}}\frac{|f(y)g(z)|}{(|x-y|+|x-z|)^{2n}}dydz\\
&\leq C\sup_{\delta>0}\Big(\delta^{-2n}\iint_{\delta\leq|x-y|+|x-z|\leq\frac{\delta}{1-2\epsilon}}|f(y)g(z)|^{r}dydz\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}[(1-2\epsilon)^{-n}-1]^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq C\epsilon^{1/r^{\prime}}[\mathcal{M}(|f|^{r},|g|^{r})(x)]^{\frac{1}{r}}.\end{split}

Therefore, we have

(3.24)

\|J_{22}^{1}\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C\epsilon,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ |J_{22}^{2}\|_{L^{p}(v_{\vec{w}})}\leq C\epsilon,

which together with (
3.21
) and (
3.23
) yields (
3.19
) and completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

∎

Efficient Concentration with Gaussian Approximation
Morgane Austern label=e1]morgane.austern@gmail.com, Lester Mackey label=e2]lmackey@microsoft.com

Abstract
Concentration inequalities for the sample mean, like those due to Bernstein and Hoeffding, are valid for any sample size but overly conservative, yielding confidence intervals that are unnecessarily wide. The central limit theorem (CLT) provides asymptotic confidence intervals with optimal width, but these are invalid for all sample sizes. To resolve this tension, we develop new computable concentration inequalities with asymptotically optimal size, finite-sample validity, and sub-Gaussian decay. These bounds enable the construction of efficient confidence intervals with correct coverage for any sample size and efficient empirical Berry-Esseen bounds that require no prior knowledge of the population variance. We derive our inequalities by tightly bounding non-uniform Kolmogorov and Wasserstein distances to a Gaussian using zero-bias couplings and Stein’s method of exchangeable pairs.

Introduction

Concentration inequalities for the sample mean are ubiquitous in probability theory, statistics, and machine learning. Given
n
independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) observations
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}
they allow us to give finite sample and high probability guarantees that the sample mean
\bar{W}_{n}\triangleq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}
is not too far away from the population mean
\mathbb{E}(W_{1})
. More specifically, they provide upper bounds for the probability
P\big(\bar{W}_{n}-\mathbb{E}(W_{1})>t/\sqrt{n}\big)
for each
t\geq 0
.
Such inequalities lie at the heart of decision-making in reinforcement learning
[
audibert2009exploration
]
,
generalization guarantees in high-dimensional statistics, machine learning, and deep learning
[
wainwright2019high
,
bartlett2002rademacher
,
zhou2018non
]
, and
the design
[
maurer2009empirical
]
and selection
[
mnih2008empirical
]
of efficient learning procedures.

However, standard concentration inequalities are overly conservative yielding confidence intervals that are unnecessarily wide and generalization guarantees that are weaker than needed. This is notably the case for the commonly used concentration inequalities of
hoeffding1963probability
and
bernstein1924modification
.
For bounded random variables
W_{i}\in[0,R]
with variance
\sigma^{2}\triangleq\mathrm{Var}(W_{i})
, the Hoeffding and Bernstein inequalities respectively state that

\textstyle\mathbb{P}\big(\bar{W}_{n}-\mathbb{E}(W_{1})>\sigma u/\sqrt{n}\big)

\textstyle\leq\exp({-\frac{2u^{2}\sigma^{2}}{R^{2}}})\quad\text{and}\quad

(2)

\textstyle\mathbb{P}\big(\bar{W}_{n}-\mathbb{E}(W_{1})>\sigma u/\sqrt{n}\big)

\textstyle\leq\exp\big({-\frac{u^{2}}{2(1+Ru/(3\sigma\sqrt{n}))}}\big).

(3)

Meanwhile, the central limit theorem (CLT) identifies the exact limit for each tail probability:

\textstyle\mathbb{P}\big(\bar{W}_{n}-\mathbb{E}(W_{1})>\sigma u/\sqrt{n}\big)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}\Phi^{c}(u),

(4)

where
\Phi
is the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of a standard normal distribution and
\Phi^{c}=1-\Phi
.
As a result, standard confidence intervals based on the CLT are asymptotically exact and often much narrower than those obtained using concentration inequalities. However, these intervals are typically only asymptotically valid and provide incorrect coverage for every sample size
n
.

The choice between loose but valid concentration inequalities and tight but invalid CLT-based approximations is very unsatisfying. In this paper we derive new bounds that offer the best of both worlds: our new concentration inequalities are both finite-sample valid and
efficient
—that is, asymptotically of minimal width when scaled by
\sqrt{n}
.
For example, our primary result,
Theorem

1

implies that

\textstyle\mathbb{P}(\bar{W}_{n}-\mathbb{E}(W_{1})>\sigma u/\sqrt{n})\leq\Phi^{c}(u)+\frac{c_{1}R}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\exp(-c_{2}\frac{u^{2}\sigma^{2}}{R^{2}})

Underlying this implication is a new, computable concentration inequality formed by explicitly bounding a non-uniform Kolmogorov distance between the sample mean and a Gaussian.

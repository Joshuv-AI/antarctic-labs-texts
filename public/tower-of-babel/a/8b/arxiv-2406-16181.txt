Constants of motion and quantum non-relativistic motion of a charged particle on a flat surface with transversal magnetic field
Gustavo V. López, Jorge A. Lizarraga

Abstract
The motion of a charged particle moving on a flat surface is studied through the constants of motion associated to the system, given the magnetic gauge. The usual Landau’ solution and the non separable solution for the Landau’s gauge are found, and new non separable solution for the symmetric gauge is given. As a consequence of this, the quantization of the magnetic flux results from the invariance of the solutions under the unitary transformations which arise from the operators constants of motion.

The finding of a non separable solution of the problem has opened up a different way to focus the problem

We study the properties of the quantum system resulting from the classical Hamiltonian with a constant magnetic field based on the conserved operators to described the main characteristics of the system.

We are interested in the Hamiltonian, which classically is written in CGS units as

H=\frac{1}{2m}\left({\bf P}-\frac{q}{c}{\bf A}\right)^{2},

(1)

where
m
is the mass of the particle,
q
is the charge of the particle,
c
is the speed of light,
{\bf P}=(p_{x},p_{y},p_{z})
is the momentum of the particle and
{\bf A}=(A_{x},A_{y},A_{z})
is the vector potential (also known as gauge) such that the magnetic field is given by
{\bf B}=\nabla\times{\bf A}
. We set our magnetic field such that is parallel to the
z
axis,
{\bf B}=B{\bf\hat{k}}
, where
B
is constant. From now on we are only interested in the dynamics of the particle in the
x-y
plane, therefore we set
p_{z}=0
and
A_{z}=0
.
Let us first use Landau’s gauge defined as
{\bf A}_{L}=B(-y,0,0)
, thus, defining the cyclotron frequency
\omega_{c}=qB/mc
, the Hamiltonian (
1
) is written as

H_{L}=\frac{1}{2m}\left(p_{x}+m\omega_{c}y\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2m}p_{y}^{2},

(2)

and the Hamiltonian dynamical system is

\frac{dx}{dt}=\frac{1}{m}(p_{x}+m\omega_{c}y)\quad\quad\frac{dp_{x}}{dt}=0,

(3)

\frac{dy}{dt}=\frac{p_{y}}{m}\quad\quad\quad\frac{dp_{y}}{dt}=-\omega_{c}(p_{x}+m\omega_{c}y),

(4)

which has the following conserved quantities (constants of motion)

p_{x},\quad\quad p_{y}+m\omega_{c}x,\quad\hbox{and}\quad H_{L}.

(5)

This Hamiltonian system generates the equations of motion

m\ddot{x}=m\omega_{c}\dot{y},\quad\quad\hbox{and}\quad\quad m\ddot{y}=-m\omega_{c}\dot{x},

(6)

which are the same equations obtained by the Lorentz force,
m\ddot{\bf x}=\frac{q}{c}({\bf v}\times{\bf B})
, as expected.
Their solutions describe circular trajectories on the
x-y
plane whose radius is
r=v/\omega_{c}
, were
v=\sqrt{v_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2}}
is the speed of the charged particle.

As it is well known, the Hamiltonian depends explicitly on the selection of the gauge. For instance, if one uses the symmetric gauge instead of Landau’s gauge,
{\bf A}_{S}=\frac{B}{2}(-y,x,0)
, the Hamiltonian is written now as

H_{S}=\frac{1}{2m}\left(p_{x}+\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}y\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2m}\left(p_{y}-\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}x\right)^{2},

(7)

and the Hamiltonian dynamical system is now given by

\frac{dx}{dt}=\frac{1}{m}\left(p_{x}+\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}y\right),\quad\frac{dp_{x}}{dt}=+\frac{\omega_{c}}{2}\left(p_{y}-\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}x\right)

(8)

\frac{dy}{dt}=\frac{1}{m}\left(p_{y}-\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}x\right),\quad\frac{dp_{y}}{dt}=-\frac{\omega_{c}}{2}\left(p_{x}+\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}y\right).

(9)

resulting the following conserved quantities (constants of motion)

p_{x}-\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}y,\quad\quad p_{y}+\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}x,\quad\quad\hbox{and}\quad H_{S}.

(10)

It is not difficult to see that one gets the same equation of motion (
6
) for the charged particle motion evolution.

II
Quantum description of a charged particle under a constant magnetic field using the Landau’s gauge

To study the quantum dynamics, we asigne to the momentum variables the Hermitian operators
\hat{p}_{k}=-i\hbar\partial/\partial x_{k}
with the conmutator relation
[{\hat{x}}_{a},{\hat{p}}_{b}]=i\hbar\delta_{a,b}
and
[{\hat{p}}_{a},{\hat{p}}_{b}]=0
(where
{\hat{x}}_{1}={\hat{x}}
,
{\hat{x}}_{2}={\hat{y}}
,
{\hat{p}}_{1}={\hat{p}}_{x}
and
{\hat{p}}_{2}={\hat{p}}_{y}
), and solve the Schrödinger equation

i\hbar\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}=\widehat{H}_{L}({\bf x},{\bf\hat{p}})\Psi,

(11)

with the Hermitian Hamiltonian operator for the Landau’s gauge (
2
)

{\hat{H}}_{L}=\frac{1}{2m}\left({\hat{p}}_{x}+m\omega_{c}{\hat{y}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2m}{\hat{p}}_{y}^{2}.

(12)

In addition, the evolution of any operator
\hat{f}
can be seen in Heisenberg picture as

i\hbar\frac{d{\hat{f}}}{dt}=[{\hat{f}},{\hat{H}}]+i\hbar\frac{\partial{\hat{f}}}{\partial t}.

(13)

Using (
13
) one can see that the operators associated to the constant of motion (
5
)
are also constant of motion for the quantum dynamics. Since they are time independent, one gets

[{\hat{p}}_{x},\widehat{H}_{L}]=0,

(14)

[{\hat{p}}_{y}+m\omega_{c}{\hat{x}},\widehat{H}_{L}]=0,

(15)

and, of course,

[{\widehat{H}}_{L},\widehat{H}_{L}]=0.

(16)

It is well known that (
14
) is used to solve the Schröndinger’s equation and to have the so called Landau’ solution of the problem. However, the second conserved operator, (
15
) has been ignored so far
Landau
, and we will show that both of them are needed to understand properly the system. It is not difficult to see that the analogous of
(
6
) is gotten for the operators,

\frac{d^{2}{\hat{x}}}{dt^{2}}=m\omega_{c}\frac{d{\hat{y}}}{dt},\quad\hbox{and}\quad m\frac{d^{2}{\hat{y}}}{dt^{2}}=-m\omega_{c}\frac{d{\hat{x}}}{dt},

(17)

Since the Hamiltonian (
12
) is time independent, we can propose on (
11
) a solution of the form

\Psi(x,y,t)=exp(-iEt/\hbar)\Phi(x,y)

(18)

which brings about the eigenvalue problem

\widehat{H}_{L}\Phi=E\Phi.

(19)

We can also use the constants of motion (
14
) and (
15
) to build up a couple of eigenvalue equations

{\hat{p}}_{x}\phi=-\lambda_{1}\phi,

(20)

({\hat{p}}_{y}+m\omega_{c}{\hat{x}})\overline{\phi}=\lambda_{2}\overline{\phi}.

(21)

Having the solutions

\phi(x,y)=\psi_{1}(y)e^{-i\lambda_{1}x/\hbar}

(22)

and

\overline{\phi}(x,y)=\psi_{2}(x)e^{i(\lambda_{2}-m\omega_{c}x)y/\hbar}.

(23)

Substituting (
22
) in (
19
), the resulting equation for
\psi_{1}
is

\left[\frac{\hat{p}^{2}_{\xi}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{c}^{2}\xi^{2}\right]\psi_{1}=E\psi_{1},

(24)

where
\xi=\lambda_{1}/m\omega_{c}+y
, obtaining the usual Landau’ solution

\Phi_{n}^{(1)}(x,y,\lambda_{1})=e^{-i\lambda_{1}x/\hbar}\psi_{1}^{(n)}\left(\sqrt{\frac{m\omega_{c}}{\hbar}}(\lambda_{1}/m\omega_{c}+y)\right),

(25)

with the eigenvalues, so called Landau’s levels,

E_{n}=\hbar\omega_{c}(n+1/2).

(26)

The function
\psi_{1}^{(n)}
is the normalized harmonic oscillator defined as

f_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}n!}}\left(\frac{m\omega_{c}}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}e^{-\frac{1}{2}z^{2}}H_{n}(z),

(27)

where
H_{n}(z)
are the Hermite polynomials.

In addition, using (
23
) in (
19
), the resulting equation for
\psi_{2}
is

\left[\frac{\hat{p}^{2}_{\sigma}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{c}^{2}\sigma^{2}\right]\psi_{2}=E\psi_{2},

(28)

where
\sigma=\lambda_{2}/m\omega_{c}-x
, and the solution is given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}^{(2)}(x,y,\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i(\lambda_{2}-m\omega_{c}x)y/\hbar}\times

(29)

\displaystyle\psi_{2}^{(n)}\left(\sqrt{\frac{m\omega_{c}}{\hbar}}(\lambda_{2}/m\omega_{c}-x)\right),

where
\psi_{2}^{(n)}
being the harmonic oscillator solution (
27
), and with the same Landau’s levels (
26
) as eigenvalues.
We want to point out that the wave function (
29
) is the displaced solution found on reference
lop2
. Therefore,
the general solution of this system would be written as

\Psi_{L}(x,y,t)=\sum_{n}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}E_{n}t}\left(a_{n}\Phi_{n}^{(1)}(x,y,\lambda_{1})+b_{n}\Phi_{n}^{(2)}(x,y,\lambda_{2})\right),

(30)

where
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
are complex numbers. However, one need additional step to consider the degeneration
of the system, and to do this, let us point out some symmetries of the system which are stablished by the constant of motion above.

As one could expected, these displaced functions (
25
) and (
29
) can be written as

\Phi_{n}^{(1)}(x,y,\lambda_{1})=\widehat{U}_{\lambda_{1}}\Phi_{n}^{(1)}(x,y,0)

(31)

and

\Phi_{n}^{(2)}(x,y,\lambda_{2})=\widehat{U}_{\lambda_{2}}\Phi_{n}^{(2)}(x,y,0),

(32)

where the unitary operators
\widehat{U}_{\lambda_{1}}
and
\widehat{U}_{\lambda_{2}}
are defined as

\widehat{U}_{\lambda_{1}}=e^{-i(\lambda_{1}/\hbar m\omega_{c})(\hat{p}_{y}+m\omega_{c}x)}

(33)

and

\widehat{U}_{\lambda_{2}}=e^{-i(\lambda_{2}/\hbar m\omega_{c})\hat{p}_{y}}.

(34)

Because of the expressions (
14
) and (
15
), these operators commuted with the Hamiltonian and are elements of the group of symmetries
of the Hamiltonian. In addition, it follows that

\widehat{H}_{L}(\hat{p}_{y}+m\omega_{c}x)\Phi_{n}^{(1)}=E_{n}(\hat{p}_{y}+m\omega_{c}x)\Phi_{n}^{(1)},

(35)

and

\widehat{H}_{L}\hat{p}_{x}\Phi_{n}^{(1)}=E_{n}\hat{p}_{x}\Phi_{n}^{(2)}

(36)

that is, the application of the operators
\hat{\pi}_{y}=\hat{p}_{y}+m\omega_{c}x
and
\hat{\pi}_{x}=\hat{p}_{x}
on their respective functions, generates new eigenfunctions. Defining these
functions as

f_{n}^{j}=\hat{\pi}_{y}^{j}\Phi_{n}^{(1)}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\tilde{f}_{n}^{j}=\hat{\pi}_{x}^{j}\Phi_{n}^{(2)},

(37)

where the index ”j” means the jth-application of the operator, and as we have just said

\widehat{H}_{L}f_{n}^{j}=E_{n}f_{n}^{j}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\widehat{H}_{L}\tilde{f}_{n}^{j}=E_{n}\tilde{f}_{n}^{j}.

(38)

This means that the most general solution is given by

\Psi_{L}(x,y,t)=\sum_{n,j}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}E_{n}t}\left(a_{nj}f_{n}^{j}+b_{nj}\tilde{f}_{n}^{j}\right),

(39)

where
a_{nj}
and
b_{nj}
are complex constants. If these constants are chosen as

a_{nj}=a_{n}\frac{1}{j!}\frac{\lambda_{1}^{j}}{(i\hbar)^{j}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad b_{nj}=b_{n}\frac{1}{j!}\frac{\lambda_{2}^{j}}{(i\hbar)^{j}},

(40)

it happens that

\sum_{j}a_{nj}f_{n}^{j}=a_{n}\widehat{U}_{\lambda_{1}}\Phi_{n}^{(1)}(x,y,0)

(41)

and

\sum_{j}b_{nj}\tilde{f}_{n}^{j}=b_{n}\widehat{U}_{\lambda_{2}}\Phi_{n}^{(2)}(x,y,0),

(42)

which reproduces the solution (
30
).
So, one sees that the displaced solutions can be seen as a consequence of the degeneration of the system.
Of course, different selection of the constants (
40
) will result on different expression for (
41
) and (
42
). In addition,
defining the functions

\Psi_{1}(x,y,t)=\sum_{n,j}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}E_{n}t}a_{nj}f_{n}^{j}

(43)

and

\Psi_{2}(x,y,t)=\sum_{n,j}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}E_{n}t}b_{nj}\tilde{f}_{n}^{j}

(44)

with the constants defined as (
40
), one can see the following effect for application of the unitary operators on these functions

\widehat{U}_{\lambda_{2}}\Psi_{1}=e^{+i(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}/m\omega_{c}\hbar)}\Psi_{1},

(45)

and

\widehat{U}_{\lambda_{1}}\Psi_{2}=e^{-i(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}/m\omega_{c}\hbar)}\Psi_{2},

(46)

which means that if
\Psi_{1}
and
\Psi_{2}
are invariant under these unitary transformations, the following relation must be satisfied

\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{m\omega_{c}\hbar}=2\pi k,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},

(47)

implying the quantization of the magnetic flux since
\omega_{c}=qB/mc
. Note that the units of
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are the same
units of the generalized linear momentun (
gr\cdot cm/sec
). Therefore, in this case it is possible to propose
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
of the form

\lambda_{1}=m\omega_{c}l_{1}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\lambda_{2}=m\omega_{c}l_{2},

(48)

where
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
have units of length. In this way, (
47
) would take the familiar form

\frac{m\omega_{c}}{\hbar}l_{1}l_{2}=2\pi k,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

(49)

Since the magnetic field is given (and therefore
\omega_{c}
), this expression suggests some type of quantization of the area defined by
l_{1}l_{2}
, see references
lop3
.

III
Quantum description of a charged particle under a constant magnetic field using the symmetric gauge

For the symmetric gauge,
{\bf A}_{S}=B(-y,x,0)/2
, the Hamiltonian (
7
) is

{\hat{H}}_{S}=\frac{1}{2m}\left({\hat{p}}_{x}+\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}{\hat{y}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2m}\left({\hat{p}}_{y}-\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}{\hat{x}}\right)^{2}.

(50)

The proposition(
18
) reduced the study to solve an eigenvalue problem

\widehat{H}_{S}\Phi=E\Phi,

(51)

and using (
13
) it follows that

[{\hat{p}}_{x}-\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}{\hat{y}},{\hat{H}}_{S}]=0,

(52)

[\hat{p}_{y}+\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}{\hat{x}},{\hat{H}}_{S}]=0,

(53)

and, of course,

[{\hat{H}}_{S},{\hat{H}}_{S}]=0.

(54)

These expressions are the operator equivalent expressions of the classical constants of motion (
10
). Let us consider the eigenvalue problem for the operators (
52
) and
(
53
),

({\hat{p}}_{x}-\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}{\hat{y}})\varphi_{1}=\gamma_{1}\varphi_{1}

(55)

and

(\hat{p}_{y}+\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}{\hat{x}})\varphi_{2}=\gamma_{2}\varphi_{2},

(56)

which have the solutions

\varphi_{1}=\tilde{\psi}_{1}(y)e^{i(\gamma_{1}+m\omega_{c}y/2)x/\hbar}

(57)

and

\varphi_{2}=\tilde{\psi}_{2}(x)e^{i(\gamma_{2}-m\omega_{c}x/2)y/\hbar}

(58)

Substituting (
57
) and (
58
) in (
51
) one gets the following equation for
\tilde{\psi}_{1}
and
\tilde{\psi}_{2}

\frac{\hat{p}_{y}^{2}}{2m}\tilde{\psi_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{c}^{2}\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}}{m\omega_{c}}+y\right)^{2}\tilde{\psi_{1}}=E\tilde{\psi_{1}}

(59)

and

\frac{\hat{p}_{x}^{2}}{2m}\tilde{\psi_{2}}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{c}^{2}\left(\frac{\gamma_{2}}{m\omega_{c}}-x\right)^{2}\tilde{\psi_{2}}=E\tilde{\psi_{2}}

(60)

which represent equations for the shifted quantum harmonic oscillator. Therefore, there are two independent solutions of the eigenvalue problem (
51
)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}^{(1)}(x,y,\gamma_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i(\gamma_{1}+m\omega_{c}y/2)x/\hbar}\times

(61)

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{(n)}\left(\sqrt{\frac{m\omega_{c}}{\hbar}}(\gamma_{1}/m\omega_{c}+y)\right)

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}^{(2)}(x,y,\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i(\gamma_{2}-m\omega_{c}x/2)y/\hbar}\times

(62)

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{(n)}\left(\sqrt{\frac{m\omega_{c}}{\hbar}}(\gamma_{2}/m\omega_{c}-x)\right),

where
\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{(n)}
and
\tilde{\psi}_{2}^{(n)}
are the shifted functions (
27
), and their eigenvalues are the same Landau’s levels (
26
). We also have that these
shifted solution can be expressed through the unitary operator

\widehat{U}_{\gamma_{1}}=e^{-i(\gamma_{1}/\hbar m\omega_{c})(\hat{p}_{x}-m\omega_{c}y/2)}

(63)

and

\widehat{U}_{\gamma_{2}}=e^{-i(\gamma_{2}/\hbar m\omega_{c})(\hat{p}_{y}+m\omega_{c}x/2)}

(64)

as

\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}^{(1)}(x,y,\gamma_{1})=\widehat{U}_{\gamma_{2}}\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}^{(1)}(x,y,0)

(65)

and

\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}^{(2)}(x,y,\gamma_{2})=\widehat{U}_{\gamma_{1}}\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}^{(2)}(x,y,0)

(66)

These unitary operators commute with the Hamiltonian and reflex the symmetries of the Hamiltonian. Thus,
the general solution of this system, without the consideration of the degeneration, would be written as

\Psi_{S}(x,y,t)=\sum_{n}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}E_{n}t}\left(\tilde{a}_{n}\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}^{(1)}(x,y,\gamma_{1})+\tilde{b}_{n}\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}^{(2)}(x,y,\gamma_{2})\right).

(67)

To see the degeneration of the system, it is not difficult to see that due to commutation relations (
52
) and (
53
) one has

\widehat{H}_{S}\left({\hat{p}}_{x}-\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}y\right)\widetilde{\Phi}_{2}^{(n)}=E_{n}\left({\hat{p}}_{x}-\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}y\right)\widetilde{\Phi}_{2}^{(n)}

(68)

and

\widehat{H}_{S}\left({\hat{p}}_{y}+\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}x\right)\widetilde{\Phi}_{1}^{(n)}=E_{n}\left({\hat{p}}_{y}+\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}x\right)\widetilde{\Phi}_{1}^{(n)}

(69)

that is, the application of the operator
\tilde{\pi}_{1}=({\hat{p}}_{x}-\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}y)
on the eigenfunction
\widetilde{\Phi}_{2}^{(n)}
and the application of the operator

\tilde{\pi}_{2}=({\hat{p}}_{y}+\frac{m\omega_{c}}{2}x)
on the eigenfunction
\widetilde{\Phi}_{1}^{(n)}
generate new eigenfunctions. In this way, one can generate a numerable set
of eigenfunctions through the applications of these operators,

g_{n}^{j}=\tilde{\pi}_{2}^{j}\widetilde{\Phi}_{1}^{(n)},\quad\hbox{and}\quad\tilde{g}_{n}^{j}=\tilde{\pi}_{1}^{j}\widetilde{\Phi}_{2}^{(n)},

(70)

getting now the general solution of the form

\Psi_{S}(x,y,t)=\sum_{n,j}e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}E_{n}t}\left(c_{nj}g_{n}^{j}+d_{nj}\tilde{g}_{n}^{j}\right),

(71)

where
c_{nj}
and
d_{nj}
are complex number. Of course, one could select these complex coefficients as was done in previous case
and to make the addition with respect the degeneration index ”j” to get solution (
67
), and different selection will bring about different expressions. On the other hand,
one must note that due in general
\lambda_{1}\not=\gamma_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}\not=\gamma_{2}
. Thus,
the solution
\Psi_{L}
( with the Landau’s gauge
{\bf A}=B(-y,0,0)

Similarly to previous case, asking for the invariance of the functions, formed from (
71

or

, and applying
the respective unitary transformations (
64
) and (
63
), one would get the same phase, and their invariance of these function under the unitary transformation
would lead us to a similar expression (

\frac{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}{m\omega_{c}\hbar}=2\pi k^{\prime},\quad k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z},

and defining the constant

and

\gamma_{1}=m\omega_{c}l_{1}^{\prime}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\gamma_{2}=m\omega_{c}l_{2}^{\prime},

(73)

having

) is gotten

\frac{m\omega_{c}}{\hbar}l_{1}^{\prime}l_{2}^{\prime}=2\pi k^{\prime},\quad k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z},

where the quantized areas

IV
Conclusions and Comments

We have studied the motion of a charged particle moving on a flat surface through the constants of motion associated to the system, and we have done this study
with the Landau’s gauge and the symmetric gauge for the constant magnetic field. The usual Landau’ solution and the non separable solution for the Landau’s gauge were found, and a new
non separable solution for the symmetric gauge was given. We also saw that, as a consequence of this, the quantization of the magnetic flux results from the invariance
of the general solution under the unitary transformation which are built from the constant of motion operators, implying also the quantization some the area covered by the magnetic field.
We considere that the meaning of this resulting area quantization
lop3
still needs to have further explanation and experimental verification. This invariance of the independent solution
allows us to see the FQHF since the Hall resistivity is given by
\rho_{{}_{H}}=(\hbar/q^{2})(m\omega_{c}l_{1}l_{2}/\hbar)
, but it does not allow us to see the IQHE, seen reference
lop3
.

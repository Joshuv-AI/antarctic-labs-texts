CP conditions for GKSL-like master equations
Akane Watanabe, Takayuki Suzuki, Makoto Unoki, Hiromichi Nakazato

Abstract
The complete positivity (CP) of a quantum dynamical map (QDM) is, in general, difficult to show when its master equation (ME) does not conform to the Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudarshan-Lindblad (GKSL) form. The GKSL ME describes the Markovian dynamics, comprising a unitary component with time-independent Hermitian operators and a non-unitary component with time-independent Lindblad operators and positive time-independent damping rates. Recently, the non-Markovian dynamics has received growing attention, and the various types of GKSL-like MEs with time-dependent operators are widely discussed; however, rigorous discussions on their CP conditions remain limited. This paper presents conditions for QDMs to be CP, whose MEs take the GKSL-like form with arbitrary time dependence. One case considered is where its ME takes the time-local integro-differential GKSL-like form, which includes CP-divisible cases. Another case considered is where the ME is time-non-local but can be approximated to be time-local in the weak-coupling regime. As a special case of the time-non-local case, the same discussion holds for the time-convoluted GKSL-like form, which should be compared to previous studies.

Introduction

The theory of open quantum systems is the essential framework for describing microscopic systems influenced by their surrounding environments
[

,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
The dynamics of these systems is described by either a quantum dynamical map (QDM), which maps an arbitrary initial state to another state at an arbitrary time, or its differential equation, known as a master equation (ME).

This framework ensures the probabilistic interpretation of quantum mechanics, guaranteed by two fundamental characteristics of a QDM.
One characteristic is the complete positivity (CP), which ensures the positivity of probabilities when the system of interest is correlated with other systems. If a QDM has the Kraus representation
[
5
,
6
]
, the QDM is CP, but the CP is generally difficult to show in the form of MEs.
The other characteristic is the trace-preserving (TP), which ensures that all probabilities always sum to unity. This property can be easily demonstrated by taking the trace of MEs.

The Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudarshan-Lindblad (GKSL) ME is one of the most significant MEs in the theory of open quantum systems
[
7
,
8
]
.
This represents the most general form of a CPTP quantum dynamical semigroup, which is used to describe the Markovian dynamics.
The GKSL ME is time-local and consists of two parts: a unitary part with time-independent Hermitian operators and a non-unitary part with time-independent Lindblad operators and their coefficients, which are time-independent and positive damping rates.
The widely accepted derivation of the GKSL ME resorts to the Born-Markov approximation, which is valid for initially uncorrelated systems in the weak coupling regime
[

,

Since the standard derivation also involves the rotating wave approximation, finding alternative methods for deriving the GKSL ME remains an important problem

2
Setup and goals

2.1
Quantum dynamical maps (QDMs) and complete positivity (CP)

For a finite
d
-dimensional open quantum system, a quantum state is described by a
d\times d
positive semidefinite and unit-trace density matrix.
The dynamics is described by a QDM, which is a CPTP superoperator
\Lambda_{t}
mapping an arbitrary initial state
\rho
to a state at a later time
t
:
\rho(t)=\Lambda_{t}\rho,\ \Lambda_{0}={\mathbbm{1}}
, where
{\mathbbm{1}}
denotes the
d
-dimensional identity superoperator.

A QDM
\Lambda_{t}
is TP iff a state at a later time
\rho(t)=\Lambda_{t}\rho
is also unit-trace, or its ME is traceless.
A QDM
\Lambda_{t}
is CP iff a superoperator
\Lambda_{t}\otimes{\mathbbm{1}}
is a positive semidefinite superoperator.
Here, the superoperator
\Lambda_{t}\otimes{\mathbbm{1}}
is positive semidefinite iff
(\Lambda_{t}\otimes{\mathbbm{1}})|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|
is positive semidefinite for an arbitrary positive state
|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|
of a
2d
-dimensional system.
Also,
\Lambda_{t}
is CP iff
\Lambda_{t}
has a Kraus representation:
\Lambda_{t}\rho=\sum_{\alpha}K_{t}^{\alpha}\rho K_{t}^{\alpha\dagger},\ \sum_{\alpha}K_{t}^{\alpha\dagger}K_{t}^{\alpha}={\mathbbm{1}}
.
In this paper, the CP of a map
\Lambda_{t}
is evaluated by the positive semidefiniteness of a measure
{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}):=\langle\Psi|\big(\Lambda_{t}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{d}\big)(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|)|\Psi\rangle
, where
|\Psi\rangle
is an arbitrary
2d
-dimensitonal vector.

2.2
GKSL-like master equations

Two types of integro-differential equations are discussed in this paper. The first is the time-local case for
\Lambda^{\rm l}_{t}
and the second is the time-non-local case for
\Lambda^{{\rm nl}}_{t}
:

\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda^{{\rm l}}_{t}\rho\equiv\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}{\cal L}_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\rm GKSL}(\Lambda^{{\rm l}}_{t}\rho),\ \ \ \frac{d}{dt}\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}_{t}\rho\equiv\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}{\cal L}_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\rm GKSL}(\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}_{t^{\prime}}\rho).

(2.1)

For both types, the GKSL-like kernel
{\cal L}^{\rm GKSL}_{t,t^{\prime}}
is considered to have an arbitrary time dependence on both
t
and the integral variable
t^{\prime}
.
Note that the only difference between them appears in the time-dependence of its argument of the kernel.
More specifically, in this paper, we consider the GKSL-like kernel
{\cal L}^{\rm GKSL}_{t,t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{\rm GKSL}_{t,t^{\prime}}\rho=-i\Big[H^{\prime}_{t,t^{\prime}},\rho\Big]+\sum_{i}\Bigg(L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}\rho L^{i\ \dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\Big\{L^{i\ \dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}}L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}},\rho\Big\}\Bigg),

(2.2)

where
H^{\prime}_{t,t^{\prime}}
and
\{L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}\}
denote a Hermitian operator and Lindblad operators, respectively.
Notice that despite the time dependence of the Lindblad operators, the overall sign of the non-unitary part is positive.
Previous studies showed that the discussion becomes simple when the GKSL-like kernel
{\cal L}^{\rm GKSL}_{t,t^{\prime}}
is divided into two superoperators
{\cal B}_{t,t^{\prime}},
and
{\cal Z}_{t,t^{\prime}}
with another operator
W_{t,t^{\prime}}
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
:

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{\rm GKSL}_{t,t^{\prime}}\rho

\displaystyle=\Big({\cal B}_{t,t^{\prime}}-{\cal Z}_{t,t^{\prime}}\Big)\rho,

(2.3)

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{t,t^{\prime}}\rho

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}\ \rho\ L^{i\ \dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}},

(2.4)

\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{t,t^{\prime}}\rho

\displaystyle=W_{t,t^{\prime}}\ \rho+\rho\ W_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\dagger},\ W_{t,t^{\prime}}=iH^{\prime}_{t,t^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}L^{i\ \dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}}L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}.

(2.5)

We may introduce two other maps
\Lambda^{\cal B}_{t},\Lambda^{\cal Z}_{t}
in association with
{\cal B}_{t,t^{\prime}}
and
{\cal Z}_{t,t^{\prime}}
for the local/non-local cases:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda^{{\rm l/nl},{\cal B}}_{t}\rho\equiv\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}{\cal B}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda^{{\rm l/nl},{\cal B}}_{t/t^{\prime}}\rho),\ \ \ \frac{d}{dt}\Lambda^{{\rm l/nl},{\cal Z}}_{t}\rho\equiv\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(-{\cal Z}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda^{{\rm l/nl},{\cal Z}}_{t/t^{\prime}}\rho)\Big).

(2.6)

To solve the time-non-local GKSL-like ME, one usually considers the weak-coupling regime.
The idea comes from the standard derivation of the GKSL ME, which starts from the Redfield equation
[
51
]
.
In the standard derivation of the Redfield equation, the coupling strength
g
between the system of interest and the environment is assumed to be small, and only terms up to the second order
O(g^{2})
are kept. We note that the time-local and time-non-local Redfield equations describe the same dynamics up to the second order
O(g^{2})
, which is shown in App.
A
.

3
Time-local GKSL-like form

3.1
\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal B}}
is CP

Consider the first-order, linear integro-differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda^{{{\rm l}},{\cal B}}_{t}\rho=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}{\cal B}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda^{{{\rm l}},{\cal B}}_{t}\rho)=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(\sum_{i}L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda^{{{\rm l}},{\cal B}}_{t}\rho)L^{i\ \dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}}\Big),\ \ \Lambda_{0}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal B}}={\mathbbm{1}}.

(3.1)

Integration of Eq. (
3.1
) between
[0,t]
explicitly shows the structure of
\Lambda^{{{\rm l}},{\cal B}}_{t}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal B}}\rho=\rho

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}dt_{2}\sum_{i_{1}}L^{i_{1}}_{t_{1},t_{2}}(\Lambda^{{{\rm l}},{\cal B}}_{t_{1}}\rho)L^{i_{1}\ \dagger}_{t_{1},t_{2}}.

(3.2)

Note that the time locality in Eq. (
3.1
) yields the
t_{1}
-dependence of
\Lambda^{{{\rm l}},{\cal B}}_{t_{1}}
on the RHS of Eq. (
3.2
).
Its formal solution is iteratively obtained

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal B}}\rho=\rho+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}dt_{2}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}dt_{3}\int^{t_{3}}_{0}

\displaystyle dt_{4}\cdots\int^{t_{2n-3}}_{0}dt_{2n-1}\int^{t_{2n-1}}_{0}dt_{2n}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{i_{1},\cdots i_{n}}L^{i_{1}}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdots L^{i_{n}}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}\ \rho\ L^{i_{n}\ \dagger}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}\cdots L^{i_{1}\ \dagger}_{t_{1},t_{2}}.

(3.3)

The solution might appear complicated at first sight, but a simple example in App.
B
helps to see the essence.

The CP of
\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal B}}
is shown by the positive semidefiniteness of
{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal B}})

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal B}})=|\langle\Psi|\Phi\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\cdots\int^{t_{2n-1}}_{0}dt_{2n}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots i_{n}}|\langle\Psi|\Big(L^{i_{1}}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdots L^{i_{n}}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}\Big)|\Phi\rangle|^{2}\geq 0.

(3.4)

3.2
\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal Z}}
is CP

Consider the first-order, linear integro-differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal Z}}\rho=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(-{\cal Z}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda^{{{\rm l}},{\cal Z}}_{t}\rho)\Big)=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(-W_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal Z}}\rho)-(\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal Z}}\rho)W_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\Big),\ \ \Lambda_{0}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal Z}}={\mathbbm{1}}.

(3.5)

Note that the equation is local in time. Therefore, the formal solution is obtained by using the chronological and anti-chronological exponential functions

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal Z}}\rho=\overleftarrow{\cal T}e^{-\int^{t}_{0}ds\int^{s}_{0}ds^{\prime}W_{s,s^{\prime}}}\ \rho\ \overrightarrow{\cal T}e^{-\int^{t}_{0}ds\int^{s}_{0}ds^{\prime}W_{s,s^{\prime}}^{\dagger}}\equiv V_{t}\rho V_{t}^{\dagger}.

(3.6)

The explicit forms of
V_{t}
and
V_{t}^{\dagger}
are the following

\displaystyle V_{t}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\int^{t}_{0}ds_{1}\int^{s_{1}}_{0}ds_{2}\cdots\int^{t}_{0}ds_{2n-1}\int^{s_{2n-1}}_{0}ds_{2n}\overleftarrow{\cal T}\big(W_{s_{1},s_{2}}\cdots W_{s_{2n-1},s_{2n}}\big),

(3.7)

\displaystyle V_{t}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\int^{t}_{0}ds_{1}\int^{s_{1}}_{0}ds_{2}\cdots\int^{t}_{0}ds_{2n-1}\int^{s_{2n-1}}_{0}ds_{2n}\overrightarrow{\cal T}\big(W_{s_{1},s_{2}}^{\dagger}\cdots W_{s_{2n-1},s_{2n}}^{\dagger}\big).

(3.8)

Note that the chronological and anti-chronological orderings are applied to the odd number of the integral variables.

The CP
\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal Z}}
is guaranteed by the positive semidefiniteness of the measure
{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal Z}})

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal Z}})=|\langle\Psi|V_{t}|\Phi\rangle|^{2}\geq 0.

(3.9)

3.3
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm l}}
is CP

Consider the first-order, linear integro-differential, time-local GKSL-like master equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda^{{{\rm l}}}_{t}\rho=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}{\cal L}_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\rm GKSL}(\Lambda^{{{\rm l}}}_{t}\rho)=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(\sum_{i}L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda^{{{\rm l}}}_{t}\rho)L^{i\ \dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}}-W_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}}}\rho)-(\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}}}\rho)W_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\Big),\Lambda_{0}^{{{\rm l}}}={\mathbbm{1}}.

(3.10)

To simplify Eq. (
3.10
), take the time derivative of the map
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm l}}
under the similarity transformation
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm l}}\equiv V_{t}\hat{\Lambda}_{t}^{{\rm l}}V_{t}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm l}}\rho=V_{t}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\Lambda}_{t}^{{\rm l}}\rho\Big)V_{t}^{\dagger}+\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(-W_{t,t^{\prime}}V_{t}(\hat{\Lambda}_{t}^{{{\rm l}}}\rho)V_{t}^{\dagger}-V_{t}(\hat{\Lambda}_{t}^{{{\rm l}}}\rho)V_{t}^{\dagger}W_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\Big).

(3.11)

Comparing Eq. (
3.10
) and Eq. (
3.11
), the first-order, linear integro-differential equation for
\hat{\Lambda}_{t}^{{\rm l}}
is obtained

\displaystyle V_{t}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\Lambda}_{t}^{{\rm l}}\rho\Big)V_{t}^{\dagger}=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(\sum_{i}L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}V_{t}(\hat{\Lambda}^{{{\rm l}}}_{t}\rho)V_{t}^{\dagger}L^{i\ \dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}}\Big).

(3.12)

Define
\hat{L}^{i}_{s,s^{\prime}}\equiv V_{s}^{-1}L^{i}_{s,s^{\prime}}V_{s}
. See the explicit form of
V_{t}^{-1}
in App.
C
.
Then, Eq. (
3.12
) appears the same as Eq. (
3.1
)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\Lambda}_{t}^{{\rm l}}\rho=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(\sum_{i}\hat{L}^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\hat{\Lambda}^{{{\rm l}}}_{t}\rho)\hat{L}^{i\ \dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}}\Big).

(3.13)

Using the same arguments as in Subsec.
3.1
, Eq. (
3.10
) is solved to give the formal solution

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm l}}\rho=V_{t}\Bigg(\rho+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\cdots\int^{t_{2n-1}}_{0}dt_{2n}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots i_{n}}\hat{L}^{i_{1}}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdots\hat{L}^{i_{n}}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}\ \rho\ \hat{L}^{i_{n}\ \dagger}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}\cdots\hat{L}^{i_{1}\ \dagger}_{t-1,t_{2}}\Bigg)V_{t}^{\dagger}.

(3.14)

The CP of
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm l}}
is guaranteed by the positive semidefiniteness of the measure
{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm l}})

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm l}})=|\langle\Psi|V_{t}|\Phi\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\cdots\int^{t_{2n-1}}_{0}dt_{2n}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots i_{n}}|\langle\Psi|V_{t}\hat{L}^{i_{1}}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdots\hat{L}^{i_{n}}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}|\Phi\rangle|^{2}\geq 0.

(3.15)

Therefore, the QDM
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm l}}
for the time-local GKSL-like ME is CP.

3.4
If the time dependence appears only through c-number functions,
\Lambda^{{{\rm l}}}_{t}
is CP divisible

Consider a special case of the time-local GKSL-like ME where the time dependence of the Lindblad operators is limited to c-number functions
h^{i}(t,t^{\prime})\in{\mathbbm{C}}
,
{\rm i.e.}
,
L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}=h^{i}(t,t^{\prime})L^{i}
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda^{{{\rm l}}}_{t}\rho=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Bigg(-i\big[H^{\prime}_{t,t^{\prime}},\Lambda^{{{\rm l}}}_{t}\rho\big]+\sum_{i}|h^{i}(t,t^{\prime})|^{2}\Big(L^{i}(\Lambda^{{{\rm l}}}_{t}\rho)L^{i\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\big\{L^{i\dagger}L^{i},\Lambda_{t}^{{{\rm l}}}\rho\big\}\Big)\Bigg),\ \Lambda_{0}^{{{\rm l}}}={\mathbbm{1}}.

(3.16)

The time-dependent damping rates
\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}|h^{i}(t,t^{\prime})|^{2}
are clearly positive semidefinite. This supports the well-known condition for the QDM to be CP-divisible
[
42
,
43
,
44
,
46
]
.

4
Time-non-local GKSL-like form

4.1
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal B}}
is CP

Consider the first-order, linear integro-differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal B}}_{t}\rho=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}{\cal B}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal B}}_{t^{\prime}}\rho)=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(\sum_{i}L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal B}}_{t^{\prime}}\rho)L^{i\ \dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}}\Big),\ \ \Lambda_{0}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal B}}={\mathbbm{1}}.

(4.1)

Integration of Eq. (
4.1
) between
[0,t]
explicitly shows the structure of
\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal B}}_{t}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal B}}\rho=\rho

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}dt_{2}\sum_{i_{1}}L^{i_{1}}_{t_{1},t_{2}}(\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal B}}_{t_{2}}\rho)L^{i_{1}\ \dagger}_{t_{1},t_{2}}.

(4.2)

Note that the time non-locality is reflected on the
t_{2}
-dependence of
\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal B}}_{t_{2}}
on the RHS of Eq. (
4.2
).
Its formal solution is obtained

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal B}}\rho=\rho+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}dt_{2}\int^{t_{2}}_{0}

\displaystyle dt_{3}\int^{t_{3}}_{0}dt_{4}\cdots\int^{t_{2n-2}}_{0}dt_{2n-1}\int^{t_{2n-1}}_{0}dt_{2n}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{i_{1},\cdots i_{n}}L^{i_{1}}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdots L^{i_{n}}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}\ \rho\ L^{i_{n}\ \dagger}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}\cdots L^{i_{1}\ \dagger}_{t_{1},t_{2}}.

(4.3)

Note that the intervals of integrations are naturally time-ordered and are different from those in Eq. (
3.3
).

The CP of
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal B}}
is shown by the positive semidefiniteness of
{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal B}})

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal B}})=|\langle\Psi|\Phi\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\cdots\int^{t_{2n-1}}_{0}dt_{2n}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots i_{n}}|\langle\Psi|\Big(L^{i_{1}}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdots L^{i_{n}}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}\Big)|\Phi\rangle|^{2}\geq 0.

(4.4)

4.2
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}
is CP only if
W_{t,t^{\prime}}
satisfies a strict condition

Consider the first-order, linear integro-differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(-{\cal Z}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}_{t^{\prime}}\rho)\Big)=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(-W_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{t^{\prime}}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho)-(\Lambda_{t^{\prime}}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho)W_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\Big),\ \ \Lambda_{0}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}={\mathbbm{1}}.

(4.5)

In this subsection, the conditions for the solution
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}
to be CP are discussed.
By integrating Eq. (
4.5
) between
[0,t]
, the structure of
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}
is explicitly shown

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho=\rho+\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}dt_{2}\Big(-{\cal Z}_{t_{1},t_{2}}(\Lambda^{{{\rm l}},{\cal Z}}_{t_{2}}\rho)\Big).

(4.6)

Iterating multiple times yields the formal solution as an infinite series expansion

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho=\rho+\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}dt_{2}\Big(-{\cal Z}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\rho\Big)+\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\cdots\int^{t_{3}}_{0}dt_{4}\Big({\cal Z}_{t_{1},t_{2}}{\cal Z}_{t_{3},t_{4}}\rho\Big)+\cdots.

(4.7)

The condition for
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}
to be CP depends on the sign of
{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}})
:

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}})=|\langle\Psi|\Phi\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}dt_{2}\langle\Psi|\Big(-{\cal Z}_{t_{1},t_{2}}|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|\Big)|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\cdots\int^{t_{3}}_{0}dt_{4}\langle\Psi|\Big({\cal Z}_{t_{1},t_{2}}{\cal Z}_{t_{3},t_{4}}|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|\Big)|\Psi\rangle+\cdots.

(4.8)

Showing the positive semidefiniteness of the measure
{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}})
seems in general difficult. Since Eq. (
4.8
) is expressed as the power series expansion by the kernel
-{\cal Z}_{t,t^{\prime}}
, the positive semidefiniteness of the second term of Eq. (
4.8
) is sufficient to guarantee
0\leq{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}})
.

It is shown, however, that the condition for the second term of Eq. (
4.8
) to be positive semidefinite imposes a strong condition on the operational structure of
W_{t,t^{\prime}}
.
Express
|\Psi\rangle=\sum_{m}\psi_{m}|m\rangle
, and an arbitrary initial state
\rho=\sum_{n}\rho_{n}|n\rangle\langle n|
in terms of the complete orthonormal basis
\{|n\rangle\}
. The condition is then expressed as

\displaystyle 0\leq\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}dt_{2}\sum_{m,n,m^{\prime}}\psi_{m}^{*}\rho_{n}\psi_{m^{\prime}}\big(-\langle m|W_{t_{1},t_{2}}|n\rangle\langle n|m^{\prime}\rangle-\langle m|n\rangle\langle n|W_{t_{1},t_{2}}^{\dagger}|m^{\prime}\rangle\big).

(4.9)

This condition is indeed satisfied for arbitrary
\psi_{m}^{*}\rho_{n}\psi_{m^{\prime}}
, by a trivial diagonal operator
W_{t_{1},t_{2}}
with some function
f(t_{1},t_{2})\in{\mathbbm{C}}
,

\displaystyle\langle m|W_{t_{1},t_{2}}|n\rangle\propto-f(t_{1},t_{2})\delta_{n,m},\ \ {\rm Re}\big[f(t_{1},t_{2})\big]<0.

(4.10)

Actually, Eq. (
4.9
) is inevitably violated if the operator
W_{t_{1},t_{2}}
has a non-vanishing off-diagonal matrix element.
Consider a normalized vector
|\Psi\rangle=\psi_{n}|n\rangle+\psi_{l}|l\rangle
, and suppose
n\neq l
,
\langle l|W_{t_{1},t_{2}}|n\rangle\neq 0
.
Eq. (
4.9
) then becomes

\displaystyle 0\leq\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}dt_{2}\sum_{n}(-2\rho_{n})\Big(|\psi_{n}|^{2}|\langle n|W_{t_{1},t_{2}}|n\rangle|+|\psi_{l}\psi_{n}||\langle l|W_{t_{1},t_{2}}|n\rangle|\cos\big(\theta_{n}-\theta_{l}+\varphi_{l,n}\big)\Big),

(4.11)

where relative phases
\varphi_{l,n}
and
\theta_{n}-\theta_{l}
are defined by
\langle l|W_{t_{1},t_{2}}|n\rangle\equiv|\langle l|W_{t_{1},t_{2}}|n\rangle|e^{i\varphi_{l,n}}
and
\psi_{l}^{*}\psi_{n}\equiv|\psi_{l}||\psi_{n}|e^{i(\theta_{n}-\theta_{l})}
.
Since
\rho_{n}
of the initial state
\rho=\sum_{n}\rho_{n}|n\rangle\langle n|
is positive semidefinite, Eq. (
4.11
) is expressed as below

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\Bigg(\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}dt_{2}\frac{|\psi_{n}|}{\sqrt{1-|\psi_{n}|^{2}}}\frac{|\langle n|W_{t_{1},t_{2}}|n\rangle|}{|\langle l|W_{t_{1},t_{2}}|n\rangle|}+\cos\big(\theta_{n}-\theta_{l}+\varphi_{l,n}\big)\Bigg)\leq 0.

(4.12)

Although the condition by Eq. (
4.12
) has to be satisfied for arbitrary
\psi_{n}\in{\mathbbm{C}}
, this can always be violated by a certain choice of the relative phase
\theta_{n}-\theta_{l}
.
This concludes that no operator
W_{t_{1},t_{2}}
with non-vanishing off-diagonal elements is allowed for the condition Eq. (
4.9
) to hold.

In summary, for the first-order, linear integro-differential equation (
4.5
), if the operator
W_{t_{1},t_{2}}
satisfies the strict condition Eq. (
4.10
), the map
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}
is CP.
The following subsections, therefore, impose additional constraints on the map
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}
to facilitate further analysis and discussion.

4.3
\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}_{t}
is CP in the weak-coupling regime

Consider the first-order, linear integro-differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(-{\cal Z}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda^{{{\rm l}},{\cal Z}}_{t^{\prime}}\rho)\Big)=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(-W_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{t^{\prime}}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal Z}}\rho)-(\Lambda_{t^{\prime}}^{{{\rm l}},{\cal Z}}\rho)W_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\Big),\ \ \Lambda_{0}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}={\mathbbm{1}}.

(4.13)

In this subsection, the formal solution is perturbatively obtained. In the weak-coupling regime, the system-bath coupling strength
g
is assumed to be sufficiently small and treated as a perturbation parameter. In the standard derivation of the GKSL-like ME, the Hermitian operator
H^{\prime}_{t,t^{\prime}}
in the interaction picture and the Lindblad operators
\{L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}\}
are in proportion to
g^{2}
. The operator
W_{t,t^{\prime}}
is therefore proportional to
g^{2}
.

To further approximate Eq. (
4.13
), integrate Eq. (
4.13
) over
[0,t]

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho=\rho+\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}dt_{2}\Big(-W_{t_{1},t_{2}}(\Lambda_{t_{2}}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho)-(\Lambda_{t_{2}}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho)W_{t_{1},t_{2}}^{\dagger}\Big).

(4.14)

Iterating Eq. (
4.14
) and neglecting the terms of order
g^{3}
and higher, a simple differential equation is obtained

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho\approx\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(-W_{t,t^{\prime}}\rho-\rho\ W_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\Big).

(4.15)

Since
W_{t_{1},t_{2}}\propto g^{2}
, a further approximation:
g^{2}\rho\approx g^{2}\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho
is valid up to
g^{2}
. By this approximation, Eq. (
4.15
) appears as a familiar form

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho\approx\Big(-\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}W_{t,t^{\prime}}\Big)\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho+\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho\Big(-\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}W_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\Big).

(4.16)

Eq. (
4.16
) coincides with Eq. (
3.5
). Thus the formal solution is the same as the time-local case

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho=\overleftarrow{\cal T}e^{-\int^{t}_{0}ds\int^{s}_{0}ds^{\prime}W_{s,s^{\prime}}}\ \rho\ \overrightarrow{\cal T}e^{-\int^{t}_{0}ds\int^{s}_{0}ds^{\prime}W_{s,s^{\prime}}^{\dagger}}\equiv V_{t}\rho V_{t}^{\dagger}.

(4.17)

The CP of
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}
is guaranteed by the positive semidefiniteness of the measure
{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}})

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}})=|\langle\Psi|V_{t}|\Phi\rangle|^{2}\geq 0.

(4.18)

In the weak-coupling regime, this type of map is CP.

4.4
\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}_{t}
is CP in the weak-coupling regime

Consider the first-order, linear integro-differential, GKSL-like master equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}_{t}\rho=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}{\cal L}_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\rm GKSL}(\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}_{t^{\prime}}\rho)

\displaystyle=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(\sum_{i}L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}_{t^{\prime}}\rho)L^{i\ \dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}}-W_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{t^{\prime}}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}\rho)-(\Lambda_{t^{\prime}}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}\rho)W_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\Big),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}

\displaystyle={\mathbbm{1}}.

(4.19)

In the weak-coupling regime, the discussion in Subsec.
4.3
holds, and Eq. (
4.19
) is approximated up to the second order of the system-bath coupling strength as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}_{t}\rho\approx\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(\sum_{i}L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}_{t^{\prime}}\rho)L^{i\ \dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle-W_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}\rho)-(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}\rho)W_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\Big),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}

\displaystyle={\mathbbm{1}}.

(4.20)

In the same approach as in Subsec.
3.3
, the contribution from the operator
W_{t,t^{\prime}}
can be incorporated by using the operators
V_{t},V_{t}^{-1}
.
Defining
\bar{L}^{i_{j}}_{s,s^{\prime}}\equiv V_{s}^{-1}L^{i_{j}}_{s,s^{\prime}}V_{s^{\prime}}
, Eq. (
4.20
) takes the same form as Eq. (
4.1
)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}\rho=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(\sum_{i}\bar{L}^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda^{{{\rm l}}}_{t^{\prime}}\rho)\bar{L}^{i\ \dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}}\Big).

(4.21)

The formal solution of Eq. (
4.20
) is therefore

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}\rho=V_{t}\Bigg(\rho+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\cdots\int^{t_{2n-1}}_{0}dt_{2n}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots i_{n}}\bar{L}^{i_{1}}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdots\bar{L}^{i_{n}}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}\ \rho\ \bar{L}^{i_{n}\ \dagger}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}\cdots\bar{L}^{i_{1}\ \dagger}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\Bigg)V_{t}^{\dagger}.

(4.22)

The CP of
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}
is guaranteed by the positive semidefiniteness of the measure
{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}})

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}})=|\langle\Psi|\Phi\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}\cdots\int^{t_{2n-1}}_{0}dt_{2n}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots i_{n}}|\langle\Psi|V_{t}\bar{L}^{i_{1}}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdots\bar{L}^{i_{n}}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}|\Phi\rangle|^{2}\geq 0.

(4.23)

Therefore, in the weak-coupling regime,
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}
of the time-non-local GKSL-like ME is CP.

4.5
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}
is CP if
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}
is CP and its Kraus representation has a specific property

Consider the first-order, linear integro-differential, GKSL-like master equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}_{t}\rho=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}{\cal L}_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\rm GKSL}(\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}_{t^{\prime}}\rho)

\displaystyle=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(\sum_{i}L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}_{t^{\prime}}\rho)L^{i\ \dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}}-W_{t,t^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{t^{\prime}}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}\rho)-(\Lambda_{t^{\prime}}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}\rho)W_{t,t^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\Big),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}

\displaystyle={\mathbbm{1}}.

(4.24)

Assume
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}
which satisfies Eq. (
4.5
) is CP. Then, it has a Kraus representation
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}\rho=\sum_{j}K_{t}^{j}\rho K_{t}^{j\dagger}
.
Further, assume that the Kraus operators satisfy specific conditions

\displaystyle\big(K_{t}^{j}\big)^{-1}K_{t}^{k}={\mathbbm{1}}\delta_{j,k},\ \ K_{t}^{j\dagger}\big(K_{t}^{k\dagger}\big)^{-1}={\mathbbm{1}}\delta_{j,k}.

(4.25)

This assumption is nothing but a technical requirement.

Consider another map
\hat{\Lambda}_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}
which is defined by the similarity transformation of
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}\rho\equiv\sum_{j}K_{t}^{j}\Big(\hat{\Lambda}_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}\rho\Big)K_{t}^{j\dagger}.

(4.26)

Comparing the time derivative of Eq. (
4.26
) and Eq. (
4.24
), a similar form to Eq. (
4.1
) is obtained for
\hat{\Lambda}_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}

\displaystyle\sum_{j}K_{t}^{j}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\Lambda}_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}\rho\Big)K_{t}^{j\dagger}=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(\sum_{i}L^{i}_{t,t^{\prime}}\big(\sum_{k}K_{t^{\prime}}^{k}(\hat{\Lambda}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}_{t^{\prime}}\rho)K_{t^{\prime}}^{k\dagger}\big)L^{i\ \dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}}\Big).

(4.27)

Using the assumed properties of the Kraus operators and defining
\bar{L}^{i,j,k}_{s,s^{\prime}}\equiv\big(K_{s}^{j}\big)^{-1}L^{i}_{s,s^{\prime}}K_{s^{\prime}}^{k}
, Eq. (
4.27
) appears the same as Eq. (
4.1
)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\Lambda}_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}\rho=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}\Big(\sum_{i}\sum_{j,k}\bar{L}^{i,j,k}_{t,t^{\prime}}(\hat{\Lambda}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}_{t^{\prime}}\rho)\bar{L}^{i,j,k\dagger}_{t,t^{\prime}}\Big).

(4.28)

According to Subsec.
4.1

4.24

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}\rho=\sum_{m}K^{m}_{t}\Bigg(\rho+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}

\displaystyle\cdots\int^{t_{2n-1}}_{0}dt_{2n}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots i_{n}}\sum_{j_{1},\cdots j_{n}}\sum_{k_{1},\cdots k_{n}}

\displaystyle\times\bar{L}^{i_{1},j_{1},k_{1}}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdots\bar{L}^{i_{n},j_{n},k_{n}}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}\ \rho\bar{L}^{i_{n},j_{n},k_{n}\ \dagger}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}\cdots\bar{L}^{i_{1},j_{1},k_{1}\dagger}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\Bigg)K_{t}^{m\dagger}.

(4.29)

The CP of
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}

{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}})

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}})=\sum_{m}\Bigg(|\langle\Psi|K_{t}^{m}|\Phi\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int^{t}_{0}dt_{1}

\displaystyle\cdots\int^{t_{2n-1}}_{0}dt_{2n}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots i_{n}}\sum_{j_{1},\cdots j_{n}}\sum_{k_{1},\cdots k_{n}}

\displaystyle\times|\langle\Psi|K_{t}^{m}\bar{L}^{i_{1},j_{1},k_{1}}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cdots\bar{L}^{i_{n},j_{n},k_{n}}_{t_{2n-1},t_{2n}}|\Phi\rangle|^{2}\Bigg)\geq 0.

(4.30)

Therefore, the QDM
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l}}
for the time-non-local GKSL-like ME is CP if its associated map
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm n}{\rm l},{\cal Z}}
is CP and its Kraus operators satisfy the specific conditions.

4.6
\Lambda^{\rm conv}_{t}
is CP if

is CP

As a special case, consider the first-order, linear integro-differential GKSL-like master equation with convolution

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda^{\rm conv}_{t}\rho=\int^{t}_{0}dt^{\prime}{\cal L}_{t-t^{\prime}}^{\rm GKSL}(\Lambda^{\rm conv}_{t^{\prime}}\rho),\ \ \ \Lambda_{0}^{\rm conv}

\displaystyle={\mathbbm{1}}.

(4.31)

Assume that the associated map
\Lambda^{{\rm conv},{\cal Z}}_{t}
is CP and its Kraus representation has a specific property:
\Lambda_{t}^{{\rm conv},{\cal Z}}\rho=\sum_{j}K_{t}^{j}\rho K_{t}^{j\dagger},\big(K_{t}^{j}\big)^{-1}K_{t}^{k}={\mathbbm{1}}\delta_{j,k},\ K_{t}^{j\dagger}\big(K_{t}^{k\dagger}\big)^{-1}={\mathbbm{1}}\delta_{j,k}.

Then, the formal solution of Eq. (

A simpler discussion is given in the previous studies
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
. In these works, the CP of
\Lambda^{\rm conv}_{t}
is shown when the associated maps
\Lambda^{{\rm conv},{\cal B}}_{t}
and

\Lambda^{{\rm conv},{\cal Z}}_{t}

5
Conclusion

This paper has examined the conditions for QDMs to be CP when their MEs take the GKSL-like form.
In the time-local case, a QDM is CP with arbitrary time dependence in the GKSL-like kernel of the integro-differential ME, which signifies that the GKSL-like form is both the necessary and sufficient condition for a QDM to be CP.
In the time-non-local case, an additional requirement is necessary for QDMs to be CP.
The condition has been proposed in the form of a specific Kraus representation of a partial map or the weak coupling regime.
The difference between the two cases lies in the locality, which arises from the Born approximation in the derivation in the weak coupling regime.

Appendix A
Approximation in the derivation of the Redfield equation

This section is aimed at showing that the time-local Redfield equation describes the same dynamics as its time-non-local counterpart in the weak-coupling regime. In the standard derivation, the microscopic Hamiltonian is given by
H=H_{\rm S}+H_{\rm B}+gSB
, where
H_{\rm S},\ H_{\rm B},\ g,\ S,
and
B
denote the system free Hamiltonian, the bath free Hamiltonian, the coupling strength, the system part of the interaction, and the bath part of the interaction, respectively. In the weak-coupling regime, the coupling strength
g
is small and only terms up to the second order have been kept. The initial state is assumed to be decoupled from the bath, and the system state is taken as an arbitrary state
\rho(0)
, while the bath state is taken as a thermal equilibrium
\rho_{\rm B}
.

In the standard derivation of the Redfield equation, an integro-differential equation in the interaction picture is derived

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\rho^{\rm I}(t)=g^{2}\int^{t}_{0}d\tau\Big(-S^{\rm I}(t)C(\tau)S^{\rm I}(-\tau)\rho^{\rm I}(t-\tau)+S^{\rm I}(t)\rho^{\rm I}(t-\tau)C^{*}(\tau)S^{\rm I}(-\tau)\Big)+{\rm h.c.},

(A.1)

where
C(\tau)={\rm Tr_{B}}\big[B^{\rm I}(t)B\rho_{\rm B}\big]
denotes the correlation function.
The concrete expression of the first term of Eq. (
A.1
) shows the
g
dependence

\displaystyle-g^{2}\int^{t}_{0}d\tau S^{\rm I}(t)C(\tau)S^{\rm I}(-\tau)\rho^{\rm I}(t-\tau)

(A.2)

\displaystyle=-g^{2}\int^{t}_{0}d\tau S^{\rm I}(t)C(\tau)S^{\rm I}(-\tau)e^{iH_{\rm S}(t-\tau)}{\rm Tr_{B}}\big[e^{iH(t-\tau)}(\rho(0)\otimes\rho_{\rm B})e^{-iH(t-\tau)}\big]e^{-iH_{\rm S}(t-\tau)}.

(A.3)

Since only the terms up to the second order of
g
are kept, any
g
-dependent parts in the partial trace can be neglected

\displaystyle{\rm Tr_{B}}\big[e^{-iH(t-\tau)}(\rho(0)\otimes\rho_{\rm B})e^{iH(t-\tau)}\big]={\rm Tr_{B}}\big[e^{iH\tau}\rho_{\rm tot}(t)e^{-iH\tau}\big]

\displaystyle={\rm Tr_{B}}\Big[\big(1+i(H_{\rm S}+H_{\rm B}+gSB)\tau-\cdots\big)\rho_{\rm tot}(t)\big(1-i(H_{\rm S}+H_{\rm B}+gSB)\tau-\cdots\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\approx{\rm Tr_{B}}\Big[e^{iH_{\rm S}\tau}\rho_{\rm tot}(t)e^{-iH_{\rm S}\tau}\Big]=e^{iH_{\rm S}\tau}\rho(t)e^{iH_{\rm S}\tau}.

(A.4)

By this approximation, Eq. (
A.1
) can be made local in time

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\rho^{\rm I}(t)=g^{2}\int^{t}_{0}d\tau\Big(-S^{\rm I}(t)C(\tau)S^{\rm I}(-\tau)\rho^{\rm I}(t)+S^{\rm I}(t)\rho^{\rm I}(t)C^{*}(\tau)S^{\rm I}(-\tau)\Big)+{\rm h.c.}.

(A.5)

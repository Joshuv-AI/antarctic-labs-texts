Neutron star crustal properties from relativistic mean-field models and bulk parameters effects
M. Dutra, C. H. Lenzi, W. de Paula, and O. Lourenço

Abstract
We calculate crustal properties of neutron stars, namely, mass ( M crust M_{\rm crust} ), radius ( R crust R_{\rm crust} ) and fraction of moment of inertia ( Δ ​ I / I \Delta I/I ) from parametrizations of hadronic relativistic mean-field (RMF) model consistent with symmetric and asymmetric nuclear matter constraints, as well as some stellar boundaries. We verify which one are also in agreement with restrictions of Δ ​ I / I ⩾ 1.4 % \Delta I/I\geqslant 1.4\% and Δ ​ I / I ⩾ 7 % \Delta I/I\geqslant 7\% related to the glitching mechanism observed in pulsars, such as the Vela one. The latter constraint explains the glitches phenomenon when entrainment effects are taken into account. Our findings indicate that these parametrizations pass in the glitching limit for a neutron star mass range of M ⩽ 1.82 ​ M ⊙ M\leqslant 1.82M_{\odot} ( Δ ​ I / I ⩾ 1.4 % \Delta I/I\geqslant 1.4\% ), and M ⩽ 1.16 ​ M ⊙ M\leqslant 1.16M_{\odot} ( Δ ​ I / I ⩾ 7 % \Delta I/I\geqslant 7\% ). We also investigate the influence of nuclear matter bulk parameters on crustal properties and find that symmetry energy is the quantity that produces the higher variations on M crust M_{\rm crust} , R crust R_{\rm crust} , and Δ ​ I / I \Delta I/I . Based on the results, we construct a particular RMF parametrization able to satisfy Δ ​ I / I ⩾ 7 % \Delta I/I\geqslant 7\% even at M = 1.4 ​ M ⊙ M=1.4M_{\odot} , the mass value used to fit data from the softer component of the Vela pulsar X-ray spectrum. The model also presents compatibility with observational data from PSR J1614-2230, PSR J0348+0432, and MSP J0740+6620 pulsars, as well as, with data from the Neutron Star Interior Composition Explorer (NICER) mission.

Introduction

There are at least two approaches widely used to describe many-body systems in which their constituents are submitted to the nuclear interaction. One of them is based on the construction of potentials where free parameters are adjusted in order to reproduce experimental data from known few-nucleon systems, such as the deuteron: a proton-neutron pair of spin 1 and isospin 0 presenting binding energy around 2 MeV

An example of many-nucleon systems described by the aforementioned hadronic models, in addition to finite nuclei and infinite nuclear matter, is the composing matter of some astrophysical objects as neutron stars, for instance. These objects are formed by protons, neutrons, leptons, and other exotic particles, interacting in such a way to ensure the

Pulsars are very stable rotating neutron stars with a period ranging between

Hessels-2006

The neutron superfluid vortices pinned to the lattice structure of nuclei contained in the inner crust rotate faster than the compact object, since the star itself loses rotational energy due to the electromagnetic radiation emission (radio waves in the case of radio pulsars). As the difference between the vortices and star angular frequencies becomes higher enough, an abrupt unpinning of some vortices takes place and their outward move is verified. By conservation of angular momentum, the star increases its rotation and the glitch is then established. Some theoretical studies show that pulsar glitches are related to the crustal fraction of the moment of inertia,

3
Crustal properties of neutron stars (results)

In order to correctly determine crustal properties of a neutron star, one needs, in principle, to have a complete description of its crust. In other words, the equation of state (EoS) for the inner and outer layers related to this part of the neutron star should be constructed. However, the description of the inner crust is a hard task since it is composed of different elements, namely, neutron-rich nuclei, nuclear clusters in the so-called pasta phase, and other. Furthermore, it is not trivial to construct a unified EoS that simultaneously describes the core and the crust of a neutron star. In particular,
gognyic
constructed a unified EoS for the inner crust and the core computed with the D1M* Gogny force. As an alternative to this development, a treatment usually adopted to compute the EoS for the entire neutron star is based on a piecewise structure. In such an approach, the EoS is divided into 3 parts. One of them describes the outer crust, estimated to exist up to densities around
10^{11}
g/cm
3

BPS-1971a
;
pieka

BPS-1971a

\rho_{t}

P(\mathcal{E})=A+B\mathcal{E}^{4/3}

A

B

Another approach proposed by

M

4
Summary and Concluding remarks

In this work we analyze the outcomes related to the crustal properties of neutron stars provided by a set of relativistic hadronic mean-field (RMF) model parametrizations consistent with different constraints coming from symmetric and asymmetric nuclear matter, pure neutron matter, and some astrophysical data related studied in Refs.
PRC90-055203
;
PRC93-025806
. We use the approach in which it is possible to calculate crust mass (
M_{\rm crust}
) and radius (

) without a specific treatment for this part of the neutron star
AA119-599
. Since this quantities are obtained, it is possible to determine analytically the ratio between the crustal fraction of the moment of inertia and the total moment of inertia,

given in Eq. (
16

and

Another investigation performed in this work was the analysis of how the nuclear matter bulk parameter, namely, incompressibility, effective mass, symmetry energy and its slope, affect the crustal properties of the neutron star. Appendix

Here we derive the mainly equations of the approach used for the crustal star properties, based on the core equation of state and macroscopic properties of the core-crust interface
AA119-599
. This formalism is based on the assumption that the mass of the crust,
M_{\rm crust}
, is much smaller than the total mass of the star,
M
. We start by constructing the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equations.

In order to describe a spherically symmetric object, we use the metric given by

ds^{2}=e^{\varphi(r)}\,c^{2}\,dt^{2}-e^{\xi(r)}dr^{2}-r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\right).

(18)

We consider the stress-energy tensor for a perfect fluid as
T_{\mu\nu}=\left(P/c^{2}+\rho\right)u_{\mu}\,u_{\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}\,P/c^{2}
, where
P
is the pressure and
\mathcal{E}
is energy density. The quadrivelocity
u_{\mu}
fulfills
u_{\mu}\,u^{\mu}=1
and
\,u^{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}u_{\mu}=0
. By defining

\displaystyle e^{-\xi}=\left[1-\frac{2Gm(r)}{rc^{2}}\right],

(19)

where
m=m(r)
represents the mass inside a sphere of radius
r
, we obtain the system of equations that governs the hydrostatic equilibrium of the compact star, namely,

\displaystyle m^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=4\pi\mathcal{E}(r)\,r^{2},

(20)

\displaystyle P^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left[P(r)/c^{2}+\mathcal{E}(r)\right]\left[\frac{4\pi G}{c^{2}}\,r\,P(r)+\frac{G\,m(r)}{r^{2}}\right]}{\left[1-\frac{2\,G\,m(r)}{rc^{2}}\right]}\,.

(21)

In the limit
M_{\rm crust}\ll M
, we have
4\pi r^{3}P(r)/[m(r)c^{2}]\ll 1
. Therefore, for the crust we can write Eq. (
21
) as

\displaystyle\frac{P^{\prime}(r)}{P(r)+\mathcal{E}(r)\,c^{2}}=-\frac{G\,m(r)}{c^{2}\,r^{2}}\left[1-\frac{2\,G\,m(r)}{rc^{2}}\right]^{-1}\,.

(22)

We can estimate the contribution of the crust mass by considering an approximation where
P(r)/c^{2}
is much smaller than
\mathcal{E}(r)
. In the crust region, for
M_{\rm crust}\ll M
and using
dm=\mathcal{E}(r)dv=\mathcal{E}(r)4\pi r^{2}dr
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{dP(r)}{dm}=-\frac{G\,M}{4\pi\,r^{4}}\left[1-\frac{2\,G\,M}{rc^{2}}\right]^{-1}\,.

(23)

Integrating Eq. (
23
) from the outer crust up to the core-crust interface, we have

\displaystyle M_{\rm crust}=\frac{4\pi P_{t}R^{4}_{\rm core}}{GM_{\rm core}}\left(1-\frac{2GM_{\rm core}}{R_{\rm core}c^{2}}\right)\,.

(24)

On the other hand, one can use the thermodynamic relation
\mu=d\mathcal{E}/d\rho
and

\mu=\left(P(r)+\mathcal{E}(r)\,c^{2}\right)/\rho

to write

\displaystyle\frac{d\mu}{\mu}=\frac{dP}{P(r)+\mathcal{E}(r)\,c^{2}}\,,

(25)

which is precisely the left hand side of Eq. (
22
). Combining those expressions and using
M_{\rm core}\ll M
, one obtains, in the crust region, the following:

\displaystyle\frac{d\mu}{\mu}=-dr\,\frac{G\,M}{c^{2}\,r^{2}}\left[1-\frac{2\,G\,M}{rc^{2}}\right]^{-1}\,.

(26)

Integrating from the outer crust up to the core-crust transition, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu_{t}}\frac{d\mu}{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{R_{\rm core}}^{R}\frac{G\,M}{c^{2}\,r^{2}}\left[1-\frac{2\,G\,M}{rc^{2}}\right]^{-1}\,dr\,,

(27)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mu_{t}}{\mu_{0}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1-\dfrac{2\,G\,M}{R\,c^{2}}}{1-\dfrac{2\,G\,M}{R_{\rm core}\,c^{2}}}\,,

(28)

where
\mu_{t}
is the baryon chemical potential at the core-crust transition and
\mu_{0}
is the chemical potential at the surface of the neutron star.

Using Eq.(
28
), one can obtain

\displaystyle R=\frac{2\,G\,M\,R_{\rm core}}{c^{2}\,R_{\rm core}+2\,G\,M\,\left(\dfrac{\mu_{t}}{\mu_{0}}\right)^{2}-c^{2}\,R_{\rm core}\left(\dfrac{\mu_{t}}{\mu_{0}}\right)^{2}}\,.

(29)

Finally, the crustal radius is
AA119-599

\displaystyle R_{\rm crust}=R-R_{\rm core}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\,G\,M\,R_{\rm core}}{c^{2}\,R_{\rm core}+2\,G\,M\,\left(\dfrac{\mu_{t}}{\mu_{0}}\right)^{2}-c^{2}\,R_{core}\left(\dfrac{\mu_{t}}{\mu_{0}}\right)^{2}}-R_{core}

\displaystyle=\phi R_{\rm core}\left[\frac{1-R_{\rm s}/R_{\rm core}}{1-\phi\left(1-R_{\rm s}/R_{\rm core}\right)}\right],

(30)

with
R_{\rm s}=2GM/c^{2}
and
\phi=[(\mu_{t}/\mu_{0})^{2}-1]R_{\rm core}/R_{\rm s}
.

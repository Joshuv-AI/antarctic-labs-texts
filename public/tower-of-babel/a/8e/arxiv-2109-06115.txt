1.
Introduction and main results

Let
S=(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a two dimensional simple random walk and
\big(\omega(n,z)\big)_{(n,z)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
a space-time field of i.i.d. random variables with
{\mathbb{E}}[\omega]=0
,
{\mathbb{E}}[\omega^{2}]=1
and
\lambda(\beta):=\log{\mathbb{E}}[e^{\beta\omega}]<\infty
for all
\beta>0
. We use the notation
\mathrm{P}_{a,x}
and
\mathrm{E}_{a,x}
to denote the probability and the expectation with respect to the distribution of the random walk when the walk starts from
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
at time
a\in\mathbb{N}
. If either

a
or
x
are zero, we will omit them from the subscripts. We consider the (point-to-point) partition function

(1.1)

\displaystyle Z_{N}^{\beta}(x,y)=\mathrm{E}_{x}\bigg[e^{\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\beta\omega(n,S_{n})-\lambda(\beta)}\,\mathbb{1}_{\{S_{N}=y\}}\bigg]\,

of the directed polymer, i.e. random walk, in the random environment
\omega
, at inverse temperature
\beta>0
. We also denote the point-to-line
partition function

(1.2)

\displaystyle Z_{N}^{\beta}(x):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}Z_{N}^{\beta}(x,y),

and simply write
Z_{N}^{\beta}
if
x=0
.

It was observed in
[
CSZ17b
]
that, while for any fixed
\beta>0
,
Z_{N}^{\beta}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0,\,\,{\mathbb{P}}-\text{a.s.}

[
CSY03
,
C17
]
,
an intermediate disorder regime with a phase transition arises when one scales the inverse temperature like

(1.3)

\displaystyle\beta_{N}\sim\hat{\beta}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\log N}}\qquad\text{ with }\hat{\beta}>0\,.

In particular, it was shown in
[
CSZ17b
]
, that for
\beta_{N}\sim\hat{\beta}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\log N}}\text{ with }\hat{\beta}\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle Z_{N}^{\beta_{N}}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{(d)}\exp(\varrho_{\hat{\beta}}\mathsf{X}-\tfrac{1}{2}\varrho^{2}_{\hat{\beta}})\,,

where
\mathsf{X}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
and
\varrho^{2}_{\hat{\beta}}=\log\big(\frac{1}{1-\hat{\beta}^{2}}\big)
, while for
\hat{\beta}\geq 1
,
Z_{N}^{\beta_{N}}
converges in distribution to
0
.

One can guess the emergence of such intermediate scaling as follows. Using gaussian environment for simplicity one has that

(1.4)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\big(Z^{\beta}_{N}\big)^{2}\Big]=\mathrm{E}^{\otimes 2}\Big[e^{\beta^{2}\,\mathsf{L}^{(1,2)}_{N}}\Big]=\mathrm{E}\Big[e^{\beta^{2}\,\mathsf{L}_{N}}\Big]\,,

where
\mathrm{E}^{\otimes 2}
denotes the law of two independent,
2d
simple random walks starting both at the origin,

\mathsf{L}^{(1,2)}_{N}:=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\mathbb{1}_{\{S^{1}_{n}=S^{2}_{n}\}}
denotes their
collision local time

up to time
N-1
and
\mathsf{L}_{N}:=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\mathbb{1}_{\{S_{2n}=0\}}
denotes the number of returns to zero,
up to time
N-1
, of a single
2d
simple random walk starting at
0
.
The second equality in (
1.4
) follows since
\mathsf{L}^{1,2}_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{law}}}{{=}}\mathsf{L}_{N}
.
A classical result of Erdös-Taylor
[
ET60
]
states that

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{\log N}\,\mathsf{L}_{N}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{(d)}\text{Exp}(1)\,,

where
\text{Exp}(1)
denotes an exponential random variable of parameter
1
.
Thus, it is not hard to see that under (
1.3
), one has
\sup_{N\geq 1}{\mathbb{E}}\big[(Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N})^{2}\big]<\infty

if and only if
\hat{\beta}<1
.

It is an interesting and non-trivial question whether all moments of the point-to-plane partition function

Z_{N}^{\beta_{N}}
remain uniformly bounded as
N\to\infty
in the same regime
of
\hat{\beta}
where the second moment remains uniformly bounded.
Information on moments higher than two in the subcritical regime has already appeared necessary in a number of situations, in particular in
proving tightness and regularity properties of the approximations to the solutions of the
2d
-KPZ
[
CD20
]
or
Edwards-Wilkinson universality for the
2d
-KPZ
[
CSZ20
,
G20
]
and
[
T22
]
for the nonlinear SHE.
The lack of control on higher moments was resulting into restrictions to strict subsets of the subcritical regime in
[
CD20
,
G20
]
, while
this was circumvented in
[
CSZ20
]
by employing hypercontractivity to show, for any
\hat{\beta}<1
, the uniform boundedness of moments

up to
certain order
h(\hat{\beta})>2
with
\lim_{\hat{\beta}\uparrow 1}h(\hat{\beta})=2
.
The case of the nonlinear SHE in the entire subcritical regime is still open
[
T22
]
and the techniques here could be useful there.
Moreover, having bounds on the moments can be useful in obtaining finer results relating to the rate of convergences
[
DG22+
]
.

The first result of this work is to show that
all
moments of the
point-to-plane
partition function
Z_{N}^{\beta_{N}}
are uniformly
bounded in the whole subcritical regime
\hat{\beta}<1
while, obviously, no moment higher than one exists in the limit at
\hat{\beta}\geq 1
.
Note that this is in contrast to what happens in the weak disorder regime of
d\geq 3
polymers, where the moments are
gradually reduced to just existence of the first moment as the critical point is approached
[
BS10
,
J22
]
.

Combining this with the distributional convergence (
1.3
) we can actually compute the limit of all moments. In particular,
our first theorem is stated as:

Theorem 1.1.

Consider the point-to-line partition function
Z_{N}^{\beta_{N}}
defined in (
1.2
)
with an intermediate disorder scaling
\beta_{N}
as in (
2.1
), which is asymptotically equivalent to
(
1.3
).
Then, for every
\hat{\beta}\in(0,1)
and
h\geq 0
, it holds that

(1.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\big(Z_{N}^{\beta_{N}}\big)^{h}\Big]=\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\hat{\beta}^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{h(h-1)}{2}}=\bigg(\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\big(Z_{N}^{\beta_{N}}\big)^{2}\Big]\bigg)^{\frac{h(h-1)}{2}}\,.

Furthermore, (
1.6
) is valid also for all
h<0
if we assume that the law of
\omega
satisfies the following concentration property:
There exists
\gamma>1
and constants
c_{1},c_{2}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n})
i.i.d. and all convex,
1
-Lipschitz functions

,

(1.7)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\big|f(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n})-M_{f}\big|\geq t\Big)\leq c_{1}\,\exp\Big(-\frac{t^{\gamma}}{c_{2}}\Big)\,,

where
M_{f}
is a median of
f
.

Remark 1.2.

We note that (
1.7
) is satisfied if
\omega
is bounded or has a density of the form
\exp(-V+U)
for
V,U:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
V
is strictly convex and
U
is bounded, see
[
Led01
]
.

Furthermore, we compute the asymptotics of the moments of the logarithmically scaled and averaged
field

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\log N}}{N}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\varphi(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{N}})\big(Z_{N}^{\beta_{N}}(x)-1\big),

for
\varphi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
In particular, we establish that

Theorem 1.3.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and consider the centred and averaged field with respect to
\varphi
, that is

\displaystyle\widebar{Z}^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(\varphi,1):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\varphi(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{N}})\,\big(Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(x)-1\big)\,\,.

Then, for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
with
h\geq 2
and
\hat{\beta}\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}(\log N)^{\frac{h}{2}}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\widebar{Z}^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(\varphi)^{h}\Big]=\begin{cases}\varrho_{\varphi}(\hat{\beta})^{h}\cdot(h-1)!!&,\text{ if $h$ is even}\\
0&,\text{ if $h$ is odd}\,,\end{cases}

where
\varrho_{\varphi}(\hat{\beta})
is defined as

\displaystyle\varrho^{2}_{\varphi}(\hat{\beta}):=\frac{\pi\,\hat{\beta}^{2}}{1-\hat{\beta}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}t\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\varphi(x)g_{t}(x-y)\varphi(y),

with
g_{t}(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi t}\,e^{-|x|^{2}/2t}
the two-dimensional heat kernel.

Theorem
1.1
in combination with an analogous to (
1.4
) computation for the
h
moment will, almost immediately,
lead us to a generalisation of the Erdös-Taylor theorem (see
[
ET60
]
and
[
GS09
]
for a quenched path generalisation).
The generalisation amounts to
the quantity of
total pairwise collision times
of
h
(instead of just two as in
[
ET60
,
GS09
]
) independent, two-dimensional simple
planar random walks.
More specifically, let
\Gamma(a,1)
denote the Gamma distribution, which is the law with density function

\tfrac{1}{\Gamma(a)}x^{a-1}e^{-x}\,\mathbb{1}_{\{x>0\}}
, where in the last expression
\Gamma(a)
is the gamma function. Then,

Theorem 1.4.

Consider
h\in\mathbb{N}
such that
h\geq 2
and for
i=1,\dots,h
let
S^{(i)}=\big(S^{(i)}_{n}\big)_{n\geq 0}
be independent simple random walks in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
starting all from the origin at time zero. Moreover, for
1\leq i<j\leq h
let

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}^{(i,j)}_{N}:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{1}_{\{S^{(i)}_{n}=S_{n}^{(j)}\}},

denote the collision local time of
S^{(i)}
and
S^{(j)}
until time
N
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{\log N}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq h}\mathsf{L}^{(i,j)}_{N}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{(d)}\Gamma\big(\tfrac{h(h-1)}{2},1\big)\,.

More precisely, if
Y_{N}:=\frac{\pi}{\log N}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq h}\mathsf{L}^{(i,j)}_{N}
,
Y
is a random variable with law
\Gamma\big(\tfrac{h(h-1)}{2},1\big)
and
M_{Y_{N}}(t)
,
M_{Y}(t)
denote the associated moment generating functions, respectively, we have that

\displaystyle M_{Y_{N}}(t)\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}M_{Y}(t)\,,

for all
t\in(0,1):=I
, which is the maximum interval
I\subset(0,\infty)
where
M_{Y}(t)<\infty,\,t\in I
.

The main step towards the above theorems is to establish that, in the subcritical regime,
the moments of the two-dimensional
point-to-line
partition function
Z_{N}^{\beta_{N}}

are uniformly bounded. To state the corresponding theorem, let us briefly introduce the

averaged partition functions
.
For test functions
\varphi,\psi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\varphi
has compact support and
\psi
is bounded, we define the averaged partition function
to be

(1.8)

\displaystyle Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(\varphi,\psi):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x,y}\varphi(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{N}})\,Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(x,y)\,\psi(\tfrac{y}{\sqrt{N}})\,.

Moreover, introduce its centred version as

\bar{Z}^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(\varphi,\psi):=Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(\varphi,\psi)-{\mathbb{E}}\big[Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(\varphi,\psi)\big]
and, similarly,
the centred version of the point-to-line partition function (
1.2
) as
\bar{Z}^{\beta_{N}}_{N}:=Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}-{\mathbb{E}}[Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}]
.

The key estimate of this paper is the following:

Theorem 1.5.

Let
\varphi,\psi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
\varphi
has compact support and
\psi
is bounded and consider the centred, averaged field
\widebar{Z}_{N}^{\beta_{N}}(\varphi,\psi)
with respect to
\varphi,\psi
, as in (
1.8
). Let also
w:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a weight function such that
\log w
is Lipschitz continuous. Then, for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
with
h\geq 3
,
\hat{\beta}\in(0,1)
, there exist
\mathsf{a}_{*}=\mathsf{a}_{*}(h,\hat{\beta},w)\in(0,1)
and
C=C(h,\hat{\beta},w)\in(0,\infty)
such that for any
p,q\in(1,\infty)
that satisfy
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and
p\,q\leq\mathsf{a}_{*}\log N
, the following inequality holds:

(1.9)

\displaystyle\bigg|{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\widebar{Z}_{N}^{\beta_{N}}(\varphi,\psi)^{h}\Big]\bigg|\leq\Big(\frac{C\,p\,q}{\log N}\Big)^{\frac{h}{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{N^{h}}\cdot\left\lVert\frac{\varphi_{N}}{w_{N}}\right\rVert^{h}_{\ell^{p}}\left\lVert\psi_{N}\right\rVert^{h}_{\infty}\left\lVert w_{N}\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}}^{h}\,,

where for
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
we have
\varphi_{N}(x):=\varphi(x/\sqrt{N})
,
\psi_{N}(x):=\psi(x/\sqrt{N})
and
w_{N}(x):=w(x/\sqrt{N})
.
Moreover,
for
\widebar{Z}_{N}^{\beta_{N}}
being the centred, point-to-line partition function, it holds that

(1.10)

\displaystyle\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigg|{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\big(\widebar{Z}_{N}^{\beta_{N}}\big)^{h}\Big]\bigg|<\infty.

Let us comment on estimate (
1.9
) and the significance of the precise dependence of the constant of
the inequality (
1.9
) in terms of
p,q
and
N
as
(pq/\log N)^{h/2}N^{-h}
(the generic constant
C
that appears in the right-hand side
of (
1.9
) does not depend on either
p
,
q
or
N
):
In the case that
\varphi,\psi,w
are nice, smooth functions, i.e. the partition function field is averaged out in an essentially uniform way,
then, by Riemann summation,
N^{-h/p}\|\frac{\varphi_{N}}{w_{N}}\|_{\ell^{p}}^{h}
will converge to
\|\varphi\|^{h}_{L^{p}}
and

N^{-h/p}\|w_{N}\|_{\ell^{q}}^{h}
will converge to
\|w\|^{h}_{L^{q}}
and then the right hand side of (
1.9
) will capture the decay
of correlations of the partition function field as
(\log N)^{-h/2}
.
In contrast to this scenario, if we want to estimate the moments of a
single
partition function, i.e. if we only restrict to
a single starting point, say
Z^{\beta}_{N}(0)
,
we would need to insert in (
1.9
) a
delta-like function

\varphi_{N}(x):=N\mathbb{1}_{\{x=0\}}
.
This choice, however, leads to a blow up in
N
in the right hand side
of (
1.9
) of the form
\big(Cpq/(\log N)\big)^{h/2}\cdot N^{h/q}
.
The idea in order to neutralise the blow up, is to optimise over the choice of
p,q
,
of the corresponding
\ell^{p}
and
\ell^{q}
spaces by choosing

q:=\mathsf{a}\log N
(for certain parameter
\mathsf{a}
).
This is why the explicit dependence of the constant in the right-hand side in terms of
p,q
is crucial
and it is remarkable that this concrete dependence leads to a sharp estimate after a suitable optimisation.
The extraction of the precise dependence on
p,q
in inequality (
1.9
) has been one of our main efforts in this work.

1.1.
Related literature.

Let us explain how our methods and results fit the various aspects of the literature as well as outline some prospects.

Moments, Functional inequalities and Schrödinger operators.

Inequality (
1.9
) controls the moments of the partition function when starting and ending points are averaged out.
A version of (
1.9
) but for
2d
polymer at the critical temperature
and without the important for our purposes dependence on
p,q
was established in
[
CSZ21
]
.
Such inequality was used there as an input to prove uniqueness of the scaling limit of the polymer
field at the critical temperature scaling.
In the continuum setting of the
2d
stochastic heat equation (again at critical temperature scaling),
existence of all moments of the fields averaged by test functions
\phi,\psi
, which are restricted
to only belong to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
was proved in
[
GQT21
]
.

Important input in all the above came from
earlier works of Dell’Antonio-Figari-Teta
[
DFT94
]
and Dimock-Rajeev
[
DR04
]

on the spectral theory of Schrödinger operators with point interactions. The objective there was to define,
via renormalisation, self-adjoint extensions of many-body hamiltonians of the form

\displaystyle\Delta+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq h}\delta(x_{i}-x_{j}),\qquad x_{1},...,x_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

with
\delta(\cdot)
being the delta function on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\Delta
the Laplacian on
(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{h}
.
This problem has a rich history, as outlined in
[
DFT94
]
.
Of particular significance for our purposes is
a functional inequality of Dell’Antonio-Figari-Teta
[
DFT94
]
, Proposition 3.1 (see also Lemma 5.1 in
[
GQT21
]
),
which essentially states that the Green’s function of a
2h-
dimensional Brownian motion

\mathfrak{G}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},...,x_{h})\in(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{h},\boldsymbol{y}=(y_{1},...,y_{h})\in(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{h}
,
when restricted to hyperplanes
\{x_{i}=x_{j}\}
and
\{y_{k}=y_{\ell}\}
with
(i,j)\neq(k,\ell)

is bounded as an operator from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2(h-1)})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2(h-1)})
.
For our purposes, the relevant operator, in the discrete setting is defined in (
3.6
) and (
3.12
) and the
corresponding estimate that establishes the boundedness of the operator in
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{2(h-1)})
,
q>1
, is proved in Proposition
3.3
. To prove this, we follow the approach of

[
CSZ21
]
, working on the real space rather than the Fourier space as
[
DFT94
]
(the latter is only suitable
for
L^{2}
estimates) paying particular attention in extracting the precise dependence on the parameters
p,q

of the
\ell^{p},\ell^{q}
spaces.
As already mentioned after Theorem
1.5
, this is crucial in order to apply
the optimisation trick, which allows us to average over a delta-like function and thus control the moment of
the, more singular,
single-starting point
polymer partition.

Collision local times.

Theorem
1.4
led us to conjecture and prove in
[
LZ22
]
the following multivariate extension of the Erdös-Taylor theorem:

Theorem 1.6 (
[
LZ22
]
).

For
h\geq 3
the joint law of the normalised pairwise collision local times
\Big\{\frac{\,\pi\mathsf{L}^{(i,j)}_{N}\,}{\log N}\Big\}_{1\leq i<j\leq h}

of
h
independent, two-dimensional simple, symmetric random walks
converges in distribution to a vector of
\tfrac{h(h-1)}{2}
independent
Exp
(1)
random variables.

The conjecture emerged from Theorem
1.4
and the fact
that a gamma distributed variable
\Gamma(a,1),a\in\mathbb{N},
may arise as a sum of
a
independent Exp
(1)
random variables,
The methods developed around Theorem
1.5
in the present paper played an important role towards the proof of Theorem
1.6

as they paved the way for a number of necessary approximations. Still, in order to establish the independence of the collision times, one had
to look carefully at the structure of the collisions: There is an intrinsic logarithmic scaling, already manifested in a sense in (
1.5
),
which introduces a certain
separation of scales
. In turn, this separation of scales leads to the phenomenon (called
rewiring
in
[
LZ22
]
)
in which the random walks forget how they tracked collisions and behave as if they only followed the pairwise collisions independently.
We refer to
[
LZ22
]
for further details.

Let us mention that a reader who has followed the literature might have thought initially that Theorem
1.6

could simply follow from an extension of the work
[
CSZ17b
]
to joint convergence of partition functions

Z_{N}^{\beta_{N,1}},...,Z_{N}^{\beta_{N,h}}
at multiple temperatures
\beta_{N,i}=\beta_{i}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\log N}}
for
i=1,...,h
,
together with the moment estimates obtained here. However, this is not sufficient as the computation of

\mathbb{E}[Z_{N}^{\beta_{N,1}}\cdots Z_{N}^{\beta_{N,h}}]
gives rise only to a functional of the form

\mathrm{E}^{\otimes h}[e^{\frac{\pi}{\log N}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq h}\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\mathsf{L}_{N}^{(i,j)}}]
, while one would need to have
\binom{h}{2}

independent parameters
\beta_{i,j},1\leq i<j\leq h
instead of just
\beta_{i}\beta_{j}
to identify the joint distribution.

Theorems
1.4
and
1.6
create some interesting connections with phenomena from planar Brownian motion.
In particular, related to windings of planar Brownian motion
[
Y91
]
and more general, so-called
log-scaling laws

[
PY86
]
,
[
Kn93
]
. A paradigm in these studies is the following:
Let
B^{1},...,B^{h}
denote
h
independent planar Brownian motions starting from distinct points
z_{1},\dots,z_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and for each pair
1\leq i<j\leq h
, we consider
\vartheta^{(i,j)}(t)
to be the total winding angle of
Z^{i,j}_{s}:=B_{s}^{i}-B_{s}^{j}
around
0
. Yor’s theorem
[
Y91
]
asserts that
\big\{\frac{2}{\log t}\,\vartheta^{(i,j)}(t)\big\}_{1\leq i<j\leq h}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{(d)}\big\{C^{(i,j)}\big\}_{1\leq i<j\leq h}
, where
\big\{C^{(i,j)}\big\}_{1\leq i<j\leq h}

are
\tfrac{h(h-1)}{2}
independent Cauchy distributed random variables. This result generalises to multiple Brownian motions the classical Spitzer’s law

[
S58
]
and bears resemblance to Theorem
1.6
.
The above works rely crucial on the continuum methods and the power of stochastic calculus. In this regard,
it would be interesting to investigate the scope of the methods we develop here (as well as in
[
LZ22
]
) for wider applications
and beyond continuum aspects.

Statistics of log-correlated fields.

Let us denote by
\mathfrak{h}_{N}(x):=\sqrt{\log N}\big(\log Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}\big(\lfloor x\sqrt{N}\rfloor-{\mathbb{E}}\log Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}\big(\lfloor x\sqrt{N}\rfloor\big)
, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
It is known
[
CSZ20
,
G20
]
that, in the subcritical regime,
\mathfrak{h}_{N}(x)

converges to the gaussian free field. This, now, raises interesting questions
[
CZ21
]

about asymptotic statistics of extrema of the field
\mathfrak{h}_{N}(x)

as well as whether the exponential of
\mathfrak{h}_{N}(x)
, normalised by its mean, converges to a
Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos (GMC).
The activity in the field of log-correlated fields and on questions of this type for various models is very
large, so we will only refer to some reviews
[
B17
,
BK22
,
BP21
,
DRSV27
,
RV14
]
for further guidance.

Since
\exp\big(\sqrt{\log N}\log Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}\Big)=\big(Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}\big)^{\sqrt{\log N}}
, questions like the above
appear naturally related to information on the asymptotic of moments of the partition function of order
\sqrt{\log N}
.
Progress in this direction, has recently been made in
[
CZ21
]
where the authors showed that there exists a
\hat{\beta}_{0}<1

such that for
\hat{\beta}\leq\hat{\beta}_{0}
and for
h=h_{N}
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{3\hat{\beta}^{2}}{1-\hat{\beta}^{2}}\frac{1}{\log N}{\binom{h}{2}}<1,

then
\mathbb{E}\big[(Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N})^{h}\big]\leq C\big(\frac{1}{1-\hat{\beta}^{2}}\big)^{\tfrac{h(h-1)}{2}(1+\varepsilon_{N})}
,
for
C=C(\hat{\beta})
and
\varepsilon_{N}=\varepsilon_{N}(\hat{\beta})\to 0
for
N\to\infty
.
Tighter estimates might be needed in order to tackle the above questions on extrema and approximation to GMC,
see discussion
[
CZ21
]
, Section 4.

Finally, let us remark that in the continuum setting of the stochastic heat equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+\beta_{\varepsilon}\,\xi^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x)u_{\varepsilon},

with
\xi^{(\varepsilon)}(t,x):=\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}j(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon})\xi(t,x)\mathrm{d}x
the mollified noise
and
\beta_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\hat{\beta}}{\sqrt{\log\varepsilon^{-1}}}
with
\hat{\beta}<\sqrt{2\pi}
, corresponding to the subcritical regime for the
stochastic heat equation, the recent work
[
CSZ22
]
yields that

{\mathbb{E}}u_{\varepsilon}^{h}\geq\big({\mathbb{E}}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\big)^{\frac{h(h-1)}{2}}
, for any positive
h
, irrespective
the dependence of
h
in
\varepsilon
(one should think of a correspondence between
\varepsilon
and
N
as
N=\varepsilon^{-2}
).

2.1.
Partition functions and chaos expansion

Let us start by denoting the transition probability kernel of the underlying, two-dimensional, simple random walk
S
by
q_{n}(x)
for

n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, that is
q_{n}(x):=\mathrm{P}(S_{n}=x)
. Recall from (
1.1
) the definition of the
point-to-plane
partition function

\displaystyle Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(x):=\mathrm{E}_{x}\bigg[e^{\,\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\,\big(\beta_{N}\,\omega(n,S_{n})-\lambda(\beta_{N})\,\big)}\bigg]\,,

where
\beta_{N}
is chosen so that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{N,\beta}:=e^{\lambda(2\beta_{N})-2\lambda(\beta_{N})}-1=\frac{\hat{\beta}^{2}}{R_{N}},

where

(2.2)

\displaystyle R_{N}:=\mathrm{E}^{\otimes 2}\Big[\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{1}_{\{S_{n}^{(1)}=S_{n}^{(2)}\}}\Big]=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}q_{n}(z)^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}q_{2n}(0)\,,

denotes the expected collisions until time
N
of two independent, two-dimensional, simple random walks, starting from the origin.
Note that
[
ET60
]

\displaystyle R_{N}=\frac{\log N}{\pi}+\frac{\alpha}{\pi}+o(1)\,,

where
\alpha:=\gamma+\log 16-\pi\simeq 0.208
and

\gamma\simeq 0.577
is the Euler constant.
By Taylor expansion in (
2.1
), this implies
the asymptotic scaling of
\beta_{N}
as
\beta_{N}\sim\hat{\beta}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\log N}}
for
N\to\infty
.

We shall also need the definition of the
point-to-point
partition functions. In particular, for
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
with
a<b
and
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
we define the
point-to-point
partition function from the space-time point
(a,x)
to
(b,y)
by

(2.3)

\displaystyle Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(x,y):=\mathrm{E}_{a,x}\bigg[e^{\,\sum_{n=a+1}^{b-1}\,\big(\beta_{N}\,\omega(n,S_{n})-\lambda(\beta_{N})\,\big)}\,\mathbb{1}_{\{S_{b}=y\}}\bigg]\,,

Note that with these definitions,

\displaystyle Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}Z^{\beta_{N}}_{0,N}(x,y)\,.

Given
\varphi,\psi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\varphi
has compact support and
\psi
is bounded, we can further define the
averaged partition functions
by,

\displaystyle Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(\varphi,y):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\varphi(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{N}})\,Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(x,y)\,,

\displaystyle Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(x,\psi):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\,Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(x,y)\,\psi(\tfrac{y}{\sqrt{N}})

and

(2.4)

\displaystyle Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(\varphi,\psi):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x,y}\varphi(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{N}})\,Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(x,y)\,\psi(\tfrac{y}{\sqrt{N}})\,.

For
(a,x),(b,y)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
a<b
, the mean of each of the quantities above is computed as

(2.5)

\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}\big[Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(\varphi,y)\big]=\,q^{N}_{a,b}(\varphi,y):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\varphi(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{N}})\,q_{a,b}(x,y)\,,\\
&{\mathbb{E}}\big[Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(x,\psi)\big]=\,q^{N}_{a,b}(x,\psi):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\,q_{a,b}(x,y)\,\psi(\tfrac{y}{\sqrt{N}})\,\end{split}

and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(\varphi,\psi)\big]=q^{N}_{a,b}(\varphi,\psi):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\varphi(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{N}})\,q_{a,b}(x,y)\psi(\tfrac{y}{\sqrt{N}})\,.

Next, we derive an expansion for the point-to-point partition function
Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(x,y)
as a multilinear polynomial, which goes by the name of
chaos expansion
. This is the starting point of our analysis. Recalling (
2.3
) we have

\displaystyle\qquad Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(x,y)=\mathrm{E}_{a,x}\bigg[\prod_{a<n<b}\prod_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{\big(\,\beta_{N}\,\omega(n,z)-\lambda(\beta_{N})\,\big)\,\mathbb{1}_{\{S_{n}=z\}}}\,\mathbb{1}_{\{S_{b}=y\}}\bigg]

and by using the fact that for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
e^{\lambda\,\mathbb{1}_{\{S_{n}=z\}}}=1+(e^{\lambda}-1)\mathbb{1}_{\{S_{n}=z\}}
we obtain

(2.6)

\displaystyle Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(x,y)=\mathrm{E}_{a,x}\bigg[\prod_{a<n<b}\prod_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\big(1+\xi(n,z)\mathbb{1}_{\{S_{n}=z\}}\big)\,\mathbb{1}_{\{S_{b}=y\}}\bigg]\,

where
\xi(n,z):=e^{\beta_{N}\,\omega(n,z)-\lambda(\beta_{N})}-1
are i.i.d. random variables with

(2.7)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\xi]=0\,,\qquad{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{2}]=e^{\lambda(2\beta_{N})-2\lambda(\beta_{N})}-1=:\sigma^{2}_{N,\hat{\beta}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\sim}}\beta_{N}^{2}\,,\quad{\mathbb{E}}\big[|\xi|^{k}\big]\leq C_{k}\,\sigma^{k}_{N,\hat{\beta}}\,\quad\text{for }k\geq 3\,,

for some constants
C_{k}\in(0,\infty)
,
k\geq 3
.
The asymptotic and the bound in (
2.7
) follow by Taylor expansion.
Expanding the product in (
2.6
) yields the following expansion of
Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(x,y)
as a multilinear polynomial of the variables
\xi(n,z)
,

\displaystyle Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(x,y)

\displaystyle=q_{a,b}(x,y)

(2.8)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a<n_{1}<\dots<n_{k}<b\\
z_{1},\dots,z_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\end{subarray}}q_{a,n_{1}}(x,z_{1})\,\xi(n_{1},z_{1})\,\bigg\{\prod_{j=2}^{k}q_{n_{j-1},n_{j}}(z_{j-1},z_{j})\,\xi(n_{j},z_{j})\bigg\}\,q_{n_{k},b}(z_{k},y)\,,

which also leads to

\displaystyle Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle:=q_{a,b}^{N}(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a<n_{1}<\dots<n_{k}<b\\
z_{1},\dots,z_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\end{subarray}}q^{N}_{a,n_{1}}(\varphi,z_{1})\,\xi(n_{1},z_{1})\,\bigg\{\prod_{j=2}^{k}q_{n_{j-1},n_{j}}(z_{j-1},z_{j})\,\xi(n_{j},z_{j})\bigg\}\,q^{N}_{n_{k},b}(z_{k},\psi)

for the averaged point-to-point partition function. Using the notation

\displaystyle\widebar{Z}^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(\varphi,\psi):=Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(\varphi,\psi)-{\mathbb{E}}\big[Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(\varphi,\psi)\big]

for the centred averaged partition function we have that

(2.9)

\displaystyle\widebar{Z}^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(\varphi,\psi):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{k}\\
0<n_{1}<\dots<n_{k}<N\end{subarray}}\hskip-8.5359ptq^{N}_{0,n_{1}}(\varphi,z_{1})\,\xi(n_{1},z_{1})\bigg\{\prod_{j=2}^{k}q_{n_{j-1},n_{j}}(z_{j-1},z_{j})\xi(n_{j},z_{j})\bigg\}q^{N}_{n_{k},N}(z_{k},\psi)\,.

For simplicity, we will denote
Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(\varphi):=Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(\varphi,1)
and

\widebar{Z}^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(\varphi):=\widebar{Z}^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(\varphi,1)
.

2.2.
Renewal representation

We will also need certain renewal representations for the second moment of the point-to-point partition functions.
These were introduced in
[
CSZ19b
]
but only mainly studied in the context of the critical directed polymer therein.
Let
(a,x),(b,y)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
a<b
. We define

(2.10)

\displaystyle U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}\big((a,x),(b,y)\big):=\sigma_{N,\hat{\beta}}^{2}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big[Z^{\beta_{N}}_{a,b}(x,y)^{2}\Big]\,.

By translation invariance

\displaystyle U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}\big((a,x),(b,y)\big)=U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(b-a,y-x):=\sigma_{N,\hat{\beta}}^{2}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big[Z^{\beta_{N}}_{b-a}(y-x)^{2}\Big],

therefore it suffices to work with
U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n,x)
. We furthermore define
U_{N}^{\beta_{N}}(n,x):=\mathbb{1}_{\{x=0\}}
if
n=0
.
Using (
2.1
) and (
2.7
) we derive the expansion

(2.11)

\displaystyle U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n,x)

\displaystyle=\sigma^{2}_{N,\hat{\beta}}\,q^{2}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\geq 1}\sigma^{2(k+1)}_{N,\hat{\beta}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<n_{1}<\dots<n_{k}<n\\
z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\end{subarray}}q^{2}_{0,n_{1}}(0,z_{1})\Big\{\prod_{j=2}^{k}q^{2}_{n_{j-1},n_{j}}(z_{j-1},z_{j})\Big\}\,q^{2}_{n_{k},n}(z_{k},x)\,.

Moreover, for
0\leq n\leq N
we define

(2.12)

\displaystyle U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n,x)\,.

We will, now, recast
U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n,x)
and
U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n)
in a renewal theory framework.
We define a family of i.i.d. random vectors
(\mathsf{t}^{(N)}_{i},\mathsf{x}^{(N)}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\Big(\,\big(\mathsf{t}^{(N)}_{1},\mathsf{x}^{(N)}_{1}\big)=(n,x)\Big)=\frac{q_{n}^{2}(x)}{R_{N}}\,\mathbb{1}_{\{n\leq N\}}\,

and moreover we let
\tau^{(N)}_{k}:=\mathsf{t}_{1}^{(N)}+\dots+\mathsf{t}_{k}^{(N)}
and
S^{(N)}_{k}:=\mathsf{x}^{(N)}_{1}+\dots+\mathsf{x}^{(N)}_{k}
if
k\geq 1
. For
k=0
we set
(\tau_{0},S_{0}):=(0,0)
. Using this framework we see by (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) that

\displaystyle U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n,x)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\hat{\beta}^{2k}\,\mathrm{P}\big(\tau_{k}^{(N)}=n,S^{(N)}_{k}=x\big)

and

\displaystyle U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\hat{\beta}^{2k}\,\mathrm{P}\big(\tau_{k}^{(N)}=n\big)\,.

Finally, we remark that

(2.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}U_{N}^{\beta_{N}}(n)={\mathbb{E}}\Big[(Z^{\beta_{N}}_{N+1})^{2}\Big]\,.

3.1.
Chaos expansion of moments

By (

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{h}}

When

Q_{n}^{I,*}(\boldsymbol{x},\psi^{\otimes h})&:=\mathbb{1}_{\{\boldsymbol{x}\sim I\}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}q^{N}_{n}(x_{i},\psi)\,,\end{split}

see also (
2.5
).
The mixed moment subject to a partition
I
will be denoted by

(3.8)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{I}]:=\prod_{1\leq j\leq|I|,\,|I_{j}|\geq 2}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{|I_{j}|}]\,.

Using this formalism, we can then write

\displaystyle M^{\varphi,\psi}_{N,h}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{h}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0:=n_{0}<n_{1}<\dots<n_{k}\leq N,\\
(I_{1},\dots,I_{k})\in\mathcal{I},\\
m_{i}:=|I_{i}|<h,\,\,\boldsymbol{y}_{i}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{m_{i}}\end{subarray}}Q_{n_{1}}^{*,I_{1}}(\varphi^{\otimes h},\boldsymbol{y}_{1}){\mathbb{E}}\Big[\xi^{I_{1}}\Big]

(3.9)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{i=2}^{k}Q_{n_{i}-n_{i-1}}^{I_{i-1},I_{i}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{i-1},\boldsymbol{y}_{i}){\mathbb{E}}\Big[\xi^{I_{i}}\Big]\cdot Q_{N-n_{k}}^{I_{k},*}(\boldsymbol{y}_{k},\psi^{\otimes h})\,,

where
\mathcal{I}
is the set of all finite sequences of partitions of
\{1,\dots,h\}
,
(I_{1},\dots,I_{k})
, which satisfy the following condition:
For every
r\in\{1,\dots,h\}
there exists
1\leq i\leq k
such that the block

I_{i}
that contains
r
is non-trivial, i.e. it has cardinality equal or larger than
2
.
This restriction comes from the fact that
M^{\varphi,\psi}_{N,h}
are centred moments and therefore every term in the expansion (
3.1
) contains the expected value of a product of disorder variables (
3.3
), which is non-zero only if the product of disorder variables does not contain standalone
\xi
variables, see the discussion below (
3.3
).

Let
B=B(0,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a ball containing the support of
\psi
(allowing the possibility of
r=\infty
, in case
\supp\psi=\mathbb{R}^{2}
). We then have that

Q^{I_{k},*}_{N-n_{k}}\big(\boldsymbol{y}_{k},\psi^{\otimes h}\big)\leq Q^{I_{k},*}_{N-n_{k}}\big(\boldsymbol{y}_{k},\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{h}_{\infty}\mathbb{1}^{\otimes h}_{B}\big)\leq\frac{c}{N}\,\sum_{n_{k+1}\in\{N+1,\dots,2N\}}Q^{I_{k},*}_{n_{k+1}-n_{k}}\big(\boldsymbol{y}_{k},\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{h}_{\infty}\mathbb{1}^{\otimes h}_{B}\big)\,,

with the latter inequality following because the probability that a random walk starting inside the ball
B(0,\sqrt{N}r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
at time
N-n_{k}
is inside
B(0,\sqrt{N}r)
at time
n_{k+1}-n_{k}
with
n_{k+1}\in\{N+1,\dots 2N\}
is uniformly bounded away from zero.

Thus,

\displaystyle\big|\,M^{\varphi,\psi}_{N,h}\,\big|\leq

\displaystyle\,\frac{c\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{h}_{\infty}}{N^{h+1}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0:=n_{0}<n_{1}<\dots<n_{k+1}\leq 2N,\\
(I_{1},\dots,I_{k})\in\mathcal{I},\\
m_{i}:=|I_{i}|<h,\,\,\boldsymbol{y}_{i}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{m_{i}}\end{subarray}}Q_{n_{1}}^{*,I_{1}}(\varphi^{\otimes h},\boldsymbol{y}_{1}){\mathbb{E}}\Big[|\xi|^{I_{1}}\Big]

(3.10)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{i=2}^{k}Q_{n_{i}-n_{i-1}}^{I_{i-1},I_{i}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{i-1},\boldsymbol{y}_{i}){\mathbb{E}}\Big[|\xi|^{I_{i}}\Big]\cdot Q_{n_{k+1}-n_{k}}^{I_{k},*}(\boldsymbol{y}_{k},\mathbb{1}^{\otimes h}_{B})\,.

We also need to define the
replica evolution
. For
I\vdash\{1,\dots,h\}
of the form
I=\{k,\ell\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{j\neq k,\ell}\{j\}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}^{I}_{n}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}):=\mathbb{1}_{\{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\sim I\}}\cdot U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n,y_{k}-x_{k})\cdot\prod_{i\neq k,\ell}q_{n}(y_{i}-x_{i})\,,

where
U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n,y_{k}-x_{k})
is defined in (
2.10
). The replica evolution operator will be used to contract consecutive appearances of the same partition
I
, with
|I|=h-1
in the right-hand side of (
3.1
). In particular, note that if
I\vdash\{1,\dots,h\}
, such that
|I|=h-1
, then

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}^{I}_{n}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})=\sum_{k\geq 0}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{2}]^{k}\,\sum_{0:=n_{0}<n_{1}<\dots<n_{k}:=n}\,\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I}\,,1\leq i\leq k-1,\\
\boldsymbol{y}_{0}:=\boldsymbol{x},\,\boldsymbol{y}_{k}:=\boldsymbol{y}\end{subarray}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{k}Q^{I;I}_{n_{i}-n_{i-1}}(\boldsymbol{y}_{i-1},\boldsymbol{y}_{i})\,.

To be able to estimate the right-hand side of (
3.1
) we will upper bound it by enlarging the domain of the temporal sum in the right-hand side of (
3.1
) from
1\leq n_{1}<\dots<n_{k+1}\leq 2N
to
n_{i}-n_{i-1}\in\{1,\dots,2N\}
for all
1\leq i\leq k+1
. This enlargement of the domain of summation deconvolves the temporal sum in the right-hand side of (
3.1
).

On this account, we introduce the discrete Laplace transforms of the operators
\mathsf{Q}
and
\mathsf{U}
,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Q}_{N,\lambda}^{I,J}(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z}):=\sum_{n=1}^{2N}e^{-\lambda\frac{n}{N}}\,Q^{I,J}_{n}(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z}),\,\qquad\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}_{N,\lambda}^{I}(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z}):=\sum_{n=0}^{2N}e^{-\lambda\frac{n}{N}}\,\mathsf{U}^{I}_{n}(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z}),\qquad\quad\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}\,,

for
\lambda\geq 0
.

Remark 3.1.

In our case, it will be sufficient to work with
\lambda=0
, however, keeping a non zero
\lambda

and tracking the dependence of the estimate on it (together with a closer tracking on the order
h
of the moment)
would be necessary in order to extend our method so that to accommodate
h
that grows with
N
.

Let us define

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}^{I;J}_{N,\hat{\beta}}=\begin{cases}\mathsf{Q}^{I;J}_{N,0}\,,&\text{ if }|J|<h-1\\
\mathsf{Q}^{I;J}_{N,0}\,\mathsf{U}^{J}_{N,0}\,,&\text{ if }|J|=h-1\,.\end{cases}

Note that the appearance of the operator
\mathsf{U}^{J}_{N,0}
is necessarily preceded by a free evolution operator
\mathsf{Q}^{I;J}_{N,0}
, with
|J|=h-1
, see also Figure
1
.
In view of (
3.1
) and the discussion above we can now write

\displaystyle\big|M^{\varphi,\psi}_{N,h}\big|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{h}_{\infty}}{N^{h+1}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{(I_{1},\dots,I_{k})\in\mathcal{I}}\Big\langle\varphi_{N}^{\otimes h},\mathsf{P}^{*,I_{1}}_{N,\hat{\beta}}\,\mathsf{P}^{I_{1},I_{2}}_{N,\hat{\beta}}\cdots\,\mathsf{P}_{N,\hat{\beta}}^{I_{k},*}\mathbb{1}^{\otimes h}_{\sqrt{N}B}\Big\rangle\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[|\xi|^{I_{i}}\Big]\,,

where we recall the definition of the pairing
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
from (
3.4
) and note that the sum runs over partitions
I_{1},\dots,I_{k}
such that
I_{j}\neq I_{j+1}
if
|I_{j}|=|I_{j+1}|=h-1
for
1\leq j\leq k-1
.

Because of the assumption of Theorem
1.5
on
\psi
being merely a bounded function we will need to introduce weighted versions of the operators
\mathsf{U}^{I}_{N,\lambda}
,
\mathsf{Q}^{I;J}_{N,\lambda}
and
\mathsf{P}^{I;J}_{N,\hat{\beta}}
. In particular, if
w:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is such that
\log w
is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant denoted by
C_{w}>0
,
and if we denote by
w_{N}(x)=w\big(\frac{x}{\sqrt{N}}\big)
, then we define for
\lambda\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}^{I;J}_{N,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}):=\frac{w_{N}^{\otimes h}(\boldsymbol{x})}{w^{\otimes h}_{N}(\boldsymbol{y})}\,\mathsf{Q}^{I;J}_{N,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\,,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{U}}^{I}_{N,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}):=\frac{w_{N}^{\otimes h}(\boldsymbol{x})}{w^{\otimes h}_{N}(\boldsymbol{y})}\,\mathsf{U}^{I}_{N,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\,,

where we recall that
w_{N}^{\otimes h}(\boldsymbol{x})=w_{N}(x_{1})\cdots w_{N}(x_{h})
, if
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{h})
. We modify accordingly the operator
\mathsf{P}^{I;J}_{N,\hat{\beta}}
into a new operator
\widehat{\mathsf{P}}^{I;J}_{N,\hat{\beta}}
,

(3.14)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{P}}^{I;J}_{N,\hat{\beta}}=\begin{cases}\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}^{I;J}_{N,0}&,\text{ if }|J|<h-1\\
\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}^{I;J}_{N,0}\,\widehat{\mathsf{U}}^{J}_{N,0}&,\text{ if }|J|=h-1\,.\end{cases}

Therefore, we can now write

(3.15)

\begin{split}\big|M^{\varphi,\psi}_{N,h}\big|\leq&\frac{c\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{h}_{\infty}}{N^{h+1}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{(I_{1},\dots,I_{k})\in\mathcal{I}}\Big\langle\frac{\varphi_{N}^{\otimes h}}{w_{N}^{\otimes h}},\widehat{\mathsf{P}}^{*,I_{1}}_{N,\hat{\beta}}\,\widehat{\mathsf{P}}^{I_{1},I_{2}}_{N,\hat{\beta}}\cdots\,\widehat{\mathsf{P}}_{N,\hat{\beta}}^{I_{k},*}\mathbb{1}^{\otimes h}_{\sqrt{N}B}w_{N}^{\otimes h}\Big\rangle\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[|\xi|^{I_{i}}\Big]\,\\
\leq&\frac{c\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{h}_{\infty}}{N^{h+1}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{(I_{1},\dots,I_{k})\in\mathcal{I}}\Big\langle\frac{\varphi_{N}^{\otimes h}}{w_{N}^{\otimes h}},\widehat{\mathsf{P}}^{*,I_{1}}_{N,\hat{\beta}}\,\widehat{\mathsf{P}}^{I_{1},I_{2}}_{N,\hat{\beta}}\cdots\,\widehat{\mathsf{P}}_{N,\hat{\beta}}^{I_{k},*}w_{N}^{\otimes h}\Big\rangle\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[|\xi|^{I_{i}}\Big]\end{split}

where we bounded the indicator function
\mathbb{1}^{\otimes h}_{\sqrt{N}B}
by
1
to obtain the second inequality.
Passing to the operator norms (see (
3.5
)) we estimate

(3.16)

\displaystyle\big|M^{\varphi,\psi}_{N,h}\big|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{h}_{\infty}}{N^{h+1}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{(I_{1},\dots,I_{k})\in\mathcal{I}}\left\lVert\widehat{\mathsf{P}}^{*,I_{1}}_{N,\hat{\beta}}\frac{\varphi_{N}^{\otimes h}}{w^{\otimes h}_{N}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\,\prod_{i=2}^{k}\,\left\lVert\widehat{\mathsf{P}}_{N,\hat{\beta}}^{I_{i-1},I_{i}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}\to\ell^{q}}\,\left\lVert\widehat{\mathsf{P}}_{N,\hat{\beta}}^{I_{k},*}w_{N}^{\otimes h}\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[|\xi|^{I_{i}}\Big]\,.

This is the key expansion we will use for the Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

3.2.
Integral inequalities for the operators
\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}^{I;J}_{N,0}
and
\widehat{\mathsf{U}}^{I}_{N,0}

At this point, we will prove the key estimates
about the operators
\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}^{I;J}_{N,0},\widehat{\mathsf{U}}^{I}_{N,0}
that we will need along the way.
In what follows we shall use the letter
C
to denote constants that may depend only on
h,\hat{\beta}
and
w
but not on
p
and
q
. We will also use the letter
c
to denote absolute constants, i.e. constants that do not depend on
h,\hat{\beta},w
or
p,q
. Their value may change from line to line.

We start this subsection by stating a lemma on the operator
\mathsf{Q}_{N,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}):=\sum_{n=1}^{2N}e^{-\tfrac{\lambda n}{N}}Q_{n}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})
from
[
CSZ21
]
which we will need in the sequel.

Lemma 3.2 (
[
CSZ21
]
).

Let
N\geq 1
,
h\geq 2
and
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}
. Then, there exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that
uniformly in
N
,
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}
and
\lambda\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{Q}_{N,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\leq\begin{cases}\,\,\frac{C}{\big(1+|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|^{2}\big)^{h-1}}&\text{ for all }\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}\,,\\
\,\,\frac{C}{N^{h-1}}\,\exp\bigg(\frac{-|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|^{2}}{C\,N}\bigg)&\text{ if }|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|>\sqrt{N}\,.\end{cases}

The next proposition contains the central estimate. It is on the operator norm of
operator
\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}^{I;J}_{N,0}
, as an operator from an
\ell^{q}\to\ell^{q}
,
containing the explicit dependence on the parameters
p,q
.

Proposition 3.3.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
such that
\tfrac{1}{p}+\tfrac{1}{q}=1
. There exists a constant
C=C(h,w)\in(0,\infty)
, independent of
p
and
q
, such that for all
I,J\vdash\{1,\dots,h\}
with
1\leq|I|,|J|\leq h-1
and
I\neq J
when
|I|=|J|=h-1
,

(3.17)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}^{I;J}_{N,0}\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}\to\ell^{q}}\leq C\,p\,q\,.

Let
I,J\vdash\{1,\dots,h\}
with
1\leq|I|,|J|\leq h-1
and
I\neq J
when
|I|=|J|=h-1
and consider
f\in\ell^{p}\big((\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I}\big)
,
g\in\ell^{q}\big((\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{J}\big)
. In view of (
3.5
), in order to prove (
3.17
), we need to prove that there exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I},\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{J}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\mathsf{Q}^{I;J}_{N,0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\frac{w^{\otimes h}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x})}{w^{\otimes h}_{N}(\boldsymbol{y})}g(\boldsymbol{y})\leq C\,p\,q\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}}\,.

Let

(3.19)

\displaystyle E_{N}:=\Big\{(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I}\times(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{J}:|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|\leq C_{0}\sqrt{N}\Big\}\,.

for some
C_{0}>0
to be determined. By the second inequality in Lemma
3.2
and the Lipschitz condition on
\log w
, we can choose
C_{0}
large enough so that for all
(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\in E_{N}^{c}
we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Q}^{I;J}_{N,0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\frac{w^{\otimes h}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x})}{w^{\otimes h}_{N}(\boldsymbol{y})}\leq\frac{C}{N^{h-1}}\exp\big(-\tfrac{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|}{\sqrt{N}}\big)\,.

Therefore, on
E_{N}^{c}
we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\in E_{N}^{c}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\mathsf{Q}^{I;J}_{N,0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\frac{w^{\otimes h}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x})}{w^{\otimes h}_{N}(\boldsymbol{y})}g(\boldsymbol{y})\leq\frac{C}{N^{h-1}}\sum_{(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\in E_{N}^{c}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\exp\big(-\tfrac{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|}{\sqrt{N}}\big)g(\boldsymbol{y})\,

and by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{h-1}}\sum_{(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\in E_{N}^{c}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\exp\big(-\tfrac{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|}{\sqrt{N}}\big)g(\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{N^{h-1}}\,\Bigg(\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I},\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{J}}|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{p}\exp\Big(-\tfrac{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|}{\sqrt{N}}\Big)\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Bigg(\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I},\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{J}}|g(\boldsymbol{y})|^{q}\exp\Big(-\tfrac{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|}{\sqrt{N}}\Big)\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\,N^{\textstyle\frac{|J|}{p}+\frac{|I|}{q}-(h-1)}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}}

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}}\,,

where the inequality in the last line of (
3.2
) follows by the assumption
|I|,|J|\leq h-1
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\in E_{N}^{c}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\mathsf{Q}^{I;J}_{N,0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\frac{w^{\otimes h}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x})}{w^{\otimes h}_{N}(\boldsymbol{y})}g(\boldsymbol{y})\leq C\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}}\,,

for a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
.

Let us now estimate the sum over
(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\in E_{N}
.
Recalling that
\log w
is Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
C_{w}
and (
3.19
), we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\in E_{N}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\mathsf{Q}^{I;J}_{N,0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\frac{w^{\otimes h}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x})}{w^{\otimes h}_{N}(\boldsymbol{y})}g(\boldsymbol{y})\leq e^{C_{w}\,C_{0}}\sum_{(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\in E_{N}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\mathsf{Q}^{I;J}_{N,0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})g(\boldsymbol{y})\,.

Therefore, using the first inequality of Lemma
3.2
, the key step is to show that there exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
that may depend on
h
and
w
but not on
p
and
q
, such that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I},\,\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{J}}\frac{f(\boldsymbol{x})g(\boldsymbol{y})}{\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{h}|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{2}\Big)^{h-1}}\leq C\,p\,q\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}}\,.

By assumption there exist
1\leq k,\ell\leq h
such that
k\stackrel{{\scriptstyle I}}{{\sim}}\ell
and
1\leq m,n\leq h
such that
m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle J}}{{\sim}}n
. Since we have assumed that
I\neq J
when
|I|=|J|=h-1
, we may assume without loss of generality that
m\neq k,\ell
. Let
a\in\big(0,\min\{p^{-1},q^{-1}\}\big)
to be determined later. By multiplying and dividing by
\frac{1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2a}}{1+|y_{k}-y_{\ell}|^{2a}}
and using Hölder’s inequality, the left-hand side of (
3.21
) is upper bounded by

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I},\,\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{J}}

\displaystyle\frac{|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{p}}{\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{h}|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{2}\Big)^{h-1}}\cdot\frac{\big(1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2a}\big)^{p}}{\big(1+|y_{k}-y_{\ell}|^{2a}\big)^{p}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(3.22)

\displaystyle\times\Bigg(\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I},\,\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{J}}\frac{|g(\boldsymbol{y})|^{q}}{\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{h}|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{2}\Big)^{h-1}}\cdot\frac{\big(1+|y_{k}-y_{\ell}|^{2a}\big)^{q}}{\big(1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2a}\big)^{q}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,.

By symmetry, it is enough to bound one of the two factors in (
3.2
). By triangle inequality and the fact that
m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle J}}{{\sim}}n
, which means that
y_{m}=y_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle|x_{m}-y_{m}|^{2}+|x_{n}-y_{n}|^{2}\geq\frac{|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2}+|x_{n}-y_{n}|^{2}}{4}\,.

Therefore,

(3.23)

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I},\,\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{J}}\frac{|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{p}}{\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{h}|x_{i}-y_{j}|^{2}\Big)^{h-1}}\cdot\frac{\big(1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2a}\big)^{p}}{\big(1+|y_{k}-y_{\ell}|^{2a}\big)^{p}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,4^{\frac{h-1}{p}}\Bigg(\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I}}|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{p}\,(1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2a})^{p}\,\,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{J}}\frac{1}{\Big(1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2}+\sum_{i\neq m}|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{2}\Big)^{h-1}\big(1+|y_{k}-y_{\ell}|^{2a}\big)^{p}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

By using (
A.1
) of Lemma
A.1
and summing successively the
y_{i}
variables for
i\neq k,\ell
we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{J}}\frac{1}{\Big(1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2}+\sum_{i\neq m}|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{2}\Big)^{h-1}(1+|y_{k}-y_{\ell}|^{2a})^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,c^{|J|-2}\sum_{y_{k},\,y_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{\Big(1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2}+|y_{k}-x_{k}|^{2}+|y_{\ell}-x_{\ell}|^{2}\Big)^{h+1-|J|}\big(1+|y_{k}-y_{\ell}|^{2a}\big)^{p}}\,.

We make a change of variables
w_{1}=y_{k}-y_{\ell}
and
w_{2}=y_{k}+y_{\ell}-2x_{k}
and observe that
\frac{w^{2}_{1}+w^{2}_{2}}{2}=|y_{k}-x_{k}|^{2}+|y_{\ell}-x_{\ell}|^{2}
, where we used that
k\stackrel{{\scriptstyle I}}{{\sim}}\ell
thus
x_{k}=x_{\ell}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle c^{|J|-2}\sum_{y_{k},\,y_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{\Big(1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2}+|y_{k}-x_{k}|^{2}+|y_{\ell}-x_{\ell}|^{2}\Big)^{h+1-|J|}\big(1+|y_{k}-y_{\ell}|^{2a}\big)^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,2^{h+1-|J|}\,c^{|J|-2}\sum_{w_{1},\,w_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{\Big(1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2}+|w_{1}|^{2}+|w_{2}|^{2}\Big)^{h+1-|J|}\big(1+|w_{1}|^{2a}\big)^{p}}\,.

By summing
w_{2}
and using (
A.1
) of Lemma
A.1
we have,

\displaystyle 2^{h+1-|J|}\,c^{|J|-2}\sum_{w_{1},\,w_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{\Big(1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2}+|w_{1}|^{2}+|w_{2}|^{2}\Big)^{h+1-|J|}\big(1+|w_{1}|^{2a}\big)^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,2^{h+1-|J|}\,c^{|J|-1}\,\,\sum_{w_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{\Big(1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2}+|w_{1}|^{2}\Big)^{h-|J|}\big(1+|w_{1}|^{2a}\big)^{p}}

By (

) of Lemma

\displaystyle 2^{h+1-|J|}\,c^{|J|-1}\,\sum_{w_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{\Big(1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2}+|w_{1}|^{2}\Big)^{h-|J|}\big(1+|w_{1}|^{2a}\big)^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,2^{h+1-|J|}\,c^{|J|}\,\frac{1}{ap(1-ap)}\frac{1}{\big(1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2}\big)^{ap+h-1-|J|}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,2^{h+1-|J|}\,c^{|J|}\frac{1}{ap(1-ap)}\frac{1}{\big(1+|x_{m}-x_{n}|^{2}\big)^{ap}}\,,

where in the last inequality we used that

by assumption. Therefore, the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle=

By symmetry we also obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I},\,\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{J}}\frac{f(\boldsymbol{x})g(\boldsymbol{y})}{\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{h}|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{2}\Big)^{h-1}}\leq\,C^{J}_{p,h}\,C^{I}_{q,h}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}}\,.

We optimise by choosing

which implies that

\displaystyle C^{J}_{p,h}\,C^{I}_{q,h}=2^{3h+1}\,\Big(\frac{c}{2}\Big)^{\frac{|J|}{p}+\frac{|I|}{q}}\,p\,q\,.

Noting that

which together with (

Proposition 3.4.

such that

.

3.3
. Let

\displaystyle\,C\,N^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{h}_{\ell^{q}}\,,

. Therefore, in light of the first inequality of Lemma

\displaystyle\sum_{(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\in E_{N}}\frac{f(\boldsymbol{x})g^{\otimes h}(\boldsymbol{y})}{\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{h}|x_{i}-y_{j}|^{2}\Big)^{h-1}}\leq C\,p\,N^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{h}_{\ell^{q}}\,.

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sum_{(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\in E_{N}}\frac{|g^{\otimes h}(\boldsymbol{y})|^{q}}{\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{h}|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{2}\Big)^{h-1}\log\Big(1+\frac{C^{2}_{0}N}{1+|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}}\Big)}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

Proposition 3.5.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. There exists a constant
C=C(h,\hat{\beta},w)\in(0,\infty)
, independent of
p
and
q
, such that for all
I\vdash\{1,\dots,h\}
with
|I|=h-1
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\widehat{\mathsf{U}}_{N,0}^{I}\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}\to\ell^{q}}\leq C\,.

Using (
3.5
) it suffices to prove that if
f\in\ell^{p}\big((\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I}\big)
,
g\in\ell^{q}\big((\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I}\big)
, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\mathsf{U}^{I}_{N,0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\frac{w^{\otimes h}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x})}{w_{N}^{\otimes h}(\boldsymbol{y})}g(\boldsymbol{y})\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\ell^{q}}\,.

By the Lipschitz condition on
\log w
we first have

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\mathsf{U}^{I}_{N,0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\frac{w^{\otimes h}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x})}{w_{N}^{\otimes h}(\boldsymbol{y})}g(\boldsymbol{y})\leq\sum_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\mathsf{U}^{I}_{N,0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})e^{C_{w}\frac{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|}{\sqrt{N}}}g(\boldsymbol{y})\,,

which by Hölder’s inequality is bounded by

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sum_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I}}|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{p}\,\mathsf{U}^{I}_{N,0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})e^{C_{w}\frac{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|}{\sqrt{N}}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\Bigg(\sum_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I}}|g(\boldsymbol{y})|^{q}\,\mathsf{U}^{I}_{N,0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})e^{C_{w}\frac{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|}{\sqrt{N}}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,.

Therefore, in order to conclude the proof of (
3.5
) it suffices to prove that there exists a constant
C
such that uniformly in
\boldsymbol{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I}
,

(3.37)

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I}}\mathsf{U}^{I}_{N,0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})e^{C_{w}\frac{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|}{\sqrt{N}}}\leq C\,.

Recall from (
3.11
) that if
I
is of the form
I=\{k,\ell\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{j\neq k,\ell}\{j\}
then for
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{h}_{I}
the operator
\mathsf{U}^{I}_{N,0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathsf{U}^{I}_{N,0}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})=\sum_{n=0}^{2N}\mathsf{U}^{I}_{n}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})=\mathbb{1}_{\{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\sim I\}}\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{2N}U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n,y_{k}-x_{k})\cdot\prod_{i\neq k,\ell}q_{n}(y_{i}-x_{i})\,.

Therefore, in view of (
3.37
), we shall prove that uniformly in
0\leq n\leq 2N
,

(3.38)

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n,z)e^{C_{w}\frac{|z|}{\sqrt{N}}}\leq C\,U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n)

and

(3.39)

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}q_{n}(z)e^{C_{w}\frac{|z|}{\sqrt{N}}}\leq C\,q_{n}(z)\,.

Inequality (
3.39
) follows easily by the local CLT, see
[
LL10
]
and Gaussian concentration.
For the sake of the presentation, we will prove (
3.38
) for
0\leq n\leq N
, that is,

(3.40)

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n,z)e^{C_{w}\frac{|z|}{\sqrt{N}}}\leq C\,U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n)\,,\qquad\forall\,\,0\leq n\leq N\,.

Note that, by (
2.13
) we have,

(3.41)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n)\leq{\mathbb{E}}\Big[(Z_{N+1}^{\beta_{N}})^{2}\Big]\leq\frac{C}{1-\hat{\beta}^{2}}\,.

Moreover, following the renewal framework we introduced in Section
2
, we have

(3.42)

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n,z)e^{C_{w}\frac{|z|}{\sqrt{N}}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}{\hat{\beta}}^{2k}\,\mathrm{E}\bigg[e^{C_{w}\frac{|S^{(N)}_{k}|}{\sqrt{N}}};\,\tau_{k}^{(N)}=n\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}{\hat{\beta}}^{2k}\sum_{n_{1}+\dots+n_{k}=n}\,\mathrm{E}\bigg[e^{C_{w}\frac{|S^{(N)}_{k}|}{\sqrt{N}}}\,\Big|\,\mathsf{t}^{(N)}_{i}=n_{i}\,,1\leq i\leq k\bigg]\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{P}\big(\mathsf{t}_{i}^{(N)}=n_{i}\big)\,.

Therefore, in order to establish (
3.40

C\in(0,\infty)
, such that for all
k\geq 1
,

(3.43)

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\bigg[e^{C_{w}\frac{|S^{(N)}_{k}|}{\sqrt{N}}}\,\Big|\,\mathsf{t}^{(N)}_{i}=n_{i}\,,1\leq i\leq k\bigg]\leq C\,.

We note that when we condition on the times
\big(\mathsf{t}^{(N)}_{i}\big)_{1\leq i\leq k}
, the space increments
\big(\mathsf{x}^{(N)}_{i}\big)_{1\leq i\leq k}
are independent with distribution

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\big(\mathsf{x}^{(N)}_{1}=x\,\big|\,\mathsf{t}_{1}^{(N)}=n_{1}\big)=\frac{q_{n_{1}}^{2}(x)}{q_{2n_{1}}(0)}\,\mathbb{1}_{\{n_{1}\leq N\}}\,.

Let
\lambda\geq 0
and
(\xi_{i})_{1\leq i\leq k}
independent random variables such that

\xi_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{law}}}{{=}}\mathsf{x}_{i}^{(N)}\big|\,\mathsf{t}^{(N)}_{i}=n_{i}
. We will show that

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\Big[e^{\lambda|\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\xi_{i}}|}\,\Big]\leq 2e^{4c\lambda^{2}n}\,,

for some
c>0
. Therefore, taking
\lambda=\frac{C_{w}}{\sqrt{N}}
will lead to (
3.43
). To this end, for each
1\leq i\leq k
, let
\xi_{i,1},\xi_{i,2}\in\mathbb{Z}
be the two components of
\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.Then we can find
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\big[e^{\pm\,\lambda\,\xi_{i,j}}\big]\leq e^{c\lambda^{2}n_{i}}

for
j=1,2
, since by the local CLT we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}(\xi_{i}=x)=\frac{q_{n_{i}}^{2}(x)}{q_{2n_{i}}(0)}\leq\Big(\frac{\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}q_{n_{i}}(x)}{q_{2n_{i}}(0)}\Big)\,q_{n_{i}}(x)\leq C^{\prime}q_{n_{i}}(x)\,

and
q_{n_{i}}(x)=2(g_{{n_{i}}/2}(x)+o(1))
, thus
q_{n_{i}}
has Gaussian tail decay. By Cauchy-Schwarz we

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\Big[e^{\lambda|\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\xi_{i}}|}\,\Big]\leq\mathrm{E}\Big[e^{2\lambda|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{i,1}|}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big[e^{2\lambda|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{i,2}|}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Also, by the inequality
e^{|x|}\leq e^{x}+e^{-x}
and independence, we obtain for
j=1,2

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\Big[e^{2\lambda|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{i,j}|}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\Bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{E}[e^{2\lambda\xi_{i,j}}]+\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{E}[e^{-2\lambda\xi_{i,j}}]\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\Big(2e^{4c\lambda^{2}n}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,

therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\Big[e^{\lambda|\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\xi_{i}}|}\,\Big]\leq 2e^{4c\lambda^{2}n}\,.

Given the inequality above and choosing
\lambda=\frac{C_{w}}{\sqrt{N}}
we get that

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\bigg[e^{C_{w}\frac{|S^{(N)}_{k}|}{\sqrt{N}}}\,\Big|\,\mathsf{t}^{(N)}_{i}=n_{i}\,,1\leq i\leq k\bigg]\leq 2e^{4c\,C_{w}^{2}}\,,

since
1\leq n\leq N
. Therefore, recalling (
3.41
) and (
3.42
),we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\\
0\leq n\leq N\end{subarray}}U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n,z)e^{C_{w}\frac{|z|}{\sqrt{N}}}\leq\,2e^{4c\,C_{w}^{2}}\,\sum_{n=0}^{N}U^{\beta_{N}}_{N}(n)\leq 2e^{4c\,C_{w}^{2}}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big[({Z_{N+1}^{\beta_{N}}})^{2}\Big]\leq C\,,

for a constant
C=C(h,\hat{\beta},w)\in(0,\infty)
.
∎

In particular,

Appendix A
Some technical estimates

We state here the integral estimates we used for proving Propositions
3.3
and
3.4
.

Lemma A.1.

Let
\lambda\geq 1
,
p>1
,
a<\tfrac{1}{p}
. Then,

(A.1)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}}\leq\frac{c}{\lambda^{r-1}}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ if }r\geq 2\,,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}\big(1+|y|^{2a}\big)^{p}}\leq\,\frac{c}{ap\,(1-ap)\lambda^{r-1+ap}}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ if }r\geq 1\,,

for a constant
c\in(0,\infty)
, that does not depend on
\lambda,p,a
or
r
.

We note that since
\displaystyle y\mapsto\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}}
and
\displaystyle y\mapsto\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}\big(1+|y|^{2a}\big)^{p}}
are decreasing in the radial direction we have that

(A.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{r}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\,\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,

and

(A.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}\big(1+|y|^{2a}\big)^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{r}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\,\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}|y|^{2ap}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,.

In order to prove (
A.1
), we switch to polar coordinates in (
A.3
), so that

(A.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\,\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}}\,\mathrm{d}y=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varrho}{(\lambda+\varrho^{2})^{r}}\,\mathrm{d}\varrho\,=\pi\cdot\frac{(\lambda+\varrho^{2})^{1-r}}{1-r}\bigg|_{\varrho=0}^{\varrho=\infty}=\frac{\pi}{r-1}\,\frac{1}{\lambda^{r-1}}\,.

Therefore, by (
A.3
) and (
A.5
) we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{r}}+\frac{\pi}{r-1}\frac{1}{\lambda^{r-1}}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{r-1}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{\pi}{r-1}\bigg)\,.

Thus, since
r\geq 2
and
\lambda\geq 1
we conclude (
A.1
)
with
c=\pi+1
.

For (
A.2
) we split the integral in (
A.4
) into two regions,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}|y|^{2ap}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,=\underbrace{\int_{|y|\leq\sqrt{\lambda}}\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}|y|^{2ap}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,}_{:=I_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{|y|>\sqrt{\lambda}}\,\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}|y|^{2ap}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,}_{:=I_{2}}\,.

First,

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{r}}\int_{|y|\leq\sqrt{\lambda}}\,\frac{1}{|y|^{2ap}}\,\mathrm{d}y=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda^{r}}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}\,\frac{1}{\varrho^{2ap-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\varrho=\frac{\pi}{\lambda^{r}}\frac{\lambda^{1-ap}}{1-ap}=\frac{\pi}{1-ap}\frac{1}{\lambda^{r-1+ap}}\,.

Similarly,

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\int_{|y|>\sqrt{\lambda}}\,\frac{1}{|y|^{2r+2ap}}\mathrm{d}y=2\pi\int_{\sqrt{\lambda}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\varrho^{2r+2ap-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\varrho\,=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{r-1+ap}\frac{-1}{\varrho^{2r+2ap-2}}\bigg|^{\varrho=\infty}_{\varrho=\sqrt{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{r-1+ap}\frac{1}{\lambda^{r-1+ap}}\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\,\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}|y|^{2ap}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,=I_{1}+I_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\,\frac{\pi}{1-ap}\frac{1}{\lambda^{r-1+ap}}+\frac{\pi}{r-1+ap}\frac{1}{\lambda^{r-1+ap}}

(A.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\pi\,r}{(1-ap)(r-1+ap)}\frac{1}{\lambda^{r-1+ap}}\,.

By (
A.4
) and (
A
) we thus obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}\big(1+|y|^{2a}\big)^{p}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{r}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\,\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}|y|^{2ap}}\,\mathrm{d}y

(A.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{r}}+\frac{\pi\,r}{(1-ap)(r-1+ap)}\frac{1}{\lambda^{r-1+ap}}\,.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\pi\,r}{(1-ap)(r-1+ap)}\leq\frac{\pi}{(1-ap)ap}\,,

since that inequality is equivalent to
(r-1)(ap-1)\leq 0
, which is valid since we have assumed that
a\,p<1
and
r\geq 2
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{r}}+\frac{\pi\,r}{(1-ap)(r-1+ap)}\frac{1}{\lambda^{r-1+ap}}\leq\,\frac{1}{\lambda^{r}}+\frac{\pi}{(1-ap)ap}\frac{1}{\lambda^{r-1+ap}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{r-1+ap}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda^{1-ap}}+\frac{\pi}{(1-ap)ap}\bigg)

(A.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{r-1+ap}}\bigg(1+\frac{\pi}{(1-ap)ap}\bigg)\,,

since
\lambda\geq 1
and
1-ap>0
, by assumption.
Last, we have that

(A.9)

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\pi}{(1-ap)ap}=\frac{\pi+ap(1-ap)}{ap(1-ap)}\leq\frac{1+\pi}{ap(1-ap)}\,.

Hence, by (
A
), (
A
) and (
A.9
),

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{\big(\lambda+|y|^{2}\big)^{r}\big(1+|y|^{2a}\big)^{p}}\leq\frac{c}{(1-ap)\,ap\,\lambda^{r-1+ap}}\,,

with
c=1+\pi
, thus concluding the proof of (
A.2
).
∎

Lemma A.2.

There exists a constant
c\in(0,\infty)
such that uniformly in
A,\lambda,p\geq 1
,

(A.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
|y|\leq\sqrt{A}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\lambda+|y|^{2}}\leq c\log\Big(1+\frac{A}{\lambda}\Big)\,\,,

(A.11)

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{1}^{A}\big(\log\big(\tfrac{A}{x}\big)\big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq p\,A^{\frac{1}{p}}\,,\,

and

(A.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
|y|\leq 2\sqrt{A}\end{subarray}}\Big(\log\Big(1+\frac{A}{1+|y|^{2}}\Big)\Big)^{p}\leq c\,A\,p^{p}\,.

For (
A.10
), using the same reasoning as in the proof of Lemma
A.1
we have

(A.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
|y|\leq\sqrt{A}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\lambda+|y|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}+\int_{|y|\leq\sqrt{A}}\,\frac{1}{\lambda+|y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,.

Switching to polar coordinates in (
A.13
) we have

(A.14)

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\leq\sqrt{A}}\frac{1}{\lambda+|y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,=2\pi\int_{0}^{\sqrt{A}}\frac{\varrho}{\lambda+\varrho^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\varrho=\pi\,\log(\lambda+\varrho^{2})\Big|_{\varrho=0}^{\varrho=\sqrt{A}}=\pi\log\Big(1+\frac{A}{\lambda}\Big)\,.

A simple computation shows that when
\lambda\geq 1
, one has that
\frac{1}{\lambda}\leq 2\log\Big(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big)\leq 2\log\Big(1+\frac{A}{\lambda}\Big)
, the latter following since
A\geq 1
, by assumption. Therefore,
by (
A.13
) and (
A.14
) we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
|y|\leq\sqrt{A}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\lambda+|y|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}+\int_{|y|\leq\sqrt{A}}\,\frac{1}{\lambda+|y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq(2+\pi)\log\Big(1+\frac{A}{\lambda}\Big)\,,

which implies (
A.10
) with
c=2+\pi
.

Let us now prove (
A.11
) and (
A.12
).
First, we prove (
A.11
).
We have

(A.15)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A}\int_{1}^{A}\big(\log\big(\tfrac{A}{x}\big)\big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle y=A\,e^{-u}}}{{=\joinrel=}}\int_{0}^{\log A}e^{-u}\,u^{p}\,\mathrm{d}u\leq\,\Gamma(p+1)\leq p^{p}\,,

since
\Gamma(p+1)\leq p^{p}
for
p\geq 1
. After raising both sides of (
A.15
) to the
\frac{1}{p}
we get (
A.11
).

To prove (
A.12
) we first note that

(A.16)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
|y|\leq 2\sqrt{A}\end{subarray}}\Big(\log\Big(1+\frac{A}{1+|y|^{2}}\Big)\Big)^{p}\leq(\log(1+A))^{p}+\int_{|y|\leq 2\sqrt{A}}\,\Big(\log\Big(1+\frac{A}{1+|y|^{2}}\Big)\Big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\,.

Using polar coordinates in (
A.16
) we compute

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\leq 2\sqrt{A}}\,\Big(\log\Big(1+\frac{A}{1+|y|^{2}}\Big)\Big)^{p}\,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}y=2\pi\int_{0}^{2\sqrt{A}}\,\varrho\Big(\log\Big(1+\frac{A}{1+\varrho^{2}}\Big)\Big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\varrho\,

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u=1+\varrho^{2}}}{{=\joinrel=}}\,\pi\int_{1}^{1+4A}\Big(\log\Big(1+\frac{A}{u}\Big)\Big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}u\,.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\pi\int_{1}^{1+4A}\Big(\log\Big(1+\frac{A}{u}\Big)\Big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}u\leq\pi\int_{1}^{1+4A}\Big(\log\Big(\frac{1+5A}{u}\Big)\Big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}u\leq\,\pi\int_{1}^{1+5A}\Big(\log\Big(\frac{1+5A}{u}\Big)\Big)^{p}\mathrm{d}u\,.

Note that by (
A.11
), we further have that

\displaystyle\pi\int_{1}^{1+5A}\Big(\log\Big(\frac{1+5A}{u}\Big)\Big)^{p}\mathrm{d}u\leq(1+5A)\,\pi\,p^{p}\,\leq 6\,A\,\pi\,p^{p}\,,

since
A\geq 1
. Combining this inequality with (
A.16
) we get that

(A.17)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
|y|\leq 2\sqrt{A}\end{subarray}}\Big(\log\Big(1+\frac{A}{1+|y|^{2}}\Big)\Big)^{p}\leq\log(1+A)^{p}+6\,A\,\pi\,p^{p}\,.

We are going to prove that for all
A\geq 1
,

\displaystyle(\log(1+A))^{p}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}-1}\,A\,p^{p}\,,

thus deducing inequality (
A.12
), via (
A.17
), with
c=\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}-1}+6\pi
. To this end, consider
k_{p}(x):=\frac{(\log(1+x))^{p}}{x}
for
x\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
. We have that

\displaystyle k^{\prime}_{p}(x):=\frac{(\log(1+x))^{p-1}}{x}\Big(\frac{p}{1+x}-\frac{\log(1+x)}{x}\Big)\,,

therefore,
k_{p}
is increasing in
[0,x_{p}]
and decreasing in
[x_{p},\infty)
, where
x_{p}\geq 0
is the solution to the equation
k^{\prime}_{p}(x_{p})=0
, or equivalently

(A.18)

\displaystyle p=\frac{(1+x_{p})\,\log(1+x_{p})}{x_{p}}\,.

By working with
g(x):=\frac{(1+x)\log(1+x)}{x}
, one can see that equation (
A.18
) has a unique solution
x_{p}\geq 0
for every
p\geq 1
, since
g^{\prime}(x)>0
for all
x>0
,
\lim_{x\downarrow 0}g(x)=1
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}g(x)=\infty
.
We distinguish two cases:

Suppose first that
x_{p}\geq 1
.
Then

(A.19)

\displaystyle\log(1+x_{p})\leq p\leq 2\,\log(1+x_{p})\,,

by (
A.18
) and since
x_{p}\geq 1
. Therefore, in this case, for all
x\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{(\log(1+x))^{p}}{x}=k_{p}(x)\leq k_{p}(x_{p})=\frac{(\log(1+x_{p}))^{p}}{x_{p}}\leq\frac{p^{p}}{x_{p}}\leq\frac{p^{p}}{e^{\frac{p}{2}}-1}\,,

where the last two inequalities follow by the first and second inequality in (
A.19
), respectively. Since,
p\geq 1
we have that
e^{\frac{p}{2}}-1\geq\sqrt{e}-1
, thus we conclude that in the case where
x_{p}\geq 1
we have for all
x\geq 1
,

(A.20)

\displaystyle k_{p}(x)=\frac{(\log(1+x))^{p}}{x}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}-1}\,p^{p}\,.

Moving to the second case, i.e.
0\leq x_{p}<1
, we have that since
k_{p}
is decreasing in
[1,\infty)\subset[x_{p},\infty)
, we have that for all
x\geq 1
,

(A.21)

\displaystyle k_{p}(x)=\frac{(\log(1+x))^{p}}{x}\leq k_{p}(1)=(\log(2))^{p}<1\,,

since
p\geq 1
and
\log 2<1
. Therefore, by (
A.20
), (
A.21
) and since
p\geq 1
, for all
x\geq 1
and
p\geq 1
we have that

(A.22)

\displaystyle k_{p}(x)\leq\max\Big\{1,\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}-1}\Big\}\,p^{p}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}-1}\,p^{p}\,.

Recalling that
k_{p}(x)=\frac{(\log(1+x))^{p}}{x}
and applying (
A.22
) to (
A.17
) for
x=A\geq 1
we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
|y|\leq 2\sqrt{A}\end{subarray}}\Big(\log\Big(1+\frac{A}{1+|y|^{2}}\Big)\Big)^{p}\leq(\log(1+A))^{p}+6\,A\,\pi\,p^{p}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}-1}\,A\,p^{p}+6\,A\,\pi\,p^{p}=c\,A\,p^{p}\,,

with
c=\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}-1}+6\pi
, thus concluding the proof of (
A.12
).
∎

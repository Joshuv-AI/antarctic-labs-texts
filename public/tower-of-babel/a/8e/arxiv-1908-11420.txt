Exponentiating virtual imaginary contributions in a parton shower Preprint: DESY 19-143
Zoltán Nagy, Davison E. Soper

Abstract
The operator in a parton shower algorithm that represents the imaginary part of virtual Feynman graphs has a non-trivial color structure and is large because it is proportional to a factor of 4 ​ π 4\pi . In order to improve the treatment of color in a parton shower, it may help to exponentiate this phase operator. We show that it is possible to do so by exponentiating matrices that are no larger than 14 × 14 14\times 14 . Using the example of the probability to have a gap in the rapidity interval between two high transverse momentum jets, we test this exponentiation algorithm by comparing to the result of treating the phase operator perturbatively. We find that the exponentiation works, but that the net effect of the exponentiated phase operator is quite small for this problem, so that one can as well use the perturbative approach.

II
Exponentiating
\mathcal{V}_{i\pi}

In order to examine whether it is practically possible to include
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
along with
\mathcal{V}^{\textsc{lc}\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{+}$}}
in the no-splitting operator, as in Eq. (
11

\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
. The structure of
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
(assuming massless partons) is given in Eq. (10.14) of Ref.

\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}(t)=-4{\mathrm{i}}\pi\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}}{2\pi}\big([(T_{\mathrm{a}}\cdot T_{\mathrm{b}})\otimes 1]-[1\otimes(T_{\mathrm{a}}\cdot T_{\mathrm{b}})]\big)\;.

(14)

Here
T_{\mathrm{a}}
represents the insertion of a color matrix

on incoming parton line “a”,
T_{\mathrm{b}}
represents the insertion of a color matrix
T^{c}
on incoming parton line “b”, and the dot in
(T_{\mathrm{a}}\cdot T_{\mathrm{b}})
represents a summation over the octet color index
c

[(T_{\mathrm{a}}\cdot T_{\mathrm{b}})\otimes 1]
the color matrices act on the ket state, while in
[1\otimes(T_{\mathrm{a}}\cdot T_{\mathrm{b}})]

\big({1}\big|\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}(t)=0

14

At first sight, the goal of exponentiating
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}

14

n

\begin{split}\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{e}}(t_{2},t_{1})&\big|{\{p,f,c^{\prime},c\}_{m}}\big)\\

(15)

\begin{split}\sum_{\{\hat{c}\}_{m}}N(t_{2},t_{1},\{p,f\}_{m},&\{\hat{c}\}_{m},\{c\}_{m})\big|{\{\hat{c}\}_{m}}\big\rangle\\

\begin{split}\omega_{2}(t_{2},{}&t_{1},\{p,f\}_{m})\\

&\qquad\times\big[a(\tau_{1},\{p,f\}_{m}),\,a(\tau_{2},\{p,f\}_{m})\big]\;.\end{split}

(36)

The higher order contributions can be obtained by recursion.

The commutator in the second order term simplifies when we use the decomposition (

a

\displaystyle\big[{}

\displaystyle a(\tau_{1},\{p,f\}_{m}),\,a(\tau_{2},\{p,f\}_{m})\big]

(37)

\displaystyle=-4{\mathrm{i}}\pi\big[a^{\textsc{lc}\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{+}$}}_{\mathrm{soft}}(\tau_{1},\{p,f\}_{m})-a^{\textsc{lc}\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{+}$}}_{\mathrm{soft}}(\tau_{2},\{p,f\}_{m}),T_{\mathrm{a}}\cdot T_{\mathrm{b}}\big].

III
Assembling the shower

It is now clear what to do to complete the shower evolution equations. Starting with states
\big|{G(1)}\big\rangle=\big|{\{c\}_{m}}\big\rangle
and
\big\langle{G^{\prime}(1)}\big|=\big\langle{\{c^{\prime}\}_{m}}\big|
, we define color states
\big|{G(n)}\big\rangle
and
\big\langle{G^{\prime}(n)}\big|
. This gives us an operator
n(t_{2},t_{1},\{p,f\}_{m})
defined as a matrix that mixes the states
\big|{G(n)}\big\rangle
and an operator
n^{\dagger}(t_{2},t_{1},\{p,f\}_{m})
that mixes the states
\big\langle{G^{\prime}(n)}\big|
according to Eqs. (
18
), (
19
), and (
33
). Then the combined no-splitting operator
\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{e}}(t_{2},t_{1})
is defined by the direct product of these matrices, according to Eqs. (
15
), (
16
), and (
17
).

Now, in a complete algorithm, one would calculate
\mathcal{X}^{\textsc{e}}(t_{2},t_{2})
as a power series in
\Delta\mathcal{V}_{\textrm{Re}}(\tau)
by using Eq. (
12
). Then one would produce the complete shower using Eq. (
13
). One would keep any number of splittings generated by
\mathcal{H}^{\textsc{lc}\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{+}$}}(\tau)
and
\Delta\mathcal{H}(\tau)
. This would generate contributions proportional to powers of
\Delta\mathcal{H}(\tau)
and
\Delta\mathcal{V}_{\textrm{Re}}(\tau)
. One would retain contributions proportional to
[\Delta\mathcal{H}]^{A}[\Delta\mathcal{V}_{\textrm{Re}}]^{B}
with
A+B\leq N_{\textrm{Re}}
for a chosen value of
N_{\textrm{Re}}
.

For this paper, we have not implemented the complete algorithm. Rather, we have set
N_{\textrm{Re}}=0
. This allows us to test whether the exponentiation of the
{\mathrm{i}}\pi
contributions as outlined above is computationally practical. It also allows us to test whether exponentiation of the
{\mathrm{i}}\pi
contributions makes a substantial numerical difference compared to simply keeping just a small number of factors of
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
. For this purpose, we will use the gap fraction, for which we have found previously that the inclusion of color beyond the LC+ approximation can be numerically important
NSGapColor
.

In this section, we explore the exponentiation of

1/N_{\mathrm{c}}^{4}\approx 0.01

V
Conclusions

The accuracy of leading order parton shower programs could be enhanced by improving the treatment of QCD color
PlatzerSjodahl
;
Seymour2018
;
PlatzerSjodahlThoren
;
Isaacson:2018zdi
;
Forshaw:2019ver
. We have worked to make our program,
Deductor
, more accurate with respect to color
NScolor
;
NSColorEffects
;
NSColorTheory
;
NSGapColor
. In the organization of
Deductor
, there are three operators that have a color structure that cannot be incorporated into the LC+ approximation that
Deductor
uses:
\Delta\mathcal{H}
,
\Delta\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Re}}
, and
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
.

We found in Ref.
NSColorTheory
that it is possible to include the operators
\Delta\mathcal{H}
,
\Delta\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Re}}
, and
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
perturbatively. That is, one can work with the result expanded in powers of these operators and retain the contributions up to some number of powers. There are practical limitations on the number of powers that one can retain, but two powers turns out to be quite practical.

The operator
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
, which corresponds to the imaginary part of one-loop virtual graphs, is of special interest because of the role that it plays in analytical summation of large logarithms and because it contains a factor of
4\pi
, which makes it a numerically large operator. One wonders if one could include
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
to all orders. That is, one wonders if one could use
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
as part of the exponentiated Sudakov factor that occurs between real emissions.

In this paper, we have found that one can include
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
in the Sudakov exponent and we show how to do that. One has to calculate numerically the exponential of matrices, but the dimensionality of the matrices is at most
14\times 14
.

In principle, one could include
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
in the Sudakov exponent while including powers of
\Delta\mathcal{H}
and
\Delta\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Re}}
perturbatively. This would, however, require substantial effort to realize in computer code, so we have not attempted this further step.

We have tested this summation numerically, using the rapidity gap survival probability, which we have found is generally sensitive to color
NSGapColor
. For the numerical test, we compare the results from the perturbative approach to the result with
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
included in the Sudakov exponent. The result of the two calculations agree that the effect of
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
is small, near the limit of what we can measure numerically.

We have also examined the effect of
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
, both in exponentiated form and perturbatively, for a wide range of the variables
\bar{p}_{\mathrm{T}}
and
y_{12}
that appear in the rapidity gap problem. We find that the effect of
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
is always quite small.

It is perhaps not a surprise that the net
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
contribution is quite small. We note that if we integrate Eq. (
14
) for
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
over a range of scales from
p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{cut}}=20\ \mathrm{GeV}
to
\bar{p}_{\mathrm{T}}=300\ \mathrm{GeV}
and leave out the color factors, we have a net phase
\phi\sim 2\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}\log((300/20)^{2})
. With
\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}\sim 0.1
, this is
\phi\sim 1
. Thus
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
is not a small operator in the context of the rapidity gap problem. However the contributions from
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
have opposite signs when acting on ket color states compared to the sign when acting on bra color states. We note that for real numbers
a
and
\phi
,
\exp(a+{\mathrm{i}}\phi)\exp(a-{\mathrm{i}}\phi)
is just
\exp(2a)
, with no contribution from the phase
\phi
. Thus the effect of
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
has to arise because the relevant color matrices don’t commute. In our numerical tests of the perturbative approach to color in Ref.
NSColorTheory
, we found that the effects of
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
separately on the bra and ket states were of order 1, but that these effects cancelled, as they should, when we examined the unitarity property of shower evolution. Only when we have a color sensitive observable can the contributions fail to cancel. Apparently, the gap fraction is not sufficiently color sensitive to prevent a high degree of cancellation.

Because the effect of
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
is small for the gap fraction observable, we believe that it suffices to examine observables that might be sensitive to color by treating
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
perturbatively, along with
\Delta\mathcal{H}
and
\Delta\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{Re}}
. If one finds an observable in which
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
has a numerically substantial effect, then we could consider exponentiating this effect in
Deductor
. Of course, other workers may want to use the method of this paper to include
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}
as part of the Sudakov exponent in other parton shower programs.

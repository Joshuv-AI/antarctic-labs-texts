A model consistent with LQCD data on ρ \rho -meson screening mass
Masahiro Ishii, Akihisa Miyahara, Hiroaki Kouno, Masanobu Yahiro

Abstract
Recently, state-of-art LQCD calculations were done for π \pi -meson and ρ \rho -meson screening mass, M π scr ​ ( T ) {M_{\pi}^{\rm scr}}(T) and M ρ scr ​ ( T ) {M_{\rho}^{\rm scr}}(T) . We consider the two-flavor system, and focus on temperature dependence T T of M π scr ​ ( T ) {M_{\pi}^{\rm scr}}(T) and M ρ scr ​ ( T ) {M_{\rho}^{\rm scr}}(T) . Our aim is to construct a model consistent with LQCD data on M ρ scr ​ ( T ) {M_{\rho}^{\rm scr}}(T) and M π scr ​ ( T ) {M_{\pi}^{\rm scr}}(T) .

Meson masses can be classified into “meson pole mass” and

In LQCD simulations at finite

meson pole (screening) masses are
calculated from the exponential decay of temporal (spatial)
mesonic correlation functions.
LQCD simulations are more difficult for pole masses

in the spatial direction. This situation becomes more serious

For this reason, meson screening Masses have been calculated

Effective models are an approach complementary to

II
MG-PNJL model

In order to construct the MG-PNJL model, we consider the two-flavor case,
since we focus on
\pi
,
\rho
mesons in the case of finite
T
.

We start with the PNJL Lagrangian density with
T
-dependent scalar four-quark coupling
G_{\rm S}(T)

and a constant vector coupling
G_{\rm V}(0)
: Namely,

\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bar{\psi}}(i\gamma_{\mu}D^{\mu}-m_{0})\psi+G_{\rm S}(T)[({\bar{\psi}}\psi)^{2}+({\bar{\psi}}i\gamma_{5}\bm{\tau}\psi)^{2}]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle G_{\rm V}(0)\left[\left(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\bm{\tau}\psi\right)^{2}+\left(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\bm{\tau}\psi\right)^{2}\right]-{\cal U}(\Phi,{\bar{\Phi}},T),

where
\psi
is the quark field with the current quark mass
m_{0}
,

{\vec{\tau}}
stands for the isospin matrix. The covariant derivative
D^{\nu}
is approximated into

\partial^{\nu}+\delta^{\nu}_{0}A^{0}_{a}{\lambda_{a}/2}
, where the time component

A_{a}^{0}
of the gauge field is treated
as a homogeneous and static background field governed by the Polyakov-loop potential
\mathcal{U}
.
For
T
dependence of
G_{\rm S}(T)
, we assume

\displaystyle G_{\rm S}(T)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}G_{\rm S}(0)&(T<T_{\rm S})\\
G_{\rm S}(0)e^{-(T-T_{\rm S})^{2}/b_{\rm S}^{2}}&(T\geq T_{\rm S})\\
\end{array}\right..

As a Polyakov-loop potential
\mathcal{U}
,
we consider two-types of
\mathcal{U}
.
One is the logarithm-type potential of Ref.
Rossner et al. 2007
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{U}_{\rm log}(\Phi,\bar{\Phi},T)}{T^{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{a(T)}{2}\Phi\bar{\Phi}+b(T){\rm ln}\left[1-6\Phi\bar{\Phi}\right.

(5)

\displaystyle\left.+4\left(\Phi^{3}+\bar{\Phi}^{3}\right)-3(\Phi\bar{\Phi})^{2}\right]

with

a(T)=a_{0}+a_{1}\left(\frac{T_{0}}{T}\right)+a_{2}\left(\frac{T_{0}}{T}\right)^{2},~~b(T)=b_{3}\left(\frac{T_{0}}{T}\right)^{3}

(6)

and another is the polynomial-type potential of Ref.
Haas:2013qwp
:

\frac{\mathcal{U}_{\rm poly}(\Phi,\bar{\Phi},T)}{T^{4}}=-\frac{b_{2}(T)}{2}\Phi\bar{\Phi}-\frac{b_{3}}{6}(\Phi^{3}+\bar{\Phi}^{3})+\frac{b_{4}}{4}(\Phi\bar{\Phi})^{2}

(7)

with

b_{2}(T)=c_{0}+c_{1}\left(\frac{T_{0}}{T}\right)+c_{2}\left(\frac{T_{0}}{T}\right)^{2}+c_{2}\left(\frac{T_{0}}{T}\right)^{3}

(8)

The parameters for each potential have been determined so as to reproduce
thermodynamic quantities calculated with LQCD simulation in pure Yang–Mills theory.
Their resultant values are summarized in Table
1
.
These potentials have one dimensionful parameter
T_{0}
. The value is
T_{0}=270
MeV in pure Yang–Mills theory.
Once one considers quark degree of freedom,
the parameter
T_{0}
should be shifted to a lower value in association with change of typical energy scale
through the QCD running coupling
g
.
Hence we treat
T_{0}
as an adjustable parameter and determine
to be consistent with full QCD data on the chiral temperature

T_{\rm c}\simeq 173
MeV with 10% error
Karsch, Leermann and Peikert 2002
.
The parameter thus obtained is
T_{0}=215
MeV for each
\mathcal{U}
.

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle={1\over{3}}{\rm tr}_{\rm c}(L),~~~~~{\bar{\Phi}}={1\over{3}}{\rm tr}_{\rm c}({L^{*}})

Making the mean field approximation (MFA), one can get

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm MFA}={\bar{\psi}}S^{-1}\psi-U_{\rm M}(\sigma)-{\cal U}(\Phi,{\bar{\Phi}},T)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\rm F}

\displaystyle=-2N_{\rm c}N_{\rm f}\int\frac{d^{3}{\bm{p}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Bigl[E_{\bm{p}}

for

,

II.1
\chi
-PV regularization

In Eq. (
15
), the momentum
\bm{p}
integral has ultraviolet divergence and it must be regularized.
As an usual regularization scheme, three-dimensional momentum cutoff has been commonly used so far,
but the regularization explicitly breaks translational invariance
that is essential for deriving the screening mass
Ishii 2013
.
Moreover, translational invariance is also necessary to maintain Ward-Takahashi identities for
SU(2)_{\rm V}
and
SU(2)_{\rm A}
currents that realize transverse and longitudinal properties of
\rho
and
A_{1}
mesons.
Hence we take a chiral version
Ishii 2013
of Pauli-Villars (PV) regularization
Pauli and Villars 1980
;
Florkowski 1997
in the present work.
We refer to it as
\chi
-PV regularization.
The original PV regularization cannot maintain chiral symmetry due to heavy masses of auxiliary particles,
but
\chi
-PV regularization of Ref.
Ishii 2013
manifestly preserves chiral symmetry and correctly reproduces low-energy relations of chiral dynamics, such as the Gell-Mann–Oakes–Renner relation, partial conserved axial-vector current relation and so on.

In
\chi
-PV regularization, the integral
\Omega_{\rm F}(M)
is simply regularized as

\Omega_{\rm F}(M)\to\Omega^{\rm reg}_{\rm F}(M)=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}C_{\alpha}\Omega_{\rm F}(M_{\alpha})

(16)

with
M_{0}=M
and the
M_{\alpha}~(\alpha\geq 1)
are masses of auxiliary
particles. The
M_{\alpha}
and the
C_{\alpha}

should satisfy the condition

\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}C_{\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}C_{\alpha}M_{\alpha}^{2}=0

to remove the logarithmic, quadratic and quartic divergence.
We then assume
(C_{0},C_{1},C_{2})=(1,-2,1)
and
(M_{1}^{2},M_{2}^{2})=(M^{2}+\Lambda^{2},M^{2}+2\Lambda^{2})
.
The dimensionful parameter
\Lambda
should be kept to finite
even after the subtraction (
16
),
since the present model is non-renormalizable.

Now we consider the Polynomial-type

\mathcal{M}_{\rm thermal}=\pi T+g_{\rm E}^{2}\frac{C_{\rm F}}{8\pi}+\mathcal{O}(g^{2}_{\rm E}/T)

(17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{j,n=0,\alpha}=\sqrt{M_{\alpha}^{2}+(\pi T+T\phi_{j})^{2}}\to\mathcal{M}_{\rm thermal},~~~

(18)

(19)

in

Appendix A
Meson screening mass

We derive the equation for
\pi
-,
\rho
- and
A_{1}
-meson screening masses by using

The mesonic correlation function corresponding to
\pi,\rho,A_{1}
meson is

J_{\xi}^{a}(x)=\bar{\psi}(x)\Gamma_{\xi}\tau^{a}\psi(x),\\

with matrices

\pi:~\Gamma_{\rm P}=i\gamma_{5},~~\rho:~\Gamma_{\rm V}=\gamma^{\mu},~~A_{1}:~\Gamma_{\rm A}=\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}.

(20)

The mesonic correlation function is defined by

\displaystyle\eta_{\xi\xi^{\prime}}^{ab}(x)\equiv\langle 0|{\rm T}\left(J_{\xi}^{a}(x)J_{\xi^{\prime}}^{b}(0)\right)|0\rangle

(21)

and their Fourier transformed functions
\chi_{\xi\xi^{\prime}}^{ab}
is then obtained by

\displaystyle\chi_{\xi\xi^{\prime}}^{ab}(q^{2})=i\int d^{4}xe^{iq\cdot x}\langle 0|{\rm T}\left(J_{\xi}^{a}(x)J_{\xi^{\prime}}^{b}(0)\right)|0\rangle.

(22)

where the indices
\xi,\xi^{\prime}
means the
\pi,\rho,A_{1}
channels as shown in (
20
) and T stands for the time ordered product.
In the isospin symmetric case , mesonic correlation
\chi_{\xi\xi^{\prime}}^{ab}

is simplified with
\delta^{ab}\chi_{\xi\xi^{\prime}}
; therefore, we abbreviate the indices
a,b
unless otherwise stated.

The ring approximation in
\chi_{\xi\xi^{\prime}}
leads to the Schwinger-Dyson equation

\displaystyle\chi_{\xi\xi^{\prime}}=\Pi_{\xi\xi^{\prime}}+\sum_{\xi^{\prime\prime}\xi^{\prime\prime\prime}}\Pi_{\xi\xi^{\prime\prime}}2G_{\xi^{\prime\prime}\xi^{\prime\prime\prime}}\chi_{\xi^{\prime\prime\prime}\xi^{\prime}},

(23)

where the one-loop polarization function

\Pi_{\xi\xi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\Pi_{\xi\xi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-2i\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}{\rm tr}_{\rm c,d}\left[\Gamma_{\xi}iS(p^{\prime}+q)\Gamma_{\xi^{\prime}}iS(p^{\prime})\right]

with
p^{\prime}=(p_{0}+iA_{4},{\bm{p}})
and trace
{\rm tr}_{\rm c,d}
of color and Dirac spaces.
We first consider the zero temperature case for simplicity.
In this case, the polarization functions are summarized by

\displaystyle\mathit{\Pi}_{\rm PP}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2i\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}{\rm tr}_{\rm c,d}\left[i\gamma_{5}iS(p^{\prime}+q)i\gamma_{5}iS(p^{\prime})\right]

(25)

Appendix B
\rho
and
A_{1}
meson screening masses at finite
T

In this section, we only consider the
\xi={\rm V,\rm A}

channels. At zero temperature, the polarization functions

\mathit{\Pi}_{\xi\xi}^{\mu\nu}
and mesonic correlation

\chi_{\xi\xi}^{\mu\nu}
are decomposed into 4-dimensionally transverse and longitudinal modes:

\displaystyle\mathit{\Pi}_{\xi\xi}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathit{\Pi}_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm T,4d})}T^{\mu\nu}+\mathit{\Pi}_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm L,4d})}L^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\chi_{\xi\xi}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm T,4d})}T^{\mu\nu}+\chi_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm L,4d})}L^{\mu\nu}

(40)

The projection tensors
T^{\mu\nu}
and
L^{\mu\nu}
are
defined by

T^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{q^{\mu}q^{\nu}}{q^{2}},~~L^{\mu\nu}=\frac{q^{\mu}q^{\nu}}{q^{2}}.

(41)

It is noted that the longitudinal element
\Pi_{\rm VV}^{({\rm L,4d})}

must vanish due to isospin symmetry
since the isovector current should be conserved (
\partial_{\mu}J_{\rm V}^{\mu a}(x)=0
)
and the corresponding Ward-Takahashi identity indicates
q_{\mu}\Pi_{\rm VV}^{\mu\nu}=0
and
q_{\mu}\chi_{\rm VV}^{\mu\nu}=0
. The vanishment of
\Pi_{\rm VV}^{({\rm L,4d})}
and

\Pi_{\rm VV}^{({\rm L,4d})}
is realized
even at finite
T
because the current conservation law holds for any
T
.
If one chooses three-dimensional momentum-cutoff regularization,
the Ward-Takahashi identity is spoiled and
the above discussion is no longer valid due to the lack of translational invariance.

At finite
T
, the 4-dimensionally transverse mode is decomposed into
3-dimensionally transverse and longitudinal modes in the polarization
function and mesonic correlation:

\displaystyle\mathit{\Pi}_{\xi\xi}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathit{\Pi}_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm T,3d})}P^{\mu\nu}_{\rm T}+\mathit{\Pi}_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm L,3d})}P^{\mu\nu}_{\rm L}+\mathit{\Pi}_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm L,4d})}L^{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle\chi_{\xi\xi}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm T,3d})}P^{\mu\nu}_{\rm T}+\chi_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm L,3d})}P^{\mu\nu}_{\rm L}+\chi_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm L,4d})}L^{\mu\nu}.

(42)

The 3-dimensional projection tensors are defined by

P^{\mu\nu}_{\rm T}=g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{q^{\mu}q^{\nu}}{q^{2}}-\frac{n^{\mu}n^{\nu}}{n^{2}},~~P^{\mu\nu}_{\rm L}=\frac{n^{\mu}n^{\nu}}{n^{2}},

(43)

where the 4-dimensional vector
n^{\mu}
is orthogonal component of heat
bath velocity
u^{\mu}
against the external momentum
q^{\mu}
, i.e.,

n^{\mu}=T^{\mu\nu}u_{\nu}
. When one takes the rest frame of heat bath
(
u^{\mu}=(1,\bm{0})
) and the static limit, the vector
n^{\mu}

turns out to be equal to
u^{\mu}
.
The general discussion of projection tensor is summarized in Appendix.

In the SD equation (
23
), 3-dimensionally transverse and
longitudinal modes has been completely decoupled, and one can
independently solve the equations for each modes. The solution of SD
equation is finally obtained as

\displaystyle\chi_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm T,3d})}=\frac{\Pi_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm T,3d})}}{1-2G_{\rm V}\Pi_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm T,3d})}},~~\chi_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm L,3d})}=\frac{\Pi_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm L,3d})}}{1-2G_{\rm V}\Pi_{\xi\xi}^{({\rm L,3d})}}

for
\xi={\rm A},{\rm V}
.
The polarization functions are simple form

\displaystyle\Pi_{\rm VV}^{({\rm T,3d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-16\bm{q}^{2}I_{3}^{\rm reg},~~\Pi_{\rm AA}^{({\rm T,3d})}=-16\bm{q}^{2}I_{3}^{\rm reg}-16M^{2}I_{2}^{\rm reg},

\displaystyle\Pi_{\rm VV}^{({\rm L,3d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-16I_{4}^{\rm reg},~~\Pi_{\rm AA}^{({\rm L,3d})}=-16I_{4}^{\rm reg}-16M^{2}I_{2}^{\rm reg},

(45)

where the functions
I_{3}^{\rm reg}
and
I_{4}^{\rm reg}
are

\displaystyle I_{3}^{\rm reg}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-T\sum_{j,n,\alpha}\int{d^{3}\bm{p}\over{(2\pi)^{3}}}\int_{0}^{1}dx~C_{\alpha}{x-x^{2}\over{\bm{p}^{2}+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{j,n,\alpha}(\bm{q}^{2})}}

\displaystyle I_{4}^{\rm reg}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{q}^{2}I_{3}^{\rm reg}-T\sum_{j,n,\alpha}\int{d^{3}\bm{p}\over{(2\pi)^{3}}}\int_{0}^{1}dx~C_{\alpha}{\omega_{n}^{{}^{\prime}2}-\bm{p}^{2}/3\over{\bm{p}^{2}+\mathcal{M}^{2}_{j,n,\alpha}(\bm{q}^{2})}}

with
\omega_{n}^{\prime}=\omega_{n}+\phi_{j}
, Feynman parameter
x
and
\mathcal{M}^{2}_{j,n,\alpha}(\bm{q}^{2})=\mathcal{M}^{2}_{j,n,\alpha}+(x-x^{2})\bm{q}^{2}

It is worth noting that the Feynman parameter must

In the present case, the all calculation has been done in the Euclid space

Appendix C
Threshold mass

In PNJL model, mesons can decay into quark-pair.
Such a effect is emerged in the functions
I_{2},I_{3},I_{4}
:

\displaystyle I_{2}^{\rm reg}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{iT}{8\pi\tilde{q}}{\rm Log}{\left(\frac{2\mathcal{M}_{j,n,\alpha}+i\tilde{q}}{2\mathcal{M}_{j,n,\alpha}-i\tilde{q}}\right)}

\displaystyle I_{3}^{\rm reg}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{iT}{64\pi\tilde{q}}\sum_{j,n,\alpha}\left[\left(1-\frac{4\mathcal{M}_{j,n,\alpha}^{2}}{\tilde{q}^{2}}\right){\rm Log}{\left(\frac{2\mathcal{M}_{j,n,\alpha}+i\tilde{q}}{2\mathcal{M}_{j,n,\alpha}-i\tilde{q}}\right)}\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\frac{4i\mathcal{M}_{j,n,\alpha}}{\tilde{q}}\right]

\displaystyle I_{4}^{\rm reg}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i\tilde{q}T}{32\pi}\sum_{j,n,\alpha}\left[\left(1+\frac{4\omega_{n}^{2}}{\tilde{q}^{2}}\right){\rm Log}{\left(\frac{2\mathcal{M}_{j,n,\alpha}+i\tilde{q}}{2\mathcal{M}_{j,n,\alpha}-i\tilde{q}}\right)}\right.

(67)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\frac{4i\mathcal{M}_{j,n,\alpha}}{\tilde{q}}\right],

as logarithmic cuts along the imaginary axis in complex
\tilde{q}
plane,
where logarithmic cuts are starting at
\tilde{q}=2i\mathcal{M}_{j,n,\alpha}

and lowest branch point is given by
\tilde{q}=i\mathcal{M}_{\rm lowest}

with

\mathcal{M}_{\rm lowest}=2\mathcal{M}_{j=1,n=0,\alpha=0}=2\mathcal{M}_{j=2,n=-1,\alpha=0}.

(68)

When
\tilde{q}\geq i\mathcal{M}_{\rm lowest}
, meson decays into quark-pair.
The
\mathcal{M}_{\rm lowest}
is thus ”threshold mass”.

PNJL model describes statistical confinement by means of Polyakov loop,
but has no information about the confinement force between quarks
because the gauge fields are treated as background field
and their nonlocal correlations are ignored.
Accordingly, PNJL model may be less predictive to meson screening mass above the threshold mass.
Hence we assume that our model results are reliable only when the following relation is satisfied:

M_{\xi}^{\rm scr}<\mathcal{M}_{\rm lowest}

(69)

for
\xi=\pi,\rho,A_{1}
mesons.

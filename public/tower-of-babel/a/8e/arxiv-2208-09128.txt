Totally Nonnegative Tropical Flags and the Totally Nonnegative Flag Dressian
Jonathan Boretsky

Abstract
We study the totally nonnegative part of the complete flag variety and of its tropicalization. We show that Lusztig’s notion of nonnegative complete flag variety coincides with the flags in the complete flag variety which have nonnegative Plücker coordinates. This mirrors the characterization of the totally nonnegative Grassmannian as those points in the Grassmannian whose Plücker coordinates are all nonnegative. We then study the tropical complete flag variety and complete flag Dressian, which are two tropical versions of the complete flag variety, capturing realizable and abstract flags of tropical linear spaces, respectively. In general, the complete flag Dressian properly contains the tropical complete flag variety. However, we show that the totally nonnegative parts of these spaces coincide.

Tropical geometry is the geometry of the tropical semiring

where multiplication is replaced by addition, and addition is replaced by minimization. Thus, roughly, if we tropicalize a polynomial, we get a minimization over a collection of sums of variables. We say a point is a solution of a tropical polynomial if that minimum is achieved at least twice. In this setting, varieties become polyhedral cell complexes, making them amenable to combinatorial study. For precise definitions, see

For

We generalize this fact to the setting of the complete flag variety:

The totally nonnegative tropical complete flag variety,
\textnormal{TrFl}_{n}^{\geq{0}}

The complete flag Dressian is the set of points satisfying the tropicalizations of a particular set of generators for the incidence Plücker ideal, called the incidence Plücker relations, which generalize the Plücker relations. 2.1
The Totally nonnegative Complete Flag Variety

We begin by defining our primarily focus on in this paper, the complete flag variety.

Definition 2.1
.

The
complete flag variety

\textnormal{Fl}_{n}
is the collection of all
complete flags
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which are collections
(V_{i})_{i=0}^{n}
of linear subspaces satisfying
\{0\}=V_{0}\subsetneq V_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq V_{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

\textnormal{Fl}_{n}
is a multi-projective variety. We can represent a flag
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
by a full rank
n
by
n
matrix
M
such that
V_{i}
equals the span of the topmost
i
rows of
M
. Let
\textnormal{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
be the group of invertible
n
by
n
real matrices and
\textnormal{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
be the
special linear group
of real matrices with determinant
1
. Let
B_{-}
be the
Borel subgroup
of
\textnormal{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
consisting of lower triangular matrices. One can check that two matrices
M
and
M^{\prime}
represent the same flag if and only if they are related by left multiplication by some
B\in B_{-}
. Thus, we can think of the complete flag variety as
\textnormal{Fl}_{n}=\{B_{-}u|u\in SL(n,\mathbb{R})\}
, where a flag in
\textnormal{Fl}_{n}
represented by a matrix
u
is identified with the set
B_{-}u
.

For
I\subset[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
M
an
n
by
n
matrix, the
Plücker coordinate
(or, alternatively,
flag minor
)
P_{I}(M)
is the determinant of the submatrix of
M
in rows
\{1,2,\ldots,|I|\}
and columns
I
. To any flag
F
, associate the collection of Plücker coordinates
(P_{I}(F))_{I\subset[n]}
, defined to be the Plücker coordinates of any matrix representative of that flag. By
[
24
, Proposition 14.2]
, this is an embedding of
\textnormal{Fl}_{n}
in
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{{\binom{n}{1}}-1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{n-1}-1}
. The Plücker coordinates of flags in
\textnormal{Fl}_{n}
are cut out by multi-homogeneous polynomials, as shown in the following statements. Note that we will often use shorthand notation such as
(S\setminus ab)\cup cd
in place of
\left(S\setminus\{a,b\}\right)\cup\{c,d\}
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
10
]
)
.

Consider
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{1}-1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{n-1}-1}
, with coordinates indexed by proper subsets of
[n]
. For
1\leq r\leq s\leq n
, the
incidence Plücker relations
for indices of size
r
and
s

\mathfrak{P}_{r,s;n}=\left\{\sum_{j\in J\setminus I}sign(j,I,J)P_{I\cup j}P_{J\setminus j}\left|I\in{\binom{[n]}{r-1}},\>J\in{\binom{[n]}{s+1}}\right.\right\},

(1)

where
sign(j,I,J)=(-1)^{|\{k\in J|k<j\}|+|\{i\in I|j<i\}|}
.

The full set of incidence Plücker relations is
\mathfrak{P}_{IP;n}=\bigcup_{1\leq r\leq s\leq n}\mathfrak{P}_{r,s;n}
. The ideal generated by
\mathfrak{P}_{IP;n}
, denoted
I_{{IP;n}}
, is called the
incidence Plücker ideal
.

Remark:
Note that the above definition allows for the option of
r=s
. The incidence Plücker relations for which

are called the (Grassmann)
Plücker relations
. When we want to emphasize that we are interested in the incidence Plücker relations for which
r\neq s
, we will call them
incidence relations
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
10
, Section 9, Proposition 1]
and discussion following its proof)
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{1}-1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{n-1}-1}
. Then
P=P(F)
for some

if and only if

satisfies the incidence Plücker relations

.

In particular, this means the incidence Plücker relations are precisely the relations between the topmost minors of a generic full rank matrix.

Definition 2.4
.

A real matrix is
totally positive
if all of its minors are positive. We denote the set of totally positive
n
by
n
matrices by
\textnormal{GL}_{n}^{>0}
.

Lusztig
[
21
]
introduced the notion of non-negativity for flag varieties as the closure of the set of flags with a totally positive representative:

Definition 2.5
.

The (Lusztig) positive part of the flag variety, denoted
\textnormal{Fl}_{n}^{>0}
, is the subset of
\textnormal{Fl}_{n}
with a representative in
\textnormal{GL}_{n}^{>0}

3

2.2
Flag Positroids

Realizable matroids and positroids relate closely to the Grassmannian and totally nonnegative Grassmannian, respectively. We now introduce flag matroids and two notions of flag positroids, which have analogous relationships to the flag variety and to the totally nonnegative flag variety, respectively. We will show that these two notions of flag positroid coincide in section

. We assume basic familiarity with the definitions of matroids and positroids and direct the reader to
[

Definition 2.6

(
[

.

A (complete)

\bm{\mathcal{M}}=(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{n})
on the ground set

equal to

•

each basis of
\mathcal{M}_{j}
is contained in some basis of
\mathcal{M}_{k}
.

•

each basis of
\mathcal{M}_{k}
contains some basis of
\mathcal{M}_{j}

The matroids

are called the

As with many matroid theoretic concepts, there are numerous cryptomorphic descriptions of flag matroids, some of which can be found in
[

Definition 2.7
.

A flag matroid on

if its bases are the non-zero Plücker coordinates of

for some
F\in\textnormal{Fl}_{n}
. Similarly, a

[n]
is a flag matroid whose bases are the non-zero Plücker coordinates of a flag

.

Definition 2.8
.

A
synthetic flag positroid
on
[n]
is a flag matroid on
[n]
whose bases are the non-zero Plücker coordinates of a flag
F\in\textnormal{Fl}_{n}
such that
P_{I}(F)\geq 0
for all
I\subseteq[n]
.

Remark:
The above definition is slightly imprecise. Since the Plücker coordinates are multi-projective, we should really insist that for each
k\in[n]
,
P_{I}
has a fixed sign for all
I
with
|I|=k
. However, throughout this paper, we will use the projective degree of freedom to fix
P_{[k]}=1
and then we will indeed want all Plücker coordinates to be nonnegative.

The totally nonnegative part of the Grassmannian coincides with the subset of the Grassmannian with all nonnegative Plücker coordinates. Thus, these are both reasonable generalizations of the notion of a positroid. We will show in
Lemma

5.22
that these two notions of flag positroid coincide. Note that both of the above definitions define stronger conditions than just being a flag of matroids in which each constituent matroid is a positroid. For instance, the definition of a synthetic flag positroid requires each of the constituent positroids to be simultaneously realizable, with nonnegative Plücker coordinates, by a single matrix. By contrast, there exist flags of positroids which are not realizable in that way.

Example 2.9
.

Consider the flag matroid
\bm{\mathcal{M}}=(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2},\mathcal{M}_{3})
on the ground set
[3]
with bases
\{1,3\},

\{12,13,23\}
and
\{123\}
, respectively. Each of these is clearly a positroid. Any three by three matrix with nonnegative Plücker coordinates which represents
\bm{\mathcal{M}}
must have the first row

\begin{pmatrix}a&0&b\end{pmatrix}

(2)

with
P_{1}=a
and
P_{3}=b
positive.

We can thus write the full matrix as

\begin{pmatrix}a&0&b\\
*&c&*\\
*&*&*\end{pmatrix},

(3)

where the
*
entries are arbitrary and not of interest to us. Note that there is no value of
c
which allows both
P_{12}>0
and
P_{23}>0
. Thus,
\bm{\mathcal{M}}
is a flag of positroids which cannot be realized by a single matrix with all nonnegative Plücker coordinates. As a result,
\bm{\mathcal{M}}
is not a synthetic flag positroid.

The Complete Flag Dressian and the Tropical Complete Flag Variety

We now introduce some notation and terminology needed for discussing

Let
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\bm{x}^{\bm{b}}=x_{1}^{{b}_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{{b}_{n}}

Definition 2.10
.

Let

\textnormal{Trop}\hskip 1.99168pt(p)=\min_{i}\left\{\bm{x}\cdot\bm{b_{i}}\right\}=\min_{i}\left\{x_{1}(b_{i})_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}(b_{i})_{n}\right\},

(4)

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}\cup\infty
. For a polynomial
p=\sum_{i}a_{i}\bm{x}^{\bm{b}_{i}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we say that a point

is a

\min_{i}\left\{\bm{x}\cdot\bm{b_{i}}\right\}
is achieved at least twice. We say that a point in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is a

if, additionally, at least one of the minima comes from a term of

Remark:

Projective tropical space

2.4

The

The construction works as follows: Let

Parametrization of the Totally nonnegative Complete Flag Variety

3.1
The Marsh-Rietsch Parametrization

As shown by Rietsch
[
29
]
,
\textnormal{Fl}_{n}^{\geq 0}
is a cell complex, whose cells
\mathcal{R}_{v,w}^{>0}
are indexed by pairs of permutations
v\leq w
in a partial order called
Bruhat order
on
S_{n}
. Each such
\mathcal{R}_{v,w}^{>0}
is given an explicit parameterization in
[
22
]
. We will describe this parameterization here, making some choices that in principle are arbitrary but will be convenient for our purposes, and invite the reader to look at the cited references for full generalities.

Any permutation
w
in
S_{n}
has at least one
expression
, which is to say, a way to write it as a product of simple transpositions
s_{i}=(i,\;i+1)
. The
length
of
w
,
\ell(w)
, is the fewest number of simple transpositions in any expression for
w
. An expression for
w
consisting of
\ell(w)
transpositions is called
reduced
. We now define Bruhat order.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
v,w\in S_{n}
. If there exists a reduced expression
\bm{w}=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
for
w
which admits a reduced subexpression
\bm{v}=s_{i_{j_{1}}}s_{i_{j_{2}}}\cdots s_{i_{j_{m}}}
for
v
where
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{m}\leq k
, we say
v\leq w
in
Bruhat order
.

We now give a useful test for when two permutations are comparable in the Bruhat order. To state it, we introduce another order, the Gale order, on subsets of
[n]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
I=\{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\}
and
J=\{j_{1}<\cdots<j_{k}\}
be subsets of
[n]
. Then
I\leq J
in the
Gale order
if
i_{r}\leq j_{r}
for every
r\in[k]
.

The following is a version of the
tableau criterion
for Bruhat order.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
v,w\in S_{n}
. For
k\in[n]
, define
v[k]
to be
\{v(1),v(2)\ldots,v(k)\}
. Then
v\leq w
in Bruhat order if and only if
v[k]\leq w[k]
in the Gale order for all
k\in[n]
.

Proof.

This is similar to
[
3
, Theorem 2.6.3]
, which says that
v\leq w
if and only if
v[k]\leq w[k]
in the Gale order for all
k
such that
v(k+1)<v(k)
. To get to our result, we are just left to show that if
v[k]\leq w[k]
in the Gale order for all
k
such that
v(k+1)<v(k)
, then
v[k]\leq w[k]
in the Gale order for all
k\in[n]
. Let
k\in[n]
be minimal such that
v[k]\nleq w[k]
. Let
k^{\prime}
be the minimal number
k^{\prime}\geq k
such that
v(k^{\prime}+1)<v(k^{\prime})
if such
k^{\prime}
exists and let
k^{\prime}=n
otherwise. By assumption and
v[k^{\prime}]\leq w[k^{\prime}]
(for the case when
k^{\prime}=n
, we use the fact that
v[n]=w[n]=[n]
). We also have
v(k)<v(k+1)<\cdots<v(k^{\prime})
. Since
v[k]\nleq w[k]
, if
v[k]=\{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\}
and
w[k]=\{j_{1}<\cdots<j_{k}\}
, then some
i_{t}>j_{t}
.

Let
v[k-1]=\{i^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<i^{\prime}_{k-1}\}
and

. Observe that for any
s\in[k-1]
, either
j_{s}=j^{\prime}_{s}
or
j_{s}=j^{\prime}_{s-1}
. In particular, this means that
j^{\prime}_{s-1}\leq j_{s}
. Similarly, whenever
i_{s}>v(k)
, we have
i_{s}=i^{\prime}_{s-1}
. By the minimality of

v[k-1]\leq w[k-1]
, and so

for all

. Thus, if
i_{s}>v(k)

i_{s}=i^{\prime}_{s-1}\leq j^{\prime}_{s-1}\leq j_{s}
. Thus,

.

To obtain

from

, we add new entries, each larger than
v(k)
. If
v[k^{\prime}]=\{i^{\prime\prime}_{1}<\cdots<i^{\prime\prime}_{k^{\prime}}\}
, then
i^{\prime\prime}_{t}=i_{t}
. To construct

from

, we add some entries to
w[k]
. It is possible some of these are smaller than

, so if
w[k^{\prime}]=\{j^{\prime\prime}_{1}<\cdots<j^{\prime\prime}_{k^{\prime}}\}

. Thus,

. This contradicts the fact that

.

∎

3.3
Extremal Non-Zero Plücker Coordinates

We define a special subset of the Plücker coordinates of a flag which we call

. For a flag

, the indices of these coordinates will depend only on which cell
\mathcal{R}_{v,w}^{>0}
contains

. In future sections, we will prove a number of useful facts about the extremal non-zero Plücker coordinates of a flag, including that the extremal non-zero Plücker coordinates determine all of the other Plücker coordinates and also form a positivity test in
\mathcal{R}_{v,w}^{>0}
. We will also see the beginnings of the parallel story about the complete flag Dressian take shape in this section.

For any
1\leq k<n
and any
P\in\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{1}-1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{n-1}-1}
, we define a map
\Xi_{P}:{\binom{[n]}{k}}\rightarrow\binom{[n]}{k}
. Intuitively, when applied to the index of a non-zero Plücker coordinate
P_{I}
, this map finds the largest member of
I
that can be increased without making the corresponding Plücker coordinate
0
and increases it maximally.

Definition 3.26
.

Let

and

\displaystyle\Xi_{P}(I)=\begin{cases}(I\setminus b)\cup a&\;\;\textnormal{if }I\textnormal{ is the index of a non-zero Pl{\"{u}}cker coordinate and $B$ is non-empty,}\\
I&\;\;\textnormal{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Recall that the indices of non-zero Plücker coordinates of a point in the TNN flag variety can be thought of as bases of a flag matroid. Thus,
\Xi_{P}
acts by basis exchange on the constituent matroids of a flag matroid. Also note that, by

, for a TNN flag

depends only on the cell

in which

.

We similarly define
\Xi_{P}^{*}
to be the map going the other way. Applied to the index of a non-zero Plücker coordinate

, it tries to find the smallest member of

which can be lowered without making the corresponding Plücker coordinate

and lowers it maximally.

Definition 3.27

. If

As a first step, we will show that the extremal indices of

coincide with the extremal indices of a point

for a flag

. By

, the extremal non-zero coordinates of a flag in

determine all the rest by three-term incidence Plücker relations. However, the Plücker coordinates of

are subject to the same incidence Plücker relations as are the Plücker coordinates of flags in

. Thus, if we show that the extremal indices of

coincide with those of

, the extremal non-zero Plücker coordinates of

will determine all the rest by the same relations. Since the only operations used in determining these coordinates are addition and division, any coordinate that is non-zero for

must also be non-zero for

and so

is a realizable flag positroid.

In a complete flag matroid, by definition, each basis lies in a flag of subsets of

consisting of bases of the constituent matroids. Thus, if we are to have

for some index

for each

. By

, each such Plücker coordinate is non-zero for

. Thus, any basis of
\bm{\mathcal{M}}
is guaranteed to be a basis of
\bm{\mathcal{M}^{v,w}}
. We are left to show that for any extremal index

of

, we have

. For indices of size

, the extremal indices are simply the Gale minimal and Gale maximal (equivalently, the minimal and maximal) indices of non-zero Plücker coordinates. By hypothesis, these are

and

, respectively, for both

and

. Pick

minimal such that the extremal indices of size

are different for

and

. Let

be the Gale minimal extremal index of
Q
of size

such that

.

Since

is an extremal index of

,

is extremal for some

. By the minimality of

is extremal for

as well and in particular,

. Recall that, for each

, the Gale minimal and Gale maximal indices of size

of non-zero Plücker coordinates of

and of

coincide, being

cannot be either the Gale minimal
or the Gale maximal extremal index of size

. Thus, we know that

is of the form

for some

and some other extremal index

which is Gale less than

. Moreover, since

is not Gale maximal, we claim it is impossible that

is Gale maximal.

Since

is the Gale minimal extremal index of size

, we have,

. Since

, we can pick

. Then,

. We have the three-term incidence Plücker relation

and

for any

. Thus,
d
must satisfy

.

Note that by

3.35
applied to

,

must be in the Gale maximal extremal index

of size

of

. Since the Gale maximal indices of non-zero Plücker coordinates of

. By dual basis exchange between

and

in the rank

of the support of

Note that the previous theorem says that the support of any TNN complete flag is a realizable flag positroid. The following result will analogously say that the support of any point in the TNN complete flag Dressian is a realizable flag positroid.

Lemma 5.23
.

The TNN complete flag Dressian decomposes as
\textnormal{FlDr}_{n}^{\geq 0}=\sqcup_{v\leq w}\textnormal{FlDr}_{v,w}^{>0}
, where the disjoint union is over all pairs

.

Proof.

By

, the support of any point in

∎

5.3
Characterizing the TNN Complete Flag Variety and Dressian

Our next goal is to show that any flag in

which is inverse to

. In this more general setting, we will have to exercise a bit more caution because our extremal non-zero Plücker coordinate are not always algebraically independent.

We require the following preliminaries.

Definition 5.24
.

Let

. Let

be the left greedy path collection with source set

Lemma 5.25
.

cannot be used in

.

The totally nonnegative tropical complete flag variety
\textnormal{TrFl}_{n}^{\geq 0}
equals the totally nonnegative complete flag Dressian
\textnormal{FlDr}_{n}^{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

It is clear by definition that
\textnormal{TrFl}_{n}^{\geq 0}\subseteq\textnormal{FlDr}_{n}^{\geq 0}
. We are left to prove the reverse inclusion.

Let
P\in\textnormal{FlDr}_{n}^{\geq 0}
. By
Lemma

5.23
,
P\in\textnormal{FlDr}^{>0}_{v,w}
for some
v\leq w\in S_{n}
. Using
Proposition

5.29
, Apply the map
\textnormal{Trop}\hskip 1.99168pt\Psi_{v,w}
to the subset
S_{v,w}
of the extremal non-infinite Plücker coordinates of
P
to get a collection of real numbers
\bm{a}
. Consider
Q=\textnormal{Trop}\hskip 1.99168pt\Phi_{v,w}(\bm{a})\in\textnormal{TrFl}_{n}^{\geq 0}
. By construction,
P
and
Q
have the same support.

Since
\textnormal{Trop}\hskip 1.99168pt\Phi_{v,w}
and
\textnormal{Trop}\hskip 1.99168pt\Psi_{v,w}
are inverse maps,
P
and
Q
agree on those Plücker coordinates whose indices lie in
S_{v,w}
. By
Lemma

5.27
and
Theorem

5.20
, the positive tropicalizations of the three-term incidence Plücker relations determine all the other Plücker coordinates in terms of those coordinates in
S_{v,w}
. Since both
\textnormal{FlDr}_{n}^{\geq 0}
and
\textnormal{TrFl}_{n}^{\geq 0}
satisfy the positive tropicalizations of the three-term incidence Plücker relations,
P
and
Q
agree on all Plücker coordinates. Thus,
P
lies in
\textnormal{TrFl}_{n}^{\geq 0}
.
∎

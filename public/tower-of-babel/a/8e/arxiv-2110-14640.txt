Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a given smooth bounded domain, with
N\geq 4
. Throughout this paper, we are concerned with the following nonlinear minimization problem :

\displaystyle Q_{\lambda}=\inf_{\displaystyle{\begin{array}[]{lll}(u,v)\in\left(H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\right)^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}\end{array}}}E_{\lambda}(u,v),

with

E_{\lambda}(u,v)=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2\|u\|_{q}^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx}}+\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2\|v(x)\|_{q}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}b(x)|\nabla v(x)|^{2}dx}-\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda}{\|u\|_{q}\|v\|_{q}}\int_{\Omega}u(x)v(x)dx}.

where
a
and
b
are positive continuous functions on
\overline{\Omega}
,
\lambda
is a real constant and
q=\frac{2N}{N-2}
is the critical exponent for the Sobolev embedding

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega).

(3)

Note that positive minimizers
u
and
v
for (
1
) are non trivial solutions of the Euler-Lagrange equation associated to (
1
) namely :

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rclll}-\div(a(x)\nabla u)-\lambda v&=&\Lambda_{1}u^{2^{*}-1}&\mbox{in}&\Omega\\
-\div(b(x)\nabla v)-\lambda u&=&\Lambda_{2}v^{2^{*}-1}&\mbox{in}&\Omega\\
u\geq 0,\quad v&\geq&0&\mbox{ in }&\Omega\\
u=v&=&0&\mbox{on}&\partial\Omega,\\
\end{array}\right.

(4)

where
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
are the Lagrange multipliers associated to (
1
) .

In the well-known article
[
5

5

and

when

and for

when

where
\lambda_{1}
denotes the first eigenvalue of

\lambda^{*}

In the presence of a non-constant positive and bounded weight

-\div(h(x)\nabla u)=\lambda u+|u|^{q-2}u\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\quad u>0\mbox{ in }\Omega,\quad u=0\quad\partial\Omega.

(5)

They proved that the existence of a solution depends on the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}^{h}
of

We refer also to

In this paper, we consider the case where

In order to state the problem and to announce our main results, we introduce some preliminaries.

Let us assume the existence of
x_{0},
in
\Omega
such that, in a neighborhood of
x_{0},

a
and
b
behave like

a(x)=a(x_{0})+{A_{k}|x-x_{0}|}^{k}+{|x-x_{0}|}^{k}\theta_{a}(x),\quad\mbox{as}\ x\rightarrow x_{0},

(6)

b(x)=b(x_{0})+{B_{l}|x-x_{0}|}^{l}+{|x-x_{0}|}^{l}\theta_{b}(x),\quad\mbox{as}\ x\rightarrow x_{0},

(7)

where

,

A_{k},

B_{l}
are positive constants,
\theta_{a}(x)
and
\theta_{b}(x)

.

The parameters

and

N\geq 4
the case

with

and

is treated through a classical procedure.

If

kA_{k}\leq\frac{\tilde{a}(x)}{{|x-x_{0}|}^{k}}\quad\mbox{a.e.}\quad x\in\Omega,

(8)

and

lB_{l}\leq\frac{\tilde{b}(x)}{{|x-x_{0}|}^{l}}\quad\mbox{a.e.}\quad x\in\Omega,

(9)

where

\tilde{a}(x):=\nabla a(x)\cdot(x-x_{0})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{b}(x):=\nabla b(x)\cdot(x-x_{0}).

In order to highlight the difficulty, we consider the blow-up of

around

, see

14

. Depending on the parameters

,

More precisely, we consider the function

\displaystyle\ u(x)=\varepsilon^{-\frac{(N-2)}{2}}w_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{\varepsilon}\right),

One has

and
\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}=\|z_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}=\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}=\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

E_{\lambda}(u,v)=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y+x_{0})|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}dy}+\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}b(\varepsilon y+x_{0})|\nabla z_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}dy}-\lambda\varepsilon^{2}\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}w_{\varepsilon}(y)z_{\varepsilon}(y)dy}.

Consequently, the blow-up around
x=x_{0}
gives

E_{\lambda}(u,v)\underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\sim}\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(x_{0})|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}dy}+\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}b(x_{0})|\nabla z_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}dy}-\lambda\varepsilon^{2}\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}w_{\varepsilon}(y)z_{\varepsilon}(y)dy}+

\displaystyle{\frac{A_{k}}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\varepsilon y+x_{0}|^{k}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}dy}+\displaystyle{\frac{B_{l}}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\varepsilon y+x_{0}|^{l}|\nabla z_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}dy}-\lambda\varepsilon^{2}\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}w_{\varepsilon}(y)z_{\varepsilon}(y)dy}.

Then, as we can see the three last terms have different weights and the exponents

and

are critical for our problem.

Let us finally point out that the lower dimension

2
Main Results

Let us suppose that there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that

a(x_{0})=\min\left\{a(x),\ x\in\bar{\Omega}\right\}.

b(x_{0})=\min\left\{b(x),\ x\in\bar{\Omega}\right\}.

In this section, we will prove the existence of a solution to the problem (
1
). For this we define:

S=\inf_{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),{\|u\|_{L^{q}}=1}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\,,

(11)

which corresponds to the best constant for the Sobolev embedding
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
into
L^{q}(\Omega).

In this paper, we assume that
a(x_{0})=b(x_{0})
, and we denote by
\gamma_{0}
this value.

We are now ready to state the main result of this paper

Theorem 1
.

Assume that
a,b\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
satisfy (
6
) and (
7
), respectively. Let
\lambda_{1}^{a}
, respectively
\lambda_{1}^{b}
, be the first eigenvalue of
-\div(a(x)\nabla\cdot)
, respectively
-\div(b(x)\nabla\cdot)
on
\Omega
with zero Dirichlet boundary condition. Define
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}=\min\{\lambda_{1}^{a},\lambda_{1}^{b}\}
. We have :

(1)
If
N\geq 4
,
k>2
and
l>2,
then
Q_{\lambda}
is achieved for every
\lambda\in\left(0,\lambda_{1}^{a}\right)\cap\left(0,\lambda_{1}^{b}\right)
.

(2)
If
N\geq 5,

k=2
and
l=2,
then there exists a constant
\gamma(N)=\frac{(N-2)N(N+2)}{8(N-1)}(A_{2}+B_{2})
such that
Q_{\lambda}
is achieved for every
\lambda\in\left(\gamma(N),\tilde{\lambda}_{1}\right)
.

(3)
If
N\geq 5,

k=2
and
l>2,
respectively
k>2
and
l=2,
then there exists a constant

\tilde{\gamma}(N)=\frac{N(N-2)(N+2)}{8(N-1)}
such that

is achieved for every

, respectively

(4)

for every

3
Sufficient Conditions for the Existence of Minimizers

We first prove the existence of
Q_{\lambda},
which is guaranteed by the following result :

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\varphi_{1}^{a}
, respectively
\varphi_{1}^{b}
, be the first eigenfunction of
-\div(a(x)\nabla\cdot)
, respectively
-\div(b(x)\nabla\cdot)
, associated to the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}^{a}
, respectively
\lambda_{1}^{b}
.
We have

(i)

Assume that
0<\lambda<\tilde{\lambda}_{1},
then

Q_{\lambda}\geq 0.

(ii)

For
\displaystyle{\lambda\geq\frac{\|\varphi_{1}^{a}\|_{L^{q}}\|\varphi_{1}^{b}\|_{L^{q}}}{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{1}^{a}\varphi_{1}^{b}dx}}|\Omega|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}}\max(\lambda_{1}^{a},\lambda_{1}^{b}),
one has

Q_{\lambda}\leq 0,

Proof

(i).

Let
0<\lambda<\tilde{\lambda}_{1}(\Omega)
, and let
u
and
v
be such that
\|u\|_{L^{q}}=\|v\|_{L^{q}}=1.

By the definitions of
\lambda_{1}^{a},\lambda_{1}^{b}
and
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}(\Omega)
one has

E_{\lambda}(u,v)\geq\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}{2}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}uv\ dx.

By applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality , we find

E_{\lambda}(u,v)\geq\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}{2}\left[\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}\right].

Thus

E_{\lambda}(u,v)\geq\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}{2}\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}}-\|v\|_{L^{2}}\right)^{2}\geq 0.

Consequently,
Q_{\lambda}\geq 0
.

(ii)

We have

Q_{\lambda}\leq E_{\lambda}(\frac{\varphi_{1}^{a}}{\|\varphi_{1}^{a}\|_{L^{q}}},\frac{\varphi_{1}^{b}}{\|\varphi_{1}^{b}\|_{L^{q}}}),

which implies that,

Q_{\lambda}\leq\frac{1}{2\|\varphi_{1}^{a}\|_{L^{q}}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla\varphi_{1}^{a}|^{2}dx+\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2\|\varphi_{1}^{b}\|_{L^{q}}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}b(x)|\nabla\varphi_{1}^{b}|^{2}dx}-\frac{\lambda}{\|\varphi_{1}^{a}\|_{L^{q}}\|\varphi_{1}^{b}\|_{L^{q}}}\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{1}^{a}\varphi_{1}^{b}dx}.

By the definitions of
\varphi_{1}^{a}
and
\varphi_{1}^{b},
one has

Q_{\lambda}\leq\frac{1}{2\|\varphi_{1}^{a}\|_{L^{q}}^{2}}\lambda_{1}^{a}\int_{\Omega}{|\varphi_{1}^{a}|}^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2\|\varphi_{1}^{b}\|_{L^{q}}^{2}}\lambda_{1}^{b}\int_{\Omega}{|\varphi_{1}^{b}|}^{2}dx-\frac{\lambda}{\|\varphi_{1}^{a}\|_{L^{q}}\|\varphi_{1}^{b}\|_{L^{q}}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{1}^{a}\varphi_{1}^{b}dx.

Using the embedding of
L^{q}
into
L^{2}
, there exists a positive constant
C_{1}=|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}-{\frac{1}{q}}}
such that

Q_{\lambda}\leq\frac{1}{2\|\varphi_{1}^{a}\|_{L^{q}}^{2}}\lambda_{1}^{a}C_{1}^{2}\|\varphi_{1}^{a}\|_{L^{q}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\|\varphi_{1}^{b}\|_{L^{q}}^{2}}\lambda_{1}^{b}C_{1}^{2}\|\varphi_{1}^{b}\|_{L^{q}}^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\|\varphi_{1}^{a}\|_{L^{q}}\|\varphi_{1}^{b}\|_{L^{q}}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{1}^{a}\varphi_{1}^{b}dx.

Thus

Q_{\lambda}\leq|\Omega|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\max(\lambda_{1}^{a},\lambda_{1}^{b})-\frac{\lambda}{\|\varphi_{1}^{a}\|_{L^{q}}\|\varphi_{1}^{b}\|_{L^{q}}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{1}^{a}\varphi_{1}^{b}dx.

Therefore,
Q_{\lambda}\leq 0
, completing the proof of the proposition.
\blacksquare

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
0<\lambda<\tilde{\lambda}_{1}.

If

, then the infimum in (
1

Proof
Let
\left\{U_{n}\right\}\subset\left(H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\right)^{2}

) that is,

\|U_{n}\|_{L^{q}}=1\quad(\mbox{which means }\|u_{n}\|_{q}=1,\quad\|v_{n}\|_{q}=1).

(12)

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}b(x)|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u_{n}v_{n}dx}=Q_{\lambda}+o(1)\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\longrightarrow\infty.

(13)

By (

) and using the definition of

and

Again using (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}b(x)|\nabla v|^{2}dx\right.+\left.\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}b(x)|\nabla z_{n}|^{2}dx\right]

(14)

On the other hand, as

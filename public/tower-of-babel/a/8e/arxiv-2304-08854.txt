Flag-transitive automorphism groups of 2 2 -designs with λ ≥ ( r , λ ) 2 \lambda\geq(r,\lambda)^{2} are not product type
Huiling Li, Zhilin Zhang, Shenglin Zhou 1. School of Mathematical Science, Zhejiang University,Hangzhou, Zhejiang 310027, PR China2. Abstract
In this note we show that a flag-transitive automorphism group G G of a non-trivial 2 2 - ( v , k , λ ) (v,k,\lambda) design with λ ≥ ( r , λ ) 2 \lambda\geq(r,\lambda)^{2} is not of product action type. In conclusion, G G is a primitive group of affine or almost simple type. 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05B05, 05C25, 20B25 Key words: 2-design; flag-transitive; product action

Introduction

A
2
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design
\cal D
is a pair
(\Omega,{\cal B})
with a set
\Omega
of
v
points and a set
\cal B
of blocks such
that each block is a
k
-subset of
\Omega
and each two distinct points are contained in
\lambda
blocks.
The design
\cal D
is nontrivial if
2<k<v
. Let
b
denote the number of blocks, then the number of blocks containing each point of
\cal D
is a constant, denoted by
r
. The positive integers
v,b,r,k,\lambda
are known as the parameters of
\cal D
.
An automorphism of a design
{\cal D}=(\Omega,\cal B)
is a
permutation
\pi
of
\Omega
such that
B\in\cal B
implies

B^{\pi}\in\cal B
. The set of all automorphisms of a design forms a group, the (full) automorphism group,
Aut({\cal D})
.
For a group
G\leq Aut({\cal D})
,
G
is said
to be point-primitive if
G
acts primitively on
\Omega
, and said to be point-imprimitive otherwise.
A flag of
\cal D
is a point-block pair
(\alpha,B)
where
\alpha
is a point and
B
is a block incident with
\alpha
.
A subgroup
G
of
Aut({\cal D})
is said to be flag-transitive if
G
acts transitively on the set of flags of
\cal D
.

This paper continues to study the
2
-
(v,k,\lambda)
designs with
\lambda\geq(r,\lambda)^{2}
. In this case, any flag-transitive automorphism group
G

must act point-primitively on
\cal D
according to a result of Dembowski
[
3
, (2.3.7)]
. In
[
8
]
, Zhou and Zhan studied this type of
designs which admit a flag-transitive automorphism group
G
.
They proved the following result.

Proposition 1.1

[
8
, Theorem 1.2]

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
2
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design with

\lambda\geq(r,\lambda)^{2}
. If
G
is a flag-transitive
automorphism group of
\mathcal{D}
, then
G
is of affine, almost simple type, or product type with
{\rm Soc}(G)\cong T\times T
, where
T
is a nonabelian simple group and
G
has rank
3
.

In this paper we focus on the last case of Proposition
1.1
in which
G
has the product action. The main result is:

Theorem 1.2

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
2
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design with
\lambda\geq(r,\lambda)^{2}
which admits a flag-transitive automorphism group
G
. Then
G
is not of product type.

Combing Theorem
1.2
with Proposition
1.1
, we obtain the following reduction theorem.

Theorem 1.3

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
2
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design with
\lambda\geq(r,\lambda)^{2}
which admits a flag-transitive automorphism group
G
. Then
G
is of affine or almost simple type.

Lemma 2.2

k=\displaystyle\frac{(c+d-2)(\omega+1)}{2}+1

Proof.
It is well known that the suborbits of

\Omega_{2}^{2}=\{(p,q)\,|\,p\in\Omega,q\in\Omega_{2}(p)\},

\Omega_{1}(p)

Clearly, if

Count the pairs

, where

\Omega_{2}^{2}
that contained in

. Hence

.

, and

Lemma 2.5

Let

has positive integer solutions if and only if

If

Lemma 2.6

Lemma 2.8

If
(m,\omega)
is a positive solution of Equation (1) for some integer

Proof.
From Equation (1), we have

(c+1)m=\frac{2c-1}{2c}(\omega+1)+\frac{1}{c}=(\omega+1)-\frac{\omega-1}{2c}.

Then
(c+1)m\geq\omega

On the other hand, from

As the application of above lemmas, we give the following general result.

Proposition 2.9

Let the notation
\Omega,\Delta
and
\omega
be as above, and let
G=(S_{\Delta}\times S_{\Delta})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, where
S_{\Delta}
denotes the symmetric group on
\Delta
. If
(m,\omega)
is a positive integer solution of Equation (1) for some positive integer
c

(\omega^{2},k,\lambda)

\mathcal{D}

acts flag-transitively on it.

Proof.
Let
G=(S_{\Delta}\times S_{\Delta})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, then the elements of the subgroup
S_{\Delta}\times S_{\Delta}
mapping
\Delta_{i}
into
\Delta_{i}
, and the elements which are not in
S_{\Delta}\times S_{\Delta}
interchange
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
. We use the same symbols such as

to denote the elements of
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}

denotes the point of
\Omega
where
\alpha
is the first component which means it come from
\Delta_{1}
,
\beta
is the second component which means it come from
\Delta_{2}

G
is a permutation group acting on
\Omega
with rank 3, and
G

has three orbits on
\Omega^{2}=\Omega\times\Omega
, denoted by
\Omega_{0}^{2}
,
\Omega_{1}^{2}
and
\Omega_{2}^{2}

Correspondingly, if

\Omega_{0}(p)=\{p\},\Omega_{1}(p)=\{(\alpha,\beta),(\beta,\alpha)\,|\,\beta\in\Delta,\alpha\neq\beta\}
and
\Omega_{2}(p)=\{(\beta,\gamma)\,|\,\beta,\gamma\in\Delta\setminus\{\alpha\}\}
of lengths
1
,
2(\omega-1)
and
(\omega-1)^{2}

Suppose that

satisfy Equation (1) where
\omega=|\Delta|
. By Lemma
2.8
, we have
(c+1)m<\omega
, it follows that there are
(c+1)m
elements in
\Delta_{1}
:

\alpha_{1}^{(1)},\alpha_{2}^{(1)},...,\alpha_{c+1}^{(1)},\alpha_{1}^{(2)},\alpha_{2}^{(2)},...,\alpha_{c+1}^{(2)},\alpha_{1}^{(m)},\alpha_{2}^{(m)},...,\alpha_{c+1}^{(m)}.

Similarly,
cm<(c+1)m<\omega
, there are
cm

\Delta_{2}
:

\beta_{1}^{(1)},\beta_{2}^{(1)},...,\beta_{c}^{(1)},...,\beta_{1}^{(m)},\beta_{2}^{(m)},...,\beta_{c}^{(m)}.

Using these distinct elements,
\alpha_{i}^{(s)}
and
\beta_{j}^{(s)}
where

,
1\leq j\leq c
and
1\leq s\leq m
, we construct the points of
\Omega
. Let
p_{ij}^{(s)}=(\alpha_{i}^{(s)},\beta_{j}^{(s)})
. Then we obtain

, and

B=\{p_{ij}^{(s)}|\,1\leq i\leq c+1,1\leq j\leq c,1\leq s\leq m\}.

Clearly,
|B|=k
. Let
{\cal B}=\{B^{g}\,|\,g\in G\}
. Then we assert that the incidence structure
{\cal D}=(\Omega,\cal B)
is a
2
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design where

.

In fact, we only need to prove that there exists a positive integer

such that each pair of points

of

occurs together in exactly
\lambda

Assume that

, and

contained in
\mu

. If there exists

such that

, then

\mu
blocks,

. Hence, in this case, the number of blocks containing

(p,q)

\Omega_{1}^{2}
or
\Omega_{2}^{2}

If
(p,q)\in\Omega_{1}^{2}

p,q\in B
, where

. By the definition of

and

, or

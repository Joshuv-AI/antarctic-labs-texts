Local smoothing estimates of fractional Schrödinger equations in α \alpha -modulation spaces with some applications
Yufeng Lu

Abstract
. We show some new local smoothing estimates of the fractional Schrödinger equations with initial data in α \alpha -modulation spaces via decoupling inequalities. Furthermore, our necessary conditions show that the local smoothing estimates are sharp in some cases. As applications, the local smoothing estimates could show some new local well-posedness on modulation spaces of the fourth-order nonlinear Schrödinger equations on the line.

1.
Introduction

Denote
S_{\beta}(t):={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}e^{it\left|\xi\right|^{\beta}}{\mathscr{F}}
, the fractional Schrödinger semigroup,
I=[0,1]
.
Local smoothing estimates in harmonic analysis, based on
L^{p}
Sobolev spaces, are as follows.

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\beta}(t)u\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{W^{s,p}},\ \forall u\in W^{s,p}.

In contrast to the fix-time estimate:

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\beta}(t_{0})u\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{W^{s_{0},p}},\ \forall u\in W^{s_{0},p},

we know that when we take the integration of
t
over
I
, the sharp regularity index
s
could be less than
s_{0}
. So, we call it the local smoothing estimate. One of the significant open problems in harmonic analysis is the local smoothing conjecture of the wave equation. Until now, we only know this conjecture is true when
d=2
. One can refer to
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

In this paper, we consider the local smoothing estimate of fractional Schrödinger semi-group on
\alpha
-modulation spaces below:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\beta}(t)u\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}},\ \forall u\in M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}.

The
\alpha
-modulation spaces
M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}
, introduced by Gröbner in
[
4
]
, are proposed to be intermediate function spaces to connect modulation and Besov spaces with respect to parameters
\alpha\in[0,1]
. The modulation spaces
M_{p,q}^{s}
, introduced by Feichtinger in
[
5
]
, are used to measure the decay and the regularity of the function by the short time Fourier transform (STFT). Unlike the dyadic decomposition in the definition of Besov spaces, the modulation spaces can be characterized by the bounded admission partition of unity (BAPU). The
\alpha
-modulation spaces
M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}
could be regarded as the generalized modulation spaces. Similarly, they could be defined by
\alpha
-BAPU. The precise definitions of these spaces are given in Section
2
. One can also refer to the textbooks by Gröchenig
[
6
]
and Wang et al.
[
7
]
.

Some recent work has been devoted to the study of
\alpha
-modulation spaces. Borup and Nielsen in
[
8
]
constructed Banach frames for
\alpha
-modulation spaces. Kobayashi et al. in
[
9
]
discussed the boundedness for a class of pseudo-differential operators with symbols in
\alpha
-modulation spaces. Wang and Han in
[
10
]
described some basic properties of these spaces including the dual spaces, embeddings, scaling and algebraic structure. The relations between
\alpha
-modulation spaces and some classical function spaces such as Sobolev spaces were given by Kato in
[
11
]
. One can also refer
[
12
,
13
]
.

One of the most significant differences between
\alpha
-modulation spaces and Sobolev spaces is the boundedness of the unimodular Fourier multiplier such as
S_{\beta}(t)={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}e^{it\left|\xi\right|^{\beta}}{\mathscr{F}}.

Take for example the case of
\alpha=0,\beta=2
. Miyachi in
[
14
]
showed that
S_{2}(t)
is bounded on
L^{p}
if and only if
p=2
. However, as showed by Bényi et al. in
[
15
]
,
S_{2}(t)
is bounded on all
M_{p,q}^{s}
with
1\leqslant p,q\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
. For general cases, we refer to
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
. In this sense, the
\alpha
-modulation spaces are better spaces for the initial data of the Cauchy problems for some nonlinear dispersive equations in contrast with the Sobolev spaces. One can refer to
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
. Most recently, Schippa in
[
25
]
gave some sufficiency and necessity conditions of the local smoothing estimates of the Schrödinger semigroup in modulation spaces when
p\geqslant 2
by the decoupling inequality. The goals of our paper are extending these results to the general cases with
\alpha,\beta>0,1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
.

Denote
M_{\beta}=\left\{(\xi,\left|\xi\right|^{\beta}):\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},1/2\leqslant\left|\xi\right|\leqslant 1\right\}
, the compact surface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
. One can easily know that the Gauss curvature of
M_{\beta}
is nonzero when
\beta\neq 1

\beta>1

2.
Preliminary

2.1.
Basic notation

The following notation will be used throughout this article. For
0<p,q\leqslant\infty
, we denote

\displaystyle\sigma(p,q)

\displaystyle:=d\left(0\wedge(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})\wedge(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}-1)\right);

\displaystyle\tau(p,q)

\displaystyle:=d\left(0\vee(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})\vee(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}-1)\right),

where
a\wedge b=\min\left\{a,b\right\},a\vee b=\max\left\{a,b\right\}
.

We write
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to denote the Schwartz space of all complex-valued rapidly decreasing infinity differentiable functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to denote the dual space of
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, all called the space of all tempered distributions. For simplification, we omit
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
without causing ambiguity. The Fourier transform is defined by
{\mathscr{F}}f(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)e^{-ix\xi}d\xi
, and the inverse Fourier transform by
{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}f(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(\xi)e^{ix\xi}d\xi
. For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we denote
\left\langle x\right\rangle=(1+\left|x\right|^{2})^{1/2}
. For
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
, we define the
L^{p}
norm:

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{p}=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|f(x)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p},&$1\leqslant p<\infty$;\\
\esssup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|f(x)\right|,&$p=\infty$.\end{cases}

We also define the
L^{p}
Sobolev norm:

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{W^{s,p}}=\left\|(I-\triangle)^{s/2}f\right\|_{p},

where
\triangle
is the Laplace operator,
(I-\triangle)^{s/2}={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{s}{\mathscr{F}}
.
Recall that the Sobolev spaces are defined by
W^{s,p}=\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}:\left\|f\right\|_{W^{s,p}}<\infty\right\}
.

We use the notation
I\lesssim J
if there is an independently constant C such that
I\leqslant CJ
. Also we denote
I\approx J
if
I\lesssim J
and
J\lesssim I
. For
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
, we denote the dual index
p^{\prime}
such that
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
.

Definition 2.1

(Dyadic decomposition)
.

Choose
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow[0,1]
be a smooth radial bump function adapted to the ball
B(0,2)
:
\psi(\xi)=1
as
\left|\xi\right|\leqslant 1
and
\psi(\xi)=0
as
\left|\xi\right|\geqslant 2
. We denote
\varphi(\xi)=\psi(\xi)-\psi(2\xi)
, and
\varphi_{j}(\xi)=\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)
for
1\leqslant j,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\varphi_{0}(\xi)=1-\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\varphi_{j}(\xi)
. Denote
\triangle_{j}={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\varphi_{j}{\mathscr{F}}
.
We say that
\left\{\triangle_{j}\right\}_{j\geqslant 0}
are the dyadic decomposition operators.

2.2.
\alpha
-modulation spaces

Definition 2.2

(
\alpha
-covering)
.

Let
\alpha<1
. A countable set
\left\{Q_{i}\right\}_{i}
, where
Q_{i}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, is called a
\alpha
-covering of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
if:

(i):

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}=\cup_{i}Q_{i}
,

(ii):

\#\left\{Q^{\prime}\in Q_{i}:Q^{\prime}\cap Q\neq\emptyset\right\}\leqslant c(d),
uniformly for
Q\in Q_{i}
,

(iii):

\left\langle x\right\rangle^{\alpha d}\approx\left|Q_{i}\right|
uniformly for
x\in Q_{i}
.

Definition 2.3

(
\alpha
-modulation spaces,
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\alpha<1
, denote
\beta=\alpha/(1-\alpha)
, suppose that
C>c>0
are two appropriate constants such that
\left\{B_{k}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
is a
\alpha
-covering of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, where
B_{k}=B(\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\beta}k,\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\beta})
. We can choose a Schwartz function sequence
\left\{\eta_{k}^{\alpha}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left|\eta_{k}^{\alpha}(\xi)\right|\gtrsim 1,\ \mbox{ if }\left|\xi-\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}k\right|<c\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}};\\
{\mbox{supp}\ }\eta_{k}^{\alpha}\subseteq\left\{\xi:\left|\xi-\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}k\right|<C\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\right\};\\
\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\eta_{k}^{\alpha}(\xi)\equiv 1,\ \forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d};\\
\left|\partial^{\gamma}\eta_{k}^{\alpha}(\xi)\right|\leqslant C_{\alpha}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-\frac{\alpha\left|\gamma\right|}{1-\alpha}},\ \forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d},\end{cases}

where
C_{\alpha}
is a positive constant depending only on
d
and
\alpha
. We usually call these
\left\{\eta_{k}^{\alpha}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
the bounded admission partition of unity corresponding (
\alpha-
BAPU) to the
\alpha
-covering
\left\{B_{k}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
. The frequency decomposition operators can be defined by

\displaystyle\Box_{k}^{\alpha}:={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\eta_{k}^{\alpha}{\mathscr{F}}.

Let
1\leqslant p,q\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}},\alpha\in[0,1)
, the
\alpha
-modulation space is defined by

\displaystyle M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}=\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}:\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}}=\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{sq/(1-\alpha)}\left\|\Box_{k}^{\alpha}f\right\|_{p}^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\infty\right\},

with the usual modification when
q=\infty
.

When
\alpha=0
, we usually denote
M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}
by
M_{p,q}^{s}
.

Remark 2.4
.

In the previous literature, researchers usually consider
\alpha
-modulation spaces in the case of
\alpha\in[0,1)
, here we extend the definition to
\alpha<0
. For the convenience of readers, we put the specific definition in Appendix
A
.

2.3.
Some lemmas

In this subsection, we gather some useful results.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
10
]
, embedding)
.

Let
1\leqslant p,q_{1},q_{2}\leqslant\infty,s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}},\alpha\in[0,1)
.

(1):

If
q_{1}>q_{2},s_{1}+d(1-\alpha)/q_{1}>s_{2}+d(1-\alpha)/q_{2}
, then
M_{p,q_{1}}^{s_{1},\alpha}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q_{2}}^{s_{2},\alpha}
.

(2):

If
q_{1}\leqslant q_{2},s_{1}\geqslant s_{2}
, then
M_{p,q_{1}}^{s_{1},\alpha}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q_{2}}^{s_{2},\alpha}
.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
10
]
, interpolation)
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1),1\leqslant p,p_{0},p_{1},q,q_{0},q_{1}\leqslant\infty,s,s_{0},s_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Suppose
0<\theta<1
and

\displaystyle s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1},\ \frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{0}},\ \frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}},

then we have

\displaystyle\left(M_{p_{0},q_{0}}^{s_{0},\alpha},M_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{s_{1},\alpha}\right)_{\theta}=M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
1\leqslant p,q\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}},\alpha\in[0,1)
. Then
M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}\hookrightarrow L^{p}
if and only if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(1):

q\leqslant p<\infty,s\geqslant-(1-\alpha)\sigma(p,q);

(2):

p<q,s>-(1-\alpha)\sigma(p,q);

(3):

p=\infty,q=1,s\geqslant-(1-\alpha)\sigma(p,q);

(4):

p=\infty,q>1,s>-(1-\alpha)\sigma(p,q).

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\beta>2,t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
. Denote
\rho_{k,t}(\xi)=e^{it\left|\xi\right|^{\beta}}\sigma(\xi-k)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\rho_{k,t}(\cdot)\right\|_{1}\lesssim\left\langle t\left|k\right|^{\beta-2}\right\rangle^{d/2}.

Then for any
u\in L^{p},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\beta}(t)\Box_{k}u\right\|_{p}\leqslant C\left\langle t\left|k\right|^{(\beta-2)}\right\rangle^{d\left|1/2-1/p\right|}\left\|u\right\|_{p}

Moreover, if we denote
\alpha=1-\beta/2<0,\Box_{k}^{\alpha}={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\sigma(\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-\alpha/(1-\alpha)}\xi-k){\mathscr{F}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\beta}(t)\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\right\|_{p}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{d\left|1/2-1/p\right|}\left\|u\right\|_{p}.

The estimates of
{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\rho_{k,t}
can be found in Lemma 3.3 of
[
16
]
. Notice that
S_{\beta}(t)\Box_{k}u={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\rho_{k,t}*u
, the
(p,p)
boundedness of
S_{\beta}(t)\Box_{k}
follows by the convolution of Young’s inequality and the interpolation of the
(2,2)
boundedness. As for
S_{\beta}(t)\Box_{k}^{\alpha}
, if we denote
\rho_{k,t}^{\alpha}(\xi)={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}e^{it\left|\xi\right|^{\beta}}\sigma(\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-\alpha/(1-\alpha)}\xi-k)
, then
S_{\beta}(t)\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\rho_{k,t}^{\alpha}*u
. Therefore, we only need to estimate
\left\|{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\rho_{k,t}^{\alpha}\right\|
. By the scaling invariance of the
{\mathscr{F}}L^{1}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-\frac{\beta+d}{p}}\left\|S_{\beta}(t)v\right\|_{L^{p}(\lambda^{\beta}I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{\beta+d}{p}+\varepsilon}\left\|\left\|S_{\beta}(t)\Box_{\lambda^{\beta/2},k}v\right\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\lambda^{\beta}I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-\frac{\beta+d}{p}+\varepsilon}\left\|\left\|S_{\beta}(\lambda^{-\beta}t)\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u(\lambda^{-1}x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\lambda^{\beta}I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\varepsilon}\left\|\left\|S_{\beta}(t)\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\right\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{2}}.

By Lemma
2.8
, for any
t\in I
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\beta}(t)\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\right\|_{p}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{d(1/2-1/p)}\left\|\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\right\|_{p}.

Taking this into the estimate of
S_{\beta}(t)u
above, we have

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\beta}(t)u\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\lambda^{\varepsilon}\left\|\left\|\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\right\|_{p}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{2}}=\lambda^{\varepsilon}\left\|u\right\|_{M_{p,2}^{0,\alpha}}.

So, by dyadic decomposition, for any
u\in\mathscr{S}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\beta}(t)u\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{M_{p,2}^{\varepsilon,\alpha}}.

Then by Lemma
2.13
, we know that
M_{p,2}^{s}\hookrightarrow M_{p,2}^{0,\beta}
if
s\geqslant-\beta\tau(p,2)=(\beta-1)d(1/2-1/p)-\beta/p
, we can get the result as desired in the proposition.

∎Then, we could give the proof of Theorem
1.9
.

(a):

By Remark
1.11
, when
p\leqslant 2
or
p=\infty
, we do not have any smooth effects. So, in these cases, the argument in Theorem
1.9
follows by the results in
[
26
]
.

(b):

Proposition
5.1
above is just the case of
(p,q)=(p_{0},2)
. Then, by Lemma
2.5
, we know that for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have
M_{p_{0},1}^{s}\hookrightarrow M_{p_{0},2}^{s}
and
M_{p_{0},\infty}^{s+d/2+\varepsilon}\hookrightarrow M_{p_{0},2}^{s}
, which means that (
1.3
) is true under the conditions of Theorem
1.9
when
(p,q)=(p_{0},1),(p_{0},\infty)
.

(c):

The rest of the cases follow by interpolation between (a) and (b) above. For example, Condition (A) in Theorem
1.9
can be obtained by interpolating between
(p,q)=(\infty,\infty),(\infty,1),(p_{0},2),(p_{0},\infty).
The other conditions are the same.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.12

Choose
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}
with
{\mbox{supp}\ }\widehat{\varphi}\subseteq\left\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:1/2\leqslant\left|\xi\right|\leqslant 1\right\}
. Then we have
\left\|\varphi\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}=\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p}\approx 1
. Recall that

\displaystyle S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{it\left|\xi\right|^{\beta}+ix\xi}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)d\xi,

so, we know that
\left|S_{\beta}(t)\varphi(x)\right|\gtrsim 1
when
\left|x\right|\ll 1,\left|t\right|\ll 1
.

(a):

For any
\lambda\gg 1
, take
u(x)=\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=\varphi(\lambda x)
, then we have
\widehat{u}(\xi)=\lambda^{-d}\widehat{\varphi}(\lambda^{-1}\xi)
, which means that
{\mbox{supp}\ }\widehat{u}\subseteq\left\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\lambda/2\leqslant\left|\xi\right|\leqslant\lambda\right\}
. Denote

\wedge_{\lambda}=\left\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}:\Box_{k}u\neq 0\right\}.

Then by the orthogonality, we know that for any
k\in\wedge_{\lambda}
, we have
\left\langle k\right\rangle\approx\lambda
. Also
{}^{\#}\wedge_{\lambda}\approx\lambda^{d}
. So, by Young’s convolution inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{s}\left\|\left\|\Box_{k}u\right\|_{p}\right\|_{\ell_{k\in\wedge_{\lambda}}^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{s}\left\|\left\|u\right\|_{1}\left\|\left\|{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\sigma(\cdot-k)\right\|_{p}\right\|\right\|_{\ell_{k\in\wedge_{\lambda}}^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{s-d+d/q}.

By scaling, we have

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\beta}(t)u\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\left\|S_{\beta}(\lambda^{\beta}t)\varphi(\lambda x)\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-(\beta+d)/p}\left\|S_{\beta}(t)\varphi\right\|_{L^{p}(\lambda^{\beta}I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\lambda^{-(\beta+d)/p},

Take these two estimates into (
1.3
), we have
s\geqslant d(1-1/p-1/q)-\beta/p.

(b):

Take
u=\varphi_{\lambda}
as above. By scaling, we have

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\beta}(t)u\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\left\|S_{\beta}(\lambda^{\beta}t)\varphi(\lambda x)\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{d(\beta-1)/p}\left\|S_{\beta}(\lambda^{\beta}t)\varphi(\lambda^{\beta}x)\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

(6.1)

\displaystyle:=\lambda^{d(\beta-1)/p}\left\|I(t,x)\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

where

I(t,x)=S_{\beta}(\lambda^{\beta}t)\varphi(\lambda^{\beta}x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{i\lambda^{\beta}(t\left|\xi\right|^{\beta}+x\xi)}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)d\xi.

By stationary phase, for any
t\in[1/2,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\left|I(t,x)\right|\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{-\beta d/2},&$\left|x\right|\leqslant 10$;\\
(\lambda^{\beta}\left|x\right|)^{-100},&$\left|x\right|\geqslant 10.$\end{cases}

So, we have
\left\|I(t,x)\right\|_{L^{p}([1/2,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\lambda^{-\beta d/2}
when
p=1,\infty
. By the
L^{2}
isometric of
S_{\beta}(t)
, we have
\left\|I(t,x)\right\|_{L^{2}([1/2,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\lambda^{-\beta d/2}
. Then by the convex inequality of
L^{p}
norm:

\displaystyle\left\|I(t,x)\right\|_{L^{2}([1/2,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|I(t,x)\right\|_{L^{p}([1/2,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\theta}\left\|I(t,x)\right\|_{L^{\infty}([1/2,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{1-\theta},\mbox{ for }p<2,\theta=p/2

\displaystyle\left\|I(t,x)\right\|_{L^{2}([1/2,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|I(t,x)\right\|_{L^{p}([1/2,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{\theta}\left\|I(t,x)\right\|_{L^{1}([1/2,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{1-\theta},\mbox{ for }p>2,\theta=p^{\prime}/2.

Then we have
\left\|I(t,x)\right\|_{L^{p}([1/2,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\geqslant\lambda^{-\beta d/2}.
Take this into (
(b):
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\beta}(t)u\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\gtrsim\lambda^{d(\beta-1)/p-\beta d/2}.

Take this estimate into (
1.3
), we have
s-d+d/q\geqslant d(\beta-1)/p-\beta d/2
, which means that
s\geqslant d(\beta-2)(1/p-1/2)+d(1/p-1/q).

(c):

When
p\leqslant 2
, for any
\left|k\right|\geqslant 10,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, take
u=M_{k}\varphi
, then
\widehat{u}(\xi)=\widehat{\varphi}(\xi-k)
. So, we know
\left\|u\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}\approx\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}.
By change of variables, we have

\displaystyle S_{\beta}(t)u(x)=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{it\left|\xi\right|^{\beta}+ix\xi}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi-k)d\xi

\displaystyle=e^{it\left|k\right|^{\beta}+ikx}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{it(h(\xi+k)-h(k)-\nabla h(k)\xi)}e^{i\xi(x+t\nabla h(k))}\varphi(\xi)d\xi,

where
h(\xi)=\left|\xi\right|^{\beta}.
Denote
h_{k}(\xi)=h(\xi+k)-h(k)-\nabla h(k)\xi
, one can easily get that

(6.2)

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{\gamma}h_{k}(\xi)\right|\lesssim\left|k\right|^{\beta-2}.

Take
L^{p}
norm on both sides above, we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\beta}(t)u\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\left\|H(t,x)\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

where
H(t,x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{ith_{k}(\xi)+ix\xi}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)d\xi
. We can choose
\eta\in\mathscr{S}
such that
\eta(\xi)=1
when
\left|\xi\right|\leqslant 1
, and
\eta(\xi)=0,
when
\left|\xi\right|>2
. So, we have
\eta\widehat{\varphi}=\widehat{\varphi}.
Denote
\eta_{t}(\xi)=e^{-ith_{k}(\xi)}\eta(\xi)
. So, we have
\eta_{t}(\xi)\cdot{\mathscr{F}}H(t,\cdot)(\xi)=\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)
, then

(6.4)

\displaystyle\varphi(x)={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\eta_{t}*H(t,\cdot)(x).

By the method of stationary phase and the estimates (
6.2
), we know that for any
t\in[1/2,1],
we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\eta_{t}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{i\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\beta-2}(th_{k}(\xi)/\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\beta-2}+x\xi)}\eta(\xi)d\xi\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-d(\beta-2)/2}.

By Young’s convolution inequality, for any
t\in[1/2,1]
, we have

\left\|{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\eta_{t}*f\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-d(\beta-2)/2}\left\|f\right\|_{1}.

Obviously,
\left\|{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\eta_{t}*f\right\|_{2}=\left\|f\right\|_{2}
.
Then, by interpolation, for any
p\leqslant 2
, we have

\left\|{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\eta_{t}*f\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\lesssim\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-d(\beta-2)(1/p-1/2)}\left\|f\right\|_{p}.

Take this into (
6.4
), we have

\displaystyle 1\approx\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\lesssim\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-d(\beta-2)(1/p-1/2)}\left\|H(t,\cdot)\right\|_{p}.

Take integration of
t
in
[1/2,1]
, we have

1\lesssim\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-d(\beta-2)(1/p-1/2)}\left\|H(t,x)\right\|_{L^{p}([1/2,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Then by (
6.3
), we have
\left\|S_{\beta}(t)u\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\gtrsim\left\langle k\right\rangle^{d(\beta-2)(1/p-1/2)}
. Then take the estimates of
u
into (
1.3
), we have
s\geqslant d(\beta-2)(1/p-1/2)
, which is the result as desired.

∎

7.
Applications

As applications of our local smoothing estimates in Theorem
1.9
, we could get the local well-posedness of the following fourth order nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
4NLS
) on modulation spaces with lower regularity request of the initial data. Also, we could get the well-posedness beyond
L^{2}
spaces. Finally, by some energy argument, we could get the global well-posedness of (
4NLS
).

(4NLS)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}iu_{t}+u_{xxxx}=-\left|u\right|^{2}u,\ (t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times{\mathbb{R}},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0},\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}.\end{cases}

Our main results are:

Theorem 7.1
.

For any
D\in\left\{M_{p,2}^{s}:10/3\leqslant p\leqslant 6,s>1/2-1/p\right\}
, (
4NLS
) is local well-posedness in
D
. In detail, for any
u_{0}\in D
, there exists
T(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D})>0
, and a unique solution
u
of (
4NLS
) with
u\in L_{t\in I_{T}}^{a(p)}L_{x}^{p}
, where
a(p)=8p/(3p-2),I_{T}=[0,T]
. Also, the date-to-solution map above is Lipschitz continuous.

For any
u_{0}\in D
, denote
I_{T}=[0,T],X=L_{t\in I_{T}}^{a(p)}L_{x}^{p}
, where
0<T\leqslant 1
, depends on
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D}
, will be chosen later. Denote
B_{X}(R)=\left\{u\in X:\left\|u\right\|_{X}\leqslant R\right\}
, the closed ball in
X
, centering at
0
with radius of
R
. Notice that when
10/3\leqslant p\leqslant 6,
we have
a(p)\leqslant p
.

For any decomposition of
u_{0}=u_{0}^{1}+u_{0}^{2}
, with
u_{0}^{1}\in M_{p,2}^{s},u_{0}^{2}\in L^{2}
. By Hölder’s inequality and the local smoothing estimates in Theorem
1.9
, we have

(7.1)

\displaystyle\left\|S_{4}(t)u_{0}^{1}\right\|_{X}\leqslant\left|T\right|^{1/a(p)-1/p}\left\|S_{4}(t)u_{0}^{1}\right\|_{L^{p}(I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\left|T\right|^{(3p-10)/8p}\left\|u_{0}^{1}\right\|_{M_{p,2}^{s}}.

By Strichartz estimates of
S_{4}(t)
(See Theorem 3.1 in
[
7
]
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|S_{4}(t)u_{0}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\gamma(p)}_{t}L_{x}^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|u_{0}^{2}\right\|_{2},

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{A}f\right\|_{L^{\gamma(p)}_{t}L_{x}^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{a(p)/3}_{t}L_{x}^{p/3}},

where
(\gamma(p),p)
is the Strichartz pair, satisfying:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma(p)}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right).

\mathcal{A}f
is defined by
\mathcal{A}f(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}S_{4}(t-\tau)f(\tau)d\tau
, is the nonlinear term. Notice that when
10/3\leqslant p\leqslant 6
, we have
a(p)\leqslant\gamma(p)
. So, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

(7.2)

\displaystyle\left\|S_{4}(t)u_{0}^{2}\right\|_{X}\leqslant\left|T\right|^{1/a(p)-1/\gamma(p)}\left\|S_{4}(t)u_{0}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\gamma(p)}_{t}L_{x}^{p}}\lesssim\left|T\right|^{1/4}\left\|u_{0}^{2}\right\|_{2}.

Combine (
7.1
) and (
7.2
), we know that there exists
M>0
, depends only on
p,s,d
, such that

(7.3)

\displaystyle\left\|S_{4}(t)u_{0}\right\|_{X}\leqslant M\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D}.

Take
f=\left|u\right|^{2}u
into the Strichartz estimate, also by Hölder’s inequality, we have

(7.4)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{A}f\right\|_{X}\leqslant\left|T\right|^{1/4}\left\|\mathcal{A}f\right\|_{L^{\gamma(p)}_{t}L_{x}^{p}}\lesssim\left|T\right|^{1/4}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{a(p)/3}_{t}L_{x}^{p/3}}\leqslant C\left|T\right|^{1/4}\left\|u\right\|_{X}^{3}.

If we choose
T\ll 1
such that
C\left|T\right|^{1/4}(2M\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D})^{2}\leqslant 1/10
. Then for any
u_{0}\in B_{X}(2M\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D})
, by (
7.3
) and (
7.4
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|S_{4}(t)u_{0}\right\|_{X}+\left\|\mathcal{A}\left(\left|u\right|^{2}u\right)\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leqslant M\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D}+C\left|T\right|^{1/4}\left\|u\right\|_{X}^{3}

\displaystyle\leqslant M\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D}+1/10\left\|u\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2M\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D}.

Therefore, if we define
\mathscr{T}:u\rightarrow S_{4}(t)u_{0}+i\mathcal{A}\left(\left|u\right|^{2}u\right)
, we know that

\mathscr{T}:B_{X}(2M\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D})\longrightarrow B_{X}(2M\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D})

is contraction. Then by the contraction mapping principle, there exists
u\in B_{X}(2M\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D})
, such that
u=\mathscr{T}u
, which means that
u\in X
is the solution of (
4NLS
), satisfying
\left\|u\right\|_{X}\leqslant 2M\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D}
. So, the date-to-solution map is Lipschitz continuous.
∎

If the initial
u_{0}
has some more regularity, out solution
u
above is actually global. Precisely, we have

Theorem 7.2
.

For any
E\in\left\{M_{p,2}^{s}+L^{2}:10/3\leqslant p\leqslant 6,s>7/2-2/p\right\}
, if initial data
u_{0}\in E
, then the solution
u
in Theorem
7.1
can be extended to
t\in[0,\infty)
.

Our proof, named the energy argument, is based on the method in
[
25
]
, one can also see
[
30
]
.

For any
u_{0}\in E
, by Theorem
7.1
, we have a unique solution
u\in X=L_{t\in I_{T}}^{a(p)}L_{x}^{p}
, such that
u=S_{4}(t)u_{0}+i\mathcal{A}\left(\left|u\right|^{2}u\right)
. Notice that when
s>7/2-2/p
, by Lemma
2.9
, we have

(7.5)

\displaystyle\left\|S_{4}(t)u_{0}\right\|_{D}\leqslant\left\|S_{4}(t)u_{0}\right\|_{M_{p,2}^{1/2-1/p+}}\lesssim\left\langle T\right\rangle^{1/2}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{M_{p,2}^{1/2-1/p+2(1/2-1/p)+}}\lesssim\left\langle T\right\rangle^{1/2}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{M_{p,2}^{s}}.

As for the nonlinear term
\mathcal{A}\left(\left|u\right|^{2}u\right)
, by Strichartz estimates, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{A}\left(\left|u\right|^{2}u\right)\right\|_{L_{t\in I_{T}}^{\infty}(D)}\leqslant\left\|\mathcal{A}\left(\left|u\right|^{2}u\right)\right\|_{L_{t\in I_{T}}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{X}^{3}.

So, we know that
\left\|u(T)\right\|_{D}<\infty
.
By the semi-group theory, we can also solve the equation (
4NLS
) at
t=T
. In this way, we have a maximal time
T^{*}>0
, where the solution
u
exists in
[0,T]
for any
T<T^{*}
. By the blow-up criteria, if
T^{*}<\infty
, it must be

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{D}=\infty.

Denote
w(t)=S_{4}(t)u_{0},v(t)=u(t)-w(t)
, by (
7.5
), we know that

\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\left\|W(t)\right\|_{D}\leqslant C(T^{*})<\infty.

Therefore, it must be

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\left\|v(t)\right\|_{D}=\infty.

Notice that
L^{2}\hookrightarrow D
, so we have

(7.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\left\|v(t)\right\|_{2}=\infty.

We only need to prove that

\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<T^{*}}\left\|v(t)\right\|_{2}\leqslant C(T^{*})<\infty.

Denote
\left\langle v,u\right\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}v\overline{u}dx
, the inner product in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. The mass and energy of
v
are defined below:

\displaystyle M_{v}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle v,v\right\rangle,

\displaystyle E_{v}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle v_{xx},v_{xx}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\left\langle v^{2},v^{2}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{v}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle v_{xx},v_{xx}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\left\langle(v+w)^{2},(v+w)^{2}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{4}\left\langle w^{2},w^{2}\right\rangle.

Note that a priori, it is not clear that the mass and energy of
v
is finite for
t\neq 0
. The following computations are carried out for initial data from a priori class, for
u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}
. This ensures all quantities to be finite and allows justifying integration by parts arguments. Since we prove bounds depending only on
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{M_{p,2}^{s}}
for
p<\infty
, the arguments are a posterior justified by density of
\mathscr{S}\subseteq D
and well-posedness.

By definition of
v=u-w
, we know that
v
satisfies the equation below:

(4NLSw)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}iv_{t}+v_{xxxx}=-\left|v+w\right|^{2}(v+w),\ (t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}\\
v(0,x)=0,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}\end{cases}

Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{v}(t)={\mathrm{Re}}\left\langle v_{t},v\right\rangle

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Re}}\left\langle iv_{xxxx}+i\left|v+w\right|^{2}(v+x),v\right\rangle

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Re}}\left\langle iv_{xx},v_{xx}\right\rangle+{\mathrm{Re}}\left\langle i\left|v+w\right|^{2}v,v\right\rangle+{\mathrm{Re}}\left\langle i\left|v+w\right|^{2}w,v\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-{\mathrm{Im}}\left\langle\left|v+w\right|^{2}w,v\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-{\mathrm{Im}}\left(\left\langle v\overline{v}w,v\right\rangle+\left\langle vw\overline{w},v\right\rangle+\left\langle\overline{v}ww,v\right\rangle+\left\langle w\overline{w}w,v\right\rangle\right).

So, we have

(7.7)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dt}M_{v}(t)\right|\lesssim\left\|v^{3}w\right\|_{1}+\left\|v^{2}w^{2}\right\|_{1}+\left\|vw^{3}\right\|_{1}.

Notice that by Lemma
2.7
, we know that for any
p\leqslant 6\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
, we have
M_{p,2}^{1/2+}\hookrightarrow M_{q,2}^{1/2+}\hookrightarrow L^{q}
, so we have

(7.8)

\displaystyle\left\|w(t)\right\|_{q}=\left\|S_{4}(t)u_{0}\right\|_{q}\lesssim\left\|S_{4}(t)u_{0}\right\|_{M_{p,2}^{1/2+}}\lesssim\left\langle T^{*}\right\rangle^{1/2}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{M_{p,2}^{1/2+2(1/2-1/p)+}}\lesssim_{T^{*}}1.

So, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|v^{3}w\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|w\right\|_{\infty}\left\|v\right\|_{2}\left\|v\right\|_{4}^{2}\lesssim_{T^{*}}\left\|v\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|v\right\|_{4}^{4};

\displaystyle\left\|v^{2}w^{2}\right\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|w\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim_{T^{*}}\left\|v\right\|_{2}^{2};

\displaystyle\left\|vw^{3}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|w\right\|_{6}^{3}\left\|v\right\|_{2}\lesssim_{T^{*}}\left\|v\right\|_{2}\lesssim_{T^{*}}\left\|v\right\|_{2}^{2}+1.

Take these estimates into (
7.7
), we have

(7.9)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dt}M_{v}(t)\right|\lesssim_{T^{*}}E_{v}(t)+M_{v}(t)+1.

By the definition of
\widetilde{E}_{v}(t)
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{v}(t)-E_{v}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\left\langle(v+w)^{2},(v+w)^{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle w^{2},w^{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle v^{2},v^{2}\right\rangle\right)

Proposition A.1
.

For
\alpha<0
, there exists a
\alpha
-covering
\left\{B_{k}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with a
\alpha
-BAPU
\left\{\eta_{k}^{\alpha}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
.

Our proof is based on Subsection 2.1 in
[
8
]
. Denote
\beta=\alpha/(1-\alpha)\in(-1,0)
. We could define a homeomorphism
\delta_{\beta}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by
\delta_{\beta}(x)=\left|x\right|^{\beta}x
. It is easy to check that
\delta_{\beta}^{-1}=\delta_{\beta^{\prime}}
, where
\beta^{\prime}=-\beta/(1+\beta)
. Also, we know that
\delta_{\beta}\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\})
.

For any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\left|k\right|\geqslant 2\sqrt{d}
, for any

, we know that
\left|x\right|\approx\left|k\right|\gtrsim 1
. Then by the mean value inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|\delta_{\beta}(x)-\delta_{\beta}(k)\right|\leqslant C_{\beta}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}\left|tx+(1-t)k\right|^{\beta}\left|x-k\right|\leqslant C_{\beta}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\beta}.

So, we know that
\delta_{\beta}\left(B(k,\sqrt{d})\right)\subseteq B(\delta_{\beta}(k),C_{\beta}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\beta}),\forall\left|k\right|\geqslant 2\sqrt{d}
.

When
\left|k\right|\leqslant 2\sqrt{d}
, it is obvious that
\delta_{\beta}\left(B(k,\sqrt{d})\right)
is bounded. So, we could choose
C_{\beta}^{\prime}>0
, such that
\delta_{\beta}\left(B(k,\sqrt{d})\right)\subseteq B(\delta_{\beta}(k),C_{\beta}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\beta}),\forall\left|k\right|\leqslant 2\sqrt{d}
.

Combine all the cases, we know that there exists
r>0
, such that

\displaystyle\delta_{\beta}\left(B(k,\sqrt{d})\right)\subseteq B(\delta_{\beta}(k),r\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\beta}),\forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

Denote
B_{k}=B(\delta_{\beta}(k),r\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\beta})
, with the condition above, we know that
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}=\bigcup_{k}B_{k}
because that
\left\{B(k,\sqrt{d})\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
is a covering of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\delta_{\beta}
is a homeomorphism.

Similarly, for the
r>0
above, we have
R>0
, such that

\displaystyle\delta_{\beta^{\prime}}\left(B(\delta_{\beta}(k),r\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\beta})\right)\subseteq B(k,R),\forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle B(\delta_{\beta}(k),r\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\beta})\subseteq\delta_{\beta}\left(B(k,R)\right),\forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

Then by the bounded overlap of
\left\{B(k,R)\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
, we know that
\left\{B_{k}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
is also bounded overlapped.

One can easily check that condition (iii) in Definition
2.2
holds for
B_{k}
given above. So, we construction a
\alpha
-covering of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, named
\left\{B_{k}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
. The corresponding
\alpha
-BAPU of this
\alpha
-covering can be given as follows.

For any
\left|k\right|\neq 0
, we can choose
r_{0}>0
, such that
r\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\beta}\leqslant r_{0}\left|k\right|^{\beta}
. Take
\rho\in\mathscr{S}
with
\rho(\xi)=1
, when
\left|\xi\right|\leqslant r_{0}
,
\rho(\xi)=1
when
\left|\xi\right|\geqslant 2r_{0}
. Denote
\rho_{0}=\rho,\rho_{k}(\xi)=\rho(\left|k\right|^{-\beta}\xi-k)
for any
\left|k\right|\neq 0
. So, we know that
\rho_{k}(\xi)=1
when
\xi\in B_{k}
and
{\mbox{supp}\ }\rho_{k}\subseteq B(\delta_{\beta}(k),2r_{0}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\beta}):=B_{k}^{\prime}
. Notice that
\left\{B_{k}^{\prime}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
is bounded overlapped and
\left\{B_{k}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. We have

\displaystyle 1\leqslant\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\rho_{k}(\xi)\leqslant C(d).

Denote

\displaystyle\psi_{k}=\frac{\rho_{k}}{\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\rho_{k}},

one can check that

is a

-BAPU.
∎

Proposition A.2
.

Let
\alpha<0,s\in{\mathbb{R}},0<p,q\leqslant\infty
. Then
M_{p,q}^{s}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}^{0,\alpha}
if and only if
s\geqslant-\alpha\tau(p,q)
.

The proof is based on Section 4 in
[
10
]
.

Sufficiency:

(1):

When
(p,q)=(2,2),\tau(p,q)=0
, we have
L^{2}=M_{2,2}=M_{2,2}^{0,\alpha}
.

(2):

For any
k,\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, denote

\displaystyle\wedge_{k}

\displaystyle=\left\{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}:\Box_{\ell}\Box_{k}^{\alpha}\neq 0\right\},

\displaystyle\vee_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}:\Box_{\ell}\Box_{k}^{\alpha}\neq 0\right\}.

By the orthogonality of
\left\{\Box_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
and
\left\{\Box_{k}^{\alpha}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
, we know that

\displaystyle\#\wedge_{k}\lesssim_{d}1,\ \ \#\vee_{\ell}\lesssim_{d}\left\langle\ell\right\rangle^{-\alpha d}.

When
p=1,
or
\infty
, we know
\tau(p,q)=d/q
. By the convolution Young’s inequality and the orthogonality of these decompositions, we have

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{0,\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\|\|\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\|_{p}\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}}=\left\|\|\|\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\|_{p}\|_{\ell_{k\in\vee_{\ell}}^{q}}\right\|_{\ell_{\ell}^{q}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\|\|\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in\wedge_{k}}\Box_{\ell^{\prime}}\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\|_{p}\|_{\ell_{k\in\vee_{\ell}}^{q}}\right\|_{\ell_{\ell}^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\|\sum_{\left|\ell-\ell^{\prime}\right|\lesssim 1}\left\|\Box_{\ell^{\prime}}u\right\|_{p}\|_{\ell_{k\in\vee_{\ell}}^{q}}\right\|_{\ell_{\ell}^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left\|\left\langle\ell\right\rangle^{\alpha d/q}\Box_{\ell}u\right\|_{p}\right\|_{\ell_{\ell}^{q}}=\left\|u\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{-\alpha d/q}}.

When
0<p<1
, we know
\tau(p,q)=d(1/p+1/q-1)
. By using the convolution inequality below instead of the convolution Young’s inequality, we could get the embedding as well.

\displaystyle\left\|\Box_{\ell}\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\right\|_{p}\lesssim\left\|{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\eta_{k}^{\alpha}\right\|_{p}\left\|\Box_{\ell}u\right\|_{p}\lesssim\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\alpha d(1-1/p)/(1-\alpha)}\left\|\Box_{\ell}u\right\|_{p}.

(3):

When
p=2,q<2
, we know
\tau(2,q)=d(1/q-1/2)
. By the Plancherel formula and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{M_{2,q}^{0,\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\|\|\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\|_{2}\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}}=\left\|\left\|\|\|\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\|_{2}\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}}\right\|_{\ell_{\ell}^{q}}\right\|_{\ell_{\ell}^{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\|\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\|_{2}\|_{\ell_{k\in\vee_{\ell}}^{q}}=\left\|\|\|\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\|_{2}\|_{\ell_{k\in\vee_{\ell}}^{2}}(\#\vee_{\ell})^{1/q-1/2}\right\|_{\ell_{\ell}^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left\|\Box_{\ell}u\right\|_{2}\left\langle\ell\right\rangle^{-\alpha d(1/q-1/2)}\right\|_{\ell_{\ell}^{q}}=\left\|u\right\|_{M_{2,q}^{-\alpha\tau(2,q)}}.

(4):

Take interpolation of (1,2,3), we could get the result for the rest cases of
(p,q)
as desired.

, then we have

(A.1)

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{0,\alpha}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}.

(a):

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{M_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left\|\Box_{\ell}u\right\|_{p}\right\|_{\ell_{\ell}^{\infty}}=\left\|\|\sum_{k\in\vee_{\ell}}\Box_{k}^{\alpha}\Box_{\ell}u\|_{p}\right\|_{\ell_{\ell}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|\left\|\|\Box_{k}^{\alpha}\Box_{\ell}u\|_{p}\right\|_{\ell_{k\in\vee_{\ell}}^{p}}\right\|_{\ell_{\ell}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|\sup_{k\in\vee_{\ell}}\left\|\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\right\|_{p}\left(\#\vee_{\ell}\right)^{1/p}\right\|_{\ell_{\ell}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|\left\|\Box_{k}^{\alpha}u\right\|_{p}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-\alpha d/(p(1-\alpha)}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{\infty}}=\left\|u\right\|_{M_{p,\infty}^{-\alpha d/p,\alpha}}.

∎

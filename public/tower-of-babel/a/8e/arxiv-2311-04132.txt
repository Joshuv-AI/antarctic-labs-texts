Homotopy groups of symmetric products
Eduardo Blanco-Gómez

Abstract
. In this paper we prove that, taking X X a Hausdorff topological space, the homotopy groups of the spaces S ​ P m ​ ( X ) SP_{m}(X) and F m ​ ( X ) F_{m}(X) , both called symmetric products, are monomorphic. We also introduce a new algebraic tool in topology: the discontinuous homotopy groups.

In this section we are going to deal with the topological spaces called
symmetric products

Let

Let

is a Hausdorff topological
space, then so it is

On the other hand, we are going to call the space

,

-

Fundamental group of symmetric products

2.1.
Discontinuous paths in cartesian products

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff topological space and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By a
general discontinuous path in

\mathbf{X^{m}}
we will
denote every function
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow X^{m}
or
\gamma:\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow X^{m}
(this last
kind of paths can be seen as paths defined in
[0,1]
with
\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)
) with a
finite or countable number of discontinuities (including the case with zero discontinuities). We will write
\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
for the set of discontinuities
of the path
\gamma
and the notation,

\text{Im}(\mathfrak{D}(\gamma))=\{\gamma(t):\ t\in\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)\}

for the image of
\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
. For a general discontinuous path
\gamma
in
X^{m}
we define the next
property: take
t_{0}\in\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)\cup\{0,1\}
; we will say that
\gamma
has the
controlled jump property in

\mathbf{t_{0}}
if for every open interval
(t_{1},t_{0})
, with
t_{1}\in[0,t_{0})
, exists some point
p\in\text{Im}(\mathfrak{D}(\gamma))
and for every open interval
(t_{0},t_{2})
, with
t_{2}\in(t_{0},1]
, exists some point
p^{\prime}\in\text{Im}(\mathfrak{D}(\gamma))
, such that,

(t_{1},t_{0})\cap\gamma^{-1}(V_{p})\neq\emptyset\quad\text{and}\quad(t_{0},t_{2})\cap\gamma^{-1}(V_{p^{\prime}})\neq\emptyset

for every
V_{p}\subset X^{m}
an open neighborhood of
p
and for every
V_{p^{\prime}}\subset X^{m}

an open neighborhood of
p^{\prime}
(the definition is valid for
t_{0}=0
using only intervals of the form
(t_{0},t_{2})
and for
t_{0}=1
using only intervals of the form
(t_{1},t_{0})
).
We will say that
\gamma
is a
path in

\mathbf{X^{m}}
or a
discontinuous path in

\mathbf{X^{m}}
if it is a general discontinuous path and it has the controlled jump property
for every
t\in\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)\cup\{0,1\}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff topological space,
p\in X^{m}
and
\gamma
a general discontinuous
path in
X^{m}
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that
\text{Im}(\mathfrak{D}(\gamma))\subset[p]_{\varphi}
.
Then
\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
is nowhere dense in
[0,1]
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
is dense into an interval
I\subset[0,1]
. Let’s obtain a contradiction. We are going to prove first,

(7)

t\in I\subset[0,1]\Rightarrow\gamma(t)\in[p]_{\varphi}

Let
t\in I
such that
\gamma(t)\notin[p]_{\varphi}
. As
X
is a Hausdorff topological space then so it is

X^{m}
; like
[p]_{\varphi}
is a finite set of
X^{m}
then it exists
V_{\gamma(t)}\subset X^{m}
an
open neighborhood of
\gamma(t)
such that
V_{\gamma(t)}\cap[p]_{\varphi}=\emptyset
. Thus, as
\gamma(t)\notin[p]_{\varphi}
for this
t\in I
, then
t\notin\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
because, by hypotheses,

\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)\subset[p]_{\varphi}
. So the path
\gamma
is continuous on
t
. Therefore
\gamma^{-1}(V_{\gamma(t)})
is an open subset of
[0,1]
containing
t
. Furthermore, as
V_{\gamma(t)}\cap[p]_{\varphi}=\emptyset
, the open set
\gamma^{-1}(V_{\gamma(t)})
does not
contain any
t^{\prime}
for which
\gamma(t^{\prime})\in[p]_{\varphi}
. But this is a contradiction because

\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
is dense in
I
, thus the hypothesis
\gamma(t)\notin[p]_{\varphi}
for a
t\in I

is false and we have stated (
7
).

Take now
t_{1}\in I\setminus\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
, with
\gamma(t_{1})=p_{1}\in[p]_{\varphi}=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\}
, and
V_{1}\subset X^{m}
an open neighborhood of
p_{1}
such that

V_{1}\cap([p]_{\varphi}\setminus\{p_{1}\})=\emptyset
(this can be done because
X
is a Hausdorff topological
space, so it is
X^{m}
, and
[p]_{\varphi}
is a finite set). By construction, for every

t\in\gamma^{-1}(V_{1})\cap I
if
\gamma(t)\in[p]_{\varphi}
then
\gamma(t)=p_{1}
. Particularly,
as
\gamma^{-1}(V_{1})\cap I\cap\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
because
\gamma^{-1}(V_{1})
is open
and
\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
is dense in
I
, for every
t\in\gamma^{-1}(V_{1})\cap I\cap\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
one has
\gamma(t)=p_{1}
. Now, by (
7
) we would have
\gamma(t)=p_{1}
for all

t\in\gamma^{-1}(V_{1})
and this is a contradiction because in that case
\gamma
would be continuous in

\gamma^{-1}(V_{1})
and we had proven before that
\gamma^{-1}(V_{1})\cap I\cap\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
.
So, we conclude that
\gamma
is discontinuous in
t_{1}
and, as this process can be done with every

t\in I\setminus\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
then
\gamma
is discontinuous in the open interval
I
. We deduce that
I\subset\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
but, as
\gamma
is a general discontinuous path,
\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
is finite or
countable and we have finished with a contradiction. We conclude the statement of the lemma.
∎

Definition 2.29
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff topological space,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
p\in X^{m}
. We will say that a map

\Delta_{H}:[0,1]\times[0,1]\rightarrow X^{m}
is a
discontinuous homotopy
if,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta_{H}(s,0)\in[p]_{\varphi}\\
\Delta_{H}(s,1)\in[p]_{\varphi}\\
\end{cases}\qquad\forall s\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\Delta_{H}(s,t)=\gamma_{s}(t)\text{ is a path such that }\text{Im}(\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s}))\subset[p]_{\varphi}\ \forall s\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s})\text{ is finite (maybe the empty set) or countable.}

Two paths
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
are
discontinuously homotopic
if it exists a discontinuous
homotopy
\Delta_{H}(s,t)
such that
\gamma_{0}(t)=\Delta_{H}(0,t)
and
\gamma_{1}(t)=\Delta_{H}(1,t)
.

Proposition 2.30
.

The relation of being discontinuously homotopic between paths (defined in
2.29
) is an equivalence relation.

Proof.

Let’s see the properties of an equivalence relation.

Reflexivity:
it is obvious that a path
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow X^{m}
is related with
itself by the dicontinuous homotopy,

\begin{array}[]{rcclc}\Delta_{H}:&[0,1]\times[0,1]&\rightarrow&X^{m}&\\
&(s,t)&\mapsto&\Delta_{H}(s,t)=\gamma(t)&\quad\forall s\in[0,1].\\
\end{array}

Symmetry:
Let
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
be two discontinuously homotopic paths, i.e.,
it exists a discontinuous homotopy
\Delta_{H}(s,t)
such that
\gamma_{0}(t)=\Delta_{H}(0,t)
and
\gamma_{1}(t)=\Delta_{H}(1,t)
. Then the map
\Delta_{H}^{-1}(s,t)=\Delta_{H}(1-s,t)
is a discontinuous
homotopy such that
\gamma_{1}(t)=\Delta_{H}^{-1}(0,t)
and
\gamma_{0}(t)=\Delta_{H}^{-1}(1,t)
, and
holding the other two properties of definition
2.29
.

Transitivity:
Let
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
be two discontinuously homotopic paths related
by the discontinuous homotopy
\Delta_{H}(s,t)
, and
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
be two discontinuously homotopic paths related by the discontinuous homotopy
\Delta^{\prime}_{H}(s,t)
. Then, it is easy to see that the map,

\widetilde{\Delta}_{H}(s,t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{H}(s,2t)&\quad&\text{if }t\in[0,1/2]\\
&\Delta^{\prime}_{H}(s,2t-1)&\quad&\text{if }t\in(1/2,1],\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

is a discontinuous homotopy that relates the path
\gamma_{0}
with the path
\gamma_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 2.34
.

Let

and
p\in X^{m}

\Delta_{H}

Proof.

Let

the discontinuous homotopy,

&(s,t)&\mapsto&\Delta_{H}(s,t)=\gamma_{s}(t),\\
\end{array}

,
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
. Define the map,

\end{array}

To prove that

is a continuous homotopy in
SP_{m}(X)
between the continuous paths
in
SP_{m}(X)
,
\widetilde{\gamma}_{0}=\varphi\circ\gamma_{0}
and
\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}=\varphi\circ\gamma_{1}

of discontinuous homotopy. Suppose

is discontinuous
in

. Then it exists an open set

is

, we deduce that

is discontinuous in
t_{0}
. Like for every

is not open being

an open neighborhood of
\widetilde{\gamma}_{s}(t)
, then

is discontinuous in all

, and using the same

\gamma_{s}
is discontinuous in all

\widetilde{\gamma}_{s}^{-1}(W)\subset\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s}).

Remembering that

is a discontinuous homotopy we have that,

Like

then

, so for every

we have that

But then,

and looking at (

\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s})=\gamma_{s}^{-1}(\varphi^{-1}(W)).

As

\end{cases}

\displaystyle(t_{0},t_{2})\cap\gamma_{s}^{-1}(V_{2})\neq\emptyset\quad\begin{cases}\forall t_{2}\in(t_{0},1]\\
\forall V_{2}\subset X^{m}\text{ open neighborhood of }p_{2}.\\
\end{cases}

Taking

,

and using (

\displaystyle(t_{1},t_{0})\cap\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s})\neq\emptyset\qquad\forall t_{1}\in[0,t_{0})

\displaystyle(t_{0},t_{2})\cap\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s})\neq\emptyset\qquad\forall t_{2}\in(t_{0},1].

is continuous in

is continuous in

2.3.

All the tools developed in the previous sections will lead us to the result announced: the fundamental
group of symmetric products are monomorphic. Thank to the next theorem, we are going to be able to

Theorem 2.37
.

X
be a Hausdorff topological space,

(33)

\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{1}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})/\text{Ker}(h_{\phi})\approx\pi_{1}(\widetilde{F}_{m}(X),[p]_{\phi}),

(34)

\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{1}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})/\text{Ker}(h_{\varphi})\hookrightarrow\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{1}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})/\text{Ker}(h_{\phi}),

with

a homomorphism between

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff topological space,
p\in X^{m}
and
\gamma
a general discontinuous
n-region in

with

. Suppose that
\text{Im}(\mathfrak{D}(\gamma))\subset[p]_{\varphi}
.
Then

is nowhere dense in

Proof.

Suppose
\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
is dense into an open subset

. Let’s obtain a contradiction. We are going to prove first,

(41)

t\in U\subset[0,1]^{n}\rightarrow\gamma(t)\in[p]_{\varphi}

Let
t\in U
such that
\gamma(t)\notin[p]_{\varphi}
. As

is a Hausdorff topological space then so it is

X^{m}
; like
[p]_{\varphi}
is a finite set of
X^{m}
then it exists
V_{\gamma(t)}\subset X^{m}
an
open neighborhood of
\gamma(t)
such that
V_{\gamma(t)}\cap[p]_{\varphi}=\emptyset
. Thus, as
\gamma(t)\notin[p]_{\varphi}
for this

, then
t\notin\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
because, by hypotheses,

\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)\subset[p]_{\varphi}
. So the n-region
\gamma
is continuous on

. Therefore
\gamma^{-1}(V_{\gamma(t)})
is an open subset of
[0,1]^{n}
containing
t
. Furthermore, as
V_{\gamma(t)}\cap[p]_{\varphi}=\emptyset
, the open set
\gamma^{-1}(V_{\gamma(t)})
does not
contain any
t^{\prime}
for which
\gamma(t^{\prime})\in[p]_{\varphi}
. But this is a contradiction because

\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
is dense in
U
, thus the hypothesis
\gamma(t)\notin[p]_{\varphi}
for a
t\in U

is false and we have stated (

).

Take now

, with
\gamma(t_{1})=p_{1}\in[p]_{\varphi}=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\}
, and
V_{1}\subset X^{m}
an open neighborhood of

such that

V_{1}\cap([p]_{\varphi}\setminus\{p_{1}\})=\emptyset
(this can be done because

is a Hausdorff topological
space, so it is
X^{m}
, and
[p]_{\varphi}
is a finite set). By construction, for every

if
\gamma(t)\in[p]_{\varphi}
then
\gamma(t)=p_{1}
. Particularly,
as
\gamma^{-1}(V_{1})\cap U\cap\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
because
\gamma^{-1}(V_{1})
is open
and
\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
is dense in

. Now, by (

\gamma(t)=p_{1}
for all

and this is a contradiction because in that case

would be continuous in

\gamma^{-1}(V_{1})
and we had proven before that
\gamma^{-1}(V_{1})\cap U\cap\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
.
So, we conclude that
\gamma
is discontinuous in

and, as this process can be done with every

is discontinuous in the open set

but, as

is a general discontinuous n-region,
\mathfrak{D}(\gamma)
is finite or
countable and we have finished with a contradiction. We conclude the statement of the lemma.

and
S_{spheric}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})
. This is the reason for which
we are going to define the
completed
\mathbf{m}
-discontinuous n-region sets
as,

(53)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})=\{&\gamma_{1}\ast\dots\ast\gamma_{k}:\ \gamma_{j}\in S_{spheric}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})\\
&\forall j=1,\dots,k\text{ and }k\in\mathbb{N}\}.\\
\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})=\{&\gamma_{1}\ast\dots\ast\gamma_{k}:\ \gamma_{j}\in S_{spheric}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})\\
&\forall j=1,\dots,k\text{ and }k\in\mathbb{N}\}.\\
\end{split}

We have to be careful because the operation
\ast
is not associative. With the expression
\gamma_{1}\ast\dots\ast\gamma_{k}
we understand that they are included into
\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
and into
\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})
all
the possible configurations depending on the set up of the brackets, i.e., for the case
k=4
we are including all
the following possibilities,

\displaystyle((\gamma_{1}\ast\gamma_{2})\ast\gamma_{3})\ast\gamma_{4}

\displaystyle(\gamma_{1}\ast(\gamma_{2}\ast\gamma_{3}))\ast\gamma_{4}

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}\ast((\gamma_{2}\ast\gamma_{3})\ast\gamma_{4})

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}\ast(\gamma_{2}\ast(\gamma_{3}\ast\gamma_{4})).

By the last definition (
53
) and the equality (
52
) the operation
\ast
is closed in
\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})

and in
\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})
.

Remark 3.15
.

We are going to see in this remark that,

S_{spheric}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})\subseteq\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})\subseteq Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi}).

If the equalities hold then no couple of points in
[p]_{\varphi}
is
arcwise connected (i.e., by a continuous path). For the n-region sets defined with
\phi
, this remark is also true. By
relations (
45
) and remark

3.14
,

S_{spheric}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})\subseteq\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})\subseteq Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi}).

For the proof, firstly we take a look to remark
3.11
. Take

\gamma\in SCR(Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi}))\setminus SLR(Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi}))

This kind of n-region exists for every
X
a Hausdorff topological space such that some couple
p_{1},p_{2}\in[p]_{\varphi}
is
arcwise connected. Then
\gamma\notin\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
because

\gamma\notin S_{spheric}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
and every
\gamma\in

\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})\setminus S_{spheric}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})

take some value
t\in(0,1)^{n}
such that
\gamma(t)\in[p]_{\varphi}
and this is not possible
by definition
3.10
because
\gamma\in SCR(Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi}))
. Thus,

\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})\neq Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi}).

Moreover taking
\mathfrak{i}_{1},\mathfrak{i}_{2}\in IR(Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi}))\subset S_{spheric}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
then by remark
3.14
and by definition (
42
)
\mathfrak{i}_{1}\ast\mathfrak{i}_{2}\in\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})\setminus S_{spheric}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
, so,

S_{spheric}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})\neq\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi}).

Remark 3.16
.

Particularly, by the last remark
3.15
,
for every disconnected Hausdorff topological space
X^{m}
where the points
p_{1},\dots,p_{m}
, with

\{p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\}=[p]_{\varphi}
, lie in different connected components we have,

S_{spheric}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})=\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})=Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi}).

3.2.
Discontinuous homotopies

Our aim now is to build disguised-groups of dicontinuous n-regions using the
m
-cubic discontinuous n-region
sets defined in (
42
) and the completed

m
-discontinuous spheric n-region sets defined in remark
3.15
.
To make that, we are going to define a new concept in topology:
discontinuous homotopies
.
We will make this in an analogue way as done with the traditional homotopies and we will see later that,
taking the quotient by discontinuous homotopies, the
m
-cubic discontinuous n-region
sets and the completed
m
-discontinuous spheric n-region sets turn into disguised-groups and, with a bit
of extra work, into groups (to remember the concept
of homotopy one can read references
[
17
]
,
[
27
]
and
[
35
]
).

Definition 3.17
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff topological space,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
p\in X^{m}
. We will say that a map

\Delta_{H}:[0,1]\times[0,1]^{n}\rightarrow X^{m}
is a
discontinuous homotopy
if,

\displaystyle\Delta_{H}(s,t)\in[p]_{\varphi}\qquad\forall(s,t)\in[0,1]\times\partial[0,1]^{n}

\displaystyle\Delta_{H}(s,t)=\gamma_{s}(t)\text{ is a n-region such that }\text{Im}(\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s}))\subset[p]_{\varphi}\ \forall s\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s})\text{ is finite (maybe the empty set) or countable.}

Two n-regions
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
are
discontinuously homotopic
if it exists a discontinuous
homotopy
\Delta_{H}(s,t)
such that
\gamma_{0}(t)=\Delta_{H}(0,t)
and
\gamma_{1}(t)=\Delta_{H}(1,t)
.

Proposition 3.18
.

The relation of being discontinuously homotopic between n-regions (defined in
3.17
) is an equivalence relation.

Proof.

Let’s see the properties of an equivalence relation.

Reflexivity:
it is obvious that a n-region
\gamma:[0,1]^{n}\rightarrow X^{m}
is related with
itself by the dicontinuous homotopy,

\begin{array}[]{rcclc}\Delta_{H}:&[0,1]\times[0,1]^{n}&\rightarrow&X^{m}&\\
&(s,t)&\mapsto&\Delta_{H}(s,t)=\gamma(t)&\quad\forall s\in[0,1].\\
\end{array}

Symmetry:
Let
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
be two discontinuously homotopic n-regions, i.e.,
it exists a discontinuous homotopy
\Delta_{H}(s,t)
such that
\gamma_{0}(t)=\Delta_{H}(0,t)
and
\gamma_{1}(t)=\Delta_{H}(1,t)
. Then the map
\Delta_{H}^{-1}(s,t)=\Delta_{H}(1-s,t)
is a discontinuous
homotopy such that
\gamma_{1}(t)=\Delta_{H}^{-1}(0,t)
and
\gamma_{0}(t)=\Delta_{H}^{-1}(1,t)
, and
holding the other two properties of definition
3.17
.

Transitivity:
Let
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
be two discontinuously homotopic n-regions related
by the discontinuous homotopy
\Delta_{H}(s,t)
, and
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
be two discontinuously homotopic n-regions related by the discontinuous homotopy
\Delta^{\prime}_{H}(s,t)
. Then, it is easy to see that the map,

\widetilde{\Delta}_{H}(s,t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{H}(s,2t)&\quad&\text{if }t\in[0,1/2]\\
&\Delta^{\prime}_{H}(s,2t-1)&\quad&\text{if }t\in(1/2,1],\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

is a discontinuous homotopy that relates the n-region
\gamma_{0}
with the n-region
\gamma_{2}
.
∎

Theorem 3.19
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff topological space,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
p\in X^{m}
. Let the
m
-discontinuous
n-region sets defined in (
42
) and
\Delta_{H}
be the relation
of being discontinuously homotopic defined in
3.17
. Then the quotient sets,

\displaystyle\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})=Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})/\Delta_{H}

\displaystyle\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})=Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})/\Delta_{H}

\displaystyle\Delta_{spheric}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})=\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})/\Delta_{H}

\displaystyle\Delta_{spheric}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})=\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})/\Delta_{H},

with the operation,

[\gamma_{1}]\circledast[\gamma_{2}]=[\gamma_{1}\ast\gamma_{2}]

are groups.

Proof.

Let’s prove first they are disguised-groups. We are going to make the proof for the quotient set
\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
because
for the other three sets is analogue.

Firstly, let’s see that the operation
\circledast
is well defined. Take
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}

related by the discontinuous homotopy
\Delta_{H}(s,t)=\gamma_{s}(t)
and
\gamma_{0}^{{}^{\prime}}
and
\gamma_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}

related by the discontinuous homotopy
\Delta^{\prime}_{H}(s,t)=\gamma_{s}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)
. Then the map,

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\overset{\ast}{\Delta}_{H}:&[0,1]\times[0,1]^{n}&\rightarrow&X^{m}\\
&(s,t)&\mapsto&\overset{\ast}{\Delta}_{H}(s,t)=\gamma_{s}(t)\ast\gamma_{s}^{{}^{\prime}}(t),\\
\end{array}

is a discontinuous homotopy that relates the n-regions
\gamma_{0}\ast\gamma_{0}^{{}^{\prime}}
and
\gamma_{1}\ast\gamma_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}
,
that is
\overset{\ast}{\Delta}_{H}=\Delta_{H}\ast\Delta_{H}^{{}^{\prime}}
.

We have proved in remark
3.14

that the operation
\ast
is closed into
Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
, thus, so it is the operation

\circledast
into the set
\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
. To prove the other three conditions
of definition 2.2 from
[
2
]
of disguised-groups, let’s begin with the concept
of
reparame-

trization
. We will say that the map
\theta:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
is a
reparame-

trization

if it is continuous, suprayective,
\theta(0)=0
and
\theta(1)=1
. If we take a n-region
\gamma:[0,1]^{n}\rightarrow X^{m}
and a reparametrization
\theta
, the map
\gamma_{\theta}(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n})=\gamma(\theta(t_{1}),t_{2},\dots,t_{n})
is a n-region, discontinuously homotopic with

\gamma
. To prove this it is just necessary to take the discontinuous homotopy,

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\Delta_{H}:&[0,1]\times[0,1]^{n}&\rightarrow&X^{m}\\
&(s,t)&\mapsto&\Delta_{H}(s,t)=(1-s)\gamma_{\theta}(t)+s\gamma(t).\\
\end{array}

Let’s prove now the associative property. Take
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}\in Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
. Then,

\displaystyle(\gamma_{1}\ast(\gamma_{2}\ast\gamma_{3}))(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\gamma_{1}(2t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n})&\quad&t\in[0,1/2]\times[0,1]^{n-1}\\
&\gamma_{2}(4t_{1}-2,t_{2},\dots,t_{n})&\quad&t\in(1/2,3/4]\times[0,1]^{n-1}\\
&\gamma_{3}(4t_{1}-3,t_{2},\dots,t_{n})&\quad&t\in(3/4,1]\times[0,1]^{n-1}\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

\displaystyle((\gamma_{1}\ast\gamma_{2})\ast\gamma_{3})(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\gamma_{1}(4t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n})&\quad&t\in[0,1/4]\times[0,1]^{n-1}\\
&\gamma_{2}(4t_{1}-1,t_{2},\dots,t_{n})&\quad&t\in(1/4,1/2]\times[0,1]^{n-1}\\
&\gamma_{3}(2t_{1}-1,t_{2},\dots,t_{n})&\quad&t\in(1/2,1]\times[0,1]^{n-1},\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

taking
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
. Defining the reparametrization,

\theta(t_{1})=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &t_{1}/2&\quad&t_{1}\in[0,1/2]\\
&t_{1}-1/4&\quad&t_{1}\in(1/2,3/4]\\
&2t_{1}-1&\quad&t_{1}\in(3/4,1],\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

we have,

((\gamma_{1}\ast\gamma_{2})\ast\gamma_{3})(\theta(t_{1}),t_{2},\dots,t_{n})=(\gamma_{1}\ast(\gamma_{2}\ast\gamma_{3}))(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}).

So, due to the beginning of the proof, the n-regions
\gamma_{1}\ast(\gamma_{2}\ast\gamma_{3})
and

(\gamma_{1}\ast\gamma_{2})\ast\gamma_{3}
belong to the same discontinuous homotopy class.

We are going to see the existence of left and right identities for every n-region
\gamma
. We will see
this just with the right identity because for the left one is analogue. Take
\gamma\in Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
and
\mathfrak{i}
one
constant n-region. Thus,

(54)

(\gamma\ast\mathfrak{i})(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\gamma(2t_{1},t_{2}\dots,t_{n})&\quad&t\in[0,1/2]\\
&\mathfrak{i}(2t_{1}-1,t_{2},\dots,t_{n})&\quad&t\in(1/2,1].\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

is in the same discontinuous homotopy class of
\gamma
, just defining the reparametrization,

\theta(t_{1})=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &2t_{1}&\quad&t\in[0,1/2]\\
&1&\quad&t\in(1/2,1],\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

and taking a look to the equality
\gamma_{\theta}=\gamma\ast\mathfrak{i}
.

Let’s prove now the existence of an inverse for every n-region
\gamma
. Take

\gamma\in Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
and take
\gamma^{-1}(t)=\gamma(1-t)
for
every
t\in[0,1]^{n}
(understanding
1-t=(1-t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n})
). We have to see that
\gamma\ast\gamma^{-1}
is in the equivalence class
of some identity (with
\gamma^{-1}\ast\gamma
is analogue), specifically, in the class
of the identity
\mathfrak{i}(t)=\gamma(0)
for all
t\in[0,1]^{n}
. Take
s\in[0,1]
and define the n-regions,

\gamma_{s}(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\gamma(t)&\quad&t\in[0,1-s]\times[0,1]^{n-1}\\
&\gamma^{-1}(t)&\quad&t\in(1-s,1]\times[0,1]^{n-1}\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}\qquad\gamma_{s}^{-1}(t)=\gamma_{s}(1-t).

Take the discontinuous homotopy,

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\overset{\ast}{\Delta}_{H}:&[0,1]\times[0,1]^{n}&\rightarrow&X^{m}\\
&(s,t)&\mapsto&\overset{\ast}{\Delta}_{H}(s,t)=\gamma_{s}(t)\ast\gamma_{s}^{-1}(t).\\
\end{array}

If
s=0
we have that
\Delta_{H}(0,t)=\gamma_{0}(t)\ast\gamma_{0}^{-1}(t)=\gamma(t)\ast\gamma^{-1}(t)
and if
s=1
,

\Delta_{H}(1,t)=\gamma_{1}(t)\ast\gamma_{1}^{-1}(t)=\gamma^{-1}(1)=\gamma(0)
, thus we conclude
that
\gamma\ast\gamma^{-1}
is in the same discontinuous homotopy class of
\mathfrak{i}
.

At this point, we have that the sets defined in theorem
3.19

are disguised-groups. We just need to see that the identity element is unique. But this is a consequence of
(
54
) and proposition 2.6 (a) of
[
2
]
.
∎

From this moment we will denote the equivalence class of a discontinuous n-region
\gamma
in

, belonging to some
of the sets defined in (

as
[\gamma]_{\Delta_{H}}
.

a discontinuous

To prove that

,
\widetilde{\gamma}_{s}^{-1}(W)
is closed being
W
an open neighborhood of
\widetilde{\gamma}_{s}(t)
, then

\widetilde{\gamma}_{s}
is discontinuous for all
t\in\widetilde{\gamma}_{s}^{-1}(W)
, and using the same
reasoning as before,
\gamma_{s}
is discontinuous for all
t\in\widetilde{\gamma}_{s}^{-1}(W)
. So,

(57)

\widetilde{\gamma}_{s}^{-1}(W)\subset\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s}).

Remembering that
\Delta_{H}
is a discontinuous homotopy we have that,

\text{Im}(\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s}))\subset[p]_{\varphi}.

Like
t_{0}\in\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s})
then
[p]_{\varphi}\in W
, so for every
p^{\prime}\in[p]_{\varphi}
we have that

p^{\prime}\in\varphi^{-1}(W)
. Thus,

[p]_{\varphi}\subset\varphi^{-1}(W).

Joining both facts,

\text{Im}(\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s}))\subset[p]_{\varphi}\subset\varphi^{-1}(W).

But then,

\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s})\subset\gamma_{s}^{-1}(\varphi^{-1}(W)),

and looking at (
57
) we have,

(58)

\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s})=\gamma_{s}^{-1}(\varphi^{-1}(W)).

As
\gamma_{s}
is a discontinuous n-region in
X^{m}
then it holds the controlled jumping property in
t_{0}

by definition
3.1
, i.e., for every open subset
U\subset[0,1]^{n}

such that
t_{0}\in\partial U
, there exists some point
p\in\text{Im}(\mathfrak{D}(\gamma))
(depending on
U
) such that,

U\cap\gamma^{-1}(V_{p})\neq\emptyset

for every
V_{p}\subset X^{m}
an open neighborhood of
p
.
Taking
V_{p}=\varphi^{-1}(W)
and using (
58
) we have that,

U\cap\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s})\neq\emptyset\qquad\forall t_{1}\in U.

In that case
\mathfrak{D}(\gamma_{s})
would be dense around
t_{0}
and this is a contradiction with
lemma
3.3
. We conclude that
\widetilde{\gamma}_{s}

is continuous in
t_{0}
, and, as this process can be done with every
t_{0}\in[0,1]^{n}
, then
\widetilde{\gamma}_{s}

is continuous in
[0,1]^{n}
. As
\gamma_{s}
is a discontinuous n-region into
Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
(or into
\overset{\ast}{S^{n}_{spheric}}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
) then

\gamma_{s}(t)\in[p]_{\varphi}
, for every
t\in\partial[0,1]^{n}
, so
\widetilde{\gamma}_{s}(t)=[p]_{\varphi}
, for every
t\in\partial[0,1]^{n}
.
We conclude that
\widetilde{\gamma}_{s}
is a continuous loop in
SP_{m}(X)
. We finish the proof saying that the
previous process can be done for every
s\in[0,1]
.
∎

3.3.
Relation between higher order homotopy groups of symmetric products

All the tools developed in the previous subsections will lead us to the result announced: the homotopy
groups of symmetric products are monomorphic. Thank to the next theorem, we are going to be able to
introduce a new algebraic tool in topology: the discontinuous homotopy groups.

Theorem 3.25
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff topological space,
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in X^{m}
. Then,

\pi_{n}(SP_{m}(X),[p]_{\varphi})\hookrightarrow\pi_{n}(F_{m}(X),[p]_{\phi}),

where the symbol
\hookrightarrow
denotes monomorphy.

Proof.

We will prove first the next monomorphy relation,

(60)

\pi_{n}(SP_{m}(X),[p]_{\varphi})\hookrightarrow\pi_{n}(\widetilde{F}_{m}(X),[p]_{\phi}).

To prove (
60
) we will
deduce the next three facts in this order,

(61)

\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})/\text{Ker}(h_{\varphi})\approx\pi_{n}(SP_{m}(X),[p]_{\varphi}),

(62)

\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})/\text{Ker}(h_{\phi})\approx\pi_{n}(\widetilde{F}_{m}(X),[p]_{\phi}),

(63)

\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})/\text{Ker}(h_{\varphi})\hookrightarrow\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})/\text{Ker}(h_{\phi}),

with
h_{\varphi}
a homomorphism between
\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
and

\pi_{n}(SP_{m}(X),[p]_{\varphi})
, and
h_{\phi}
a homomorphism between
\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})
and

\pi_{n}(\widetilde{F}_{m}(X),[p]_{\phi})
; both homomorphisms
h_{\varphi}
and
h_{\phi}

will be built later.

Let’s begin with the proof of (
60
). Define the morphism
h_{\varphi}
like,

(64)

\begin{array}[]{rccl}h_{\varphi}:&\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})&\rightarrow&\pi_{n}(SP_{m}(X),[p]_{\varphi})\\
&[\gamma]_{\Delta_{H}}&\mapsto&h_{\varphi}([\gamma]_{\Delta_{H}})=[\varphi\circ\gamma]_{H_{SP_{m}}^{\Delta}}.\\
\end{array}

The morphism
h_{\varphi}
is well defined because of lemma
3.22
. Denote by
\circledast
the operation defined in theorem
3.19
and denote the operation between equivalence classes of loops in

\pi_{n}(SP_{m}(X),[p]_{\varphi})
with the same symbol. To prove that
h_{\varphi}
is a homomorphism we
need to see the next identity,

(65)

h_{\varphi}([\gamma_{1}]_{\Delta_{H}}\circledast[\gamma_{2}]_{\Delta_{H}})=h_{\varphi}([\gamma_{1}]_{\Delta_{H}})\circledast h_{\varphi}([\gamma_{2}]_{\Delta_{H}}),

for every
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
. Using lemma

3.22
,

\begin{split}h_{\varphi}([\gamma_{1}]_{\Delta_{H}}\circledast[\gamma_{2}]_{\Delta_{H}})&=h_{\varphi}([\gamma_{1}\ast\gamma_{2}]_{\Delta_{H}})=[\varphi\circ(\gamma_{1}\ast\gamma_{2})]_{H_{SP_{m}(X)}^{\Delta}}\\
&=[(\varphi\circ\gamma_{1})\ast(\varphi\circ\gamma_{2})]_{H_{SP_{m}(X)}^{\Delta}}\\
&=[\varphi\circ\gamma_{1}]_{H_{SP_{m}(X)}^{\Delta}}\circledast[\varphi\circ\gamma_{2}]_{H_{SP_{m}(X)}^{\Delta}}\\
&=h_{\varphi}([\gamma_{1}]_{\Delta_{H}})\circledast h_{\varphi}([\gamma_{2}]_{\Delta_{H}})\end{split}

So we have stated (
65
). As
h_{\varphi}
is onto by lemma

3.23
and

\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
is a group by theorem
3.19
, then using the first isomorphy theorem for groups,
[
11
]
or
[
12
]
or
[
20
]
, we conclude (
61
).

Making an analogous deduction changing
\varphi
by
\phi
and
SP_{m}(X)
by
\widetilde{F}_{m}(X)

we conclude (
62
).
Now we need to prove (
63
). By remark

3.21
we know that,

\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})\subset\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi}).

Take
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Delta_{cubic}\pi_{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})
. By definition of
\text{Ker}(h_{\varphi})
,
by corolary 2.19 of
[
2
]
(or the analogous case for groups) and using relation (
6
),

[\gamma_{1}]_{\Delta_{H}}\in[[\gamma_{2}]_{\Delta_{H}}]_{\text{Ker}(h_{\varphi})}\Leftrightarrow[\gamma_{1}]_{\Delta_{H}}\circledast[\gamma_{2}]_{\Delta_{H}}^{-1}\in\text{Ker}(h_{\varphi})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow[\gamma_{1}]_{\Delta_{H}}\circledast[\gamma_{2}]_{\Delta_{H}}^{-1}=[\mathfrak{i}]_{\Delta_{H}}\text{ for some identity }\mathfrak{i}\in Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\varphi})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow[\gamma_{1}]_{\Delta_{H}}\circledast[\gamma_{2}]_{\Delta_{H}}^{-1}=[\mathfrak{i}]_{\Delta_{H}}\text{ for some identity }\mathfrak{i}\in Q_{cubic}^{n}(X^{m},[p]_{\phi})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow[\gamma_{1}]_{\Delta_{H}}\circledast[\gamma_{2}]_{\Delta_{H}}^{-1}\in\text{Ker}(h_{\phi})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow[\gamma_{1}]_{\Delta_{H}}\in[[\gamma_{2}]_{\Delta_{H}}]_{\text{Ker}(h_{\phi})}.

With the last deduction and using, once again, remark
3.21

we have (
63
).

Now joining (
61
),
(
62
) and
(
63
) we have,

\pi_{n}(SP_{m}(X),[p]_{\varphi})\hookrightarrow\pi_{n}(\widetilde{F}_{m}(X),[p]_{\phi}).

But, by a theorem of Ganea, see Hilfssatz 2 of
[
15
]
, we get the homeomorphism,

\widetilde{F}_{m}(X)\cong F_{m}(X),

so using this last two facts and having account that the homotopy groups of a topological
space are topologically invariant we finish.
∎

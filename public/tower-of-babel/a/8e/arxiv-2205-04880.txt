Consensus based optimization via jump-diffusion stochastic differential equations
D. Kalise, A. Sharma, M.V. Tretyakov

Abstract
We introduce a new consensus based optimization (CBO) method where interacting particle system is driven by jump-diffusion stochastic differential equations. We study well-posedness of the particle system as well as of its mean-field limit. The major contributions of this paper are proofs of convergence of the interacting particle system towards the mean-field limit and convergence of a discretized particle system towards the continuous-time dynamics in the mean-square sense. We also prove convergence of the mean-field jump-diffusion SDEs towards global minimizer for a large class of objective functions. We demonstrate improved performance of the proposed CBO method over earlier CBO methods in numerical simulations on benchmark objective functions.



1
Introduction

Large-scale individual-based models have become a well-established modelling tool in modern science and engineering, with applications including pedestrian motion, collective animal behaviour, swarm robotics and molecular dynamics, among many others. Through the iteration of basic interactions forces such as attraction, repulsion, and alignment, these complex systems of exhibit a rich self-organization behaviour (see e.g.
[
BFM97
,
CS07
,
CFRT10
,
MT14
,
BRSW15
,
ABF+19
]
).

Over the last decades, individual-based models have also entered the field of global optimization and its many applications in operations research, control, engineering, economics, finance, and machine learning. In many applied problems arising in the aforementioned fields, the objective function to be optimized can be non-convex and/or non-smooth, disabling the use of traditional continuous/convex optimization technique. In such scenarios, individual-based metaheuristic models have been proven surprisingly effective. Examples include genetic algorithms, ant colony optimization, particle swarm optimization, simulated annealing, etc. (see
[
HKS89
,
DB05
,
Ken10
]
and references therein). These methods are probabilistic in nature which set them apart from other derivative-free algorithms
[
CSV09
]
. Unlike many convex optimization methods, metaheuristic algorithms, are relatively simple to implement and easily parallelizable. This combination of simplicity and effectiveness has fuelled the application of metaheuristic in complex engineering problems such as shape optimization, scheduling problems, and hyper-parameter tuning in machine learning models. However, it is often the case that metaheuristics lack rigorous convergence results, a question which has become an active area of research
[
GP21
,
GHPQ21
]
.

In
[
PTTM17
]
, the authors introduced a optimization algorithm which employs an individual-based model to frame a global minimization

\min\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x),

where
f(x)
is a positive function from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}
, as a consensus problem . In this model, each individual particle explores the energy landscape given by
f(x)
, broadcasting its current value to the rest of the ensemble through a weighted average. This iterated interaction generates trajectories which flock towards a consensus point which correspond to a global minimizer of
f(x)
, hence the name
Consensus Based Optimization
(CBO). We refer to
[
Tot22
,
GHPQ21
]
for two recent surveys on the topic. The dynamics of existing CBO models are governed by stochastic differential equations with Wiener noise
[
PTTM17
,
CCTT18
,
CJLZ21
]
. Hence, we can resort to a toolbox from stochastic calculus and stochastic numerics to perform analysis of these models. This amenability of CBO models to theoretical as well as numerical analysis differentiates them from other agent based optimization algorithms.

In this paper, we propose a new CBO model which is governed by jump-diffusion stochastic differential equations. This means randomness in the dynamics of the proposed CBO models comes from Wiener noise as well as compound Poisson process. The following are the contributions of this paper:

(i)

We prove the well-posedness of the interacting-particle system and of its mean-field limit driven by jump-diffusion SDEs and convergence of the mean-field SDEs to the global minimum. The approach to study well-posedness and convergence to the global minimum is similar to
[
CCTT18
]
but adapted to the jump-diffusion case with time-dependent coefficients.

(ii)

The major contribution of the paper is that we prove mean-square convergence of the interacting particle system to the mean-field limit when number of particles,
N
, tend to
\infty
. This also implies convergence of the particle system towards the mean-field limit in
2-
Wasserstein metric. Let us emphasize that we prove this result for quadratically growing objective function. We also study convergence of the implementable discretized particle system towards the jump-diffusion SDEs as the discretization step,
h
, goes to
0
. Our results can be utilized for the earlier CBO models
[
PTTM17
,
CCTT18
,
CJLZ21
]
.

(iii)

As illustrated in the numerical experiments, the addition of a jump-diffusion process in the particle system leads to a more effective exploration of the energy landscape. This particularly relevant when a good prior knowledge of the optimal solution for initialization of the CBO is not available.

As was highlighted in
[
CCTT18
, Remark 3.2]
, it is not straightforward to prove convergence of the interacting particle system towards its mean-field limit, even after proving uniform in
N
moment bound of the solutions of the SDEs driving particles system. Convergence results of this type have been proved for special cases of compact manifolds (see
[
FHPS20
]
for compact hypersurfaces and
[
HKK+22
]
for Stiefel manifolds) and globally Lipschitz continuous objective functions. In this case, not only the objective function is bounded but also particles are evolving on a compact set. Under the assumptions on the objective function as in our paper, in the diffusion case weak convergence of the empirical measure of a particle system to the law of the corresponding mean field SDEs has been proved in
[
GHPQ21
,
HQ21
]
exploiting Prokhorov’s theorem. Here we prove convergence of the particle system to the mean-field SDEs in the mean-square sense for a quadratically growing locally-Lipschitz objective function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Furthermore, practical implementation of the particle system corresponding to a CBO model needs a numerical approximation in the mean-square sense. We utilize an explicit Euler scheme to implement the proposed jump-diffusion CBO model. This leads to the question whether the Euler scheme converges to the CBO model taking into account that the coefficients of the particle system are not globally Lipschitz and the Lipschitz constants grow exponentially when the objective function is not bounded. At the same time, the coefficients of the particle system have linear growth at infinity. In the case of jump-diffusion SDEs, earlier works either showed convergence of the Euler scheme in the case of globally Lipschitz coefficients
[
PBL10
]
or proposed special schemes in the case of non-globally Lipschitz coefficients with super-linear growth, e.g. a tamed Euler scheme
[
DKS16
]
. Here we prove mean-square convergence of the Euler scheme and we show that this convergence is uniform in the number of particles
N
, i.e. the choice of a discretization time-step
h
is independent of
N
. Our convergence result also holds for earlier CBO models
[
PTTM17
,
CCTT18
,
CJLZ21
]
.

In Section
2
, we first present a review of existing CBO models and then describe our CBO model driven by jump-diffusion SDEs. We also formally introduce mean-field limit of the new CBO model. In Section

, we focus on well-posedness of the interacting particle system behind the new CBO model and its mean-field limit. In Section

, we discuss convergence of the mean field limit towards a point in

which approximates the global minimum, convergence of the interacting particle system towards mean field limit, and convergence of the implementable discretized particle system towards the particle system. We present results of numerical experiments in Section
5
to compare performance of our model and the existing CBO models.

C

a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

2.1
Review of the existing CBO models

Let

\displaystyle=-\beta(X^{i}_{N}(t)-\bar{X}^{\alpha,f}_{N}(t))H^{\epsilon}(f(X^{i}_{N}(t))-f(\bar{X}^{\alpha,f}_{N}(t)))dt

represent

-dimensional standard Wiener processes,
\beta>0
,
\sigma>0
, and
\bar{X}^{\alpha,f}_{N}(t)

\bar{X}^{\alpha,f}_{N}(t)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{i}_{N}(t)w_{f}^{\alpha}(X^{i}_{N}(t))}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{f}^{\alpha}(X^{i}_{N}(t))},

(2.2)

with

,
\alpha>0
.

Each particle
X^{i}_{N}
at time
t
is assigned an opinion
f(X^{i}_{N}(t))
. The lesser the value of

for a particle, the more is the influence of that particle, i.e. the more weight is assigned to that particle at that time as can be seen in (
2.2
) of the instantaneous weighted average. If the value

at the instantaneous weighted average
\bar{X}_{N}^{\alpha,f}(t)
then the regularised Heaviside function forces the particle

to drift towards
\bar{X}_{N}^{\alpha,f}
. If the opinion of
i

, then it is not beneficial for it to move towards

The noise intensity induced in the dynamics of the
i-
th particle at time

takes into account the distance of the particle from the instantaneous weighted average,

. Over a period of time as the particles start moving towards a consensus opinion, the coefficients in (

) go to zero.

One can observe that the more influential opinion a particular particle has, the higher is the weight assigned to that particle in the instantaneous weighted average (
2.2
). Based on this logic, in
[
CCTT18
]
the authors dropped the regularised Heaviside function in the drift coefficient and the model (

) was simplified as follows:

dX^{i}_{N}(t)=-\beta(X^{i}_{N}(t)-\bar{X}_{N}^{\alpha,f}(t))dt+\sigma|X^{i}_{N}(t)-\bar{X}_{N}^{\alpha,f}(t)|dW^{i}(t),\;\;\;i=1,\dots,N,

(2.3)

with
\beta
,
\sigma
,
\bar{X}_{N}^{\alpha,f}
as in (
2.1
)-(
2.2
).

The major drawback of the consensus based models (

) and (
2.3
) is that the parameters
\beta
and
\sigma
are dependent on the dimension
d
. To illustrate this fact, we replace
\bar{X}_{N}^{\alpha,f}
in (

(2.4)

As one can notice, for particles to reach the consensus point whose position vector is

. To overcome this deficiency, the authors of

]
proposed the following model which is based on component-wise noise intensity instead of isotropic noise used in (

(2.5)

where
\beta,\sigma
, and
\bar{X}_{N}^{\alpha,f}
are as in (
2.1
)-(
2.2
), and

is a diagonal matrix whose diagonal is a vector

Now, if we replace
\bar{X}_{N}^{\alpha,f}
by a fixed vector

and then use Ito’s formula for (

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}|X^{i}_{N}(t)-V|^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\beta\mathbb{E}|X^{i}_{N}(t)-V|^{2}+\sigma^{2}\mathbb{E}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}(X^{i}_{N}(t)-V)_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=(-2\beta+\sigma^{2})\mathbb{E}|X^{i}_{N}(t)-V|^{2},\;\;\;\;i=1,\dots,N,

(2.6)

where
(X_{N}^{i}(t)-V)_{j}
denotes the
j-
th component of
(X_{N}^{i}(t)-V)
. It is clear that in this model there is no dimensional restriction on
\beta
and
\sigma
.

Other CBO models
[
HJK20
,
HJK21
]
are based on interacting particles driven by common noise. Since the same noise drives all the particles, the exploration is not effective. Therefore, they are not scalable with respect to dimension and do not perform well in contrast to the CBO models (

2.2.1
Other jump-diffusion CBO models

Although the aim of the paper is it to analyse the CBO model (
2.2
), we discuss three other jump-diffusion CBO models of interest.

Additional Model 1 :
Writing (
2.2
) in terms of Poisson random measure suggests that we can also consider an infinite activity Lévy process, e.g. an
\alpha-
stable process, to introduce jumps in dynamics of particles. We can write the CBO model as

\displaystyle dX^{i}_{N}(t)

\displaystyle=-\beta(t)(X^{i}_{N}(t)-\bar{X}_{N}(t))dt+\sqrt{2}\sigma(t)\diag(X^{i}_{N}(t)-\bar{X}_{N}(t))dW^{i}(t)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\gamma(t)\diag(X^{i}_{N}(t^{-})-\bar{X}_{N}(t^{-}))z\mathcal{N}^{i}(dt,dz),

(2.15)

However, numerical approximation of SDEs driven by infinite activity Lévy processes is computationally more expensive (see e.g.
[
PBL10
,
DMT21
]
), hence it can be detrimental for the overall CBO performance.

Additional Model 2 :
In the SDEs (
2.2
), the intensity of Poisson process
\lambda
is constant. If we take jump intensity as
\lambda(t)
, i.e. a function of
t
then the corresponding SDEs will be as follows:

\displaystyle dX^{i}(t)

\displaystyle=-\beta(t)(X^{i}_{N}(t)-\bar{X}_{N}(t))dt+\sqrt{2}\sigma(t)\diag(X^{i}_{N}(t)-\bar{X}(t))dW^{i}(t)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\diag(X^{i}_{N}(t^{-})-\bar{X}_{N}(t^{-}))dJ^{i}(t),\;\;i=1,\dots,N,

(2.16)

where all the notation are as in (
2.2
) and (

) except here the intensity of the Poisson processes

is a time-dependent function
\lambda(t)
. It is assumed that
\lambda(t)
is a decreasing function such that

as

. Also, in comparison with (
2.2
), there is no

in the jump component of (
2.2.1
).
Note that, the compound Poisson process with constant jump intensity

is a Lévy process but with time-dependent jump intensity

]
. The SDEs (
2.2.1
) present another jump-diffusion CBO model driven by additive process. The analysis of model (
2.2.1
) follows similar arguments since the jump-diffusion SDEs (
2.2.1
) can also be written in terms of the Poisson random measure with intensity measure

, where

Additional Model 3 :
In model (
2.2
), the particles have idiosyncratic noise which means they are driven by different Wiener processes and different compound Poisson processes. Instead, we can have a different jump-diffusion model in which the same Poisson noise drives particle system but jumps sizes still independently vary for all particles. This means jumps arrive at the same time for all particles, but particles jump with different jump-sizes. We can write CBO model as

\displaystyle dX^{i}_{N}(t)

\displaystyle=-\beta(t)(X^{i}_{N}(t)-\bar{X}_{N}(t))dt+\sqrt{2}\sigma(t)\diag(X^{i}_{N}(t)-\bar{X}_{N}(t))dW^{i}(t)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\gamma(t)\diag(X^{i}_{N}(t^{-})-\bar{X}_{N}(t^{-}))z\mathcal{N}(dt,dz).

(2.17)

We compare performance of the jump-diffusion CBO models (
2.2
) and (
2.2.1
) in Section
5
.

2.2.2
Discussion

Firstly, we will discuss dependence of the parameters

,

,

In the previous CBO models, there were only two terms namely, the drift term and the diffusion term. The drift tries to take the particles towards their instantaneous weighted average. The diffusion term helps in exploration of the state space with the aim to find a state with better weighted average than the current one. The model (
2.2
) contains one extra term, which we call the jump term. Jumps help in intensifying the search in a search space and aids in avoiding premature convergence or trapping in local minima. This results in more effective use of the interaction of particles.

Moreover, the effect of jumps decays with time in (
2.2
) by virtue of decreasing
\gamma(t)
. The reason for considering the model (
2.2
) where jumps affect only the initial period of time is that we want particles to explore more space faster at the beginning of simulation and, as soon as the weighted average of particles is in a vicinity of the global minimum, we do not want jumps to affect convergence of particles towards that consensus point lying in the close neighbourhood of the global minimum. Therefore, the time-dependent parameters and degeneracy of the coefficients help in exploiting the searched space.

As a consequence, the jump-diffusion noise and degenerate time-dependent coefficients in model (
2.2
) may help in keeping the balance of
exploration
and
exploitation
by interacting particles over a period of time. We will continue this discussion on exploration and exploitation in Section

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}|X^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p-2}\big(X_{N}^{i}(s)\cdot\diag(X_{N}^{i}(s)-\bar{X}_{N}(s))dW^{i}(s)\big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X^{i}_{N}(t)|^{2p}+C\mathbb{E}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|X_{N}^{i}(s)-\bar{X}_{N}(s))|^{2}ds\bigg)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X^{i}_{N}(t)|^{2p}+C\mathbb{E}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|X_{N}^{i}(s)-\bar{X}_{N}(s))|^{2p}ds\bigg),

(3.7)

where in the last step we have utilized Holder’s inequality.

Now, we move on to obtain estimates which are required to deal with fourth and fifth term in (
3.1
). Using Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle A_{1}:=|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p-4}(|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2}

\displaystyle-(X_{N}^{i}(s)\cdot\bar{X}_{N}(s)))^{2}\leq 2|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}+2|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p-2}|\bar{X}_{N}(s)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4p-2}{p}|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}+\frac{2}{p}|\bar{X}_{N}(s)|^{2p}.

(3.8)

In the same way, applying Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle A_{2}:=|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p-2}|\diag(X_{N}^{i}(s)

\displaystyle-\bar{X}_{N}(s))|^{2}\leq 2|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}+2|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p-2}|\bar{X}_{N}(s)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4p-2}{p}|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}+\frac{2}{p}|\bar{X}_{N}(s)|^{2p}.

(3.9)

Following the same procedure based on (
3.4
), which we followed to obtain bound (
3.6
), we also get

\displaystyle A_{1}+A_{2}\leq C\Big(1+|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}+\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}\Big),

(3.10)

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
N
.

It is left to deal with the last term in (
3.1
). Using the Cauchy-Bunyakowsky-Schwartz inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(|X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})+\gamma(s)\diag(X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})-\bar{X}_{N}(s^{-}))z|^{2p}-|X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})|^{2p}\big)\mathcal{N}^{i}(ds,dz)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(2^{2p-1}\big(|X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})|^{2p}+|\gamma(s)\diag(X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})-\bar{X}_{N}(s^{-}))z|^{2p}\big)-|X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})|^{2p}\bigg)\mathcal{N}^{i}(ds,dz)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(|X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})|^{2p}+|\gamma(s)\diag(X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})-\bar{X}_{N}(s^{-}))z|^{2p}\big)\mathcal{N}^{i}(ds,dz)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}+|\gamma(s)\diag(X_{N}^{i}(s)-\bar{X}_{N}(s))z|^{2p}\big)\rho_{z}(z)dz

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}+|X_{N}^{i}(s)-\bar{X}_{N}(s)|^{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{2p}\rho_{z}(z)dz\Big)ds.

We have

\displaystyle|X_{N}^{i}(s)-\bar{X}_{N}(s)|^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2p-1}\big(|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}+|\bar{X}_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}\big)\leq C\Big(1+|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}+\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}\Big),

and hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(|X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})+\gamma(s)\diag(X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})-\bar{X}_{N}(s^{-}))z|^{2p}-|X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})|^{2p}\big)\mathcal{N}^{i}(ds,dz)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(1+|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}+\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}\Big)ds,

(3.11)

where
C>0
does not depend on
N
.

Using (
3.6
), (
3.7
), (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) in (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{N}^{i}(t)|^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}|X_{N}^{i}(0)|^{2p}+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(1+|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}+\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|X_{N}^{i}(s)|^{2p}\Big)ds

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{N}^{i}(t)|^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}|X_{N}^{i}(0)|^{2p}+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(1+\sup_{0\leq u\leq s}|X_{N}^{i}(u)|^{2p}+\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\sup_{0\leq u\leq s}|X_{N}^{i}(u)|^{2p}\Big)ds.

Taking supremum over
\{1,\dots,N\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{N}^{i}(t)|^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sup_{i=\{1,\dots,N\}}\mathbb{E}|X_{N}^{i}(0)|^{2p}+C\bigg(1+\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq u\leq s}|X_{N}^{i}(u)|^{2p}ds\bigg),

which gives our targeted result for positive integer valued
p
by applying Grönwall’s lemma (note that we can apply Grönwall’s lemma due to (
3.3
)). We can extend the result to non-integer values of
p\geq 1
using Holder’s inequality.
∎

3.2
Well-posedness of mean-field jump-diffusion SDEs

In this section, we first introduce Wasserstein metric and state Lemma
3.4
which is crucial for establishing well-posedness of the mean-field limit. Then, we prove existence and uniqueness of the McKean-Vlasov jump-diffusion SDEs (
2.2
) in Theorem
3.5
.

Let
\mathbb{D}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the space of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
valued cádlág functions and
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\;p\geq 1
, be the space of probability measures on the measurable space
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
such that for any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}\mu(dx)<\infty
, and which is equipped with the
p
-Wasserstein metric

\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mu,\vartheta):=\inf_{\pi\in\prod(\mu,\vartheta)}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{p}\pi(dx,dy)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\prod(\mu,\vartheta)
is the set of couplings of
\mu,\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

[
Vil03
]
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}\mu(dx)\leq K
. Then, using Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle e^{-\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\mu(dx)}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\alpha f(x)}\mu(dx),

and the simple rearrangement together with Assumption
3.4
, gives

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\alpha f_{m}}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\alpha f(x)}\mu(dx)}\leq e^{\alpha(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\mu(dx)-f_{m})}\leq e^{\alpha K_{u}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|^{2})\mu(dx)}\leq C_{K},

(3.12)

where
C_{K}>0
is a constant. We will also need the following notation:

\displaystyle\bar{X}^{\mu}=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}xe^{-\alpha f(x)}\mu(dx)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\alpha f(x)}\mu(dx)},

where
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The next lemma is required for proving well-posedness of the McKean-Vlasov SDEs (
2.2
). Its proof is available in
[
CCTT18
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let Assumptions
3.1
,
3.3
-
3.5
hold and there exists a constant
K>0
such that
\int|x|^{4}\mu(dx)\leq K
and
\int|y|^{4}\vartheta(dy)\leq K
for all
\mu,\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then the following inequality is satisfied:

|\bar{X}^{\mu}-\bar{X}^{\vartheta}|\leq C\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\vartheta),

where
C>0
is independent of
\mu
and
\vartheta
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let Assumptions
3.1
,
3.3
-
3.5
hold, and let
\mathbb{E}|X(0)|^{4}<\infty
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{4}\rho_{z}(z)dz<\infty
. Then, there exists a unique nonlinear process
X\in\mathbb{D}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
T>0
which satisfies the McKean-Vlasov SDEs (
2.2
) in the strong sense.

Proof.

Let
v\in C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Consider the following SDEs:

\displaystyle dX_{v}(t)

\displaystyle=-\beta(t)(X_{v}(t)-v(t))dt+\sigma(t)\diag(X_{v}(t)-v(t))dW(t)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\gamma(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\diag(X_{v}(t^{-})-v(t)))z\mathcal{N}(dt,dz)

(3.13)

for any
t\in[0,T]
.

Note that
v(t)
is a deterministic function of
t
, therefore the coefficients of SDEs (
3.13
) only depend on
x
and
t
. The coefficients are globally Lipschitz continuous and have linear growth in
x
. The existence and uniqueness of a process
X_{v}\in\mathbb{D}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying SDEs with Lévy noise (
3.13
) follows from
[
App04
, pp. 311-312]
. We also have
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{4}\mathcal{L}_{X_{v}(t)}(dx)=\mathbb{E}|X_{v}(t)|^{4}\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{v}(t)|^{4}\leq K
, where
K
is a positive constant depending on
v
and
T
, and
\mathcal{L}_{X_{v}(t)}
represents the law of
X_{v}(t)
.

We define a mapping

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}:C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d}),\;\;\mathbb{T}(v)=\bar{X}_{v},

(3.14)

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}v(t)

\displaystyle=\bar{X}_{v}(t)=\mathbb{E}(X_{v}(t)e^{-\alpha f(X_{v}(t))})\Big/\mathbb{E}(e^{-\alpha f(X_{v}(t))})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}xe^{-\alpha f(x)}\mathcal{L}_{X_{v}(t)}(dx)\bigg/\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\alpha f(x)}\mathcal{L}_{X_{v}(t)}(dx)=\bar{X}^{\mathcal{L}_{X_{v}(t)}}(t).

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
. For all
t,t+\delta\in(0,T)
, Ito’s isometry provides

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{v}(t+\delta)-X_{v}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\mathbb{E}|X_{v}(s)-v(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}|X_{v}(s)-v(s)|^{2}|z|^{2}\rho(z)dzds\leq C\delta,

(3.15)

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
\delta
. Using Lemma
3.4
and (
3.15
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\bar{X}_{v}(t+\delta)-\bar{X}_{v}(t)|

\displaystyle=|\bar{X}^{\mathcal{L}_{X_{v}(t+\delta)}}(t+\delta)-\bar{X}^{\mathcal{L}_{X_{v}(t)}}(t)|\leq C\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{v}(t+\delta)},\mathcal{L}_{X_{v}(t)})

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\mathbb{E}|X_{v}(t+\delta)-X_{v}(t)|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\leq C|\delta|^{1/2},

where
C
is a positive constant independent
\delta
. This implies the Hölder continuity of the map
t\rightarrow\bar{X}_{v}(t)
. Therefore, the compactness of
\mathbb{T}
follows from the compact embedding
C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Using Ito’s isometry, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{v}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\bigg(\mathbb{E}|X_{v}(0)|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)(X_{v}(s)-v(s))ds\bigg|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s)\diag(X_{v}(s)-v(s))dW(s)\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\mathbb{E}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s)\diag(X_{v}(s^{-})-v(s))z\mathcal{N}(ds,dz)\bigg|^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(1+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|X_{v}(s)-v(s)|^{2}ds\bigg)\leq C\bigg(1+\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{E}|X_{v}(s)|^{2}+|v(s)|^{2})ds\bigg),

(3.16)

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
v
.
Moreover, we have the following result under Assumptions
3.1
,
3.3
-
3.5

[
CCTT18
, Lemma 3.3]
:

\displaystyle|\bar{X}_{v}(t)|^{2}\leq L_{1}+L_{2}\mathbb{E}|X_{v}(t)|^{2},

(3.17)

where
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are from (
3.4
).
Consider a set
\mathcal{S}=\{v\in C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d}):v=\epsilon\mathbb{T}v,\;0\leq\epsilon\leq 1\}
. The set
\mathcal{S}
is non-empty due to the fact that
\mathbb{T}
is compact (see the remark after Theorem 10.3 in
[
GT83
]
). Therefore, for any
v\in\mathcal{S}
, we have the corresponding unique process
X_{v}(t)\in\mathbb{D}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying (
3.13
), and
\mathcal{L}_{X_{v}(t)}
represents the law of
X_{v}(t)
, such that the following holds due to (
3.17
):

\displaystyle|v(s)|^{2}=\epsilon^{2}|\mathbb{T}v(s)|^{2}=\epsilon^{2}|\bar{X}_{v}(s)|^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}\big(L_{1}+L_{2}\mathbb{E}|X(s)|^{2})

(3.18)

for all
s\in[0,T]
. Substituting (
3.18
) in (
3.16
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{v}(t)|^{2}\leq C\bigg(1+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|X_{v}(s)|^{2}ds\bigg),

which on applying Grönwall’s lemma gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{v}(t)|^{2}\leq C,

(3.19)

where
C
is independent of
v
. Due to (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), we can claim the boundedness of the set
\mathcal{S}
. Therefore, from the Leray-Schauder theorem
[
GT83
, Theorem 10.3]
there exists a fixed point of the mapping
\mathbb{T}
. This proves existence of the solution of (
2.2
).

Let
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
be two fixed points of the mapping
\mathbb{T}
and let us denote the corresponding solutions of (
3.13
) as
X_{v_{1}}
and
X_{v_{2}}
. Using Ito’s isometry, we can get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{v_{1}}(t)-X_{v_{2}}(t)|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}|X_{v_{1}}(0)-X_{v_{2}}(0)|^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\big(\mathbb{E}|X_{v_{1}}(s)-X_{v_{2}}(s)|^{2}+|v_{1}(s)-v_{2}(s)|^{2}\big)ds.

(3.20)

Note that
\mathcal{S}
is a bounded set and by definiiton
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
belong to
\mathcal{S}
. Then, we can apply Lemma
3.4
to ascertain

\displaystyle|v_{1}(s)-v_{2}(s)|^{2}=|\bar{X}_{v_{1}}(s)-\bar{X}_{v_{2}}(s)|^{2}\leq C\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{v_{1}}(s)},\mathcal{L}_{X_{v_{2}}(s)})\leq C\mathbb{E}|X_{v_{1}}(s)-X_{v_{2}}(s)|^{2}.

Using the above estimate, Grönwall’s lemma and the fact
X_{v_{1}}(0)=X_{v_{2}}(0)
in (
3.20
), we get uniqueness of the solution of (
2.2
).
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let Assumptions
3.1
,
3.3
-
3.5
are satisfied. Let
p\geq 1
,
\mathbb{E}|X(0)|^{2p}<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}|Z|^{2p}<\infty
, then the following holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X(t)|^{2p}\leq K_{p},

where
X(t)
satisfies (
2.2
) and
K_{p}
is a positive constant.

Proof.

Recall that under the assumptions of this theorem, Theorem
3.5
guarantees existence of a strong solution of (
2.2
).

Let
p
be a positive integer. Let us denote
\theta_{R}=\inf\{s\geq 0\;;\;|X(s)|\geq R\}
. Using Ito’s formula, we obtain

\displaystyle|X(t)|^{2p}

\displaystyle=|X(0)|^{2p}-2p\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)|X(s)|^{2p-2}\big(X(s)\cdot(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))\big)ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+2\sqrt{2}p\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s)|X(s)|^{2p-2}\big(X(s)\cdot(\diag(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))dW(s))\big)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+4p(p-1)\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}(s)|X(s)|^{2p-4}|\diag(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))X(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+2p\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}(s)|X(s)|^{2p-2}|\diag(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|X(s^{-})+\gamma(s)\diag(X(s^{-})-\bar{X}(s^{-}))z|^{2p}-|X(s^{-})|^{2p})\mathcal{N}(ds,dz).

First taking suprema over
0\leq t\leq T\wedge\theta_{R}
and then taking expectation on both sides, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\theta_{R}}|X(t)|^{2p}\leq\mathbb{E}|X(0)|^{2p}+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\theta_{R}}|X(s)|^{2p-2}\big|X(s)\cdot(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))\big|ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+C\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\theta_{R}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}|X(s)|^{2p-2}\big(X(s)\cdot(\diag(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))dW(s))\big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\theta_{R}}|X(s)|^{2p-4}|\diag(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))X(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\theta_{R}}|X(s)|^{2p-2}|\diag(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\theta_{R}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|X(s^{-})+\gamma(s)\diag(X(s^{-})-\bar{X}(s^{-}))z|^{2p}-|X(s^{-})|^{2p})\mathcal{N}(ds,dz).

(3.21)

To deal with the second term in (
3.21
), we use Young’s inequality and ascertain

\displaystyle|X(s)|^{2p-2}\big|X(s)\cdot(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))\big|\leq|X(s)|^{2p}+|X(s)|^{2p-1}|\bar{X}(s)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4p-1}{2p}|X(s)|^{2p}+\frac{1}{2p}|\bar{X}(s)|^{2p}\leq C(|X(s)|^{2p}+|\bar{X}(s)|^{2p}).

(3.22)

Using Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\theta_{R}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}|X(s)|^{2p-2}\big(X(s)\cdot(\diag(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))dW(s))\big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\theta_{R}}|X(s)|^{4p-2}|X(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2}ds\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigg(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\theta_{R}}|X(t)|^{2p-1}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\theta_{R}}|X(s)-\bar{X}(s))|^{2}ds\bigg)^{1/2}\Bigg).

(3.23)

We apply generalized Young’s inequality
\big(ab\leq(\epsilon a^{q_{1}})/q_{1}+b^{q_{2}}/(\epsilon^{q_{2}/q_{1}}q_{2}),\;\epsilon,q_{1},q_{2}>0,1/q_{1}+1/q_{2}=1
)
and Holder’s inequality on the right hand side of (
3.23
) to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\theta_{R}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}|X(s)|^{2p-2}\big(X(s)\cdot\diag(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))dW(s)\big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\theta_{R}}|X(t)|^{2p}+C\mathbb{E}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\theta_{R}}|X(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2}ds\bigg)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\theta_{R}}|X(t)|^{2p}+C\mathbb{E}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\theta_{R}}|X(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2p}ds\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\theta_{R}}|X(t)|^{2p}+C\mathbb{E}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\theta_{R}}\big(|X(s)|^{2p}+|\bar{X}(s)|^{2p}\big)ds\bigg).

(3.24)

We have the following estimate to use in the fourth term in (
3.21
):

\displaystyle|X(s)|^{2p-4}

\displaystyle|\diag(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))X(s)|^{2}\leq|X(s)|^{2p-4}(|X(s)|^{2}+(X(s)\cdot\bar{X}(s)))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|X(s)|^{2p}+2|X(s)|^{2p-2}|\bar{X}(s)|^{2}\leq C\big(|X(s)|^{2p}+|\bar{X}(s)|^{2p}\big).

(3.25)

We make use of Minkowski’s inequality to get

\displaystyle|X(s)|^{2p-2}|\diag(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))|^{2}=|X(s)|^{2p-2}|X(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2}\leq 2|X(s)|^{2p}+2|X(s)|^{2p-2}|\bar{X}(s)|^{2},

then Young’s inequality implies

\displaystyle|X(s)|^{2p-2}|X(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2}\leq C(|X(s)|^{2p}+|\bar{X}(s)|^{2p}).

(3.26)

Now, we find an estimate for the last term in (
3.21
). Using the Cauchy-Bunyakowsky-Schwartz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\theta_{R}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|X(s^{-})+\gamma(s)\diag(X(s^{-})-\bar{X}(s^{-}))z|^{2p}-|X(s^{-})|^{2p})\mathcal{N}(ds,dz)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\theta_{R}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}2^{2p-1}(|X(s^{-})|^{2p}+|\gamma(s)\diag(X(s^{-})-\bar{X}(s^{-}))z|^{2p})-|X(s^{-})|^{2p}\mathcal{N}(ds,dz)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\theta_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|X(s^{-})|^{2p}+|\gamma(s)\diag(X(s^{-})-\bar{X}(s^{-}))z|^{2p})\mathcal{N}(ds,dz).

Using Doob’s optional stopping theorem
[
App04
, Theorem 2.2.1]
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\theta_{R}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|X(s^{-})+\gamma(s)\diag(X(s^{-})-\bar{X}(s^{-}))z|^{2p}-|X(s^{-})|^{2p})\mathcal{N}(ds,dz)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\theta_{R}}\big(|X(s)|^{2p}+|\bar{X}(s)|^{2p}\big)ds.

We have the following result under Assumptions
3.1
,
3.3
-
3.5

[

, Lemma 3.3]
:

\displaystyle|\bar{X}(s)|^{2}\leq L_{1}+L_{2}\mathbb{E}|X(s)|^{2},

(3.28)

where

and
L_{2}
are from (
3.4

Substituting (

)-(

), using Holder’s inequality, we arrive at the following bound:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T\wedge\theta_{R}}|X(t)|^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}|X(0)|^{2p}+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\theta_{R}}(|X(s)|^{2p}+|\bar{X}(s)|^{2p})ds

\displaystyle\leq C+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\theta_{R}}(1+|X(s)|^{2p}+\mathbb{E}|X(s)|^{2p})ds

which on using Grönwall’s lemma gives

∎

The aim of this section is to show that the non-linear process

Lemma 4.1
.

Under Assumptions

Using Ito’s formula, we have

\displaystyle|X(t)

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}X(t)|^{2}=|X(0)-\mathbb{E}X(0)|^{2}-2\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)(X(s)-\mathbb{E}X(s))\cdot(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))ds

\displaystyle\var(t)=\var(0)-2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)\mathbb{E}\big((X(s)-\mathbb{E}X(s))\cdot(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))\big)dt+2\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}(s)\mathbb{E}|X(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\lambda\gamma^{2}(s)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}|\diag(X(s)-\bar{X}(s))z|^{2}\rho_{z}(z)dzds

\displaystyle=\var(0)-2\int_{0}^{t}\big(\beta(s)\var(s)+2\sigma^{2}(s)\mathbb{E}|X(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2}+\lambda\gamma^{2}(s)\mathbb{E}|\text{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\raisebox{0.86108pt}{--}\cr$Z$\cr}}}|^{2}\mathbb{E}|X(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2}\big)ds,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big((X(t)-\mathbb{E}X(t))\cdot(\mathbb{E}X(t)-\bar{X}(t))\big)=0,

\displaystyle|X(t)-\mathbb{E}X(t)+\diag(X(t)-\bar{X}(t))z|^{2}=|X(t)-\mathbb{E}X(t)|^{2}+|\diag(X(t)-\bar{X}(t))z|^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;+2\big((X(t)-\mathbb{E}X(t))\cdot\diag(X(t)-\bar{X}(t))z\big),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big((X(t)-\mathbb{E}X(t))\cdot\diag(X(t)-\bar{X}(t))z\big)\rho_{z}(z)dz=0.

Moreover,

, since each component
Z_{l}
of
Z
is distributed as

–

Z

.

We also have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X(t)-\bar{X}(t)|^{2}=\var(t)+|\mathbb{E}X(t)-\bar{X}(t)|^{2}.

(4.5)

We estimate the term

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}X(t)-\bar{X}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg|\mathbb{E}X(t)-\frac{\mathbb{E}X(t)e^{-\alpha f(X(t))}}{\mathbb{E}e^{-\alpha f(X(t))}}\bigg|^{2}=\bigg|\mathbb{E}\bigg(\Big(\mathbb{E}X(t)-X(t)\Big)\frac{e^{-\alpha f(X(t))}}{\mathbb{E}e^{-\alpha f(X(t))}}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(\mathbb{E}X(t)-x\big)\vartheta_{X(t)}(dx)\bigg|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|\mathbb{E}X(t)-x\big|^{2}\vartheta_{X(t)}(dx)

Using (

from (

) towards the global minimizer. However, the CBO method is based on the system (
2.2
) of finite particles. This means there is a missing link in the theoretical analysis which we fill in this section by showing convergence of the particle system (
2.2
) to the mean-field limit in mean-square sense (
2.2
) as the number of particles tends to infinity. The proof of this result has some ingredients inspired from
[
MT05
]
(see also
[
MT21
]
), precisely where we partition the sample space (cf. Theorem
4.7
). Further, it is clear from the proof that we need stronger moment bound result like in Lemmas
3.3
and
3.6
, as compared to
[
CCTT18
, Lemma 3.4]
.

We first discuss some concepts necessary for later use in this section. We introduce the following notation for the empirical measure of i.i.d. particles driven by the McKean-Vlasov SDEs (
2.2
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{t}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X^{i}(t)},

(4.14)

where
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac measure at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We will also need the following notation:

\displaystyle\bar{X}^{\mathcal{E}_{t}}(t)=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}xe^{-\alpha f(x)}\mathcal{E}_{t}(dx)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\alpha f(x)}\mathcal{E}_{t}(dx)}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{i}(t)e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(t))}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(t))}}.

(4.15)

Using discrete Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\exp{\bigg(-\alpha\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}f(X^{i}(t))\bigg)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\exp{\Big(-\alpha f(X^{i}(t))\Big)},

which, on rearrangement and multiplying both sides by
e^{-\alpha f_{m}}
, gives

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\alpha f_{m}}}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(t))}}

\displaystyle\leq\exp{\bigg(\alpha\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}f(X^{i}(t))-f_{m}\Big)\bigg)}\leq e^{\alpha K_{u}}\exp{\Big(\frac{\alpha K_{u}}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}(t)|^{2}\Big)},

(4.16)

where we have used Assumption
3.4
for the second inequality.

We recall that a random variable
\zeta(\omega)
is a.s. finite if there is an increasing sequence
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with
e_{k}\rightarrow\infty
as
k\rightarrow\infty
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}\{\omega\;:\;|\zeta(\omega)|<e_{k}\}\big)=1,

which means

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\cap_{k=1}^{\infty}\{\omega\;:\;|\zeta(\omega)|\geq e_{k}\}\big)=0,\;\;\;\;\text{i.e.}\;\;\;\;\;\;\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\{\omega\;:\;|\zeta(\omega)|\geq e_{k}\}\big)=0.

Let
g(x)
be an increasing continuous function of
x\in\mathbb{R}
then
g(\zeta(\omega))
is a.s. finite random variable as well. Also, if
\zeta_{1}(\omega)
and
\zeta_{2}(\omega)
are a.s. finite random variables then
\zeta_{1}(\omega)\vee\zeta_{2}(\omega)
is also an a.s. finite random variable.
If
\zeta(\omega)
is a.s. finite then by continuity of probability we have
[
Shi13
]
:

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(\{\omega\;:\;|\zeta(\omega)|\geq e_{k}\})=0.

(4.17)

We know that
X^{i}(t)
, governed by the McKean-Vlasov SDEs (
2.2
), are i.i.d. random variables for every
t\geq 0
, therefore using Chebyshev’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}(t)|^{2}-\mathbb{E}|X(t)|^{2}\geq N^{(\epsilon-1)/4}\Big)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}(t)|^{2}-\mathbb{E}|X(t)|^{2}\Big|^{4}}{N^{(\epsilon-1)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(|X^{i}(t)|^{2}-\mathbb{E}|X(t)|^{2}\big)\Big|^{4}}{N^{3+\epsilon}}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}U_{i}^{4}}{N^{3+\epsilon}}+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}U_{i}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}U_{j}^{2}}{N^{3+\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{N^{1+\epsilon}},

where we have used Lemma
3.6
,
U_{i}=|X^{i}(t)|^{2}-\mathbb{E}|X(t)|^{2}
and
C
is independent of
N
.
We take
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and define
E_{N}=\left\{\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}(t)|^{2}-\mathbb{E}|X(t)|^{2}>\frac{1}{N^{(1-\epsilon)/4}}\right\}
then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{N=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(E_{N})<\infty.

The Borel-Cantelli lemma implies that the random variable

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}(t):=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}N^{(1-\epsilon)/4}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}(t)|^{2}-\mathbb{E}|X(t)|^{2}\Big)

is a.s. finite. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}(t)|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}|X(t)|^{2}+\zeta_{1}(t,\omega)N^{(-1+\epsilon)/4},\;\;\;\;a.s.,

(4.18)

for all
t\in[0,T]
. Using (
4.18
) in (
4.16
) and Lemma
3.6
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\alpha f_{m}}}{\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(t))}}\leq e^{\alpha K_{u}(1+K_{p}+\zeta_{1}(t,\omega)N^{(-1+\epsilon)/4})},\;\;\;\;a.s.

(4.19)

This show that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{e^{-\alpha f_{m}}}{\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(t))}}\leq e^{\alpha K_{u}(1+K_{p})},\;\;\;\;a.s.

(4.20)

Lemma 4.4
.

Let Assumptions
3.1
,
3.3
-
3.5
be satisfied. Let
\mathbb{E}|X(0)|^{4}<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}|Z|^{4}<\infty
. Then, the following bound holds for all
t\in[0,T]
and sufficiently large
N
:

\displaystyle|\bar{X}^{\mathcal{E}_{t}}(t)-\bar{X}(t)|\leq\frac{\zeta(t,\omega)}{N^{(1-\epsilon)/4}},\;\;\;\;a.s.,

(4.21)

where
\bar{X}^{\mathcal{E}_{t}}(t)
is from (
4.15
),
\bar{X}(t)
is from (
2.13
),
\zeta(t,\omega)
is an
a.s.
finite
\mathscr{F}_{t}-
measurable random variable and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle|\bar{X}^{\mathcal{E}_{t}}(t)

\displaystyle-\bar{X}(t)|=\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{i}(t)\frac{e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(t))}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(X^{j}(t))}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}x\frac{e^{-\alpha f(x)}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\alpha f(x)}\mathcal{L}_{X(t)}(dx)}\mathcal{L}_{X(t)}(dx)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(X^{j}(t))}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{i}(t)e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(t))}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}xe^{-\alpha f(x)}\mathcal{L}_{X(t)}(dx)\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}xe^{-\alpha f(x)}\mathcal{L}_{X(t)}(dx)\bigg(\frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(X^{j}(t))}}-\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\alpha f(x)}\mathcal{L}_{X(t)}(dx)}\bigg)\bigg|.

(4.22)

Let
Y^{i}(t)=X^{i}(t)e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(t))}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}xe^{-\alpha f(x)}\mathcal{L}_{X(t)}(dx)
. Note that
\mathbb{E}Y^{i}(t)
is a
d-
dimensional zero vector and
\mathbb{E}(Y^{i}(t)\cdot Y^{j}(t))=0
,
i\neq j
. Then, using Theorem
3.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{i}(t)e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(t))}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}xe^{-\alpha f(x)}\mathcal{L}_{X(t)}(dx)\Big|^{4}=\frac{1}{N^{4}}\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}Y^{i}(t)\Big|^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{4}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|Y^{i}(t)|^{4}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}|Y^{i}(t)|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|Y^{j}(t)|^{2}\bigg)\leq\frac{C}{N^{2}},

(4.23)

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
N
.
As a consequence of above estimate and using Chebyshev’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{i}(t)e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(t))}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}X(t)e^{-\alpha f(X(t))}\mathcal{L}_{X(t)}(dx)\Big|\geq N^{(\epsilon-1)/4}\bigg)\leq\frac{C}{N^{1+\epsilon}}.

Therefore, by the Borel-Cantelli lemma there exists an a.s. finite
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable random variable
\zeta_{2}(t,\omega)
such that the following bound holds:

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{i}(t)e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(t))}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}X(t)e^{-\alpha f(X(t))}\mathcal{L}_{X(t)}(dx)\Big|\leq\frac{\zeta_{2}(t,\omega)}{N^{(1-\epsilon)/4}},\;\;\;\;a.s.

(4.24)

In the same manner, we can ascertain

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(t))}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\alpha f(X(t))}\mathcal{L}_{X(t)}(dx)\Big|\leq\frac{\zeta_{3}(t,\omega)}{N^{(1-\epsilon)/4}},\;\;\;\;a.s.,

(4.25)

where
\zeta_{3}(t,\omega)
is an a.s. finite
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable random variable. Substituting (
4.19
), (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) in (
4.22
), we conclude that (
4.21
) is true for sufficiently large
N
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let Assumptions
3.1
,
3.3
-
3.5
be satisfied. Then, the following inequality holds for all

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}|\bar{X}_{N}(s)

\displaystyle-\bar{X}^{\mathcal{E}_{s}}(s)|^{2}ds\leq CRe^{4\alpha K_{u}\sqrt{R}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}|X^{i}_{N}(s\wedge\tau_{R})-X^{i}(s\wedge\tau_{R})|^{2}ds,

(4.30)

where
\tau_{R}
is from (
4.27
),
\bar{X}_{N}(s)
is from (
2.10
),
\bar{X}^{\mathcal{E}_{s}}(s)
is from (
4.15
),
C>0
is independent of

and

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle|\bar{X}_{N}(s)-\bar{X}^{\mathcal{E}_{s}}(s)|=\bigg|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}X^{i}_{N}(s)\frac{e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}_{N}(s))}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(X_{N}^{j}(s))}}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}X^{i}(s)\frac{e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(s))}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(X^{j}(s))}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\big(X_{N}^{i}(s)-X^{i}(s)\big)\frac{e^{-\alpha f(X_{N}^{i}(s))}}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(X^{j}_{N}(s))}}\Bigg|+\Bigg|\frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{i}(s)\big(e^{-\alpha f(X_{N}^{i}(s))}-e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(s))}\big)}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(X^{j}_{N}(s))}}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\Bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{i}(s)e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(s))}\bigg(\frac{1}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(X^{j}_{N}(s))}}-\frac{1}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(X^{j}(s))}}\bigg)\Bigg|.

Using the discrete Jensen inequality, we get

\displaystyle|\bar{X}_{N}(s)-\bar{X}^{\mathcal{E}_{s}}(s)|\leq C\Bigg(e^{\frac{\alpha}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}f(X^{j}_{N}(s))}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}_{N}(s)-X^{i}(s)|

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+e^{\frac{\alpha}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}f(X^{j}_{N}(s))}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}(s)||e^{-\alpha f(X_{N}^{i}(s))}-e^{-\alpha f(X^{i}(s))}|

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+e^{\frac{\alpha}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(f(X^{j}_{N}(s))+f(X^{j}(s)))}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}(s)|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|e^{-\alpha f(X_{N}^{j}(s))}-e^{-\alpha f(X^{j}(s))}|\Bigg),

(4.31)

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
N
.
Applying Assumptions
3.3
-
3.4
, the Cauchy-Bunyakowsky-Schwartz inequality and Young’s inequality,
ab\leq a^{2}/2+b^{2}/2
,
a,b>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\bar{X}_{N}(s)-\bar{X}^{\mathcal{E}_{s}}(s)|\leq C\Bigg(e^{\frac{\alpha K_{u}}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|X^{j}_{N}(s)|^{2}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}_{N}(s)-X^{i}(s)|

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+e^{\frac{\alpha K_{u}}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|X^{j}_{N}(s)|^{2}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}(s)|\big(1+|X^{i}_{N}(s)|+|X^{i}(s)|\big)|X_{N}^{i}(s)-X^{i}(s)|

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+e^{\frac{\alpha K_{u}}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(|X^{j}_{N}(s)|^{2}+|X^{j}(s)|^{2})}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}(s)|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\big(1+|X^{j}_{N}(s)|+|X^{j}(s)|\big)|X_{N}^{j}(s)-X^{j}(s)|\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(e^{\frac{\alpha K_{u}}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|X^{j}_{N}(s)|^{2}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}_{N}(s)-X^{i}(s)|

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+e^{\frac{\alpha K_{u}}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(|X^{j}_{N}(s)|^{2}+|X^{j}(s)|^{2})}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(1+|X^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2}+|X^{i}(s)|^{2}\big)|X_{N}^{i}(s)-X^{i}(s)|

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+e^{\frac{\alpha K_{u}}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(|X^{j}_{N}(s)|^{2}+|X^{j}(s)|^{2})}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}(s)|^{2}\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|X^{j}_{N}(s)-X^{j}(s)|\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(e^{\frac{\alpha K_{u}}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|X^{j}_{N}(s)|^{2}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X^{i}_{N}(s)-X^{i}(s)|+e^{\frac{\alpha K_{u}}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(|X^{j}_{N}(s)|^{2}+|X^{j}(s)|^{2})}

(4.32)

On squaring both sides, we ascertain

Using Holder’s inequality, we have

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}|\bar{X}_{N}(s)

\displaystyle-\bar{X}^{\mathcal{E}_{s}}(s)|^{2}ds\leq CRe^{4\alpha K_{u}\sqrt{R}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}|X^{i}_{N}(s\wedge\tau_{R})-X^{i}(s\wedge\tau_{R})|^{2}ds,

where

is independent of

and

. ∎

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}U^{i}_{1}(s\wedge\tau_{R})\bigg|^{2}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}|U_{1}^{i}(s\wedge\tau_{R})|^{2}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1,\;i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{N}\mathbb{E}\big(U^{i}_{1}(s\wedge\tau_{R})\cdot U_{1}^{j}(s\wedge\tau_{R})\big).

\mathbb{E}\big(U^{i}_{1}(s)\cdot U_{1}^{j}(s)\big)=0
for

and

is a bounded stopping time then
\mathbb{E}\big(U^{i}_{1}(s\wedge\tau_{R})\cdot U_{1}^{j}(s\wedge\tau_{R})\big)=0
for
i\neq j
because of Doob’s optional stopping theorem
[
App04
, Theorem 2.2.1]
. Using Theorem
3.6
, we deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}U^{i}_{1}(s\wedge\tau_{R})\bigg|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{N},

(4.34)

where
C
is independent of

. In the similar manner, we can obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}U^{i}_{2}(s\wedge\tau_{R})\bigg|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{N},

(4.35)

where
C
is independent of
N
. Using (
4.34
) and (
4.35
), we get the following estimate:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}|\bar{X}^{\mathcal{E}_{s}}(s)

\displaystyle-\bar{X}(s)|^{2}ds\leq C\frac{e^{2\alpha K_{u}\sqrt{R}}}{N},

where
C
is independent of
N
and
R
.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let Assumptions
3.1
,
3.3
-
3.5
be satisfied. Let
X_{N}^{i}(t)
solve (
2.2
). Let
X^{i}(t)
represent independent processes which solve (
2.2
). Let us assume that
X^{i}_{N}(0)=X^{i}(0)
, a.s.,
i=1\dots,N
. Let
\mathbb{E}|Z|^{4}\leq C
,
\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}|X^{i}(0)|^{4}\leq C
, and
\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}|X^{i}_{N}(0)|^{4}\leq C
. Then, the following mean-square convergence result holds for all

:

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}|X_{N}^{i}(t)-X^{i}(t)|^{2}=0.

(4.36)

Proof.

Let
t\in(0,T]
. We can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{N}^{i}(t)-X^{i}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big(|X_{N}^{i}(t)-X^{i}(t)|^{2}I_{\Omega_{1}(t)}\big)+\mathbb{E}\big(|X_{N}^{i}(t)-X^{i}(t)|^{2}I_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\big)

\displaystyle=:E_{1}(t)+E_{2}(t),

where
\Omega_{1}(t)
and
\Omega_{2}(t)
are from (
4.28
) and (
4.29
), respectively.
Using the Cauchy-Bunyakowsky-Shwartz inequality and Chebyshev’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{1}(t):

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big(|X_{N}^{i}(t)-X^{i}(t)|^{2}I_{\Omega_{1}(t)}\big)\leq\big(\mathbb{E}|X_{N}^{i}(t)-X^{i}(t)|^{4}\big)^{1/2}\big(\mathbb{E}I_{\Omega_{1}(t)}\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\mathbb{E}|X_{N}^{i}(t)|^{4}+\mathbb{E}|X^{i}(t)|^{4}\big)^{1/2}\bigg(\frac{1}{RN}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X^{i}_{N}(s)|^{4}+\frac{1}{RN}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X^{i}(s)|^{4}\bigg)^{1/2}.

We get the following estimate for
E_{1}(t)
by applying Lemma
3.3
and Theorem
3.6
:

\displaystyle E_{1}(t)\leq\frac{C}{R},

(4.37)

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
N
and
R
.

Now, we estimate
E_{2}(t)
. We have
\mathbb{E}(|X_{N}^{i}(t)-X^{i}(t)|^{2}I_{\Omega_{2}(t)})\leq\mathbb{E}(|X_{N}^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})-X^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})|^{2})
. Using Ito’s formula, we have

\displaystyle|X_{N}^{i}

\displaystyle(t\wedge\tau_{R})-X^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})|^{2}=|X^{i}_{N}(0)-X^{i}(0)|^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;-2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\beta(s)(X_{N}^{i}(s)-X^{i}(s))\cdot(X_{N}^{i}(s)-\bar{X}_{N}(s)-X^{i}(s)+\bar{X}(s))ds

\displaystyle\;\;+2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\sigma^{2}(s)|\diag(X_{N}^{i}(s)-\bar{X}_{N}(s)-X^{i}(s)+\bar{X}(s))|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\;\;+2\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\sigma(s)\big((X_{N}^{i}(s)-X^{i}(s))\cdot\diag(X_{N}^{i}(s)-\bar{X}_{N}(s)-X^{i}(s)+\bar{X}(s))dW^{i}(s)\big)

\displaystyle\;\;+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(|X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})-X^{i}(s^{-})+\gamma(s)\diag(X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})-\bar{X}_{N}(s^{-}))z

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;-\gamma(s)\diag(X^{i}(s^{-})-\bar{X}(s^{-}))z|^{2}-|X^{i}_{N}(s^{-})-X^{i}(s^{-})|^{2}\Big)\mathcal{N}^{i}(ds,dz).

(4.38)

The Cauchy-Bunyakowsky-Schwartz inequality and Young’s inequality provide the following estimates:

\displaystyle(X_{N}^{i}(s)-X^{i}(s))\cdot(X_{N}^{i}(s)-\bar{X}_{N}(s)-X^{i}(s)+\bar{X}(s))\leq C(|X^{i}_{N}(s)-X^{i}(s)|^{2}+|\bar{X}_{N}(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2}),

(4.39)

\displaystyle|\diag(X_{N}^{i}(s)-\bar{X}_{N}(s)-X^{i}(s)+\bar{X}(s))|^{2}\leq C(|X^{i}_{N}(s)-X^{i}(s)|^{2}+|\bar{X}_{N}(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2}),

(4.40)

and

\displaystyle\Big(|X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})-X^{i}(s^{-})+\gamma(s)\diag(X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})-\bar{X}_{N}(s^{-}))z-\gamma(s)\diag(X^{i}(s^{-})

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;-\bar{X}(s^{-}))z|^{2}-|X^{i}_{N}(s^{-})-X^{i}(s^{-})|^{2}\Big)=\gamma^{2}(s)|\big((X^{i}_{N}(s^{-})-\bar{X}_{N}(s^{-})-X^{i}(s^{-})+\bar{X}(s^{-}))\cdot z\big)|^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+2\gamma(s)\Big(\big(X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})-X^{i}(s^{-})\big)\cdot\big(\diag(X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})-\bar{X}_{N}(s^{-})-X^{i}(s^{-})+\bar{X}(s^{-}))z\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C(|X^{i}_{N}(s^{-})-X^{i}(s^{-})|^{2}+|\bar{X}_{N}(s^{-})-\bar{X}(s^{-})|^{2})|z|^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+2\gamma(s)\Big(\big(X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})-X^{i}(s^{-})\big)\cdot\big(\diag(X_{N}^{i}(s^{-})-\bar{X}_{N}(s^{-})-X^{i}(s^{-})+\bar{X}(s^{-}))z\big)\Big).

(4.41)

Taking expectations on both sides of (
4.38
), using estimates (
4.39
)-(
4.41
) and applying Doob’s optional stopping theorem
[
App04
, Theorem 2.2.1]
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|X_{N}^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})-X^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}|X_{N}^{i}(0)-X^{i}(0)|^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big(|X_{N}^{i}(s)-X^{i}(s)|^{2}+|\bar{X}_{N}(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2}\big)ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|X^{i}_{N}(s)-X^{i}(s)|^{2}+|\bar{X}_{N}(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2})|z|^{2}\rho_{z}(z)dzds

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}|X_{N}^{i}(0)-X^{i}(0)|^{2}+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}|X_{N}^{i}(s)-X^{i}(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}|\bar{X}_{N}(s)-\bar{X}^{\mathcal{E}_{s}}(s)|^{2}ds+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}|\bar{X}^{\mathcal{E}_{s}}(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2}ds.

(4.42)

Substituting (
4.30
) and (
4.33
) in (
4.42
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\big(|X_{N}^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})-X^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})|^{2}\big)\leq\mathbb{E}|X_{N}^{i}(0)-X^{i}(0)|^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+CRe^{4\alpha K_{u}\sqrt{R}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\big(|X^{i}_{N}(s\wedge\tau_{R})-X^{i}(s\wedge\tau_{R})|^{2}\big)ds+C\frac{e^{2\alpha K_{u}\sqrt{R}}}{N},

where
C>0
is independent of
N
and
R
.
Taking supremum over
i=1,\dots,N
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}\big(|

\displaystyle X_{N}^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})-X^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})|^{2}\big)\leq\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}|X_{N}^{i}(0)-X^{i}(0)|^{2}

\displaystyle+CRe^{4\alpha K_{u}\sqrt{R}}\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}\big(|X^{i}_{N}(s\wedge\tau_{R})-X^{i}(s\wedge\tau_{R})|^{2}\big)ds+C\frac{e^{2\alpha K_{u}\sqrt{R}}}{N}.

Using Grönwall’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}\big(|X_{N}^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})

\displaystyle-X^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})|^{2}\big)\leq\frac{C}{N}e^{CRe^{4\alpha K_{u}\sqrt{R}}}e^{2\alpha K_{u}R}\leq\frac{C}{N}e^{e^{C_{u}\sqrt{R}}},

(4.43)

where
C>0
and
C_{u}>0
are constants independent of
N
and
R
. In the above calculations, we have used the facts that
R<e^{2\alpha K_{u}\sqrt{R}}
and
2\alpha K_{u}\sqrt{R}<e^{2\alpha K_{u}\sqrt{R}}
for sufficiently large

.

We choose
R=\frac{1}{C_{u}^{2}}(\ln{(\ln({N^{1/2})})})^{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}(|X_{N}^{i}(t)-X^{i}(t)|^{2}I_{\Omega_{2}(t)})\leq\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}\big(|X_{N}^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})

\displaystyle-X^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})|^{2}\big)\leq\frac{C}{N^{1/2}},

which implies

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}(|X_{N}^{i}(t)-X^{i}(t)|^{2}I_{\Omega_{2}(t)})=\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}\big(|X_{N}^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})

\displaystyle-X^{i}(t\wedge\tau_{R})|^{2}\big)=0.

(4.44)

The term (
4.37
) and the choice of
R
provide the following estimate:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(|X_{N}^{i}(t)-X^{i}(t)|^{2}I_{\Omega_{1}}(t)\big)\leq\frac{C}{R}\leq\frac{C}{(\ln{(\ln({N^{1/2})}))^{2}}},

where
C>0
is independent of
N
and
R
. This yields

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}\big(|X^{i}_{N}(t)-X^{i}(t)|^{2}I_{\Omega_{1}(t)}\big)=0.

(4.45)

As a consequence of (
4.44
) and (
4.45
) , we get

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}|X_{N}^{i}(t)-X^{i}(t)|^{2}=0,

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
∎

4.3
Convergence of the numerical scheme

To implement the particle system (
2.2
), we have proposed to utilize the Euler scheme introduced in Section
2.2.3
. The jump-diffusion SDEs (
2.2
), governing interacting particle system, have locally Lipschitz and linearly growing coefficients. Due to non-global Lipschitzness of the coefficients, it is not straightforward to deduce convergence of the Euler scheme to (
2.2
). In this section, we go one step further and prove this convergence result uniform in
N
. To this end, we introduce the function
\kappa_{h}(t)=t_{k}
,
t_{k}\leq t<t_{k+1}
, where
0=t_{0}<\dots<t_{n}=T
is a uniform partition of
[0,T]
, i.e.
t_{k+1}-t_{k}=h
for all
k=0,\dots,n-1
. We write the continuous version of the numerical scheme (
2.2.3
) as follows:

\displaystyle dY^{i}_{N}(t)

\displaystyle=-\beta(t)(Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(t))-\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(t)))dt+\sqrt{2}\sigma(t)\diag(Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(t))-\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(t)))dW^{i}(t)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\diag(Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(t))-\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(t)))z\mathcal{N}^{i}(dt,dz).

(4.47)

In this section, our aim is to show mean-square convergence of
Y^{i}_{N}(t)
to
X^{i}_{N}(t)
uniformly in
N
, i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}|Y^{i}_{N}(t)-X^{i}_{N}(t)|^{2}=0,

(4.48)

where
h\rightarrow 0
means that keeping
T
fixed the time-step of uniform partition of
[0,T]
goes to zero.

Let Assumptions
3.1
-
3.2
hold. Let
\mathbb{E}|Y^{i}_{N}(0)|^{2}<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}|Z|^{2}<\infty
, then the particle system (
4.3
) is well-posed (cf. Theorem
3.2
). Moreover, if
\mathbb{E}|Y^{i}_{N}(0)|^{2p}<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}|Z|^{2p}<\infty
for some
p\geq 1
, then, due to Lemma
3.3
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{i}_{N}(t)|^{2p}\leq K,

(4.49)

where we cannot say that
K
is independent of
h
. However, to prove the convergence of numerical scheme we need the uniform in
h
and
N
moment bound, which we prove in the next lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let Assumptions
3.1
,
3.3
-
3.5
hold. Let
p\geq 1
,
\mathbb{E}|Y^{i}_{N}(0)|^{2p}<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}|Z|^{2p}<\infty
. Then, the following holds:

\displaystyle\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{i}_{N}(t)|^{2p}\leq K_{d},

(4.50)

where
K_{d}
is a positive constant independent of
h
and
N
.

Proof.

Let
p
be a positive integer. Using Ito’s formula, the Cauchy-Bunyakowsky-Schwartz inequality and Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle|Y^{i}_{N}

\displaystyle(t)|^{2p}=|Y^{i}_{N}(0)|^{2p}-2p\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p-2}\big(Y^{i}_{N}(s)\cdot(Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))-\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s)))\big)ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+2\sqrt{2}p\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s)|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p-2}\big(Y^{i}_{N}(s)\cdot\diag(Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))-\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s)))dW^{i}(s)\big)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+4p(p-1)\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}(s)|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p-4}|\diag(Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))-\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s)))Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+2p\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}(s)|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p-2}|\diag(Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))-\bar{Y}_{N}(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(|Y^{i}_{N}(s^{-})+\gamma(s)\diag(Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))-\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s)))z|^{2p}-|Y^{i}_{N}(s^{-})|^{2p}\Big)\mathcal{N}^{i}(ds,dz)

\displaystyle\leq|Y^{i}_{N}(0)|^{2p}+C\int_{0}^{t}(|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p}+|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p}+|\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p})ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+2\sqrt{2}p\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s)|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p-2}(Y^{i}_{N}(s)\cdot\diag(Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))-\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s)))dW^{i}(s))

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(|Y^{i}_{N}(s^{-})|^{2p}+(|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p}+|\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p})(1+|z|^{2p})\Big)\mathcal{N}^{i}(ds,dz).

First taking supremum over
0\leq t\leq T
and then expectation, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{i}_{N}(t)|^{2p}\leq\mathbb{E}|Y^{i}_{N}(0)|^{2p}+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p}+|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p}+|\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p}\Big)ds

\displaystyle+2\sqrt{2}p\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s)|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p-2}(Y^{i}_{N}(s)\cdot\diag(Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))-\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s)))dW^{i}(s))\bigg|

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(|Y^{i}_{N}(s^{-})|^{2p}+(|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p}+|\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p})(1+|z|^{2p})\Big)\mathcal{N}^{i}(ds,dz),

where
C
is independent of
h
and
N
.
Using the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality (note that we can apply this inequality due to (
4.49
)) and the fact that
\mathbb{E}|Z|^{2p}<\infty
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{i}_{N}(t)|^{2p}\leq\mathbb{E}|Y^{i}_{N}(0)|^{2p}+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p}+|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p}+|\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p}\Big)ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+C\mathbb{E}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{4p-4}\big(Y^{i}_{N}(s)\cdot(Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))-\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s)))\big)^{2}ds\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p}+(|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p}+|\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p})(1+|z|^{2p})\Big)\rho_{z}(z)dzds

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}|Y^{i}_{N}(0)|^{2p}+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p}+|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p}+|\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p}\Big)ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{i}_{N}(t)|^{2p-1}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))-\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2}ds\bigg)^{1/2}.

Applying Young’s inequality and Holder’s inequality,
we ascertain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{i}_{N}(t)|^{2p}\leq\mathbb{E}|Y_{N}^{i}(0)|^{2p}+C\int_{0}^{T}(|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p}+|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p}+|\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p})ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{i}_{N}(t)|^{2p}+C\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))-\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2}ds\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}|Y_{N}^{i}(0)|^{2p}+C\int_{0}^{t}(|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p}+|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p}+|\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p})ds

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{i}_{N}(t)|^{2p}+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))-\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p}ds.

(4.51)

Using Jensen’s inequality and (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle|\bar{Y}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2}\frac{e^{-\alpha f(Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s)))}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha f(Y^{j}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s)))}}\leq L_{1}+\frac{L_{2}}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2}.

(4.52)

Therefore, substituting (
4.52
) in (
4.51
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{i}_{N}(t)|^{2p}\leq 2\mathbb{E}|Y_{N}^{i}(0)|^{2p}+C+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(|Y^{i}_{N}(s)|^{2p}+|Y^{i}_{N}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{p}+\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|Y_{N}^{i}(\kappa_{h}(s))|^{2p}\Big)ds

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}|Y_{N}^{i}(0)|^{2p}+C+C\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq u\leq s}|Y^{i}_{N}(u)|^{2p}+\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq u\leq s}|Y_{N}^{i}(u)|^{2p}\Big)ds,

where
C>0
is independent of
h
and
N
.
Taking supremum over
i=1,\dots,N
, we get

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{i}_{N}(t)|^{2p}\leq 2\mathbb{E}|Y^{i}_{N}(0)|^{2p}+C+C\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{i=1,\dots,N}\mathbb{E}\sup_{0\leq u\leq s}|Y^{i}_{N}(u)|^{2p}ds,

where
C>0
is independent of
h
and
N
.
Using Grönwall’s lemma, we have the desired result.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let Assumptions
3.1
,
3.3
-

6
Concluding remarks

We have developed a new CBO algorithm with jump-diffusion SDEs, for which we have studied its well-posedness both at the particle level and its mean-field approximation. The key feature of the jump-diffusion CBO is a more effective energy landscape exploration driven by the randomness introduced by both Wiener and Poisson processes. In practice, this translates into better success rates in finding the global minimizer, and a more robust initialization, which can be located far away from the global minimizer.
A natural extension of the current work is a systematic study of CBO with constraints in the search space as recently discussed in
[
GP21
,
CTV21
,
FHPS21
,
BHK+22
]
. This is particularly challenging because of the need to accurately treat boundary conditions for the SDEs (see e.g.
[
MT21
]
). Another interesting research direction is the exploration of jump-diffusion processes in the framework of kinetic-type CBO models
[
BBP22
,
KHJK22
]
.

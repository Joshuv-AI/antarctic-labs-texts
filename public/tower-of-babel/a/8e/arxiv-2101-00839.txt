The Green-Tao theorem for affine curves over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q}
Wataru Kai

Abstract
. Green and Tao famously proved in a 2008 paper that there are arithmetic progressions of prime numbers of arbitrary lengths. Soon after, analogous statements were proved by Tao for the ring of Gaussian integers and by Lê for the polynomial rings over finite fields. In 2020 this was extented to orders of arbitrary number fields by Kai-Mimura-Munemasa-Seki-Yoshino. We settle the case of the coordinate rings of affine curves over finite fields. The main contribution of this paper is subtle choice of a polynomial subring of the given ring which plays the role of ℤ \mathbb{Z} in the number field case. This choice and the proof of its pleasant properties eventually depend on the Riemann-Roch formula.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p
be a prime.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{0}
be an integral domain finitely generated over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and whose fraction field has transcendence degree
1
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Then for any positive integer
k\geq 1
, the set of prime elements of
\mathcal{O}_{0}
contains
a
k
-dimensional affine subset.

Recall that an
affine subset
of a vector space is by definition a translate of a vector subspace (necessarily unique).
Its
dimension
is defined to be that of the corresponding vector subspace.

We actually prove a density version of the theorem which we now formulate.
Let
\mathcal{O}
be the integral closure of
\mathcal{O}_{0}
in its fraction field.
It is a Dedekind domain finite over
\mathcal{O}_{0}
.
There is a canonical linear norm, defined in §
2
,

(1)

\left\|-\right\|\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

which gives an increasing exhaustive filtration by finite subsets

\mathcal{O}_{\leq N}:=\left\{\alpha\in\mathcal{O}\mid\left\|\alpha\right\|\leq N\right\}\quad(N\geq 0).

For an inclusion
A\subset X\neq\varnothing
of subsets of
\mathcal{O}
,
one can consider the
upper relative density
:

(2)

\bar{\delta}_{X}(A):=\limsup_{N\to+\infty}\frac{\left|A\cap\mathcal{O}_{\leq N}\right|}{\left|X\cap\mathcal{O}_{\leq N}\right|}.

The density statement is formulated as follows.

Theorem 1.2

(Green-Tao theorem in positive characteristic; see Theorem
5.2
)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a Dedekind domain finitely generated over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}}
be the set of its prime elements.
Then every subset
A\subset\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}}
with
\bar{\delta}_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}}}(A)>0

contains a
k
-dimensional affine subset for an arbitrary
k\geq 0
.

This implies Theorem
1.1
because the prime elements of
\mathcal{O}_{0}
have positive upper density in

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}}
as we recall in §
5.2
.

In fact, in our proof of Theorem
1.2
,
we search for
k
-dimensional affine subsets of a very specific form.

Definition 1.3
.

For a subring
\mathfrak{o}\subset\mathcal{O}
and a finite subset

,
by an
\mathfrak{o}
-homothetic copy of

of the form

(3)

a\cdot S+\beta=\left\{a\alpha+\beta\mid\alpha\in S\right\}

a\in\mathfrak{o}
and
\beta\in\mathcal{O}
.
Let us say it is

For later use, note that these notions make perfect sense for any integral domain

, a torsion-free
\mathfrak{o}
-module

For a suitable

-homothetic copy of it is a

-dimensional affine subset.
This is why we propose to call Theorem

In

Here, the relative Szemerédi theorem (Theorem

The construction of
\lambda\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is ideal-theoretic in nature. The proof that it is pseudorandom ultimately relies on the knowledge that the zeta function
\zeta_{\mathcal{O}}
has a simple pole at

That the prime elements have positive density with respect to
\lambda

A pitfall in the construction of
\lambda
is that there is a somewhat natural fuction
\lambda^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
but
we are not going to use it directly
because we do not know if it is pseudorandom in our sense.
Instead, we choose an element
W\in\mathfrak{o}
with sufficiently many different prime factors
and

coprime to
W
,
and define the function
\lambda
as the composite
\mathcal{O}\xrightarrow{W(-)+b}\mathcal{O}\xrightarrow{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
times a normalizing factor.
This enables us to prove the pseudorandomness.
Thus, the set
A\subset\mathcal{O}
in the relative Szemerédi theorem is going to be taken as the inverse image of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}}
by the affine linear map
W(-)+b
;
this is of course equivalent to considering the prime elements which are congruent to
b

W
.
The reader will see how this trick (so-called
W

–
5

Notation

For a function on a non-empty finite set
f\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
, we use the standard expectation notation:

(5)

\mathbb{E}(f\,|\,X)=\mathbb{E}(f(x)\,|\,x\in X):=\frac{1}{\left|X\right|}\sum_{x\in X}f(x).

From §
4

O
notation with dependence parameters
as in
[
6
, Notation in §2]
:
let

be
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions on a set

which depend on additional parameters

.
Assume that the values of

are positive real numbers.
We write

(6)

f(x)=O_{b,c,\dots}(g(x))\quad(x\in X)

to mean that there is a positive constant
C=C_{b,c,\dots}>0
depending only on the parameters in the subscript
such that the inequality
|f(x)|<Cg(x)
holds for all
x\in X
.
(Thus in this case the implied constant

can be taken independent of

An expression like
O_{b,c,\dots}(1)
would mean a positive constant depending only on the parameters
b,c,\dots
in the subscript.
Note in particular that

without subscript would mean an absolute constant depending on nothing at all.
When

is a heavy formula, the notation

is sometimes preferred.
A quantity written in the form

is one whose difference with

When we say something like “

we are taking the domain

to be a subset of

consisting of the pairs of real numbers larger than certain thresholds.
In practice, the thresholds often depend on the parameters

. We usually indicate how the thresholds depend on the parameters, especially when that piece of information is relevant.

2.1.
The canonical linear norm

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a
Dedekind domain
finitely generated over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
The purpose of this subsection is to describe the canonical submultiplicative linear norm
on the ring
\mathcal{O}
.

By a
linear norm
or an
ultrametric norm
on an abelian group
G
let us mean a non-negatively valued function
\left\|-\right\|\colon G\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

which is
ultrametric
:

(7)

\left\|a+b\right\|\leq\max\left\{\left\|a\right\|,\,\left\|b\right\|\right\}\quad\text{ for all }a,b\in G

and
non-degenerate
in that the only element with norm
0
is the zero element.
A
submultiplicative
linear norm on an (always commutative) ring
A
is a linear norm on the abelian group
A
which moreover satisfies

(8)

\left\|\alpha\beta\right\|\leq\left\|\alpha\right\|\cdot\left\|\beta\right\|.

In our examples the multiplicative unit will always have norm
1
.
A norm
\left\|-\right\|
is said to be
multiplicative
if the above inequality is always an equality.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the integral closure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
in
\mathcal{O}
.
Let
\overline{X}
be the complete non-singular curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
which contains

X:=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}
as an open subscheme.
Let
n
be the cardinality of the complement:

(9)

n:=\left|\overline{X}\smallsetminus X\right|.

Regard each
v\in\overline{X}
as a discrete valuation
\operatorname{Frac}(\mathcal{O})^{*}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\mathbb{F}(v)
be its residue field and

\deg(v):=[\mathbb{F}(v):\mathbb{F}_{q}]

its degree.
Define a linear norm
\|-\|_{v}
on
\mathcal{O}
by:

(10)

\left\|-\right\|_{v}\colon\mathcal{O}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R};\quad\alpha\mapsto\left(\frac{1}{\left|\mathbb{F}(v)\right|}\right)^{v(\alpha)}=q^{-v(\alpha)\cdot\deg(v)}.

The value
\left\|0\right\|_{v}
is understood to be
0
.
The
canonical norm

\left\|-\right\|
on
\mathcal{O}
is defined by

(11)

\left\|\alpha\right\|:=\max_{v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X}\{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{v}\}.

This is a submultiplicative linear norm because the following formulas hold
for all
v\in\overline{X}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Frac}(\mathcal{O})^{*}
:

(12)

v(\alpha+\beta)\geq\min\left\{v(\alpha),v(\beta)\right\},\quad v(\alpha\beta)=v(\alpha)+v(\beta).

Define the
norm
of a non-zero ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}
as the cardinality of the quotient

\Nrm(\mathfrak{a}):=\left|\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{a}\right|
,
and for
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}\smallsetminus\{0\}
write
\Nrm(\alpha):=\Nrm(\alpha\mathcal{O})
for short.
By convention we define
\Nrm(0):=0
.
We call it the
ideal norm
of
\alpha
to avoid confusion with the linear norm
\left\|-\right\|
.
For
\alpha\neq 0
we know

\Nrm(\alpha)=\prod_{v\in X}\left|\mathbb{F}(v)\right|^{v(\alpha)}

say by prime decomposition of ideals in
\mathcal{O}
.
It follows by the product formula for complete algebraic curves (e.g.
[
8
, Proposition 5.1, p.47]
) that the following equality holds for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
:

(13)

\Nrm(\alpha)=\prod_{v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X}\left\|\alpha\right\|_{v}.

In particular
\alpha
is in
\mathcal{O}^{*}
if and only if
\prod_{v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X}\left\|\alpha\right\|_{v}=1
.

For positive real numbers
N>0
, set:

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\leq N}:=

\displaystyle\left\{\alpha\in\mathcal{O}\mid\left\|\alpha\right\|\leq N\right\}

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\alpha\in\mathcal{O}\mid v(\alpha)+\frac{\log_{q}N}{\deg(v)}\geq 0\text{ for all }v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X\right\}.

By the Riemann-Roch theorem for curves
[
8
, Corollary 4 of Theorem 5.4, p.49]
,
we know that
\left|\mathcal{O}_{\leq N}\right|
is approximately proportional to
N^{n}
.
To be more precise, consider the following invariants:

(16)

\begin{array}[]{cl}g&:=\text{ the genus of $\overline{X}$},\\
d_{0}&:=\text{ the least common multiple of $\deg(v)$'s for $v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X$}.\end{array}

Let us denote by
\lfloor-\rfloor
the floor function

x\mapsto
(the largest integer not exceeding
x
)
and
consider the next divisor on
\overline{X}
for
N\geq 1
:

(17)

D_{N}:=\sum_{v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X}\left\lfloor\frac{\log_{q}N}{\deg(v)}\right\rfloor v.

Its degree is
\sum_{v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X}\left\lfloor\frac{\log_{q}N}{\deg(v)}\right\rfloor\deg(v)

which is
\leq\log_{q}N\cdot\left|\overline{X}\smallsetminus X\right|=n\log_{q}N
.
We have by definition
\mathcal{O}_{\leq N}=\Gamma(\overline{X},\mathcal{O}_{\overline{X}}(D_{N}))
,
or
\mathcal{O}_{\leq N}=L(D_{N})
in the notation of
[
8
]
.
Therefore by Riemann-Roch
[
8
, Corollary 4 of Theorem 5.4, p.49]

we get
\left|\mathcal{O}_{\leq N}\right|=N^{n}/q^{g-1}

for every
N\geq q^{(2g-1)/n}
which is a power of
q^{d_{0}}
.
From this we also get the following bound valid for all real numbers
N\geq q^{(2g-1)/n}
:

(18)

\left(\frac{N}{q^{d_{0}}}\right)^{n}/q^{g-1}<\left|\mathcal{O}_{\leq N}\right|\leq N^{n}/q^{g-1}.

When we consider a non-zero ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}

(which is relevant only if the reader is interested in the case
\mathfrak{a}\neq\mathcal{O}
of Theorem
5.2
),
we endow
\mathfrak{a}
the induced linear norm and write

(19)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\leq N}:=

\displaystyle\left\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}\mid\left\|\alpha\right\|\leq N\right\}

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}\mid v(\alpha)+\frac{\log_{q}N}{\deg(v)}\geq 0\text{ for all }v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X\right\}.

By the Riemann-Roch formula again (or from (
18
)) we get

(21)

\frac{N^{n}}{\Nrm(\mathfrak{a})q^{nd_{0}+g-1}}<\left|\mathfrak{a}_{\leq N}\right|\leq\frac{N^{n}}{\Nrm(\mathfrak{a})q^{g-1}}\quad\text{ if }N^{n}\geq\Nrm(\mathfrak{a})q^{2g-1}.

Remark 1
.

The use of the canonical norm among other norms is not essential.
We could have chosen an arbitrary positive intger
d_{v}\geq 1
for each
v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X
and defined

\left\|\alpha\right\|_{v}:=q^{-d_{v}\cdot v(\alpha)}
.
The content of this paper would remain valid with minor modifications.
However, it did not seem appealing to the author to allow the freedom of this choice
at the cost of heavier notation.

Remark 2
.

The
A=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}}
case of Theorem
1.2
can be reduced to the case where
n=\left|\overline{X}\smallsetminus X\right|=1
(with a general
A\subset\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}}
),
for which the treatment in the rest of §
2
can be much simpler
because then we have
\left\|-\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}=\left\|-\right\|_{v}=\Nrm(-)
(where
v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X
is the unique element).
Since we eventually prove Theorem
1.2
in full strength, we only give a sketch of this reduction argument.
Take any point
v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X
and set
X^{\prime}:=\overline{X}\smallsetminus\left\{v\right\}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}:=\Gamma(X^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\overline{X}})
.
We have a canonical injection
i\colon\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}
and know that
all but finitely many associate classes (corresponding to a subset of
X^{\prime}\smallsetminus X
) of prime elements of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
remain prime elements in
\mathcal{O}
.
Since
\mathcal{O}^{\prime*}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, those exceptional prime elements are finite in number, so in particular have density zero in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}^{-}
be the set of remaining prime elements.
Now we apply Theorem
1.2
to
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}^{-}\subset\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}

to find a
k
-dimensional affine subset contained in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}^{-}
.
Since the canonical injection
i
(which is of course
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear) carries
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}^{-}
into
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}}
,
we have found a
k
-dimensional affine subset in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}}
.
This proves Theorem
1.2
for
\mathcal{O}
in the special case
A=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}}
.

2.2.
The choice of a subring

The constellation theorem
[
6
]
for the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of a number field
K

ensures that the set of prime elements of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
contains a
\mathbb{Z}
-homothetic copy of any given finite subset
S\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
In this subsection we choose a subring
\mathfrak{o}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
of
\mathcal{O}
which plays the role of

\mathbb{Z}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Recall the definition

X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}
and that
\overline{X}
is the complete non-singular curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

containing
X
as an open subscheme.
Also
n=\left|\overline{X}\smallsetminus X\right|
.

Proposition 2.1
.

There exists an element
t\in\mathcal{O}
such that the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\mathcal{O}
is finite over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, say of rank
r
;

(2)

the value
\left\|t\right\|_{v}
is independent of
v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X
, say
q^{d}
.

Furthermore we have
r=nd
and
the canonical linear norms of
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathfrak{o}:=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
satisfy
the following compatibility:
if we write
\left\|-\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}
for the canonical norm of
\mathcal{O}
and
\left\|-\right\|_{\mathfrak{o}}
for that of
\mathfrak{o}
, then
we have
\left\|-\right\|_{\mathfrak{o}}^{d}=\left\|-\right\|_{v}
as functions on
\mathfrak{o}
for all
v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X
and in particular

\left\|-\right\|_{\mathfrak{o}}^{d}=\left\|-\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}
.

Proof.

Let
d
be a large enough common multiple of
\deg(v)
(
v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X
).
We claim that there exists a function
\phi\in\mathcal{O}
which has a pole at each
v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X
of order exactly
d/\deg(v)
.
For this, for each
v
consider the set
\Gamma(\overline{X},\mathcal{O}_{\overline{X}}(\frac{d}{\deg v}v))
of rational functions on
\overline{X}

whose only possible pole is
v
with order
\leq d/\deg(v)
.
By the Riemann-Roch formula
[
8
, Corollary 4 to Theorem 5.4, p.49]
, if
d
is large enough the inclusion

(22)

\Gamma\left(\overline{X},\mathcal{O}_{\overline{X}}(\left(\frac{d}{\deg v}-1\right)v)\right)\quad\subset\quad\Gamma\left(\overline{X},\mathcal{O}_{\overline{X}}(\frac{d}{\deg v}v)\right)

is a proper one so there is a function
\phi_{v}
whose only pole is at
v
and of order exactly
d/\deg(v)
.
Choose one such
\phi_{v}
for each
v
with a common
d
.
Then the function
\phi:=\sum_{v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X}\phi_{v}
has the claimed property.

Denote also by
\phi
the corresponding finite map of curves
\phi\colon\overline{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
By the choice of
\phi
we have an equality of divisors on
\overline{X}
:

(23)

\phi^{*}(\infty)=\sum_{v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X}\frac{d}{\deg(v)}v=:D.

One can also see that the degree
r
of the map
\phi
equals
\deg(D)=nd
.
Let
t
be the coordinate of
\mathbb{A}^{1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
We have
\phi^{-1}(\mathbb{A}^{1})=\overline{X}\smallsetminus|D|=X

so
t
can be seen as an element of
\mathcal{O}
and assertion (
1
) holds.

Next, let
f(t)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
be a polynomial of degree
e
.
Since the valuation
v_{\infty}\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}
at
\infty\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
agrees with the degree function when restricted to
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
, we have
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathfrak{o}}:=\left\|f\right\|_{v_{\infty}}=q^{e}
.
By (
23
) we know
v(f)=-\frac{de}{\deg(v)}
for each
v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X
. It follows that

(24)

\left\|f\right\|_{v}=\left({q^{\deg(v)}}\right)^{\frac{de}{\deg(v)}}=q^{de}=\left\|f\right\|_{\mathfrak{o}}^{d}

for all
v
.
Therefore the assertion (
2
) and the compatibility assertion holds.
∎

We fix an
\mathfrak{o}\subset\mathcal{O}
as in Proposition
2.1
throughout the paper.
To avoid overloaded notation, we will avoid the use of the canonical norm of
\mathfrak{o}
as much as possible and reserve the symbol
\left\|-\right\|
for the canonical norm of
\mathcal{O}
.
As a consequence we use the following potentially confusing piece of notation:

(25)

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathfrak{o}_{\leq N}&:=\left\{f\in\mathfrak{o}\mid\left\|f\right\|\leq N\right\}\\
&=\left\{f\in\mathfrak{o}\mid\left\|f\right\|_{\mathfrak{o}}\leq N^{1/d}\right\}.\end{array}

Note that therefore the cardinality
\left|\mathfrak{o}_{\leq N}\right|
is equal, up to a bounded constant, to

N^{1/d}
.
Despite this potential confusion, this notation is convenient in the bulk of our discussion.

2.3.
Equivalence of linear norms

Let
\mathfrak{o}\subset\mathcal{O}
be as in Proposition
2.1
.
We know
\mathcal{O}
is a free
\mathfrak{o}
-module of rank
r
.
Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in\mathcal{O}
be a basis.
One can consider the
max norm
on
\mathcal{O}
with respect to this basis:

(26)

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}\alpha_{i}\right\|_{\bm{\alpha}}:=\max_{i}\{\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}\}.

It is an ultrametric norm on the abelian group
\mathcal{O}
.

Let us recall that two norms
\left\|-\right\|_{1}
and
\left\|-\right\|_{2}

on an abelian group
G
are said to be
equivalent

if there are positive real numbers
c,C>0
such that
the next inequality holds on
G
:

(27)

c\left\|-\right\|_{2}\quad\leq\quad\left\|-\right\|_{1}\quad\leq\quad C\left\|-\right\|_{2}.

It is easy to see that the equivalence class of the norm
\left\|-\right\|_{\bm{\alpha}}
is independent of the choice of the basis

\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r})
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For any given
\mathfrak{o}
-basis
\bm{\alpha}
of
\mathcal{O}
,
the associated norm
\left\|-\right\|_{\bm{\alpha}}
is equivalent to the canonical norm
\left\|-\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}
.

Proof.

First, if
\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}\leq N
holds for all
i
,
then by the ultrametricity and submultiplicativity of
\left\|-\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}
we have

(28)

\left\|\sum_{i}f_{i}\alpha_{i}\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}\leq N\cdot\left(\max_{i}\left\|\alpha_{i}\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}\right)

so that we have
\left\|-\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}\leq\left\|-\right\|_{\bm{\alpha}}\cdot\left(\max_{i}\left\|\alpha_{i}\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}\right)
.

The inequality in the other direction is slightly harder.
For a notational reason, let us introduce
the degree function
\deg\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\left\{-\infty\right\}
defined by

\deg(\alpha):=\log_{q}(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}).

For integers
M\geq 0
, denote by
\mathcal{O}_{\deg\leq M}
the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector subspace of elements with degree
\leq M
;
of course one has
\mathcal{O}_{\deg\leq M}=\mathcal{O}_{\leq(q^{M})}
.
Note that by Proposition
2.1
(
2
), the element
t\in\mathcal{O}
is
multiplicative
in the sense that the equality

(29)

\left\|t\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}=\left\|t\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}\cdot\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{O}},\quad\text{i.e., }\deg(t\alpha)=\deg(t)+\deg(\alpha)

holds for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
rather than a mere inequality.
(Actually,
all
elements of
\mathfrak{o}
are multiplicative by (
24
).)
It follows that the following mutiplication by
t
map
is injective for all
M\geq 0
, where we recall from Proposition
2.1
that
d=\deg(t)
:

(30)

\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\deg\leq M}}{\mathcal{O}_{\deg\leq M-d}}\xrightarrow{\times t}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\deg\leq M+d}}{\mathcal{O}_{\deg\leq M}}.

We claim that it is also surjective for all sufficiently large
M\geq 0
.
There are at least two ways to see this.
One is to use the Riemann-Roch theorem which tells us that both sides
of (
30
) have the same dimension for
M
large enough.

The second is more down-to-earth.
Let
M_{0}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq r}\{\deg(\alpha_{i})\}
and suppose
M\geq M_{0}
.
Write an arbitrary element
\alpha\in\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\deg\leq M+d}}{\mathcal{O}_{\deg\leq M}}

in the form

(31)

\alpha=\sum_{i}f_{i}(t)\alpha_{i}\mod\mathcal{O}_{\deg\leq M},\quad f_{i}(t)\in\mathfrak{o}.

Since
\alpha_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{\deg\leq M_{0}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\deg\leq M}

is zero in the group in question,
we may assume
f_{i}(t)
has no constant term.
Then we have a well-defined element
\alpha/t:=\sum_{i}\frac{f_{i}(t)}{t}\cdot\alpha_{i}
which is in
\mathcal{O}_{\deg\leq M}

because
t
is a multiplicative element.
Then
\alpha
is the image of
\alpha/t
under the map (
30
).

In any case let
M_{0}
be such that (
30
) is surjective for all
M\geq M_{0}
.
Now since
\mathcal{O}_{\deg\leq M_{0}}
is a finite set, there trivially exists an
e_{0}\geq 0
such that
all
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{\deg\leq M_{0}}
can be written in the form

(32)

\alpha=\sum_{i}f_{i}(t)\alpha_{i}\quad\text{ with }\deg(f_{i}(t))\leq\deg(\alpha)+e_{0}.

By induction on
\deg(\alpha)
using the bijection (
30
), the same holds for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
.
In multiplicative terms, this precisely says there is a positive constant
C=q^{e_{0}}
such that

(33)

\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\bm{\alpha}}\leq C\cdot\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}\quad\text{ for all }\alpha\in\mathcal{O}.

This complets the proof of Proposition
2.2
.
∎

For the “prime elements in an ideal” case of Theorem
5.2
, let
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}
be a non-zero ideal. It is also a rank
r
free
\mathfrak{o}
-module.
We can consider the restriction of the canonical norm
\left\|-\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}
to
\mathfrak{a}

and the max norm
\left\|-\right\|_{\bm{\beta}}
with respect to an
\mathfrak{o}
-basis
\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r})
of
\mathfrak{a}
.

Corollary 2.3
.

The two linear norms
\left\|-\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}
and
\left\|-\right\|_{\bm{\beta}}
on
\mathfrak{a}
are equivalent.

Proof.

While the proof of Proposition
2.2
works for this case just as well,
here we present a proof using the proposition.
Let
\bm{\alpha}
continue to be an
\mathfrak{o}
-basis of
\mathcal{O}
.
By Proposition
2.2
, it suffices to show that the restriction of
\left\|-\right\|_{\bm{\alpha}}
to
\mathfrak{a}

and
\left\|-\right\|_{\bm{\beta}}
are equivalent.

Each
\beta_{i}
can be written (uniquely) as
\beta_{i}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}g_{ij}\alpha_{j}
.
Take a positive number
C
such that
C\geq\left\|g_{ij}\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}
for all
i,j
.
For an element
x=\sum_{i}f_{i}\beta_{i}=\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{i}f_{i}g_{ij}\right)\alpha_{j}
,
by the ultrametricity of
\left\|-\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}
and the choice of
C
we have:

(34)

\left\|x\right\|_{\bm{\alpha}}=\max_{j}\left\{\left\|\sum_{i}f_{i}g_{ij}\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}\right\}\leq\max_{i,j}\left\{\left\|f_{i}g_{ij}\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}\right\}\leq C\max_{i}\left\{\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}\right\}=C\left\|x\right\|_{\bm{\beta}}.

Next, by the theory of finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain (say),
we know that there is a non-zero element
f(t)\in\mathfrak{o}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
such that
f(t)\mathcal{O}\subset\mathfrak{a}
.
The previous argument applied to the element
f(t)x\in f(t)\mathcal{O}
gives

\left\|f(t)x\right\|_{\bm{\beta}}\leq C\left\|f(t)x\right\|_{f(t)\bm{\alpha}}
.
By the definition of the max norm we can isolate the
f(t)
-factors so that:

(35)

\left\|x\right\|_{\bm{\beta}}\leq\frac{C}{\left\|f(t)\right\|_{\mathcal{O}}}\cdot\left\|x\right\|_{\bm{\alpha}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3
.

Propositoin
2.2
fails if
\mathfrak{o}
is not chosen as in Proposition
2.1
even if
\mathcal{O}
is finite over
\mathfrak{o}
.
For example, set
\mathfrak{o}:=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
and
\mathcal{O}:=\mathbb{F}_{q}[s,s^{-1}]
and consider the homomorphism

(36)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{o}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}

(37)

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{(s-1)^{3}}{s}.

If we let
f
be the induced finite map
f\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we have
f^{*}(\left\{\infty\right\})=2\left\{\infty\right\}+\left\{0\right\}
as divisors.
The element
t
is not a multiplicative element for the canonical norm
\left\|-\right\|
because for example
\left\|t/s\right\|=\left\|(s-1)^{3}/s^{2}\right\|=q^{2}
which is not equal to

\left\|t\right\|\cdot\left\|1/s\right\|=q^{2}\cdot q^{1}
.
Instead
t
is a multiplicative element for the following linear norm
\left\|-\right\|^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
:

(38)

\left\|\alpha\right\|^{\prime}:=\max\left\{q^{-v_{\infty}(\alpha)},q^{-2v_{0}(\alpha)}\right\}.

The arguments of the proof of Proposition
2.2
show that
the max norm on
\mathcal{O}\cong\mathfrak{o}^{3}
is equivalent to
\left\|-\right\|^{\prime}
.
However, one easily sees that
\left\|-\right\|^{\prime}
is not equivalent to
\left\|-\right\|
;
for example, one has
\left\|1/s^{n}\right\|=q^{n}
and
\left\|1/s^{n}\right\|^{\prime}=q^{2n}
so their ratio is not bounded.

Recall from (
25
) that we endow
\mathfrak{o}
with the induced norm from
\mathcal{O}

and so we have
N^{1/d}<\left|\mathfrak{o}_{\leq N}\right|\leq q\cdot N^{1/d}
where the right hand inequality becomes an equality when
N
is a power of
q^{d}
.
Let us note the following simple observation.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
t,r\geq 1
be positive integers and
\phi\colon\mathfrak{o}^{t}\to\mathfrak{o}^{r}

a surjective
\mathfrak{o}
-linear map.
Then there exists a positive number
U>0
such that for all
N>0
and
x\in(\mathfrak{o}_{\leq N})^{r}
,
the following set

(39)

\phi^{-1}(x)\cap(\mathfrak{o}_{\leq UN})^{t}

contains at least
\left|\mathfrak{o}_{\leq N}\right|^{t-r}
elements.

Proof.

Let us denote by
\left\|-\right\|_{\mathfrak{o}^{t}}
and
\left\|-\right\|_{\mathfrak{o}^{r}}
the max norms on
\mathfrak{o}^{t}
and
\mathfrak{o}^{r}
with respect to the standard bases.
Choose an
\mathfrak{o}
-linear section
\sigma\colon\mathfrak{o}^{r}\to\mathfrak{o}^{t}
to
\phi
.
Let
\bm{e}_{i}\in\mathfrak{o}^{r}
be the standard basis (
1\leq i\leq r
)
and set
\bm{f}_{i}:=\sigma(\bm{e}_{i})
.
We know
\ker(\phi)
is a free
\mathfrak{o}
-module of rank
t-r
;
choose a basis
\bm{f}_{r+1},\dots,\bm{f}_{t}
of
\ker(\phi)
.
Let
U>0
be larger than
\left\|\bm{f}_{i}\right\|_{\mathfrak{o}^{t}}
for all
1\leq i\leq t
.
We claim this
U
works.

Suppose we are given
N>0
and
x\in(\mathfrak{o}_{\leq N})^{r}
.
Writing
x
in the form
x=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\bm{e}_{i}
, we see

(40)

\left\|\sigma(x)\right\|_{\mathfrak{o}^{t}}=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\bm{f}_{i}\right\|_{\mathfrak{o}^{t}}\leq(\max_{i}\left\|a_{i}\right\|)\cdot U\leq NU.

Also, for each choice of
a_{r+1},\dots,a_{t}\in\mathfrak{o}_{\leq N}

we have by the same reasoning:

\left\|\sum_{i=r+1}^{t}a_{i}\bm{f}_{i}\right\|_{\mathfrak{o}^{t}}\leq UN.

Therefore we get
\left\|\sigma(x)+\sum_{i=r+1}^{t}a_{i}\bm{f}_{i}\right\|_{\mathfrak{o}^{t}}\leq UN

for all choices of
a_{i}
’s as above.
Since this last element is in
\phi^{-1}(x)
, our claim follows.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\phi\colon\mathfrak{o}^{t}\to\mathfrak{a}

be a surjective
\mathfrak{o}
-linear map.
Then there exists a positive number
U>0
such that for all
N>0
and
x\in\mathfrak{a}_{\leq N}
,
the following set

(41)

\phi^{-1}(x)\cap(\mathfrak{o}_{\leq UN})^{t}

contains at leat
\left|\mathfrak{o}_{\leq N}\right|^{t-r}

elements.

Proof.

This immediately follows from Proposition
2.2
, Corollary
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
.
∎

2.4.
“Geometry of numbers”

Consider the group homomorphism
\phi\colon\mathcal{O}^{*}\to\bigoplus_{v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X}\mathbb{Z}
defined by

\alpha\mapsto(v(\alpha))_{v}
.
It is known that its image has rank
n-1
:

(42)

{\mathsf{rank}}\bigl(\image(\phi)\bigr)=n-1.

For a proof, see
[
8
, Proposition 14.2, p.243]
.
For the convenience of the reader
let us recall a proof in the language of sheaves.
Let
j\colon X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}\hookrightarrow\overline{X}
be the open immersion.
By the short exact sequence of sheaves on
\overline{X}

(where
i_{v}\colon\left\{v\right\}\hookrightarrow\overline{X}
is the closed immersion):

(43)

1\to\mathcal{O}_{\overline{X}}^{*}\to j_{*}\mathcal{O}^{*}_{X}\xrightarrow{\phi}\bigoplus_{v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X}i_{v*}\mathbb{Z}\to 0,

we get an exact sequence
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}^{*}\xrightarrow{\phi}\bigoplus_{v}\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\delta}\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{X})
.
Since
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{X})=\mathbb{Z}\oplus(\text{finite})

and the connecting map
\delta
has nontrivial image into the
\mathbb{Z}
-part,
the claim (
42
) follows.

Now consider the map
\mathcal{L}\colon\mathcal{O}\smallsetminus\{0\}\to\bigoplus_{v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X}\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

(44)

\mathcal{L}(\alpha):=(\log\left\|\alpha\right\|_{v})_{v}.

It is an obvious analog of the multiplicative Minkowski map
\mathcal{L}
that was also used in
[
6
, §4]
.
By (
13
) we know that
\mathcal{L}
maps the subset
\mathcal{O}^{*}
into the hyperplane
H
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

defined by:

(45)

H=\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mid x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}=0\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

and by (
42
) that
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{O}^{*})\subset H
is a full-rank lattice.

Let us say a subset
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{O}\smallsetminus\{0\}
is
norm-length compatible

if the set

(46)

\left\{\frac{\left\|\alpha\right\|^{n}}{\Nrm(\alpha)}\ \middle|\ \alpha\in\mathcal{D}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}

is bounded from above.
Note from (
11
) and (
13
) that
this set is always bounded from below by
1
.
As usual, a subset
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{O}\smallsetminus\{0\}
is called an
\mathcal{O}^{*}
-fundamental domain
of
\mathcal{O}\smallsetminus\{0\}

(or of
\mathcal{O}
by slight abuse of terminology)
if the composite map
\mathcal{D}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}\smallsetminus\{0\}\twoheadrightarrow(\mathcal{O}\smallsetminus\{0\})/\mathcal{O}^{*}
is a bijection.
As in
[
6
, §4.3]
, the following statement holds.

Proposition 2.6
.

There exist norm-length compatible
\mathcal{O}^{*}
-fundamental domains
of
\mathcal{O}
.

Proof.

Consider the function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
;
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto n\max_{i}\{x_{i}\}-\sum_{i}x_{i}
.
It fits into the following commutative diagram:

(47)

\textstyle{\mathcal{O}\smallsetminus\{0\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathcal{L}}
\scriptstyle{\frac{\left\|-\right\|^{n}}{\Nrm(-)}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{R}^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{\mathbb{R}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\log}
\textstyle{\mathbb{R}.}

Let
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be as in (
45
) and

\pi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to H
the projection along the vector
(1,\dots,1)
.
Since the value of
f
is unchanged by the translation along the vector
(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
it follows that the norm-length compatibility of a subset
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{O}\smallsetminus\{0\}
is equivalent to the boundedness
of the non-negatively valued function
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto\max_{i}x_{i}
on the set
\pi(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{D}))\subset H
.
This is equivalent to the boundedness of the set
\pi(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{D}))
itself;
recall that one can define the notion of
boundedness
of a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

using any choice of a linear norm and the resulting notion is independent of the choice.
It follows that for any given bounded subset
\Delta\subset H
, the set
\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\pi^{-1}(\Delta))
is a norm-length compatible subset of
\mathcal{O}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.

Recall by (
13
) and (
42
) that
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{O}^{*})
is a full-rank lattice of
H
.
Let
\Delta\subset H
be any bounded complete set of representatives for the quotient
H/\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{O}^{*})
(say a fundamental parallelogram).
The inverse image
\pi^{-1}(\Delta)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a complete set of representatives for the quotient
\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{O}^{*})
.
By the previous paragraph, the set
\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\pi^{-1}(\Delta))\subset\mathcal{O}\smallsetminus\{0\}
is norm-length compatible.
It is acted on by the group
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=\ker(\mathcal{L}\colon\mathcal{O}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the natural map of quotient sets

\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\pi^{-1}(\Delta))/\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\to(\mathcal{O}\smallsetminus\{0\})/\mathcal{O}^{*}

is a bijection.
Therefore, any choice of an
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
-fundamental domain of
\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\pi^{-1}(\Delta))
gives a norm-length compatible

\mathcal{O}^{*}
-fundamental domain of
\mathcal{O}
.
∎

For
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
, one can consider its
\mathcal{O}^{*}
-orbit
\alpha\mathcal{O}^{*}
.
We will need the following bound.
For the big-
O
notation, see Notation at the end of Introduction.

Proposition 2.7
.

For all
N\geq q
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{\leq N}
, we have

(48)

\left|(\alpha\mathcal{O}^{*})\cap\mathcal{O}_{\leq N}\right|=O_{\mathcal{O}}\Bigl((\log N-\frac{1}{n}\log\Nrm(\alpha))^{n-1}+1\Bigr).

The term “
+1
” is there only to cover the rare case
\Nrm(\alpha)=N^{n}
(the largest possible). For the application in this paper, its corollary
\left|(\alpha\mathcal{O}^{*})\cap\mathcal{O}_{\leq N}\right|=O_{\mathcal{O}}((\log N)^{n-1})
suffices and saves space.

Proof.

We may assume
\alpha\neq 0
.
For a real number
C
, let
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\leq C}
be the set of points
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
with
x_{i}\leq C
for all
i
.
Let
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
as in (
45
) and define
\Delta_{\leq C}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\leq C}\cap H
, which is an
(n-1)
-dimensional simplex.
Let us use the symbol
\Delta^{\prime}_{\leq C}
to denote translations of
\Delta_{\leq C}
inside
H
.
Write
\Gamma:=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{O}^{*})
.
Sending the set in question by
\mathcal{L}
, we see:

(49)

\left|(\alpha\mathcal{O}^{*})\cap\mathcal{O}_{\leq N}\right|=\left|\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\right|\cdot\left|(\mathcal{L}(\alpha)+\Gamma)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\leq\log N}\right|.

So it suffices to bound the size of the set on the right hand side.
By the translation “
-\mathcal{L}(\alpha)
” we have a bijection

(50)

(\mathcal{L}(\alpha)+\Gamma)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\leq\log N}\cong\Gamma\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\leq\log N}-\mathcal{L}(\alpha)).

Next
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\leq\log N}-\mathcal{L}(\alpha)
is the translation of
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\leq\log N-\frac{1}{n}\log\Nrm(\alpha)}
by the vector

-\mathcal{L}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{n}\log\Nrm(\alpha)\cdot(1,\dots,1)\in H
.
Therefore the intersection
H\cap\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\leq\log N}-\mathcal{L}(\alpha)\bigr)
is a
\Delta^{\prime}_{\leq\log N-\frac{1}{n}\log\Nrm(\alpha)}
.
Since
\Gamma
is contained in
H
anyway, we can write

(51)

\Gamma\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\leq\log N}-\mathcal{L}(\alpha))=\Gamma\cap\Delta^{\prime}_{\leq\log N-\frac{1}{n}\log\Nrm(\alpha)}.

Now since
\Gamma
is a lattice in
H
, the cardinality of a set of the form
\Gamma\cap\Delta^{\prime}_{\leq C}

is asymptotically proportional to
C^{n-1}
as
C\to+\infty
with error bounded independently of the specific translation
\Delta_{\leq C}\leadsto\Delta^{\prime}_{\leq C}
;
see for example
[
4
, Appendix A]
.
This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Szemerédi’s theorem

Let us keep the notation
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathfrak{o}
from the previous section.
Namely
\mathcal{O}
is a Dedekind domain finitely generated over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

in which
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is integrally closed
and
\mathfrak{o}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
is a subring of
\mathcal{O}
as in Proposition
2.1
.
In case the reader is interested in the “prime elements of ideals” case of Theorem
5.2
, let
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}
be a non-zero ideal.
(If not, they can always assume
\mathfrak{a}=\mathcal{O}
.)
Recall that an
\mathfrak{o}
-homothetic copy
of a (finite) subset
S\subset\mathfrak{a}

is by definition
a subset of
\mathfrak{a}
of the form

a\cdot S+\beta=\left\{a\alpha+\beta\mid\alpha\in S\right\}

with
a\in\mathfrak{o}
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{a}
.
It it said to be
non-trivial
if we can take
a\neq 0
.

The next definition is a rather straightforward translation of
[
6
, Definition 5.3]
.

Definition 3.1
.

(1)

A finite subset
S\subset\mathfrak{a}
is said to be a
standard shape

if it contains
0
and generates
\mathfrak{a}
as an
\mathfrak{o}
-module.
(The word “shape” is used because
\mathbb{Z}
-homothetic copies of
S
in an abelian group are also called
constellations with shape
S
especially in the context of torsion-free abelian groups.)
In the rest of this Definition, assume
S
is a standard shape.

(2)

Write
k=\left|S\right|
and give
S
a numbering
S=\left\{s_{1},\dots,s_{k-1},s_{k}=0\right\}
for the ease of notation. Let

(52)

\phi_{S}\colon\mathfrak{o}^{k-1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{a}

be the surjective
\mathfrak{o}
-linear map which sends the standard vector
\bm{e}_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq k-1
) to
s_{i}
.

Let us denote elements of the sum
\mathfrak{o}^{k}\oplus\mathfrak{o}^{k}
by symbols like
(x_{i}^{\pm})_{1\leq i\leq k}
.
Given an index
1\leq j\leq k
and a function
\omega\colon\left\{1,\dots,\hat{j},\dots,k\right\}\to\{\pm\}
, we obtain an element
(x_{i}^{\omega(i)})_{i\in\left\{1,\dots,k\right\}\smallsetminus\left\{j\right\}}
of
\bigoplus\limits_{i\in\left\{1,\dots,k\right\}\smallsetminus\left\{j\right\}}\mathfrak{o}\cong\mathfrak{o}^{k-1}
.
Let us call this map the
restriction
along
\omega
and denote it by
\operatorname{res}_{\omega}\colon\mathfrak{o}^{k}\oplus\mathfrak{o}^{k}\to\mathfrak{o}^{k-1}
.

For
1\leq j\leq k
, we define a surjective
\mathfrak{o}
-linear map

(53)

\psi_{S,j}\colon\bigoplus_{i\in\left\{1,\dots,k\right\}\smallsetminus\left\{j\right\}}\mathfrak{o}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{a}

as follows:
we define
\psi_{S,k}:=\phi_{S}
.
For
1\leq j\leq k-1
we define
\psi_{S,j}(x_{1},\dots\widehat{x_{j}},\dots,x_{k}):=x_{k}s_{j}+\sum\limits_{i\in\left\{1,\dots,k-1\right\}\smallsetminus\left\{j\right\}}x_{i}(s_{i}-s_{j})
.

(3)

Let
\rho>0
be a positive real number and

N_{0}>0
a positive integer.
A non-negatively valued function
\lambda\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is said to be
an
(S,N_{0},\rho,\mathfrak{o})
-pseudorandom measure
if for every choice of the data below:

•

a subset
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathfrak{o}^{k}
which is the product of translates of
\mathfrak{o}_{\leq N}
with
N\geq N_{0}
,

•

a subset
\Omega\subset\bigsqcup_{1\leq j\leq k}\left\{\pm\right\}^{\left\{1,\dots,\hat{j},\dots,k\right\}}

(namely a set of pairs
(j,\omega)
of an index
1\leq j\leq k
and a function
\omega\colon\left\{1,\dots,\hat{j},\dots,k\right\}\to\left\{\pm\right\}
),

we have the inequality:

(54)

\left|\mathbb{E}\left(\prod_{(j,\omega)\in\Omega}(\lambda\circ\psi_{S,j}\circ\operatorname{res}_{\omega})\,\middle|\,\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}\right)-1\right|\quad<\quad\rho.

We will need the following form of relative Szemerédi theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
S\subset\mathfrak{a}
be a standard shape and
\delta>0
a positive real number.
Then there exist positive real numbers

\rho=\rho(\mathfrak{o},\mathfrak{a},S,\delta)
and
\gamma=\gamma(\mathfrak{o},\mathfrak{a},S,\delta)>0

such that the following holds.
Let
\lambda\colon\mathfrak{a}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
satisfy the

(S,N_{0},\rho,\mathfrak{o})
-psedorandomness condition for some
N_{0}\geq\Nrm(\mathfrak{a})q^{2g-1}

(see Definition
3.1
for this condition).
Let
N\geq N_{0}
and
A\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\leq N}
.
Suppose the following two inequalities are satisfied:

(55)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\lambda\cdot\bm{1}_{A}\,|\,\mathfrak{a}_{\leq N})

\displaystyle\geq\delta,

(56)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\lambda^{k}\cdot\bm{1}_{A}\,|\,\mathfrak{a}_{\leq N})

\displaystyle\leq\gamma N,\quad\text{ where }k:=\left|S\right|.

Then the following inequality holds:

(57)

\mathbb{E}\left(\prod_{s\in S}(\lambda\cdot\bm{1}_{A})(as+\beta)\,\middle|\,(a,\beta)\in\mathfrak{o}_{\leq N}\times\mathfrak{a}_{\leq N}\right)>\gamma.

In particular there exist non-trivial
\mathfrak{o}
-homothetic copies of
S
contained in
A
.

This statement and its proof are an immediate analog of

[
6
, Theorem 5.4]
.
Nonetheless we write down the proof for the convenience
of the reader.
For this we have to recall the notion of
weighted hypergraphs
and borrow results on them from combinatorics.
We will content ourselves with the following narrower definition than usual.

Definition 3.3
.

An
r
-uniform weighted hypergraph
consists of the following data:

•

a finite set
J
;

•

a finite set
V_{i}
of vertices given for each
i\in J
;

•

for each subset
e\subset J
with cardinality
r
, a weight function
\nu_{e}\colon\prod_{i\in e}V_{i}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

The case where
\nu_{e}
have values in
\left\{0,1\right\}
recovers the notion of an
r
-uniform hypergraph

by interpreting the value
0
as “no
r
-edge” and
1
as “an
r
-edge.”
The case

corresponds to classical (weighted,
\left|J\right|
-partite) graphs.

Consider the product
\prod_{i\in J}V_{i}\times\prod_{i\in J}V_{i}
and denote its elements by symbols
like
(x_{i}^{\pm})_{i}
.
Paralelly to the above, for a subset
e\subset J
and a function
\omega\colon e\to\left\{\pm\right\}

we get an element
(x_{i}^{\omega(i)})_{i\in e}\in\prod_{i\in e}V_{i}
.
This defines the
restriction
along
\omega
, denoted by

\operatorname{res}_{\omega}\colon\prod_{i\in J}V_{i}\times\prod_{i\in J}V_{i}\to\prod_{i\in e}V_{i}
.

Definition 3.4
.

For a positive real number
\rho>0
, an
r
-uniform weighted hypergraph as above is said to be
\rho
-pseudorandom

if for all choices of
a subset
\Omega\subset\bigsqcup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\subset J\\
\text{with }\left|e\right|=r\end{subarray}}\left\{\pm\right\}^{e}

(namely a set of pairs
(e,\omega)
of a subset
e\subset J
with
\left|e\right|=r
and a function
\omega\colon e\to\left\{\pm\right\}
),
the following estimate holds:

(58)

\left|\mathbb{E}\left(\prod_{(e,\omega)\in\Omega}(\nu_{e}\circ\operatorname{res}_{\omega})\,\middle|\,\prod_{i\in J}V_{i}\times\prod_{i\in J}V_{i}\right)-1\right|<\rho.

Theorem 3.5

(Relative Hypergraph Removal Lemma
[
2
]
)
.

Let
0<r\leq k
be positive integers and
\varepsilon>0
be a positive real number.
Then there exist positive real numbers
\gamma=\gamma(r,k,\varepsilon)
and

\rho=\rho(r,k,\varepsilon)>0
such that the following holds.

Let
((V_{i})_{i\in J},(\nu_{e})_{e\subset J,\left|e\right|=r})
be a
\rho
-pseudorandom
r
-uniform weighted hypergraph.
Suppose given a subset
E_{e}\subset\prod_{i\in e}V_{i}
for each
e\subset J
with
\left|e\right|=r
and
suppose that the following inequality holds:

(59)

\mathbb{E}\left(\prod_{e\subset J,\left|e\right|=r}(\nu_{e}\bm{1}_{E_{e}})((x_{i})_{i\in e})\,\middle|\,(x_{i})_{i\in J}\in\prod_{i\in J}V_{i}\right)\leq\gamma.

for

with

such that:

(60)

\displaystyle\bigcap_{e\subset J,\ \left|e\right|=r}E_{e}^{\prime}\times V_{J\smallsetminus e}=\varnothing,\quad\text{ and }

(61)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\nu_{e}\bm{1}_{E_{e}\smallsetminus E_{e}^{\prime}}\,|\,V_{e})\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{ for all }e.

Proof.

Conlon-Fox-Zhao

4.
The von Mangoldt function

The definitions and results are parallel to those in
[
6
, §6]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\chi\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a
C^{\infty}
function with support
\subset[-1,1]

and
\chi(0)=1
, which we fix throughout the paper.
Let
R>0
be a positive number. In the proof of the main result we will need to take
R
very large.
Let
\mathrm{Ideals}_{\mathcal{O}}
be the multiplicative monoid of non-zero ideals of
\mathcal{O}
.
We define the function
\Lambda_{R,\chi}\colon\mathrm{Ideals}_{\mathcal{O}}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(88)

\Lambda_{R,\chi}(\mathfrak{b}):=\log R\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{c}\in\mathrm{Ideals}_{\mathcal{O}}\\
\text{with }\mathfrak{c}|\mathfrak{b}\end{subarray}}\mu(\mathfrak{c})\chi\left(\frac{\log\Nrm(\mathfrak{c})}{\log R}\right).

Given a non-zero ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathcal{O}
, we define the function

\Lambda_{R,\chi}^{\mathfrak{a}}\colon\mathfrak{a}\to\mathbb{R}
by the composition:

\Lambda_{R,\chi}^{\mathfrak{a}}\colon\mathfrak{a}\xrightarrow{\alpha\mapsto\alpha\mathfrak{a}^{-1}}\mathrm{Ideals}_{\mathcal{O}}\xrightarrow{\Lambda_{R,\chi}}\mathbb{R}.

Note that the membership
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}
implies that
\alpha\mathfrak{a}^{-1}
is a (non-zero)
ideal of
\mathcal{O}
so that the above composition is well defined.

Below we use the notation
\Nrm(-)
also for non-zero ideals of
\mathfrak{o}
.
This does not cause confusion because a non-zero ideal of
\mathfrak{o}
is never an ideal of
\mathcal{O}

and vice versa.
Of course every non-zero ideal of
\mathfrak{o}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
is a principal ideal
f\mathfrak{o}

and if
f
has degree
e
as a polynomial then we have
\Nrm(f\mathfrak{o})=q^{e}
(
=\left\|f\right\|_{\mathfrak{o}}
).
For
N>0
we have
\mathfrak{o}_{\leq N^{d}}=\left\{f\in\mathfrak{o}\mid\Nrm(f\mathfrak{o})\leq N\right\}
by Proposition
2.1
.
For non-zero ideals
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathcal{O}
, by the canonical injection
\mathfrak{o}/\mathfrak{o}\cap\mathfrak{b}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{b}
, we know
\Nrm(\mathfrak{o}\cap\mathfrak{b})\leq\Nrm(\mathfrak{b})
.

For
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathrm{Ideals}_{\mathcal{O}}
, define
\varphi_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{b}):=\left|(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{b})^{*}\right|
.
By Chinese Remainder Theorem we know
\varphi_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{b})=\Nrm(\mathfrak{b})\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{b}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\Nrm(\mathfrak{p})}\right)

where
\mathfrak{p}
runs through the prime ideal divisors of
\mathfrak{b}
.
For elements
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}\smallsetminus\{0\}
let us write
\varphi_{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha):=\varphi_{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha\mathcal{O})
.

Theorem 4.2

(of Goldston-Yıldırım type)
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a non-zero ideal of
\mathcal{O}
.
Let
m,t\geq 0
be non-negative integers.
Let

\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{m}\colon\mathfrak{o}^{t}\to\mathfrak{a}

be
\mathfrak{o}
-linear maps
whose cokernels are finite and such that

does not contain
\ker(\phi_{j})
whenever

.

Then there are large positive numbers

,
w_{0}>1
and a small one

such that
for every choice of the quantities below:

•

and

such that

,

•

whose prime factors are exactly

,

•

of a product

with

•

we have an estimate

(89)

\Ex\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Lambda_{R,\chi}^{\mathfrak{a}}(W\phi_{i}(x)+b_{i})^{2}\,\middle|\,x\in B\right)\\
=(1+O_{\chi,m,r}\left(\frac{1}{w\log_{q}w}\right)+O_{\chi,m,r,\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\log_{q}w}{\sqrt{\log R}}\right))\left(\log R\cdot\frac{\Nrm(W\mathcal{O})}{\varphi_{\mathcal{O}}(W)}\frac{C_{\chi}}{\kappa_{\mathcal{O}}}\right)^{m},

and

That the error terms can be taken entirely independent of the choice of

∎

Now we want to plug our results here into (

4.2.
Fourier transform

Let
\widehat{\chi}
be the Fourier transform of the function

so that by inverse Fourier transform:

(101)

e^{x}\chi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\chi}(\xi)e^{\xi x\sqrt{-1}}d\xi.

It follows that
\chi(\frac{\log\Nrm(\mathfrak{b})}{\log R})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Nrm(\mathfrak{b})^{\frac{1}{\log R}(-1+\xi\sqrt{-1})}\widehat{\chi}(\xi)d\xi
for ideals
\mathfrak{b}
.
By the theory of Fourier analysis, we know that
\widehat{\chi}
decays rapidly:

Lemma 4.6
.

For any given positive numbers
A
and
b\geq 1
, we have

\widehat{\chi}(\xi)=O_{A,\chi}(1+|\xi|^{-A})
and hence

(102)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{\chi}(\xi)|d\xi<\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad\left(\int_{-\infty}^{-b}+\int_{b}^{+\infty}\right)|\widehat{\chi}(\xi)|d\xi=O_{A,\chi}(b^{-A}).

Proof.

See any textbook on Fourier analysis or
[
6
, Lemma 6.15 and its corollary]
.
∎

The right hand side of (
90
) is written as:

(103)

\sum_{(\mathfrak{b}_{i},\mathfrak{c}_{i})_{i}\in\mathrm{Ideals}_{\mathcal{O}}^{2m}}(\log R)^{2m}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mu(\mathfrak{b}_{i})\mu(\mathfrak{c}_{i})\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Nrm(\mathfrak{b}_{i})^{\frac{1}{\log R}(-1+\xi_{i}\sqrt{-1})}\widehat{\chi}(\xi_{i})d\xi_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Nrm(\mathfrak{c}_{i})^{\frac{1}{\log R}(-1+\eta_{i}\sqrt{-1})}\widehat{\chi}(\eta_{i})d\eta_{i}\\
\cdot\mathbb{E}((\underline{\mathfrak{b}},\underline{\mathfrak{c}});W,b).

Let
I
be the interval
I:=[-\sqrt{\log R},\sqrt{\log R}]
.
For tuples
(\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta})\in I^{2m}
, consider the infinite sum

(104)

E((\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta}),R,w,b):=\\
\sum_{(\mathfrak{b}_{i},\mathfrak{c}_{i})_{i}\in(\mathrm{Ideals}_{\mathcal{O}})^{2m}}\mathbb{E}((\underline{\mathfrak{b}},\underline{\mathfrak{c}});W,b)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mu(\mathfrak{b}_{i})\mu(\mathfrak{c}_{i})\Nrm(\mathfrak{b}_{i})^{\frac{1}{\log R}(-1+\xi_{i}\sqrt{-1})}\Nrm(\mathfrak{c}_{i})^{\frac{1}{\log R}(-1+\eta_{i}\sqrt{-1})}.

This subsection is devoted to the proof of:

Proposition 4.7
.

The sum
E((\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta}),R,w,b)
converges absolutely
and uniformly in
(\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta})\in\mathbb{R}^{2m}
.
For any given
A>0
, the quantity (

up to an error
\pm O_{A,\chi,m,r}((\log R)^{-A})
, to:

(105)

(\log R)^{2m}\int_{I^{2m}}E((\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta}),R,w,b)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\widehat{\chi}(\xi_{i})\widehat{\chi}(\eta_{i})\right)d\underline{\xi}d\underline{\eta}.

4.2.1.
Convergence

Note that by the presence of the Möbius function,
only those terms where all
\mathfrak{b}_{i}
and
\mathfrak{c}_{i}
are square-free contribute to the sum
(
104
) and
that the sum decomposes into the product of its
\pi
-parts by the multiplicativity of the functions involved (see Lemma
4.4
for the multiplicativity of
\mathbb{E}((\underline{\mathfrak{b}},\underline{\mathfrak{c}});W,b)
).
Namely:

(106)

E((\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta}),R,w,b)=\prod_{(\pi)}E^{(\pi)}((\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta}),R,w,b),\quad\text{where }E^{(\pi)}((\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta}),R,w,b):=\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\beta_{i},\gamma_{i})_{i}\\
\text{$\pi$-ideals,}\\
\text{square-free}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}((\underline{\beta},\underline{\gamma});W,b)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mu(\beta_{i})\mu(\gamma_{i})\Nrm(\beta_{i})^{\frac{1}{\log R}(-1+\xi_{i}\sqrt{-1})}\Nrm(\gamma_{i})^{\frac{1}{\log R}(-1+\eta_{i}\sqrt{-1})}\right).

Note that there are at most
r
prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}
over a given
(\pi)
and hence
there are at most
2^{r}
square-free
\pi
-ideals.

By Lemma
4.5
, if
\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})\leq w
then
E^{(\pi)}((\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta}),R,w,b)=1
; we also know the following when
\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})>w
, supposing (as we shall always do) that
w
is large enough to invoke the lemma:

•

Unless
(\beta_{i},\gamma_{i})=(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})
,

(\mathcal{O},\mathfrak{p})
,
(\mathfrak{p},\mathcal{O})
or
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p})
for some prime
\pi
-ideal
\mathfrak{p}
with
\Nrm(\mathfrak{p})=\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})
,
we have
(0\leq)\ \mathbb{E}((\underline{\beta},\underline{\gamma});W,b)\leq 1/\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})^{2}
;

•

For those exceptional cases in the previous item, we know
\mathbb{E}((\underline{\beta},\underline{\gamma});W,b)=1
for the first case and
=1/\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})
for the others.

This gives us the following crude estimate, where
O_{r}(1)
can be taken to be

:

(107)

E^{(\pi)}((\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta}),R,w,b)=\\
1+\sum_{\mathfrak{p},\deg=1}\left(-\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})^{-1+\frac{1}{\log R}(-1+\xi_{i}\sqrt{-1})}-\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})^{-1+\frac{1}{\log R}(-1+\eta_{i}\sqrt{-1})}+\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})^{-1+\frac{1}{\log R}(-2+(\xi_{i}+\eta_{i})\sqrt{-1})}\right)\\
+O_{r}\left(1\right)\cdot\frac{1}{\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})^{2}}.

In particular we have
E((\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta}),R,w,b)=1+O_{r}\left(\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})^{-1-\frac{1}{\log R}}\right)
uniformly in
\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta}
.
Therefore by basic facts on Euler products (such as
[
6
, Lemma 6.19]
)
we conclude that the product
\prod_{(\pi)}E^{(\pi)}((\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta}),R,w,b)
converges absolutely.
As a result, the sum of absolute values associated with the sum

(
104
)
can be estimated as:

(108)

\sum_{(\mathfrak{b}_{i},\mathfrak{c}_{i})_{i}}\mathbb{E}((\underline{\mathfrak{b}},\underline{\mathfrak{c}});W,b)\cdot\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Nrm(\mathfrak{b}_{i})^{\frac{1}{\log R}}\Nrm(\mathfrak{c}_{i})^{\frac{1}{\log R}}\right)\\
=\prod_{(\pi)}\left(1+O_{r}\left(\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})^{-1-\frac{1}{\log R}}\right)\right)=(\log R+O(1))^{O_{r}(1)},

proving the convergence claim in Proposition
4.7
.

Now let us move on to the comparison of (

) and (

We want to replace the domain

Lemma 4.8

Regarding the expression (

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}

Proof.

(1)

(2)
Apply (1) to each of
\mathfrak{b}_{i}
and
\mathfrak{c}_{i}

∎

Apply the operation

to the estimate of

4.3.
Intermission

Before proceeding further, let us recall basic facts from elementary calculus and the theory of the zeta function.
The absolute constans
c_{i}
and
C_{i}
appearing below can be made explicit, but we do not seek to do so because their precise values are not important.
Potentially big constants are denoted in upper case and
potentially small ones in lower case.
From §
4.4
on, when we say some quantities should be small or large enough, we will be implicitly using these constants to specify the thresholds.

4.3.1.
Some calculus

There is a positive real number
c_{1}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\varepsilon|\leq c_{1}
one has

(117)

\displaystyle e^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=1+O(1)\cdot\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }

(118)

\displaystyle\log(1+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=1+O(1)\cdot\varepsilon

with both
O(1)\leq 2
. (Actually one can take
c_{1}:=1/2
, say.)
Next, for real numbers
A\geq 2
we have Taylor expansion

(119)

A^{-1+\varepsilon}=A^{-1}+\frac{\log A}{A}\varepsilon+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\log(A)^{2}}{A}\varepsilon^{2}+\cdots.

Noting that the positively valued function
A\mapsto\frac{\log A}{A}
has bounded range, there are
c_{2}>0

and
C_{3}>0
such that for all
A\geq 2
and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\varepsilon|\leq\frac{c_{2}}{\log A}
we have

(120)

1-A^{-1+\varepsilon}=1-A^{-1}+O(1)\cdot\frac{\log A}{A}\varepsilon

with
O(1)\leq C_{3}
. It follows that

(121)

\frac{1-A^{-1+\varepsilon}}{1-A^{-1}}=1+\frac{O(1)}{1-A^{-1}}\frac{\log A}{A}\varepsilon=1+O(1)\frac{\log A}{A}\varepsilon

with a new
O(1)
constant
\leq 2C_{3}
.
Also, since the function
A\mapsto\frac{\log A}{A}
decreases for
A\geq e
, for prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}
with
\mathfrak{p}\cap\mathfrak{o}=(\pi)
we have

(122)

\frac{\log\Nrm(\mathfrak{p})}{\Nrm(\mathfrak{p})}\leq\frac{\log\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})}{\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})}

at least if
\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})\geq 3
.
This inequality happens to be true even when
\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})=2
thanks to the equality
\frac{\log 4}{4}=\frac{\log 2}{2}
.

4.3.2.
The zeta function

We need to recall the zeta function of
\mathcal{O}
.
For
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Real(s)>1
, we set:

(123)

\zeta_{\mathcal{O}}(s):=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in X}\left(1-\frac{1}{\Nrm(\mathfrak{p})^{s}}\right)^{-1}\quad\left(=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\in\mathrm{Ideals}_{\mathcal{O}}}\frac{1}{\Nrm(\mathfrak{a})^{s}}\right).

It is known that
\zeta_{\mathcal{O}}(s)
extends to a meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}
.
Actually we know
[
8
, Theorem 5.9, p.53]
that there is a polynomial

L(u)\in\mathbb{Z}[u]
of degree
2g
, with
L(q^{-1})=\left|\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(\overline{X})\right|/q^{g}
, such that:

(124)

\zeta_{\mathcal{O}}(s)\cdot\prod_{v\in\overline{X}\smallsetminus X}\left(1-\frac{1}{\left|\mathbb{F}(v)\right|^{s}}\right)^{-1}=\frac{L(q^{-s})}{(1-q^{-s})(1-q^{1-s})}.

It follows that
\zeta_{\mathcal{O}}(s)
has a simple pole at
s=1
with positive residue, say
\kappa_{\mathcal{O}}>0
:

(125)

\zeta_{\mathcal{O}}(s)=\frac{\kappa_{\mathcal{O}}}{s-1}+O_{\mathcal{O}}(1)\quad\text{ as }s\to 1.

Moreover, by Weil’s Riemann Hypothesis for algebraic curves
[
8
, Theorem 5.10, p.55]
, we know that
all the roots of
L(u)
in
\mathbb{C}
have magnitude
q^{-1/2}
.
Chebotarëv’s density theorem
5.3

below is an important consequence of this.

By explicit computation we know
\zeta_{\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]}(s)=1/\left(1-{q^{1-s}}\right)
(see
[
8
, p.11]
).
From this it follows for integers
i\geq 1
:

(126)

\left|\left\{\text{maximal ideals }\pi\mathfrak{o}\subset\mathfrak{o}\mid\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})=q^{i}\right\}\right|=\frac{q^{i}}{i}+O\left(\frac{q^{i/2}}{i}\right),

see
[
8
, Theorem 2.2, p.14]
.
This
gives the following.
The sums are over maximal ideals
\pi\mathfrak{o}\subset\mathfrak{o}
satisfying the indicated conditions:

(127)

\displaystyle\sum_{\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})>w}\frac{1}{\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\frac{1}{w\log_{q}w}\quad\text{ and }

(128)

\displaystyle\sum_{\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})\leq w}\frac{\log_{q}\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})}{\Nrm(\pi\mathfrak{o})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\log_{q}w

for some
C_{4},C_{5}>0
and all
w>1
.

Euler product

Now we compute the main term (

Proposition 4.9
.

5.
Chebotarëv and the end of proof

As always, let
\mathcal{O}
continue to be a Dedekind domain finitely generated over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
We restate Theorem
1.2
in a slightly broader generality.
In the number field case
[
6
]
, the extra generality allowed one to prove a constellation theorem for prime-valued points on a binary quadratic form
ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}
over
\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}
be a non-zero ideal.
Let us define the set
\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{a}}
of
prime elements of
\mathfrak{a}

by

(158)

\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{a}}:=\left\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}\mid\mathfrak{a}/\alpha\mathcal{O}\cong\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}\text{ as an $\mathcal{O}$-module for some maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p}$}\right\}.

Note that
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}}
is exactly the set of prime elements of
\mathcal{O}
because an isomorphism

\mathcal{O}/\alpha\mathcal{O}\cong\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}
-modules forces the equality
\alpha\mathcal{O}=\mathfrak{p}
.

Now we can state our main theorem in its proper generality.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a Dedekind domain finitely generated over
\mathbb{F}_{p}

and
\mathfrak{o}\subset\mathcal{O}
as in Proposition
2.1
.
Let
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}
be a non-zero ideal and
S\subset\mathfrak{a}
a finite subset of it.
Then any subset
A\subset\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{a}}
of positive relative density
contains a non-trivial
\mathfrak{o}
-homothetic copy of
S
.

For the proof, we need to recall Chebotarëv’s density theorem.
This is by far the deepest imput from algebraic geometry in this work.
Let
\overline{X}
be a complete non-singular geometrically irreducible curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{X})\xrightarrow{\phi}G
be a finite quotient of
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{X})
.
The restriction of the degree map
\deg\colon\ker(\phi)\to\mathbb{Z}
is necessarily non-trivial.
Let
D\geq 1
be the order of its cokernel.
(Let us always use
D
in this sense when
G
is understood.)
It follows that we have the degree map of the following form:

(159)

\deg\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}/D\mathbb{Z}.

The next result is a consequence of Weil’s Riemann Hypothesis for algebraic curves over finite fields.

Theorem 5.3

(Chebotarëv’s density theorem)
.

Let
G
be a finite quotient of
\operatorname{Pic}(\overline{X})

and
P\in G
.
Then for positive integers
n>0
, we have the following cardinality estimate:

(160)

\left|\Bigl\{x\in\overline{X}\mid\deg(x)=n\text{ in $\mathbb{Z}$ and }[x]=P\text{ in }G\Bigr\}\right|=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{D}{\left|G\right|}\frac{q^{n}}{n}+O_{\overline{X}}\left(\frac{q^{n/2}}{n}\right)&\text{ if }n\equiv\deg(P)\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}/D\mathbb{Z},\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle O_{\overline{X}}\left(\frac{q^{n/2}}{n}\right)&\text{ else. }\end{cases}

Proof.

A slightly weaker version of this statement can be found in
[
8
, Theorem 9.13B, p.125]
.
Our statement can be obtained by the same argument
by using
[
8
, Proposition 9.21, p.137]
and
[
8
, Theorem 9.24, p.141]
in place of
[
8
, Theorem 9.16B, p.129]
.
∎

Via the transition of parameters from the degree
n=\deg(x)
to the norm
L=q^{n}=:\Nrm(x)
, we get the following:

Corollary 5.4
.

For all sufficiently large positive numbers
L>0
, we have the following estimate:

(161)

\frac{1}{q^{D}}\frac{D}{\left|G\right|}\frac{L}{\log_{q}L}\leq\left|\Bigl\{x\in\overline{X}\Bigm|\Nrm(x)\leq L\text{ and }[x]+P=0\text{ in }G\Bigr\}\right|\leq 2\frac{D}{\left|G\right|}\frac{L}{\log_{q}L}.

The following is clear from definitions.

Lemma 5.5
.

If
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}
is a prime element, then:

(162)

0\leq\Lambda_{R,\chi}^{\mathfrak{a}}(\alpha)\leq\log R.

The right-hand ‘

’ is an equality if
\Nrm(\alpha)\geq\Nrm(\mathfrak{a})R
.

When we apply Lemma
5.5
, it will be convenient to have a bound for the number of
elements
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}

with
\Nrm(\alpha)<\Nrm(\mathfrak{a})R
.
For
L\geq 1
, let us write

(163)

\mathfrak{a}(L):=\left\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}\mid\Nrm(\alpha)\leq L\right\}.

Corollary 5.6
.

For positive real numbers
L>1
and
M>1
, we have the bound

(164)

\left|\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{a}}\cap\mathfrak{a}(\Nrm(\mathfrak{a})L)\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\leq M}\right|=O_{\mathcal{O}}\left((\log M)^{n-1}\frac{L}{\log_{q}L}\right).

Proof.

Given
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{a}}\cap\mathfrak{a}(\Nrm(\mathfrak{a})L)
, the ideal
\mathfrak{p}:=\alpha\mathfrak{a}^{-1}\subset\mathcal{O}
is a prime ideal
whose class in
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{O})
equals
-[\mathfrak{a}]
and norm equals
\Nrm(\alpha)/\Nrm(\mathfrak{a})
(
\leq L
).
Two elements

give the same

if and only if they are associate to each other.
By Corollary
5.4
, it follows that there are at most
2\frac{D}{\left|\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{O})\right|}\frac{L}{\log_{q}L}

By Proposition
2.7

This completes the proof.

.
The generators of
\mathfrak{a}/W\mathfrak{a}
correspond to the elements of
(\mathcal{O}/W\mathcal{O})^{*}
.
It follows that:

(179)

\left|\left\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}/W\mathfrak{a}\mid\alpha\text{ generates }\mathfrak{a}/W\mathfrak{a}\right\}\right|=\varphi_{\mathcal{O}}(W).

By Lemma
5.7
, we see that the set
A\cap\left(\mathfrak{a}_{\leq M}\smallsetminus\mathfrak{a}(\Nrm(\mathfrak{a})R)\right)

decomposes into the sum of
\varphi_{\mathcal{O}}(W)
disjoint subsets according to the mod
W
classes.
By the pigeonhole principle, it follows that for some residue class
[b]\in\mathfrak{a}/W\mathfrak{a}
we have

(180)

\left|\left\{\alpha\in A\cap\left(\mathfrak{a}_{\leq M}\smallsetminus\mathfrak{a}(\Nrm(\mathfrak{a})R)\right)\mid\alpha=[b]\text{ in }\mathfrak{a}/W\mathfrak{a}\right\}\right|\geq\frac{1}{\varphi_{\mathcal{O}}(W)}\cdot(\text{R.H.S of}\eqref{eq:L/N(a)-bis}).

Choose one such
[b]\in\mathfrak{a}/W\mathfrak{a}
.
Let us fix a
C>0
depending only on
\mathfrak{a}
and
W
such that the projection
\mathfrak{a}_{\leq C}\to\mathfrak{a}/W\mathfrak{a}
is surjective (which exists because the target is a finite set)
and choose a lift
b\in\mathfrak{a}_{\leq C}
of
[b]
.
Let
\Aff_{W,b}\colon\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{a}
be the affine linear map
\alpha\mapsto W\alpha+b
.
Set

(181)

B:=\Aff_{W,b}^{-1}(A\smallsetminus\mathfrak{a}(\Nrm(\mathfrak{a})R))\subset\mathfrak{a}.

We have the following inclusion if
e>1
is large enough:

(182)

\Aff_{W,b}(\mathfrak{a})\cap(\mathfrak{a}_{\leq M})\subset\Aff_{W,b}(\mathfrak{a}_{\leq N}).

Indeed, suppose
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}
satisfies
\left\|W\alpha+b\right\|\leq{M}={N\left\|W\right\|}
.
Since
\left\|b\right\|\leq C<N\left\|W\right\|
for
e
large enough, by the ultrametricity of
\left\|-\right\|
this implies
\left\|W\alpha\right\|\leq N\left\|W\right\|
.
We get
\left\|\alpha\right\|\leq N
because
W
is a multiplicative element for the norm
\left\|-\right\|
.
This proves the inclusion (
182
).
In particular the set on the left hand side of (
180
) is contained in
\Aff_{W,b}(B\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\leq N})
.

Having fixed
W
and
b
, we can finally define a function
\lambda\colon\mathfrak{a}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by the formula:

(183)

\lambda(\alpha):=\frac{1}{\log R}\frac{\varphi_{\mathcal{O}}(W)}{\left\|W\right\|^{n}}\frac{\kappa_{\mathcal{O}}}{C_{\chi}}\Lambda_{R,\chi}^{\mathfrak{a}}(W\alpha+b)^{2}.

By (
170
) the function
\lambda
is
(R^{2m}/q,\rho,S,\mathfrak{o})
-pseudorandom.
Let us verify the other hypotheses in the Szemerédi theorem
3.2
.

By Lemma
5.5
, the restriction of
\lambda
to
B
equals the constant function
\frac{\varphi_{\mathcal{O}}(W)}{\left\|W\right\|^{n}}\frac{\kappa_{\mathcal{O}}}{C_{\chi}}\log R
.
This together with
(
177
)
and
(
182
)
implies:

(184)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\lambda\bm{1}_{B}\Bigm|\mathfrak{a}_{\leq N}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\left.\frac{1}{\varphi_{\mathcal{O}}(W)}\delta C_{\mathcal{O}}\frac{L}{\log_{q}L}\cdot\frac{\varphi_{\mathcal{O}}(W)}{\left\|W\right\|^{n}}\frac{\kappa_{\mathcal{O}}}{C_{\chi}}\log R\right/\left|\mathfrak{a}_{\leq N}\right|.

We have
\left|\mathfrak{a}_{\leq N}\right|\leq M^{n}/\left\|W\right\|^{n}\Nrm(\mathfrak{a})q^{g-1}
(which is an equality if
N
happens to be a power of
q^{d}
).
By the definition (
171
) of our parameters
we get for all sufficiently large
e>1
:

(185)

\geq\frac{1}{2}\delta C_{\mathcal{O}}\frac{\kappa_{\mathcal{O}}}{C_{\chi}}\frac{c_{\mathcal{D}}q^{g-1}\log(q)}{n(2m+1)}=:\delta_{1}.

This establishes one of the two requirements in the relative Szemerédi theorem
3.2
.

We have to establish one more inequality to invoke the relative Szemerédi theorem. By Lemma
5.5
we have

\lambda^{k}\bm{1}_{B}\leq\mathrm{const.}\cdot(\log R)^{2k}

where the constant comes from the coefficient in the definition of
\lambda
in (
183
). So:

(186)

\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\lambda^{k}\bm{1}_{B}\Bigm|\mathcal{O}_{\leq N}\Bigr)\leq\mathrm{const.}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2m+1}\log N\right)^{2k},

which is
<\gamma N
for
e
sufficiently large.

Now fix
e
so that it satisfies (
172
) and
is large enough to make all the above inequalities true. We can apply the relative Szemerédi theorem
3.2
to the current situation
by (
185
), (
186
) and the pseudorandomness of
\lambda
.
It follows that
B
contains an
\mathfrak{o}
-homothetic copy of
S
.
Sending it by the affine
\mathfrak{o}
-linear map
\Aff_{W,b}\colon B\to A
, we get an
\mathfrak{o}
-homothetic copy of
S
in
A
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
5.2
.
∎

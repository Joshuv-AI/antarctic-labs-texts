Abundant lattice instability in kagome metal ScV 6 Sn 6
Hengxin Tan, Binghai Yan

Abstract
Kagome materials are emerging platforms for studying charge and spin orders. In this work, we have revealed a rich lattice instability in a ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} kagome metal ScV 6 Sn 6 by first-principles calculations. Beyond verifying the 3 × 3 × 3 \sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{3}\times 3 charge density wave (CDW) order observed by the recent experiment, we further identified three more possible CDW structures, i.e., 3 × 3 × 2 \sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{3}\times 2 CDW with P ​ 6 / m ​ m ​ m P6/mmm symmetry, 2 × 2 × 2 2\times 2\times 2 CDW with I ​ m ​ m ​ m Immm symmetry, and 2 × 2 × 2 2\times 2\times 2 CDW with P ​ 6 / m ​ m ​ m P6/mmm symmetry. The former two are more energetically favored than the 3 × 3 × 3 \sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{3}\times 3 phase, while the third one is comparable in energy. These CDW distortions involve mainly out-of-plane motions of Sc and Sn atoms, while V atoms constituting the kagome net are almost unchanged. We attribute the lattice instability to the smallness of Sc atomic radius. In contrast, such an instability disappears in its sister compounds R R V 6 Sn 6 ( R R is Y, or a rare-earth element) because R R has a larger radius. Our work indicates that ScV 6 Sn 6 might exhibit varied CDW phases in different experimental conditions and provides insights to explore rich charge orders in kagome materials.

An emergent question is about the origin of CDW phase transition in ScV

In kagome lattices with van Hove singularity close to the Fermi energy, Fermi surface nesting is a potential mechanism for different charge orders.
As shown in Fig.
1

The Fermi surface nesting function is estimated by the imaginary part of the bare charge susceptibility under zero-frequency limit,

\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\chi^{\prime\prime}_{0}(\mathbf{q},\omega)/\omega=\sum_{nn^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}\delta(\varepsilon_{n,\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{0})\delta(\varepsilon_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\varepsilon_{0}),

(1)

where
\delta
is the Dirac-delta function.
\varepsilon_{n,\mathbf{k}}
is the eigenvalue of band
n
at
\mathbf{k}
and
\varepsilon_{0}
is the Fermi energy.
\mathbf{q}
is the nesting vector. Generally, the Fermi surface nesting is responsible for the CDW phase transition only when both the imaginary and real parts of the bare charge susceptibility diverge at the same wave vector
\mathbf{q}
(see discussions in, e.g., Ref.
Johannes and Mazin 2008
). The real part defining the stability of charge density reads

\chi^{\prime}_{0}(\mathbf{q})=\sum_{nn^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}\frac{\it{f}(\varepsilon_{n,\mathbf{k}})-\it{f}(\varepsilon_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})}{\varepsilon_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\varepsilon_{n,\mathbf{k}}},

(2)

where
f(\varepsilon)
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution.

We hope our work will inspire more experiments to investigate the complex lattice instabilities of related kagome materials.

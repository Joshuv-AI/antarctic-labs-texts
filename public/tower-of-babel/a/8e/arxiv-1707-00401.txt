2
Some notions and notation

A
general directed graph

D

(or
a digraph with possible multiple arcs and loops
)
is a triple
(V,E,\psi)
, where
V
and

E
are finite sets,
V
is non-empty,
and
\psi
is a function from
E
to

V\times V
(and so

\psi(e)
is the ordered pair of
ends
of arc
e
in
E
). If
\psi(e)=(v,v)
for some

v\in V
, then arc
e
is called a
loop
in
D
.
The sets
V
and
E
are called the
vertex set
and the
arc set
of digraph
D
and denoted by
V(D)
and
E(D)
, respectively.
Let
v(D)=|V(D)|
and
e(D)=|E(D)|
.

Given two general digraphs

D_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1},\psi_{1})
and

D_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2},\psi_{2})
, a pair

(\alpha_{v},\alpha_{e})
of bijections

\alpha_{v}:V_{1}\to V_{2}
and

\alpha_{e}:E_{1}\to E_{2}
is called an
isomorphism from
D_{1}
to
D_{2}
if for every
a\in E_{1}
,

\psi_{1}(a)=(x,y)\Leftrightarrow\psi_{2}(\alpha_{e}(a))=(\alpha_{v}(x),\alpha_{v}(y))
.
We say that
digraph
D
is isomorphic to digraph
F
(or equivalently,
D
and
F
are isomorphic
) and write
D\cong F
if there exists an isomorphism from
D
to
F
.

A
directed graph
(or a
digraph
) is a general digraph
(V,E,\psi)
, where function

\psi:E\to V\times V
is injective.
In other words, a digraph

D
is a pair
(V,E)
, where
V
is a non-empty set and
E\subseteq V\times V
, and so
D
has no multiple arcs but may have at most one loop in each vertex.
If
E=V\times V
, then digraph
D
is called
a
complete digraph
and denoted by
K_{\circ}
.

Given two digraphs

D_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1})
and

D_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
, a bijection

\alpha:V_{1}\to V_{2}
is called an
isomorphism from
D_{1}
to
D_{2}
if

(x,y)\in E_{1}\Leftrightarrow(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))\in E_{2}
.
As above, we say that
digraph
D
is isomorphic to digraph
F
(or equivalently,
D
and
F
are isomorphic
) and write
D\cong F
if there exists an isomorphism from
D
to
F
.

A digraph
D=(V,E)
is called
simple
if
D
has no loops, and so
E\subseteq\{V\times V\}
, where

\{V\times V\}=V\times V\setminus\{(x,x):x\in V\}
.
Given a digraph
D
with
V=V(D)=V(K_{\circ})
, let

D^{c}_{\circ}=K_{\circ}\setminus E(D)
. Digraph

D^{c}_{\circ}
is called the
K_{\circ}
-complement of

D
.
Let
K
be the graph obtained from
K_{\circ}
by removing all its loops, i.e.
E(K)=\{V\times V\}
. We call
K

a
simple complete digraph
.
Given a simple digraph
D
, let

D^{c}=K\setminus E(D)
.
Digraph
D^{c}
is called the

K
-complement
(or simply,
complement
)
of
D
.

A digraph
D=(V,E)
is called
(X,Y)
-bipartite
if

V=X\cup Y
,
X\cap Y=\emptyset
, and
E\subseteq X\times Y
. If, in addition,
E=X\times Y
,
then

is called a
complete
(X,Y)
-bipartite digraph
and is denoted by
K_{XY}
.
Given an
(X,Y)
-bipartite digraph, let

D^{cb}=K_{XY}\setminus E(D)
. Digraph
D^{cb}
is called the
(X,Y)
-bipartite complement of

D
.

If
e\in E
is an arc in
D
and

\psi(e)=(u,v)

(possibly,

), then

is called the
tail
of arc
e
and

called the
head
of arc

and we put

and

.
Obviously,

and

are functions:

and

.
The functions

and
h
can also be described by the corresponding

-matrices

and

(t)

the

of
D
, where

if

and

, otherwise,
and

(h)

the

,
where

if
v_{i}=h(e_{j})
and

The

of

Obviously,

For

.

A digraph

, and

An undirected graph

and

A digraph

not connected

.
Obviously, two different components of

A digraph

For a digraph

, let

. The digraph

.
Let

and with no arcs,
D^{1}
the simple complete digraph with vertex set

,

(
{\cal T}^{cb}(D)
) denote the digraph with vertex set

and such that

,

, and vertex

(resp.,

) in

, and so

is an arc in

,

) in

Given two digraphs

, let

6
Digraph-functions and their
xyz
-transformations

A digraph
D=(V,E)
is called a
digraph-function

[
15
]

if there exists a function

f:V\to V
such that

(x,y)\in E
if and only if
y=f(x)
. Similar digraphs were considered in
[
10
]
.

A digraph
D
is called a
directed cycle
or simply,
dicycle
if
D
is connected and

d_{in}(x)=d_{out}(x)=1
for every
x\in V(D)
.
A
directed
xy
-path
or simply,
xy
-dipath
is a digraph obtained from a dicycle with an arc
(y,x)
by removing arc
(y,x)
.

Let
{\cal F}
denote the set of digraphs
F
such that
each component of
F
is either a digraph-function or its inverse. Let
{\cal CF}
denote the set of connected digraphs in
{\cal F}
.
It is easy to see that if
F\in{\cal CF}
,
then
F
has a unique directed cycle
C

(possibly, a loop). Let
c(F)=v(C)
.

6.1
Digraph-function criteria

The following simple observation provides different digraph-function criteria.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
D
be a connected digraph with
n
vertices. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(a1)

D
is a digraph-function
(
resp., the inverse of digraph-function
)
,

(a2)
each vertex of
D
has out-degree one
(
resp., in-degree one
)
,

(a3)

t:E(D)\to V(D)

(
resp.,
h:E(D)\to V(D)
)
is an isomorphism from
D^{l}
to

,

(a4)

if
V(D)=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
e_{i}=t^{-1}(i)
, then
T(D)=I_{n}
and

H^{\top}(D)=A(D)

(
resp.,
H(D)=I_{n}
and
T(D)=A(D)
, and

)
, and
A(D^{l})=H^{\top}(D)=A(D)
,

Here is another interesting criterion for a digraph to be a digraph-function or its inverse.

Theorem 6.2

Proof

It is sufficient to prove our claim for a connected digraph

By

Suppose, on the contrary, that

First we define special digraphs which we call

Given three disjoint dipaths

q^{\prime}Qq

rRr^{\prime}

Let

\Box

\end{array}\right|=\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda I_{n}-A&~~-I_{n}\\[3.01385pt]
-A&~~\lambda I_{n}-A\\
\end{array}\right|.

Adding the second row of the above block matrix to the first row, we obtain

\displaystyle A(\lambda,F^{+++})=\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda I_{n}&~~-(\lambda+1)I_{n}+A\\[3.01385pt]
-A&~~\lambda I_{n}-A\\
\end{array}\right|.

By Lemma
3.6
, we have from
(
6.2
):

\displaystyle A(\lambda,F^{+++})=|\lambda I_{n}|~|\lambda I_{n}-A-\lambda^{-1}A((\lambda+1)I_{n}-A)|=|\lambda^{2}I_{n}-(2\lambda+1)A+A^{2}|.

Therefore

A(\lambda,F^{+++})=A(x_{1},F)~A(x_{2},F)
,
where

and
x_{2}
are the roots of

and

x_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(2\lambda+1-\sqrt{4\lambda+1})
.

\Box

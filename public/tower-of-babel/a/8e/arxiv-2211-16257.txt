The vanishing limit of a rigid body in three-dimensional viscous incompressible fluid The authors would like to thank Sylvain Ervedoza (Université de Bordeaux) for helpful discussions and also Takéo Takahashi (Inria Nancy) for suggestions. P. Su is partially supported by ERC-CZ grant LL2105 and the University Centre UNCE/SCI/023 of Charles University.
Jiao He 1, Pei Su 2

Abstract
We consider the evolution of a small rigid body in an incompressible viscous fluid filling the whole space ℝ 3 {\mathbb{R}}^{3} . When the small rigid body shrinks to a “massless” point in the sense that its density is constant, we prove that the solution of the fluid-rigid body system converges to a solution of the Navier-Stokes equations in the full space. Based on some L p − L q L^{p}-L^{q} estimates of the fluid-structure semigroup and a fixed point argument, we obtain a uniform estimate of velocity of the rigid body. This allows us to construct admissible test functions which plays a key role in the procedure of passing to the limit.



Key words.
Fluid-rigid body,
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimate, fluid-structure semigroup, 3D viscous incompressible fluid, singular limit.

1
Introduction

In this work, we investigate the interaction between a viscous
incompressible fluid and a small rigid body, which is completely
immersed in the fluid. The fluid we consider here is governed
by the three dimensional Navier-Stokes equations. The motion
of the rigid body obeys the conservation of linear and angular
momentum, in particular, without taking into account the influence
of the gravity. Here we are interested in describing the dynamics of
the fluid-body system as the rigid body shrinks to a point.
Now let us precisely state the problem.

1.1
Statement of the problem

We assume that the rigid body is a ball of radius
{\varepsilon}>0
and
centered at
h_{\varepsilon}(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, occupying the area

\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(t)=\overline{B(h_{\varepsilon}(t),{\varepsilon})}

in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. The fluid domain, denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(t)
, is
the exterior part of
\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(t)
, i.e.

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(t)={\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(t).

We denote by

the unit normal vector field of

{\partial}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(t)
, which directs toward the interior of

\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(t)
and is independent of the size of the body
{\varepsilon}
.
The fluid is supposed to be homogeneous with
density
\rho_{\varepsilon}^{f}=1
and constant viscosity
\nu>0
.
The velocity and the pressure in the fluid
are denoted by
u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)
and
p_{\varepsilon}(t,x)

With the above notation, the system describing

Fluid equations:

\displaystyle{}\begin{split}\partial_{t}u_{{\varepsilon}}+(u_{{\varepsilon}}\cdot\nabla)u_{{\varepsilon}}-\nu\Delta u_{{\varepsilon}}+\nabla p_{{\varepsilon}}=0{\hbox{$\hskip 31.29802pt\forall\;$}}y\in\mathcal{F}_{{\varepsilon}}(t),\\

\displaystyle J_{{\varepsilon}}\dot{\omega}_{{\varepsilon}}(t)=-\int_{\partial\mathcal{S}_{{\varepsilon}}(t)}(y-h_{{\varepsilon}})\times(\sigma(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})n){\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}{\rm d}\hbox{\hskip 0.5pt}s,\;\;\;{}

\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{{\varepsilon}}(t,{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}y)=\dot{h}_{{\varepsilon}}(t)+\omega_{{\varepsilon}}(t)\times\left(y-h_{{\varepsilon}}(t)\right){\hbox{$\hskip 31.29802pt\forall\;$}}y\in\partial\mathcal{S}_{{\varepsilon}}(t).\end{split}

\displaystyle m_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(t)}\rho{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}{\rm d}\hbox{\hskip 0.5pt}y,

Remark 1.1

(Energy estimates)
.

Taking the inner product of (
1.1
) with
u_{\varepsilon}
, integrating the result by parts and using the equations (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), we get the following energy estimate (see, for instance, Gunzburger et al.
[
13
]

\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{{\varepsilon}}(t))}^{2}+m_{\varepsilon}|\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}+\left(J_{\varepsilon}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\omega_{{\varepsilon}}(t)\right)\cdot\omega_{{\varepsilon}}(t)+4{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\nu\int_{0}^{t}\|D(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{{\varepsilon}}(t))}^{2}\\
\leqslant\|u_{\varepsilon}^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{{\varepsilon}}(0))}^{2}+m_{\varepsilon}|{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}l_{\varepsilon}^{0}|^{2}+\left(J_{\varepsilon}\omega_{{\varepsilon}}^{0}\right)\cdot\omega_{{\varepsilon}}^{0}.

Note that the tensor of inertia

is positive.
We observe that if the initial data
u_{\varepsilon}^{0}
,

and
\omega_{{\varepsilon}}^{0}
are bounded, we then have the boundedness of

and
m_{\varepsilon}|\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}
. Nevertheless, the energy estimate above is not enough to obtain a uniform estimate of the velocity of the rigid body i.e.
\dot{h}_{\varepsilon}(t)
when the mass of the rigid body tends to zero.

1.2
Weak solutions and main results

In order to introduce the

and state the existence of solutions of the system (

1.5

u_{\varepsilon}(t,y)=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(t)}u_{\varepsilon}(t,y)+\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(t)}\left(\dot{h}_{{\varepsilon}}(t)+\omega_{{\varepsilon}}(t)\times\left(y-h_{{\varepsilon}}(t)\right)\right){\hbox{$\hskip 31.29802pt\forall\;$}}y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3},

Clearly, with conditions (

u_{\varepsilon}^{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),\quad{\rm div\,}u_{\varepsilon}^{0}=0\;\;\text{in}\;\;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.\\

We also define the global density of the fluid-rigid body system in

as follows:

\rho_{\varepsilon}(t,y)=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(t)}(y)+\rho\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(t)}(y){\hbox{$\hskip 31.29802pt\forall\;$}}t>0,\,\,\,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.

(1.10)

Theorem 1.3

(Existence result)
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}^{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
be divergence free such that
D(u_{\varepsilon}^{0})=0
in
\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(0)
.
Then there exists at least one global weak solution

. Moreover,

\displaystyle{}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\rho_{{\varepsilon}}|u_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}+4\nu\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|D(u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\rho_{{\varepsilon}}(0)|u_{\varepsilon}^{0}|^{2}{\hbox{$\hskip 31.29802pt\forall\;$}}t>0.

Our purpose in this article is to investigate the asymptotic limit of the system (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) when the size of the rigid body goes to
0
. Before stating our main result, let us first review some related references. The vanishing limit of small obstacle problem has been studied by a number of work, in both incompressible and compressible cases, with various assumptions on the fluid and, in particular, on the parameters of the body.

When the small rigid body is fixed inside the fluid, for instance, Iftimie et al.
[
16
]
proved the limit of
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the 2D Navier-Stokes equations, with assumptions on the initial vorticity and the circulation of the velocity around the body. Later on, Iftimie and Kelliher
[
17
]
considered the same problem in 3D. Moreover, the influence of the precise shape of the small obstacle in the fluid is of particular interest. Lacave studied the case of a thin obstacle tending to a curve in
[
18
]
for 2D and in
[
19
]
for 3D. More recently, Chipot et al. considered in
[
3
]
the vanishing limit problem in a 2D periodic flow where the domain of the fluid is punctured by a star-shaped open connected set.

In the case that the obstacle can move under the influence of the fluid, the asymptotic behaviour of the fluid-rigid body system becomes more complicated. Lacave and Takahashi in
[
20
]
considered a small moving disk into a viscous incompressible fluid in 2D with
the assumption that the mass of the obstacle tends to zero along with its size and some small initial data. When the density of the body goes to infinity as its size goes to zero, the convergence has been proved by He and Iftimie
[
14
]
for 2D and
[
15
]
for 3D only using energy estimates and without small initial data. More recently,
Feireisl et al.
[
9
]
extended the result of
[
20
]
by removing the restriction on the initial data and the shape of the rigid body for both 2D and 3D. In particular, their assumptions allow the density of the body to be asymptotically small. Moreover, Bravin and Nečasová

[
2
]
obtained some estimates of the velocity of the rigid body under the assumptions
m_{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}\to+\infty
for 3D and
m_{\varepsilon}\geqslant C>0
for 2D, which seems helpful for vanishing rigid body problem.
In addition, there are some recent work that dealt with the similar problem in viscous compressible fluid, please refer to
[
1
,
10
,
11
]
for more details.

Intuitively, the vanishing process of the body should be induced directly by the decreasing of the volume of the body, rather than the variation of the density. With constant density, the technique employed in
[
20
]
is the so-called
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimate of the
fluid-structure semigroup
established in Ervedoza et al.
[
6
]
, which gives a uniform estimate of the obstacle velocity.
Here we are interested in studying the corresponding problem with the similar setting in 3D, based on the recent long time behaviour work by Ervedoza et al.
[
7
]
. We finally remark that the long-time behavior of solutions to a fluid-rigid disk system in 2D case has been also studied in Ferriere and Hillairet
[
12
]
with somewhat different setting.

Still using the notation introduced at the beginning of this section, our main theorem can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.4
.

There exists a constant
\gamma_{0}>0
such that if

where

, i.e.,

(1.11)

for any

.

The main novelty brought by the present paper is twofold. Firstly, there is no specific assumption for the density of the rigid body
\rho
, which is constant and independent of its size
{\varepsilon}
. That is to say, we deal with the case where the rigid body shrinks to a
massless
point:

m_{\varepsilon}={\varepsilon}^{3}m_{1},\qquad J_{\varepsilon}={\varepsilon}^{5}J_{1}.

Secondly, as already mentioned in Remark
1.1
, energy estimate is not enough to get such estimate when the mass changes along with
{\varepsilon}
. We thus showed that the
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimate, associated with the
fluid-structure semigroup
, in 3D is
{\varepsilon}-
invariant, i.e. independent of the size of body, allowing us to estimate the velocity of the rigid body in a uniform way. This therefore implies the uniform convergence of

, i.e., up to a subsequence, as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
,

h_{{\varepsilon}}\longrightarrow h\quad\text{uniformly in}\quad[0,T],

where
h\in W^{1,q}(0,T)
for
1<q<\frac{4}{3}
.
Please refer to subsection
2.3
for more details.

Remark 1.5
.

By constructing an appropriate approximation sequence of the test function, we actually obtain a local strong convergence for the solution of the fluid-body system, up to a subsequence, i.e. for every
T>0
,

u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\longrightarrow u\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\text{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})).

The proof is presented in Section
4
.

Remark 1.6
.

Although our main result is stated for constant density, presumably our proof still works for the case where the density of the rigid body is larger than some positive
\rho_{0}
independent of
{\varepsilon}
. Moreover, when the rigid body shrinks to
massive
point:

m_{\varepsilon}=m_{1},\qquad J_{\varepsilon}={\varepsilon}^{2}J_{1},

a similar result can be proven without using the
L^{p}-L^{q}
analysis.
The above results should hold as well in the case of several bodies, as long as there is no collision.

1.3
Organization of the paper

In Section
2
, we first introduce change of variables to formulate the fluid-body system in a fixed space domain. In particular, we give the uniform estimate for the solid velocity which is based on the properties of the fluid-structure semi-group. Then we constructed in Section
3
an approximation sequence of the test function for the fluid-body system and this will play a key role in the justification of the limit. To deal with the nonlinear term, we show a strong convergence of subsequence for the solution of fluid-body system in Section
4
.
Section

is devoted to passing to the limit from the fluid-body system to the pure fluid system.

2.2

Recalling the formula of the mass

(2.9)

, we estimate
the last two terms on the above expression:

{\varepsilon}^{-1}\lVert\varphi(\chi_{\varepsilon}-1)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})},

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\left(\varphi\chi_{\varepsilon}-\varphi\right)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}

\displaystyle=\lVert\nabla\varphi(\chi_{\varepsilon}-1)+\varphi\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}.

Hence, we conclude that

{\varepsilon}^{-1}\lVert\varphi_{\varepsilon}-\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}+\left\lVert\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})},

which implies directly that

\varphi_{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow\varphi\quad\text{strongly
in }\quad L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})).

For the time derivative, using
(
3.9
)
we compute:

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon}-{\partial}_{t}\varphi

\displaystyle=\chi_{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}\varphi+\varphi\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon}-{\partial}_{t}g_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+\nabla\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon}(t,y)\left(-\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\dot{h}(t)\right)-{\partial}_{t}\varphi

(3.11)

\displaystyle={\partial}_{t}\varphi{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}(\chi_{\varepsilon}-1)+\varphi\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon}-\dot{h}(t)\nabla g_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-{\partial}_{t}g_{\varepsilon}\left(t,x\right).

We estimate the terms on the right hand side of
(
3.11
)

one by one:

\lVert{\partial}_{t}\varphi(\chi_{\varepsilon}-1)\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leqslant\lVert{\partial}_{t}\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\lVert\chi_{\varepsilon}-1\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert{\partial}_{t}\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})},

\lVert\varphi\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})},

\lVert\dot{h}(t)\nabla g_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}|\dot{h}(t)|{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})},

\lVert{\partial}_{t}g_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leqslant{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert{\partial}_{t}\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})},

where we used
(
3.10
)
and Lemma
3.1
.
Combining with all estimates above, we obtain from
(
3.11
)
that

\lVert{\partial}_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon}-{\partial}_{t}\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leqslant{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert{\partial}_{t}\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\dot{h}(t)|{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert{\partial}_{t}\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}.

Taking the
L^{q}
norm with respect to time
t
on
(0,T)
and using the embedding
L^{\infty}(0,T)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(0,T)
for
1<q<\frac{4}{3}
, we have

\lVert{\partial}_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon}-{\partial}_{t}\varphi\rVert_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+C{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert{\partial}_{t}\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}.

(3.12)

Moreover, note that

\lVert{\partial}_{t}\tilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}=\lVert\nabla\chi{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}{\partial}_{t}\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leqslant\lVert{\partial}_{t}\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\lVert\nabla\chi\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leqslant C\lVert{\partial}_{t}\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}.

(3.13)

Putting
(
3.8
)
,
(
3.12
)
and
(
3.13
)
together, we derive that

\lVert{\partial}_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon}-{\partial}_{t}\varphi\rVert_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}.

This immediately gives that

{\partial}_{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow{\partial}_{t}\varphi\quad\text{strongly in}\quad L^{q}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})).

Finally, according to the structure of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
in

(
3.9
)
, it is obvious to see that
(
3.3
)

and
(
3.4
)
hold, which ends the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Compared with the result in
[
20
]
,
here we have strong convergence of the approximate sequence

(\varphi_{\varepsilon})
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))

and
L^{q}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
. This benefits from that
we consider the convergence in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
,
rather than in
L^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, which is
to match the regularity of the weak solution for the
fluid-body system and for the Navier-Stokes equations.

.

We claim that the sequence
(\varphi_{\varepsilon})
constructed
in Proposition
3.2
is an admissible test
functions for fluid-body system. Indeed, we know from the
strong convergence
h_{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow h
that there
exists
\delta_{0}>0
, such that for every
{\varepsilon}<\delta_{0}
,
we have
|h_{\varepsilon}-h|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}
. In this case,
we obtain from
(
3.4
)
that

\varphi_{\varepsilon}\equiv 0\quad\text{in}\quad B(h_{\varepsilon}(t),{\varepsilon}).

4
Strong convergence

In order to pass to the limit for the non-linear term, we derive in this part the strong convergence of some sub-sequence of
(u_{{\varepsilon}})
in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\text{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
. Precisely, we prove the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

There exists a sub-sequence
(u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}})
of
(u_{\varepsilon})
which converges strongly in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
.

Proof.

We derive a time estimate and use the Arzelà-Ascoli lemma to get a strong convergence. By the definition of the test function constructed in Section
3
(see (
3.9
)), we find that even if
\varphi
does not depend on time variable, the modified test function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
still depends on. Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
be a test function with
{\rm div\,}\varphi=0
, we construct a new test function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
as in Section
3
,

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,x):=\varphi(x)\chi_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-g_{\varepsilon}(t,x),

(4.1)

where the function
\chi_{\varepsilon}
and
g_{\varepsilon}
has been introduced in
(
3.2
)
and
(
3.9
)
, respectively. It is not difficult to see that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,x)
defined in
(
4.1
)
satisfies the properties of Proposition
3.2
.

We first estimate the following term:

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\cdot\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,{\rm d}\hbox{\hskip 0.5pt}x\Bigr|

\displaystyle=\Bigl|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\left(\varphi(x)\chi_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-g_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\right)\,{\rm d}\hbox{\hskip 0.5pt}x\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\chi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert g_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left(\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}}+{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\right),

where we used Lemma
3.1
and estimate (
3.10
). Then by the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
in 3D, we get that

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\cdot\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,{\rm d}\hbox{\hskip 0.5pt}x\Bigr|\leqslant C\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}}\leqslant C_{1}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}},

where we used the boundedness of
u_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
.

Based on the above estimate we find that, for fixed
t
and for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with divergence free condition, the map

\varphi\longmapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\cdot\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,{\rm d}\hbox{\hskip 0.5pt}x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

is linear and continuous with respect to the
H^{2}
-norm. Therefore, according to the Lax-Milgram theorem, there exists some function
U_{\varepsilon}(t)\in(H_{\sigma}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))^{\prime}
, such that

\left\langle U_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi\right\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\cdot\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,{\rm d}\hbox{\hskip 0.5pt}x{\hbox{$\hskip 31.29802pt\forall\;$}}\varphi\in H_{\sigma}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

Based on this notation, we note that for
\psi\in H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
\mathbb{P}\psi\in H^{2}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})

|\left\langle\mathbb{P}U_{\varepsilon},\psi\right\rangle|=|\left\langle U_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{P}\psi\right\rangle|=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot(\mathbb{P}\psi)_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}x\right|\leqslant C\lVert\mathbb{P}\psi\rVert_{H^{2}}\leqslant C\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{2}}.

This gives us that

\|\mathbb{P}U_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H_{\sigma}^{-2}}\leqslant C_{1}{\hbox{$\hskip 31.29802pt\forall\;$}}t\geqslant 0,

(4.2)

where
\mathbb{P}
denotes the usual Leray projector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, i.e. the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection on the subspace of divergence-free vector fields.

Recalling Remark
3.4
, for small
{\varepsilon}>0
, the function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,x)
constructed in
(
4.1
)
is an admissible test functions for the fluid-body system.
Thereby, using the definition of
\langle U_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi\rangle
, we obtain from the weak formulation of
u_{\varepsilon}
on the time interval
(s,t)
that

\displaystyle\langle U_{\varepsilon}(t)-U_{\varepsilon}(s),\varphi\rangle

(4.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\cdot\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,{\rm d}\hbox{\hskip 0.5pt}x-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}(s,x)\cdot\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s,x)\,{\rm d}\hbox{\hskip 0.5pt}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\partial_{\tau}\varphi_{\varepsilon}+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\right)u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi_{\varepsilon}-2\nu\int_{s}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}D(u_{\varepsilon}):D(\varphi_{\varepsilon}).

We shall bound the three terms in the right-hand side above.
Since there is
\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}
on the right side of
(
4.3
)
, we need
H^{1}
-estimate of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
.
According to Proposition
3.2
, we know that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
converges strongly to
\varphi
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
, which implies that

\lVert\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})}\leqslant\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{1}}+\lVert\varphi_{\varepsilon}-\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})}\leqslant C\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}}.

(4.4)

Thus, by using Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\left|\nu\int_{s}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}D(u_{\varepsilon}):D(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\nu\int_{s}^{t}\lVert D(u_{\varepsilon})\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert D(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\nu(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}}\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\nu(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}},

where we used
(
4.4
)
and the boundedness of
u_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
.
Then we treat the nonlinear term in (
4.3
). Using the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{6}
and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
\lVert f\rVert_{L^{3}}\leqslant C\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we bound

\displaystyle\left|\int_{s}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\right)u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi_{{\varepsilon}}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{s}^{t}\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{3}}\lVert\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\varphi_{{\varepsilon}}\rVert_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int_{s}^{t}\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert\varphi_{{\varepsilon}}\rVert_{H^{1}}.

Hence, by Hölder’s inequality in time, we get that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{s}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\right)u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi_{{\varepsilon}}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C(t-s)^{\frac{1}{4}}\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert^{\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})}\lVert\varphi_{{\varepsilon}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C(t-s)^{\frac{1}{4}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}},

where we used the estimate (
4.4
) and the boundedness of
u_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})
.

Now, we consider the most complicated term, i.e. the term with time-derivative in the right-hand side of (
4.3
). Recalling the construction of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
(see (
3.11
) for the explicit expression of
{\partial}_{\tau}\varphi_{\varepsilon}
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\partial_{\tau}\varphi_{\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\left(\chi_{\varepsilon}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}{\partial}_{\tau}\varphi+\varphi{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon}-{\partial}_{\tau}g_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)-\nabla g_{\varepsilon}\dot{h}(\tau)\right).

(4.5)

We conclude from the proof of Proposition
3.2
that

\lVert{\partial}_{\tau}g_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert{\partial}_{\tau}\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}.

As
\varphi
does not depend on time, thereby we observe that the first term and the third term on the right side of
(
4.5
)
disappear. Thus, we need to bound the remaining two terms. Then
(
4.5
)
becomes

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\partial_{\tau}\varphi_{\varepsilon}=\int_{s}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot(\varphi\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon})-\int_{s}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\left(\nabla g_{\varepsilon}\dot{h}(\tau)\right).

(4.6)

Recalling the property of the cut-off function
\chi_{\varepsilon}
in Lemma
3.1
, we derive that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{s}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot(\varphi{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon})\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{s}^{t}\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert\nabla\chi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-s)\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}}\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\tilde{C}(t-s){\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}}.

For the second term in the right-hand side of (
4.6
), we bound

\displaystyle\left|\int_{s}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\left(\nabla g_{\varepsilon}(\tau,y)\dot{h}(\tau)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{s}^{t}\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\dot{h}(\tau)\nabla g_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}}\int_{s}^{t}\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{2}}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}|\dot{h}(\tau)|

\displaystyle\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-s)^{1/q^{\prime}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}}\lVert h\rVert_{W^{1,q}}\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\tilde{C}(t-s)^{1/q^{\prime}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}}\quad\quad 4<q^{\prime}<\infty,

where we used the estimates of
g_{\varepsilon}
in
(
3.10
)
.
Gathering the two estimates above, we get that

\left|\int_{s}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\partial_{\tau}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right|\leqslant\tilde{C}(t-s)^{1/q^{\prime}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}},

with
4<q^{\prime}<\infty
.
Consequently, combining all the estimates above, we have, for some constant
C
depending on
\nu
,

\displaystyle\left|\langle U_{\varepsilon}(t)-U_{\varepsilon}(s),\varphi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(C\nu(t-s)^{1/2}+C(t-s)^{1/4}+\tilde{C}(t-s)^{1/q^{\prime}}\right)\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C(t-s)^{1/q^{\prime}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}}.

It implies that
(\mathbb{P}U_{\varepsilon})
is equi-continuous in time with value in
H_{\sigma}^{-2}
. Since
(\mathbb{P}U_{\varepsilon})
is uniformly bounded in
H^{-2}_{\sigma}
(see (
4.2
)), according to the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem and compact embedding
H^{-2}\hookrightarrow H^{-3}_{\text{loc}}
, we are able to extract a sub-sequence
(\mathbb{P}U_{{\varepsilon}_{k}})
of
(\mathbb{P}U_{\varepsilon})
which converges strongly in
C^{0}(0,T;H^{-3}_{\text{loc}})
. We denote the limit of
(\mathbb{P}U_{{\varepsilon}_{k}})
by
u
, i.e.

\mathbb{P}U_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\longrightarrow u\quad\text{strongly in}\quad L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-3}_{\text{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})).

(4.7)

As
\varphi
is divergence free, we have
\mathbb{P}\varphi=\varphi
. It then follows from the property of the Leray projector that

\langle\mathbb{P}U_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}},\varphi\rangle=\langle U_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}},\varphi\rangle.

Recalling the definition of
U_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}
and the test function
\varphi_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}
, we can bound

\displaystyle\left|\langle\mathbb{P}U_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}},\varphi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}(t,x)\cdot\left(\varphi_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}(t,x)-\varphi(x)\right)\,{\rm d}\hbox{\hskip 0.5pt}x\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\varphi_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\varphi\left(\chi_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-1\right)-g_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert\chi_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-1\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert g_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C{{\varepsilon}_{k}}^{3/2}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C{{\varepsilon}_{k}}^{3/2}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}},

where we used Lemma
3.1
and
(
3.10
)
. By the density of
C^{\infty}_{0,\sigma}
in
H^{2}_{\sigma}
, it gives that

\lVert\mathbb{P}U_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{H^{-2}}\leqslant C{{\varepsilon}_{k}}^{3/2}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}}.

As
u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})
, we get that

\lVert\mathbb{P}U_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-2})}\leqslant C{{\varepsilon}_{k}}^{3/2}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\varepsilon}\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

This, together with
(
4.7
)
, implies that

u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\varepsilon}_{k}\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}u\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-3}_{\text{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})).

Finally, by using the interpolation inequality:

\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{\frac{8}{3}}(0,T;L_{\rm loc}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}\leqslant C\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H_{\rm loc}^{-3}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}^{\frac{1}{4}}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{\rm loc}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}^{\frac{3}{4}},

we conclude that

u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\longrightarrow u\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\text{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})).

This completes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

5
Proof of the main Theorem

Combined with the modified test function constructed in Proposition
3.2
and the strong convergence in Proposition
4.1
, now we are able to prove our main result, i.e. Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We aim to prove that the limit
u
obtained in Proposition
4.1
is a solution of the Navier-Stokes equations in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with initial data
u_{0}(x)
. For every fixed and finite
T>0
, we need to show that the limit
u
verifies

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u\cdot\left({\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi+(u\cdot\nabla){\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s+\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\nabla u:\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}u^{0}(x)\cdot\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}([0,T);H_{\sigma}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})).

Based on our assumptions in Theorem
1.4
, we know that

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}\;\text{is bounded in}\;L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\right)\cap L^{2}\left(0,T;H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\right).

According to weak compactness, for the limit
u
in Proposition
4.1
, there exists sub-sequence
(u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}})
of
(u_{\varepsilon})
such that

\displaystyle u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{weak-$\ast$ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})),

\displaystyle u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})).

(5.1)

The above subsequence
(u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}})
can be chosen by diagonalization method.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
{\rm div\,}\varphi=0
. For small
{\varepsilon}>0
, we can take the family
(\varphi_{\varepsilon})_{{\varepsilon}>0}
obtained in Proposition
3.2
as the test functions of the fluid-solid system (see Remark
3.4
). According to Definition
1.2
, we have

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi_{{\varepsilon}}\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot(u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla){\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi_{{\varepsilon}}\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s\\
+2\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}D(u_{\varepsilon}):D(\varphi_{{\varepsilon}})\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}u_{\varepsilon}^{0}(x)\cdot\varphi_{{\varepsilon}}(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(5.2)

We pass to the limit
{\varepsilon}\to 0
for the four terms above.

Firstly, recalling the convergence results
(
3.6
)
in Proposition
3.2
and the continuous embedding
W^{1,q}[0,T)\hookrightarrow C^{0}[0,T)
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{{\varepsilon}}(0,x)\longrightarrow\varphi(0)\;\text{strongly in}\;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

Together with the assumption that
u_{\varepsilon}^{0}(x)
converges weakly to
u^{0}(x)
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, we derive that

\displaystyle{}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{{\varepsilon}}^{0}(x)\cdot\varphi_{{\varepsilon}}(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\longrightarrow\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u^{0}(x)\cdot\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(5.3)

Putting together the weak convergence in (
5.1
) and the strong convergence of
\varphi_{{\varepsilon}}
in
(
3.5
)
,
we immediately obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}D(u_{{\varepsilon}}):D(\varphi_{{\varepsilon}}){\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s\longrightarrow\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}D(u):D(\varphi){\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s.

Moreover, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}D(u):D(\varphi){\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\nabla u:\nabla\varphi{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}{\rm div\,}u\,{\rm div\,}\varphi{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\nabla u:\nabla\varphi{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}x.

Thus, we further have

\displaystyle{}2\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}D(u_{{\varepsilon}}):D(\varphi_{{\varepsilon}}){\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s\longrightarrow\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\nabla u:\nabla\varphi{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s.

(5.4)

For the term with time derivative in (
5.2
),
we decompose it as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\cdot{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}}_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{{{\varepsilon}}_{k}}\cdot({\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}}_{k}}-{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u)\cdot{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u\cdot{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s

The strong convergence of
{\partial}_{t}\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}}_{k}}
in
(
3.6
)
, together with the boundedness of the subsequence
u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})
, implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{{{\varepsilon}}_{k}}\cdot({\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}}_{k}}-{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}}_{k}}-{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant T^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}\lVert{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}}_{k}}-{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi\rVert_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\varepsilon}_{k}\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

where
q^{\prime}
is the conjugate of
q
.

Note that
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, according to the strong convergence of
u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}
in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\text{loc}})
in Proposition
4.1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u)\cdot{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u\rVert_{L^{2}_{\text{loc}}}\lVert{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\text{loc}})}\lVert{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\varepsilon}_{k}\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Gathering the estimates above, we get that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\cdot{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}}_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s\longrightarrow\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u\cdot{\partial}_{s}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s.

(5.5)

Now it remains to treat the nonlinear term in (
5.2
). That is to prove the convergence

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\otimes u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}):\nabla\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}_{k}}}\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\varepsilon}_{k}\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(u\otimes u):\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s.

(5.6)

To do this, we decompose it as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\otimes u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}):\nabla\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}_{k}}}\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\otimes u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}):(\nabla\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}_{k}}}-\nabla\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\otimes u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u\otimes u):\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(u\otimes u):\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s.

To treat the first term on the right-hand side, we use Hölder’s inequality and the Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality
\lVert f\rVert_{L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leqslant C\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{\frac{3}{4}}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{4}}
to derive that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\otimes u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}):(\nabla\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}_{k}}}-\nabla\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}_{k}}}-\nabla\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\nabla u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}_{k}}}-\nabla\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle T^{\frac{1}{4}}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})}^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}_{k}}}-\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\varepsilon}_{k}\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}0,

where we used the strong convergence of
\varphi_{{{\varepsilon}_{k}}}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})
(see (
3.5
) in Proposition
3.2
) and the boundedness of
u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}
in
L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{2}\right)\cap L^{2}\left(0,T;H^{1}\right)
.

For the second term on the right-hand side, we write it as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\otimes u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u\otimes u):\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s

(5.7)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u)\otimes u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}:\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}u\otimes(u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u):\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s.

Recalling that
\varphi
is compactly supported, by Hölder’s inequality and the strong convergence of
u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}
in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\text{loc}})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u)\otimes u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}:\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x{\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u\rVert_{L^{2}_{\text{loc}}}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle T^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}-u\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{\text{loc}})}\lVert u_{{\varepsilon}_{k}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\varepsilon}_{k}\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Doing a similar manner for the last term in
(
5.7
)
, the convergence (
5.6
) holds by combining the above estimates. Gathering the convergence (
5.3
)-(
5.6
), we conclude that the limit
u
verifies (
1.11
) and it is a Leray weak solution of the Navier-Stokes equations.

This ends the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

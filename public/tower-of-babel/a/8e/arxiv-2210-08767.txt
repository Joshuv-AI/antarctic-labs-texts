Bogomolov’s inequality and Higgs sheaves on normal varieties in positive characteristic
Adrian Langer

Abstract
We prove Bogomolov’s inequality on a normal projective variety in positive characteristic and we use it to show some new restriction theorems and a new boundedness result. Then we redefine Higgs sheaves on normal varieties and we prove restriction theorems and Bogomolov type inequalities for semistable logarithmic Higgs sheaves on some normal varieties in an arbitrary characteristic.

Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
defined over an algebraically closed field
k
and let
H
be an ample line bundle on
X
. Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a coherent torsion free
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module of rank
r
and let us set
\Delta({\mathcal{E}}):=2rc_{2}({\mathcal{E}})-(r-1)c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})^{2}
.

A well known Bogomolov’s theorem says that if
k
has characteristic zero and
{\mathcal{E}}
is slope
H
-semistable then

\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})H^{n-2}\geq 0.

This theorem was first proven by F. Bogomolov in the surface case. The higher dimensional case follows from restriction theorems for semistability (e.g., one can use the Mehta–Ramanathan restriction theorem).
An analogue of this theorem for slope
H
-stable Higgs bundles was proven by C. Simpson in
[
Si
]
using analytic methods. Simpson’s paper contains also applications of this result to uniformization and the Miyaoka –Yau inequality in higher dimensions (although only in the non-logarithmic case).
Later, T. Mochizuki in
[
Mo
]
generalized this inequality to the logarithmic case (he also used analytic methods). An algebraic proof of Bogomolov’s inequality for Higgs sheaves
appeared in
[
La5
]
and in the logarithmic case in
[
La6
]
. These papers contained also generalization of these results to positive characteristic.

More recently, in characteristic zero the above results have been generalized in
[
GKPT1
]

to projective varieties with klt singularities (but not in the logarithmic case). In the mildly singular logarithmic case one also knows the Miyaoka–Yau inequality (see
[
Ko
, Chapter 10]
and
[
La1
]
for the
2
-dimensional case and
[
GT
]
for higher dimensions).

One of the main motivations behind this paper is generalization of the above results to positive characteristic and strengthening of the results known in the characteristic zero. We also deal with semistability defined by a collection of nef line bundles instead of one ample line bundle. An importance of considering this generalized situation was first recognized by
Y. Miyaoka in
[
Mi
]
, who proved Bogomolov’s inequality for torsion free sheaves on normal
varieties smooth in codimension
2
in case of collections of ample and one nef line bundles.
However, it is not completely clear to the author if the original proof of Mehta–Ramanathan’s restriction theorem works so easily for multipolarizations on normal varieties as claimed in
[
Mi
, Corollary 3.13]
. In case of one ample line bundle on a normal projective variety defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, restriction theorem for semistable sheaves has been proven by H. Flenner in
[
Fl
]
. However, it seems that his proof cannot be generalized to multipolarizations.

In case of smooth projective varieties the corresponding Bogomolov’s inequality in any characteristic was proven in
[
La2
]
(however, the proof uses a different, new restriction theorem).
Using resolution of singularities one can use this to obtain Mehta–Ramanathan’s restriction theorem
for multipolarizations on normal varieties in characteristic zero.
In case of Higgs sheaves on smooth projective varieties, restriction theorem and Bogomolov’s inequality for multipolarizations has been proven in
[
La5
]
and in the logarithmic case in
[
La6
]
.

Our first main result is a strong restriction theorem for multipolarized normal varieties in positive characteristic, analogous to
[
La2
, Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 5.4]
. One of the problems here is with the definition of Chern classes. Below we use Chern classes of reflexive sheaves defined in
[
La9
]
(see Subsection
1.3
for a few basic properties).

Let
X
be a normal projective variety of dimension
n
defined over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p>0
. Let us fix a collection
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
of ample line bundles on
X

(in fact, we usually need weaker assumptions on this collection). Let
us set
d=L_{1}^{2}L_{2}...L_{n-1}
. Then we have the following
result (see Subsection
3.1
for the definition of
\beta_{r}
).

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module of rank
r\geq 2
. Let
m
be an integer such that

m>\left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{r}\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}+\frac{1}{dr(r-1)}+\frac{(r-1)\beta_{r}}{dr}\right\rfloor

and let
H\in|L_{1}^{\otimes m}|
be a normal hypersurface.
If
{\mathcal{E}}
is slope
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
-stable then
{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}
is slope
(L_{2}|_{H},\dots,L_{n-1}|_{H})
-stable.

The above theorem implies the following boundedness result.

Theorem 0.2
.

Let us fix some positive integer
r
, integer
{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{1}
and some real
numbers
{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}
and
\mu_{\max}
.
Then the set of reflexive coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules
{\mathcal{E}}
of rank
r
with
\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{1}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-1}={\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{1}
,
\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}\geq{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}
for
i=1,...,n-1
, and
\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{E}})\leq\mu_{\max}
is bounded.

In the statement above it is not even clear that the number of Hilbert polynomials of sheaves in the considered family is finite. In case
L_{1}=...=L_{n-1}
the above theorem follows from
[
La2
, Theorem 4.4]
. If
X
is smooth then the above theorem follows from
[
La2
, Corollary 5.4]
. But
it is no longer the case if we consider multipolarizations on normal varieties.

We also prove an analogue of Theorem
0.1
for (semi)stable Higgs sheaves on normal varieties (see Theorem
5.4
for a more precise version).

Theorem 0.3
.

Let
D\subset X
be an effective reduced Weil divisor and let
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
be a reflexive logarithmic Higgs sheaf of rank
r\geq 2
on
(X,D)
.
Let
m_{0}
be a non-negative integer such that
T_{X}(\log\,D)\otimes L_{1}^{\otimes m_{0}}
is globally generated. Let
m
be an integer such that

m>\max\left(\left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{r}\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}+\frac{1}{dr(r-1)}+\frac{(r-1)\beta_{r}}{dr}\right\rfloor,2(r-1)m_{0}^{2}\right).

and let
H\in|L_{1}^{\otimes m}|
be a general divisor. If
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
is slope
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
-stable then the logarithmic Higgs sheaf
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)|_{H}
on
(H,D\cap H)
is slope
(L_{2}|_{H},\dots,L_{n-1}|_{H})
-stable.

This theorem generalizes
[
La5
, Theorem 10]
that works for smooth varieties liftable to
W_{2}(k)
.

Finally, we use the above results to prove the following Bogomolov’s inequality for reflexive Higgs sheaves on mildly singular normal varieties. Note that, unlike previously known results for singular varieties in characteristic zero, our theorem holds for log pairs.

Theorem 0.4
.

Let
D\subset X
be an effective reduced Weil divisor such that the pair
(X,D)
is almost liftable to
W_{2}(k)
and it has
F
-liftable singularities in codimension
2
. Then for any
slope
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
-semistable logarithmic reflexive Higgs sheaf
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
of rank

r\leq p
we have

\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq 0.

For the meaning of almost liftable log pair and
F
-liftable singularities we refer the reader to Definitions
1.3
and
1.5
. If
X
is liftable to
W_{2}(k)
then it is almost liftable to
W_{2}(k)
and almost all reductions of varieties from characteristic zero satisfy this condition.
To understand the second notion we note that a reduction of quotient surface singularity is
F
-liftable in large characteristics (see Subsection
1.2
). In fact, for a dense set of primes, reductions of surfaces with log canonical singularities have
F
-liftable singularities (we do not prove this non-trivial fact as we will not need it in the following).

Now let
X
be a normal projective variety of dimension
n
defined over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
. Assume that
X
has at most quotient singularities in codimension
2
.
Let us fix a collection
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
of ample line bundles on
X
and set
d=L_{1}^{2}L_{2}...L_{n-1}
. Then Theorem
0.1
implies the following restriction theorem:

Theorem 0.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module of rank
r\geq 2
. Let
m
be an integer such that

m>\left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{r}\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}+\frac{1}{dr(r-1)}\right\rfloor

and let
H\in|L_{1}^{\otimes m}|
be a normal hypersurface.
If
{\mathcal{E}}
is slope
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
-stable then
{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}
is slope
(L_{2}|_{H},\dots,L_{n-1}|_{H})
-stable.

Now let us also fix an effective reduced Weil divisor
D\subset X
such that the pair
(X,D)
is log canonical in codimension
2
. Theorem
0.3
implies the following result:

Theorem 0.6
.

Let
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
be a reflexive logarithmic Higgs sheaf of rank
r\geq 2
on
(X,D)
.
Let
m_{0}
be a non-negative integer such that
T_{X}(\log\,D)\otimes L_{1}^{\otimes m_{0}}
is globally generated. Let
m
be an integer such that

m>\max\left(\left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{r}\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}+\frac{1}{dr(r-1)}\right\rfloor,2(r-1)m_{0}^{2}\right).

and let
H\in|L_{1}^{\otimes m}|
be a general divisor. If
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
is slope
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
-stable then the logarithmic Higgs sheaf
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)|_{H}
on
(H,D\cap H)
is slope
(L_{2}|_{H},\dots,L_{n-1}|_{H})
-stable.

Theorem
0.6
generalizes
[
GKPT1
, Theorem 5.22]
and
[
GKPT2
, Theorem 6.1]
, which are non-effective. In fact, we prove a stronger version of Theorem
0.6
(see Theorem
7.2
) that works for all normal divisors
H
for which restriction of
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
to
H
gives a logarithmic Higgs sheaf on
(H,D\cap H)
.

Similarly, Theorem
0.4
can be used to prove the following inequality generalizing
[
GKPT1
, Theorem 6.1]
.

Theorem 0.7
.

For any slope
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
-semistable logarithmic reflexive Higgs sheaf
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
we have

\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq 0.

Note that Bogomolov’s inequality for logarithmic Higgs sheaves has not been known so far even
on klt pairs. As in
[
Si
]
and
[
GKPT1
]
the above theorem implies Miyaoka–Yau inequalities for singular log pairs. Here we show only some simple applications of Theorem
0.4
to general Miyaoka–Yau inequalities in positive characteristic (see Section
6
), leaving statement of general results in characteristic zero to the interested reader. Let us remark that unlike previous works on Chern number inequalities in higher dimensions (e.g.,
[
GKPT1
]
and
[
GT
]
)
our method should work in much more general situations in characteristic zero, the only obstacle being unknown behaviour of Chern classes of reflexive sheaves under tensor operations on normal surfaces (see
[
La9
]
). In particular, an analogue of Theorems
0.1
,
0.2
and
0.3

should hold on any normal variety in characteristic zero and an analogue of Theorem
0.4
should hold for any pair
(X,D)
which is log canonical in codimension
2
. Appropriate versions are also expected if
D
is an arbitrary effective Weil
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor.

Here we should warn the reader that our definition of a reflexive Higgs sheaf is weaker than the one used in
[
GKPT1
]
and
[
GKPT2
]
. More precisely, a logarithmic reflexive Higgs sheaf
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
in our sense is a pair consisting of a coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
{\mathcal{E}}
and an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear map
T_{X}(\log D)\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
satisfying additional integrability condition. In the situation of
[
GKPT1
]
this would correspond to considering

{\mathcal{E}}\to({\mathcal{E}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\Omega_{X})^{**}
instead of
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}(\Omega_{X})^{**}
.
This is why in Section
4
we carefully explain differences between our approach and
[
GKPT1
]
. D. Greb et al. use a different definition as they need to pullback Higgs sheaves by birational morphisms to pass to a resolution of singularities. On the other hand, they cannot take duals or pushforward Higgs sheaves by open embeddings as is allowed in our approach. In this paper we do not use Kebekus’s pullback functor for reflexive differentials on klt pairs (see
[
Ke
]
) and we do not pullback Higgs sheaves by birational morphisms (cf. Subsection
4.8
). This allows us to obtain stronger results, e.g., Bogomolov’s inequality for reflexive extensions of semistable Higgs sheaves on the regular locus (cf.
[
GKPT1
, Theorem 6.1]
).

Further applications of the obtained results to non-abelian Hodge theory and Simpson’s correspondence are
postponed to
[
La10
]
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In the first section we gather some auxiliary results and introduce some notation. In Section 2 we prove a few results on Chern classes of reflexive sheaves on normal varieties in positive characteristic. These resuts are used in Section 3 to prove generalized versions of Theorems
0.1
and
0.2
. In Section 4 we study modules over Lie algebroids and generalized Higgs sheaves on normal varieties. In Section 5 we prove generalized versions of Theorems
0.3
and
0.4
. In Section 6 we apply these results to obtain the Miyaoka–Yau inequality for some normal varieties in positive characteristic. In Section 7 we show some applications of the obtained results in characteristic zero, proving Theorems
0.5
,
0.6
and
0.7
.
Section 8 contains an appendix in which we recall construction of the inverse Cartier transform used in Section 5.

Notation

If
f:X\to Y
is a morphism between normal schemes and
{\mathcal{E}}

is a coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
-module then we set

f^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}=(f^{*}{\mathcal{E}})^{**}.

If
f
is flat then we have
f^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}=f^{*}{\mathcal{E}}
.
But if
f
is not flat then usually the canonical map
f^{*}{\mathcal{E}}\to f^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}
is not an isomorphism.

If
X
is a normal scheme of characteristic
p
then we denote by
F_{X}
its absolute Frobenius morphism. If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module then for any positive integer
m
we set

F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{E}}=(F_{X}^{m})^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}.

Preliminaries

1.1
Reflexive sheaves

In this subsection
X
is an integral normal scheme, which is locally of finite type over a field
k
.
By
{\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}\,}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
we denote the category of coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules. It is a full subcategory of the category
{\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}\,}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
of coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules. The inclusion functor
{\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}\,}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})\to{\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}\,}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
comes with a left adjoint
(\cdot)^{**}:{\mathop{\operatorname{Coh}\,}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})\to{\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}\,}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
given by the reflexive hull. The category
{\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}\,}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})

comes with an associative and symmetric tensor product
\hat{\otimes}
given by

{\mathcal{E}}\hat{\otimes}{\mathcal{F}}:=({\mathcal{E}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{F}})^{**}.

An open subset
U\subset X
is called
big
if its complement
X\backslash U
has codimension
\geq 2
in
X
. If we consider
U
as a subscheme of
X
then we talk about a big open subscheme.
The following well-known lemma can be found in
[
SP
, Lemma 0EBJ]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
j:U\hookrightarrow X
be a big open subscheme. Then
j_{*}
and
j^{*}
define adjoint equivalences of categories
{\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}\,}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
and
{\mathop{\operatorname{Ref}\,}}({\mathcal{O}}_{U})
.

Since
X
is normal, its regular locus
X_{\mathrm{reg}}\subset X
is a big open subset.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a finite dominant morphism of integral locally Noetherian normal schemes.
If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
-module then we have a canonical isomorphism

f^{[*]}({\mathcal{E}}^{*})\simeq(f^{*}{\mathcal{E}})^{*}.

Proof.

We have a natural map

f^{*}({\mathcal{E}}^{*})=f^{*}{\mathop{{\cal H}om}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})\to{\mathop{{\cal H}om}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}(f^{*}{\mathcal{E}},f^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})=(f^{*}{\mathcal{E}})^{*}.

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is torsion free then there exists a big open subset
V\subset Y
such that
{\mathcal{E}}_{V}
is finite locally free.
Then the above map is an isomorphism on
U=f^{-1}(V)
. This subset of
X
is big because
f
is finite and dominant. Since
(f^{*}{\mathcal{E}})^{*}
is reflexive, this induces an isomorphism
f^{[*]}({\mathcal{E}}^{*})\simeq(f^{*}{\mathcal{E}})^{*}.

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is not torsion free, then the pullback of the quotient map
{\mathcal{E}}\to\tilde{\mathcal{E}}:={\mathcal{E}}/{\mathrm{Torsion}}
is surjective.
Hence the dual map
(f^{*}\tilde{\mathcal{E}})^{*}\to(f^{*}{\mathcal{E}})^{*}
is an isomorphism. But if we apply the lemma to
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}

then we get
f^{[*]}({\mathcal{E}}^{*})\simeq(f^{*}\tilde{\mathcal{E}})^{*}
, which proves the required assertion.
∎

1.2
F-liftable schemes

A Weil divisor on a locally Noetherian integral scheme
X
is a formal sum
\sum a_{i}D_{i}
,
where
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
D_{i}
are prime divisors. However, if all nonzero
a_{i}
are equal to
1
then we can consider an effective reduced Weil divisor as a reduced induced scheme structure on
D:=\bigcup_{\{i:a_{i}\neq 0\}}D_{i}\subset X
. If
f:X\to S
is a morphism of schemes and
X
is an integral locally Noetherian normal scheme then we say that a subscheme
D\subset X
is a
relative effective reduced Weil divisor
on
X/S
if
D
is an effective reduced Weil divisor and
D\to S
is a flat morphism.

A
log pair

(X,D)
is a pair consisting of a normal variety
X
defined over a perfect field
k
and an effective reduced Weil divisor
D
on
X
(we allow
D=0
). We say that
(X,D)
is
log smooth
if
X
is smooth and
D
is a normal crossing divisor. In this subsection we assume that
k
has positive characteristic
p
. We also set
S={\mathop{{\rm Spec\,}}}k
and
\tilde{S}={\mathop{{\rm Spec\,}}}W_{2}(k)
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
(X,D)
be a log pair and let us write
D=\sum D_{i}
, where
D_{i}
are irreducible.
We say that
(X,D)
is

1.

liftable to
W_{2}(k)
if there exists a flat morphism
\tilde{X}\to\tilde{S}
and
a relative effective Weil divisor
\tilde{D}=\sum\tilde{D}_{i}
on
\tilde{X}/\tilde{S}
such that
(X,D_{i})=(\tilde{X}\times_{\tilde{S}}S,\tilde{D}_{i}\times_{\tilde{S}}S)
for all
i
.
Such a pair

(\tilde{X},\tilde{D})
is called a
lifting
of
(X,D)
to
W_{2}(k)
.

2.

F
-liftable
if there exists a lifting
(\tilde{X},\tilde{D})
of
(X,D)
to
W_{2}(k)

and a morphism
\tilde{F}_{X}:\tilde{X}\to\tilde{X}
restricting to
F_{X}
modulo
p
such that
for each
D_{i}
the image of
\tilde{F}_{X}^{*}I_{\tilde{D}_{i}}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{\tilde{X}}
is contained in
I_{\tilde{D}_{i}}^{p}
. In this case we say that
\tilde{F}_{X}

is compatible with

\tilde{D}
and we call
\tilde{F}_{X}
an
F
-lifting of
(X,D)
(compatible with
(\tilde{X},\tilde{D})
)
.

3.

almost liftable to
W_{2}(k)
if there exists a big open subset
U\subset X
such that the pair
(U,D_{U}=D\cap U)
is liftable to
W_{2}(k)
. The corresponding lifting of
(U,D_{U})
is called an
almost lifting
of
(X,D)
.

4.

almost
F
-liftable
if there exists a big open subset
U\subset X
such that the pair

(U,D_{U})
is
F
-liftable. The corresponding lifting is called an
almost
F
-lifting
of
(X,D)
.

Remark 1.4
.

1.

If
U\subset X
as in (3)-(4) exists then we can always find a big open subset
V\subset X

such that
(V,D_{V})
is log smooth and the corresponding condition is satisfied.

2.

If
(X,D)
is almost

-liftable and

, where

(D_{i},(\bigcup_{j\neq i}D_{j})\cap D_{i})

.

Remark 1.6
.

1.

If
(X,D)
is log smooth and (almost) liftable to

then it is also locally

2.

Note that if there exists a big open subset

A characteristic
p
scheme
X
is called
F
-split
if there exists an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear map
\varphi:(F_{X})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}

F_{X}^{\sharp}:{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to(F_{X})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
.
If
Y_{1},...,Y_{s}
are closed subschemes of

then we say that they are
compatibly
F
-split

for all

.
For the basic facts about these notions we refer the reader to
[

]
.
In the proof of the next proposition we need the following generalization of the second part of
[
BK
, Proposition 1.3.11]
.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
.
Let us assume that
\varphi\in H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{1-p})\simeq{\mathop{{\rm Hom}}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}((F_{X})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X},{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
splits
X
. Let
Z(\varphi)=(p-1)D+D^{\prime}
be the divisor of zeroes of
\varphi
, where
D
and
D^{\prime}

are effective divisors. Then
D
is reduced and
\varphi
splits
X
compatibly with all irreducible components of
D
.

Proof.

Let
Y
be an irreducible component of
D
and let
x
be a smooth point of the support of
Z(\varphi)
that belongs to
Y
. Then we can choose a system of local coordinates
(t_{1},...,t_{n})
at
x
such that the local equation of
Y
is given by
t_{1}=0
.
Note that by assumption the local expansion of
\varphi
at
x
is given by

t_{1}^{m(p-1)}g(t_{1},...,t_{n})(dt_{1}\wedge...\wedge dt_{n})^{1-p},

where
g(t_{1},...,t_{n})
is not divisible by
t_{1}
and
m\geq 1
is the multiplicity of
Y
in
D
. Since
\varphi
splits
X
by
[
BK
, Theorem 1.3.8]
the coefficient of the monomial
(t_{1}...t_{n})^{p-1}
in
t_{1}^{m(p-1)}g(t_{1},...,t_{n})
is nonzero. Hence
m=1
and the splitting
\varphi
is compatible with
Y
at
x
. It follows that
\varphi
is compatible with
Y
at smooth points of the support of
Z(\varphi)
. So the required assertion follows from
[
BK
, Lemma 1.1.7, (ii)]
.
∎

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
(X,D)
be a log pair.

1.

If
X
is
F
-split compatibly with all irreducible components of
D
then
(X,D)
is liftable to
W_{2}(k)
.

2.

If
(X,D)
is almost
F
-liftable then
X
is
F
-split compatibly with all irreducible components of
D
.

Proof.

In case
D=0
the first part is contained in
[
La5
, Proposition 4]

and the second one follows from
[
BTLM
, Theorem 2]
(see also
[
AWZ2
, Section 2]
).
In general, the first part follows from
[

, Lemma 5.2.1]
. By
[

, Lemma 1.1.7, (ii) and (iii)]
to prove the second part it is sufficient to prove that if
(X,D)
is log smooth and

-liftable then irreducible components of

are compatibly
F
-split.
Note that the

-splitting induced by a lifting

that is compatible with

vanishes to order

along

∎

Remark 1.9
.

If

The problem is that the Frobenius splitting coming from the lifting of the Frobenius morphism to

(p-1)

Example 1.10

Let us consider divisor

\tilde{D}:=(x_{1}=0)\subset\tilde{X}

Let us consider a lifting

Lemma 1.12
.

Let

Theorem 1.13
.

Let

Implications

Then every point

1.3
Intersection theory on normal varieties

Let
X
be a normal projective variety of dimension
n
defined over an algebraically closed field
k
.
In the following we write
A^{1}(X)
for the class group of
X
, i.e., the group of Weil divisors modulo
rational equivalence on
X
. If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module of rank
r\geq 1
then the sheaf
\det{\mathcal{E}}=(\bigwedge^{r}{\mathcal{E}})^{**}
is reflexive of rank
1
and we can consider the associated class
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})\in A^{1}(X)
of Weil divisors on
X
.

More generally, we write
A_{m}(X)
for the group of
m
-cycles modulo rational equivalence on
X
. Chern classes of vector bundles on
X
are considered as in
[
Fu
]
as operations on
A_{*}(X)
.

We say that two line bundles
L
and
M
on
X
are
numerically equivalent

if for every proper curve
C\subset X
we have

\int_{X}c_{1}(L)\cap[C]=\int_{X}c_{1}(M)\cap[C].

If
L
is numerically equivalent to
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
then we say that
L
is
numerically trivial
. The group of line bundles modulo numerical equivalence is denoted by

N^{1}(X)
. This is a torsion free quotient of the Néron–Severi group of
X
. So by theorem of the base,
N^{1}(X)
is a free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module of finite rank.

Below we recall some results from
[
La9
]
.

Theorem 1.17
.

For any Weil divisors
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
on
X
there exists a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-multilinear symmetric form
N^{1}(X)^{\times(n-2)}\to{\mathbb{Q}}
,
(L_{1},...,L_{n-2})\to D_{1}.D_{2}.L_{1}...L_{n-2}
such that:

1.

If both
D_{1}
anf
D_{2}
are Cartier then

D_{1}.D_{2}.L_{1}...L_{n-2}=\int_{X}c_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D_{1}))\cap c_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D_{2}))\cap c_{1}(L_{1})\cap...\cap c_{1}(L_{n-1})\cap[X].

2.

If
D_{2}
is a Cartier divisor then

D_{1}.D_{2}.L_{1}...L_{n-2}=\int_{X}c_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D_{2}))\cap c_{1}(L_{1})\cap...\cap c_{1}(L_{n-2})\cap[D_{1}]\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

3.

If
L_{1},...,L_{n-2}
are very ample
then for a general complete intersection surface
S\in|L_{1}|\cap...\cap|L_{n-2}|
we have

D_{1}.D_{2}.L_{1}...L_{n-2}=D_{1}|_{S}.D_{2}|_{S},

where on the right hand side we have Mumford’s intersection of Weil divisors on a normal surface.

Theorem 1.18
.

Assume that
k
has positive characteristic. For any normal projective variety
X/k
and for any
coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
{\mathcal{E}}
on
X
there exists a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-multilinear symmetric
form
\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}}):N^{1}(X)^{\times(n-2)}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that:

1.

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a vector bundle on
X
then

\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}=\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})\cap c_{1}(L_{1})\cap...\cap c_{1}(L_{n-2})\cap[X].

2.

If
k\subset K
is an algebraically closed field extension then

\int_{X_{K}}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{K})(L_{1})_{K}...(L_{n-2})_{K}=\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}.

3.

If
n>2
and
L_{1}
is very ample then for a very general hypersurface
H\in|L_{1}|
we have

\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}=\int_{H}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}}|_{H})L_{2}|_{H}...L_{n-2}|_{H}.

4.

If
X
is a surface then

\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\chi(X,F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{E}})}{p^{2m}}.

5.

We have

\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}(F_{X}^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}=p^{2}\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}.

Once we have the above theorems we can define some other Chern numbers as follows.

Definition 1.19
.

For any reflexive coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
{\mathcal{E}}
of rank
r
and any line bundles
L_{1},...,L_{n-2}
on
X

we define the following Chern numbers:

\int_{X}c_{1}^{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}:=c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})^{2}.L_{1}...L_{n-2},

\int_{X}c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}c_{1}^{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}-\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2},

\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}=2r\int_{X}c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}-(r-1)\int_{X}c_{1}^{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}.

By linearity we can also extend obtained forms to
{\mathbb{Q}}
-line bundles. In this way we get symmetric
{\mathbb{Q}}
-multilinear forms
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}^{n-2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, where
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}=N^{1}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
.

1.4
Numerical groups of divisors

Let
X
be an irreducible normal scheme defined over an algebraically closed field
k
.
We say that a Weil divisor
D
is
algebraically equivalent to zero
if there exists a smooth variety
T
, a Weil divisor
G
on
X\times_{k}T
and
k
-points
t_{1},t_{2}\in T

such that
D=G_{t_{1}}-G_{t_{2}}
in
A^{1}(X)
(see
[
Fu
, 10.3]
). Then we write
D\sim_{alg}0
.
The group of algebraic equivalence classes of Weil divisors on
X
is denoted by
B^{1}(X)
.
By
\bar{B}^{1}(X)
we denote the quotient of
B^{1}(X)
by torsion.

Let us recall that if
X
is proper then
N^{1}(X)\simeq{\mathop{\rm Pic\,}}X/{\mathop{\rm Pic\,}}^{\tau}X
.
If
X
is also smooth then
N^{1}(X)\simeq\bar{B}^{1}(X)
(see
[
Kl
, Theorem 9.6.3]
).
We will need the following variant of theorem of the base. It is a special case of
[
Ka
, Théorème 3]
but we provide a different simple proof in the case used in the paper.

Lemma 1.20
.

Let
X
be an irreducible, normal, proper scheme defined over an algebraically closed field
k
. Then
\bar{B}^{1}(X)
is a free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module of finite rank.

Proof.

By definition
\bar{B}^{1}(X)
is torsion free. So it is sufficient to prove that
\bar{B}^{1}(X)
is finitely generated as a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module. By
[
dJ
]
there exists an alteration
\tilde{Y}\to X
from a smooth projective variety
\tilde{Y}
. Taking Stein’s factorization we get a proper birational map

g:\tilde{Y}\to Y
to normal variety and a finite surjective morphism
\pi:Y\to X
.

Let
E
be the exceptional locus of
g
. Then using the localization sequence (see
[
Fu
, Example 10.3.4]
) we get a surjective map

B^{1}(\tilde{Y})\twoheadrightarrow B^{1}(\tilde{Y}\backslash E)\simeq B^{1}(Y\backslash g(E))\simeq B^{1}(Y),

showing that
\bar{B}^{1}(Y)
is finitely generated.

There exists a big open subset
U\subset X
such that
\pi:V:=\pi^{-1}(U)\to U
is flat.
Then using flat pullback and the localization sequence (see
[
Fu
, Proposition 10.3 and Example 10.3.4]
) we have a well defined map

B^{1}(X)\simeq B^{1}(U)\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\pi^{*}}B^{1}(V)\simeq B^{1}(Y)

induced by pullback of Weil divisors. Since
\pi_{*}\pi^{*}
is multiplication by the degree of
\pi
on
A^{1}(U)
(and hence also on
B^{1}(U)
), the induced map
\bar{B}^{1}(X)\to\bar{B}^{1}(Y)
is injective.
This implies that
\bar{B}^{1}(X)
is also finitely generated.
∎

From now one in this subsection
X
is a normal projective variety of dimension
n
defined over an algebraically closed field
k
.

Lemma 1.21
.

If a Weil divisor
D_{1}
is algebraically equivalent to zero then for every Weil divisor
D_{2}

and all line bundles
L_{1},...,L_{n-2}
we have

D_{1}.D_{2}.L_{1}...L_{n-2}=0.

Proof.

Let us first assume that
X
is a surface and let
f:\tilde{X}\to X
be a resolution of singularities. By assumption there exists a smooth variety
T
, a Weil divisor
G
on
X\times_{k}T
and
k
-points
t_{1},t_{2}\in T
such that
D=G_{t_{1}}-G_{t_{2}}
in
A^{1}(X)
.
Let us consider the map
g:=f\times\mathop{\rm Id}:\tilde{X}\times_{k}T\to X\times_{k}T
. One can use Mumford’s construction of pullback to define
g^{*}G
that restricts to
f^{*}(G_{t})
on
\tilde{X}\times\{t\}

for every
t\in T(k)
.
Then we have
f^{*}D=(g^{*}G)_{t_{1}}-(g^{*}G)_{t_{2}}
in
A^{1}(\tilde{X})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
. This implies that some multiple of
f^{*}D_{1}
is algebraically equivalent to zero and hence
f^{*}D_{1}.f^{*}D_{2}=D_{1}.D_{2}=0
.

In general, by linearity of the intersection product it is sufficient to prove that
D_{1}.D_{2}.L_{1}...L_{n-2}=0
assuming that
L_{1},...,L_{n-2}
are very ample.
Let
S\in|L_{1}|\cap...\cap|L_{n-2}|
be a general complete intersection surface.
Since cycles algebraically equivalent to zero are preserved by Gysin homomorphisms (see
[
Fu
, Proposition 10.3]
) the restriction
D_{1}|_{S}
is algebraically equivalent to zero.
So by Theorem
1.17
we have

D_{1}.D_{2}.L_{1}...L_{n-2}=D_{1}|_{S}.D_{2}|_{S}=0.

∎

The above lemma shows that the intersection pairing
(D_{1},D_{2},L_{1},...,L_{n-2})\to D_{1}.D_{2}.L_{1}...L_{n-2}
induces a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-multilinear map

B^{1}(X)\times B^{1}(X)\times N^{1}(X)^{\times(n-2)}\to{\mathbb{Q}}.

Let us fix a collection
L=(L_{2},...,L_{n-1})
of nef line bundles on
X
.
Assume that there exists a nef line bundle
L_{1}
such that
L_{1}L_{2}....L_{n-1}
is numerically non-trivial, i.e., there exists some Weil divisor
D
such that
D.L_{1}...L_{n-1}\neq 0
. Let us consider a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-valued intersection pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L}:B^{1}(X)\times B^{1}(X)\to{\mathbb{Q}}
defined by

\langle D_{1},D_{2}\rangle_{L}:=D_{1}.D_{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1}.

Let us write
N_{L}(X)
for the quotient of
B^{1}(X)
modulo the radical of this intersection pairing.
Then we have an induced non-degenerate intersection pairing

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L}:N_{L}(X)\times N_{L}(X)\to{\mathbb{Q}}.

Lemma 1.22
.

Let us assume that
L_{1}^{2}L_{2}...L_{n-1}>0
. If
D_{1}.L_{1}L_{2}...L_{n-1}=0
then
D_{1}^{2}L_{2}...L_{n-1}\leq 0

with equality if and only if the class
[D_{1}]\in N_{L}(X)
is zero.

Proof.

Let us fix some ample line bundle
A
. Then the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-line bundles
L_{i}+\epsilon A
are ample for
\epsilon\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{>0}
. So by
[
La9
, Lemma 2.6]
we have inequalities

D_{1}^{2}(L_{2}+\epsilon A)...(L_{n-1}+\epsilon A)\cdot(L_{1}+\epsilon A)^{2}(L_{2}+\epsilon A)...(L_{n-1}+\epsilon A)\leq\left(D_{1}.(L_{1}+\epsilon A)...(L_{n-1}+\epsilon A)\right)^{2}.

Taking the limit when
\epsilon\to 0
, we get
D_{1}^{2}L_{2}...L_{n-1}\leq 0
. Now let us assume that
D_{1}^{2}L_{2}...L_{n-1}=0
but

D_{1}.D_{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1}\neq 0
for some Weil divisor
D_{2}
. Replacing
D_{2}
by
(L_{1}^{2}L_{2}...L_{n-1})D_{2}-(D_{2}.L_{1}...L_{n-1})L_{1}

we can assume that
D_{2}.L_{1}...L_{n-1}=0
.
Therefore we have

0\geq(tD_{1}+D_{2})^{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1}=2tD_{1}.D_{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1}+D_{2}^{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1},

which gives a contradiction with some
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. ∎

The following lemma generalizes
[
La9
, Lemma 2.6]
.

Lemma 1.23
.

N_{L}(X)
is a free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module of finite rank. If
{\mathop{\rm rk\,}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}N_{L}(X)=s
then the intersection pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L}
has signature
(1,s-1)
.

Proof.

By definition
N_{L}(X)
is torsion free and it is a quotient of
B^{1}(X)
. So by Lemma
1.20

N_{L}(X)
is also a free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module of finite rank.
If
L_{1}^{2}L_{2}...L_{n-1}>0
then the second assertion follows from Lemma
1.22
.
In general, there exists some Weil divisor
D
such that
D.L_{1}...L_{n-1}\neq 0
. Without loss of generality we can assume that

D.L_{1}...L_{n-1}>0
. Then for every ample Cartier divisor
H
we have
H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mH-D))\neq 0

for
m\gg 0
. So
mHL_{1}...L_{n-1}\geq D.L_{1}...L_{n-1}>0
. Then
M=(L_{1}+H)
is ample and
M^{2}L_{2}....L_{n-1}>0
. Since the definition of
N_{L}(X)
does not depend on
L_{1}
, we get the required assertion from the previous case.
∎

As in
[
La8
, Lemma 2.1]
the above lemma implies the following result (in fact, the first part follows from the proof of Lemma
1.23
).

Corollary 1.24
.

If
H
is an ample line bundle then
HL_{1}L_{2}...L_{n-1}>0
. Moreover, if

D.L_{1}L_{2}...L_{n-1}=0
for some Weil divisor
D
then
then
D^{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1}\leq 0
.

2
Several auxiliary results on Chern classes

In this section we prove several results on Chern classes of reflexive sheaves that will be needed throughout the paper.
We assume that
X
is a normal projective variety of dimension
n
defined over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p>0
. We also fix a collection

(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
of nef line bundles on
X
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

0\to{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\to 0

be a left exact sequence of reflexive sheaves on
X
, which is also right exact
on some big open subset of
X
. Then we have

\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}\leq\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{1})L_{1}...L_{n-2}+\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{2})L_{1}...L_{n-2}.

Moreover, if the above sequence is exact on
X
and locally split in codimension
2
then we have equality

\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}=\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{1})L_{1}...L_{n-2}+\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{2})L_{1}...L_{n-2}.

Proof.

Since numerical equivalence classes of nef line bundles are limits of classes of ample
{\mathbb{Q}}
-line bundles, we can by continuity assume that all
L_{i}
are ample
{\mathbb{Q}}
-line bundles. Passing to their multiples we can also assume that all
L_{i}
are very ample line bundles. By Theorem
1.18
, (2) and (3), we can assume that the base field
k
is uncountable and then
by restricting to a very general complete intersection surface
S\in|L_{1}|\cap...\cap|L_{n-2}|
we can assume that
X
is a surface.

Let
U
be a big open subset on which all
{\mathcal{E}}
,
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
are locally free and let
j:U\hookrightarrow X

denote the open embedding. Since
X
is normal, we can also assume that
U
is contained in the regular locus
X_{reg}
of
X
. Since
F_{U}^{*}
is exact, the sequences

0\to(F_{U}^{m})^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to(F_{U}^{m})^{*}{\mathcal{E}}\to(F_{U}^{m})^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\to 0

are exact. This implies that sequences

0\to F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{E}}\to F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}

are exact and the cokernel of the last map is supported on the closed subset
X\backslash U
of codimension
\geq 2
. So we get inequalities

\chi(X,F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{E}}))\leq\chi(X,F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{E}}_{1})+\chi(X,F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}).

Dividing by
p^{2m}
, passing to the limit and using Theorem
1.18
, (3), we get the required inequality.
Equality follows from the fact that
the above mentioned left exact sequence becomes right exact if the sequence

0\to{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\to 0

is locally split.
∎

Example 2.2
.

If the short exact sequence in the above lemma is not locally split, then it is well known that the inequality can be strict. For example, if
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
is the cone over a smooth quadric curve in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
and
D
is its generator, then we have a short exact sequence

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-D)\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\to 0

with

\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{X})=0<\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-D))+\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D))=D^{2}=\frac{1}{2}.

This example shows also that Chern classes of reflexive sheaves on a normal projective surface
are not deformation invariant, i.e., they can change in flat families.

Remark 2.3
.

Equality in Lemma
2.1
is one of the results that is not known for general normal surfaces defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
(see
[
La9
]
).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a reflexive coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module and let
{\mathcal{E}}=N^{0}\supset N^{1}\supset...\supset N^{s}=0
be a filtration such that the associated graded
{\mathop{Gr}}_{N}({\mathcal{E}})=\bigoplus_{i}N^{i}/N^{i+1}
is torsion free. Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be the reflexive hull of
{\mathop{Gr}}_{N}({\mathcal{E}})
. Then the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})
,

2.

\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}\leq\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{F}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}
,

3.

\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}\geq\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{F}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}
.

Proof.

The first condition is clear as
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=c_{1}({\mathop{Gr}}_{N}({\mathcal{E}}))=c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})
.
We prove the second condition by induction on the length
s
of the filtration.
If
s>1
then
N^{1}
is reflexive as
N^{0}/N^{1}
is torsion free. So by Lemma
2.1

we have

\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}\leq\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}(N^{1})L_{1}...L_{n-2}+\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}((N^{0}/N^{1})^{**})L_{1}...L_{n-2}.

Applying the induction assumption to the filtration
N^{1}\supset N^{2}\supset...\supset N^{s}=0
of
N^{1}
,
we get the required inequality. The last condition follows from (1) and (2).
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a rank
r
reflexive coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module and let
{\mathcal{E}}=N^{0}\supset N^{1}\supset...\supset N^{s}=0
be a filtration such that all
N^{i}/N^{i+1}
are torsion free.
Let us assume that
L
is numerically nontrivial and let us
set
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}:=(N^{i}/N^{i+1})^{**}
,
r_{i}:={\mathop{\rm rk\,}}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
and
\mu_{i}:=\mu_{L}({\mathcal{F}}_{i})
.

1.

If
d:=L_{1}^{2}L_{2}....L_{n-1}>0
then

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}}{r}\geq\sum_{i}\frac{\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{F}}_{i})L_{2}...L_{n-1}}{r_{i}}-\frac{1}{rd}\sum_{i<j}r_{i}r_{j}\left(\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}\right)^{2}.

2.

If
\mu_{i}=\mu_{L}({\mathcal{E}})
for all
i
then

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}}{r}\geq\sum_{i}\frac{\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{F}}_{i})L_{2}...L_{n-1}}{r_{i}}.

Proof.

Passing to an algebraically closed and uncountable field extension of
k
we can assume that
k
is uncountable. If we set
{\mathcal{F}}:=\bigoplus{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
then Lemma
2.1
gives

\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{F}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}=\sum_{i}\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{i})L_{1}...L_{n-2}.

After rewriting this gives

\frac{\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{F}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}}{r}=\sum_{i}\frac{\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{F}}_{i})L_{2}...L_{n-1}}{r_{i}}-\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i<j}r_{i}r_{j}\left(\frac{c_{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}}{r_{i}}-\frac{c_{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{j}}{r_{j}}\right)^{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1}.

But by the Hodge index theorem (see Lemma
1.23
) we have

\left(\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}\right)^{2}=\left(\left(\frac{c_{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}}{r_{i}}-\frac{c_{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{j}}{r_{j}}\right).L_{1}...L_{n-1}\right)^{2}\geq d\cdot\left(\frac{c_{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}}{r_{i}}-\frac{c_{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{j}}{r_{j}}\right)^{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1}.

So (1) follows from Lemma
2.4
, (3). Under assumption (2), Corollary
1.24

implies that

\left(\frac{c_{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}}{r_{i}}-\frac{c_{1}{\mathcal{F}}_{j}}{r_{j}}\right)^{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1}\leq 0,

so again the inequality follows from Lemma
2.4
, (3).
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
H\in|L_{1}|
be a normal variety and let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a rank
\tau
torsion free
{\mathcal{O}}_{D}
-module and let
i:H\hookrightarrow X
be the closed embedding. Let

0\to{\mathcal{G}}\to{\mathcal{E}}\to i_{*}{\mathcal{T}}\to 0

be a short exact sequence of coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules, where
{\mathcal{E}}
is reflexive.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{G}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}-\tau(r-\tau)L_{1}^{2}L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}

\displaystyle+2(rc_{1}({\mathcal{T}})-\tau c_{1}(i^{*}{\mathcal{E}})).i^{*}L_{2}\dots i^{*}L_{n-1}.

Proof.

Note that
{\mathcal{G}}
is a coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module.
Since both sides of our inequality depend continuously on
L_{2},...,L_{n-1}
when considered as functions on
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
, and the inequality does not change when we pass to multiples, we can assume that
L_{2},...,L_{n-1}
are very ample. By Theorems
1.17
and
1.18
we can assume that the base field
k
is uncountable and then we can restrict to a very general complete intersection surface in
|L_{2}|\cap...\cap|L_{n-1}|
to reduce the assertion to the surface case.
An exact sequence

(F_{X}^{m})^{*}{\mathcal{G}}\to(F_{X}^{m})^{*}{\mathcal{E}}\to(F_{X}^{m})^{*}(i_{*}{\mathcal{T}})\to 0

leads to

0\to F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{G}}\to F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{T}}_{m}\to 0,

where
{\mathcal{T}}_{m}
is set-theoretically supported on
H
.
Moreover, we have a canonical map
(F_{X}^{m})^{*}(i_{*}{\mathcal{T}})\to{\mathcal{T}}_{m}
, which is an isomorphism
on the set where
F_{X}
is flat, i.e., on
X_{reg}
.
But
X
is a surface and
H
is a smooth curve, which is also a Cartier divisor. So
H
is contained in

and hence
{\mathcal{T}}_{m}\simeq(F_{X}^{m})^{*}(i_{*}{\mathcal{T}})
.
This gives

\displaystyle\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\chi(X,F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{E}})}{p^{2m}}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\chi(X,F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{G}})}{p^{2m}}+\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\chi(X,(F_{X}^{m})^{*}(i_{*}{\mathcal{T}}))}{p^{2m}}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{G}})+\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\chi(X,(F_{X}^{m})^{*}(i_{*}{\mathcal{T}}))}{p^{2m}}.

To compute the last limit, let us consider a resolution of singularities
f:\tilde{X}\to X
, which is an isomorphism on
X_{reg}
. So we have a closed embedding
\tilde{i}:H\hookrightarrow\tilde{X}
such that

. Then we have a short exact sequence

0\to\tilde{\mathcal{G}}\to f^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}\to\tilde{i}_{*}{\mathcal{T}}\to 0,

where
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
is a rank
r
vector bundle.
Then by the same arguments as above we have

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\chi(X,(F_{X}^{m})^{*}(i_{*}{\mathcal{T}}))}{p^{2m}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\chi(\tilde{X},(F_{\tilde{X}}^{m})^{*}(\tilde{i}_{*}{\mathcal{T}}))}{p^{2m}}=\int_{\tilde{X}}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}(f^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}})-\int_{\tilde{X}}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}(\tilde{\mathcal{G}})

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{X}}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}(\tilde{i}_{*}{\mathcal{T}}).

To compute
\int_{\tilde{X}}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}(\tilde{i}_{*}{\mathcal{T}})
one can use the Riemann–Roch theorem on
\tilde{X}
and on
H
to get

\deg_{H}{\mathcal{T}}+\tau\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{H})=\chi(H,{\mathcal{T}})=\chi(X,\tilde{i}_{*}{\mathcal{T}})=\int_{\tilde{X}}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}(\tilde{i}_{*}\tilde{\mathcal{T}})-\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(\tilde{i}_{*}\tilde{\mathcal{T}})K_{\tilde{X}}

Since
c_{1}(\tilde{i}_{*}{\mathcal{T}})=\tau H
and
\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{H})=-\frac{1}{2}H(K_{\tilde{X}}+H)
(e.g, because

), we get

\int_{\tilde{X}}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}(\tilde{i}_{*}{\mathcal{T}})=\deg_{H}{\mathcal{T}}-\frac{\tau}{2}H^{2}.

Summing up, we get

\displaystyle\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{G}})+\deg_{H}{\mathcal{T}}-\frac{\tau}{2}L_{1}^{2}.

After rewriting, using
2r\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=c_{1}^{2}({\mathcal{E}})-{\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})}
,

∎

3
Boundedness on normal varieties in positive characteristic

In this section we prove strong restriction theorems for semistable sheaves and we show some boundedness results. In particular we prove Theorems
0.1
and
0.2
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety of dimension
n
defined over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p>0
and let
L=(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
be a collection of nef line bundles on
X
.

3.1
Slope semistability and its behaviour under pullbacks

For a coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
{\mathcal{E}}
of positive rank
r
we define its
slope
with respect to the collection
L
by

\mu_{L}({\mathcal{E}}):=\frac{c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}).L_{1}...L_{n-1}}{r}.

Note that by definition
\mu_{L}({\mathcal{E}}^{**})=\mu_{L}({\mathcal{E}})
.

We say that a coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
{\mathcal{E}}
is
slope
L
-semistable
if either it is torsion
or for every coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-submodule
{\mathcal{F}}\subset{\mathcal{E}}

of positive rank we have
\mu_{L}({\mathcal{F}})\leq\mu_{L}({\mathcal{E}})
. We say that
{\mathcal{E}}
is
strongly slope
L
-semistable
if for all
m\geq 0
the Frobenius pullbacks
(F_{X}^{m})^{*}{\mathcal{E}}
are slope
L
-semistable.
In this section we usually consider slope semistability with respect to our fixed collection
L
(unless explicitly stated). So for simplicity of notation we usually ignore dependence of slopes on
L
.

Every coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
{\mathcal{E}}
of positive rank admits the Harder–Narasimhan filtration

0\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\subset...\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{s}={\mathcal{E}}
. It is a unique filtration by coherent

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-submodules such that
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
is torsion, quotients
{\mathcal{E}}^{i}:={\mathcal{E}}_{i}/{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1}
are torsion free
and slope
L
-semistable for
i=1,...,s
and we have

\mu_{1}=\mu({\mathcal{E}}^{1})>...>\mu_{s}=\mu({\mathcal{E}}^{s})
. In the following we write
\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{E}})
for
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{\min}({\mathcal{E}})
for
\mu_{s}
.

The proof of
[
La2
, Theorem 2.7]
works on normal varieties and it gives the following result:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module of positive rank. Then there exists
m_{0}
such that for all

all quotients in the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of

-semistable.

For a coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
{\mathcal{E}}
we set

L_{\max}({\mathcal{E}})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\mu_{\max}(F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{E}})}{p^{m}}

and

L_{\min}({\mathcal{E}})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\mu_{\min}(F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{E}})}{p^{m}}.

By the above theorem these numbers are well defined rational numbers.

Now choose a nef Cartier divisor
A
on
X
such that

T_{X}(A)
is globally generated. Then
[
La2
, Corollary 2.5]
still holds and it implies the following

Lemma 3.2
.

For any coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
{\mathcal{E}}
of rank

we have

\max(L_{\max}({\mathcal{E}})-\mu({\mathcal{E}}),\mu({\mathcal{E}})-L_{\min}({\mathcal{E}}))\leq AL_{1}...L_{n-1}.

As in

]
we also set

\beta_{r}:=\left(\frac{r(r-1)}{p-1}AL_{1}\dots L_{n-1}\right)^{2}.

Restriction theorem and Bogomolov’s inequality

As in the smooth case, by Lemma

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}

.
Using Lemma

.

.

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is strongly slope

.

If

is slope

If

\left(\frac{c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})}{r^{\prime}}-\frac{c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})}{r}\right)
lies in
K^{+}_{L}.

The only difference in proofs with respect to
[
La2
]
is that one should consider
F_{X}^{[m]}{\mathcal{E}}
instead of
(F_{X}^{m})^{*}{\mathcal{E}}
. Also in the surface case one needs to use
[
La9
, Corollary 6.6]
instead of using the arguments of
[
La2
]
that do not work for normal surfaces.

3.3
Strong restriction theorems

In this subsection we assume that
d=L_{1}^{2}L_{2}...L_{n-1}>0
. As in
[
La2
, Theorem 5.1]
, Lemma
2.5
and Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
imply the following
Bogomolov’s inequality for all reflexive sheaves.

Theorem 3.7
.

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module then

L_{1}^{2}L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}\cdot\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}+r^{2}(L_{\max}({\mathcal{E}})-\mu({\mathcal{E}}))(\mu({\mathcal{E}})-L_{\min}({\mathcal{E}}))\geq 0

and

L_{1}^{2}L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}\cdot\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}+r^{2}(\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{E}})-\mu({\mathcal{E}}))(\mu({\mathcal{E}})-\mu_{\min}({\mathcal{E}}))+\beta_{r}\geq 0.

This immediately implies the following corollary.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let us fix some positive integer
r
and some non-negative rational number
\alpha
.
There exists some constant
C=C(X,L,r,\alpha)
depending only on
X
,
L
,
r
and
\alpha
such that for every coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
{\mathcal{E}}
of rank
r
with

\mu_{\max,L}({\mathcal{E}})-\mu_{L}({\mathcal{E}})\leq\alpha
we have

\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq C.

As in
[
La2
]
we can use Theorem
3.7
to prove strong restriction theorems for reflexive sheaves on normal varieties. We take this opportunity to
show proof of a stronger result that combines
[
La2
, Theorem 5.2]
with
[
La8
, Theorem 3.7]
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module of rank
r\geq 2
. Assume that
{\mathcal{E}}
is slope
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
-stable. Let
H\in|L_{1}^{\otimes m}|
be an irreducible normal divisor.

1.

If

m>\left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{r}\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}+\frac{1}{dr(r-1)}+\frac{(r-1)\beta_{r}}{dr}\right\rfloor

then
{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}
is slope
(L_{2}|_{H},...,L_{n-1}|_{H})
-stable.

2.

If

m\leq\left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{r}\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}+\frac{1}{dr(r-1)}+\frac{(r-1)\beta_{r}}{dr}\right\rfloor

then

\displaystyle\mu_{\max,L_{2}|_{H},...,L_{n-1}|_{H}}

\displaystyle({\mathcal{E}}|_{H})-\mu_{L_{2}|_{H},...,L_{n-1}|_{H}}({\mathcal{E}}|_{H})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2r}\left(d\left(\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}-\frac{r}{r-1}m\right)+\frac{1}{(r-1)^{2}}+{\beta_{r}}\right).

Proof.

Let
i:H\hookrightarrow X
denote the closed embedding.
Since
{\mathcal{E}}
is reflexive,
i^{*}{\mathcal{E}}
is a torsion free
{\mathcal{O}}_{H}
-module.
Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a saturated subsheaf of
i^{*}{\mathcal{E}}

of rank
\rho
. Set
{\mathcal{T}}:=(i^{*}{\mathcal{E}})/{\mathcal{S}}
and let
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
be the kernel of the composition

{\mathcal{E}}\to i_{*}(i^{*}{\mathcal{E}})\to i_{*}{\mathcal{T}}
. Since
{\mathcal{T}}
is a torsion free
{\mathcal{O}}_{H}
-module,
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
is a coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module and we have two short exact sequences:

0\to{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{E}}\to i_{*}{\mathcal{T}}\to 0

and

0\to{\mathcal{E}}(-H)\to{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\to i_{*}{\mathcal{S}}\to 0.

Lemma
2.6
implies that

\displaystyle\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}-\rho(r-\rho)H^{2}L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}

\displaystyle+2(rc_{1}({\mathcal{T}})-(r-\rho)c_{1}(i^{*}{\mathcal{E}})).L_{2}|_{H}\dots L_{n-1}|_{H}.

Since
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}
is slope
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
-stable
we have

\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})-\mu({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})=\frac{r-\rho}{r}HL_{1}...L_{n-1}+\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})-\mu({\mathcal{E}})\leq\frac{r-\rho}{r}md-\frac{1}{r(r-1)}.

Similarly, since
{\mathcal{E}}(-H)\subset{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
we have

\mu({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})-\mu_{\min}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})=\frac{\rho}{r}HL_{1}...L_{n-1}+\mu({\mathcal{E}}(-H))-\mu_{\min}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})\leq\frac{\rho}{r}md-\frac{1}{r(r-1)}.

So Theorem
3.7
gives

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle d\cdot\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}+r^{2}(\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})-\mu({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}))(\mu({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})-\mu_{\min}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}))+\beta_{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d\cdot\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}-\rho(r-\rho)m^{2}d^{2}-2r\rho(\mu({\mathcal{S}})-\mu(i^{*}{\mathcal{E}}))

\displaystyle+\left(\rho md-\frac{1}{r-1}\right)\left((r-\rho)md-\frac{1}{r-1}\right)+\beta_{r}.

If
\mu({\mathcal{S}})\geq\mu(i^{*}{\mathcal{E}})
then we get

\displaystyle\frac{2(r-1)}{d}(\mu({\mathcal{S}})-\mu(i^{*}{\mathcal{E}}))+m\leq\frac{r-1}{r}\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}+\frac{1}{dr(r-1)}+\frac{(r-1)\beta_{r}}{dr},

which implies the required assertions. ∎

Remark 3.10
.

The above proof works also for an arbitrary irreducible normal divisor
D\subset X
, which is nef and Cartier. In this way one gets restriction theorems taking into account
the difference of directions of lines given by classes of
D
and
L_{1}
in
N_{L}(X)
. We leave the details of proof to the interested reader.

As in
[
La2
, Corollary 5.4]
the above theorem together with Lemma
2.5
implies the following result:

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module of rank
r\geq 2
. Assume that
{\mathcal{E}}
is slope
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
-semistable and let
H\in|L_{1}^{\otimes m}|
be an irreducible normal divisor.
Let
0={\mathcal{E}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\subset...\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{s}={\mathcal{E}}
be a
Jordan–Hölder filtration of
{\mathcal{E}}
and let us assume that all
({\mathcal{E}}_{i}/{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1})|_{H}
are torsion free.

1.

If

m>\left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{r}\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}+\frac{1}{dr(r-1)}+\frac{(r-1)\beta_{r}}{dr}\right\rfloor

then
{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}
is slope
(L_{2}|_{H},...,L_{n-1}|_{H})
-semistable.

2.

If

m\leq\left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{r}\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}+\frac{1}{dr(r-1)}+\frac{(r-1)\beta_{r}}{dr}\right\rfloor

then

\displaystyle\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{E}}|_{H})\leq\mu({\mathcal{E}})+\frac{1}{2r}\left(d\left(\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}-\frac{r}{r-1}m\right)+\frac{1}{(r-1)^{2}}+{\beta_{r}}\right).

Proof.

Note that all
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
are reflexive as they are saturated in

{\mathcal{E}}
. Let us set
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}:=({\mathcal{E}}_{i}/{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1})^{**}
and
r_{i}={\mathop{\rm rk\,}}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
.
By Lemma
2.5
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}}{r}\geq\sum_{i}\frac{\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{F}}_{i})L_{2}...L_{n-1}}{r_{i}}.

In the first case either
r_{i}=1
or
r_{i}\geq 2
and

m>\left\lfloor\frac{r_{i}-1}{r_{i}}\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{F}}_{i})L_{2}\dots L_{n-1}+\frac{1}{dr_{i}(r_{i}-1)}+\frac{(r_{i}-1)\beta_{r_{i}}}{dr_{i}}\right\rfloor.

Note that in the above inequality we need to worry about the term

\frac{1}{dr_{i}(r_{i}-1)}
, which can be larger than
\frac{1}{dr(r-1)}
. However, the difference is compensated by the other terms unless both of them are 0 in which case
\lfloor\frac{1}{dr_{i}(r_{i}-1)}\rfloor=0
.
Applying Theorem
3.9
we see that
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}|_{H}
is stable
with the same slope as
{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}
.
Since
({\mathcal{E}}_{i}/{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1})|_{H}
are torsion free, the sequences

0\to{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1}|_{H}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{i}|_{H}\to{\mathcal{F}}_{i}|_{H}

are exact. Now a simple induction show that all
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}|_{H}
are slope
(L_{2}|_{H},...,L_{n-1}|_{H})
-semistable.

The second case is completely analogous. We just need to use the fact that

\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{E}}|_{H})\leq\max_{i}\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{F}}_{i}|_{H}).

∎

Remark 3.12
.

The above results give also restriction theorems for torsion free sheaves. More precisely,
let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a coherent torsion free
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module, which is slope
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
-(semi)stable. Then its reflexive hull
{\mathcal{E}}^{**}
is also slope
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
-(semi)stable, so we can apply Theorem
3.9

and Corollary
3.11
to
{\mathcal{E}}^{**}
. If
{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}
is torsion free then it is slope
(L_{2}|_{H},...,L_{n-1}|_{H})
-(semi)stable if (and only if)
{\mathcal{E}}^{**}|_{H}
is slope
(L_{2}|_{H},...,L_{n-1}|_{H})
-(semi)stable.

3.4
Boundedness

In this subsection we assume that
n\geq 2
and all line bundles
L_{1},...,L_{n-1}
are ample.
For
\alpha\in B^{1}(X)
we write
\alpha\sim 0
if
\alpha.L_{1}...L_{n-1}=0
and

\alpha^{2}.L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}=0

for
i=1,...,n-1
. Note that the subset
S:=\{\alpha\in B^{1}(X):\alpha\sim 0\}
forms a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-submodule of
B^{1}(X)
.
Indeed, Lemma
1.22
implies that
\alpha\in S
if and only if the class of
\alpha
in
N_{(L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1})}(X)
vanishes for

i=1,...,n-1
. So
S
is the intersection of kernels of quotient maps

B^{1}(X)\to N_{(L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1})}(X).

In the following we set
C^{1}(X;L_{1},...,L_{n-1}):=B^{1}(X)/S
.

Lemma 3.13
.

C^{1}(X;L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
is a free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module of finite rank. Moreover, for
a general divisor
H\in|L_{1}|
we have a well-defined Gysin homomorphism

C^{1}(X;L_{1},...,L_{n-1})\to C^{1}(H;L_{2}|_{H},...,L_{n-1}|_{H}).

Proof.

The first assertion follows from the definition and Lemma
1.20
.
By
[
Fu
, Proposition 10.3]
we have a Gysin homomorphism
B^{1}(X)\to B^{1}(H)
. Let us denote the image of

\alpha\in B^{1}(X)
in
B^{1}(H)
by
\alpha|_{H}
. It is sufficient to show that if
\alpha\sim 0
in
B^{1}(X)
then
\alpha|_{H}\sim 0
in
B^{1}(H)
. Since
S\subset B^{1}(X)
is a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}
-submodule, it is sufficient to check this condition for finitely finitely many generators of
S
. But for a general divisor
H\in|L_{1}|

we have

\alpha|_{H}^{2}.L_{2}|_{H}...\widehat{L_{i}|_{H}}...L_{n-1}|_{H}=\alpha^{2}.L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}=0

for
i=1,...,n-1
. We also have
\alpha|_{H}.L_{1}|_{H}...L_{n-1}|_{H}=\alpha.L_{1}...L_{n-1}=0
, which proves that
the class of
\alpha|_{H}
in
C^{1}(H;L_{2}|_{H},...,L_{n-1}|_{H})
vanishes.
∎

Corollary
3.11
can be used to prove the following boundedness result, which
does not immediately follow from Kleiman’s criterion. Part of the proof follows the idea of proof of
[
La3
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let us fix some classes
c_{1}\in C^{1}(X;L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
, a positive integer
r
and some real numbers
c_{2}
and
\mu_{\max}
.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the set of reflexive coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules
{\mathcal{E}}
such that

1.

{\mathcal{E}}
has rank
r
,

2.

the class of
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})
in
C^{1}(X;L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
is equal to
c_{1}
,

3.

\int_{X}c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}\leq c_{2}
for
i=1,...,n-1
,

4.

\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{E}})\leq\mu_{\max}
.

Then the set
{\mathcal{A}}
is bounded.

Proof.

For
n=2
the assertion is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
La2
, Theorem 4.4]
), so we can assume that
n\geq 3
. Without loss of generality we can also assume that all
L_{i}
are very ample.
Note that if
k\subset K
is an algebraically closed field extension then the set
{\mathcal{A}}
is bounded if and only if the set
{\mathcal{A}}_{K}:=\{{\mathcal{E}}_{K}:{\mathcal{E}}\in{\mathcal{A}}\}
of sheaves on
X_{K}
is bounded. This follows from

[
HL
, Lemma 1.7.6]
and the fact that the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of
{\mathcal{E}}
(with respect to some fixed very ample line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1)
) coincides with the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of
{\mathcal{E}}
(here we use the fact that
H^{i}(X_{K},{\mathcal{E}}(j)_{K})=H^{i}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(j))\otimes_{k}K
).
So by Theorem
1.18
, (2) and an analogue of
[
HL
, Theorem 1.3.7]
for slope semistability, we can assume that the base field
k
is uncountable.

For fixed
{\mathcal{E}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
and for a very general divisor
H\in|L_{1}|
,
the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}
is reflexive as an
{\mathcal{O}}_{H}
-module (by
[
HL
, Corollary 1.1.14]
),

2.

for
i=2,...,n-1
we have

\int_{H}c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}|_{H})^{2}L_{2}|_{H}...\widehat{L_{i}|_{H}}...L_{n-1}|_{H}=\int_{X}c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})^{2}L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}=c_{1}^{2}.L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}

(by Theorem
1.17
),

3.

for
i=2,...,n-1
we have

\int_{H}c_{2}({\mathcal{E}}|_{H})L_{2}|_{H}...\widehat{L_{i}|_{H}}...L_{n-1}|_{H}=\int_{X}c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}\leq c_{2}

(by Theorem
1.18
),

4.

if
0={\mathcal{E}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\subset...\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{s}={\mathcal{E}}
be the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of
of
{\mathcal{E}}
then all
({\mathcal{E}}_{i}/{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1})|_{H}
are torsion free and
restriction of any quotient of a Jordan–Hölder filtration of
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}:=({\mathcal{E}}_{i}/{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1})^{**}
to
H
is torsion free as an
{\mathcal{O}}_{H}
-module (by
[
HL
, Corollary 1.1.14]
).

Let us fix a normal hypersurface
H\in|L_{1}|
such that the Gysin homomorphism

C^{1}(X;L_{1},...,L_{n-1})\to C^{1}(H;L_{2}|_{H},...,L_{n-1}|_{H})
is well defined (see Lemma
3.13
).
Let us consider the set
{\mathcal{A}}_{H}
of all sheaves
{\mathcal{E}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
that satisfy the above conditions (1)–(4).
By Corollary
3.8
there exists
C
such that for every slope
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
-semistable reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
{\mathcal{F}}
of rank
\leq r
we have

\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{F}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq C.

Let us set
r_{i}={\mathop{\rm rk\,}}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
and
\mu_{i}:=\mu_{L}({\mathcal{F}}_{i})
.
By Lemma
2.5
and
[
La2
, Lemma 1.4]
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{F}}_{i})L_{2}...L_{n-1}}{r_{i}}\leq\sum_{j}\frac{\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{F}}_{j})L_{2}...L_{n-1}}{r_{j}}-(s-1)C

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}}{r}+\frac{1}{rd}\sum_{i<j}r_{i}r_{j}\left(\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}\right)^{2}-(s-1)C

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2rc_{2}-(r-1)c_{1}^{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1}}{r}+\frac{r}{d}\left(\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{E}})-\mu({\mathcal{E}})\right)\left(\mu({\mathcal{E}})-\mu_{\min}({\mathcal{E}})\right)-(s-1)C

Since

\mu({\mathcal{E}})-\mu_{\min}({\mathcal{E}})\leq(s-1)\left(\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{E}})-\mu({\mathcal{E}})\right)\leq(s-1)\left(\mu_{\max}-\frac{1}{r}c_{1}.L_{1}...L_{n-1}\right),

Corollary
3.11
and condition (4) imply that for all
i
we have

\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{F}}_{i}|_{H})\leq C_{1}

for some
C_{1}
that depends only on
r
,
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
and
\mu_{\max}
.
Condition (4) implies also that the sequences

0\to{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1}|_{H}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{i}|_{H}\to{\mathcal{F}}_{i}|_{H}

are exact, so

\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{E}}|_{H})\leq\max_{i}\mu_{\max}({\mathcal{F}}_{i}|_{H})\leq C_{1}.

For any
{\mathcal{E}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
the class of
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}|_{H})
in
C^{1}(H;L_{2}|_{H},...,L_{n-1}|_{H})
coincides with
c_{1}|_{H}
.
This follows from the fact that
{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}
is torsion free, so also locally free on a big open subset of
H
.
So already the class of
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}|_{H})
coincides with
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})|_{H}\in A^{1}(H)
.
By the induction assumption this implies that the set of sheaves
\{{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}\}_{{\mathcal{E}}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{H}}
is bounded.
To simplify notation we write
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1)=L_{1}
.
There exists some integers
a
,
b
and
c
such that for all
{\mathcal{E}}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{H}
the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

H^{i}(H,{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}(m))=0
for all
m\geq a
and all
i>0
,

2.

H^{1}(H,{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}(-m))=0
for
m\geq b
,

3.

h^{1}(H,{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}(m))\leq c
for all
m
.

The second condition above follows from the well-known Enriques–Severi–Zariski lemma for reflexive sheaves on normal varieties (see
[
SP
, Lemma 0FD8]
).
Let us fix
{\mathcal{E}}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{H}
.
For all
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we have short exact sequences

0\to{\mathcal{E}}({m-1})\to{\mathcal{E}}(m)\to{\mathcal{E}}(m)|_{H}\to 0.

Let us take
i\geq 2
. Then for all
m\geq a
we have
H^{i}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m-1))=H^{i}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m))
. So by Serre’s vanishing theorem we get
H^{i}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m-1))=0
for
m\geq a
.
We also know that
h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m))\leq h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m-1))
for all
m\geq a
.
Let us consider the embedding
X\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
given by the linear system
|{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1)|

and let
\tilde{H}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
be the hyperplane defining
H
. For any
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we have a commutative diagram

\textstyle{H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{N},{\mathcal{E}}(m))\otimes H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{N},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{N}}(1))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{1}}
\textstyle{H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{N},{\mathcal{E}}(m+1))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta_{2}}
\textstyle{H^{0}(\tilde{H},{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}(m))\otimes H^{0}(\tilde{H},{\mathcal{O}}_{\tilde{H}}(1))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{2}}
\textstyle{H^{0}(\tilde{H},{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}(m+1)).}

Assume that
h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m))=h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m-1))
for some
m\geq a+n-1
. Then the map
\beta_{1}
in the above diagram is surjective. Since
m\geq a+n-1
, we also know that
H^{i}(H,{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}(m-i))=0
for
i>0
so by the Castelnuovo–Mumford theorem the map
\alpha_{2}
is also surjective. It follows that
\beta_{2}
is surjective. This implies that
h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m+1))=h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m))
. So by Serre’s vanishing theorem we see that
h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m))=0
.
This shows that for
m\geq a+n-2
the sequence
\{h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m))\}
is strictly decreasing until it reaches
0
. So
h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(l))=0
for
l\geq h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(a+n-2))+a+n-2
.
Since
{\mathcal{E}}
is reflexive and
X
is normal we know that
h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(-l))=0
for
l\gg 0
(here we again use
[
SP
, Lemma 0FD8]
). So for all
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we have

\displaystyle h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m))

\displaystyle\leq h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m-1))+h^{1}(H,{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}(m))\leq h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m-2))+h^{1}(H,{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}(m))

\displaystyle+h^{1}(H,{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}(m-1))\leq...\leq\sum_{l\geq 0}h^{1}(H,{\mathcal{E}}|_{H}(m-l))\leq(m+b)c.

In particular,
h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(a+n-2))\leq(a+n-2+b)c
. Therefore
h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{E}}(m))=0
for

m\geq(a+n-2)(c+1)+bc
. This shows that there exists a constant
m_{0}
such that all
{\mathcal{E}}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{H}
are
m
-regular for all
m\geq m_{0}
and hence
{\mathcal{A}}_{H}
is a bounded family (see
[
HL
, Lemma 1.7.6]
). Since the family of divisors
H\in|L_{1}|
is bounded, this also gives boundedness of the family
{\mathcal{A}}
.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

If
L_{1}=...=L_{n-1}
then the above theorem follows from
[
La2
, Theorem 4.4]
(see
[
La9
, Theorem 6.4]
). In general, the problem is that we need restriction theorems for multipolarizations on normal varieties and the method of proof of
[
La2
, Theorem 4.4]
for singular varieties depends on the projection method that works only if we have one polarization. In case of characteristic zero, restriction theorems needed for multipolarizations follow easily from the results of
[
La2
]
by passing to the resolution of singularities and using Bertini’s theorem. Unfortunately, this method also does not work for varieties defined over a field of positive characteristic. However, even in this case Theorem
3.14
is new.

As in
[
La9
, Corollary 6.7]
, Corollary
3.8
and the above theorem imply the following result.

Corollary 3.16
.

Let us fix some positive integer
r
, integer
{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{1}
and some real
numbers
{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}
and
\mu_{\max}
.
Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the set of reflexive coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules
{\mathcal{E}}
such that

1.

{\mathcal{E}}
has rank
r
,

2.

\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{1}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-1}={\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{1}
,

3.

\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}\geq{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}
for
i=1,...,n-1
,

4.

\mu_{\max,L}({\mathcal{E}})\leq\mu_{\max}
.

Then the set
{\mathcal{B}}
is bounded.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.8
there exists a constant
C

such that for all
{\mathcal{E}}\in{\mathcal{B}}
we have

C\leq\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}=c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})^{2}.L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}-2r\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}}).L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}

for
i=1,...,n-1
.
Therefore
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})^{2}.L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}\geq C+2r\,{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}
. Let us write
[c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})]=\alpha_{i}[L_{i}]+D_{i}\in N^{1}_{(L_{1},...,\widehat{L_{i}},...,L_{n-1})}(X)
, where
\alpha_{i}=\frac{{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{1}}{L_{1}...L_{i}^{2}...L_{n-1}}

Then
D_{i}.L_{1}...L_{n-1}=0
and

c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})^{2}.L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}=\alpha_{i}^{2}+D_{i}^{2}.L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}.

Therefore

D^{2}_{i}.L_{1}...\widehat{L_{i}}...L_{n-1}\geq C+2r\,{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}-\alpha_{i}^{2}\cdot L_{1}...L_{i}^{2}...L_{n-1}.

But by the Hodge index theorem (see Lemma
1.22

, so there are only finitely many possibilities for

Since the canonical map

C^{1}(X;L_{1},...,L_{n-1})\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n-1}N^{1}_{(L_{1},...,\widehat{L_{i}},...,L_{n-1})}(X)

is injective, there are also only finitely many possibilities for the classes
[c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})]\in C^{1}(X;L_{1},...,L_{n-1})

3.14
.
∎

The above corollary has some nontrivial implications even in the rank one case:

Corollary 3.17
.

The canonical map
\bar{B}^{1}(X)\to C^{1}(X;L_{1},...,L_{n-1})

Proof.

Let

implies that the set

the set
\{[mD]\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
of the corresponding classes in

is finite. Therefore
[D]=0\in\bar{B}^{1}(X)

∎

Remark 3.18
.

The above corollary implies that some multiple of a Weil divisor

on

4
Modules over Lie algebroids and Higgs sheaves

In this section we show various definitions and simple results on modules over Lie algebroids and on generalized Higgs sheaves. We also compare our notion with the one used in
[
GKPT1
]
. We finish the section with definition of semistability and with a restriction theorem for generalized Higgs sheaves. In the whole section
X
is a scheme over some fixed field
k
.

4.1
Basic definitions

Let us recall that a tangent sheaf
T_{X/k}
is defined as
{\mathop{{\cal H}om}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}(\Omega_{X/k},{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
.
For every open subset
U\subset X
we have a canonical isomorphism

T_{X/k}(U)={\mathop{{\rm Hom}}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{U}}(\Omega_{X/k}|_{U},{\mathcal{O}}_{U})\to\mathop{\rm Der}\,_{k}({\mathcal{O}}_{U},{\mathcal{O}}_{U}),\quad\delta\to\delta\circ d_{X/k}.

So in the following we will identify sections of
T_{X}/k
with
k
-derivations of the structure sheaf
without mentioning it. Now let us recall the following definitions (see
[
La4
, Sections 2 and 3]
).

Definition 4.1
.

A
Lie algebroid on
X/k
is a triple
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\alpha)
consisting of

1.

a quasi-coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
L
,

2.

a morphism of sheaves of
k
-vector spaces
[\cdot,\cdot]_{L}:L\otimes_{k}L\to L
,

3.

an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear map
\alpha:L\to T_{X/k}
,
x\to\alpha_{x}
, called
anchor map
,

such that the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L})
is a sheaf of
k
-Lie algebras,

2.

\alpha
is a homomorphism of sheaves of
k
-Lie algebras,

3.

We have

[x,fy]_{L}=\alpha_{x}(f)y+f\,[x,y]_{L}

for all local sections
f\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
and
x,y\in L
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
L
be a Lie algebroid on
X/k
. An
L
-module is a pair
({\mathcal{E}},\nabla)
consisting of
a quasi-coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
{\mathcal{E}}
and an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear map of left
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules

\nabla:L\to{\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}nd}}_{k}{\mathcal{E}}
, which is also a map of sheaves of

k
-Lie algebras and which satisfies Leibniz’s rule

\nabla(x)(fe)=\alpha_{x}(f)e+\nabla(fx)(e)

for all local sections
f\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
,
x\in L
and
e\in{\mathcal{E}}
.

In the above definition (and also below) we use the following identifications. For every open subset
U\subset X
we have a map
\nabla(U):L(U)\to({\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}nd}}_{k}{\mathcal{E}})(U)={\mathop{{\rm Hom}}}_{k}({\mathcal{E}}|_{U},{\mathcal{E}}|_{U})
. So every
x\in L(U)
gives a
k
-linear map
{\mathcal{E}}|_{U}\to{\mathcal{E}}|_{U}
that is denoted by
\nabla(x)
.
Now the above Leibniz’s rule should be interpreted as equality for all open
V\subset U\subset X

for
x\in L(U)
,
f\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(V)
and
e\in{\mathcal{E}}(V)
, where
fx
denotes
f(x|_{V})
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
({\mathcal{E}}_{1},\nabla_{1})
and
({\mathcal{E}}_{2},\nabla_{2})
be
L
-modules for some Lie algebroid
L
.
A
morphism of
L
-modules

\varphi:({\mathcal{E}}_{1},\nabla_{1})\to({\mathcal{E}}_{2},\nabla_{2})
is
an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear map
\varphi:{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
such that for every open subset
U\subset X
and
every
x\in L(U)
the diagram

\textstyle{{\mathcal{E}}_{1}|_{U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nabla_{1}(x)}
\scriptstyle{\varphi|_{U}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{E}}_{1}|_{U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi|_{U}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{E}}_{2}|_{U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nabla_{2}(x)}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{E}}_{2}|_{U}}

is commutative.

The above definitions allow us to talk about the category
\mathop{\operatorname{{L}-Mod}\,}(X)
of
L
-modules.

4.2
Extensions of modules over Lie algebroids

Let
X
be an integral normal locally Noetherian scheme over some field
k
and let
L
be a Lie algebroid on
X/k
, whose underlying
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module is coherent and reflexive. By abuse we will call such Lie algebroid
reflexive
.

Let
j:U\hookrightarrow X
be a big open subscheme
X
. Let
({\mathcal{E}},\nabla:L_{U}\to{\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}nd}}_{k}{\mathcal{E}})
be an
L_{U}
-module. By assumption
L=j_{*}L_{U}
, so we can set
j_{*}({\mathcal{E}},\nabla):=(j_{*}{\mathcal{E}},j_{*}\nabla)
, where
j_{*}\nabla

acts as
\nabla
on the sections of
j_{*}{\mathcal{E}}
(which are the same as sections of
{\mathcal{E}}
).
In this way we can define the functor
j_{*}:\mathop{\operatorname{{L_{U}}-Mod}\,}(U)\to\mathop{\operatorname{{L}-Mod}\,}(X)
.

We say that an
L
-module
({\mathcal{E}},\nabla)
is
reflexive
if
{\mathcal{E}}
is coherent and reflexive
as an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module. By

\mathop{\operatorname{{L}-Mod}^{\mathop{\rm ref}}\,}(X)
we denote the full subcategory of
\mathop{\operatorname{{L}-Mod}\,}(X)
, whose objects are
reflexive
L
-modules. Note that after restricting to reflexive modules

j_{*}
and
j^{*}
define adjoint equivalences of categories
\mathop{\operatorname{{L_{U}}-Mod}^{\mathop{\rm ref}}\,}(U)
and
\mathop{\operatorname{{L}-Mod}^{\mathop{\rm ref}}\,}(X)
.

4.3
Tensor operations on modules over Lie algebroids

If
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
are
L
-modules then we can define natural
L
-module structures on
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
and on
{\mathop{{\cal H}om}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{E}}_{1},{\mathcal{E}}_{2})
. In particular, if
{\mathcal{E}}
is an
L
-module then

{\mathcal{E}}^{*}
has a canonical
L
-module structure.

If

\nabla_{1}:L\to{\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}nd}}_{k}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
and
\nabla_{2}:L\to{\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}nd}}_{k}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}

are
L
-module structures on
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
then we define an
L
-module structure

\nabla:L\to{\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}nd}}_{k}({\mathcal{E}}_{1}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}_{2})
on
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
by the formula

\nabla(x)=\nabla_{1}(x)\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}+\mathop{\rm Id}\otimes\nabla_{2}(x).

Similarly, we define the
L
-module structure
\nabla:L\to{\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}nd}}_{k}({\mathop{{\cal H}om}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{E}}_{1},{\mathcal{E}}_{2}))
on
{\mathop{{\cal H}om}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{E}}_{1},{\mathcal{E}}_{2})

by the formula

(\nabla(x))(\psi)=(\nabla_{2}(x))\circ\psi-\psi\circ(\nabla_{1}(x)).

This shows that for any
L
-module
{\mathcal{E}}
we can define a natural
L
-module structure on
{\mathcal{E}}^{*}={\mathop{{\cal H}om}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
. So we can also define a reflexive hull
{\mathcal{E}}^{**}
of an
L
-module.
As in the case of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules (see Subsection
1.1
)
the inclusion functor
\mathop{\operatorname{{L}-Mod}^{\mathop{\rm ref}}\,}(X)\to\mathop{\operatorname{{L}-Mod}\,}(X)
comes with a left adjoint
(\cdot)^{**}:\mathop{\operatorname{{L}-Mod}\,}(X)\to\mathop{\operatorname{{L}-Mod}^{\mathop{\rm ref}}\,}(X)
given by the reflexive hull.
In particular, we have a natural map
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}^{**}
of
L
-modules coming from the adjoint map to the identity on

{\mathcal{E}}^{**}
.

Remark 4.4
.

Note that if we have two Higgs sheaves

({\mathcal{E}}_{1},\theta_{1}:{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{1}{\otimes}\Omega_{X}^{[1]})
and
({\mathcal{E}}_{2},\theta_{2}:{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{2}{\otimes}\Omega_{X}^{[1]})

in the sense of
[
GKPT1
]
then we can define the Higgs sheaf structure on

{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
but not on
{\mathop{{\cal H}om}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{E}}_{1},{\mathcal{E}}_{2})
. In particular, if
\Omega_{X}^{[1]}
is not locally free one cannot
define the dual of a Higgs sheaf as a Higgs sheaf. So we cannot also take a reflexivization of a (torsion free) Higgs sheaf in the sense of
[
GKPT1
]
. This is one of the main reasons why we need to use a different definition of a Higgs sheaf.

4.4
Generalized Higgs sheaves

Let
X
be a scheme and let
L
be a quasi-coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module. We can equip
L
with a trival Lie algebroid structure with zero Lie bracket and zero anchor map.

Definition 4.5
.

An
L
-Higgs sheaf
is an
L
-module for the trivial Lie algebroid structure on
L
. In other words, it is a pair
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
consisting of a quasi-coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{E}}
of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules and an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear map
\theta:L\to{\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}nd}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{E}})
of sheaves of Lie rings, where
L
is equipped with the trivial Lie bracket.
An
L
-Higgs sheaf
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
is
reflexive
if

{\mathcal{E}}
is a coherent reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module.

On any scheme
X
, if
{\mathcal{E}}
,
{\mathcal{F}}
,
{\mathcal{G}}
are sheaves of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules then we have a functorial isomorphism of
\Gamma(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
-modules

{\mathop{{\rm Hom}}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{E}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\simeq}{\mathop{{\rm Hom}}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{E}},{\mathop{{\cal H}om}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})).

In particular, we have an isomorphism

\alpha:{\mathop{{\rm Hom}}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}(L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{E}})\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\simeq}{\mathop{{\rm Hom}}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}(L,{\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}nd}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{E}})).

This shows that we can replace
\theta
by an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear map
L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
that by abuse of notation will also be denoted by
\theta
.

Note that we have a map
L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}L\to L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}L
given by sending
x\otimes y
to
x\otimes y-y\otimes x
. Since it maps
x\otimes x
to
0
, this map factors through the canonical projection
L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}L\to{\bigwedge}^{2}L
. Hence we get the map
\iota:{\bigwedge}^{2}L\to L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}L
fitting into an exact sequence

{\bigwedge}^{2}L\to L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}L\to{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{2}L\to 0,

where the second map is the canonical projection.

The following lemma explains how to check when an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear map
L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
gives rise to an
L
-Higgs sheaf.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let us fix an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear map
\theta:L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

1.

The composition

\textstyle{{\bigwedge}^{2}L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}}
\textstyle{L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathop{\rm Id}{\otimes}\theta}
\textstyle{L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\theta}
\textstyle{\mathcal{E}}

vanishes.

2.

The map
\bar{\theta}:=\alpha(\theta):L\to{\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}nd}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{E}})
is a homomorphism of sheaves of Lie rings.

3.

The map
\theta
extends to a
{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{\bullet}L
-module structure on
{\mathcal{E}}
, i.e., there exists an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear map
\tilde{\theta}:{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{\bullet}L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
such that

\tilde{\theta}|_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}:{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
is the identity,
\tilde{\theta}|_{L}=\theta

and the diagram

\textstyle{{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{\bullet}L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{\bullet}L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathop{\rm Id}\otimes\tilde{\theta}}
\scriptstyle{\mu\otimes\mathop{\rm Id}}
\textstyle{{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{\bullet}L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{\theta}}
\textstyle{{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{\bullet}L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{\theta}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{E}},}

where
\mu
is the multiplication in
{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{\bullet}L
, is commutative.

Proof.

Let
x,y
be local sections of
L
and
e
a local section of
{\mathcal{E}}
.
Then the first conditions means that

\theta(x\otimes\theta(y\otimes e))=\theta(y\otimes\theta(x\otimes e)),

which can be rewritten as
\bar{\theta}(x)\bar{\theta}(y)=\bar{\theta}(y)\bar{\theta}(x)
, i.e.,

[\bar{\theta}(x),\bar{\theta}(y)]=0=\bar{\theta}([x,y])
. This shows equivalence of the first two conditions.

If these conditions are satisfied then there exists a homomorphism of sheaves of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-algebras

{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{\bullet}L\to{\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}nd}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{E}})
extending
\alpha(\theta)
. This follows from the definition of
{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{\bullet}L
as the quotient of the tensor algebra of

by the two-sided ideal generated by local sections of the form
x\otimes y-y\otimes x
.

This map provides
{\mathcal{E}}
with a
{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{\bullet}L
-module structure. Clearly, if we have such a structure then also the second condition is satisfied.
∎

Interpretation of a Higgs field as an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear map

A

-Higgs

4.5

4.6
Reflexive Higgs sheaves

In this subsection we assume that

We set
\Omega_{L}^{[m]}=({\bigwedge}^{m}L)^{*}

. So in particular we have

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}

By

the sheaf

is also reflexive. In particular, if

is normal
then
{\mathop{{\cal H}om}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}(L,{\mathcal{E}})\simeq{\mathcal{E}}\hat{\otimes}\Omega_{L}^{[1]}

4.7
Pullback of generalized Higgs sheaves

If
f:X\to Y
is a morphism of schemes and
L
is a quasi-coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
-module then we can easily define the pullback of
L
-Higgs sheaves. Namely, if
({\mathcal{E}},\theta:L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}})
is an
L
-Higgs sheaf then it is easy to see that

(f^{*}{\mathcal{E}},f^{*}\theta:f^{*}L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}f^{*}{\mathcal{E}}=f^{*}(L\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}{\mathcal{E}})\to f^{*}{\mathcal{E}})

is an
f^{*}L
-Higgs sheaf (for example one can check condition 1 from Lemma
4.6
). This defines the pullback functor on the corresponding categories of generalized Higgs sheaves, which is functorial with respect to morphisms between schemes.

4.8
Pullback of Higgs sheaves in characteristic zero

Let
(X,D)
be a klt pair in the characteristic zero case. Then
[
Ke
, Theorems 1.3 and 5.2]
constructs pullback functor for reflexive differentials on klt pairs that is compatible with the usual pullback of Kähler differentials. More precisely, if
f:Y\to X
is a morphism from a normal variety
Y
then we get an
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
-linear map
d_{ref}f:f^{*}\Omega_{X}^{[1]}\to\Omega_{Y}^{[1]}
. This gives rise to the dual map
T_{Y/k}\to(f^{*}\Omega_{X}^{[1]})^{*}
.

If
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
is a Higgs sheaf then the above construction gives a structure of

f^{*}T_{X/k}
-Higgs sheaf on
f^{*}{\mathcal{E}}
. By Corollary
4.10
this induces a
f^{[*]}T_{X/k}
-Higgs sheaf structure on
f^{*}{\mathcal{E}}
. Unfortunately, the canonical map
f^{[*]}T_{X/k}\to(f^{*}\Omega_{X}^{[1]})^{*}
is not an isomorphism in general and we do not have any canonical map
T_{Y}\to f^{[*]}T_{X/k}
. So we cannot pullback general Higgs sheaves on
X
to Higgs sheaves on
Y
.
However, if
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
is a Higgs bundle then we can define its pullback by taking
the composition

f^{*}{\mathcal{E}}=f^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}\to f^{[*]}({\mathcal{E}}\hat{\otimes}\Omega_{X})=f^{*}{\mathcal{E}}\otimes f^{[*]}\Omega_{X}\to f^{*}{\mathcal{E}}\hat{\otimes}\Omega_{Y}^{[1]}.

This construction should be compared to
[
GKPT1
, 5.3]
and the last sentence in
[
GKPT1
, 5.2]
.

Remark 4.13
.

T_{X/k}
has a canonical Lie algebroid structure with the standard Lie bracket and identity anchor map.
A
module with an integrable connection
is a
T_{X/k}
-module for the above Lie algebroid structure.
Note that one cannot define reflexive pullback for reflexive modules with an integrable connection even if
Y
is smooth. The problem is that the pullback would give a reflexive module with an integrable connection.
Such modules are locally free and have vanishing Chern classes. However, one can show explicit examples where the reflexive pullback of a reflexive sheaf underlying a module with an integrable connection does not have vanishing Chern classes.

4.9
Reflexive pullback for Higgs sheaves under finite morphisms

Let
f:X\to Y
be a finite dominant morphism of integral normal schemes, locally of finite type over
k
.

4.9.1
Pullback in the separable case

Lemma
1.2

implies that
f^{[*]}T_{Y/k}=(f^{*}\Omega_{Y/k})^{*}
. So
we have a canonical map
T_{X/k}\to f^{[*]}T_{Y/k}
dual to
df:f^{*}\Omega_{Y/k}\to\Omega_{X/k}.

Since this map is non-interesting for purely inseparable morphisms, from now on

is separable.
In this case the map
T_{X/k}\to f^{[*]}T_{Y/k}
is injective and it uniquely extends to a homomorphism of sheaves of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-algebras
{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{\bullet}T_{X/k}\to{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{\bullet}f^{[*]}T_{Y/k}

If
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
be a Higgs sheaf on
Y
then

(f^{*}{\mathcal{E}},f^{*}\theta)
is an
f^{*}T_{X/k}
-Higgs sheaf. Assume that
{\mathcal{E}}
is reflexive.
Taking reflexivization we get an
f^{*}T_{X/k}
-Higgs module structure on
f^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}
.
By Corollary

-Higgs module

f^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}

{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{\bullet}T_{X/k}\to{\mathop{{\rm Sym}}}^{\bullet}f^{[*]}T_{Y/k}

with a canonical Higgs module structure. This Higgs module will be denoted by

One can also describe the above construction explicitly in the following way (this will be useful in the next construction). Namely, let
({\mathcal{E}},\theta:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}\hat{\otimes}\Omega_{Y}^{[1]})
be a reflexive Higgs sheaf on
Y
(see Lemma

).
Then there exists a big open subset

such that
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
is (log) smooth on

Since

is finite,

is a big open subset of

be the corresponding open embedding. Then we can define the map

(f^{*}{\mathcal{E}})_{U}=f^{*}({\mathcal{E}}_{V})\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{f^{*}\theta}f^{*}({\mathcal{E}}_{V}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{V}}\Omega_{V})=f^{*}({\mathcal{E}}_{V})\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{U}}f^{*}\Omega_{V}\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\mathop{\rm Id}\otimes df}(f^{*}{\mathcal{E}})_{U}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{U}}\Omega_{U}.

This gives the map

T_{X}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}f^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}=j_{*}(T_{U})\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}j_{*}((f^{*}{\mathcal{E}})_{U})\to j_{*}(T_{U}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{U}}(f^{*}{\mathcal{E}})_{U})\to j_{*}((f^{*}{\mathcal{E}})_{U})=f^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}.

Unfortunately, the above construction is rather useless in case

5

This section contains proofs of Theorems

5.1
Ogus–Vologodsky’s correspondence on normal varieties

Let
X
be a normal variety defined over an algebraically closed field
k
of positive characteristic
p
.
Let
D
be an effective reduced Weil divisor on
X

Let us consider a (reflexive) Lie algebroid
L

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module is
T_{X}(\log D)
with the
anchor map
T_{X}(\log D)\hookrightarrow T_{X/k}
and the Lie bracket induced from

An

an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module with an integrable logarithmic connection
on
(X,D)
. In fact,

carries a restricted Lie algebroid structure
(see
[

This allows us to talk about

of an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module with an integrable logarithmic connection.

If
({\mathcal{E}},\nabla:T_{X}(\log D)\to{\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}nd}}_{k}{\mathcal{E}})
is a reflexive
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module with an integrable logarithmic connection then we can also define its residues in the following way.
For every open subset
U\subset X
an element
\delta\in(T_{X}(\log D))(U)
can be considered as a logarithmic
k

{\mathcal{O}}_{U}
. Let
J
be the ideal subsheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{U}
generated by the image of

{\mathcal{E}}|_{U}\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\nabla(\delta)}{\mathcal{E}}|_{U}\to({\mathcal{E}}|_{U})/(J{\mathcal{E}}|_{U})

induces an endomorphism
\rho_{\delta}
of
({\mathcal{E}}|_{U})/(J{\mathcal{E}}|_{U})
called the residue associated to
\delta
.
We say that the residues of
({\mathcal{E}},\nabla)
are
nilpotent of order
\leq p
if for every
U\subset X
and
\delta\in(T_{X}(\log D))(U)
we have
\rho_{\delta}^{p}=0
.

Similarly, one can consider

with the trivial Lie bracket and zero anchor map.
Modules over this reflexive Lie algebroid are called
logarithmic Higgs sheaves
on
(X,D)

We say that a logarithmic Higgs sheaf
({\mathcal{E}},\theta:T_{X}(\log D)\to{\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}nd}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{E}})

a nilpotent Higgs field of level
\leq(p-1)
if for every open subset

and every

\delta\in(T_{X}(\log D))(U)

, where
\theta(\delta)
is considered as an
{\mathcal{O}}_{U}
-linear endomorphism of
{\mathcal{E}}|_{U}
.

The following theorem generalizes Ogus–Vologodsky’s correspondence to normal varieties:

Proof.

As remarked in Subsection

and any big open subset

, we have an equivalence of categories of reflexive
L
-modules on
X
and reflexive
L_{U}
-modules on
U
.
So the results of Ogus and Vologodsky in the usual case (see
[

]
) and Schepler in the logarithmic one (see
[

]
; see also
[

, Theorem 2.5]
and
[

, Appendix]
)
give the above correspondence on

preserves the remaining conditions. For Higgs modules it is clear that having a nilpotent Higgs field of level

on

gives the same condition on

. Similarly, for modules with logarithmic connections checking nilpotency of the logarithmic

-curvature on

implies the one on

.
∎

A quasi-inverse to

is denoted by

(or simply

) and it is called the

.

5.2
Strong restriction theorem for logarithmic Higgs sheaves

We keep the notation from the previous subsection.

Definition 5.2

5.4
Higgs–de Rham sequences on normal varieties

Let
X
be a normal projective variety defined over an algebraically closed field
k
of positive characteristic
p
.
Let
D
be an effective reduced Weil divisor on
X
.

Let us assume that
(X,D)
is almost liftable to
W_{2}(k)
. Then we can
find a big open subset
U\subset X
such that the pair

(U,D_{U}=D\cap U)
is log smooth and liftable to
W_{2}(k)
. Let us fix
a lifting
(\tilde{U},\tilde{D}_{U})
of

Let
({\mathcal{E}},\theta:T_{X}(\log D)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}})

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module of rank

. Let us assume that
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
is slope semistable. Then
by Corollary
5.7

on
{\mathcal{E}}
such that the associated graded
(\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{0},\bar{\theta}_{0})

is a slope semistable system of logarithmic Hodge sheaves.

({\mathcal{E}}_{0},\theta_{0})

-module with a nilpotent Higgs field of level less or equal
to

. So we can define

(V_{0},\nabla_{0}):=C_{(\tilde{U},\tilde{D}_{U})}^{[-1]}({\mathcal{E}}_{0},\theta_{0})

Let

be (decreasing) Simpson’s filtration on

and let

({\mathcal{E}}_{1},\theta_{1}):=((\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{1})^{**},{\bar{\theta}_{1}}^{**})
and repeat the procedure. In this way we get the following sequence

\textstyle{({\mathcal{E}},\theta)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{({\mathop{Gr}}_{N})^{**}}
\textstyle{(V_{0},\nabla_{0})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{({\mathop{Gr}}_{S_{0}})^{**}}
\textstyle{(V_{1},\nabla_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{({\mathop{Gr}}_{S_{1}})^{**}}
\textstyle{({\mathcal{E}}_{0},\theta_{0})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{C^{[-1]}}
\textstyle{({\mathcal{E}}_{1},\theta_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{C^{[-1]}}
\textstyle{...}

in which each logarithmic Higgs sheaf

and slope semistable.

of

.

Remark 5.8
.

Higgs de Rham sequences were invented by G. Lan. M. Sheng and K. Zuo in
[
LSZ2
]
and their existence was proven in
[
LSZ2
]
and
[

]
. Canonical Higgs–de Rham sequences in the above sense first appeared in the proof of

. They are better suited to dealing with normal varieties as one cannot define suitable Chern classes for torsion free sheaves on normal varieties.

Remark 5.9
.

Although the above construction is very general, it does not seem easy to compare numerical invariants of

without some further assumptions on the singularities of the pair

.

5.5
Inverse Cartier transform on log varieties with locally

-liftable singularities.

Let
X
be a normal variety defined over an algebraically closed field

of positive characteristic

.
We define the

\textstyle{(V_{0},\nabla_{0})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{({\mathop{Gr}}_{S_{0}})^{**}}
\textstyle{(V_{1},\nabla_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{({\mathop{Gr}}_{S_{1}})^{**}}
\textstyle{({\mathcal{E}}_{0},\theta_{0})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{C^{[-1]}}
\textstyle{({\mathcal{E}}_{1},\theta_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{C^{[-1]}}
\textstyle{...}

be the canonical Higgs–de Rham sequence of
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
.

By Lemma
4.15
there exists
\alpha
such that
\mu_{\max,L}({\mathcal{E}}_{m})-\mu_{L}({\mathcal{E}}_{m})\leq\alpha
for all

. So by Corollary
3.8
there exists some

such that for every non-negative integer

\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}}_{m})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq C.

Lemma
2.4
implies that

\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}}_{0})L_{2}...L_{n-1}

and

\int_{X}\Delta(V_{m})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}}_{m+1})L_{2}...L_{n-1}.

By Corollary

we have

\int_{X}\Delta(V_{m})L_{2}...L_{n-1}=p^{2}\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}}_{m})L_{2}...L_{n-1}.

Therefore

C\leq\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}}_{m})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\leq p^{2m}\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}.

Dividing by
p^{2m}
and passing with

to infinity, we get
\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq 0
.
∎

Remark 5.13
.

The above theorem holds also for reflexive sheaves with an integrable logarithmic connection.
Indeed, if
({\mathcal{E}},\nabla)
is a rank

slope
L
-semistable reflexive sheaf with an integrable
logarithmic connection and

is its Simpson’s filtration then
by the above theorem and Lemma

we have

\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq\int_{X}\Delta(({\mathop{Gr}}_{S}{\mathcal{E}})^{**})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq 0.

6
The Miyaoka–Yau inequality on singular varieties in positive characteristic

In this section we prove the Miyaoka–Yau inequality on some mildly singular varieties in positive characteristic. The ideas are similar to that from
[
Si
]
and
[
La6
]
but we show full proofs to show where they need additional facts related to the use of our Chern classes.

We fix a log pair
(X,D)
defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
. We assume that
(X,D)
is almost liftable to
W_{2}(k)
and it has
F
-liftable singularities in codimension
2
. Let
n=\dim X
and let us fix a collection
L=(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
of nef line bundles on
X
such that
L_{1}^{2}L_{2}....L_{n-1}>0
. As in Subsection
3.2
we consider a positive open cone
K^{+}_{L}\subset N_{L}(X)
.

The proof of the following proposition is essentially the same as that of
[
La6
, Proposition 4.1]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{L}}
be a rank
1
reflexive sheaf contained in

\Omega_{X}^{[1]}(\log D)
. Then
c_{1}({\mathcal{L}})\not\in K^{+}_{L}
.

Proof.

Assume that
c_{1}({\mathcal{L}})\in K^{+}_{L}
and consider a system of logarithmic Hodge sheaves

({\mathcal{E}}:={\mathcal{E}}^{1}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}^{0},\theta)
with
{\mathcal{E}}^{1}={\mathcal{L}}
,
{\mathcal{E}}^{0}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}
and

\theta:{\mathcal{E}}^{1}\to{\mathcal{E}}^{0}\hat{\otimes}\Omega_{X}^{[1]}(\log D)=\Omega_{X}^{[1]}(\log D)
given by the
inclusion. Then
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
is slope
L
-stable since the
only rank
1
logarithmic subsystem of Hodge sheaves of
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
is of the form

({\mathcal{O}}_{X},0)
. Therefore by Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
5.12

we have

0=4\int_{X}c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq\int_{X}c_{1}^{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}=c_{1}({\mathcal{L}})^{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1},

a contradiction.
∎

Similarly as
[
La6
, Theorem 4.4]
one can also get the following theorem generalizing the Miyaoka–Yau inequality in the surface case:

Theorem 6.2
.

Let us assume that
p\geq 3
and let
{\mathcal{F}}\subset\Omega_{X}^{[1]}(\log D)
be a rank
2
reflexive subsheaf with
c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})\in\overline{K^{+}_{L}}
. Then

3\int_{X}c_{2}({\mathcal{F}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq\int_{X}c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})^{2}L_{2}...L_{n-1}.

Proof.

Let us consider the system of logarithmic Hodge sheaves
({\mathcal{E}}:={\mathcal{E}}^{1}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}^{0},\theta)
given
by
{\mathcal{E}}^{1}={\mathcal{F}}
,
{\mathcal{E}}^{0}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}
and
\theta:{\mathcal{E}}^{1}={\mathcal{E}}\hookrightarrow\Omega_{X}^{[1]}(\log D)={\mathcal{E}}^{0}\hat{\otimes}\Omega_{X}^{[1]}(\log D)
.
If
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
is slope
L
-semistable, then by Lemma
2.1
and
Theorem
5.12
we have

3\int_{X}c_{2}({\mathcal{F}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}=3\int_{X}c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq\int_{X}c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})^{2}L_{2}...L_{n-1}=\int_{X}c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})^{2}L_{2}...L_{n-1}.

So we can assume that
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
is not slope
L
-semistable. Let

({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
be its maximal destabilizing subsystem of logarithmic Hodge subsheaves.

({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
contains only one saturated rank
1
system of logarithmic Hodge subsheaves,
namely
({\mathcal{O}}_{X},0)
. Since this subsystem does not destabilize
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
, the sheaf

{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
has rank
2
. Note that
({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
is slope
L
-stable so
c_{1}({\mathcal{M}}).L_{1}...L_{n-1}>0
.
We can decompose
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
into a direct sum
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\oplus{\mathcal{M}}
, where
{\mathcal{M}}
is a saturated
rank
1
reflexive sheaf contained in
{\mathcal{F}}
. By assumption
({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
destabilizes
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
so

\mu_{L}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})=\frac{c_{1}({\mathcal{M}}).L_{1}...L_{n-1}}{2}>\mu_{L}({\mathcal{E}})=\frac{c_{1}({\mathcal{F}}).L_{1}...L_{n-1}}{3}.

Therefore
(3c_{1}({\mathcal{M}})-2c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})).L_{1}...L_{n-1}>0
. If
(3c_{1}({\mathcal{M}})-2c_{1}({\mathcal{F}}))\in K_{L}^{+}
then

3c_{1}({\mathcal{M}})=(3c_{1}({\mathcal{M}})-2c_{1}({\mathcal{F}}))+2c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})\in K_{L}^{+}
, which contradicts Proposition

6.1
. This shows that

(3c_{1}({\mathcal{M}})-2c_{1}({\mathcal{F}}))^{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1}\leq 0.

Let us set
{\mathcal{L}}:=({\mathcal{F}}/{\mathcal{M}})^{**}
. Then the sequence

0\to{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{F}}\to{\mathcal{L}}

satisfies assumptions of Lemma
2.1
and hence

\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{F}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\leq\frac{1}{2}c_{1}({\mathcal{M}})^{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1}+\frac{1}{2}c_{1}({\mathcal{L}})^{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1},

which after rewriting gives

\displaystyle\int_{X}(3c_{2}({\mathcal{F}})-c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})^{2})L_{2}...L_{n-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{4}c_{1}({\mathcal{M}})^{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{4}(3c_{1}({\mathcal{M}})-2c_{1}({\mathcal{F}}))^{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq 0.

Since by Proposition
6.1
we have
c_{1}({\mathcal{M}})^{2}.L_{2}...L_{n-1}\leq 0
, this implies the required inequality.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

Although Theorem

works for any normal varieties in positive characteristic, it does not seem easy to use a similar spreading out argument to obtain even the usual Mehta–Ramanathan theorem for ample multipolarizations on a general normal projective variety in characteristic zero.

7.2
Bogomolov’s inequality for logarithmic Higgs sheaves

We will need an analogue of the first part of Lemma
2.1
in the characteristic zero case (the analogue of the second part also holds but we will not need it):

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
L=(L_{1},...,L_{n-2})
be a collection of nef line bundles on
X
.
If

0\to{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\to 0

is a left exact sequence of reflexive sheaves on
X
, which is also right exact
on some big open subset of
X
then

\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}})L_{1}...L_{n-2}\leq\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{1})L_{1}...L_{n-2}+\int_{X}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{2})L_{1}...L_{n-2}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
2.1
we first reduce to the surface case. Then by Lemma
7.1
there exists a normal projective surface
Y
and a finite morphism
\pi:Y\to X

such that
\pi^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}
,
\pi^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
and
\pi^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
are locally free.
As in the proof of Lemma
2.1
we see that the sequence

0\to\pi^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to\pi^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}\to\pi^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\to 0

is left exact on
Y
and right exact on some big open subset of
Y
. Therefore

\chi(Y,\pi^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}})\leq\chi(Y,\pi^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}_{1})+\chi(Y,\pi^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}).

Using the Riemann–Roch theorem for locally free sheaves on normal projective surfaces this can be rewritten as

\int_{Y}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}(\pi^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}})\leq\int_{Y}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}(\pi^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}_{1})+\int_{Y}{\mathop{\rm ch\,}}_{2}(\pi^{[*]}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}).

Dividing by the degree of
\pi
, we get the required inequality from the second part of Lemma
7.1
. ∎

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
L=(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
be a collection of nef line bundles on
X
such that
L_{1}^{2}L_{2}....L_{n-1}>0
.
For any slope
(L_{1},...,L_{n-1})
-semistable logarithmic reflexive Higgs sheaf
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
we have

\int_{X}\Delta({\mathcal{E}})L_{2}...L_{n-1}\geq 0.

Proof.

First assume that
L_{1},...,L_{n-1}
are ample. Then by the above theorem we can restrict to the surface case. Since finite quotients of smooth affine log surface pairs are
F
-liftable in large characteristics (cf.
[

, Lemma 4.21]
), we can apply Theorem
5.12
and an easy spreading out argument.

In general, we reduce to the above case by an argument analogous to that from the proof of
[

, 3.6]
. Namely, we fix an ample line bundle

and consider the classes
L_{i}(t)=c_{1}(L_{i})+tc_{1}(A)
in

for

. These classes are ample and the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
with respect to
(L_{1}(t),...,L_{n-1}(t))
is independent of

for small

.
We have an analogue of Lemma
2.5
for normal projective varieties in characteristic zero that have quotient singularities in codimension
2
(this follows from Lemma

in the same way as Lemma
2.5
follows from Lemma

).
Applying this result to the above filtration, using the inequality for ample collections of line bundles and taking the limit as

Depth and regularity of tableau ideals
Do Trong Hoang, Thanh Vu

Abstract
. We compute the depth and regularity of ideals associated with arbitrary fillings of positive integers to a Young diagram, called the tableau ideals.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
13D02, 05E40, 13F55

1.
Introduction

A Young diagram, also called a Ferrers diagram, is a collection of boxes arranged in left-justified rows with row lengths weakly decreasing. Any way of putting a positive integer in each box of a Young diagram will be called a
filling
of the diagram. A filling
Y
is called weakly increasing if it is weakly increasing across each row and down each column of the diagram. (In standard terminology, a semistandard Young tableau requires strictly increasing down each column of the diagram). The shape of a tableau
Y
is the corresponding partition
\lambda
whose
\lambda_{i}
is the number of boxes in the
i
th row of
Y
. Young tableaux play an important role in reprensentation theory of symmetric groups and general linear groups, as well as Schubert calculus. They also have wide applications in other fields (see
[
Ful
]
for more detail).

For each Young diagram
\lambda
, Corso and Nagel
[
CN
]
introduced and studied the so-called Ferrers ideal,
I_{\lambda}
. They described its minimal free resolution. To each filling
Y
, we also have a natural associated monomial ideal, which generalizes the Ferrers ideal. More precisely, assume that
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
and
Y
is the filling of
\lambda
whose value at the
ij
box is
w(i,j)
. The
tableau ideal
associated with
Y
is the following ideal inside a standard graded polynomial ring
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
over a field
\mathrm{k}
, where
m=\lambda_{1}

I(Y)=\left((x_{i}y_{j})^{w(i,j)}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}\right).

The tableau ideal is the edge ideal of an edge-weighted graph introduced by Paulsen and Sather-Wagstaff
[
PS
]
. Recently, there has been a surge of interest in characterizing weights for which the edge ideals of edge-weighted graphs are Cohen-Macaulay
[
FSTY
,
DMV
,
Hi
,
W
]
. Nonetheless, computing the exact value of the depth and regularity of edge ideals of edge-weighted graphs is more subtle. In this paper, we give a simple recursive formula for the depth and regularity of
I(Y)
for arbitrary fillings
Y
. Our work is the first result to compute the depth and regularity of edge ideals of a family of edge-weighted graphs for arbitrary weights.

To describe our main results, we first introduce some notation. Let

\omega=\min\left\{w(i,j)\mid 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}\right\}

be the minimum weight. A box
(\gamma,\delta)
is called a
minimal box
of
Y
if
w(\gamma,\delta)=\omega
and
w(i,j)>\omega
for all
(i,j)\neq(\gamma,\delta)
such that
i\leq\gamma
and
j\leq\delta
. We have

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Y
be an arbitrary filling of a Young diagram
\lambda
. Let
(\gamma,\delta)
be a minimal box of
Y
. Then

\depth S/I(Y)=\min\left\{\depth(S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma}))),\depth(S/(I(Y)+(y_{\delta})))\right\}.

Consequently, we deduce that the depth of
I(Y)
is the same as that of the corresponding Ferrers ideal when
Y
is weakly increasing and classify all weights for which
I(Y)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Corollary 1.2
.

Assume that
Y
is a weakly increasing filling of a Young diagram
\lambda
. Then

\depth S/I(Y)=\depth S/I_{\lambda}=m+n-\max\{\lambda_{i}+i-1\mid i=1,\ldots,n\}.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
Y
be an arbitrary filling of a Young diagram
\lambda
. Then
I(Y)
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
\lambda=(n,n-1,\ldots,2,1)
and
Y
is weakly increasing.

We also have a similar formula for the regularity of
I(Y)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
Y
be an arbitrary filling of a Young diagram
\lambda
with at least two boxes. Let
(\gamma,\delta)
be a minimal box of
Y
. Then,

\reg S/I(Y)=\omega-1+\max\left\{\reg S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})),\reg S/(I(Y)+(y_{\delta}))\right\}.

Note that
I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})
and
I(Y)+(y_{\delta})
correspond to the tableau ideals obtained by deleting the
\gamma
th row and
\delta
th column of
Y
respectively. Thus, Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.4
give us simple recursive formulae for computing the depth and regularity of
I(Y)
. Here is an example to illustrate our result.

Example 1.5
.

Consider the following filling of a Young diagram

3

The tableau ideal associated with
Y
is

\displaystyle I(Y)=(

First, the red box is the one with the smallest weight. Hence, we have

\depth S/I(Y)=\min\{\depth S/(I(Y)+(x_{1})),\depth S/(I(Y)+(y_{2}))\}.

After deleting either the first row or the second column, the yellow box will become the minimal box. Repeating the process, we get

.

\reg S/I(Y)=1+3+5+5+6=20.

We now describe the idea of proofs of the main results. For a monomial ideal
I
, Hochster
[
Hoc
]
introduced the set of
associated radicals
of
I
, namely
\sqrt{I:u}
for monomials
u\notin I
and proved that

\depth S/I=\min\{\depth S/J\mid J\text{ is an associated radical of }I\}.

In
[
MNPTV
]
, Minh, Nam, Phong, Thuy, and Vu proved that the regularity of
I
can also be computed via the associated radicals of
I
. Thus, a key step in proving our main results is to understand the associated radicals of tableau ideals. More precisely, let
Y^{\prime}
be the filling of
\lambda
obtained by replacing the weight
\omega
on the
(\gamma,\delta)
box of
Y
by
1
. The steps to prove Theorem
1.1
are

(1)

By comparing the associated radicals of
I(Y)
and
I(Y^{\prime})
, we prove in Lemma
3.3
that
\depth S/I(Y)\geq\depth S/I(Y^{\prime})
.

(2)

In Lemma
3.4
, we prove that
\depth S/I(Y)\leq\depth S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma}))
by using the structure of Ferrers ideals to reduce to the case where
I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})
has an associated radical corresponding to a complete bipartite graph (i.e., a Ferrers ideal of a rectangular partition).

(3)

By using the result of Caviglia, Ha, Herzog, Kummini, Terai, and Trung
[
CHHKTT
]
stating that
\depth S/I\in\{\depth S/(I,x),\depth(S/I:x)\}
, we deduce the conclusion.

Similarly, the steps to prove Theorem
1.4
are

(1)

By analyzing the structure of the degree complexes of
I(Y)
, in Lemma
4.4
we prove that
\reg S/I(Y)\leq\reg S/I(Y^{\prime})+(\omega-1)
.

(2)

Similarly, by analyzing the degree complexes of
I(Y)
and
I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})
, in Lemma
4.5
, we prove that
\reg S/I(Y)\geq(\omega-1)+\reg S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma}))
.

(3)

By using the result of Dao, Huneke, and Schweig
[
DHS
]
stating that
\reg S/I\in\{\reg S/(I,x),\reg S/I:x+1\}
, we deduce the conclusion.

While the steps to establish the formulae for the depth and regularity of tableau ideals are similar, the proofs are on different extremes. The formulae in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.4
can be restated in terms of combinatorics of the tableau
Y
as follows. Each box
(i,j)
of
Y
is marked with either
r
or
c
, corresponding to the
i
th row or the
j
th column of
Y
. A marked
(i,j)
box is denoted by
(i,j,m)
with
m\in\{r,c\}
. For a marked box
(i,j,m)
, we let

L(i,j,m)=\begin{cases}\text{the }i\text{th row of }Y&\text{ if }m=r,\\
\text{the }j\text{th column of }Y&\text{ if }m=c.\end{cases}

A collection of marked boxes
M=\{(i_{1},j_{1},m_{1}),\ldots,(i_{s},j_{s},m_{s})\}
is called an
admissible collection
if
Y=L(i_{1},j_{1},m_{1})\cup\cdots\cup L(i_{s},j_{s},m_{s})
and
(i_{t},j_{t})
is a minimal box of the tableau
Y_{t}
obtained by deleting
L(i_{1},j_{1},m_{1}),\ldots,L(i_{t-1},j_{t-1},m_{t-1})
. The shape of
Y_{t}
is denoted by
\lambda^{t}
. Its conjugate partition is denoted by
\mu^{t}
. For each
t=1,\ldots,s
, we set

d_{t}=\begin{cases}(\lambda^{t})_{1}-(\lambda^{t})_{2}&\text{ if }m_{t}=c\text{ and }L(i_{t},j_{t},m_{t})\text{ is the first column of }Y_{t},\\
(\mu^{t})_{1}-(\mu^{t})_{2}&\text{ if }m_{t}=r\text{ and }L(i_{t},j_{t},m_{t})\text{ is the first row of }Y_{t},\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

The depth and regularity of
M
with respect to
Y
are defined by

\displaystyle d(M,Y)

\displaystyle=d_{1}+\cdots+d_{s},

\displaystyle r(M,Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{s}(w(i_{1},j_{1})-1)+w(i_{s},j_{s}).

Then we have

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
Y
be an arbitrary filling of a Young diagram
\lambda
. Denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of all admissible collections of marked boxes of
Y
. Then

\displaystyle\depth S/I(Y)

\displaystyle=\min\{d(M,Y)\mid M\in\mathcal{M}\},

\displaystyle\reg S/I(Y)

\displaystyle=\max\{r(M,Y)\mid M\in\mathcal{M}\}.

We now describe the organization of the paper. In Section
2
, we first state definitions and standard results about the depth and regularity of monomial ideals. Next, we recall the notion of edge ideals of edge-weighted graphs. Finally, we consider Ferrers ideals and tableau ideals. In Section
3
, we compute the depth of tableau ideals. In Section
4
, we compute the regularity of tableau ideals and prove Theorem
1.6
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first state definitions and some properties concerning the depth and regularity of monomial ideals. Next, we recall edge ideals of edge-weighted graphs and prove some results about their associated radicals. We then recall the results of Corso and Nagel
[
CN
]
about Ferrers ideals. Finally, we introduce tableau ideals and establish some properties of their associated radicals.

In the first three subsections, we let
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
\mathrm{k}
with the maximal homogeneous ideal
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
. We first recall the notion of depth and regularity. For more information, we refer to
[
BH
]
.

2.1.
Depth and regularity

For a finitely generated graded
S
-module
L
, the depth of
L
is defined to be

\depth(L)=\min\{i\mid H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(L)\neq 0\},

where
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)
denotes the
i
-th local cohomology module of
L
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
.

The regularity of
L
is defined to be

\reg(L)=\max\{i+j\mid H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(L)_{j}\neq 0\}.

Definition 2.1
.

A finitely generated graded
S
-module
L
is called Cohen-Macaulay if
\depth L=\dim L
. A homogeneous ideal
I\subseteq S
is said to be Cohen-Macaulay if
S/I
is a Cohen-Macaulay
S
-module.

We have the following result of Hochster
[
Hoc
]
on the depth of monomial ideals.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal. Then

\depth S/I=\min\{\depth S/\sqrt{I:f}\mid f\text{ is a monomial such that }f\notin I\}.

We also have the following results on the depth and regularity of monomial ideals modulo a variable.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal and
x
a variable. Then

\displaystyle\depth S/I

\displaystyle\in\{\depth(S/I:x),\depth(S/(I,x))\}

\displaystyle\reg S/I

\displaystyle\in\{\reg(S/I:x)+1,\reg(S/(I,x))\}.

Proof.

This is
[
CHHKTT
, Corollary 3.3]
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal and
x
a variable. Then

(1)

\depth S/(I,x)\geq\depth S/I-1,

(2)

\reg S/I\geq\reg S/(I,x)
.

Proof.

For the first statement, let
J
be the restriction of
I
to the polynomial subring
T
on the variable
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\setminus\{x\}
. By
[
HHZ
, Lemma 4.4]
,
\pd(T/J)\leq\pd(S/I)
. The conclusion follows from the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula.

The second statement follows from
[
CHHKTT
, Corollary 4.8]
.
∎

In the ideals of the form
I+(x)
and
\sqrt{I:f}
, some variables will be part of the minimal generators, and some will not appear in any of the minimal generators. A variable that does not divide any minimal generators of a monomial ideal
J
will be called a free variable of
J
. We have

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
I=J+(x_{a},\ldots,x_{b})
and
x_{b+1},\ldots,x_{n}
are free variables of
J
where
J
is a monomial ideal in
R=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{a-1}]
. Then

\displaystyle\depth S/I

\displaystyle=\depth R/J+(n-b),

\displaystyle\reg S/I

\displaystyle=\reg R/J.

Proof.

Since free variables do not contribute to the minimal free resolution of
I
, we have
\pd(S/I)=\pd(T/I)
and
\reg S/I=\reg T/I
where
T=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{b}]
. Now,
I
is the mixed sum of
J
and a linear ideal. The conclusion follows from the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula and
[
NV
, Theorem 1.1]
.
∎

To compute the regularity of monomial ideals, we use the following result of Minh, Nam, Phong, Thuy, and Vu
[
MNPTV
]
. We first introduce some notation. For an exponent
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\NN^{n}
, we set
x^{\mathbf{a}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
and
|\mathbf{a}|=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}
. The support of
\mathbf{a}
is

\supp\mathbf{a}=\{i\in[n]\mid a_{i}\neq 0\}.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
S
. Then

\reg(S/I)=\max\{|\mathbf{a}|+i\mid\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n},i\geq 0,\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}F;\mathrm{k})\neq 0\\
\text{ for some $F\in\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)$ with $F\cap\supp\mathbf{a}=\emptyset$}\},

where
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)=\Delta(\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbf{a}}})
is the degree complex of
I
in degree
\mathbf{a}
.

Proof.

Follows from Lemma 2.12 and Lemma 2.19 of
[
MNPTV
]
.
∎

Note that
\Delta(J)
is the Stanley-Reisner complex corresponding to a squarefree monomial ideal
J
(see the next section for more detail).

Definition 2.7
.

A pair
(\mathbf{a},i)\in\NN^{n}\times\NN
is called a
critical pair
of
I
if there exists a face
F
of
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
with
F\cap\supp\mathbf{a}=\emptyset
and an index
i
such that
\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}F;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. It is called an
extremal pair
of
I
if furthermore,
\reg S/I=|\mathbf{a}|+i
.

An exponent
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
is called a
critical exponent
(respectively an
extremal exponent
) of
I
if
(\mathbf{a},i)
is a critical pair (respectively an extremal pair) of
I
for some
i\geq 0
.

When
(\mathbf{a},i)
is a critical pair (respectively extremal pair) of
I
, we also call
(x^{\mathbf{a}},i)
a critical pair (respectively extremal pair) of
I
.

For a monomial
f
in S and
i\in[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
\deg_{i}(f)=\max(t\mid x_{i}^{t}\text{ divides }f)
denotes the degree of
x_{i}
in
f
. For a monomial ideal
I
,
\rho_{i}(I)
is defined by

\rho_{i}(I)=\max(\deg_{i}(u)\mid u\text{ is a minimal monomial generator of }I).

By
[
MNPTV
, Remark 2.13]
, we have

Remark 2.8
.

A critical exponent of a monomial ideal
I
belongs to the finite set

\Gamma(I)=\{\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}\mid a_{j}<\rho_{j}(I)\text{ for all }j=1,\ldots,n\}.

We now demonstrate a situation where one can reduce the computation of the regularity of a monomial ideal
I
by adding a variable.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal. Assume that
(\mathbf{a},i)
is an extremal pair of
I
and
F
is a face of
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
such that
F\cap\supp\mathbf{a}=\emptyset
and
\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}F;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. Let
x
be a variable in
F
. Then
\reg S/I=\reg S/(I,x)
.

Proof.

Let
J=I+(x)
. Since
x\in F
,
x\notin\supp\mathbf{a}
. By
[
MNPTV
, Lemma 2.24]
,
\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}=\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbf{a}}}+(x)
. In other words,
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
is the restriction of
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
to
[n]\setminus\{x\}
. Let
F^{\prime}=F\setminus\{x\}
. Then,
\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}F=\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)}F^{\prime}
. Thus,
(\mathbf{a},i)
is a critical pair of
J
. By Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.6
,

\reg S/J\geq|\mathbf{a}|+i=\reg S/I\geq\reg S/J.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Since we will compute the depth and regularity of monomial ideals via their associated radicals, we now introduce the notion of simplicial complexes and Stanley-Reisner correspondence.

2.2.
Simplicial complexes and Stanley-Reisner correspondence

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
that is a collection of subsets of
[n]
closed under taking subsets. The set of its maximal elements under inclusion, called facets, is denoted by
\mathfrak{F}(\Delta)
.

A simplicial complex
\Delta
is called a cone over
x\in[n]
if
x\in B
for any
B\in\mathfrak{F}(\Delta)
.

For a face
F\in\Delta
, the link of
F
in
\Delta
is the subsimplicial complex of
\Delta
defined by

\lk_{\Delta}F=\{G\in\Delta\mid F\cup G\in\Delta,F\cap G=\emptyset\}.

For each subset
F
of
[n]
, let
x_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
be a squarefree monomial in
S
. We now recall the Stanley-Reisner correspondence.

Definition 2.10
.

For a squarefree monomial ideal
I
, the Stanley-Reisner complex of
I
is defined by

\Delta(I)=\{F\subseteq[n]\mid x_{F}\notin I\}.

For a simplicial complex
\Delta
, the Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta
is defined by

I_{\Delta}=(x_{F}\mid F\notin\Delta).

The Stanley-Reisner ring of
\Delta
is
\mathrm{k}[\Delta]=S/I_{\Delta}.

Definition 2.11
.

The
q
-th reduced homology group of
\Delta
with coefficients over
\mathrm{k}
, denoted
\widetilde{H}_{q}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})
is defined to be the
q
-th homology group of the augmented oriented chain complex of
\Delta
over
\mathrm{k}
.

A simplicial complex
\Delta
is called
acyclic
if
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})=0
for all
i
.

Remark 2.12
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex. Then

(1)

\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
if and only if
\Delta
is the empty complex (i.e.,
\Delta=\{\emptyset\}
).

(2)

If
\Delta
is a cone over some
t\in[n]
or
\Delta
is the void complex (i.e.,
\Delta=\emptyset
), then it is acyclic.

2.3.
Edge ideals of edge-weighted graphs and their associated radicals

Let
G
denote a finite simple graph over the vertex set
V(G)=[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and the edge set
E(G)
. For a vertex
x\in V(G)
,
N_{G}(x)=\{y\in V(G)\mid\{x,y\}\in E(G)\}
denotes the set of neighbours of
x
. For a subset
U
of vertices of
G
,
N_{G}(U)=\cup_{u\in U}N_{G}(u)
denotes the set of all neighbours of
U
. The induced subgraph of
G
on
U
, denoted by
G_{U}
, is a graph whose vertex set is
U
and for any
u,v\in U
,
\{u,v\}
is an edge of
G_{U}
if and only if it is an edge of
G
. We refer to
[
Di
]
for more information on graph theory.

The edge ideal of
G
is defined to be

I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}\mid\{i,j\}\in E(G))\subseteq S.

Let
\mathbf{w}:E(G)\rightarrow\ZZ_{>0}
be a weight function on edges of
G
. Paulsen and Sather-Wagstaff
[
PS
]
introduced and studied the edge ideals of edge-weighted graphs of
G

I(G_{\mathbf{w}})=\big((x_{i}x_{j})^{w(i,j)}\mid\{i,j\}\in E(G)\big)\subseteq S.

When
I(G_{\mathbf{w}})
is Cohen-Macaulay, we call
(G,\mathbf{w})
a Cohen-Macaulay edge-weighted graph. We have the following properties of associated radicals of edge ideals of edge-weighted graphs.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
G_{\mathbf{w}}
be a graph with a weight function
\mathbf{w}:E(G)\to\NN
. For any
x^{\mathbf{a}}
, let
U=\{i\mid\text{there exists }j\text{ such that }\{i,j\}\in E(G)\text{ and }a_{i}<w(i,j)\leq a_{j}\}
. Then

\sqrt{I(G_{\mathbf{w}}):x^{\mathbf{a}}}=I(G\backslash U)+(x_{i}\mid i\in U),

where
I(G\backslash U)
is the edge ideal of the induced subgraph of
G
on
[n]\backslash U
.

Proof.

Let
J=\sqrt{I(G_{\mathbf{w}}):x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. By
[
MNPTV
, Lemma 2.24]
, generators of
J
are
x_{i}x_{j}
with
x_{i}x_{j}\in I
and
x_{i}
for some variables
i
. Now
x_{i}\in J
if and only if there exists an index
j
such that

x_{i}=\sqrt{(x_{i}x_{j})^{w(i,j)}/\gcd((x_{i}x_{j})^{w(i,j)},x^{\mathbf{a}})}

In particular, we must have
a_{i}<w(i,j)\leq a_{j}
. The conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
G_{\mathbf{w}}
be an edge-weighted graph. Assume that
J=\sqrt{I(G_{\mathbf{w}}):x^{\mathbf{a}}}
is an associated radical of
I(G_{\mathbf{w}})
and
K
is an associated radical of
J
. Then
K
is also an associated radical of
I(G_{\mathbf{w}})
.

Proof.

Since
J
is radical, we may assume that
K=J:x^{\mathbf{b}}
. Let
\mathbf{c}
be an exponent such that

c_{i}=\begin{cases}\max\{w(i,j)\mid j\in N(i)\}&\text{ if }i\in\supp\mathbf{b}\\
a_{i}&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We first claim that
x^{\mathbf{c}}\notin I(G_{\mathbf{w}})
. By definition of
\mathbf{c}
, we need to prove that
x_{i}^{a_{i}}x_{j}^{c_{j}}\notin I(Y)
for all
i\in\supp\mathbf{a}
and all
j\in\supp\mathbf{b}
. Indeed, since
j\in\supp\mathbf{b}
,
x_{j}\notin J=\sqrt{I(G_{\mathbf{w}}):x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. Hence,
a_{i}<w(i,j)
for all
j\in N(i)
. Thus,
x_{i}^{a_{i}}x_{j}^{c_{j}}\notin I(G_{\mathbf{w}})
.

Let

U=\{i\mid\text{ there exists }j\text{ such that }a_{i}<w(i,j)\leq a_{j}\}.

By Lemma
2.13
,
J=I(G_{\mathbf{w}})+(x_{i}\mid i\in U)
. Thus,

\sqrt{I(G_{\mathbf{w}}):x^{\mathbf{c}}}=I(G_{\mathbf{w}})+(x_{i}\mid i\in U\cup N(\supp\mathbf{b}))=J:x^{\mathbf{b}}.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
G_{\mathbf{w}}
be an edge-weighted graph. Assume that
\sqrt{I(G_{\mathbf{w}}):x^{\mathbf{a}}}=I(G_{\mathbf{w}})+(x_{i}\mid i\in U)
for some
U\subseteq[n]
. Let
I(G_{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime})=I(G_{\mathbf{w}})+(x_{i}\mid i\in U)
. Then an associated radical of
I(G_{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime})
is also an associated radical of
I(G_{\mathbf{w}})
. In particular,
\depth S/I(G_{\mathbf{w}})\leq\depth S/I(G_{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
K=\sqrt{I(G_{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime}):x^{\mathbf{b}}}
be an associated radical of
I(G_{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime})
. Let
\mathbf{c}=\max(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
, i.e.,
c_{i}=\max(a_{i},b_{i})
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. We first prove that
x^{\mathbf{c}}\notin I(G_{\mathbf{w}})
. By definition, it suffices to prove that
x_{i}^{a_{i}}x_{j}^{b_{j}}\notin I
for all
i\in\supp\mathbf{a}
and
j\in\supp\mathbf{b}
such that
a_{i}\geq b_{i}
and
a_{j}\leq b_{j}
. Since
x_{j}^{b_{j}}\notin I(G_{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime})
,
a_{i}<w(i,j)
. Hence,
x^{a_{i}}x_{j}^{b_{j}}\notin I(Y)
.

Let
V=\{i\mid\text{ there exists }j\text{ such that }w(i,j)\leq b_{j}\}
. By Lemma
2.13
,

K=I(G_{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime})+(x_{i}\mid i\in V)=I(G_{\mathbf{w}})+(x_{i}\mid i\in U\cup V)=\sqrt{I(G_{\mathbf{w}}):x^{\mathbf{c}}}.

The last statement follows from the first statement and Theorem
2.2
.
∎

2.4.
Ferrers ideals and their depth and regularity

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
be a partition with

\mu

Corso and Nagel
[

Proposition 2.16

[

(1)

\height(I_{\lambda})=\min\{\min_{j}\{\lambda_{j}+j-1\},n\}

(2)

\pd(S/I_{\lambda})=\max_{j}\{\lambda_{j}+j-1\}

(3)

\reg(S/I_{\lambda})=1
.

Proposition 2.17
.

[
CN
, Corollary 2.8]

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

I_{\lambda}
is a Cohen-Macaulay ideal.

(2)

n=m
and
\lambda=(n,n-1,\ldots,2,1)
;

The depth and regularity of powers of

are also well-known.

Proposition 2.18

For all

Proof.

It is easy to see that

. Hence, by Theorem
2.2

\depth S/I_{\lambda}^{2}\leq\depth S/I_{\lambda}^{2}:(x_{1}y_{1})=1

[
T
]
. ∎

Proof.

The Ferrers ideals is the edge ideal of a cochordal graph. The conclusion follows from

Lemma 2.20

Let
\lambda

of

and

\displaystyle J

In particular,

and

are the sets of free variables of

. By Lemma
2.5
,
\depth S/J=|U|+|V|+1
. The conclusion follows from Proposition
2.16
.
∎

3.
Depth of tableau ideals

In this section, we compute the depth of tableau ideals. We fix the following notation throughout the section:

(1)

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
is a partition with
\lambda_{1}=m
.

(2)

\alpha=\alpha(\lambda)
is the smallest index
i
such that
\lambda_{i}+i=\max(\lambda_{j}+j\mid j=1,\ldots,n).

(3)

Y
is a filling of
\lambda
with the value
w_{ij}
on the
ij
-th box of
\lambda
.

(4)

\omega=\min(w(i,j)\mid 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i})
is the minimum weight.

Definition 3.1
.

A box
(\gamma,\delta)
is called a
minimal box
of
Y
if
w(\gamma,\delta)=\omega
and
w(i,j)>\omega
for all
(i,j)\neq(\gamma,\delta)
such that
i\leq\gamma
and
j\leq\delta
.

We then fix
(\gamma,\delta)
a minimal box of
Y
. We first see the change in the depth of Ferrers ideals after adding a variable.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
J=I_{\lambda}+(x_{a})
. Then

\depth S/J=\begin{cases}\depth S/I_{\lambda}-1&\text{ if }a\geq\alpha+1,\\
\depth S/I_{\lambda}&\text{ if }a\leq\alpha-1,\\
\depth S/I_{\lambda}+\epsilon&\text{ if }a=\alpha,\end{cases}

where
\epsilon
is a non-negative integer.

Proof.

Denote by
\mu
the partition obtained by deleting the
a
th row of
\lambda
, i.e.,
\mu=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{a-1},\lambda_{a+1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
. Then
J
is isomorphic to
I_{\mu}+(x_{a})
. Let
R
be the polynomial subring on
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{a-1},x_{a+1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}
. By Proposition
2.16
,

\displaystyle\pd(R/I_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\max_{i}\{\mu_{i}+i-1\}

\displaystyle=\max(\max\{\lambda_{i}+i-1\mid i\leq a-1\},\max\{\lambda_{i}+i-2\mid i\geq a+1\}).

We next distinguish some cases:

Case 1.

a\geq\alpha+1
. In this case, we have
\pd(R/I_{\mu})=\pd(S/I_{\lambda})
. By the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula, we deduce that
\depth S/J=\depth S/I-1
.

Case 2.

a\leq\alpha-1
. In this case, we have
\pd(R/I_{\mu})=\pd(S/I_{\lambda})-1
. In particular, we have
\depth S/J=\depth S/I
.

Case 3.

a=\alpha
. Since
\alpha
is the smallest index such that
\lambda_{i}+i=\max(\lambda_{j}+j)
, we have
\lambda_{i}+i<\lambda_{\alpha}+\alpha
for all
i<\alpha
. In particular, we deduce that
\pd(R/I_{\mu})\leq\pd(S/I_{\lambda})-1
. Hence,
\depth S/J=\depth S/I+\epsilon
for a non-negative integer
\epsilon
.
∎

We now have the following two key lemmas to compute the depth of tableau ideals.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
Y
is a filling of a Young diagram
\lambda
. Let
(\gamma,\delta)
be a minimal box of
Y
. Let
Y^{\prime}
be a filling of
\lambda
obtained by replacing the value
\omega
on the
(\gamma,\delta)
box by
1
. Then

\depth S/I(Y)\geq\depth S/I(Y^{\prime}).

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
, there exists an exponent
x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}
such that
\depth S/I(Y)=\depth S/\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}
. Since
x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}\notin I(Y)
, we must have either
a_{\gamma}
or
b_{\delta}<\omega
. We may assume that
a_{\gamma}<\omega
. Let
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
be an exponent such that

a^{\prime}_{i}=\begin{cases}a_{i}&\text{ if }i\neq\gamma\\
0&\text{ if }i=\gamma.\end{cases}

There are two cases:

Case 1.

b_{\delta}<\omega
. Let
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
be an exponent such that

b^{\prime}_{j}=\begin{cases}b_{j}&\text{ if }j\neq\delta\\
0&\text{ if }j=\delta.\end{cases}

By Lemma
2.13
,

\sqrt{I(Y^{\prime}):x^{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}y^{\mathbf{b}^{\prime}}}=\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}.

Case 2.

b_{\delta}\geq\omega
. By Lemma
2.13
,

\sqrt{I(Y^{\prime}):x^{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}.

In either cases, from Theorem
2.2
we deduce that
\depth S/I(Y)\geq\depth S/I(Y^{\prime})
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(\gamma,\delta)
be a minimal box of
Y
. Then

\depth S/I(Y)\leq\depth S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.22
, there exists an exponent
x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}
such that

J=\sqrt{I(Y)+(x_{\gamma}):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=(x_{j}\mid j\in U_{1})(y_{k}\mid k\in V_{1})+(x_{\gamma},x_{j},y_{k}\mid j\in U_{2},k\in V_{2})

for some subsets
U_{1},U_{2}\subseteq[n]
and
V_{1},V_{2}\subseteq[m]
and
\depth S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma}))=\depth S/J.
Since
x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}\notin(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma}))
,
a_{\gamma}=0
. For ease of reading, we divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1.
Reduction to the case
x_{\gamma}\notin\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}
. Assume that
x_{\gamma}\in\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}
. By Lemma
2.13
,

\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=J
. By Theorem
2.2
,
\depth S/I(Y)\leq\depth S/J=\depth S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma}))
. Thus, we may assume that
x_{\gamma}\notin\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}
.

Step 2.
Reduction to the case
b_{j}=0
for all
j=1,\ldots,m
. If
U_{2}=\emptyset
then we may replace
\mathbf{b}
by
\bf{0}
without changing the radical ideal and obtain the reduction step. Thus, we assume that
b_{j}\neq 0
for some
j
and
U_{2}
is non-empty. By Step 1,
\gamma\notin U_{2}
. Let
I(Z)=I(Y)+(x_{i}\mid i\in U_{2})
. By Lemma
2.13
,
J=\sqrt{(I(Z)+(x_{\gamma})):x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. By Theorem
2.2
,
\depth S/(I(Z)+(x_{\gamma}))\leq\depth S/J=\depth S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma}))
. By Lemma
2.15
,
\depth S/(I(Z)+(x_{\gamma}))\geq\depth S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma}))
. By induction, we deduce that

\depth S/I(Z)\leq\depth S/I(Z)+(x_{\gamma}))=\depth S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})).

By Lemma
2.15
,
\depth S/I(Y)\leq\depth S/I(Z)
. Thus, we may assume that
b_{j}=0
for all
j
. In particular, we have

(3.1)

J=\sqrt{(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})):x^{\mathbf{a}}}=(x_{j}\mid j\in U_{1})(y_{k}\mid k\in V_{1})+(x_{\gamma},y_{k}\mid k\in V_{2}).

Step 3.
Reduction to the case
a_{i}=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. Similar to Step 2, assume that
a_{i}\neq 0
for some
i
and
V_{2}
is non-empty. Let
I(Z)=I(Y)+(y_{k}\mid k\in V_{2})
. By definition, we have
J=\sqrt{I(Z)+(x_{\gamma})}
. By Theorem
2.2
and Lemma
2.15
,
\depth S/(I(Z)+(x_{\gamma}))=\depth S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})
. By induction and Lemma
2.15
, we deduce that

\depth S/I(Y)\leq\depth S/I(Z)\leq\depth S/(I(Z)+(x_{\gamma}))=\depth S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})).

Thus, we may assume that
a_{i}=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. In particular, we have

(3.2)

J=\sqrt{I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})}=I_{\lambda}+(x_{\gamma})=(x_{j}\mid j\in U_{1})(y_{k}\mid k\in V_{1})+(x_{\gamma}).

Step 4.
Reduction to the case
x_{\gamma}
is the last variable, i.e.,
\gamma=n
. By Eq. (
3.2
), we have
U_{1}\cup\{\gamma\}=[n]
. Let
V_{2}=[m]\setminus V_{1}
. By Lemma
2.5
,
\depth S/J=|V_{2}|+1
. Assume that
\gamma<n
. If
\gamma>1
then from Eq. (
3.2
), we deduce that
\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{n}
and
V_{2}=\emptyset
. In this case, we have
\depth S/I(Y)\leq\depth S/I_{\lambda}=1=\depth S/J
. Now, assume that

3.2

\depth S/I_{\lambda}\leq\depth S/(I_{\lambda}+(x_{1}))=\depth S/J.

The conclusion follows from Theorem
2.2
. Thus, we may assume that

Step 5.
Conclusion step. By Step 3 and Step 4,
\gamma=n
and

. Since

for all

\sqrt{I(Y):y_{\delta}^{\omega}}=I_{\lambda}+(x_{\gamma})=J.

The conclusion follows from Theorem
2.2
.

We are now ready for the main result of the section.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
Y
be an arbitrary filling of a Young diagram. Let
(\gamma,\delta)
be a minimal box of
Y
. Then

\depth S/I(Y)=\min\{\depth S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})),\depth S/(I(Y)+(y_{\delta}))\}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
, we may assume that
\omega=1
and we need to prove that

\depth S/I(Y)\geq\min\{\depth S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})),\depth S/(I(Y)+(y_{\delta}))\}.

By Lemma
2.3
, it suffices to prove that
\depth S/I(Y):x_{\gamma}\geq\depth S/(I(Y)+(y_{\delta}))
. By Theorem
2.2
, there exists an exponent
x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}
such that
\depth S/I(Y):x_{\gamma}=\depth S/\sqrt{I(Y):x_{\gamma}x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}
. Since
x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}\notin I(Y):x_{\gamma}
,
b_{\delta}=0
. In other words,
x_{\gamma}x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}\notin I(Y)+(y_{\delta})
. By Lemma
2.13
,

\sqrt{(I(Y)+(y_{\delta})):x_{\gamma}x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=\sqrt{I(Y):x_{\gamma}x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}.

The conclusion follows from Theorem
2.2
.
∎

As a corollary, we have

.

Assume that

By Theorem

, we also have

\depth S/I_{\lambda}=\min\{\depth(S/I_{\lambda}+(x_{1})),\depth S/(I_{\lambda}+(y_{1}))\}.

The conclusion follows from induction and Proposition
2.16
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
Y
be an arbitrary filling of a Young diagram

In this section, we compute the regularity of tableau ideals. First, we have the following remark about the homology of the degree complexes of tableau ideals.

Lemma 4.1

Let
Y
be an arbitrary filling of a Young diagram
\lambda
. Let
(x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}},i)
be a critical pair of

. Then
i=0
or
i=1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.13
,
J=\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=I_{\lambda}+(x_{i}\mid i\in U)+(y_{j}\mid j\in V)
for some subsets
U
and
V
of
[n]
and
[m]
respectively. The conclusion follows from Lemma
2.6
and the fact that
\reg(S/J)\leq 1
.
∎

We now have a simple formula when
\lambda
has only one row (or one column).

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
\lambda=(\lambda_{1})
has only one row. Then

\reg S/I(Y)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(w(1,j)-1)+\max\{w(1,j)\mid j=1,\ldots,m\}.

Proof.

Let
(x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}},i)
be an extremal pair of
I(Y)
. By Remark
2.8
, we must have
b_{j}\leq w(1,j)-1
and
a_{1}\leq\max\{w(1,j)\mid j=1,\ldots,m\}-1

, we deduce that

. Hence,

\reg S/I(Y)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}(w(1,j)-1)+\max\{w(1,j)\mid j=1,\ldots,m\}.

Now, we choose
a_{1}=\max\{w(1,j)\mid j=1,\ldots,m\}-1
and
b_{j}=w(1,j)-1
. Then

\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=I_{\lambda}+(y_{j}\mid w(1,j)\leq a_{1}).

In particular,
\widetilde{H}_{0}(\Delta_{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})}(I(Y));\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. The conclusion follows from Lemma
2.6
.
∎

We may now assume that
Y
has at least two rows and two columns. As in the previous section, we fix the following notation throughout the section:

(1)

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
is a partition with
\lambda_{1}=m
.

(2)

Y
is a filling of
\lambda
with the value
w_{ij}
on the

-th box of
\lambda
.

(3)

\omega=\min(w(i,j)\mid 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i})
is the minimum weight.

(4)

(\gamma,\delta)
is a minimal box of
Y
.

We now give a structure result about Stanley-Reisner complexes of bipartite graphs that will be useful later.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Delta=\Delta(I(G))
be the simplicial complex of the edge ideal of a non-empty bipartite graph

. Assume that
\widetilde{H}_{0}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. Then
G
is a complete bipartite graph. In other words,
\Delta=\langle X,Y\rangle
with
X,Y
are the partitions of
V(G)
.

Proof.

Since
\widetilde{H}_{0}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
,
\depth S/I(G)\leq 1
. Since
G
is bipartite, we deduce that
\depth S/I(G)=1
. The conclusion follows from
[
T
, Lemma 3.1]
.
∎

We also have the following two key lemmas to compute the regularity of
I(Y)
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that
Y
has more than one box. Let
(\gamma,\delta)
be a minimal box of
Y
. Let
Y^{\prime}
be a filling obtained by replacing the value
\omega
on the
(\gamma,\delta)
box by
1
. Then

\reg S/I(Y)\leq\reg S/I(Y^{\prime})+(\omega-1).

Proof.

If
\omega=1
the conclusion is vacuous. Thus, we may assume that
\omega>1
. Let
(x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}},i)
be an extremal pair of
S/I(Y)
, i.e., we have
\reg S/I(Y)=|\mathbf{a}|+|\mathbf{b}|+i
and there exists a face
F
of
\Delta=\Delta_{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})}(I(Y))
such that
\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\lk_{\Delta}F;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. For ease of reading we divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1.
Reduction to the case
a_{\gamma}<\omega
and
b_{\delta}<\omega
. Indeed, assume that
a_{\gamma}\geq\omega
. Then
b_{\delta}<\omega
. Let
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
be an exponent such that

(4.1)

b^{\prime}_{j}=\begin{cases}b_{j}&\text{ if }j\neq\delta,\\
0&\text{ if }j=\delta.\end{cases}

By Lemma
2.13
,

\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=\sqrt{I(Y^{\prime}):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}^{\prime}}}.

By Lemma
2.6
,
(x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}^{\prime}},i)
is a critical exponent of
I(Y^{\prime})
. Hence,

\reg S/I(Y^{\prime})\geq|\mathbf{a}|+|\mathbf{b}^{\prime}|+i\geq\reg S/I(Y)-(\omega-1).

Thus, we may assume that
a_{\gamma}<\omega
and
b_{\delta}<\omega
.

Step 2.
Reduction to the case
a_{\gamma}>0
and
b_{\delta}>0
. Assume that
a_{\gamma}=0
, we set
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
as in Eq. (
4.1
). Then,

\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=\sqrt{I(Y^{\prime}):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}^{\prime}}},

and we deduce the required inequality by Lemma
2.6
. Thus, we may assume that
0<a_{\gamma}<\omega
and
0<b_{\delta}<\omega
.

Step 3.
Reduction to the case
x_{\gamma},y_{\delta}\notin\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}
. Let
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
be an exponent such that

(4.2)

a^{\prime}_{j}=\begin{cases}a_{j}&\text{ if }j\neq\gamma,\\
0&\text{ if }j=\gamma.\end{cases}

If
x_{\gamma}\in\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}
then

\sqrt{I(Y^{\prime}):x^{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}.

In other words
(x^{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}y^{\mathbf{b}},i)
is a critical pair of
I(Y^{\prime})
. By Step 1,
|\mathbf{a}^{\prime}|\geq|\mathbf{a}|-(\omega-1)
. The conclusion follows from Lemma
2.6
. Thus, we obtain the reduction step.

Step 4.
Reduction to the case
F=\emptyset
. By Step 2,
a_{\gamma},b_{\delta}>0
. Thus,
x_{\gamma},y_{\delta}\notin\supp F
. Assume that
F\neq\emptyset
, say
z\in F
. By Lemma
2.9
,
\reg S/I(Y)=\reg S/(I(Y)+(z))
. By induction, we have

\reg S/(I(Y)+(z))\leq\reg S/(I(Y^{\prime})+(z))+(\omega-1).

By Lemma
2.4
, we deduce that

\reg S/I(Y)\leq\reg S/I(Y^{\prime})+(\omega-1),

as required. Thus, we may assume that
F=\emptyset.

Step 5.
Reduction to the case
i=1
. Let

\Delta=\Delta_{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})}(I(Y)),\Gamma=\Delta_{(\mathbf{a}^{\prime},\mathbf{b})}(I(Y^{\prime})),\text{ and }\Sigma=\Delta_{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}^{\prime})}(I(Y^{\prime})).

We have
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
are the restriction of
\Delta
to
X\cup Y\setminus\{x_{\gamma}\}
and
X\cup Y\setminus\{y_{\delta}\}
respectively. By Lemma
4.1
,
i=0
or
i=1
. Assume that
i=0
. From the exact sequence

\cdots\to\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\lk_{\Delta}x_{\gamma};\mathrm{k})\to\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\Gamma;\mathrm{k})\to\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})\to 0

we deduce that
\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\Gamma;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. Thus,
(x^{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}y^{\mathbf{b}},i)
is a critical pair of
I(Y^{\prime})
. By Lemma
2.6
, the conclusion follows. Thus, we may assume that
i=1
.

Step 6.
Reduction to the case
\Delta=\langle\{x_{\gamma}\},\{y_{\delta}\}\rangle
. By Step 5, we assume that
i=1
, i.e.,
\widetilde{H}_{0}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. Consider the long exact sequence

\cdots\to\widetilde{H}_{0}(\Gamma;\mathrm{k})\to\widetilde{H}_{0}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})\to\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\lk_{\Delta}x_{\gamma};\mathrm{k})\to\cdots\to 0.

If
\widetilde{H}_{0}(\Gamma;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
, the conclusion also follows from Lemma
2.6
. Thus, we may assume that
\widetilde{H}_{0}(\Gamma;\mathrm{k})=0
. Hence,
\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\lk_{\Delta}x_{\gamma};\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. Similarly, we may assume that
\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\lk_{\Delta}y_{\delta};\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. Thus, we have
\Delta=\{x_{\gamma}\}\cup\{y_{\delta}\}\cup\Delta^{\prime}
where
\Delta^{\prime}
is a simplicial complex on
X\cup Y\setminus\{x_{\gamma},y_{\delta}\}
. If
x_{j}\in\Delta^{\prime}
then
x_{\gamma}x_{j}\in I_{\Delta}
, which is a contradiction. Thus, we must have
\Delta^{\prime}
is the empty simplicial complex. In other words,
\Delta=\langle\{x_{\gamma}\},\{y_{\delta}\}\rangle
and
I_{\Delta}=\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=(x_{\gamma}y_{\delta})+(x_{j}\mid j\neq\gamma)+(y_{\ell}\mid\ell\neq\delta)
.

Step 7.
Conclusion step. Let
U=\{j\mid j\neq\gamma\text{ and }a_{j}\neq 0\}
and
V=\{j\mid j\neq\delta\text{ and }b_{j}\neq 0\}
. Let
D
be a directed bipartite graph on vertices
U\cup V
whose edges are
(j,\ell)
if
a_{j}<w(j,\ell)\leq b_{\ell}
and
(\ell,k)
if
b_{\ell}<w(k,\ell)\leq a_{k}
. If
a_{j}=0
and
b_{\ell}=0
for all
j\neq\gamma
and all
\ell\neq\delta
then
\reg S/I(Y)=2\omega-1
. Since
Y
has at least two boxes, by degree reason, we also have
\reg S/I(Y^{\prime})\geq 2\omega-1
. Thus, the conclusion is clear. Hence, we may assume that
|U|+|V|\geq 1
. By Step 6, for any
j\in U
,
x_{j}\in\sqrt{x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}
. Thus, there must exist
\ell\in V
such that
a_{j}<w(j,\ell)\leq b_{\ell}
. In other words, in
D
, there is an edge going out from
j
. Similarly, if
\ell\in V
, there must exist an edge going out from
\ell
. Thus
U
and
V
are non-empty sets and
D
has a directed cycle (the directed cycle might have length
2
). Assume that
u_{1},v_{1},u_{2},v_{2},\ldots,u_{s},v_{s}
is a directed cycle of
D
where
u_{j}\in U
and
v_{j}\in V
. By definition,
a_{u_{j}}<w(u_{j},v_{j})\leq b_{v_{j}}
and
b_{v_{j}}<w(u_{j+1},v_{j})\leq a_{u_{j+1}}
for any
j=1,\ldots,s
where
u_{s+1}=u_{1}
. This is a contradiction. That concludes the proof of the Lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
Y
be a filling of a Young diagram with at least two boxes. Let
(\gamma,\delta)
be a minimal box of
Y
with
w(\gamma,\delta)=\omega
. We have

\reg S/I(Y)\geq(\omega-1)+\reg(S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})).

Proof.

If
\omega=1
the conclusion follows from Lemma
2.4
. Thus, we may assume that
\omega>1
. Let
(x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}},i)
be an extremal pair of
J=I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})
. In particular,
a_{\gamma}=0
. Let
\Gamma=\Delta_{(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})}(J)
and
F
is a face of
\Gamma
such that
F\cap(\supp\mathbf{a}\cup\supp\mathbf{b})=\emptyset
and
\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\lk_{\Gamma}F;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. For ease of reading, we divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1.
Reduction to the case
b_{j}\leq w(\gamma,j)-1
for all
j=1,\ldots,\lambda_{\gamma}
. Let
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
be an exponent such that
a^{\prime}_{\gamma}=\omega-1
and
a^{\prime}_{i}=a_{i}
otherwise. If
\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}

(x^{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}y^{\mathbf{b}},i)

I(Y)
. By Lemma
2.6
, the conclusion follows. By Lemma
2.13
, we may assume that

(4.3)

\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}+(x_{\gamma}).

.

Reduction to the case

Assume that

and
z
is an element of

2.9
,

. Since

\reg S/(I(Y),z)\geq(\omega-1)+\reg(S/(J,z)).

By Lemma
2.4
, we deduce that

\reg S/I(Y)\geq\reg S/(I(Y),z)\geq(\omega-1)+\reg S/J.

Thus, we may assume that
F=\emptyset
.

Step 3.
Reduction to the case
b_{j}>0
for all
j=1,\ldots,\lambda_{\gamma}
. Assume that
b_{j}=0
for some
j<\lambda_{\gamma}
. Let
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
be an exponent such that

(4.4)

b^{\prime}_{\ell}=\begin{cases}b_{\ell}&\text{ if }\ell\neq j,\\
\omega-1&\text{ if }\ell=j.\end{cases}

By Lemma
2.13
,

\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}^{\prime}}}.

By Step 2,

. Thus
(x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}^{\prime}},i)

b_{j}>0
for all
j=1,\ldots,\lambda_{\gamma}
.

Step 4.
Reduction to the case
w(\gamma,j)=\omega
for all
j=1,\ldots,\lambda_{\gamma}
. Let
Z
be the tableau obtained by replacing all weights on the
\gamma
th row of

by
\omega
. Then

. We will now claim that
\reg S/I(Y)\geq\reg S/I(Z)

. By Remark

, we have
a_{\gamma}\leq\omega-1

\sqrt{I(Z):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=\sqrt{I(Y):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}},

2.6
,
\reg S/I(Y)\geq\reg S/I(Z)
. Thus, we may replace

by

and obtain the reduction step.

Step 5.
Reduction to the case
i=1
. Assume that
i=0
, i.e.,
\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\Gamma;\mathrm{k})\neq 0
. Let
\Delta=\Delta_{(\mathbf{a}^{\prime},\mathbf{b})}(I(Y))
. Then
\Gamma
is the restriction of
\Delta
to
X\cup Y\setminus\{x_{\gamma}\}
. Consider the long exact sequence

\cdots\to\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\lk_{\Delta}x_{\gamma};\mathrm{k})\to\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\Gamma;\mathrm{k})\to\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})\to 0.

If

. Thus, we may assume that

\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\lk_{\Delta}x_{\gamma};\mathrm{k})\neq 0

Step 6.

\widetilde{H}_{0}(\Gamma;\mathrm{k})\neq 0

4.3

and

\widetilde{H}_{0}(\Delta;\mathrm{k})\neq 0

is a critical pair of

. Hence, the conclusion follows from Lemma
2.6

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
Y
be a filling of a Young diagram with at least two boxes. Let
(\gamma,\delta)
be a minimal box of
Y
with
w(\gamma,\delta)=\omega
. We have

\reg S/I(Y)=\omega-1+\max\{\reg S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})),\reg S/(I(Y)+(y_{\delta}))\}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
, it remains to prove that

\reg S/I(Y)\leq\max\{\reg S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})),\reg S/(I(Y)+(y_{\delta}))\}

when
\omega=w(\gamma,\delta)=1
.
By Lemma
2.3
, we may assume that

\reg S/I(Y)=\reg S/I(Y):x_{\gamma}+1>\reg S/(I(Y)+(x_{\gamma})).

Let
(x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}},i)
be an extremal pair of
I(Y):x^{\gamma}
. In particular,
b_{\delta}=0
. By Lemma
2.13
,

\sqrt{(I(Y):x_{\gamma}):x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}}}=\sqrt{(I(Y)+(y_{\delta})):(x_{\gamma}x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}})}.

Hence,
(x_{\gamma}x^{\mathbf{a}}y^{\mathbf{b}},i)
is a critical exponent of
I(Y)+(y_{\delta})
. By Lemma
2.6
,

\reg S/(I(Y)+(y_{\delta}))\geq|\mathbf{a}|+|\mathbf{b}|+1+i=\reg S/I(Y):x_{\gamma}+1.

The conclusion follows.
∎

We now establish Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

We prove by induction on the number of boxes of
Y
. If
Y
has only one box the conclusion is clear. Now assume that
Y
has at least two boxes and
(\gamma,\delta)
is a minimal box of
Y
. The shape of
Y
is
\lambda
and its conjugate partition is denoted by
\mu
. Let
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
be the tableaux obtained by deleting the
\gamma
th row and
\delta
th column of
Y
respectively. Also, we let
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
be polynomial rings on the variables corresponding to
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
. By induction, there exist admissible collections of marked boxes
P_{1},Q_{1}
and
P_{2},Q_{2}
of
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
respectively such that
\depth R_{i}/I(Y_{i})=d(P_{i},Y_{i})
and
\reg R_{i}/I(Y_{i})=r(Q_{i},Y_{i})
. If

then

. By Lemma

and definition, we see that

\reg S/(I(Y)+(y_{\delta}))=\reg R_{2}/I(Y_{2})

3.5
, Theorem
4.6

.

Nonlocal Sublinear Elliptic Problems Involving Measures
Aye Chan May, Adisak Seesanea

Abstract
. We study Dirichlet problems for fractional Laplace equations of the form ( − Δ ) α 2 ​ u = f ⁡ ( x , u ) (-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u=f(x,u) in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} for 0 < α < n 0<\alpha<n where the nonlinearity f ⁡ ( x , u ) = ∑ i = 1 M σ i ​ u q i + ω f(x,u)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\sigma_{i}u^{q_{i}}+\omega involves sublinear terms with 0 < q i < 1 0<q_{i}<1 and the coefficients σ i , ω \sigma_{i},\omega are nonnegative locally finite Borel measures on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . We develop a potential theoretic approach for the existence of positive minimal solutions in Lorentz spaces to the problems under certain assumptions on σ i \sigma_{i} and ω \omega . The uniqueness properties of such solutions are discussed. Our techniques are also applicable to similar sublinear problems on uniform bounded domains when 0 < α < 2 0<\alpha<2 , or on arbitrary domains with positive Green’s functions in the classical case α = 2 \alpha=2 .

1.
Introduction

We consider Dirichlet problems of the type

(1.1)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u=f(x,u),\;\;\;u\geq 0,\;\;\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\liminf\limits_{x\to\infty}u(x)=0,\end{cases}

where
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
is the fractional Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
0<\alpha<n
and
n\geq 2
. The nonlinearity

f(x,u)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\sigma_{i}u^{q_{i}}+\omega
is driven by several sublinear terms where
0<q_{i}<1
and the coefficients
\sigma_{i},\omega
are nonnegative locally finite Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This class of measures will be denoted by
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

A solution
u\geq 0
to (
1.1
) will be understood
as a measurable function of the class
L^{q_{i}}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\sigma_{i})
for all
i=1,2,\dots,M
(so that each
u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) which satisfies the corresponding integral equation

(1.2)

u=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\omega\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Here
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\sigma
stands for the Riesz potential of order
\alpha\in(0,n)
of a measure
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is defined by

\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\sigma(x)=(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\sigma(x)=(n-\alpha)c(n,\alpha)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma(B(x,r))}{r^{n-\alpha}}\frac{dr}{r},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

where
B(x,r)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-y|<r\}
is the ball centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of radius
r>0
.
Throughout, the normalization constant will be omitted for ease of notation.

The main purpose of this paper is to investigate the existence and uniqueness problems for positive solutions in Lorentz spaces
L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
0<r,\rho<\infty
, to Dirichlet problems involving several sublinear terms of the type (
1.1
).

1.1
) with a single sublinear term (
M=1
), involving elliptic operators, including

\mathcal{A}
-Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

by employing the norm and pointwise estimates of the nonlinear Wolff potentials. To the extent of the authors’ knowledge, their

We also note that two-sided pointwise estimates of a general positive solution

(1.3)

c^{-1}\big[\big(\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\sigma_{1}\big)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}+\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\sigma_{1}\big]+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\omega\leq u\leq c\big[\big(\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\sigma_{1}\big)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}+\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\sigma_{1}\big]+\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\omega

where
c=c(\alpha,q)
is a positive constant.

The fractional
intrinsic
potential
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\sigma

of a measure
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, introduced in
[

, is defined for

0<\alpha<n
and
0<q<1
by

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\sigma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varkappa(B(x,r))^{\frac{q}{1-q}}}{r^{n-\alpha}}\frac{dr}{r},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

where
\varkappa(B(x,r))
is the least positive constant in the localized weighted norm inequality,
for
\sigma_{B}=\sigma|_{B(x,r)}

\|\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\nu\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\,d\sigma|_{B(x,r)})}\leq\varkappa(B(x,r))\,\nu(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\qquad\forall\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

In light of (
1.3

1.1

M=1

\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\sigma_{1}\in L^{\frac{r}{1-q},\frac{\rho}{1-q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\sigma_{1}\in L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\omega\in L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Nevertheless, it is not quite easy to verify the second condition due to the way the intrinsic potential

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\sigma_{1}
is defined.

(1.4)

\sigma_{i}\in L^{s_{i},t_{i}}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\;\;\ i=1,\dots,m\qquad\text{and}\qquad\omega\in L^{\tilde{s},\tilde{t}}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

Our main results can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{M},\omega\in\mathcal{M^{+}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
(\sigma_{1}\dots,\sigma_{M},\omega)\neq(0,\dots 0,0)
. Let
\{q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}\subset(0,1)
and
0<\alpha<n
with
n\geq 2
. Suppose

(1.5)

\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\sigma_{i}\in L^{\frac{\gamma+q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\sigma_{i}),\qquad i=1,2,\dots,M

and

(1.6)

\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\omega\in L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\omega)

for some
0<\gamma<\infty.
Then there exists a positive solution
u\in L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to (
1.1
) where
r=\frac{n(\gamma+1)}{n-\alpha}
and
\rho=\gamma+1
. Further,
u
is minimal in the sense that
u\leq v
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

for any supersolution
v
to (
1.1
).

In particular, (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) are valid with
\gamma=1

if and only if there exists (minimal) finite energy solution
u\in\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to (
1.1
).
In this case,
u
is the only solution in
\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
provided
0<\alpha\leq 2
.

Here the fractional homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined in terms
of Riesz potential for
0<\alpha<n
as

\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u:u=\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f,\;f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}

equipped with the norm
\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

A sufficient condition for (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) is given by (
1.4
)
with exponents:

(1.7)

\begin{split}s_{i}=\frac{n(\gamma+1)}{n(1-q_{i})+\alpha(\gamma+q_{i})},\quad&t_{i}=\frac{\gamma+1}{1-q_{i}},\quad\text{for}\;\;i=1,\dots,m,\\[6.00006pt]
\tilde{s}=\frac{n(\gamma+1)}{n+\alpha\gamma}\quad\;\text{and}\quad\;&\tilde{t}=\gamma+1.\end{split}

Thus, in view of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following consequence.

Corollary 1.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, if (
1.4
) with exponents (
1.7
) is fulfilled, then there exists a positive minimal solution
u\in L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to (
1.1
).

In our method of proof of Theorem
1.1
, we consider a general framework of corresponding integral equations of the form:

(1.8)

u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{G}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+\mathcal{G}\omega\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega

under some mild hypotheses on kernel
G
, including
quasi-symmetric
and
weak maximum principle
(see Section
2
). These properties are satisfied by kernels associated with elliptic operators, for instance, the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

0<\alpha<n
, and the classical Laplacian
-\Delta
, or more generally, the linear uniformly elliptic operators with bounded measurable coefficients
-\text{div}(\mathcal{A}\,\nabla\cdot)
in
\Omega
, see
[
2
]
,
[
15
]
.

Here
\mathcal{G}\sigma
stands for the potential of measure
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)

associated with positive lower semicontinuous
G
on
\Omega\times\Omega
, which is defined by

\mathcal{G}\sigma(x)=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)d\sigma(y),\quad x\in\Omega.

To outline the ideas of our approach, we first establish, in Theorem
3.2
, necessary and sufficient conditions:

(1.9)

\mathcal{G}\sigma_{i}\in L^{\frac{\gamma+q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i}),\quad i=1,2,\dots,M,

and

(1.10)

\mathcal{G}\omega\in L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i}),\quad i=1,2,\dots,M.

for the existence of a positive (minimal) solution

that belongs to

for all

to the integral equation (

). The construction of such a solution relies on the use of a characterization of (
1.9

2.9

and

(see Theorem

The main new phenomena in this step are possible interactions, not only between
\sigma_{i}
and

, but also between

Moreover, our techniques are adapted to establish a sufficient condition for the existence of a positive superharmonic solution

to the problem

(1.11)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\sigma_{i}u^{q_{i}}+\omega\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\

.

Let

2.
Preliminaries

Function spaces

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an arbitrary domain (possibly unbounded). We denote by
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
the set of all nonnegative locally finite Borel measures in
\Omega
and by
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
the set of all smooth compactly supported functions in
\Omega
.

For
\omega\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we denote by
L^{p}(\Omega,d\omega)
-space of all real-valued mesurable functions
u
on
\Omega
such that

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,d\omega)}=\Big(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}d\omega\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}<+\infty.

We write
L^{p}(\Omega,dx)
as
L^{p}(\Omega)
simply when
dx=d\omega
and denote Lebesgue measure.

Next we recall the definition of
Lorentz space
.
For
0<r,\rho\leq\infty
, we define

\|f\|_{L^{r,\rho}(\Omega)}:=\begin{cases}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(t^{\frac{1}{r}}f^{*}(t)\big)^{\rho}\,\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}&\text{if}\quad\rho<\infty;\\
\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{1}{r}}f^{*}(t)\ &\text{if}\quad\rho=\infty,\end{cases}

where
f^{*}
is the decreasing rearrangement of
f
which is defined by

f^{*}(t)=\inf\{\alpha>0:|\{x\in\Omega:|f(x)|>\alpha\}|\leq t\}.

The Lorentz space, denoted by
L^{r,\rho}(\Omega)
, is the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\Omega
such that
\|f\|_{L^{r,\rho}(\Omega)}<+\infty
.

Notice that Lebesgue spaces are Lorentz spaces, i.e.,
L^{r,r}=L^{r}
for
0<r\leq\infty
. Moreover, for
0<p<q\leq\infty
,
L^{r,p}
is a subspace of
L^{r,q}
.
More details of Lorentz spaces can be found in
[
14
]
.

Kernels and potentials theory

Let
G:\Omega\times\Omega\to(0,\infty]
be a positive lower semicontinuous kernel. Every kernels in this paper will be considered to be this type, even if not stated explicitly.

The potential
\mathcal{G}\nu
of a measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega)
is given by

\mathcal{G}\nu(x)=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)\,d\nu(y),\quad x\in\Omega.

The kernel
G
on
\Omega\times\Omega
is said to satisfy the
weak maximum principle
(WMP), with constant
h\geq 1
, if

\mathcal{G}\sigma(x)\leq 1,\quad\forall x\in\text{supp}(\sigma)\implies\mathcal{G}\sigma(x)\leq h,\quad\forall x\in\Omega,

for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\Omega).
Here we use the notation supp(
\sigma
) for the support of
\sigma.

When
h=1,
we say that the kernel
G
satisfies the
strong maximum principle
.

Remark 2.1
.

For every domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
which possesses a Green function, the strong maximum principle is fulfilled for the Green functions associated with the classical Laplacian
-\Delta
and for the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
in the case where
0<\alpha\leq 2.
Moreover, it is also satisfied for Green kernel of uniformly elliptic operators in divergence form
\mathcal{L}=-\text{div}(\mathcal{A}\nabla\cdot)
in place of
-\Delta.

Remark 2.2
.

The WMP (or boundedness principle) holds true for Riesz kernels on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
associated with
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
in the full range
0<\alpha<n,
and more generally for all radially nonincreasing kernels on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, see
[
1
]
.

A kernel
G:\Omega\times\Omega\to(0,+\infty]
is
quasi-symmetric
provided there exists a positive constant
a
such that

a^{-1}G(y,x)\leq G(x,y)\leq aG(y,x)\quad\forall x,y,\in\Omega.

There are numerous kernels connected to elliptic operators that are quasi-symmetric and satisfy the WMP, see
[
2
]
.

In order to proceed further in the investigation of solutions, we need the extra restriction on the kernel
G
studied here, including that
G
is bounded above by the Riesz kernel of order
\alpha
for some
0<\alpha<n

(2.1)

I_{\alpha}(x-y)=c|x-y|^{\alpha-n},\quad\forall x,y\in\Omega,

where
c
is a positive constant independent of
x
and
y.

Remark 2.3
.

It is well-known that
\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\Omega)}
is equivalent to the Gagliardo seminorm

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha}}\;dx\;dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

when
0<\alpha<2
, see
[
1
]
.

We will use the dual space of
\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in Theorem
1.1
. The dual space is denoted by
\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, consists of distributions
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\|\sigma\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\sup\frac{|\langle\sigma,u\rangle|}{\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}<+\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all nontrivial functions
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
0<\alpha<2
. Thus, by duality, for a measure
\omega\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have
\sigma\in\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\|\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<+\infty,

or equivalently, by
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\sigma\;d\sigma<+\infty.

We observe that
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}\sigma\in L^{\frac{1+q}{1-q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\sigma)

is equivalent to the trace inequality
[
11
]

(2.2)

\|\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}g\|_{L^{1+q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\sigma)}\leq c\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad\text{for all}\;\;g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

wher
c
is a positive constant independent of
g
.

The following theorem was established by Grigor’yan and Verbitsky
[
15
]
with more general form. It gives the pointwise estimates for supersolutions to sublinear elliptic equations.

Theorem 2.4

(
S
ee
[
15
]
)
.

Let
0<q<1
and let
\sigma\in\mathcal{M^{+}}(\Omega)
and let
G
be a positive lower semicontinuous kernel on
\Omega\times\Omega
that satisfies the WMP with constant

(2.4)

u(x)\geq(1-q)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}h^{-\frac{q}{1-q}}\big[\mathcal{G}\sigma(x)\big]^{\frac{1}{1-q}},\quad x\in\Omega.

Theorem 2.6

then there exists a positive (super)solution
u\in L^{\gamma+q}(\Omega,d\sigma)
to sublinear integral equation (
2.3
) if and only if the weighted norm inequality (
2.7
) is fulfilled with
r=\gamma+q
and
p=\frac{\gamma+q}{q},
i.e.,

(2.9)

\|\mathcal{G}(fd\sigma)\|_{L^{\gamma+q}(\Omega,d\sigma)}\leq c\|f\|_{L^{\frac{\gamma+q}{q}}(\Omega,d\sigma)},\quad f\in L^{\frac{\gamma+q}{q}}(\Omega,d\sigma),

or equivalently,

(2.10)

\mathcal{G}\sigma\in L^{\frac{\gamma+q}{1-q}}(\Omega,d\sigma).

Quinn and Verbitsky
[
22
]
described the existence of solutions
u\in L^{q}(\Omega,d\sigma),
and more generally
u\in L^{q}_{loc}(\Omega,d\sigma),
by
(1,q)
-weighted norm inequalities, which corresponds to the case where
\gamma=0
in (
2.10
).

In
[
20
]
, V.P. Havin and V.G. Maz’ya introduced the potential which is defined by

\mathcal{V}_{\alpha,p}f(x)=\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}|f|)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}(x)\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

for
p\in(1,\infty),\alpha\in(0,n)
and
f
is a locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
If
2\alpha<n
, then
\mathcal{V}_{\alpha,2}f=c\mathcal{I}_{2\alpha}|f|
for a suitable constant
c=c(\alpha,n)
.
It means these potentials extend the Riesz potentials.

Theorem 2.7

(
S
ee
[
13
]
)
.

Let
p>1
,
n\geq 2
,
0<\alpha<\frac{n}{p}
and
\Omega
be a measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
If
0<t\leq\infty
and
1<s<\frac{n}{\alpha p},
then there exists a constant
C=C(\alpha,p,n,s,t)
such that

(2.11)

\|\mathcal{V}_{\alpha,p}f\|_{L^{\frac{sn(p-1)}{n-s\alpha p},t(p-1)}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}_{L^{s,t}(\Omega)}

for every
f\in L^{s,t}(\Omega)
.

3.
Solutions in Lorentz Spaces

In this section, we start with an important link among coefficients
\sigma
and data
\omega
, which follows from conditions

(3.1)

\mathcal{G}\sigma\in L^{\frac{\gamma+q}{1-q}}(\Omega,d\sigma)

together with (
1.12
) yields an essential two-weight condition

(3.2)

\mathcal{G}\omega\in L^{\gamma+q}(\Omega,d\sigma).

The first lemma gives a sharp estimate for interaction between the coefficient
\sigma
and the data
\omega
appear in the sublinear problem of the form (
1.1
). This lemma was stated in
[
23
, Lemma 4.3]
. However, the proof given there is valid only for sublinear growth case
0<q<1.
Here, we give a simple proof in the extended to the range
-\gamma<q<1.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
0<\gamma<\infty,-\gamma<q<1,
and
\sigma,\omega\in\mathcal{M^{+}}(\Omega)
. Suppose

G
is a positive lower semicontinuous kernel on
\Omega\times\Omega
that satisfies WMP. Then

\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma+q}d\sigma\leq C\Big[\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma}d\omega\Big]^{\frac{\gamma+q}{\gamma+1}}\times\Big[\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{\gamma+q}{1-q}}d\sigma\Big]^{\frac{1-q}{\gamma+1}}

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
\gamma
and
q
.

Proof.

If (
3.1
) and (
1.12
) are infinite, the desired estimate is trivial.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
(\sigma,\omega)\not\equiv(0,0)
and (
3.1
) and (
1.12
) are finite. Consider the following two cases:

•

Case
\gamma+q>1
.

Applying the iterated inequality (
2.5
) with
a=\gamma+q
together with Fubini’s theorem and Hölder’s inequality with the exponents
\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+q-1}
and
\frac{\gamma}{1-q},
we obtain

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma+q}d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq c\int\mathcal{G}\big((\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma+q-1}d\omega\big)\;d\sigma

\displaystyle=c\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma+q-1}\mathcal{G}\sigma\;d\omega

\displaystyle\leq c\Big[\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma}d\omega\Big]^{\frac{\gamma+q-1}{\gamma}}\Big[\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{\gamma}{1-q}}\;d\omega\Big]^{\frac{1-q}{\gamma}}.

The second integral on the right-hand side of (
3.3
) is estimated by a similar argument as above. In fact, applying (
2.5
) again with
a=\frac{\gamma}{1-q},
along with Fubini’s theorem and Hölder inequality with the exponents
\frac{\gamma+q}{\gamma+q-1}
and
\gamma+q,
we deduce

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{\gamma}{1-q}}\;d\omega

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}\Big((\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{\gamma}{1-q}-1}d\sigma\Big)\;d\omega

\displaystyle=c\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{\gamma+q-1}{1-q}}\mathcal{G}\omega\;d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq c\Big[\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{\gamma+q}{1-q}}d\sigma\Big]^{\frac{\gamma+q-1}{\gamma+q}}\Big[\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma+q}d\sigma\Big]^{\frac{1}{\gamma+q}}.

By (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we have

\begin{split}\Big[\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma+q}d\sigma\Big]^{1-\frac{1-q}{\gamma(\gamma+q)}}&\leq c\Big[\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma}\;d\omega\Big]^{\frac{\gamma+q-1}{\gamma}}\\
&\times\Big[\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{\gamma+q}{1-q}}\;d\sigma\Big]^{\frac{(\gamma+q-1)(1-q)}{\gamma(\gamma+q)}},\end{split}

which is finite by (
3.1
) and (
1.12
), and hence (
3.2
) holds. Letting
C_{1}=1-\frac{1-q}{\gamma(\gamma+q)}.
Multiplying exponents of both side of previous estimate by
\frac{1}{C_{1}}
, we obtain the desired estimate.

•

Case 2:
\gamma+q\leq 1.
Write

\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma+q}d\sigma=\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma+q}F^{t-1}F^{1-t}d\sigma,

where
\frac{\gamma+q}{\gamma+1}<t<\gamma+q
and
F
is a positive
\sigma
-measurable function to be determined later. Applying Hölder’s inequality with the exponents
\frac{1}{t}
and
\frac{1}{1-t},
we get

(3.5)

\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma+q}d\sigma\leq\Big(\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\frac{\gamma+q}{t}}F^{\frac{t-1}{t}}d\sigma\Big)^{t}\Big(\int_{\Omega}Fd\sigma\Big)^{1-t}.

Setting
F=(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{\gamma+q}{1-q}}
in (
3.5
), we obtain

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma+q}d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\frac{\gamma+q}{t}}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\big(\frac{\gamma+q}{1-q}\big)\big(\frac{t-1}{t}\big)}d\sigma\Big)^{t}

\displaystyle\times\Big(\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{\gamma+q}{1-q}}d\sigma\Big)^{1-t}.

The first integral on the right-hand side of (
3.6
) is estimated by using iterated inequality (
2.5
) with
a=\frac{\gamma+q}{t},
followed by Fubini’s theorem, we get

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\frac{\gamma+q}{t}}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{(\gamma+q)(t-1)}{(1-q)t}}d\sigma&\leq c\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}\big((\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\frac{\gamma+q-t}{t}}d\omega\big)(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{(\gamma+q)(t-1)}{(1-q)t}}d\sigma\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\big(\mathcal{G}\omega\big)^{\frac{\gamma+q-t}{t}}\big(\mathcal{G}\big(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{(\gamma+q)(t-1)}{(1-q)t}}d\sigma\big)\big)d\omega.\end{split}

We proceed by further estimating the second integral on the right-hand side of last display applying (
2.6
) with
0<a=\frac{(\gamma+q)(t-1)}{(1-q)t}+1\leq 1
and Hölder’s inequality with the exponents
\frac{\gamma t}{\gamma+q-t}
and
\frac{\gamma t}{a(\gamma+1)-(\gamma+q)},
we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\frac{\gamma+q}{t}}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{(\gamma+q)(t-1)}{(1-q)t}}d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\Omega}\big(\mathcal{G}\omega\big)^{\frac{\gamma+q-t}{t}}\big(\mathcal{G}\sigma\big)^{\frac{t(\gamma+1)-(\gamma+q)}{(1-q)t}}d\omega

\displaystyle\leq c\Big(\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma}d\omega\Big)^{\frac{\gamma+q-t}{\gamma t}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{\gamma}{1-q}}d\omega\Big)^{\frac{t(\gamma+1)-(\gamma+q)}{\gamma t}}.

Similarly, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{\gamma}{1-q}}d\omega

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}\sigma(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma+q-1}d\omega\Big)^{\frac{\gamma}{1-q}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma}d\omega\Big)^{\frac{1-\gamma-q}{1-q}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}\big((\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma+q-1}d\omega\big)d\sigma\Big)^{\frac{\gamma}{1-q}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma}d\omega\Big)^{\frac{1-\gamma-q}{1-q}}

\displaystyle\leq c\Big(\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma+q}d\sigma\Big)^{\frac{\gamma}{1-q}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma}d\omega\Big)^{\frac{1-\gamma-q}{1-q}}.

Combining the preceding estimates and (
3.6
), we have

\Big(\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma+q}d\sigma\Big)^{1-\frac{t(\gamma+1)-(\gamma+q)}{1-q}}\leq c\Big[\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\omega)^{\gamma}d\omega\Big]^{\frac{(\gamma+q)(1-t)}{1-q}}\Big[\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\frac{\gamma+q}{1-q}}\Big]^{1-t}.

This proves (
3.2
) holds, since both integrals on the right-side are finite by (
3.1
) and (
1.12
). Letting
C_{2}=1-\frac{t(\gamma+1)-(\gamma+q)}{1-q}.
By multiplying exponents of both side of above equation by
\frac{1}{C_{2}},
we obtain the required estimate.
This completes the proof of lemma.
∎

The next theorem provides necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of a positive solution (minimal)
u\in L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i})
for each
i=1,2,\dots,M
to the corresponding integral equation in the sublinear case. A complete proof for general kernels is given below.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}\subseteq(0,1)
and
0<\gamma<\infty.
Let
(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{M},\omega)\in\mathcal{M^{+}}(\Omega)
such that
(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{M},\omega)\neq(0,\dots,0,0)
. Suppose
G
is a positive quasi-symmetric lower semicontinuous kernel on
\Omega\times\Omega
satisfying the WMP. If (
1.9
) together with (
1.10
) is valid, then there exists a positive solution
u\in L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i})
for all
i=1,2,\dots,M
to
(
1.8
).

Moreover,
u
is minimal in the sense that
u\leq v
in
\Omega
for any supersolution
v
to (
1.8
).

Conversely, without quasi-symmetric assumption on
G,
if there is a supersolution
u\in L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i})
to (
1.8
), then
(
1.9
) and (
1.10
) must be valid.

Proof.

Suppose that (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) are valid. For each
i=1,2,\dots,M,
we define

u_{i}^{(0)}=\kappa(\mathcal{G}\sigma_{i})^{\frac{1}{1-q_{i}}}

where
\kappa
is a positive constant to be determined later. Then
u_{i}^{(0)}
satisfies

u_{i}^{(0)}\leq\kappa^{1-q}c\;\mathcal{G}\big((u_{i}^{(0)})^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)

where
q=\min\limits_{i=1,2,\dots,M}q_{i}
and
c
is constant in (
2.5
). Then

(3.7)

u_{i}^{(0)}\leq\mathcal{G}\big((u_{i}^{(0)})^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)

provide the constant
\kappa
small enough so that
\kappa\leq\frac{\kappa^{q}}{c},
i.e.
\kappa\leq\big(\frac{1}{c}\big)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}
. Moreover, we can choose small constant
k=\min\{\big(\frac{1}{c}\big)^{\frac{1}{1-q}},\tilde{C}\}
where
\tilde{C}
is the constant in Theorem
2.4
, such that

u_{i}^{(0)}\leq w

whenever
w
is a nonnegative supersolution to (
1.1
).
Next we construct a nondecreasing sequence of positive functions
\{u_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=0}\subseteq L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i}),

\forall i=1,2,\dots,M
as follows:

u_{0}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{G}\big((u_{i}^{(0)})^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)+\mathcal{G}\omega

and

u_{j+1}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{G}\big(u_{j}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)+\mathcal{G}\omega\quad\text{for}\quad j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

It clearly see that
u_{0}
is positive function as well since the two measures
(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{M},\omega)\neq(0,\dots,0,0)
. Moreover, by (
3.7
),
u_{i}^{(0)}\leq u_{0}
. Consequently,

u_{1}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{G}\big(u_{0}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)+\mathcal{G}\omega\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{G}\big((u_{i}^{(0)})^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)+\mathcal{G}\omega=u_{0}.

Suppose
u_{0}\leq u_{1}\leq\dots\leq u_{j}
for some
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.
Then

u_{j+1}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{G}\big(u_{j}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)+\mathcal{G}\omega\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{G}\big(u_{j-1}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)+\mathcal{G}\omega=u_{j}.

Therefore, by induction,
\{u_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=0}
is defined as an increasing sequence of positive functions.

We now claim that each
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\subseteq L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i})\;\forall i=1,2,\dots,M.

For each
i,

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}\leq\sum\limits_{l=1}^{M}\|\mathcal{G}\big((u_{l}^{(0)})^{q_{l}}d\sigma_{l}\big)\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}+\|\mathcal{G}\omega\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}.

By assumption (
1.10
),

(3.8)

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}\leq\sum\limits_{l=1}^{M}\|\mathcal{G}\big((u_{l}^{(0)})^{q_{l}}d\sigma_{l}\big)\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}+C.

The last term is finite by (
1.10
). Now, by applying Lemma
3.1
with
\sigma=\sigma_{i},

\omega=(u_{l}^{(0)})^{q_{l}}d\sigma_{l}
and
q=q_{i}
, we find that

(3.9)

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{G}\big((u_{l}^{(0)})^{q_{l}}d\sigma_{l}\big)\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}&\leq C\Big(\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}\big((u_{l}^{(0)})^{q_{l}}d\sigma_{l}\big)^{\gamma}(u_{l}^{(0)})^{q_{l}}\;d\sigma_{l}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\gamma+1}}\\
&\quad\times\Big(\int_{\Omega}\big(\mathcal{G}\sigma_{i}\big)^{\frac{\gamma+q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}\;d\sigma_{i}}\Big)^{\frac{1-q_{i}}{(\gamma+1)(\gamma+q_{i})}}.\end{split}

By Hölder’s inequality with the exponents
\frac{\gamma+q_{l}}{\gamma}
and
\frac{\gamma+q_{l}}{q_{l}},
we obtain

(3.10)

\begin{split}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}\big((u_{l}^{(0)})^{q_{l}}d\sigma_{l}\big)^{\gamma}(u_{l}^{(0)})^{q_{l}}\;d\sigma_{l}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\gamma+1}}&\leq\|\mathcal{G}\big((u_{l}^{(0)})^{q_{l}}d\sigma_{l}\big)\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{l}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{l})}^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}}\\
&\quad\|u_{l}^{(0)}\|^{\frac{q_{l}}{\gamma+1}}_{L^{\gamma+q_{l}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{l})}.\end{split}

Since (
1.9
) is valid, we employ the weighted norm inequality (
2.9
) with
f=(u_{l}^{(0)})^{q_{l}}\in L^{\frac{\gamma+q_{l}}{q_{l}}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{l}),

(3.11)

\|\mathcal{G}\big((u_{l}^{(0)})^{q_{l}}d\sigma_{l}\big)\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{l}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{l})}\leq c\|u_{l}^{(0)}\|^{q_{l}}_{L^{\gamma+q_{l}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{l})}.

By combining (
3.8
), (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), we obtain

\sum_{l=1}^{M}\|\mathcal{G}\big((u_{l}^{(0)})^{q_{l}}d\sigma_{l}\big)\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}\leq Cc\sum_{l=1}^{M}\|u_{l}^{(0)}\|^{q_{l}}_{L^{\gamma+q_{l}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{l})}\|\mathcal{G}\sigma_{i}\|_{L^{\frac{\gamma+q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i})}^{\frac{1}{\gamma+q_{i}}}.

Then, by (
1.9
) and (
3.8
) becomes

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}\leq Cc\sum_{l=1}^{M}\|u_{l}^{(0)}\|^{q_{l}}_{L^{\gamma+q_{l}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{l})}+C.

Hence,

u_{0}\in{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i})}\quad i=1,2,\dots,M.

Suppose
u_{0},\dots,u_{j}\in L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i})
for some
j\in\mathbb{N}.

Observe that

\|u_{j+1}\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}\leq\sum\limits_{l=1}^{M}\|\mathcal{G}\big(u_{j}^{q_{l}}d\sigma_{l}\big)\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}+\|\mathcal{G}\omega\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}.

By using the same argument as above, we observe that

\|u_{j+1}\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i})}\leq Cc\sum\limits_{l=1}^{M}\|u_{j+1}\|^{q_{l}}_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}+C_{2}

\quad\qquad\qquad\leq C_{1}M\|u_{j+1}\|^{q}_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}+C_{2}.

where
q=\max\limits_{i=1,2,\dots,M}q_{i}.

We estimate the first term on the right-hand side of above equation using Young’s inequality,

\|u_{j+1}\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}\leq(1-q)(C_{1}M)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}+q\|u_{j+1}\|^{q}_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}+C_{2}.

Hence,

\|u_{j+1}\|_{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,\;d\sigma_{i})}\leq(C_{1}M)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}+\frac{C_{2}}{1-q}.

This implies that

u_{j+1}\in{L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i})}.

We have shown that
\{u_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
is an increasing sequence of positive functions in
L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i}),\;\forall i=1,2,\dots,M.
Finally, using the monotone convergence theorem to the sequence
\{u_{j}\}^{\infty}_{j=0},
the pointwise limit

u:=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}{u_{j}}\in L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i})\quad\forall i=1,2,\dots,M

and satisfies (
1.8
).

Now, we construct a minimal solution to (
1.8
) in
L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i})
.

u_{0}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{G}\big((u_{i}^{(0)})^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)+\mathcal{G}\omega\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{G}\big(v^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)+\mathcal{G}\omega=v.

Assume
u_{j}\leq v
for some
j\in\mathbb{N}.
Then,

u_{j+1}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{G}\big(u_{j}^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)+\mathcal{G}\omega\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{G}\big(v^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i}\big)+\mathcal{G}\omega=v.

Arguing, by induction, we find that

u_{j-1}\leq u_{j}\leq u\quad\text{for all}\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence,
u=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}u_{j}\leq v.
This proves the minimality of
u.

Conversely, if there exists a positive solution
u\in L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i})
to (
1.8
), it is obvious that
\mathcal{G}\omega\in L^{\gamma+q_{i}}(\Omega,d\sigma_{i})
holds. Moreover, (
1.9
) follows from the global pointwise lower bound (
2.4
):

u(x)\geq C_{i}[\mathcal{G}\sigma_{i}(x)]^{\frac{q_{i}}{1-q_{i}}},\quad x\in\Omega,

which does not require quasi-symmetry of
G
and
C_{i}
is the constant in (
2.4
).
∎

The main key inequality for existence of solutions in Lorentz space
L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is provided in the next lemma.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
0<\beta<\infty
and
G
be a positive lower semicontinuous kernel on
\Omega\times\Omega
that satisfies (
2.1
) for some
0<\alpha<n
. Then

\|\mathcal{G}\sigma\|_{L^{r,\rho}(\Omega)}\leq C\Big(\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\beta}\,d\sigma\Big)^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}}

for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{M^{+}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
where
r=\frac{n(\beta+1)}{n-\alpha},\rho=\beta+1
and
C
is a positive constant depending only on
\alpha,n,r
and
\rho.

Proof.

We have known that for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{M^{+}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\mathcal{G}\sigma(x)\leq c(\alpha,n)\mathcal{V}_{\alpha,2}\sigma(x)

for all
x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\mathcal{V}_{\alpha,2}\sigma(x)=c\mathcal{I}_{2\alpha}\sigma(x)
is the Havin-Maz’ya potential of
\sigma
for a suitable constant
c=c(\alpha,n)
(see
[
20
]
). By Theorem
2.7
, boundedness of the Havin-Maz’ya potential in Lorentz spaces, we obtain

\|\mathcal{G}(f\,dx)\|_{L^{r,\rho}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\frac{r}{r-\beta},\frac{\rho}{\rho-\beta}}(\Omega)}

for the nonnegative function
f\in L^{\frac{r}{r-\beta},\frac{\rho}{\rho-\beta}}(\Omega).

Applying Holder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}(f\,dx))^{\beta}f\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\|(\mathcal{G}(f\,dx))^{\beta}\|_{L^{\frac{r}{\beta},\frac{\rho}{\beta}}(\Omega)}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{r}{r-\beta},\frac{\rho}{\rho-\beta}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{G}(f\,dx)\|^{\beta}_{L^{r,\rho}(\Omega)}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{r}{r-\beta},\frac{\rho}{\rho-\beta}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq c\|f\|^{\beta}_{L^{\frac{r}{r-\beta},\frac{\rho}{\rho-\beta}}(\Omega)}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{r}{r-\beta},\frac{\rho}{\rho-\beta}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=c\|f\|^{\beta+1}_{L^{\frac{r}{r-\beta},\frac{\rho}{\rho-\beta}}(\Omega)}.

By Lemma
3.1
with
\sigma=fdx
,
q=0
and
\gamma=\beta
, we get

(3.12)

\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\beta}f\,dx\leq C\Big[\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{G}\sigma)^{\beta}\,d\sigma\Big]^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{r}{r-\beta},\frac{\rho}{\rho-\beta}}(\Omega)}.

Thus, by a dual characterization of
L^{\frac{r}{\beta},\frac{\rho}{\beta}}(\Omega)

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
0<q<1
and let

.
Let

Proof.

Set the following problem

(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}u=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})+\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\omega\quad a.e.\quad\mathbb{R}^{n},

for

Suppose that the condition (
1.5
) and (

) hold with
\gamma=1.

In order to prove

by duality, it is sufficient to show that there exists a positive constant
C
such that

(4.1)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\,\psi dx\Big|\leq C\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

By the semigroup property of the Riesz potentials, Tonelli’s Theorem and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\,\psi dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})|\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\psi|\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\omega\,|\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\psi|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\omega\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\omega\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big]\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since
\|\omega\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<+\infty
, we see that, now, it remains to show that

(4.2)

\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<+\infty.

By duality, (
2.2
) with
\sigma=\sigma_{i}
and
q=q_{i}
is equivalent to

(4.3)

\|\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\psi d\sigma_{i})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\psi\|_{L^{\frac{1+q_{i}}{q_{i}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\sigma_{i})}\quad\text{for all}\;\;\psi\in L^{\frac{1+q_{i}}{q_{i}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\sigma_{i}),

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
\psi
. We put
\psi:=u^{q_{i}}\in L^{\frac{1+q_{i}}{q_{i}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\sigma_{i})
in (

\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|u\|^{q_{i}}_{L^{1+q_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\;d\sigma_{i})}<+\infty,

which prove (
4.2
), and hence
u\in\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Conversely, suppose that
u
is a positive finite energy solution to (
1.1
) in
\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
It can be easily seen that the second condition (
1.6
) holds. The first condition can be verified by showing that
u\in L^{1+q_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\sigma_{i})

For each nonnegative function
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}u]\phi\;dx=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{R^{n}}[\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})]\phi\;dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\omega]\phi\;dx

Next, by the Tonelli’s Theorem and Schwarz’s inequality, we obtain

(4.4)

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{q_{i}}[\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\phi]d\sigma_{i}\Big|&=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})]\phi\;dx\Big|\\
&\leq\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}u]\phi\;dx\Big|+\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\omega)\phi\;dx\Big|\\
&\leq c\|\phi\|_{L^{2}{(\mathbb{R}^{n})}},\end{split}

where
c:=\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\omega\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<+\infty,
since
u\in\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\omega\in\dot{H}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Setting
\phi:=(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}u,
which is a nonnegative function of class

in (

\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|u\|^{1+q_{i}}_{L^{1+q_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\sigma_{i})}\leq c\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<+\infty.

This show that
u\in L^{1+q_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\sigma_{i})
for each
i=1,2,\dots,M.
This, together with Theorem
3.2
implies that the first condition (

Now, we are ready to prove the uniqueness result. Suppose that
u

\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to (
1.1
). We know that
u=v

\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

if

-a.e.We claim that if
u\geq v\;\text{a.e}

u=v\;d\sigma_{i}
-a.e.
Suppose that
u\geq v
a.e. in

In fact, using the testing functions
\phi,\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}u\phi\,dx=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(u^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\phi\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\omega)\phi\,dx

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}v\psi\,dx=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(v^{q_{i}}d\sigma_{i})\psi\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\omega)\psi\,dx.

Testing the equations

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}u\phi\,dx=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{q_{i}}(\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\phi)\,d\sigma_{i}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\phi\,d\omega,\quad\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

and

(4.5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}v\psi\,dx=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{q_{i}}(\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\psi)\,d\sigma_{i}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{I}_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\psi\,d\omega,\quad\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

with
\phi=(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}u
and
\psi=(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}v
, respectively, we obtain

\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}u\big)^{2}dx=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{1+q_{i}}\;d\sigma_{i}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\;d\omega

and

\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}v\big)^{2}dx=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{1+q_{i}}\;d\sigma_{i}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\;d\omega.

By applying the Discrete Hidden convexity, [see
[
5
]
, Proposition 4.2], along the curve

\Gamma_{t}(x):=[(1-t)v^{2}(x)+tu^{2}(x)]^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad t\in[0,1],

we obtain

|\Gamma_{t}(x)-\Gamma_{t}(y)|^{2}\leq(1-t)|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}+t|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\Gamma_{t}(x)-\Gamma_{t}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha}}\;dx\;dy

\displaystyle\leq(1-t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha}}\;dx\;dy

\displaystyle\quad\;+t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha}}\;dx\;dy

Since
\|.\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is equivalent to the Gagliardo seminorm,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}\Gamma_{t}\big)^{2}dx\leq(1-t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}v\big)^{2}dx+t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}u\big)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\big[\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}\Gamma_{t}\big)^{2}-\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}\Gamma_{0}\big)^{2}\big]}{t}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big[\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}u\big)^{2}-\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}v\big)^{2}\big]\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u^{1+q_{i}}-v^{1+q_{i}})\,d\sigma_{i}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u-v)\,d\omega

We deduct the above equation by applying the following inequality

a^{2}-b^{2}\geq 2b\cdot(a-b)\quad\text{for}\;\;a,b\in\mathbb{R}

to the left-hand side of equation, and hence we obtain

(4.6)

2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}\Gamma_{0}\frac{\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}\Gamma_{t}-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}\Gamma_{0}\big)}{t}\,dx\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u^{1+q_{i}}-v^{1+q_{i}})\,d\sigma_{i}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u-v)\,d\omega.

Testing (

\psi=(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}(\Gamma_{t}-\Gamma_{0})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
we find that

(4.7)

2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}v\frac{\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}\Gamma_{t}-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}\Gamma_{0}\big)}{t}\,dx=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{v^{q_{i}}(\Gamma_{t}-\Gamma_{0})}{t}\,d\sigma_{i}+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Gamma_{t}-\Gamma_{0}}{t}\,d\omega.

Thus, by (

(4.8)

2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{q_{i}}\frac{(\Gamma_{t}-\Gamma_{0})}{t}\,d\sigma_{i}+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Gamma_{t}-\Gamma_{0}}{t}\,d\omega\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u^{1+q_{i}}-v^{1+q_{i}})\,d\sigma_{i}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u-v)\,d\omega.

Meanwhile, by Fatou’s Lemma,

(4.9)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{q_{i}}(\frac{u^{2}-v^{2}}{v})\,d\sigma_{i}\leq\liminf_{t\to 0}2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{q_{i}}(\frac{\Gamma_{t}-\Gamma_{0}}{t})\,d\sigma_{i},

and

(4.10)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u^{2}-v^{2}}{v}\,d\omega\leq\liminf_{t\to 0}2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\frac{\Gamma_{t}-\Gamma_{0}}{t})\,d\omega

for each
i.

Since (
4.8
) holds for all

, it follows from (

) and (

) that

\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{v^{q_{i}}(u^{2}-v^{2})}{v}\,d\sigma_{i}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u^{2}-v^{2}}{v}\,d\omega\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u^{1+q_{i}}-v^{1+q_{i}})\,d\sigma_{i}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u-v)\,d\omega.

that is,

\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\frac{v^{q_{i}}u^{2}}{v}-u^{1+q_{i}})\,d\sigma_{i}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\frac{u^{2}}{v}-u)\,d\omega\leq 0.

Here both integral in the left-hand side are nonnegative since
u\geq v\;d\sigma_{i}
-a.e. and
u\geq v\;d\omega
-a.e. Nevertheless,

\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\frac{v^{q_{i}}u^{2}}{v}-u^{1+q_{i}})\,d\sigma_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\frac{u^{1+{q_{i}}}v^{q_{i}}(u^{1-q_{i}}-v^{1-q_{i}})}{v})\,d\sigma_{i}\geq 0

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\frac{u^{2}}{v}-u)\,d\omega=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u^{2}-uv}{v}\,d\omega\geq 0

Therefore, both integrals must vanish, and thus
u=v\;d\sigma_{i}
-a.e. and
u=v\;d\omega
-a.e.
By definition of solution (
1.2
),
u=v
everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

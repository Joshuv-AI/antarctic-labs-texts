Resolvent Estimates for the Stokes Operator in Bounded and Exterior C 1 C^{1} Domains
Jun Geng, Zhongwei Shen

Abstract
We establish resolvent estimates in L q L^{q} spaces for the Stokes operator in a bounded C 1 C^{1} domain Ω \Omega in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . As a corollary, it follows that the Stokes operator generates a bounded analytic semigroup in L q ​ ( Ω , ℂ d ) L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d}) for any 1 < q < ∞ 1<q<\infty and d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . The case of an exterior C 1 C^{1} domain is also studied. Keywords : Resolvent Estimate; Stokes Operator; C 1 C^{1} Domain. MR (2010) Subject Classification : 35Q30.

Introduction

In this paper we study the resolvent problem for the Stokes operator with the Dirichlet condition,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\nabla p+\lambda u&=F&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\text{\rm div}(u)&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
is a parameter and

\Sigma_{\theta}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}:\ |\text{arg}(z)|<\pi-\theta\right\}

(1.2)

for
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
.
The following two theorems are the main results of the paper.
The first one covers the case of bounded domains with
C^{1}
boundaries,
while the second treats the case of exterior
C^{1}
domains.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
Let
1<q<\infty
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
.
Then for any
F\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
, the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) has a unique solution
(u,p)
in

W_{0}^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})\times L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
with
\int_{\Omega}p=0
.
Moreover, the solution satisfies the estimate,

(|\lambda|+1)^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+(|\lambda|+1)\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)},

(1.3)

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
,
\theta
and
\Omega
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be an exterior domain with
C^{1}
boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
Let
1<q<\infty
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
with
|\lambda|\geq\delta>0
.
Then for any
F\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
, the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) has a unique solution
(u,p)
in

W_{0}^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})\times L^{q}_{{\rm loc}}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{C})
.
Moreover, the solution satisfies the estimate,

|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)},

(1.4)

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
,
\theta
,
\delta
and
\Omega
.
Furthermore, if
d\geq 3
, the estimate,

|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)},

(1.5)

holds with
C
independent of
\delta
.

Resolvent estimates for the Stokes operator play an essential role in the functional analytic approach of Fujita and Kato
[
11
]

to the nonlinear Navier-Stokes equations.
The resolvent estimate (
1.5
) in domains with smooth boundaries
has been studied extensively since 1980’s.
Under the assumption that
\Omega
is a bounded or exterior domain with
C^{1,1}
boundary, the estimate (
1.5
)
holds for any
1<q<\infty

[
21
,
16
,
2
,
10
]
.
We refer the reader to
[
10
]
for a review as well as a comprehensive list of references in the case of smooth domains.
The recent work in this area focuses on domains with nonsmooth boundaries.
If
\Omega
is merely a bounded Lipschitz domain,
it was proved by one of the present authors
[
20
]
that the resolvent estimate (
1.5
) holds if
d\geq 3
and

\left|\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}\right|<\frac{1}{2d}+\varepsilon,

(1.6)

where
\varepsilon>0
depends on
\Omega
.
In particular,
in the case
d=3
, this shows that the estimate (
1.5
) holds for
(3/2)-\varepsilon<q<3+\varepsilon

and gives an affirmative answer to a conjecture of M. Taylor
[
23
]
.
For a two-dimensional bounded Lipschitz domain, F. Gabel and P. Tolksdorf
[
12
]
were able to
establish the resolvent estimate
(
1.5
) for

(4/3)-\varepsilon<q<4+\varepsilon
.
It is not known whether the range in (
1.6
) is sharp for Lipschitz domains.
In
[
6
]
P. Deuring constructed an interesting example of an unbounded Lipschitz domain for which the resolvent
estimate fails for large
q
.
For related work on the Stokes and
Navier-Stokes equations in Lipschitz or
C^{1}
domains,
we refer to the reader to
[

,

,

,
17
,
18
,
19
,
14
,
24
,

The main contribution of this paper lies in the smoothness assumption for the domain
\Omega
.
We are able to establish the resolvent estimates for the full range
1<q<\infty
under the assumption
that
\partial\Omega
is
C^{1}
. In view of the example by P. Deuring
[
6
]
, this assumption is more or less
optimal. As we mentioned earlier, the full range is known previously for
C^{1,1}
domains
[
10
]
.
A recent result of D. Breit
[
4
]
implies the resolvent estimates for a three-dimensional Lipschitz domain satisfying certain
Besov-type conditions, which are
weaker than
C^{1,1}
and somewhat close to
C^{1,\alpha}
for certain
\alpha>0
.
Note that in the case of smooth domains, in addition to the
L^{q}
estimates for
u
and
\nabla u
in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
),
one also obtains an estimate for
\nabla^{2}u
,

\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)},

(1.7)

for

, if
\Omega
is bounded (some restrictions on

are needed if
\Omega
is an exterior domain; see
[
10
]
).
However, such

estimates fail in

domains, even for the Laplace operator.

Let
C^{\infty}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d}):\text{\rm div}(u)=0\right\}

and

L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)=\text{\rm the closure of }C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(\Omega)\text{ in }L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d}).

(1.8)

For
1<q<\infty
, we define the Stokes operator
A_{q}
in
L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
by

A_{q}(u)=-\Delta u+\nabla p,

(1.9)

with the domain

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(A_{q})=\Big\{u\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d}):

\displaystyle\text{\rm div}(u)=0\text{ in }\Omega\text{ and }

(1.10)

\displaystyle-\Delta u+\nabla p\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\text{ for some }p\in L_{{\rm loc}}^{q}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{C})\Big\}.

It follows from Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
that for
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
and

, the inverse operator

(\lambda+A_{q})^{-1}
exists as a bounded operator on
L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
the estimate,

\|(\lambda+A_{q})^{-1}F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C|\lambda|^{-1}\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)},

(1.11)

holds, where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
,
\theta
and
\Omega
, if
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
or
an exterior
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
.
As a corollary, we obtain the following.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
or an exterior
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
.
Then the Stokes operator
-A_{q}
generates a uniformly bounded analytic semigroup
\{e^{-tA_{q}}\}_{t\geq 0}
in

L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
for
1<q<\infty
.

The uniform boundedness of the semigroup in the case of two-dimensional exterior
C^{1}
domains is left open by Corollary
1.3
.
We note that the uniform boundedness for the two-dimensional exterior
C^{2}
domains was established in
[
3
]
by
using the method of layer potentials for
\lambda
near
0
.

We now describe our approach to Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, which is based on a perturbation argument of R. Farwig and
H. Sohr
[
10
]
.
The basic idea is to work out first the cases of the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
.
One then uses a perturbation argument to treat the case of a region above a graph,

\mathbb{H}_{\psi}=\left\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\text{ and }x_{d}>\psi(x^{\prime})\right\},

where
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Finally, a localization procedure, together with some compactness argument, is performed to handle the cases of bounded or exterior domains.
To establish the resolvent estimates for
C^{1}
domains, the key step is to carry out the perturbation argument
under the assumption that
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous
and to show that the error terms are bounded by the Lipschitz norm
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}
,
where
\nabla^{\prime}
denotes the gradient with respect to
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d-1})
.

To this end, we consider a more general Stokes resolvent problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\nabla p+\lambda u&=F+\text{\rm div}(f),\\
\text{ \rm div}(u)&=g,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.12)

in
\mathbb{H}_{\psi}
with the boundary condition
u=0
on
\partial\mathbb{H}_{\psi}
,
where
F\in L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C}^{d})
and
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})
.
We introduce two Banach spaces,

X_{\psi}^{q}=W^{1,q}_{0}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C}^{d})\times A_{\psi}^{q}\quad\text{ and }\quad Y_{\psi}^{q}=W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C}^{d})\times B_{\psi}^{q},

(1.13)

where
A_{\psi}^{q}
and
B_{\psi}^{q}
are two spaces defined by (
4.2
).
In comparison with the spaces used in
[
10
]
for
C^{1,1}
domains,
we point out that since we work with
C^{1}
domains, no
W^{2,q}
spaces can be used.
Note that the scaling-invariant property of the Lipschitz norm
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}
allows us to
fix
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
with
|\lambda|=1
. Consider the linear operator

S_{\psi}^{\lambda}(u,p)=(-\Delta u+\nabla p+\lambda u,\text{\rm div}(u)).

(1.14)

We are able to show that
S_{\psi}^{\lambda}:X_{\psi}^{q}\to Y_{\psi}^{q}
is a bijection and that

\|(S_{\psi}^{\lambda})^{-1}\|_{Y_{\psi}^{q}\to X_{\psi}^{q}}\leq C(d,q,\theta)

(1.15)

for
1<q<\infty
, provided that
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq c_{0}
and
c_{0}=c_{0}(d,q,\theta)>0
is sufficiently small.
See Theorem
4.3
.
To prove (
1.15
), one first considers the special case
\psi=0
; i.e.,
\mathbb{H}_{\psi}=\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
.
The general case follows from the facts that

S_{\psi}^{\lambda}(u,p)=S_{0}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})\circ\Psi+R(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})\circ\Psi,

(1.16)

and that the operator norm of the second term in the right-hand side of (
1.16
) is bounded by
C\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}

if
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.
As a by-product, we also obtain the resolvent estimate (
1.4
) in the case
\Omega=\mathbb{H}_{\psi}
if

\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq c_{0}(d,q,\theta)
.
See Theorem
4.1
.

The whole space

In this section we study the resolvent problem for the Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
The results in Theorem
2.1
are more or less standard.
Since the Stokes equations are considered with a more general data set,
we provide a proof for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
1<q<\infty
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
.
For any
F\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d})
,
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})
, and
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})\cap\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
,
there exists a unique
u\in W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d})
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\nabla p+\lambda u&=F+\text{\rm div}(f),\\
\text{\rm div}(u)&=g\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

hold in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
p\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
in the sense of distributions.
Moreover, the solution satisfies the estimate,

\left\{\begin{aligned} |\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right\},\\
|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|\lambda|\|g\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right\},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

and
p\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})+\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
,
where
C
depends on
d
,
q
and
\theta
.

Proof.

Step 1. We establish the existence of the solution and the estimates in (
2.2
).

By rescaling we may assume
|\lambda|=1
.
By linearity, it suffices to consider two cases: (I)
g=0
; (II)
f=0
and
F=0
.

Case I. Assume
g=0
.
Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the Fourier transform defined by

\mathcal{F}(h)(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}h(x)\,dx,

where
i=\sqrt{-1}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
u=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{d})
,
F=(F_{1},F_{2},\dots,F_{d})
and
f=(f_{jk})
.
By applying
\mathcal{F}
to (
2.1
) with
g=0
, we obtain

\left\{\begin{aligned} (|\xi|^{2}+\lambda)\mathcal{F}(u_{j})+i\xi_{j}\mathcal{F}(p)&=\mathcal{F}(F_{j})+i\xi_{\ell}\mathcal{F}(f_{\ell j})&\quad&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\xi_{\ell}\mathcal{F}(u_{\ell})&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.3)

where the repeated index
\ell
is summed from
1
to
d
.
A solution of (
2.3
) is given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{F}(u_{j})&=(\lambda+|\xi|^{2})^{-1}\left(\delta_{jk}-\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\left(\mathcal{F}(F_{k})+i\xi_{\ell}\mathcal{F}(f_{\ell k})\right),\\
\mathcal{F}(p)&=\frac{-i\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\left(\mathcal{F}(F_{k})+i\xi_{\ell}\mathcal{F}(f_{\ell k})\right),\end{aligned}\right.

(2.4)

where the repeated indices
k,\ell
are summed from
1
to
d
.
Since
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
and
|\lambda|=1
, we have
|\lambda+|\xi|^{2}|\approx 1+|\xi|^{2}
.
Thus, by the Mikhlin multiplier theorem, there exist
u\in W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d})
and
p\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})+\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
,
satisfying (
2.1
) and

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left\{\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right\},

(2.5)

for
1<q<\infty
, where
C
depends on
d
,
q
and
\theta
.

Case II. Assume that
F=0
and
f=0
.
Since
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})\cap\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
,
there exists
G\in\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
such that
\nabla G\in W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d})
,
\Delta G=g
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|\nabla G\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|g\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\nabla^{2}G\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Let
u=\nabla G
and
p=g-\lambda G
. Then

u\in W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d})
,
p\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})+\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
, and
(u,p)
satisfies (
2.1
)
with
F=0
and
f=0
.
Moreover,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|g\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Step 2. We establish the uniqueness of the solution.

Let
u\in W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d})
be a solution of (
2.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

F=0
,
f=0
and
g=0
.
It follows that for any
w\in C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla w+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\cdot w=0,

(2.6)

where
C^{\infty}_{0,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{w\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d}):\text{\rm div}(w)=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
. Since
u\in W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d})
,
by a density argument, we deduce that (
2.6
) holds for any
w\in W^{1,q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d})
with div
(w)=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
w
be a solution in
W^{1,q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d})
of the Stokes equations,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta w+\nabla\phi+\lambda w&=|u|^{q-2}\overline{u},\\
\text{\rm div}(w)&=0\end{aligned}\right.

(2.7)

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\overline{u}
denotes the complex conjugate of
u
.
Since
|u|^{q-2}\overline{u}\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d})
, such solution exists in
W^{1,q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d})

by Step 1. Again by a density argument, we may deduce from (
2.7
) that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla u+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w\cdot u=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{q}.

(2.8)

In view of (
2.6
) and (
2.8
), we obtain
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{q}=0
and thus
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
F
,
f
,
g
,
(u,p)
be the same as in Theorem
2.1
.
Let
F=(F_{1},F_{2},\dots,F_{d})
and
f=(f_{jk})
. The
k

\text{\rm div}(f)
is given by

\sum_{j}\partial_{j}f_{jk}
, where
\partial_{j}
denotes
\partial/\partial x_{j}
.
Let
x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})
, where
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.
Suppose that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{
$F_{j}$ is even in $x_{d}$ for $1\leq j\leq d-1$ and $F_{d}$ is odd, }\\
&\text{
$g$ is even in $x_{d}$, }\\
&\text{
$f_{jk}$ is even in $x_{d}$ for $1\leq j,k\leq d-1$,}\\

3
A half-space

In this section we consider the resolvent problem for the Stokes equations in the half-space

Recall that
\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};\mathbb{C})
is the homogeneous

space in
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
defined by (

\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};\mathbb{C})
denotes the dual of
\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};\mathbb{C})

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1<q<\infty
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
.
Let

,

, and

.
Then there exists a unique

such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\nabla p+\lambda u&=F+\text{\rm div}(f),\\
\text{\rm div}(u)&=g\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

hold in
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
for some
p\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};\mathbb{C})
in the sense of distributions.
Moreover, the solution

satisfies the estimate,

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}+|\lambda|\|g\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\right\},

and
p\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};\mathbb{C})+\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};\mathbb{C})
,
where
C
depends on
d
,
q
and
\theta
.

Our proof of Theorem
3.1
follows closely a line of argument in
[
10
]
.

For a function

in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, we use
\widehat{h}
to denote the Fourier transform of
h
,

\widehat{h}(\xi^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}e^{-i\xi^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime}}h(x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime},

(3.3)

for
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1};\mathbb{C}^{m})
.
Suppose that

\widehat{Tf}(\xi^{\prime})=m(\xi^{\prime})\widehat{f}(\xi^{\prime})

and that the multiplier
m(\xi^{\prime})
satisfies the estimate,

|\xi^{\prime}|^{|\alpha|}|D^{\alpha}m(\xi^{\prime})|\leq M,

(3.4)

for
|\alpha|\leq\left[\frac{d-1}{2}\right]+1
, where
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d-1})
and

D^{\alpha}=\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{d-1}^{\alpha_{d-1}}
.
Then

\|Tf\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}\leq CM\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}

for
1<q<\infty
, where
C
depends on
d
and
q
.

Proof.

This is the well known Mikhlin multiplier theorem in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.
∎

We use
W^{1-\frac{1}{q},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1};\mathbb{C}^{m})
to denote the trace space of
W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};\mathbb{C}^{m})
on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
.
Suppose that

\widehat{Tf}(\xi^{\prime},x_{d})=m(\xi^{\prime},x_{d})\widehat{f}(\xi^{\prime})

and that
m(\xi^{\prime},x_{d})
satisfies the condition

|\xi^{\prime}|^{|\alpha|}|D^{\alpha}m(\xi^{\prime},x_{d})|+|\xi^{\prime}|^{|\alpha|-1}|D^{\alpha}\partial_{d}m(\xi^{\prime},x_{d})|\leq\frac{M_{0}e^{-\delta|\xi^{\prime}|x_{d}}}{1+x_{d}}

(3.5)

for
x_{d}>0
,
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and
|\alpha|\leq\left[\frac{d-1}{2}\right]+1
, where
\delta>0
.
Then

\left\{\begin{aligned} \|Tf\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}&\leq C\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})},\\
\|\nabla T(f)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}&\leq C\|f\|_{W^{1-\frac{1}{q},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.6)

for
1<q<\infty
, where
C
depends on
d
,
q
,
\delta
and
M_{0}
.

Proof.

Note that for each
x_{d}>0
,

satisfies (
3.4
) with
M=M_{0}(1+x_{d})^{-1}
.
It follows from Lemma
3.2
that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|Tf(x^{\prime},x_{d})|^{q}\,dx^{\prime}dx_{d}

\displaystyle\leq CM_{0}^{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{|f(x^{\prime})|^{q}}{(1+x_{d})^{q}}\,dx^{\prime}dx_{d}

\displaystyle\leq CM_{0}^{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}|f|^{q}\,dx^{\prime}.

To prove the second inequality in (
3.6
), we write

\widehat{\partial_{j}Tf}(\xi^{\prime},x_{d})=e^{\delta_{0}x_{d}|\xi^{\prime}|}m(\xi^{\prime},x_{d})\cdot i\xi_{j}e^{-\delta_{0}x_{d}|\xi^{\prime}|}\widehat{f}(\xi^{\prime})

for
1\leq j\leq d-1
, and

\widehat{\partial_{d}Tf}(\xi^{\prime},x_{d})=|\xi^{\prime}|^{-1}e^{\delta_{0}x_{d}|\xi^{\prime}|}\partial_{d}m(\xi^{\prime},x_{d})\cdot|\xi^{\prime}|e^{-\delta_{0}x_{d}|\xi^{\prime}|}\widehat{f}(\xi^{\prime}),

where
\delta_{0}=\delta/2
.
Using (

), it is not hard to show that for each

, both

e^{\delta_{0}x_{d}|\xi^{\prime}|}m(\xi^{\prime},x_{d})\quad\text{ and }\quad|\xi^{\prime}|^{-1}e^{\delta_{0}x_{d}|\xi^{\prime}|}\partial_{d}m(\xi^{\prime},x_{d})

satisfy the condition (
3.4
) with
M
independent of
x_{d}
.
This implies that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|\nabla Tf(x^{\prime},x_{d})|^{q}\,dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|\nabla v(x^{\prime},x_{d})|^{q}\,dx,

where

is defined by

\widehat{v}(\xi^{\prime},x_{d})=e^{-\delta_{0}x_{d}|\xi^{\prime}|}\widehat{f}(\xi^{\prime}).

Finally, we note that if

,

is a solution of the Dirichlet problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(\partial_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\partial_{d-1}^{2}+\partial_{d}^{2}\right)v&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},\\
v&=f&\quad&\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{0\},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.7)

given by the Poisson integral of

It is well known that

satisfies the estimate,

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{1-\frac{1}{q},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})},

where

depends on

and

[
22

The general case follows from the case

As a result, we obtain the second inequality in (

\|\Lambda\|_{W^{-1,q}(\Omega)}\approx\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
with
|\lambda|=1
.
Let
1<q<\infty
.
There exists
c_{0}\in(0,1)
, depending only on
d
,
q
and
\theta
, such that
if
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq c_{0}
, then

S_{\psi}^{\lambda}:X_{\psi}^{q}\to Y_{\psi}^{q}
is a bijection and

\|(S_{\psi}^{\lambda})^{-1}\|_{Y_{\psi}^{q}\to X_{\psi}^{q}}\leq C,

(4.8)

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
and
\theta
.

Proof.

Suppose
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.
Define a bi-Lipschitz map
\Psi:\mathbb{H}_{\psi}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
by

\Psi(x^{\prime},x_{d})=(x^{\prime},x_{d}-\psi(x^{\prime})).

Note that
\Psi^{-1}(x^{\prime},x_{d})=(x^{\prime},x_{d}+\psi(x^{\prime}))
.
For a function
u
in
\mathbb{H}_{\psi}
, let
\widetilde{u}=u\circ\Psi^{-1}
, defined in
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
.
Thus,
u=\widetilde{u}\circ\Psi
and

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{j}u&=\partial_{j}\widetilde{u}\circ\Psi-\partial_{d}(\widetilde{u}\partial_{j}\psi)\circ\Psi\quad\text{ for }1\leq j\leq d-1,\\
\partial_{d}u&=\partial_{d}\widetilde{u}\circ\Psi.\end{aligned}\right.

A computation shows that

\Delta u=\Delta\widetilde{u}\circ\Psi-\partial_{d}(\partial_{k}\widetilde{u}\partial_{k}\psi)\circ\Psi-\partial_{k}(\partial_{d}\widetilde{u}\partial_{k}\psi)\circ\Psi+\partial_{d}(\partial_{d}\widetilde{u}|\nabla^{\prime}\psi|^{2})\circ\Psi,

where
|\nabla^{\prime}\psi|^{2}=|\partial_{1}\psi|^{2}+\cdots+|\partial_{d-1}\psi|^{2}
and the repeated index
k
is summed from
1
to
d-1
.
For
(u,p)\in X_{\psi}^{q}
, let
\widetilde{u}=u\circ\Psi^{-1}
and
\widetilde{p}=p\circ\Psi^{-1}
. Then

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{j}+\partial_{j}p+\lambda u_{j}=-

\displaystyle\Delta\widetilde{u}_{j}\circ\Psi+\partial_{j}\widetilde{p}\circ\Psi+\lambda\widetilde{u}_{j}\circ\Psi

(4.9)

\displaystyle+\partial_{d}(\partial_{k}\widetilde{u}_{j}\partial_{k}\psi)\circ\Psi+\partial_{k}(\partial_{d}\widetilde{u}_{j}\partial_{k}\psi)\circ\Psi

\displaystyle-\partial_{d}(\partial_{d}\widetilde{u}_{j}|\nabla^{\prime}\psi|^{2})\circ\Psi-\partial_{d}(\widetilde{p}\partial_{j}\psi)\circ\Psi

for
1\leq j\leq d-1
, and

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{d}+\partial_{d}p+\lambda u_{d}=

\displaystyle-\Delta\widetilde{u}_{d}\circ\Psi+\partial_{d}\widetilde{p}\circ\Psi+\lambda\widetilde{u}_{d}\circ\Psi

(4.10)

\displaystyle+\partial_{d}(\partial_{k}\widetilde{u}_{d}\partial_{k}\psi)\circ\Psi+\partial_{k}(\partial_{d}\widetilde{u}_{d}\partial_{k}\psi)\circ\Psi

\displaystyle-\partial_{d}(\partial_{d}\widetilde{u}_{d}|\nabla^{\prime}\psi|^{2})\circ\Psi,

where the repeated index
k
is summed from
1
to
d-1
.
Also, note that

\text{\rm div}(u)=\text{\rm div}(\widetilde{u})\circ\Psi-\partial_{d}(\widetilde{u}_{k}\partial_{k}\psi)\circ\Psi.

(4.11)

In view of (
4.9
), (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we obtain

S^{\lambda}_{\psi}(u,p)=S^{\lambda}_{0}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})\circ\Psi+R(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})\circ\Psi,

(4.12)

where
R(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})=(R_{1}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p}),\dots,R_{d}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p}),R_{d+1}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p}))
with

R_{j}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})=\partial_{d}(\partial_{k}\widetilde{u}_{j}\partial_{k}\psi)+\partial_{k}(\partial_{d}\widetilde{u}_{j}\partial_{k}\psi)-\partial_{d}(\partial_{d}\widetilde{u}_{j}|\nabla^{\prime}\psi|^{2})-\partial_{d}(\widetilde{p}\partial_{j}\psi)\\

(4.13)

for
1\leq j\leq d-1
, and

\left\{\begin{aligned} R_{d}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})&=\partial_{d}(\partial_{k}\widetilde{u}_{d}\partial_{k}\psi)+\partial_{k}(\partial_{d}\widetilde{u}_{d}\partial_{k}\psi)-\partial_{d}(\partial_{d}\widetilde{u}_{d}|\nabla^{\prime}\psi|^{2}),\\
R_{d+1}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})&=-\partial_{d}(\widetilde{u}_{k}\partial_{k}\psi).\end{aligned}\right.

(4.14)

We claim that for any
(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})\in X_{0}^{q}
,

\|R(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})\|_{Y_{0}^{q}}\leq C\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\|(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})\|_{X_{0}^{q}},

(4.15)

where
C
depends only on
d
and
q
.
To show (
4.15
), we note that

\|R_{j}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq C\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}+\|\partial_{d}(\widetilde{p}\partial_{j}\psi)\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}

(4.16)

for
1\leq j\leq d-1
, and

\|R_{d}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq C\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})},

(4.17)

where we have used the assumption
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.
To bound the second term in the right-hand side of (
4.16
), we let

\widetilde{p}=\widetilde{p}_{1}+\widetilde{p}_{2}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};\mathbb{C})+\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+};\mathbb{C})=A_{0}^{q}.

Then

\displaystyle\|\partial_{d}(\widetilde{p}\partial_{j}\psi)\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{d}(\widetilde{p}_{1}\partial_{j}\psi)\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}+\|\partial_{d}(\widetilde{p}_{2}\partial_{j}\psi)\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\|\widetilde{p}_{1}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}+C\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\|\partial_{d}\widetilde{p}_{2}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}.

This shows that

\|\partial_{d}(\widetilde{p}\partial_{j}\psi)\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq C\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\|\widetilde{p}\|_{A_{0}^{q}}.

As a result, we have proved that

\|R_{j}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq C\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\|(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})\|_{X_{0}^{q}}

(4.18)

for
1\leq j\leq d
. This, together with the estimates,

\|R_{d+1}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}+\|R_{d+1}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{p})\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq C\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\left(\|\nabla\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}+\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\right),

gives (
4.15
).

By Lemma
4.2
,
S_{0}^{\lambda}:X_{0}^{q}\to Y_{0}^{q}
is bounded and invertible
for
1<q<\infty
.
It follows by a standard perturbation argument that

is bounded and invertible if

\|R(S_{0}^{\lambda})^{-1}\|_{Y_{0}^{q}\to Y_{0}^{q}}<1.

Moreover, we have

\|(S_{0}^{\lambda}+R)^{-1}\|_{Y_{0}^{q}\to X_{0}^{q}}\leq\frac{\|(S_{0}^{\lambda})^{-1}\|_{Y_{0}^{q}\to X_{0}^{q}}}{1-\|R(S_{0}^{\lambda})^{-1}\|_{Y_{0}^{q}\to Y_{0}^{q}}}.

By (
4.7
) and (
4.15
),

\displaystyle\|R(S_{0}^{\lambda})^{-1}\|_{Y_{0}^{q}\to Y_{0}^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\|R\|_{X_{0}^{q}\to Y_{0}^{q}}\|(S_{0}^{\lambda})^{-1}\|_{Y_{0}^{q}\to X_{0}^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty},

where
C_{0}
depends only on
d
,
q
and
\theta
.
As a result, we have proved that if
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq(2C_{0})^{-1}
, then

S_{0}^{\lambda}+R:X_{0}^{q}\to Y_{0}^{q}
is invertible and

\|(S_{0}^{\lambda}+R)^{-1}\|_{Y_{0}^{q}\to X_{0}^{q}}\leq C

for some
C
depending on
d
,
q
and
\theta
.
Finally, we note that

\|(u\circ\Psi^{-1},p\circ\Psi^{-1})\|_{X_{0}^{q}}\approx\|(u,p)\|_{X_{\psi}^{q}}

for any
(u,p)\in X_{\psi}^{q}
, and

\|(\Lambda\circ\Psi^{-1},g\circ\Psi^{-1})\|_{Y_{0}^{q}}\approx\|(\Lambda,g)\|_{Y_{\psi}^{q}}

for any

.
By (
4.12
), we deduce that if
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq c_{0}(d,q,\theta)
, then
S_{\psi}^{\lambda}:X_{\psi}^{q}\to Y_{\psi}^{q}
is invertible and (
4.8
) holds.
This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

\text{\rm div}(u)=0

\Omega

\|\text{\rm div}(u\varphi)\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C(\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{\infty})\|u\|_{W^{-1,q}(\Omega)},

(5.5)

where
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})

and

depends on

,
q
, diam
(\Omega)
and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega

Proof.

Let

.
Note that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{\rm div}(u\varphi)\cdot h=\int_{\Omega}(u\cdot\nabla\varphi)\left(h-\fint_{\Omega}h\right),

where we have used the assumption
\text{\rm div}(u)=0
in
\Omega

It follows that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{\rm div}(u\varphi)\cdot h\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{W^{-1,q}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\varphi(h-\fint_{\Omega}h)\|_{W_{0}^{1,q^{\prime}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{\infty})\|u\|_{W^{-1,q}(\Omega)}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where we have used a Poincaré inequality in
\Omega
. This gives (
5.5
).
∎

Lemma 5.4

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla(u\varphi)\|_{L^{q}(D)}+|\lambda|\|u\varphi\|_{L^{q}(D)}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\|F\varphi\|_{L^{q}(D)}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|f\varphi\|_{L^{q}(D)}+\|f\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{q}(D)}+\|p\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{q}(D)}+\|u\Delta\varphi\|_{L^{q}(D)}

\displaystyle+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|u\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{q}(D)}+|\lambda|\|\text{div}(u\varphi)\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(D)}\Big\},

which yields

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B(z,r_{0}))}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B(z,r_{0}))}

(5.12)

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{0}^{-2}\Big\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+(1+|\lambda|^{1/2})\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|p\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+(1+|\lambda|^{1/2})\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{W_{0}^{-1,q}(\Omega)}\Big\},

where we have used the estimate in Remark
5.3
and the fact
\varphi=1
in

.

We now cover
\Omega
by a finite number of balls
\{B(z_{k},r_{0})\}

or

.
In view of (

), by summation, we deduce that

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

(5.13)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+(1+|\lambda|^{1/2})\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|p\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+(1+|\lambda|^{1/2})\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{W_{0}^{-1,q}(\Omega)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+(1+|\lambda|^{1/2})\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+(1+|\lambda|^{1/2})\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{W_{0}^{-1,q}(\Omega)}\right\},

where we have used Lemma
5.4

and the equation
\nabla p=\Delta u-\lambda u+F+\text{\rm div}(f)

for the last inequality.
The constant
C
in (
5.13
) depends only on
d
,
q
,
\theta
and
\Omega
.
We obtain (
5.9
) by choosing
\lambda_{0}>1
so large that
|\lambda|\geq 4C|\lambda|^{1/2}
for
|\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
2\leq q<\infty
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
.
Let
(u,p)\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})\times L_{0}^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
be a solution of (
5.3
)
with
F\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
,
f\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})
and
g=0
.
Then,

(|\lambda|+1)^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+(|\lambda|+1)\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+(|\lambda|+1)^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right\},

(5.14)

where
C>0
depends on
d
,
q
,
\theta
, diam
(\Omega)
and the
C^{1}
character of
\Omega
.

Proof.

The case
q=2
is well known and follows from the energy estimates.
For
q>2
, we first consider the case
|\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0}
,
where
\lambda_{0}>1
is given by Lemma
5.5
.
Since
\Omega
is bounded, by Lemma
5.5
, the estimate

|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{W_{0}^{-1,s}(\Omega)}\right\}

holds for any

.
By Sobolev imbedding,
L^{t}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})\subset W_{0}^{-1,s}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
, where

and
\frac{1}{t}=\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{d}
.
In particular, if
2<s\leq\frac{2d}{d-2}
, then

and

\displaystyle|\lambda|\|u\|_{W_{0}^{-1,s}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\right\}.

This, together with (
5.15
), gives (

) for
2<q\leq\frac{2d}{d-2}
.
By a bootstrapping argument, one may show that the estimate (

) holds for any
2<q<\infty

in a finite number of steps.

We now consider the case
|\lambda|<\lambda_{0}
.
We rewrite the Stokes equations as

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\nabla p+(\lambda+2\lambda_{0})u&=F+\text{\rm div}(f)+2\lambda_{0}u,\\
\text{\rm div}(u)&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.16)

Since
\lambda+2\lambda_{0}\in\Sigma_{\theta}
and
|\lambda+2\lambda_{0}|>\lambda_{0}
,
it follows from the previous case that

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right\}.

(5.17)

Since
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})\subset L^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
for

, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right\}

for
2<q\leq\frac{2d}{d-2}
, where we have used the estimate (
5.14
) for
q=2

for the last inequality.
As before, a bootstrapping argument, using (
5.17
), gives

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right\}

for
2<q<\infty
in a finite number of steps.
This, together with a Poincaré inequality, yields (
5.14
) for the case
|\lambda|<\lambda_{0}
.
∎

B_{0}=B(0,2R_{0})
,
it follows by Theorem
2.1
and Remark

that
u\varphi\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d})
and

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla(u\varphi)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|\lambda|\|u\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\|F\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|f\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|f\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|p\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle+\|u\Delta\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|u\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|\lambda|\|\text{div}(u\varphi)\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big\}.

Note that the same argument as in the proof of Lemma

also yields

Hence,

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\setminus B_{0})}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\setminus B_{0})}

(6.6)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{0})}+\|p\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{0})}

\displaystyle+(1+|\lambda|^{1/2})\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{0})}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{W_{0}^{-1,q}(\Omega\cap B_{0})}\Big\}.

Since
\Omega\cap B_{0}
is a bounded
C^{1}
domain,
it follows from the proof of Lemma
5.5
that

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{0})}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap 2B_{0})}+\|p\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap 2B_{0})}

\displaystyle+(1+|\lambda|^{1/2})\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap 2B_{0})}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{W_{0}^{-1,q}(\Omega\cap 2B_{0})}\Big\}.

This, together with (
6.6
), gives

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

(6.7)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+(|\lambda|^{1/2}+1)\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|p\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap 2B_{0})}

\displaystyle+(|\lambda|^{1/2}+1)\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap 2B_{0})}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{W_{0}^{-1,q}(\Omega\cap 2B_{0})}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+(|\lambda|^{1/2}+1)\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap 2B_{0})}

\displaystyle+(|\lambda|^{1/2}+1)\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap 2B_{0})}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{W_{0}^{-1,q}(\Omega\cap 2B_{0})}\Big\},

where we have assumed
\int_{\Omega\cap 2B_{0}}p=0
and used Lemma
5.4
for the last inequality.
As a result, we have proved that
u\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
. Moreover,
we obtain (
6.5
) if
|\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0}
and
\lambda_{0}>1
is sufficiently large.
∎

Lemma 6.4

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
2\leq q<\infty
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta,\delta}
.
Let
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
be an energy solution of (
6.1
)
with
F\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
and
f\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})\cap L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})

|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right\},

(6.9)

where
C
depends on
d
,
q
,
\theta
,
\delta
and
\Omega
.

Proof.

The case
q=2
is the well known energy estimate.
To handle the case
q>2
, we argue by contradiction.
Note that by Lemma
6.2
,
u\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
Suppose the estimate (
6.9
) is not true.
Then there exist sequences
\{u^{\ell}\}\subset W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
,

\{F^{\ell}\}\subset L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
,

\{f^{\ell}\}\subset L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})\cap L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})
and
\{\lambda^{\ell}\}\subset\Sigma_{\theta,\delta}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u^{\ell}+\nabla p^{\ell}+\lambda^{\ell}u^{\ell}&=F^{\ell}+\text{\rm div}(f^{\ell})&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\text{\rm div}(u^{\ell})&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(6.10)

for some
p^{\ell}\in L^{2}_{{\rm loc}}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{C})
,

|\lambda^{\ell}|^{1/2}\|\nabla u^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda^{\ell}|\|u^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}=1,

(6.11)

and

\|F^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda^{\ell}|^{1/2}\|f^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\to 0\quad\text{ as }\ell\to\infty.

(6.12)

Since
|\lambda^{\ell}|\geq\delta
, it follows from (
6.11
) that
\|u^{\ell}\|_{W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C
.
By passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
u^{\ell}\to u
weakly in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
We may also assume that either
|\lambda^{\ell}|\to\infty
or

\lambda^{\ell}\to\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.

We consider three cases: (1)
\lambda^{\ell}\to\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
|\lambda|>2\lambda_{0}
, where
\lambda_{0}>1
is given by
Lemma
6.2
; (2)
\lambda^{\ell}\to\lambda
and
|\lambda|\leq 2\lambda_{0}
; and (3)
|\lambda^{\ell}|\to\infty
.

Case (1). Suppose
\lambda^{\ell}\to\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
|\lambda|>2\lambda_{0}
.
It follows that
u\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
is a solution of (
6.1
) in
\Omega
with

F=0
and
f=0
. By Lemma
6.4
, we obtain
u=0
in
\Omega
.
Thus,
u^{\ell}\to 0
weakly in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
This implies that
u^{\ell}\to 0
strongly in
W_{0}^{-1,q}(\Omega\cap 2B_{0};\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
However, by (
6.5
) and (
6.11
)-(
6.12
), we have

\displaystyle 1=

\displaystyle|\lambda^{\ell}|^{1/2}\|\nabla u^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda^{\ell}|\|u^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

(6.13)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|F^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda^{\ell}|^{1/2}\|f^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda^{\ell}|\|u^{\ell}\|_{W_{0}^{-1,q}(\Omega\cap 2B_{0})}\right\}\to 0,

which yields a contradiction.

Case (2). Suppose
\lambda^{\ell}\to\lambda
and
|\lambda|\leq 2\lambda_{0}
.
As in case (1),
u^{\ell}\to 0
weakly in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
It follows from (
6.8
) that

\displaystyle 1=|\lambda^{\ell}|^{1/2}\|\nabla u^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda^{\ell}|\|u^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|F^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|f^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|u^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap 3B_{0})}\right\}.

This gives us a contradiction, as

strongly in
L^{q}(\Omega\cap 3B_{0};\mathbb{C}^{d})
.

Case (3). Suppose that
|\lambda^{\ell}|\to\infty
. In view of (
6.11
), we have

strongly in
L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
By passing to a subsequence, we assume that
\lambda^{\ell}u^{\ell}\to v
weakly in

.
Note that if
w\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
and
\text{\rm div}(w)=0

, then

, we obtain

This implies that

for some
\phi\in\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
.
Since
\lambda^{\ell}u^{\ell}\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
and
\text{\rm div}(\lambda^{\ell}u^{\ell})=0

, we also have

\int_{\Omega}v\cdot\nabla\varphi=0

\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
.
It follows that
\phi\in\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
is a solution of the Neumann problem:

\Delta\phi=0
in
\Omega
and
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=0

on
\partial\Omega
.
Since
\nabla\phi\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
, we conclude that
v=\nabla\phi=0
in
\Omega
. See Lemma
7.1
in Appendix.
Thus,
\lambda^{\ell}u^{\ell}\to 0
weakly in
L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
and thus strongly in
W^{-1,q}(\Omega\cap 2B_{0};\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
Consequently, (
6.13
) holds and gives us a contradiction.
This completes the proof.
∎

In this Appendix we prove several uniqueness and regularity results in exterior

In the case of exterior domains with

.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an exterior
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
and
1<q<\infty
.
Suppose that
\phi\in\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
,
\Delta\phi=0
in
\Omega
and
n\cdot\nabla\phi=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
Then
\phi
is constant in
\Omega
.

Proof.

By using the mean value property for harmonic functions and
|\nabla\phi|\in L^{q}(\Omega)
, we obtain

\nabla\phi(x)=o(1)
as
|x|\to\infty
.
By the expansion theorem at
\infty
for harmonic functions
[
1
]
, we deduce that

\nabla\phi(x)=O(|x|^{-1})
for
d=2
.
In the case
d\geq 3
, we obtain
\nabla\phi(x)=O(|x|^{2-d})
.
It follows that
\phi(x)=O(\log|x|)
for
d=3
and
\phi(x)=O(1)
for
d\geq 4
.
Since
\phi
is harmonic, by the expansion theorem, this implies that
\phi(x)=L+O(|x|^{2-d})
for some
L\in\mathbb{C}
and that

\nabla\phi(x)=O(|x|^{1-d})
as
|x|\to\infty
for
d\geq 3
.
As a result, we have proved that
\nabla\phi(x)=O(|x|^{1-d})
as
|x|\to\infty
for
d\geq 2
.

Next, note that since
\partial\Omega
is
C^{1}
and
n\cdot\nabla\phi=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we have
\nabla\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega\cap B(0,R);\mathbb{C}^{d})
for any
R>1
.
Moreover, for
R
sufficiently large,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap B(0,R)}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B(0,R)}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}(\phi-\beta)

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\partial B(0,R))}\|\phi-\beta\|_{L^{2}(\partial B(0,R))}\leq CR\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B(0,R))},

where
\beta=\fint_{\partial B(0,R)}\phi
and we have used a Poincaré inequality on
\partial B(0,R)
.
By letting
R\to\infty
and using
\nabla\phi(x)=O(|x|^{1-d})
as
|x|\to\infty
for
d\geq 2
,
we see that
\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0
if
d\geq 3
and

\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\infty
if
d=2
.
As a result,
\nabla\phi=0
and
\phi
is constant in
\Omega
for
d\geq 3
.
Finally, to handle the case
d=2
, we use the Caccioppoli inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap B(0,R)}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}\int_{B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)}|\phi-\alpha|^{2}

(7.1)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\int_{B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)}|\nabla\phi|^{2},

for
R
large,
where
\alpha=\fint_{B(0,2R)\setminus B(0,R)}\phi
and we have used a Poincaré inequality.
It follows that

\int_{\Omega\cap B(0,R)}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{C_{0}+1}\int_{\Omega\cap B(0,2R)}|\nabla\phi|^{2}.

By letting
R\to\infty
, we obtain
\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c_{0}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
for some
c_{0}<1
.
This implies that
\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0
if
\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\infty
.
Consequently, we conclude that
\nabla\phi=0
and
\phi
is constant in
\Omega
for
d\geq 2
.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\Omega
be an exterior
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
. Let
1<q<d
and
\frac{1}{q_{*}}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{d}
.
Suppose that
u\in L^{q_{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
,
\nabla u\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})
,
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
, and

-\Delta u+\nabla p=0\quad\text{ and}\quad\text{\rm div}(u)=0

(7.2)

hold in
\Omega
in the sense of distributions. Then
u=0
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
7.1
for the case
d\geq 3
.
By the interior estimates for the Stokes equations,

|x||\nabla^{2}u(x)|+|\nabla u(x)|\leq C\left(\fint_{B(x,R/4)}|\nabla u|^{q}\right)^{1/q},

(7.3)

where
R=|x|
is sufficiently large. It follows from
|\nabla u|\in L^{q}(\Omega)
that

\nabla u(x)=o(|x|^{-\gamma})
as
|x|\to\infty
, where
\gamma=(d/q)
.
Since
\gamma>1
, this implies that
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u(x)
exists.
Using
u\in L^{q_{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
, we deduce that
u(x)=o(1)
as
|x|\to\infty
.
Also note that by the interior estimates,

\nabla^{2}u(x)=o(|x|^{-\gamma-1})
as
|x|\to\infty
.
Thus,
\nabla p(x)=o(|x|^{-\gamma-1})
.
It follows that
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}p(x)
exists.
By subtracting a constant, we may assume that
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}p(x)=0
.
As a result, we obtain
p(x)=o(|x|^{-\gamma})
as
|x|\to\infty
.

Next, assume
d\geq 3
. We use the Green representation formula for the Stokes equations in the domain
D_{R}=\{x:R_{0}<|x|<R\}
to write
(u(x),p(x))
as
a sum of layer potentials on
\partial D_{R}=\partial B(0,R)\cup\partial B(0,R_{0})
.
Since
|\nabla u(x)|+|p(x)|=o(|x|^{-\gamma})
, where
\gamma>1
, and
|u(x)|=o(1)
as
|x|\to\infty
, it is not hard to see that the
layer potentials on
\partial B(0,R)
converge to
0
as
R\to\infty
.
This allows to upgrade the decay of
(u,p)
at
\infty
to

|x|^{-1}|u(x)|+|\nabla u(x)|+|p(x)|=O(|x|^{1-d})\quad\text{ as }|x|\to\infty

(7.4)

for
d\geq 3
.

Finally, we note that since
\partial\Omega
is
C^{1}
and
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we have

u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega\cap B(0,R);\mathbb{C}^{d})
for any
R>1
.
Moreover, for
R>1
large,

\int_{\Omega\cap B(0,R)}|\nabla u|^{2}=\int_{\partial B(0,R)}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}-np\right)\cdot u.

In view of (
7.4
) for
d\geq 3
as well as the decay estimates,
u(x)=o(1)
and

|\nabla u(x)|+|p(x)|=o(|x|^{-\gamma})
for
d=2
, by letting
R\to\infty
,
we obtain
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0
.
Since
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
, it follows that
u=0
in
\Omega
.
∎

The following theorem is used in the proof of the estimate (
1.7
) for small
|\lambda|
.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\Omega
be an exterior
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
and
1<q<(d/2)
.
Let
u\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
be
a solution of

-\Delta u+\nabla p=F\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{\rm div}(u)=0

(7.5)

in
\Omega
, where
F\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
. Then
u\in W^{1,s}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
and

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)},

(7.6)

where
\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{d}
and
C
depends on
d
,
q
and
\Omega
.

Proof.

Since
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})\subset L^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
It suffices to prove (
7.6
). We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1. We show that the solution
u
satisfies the estimate,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{0})}\right\},

(7.7)

where
B_{0}=B(0,2R_{0})
and
R_{0}>1
is sufficiently large.
To this end, we choose
R_{0}>1
such that
\Omega\setminus B(0,R_{0})=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,R_{0})
and

\Omega\cap B(0,2R_{0})
is a bounded
C^{1}
domain.
Choose
\varphi_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
such that
\varphi_{1}=1
in
\Omega\setminus B(0,(3/2)R_{0})
and

\varphi_{1}=0
in
B(0,(5/4)R_{0})
.
Let
\varphi_{2}=1-\varphi_{1}
.
Then

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta(u\varphi_{1})+\nabla(p\varphi_{1})&=F\varphi_{1}-2(\nabla u)(\nabla\varphi_{1})-u\Delta\varphi_{1}+p\nabla\varphi_{1},\\
\text{\rm div}(u\varphi_{1})&=u\cdot\nabla\varphi_{1}\end{aligned}\right.

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
It follows from the
W^{2,q}
estimates
[
13
]
for the Stokes equations (with
\lambda=0
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that

\displaystyle\|\nabla(u\varphi_{1})\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\|F\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|(\nabla u)(\nabla\varphi_{1})\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

(7.8)

\displaystyle+\|u\Delta\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|p\nabla\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\nabla(u\nabla\varphi_{1})\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big\}.

Let
\Omega_{0}=\Omega\cap B(0,2R_{0})
.
Note that
u\varphi_{2}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{0}=\partial\Omega\cup\partial B(0,2R_{0})
and

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta(u\varphi_{2})+\nabla(p\varphi_{2})&=F\varphi_{2}-2(\nabla u)(\nabla\varphi_{2})-u\Delta\varphi_{2}+p\nabla\varphi_{2},\\
\text{\rm div}(u\varphi_{2})&=u\cdot\nabla\varphi_{2}\end{aligned}\right.

in
\Omega_{0}
.
It follows from the
W^{1,q}
estimates for the Stokes equations (with
\lambda=0
) in the
C^{1}
domain
\Omega_{0}
that

\displaystyle\|\nabla(u\varphi_{2})\|_{L^{s}(\Omega_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\|F\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{0})}+\|(\nabla u)(\nabla\varphi_{2})\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{0})}+\|u\Delta\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{0})}

(7.9)

\displaystyle+\|p\nabla\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{0})}+\|u\nabla\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{0})}\Big\}.

See Remark
5.7
.
The estimate (
7.7
) follows from (
7.8
) and (
7.9
) as well as the interior estimates for
the Stokes equations.

Step 2. We establish the estimate (
7.6
) by a compactness argument.

Suppose (
7.6
) is not true.
Then there exist sequences
\{F^{\ell}\}\subset L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
,
\{u^{\ell}\}\subset W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})\cap W^{1,s}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
, such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u^{\ell}+\nabla p^{\ell}&=F^{\ell},\\
\text{\rm div}(u^{\ell})&=0\end{aligned}\right.

(7.10)

hold in
\Omega
for some
p^{\ell}\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
,

\|\nabla u^{\ell}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}=1,

(7.11)

and
\|F^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\to 0
, as
\ell\to\infty
.
Since
\|u^{\ell}\|_{L^{s_{*}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u^{\ell}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}=C
, where
\frac{1}{s_{*}}=\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{d}
,
by passing to a subsequence, we may assume
u^{\ell}\to u
weakly in
L^{s_{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
and

\nabla u^{\ell}\to\nabla u
weakly in
L^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})
.
It follows that
u
is a solution of (
7.5
) with
F=0
.
Note that
q<(d/2)
implies
s=q_{*}<d
.
Thus, by Lemma
7.2
,
u=0
in
\Omega
.
This implies that
u^{\ell}\to 0
strongly in
L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{0};\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
However, by (
7.7
),

\|\nabla u^{\ell}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\{\|F^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|u^{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{0})}\right\},

which leads to a contradiction with (
7.11
) if we let
\ell\to\infty
.
∎

Recall that

C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d}):\text{\rm div}(u)=0\text{ in }\Omega\right\}.

Let
L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
denote the closure of
C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
and

G_{q}(\Omega)=\left\{u:u=\nabla p\text{ for some }p\in\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})\right\}.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded or exterior domain with
C^{1}
boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
. Then

L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})=L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\oplus G_{q}(\Omega)

(7.12)

for
1<q<\infty
. That is, for any
u\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
, there exists a unique
(v,w)\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\times G_{q}(\Omega)
such that
u=v+w
in
\Omega
and

\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|w\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)},

(7.13)

where
C
depends on
d
,
q
and
\Omega
.

The formula (
7.12
) is referred to as the Helmholtz decomposition,
which is well known in the case of bounded or exterior domains with smooth boundaries (see
[
10
]
for references).
In the case of bounded or exterior domains with
C^{1}
boundaries, a sketch of the proof for (
7.12
)
may be found in
[
10
]
. Also see
[
9
]
.
The decomposition also holds for
1<q<\infty
if
\Omega
is a bounded convex domain
[
15
]
.
If
\Omega
is a bounded or exterior domain with Lipschitz boundaries,
the Helmholtz decomposition (
7.12
) holds if

\left\{\begin{aligned} (3/2)-\varepsilon<&q<3+\varepsilon&\quad&\text{ for }d\geq 3,\\
(4/3)-\varepsilon<&q<4+\varepsilon&\quad&\text{ for }d=2,\end{aligned}\right.

(7.14)

where
\varepsilon>0
depends on
\Omega
. The ranges in (
7.14
) are known to be sharp. See
[
9
]
.
We remark that Theorem
7.4
is not used in this paper.

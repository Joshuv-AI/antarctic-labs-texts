A Hofmann-Mislove theorem for approach spaces
Junche Yu, Dexue Zhang

Abstract
The Hofmann-Mislove theorem says that the ordered set of open filters of the open-set lattice of a sober topological space is isomorphic to the ordered set of compact saturated sets (ordered by reverse inclusion) of that space. This paper concerns a metric analogy of this result. To this end, the notion of compact functions of approach spaces is introduced. Such functions are an analog of compact subsets in the enriched context. It is shown that for a sober approach space X X , the metric space of proper open [ 0 , ∞ ] [0,\infty] -filters of the metric space of upper regular functions of X X is isomorphic to the opposite of the metric space of inhabited and saturated compact functions of X X , establishing a Hofmann-Mislove theorem for approach spaces. Keywords Approach space, compact function, sober approach space, metric space, [ 0 , ∞ ] [0,\infty] -ideal, open [ 0 , ∞ ] [0,\infty] -filter MSC(2020) 18B35, 18F60, 54A05, 54B30

In this section we recall some basic notions about metric spaces, viewed as categories enriched over Lawvere’s quantale
([0,\infty],\geq,+,0)

[
18
,
4
]
, the aim is to fix terminologies and notations. Only Proposition
2.11
and Proposition

are new.

A metric space is a pair
(X,d)
consisting of a set
X
and a map
d\colon X\times X\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
, called a metric on
X
, such that
d(x,x)=0
and
d(x,y)+d(y,z)\geq d(x,z)
for all
x,y,z\in X
. For each metric
d
on
X
,
d^{\rm op}(x,y)\coloneqq d(y,x)
is also a metric on
X
, called the opposite of
d
. A metric
d
is

•

symmetric, if
d=d^{\rm op}
; that is,
d(x,y)=d(y,x)
for all
x,y\in X
;

•

separated, if
x=y
whenever
d(x,y)=d(y,x)=0
;

•

finitary, if
d(x,y)<\infty
for all
x,y\in X
.

A “classical metric” is just a symmetric, separated and finitary one.

A map
f\colon(X,d_{X})\longrightarrow(Y,d_{Y})
between metric spaces is
non-expansive
if, for all
x,y\in X
,

d_{X}(x,y)\geq d_{Y}(f(x),f(y)).

A map
f\colon(X,d_{X})\longrightarrow(Y,d_{Y})
between metric spaces is
isometric
if, for all
x,y\in X
,

d_{X}(x,y)=d_{Y}(f(x),f(y)).

Non-expansive maps are just
[0,\infty]
-functors when metric spaces are viewed as
[0,\infty]
-categories.
Metric spaces and non-expansive maps form a category

\sf Met.

For all
a,b\in[0,\infty]
, let

d_{L}(a,b)=b\ominus a\coloneqq\max\{0,b-a\}.

Then
d_{L}
is a metric on
[0,\infty]
. The opposite of
d_{L}
is denoted by
d_{R}
; that is,

d_{R}(a,b)=a\ominus b.

Both
{([0,\infty],d_{L})}
an
{([0,\infty],d_{R})}
are separated, non-symmetric, and non-finitary metric spaces. We note here that the operations
\ominus,+\colon[0,\infty]\times[0,\infty]\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
are interlocked by the adjoint property:

a\ominus b\leq c\iff a\leq b+c

for all
a,b,c\in[0,\infty]
.

The category
\sf Met
is complete and cocomplete.
In particular, for every set
X
, the product metric on the
X
-power of
{([0,\infty],d_{L})}
is given by

\rho_{X}\colon[0,\infty]^{X}\times[0,\infty]^{X}\longrightarrow[0,\infty],\quad\rho_{X}(\lambda,\mu)=\sup_{x\in X}\mu(x)\ominus\lambda(x).

The
underlying order

\sqsubseteq_{d}
of a metric space
(X,d)
refers to
the order relation on
X
defined by

x\sqsubseteq_{d}y\quad\text{if $d(x,y)=0.$}

It is clear that a metric space is separated if and only if its underlying order is a partial order and that any non-expansive map preserves the underlying order.

The underlying order
\sqsubseteq
of
([0,\infty]^{X},\rho_{X})
is given by

\lambda\sqsubseteq\mu\iff\forall x\in X,\lambda(x)\geq\mu(x).

Particularly, the underlying order of
([0,\infty],d_{L})
is opposite to the usual less-than-or-equal-to relation.

Proposition 3.13

\displaystyle b\ominus((\sup_{j\in J}\phi_{j})\cpt\theta)

\displaystyle=\rho_{X}(\theta,b\ominus\sup_{j\in J}\phi_{j})

If

\displaystyle(\sup_{j\in J}\phi_{j})\cpt\theta

\displaystyle=\sup_{r\in[0,\infty)}((\sup_{j\in J}\inf\{r_{X},\phi_{j}\})\cpt\theta)

\displaystyle=\sup_{r\in[0,\infty)}\sup_{j\in J}(\inf\{r_{X},\phi_{j}\}\cpt\theta)

\displaystyle=\sup_{j\in J}\sup_{r\in[0,\infty)}(\inf\{r_{X},\phi_{j}\}\cpt\theta)

\displaystyle\rho_{X}(\theta,\inf_{i\in I}\lambda_{i})

\displaystyle=b\ominus((b\ominus\inf_{i\in I}\lambda_{i})\cpt\theta)

(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
This follows from the dual of Proposition

In the following we show that compact functions in approach spaces are an
extension
of compact subsets in topological spaces and totally bounded subsets in metric spaces.

For each topological space
X
, the map

\delta_{X}\colon X\times 2^{X}\longrightarrow[0,\infty],\quad\delta_{X}(x,A)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $x$ is in the closure of $A$},\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

is an approach structure on the set
X
. The correspondence
X\mapsto\omega(X)\coloneqq(X,\delta_{X})
defines a full and faithful functor

\omega\colon\sf Top\longrightarrow\sf App.

Approach spaces of the form
\omega(X)
are said to be
topologically generated

[
23
,
24
]
. It is clear that a function
\phi\colon X\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
is a lower regular function of
\omega(X)
if and only if
\phi
is lower semicontinuous; and that
\lambda\colon X\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
is an upper regular function of
\omega(X)
if and only if either
\lambda=\infty_{X}
or
\lambda
is bounded and upper semicontinuous.

For a topological space
X
, it is shown in
[

, Proposition 4.3.11]
that the approach space
\omega(X)
is compact if and only if the topological space
X
is compact. This fact together with Proposition
3.14
yields the following proposition, which shows that the notion of compact functions of approach spaces extends that of compact subsets of topological spaces.

Proposition 3.22
.

Let

be a topological space and
A
a subset of
X
. Then

is a compact function of
\omega(X)
if and only if
A
is a compact subset of
X
.

Proposition 3.23
.

Let
X
be a topological space and let
\theta\colon X\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
be a lower semicontinuous function. Then
\theta
is a compact function of
\omega(X)
if and only if
\theta^{-1}([0,r])
is a compact subset of

.

Proof.

We check the necessity first. Let

. Since
\theta
is lower semicontinuous,

is a closed set in X. Let
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a filtered family of non-empty closed sets of
A
. Since
A
is closed, it follows that
\{0_{F_{i}}\}_{i\in I}
is a directed family of lower regular functions of
\omega(X)
, hence

(\sup_{i\in I}0_{F_{i}})\cpt\theta=\sup_{i\in I}(0_{F_{i}}\cpt\theta)\leq r,

which shows that
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
has a nonempty intersection and consequently,

is compact.

As for sufficiency, it suffices to prove that, for each directed family of lower semicontinuous functions
\{\phi_{i}\colon X\longrightarrow[0,\infty]\}_{i\in I}

(\sup_{i\in I}\phi_{i})\cpt\theta\leq\sup_{i\in I}(\phi_{i}\cpt\theta).

If
\sup_{i\in I}(\phi_{i}\cpt\theta)<r<\infty

\inf_{x\in X}(\phi_{i}(x)+\theta(x))<r.

Let

for each

. By lower semicontinuity of

and
\phi_{i}
, one sees that
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a filtered family of non-empty closed sets contained in the closed set

. Since

is compact, then

(\sup_{i\in I}\phi_{i})\cpt\theta=\inf_{x\in X}(\sup_{i\in I}\phi_{i}(x)+\theta(x))\leq r

and consequently,

(\sup_{i\in I}\phi_{i})\cpt\theta\leq\sup_{i\in I}(\phi_{i}\cpt\theta)

as desired.

(X,d)
.
Then, by the dual statement of the Yoneda lemma (Lemma
2.2
) one sees that for each
x\in X
,
d(x,-)\colon X\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
is a compact function of
(X,\Gamma(d))
. So, by Proposition
3.15
(i), for each finite subset
F
of
X

is a compact function of
(X,\Gamma(d))
.

A metric space
(X,d)
is
precompact
if for each
r>0
, there exists a finite subset
A
of
X
, such that

X=\bigcup_{x\in A}B(x,r),

where
B(x,r)=\{y\in X\mid d(x,y)<r\}
. A subset

is called a precompact subset if
(K,d)
is a precompact metric space.

Proposition 3.25
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
K
a subset of
X
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

K
is a precompact subset of
(X,d)
.

(2)

d(K,-)
is a compact function of
(X,\Gamma(d))
.

(3)

is a compact function of
(X,\Gamma(d))
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
Since
K
is a precompact subset of
X
, for each
r>0
there exists a finite subset
F
of
K
such that,

K\subseteq\bigcup_{x\in F}B(x,r).

It follows that

d(K,y)\leq d(F,y)<d(K,y)+r

for each
y\in X
. Since
F
is finite,
d(F,-)
is a compact function, so,

is compact by Proposition
3.17
.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
This follows from that for each weight
\phi
of
(X,d)
,

\displaystyle\phi\cpt d(K,-)

\displaystyle=\inf_{x\in X}(\phi(x)+d(K,x))

\displaystyle=\inf_{x\in K}\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\phi\cpt 0_{K}.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
Suppose on the contrary that
K
is not precompact. Then there exists

such that

for any finite subset

of

(\sup_{x\in K}\phi_{x})\cpt 0_{K}=\inf_{y\in K}\sup_{x\in K}\phi_{x}(y)\geq r.

of

which contradicts the compactness of

A Hofmann-Mislove theorem

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(X,\delta)
be an approach space and let
\Lambda
be a
[0,\infty]
-filter of the metric space
(\mathfrak{U}X,\rho_{X})
of upper regular functions of
(X,\delta)
. We say that
\Lambda
is

(i)

proper if
\Lambda(r_{X})=r
for each
r\in[0,\infty]
;

(ii)

decent if
\Lambda(\lambda)\neq 0
for some
\lambda\neq\infty_{X}
.

For each approach space
(X,\delta)
with
X
being non-empty, the metric space
(\mathfrak{U}X,\rho_{X})
has exactly two
[0,\infty]
-filters that are not decent, both of them are open. The first is

\rho_{X}(\infty_{X},-)
and the second is given by

\Lambda(\lambda)=\begin{cases}0,&\lambda\neq\infty_{X},\\
\infty,&\lambda=\infty_{X}.\end{cases}

The following proposition is obvious.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\theta
be a compact saturated function of an approach space
(X,\delta)
.

(i)

If
\theta
is inhabited then
\rho_{X}(\theta,-)
is a proper open
[0,\infty]
-filter of
(\mathfrak{U}X,\rho_{X})
.

(ii)

If
\theta
is non-vacuous then
\rho_{X}(\theta,-)
is a decent open
[0,\infty]
-filter of
(\mathfrak{U}X,\rho_{X})
.

Our aim is to show that for a sober approach space
(X,\delta)
, every proper open
[0,\infty]
-filter of
(\mathfrak{U}X,\rho_{X})
is of the form
\rho_{X}(\theta,-)
for a unique inhabited and saturated compact function.

A lower regular function
\phi
of an approach space
(X,\delta)
is said to be
irreducible
if for all lower regular functions
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
,

\inf\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\}\leq\phi\implies\text{either $\phi_{1}\leq\phi$ or $\phi_{2}\leq\phi$}.

Definition 4.3
.

(
[
2
]
)
An approach space
(X,\delta)
is sober if for each inhabited and irreducible lower regular function
\phi
of
(X,\delta)
, there is a unique
x\in X
such that
\phi=\delta(-,\{x\})
.

Example 4.4
.

(a)

(
[
2
]
) For each topological space

, the approach space

(b)

(
[

]
) For each metric space

.

An approach space

Consider the lower regular function

Now we state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(X,\delta)
be a sober approach space.

(i)

For each proper open
[0,\infty]
-filter
\Lambda
of
(\mathfrak{U}X,\rho_{X})
, there is a unique inhabited compact saturated function
\nu
of
(X,\delta)
such that
\Lambda=\rho_{X}(\nu,-)
.

(ii)

For each decent open
[0,\infty]
-filter
\Lambda
of
(\mathfrak{U}X,\rho_{X})
, there is a unique non-vacuous compact saturated function
\mu
of
(X,\delta)
such that
\Lambda=\rho_{X}(\mu,-)
.

Therefore, for a sober approach space
(X,\delta)
, there is a bijection between proper (decent, resp.) open
[0,\infty]
-filters of the metric space of upper regular functions of
(X,\delta)
and inhabited (non-vacuous, resp.) compact saturated functions of
(X,\delta)
. Furthermore, this bijection is an isomorphism between metric spaces.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(X,\delta)
be an approach space. If
\lambda
is a compact saturated function of
(X,\delta)
, then the map

\Phi\colon\mathfrak{L}X\longrightarrow[0,\infty],\quad\Phi(\phi)=\phi\cpt\lambda

satisfies the following conditions:

(C1)

For each directed subset
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i}
of
\mathfrak{L}X
,
\Phi(\sup\limits_{i\in I}\phi_{i})=\sup\limits_{i\in I}\Phi(\phi_{i})
.

(C2)

For all
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathfrak{L}X
,
\Phi(\inf\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\})=\min\{\Phi(\phi_{1}),\Phi(\phi_{2})\}
.

(C3)

For all
\phi\in\mathfrak{L}X
and
r\in[0,\infty]
,
\Phi(\phi+r)=\Phi(\phi)+r
.

Conversely, if
(X,\delta)
is a sober approach space, then for each map
\Phi\colon\mathfrak{L}X\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
that satisfies
(C1)-(C3)
, there is a compact saturated function
\lambda
of
(X,\delta)
such that
\Phi(\phi)=\phi\cpt\lambda
.

Proof.

We leave it to the reader to check that for each compact saturated function
\lambda
of
(X,\delta)
, the map
\Phi(\phi)=\phi\cpt\lambda
satisfies (C1)-(C3).

For the converse part, suppose that
\Phi\colon\mathfrak{L}X\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
satisfies (C1)-(C3).

First of all, by (C2), (C3) and Proposition
2.3
one sees that
\Phi\colon(\mathfrak{L}X,\rho_{X})\longrightarrow([0,\infty],d_{L})
is a non-expansive map. We claim that the function

\lambda\colon X\longrightarrow[0,\infty],\quad\lambda(x)=\Phi(\delta(-,\{x\}))

satisfies the requirement; that is,
\lambda
is a compact saturated function of
(X,\delta)
and
\Phi(\phi)=\phi\cpt\lambda
.

Since

\lambda(x)+\delta(x,\{y\})=\Phi(\delta(-,\{x\}))+\rho_{X}(\delta(-,\{x\}),\delta(-,\{y\}))\geq\Phi(\delta(-,\{y\}))=\lambda(y),

it follows that
\lambda
is saturated.

Next we show that for each
\phi\in\mathfrak{L}X
,
\Phi(\phi)=\phi\cpt\lambda
. This equality together with (C1) implies that
\lambda
is compact. On one hand,

\displaystyle\phi\cpt\lambda

\displaystyle=\inf_{x\in X}(\phi(x)+\Phi(\delta(-,\{x\})))

\displaystyle=\inf_{x\in X}(\rho_{X}(\delta(-,\{x\}),\phi)+\Phi(\delta(-,\{x\})))

(Equation (
3.i
))

\displaystyle\geq\Phi(\phi).

On the other hand, for each
r\in[0,\infty)
with
\Phi(\phi)\leq r
, let

\mathcal{A}_{r}=\{\xi\in\mathfrak{L}X\mid\phi\leq\xi,\Phi(\xi)\leq r\}.

By (C1) and Zorn’s lemma,
\mathcal{A}_{r}
has a maximal element, say
\psi
. By (C2) one sees that
\psi
is an irreducible lower regular function; by (C3) one sees that
\psi\neq\infty_{X}
. Since
(X,\delta)
is sober, there exist a unique
x
and a unique
s\in[0,\infty)
such that
\psi=s+\delta(-,\{x\})
. Since
\psi\in\mathcal{A}_{r}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\phi\cpt\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\psi\cpt\lambda

\displaystyle=\inf_{z\in X}(s+\delta(z,\{x\})+\Phi(\delta(-,\{z\})))

\displaystyle=s+\inf_{z\in X}\Big(\rho_{X}(\delta(-,\{z\}),\delta(-,\{x\}))+\Phi(\delta(-,\{z\}))\Big)

\displaystyle=s+\Phi(\delta(-,\{x\}))

(
\Phi
is non-expansive)

\displaystyle=\Phi(\psi)

(C3)

\displaystyle\leq r.

Therefore,
\phi\cpt\lambda\leq\Phi(\phi)
by arbitrariness of
r
. ∎

Proof of Theorem
4.6
.

(i) Suppose that
\Lambda\colon\mathfrak{U}X\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
is a proper open
[0,\infty]
-filter of
(\mathfrak{U}X,\rho_{X})
. First of all, we assert that
\Lambda
satisfies that

\Lambda(\lambda+r)=\Lambda(\lambda)+r

for all
\lambda\in\mathfrak{U}X
and
r\in[0,\infty]
. If
\lambda=\infty_{X}
, the equality is obvious; if
\lambda
is bounded, then by Proposition
2.12
we have that
\Lambda(\lambda+r)\geq r
and

\Lambda(\lambda)=\Lambda((\lambda+r)\ominus r)=\Lambda(\lambda+r)-r,

hence
\Lambda(\lambda+r)=\Lambda(\lambda)+r
.

We turn to the proof of the conclusion. Uniqueness of
\nu
follows from Corollary
3.7
; as for existence, define a map
\Phi\colon\mathfrak{L}X\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
as follows: for each lower regular function
\phi
of
(X,\delta)
, let

\Phi(\phi)=\sup_{x\in X}\phi(x)\ominus\Lambda\Big(\sup_{x\in X}\phi(x)\ominus\phi\Big)

if
\phi
is bounded; and let

\Phi(\phi)=\sup_{r\in[0,\infty)}\Phi(\inf\{\phi,r_{X}\})

if
\phi
is not bounded.

Before proceeding, we notice that, as a consequence of the equality
\Lambda(\lambda+r)=\Lambda(\lambda)+r
, for each bounded
\phi
and each bound
b
of
\phi
, it holds that

\Phi(\phi)=b-\Lambda(b-\phi).

We claim that
\Phi\colon\mathfrak{L}X\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
satisfies the conditions (C1)-(C3) in Lemma
4.7
. Here we check (C1) for example. Let
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a directed family of lower regular functions. If
\sup_{i\in I}\phi_{i}
is bounded, pick an upper bound
b
of all
\phi_{i}
, then

\displaystyle\Phi(\sup_{i\in I}\phi_{i})

\displaystyle=b\ominus\Lambda(b\ominus\sup_{i\in I}\phi_{i})

\displaystyle=b\ominus\Lambda(\inf_{i\in I}b\ominus\phi_{i})

\displaystyle=b\ominus\inf_{i\in I}\Lambda(b\ominus\phi_{i})

\displaystyle=\sup_{i\in I}b\ominus\Lambda(b\ominus\phi_{i})

\displaystyle=\sup_{i\in I}\Phi(\phi_{i}).

If
\sup_{i\in I}\phi_{i}
is not bounded, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(\sup_{i\in I}\phi_{i})

\displaystyle=\sup_{r\in[0,\infty)}\Phi(\inf\{\sup_{i\in I}\phi_{i},r_{X}\})

\displaystyle=\sup_{r\in[0,\infty)}\Phi(\sup_{i\in I}\inf\{\phi_{i},r_{X}\})

\displaystyle=\sup_{r\in[0,\infty)}\sup_{i\in I}\Phi(\inf\{\phi_{i},r_{X}\})

\displaystyle=\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{r\in[0,\infty)}\Phi(\inf\{\phi_{i},r_{X}\})

\displaystyle=\sup_{i\in I}\Phi(\phi_{i}).

Since
(X,\delta)
is sober, by Lemma
4.7
, there is a compact saturated function
\nu
of
(X,\delta)
such that
\Phi(\phi)=\phi\cpt\nu
. We show that
\nu
satisfies the requirement. First, it follows from
0_{X}\cpt\nu=\Phi(0_{X})=0
that
\nu
is inhabited. Second, we show that
\rho_{X}(\nu,\lambda)=\Lambda(\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in\mathfrak{U}X
. The equality is trivial if
\lambda=\infty_{X}
; if
\lambda
is bounded, pick a bound
b
of
\lambda
, then

\displaystyle\rho_{X}(\nu,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\rho_{X}(\nu,b\ominus(b\ominus\lambda))

\displaystyle=b\ominus((b\ominus\lambda)\cpt\nu)

(Lemma
2.7
)

\displaystyle=b\ominus\Phi(b\ominus\lambda)

\displaystyle=b\ominus(b-\Lambda(b-(b-\lambda)))

(
b
is a bound of
b-\lambda
)

\displaystyle=b-(b-\Lambda(\lambda))

\displaystyle(\Lambda(\lambda)\leq b)

\displaystyle=\Lambda(\lambda).

(ii) Suppose
\Lambda\colon\mathfrak{U}X\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
is a decent open
[0,\infty]
-filter of
(\mathfrak{U}X,\rho_{X})
. Let

s=\sup\{r\in[0,\infty)\mid\Lambda(r_{X})=0\}.

Since
\Lambda
is decent,
s<\infty
. Then for each
r\in[0,\infty)
,
\Lambda(r_{X}+s)=r
. Hence the map

\Lambda^{\prime}\colon\mathfrak{U}X\longrightarrow[0,\infty],\quad\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)=\Lambda(\lambda+s)

is a proper open
[0,\infty]
-filter of
(\mathfrak{U}X,\rho_{X})
. By (i), there exists a unique inhabited compact saturated function
\nu
of
(X,\delta)
such that
\Lambda^{\prime}=\rho_{X}(\nu,-)
. Since for each
\lambda\in\mathfrak{U}X
we have

\displaystyle\Lambda(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Lambda((\lambda+s)\ominus s)

\displaystyle=\Lambda(\lambda+s)\ominus s

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)\ominus s

\displaystyle=\rho_{X}(\nu,\lambda)\ominus s

\displaystyle=\rho_{X}(\nu+s,\lambda),

then
\mu\coloneqq\nu+s
is a compact saturated function of
(X,\delta)
such that
\Lambda=\rho_{X}(\mu,-)
.

\theta

\lambda

\rho_{X}(\theta,\lambda)=\rho_{\mathfrak{U}X}(\rho_{X}(\lambda,-),\rho_{X}(\theta,-)).

For this we only need to check that
\rho_{X}(\theta,\lambda)\leq\rho_{\mathfrak{U}X}(\rho_{X}(\lambda,-),\rho_{X}(\theta,-))
. To this end, we show that for each

. Assume that

\displaystyle\rho_{X}(\theta,\mu)

\displaystyle=\lambda(x)\ominus(\delta(-,\{x\})\cpt\theta)

\displaystyle=\lambda(x)\ominus\theta(x)

The Hofmann-Mislove theorem for topological spaces is contained in Theorem
4.6
. To see this, let
X
be a sober topological space, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a proper open filter of the open-set lattice of
X
.
It is readily verified that

\Lambda\colon\mathfrak{U}(\omega(X))\longrightarrow[0,\infty],\quad\Lambda(\lambda)=\inf\{\rho_{X}(0_{U},\lambda)\mid U\in\mathcal{F}\}

is a proper open
[0,\infty]
-filter of the metric space of upper regular functions of the approach space
\omega(X)
. Since
\omega(X)
is sober, there is a unique inhabited and compact saturated function
\mu
of
\omega(X)
such that

\Lambda(\lambda)=\rho_{X}(\mu,\lambda)

for all
\lambda\in\mathfrak{U}(\omega(X))
. We leave it to the reader to check that
\mu
takes value either
0
or
\infty
. Let

Q=\{x\in X\mid\mu(x)=0\}.

Then
Q
is a compact saturated subset of
X
such that for each open set
U
of
X
,

Q\subseteq U
if and only if
U\in\mathcal{F}
. This gives the Hofmann-Mislove theorem for topological spaces:

Corollary 4.8
.

(Hofmann-Mislove theorem for topological spaces)

Let
X
be a sober topological space, the ordered set of open filters of the open-set lattice and the ordered set (ordered by reverse inclusion) of compact saturated subsets of
X
are isomorphic.

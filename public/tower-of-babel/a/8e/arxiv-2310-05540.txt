On splitting bi-unitary perfect polynomials over 𝔽 p 2 \mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
Luis H. Gallardo, Olivier Rahavandrainy

Abstract




Mathematics Subject Classification (2010): 11T55, 11T06.

Abstract

We give all splitting bi-unitary perfect polynomials
over the field
\mathbb{F}_{4}
and some splitting ones over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, if
p
is an odd prime.

1
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number and let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field of
characteristic
p
with
q
elements.
Let
S\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a nonzero polynomial.
A divisor
D
of
S
is called unitary if
\gcd(D,S/D)=1
. We designate by
\gcd_{u}(S,T)
the greatest
common unitary divisor of
S
and
T
.
A divisor
D
of
S
is called bi-unitary if
\gcd_{u}(D,S/D)=1
.
We denote by
\sigma(S)
(resp.
\sigma^{*}(S)
,
\sigma^{**}(S)
) the sum of all divisors
(resp. unitary divisors, bi-unitary divisors)
of
S
.
The functions
\sigma
,
\sigma^{*}
and
\sigma^{**}
are all multiplicative.
We say that
S
is
perfect
(resp.
unitary perfect
,

bi-unitary perfect
) if
\sigma(S)=S

(resp.
\sigma^{*}(S)=S
,
\sigma^{**}(S)=S
).
As usual,
\omega(S)
designates the number of distinct irreducible
factors of
S
.

We denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of non-negative
integers and by
\mathbb{N}^{*}
the set of positive integers.

Several studies are done about perfect, unitary and bi-unitary perfect polynomials (see
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
,

[

]
,
[

]
,
[
14
]
and references therein).

In this paper, we are interested in splitting polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
which are bi-unitary perfect (b.u.p.). We treat the case where
p=2
in Section

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
T
be an irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
and
k,l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.
Then,
\gcd_{u}(T^{k},T^{l})=1\ (
resp.
T^{k})
if
k\not=l\ (
resp.
k=l)
.

In particular,
\gcd_{u}(T^{k},T^{2n-k})=1
for
k\not=n
,
\gcd_{u}(T^{k},T^{2n+1-k})=1
for
any
0\leq k\leq 2n+1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
T\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}[x]
be irreducible. Then

i)
\sigma^{**}(T^{2n})=(1+T^{n+1})\sigma(T^{n-1}),\ \sigma^{**}(T^{2n+1})=\sigma(T^{2n+1})
.

ii) For any
c\in\mathbb{N}
,
T
does not divide
\sigma^{**}(T^{c})
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
A=A_{1}A_{2}
is b.u.p. over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
and if

\gcd(A_{1},A_{2})=~1
, then
A_{1}
is b.u.p. if and only if

A_{2}
is b.u.p.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
A
is b.u.p. over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, then the polynomial
A(x+t)

is also b.u.p. over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, for any
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

i)
\sigma^{**}(x^{2k+1})
splits over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
if and only if
2k+1=N\cdot 2^{n}-1
where
N\in\{1,3\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

ii)
\sigma^{**}(x^{2k})
splits over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
if and only if
2k\in\{2,4,6\}
.

Proof.

i) holds since
\displaystyle{\sigma^{**}(x^{2k+1})=\sigma(x^{2k+1})=\frac{x^{2k+2}-1}{x-1}}
must split over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
.

ii): One has
\sigma^{**}(x^{2k})=(1+x^{k+1})\cdot\sigma(x^{k-1})
,
\sigma(x^{k-1})

splits if and only if
k=N\cdot 2^{n}
where
N\in\{1,3\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Now,
1+x^{k+1}
splits if and only if
2N\cdot 2^{n}+2=2k+2=M\cdot 2^{m}
, where
M\in\{1,3\}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Therefore,
2^{n+1}+2
and
3\cdot 2^{n+1}+2
belong to the set
\{2^{m},3\cdot 2^{m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}
. So, (
N=1
,
n=0
and
m=2
) or (
N=1
,
n=1
and
m=1
) or (
N=3
,
n=0
and
m=3
). Thus,
k\in\{1,2,3\}
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

i)
\sigma^{**}(x^{2k+1})

\sigma^{**}(x^{2k})

Proof.

i) holds since
\displaystyle{\sigma^{**}(x^{2k+1})=\sigma(x^{2k+1})=\frac{x^{2k+2}-1}{x-1}}
must split over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.

ii): One has
\sigma^{**}(x^{2k})=(1+x^{k+1})\cdot\sigma(x^{k-1})
,
\sigma(x^{k-1})

splits if and only if
k=Np^{n}
where
N
divides
p^{2}-1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Now,
1+x^{k+1}
splits if and only if
2Np^{n}+2=2k+2=Mp^{m}
, where
M
divides
p^{2}-1
and

.

- If
n,m\geq 1
, then

- If

- If

.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

One has
\Omega_{2}=\{p\}
,
\Omega_{3}=\{p-1\}
and
\Omega_{4}=\{p^{2}-1\}
.

Proof.

We clearly see that
p\in\Omega_{2},\ p-1\in\Omega_{3}
and
p^{2}-1\in\Omega_{4}
. Now, if
pN\in\Omega_{2}
, we prove that
N=1
. Since
pN+1
divides
(p-1)(p+1)
, we write
(p-1)(p+1)=(pN+1)\cdot\omega
. Thus,
\omega\equiv-1\mod p
. Set
w=\lambda p-1
. One has
(p-1)(p+1)=(pN+1)(\lambda p-1)
with
\lambda,N\geq 1
. It follows that
\lambda=N=1
and
p=pN\in\Omega_{2}
.

If
N\in\Omega_{3}
, then
M
must be even,
\displaystyle{N+1=\frac{M}{2}p}
with
N
and
M
divide
p^{2}-1
. Thus,
\displaystyle{(p-1)(p+1)=N\omega=(\frac{M}{2}p-1)\omega}
and
\omega\equiv 1\mod p
. Set
w=\lambda p+1
. One has
\displaystyle{(p-1)(p+1)=(\frac{M}{2}p-1)(\lambda p+1)}
with
\displaystyle{\lambda,\frac{M}{2}\geq 1}
. It follows that
\displaystyle{\lambda=\frac{M}{2}=1}
and
p-1=N\in\Omega_{3}
.

Finally, if
N\in\Omega_{4}
, then
2p^{2}\geq 2N+2=Mp^{2}
,
M\leq 2
and thus
M=2
,
p^{2}-1=N\in\Omega_{4}
.
∎

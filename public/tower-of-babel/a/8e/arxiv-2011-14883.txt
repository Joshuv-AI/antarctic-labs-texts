Birefringence of electromagnetic waves in the relic neutrino gas
Victor B. Semikoz

Abstract
We reconsider the problem of the birefringence of electromagnetic (EM) waves in a medium consisting of a plasma and a ν ​ ν ¯ \nu\bar{\nu} -gas within the Standard Model of particle physics. The considered effect arises in such a medium due to the parity violation for the electroweak neutrino-electron interaction. Our recent calculations of the electroweak correction to the photon polarization operator in the electroweak plasma allow us to significantly improve some previous estimates of such effect in astrophysics. We estimate the rotary power for EM waves propagating in a non-relativistic plasma in the intergalactic space and interacting with the gas of relic neutrinos and antineutrinos there. We show that, in presence of a plasma, the EM wave birefringence effect in a ν ​ ν ¯ \nu\bar{\nu} -gas exceeds significantly that effect in a ν ​ ν ¯ \nu\bar{\nu} -gas in empty space considered earlier. These previous treatments of the birefringence relied on the calculations of the refraction index for on-shell photons in vacuum using the forward scattering amplitude γ ​ ν → γ ​ ν \gamma\nu\to\gamma\nu with virtual charged leptons in Feynman diagrams. The possibility to observe experimentally the new effect suggested here is discussed.

\displaystyle{\bf k}\cdot{\bf B}=0,\quad{\bf k}\times{\bf E}=\omega{\bf B},\quad\mathrm{i}\epsilon{\bf k}\cdot{\bf E}=\rho_{\mathrm{ext}},

\displaystyle\mu^{-1}\mathrm{i}{\bf k}\times{\bf B}+\mathrm{i}\omega\epsilon{\bf E}+\mathrm{i}\zeta\omega{\bf B}={\bf j}_{\mathrm{ext}},

{\bf B}

is purely imaginary and odd function of
\omega

[
17
]
.

Note that, in isotropic plasma, one can put
\mu=1

gives

In ref.

6
Discussion

In the present work, we have reconsidered the possibility of the detection C
\nu
B using a birefringence of electromagnetic waves in the relic
\nu\bar{\nu}
gas. In an intergalactic region at present, in addition to a sea of relic neutrinos and antineutrinos, there are a sparse isotropic plasma with the electron density
n_{e}\sim(3\times 10^{-4}\div 10^{-3})\,\text{cm}^{-3}\ll n_{\nu,\bar{\nu}}\sim 56\,\text{cm}^{-3}
,
and cosmological IGMF’s with the strength
B<10^{-9}\,\mathrm{G}
. There are also relic photons and the Extragalactic Background Light (EBL) as a visible light from stars and photons in the infrared range from the re-scattering of light on a dust in voids. In our study, we do not involve the two last ingredients with the densities
n_{\gamma}\simeq 400\,\text{cm}^{-3}
and
n_{\mathrm{EBL}}\simeq 10^{-2}\div O(1)\,\text{cm}^{-3}\ll n_{\gamma}
. They are essential in the problem of a lower bound on IGMF
[
24
]
, when processes
\gamma+\gamma_{\mathrm{EBL}}\to e^{+}+e^{-}
and the inverse Compton scattering
e^{\pm}+\gamma_{\mathrm{CMB}}\to e^{\pm}+\gamma^{\prime}
are taken into account. In our scenario, the electromagnetic waves propagate from a remote source through the relic neutrino sea and a plasma, where IGMF is presented as well. We have estimated above how large different competitive birefringence effects are there; cf. figure
1
.

Accounting for the surrounding plasma, for which off-shell photons with
q^{2}=\omega_{p}^{2}
are applied, we consider additionally real charged particles instead of virtual charged leptons in Feynman diagrams, accounted for in refs.
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
14
]
, to calculate forward scattering amplitudes
\gamma\nu\to\gamma\nu
and the corresponding refraction index for on-shell photons
q^{2}=0
. In our case, one gets the new form factor
\Pi_{2}^{(\mathrm{T})}\sim G_{\mathrm{F}}
in eq. (
2.5
), which is linear in the Fermi constant
G_{\mathrm{F}}
. This result is obtained in calculations of the relevant polarization operator in eq. (
A.5
) using the Matsubara technique.

This form factor, namely its representation for the non-relativistic plasma in eq. (
2.6
), is much greater than
\Pi_{2}^{(\nu)}
in eq. (
2.4
),
\Pi_{2}^{(\mathrm{T})}\gg\Pi_{2}^{(\nu)}
. Note that the term
\Pi_{2}^{(\nu)}
in eq. (
2.4
) stems from the correct substitution of the off-shell photon spectrum
q^{2}=\omega_{p}^{2}
to the vacuum result derived in ref.
[
14
]
. We also mention that the result of ref.
[
14
]
coincides with eq. (
2.3
) in the present work. Equation (
2.3
) is obtained from eq. (
A.5
) using the dimensional regularization method instead of the calculation of the Feynman diagram in figure 2(a) in ref.
[
14
]
. Thus, the birefringence of EM waves in the relic
\nu\bar{\nu}
-gas embedded to plasma is given by the rotation of the polarization vector on the angle
\phi(l)=\Pi_{2}^{(\mathrm{T})}l
in eq. (
4.6
). It corresponds to the rotary power in eq. (
5.5
).

In the case of anisotropic plasma the refraction index for ordinary and extraordinary transversal EM waves in a cold magnetized plasma reads

n^{\pm}=\frac{k}{\omega}\sqrt{1-\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{\omega(\omega\pm\omega_{\mathrm{B}})}},

(6.1)

where
\omega_{\mathrm{B}}=eB_{\parallel}/m_{e}\simeq 6\times 10^{-17}\,\mathrm{eV}
is the cyclotron frequency in the IGMF parallel to the photon momentum and
B_{\parallel}=({\bf B}\cdot{\bf k})/k\leq 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{G}
. Equation (
6.1
) provides the birefringence in IGMF’s [see eq. (
5.7
)],

\phi=\omega(n^{+}-n^{-})l=\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}\omega_{\mathrm{B}}l}{2\omega^{2}}={\rm RM}\cdot\lambda^{2}.

(6.2)

In eq. (
6.2
), the cyclotron frequency
\omega_{\mathrm{B}}
is much less than the plasma frequency
\omega_{p}=6.5\times 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{eV}
in the intergalactic electron gas with the density
n_{e}\simeq 3\times 10^{-4}\,\text{cm}^{-3}
,
\omega_{\mathrm{B}}\ll\omega_{p}
.
The plasma frequency, in its turn, is much less, for instance, than a radio wave frequency in the GHz range,
\omega\simeq k=4\times 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{eV}
, therefore
\omega_{\mathrm{B}}\ll\omega_{p}\ll\omega
.

In the case of an isotropic plasma,
\omega_{\mathrm{B}}=0
, while
\omega_{p}^{2}\neq 0
, the birefringence effect is possible accounting for the electroweak
\nu e
interaction in SM in the lowest order in
G_{\mathrm{F}}
,
\phi\sim G_{\mathrm{F}}
. We have obtained that the rotary power in eq. (
5.5
) is much larger than competing outcomes in refs.
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
for EM frequencies below
\omega<O({\rm MeV})
; cf. figure
1
.

Nevertheless, the predicted value of the rotary power (
5.5
) remains negligible for observations by the present instruments at the background of
\Phi=\phi/l
for EM waves in IGMF, which can be seen by comparing the black solid line and the blue line in figure
1
.

We do not extrapolate the curves
\Phi(\omega)
in figure
1
to more hard photon energies
\omega\gg{\rm MeV}
. Note that our outcome in eq. (
5.5
) becomes greater than the competing Faraday effect for IGMF
B_{\rm{IGMF}}/\mu{\rm G}\leq 10^{-3}
in eq. (
5.9
) at huge photon energies
\omega\geq 4~{\rm PeV}
remaining at the immeasurable level
|\phi|\sim 10^{-40}\,{\rm rad}
.

Abstract
In recent years, several problems regarding the partition regularity of exponential configurations have been studied in the literature, in some cases using the properties of specific ultrafilters. In this paper, we start to lay down the foundations of a general theory of exponentiations of ultrafilters, with a particular focus on their combinatorial properties and the existence of idempotents.

Keywords:
Stone-Čech compactification, idempotents, exponential triples, partition regularity.

1  Introduction

A large class of problems in arithmetic Ramsey theory regards the so-called partition regular configurations and equations on
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
. We let
\omega=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f_{1}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right),\ldots,f_{m}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right):\mathbb{N}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
. We say that the configuration
\{f_{1}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right),\ldots,f_{m}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\}
is partition regular on
\mathbb{N}
(PR from now on) if for all finite partitions
\mathbb{N}=A_{1}\cup\ldots\cup A_{k}
there are
i\leq k
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
j\leq m

f_{j}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\in A_{i}
.

Analogously, if
f_{1}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right),\ldots,f_{m}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right):\mathbb{N}^{n}\rightarrow\omega
and the system
\sigma\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)
is defined by

\begin{cases}f_{1}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)=0,\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \vdots\\
f_{m}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)=0\end{cases}

we say that
\sigma\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)
is PR if for every finite partition
\mathbb{N}=A_{1}\cup\ldots\cup A_{k}
, there are
i\leq k
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in A_{i}
such that
\sigma\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)=\vec{0}
.

In
[
8
]
, Schur proved one of the first and most important results in rithmetic Ramsey theory.

Theorem 1.2

(Additive Schur’s Theorem)
.

The configuration
\{x,y,x+y\}
is PR.

Equivalently, the additive Schur’s Theorem states that the equation
x+y=z
is PR.

Applying transformations to partitions allows to easily deduce new results by just considering the shape of transformed partition regular configurations. A useful trick, that sometimes allows turning additive structures in multiplicative structures, and multiplicative structures in exponential ones, is that of considering
\log_{2}
-base partitions: for
B\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, let
2^{-B}=\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid 2^{n}\in B\}
and, given a partition
\mathbb{N}=A_{1}\cup\ldots\cup A_{k}
, let the associated
\log_{2}
-base partition be given by
\mathbb{N}=2^{-A_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup 2^{-A_{k}}
. For example, given any finite partition of
\mathbb{N}
, in the
\log_{2}
-base partition we find monochromatic
x,y,z
with
x+y=z
by Theorem
1.2
; hence,
2^{x},2^{y},2^{z}=2^{x+y}=2^{x}\cdot 2^{y}
were monochromatic with respect to the original partition. This proves the multiplicative version of Schur.

Theorem 1.3

(Multiplicative Schur’s Theorem)
.

The configuration
\{x,y,x\cdot y\}
is PR.

Equivalently, the multiplicative Schur Theorem states that the equation
xy=z
is PR.

Recently, in
[
9
]
, the question about the partition regularity of
\{x,y,x^{y}\}
, which we will call an exponential triple, was posed and partially answered, by using ultrafilters, by Sisto; its full solution was first given in
[
7
]
by Sahasrabudhe who, among other generalizations, proved the exponential Schur’s Theorem

Theorem 1.4

(Exponential Schur’s Theorem)
.

The configuration
\{x,y,x^{y}\}
is PR.

Again, the above theorem can be rephrased in terms of equations: it states that
x^{y}=z
is PR. Sahasrabudhe’s proof used the compactness property on the convergence of subsequences in the space of

\mathbb{N}

Theorem 1.5

(Van der Waerden’s Theorem)
.

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
the configuration
\{x,x+y,\ldots,x+ky\}
is PR.

One of the key ingredients in Sahasrabudhe’s proof was the use of the function
f(n,m):=2^{n}m
, combined with associated
\log_{2}
-base partitions. Starting from observations about this function, in
[

Di Nasso and Ragosta gave a very simple and elegant ultrafilters proof of the partition regularity of the configuration
\{x,y,2^{x}y\}

-partition trick that allows proving the multiplicative Schur’s Theorem from the additive one (see
[

and the extension to

Our main goal is to start a systematic study of which ultrafilters are related to exponential triples, and what other combinatorial properties such ultrafilters have. This will be done by introducing, in Section

2  Exponentiations of ultrafilters

Preliminaries

As ultrafilters are the key notion of this paper, we assume the reader to have a basic knowledge of the algebra of
\beta\mathbb{N}
. We just briefly recall a few definitions, mostly so that we can fix some notations.

Sums and products of ultrafilters will be denoted by
\oplus,\odot
respectively. They are related to tensor products of ultrafilters which, over
\mathbb{N}
, are defined as follows:

Definition 2.1
.

Given
p,q\in\beta\mathbb{N}
, the tensor product
p\otimes q
of
p,q
is the ultrafilter on
\beta(\mathbb{N}^{2})
defined as follows: for all
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{2}

A\in p\otimes q\Leftrightarrow\{a\in\mathbb{N}\mid\{b\in\mathbb{N}\mid(a,b)\in A\}\in q\}\in p.

Moreover, we will need to talk about extensions of functions to
\beta\mathbb{N}
: for
f:\mathbb{N}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
, we will denote by
\widehat{f}
its unique continuous extension
\widehat{f}:\beta(\mathbb{N}^{n})\rightarrow\beta\mathbb{N}
. As usual, to simplify notations we identify each natural number
n\in\mathbb{N}
with the principal idempotent it generates, so that
\mathbb{N}\subseteq\beta\mathbb{N}
, and we convene that, for
a\in\mathbb{N},p\in\beta\mathbb{N}
, we will write
ap
instead of
a\odot p
. As in Proposition
2.13
we will use ultrafilters over
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{d}
, we recall that
\beta\mathbb{R}^{+}_{d}
is the Stone-Čech compactification of
\mathbb{R}^{+}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid x>0\}
endowed with the discrete topology. We adopt for
\beta\mathbb{R}^{+}_{d}
the analogous notational conventions we are using for
\beta\mathbb{N}
.

As well-known, ultrafilters are often a fundamental tool to study PR problems. For configurations, the connection between ultrafilters and partition regularity reads as follows (see e.g.
[
5
, Theorem 5.7]
):

Fact 2.2
.

Let
f_{1}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right),\ldots,f_{m}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right):\mathbb{N}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
. The following are equivalent:

1.

\{f_{1}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right),\ldots,f_{m}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\}
is PR;

2.

\exists p\in\beta\mathbb{N}\ \forall A\in p\ \exists x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{N}\ f_{1}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right),\ldots,f_{m}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\in A
.

Analogously, for systems the following holds:

Fact 2.3
.

Let
f_{1}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right),\ldots,f_{m}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right):\mathbb{N}^{n}\rightarrow\omega
, and let the system
\sigma\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)
be defined by

\begin{cases}f_{1}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)=0,\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \vdots\\
f_{m}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)=0.\end{cases}

The following are equivalent:

1.

\sigma\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)=\vec{0}
is PR;

2.

\exists p\in\beta\mathbb{N}\ \forall A\in p\ \exists x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in A\ \sigma\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)=\vec{0}
.

The above facts lead to the following definitions:

Definition 2.4
.

Let
p\in\beta\mathbb{N}
and let
f_{1}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right),\ldots,f_{m}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right):\mathbb{N}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
. We say that
p
witnesses the partition regularity of the configuration
\{f_{1}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right),\ldots,f_{m}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\}
if for all
A\in p
there exist
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\in A
. In this case, we write
p\models\{f_{1}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right),\ldots,f_{m}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\}
. Analogously, if the system
\sigma\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)
is defined by

\begin{cases}f_{1}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)=0,\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \vdots\\
f_{m}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)=0\end{cases}

we say that
p
witnesses the partition regularity of
\sigma\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)=\vec{0}
if
\forall A\in p\ \exists x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in A\ \sigma\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)=\vec{0}
. In this case, we write
p\models\sigma\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\right)=\vec{0}
.

Two extensions of exponentiation to ultrafilters

As it is well known, there are two natural ways to extend the sum from
\mathbb{N}
to
\beta\mathbb{N}
: the usual one
(p,q)\rightarrow p\oplus q
defined by letting, for every
A\in\mathbb{N}
,

A\in p\oplus q\Leftrightarrow\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid n+m\in A\}\in q\}\in p,

and the reverse sum
p\tilde{\oplus}q:=q\oplus p
; if
S(n,m):=n+m
denotes the sum of natural numbers, then
p\oplus q=\widehat{S}(p\otimes q)
and
q\oplus p=\widehat{S}(q\otimes p)
. Whilst different,
\oplus
and
\tilde{\oplus}
have the same properties, modulo properly translating their meaning by switching “left” with “right” notions. This is a consequence of the sum being commutative on
\mathbb{N}
.

Exponentiation, on the other hand, is not commutative nor associative. Hence, when extending it to
\beta\mathbb{N}
, we have at least two natural and truly distinct possible definitions to consider
4
4

4

Not including those that could be obtained trivially by reversing the order of the variables in the operations.
.

Definition 2.10
.

Given
p,q\in\beta\mathbb{N}
we let

E_{1}(p,q):=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}\mid\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid n^{m}\in A\}\in q\}\in p\},

and

E_{2}(p,q):=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}\mid\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid m^{n}\in A\}\in q\}\in p\}.

Equivalently, if
e_{1},e_{2}:\mathbb{N}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
are the exponentiations
e_{1}(n,m)=n^{m},e_{2}(n,m)=m^{n}
, then
E_{1}(p,q)=\widehat{e_{1}}(p\otimes q)
and
E_{2}(p,q)=\widehat{e_{2}}(p\otimes q)
.

For
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, trivially we have that
E_{1}(n,m)=E_{2}(m,n)=n^{m}
; more in general, for
n\in\mathbb{N},p\in\beta\mathbb{N}
we have that
E_{1}(n,p)=E_{2}(p,n)
and
E_{1}(p,n)=E_{2}(n,p)
as, for all
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle A\in E_{1}(n,p)\Leftrightarrow\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid n^{m}\in A\}\in p\Leftrightarrow A\in E_{2}(p,n),

\displaystyle A\in E_{1}(p,n)\Leftrightarrow\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid m^{n}\in A\}\in p\Leftrightarrow A\in E_{2}(n,p).

Notation:
we will use the simplified notation
n^{p}
to denote any of
E_{1}(n,p),E_{2}(p,n)
, and we will use the simplified notation
p^{n}
to denote any of
E_{1}(p,n),E_{2}(n,p)
.

At first sight, it might look like the equality
E_{1}(p,q)=E_{2}(q,p)
could be true for all
p,q\in\beta\mathbb{N}
; however, this is not the case.

Example 2.11
.

Let both
p,q
be nonprincipal ultrafilters that contain the set
P
of primes. Then

P_{1}=\{a^{b}\mid a,b\in P,a<b\}\in E_{1}(p,q),P_{2}=\{a^{b}\mid a,b\in P,a>b\}\in E_{2}(p,q)

but
P_{1}\cap P_{2}=\emptyset
, so
E_{1}(p,q)\neq E_{2}(q,p)
.

We will provide some examples of nonprincipal ultrafilters
p,q
satisfying
E_{1}(p,q)=E_{2}(q,p)
after Proposition
2.13
. To prove this proposition, it is helpful to use fractional powers of ultrafilters; to make proper sense of these powers, we prefer to state and prove Proposition
2.13
for ultrafilters in
\beta\mathbb{R}^{+}_{d}
. We extend the exponentiations to
\beta\mathbb{R}_{d}
as follows: given
p,q\in\beta\mathbb{R}^{+}_{d}
, we let:

E_{1}(p,q):=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid x^{y}\in A\}\in q\}\in p\},

and

E_{2}(p,q):=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid y^{x}\in A\}\in q\}\in p\}.

For
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
p\in\beta\mathbb{R}^{+}_{d}
, we will keep the simplified notation
r^{p}
to denote any of
E_{1}(r,p),E_{2}(p,r)
(which are equal), and
p^{r}
to denote any of
E_{1}(p,r),E_{2}(r,p)
(which are also equal).

We start with a preliminary lemma generalizing standard laws of exponents and logarithms.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
p\in\beta\mathbb{R}_{d}^{+}
and let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\{1\}
. The following facts hold:

1.

\widehat{\log_{a}}\left(b^{q}\right)=\log_{a}(b)\odot q
;

2.

\left(a^{b}\right)^{q}=a^{bq}
;

3.

\left(p^{a}\right)^{b}=p^{ab}
.

Proof.

We prove only
(1)
, as
(2)-(3)
are similar. Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then

\displaystyle A\in\widehat{\log_{a}}\left(b^{q}\right)\Leftrightarrow\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\log_{a}(x)\in A\}\in b^{q}\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid b^{z}\in\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\log_{a}(x)\in A\}\}\in q\Leftrightarrow\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\log_{a}(b^{z})\in A\}\in q\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid z\cdot\log_{a}(b)\in A\}\in q\Leftrightarrow A\in\log_{a}(b)\odot q.\qed

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
p,q,r\in\beta\mathbb{R}_{d}^{+}\setminus\{1\}
. Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
a\geq b>1
. The following properties hold:

1.

E_{1}\left(p^{a},q\right)=E_{1}\left(p,aq\right)
and
E_{2}\left(q,p^{a}\right)=E_{2}\left(aq,p\right)
;

2.

a^{p}=b^{q}\Leftrightarrow\left(\log_{a}(b)\right)\odot p=q
;

3.

p^{a}=q^{b}\Leftrightarrow p^{\frac{a}{b}}=q
;

4.

a^{p}\odot a^{q}=a^{p\oplus q}
;

5.

E_{1}\left(p,q\odot r\right)=E_{1}\left(E_{1}(p,q),r\right)
; in particular, if
q
is multiplicatively idempotent,
E_{1}(p,q)=E_{1}\left(E_{1}(p,q),q\right)
;

6.

E_{2}\left(p\odot q,r\right)=E_{2}\left(p,E_{2}(q,r)\right)
; in particular, if
p
is multiplicatively idempotent,
E_{2}(p,q)=E_{2}\left(p,E_{2}(p,q)\right)
.

Proof.

(1)
Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then

\displaystyle A\in E_{1}\left(p^{a},q\right)\Leftrightarrow\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid x^{y}\in A\}\in q\}\in p^{a}\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid z^{a}\in\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid x^{y}\in A\}\in q\}\}\in p\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\left(z^{a}\right)^{y}\in A\}\in q\}\in p\Leftrightarrow\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid z^{\left(ay\right)}\in A\}\in q\}\in p\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid z^{t}\in A\}\in aq\}\in p\Leftrightarrow A\in E_{1}(p,aq).

The
E_{2}
-case is analogous.

(2)
If
a^{p}=b^{q}
, by Lemma
2.12
.(1)
p=\widehat{\log_{a}}\left(a^{p}\right)=\widehat{\log_{a}}\left(b^{q}\right)=\left(\log_{a}(b)\right)\odot q
; conversely, if
p=\left(\log_{a}(b)\right)\odot q
then
a^{p}=a^{\left(\log_{a}(b)\right)\odot q}=\left(a^{\log_{a}(b)}\right)^{q}=b^{q}
by Lemma
2.12
.(2).

(3)
If
p^{a}=q^{b}
, by Lemma
2.12
.(3)
p^{\frac{a}{b}}=\left(p^{a}\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}=\left(q^{b}\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}=q
; conversely, again by Lemma
2.12
.(3), if
p^{\frac{a}{b}}=q
then
q^{b}=\left(p^{\frac{a}{b}}\right)^{b}=p^{\frac{ab}{b}}=p^{a}
.

(4)
Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then

\displaystyle A\in a^{p}\odot a^{q}\Leftrightarrow\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid xy\in A\}\in a^{q}\}\in a^{p}\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid a^{t}\in\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid xy\in A\}\}\in q\}\in a^{p}\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid x\cdot a^{t}\in A\}\in q\}\in a^{p}\Leftrightarrow\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid a^{z}\in\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid x\cdot a^{t}\in A\}\in q\}\in p\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid a^{t+z}\in A\}\in q\}\in p\Leftrightarrow A\in a^{p\oplus q}.

(5)
Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then

\displaystyle A\in E_{1}(p,q\odot r)\Leftrightarrow\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid x^{y}\in A\}\in q\odot r\}\in p\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid x^{tz}\in A\}\in r\}\in q\}\in p\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\left(x^{t}\right)^{z}\in A\}\in r\}\in q\}\in p\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid s^{z}\in A\}\in r\}\in E_{1}\left(p,q\right)\Leftrightarrow A\in E_{1}\left(E_{1}\left(p,q\right),r\right).

This is analogous to
(5)
. Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
p\in\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}

a,b\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
p^{a}\odot p^{b}\neq p^{a+b}

Proof.

Assume, by contrast, that
p^{a}\odot p^{b}=p^{a+b}
.

Let
F:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be the function that maps a number
n
to its largest prime divisor. Observe that, for all
p_{1},p_{2}\in\beta\mathbb{N}

\widehat{F}(E_{2}(p_{1},p_{2}))=p_{1}\odot\widehat{F}(p_{2})
. We consider two cases.

Case 1:

\widehat{F}(p)=i\in\mathbb{N}
. In this case, consider the function

that maps a number
n

\widehat{\Omega}(p)\notin\mathbb{N}

\widehat{\Omega}(p)=j\in\mathbb{N}

whilst

\widehat{\Omega}\left(p^{a+b}\right)=(a+b)\widehat{\Omega}(p).

We conclude as

Case 2:

\widehat{F}(p)\notin\mathbb{N}

G:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be the function

\widehat{F}(p)
is nonprincipal, also

is nonprincipal. Notice that:

•

\widehat{H}\left(p^{a}\odot p^{b}\right)=b\widehat{H}(p)

(a+b)\widehat{H}(p)=\widehat{H}\left(p^{a+b}\right)

Theorem 2.20
.

Let

. If

Let

, where
A/n^{a}=\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid n^{a}m\in A\}

A^{\prime}\cap A/n^{a}\in p
, there are
b,c,b+n^{a}c\in A/n^{a}\cap A^{\prime}

as

\widehat{\Omega}(p)=\widehat{\Omega}(E_{2}(p,p))=p\odot\widehat{\Omega}(p)
and
\widehat{\Omega}(p)=\widehat{\Omega}(p\odot p)=\widehat{\Omega}(p)\oplus\widehat{\Omega}(p)
, therefore
\widehat{\Omega}(p)
is additively idempotent and
p\odot\widehat{\Omega}(p)=\widehat{\Omega}(p)\oplus\widehat{\Omega}(p)
. If
\widehat{\Omega}(p)\in\mathbb{N}
, then by additive idempotency we would get that
\widehat{\Omega}(p)=0
, from which
p=1
, which is a contradiction as
p\in\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}
. So
\widehat{\Omega}(p)
is additively idempotent and nonprincipal, hence
n\mathbb{N}\in\widehat{\Omega}(p)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus we can apply Theorem
2.8
to deduce that
p\odot\widehat{\Omega}(p)\neq\widehat{\Omega}(p)\oplus\widehat{\Omega}(p)
, reaching the desired contradiction. ∎

3  Open questions

In this section, we summarize some questions which we could not solve, as well as some possible directions we believe it could be interesting to pursue in the future.

The first question originates from the fact that, in
[
7
]
, Sahasrabudhe proved the partition regularity of the configuration
\{x,y,xy,x^{y}\}
. We tried to recover a more direct proof of this fact, along the lines of what we developed in Section
2.2
, but we failed. Our guess is that, by choosing
p
in a reasonable way, the partition regularity of
\{x,y,xy,x^{y}\}
should be witnessed by
2^{p}
. We conjecture that a family of ultrafilters
p
that should make our claim true is the family of those
p\in E\left(K(\beta\mathbb{N},\odot)\right)
such that each member of
p
is also additive central. Summarizing, our first question is the following:

Question 3.1
.

Let
p\in E\left(K(\beta\mathbb{N},\odot)\right)
be such that each member of
p
is also additively central. Is it true or not that each
A\in p
contains a configuration of the form
\{m,n,m+n,m2^{n}\}
?

Notice that an affirmative answer to the above question would immediately entail, by using the same methods adopted in this paper, that each member of
2^{p}
contains patterns of the form
\{x,y,xy,x^{y}\}
.

The following question regards several technical properties of the exponential operations
E_{1},E_{2}
. We proved the existence and non-existence of ultrafilters satisfying certain equalities; for some other similar ones, we did not manage to find a definitive answer.

Question 3.2
.

Prove that there exist
p,q,r\in\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}
that solve any of the following equations

1.

E_{1}(p,q)=E_{1}(q,p)
;

2.

E_{2}(p,q)=E_{2}(q,p)
;

3.

E_{1}(p,q)=E_{2}(q,p)
;

4.

E_{1}(q,p)\odot p=p
;

5.

E_{1}(q,p)\odot q=q
;

6.

E_{1}(r,p)\odot E_{1}(r,q)=E_{1}(r,p\oplus q)
;

7.

E_{2}(q,p)\odot p=p
;

8.

E_{2}(q,p)\odot q=q
;

9.

E_{2}(p,r)\odot E_{2}(q,r)=E_{2}(p\oplus q,r)
.

One particular case of equality was discussed in Example
2.15
, from which the following question arises:

Question 3.3
.

For which
p,q\in\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}
does the equality
E_{1}(p,q)=E_{2}(p,q)
hold? Does it exist
p\in\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}
such that
E_{1}(p,p)=E_{2}(p,p)
?

Of course, as discussed in the paper, more relevant questions are the following:

Question 3.4
.

Does it exist
p
that is
E_{2}
-idempotent?

Finally, as a general direction connected to all the above questions, we believe that it would be interesting to further study the properties of exponentially rich ultrafilters; of course, this rather vague idea could be made precise in several different ways. We believe it would be interesting to start by studying the following:

Question 3.5
.

What are the algebraical properties of
\mathrm{ESW}
?

Stability of conforming space–time isogeometric methods for the wave equation
Matteo Ferrari, Sara Fraschini

Abstract
We consider a family of conforming space–time finite element discretizations for the wave equation based on splines of maximal regularity in time. Traditional techniques may require a CFL condition to guarantee stability. Recent works by O. Steinbach and M. Zank (2018), and S. Fraschini, G. Loli, A. Moiola, and G. Sangalli (2023), have introduced unconditionally stable schemes by adding non-consistent penalty terms to the underlying bilinear form. Stability and error analysis have been carried out for lowest order discrete spaces. While higher order methods have shown promising properties through numerical testing, their rigorous analysis was still missing. In this paper, we address this stability analysis by studying the properties of the condition number of a family of matrices associated with the time discretization. For each spline order, we derive explicit estimates of both the CFL condition required in the unstabilized case and the penalty term that minimises the consistency error in the stabilized case. Numerical tests confirm the sharpness of our results.



1
Introduction

The foundations of space–time Finite Element Methods (FEMs) for wave propagation problems have been laid in the seminal paper
[
17
]
. Unlike standard time-stepping techniques, space–time methods involve the simultaneous discretization of space and time variables, yielding numerical solutions available at all time instants. The popularity of these space–time schemes has been promoted in the last decades by recent advancements in computer technology, along with the remarkably properties of space–time discretizations. These include effective handling of moving boundaries, high order accuracy both in space and in time, multilevel preconditioning, local mesh refinement and adaptivity, and space–time parallelization.

The majority of space–time methods for the wave equation belong to the discontinuous Galerkin family (see, e.g.
[
19
,
24
,
25
]
and references therein). In contrast, there has been much less work dedicated to space–time conforming Galerkin methods, for which only a few works are available. Among these, we mention the recent works
[
2
,
10
,
22
,
29
,
30
]
. It has been shown in
[
9
,
29
]
that stability of conforming space–time methods for the second order acoustic wave equation may be subject to a CFL condition. This limitation has been overcome in
[
29
,
34
]
, where an unconditionally stable space–time piecewise continuous FEM has been designed by stabilizing the bilinear form coming from integration by parts. In
[
9
,
10
]
, generalizing the idea from
[
29
]
, a high order unconditionally stable space–time isogeometric method has been devised.

The isogeometric method, also known as Isogeometric Analysis (IgA), has been introduced in
[
16
]
as an advancement from classical FEMs, aiming to streamline the integration of computer-aided design with numerical simulations. Essentially, the isoparametric concept is invoked, since IgA employs spline functions, or their generalizations, to both parametrize the computational domain and approximate the differential equation of interest. Leveraging the smoothness of splines leads to increased accuracy per degree of freedom when compared to classical
C^{0}
piecewise polynomial discretizations (see, e.g.
[
4
,
8
]
), and superior spectral approximation properties
[
18
]
.

In this paper, we consider the space–time isogeometric method introduced in
[
10
]
. We provide the first theoretical justification of its unconditional stability. As in
[
29
]
, we carry out our analysis focusing on an ordinary differential equation strongly related to the time part of the acoustic wave equation. Our theoretical results are derived by exploiting the algebraic structure of the matrices involved, and are aimed at studying the properties of their condition numbers. The analysis we perform combines two main tools: properties of the so-called symbols
[
13
]
of the matrices associated with IgA discretizations, and the behaviour of the condition number of families of Toeplitz band matrices characterized in
[
1
]
. All at once we are able to obtain, for each spline degree, sharp estimates on the CFL condition required in the unstabilized case, and to derive which penalty term guarantees unconditional stability while minimising the consistency error. Moreover, we apply our analysis technique to different choices for the penalty term, and show that, among those, the one proposed in
[
10
]
is the best one.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we revisit the framework considered in
[
9
,
10
,
29
]
, detailing the variational setting and the proposed stabilized bilinear forms. In Section
3
, we provide an overview of splines with maximal regularity and of the properties of the associated Galerkin matrices. In Section
4
, we recall and extend results on the conditioning behaviour of families of Toeplitz band matrices, with emphasis on those exhibiting particular symmetries in their coefficients. In Section
5
, we present the proof of our main result on the CFL condition and the optimal penalty term. In Section
6
, we consider and analyze other possible stabilizations terms. Finally, in Section
7
, we present numerical tests that demonstrate that our estimates are sharp.

2
Model problem and stabilized variational formulations

We consider the linear acoustic wave equation defined on the space–time cylinder
Q=\Omega\times(0,T)
,

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}U(\boldsymbol{x},t)-\Delta_{\boldsymbol{x}}U(\boldsymbol{x},t)=F(\boldsymbol{x},t),&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in Q,\\
U(\boldsymbol{x},t)=0,&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
U(\boldsymbol{x},0)=0,\quad\partial_{t}U(\boldsymbol{x},t)_{|_{t=0}}=0,&\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\in\mathbb{N}
) is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
T>0
is a finite time, and
F\in L^{2}(Q)
is a given source term.

To recall the space–time variational formulations considered in
[
10
,
29
]
, we introduce the Hilbert spaces

\displaystyle H^{1,1}_{0;0,\bullet}(Q)

\displaystyle=L^{2}\big(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\cap H^{1}_{0,\bullet}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\big),\quad H^{1,1}_{0;\bullet,0}(Q)

\displaystyle=L^{2}\big(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\cap H^{1}_{\bullet,0}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\big),

where we denote
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)=\left\{w\in H^{1}(\Omega):\ w_{|_{\partial\Omega}}=0\right\}
, and the Sobolev-Bochner spaces

\displaystyle H^{1}_{0,\bullet}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\big)

\displaystyle=\{W\in H^{1}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\big):\ W(\cdot,0)=0\},

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\bullet,0}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\big)

\displaystyle=\{W\in H^{1}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\big):\ W(\cdot,T)=0\}.

After integration by parts, the space–time variational formulation for (
2.1
) reads as: find
U\in H^{1,1}_{0;0,\bullet}(Q)
such that

-(\partial_{t}U,\partial_{t}V)_{L^{2}(Q)}+(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}V)_{L^{2}(Q)}=(F,V)_{L^{2}(Q)},\,\text{ for all }V\in H^{1,1}_{0;\bullet,0}(Q),

(2.2)

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}(Q)}
denotes the inner product in
L^{2}(Q)
. Note that the initial condition
U(\boldsymbol{x},0)=0
and the homogeneous Dirichlet condition are considered in a strong sense, while
\partial_{t}U(\boldsymbol{x},t)_{|_{t=0}}\hskip-1.42271pt=0
is incorporated in the variational formulation.

Exploiting the Fourier expansion of the trial and tests functions, it has been shown (see, e.g.
[
33
]
) that there exists a unique solution
U\in H^{1,1}_{0;0,\bullet}(Q)
of the variational formulation (
2.2
), stable with respect to the source term
F
. In greater details, let
\{\psi_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
be an orthogonal basis in
H^{1}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)=\{w\in H^{1}(0,T):\ w(0)=0\}
composed by the eigenfunctions of the second time derivative with zero initial condition, and let
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j}
be the eigenfunctions of the space Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Any
U\in H^{1,1}_{0;0,\bullet}(Q)
admits the representation

U(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\sum_{j}\sum_{\ell}U_{j,\ell}\psi_{\ell}(t)\lambda_{j}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{j}u_{j}(t)\lambda_{j}(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad u_{j}(t)=\sum_{\ell}U_{j,\ell}\psi_{\ell}(t),

with suitable coefficients
\{U_{j,\ell}\}_{j,\ell}\subset\mathbb{R}
. Choosing in (
2.2
) a test function
v(t)\lambda_{j}(\boldsymbol{x})
with
v\in H^{1}_{\bullet,0}(0,T)=\{w\in H^{1}(0,T):\ w(T)=0\}
, it follows that the variational problem (
2.2
) is equivalent to finding, for each
j
, the coefficient functions
u_{j}\in H^{1}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)
such that

-(\partial_{t}u_{j},\partial_{t}v)_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\mu_{j}(u_{j},v)_{L^{2}(0,T)}=(f_{j},v)_{L^{2}(0,T)},\quad\text{ for all }v\in H^{1}_{\bullet,0}(0,T),

where
\{\mu_{j}\}_{j}
is the non-decreasing, positive and divergent sequence of the eigenvalues of the space Dirichlet Laplacian, and
f_{j}(t)=(F(\cdot,t),\lambda_{j})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

Our main focus lies in providing a finite element discretization for the initial value problem: find
u\in H_{0,\bullet}^{1}(0,T)
such that

a_{\mu}(u,v)=(f,v)_{L^{2}(0,T)}\quad\text{for all~}\,v\in H^{1}_{\bullet,0}(0,T),

(2.3)

with
f\in L^{2}(0,T)
, and the bilinear form
a_{\mu}:H^{1}_{0,\bullet}(0,T)\times H^{1}_{\bullet,0}(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

a_{\mu}(u,v)=-(\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v)_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\mu(u,v)_{L^{2}(0,T)},\quad\text{for~}\mu>0.

(2.4)

If this method is stable independently of the mesh parameter that characterizes the finite element subspace and
\mu
, then we expect unconditional stability for the associated space–time discretization of the wave problem (
2.2
).

In
[
30
, Theorem 4.1]
it has been shown unique solvability of (
2.3
) by a compact perturbation argument. However, this result is not sufficient to guarantee unconditional stability (with respect to
\mu
) for each numerical discretization of (
2.3
). It has been observed in
[
30
, Remark 4.9]
that a piecewise-linear continuous Galerkin-Petrov FEM discretization of (
2.3
), with a uniform mesh size
h
, is stable if and only if
h^{2}\mu<12
. This mesh condition turns out to be a CFL of the type
h_{t}<C_{\Omega}h_{x}
in the associated space–time variational formulation for the wave equation, with
C_{\Omega}>0
depending on the domain
\Omega
, and
h_{t}
and
h_{x}
the temporal and spatial mesh parameters, respectively. To overcome this mesh condition, the bilinear form (
2.4
) is stabilized in
[
29
]
. Specifically, an unconditionally stable Galerkin-Petrov discretization is proposed by perturbing the bilinear form (
2.4
) with a suitable penalty term
[
29
, Corollary 17.1]
, i.e., considering

a_{\mu,h}(u_{h},v_{h})=-(\partial_{t}u_{h},\partial_{t}v_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\mu(u_{h},v_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}-\frac{\mu h^{2}}{12}(\partial_{t}u_{h},\partial_{t}v_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

For all
h
and
\mu
, the perturbed bilinear form
a_{\mu,h}
satisfies a discrete inf–sup condition
[
29
, Lemma 17.6]
, and ensures optimal error estimates in
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
norms. An extension of this stabilization to high order Galerkin-Petrov
C^{0}
finite elements has been proposed and numerically verified in
[
34
]
.

In
[
9
,
10
]
, in the same spirit, a stabilized numerical scheme for isogeometric discretizations with maximal regularity splines has been proposed adding a suitable non-consistent penalty term. For a spline space of degree
p
with maximal regularity
C^{p-1}
, the proposed stabilized bilinear form reads as

\displaystyle a^{p}_{\mu,h}(u_{h},v_{h})=-(\partial_{t}u_{h},\partial_{t}v_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle+\mu(u_{h},v_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\mu\delta h^{2p}(\partial_{t}^{p}u_{h},\partial_{t}^{p}v_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)},

(2.5)

for a suitable parameter
\delta\leq 0
. The stabilized bilinear form (
2.5
) is then employed in the space–time finite element discretization for the wave equation (
2.1
) defining

\displaystyle a^{p}_{h}(U_{h},V_{h})=-(\partial_{t}U_{h},\partial_{t}V_{h})_{L^{2}(Q)}

\displaystyle+(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}U_{h},\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}V_{h})_{L^{2}(Q)}+\delta h_{t}^{2p}(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\partial_{t}^{p}U_{h},\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\partial_{t}^{p}V_{h})_{L^{2}(Q)}.

(2.6)

Even if the proposed stabilized isogeometric formulation of
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
is supported by a wide range of numerical experiments highlighting its stability and approximation properties, rigorous theoretical results are still missing. In the next sections, we address this theoretical analysis. Precisely, in Theorem
5.10
below, we show that there exist two computable quantities
\delta_{p}<0
and
\rho_{p}>0
such that the Galerkin method associated with the bilinear form (
2.5
) is stable, in a sense that will be specified later,

•

for all
h
and
\mu
, if and only if
\delta\leq\delta_{p}
,

•

if and only if
\mu h^{2}\leq\rho_{p}
, when
\delta=0
.

Maximal regularity splines and associated matrices

In this section, we describe the maximal regularity spline spaces and leverage certain properties of the matrices representing the bilinear form (
2.5
).

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we introduce the uniform mesh parameter
h=T/N
and the break points
t_{\ell}=\ell h
, for
\ell=0,\ldots,N
. For a positive integer
p\geq 1
, let
\{\xi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{2p+N}\subset[0,T]
be an open knot vector. According to the Cox-De Boor recursion formulas, spline basis functions are piecewise polynomials defined recursively as

\displaystyle\varphi^{p}_{j}(t)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{t-\xi_{j}}{\xi_{j+p}-\xi_{j}}\varphi_{j}^{p-1}(t)+\dfrac{\xi_{j+p+1}-t}{\xi_{j+p+1}-\xi_{j+1}}\varphi_{j+1}^{p-1}(t)&{\text{if }}t\in[\xi_{j},\xi_{j+p+1}),\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(3.1)

with

, and

\displaystyle\xi_{0}=\xi_{1}=\ldots=\xi_{p}=t_{0}=0,\quad\xi_{p+1}=t_{1},\quad\quad\ldots\quad\quad\xi_{p+N-1}=t_{N-1},\quad\xi_{p+N}=\ldots=\xi_{2p+N}=t_{N}=T.

In their purely Toeplitz band parts, the matrices exhibit symmetry in the entries with respect to the first lower codiagonal. Specifically, the central non-vanishing elements can be expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\mathbf{M}^{p}_{h}[\ell,\ell-1\pm j]=\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j),

\displaystyle h\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h}[\ell,\ell-1\pm j]=-\partial_{s}^{2}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j),

(3.6)

\displaystyle h^{2p-1}\mathbf{D}^{p}_{h}[\ell,\ell-1\pm j]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}\partial_{s}^{2p}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j),

for

and

Proof.

The first three properties readily follow from the definition of the matrices (

), and the B-splines definition (

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial^{k_{1}}_{s}\Phi_{p}(s)\partial^{k_{2}}_{s}\Phi_{p}(s+j)\differential s=(-1)^{k_{2}}\partial^{(k_{1}+k_{2})}_{s}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j)

and their symmetry property

\partial_{s}^{k}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1+s)=(-1)^{k}\partial_{s}^{k}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-s).

(3.8)

4
Conditioning of Toeplitz band matrices

In this section, after reviewing some results related to the conditioning of families of Toeplitz band matrices, we extend them to certain families of perturbed Toeplitz band matrices (with detailed proof in Appendix
A
). Then, we derive an easily verifiable necessary condition for the well-conditioning of these families when the corresponding matrices exhibit symmetry with respect to the first lower codiagonal (Section
4.1
).

A family of nonsingular matrices
\{\mathbf{C}_{n}\}_{n}
, with
\mathbf{C}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, is said to be
well-conditioned
if the condition numbers
{\kappa(\mathbf{C}_{n})}
are uniformly bounded with respect to
n
. It is said to be
weakly well-conditioned
if
{\kappa(\mathbf{C}_{n})}
grows as a power of
n
. Here and in the subsequent sections, the condition numbers are computed using either the
\|\cdot\|_{1}
or
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
matrix norms.

Given the coefficients
\{c_{-m},\ldots,c_{0},\ldots,c_{k}\}
, consider the family of Toeplitz band matrices
\{\mathbf{C}_{n}\}_{n}
,

\mathbf{C}_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}c_{0}&\ldots&c_{k}&&\\
\vdots&\ddots&&\ddots\\
c_{-m}&&\ddots&&c_{k}\\
&\ddots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&c_{-m}&\ldots&c_{0}\\
\end{pmatrix}_{n\times n}\hskip-17.07182pt.

(4.1)

We associate to this family the polynomial
q^{\mathbf{C}}\in\mathbb{P}_{m+k}(\mathbb{R})

q^{\mathbf{C}}(z)=\sum_{j=-m}^{k}c_{j}z^{m+j}.

(4.2)

Without loss of generality, we assume that
c_{-m}c_{k}\neq 0
. In
[
1
]
, the authors show that the well-conditioning (or weakly well-conditioning) of the family
\{\mathbf{C}_{n}\}_{n}
depends on the location of zeros of
q^{\mathbf{C}}
.

We define a polynomial as being of type
(s,u,\ell)
if it has
s
zeros with modulus smaller than
1
,
u
zeros with unit modulus, and
\ell
zeros with modulus larger than
1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
1
, Theorem 3]

Let
\{\mathbf{C}_{n}\}_{n}
represent a family of nonsingular Toeplitz band matrices, and let
q^{\mathbf{C}}
in (
4.2
) be the associated polynomial. Then, the family
\{\mathbf{C}_{n}\}_{n}
exhibits the following behaviour:

•

it is well-conditioned if
q^{\mathbf{C}}
is of type
(m,0,k)
;

•

it is weakly well-conditioned if
q^{\mathbf{C}}
is of type
(m_{1},m_{2},k)
or
(m,k_{1},k_{2})
, where
m_{1}+m_{2}=m
and
k_{1}+k_{2}=k
. In this scenario,
\kappa(\mathbf{C}_{n})
grows at most as
\mathcal{O}(n^{\eta})
, with
\eta
representing the highest multiplicity among the zeros of unit modulus of
q^{\mathbf{C}}
.

Additionally, if
q^{\mathbf{C}}
is of type
(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})
for
q_{1},q_{2}
and
q_{3}
such that
q_{1}+q_{3}>0
and simultaneously
q_{1}\neq m
and
q_{3}\neq k
, then
\kappa(\mathbf{C}_{n})
grows exponentially as
n
increases.

Remark 4.2
.

Weakly well-conditioning may be true for families of type
(0,m+k,0)
. Consider indeed the family
\{\mathbf{C}_{n}\}_{n}
of symmetric tridiagonal Toeplitz matrices with
c_{-1}=1,c_{0}=2
, and
c_{1}=1
. The polynomial
q^{\mathbf{C}}(z)=1+2z+z^{2}
is of type
(0,2,0)
, and the eigenvalues of matrices of this family are explicitly known (see, e.g.
[
28
, Pag. 59]
)

\left\{\lambda_{j}=2+2\cos\left(\frac{j\pi}{n+1}\right)\quad j=1,\ldots,n\right\}.

From the latter expression we readily obtain that
\kappa_{2}(\mathbf{C}_{n})=\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
.

Remark 4.3
.

Property that
q^{\mathbf{C}}
is of type
(m_{1},m_{2},k)
or
(m,k_{1},k_{2})
, where
m_{1}+m_{2}=m
and
k_{1}+k_{2}=k
actually implies nonsingularity, for
n
sufficiently large, of the elements of the family
\{\mathbf{C}_{n}\}_{n}
(see
[
1

).

Remark 4.4
.

Note that similar results can be obtained in the
2
-norm by recalling that the following inequality is valid (see, for example,
[
14

)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\|\mathbf{C}\|_{1}\leq\|\mathbf{C}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{n}\|\mathbf{C}\|_{1},\quad\text{for all~}\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}.

In particular, weakly well-conditioning with respect to a given norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
,
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
or
\|\cdot\|_{2}
is equivalent to weakly well-conditioning in any other of the two norms.

4.1
Conditioning of Toeplitz band matrices with symmetries

For a given
p\geq 1
and a set of
p+1
real numbers
\{g_{j}\}_{j=0}^{p}
with
g_{0}\neq 0
, we consider the family of Toeplitz band matrices denoted by
\{\mathbf{G}_{n}^{p}\}_{n}
, with

\mathbf{G}_{n}^{p}=\begin{pmatrix}g_{p-1}&g_{p-2}&\dots&g_{1}&g_{0}&&&\\
g_{p}&\ &\ &\ &\ &\ddots\\
g_{p-1}&\ &\ &\ &\ &\ &\ddots\\
\vdots&\ &\ &\ &\ &\ &&g_{0}\\
g_{1}&\ &\ &\ &\ &\ &&g_{1}\\
g_{0}&\ &\ &\ &\ &\ &&\vdots\\
\ &\ddots&\ &\ &\ &\ &&g_{p-2}\\
\ &\ &g_{0}&g_{1}&\dots&g_{p-1}&g_{p}&g_{p-1}\end{pmatrix}_{n\times n}\hskip-17.07182pt.

(4.4)

These matrices align with the notations established in Theorem
4.1
, with
m=p+1
and
k=p-1
. Consequently, investigating the conditioning behaviour of such matrices entails analyzing the associated polynomial of degree
2p

q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(z)=g_{0}+g_{1}z+\ldots+g_{p}z^{p}+\ldots+g_{1}z^{2p-1}+g_{0}z^{2p}.

(4.5)

Leveraging the specific structure of the polynomial
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
, we deduce the following result.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let the family of matrices
\{\mathbf{G}_{n}^{p}\}_{n}
be as in (
4.4
), and, if
p>1
, assume that not all the zeros of
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
, defined in (
4.5
), are on the boundary of the unit circle. Then,
\{\mathbf{G}_{n}^{p}\}_{n}
is weakly well-conditioned if and only if the associated polynomial
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
has exactly two zeros of unit modulus.

Proof.

Assume
p>1
, if
p=1
the proof is analogous. According to Theorem
4.1
, the matrix family
\{\mathbf{G}_{n}^{p}\}_{n}
exhibits weakly well-conditioned, or well-conditioned, behaviour if and only if
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
falls into one of the following types:
(r_{1},p+1-r_{1},p-1)
for some
r_{1}\in\{0,\ldots,p+1\}
or
(p+1,p-1-r_{2},r_{2})
for some
r_{2}\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
. It is worth noting that if
\xi
is a zero of
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
, then
\xi^{-1}
is also a zero. Hence,
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
necessarily is a polynomial of type
(r,2p-2r,r)
for some
r\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
. Consequently, the only admissible choice for weakly well-conditioning is
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
of type
(p-1,2,p-1)
.
∎

We aim to investigate the distribution of zeros of the polynomial
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
. This polynomial belongs to the category of self-reciprocal polynomials (refer to
[
20
]
). In other words,
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
can be expressed as
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{2p}g_{j}z^{j}
, where
g_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and it exhibits symmetry
g_{j}=g_{2p-j}
for
j=0,\dots,p
. These polynomials can be represented as

q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(z)=z^{p}\left[g_{0}\left(\frac{1}{z^{p}}+z^{p}\right)+g_{1}\left(\frac{1}{z^{p-1}}+z^{p-1}\right)+\ldots+g_{p-1}\left(\frac{1}{z}+z\right)+g_{p}\right].

Moreover, each term
z^{-j}+z^{j}
can be expressed as a polynomial in the new variable
x(z)=z^{-1}+z
. Specifically, let
T_{j}
denote the
j^{\thh}
Chebyshev polynomial defined via the recurrence relation
[
23
, Section 1.2.1]

T_{0}(x)=1,\quad T_{1}(x)=x,\quad T_{j+1}(x)=2xT_{j}(x)-T_{j-1}(x),\quad\text{for }j\geq 1.

This recursive formula has the closed form

T_{j}(x)=\frac{(x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1})^{j}+(x-\sqrt{x^{2}-1})^{j}}{2}.

Therefore, by substituting
x(z)=z^{-1}+z
, we obtain
2T_{j}(x(z)/2)=z^{-j}+z^{j}
. Exploiting this property, we introduce the so-called Chebyshev transform
\mathcal{T}q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}\in\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
of the self-reciprocal polynomial
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
in (
4.5
), defined as

\mathcal{T}q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(x)=2g_{0}T_{p}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)+2g_{1}T_{p-1}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)+\ldots+2g_{p-1}T_{1}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)+g_{p}T_{0}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right).

(4.6)

By definition, we have
\mathcal{T}q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(x(z))=q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(z)/z^{p}
where
x(z)=z^{-1}+z
. Additionally, observe that the leading coefficient of
\mathcal{T}q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
is
g_{0}
for
p\geq 1
. This follows from the well-known fact that the leading coefficient of
T_{p}(x)
is
2^{p-1}
for
p\geq 0
(see
[
27
, Equation 1.14]
).

As noted in
[
32
, Theorem 3]
, for every pair of roots
(\xi,\xi^{-1})
of
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(z)
located on the boundary of unit circle, there exists a corresponding zero of the polynomial
\mathcal{T}q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(x)
within the interval
[-2,2]
on the real line. This leads us to the following conclusion.

Lemma 4.8
.

If
p>1
, assume that not all the zeros of
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
in (
4.5
) are on the boundary of the unit circle. Then, the family of matrices
\{\mathbf{G}_{n}^{p}\}_{n}
as defined in (
4.4
) exhibits weakly well-conditioned behaviour if and only if the Chebyshev transform
\mathcal{T}q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
(
4.6
) of the polynomial
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
has exactly one real root in the interval
[-2,2]
.

Let
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{p}\}
be the roots of
\mathcal{T}q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
. A necessary condition such that only one root lies in the interval
[-2,2]
reads as

\prod_{j=1}^{p}(x_{j}^{2}-4)\leq 0.

(4.7)

This condition becomes evident when all the roots are real, and even in the case of a pair of complex conjugate roots
x
and
\bar{x}
, (
4.7
) holds true since

(x^{2}-4)(\bar{x}^{2}-4)=(\text{Re}(x^{2})-4)^{2}+\text{Im}(x^{2})^{2}\geq 0.

In the following, we obtain a simple criterion for evaluating the product in (
4.7
).

Lemma 4.9
.

Consider
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(z)=g_{0}z^{2p}+g_{1}z^{2p-1}+\ldots+g_{1}z+g_{0}
, a self-reciprocal polynomial of degree
2p
. Let
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{p}\}
denote the roots of its Chebyshev transform
\mathcal{T}q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
. Assuming
g_{0}\neq 0
, the following formulas are valid

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{p}(x_{j}-2)=(-1)^{p}\,\frac{q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(1)}{g_{0}},\quad\quad\quad\prod_{j=1}^{p}(x_{j}+2)=\frac{q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(-1)}{g_{0}}.

Proof.

To prove the first formula, let us consider
f(x)=\mathcal{T}q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(x+2)
. For all roots
x_{j}
of
\mathcal{T}q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
, we have
f(x_{j}-2)=\mathcal{T}q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(x_{j})=0
. Therefore, recalling Vieta’s formulas the product
(x_{1}-2)\dots(x_{p}-2)
is equal to
(-1)^{p}f(0)/g_{0}
. We can compute
f(0)=\mathcal{T}q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(2)=q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(1)
, the latter because
x(z)=z^{-1}+z=2
if and only if
z=1
. For the second formula, the proof follows a similar reasoning.
∎

We conclude collecting the results from the previous lemmas.

Proposition 4.10
.

If the family of matrices
\{\mathbf{G}_{n}^{p}\}_{n}
in (
4.4
) is weakly well-con-

ditioned, and if
p>1
not all zeros of the associated polynomial
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
, as defined in (
4.5
), are on the boundary of the unit circle, then
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}
satisfies the following condition

(-1)^{p}q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(-1)q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(1)\leq 0.

(4.8)

Proof.

Result follows from Lemmas
4.8
and
4.9
.
∎

In general, (
4.8
) is clearly not sufficient for weakly well-conditioning. Indeed, if
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(1)=0
, then this condition is always satisfied. In the next section, we will explicitly calculate the quantities
q^{\mathbf{G}^{p}}(\pm 1)
associated to the matrices (
3.10
), and we will show that (
4.8
) is also sufficient for the weakly well-conditioning in this particular case.

\{\mathbf{K}_{n}^{p}(\rho,\delta)\}_{n}

5.1
Necessary condition for the weakly well-conditioning of
\{\mathbf{K}_{n}^{p}(\rho,\delta)\}_{n}

Recalling the explicit formula (
3.11

q^{\mathbf{K}^{p}(\rho,\delta)}

\displaystyle q^{\mathbf{K}^{p}(\rho,\delta)}(z)

\displaystyle=k_{0}^{p}(\rho,\delta)+k_{1}^{p}(\rho,\delta)z+\ldots+k_{p}^{p}(\rho,\delta)z^{p}+\ldots+k_{1}^{p}(\rho,\delta)z^{2p-1}+k_{0}^{p}(\rho,\delta)z^{2p}

\displaystyle=z^{p}\left(k_{p}^{p}(\rho,\delta)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}k_{p-j}^{p}(\rho,\delta)(z^{-j}+z^{j})\right)

\Phi_{2p+1}

\displaystyle k_{p-j}^{p}(\rho,\delta)=\partial_{s}^{2}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j)+\rho\bigl(\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j)+\delta(-1)^{p}\partial_{s}^{2p}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j)\bigr)

j=0,\ldots,p

To investigate the number of zeros of
q^{\mathbf{K}^{p}(\rho,\delta)}
on the boundary of the unit circle, we express
q^{\mathbf{K}^{p}(\rho,\delta)}(z)
explicitly for
z=e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}
and
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]

Proposition 5.1
.

For
p\geq 1
and
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
, let the functions
M_{p},B_{p},D_{p}:[-\pi,\pi]\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as

\displaystyle M_{p}(\theta)=(2-2\cos\theta)^{p+1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p+2}},

\displaystyle\quad B_{p}(\theta)=-(2-2\cos\theta)^{p+1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p}},

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912ptD_{p}(\theta)=(-1)^{p}(2-2\cos\theta)^{p}.

Then, the following relations hold true

\displaystyle\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j)(e^{-\mathrm{i}j\theta}+e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta})

\displaystyle=M_{p}(\theta),

(5.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\partial_{s}^{2}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j)(e^{-\mathrm{i}j\theta}+e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta})

\displaystyle=B_{p}(\theta),

(5.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2p}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\partial_{s}^{2p}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j)(e^{-\mathrm{i}j\theta}+e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta})

\displaystyle=D_{p}(\theta).

(5.3)

Therefore, we can express
q^{\mathbf{K}^{p}(\rho,\delta)}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})
as

q^{\mathbf{K}^{p}(\rho,\delta)}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})=e^{\mathrm{i}p\theta}\left(B_{p}(\theta)+\rho\left(M_{p}(\theta)+\delta(-1)^{p}D_{p}(\theta)\right)\right).

(5.4)

Proof.

There is already a proof for the explicit expressions of
M_{p}(\theta)
and
B_{p}(\theta)
in
[
12
, Lemmas 7 and 6]
. For the sake of completeness, we report the details for
M_{p}(\theta)
, and present a different proof for
B_{p}(\theta)
, which is useful for calculating
D_{p}(\theta)
.

We recall that the Fourier transform of a cardinal spline
\Phi_{p}
is

\widehat{\Phi}_{p}(\xi)=\left(\frac{1-e^{-\mathrm{i}\xi}}{\mathrm{i}\xi}\right)^{p+1},\quad\left|\widehat{\Phi}_{p}(\xi)\right|^{2}=\left(\frac{2-2\cos\xi}{\xi^{2}}\right)^{p+1},\quad\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}

(see
[
5
, Example 3.4]
), and satisfies the hypothesis of a Poisson summation formula
[
5
, Theorem 2.28]
, i.e., for all
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi_{p}(s)\Phi_{p}(s+j)\differential s\right)e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\widehat{\Phi}_{p}(\theta+2j\pi)\right|^{2}.

To show (
5.1
), recalling (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), we compute for
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j)(e^{-\mathrm{i}j\theta}+e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta})=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j)e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi_{p}(s)\Phi_{p}(s+j)\differential s\right)e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\widehat{\Phi}_{p}\left(\theta+2j\pi\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=(2-2\cos\theta)^{p+1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2(p+1)}}.

Using the recurrence relation for derivatives of cardinal splines
[
5
, Theorem 4.3]

\partial_{s}^{k}\Phi_{p}(s)=\partial_{s}^{k-1}\Phi_{p-1}(s)-\partial_{s}^{k-1}\Phi_{p-1}(s-1),

and considering that
e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta}-2e^{\mathrm{i}(j+1)\theta}+e^{\mathrm{i}(j+2)\theta}=e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta}(1-e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})^{2}=-e^{\mathrm{i}(j+1)\theta}(2-2\cos\theta)
, we can compute

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\partial_{s}^{2}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j)(e^{-\mathrm{i}j\theta}+e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\partial_{s}^{2}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j)e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\partial_{s}\Phi_{2p}(p+1-j)-\partial_{s}\Phi_{2p}(p-j)\right)e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\Phi_{2p-1}(p+1-j)-2\Phi_{2p-1}(p-j)+\Phi_{2p-1}(p-j-1)\right)e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta}

\displaystyle=-(2-2\cos\theta)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi_{2p-1}(p+1-j)e^{\mathrm{i}(j+1)\theta}

\displaystyle=-(2-2\cos\theta)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi_{2p-1}(p-j)e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta}

\displaystyle=-(2-2\cos\theta)^{p+1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2p}},

where in the last equality we used the same procedures as in the proof for
M_{p}(\theta)
. Similarly, we can iteratively make the same steps as before to show that

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2p}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\partial_{s}^{2p}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j)(e^{-\mathrm{i}j\theta}+e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}(2-2\cos\theta)^{p}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi_{1}(1-j)e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}(2-2\cos\theta)^{p}.

∎

5.2
Sufficient condition for the weakly well-conditioning of
\{\mathbf{K}_{n}^{p}(\rho,\delta)\}_{n}

We finally establish that the conditions stated in Proposition

[

In the following lemma, we investigate when

Lemma 5.9
.

For
p\geq 1
, let
\delta_{p}
and
\rho_{p}
be defined as in (
5.6
). Define the function

F_{p}(\theta;\rho,\delta)=e^{-\mathrm{i}p\theta}q^{\mathbf{K}^{p}(\rho,\delta)}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})=B_{p}(\theta)+\rho(M_{p}(\theta)+\delta(-1)^{p}D_{p}(\theta))

with
M_{p},B_{p}
and
D_{p}
as in Proposition
5.1
. Then, the following statements hold true.

•

The equation (in terms of
\theta
)
F_{p}(\theta;\rho,\delta)=0
has exactly two simple zeros in
[-\pi,\pi]
for all
\rho>0
if and only if
\delta\leq\delta_{p}
;

•

The equation (in terms of
\theta
)
F_{p}(\theta;\rho,0)=0
has exactly two simple zeros in
[-\pi,\pi]
if and only if
\rho\leq\rho_{p}
.

Proof.

Let
p\geq 1
. We study the equation only in the interval
[0,\pi]
since
F_{p}(\theta;\rho,\delta)
is even in
\theta
. From (
5.5
) and the definition in Proposition
5.1
, we observe that
M_{p}(\theta)>0
for all
\theta\in[0,\pi]
. Hence, the ratio
F_{p}(\theta;\rho,\delta)/M_{p}(\theta)
is well defined for
\theta\in[0,\pi]
. Our objective is to show that for all
p\geq 1
,
\rho>0
and
\delta\leq 0
, the function
F_{p}(\theta;\rho,\delta)/M_{p}(\theta)
is strictly decreasing in
\theta
for
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. We begin by computing

\frac{d}{d\theta}\frac{F_{p}(\theta;\rho,\delta)}{M_{p}(\theta)}=\frac{d}{d\theta}\frac{B_{p}(\theta)}{M_{p}(\theta)}+\rho\delta(-1)^{p}\left(\frac{D^{\prime}_{p}(\theta)M_{p}(\theta)-D_{p}(\theta)M_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)}{(M_{p}(\theta))^{2}}\right).

It is shown in
[
7
, Theorem 2]
that
\frac{d}{d\theta}\left[B_{p}(\theta)/M_{p}(\theta)\right]<0
for all
p\geq 1
and
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. Additionally, we calculate
(-1)^{p}D_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)=2p\sin\theta(2-2\cos\theta)^{p-1}>0
for all
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. Therefore, combining
[
6
, Lemma A.2.]
that tell us that
M_{p}^{\prime}(\theta)<0
for all
p\geq 1
and
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, and the fact that
M_{p}(\theta)>0
, and
(-1)^{p}D_{p}(\theta)>0
, we conclude that for all
\rho>0
and
\delta\leq 0
,
\frac{d}{d\theta}[F_{p}(\theta;\rho,\delta)/M_{p}(\theta)]<0
for all
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. This confirms that
F_{p}(\theta;\rho,\delta)/M_{p}(\theta)
is strictly decreasing in
\theta
for
\rho>0
and
\delta\leq 0
.

We can state that
F_{p}(\theta;\rho,\delta)
has exactly one zero in
[0,\pi]
if and only if
F_{p}(0;\rho,\delta)\geq 0
and
F_{p}(\pi;\rho,\delta)\leq 0
. Both quantities have been computed in Corollary
5.4
, leading to the desired result.
∎

6
Alternative IgA stabilizations

In this section, we briefly analyze other possible stabilization terms, and show that, in a certain sense, the one proposed in
[
10
]
turns out to be the most effective.

Let us consider, for
k\geq 0
and a suitable
\delta\leq 0
, the perturbed bilinear form
a_{\mu,h}^{p,k}:S_{0,\bullet,h}^{p}\times S_{\bullet,0,h}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

\displaystyle a^{p,k}_{\mu,h}(u_{h},v_{h})=-(\partial_{t}u_{h},\partial_{t}v_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle+\mu(u_{h},v_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\mu\delta h^{2k}(\partial_{t}^{k}u_{h},\partial_{t}^{k}v_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

(6.1)

Clearly, when
k=p
, this coincides with the stabilized bilinear form
a_{\mu,h}^{p}
defined in (
2.5
), and for
k\geq p+1
the stabilizing term is null, reducing to the unstabilized bilinear form as in (
2.4
).
Moreover, since we are ultimately employing FEM, the consistency term should be at least of order
p+1
to ensure optimal convergence properties in both the energy and
L^{2}
-norms, that is
2k\geq p+1
.

Remark 6.1
.

Another possibility, mantaining the symmetric structure of the numerical scheme, could have been to include the more general stabilizing term:

\mu\delta h^{k_{1}+k_{2}}\left((\partial_{t}^{k_{1}}u_{h},\partial_{t}^{k_{2}}v_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}+(\partial_{t}^{k_{2}}u_{h},\partial_{t}^{k_{1}}v_{h})_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right),

for some

. However, note that if
k_{1}+k_{2}\equiv 0\ (\bmod\,2)
, then after integration by parts, this term coincides with the one added in (
6.1
) with
k=(k_{1}+k_{2})/2
. Conversely, if
k_{1}+k_{2}\equiv 1\ (\bmod\,2)
, recalling (
3.7
), this term vanishes when computed on central splines.

Again for
\rho=\mu h^{2}
and
n=N+p-1
, the associated linear system reads:

\mathbf{K}_{\mu,h}^{p,k}(\delta)=-\mathbf{B}_{h}^{p}+\mu\mathbf{M}_{h}^{p}+\mu\delta h^{2k}\mathbf{D}_{h}^{p,k},\quad\mathbf{K}_{n}^{p,k}(\rho,\delta)=h\mathbf{K}_{\mu,h}^{p,k}(\delta),

with

and

\mathbf{D}^{p,k}_{h}[\ell,j]=\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}^{k}\varphi_{j}^{p}(t)\partial_{t}^{k}\varphi^{p}_{\ell-1}(t)\differential t

h^{2k-1}\mathbf{D}^{p,k}_{h}[\ell,\ell-1\pm j]=(-1)^{k}\partial_{s}^{2k}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j)

for

\mathbf{D}_{h}^{p,0}=\mathbf{M}^{p}_{h},\quad\mathbf{D}_{h}^{p,1}=\mathbf{B}^{p}_{h}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{D}_{h}^{p,p}=\mathbf{D}_{h}^{p}.

Proposition
5.1
and Corollary
5.4
can be readily generalized for
0\leq k\leq p
.

Proposition 6.2
.

For
0\leq k\leq p
and
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
, the following relation is valid

\partial_{s}^{2k}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\partial_{s}^{2k}\Phi_{2p+1}(p+1-j)(e^{-\mathrm{i}j\theta}+e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta})=D^{k}_{p}(\theta),

with

as in Proposition

. More explicitly,

\displaystyle D_{p}^{k}(\theta)=(-1)^{k}(2-2\cos\theta)^{p+1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(\theta+2j\pi)^{2(p+1-k)}}.

In particular, we can evaluate
D_{p}^{0}(0)=1
and
D_{p}^{k}(0)=0
for
k\geq 1
, and

\displaystyle D_{p}^{k}(\pi)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}2^{2(k+1)}M_{p-k}(\pi)=(-1)^{k}2^{2k+1}\frac{2^{2(p+1-k)}-1}{\pi^{2(p+1-k)}}\zeta\left(2(p+1-k)\right),

.

Remark 6.3
.

Given that

The polynomial associated with the Toeplitz band parts of the family of matrices

q^{\mathbf{K}^{p,k}(\rho,\delta)}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})=e^{\mathrm{i}p\theta}\left(B_{p}(\theta)+\rho\left(M_{p}(\theta)+\delta(-1)^{k}D_{p}^{k}(\theta)\right)\right).

Generalizing Theorem
5.10
, we obtain the main result of this section.

Theorem 6.4
.

For
p\geq 1
and
1\leq k\leq p
, let us define

\delta_{p}^{k}=-\frac{M_{p}(\pi)}{(-1)^{k}D_{p}^{k}(\pi)}=-\frac{1}{2^{2k}\pi^{2k}}\frac{(2^{2(p+1)}-1)}{(2^{2(p+1-k)}-1)}\frac{\zeta\left(2(p+1)\right)}{\zeta\left(2(p+1-k)\right)}.

(6.3)

Let assume the nonsingularity of the matrices
\mathbf{K}_{n}^{p,k}(\rho,\delta)
and that all the elements on the outer diagonals of the top-left block of size
2p\times 2p
of each
\mathbf{K}_{n}^{p,k}(\rho,\delta)
are non-zeros. Then, the family
\{\mathbf{K}_{n}^{p,k}(\rho,\delta)\}_{n}
is weakly well-conditioned for all
\rho>0
if and only
\delta\leq\delta_{p}^{k}
.

Proof.

Let us consider the function
F_{p}^{k}(\theta;\rho,\delta)=e^{-\mathrm{i}p\theta}q^{\mathbf{K}^{p,k}(\rho,\delta)}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})
. We aim to show that the equation (in terms of
\theta
)
F^{k}_{p}(\theta;\rho,\delta)=0
has exactly one simple zero in
[0,\pi]
for all
\rho>0
if and only if
\delta\leq\delta_{p}^{k}
. Establishing this fact leads to the conclusion by Lemma
4.7
and Theorem
4.5
.

As in the proof of Lemma
5.9
, for all
p\geq 1
,
\rho>0
, and
\delta<0
, we show that the function
F_{p}^{k}(\theta;\rho,\delta)/M_{p}(\theta)
is strictly decreasing in
\theta
. Explicitly, we can express it as

\frac{F_{p}^{k}(\theta;\rho,\delta)}{M_{p}(\theta)}=\frac{B_{p}(\theta)}{M_{p}(\theta)}+\rho+\rho\delta(-1)^{k}\frac{D_{p}^{k}(\theta)}{M_{p}(\theta)}.

By virtue of
[
7
, Theorem 2]
, showing that the last term is a decreasing functions suffices. From Proposition
6.2
,

(-1)^{k}\frac{D^{k}_{p}(\theta)}{M_{p}(\theta)}=(2-2\cos\theta)^{k}\frac{M_{p-k}(\theta)}{M_{p}(\theta)}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(2-2\cos\theta)\frac{M_{p-k+j}(\theta)}{M_{p-k+j+1}(\theta)}.

Each factor here is a decreasing function (as established in
[
6
, Lemma A.2.]
) and positive, hence the product is decreasing as well. Consequently,
F_{p}^{k}(\theta;\rho,\delta)
has only one simple zero in
[0,\pi]
if and only if
F_{p}^{k}(0;\rho,\delta)\geq 0
and
F_{p}^{k}(\pi;\rho,\delta)\leq 0
. This holds true for all
\rho>0
if and only if
\delta\leq\delta_{p}^{k}
, given that
F_{p}^{k}(0;\rho,\delta)=\rho
. The explicit expression of
\delta_{p}^{k}
is a consequence of (
6.2
).

Remark 6.5
.

We note two distinct trends of

as

•

for

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we have examined the conditioning behaviour of a family of matrices related to conforming space–time discretizations of the wave equation, which uses splines of maximum regularity in time. We have provided a theoretical explanation of the results presented in
[
10
]
, and obtained precise estimates on two crucial parameters: the CFL condition for the unstabilized space–time bilinear form, and the optimal penalty parameter for the stabilized method. Our analysis is based on results from numerical linear algebra and properties of symbols associated with spline discretizations. Finally, we have shown how the stabilization term proposed in
[
10
]
is optimal from several points of view, and that the presented results are sharp.

Step 1.
Define the lower triangular block matrices

(A.7)

with

\mathbf{E}_{11}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{k}&\mathbf{0}_{k,n-k}\end{pmatrix}_{k\times n}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\mathbf{E}_{22}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{0}_{n-k,k}\\[4.26773pt]
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}c_{k}&\\
\vdots&\ddots\\
c_{1}&\ldots&c_{k}\end{matrix}\end{array}\right)_{n\times k}\hskip-18.49411pt,

Exactly as in

\{\mathbf{C}_{n}^{(3)}\}_{n}

\{\mathbf{C}_{n}^{(2)}\}_{n}
and
\{\mathbf{C}_{n}^{(3)}\}_{n}

Finally, we consider the families

Remark A.6
.

With a similar idea as in the previous remark, we also deduce a criterion to guarantee the nonsingularity of the elements of the family
\{\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{n}\}_{n}
. Let the family of nonsingular Toeplitz band matrices
\{\mathbf{C}_{n}\}_{n}
be weakly well-conditioned with
q^{\mathbf{C}}
of type
(m_{1},m_{2},k)
with
m_{1}+m_{2}=m
, and let
\{\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{n}\}_{n}
be a family of perturbed matrices with perturbation as in the assumption of Theorem
4.5
. If the matrix
\mathbf{G}_{2}^{(1)}
associated with the family
\{\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{n}\}_{n}
and
\mathbf{G}_{2}^{(1)}
associated with
\{\mathbf{J}_{n}(\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{n})^{\top}\mathbf{J}_{n}\}_{n}
, being
\mathbf{J}_{n}
as defined in (
A.13
), are nonsingular, then for
n
sufficiently large, each
\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{n}
is nonsingular. In our case, it is difficult to verify this criterion for the families
\{\mathbf{K}_{n}^{p}(\rho,\delta)\}_{n}
since the Casorati matrix
\mathbf{W}
involves the zero of the associated polynomial
q^{\mathbf{K}^{p}(\rho,\delta)}
, which are difficult to obtain due to dependence on the parameters
\rho
and
\delta
.

Example A.7
.

In this example we show that, to guarantee the nonsingularity of each
\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{n}
, it is not enough that the family
\{\mathbf{C}_{n}\}_{n}
is weakly well-conditioned and that the top-left and bottom-right blocks of size
(m+k)\times(m+k)
of each element of
\{\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{n}\}_{n}
are full rank. Consider the family
\{\mathbf{C}_{n}\}_{n}
with
c_{-3}=1,c_{-2}=2,c_{-1}=-6,c_{0}=2
and
c_{1}=1
. The associated polynomial
q^{\mathbf{C}}(z)=1+2z-6z^{2}+2z^{3}+z^{4}
is of type
(1,2,1)
, since it has roots
-2\pm\sqrt{3}
and double root
1
. Therefore, the family
\{\mathbf{C}_{n}\}_{n}
is weakly well-conditioned. Consider the family of perturbed matrices
\{\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{n}\}_{n}
with perturbation only in the top-left block defined as

\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}-6&2\\
-8&1&1\\
1&-6&2&1\\
1&2&-6&2&1\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&1&2&-6&2&1\\
&&&1&2&-6&2\\
\end{pmatrix}_{n\times n}\hskip-14.22636pt.

According to the notation in the proof of Theorem
4.5
, we can compute with (
A.15
)

\mathbf{G}_{2}^{(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-\frac{3}{2}&-\frac{1}{2}\vskip 2.84544pt\\
-\frac{2}{3}&-1&-\frac{1}{3}\vskip 2.84544pt\\
-\frac{23}{18}\sqrt{3}-\frac{13}{6}&-\frac{19}{12}\sqrt{3}-\frac{11}{4}&\frac{19}{36}\sqrt{3}+\frac{11}{12}\\
\end{pmatrix}

associated with the family
\{\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{n}\}_{n}
. This matrix is not full rank. Therefore, for
n
sufficiently large, each
\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{n}
is singular, even if the top-left block of size
4\times 4
of each
\widehat{\mathbf{C}}_{n}
is full rank.

Appendix B
The last assumption of Theorem
4.5
for
\{\mathbf{K}_{n}^{p}(\rho,\delta)\}_{n}

In this section, we explicitly show that the entries of the outer codiagonals, i.e., the
(p-1)^{\thh}
and the
(-p-1)^{\thh}
codiagonals, of the matrices
\mathbf{K}_{n}^{p}(\rho,\delta)
are nonzero for all
p=2,\ldots,8
,
\rho>0
and
\delta\leq 0
. For even
p
, we have the exception of a single value
\rho_{p}^{*}(\delta)>0
, which depends on
\delta
, and for which all the entries of these codiagonals are null. This case does not pose a problem since, in this critical case, the matrices
\mathbf{K}_{n}^{p}(\rho_{p}^{*}(\delta),\delta)
are of type
m=p+1
and
k=p-2
and we have verified that no entries of the new outer codiagonals, i.e., the
(p-2)^{\thh}
and
(-p)^{\thh}
codiagonals, have null entries. Note that, thanks to the symmetry and persymmetry properties of each
\mathbf{K}_{n}^{p}(\rho,\delta)
(see Proposition
3.2
), it is sufficient to analyze the first
p+1
entries of the
(p-1)^{\thh}
codiagonal, and in the critical case
\rho=\rho_{p}^{*}(\delta)
, the first
p+2
entries of the
(p-2)^{\thh}
codiagonal.

Let us denote by
\mathbf{K}_{*}^{p}(\rho,\delta)
and
\mathbf{K}_{\#}^{p}(\rho,\delta)
the vectors with the first
p+1
and
p+2
entries of the
(p-1)^{\thh}
and the
(p-2)^{\thh}
codiagonals of
\mathbf{K}_{n}^{p}(\rho,\delta)
, respectively.

The case
p=2

We explicitly obtain the entries

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{*}^{2}(\rho,\delta)=\left(\tfrac{1}{3}+\rho(\tfrac{1}{60}+2\delta)\right)\cdot\begin{bmatrix}1&\frac{1}{2}&\frac{1}{2}\end{bmatrix}.

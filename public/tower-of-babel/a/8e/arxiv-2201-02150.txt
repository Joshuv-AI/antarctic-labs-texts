K3 surfaces with action of the group M 20 M_{20}
Paola Comparin, Romain Demelle

Abstract
. It was shown by Mukai that the maximum order of a finite group acting faithfully and symplectically on a K3 surface is 960 and if such a group has order 960, then it is isomorphic to the Mathieu group M 20 M_{20} . In this paper, we are interested in projective K3 surfaces admitting a faithful symplectic action of the group M 20 M_{20} . We show that there are infinitely many K3 surfaces with this action and we describe them and their projective models, giving some explicit examples.

Introduction

A
K3 surface

X
is a compact complex smooth surface which is simply connected and admits a nowhere vanishing holomorphic 2-form
\omega_{X}
, unique up to scalar multiplication.
The study of finite groups acting on K3 surfaces and their action in cohomology goes back to the first works by Nikulin and Mukai. In particular, given a finite group
G
acting on a K3 surface
X
, one can study the action of
G
according to the action on the 2-form
\omega_{X}
: an automorphism
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is said to be
symplectic
if it acts as the identity on
\omega_{X}
and
non-symplectic
otherwise. In this last case
\sigma^{*}
acts as multiplication by some root of unity
\zeta_{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
Given a finite group of automorphisms
G
, let
\alpha:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
corresponding to the action on
\omega_{X}
. The following short exact sequence holds for some positive integer
m
:

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{G_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{1}

where
G_{0}=\ker\alpha
corresponds to the automorphisms acting symplectically on
X
.
A first result in the classification of possible groups
G
acting on a K3 surface is given by Nikulin in
[
16
]
, where the author classified abelian finite groups acting symplectically and showed that there is only a finite number of them.

Without the assumption of being abelian, Mukai in
[
15
]
studied finite groups acting faithfully and symplectically on K3 surfaces and showed that every such group is a subgroup of the Mathieu group
M_{23}
. Xiao in
[
23
]
gave an explicit list of all possible finite groups acting symplectically, finding 81, and classified combinatorial types of the action of these groups, i.e. computed the number of fixed points in each case.

Mukai had also shown that the order of such a group is at most 960, being equal to 960 if and only if
G
is the Mathieu group
M_{20}
, that one can describe also as
A_{5}\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{4}
.

In this paper we are concerned with projective K3 surfaces admitting a faithful and symplectic action of
M_{20}
. We first remark that a classification of possibilities for maximal groups
\Gamma
acting on a K3 surface
X
and such that
\Gamma
contains
M_{20}
strictly is given in
[
3
]
and
[
5
]
. There are three such groups and their order is
3840=4\cdot|M_{20}|
in one case and
1920=2\cdot|M_{20}|
in the two other cases. The K3 surfaces where these groups act first appeared in
[
3
,
5
,
13
,
15
]
.

Let
X
be a K3 surface that admits a faithful and symplectic action of the Mathieu group
M_{20}
. The surface
X
has necessarily Picard number
\rho(X):=\mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{NS}(X)
equal to 20 by Xiao’s classification
[

]
.
A first question arising is how many K3 surfaces admit a symplectic and faithful action of

[

it is already known that there are at most countably many such surfaces. In Section

Theorem 1
.

There exists an infinite number of K3 surfaces admitting a faithful and symplectic action of
M_{20}
.

Let
\mathbb{L}_{20}
be the lattice defined by the following matrix:

\mathbb{L}_{20}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}4&0&-2\\
0&4&-2\\

We show in Section

We consider

is a multiple of 4, we automatically get

. The second main result of the paper is the following:

Theorem 2

There exists an embedding

if and only if

, with

and we have the following results:

1.

L
is an ample class;

2.

the linear system
|L|
is not hyperelliptic and is base point free;

3.

for

, the projective model is only defined by quadrics.

Morevover, in this last case, the number of quadrics

defining

is

The paper is organized as follows: in Section

Section

On the one hand, by the Lefschetz fixed point formula

. On the other hand, we can compute

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite group and
f:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a function which is the difference of two characters of representation. Then

f(1)\equiv-\sum\limits_{\underset{\gamma\neq 1}{\gamma\in\Gamma}}f(\gamma)\mod|\Gamma|\,.

Before proving this result, we need to recall some definitions and results coming from representation theory
[
20
, Chapters 1 and 2]
.

Let
\left(E,\rho_{E}\right)
be a linear representation of the group
\Gamma
. This means that
E
is a complex vector space and
\rho_{E}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(E)
is a group morphism. In particular,
\rho_{\mathbb{C}}
is the so-called
trivial representation
, that is for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
\rho_{\mathbb{C}}(\gamma)=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{C}}
. We call a
character
of the representation
\rho_{E}
the morphism
\chi_{E}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by

\chi_{E}(\gamma)=\mathrm{Tr}\ \rho_{E}(\gamma),\ \gamma\in\Gamma.

Finally,
we denote by
p_{S}^{E}
the projection on the isotypic component associated with
S
. If
S
is an irreducible representation of
\Gamma
, we have a characterization of this endomorphism which is

p_{S}^{E}=\frac{\dim S}{|\Gamma|}\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\overline{\chi_{S}(\gamma)}\rho_{E}(\gamma).

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let
S=\mathbb{C}
. For the endomorphism
p_{\mathbb{C}}^{E}
we have

p^{E}_{\mathbb{C}}=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\overline{\chi_{\mathbb{C}}(\gamma)}\rho_{E}(\gamma).

As
\rho_{\mathbb{C}}
is the trivial representation of
\Gamma
, for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
we have
\chi_{\mathbb{C}}(\gamma)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathrm{Id_{\mathbb{C}}}\right)
and then

p^{E}_{\mathbb{C}}=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\rho_{E}(\gamma).

Taking the trace of this expression, with
f=\chi_{E}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and multiplying it by
|\Gamma|
we obtain the following:

f(1)=|\Gamma|\mathrm{Tr}\left(p_{\mathbb{C}}^{E}\right)-\sum\limits_{\underset{\gamma\neq 1}{\gamma\in\Gamma}}f(\gamma).

As
\mathrm{Tr}\left(p_{\mathbb{C}}^{E}\right)=\dim\mathrm{Im}\left(p_{\mathbb{C}}^{E}\right)
is a positive integer, we can look at this expression modulo
|\Gamma|
.
Similarly, if
f:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is as in the assumption, we obtain the thesis.

∎

In order to describe the projective models of K3 surfaces with an action of

is

, and when

defines an embedding. With the previous notations, we will show the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

Given the K3 surface

with the embedding given by

and

, we have the following results:

1.

is an ample class;

2.

Before we start proving the Theorem, we introduce the following two Lemmas which will be very useful for the following Sections.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
L
be a primitive ample class which is
M_{20}
-invariant on the K3 surface
X
. We assume that there exists a curve
C
such that

. Then
4n
divides
kI
, where
I:=\left[\mathrm{NS}(X):\mathbb{Z}L\oplus\mathbb{L}_{20}^{\perp}\right]
, and
n
divides
10k^{2}
.

Proof.

First we will show that
4n
divides
kI
. Note that
(k\mathbb{Z}+4n\mathbb{Z})/4n\mathbb{Z}
is a subquotient of
\mathrm{NS}(X)/\left(\mathbb{Z}L\oplus\mathbb{L}_{20}^{\perp}\right)
. In fact the map

\begin{array}[]{cccl}\mathrm{NS}(X)&\longrightarrow&\mathrm{NS}(X)\cdot L&\subset\mathbb{Z}\\
v&\longmapsto&v\cdot L&\end{array}

induces a surjection

{\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$\mathrm{NS}(X)$}\left/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$\mathbb{Z}L\oplus\mathbb{L}_{20}^{\perp}$}\right.}\twoheadrightarrow{\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$\mathrm{NS}(X)\cdot L$}\left/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$4n\mathbb{Z}$}\right.}\,.

By assumption there exists a curve
C
such that
L\cdot C=k
, so
\mathrm{NS}(X)\cdot L\supset k\mathbb{Z}+4n\mathbb{Z}
. Thus
I^{\prime}:=\left[k\mathbb{Z}+4n\mathbb{Z}:4n\mathbb{Z}\right]
divides

and, since
I^{\prime}=\frac{4n}{\gcd\{k,4n\}}\in\frac{4n}{k}\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain

. Hence

divides

.

Now we can deduce that

divides

. By
[

, Chapter I, Lemma 2.1]
we have

I^{2}=\frac{\det(\mathbb{Z}L)\cdot\det\left(\mathbb{L}_{20}^{\perp}\right)}{\det\mathrm{NS}(X)}=\frac{640n}{\det\mathrm{T}_{X}}\,.

Since

is an integer, so is its square

\frac{k^{2}I^{2}}{16n^{2}}=\frac{10k^{2}}{n\left(\det\mathrm{T}_{X}/4\right)}.

Note that

is always divisible by

, thus we get the Lemma.
∎

), one could remark that there are similarities between some cases. For example, we get the same lattice
T_{X}
when
L^{2}=4
and
L^{2}=16
, as well as in cases
L^{2}=8
and
L^{2}=32
.
Also, there is a relation between these embeddings. Actually, we have the following result:

Proposition 5.1
.

Given the embedding
\langle 4n\rangle\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}_{20}
, then, for all integers
r\geq 1
, there exists an embedding
\langle r^{2}4n\rangle\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}_{20}
and such that the lattice
\mathrm{T}_{X}
obtained for
L
is the same as the one for
rL
.

Proof.

For the existence of the embedding
\langle r^{2}4n\rangle\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}_{20}
, we can assume that there exists
\lambda_{0},\mu_{0},\delta_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
L\mapsto\lambda_{0}e+\mu_{0}f+\delta_{0}h
. Hence
rL\mapsto\left(r\lambda_{0}\right)e+\left(r\mu_{0}\right)f+\left(r\delta_{0}\right)h
is the desired embedding.
The transcendental lattice
\mathrm{T}_{X}
is obviously the same.
∎

The previous proposition allows to describe explicitly some non-primitive cases, i.e. cases where the polarization is multiple of some other polarization
L
.

Throughout this section,

will denote the K3 surface admitting the polarization

with

Thanks to Proposition
4.4

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\nu_{d}^{n}:&\mathbb{P}^{n}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{P}^{m}\\

6.
Existence of infinitely many K3 surfaces with an action of
M_{20}

It is known by
[
3
, Propostion 2.1]
that there are at most countably many K3 surfaces with a symplectic and faithful action of
M_{20}
.
It is interesting to ask whether there exists an infinite number of such K3 surfaces.
According to previous Sections, one can consider the polarization
L^{2}=4p
with
p
a prime. If equation (
2
) admits a solution for
L^{2}=4p
, one can repeat the arguments used before and by Theorem
1.3
one obtains a K3 surface with the action of
M_{20}
. Moreover, since
p
is a prime, this surface is a new one, meaning that it does not admit a polarization
L^{2}=4m
with
m<p
.

Theorem 6.1
.

There exist infinitely many K3 surfaces admitting the action of
M_{20}
.

Proof.

The proof will be done in two steps. First we will show that there exists infinitely many K3 surfaces with the previous assumptions. Then we will show that these surfaces admit an action of
M_{20}
by using
[
14
, Lemma 8.24]
.

In order to prove that there exists infinitely many such K3 surfaces, it suffices to show that there are infinite number of primes
p
such that the polarization
L^{2}=4p
defines an embedding and thus a K3 surface.
In order to answer this, we need to check that there is an infinite number of primes
p
such that the equation

(8)

4p=(2\lambda-\delta)^{2}+(2\mu-\delta)^{2}+10\delta^{2}.

admits a solution
(\lambda,\mu,\delta)
.

We observe that if
p\equiv 1\ (4)
, one can take
\delta=0
. The equation becomes

p=\lambda^{2}+\mu^{2}

and Fermat’s theorem of two squares
[
7
, Chapter 1.1]
ensures the existence of two integers
\lambda,\mu
.

By the weak form of Dirichlet theorem, there exists infinitely many primes congruent to
1
modulo
4
.
Then there exists infinitely many primes such that the equation (
8
) admits a solution
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu},0)
. It follows that there is an infinite number of K3 surfaces with an action of
M_{20}
.

It remains to prove that the group
M_{20}
acts on these surfaces.
First we consider an embedding of
\mathbb{L}_{20}
in
\Lambda_{K3}
that is isometric to the embedding of
\mathrm{H}^{2}\left(X^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}\right)^{M_{20}}
in
\mathrm{H}^{2}\left(X^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}\right)
for some K3 surface
X^{\prime}
admitting an action of the group
M_{20}
.

We take an element
L\in\mathbb{L}_{20}
such that
L^{2}=4p
as above and a line
\mathbb{C}\omega\subset\mathbb{L}_{20}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
that is orthogonal to
L
and such that
\omega\cdot\omega=0
and
\omega\cdot\bar{\omega}>0
. In this way
\omega
can be viewed as an element of the period domain
\Omega_{\Lambda_{K3}}
. By the surjectivity of the period map of K3 surfaces (see for example
[
14
, Chapter 7]
), there exists a K3 surface
X
and an ample line bundle
L_{X}
on
X
such that the isometry
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
allows us to identify
L_{X}
to
L
and the 1-dimensional subset
\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(X,\Omega^{2}_{X}\right)\subset\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
to
\mathbb{C}\omega
.

We can now apply
[
14
, Lemma 8.24]
to prove that
M_{20}
acts on
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. Condition (1) of
[
14
, Lemma 8.24]
states that the group
M_{20}
has to preserve
\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(X,\Omega^{2}_{X}\right)
and this the case by construction. For condition (2) the class
L_{X}
clearly satisfies the conditions.
Finally, condition (3) is also satisfied since
\mathbb{L}_{20}^{\perp}
contains no element of norm
-2
by
[
16
, Lemma 4.2]
. As a consequence the Mathieu group
M_{20}
acts on
X
with
L_{X}\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{M_{20}}
as desired.
∎

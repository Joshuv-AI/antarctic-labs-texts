Abstract
. We determine the rational Chow ring of the moduli space ℋ g , n \mathcal{H}_{g,n} of n n -pointed smooth hyperelliptic curves of genus g g when n ≤ 2 ​ g + 6 n\leq 2g+6 . We also show that the Chow ring of the partial compactification ℐ g , n \mathcal{I}_{g,n} , parametrizing n n -pointed irreducible nodal hyperelliptic curves, is generated by tautological divisors. Along the way, we improve Casnati’s result that ℋ g , n \mathcal{H}_{g,n} is rational for n ≤ 2 ​ g + 8 n\leq 2g+8 to show ℋ g , n \mathcal{H}_{g,n} is rational for n ≤ 3 ​ g + 5 n\leq 3g+5 .

The intersection theory of the moduli space of genus

is a fundamental first step towards understanding

, which is only completely understood in genus
2
and
3

[

,
14
]
.

Recent progress in the unpointed case has been based off of studying Hurwitz spaces parametrizing curves of low gonality and arbitrary genus
[

. In this paper, we begin to pursue the same strategy in the pointed case by studying moduli spaces of pointed hyperelliptic curves
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
. The case
n=0
is straightforward. The coarse space of
\mathcal{H}_{g}
is a quotient of an open subset of affine space by a finite group action. Thus, with rational coefficients, one has
A^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{g})=\mathbb{Q}
. (With

[

g

[
11
]
for

When one adds in marked points, however, the geometry and intersection theory of
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
becomes more interesting. With regards to its birational geometry, the moduli space
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
is known to be (see Figure

•

rational when

We use the birational geometry of
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
as a proxy for its complexity.
In particular, the nice sorts of presentations that are typically used in calculating Chow rings often show a space is (uni)rational. Thus, we might expect that the intersection theory of
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
becomes more complicated as
n

The Chow ring is usually more difficult to compute than the birational type.
Indeed, to prove rationality, one must understand a dense open subset of the space, whereas to determine the Chow ring, one must understand a full stratification and how the pieces fit together.
Previous work on the intersection theory of

Here, we determine the full rational Chow ring

1.1.
Statement of results

The main part of our work is to establish that the Chow ring of
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
is generated by divisors when
n\leq 2g+6
. Our techniques for doing so naturally extend over a partial compactification of the moduli space.
Let
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
be the stack parametrizing irreducible, nodal
n
-pointed hyperelliptic curves. Equivalently, if
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{irr}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
denotes the locus of irreducible curves, then
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
is the closure of
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
in
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{irr}}
.
Let
\delta\in A^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
be the class of the locus of singular curves, and let
\psi_{i}\in A^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
denote the restriction of the
i^{\text{th}}
psi class on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
to
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. We work over an arbitrary algebraically closed field of characteristic not
2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
g\geq 2
and
n\leq 2g+6
. Then
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
is generated by the divisor classes
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n}
and
\delta
.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
is used in our forthcoming work
[
8
]
, which makes significant progress towards determining the pairs
(g,n)
for which
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
is tautological. When
n\leq 2g+6
, Theorem
1.1
guarantees that the failure of
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
to be tautological will not be the fault of classes supported on
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. For this application, it is advantageous to know the result for the larger locus
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
, instead of just
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
.

On the smooth locus
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
, it is not hard to determine all relations among the psi classes for any
n
.
The tautological ring of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
is generated by the psi classes and pullbacks of kappa classes from
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.
Since the kappa classes restrict to zero on
\mathcal{H}_{g}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
, it follows that the psi classes generate the tautological ring
R^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})\subseteq A^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})
.
The following proposition shows that the structure of the tautological ring is quite simple.

Proposition 1.3
.

For
g\geq 2
and any
n
, we have

R^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})=\mathbb{Q}[\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n}]/(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n})^{2}.

Remark 1.4
.

In
[
24
]
, Tavakol determines the tautological ring of a different partial compactification
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{rt}}\supset\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
, which implies Proposition
1.3
by excision. In Lemma
3.9
, we nearly determine the tautological ring of our partial compactification
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
, which also immediately implies Proposition
1.3
.

Combining Proposition
1.3
with Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 1.5
.

If
n\leq 2g+6
, then

A^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})=R^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})=\mathbb{Q}[\psi_{1},...,\psi_{n}]/(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n})^{2}.

Remark 1.6
.

Note that Corollary
1.5
and Theorem
1.1
do
not
hold for all
n
. In
[
16
, Theorem 3]
, Graber and Pandharipande prove that
A^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{2,20})\neq R^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{2,20})
by producing an explicit non-tautological algebraic cycle. (Note that
n=20
falls in the range where
\mathcal{M}_{2,n}=\mathcal{H}_{2,n}
is of general type.)

Our approach to proving Theorem
1.1
is inspired by Casnati’s method of constructing models in
\mathbb{P}^{2}

[
9
]
, but we use models on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
instead. One consequence of our approach is an improvement on Casnati’s bound for when these spaces are rational.

Theorem 1.7
.

If
n\leq 3g+6
, then
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
is rational.

The discrepancy between the range where we can prove
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
is rational
and where we can determine the Chow ring is the difference between when a
general
collection of
n
points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
impose independent conditions on a certain linear system versus when
every
configuration of
n
points we care about imposes independent conditions.

1.2.
Structure of the paper

In Section
2
, we define stacks
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
of irreducible, nodal pointed hyperelliptic curves and the locally closed substacks in the stratification we shall use.
In Section
3
, we define the tautological classes on
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
and prove several relations among them. This involves constructing some explicit quotient stacks with the same coarse spaces as
\mathcal{I}_{g,0}
and
\mathcal{I}_{g,1}
. For
n\geq 2
however, we cannot give a global quotient description of
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
. Instead, we build each of the pieces of our stratification using models on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
in Section
4
.
The two maps to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in forming these models are the hyperelliptic map and the complete linear series of a (weighted) sum of marked points having degree
g+1
. For this linear series to be base point free, certain points are prohibited from being conjugate or Weierstrass.
An inductive argument allows us to eliminate strata where a pair of points is conjugate.
Meanwhile, we construct strata with marked Weierstrass points by imposing tangency conditions to vertical lines of the ruling in our models.
At the end of Section
4
, we present a proof of Theorem
1.1
, relying on Scavia’s result over
\mathbb{C}
. In Section
5
, we work to establish the needed parts of Scavia’s theorem over an arbitrary algebraically closed field of characteristic not
2
.

1.3.
Notations and Conventions

If the ground field is not mentioned, we are working over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic not
2
. We will explicitly mention where we are using results that are only known to hold over
\mathbb{C}

2.
Pointed irreducible, nodal hyperelliptic curves

Let
S
be a
k
-scheme. A family of irreducible, nodal hyperelliptic curves of genus
g
over
S
is a morphism of
S
schemes
C\rightarrow P\rightarrow S
where
C\rightarrow S
is a family of irreducible, nodal curves of genus
g
,
P\rightarrow S
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibration, and
C\rightarrow P
is finite and flat of degree
2
. An
n
-pointed family of irreducible, nodal hyperelliptic curves over
S
is a family of irreducible, nodal hyperelliptic curves
C\rightarrow P\rightarrow S
together with
n
disjoint sections
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}:S\rightarrow C
of
C\rightarrow S
such that the sections are disjoint from the nodes in fibers of
C\to S
.
An arrow between families
(C\rightarrow P\rightarrow S,p_{1},\dots,p_{n}:S\rightarrow C)
and
(C^{\prime}\rightarrow P^{\prime}\rightarrow S^{\prime},p_{1}^{\prime},\dots,p_{n}^{\prime}:S\rightarrow C)
is simply a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\prime}}

that also commutes the sections.
For brevity, we sometimes omit
S
and
P
, and write
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
for a family of
n
-pointed irreducible, nodal hyperelliptic curves.

Definition 2.1
.

The stack of
n
-pointed irreducible, nodal genus
g
hyperelliptic curves

\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
is the stack whose objects are families of
n
-pointed irreducible, nodal hyperelliptic curves with morphisms defined as above.

The stack of
n
-pointed genus
g
hyperelliptic curves

\mathcal{H}_{g,n}\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
is the substack defined by the additional condition that
C\to S
is smooth.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{irr}}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
denote the substack of pointed, irreducible curves.
One can also describe
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
as follows.

Lemma 2.2
.

\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
is the closure in
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{irr}}
of
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
.

Proof.

The closure of
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
inside
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is the preimage along
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
of

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
.
The locus
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is well-understood as the image of the space of admissible degree
2
covers.
The admissible covers with irreducible source are exactly the irreducible nodal hyperelliptic curves. When we add markings, we require that they do not meet the node, so the curve stays irreducible.
∎

2.1.
The hyperelliptic involution and the dualizing sheaf

Given an irreducible, nodal hyperelliptic curve
C
, let
\nu:\widetilde{C}\to C
be the normalization. Write
q_{i},q_{i}^{\prime}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
for the pairs of points lying over the nodes of
C
.
Considering the composition
\widetilde{C}\to C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we see that
\widetilde{C}
admits a degree
2
map
\widetilde{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
and each pair
q_{i},q_{i}^{\prime}
lies in the same fiber of this map.

If
g(\widetilde{C})\geq 2
, then
\widetilde{C}
is a hyperelliptic curve and
q_{i},q_{i}^{\prime}
are conjugate under the hyperelliptic involution on
\widetilde{C}
.
If
g(\widetilde{C})=1
, then we have the condition
q_{i}+q_{i}^{\prime}\sim q_{j}+q_{j}^{\prime}
for all
i,j
. If
g(\widetilde{C})=0
, then every pair of points are linearly equivalent, but the following condition on triples
(i,j,k)
must be satisfied:
the degree
2
polynomial vanishing at
q_{i}+q_{i}^{\prime}
must lie in the span of the degree
2
polynomial vanishing at
q_{j}+q_{j}^{\prime}
and the degree
2
polynomial vanishing at
q_{k}+q_{k}^{\prime}
.

In summary, irreducible nodal hyperelliptic curves are just built by gluing together conjugate points on a hyperelliptic curve
\widetilde{C}
of lower genus (with a suitable modification when
\widetilde{C}
has genus
1
or
0
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
C
be an irreducible, nodal hyperelliptic curve of genus
g\geq 2
. Then
C
admits a unique degree
2
map
\alpha:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
(up to automorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
).

Proof.

In this proof, we write that two maps to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
are equal if they differ by composition by an automorphism of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Let
\nu:\widetilde{C}\to C
be the normalization.
Suppose
\alpha^{\prime}:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is another degree
2
map. Then
\alpha^{\prime}\circ\nu
and
\alpha^{\prime}\circ\nu
are both degree
2
maps
\widetilde{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. If
g(\widetilde{C})\geq 2
, then we immediately see
\alpha^{\prime}\circ\nu=\alpha\circ\nu
, and hence
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha
.

If
g(\widetilde{C})=1
, then there is a unique degree two map
\widetilde{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
that sends
q_{1}
and
q_{1}^{\prime}
to the same point, namely the complete linear system of
\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C}}(q_{1}+q_{1}^{\prime})
. As
\alpha^{\prime}\circ\nu
and
\alpha\circ\nu
both send
q_{1}
and
q_{1}^{\prime}
to the same point, we see
\alpha^{\prime}\circ\nu=\alpha\circ\nu
, and hence
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha
.

If
g(\widetilde{C})=0
, then since
g(C)\geq 2
, we know
C
has at least two nodes. But there is a unique degree two map
\widetilde{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
which sends
q_{1}
and
q_{1}^{\prime}
to the same point
and
sends
q_{2}
and
q_{2}^{\prime}
to the same point. Hence, again we see
\alpha^{\prime}\circ\nu=\alpha\circ\nu
, and so
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha
.
∎

Definition 2.4
.

We call the unique degree two map
\alpha:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
the hyperelliptic map. We write
L:=\alpha^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)
for the corresponding degree
2
line bundle.
Given a point
p\in C
, we write
\overline{p}
for its conjugate under the hyperelliptic involution. Nodes are always fixed under the hyperelliptic involution.

We now note that two facts, which are well-known for smooth hyperelliptic curves, also hold in the irreducible nodal case (by the same arguments as in the smooth case).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
C
be an irreducible, nodal hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
,
\alpha:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the hyperelliptic map, and let
L=\alpha^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)
be the corresponding degree
2
line bundle. Then the dualizing sheaf satisfies
\omega_{C}\cong L^{\otimes g-1}
.

Proof.

By Riemann–Roch,

h^{0}(C,L^{\otimes g-1})-h^{0}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes(L^{\otimes g-1})^{\vee})=(2g-2)-g+1=g-1.

Meanwhile, we have
h^{0}(C,L^{\otimes g-1})\geq h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(g-1))=g
, so
h^{0}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes(L^{\otimes g-1})^{\vee})\geq 1
. But
\omega_{C}\otimes(L^{\otimes g-1})^{\vee}
has degree
0
, so it has a non-trivial section if and only
if it trivial, which means
\omega_{C}\cong L^{\otimes g-1}
.
∎

Geometrically, this tells us that the complete linear system for
\omega_{C}
sends
C
to a double cover of a degree
g-1
rational normal curve in
\mathbb{P}^{g-1}
. This implies the following.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
D=x_{1}+\ldots+x_{g}
is an effective degree
g
divisor with
h^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}(D))\geq 2
. Then
x_{i}=\overline{x}_{j}
for some
i\neq j
.

Proof.

By Riemann–Roch, if
h^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}(D))\geq 2
, then
h^{0}(C,\omega_{C}(-D))\geq 1
, which is to say the image of
D
under the canonical embedding
C\to\mathbb{P}^{g-1}
lies in a hyperplane.
However, any degree
g
divisor on a rational normal curve in
\mathbb{P}^{g-1}
spans
all of
\mathbb{P}^{g-1}
. Therefore, a length two subscheme of
D
must be sent to a length one subscheme under the canonical map, which is to say
x_{i}=\overline{x}_{j}
for some
i\neq j
.
∎

2.2.
Our stratification

In order to compute the Chow ring of
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
, we will introduce a stratification. It keeps track of the two interesting geometric phenomena that happen on pointed hyperelliptic curves:

(1)

a marked point is fixed by the hyperelliptic involution (this is called a Weierstrass point), or

(2)

two marked points are conjugate under the hyperelliptic involution.

Recall that, if they exist, the nodes are not allowed to be marked. Recall that we write
\overline{p}
for the conjugate of
p
under the hyperelliptic involution.

First, define divisors

D_{ij}=\{(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in\mathcal{I}_{g,n}:p_{i}=\overline{p}_{j}\},

so
D_{ij}
is the locus where
p_{i}
and
p_{j}
are conjugate under the hyperelliptic involution, and
D_{ii}
is the locus where
p_{i}
is a Weierstrass point.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
i\neq j
and suppose that
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n-1})
is generated by divisors. Then the Chow ring of
D_{ij}\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
is generated by restrictions of divisors from
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
.

Proof.

There is a commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{ij}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{I}_{g,n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{I}_{g,n-1}\smallsetminus D_{ii}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{I}_{g,n-1},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

where the vertical maps forget the
j^{\mathrm{th}}
marked point. The left vertical map is an isomorphism. Note that above
D_{ij}\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
, while
D_{ii}\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,n-1}
.
Taking Chow rings, we have

A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n-1})\twoheadrightarrow A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n-1}\smallsetminus D_{ii})\cong A^{*}(D_{ij}).

By the assumption that
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n-1})
is generated by divisors, it follows that
A^{*}(D_{ij})
is generated by restrictions of divisors from
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

A similar idea to the above — relating divisors where a certain linear equivalence holds to an open subset of the moduli space with one less point — was used by Belorousski
[
3
]
in studying the Chow rings of
\mathcal{M}_{1,n}
for
n\leq 10
.

Thus, if
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n-1})
is generated by divisors, then using the push-pull formula, every class supported on
D_{ij}\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
for
i\neq j
is a polynomial in divisor classes. Let us write

\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ}:=\mathcal{I}_{g,n}\smallsetminus\bigcup_{i<j}D_{ij}.

On
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ}
, no pair of marked points is conjugate, but the marked points may still be Weierstrass.
Thus, by excision and Proposition
2.7
, to establish that
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
is generated by divisors, it will suffice to show that
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ})
is generated by divisors.
When
g+1\leq n\leq 2g+6
we construct
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ}
directly as quotient of an open subset of a projective bundle over an open subset of affine space.

Meanwhile, for
n\leq g
, we must further stratify
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ}
.
To set this up, we will also require the slightly smaller open where no pair of points are conjugate and the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
point is prohibited from being Weierstrass:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,i}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{I}_{g,n}\smallsetminus\left(D_{ii}\cup\bigcup_{j<k}D_{jk}\right)

\displaystyle(\text{if $n<g+1$})

In order to combine arguments in the cases
n<g+1
and
n\geq g+1
, we use the convention that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,i}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ}

\displaystyle(\text{if $n\geq g+1$}).

The basic geometric loci left on
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ}
are the loci where some collection of points are Weierstrass. When
n<g+1
, we are going to stratify
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ}
into disjoint locally closed strata
W_{0}\cup W_{1}\cup\cdots\cup W_{n}
.
The subscript
i
will measure the number of initial consecutive Weierstrass points when the points are read in order.
Precisely,
W_{i}
is the locally closed stratum

(2.3)

\displaystyle W_{i}

\displaystyle:=\{(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ}:p_{j}=\overline{p}_{j}\text{ for $j\leq i$ and }p_{i+1}\neq\overline{p}_{i+1}\}

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ}\cap\left(\bigcap_{j\leq i}D_{jj}\smallsetminus\bigcap_{j\leq i+1}D_{jj}\right).

Note that
W_{i}
is a closed subset of
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,i+1}
.
In particular,
W_{0}=\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,1}
.
Generically, curves in
W_{i}
have
i
marked Weierstrass points, so
W_{i}
has codimension
i
.
For each
i
, we have
\overline{W}_{i}=W_{i}\cup\cdots\cup W_{n}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

The fundamental class
[\overline{W}_{i}]
is a product of divisors.

Proof.

By (
2.4
), we see that
\overline{W}_{i}=\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ}\cap(\bigcap_{j\leq i}D_{jj})
is a dimensionally transverse intersection of
i
divisors.
∎

Thus, to show that
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ})
is generated by divisors, it will suffice to
show that each
A^{*}(W_{i})
is generated by restrictions of divisors from
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ}
.
We shall show this by constructing
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,i+1}
as a quotient of an open subset of a projective bundle over an open subset of affine space. Furthermore,
W_{i}\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,i+1}
will be the quotient of an open subset of the projectivization of a particular subbundle. This will allow us to see that
A^{*}(W_{i})
is generated by restrictions of classes from
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,i+1})
, which we in turn show is generated by divisors (note that such divisors are restrictions from
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ}
by excision).

The case
W_{n}
is slightly different and will be treated in Lemma
3.8
.

3.
Tautological classes and relations

We define the
tautological ring

R^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})\subseteq A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
(resp.
R^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})\subseteq A^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})
) to be the subring generated restrictions of tautological classes from
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

3.1.
The psi and boundary divisors

Let

\pi:\mathcal{C}_{g,n}\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{g,n}

be the universal family of curves over
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
. The morphism
\pi
has
n
universal pairwise disjoint sections

p_{1},\dots,p_{n}:\mathcal{I}_{g,n}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{g,n},

which are also disjoint from the singular locus of
\mathcal{C}_{g,n}\to\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
.

Definition 3.1
.

The cotangent line bundles are the bundles

\mathbb{L}_{i}:=p_{i}^{*}\omega_{\pi}.

The
\psi
classes are the divisors

\psi_{i}:=c_{1}(\mathbb{L}_{i})\in A^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n}).

The
\psi
classes behave well with respect to pulling back by the morphisms that forget marked points.
There are natural maps
\pi_{j}:\mathcal{I}_{g,n}\to\mathcal{I}_{g,n-1}
where we forget the
j^{\mathrm{th}}
marked point, under which we have
\pi_{j}^{*}\psi_{i}=\psi_{i}
(this equality uses that our curve is irreducible and the markings do not meet the singular locus).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\Delta\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
be the divisor of singular curves, so
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}\smallsetminus\Delta=\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
. Define

\delta:=[\Delta]\in A^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n}).

Lemma 3.3
.

The divisor
\Delta\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
is irreducible.

Proof.

By the discussion in Section
2.1
, we see that
\Delta
is the image of
D_{12}\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,n+2}
under the map
D_{12}\to\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
that glues together the first two marked points, which are conjugate.
As in Lemma
2.7
, we have
D_{12}
isomorphic to
\mathcal{I}_{g,n+1}\smallsetminus D_{11}
, which is irreducible.
∎

In
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
, Scavia shows that, over the complex numbers, the
\psi
classes and boundary divisors form a basis for the Picard group of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n}
. Our
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
is the complement of all boundary divisors in
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n}
besides the divisor
\Delta
of irreducible nodal curves. Because
\Delta
is irreducible, we obtain the following.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
k=\mathbb{C}
and take
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
as a stack over
\Spec\mathbb{C}
. For every
g\geq 2
and
n\geq 0
,
A^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
has a basis given by
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}
and
\delta
.

We will prove
Theorem
1.1
for
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
defined over any algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic not
2
. Using Scavia’s result (Theorem
3.4
), we can simplify the proof when we work over the complex numbers: to prove Theorem
1.1
over
\mathbb{C}
it remains to prove that
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
is generated by divisors. We have organized the paper so that the reader who is only interested in the case over
\mathbb{C}
need not read the final Section
5
.

Finally, let us relate the geometric classes in our stratification to the tautological classes we have just defined. In
[
12
]
, Edidin and Hu use the method of test curves to compute compute the classes of
D_{ij}
in
A^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n})
. Restricting to
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle[D_{ij}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{g-1}(\psi_{i}+\psi_{j})-\frac{1}{2(2g+1)(g-1)}\delta

(3.2)

\displaystyle[D_{ii}]

\displaystyle=\frac{g+1}{g-1}\cdot\psi_{i}-\frac{1}{2(2g+1)(g-1)}\delta.

Although Edidin–Hu cite Scavia’s result over
\mathbb{C}
that the
\psi
classes and boundary divisors generate the Picard group, their argument does not use the complex numbers in any other way. Thus, by our work in Section
5
generalizing Scavia’s result, these formulas will be shown to hold over any ground field of characteristic not
2
.

3.2.
Relations

We now calculate several relations among the
\psi
and
\delta
classes on
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
. These relations nearly determine
R^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
and fully determine
R^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})
.

First, we compute
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,0})
.

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,0})=\mathbb{Q}[\delta]/(\delta^{3})
.

Proof.

An irreducible nodal hyperelliptic curve is determined by its branch divisor. The branch divisor is a degree
2g+2
divisor on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with no point of multiplicity
3
or more (to make the double cover at worst nodal) and at least one point of multiplicity
1
(to make the cover irreducible).
Let
\mathcal{V}
be the universal rank
2
vector bundle on
\BGL_{2}
.
Define
\Delta_{3}\subset\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}
to be the degree
2g+2
forms on
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}
with a root of multiplicity
3
or more. Let
R\subset\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}
be the forms with no simple roots. Note that
R
has codimension
g+1\geq 3
.

Then the coarse space of
\mathcal{I}_{g,0}
is the same as the coarse space of

\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}\smallsetminus(\Delta_{3}\cup R),

which we think of as an open subset of a vector bundle over
\BGL_{2}
. We therefore identify the Chow ring of
\mathcal{I}_{g,0}
with the Chow ring of
\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}\smallsetminus(\Delta_{3}\cup R)

We can determine the relations that come from excising
\Delta_{3}
using a diagram of the form
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\Delta}_{3}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\BGL_{2},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

where
\widetilde{\Delta}_{3}
is the kernel of the principal parts evaluation map

\pi^{*}\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}=\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2)\rightarrow P^{2}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}/\BGL_{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2)).

The space
\widetilde{\Delta}
parametrizes points on
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}
together with forms having a root of multiplicity
3
or more at that point. In particular,
\widetilde{\Delta}_{3}
surjects onto
\Delta_{3}\subset\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}
. Since we work with rational coefficients, all classes supported on
\Delta_{3}
are pushforwards of classes from
\widetilde{\Delta}_{3}
.

Let
c_{1},c_{2}
be the generators of
A^{*}(\BGL_{2})
and let
z=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}V}(1))
.
By the projective bundle theorem,
A^{*}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V})=\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},c_{2},z]/(z^{2}+c_{1}z+c_{2})
.
The fundamental class of
\widetilde{\Delta}_{3}\subset\pi^{*}\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}
is the top Chern class of the principal parts bundle
c_{3}(P^{2}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}/\BGL_{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2)))
.
To calculate this top Chern class, we use that
P^{2}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}/\BGL_{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2))
is filtered by the line bundles

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2),\qquad\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2)\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}/\BGL_{2}},\qquad\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2)\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}/\BGL_{2}}^{\otimes 2}

and the splitting principle.
By the relative Euler sequence,
c_{1}(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}/\BGL_{2}})=-2z-c_{1}
.
By
[
6
, Trapezoid Lemma 2.1]
, the image of
A^{*-1}(\widetilde{\Delta}_{3})\to A^{*}(\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee})
is the ideal in
A^{*}(\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee})\cong A^{*}(\BGL_{2})
generated by

(3.3)

\displaystyle\pi_{*}(c_{3}(P^{2}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}/\BGL_{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2))))

\displaystyle=(8g^{3}+12g^{2}+4g)c_{1}^{2}+(-8g^{3}+8g)c_{2}

and

(3.4)

\displaystyle c_{3}(P^{2}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}/\BGL_{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2))\cdot z)

\displaystyle=(-8g^{3}-12g^{2}-4g)c_{1}^{3}+(16g^{3}+12g^{2}-8g-4)c_{1}c_{2}.

Setting the right-hand side of (
3.3
) to zero tells us that
c_{2}
is a non-zero multiple of
c_{1}^{2}
; then, setting (
3.4
) to zero tells us
c_{1}^{3}=0
.
From this, it follows that
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,0})\cong\mathbb{Q}[c_{1}]/(c_{1}^{3})
.

Finally, we express the class
\delta
in terms of
c_{1}
. The divisor
\Delta\subset\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}
corresponds to the degree
2g+2
forms on
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}
with a double root. In a similar fashion to the above argument, we may realize
\Delta
as the image of the kernel of the principal parts evaluation map

\pi^{*}\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}=\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2)\to P^{1}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}/\BGL_{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2)).

Pushing forward, we have

(3.5)

\delta=\pi_{*}(c_{2}(P^{1}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}/\BGL_{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2))))=(-4g^{2}-6g-2)c_{1}.

In particular,
\delta
is a non-zero multiple of
c_{1}
,
so we get the claimed presentation of
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,0})
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

The tautological ring
R^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
is generated by the
\psi
classes and
\delta
.

Proof.

By definition,
R^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
is generated by the restrictions of psi classes, the kappa classes, and boundary strata from
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.
Since we require our curves to be irreducible, the only boundary strata that meet
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
are irreducible curves with some number of nodes, which are all pulled back from
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
.
On
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, the kappa classes are expressible as polynomials in the pullbacks of kappa classes from
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
and psi classes and boundary classes. Considering the commutative square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{I}_{g,n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{I}_{g,0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}

and the fact that
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,0})
is generated by
\delta
,
we see that
R^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
is generated by
\delta
and the
\psi
classes.
∎

Next, we shall compute
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,1})
. This determines some relations among
\psi
and
\delta
.

Lemma 3.7
.

We have

A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,1})=\frac{\mathbb{Q}[\psi_{1},\delta]}{(\delta\psi_{1}+(2g-1)\delta^{2},\psi_{1}^{2}+a_{g}\delta^{2},\delta^{3})}

where
a_{g}
is a constant depending on
g
.

Proof.

In the coarse space of
\mathcal{I}_{g,1}
, the points
(C,p)
and
(C,\overline{p})
are identified. This allows us to easily construct another stack with the same coarse space: we need a degree
2g+2
form
F
on
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}
with no roots of multiplicity
3
or more (and at least one root of multiplicity
1
), together with a point
p\in\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}
which is not a double root of
V(F)
. (The point
p
should not be a double root because we marked points are not allowed to be singular.)
Let

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\{(p,F):p\text{ has multiplicity $\geq 2$ in $V(F)$}\}

\displaystyle\subset\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times_{\BGL_{2}}(\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}\smallsetminus(\Delta_{3}\cup R))=\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times_{\BGL_{2}}\mathcal{I}_{g,0}.

Then the coarse space of
\mathcal{I}_{g,1}
is the same as the coarse space of

(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times_{\BGL_{2}}\mathcal{I}_{g,0})\smallsetminus D.

We have
A^{*}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times_{\BGL_{2}}\mathcal{I}_{g,0})=A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,0})/(z^{2}+c_{1}z+c_{2})
. In terms of the generator
\delta
, we have

(3.6)

0=z^{2}+c_{1}z+c_{2}=z^{2}-\frac{1}{2(2g+1)(g+1)}\delta z-\frac{1}{8(2g+1)(g-1)(g+1)^{2}}\delta^{2},

where we have used (
3.3
) and (
3.5
) to write
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
in terms of
\delta
.

It remains to find the relations imposed by excising
D
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\to\BGL_{2}
be the projection. We have that
D
is the total space of the kernel of

\pi^{*}\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}=\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2)\to P^{1}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}/\BGL_{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2)).

Because
D
is a vector bundle over
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}
, all classes supported on it are multiples of its fundamental class.
The fundamental class of
D
is the top Chern class of the principal parts bundle

\displaystyle c_{2}(P^{1}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}/\BGL_{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2)))

\displaystyle=((-4g^{2}-6g-2)c_{1})z+(-4g^{2}-4g)c_{2}

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\delta z+\frac{g}{2(2g+1)(g-1)(g+1)}\delta^{2}.

We now wish to express
z
in terms of
\psi_{1}
. The divisor
D_{11}\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,1}
is

D_{11}=\{(p,F):p\in V(F)\}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times_{\BGL_{2}}\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee},

which is the total space of the kernel of the regular evaluation map

\pi^{*}\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}=\pi^{*}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2).

Hence,
[D_{11}]=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2))=(2g+2)z
. Using (
3.2
), we find

z=\frac{1}{2g+2}[D_{11}]=\frac{1}{2g+2}\left(\frac{g+1}{g-1}\psi_{1}-\frac{1}{2(2g+1)(g-1)}\delta\right).

When we plug this into (
3.7
) and divide out by non-zero constants, we find

\delta\psi_{1}+(2g-1)\delta^{2}=0.

Finally, we plug the formula for
z
into (
3.6
) and use the above to eliminate the
\delta\psi_{1}
terms. This gives

\psi_{1}^{2}+\frac{16g^{4}-24g^{3}+16g^{2}+8g-3}{4(2g+1)^{2}(g+1)^{2}}\delta^{2}=0,

so we obtain the desired presentation.
∎

Similar techniques to the previous lemmas allow us find the Chow ring of the stratum
W_{n}\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
where all marked points are Weierstrass. We deal with these strata now, since our method of constructing models of curves on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
does not work on them.

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose
2\leq n\leq 2g+2
. The stratum
W_{n}\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
has
A^{*}(W_{n})=\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

As before, let
\mathcal{V}
be the tautological rank
2
bundle on
\BGL_{2}
. Let
\sigma_{i}:(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V})^{n}\to\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}
be projection onto the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
factor, and write
z_{i}:=\sigma_{i}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(1)
. Let
U\subset(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V})^{n}
be the complement of all the big diagonals. The locus where
p_{i}=p_{j}
is defined by the vanishing of the composition
\sigma_{i}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(-1)\to\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}/\sigma_{j}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(-1)
.
Thus, we obtain relations
z_{i}+z_{j}+c_{1}=0\in A^{*}(U)
for each
i\neq j
.

Now consider

X=\{(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},F):p_{i}\in V(F)\}\subset U\times_{\BGL_{2}}\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee},

which is the total space of a vector bundle over
U
. In particular,
A^{*}(X)\cong A^{*}(U),
which is generated by
c_{1},c_{2},z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}
.
Since the marked points are not allowed to be singular, we wish to remove from
X
the divisors
X_{i}
where
V(F)
has multiplicity
2
or more at
p_{i}
. Each
X_{i}\subset X
is a subbundle of
X
whose cokernel is
\sigma_{i}^{*}(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}V/\BGL_{2}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(2g+2))
. Hence, the fundamental class of
X_{i}\subset X
is
2g\cdot z_{i}
. It follows that
z_{i}=0\in A^{*}(X\smallsetminus(X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{n}))
. Since
0=z_{1}+z_{2}+c_{1}
, we also see that
c_{1}=0\in A^{*}(X\smallsetminus(X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{n}))
.
Finally, the form
F
should not have any triple roots and must have at least one simple root, so we also wish to remove
U\times_{\BGL_{2}}(\Delta_{3}\cup R)\subset U\times_{\BGL_{2}}\Sym^{2g+2}\mathcal{V}^{\vee}=X
. This introduces the relations from Lemma
3.5
, which showed
c_{2}
is a multiple of
c_{1}^{2}
.
In particular,

A^{*}(X\smallsetminus(X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{n}\cup U\times_{\BGL_{2}}(\Delta_{3}\cup R)))=\mathbb{Q}.

The above open subset of
X
has the same coarse space as
W_{n}
, so
A^{*}(W_{n})=\mathbb{Q}
as well.
∎

We now describe many relations in
R^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
, which almost determine this ring.

Lemma 3.9
.

All classes in
R^{2}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
are proportional to
\delta^{2}
. In other words,

\dim R^{2}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})\leq 1
.
Moreover,
R^{i}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})=0
for all
i\geq 3
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.6
, we know
R^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
is generated by the
\psi
classes and
\delta
. Moreover, by Lemma
3.5
, we know
\delta^{3}=0
. Hence, it suffices to show
\psi_{i}^{2}
and
\psi_{i}\psi_{j}
are proportional to
\delta^{2}
.

The class
\psi_{i}
is pulled back from the map
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}\to\mathcal{I}_{g,1}
that forgets all but the
i^{\text{th}}
marked point, so Lemma
3.7
shows that
\psi_{i}^{2}
and
\psi_{i}\delta
are proportional to
\delta^{2}
.

For
i\neq j
, the intersection of
D_{ii}
and
D_{ij}
in
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
is empty because at any point of their intersection we would have
p_{i}=\overline{p}_{i}=p_{j}
, which is impossible.
Using (
3.2
) and (
3.1
), we see

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=[D_{ii}][D_{ij}]=\frac{g+1}{(g-1)^{2}}\psi_{i}\psi_{j}+\text{terms proportional to $\delta^{2}$}.

so
\psi_{i}\psi_{j}
is also proportional to
\delta^{2}
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Lemma
3.9
nearly determines
R^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
. All that remains unknown is if
\delta^{2}
is non-zero. (We know
\delta^{2}
is non-zero on
\mathcal{I}_{g,0}
and
\mathcal{I}_{g,1}
, but it is possible that
\delta^{2}
could lie in the kernel of the pullback to
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
for some
n>1
.)

Since
\delta
lies in the kernel of the restriction map
R^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})\to R^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})
and
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n}
are independent in
A^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})
, Lemma
3.9
implies
Proposition
1.3
from the introduction:

Corollary 3.11
.

We have
R^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})=\mathbb{Q}[\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n}]/(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n})^{2}
.

4.
Quotient stack presentations

The purpose of this section is to give quotient stack presentations for the stacks
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,i}
and
W_{i}
(defined in (
2.1
)–(
2.3
)). Using these presentations, we will show that
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,i})
is generated by divisors and
A^{*}(W_{i})
is generated by restrictions of divisors from
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}
.

We begin by constructing models for our hyperelliptic curve lying on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, in which we will be able to keep track of our marked points. Our method is inspired by Casnati’s work
[
9
]
, which used plane models of hyperelliptic curves. One of the maps to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is given by the hyperelliptic map
\alpha:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We denote the corresponding degree two line bundle by
L:=\alpha^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)
. The other map to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
has degree
g+1
and comes from the following lemma.

Recall our convention (
2.2
) that
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,i}=\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ}
if
n\geq g+1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Given

is base point free and has exactly

Let

. Then

has degree

. By Lemma

, we see

, so

It follows that

If

, the assumption

means

is not among the points

Thus,

. By Lemma

, we would have

for some

, but this contradicts the fact that
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,i}

If

Lemma 4.2

Let

The evaluation map (

The map

A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&c\end{pmatrix}\mapsto(a/c,b/c)

is an isomorphism. This induces a natural map

which is an affine bundle with fiber

, so the pullback map induces an isomorphism on Chow rings. The Chow ring

is generated by divisors, corresponding to the first Chern classes of the two universal line bundles.

To prove the second claim, note that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}}(1)
restricts to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}_{i-1}}(1)
. Thus, by the projective bundle theorem,
A^{*}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F})\to A^{*}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}_{i-1})
is surjective. By excision
A^{*}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}_{i-1})\to A^{*}(W_{i-1})
is surjective. Hence,
A^{*}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F})\to A^{*}(W_{i-1})
is surjective. But since the top square commutes, all classes in the image of
A^{*}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F})\to A^{*}(W_{i-1})

A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})\to A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,i})
is surjective by excision, so
A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})\to A^{*}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,i})\to A^{*}(W_{i-1})
is surjective.
∎

5.1.

Our proof of independence is geometric, using test curves on a partial compactification of

Remark 5.1
.

Edidin–Hu work over
\mathbb{C}
in order to make use of Scavia’s result that gives a basis for

The following lemma is standard, but we include a proof for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose

We have

In particular, the intersection matrix is full rank for all
g\geq 2
and any
n
. It follows that
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n}
and the boundary divisors
\delta_{ij}
are independent on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n}
.
∎

5.2.
Spanning of
\psi
classes

Our goal in this section is to show that
A^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})
is spanned by
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n}
when
n\leq 2g+6
. The first step is the following.

Lemma 5.4
.

For
n\leq 2g+6
, we have
A^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,1}\smallsetminus\Delta)=0
.

Proof.

We described
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,1}
in Section
4.4
as an open subset of a projective bundle
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}
over
\mathcal{B}^{\circ}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{g,n}
, which in turn is an open subset of an affine bundle over
\BPU\times\BPU
.
By the projective bundle theorem
A^{1}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F})
is generated by
\zeta:=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}}(1))
and the generators
c_{1}:=c_{1}(\mathcal{V})
and
d_{1}:=c_{1}(\mathcal{W})
of
A^{1}(\BPU\times\BPU)
. Our goal is thus to show that
\zeta,c_{1},d_{1}
all restrict to zero on
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,1}\smallsetminus\Delta\subset\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}
.

By construction, the pullback of
\mathcal{V}
to
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,1}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}\to\BPU\times\BPU
is the rank two bundle
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}(p_{1}+\overline{p}_{1})
, whose projectivization is the universal
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundle over
\mathcal{I}_{g,1}
. Therefore,
c_{1}
is the pullback of
c_{1}
from Lemmas
3.7
and
3.5
in Section
3.2
. In particular, Equation
3.5
says
\delta
is a non-zero multiple of
c_{1}
, so
c_{1}=0\in A^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,1}\smallsetminus\Delta)
.
Below, we find two more independent relations from studying components of the complement of
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,1}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}
. Let
e=g-n+1
if
n\leq g
and let
e=0
if
n\geq g+1
so that

\mathcal{F}=\ker\left(\phi^{*}\mathcal{E}\to\sigma_{1}^{*}P^{e}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}/\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(\mathcal{N})\oplus\bigoplus_{2\leq j\leq n}\sigma_{j}\mathcal{N}\right).

5.2.1.
Meeting the horizontal ruling with too high multiplicity at
\sigma_{1}

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{F}
be the kernel of

\phi^{*}\mathcal{E}\to\sigma_{1}^{*}P^{e+1}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}/\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(\mathcal{N})\oplus\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq j\leq n\\
j\neq g+1\end{subarray}}\sigma_{j}\mathcal{N}

Equations in
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
vanish to order
e+2
along
\pi_{2}^{-1}(\pi_{2}(\sigma_{1}))
.
If
n\leq g
, then taking into account vanishing along the other sections, the vanishing of this equation meets the ruling with multiplicity
g+2
, so contains the entire ruling. On the other hand, if
n\geq g+1
, then the vanishing of such an equation cannot be
g+1
distinct points.
In either case,
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}
lies in the complement of
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,1}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}
.
By the snake lemma, we have

\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\cong\ker(\sigma_{1}^{*}P^{e+1}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}/\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(\mathcal{N})\to\sigma_{1}^{*}P^{e}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}/\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(\mathcal{N}))\cong\sigma_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{N}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}/\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}^{\otimes e+1}).

The divisor
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}
is defined by the vanishing of the composition

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}}(-1)\to\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\cong\sigma_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{N}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}/\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}^{\otimes e+1}).

Therefore, the fundamental class of
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}
is

\displaystyle c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}}(1)\otimes\sigma_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{N}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}/\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}^{\otimes g}))

\displaystyle=\zeta+c_{1}(\sigma_{1}^{*}\pi_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(g+1))

\displaystyle\qquad+c_{1}(\sigma_{1}^{*}\pi_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(2))+c_{1}(\sigma_{1}^{*}\Omega_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}/\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}^{\otimes e+1}).

First, note that
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}/\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}\cong\pi_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(-2)
.
Now,
v=\pi_{1}\circ\sigma_{1}
represents the class
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(1)
, so
v^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(1)=\pi_{1*}(c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(1))^{2})=-c_{1}(\mathcal{V})=-c_{1}
.
Similarly,
\sigma_{1}^{*}\pi_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(1)=-c_{1}(\mathcal{W})=-d_{1}
. Therefore, the above becomes

\displaystyle[\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=\zeta-(g+1)c_{1}-2d_{1}+2(e+1)c_{1}

\displaystyle=\zeta+(2e-g+1)c_{1}-2d_{1}.

Hence, we obtain the relation
\zeta+(2e-g+1)c_{1}-2d_{1}=0\in A^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,1})
.

5.2.2.
Meeting the vertical line with too high multiplicity at
\sigma_{1}

In
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,1}
, the first marked point is not Weierstrass. Therefore, equations in
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}
that are tangent to the vertical ruling at
\sigma_{1}
lie in the complement of
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,1}
.
Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}\subset\mathcal{F}
be the subbundle of equations whose vanishing is tangent to the vertical ruling at
\sigma_{1}
. Then

\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}\cong\ker(\sigma_{1}^{*}P^{1}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}/\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{N})\to\sigma_{1}^{*}\mathcal{N})\cong\sigma_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{N}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}/\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}).

Similarly to before, we calculate

\displaystyle c_{1}(\sigma_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{N}\otimes\Omega_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}/\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}))

\displaystyle=c_{1}(\sigma_{1}^{*}\pi_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}}(g+1))+c_{1}(\pi_{1}^{*}\pi_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}}(2))+c_{1}(\sigma_{1}^{*}\Omega_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}\times\mathbb{P}\mathcal{W}/\mathbb{P}\mathcal{V}})

\displaystyle=-(g+1)c_{1}-2d_{1}+2d_{1}=-(g+1)c_{1}.

Hence, the fundamental class of
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathcal{F}
is
\zeta-(g+1)c_{1}
. Thus, we obtain a relation
\zeta-(g+1)c_{1}=0\in A^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n}^{\circ,1}\smallsetminus\Delta)
. Since we have already said
c_{1}=0
, this shows
\zeta=0
. Then the relation in
5.2.1
shows
d_{1}=0
too.
∎

First, we conclude the case
n=2
.

Lemma 5.5
.

The classes
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
form a basis for
A^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{g,2})
. Hence
A^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{g,2})
is spanned by
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},
and
\delta
.

Proof.

We have
\mathcal{H}_{g,2}\smallsetminus(D_{11}\cup D_{12})=\mathcal{I}_{g,2}^{\circ,1}\smallsetminus\Delta
. Hence
A^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{g,2}\smallsetminus(D_{11}\cup D_{12}))=0
by Lemma
5.4
. It follows that
\dim A^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{g,2})\leq 2
. Meanwhile, Lemma
5.3
shows
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
are independent in
A^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{g,2})
,
so we can conclude that
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
are a basis for
A^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{g,2})
.
∎

This allows us to see that the divisors we have removed are tautological for any
n
.

Lemma 5.6
.

For any
g,n
, the class
[D_{ij}]\in A^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
lies in the span of
\psi_{i},\psi_{j}
and
\delta
.

Proof.

If
i\neq j
, the divisor
D_{ij}
is the pullback of
D_{12}\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,2}
under the map
\mathcal{I}_{g,n}\to\mathcal{I}_{g,2}
that forgets all but the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
and
j^{\mathrm{th}}
marked points. Then this follows from the previous lemma which said
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\delta
span
A^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{g,2})
.

If
i=j
, then
D_{ii}
is the pullback of
D_{11}\subset\mathcal{I}_{g,1}
, so this follows from Lemma
3.7
which showed
A^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{g,1})
is spanned by
\psi_{1}
and
\delta
.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

If
n\leq 2g+6
, then
the classes
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n}
form a basis for
A^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})
. Hence,
A^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{g,n})
is spanned by
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n}
and
\delta
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.4
, we see
A^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n}\smallsetminus\bigcup_{i,j}D_{ij})=0
. But by Lemma
5.6
, each
[D_{ij}]
lies in the span of
\psi_{i}
and
\psi_{j}
. It follows that
A^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})
is spanned by
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n}
. They are a basis by Lemma
5.3
.
∎

Having established (a) and (b) from the beginning of this section, our previous proof of Theorem
1.1
now gives the result in any characteristic
\neq 2
.

Tikhonov regularized exterior penalty dynamics for constrained variational inequalities
Mathias Staudigl, Siqi Qu

Abstract
Solving equilibrium problems under constraints is an important problem in optimization and optimal control. In this context an important practical challenge is the efficient incorporation of constraints. We develop a continuous-time method for solving constrained variational inequalities based on a new penalty regulated dynamical system in a general potentially infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. In order to obtain strong convergence of the issued trajectory of our method, we incorporate an explicit Tikhonov regularization parameter in our method, leading to a class of time-varying monotone inclusion problems featuring multiscale aspects. Besides strong convergence, we illustrate the practical efficiency of our developed method in solving constrained min-max problems.

Example 1
(Generalized Nash equilibrium Problems)

The GNEP is a very important class of multi-agent optimization problems in which non-cooperatively agents aim to minimize their private cost functions, given the other agents’ decisions and eventually also joint coupling constraints restricting the feasible set for each player. It has attracted enormous attention within the systems and control community as a general mathematical template to design distributed control strategies in complex networked systems
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
. Consider
N
agents where the local optimization problem of agent
i
reads as

\min_{x_{i}}J_{i}(x_{i},x_{-i})=\ell_{i}(x_{i},x_{-i})+r_{i}(x_{i})\quad\text{s.t.: }(x_{i},x_{-i})\in\mathsf{C}.

The constraint
x=(x_{i},x_{-i})\in\mathsf{C}
represents a coupling constraint and for simplicity we assume this set of to be described by linear restrictions in the control variables of the agents: Denote by
\mathcal{H}=\prod_{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}
, we assume that the jointly feasible set of the game is described as
\mathsf{C}=\{x\in\mathcal{H}|\sum_{i=1}^{N}T_{i}x_{i}=b\}
. Here

are bounded linear operators between two real Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{i}

, and

is given. Assuming that the cost functions
\ell_{i}(\cdot,x_{-i})
and
r_{i}(\cdot)
are convex in the own control variable, we arrive at jointly convex GNEP for which we can use variational inequality methods to compute Nash equilibrium points. In this paper we follow penalty techniques to enforce the joint feasibility restrictions
x\in\mathsf{C}

[
18
,
19
]
. Consider the function
g(x)=\frac{1}{2}\norm{\sum_{i=1}^{N}T_{i}x_{i}-b}^{2}
and
\mathsf{B}(x)=\nabla g(x)
. Then, it is clear that
\mathsf{C}=\zer(\mathsf{B})=\argmin_{x\in\mathcal{H}}g(x)
. Assuming additionally that the functions
\ell_{i}(\cdot,x_{-i})
are continuously differentiable for all
x_{-i}
, we can define the operator
\mathsf{D}(x):=[\nabla_{x_{1}}\ell_{1}(x);\ldots;\nabla_{x_{N}}\ell_{N}(x)]
, as well as the set-valued operator
\mathsf{A}(x)=\partial r_{1}(x_{1})\times\cdots\times\partial r_{N}(x_{N})
. Finding a Nash equilibrium of the game can in this setting by casted as the search for a solution to the variational inequality (
P
) with the data just defined.
\Diamond

To deal with the constraints present in the general formulation (
P
), we study the trajectories defined by the continuous-time dynamical system with components
(p,x)
, defined as

\left\{\begin{split}p(t)&=\res_{\lambda(t)A}(x(t)-\lambda(t)V(t,x(t)),\\
\dot{x}(t)&=p(t)-x(t)+\lambda(t)[V(t,x(t))-V(t,p(t))]\end{split}\right.

where
V(t,x):=\mathsf{D}(x)+\varepsilon(t)x+\beta(t)\mathsf{B}(x)
and

acts as a step-size parameter. This dynamical system is a continuous-time version of Tseng’s modified extragradient method
[
20
,
16
]
. The vector field defining this dynamics carries two parameters in its description. The addendum
\varepsilon(t)x
acts as a Tikhonov regularization on the trajectories, which will allow us to prove strong convergence (i.e. convergence in norm) to the least norm solution of the original problem (
P
). The second addendum
\beta(t)\mathsf{B}(x)
acts as an exterior penalty to the method which forces the trajectory to move towards the set
\zer(\mathsf{B})=\mathsf{C}
over time. The main result (Theorem
10
) of this note establishes the strong convergence of the trajectory
x(t)
to the least norm solution of the underlying constrained VI (
P
) whenever the Tikhonov parameter
\varepsilon(t)
vanishes and the penalty parameter
\beta(t)
grows to infinity, subject to some verifiable conditions.

I-A

Related Literature

Continuous time methods for variational inequalities have received a lot of attention in the last decades due to their close link to iterative methods via time-discretization methods, and the availability of Lyapunov analysis to assess their asymptotic properties. The dynamical system (
1
) is an example of a modified extragradient method, which received a lot of attention recently within the machine learning community. For our developments, an important feature of this dynamical system is the presence of multi-scale aspects, embodied by the functions
\varepsilon(t)
(Tikhonov) and
\beta(t)
(Penalty). The impact of such dynamical features in a continuous-time system are well-documented within optimization
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
. At the same time, Tikhonov regularization is a classical tool in variational analysis, that has been studied in connection with variational inequalities in many papers (see e.g.
[
25
,
26
]
). The contribution we are making here is to explicitly study the interplay of both effects simultaneously. We give verifiable conditions that guarantee asymptotic convergence the least norm solution of the original problem using our exterior penalty approach. While a seemingly natural approach, we could not identify a similar analysis in the literature, and therefore belief that this constitutes a new results. Besides theoretical convergence statements, we report on the empirical performance of the method in solving convex-concave saddle point problems.

Properties of perturbed solutions

(\varepsilon,\beta)\in(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)

(2)

This VI involves the parametric family of mappings

for all

Assumption 1

\mathsf{A}:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is maximally monotone.
\mathsf{D}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}

We see that the family of auxiliary problems (

The solution mapping

III
Penalty regulated dynamical systems

As solutions of the proposed dynamical system (
1
) we consider absolutely continuous functions
[
28
]
. Recall that a function
f:[0,b]\to\mathcal{H}
(where
b>0
) is said to be absolutely continuous if there exists an integrable function

such that

f(t)=f(0)+\int_{0}^{t}g(s)\dif s\quad\forall t\in[0,b].

Given functions
\varepsilon,\beta,\lambda:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)

V(t,x)\equiv V_{\varepsilon(t),\beta(t)}(x)
, and introduce the reflection
R_{t}(x)\triangleq x-\lambda(t)V(t,x)
. Furthermore, define the vector field
f(t,x):[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
by

f(t,x)\triangleq\left(R_{t}\circ\res_{\lambda(t)A}\circ R_{t}\right)(x)-R_{t}x

(4)

The first-order dynamical system (
1
) reads then compactly as

\dot{x}(t)=f(t,x(t)),x(0)=x^{0}\in\mathcal{H}\text{ given. }

(5)

We say that

is a strong solution of (
5
) if

is absolutely continuous on each interval
[0,b],0<b<\infty
and
\frac{\dif}{\dif t}x(t)=f(t,x(t))
for almost every

.

Lemma 8

\displaystyle-\norm{x(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}\leq(1+2\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t))\|p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\lambda(t)\langle V(t,p(t))-V(t,x(t)),p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\rangle

Proof:

From (

It follows,

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}(p(t))

\displaystyle=A(p(t))+V(t,p(t))

\displaystyle\ni\frac{x(t)-p(t)}{\lambda(t)}-V(t,x(t))+V(t,p(t))=-\frac{\dot{x}(t)}{\lambda(t)}.

By Assumption

Using this property, we see

\displaystyle 2\lambda(t)\varepsilon(t)\norm{p(t)-\bar{x}(t)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\langle x(t)-p(t),p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle~~~+2\lambda(t)\langle V(t,p(t))-V(t,x(t)),p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=-\|x(t)-p(t)\|^{2}+\|x(t)-\bar{x}(t)\|^{2}-\|p(t)-\bar{x}(t)\|^{2}

Suppression of Pulsed Dynamic Nuclear Polarization by Many-Body Spin Dynamics
Kento Sasaki, Eisuke Abe

Abstract
We study a mechanism by which nuclear hyperpolarization due to the polarization transfer from a microwave-pulse-controlled electron spin is suppressed. From analytical and numerical calculations of the unitary dynamics of multiple nuclear spins, we uncover that, combined with the formation of the dark state within a cluster of nuclei, coherent higher-order nuclear spin dynamics impose limits on the efficiency of the polarization transfer even in the absence of mundane depolarization processes such as nuclear spin diffusion and relaxation. Furthermore, we show that the influence of the dark state can be partly mitigated by introducing a disentangling operation. Our analysis is applied to the nuclear polarizations observed in 13 C nuclei coupled with a single nitrogen-vacancy center in diamond [Science 𝟑𝟕𝟒 \bm{374} , 1474 (2021) by J. Randall et al. ]. Our work sheds light on collective engineering of nuclear spins as well as future designs of pulsed dynamic nuclear polarization protocols.

Up to now, we have relied on the analytical expressions for

to gain physical insight into the higher-order spin dynamics.
We turn to numerical calculation of the nuclear polarization for

The computational procedure involves

It is known that in a coherent nuclear spin ensemble the collective dark state develops and saturates the polarization

;
Imamoḡlu et al. 2003
;
Taylor et al. 2003b

This effect is noticeable in the case of the homogeneous hyperfine coupling,
because a flip of one nuclear spin, without the knowledge on which nuclear spin to have flipped, necessarily creates a quantum superposition across the ensemble.
The higher-order spin dynamics and the formation of the dark state can spread cooperatively, and it is not straightforward to clearly separate the two effects.
Nonetheless, as a way to formally distinguish them, we also evaluate

\text{diag}(\rho_{\text{n}}^{\prime})

In this manner, the system is incoherent and no dark state is formed.

In summary, we analyzed the impact of higher-order nuclear spin dynamics induced by the pulsed DNP sequence,
in conjunction with the formation of the collective dark state, both many-body in nature, on the nuclear polarization.
The usefulness and limitation of disentangling operations were also discussed.
We were able to understand the nuclear polarizations observed in the system of a single NV center in diamond reported in
Randall et al. 2021
.
While we focused on a particular DNP sequence (PulsePol), we show in
SM
that these dynamics are also responsible for another widely-used sequence (NOVEL
Henstra et al. 1988a
).
In future studies, the effect of finite pulse lengths on higher-order spin dynamics will require a careful analysis.
Systematic Hamiltonian engineering, applicable to multi-spin systems, may be used to achieve higher polarization even at low magnetic fields
Choi et al. 2020
.
We also remark that, via an appropriate control over environmental nuclear spins which otherwise cause decoherence of an electron spin qubit,
they can be converted into a resource valuable to quantum technology
Taylor et al. 2003a
;
Imamoḡlu et al. 2003
;
Taylor et al. 2003b
;
Reilly et al. 2008
;
Chekhovich et al. 2013
;
Gangloff et al. 2021
.
Our work sheds light on collective engineering of nuclear spin ensemble as well as future designs of DNP protocols.

With more than two nuclear spins (

Still, spins #3, 5, and 7 are able to escape from being trapped in a dark state via phase-flip.

IV
A case of NOVEL

The spin dynamics of another DNP sequence NOVEL, a widely used protocol in DNP-NMR spectroscopy, are examined for comparison.
The NOVEL Hamiltonian is defined as

\hat{H}_{\text{NOVEL}}=f_{\text{t}}\hat{S}_{y}+\sum_{l=1}^{N_{\text{nuc}}}-f_{\text{n}}\hat{I}_{z}^{(l)}+A_{\perp}^{(l)}\hat{S}_{z}\hat{I}_{x}^{(l)},

where

is the target frequency and

and

Heterotic Quantum Cohomology
Jock McOrist

Abstract




\proofmodefalse

We reexamine the massless spectrum of a heterotic string vacuum at large radius and present two results. The first result is to construct a vector bundle
\mathcal{Q}
and operator
{\overline{\mathcal{D}}}
whose kernel amounts to deformations solving ‘F-term’ type equations. This resolves a dilemma in previous works in which the spin connection is treated as an independent degree of freedom, something that is not the case in string theory. The second result is to utilise the moduli space metric, constructed in previous work, to define an adjoint operator
{\overline{\mathcal{D}}}^{\dagger}
. The kernel of
{\overline{\mathcal{D}}}^{\dagger}
amounts to deformations solving ‘D-term’ type equations. Put together, we show there is a vector bundle
\mathcal{Q}
with a metric, a
{\overline{\mathcal{D}}}
operator and a gauge fixing (holomorphic gauge) in which the massless spectrum are harmonic representatives of
{\overline{\mathcal{D}}}
. This is remarkable as previous work indicated the Hodge decomposition of massless deformations were complicated and in particular not harmonic except at the standard embedding.

2.4
The Hodge decomposition

The balanced and HYM equations play a role in determining the exact and co–exact terms in the Hodge decomopsition of fields. We present this to emphasise the fact that deformations of a heterotic theory are not simply the harmonic representatives. Taking into account the F-term equations, the Hodge decomposition is

\begin{split}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{(1,1)}&\;=\;\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{(1,1)\,harm}+{\overline{\partial}}^{\dagger}\xi_{\alpha}^{(1,2)}~,\\
\Delta_{\alpha}&\;=\;\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{harm}+{\overline{\partial}}\kappa_{\alpha}~,\\
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}&\;=\;{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{harm}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\Phi_{\alpha}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}\Psi_{\alpha}^{(0,2)}~.\\
\end{split}

(2.16)

The role of the cohomology we aim to compute is to tell us what linear combinations of harmonic terms in (
2.16
) correspond to unobstructed deformations. The exact and co–exact terms in the Hodge decomposition are physical and so are important and are determined by substituting into the HYM and balanced equations and solving the corresponding Poission equations:

\displaystyle\xi_{\alpha}^{(1,2)}

\displaystyle\;=\;\Box_{\overline{\partial}}^{-1}\Big(\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}({\partial}\omega)_{\mu}-\frac{\text{i}{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}F)

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+\frac{\text{i}{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\,\big(\nabla_{\nu}\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla^{\mu}\,\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(0,1)}\big)R^{\nu}{}_{\mu}\Big)+{\overline{\partial}}^{\dagger}\text{--\,closed}~,

(2.17a)

\displaystyle\kappa_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}

\displaystyle\;=\;\Box_{{\overline{\partial}}}^{-1}\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\,\big({\partial}^{\dagger}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}\big)^{\mu}+\frac{1}{4\text{i}}(\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\rho}}})({\partial}\omega)^{{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\rho}}\mu}\Big)~,

(2.17b)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\alpha}^{(0,2)}

\displaystyle\;=\;\Box^{-1}_{{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}F_{\mu})+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}\text{--\,closed}~,\qquad\Phi_{\alpha}\;=\;-\frac{1}{2}\Box_{{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}}^{-1}\Big((\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}})F_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}\Big)~.

(2.17c)

The point here is that deformations of fields are not only highly coupled but they are not harmonic representatives. A natural question to ask is: can I change the gauge to find a harmonic decomposition? The answer is not likely without significant sacrifice. For example, one easily loses the holomorphic dependence of deformations on parameters. This is unlike the study of CY manifolds in which one studies holomorphic deformations that are also harmonic, viz.
{\overline{\partial}}
–harmonic
(1,1)
forms and
(2,1)
–forms. This derives from
H=0
, an additional constraint that we do not have in the more general situation.

One point of this paper is to point out that without sacrificing holomorphy, the complicated Hodge decomposition beautifully reorganises itself into harmonic representatives of a
{\overline{\mathcal{D}}}
–operator on a certain extension bundle.

\begin{split}\delta_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{(p)}&\longrightarrow\delta_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{(p)}+\varepsilon_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}F_{\mu}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\phi_{\alpha}~,\quad\delta_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{(p)\,{\dagger}}\longrightarrow\delta_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{(p)\,{\dagger}}+\varepsilon_{\alpha}{}^{\overline{\mu}}F_{\overline{\mu}}-{\partial}_{\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}}\phi_{\alpha}~,\\[4.0pt]

6

The Kaluza–Klein reduction of heterotic supergravity at the standard embedding is almost always performed in harmonic gauge. Deformations of the complexified Kähler form and deformations of complex structure are then zero modes of Laplacians. Quantities such as the moduli space metric
[
17
]
are calculated with this particular choice of representative. Interestingly, this moduli space metric is not manifestly gauge invariant. Also note that the standard embedding holomorphic gauge and harmonic gauge are the same. Outside the standard embedding, holomorphic gauge is preferred as it naturally connects the complex structure of
X
with the moduli space
\mathcal{M}
.

Setting this issue aside one can use a long exact sequence in cohomologies inherited from the short exact sequence to show

H^{(0,1)}(\mathpzc X,Q_{1})\;=\;H^{(0,1)}_{{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathpzc X,{\text{End}\,E})\oplus{\rm ker~}\mathcal{F}~.

(3.3)

The Hodge decomposition of an element of
H^{1}_{{\overline{D}}_{1}}(\mathpzc X,Q_{1})
is

\Delta_{\alpha}\;=\;\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{harm}+{\overline{\partial}}\kappa_{\alpha}~,\qquad{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\;=\;{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{harm}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\Phi_{\alpha}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}\Psi_{\alpha}^{(0,2)}~.\\

Equation (
3.3
) implies the tangent space to the moduli space is a direct sum. So there is a choice of coordinates for the moduli space in which parameters decompose into two components
y^{\alpha}=(y^{\alpha_{1}},y^{\alpha_{2}})
. The first component is one-to-one with
H^{1}(\mathpzc X,{\text{End}\,E})
:

\Delta_{\alpha_{1}}\;=\;{\overline{\partial}}\kappa_{\alpha_{1}}~,\qquad{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha_{1}}\mathcal{A}\;=\;{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha_{1}}\mathcal{A}^{harm}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\Phi_{\alpha_{1}}~.\\

where we use
{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha_{1}}\mathcal{A})=0
and so
\Delta_{\alpha_{1}}{}^{\mu}F_{\mu}=0
. This implies
\kappa_{\alpha_{1}}{}^{\mu}F_{\mu}={\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\cdots)
, which has a solution provided we can invert
F_{\mu}
and solve for
\kappa_{\alpha_{1}}{}^{\mu}
. The terms
\kappa_{\alpha_{1}}
and
\Phi_{\alpha_{1}}
are completely undetermined at this point and so there is an infinite dimensional space of solutions. In the full heterotic theory, there are additional equations of motion, and these fix
\kappa_{\alpha_{1}}
and
\Phi_{\alpha_{1}}
.

The second component of solutions is one-to-one with
{\rm ker~}\mathcal{F}\subset H^{1}(\mathpzc X,\mathpzc TX^{(1,0)})
:

\delta y^{\alpha_{2}}\Delta_{\alpha_{2}}\;=\;\delta y^{\alpha_{2}}\left(\Delta_{\alpha_{2}}^{harm}+{\overline{\partial}}\kappa_{\alpha_{2}}\right)~,\qquad\delta y^{\alpha_{2}}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha_{2}}\mathcal{A}\;=\;\delta y^{\alpha_{2}}\left({\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\Phi_{\alpha_{2}}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}\Psi^{(0,2)}_{\alpha_{2}}\right)~,\\

where
\delta y^{\alpha_{2}}
is a vector in
{\rm ker~}\mathcal{F}
. That is, the cohomology tells us what harmonic representative of
H^{1}(\mathpzc X,\mathpzc TX^{(1,0)})
appear in this equation. The Atiyah equation implies
\Psi^{(0,2)}_{\alpha_{2}}=\Box^{-1}_{{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\Delta_{\alpha_{2}}{}^{\mu}F_{\mu})
while
\kappa_{\alpha_{2}}
and
\Phi_{\alpha_{2}}
are undetermined.

The lesson here is that in writing
H^{(0,1)}(\mathpzc X,Q_{1})\;=\;H^{(0,1)}_{{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathpzc X,{\text{End}\,E})\oplus{\rm ker~}\mathcal{F}
we do not mean that the field deformations decompose into a direct product. Instead, it is telling us about what combinations of harmonic forms can appear and that they have a Hodge decomposition. This example, we have some undetermined exact terms; in the full heterotic theory these are all fixed by the equations of motion and gauge fixing.

20

, particularly in light of the D-term calculations we perform below.

The lesson is that the cohomological property

\mathcal{H}:H^{(0,p)}_{{\overline{D}}_{1}}(X,Q_{1})\longrightarrow H_{\overline{\partial}}^{(1,p+1)}(\mathpzc X)~,

is subtle. A better understanding of complexified gauge symmetries and GIT would probably help.

Setting this issue aside, it is shown in
[
7
]
that there is a long exact sequence in cohomology to describe first order deformations of
{\overline{D}}
:

H^{1}_{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\overline{D}$}}(\mathpzc X,Q)=H^{1}(\mathpzc X,\mathpzc T^{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$*$}}X)\oplus{\rm ker~}\!\mathcal{H}~,\qquad{\rm ker~}\!\mathcal{H}\subseteq H^{1}(\mathpzc X,Q_{2})~,

(4.2)

where

H^{1}(\mathpzc X,Q_{2})=H^{1}(\mathpzc X,{\rm End}\,\mathpzc TX)\oplus H^{1}(\mathpzc X,\text{End}\,E)\oplus({\rm ker~}\!\mathcal{F}\cap{\rm ker~}\!\mathcal{R})~.

(4.3)

There are the hermitian Yang–Mills and balanced equations to take into account. We show their role appears in the adjoint operator
{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}
.

4.2
The D-terms and the adjoint operator
\overline{D}^{\dagger}

A metric on
Q
describes how to pair field deformations to produce a real number. In string theory, a natural metric to use is the moduli space metric. As we preserve
N=1
supersymmetry in spacetime, the metric is Kähler. With the spurious degrees of freedom, this was first derived in
[
6
]
using a dimensional reduction of the
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
–corrected supergravity theory considered here. The result in our notation is

\begin{split}g^{\sharp}_{\alpha{\overline{\beta}}}&\;=\;\frac{1}{V}\int_{X}\Big(\frac{1}{4}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{(1,1)}\star{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{\overline{\beta}}^{(1,1)}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\theta\star{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}\theta)}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\star{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}\mathcal{A}^{\dagger})}+\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\star\Delta_{{\overline{\beta}}}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}\,g_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}\Big)~,\end{split}

(4.4)

We have written the antihermitian connections as
A=\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}
,
\mathcal{A}=A^{(0,1)}
and
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\beta}}A=-{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\beta}}\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}
with similar expressions for
\Theta=\theta-\theta^{\dagger}
.

We take the metric on a pair of sections
\mathpzc Y_{\alpha}^{(p)}
and
\mathpzc Y_{\beta}^{(p)}
to be the natural generalisation of the moduli space metric:

\begin{split}\langle\mathpzc Y^{(p)}_{\alpha},\mathpzc Y^{(p)}_{\beta}\rangle&\;=\;\frac{1}{V}\int_{X}\left(\frac{1}{4}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(p)}\star{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{{\overline{\beta}}\,{\overline{\mu}}}^{(p)}\,g^{\nu{\overline{\mu}}}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace{(\delta_{\alpha}\theta^{(p)}\star\delta_{{\overline{\beta}}}\theta^{(p)}{}^{\dagger})}\right.\\[3.0pt]
&\qquad\left.-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace{(\delta_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{(p)}\star\delta_{{\overline{\beta}}}\mathcal{A}^{(p)}{}^{\dagger}})+\Delta^{(p)}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\star\Delta^{(p)}_{{\overline{\beta}}}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}\,g_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}\right)~,\end{split}

(4.5)

where we hermitian conjugate in the appropriate way so the metric is real.

The metric allows us to define an adjoint operator
{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}
.

\langle{\overline{D}}\mathpzc Y_{\alpha}^{(p)},\mathpzc Y_{\beta}^{(p+1)}\rangle\;=\;\langle\mathpzc Y_{\alpha}^{(p)},{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}\mathpzc Y_{\beta}^{(p+1)}\rangle~.

(4.6)

It is instructive to explicitly compute:

\begin{split}\langle{\overline{D}}\mathpzc Y_{\alpha}^{(p)},\mathpzc Y_{\beta}^{(p+1)}\rangle&\;=\;\frac{1}{4V}\int X\Big(-{\overline{\partial}}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(p)}+2\text{i}\,\Delta^{(p)}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}({\partial}\omega)_{\mu\nu}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\text{Tr}\thinspace\big(\delta_{\alpha}\theta^{(p)}R_{\nu}\big)+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\text{Tr}\thinspace\big(\delta_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{(p)}\,F_{\nu}\big)\Big)\star{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{{\overline{\beta}}\,{\overline{\lambda}}}^{(p+1)}g^{\nu{\overline{\lambda}}}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4V}\text{Tr}\thinspace\int({\overline{\partial}}_{\theta}\,(\delta_{\alpha}\theta^{(p)})+R_{\mu}\Delta^{(p)}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu})\star\delta_{\overline{\beta}}\theta^{{\dagger}\,(p+1)}\\
&\qquad\qquad-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4V}\text{Tr}\thinspace\int({\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\delta_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{(p)})+F_{\mu}\Delta^{(p)}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu})\star\delta_{\overline{\beta}}\mathcal{A}^{(p+1)\,{\dagger}}+\frac{1}{V}\int{\overline{\partial}}\Delta^{(p)}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\star\Delta^{(p+1)}_{\overline{\beta}}{}^{\overline{\nu}}g_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}\\
&\;=\;\frac{1}{V}\int X\left\{\frac{1}{4}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{(p)}\star(-{\partial}^{\dagger}{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{\overline{\beta}}^{(p+1)})+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace\left(\delta_{\alpha}\theta^{(p)}\star\left((-1)^{p+1}\frac{1}{2}R_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{\overline{\beta}}{}^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}\,(p+1)}+{\partial}^{\dagger}_{\theta^{\dagger}}\delta_{\overline{\beta}}\theta^{{\dagger}\,(p+1)}\right)\right)\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace\left(\delta_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{(p)}\star\left((-1)^{p+1}\frac{1}{2}F_{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{\overline{\beta}}{}^{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}\,(p+1)}+{\partial}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}}\delta_{\overline{\beta}}\mathcal{A}^{{\dagger}\,(p+1)}\right)\right)\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!+\left.\Delta^{(p)}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\star\left((-1)^{p}\frac{\text{i}}{2}({\partial}\omega)_{\mu}{}^{\rho{\overline{\nu}}}{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{{\overline{\beta}}\,\rho{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{(p+1)}{+}\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace R_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}\,\delta_{\overline{\beta}}\theta^{{\dagger}\,{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\,(p+1)}{-}\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace F_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}\,\delta_{\overline{\beta}}\mathcal{A}^{{\dagger}\,{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{(p+1)}{+}{\partial}^{\dagger}\Delta^{(p+1)}_{{\overline{\beta}}}{}_{\mu}\right)\right\}\\
&\;=\;\langle\mathpzc Y_{\alpha}^{(p)},{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}\mathpzc Y_{\beta}^{(p+1)}\rangle~,\end{split}

where our notation is, for example,
\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}{}^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}\,(p+1)}=\frac{1}{p!}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}{}^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{{\overline{\lambda}}_{1}\cdots{\overline{\lambda}}_{p}}{\text{d}}x^{{\overline{\lambda}}_{1}\cdots{\overline{\lambda}}_{p}}
.
Comparing with (
4.5
) we identify

{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}-{\overline{\partial}}^{\dagger}&0&0&0\\
\widetilde{R}^{\dagger}&{\overline{\partial}}_{\theta}^{\dagger}&0&0\\
\widetilde{F}^{\dagger}&0&{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}&0\\
\mathcal{H}^{\dagger}&\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{\dagger}&\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{\dagger}&{\overline{\partial}}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}~,

(4.7)

where

\begin{split}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\dagger}:\Omega^{(0,p+1)}(\mathpzc TX^{*\,(1,0)})\longrightarrow\Omega^{(0,p)}({\rm End}~\mathpzc TX)~,\qquad&\widetilde{R}^{\dagger}(\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(p+1)})\;=\;(-1)^{p}\frac{1}{2}R^{\nu{\overline{\rho}}}\mathpzc Z^{(p)}_{\alpha\,\nu{\overline{\rho}}}~,\\
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\dagger}:\Omega^{(0,p+1)}(\mathpzc TX^{(0,1)})\longrightarrow\Omega^{(0,p)}({\rm End}~\mathpzc TX)~,\qquad&\widetilde{F}^{\dagger}(\mathpzc Z^{(p+1)}_{\alpha})=(-1)^{p}\frac{1}{2}F^{\rho{\overline{\nu}}}\mathpzc Z^{(p)}_{\alpha\,\rho{\overline{\nu}}}~,\\
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{\dagger}:\Omega^{(0,p+1)}({\rm End}\mathpzc TX)\longrightarrow\Omega^{(0,p)}(\mathpzc TX^{(0,1)})~,\qquad&\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{\dagger}(\delta_{\alpha}\theta^{(p+1)})\;=\;-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace(R^{\nu{\overline{\rho}}}\,\delta_{\alpha}\theta^{(p)}_{\overline{\rho}})~,\\
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{\dagger}:\Omega^{(0,p+1)}({\rm End}\mathpzc E)\longrightarrow\Omega^{(0,p)}(\mathpzc TX^{(0,1)})~,\qquad&\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{\dagger}(\delta_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{(p+1)})\;=\;\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace(F^{\nu{\overline{\rho}}}\,\delta_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}_{\overline{\rho}}^{(p)})~,\\
\mathcal{H}^{\dagger}:\Omega^{(0,p+1)}(\mathpzc TX^{*\,(1,0)})\longrightarrow\Omega^{(0,p)}(\mathpzc TX^{(1,0)})~,\qquad&\mathcal{H}^{\dagger}(\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(p+1)})\;=\;(-1)^{p+1}\frac{\text{i}}{2}({\overline{\partial}}\omega)^{\nu\rho{\overline{\lambda}}}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\rho{\overline{\lambda}}}^{(p+1)}~.\end{split}

(4.8)

The action of
{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}
on
\mathpzc Y_{\alpha}^{(1)}
is

{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(1)}\\
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\theta\\
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}A\\
\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\end{pmatrix}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}\nabla^{\overline{\mu}}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu{\overline{\mu}}}\\
{\overline{\partial}}_{\theta}^{\dagger}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\theta+\frac{1}{2}R^{\nu{\overline{\mu}}}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu{\overline{\mu}}}\\
{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}+\frac{1}{2}F^{\nu{\overline{\mu}}}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu{\overline{\mu}}}\\
-\frac{\text{i}}{2}({\overline{\partial}}\omega)^{\mu\rho{\overline{\lambda}}}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\rho{\overline{\lambda}}}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace(R^{\mu{\overline{\rho}}}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\theta_{\overline{\rho}})+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace(F^{\mu{\overline{\rho}}}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}_{\overline{\rho}})-\nabla^{{\rm Ch/H}\,{\overline{\mu}}}\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\mu}}}{}^{\nu}\end{pmatrix}~,

(4.9)

where we use that
R
and
F
are antihermitian and in the last line, we used the balanced equation to derive an identity

{\overline{\partial}}^{\dagger}\Delta^{\mu}~=-\star\frac{\text{i}\omega^{2}}{2}\,\big({\partial}\Delta^{\mu}+g^{\mu{\overline{\sigma}}}{\partial}g_{{\overline{\sigma}}\rho}~\Delta^{\rho}\big)~=-\nabla^{{\rm Ch/H}\,{\overline{\mu}}}\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\mu}}}{}^{\nu}~.

(4.10)

We then find that

{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}\mathpzc Y^{(1)}_{\alpha}\;=\;0~,

is precisely the D-term equations (
2.13
)-(
2.15
).

Hence, in holomorphic gauge with spurious degrees of freedom we find that

{\overline{D}}\mathpzc Y_{\alpha}^{(1)}\;=\;0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{F-terms}~,\qquad\qquad{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}\mathpzc Y_{\alpha}^{(1)}\;=\;0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{D-terms}~.

This is despite the fact the individual deformations such as
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}
or
\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}
are not harmonic representatives as demonstrated explicitly in (
2.16
), (
2.17a
)–(
2.17c
). Instead, if we work
(0,1)
–forms valued in
Q
then all we need to do is study harmonic representatives of this
{\overline{D}}
–operator. However, we are not yet at the physical case yet: we still need to eliminate the spurious degrees of freedom.

5
Eliminating the spurious degrees of freedom

The space of deformations of
{\overline{D}}
on the bundle
Q
is not the moduli space of string theory. String scattering amplitudes and/or supersymmetry tell us that
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\Theta
is not an independent degree of freedom, but determined in terms of the remaining fields. In
[
3
,
5
]
this is calculated to lowest order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
:

{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\Theta{}_{\overline{\mu}}{}^{\nu}{}_{\sigma}\;=\;\nabla_{\sigma}\,\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\mu}}}{}^{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla^{\nu}\,\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\sigma{\overline{\mu}}}~,

(5.1)

where

\nabla_{\sigma}\,\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\mu}}}{}^{\nu}\;=\;{\partial}_{\sigma}\,\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\mu}}}{}^{\nu}+\Gamma_{\sigma}{}^{\nu}{}_{\lambda}\,\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\mu}}}{}^{\lambda}~,\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla_{{\overline{\mu}}}\,\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\sigma{\overline{\nu}}}\;=\;{\partial}_{{\overline{\mu}}}\,\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\sigma{\overline{\nu}}}-\Gamma_{{\overline{\mu}}}{}^{\overline{\lambda}}{}_{\overline{\nu}}\,\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\sigma{\overline{\lambda}}}~.

The
\Gamma
are the Levi–Civita symbols, which because this result is derived for solutions which have a smooth limit
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\to 0
with
H\to 0
, are the same as the Chern connection. The dilaton is constant to
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{3}

[
3
]
by a choice of gauge fixing; see also appendix C of
[
4
]
for the calculation in the notation of this paper.

5.1
Constructing the bundle
\mathcal{Q}

We now turn to constructing an extension bundle
\mathcal{Q}
whose sections describe the physical degrees of freedom.
Recall that to first order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
, the first order deformations satisfy an Atiyah equation
{\overline{\partial}}_{\theta}({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\theta)=\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}R_{\mu}
. We check (
1.1
) satisfies this explicitly. First, note

\begin{split}\nabla_{\overline{\rho}}\nabla_{\nu}\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}&=\nabla_{\overline{\rho}}\left({\partial}_{\nu}\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}+\Gamma_{\nu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\lambda}\,\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\lambda}\ \right)~,\\
[\nabla_{\overline{\rho}},\nabla_{\nu}]\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\sigma}}\,}{}^{\mu}&=({\partial}_{\overline{\rho}}\Gamma_{\nu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\lambda})\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\sigma}}\,}{}^{\lambda}+({\partial}_{\nu}\Gamma_{\overline{\rho}}{}^{\overline{\lambda}}{}_{\overline{\sigma}})\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\lambda}}\,}{}^{\mu}\;=\;R^{\mu}{}_{\lambda{\overline{\rho}}\nu}\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\sigma}}\,}{}^{\lambda}-R^{\overline{\lambda}}{}_{{\overline{\sigma}}{\overline{\rho}}\nu}\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\lambda}}\,}{}^{\mu}~,\end{split}

where
R^{\mu}{}_{\lambda{\overline{\rho}}\nu}
is the Riemann curvature tensor on a complex manifold.
Second,

\begin{split}{\overline{\partial}}({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\Theta)^{\mu}{}_{\nu}&={\text{d}}x^{\overline{\rho}}\,\nabla_{\overline{\rho}}\left(\nabla_{\nu}\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}+\text{i}\nabla^{\mu}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega_{\nu}^{(0,1)}\right)\\
&={\text{d}}x^{\overline{\rho}}[\nabla_{\overline{\rho}},\nabla_{\nu}]\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}{\text{d}}x^{\overline{\rho}}[\nabla_{\overline{\rho}},\nabla^{\mu}]\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\nu}^{(0,1)}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\mu}({\overline{\partial}}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\nu}^{(0,1)})\\
&={\text{d}}x^{{\overline{\rho}}{\overline{\sigma}}}R^{\mu}{}_{\lambda{\overline{\rho}}\nu}\Delta_{\alpha\,{\overline{\sigma}}}{}^{\lambda}+{\text{d}}x^{{\overline{\rho}}{\overline{\sigma}}}R^{\overline{\lambda}}{}_{{\overline{\sigma}}\nu{\overline{\rho}}}\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\lambda}}\,}{}^{\mu}\\
&=\Delta_{\alpha\,}{}^{\lambda}R^{\mu}{}_{\nu\lambda}{}^{(0,1)}~,\end{split}

(5.2)

where
R^{\overline{\lambda}}{}_{{\overline{\sigma}}\nu{\overline{\rho}}}=R^{\overline{\lambda}}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}
is used in the penultimate line. We use that
R^{(0,2)}=0
and
{\overline{\partial}}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
. In fact because
\theta
is an instanton (to first order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
),
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\Theta
obeys an Atiyah equation and so we conclude that this result actually holds up to and including first order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
. Hence, if there are any
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
corrections to (
1.1
) then we know that in (
5.2
) that they must cancel.

A corollary of (
5.2
) is that the expression (
5.1
) is a solution of
{\overline{D}}\mathpzc Y^{(1)}_{\alpha}=0
and so bona-fide element of the cohomology
H_{\overline{D}}^{1}(\mathpzc X,Q)
. This is serves as another consistency check and is part of our intuition that the true moduli space is some subspace of
H_{\overline{D}}^{1}(\mathpzc X,Q)
.

Using the symmetry
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\Theta{}_{\overline{\mu}}{}^{\overline{\nu}}{}_{{\overline{\sigma}}}\;=\;\!-g^{{\overline{\nu}}\lambda}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\Theta_{{\overline{\mu}}}{}^{\rho}{}_{\lambda}\,g_{\rho{\overline{\sigma}}}
we can write

\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\text{Tr}\thinspace\big({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\Theta\,R\big)\;=\;{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\,\big(\nabla_{\nu}\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla^{\mu}\,\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(0,1)}\big)R^{\nu}{}_{\mu}~,

(5.3)

where
R^{\nu}{}_{\mu}=R_{\rho{\overline{\sigma}}}{}^{\nu}{}_{\mu}\,{\text{d}}x^{\rho{\overline{\sigma}}}
is the Riemann tensor and we have used
R_{{\overline{\mu}}\nu}=-R_{\nu{\overline{\mu}}}
.

Now, using (
5.2
) with the Bianchi identity for
R
, (
A.1
) we find

{\overline{\partial}}\,\left(\text{Tr}\thinspace{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\Theta^{(0,1)}R\right)\;=\;\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\,\text{Tr}\thinspace R_{\mu}{\,\wedge\,}R~+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~.

(5.4)

Using (
5.3
) we see that a consequence of (
5.4
) is that

{\overline{\partial}}\Big((\nabla^{\mu}\,\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(0,1)})R^{\nu}{}_{\mu}\Big)\;=\;0~.

and so it should be
{\overline{\partial}}
–exact. Indeed, this is the case and we now derive an explicit expression. Start by recalling that
R^{\nu}{}_{\mu}
obeys a hermitian Yang-Mills equation
\nabla^{\mu}R^{\nu}{}_{\mu\rho{\overline{\sigma}}}=0
to first order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
and so

(\nabla^{\mu}\,\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(0,1)})R^{\nu}{}_{\mu}\;=\;\nabla^{\mu}\,(\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(0,1)}R^{\nu}{}_{\mu})~.

As the Levi–Civita symbols are pure to this order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
we find the term in parenthesis can be written as

\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(0,1)}R^{\nu}{}_{\mu}\;=\;\!-{\text{d}}x^{\rho}[\nabla_{\rho},{\overline{\partial}}]\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\mu}^{(0,1)}~,

so that

(\nabla^{\mu}\,\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(0,1)})R^{\nu}{}_{\mu}\;=\;\!-{\text{d}}x^{\rho}\nabla^{\mu}\left([\nabla_{\rho},{\overline{\partial}}]\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\mu}^{(0,1)}\right)~.

Using that
{\overline{\partial}}(\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\mu}^{(0,1)})=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
and that
R^{(0,2)}=0
we find

(\nabla^{\mu}\,\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(0,1)})R^{\nu}{}_{\mu}\;=\;\!-{\overline{\partial}}\left({\text{d}}x^{\rho}\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\rho}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\mu}^{(0,1)}\right)~.

Note that
\nabla_{\rho}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\mu}^{(0,1)}={\partial}_{\rho}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\mu}^{(0,1)}-\Gamma_{\rho}{}^{\lambda}{}_{\mu}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\lambda}^{(0,1)}
and so does not become
{\partial}
.

Using these results (
2.8
) becomes:

{\overline{\partial}}\Big(\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{(1,1)}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}{\text{d}}x^{\rho}\left(\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\rho}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\mu}^{(0,1)}\right)\Big)-2\text{i}\,\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}({\partial}\omega)_{\mu}+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}R^{\nu}{}_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\big(\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\big)-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\text{Tr}\thinspace\big({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\,F\big)\;=\;0~.

(5.5)

It is natural to perform a field redefinition

\widetilde{\mathpzc}Z_{\alpha}^{(1,1)}\;=\;\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{(1,1)}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}{\text{d}}x^{\rho}\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\rho}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\mu}^{(0,1)}~,

(5.6)

which is well–defined in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
–perturbation theory, and in particular, invertible. It is not overly surprising that the complexified hermitian form needs to be corrected in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
. The field redefinition will propagate into other relevant equations, such as deformations of the HYM and balanced equations and the moduli space metric. But it is the natural thing to do to realise a holomorphic structure on the extension bundle.

There is a cohomology that captures these field deformations. To describe it, we need to generalise some of the above results above to
(0,p)
–forms. First, we ansatz that (
1.1
) generalises to

\delta_{\alpha}\theta^{(p)\,\nu}{}_{\sigma}\;=\;\nabla_{\sigma}\,\Delta^{(p)}_{\alpha}{}^{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla^{\nu}\,\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\sigma}^{(p)}~.

(5.7)

Second we need the generalisation of the
{\overline{D}}
–operator for the spurious case, written in components in (
C.10
). The only non–trivial part is to evaluate
\text{Tr}\thinspace\delta_{\alpha}\theta^{(p)}R_{\nu}
using (
5.7
) and substitute into the first line of (
C.10
) giving

\widehat{\widetilde{\mathpzc}Z}^{(p+1)}_{\nu}\;=\;-{\overline{\partial}}{\widetilde{\mathpzc}Z}_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(p)}+2\text{i}\,\Delta^{(p)}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}({\partial}\omega)_{\mu\nu}+{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\nabla_{\sigma}\big(\Delta^{(p)}_{\alpha}{}^{\tau}\big)R^{\sigma}{}_{\tau}{}_{\nu{\overline{\lambda}}}{\text{d}}x^{\overline{\lambda}}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\text{Tr}\thinspace\big(\delta_{a}\mathcal{A}^{(p)}\,F_{\nu}\big)~.

A consistency check is that the relation (
5.7
) satisfies the second line of (
C.10
). We also have the field redefinition

\widetilde{\mathpzc}Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(p)}\;=\;\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\nu}^{(p)}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}(-1)^{p}\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\mu}^{(p)}~.

(5.8)

Q_{1}
defined in (
3.1
) is unchanged.

is now not necessary. Consider a short exact sequence

6
Conclusion and Outlook

So we have shown that physical field deformations in holomorphic gauge are given by harmonic representatives of
{\overline{\mathcal{D}}}
operator on the new
\mathcal{Q}
bundle with the spurious degrees of freedom eliminated. These are harmonic with respect to the moduli space metric in
[
4
]
. We see

{\overline{\mathcal{D}}}\mathpzc Y_{\alpha}^{(1)}\;=\;0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{F-terms}~,\qquad\qquad{\overline{\mathcal{D}}}^{\dagger}\mathpzc Y_{\alpha}^{(1)}\;=\;0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{D-terms}~.

Along the way we also demonstrated that the non–physical calculation, in which one has additional spurious degrees of freedom, are captured by a
{\overline{D}}
–operator with an analogous relation between the F-terms and D-terms and
{\overline{D}}
–closure and
{\overline{D}}
–coclosure. That means that with respect to the
{\overline{D}}
–operator we could equally well call holomorphic gauge, harmonic gauge.

It would be interesting to reconcile these results with the complexified gauge transformations and moment maps in a GIT-type analysis of these solutions studied in
[
21
,
20
]
. There are also examples of mathematical interest, such as that related to the Hopf surface which do not have an obvious valid
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
–expansion, yet appear to be governed by a
(0,2)
superconformal algebra
[
22
]
. Nonetheless, one could still try to construct the adjoint operator
{\overline{\mathcal{D}}}^{\dagger}
and compare with the space of deformations obtained. It would also be interesting to see to what extent the structure discovered in this paper extends to next order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
. We expect the generalised geometry approach of
[
9
,
21
,
23
,
25
,
20
]
to be relevant for these scenarios.

One could also ask about higher orders in deformation theory. Presumably at second and third order in deformation theory there are relations with the
L_{3}
algebra described in
[
16
]
. It is known in the study of CY manifolds that special geometry involves relations between the moduli and matter sectors. It would be interesting to extend these results to the charged matter sector utilising the matter metric
[
26
]
. The results here are reminiscent of the study of the
tt^{*}
equations of the special geometry of CY manifolds
[
27
]
, in which one studies a fiber bundle whose base manifold is the moduli space and fibres are harmonic elements of the cohomologies
H^{(2,1)}\oplus H^{(1,1)}
. Until now, we haven’t been able to demonstrate a cohomology with harmonic representative capturing the deformations of the full heterotic theory. This might be a new door worth opening.

Appendix B
A supergravity no-go theorem

No–go theorems are well-known in the context of type II and M-theory flux vacua. Classical supergravity equations of motion, when integrated over a compact manifolds, force all non–trivial fluxes to vanish. The evasion of these theorems comes in the form of
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
–corrections to supergravity, interpreted as the contribution of orientifold planes, objects with negative stress–energy tensor. Here we briefly recount an analogous result in heterotic string theory; this demonstrates that if one wants solutions of supergravity then

.

In this section only we work to
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{3}

(B.2)

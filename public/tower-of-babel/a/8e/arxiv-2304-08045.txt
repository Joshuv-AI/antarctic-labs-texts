Singularities of focal sets of pseudo-spherical framed immersions in the three-dimensional anti-de Sitter space Thanks: E-mail address: otuncer@hacettepe.edu.tr


Abstract
We introduce pseudo-spherical non-null framed curves in the three-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime and establish the existence and uniqueness of these curves. We then give moving frames along pseudo-spherical framed curves, which are well-defined even at singular points of the curve. These moving frames enable us to define evolutes and focal surfaces of pseudo-spherical framed immersions. We investigate the singularity properties of these evolutes and focal surfaces. We then reveal that the evolute of a pseudo-spherical framed immersion is the set of singular points of its focal surface. We also interpret evolutes and focal surfaces as the discriminant and the secondary discriminant sets of certain height functions, which allows us to explain evolutes and focal surfaces as wavefronts from the viewpoint of Legendrian singularity theory. Examples are provided to flesh out our results, and we use the hyperbolic Hopf map to visualize these examples. Keywords: evolute; focal surface; framed curve; anti-de Sitter space; singularity Mathematics Subject Classification: 53A35; 57R45; 58K05

Introduction

The main tool for investigating the local differential geometry of regular curves is the well-known Frenet frame. This frame is well-defined at any point of the regular curve. However we cannot use the Frenet frame to investigate the local differential geometry of general curves that may have singularities. Therefore having a well-defined moving frame along these curves becomes crucial. The good news is several authors have defined such moving frames for certain families of curves having singularities in different ambient spaces. This journey has begun with the work of Fukunaga and Takahashi
[
7
]

This paper serves two purposes: to extend the regular curves in the anti-de Sitter 3-space to pseudo-spherical framed curves that may have singularities and to investigate evolutes and focal surfaces of these framed curves in terms of singularity theory. The first goal is not hard to achieve; we make use of the Legendrian dualities for pseudo-spheres in semi-Euclidean space with index 2
[
5
]
. To achieve the second goal, our main tool is a moving frame along the pseudo-spherical framed curve that is well-defined at even singular points. We also use certain height functions to explain evolutes and focal surfaces as wavefronts from the viewpoint of Legendrian singularity theory.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we begin with a brief review of the semi-Euclidean 4-space with index 2 and the local differential geometry of regular curves in the anti-de Sitter 3-space. In Section 3 we introduce pseudo-spherical spacelike and timelike framed curves in the anti-de Sitter 3-space. We give the existence and uniqueness theorems for these curves. We also define three types of moving frames along these curves that are isometric under rotations. We then define parallel curves of pseudo-spherical framed curves and show that these parallel curves are also pseudo-spherical framed curves in the anti-de Sitter 3-space. In Section 4 we introduce evolutes and focal surfaces of pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersions. We show that evolutes are also pseudo-spherical framed immersions. We then obtain the evolute of a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed curve as the set of singular values of the focal surface of the same curve. We also define certain height functions for pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersions and deduce that the discriminant and secondary discriminant sets of these height functions coincide with the evolute and the focal surface of this immersion. We finally give an example of pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersion and visualize the projections on the hyperbolic 2-space of this immersion and its evolute by using the hyperbolic Hopf map. In Section 5 we obtain similar results to those in Section 4 for pseudo-spherical timelike framed immersions in the anti-de Sitter 3-space.

Preliminaries

The semi-Euclidean 4-space with index
2
denoted by
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
is the real vector space with a pseudo-scalar product given by

\langle u,w\rangle=-u_{1}w_{1}-u_{2}w_{2}+u_{3}w_{3}+u_{4}w_{4},

where
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}),\,w=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},w_{4})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

Vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
are classified depending on this pseudo-scalar product. Consider a non-zero vector
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
. The vector
u
is called spacelike, timelike, or lightlike (null) if
\langle u,u\rangle>0
,
\langle u,u\rangle<0
or
\langle u,u\rangle=0
, respectively. The pseudo-norm of the vector
u
is given by
\|u\|=\sqrt{\lvert\langle u,u\rangle\rvert}
. For three arbitrary vectors
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})
,
v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})
, and
w=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},w_{4})
, the triple vector product is defined by

u\times v\times w=\begin{vmatrix}-e_{1}&-e_{2}&e_{3}&e_{4}\\
u_{1}&u_{2}&u_{3}&u_{4}\\
v_{1}&v_{2}&v_{3}&v_{4}\\
w_{1}&w_{2}&w_{3}&w_{4}\end{vmatrix}

where the set
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
.

In the semi-Euclidean 4-space with index 2, curves are classified depending on their tangent vectors. A curve is said to be spacelike, timelike, or lightlike (null) if the tangent vector of the curve is spacelike, timelike, or lightlike (null), respectively.

There are three types of pseudo-spheres in the semi-Euclidean 4-space with index 2. The anti-de Sitter 3-space, pseudo
3
-sphere with index 2, and nullcone at the origin
are respectively defined by

\displaystyle AdS^{3}=\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}\,|\,\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}\rangle=-1\},

\displaystyle S^{3}_{2}=\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}\,|\,\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}\rangle=1\},

\displaystyle\Lambda^{3}=\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}\,|\,\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}\rangle=0\}.

We will make use of the hyperbolic Hopf map
\mathbf{h}
defined by

]

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}:

\displaystyle AdS^{3}\to H^{2}(1/2)

\displaystyle(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})\mapsto\left(u_{1}u_{3}+u_{2}u_{4},u_{1}u_{4}-u_{2}u_{3},\dfrac{u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}+u_{3}^{2}+u_{4}^{2}}{2}\right),

(2.1)

where
H^{2}(1/2)=\{(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{1}\>|\>y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}-y_{3}^{2}=-1/4\,\text{and}\,y_{3}>0\}

This map will allow us to get projections on

We now discuss the local differential geometry of regular curves in the anti-de Sitter 3-space. Let us begin with spacelike curves in
AdS^{3}
. Let
\gamma:I\to AdS^{3}
be a regular spacelike curve.
\gamma^{\prime}=d\gamma/dt
is a spacelike vector and
\|\gamma^{\prime}(t)\|\neq 0
for all
t\in I
. Since
\gamma
is a spacelike regular curve, it admits an arc-length parametrization
s=s(t)
. So we may assume that
\gamma(s)
is a unit-speed curve. Let
T(s)=\gamma^{\prime}(s)
be the unit tangent vector. Since
\langle\gamma(s),\gamma(s)\rangle=-1
, we have
\langle\gamma(s),T(s)\rangle=0
. From a direct calculation, we find that
\langle\gamma(s),T^{\prime}(s)\rangle=-1
. We now take
N_{1}(s)=T^{\prime}(s)-\gamma(s)
and
N_{2}(s)=\gamma(s)\times T(s)\times N_{1}(s)
. It is easy to check that
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are normal vectors of the spacelike curve
\gamma
in
AdS^{3}
. These normal vectors can be spacelike or timelike vectors. We also define the curvature by
\kappa_{g}(s)=\|T^{\prime}(s)-\gamma(s)\|
. So we say that the spacelike curve
\gamma
is a geodesic in
AdS^{3}
if
\kappa_{g}(s)=0
and
N_{1}(s)=0
. In the case of
\kappa_{g}(s)\neq 0
, we are able to define the following unit vectors.

n_{1}(s)=\dfrac{T^{\prime}(s)-\gamma(s)}{\|T^{\prime}(s)-\gamma(s)\|}=\dfrac{N_{1}(s)}{\|N_{1}(s)\|},\qquad n_{2}(s)=\gamma(s)\times T(s)\times n_{1}(s).

Hence the set
\{\gamma(s),T(s),n_{1}(s),n_{2}(s)\}
forms a pseudo-orthonormal frame along the spacelike curve
\gamma
. Then the Frenet-Serret type formulas are governed by

\begin{pmatrix}\gamma^{\prime}(s)\\
T^{\prime}(s)\\
n_{1}^{\prime}(s)\\
n_{2}^{\prime}(s)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&0\\
1&0&\kappa_{g}(s)&0\\
0&-\delta\kappa_{g}(s)&0&\tau_{g}(s)\\
0&0&\tau_{g}(s)&0\par\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\gamma(s)\\
T(s)\\
n_{1}(s)\\
n_{2}(s)\end{pmatrix},

where
\delta=\langle n_{1}(s),n_{1}(s)\rangle
and
\tau_{g}(s)=\frac{\delta}{\kappa_{g}^{2}(s)}\det(\gamma(s),\gamma^{\prime}(s),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s),\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}(s))
.

We now describe the local differential geometry of regular timelike curves in
AdS^{3}
. Let
\gamma(s):I\to AdS^{3}
be a regular unit-speed timelike curve. Then
T(s)=\gamma^{\prime}(s)
is the unit tangent vector to
\gamma
. It is easy to see that
\langle\gamma(s),T(s)\rangle=0
and then
\langle\gamma(s),T(s)\rangle=1
. The vector
N_{1}(s)=T^{\prime}(s)+\gamma(s)
is pseudo-orthogonal to both
\gamma(s)
and
T(s)
. Let
N_{2}(s)=\gamma(s)\times T(s)\times N_{1}(s)
. It is easy to check that
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are spacelike normal vectors of the timelike curve
\gamma
in
AdS^{3}
. Define
\kappa_{g}(s)=\|T^{\prime}(s)+\gamma(s)\|
. Then in the case of
\kappa_{g}(s)\neq 0
, we define the unit spacelike vectors

n_{1}(s)=\dfrac{T^{\prime}(s)+\gamma(s)}{\|T^{\prime}(s)+\gamma(s)\|}=\dfrac{N_{1}(s)}{\|N_{1}(s)\|},\qquad n_{2}(s)=\gamma(s)\times T(s)\times n_{1}(s).

The set
\{\gamma(s),T(s),n_{1}(s),n_{2}(s)\}
forms a pseudo-orthonormal frame along the timelike curve
\gamma
. Then the Frenet-Serret type formulas are given by

\begin{pmatrix}\gamma^{\prime}(s)\\
T^{\prime}(s)\\
n_{1}^{\prime}(s)\\
n_{2}^{\prime}(s)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&0\\
-1&0&\kappa_{g}(s)&0\\
0&\kappa_{g}(s)&0&\tau_{g}(s)\\
0&0&-\tau_{g}(s)&0\par\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\gamma(s)\\
T(s)\\
n_{1}(s)\\
n_{2}(s)\end{pmatrix},

where
\tau_{g}(s)=-\frac{1}{\kappa_{g}^{2}(s)}\det(\gamma(s),\gamma^{\prime}(s),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s),\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}(s))
.

Pseudo-spherical framed curves in the anti-de Sitter 3-space

In this section, we consider the local differential geometry of smooth curves in the three-dimensional anti-de Sitter space. If the curve has singular points, we cannot define the pseudo-orthonormal Frenet-type frame at these
singular points given in the previous section. We also cannot use the Frenet–Serret type formulas to study the properties of the original
curve. In order to overcome this obstacle, we shall take advantage of the way developed by
[
7
,
15
]
. So we shall introduce pseudo-spherical framed curves in the three-dimensional anti-de Sitter space. Similar to the regular case, we have two types of these framed curves.

3.1
Pseudo-spherical spacelike framed curves in
AdS^{3}

Let
\gamma_{s}:I\to AdS^{3}
be a smooth curve. Then
(\gamma_{s},v_{1},v_{2}):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{1}
is called a
pseudo-spherical spacelike framed curve
if
(\gamma_{s}(s),v_{1}(s))^{*}\theta=0
and
(\gamma_{s}(s),v_{2}(s))^{*}\theta=0
for all
s\in I
, where

\Delta_{1}=\{(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})\,|\,\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}\rangle=0\}\subset AdS^{3}\times{S}^{3}_{2}\,\,(\text{or}\,\,{S}^{3}_{2}\times AdS^{3})

is a
4
-dimensional contact manifold, and
\theta
is a canonical contact
1
-form on
\Delta_{1}

[
5
]
. The condition
(\gamma_{s}(s),v_{i}(s))^{*}\theta=0
(
i=1,2
) is equivalent to
\langle\gamma_{s}^{\prime}(s),v_{i}(s)\rangle=0
(
i=1,2
) for all
s\in I
. If
(\gamma_{s},v_{1},v_{2})
is an immersion, then it is called a
pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersion
.

We call
\gamma_{s}:I\to AdS^{3}
a
pseudo-spherical spacelike framed base curve
if there exists a smooth map
(v_{1},v_{2}):I\to\Delta_{1}
for which
(\gamma_{s},v_{1},v_{2})
is a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed curve.

Define
\mu(s)=\gamma_{s}(s)\times v_{1}(s)\times v_{2}(s)
. Then the set
\{\gamma_{s}(s),v_{1}(s),v_{2}(s),\mu(s)\}
is a pseudo-orthonormal frame along
\gamma_{s}
. This frame is well-defined even at singular points of
\gamma_{s}
. The Frenet-Serret type formulas for this frame are given by

\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{s}^{\prime}(s)\\
v_{1}^{\prime}(s)\\
v_{2}^{\prime}(s)\\
\mu^{\prime}(s)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&\alpha(s)\\
0&0&\ell(s)&m(s)\\
0&\ell(s)&0&n(s)\\
\alpha(s)&-\epsilon m(s)&\epsilon n(s)&0\par\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{s}(s)\\
v_{1}(s)\\
v_{2}(s)\\
\mu(s)\end{pmatrix},

(3.1)

where
\epsilon=\langle v_{1}(s),v_{1}(s)\rangle
,
\alpha(s)=\langle\gamma_{s}^{\prime}(s),\mu(s)\rangle
,
\ell(s)=-\epsilon\langle v_{1}^{\prime}(s),v_{2}(s)\rangle
,
m(s)=\langle v_{1}^{\prime}(s),\mu(s)\rangle
, and
n(s)=\langle v_{2}^{\prime}(s),\mu(s)\rangle

(\alpha,\ell,m,n):I\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
the

of the pseudo-spherical spacelike framed curve
(\gamma_{s},v_{1},v_{2})
. Notice that
s_{0}
is a singular point of
\gamma_{s}
if and only if
\alpha(s_{0})=0
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\gamma,v_{1},v_{2})
and

\tilde{\gamma}(s)=A(\gamma(s)),\quad\tilde{v}_{1}(s)=A(v_{1}(s)),\quad\tilde{v}_{2}(s)=A(v_{2}(s)).

Theorem 3.2

(Existence of pseudo-spherical spacelike framed curves)
.

For a smooth mapping
(\alpha,\ell,m,n):I\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
, there exists a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed curve
(\gamma_{s},v_{1},v_{2})
such that
\alpha

\ell

Proof.

There are two cases we need to deal with since

is timelike and so

\dfrac{d}{ds}F(s)=A(s)F(s),\quad F(s_{0})=\eta,

0&0&\ell(s)&m(s)\\
0&\ell(s)&0&n(s)\\
\alpha(s)&m(s)&-n(s)&0\end{pmatrix}.

By the existence theorem of a solution of a system of linear ordinary differential equations, there exists a solution

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{ds}(F^{t}(s)\eta F(s))

Proposition 3.4

If
(\gamma_{s},v_{1},v_{2})

are parametrically equivalent by the change of parameter

, that is,
(\tilde{\gamma}_{s}(s),\tilde{v}_{1}(s),\tilde{v}_{2}(s))=(\gamma_{s}(u(s)),v_{1}(u(s)),v_{2}(u(s)))
for all

Hence the curvature depends on the parametrization.
∎

Let
(\gamma_{s},v_{1},v_{2}):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{1}
be a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed curve with the curvature

. Now we shall show that it is possible to construct a new frame along

similar to the Bishop frame

Then
(\gamma_{s},\bar{v}_{1},\bar{v}_{2}):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{1}

. So the set
\{\gamma_{s},\bar{v}_{1},\bar{v}_{2},\mu(s)\}
is a pseudo-orthonormal frame along
\gamma_{s}
. Using the formulas in (
3.11
), we find that

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{1}(s)

\displaystyle=(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\ell(s))\sinh\theta(s)\,v_{1}(s)+(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\ell(s))\cosh\theta(s)\,v_{2}(s)

\displaystyle\qquad+(m(s)\cosh\theta(s)+n(s)\sinh\theta(s))\mu(s),

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{2}(s)

\displaystyle=(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\ell(s))\cosh\theta(s)\,v_{1}(s)+(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\ell(s))\sinh\theta(s)\,v_{2}(s)

\displaystyle\qquad+(m(s)\sinh\theta(s)+n(s)\cosh\theta(s))\mu(s).

Now set
\theta^{\prime}(s)=-\ell(s)
. In this case we call the set
\{\gamma_{s},\bar{v}_{1},\bar{v}_{2},\mu(s)\}
the
Bishop-type frame
along
\gamma_{s}
. We have the following derivative formulas.

\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{s}^{\prime}(s)\\
\bar{v}_{1}^{\prime}(s)\\
\bar{v}_{2}^{\prime}(s)\\
\mu^{\prime}(s)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&\alpha(s)\\

0&0&0&\bar{n}(s)\\
\alpha(s)&-\epsilon\bar{m}(s)&\epsilon\bar{n}(s)&0\par\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{s}(s)\\
\bar{v}_{1}(s)\\
\bar{v}_{2}(s)\\
\mu(s)\end{pmatrix},

(3.7)

where

\begin{pmatrix}\bar{m}(s)\\
\bar{n}(s)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cosh\theta(s)&\sinh\theta(s)\\
\sinh\theta(s)&\cosh\theta(s)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}m(s)\\
n(s)\end{pmatrix}.

Let
(\gamma_{s},v_{1},v_{2})
be a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersion.
We will mainly use another moving frame along
\gamma_{s}
defined as follows. Let

defined by

\{\gamma_{s},f_{1},f_{2},\mu=\gamma_{s}\times f_{1}\times f_{2}\}

(3.8)

We close this section by defining anti-de Sitter parallel curves of pseudo-spherical spacelike framed curves. Let
(\gamma_{s},v_{1},v_{2}):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{1}
be a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed curve with the curvature

. We define a mapping
\gamma_{s}^{\phi}:I\to AdS^{3}

Moreover, if

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(\gamma_{s},v_{1},v_{2}):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{1}
be a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed curve with the curvature
(\alpha,\ell,m,n)
. For a fixed real number
\phi
and
\theta^{\prime}(s)=-\ell(s)
, the anti-de Sitter parallel
(\gamma_{s}^{\phi},v_{1}^{\phi},v_{2}^{\phi}):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{1}
is a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed curve with the curvature
(\alpha^{\phi},0,m^{\phi},n^{\phi})
, where

\displaystyle v_{1}^{\phi}(s)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(P^{2}(\theta(s))-R^{2}(\theta(s)))g(\phi)\gamma_{s}(s)+f(\phi)\left(P(\theta(s))v_{1}(s)+R(\theta(s))v_{2}(s)\right),

\displaystyle v_{2}^{\phi}(s)

\displaystyle=R(\theta(s))v_{1}(s)+P(\theta(s))v_{2}(s),

\displaystyle\alpha^{\phi}(s)

\displaystyle=\alpha(s)f(\phi)+g(\phi)\big(P(\theta(s))m(s)+R(\theta(s))n(s)\big),

\displaystyle m^{\phi}(s)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(P^{2}(\theta(s))-R^{2}(\theta(s)))g(\phi)\alpha(s)+f(\phi)(P(\theta(s))m(s)+R(\theta(s))n(s)),

\displaystyle n^{\phi}(s)

\displaystyle=R(\theta(s))m(s)+P(\theta(s))n(s).

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\langle\gamma_{s}^{\phi},v_{1}^{\phi}\rangle=0
,
\langle\gamma_{s}^{\phi},v_{2}^{\phi}\rangle=0
, and
\langle v_{1}^{\phi},v_{2}^{\phi}\rangle=0
. The derivative of (
3.9
) gives

(\gamma_{s}^{\phi})^{\prime}(s)=\left(\alpha(s)f(\phi)+g(\phi)\big(P(\theta(s))m(s)+R(\theta(s))n(s)\big)\right)\mu(s).

Hence
\langle(\gamma_{s}^{\phi})^{\prime},v_{1}^{\phi}\rangle=0
and
\langle(\gamma_{s}^{\phi})^{\prime},v_{2}^{\phi}\rangle=0
. So
(\gamma_{s}^{\phi},v_{1}^{\phi},v_{2}^{\phi})
is a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed curve. We also have
\mu^{\phi}(s)=\mu(s)
. Therefore
\alpha^{\phi}
is just the coefficient of
\mu(s)
in the above equation. An easy calculation shows that

\displaystyle(v_{1}^{\phi})^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\left(\epsilon(P^{2}(\theta(s))-R^{2}(\theta(s)))g(\phi)\alpha(s)+f(\phi)(P(\theta(s))m(s)+R(\theta(s))n(s))\right)\mu(s),

\displaystyle(v_{2}^{\phi})^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\left(R(\theta(s))m(s)+P(\theta(s))n(s)\right)\mu(s),

which concludes the proof. Note that if
(\gamma_{s},v_{1},v_{2})
is a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersion, then
(\gamma_{s}^{\phi},v_{1}^{\phi},v_{2}^{\phi})
is also a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersion.
∎

3.2
Pseudo-spherical timelike framed curves in
AdS^{3}

Let
\gamma_{t}:I\to AdS^{3}
be a smooth curve. Then
(\gamma_{t},v_{1},v_{2}):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{5}
is called a
pseudo-spherical timelike framed curve
if
(\gamma_{t}(s),v_{1}(s))^{*}\phi=0
and
(\gamma_{t}(s),v_{2}(s))^{*}\phi=0
for all
s\in I
, where

\Delta_{5}=\{(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})\,|\,\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}\rangle=0\}\subset S^{3}_{2}\times S^{3}_{2}

is a
4
-dimensional contact manifold, and
\phi
is a canonical contact
1
-form on
\Delta_{5}

[
5
]
. The condition
(\gamma_{t}(s),v_{i}(s))^{*}\phi=0
(
i=1,2
) is equivalent to
\langle\gamma_{t}^{\prime}(s),v_{i}(s)\rangle=0
(
i=1,2
) for all
s\in I
. If
(\gamma_{t},v_{1},v_{2})
is an immersion, then it is called a
pseudo-spherical timelike framed immersion
.

We call
\gamma_{t}:I\to AdS^{3}
a
pseudo-spherical timelike framed base curve
if there exists a smooth map
(v_{1},v_{2}):I\to\Delta_{5}
for which
(\gamma_{t},v_{1},v_{2})
is a pseudo-spherical timelike framed curve.

Let
\mu(s)=\gamma_{t}(s)\times v_{1}(s)\times v_{2}(s)
. Then the set
\{\gamma_{t}(s),v_{1}(s),v_{2}(s),\mu(s)\}
is a pseudo-orthonormal frame along
\gamma_{t}
. This frame is well-defined even at singular points of
\gamma_{t}
. The Frenet-Serret type formulas for this frame are

\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{t}^{\prime}(s)\\
v_{1}^{\prime}(s)\\
v_{2}^{\prime}(s)\\
\mu^{\prime}(s)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&\alpha(s)\\
0&0&\ell(s)&m(s)\\
0&-\ell(s)&0&n(s)\\
-\alpha(s)&m(s)&n(s)&0\par\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{t}(s)\\
v_{1}(s)\\
v_{2}(s)\\
\mu(s)\end{pmatrix},

(3.10)

where
\alpha(s)=-\langle\gamma_{t}^{\prime}(s),\mu(s)\rangle
,
\ell(s)=\langle v_{1}^{\prime}(s),v_{2}(s)\rangle
,
m(s)=-\langle v_{1}^{\prime}(s),\mu(s)\rangle
, and
n(s)=-\langle v_{2}^{\prime}(s),\mu(s)\rangle
. We call the mapping
(\alpha,\ell,m,n):I\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
the
curvature
of the pseudo-spherical timelike framed curve
(\gamma_{t},v_{1},v_{2})
. Notice that
s_{0}
is a singular point of
\gamma_{t}
if and only if
\alpha(s_{0})=0
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
(\gamma,v_{1},v_{2})
and
(\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{v}_{2})
be two pseudo-spherical timelike framed curves in
AdS^{3}
. We say that
(\gamma,v_{1},v_{2})
and
(\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{v}_{1},\tilde{v}_{2})
are congruent as pseudo-spherical framed curves if there exists a matrix
A\in SO(2,2)
such that for all
s

\tilde{\gamma}(s)=A(\gamma(s)),\quad\tilde{v}_{1}(s)=A(v_{1}(s)),\quad\tilde{v}_{2}(s)=A(v_{2}(s)).

Theorem 3.7

(Existence and uniqueness of pseudo-spherical timelike framed curves)
.

For a smooth mapping
(\alpha,\ell,m,n):I\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
, there exists a pseudo-spherical timelike framed curve
(\gamma_{t},v_{1},n_{2})
such that
\alpha
,
\ell
,
m
, and
n
are the curvatures of
\gamma_{t}
. Any other pseudo-spherical timelike framed curve
\tilde{\gamma}_{t}
in
AdS^{3}
with the same curvature
(\alpha,\ell,m,n)
is congruent to
\gamma_{t}
.

4
Evolutes and focal surfaces of pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersions in the anti-de Sitter 3-space

We now define evolutes of pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersions in
AdS^{3}
and investigate properties of these evolutes. Throughout this section we assume
\alpha^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\hat{n}^{2}(s)\neq 0
for all
s\in I
unless otherwise stated.

Definition 4.1
.

The
total evolute

\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{s})
of a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersion
(\gamma_{s},f_{1},f_{2})
is defined by

\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{s})(s)=\pm\dfrac{\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s)\,(\hat{n}(s)\gamma_{s}(s)-\alpha(s)f_{2}(s))-(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))f_{1}(s)}{\sqrt{\big|\hat{\epsilon}(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))^{2}-\hat{\ell}^{2}(s)\hat{n}^{2}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))\big|}},

(4.1)

where
g(s):=\hat{\epsilon}(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))^{2}-\hat{\ell}^{2}(s)\hat{n}^{2}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))\neq 0
.
If
g(s)>0
, then
\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{s})(s)\in S^{3}_{2}
. In this case we denote it by
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\gamma_{s})(s)
and call it the
PS-evolute
of
\gamma_{s}
. If
g(s)<0
, then
\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{s})(s)\in AdS^{3}
. In this case we denote it by
\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{s})(s)
and we call it the
AdS-evolute
of
\gamma_{s}
. Note that if
g(s)>0
and
\hat{\epsilon}=-1
, then we must assume that
\hat{n}^{2}(s)<\alpha^{2}(s)
since otherwise
\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{s})
is not well-defined.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(\gamma_{s},f_{1},f_{2}):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{1}
be a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersion with the curvature
(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\ell},\hat{m},\hat{n})
. Then the totally evolute
\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{s})
of
\gamma_{s}
is independent of the parametrization of
(\gamma_{s},f_{1},f_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
(\tilde{\gamma}_{s},\tilde{f}_{1},\tilde{f}_{2})
be a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersion with the curvature
(\hat{\alpha}_{1},\hat{\ell}_{1},\hat{m}_{1},\hat{n}_{1})
. Suppose that
(\gamma_{s},f_{1},f_{2})
and
(\tilde{\gamma}_{s},\tilde{f}_{1},\tilde{f}_{2})
are parametrically equivalent by a change of parameter
u:\tilde{I}\to I
. Similar to Proposition
3.4
, we have

\hat{\alpha}_{1}(s)=u^{\prime}(s)\hat{\alpha}(u(s)),\>\>\hat{\ell}_{1}(s)=u^{\prime}(s)\hat{\ell}(u(s)),\>\>\hat{m}_{1}(s)=u^{\prime}(s)\hat{m}(u(s)),\>\>\hat{n}_{1}(s)=u^{\prime}(s)\hat{n}(u(s)).

(4.2)

Moreover we find that

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=u^{\prime\prime}(s)\hat{\alpha}(u(s))+(u^{\prime}(s))^{2}\hat{\alpha}^{\prime}(u(s)),

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{1}^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=u^{\prime\prime}(s)\hat{n}(u(s))+(u^{\prime}(s))^{2}\hat{n}^{\prime}(u(s)),

which from (
4.2
) yields

\hat{\alpha}_{1}(s)\hat{n}_{1}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}_{1}(s)\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{\prime}(s)=(u^{\prime}(s))^{3}\left(\hat{\alpha}^{\prime}(u(s))\hat{n}(u(s))-\hat{\alpha}(u(s))\hat{n}^{\prime}(u(s))\right).

(4.3)

It follows easily from (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) that

\mathcal{E}(\tilde{\gamma}_{s})(s)=\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{s})(u(s)).

Therefore the totally evolute of a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersion is independent of the parametrization.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

The AdS-evolute
\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{s})
of
\gamma_{s}
is a pseudo-spherical framed base curve in
AdS^{3}
. More precisely,
(\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{s}),\mu,\eta)

(\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}},\hat{\ell}_{\mathcal{E}_{a}},0,\hat{n}_{\mathcal{E}_{a}})
, where

\displaystyle\eta(s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\alpha(s)\gamma_{s}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\hat{n}(s)f_{2}(s)}{\sqrt{|\alpha^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\hat{n}^{2}(s)|}},

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))(\hat{n}(s)\gamma_{s}(s)-\alpha(s)f_{2}(s))-\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s)(\hat{\epsilon}\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\alpha^{2}(s))f_{1}(s)}{\sqrt{|\alpha^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\hat{n}^{2}(s)|}\sqrt{\hat{\ell}^{2}(s)\hat{n}^{2}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))-\hat{\epsilon}(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))^{2}}},

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s)

\displaystyle=\hat{\epsilon}\sqrt{|\alpha^{2}+\hat{\epsilon}n^{2}|}\dfrac{\hat{n}\big(2\hat{\ell}\alpha^{\prime}\hat{n}^{\prime}+\hat{n}(\alpha^{\prime}\hat{\ell}^{\prime}-\hat{\ell}\alpha^{\prime\prime})\big)-\alpha\big(-\hat{\ell}^{3}\hat{n}^{2}+\hat{n}\hat{\ell}^{\prime}\hat{n}^{\prime}+\hat{\ell}(2(\hat{n}^{\prime})^{2}-\hat{n}\hat{n}^{\prime\prime})\big)}{\hat{\ell}^{2}(s)\hat{n}^{2}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))-\hat{\epsilon}(\alpha\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))^{2}},

\displaystyle\hat{\ell}_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{|\alpha^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\hat{n}^{2}(s)|},

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s)

\displaystyle=-\hat{\epsilon}\dfrac{\sqrt{\hat{\ell}^{2}(s)\hat{n}^{2}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))-\hat{\epsilon}(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))^{2}}}{\alpha^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\hat{n}^{2}(s)}.

Proof.

We directly have
\langle\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{s}),\mu\rangle=0
and
\langle\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{s}),\eta\rangle=0
since

is a pseudo-orthonormal frame. By differentiating
\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{s})
we find that

\mathcal{E}_{a}^{\prime}(\gamma_{s})(s)=\Lambda(s)\left((\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))(\hat{n}(s)\gamma_{s}(s)-\alpha(s)f_{2}(s))-\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s)(\hat{\epsilon}\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\alpha^{2}(s))f_{1}(s)\right),

where

\Lambda(s)=\hat{\epsilon}\dfrac{\hat{n}\big(2\hat{\ell}\alpha^{\prime}\hat{n}^{\prime}+\hat{n}(\alpha^{\prime}\hat{\ell}^{\prime}-\hat{\ell}\alpha^{\prime\prime})\big)-\alpha\big(-\hat{\ell}^{3}\hat{n}^{2}+\hat{n}\hat{\ell}^{\prime}\hat{n}^{\prime}+\hat{\ell}(2(\hat{n}^{\prime})^{2}-\hat{n}\hat{n}^{\prime\prime})\big)}{\left(\hat{\ell}^{2}(s)\hat{n}^{2}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))-\hat{\epsilon}(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))^{2}\right)^{3/2}}.

Using this equation, we see that

\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{a}(\gamma_{s}),\eta\rangle=\Lambda(s)\big((\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))(-\hat{n}(s)\alpha(s)+\alpha(s)\hat{n}(s))\big)=0.

Note that

. Or it is easy to show that

defined in this theorem is a unit vector and it is pseudo-orthogonal to
\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{s})
,
\mu
, and
\eta
. Then we immediately get

from the equality
\mathcal{E}_{a}^{\prime}(\gamma_{s})=\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}\mu_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}
. Similarly
\hat{\ell}_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}
and

along

∎

4.1
Focal surfaces of pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersions

The set of singular values of the focal surface of a curve gives the evolute of the same curve. This fact has been proved for many types of curves in different spaces
[
13
,
15
,
16
,
25
]
. Our aim in this section is to show that this important relationship between evolutes and focal surfaces also holds for pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersions. We also give relationships between singularities of the evolute and of the focal surface.

Let
(\gamma_{s},f_{1},f_{2})
be a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersion with the curvature
(\alpha,\hat{l},0,\hat{n})
in
AdS^{3}
. Define

\zeta(s)=\dfrac{\hat{n}(s)\gamma_{s}(s)-\alpha(s)f_{2}(s)}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{\zeta}(-\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))}},

where
\epsilon_{\zeta}=\langle\zeta,\zeta\rangle=\text{sgn}(-\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))
. Note that for
\eta
defined in Theorem
4.3
,
\langle\zeta,\eta\rangle=0
and
\epsilon_{\zeta}\hat{\epsilon}=\epsilon_{\eta}=\langle\eta,\eta\rangle
. So we consider the following cases depending upon
\epsilon_{\zeta}
and
\hat{\epsilon}
.

Case 1.

Let
\epsilon_{\zeta}\hat{\epsilon}=1
. Then
\zeta
and
f_{1}
are both timelike vectors. In this case we define the focal surface of
\gamma_{s}
by

\mathcal{F}_{1}(s,\theta)=\cos\theta\,\zeta(s)+\sin\theta f_{1}(s).

(4.4)

Note that
\mathcal{F}_{1}(s,\theta)\in AdS^{3}
.
Similar to the case with evolutes, it is easy to show that this focal surface of a pseudo-spherical spacelike immersion is independent of the choice of parametrization. Let us calculate the partial derivatives of
\mathcal{F}_{1}(s,\theta)
. Differentiating (
4.4
) with respect to
s
and using (
3.8
), we find that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathcal{F}_{1}(s,\theta)=

\displaystyle\dfrac{-\cos\theta}{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}\bigg(\dfrac{\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s)}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}}(\alpha(s)\gamma(s)-\hat{n}(s)f_{2}(s))

\displaystyle+\alpha(s)\hat{\ell}(s)\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}f_{1}(s)\bigg)+\sin\theta\hat{\ell}(s)f_{2}(s).

(4.5)

Differentiating (
4.4
) with respect to
\theta
yields

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\mathcal{F}_{1}(s,\theta)=-\sin\theta\,\zeta(s)+\cos\theta f_{1}(s).

(4.6)

The focal surface of
\gamma_{s}
defined by (
4.4
) has singularity at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
if and only if
\mathcal{F}_{1}\times\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathcal{F}_{1}\times\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\mathcal{F}_{1}(s_{0},\theta_{0})=0
. Then from (
4.4
), (
4.5
), and (
4.6
), this triple vector product is equal to

-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}}\left(\dfrac{\cos\theta(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}}+\sin\theta\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s)\right)\mu(s),

which becomes zero at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
if and only if

\cos\theta_{0}(\alpha(s_{0})\hat{n}^{\prime}(s_{0})-\hat{n}(s_{0})\alpha^{\prime}(s_{0}))+\sin\theta_{0}\hat{\ell}(s_{0})\hat{n}(s_{0})\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})-\alpha^{2}(s_{0})}=0.

(4.7)

Substituting this into (
4.4
) gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}(s_{0},\theta_{0})

\displaystyle=\pm\dfrac{\hat{\ell}(s_{0})\hat{n}(s_{0})\,(\hat{n}(s_{0})\gamma_{s}(s_{0})-\alpha(s_{0})f_{2}(s_{0}))-(\alpha(s_{0})\hat{n}^{\prime}(s_{0})-\hat{n}(s_{0})\alpha^{\prime}(s_{0}))f_{1}(s_{0})}{\sqrt{(\alpha(s_{0})\hat{n}^{\prime}(s_{0})-\hat{n}(s_{0})\alpha^{\prime}(s_{0}))^{2}+\hat{\ell}^{2}(s_{0})\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})(\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})-\alpha^{2}(s_{0}))}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{s})(s_{0}).

So the set of singular points of
\mathcal{F}_{1}(s,\theta)
coincides with the AdS-evolute
\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{s})(s)
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Suppose that the focal surface
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\gamma_{s})(s,\theta)
has singularity at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
. Then

(i)

\mathcal{F}_{1}(\gamma_{s})(s,\theta)
is locally diffeomorphic to the cuspidal edge at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
if and only if
\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s_{0})\neq 0
, i.e., the AdS-evolute
\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{s})(s)
is regular at
s_{0}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{F}_{1}(\gamma_{s})(s,\theta)
is locally diffeomorphic to the swallowtail at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
if and only if
\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s_{0})=0
and
\alpha^{\prime}_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s_{0})\neq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\gamma_{s})(s,\theta)
has singularity at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
. Then from (
4.7
) we have

\tan\theta_{0}=\dfrac{-\alpha(s_{0})\hat{n}^{\prime}(s_{0})+\hat{n}(s_{0})\alpha^{\prime}(s_{0})}{\hat{\ell}(s_{0})\hat{n}(s_{0})\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})-\alpha^{2}(s_{0})}}.

The proof of this theorem is based on the well-known criteria for the cuspidal edge and the swallowtail (See
[
18
,
19
]
for details). We consider the signed density function

\displaystyle\lambda(s,\theta)

\displaystyle=\det\left(\mathcal{F}_{1},\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathcal{F}_{1},\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\mathcal{F}_{1},\mu\right)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}}\left(\dfrac{\cos\theta(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}}+\sin\theta\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s)\right).

Set
\lambda^{-1}(0)=\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\gamma_{s}))
. We see that
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\gamma_{s}))=\{(s,\theta(s))\}
, where
\theta(s)
is a function satisfying
\lambda(s,\theta(s))=0
. Then we have

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\lambda(s,\theta)=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}}\left(\dfrac{-\sin\theta(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}}+\cos\theta\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s)\right)\neq 0,

since
(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s),\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s))\neq(0,0)
. Therefore any
p\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\gamma_{s}))
is non-degenerate. Let
p
be a non-degenerate singular point. Then there exists a regular curve
c:I\to I\times\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
c(s_{0})=p
and
\text{image}(c)=\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F}_{1}(\gamma_{s}))
near
p
. Let
c(s)=(s,\theta(s))
. Consider the null vector field
\xi:I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
along
c(s)
given by
\xi(s)=(1,-\alpha(s)\hat{\ell}(s)/\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)})
. Then from (
4.7
)

\displaystyle\det(c^{\prime}(s_{0}),\xi(s_{0}))

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{\alpha(s_{0})\hat{\ell}(s_{0})}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})-\alpha^{2}(s_{0})}}+\theta^{\prime}(s_{0})

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{\alpha(s_{0})\hat{\ell}(s_{0})}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})-\alpha^{2}(s_{0})}}+\dfrac{d}{ds}\arctan\left(\dfrac{-\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)+\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s)}{\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s)\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}}\right)\bigg|_{s=s_{0}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s_{0}).

Thus from
[
18
, Theorem 6.1(A)]
,
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\gamma_{s})
is locally diffeomorphic to the cuspidal edge at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
if and only if
\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s_{0})\neq 0
.

From
[
18
, Theorem 6.1(B)]
,
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\gamma_{s})
is locally diffeomorphic to the swallowtail at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
if and only if
\det(c^{\prime}(s_{0}),\xi(s_{0}))=\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s_{0})=0
and
(d/ds)\det(c^{\prime}(s),\xi(s))\big|_{s=s_{0}}=\alpha^{\prime}_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s_{0})\neq 0
.
∎

Case 2.

Let
\epsilon_{\zeta}\hat{\epsilon}=-1
, i.e.,
\epsilon_{\zeta}=-\hat{\epsilon}
. Then one of
\zeta
and
f_{1}
is a timelike vector, and the other one is a spacelike vector. In this case we have four subcases depending on the causal characters of
\zeta
and
f_{1}
and the causal character of the focal surface. We can express these four cases with a single equation:

\mathcal{F}_{2}(s,\theta)=P(\theta)\,\zeta(s)+R(\theta)f_{1}(s),

(4.8)

where
(P(\theta),R(\theta))\in\{(\cosh\theta,\sinh\theta),(\sinh\theta,\cosh\theta)\}
.
It is easy to see that
\mathcal{F}_{2}(s,\theta)\in AdS^{3}
or
\mathcal{F}_{2}(s,\theta)\in S^{3}_{2}
depending upon
\hat{\epsilon}
,
P(\theta)
, and
R(\theta)
. This focal surface is also independent of the choice of parametrization. Taking partial derivatives of (
4.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathcal{F}_{2}(s,\theta)=

\displaystyle\dfrac{P(\theta)}{\hat{\epsilon}(\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))}\bigg(\dfrac{\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s)}{\sqrt{\hat{\epsilon}(\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))}}(\alpha(s)\gamma(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\hat{n}(s)f_{2}(s))

\displaystyle-\alpha(s)\hat{\ell}(s)\sqrt{\hat{\epsilon}(\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))}f_{1}(s)\bigg)+R(\theta)\hat{\ell}(s)f_{2}(s),

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\mathcal{F}_{2}(s,\theta)=

\displaystyle R(\theta)\,\zeta(s)+P(\theta)f_{1}(s).

From these partial derivatives and (
4.8
)

\mathcal{F}_{2}\times\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathcal{F}_{2}\times\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\mathcal{F}_{2}=\dfrac{R^{2}(\theta)-P^{2}(\theta)}{\sqrt{\hat{\epsilon}(\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))}}\left(\dfrac{P(\theta)(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))}{\sqrt{\hat{\epsilon}(\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))}}+R(\theta)\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s)\right)\mu(s).

Therefore, the focal surface
\mathcal{F}_{2}
has singularity at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
if and only if

P(\theta_{0})(\alpha(s_{0})\hat{n}^{\prime}(s_{0})-\hat{n}(s_{0})\alpha^{\prime}(s_{0}))+R(\theta_{0})\hat{\ell}(s_{0})\hat{n}(s_{0})\sqrt{\hat{\epsilon}(\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))}=0.

Introducing this equation into (
4.8
) simply gives the point
\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{s})(s_{0})
on the evolute (
4.1
) of
\gamma_{s}
. We conclude again that the set of singular points of
\mathcal{F}_{2}(s,\theta)
coincides with the evolute of
\gamma_{s}
. Following similar steps in Theorem
4.8
, one can easily prove a similar theorem for
\mathcal{F}_{2}(s,\theta)
.

4.2
PS-height functions

Let
(\gamma_{s},f_{1},f_{2}):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{1}
be a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersion. We now show how to explain the focal surface and the evolute of
\gamma_{s}
as a wavefront from the viewpoint of Legendrian singularity theory. Define two families of functions
F^{T}:I\times AdS^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
by
F^{T}(s,\mathbf{v})=\langle\mu(s),\mathbf{v}\rangle
called
the PS-timelike height function
, and
F^{S}:I\times S^{3}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
by
F^{S}(s,\mathbf{v})=\langle\mu(s),\mathbf{v}\rangle
called
the PS-spacelike height function
on
(\gamma_{s},f_{1},f_{2})
. By a direct calculation, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
(\gamma_{s},f_{1},f_{2}):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{1}
be a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersion with
g(s):=\hat{\epsilon}(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))^{2}-\hat{\ell}^{2}(s)\hat{n}^{2}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s))\neq 0
.

(i)

Suppose that
g(s_{0})<0
for
s_{0}\in I
.

(a)

F^{T}(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=0
if and only if there exist real numbers
a,b,
and
c
such that
\mathbf{v}_{0}=a\gamma_{s}(s_{0})+bf_{1}(s_{0})+cf_{2}(s_{0})
with
-a^{2}+\hat{\epsilon}b^{2}-\hat{\epsilon}c^{2}=-1
.

(b)

F^{T}(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=(\partial F^{T}/\partial s)(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=0
if and only if there exist real numbers
a
and
b
such that
\mathbf{v}_{0}=a\gamma_{s}(s_{0})+bf_{1}(s_{0})+(-a\alpha(s_{0})/\hat{n}(s_{0}))f_{2}(s_{0})
with
\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})(1+\hat{\epsilon}b^{2})=a^{2}(\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s_{0}))
.

(c)

F^{T}(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=(\partial F^{T}/\partial s)(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=(\partial^{2}F^{T}/\partial s^{2})(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=0
if and only if
\mathbf{v}_{0}=\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{s})(s_{0})
.

(ii)

Suppose that
g(s_{0})>0
for
s_{0}\in I
.

(a)

F^{S}(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=0
if and only if there exist real numbers
a,b,
and
c
such that
\mathbf{v}_{0}=a\gamma_{s}(s_{0})+bf_{1}(s_{0})+cf_{2}(s_{0})
with
-a^{2}+\hat{\epsilon}b^{2}-\hat{\epsilon}c^{2}=1
.

(b)

F^{S}(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=(\partial F^{S}/\partial s)(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=0
if and only if there exist real numbers
a
and
b
such that
\mathbf{v}_{0}=a\gamma_{s}(s_{0})+bf_{1}(s_{0})+(-a\alpha(s_{0})/\hat{n}(s_{0}))f_{2}(s_{0})
with
\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})(\hat{\epsilon}b^{2}-1)=a^{2}(\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})+\hat{\epsilon}\alpha^{2}(s_{0}))
. Note that if
\hat{\epsilon}=-1
, then
\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})<\alpha^{2}(s_{0})
must be satisfied.

(c)

F^{S}(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=(\partial F^{S}/\partial s)(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=(\partial^{2}F^{S}/\partial s^{2})(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=0
if and only if
\mathbf{v}_{0}=\mathcal{E}_{p}(\gamma_{s})(s_{0})
.

For both height functions
F^{T}
and
F^{S}
defined above, the discriminant sets
\mathcal{D}_{F^{T}}

and
\mathcal{D}_{F^{S}}
coincide with the images of the focal surfaces in Section
4.1

5
Evolutes and focal surfaces of pseudo-spherical timelike framed immersions in the anti-de Sitter 3-space

In this section, we introduce evolutes of pseudo-spherical timelike framed immersions in
AdS^{3}
and investigate the properties of these evolutes. Throughout this section we assume
\alpha^{2}(s)\neq\hat{n}^{2}(s)
for all
s\in I
unless otherwise stated.

Definition 5.1
.

The
total evolute

\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{t})
of a pseudo-spherical timelike framed immersion
(\gamma_{t},f_{1},f_{2})
is defined by

\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{t})(s)=\pm\dfrac{\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s)\,(\hat{n}(s)\gamma_{t}(s)-\alpha(s)f_{2}(s))-(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))f_{1}(s)}{\sqrt{\big|(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))^{2}-\hat{\ell}^{2}(s)\hat{n}^{2}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s))\big|}},

(5.1)

where
f(s):=(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))^{2}-\hat{\ell}^{2}(s)\hat{n}^{2}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s))\neq 0
.
Notice that if
f(s)>0
, then
\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{t})(s)\in S^{3}_{2}
. This evolute is denoted by
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\gamma_{t})(s)
and called the
PS-evolute
of
\gamma_{t}
. If
f(s)<0
, then
\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{t})(s)\in AdS^{3}
. In this case, we denote this evolute by
\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t})(s)
and we call it the
AdS-evolute
of
\gamma_{t}
. Note that if
f(s)<0
, then we must assume that
\hat{n}^{2}(s)>\alpha^{2}(s)
since otherwise
\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{t})
is not well-defined.

We will explain how Equation (
5.1
) can be derived shortly by using focal surfaces or certain height functions. For now, we investigate some geometric properties of these evolutes. The following proposition follows similarly to Proposition
4.2
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(\gamma_{t},f_{1},f_{2}):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{5}
be a pseudo-spherical timelike framed immersion with the curvature
(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\ell},\hat{m},\hat{n})
. Then the total evolute
\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{t})
of
\gamma_{t}
is independent of the parametrization of
(\gamma_{t},f_{1},f_{2})
.

Theorem 5.3
.

The AdS-evolute
\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t})
of
\gamma_{t}
is a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed base curve in
AdS^{3}
. More precisely,
(\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t}),\mu,\eta)
is a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersion with the curvature
(\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}},\hat{\ell}_{\mathcal{E}_{a}},0,\hat{n}_{\mathcal{E}_{a}})
, where

\displaystyle\eta(s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\alpha(s)\gamma_{t}(s)-\hat{n}(s)f_{2}(s)}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}},

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))(\hat{n}(s)\gamma_{t}(s)-\alpha(s)f_{2}(s))-\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s))f_{1}(s)}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}\sqrt{\hat{\ell}^{2}(s)\hat{n}^{2}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s))-(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))^{2}}},

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}\dfrac{\hat{n}\big(2\hat{\ell}\alpha^{\prime}\hat{n}^{\prime}-\hat{n}(-\alpha^{\prime}\hat{\ell}^{\prime}+\hat{\ell}\alpha^{\prime\prime})\big)-\alpha\big(\hat{\ell}^{3}\hat{n}^{2}+\hat{n}\hat{\ell}^{\prime}\hat{n}^{\prime}+\hat{\ell}(2(\hat{n}^{\prime})^{2}-\hat{n}\hat{n}^{\prime\prime})\big)}{\hat{\ell}^{2}(s)\hat{n}^{2}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s))-(\alpha\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))^{2}},

\displaystyle\hat{\ell}_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)},

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sqrt{\hat{\ell}^{2}(s)\hat{n}^{2}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s))-(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))^{2}}}{\alpha^{2}(s)-\hat{n}^{2}(s)}.

Proof.

We show that
(\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t}),\mu,\eta)
satisfies the conditions for being a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed immersion. It is easy to see that
\langle\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t}),\mu\rangle=0
and
\langle\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t}),\eta\rangle=0
since
\{\gamma_{t},f_{1},f_{2},\mu\}
is a pseudo-orthonormal frame. By a straightforward calculation we get

\mathcal{E}_{a}^{\prime}(\gamma_{t})(s)=\Omega(s)\left((\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))(\hat{n}(s)\gamma(s)-\alpha(s)f_{2}(s))-\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s))f_{1}(s)\right),

where

\Omega(s)=\dfrac{\hat{n}\big(2\hat{\ell}\alpha^{\prime}\hat{n}^{\prime}-\hat{n}(-\alpha^{\prime}\hat{\ell}^{\prime}+\hat{\ell}\alpha^{\prime\prime})\big)-\alpha\big(\hat{\ell}^{3}\hat{n}^{2}+\hat{n}\hat{\ell}^{\prime}\hat{n}^{\prime}+\hat{\ell}(2(\hat{n}^{\prime})^{2}-\hat{n}\hat{n}^{\prime\prime})\big)}{\left(\hat{\ell}^{2}(s)\hat{n}^{2}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s))-(\alpha\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))^{2}\right)^{3/2}}.

Hence we have
\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{a}(\gamma_{t}),\mu\rangle=0
and

\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{a}(\gamma_{t}),\eta\rangle=\Omega(s)\big((\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))(-\hat{n}(s)\alpha(s)+\alpha(s)\hat{n}(s))\big)=0.

Therefore, since
\mu\in AdS^{3}
and
\eta\in S^{3}_{2}
,
(\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t}),\mu,\eta):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{1}
is a pseudo-spherical spacelike framed curve. Then it is easy to calculate
\mu_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}=\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t})\times\mu\times\eta
which directly gives
\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}
from the equality
\mathcal{E}_{a}^{\prime}(\gamma_{t})=\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}\mu_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}
. Similarly
\hat{\ell}_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}
and
\hat{n}_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}
can be directly calculated by using derivative formulas of the pseudo-orthonormal frame
\{\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t}),\mu,\eta,\mu_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}\}
along
\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t})
.
∎

5.1
Focal surfaces of pseudo-spherical timelike framed immersions

In this section we obtain evolutes as the set of singular values of focal surfaces of pseudo-spherical timelike framed immersions. We also give relationships between singularities of the evolute and of the focal surface.

Let
(\gamma_{t},f_{1},f_{2})
be a pseudo-spherical timelike framed immersion with the curvature
(\alpha,\hat{l},0,\hat{n})
in
AdS^{3}
. Define

\zeta(s)=\dfrac{\hat{n}(s)\gamma_{t}(s)-\alpha(s)f_{2}(s)}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{\zeta}(\alpha^{2}(s)-\hat{n}^{2}(s))}},

where
\epsilon_{\zeta}=\langle\zeta,\zeta\rangle=\text{sgn}(\alpha^{2}-\hat{n}^{2})
. We will consider two cases depending upon
\epsilon_{\zeta}
.

Case 1.

Let
\epsilon_{\zeta}=-1
, i.e.,
\zeta
is a timelike vector. We define the focal surface
\mathcal{F}_{3}(s,\theta):I\times\mathbb{R}\to AdS^{3}
of
\gamma_{t}
as

\mathcal{F}_{3}(s,\theta)=\cosh\theta\,\zeta(s)+\sinh\theta f_{1}(s).

(5.2)

Similar to the case with evolutes, it is easy to show that this focal surface of a pseudo-spherical timelike immersion is independent of the choice of parametrization. Now we find the partial derivatives of
\mathcal{F}_{3}(s,\theta)
. Differentiating (
5.2
) with respect to
s
and using (
3.13
) yields

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathcal{F}_{3}(s,\theta)=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\cosh\theta}{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}\bigg(\dfrac{-(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}}(\alpha(s)\gamma(s)-\hat{n}(s)f_{2}(s))

\displaystyle+\alpha(s)\hat{\ell}(s)\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}f_{1}(s)\bigg)+\sinh\theta\,\hat{\ell}(s)f_{2}(s).

(5.3)

The derivative of (
5.2
) with respect to
\theta
is easily obtained as

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\mathcal{F}_{3}(s,\theta)=\sinh\theta\zeta(s)+\cosh\theta f_{1}(s).

(5.4)

The focal surface
\mathcal{F}_{3}
of
\gamma_{t}
has singularity at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
if and only if
\mathcal{F}_{3}\times\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathcal{F}_{3}\times\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\mathcal{F}_{3}(s_{0},\theta_{0})=0
. Then from (
5.2
), (
5.3
), and (
5.4
)

-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}}\left(\dfrac{\cosh\theta(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)}}+\sinh\theta\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s)\right)\mu(s),

which vanishes at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
if and only if

\cosh\theta_{0}(\alpha(s_{0})\hat{n}^{\prime}(s_{0})-\hat{n}(s_{0})\alpha^{\prime}(s_{0}))+\sinh\theta_{0}\hat{\ell}(s_{0})\hat{n}(s_{0})\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})-\alpha^{2}(s_{0})}=0.

(5.5)

Introducing this into (
5.2
), we find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3}(s_{0},\theta_{0})

\displaystyle=\pm\dfrac{\hat{\ell}(s_{0})\hat{n}(s_{0})\,(\hat{n}(s_{0})\gamma_{t}(s_{0})-\alpha(s_{0})f_{2}(s_{0}))-(\alpha(s_{0})\hat{n}^{\prime}(s_{0})-\hat{n}(s_{0})\alpha^{\prime}(s_{0}))f_{1}(s_{0})}{\sqrt{\hat{\ell}^{2}(s_{0})\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})(\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})-\alpha^{2}(s_{0}))-(\alpha(s_{0})\hat{n}^{\prime}(s_{0})-\hat{n}(s_{0})\alpha^{\prime}(s_{0}))^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t})(s_{0}).

Therefore, the set of singular points of
\mathcal{F}_{3}(s,\theta)
coincides with the AdS-evolute
\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t})(s)
.

Theorem 5.8
.

Suppose that the focal surface
\mathcal{F}_{3}(s,\theta)
has singularity at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
. Then

(i)

\mathcal{F}_{3}(s,\theta)
is locally diffeomorphic to the cuspidal edge at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
if and only if
\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s_{0})\neq 0
, i.e., the AdS-evolute
\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t})(s)
is regular at
s_{0}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{F}_{3}(s,\theta)
is locally diffeomorphic to the swallowtail at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
if and only if
\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s_{0})=0
and
\alpha^{\prime}_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s_{0})\neq 0
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{F}_{3}(s,\theta)
has singularity at
(s_{0},\theta_{0})
, then from (
5.5
) we have

\tanh\theta_{0}=\dfrac{-\alpha(s_{0})\hat{n}^{\prime}(s_{0})+\hat{n}(s_{0})\alpha^{\prime}(s_{0})}{\hat{\ell}(s_{0})\hat{n}(s_{0})\sqrt{-\alpha^{2}(s_{0})+\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})}}.

We shall prove this theorem by using the well-known criteria for the cuspidal edge and the swallowtail (See
[
18
,
19
]
for details). Now consider the signed density function

\lambda(s,\theta)=\det\left(\mathcal{F}_{3},\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathcal{F}_{3},\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\mathcal{F}_{3},\mu\right)=\dfrac{\cosh\theta(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))}{\sqrt{-(\alpha^{2}(s)-\hat{n}^{2}(s))}}+\sinh\theta\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s).

Set
\lambda^{-1}(0)=\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F}_{3}(\gamma_{t}))
. We see that
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F}_{3}(\gamma_{t}))=\{(s,\theta(s))\}
, where
\theta(s)
is a function satisfying
\lambda(s,\theta(s))=0
. Then we have

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\lambda(s,\theta)=\sinh\theta\dfrac{(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))}{\sqrt{-(\alpha^{2}(s)-\hat{n}^{2}(s))}}+\cosh\theta\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s)\neq 0,

since
(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s),\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s))\neq(0,0)
. Therefore any
p\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F}_{3}(\gamma_{t}))
is non-degenerate. Let
p
be a non-degenerate singular point. Then there exists a regular curve
c:I\to I\times\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
c(s_{0})=p
and
\text{image}(c)=\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{F}_{3}(\gamma_{t}))
near
p
. Let
c(s)=(s,\theta(s))
. Consider the null vector field
\xi:I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
along
c(s)
given by
\xi(s)=(1,-\alpha(s)\hat{n}(s)/\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s)})
. Then from (
5.5
) and Remark
5.5

\displaystyle\det(c^{\prime}(s_{0}),\xi(s_{0}))

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{\alpha(s_{0})\hat{\ell}(s_{0})}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})-\alpha^{2}(s_{0})}}-\theta^{\prime}(s_{0})

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{\alpha(s_{0})\hat{\ell}(s_{0})}{\sqrt{\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})-\alpha^{2}(s_{0})}}-\dfrac{d}{ds}\arctanh\left(\dfrac{-\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)+\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s)}{\hat{\ell}(s)\hat{n}(s)\sqrt{-\alpha^{2}(s)+\hat{n}^{2}(s)}}\right)\bigg|_{s=s_{0}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s_{0}).

Thus from
[

,
\mathcal{F}_{3}(\gamma_{t})
is locally diffeomorphic to the cuspidal edge at

if and only if
\alpha_{\mathcal{E}_{a}}(s_{0})\neq 0
.

, Theorem 6.1(B)]

\mathcal{F}_{3}(\gamma_{t})
is locally diffeomorphic to the swallowtail at

if and only if

and

To avoid repeating the same process, we leave analyzing this focal surface as an exercise to the reader. It is not surprising that the set of singular values of
\mathcal{F}_{5}
coincides with the evolute
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\gamma_{t})
, and a theorem similar to Theorem
5.8
is also satisfied for
\mathcal{F}_{5}
.

5.2
Anti-de Sitter height functions

Let
(\gamma_{t},f_{1},f_{2}):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{5}
be a pseudo-spherical timelike framed immersion. In this section we see that it is possible to explain the evolute of
\gamma_{t}
as a wavefront from the viewpoint of Legendrian singularity theory as follows. We define two families of functions
H^{T}:I\times AdS^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
by
H^{T}(s,\mathbf{v})=\langle\mu(s),\mathbf{v}\rangle
called the
AdS-timelike height function
, and
H^{S}:I\times S^{3}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
by
H^{S}(s,\mathbf{v})=\langle\mu(s),\mathbf{v}\rangle
called the
AdS-spacelike height function
on
(\gamma_{t},f_{1},f_{2})
. The following proposition follows from a straightforward calculation.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
(\gamma_{t},f_{1},f_{2}):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{5}
be a pseudo-spherical timelike framed immersion with
f(s)=(\alpha(s)\hat{n}^{\prime}(s)-\hat{n}(s)\alpha^{\prime}(s))^{2}-\hat{\ell}^{2}(s)\hat{n}^{2}(s)(\hat{n}^{2}(s)-\alpha^{2}(s))\neq 0
.

(i)

Suppose that
f(s)<0
and
\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})>\alpha^{2}(s_{0})
.

(a)

H^{T}(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=0
if and only if there exist real numbers
a,b,
and
c
such that
\mathbf{v}_{0}=a\gamma_{t}(s_{0})+bf_{1}(s_{0})+cf_{2}(s_{0})
with
-a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=-1
.

(b)

H^{T}(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=(\partial H^{T}/\partial s)(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=0
if and only if there exist real numbers
a
and
b
such that
\mathbf{v}_{0}=a\gamma_{t}(s_{0})+bf_{1}(s_{0})+(-a\alpha(s_{0})/\hat{n}(s_{0}))f_{2}(s_{0})
with
\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})(1+b^{2})=a^{2}(\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})-\alpha^{2}(s_{0}))
.

(c)

H^{T}(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=(\partial H^{T}/\partial s)(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=(\partial^{2}H^{T}/\partial s^{2})(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=0
if and only if
\mathbf{v}_{0}=\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t})(s_{0})
.

(ii)

Suppose that
f(s)>0
.

(a)

H^{S}(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=0
if and only if there exist real numbers
a,b,
and
c
such that
\mathbf{v}_{0}=a\gamma_{t}(s_{0})+bf_{1}(s_{0})+cf_{2}(s_{0})
with
-a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=1
.

(b)

H^{S}(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=(\partial H^{S}/\partial s)(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=0
if and only if there exist real numbers
a
and
b
such that
\mathbf{v}_{0}=a\gamma_{t}(s_{0})+bf_{1}(s_{0})+(-a\alpha(s_{0})/\hat{n}(s_{0}))f_{2}(s_{0})
with
\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})(b^{2}-1)=a^{2}(\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})-\alpha^{2}(s_{0}))
, where
b^{2}-1
and
\hat{n}^{2}(s_{0})-\alpha^{2}(s_{0})
have the same sign.

(c)

H^{S}(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=(\partial H^{S}/\partial s)(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=(\partial^{2}H^{S}/\partial s^{2})(s_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})=0
if and only if
\mathbf{v}_{0}=\mathcal{E}_{p}(\gamma_{t})(s_{0})
.

Notice that for both height functions
H^{T}
and
H^{S}
defined above, the discriminant sets
\mathcal{D}_{H^{T}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{H^{S}}
coincide with the images of the focal surfaces defined in the previous section. Moreover the secondary discriminant sets
\mathcal{D}^{2}_{H^{T}}
and
\mathcal{D}^{2}_{H^{S}}
coincide with the images of the evolutes
\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t})
and
\mathcal{E}_{p}(\gamma_{t})
, respectively.

Example 5.11
.

Consider the smooth curve
\gamma_{t}:I\to AdS^{3}
defined by

\gamma_{t}(s)=\left(\sqrt{2}\cosh(\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}}),\cosh(\sqrt{2}s)+\sqrt{2}\sinh{(\sqrt{2}s)},\sqrt{2}\sinh{(\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}})},\sqrt{2}\cosh{(\sqrt{2}s)}+\sinh(\sqrt{2}s)\right).

The derivative of this curve with respect to
s
is

\gamma_{t}^{\prime}(s)=\left(\sinh{(\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}})},2\cosh(\sqrt{2}s)+\sqrt{2}\sinh{(\sqrt{2}s)},\cosh{(\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}})},\sqrt{2}\cosh{(\sqrt{2}s)}+2\sinh(\sqrt{2}s)\right).

We see that
\langle\gamma_{t}^{\prime},\gamma_{t}^{\prime}\rangle=-1
, that is, the curve
\gamma_{t}
is a regular timelike curve in
AdS^{3}
. We define
v_{1}:I\to S^{3}_{2}
and
v_{2}:I\to S^{3}_{2}
by

\displaystyle v_{1}(s)

\displaystyle=\left(\cosh(\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}}),\sqrt{2}\cosh(\sqrt{2}s)+2\sinh{(\sqrt{2}s)},\sinh(\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}}),\sqrt{2}\sinh(\sqrt{2}s)+2\cosh{(\sqrt{2}s)}\right),

\displaystyle v_{2}(s)

\displaystyle=\bigg(-\sqrt{2}\sinh(\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}}),-(\sqrt{2}\cosh(\sqrt{2}s)+\sinh{(\sqrt{2}s)}),-\sqrt{2}\cosh(\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}}),

\displaystyle\qquad-(\sqrt{2}\sinh(\sqrt{2}s)+\cosh{(\sqrt{2}s)})\bigg).

So
\langle v_{1},\gamma_{s}\rangle=0
,
\langle v_{2},\gamma_{s}\rangle=0
,
\langle v_{1},\gamma_{s}^{\prime}\rangle=0
, and
\langle v_{2},\gamma^{\prime}\rangle=0
. Thus
(\gamma_{t},v_{1},v_{2}):I\to AdS^{3}\times\Delta_{5}
is a pseudo-spherical timelike framed curve in
AdS^{3}
. We then find that

\mu(s)=\left(\sinh{(\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}})},2\cosh(\sqrt{2}s)+\sqrt{2}\sinh{(\sqrt{2}s)},\cosh{(\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}})},\sqrt{2}\cosh{(\sqrt{2}s)}+2\sinh(\sqrt{2}s)\right).

The curvature of
\gamma_{t}
is given by
(\alpha,\ell,m,n)
, where

\alpha(s)=1,\quad\ell(s)=1,\quad m(s)=3/\sqrt{2},\quad n(s)=0.

Thus
(\gamma_{t},v_{1},v_{2})
is a pseudo-spherical timelike framed immersion.
Using (
3.12
), we easily obtain

f_{1}(s)=-v_{2}(s),\quad f_{2}(s)=v_{1}(s),\quad\hat{\ell}(s)=\ell(s),\quad\hat{n}(s)=m(s).

Therefore from (
5.1
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t})(s)=

\displaystyle\bigg(\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{7}}\cosh(\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}}),\dfrac{\cosh(\sqrt{2}s)+\sqrt{2}\sinh(\sqrt{2}s)}{\sqrt{7}},\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{7}}\sinh(\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}}),

\displaystyle\quad\dfrac{\sinh(\sqrt{2}s)+\sqrt{2}\cosh(\sqrt{2}s)}{\sqrt{7}}\bigg).

By using the hyperbolic Hopf map (
2.1
), we are able to visualize the projections of
\gamma_{t}
and
\mathcal{E}_{a}(\gamma_{t})

H^{2}(1/2)

Log motivic exceptional direct image functors
Doosung Park

Abstract
. In this paper, we construct the motivic exceptional direct image functors for fs log schemes. This construction is a part of the motivic six-functor formalism for fs log schemes.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 14F42; Secondary 14A21

1.
Introduction

The Grothendieck six-functor formalism of schemes consists of the functors
f^{*}
,
f_{*}
,
f_{!}
,
f^{!}
,
\otimes
, and
Hom
(internal Hom) together with the relations among them.
Grothendieck and his collaborators
[
1
]
show that the derived categories of
\ell
-adic sheaves satisfy this formalism.
Ayoub
[
2
]
establishes this formalism in the motivic setting,
and he defines
f_{!}
for quasi-projective morphisms.

In both the
\ell
-adic and motivic settings,
one can construct
f^{*}
,
f_{*}
,
\otimes
, and
Hom
by formal categorical methods.
On the other hand,
defining
f_{!}
requires an extra effort.
A typical restriction on
f
is that
f
is a compactifiable morphism,
which means that
f
admits a factorization
X\xrightarrow{j}Y\xrightarrow{g}S
such that
j
is an open immersion and
g
is proper.
Then one can define
f_{!}:=p_{*}j_{!}
,
and one needs to show that this
f_{!}
is independent of the compactifications.
After that,
f^{!}
is defined to be a right adjoint of
f_{!}
.
This is Deligne’s argument in
[
1
]
.
Cisinski and Déglise
[
6
]
use this argument to construct
f_{!}
in the motivic setting for compactifiable morphisms.

This paper is the continuation of the series developed in
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
, and
[
20
]
, which explores
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory of fs log schemes.
The purpose of this paper is to construct the exceptional direct image functor
f_{!}
in the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant log motivic setting.
We plan to study further properties of
f_{!}
including the Poincaré duality in
[
21
]
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be a morphism of fs log schemes such that its underlying morphism of schemes
\underline{f}\colon\underline{X}\to\underline{S}
is compactifiable.
Then the morphism
f
admits the induced factorization

X\xrightarrow{q}\underline{X}\times_{\underline{S}}S\xrightarrow{p}S,

where
p
is the projection.
One can define
f_{!}:=q_{*}p_{!}
,
where
p_{!}
is further defined using a compactification of
\underline{f}\colon\underline{X}\to\underline{S}
.
This is Nakayama’s argument in
[
15
, §5.4]
.
Since
q
is canonical,
we only need to show that
p_{!}
is independent of the compactifications to achieve the main goal.

1.1.
Log motivic
\infty
-categories

To provide an axiomatic argument,
we introduce the notion of a

\infty
-category
,
whose terminology is imitating a motivic triangulated category due to Cisinski-Déglise
[
6
, Definition 2.4.45]
.
A log motivic
\infty
-category

is a dividing Nisnevich sheaf of symmetric monoidal presentable
\infty
-categories

\mathscr{T}\in\mathcal{S}\mathrm{hv}_{dNis}(\mathbf{lSch}/B,\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}))

is a finite dimensional noetherian base scheme throughout the paper.
See Definition

The fundamental
\infty

\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory of fs log schemes is

in
[
18
, Definition 2.5.5]

which is an extension of the original

due to Morel-Voevodsky in
[

]
from schemes to fs log schemes.

The theory of log transfers over a perfect field is introduced in

Hence we do not know how to extend the

Instead, we consider the functor

for a commutative algebra object

is the symmetric monoidal

.
Let

If

let
\mathbb{E}
be a commutative algebra object of

.
Then

\infty

Nakayama and Ogus

2.5.

Throughout this subsection,

Definition 2.5.1

Proposition 2.5.2
.

We need to show

.
By Zariski descent, the question is Zariski local on

.
Use
[
8
, Proposition IV.18.1.1]
to assume that there exists

such that

by
[

, Proposition IV.18.1.1]
.
The cofiber sequence

j_{\sharp}j^{*}M_{S}(X)\xrightarrow{ad^{\prime}}M_{S}(X)\xrightarrow{ad}i_{*}i^{*}M_{S}(X)

obtained from (Loc) finishes the proof since

.
∎

For a sharp fs monoid

,
we set
\mathrm{pt}_{P}:=\mathbb{A}_{P}\times_{\underline{\mathbb{A}_{P}}}\{0\}
,
where

is the origin of
\underline{\mathbb{A}_{P}}
.

Proposition 2.5.3
.

Let

be a saturated log smooth morphism in

.
Then

.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on

where

runs over the points of

.
If

is strict, so the claim is trivial.
Hence assume

.

By Zariski descent, the question is Zariski local on

and

.
Use

to assume that

admits a neat chart

at

is neat at

,

is neat at

, and the induced morphism

and

Suppose that

is a strict closed immersion and

is its open complement.
By (Loc),
we have the cofiber sequence

Due to Proposition

,
we reduce to showing

and

.
After suitable stratification,

.

In this case, let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\times_{\mathbb{A}_{Q}}\mathrm{pt}_{Q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times_{\mathbb{A}_{P}}\mathrm{pt}_{Q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

Let

The localization property yields a cofiber sequence

We fix a log motivic
\infty
-category
\mathscr{T}
throughout this section.

Definition 3.0.1

The organization of the proof is as follows.
In §

In §

In §

We also show that the vertically universal support property implies the projection formula.
In §

we show that some (not all) vertical morphisms satisfy the strictly or vertically universal support property.
The crucial input for this is

which shows that a certain compactification of the multiplication morphism

(not
\mathscr{T}^{\mathrm{ex}}
).
In §
3.5

we show that some (not all) morphisms to the standard log point

satisfy the strictly universal support property.
To conclude the proof of the support property in §

we need the last two axioms of log motivic
\infty
-categories.
The verifications of these axioms for

is due to
[

Hence
[

]

3.2.

Definition 3.2.1
.

if for every strict morphism

,

satisfies the support property.

Observe that

satisfies the strictly universal support property if and only if the induced morphism
X\to\underline{X}\times_{\underline{S}}S
satisfies the strictly universal support property.

Proposition 3.2.2
.

be a proper morphism in
\mathbf{lSch}/B

The first morphism satisfies the support property since the pullback
\underline{X}\times_{\underline{S}}S^{\prime}\to\underline{X}\times_{\underline{S}}S
is strict, and the second morphism satisfies the support property since it is strict proper.
Proposition
3.1.2
shows that
f^{\prime}
satisfies the support property.

Conversely, assume that for every strict morphism
S^{\prime}\to S
, the projection
f^{\prime}\colon X\times_{S}S^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}
satisfies the support property.
We set

for simplicity of notation.
If
U\to Y
is a strict morphism, then the projection

admits the induced factorization

Since

f
and

is strict, our assumption implies that
q
satisfies the support property.
The diagonal morphism

is a strict closed immersion, so its pullback

is also a strict closed immersion.
It follows that

satisfies the support property.
Proposition
3.1.2
shows that
p^{\prime}
satisfies the support property.
∎

Theorem 3.7.5
.

There exists a functor

\displaystyle(\mathscr{T}^{\mathrm{ex}})_{!}^{*}\colon\mathrm{Corr}(\mathbf{lSch}/B,\mathrm{compactifiable},\mathrm{exact})\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}

sending a morphism
(Y\xleftarrow{g}X\xrightarrow{f}S)
in the correspondence category to

g_{!}f^{*}\colon\mathscr{T}^{\mathrm{ex}}(S)\to\mathscr{T}^{\mathrm{ex}}(Y).

Furthermore,
g_{!}\simeq g_{\sharp}
if
g
is an open immersion,
and
g_{!}\simeq g_{*}
if
g
is proper.

Proof.

Only the last claim requires attention by Construction
3.7.4
.
If
g\colon X\to Y
is a proper morphism in
\mathbf{lSch}/B
,
then
g
admits the induced factorization

X\xrightarrow{i}\underline{X}\times_{\underline{S}}S\xrightarrow{p}S.

Since
\underline{i}
is an isomorphism and
p
is strict proper,
we have
p_{!}\simeq p_{*}
and
i_{!}\simeq i_{*}
by Construction
3.7.4
.
Hence we have
g_{!}\simeq p_{!}i_{!}\simeq p_{*}i_{*}\simeq g_{*}
.
∎

A Unified Approach to Compound Poisson Process and its Time fractional Versions
Palaniappan Vellaisamy and Tomoyuki Ichiba

Abstract
. We consider a weighted sum of a series of independent Poisson random variables and show that it results in a new compound Poisson distribution which includes the Poisson distribution and Poisson distribution of order k k and Poisson distribution of order infinity. An explicit representation for its distribution is obtained in terms of Bell polynomials. We then extend it to a compound Poisson process and time fractional compound Poisson process (TFCPP). It is shown that the one-dimensional distributions of the TFCPP exhibit over-dispersion property, are not infinitely divisible and possess the long-range dependence property. Also, their moments and factorial moments are derived. Finally, the fractional differential equation associated with the TFCPP is also obtained. Some possible applications to insurance are pointed out.

In this paper, we look at the sum of a series of independent weighted Poisson random variables (rvs), which leads to a new compound Poisson distribution (CPD). By suitably choosing the associated sequence of parameters, we show that the CPD includes the Poisson distribution, the Poisson distribution of order
k
and infinity, and so its study leads to a unified approach. Replacing Poisson rvs by Poisson processes, we extend it to a new compound Poisson process (CPP). Later, as a natural extension, we consider its time fractional version and call it TFCPP.

In Section 2, we introduce the notations and required preliminary results
and the CPD is introduced and studied in Section 3. In Section 4, the associated CPP
is studied and its time fractional version TFCPP is investigated in Section 5.
In particular, the one-dimensional distributions of the TFCPP have the over-dispersion
property, are not infinitely divisible, and possess the long-range dependence property.
The moments and the factorial moments of the TFCPP are derived. Finally, its factional differential
equation satisfied by the TFCPP is also derived.

3.
A Compound Poisson Distribution

Let
\{N_{j}\}
be a sequence of independent Poisson variables, where
\{N_{j}\}\sim\text{Poi}(\lambda_{j}),j\geq 1.
Henceforth, we define
N_{j}\equiv 0\penalty\ a.s

, that is,
a Poisson distribution with mean zero is defined to be the degenerate distribution at zero.
Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}

associated parameters such that
\delta=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}<\infty

and
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}t^{j}\lambda_{j}<\infty,
for

We call the distribution of

a generalized Poisson distribution (GPD). Note that

11

where a two-parameter generalization of the Poisson distribution is obtained as a limiting form of the generalized negative binomial distribution.

Remarks 3.1
.

(i) Let now
\lambda_{j}=0
for
j\geq 2.
Then, obviously,
G_{P}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}){=}N_{1}\sim\text{Poi}(\lambda_{1})
,
the Poisson distribution with mean
\lambda_{1}.

(ii) Let
\lambda_{j}=0
, for

Then, clearly,
G_{P}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}){=}N_{1}+2N_{2}+\ldots+kN_{k}=W_{k}\sim\text{Poi}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
, the Poisson distribution of order
k.

The study of the GPDs in (
3.1
) provides a unified approach and has not been addressed in the literature. In view of the form of (
3.1

First, we show that the series in the right-hand side of (
3.1
) indeed follows a compound Poisson distribution. One could also apply Kolmogorov’s three series theorem to check the convergence.

Let now
Y
be a positive integer-valued random variable with the distribution

(3.2)

\mathbb{P}\{Y=j\}=\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\delta},\penalty\ j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

where
\delta=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}.

Observe that given a sequence

N\sim\text{Poi}\penalty\ (\delta)
and

can be determined.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

be the GPD defined in (

\{Y_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of i.i.d. positive-integer valued rvs with distribution defined in
(
3.2

be a Poisson rv with mean
\delta

\{Y_{j}\}
. Then

(3.3)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}jN_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{L}}}{{=}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}Y_{j}.

Proof.

First note that the probability generating function (PGF) of

\displaystyle=

and so it has the LRD property.

Lemma 4.3

(4.13)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{H(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})}{t}{=}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j\lambda_{j}\penalty\ \penalty\ a.s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\delta\mathbb{E}(Y_{1})\penalty\ \penalty\ a.s.

∎

\frac{H(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})}{t}\penalty\ {\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{L}}}{{\to}}}\penalty\ \delta\mathbb{E}(Y_{1}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{as}\penalty\ t\to\infty.

the Mittag-Leffler distribution with density (see (

))

f_{U_{1}}(u)=\lambda u^{\beta-1}M_{\beta,\beta}(-\lambda u^{\beta}),\penalty\ u\geq 0.

The sequence of the epochs, denoted by

, is given by the sums of the inter-arrival
times

(5.2)

V_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{j},\penalty\ n\geq 1.

The epochs represent the times in which events arrive or occur. The PDF of the
n

the
n
-fold convolution of
U_{j}

2.8
))

f_{V_{n}}(v)=f^{*n}_{U}(v)=\beta\lambda^{n}\frac{v^{n\beta-1}}{(n-1)!}M^{(n)}_{\beta}(-\lambda v^{\beta}).

The counting process
N_{\beta}(t,\lambda)
that counts the number of epochs (events) up to time
t
, assuming that

U_{0}=0
, is called the TFPP. Note
N_{\beta}(t,\lambda)
is given by

(5.4)

N_{\beta}(t,\lambda)=\max\{n:\;V_{n}\leq t\}.

The PMF

of the TFPP is given by (see Laskin
[
22
]
or Meerschaert et al.
[
25
]
)

\displaystyle p_{{}_{\beta}}(n|t,\lambda)=

\displaystyle{(\lambda t^{\beta})^{n}}M_{\beta,n\beta+1}^{n+1}(-\lambda t^{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\lambda t^{\beta})^{n}}{n!}M_{\beta}^{(n)}(-\lambda t^{\beta}),\penalty\

using (
2.4
). Also,
p_{{}_{\beta}}(n|0,\lambda)=1\text{ if }n=0\text{ and is zero if }n\geq 1.

Also, the PMF

satisfies (see Begin and Orsingher
[
5
]
)

(5.6)

\partial_{t}^{\beta}p_{{}_{\beta}}(n|t,\lambda)=\begin{cases}-\lambda p_{{}_{\beta}}(0|t,\lambda),\quad\text{if}\penalty\ \penalty\ n=0,\\
-\lambda\Big[p_{{}_{\beta}}(n|t,\lambda)-p_{{}_{\beta}}(n-1|t,\lambda)\Big],\quad\text{if}\penalty\ \penalty\ n\geq 1,\end{cases}

where
\partial_{t}^{\beta}
denotes the Caputo fractional derivative defined in (

).

The mean and the variance of the TFPP are given by (see
[
21
]
)

(5.7)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big(N_{\beta}(t,\lambda)\Big)

\displaystyle=qt^{\beta},\quad q=\lambda/\Gamma(1+\beta);

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}\Big(N_{\beta}(t,\lambda)\Big)

\displaystyle=qt^{\beta}\Big[1+qt^{\beta}\Big(\frac{\beta B(\beta,1/2)}{2^{2\beta-1}}-1\Big)\Big],

where

denotes the beta function. An alternative form for Var[

] is given in

as

(5.9)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}\Big(N_{\beta}(t,\lambda)\Big)=

\displaystyle qt^{\beta}+\lambda^{2}t^{2\beta}Q(\beta),

where

(5.10)

Q{(\beta)}=\frac{1}{\beta}\Big(\frac{1}{\Gamma(2\beta)}-\frac{1}{\beta\Gamma^{2}(\beta)}\Big).

Also, the covariance functions (see Leonenko et al.
[
23
]
) of the TFPP is given by

(5.11)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}\Big(N_{\beta}(s,\lambda),N_{\beta}(t,\lambda)\Big)

\displaystyle=qs^{\beta}+\lambda^{2}\operatorname{Cov}(E_{\beta}(s),E_{\beta}(s))

\displaystyle=qs^{\beta}+ds^{2\beta}+q^{2}[\beta t^{2\beta}B(\beta,1+\beta;s/t)-(st)^{\beta}],

0<s\leq t
, where
d=\beta q^{2}B(\beta,1+\beta)
, and
B(a,b;x)=\int_{0}^{x}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}dt,\penalty\ 0<x<1
, is the incomplete beta function.

It is also known that (see Meerschaert et al.
[
25
]
) when

(5.12)

N_{\beta}(t,\lambda)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{L}}}{{=}}N(E_{\beta}(t),\lambda),

where
\{E_{\beta}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is the inverse
\beta
-stable subordinator and is independent of
\{N(t,\lambda)\}_{t\geq 0}
.

The PGF of the TFPP is given by

(5.13)

\displaystyle H_{N_{\beta}(t,\lambda)}(z)={\mathbb{E}}(z^{N_{\beta}(t,\lambda)})=M_{\beta}(-\lambda t^{\beta}(1-z)),\penalty\ |z|\leq 1,

see for example Maheshwari and Vellaisamy
[

]

5.2.
Time Fractional Compound Poisson Process.

The study of compound fractional Poisson processes has been of recent interest; see, for example, Begin and Macci
[
8
]
and Kataria and Khandakar
[
17
]
. In this section, we introduce a new time-fractional compound Poisson process, motivated by the compound Poisson process discussed in Section 4.

Definition 5.1
.

(i) Let
\{N_{\beta}(t,\lambda_{j})\},j\geq 1,
be a sequence of independent time fractional Poisson processes with
parameters
\beta
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
. Let
Y_{j}
’s be i.i.d with distribution given in (
3.1
) and is independent of the process

\{N_{\beta}(t,\delta)\}
, where
\delta=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}.
We call the process

(5.14)

\displaystyle G_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}jN_{\beta}(t,{\lambda}_{j}),

a time fractional generalized Poisson process (TFGPP).

The next result shows that
G_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
is indeed a time fractional compound Poisson processes, and is also a subordinated GPP.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
G(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
and
G_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
be the GPP and TFGPP respectively.
For fixed
t>0
,

(5.15)

G_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{L}}}{{=}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\beta}(t,\delta)}Y_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{L}}}{{=}}G_{P}(E_{\beta}(t),\delta),

where
\{E_{\beta}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is an independent inverse
\beta
-stable subordinator.

Proof.

Note, for fixed
t>0
,

\displaystyle G_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}jN_{\beta}(t,{\lambda}_{j})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{L}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}jN(E_{\beta}(t),{\lambda}_{j})\penalty\ \penalty\ (\text{using \eqref{n511}})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{L}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N(E_{\beta}(t),{\delta})}Y_{j}\penalty\ \penalty\ (\text{using \eqref{43a}})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{L}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\beta}(t,\delta)}Y_{j}\penalty\ \penalty\ (\text{using \eqref{n511}}).

From the above results and (
3.3
), we have

(5.16)

\displaystyle G_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{L}}}{{=}}\sum_{j=1}^{N(E_{\beta}(t),{\delta})}Y_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{L}}}{{=}}G_{P}(E_{\beta}(t),\delta),

showing that it is a subordinated GPP.
∎

Henceforth, we call the process
\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\beta}(t,\delta)}Y_{j}
the time fractional compound Poisson process (TFCPP) and denote it by
H_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda}).
Similarly, the time fractional compound Poisson process
of order
k
, denoted by
H_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{(k)})
, can be defined by replacing
\delta
by
\delta_{k}
in the result given in (
5.16
).

The PGF of the TFCPP is

\displaystyle K_{H_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})}(z)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(z^{H_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})}\Big)

\displaystyle=K_{N_{\beta}(t,{\delta})}(K_{Y_{1}}(z))

\displaystyle=M_{\beta}\Big(-\delta t^{\beta}\Big(1-K_{Y_{1}}(z)\Big)\Big)\penalty\ \penalty\ (\text{using \eqref{n512}})

(5.17)

\displaystyle=M_{\beta}\Big(-t^{\beta}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}\Big(1-z^{j}\Big)\Big),

where
M_{\beta}
is defined in (
2.1
).

Remark 5.1

Theorem 5.1

Remark 5.2

The one-dimensional distributions of

Proof.

Using the well-known result (see

5.4.

For real-valued functions
f

denote the composite function. The following two results are from Johnson

Hoppe’s formula
. If

(g(f))^{(m)}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{g^{(k)}(f)}{k!}A_{k,m}(f),

The next lemma is from
[

.

Lemma 5.1
.

(i): If

\Big(f_{1}f_{2}\cdots f_{k}\Big)^{(m)}=\sum_{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=m}\binom{m}{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}}f_{1}^{\left(j_{1}\right)}\cdots f_{k}^{\left(j_{k}\right)}.

(ii): When

(5.26)

(f^{k})^{(m)}=\sum_{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=m}\binom{m}{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}}f^{\left(j_{1}\right)}\cdots f^{\left(j_{k}\right)}.

\displaystyle\quad=\Big.t^{k\beta}\frac{d^{r}}{dz^{r}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}\Big(z^{j}-1\Big)\Big)^{k}\Big|_{z=1}

\displaystyle=t^{k\beta}\sum_{n_{1}+\ldots+n_{k}=r}\binom{r}{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{d^{n_{i}}}{dz^{n_{i}}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}\Big(z^{j}-1\Big)\Big)\Biggr|_{z=1}

\displaystyle=t^{k\beta}\sum_{n_{1}+\ldots+n_{k}=r}\binom{r}{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}j(j-1)\cdots(j-n_{i}+1)

(5.34)

\displaystyle=t^{k\beta}\delta^{k}\sum_{n_{1}+\ldots+n_{k}=r}\binom{r}{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{E}}(Y^{(n_{i})}).

Also, as seen in (
5.29
),

(5.35)

\displaystyle\Big.M_{\beta}^{(k)}\Big(t^{\beta}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}\Big(z^{j}-1\Big)\Big)\Big|_{z=1}

\displaystyle=\frac{k!}{\Gamma(k\beta+1)}.

The result now follows by using (
5.33
)-(
5.35
) in (
5.32
).
∎

5.5.
Long Range Dependence Properties

Here, we discuss the long-range property (LRD) of the TFCPP. An important property of the FPP is that it, unlike the classical Poisson process, has the LRD property
as shown in Biard and Saussereau
[
9
]
. This makes the FPP quite useful
in many applications that arise in finance, econometrics and hydrology.
The LRD property of the
fractional negative binomial process is studied by Maheshwari and Vellaisamy

[
31
]
.

The mean and variance functions of the inverse
\beta
-stable subordinator are given by (see Leonenko et al.,
[
23
]
, Equation (8) and Equation (11))

\mathbb{E}\Big(E_{\beta}(t)\Big)=\frac{t^{\beta}}{\Gamma(\beta+1)}

and

\operatorname{Var}\Big(E_{\beta}(t)\Big)=t^{2\beta}Q(\beta),

where
Q(\beta)
is defined in (
5.10
).

Also, for
0<s\leq t
, we have from (
5.20
) and (
5.11
),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}(H_{\beta}(s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}),H_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Var}(Y_{1})qs^{\beta}+\mathbb{E}^{2}(Y_{1})\operatorname{Cov}(N_{\beta}(s,\delta),N_{\beta}(t,\delta))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Var}(Y_{1})qs^{\beta}+\mathbb{E}^{2}(Y_{1})\Big(qs^{\beta}+\delta^{2}\operatorname{Cov}\Big(E_{\beta}(s),E_{\beta}(t)\Big)\Big)

(5.36)

\displaystyle=qs^{\beta}\mathbb{E}(Y_{1}^{2})+\delta^{2}\mathbb{E}^{2}(Y_{1})\operatorname{Cov}\Big(E_{\beta}(s),E_{\beta}(t)\Big).

For large
t
, it is known that (see Leonenko et al.
[
23
]
)

(5.37)

\operatorname{Cov}\Big(E_{\beta}(s),E_{\beta}(t)\Big)\sim\frac{s^{2\beta}}{\Gamma(2\beta+1)}.

Thus, form (
5.37
) in (
5.36
), we get

(5.38)

\operatorname{Cov}(H_{\beta}(s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}),H_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda}))\sim{\mathbb{E}}(Y_{1}^{2})+\delta^{2}\mathbb{E}^{2}(Y_{1})\frac{s^{2\beta}}{\Gamma(2\beta+1)},

as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Using the above result, we prove the following result.

Theorem 5.5
.

The TFCPP
H_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
possesses the LRD property.

Proof.

For fixed
s>0
and large
t
, we have, from (
5.38
) and (
5.19
),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Corr}\big(H_{\beta}(s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}),\penalty\

\displaystyle H_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}\Big(H_{\beta}(s,\boldsymbol{\lambda}),H_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\Big)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}\Big(H_{\beta}(s,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\Big)}\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}\Big(H_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\Big)}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{{\mathbb{E}}(Y_{1}^{2})+\delta^{2}\mathbb{E}^{2}(Y_{1})\frac{s^{2\beta}}{\Gamma(2\beta+1)}}{\Big\{H_{\beta}(s,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\Big[\mathbb{E}(Y_{1}^{2})qt^{\beta}+\delta^{2}\mathbb{E}^{2}(Y_{1})t^{2\beta}Q(\beta)\Big]\Big\}^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\sim Z(s)t^{-\beta},\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for \penalty\ large}\penalty\ t,

where

Z(s)=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}(Y_{1}^{2})+\delta^{2}\mathbb{E}^{2}(Y_{1})\frac{s^{2\beta}}{\Gamma(2\beta+1)}}{\Big\{\delta^{2}H_{\beta}(s,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\mathbb{E}^{2}(Y_{1})Q(\beta)\Big\}^{1/2}}.

Since
0<\beta<1
, the TFCPP possesses the LRD property.
∎

5.7.
Application to Risk Modeling

The TFCPP
H_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
can serve
an important stochastic model for several disciplines. Note that integer-valued Levy process have been used in financial
econometrics (see Ole Bandorff-Nielsen et al.
[
34
]
).
As the TFCPP has the long range dependence, over-dispersion property, flexibility of the claim size distribution, it can be a more realistic and useful model for financial and insurance modeling and risk theory.

Following Beghin and Macci
[
4
]
, consider the risk model

(5.41)

R_{\beta}(t)=\,ct-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\beta}(t,\delta)}Y_{j}=ct-H_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda}),\\

where
c
denotes the constant premium rate. Here
N_{\beta}(t,\delta)
denotes the number of claims at time
t
and
Y_{j}
’s represent discrete claim sizes which are assumed to be independent of
N_{\beta}(t,\delta).

The expected value of
R_{\beta}(t)
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(R_{\beta}(t))=

\displaystyle\,ct-\mathbb{E}(H_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,ct-qt^{\beta}\mathbb{E}(Y_{1}),

using (
5.18
) and from (
5.19
)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(R_{\beta}(t))=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(H_{\beta}(t,\boldsymbol{\lambda}))=\mathbb{E}(Y_{1}^{2})qt^{\beta}+\mathbb{E}^{2}(Y_{1})\lambda^{2}t^{2\beta}Q(\beta).

Note that the claim-size distribution of
Y_{1}
is given in (
3.2
) and so
\mathbb{E}(Y_{1})
and
\mathbb{E}(Y_{1}^{2})
can be computed easily.

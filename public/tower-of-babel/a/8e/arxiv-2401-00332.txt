Abstract
. We construct various statistical ensembles associated to the 3D Euler equations and prove global regularity of these equations for data living on these sets. Similar results are also proven for generalized SQG equations and some shell models. Qualitative properties of the ensembles and the constructed flows are also given. Keywords: 3D Euler equation, SQG equations, shell models, global regularity, invariant measure, long time behavior, fluctuation-dissipation, statistical ensemble. 2020 MSC: 35B40, 35B44, 35B65, 35Q31, 35Q35, 76D03.

Theorem 1.1

Assume (

is constant in time (see assumptions (

(2)

The constructed solutions enjoy a slow growth property as

(3)

(4)

If in addition the equation (

Our method is based on the fluctuation-dissipation method for
Galerkin approximations and a passage to the limit with the help of the Bourgain globalization technique
[
5
]
(this approach, called IID limit
1
1

1

Inviscid-Infinite-dimensional limit

[

Specifically, a key idea that enables us to treat the 3D Euler relies on analysis of the statistical ensemble rather than the infinite-dimensional limiting measure. It turns out that this new perspective circumvents several difficulties related to lack of conservation inherent to the Euler system. Recall that the fluctuation-dissipation method
introduces a stochastic forcing to the equation, which randomizes the solutions and presumably chooses stable orbits. In order to balance the energy, one adds a dissipation, which
allows for a use of techniques from dissipative equations. Then, one passes the strength of dissipation and the forcing to zero and recovers solutions of the original equation (
1.1

Let us first fix the notation and provide motivation for our study. We assume that the equation (

Since we work on a torus, as usual we assume that the initial condition

Equations of the form (

Mathematically, under mild additional assumptions, (

Note that local well posedness of SQG equation was proved by geometrical arguments in

In Section

However an understanding of the global well posedness of smooth solutions of (
1.1

[

, however they require stronger
assumptions than known

(1.4)

\mathcal{E}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{3}}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx\,.

We remark that the conservation of the energy

also plays a crucial rule in the present manuscript, as detailed below.
More geometrical conditions on the global existence were first obtained by
[

[

and references therein, however the validity of the conditions for particular problems is not known.
We remark that in two dimensions (2D), the Euler equation is
known to be globally well posed in

see
[
32

, due to different structure of the vorticity equation (vortex stretching term

On the other hand, it is known that some solutions of (

can grow-up (diverge to infinity in some norm as

)
or even blow-up (diverge to infinity in some norm as

Indeed, even for 2D Euler equation it was proved in

[

The existence of blow-up for 3D Euler equation was a long standing open problem and it was predicted in several numerical attempts, see
[
26
]
and references therein for older results. More recently, a convincing numerical scenario was proposed in
[

Recently, the blow-up solutions for 3D Euler equation on

[

On the other hand, it was shown in
[

that if there are blowing-up solutions of 3D Euler equation, then they are unstable. Tao
[

has introduced an averaged version of the

3D
Euler (Tao’s model with zero viscosity). Let’s notice that the nonlinear averaged operator of Tao’s model satisfies the assumptions on

For the generalized SQG equation (see Example

1.1
) can develop a singularity from regular initial conditions. A central question we are interested in, is whether the blow up is generic, or it occurs only for well prepared initial data. Before proceeding, let us first discuss known global solutions. Again, our problem (
1.1
) is very general, there is a huge amount of the fluid literature, and therefore we restrict our discussion to the Euler and SQG equations.

First, observe that any solution
(u_{1},u_{2})
of 2D Euler equation on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
, that is depending on
(x_{1},x_{2})
, can be extended to a global solution
(u_{1},u_{2},0)
of 3D Euler equation on
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
, still depending on
(x_{1},x_{2})
. Since 2D Euler equation
is globally well posed in
L^{\infty}
, we obtain large class of global solutions, which are, however, not truly three dimensional, and we believe that these flows are not stable in 3D.
There are also several special solutions, see for example the
construction of DiPerna and Lions
[
45
]
or Yudovich
[
66
]
of
global solutions of the form (shear flows)

(1.5)

u(t,x)=(u_{1}(x_{2}),0,u_{3}(x_{1}-tu_{1}(x_{2})))

of 3D Euler equation with
p\equiv 0
. Note, that for smooth, periodic functions

one obtains that
\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{1,p}}
,

, diverges to infinity
as

. Also, there are infinitely many equilibria for 3D Euler equation called Beltrami flows, that satisfy

(1.6)

\textrm{curl}u(x)=\lambda(x)u(x)

for some

, see see
[

. However, most of the known solutions of (

flows depend on two variables only, the exception is GABC flow, see
[

. It is also not expected that these stationary flows are generic solutions of the 3D Euler equation.
Finally, we remark that there are also global solutions on different domains such are shear flows in the whole space, axi-symmetric flows in cylindrical domains, or Hill’s spherical vortex for the rotating fluid, see
[
48
]
.

There are also weak solutions to (
1.1
), and in particular to the 3D Euler equation, that are global in time. However, weak solutions possess a low regularity and it is not known if they are unique. In fact, the non-uniqueness is known if the assumed regularity is sufficiently small, see for example

[

,

,

[

that the vanishing viscosity can serve as a selector for the weak solutions.

In the present manuscript we are interested in the global smooth solutions of (
1.1
) which are stable in a probabilistic sense as detailed below. As in our previous work
[
25
]
, an important step of our methods relies on the fluctuation-dissipation method. Specifically, we add a stochastic forcing of order
\sqrt{\varepsilon}
to (
1.1
) which randomizes the dynamics and presumably make the solutions generic. In order to balance the influx of the (stochastic) energy to the system, we also add a dissipation term of order
\varepsilon
. The dissipation term is carefully chosen so that its smoothing effect allows us to prove the well posedness of the dynamics. In addition, we show that for each
\varepsilon>0
there are invariant measures
\mu_{\varepsilon}
and in the support of
\mu_{\varepsilon}
are almost surely global solutions of the modified dynamics with a controlled growth. By passing
\varepsilon\to 0
, we show that
\mu_{\varepsilon}
converge to the limiting measure
\mu
, which is invariant under the dynamics of (
1.1
) and supports global solutions.

The fluctuation-dissipation method naturally leads to a study of dissipative and stochastic problems arising in fluid dynamics. Probabilistic well-posedness, invariant measures, and related questions were investigated in the context of fluid dynamics in the last decades under various assumptions on the noise and underlying problem, see for example

[
2
,
15
,
23
,
21
,
22
,
29
,
37
]
. More specifically, the fluctuation-dissipation was used for the 2D Euler equation in
[
38
,
37
]
, SQG equation
[
25
]
, as well as KdV, Benjamin-Ono, Klein-Gordon, or NLS equations
[
36
,
57
,
56
]
.
Note that there is another construction of
invariant states based on the Gibbs measures (see e.g.
[
5
,
6
,
69
,

,
67
,
63
,

,

). Notice, however, that, as already identified in
[

, a ‘traditional way’ of constructing Gibbs measure cannot be used for SQG or 3D Euler equation since the nonlinearities cannot be defined in the sense of distributions.

The important feature of our setting is that we assume the existence of only one conserved quantity, the
L^{2}
norm. The other invariants are only used in derivation of quantitative properties of the support of the invariant measure. In addition, we can only rely on the local-well well-posedness of (
1.1
) since in general the global well-posedness is either not known or does not hold true. The major advantage of the
probabilistic approach is that we obtain a priori bounds on the invariant measures that are not available for the deterministic dynamics. Specifically, due to our choice of the dissipation operator, we obtain exponential moment bounds on high Sobolev norms. We would like to stress, that our main goal is to pass
\varepsilon\to 0
in front of the stochastic and dissipative terms. In particular, if
\varepsilon\approx 0
, then the smoothing and random effects are very small compared to the deterministic evolution of (
1.1
).
Moreover, even the moment bounds in high Sobolev norms were not sufficient for proofs of our main results.

To resolve these issues, we work with the finite dimensional projections of (
1.1
) onto Fourier modes, see (
2.1
), (
2.2
) below. The fluctuation-dissipation method for the finite dimensional projections follow standard arguments, but very special care should be taken in the choice of the smoothing operator. It should provide sufficient smoothing and control on the temporal growth of the norms. However, at the same time, it must give enough flexibility for integration against non-linear functions needed in the proof of infinite-dimensionality. We establish that for each projection and
\varepsilon>0
there is an invariant measure of the finite dimensional dynamics with the moment bounds that are independent of
\varepsilon>0
. Afterwards, we pass to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
and obtain an invariant measure of the deterministic finite dimensional system. This result is probably known to experts, but we could not find it in the literature in full generality. It is essential to obtain moment bounds that are indepedendent of the dimension, which allows us to pass to the limit and treat the full infinite-dimensional problem. The proof of the invariance of the measure is already non-trivial and requires rather delicate decomposition of the probability space.

The passage of the dimension
N
of the projection to infinity is the main technical achievement of the present manuscript. By using the uniform in dimension estimates, we show that there exists an invariant measure
\mu
of (
1.1
). The study of
\mu

requires a delicate application of the Bourgain’s argument
[
5
]
and deterministic estimates on the maximal existence time. As such we establish that for any projection the solutions have controlled growth with high probability. Importantly neither the probability nor the growth depends on dimension of the projection. This observation allows us to pass to the limit and obtain that the functions in the support of finite-dimensional invariant measures are in fact global solutions of the full dynamics. As mentioned above, the approach that consists in combining fluctuation-dissipation and Bourgain’s Gibbs measure argument has been originally developed in
[
58
]
. However, some important conclusions of
[
58
]
heavily depended on the existence of two coercive conservation laws; this property is not satisfied by the
3D
Euler equations. In the present work, we were able to tackle this problem by adopting a different perspective: instead of relying our analysis on the support of the infinite-dimensional limiting measure
\mu
, we make a careful analysis of the statistical ensemble to obtain global solutions for data living in the statistical ensemble and not necessarily belonging the support of
\mu
.
It is important to notice that the concerned data are in the statistical ensembles of the finite dimensional projections. To the authors’ best knowledge this observation was not used in the previous works. We also show that the flow of (
1.1
) on the support of
\mu
is well defined and global.

The rest of the manuscript is dedicated to quantitative properties of
\mu
if the system possess additional conservation laws. In particular, if there is another coercive conservation law as for some shell models, or generalized SQG equation, we show that the support of
\mu
contains arbitrary large functions. Of course, these functions lie on the global trajectories, but the large global solutions arise from the global ensemble as well (see remarks above). The arguments follow standard procedures, only the extra care need to be taken when passing to appropriate limits. The final section is dedicated to the infinite-dimensionality of the support of the invariant measure, which follows if there are infinitely many conserved quantities. We remark that if there is only finitely many invariants, say
K
, then it is possible to show that the support of
\mu
is at least
K
-dimensional. However, we were not able to locate in the literature any good example, thus for the sake of clarity we only discuss a specific model given by Casimirs of the generalized SQG equation. However, we believe that our argument can be generalized if necessary. The main challenge of this part is an appropriate choice of the test function in conjunction with the properties of our dissipation operator. At this moment we justify our choice of non-linear operator (combination of
p
-Laplacian with its higher order version) rather than a simpler linear one.

Next, let us provide assumptions on the bi-linear term

in (

k\geq 1
, usually

or

Assume a solution of (

) is
k

u:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
or
\mathbb{C}^{k}

For any

define
\mathcal{H}^{m}=\{u\in(H^{m})^{k}:\textrm{div }u=0\textrm{ if }k\geq 2\}
, where
H^{m}
is the usual Sobolev-Slobodeckij, or equivalently potential space.
Observe that if

, the divergence is well defined point-wise since

is sufficiently smooth.
We assume that
B:\mathcal{H}^{m}\times\mathcal{H}^{m}\to\mathcal{H}^{m-1}

is a bilinear map that satisfies for any
2\leq k\leq m-1
and any
u,v,w\in\mathcal{H}^{k}

(1.7)

\displaystyle\langle B(v,u),w\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle B(v,w),u\rangle\,,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\|B(u,v)\|_{H^{k}}

\displaystyle{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0ptk\hskip 2.0pt}\|u\|_{H^{k}}\|v\|_{H^{k+1}}\,,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\|B(u,v)\|

\displaystyle{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0pt\hskip 2.0pt}\|u\|_{H^{\ell}}\|v\|_{H^{3-\ell}}\qquad\textrm{for any}\quad\ell\in\{0,1,2\}\,,

(1.10)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}B(u,u)dx

\displaystyle=0\,.

By setting
w=u
in (
1.7

(1.11)

\langle B(v,u),u\rangle=0\,.

There are many examples of
B
in the fluid dynamics that satisfy the above hypotheses. We provide some for illustration, but we do not
attempt to give a complete list or optimal conditions.

Example 1.1
.

The first example is the classical Euler equation in two or three dimensions (
d=2,3
), in which case
u:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a vector field. Then,

(1.12)

B(u,v)=\Pi((u\nabla)v)\,,

where
\Pi
is the projection on divergence free vector fields. The property (
1.7
) follows directly from the integration by parts and the fact that
u
is divergence free. Since

H^{k}
in 2D or 3D is an algebra for
k\geq 2
, by classical properties of Helmholtz-Leray projection
\Pi
one has

(1.13)

\|B(u,v)\|_{H^{k}}{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0ptk\hskip 2.0pt}\|(u\nabla)v\|_{H^{k}}{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0ptk\hskip 2.0pt}\|u\|_{H^{k}}\||\nabla v|\|_{H^{k}}{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0ptk\hskip 2.0pt}\|u\|_{H^{k}}\|v\|_{H^{k+1}}

and (
1.8
) follows. Also, since
H^{2}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
when
d=2,3
,

(1.14)

\|B(u,v)\|{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0pt\hskip 2.0pt}\|(u\nabla)v\|{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0pt\hskip 2.0pt}\|u\|\||\nabla v|\|_{L^{\infty}}{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0ptk\hskip 2.0pt}\|u\|\|v\|_{H^{3}}

and (
1.9
) follows for
\ell=0
. Similarly one obtains (
1.9
) for
\ell=2
. Finally, since
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{4}

(1.15)

\|B(u,v)\|{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0pt\hskip 2.0pt}\|(u\nabla)v\|{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0pt\hskip 2.0pt}\|u\|_{L^{4}}\||\nabla v|\|_{L^{4}}{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0ptk\hskip 2.0pt}\|u\|_{H^{1}}\|v\|_{H^{2}}\,,

and we established (
1.9
). Finally, by the properties of
P
,
B(u,u)=\textrm{div}(u\otimes u)+\nabla p
for appropriate function
p
, and (
1.10
) follows
from divergence theorem and periodicity of
p
.

We remark that if
d=3
, then one can add Coriolis force
\Pi(f\times u)
to
B
as

(1.16)

B_{f}(u,v):=B(u,v)+\frac{1}{2}(\Pi(f\times u)+\Pi(f\times v))\,,

where
f
is a fixed vector field on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
. Then, by properties of triple product, (
1.7
) follows from

(1.17)

\langle f\times u,w\rangle=-\langle f\times w,u\rangle\,.

The remaining assumptions on
B
hold if

The Euler’s equation has also the vorticity formulation. Specifically, if we define

\omega=\textrm{curl}(u)
, then
\omega
satisfies

(1.18)

0=\partial_{t}\omega+u\nabla\omega+\omega\nabla u=:\partial_{t}\omega+\tilde{B}(u,\omega)\,.

Note that the new bi-linear form
\tilde{B}
in general does not satisfy (
1.11
) due to the presence of the vorticity stretching term
\omega\nabla u
. Hence if

, we have to work with the velocity formulation (

) and since there are no other (useful) conservation laws, the results of Section
8
and
9

If

, then the situation changes and
\omega=\textrm{curl}(u)
can be interpreted as a
scalar function. Also, the vorticity stretching term
\omega\nabla u
vanishes and we obtain that

\tilde{B}(u,v)=u\nabla v
and all assumptions (

)-(

) are satisfied. In fact, due to the Biot-Savart law,
u=\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\omega
with
\nabla^{\perp}g=(-\partial_{x_{2}}g,\partial_{x_{1}}g)
, and therefore 2D Euler equation is a special of an active scalar equation discussed in Example

. Thus, if one can works with 2D Euler equation in the velocity formulation (unknown field is velocity), then all results in Sections (

) - (

(1.19)

H(\omega)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\omega|^{2}\,dx

and in addition to the results of Sections (
2
) - (
6
)
the conclusions of Section

are valid as well (for details see the discussion at the beginning of Section

).

Also, 2D Euler equation has other invariants called Casimirs (see
[

,
27
,
48
]

(1.20)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}f(\omega)\,dx\,,

where
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is any sufficiently regular function. Hence, the results of Section

are also valid for 2D Euler equation in the vorticity formulation.

Example 1.2
.

Another important class of examples covered in the present paper are active scalar equations in two or three dimensions (

\|B(u,v)\|{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0pt\hskip 2.0pt}\|K[u]\|\||\nabla v|\|_{L^{\infty}}{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0ptk\hskip 2.0pt}\|u\|\|v\|_{H^{3}}

Remark 1.4

(Remark on the statistical ensemble

associated to the

Euler system)
.

Let’s start by describing the statistical ensemble

. It is roughly the union of sets

, where

is constructed on the basis of

). Namely, we couldn’t rule out singularity of its support. However, the remaining parts of the statistical ensemble, namely the sub-ensembles

, where

, satisfies (

). Similar consideration gives rise to the large data argument for the

Euler equation.

The Euler statistical ensemble

is indeed invariant under the constructed flow. Which means that the evolution of any datum

will give, at any time, an element of

should not be trivial so that it can provides functions supported on infinite number of Fourier modes. This point remains a conjecture.

\displaystyle I_{1}=-\sum_{|\alpha|=3}\langle D^{\alpha}\Pi_{N}w_{N},B_{N}(u,D^{\alpha}w_{N})\rangle-\sum_{|\alpha|=3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta+\gamma=\alpha\\
\beta\neq 0\end{subarray}}c_{\beta}\langle D^{\alpha}\Pi_{N}w_{N},B_{N}(\nabla^{\beta}u,D^{\gamma}w_{N})\rangle=-J_{1}-J_{2}.

In addition, using the definition of

, (

), and (

)

(2.82)

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|=3}\langle D^{\alpha}\Pi_{N}w_{N},\Pi_{N}B(u,D^{\alpha}\Pi_{N}w_{N})\rangle+\langle D^{\alpha}\Pi_{N}w_{N},\Pi_{N}B(u,D^{\alpha}(I-\Pi_{N})w_{N})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|=3}\langle D^{\alpha}\Pi_{N}w_{N},B(u,D^{\alpha}(I-\Pi_{N})w_{N})\rangle=-\sum_{|\alpha|=3}\langle D^{\alpha}(I-\Pi_{N})w_{N},B(u,D^{\alpha}\Pi_{N}w_{N})\rangle\,,

and therefore (

), Poincaré inequality, and (

) imply

(2.84)

\displaystyle|J_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\|(I-\Pi_{N})w_{N}\|_{H^{3}}\|u\|_{H^{2}}\|\Pi_{N}w_{N}\|_{H^{4}}{\ \lesssim\ \ }\lambda_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{H^{2}}\|w_{N}\|_{H^{4}}^{2}

(2.85)

\displaystyle{\ \lesssim\ \ }\lambda_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{H^{2}}(\|u_{N}\|_{H^{4}}+\|u\|_{H^{4}})^{2}{\ \lesssim\ \ }\lambda_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}R^{3}.

To estimate
J_{2}
, we first treat the case
|\beta|=k\in\{1,\ 3\}
with the help of (

) with
\ell=2
and
\ell=0
respectively

|\langle D^{\alpha}\Pi_{N}w_{N},B_{N}(\nabla^{\beta}u,D^{\gamma}w_{N})\rangle|\leq\|w_{N}\|_{H^{3}}\|B(\nabla^{\beta}u,D^{\gamma}w_{N})\|{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0pt\hskip 2.0pt}\|w_{N}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\|u\|_{H^{3}}\,.

If
k=2

) with
\ell=1
yields

(2.87)

|\langle D^{\alpha}\Pi_{N}w_{N},B_{N}(\nabla^{\beta}u,D^{\gamma}w_{N})\rangle|\leq\|w_{N}\|_{H^{3}}\|B(\nabla^{\beta}u,D^{\gamma}w_{N})\|{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0pt\hskip 2.0pt}\|w_{N}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\|u\|_{H^{3}}\,,

and overall

(2.88)

\displaystyle|I_{1}|{\ \lesssim\ \ }\|w_{N}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\|u\|_{H^{3}}+\lambda_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}R^{3}.

Next, using (
1.8
)

(2.89)

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leq\|w_{N}\|_{H^{3}}\|B(w_{N},u_{N})\|_{H^{3}}{\ \lesssim\ \ }\|w_{N}\|_{H^{3}}\|w_{N}\|_{H^{3}}\|u_{N}\|_{H^{4}}=\|w_{N}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\|u_{N}\|_{H^{4}}.

Finally, from Poincaré inequality, (

), and (

(2.90)

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq\|w_{N}\|_{H^{4}}\|(I-\Pi_{N})B(u,u)\|_{H^{2}}{\ \lesssim\ \ }\lambda_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\|u_{N}\|_{H^{4}}+\|u\|_{H^{4}})\|B(u,u)\|_{H^{3}}

\displaystyle{\ \lesssim\ \ }\lambda_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}R\|u\|_{H^{3}}\|u\|_{H^{4}}{\ \lesssim\ \ }\lambda_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}R^{3}\,.

Hence, we proved that

(2.92)

{\partial_{t}}\|w_{N}(t)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}{\ \lesssim\ \ }\|w_{N}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}(\|u\|_{H^{3}}+\|u_{N}\|_{H^{4}})+\lambda_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}R^{3}{\ \lesssim\ \ }R\|w_{N}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\lambda_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}R^{3}\,,

and consequently for any

The first assertion of the lemma follows after passing

3.
Fluctuation-Dissipation and Galerkin approximation

In this section, we introduce the fluctuation-dissipation method for (
2.8
). Specifically, we add an dissipative term with a
small coefficient
\alpha
which regularizes the dynamics and allows us to prove the global well posedness of solutions. In addition, to balance the energy, we introduce
a stochastic forcing multiplied by a small parameter
\sqrt{\alpha}
. This generic forcing presumably stirs the dynamics such that only observable solutions persist.

Once the well-posedness of the stochastic flow is established, we prove the existence of invariant measures and obtain the moment bounds. The dependence of measures on

\alpha
is crucial, since in the sections below we pass
\alpha\to 0
.

Before we proceed, let us recall our stochastic framework.
Let
(\beta_{m}(t))
be a sequence of independent real standard Brownian motions on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{\mathbb{P}})
and for an integer
N\geq 1
define

(3.1)

\displaystyle\zeta(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}^{d}}a_{m}e_{m}(x)\beta_{m}(t)\,,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\zeta_{N}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{N}\zeta.

Recall that the functions
e_{m}=e^{{\textbf{i}}mx}
are eigenfuctions of Laplacian on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, and therefore form an orthogonal basis of
L^{2}
.
To preserve zero mean condition, we always assume
a_{0}=0
.
As above,
we denote
\Pi_{N}
the projection of
L^{2}
to the finite dimensional space
E_{N}

spanned by the
eigenfunctions of
(-\Delta)
corresponding to eigenvalues
\lambda
with
\lambda\leq N
.
The sequence of vectors
a_{m}=(a_{m}^{1},\cdots,a_{m}^{d})
is such that
|a_{m}|^{2}=|a_{m}^{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|a_{m}^{3}|^{2}
converges sufficiently fast to
0
as
|m|\to\infty
(specified below). We assume that
a_{m}^{i}\neq 0
for all
m
and
i
.
If the solution is vector valued, we in addition assume that
\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}a_{m}^{\ell}m_{\ell}=0
so that
\nabla\cdot\zeta=\nabla\cdot\zeta_{N}=0
. Note that this is assumption poses no restriction on the noise, since
the gradient part of the noise
can be absorbed into the pressure, which compensates diverge free condition.
Let us denote by
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
the natural filtration of the Brownian motion.

Define the numbers

(3.3)

\displaystyle A_{0,N}=\sum_{|m|\leq N}|a_{m}|^{2},\qquad A_{0}=\sum_{m}|a_{m}|^{2}\,.

Throughout the rest of the paper, we assume that
A_{0,N}\leq A_{0}<\infty
.

3.1.
Stochastic Global well posedness

For any finite
N=\{1,2,\cdots\}
consider the stochastic
problem

(3.4)

\displaystyle du_{N}

\displaystyle=\left(-B_{N}[(u_{N},u_{N}]-\alpha\mathcal{A}(u_{N})\right)dt+\sqrt{\alpha}d\zeta_{N}\,,

(3.5)

\displaystyle u_{N}|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=u_{0}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}\,,

where for any smooth
u
, the operator
\mathcal{A}
satisfies for some
{s^{*}}\geq 4
and each
u\in\mathcal{E}_{N}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}(u),u\rangle:=\mathcal{G}(u)\geq\kappa_{N}\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}}}^{4}\,.

Moreover,
\mathcal{G}
satisfies for every
N\geq 1

(3.7)

\mathcal{G}(u)\geq\mathcal{G}(\Pi_{N}u)\qquad\textrm{and}\quad\mathcal{G}(\Pi_{N}u)\nearrow\mathcal{G}(u)\quad\textrm{as}\quad N\to\infty\,.

We also require
\mathcal{A}
to satisfy an upper bound, which although not too restrictive, is technical to formulate in full generality. For that purpose, we
decided to proceed with a concrete operator
\mathcal{A}
. Fix any
{s^{*}}\geq 4
,
{s^{*}_{1}}>{s^{*}}
, and
q\geq 2
and define
\mathcal{A}
as

(3.8)

\mathcal{A}(u)=e^{\rho(\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}}})}(a_{1}(-\Delta)^{s^{*}_{1}}u+a_{2}\Delta(|\Delta u|^{q-2}\Delta u)-a_{3}\nabla\cdot(|\nabla u|^{2q-2}\nabla u))\,,

where
\rho:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a strictly increasing, convex function, and
a_{i}
are numbers in
\{0,1\}
, not all equal to zero. Unless otherwise specified,
a_{i}=1
.
Let us remark that
\rho(x)\geq\delta x
for some
\delta=\rho^{\prime}(0)>0
.

To easier locate the arguments, where we used specific
structure of
\mathcal{A}
, we use the phrase: ‘due to structural properties of
\mathcal{A}
’.
Notice that for (
3.8
) one has

(3.9)

\mathcal{G}(u)=e^{\rho(\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}}})}\left(a_{1}\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}_{1}}}^{2}+a_{2}\|u\|_{W^{2,q}}^{q}+a_{3}\|u\|_{W^{1,2q}}^{2q}\right)

and (
3.6
) holds due to the equivalence of norms on finite dimensional space
\mathcal{E}_{N}
and
\rho(x)\geq\delta x
. In addition, (
3.7
) is satisfied since
\rho
is increasing and
we work on the finite dimensional spaces spanned by eigenfunctions of the Laplacian. We remark that although the argument of
\rho
contains
H^{{s^{*}}}
norm, it does not play important role in
this section, as we work in finite dimensional spaces. We keep this notation for easier comparison below.

First, we establish the global well-posedness.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
N<\infty
. The problem (
3.4
), (
3.5
) is stochastically globally well-posed on
\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}
. More precisely,
for any
\mathcal{F}_{t>0}
-independent random variable
u_{0}\in\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}
, there is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
adapted process
u_{\alpha,N}
in
C_{t}\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}
, that is almost surely unique and
depends continuously on initial condition
u_{0}\in\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}
.

Sketch of the proof.

Fix
T>0
. Since we are working in finite dimensional spaces, it suffices to consider only the
L^{2}
norm of functions (all norms are equivalent and constants depend on
N
).
We keep
H^{s^{*}}
norm only for
easier comparison below. First, note that if
(z_{\alpha},v_{\alpha})
solves

(3.10)

\displaystyle dz_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\alpha}d\zeta_{N},\quad z_{\alpha}(0)=0\,,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\partial_{t}}v_{\alpha}+B_{N}(v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha},v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha})&=-\alpha\mathcal{A}(v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha}),\\
v_{\alpha}(0)&=u_{0}\,,\end{split}

then
u_{\alpha,N}=z_{\alpha}+v_{\alpha}
solves (
3.4
), by linearity.
In addition, if
u_{\alpha,N}
is vector valued, then since
\zeta_{N}
is divergence free so is
z_{\alpha}
, and then
u_{\alpha,N}
is divergence free as well.
The solution of (
3.10
) is given by

(3.12)

\displaystyle z_{\alpha}(t)=\sqrt{\alpha}\zeta_{N}(t)

and by Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality and equivalence of norms, for any
k\geq 1,\ l\geq 1,s\geq 0
there holds

(3.13)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|z_{\alpha}(t)\|_{W^{s,k}}^{2l}{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0ptT,k,A_{0},l,N,s\hskip 2.0pt}\ \alpha^{p}.

Denote

\mathcal{M}(u):=\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}_{1}}}^{2}+\|u\|_{W^{2,q}}^{q}+\|u\|_{W^{1,2q}}^{2q}\,.

To prove well-posedness of (
3.10
) – (
3.11
), we fix a realization of the process
z_{\alpha}
and consider the corresponding deterministic equation (
3.11
).
Since the local well posedness of (
3.11
) is established by standard arguments, see for example techniques in Section
2
and

[
52
,
58
]
, we only derive a priori
L^{2}
bounds (which are equivalent to
H^{s^{*}}
bounds in the finite dimensional space
\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}
),
that imply global well-posedness.
Evaluating
{\partial_{t}}\|v_{\alpha}\|^{2}

and using (
3.11
) with (
1.11
), we obtain

(3.14)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\partial_{t}}\|v_{\alpha}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\langle v_{\alpha},B(v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha},v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha})\rangle-\alpha\langle\mathcal{A}(v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha}),v_{\alpha}\rangle

(3.15)

\displaystyle=\langle z_{\alpha},B(v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha},v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha})\rangle-\alpha\mathcal{G}(v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha})-\alpha\langle\mathcal{A}(v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha}),z_{\alpha}\rangle\,.

Since all norms are equivalent in finite dimensions, by (
1.8
) and (
3.6
)

(3.16)

\displaystyle|\langle z_{\alpha},B(v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha},v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha})\rangle|

\displaystyle{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0ptN\hskip 2.0pt}\|z_{\alpha}\|\|z_{\alpha}+v_{\alpha}\|^{2}\leq C_{N,\kappa_{N},{s^{*}}}\frac{\|z_{\alpha}\|^{2}}{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha\kappa_{N}}{2}\|z_{\alpha}+v_{\alpha}\|^{4}_{H^{s^{*}}}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq C_{N,\rho,{s^{*}}}\frac{\|z_{\alpha}\|^{2}}{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{4}\mathcal{G}(v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha})\,.

In addition, due to structural properties of
\mathcal{A}
, integration by parts, and Hölder inequality

(3.18)

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{A}(v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha}),z_{\alpha}\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{\rho(\|v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha}\|_{H^{s^{*}}})}(\|v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha}\|_{H^{s^{*}_{1}}}\|z_{\alpha}\|_{H^{s^{*}_{1}}}+\|v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha}\|_{W^{2,q}}^{q-1}\|z_{\alpha}\|_{W^{2,q}}+\|v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha}\|_{W^{1,2q}}^{2q-1}\|z_{\alpha}\|_{W^{1,2q}})

(3.19)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{G}(v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha})+Ce^{\rho(\|v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha}\|_{H^{s^{*}}})}\mathcal{M}(z_{\alpha})\,.

Hence, using equivalence of norms in finite dimensions

(3.20)

\displaystyle{\partial_{t}}\|v_{\alpha}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{N,\rho,{s^{*}}}}{\alpha}\|z_{\alpha}\|^{2}+\alpha\left(C_{N,{s^{*}},{s^{*}_{1}}}^{*}\mathcal{M}(z_{\alpha})-\mathcal{M}(v_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha})\right)=:J(t).

From (
3.13
) follows that for
{\mathbb{P}}-
almost all
\omega\in\Omega
there is
C(\omega,T)>0
such that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{M}(z_{\alpha}(t))\leq C(\omega,T,{s^{*}_{1}},q).

Fix any such
\omega
. Then,
for any fixed
t\in[0,T]
we have either
\mathcal{M}(z_{\alpha}(t)+v_{\alpha}(t))\leq C_{N,{s^{*}},{s^{*}_{1}}}^{*}C(\omega,T,{s^{*}},q)
, and therefore

(3.22)

\displaystyle J(t)\leq\frac{C_{N,\rho,{s^{*}}}}{\alpha}\|z_{\alpha}(t)\|^{2}+C_{\rho,{s^{*}},\omega,T}\leq C_{N,\rho,{s^{*}},{s^{*}_{1}},\omega,T,\alpha}

or
\mathcal{M}(z_{\alpha}(t)+v_{\alpha}(t))\geq C_{N,{s^{*}}}^{*}C(\omega,T,{s^{*}},q)
, which implies

(3.23)

\displaystyle J(t)\leq\frac{C_{N,\rho,{s^{*}}}}{\alpha}\|z_{\alpha}(t)\|^{2}\leq C_{N,\rho,{s^{*}},{s^{*}_{1}},\omega,T,\alpha}\,.

Thus,

(3.24)

\displaystyle{\partial_{t}}\|v_{\alpha}\|^{2}\leq C_{N,\rho,{s^{*}},{s^{*}_{1}},\omega,T,\alpha}

and in particular

(3.25)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|v_{\alpha}\|^{2}\leq\|u_{0}\|^{2}+C_{N,\rho,{s^{*}},{s^{*}_{1}},\omega,T,\alpha}

and the global existence of
v_{\alpha}
and
u_{\alpha,N}
follows from usual iteration.

Uniqueness and the continuity with respect to data follows standard arguments (see for example
[
58
]
).
∎

3.2.
Stationary measures and moment bounds

Denote
\text{Bor}(X)
and
\mathfrak{p}(X)
respectively the collection of Borel sets and Borel probability measures on a Banach space
X
.
For fixed
N\geq 1
and
{s^{*}}\geq 0
and
w\in\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}
, let
u_{\alpha,N}(t,w)
be the solution of (
3.4
) with
u_{\alpha,N}(0,w)=w

(see Proposition
3.1
for well-posedness).
We define the transition probability

\displaystyle T_{\alpha,t}^{N}(w,\Gamma)={\mathbb{P}}(u_{\alpha,N}(t,w)\in\Gamma)\qquad\Gamma\in\text{Bor}(\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}),\ \ t\geq 0,

and define the Markov semi-group
\mathfrak{P}_{\alpha,t}^{N}:L^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}},{\mathbb{R}})\to L^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}},{\mathbb{R}})

and its dual
\mathfrak{P}_{\alpha,t}^{N*}:\mathfrak{p}(\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}})\to\mathfrak{p}(\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}})
as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}_{\alpha,t}^{N}f(w)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}f(u_{\alpha,N}(t,w))=\int_{\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}}f(w)T_{\alpha,t}^{N}(w,dv)\,,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}_{\alpha,t}^{N*}\lambda(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}}\lambda(dw)T_{\alpha,t}^{N}(w,\Gamma)\,.

Since by Proposition
3.1
the solution
u_{\alpha,N}(t,u_{0})
is continuous in
u_{0}
, the Markov semi-group
\mathfrak{P}_{\alpha,t}^{N}
is Feller, that is,
for any
t\geq 0
, one has
\mathfrak{P}_{\alpha,t}^{N}C_{b}(\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}})\subset C_{b}(\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}})
, where
C_{b}(X)
denotes the space of bounded continuous functions on
X
.

The following two propositions establish the existence of stationary measures together with their moment bounds. Since
\alpha
is fixed, we omit the
explicit dependence of
u
on
\alpha
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Fix an integer
N\geq 1
,
\alpha>0
, and a smooth function
F:[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
. If

u_{0}
is a random variable on the space
\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}
with
{\mathbb{E}}F(\|u_{0}\|^{2})<\infty
and
u_{N}
is the solution
of (
3.4
) with
u_{N}(0)=u_{0}
, then

(3.26)

{\mathbb{E}}F(\|u_{N}(t)\|^{2})+2\alpha{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime}(\|u_{N}(\tau)\|^{2})\mathcal{G}(u_{N}(\tau))d\tau\\
={\mathbb{E}}F(\|u_{0}\|^{2})+\alpha A_{0,N}{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime}(\|u_{N}(\tau)\|^{2})d\tau+2{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime\prime}(\|u_{N}(\tau)\|^{2})\sum_{m}a_{m}^{2}\langle u_{N}(\tau),e_{m}\rangle^{2}d\tau

and in particular if
F(x)=x
, then

(3.27)

{\mathbb{E}}\|u_{N}(t)\|^{2}+2\alpha{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}(u_{N}(\tau))d\tau={\mathbb{E}}\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\alpha A_{0,N}t\,.

Proof.

Since by (
1.11
)

(3.28)

F^{\prime}(\|u_{N}(t)\|^{2})\langle B(u_{N},u_{N}),u_{N}\rangle=0\,,

then, Itô formula applied to
F(\|u_{N}\|^{2})
imply (after omission of the argument
\|u_{N}\|^{2}
of
F,F^{\prime}
, and
F^{\prime\prime}
)

(3.29)

dF+2\alpha F^{\prime}\mathcal{G}(u_{N})dt=\alpha\left(F^{\prime}A_{0,N}+2F^{\prime\prime}\sum_{m}a_{m}^{2}\langle u_{N},e_{m}\rangle^{2}\right)dt+\sqrt{\alpha}d\mathcal{M}

where
\mathcal{M}
is a martingale. An integration and taking the expectation yields (
3.26
) and in particular (
3.27
).
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

For any integer
N\geq 1
and any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the equation (
3.4
) admits a stationary measure
\mu_{\alpha,N}
satisfying the identity

(3.30)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}}\mathcal{G}(v)\mu_{\alpha,N}(dv)

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{0,N}}{2}\,.

Also, for any
m\geq 0
one has

(3.31)

\int_{\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}}\|v\|^{2m}\mathcal{G}(v)\mu_{\alpha,N}(dv)\leq C\,,

where
C
depends only on
m,{s^{*}},\rho
.

Proof.

Since the proof follows standard lines, we only highlight crucial steps. Set the initial condition
u_{0}\equiv 0
and define measures

(3.32)

\mu_{\alpha,N}^{t}(\Gamma)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{\alpha,\tau}^{N}(u_{0},\Gamma)d\tau\,,\qquad\Gamma\in\textrm{Bor}(\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}})\,.

Then, for any
R>0
, interpolation, Chebyshev inequality, (
3.6
), and Proposition
3.2
yield

(3.33)

\displaystyle\mu_{\alpha,N}^{t}(\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}\setminus B_{R}(H^{s^{*}}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{P}(\|u_{\alpha,N}\|_{H^{s^{*}}}>R)d\tau\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\|u_{\alpha,N}\|^{4}_{H^{s^{*}}}}{R^{4}}d\tau

(3.34)

\displaystyle{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0pt\rho\hskip 2.0pt}\frac{1}{tR^{4}}\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{E}}\mathcal{G}(u_{\alpha,N}(\tau))d\tau{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0pt\rho\hskip 2.0pt}\frac{A_{0,N}}{2R^{4}}\,.

Since
B_{R}(\mathcal{H}^{s^{*}})\cap\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}
is compact, we obtain that for fixed
\alpha>0

the collection of measures
(\mu_{\alpha,N}^{t})_{t>0}
is tight in
\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}
, and therefore by Prokhorov theorem it is compact.
Let
\mu_{\alpha,N}
be the limiting point as
t_{n}\to\infty
and by Krylov-Bogoliubov argument we obtain that
\mu_{\alpha,N}
is an invariant measure of (
3.4
).

Next, we derive (
3.30
) and (

). For any

, Proposition
3.2
implies

(3.35)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}}\min\left\{\mathcal{G}(v),R\right\}\mu_{\alpha,N}(dv)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}}\min\left\{\mathcal{G}(v),R\right\}\mu_{\alpha,N}^{t_{n}}(dv)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}}\mathcal{G}(v)\mu_{\alpha,N}^{t_{n}}(dv)\leq\frac{A_{0,N}}{2}

and by the monotone convergence theorem for

, one has

(3.36)

\int_{\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}}\mathcal{G}(v)\mu_{\alpha,N}(dv)\leq\frac{A_{0,N}}{2}\,.

On the other hand, if the initial condition
u_{0}
is distributed as stationary measure
\mu_{\alpha,N}
, then
{\mathbb{E}}\|u_{0}\|^{2}<\infty
by (
3.36
). The
corresponding solution

is stationary and by Proposition
3.2

(3.37)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{E}_{N,{s^{*}}}}\mathcal{G}(v)\mu_{\alpha,N}(dv)={\mathbb{E}}\mathcal{G}(u_{\alpha,N})=\frac{1}{t}{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{\alpha,N}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\mathcal{G}(u_{\alpha,N})d\tau=\frac{A_{0,N}}{2}

as desired.

We show (
3.31
) only for any
m\geq 2
, for
m\in(0,2)
, (
3.31
) follows from interpolation with
m=0
.
Similarly as above, set
u_{0}\equiv 0
and for any
R>0
, Proposition
3.2
with

implies

Let

Lemma 3.4

For any

Proof.

Since the proof is rather standard, let us only highlight key steps.
If

) with initial condition distributed as

, lead to globally well-posed solutions that enjoy the slow growth bound (

). Other properties of this set of data will be proved in Section

.

Remark 5.10

(Persistence of regularity)
.

Since any finite-dimensional datum in
\Sigma_{s^{*}}^{\text{Euler}}
is of
C^{\infty}
-regularity, a crucial question is about the preservation by the flow of such regularity. Initially, the estimate established in (
5.26
) provides only a bound on the
H^{s}
-regularity, where

is the regularity order of the dissipation operator used in the construction of underlying measures. We rely here on an argument of Beale-Kato-Majda type to ensure persistence of regularity.

To proceed to it, we remark that a similar energy estimation procedure as in the proof of Proposition
2.1
shows the following control (see also
[
BealeKatoMajda1984
]
):

(5.42)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{m}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}}e^{C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}ds},

for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Using the fact that we consider here
s>\frac{5}{2}
, and thanks to estimate (
5.26
), we have that

(5.43)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s}}\leq\tilde{C}\xi(1+|t|).

We then obtain for any
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{3})
, for all integer
m
, that

(5.44)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{m}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{m}}e^{\tilde{C}\int_{0}^{t}\xi(1+|s|)ds},

which implies regularity for all times due to control on
\xi
.

We could improve the estimate (
5.44
) above by using, in (
5.43
), an interpolation between
L^{2}
and
H^{s}
with a use of
L^{2}
-conservation, resulting in a power gain in the integrand.

6.
Invariance of the measure

Recall that
\phi_{t}
is the flow of (
1.1
)
on the
\Sigma_{s^{*}}
as shown in Proposition
5.6
.

Theorem 6.1
.

The measure
\mu
is invariant under
\phi_{t}
, that is, for each
\Gamma\in\textrm{Bor}(\mathcal{H}^{s^{*}})
and each
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
one has
\mu(\Gamma)=\mu(\phi_{-t}\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Since
\mu
is a Borel probability defined on a Polish space, the Ulam’s theorem (see Theorem
7.1.4
in
[
17
]
) states that
\mu
is regular:
for any
S\in\text{Bor}(\mathcal{H}^{s^{*}})

\displaystyle\mu(S)=\sup\{\mu(K),\ K\subset S\ compact\}.

Therefore, it suffices to prove invariance for compact sets. Indeed for any
t
, using
\mu(\phi_{t}K)=\mu(K)
we obtain

(6.1)

\displaystyle\mu(\phi_{-t}S)

\displaystyle=\sup\{\mu(K),\ K\subset\phi_{-t}S\ compact\}=\sup\{\mu(\phi_{t}K),\ K\subset\phi_{-t}S\ compact\}

(6.2)

\displaystyle=\sup\{\mu(\phi_{t}K),\ \phi_{t}K\subset S,\ K\ compact\}\leq\sup\{\mu(C),\ C\subset S\ compact\}=\mu(S),

where we used that
\phi_{t}
is continuous, and therefore
\phi_{t}
maps compact sets into compact sets.
In addition, for any
t
we have

\displaystyle\mu(S)=\mu(\phi_{-t}\phi_{t}S)\leq\mu(\phi_{t}S).

Since
t
is arbitrary, we then obtain
\mu(S)=\mu(\phi_{t}S)
.

Next, we claim that it suffices to show the invariance only on a fixed interval
[-\tau,\tau],
where
\tau>0
can be as small as we want.
Indeed, if
\mu(K)=\mu(\phi_{t}K)
for any
t\in[-\tau,\tau]
with
\tau>0
, then for
t\in[\tau,2\tau]
, one has
\mu(\phi^{-t}K)=\mu(\phi^{-\tau}\phi^{t-\tau}K)=\mu(\phi^{t-\tau}K)=\mu(K)
,
where use used the invariance for
t-\tau\in[0,\tau]
. For larger
t
we can iterate and the same argument applies for
t<0
.

Our proof is then reduced to showing invariance for compact sets on a small time interval.
Recall that in Section
4
we defined the Markov group
\Phi^{N}_{t}
and its dual
\Phi^{N*}_{t}
corresponding to the flow
\phi^{N}_{t}
.
Analogously, for the flow
(\phi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

let us define Markov group
\Phi_{t}:L^{\infty}(L^{2};{\mathbb{R}})\to L^{\infty}(L^{2};{\mathbb{R}})
and its dual

\Phi_{t}^{*}:\mathfrak{p}(L^{2})\to\mathfrak{p}(L^{2})
as

(6.3)

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}f(v)=f(\phi_{t}v),\qquad\Phi_{t}^{*}\nu(\Gamma)=\nu(\phi_{-t}\Gamma)\qquad\textrm{ for }t\in{\mathbb{R}}\,.

The idea of the proof is indicated in the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{\Phi^{N*}_{t}\mu_{N}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(I)}
\scriptstyle{(III)}
\textstyle{\mu_{N}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(II)}
\textstyle{\Phi_{t}^{*}\mu\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(IV)}
\textstyle{\mu}

The equality
(I)
is the invariance of
\mu_{N}
under
\Phi_{N}^{t}
, and
(II)
is the weak convergence
\mu_{N}\to\mu^{a}
. Then
(IV)
is proved once
(III)
is verified.

As discussed above, it suffices to show (III) on compact sets and any small
t
. Fix
r\in(3,{s^{*}})
, a compact set
K
and a large
R
such that

K\subset B_{R}(\mathcal{H}^{s^{*}})\subset B_{cR}(\mathcal{H}^{r})
, where
c=c({s^{*}},r)
is a constant from Poincaré inequality.
Also, let
f:\mathcal{H}^{r}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a bounded, Lipschitz function supported on
B_{cR}(\mathcal{H}^{s^{*}})
. Note that for any
u,v\in B_{R}(\mathcal{H}^{s^{*}})

(6.4)

|f(u)-f(v)|\leq C_{f}\|u-v\|_{H^{r}}{\ \lesssim\ \ }_{\hskip-4.0ptr,{s^{*}}\hskip 2.0pt}\|u-v\|_{H^{s^{*}}}\,,

and therefore
the restriction of
f
to
H^{s^{*}}
is also Lipschitz, and in particular continuous.
Let
T
be the existence time given by Proposition
2.1
corresponding to the fixed
R
. Then, for
|t|<T
we have

\displaystyle(\Phi_{t}^{N*}\mu_{N},f)-(\Phi_{t}^{*}\mu,f)

\displaystyle=(\mu_{N},\Phi_{t}^{N}f)-(\mu,\Phi_{t}f)

\displaystyle=(\mu_{N},\Phi^{N}_{t}f-\Phi_{t}f)-(\mu-\mu_{N},\Phi_{t}f)

\displaystyle=A-B.

By the continuity of
\phi^{t}
,
\Phi_{t}f
is bounded and continuous on
\mathcal{H}^{s^{*}}
and by the weak convergence
\mu_{N}\to\mu
in
\mathcal{H}^{s^{*}}
(up to a subsequence,
see Proposition
5.1
), we have
B\to 0
as
N\to\infty
.

Finally, from the Lipschitz property of
f
on
H^{r}
and Lemma
2.3
follows

\displaystyle|A|\leq C_{f}\sup_{u\in B_{R}(\mathcal{H}^{s})}\|\phi^{N}_{t}(u)-\phi^{t}(u)\|_{H^{r}}\leq C_{f}\sup_{u\in B_{R}(\mathcal{H}^{s})}\|\phi_{N}^{t}(u)-\phi^{t}(u)\|_{H^{r}}\to 0,\ \ as\ N\to\infty.

We obtain the claim.
∎

In this section we restrict ourselves to the 3D Euler case, we prove that the measures

Theorem 7.1
.

For any

denotes the energy of the solutions of the Euler equations. Namely,

(7.1)

\displaystyle E_{*}\mu_{N}(\Gamma)={\mathbb{P}}(\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\in\Gamma)\leq h(\ell(\Gamma)),\quad\text{for all Borel set $\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}$},

where

satisfies

.
In particular, for any
N
,
\Sigma_{N}
is not included in a countable union of
(2N-1)
-spheres.

Proof.

We employ the local time method introduced by Shirikyan
[
54
]
. Notice that the original approach used two coercive conservation laws to obtain the needed bound. Here we show that, despite the lack of a second coercive conservation laws, we can achieve the same conclusion by making use of the finite-dimensional context.
Let us first notice, by invoking regularity of the Lebesgue measures, that is suffices to show (
7.1
) for
\mu_{\alpha,N}
with
h
independent of
\alpha
. For simplicity of the notations, in this proof we use the notational abuse

, where

is the stationary process constructed for the equation (
3.4
).

Let’s now establish a key property of absolute continuity around
0
, namely

(7.2)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(0\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\delta)\leq C(N)\delta,

where
C
depends only on
N
.

To prove it, we proceed by the relation (
7.3
) below, which is derived from the local time properties of the process
\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. The detailed proof follows
[
54
]
.

(7.3)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\Gamma}1_{(a,\infty)}(g(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}))\left(g^{\prime}(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\left(\frac{A_{0,N}}{2}-e^{\rho(\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}}})}\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}_{1}}}^{2}\right)+g^{\prime\prime}(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\sum_{|m|\leq N}a_{m}^{2}|\langle u,e_{m}\rangle|^{2}\right)da

\displaystyle+\sum_{|m|\leq N}a_{m}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left(1_{\Gamma}(g(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}))(g^{\prime}(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}))^{2}|\langle u,e_{m}\rangle|^{2}\right)=0.

In the this relation

\Gamma

{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then, for any such

(7.4)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\Gamma}1_{(a,\infty)}\left(g(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\right)\left(g^{\prime}(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\left(\frac{A_{0,N}}{2}-e^{\rho(\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}}})}\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}_{1}}}^{2}\right)+g^{\prime\prime}(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\sum_{|m|\leq N}a_{m}^{2}|\langle u,e_{m}\rangle|^{2}\right)da\leq 0.

Consider

with

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\beta}^{\gamma}1_{(a,\infty)}\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{A_{0,N}-2e^{\rho(\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}}})}\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}_{1}}}^{2}}{4\|u\|_{L^{2}}}-\frac{1}{4\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{3}}\sum_{|m|\leq N}a_{m}^{2}|\langle u,e_{m}\rangle|^{2}\right)da\leq 0.

Now,

(7.7)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\frac{2e^{\rho(\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}}})}\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}_{1}}}^{2}}{{\|u\|_{L^{2}}}}\leq C_{2}(N){\mathbb{E}}e^{\rho(\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}}})}(1+\|u\|_{H^{s^{*}_{1}}}^{2})\leq C_{3}(N).

Also, since
a_{m}\neq 0

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\beta}^{\gamma}1_{(a,\delta)}(\|u\|_{L^{2}})\|u\|_{L^{2}}da\leq C_{4}(N)(\gamma-\beta)

where

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{0}^{\gamma}{\mathbb{P}}(a\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\delta)\,da\leq C_{4}(N)\delta.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(0<\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\delta)\leq C_{4}(N)\delta

\displaystyle={\mathbb{P}}(\{\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\in\Gamma\}\cap\{\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\delta\})+{\mathbb{P}}(\{\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\in\Gamma\}\cap\{\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}>\delta\})

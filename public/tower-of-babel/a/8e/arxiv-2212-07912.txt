The Tor Spectral Sequence and Flat Morphisms in Homotopical 𝒟 {\cal D} -Geometry
Alisa Govzmann, Damjan Pištalo, Norbert Poncin

Abstract
Homotopical algebraic 𝒟 {\cal D} -geometry combines aspects of homotopical algebraic geometry [ 29 , 30 ] and 𝒟 {\cal D} -geometry [ 2 ] . It was introduced in [ 8 ] as a suitable framework for a coordinate-free study of the Batalin-Vilkovisky complex and more generally for the study of non-linear partial differential equations and their symmetries [ 6 , 23 ] . In order to consolidate the foundation of the theory, we have to prove that the standard methods of linear and commutative algebra are available in the context of homotopical algebraic 𝒟 {\cal D} -geometry, and we must show that in this context the étale topology is a kind of homotopical Grothendieck topology and that the notion of smooth morphism is, roughly speaking, local for the étale topology. The first half of this work was done in [ 8 ] . The remaining part covers the study of étale and flat morphisms in the category of differential graded 𝒟 {\cal D} -algebras and is based on the Tor \!\!\mathop{\rm~Tor}\nolimits spectral sequence which connects the graded Tor functors in homology with the homology of the derived tensor product of two differential graded 𝒟 {\cal D} -modules over a differential graded 𝒟 {\cal D} -algebra.

Introduction

Although deep insights into theoretical physics are often gained from calculations in coordinates, since the discovery of the general theory of relativity, geometers have been striving again for coordinate-free concepts and results. As many mathematical models reduce real-world problems to solving systems of partial differential equations (PDE-s), a coordinate-independent theory of PDE-s and related aspects is essential. In

Recall first that to any first quadrant double complex
(C_{\bullet\bullet},d^{h},d^{v})
in an abelian category, one associates its total complex
(\!\!\mathop{\rm~Tot}\nolimits(C)_{\bullet},d)\,,
whose grading is

The total complex admits two filtrations, the horizontal filtration
F^{h}_{p}(\!\!\mathop{\rm~Tot}\nolimits(C))=\oplus_{r\leq p}C_{r\bullet}
and the vertical filtration
F^{v}_{p}(\!\!\mathop{\rm~Tot}\nolimits(C))=\oplus_{s\leq p}C_{\bullet s}\,.
The spectral sequence of the resulting horizontally filtered chain complex
(\!\!\mathop{\rm~Tot}\nolimits(C)_{\bullet},d,F^{h}_{\bullet})
(resp., the vertically filtered chain complex
(\!\!\mathop{\rm~Tot}\nolimits(C)_{\bullet},d,F^{v}_{\bullet})

{}^{h}\!E^{\bullet}_{pq}
(resp.,
{}^{v}\!E^{\bullet}_{pq}
) of the double complex considered. The second sheet of the horizontal (resp., vertical) spectral sequence is

{}^{h}\!E^{2}_{pq}=H_{p}^{h}(H_{q}^{v}(C_{\bullet\bullet}))\hskip 9.24994pt(\text{resp.,}\;{}^{v}\!E^{2}_{pq}=H_{p}^{v}(H_{q}^{h}(C_{\bullet\bullet})))\;.

Moreover, this spectral sequence converges to the homology of the total complex:

{}^{h}\!E^{2}_{pq}=H_{p}^{h}(H_{q}^{v}(C_{\bullet\bullet}))\Rightarrow H_{p+q}(\!\!\mathop{\rm~Tot}\nolimits(C))\hskip 9.24994pt(\text{resp.,}\;{}^{v}\!E^{2}_{pq}=H_{p}^{v}(H_{q}^{h}(C_{\bullet\bullet}))\Rightarrow H_{p+q}(\!\!\mathop{\rm~Tot}\nolimits(C)))\;.

This means that for any

the horizonal spectral sequence

) stabilizes at some

where the RHS is the
p

whose filtration is induced by the filtration

of

[

6.1

Denote by

7.2
Postnikov towers

The proof that all flat modules are strognly flat uses the machinery of Postnikov towers, which is – for the category
{\tt Mod}({{\cal A}})
– recalled in this section.

For given
M\in{\tt Mod}({{\cal A}})
, set
M_{\leq n}
to be the complex

\ldots\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\!\!\mathop{\rm~Im}\nolimits(d_{n+1})\hookrightarrow M_{n}\xrightarrow{d_{n}}M_{n-1}\xrightarrow{d_{n-1}}\ldots\xrightarrow{d_{1}}M_{0}.

(54)

Degreewise surjection
\phi_{n}:M\to M_{\leq n}
is defined in degrees
\leq n
as identity, and in degree
n+1
as
d_{n+1}
. Degreewise surjections
p_{n}:M_{\leq n}\to M_{\leq n-1}
are defined alike.

M_{\leq n}
is an
{\cal A}
-module, with the
{\cal A}
-action defined as follows:

•

For
a\in A
and
m\in M_{k},k\leq n
, we set
a\cdot m
to be image of
a\cdot m\in M
under
\phi_{n}
.

•

For
dm\in\!\!\mathop{\rm~Im}\nolimits(d_{n+1})
,
a\cdot dm
is set to zero unless
a\in A_{0}
, and for
a\in A_{0}
we have
a\cdot dm=d(a\cdot m)\in\!\!\mathop{\rm~Im}\nolimits(d_{n+1})
.

Maps
p_{n}
and
\phi_{n}
are compatible in the sense that
p_{n}\circ\phi_{n}=\phi_{n-1}
, and are easily checked to be morphisms of
{\cal A}
-modules. In fact, being degreewise surjective, the maps are fibrations.

Definition 6
.

Postnikov tower of
M\in{\tt Mod}({{\cal A}})
is the inverse system

\ldots\to M_{\leq n}\xrightarrow{p_{n}}M_{\leq n-1}\xrightarrow{p_{n-1}}\ldots\xrightarrow{p_{2}}M_{\leq 1}\xrightarrow{p_{1}}M_{\leq 0},

together with the sequence of compatible maps
\phi_{n}:M\to M_{\leq n}
.

Indeed, so-defined Postnikov towers satisfy the defining properties of the more familiar topological concept:

Proposition 8
.

1.

H_{k}(\phi_{n}):H_{k}(M)\to H_{k}(M_{\leq n})
is an isomorphism for
k\leq n
;

2.

H_{k}(M_{\leq n})=0
for
k>n
;

3.

the homotopy fiber of the fibration
M_{\leq n}\to M_{\leq n-1}
is
H_{n}(M)
, viewed as a complex concentrated in degree
n
.

Proof.

The first two statements are obvious. For the third, it suffices to notice that the homotopy fiber in question coincides with the kernel of
p_{n}
(on the nose), as the map in question is a fibration, and all objects in
{\tt Mod}({{\cal A}})
are fibrant.
∎

As with CW complexes,
M
can be recovered as the homotopy limit of the inverse system
(M_{\leq n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Namely, given any model category
\tt M
, the functor category
\!\!\mathop{\rm~Fun}\nolimits(\mathbb{N}^{\!\!\mathop{\rm~op}\nolimits},\tt M)
has an injective model structure whose weak equivalences and cofibrations are objectwise
[
20
, Theorem 5.3.1.]
. Fibrant objects are sequences of fibrations between fibrant objects in
\tt M
. Especially, Postnikov tower
\ldots M_{\leq n}\twoheadrightarrow M_{\leq n-1}\twoheadrightarrow\ldots\twoheadrightarrow M_{\leq 0}
is a fibrant object in
\!\!\mathop{\rm~Fun}\nolimits(\mathbb{N}^{\!\!\mathop{\rm~op}\nolimits},{\tt Mod}({{\cal A}}))
, so that its homotopy limit coincides with its projective limit on the nose, which is clearly
M
.

7.3
Flat modules are strongly flat

Given a morphism
f:{\cal A}\to{\cal B}
in
\tt DG{\cal D}A
, any
{\cal B}
-module has an induced
{\cal A}
-module
structure defined as
a\cdot m:=f(a)\cdot m
. For
{\cal A}\in\tt DG{\cal D}A
, both
{\cal A}_{0}
, and
H_{0}{\cal A}
are commutative
{\cal D}
-algebras, and can be viewed as differential graded
{\cal D}
-algebras concentrated in degree zero. Due to
\tt DG{\cal D}A
morphisms
{\cal A}_{0}\hookrightarrow{\cal A}\twoheadrightarrow H_{0}{\cal A}
, any
H_{0}{\cal A}
-module is also an
{\cal A}
–module, and any
{\cal A}
–module is also an
{\cal A}_{0}
–module. These facts will be used repeatedly throughout this subsection. For
D\in\tt Mod({\cal A})
, we will denote by
\pi_{0}:D\to H_{0}(D)
the natural map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ldots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{0}(D)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{1}}

(55)

Suppose that
M
is a flat
{\cal A}
-module. As in the subsection
7.1
, we assume
M
to be cofibrant. Any exact sequence

0\to N\hookrightarrow P

of
H_{0}{\cal A}
-modules is a homotopy fiber sequence in
\tt Mod({\cal A})
, since any map between complexes concentrated in degree zero is a fibration. Since
M
is flat,

(56)

is a homotopy fiber sequence as well.
From the associated long exact sequence in homology, we conclude that

(57)

is an exact sequence of
H_{0}{\cal A}
-modules.

Lemma 1
.

For
N\in{\tt Mod}(H_{0}{\cal A})
,
M\in\tt Mod({\cal A})
, there exists a natural isomorphism

\phi:H_{0}(N\otimes_{{\cal A}}M)\to N\otimes_{H_{0}{\cal A}}H_{0}M,\hskip 10.0pt[n\otimes m_{0}]\mapsto n\otimes[m_{0}]

of
H_{0}{\cal A}
–modules.

From here and (
57
), it follows that
H_{0}M
is a flat
H_{0}{\cal A}
–module.

Proof.

For
{\cal A}\in\tt DG{\cal D}A
, and
P,Q\in\tt Mod({\cal A})
, the tensor product
P\otimes_{\cal A}Q
is the quotient of
P\otimes Q
by the graded submodule
{\cal I}_{A}
generated by the homogeneous elements
p\cdot a\otimes q-p\otimes a\cdot q
, together with the induced differential, and the
{\cal A}
-action induced by the left
{\cal A}
-action on
P
. For
P,Q\in\tt Mod({\cal B})
, the inclusion
{\cal I}_{A}\subseteq{\cal I}_{B}
of submodules in
P\otimes Q
induces the quotient map

P\otimes_{\cal A}Q\twoheadrightarrow P\otimes_{\cal B}Q
in
\tt Mod({\cal B})
.

For
m\in M
,
a\in{\cal A}
, and
n\in N
,

is a homogenious element of degree zero in
M\otimes N
if and only if both

and

are of degree zero. Thus, in degree zero, the projection

is isomorphism. As,
p
is degreewise surjective,
H_{0}p
is an isomorphism of
H_{0}{\cal A}
-modules. Due to right exactness of the tensor product, the universal map

\begin{split}H_{0}(N\otimes_{{\cal A}_{0}}M)=&\!\!\mathop{\rm~coker}\nolimits(N\otimes_{{\cal A}_{0}}M_{1}\xrightarrow{\!\!\mathop{\rm~id}\nolimits\otimes d}N\otimes_{{\cal A}_{0}}M_{0})\\
\to&N\otimes_{{\cal A}_{0}}\!\!\mathop{\rm~coker}\nolimits(M_{1}\xrightarrow{d}M_{0})=N\otimes_{{\cal A}_{0}}H_{0}M,\\
&[n\otimes m_{0}]\mapsto n\otimes[m_{0}]\end{split}

is an isomorphism.
Composing with inverse of
H_{0}p

H_{0}(N\otimes_{{\cal A}}M)\to N\otimes_{{\cal A}_{0}}H_{0}M,\hskip 10.0pt[n\otimes m_{0}]\mapsto n\otimes[m_{0}].

(58)

As the map
{\cal A}_{0}\twoheadrightarrow H_{0}({\cal A})
is surjective, the quotient map

N\otimes_{{\cal A}_{0}}H_{0}M\to N\otimes_{H_{0}{\cal A}}H_{0}M

is an isomorphism as well. Composing with (

), we get the desired result.

∎

We now show that that
M

{\cal A}
-module.

Proposition 9
.

Let
M,N\in\tt Mod({\cal A})
, with
M
flat and cofibrant. There exists a natural isomorphism of graded
H_{0}({\cal A})
-modules

\phi_{\bullet,N,M}:H_{\bullet}(N)\otimes_{H_{0}({\cal A})}H_{0}(M)\to H_{\bullet}(N\otimes_{\cal A}M),\hskip 10.0pt[n]\otimes[m_{0}]\mapsto[n\otimes m_{0}].

(59)

Applying to
N={\cal A}
, we find that
M
is a strong
{\cal A}
-module.

Proof.

Taking
N=0
, from the long exact sequence in homology associated to the fiber sequence
56
, we conclude that
H_{i}(P\otimes_{{\cal A}}M)=0
, for any
H_{0}{\cal A}
-module
P
and
i>0
. Thus, the map

\pi_{0}:P\otimes_{{\cal A}}M\to H_{0}(P\otimes_{{\cal A}}M)

(60)

is a weak equivalence. Since
(n)
–shifts preserve weak equivalences, for any
H_{0}(A)
-module
P
, and
n>0
, the map

P[n]\otimes_{{\cal A}}M=(P\otimes_{{\cal A}}M)[n]\xrightarrow{(\phi\circ\pi_{0})[n]}(P\otimes_{H_{0}{\cal A}}H_{0}M)[n]=P[n]\otimes_{H_{0}{\cal A}}H_{0}M

(61)

which is in degree
n
given by

p\otimes m_{0}\mapsto p\otimes[m_{0}]

is a weak equivalence as well.

Notice that for any
n\in\mathbb{N},
and
i<n

H_{i}(\phi_{n}\otimes\!\!\mathop{\rm~id}\nolimits_{M}):H_{i}(N\otimes_{{\cal A}}M)\to H_{i}(N_{\leq n}\otimes_{{\cal A}}M)

(62)

is an isomorphism. Indeed, in degrees
\leq n

\phi_{n}
is identity. The same goes for
\phi_{n}\otimes\!\!\mathop{\rm~id}\nolimits_{M}

n-1
.

Consequently, it suffices to prove that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the map
\phi_{\bullet,N_{\leq n},M}
, is an isomorphism. We proceed by induction.

For
n=0
, the projection
\pi_{0}:N_{\leq 0}\to H_{0}(N_{\leq 0})

is a weak equivlence. As
M
is a cofibrant
{\cal A}
-module,

preserves weak equivalences. Consequenlty, the composition

is a weak equivalence as well, and the induced isomorphism in homology is exactly the inverse of (
59

Suppose now that
\phi_{\bullet,N_{\leq n-1},M}

n

. As

is flat and cofibrant,

•

is isomorphism. Degree

is injective. The map is surjective as

H_{n}(\!\!\mathop{\rm~ker}\nolimits p_{n}\otimes M)

hence isomorphism.

For

,

, and

∎

.

For
M\in\tt Mod({\cal A})
, the following properties are equivalent:

1.

M
is flat,

2.

the derived tensor product
-\otimes_{\cal A}^{\mathbb{L}}M
preserves homotopy fiber sequences,

3.

M
is strongly flat.

Corollary 2
.

Given
M,N\in\tt Mod({\cal A})
, if
M

{\cal A}
-module, the above Tor spactral sequence collapses on the second page, yielding a natural isomorphism

(HM\otimes_{H{\cal A}}HN)_{q}\cong H_{q}(M\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\cal A}N).

Proof.

Since
M

7.1
.
∎

Abstract




Euler-type integrals for the generalized hypergeometric matrix function

Abstract:

In this paper, we investigate the Euler-type integral representations for the generalized hypergeometric matrix function and develop some transformations in terms of hypergeometric matrix functions. Furthermore, unit and half arguments have been provided for several particular cases.

Keywords:
Generalized hypergeometric matrix function, Matrix functional calculus, Gamma and Beta matrix function.

AMS Subject Classification (2010):
15A15, 33C05, 33C20, 44A99.

Throughout this work, we consider the complex space
\mathbf{C}^{R\times R}

R

P\in\mathbf{C}^{R\times R}

\sigma(P)
is the spectrum of
P

\displaystyle a(P)=\max\left\{\Re(z):z\in\sigma(P)\right\},\quad b(P)=\min\left\{\Re(z):z\in\sigma(P)\right\},

(1.1)

where
a(P)

P

b(P)=-a(-P)
. A Hermitian matrix
P

\mathbf{C}^{R\times R}

\Re(\lambda)>0

\lambda\in\sigma(P)
, where
\sigma(P)

P
or spectrum of
A
and its two-norm is given by

\displaystyle||P||=\sup_{x\neq 0}\frac{||Px||_{2}}{||x||_{2}}=\max\left\{\sqrt{\lambda}:\lambda\in\sigma(P^{*}P)\right\},

where for any vector
x\in\mathbf{C}^{r}

||x||_{2}=(x^{*}x)^{1/2}
is the Euclidean norm of
x

P^{*}

P
.
I

\mathbf{0}
stands for the identity matrix and null matrix in
\mathbf{C}^{R\times R}

P

[
8

\displaystyle\left\|e^{Pt}\right\|\leq e^{ta(P)}\sum_{u=0}^{r-1}\frac{\left(\left\|P\right\|r^{1/2}t\right)^{u}}{u!}\quad(t\geq 0),

which yields

\displaystyle\left\|t^{P}\right\|\leq\left\|e^{P\ln t}\right\|\leq t^{a(P)}\sum_{u=0}^{r-1}\frac{\left(\left\|P\right\|r^{1/2}\ln t\right)^{u}}{u!}\quad(t\geq 1).

\displaystyle f(P)g(P)=g(P)f(P).

PQ=QP

\displaystyle f(P)g(Q)=g(Q)f(P).

\displaystyle\Gamma^{-1}(P)=P(P+I)\dots(P+(n-1)I)\ \Gamma^{-1}(P+nI),\ \ n\geq 1.

2
Main results

Dwivedi and Sahai
[
6
]
introduced a natural generalization of hypergeometric matrix function called generalized hypergeometric matrix function and defined by

\displaystyle{}_{p}F_{q}\left(P_{1},\dots,P_{p};Q_{1},\dots,Q_{q};z\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(P_{1})_{m}\dots(P_{p})_{m}(Q_{1})_{m}^{-1}\dots(Q_{q})_{m}^{-1}}{m!}z^{m},

(2.1)

where
P_{i},Q_{j}\in\mathbf{C}^{R\times R},1\leq i\leq p,1\leq j\leq q
, such that
Q_{j}+mI,1\leq j\leq q
are invertible for all integers
m\geq 0
.

In the present work, we consider an Euler-type integral and present some integral representations of
{}_{3}F_{2}(\cdot)
matrix function using the suitable adjustment of matrix parameters.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
P,Q
and
R
be commuting matrices in
\mathbf{C}^{R\times R}
such that
Q,R
and
R-Q
are positive stable. Then, for
|z|<1
, we have the following integral representation:

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(P,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};z\right)=\frac{\Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q-I}(1-u)^{R-Q-I}(1-zu^{2})^{-P}du.

(2.2)

Proof.

From the left-hand side of (
2.2
), we find that

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(P,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};z\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(P\right)_{m}\left(\frac{Q}{2}\right)_{m}\left(\frac{Q+I}{2}\right)_{m}}{\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)_{m}\left(\frac{R+I}{2}\right)_{m}}\frac{z^{m}}{m!}.

(2.3)

Using the relation

\displaystyle\left(P\right)_{2m}=2^{2m}\left(\frac{P}{2}\right)_{m}\left(\frac{P+I}{2}\right)_{m},

equation (
2.3
) becomes

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(P,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};z\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(P\right)_{m}\left(Q\right)_{2m}}{\left(R\right)_{2m}}\frac{z^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q-I}(1-u)^{R-Q-I}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(P\right)_{m}(zu^{2})^{m}}{m!}\ du

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q-I}(1-u)^{R-Q-I}(1-zu^{2})^{-P}du.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Corollary 2.1
.

Under the conditions stated in Theorem
2.1
, the following integral relation holds true:

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(-kI,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};z\right)=\frac{\Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q-I}(1-u)^{R-Q-I}(1-zu^{2})^{kI}du.

(2.4)

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
P,Q
and
R
be commuting matrices in
\mathbf{C}^{R\times R}
such that
R,R-P,R-Q
and
R-Q-P
are positive stable. Then the following integral representation holds true:

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(P,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};1\right)=\frac{\Gamma(R)\Gamma(R-Q-P)}{\Gamma(R-P)\Gamma(R-Q)}\ {}_{2}F_{1}\left(P,Q;R-P;-1\right).

(2.5)

Proof.

Substituting
z=1
in (
2.2
), it becomes

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(P,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q-I}(1-u)^{R-Q-P-I}(1+u)^{-P}du

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{-P\choose m}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q+(m-1)I}(1-u)^{R-Q-P-I}\ du

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{-P\choose m}\frac{\Gamma(Q+mI)\Gamma(R-Q-P)}{\Gamma(R-P+mI)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(R)\Gamma(R-Q-P)}{\Gamma(R-P)\Gamma(R-Q)}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(P)_{m}(Q)_{m}}{(R-P)_{m}}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(R)\Gamma(R-Q-P)}{\Gamma(R-P)\Gamma(R-Q)}\ {}_{2}F_{1}\left(P,Q;R-P;-1\right),

which completes the proof of Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Under the conditions stated in Theorem
2.2
, the following integral relation holds true:

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(-nI,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};1\right)=\frac{\left(R-Q\right)_{n}}{\left(R\right)_{n}}\ {}_{2}F_{1}\left(-nI,Q;R+nI;-1\right).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
P,Q
and
R
be commuting matrices in
\mathbf{C}^{R\times R}
such that
Q,R
and
R-Q
are positive stable. Then the following integral representation holds true:

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(P,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)=2^{P}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{-P\choose m}\frac{(R-Q)_{m}}{(R)_{m}}\times{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-mI,Q;R+mI;-1\right).

(2.6)

Proof.

Substituting
z=\frac{1}{2}
in Theorem
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(P,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q-I}(1-u)^{R-Q-I}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\right)^{-P}du

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{2^{P}\ \Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q-I}(1-u)^{R-Q-I}\left(2-u^{2}\right)^{-P}du

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{2^{P}\ \Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{-P\choose m}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q-I}(1-u)^{R-Q+(m-1)I}\left(1+u\right)^{m}du

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{2^{P}\ \Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{m}{-P\choose m}{m\choose k}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q+(k-1)I}(1-u)^{R-Q+(m-1)I}du

\displaystyle\quad=2^{P}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{m}{-P\choose m}{m\choose k}\frac{(Q)_{k}(R-Q)_{m}}{(R)_{k+m}}.

Using the transformation
(R)_{k+m}=(R)_{m}(R+m)_{k}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(P,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=2^{P}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{-P\choose m}\frac{(R-Q)_{m}}{(R)_{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose k}\frac{(Q)_{k}}{(R+m)_{k}}

\displaystyle\quad=2^{P}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{-P\choose m}\frac{(R-Q)_{m}}{(R)_{m}}\times{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-mI,Q;R+mI;-1\right).

This completes the proof of Theorem
2.3
.
∎

Corollary 2.3

Under the conditions stated in Theorem
2.3

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(P,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)=2^{P}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{-P\choose m}\ {}_{3}F_{2}\left(-mI,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};1\right)

(2.7)

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
P,Q
and
R
be commuting matrices in
\mathbf{C}^{R\times R}
such that
Q,R
and
R-Q
are positive stable and

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(P,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};\frac{1}{w+1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\left(\frac{w+1}{w}\right)^{P}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{-P\choose m}w^{-m}{}_{3}F_{2}\left(-mI,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};1\right).

(2.8)

Proof.

Substituting
z=\frac{1}{w+1}
in Theorem
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(P,\frac{Q}{2},\frac{Q+I}{2};\frac{R}{2},\frac{R+I}{2};\frac{1}{w+1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q-I}(1-u)^{R-Q-I}\left(1-\frac{u^{2}}{w+1}\right)^{-P}du

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{(w+1)^{P}\ \Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q-I}(1-u)^{R-Q-I}\left(w+1-u^{2}\right)^{-P}du

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\frac{w+1}{w}\right)^{P}\frac{\Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q-I}(1-u)^{R-Q-I}\left(1+\frac{1-u^{2}}{w}\right)^{-P}du

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\frac{w+1}{w}\right)^{P}\frac{\Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q-I}(1-u)^{R-Q-I}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{-P\choose m}\left(\frac{1-u^{2}}{w}\right)^{m}du

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\frac{w+1}{w}\right)^{P}\frac{\Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{-P\choose m}w^{-m}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q-I}(1-u)^{R-Q+(m-1)I}\left(1+u\right)^{m}du

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\frac{w+1}{w}\right)^{P}\frac{\Gamma(R)}{\Gamma(Q)\Gamma(R-Q)}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{m}{-P\choose m}{m\choose k}w^{-m}\int_{0}^{1}u^{Q+(k-1)I}(1-u)^{R-Q+(m-1)I}du

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\frac{w+1}{w}\right)^{P}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{m}{-P\choose m}{m\choose k}w^{-m}\frac{(Q)_{k}(R-Q)_{m}}{(R)_{m}(R+m)_{k}}

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\frac{w+1}{w}\right)^{P}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{-P\choose m}w^{-m}\frac{(R-Q)_{m}}{(R)_{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{k}(-m)_{k}}{k!}\frac{(Q)_{k}}{(R+m)_{k}}

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\frac{w+1}{w}\right)^{P}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{-P\choose m}w^{-m}\frac{(R-Q)_{m}}{(R)_{m}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-mI,Q;R+mI;-1\right).

Using Corollary
2.2
, this yields the right hand side of Theorem
2.4
.
∎

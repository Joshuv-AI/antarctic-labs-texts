On the p p -ranks of class groups of certain Galois extensions
Ufuoma Asarhasa, Rusiru Gambheera, Debanjana Kundu, Enrique Nunez Lon-wo, Arshay Sheth

Abstract
. Let p p be an odd prime, let N N be a prime with N ≡ 1 ( mod p ) N\equiv 1\pmod{p} , and let ζ p \zeta_{p} be a primitive p p -th root of unity. We study the p p -rank of the class group of ℚ ⁡ ( ζ p , N 1 / p ) \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p},N^{1/p}) using Galois cohomological methods and obtain an exact formula for the p p -rank in terms of the dimensions of certain Selmer groups. Using our formula, we provide a numerical criterion to establish upper and lower bounds for the p p -rank, analogous to the numerical criteria provided by F. Calegari–M. Emerton and K. Schaefer–E. Stubley for the p p -ranks of the class group of ℚ ⁡ ( N 1 / p ) \mathbb{Q}(N^{1/p}) . In the case p = 3 p=3 , we use Redei matrices to provide a numerical criterion to exactly calculate the 3 3 -rank, and also study the distribution of the 3 3 -ranks as N N varies through primes which are 4 , 7 ( mod 9 ) 4,7\pmod{9} .

Introduction

Background and motivation

Let
p
be an odd prime.
The study of the
p
-torsion of the class groups of cyclotomic fields has a rich history, dating back to the work of E. Kummer in the middle of the nineteenth century.
He showed that
p
divides the class number of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
if and only if there exists an even positive integer
k
with
2\leq k\leq p-3
such that
p
divides the numerator of the
k
-th Bernoulli number
B_{k}
.
The primes that satisfy the above condition are called irregular, and primes which are not irregular are called regular.
The celebrated Herbrand–Ribet theorem, which we now proceed to briefly recall, provides a refinement to Kummer’s criterion.
Let
\Delta=\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})/\mathbb{Q})
and note that
\Delta
acts on the class group
A\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\Cl(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))
.
Let
C=A/A^{p}
; then
C
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space and is also equipped with an induced action of
\Delta
.
Let
\chi
denote the mod
p
cyclotomic character

\chi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Delta\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}

defined via
\sigma(\zeta_{p})=\zeta_{p}^{\chi(\sigma)}
. We consider the decomposition

C=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p-1}C(\chi^{i}),

where
C(\chi^{i})=\{c\in C\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\sigma\cdot c=\chi^{i}(\sigma)\cdot c\text{ for all }\sigma\in\Delta\}
.
The Herbrand–Ribet theorem (see
[
Rib76
, Theorem 1.1]
) states that if
k
is a positive even integer with
2\leq k\leq p-3
, then
C(\chi^{1-k})\neq 0
if and only if
p
divides the numerator of
B_{k}
.

The dimension of
C
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space is called the
p
-rank of
\Cl(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))
.
Thus, both Kummer’s criterion and the Herbrand–Ribet theorem can be regarded as assertions about the
p
-rank of
\Cl(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))
.
Let
N>1
be a positive integer; it is natural to consider the more general problem of investigating the
p
-ranks of the class groups of the number field
F=\mathbb{Q}(N^{1/p})
; the case
N=1
being the subject of the above results.
In this direction, progress has been made in the case that
N
is prime and
N\equiv 1\pmod{p}
; we restrict to this case in the discussion below and denote the
p
-rank of the class group of a number field
K
by
\rk_{p}(\Cl(K))
.
An argument via genus theory can be used to show that we always have
\rk_{p}(\Cl(F))\geq 1
.
In
[
CE05
]
, using techniques from the deformation theory of Galois representations, F. Calegari–M. Emerton proved that if
p\geq 5
and if
M=\prod_{k=1}^{\frac{N-1}{2}}k^{k}
is a
p
-th power in
\mathbb{F}_{N}^{\times}
, then
\rk_{p}(\Cl(F)))\geq 2
.
E. Lecouturier noticed that the converse of the Calegari–Emerton result fails when
p=7,N=337
; see
[
Lec18
]
.
Recently, P. Wake–C. Wang-Erikson
[
WWE20
]
gave a new proof of the theorem of Calegari–Emerton by showing that
M
being a
p
-th power in
\mathbb{F}_{N}^{\times}
is equivalent to the vanishing of a certain cup product in Galois cohomology.
Building on the techniques of Wake–Wang-Erikson, the
p
-rank distribution of the class group of
\mathbb{Q}(N^{1/p})
was subsequently studied in great detail by K. Schaefer–E. Stubley in
[
SS19
]
.
To explain their results, for odd
i
in the range
1\leq i\leq p-4
, let

M_{i}=\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\prod_{a=1}^{k-1}k^{a^{i}}.

The tuple
(p,-i)
is called a regular pair if the
\chi^{-i}
eigenspace of
\Cl(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))
is trivial.
Schaefer–Stubley prove an upper bound

\rk_{p}(\Cl(F))\leq\rk_{p}(\Cl(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})))+p-2-2\mu,

where
\mu
is the number of odd
i
in the range
1\leq i\leq p-4
such that
(p,-i)
is a regular pair and
M_{i}
is not a
p
-th power in
\mathbb{F}_{N}^{\times}
.
Using this upper bound, they show that if
p
is regular and
\rk_{p}(F)\geq 2
, then at least one of the
M_{i}
is a
p
-th power in
\mathbb{F}_{N}^{\times}
.
Since
M
is a
p
-th power in
\mathbb{F}_{N}^{\times}
if and only if
M_{1}
is, their result can be regarded as a partial converse to theorem of Calegari–Emerton.

Our results and proof techniques

Motivated by the above works, we investigate the
p
-ranks of the class group of the Galois closure of
\mathbb{Q}(N^{1/p})
.
Specifically, we study the
p
-rank of the class group of the number field
L=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p},N^{1/p})
where
p
is an odd prime and
N
is a prime with
N\equiv 1\pmod{p}
.
For a regular prime
p
, we prove the following inequalities
in Theorems
3.2
and
3.6
,

(1)

\frac{p-1}{2}\leq\rk_{p}(\Cl(L))\leq(p-1)(p-2).

The lower bound is independent of the regularity hypothesis.
Our theorem(s) can handle the upper bound even when
p
is irregular, but the bounds are weaker.

A priori, it is not clear whether all the values in the above range can actually be attained.
Even if they are attained, do they occur infinitely often?
What is the
p
-rank distribution?

In this paper, we make a modest attempt at addressing these questions using two different approaches, namely, class field theory (and Redei matrices) and Galois cohomology.
First, we focus on the results and predictions obtained via class field theory.
In the second half, we shift our focus towards cohomological methods to refine (
1
).

Prediction

In the simplest case that
p=3
, results in
[
SS19
, Section 3]
assert that the
3
-rank of the class group of
F=\mathbb{Q}(N^{1/3})
is
always
1.
By (
1
), the
3
-rank of
\Cl(L)
is either 1 or 2.
Computer experiments done via SAGE suggested the following
3
-rank distribution of
\Cl(L)
when
N
is varied over all primes of the form
1\pmod{3}
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\rk_{3}(\Cl(L))=1\right)=\frac{2}{3}\textrm{ and }\mathbb{P}\left(\rk_{3}(\Cl(L))=2\right)=\frac{1}{3}.

Here, we write
\mathbb{P}\left(\rk_{3}(\Cl(L))=r\right)
to mean

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{N\leq x\ \mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\ N\equiv 1\pmod{3}\textrm{ is a prime and }\rk_{3}(\Cl(L))=r\}}{\#\{N\leq x\ \mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\ N\equiv 1\pmod{3}\textrm{ is a prime}\}}.

Moreover, the distribution remains the same when restricted to the cases
N\equiv 1\pmod{9}
and
N\equiv 4,7\pmod{9}
.

Results obtained via class field theory and Redei matrices

Progress towards the prediction

We provide a characterization in terms of divisibility conditions to ascertain when the
3
-rank of
\Cl(L)
is 1 or 2.
When
N\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, it is possible to write

4N=A^{2}+27B^{2}

for some integers
A,B
(unique up to sign).
We then prove the following result.

Theorem A

(Theorem
3.11
)
.

With notation as above

(i)

if
N\not\equiv 1\pmod{9}
, then
\rk_{3}\left(\Cl(L)\right)=2
if and only if
3\mid B
.

(ii)

if
N\equiv 1\pmod{9}
, then
\rk_{3}\left(\Cl(L)\right)=2
if and only if
A
is a 9th power modulo
N
.

In Section
3.3.2
, using Theorem
A
and tools from class field theory, we prove the following result.

Theorem B
.

Set
L=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3},N^{1/3})
.
As
N
varies over all primes of the form
4,7\pmod{9}
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\rk_{3}(\Cl(L))=1\right)=\frac{2}{3}\textrm{ and }\mathbb{P}\left(\rk_{3}(\Cl(L))=2\right)=\frac{1}{3}.

On the other hand, when
N
varies over primes of the form
1\pmod{9}
, the analysis is more difficult.
In Section
3.3.3
we provide heuristic reasons for the above proportions to hold, but are unable to provide a rigorous proof.
The key difficulty lies in the fact that when
N\equiv 1\pmod{9}
the ambiguous classes (see Definition
3.17
) are not always strictly ambiguous.
This can be reinterpreted in term of whether
\zeta_{3}
is a unit norm or not, but calculating statistics for this description was not possible.
Contrary to our initial expectation, when
N\equiv 1\pmod{9}
it is more frequent that the ambiguous classes are
not
strongly ambiguous.
When the coincidence occurs, it follows that
\rk_{3}(\Cl(L))
is always 2; see Lemma
3.18
.
But when there is non-coincidence of the two classes, both possibilities arise.
One of our main tools is the machinery developed by F. Gerth to calculate the
3
-rank of
\Cl(L)
using the rank of certain (Redei) matrices whose entries are determined by cubic Hilbert symbols.
However, when there is non-coincidence of the two classes calculating some of the entries of the matrix is rather abstract and can not be made precise.

Results obtained via Galois cohomology

The initial goal of the project was to completely determine the
p
-rank distribution of the class group of
L
.
This is a rather difficult problem; building on the work of Schaefer–Stubley
[
SS19
]
we prove results in this direction which is explained below.

Some abstract but precise results

The first main result is Theorem
4.10
.
The exact statement is technical and would require us to introduce a lot of notation, which we avoid here.
In words, the theorem provides a concrete description of how many unramified Galois extensions with a specified Galois group the number field
L
can have.
For a precise count of such extensions see Corollary
4.12
.
This count depends on the dimension of certain well-defined Selmer groups (Galois cohomology groups) with coefficients in abstract Galois modules.

Next, we prove an exact formula for the
p
-rank of the class group of
L
as sums of these cohomology dimensions.
This is done by studying a more intricate problem which is the structure of
\Cl(L)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
as a
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})
-module.
First, break
\Cl(L)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
into its constituent pieces (as
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})
-modules).
More precisely, denote the
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
-representation
\Sym^{i}(V)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}(j)
by
A^{i,j}
where
V\simeq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
with a prescribed Galois-action and write

\Cl(L)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}\cong\bigoplus_{i,j}(A^{i,j})^{m_{ij}}

We relate the number of the constituent pieces (with multiplicity) to the dimensions of the Selmer groups above.
In fact, we can write down a precise count for the multiplicities as well, namely

Theorem C

(Corollary
4.24
)
.

Let
r_{ij}
be defined in terms of the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dimensions of
\Lambda
-Selmer groups
H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},A^{i,j})
; see Corollary
4.12
for precise definitions.
For all
i,j
,

m_{ij}=r_{ij}+r_{i,j+1}-r_{i-1,j+1}-r_{i+1,j}.

This gives a precise description of the Galois module structure of
\Cl(L)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
From this precise Galois module structure, we conclude the
p
-rank of
L
as desired.

Theorem D

(Theorem
4.15
)
.

With notation as above,

\rk_{p}(\Cl(L))=\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}r_{p-1,j}.

When
p
is a regular prime, we obtain a slight refinement of the above equality; see the second assertion of Theorem
4.15
.
As a consequence of the above results, we deduce a relation between the
\rk_{p}(\Cl(F))
and
\rk_{p}(\Cl(L))
when
p
is regular.

Corollary

(Corollary
4.33
)
.

Let
p
be a regular prime

Explicit upper and lower bounds

The results discussed above are exact formulae, but the terms appearing in them are hard to compute since the Galois cohomology groups in question have coefficients in abstract Galois modules.
In Sections
4.3
and
4.4
, we replace the abstract Selmer groups appearing in the formulae of
\rk_{p}(\Cl(L))
(proved in Theorem
4.15
) with more explicit ones, i.e., Galois cohomology groups with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
(or some twist); see also Remark
4.36
(i). The advantage of working with these groups is that they are easier to compute; however,
this replacement also comes at a cost: we no longer have precise formulae as before, instead we can only give lower and upper bounds. However, these bounds are significantly sharper than the bounds in Equation (
1
) obtained via classical class field theoretic methods; see Remark
4.36
(ii). The following is a quintessential example of the kind of results proven in Sections
4.3
and
4.4
.

Theorem E

(Theorems
4.27
and
4.35
)
.

Let
p
be a regular prime.
Then

\rk_{p}(\Cl(L))\leq\frac{3p-5}{2}+(p-2)\sum_{i=2}^{p-2}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(H^{1}_{\Sigma}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(i))\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=2\\
\textrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{p-3}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(i))\right).

Here
H^{1}_{*}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(i))
where
*\in\{\Sigma,\Lambda\}
are certain Selmer conditions.
On the other hand,

\rk_{p}\left(\Cl(L)\right)\geq\frac{p-1}{2}+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(H^{1}_{\Sigma}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(-1))\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=2\\
i\text{ even}\end{subarray}}^{p-3}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(i))\right).

As mentioned above, the Selmer groups appearing in Theorem
E
are easier to compute.
In Theorem
4.38
we provide a numerical criterion to determine when
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(i))\right)=1
.
This allows us to deduce a simple numerical criterion on upper and lower bounds for
\rk_{p}(\Cl(L))
.

Corollary

(Corollary
4.39
)
.

Let
p
be a regular prime and
i
vary over even integers in the range
\{1,\ldots,p-2\}
.
Let
f
be any element of order
p
in
\mathbb{F}_{N}^{\times}
.
For an integer
0<k<p-1
, define

\mathcal{M}_{k}=(1-f)(1-f^{2})^{2^{k}}\ldots(1-f^{p-1})^{(p-1)^{k}}.

Then

\frac{p-1}{2}+\alpha\leq\rk_{p}\left(\Cl(L)\right)\leq(p-1)(p-2)-(p-1)\left(\frac{p-1}{2}-1-\alpha\right),

where
\alpha
is the number of
i\pmod{p-1}
which are positive, even, and such that
\mathcal{M}_{p-1-i}
is a
p
-th power in
\mathbb{F}_{N}^{\times}
.

Since our corollary states that the size of
\rk_{p}(\Cl(L))
depends on whether certain explicitly defined numbers are
p
-th powers in
\mathbb{F}_{N}^{\times}
, it can be regarded as an analogue of the results of Calegari–Emerton and Schaefer–Stubley mentioned at the start of the article.

The results obtained by using tools from Galois cohomology are significantly stronger than those obtained via classical class field theoretic methods because the techniques in the latter case only consider the action of the Galois group
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))
on the class group of
L
, whereas techniques in the former case in addition also involve the Galois action by
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})
on
\Cl(L)
.

Future Directions

An obvious problem to tackle in the future is to focus on the case
p=3
and prove the heuristics when
N\equiv 1\pmod{9}
.
For this, we believe it would be required to develop new techniques which can detect how often ambiguous classes do not coincide with the strong ambiguous classes
and
also qualitatively understand what determines growth in the
p
-rank of class group in going from a number field
F=\mathbb{Q}(N^{1/p})
to its Galois closure
L
.
Equivalently, it would be interesting to determine what ‘causes’ the
3
-part of the class number to be at least 9.

We were able to do limited computations in the case
p=5
.
By results in
[
SS19
]
, it is known that
\rk_{5}(\Cl(F))
can take values 1,2, or 3.
Furthermore, it was predicted in
[
SS19
]
that

\mathbb{P}(\rk_{5}(\Cl(F))=1)=\left(1-\frac{1}{5}\right)=\frac{4}{5}.

Our data suggests that the following prediction might be reasonable

\mathbb{P}(\rk_{5}(\Cl(L))=2)=\frac{2}{3}\left(1-\frac{1}{5}\right).

When
\rk_{5}(\Cl(F))=1,2
, our data suggests that

\rk_{5}(\Cl(L))\geq 2\rk_{5}(\Cl(F)).

On the other hand, when
\rk_{5}(\Cl(F))=3
, the dataset is small and we observe that
\rk_{5}(\Cl(L))\geq 8
.
Theoretically the best result we have proven in this direction is Corollary
4.32
but we do not know whether the theoretical bounds are in fact attainable.
In the same vein, our data for
p=5
suggests that
\rk_{5}(\Cl(L))
is at most 10 (even though a priori the 5-rank may be 11 or 12).
Can our methods be refined to obtain even sharper upper bounds?
Further calculations for
p\geq 7
will assist in making a general prediction for the distribution of
p
-ranks of the class group of
L
and also determine whether our (theoretical) results are sharp.

Finally, it would be interesting to study the quantity
\alpha
and the numbers
\mathcal{M}_{k}
in greater detail.
It was pointed out to us by experts that it might be reasonable to expect that these quantities are related to zeta values.
In the future, it would be interesting to study the distribution of
\log(\mathcal{M}_{k})
.

Organization

Including the introduction, this article has
four
sections.

We record useful results and provide proofs of basic facts that will be required throughout the paper.
Section

In the case that

This section is devoted to study the

Definition 3.1

For any (finite) abelian group

We use elementary methods to show (naïve) upper and lower bounds on the

When

In the special case of

Our data

3.1.
Upper and lower bounds for

Set

By genus theory, it is possible to show that the degree-

Since the

Our first order of business is to find an

The first result is to provide a lower bound for the

Theorem 3.2
.

With notation as above,
\rk_{p}\left(\Cl(L)\right)\geq\frac{p-1}{2}

Proof.

Let

[

asserts that

\rk_{p}\left(\Cl(L)\right)\geq t+r_{1}^{c}-1-\rk_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}/\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\cap\Norm_{L/K}(I_{L}))

Note that

Case 1: when

Lemmas

By Dirichlet unit theorem
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\cong\langle\zeta_{2p}\rangle\times\mathbb{Z}^{r}

Also
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times p}\subseteq\Norm_{L/K}(I_{L})

Together with Theorem

\langle\zeta_{p},\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times p}\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\cap\Norm_{L/K}(I_{L}).

Via the isomorphism coming from the Dirichlet unit theorem, we have
\langle\zeta_{p},\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times p}\rangle\cong\langle\zeta_{2p}\rangle\times(p\mathbb{Z})^{r}

Moreover,

(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{r}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}/\langle\zeta_{p},\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times p}\rangle\twoheadrightarrow{}\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}/\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\cap\Norm_{L/K}(I_{L}).

Therefore,
\rk_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}/\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\cap\Norm_{L/K}(I_{L}))\leq r

Hence, (

\rk_{p}\left(\Cl(L)\right)\geq(p-1)+0-1-\left(\frac{p-1}{2}-1\right)=\frac{p-1}{2}.

Case 2: when

Applying Lemmas

It is trivially true that

Therefore, (

\rk_{p}\left(\Cl(L)\right)\geq p+0-1-\frac{p-1}{2}=\frac{p-1}{2}.\qed

We now work towards proving an upper bound of the

\Cl(L)

Lemma 3.3

As before, write

\rk_{p}(\Cl(L)^{G})\leq\rk_{p}(\Cl(K))+\frac{3}{2}(p-1).

Proof.

Set

to denote the quantity
\rk_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\cap\Norm_{L/K}(L^{\times})/\Norm_{L/K}(\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}))
and write

to denote the number of finite places that ramify in

Claim:
With notation as above,

Justification:

We prove the claim case-by case.

Case 1: When

.

In this case,
t=(p-1)+1=p
.
Recall from Theorem
2.4
that
\zeta_{p}\not\in\Norm_{L/K}(L^{\times})

\rk_{p}(\Cl(L)^{G})\leq\rk_{p}(\Cl(K))+t+s\leq\rk_{p}(\Cl(K))+\frac{3}{2}(p-1).\qed

3.2.

When

A prime

Lemma 3.5

If

\rk_{p}(\Cl(L)^{G})\leq p-2.

We can now state and prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.6
.

With notation as above,

\rk_{p}\left(\Cl(L)\right)\leq p\rk_{p}(\Cl(K))+\frac{3}{2}(p-1)^{2}.

\rk_{p}\left(\Cl(L)\right)\leq(p-1)(p-2).

It follows from

\rk_{p}\left(\Cl(L)\right)\leq\rk_{p}(\Cl(K))+(p-1)\left(\rk_{p}(\Cl(K))+\frac{3}{2}(p-1)\right)=p\rk_{p}(\Cl(K))+\frac{3}{2}(p-1)^{2}.

Basic Notation.

Let

The absolute Galois group
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
is denoted by

.

The main goal of this section is to provide sharper upper and lower bounds for the

-rank of

.
The first main result towards attaining the goal is proving Theorem

where we characterize unramified extensions of
L
with specified Galois group structure and then using combinatorics, provide an explicit count of such extensions in Corollary
4.12
.
What the corollary says is that the number of extensions can be written explicitly in terms of the dimension of certain Galois cohomology groups.
Next, we prove an abstract formula for the

-rank of

in Theorem

involving the sum of dimensions of the Galois cohomology groups.
These individual terms are difficult to compute explicitly.
However, building on
[

This comes at the cost that we are no longer able to prove an exact formula, but instead have inequalities; see Theorems

4.1.
Unramified Extensions of

In this section we provide an explicit count of how many unramified Galois extensions with specified Galois groups the number field

can have.

Consider the mod

\chi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}G_{\mathbb{Q},S}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}

and let

denote the map

b\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}G_{\mathbb{Q},S}\longrightarrow\langle\zeta_{p}\rangle\textrm{

We often consider

and view the image of

in this additive group.
Note that

and

Observe that

Set
\mathbb{F}_{p}(i)
to denote the module
\mathbb{F}_{p}

Next define

to be the vector space on which
G_{\mathbb{Q},S}
acts via the representation

(4)

\begin{split}G_{\mathbb{Q},S}&\longrightarrow\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\\
\sigma&\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}\chi(\sigma)&b(\sigma)\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

For ease of notation, henceforth denote the
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
-representation
\Sym^{i}(V)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}(j)
by

.
Pick a basis for

such that the
G_{\mathbb{Q},S}
-representation

\rho_{ij}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}G_{\mathbb{Q},S}\longrightarrow\GL_{i+1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

is given by the matrix

[\rho_{ij}]\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\begin{pmatrix}\chi^{i+j}&\chi^{i+j-1}b&\chi^{i+j-2}\frac{b^{2}}{2}&\ldots&\chi^{j}\frac{b^{i}}{i!}\\
&\chi^{i+j-1}&\chi^{i+j-2}b&\ldots&\vdots\\
&&\chi^{i+j-2}&\ldots&\vdots\\
&&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
&&&&\chi^{j}\end{pmatrix}

Observe that
\rho_{ij}
is trivial on

and so there is an induced action of
\mathcal{G}
on

.
With this setup, there is an injection of

Thus, we obtain a filtration

(5)

A^{0,j+i}\subseteq A^{1,j+i-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A^{i-1,j+1}\subseteq A^{i,j}\subseteq A^{i+1,j-1}\cdots

In the following proposition we describe the centralizer of the set of matrices
[\rho_{ij}](g)
for all

, which is henceforth denoted by

.
As is be clarified in the proof below, the centralizer has a different description based on whether

or

.

Proposition 4.1
.

With notation as introduced above and writing
\Id
to denote the identity matrix,

C([\rho_{ij}],G_{\mathbb{Q}})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\Id_{i+1}\textrm{ when }i<p-1\\
\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&0&\ldots&c\\
0&\lambda&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\ldots&\lambda\end{pmatrix}\textrm{ such that }\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times},c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\right\}\textrm{ when }i=p-1.\end{cases}

Notation:
The matrix in the case
i=p-1
will be denoted by
\mathcal{M}(\lambda,c)
for ease of notation.

Proof.

Case 1: When
i<p-1
.

Let
h
be an element in
G_{F}
where
F=\mathbb{Q}(N^{1/p})
.
Then
b(h)=0
. This implies that
\rho(h)
is a diagonal matrix with distinct entries.
The only matrices that commute with such matrices are diagonal matrices.
Therefore,

C([\rho_{ij}],G_{F})\subseteq\{\textrm{Diag}(\lambda_{1},\;\ldots,\;\lambda_{i+1})\ \mid\ \lambda_{t}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\textrm{ for all }t\}.

On the other hand if
g\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus G_{F}
, then
\rho_{ij}(g)
is an upper triangular matrix with non-zero entries above the diagonal.
The only non-zero diagonal matrices that commute with such a
\rho_{ij}(g)
are (non-zero) scalar multiples of
\Id_{i+1}
.
This completes the proof when
i<p-1
.

Case 2: When
i=p-1
.

When
i=p-1
then notice that for
h\in G_{F}
, the matrix
\rho_{ij}(h)
is a diagonal matrix but not all entries are distinct.
Indeed, the (1,1)-entry is
\chi^{p-1+j}(h)=\chi^{j}(h)
and the (
i+1
,
i+1
)-entry is
\chi^{j}(h)
.
In this case the set of matrices that commute with
\rho_{ij}(g)
are given by

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}&0&\ldots&x\\
0&\lambda_{2}&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
y&0&\ldots&\lambda_{p}\end{pmatrix}\textrm{ where }\lambda_{t}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times},\ x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\right\}=\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}M((\lambda_{t}),x,y).

If

, and if we further require that

(6)

\rho(g)\cdot M((\lambda_{t}),x,y)=M((\lambda_{t}),x,y)\cdot\rho(g)

then upon comparing the (1,1)-entry, we note that

\chi^{j}(g)\lambda_{1}+\frac{b^{p-1}(g)}{(p-1)!}\chi^{j}(g)y=\chi^{j}(g)\lambda_{1}.

This implies

.
Comparing the upper and lower

-blocks of both sides of (

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\ldots=\lambda_{p}=\lambda\textrm{ (say)}.

The claim of the proposition now follows immediately.
∎

Remark 4.2

Proposition 4.3
.

In view of (

Base case:

Remark 4.11
.

(a)

When

given by the map

g\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}\chi^{j}&a(g)\\

\end{pmatrix}.

Consider the 1-cocycle

given by
g\mapsto a(g)
.
This produces a class
[a_{\uline{A}}]\in H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},A^{0,j})
and the rest of the proof goes through verbatim.

(b)

When

and

by
H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},A^{i,1})/\langle\uline{\boldsymbol{b}}^{(i)}\rangle

\overline{\Res}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},A^{i,1})/\langle\uline{\boldsymbol{b}}^{(i)}\rangle\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{L},A^{i,1})

and the rest of the proof goes through verbatim.

In the proof of Theorem
4.10
we constructed a multi-valued map
M_{\uline{A}}\mapsto[a_{\uline{A}}]
where

is an element in

.
When

This is why the map can be converted into a function by taking the projective spaces.

Now suppose that

.
For a random choice of basis

A different basis

.

For

, define

Some refined results when
p
is a regular prime

The results we have proven so far do not require the assumption that
p
is regular.
In the special case when
p
is a regular prime, one can prove some stronger results.
The main result we prove next is the following.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
p
be a regular prime.
With notation introduced before, there are no unramified
A^{p-1,j}
extensions of
L
.

To prove this theorem we first prove a technical result which is interesting in its own right.
We emphasize that the following result
does not
require the hypothesis that
p
is a regular prime.

Theorem 4.14
.

The cohomology group
H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(1))
is 1 -dimensional and generated by
b
.

Proof.

[
SS19
, Theorem 2.3.3(2)]
asserts that
H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q},S},\mathbb{F}_{p}(1))
is 2-dimensional and is generated by
b
and the cocycle

\displaystyle c\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}G_{\mathbb{Q}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}(1)

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{\sigma(p^{1/p})}{p^{1/p}}.

Note that
b
satisfies the Selmer condition
\Lambda
.
We now show that the cocycle
c
does not satisfy the Selmer condition by proving that
c
violates the Selmer condition at
p
.
Consider the map

\Res\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(1))\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{L},\mathbb{F}_{p}(1))=\Hom(G_{L},\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Note that
\Res(c)
determines the extension
L(p^{1/p})/L
, so it is enough to show that this extension is ramified at
p
.
Working locally at
p
, we have the following field diagram
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})
L_{p}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p},N^{1/p})
L^{\prime}_{p}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p},N^{1/p},p^{1/p})
\widetilde{L}_{p}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p},p^{1/p})

Case 1: when
N\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}
.

Lemma
2.2
implies that
L_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})
is unramified.
But
\widetilde{L}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})
is a ramified extension which forces that
L^{\prime}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})
is ramified.
In turn,
L^{\prime}_{p}/L_{p}
is ramified as desired.

Case 2: when
N\not\equiv 1\pmod{p^{2}}
.

Set
N=1+kp
such that
p\nmid k
.
Since ramification indices are multiplicative, it suffices to show that
L^{\prime}_{p}/\widetilde{L}_{p}
is a ramified extension.
If the extension is
not
ramified, it is proven in
[
Gra03
, Chapter I, Theorem 6.3(ii)]
(i.e., a general version of Theorem
2.1
) that there exists
y\in U^{(1)}_{\widetilde{L}_{p}}
such that

\mathfrak{p}^{p^{2}}\mid(N-y^{p})
.
Write
y=1+\mathfrak{p}\theta
and observe that

\displaystyle N-y^{p}

\displaystyle=(1+kp)-(1+\mathfrak{p}\theta)^{p}

\displaystyle=kp-p\mathfrak{p}\theta-{p\choose{2}}\mathfrak{p}^{2}\theta^{2}-\ldots.

Since
(p)=\mathfrak{p}^{p(p-1)}
,

\val_{\mathfrak{p}}(N-y^{p})=p(p-1)<p^{2}.

But this is a contradiction.
Hence,
L^{\prime}_{p}/\widetilde{L}_{p}
is a ramified extension.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

In the following discussion, we impose the condition that
p
is regular.

Proof of Theorem
4.13
.

Suppose there exists such an unramified extension.
By our earlier discussion (see, for example proof of Theorem

) we have a representation

defined in exactly the same way. By looking at the lower

corner of the above matrix, we get a non-zero class

.

Case 1: when

.

We arrive at a contradiction by using
[
SS19
, proof of Lemma 3.1.10]
.

Case 2: when
j\neq 0,1
.

In this situation,
a_{p-1}
cuts out an
\mathbb{F}_{p}(j)
-extension
E
of
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
.
The following field diagram will simplify the proof
K
L=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p},N^{1/p})
E(N^{1/p})
E
\mathbb{F}_{p}(1)
\mathbb{F}_{p}(j)

Note that
E(N^{1/p})
is inside the initial

-extension; hence,

is unramified at

.
It follows from
[

, Lemma 3.1.4]
that

.
Moreover,

Since

Case 3: when

This means

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

4.2.
An explicit description of the
p
-rank of
\Cl(L)

The main theorem that we prove in this section is the following:

Theorem 4.15
.

With notation introduced in Corollary
4.12
,

\rk_{p}(\Cl(L))=\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}r_{p-1,j}.

Furthermore, when
p
is a regular prime,

\rk_{p}(\Cl(L))=\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}r_{p-2,j}.

To prove this result we first write

(11)

\Cl(L)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}\cong\bigoplus_{i,j}(A^{i,j})^{m_{ij}}\textrm{ as a }\mathcal{G}\textrm{-module}.

Observe that, by
[
SS19
, Theorem 3.1.6]
any
\mathcal{G}
-module can be written in this way. The key idea of this proof is to compute the number of
A^{i_{0},j_{0}}
quotients of
\Cl(L)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
in terms of
\{m_{ij}\}
.
Then we relate the count to Theorem
4.12
to get more information about
\{m_{ij}\}
.
The precise relationship between the multiplicities
m_{ij}
and the ranks
r_{ij}
is clarified in Corollary
4.24
.

For a
\mathcal{G}
-module
X
, write
X^{\vee}=\Hom(X,\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

Lemma 4.16
.

For
i\leq p-1
,

\left(\Sym^{i}(V)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}(j)\right)^{\vee}\cong\Sym^{i}(V)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}(-i-j).

Proof.

This lemma generalizes
[
SS19
, Lemma 2.2.1]
.
Recall that
A^{i,j}
are indecomposable
\mathcal{G}
-representations; see
[
SS19
, Theorem 3.1.6]
.
The dual of an indecomposable representation is also indecomposable, so
(A^{i,j})^{\vee}=A^{s,t}
for some
s,t
by
[
SS19
, Theorem 3.1.6]
.
Considering dimensions, note that
i=s
.
Consider the perfect pairing

A^{i,j}\times(A^{i,j})^{\vee}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}.

Now,
\mathbb{F}_{p}(i+j)\cong A^{0,i+j}\subseteq A^{i,j}
and its annihilator in
A^{i,t}
must be
i
-dimensional.
We proved earlier that the unique
i
-dimensional subrepresentation of
A^{i,t}
is
A^{i-1,t+1}
.
We have the perfect pairing

\mathbb{F}_{p}(i+j)\times\frac{A^{i,t}}{A^{i-1,t+1}}\cong A^{0,t}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}.

Recall that
\frac{A^{i,t}}{A^{i-1,t+1}}\cong A^{0,t}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)
.
The perfect pairing is possible only when
t=-(i+j)
.
∎

In view of the above lemma, the number of
A^{i_{0},j_{0}}
quotients of
\Cl(L)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
is the same as the number of
A^{i_{0},-i_{0}-j_{0}}
-subrepresentations of
(\Cl(L)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\vee}
.
So, let
X\cong\bigoplus_{i,j}(A^{i,j})^{n_{ij}}
be a
\mathcal{G}
-representation and we now count the
A^{i_{0},j_{0}}
-subrepresentations of
X
.

Definition 4.17
.

A
principal generator
of
A^{i_{0},j_{0}}
is a vector
v
such that
g\cdot v=\chi^{j_{0}}(g)v
for all
g\in G^{\prime}=\Gal(L/F)
where
F=\mathbb{Q}(N^{1/p})
and
v
generates
A^{i_{0},j_{0}}
as a
\mathcal{G}
-module.

The purpose of the next two results is to count the number of principal generators of
A^{i_{0},j_{0}}
.

Proposition 4.18
.

When
i_{0}<p-1
, the
\mathcal{G}
-module
A^{i_{0},j_{0}}
has
p-1
principal generators.

Proof.

Pick a basis of
A^{i_{0},j_{0}}
such that the
\mathcal{G}
-action is give by the matrix
[\rho_{i_{0},j_{0}}](g)
where
g\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
For all
g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}
, the action of
g^{\prime}
is given by the diagonal matrix
D_{i_{0},j_{0}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\textrm{Diag}(\chi^{i_{0}+j_{0}}(g^{\prime}),\;\ldots,\;\chi^{j_{0}}(g^{\prime}))
.
Note that the first condition to be a principal generator is satisfied exactly by (the set of) column vectors
\{(0,\;\ldots,0,\;x)^{T}\ \mid\ x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\}
.

Claim:
For all
i,j
, the set of column vectors
\{(*,\;\ldots,*,\;1)^{T}\}
generates
A^{i,j}
as a
\mathcal{G}
-representation.

Justification:
The proof of the claim is by induction.
There is nothing to prove when
i=0
and assume that the result is true for all
k<i
.
For each element
g\in G=\Gal(L/K)
,

([\rho_{ij}](g)-\Id)\cdot\begin{pmatrix}*\\
\vdots\\
\alpha\\
1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}*\\
\vdots\\
b(g)\\
0\end{pmatrix}

Let
g
be such that
b(g)\neq 0
.
By induction hypothesis, the column vector
(*,\;\ldots,*,\;b(g),\;0)^{T}
generates
A^{i-1,j+1}\subseteq A^{i,j}
.
This together with the vector
(*,\;\ldots,*,\;1)^{T}
generates
A^{i,j}
.

We now conclude that
\{(0,\;\ldots,0,\;x)^{T}\ \mid\ x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\}
is precisely the set of principal generators and this set has
p-1
many elements.
∎

Proposition 4.19
.

The
\mathcal{G}
-module
A^{p-1,j_{0}}
has
p(p-1)
many principal generators.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Proposition
4.18
.
More precisely, observe that the condition to be a principal generator is satisfied by (the set of) column vectors
\{(y,\;0\;\ldots,0,\;x)^{T}\ \mid\ x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
.
But in view of the claim (in the proof of the previous proposition) we can generate
A^{p-1,j_{0}}
when
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
.
Thus there are
p(p-1)
many principal generators.
∎

Remark 4.20
.

(a)

The elements of the centralizer acts transitively on the set of principal generators.

(b)

The set of column vector
\{(0,\ \ldots,\ x,\ 0,\ \ldots 0)^{\textrm{T}}\mid x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\textrm{ and }x\textrm{ in }k\textrm{-th place}\}\subseteq A^{i,j}
is the set of principal generators for
A^{k,i+j-k}\subseteq A^{i,j}
.

Proposition 4.21
.

Fix
0\leq\theta\leq p-2
.
Let

v=\sum\gamma_{ij}^{(r)}v_{ij}^{(r)}\in X\cong\bigoplus_{i,j}(A^{i,j})^{n_{ij}},

where
\gamma_{ij}^{(r)}
is in the centralizer (possibly also the zero matrix) described previously and
v_{ij}^{(r)}
is in the
r
-th copy of
A^{i,j}
.
Moreover,
v_{ij}^{(r)}
is a column vector with 1 in the
\delta_{ij}^{(r)}
-th position and 0 everywhere else and it is a principal generator of
A^{\delta_{ij}^{(r)},\theta}\subseteq A^{i,j}
.
Let
\ell(v)=\ell\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\max\{\delta_{ij}^{(r)}\ \mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\ \gamma_{ij}^{(r)}\neq 0\}
.
Then

\mathcal{G}\cdot v\cong A^{\ell,\theta}.

Proof.

In view of Remark
4.20
(a) note that without loss of generality,
\gamma_{ij}^{(r)}\in\{0,I_{i+1}\}
.
The isomorphism in the statement of the proposition is then induced by the map

v\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
1\end{pmatrix}.\qed

Any
v\in X
satisfying
g^{\prime}\cdot v=\chi^{\theta}(g)v
for all
g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}
are in the above form.
Therefore, it follows that there is a map

\displaystyle\{v\in X\ \mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\ g^{\prime}\cdot v=\chi^{j_{0}}(g^{\prime})v\textrm{ for all }g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}\textrm{ and }j_{0}=\ell(v)\}

\displaystyle\mapsto\{A^{i_{0},j_{0}}\textrm{ subrepresentations of }X\}

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathcal{G}\cdot v

Moreover, the above map is surjective; it is of the form
p-1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}1
when
i_{0}<p-1
and of the form
p(p-1)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}1
when
i_{0}=p-1
.
To calculate the number of subrepresentations it suffices to count the number of vectors of the form arising in Proposition
4.21
.
We record this in the proposition below.

Proposition 4.22
.

Define

\displaystyle u_{i_{0},j_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=i_{0}}^{p-1}n_{\alpha,j_{0}+i_{0}-\alpha}+\sum_{\alpha=0}^{i_{0}-1}\sum_{\beta=j_{0}-\alpha}^{j_{0}}n_{\alpha\beta}+\sum_{\alpha=i_{0}}^{p-1}\sum_{\beta=j_{0}-\alpha}^{i_{0}+j_{0}-\alpha-1}n_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle v_{i_{0},j_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{i_{0}-1}\sum_{\beta=j_{0}-\alpha}^{j_{0}}n_{\alpha\beta}+\sum_{\alpha=i_{0}}^{p-1}\sum_{\beta=j_{0}-\alpha}^{i_{0}+j_{0}-\alpha-1}n_{\alpha\beta}.

Then the number of
A^{i_{0},j_{0}}
subrepresentations of
X
is given by

\begin{cases}\frac{p^{u_{i_{0},j_{0}}}-p^{v_{i_{0},j_{0}}}}{p-1}&\textrm{ if }i_{0}<p-1\\
\frac{p^{u_{i_{0},j_{0}}}-p^{v_{i_{0},j_{0}}}}{p(p-1)}&\textrm{ if }i_{0}=p-1.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

This is obtained by a careful counting.
∎

In the following proposition we count the number of
A^{i_{0},j_{0}}
quotients of
\Cl(L)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}\simeq\bigoplus_{ij}(A^{i,j})^{m_{ij}}
.

Proposition 4.23
.

Define

w_{ij}=\begin{cases}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{i}\sum_{\beta=i+j-\alpha}^{i+j}m_{\alpha\beta}+\sum_{\alpha=i+1}^{p-1}\sum_{\beta=j}^{i+j}m_{\alpha\beta}&\textrm{ when }i\geq 0\\
0&\textrm{ when }i<0.\end{cases}

Then the number of
A^{i_{0},j_{0}}
quotients of
\Cl(L)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
is given by

\begin{cases}\frac{p^{w_{i_{0},j_{0}}}-p^{w_{i_{0}-1,j_{0}+1}}}{p-1}&\textrm{ if }i_{0}<p-1\\
\frac{p^{w_{i_{0},j_{0}}}-p^{w_{i_{0}-1,j_{0}+1}}}{p(p-1)}&\textrm{ if }i_{0}=p-1.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof follows from Proposition
4.22
, Lemma
4.16
, and the discussion after the lemma.
∎

We now present the proof of the main theorem of this section.

Proof of Theorem
4.15
.

Recall the definition of
r_{ij}
introduced in Corollary
4.12
.
For all
i,j
note that
r_{ij}=w_{ij}
.
Also, for all
i,j
it follows from our earlier discussions that

w_{ij}-w_{i-1,j+1}=\sum_{\alpha=i}^{p-1}m_{\alpha j}.

Putting all of this together

\displaystyle\rk_{p}(\Cl(L))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}(i+1)m_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\sum_{\alpha=i}^{p-1}m_{\alpha j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\left(w_{ij}-w_{i-1,j+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}w_{p-1,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}r_{p-1,j}.

With this the proof is now complete.

When
p
is regular, the claim is immediate from Corollary
4.12
and Theorem
4.13
.
∎

From the proof of Theorem
4.15
we now derive a precise formula for the
m_{ij}
introduced in (
11
).

Corollary 4.24
.

For all
i,j
, the multiplicities
m_{ij}=r_{ij}+r_{i,j+1}-r_{i-1,j+1}-r_{i+1,j}
.

Proof.

It follows from the proof of Theorem
4.15
that

\displaystyle r_{ij}-r_{i-1,j+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=i}^{p-1}m_{\alpha j}

\displaystyle r_{i+1,j}-r_{i,j+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=i+1}^{p-1}m_{\alpha j}

Subtracting the two equations we get the desired result.
∎

When
p=3
, we prove a precise result regarding the
\mathcal{G}
-module structure of
\Cl(L)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{3}
.

Corollary 4.25
.

When
p=3
,

\Cl(L)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{F}_{p}\textrm{ when }\rk_{3}(L)=1\\
V\textrm{ when }\rk_{3}(L)=2,\end{cases}

where
V\simeq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
with Galois action as defined in (
4
).

Proof.

By Theorem
4.15
,

\rk_{p}(\Cl(L))=r_{1,0}+r_{1,1}.

But, we also have the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p})\longrightarrow\frac{H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},V)}{\langle\underline{\textbf{b}}^{(2)}\rangle}\longrightarrow\frac{H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(1))}{\langle b\rangle}.

The first term is 1-dimensional by
[
SS19
, Remark 3.2.1]
and the last term is trivial by Theorem
4.14
.
So,
r_{1,1}=r_{0,0}=1
and
r_{0,1}=0
.
On the other hand,
r_{1,0}
is either 0 or 1, depending on
\rk_{p}(\Cl(L))
.
The result follows from Corollary
4.24
.
∎

4.3.
Improved lower bounds

The purpose of this section is to use tools from Galois cohomology that we have developed in this section to provide a refinement of Theorem
3.2
when
p
is a regular prime.
We provide an elegant relationship between
\rk_{p}(\Cl(L))
and
\rk_{p}(\Cl(F))
in Corollary
4.33
.

Before stating the main result, we introduce another Selmer condition from
[
SS19
]
.

Definition 4.26
.

Let
A
be a
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
-module.
Define
\Sigma=\{L_{v}\}
to be the
Selmer condition
given by

•

L_{\ell}=H^{1}_{\ur}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}},A)=H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}/I_{\ell},A^{I_{\ell}})
for
\ell\not\in S
.

•

L_{N}=\ker\left(\Res\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{N}},A)\rightarrow H^{1}(G_{F_{N}},A)\right)
where
F_{N}=\mathbb{Q}_{N}(N^{1/p})
.

•

L_{p}=0
.

The
Selmer group
associated to the Selmer condition
\Sigma
is defined as

H^{1}_{\Sigma}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},A)=\ker\left(H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},A)\longrightarrow\prod_{v}\frac{H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}},A)}{L_{v}}\right).

More precisely, we prove the following theorem

Theorem 4.27
.

Let
p
be a regular prime.
Then

\displaystyle\rk_{p}\left(\Cl(L)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\rk_{p}\left(\Cl(F)\right)+\frac{p-3}{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
j\text{ even}\end{subarray}}^{p-3}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(j))\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p-1}{2}+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(H^{1}_{\Sigma}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(-1))\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
j\text{ even}\end{subarray}}^{p-3}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(j))\right).

The proof will occupy the remainder of this section.

Proposition 4.28
.

Consider the map

\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}H^{1}_{\Lambda}\left(G_{\mathbb{Q}},A^{i-1,j+1}\right)\longrightarrow H^{1}_{\Lambda}\left(G_{\mathbb{Q}},A^{i,j}\right)

induced by the inclusion
A^{i-1,j+1}\subseteq A^{i,j}
.
If
p-1\nmid j
, then
\iota
is injective; whereas if
p-1\mid j
then
\ker(\iota)
is 1-dimensional.

Proof.

Consider the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow A^{i-1,j+1}\longrightarrow A^{i,j}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}(j)\longrightarrow 0

and take the
G_{\mathbb{Q},S}
cohomology to obtain

\mathbb{F}_{p}(j)^{G_{\mathbb{Q},S}}\xrightarrow{\theta}H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q},S},A^{i-1,j+1})\xrightarrow{\iota}H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q},S},A^{i,j}).

When
p-1\nmid j
, observe that
\mathbb{F}_{p}(j)^{G_{\mathbb{Q},S}}=0
which implies the first claim.

When
p-1\nmid j
, the generator of
\ker(\iota)=\Image(\theta)
.
A careful diagram chase shows that the kernel is generated by the cocycle
[\uline{\boldsymbol{b}}^{(i)}]
.
∎

.

The following map is injective

\widetilde{\iota}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},A^{i-1,2})\longrightarrow\frac{H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},A^{i-1,1})}{\langle\underline{\textbf{b}}^{(i+1)}\rangle}

Let us first consider the following commutative diagram:

We have the following filtration

Proposition 4.31
.

Let
p
be a regular prime and

Then,

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(H^{1}_{\Lambda}\left(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(j)\right))=1.

We first begin with the observation that for the

H^{1}_{N}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(j))\subseteq H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(j))\subseteq H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q},S},\mathbb{F}_{p}(j)).

It follows from
[

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(j))\right)

Suppose that the dimension is 2, then

H^{1}_{p}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(j))\subseteq H^{1}_{S}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(j))=H^{1}_{\Lambda}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(j)).

Consider a non-zero element

Since

\Res_{p}(x)\in H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(j))

\mathcal{F}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})

\Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})/\mathbb{Q}_{p})

Consider the following diagram

\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})
L_{p}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p},N^{1/p})
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}L_{p}
\mathcal{F}
\mathbb{F}_{p}(1)
\mathbb{F}_{p}(j)

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}/L_{p}

[
SS19

Since

This contradicts (

Corollary 4.32
.

Let

\rk_{5}\left(\Cl(L)\right)\geq\begin{cases}2&\textrm{ if }\rk_{5}\left(\Cl(F)\right)=1\\

Proof.

Recall from
[

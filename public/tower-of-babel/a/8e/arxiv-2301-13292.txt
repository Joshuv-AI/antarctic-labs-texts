On the structure of entropy solutions to the Riemann problem for a degenerate nonlinear parabolic equation
Evgeny Yu. Panov

Abstract
We find an explicit form of entropy solutions to a Riemann problem for a degenerate nonlinear parabolic equation with piecewise constant velocity and diffusion coefficients. It is demonstrated that this solution corresponds to the minimum point of some strictly convex function of a finite number of variables.

Introduction

In a half-plane
\Pi=\{(t,x)\ |\ t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
, we consider a nonlinear parabolic equation

u_{t}+v(u)u_{x}-t(a^{2}(u)u_{x})_{x}=0,

(1)

u_{t}+\varphi(u)_{x}=0,

(2)

\varphi^{\prime}(u)=v(u)
. Similarly, a general equation (
1

u_{t}+\varphi(u)_{x}-tA(u)_{xx}=0

A^{\prime}(u)=a^{2}(u)
, which allows to define weak solutions of this equation. Unfortunately, weak solutions to a Cauchy problem for equation (
1
) are not unique in general, and some additional entropy conditions are required. We consider the Cauchy problem with initial data

u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),

(3)

where
u_{0}(x)\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
. Recall the notion of entropy solution (e.s. for short) in the sense of Carrillo
[
1

Definition 1

A function

(i) the distribution

(ii) for all

(4)

{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Pi)

(iii)
\displaystyle\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,lim}_{t\to 0}u(t,\cdot)=u_{0}

L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}})

Entropy condition (
4

\int_{\Pi}[|u-k|f_{t}+\operatorname{sign}(u-k)((\varphi(u)-\varphi(k))f_{x}+t(A(u)-A(k))f_{xx})]dtdx\geq 0.

(5)

In the case of conservation laws (

Taking in (

u_{t}+\varphi(u)_{x}-tA(u)_{xx}=0\ \mbox{ in }{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Pi),

The entropy function

We introduce the convex cone

\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

\xi_{1}\leq\xi_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\xi_{d}

Each point

Lemma 1
.

The function
P(x,y)=-\ln(F(x)-F(y))
is strictly convex in the half-plane
x>y
.

Proof.

The function
P(x,y)
is infinitely differentiable in the domain
x>y
. To prove the lemma, we need to establish that the Hessian
D^{2}P
is positive definite at every point. By the direct computation we find

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}P(x,y)=\frac{(F^{\prime}(x))^{2}-F^{\prime\prime}(x)(F(x)-F(y))}{(F(x)-F(y))^{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}P(x,y)=\frac{(F^{\prime}(y))^{2}-F^{\prime\prime}(y)(F(y)-F(x))}{(F(x)-F(y))^{2}},\ \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x\partial y}P(x,y)=-\frac{F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y)}{(F(x)-F(y))^{2}}.

We have to prove positive definiteness of the matrix
Q=(F(x)-F(y))^{2}D^{2}P(x,y)
with the components

\displaystyle Q_{11}=(F^{\prime}(x))^{2}-F^{\prime\prime}(x)(F(x)-F(y)),

\displaystyle Q_{22}=(F^{\prime}(y))^{2}-F^{\prime\prime}(y)(F(y)-F(x)),\ Q_{12}=Q_{21}=-F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y).

Since
F^{\prime}(x)=e^{-x^{2}/2}
, then
F^{\prime\prime}(x)=-xF^{\prime}(x)
and the diagonal elements of this matrix can be written in the form

\displaystyle Q_{11}=F^{\prime}(x)(x(F(x)-F(y))+F^{\prime}(x))=

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(x)(x(F(x)-F(y))+(F^{\prime}(x)-F^{\prime}(y)))+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y),

\displaystyle Q_{22}=F^{\prime}(y)(y(F(y)-F(x))+(F^{\prime}(y)-F^{\prime}(x)))+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y).

By Cauchy mean value theorem there exists such a value
z\in(y,x)
that

\frac{F^{\prime}(x)-F^{\prime}(y)}{F(x)-F(y)}=\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(z)}{F^{\prime}(z)}=-z.

Therefore,

\displaystyle Q_{11}=F^{\prime}(x)(F(x)-F(y))(x-z)+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y),

\displaystyle Q_{22}=F^{\prime}(y)(F(x)-F(y))(z-y)+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y),

and it follows that
Q=R_{1}+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y)R_{2}
, where
R_{1}
is a diagonal matrix with the positive diagonal elements

F^{\prime}(x)(F(x)-F(y))(x-z)
,
F^{\prime}(y)(F(x)-F(y))(z-y)
while
R_{2}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Since
R_{1}>0
,
R_{2}\geq 0
, then the matrix
Q>0
, as was to be proved.
∎

Corollary 1
.

The functions
P(x,-\infty)=-\ln F(x)
,
P(+\infty,x)=-\ln(1-F(x))
of single variable are strictly convex.

Proof.

Since
1-F(x)=F(-x)
, we see that
P(+\infty,x)=P(-x,-\infty)
, and it is sufficient to prove the strict convexity of the function

P(x,-\infty)=-\ln F(x)
. By Lemma
1
in the limit as
y\to-\infty
we obtain that this function is convex, moreover,

0\leq(F(x))^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}P(x,-\infty)=\lim_{y\to-\infty}Q_{11}=F^{\prime}(x)(xF(x)+F^{\prime}(x)).

If
\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}P(x,-\infty)=0
at some point
x=x_{0}
then
0=x_{0}F(x_{0})+F^{\prime}(x_{0})
is the minimum of the nonnegative function
xF(x)+F^{\prime}(x)
. Therefore, its derivative
(xF+F^{\prime})^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
. Since
F^{\prime\prime}(x)=-xF^{\prime}(x)
, this derivative

(xF+F^{\prime})^{\prime}(x_{0})=F(x_{0})+x_{0}F^{\prime}(x_{0})+F^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})=F(x_{0})>0.

But this contradicts our assumption. We conclude that
\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}P(x,-\infty)>0
and the function
P(x,-\infty)
is strictly convex.
∎

Proposition 2

(convexity)
.

The entropy function
E(\bar{\xi})
is strictly convex on
\Omega
.

Proof.

For
k=0,\ldots,n-1
we denote
P_{k}(\bar{\xi})=-\ln(F((\xi_{k+1}-v_{k})/a_{k})-F((\xi_{k}-v_{k})/a_{k}))
if
a_{k}>0
,
and
P_{k}(\bar{\xi})=(\xi_{k+1}-v_{k})^{2}
if
a_{k}=0
. In view of (
3
) the entropy
E(\bar{\xi})
is a linear combination of the functions
P_{k}
with positive coefficients, and convexity of the entropy readily follows from the statements of
Lemma
1
and Corollary
1
. To establish the strict convexity, we have to demonstrate that the Hessian matrix
D^{2}E(\bar{\xi})
is strictly positive. Assume that for some
\zeta=(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

D^{2}E(\bar{\xi})\zeta\cdot\zeta=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}E(\bar{\xi})}{\partial\xi_{i}\partial\xi_{j}}\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}=0.

(34)

Since
E(\bar{\xi})
is a linear combination of convex functions
P_{k}(\bar{\xi})
with positive coefficients, we find that

D^{2}P_{k}(\bar{\xi})\zeta\cdot\zeta=0\quad\forall k=0,\ldots,n-1.

This can be written in the form

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=k,k+1}\frac{\partial^{2}P_{k}(\bar{\xi})}{\partial\xi_{i}\partial\xi_{j}}\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}=0\ \mbox{ if }0<k<n-1,a_{k}>0;

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}P_{k}(\bar{\xi})}{\partial\xi_{k+1}^{2}}\zeta_{k+1}^{2}\ \mbox{ if }k=0\mbox{ or }a_{k}=0.

In view of Lemma
1
and Corollary
1
the functions
P_{k}
in above equalities are strictly convex as functions of either two variables

(\xi_{k},\xi_{k+1})
or single variable
\xi_{k+1}
. Therefore, these equalities imply that in any case
\zeta_{k+1}=0
,
k=0,\ldots,n-2
, and
\zeta_{n}=0
if
a_{n-1}=0
(when
d=n
). We conclude that all coordinates
\zeta_{i}=0
,
i=1,\ldots,d
. Hence, equality (
34
) can hold only for
\zeta=0
and the matrix
D^{2}P(\bar{\xi})>0
for all
\bar{\xi}\in\Omega
. This completes the proof.
∎

4
The variational formulation

Let
\bar{\xi}_{0}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d})\in\Omega
be the unique minimum point of
E(\bar{\xi})
. The necessary and sufficient condition for
\bar{\xi}_{0}
to be a minimum point is the following one

\nabla E(\bar{\xi}_{0})\cdot p\geq 0\quad\forall p\in T(\bar{\xi}_{0})=\{\ p\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\ |\ \exists h>0\ \bar{\xi}_{0}+hp\in\Omega\ \},

(35)

so that
T(\bar{\xi}_{0})
is the tangent cone to
\Omega
at the point
\bar{\xi}_{0}
. If
\bar{\xi}_{0}\in\operatorname{Int}\Omega
then

T(\bar{\xi}_{0})={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and (
35
) reduces to the requirement
\nabla E(\bar{\xi}_{0})=0
. As we have already demonstrated, this requirement coincides with jump conditions (
2
), (
19
), (
20
), (
21
) for all
k=1,\ldots,d
.
But these conditions are equivalent to the statement that the function (
2
) is an e.s. of (
1
), (
3
).
In the general situation when
\bar{\xi}_{0}
can belong to the boundary of
\Omega
, the coordinates of
\bar{\xi}_{0}
may coincides. Let
\xi_{k}=\cdots=\xi_{l}=c
be a maximal family of coinciding coordinates, that is,
\xi_{k-1}<\xi_{k}=\xi_{l}<\xi_{l+1}
(it is possible here that
k=l
). Then, as is easy to realize, the vector
p=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})
, with arbitrary increasing coordinates
p_{k}\leq\cdots\leq p_{l}
and with zero remaining coordinates, belong to the tangent cone
T(\bar{\xi}_{0})
. In view of (
35
)

\sum_{i=k}^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}E(\bar{\xi}_{0})p_{i}\geq 0

for any such a vector. Using the summation by parts formula, we realize that the above condition is equivalent to the following requirements

\displaystyle\sum_{i=k}^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}E(\bar{\xi}_{0})=0,

(36)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=j}^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}E(\bar{\xi}_{0})\geq 0,\ k<j\leq l.

(37)

Recall that
a_{i}=0
for
k\leq i<l
. By the direct computation we find

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}E(\bar{\xi}_{0})=(u_{i}-u_{i-1})(\xi_{i}-v_{i-1}),\quad k<i<l,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{k}}E(\bar{\xi}_{0})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}(u_{k}-u_{k-1})(\xi_{k}-v_{k-1})&,&a_{k-1}=0,\\
-A(u)^{\prime}(c-)&,&a_{k-1}>0;\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{l}}E(\bar{\xi}_{0})=A(u)^{\prime}(c+),

where
A(u)^{\prime}(c\pm)
are given by (
2
), (
2
). Putting these expressions into (
36
), (

), we obtain exactly the jump conditions (

), (

).
Therefore, the function (
2
) corresponding to the point
\bar{\xi}_{0}
is an e.s. of (
1
), (
3
).
Conversely, if (
2
) is an e.s. then relations (
36
), (
37
) holds for all groups of coinciding
coordinates. As is easy to verify, this is equivalent to the criterion (
35
). We have proved our main result.

Theorem 1
.

The function (
2
) is an e.s. of (
1
), (
3
) if and only if
\bar{\xi}_{0}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d})
is the minimum point of the entropy

.

Remark 1
.

Adding to the entropy (
3
) the constant

\sum_{k=0,\ldots,n-1,a_{k}>0}(a_{k})^{2}(u_{k+1}-u_{k})\ln((u_{k+1}-u_{k})/a_{k}),

we obtain the alternative variant of the entropy

\displaystyle E_{1}(\bar{\xi})=-\sum_{k=0,\ldots,n-1,a_{k}>0}(a_{k})^{2}(u_{k+1}-u_{k})\ln\left(\frac{F((\xi_{k+1}-v_{k})/a_{k})-F((\xi_{k}-v_{k})/a_{k})}{(u_{k+1}-u_{k})/a_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0,\ldots,n-1,a_{k}=0}(u_{k+1}-u_{k})(\xi_{k+1}-v_{k})^{2}.

(38)

If we consider the values
v_{k},a_{k}
as a piecewise constant approximation of an arbitrary velocity function
v(u)
and, respectively, a diffusion function
a(u)\geq 0
then, passing in (
1
) to the limit as
\max(u_{k+1}-u_{k})\to 0
, we find that the entropy
E_{1}(\bar{\xi})
turns into the variational functional

\displaystyle J(\xi)=-\int_{\{u\in[\alpha,\beta],a(u)>0\}}(a(u))^{2}\ln(F^{\prime}((\xi(u)-v(u))/a(u))\xi^{\prime}(u))du+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\{u\in[\alpha,\beta],a(u)=0\}}(\xi(u)-v(u))^{2}du,

where
\xi(u)
is an increasing function on
[\alpha,\beta]
, which is expected to be the inverse function to a self-similar solution
u=u(\xi)
of the problem (
1
), (
3
). Taking into account that

\displaystyle\ln(F^{\prime}((\xi(u)-v(u))/a(u))\xi^{\prime}(u))=\ln F^{\prime}((\xi(u)-v(u))/a(u))+\ln\xi^{\prime}(u)=

\displaystyle-\frac{(\xi(u)-v(u))^{2}}{2a^{2}(u)}+\ln\xi^{\prime}(u),

we may simplify the expression for the functional
J(\xi)

J(\xi)=\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}[(\xi(u)-v(u))^{2}/2-(a(u))^{2}\ln(\xi^{\prime}(u))]du.

(39)

We see that this functional is strictly convex. The corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation has the form

\xi(u)-v(u)+((a(u))^{2}/\xi^{\prime}(u))^{\prime}=0.

(40)

Since
u^{\prime}(\xi)=1/\xi^{\prime}(u)
,
u=u(\xi)
, we can transform (
40
) as follows

\xi(u)-v(u)+((a(u))^{2}u^{\prime}(\xi))^{\prime}_{u}=0.

Multiplying this equation by
u^{\prime}(\xi)
, we obtain the equation

(a^{2}u^{\prime})^{\prime}=(v-\xi)u^{\prime},\quad u=u(\xi),

which is exactly our equation (
1
) written in the self-similar variable.

Remark 2
.

In the case of conservation laws (
2

of (
2

Existence and uniqueness of a minimal point in this case is trivial. By Theorem

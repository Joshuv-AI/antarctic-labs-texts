Results about Sets of Desirable Gamble Sets
Catrin Campbell-Moore

Abstract
Coherent sets of desirable gamble sets is used as a model for representing an agents opinions and choice preferences under uncertainty. In this paper we provide some results about the axioms required for coherence and the natural extension of a given set of desirable gamble sets. We also show that coherent sets of desirable gamble sets can be represented by a proper filter of coherent sets of desirable gambles.

2
Natural Extension for sets of desirable gamble sets

2.1
The key result about coherence for sets of desirable gamble sets

This section gives a result which turns out to be the key important property of coherent sets of desirable gamble sets. We will afterwards show that this is is a characteristic result of coherence, i.e., it gives us the natural extension.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{K}
is coherent and
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\in\mathcal{K}
.

If
B
is such that for each sequence
\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\rangle\in A_{1}\times\ldots\times A_{n}

0\in\mathrm{posi}(\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\}\cup\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0})

\cup\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0})

Then

.

Proof.

We start with a (not terribly interesting) lemma:

Sublemma 2.1.1
.

For
f\in\mathrm{posi}(\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\}\cup\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0})
, whenever
f\notin\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}

h\in\mathrm{posi}(\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\})

Proof.

f\in\mathrm{posi}(\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\}\cup\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0})

and

, with at least one
>0
.

Suppose

. Then some
\lambda_{i}>0
because otherwise

with each
p_{j}\in\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}
, and so we’d also have that
f\in\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}
.

So, let

, and we then know that

∎

Assume we have

and

.

We know that for any sequence

_{\gneq 0})

When

denote

,

_{\gneq 0})
.

If there is some
f\in\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}
with

,

.
So we can assume that each

2.2
Understanding this

This will in fact give us maximal information about coherence.

But first we give an idea of why this might be the right thing to do and to help understand the criterion.

Suppose
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{K}
. We can then consider what else should be in
\mathcal{K}

You think that

contains some desirable gamble, and so does

. But you leave it open which member of

is desirable and which member of
A_{2}

We can go through each member of

contains a desirable gamble. Similarly for

is desirable in virtue of

A_{2}
is desirable in virtue of
g_{2}

is desirable, and more generally any

is desirable; so any

which contains some member of
\mathrm{posi}(\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\cup\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0})
, i.e., when
\varnothing\neq B\cap\mathrm{posi}(\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\cup\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0})
, contains some desirable gamble.

\mathrm{posi}(\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\cup\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0})
g_{1}
g_{2}
g_{1}+g_{2}

But this is all conditional on

and
g_{2}
being the desirable members. If
B
is a set such that

A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are considered to be the relevant ones in virtue of which
A_{i}
desirable, we can have that some member of
B
is desirable, then we can outright conclude that

contains a desirable gamble. We needn’t be able to point to any particular member of

which we evaluate as desirable. But whenever a member of
A_{1}
and a member of
A_{2}
are desirable, then so is a member of
B

This motivates the general constraint:

If for each pair
\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle\in A_{1}\times A_{2}
, there is some
f_{\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle}\in\mathrm{posi}(\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\cup\mathcal{G}_%
{\gneq 0})
with
f_{\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle}\in B
.
Then
B\in\mathcal{K}
. (
*
)

To see how this works in practice, consider

.

Figure 1:

Suppose

A proof of the legitimacy of this, given the axioms on coherence, is in

This story can easily be extended to multiple sets, if
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
are all taken to contain at least one desirable gamble, and whenever we pick some particular members of them and consider those to be desirable, a member of
B
is also desirable, then we conclude that

contains a desirable gamble.
That is:

If for each sequence
\langle g_{i}\rangle\in\bigtimes A_{i}
, there is some
f_{\langle g_{i}\rangle}\in\mathrm{posi}(\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\}\cup\mathcal{G}%
_{\gneq 0})
with
f_{\langle g_{i}\rangle}\in B
.
Then
B\in\mathcal{K}
.

Can it be extended to infinitely many sets? Well, this will go beyond the axioms that have been imposed for coherence. A thought behind this: I am unable to perform infinite reasoning with desirability. Or alternatively, maybe I am unable to simultaneously consider the infinitely many sets and pick the relevant members. Or perhaps one can only make finitely many conditional assumptions.

So, we have motivated something quite close to that of
theorem

2.1
.
However, it misses the clause that says that when

.
This is because when

,

are mutually incompatible. Suppose we have
g_{1}\in A_{1}
and
g_{2}\in A_{2}
with
0\in\mathrm{posi}(\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\cup\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0})
. It cannot be that, simultaneously,
A_{1}
is desirable in virtue of
g_{1}
and
A_{2}
is desirable in virtue of
g_{2}
. The desirability of
g_{1}
rules out the desirability of
g_{2}
. So we can ignore this choice of
\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle
. We only need to find the relevant

when the choice of these
g_{i}
are mutually compatible.

2.3
Defining the Natural Extension

If we start with a given set of desirable gamble sets,
\mathcal{A}
, we define
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathcal{A})
by closing it under the construction of

Definition 2.2
.

For

be given by:

When
\mathcal{A}\neq\varnothing
:

•

B\in\mathrm{Ext}(\mathcal{A})

\cup\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0})

When
\mathcal{A}=\varnothing

•

B\in\mathrm{Ext}(\varnothing)
iff there is some
f\in B
where also
f\in\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}

{\gneq 0})
. This follows from

and each

{G}_{\gneq 0})
(since it is in
\mathrm{posi}(\{e^{i}_{j}\>|\>\mathopen{}\allowbreak j\}\cup\mathcal{G}_{\gneq
0})
).

So

. ∎

So we have shown that when
\varnothing\notin\mathrm{Ext}(\mathcal{A})
,
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathcal{A})
is the minimal coherent extension of
\mathcal{A}
.

We now just need to check that when
\varnothing\in\mathrm{Ext}(\mathcal{A})
, there is no coherent

extending
\mathcal{A}
. This follows from
theorem

2.1
because then
\varnothing
is in any coherent
\mathcal{K}
extending
\mathcal{A}
, contradicting the supposed coherence of
\mathcal{K}
.
∎

2.4.1
Quick notes

One component of our natural extension formulation is:

We can also phrase things with intersections. I.e.,

Or without the reference to

as:

•

iff

I quite like the reference to the particular member, rather than talking about the intersection being non-empty, because I think it helps thinking about it and is useful to have for proofs, which is why I’ve opted for that.

2.4.2
On the case when
\mathcal{A}=\varnothing

I have chosen to define the case of

differently from when
\mathcal{A}=\varnothing
.

To give a single definition we could include an additional separate clause for the case where

. So, we would offer:

An alternative would be to first add something that we know to be in all coherent

2.5
Relationship to
De Bock and de Cooman (
2018
)

De Bock and de Cooman (
2018
)
also give a formulation of the natural extension.
Since they both characterise natural extensions, they’ll be equivalent, but in this section we consider the difference in more detail.

They:

(i)

Add the singletons from
\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}
to
\mathcal{A}
. This gives us
\mathcal{A}\cup\{\{g\}\>|\>\mathopen{}\allowbreak g\in\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}\}
.

(ii)

We add sets using
\mathrm{posi}
from these sets. More carefully: For any
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}
in
\mathcal{A}\cup\{\{g\}\>|\>\mathopen{}\allowbreak g\in\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}\}
(from
(i)
), if for each sequence
\langle g_{i}\rangle\in\bigtimes C_{i}
,
f_{\langle g_{i}\rangle}\in\mathrm{posi}(\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\})
, then we add
\{f_{\langle g_{i}\rangle}\>|\>\mathopen{}\allowbreak\langle g_{i}\rangle\in%
\bigtimes C_{i}\}
. Our resultant set is called
\mathrm{Posi}(\mathcal{A}\cup\{\{g\}\>|\>\mathopen{}\allowbreak g\in\mathcal{G%
}_{\gneq 0}\})
.

(iii)

Add any sets obtained by removing some gambles which are
\leqslant 0
.

(iv)

Add any supersets thereof.

This gives us the final set, called:
\mathrm{Rs}(\mathrm{Posi}(\mathcal{A}\cup\{\{g\}\>|\>\mathopen{}\allowbreak g%
\in\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}\}))
. They show that this is the natural extension in the finite setting. So in this setting, we know that it will be equivalent to ours, however it’s open whether it gives the natural extension in the infinite setting.

It’s got the same sort of moving parts as our definition: there’s addition of some
\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}
(in
(i)
), there’s taking posi’s (in
(ii)
), there’s removing some obviously-bad stuff (in
(iii)
) and taking supersets (in
(iv)
). These are the same sorts of moving parts that I have.

Let’s try to rework their formulation to present it in a way that is more similar to our presentation so we can evaluate how/if they are different. Firstly, we can summarise the construction to:

•

B\in\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{DBdC}}(\mathcal{A})
iff there are some
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}\in\mathcal{A}\cup\{\{g\}\>|\>\mathopen{}\allowbreak g\in%
\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}\}
with
\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\rangle\in C_{1}\times\ldots\times C_{n}
, there is some
f_{\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\rangle}\in B\cup\mathcal{G}_{\leqslant 0}
where also
f_{\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\rangle}\in\mathrm{posi}(\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\})
.

We can then split the choice of
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}
from
\mathcal{A}\cup\{\{g\}\>|\>\mathopen{}\allowbreak g\in\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}\}
into a choice separately of
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
, and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}
. Sometimes no members of
\mathcal{A}
are chosen, which should be presented as a separate clause. So we see that:

•

B\in\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{DBdC}}(\mathcal{A})
iff either:

–

There are some
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}
such that for each sequence
\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\rangle\in A_{1}\times\ldots\times A_{n}
, there is some
f_{\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\rangle}\in B\cup\mathcal{G}_{\leqslant 0}
where also
f_{\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\rangle}\in\mathrm{posi}(\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n},p_{%
1},\ldots,p_{m}\})
.

–

There are some
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}
such that there is some
f\in B\cup\mathcal{G}_{\leqslant 0}
where also
f\in\mathrm{posi}(\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\})
.

Note that this is different to our:

•

B\in\mathrm{Ext}(\mathcal{A})
iff there are some
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
and for each sequence
\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\rangle\in A_{1}\times\ldots\times A_{n}
, there is some
f_{\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\rangle}\in B\cup\{0\}
where also
f_{\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\rangle}\in\mathrm{posi}(\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\}%
\cup\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0})
.

Some of the differences between these are simply choices of presentation.

However, substantial differences are:

•

DBdC allow the choice of the sequence
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}
to contain no members of
\mathcal{A}
whatsoever. This allows their definition to apply properly to the case of
\mathcal{A}=\varnothing
, whereas we need a separate definition for that case.

•

Their
\mathsf{Posi}
is defined working with finitely many

, which means in any use of it we can only use finitely many members of
\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}
.
Is that enough? It’s closely related to my addition of axiom
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{Dom}}
.

For example, suppose
A\in\mathcal{A}
and we have for each
g
in
A
a distinct
p_{g}\in\mathcal{G}_{\gneq 0}
, we want to ensure that
\{g+p_{g}\>|\>\mathopen{}\allowbreak g\in A\}\in\mathrm{Ext}(\mathcal{A})
.

This doesn’t immediately follow from the construction of
\mathsf{Posi}
because it involves infinitely many sets:
A
plus the infinitely many singletons
\{p_{g}\}
.

In fact, like the dominance axiom, when
\Omega
is finite it’ll be fine because
g+p_{g}\in\mathrm{posi}(\{g,I_{\omega_{1}},\ldots,I_{\omega_{n}}\})
so we can just work with the finitely many sets
A,\{I_{\omega_{1}}\},\ldots,\{I_{\omega_{n}}\}
.
But perhaps when
\Omega
is infinite it’ll be different.

2.6.1
Regularity

We can replace

\mathfrak{D}

\displaystyle B\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{D}}

\displaystyle\text{ iff }\begin{split}&\text{there are some $\mathbb{D}_{1},%

Now we get axiom
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{Add}}
for free, however axiom
\mathrm{K}_{0}
might now fail. We still need a “coherence” requirement on this. The relevant requirement is that
\mathfrak{D}

\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\ldots\cap\mathbb{D}_{n}=\varnothing
. It is then equivalent.

Corollary 4.4
.

\mathcal{K}
is coherent iff there is some
\mathfrak{D}
a non-empty set of

\mathfrak{D}

\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\ldots\cap\mathbb{D}_{n}=\varnothing

\displaystyle B\in\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle\text{ iff }\begin{split}&\text{there are some $\mathbb{D}_{1},%
\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{n}\in\mathfrak{D}$}\\
&\text{ s.t.~{}for all ${D}\in\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\ldots\cap\mathbb{D}_{n}$, %
there is some $g_{D}\in B\cap{D}$}\end{split}

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{D}
is such a set which has the finite intersection property, consider
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
which closes it under finite intersections, i.e.,
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}:=\{\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\ldots\cap\mathbb{D}_{n}\>|\>%
\mathopen{}\allowbreak\text{$\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{n}$ are %
finitely many members of $\mathfrak{D}$}\}
. By assumption that
\mathfrak{D}
has the finite intersection property,
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
doesn’t contain the emptyset. It immediately satisfies all the other assumptions of
theorem

4.2
.

Also observe that there are some
\mathbb{D}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{D}_{n}\in\mathfrak{D}
s.t. for all
{D}\in\mathbb{D}_{1}\cap\ldots\cap\mathbb{D}_{n}
,
\varnothing\neq B\cap{D}
iff there is some
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
s.t. for all
{D}\in\mathbb{D}^{\prime}

\varnothing\neq B\cap{D}
.

So we see that for any such
\mathfrak{D}

4.2

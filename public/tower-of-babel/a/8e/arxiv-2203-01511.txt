Measurable tilings by abelian group actions
Jan Grebík, Rachel Greenfeld, Václav Rozhoň, Terence Tao

Abstract
. Let X X be a measure space with a measure-preserving action ( g , x ) ↦ g ⋅ x (g,x)\mapsto g\cdot x of an abelian group G G . We consider the problem of understanding the structure of measurable tilings F ⊙ A = X F\odot A=X of X X by a measurable tile A ⊂ X A\subset X translated by a finite set F ⊂ G F\subset G of shifts, thus the translates f ⋅ A f\cdot A , f ∈ F f\in F partition X X up to null sets. Adapting arguments from previous literature, we establish a “dilation lemma” that asserts, roughly speaking, that F ⊙ A = X F\odot A=X implies F r ⊙ A = X F^{r}\odot A=X for a large family of integer dilations r r , and use this to establish a structure theorem for such tilings analogous to that established recently by the second and fourth authors. As applications of this theorem, we completely classify those random tilings of finitely generated abelian groups that are “factors of iid”, and show that measurable tilings of a torus 𝕋 d \mathbb{T}^{d} can always be continuously (in fact linearly) deformed into a tiling with rational shifts, with particularly strong results in the low-dimensional cases d = 1 , 2 d=1,2 (in particular resolving a conjecture of Conley, the first author, and Pikhurko in the d = 1 d=1 case).



1.
Introduction

In this paper we establish a “dilation lemma” and “structure theorem” for abelian measurable tilings, and apply this to obtain new results on factor-of-iid tilings, as well as measurable translational tilings of tori.

1.1.
Dilation lemmas and structure theorems for abelian tilings

Let
G=(G,\cdot)
be a (discrete) group. By a
(translational) tiling

F\odot A=G
of

of

of

G

One can also consider translational tilings involving multiple pairs

1.2.
First application: factor of iid tilings

We now turn to applications of
Theorem

1.2
. We first consider a class of random tilings of a group known as
factor of iid tilings
.

Definition 1.3

(Factor of iid)
.

Let
G=(G,+)
be a group. For each element
x
of
G
, let
\lambda(x)
be an iid element of the unit interval
[0,1]
, thus the
(\lambda(x))_{x\in G}
are jointly independent random variables, each drawn uniformly at random from
[0,1]
. A random subset
A
of
G
is said to be a
factor of iid process
if there exists a Borel measurable function
\Phi\colon[0,1]^{G}\to\{0,1\}
such that

(2)

{\bf 1}_{A}(x_{0})=\Phi\left((\lambda(x_{0}+x))_{x\in G}\right)

almost surely for all
x_{0}\in G
. (In particular,
A
is a stationary process.) More generally, a finite collection
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}
of random subsets of
G
is a
(joint) factor of iid process
if there exist Borel measurable functions
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{k}\colon[0,1]^{G}\to\{0,1\}
such that

{\bf 1}_{A_{i}}(x_{0})=\Phi_{i}\left((\lambda(x_{0}+x))_{x\in G}\right)

almost surely for all
x_{0}\in G
.

If
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}
are finite subsets of
G
, a
factor of iid tiling
of
G
by
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}
is a joint factor of iid process
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}
such that

(F_{1}\oplus A_{1})\uplus\dots\uplus(F_{k}\oplus A_{k})=G

almost surely.

Informally, a factor of iid tiling is a tiling which is generated in a “local” fashion, in the sense that the behavior of the tiling sets
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}
in some finite region
\Omega
of
G
is primarily determined by the random variables
\lambda(x)
for
x
near
\Omega
. We illustrate the concept with the following example

Example 1.4
.

We can generate a factor of iid tiling of the integers
\mathbb{Z}
by the tiles
F_{1}\coloneqq\{0,1\}
,
F_{2}\coloneqq\{0,1,2\}
by performing the following procedure.

(i)

First we generate jointly independent random variables
\lambda(x)\in[0,1]
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(ii)

We construct the factor of iid process

S\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{Z}:\lambda(x)<\lambda(x-1),\lambda(x+1)\}

of “local minima” of
\lambda
. We enumerate
S=\{s_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
by order (i.e.,
s_{n}<s_{m}
if
n<m
). Note that
S
is almost surely unbounded both above and below, and is
2
-separated, in the sense that
s_{n+1}-s_{n}\geq 2
for any two consecutive elements
s_{n},s_{n+1}
of
S
.

(iii)

Using the process
S=\{s_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, we construct the factor of iid process
S^{\prime}
by

S^{\prime}\coloneqq\{s_{n}+2j\in\mathbb{Z}\colon n,j\in\mathbb{Z},s_{n}\leq s_{n}+2j\leq s_{n+1}-2\}.

We enumerate
S^{\prime}=\{s^{\prime}_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
by order.
Note that
S^{\prime}
is also almost surely unbounded above and below, and one has
2\leq s^{\prime}_{n+1}-s^{\prime}_{n}\leq 3
for any consecutive elements
s^{\prime}_{n},s^{\prime}_{n+1}
of
S^{\prime}
.

(iv)

Using the process
S^{\prime}=\{s^{\prime}_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

A_{1},A_{2}
by setting
A_{1}
to consist of those elements
s^{\prime}_{n}
of
S^{\prime}
for which
s^{\prime}_{n+1}-s^{\prime}_{n}=2
, and
A_{2}
to consist of those elements of
s^{\prime}_{n}

S^{\prime}

One then easily verifies that

\mathbb{Z}
by

On the other hand, there is no factor of iid tiling

of the integers
\mathbb{Z}
just by

, due to the “rigid” nature of this tiling equation. Indeed, the only possible values of

are the even integers

or the odd integers

The above argument can be strengthened to show that the tiling with the two tiles

is possible not only as a factor of iid, but even in other, more restrictive, models.

(Tiles admitting a factor of iid tiling)

1.3.
Second application: measurable tilings of tori

Our second application of
Theorem

1.2
concerns measurable tilings
F\oplus A=_{\mathrm{a.e.}}\mathbb{T}^{d}
of a torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
using the standard translation action of
\mathbb{R}^{d}=(\mathbb{R}^{d},+)
, thus
F
is a finite subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
A
is a measurable subset of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. We say that such a tiling is
rational
if the set
F-F=\{f^{\prime}-f\colon f,f^{\prime}\in F\}
lies in
\mathbb{Q}^{d}
, that is to say that all the shifts differences
f^{\prime}-f
,
f^{\prime},f\in F
have rational coordinates. Not all measurable tilings are rational; however, our main result below shows that all measurable tilings can be continuously deformed to a rational tiling, with the results particularly strong in the low dimensional cases
d=1,2
. More precisely, we have

Theorem 1.7

(Measurable tilings of a torus)
.

Let
d\geq 1
, and suppose that we have a measurable tiling
F\oplus A=_{\mathrm{a.e.}}\mathbb{T}^{d}
of the
d
-torus by some finite subset
F=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and some measurable subset
A
of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Then there exists a rational tiling
F^{0}\oplus A=_{\mathrm{a.e.}}\mathbb{T}^{d}
of the
d
-torus by some finite subset
F^{0}=\{f_{1}^{0},\dots,f_{n}^{0}\}
of
\mathbb{Q}^{d}
, obeying the following additional properties:

(i)

If we define the velocities
v_{i}\coloneqq f_{i}-f_{i}^{0}
for
i=1,\dots,n
and the sets
F^{t}=\{f_{1}^{0}+tv_{1},\dots,f_{n}^{0}+tv_{n}\}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then we have
F^{t}\oplus A=_{\mathrm{a.e.}}\mathbb{T}^{d}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, one can continuously (and linearly) deform the original tile set
F=F^{1}
to the rational tile set
F^{0}
while retaining the measurable tiling property throughout.

(ii)

If
d=2
and we impose the normalization
0\in F
, then all the velocities
v_{i}
are scalar multiples
v_{i}=\alpha_{i}v
of a single vector
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
for some real numbers
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
. Furthermore, we can partition
F
into subsets
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}
such that for each
1\leq j\leq k
, the elements of
F_{j}
have the same velocity (thus
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i^{\prime}}
whenever
f_{i},f_{i^{\prime}}\in F_{j}
), and the set
F_{j}\oplus A\coloneqq\{f_{i}+a:f_{i}\in F_{j},a\in A\}
is
\mathbb{R}v
-invariant in the sense that
tv+F_{j}\oplus A=_{\mathrm{a.e.}}F_{j}\oplus A
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(iii)

If the hypotheses are as in (ii), and furthermore the tile
A
is open and connected, then we can furthermore assume that either all the velocities
v_{i}
vanish (so in particular
F=F^{0}
is rational), or else for each
1\leq j\leq m
, the set
F_{j}\hbox{ mod }\mathbb{Z}^{2}
lies in a coset of
\mathbb{R}v\hbox{ mod }\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and all the
F_{j}
have the same cardinality.

(iv)

If
d=1
, then
F
is rational; in other words, we have
F=F^{0}+v
for some
v\in\mathbb{R}
.

(Initial properties)

We begin with (i). From

{\bf 1}_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}={\bf 1}_{A}+\sum_{f\in F\backslash\{0\}}\varphi_{f}

Remark 5.3

Using

First proof.

The two-dimensional connected case

Now we can prove

. For each

On integrating out the horizontal variable we have

(28)

{\bf 1}_{I_{i,j}}=_{\mathrm{a.e.}}{\bf 1}_{\pi(F_{i,j})}*\psi

Note that as

\psi
is supported on some interval supported inside some translate of

, and we can lift

Remark 5.7

The hypothesis that

Shimura operators for certain Hermitian symmetric superpairs
Songhao Zhu

Abstract
We give a partial super analog of a result obtained by Sahi and Zhang relating Shimura operators and certain interpolation symmetric polynomials. In particular, we study the pair ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ​ p | 2 ​ q ) , 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( p | q ) ⊕ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( p | q ) ) (\mathfrak{gl}(2p|2q),\mathfrak{gl}(p|q)\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(p|q)) , define the Shimura operators in 𝔘 ​ ( 𝔤 ) 𝔨 \mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{k}} , and using a new method, prove that their images under the Harish-Chandra homomorphism are proportional to Sergeev and Veselov’s Type B ​ C BC interpolation supersymmetric polynomials under the assumption that a family of irreducible 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -modules are spherical. We prove this conjecture using the notion of quasi-sphericity for Kac modules when p = q = 1 p=q=1 , and give explicit coordinates of (quasi-)spherical vectors.

Introduction

In

These eigenvalues can be expressed in terms of the images of these operators under the Harish-Chandra homomorphism, and Shimura’s problem was solved in

where it was shown that such images are specializations of the interpolation polynomials of Type

introduced by Okounkov

In this paper, we propose the super analog (Theorem

-sphericity of certain finite dimensional irreducible

-modules

Lemma 3.7

Let

Proposition 3.9
.

Let

{\Gamma}:\mathfrak{U}^{\mathfrak{k}}(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{a})\cong\mathfrak{P}(\mathfrak{a}^{*})

4
Spherical Representations

We study finite dimensional irreducible highest weight modules of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}(2p|2q)
which are
\mathfrak{k}
-spherical for
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{gl}(p|q)\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(p|q)
. In this section, we discuss generalities, specialize the criteria in
[
2
]
, and prove Theorem
B
in the end.

4.1
Generalities

We first present certain general statements regarding spherical representations.
Let
\mathfrak{G}
be a Lie (super)algebra admitting the Iwasawa decomposition
\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{K}\oplus\mathfrak{A}\oplus\mathfrak{N}
. Here
\mathfrak{K}
is the subalgebra fixed by the Cartan involution
\theta
,
\mathfrak{A}\subseteq\mathfrak{P}_{\overline{0}}
is maximal toral where
\mathfrak{P}
is the
(-1)
-eigenspace of
\theta
, and
\mathfrak{N}
is a nilpotent subalgebra.
Recall that for a
\mathfrak{G}
-module
V
, if
V^{\mathfrak{K}}:=\{v\in V:X.v=0\textup{ for all }X\in\mathfrak{K}\}\neq\{0\}
, then
V
is called (
\mathfrak{K}
-)spherical.
We denote
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{G})
as
\mathfrak{U}
, the
\mathfrak{K}
-centralizer of
\mathfrak{U}
as
\mathfrak{U}^{\mathfrak{K}}
, and the set of weights of a module
V
as
\mathrm{Supp}\left(V\right)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
V
is a finite dimensional and irreducible
\mathfrak{G}
-module, then
\dim V^{\mathfrak{K}}\leq 1
.

Proof 4.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}=\mathfrak{H}\oplus\mathfrak{N}
be a Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{G}
where
\mathfrak{H}\supseteq\mathfrak{A}
is a
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra. Denote the
\mathfrak{B}
-highest weight vector of
V
by
v
. Suppose on the contrary that there are two linearly independent spherical
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
in
V^{\mathfrak{K}}
. Then we may find a non-zero linear combination
w=c_{1}v_{1}+c_{2}v_{2}
when expressed as a combination of weight vectors, the coefficient of
v
is 0. Then the submodule

\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})w=\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{N}^{-})\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{A})\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{K})w

does not contain
v
and is thus proper, contradicting the irreducibility of
V
.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
V
is a
\mathfrak{G}
-module with
\dim V^{\mathfrak{K}}=1
, then
u\in\mathfrak{U}^{\mathfrak{K}}
acts by a scalar on
V^{\mathfrak{K}}
.

Proof 4.4
.

If
v\in V^{\mathfrak{K}}
, then
X.(u.v)=(-1)^{|u||X|}u.(X.v)=0
for any homogeneous
X\in\mathfrak{K}
and
u\in\mathfrak{U}^{\mathfrak{K}}
.

A
\mathfrak{G}
-module is said to be
cospherical
if
V^{*}
is spherical. Equivalently, there exists a non-zero functional on
V
such that
\mathfrak{K}
acts by 0 contragrediently. We assume that
\mathfrak{H}
is an even Cartan subalgebra containing
\mathfrak{A}
, and is stable under
\theta
which defines the superpair
(\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{K})
. Let
\mathfrak{T}=\mathfrak{H}\cap\mathfrak{K}
. With slight abuse of notation,
\theta
also denotes the involution on
\mathfrak{H}^{*}
induced from
\theta
.

Let
U_{\lambda}
denote a finite dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{G}
-module of highest weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{H}^{*}
with respect to a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{B}
containing
\mathfrak{H}
and
\mathfrak{N}
. Let
v\in U_{\lambda}
be a non-zero highest weight vector.

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose
U_{\lambda}
is cospherical and
\kappa\in(U_{\lambda}^{*})^{\mathfrak{K}}
is non-zero. Then the canonical pairing
\langle v,\kappa\rangle
is non-zero.

Proof 4.6
.

We have
\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{K}+\mathfrak{B}
from the Iwasawa decomposition.
We prove the claim by contradiction. Suppose
\langle v,\kappa\rangle=0
. Consider
\langle u.v,\kappa\rangle
for any
u\in\mathfrak{U}
. By the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem, we write
u=u_{K}u_{B}
with
u_{K}\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{K}),u_{B}\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{B})
. Note that
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{B})
acts on
v
via a character and so does
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{K})
on
\kappa
.
Then

\langle u.v,\kappa\rangle=\langle u_{K}u_{B}.v,\kappa\rangle=c\langle u_{K}.v,\kappa\rangle

where
c
is a scalar determined by
u_{B}
. Notice
u_{K}\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{K})=\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathfrak{K}\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{K})
.
For any
u\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{K})
and
K\in\mathfrak{K}
, we have
\langle Ku.v,\kappa\rangle=0
. Therefore, we get
\langle u.v,\kappa\rangle=cd\langle v,\kappa\rangle
for another scalar
d
determined by
u_{B}
.
Thus,
\kappa
vanishes on
\mathfrak{U}.v=U_{\lambda}
which implies
\kappa=0
, a contradiction.

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose
U_{\lambda}
is cospherical. Then
\lambda|_{\mathfrak{T}}=0
. That is,
\theta\lambda=-\lambda
.

Proof 4.8
.

Let
\kappa
be cospherical on
U_{\lambda}
and
v\in U_{\lambda}
be highest. Let
t\in\mathfrak{T}
. Then
\langle t.v,\kappa\rangle=\lambda(t)\langle v,\kappa\rangle
. On the other hand,
\langle t.v,\kappa\rangle=-\langle v,t.\kappa\rangle=0
since
\mathfrak{T}\subseteq\mathfrak{K}
. By Proposition
4.5
,
\lambda(t)=0
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Suppose
U_{\lambda}
is cospherical, then
U_{\lambda}
is also spherical. Thus sphericity and cosphericity are equivalent for
U_{\lambda}
.

Proof 4.10
.

Let
U_{\lambda}^{\theta}
be the
\mathfrak{G}
-module with the same vector (super)space as
U_{\lambda}
but the representation map be pre-composed by
\theta
.
Then as
\mathfrak{K}
-modules,
U_{\lambda}^{\theta}\cong U_{\lambda}
since
\theta
fixes
\mathfrak{K}
. Thus
(U_{\lambda}^{\theta})^{\mathfrak{K}}\cong U_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{K}}
.
We also see that
\mathrm{Supp}\left(U_{\lambda}^{*}\right)=\{-\mu:\mu\in\mathrm{Supp}\left(U_{\lambda}\right)\}
. By Lemma
4.7
, we get
\theta\lambda=-\lambda
, and this implies
U_{\lambda}^{*}\cong U_{\lambda}^{\theta}
. Then as
(U_{\lambda}^{*})^{\mathfrak{K}}
is non-zero,
(U_{\lambda}^{\theta})^{\mathfrak{K}}\cong U_{\lambda}^{\mathfrak{K}}
is also non-zero. We refer to
[
29
]
*Proposition 6.4.2 for a similar construction and argument.

4.2

Recall in the Cheng–Wang decomposition (Proposition
3.1
), each component

has the following highest weight with respect to the Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}^{\opp}\oplus\mathfrak{b}^{\std}

-\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}\left(\epsilon_{i}^{+}-\epsilon_{i}^{-}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{q}\langle\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-p\rangle(\delta_{j}^{+}-\delta_{j}^{-})=-\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{i}^{\mathrm{B}}-\sum_{j=1}^{q}\langle\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-p\rangle\gamma_{j}^{\mathrm{F}}\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}.

(31)

Let us denote the above weight as
-\lambda^{\natural}
by a slight abuse of notation.
Recall
\mathfrak{b}_{\natural}^{-}=\mathfrak{b}^{\opp}\oplus\mathfrak{b}^{\std}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{-}
from (
25
).
We are interested in
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
V_{\lambda}:=V(-\lambda^{\natural},\mathfrak{b}_{\natural}^{-})
and study which of them are
\mathfrak{k}
-spherical.
We show that
V_{\lambda}
are always finite dimensional using the results in Section

.

We first introduce some notation. For
\mathfrak{gl}(2p|2q)
, any highest weight in the form of

\sum x_{i}^{\pm}\epsilon_{i}^{\pm}+\sum y_{j}^{\pm}\delta_{j}^{\pm}.

are specified by (1) the coefficients
x_{i}^{\pm},y_{j}^{\pm}
, and (2) the order of
\epsilon^{\pm}
and
\delta^{\pm}
when describing the Borel subalgebra. We write the above weight as

(\dots,\overset{\bullet}{x_{i}^{\pm}},\dots,\overset{\times}{y_{j}^{\pm}},\dots)

where the coefficients are in the order of the
\epsilon\delta
-chain and we put a dot
\bullet
(respectively a cross
\times
) over a coefficient of an
\epsilon_{i}^{\pm}
(respectively
\delta_{j}^{\pm}
). Odd reflections only swap a
\bullet
with a
\times
.
As Borel subalgebras are conjugate under the usual (even) reflections, swapping a
\bullet
with a
\bullet
or a
\times
with a
\times
will not change the order of these coefficients.

In this notation, if we take
\alpha=\delta^{\pm}_{j}-\epsilon^{\pm}_{i}
in Theorem
2.2
, we then have the following rule.
Let
\mathfrak{b}^{(1)}
and
\mathfrak{b}^{(2)}
be adjacent. If the
\mathfrak{b}^{(1)}
-highest weight
\lambda^{(1)}
is given by

(\cdots,\overset{\times}{x}|\overset{\bullet}{y},\cdots)
where
x,y
are the entries where the odd reflection is applied, then the
\mathfrak{b}^{(2)}
-highest weight
\lambda^{(2)}
is given by

1.

(\cdots,\overset{\bullet}{y}|\overset{\times}{x},\cdots)
if
x=-y
(i.e.
(\lambda,\alpha)=0
), or

2.

(\cdots,\overset{\bullet}{y+1}|\overset{\times}{x-1},\cdots)
if
x\neq-y
(i.e.
(\lambda,\alpha)\neq 0
).

By Theorem
2.2
, an odd reflection applied on a highest weight produce another highest weight with respect to the new Borel subalgebra. Therefore, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
(\overset{\times}{x},\overset{\times}{y}|\overset{\bullet}{z})
be with
x\geq y
.
If
(\overset{\bullet}{u}|\overset{\times}{v},\overset{\times}{w})
is the result of applying the two odd reflections that switch
z
with
y
and then
x
, then
v\geq w
.

Theorem 4.12
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(p,q)
,
V_{\lambda}
is finite dimensional.

Proof 4.13
.

In view of Lemma
2.3
, we may find certain Borel subalgebras (via odd reflections) so that any even simple root is simple in some Borel subalgebra.
If each corresponding
\lambda^{\alpha}
is dominant as in Theorem

, then we have the desired result.

Recall the Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}_{\natural}^{-}
of
\mathfrak{g}
corresponds to the following chain (

[\epsilon_{1}^{-}\cdots\epsilon_{p}^{-}|\delta_{1}^{-}\cdots\delta_{q}^{-}\delta_{q}^{+}\cdots\delta_{1}^{+}|\epsilon_{p}^{+}\cdots\epsilon_{1}^{+}],

and all the simple roots of
(\mathfrak{b}_{\natural}^{-})_{\overline{0}}
are
\epsilon_{1}^{-}-\epsilon_{2}^{-},\dots,\epsilon_{p}^{-}-\epsilon_{p}^{+},\epsilon_{p}^{+}-\epsilon_{p-1}^{+},\dots,\epsilon_{2}^{+}-\epsilon_{1}^{+}
and
\delta_{1}^{-}-\delta_{2}^{-},\dots,\delta_{q}^{-}-\delta_{q}^{+},\delta_{q}^{+}-\delta_{q-1}^{+},\dots,\delta_{2}^{+}-\delta_{1}^{+}
. In the above chain, the only missing even simple root is
\epsilon_{p}^{-}-\epsilon_{p}^{+}
, which is present in the following chain:

[\epsilon_{1}^{-}\cdots\epsilon_{p}^{-}\epsilon_{p}^{+}|\delta_{1}^{-}\cdots\delta_{1}^{+}|\epsilon_{p-1}^{+}\cdots\epsilon_{1}^{+}].

(32)

Let us denote the Borel subalgebra and the highest weight with respect to (

\mathfrak{b}^{\sharp}
and
\lambda^{\sharp}
respectively.
We may choose the form
(-,-)
on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
to be induced from the supertrace form on
\mathfrak{t}
. Then the
\mathfrak{b}
-dominance condition in Section
2
just means the integral coefficients of adjacent
\epsilon^{\pm}
(respectively
\delta^{\pm}
) are non-increasing. Note there are no odd and non-isotropic roots in this case.

Clearly, the original
-\lambda^{\natural}
is
\mathfrak{b}_{\natural}^{-}
-dominant. The question is now whether or not
\lambda^{\sharp}
is
\mathfrak{b}^{\sharp}
-dominant. By Theorem
2.2
, we can obtain
\lambda^{\sharp}
by a sequence of odd reflections performed on:

-\lambda^{\natural}=(\overset{\bullet}{\lambda_{1}},\dots,\overset{\bullet}{\lambda_{p}}|\overset{\times}{\nu_{1}},\dots\overset{\times}{\nu_{q}},\overset{\times}{-\nu_{q}},\dots,\overset{\times}{-\nu_{1}}|\overset{\bullet}{-\lambda_{p}},\dots,\overset{\bullet}{-\lambda_{1}})

where
\nu_{j}=\langle\lambda^{\prime}_{j}-p\rangle
for
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(p,q)
. Denote the number of strictly positive entries in
(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{q})
as
l
. Then
\lambda_{p}\geq l
as
\lambda_{p}\geq q\geq\lambda_{p+1}=l
.

Denote
\lambda^{\sharp}=(\overset{\bullet}{\lambda_{1}},\dots,\overset{\bullet}{\lambda_{p}},\overset{\bullet}{\tau_{1}}|\overset{\times}{\sigma_{1}},\dots,\overset{\times}{\sigma_{2q}}|\overset{\bullet}{-\lambda_{p-1}},\dots,\overset{\bullet}{-\lambda_{1}})
. By Lemma
4.11

\sigma_{i}\geq\sigma_{j}
for any

by induction. Therefore, we only need to prove
\lambda_{p}\geq\tau_{1}
for
\mathfrak{b}^{\sharp}
-dominance.

Case (i) Suppose
\lambda_{p}\geq q
, then the sequence of
2q
odd reflections performed on
-\overset{\bullet}{\lambda_{p}}
adds at most
2q
to
-\lambda_{p}
and we have
\tau_{1}\leq-\lambda_{p}+2q\leq q\leq\lambda_{p}
.

Case (ii) Otherwise
q>\lambda_{p}\geq l
and 0 occurs in
(\dots,|\overset{\times}{\nu_{1}},\dots,\overset{\times}{-\nu_{1}},|\dots)
, and

-\lambda^{\natural}=(\dots,\overset{\bullet}{\lambda_{p}}|\overset{\times}{\nu_{1}},\dots\overset{\times}{\nu_{l}},\underbrace{\overset{\times}{0},\dots,\overset{\times}{0}}_{q-l},\underbrace{\overset{\times}{0},\dots,\overset{\times}{0}}_{q-l},\overset{\times}{-\nu_{l}},\dots,\overset{\times}{-\nu_{1}}|-\overset{\bullet}{\lambda_{p}},\dots).

We claim that
\tau_{1}=l
. To get
{\tau_{1}}
, we first push
-\overset{\bullet}{\lambda_{p}}
through
\overset{\times}{-\nu_{l}},\dots,\overset{\times}{-\nu_{1}}
. Since all of these numbers are negative, each of the
l
odd reflections adds 1 to
\overset{\bullet}{-\lambda_{p}}
, after which we get

(\dots,\overset{\bullet}{\lambda_{p}}|\overset{\times}{\nu_{1}},\dots\overset{\times}{\nu_{l}},\underbrace{\overset{\times}{0},\dots,\overset{\times}{0}}_{q-l},\underbrace{\overset{\times}{0},\dots,\overset{\times}{0}}_{q-l}|\overset{\bullet}{-\lambda_{p}+l}|\overset{\times}{-\nu_{l}-1},\dots,\overset{\times}{-\nu_{1}-1}|\overset{\bullet}{-\lambda_{p-1}},\dots).

Notice
-\lambda_{p}+l\leq 0
by assumption.
Now as we keep applying the odd reflections,
\overset{\bullet}{-\lambda_{p}+l}
will encounter the
2(q-l)
zeros.
But
q-l>\lambda_{p}-l
by the assumption. This means there are more than enough zeros to make
-\lambda_{p}+l
zero, since each time we have
(\dots,\overset{\times}{0}|\overset{\bullet}{x},\dots)
with
x<0
, the odd reflection adds 1 to
x
.
If
x
becomes 0, then the odd reflections just flip
\bullet
with
\times
after which we have the following:

(\dots,\overset{\times}{\nu_{1}},\dots\overset{\times}{\nu_{l}}|\overset{\bullet}{0}|\overset{\times}{0},\dots,\overset{\times}{-1},\overset{\times}{-\nu_{l}-1},\dots).

Finally, we apply
l
more odd reflections to push
\overset{\bullet}{0}
through the
\times
sequence to the left. Each reflection adds 1 to it. This proves
\tau_{1}=l\leq\lambda_{p}
.

Therefore, we always have
\lambda_{p}\geq\tau_{1}
, and
\lambda^{\sharp}
is
\mathfrak{b}^{\sharp}
-dominant.

4.3
The High Enough Constraints

We now specialize what the high enough condition (see Section
2
) means in our case.
By Proposition

, the highest weight of

on
\mathfrak{h}
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}^{-}
(see (

-2\lambda_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\natural}:=-\sum_{i=1}^{p}2\lambda_{i}\alpha_{i}^{\mathrm{B}}-\sum_{j=1}^{q}2\langle\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-p\rangle\alpha_{j}^{\mathrm{F}}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(p,q)
and
V_{\lambda}
be as above. If
\lambda_{p}>\langle\lambda_{1}^{\prime}-p\rangle
,
then

is spherical.

Proof 4.15
.

Let us apply the sphericity condition given in Theorem

which prescribes the following:

1.

\frac{(-2{\lambda^{\natural}},\alpha)}{\left(\alpha,\alpha\right)}\in\mathbb{N}
where
\alpha\in\Sigma^{-}_{\overline{0}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
(note that we picked
\mathfrak{b}^{-}
here),

2.

(-2{\lambda^{\natural}},\beta)>0
for any isotropic
\beta\in\Sigma^{-}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
.

Note that
-2\lambda_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\natural}|_{\mathfrak{t}_{+}}=0
as
\mathfrak{t}_{+}=\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{k}
. The root data in Section
3
shows:

\Sigma^{-}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})=\left\{-\alpha_{j}^{\mathrm{B}}-\alpha_{k}^{\mathrm{B}},-\alpha_{j}^{\mathrm{F}}-\alpha_{k}^{\mathrm{F}}\right\}\sqcup\left\{-\alpha_{j}^{\mathrm{B}}+\alpha_{k}^{\mathrm{B}},-\alpha_{j}^{\mathrm{F}}+\alpha_{k}^{\mathrm{F}}:j<k\right\}\sqcup\left\{-\alpha_{j}^{\mathrm{B}}\mp\alpha_{k}^{\mathrm{F}}\right\}.

Take
\alpha=-2\alpha_{j}^{\mathrm{B}},-2\alpha_{j}^{\mathrm{F}},-\alpha_{j}^{\mathrm{B/F}}\mp\alpha_{k}^{\mathrm{B/F}}
respectively. Then Condition (1) gives

\displaystyle\frac{(-2{\lambda^{\natural}},-2\alpha_{j}^{\mathrm{B}})}{4}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j}\geq 0,\;

\displaystyle\frac{(-2{\lambda^{\natural}},-\alpha_{j}^{\mathrm{B}}\mp\alpha_{k}^{\mathrm{B}})}{2}=\lambda_{j}\pm\lambda_{k}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\frac{(-2{\lambda^{\natural}},-2\alpha_{j}^{\mathrm{F}})}{-4}

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-p\rangle\geq 0,\;

\displaystyle\frac{(-2{\lambda^{\natural}},-\alpha_{j}^{\mathrm{F}}\mp\alpha_{k}^{\mathrm{F}})}{-2}=\langle\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-p\rangle\pm\langle\lambda_{k}^{\prime}-p\rangle\geq 0,

by (

) and (

), and all of them are vacuously true for

.

As for Condition (2), we write

(-2{\lambda^{\natural}},-\alpha_{j}^{\mathrm{B}}\mp\alpha_{k}^{\mathrm{F}})=2\lambda_{j}\mp 2\langle\lambda_{k}^{\prime}-p\rangle>0

for any
j,k
which implies
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{p}>\langle\lambda_{1}^{\prime}-p\rangle\geq\cdots\geq\langle\lambda_{q}^{\prime}-p\rangle\geq 0
.

Note this is only a
sufficient
condition for sphericity.
In particular, we see that
such a partition must have a
p
-th part
. Clearly, the trivial representation, albeit spherical, is not high enough. We would like to point out that the above condition is insufficient to show the vanishing properties for proving Theorem

. See Remark

for an example.
This means a new approach is needed.

when

We use the following matrix to show our choice of coordinates:

\begin{pmatrix}\ast&X_{1}&\eta_{11}&\eta_{12}\\

For a non-empty (1, 1)-hook partition

-\lambda^{\natural}=(a|b,-b|-a)=a(\epsilon^{-}-\epsilon^{+})+b(\delta^{-}-\delta^{+})

\mathfrak{b}_{\natural}^{-}

[\epsilon^{-}|\delta^{-}\delta^{+}|\epsilon^{+}]

We apply two odd reflections (swapping
\delta^{+}
with
\epsilon^{+}
and then
\delta^{-}
with
\epsilon^{+}

[\epsilon^{-}\epsilon^{+}|\delta^{-}\delta^{+}]
which is conjugate to the standard Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}^{\std}
(corresponding to
[\epsilon^{+}\epsilon^{-}|\delta^{+}\delta^{-}]

\displaystyle\breve{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(a,-a+2|b-1,-b-1)&\text{ if }b\neq a-1\\

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}a\epsilon^{+}+(-a+2)\epsilon^{-}+(b-1)\delta^{+}+(-b-1)\delta^{-}&\text{ if }b\neq a-1\\

(35)

Thus

.
The advantage of using

(which is

using an irreducible highest weight

Then

is its irreducible quotient.

is

instead

Let us begin with the simplest example where

is given by a single box

. This is the second case in (

Proposition 4.16
.

Following the above notation,
V
is spherical, while
K
is not.

Proof 4.17
.

The claim that

is spherical follows from Proposition

.
A more direct way to see this is to observe that

since

is simple as a Lie superalgebra and as a

-module. Its highest weight is

as a quotient of the adjoint representation of

.
Then
\mathfrak{k}
acts trivially on the equivalent class of

in
\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)

and

have weights

and

respectively under the adjoint action; the two

have opposite weights. All the basis vectors in

act as 0 on

We show that

is not spherical by way of contradiction. Suppose a spherical vector

exists, where

are some constants. Then

and

and

The contrast between the two modules reveals an important fact: the Kac module

must have a non-zero vector that descends to its irreducible quotient
V
such that the image is spherical.
This motivates the following definition of a
quasi-spherical vector
.

{Definition}

Let
\mathfrak{G}
and
\mathfrak{K}
be two Lie (super)algebras such that
\mathfrak{K}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}
.
Let
U
be a
\mathfrak{G}
-module with a unique maximal submodule
M
. A non-zero vector

is said to be
\mathfrak{K}
-
quasi-spherical
if
\mathfrak{K}.v\subseteq M
and
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{G}).v=U
, and
U
is said to be
\mathfrak{K}
-
quasi-spherical
.

We suppress “
\mathfrak{K}
-” if it is clear from the context.
If
U
is a highest weight module, then
U
has unique maximal submodule. If
v\in U
is cyclic (
\mathfrak{U}.v=U
), then it descends to the unique irreducible quotient of
U
. If we want to show that
u\in U
is in a proper submodule, it suffices to prove that
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{G}).u
does not contain any cyclic vectors. Note that a quasi-spherical vector need not be cyclic.

We first prove that for typical
\breve{\lambda}
(

in (

is spherical (so trivially quasi-spherical).

We show that

\displaystyle\eta_{11}\xi_{11}\xi_{22}

\displaystyle=\langle 1010\rangle\xi_{22}+\xi_{11}\xi_{22}\eta_{11}

(36)

\displaystyle\eta_{22}\xi_{11}\xi_{22}

\displaystyle=-\xi_{11}\eta_{22}\xi_{22}

\displaystyle\equiv-\xi_{11}\langle 0101\rangle.

Note that the Weyl vector

The following discussion is needed for the second case.

Now we let

. Thus
F
satisfying Condition (i) is equivalent to

Condition (ii’) (specified from (ii) to our root system) says
F|_{\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}}(X)=F|_{\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}}(X+\alpha)
for any
\alpha\in{\Sigma^{\texttt{iso}}}
. The
W_{0}\ltimes{\mathcal{S}^{\texttt{iso}}}

F|_{\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}}(\sigma X+\alpha)

\sigma=1

F

X\in\mathbb{H}_{\sigma^{-1}\alpha}

F|_{\mathbb{H}_{\sigma^{-1}(\alpha)}}(X)=F|_{\mathbb{H}_{\sigma^{-1}(\alpha)}}(X+\sigma^{-1}(\alpha))

F|_{\mathbb{H}_{(\alpha)}}(\sigma X+\alpha)
by Condition (i).

Thus,
F\in\Lambda^{\mathtt{0}}(\mathfrak{a}^{*})

F\in\mathfrak{P}(\mathfrak{a}^{*})^{\mathfrak{W}}

Note on the Theorem of Balog, Szemerédi, and Gowers
Christian Reiher, Tomasz Schoen

Abstract
. We prove that every additive set A A with energy E ⁡ ( A ) ≥ | A | 3 / K E(A)\geq|A|^{3}/K has a subset A ′ ⊆ A A^{\prime}\subseteq A of size | A ′ | ≥ ( 1 − ε ) K − 1 / 2 | A | |A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\varepsilon)K^{-1/2}|A| such that | A ′ − A ′ | ≤ O ε ​ ( K 4 ​ | A ′ | ) |A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}|\leq O_{\varepsilon}(K^{4}|A^{\prime}|) . This is, essentially, the largest structured set one can get in the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem.



1.
Introduction

An
additive set
is a nonempty finite subset of an abelian group.
The
energy
of an additive set
A
is defined to be
the number
E(A)
of quadruples
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})\in A^{4}
solving the
equation
a_{1}+a_{2}=a_{3}+a_{4}
.
An easy counting argument shows

(1.1)

E(A)=\sum_{d\in A-A}r_{A-A}(d)^{2}\,,

where
r_{A-A}(d)
indicates the number of representations of
d
as a difference
of two members of
A
. So the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields
E(A)\geq|A|^{4}/|A-A|

and, in particular, every additive set
A
with small difference set
A-A
contains
a lot of energy.
In the converse direction Balog and Szemerédi
[
1
]
proved that large energy
implies the existence of a substantial subset whose difference set is small. After
several quantitative improvements (see e.g., Gowers
[
3
]
and Balog
[
2
]
)
the hitherto best version of this result was obtained by the second author
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Given a real
K\geq 1
every additive set
A
with energy
E(A)\geq|A|^{3}/K

has a subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size
|A^{\prime}|\geq\Omega(|A|/K)

such that
|A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}|\leq O(K^{4}|A^{\prime}|)
. ∎

When investigating the question how a quantitatively optimal version of this
result might read, there are two different directions one may wish to pursue.
First, there is the obvious problem whether the exponent
4
can be replaced
by some smaller number. Second, one may try to find “the largest” set

A^{\prime}\subseteq A
such that
|A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}|\leq O_{K}(|A^{\prime}|)
holds. As the following
example demonstrates, there is no absolute constant
\varepsilon_{\star}>0

such that
|A^{\prime}|\geq(1+\varepsilon_{\star})K^{-1/2}|A|
can be achieved in general.

Fix an arbitrary natural number
n
. For a very large finite abelian group
G

we consider the additive set

A=\bigl\{(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})\in G^{n}\colon\text{ there is at most one index $i$
such that $g_{i}\neq 0$}\bigr\}

whose ambient group is
G^{n}
. Obviously we have

|A|=|G|n+O_{n}(1)\quad\text{ and }\quad E(A)=|A|^{3}/n^{2}+O_{n}(|A|^{2})\,,

so the real number
K
satisfying
E(A)=|A|^{3}/K
is roughly
n^{2}
.
However, every
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size
|A^{\prime}|\geq(1+\varepsilon)|G|
satisfies

|A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon^{2}|G|^{2}
.
Our main result implies that this is, in some sense, already the worst example.
More precisely, for every fixed
\varepsilon>0
the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem
holds with
|A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\varepsilon)K^{-1/2}|A|
. Perhaps surprisingly, we can also
reproduce the best known factor
K^{4}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Given real numbers
K\geq 1
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, and an additive set
A

with energy
E(A)\geq|A|^{3}/K
there is a subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
such that

|A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\varepsilon)K^{-1/2}|A|\quad\text{ and }\quad|A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}|\leq 2^{33}\varepsilon^{-9}K^{4}|A^{\prime}|=O_{\varepsilon}(K^{4}|A^{\prime}|)\,.

Our proof has two main cases and in one of them (see Lemma
3.1
below)
we even get the stronger bound
|A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}|\leq O_{\varepsilon}(K^{3}|A^{\prime}|)
. It would be interesting
to prove this in the second case as well.
Using examples of the form
A=\{x\in{\mathds{Z}}^{d}\colon\|x\|\leq R\}
one can show that
the exponent
4
cannot be replaced by any number smaller than

\log(4)/\log(27/16)\approx 2.649
(see
[
5
]
).

2.
Preliminaries

This section discusses two auxiliary results we shall require for the proof
of Theorem
1.2
.
The first of them is similar to
[
6
]
*Lemma 6.19.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\delta,\xi\in(0,1]
and
R\subseteq A^{2}
denotes a binary relation
on a set
A
such that
|R|\geq\delta|A|^{2}
, then there is a set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A

of size
|A^{\prime}|\geq\delta(1-\xi)|A|
which possesses the following property:
For every pair
(a_{1},a_{2})\in A^{\prime 2}
there are at least
2^{-7}\delta^{4}\xi^{4}|A|^{2}|A^{\prime}|

triples
(x,b,y)\in A^{3}
such that
(a_{1},x),(b,x),(b,y),(a_{2},y)\in R
.

Proof.

Set
N(x)=\{a\in A\colon(a,x)\in R\}
for every
x\in A
.
Since
\sum_{x\in A}|N(x)|=|R|\geq\delta|A|^{2}
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

(2.1)

\sum_{x\in A}|N(x)|^{2}\geq\delta^{2}|A|^{3}\,.

Setting
K(a,a^{\prime})=\{x\in A\colon a,a^{\prime}\in N(x)\}
for every pair
(a,a^{\prime})\in A^{2}

and

\displaystyle\Omega=\bigl\{(a,a^{\prime})\in A^{2}\colon|K(a,a^{\prime})|\leq\delta^{2}\xi^{2}|A|/8\bigr\}

a double counting argument yields

\sum_{x\in A}|N(x)^{2}\cap\Omega|=\sum_{(a,a^{\prime})\in\Omega}|K(a,a^{\prime})|\leq\delta^{2}\xi^{2}|A||\Omega|/8\leq\delta^{2}\xi^{2}|A|^{3}/8\,.

Together with (
2.1
) we obtain

\sum_{x\in A}\bigl(|N(x)|^{2}-8\xi^{-1}|N(x)^{2}\cap\Omega|\bigr)\geq\delta^{2}(1-\xi)|A|^{3}

and, hence, there exists some
x_{\star}\in A
such that the set
A_{\star}=N(x_{\star})

satisfies

(2.2)

|A_{\star}|^{2}-8\xi^{-1}|A_{\star}^{2}\cap\Omega|\geq\delta^{2}(1-\xi)|A|^{2}\,.

We shall prove that the set

A^{\prime}=\{a\in A_{\star}\colon\text{ the number of all $a^{\prime}\in A_{\star}$
with $(a,a^{\prime})\in\Omega$ is at most $|A_{\star}|/4$}\}

has all required properties.
By (
2.2
) we have

|A_{\star}\smallsetminus A^{\prime}||A_{\star}|/4\leq|A_{\star}^{2}\cap\Omega|\leq\xi|A_{\star}|^{2}/8\,,

for which reason

|A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\xi/2)|A_{\star}|\geq(1-\xi)^{1/2}|A_{\star}|\overset{\eqref{eq:4819}}{\geq}\delta(1-\xi)|A|\,,

meaning that
A^{\prime}
is indeed sufficiently large. To conclude the proof we need to show

\sum_{b\in A}|K(a_{1},b)\times K(b,a_{2})|\geq 2^{-7}\delta^{4}\xi^{4}|A|^{2}|A^{\prime}|

for every pair
(a_{1},a_{2})\in A^{\prime 2}
. This follows from the fact that due to
the definition of
A^{\prime}
there are at least
|A_{\star}|/2
elements
b\in A_{\star}

such that the sets
K(a_{1},b)
and
K(b,a_{2})
both have at least the
size
\delta^{2}\xi^{2}|A|/8
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that the real numbers
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in[0,1]
do not vanish
simultaneously.
Denote their sum by
S
and the sum of their squares by
T
.
For every
\alpha\in(0,1)
there exists
a set
I\subseteq[n]
such that

\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}\geq\max\left\{\alpha T,\biggl(\frac{(1-\alpha)^{5}|I|^{4}T^{4}}{2^{10}S^{2}}\biggr)^{1/6}\right\}\,.

Proof.

For reasons of symmetry we may assume
x_{1}\geq\dots\geq x_{n}
.
Set
S_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}x_{j}
for every nonnegative
i\leq n
.
Due to
T\leq x_{1}S
and
x_{1}\leq 1
we have
T\leq S=S_{n}
and
thus there exists a smallest index
k\in[n]
satisfying

S_{k}\geq\alpha T
. Notice that

\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}x_{i}^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}x_{i}=S_{k-1}\leq\alpha T\,.

Moreover
x_{1}\geq T/S
implies the existence of a largest index
\ell

such that
x_{\ell}\geq(1-\alpha)T/(2S)
.
Due to

\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}\leq\frac{(1-\alpha)T}{2S}\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{n}x_{i}\leq\frac{(1-\alpha)T}{2}\,,

we have

(2.3)

\sum_{i=k}^{\ell}x_{i}^{2}\geq\frac{(1-\alpha)T}{2}\,,

whence, in particular,
\ell\geq k
. Next,

\ell\biggl(\frac{(1-\alpha)T}{2S}\biggr)^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}x_{i}^{2}\leq T

entails

(2.4)

(1-\alpha)^{2}\ell T\leq 4S^{2}\,.

Now assume for the sake of contradiction that our claim fails.
Every
i\in[k,\ell]
satisfies
S_{i}\geq S_{k}\geq\alpha T

and thus the failure of
I=[i]
discloses

S_{i}<\biggl(\frac{(1-\alpha)^{5}i^{4}T^{4}}{2^{10}S^{2}}\biggr)^{1/6}\,.

Combined with
ix_{i}\leq S_{i}
this entails

\sum_{i=k}^{\ell}x_{i}^{2}\leq\biggl(\frac{(1-\alpha)^{5}T^{4}}{2^{10}S^{2}}\biggr)^{1/3}\sum_{i=k}^{\ell}i^{-2/3}\,.

In view of (
2.3
) we are thus led to

\biggl(\frac{2^{7}S^{2}}{(1-\alpha)^{2}T}\biggr)^{1/3}\leq\sum_{i=k}^{\ell}i^{-2/3}\leq\int_{0}^{\ell}x^{-2/3}\mathrm{d}x=3\ell^{1/3}\,,

i.e.,
2^{7}S^{2}\leq 27(1-\alpha)^{2}\ell T
, which contradicts (
2.4
).
∎

3.
The proof of Theorem
1.2

Let us fix two real numbers
K\geq 1
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
as well as
an additive set
A
satisfying
E(A)\geq|A|^{3}/K
. We consider the partition

A-A=P\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}Q

defined by

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\bigl\{d\in A-A\colon r_{A-A}(d)\geq K^{-1/2}|A|\bigr\}

\displaystyle\text{ and }\quad Q

\displaystyle=\bigl\{d\in A-A\colon r_{A-A}(d)<K^{-1/2}|A|\bigr\}\,.

According to (
1.1
) at least one of the cases

(3.1)

\sum_{d\in P}r_{A-A}(d)^{2}\geq\frac{\varepsilon|A|^{3}}{4K}\quad\text{ or }\quad\sum_{d\in Q}r_{A-A}(d)^{2}\geq\frac{(4-\varepsilon)|A|^{3}}{4K}

needs to occur and we begin by analysing the left alternative.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\sum_{d\in P}r_{A-A}(d)^{2}\geq\varepsilon|A|^{3}/(4K)
, then there exists
a set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size
|A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\varepsilon)K^{-1/2}|A|
such that

|A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}|\leq 2^{10}\varepsilon^{-4}K^{3}|A^{\prime}|
.

Proof.

For every difference
d\in P
we set
A_{d}=A\cap(A+d)
. Due to
|A_{d}|=r_{A-A}(d)

the hypothesis implies

(3.2)

\sum_{d\in P}|A_{d}|^{2}\geq\varepsilon|A|^{3}/(4K)\,.

For every pair
(x,y)\in A^{2}
the set
L(x,y)=\{d\in P\colon x,y\in A_{d}\}

has at most the cardinality
|L(x,y)|\leq r_{A-A}(x-y)
, because every
difference
d\in L(x,y)
corresponds to its own representation
x-y=(x-d)-(y-d)
of

x-y
as a difference of two members of
A
. Applying this observation to all pairs
in

\Xi=\bigl\{(x,y)\in A^{2}\colon r_{A-A}(x-y)\leq\varepsilon^{2}|A|/(16K)\bigr\}

we obtain

\sum_{d\in P}|A_{d}^{2}\cap\Xi|=\sum_{(x,y)\in\Xi}|L(x,y)|\leq\sum_{(x,y)\in\Xi}r_{A-A}(x-y)\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}|A||\Xi|}{16K}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}|A|^{3}}{16K}\,.

Together with (
3.2
) this yields

\sum_{d\in P}\bigl(\varepsilon|A_{d}^{2}|-4|A_{d}^{2}\cap\Xi|\bigr)\geq 0

and, consequently, for some element
d(\star)\in P
the set

A_{\star}=A_{d(\star)}
satisfies
|A_{\star}^{2}\cap\Xi|\leq\varepsilon|A_{\star}|^{2}/4
.
We contend that the set

A^{\prime}=\bigl\{a\in A_{\star}\colon\text{ There are at most $|A_{\star}|/4$ pairs of
the form $(a,x)$ in $\Xi$}\bigr\}

has the required properties.
As in the proof of Lemma
2.1
we obtain

|A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\varepsilon)|A_{\star}|=(1-\varepsilon)r_{A-A}(d(\star))\geq(1-\varepsilon)K^{-1/2}|A|\,;

so it remains to derive the required upper bound on
|A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}|
.

To this end we consider an arbitrary pair
(a,a^{\prime})
of elements of
A^{\prime}
. Owing to
the definition of
A^{\prime}
there are at least
|A_{\star}|/2
elements
x\in A_{\star}

such that
(a,x)\not\in\Xi
and
(a^{\prime},x)\not\in\Xi
. For each of them we have

a-a^{\prime}=(a-x)-(a^{\prime}-x)
, there are at least
\varepsilon^{2}|A|/(16K)
pairs
(a_{1},a_{2})\in A^{2}

solving the equation
a-x=a_{1}-a_{2}
and at least the same number of pairs

(a_{3},a_{4})\in A^{2}
such that
a^{\prime}-x=a_{3}-a_{4}
. Altogether there are at least

\varepsilon^{4}|A|^{2}|A_{\star}|/(2^{9}K^{2})\geq 2^{-9}\varepsilon^{4}K^{-5/2}|A|^{3}

possibilities of writing
a-a^{\prime}=(a_{1}-a_{2})-(a_{3}-a_{4})
and for this reason we have

|A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}|\leq\frac{|A|^{4}}{2^{-9}\varepsilon^{4}K^{-5/2}|A|^{3}}=2^{9}\varepsilon^{-4}K^{5/2}|A|\leq 2^{10}\varepsilon^{-4}K^{3}|A^{\prime}|\,.\qed

We conclude the proof of Theorem
1.2
by taking care of the right case in (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\sum_{d\in Q}r_{A-A}(d)^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon/4)|A|^{3}/K
, then there is a
set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size
|A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\varepsilon)K^{-1/2}|A|
such that

|A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}|\leq 2^{33}\varepsilon^{-9}K^{4}|A^{\prime}|
.

Proof.

Let
Q=\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{|Q|}\}
enumerate
Q
. By the definition
of
Q
there are real numbers
x_{1},\ldots,x_{|Q|}\in[0,1]
such that

r_{A-A}(d_{i})=x_{i}K^{-1/2}|A|\quad\text{ holds for every }i\in[|Q|]\,.

Owing to
\sum_{d\in A-A}r_{A-A}(d)=|A|^{2}
and the hypothesis we have

\sum_{i=1}^{|Q|}x_{i}\leq K^{1/2}|A|\quad\text{ as well as }\quad\sum_{i=1}^{|Q|}x_{i}^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon/4)|A|\,.

By Lemma
2.2
applied with
\alpha=1-\varepsilon/4
there exist an index
set
I\subseteq[|Q|]
such that

(3.3)

\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}\geq\max\left\{(1-\varepsilon/2)|A|,\bigl(2^{-21}\varepsilon^{5}K^{-1}|I|^{4}|A|^{2}\bigr)^{1/6}\right\}\,.

Now we set
Q^{\prime}=\{d_{i}\colon i\in I\}
, consider the relation

R=\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in A^{2}\colon a_{1}-a_{2}\in Q^{\prime}\}

and define
\delta\in(0,1]
by
|R|=\delta|A|^{2}
.
Due to

\delta=|A|^{-2}\sum_{i\in I}r_{A-A}(d_{i})=\frac{1}{K^{1/2}|A|}\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}

the bounds in (
3.3
) imply both

(3.4)

\delta\geq(1-\varepsilon/2)K^{-1/2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{|I|^{4}}{\delta^{6}|A|^{4}}\leq 2^{21}\varepsilon^{-5}K^{4}\,.

By Lemma
2.1
applied to
\xi=\varepsilon/2
and
R
there exists a
set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size

|A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\varepsilon/2)\delta|A|\geq(1-\varepsilon)K^{-1/2}|A|

such that for every pair
(a_{1},a_{2})\in A^{\prime 2}
there are at least

2^{-11}\varepsilon^{4}\delta^{4}|A|^{2}|A^{\prime}|
triples
(x,b,y)\in A^{3}
with

(a_{1},x),(b,x),(b,y),(a_{2},y)\in R
.
Due to the equation

(a_{1}-a_{2})=(a_{1}-x)-(b-x)+(b-y)-(a_{2}-y)

this means that every difference
a_{1}-a_{2}\in A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}
has at least

2^{-11}\varepsilon^{4}\delta^{4}|A|^{2}|A^{\prime}|
representations of the form

q_{1}-q_{2}+q_{3}-q_{4}
with
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4}\in Q^{\prime}
, whence

|A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}|\leq\frac{|Q^{\prime}|^{4}}{2^{-11}\varepsilon^{4}\delta^{4}|A|^{2}|A^{\prime}|}\overset{\eqref{eq:3858}}{\leq}2^{32}\varepsilon^{-9}K^{4}(\delta|A|/|A^{\prime}|)^{2}|A^{\prime}|\,.

Due to
|A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\varepsilon/2)\delta|A|\geq\delta|A|/\sqrt{2}
the result follows.
∎

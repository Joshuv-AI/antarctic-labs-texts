Parameter estimation and long-range dependence of the fractional binomial process
Meena Sanjay Babulal ∗ a , Sunil Kumar Gauttam ∗ b and Aditya Maheshwari # c

Abstract
. In 1990, Jakeman (see [ 21 ] ) defined the binomial process as a special case of the classical birth-death process, where the probability of birth is proportional to the difference between a fixed number and the number of individuals present. Later, a fractional generalization of the binomial process was studied by Cahoy and Polito (2012) (see [ 7 ] ) and called it as fractional binomial process (FBP). In this paper, we study second-order properties of the FBP and the long-range behavior of the FBP and its noise process. We also estimate the parameters of the FBP using the method of moments procedure. Finally, we present the simulated sample paths and its algorithm for the FBP.

The parameter estimation of the the FBP is not known in the literature and in this paper we discuss the same using the method of moments. The simulated sample paths of the FBP gives an visual idea of the evolution of the process and in this paper we present simulated sample paths for the FBP.

p_{n}(0)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{$n=M,$}\\
0&\mbox{$n\neq M,$}\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right.

where,
M\geq 1
is the initial population. An LBD process have applications in several areas, such as, modelling population dynamics (see
[
2
,

,

]
), queuing systems (see
[

]
) and biological systems (see

,

).
Jakeman (see
[

]
) studied linear birth and death process with some modifications and obtained the binomial process. We next present some preliminary results of the binomial process that will be needed later in this paper.

2.1.
Binomial process

Jakeman (see
[
21
]
) introduced the classical binomial process
\left\{\mathcal{N}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
with birth rate
\lambda>0
and death rate
\mu>0
has initial value problem for the state probability
p_{n}(t)
given by

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{d}{dt}p_{n}(t)=\mu(n+1)p_{n+1}(t)-\mu np_{n}(t)-\lambda(N-n)p_{n}(t)\\
\qquad\qquad+\lambda(N-n+1)p_{n-1}(t),\quad\mbox{if $0\leq n\leq N$, }\\
p_{n}(0)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{$n=M,$}\\
0&\mbox{$n\neq M,$}\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right.

where
p_{n}(t)=\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{N}(t)=n|\mathcal{N}(0)=M\right\}
,
M
is the initial population and
N
is the maximum attainable population.
The state space of the binomial process is
\{0,\ldots,N\}
. The
generating function for
\mathcal{N}(t)
is defined as

\mbox{Q}(u,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(1-u)^{n}p_{n}(t),

and it satisfies the following partial differential equation (pde)

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\mbox{Q}(u,t)=-\mu u\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\mbox{Q}(u,t)-\lambda u(1-u)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\mbox{Q}(u,t)-\lambda Nu\mbox{Q}(u,t)\\
\\
\mbox{Q}(u,0)=(1-u)^{M},\qquad|1-u|\leq 1,\end{array}\right.

where
M\geq 1
is the initial number of individuals and
N\geq M
. The solution of the above equation (
2.3
) is given by

(2.4)

Q(u,t)=[1-(1-\theta)\xi u]^{N}\left(\frac{1-[(1-\theta)\xi+\theta]u}{1-(1-\theta)\xi u}\right)^{M},

where
\xi=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+\mu}
and
\theta(t)=\exp[-(\mu+\lambda)t].

The joint probability generating function of the binomial process is given as (see
[
21
]
)

(2.5)

\displaystyle Q(u,u^{\prime},t)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(1-u)^{n}P_{n}Q_{n}(u^{\prime},t),

where
Q_{n}(u^{\prime},t)
is given by (
2.4
) and the subscript
n
denotes the initial population. The probability of finding
n
individuals
P_{n}
given by (see
[
21
]
)

(2.6)

P_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\binom{N}{n}\xi^{n}(1-\xi)^{N-n}\qquad n\leq N,\\
0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad n>N.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, it is observed in
[
21
]
that as time tends to infinity, the evolving population follows a binomial distribution with parameters
N
and
\lambda/(\lambda+\mu).

2.2.
Fractional Binomial process

The FBP (see
[
7
]
)
\left\{\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
is obtained by taking fractional-order derivative in place of the integer-order derivative in the governing differential equation of the binomial process given in (
2.2
). The governing differential equation of the FBP
\left\{\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
with birth rate
\lambda>0
and death rate
\mu>0
is given by

(2.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{d^{\nu}}{dt^{\nu}}p_{n}^{\nu}(t)=\mu(n+1)p_{n+1}^{\nu}(t)-\mu np_{n}^{\nu}(t)-\lambda(N-n)p_{n}^{\nu}(t)+\lambda(N-n+1)p_{n-1}^{\nu}(t),&\mbox{$0\leq n\leq N$,}\\
p_{n}^{\nu}(0)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{$n=M$},\\
0&\mbox{$n\neq M$}.\end{array}\right.\par\end{array}\right.

The inverse
\nu
-stable subordinator is defined as the right-continuous of the
\nu
-stable subordinator
\{D_{\nu}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
(see
[
6
,
34
]
)

E_{\nu}(t)=\inf\{x>0|D_{\nu}(t)>x\},\quad 0<\nu<1,\quad t\geq 0.

It is observed (see
[
7
]
) that the one-dimensional distribution of the FBP
\left\{\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
can be written as time-changed binomial process
\left\{\mathcal{N}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
by an independent inverse
\nu
-stable subordinator
E_{\nu}(t)
,

i.e.
,

(2.8)

\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)\overset{d}{=}\mathcal{N}(E_{\nu}(t)),

where
t\geq 0
and
\nu\in(0,1).

It is known that (see
[
7
]
) the generating function of the FBP
Q^{\nu}(u,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(1-u)^{n}p_{n}^{\nu}(t)
solves the following differential equation

(2.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial^{\nu}}{\partial t^{\nu}}\mbox{Q}^{\nu}(u,t)=-\mu u\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\mbox{Q}^{\nu}(u,t)-\lambda u(1-u)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\mbox{Q}^{\nu}(u,t)-\lambda Nu\mbox{Q}^{\nu}(u,t),\\
\\
\mbox{Q}^{\nu}(u,0)=(1-u)^{M},\qquad|1-u|\leq 1,\end{array}\right.

where the initial number of individuals is
M\geq 1
, and
N\geq M
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f(x)
and
g(x)
be two functions, then they are called asymptotically equivalent denoted by
f(x)\sim g(x)
, if

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=1.

The Mittag–Leffler function can be defined as (see
[
17
]
)

E_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{z^{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+1)}\quad\alpha,z\in\mathbb{C},\quad\mathbb{R}(\alpha)>0.

Now, using expansion of
E_{\nu}(-x)=\tfrac{1}{\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{a_{n}(\nu)}{x^{n+1}}
, where
0<\nu<1
(see
[
5
]
), we will show that
E_{\nu}(-x)
asymptotically equivalent to
\tfrac{a_{0}(\nu)}{\pi x}
,
that is

\displaystyle E_{\nu}(-x)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{x\pi}\left[a_{0}(\nu)+\dfrac{a_{1}(\nu)}{x}+\dfrac{a_{2}(\nu)}{x^{2}}+\cdot\cdots\right]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\pi}\left[\dfrac{a_{0}(\nu)}{x}+\dfrac{a_{1}(\nu)}{x^{2}}+\dfrac{a_{2}(\nu)}{x^{3}}+\cdots\right]

(2.10)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\pi}\left[\dfrac{a_{0}(\nu)}{x}+O\left(\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}\right)\right]\sim\dfrac{a_{0}(\nu)}{\pi x}.

The mean and variance of the FBP
\left\{\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
are
given by (see
[
7
]
)

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]

\displaystyle=\left(M-\dfrac{N\lambda}{\lambda+\mu}\right)E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu})+\dfrac{N\lambda}{\lambda+\mu}

\displaystyle\mbox{Var}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]

\displaystyle=\left({\dfrac{\lambda^{2}N(N-1)}{(\lambda+\mu)^{2}}}-\dfrac{2\lambda M(N-1)}{\lambda+\mu}+M(M-1)\right)E_{\nu}(-2(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu})

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\dfrac{2\lambda^{2}N}{(\lambda+\mu)^{2}}-\dfrac{\lambda}{\lambda+\mu}(N+2M)+M\right)E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu})

(2.12)

\displaystyle\qquad-\left(M-N\dfrac{\lambda}{\lambda+\mu}\right)^{2}E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu})^{2}+\dfrac{N\lambda\mu}{(\lambda+\mu)^{2}},

where
E_{\alpha}(\xi)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{\xi^{r}}{\Gamma(\alpha r+1)}
is the Mittag–Leffler function.

2.3.
The long and short range dependence

In the literature, there are various definitions of the LRD and SRD characteristics of a stochastic process. However, for the purpose of this paper, we will utilize the following definition (see
[
4
,
16
,
32
]
) for non stationary process.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
d>0
and
\left\{X(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
be a stochastic process and the asymptotic behaviour of its correlation function is given by

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mbox{Corr}\left[X(s),X(t)\right]}{t^{-d}}=c(s),\qquad 0<s<t

for fixed
s
and
c(s)>0
.
Then, we say that the stochastic process
\left\{X(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
has the LRD property if
d\in\left(0,1\right)
, otherwise it is said to have the SRD property when
d\in\left(1,2\right)
.

3.
Dependence structure for the FBP

The aim of this section is to examine the LRD and SRD property of the FBP
\left\{\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
and the fractional binomial noise (FBN)
\left\{Z^{\delta}_{\nu}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
respectively. Now, we derive some results which are needed to prove it. First, we derive the recurrence relation for the joint probability generating function (pgf) of the FBP.

Lemma 3.1
.

The joint pgf of the FBP satisfies the following relationship

(3.1)

\displaystyle Q^{\nu}(u,u^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(1-u)^{n}P_{n}Q^{\nu}_{n}(u^{\prime},t),

where
P_{n}
is given by (
2.6
)
and
\mbox{Q}_{n}^{\nu}
is the solution of (
2.9
) with initial population
n
.

Proof.

Let
P^{\nu}_{nn^{\prime}}
denote the probability of finding
n
individuals present at time
t_{0}
and
n^{\prime}
individuals at time

t_{0}+t
, and is given by

\displaystyle P^{\nu}_{nn^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t_{0}+t)=n’,\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t_{0})=n\right)

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t_{0}+t)=n’|\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t_{0})=n\right)\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t_{0})=n).

Now, we have the generating function for the FBP as

\displaystyle Q^{\nu}(u,u^{\prime};t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,n^{\prime}=0}^{N}(1-u)^{n}(1-u^{\prime})^{n^{\prime}}P^{\nu}_{nn^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,n^{\prime}=0}^{N}(1-u)^{n}(1-u^{\prime})^{n^{\prime}}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t_{0}+t)=n’|\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t_{0})=n\right)\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t_{0})=n)\mbox{ (using \eqref{pnn'}) }

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(1-u)^{n}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t_{0})=n)\sum_{n^{\prime}=0}^{N}(1-u^{\prime})^{n^{\prime}}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t_{0}+t)=n’|\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t_{0})=n\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(1-u)^{n}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t_{0})=n)\mbox{Q}^{\nu}_{n}(u^{\prime},t),

using (
2.5
). Now, using
\lim_{t_{0}\rightarrow\infty}
\mathbb{P}(X(t_{0})=n)=P_{n}
and we have that

(3.3)

\displaystyle Q^{\nu}(u,u^{\prime};t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(1-u)^{n}P_{n}Q^{\nu}_{n}(u^{\prime},t).\qed

We next evaluate
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s)\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]
function for the FBP.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
0<\nu<1
and
\left\{\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
be the FBP, then

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s)\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]=(N\xi)^{2}-N\xi(\xi-1)(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})).

Proof.

Using equation (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u^{\prime}}Q^{\nu}(u,u^{\prime};t-s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N}(1-u)^{n}P_{n}Q^{\nu}_{n}(u^{\prime},t-s)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N}(1-u)^{n}P_{n}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u^{\prime}}Q^{\nu}_{n}(u^{\prime},t-s)\right).

We have that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u\partial u^{\prime}}Q^{\nu}(u,u^{\prime};t-s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{N}(1-u)^{n}P_{n}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u^{\prime}}Q^{\nu}_{n}(u^{\prime},t-s)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}-n(1-u)^{n-1}P_{n}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial u^{\prime}}Q^{\nu}_{n}(u^{\prime},t-s))

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u\partial u^{\prime}}Q^{\nu}(u,u^{\prime})\big|_{u=0,u^{\prime}=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}-nP_{n}(-\mathbb{E}\mathbb{N}^{\nu}(t-s))

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}n[(n-N\xi)E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})+N\xi]P_{n}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{2}(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu}))P_{n}-\sum_{n=1}^{N}nN\xi(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})-1)P_{n}.

Solving both parts separately in the above expression, we have the following

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{2}(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu}))P_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{2}E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu})[^{N}C_{n}\xi^{n}(1-\xi)^{N-n}]

\displaystyle=NE_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu})\xi\sum_{n=1}^{N}\dfrac{(N-1)!}{(N-n)!(n-1)!}n\xi^{n-1}(1-\xi)^{N-n}

\displaystyle=\left(N(N-1)E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu})\xi^{2}\sum_{n=2}^{N}\dfrac{(N-2)!}{(N-n)!(n-2)!}\xi^{n-2}(1-\xi)^{N-n}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+NE_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu})\xi\sum_{n=1}^{N}\dfrac{(N-1)!}{(N-n)!(n-1)!}\xi^{n-1}(1-\xi)^{N-n}\right)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=N(N-1)(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu}))\xi^{2}+N(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})\xi.

Now, considering second part, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}nN\xi(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})-1)P_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}nN\xi(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})-1)[^{N}C_{n}\xi^{n}(1-\xi)^{N-n}]

\displaystyle=N(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})-1)\sum_{n=1}^{N}\dfrac{N!}{(N-n)!n!}n\xi^{n+1}(1-\xi)^{N-n}

\displaystyle=(N\xi)^{2}(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})-1)[1-\xi]^{N-1}

(3.7)

\displaystyle=(N\xi)^{2}\left(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})-1\right).

Using equations (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u\partial u^{\prime}}Q^{\nu}(u,u^{\prime})\big|_{u=0,u^{\prime}=0}

\displaystyle=\left[N(N-1)(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu}))\xi^{2}+N(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})\xi\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.-(N\xi)^{2}\left(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})-1\right)\right]

\displaystyle=(N\xi)^{2}\left(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})\right)-N\xi^{2}\left(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+N\xi\left(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})\right)-(N\xi)^{2}\left(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})\right)+(N\xi)^{2}

\displaystyle=(N\xi)^{2}+N\xi(1-\xi)(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})).

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s)\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u\partial u^{\prime}}Q^{\nu}(u,u^{\prime})\big|_{u=0,u^{\prime}=0}\right)=(N\xi)^{2}+N\xi(1-\xi)(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})).\qquad\qed

Next, we compute autocovariance function of the FBP.

Theorem 3.3
.

The autocovariance function of the FBP

Cov

\displaystyle[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s),\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]=(N\xi(1-\xi)(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})))-\left((M^{2}-2MN\xi+N^{2}\xi^{2})\right.

(3.8)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left.E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)s^{\nu})E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu})\right)-\left\{(M-N\xi)N\xi[E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)s^{\nu})+E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu})]\right\}.

Proof.

Using (
2.11
), we obtain the following expression

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s)]\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]

\displaystyle=(M^{2}-2MN\xi+N^{2}\xi^{2})E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)s^{\nu})E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu})

\displaystyle\qquad+(M-N\xi)N\xi[E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)s^{\nu})+E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu})]+N^{2}\xi^{2}.

Using equation (
3.4

\displaystyle[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s),\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s)\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s)]\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]

\displaystyle=((N\xi)^{2}+N\xi(1-\xi)(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t-s)^{\nu})))-\left((M^{2}-2MN\xi+N^{2}\xi^{2})E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)s^{\nu})\right.

]

Theorem 3.7
.

The FBN
\left\{Z^{\delta}_{\nu}(t)_{t\geq 0}\right\}
has the SRD property.

Proof.

Let
s,\delta\geq 0
be fixed, and
0\leq s+\delta\leq t
. We begin with

\displaystyle\mbox{Cov}[Z^{\delta}_{\nu}(s),Z^{\delta}_{\nu}(t)]

\displaystyle=\mbox{Cov}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s+\delta)-\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s),\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t+\delta)-\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]

\displaystyle=\mbox{Cov}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s+\delta),\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t+\delta)]+Cov[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s),\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]-\mbox{Cov}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s+\delta),\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]

(3.11)

\displaystyle\qquad-\mbox{Cov}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s),\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t+\delta)].

From (
3.10
), we have

\mbox{Cov}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(s),\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]\sim\dfrac{r}{t^{\nu}}\left[N\xi(1-\xi)-\left((M-N\xi)^{2}E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)s^{\nu})\right)-\left\{(M-N\xi)N\xi\right\}\right],

where
r=\frac{a_{0}(\nu)}{\pi(\mu+\lambda)}
.
Using above equation, we get

\displaystyle\mbox{Cov}[Z^{\delta}_{\nu}(s),Z^{\delta}_{\nu}(t)]

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{r}{(t+\delta)^{\nu}}\left[N\xi(1-\xi)-\left((M-N\xi)^{2}E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(s+\delta)^{\nu})\right)-\left\{(M-N\xi)N\xi\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\dfrac{r}{t^{\nu}}\left[N\xi(1-\xi)-\left((M-N\xi)^{2}E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)s^{\nu})\right)-\left\{(M-N\xi)N\xi\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-\dfrac{r}{t^{\nu}}\left[N\xi(1-\xi)-\left((M-N\xi)^{2}E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(s+\delta)^{\nu})\right)-\left\{(M-N\xi)N\xi\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-\dfrac{r}{(t+\delta)^{\nu}}\left[N\xi(1-\xi)-\left((M-N\xi)^{2}E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)s^{\nu})\right)-\left\{(M-N\xi)N\xi\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip-91.04872pt\sim r(M-N\xi)^{2}\left[-\dfrac{E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(s+\delta)^{\nu})}{(t+\delta))^{\nu}}-\dfrac{E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)s^{\nu})}{t^{\nu}}+\dfrac{E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(s+\delta)^{\nu})}{t^{\nu}}+\dfrac{E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)s^{\nu})}{(t+\delta)^{\nu}}\right]

\displaystyle\sim r(M-N\xi)^{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{(t+\delta)^{\nu}}-\dfrac{1}{t^{\nu}}\right)\left(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)s^{\nu})-E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(s+\delta)^{\nu})\right)

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{r(M-N\xi)^{2}}{t^{\nu}}\left(\dfrac{-\nu\delta}{t}\right)\left(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)s^{\nu})-E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(s+\delta)^{\nu})\right)

Observe that

\displaystyle\mbox{Var}[Z^{\delta}_{\nu}(t)]

\displaystyle=\mbox{Var}[N^{\nu}(t+\delta)]+\mbox{Var}[N^{\nu}(t)]-2\mbox{Cov}[N^{\nu}(t+\delta),N^{\nu}(t)]

\displaystyle\mbox{Var}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{r}{t^{\nu}}\left[\dfrac{\left(\xi^{2}N(N-1)-2\xi M(N-1)+M(M-1)\right)}{2}+\left(2\xi^{2}N-\xi(N+2M)+M\right)\right]

\displaystyle\mbox{Cov}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t+\delta),\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]

\displaystyle=(N\xi(1-\xi)(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(\delta)^{\nu})))

\displaystyle\qquad-\left((M^{2}-2MN\xi+N^{2}\xi^{2})E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t+\delta))^{\nu})E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\left\{(M-N\xi)N\xi[E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(t+\delta))^{\nu}+E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)t^{\nu})]\right\}

\displaystyle\sim-\left(r^{2}(M^{2}-2MN\xi+N^{2}\xi^{2})\dfrac{1}{(t(t+\delta))^{\nu}}\right)-\left\{(M-N\xi)N\xi r\left[\dfrac{1}{(t+\delta)^{\nu}}+\dfrac{1}{t^{\nu}}\right]\right\}.

Using above equations, we get

\displaystyle\mbox{Var}[Z^{\delta}_{\nu}(t)]=\mbox{Var}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t+\delta)]+\mbox{Var}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]-2\mbox{Cov}[\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t+\delta),\mathcal{N}^{\nu}(t)]

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{r}{t^{\nu}}\left[\dfrac{\left(\xi^{2}N(N-1)-2\xi M(N-1)+M(M-1)\right)}{2}+\left(2\xi^{2}N-\xi(N+2M)+M\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\dfrac{r}{(t+\delta)^{\nu}}\left[\dfrac{\left(\xi^{2}N(N-1)-2\xi M(N-1)+M(M-1)\right)}{2}+\left(2\xi^{2}N-\xi(N+2M)+M\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+2\left((M^{2}-2MN\xi+N^{2}\xi^{2})r^{2}\dfrac{1}{(t(t+\delta))^{\nu}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+2\left((M-N\xi)N\xi\left[\dfrac{r}{(t+\delta)^{\nu}}+\dfrac{r}{t^{\nu}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{r}{t^{\nu}}\left(\left[\dfrac{\left(\xi^{2}N(N-1)-2\xi M(N-1)+M(M-1)\right)}{2}+\left(2\xi^{2}N-\xi(N+2M)+M\right)\right]\left(1+\frac{1}{\left(1+\frac{\delta}{t}\right)^{\nu}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{2r}{t^{\nu}}\left[\dfrac{\left(\xi^{2}N(N-1)-2\xi M(N-1)+M(M-1)\right)}{2}+\left(2\xi^{2}N-\xi(N+2M)+M\right)+2(M-N\xi)N\xi\right].

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{\left(\frac{-\nu\delta}{t^{1+\frac{\nu}{2}}}\right)\sqrt{r}(M-N\xi)^{2}\left(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)s^{\nu})-E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(s+\delta)^{\nu})\right)}{\left\{2\left[\frac{\left(\xi^{2}N(N-1)-2\xi M(N-1)+M(M-1)\right)}{2}+\left(2\xi^{2}N-\xi(N+2M)+M\right)+2(M-N\xi)N\xi\right]Var[Z^{\delta}_{\nu}(s)]\right\}^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\sim\left(\frac{1}{t^{1+\frac{\nu}{2}}}\right)c(s),\mbox{ where}

c(s)=\dfrac{\left(\frac{-\nu\delta}{t^{1+\frac{\nu}{2}}}\right)\sqrt{r}(M-N\xi)^{2}\left(E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)s^{\nu})-E_{\nu}(-(\lambda+\mu)(s+\delta)^{\nu})\right)}{\left\{2\left[\frac{\left(\xi^{2}N(N-1)-2\xi M(N-1)+M(M-1)\right)}{2}+\left(2\xi^{2}N-\xi(N+2M)+M\right)+2(M-N\xi)N\xi\right]Var[Z^{\delta}_{\nu}(s)]\right\}^{1/2}}

Since

Concluding Remarks

We have investigated that the FBP has the LRD property and its increment exhibits the SRD property. We have used the one-dimensional distributions of the FBP to simulate sample path for the process. We have derived the distribution of sojourn time of the FBP and used it to simulate sample trajectories. We have used MoM estimation technique to estimate parameters of the FBP. On comparing generated sample path in Figure
1
, we can see that time taken to occur next birth or death reduces and population burst occurs. This behaviour makes the FBP more applicable in nature as such incidences occurs in real life, for example during Covid-
19
demand of masks, sanitizer, oxygen cylinder and many other things saw a burst in their demands.

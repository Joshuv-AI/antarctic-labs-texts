Electric/magnetic duality of dyonic Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS spacetimes
Cristóbal Corral, Rodrigo Olea

Abstract
We study the (anti-)self-duality conditions under which the electric and magnetic parts of the conserved charges of the dyonic Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS solution become equivalent. Within a holographic framework, the stress tensor and the boundary Cotton tensor are computed from the electric/magnetic content of the Weyl tensor. The holographic stress tensor/Cotton tensor duality is recovered along the (anti-)self-dual curve in parameter space. We show that the latter not only implies a duality relation for the mass but also for the angular momentum. The partition function is computed to first order in the saddle-point approximation and a BPS bound is obtained. The ground state of the theory is enlarged to all the (anti-)self-dual configurations when the S ​ O ​ ( 4 ) SO(4) and U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) Pontryagin densities are introduced. We demonstrate this at the level of the action and variations thereof.

The Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS black hole

The Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS black hole is a five-parameter family of solutions of the Einstein-Maxwell system with negative cosmological constant. It was first found by Carter
[
58

and their global properties were studied by Miller in Ref.
[
59

[

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\Sigma(r,\vartheta)}{\Delta_{\vartheta}(\vartheta)}\text{d}\vartheta^{2}+\frac{\Delta_{\vartheta}(\vartheta)\sin^{2}\vartheta}{\Xi^{2}\Sigma(r,\vartheta)}\left[a\text{d}t-\left(r^{2}+a^{2}+n^{2}-2anC\right)\text{d}\varphi\right]^{2}\,,

(1a)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=A_{\mu}\text{d}x^{\mu}=a_{t}(r,\vartheta)\text{d}t+a_{\varphi}(r,\vartheta)\text{d}\varphi\,,

(1b)

The Einstein-AdS-Maxwell system is described by the field equations

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R-\frac{3}{\ell^{2}}g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\kappa}T_{\mu\nu}\,,

(2a)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=F_{\mu\lambda}F_{\nu}^{\ \lambda}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}F_{\lambda\rho}F^{\lambda\rho}\,.

(3)

In four dimensions, the trace of the stress-energy tensor for the Maxwell fields vanishes identically as a consequence of its conformal invariance.

The Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS spacetime is a solution of the Einstein-AdS-Maxwell field equations (

(4a)

\displaystyle=r^{2}+(n+a\cos\vartheta)^{2}\,,

(4b)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\vartheta}(\vartheta)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{a\cos\vartheta(4n+a\cos\vartheta)}{\ell^{2}}\,,

(4c)

\displaystyle a_{t}(r,\vartheta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{qr+p(n+a\cos\vartheta)}{\Xi\Sigma(r,\vartheta)}\,,

(4d)

\displaystyle a_{\varphi}(r,\vartheta)

\displaystyle=\frac{qr\left[a^{2}+n^{2}-2anC-(n+a\cos\vartheta)^{2}\right]}{a\Xi\Sigma(r,\vartheta)}+\frac{p\left(n+a\cos\vartheta\right)\left(r^{2}+a^{2}+n^{2}-2anC\right)}{a\Xi\Sigma(r,\vartheta)}\,,

(4e)

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}R^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}=\frac{H(r,\vartheta)}{\left[r^{2}+(n+a\cos\vartheta)^{2}\right]^{6}}\,,

(5)

The solution has an event horizon located at the locus

\displaystyle\xi=\partial_{t}+\Omega_{+}\partial_{\varphi}\,,\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;\;\;\;\Omega_{+}=\frac{a}{r_{+}^{2}+a^{2}+n^{2}-2anC}\,,

\displaystyle T_{H}=\frac{\Omega_{+}}{4\pi ar_{+}\Xi}\left\{4\pi G\left(q^{2}+p^{2}\right)-\left(r_{+}^{2}+n^{2}-a^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\left[3\left(r_{+}^{2}+n^{2}\right)^{2}+a^{2}\left(r_{+}^{2}-3n^{2}\right)\right]\right\}\,.

In the next section, we show that the Gauss-Bonnet term can be used to renormalize the Einstein-AdS-Maxwell action. This is a well-established result at the level of vacuum solutions

III
Topological renormalization of Einstein-AdS-Maxwell

The bulk dynamics of the four-dimensional Einstein-AdS-Maxwell theory is given by the field equations (
2
), that can be obtained by performing arbitrary variations of the action principle

\displaystyle I_{\rm EM}[g_{\mu\nu},A]=\kappa\int_{\mathcal{M}}\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\left(R+\frac{6}{\ell^{2}}+\alpha\mathcal{G}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\,F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\,.

(9)

Here,
\alpha
is the coupling constant of the Gauss-Bonnet term; the latter is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}=\frac{1}{4}\delta^{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{4}}_{\nu_{1}\ldots\nu_{4}}R^{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}R^{\nu_{3}\nu_{4}}_{\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}=R^{2}-4R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}+R_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}R^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}\,,

(10)

with
\delta^{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{p}}_{\nu_{1}\ldots\nu_{p}}=p!\,\delta^{[\mu_{1}}_{[\nu_{1}}\dots\delta^{\mu_{p}]}_{\nu_{p}]}
being the generalized Kronecker delta. The inclusion of the Gauss-Bonnet term does not modify the bulk dynamics since it can be written as a boundary integral of the Chern form plus the Euler characteristic,
\chi(\mathcal{M})
, upon proper use of the Gauss-Bonnet theorem. Defining the evolution of surfaces in terms of a radial foliation of the spacetime, we can write the metric in Gauss normal coordinates as

\displaystyle\text{d}s^{2}=N^{2}(z)\text{d}z^{2}+h_{ij}(z,x)\text{d}x^{i}\text{d}x^{j}\,,

(11)

where the set
\{x^{i}\}
denotes codimension-1 boundary coordinates. Then, the Gauss-Bonnet theorem can be written as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{M}}\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\,\mathcal{G}=32\pi^{2}\chi(\mathcal{M})+4\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\text{d}^{3}x\sqrt{|h|}\delta^{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}_{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}}K^{j_{1}}_{i_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{R}^{j_{2}j_{3}}_{i_{2}i_{3}}-\frac{1}{3}K^{j_{2}}_{i_{2}}K^{j_{3}}_{i_{3}}\right)\,.

(12)

where
\mathcal{R}^{ij}_{kl}=\mathcal{R}^{ij}_{kl}(h)
and
K_{ij}=-\tfrac{1}{2N}\partial_{z}h_{ij}
are the intrinsic and extrinsic curvatures, respectively.

Although the Gauss-Bonnet term does not modify the field equations, it changes the action and conserved charges nontrivially. Indeed, if

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}\,,

(13)

the action and variations thereof become finite upon evaluation on asymptotically AdS spacetimes
[
68
,
69
,
70
,
37
]
. In the Fefferman-Graham gauge, the latter can be expressed as

\displaystyle\text{d}s^{2}=\frac{\ell^{2}}{z^{2}}\left(\text{d}z^{2}+\bar{g}_{ij}(z,x)\text{d}x^{i}\text{d}x^{j}\right)\,,

(14)

where
z\to 0
denotes the location of the conformal boundary. In general,
\bar{g}_{ij}
admits a series expansion in the holographic coordinate as

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{ij}(z,x)=g_{(0)ij}(x)+\frac{z}{\ell}g_{(1)ij}(x)+\frac{z^{2}}{\ell^{2}}g_{(2)ij}(x)+\frac{z^{3}}{\ell^{3}}g_{(3)ij}(x)+\mathcal{O}(z^{4})\,.

(15)

In pure gravity, for Einstein spaces, the condition
g_{(1)ij}=0
is implied by the field equations. Furthermore, even in Einstein-AdS-Maxwell theory, the falloff of the matter sector still implies the vanishing of
g_{(1)}

[
72
]
. Then, taking into account Eqs. (
13

10

9

[
73
]
augmented by the Maxwell term, that is,

\displaystyle I_{\rm EM}^{\rm(ren)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa\ell^{2}}{16}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\left[\delta^{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{4}}_{\nu_{1}\ldots\nu_{4}}\left(R^{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}+\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\delta^{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}\right)\left(R^{\nu_{3}\nu_{4}}_{\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}+\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\delta^{\nu_{3}\nu_{4}}_{\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}\right)-\frac{4}{\kappa\ell^{2}}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\right]\,.

(16)

To evaluate this action on-shell, we use the irreducible decomposition of the Riemann tensor in arbitrary
D
dimensions

\displaystyle R^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}=\frac{1}{D(D-1)}\delta^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}R+\frac{4}{D-2}\delta^{[\mu}_{[\lambda}H^{\nu]}_{\rho]}+W^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}\,,

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}=R^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}+\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\delta^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\,\delta^{[\mu}_{[\lambda}\,T^{\nu]}_{\rho]}\,,

\displaystyle I_{\rm EM}^{\rm(ren)}\Big|_{\rm on-shell}=\frac{\kappa\ell^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\left(W^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}W^{\lambda\rho}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{\kappa\ell^{2}}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}T_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}\right)\,.

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}W^{\lambda\rho}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=W^{ij}_{kl}W^{kl}_{ij}+4W^{iz}_{jz}W^{jz}_{iz}+4W^{iz}_{jk}W^{jk}_{iz}\sim\mathcal{O}(z^{6})\,,

\displaystyle=F_{ij}F^{ij}+2F_{iz}F^{iz}\sim\mathcal{O}(z^{4})\,,

IV
Self-duality of Euclidean Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS instanton

In the Euclidean section, the dyonic Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS solution enjoys an (anti-)self-dual curve in parameter space where both Maxwell’s field strength and the Weyl tensor satisfy

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}=\pm\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\;\;W_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}=\pm\tilde{W}_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}\,,

(22)

respectively, where
\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}F^{\lambda\rho}
and
\tilde{W}_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}=\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}W^{\alpha\beta}_{\lambda\rho}
. This condition is achieved if the following relations on the parameters are met

\displaystyle\hat{q}=\pm\,\hat{p}\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\;\;m=\pm\frac{\hat{n}}{G}\left(1+\frac{\hat{a}^{2}-4\hat{n}^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)\,.

(23)

The stress-energy tensor of Maxwell fields in Eq. (
3
) vanishes identically for (anti-)self-dual configurations, even though the field strength is nontrivial. This can be seen directly from the identity
[
77
]

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=F_{\mu\lambda}F_{\nu}{}^{\lambda}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}F_{\lambda\rho}F^{\lambda\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\left(F_{\mu\lambda}+\tilde{F}_{\mu\lambda}\right)\left(F_{\nu\rho}-\tilde{F}_{\nu\rho}\right)g^{\lambda\rho}\,.

(24)

Thus, (anti-)self-dual Maxwell solutions do not backreact; they resemble stealth configurations of Einstein gravity (non-)minimally coupled to scalar fields
[
78
,
79
,
80
]
. Then, using the off-shell identity
\tilde{W}_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}W^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}=\tilde{R}_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}R^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}
where
\tilde{R}_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}=\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}R^{\alpha\beta}_{\lambda\rho}
is the dual Riemann tensor, we find that the Euclidean on-shell action (
19
) can be written as the linear combination of the
SO(4)
and
U(1)
Chern-Pontryagin indices, that is,

\displaystyle I_{E}|_{\rm on-shell}=\pm 4\pi^{2}\left(\kappa\ell^{2}P_{1}[\mathcal{M}]-C_{2}[A]\right)\,.

(25)

This is a topologically-nontrivial minimum of the action and it represents the saturated BPS bound of Einstein-AdS-Maxwell theory. For the (anti-)self-dual dyonic Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS solution in Eq. (
4
), we find that these topological terms are explicitly given by

\displaystyle P_{1}[\mathcal{M}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\,\tilde{R}^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}R_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda\rho}=\pm\frac{2\beta_{\tau}\beta_{\varphi}(mG)^{2}\left[2r_{+}\left(r_{+}^{2}+\hat{a}^{2}\right)\pm\hat{n}\left(3r_{+}^{2}+\hat{a}^{2}-\hat{n}^{2}\right)\right]}{\pi^{2}\Xi^{2}\left[(r_{+}\pm\hat{n})^{2}-\hat{a}^{2}\right]^{3}}\,,

(26)

\displaystyle C_{2}[A]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\,\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=\pm\frac{\beta_{\tau}\beta_{\varphi}r_{+}\hat{p}^{2}}{2\pi^{2}\Xi^{2}\left[(r_{+}\pm\hat{n})^{2}-\hat{a}^{2}\right]}\,,

(27)

where
m
and
\hat{p}
satisfy the conditions in Eq. (
23
). This implies that the (anti-)self-dual dyonic Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS space is a BPS state that preserves only a fraction of the supersymmetries
[
51
]
. These terms, in addition to the boundary contributions coming from the Chern-Simons form and the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer
\eta
-invariant, can be used to compute the Dirac index and to see whether this background breaks the chiral symmetry of massless fermionic fields or not (see
[
81
]
and references therein).

Since the Euclidean on-shell action becomes the sum of two topological terms when evaluated at (anti-)self-dual configurations, it is natural to modify the action (
16
) as

\displaystyle\bar{I}_{\rm EM}^{\rm(ren)}

\displaystyle=I_{\rm EM}^{\rm(ren)}-\frac{\kappa\ell^{2}}{4}\left(\Theta_{\tilde{R}R}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\,\tilde{R}^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}R_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda\rho}-\frac{\Theta_{\tilde{F}F}}{\kappa\ell^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\,\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\right)\,,

(28)

with
\Theta_{\tilde{R}R}
and
\Theta_{\tilde{F}F}
two dimensionless constants. These two parity-odd topological terms modify neither the bulk dynamics nor the infrared divergences for asymptotically locally AdS spaces. Nevertheless, they do modify the finite part of the action. Indeed, if
\Theta_{\tilde{R}R}=\Theta_{\tilde{F}F}=\pm 1
, the Euclidean on-shell action (
28
) can be written as

\displaystyle\bar{I}_{\rm EM}^{\rm(ren)}\Big|_{\rm on-shell}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa\ell^{2}}{8}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\Bigg[\left(W^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}\mp\tilde{W}^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}\right)\left(W_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda\rho}\mp\tilde{W}_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda\rho}\right)-\frac{1}{\kappa\ell^{2}}\left(F_{\mu\nu}\mp\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}\right)\left(F^{\mu\nu}\mp\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8\kappa^{2}}\left(F_{\mu\lambda}+\tilde{F}_{\mu\lambda}\right)\left(F_{\nu\rho}-\tilde{F}_{\nu\rho}\right)\left(F^{\mu\sigma}+\tilde{F}^{\mu\sigma}\right)\left(F^{\nu\tau}-\tilde{F}^{\nu\tau}\right)g^{\lambda\rho}g_{\sigma\tau}\Bigg]\,,

(29)

where we have used Eqs. (
16
) and (
24
). Thus, Euclidean on-shell action (
29
) vanishes identically for (anti-)self-dual electrovacuum solutions with negative cosmological constant—see Eq. (
22
). Then, it becomes evident that adding topological terms of the Pontryagin class enlarges the ground state to all (anti-)self-dual configurations of Einstein-AdS-Maxwell theory, similar to what happens with the gravity and matter sectors separately.

Conserved charges in AdS gravity and electric/magnetic duality

The (anti-)self-duality condition on the Weyl tensor allows one to establish an electric/magnetic duality between the AMD charges of the Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS solution.

To show this, we consider the electric and magnetic parts of the Weyl tensor defined as

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{ij}=W_{i\lambda j\rho}n^{\lambda}n^{\rho}\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\;\;\mathscr{B}_{ij}=\tilde{W}_{i\lambda j\rho}n^{\lambda}n^{\rho}\,,

(30)

respectively, where
n^{\mu}
is the unit-normal spacelike vector that defines radial foliation. Since the Maxwell fields decay sufficiently fast towards the asymptotic boundary, its presence does not affect the condition
g_{(1)}=0
in Einstein gravity (see Ref.
[

]
and references therein).

To relevant order in the FG expansion, variations of the action (

\displaystyle\tau_{ij}=-\frac{\ell}{8\pi G}\,\mathscr{E}_{ij}\,.

(31)

On the other hand, near the conformal boundary, the magnetic part of the Weyl tensor can be expressed to the relevant holographic order as

\displaystyle-\ell\,C_{ij}(g_{(0)})=\mathscr{B}_{ij}\,,\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;\;\;\;C^{ij}(g_{(0)})=\varepsilon^{(0)ilm}\nabla_{(0)m}(R^{j}_{(0)l}-\frac{1}{4}R_{(0)}\delta^{j}_{l})

(32)

is the Cotton tensor and all quantities in the last expression are constructed out of
g_{(0)}
. Explicitly, the components of the holographic stress tensor and Cotton tensor are

\displaystyle\tau_{ij}=\frac{m}{8\pi\ell\Xi^{2}}\,\sigma_{ij}(\vartheta)\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\;\;C_{ij}=\frac{n}{\Xi^{2}\ell^{3}}\left(1-\frac{a^{2}-4n^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)\sigma_{ij}(\vartheta)\,,

(33)

respectively, where we have defined the codimension-1 boundary tensor as

\displaystyle\sigma_{ij}(\vartheta)=\begin{pmatrix}2&0&4n(C+\cos\vartheta)-2a\sin^{2}\vartheta\\

4n(C+\cos\vartheta)-2a\sin^{2}\vartheta&0&\ell^{2}\sin^{2}\vartheta\Delta_{\vartheta}(\vartheta)+2\left[2n(C+\cos\vartheta)-a\sin^{2}\vartheta\right]^{2}\end{pmatrix},

(34)

and we have chosen the orientation
(t,\vartheta,\varphi)
. From Eq. (

), we conclude that the holographic stress tensor can be written in terms of the Cotton tensor as

\displaystyle\tau_{ij}=\frac{m\ell^{2}}{8\pi n}\left(1-\frac{a^{2}-4n^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)^{-1}\,C_{ij}\,.

(35)

This relation is known as the stress tensor/Cotton tensor duality and it appears also in the fluid interpretation of the holographic stress tensor

For a Killing vector
\xi=\xi^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
, one can define conserved charges associated with the electric and magnetic parts of the Weyl tensor as long as the matter flux vanishes at the asymptotic boundary
[

,

]
. Thus, the conserved electric and magnetic charges are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}[\xi]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\ell}{8\pi G}\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}\text{d}^{2}x\sqrt{|\gamma|}\;\mathscr{E}^{i}_{j}\,\xi^{j}\,u_{i}\,,

(36a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}[\xi]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\ell}{8\pi G}\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}\text{d}^{2}x\sqrt{|\gamma|}\;\mathscr{B}^{i}_{j}\,\xi^{j}\,u_{i}\,,

(36b)

where
\Sigma_{\infty}
is a codimension-2 asymptotic boundary with
u^{i}
its timelike unit normal, while
\gamma
denotes the determinant of the codimension-2 boundary metric. For an asymptotically timelike Killing vector field
\xi=\partial_{t}
, direct evaluation of these integrals yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}[\partial_{t}]

\displaystyle:=M=\frac{m}{\Xi^{2}}\,,

(37a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}[\partial_{t}]

\displaystyle:=\tilde{M}=\frac{n}{G\Xi^{2}}\left(1-\frac{a^{2}-4n^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)\,.

(37b)

The (anti-)self-duality condition
\mathscr{E}_{ij}=\pm\mathscr{B}_{ij}
(cf. Eq. (
23
) in Euclidean signature) implies that
M=\tilde{M}
; the latter is usually interpreted as the magnetic mass
[

,

]
. Indeed, to relevant holographic order, the (anti-)self-duality condition yields

\displaystyle\tau_{ij}=\pm\frac{\ell^{2}}{8\pi G}C_{ij}\,.

(38)

For the spacelike Killing vector that generates the axial isometry, i.e.
\xi=\partial_{\varphi}
, the electric and magnetic charges are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}[\partial_{\varphi}]

\displaystyle:=J=M(3Cn-a)\,,

(39a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}[\partial_{\varphi}]

(39b)

Therefore, the (anti-)self-duality of the dyonic Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS solution is not only translated into an equivalence between the electric and magnetic masses but also provides a duality between the electric and magnetic parts of the angular momentum. Notice that the position of the Misner string parametrized by
C=\pm 1,0
induces a nontrivial contribution to the angular momentum as well.

The Noether-Wald formalism
[

,

,

]
, on the other hand, provides a covariant framework to compute conserved charges. The latter is based upon the on-shell conservation of the Noether current associated with diffeomorphism invariance. Locally, the Poincaré Lemma allows one to write the Noether current in terms of a Noether prepotential, that is,

\displaystyle q^{\mu\nu}=-2\left(E^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}\nabla^{\lambda}\xi^{\rho}+2\xi^{\lambda}\nabla^{\rho}E^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}\right)\,.

(40)

Here,
\xi=\xi^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
is a Killing vector field and
E^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}
denotes the functional derivative of the Lagrangian with respect to the Riemann tensor. For the action (
16
), it is explicitly given by

\displaystyle E^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}=\frac{\kappa\ell^{2}}{8}\delta^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{\lambda\rho\gamma\delta}\left(R^{\gamma\delta}_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\delta^{\gamma\delta}_{\alpha\beta}\right)=\frac{\kappa\ell^{2}}{2}\left(W^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}+\frac{1}{\kappa}\delta^{[\mu}_{[\lambda}T^{\nu]}_{\rho]}\right)\,.

(41)

In the last equality, we have used the on-shell relation of Eq. (
18
). Conserved charges associated with a Killing vector field
\xi
can be defined in terms of the Noether prepotential as

\displaystyle Q[\xi]=\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}q^{\mu\nu}\text{d}\Sigma_{\mu\nu}\,,

(42)

where
\Sigma_{\infty}
is a codimension-2 asymptotic boundary with
\text{d}\Sigma_{\mu\nu}
being its area element. Notice that, as shown in Eq. (
20a
), the stress-energy tensor falloff is subleading as compared with the Weyl tensor. Therefore, it contributes neither to the mass nor the angular momentum of the solution.

The conserved charges of the dyonic Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS black hole associated with the Killing vectors
\partial_{t}
and
\partial_{\varphi}
can be computed directly from the topologically-renormalized conserved charges in Eq. (

\displaystyle Q[\partial_{t}]

\displaystyle:=M=\frac{m}{\Xi^{2}}\,,

(43a)

\displaystyle Q[\partial_{\varphi}]

\displaystyle:=J=\frac{m(3Cn-a)}{\Xi^{2}}\,,

(43b)

respectively. These quantities coincide with those obtained in Sec.
V
and in Ref.
[
50
]
up-to-the choice of rotation’s direction. Notice that the position of the Misner string parametrized by
C=\pm 1,0
contributes nontrivially to the angular momentum as in the AMD charges. Indeed, these values lead to the correct thermodynamic description of the solution in the absence of Maxwell fields and it coincides with Brown-York charges as shown in Ref.
[
50
]
.

Finally, as discussed in Sec.
IV
, the inclusion of the Pontryagin densities to the Einstein-AdS-Maxwell action [cf. Eq. (
28
)] does modify the action and variations thereof without affecting the bulk dynamics. In the case of Noether-Wald charges, this can be seen by taking the functional derivative of the corresponding Lagrangian in Eq. (
28
) with respect to the Riemann tensor, which yields

\displaystyle\bar{E}^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}=E^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}\mp\frac{\kappa\ell^{2}}{2}\tilde{R}^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}=\frac{\kappa\ell^{2}}{2}\left(W^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}\mp\tilde{W}^{\mu\nu}_{\lambda\rho}\right)+\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}\delta^{[\mu}_{[\lambda}T^{\nu]}_{\rho]}\,.

(44)

In the last equation, we used the on-shell relation given by Eq. (
18
). Taking into account the identity (
24
), it is clear that the Noether prepotential vanishes identically when Eq. (
22
) holds. Therefore, the addition of the
SO(4)
and
U(1)
Pontryagin densities to the Einstein-AdS-Maxwell action not only fixes self-dual configurations as the ground state at the level of the action but also at the level of conserved charges. Indeed, the same conclusion is implied by the modification generated by the Pontryagin density on the holographic stress tensor, that is
[

52

,

\displaystyle\tau_{ij}^{\pm}=\tau_{ij}\mp\frac{\ell^{2}}{8\pi G}C_{ij}\,,

(45)

where the last piece comes from variations of the Pontryagin density. Thus, since the (anti-)self-duality condition implies that the holographic stress tensor and the Cotton tensor are related through Eq. (
38
), it becomes clear that
\tau_{ij}^{\pm}
vanishes identically along such a curve of parameter space.

VI
Discussion

In this work, we studied the (anti-)self-dual conditions of Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS spacetimes. We showed that a BPS bound is saturated along the curve in parameter space given by Eq. (
23
). This condition not only provides an electric/magnetic duality between the mass and the NUT charge but at the level of the angular momentum as well. This is shown by computing explicitly the Noether-Wald and Ashtekar-Magnon-Das charges, alongside the holographic stress tensor and boundary Cotton tensor, rendering the duality of Refs.
[
32
,
21
,
33
,
34
,
37
,
35
,
36
,
20
,
22
,
38
]
manifest.

Using topological renormalization, we computed the Euclidean on-shell action of the Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS spacetime. At the (anti-)self-dual curve, we showed that it can be written as the linear combination of the
SO(4)
and
U(1)
Chern-Pontryagin indices, providing a BPS bound for this five-parameter family of solutions. Thus, adding topological terms of the Pontryagin class, the ground state of the theory is enlarged to all (anti-)self-dual configurations of Einstein-AdS-Maxwell theory. We prove this at the level of the action and conserved charges.

Interesting questions remain open. As a matter of fact, a holographic fluid interpretation of the Taub-NUT-AdS and Kerr-AdS metric was given in Refs.
[
32
,
21
,
33
]
. The dyonic Kerr-Newman-NUT-AdS solution is a natural generalization of these spacetimes and its dual interpretation is certainly relevant for describing charged holographic fluids with vorticity. On the other hand, the thermodynamics of dynamic black holes has been a subject of great interest, in particular, those belonging to the Plebanski-Demianski family. It would be interesting to extend these results in the presence of acceleration. We postpone these and other questions for future work.

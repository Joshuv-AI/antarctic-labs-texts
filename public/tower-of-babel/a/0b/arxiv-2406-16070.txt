Iwahori Matsumoto presentation for modules of Iwahori fixed functions on symmetric spaces
Guy Shtotland

Abstract
We study the space S ​ ( X ) I S(X)^{I} of smooth functions on a symmetric space X = H \ G X=H\backslash G invariant to the action of an Iwahori subgroup I I , as a module over ℋ ⁡ ( G , I ) \mathcal{H}(G,I) , the Iwahori Hecke algebra of a reductive p p -adic group G G . We present a description of this module that generalizes the description given to ℋ ⁡ ( G , I ) \mathcal{H}(G,I) by Iwahori and Matsumoto.

Introduction

Let
F
be a non archimedean local field with residue field
k
. We assume that the characteristic of
k
is not 2.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group split over
F
and let
G=\mathbf{G}(F)
.
Let
\sigma:\mathbf{G}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}
be an algebraic involution defined over
F
. We denote by
\mathbf{H}
the algebraic group of fixed points of
\sigma
, and by
H
the
F
-points of
\mathbf{H}
. We call

X=H\backslash G
a
p
-adic symmetric space.

A fundamental problem in relative representation theory is the study of
H
-distinguished representations of
G
, that is, smooth representations
\pi
of
G
such that
Hom_{H}(\pi,\mathbb{C})\neq 0
. By Frobenius reciprocity the study of irreducible
H
-distinguished representations is the same (up to duality) as the study of quotients of the
G
module
S(X)
, the space of locally constant compactly supported functions on
X
.

Let
I
be an Iwahori subgroup of
G
(defined in
[
18
]
). The group
I
splits the category
\mathcal{M}(G)
of smooth representations of
G
(see
[
4
]
). The subcategory of representations generated by their
I
fixed vectors is the so-called principal block. The category of admissible representations generated by their
I
fixed vectors was studied in
[
6
]
and was shown to be equivalent to the category of finite dimensional
\mathcal{H}(G,I)
modules, the equivalence is given by
V\rightarrow V^{I}
.
Here,
\mathcal{H}(G,I)=S(G)^{I\times I}
is the Hecke Iwahori algebra of
G
consisting of compactly supported locally constant
I
bi-invariant functions on
G
.

A first step towards the study of smooth
H
-distinguished representations is the study of
\mathcal{H}(G,I)
modules with a non-trivial equivariant map from
S(X)^{I}
. Thus, a description of
S(X)^{I}
as a module over
\mathcal{H}(G,I)
is a crucial step. In the present work, we do exactly this. Below we describe our main results in detail: Using the affine building
\mathcal{B}
of
G
we are able to describe the
I
orbits on
X
, which give a linear basis of
S(X)^{I}
over
\mathbb{C}
. Then we describe the action of the Iwahori Matsumoto generators of
\mathcal{H}(G,I)
on a characteristic function of an
I
orbit.

This paper is the first in a series of three papers about the module
S(X)^{I}
over
H(G,I)
. In a future paper, we will describe the structure of the module
S(X)^{I}
in the special case of
X
being of minimal rank (see
[
27
]
for a definition). In an additional future paper we will use the results of this paper to answer the question of when the Steinberg representation of
G
is distinguished with respect to
H
, under the assumption of
H
being split.

1.1
Main results

Let

We consider

Theorem 1.1
.

There is an explicit action of

on

Thus,

W^{H}_{aff}(T)=W_{H,aff}(T)\backslash W_{aff}(T)

It is known that

acts on

with finitely many orbits (see
[
15
]
) and we let
A=H\backslash\mathcal{T}_{\sigma}
be a set of representatives.

Theorem
1.1
follows from the following description of the set
X/I
.

Theorem 1.2

There is an explicit bijection between the
I
orbits on

and
\cup_{T\in A}W^{H}_{aff}(T)

The proof is given in Section

.

Remark 1.3

The same description was obtained independently by Paul Broussous, see

Using this bijection we define a right action of

, the extended affine Weyl group of

We denote this action by

.

We now turn our attention to the description of the action of the Iwahori Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(G,I)
on

.
We prove two results regarding this action: the first is a generalization of the classical Iwahori-Matsumoto relations, but it is not a complete description. The second result completes the description but is more involved.
We begin with the first result.

Let
\tilde{\Delta}
be the set of simple reflections inside
W_{aff}
and let
\Omega
be the group of length zero elements in
W_{aff}
(see
2.5

.

For any

we denote
T_{w}=1_{IwI}\in\mathcal{H}(G,I)
the characteristic function of
IwI
.

The algebra

is generated (as an algebra) by
\{T_{s}|s\in\tilde{\Delta}\}
and by
\{T_{o}|o\in\Omega\}
. Thus, in order to describe the action of
\mathcal{H}(G,I)
it is enough to describe the actions of each
T_{s}
for

and each
T_{o}
for

.

In Section 5 (see Definition
5.1
) we define a length function,

, on the set of
I
orbits on
X
. This length function is similar to the standard length function on

. We prove the following (see Theorem

).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
x
be an
I
orbit on
X
, let
s\in\tilde{\Delta}
be a simple reflection, denote by
1_{x}
the characteristic function of
x
.

if
l_{\sigma}(x\times s)>l_{\sigma}(x)
then
1_{x}T_{s}=1_{x\times s}

if
l_{\sigma}(x\times s)<l_{\sigma}(x)
then
1_{x}T_{s}=(q-1)1_{x}+q1_{x\times s}

Unlike in the description of
\mathcal{H}(G,I)
given by Iwahori and Matsumoto in
[
18
]
,
l_{\sigma}(x\times s)=l_{\sigma}(x)
is a possibility and in this case the action of
T_{s}
is more complicated and is not described by the Theorem above. Nevertheless, this result is sufficient to deduce that

is finitely generated over
\mathcal{H}(G,I)
.

For the second result we rely heavily on the geometry of the affine building of
G
.
We first prove that
1_{x}(T_{s}+1)
is constant on its support and we present a description of this support in terms of chambers in the affine building of
G
.

It turns out that there are four options for the size of the support of
1_{x}(T_{s}+1)
.
Moreover, we give a way to determine the value of
1_{x}(T_{s}+1)
on its support by comparing the

lengths of the elements in the support.
To formulate the result we need further notations.

Notation 1.5
.

Let
x
be an
I
orbit on
X
,

be a simple reflection, and let

be the support of

.

Denote by

be the number of elements in

length respectively.
Meaning, let
l_{max}=\text{max}\{l_{\sigma}(z)|z\in Z\}
and let

. The value

is defined similarly.

Let

be 1 if

is defined similarly.

Theorem 1.6
.

1_{x}(T_{s}+1)
is constant on its support, denote this constant by

.
We have

If

\gamma=\frac{q-1}{n_{max}}\delta_{x,max}+\frac{2}{n_{min}}\delta_{x,min}

This result is proven in Section

Our final result is the construction of a generic module

1.2
Methods of proof

We begin by noting that the study of
I
orbits on
X
can be translated into the study of
H
orbits on
G/I
. One can identify
G/I
with the set of chambers in the affine building of
G
together with a
\Omega
coloring. Thus
X/I
can be replaced with
H
orbits on the
\Omega
colored chambers in the affine building
\mathcal{B}_{G}
.

Taking a cue from
[
12
]
, the starting point of our work is the following:

Proposition 1.7
.

Any chamber in
\mathcal{B}
is contained in a
\sigma
stable apartment. This apartment is
unique
up to an action by an element of
H
.

This proposition is the key to the classification of orbits described in Theorem
3.4
. The action of
W_{aff}
on them in an immediate consequence.

To any element of
x\in X/I
we attach the characteristic function
1_{x}
. These functions form a basis for the vector space
S(X)^{I}
. Here
x
is thought of as a
H
orbit of
\Omega
colored chambers in
\mathcal{B}_{G}
, the affine building of
G
.
A second tool we use, is our length function

l_{\sigma}:X/I\to\mathbb{N}

that is defined to be the distance between a representative
\mathcal{C}_{x}
of the orbit
x
, and
\sigma(\mathcal{C}_{x})
in the building
\mathcal{B}_{G}
.

Theorem
1.4
describes the action
T_{s}
on
1_{x}
, where
s
is a simple reflection, in the case where
l_{\sigma}(x\times s)\neq l_{\sigma}(x)
.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
relies on the following Lemma (see Lemma
5.5
in the text):

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
x\in H\backslash G/I
and let
s\in\tilde{\Delta}
be a simple reflection.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be the chamber fixed by
I
. Choose
g\in G
a representative of the orbit
x
and choose a
\sigma
-stable apartment
\mathcal{A}
containing both
g\mathcal{C},gs\mathcal{C}
.
Finally let
f=g\mathcal{C}\cap gs\mathcal{C}
and let
\Pi
be the intersection of all
\sigma
stable apartments that contain
f
.

Then we have the following criteria:

•

l_{\sigma}(x\times s)>l_{\sigma}(x)
if and only if
g\mathcal{C}\subset\Pi
.

•

l_{\sigma}(x\times s)<l_{\sigma}(x)
if and only if
gs\mathcal{C}\subset\Pi
.

The case
l_{\sigma}(x\times s)=l_{\sigma}(x)
is more complicated and requires a different approach.
We introduce a group
G_{f}
obtained as the reductive quotient associated to
f\cap\sigma(f)
(see subsection
2.3
for properties of the reductive quotient). The group
G_{f}
is a reductive group of semisimple rank one, we make the observation that the
H
orbits appearing in the support of
1_{x}(T_{s}+1)
are in bijection with the orbits of a certain subgroup
\tilde{H}
of
G_{f}
on the flag variety of
G_{f}
(See Proposition
6.6
). All this is relevant to the calculation of
1_{x}T_{s}
because a closer look at the key calculation (see Proposition
4.3
):

1_{x}(T_{s}+1)=\gamma_{f,g}D_{f}

show that
\gamma=\gamma_{f,g}
is the size of the orbit of
P_{f}\cap H
on
g\mathcal{C}
(see Proposition
7.1
), Here
P_{f}
is the point-wise stabilizer of
f
. Using the classification of reductive groups of semisimple rank 1 we are able to describe the possible values of
\gamma
.

1.3
Structure of the paper

In Section 2 we provide some necessary background on the affine Weyl group of
G
, the affine building of
G
, parahoric group schemes, the Iwahori Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(G,I)
and symmetric spaces.

In Section 3 we prove Theorem
3.4
describing the set
X/I
of
I

in terms of quotients of the extended affine Weyl group. We then introduce an action of

on

and verify Theorem

In Section 4 we perform a key computation describing the action of a Iwahori-Matsumoto generator

of

on a characteristic function

for

. The answer we obtain is in terms of a combinatorial quantity

attached to

.

In Section 5 we prove Theorem
5.3
that provides Iwahori-Matsumoto type formulas for the action of

on

. Although our formulas do not provide a full description of the module

, they are sufficient to deduce that

In Section 6 we discuss actions of involution on parahoric group schemes and obtain a bijection between

and the orbits of a symmetric subgroup of

In Section 7 we use the results of Section 6 to conclude the analysis of the action of an Iwahori-Matsumoto generator of

In Section 8 we use the results of the previous sections to introduce a generic module

over the generic Iwahori-Hecke algebra that is described using generators and relations.

In Section 9 we provide two examples for the results of the previous sections.

be a simple reflection.

We have:

1.

If
l_{\sigma}(x\times s)>l_{\sigma}(x)
then
1_{x}T_{s}=1_{x\times s}
.

2.

If
l_{\sigma}(x\times s)<l_{\sigma}(x)
then
1_{x}T_{s}=(q-1)1_{x}+q1_{x\times s}
.

Remark 5.4
.

In the group case it is always true that
l_{\sigma}(x\times s)\neq l_{\sigma}(x)
, thus the theorem indeed generalizes the classical description of Iwahori and Matsumoto. However, for a general symmetric space, it may happen that
l_{\sigma}(x\times s)=l_{\sigma}(x)
. The general case will be addressed in Theorem
7.13
.

For the proof of Theorem
5.3
we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
x\in H\backslash G/I
and let
s\in\tilde{\Delta}
be a simple reflection.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be the chamber fixed by
I
. Choose
g\in G
and choose a
\sigma
-stable apartment
\mathcal{A}
such that
g
is a representative of the orbit
x
and
g\mathcal{C},gs\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{A}
(see Remark

). Let
f=g\mathcal{C}\cap gs\mathcal{C}
.

Let
\Pi
be the intersection of all
\sigma
stable apartments that contain
f
.
Then we have the following criteria:

1.

l_{\sigma}(x\times s)>l_{\sigma}(x)
if and only if
g\mathcal{C}\subset\Pi
.

2.

l_{\sigma}(x\times xs)<l_{\sigma}(x)
if and only if
gs\mathcal{C}\subset\Pi
.

Proof.

Consider all the
\sigma
stable apartments that contain
f
, there are three cases:

1.

All of them contain
g\mathcal{C}
.

2.

All of them contain
gs\mathcal{C}
.

3.

Neither case 1 nor case 2 happens.

We will begin by showing that in case 3 we necessarily have
l_{\sigma}(x\times s)=l_{\sigma}(x)
and thus there is nothing to prove.

The intersection of all
\sigma
stable apartments that contain
f
is a convex set in
\mathcal{A}
(by Theorem
2.17
) that contains
f
. In case 3,
\Pi
does not contain any chamber in
\mathcal{A}
that contains
f
so
\Pi
is contained in
P
, the affine hyper-plane spanned by
f
.

P
is
\sigma
stable as it is spanned by
\Pi
, the intersection of
\sigma
stable apartments.

The fact that
P
is
\sigma
stable implies that
r_{P}
, the reflection across
P
, commutes with
\sigma
on
\mathcal{A}
.

Let
(\mathcal{C}_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
be a minimal gallery that starts at
\mathcal{C}_{1}=g\mathcal{C}
and ends at
\mathcal{C}_{m}=\sigma(g\mathcal{C}).
Observe that by Theorem
2.19
these chambers are all contained in the apartment
\mathcal{A}.
Applying the reflection
r_{P}
we obtain a minimal gallery
(r_{P}(\mathcal{C}_{i}))_{i=1}^{m}
that starts with
r_{P}(g\mathcal{C}_{1})=gs\mathcal{C}
and ends with
r_{P}(\sigma(g\mathcal{C}))=\sigma(r_{P}(g\mathcal{C}))=\sigma(gs\mathcal{C})
which means that
l_{\sigma}(x\times s)=l_{\sigma}(gs\mathcal{C})=l_{\sigma}(g\mathcal{C})=l_{\sigma}(x)
.

This shows that in case 3 we have
l_{\sigma}(x\times s)=l_{\sigma}(x)
.

To handle the other cases, it suffices to show that if
l_{\sigma}(gs\mathcal{C})>l_{\sigma}(g\mathcal{C})
then case 1 occurs. That is, all
\sigma
stable apartments that contain
f
also contain
g\mathcal{C}
.

If
l_{\sigma}(gs\mathcal{C})>l_{\sigma}(g\mathcal{C})
then
l_{\sigma}(gs\mathcal{C})=l_{\sigma}(g\mathcal{C})+2
as
l_{\sigma}
is constant modulo
2
on each apartment. This means that we may choose a minimal gallery of chambers from
g\mathcal{C}
to
\sigma(g\mathcal{C})
and then add
gs\mathcal{C}
at the start and
\sigma(gs\mathcal{C})
at the end and get a minimal gallery of chambers from
gs\mathcal{C}
to
\sigma(gs\mathcal{C})
. By this argument and Theorem
2.19
any
\sigma
stable apartment that contains
gs\mathcal{C}
has to contain
g\mathcal{C}
. Therefore either case 1 or case 3 happen and we know that it isn’t case 3.

The case of
l_{\sigma}(gs\mathcal{C})<l_{\sigma}(g\mathcal{C})
is treated in a similar way.

∎

Now we can deduce Theorem
5.3
.

Proof of Theorem

.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the chamber fixed by
I
, let
g\in G
be a representative of the orbit

and let

be a

Let

there is

that fixes

By Proposition

this yields
1_{x}(T_{s}+1)=1_{x\times s}+1_{x}

The second case is similar.
∎

The next corollary provides a simple formula for the action of
T_{w}
in some cases.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
x\in H\backslash G/I
be as in the previous theorem, let

and let

be the usual length function on

(see Section

for a definition).

If
l_{\sigma}(x\times w)=2\cdot l(w)+l_{\sigma}(x)
then

1_{x}T_{w}=1_{x\times w}

Proof.

Write
w=os_{1},...,s_{l(w)}
as a product of simple reflections
s_{i}\in\tilde{\Delta}
and
o\in\Omega
.

By Theorem
2.32

T_{w}=T_{o}T_{s_{1}}...T_{s_{l(w)}}
.

For any
1\leq j\leq l(w)
we have
l_{\sigma}(x\times o\times s_{1}\times....\times s_{j})\leq l_{\sigma}(x)+2j

. We must have equality; otherwise, the equality in the assumption would fail, as acting by each

can increase the length by at most

.

By Theorem
5.3
, Proposition
4.1
and induction we obtain that
1_{x}T_{o}T_{s_{1}}...T_{s_{j}}=1_{x\times o\times s_{1}\times....\times s_{j}}.
For

, we obtain the required result.
∎

This result is enough to prove that
S(X)^{I}
is finitely generated over
{\mathcal{H}}(G,I).

First we need the following two propositions.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
T
be a
\sigma
stable maximal split torus of
G
and let
\mathcal{A}
be the apartment that corresponds to
T
. The torus
T
acts on
\mathcal{A}
by translations. Let

such that its action on
\mathcal{A}
commutes with
\sigma
, then there is
t^{\prime}\in T\cap H
whose action on
\mathcal{A}
is the same as

.

Proof.

Let
\pi
be a uniformizer of
\mathcal{O}
and let

. The torus

is a direct product of
\Lambda(T)
and

(see 3.6 in
[

]
). Write

with

and

.

Choosing a chamber

, we have
\sigma(t)\sigma(\mathcal{C})=\sigma(t\mathcal{C})=t\sigma(\mathcal{C})
, so
t^{-1}\sigma(t)
acts as a translation that fixes
\sigma(\mathcal{C})
and thus it acts trivially, hence
t^{-1}\sigma(t)\in T^{0}
. Thus
t_{1}^{-1}\sigma(t_{1})\in T^{0}
. The set
\Lambda(T)
is
\sigma
stable so we obtain

or

. The elements

and

act the same way on

and the claim follows.
∎

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\sigma
stable apartment and let

. The number of
H
orbits of chambers
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathcal{A}
with

is finite.

Proof.

Let

be the maximal torus which corresponds to
\mathcal{A}
. Notice that modulo the action of

the number of chambers is finite (in fact equal to

). Thus modulo the action of

the number of pairs of chambers

in
\mathcal{A}
such that

such that

and such that

and

and

. This implies that the action of

.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group split over a
p
-adic field
F.
Let
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{G}^{\sigma}
for some algebraic involution
\sigma
and let
X=\mathbf{H}(F)\backslash\mathbf{G}(F)
be a
p
-adic symmetric space. Then
S(X)^{I}
is a finitely generated module over
{\mathcal{H}}(G,I).

Proof.

Consider the collection
\mathcal{Y}
of

H
orbits of chambers that contain a point fixed by
\sigma
. We claim that
\mathcal{Y}
is finite and that the characteristic functions of these chambers (together with some
\Omega
color) generate
S(X)^{I}
over
{\mathcal{H}}(G,I).

We first show that
\mathcal{Y}
is finite. We know that there is a finite number of
H
orbits of
\sigma
stable apartments. It is enough to fix a
\sigma
stable apartment
\mathcal{A}
and to show that in
\mathcal{A}
the number of
H
orbits of chamber with a
\sigma
fixed point is finite. If
\mathcal{C}
is a chamber that has a
\sigma
fixed point then
\sigma(\mathcal{C})\cap\mathcal{C}\neq\varnothing
. It is clear that the distance between two intersecting chambers is bounded, and thus by the previous proposition there is a finite number of
H
orbits of such chambers.

In order to prove the second part, we again fix a
\sigma
stable apartment
\mathcal{A}
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a chamber in
\mathcal{A}
without a
\sigma
fixed point. It is enough to show that there is a minimal gallery between
\mathcal{C}
and
\sigma(\mathcal{C})
such that the chamber
\mathcal{C}_{1}
which appears immediately after
\mathcal{C}_{0}=\mathcal{C}
satisfies
l_{\sigma}(\mathcal{C}_{1})=l_{\sigma}(\mathcal{C})-2
. If we show this, we can then conclude using Corollary
5.6
, by induction on
l_{\sigma}(\mathcal{C})
.

It is enough to show that there is a point
x\in\mathcal{C}
such that the line connecting
x
and
\sigma(x)
intersects some chamber adjacent to
\mathcal{C}
. Assuming the contrary we can find
x
such that
\frac{x+\sigma(x)}{2}\in\mathcal{C}
. This is a contradiction as
\mathcal{C}
does not contain
\sigma
fixed points.

∎

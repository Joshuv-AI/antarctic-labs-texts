Asymptotic decay towards steady states of solutions to very fast and singular diffusion equations.
Georgy Kitavtsev, Roman M. Taranets

Abstract
We analyze long-time behavior of solutions to a class of problems related to very fast and singular diffusion porous medium equations having nonhomogeneous in space and time source terms with zero mean. In dimensions two and three, we determine critical values of porous medium exponent for the asymptotic H 1 H^{1} -convergence of the solutions to a unique nonhomogeneous positive steady state generally to hold.

Basic estimates

By multiplying (
1a
) with
\Delta v-f(x,t)
and integrating by parts one shows the energy equality

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{dt}E(v(t))+\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}(\Delta v-f)^{2}\,dx=\int\limits_{\Omega}vf_{t}\,dx

(14)

with

E(v)=\int\limits_{\Omega}[\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+fv]\,dx,

(15)

that consequently implies

\|\nabla v(t)\|_{2}\leq C\text{ for all }t>0

(16)

provided (
11
) holds, with constant
C
depending on the value of integral in (
11
) as well as on b
E(v_{0}),\,\|f_{0}\|_{2},\,M,\,|\Omega|
. Proof of (
16
) is given in Appendix B.

Let us denote the difference between dynamical and stationary solutions by
w=v(x,t)-v_{\infty}(x)
. Then one
can rewrite (
1a
) as follows

v_{t}=v^{k}(\Delta v-f(x,t))=v^{k}\Delta w+v^{k}(f_{\infty}(x)-f(x,t)).

Multiplying the last equation by
-\Delta w
and integrating by parts results in

\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}|\Delta w|^{2}\,dx=\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}(f(x,t)-f_{\infty}(x))\Delta w\,dx.

(17)

Additionally, we will use the following estimate

\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx\leq C_{S,l}^{2}\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}|\Delta w|^{2}\,dx\Bigr)\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{\frac{kl}{2-l}}\,dx\Bigr)^{\frac{l-2}{l}},

(18)

which holds for any
l>2
in the case
N=2
and for
l\in(2,\,6]
in the case
N=3
with
C_{S,l}
being the constant in Poincare-Sobolev embedding

\|w-\bar{w}\|_{l}\leq C_{S,l}\|\nabla w\|_{2},\quad\text{where}\quad\bar{w}=\tfrac{1}{|\Omega|}\int\limits_{\Omega}w\,dx.

Combining the latter estimate with the following calculation, taking into account
\nabla w\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
on
\partial\Omega
,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\limits_{\Omega}(w-\bar{w})\Delta w\,dx\leq\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{\frac{k}{2}}|\Delta w|^{2}\,dx\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w-\bar{w}\|_{l}\|v^{-\frac{k}{2}}\|_{\frac{2l}{l-2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{S,l}\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{\frac{k}{2}}|\Delta w|^{2}\,dx\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla w\|_{2}\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{\frac{kl}{2-l}}\,dx\Bigr)^{\frac{l-2}{2l}},

which implies (
18
).

Finally, multiplying (
1a
) by
v^{-p-1}
and integrating by parts one obtains

\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v^{p}}+\tfrac{4(p+1-k)p}{(p-k)^{2}}\int\limits_{\Omega}\bigl|\nabla\bigl(\tfrac{1}{v^{(p-k)/2}}\bigr)\bigr|^{2}\,dx=p\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{f\,dx}{v^{p+1-k}}

(19)

holding for any
p>k-1>0
and
p\neq k
.

y^{\prime}(t)+\tfrac{2(p+1-k)p}{C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{2}(p-k)^{2}}y(t)^{\frac{p-k}{p}}\leq\tfrac{4(p+1-k)p}{C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{2}(p-k)^{2}}\bigl(\tfrac{M}{|\Omega|}\bigr)^{\frac{p-k}{k-1}}|\Omega|^{\frac{p-k}{p}}+p\|f\|_{r}M^{\frac{1+(p-k)(2-k)}{(p-k+1)(k-1)}}y(t)^{\frac{p-k}{p-k+1}}.

(33)

We rewrite (
33
) in the following form

y^{\prime}(t)\leq C_{1}z^{\frac{p}{p-k+1}}(t)-C_{2}z(t)+C_{3},

(34)

where we introduced the following notation:

\displaystyle z(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y(t)^{\frac{p-k}{p}},\ C_{1}=p\|f\|_{r}M^{\frac{1+(p-k)(2-k)}{(p-k+1)(k-1)}},

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{2(p+1-k)p}{C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{2}(p-k)^{2}},\quad C_{3}=2C_{2}\bigl(\tfrac{M}{|\Omega|}\bigr)^{\frac{p-k}{k-1}}|\Omega|^{\frac{p-k}{p}}.

One observes that constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
are positive if
p
belongs to the range (
32
).
The polynomial at the right-hand side of (
34
) has two roots
\lambda_{\pm}
:

0<\lambda_{-}<\bigl(\tfrac{C_{2}(p-k+1)}{pC_{1}}\bigr)^{\frac{p-k+1}{k-1}}<\lambda_{+}

(35)

provided

C_{3}<\tfrac{k-1}{p}\bigl(\tfrac{p-k+1}{pC_{1}}\bigr)^{\frac{p-k+1}{k-1}}C_{2}^{\frac{p}{k-1}}.

The latter condition can be rewritten as

\tfrac{2p}{k-1}\bigl(\tfrac{M}{|\Omega|}\bigr)^{\frac{p-k}{k-1}}|\Omega|^{\frac{p-k}{p}}\bigl(\tfrac{pC_{1}}{p-k+1}\bigr)^{\frac{p-k+1}{k-1}}<C_{2}^{\frac{p-k+1}{k-1}}.

(36)

Observe that

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{p\rightarrow k^{+}}\tfrac{2p}{k-1}\bigl(\tfrac{M}{|\Omega|}\bigr)^{\frac{p-k}{k-1}}|\Omega|^{\frac{p-k}{p}}\bigl(\tfrac{pC_{1}}{p-k+1}\bigr)^{\frac{p-k+1}{k-1}}=\tfrac{2k}{k-1}(k^{2}\|f\|_{r}M^{\frac{1}{k-1}})^{\frac{1}{k-1}}<+\infty,

while

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{p\rightarrow k^{+}}C_{2}^{\frac{p-k+1}{k-1}}=+\infty,

where we use asymptotics

C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}\sim\tfrac{C(|\Omega|,k)}{\sqrt{p-k}}\rightarrow\infty\quad\text{as}\quad p\rightarrow k^{+},

see Theorem 3.2 of
[
11
]
(also Theorem 8.5(ii) of
[
12
]
).

Therefore, for
p
being sufficiently close to value
k
from above (depending on the given values
M,\,|\Omega|,\,\|f\|_{r}
and
k=k_{cr}(r)
) inequality (
36
) and, consequently, (
35
) hold with
\lambda_{+}\rightarrow+\infty
as
p\rightarrow k^{+}
.

Now, to obtain a uniform bound on
y(t)
for all
t>0
we choose
p
being sufficiently close to value
k
from above, such that (
36
) holds as well as

y(0)\leq\lambda_{+}^{\frac{p}{p-k}}.

(37)

Such choice of
p
is possible, because
y(0)
stays bounded for
p
being close to
k
provided (
8
) holds.
Then from (
34
) and (
35
) it follows that

y(t)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v^{p}}\leq C=\min\Bigl\{y(0),\lambda_{+}^{\frac{p}{p-k}}\Bigr\}\quad\forall\,t>0.

(38)

Finally, fixing
p>k
as above and choosing

l=\tfrac{2p}{p-k}\in(2,+\infty),\quad\text{i.e. so that}\quad p=\tfrac{kl}{l-2},

in estimates (
17
)–(
18
) together with (
38
) gives (
21
) with

\bar{C}=C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{-2}C^{-\frac{k}{p}},

and, consequently, decay estimate (
10
) holds.

We note that the derived exponential asymptotic rate
\bar{C}
degenerates in the limit
p\rightarrow k^{+}(r)
, namely

C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{-2}C^{-\frac{k}{p}}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad p\rightarrow k^{+}.

This follows from unboundedness of
\lambda_{+}
in (
35
) and
C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}
as

.

3.2
Time-inhomogeneous case

In this section, we collect and prove results on asymptotic decay to the steady state
v_{\infty}(x)
of solutions to problem (
1a
)–(
1c
) considered with time-dependent source function
f(x,\,t)
in (
2
). We separate cases
k\geq k_{cr}(r)
and
k\in(\frac{r}{r-1},\,k_{cr}(r))
with
k_{cr}(r)
being defined in (
20
). It turns out that in the first case one can take
s=\infty
in (
2
) (similar to to the time-homogeneous case
f(x,t)=f(x)
), while in case
k\in(\frac{r}{r-1},\,k_{cr}(r))
for a given space integrability
r\in(1,\infty]
we can identify a maximal possible
s_{cr}=s_{cr}(k,r)\in[1,\infty)
, such that asymptotic decay to the steady state
v_{\infty}(x)
is shown for any time integrability
s\in[1,\,s_{cr}(k,r))
in (
2
). For
k\leq\frac{r}{r-1}
possibility for an asymptotic decay to
v_{\infty}(x)
remains then an open problem.

We start with the following estimation of the right-hand side of (
17
)

\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}(f-f_{\infty})\Delta w\,dx\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}(f-f_{\infty})^{2}\,dx+\tfrac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}|\Delta w|^{2}\,dx.

Combined with (
17
) this implies

\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}|\Delta w|^{2}\,dx\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}(f-f_{\infty})^{2}\,dx.

(39)

For any real
a>1
one can estimate the right-hand side of (
39
) as

\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}(f-f_{\infty})^{2}\,dx\leq\|v\|_{ak}^{k}\|f-f_{\infty}\|_{\frac{2a}{a-1}}^{2}.

Using Poincare-Sobolev embedding and (
3
) one shows

\|v\|_{ak}^{k}\leq\bigl(C_{S,ak}\|\nabla v\|_{2}+\bigl(\tfrac{|\Omega|}{M}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{ak}}\bigr)^{k}.

Choosing
a=1+2/\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon>0
small enough one derives from the last two inequalities

\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}(f-f_{\infty})^{2}\,dx\leq\tfrac{\bar{C}}{\varepsilon^{k/2}}\|f-f_{\infty}\|_{2+\varepsilon}^{2},

(40)

where we use (
16
) and asymptotics

C_{S,1+2/\varepsilon}\sim\tfrac{C(|\Omega|)}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\text{ as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

Finally, combining (
40
) with (
39
) gives

\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}|\Delta w|^{2}\,dx\leq\tfrac{\bar{C}}{\varepsilon^{k/2}}\|f-f_{\infty}\|_{2+\varepsilon}^{2}.

(41)

Below, in subsections 3.2.1 and 3.2.2, we derive uniform in time bounds on
\|\frac{1}{v}\|_{\frac{kl}{l-2}}
for certain
l>2
.
Combining them with (
18
) and (
41
) will yield asymptotic decay estimate (
10
) for any
k>1
and
r>2
. We note that this proof can be also generalized for the case
r\in(1,\,2]
if one treats (
17
) similarly as in section 4.2 for
N=3
.

3.2.1
Case
k\geq k_{cr}(r)

Here, w.l.o.g., we assume that
s=\infty
and
r\in(2,\,+\infty]
in (
2
). In the first part of the proof, we follow exactly the lines of subsection 3.1.1 (in the case
k>k_{cr}(r)
) or subsection 3.1.2 (in the case
k=k_{cr}(r)
) establishing uniform in time bounds (
27
) or (
38
), respectively, i.e.

\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v^{p}}\leq C\quad\forall t>0\quad\text{and fixed}\quad p\in(k,\,+\infty),\ C>0.

We note just that by deriving bounds (
27
) or (
38
)
\|f\|_{r}
should be now replaced by

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,+\infty;L^{r}(\Omega))}
.

Next, the latter bound combined with (
41
) and (
18
) taken with
l=\frac{2p}{p-k}
implies

\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx+C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{-2}C^{-\frac{k}{p}}\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx\leq\tfrac{\bar{C}}{\varepsilon^{k/2}}\|f-f_{\infty}\|_{2+\varepsilon}^{2}.

Applying comparison principle to the last inequality one deduces that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\Bigl\{-C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{-2}C^{-\frac{k}{p}}\,t\Bigr\}\Big(\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{0}|^{2}\,dx+

(42)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\tfrac{\bar{C}}{\varepsilon^{k/2}}\int_{0}^{t}\exp\Bigl\{C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{-2}C^{-\frac{k}{p}}\,\tau\Bigr\}\|f(\cdot,\tau)-f_{\infty}(.)\|_{2+\varepsilon}^{2}\,d\tau\Big).

The last estimate would imply asymptotic decay

\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow+\infty

(43)

provided

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\exp\Bigl\{-C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{-2}C^{-\frac{k}{p}}\,t\Bigr\}\int_{0}^{t}\exp\Bigl\{C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{-2}C^{-\frac{k}{p}}\,\tau\Bigr\}\|f(\cdot,\tau)-f_{\infty}(.)\|_{2+\varepsilon}^{2}\,d\tau=0,

which is true by (
12
).
Using L’Hospital’s rule one observes that the last limit holds if and only if

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|f(\cdot,t)-f_{\infty}(.)\|_{2+\varepsilon}^{2}=0.

In that case (
43
) holds and, after substituting
w=v(x,t)-v_{\infty}(x)
, one has

\|\nabla(v(\cdot,t)-v_{\infty}(.))\|_{2}\rightarrow 0\text{ and }\|v(\cdot,t)-v_{\infty}(.)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }t\rightarrow+\infty.

We note that the decay rate here is determined by the slowest one between two: given convergence rate of
\|f(\cdot,\tau)-f_{\infty}(.)\|_{2+\varepsilon}
to zero and exponential
C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{-2}C^{-\frac{k}{p}}
one.

3.2.2
Case
\tfrac{r}{r-1}<k<k_{cr}(r)

Here, we assume
f\in L^{s}(0,+\infty;L^{r}(\Omega))
with
s\in[1,\infty)
and
r\in(2,\,+\infty]
.
First, for any parameter
\beta\in[0,k-1)
one estimates the last term in (
19
), using Hölder inequality and (
3

\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{f\,dx}{v^{p+1-k}}=\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{f\,dx}{v^{p+1-k-\beta}\cdot v^{\beta}}\leq\|f\|_{r}M^{\tfrac{\beta}{k-1}}\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v^{(p+1-k-\beta)k^{\prime}}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{k^{\prime}}},

(44)

where

(45)

Let us choose
\beta
so that
(p+1-k-\beta)k^{\prime}=p
, i.e.

\beta=\tfrac{(k-1)^{2}r-p(k-1)}{r(p-k+1)}.

(46)

Condition
\beta\in[0,k-1)
implies the following constraints on
p
:

p\in\bigl(\tfrac{2(k-1)r}{r+1},\,(k-1)r\bigr]

(47)

with the latter interval having non-zero length for
r>1
. Additionally, we demand
p+1-k-\beta>0
,
which, in view of (
46
), implies

p>\tfrac{(k-1)(2r-1)}{r}.

We note that for
1<k<k_{cr}(r)
and
r>1
one has

\tfrac{2(k-1)r}{r+1}<\tfrac{(k-1)(2r-1)}{r}<k,

and, therefore, we update the constraints on
p
in (
47
) to

p\in\bigl(k,\,(k-1)r\bigr]

(48)

with the latter interval having non-zero length, because
\frac{r}{r-1}<k
.
We conclude that for any
p
taken from (
48
) by (
19
) and (
44
)–(
46
)

\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v^{p}}\leq p\|f\|_{r}M^{\frac{(k-1)r-p}{r(p-k+1)}}\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v^{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{pr-(k-1)(2r-1)}{r(p-k+1)}}

holds. Applying Grönwall inequality to this estimate results in the following bound

\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v^{p}}\leq\Bigl[\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v_{0}^{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{(k-1)(r-1)}{r(p-k+1)}}+\tfrac{p(k-1)(r-1)}{r(p-k+1)}\|f\|_{L^{s}(L^{r})}M^{\frac{(k-1)r-p}{r(p-k+1)}}\,t^{\frac{s-1}{s}}\Bigr]^{\frac{r(p-k+1)}{(k-1)(r-1)}}

(49)

holding for any
p
from interval (
48
).

Finally, choosing

l=\tfrac{2p}{p-k}\in(2,\infty),\text{ i.e. so that}\quad p=\tfrac{kl}{l-2},

in estimate (
18
) together with (
49
) implies

g(t)\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}|\Delta w|^{2}\,dx

with

g(t)=C_{S,\,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{-2}\Bigl[\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v_{0}^{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{(k-1)(r-1)}{r(p-k+1)}}+\tfrac{p(k-1)(r-1)}{r(p-k+1)}\|f\|_{L^{s}(L^{r})}M^{\frac{(k-1)r-p}{r(p-k+1)}}t^{\frac{s-1}{s}}\Bigr]^{\frac{-kr(p-k+1)}{p(k-1)(r-1)}}_{.}

(50)

Combining that with (
41
) (while noting that
f_{\infty}=0
) gives

\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx+g(t)\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx\leq\tfrac{\bar{C}}{\varepsilon^{k/2}}\|f\|_{2+\varepsilon}^{2}.

Applying comparison principle to the last inequality one deduces that

\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx\leq\exp\Bigl\{-\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\}\Big(\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{0}|^{2}\,dx+\\
\tfrac{\bar{C}}{\varepsilon^{k/2}}\int_{0}^{t}\exp\Bigl\{\int_{0}^{\tau}g(\tilde{\tau})\,d\tilde{\tau}\Bigr\}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{2+\varepsilon}^{2}\,d\tau\Big).

The last estimate would imply asymptotic decay

\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow+\infty

(51)

provided

\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,d\tau\rightarrow\infty\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty,

(52)

as well as,

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\exp\Bigl\{-\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\}\int_{0}^{t}\exp\Bigl\{\int_{0}^{\tau}g(\tilde{\tau})\,d\tilde{\tau}\Bigr\}\|f(\cdot,\tau)\|_{2+\varepsilon}^{2}\,d\tau=0

hold together.
Using L’Hospital’s rule and (
50
), one observes that the last limit holds if and only if

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{2+\varepsilon}^{2}t^{\frac{(s-1)kr(p-k+1)}{sp(k-1)(r-1)}}=0.

(53)

Similarly, (
52
) holds provided

\tfrac{(s-1)kr(p-k+1)}{sp(k-1)(r-1)}\leq 1.

The last condition together with the constraint (
48
) on
p
implies necessarily
that

s\in[1,\,s_{cr}(k,r))\quad\text{with}\quad s_{cr}(k,r)=\tfrac{r}{r-(k-1)(r-1)}.

Note that
s_{cr}>1
for
k<k_{cr}(r)
and
r>1
.

Exploring additionally (
53
) we conclude that for any

f\in L^{s}(0,+\infty;L^{r}(\Omega))

(54)

with
s\in[1,\,s_{cr}(k,r))
and
r>2
asymptotic decay (
51
) holds.
Substituting
w=v(x,t)-v_{\infty}(x)
then yields

\|\nabla(v(\cdot,t)-v_{\infty}(.))\|_{2}\rightarrow 0\text{ and }\|v(\cdot,t)-v_{\infty}(.)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }t\rightarrow+\infty.

4.2
Time-inhomogeneous case

In this section, we consider time-dependent source function
f(x,\,t)
in (
2
)
and separate cases
k\in(k_{cr}(r),\,+\infty)
and
k\in[\frac{r}{r-3/2},\,k_{cr}(r)]
with
k_{cr}(r)
given in (
55
). It turns out that in the first case one can take
s=\infty
in (
2
) (similar to to the time-homogeneous case
f(x,t)=f(x)

k\in[\frac{r}{r-3/2},\,k_{cr}(r)]
for the given space integrability exponent

we can identify a maximal possible
s_{cr}=s_{cr}(k,r)\in[1,\infty)
, such that asymptotic decay to the steady state
v_{\infty}(x)
is shown for any time integrability exponent

in (
2
). For
k<\frac{r}{r-3/2}
possibility for an asymptotic decay to
v_{\infty}(x)
remains then an open problem.

Our starting point is given by estimate (
17
). Due to lack of continuous embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
for
p>6
and
N=3
, we estimate the right-hand side of (
17
) in a different way then in subsection 3.2. For
f
in (
2
) we introduce the Bogovskii’s vector function
\mathbf{B}f(x,\,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
having properties

\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{B}f(x,\,t)=f(x,t)\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in\Omega\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{B}f(x,t)=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,

(63)

for all
t>0
, and such that

\|\mathbf{B}f(\cdot,\,t)\|_{W_{0}^{1,\,r}}\leq C_{r}\|f(\cdot,\,t)\|_{r}\quad\text{for all}\quad t>0

(64)

with constant
C_{r}>0
depending only on
r
. Existence of
\mathbf{B}f
having these properties was shown in
[
14
]
.
We note, in particular, due to continuous embedding
W^{1,r}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{Nr}{N-r}}(\Omega)
for
r\leq N
that (
12
) implies for
N\geq 2

\|\mathbf{B}f(\cdot,\,t)-\mathbf{B}f_{\infty}(.)\|_{2}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow+\infty,

(65)

while (
11
) implies

\|\mathbf{B}(f_{t})(.,t)\|_{2}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow+\infty.

(66)

Next, let us rewrite formula (
17
) in the form

\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx+\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}v(f-f_{\infty})\,dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}(\Delta w-f+f_{\infty})^{2}\,dx=\int\limits_{\Omega}vf_{t}\,dx.

Using integration by parts and (
63
) the last identity transforms as

\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla w-\mathbf{B}(f-f_{\infty})|^{2}\,dx-\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}|\mathbf{B}(f-f_{\infty})|^{2}\,dx+\\
\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}(\nabla\cdot[\nabla w-\mathbf{B}(f-f_{\infty})])^{2}\,dx=\int\limits_{\Omega}wf_{t}\,dx=-\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla w\cdot\mathbf{B}(f_{t})\,dx=\\
-\int\limits_{\Omega}(\nabla w-\mathbf{B}(f-f_{\infty}))\cdot\mathbf{B}(f_{t})\,dx-\int\limits_{\Omega}\mathbf{B}(f-f_{\infty})\cdot\mathbf{B}(f_{t})\,dx.

This yields

\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}z^{2}\,dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}(\nabla\cdot z)^{2}\,dx=-\int\limits_{\Omega}z\cdot\mathbf{B}(f_{t})\,dx

with
z:=\nabla w-\mathbf{B}(f-f_{\infty})
. Additionally, arguing as in the proof of estimate (
18
), one shows

\int\limits_{\Omega}z^{2}\,dx\leq C_{S,l}^{2}\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{k}|\nabla z|^{2}\,dx\Bigr)\Bigl(\|\tfrac{1}{v}\|_{\frac{kl}{l-2}}\Bigr)^{k}.

Finally, combining the last two inequalities and using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}z^{2}\,dx+\Bigl(C_{S,l}^{2k}\|\tfrac{1}{v}\|_{\frac{kl}{l-2}}\Bigr)^{-k}\int\limits_{\Omega}z^{2}\,dx\leq\Bigl(C_{S,l}^{2k}\|\tfrac{1}{v}\|_{\frac{kl}{l-2}}\Bigr)^{k}\|\mathbf{B}(f_{t})\|_{2}^{2}.

(67)

In subsections 4.2.1 and 4.2.2 below, we derive uniform in time bounds on
\|\frac{1}{v}\|_{\frac{kl}{l-2}}
for certain
l>2
. These bounds combined with (
67
) will then imply the asymptotic decay (
13
).

4.2.1
Case
k>k_{cr}(r)

Here, w.l.o.g., we assume that
s=\infty
and
r\in(3/2,\,+\infty]
in (
2
). In the first part of the proof,
we follow exactly the lines of subsection 4.1.1 (in the case
k\in[\frac{4r-2}{r-2},\,+\infty)
) or subsection 4.1.2 (in the case
k\in(\frac{4r-2}{r-2},\,k_{cr}(r))
) establishing uniform in time bounds (
27
) or (
59
), receptively, i.e.

\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v^{p}}\leq C\quad\forall\,t>0\text{ and fixed }p\in[\tfrac{3k}{2},\,+\infty),\ C>0.

We note just that by deriving (
27
) or (
59
)
\|f\|_{r}
should be now replaced by
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,+\infty;L^{r}(\Omega))}
.

Next, the latter bound combined with (
67
) taken with
l=\frac{2p}{p-k}
imply

\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}z^{2}\,dx+C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{-2}C^{-\frac{k}{p}}\int\limits_{\Omega}z^{2}\,dx\leq C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{2}C^{\frac{k}{p}}\|\mathbf{B}(f_{t})\|_{2}^{2}.

Applying comparison principle to the last inequality one deduces that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla z|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\Bigl\{-C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{-2}C^{-\frac{k}{p}}\,t\Bigr\}\Big(\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla z_{0}|^{2}\,dx+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{2}C^{\frac{k}{p}}\int_{0}^{t}\exp\Bigl\{C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{-2}C^{-\frac{k}{p}}\,\tau\Bigr\}\|\mathbf{B}(f_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\,d\tau\Big).

The last estimate would imply asymptotic decay

\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla z|^{2}\,dx\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow+\infty

(68)

provided

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\exp\Bigl\{-C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{-2}C^{-\frac{k}{p}}\,t\Bigr\}\int_{0}^{t}\exp\Bigl\{C_{S,\frac{2p}{p-k}}^{-2}C^{-\frac{k}{p}}\,\tau\Bigr\}\|\mathbf{B}(f_{t})\|_{2}^{2}\,d\tau=0.

Using L’Hospital’s rule one observes that this holds because of (
66
). Hence, (
68
) holds and, after substituting
z=\nabla w-\mathbf{B}(f-f_{\infty})
and

, one obtains

\|\nabla(v(\cdot,t)-v_{\infty}(.))\|_{2}\rightarrow 0\text{ and }\|v(\cdot,t)-v_{\infty}(.)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }t\rightarrow+\infty,

where we also use (
65
).

4.2.2
Case
\frac{r}{r-3/2}\leq k\leq k_{cr}(r)

Here, we assume
f\in L^{s}(0,+\infty;L^{r}(\Omega))
with
s\in[1,\infty)
and
r\in(3/2,\,+\infty)
and argue similarly as in subsection 3.2.2. First, we set
p=\frac{3k}{2}
and consider estimate (
44
) with
k^{\prime}
and
\beta
given by (
45
) and (
46
), respectively. Note that both conditions
\beta\in[0,\,k-1)
and
p+1-k-\beta>0
hold for our range of
k
. Then from (
19
) and (
44
)–(
46
) considered with
p=3k/2
one gets

\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v^{\frac{3k}{2}}}\leq\tfrac{3k}{2}\|f\|_{r}M^{\frac{2(k-1)r-3k}{r(k+2)}}\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v^{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{3kr-2(k-1)(2r-1)}{r(k+2)}}.

Next, applying Grönwall’s inequality to this estimate results in the bound

\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v^{\frac{3k}{2}}}\leq\Bigl[\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v_{0}^{\frac{3k}{2}}}\Bigr)^{\frac{2(k-1)(r-1)}{r(k+2)}}+\tfrac{3k(k-1)(r-1)}{r(k+2)}\|f\|_{L^{s}(L^{r})}M^{\frac{2(k-1)r-3k}{r(k+2)}}\,t^{\frac{s-1}{s}}\Bigr]^{\frac{r(k+2)}{2(k-1)(r-1)}}.

(69)

Choosing

l=6,\text{ i.e. so that }p=\tfrac{kl}{l-2},

in estimate (
67
) together with (
69
) imply

\tfrac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{\Omega}z^{2}\,dx+g(t)\int\limits_{\Omega}z^{2}\,dx\leq\tfrac{\|\mathbf{B}(f_{t})\|_{2}^{2}}{g(t)}.

with

g(t)=C_{S,\,6}^{-2}\Bigl[\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v_{0}^{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{2(k-1)(r-1)}{r(k+2)}}\!\!\!\!+\tfrac{3k(k-1)(r-1)}{r(k+2)}\|f\|_{L^{s}(L^{r})}M^{\frac{2(k-1)r-3k}{r(k+2)}}t^{\frac{s-1}{s}}\Bigr]^{\frac{-r(k+2)}{3(k-1)(r-1)}}\!\!.

(70)

Applying comparison principle to the last inequality one deduces that

\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla z|^{2}\,dx\leq\exp\Bigl\{-\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\}\times\\
\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla z_{0}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\exp\Bigl\{\int_{0}^{\tau}g(\tilde{\tau})\,d\tilde{\tau}\Bigr\}\tfrac{\|\mathbf{B}(f_{\tau})\|_{2}^{2}}{g(\tau)}\,d\tau\Bigr).

\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla z|^{2}\,dx\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow+\infty

(71)

provided

\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,d\tau\rightarrow\infty\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty,

(72)

as well as,

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\exp\Bigl\{-\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\}\int_{0}^{t}\exp\Bigl\{\int_{0}^{\tau}g(\tilde{\tau})\,d\tilde{\tau}\Bigr\}\tfrac{\|\mathbf{B}(f_{t})\|_{2}^{2}}{g(t)}\,d\tau=0

hold together.
Using L’Hospital’s rule and (

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathbf{B}(f_{t})\|_{2}\cdot t^{\frac{(s-1)r(k+2)}{6s(k-1)(r-1)}}=0.

(73)

Similarly, (
72
) holds provided

\tfrac{(s-1)r(k+2)}{3s(k-1)(r-1)}\leq 1.

The last condition implies, necessarily,
that

s\in[1,\,s_{cr}(k,r)]\quad\text{with}\quad s_{cr}(k,r)=\tfrac{r(k+2)}{r(5-2k)+3(k-1)}.

Note that

for

and

.

Exploring additionally (
73
) and using (
64

11
) we conclude that for any

Substituting there

z=\nabla w-\mathbf{B}(f-f_{\infty})
and

\|\nabla(v(\cdot,t)-v_{\infty}(.))\|_{2}\rightarrow 0\text{ and }\|v(\cdot,t)-v_{\infty}(.)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }t\rightarrow+\infty,

where we also use (
65
).

5
Discussion

In this study, we identified critical mobilities
k=k_{cr}(r)
(
20
) and (
55
) for problem (
1a
)–(
1c
) considered with source term (
2
) in dimensions
N=2
and
N=3
. In dimension
N=2
, Theorems 1.1–1.2 imply asymptotic decay in
H^{1}
-norm of dynamical solutions to the unique positive state
u_{\infty}
for
k\geq k_{cr}(r)
and provide with explicit decay rates. In dimension
N=3
, the same is shown for
k>k_{cr}(r)
and possibility of asymptotic decay for
k=k_{cr}(r)
remains then an open question. Interestingly, condition for existence of a unique positive steady state
v_{\infty}
has also form
k\geq k_{cr}(r)
for
r\in(N/2,\,+\infty]
(see Appendix A.2). This suggests that for
k<k_{cr}(r)
possibility of decay to a nonhomogeneous steady state may be conditional to certain relations between given
M
,
|\Omega|
and
\|f\|_{r}
.
Besides that, possibility of decay to constant profile
v_{\infty}
corresponding to
f_{\infty}=0
(see (
76
) in Appendix A.2) for
k\in(1,\,\frac{r}{r-1}]
for
N=2
and
k\in(1,\,\frac{r}{r-3/2})
for
N=3
is unknown.

Other open questions related to the assumptions of Theorems 1.1-1.2 include question about positivity of solutions for all
t>0
for positive initial profiles
v_{0}>0
in case
r\in(N/2,\,+\infty)
as well as existence and regularity properties of solutions starting from nonnegative initial data
v_{0}\geq 0
. Possible existence and decay of sign-changing solutions to (
1a
)–(
1c
) considered with (
2
) in certain rages of
k>1
and
p=1
remained also out of the scope of this study.

Finally, investigation of the limiting cases
k\rightarrow 1^{+}
or
k\rightarrow+\infty
in (
1a
)–(
1c
) corresponding to
m\rightarrow+\infty
and
m\rightarrow 1
in the PME problem (
5a
)–(
5c
) might be of interest. In both of the limits
many of our estimates degenerate.

Appendix A
Positivity of solutions

In this section, we discuss positivity properties of solutions
v(x,t)
to problem (
1a
)–(
1c
) and
of the stationary one
v_{\infty}(x)
to (
7a
)–(
7c
).

A.1
Positivity of dynamic solutions

Using a priori estimate (
19
), we can show that solutions
v(x,t)
to (
1a
)–(
1c
) stay positive for all
t>0
and
x\in\bar{\Omega}
provided initially
v_{0}(x)>0
. This result is shown here for all parameter values
k\in(1,\,+\infty)
and in any dimension
N\geq 1
, but only in case
r=+\infty
considered in (
2
). Positivity of solutions in cases
r\in(N/2,\,+\infty)
then remains an open problem.

The proof in case
r=+\infty
proceeds as follows. Let us estimate the term at the right-hand side of (
19
) as

\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{f\,dx}{v^{p+1-k}}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v^{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{p+1-k}{p}}|\Omega|^{\frac{k-1}{p}},

and denote

y(t)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v^{p}}.

Then (
19
) implies

y^{\prime}(t)\leq p\|f\|_{\infty}|\Omega|^{\frac{k-1}{p}}y^{\frac{p+1-k}{p}}(t).

Applying Grönwall’s inequality to this estimate results in the following bound

y(t)\leq\Bigl(y(0)^{\frac{k-1}{p}}+(k-1)\|f\|_{L^{s}(0,+\infty;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}|\Omega|^{\frac{k-1}{p}}t^{\frac{s}{s-1}}\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{k-1}},

i.e.

\|v(\cdot,\,t)^{-1}\|_{p}\leq\Bigl(\|v_{0}^{-1}\|_{p}^{k-1}+(k-1)\|f\|_{L^{s}(0,+\infty;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}|\Omega|^{\frac{k-1}{p}}t^{\frac{s}{s-1}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}.

Letting
p\rightarrow\infty
in this inequality gives

\|v(\cdot,\,t)^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq\Bigl(\|v_{0}^{-1}\|_{\infty}^{k-1}+(k-1)\|f\|_{L^{s}(0,+\infty;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}t^{\frac{s}{s-1}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}.

Hence,
v(x,t)>0
for all
t>0
and
x\in\bar{\Omega}
provided
v_{0}>0
. We note that the obtained bound from below on

v(x,t)
is not uniform in time, but still implies smoothness and uniqueness of solutions
v(\cdot,t)>0
for all
t>0
.

A.2
Existence of positive steady states
v_{\infty}(x)

Let us recall the definitions (
20
) and (
55
) for the critical value of exponent
k
in dimensions
N=2
and
N=3
, respectively. First, we show that for any parameter

there exists a unique positive stationary solution

to problem (
7a
)–(

Let

be a solution to (
7a
)–(
7b

holds for any

and some constant

Let us further consider nonnegative solutions

For them (

M=\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{v_{\infty}^{k-1}}\geq\frac{1}{L^{k-1}}\int\limits_{\Omega}\tfrac{dx}{|x-x_{0}|^{\lambda(k-1)}}

Appendix B
Boundedness of
\|\nabla v\|_{2}

Here, we prove formula (
16
) provided (
11
)–(
12
) hold. From energy inequality (
14
) one obtains

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E(v_{0})-\int\limits_{\Omega}fv\,dx+\iint\limits_{Q_{t}}{vf_{t}\,dx\,d\tau}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E(v_{0})+\|v\|_{2}\|f\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|v\|_{2}\|f_{\tau}\|_{2}\,d\tau.

Combining this estimate with Poincare inequality

\|v\|_{2}-\Bigr(\tfrac{|\Omega|}{M}\Bigl)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|v-\Bigr(\tfrac{|\Omega|}{M}\Bigl)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\|_{2}\leq C_{P}\|\nabla v\|_{2},

gives

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E(v_{0})+\|f\|_{2}\Bigl(C_{P}\|\nabla v\|_{2}+\Bigr(\tfrac{|\Omega|}{M}\Bigl)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigr)

(77)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Bigr(C_{P}\|\nabla v\|_{2}+\bigl(\tfrac{|\Omega|}{M}\bigl)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigr)\|f_{\tau}\|_{2}\,d\tau.

Additionally, one has

\tfrac{d}{dt}\|f\|_{2}=\tfrac{<f,\,f_{t}>}{\|f\|_{2}}\leq\|f_{t}\|_{2},

i.e.

\|f\|_{2}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|f_{\tau}\|_{2}\,d\tau.

Combining the last estimate with (
77
), while denoting

m(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\|f_{\tau}\|_{2}\,d\tau\quad\text{and}\quad m_{\infty}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\|f_{\tau}\|_{2}\,d\tau<\infty,

yields

(\|\nabla v\|_{2}-C_{P}\|f_{0}\|_{2}-C_{P}m_{\infty})^{2}\leq 2E(v_{0})+C_{P}^{2}(\|f_{0}\|_{2}+m_{\infty})^{2}+\\
2C_{P}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v\|_{2}m^{\prime}(\tau)\,d\tau+2(\|f_{0}\|_{2}+2m_{\infty})\Bigl(\tfrac{|\Omega|}{M}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq a_{0}+2\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl|\|\nabla v\|_{2}-C_{P}\|f_{0}\|_{2}-C_{P}m_{\infty}\Bigr|m^{\prime}(\tau)\,d\tau,

(78)

where we denoted

a_{0}=2E(v_{0})+C_{P}^{2}(\|f_{0}\|_{2}+m_{\infty})^{2}+2m_{\infty}C_{P}(\|f_{0}\|_{2}+\\
2m_{\infty})+2(\|f_{0}\|_{2}+2m_{\infty})\Bigl(\tfrac{|\Omega|}{M}\Bigl)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The last inequality after an application of Bihari-LaSalle lemma to

(\|\nabla v\|_{2}-\|f_{0}\|_{2}-m_{\infty})^{2}
, while
using
m^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
and
m(0)=0
, implies

|\|\nabla v\|_{2}-C_{P}\|f_{0}\|_{2}-C_{P}m_{\infty}|\leq\sqrt{2a_{0}}+m(t)\leq\sqrt{2a_{0}}+m_{\infty},

whence we obtain the sought estimate (
16
) holding for all
t>0
with

C=\sqrt{2a_{0}}+(C_{P}+1)m_{\infty}+C_{P}\|f_{0}\|_{2}.

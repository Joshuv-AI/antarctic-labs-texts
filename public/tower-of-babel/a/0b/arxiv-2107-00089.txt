Homogenization estimates for high order elliptic operators
S. E. Pastukhova

Abstract




In the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, we study homogenization of a divergence form elliptic operator

A_{\varepsilon}
of order
2m\geq 4
with measurable
\varepsilon
-periodic coefficients, where

\varepsilon
is a small parameter.
For the resolvent
(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}
, we construct an approximation
with the remainder term of order
\varepsilon^{2}

in the operator

(L^{2}{\to}H^{m})
-norm, using the resolvent of the homogenized operator, solutions of several auxiliary periodic problems on the unit cube, and smoothing operators. The homogenized operator here
differs from the one commonly employed in homogenization.

1
Introduction

The present paper is devoted to the homogenization of high order elliptic operators with periodic coefficients. We mean
operators of an arbitrary even order
2m\geq 4
.
The first qualitative results in this topic were obtained long ago in 70s (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
). In this paper, we are interested in estimates for the homogenization error, which are of operator type, and continue the recent studies of
[
3
]
–
[
8
]
, where the approach proposed in
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
was applied in different situations concerning high order elliptic operators. Here, we construct approximations for the resolvents of high order operators with the remainder term
O(\varepsilon^{2})
as
\varepsilon\to 0

in the energy operator norm, i.e., in the operator

(L^{2}{\to}H^{m})
-norm.

1.1.
In the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d{\geq}2
, we consider the following equation of an even order
2m{\geq}4
:

\begin{array}[]{cc}u^{\varepsilon}\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad(A_{\varepsilon}+1)u^{\varepsilon}=f,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),&\\
A_{\varepsilon}=(-1)^{m}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}D^{\alpha}(a^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}(x)D^{\beta}),&\\
\end{array}

(1.1)

with rapidly
oscillating
\varepsilon
-periodic coefficients

a^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}(x)=a_{\alpha\beta}(y)|_{y=\varepsilon^{-1}x},

for small
\varepsilon{\in}(0,1)
.
Here,
D^{\alpha}
denotes the multiderivative

D^{\alpha}=D_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots D_{d}^{\alpha_{d}},\quad D_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\quad i=1,\ldots,d,

where

\alpha{=}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})
is the multiindex of length

|\alpha|{=}\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{d}
with
\alpha_{j}{\in}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
; the coefficients

a_{\alpha\beta}(y)
are measurable periodic real-valued functions, and
the unit cube
Y{=}[-1/2,1/2)^{d}
is the periodicity cell.

We assume that the following boundedness and ellipticity conditions hold:

\displaystyle{\|a_{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}(Y)}\leq\lambda_{1},}\atop\displaystyle{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}a_{\alpha\beta}(x)D^{\beta}\varphi D^{\alpha}\varphi\,dx\geq\lambda_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=m}|D^{\alpha}\varphi|^{2}\,dx\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

(1.2)

for some positive constants
\lambda_{0}
,
\lambda_{1}
and all multiindices
\alpha
and
\beta
of length
m
. No symmetry conditions are imposed on the coefficients
a_{\alpha\beta}
.

In (
1.1
), we use the Sobolev space
H^{m}{=}H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, equipped with the norm

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{m}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leq m}|D^{\alpha}u|^{2}\,dx.

As known, the set
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of smooth compactly supported functions is dense in
H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the norm can be equivalently introduced by a simpler way

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{m}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=m}|D^{\alpha}u|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{2}\,dx,

G
-convergence and homogenization issues for differential operators
A_{\varepsilon}
in (
1.1
) have been studied since the 70s.
Even more general operators were considered from this point of view in
[
2
]
, namely, divergence form operators

A_{\varepsilon}=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leq m,|\beta|\leq m}(-1)^{|\alpha|}D^{\alpha}(a^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha\beta}(x)D^{\beta}),

(1.3)

with lower order terms. Moreover, the periodicity of coefficients was not necessarily required for (
1.3
), instead of which the so called

-condition was assumed in
[
2
]
.
The well known result on homogenization of the operator
A_{\varepsilon}
in (
1.1
) means the closeness, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, in the sense of the strong operator topology between the resolvent
(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}
, regarded as an operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and the resolvent
(\hat{A}+1)^{-1}f
of the homogenized operator
\hat{A}
.
More exactly,

(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}f
converge to

(\hat{A}+1)^{-1}f
in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm as
\varepsilon\to 0
for any

. The homogenized operator
\hat{A}
is of the same class (
1.2
) as the original operator

, but much simpler:

\hat{A}=(-1)^{m}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}D^{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\alpha\beta}D^{\beta},

(1.4)

where the coefficients
\hat{a}_{\alpha\beta}
are constant and can be defined with the help of auxiliary problems on the periodicity cell

1.1

u\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\hat{A}u+u=f,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(1.5)

and the above result about the strong resolvent convergence means that the solutions to the problems
(
1.1
) and (
1.5
) are connected by the limit relation

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|u^{\varepsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0

(1.6)

for any right-hand side function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

1.2.
In
[
11
]
,
[
3
]
and
[

,
the stronger operator convergence of

-norm; moreover, the convergence rate estimate with respect to the parameter

\|(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}-(\hat{A}+1)^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\varepsilon,

(1.7)

[
3
]

We note that

\|\varepsilon^{m}\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}{\leq}c,\quad\|\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}{\leq}c.

(1.9)

Thus, the estimate (
1.7

In
[
5

[

(\hat{A}+1)^{-1}

with a remainder of order

Indeed, the following estimate holds

\|(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}{-}(\hat{A}+1)^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\varepsilon^{2},

(1.10)

where the constant
C

1.2

In terms of the solutions to the problems (
1.1
) and (
1.5

\|u^{\varepsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

(1.11)

1.6

Without the symmetry condition on coefficients, it is shown
[
5

that
the approximation of
\varepsilon^{2}
order for the resolvent

in the

-operator norm becomes more complicated, namely,

(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}=(\hat{A}+1)^{-1}+\varepsilon\mathcal{K}_{1}+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

(1.12)

where the correcting operator

\varepsilon

1.8

The question arises how to construct the asymptotic expansion for

2
Improved
H^{m}
-approximation

2.1.
Our purpose is to obtain an
\varepsilon^{2}
-order approximation of the resolvent
(A_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}
in
the operator
(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}){\to}H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
-norm. It will be the sum of the zeroth approximation and
several correctors, the number of which increases in comparison with
the approximation from (
1.8
). By contrast to (
1.8
), one should take for the zeroth approximation, instead of
(\hat{A}+1)^{-1}
, the resolvent
(\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}+1)^{-1}
of the operator
\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}

\hat{A}

\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}=(-1)^{m}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=m}D^{\alpha}(\sum\limits_{|\beta|=m}\hat{a}_{\alpha\beta}D^{\beta}+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{|\delta|=m+1}b_{\alpha\delta}D^{\delta}),

(2.1)

where the constant coefficients
\hat{a}_{\alpha\beta}
, which
are the same as in (
1.4
), and
b_{\alpha\delta}
are determined via solutions to cell problems in Section 3.
Clearly,

\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}=\hat{A}+\varepsilon B,

where the differential operator
B
is of order
2m+1
; thereby,
\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}
is obtained from the commonly used homogenized operator
\hat{A}
by a singular perturbation.

We introduce a new version of the homogenized equation with the operator (

(\hat{A}_{\varepsilon}+1)\hat{u}^{\varepsilon}=f,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(2.2)

Since the coefficients of the equation are constant, its solution can be obtained with the help of
the Fourier transform.

Applying the Fourier transform to Equation
(
2.2
), we get the equality

\left(1+\Lambda(\xi)+i\varepsilon\Lambda_{0}(\xi)\right)F[\hat{u}^{\varepsilon}]=F[f],\quad i=\sqrt{-1},

(2.3)

where
f(x)\to F[f](\xi)
is the Fourier transform and

\Lambda(\xi){=}\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\hat{a}_{\alpha\beta}\xi^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta},\quad\Lambda_{0}(\xi){=}\sum_{|\alpha|=m,|\delta|=m+1}b_{\alpha\delta}\xi^{\alpha}\xi^{\delta},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Given any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and multiindex
\alpha{=}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})
, we have

\xi^{\alpha}=\xi_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\xi_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
according to our convention (see it after (
1.1
)).
The coefficients
\hat{a}_{\alpha\beta}
and
b_{\alpha\delta}
are real; therefore,
from (
2.2
) the inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1+\Lambda(\xi)\right)^{2}|F[\hat{u}^{\varepsilon}]|^{2}\,d\xi\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F[f]|^{2}\,d\xi

(2.4)

follows. By the ellipticity property of

inherited from the

and the Plancherel identity, (

yields the

\|\hat{u}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{2m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(2.5)

where the constant
C
depends only on the spatial dimension and the constants
from (
1.2
).

A few words about the statement of the above theorems and the technique we use.
It is not at once apparent that the functions given in (

with the constant
C
depending only on the spatial dimension and the constants in (

Being the counterparts of (
1.9
)
1
, these inequalities show that, generally,
the correctors
\varepsilon^{m}\mathcal{K}_{2}(\varepsilon)
and

3
Cell problems

3.1.
On the set of smooth 1-periodic functions

u{\in}C_{\rm per}^{\infty}(Y)
with zero mean

\langle u\rangle=\int_{Y}u(y)\,dy=0,

the norm is determined by the expression

\left(\int_{Y}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|D^{\alpha}u|^{2}\,dy\right)^{1/2}.

The completion of this set in this norm is denoted by
\mathcal{W}
.

Since the coefficients
\{a_{\alpha\beta}(x)\}
are periodic, then the inequality (
1.2
) on smooth compactly
supported functions yields a similar inequality on periodic functions:

\int_{Y}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}a_{\alpha\beta}(y)D^{\beta}uD^{\alpha}u\,dy\geq\lambda_{0}\int_{Y}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=m}|D^{\alpha}u|^{2}\,dy\quad\forall u\in C_{\rm per}^{\infty}(Y),

(3.1)

(see Lemma 3.1 in
[
3
]
).
The inequality (
3.1
) can be extended by closure to
u{\in}\mathcal{W}
; thereby,
the operator

A=(-1)^{m}\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}D^{\alpha}(a_{\alpha\beta}(y)D^{\beta})

acting from
\mathcal{W}
to its dual
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
is coercive.

For any multiindex
\gamma
,
|\gamma|{=}m
, we consider the problem on the cell

N_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{W},\quad\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}D^{\alpha}(a_{\alpha\beta}(y)D^{\beta}N_{\gamma}(y))=-\sum_{|\alpha|=m}D^{\alpha}(a_{\alpha\gamma}(y)).

(3.2)

The right-hand side of (
3.2
) naturally defines the functional
F_{\gamma}

on
\mathcal{W}
, and the equation can be written as

AN_{\gamma}{=}F_{\gamma}\quad(N_{\gamma}{\in}\mathcal{W}).

(3.3)

Therefore, the unique solvability of (
3.2
) with the estimate for the solution

\|N_{\gamma}\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq c,\quad c=const(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}),

(3.4)

is guaranteed by the following well known abstract assertion for operators acting from a real reflexive Banach space
V
to its dual
V^{\prime}
.

Theorem 3.1

Let
L:V\to V^{\prime}
be a continuous linear operator satisfying the coercitivity condition:

\langle Lv,v\rangle\geq\lambda\|v\|^{2}_{V}

for any
v{\in}V
.
Then the equation
Lv{=}f\,\,(v{\in}V)

is uniquely solvable for any
f{\in}V^{\prime}
,
and the solution satisfies the estimate

\|v\|_{V}\leq\lambda^{-1}\|f\|_{V^{\prime}}.

The coefficients of the homogenized operator
\hat{A}
defined in
(
1.4
) are found through the solutions to the problems (
3.2
) as follows:

\hat{a}_{\alpha\beta}=\langle a_{\alpha\beta}(\cdot)+\sum\limits_{|\gamma|=m}a_{\alpha\gamma}(\cdot)D^{\gamma}N_{\beta}(\cdot)\rangle,\quad|\alpha|=m,|\beta|=m.

(3.5)

We set

e_{\alpha\beta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}1,\text{ if }\alpha=\beta,\\
0,\text{ if }\alpha\neq\beta.\\
\end{array}\right.

Then

\hat{a}_{\alpha\beta}=\langle\sum\limits_{|\gamma|=m}a_{\alpha\gamma}(\cdot)(e_{\gamma\beta}+D^{\gamma}N_{\beta}(\cdot))\rangle,

4
Smoothing operators

In this paper, we prove homogenization estimates by method, coming from

We start with

:

\|S^{\varepsilon}\varphi\|\leq\|\varphi\|,

(4.1)

\|S^{\varepsilon}\varphi-\varphi\|\leq(\sqrt{d}/2)\varepsilon\|\nabla\varphi\|\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(4.2)

Here and throughout this Section,

denotes, for brevity, the norm in

.
We mention also the evident property
S^{\varepsilon}(D^{\alpha}\varphi)=D^{\alpha}(S^{\varepsilon}\varphi)
for any derivative
D^{\alpha}
, which is exploited systematically in the sequel.

The following property of the Steklov smoothing operator

to interrelate with
\varepsilon
-periodic factors is pivotal for our method (see its simple proof, e.g., in
[
10
]
or

[

]

Lemma 4.1

If
\varphi{\in}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
b{\in}L^{2}_{\rm per}(Y)
,
and
b^{\varepsilon}(x){=}b(\varepsilon^{-1}x)
,
then

\|b^{\varepsilon}S^{\varepsilon}\varphi\|\leq\langle b^{2}\rangle^{1/2}\|\varphi\|.

(4.3)

The above estimate (
4.2
)
can be improved under higher regularity conditions. For example,

\|S^{\varepsilon}\varphi-\varphi\|\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad C=const(d).

(4.4)

By duality, from (
4.4
) we obtain

\|S^{\varepsilon}\varphi-\varphi\|_{H^{-2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad\forall\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad C=const(d).

(4.5)

Instead of Steklov‘s smoothing operator, we can often consider the general smoothing operator with an arbitrary kernel. This is

\Theta^{\varepsilon}\varphi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x-\varepsilon\omega)\theta(\omega)\,d\omega,

(4.6)

where
\theta\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

is compactly supported,
\theta\geq 0
, and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\theta(x)dx{=}1
.

The estimates (
4.1
)–(
4.3
) formulated for the Steklov smoothing operator remain valid for the general smoothig operator
(
4.6
) with only one note that right-hand side contains constants depending not only on the dimension

, but also on the kernel
\theta
. If, in addition, the kernel
\theta
is even,
then
\Theta^{\varepsilon}
possesses the properties of type (
4.4
) and (
4.5
).

The following useful properties of the operator (
4.6
) are highlighted in
[
14
]
and
[
8
]
.

Lemma 4.2

Assume that
\theta
is piecewise

-smooth,

is a natural number,

b\in L^{2}_{\rm per}(Y)
,
b^{\varepsilon}(x)=b(x/\varepsilon)

\|\Theta^{\varepsilon}\nabla^{k}\varphi\|\leq C\varepsilon^{-k}\|\varphi\|,\quad C=const(\theta),

(4.7)

\|b^{\varepsilon}\Theta^{\varepsilon}\nabla^{k}\varphi\|\leq C\varepsilon^{-k}\langle b^{2}\rangle^{1/2}\|\varphi\|,\quad C=const(\theta).

(4.8)

It is obvious that the Steklov smoothing operator
S^{\varepsilon}
can be defined by (
4.6
) if the smoothing kernel

is the characteristic function of the cube

The double Steklov smoothing operator

4.6

The kernels

(see computations for it in

-smooth. As a consequence, the properties (

) and (

Remark 6.1

The approximation (

Remark 6.2

There is another approach to prove operator type estimates in homogenization. It relies on the
Floquet–Bloch transform and spectral expansions; thus, it is often called the spectral method. This method is tightly linked with periodic setting because of the
Floquet–Bloch transform which fits only this setting.
One of the first applications of the spectral approach
for homogenization estimates can be found in
[
15
]
where diffusion problems were studied. As for
high order operators, this method was applied,
e.g., in
[
11
]
,
[
16
]
, and
[
17
]
.

Remark 6.3

The method demonstrated to prove estimates (
2.10
) and (
2.13
) can be extended to more general situations when operators are matrix-valued. We choose to consider here the scalar problem only for brevity of exposition.

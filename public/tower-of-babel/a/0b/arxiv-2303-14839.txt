Dynamical transition from localized to uniform scrambling in locally hyperbolic systems Preprint: PRE
Mathias Steinhuber, Peter Schlagheck, Juan Diego Urbina, Klaus Richter

Abstract
Fast scrambling of quantum correlations, reflected by the exponential growth of Out-of-Time-Order Correlators (OTOCs) on short pre-Ehrenfest time scales, is commonly considered as a major quantum signature of unstable dynamics in quantum systems with a classical limit. In two recent works [Phys. Rev. Lett. 123 , 160401 (2019)] and [Phys. Rev. Lett. 124 , 140602 (2020)], a significant difference in the scrambling rate of integrable (many-body) systems was observed, depending on the initial state being semiclassically localized around unstable fixed points or fully delocalized (infinite temperature). Specifically, the quantum Lyapunov exponent λ q \lambda_{\rm q} quantifying the OTOC growth is given, respectively, by λ q = 2 ​ λ s \lambda_{\rm q}=2\lambda_{\rm s} or λ q = λ s \lambda_{\rm q}=\lambda_{\rm s} in terms of the stability exponent λ s \lambda_{\rm s} of the hyperbolic fixed point. Here we show that a wave packet, initially localized around this fixed point, features a distinct dynamical transition between these two regions. We present an analytical semiclassical approach providing a physical picture of this phenomenon and support our findings by extensive numerical simulations in the whole parameter range of locally unstable dynamics of a Bose-Hubbard dimer. Our results suggest that the existence of this crossover is a hallmark of unstable separatrix dynamics in integrable systems, thus opening the possibility to distinguish the latter, on the basis of this particular observable, from genuine chaotic dynamics generally featuring uniform exponential growth of the OTOC.

II
Out-of-Time-Order Correlator in integrable systems with local hyperbolicity

Our goal in this section is to refine the pre-Ehrenfest theory for scrambling around hyperbolic fixed points in integrable systems. In particular we attempt to relax the localization properties of the initial state considered in
Hummel et al. 2019
;
Xu, Scaffidi, and Cao 2020
.
The OTOC for two operators
\hat{A}
,
\hat{B}
with respect to a state
\hat{\rho}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}(t)\,=\,\operatorname{tr}\big\{\hat{\rho}\big|[\hat{A}(t),\hat{B}]\big|^{2}\big\},

(1)

which is by itself a modulus-squared commutator. When this squared commutator is expanded in individual correlators one obtains, besides contributions that admit a standard time ordering, extra irreducibly un-ordered correlations
Maldacena, Shenker, and Stanford 2016
, with anomalous dynamical behavior that are the central object of study.

The long-time (post-Ehrenfest) saturation of generic OTOCs has been subject of several studies, both in the chaotic
Rammensee, Urbina, and Richter 2018
;
García-Mata et al. 2018
and integrable
Hummel et al. 2019
;
Fortes et al. 2019
regimes where interference effects beyond a pure quasiclassical (Truncated-Wigner like-) approach appear
Polkovnikov et al. 2011
.

Here, however, our focus is the short time scales, where a quasiclassical approach based on the Wigner-Moyal expansion, which is a regular expansion around
{\hbar_{\text{eff}}}=0

Schleich 2001
;
Kim and Noz 1991
;
O’Connell 2008
, is perfectly appropriate. Keeping only leading-order terms in
{\hbar_{\text{eff}}}
, one obtains

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{C}(t)\,=\,&{\hbar_{\text{eff}}}^{2}\langle W_{\rho}(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})\big|\big\{A_{\rm W}(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0},t),B_{\rm W}(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})\big\}\big|^{2}\rangle_{\text{PS}}\\
&+O({\hbar_{\text{eff}}}^{3}),\end{split}

(2)

where
A_{W}
,
B_{W}
are the Wigner-transforms of the operators
\hat{A},\hat{B}
and
W_{\rho}(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})
is the Wigner-distribution corresponding to the state
\hat{\rho}
. A detailed derivation of Eq. (
2
) is in App.
V.1
.

Further,
\langle.\rangle_{\text{PS}}
indicates integration over the whole classical (mean-field) phase space parametrized by the canonical pairs
(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})
. The Heisenberg time dynamics of the quantum operator is mapped to time dynamics of the classical observable
A(q_{0},p_{0},t)=A(q(q_{0},p_{0},t),p(q_{0},p_{0},t))
which arises from the classical propagation of the initial condition
(q_{0},p_{0})
.

The effective Plank constant
{\hbar_{\text{eff}}}
has different expressions in different contexts. It is given by the usual Plank constant divided a typical action
\hbar/S_{\rm typ}
in single particle cases
Gutzwiller 1991
;
Haake 2001
;
Brack and Bhaduri 1997
, and the inverse of the total spin quantum number
1/S
for spin systems
Akila et al. 2017
;
Braun et al. 2020
. In the case of interest here, interacting bosonic systems,
{\hbar_{\text{eff}}}=\frac{1}{N}
is given by the inverse of the total particle number
N
after convenient rescaling of the interaction strength
Richter, Urbina, and Tomsovic 2022
;
Engl, Urbina, and Richter 2015
.

Without loss of generality, we choose the operators
\hat{A}=\hat{q}
and
\hat{B}=\hat{p}
, as they are hermitian and their classical counterparts are generalized coordinates or momenta. Therefore, at leading order in the Wigner-Moyal expansion, Eq. (
2
),
we drop the index
W
and take the pure classical phase space functions. This choice of the operators simplifies the classical Poisson-brackets, that are now given by an element of the stability matrix
\frac{\partial\vec{x}(t)}{\partial\vec{x}(0)}
with
\vec{x}=(\vec{q},\vec{p})

Tabor 1989
;
Wiggins 2003
.

The classical limit of the OTOC in Eq. (
2
) is so far a completely general result. Under the assumption of local instability, however, the leading order of exponential growth is given by the maximal local exponent
\lambda(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})
of the stability matrix:

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}(t)~\sim~

\displaystyle{\hbar_{\text{eff}}}^{2}\langle W_{\rho}(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})\exp\{2\lambda(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})t\big\}\rangle_{\text{PS}}.

(3)

At this point, it is convenient to introduce two local time-scales:

•

After the local ergodic time
\tau_{\rm s}=1/\lambda(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})
, the exponential growth of the OTOC begins to be visible. Before
\tau_{\rm s}
, we have sub-exponential/polynomial behavior linked to system-specific mechanisms.

•

The Wigner-Weyl approximation breaks down when the leading order of the integrand in Eq. (
2
) becomes large compared to
{\hbar_{\text{eff}}}^{2}
. This breakdown defines the local Ehrenfest time
\tau_{\rm E}=\lambda^{-1}(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})\log(1/\hbarE)
and in our many-body case
\tau_{\rm E}=\lambda^{-1}(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})\log N
.

We exploit the experimentally tunable localization features of quantum mechanical states
Estève et al. 2008
as a tool to probe the local unstable dynamics around a hyperbolic fixed point (FP) and consider a coherent-like state
\hat{\rho}
centered around it.
In the linearized region around the FP the dynamics can be precisely described, and we can express
\lambda(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})
by the maximal stability exponent
\lambda_{\rm s}
of the FP.
In general, however, the linearized region is bounded and we express this by the fact that the relation
\{A(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0},t),B(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})\}=e^{\lambda_{\rm s}t}
is valid only if the unstable manifold coordinate/projection
u(\vec{q}_{0}
,
\vec{p}_{0})
is smaller than a threshold
c>0
.
For times larger than zero, the exponential growth
u(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0},t)=u(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})e^{\lambda_{\rm s}t}
is only valid if the linearized region
u(\vec{q}(t),\vec{p}(t),t)<c
is still fulfilled. Afterwards we need to replace the time dynamics of the unstable manifold by a sub-exponential function. We refer to this mechanism as a kind of
leaking
from the linearized region
Xu, Scaffidi, and Cao 2020
.

The key observation is that, although the sub-exponential function describes the classical evolution outside and it is therefore negligibly small compared to the exponential growth in the linearized region, its contribution is weighted by a portion of phase space that grows exponentially. These considerations allow us to heuristically account for the leaking mechanism, by modifying the Eq. (
3
) as

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}(t)~\sim~

\displaystyle{\hbar_{\text{eff}}}^{2}e^{2\lambda_{\rm s}t}\langle W_{\rho}(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})\Theta(c-e^{\lambda_{\rm s}t}u(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0}))\rangle_{\text{LR}},

(4)

where we restrict the phase-space integration to the dominant linearized region (LR) around the FP.

For definiteness, let us consider now a Gaussian wave-packet with an initial linear width
\Delta u
along the unstable manifold
u(\vec{q},\vec{p})
.
In this situation, the wave-packet reaches the boundary of the linear region by a finite time
\tau_{\rm L}=\lambda_{\rm s}^{-1}\ln\big( c/\Delta u \big)
, which we correspondingly call the leaking time.
After
\tau_{\rm L}
, we must take the leaking of the wave-packet into account, i.e., the phase-space volume causing the exponential growth shrinks exponentially with
e^{-\lambda_{\rm s}t}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\langle W_{\rho}(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0})\Theta(c-e^{\lambda_{\rm s}t}u(\vec{q}_{0},\vec{p}_{0}))\rangle_{\text{LR}}\,\sim\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{const.}&,t<\tau_{\rm L}\\
e^{-\lambda_{\rm s}t}&,t>\tau_{\rm L}\\
\end{array}\right.\!.

Hence, the exponential growth of the OTOC in Eq. (
4

e^{\lambda_{\rm s}t}
.
This finally leads to a short-time behavior of the OTOC around an unstable fixed point given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}(t)~\sim~\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{poly.}&,t<\tau_{\rm s}\\
e^{2\lambda_{\rm s}t}&,\tau_{\rm s}<t<\tau_{\rm L}\\
e^{\lambda_{\rm s}t}&,\tau_{\rm L}<t<\tau_{\rm E}\\

It is important to note that there is a hierarchy of time scales: the leaking time is only relevant if
\tau_{\rm L}<\tau_{\rm E}

The initial linear width

We see then, that one can clearly distinguish three parametric regions:
\tau_{\rm L}<\tau_{\rm s}
,
\tau_{\rm s}<\tau_{\rm L}<\tau_{\rm E}

i)

Delocalized/uniform states:
\tau_{\rm L}<\tau_{\rm s}
,

Under the assumption that there is only one unstable FP of the classical dynamics, the OTOC is still governed by Eq. (

-regime vanishes.
A typical examples here are high temperature states (

ii)

Localized states:
\tau_{\rm s}<\tau_{\rm L}<\tau_{\rm E}
,

In this case we have the
2\lambda_{\rm s}-\lambda_{\rm s}

.
The prime example are the coherent states centered at a FP. They usually have a linear size of
{\hbar_{\text{eff}}}^{1/2}
in all phase space directions.

iii)

Well-localized states:
\tau_{\rm L}\approx\tau_{\rm E}
,
\alpha\approx 1

The second

-region is vanishing, only the one

-region is visible.
Fock states are candidates for the third class. Their linear width is
{\hbar_{\text{eff}}}
in the classical occupation numbers, such that

The case
\alpha>1

At this point, we can explain the dynamical behavior of OTOCs reported in

III
Bose-Hubbard Dimer

The Bose-Hubbard dimer describes bosonic degrees of freedom occupying two discrete levels or sites. Prime physical setups are individual Josephson-junctions
Il’ichev et al. 2001
or cold atoms within a small two-sited optical lattice
Albiez et al. 2005
;
Fölling et al. 2007
;
Witthaut, Trimborn, and Wimberger 2008
;
Cheinet et al. 2008
;
Kierig et al. 2008
;
Tomkovič et al. 2017
. In all these cases, one ends with an effective description in terms of the following Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}=-2J\big(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}_{1}+\hat{a}_{2}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\big)+\frac{g}{2}\big(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}{}^{2}\hat{a}_{1}^{2}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}{}^{2}\hat{a}_{2}^{2}\big),

(9)

where the parameter
J
is the hopping and
g
is the (local) interaction strength between particles given in units of energy. Our Hamiltonian differs from the usual dimer Hamiltonian, the hopping coefficient
2J
(instead of
J
) is motivated to be consistent with a ring topology for higher number of wells. Consequently, the two-site ring has a doubly counted hopping term. We also introduce a new dimensionless parameter
\Theta
such that we have
J=\epsilon_{0}\cos\Theta
and
g=\epsilon_{0}\frac{2}{N}\sin\Theta
, with
\epsilon_{0}=\sqrt{J^{2}+\big(gN/2\big)^{2}}
representing a global energy scale. We set
\epsilon_{0}=1
for a convenient unit system, yielding also the time unit
\hbar/\epsilon_{0}=1
.
The parameter space is thus compactified to
\Theta\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
.

III.1
Classical mean-field limit

We follow the standard approach
Negele and Orland 1995
to derive the classical limit for bosons and replace the operators by complex numbers

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{j},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}~\longmapsto

\displaystyle~\psi_{j},\psi^{\ast}_{j}

within the normal-ordered quantum Hamiltonian in Eq. (
9
) to obtain a classical mean-field system.
The discrete nonlinear Schrödinger equation
i\dot{\psi}_{j}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial\psi_{j}^{\ast}}
yields Hamilton’s equations of motion that define the classical dynamics.
Due to the conserved total particle number
N
, we define new set of conjugated classical variables

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}[l]N=n_{1}+n_{2}\,,&\phi=\frac{1}{2}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})\,,\\
n=\frac{1}{2}(n_{1}-n_{2})\,,&\varphi=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}-\pi\,,\end{matrix}

where the two mean fields
\psi_{j}=\sqrt{n_{j}}e^{i\varphi_{j}}
are written in phase
\varphi_{j}
and occupations

. Hence, the Hamiltonian takes the form

\displaystyle H(N,\phi,n,\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\cos{\Theta}\sqrt{N^{2}-4n^{2}}\cos\varphi

(10)

\displaystyle+\sin\Theta\Big(\frac{2n^{2}}{N}+\frac{N}{2}\Big)\,.

We can reduce the dynamics to a one-dimensional system with a single conjugated pair
(z=2n/N,\varphi=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}-\pi)
given by the population inversion and relative phase

.
With these coordinates, we can reduce the equations of motion to two coupled real-valued ODEs

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{z}&=-4\cos\Theta\sqrt{1-z^{2}}\sin\varphi,\\
\dot{\varphi}&=4\cos\Theta\frac{z\cos\varphi}{\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}-2\sin\Theta\,z,\end{split}

(11)

which is a one-degree-of-freedom system.
Conveniently, the mean-field system deriving from Eq. (
11
) is
integrable and can thus be exactly solved up to quadrature.

\hat{n}_{1}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}
at the first site. Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}(t)=\langle||[\hat{n}_{1}(t),\hat{n}_{1}]||^{2}\rangle=\langle||[\hat{n}(t),\hat{n}]||^{2}\rangle\,,

(12)

where
\hat{n}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{n}_{1}-\hat{n}_{2})
. For this OTOC, we are interested in evaluating the classical expression which is
given by

O(t)=\iint dnd\varphi\,W(n,\varphi)\left(\frac{\partial n_{t}}{\partial\varphi_{0}}\right)^{2}\,,

(13)

with
W(n,\varphi)
the Wigner function associated with the initial state.
The latter is, for the sake of simplicity, modeled as a coherent quantum state

\exp(\sqrt{N_0}(\hat{a}_-^\dagger- \hat{a}_-))\ket{0}
with
\hat{a}_{-}=\hat{a}_{1}-\hat{a}_{2}
, keeping in mind that for large

N_{0}
this coherent state features very similar properties as a
number-projected coherent state with total particle number
N_{0}
as far as the
site population exchange dynamics is concerned.
The Wigner function associated with this initial state would be given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}W(N&,\phi,n,\varphi)\simeq\\
\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}&\exp\left(-\frac{(N-N_{0})^{2}}{2N_{0}}-2N_{0}\phi^{2}-\frac{2n^{2}}{N_{0}}-\frac{N_{0}\varphi^{2}}{2}\right)\end{split}

(14)

in the framework of a quadratic expansion valid for
N_{0}\gg 1
.
Since the Wigner function
W
describes a tight localization of
N
about
N_{0}
, we set

N_{0}=N
henceforth and model the initial quantum state concerning the
inter-site population exchange dynamics by the Wigner function

W(n,\varphi)=\frac{1}{\pi}\exp\left(-\frac{2n^{2}}{\omega N}-\frac{N\omega\varphi^{2}}{2}\right)\,,

(15)

where the squeezing parameter
\omega
allows for some flexibility
in the definition of the initial quantum state.

Let us first discuss the linearized dynamics in the near vicinity of
the FP
(n,\varphi)=(0,0)
.
Linearizing Eq. (
11
), we obtain
the system of equations

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{z}&=-4\cos\Theta\varphi\,,\\
\dot{\varphi}&=-2(\sin\Theta-2\cos{\Theta})z\,,\end{split}

(16)

which is readily solved as

\displaystyle\begin{split}z_{t}&=z_{0}\cosh\lambda_{\rm s}t-\frac{4\cos{\Theta}\varphi_{0}}{\lambda_{\rm s}}\sinh\lambda_{\rm s}t\,,\\
\varphi_{t}&=\varphi_{0}\cosh\lambda_{\rm s}t-\frac{\lambda_{\rm s}z_{0}}{4\cos{\Theta}}\sinh\lambda_{\rm s}t\end{split}

(17)

in terms of the stability exponent

\lambda_{\rm s}=4\cos{\Theta}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{2}-1}\,,

(18)

where we defined the nonlinearity parameter
\gamma=\tan\Theta=gN/(LJ)
.
The latter becomes purely imaginary for
\gamma<2
, which implies that

(n,\varphi)=(0,0)
turns into a stable fixed point if the nonlinearity
parameter
\gamma
is decreased below two, as it is plotted in Fig.
2
.

Considering
\gamma>2
henceforth, and assuming that the point

(z_{0},\varphi_{0})
is located very closely to the origin in this phase space,
we can, as in the previous section, identify a time scale

\tau_{\rm s}\gg\lambda_{\rm s}^{-1}
for which we still have
|z_{\tau_{\rm s}}|\ll 1
and

|\varphi_{\tau_{\rm s}}|\ll 1
, such that the above linearization Eq. (
16
) of the classical
equations of motion remains valid until
t=\tau_{\rm s}
.
Since at the same time we have
\lambda_{\rm s}\tau_{\rm s}\gg 1
by assumption,
the solution Eq. (
17
) of the linearized
equation Eq. (
16
) for
t=\tau_{\rm s}

simplifies as

\displaystyle z_{\tau_{\rm s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{z_{0}}{2}-\frac{2\cos\Theta\varphi_{0}}{\lambda_{\rm s}}\right)e^{\lambda_{\rm s}\tau_{\rm s}}\,,

(19)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tau_{\rm s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\varphi_{0}}{2}-\frac{\lambda_{\rm s}z_{0}}{8\cos\Theta}\right)e^{\lambda_{\rm s}\tau_{\rm s}}\,.

(20)

From the time
\tau_{\rm s}
on, we can safely assume that the trajectory
under consideration very closely follows the separatrix structure emanating
from the unstable
off-phase
fixed point
(z,\varphi)=(0,0)
.
This separatrix structure is obtained through the identification of the
energy

H(n,\varphi)=2\cos\Theta N+\sin{\Theta}\frac{N}{2}

(21)

of the classical Hamiltonian Eq. (
10
), from which follows the identity

\cos\varphi=\frac{1-\frac{\gamma}{4}z^{2}}{\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}\,.

Inserting this expression into Eq. (
11
) yields the differential
equation

\dot{z}=z\sqrt{\lambda_{\rm s}^{2}-\sin^{2}\Theta z^{2}}

describing the motion along the upper or lower separatrix branch.
This equation is straightforwardly integrated yielding

\displaystyle t-\tau_{\rm s}=

\displaystyle\int_{z_{\tau_{\rm s}}}^{z_{t}}\frac{\operatorname{d}\!z}{z\sqrt{\lambda_{\rm s}^{2}-\sin^{2}\Theta z^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\lambda_{\rm s}}\left[\mathrm{arcosh}\left(\frac{\lambda_{\rm s}}{\sin\Theta|z_{t}|}\right)-\mathrm{arcosh}\left(\frac{\lambda_{\rm s}}{\sin\Theta|z_{\tau_{\rm s}}|}\right)\right]\,,

from which we obtain

z_{t}=\frac{\mathrm{sgn}(z_{\tau_{\rm s}})\lambda_{\rm s}/\sin\Theta}{\cosh\left[\mathrm{arcosh}\left(\frac{\lambda_{\rm s}}{\sin\Theta|z_{\tau_{\rm s}}|}\right)-\lambda_{\rm s}(t-\tau_{\rm s})\right]}\,.

(22)

Using
|z_{\tau_{\rm s}}|\ll 1
and hence also
\sin\Theta|z_{\tau_{\rm s}}|/\lambda_{\rm s}\ll 1

for finite values of
\sin\Theta
and
\lambda_{\rm s}
, we define

\displaystyle\begin{split}x_{t}&=\mathrm{sgn}(z_{\tau_{\rm s}})\exp\left[-\mathrm{arcosh}\left(\frac{\lambda_{\rm s}}{\gamma|z_{\tau_{\rm s}}|}\right)+\lambda_{\rm s}(t-\tau_{\rm s})\right]\\
&\simeq\frac{\sin\Theta}{2\lambda_{\rm s}}\left(\frac{z_{0}}{2}-2\cos\Theta\frac{\varphi_{0}}{\lambda_{\rm s}}\right)e^{\lambda_{\rm s}t}\\
&\simeq\frac{\sin\Theta}{\lambda_{\rm s}}\left(\frac{n_{0}}{N}-2\cos\Theta\frac{\varphi_{0}}{\lambda_{\rm s}}\right)\sinh(\ls t)\,,\end{split}

(23)

where we make use of the asymptotic expression

\mathrm{arcosh}(u)=\ln\left(u+\sqrt{u^{2}-1}\right)\simeq\ln(2u)+O(u^{-2})

(24)

for large
u
, in combination with Eq. (
19
).
With
(\cosh u)^{-1}=2e^{u}/(1+e^{2u})
we can thus rewrite Eq. (
22
) in terms of the expression Eq. (
23
) as

z_{\tau}=\frac{2\lambda_{\rm s}}{\sin\Theta}\frac{x_{t}}{1+x_{t}^{2}}\,,

(25)

which yields

n_{t}=\frac{N\lambda_{\rm s}}{\sin\Theta}\frac{x_{t}}{1+x_{t}^{2}}\,.

(26)

Replacing
e^{\lambda_{\rm s}t}
with
2\sinh(\ls t)
in Eq. (
23
)
is clearly valid for large
\lambda_{\rm s}t\gg 1
and has the additional advantage
that the short-time regime in the time evolution of
n_{t}
will thereby be
correctly captured as well within Eq. (
26
).

With Eq. (
26
), we write down the needed derivative

\displaystyle\frac{\partial n_{t}}{\partial\varphi_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{N\lambda_{\rm s}}{\sin\Theta}\frac{1-x_{t}^{2}}{(1+x_{t}^{2})^{2}}\frac{\partial x_{t}}{\partial\varphi_{0}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{-2\cos\Theta}{\lambda_{\rm s}}\sinh(\ls t)\frac{1-x_{t}^{2}}{(1+x_{t}^{2})^{2}}.

The classical limit of the quantum OTOC, Eq. (
13
),
is then evaluated by substituting
n_{0}
by
x_{t}
via Eq. (
23
) and integrating out the Gaussian integral in
\varphi_{0}
, yielding

\displaystyle\begin{split}O(t)=\frac{2\cos^{2}\Theta N^{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}a\lambda_{\rm s}^{2}}&\sinh(\ls t)\\
\int\limits_{{-\infty}}^{{\infty}}\frac{(1-x^{2})^{2}}{(1+x^{2})^{4}}&\exp\left[-\left(\frac{x}{2a\sinh(\ls t)}\right)^{2}\right]\operatorname{d}\!x\end{split}

(27)

where the dimensionless scale is defined as

a=\frac{\sin{\Theta}/\lambda_{\rm s}}{\sqrt{8\omega N}}\sqrt{\omega^{2}+\frac{16\cos^{2}\Theta}{\lambda_{\rm s}^{2}}}\,.

(28)

The short-time behavior of the OTOC, for
t\ll\tau_{\rm L}=-\lambda_{\rm s}^{-1}\ln a
,
is yielded as

O(t)\simeq 4\cos^{2}\Theta\frac{N^{2}}{\lambda_{\rm s}^{2}}\sinh^{2}(\lambda_{\rm s}t)\,,

(29)

while for
t\gg\tau_{\rm L}
we obtain

O(t)\simeq\cos^{2}\Theta\frac{\sqrt{\pi}N^{2}}{4a\lambda_{\rm s}^{2}}e^{\lambda_{\rm s}t}\,.

(30)

These two limits correspond to the
2\lambda_{\rm s}-\lambda_{\rm s}
transition
that we heuristically derived in Sec.
II
, as expressed in
Eq. (
II
). Note that the here defined
\tau_{\rm L}
agrees with the case ii) in Sec.
II
.
The dimensionless constant
\ln a
encodes the linear width of the wave-packet along the unstable direction.

With this detailed classical calculation at hand, we analyze the OTOC centered around this local hyperbolic off-phase FP, considering the parameter
\Theta_{\ast}
at which the value of the stability exponent is maximal,
\lambda_{\rm s}=0.97
.

III.5
Squeezing – engineering the leaking time
\tau_{\rm L}

An important consequence of the leaking mechanism is that the linear extent
of the initial state along the unstable manifolds is the key ingredient for the exact position of the
2\lambda_{\rm s}-\lambda_{\rm s}
transition.
In order to check this, we squeeze the coherent state on the off-phase FP and subsequently calculate the OTOC.

As a matter of fact,
squeezed states in optical lattices can be achieved experimentally to an exquisite degree
Estève et al. 2008
.
A squeezing protocol that is convenient for our purpose can be effectively (and unitarily) realized by reversing the time evolution, which in the experimental practice would amount to forward time propagation with reversed signs of the hopping parameter and the interaction parameter (to be done by Floquet engineering
Lignier et al. 2007
;
Kierig et al. 2008
combined with Feshbach tuning).
This means, we replace
\ket{\xi}
by

\displaystyle\ket{\xi(t_{0})}=\hat{U}(t_{0})\ket{\xi}

with
t_{0}=-3\tau_{\rm E}/4
, where
\hat{U}(t_{0})
is the time-evolution operator, and then calculate the OTOC, Eq. (
1
), for the initial state
\hat{\rho}(t_{0})=\ket{\xi(t_{0}) }\bra{\xi(t_{0})}
. The corresponding scrambling dynamics is shown in then the left panel of Fig.
7
for
N=10^{3}
, while the right panel depicts the initial Husimi distribution of the squeezed state.

is now right at the Ehrenfest time, and no

IV
Conclusion

The OTOC associated with a wave packet that is localized around a hyperbolic fixed point in a quantum system with integrable classical (mean-field) limit undergoes a transition between different dynamical regimes, which is driven by a leaking mechanism of phase space volume along classical separatrices. If located within the pre-Ehrenfest time scale, this dynamical crossover imprints a characteristic kink structure to the scrambling as measured by the exponential form of out-of-time-order correlators. Specifically, the exponential growth changes from
2\lambda_{\rm s}
to
\lambda_{\rm s}
, and the kink develops for
{\hbar_{\text{eff}}}\to 0
, where
\lambda_{\rm s}
is the stability exponent of the fixed point. We derived an analytical theory and showed how this behavior is directly related to the classical limit of the out-of-time-order correlators when their time dependence is governed by the separatrix dynamics emerging around an unstable fixed point.

Following this picture, we showed that squeezing the initial coherent state allows us to engineer the leaking time and thus the dynamical transition itself exactly as predicted by our analytical considerations.

If the phase-space localization scale of the initial state is strong enough, the leaking time is beyond the Ehrenfest time and we obtain the standard
2\lambda_{\rm s}
exponent. In contrast, an uniform state starts to leak immediately and even before the ergodic time. Therefore, the infinite temperature OTOC grows only with the reduced exponent
\lambda_{\rm s}
.

We supported our picture of the dynamical crossover by means of extensive simulations on the experimentally accessible, and integrable, Bose-Hubbard dimer. The extremely clean fixed point and separatrix structure of this systems allows us for a detailed study of the mechanism, and the analytical expectations of Sec.
II
are verified to an excellent degree.

In order to focus on separatrix effects like the leaking mechanism, requiring a very well controlled classical phase space, we restricted our numerical findings and the corresponding analytical theory to integrable systems for which the theory in Sec.
II
assumes a bounded linearized regime around the fixed point.
An ansatz generalizing these concepts to the realm of chaotic systems is that
the role of the stability exponent
\lambda_{\rm s}
from the fixed point is translated to the Lyapunov exponent
\lambda_{\rm L}
from the chaotic sea, thus providing a bridge to
exponential growth laws of OTOCs that were found for quantum maps with the exponents
2\lambda_{\rm L}

Rozenbaum, Ganeshan, and Galitski 2017
;
García-Mata et al. 2018
and
\lambda_{\rm L}

Lakshminarayan 2019
, respectively. A first numerical exploration of this matter, focusing on the behaviour of OTOCs in chaotic separatrix layers, was carried out in
Meier et al. 2023
, where the transition from the stability exponent
\lambda_{\rm s}
associated with an unstable fixed point to the Lyapunov exponent
\lambda_{\rm L}
characterizing the chaotic layer was investigated for pre-Ehrenfest time scales.
In such a situation, it is an open and thrilling question to which extent the
2\lambda_{\rm s}
-
\lambda_{\rm s}
transition could distinguish a chaotic from an integrable system
and can be generalized to mixed regular-chaotic dynamics.

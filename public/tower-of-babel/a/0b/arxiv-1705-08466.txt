The Bloch space and the dual space of a Luecking-type subspace of A 1 A^{1} Thanks: The work was supported by NNSF of China (No. 11371234 and No. 11571217).
Guanlong Bao and Fangqin Ye

Abstract
. Let X X be the dual space of a Luecking-type subspace of the Bergman space A 1 A^{1} . It is known that the Bloch space ℬ \mathcal{B} is a subset of X X . In 1990, Ghatage and Sun asked whether ℬ \mathcal{B} is dense in X X . They also asked whether the little version of X X is a subset of ℬ \mathcal{B} . In this note, based on results and methods of Girela, Peláez, Pérez-González and Rättyä in 2008, we answer the two questions in the negative.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
be the open unit disk in the complex plan
\mathbb{C}
. Denote by
H(\mathbb{D})
the space of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
. For
0<p<\infty
,
a function
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
belongs to the Bergman
space
A^{p}
if

\|f\|_{A^{p}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

where
dA
is the normalized area measure of
\mathbb{D}
. Coifman and Rochberg
[
6
]
established atomic decomposition theorem for the Bergman space
A^{p}
,
0<p<\infty
.
In
[
15
, p. 329]
, Luecking proved that there exists a sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{D}
such that every
f\in A^{p}
,
p>1
, can be written in the form

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}(1-|a_{n}|)^{(1-1/p)+\eta}}{(1-\overline{a_{n}}z)^{1+1/p+\eta}}

for an arbitrary
\eta>0
and
\{c_{n}\}\in\ell^{p}
. As pointed out in
[
15
]
, the range of the parameter
\eta
in the above formula is larger than the corresponding result in
[
6
]
.
To determine the extent to which Luecking’s decomposition of Bergman spaces
A^{p}
,
1<p<\infty
, can be extended to
A^{1}
, Ghatage and Sun
[
9
]
introduced a Banach space of analytic functions, denoted by
Y
. More precisely,
Y
is the completion (in the norm
\|.\|_{Y}
defined later) of the set of functions
f\in A^{1}
with the form

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}k_{\lambda_{n}}(z),

where
a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
,
\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\|k_{\lambda_{n}}\|_{A^{1}}<\infty.

Here

k_{w}(z)=\frac{1}{(1-\overline{w}z)^{2}},\ w\in\mathbb{D},

is the reproducing kernel of
A^{2}
. A norm of
Y
is

\|f\|_{Y}=\inf\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\|k_{\lambda_{n}}\|_{A^{1}},

where the infimum is taken over the set of all such decompositions of
f
. From
[
9
]
,
Y
is a proper subset of
A^{1}
and
Y
is said to be a Luecking-type subspace of
A^{1}
.

Ghatage and Sun
[
9
]
gave a series of interesting results of the space
Y
. In particular, they described its dual and predual. Let
X
be the space of functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with

\|f\|_{X}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|\|k_{z}\|^{-1}_{A^{1}}<\infty.

Denote by
X_{0}
the little version of
X
. Namely, the space
X_{0}
consists of functions
f\in X
such that

\lim_{|z|\rightarrow 1}|f(z)|\|k_{z}\|^{-1}_{A^{1}}=0.

It is known that
X_{0}
is a closed subspace of
X
. Ghatage and Sun
[
9
]
proved that the dual of
X_{0}
can be identified with
Y
, and the dual of
Y
can be
identified with
X
. Recall that the Bloch space
\mathcal{B}
is the set of functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
for which

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|<\infty.

The little Bloch space
\mathcal{B}_{0}
consists of those functions
f\in\mathcal{B}
with

\lim_{|z|\rightarrow 1}(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|=0.

By
[
9
]
,
\mathcal{B}\subsetneqq X
and
\mathcal{B}_{0}\subsetneqq X_{0}
. See
[
3
,
4
,
8
,
13
]
for the further study associated with
X
or
Y
.

In
[
9
, p. 771]
, Ghatage and Sun asked whether
\mathcal{B}
is dense in
X
.
In
[
9
, p. 773]
, they asked whether
X_{0}
is a subset of
\mathcal{B}
.
In this note, we answer the two questions in the negative.

2.
The Bloch space and the space
X

The section is devoted to answer the two questions of Ghatage and Sun. By results and methods of Girela, Peláez, Pérez-González and Rättyä
[
10
]
, the proof given here is elementary.

Denote by
H_{\log}^{\infty}
the Banach space of functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
satisfying

\|f\|_{H_{\log}^{\infty}}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f(z)|}{\log\frac{e}{1-|z|}}<\infty.

See
[
10
]
for the study of
H_{\log}^{\infty}
.
From
[
8
]
or
[
9
]
, we know that

\|k_{w}\|_{A^{1}}=\begin{cases}1,\ \ w=0,\\
|w|^{-2}\log\frac{1}{(1-|w|^{2})},\ w\neq 0.\end{cases}

Note that

\lim_{|w|\rightarrow 0}|w|^{-2}\log\frac{1}{(1-|w|^{2})}=1.

Then it is easy to see that
X=H_{\log}^{\infty}
. Hence another norm of
X
can be defined by

|||f|||_{X}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f(z)|}{\log\frac{e}{1-|z|}},\ \ f\in X.

Also,
X_{0}
is the set of functions
f\in X
with

\lim_{|z|\rightarrow 1}\frac{|f(z)|}{\log\frac{e}{1-|z|}}=0.

From
[
9
]
,
\mathcal{B}_{0}
is densely contained in
X_{0}
. But the following result shows that the case for
\mathcal{B}
and
X
is different.

Theorem 2.1
.

The Bloch space is not dense in the space
X
.

Proof.

By
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
, there exist two functions
f_{1}
,
f_{2}\in X
such that

(2.1)

|f_{1}(z)|+|f_{2}(z)|\geq\log\frac{1}{1-|z|},\ z\in\mathbb{D}.

Suppose
\mathcal{B}
is dense in
X
. Then there are two functions
g_{1}
,
g_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
satisfying

|||f_{i}-g_{i}|||_{X}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f_{i}(z)-g_{i}(z)|}{\log\frac{e}{1-|z|}}<\frac{1}{3},\ \ i=1,2.

Hence,

|f_{i}(w)-g_{i}(w)|\leq\frac{1}{3}\log\frac{e}{1-|w|},\ \ i=1,2,

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}
. Consequently,

|g_{i}(w)|\geq|f_{i}(w)|-\frac{1}{3}\log\frac{e}{1-|w|},\ \ w\in\mathbb{D},\ \ \ \ i=1,2.

Combining this with (
2.1
), one gets

|g_{1}(w)|+|g_{2}(w)|\geq\log\frac{1}{1-|w|}-\frac{2}{3}\log\frac{e}{1-|w|},\ \ w\in\mathbb{D}.

Thus,

|g_{1}(w)|+|g_{2}(w)|\geq\frac{1}{12}\log\frac{e}{1-|w|},\ \ 1-e^{-3}<|w|<1,

which will produce a contradiction (see
[
10
, p. 514]
). In fact, for
1-e^{-3}<r<1
, we have

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(|g_{1}(re^{i\theta})|+|g_{2}(re^{i\theta})|\right)d\theta\geq\frac{1}{12}\log\frac{e}{1-r}.

Thus,

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|g_{j}(re^{i\theta})|d\theta\geq\frac{1}{24}\log\frac{e}{1-r},

where
j
is equal to 1 or 2. This gives

(2.2)

\varlimsup_{r\rightarrow 1}\frac{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|g_{j}(re^{i\theta})|d\theta}{\left(\log\frac{1}{1-r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\infty.

But a result of Clunie and MacGregor
[
5
]
or Makarov
[
16
]
asserts that

(2.3)

\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|h(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=O\left(\left(\log\frac{1}{1-r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ r\rightarrow 1,

for all
0<p<\infty
and
h\in\mathcal{B}
. We see that condition (
2.2
) contradicts condition (
2.3
) with
p=1
. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 1
. The first construction of the same fashion as condition (2.1) was given by Ramey and Ullrich
[
18
]
. More precisely, Ramey and Ullrich proved that there exist
f
,
g\in\mathcal{B}
such that

|f^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|\geq\frac{1}{1-|z|}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. In 2011, Kwon and Pavlović
[
12
]
generalized Ramey and Ullrich’s result by considering a wide class of weights.
We refer to
[
1
,
11
,
14
]
for more results related to these constructions.

Remark 2
. Choe and Lee
[
4
, p. 162]
also posed a question that is
\mathcal{B}
dense in
X
for the corresponding case in the unit ball of the complex space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
? Note that the results in the unit ball of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
corresponding to conditions (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) can be found in

[
1
, p. 400]
and
[
7
, p. 2808]
respectively. The same arguments as the proof of Theorem
2.1
yield that the answer to Choe and
Lee’s question is also negative.

Girela, Peláez, Pérez-González and Rättyä
[
10
, Theorem 8.1]

characterized certain lacunary series in
H_{\log}^{\infty}
(see
[
17
, Theorem 14]
for a more general result). We get the corresponding result of
X_{0}
as follows. As an application, we show that
X_{0}\nsubseteq\mathcal{B}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with the power series expansion
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{n_{k}}
and suppose there exist
\alpha>1
and
\beta>1
such that
n_{k}^{\alpha}\leq n_{k+1}\leq n_{k}^{\alpha\beta}
for all
k
. Then
f\in X_{0}
if and only if

(2.4)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|a_{k}|}{\log n_{k}}=0.

Furthermore, the space
X_{0}
is not a subset of the Bloch space.

Proof.

Let
f\in X_{0}
. Then
for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
, such that
|f(z)|<\varepsilon\log\frac{e}{1-|z|}
for
\delta<|z|<1
.
For this
\delta
, there exists a positive integer
N
, such that if
k>N
, then
1-\frac{1}{n_{k}}>\delta
. By Cauchy’s integral formula, one gets

|a_{k}|=\frac{|f^{(n_{k})}(0)|}{n_{k}!}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|z|=1-\frac{1}{n_{k}}}\frac{|f(w)|}{|w|^{n_{k}+1}}|dw|\leq\varepsilon\log(en_{k})\left(1-\frac{1}{n_{k}}\right)^{-n_{k}}\leq 2\varepsilon e\log(en_{k}).

Thus condition (
2.4
) holds.

On the other hand, suppose condition (
2.4
) is true. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a positive integer
K
, such that if
k>K
, then
|a_{k}|<\varepsilon\log n_{k}
. There also exists a positive constant
\eta
such that if
\eta<|z|<1
, then

\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{K}|a_{k}|}{\log\frac{e}{1-|z|}}<\varepsilon.

It is clear that condition (
2.4
) implies

\sup_{k}\frac{|a_{k}|}{\log n_{k}}<\infty.

Hence, by Girela, Peláez, Pérez-González and Rättyä
[
10
, p. 536]
, we see that there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
\alpha

and
\beta
, such that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\log n_{k})r^{n_{k}}\leq C\log\frac{e}{1-r},\ \ 0\leq r<1.

Therefore, for
\eta<|z|<1
, we obtain

\frac{|f(z)|}{\log\frac{e}{1-|z|}}\leq\frac{{\sum_{k=0}^{K}|a_{k}|}+\varepsilon\sum_{k=K+1}^{\infty}(\log n_{k})|z|^{n_{k}}}{\log\frac{e}{1-|z|}}\leq(1+C)\varepsilon,

which gives

\lim_{|z|\rightarrow 1}\frac{|f(z)|}{\log\frac{e}{1-|z|}}=0.

Namely
f\in X_{0}
. Consequently,
f\in X_{0}
if and only if condition (
2.4
) holds.

Let
h(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kz^{2^{2^{k}}}
. Take
\alpha=2
and
\beta=3/2
. Then

(2^{2^{k}})^{\alpha}=2^{2^{k+1}}<(2^{2^{k}})^{\alpha\beta}
and

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{k}{2^{k}\log 2}=0.

Thus
h\in X_{0}
. It is well known (cf.
[
2
]
) that if
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}
,
then the sequence
\{b_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is bounded. Hence
h\not\in\mathcal{B}
. In other words, the space
X_{0}
is not a subset of the Bloch space.
The proof is complete.
∎

Abstract
. Let C C be a curve over a non-archimedean local field of characteristic zero. We formulate algebro-geometric statements that imply boundedness of functions on the moduli space of stable bundles of rank 2 2 and fixed odd degree determinant over C C , coming from the Schwartz space of κ \kappa -densities on the corresponding stack of bundles (earlier we proved that these functions are locally constant on the locus of very stable bundles). We prove the relevant algebro-geometric statements for curves of genus 2 2 and for non-hyperelliptic curves of genus 3 3 .



1.
Introduction

We work over a non-archimedean local field
k
of characteristic zero.
Let
C
be an irreducible smooth proper curve over
k
.

The analytic Langlands program is concerned with the study of Hecke operators on certain spaces of functions
associated with the stack of
G
-bundles on
C
(see
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
,

[
4
]
and
[
5
]
). It is conjectured in this program that the Hecke operators act on appropriate
L^{2}
-spaces
and that their eigenfunctions belong to the Schwartz space of half-densities on the stack of
G
-bundles defined in
[
14
]
.

Let
\operatorname{Bun}_{\Lambda_{0}}
denote the moduli stack of rank
2
bundles over
C
with determinant
\Lambda_{0}
, and let
{\mathcal{M}}_{\Lambda_{0}}
be the coarse moduli space
of stable bundles of rank
2
with determinant
\Lambda_{0}
.
In
[
6
]
we considered the Schwartz spaces
{\mathcal{S}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{\Lambda_{0}},|\omega|^{\kappa})
(defined as in
[
14
]
), where
\kappa\in\C
, and defined natural maps
for
\operatorname{Re}(\kappa)\geq 1/2
,

\pi_{\kappa}:{\mathcal{S}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{\Lambda_{0}},|\omega|^{\kappa})\to C^{\infty}({\mathcal{M}}^{vs}_{\Lambda_{0}},|\omega|^{\kappa}),

given by integration over orbits, where
{\mathcal{M}}^{vs}_{\Lambda_{0}}
is the open subset of very stable bundles,
C^{\infty}(\cdot)
denotes the space of locally constant sections.

In the present work,
assuming
\deg(\Lambda_{0})
is odd,
we study the behavior of locally constant functions on
{\mathcal{M}}^{vs}_{\Lambda_{0}}
obtained as above near stable (but not very stable) bundles.
We pose the following conjecture about this behavior.

Conjecture A
.
Assume
\deg(\Lambda_{0})
is odd.
For
\operatorname{Re}(\kappa)>1/2
or
\kappa=1/2
, and any
\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{\Lambda_{0}},|\omega|^{\kappa})
, the locally constant
\kappa
-density
\pi_{\kappa}(\mu)
on
{\mathcal{M}}^{vs}_{\Lambda_{0}}
extends to
a continuous section of
|\omega|^{\kappa}
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\Lambda_{0}}
.

We will check this for genus
2
and
\kappa=1/2
(as a consequence of Theorems
3.2
and
4.20
).
We also consider a weaker conjecture, in which continuity is replaced by a weaker notion of a bounded (
\kappa
-twisted) distribution (see Def.
2.2
).

Conjecture A’
.
Assume
\deg(\Lambda_{0})
is odd.
For
\operatorname{Re}(\kappa)\geq 1/2
and any
\mu\in{\mathcal{S}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{\Lambda_{0}},|\omega|^{\kappa})
,
\pi_{\kappa}(\mu)
extends to
a bounded distribution in
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{M}}_{\Lambda_{0}},|\omega|^{\kappa})
. In particular, for
\kappa=1/2
,
\pi_{1/2}(\mu)
is an
L^{2}
-half-density on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\Lambda_{0}}
.

We will check Conjecture A’ for non-hyperelliptic curves of genus
3
(as a consequence of Theorems
3.2
and
4.34
).

In general, we formulate a set of algebro-geometric conjectures related to Brill-Noether loci associated with stable bundles of rank
2
and degree
2g-1
,
most notably, Conjecture B in Sec.
3.1
stating
that a certain scheme
F_{E}
associated with such a bundle
E

has rational singularities. More precisely,
F_{E}
is the total space of the family of projective spaces
{\mathbb{P}}H^{0}(C,\xi\otimes E)
, as
\xi
varies through line bundles of degree
0
.
Our main result, Theorem
3.2
, is that these algebro-geometric statements imply Conjecture A’.
We make some partial checks for these algebro-geometric statements (including verification for curves of genus
2
and non-hyperelliptic curves of genus
3
).
Debarre in
[
7
]
gave another proof of Conjecture B for genus
2
as well as a proof of the fact that each scheme
F_{E}
is irreducible of dimension
g
.

Our main tool to deduce results about distributions over
p
-adic fields from algebro-geometric results is
the Aizenbud-Avni’s theorem on continuity of push-forwards of smooth distributions under flat morphisms whose fibers have rational singularities
(see
[
1
]
). We deduce from this theorem a slightly more general version involving bounded distributions (see Prop.
2.6
).
On the other hand, we use a geometric picture developed by Bertram
[
3
]
and Thaddeus
[
24
]
that tells how
to go from the projective space of extensions to the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\Lambda_{0}}
through a series of controled birational transformations.

The difficulty of checking our conjecture on rationality of singularities of
F_{E}
is the absence of any obvious resolution of singularities.
The way we prove this for genus
2
and
3
is by listing possible types of singularities explicitly, showing that each of them can be specialized
to a normal toric singularity known to be rational, and using Elkik’s theorem that deformations of rational singularities are rational (see
[
12
]
).

2.1.
Background on Schwartz spaces and distributions

For a smooth variety
X
over
k
, a line bundle
L
on
X
, and a complex number
\kappa
,
we denote by
|L|^{\kappa}
the complex line bundle over
X(k)
, obtained from
L
and from the character

|\cdot|^{\kappa}:k^{*}\to\C^{*}
. The transition functions of
|L|^{\kappa}
are locally constant, so one can
define the space
C^{\infty}(X(k),|L|^{|}\kappa|)
(resp.,
{\mathcal{S}}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa})
) of locally constant sections of
|L|^{\kappa}
(resp., with compact support),
More generally, we can consider similar spaces fo
|L_{1}|^{\kappa_{1}}\otimes|L_{2}|^{\kappa_{2}}
, etc.
For a nonvanishing section
s:{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to L
we denote by
|s|^{\kappa}
the corresponding trivialization of
|L|^{\kappa}
.

We define the space of distributions
{\mathcal{D}}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa})
as the dual to
{\mathcal{S}}(X(k),|\omega_{X}|\otimes|L|^{-\kappa})
.
For an open subset
U\subset X
, there is a natural restriction map
{\mathcal{D}}(X,|L|^{\kappa})\to{\mathcal{D}}(U,|L|_{U}|^{\kappa})
, in particular, we can talk about the support
of a distribution.

The reason for the above notation is that the space of appropriate (e.g., locally constant) sections of
|L|^{\kappa}
embeds into
{\mathcal{D}}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa})
,
as we will recall now.

Recall that for every global nonvanishing section
\eta\in\omega_{X}
, the corresponding density
|\eta|
defines a positive measure on
X(k)
.
We have natural spaces
L^{1}(X(k),|\omega_{X}|)
(resp.,
L^{1}_{loc}(X(k),|\omega_{X}|)
) of (resp., locally) absolutely integrable densities defined as follows.
Let
U|\subset X
be a dense open subvariety with a nonvanishing section
\eta\in\Gamma(U,\omega_{X})
. Then for any
\nu\in\Gamma(X(k),|\omega_{X}|)
on
X(k)
, we write

\nu|_{U}=f\cdot|\eta|
, and require the positive density
|f|\cdot|\eta|
to be integrable on
U(k)
(resp., on
U(k)\cap K
for any compact open
K\subset X(k)
). This condition does not depend on a choice
of
(U,\eta)
. We identify
L^{1}
or
L^{1}_{loc}
densities which differ on a subset of measure zero, so that we have embeddings

L^{1}(X(k),|\omega_{X}|)\subset L^{1}_{loc}(X(k),|\omega_{X}|)\subset{\mathcal{D}}(X(k),|\omega_{X}|).

Definition 2.1
.

More generally, we define a subspace
L^{1}_{loc}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa})\subset{\mathcal{D}}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa})
as the subspace of distributions
\nu
, for which
there exists an open subvariety
U\subset X
, trivializations
\eta_{U}
of
\omega_{U}
and

s_{U}
of
L|_{U}
and a function
f
on
U(k)
, such that
|f|\cdot|\eta_{U}|
is integrable on
U(k)\cap K
for any compact open
K\subset X(k)
, and
for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X(k),|\omega_{X}|\otimes|L|^{-\kappa})
, one has

\nu(\varphi)=\int f|s_{U}|^{\kappa}\varphi

(this condition does not depend on a choice of
\eta_{U}
and
s_{U}
).

We have embeddings

C^{\infty}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa})\subset C^{0}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa})\subset L^{1}_{loc}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa})\subset{\mathcal{D}}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa}),

where
C^{0}
denotes the space of continuous sections.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a distribution
\nu\in{\mathcal{D}}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa})
is

bounded
if for every point
x
, and for trivializations
s
of
L
and
\eta
of
\omega_{X}
near
x
, there exists a compact neighborhood
B
of
x
and a positive constant
C
,
such that we have

|\nu(f\cdot|\eta|/|s|^{\kappa})|\leq C\cdot\int|f|\cdot|\eta|

for every locally constant function
f
supported on
B
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(i) If
\nu\in{\mathcal{D}}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa})
is bounded then for any
g\in C^{\infty}(X(k))
,
\nu\cdot f
is also bounded.

(ii) Let
X
be a smooth
k
-variety,
U\subset X
an open subvariety,
\nu\in{\mathcal{D}}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa})
a bounded element, such that
\nu|_{U}
is locally constant.
Then for any trivialization
s
of
L
on an open
V\subset X
, and any compact
K\subset V(k)
,
the function
\nu|_{U}/|s|^{\kappa}
on
U(k)\cap K
is bounded.

(iii) Assume in addition
X
is proper, and
\nu\in{\mathcal{D}}(X(k),|\omega|^{1/2})
is a bounded element such that
\nu|_{U}
is locally constant. Then
\nu|_{U}
is in
L^{2}
.

Proof.

(i) We have for
f
with compact support
B
,

|\nu(g\cdot f\cdot|\eta|/|s|^{\kappa})|\leq C\cdot\int|g|\cdot|f|\cdot|\eta|\leq C\cdot\max_{B}|g|\cdot\int|f|\cdot|\eta|.

(ii) It is enough to prove this with
K
being a sufficiently small open compact neighborhood of any point
x\in V(k)
. In particular, we can assume that there exists a trivialization

\eta
of
\omega_{X}
over
V
.
Since
\nu
is bounded, we can assume that there exists
a positive constant
C
such that
\nu(f|\eta|/|s|^{\kappa})|\leq C\cdot\int|f|\cdot|\eta|
, for any locally constant function
f
supported on
K
.
We have
\nu|_{U}=g\cdot|s|^{\kappa}
, with
g
locally constant on
U(k)
.
For a given point
y\in U(k)\cap K
, let
B_{y}\subset U(k)\cap K
be an open compact neighborhood such that
g|_{B_{y}}
is constant. Then
we have

|\nu(\delta_{B_{y}}\cdot|\eta|/|s|^{\kappa})=|g(y)|\cdot{\operatorname{vol}}_{|\eta|}(B_{y})\leq C\cdot{\operatorname{vol}}_{|\eta|}(B_{y}),

hence
|g|\leq C
on
U(k)\cap K
.

(iii) Let
X(k)=\sqcup V_{i}
be a disjoint covering by compact open subsets, such that each
V_{i}
is contained in a Zariski open
U_{i}
, such that there exists a trivialization
\eta_{i}
of

\omega_{X}
on
U_{i}
. Then we can write
\nu=\sum\delta_{V_{i}}\cdot\nu
, and it is enough to check that each
\delta_{V_{i}}\cdot\nu
is in
L^{2}
.
By (i), each
\delta_{V_{i}}\cdot\nu
is bounded, so by (ii), we have

\delta_{V_{i}}\cdot\nu|_{U}=g\cdot|\eta_{i}|^{1/2}
, with
g
locally constant, bounded and supported on
U(k)\cap V_{i}
. Hence,

|g|^{2}
is also such, and so
|g|^{2}\cdot|\eta_{i}|
is integrable (since
\mu_{|\eta_{i}|}(U(k)\cap V_{i})<\infty
).
∎

For an admissible stack
{\mathcal{X}}
over
k
and a line bundle
L
on
{\mathcal{X}}
, one defines the Schwartz space
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{X}}(k),|L|^{\kappa})
using smooth presentations
surjective on points (see
[
14
]
). In particular, if

is smooth then we have a well defined push-forward map

f_{*}:{\mathcal{S}}(X(k),|\omega_{f}|\otimes|f^{*}L|^{\kappa})\to{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{X}}(k),|L|^{\kappa}).

In the case
{\mathcal{X}}=[X/G]
, with

, the space
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{X}}(k),|L|^{\kappa})
can be identified with the space of

-coinvariants of

{\mathcal{S}}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa}\otimes|{\bigwedge}^{top}{\mathfrak{g}}|^{-1})

2.2.
Push-forwards for varieties

Let
f:X\to Y
be a morphism between smooth varieties. Sometimes it is possible to define push-forwards of distributions along
f
.
Let
L
be a line bundle on
Y
.

Definition 2.4
.

Given
\nu\in{\mathcal{D}}(X(k),|\omega_{f}|\otimes|f^{*}L|^{\kappa})
, let us say that
\nu
is
f
-integrable
if for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(Y(k),|\omega_{Y}|\otimes|L|^{-\kappa})
,
the distribution
\nu\cdot f^{*}\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}(X(k),|\omega_{X}|)
is in
L^{1}(X(k),|\omega_{X}|)
.

If
\nu
is
f
-integrable then we have a well defined push-forward
f_{*}\nu
given by

f_{*}\nu(\varphi)=\int\nu\cdot f^{*}\varphi.

For example, if
\nu\in L^{1}_{loc}(X(k),|\omega_{f}|\otimes|f^{*}L|^{\kappa})
has compact support then it is
f
-integrable.

Lemma 2.5
.

(i) Let
g:T\to X
be a smooth morphism. Assume
\nu\in C^{\infty}(T(k),|\omega_{fg}|\otimes|(fg)^{*}L|^{\kappa})
has relatively compact support over
X
, so
we have
g_{*}\nu\in C^{\infty}(X(k),|\omega_{f}|\otimes|f^{*}L|^{\kappa})
. If
\nu
is
fg
-integrable then

g_{*}\nu
is
f
-integrable. The converse is true if there exist trivializations
s
of
L
on
Y
,
\eta_{Y}
of
\omega_{Y}
,
\eta_{f}
of
\omega_{f}
and
\eta_{g}
of
\omega_{g}
such that

\nu=\psi\cdot|\eta_{f}\cdot f^{*}\eta_{g}|\cdot|(fg)^{*}s|^{\kappa},

(2.1)

with
\psi\geq 0
.
In either case

(fg)_{*}\psi=f_{*}(g_{*}\psi).

(ii) Let
U\subset Y
be an open subvariety, and let
f_{U}:f^{-1}(U)\to U
denote the induced morphism.
If
\nu\in{\mathcal{D}}(X(k),|\omega_{f}|\otimes|f^{*}L|^{\kappa})
is
f
-integrable then
\nu|_{f^{-1}(U)}
is
f_{U}
-integrable
and
f_{*}\nu|_{U}=(f_{U})_{*}(\nu|_{f^{-1}(U)})
.

Proof.

(i) Given
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(Y(k),|\omega_{Y}|\otimes|L|^{-\kappa})
, we look at the density
\eta:=\nu\cdot(fg)^{*}\varphi\in C^{\infty}(T(k),|\omega_{T}|)
with relatively compact support over
X
.
We have a well defined density
g_{*}\eta\in C^{\infty}(X(k),|\omega_{X}|)
. If
\eta\in L^{1}
then by Fubini’s theorem,
g_{*}\eta\in L^{1}
and

\int\eta=\int g_{*}\eta
, which proves the first assertion.

Now assume
\nu
has form (
2.1
), and take
\varphi\cdot|\eta_{Y}|\cdot|s|^{-\kappa}\in{\mathcal{S}}(Y(k),|\omega_{Y}|\otimes|L|^{-\kappa})
, where
\varphi
is locally constant
with compact support. We want to prove that the density on
T
,

\eta:=\nu\cdot(fg)^{*}(\varphi\cdot|\eta_{Y}|\cdot|s|^{-\kappa})=\psi\cdot(fg)^{*}\varphi\cdot|\eta_{f}\cdot f^{*}\eta_{g}\cdot(fg)^{*}\eta_{Y}|,

is in

. Without loss of generality we can assume that
\varphi\geq 0
. Then
\eta
is non-negative, so by Fubini’s theorem, if

g_{*}\eta=g_{*}\nu\cdot f^{*}(\varphi\cdot|\eta_{Y}|\cdot|s|^{-\kappa})
is in

then so is
\eta
.

(ii) This follows easily from definitions.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
f:S\to T
be a flat morphism of smooth varieties such that the fibers of
f
have at most rational singularities.
Then for a line bundle
L
on
T
and a bounded element
\nu\in{\mathcal{D}}(S(k),|\omega_{f}|\otimes|f^{*}L|^{\kappa})
with compact support, the push-forward

f_{*}\nu\in{\mathcal{D}}(T(k),|L|^{\kappa})
is bounded
(with compact support).

Proof.

Given a point
t\in T
, we can choose trivializations
\eta_{T}
of
\omega_{T}
and
s
of
L
near
t
, as well as a locally constant function
g
supported on a small
compact neighborhood of
t
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
\nu

is supported on a small compact neighborhood
B
of a point

such that

. Let
\eta_{f}
be a trivialization of
\omega_{f}
on a Zariski neighborhood of

containing

,
and let
\eta_{S}:=\eta_{f}\otimes f^{*}\eta_{T}
be the induced trivialization of
\omega_{S}
.
Then there exists a constant

such that

|f_{*}\nu(g\cdot|\eta_{T}|/|s|^{\kappa})|=|\nu(f^{*}g\cdot|f^{*}\eta_{T}|/|f^{*}s|^{\kappa})|\leq C\cdot\int_{B}|f^{*}g||\eta_{S}|=C\cdot f_{*}(\delta_{B}\cdot|\eta_{S}|)(g).

By Aizenbud-Avni’s theorem (see
[
1
]
),

has form

, for a continuous function

. Hence, there exists a constant

such that

, which proves boundedness of

.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let

{diagram}

For simplicity we assume that

We can assume that

Then

(resp.,

(resp.,

)

(resp.,

)

(resp.,

for every point

and trivializations

of
\omega_{Y}

2.3.
Push-forwards for admissible stacks and an extension result

Let
X
be a smooth variety over
k
with an action of
G=\operatorname{GL}_{N}
,
L
a
G
-equivariant line bundle on
X
,
U_{0}\subset X
an open
G
-invariant subset,
such that
G
acts freely on
U_{0}
and the quotient
U_{0}/G
exists. We pick a trivialization
{\operatorname{vol}}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\in|{\bigwedge}^{top}{\mathfrak{g}}|^{-1}

Let us denote by
\pi_{U_{0}}:U_{0}\to U_{0}/G
the natural projection.

For each
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa})
we can consider the restriction
\varphi|_{U_{0}}
and ask whether the push forward

\pi_{U_{0},*}({\operatorname{vol}}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\cdot\varphi|_{U_{0}})
is well defined, and if so, whether it is smooth, continuous, or bounded.

Definition 2.8
.

We say that a pair
(U_{0},X)
is

-
admissible
(resp.,
smooth
, resp.,
continuous
, resp.,
bounded
) if for every

\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(X(k),|L|^{\kappa})
, the distribution
{\operatorname{vol}}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\cdot\varphi|_{U}
is
\pi_{U_{0}}
-integrable in the sense of Definition
2.4

4.1.
Degeneracy loci and their partial resolutions

Let
\phi:{\mathcal{V}}\to{\mathcal{W}}
be a morphism of vector bundles on a scheme
S
, where
{\operatorname{rk}}{\mathcal{V}}=m+1
,
{\operatorname{rk}}{\mathcal{W}}=m
. Recall that the
0
th Fitting ideal of

is locally generated by the
m\times m
-minors of
\phi

the corresponding subscheme of

On the other hand, we can view

p^{*}{\mathcal{W}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(1)
on the projective bundle

Let

denote the zero locus of

as the family of projectivizations of the kernels of

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that

is integral and
\phi
is generically surjective.

(i) Let

be the closure of

.
Then there is a natural identification of closed subschemes in
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{V}})

X^{\prime}(\phi)=B_{Z_{\phi}}S,

where on the right we have the blow up of
S
at
Z_{\phi}
.

(ii) Assume in addition that

is smooth.
Every component of
Z_{\phi}
has codimension
\leq 2
, and every component of
X(\phi)
has dimension
\geq\dim S
.
If
X(\phi)
has dimension
\dim S
then it is a local complete intersection. If in addition

is irreducible then

.

(iii) Assume that
S
is smooth irreducible.
For every

, set

Then

is irreducible of dimension
\dim S
if and only if

for every

Under these assumptions,

Proof.

(i) Let
\pi:X^{\prime}(\phi)\to S
denote the projection.
By definition, the restriction of the composition

p^{*}{\mathcal{W}}^{\vee}\rTo{p^{*}\phi^{\vee}}p^{*}{\mathcal{V}}^{\vee}\to{\mathcal{O}}(1)

to

vanishes. Hence,

factors through a map of bundles of rank

\widetilde{\phi}:\pi^{*}{\mathcal{W}}^{\vee}\to\Omega^{1}_{p}(1)|_{X(\phi)}.

Hence, locally the pullback to
X^{\prime}(\phi)
of the ideal of

is given by one equation,

, which is not a zero divisor
since
X^{\prime}(\phi)
is irreducible and generically
\phi^{\vee}
is an embedding of a subbundle.

It follows that we have regular morphism
X^{\prime}(\phi)\to B_{Z_{\phi}}S
over

. On the other hand, by
[
21
, Prop. 3.1.1]
, we get a regular morphism

sending a generic

to the line

. Its image is contained in
X(\phi)
and contains

. Hence, it is contained in

, and we get the assertion.

(ii) Let

denote the space of
m\times(m+1)
matrices,
\phi_{m}:{\mathcal{O}}^{m+1}\to{\mathcal{O}}^{m}
the universal matrix over

, and

. Then locally we have a morphism

such that

is the pull-back of
\phi_{m}
.
Hence, locally

can be identified with the preimage of

under

.
Similarly,

can be identified with the preimage of

under the induced map

Equivalently,

is given as the intersection of the graph of

,
\Gamma(f)\subset S\times M_{m}
with

in

, while

is given as the intersection of
\Gamma(f)\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
with

in

It is well known that

is smooth of codimension

Similarly, since

is smooth of codimension

Furthermore, if

If in addition

(iii) Assume first that

Then the preimage of

in

has dimension

has dimension

is irreducible of
dimension
\dim S

Conversely, suppose

is irreducible of dimension
\dim S

the preimage of

is a closed subset of dimension

∎

Lemma 4.9

Proposition 4.12
.

Assume
C
has genus
2
. Then for any
E\in{\mathcal{M}}_{3}
,
F_{E}
is irreducible.

Proof.

Since we can replace
E
by
\xi E
with
\xi\in J
, we can assume that
E
is an extension

0\to KL^{-1}\to E\to K\to 0

with
\deg(L)=1
. The class of this extension

lives in
H^{1}(L^{-1})\simeq H^{0}(KL)^{*}
. Let
S\subset F_{E}
denote the closed subset where

H^{0}(\xi KL^{-1})\neq 0
. We will check that
\dim S\leq 1
and that
F_{E}\setminus S
is irreducible of dimension
2
. Since every irreducible component of

F_{E}
has dimension
\geq 2
, this will imply that
F_{E}
is irreducible.

Note that for every
\xi\in F_{E}\setminus S
, the induced map
\xi^{-1}\to K
is nonzero, so we have
\xi^{-1}\simeq K(-D)
for

. Furthermore, by assumption,

H^{0}(\xi KL^{-1})=H^{0}(L^{-1}(D))=0
, or equivalently,
H^{1}(L^{-1}(D))=0
. We claim that we get an isomorphism of
F_{E}\setminus S

consisting of

D
such that
H^{0}(L^{-1}(D))=0

\xi^{-1}\to K

It remains to check that
\dim S\leq 1

\xi^{-1}\simeq KL^{-1}(-p)
for some

. Then
\xi E
fits into an exact sequence

0\to{\mathcal{O}}(p)\to\xi E\to L(p)\to 0.

If

then

is
1
-dimensional. We claim that there is only finitely many such

for which

, or equivalently, the connecting homomorphism
H^{0}(L(p))\to H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}(p))
is zero.
Indeed, this connecting homomorphism is obtained by dualization from the pairing

H^{0}(L(p))\otimes H^{0}(K(-p))\rTo{b_{L,p}}H^{0}(KL)\rTo{e}k.

Thus, we need to check that there is only finitely many
p
such that the image of
b_{L,p}
is contained in
\ker(e)
.
But the image of
b_{L,p}
is precisely the
1
-dimensional subspace
H^{0}(KL(-\tau(p)))
, where

is the hyperelliptic involution
(so

\tau(p)
is in the preimage of

under the map to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
given by the linear system
|KL|
,
so there is only finitely many.

On the other hand, if
L(p)\simeq K
(so
\xi={\mathcal{O}}
) then we claim that the connecting homomorphism

is nonzero,
which implies that

, so this case contributes at most
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
as a component of

. Indeed, the connecting homomorphism is obtained by dualization
from the composition

H^{0}(K)\otimes H^{0}(K(-p))\to H^{0}(K^{2}(-p))\rTo{e}k.

But for any nonzero

is

-dimensional, so it cannot be contained in
\ker(e)
.
∎

4.3.
On some cases of Conjecture C

Theorem 4.13
.

Conjecture C(i) is true for any
E\in{\mathcal{M}}_{2g-1}
on a curve
C
of genus
\geq 2
such that
\operatorname{End}(J_{C})=\Z
(so it is true for general curve).

Proof.

Let
E
be a stable vector bundle in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\Lambda_{0}}
. We define the bundle
E^{\prime}
as a generic Hecke transformation of
E
, so that we have exact sequence

0\to E^{\prime}\to E\to{\mathcal{O}}_{p}\to 0

(4.1)

We immediately see that
E^{\prime}
is semistable.
Let us consider the generalized theta-divisor
\Theta_{E^{\prime}}=\{\xi\in E^{\prime}\ |\ H^{1}(\xi\otimes E^{\prime})\neq 0\}
(it is always of codimension
1
in
J
by the result of Raynaud
[
23
]
). Then we necessarily have

Z_{E}\subset\Theta_{E^{\prime}}
. We want to check that for an appropriate choice of the Hecke transformation we have
Z_{E}\neq\Theta_{E^{\prime}}
.

Let us pick a generic
\xi\in J
, so that
h^{1}(\xi\otimes E)=0
, i.e.,
H^{0}(\xi\otimes E)
is
1
-dimensional. Let
s_{\xi}\in H^{0}(\xi\otimes E)
be a nonzero element. Then for generic point
p\in C
, we have
s_{\xi}|_{p}\neq 0
.
and we take the line
\ell=\langle s_{\xi}|_{p}\rangle\otimes\xi^{-1}|_{p}\subset E|_{p}
. Then
the composition

H^{0}(\xi\otimes E)\to\xi|_{p}\otimes E|_{p}/\ell
will be zero, and we deduce that
h^{0}(\xi\otimes E^{\prime})=h^{0}(\xi\otimes E)=1
, i.e.,
\xi\in\Theta_{E^{\prime}}\setminus Z_{E}
.

Next, we claim that for a generic choice of
\ell
and
p\in C
,
E^{\prime}
will be stable. Indeed, one can show that there is finitely many subbundles
L\subset E
of degree
g-1
.
We need to make sure that
\ell\neq L|_{p}\subset E|_{p}
. But if
\xi
is generic then
H^{0}(\xi\otimes L)=0
, so
s_{\xi}
projects to a nonzero section
\overline{s}_{\xi}
of
\xi\otimes(E/L)
. For a generic
p\in C

we will have
\overline{s}_{\xi}|_{p}\neq 0
, so
\ell=\langle s_{\xi}|_{p}\rangle\neq L|_{p}
as required.

Finally, we claim that
\Theta_{E^{\prime}}
is irreducible.
Indeed, since
\operatorname{End}(J)=\Z
, and
\Theta_{E^{\prime}}
is algebraically equivalent to
2\theta
on
J
, if it is reducible it has form
\Theta_{L}+\Theta_{L^{\prime}}
for some line bundles
L
and
L^{\prime}
of degree
g-1
.
In other words, it coincides with the generalized theta-divisor
\Theta_{L\oplus L^{\prime}}
. It is known that the map from the moduli space of semistable bundles to the linear system
|2\theta|
is injective,
so this would contradict stability of
E^{\prime}
.

Since
Z_{E}
is properly contained in
\Theta_{E^{\prime}}
it has dimension
\leq g-2
. On the other hand,
Z_{E}
is obtained as the intersection of
Z^{\prime}
with
\{E\}\times J
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\Lambda_{0}}\times J
, so
each component of
Z_{E}
has dimension
\geq g-2
.
∎

y&z&x\end{matrix}\right)

has rational singularities.

Proof.

(i) Let us rewrite the equation as
x_{1}(x_{2}-M_{1})=x_{2}^{d}M_{2}
. Changing
x_{2}
to
x_{2}+M_{1}
, we get
x_{1}x_{2}=(x_{2}+M_{1})^{d}M_{2}

The latter hypersurface fits into an isotrivial family

x_{1}x_{2}=(tx_{2}+M_{1})^{d}M_{2}.

Since the limiting hypersurface
x_{1}x_{2}=M_{1}^{d}M_{2}
is normal toric, it has rational singularities.
Hence, by Elkik’s Theorem (see
[
12
, Thm. IV]
), so does the original hypersurface.

(ii) Changing variables we reduce to considering the ideals,

I_{1}=(xz,yz,xy),\ \ I_{2}=(xz,yz,x^{2}),

which are degeneration ideals of the matrices

\phi_{1}=\left(\begin{matrix}x&y&0\\
0&y&z\end{matrix}\right)\ \text{ and }\phi_{2}=\left(\begin{matrix}x&y&0\\
0&x&z\end{matrix}\right).

By Proposition
4.1
(iii), we have identitications of

with the corresponding blow ups.

By definition
X(\phi_{1})
is the closed subscheme of
{\mathbb{A}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
given by the equations

\displaystyle x\alpha+y\beta=0

\displaystyle y\beta+z\gamma=0,

where
(\alpha:\beta:\gamma)
are homogeneous coordinates on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. Over each standard affine open in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
we get a normal toric hypersurface,
so
X(\phi_{1})
has rational singularities. For
X(\phi_{2})
the equations are

\displaystyle x\alpha+y\beta=0

\displaystyle x\beta+z\gamma=0,

which still gives normal toric hypersurfaces on the standard affine opens, the most interesting one being

y\beta^{2}=z\gamma

obtained for
\alpha=1
.

(iii) The equations of
X(\mu)
are

\displaystyle x\alpha+y\beta=0

\displaystyle y\alpha+z\beta+x\gamma=0.

Clearly, the open corresponding to
\beta=1
is smooth. For
\alpha=1
, we get the hypersurface

y+z\beta=y\beta\gamma,

which has rational singularities (e.g., by part (i)).
Finally, for
\gamma=1
, we get the hypersurface

y\beta=(y\alpha+z\beta)\alpha

which has rational singularities by part (i).
∎

Homogenization of non-local energies on disconnected sets
Andrea Braides, Sergio Scalabrino, Chiara Trifone

Abstract
We consider the problem of the homogenization of non-local quadratic energies defined on δ \delta -periodic disconnected sets defined by a double integral, depending on a kernel concentrated at scale ε \varepsilon . For kernels with unbounded support we show that we may have three regimes: (i) ε << δ \varepsilon<\!<\delta , for which the Γ \Gamma -limit even in the strong topology of L 2 L^{2} is 0 0 ; (ii) ε δ → κ \frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\to\kappa , in which the energies are coercive with respect to a convergence of interpolated functions, and the limit is governed by a non-local homogenization formula parameterized by κ \kappa ; (iii) δ << ε \delta<\!<\varepsilon , for which the Γ \Gamma -limit is computed with respect to a coarse-grained convergence and exhibits a separation-of-scales effect; namely, it is the same as the one obtained by formally first letting δ → 0 \delta\to 0 (which turns out to be a pointwise weak limit, thanks to an iterated use of Jensen’s inequality), and then, noting that the outcome is a nonlocal energy studied by Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu, letting ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 . A slightly more complex description is necessary for case (ii) if the kernel is compactly supported. Keywords: homogenization, non-local functionals, perforated domains, separation of scales, Gamma-convergence. MSC Class (2020): 35B27, 74Q05, 49J45, 26A33, 74A70

The first issue that we examine for such energies is their coerciveness. Since the domain of

In the case of

(i)

(ii)

since a general

Finally, for

4.1
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(Q_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{d}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{l_{\varepsilon}^{2d+2}}{\rho^{d}\varepsilon^{d+2}}M_{\varepsilon}^{d}\inf_{w\,\frac{1}{M_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\text{-per.}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(Q_{\frac{1}{M_{\varepsilon}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\cap\left(\frac{E}{M_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{2}\\
\{|x-y|<\frac{\varepsilon}{l_{\varepsilon}}\}\end{subarray}}\left(w(x)-w(y)+\nabla u(x_{0})\cdot(x-y)\right)^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\frac{l_{\varepsilon}^{d}}{\rho^{d}}}_{\sim 1\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\delta^{d+2}}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\inf_{w\,\frac{1}{M_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\text{-per.}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(Q_{\frac{1}{M_{\varepsilon}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\cap\left(\frac{E}{M_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{2}\\
\{|x-y|<\frac{\varepsilon}{l_{\varepsilon}}\}\end{subarray}}M_{\varepsilon}^{2d+2}\left(w(x)-w(y)+\nabla u(x_{0})\cdot(x-y)\right)^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{d+2}}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\inf_{w\,\frac{1}{M_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\text{-per.}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(Q_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\cap(E)^{2}\\
\{|x-y|<\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\}\end{subarray}}\left(w\left(\frac{x}{M_{\varepsilon}}\right)-w\left(\frac{y}{M_{\varepsilon}}\right)+\nabla u(x_{0})\cdot(x-y)\right)^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{d+2}}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\inf_{w\,\mathbb{Z}^{d}\text{-per.}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(Q_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\cap(E)^{2}\\
\{|x-y|<\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\}\end{subarray}}\left(w(x)-w(y)+\nabla u(x_{0})\cdot(x-y)\right)^{2}dxdy,

where the second equality holds from a change of variable and the same value of the inf with
M_{\varepsilon}w
, and the last inequality comes from the fact that
x\mapsto w\big(\frac{x}{M_{\varepsilon}}\big)
is
1
periodic if
w
is
\frac{1}{M_{\varepsilon}}
periodic, and the infimum decreases. Finally, with fixed
\eta>0
, for
\varepsilon
small enough we have

\eta>\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\text{diam}(K)
,
and we set
Z_{\varepsilon}=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}|k|<1-\eta\}
. Note that, when
k\in Z_{\varepsilon}
we have

|x-y-k|\leq\text{diam}(K)+|k|<\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}-\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\eta+\text{diam(K)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}

so that
(x,y)\in(K\times E)\cap\{|x-y|<\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\}=(K\times(K+k))\cap\{|x-y|<\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\}
. By a double Jensen’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\inf_{w\,\mathbb{Z}^{d}\text{-per.}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}(Q_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap(E)^{2}\\
\{|x-y|<\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\}\end{subarray}}\left(w(x)-w(y)\right.+\nabla u(x_{0})\cdot(x-y))^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=\inf_{w\,\mathbb{Z}^{d}\text{-per.}}\int_{(K\times E)\cap\{|x-y|<\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\}}\left(w\left(x\right)-w\left(y\right)+\nabla u(x_{0})\cdot(x-y)\right)^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{w\,\mathbb{Z}^{d}\text{-per.}}\sum_{k\in Z_{\varepsilon}}\int_{K\times(K+k)}\left(w\left(x\right)-w\left(y\right)+\nabla u(x_{0})\cdot(x-y)\right)^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{w\,\mathbb{Z}^{d}\text{-per.}}\sum_{k\in Z_{\varepsilon}}|K|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{|K|^{2}}\int_{K\times(K+k)}w\left(x\right)-w\left(y\right)+\nabla u(x_{0})\cdot(x-y)dxdy\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in Z_{\varepsilon}}|K|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{|K|^{2}}\int_{K\times K}\nabla u(x_{0})\cdot k\,dxdy\right)^{2}=|K^{2}|\sum_{k\in Z_{\varepsilon}}\left(\nabla u(x_{0})\cdot k\right)^{2},

which is independent of
w

1
-periodic. Thus

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(Q_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{d}}\geq\frac{\delta^{d+2}}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}|K^{2}|\sum_{k\in Z_{\varepsilon}}\left(\nabla u(x_{0})\cdot k\right)^{2}

and

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(Q_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{d}}\geq|K^{2}|\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sum_{k\in Z_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d}\left(\nabla u(x_{0})\cdot\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}k\right)^{2}=|K|^{2}\int_{B_{1-\eta}}|\nabla u(x_{0})\cdot\xi|^{2}d\xi.

Using the lower semicontinuity of the mass,

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\mu(\Omega)\geq\int_{\Omega}\frac{d\mu}{dx}dx,

we get

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq|K|^{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u(x)\cdot\xi|^{2}d\xi dx=|K|^{2}C(d)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx,

with
C_{d}:=\frac{1}{d}\int_{B_{1}}|\xi|^{2}d\xi
as in
[
5
]
(for us
\varphi(z)
was the characteristic function of the unit ball).

4.2
Limsup inequality

Let
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
and let
\eta>0
be fixed. Let
\Omega\Subset\Omega^{\prime}
and set
Z_{\delta}:=\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:Q_{\delta}^{k}\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset\right\}
.
For
\delta\ll 1
we have
\bigcup_{k\in Z_{\delta}}Q_{\delta}^{k}\subseteq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\text{dist}(x,\Omega)\leq 2\delta\}\subseteq\Omega^{\prime}
. Moreover, without loss of generality we can assume
u\in C^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})
.

By the regularity assumption on
u
and a weighted Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\nabla u(x)\cdot(x-y)+R(x,y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\eta)|\nabla u(x)\cdot(x-y)|^{2}+\Big(1+\frac{1}{\eta}\Big)|R(x,y)|^{2},

with
|R(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{2}.

Moreover, in the assumption
\varepsilon\gg\delta
, it also holds

|k\delta-k^{\prime}\delta|\leq|k\delta-x|+|k^{\prime}\delta-y|+\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon+2\delta\leq\varepsilon(1+\eta)

for all
k,k^{\prime}\in Z_{\delta}
and
x,y\in Q_{\delta}^{k}\times Q_{\delta}^{k^{\prime}}
such that
|x-y|<\varepsilon
, which implies

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\Omega\cap\delta E)^{2}\\
\{|x-y|<\varepsilon\}\end{subarray}}|\nabla u(x)\cdot(x-y)|^{2}dxdy+C_{\eta}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\Omega\cap\delta E)^{2}\\
\{|x-y|<\varepsilon\}\end{subarray}}|x-y|^{4}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in Z_{\delta}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}(Q_{\delta}^{k}\times Q_{\delta}^{k^{\prime}})\cap(\delta E)^{2}\\
\{|x-y|<\varepsilon\}\end{subarray}}|\nabla u(x)\cdot(x-y)|^{2}dxdy+C_{\eta}\varepsilon^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,k^{\prime}\in Z_{\delta}\\
\delta|k-k^{\prime}|<(1+\eta)\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\int_{(Q_{\delta}^{k}\times Q_{\delta}^{k^{\prime}})\cap(\delta E)^{2}}|\nabla u(x)\cdot(x-y)|^{2}dxdy+C_{\eta}\varepsilon^{2}.

Note that for all
k,k^{\prime}\in Z_{\delta}
and for all
x=\delta k+z
and
y=\delta k^{\prime}+w
, with
z,w\in\delta Q
,

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x)\cdot(x-y)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x)\cdot(\delta k-\delta k^{\prime})+\nabla u(x)\cdot(z-w)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x)\cdot(\delta k-\delta k^{\prime})|+\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}\delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\nabla u(k\delta)\cdot(\delta k-\delta k^{\prime})|+\|D^{2}u\|_{\infty}\delta+\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}\delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\nabla u(k\delta)\cdot(\delta k-\delta k^{\prime})|+C\delta,

so that
|\nabla u(x)\cdot(x-y)|^{2}\leq(1+\eta)|\nabla u(k\delta)\cdot(\delta k-\delta k^{\prime})|^{2}+C_{\eta}\delta^{2}
.
Therefore we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,k^{\prime}\in Z_{\delta}\\
\delta|k-k^{\prime}|<(1+\eta)\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\int_{(Q_{\delta}^{k}\times Q_{\delta}^{k^{\prime}})\cap(\delta E)^{2}}|\nabla u(x)\cdot(x-y)|^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,k^{\prime}\in Z_{\delta}\\
\delta|k-k^{\prime}|<(1+\eta)\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\int_{(Q_{\delta}^{k}\times Q_{\delta}^{k^{\prime}})\cap(\delta E)^{2}}|\nabla u(k\delta)\cdot(k\delta-k^{\prime}\delta)|^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle+C_{\eta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,k^{\prime}\in Z_{\delta}\\
\delta|k-k^{\prime}|<(1+\eta)\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\delta^{2d}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,k^{\prime}\in Z_{\delta}\\
\delta|k-k^{\prime}|<(1+\eta)\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\int_{(Q_{\delta}^{k}\times Q_{\delta}^{k^{\prime}})\cap(\delta E)^{2}}|\nabla u(k\delta)\cdot(k\delta-k^{\prime}\delta)|^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle+C_{\eta}\sum_{k\in Z_{\delta}}\sum_{|j|<\frac{(1+\eta)\varepsilon}{\delta}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\delta^{2d}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,k^{\prime}\in Z_{\delta}\\
\delta|k-k^{\prime}|<(1+\eta)\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\int_{(Q_{\delta}^{k}\times Q_{\delta}^{k^{\prime}})\cap(\delta E)^{2}}|\nabla u(k\delta)\cdot(k\delta-k^{\prime}\delta)|^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle+C_{\eta}\frac{1}{\delta^{d}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\right)^{d}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\delta^{2d}\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,k^{\prime}\in Z_{\delta}\\
\delta|k-k^{\prime}|<(1+\eta)\varepsilon\end{subarray}}|(Q_{\delta}^{k}\times Q_{\delta}^{k^{\prime}})\cap(\delta E)^{2}||\nabla u(k\delta)\cdot(k\delta-k^{\prime}\delta)|^{2}+C_{\eta}\left(\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}.

Finally, by the periodicity of the set
E

\displaystyle|(Q_{\delta}^{k}\times Q_{\delta}^{k^{\prime}})\cap(\delta E)^{2}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|Q_{\delta}^{k}\cap\delta E||Q_{\delta}^{k^{\prime}}\cap\delta E|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{2d}|(k+Q)\cap E||(k^{\prime}+Q)\cap E|=\delta^{2d}|Q\cap E|^{2}=\delta^{2d}|K|^{2},

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,k^{\prime}\in Z_{\delta}\\
\delta|k-k^{\prime}|<(1+\eta)\varepsilon\end{subarray}}|(Q_{\delta}^{k}\times Q_{\delta}^{k^{\prime}})\cap(\delta E)^{2}||\nabla u(k\delta)\cdot(k\delta-k^{\prime}\delta)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,k^{\prime}\in Z_{\delta}\\
\delta|k-k^{\prime}|<(1+\eta)\varepsilon\end{subarray}}|K|^{2}\delta^{2d}|\nabla u(k\delta)\cdot(k\delta-k^{\prime}\delta)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|K|^{2}\sum_{k\in Z_{\delta}}\delta^{d}\sum_{|j|<\frac{(1+\eta)\varepsilon}{\delta}}\Big(\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{d}\Big|\nabla u(k\delta)\cdot\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}j\Big|^{2}.

It follows

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq(1+\eta)^{2}|K|^{2}\sum_{k\in Z_{\delta}}\delta^{d}\sum_{\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}|j|<1+\eta}\Big(\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{d}\Big|\nabla u(k\delta)\cdot\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}j\Big|^{2}+C_{\eta}\Big(\Big(\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\Big),

and, thus,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq(1+\eta)^{2}|K|^{2}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}\times B_{1+2\eta}}|\nabla u(x)\cdot\xi|^{2}dxd\xi.

Sending
\eta\to 0
and
\Omega^{\prime}\downarrow\Omega
we conclude

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq|K|^{2}\int_{\Omega\times B_{1}}|\nabla u(x)\cdot\xi|^{2}dxd\xi=|K|^{2}C(d)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx,

with
C_{d}=\frac{1}{d}\int_{B_{1}}|\xi|^{2}d\xi
.

4.3
\Gamma
-convergence with general convolution kernel
\varphi_{\varepsilon}

We recall that the general form of the functional we consider is

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{(\Omega\cap\delta E)\times(\Omega\cap\delta E)}\varphi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)(u(x)-u(y))^{2}dxdy

(26)

and our preliminary case considered is
\varphi(\xi)=\chi_{(0,1)}(|\xi|)
.
In the case of a general
\varphi
we can proceed by approximation. As stated at the beginning of Section
2
, we consider a radial convolution kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
; that is, a function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty)
such that a decreasing function
\varphi_{0}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
exists satisfying
\varphi(\xi)=\varphi_{0}(|\xi|)
. We further assume that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(\xi)(1+|\xi|^{2})dx<+\infty,

and for each
\varepsilon>0
we define the scaled kernel
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
by
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi)=\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\varphi(\tfrac{\xi}{\varepsilon})
.

Since
\varphi_{0}
is decreasing, we can write a ‘staircase’ approximation made of characteristic functions of nested intervals
(0,r_{k})
(where
r_{k}
are defined below), that is

\varphi_{0,n}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n2^{n}}\chi_{(0,r_{k})}(t)

(27)

where for each
k=0,\dots,n2^{n}

r_{k}=\sup\left\{t:\varphi_{0}(t)\geq(k+1)2^{-n}\right\}

and
r_{k}=0
if the set of such
t
is empty. With this choice,
r_{j}
is decreasing by the monotonicity of
\varphi_{0}
. Accordingly, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
define the simple function

\varphi_{n}(\xi)=\sum_{k=0}^{n2^{n}}\chi_{(0,r_{k})}(|\xi|).

Note that
\varphi_{n}(\xi)\leq\varphi(\xi)
for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By standard approximation through simple functions,
\varphi_{n}(\xi)\to\varphi(\xi)
pointwise almost everywhere, in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by dominated convergence and, since
\varphi
is in particular bounded, it converges also uniformly.

We define as
F^{n}_{\varepsilon}
the same functional (
26
) with
\varphi_{n}
in place of
\varphi
.
Since
\varphi
is an upper bound for any
\varphi_{n}
, we have for any
u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
fixed

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F^{n}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{(\Omega\cap\delta E)^{2}}\varphi_{n}\left(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\right)(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y))^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{n2^{n}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\Omega\cap\delta E)^{2}\\
\{|x-y|<r_{k}\varepsilon\}\end{subarray}}(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y))^{2}dxdy\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=0}^{n2^{n}}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\Omega\cap\delta E)^{2}\\
\{|x-y|<r_{k}\varepsilon\}\end{subarray}}(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y))^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=0}^{n2^{n}}|K|^{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{B_{r_{k}}}|\nabla u(x)\cdot\xi|^{2}d\xi dx=

\displaystyle=|K|^{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{n}(\xi)|\nabla u(x)\cdot\xi|^{2}d\xi dx,

by the result of Section
4.1
. We can pass to the limit for
n\to\infty
by monotone convergence and we get

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq|K|^{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(\xi)|\nabla u(x)\cdot\xi|^{2}d\xi dx=C_{\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx,

with
C_{\infty}=|K|^{2}\frac{1}{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(\xi)|\xi|^{2}d\xi
. Hence, the lower bound is satisfied.

As for the upper bound, we claim that it is enough to consider the function
\varphi
with compact support. Indeed, for any
R>0
we can split the functional

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{(\Omega\cap\delta E)^{2}}\varphi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)(u(x)-u(y))^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{(\Omega\cap\delta E)^{2}}\varphi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,\chi_{B_{R}}\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)(u(x)-u(y))^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{(\Omega\cap\delta E)^{2}}\varphi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}}\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)(u(x)-u(y))^{2}dxdy.

In the second term of the sum we can perform the change of variables
y=x+\varepsilon\xi
, so that, setting
\Omega(\xi)=\left\{x\in\Omega\cap\delta E:x+\varepsilon\xi\in\Omega\cap\delta E\right\}\subset\Omega
for each
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{(\Omega\cap\delta E)^{2}}\varphi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}}\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)(u(x)-u(y))^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}}\varphi(\xi)\int_{\Omega(\xi)}\bigg(\frac{u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)}{\varepsilon}\bigg)^{2}dxd\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}}\varphi(\xi)\int_{\Omega}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\xi|\right)^{2}dxd\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\Omega|\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}}\varphi(\xi)|\xi|^{2}d\xi=o(1)

as
R\to\infty
, uniformly in
\varepsilon
. Thus we are left with the first term where
\varphi\chi_{B_{R}}
has compact support, so that the claim holds.
For a general
\varphi_{0}
bounded, decreasing and with compact support, we can approximate it from above in the same way of (
27
), since for
\varphi_{0,n}
it holds

\varphi_{0,n}\leq\varphi_{0}\leq\varphi_{0,n}+2^{-n}

for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, so it it enough to take
\tilde{\varphi}_{0,n}(t)=\varphi_{0,n}(t)+2^{-n}
for
t\in\text{supp}(\varphi_{0})=(0,R)
, and
0
otherwise. Accordingly for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
define

\tilde{\varphi}_{n}(\xi)=\tilde{\varphi}_{0,n}(|\xi|)=\sum_{k=0}^{n2^{n}}\chi_{(0,r_{k})}(|\xi|)+2^{-n}\chi_{(0,R)}(|\xi|)=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}\chi_{(0,r_{k})}(|\xi|)

(
\mathcal{I}_{n}
is just a finite set of indices for each
n
) for which
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}\geq\varphi
and it converges to
\varphi
uniformly and in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Finally, for
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
fixed, by the result of Section
4.2

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{(\Omega\cap\delta E)^{2}}\tilde{\varphi}_{n}\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)(u(x)-u(y))^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\bigg(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\Omega\cap\delta E)^{2}\\
\{|x-y|<r_{k}\varepsilon\}\end{subarray}}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}dxdy\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\Omega\cap\delta E)^{2}\\
\{|x-y|<r_{k}\varepsilon\}\end{subarray}}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}|K|^{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{B_{r_{k}}}|\nabla u(x)\cdot\xi|^{2}d\xi dx=

\displaystyle=|K|^{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\varphi}_{n}(\xi)|\nabla u(x)\cdot\xi|^{2}d\xi dx,

and finally pass to the limit as
n\to\infty
to get the upper bound

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq|K|^{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(\xi)|\nabla u(x)\cdot\xi|^{2}d\xi dx=C_{\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx.

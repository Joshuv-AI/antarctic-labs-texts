Distributed Quantum Sensing with Squeezed-Vacuum Light in a Configurable Network of Mach-Zehnder Interferometers
Marco Malitesta, Augusto Smerzi, Luca Pezzè

Abstract
We study a sensor network of distributed Mach-Zehnder interferometers (MZIs) for the parallel (simultaneous) estimation of an arbitrary number d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 of phase shifts. The scheme uses a squeezed-vacuum state that is split between d d modes by a quantum circuit (QC). Each output mode of the QC is the input of one of d d MZIs, the other input of each MZI being a coherent state. In particular, i ) we identify the optimal configuration of the sensor network that allows the estimation of any linear combination of the d d phases with maximal sensitivity. The protocol overcomes the shot-noise limit and reaches Heisenberg scalings with respect to the total average number of particles in the overall probe state, the multiphase estimation only requiring local photocounting. Furthermore, the parallel multiphase estimation overcomes optimal separable strategies for the estimation of any linear combination of the phases: the sensitivity gain being a factor d d , at most. Viceversa, ii ) given a specific QC, we identify the optimal linear combination of the phases that maximizes the sensitivity and show that results are robust against random choices of the QC. Our scheme paves the ways to a variety of applications in distributed quantum sensing.

II
Distributed quantum sensing

II.1
Definition and strategies

In a distributed quantum sensing problem,
d
unknown parameters
\theta_{1},...,\theta_{d}
, are encoded in independent (e.g. spatially-separated) modes or interferometers.
Parameter encoding is described by commuting transformations.
In the ideal noiseless scenario, this is given by the unitary evolution

e^{-i\bm{\theta}\cdot\hat{\bm{H}}}=\otimes_{j=1}^{d}e^{-i\hat{H}_{j}\theta_{j}}
, where
\hat{\bm{H}}=\{\hat{H}_{1},...,\hat{H}_{d}\}
is a set of commuting Hermitian operators,
[\hat{H}_{i},\hat{H}_{j}]=0
for
i,j=1,...,d
, and
\bm{\theta}\cdot\hat{\bm{H}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\theta_{j}\hat{H}_{j}
.

The sensing scheme can follow an
entangled
(also indicated as parallel, or global, in the literature) or a
separable
(sequential, or local) strategy
ProctorPRL2018
;
GePRL2018
;
GessnerPRL2018
;
KnottPRA2016
;
GuoNATPHYS2020
;
XiaPRL2020
.
In an entangled strategy, the overall probe state
\hat{\rho}
of the sensor network is prepared in a mode-entangled state.
In contrast, a
separable
strategy uses the product state
\hat{\rho}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{d}\hat{\rho}_{j}
, where
\hat{\rho}_{j}
is the probe state of the
j
th sensor:
the different sensors are thus uncorrelated and the parameters
\theta_{1},...,\theta_{d}
are estimated independently.
Notice that classical correlations among the different
\hat{\rho}_{j}
are not useful to increase the multiparameter sensitivity, in general
GessnerPRL2018
.
Finally, an interesting possibility is to consider local measurements at each sensor, without requiring a mode entangled measures (although also distributed sensing scheme based on a final recombination of parameter-sensing modes have been considered
CiampiniSCIREP2016
;
GePRL2018
;
TriggianiARXIV
;
PolinoOPTICA2019
;
ValeriNPJ2020
;
OhPRR2020
): local measurements are advantageous when the sensing modes are spatially delocalized
ZhaoPRX2021
.

For the entangled multiparameter scenario, the method achieves an uncertainty

We also calculate analytically the QFIM (see Appendix A):

(\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}})_{ij}=|\alpha_{i}|(e^{2r}-1)|\alpha_{j}|\tilde{u}_{i}\tilde{u}_{j}+(|\alpha_{j}|^{2}+\tilde{u}_{i}^{2}\bar{n}_{s})\delta_{ij},

(6)

which is independent from
\bm{\theta}
.
We finally notice that Eqs. (
5
) and (
6
) do not depend on the QC transformation
\bm{U}
as a whole, but only on the vector
\bm{\tilde{u}}=\{\tilde{u}_{1},\dots,\tilde{u}_{d}\}
.
Given a specific QC transformation
\bm{U}
and the actual input port
D
in which
|\xi\rangle
is inserted, we can identify
2^{d}
different (non-orthogonal) vectors
\{\tilde{u}_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,d}
, depending on the choice of sign for each
\tilde{u}_{i}=\pm|\bm{U}_{Di}|
.
We recall that
\bm{U}_{Di}
is the element of the matrix
\bm{U}
at
row
D
and column
i
.

In the separable strategy of Fig.
1
(b), the
j
th MZI is fed with a coherent state
|\alpha_{j}^{\prime}\rangle
in mode
a_{j}
and a squeezed vacuum state
|\xi^{\prime}\rangle
in mode
b_{j}
, where
\alpha_{j}^{\prime}=|\alpha_{j}^{\prime}|e^{i\phi_{j}^{\prime}}
and
\xi_{j}^{\prime}=r_{j}^{\prime}e^{i\varphi_{j}^{\prime}}
.
Under the optimal condition
\chi_{j}^{\prime}=\phi_{j}^{\prime}-\varphi_{j}^{\prime}=0
(requiring phase locking between
|\alpha_{j}^{\prime}\rangle
and
|\xi^{\prime}\rangle
), we find
CavesPRD1981
;
ParisPLA1995
;
PezzePRL2008

\frac{\Delta^{2}\hat{X}_{j}}{(d\big\langle\hat{X}_{j}\big\rangle/d\theta_{j})^{2}}=\frac{|\alpha_{j}^{\prime}|^{2}e^{-2r_{j}^{\prime}}+(\bar{n}_{s}^{\prime})_{j}}{[|\alpha_{j}^{\prime}|^{2}-(\bar{n}_{s}^{\prime})_{j}]^{2}},

(7)

and the quantum Fisher information
PezzePRL2008
;
LangPRL2013

\mathcal{F}_{j}=|\alpha_{j}^{\prime}|^{2}e^{2r_{j}^{\prime}}+(\bar{n}_{s}^{\prime})_{j},

(8)

where
(\bar{n}_{s}^{\prime})_{j}=\sinh^{2}r_{j}^{\prime}
is the mean number of photon in the state
|\xi^{\prime}\rangle
.
Differently from the entangled strategy, the separable strategy uses
d
squeezed-vacuum states.
The total average number of particles in the separable sensor is thus
\bar{n}_{T}^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j}^{\prime}|^{2}+\bar{n}_{s}^{\prime}
, where
\bar{n}_{s}^{\prime}=\sinh^{2}r_{j}^{\prime}
is the total average number of photons in the
d
squeezed states.
When
d=1
, Eqs. (
5
) and (
6
) agree with Eqs. (
7
) and (
8
), respectively, and recover a single MZI with coherent
\otimes
squeezed-vacuum input state
CavesPRD1981
;
ParisPLA1995
;
BarnettEPJD2003
;
PezzePRL2008
;
LangPRL2013
;
Ruo-BercheraPRA2015
;
SparaciariPRA2016
.

IV
Optimal sensing configuration

In this section, we study the following problem: given a linear combination of parameters
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta}
, we want to find the optimal configuration of the Mach-Zehnder sensor network of Fig.
1
(a) that minimizes the phase uncertainty
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}
, for instance when using the method of moment as estimation strategy.
In other words, for a given
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we search for

\min_{\bm{U},\,|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2},r}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}.

(9)

We recall that the minimization over the QC transformation
\bm{U}
corresponds to a minimization over the vector
\bm{\tilde{u}}
defined above.
Here, we approach Eq. (
9
) by a direct calculation of the inverse moment matrix which, in some limits, assumes a convenient form suitable for analytical optimization.
Clearly, Eq. (
9
) can be also generalized to the quantum Cramer-Rao bound, namely

\min_{\bm{U},\,|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2},r}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}.

(10)

In Sec.
IV.1
, we derive upper and lower bounds to Eqs. (
9
) and (
10
) that hold for every
\bm{v}
.
We then compare, in Sec.
IV.2
, the optimized entangled and separable strategies, under different constraints.
In particular, numerical studies for
d=2
and
d=3
show that the optimal parallel strategy overcomes a corresponding optimal sequential strategy for every
\bm{v}
.

IV.1
Bounds and scalings

For fixed
\bar{n}_{T}
and
\bar{n}_{s}
, it is possible to find upper and lower bounds to Eq. (
9
), for every
\bm{v}
:

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}\geq\frac{e^{-2r}}{d\bar{n}_{T}}+\frac{\bar{n}_{s}}{d\bar{n}_{T}^{2}},

(11)

that holds for
\bar{n}_{T}\gg\bar{n}_{s}
, and

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}\leq\frac{e^{-2r}}{\bar{n}_{T}}+\frac{\bar{n}_{s}\mathcal{W}}{\bar{n}_{T}^{2}},

(12)

that holds for
\bar{n}_{T}\gg(d+1)\bar{n}_{s}
, where
\mathcal{W}=d^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{d}v_{j}^{4}
.
The above inequalities are derived in Appendix B.
As shown below, the upper bound is tight for
\bm{v}=\bm{v}_{\rm ave}=(\pm 1,\pm 1,...,\pm 1)/d
such that
\bm{v}_{\rm ave}\cdot\bm{\theta}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\pm\theta_{j}/d
(that, for brevity, we indicate as generalized average).
The lower bound is tight in the trivial case when
v_{j}=1/\sqrt{d}
and
v_{i\neq j}=0
such that
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta}=\theta_{j}/\sqrt{d}
(the factor
\sqrt{d}
is due to consistency with the normalization
|\bm{v}|^{2}=1/d
).
In this case, the optimal scheme consists of a single MZI with a squeezed vacuum in one port and a coherent state of
\bar{n}_{T}-\bar{n}_{s}
particles in the other port.
The bounds (
11
) and (
12
) are characterized by different regimes and scalings.

•

Regime
\bar{n}_{T}\gg\bar{n}_{s}e^{2r}\mathcal{W}
.

Noticing that
\mathcal{W}\geq 1
, this regime also implies
\bar{n}_{T}\gg\bar{n}_{s}e^{2r}
.
In this case, the first term in both Eqs. (
11
) and (
12
) dominates over the second one, giving
notaEq13

\frac{e^{-2r}}{d\bar{n}_{T}}\leq\min_{\bm{U},|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}\leq\frac{e^{-2r}}{\bar{n}_{T}}.

(13)

Both terms correspond to a sub-SN uncertainty with prefactor related to the squeeze parameter
r
.

•

Optimal squeezing.

We minimize Eqs. (
11
) and (
12
) with respect to
\bar{n}_{s}
, for a fixed
\bar{n}_{T}
.
Considering
\bar{n}_{s}\gg 1
(such that
e^{2r}\approx 4\bar{n}_{s}
) and taking the derivative with respect to
\bar{n}_{s}
, one finds

\frac{1}{d\bar{n}_{T}^{3/2}}\leq\min_{U,|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2},r}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{W}}}{\bar{n}_{T}^{3/2}}.

(14)

The optimal value of
\bar{n}_{s}
minimizing the upper (lower) bound
\bar{n}_{s}\approx\sqrt{\bar{n}_{T}/4\mathcal{W}}
(
\bar{n}_{s}\approx\sqrt{\bar{n}_{T}/4}
).
These values are consistent with the validity conditions of Eq. (
11
), namely
\bar{n}_{T}\gg\bar{n}_{s}
.
Furthermore, the upper bound in Eq. (
14
) holds under the additional condition
\bar{n}_{s}\gg(d+1)/(4\mathcal{W})
.
Overall, both bounds in Eq. (
14
) corresponds to a scaling of phase uncertainty faster than the SN.

•

Transient Heisenberg scaling for
(d+1)\bar{n}_{s}\ll\bar{n}_{T}\ll\bar{n}_{s}e^{2r}
.

In this regime, which requires
e^{2r}\gg d+1
, both Eq. (
11
) and Eq. (
12
) show a transient Heisenberg scaling
notaEq15
:

\frac{\bar{n}_{s}}{d\bar{n}_{T}^{2}}\leq\min_{U,|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}\leq\frac{\bar{n}_{s}\mathcal{W}}{\bar{n}_{T}^{2}}.

(15)

Equation (
15
)
is understood as
1/\bar{n}_{T}^{2}
scaling that holds as a function of
\bar{n}_{T}
in a restricted regime, and for a fixed value of
\bar{n}_{s}
.

In analogy to Eqs. (
11
) and (
12
), we can find an upper and a lower bound to Eq. (
10
):

\min_{\bm{U},\,|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}\geq\frac{1}{d[\bar{n}_{T}e^{2r}-\bar{n}_{s}(e^{2r}-1)]},

(16)

and

\min_{\bm{U},\,|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}\leq\frac{e^{-2r}}{\bar{n}_{T}-\bar{n}_{s}}.

(17)

The bounds holds for every
\bm{v}
and do not require additional conditions on
\bar{n}_{T}
and
\bar{n}_{s}
.
The demonstration of the inequalities (
17
) and (
16
) is detailed in Appendix C.
Also in this case, the upper bound is tight for the estimation of
\bm{v}_{\rm ave}\cdot\bm{\theta}
, while the lower bound is tight for the estimation of a single
\theta_{j}
.
We distinguish different regimes:

•

For
\bar{n}_{T}\gg\bar{n}_{s}
,
Eqs. (
16
) and (
17
) simplify and we have

\frac{e^{-2r}}{d\bar{n}_{T}}\leq\min_{\bm{U},\,|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}\leq\frac{e^{-2r}}{\bar{n}_{T}}.

(18)

Equation (
18
) holds, in particular, also in the regime
\bar{n}_{T}\gg\bar{n}_{s}e^{2r}\mathcal{W}
, where the upper and lower bounds to Eq. (
18
) coincide with that of Eq. (
13
).

•

Heisenberg limit for
\bar{n}_{T}\approx 2\bar{n}_{s}
.

Taking
\bar{n}_{s}\gg 1
(so that
\bar{n}_{s}\approx e^{2r}/4
), we can immediately optimize Eqs. (
16
) and (
17
) with respect to
\bar{n}_{s}
, and for fixed
\bar{n}_{T}
:

\frac{1}{d\bar{n}_{T}^{2}}\leq\min_{\bm{U},\,|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2},r}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}\leq\frac{1}{\bar{n}_{T}^{2}}.

(19)

It should be noticed that the value of the squeeze parameter that minimizes both bounds (
16
) and (
17
) is
\bar{n}_{s}=\bar{n}_{T}/2
.
This is different from the value that minimizes the bounds in Eq. (
14

The different optimizations correspond to different scalings,
O(\bar{n}_{T}^{-3/2})
and
O(\bar{n}_{T}^{-2})
, respectively.

IV.2
Comparison between optimal entangled and separable strategies

In the following, we compare optimal entangled and separable strategies for an arbitrary linear combination of parameters
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta}
.
Let us define the gain factor

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}=\frac{\min_{\{|\alpha_{1}^{\prime}|^{2},r_{1}^{\prime},...,|\alpha_{d}^{\prime}|^{2},r_{d}^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{C}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}}{\min_{\{\bm{U},\,|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2},r\}\in\mathcal{C}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}},

(22)

where
\mathcal{C}
indicates a common constraint on resources for both cases.
Different constraints are discussed below.
We recall that the uncertainties
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}
and
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}
in Eq. (
22
) are already optimized with respect to the relative phase between the squeezed-vacuum state(s) and the
d
coherent states.

The minimizations in Eq. (
22
) are performed numerically for
d=2
and
d=3
by using a variational approach, see Fig.
3
.
Analytical predictions for any
d
can be derived in interesting cases.
For
d=2
, the QC consists of a generalized beam-splitter that implements the mode transformation

\bm{\hat{b}}={U}^{\dagger}\bm{\hat{b}_{\rm in}}
, where

U=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&\varrho\\
-\varrho^{*}&\tau^{*}\end{pmatrix}
.
Without loss of generality, we consider
\tau
and
\varrho
being real numbers with
\tau^{2}+\varrho^{2}=1
.
The sensor network is given by two MZIs and

\bm{v}^{T}=(\cos\phi_{v},\sin\phi_{v})/\sqrt{2}
is expressed as a function of
\phi_{v}\in[0,2\pi]
.
For
d=3
, the sensing scheme consists of three MZIs and the goal is to estimate a linear combinations of three relative phases,
\theta_{1}
,
\theta_{2}
and
\theta_{3}
.
The QC is implemented as a general
3\times 3
orthogonal transformation.
Any such transformation can be realized, in general, by a sequence of three two-mode beam splitters
ReckPRL1994
.
For
d=3
, the vector
\bm{v}
is parametrized as
\bm{v}^{T}=(\sin\theta_{v}\cos\phi_{v},\sin\theta_{v}\sin\phi_{v},\cos\phi_{v})/\sqrt{3}
, as a function of
\phi_{v}\in[0,2\pi]
and
\theta_{v}\in[0,\pi]
.

Upon imposing different constraints, we observe

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(\bm{v})\geq 1
for every
\bm{v}
, see Fig.
3
.
In particular, we obtain
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(\bm{v})=1
when the problem reduces to the estimation of a single phase [e.g. for
\phi_{v}=0,\pi/2
and
d=2
, corresponding to
\bm{v}=(1/\sqrt{d},0)
and
(0,1/\sqrt{d})
, respectively].
The maximum gain
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(\bm{v})
is obtained for the estimation of the generalized
average phase
\bm{v}_{\rm ave}\cdot\bm{\theta}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\pm\theta_{d}/d
, that is
\phi_{v}=\pm\pi/4
for
d=2
and
\phi_{v}=\pm\pi/4
,
\theta_{v}=\arccos(1/\sqrt{3})
, for
d=3
(notice that the figure shows the case
v_{j}\geq 0
, while
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}(\bm{v})
is symmetric under
v_{j}\to-v_{j}
).

IV.2.1
\mathcal{C}_{1}
: same total average number of particles

We impose here the only constraint of having the same total average number of particles for both strategies:
\bar{n}_{T}=\bar{n}_{T}^{\prime}
.
The entangled strategy is thus optimized
over the QC transformation
\bm{U}
, the average number of particles of each coherent state,
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}
, and the squeeze parameter
r
.
The sequential strategy is instead optimized over the input states of each independent MZI, namely over each
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}
and squeeze parameter
r_{j}
, for
j=1,...,d
.
The gain factor is thus

\mathcal{G}_{1}(\bm{v})=\frac{\min_{|\alpha_{1}^{\prime}|^{2},r_{1}^{\prime},...,|\alpha_{d}^{\prime}|^{2},r_{d}^{\prime}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}}{\min_{\bm{U},|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2},r}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}}.

(23)

Numerical results for
d=2
and
d=3
are shown in Fig.
3
(a) and (b), respectively.

In the case
\bm{v}=\bm{v}_{\rm ave}

IV.2.2
\mathcal{C}_{2}
: same total average number of particles and same coherent state intensities

Here, we consider the same total average number of particles for both strategies,

, and the same coherent state intensities,

The entangled strategy uses a single squeezed-vacuum state and we optimize the multimode splitting in the QC.
The separable strategy is instead optimized with respect to the

(

The gain factor writes

\mathcal{G}_{2}(\bm{v})=\frac{\min_{r_{1}^{\prime},...,r_{d}^{\prime}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}}{\min_{\bm{U}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}}.

(25)

In the following, for simplicity, we can take

In Fig.

They agree well with the analytical prediction

that can be derived under the conditions

and in the regime
\bar{n}_{c}\gg\bar{n}_{s}e^{2r}
, see Appendix E.
We observe

for every
\bm{v}
, with maximum gain equal for
\bm{v}=\bm{v}_{\rm ave}
.

Let us now focus the discussion on the maximum gain point
\bm{v}=\bm{v}_{\rm ave}
and

\bar{n}_{c}\gg\bar{n}_{s}

In this case, the optimal entangled strategy is obtained for
\tilde{u}_{j}=1/\sqrt{d}
and we have

\min_{\bm{U}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}_{\rm ave}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}=\frac{e^{-2r}\bar{n}_{c}+\bar{n}_{s}/d}{d\bar{n}_{c}^{2}},

(27)

The optimal separable strategy is obtained using

r_{j}^{\prime}=r^{\prime}={\rm arcsinh}\sqrt{\bar{n}_{s}/d}
[since

(\bar{n}_{s}^{\prime})_{j}=\bar{n}_{s}/d

As shown in Appendix F, for
\bar{n}_{c}\gg\bar{n}_{s}
, we obtain

\min_{r^{\prime}_{1},...,r^{\prime}_{d}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}_{\rm ave}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}=\frac{\bar{n}_{c}e^{-2r^{\prime}}+\bar{n}_{s}/d}{d\bar{n}_{c}^{2}}.

(28)

Finally, the gain factor

\mathcal{G}_{2}(\bm{v}_{\rm ave})=\frac{\bar{n}_{c}e^{-2r^{\prime}}+\bar{n}_{s}/d}{\bar{n}_{c}e^{-2r}+\bar{n}_{s}/d}

(29)

is obtained by taking the ratio between Eqs. (

) and (

It is interesting to consider different limits of Eq. (

), see also Fig.

.

Let us consider

\bar{n}_{s}/d\gg 1
, such that
r,r^{\prime}\gg 1
, and
d\bar{n}_{c}\gg\bar{n}_{s}e^{2r}

\mathcal{G}_{2}(\bm{v}_{\rm ave})=\frac{e^{-2r^{\prime}}}{e^{-2r}}=d.

(30)

The gain factor

is obtained taking into account that, for

, we have

,
e^{-2r}/4\approx\bar{n}_{s}

A maximum gain close to

Let us consider the opposite limit,
\bar{n}_{s}/d\ll 1
.
The discussion includes the limit

d\to\infty
that is peculiar of the multiparameter problem.
First, we notice that, for any fixed value of
\bar{n}_{s}
, in the limit
d\to\infty
, we have
r^{\prime}={\rm arcsinh}\sqrt{\bar{n}_{s}/d}\to 0
.
The separable sensing scheme thus reduces to
d
MZIs fed with coherent state in one port and vacuum in the other port.
As shown in Eq. (
28

\min_{r^{\prime}_{1},...,r^{\prime}_{d}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}\to 1/(d\bar{n}_{c})=1/\bar{n}_{T}
.
It is easy to show that the same sensitivity is also achieved for the optimized quantum Cramér-Rao bound
\min_{r^{\prime}_{1},...,r^{\prime}_{d}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm sQCR}
.
The situation is completely different for the entangled scheme.
In this case,
r
remains finite in the limit
d\to\infty
and, according to Eq. (
27
), we have
\min_{\bm{U}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}_{\rm ave}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}=e^{-2r}/(d\bar{n}_{c})=e^{-2r}/\bar{n}_{T}
.
The entangled strategy still achieves a sub-SN sensitivity, with a gain factor

\mathcal{G}_{2}(\bm{v}_{\rm ave})\to e^{2r},\qquad{\rm for}\,\,d\to\infty.

(31)

Surprisingly, a finite gain is obtained when a single squeezed-vacuum state is mixed, by the QC, with a diverging number (
d-1
) of vacuum states
|0\rangle
.
The physical reason for the finite gain Eq. (
31
) is due to quantum correlations in the covariance matrix
\bm{\Gamma}
.
Under the above conditions
\bm{\Gamma}
reads
(see Appendix A for the general expression)

4\bm{\Gamma}=\frac{\bar{n}_{c}(e^{2r}-1)}{d}\begin{pmatrix}1&\ldots&1\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\ldots&1\end{pmatrix}+\Big(\bar{n}_{c}+\frac{\bar{n}_{s}}{d}\Big)\begin{pmatrix}1&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&1\end{pmatrix}.

(32)

In the limit
d\to\infty
, the prefactor of the all-ones matrix tends to zero while the prefactor of the identity matrix remains finite.
However, the all-ones matrix is characterized by perfect correlations between all its
d^{2}
elements, while the identity matrix does not feature any correlation.
When calculating
\tfrac{(1,1,...,1)^{T}}{\sqrt{d}}4\bm{\Gamma}\tfrac{(1,1,...,1)}{\sqrt{d}}=\bar{n}_{c}e^{2r}+\bar{n}_{s}/d\approx\bar{n}_{c}e^{2r}
[notice that the vector
(1,1,...,1)/\sqrt{d}
is normalized to one] the contributions coming from the two terms in Eq. (
32
) have the same magnitude with respect to
d
.
The finite mean value of the correlation matrix is responsible for the finite phase sensitivity and gain.

\mathcal{C}_{3}

We consider here
\bar{n}_{T}=\bar{n}_{T}^{\prime}

For the entangled strategy, we fix the ratio

When considering

IV.2.4
\mathcal{C}_{4}
: same total average number of particles and same squeezed-vacuum strength

Here, we compare the entangled strategy using a single squeezed-vacuum state
|\xi\rangle
with the separable strategy using
d
copies of the same squeezed-vacuum states
|\xi\rangle
.
In other words, here,
r_{j}^{\prime}=r
for all
j
.
For simplicity, we set
|\alpha_{j}^{\prime}|^{2}=\bar{n}_{c}^{\prime}
and
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}=\bar{n}_{c}
for all

We also consider the same total average number of particles,
\bar{n}_{T}=\bar{n}_{T}^{\prime}

\bar{n}_{T}=d\bar{n}_{c}+\bar{n}_{s}

\bar{n}_{T}^{\prime}=d(\bar{n}_{c}^{\prime}+\bar{n}_{s})

In this case, using Eqs. (

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}=\frac{\bar{n}_{c}^{\prime}e^{-2r}+\bar{n}_{s}}{d(\bar{n}_{c}^{\prime}-\bar{n}_{s})^{2}}

\bar{n}_{c}^{\prime}=\bar{n}_{s}

\bar{n}_{T}=2d\bar{n}_{s}

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm sQCR}=1/[d(\bar{n}_{c}^{\prime}e^{2r}+\bar{n}_{s})]

\bar{n}_{T}\gg e^{2r}\bar{n}_{s}

.
Instead, the entangled strategy, should be optimized over the QC transformation, thus giving

\mathcal{G}_{4}(\bm{v})=\frac{\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}}{\min_{\bm{U}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}}.

(35)

Let us focus on the case
\bm{v}=\bm{v}_{\rm ave}
and take the limit
\bar{n}_{c},\bar{n}_{c}^{\prime}\gg\bar{n}_{s}
[that, in particular imply
\bar{n}_{T}\gg(d+1)\bar{n}_{s}

\mathcal{G}_{4}(\bm{v_{\rm ave}})=1.

The opposite regime,

V
Optimal linear combination of phases

In the previous section, we have discussed the optimal configuration of the sensor network of Fig.
1
(a) that maximizes the sensitivity for the estimation of a fixed a linear combination of phases
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta}
.
Here we consider the opposite problem.
Given a specific configuration of the sensor network, namely a specific QC transformation
U
, coherent state intensities and squeezed parameter, we discuss the optimal linear combination of phases
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta}
that can estimated with the smallest possible uncertainty.

In the following, we fist provide a general framework for the minimization problem considered here, namely we introduce the notion of Fisher and squeezing spectra.
We then apply this formalism to the Mach-Zehnder sensor network of Fig.
1
(a) for random choices of the QC and discuss different sensitivity limits and regimes.

V.1
Fisher spectrum and squeezing spectrum

Finding the optimal vector
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
that minimizes Eq. (
1
) and/or Eq. (
3
) is solved by calculating the spectrum of the matrices
\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}}
and
\bm{\mathcal{M}}
, that we indicate as Fisher and squeezing spectrum, respectively.
These spectra contain useful information regarding the multiparameter problem, in general.
We have

\min_{\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}=\frac{1}{\mu_{\rm max}d},

(38)

where
\mu_{\rm max}
is the largest
eigenvalue of
\bm{\mathcal{M}}
.
The corresponding optimal eigenvector
\bm{v}_{\mu_{\rm max}}

gives the linear combination of parameters
\bm{v}_{\mu_{\rm max}}\cdot\bm{\theta}
that can be
estimated with the smallest possible uncertainty when using the specific method of moments considered (namely, based on the chosen measurement observables
\hat{X}_{j}
, probe state and phase encoding transformation).
Following the inequality

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}\leq\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}
,
we have
f_{\rm max}\geq\mu_{\rm max}
, where

f_{\rm max}
is the largest
eigenvalue of the QFIM and satisfies

\min_{\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}=\frac{1}{f_{\rm max}d}.

(39)

The corresponding optimal eigenvector
\bm{v}_{f_{\rm max}}
(in general,
\bm{v}_{f_{\rm max}}\neq\bm{v}_{\mu_{\rm max}}
) gives the linear combinations of parameters,
\bm{v}_{f_{\rm max}}\cdot\bm{\theta}
, that can be estimated with the highest possible sensitivity (when optimized over all generalized output measurements and all possible estimation strategies) for the given probe state and phase encoding transformation.
The demonstration of Eqs. (
38
) and (
39
) is reported in Appendix H.
Furthermore, a degeneracy (e.g. in the squeezing spectrum) reveals independent linear combinations of parameters that can be estimated with the same sensitivity.
Specifically, if
d_{\mu}
is the degeneracy of the eigenvalue
\mu
of
\bm{\mathcal{M}}
, then
the sensitivity
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}=1/(\mu d)

is the same for any
\bm{v}
given by a linear combination of the
d_{\mu}
orthonormal eigenvectors
\bm{v}_{\mu}^{(1)},...,\bm{v}_{\mu}^{(d_{\mu})}
.
In particular,
\bm{\mathcal{M}}^{-1}
is defined on the subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
generated by a basis of eigenvectors of
\bm{\mathcal{M}}
corresponding to finite eigenvalues (and similarly for
\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}}^{-1}
).

\bm{\mathcal{M}}

VI
Conclusions and Discussion

This work generalizes one of the most important archetype of quantum interferometry – namely, the single MZI with coherent
\otimes
squeezed-vacuum light
CavesPRD1981
;
ParisPLA1995
;
BarnettEPJD2003
;
PezzePRL2008
;
LangPRL2013
;
Ruo-BercheraPRA2015
;
SparaciariPRA2016
– to a distributed sensor network composed by
d
MZIs, see Fig.
1
(a).
The multiphase estimation analysis is based on a method of moments requiring local and independent photocounting at the output of each MZI.
This avoids to recombine the phase-shifted modes using a second multimode beam splitter
TriggianiARXIV
;
OhPRR2020
;
GePRL2018
.
The scheme is thus optimal to realize a highly spatially-separated sensor using a multimode entangled state of a large number of particles.
In particular, our MZI sensor network is characterized by different regimes reaching
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}=O(\bar{n}_{T}^{-3/2})
and the Heisenberg scaling
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}=O(\bar{n}_{T}^{-2})
.

The manuscript is focused on two different problems:

i) We have optimized the full Mach-Zehnder sensor network in order to minimize the uncertainty,
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})
, for the estimation of an
arbitrary
fixed linear combination of phase shifts
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta}
.
Studying the cases
d=2
and
d=3
, we have shown that the optimized parallel strategy, exploiting the mode entanglement generated by the linear multimode splitting is never surpassed by an optimized sequential strategy using mode-separable squeezed-vacuum states, when considering different constraints.
For any number of parameters, the maximum gain of the parallel over the sequential strategy is a factor
d
, for the estimation of the generalized average phase
\bm{v}_{\rm ave}\cdot\bm{\theta}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\pm\theta_{j}/d
. While the literature on distributed quantum sensing has mainly focused on the estimation of specific linear combinations of different parameters, the possibility to optimize the sensor network for the estimation of any desired
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta}
, as shown here, is generally highly desirable.
This possibility has only been shown in the configurable multimode displacement sensor of Ref.
XiaPRL2020
and for multipass phase sensing
GebhartARXIV
using a photonic Bell state
ZhaoPRX2021
.
Further configurable approaches include the splitting and multimode recombination of squeezed-vacuum light
TriggianiARXIV
and twin-Fock states
GePRL2018
.
In our case, the optimized sensing scheme uses local measurements and avoids the recombination of the states in a second quantum circuit.

ii) For arbitrary unitary splitting the squeezed vacuum state, we have identified optimal linear combinations of the
d
phases that minimize the uncertainty
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})
.
Typical results and scalings with the total average number of particles
\bar{n}_{T}
hold regardless the random choice of unitary QC transformation.

A further interesting problems raised in the context of multiparameter estimation is whether a single sensor network allows the estimation of multiple linear combination of phases at the same time
RubioJPA2020
.
This problem is solved here by changing the mode
D
of the QC where the squeezed vacuum state is injected, see Fig.
1
(a).
Indeed, a single
d
-mode QC can be optimized to estimate
d
different (e.g. orthogonal) linear combinations
\bm{v}_{D}\cdot\bm{\theta}
(with
D=1,...,d
and
\bm{v}_{i}\cdot\bm{v}_{j}=\delta_{ij}
) with the same sensitivity: each input mode
D
of the QC corresponds to a specific optimal
\bm{v}_{D}\cdot\bm{\theta}
.

It is also worth comparing here directly with the results of Ref.
XiaPRL2020
that considered a sensor network based on the linear splitting of a squeezed-vacuum state, displacement operations and homodyne measurements on each output mode.
It should be noticed that displacement sensing and phase sensing using a network of MZIs (as considered here) corresponds, in general, different parameter-encoding transformations.
A precise mapping is obtained within a (mean-field) Holstein-Primakof approximation, where the mode operator
\hat{a}_{j}
, see Fig.
1
, is replaced by the classical number
|\alpha_{j}|
.
In this case, the
j
th Mach-Zehnder transformation
e^{-\theta_{j}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{b}_{j}-\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j})/2}
reduces to the single-mode displacement operator
\hat{D}(q_{j})
with
q_{j}=|\alpha_{j}|/2
.
Our results predict
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{q})_{\rm eQCR}=\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}\times\bar{n}_{c}/4=e^{-2r}/4

for
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}=\bar{n}_{c}
, in agreement with Refs.
XiaPRL2020
;
ZhuangPRA2018
.
Yet, while the phase uncertainty is characterized by a convenient scaling with
\bar{n}_{T}
,

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}=e^{-2r}/\sqrt{\bar{n}_{T}}
, the displacement uncertainty does not scale with
\bar{n}_{T}
.
In this regime, the quantum Cramer-Rao bound is saturated by the estimation strategy based on the multiparameter method of moments
GessnerNATCOMM2020
based on local photodetection.
Furthermore, our formalism allows to go beyond the homodyne limit of Refs.
XiaPRL2020
;
ZhuangPRA2018
and discuss the phase sensitivity of a network of MZIs with respect to the total number of particles
\bar{n}_{T}
used: this is crucial to discuss scalings of phase variance that are faster than
O(1/\bar{n}_{T})
.

The results of this work are relevant in current experiments realizing squeezed vacuum-light and multimode linear splitting transformations
GuoNATPHYS2020
;
XiaPRL2020
;
NokkalaNJP2018
.
They pave the way to sensor networks using multiple MZIs – in both optical and atomic systems – with a large variety of applications ranging from field and biological sensing, gravitational wave detection, quantum clocks and inertial measurements.

We consider the general case where a product of
d^{\prime}

Here,

is the coherent amplitude of the state and

its squeeze parameter.
In the following, we will assume that
\hat{A}^{\dagger}
is a Gaussian unitary, that is, a unitary operator which transforms Gaussian states into Gaussian states.
Because
\hat{A}^{\dagger}
is also a passive, i.e. particle-number preserving, transformation, if we set
\hat{\bm{c}}=(\hat{c}_{1},\dots,\hat{c}_{d^{\prime}})^{T}

\hat{A}\hat{\bm{c}}\hat{A}^{\dagger}=\hat{\bm{c}}^{\prime}=\bm{\mathcal{A}}\hat{\bm{c}}

\bm{\mathcal{A}}
a unitary matrix (similarly,
\hat{A}\hat{\bm{c}}^{\dagger}\hat{A}^{\dagger}=\bm{\mathcal{A}}^{*}\hat{\bm{c}}^{\dagger}

Here,
\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{k}
and
\hat{c}_{k}
are bosonic creation and annihilation operators, respectively,

We recall the definition of the Q-function for the state
|\Phi\rangle
of a generic
d^{\prime}
-mode system:

Q(\bm{\alpha})=\frac{|\langle\bm{\alpha}|\Phi\rangle|^{2}}{\pi},

(48)

where
|\bm{\alpha}\rangle=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{d^{\prime}}|\alpha_{k}\rangle
,
|\alpha_{k}\rangle
being an arbitrary single-mode coherent state in mode

.
From the point of view of the Q-function, a transformation
\hat{A}^{\dagger}|\Phi\rangle
is equivalent to a transformation
\hat{A}|\bm{\alpha}\rangle
of the coherent states; moreover, it is a well know property that a Gaussian passive transformation
\hat{A}
sends a product of coherent states into another product of coherent states, in particular:
\hat{A}|\bm{\alpha}\rangle=|\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle=|\bm{A}\bm{\alpha}\rangle

As the notation just used suggests, and according to Ref.
GagatsosPRA2016

\bm{A}
is the matrix that implements the transformation
\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}=\bm{A}\bm{\alpha}
of the amplitudes of the coherent states associated with the Q-function. It is possible to show that this matrix is the same as the one which describes the transformation of the annihilation operators implemented by
\hat{A}
, that is
\hat{A}^{\dagger}\hskip 2.84544pt\bm{\hat{c}}\hskip 2.84544pt\hat{A}=\bm{\hat{c}}^{\prime}=\bm{A}\bm{\hat{c}}
, and, correspondingly, the hermitian conjugate of the matrix which describes the transformation of the annihilation operators implemented by
\hat{A}^{\dagger}
, which we have denoted as
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
above. The relation
\bm{\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}=\bm{A}
will be frequently used in what follows.

Using the Q-function representation of the states, (
47
), Ref.

showed that

\displaystyle\langle\hat{n}_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=-1+\partial_{i}\partial_{i}^{*}G(\bm{\mu})\Bigr\rvert_{\bm{\mu}=0},

where

\displaystyle\bm{EN}\circ(\bm{EN})^{*}-\bm{EN}\circ\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{T}-(\bm{EN})^{*}\circ\left(\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{T}\right)^{*}+\bm{E}\circ\bm{E}^{*}+\bm{E}\circ\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{\dagger}+\bm{E}^{*}\circ\left(\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{\dagger}\right)^{*}-\left(\bm{E}+\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{\dagger}\right)\circ\bm{I}.

(68)

It is evident that matrix
\bm{h}
is real and symmetric. An expression similar to Eq. (
68
) was derived in Ref.
GagatsosPRA2016
, see Eq. (14) in that reference.
There are however important differences with respect to Eq. (
68
) due to flaws in the derivation reported in Ref.
GagatsosPRA2016
.

VII.1.2
Quantum Fisher information matrix, Eq. (
6
)

Using Eq. (
68
), we now show how to express the QFIM in terms of the matrices
\bm{E}
,
\bm{EN}
, etc., which were introduced in the previous paragraph and will here be evaluated for the specific case of the Mach-Zehnder sensor network of Fig.
1
.
In our sensing scheme, the initial state is given by

|\Psi_{\rm in}\rangle=\left(|\alpha_{1}\rangle\otimes\dots\otimes|\alpha_{d}\rangle\right)\otimes\left(|0\rangle\otimes\dots\otimes|\xi\rangle\otimes\dots\otimes|0\rangle\right).

It is a product state of coherent states in modes
a_{1},...,a_{d}
, a squeezed-vacuum state
|\xi\rangle
in mode
(b_{\rm in})_{D}
and the vacuum
|0\rangle
in modes
(b_{\rm in})_{j}
for
j=1,...,d
and
j\neq D
.
This initial state should be compared with the product state in Eq. (
47
).
In order to facilitate the identification of the two cases, we can set
\hat{a}_{j}\equiv\hat{c}_{j}
and
(\hat{b}_{\rm in})_{j}\equiv\hat{c}_{j+d}

(j=1,\dots,d)
, thus introducing a more homogeneous notation valid for all of the
2d=d^{\prime}
input modes of the sensing apparatus. We then identify the
2d\times 2d
unitary matrix corresponding to the mode transformation performed by the QC as

\bm{U}_{\rm QC}^{\dagger}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{d}&0\\
0&\bm{U}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}.

The
d\times d
identity matrix
\bm{I}_{d}
describes the action of the QC on the coherent states, while
\hat{b}_{j}=\sum_{k}(\bm{U}^{\dagger})_{jk}(\hat{b}_{\rm in})_{k}

(j=1,\dots,d)
,
\bm{U}^{\dagger}
being a unitary
d\times d
matrix.
We denote as
|\Psi_{0}\rangle
the output state of the QC:
|\Psi_{0}\rangle=\hat{U}_{\rm QC}^{\dagger}|\Psi_{\rm in}\rangle
.

The phases
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d}
to be estimated are encoded in
|\Psi_{0}\rangle
through the unitary transformation
\otimes_{j=1}^{d}e^{-i\theta_{j}(\hat{J}_{y})_{j}}
, where the transformation
e^{-i\theta_{j}(\hat{J}_{y})_{j}}
identifies the
j
th MZI in the network, with
\hat{H}_{j}=(\hat{J}_{y})_{j}=(\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j}-\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j})/2i
. This is equivalent to the phases being encoded in the state
|\Psi\rangle=\otimes_{j=1}^{d}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}(\hat{J}_{x})_{j}}|\Psi_{0}\rangle
, where
(\hat{J}_{x})_{j}=(\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j}+\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j})/2
, through the unitary transformation
\otimes_{j=1}^{d}e^{-i\theta_{j}(\hat{J}_{z})_{j}}
, with
(\hat{J}_{z})_{j}=(\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}-\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j})/2
. This alternative formulation is more convenient here. Overall,
|\Psi\rangle
can be expressed as

|\Psi\rangle=\left(\otimes_{j=1}^{d}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}(\hat{J}_{x})_{j}}\right)\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{\rm QC}|\Psi_{\rm in}\rangle.

This equation should be compared with Eq. (
47
): the identification
\hat{A}^{\dagger}=(\otimes_{j=1}^{d}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}(\hat{J}_{x})_{j}})\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{\rm QC}
is straightforward. The action of
\hat{A}^{\dagger}
on the annihilation operators of the input modes can be represented by the relation
\hat{c}_{j}^{\prime}=\sum_{k}\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{jk}\hat{c}_{k}
(
j=1,\dots,2d
) with

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{A}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{d}&-i\bm{I}_{d}\\
-i\bm{I}_{d}&\bm{I}_{d}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{d}&0\\
0&\bm{U}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{d}&-i\bm{U}^{\dagger}\\
-i\bm{I}_{d}&\bm{U}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}.

(69)

Notice that the matrix
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{d}&-i\bm{I}_{d}\\
-i\bm{I}_{d}&\bm{I}_{d}\end{pmatrix}
in the product above describes an array of balanced beam splitters working in parallel, corresponding to
\otimes_{j=1}^{d}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}(\hat{J}_{x})_{j}}
.
Under the hypotheses of a pure state
|\Psi\rangle
for the system and of a phase-imprinting transformation of the form
\otimes_{j=1}^{d}e^{-i\theta_{j}(\hat{J}_{z})_{j}}
(see above), the QFIM is given by

\left(\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}}\right)_{ij}=4\left(\langle\Psi|(\hat{J}_{z})_{i}(\hat{J}_{z})_{j}|\Psi\rangle-\langle\Psi|(\hat{J}_{z})_{i}|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|(\hat{J}_{z})_{j}|\Psi\rangle\right)\hskip 14.22636pt(i,j=1,\dots,d).

Since
(\hat{J}_{z})_{j}=(\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}-\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j})/2=(\hat{c}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{j}-\hat{c}_{j+d}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{j+d})/2=(\hat{n}_{j}-\hat{n}_{j+d})/2
, after simple calculations we get to express
\left(\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}}\right)_{ij}
as

\left(\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}}\right)_{ij}=\bm{h}_{i,j}+\bm{h}_{i+d,j+d}-\bm{h}_{i,j+d}-\bm{h}_{i+d,j},\hskip 14.22636pt(i,j=1,\dots,d)

(70)

where the expression of
\bm{h}_{ij}
,
\bm{h}_{ij}=\langle\Psi|\hat{n}_{i}\hat{n}_{j}|\Psi\rangle-\langle\Psi|\hat{n}_{i}|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|\hat{n}_{j}|\Psi\rangle
, is exactly the one already given in Eq. (
67
). Notice that
\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}}
is a
d\times d
matrix, whose elements, according to Eq. (
70
), can be obtained as combinations of the elements of
\bm{h}
, a
2d\times 2d
matrix.

Equation (
68
) from the previous paragraph expresses
\bm{h}
in terms of the two matrices
\bm{E}
and
\bm{EN}
and the vector
\bm{\gamma}
. The two matrices are derived by referring to Eqs. from (
55

\displaystyle\bm{C}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{d}&0\\
0&\bm{C}_{1}\end{pmatrix},

(71)

and

\displaystyle\bm{CD}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\bm{C}_{1}\bm{D}_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

(72)

In the above equations,
\bm{C_{1}}
and
\bm{C_{1}D_{1}}
are
d\times d
matrices with elements

(\bm{C_{1}})_{ij}=\delta_{ij}\left[\left(1-\delta_{Dj}\right)+\delta_{Dj}c^{2}\right],

(73)

and

(74)

,

(these shortcuts will be repeatedly used below).
On account of Eqs. (
55

56

discussed above, we have (
\bm{U}\equiv\bm{A_{1}}

\displaystyle\bm{E}=

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{\dagger}\bm{C}\bm{A}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{d}+\bm{E_{1}}&i(\bm{I}_{d}-\bm{E_{1}})\\
-i(\bm{I}_{d}-\bm{E_{1}})&\bm{I}_{d}+\bm{E_{1}}\end{pmatrix},

with

, and

\displaystyle\bm{EN}=

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{\dagger}\bm{CD}\bm{A}^{*}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}-\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}&-i\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\\
-i\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}&\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\end{pmatrix},

It is not difficult to see that, when none of the input modes is in a state

with both

and
\xi\neq 0

\bm{b}=\bm{A}^{\dagger}\bm{\beta}

and

\displaystyle\bm{\beta_{0}}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{d}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}|\alpha_{1}|e^{i\phi_{1}}\\

\displaystyle\bm{\gamma}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\beta_{0}}^{*}\\

i\bm{\gamma_{0}}\end{pmatrix},

and

\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\gamma_{0}}\bm{\gamma_{0}}^{\dagger}&-i\bm{\gamma_{0}}\bm{\gamma_{0}}^{\dagger}\\
i\bm{\gamma_{0}}\bm{\gamma_{0}}^{\dagger}&\bm{\gamma_{0}}\bm{\gamma_{0}}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\bm{\gamma_{0}}\bm{\gamma_{0}}^{T}=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}|\alpha_{1}|^{2}e^{-2i\phi_{1}}&|\alpha_{1}||\alpha_{2}|e^{-i(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})}&\ldots&|\alpha_{1}||\alpha_{d}|e^{-i(\phi_{1}+\phi_{d})}\\

and

\displaystyle\bm{\gamma_{0}}\bm{\gamma_{0}}^{\dagger}=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}|\alpha_{1}|^{2}&|\alpha_{1}||\alpha_{2}|e^{-i(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})}&\ldots&|\alpha_{1}||\alpha_{d}|e^{-i(\phi_{1}-\phi_{d})}\\

According to Eq. (
68

\displaystyle\bm{h}=

\displaystyle\bm{EN}\circ(\bm{EN})^{*}-\bm{EN}\circ\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{T}-(\bm{EN})^{*}\circ\left(\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{T}\right)^{*}+\bm{E}\circ\bm{E}^{*}+\bm{E}\circ\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{\dagger}+\bm{E}^{*}\circ\left(\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{\dagger}\right)^{*}-\left(\bm{E}+\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{\dagger}\right)\circ\bm{I},

\displaystyle\bm{EN}\circ(\bm{EN})^{*}=\frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\circ(\bm{E_{1}N_{1}})^{*}&\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\circ(\bm{E_{1}N_{1}})^{*}\\

(75)

\displaystyle-\bm{EN}\circ\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{T}=\frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\circ\bm{\phi_{+}}&-\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\circ\bm{\phi_{+}}\\

(76)

(77)

(78)

\displaystyle-\left(\bm{E}+\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{\dagger}\right)\circ\bm{I}_{d}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{d}+\bm{E_{1}}\circ\bm{I}_{d}+\bm{I}_{d}\circ\bm{\phi_{-}}&0\\

(79)

The above set of equations shows that

is a sum of

matrices of the form
\begin{pmatrix}\bm{X}^{(k)}&\bm{Y}^{(k)}\\

and
\bm{Y}=\sum_{k}\bm{Y}^{(k)}
is enough to determine
\bm{h}
completely. We find

\displaystyle\bm{X}=\frac{1}{4}\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\circ(\bm{E_{1}N_{1}})^{*}+\frac{1}{4}\bm{E_{1}}\circ\bm{E_{1}}^{*}+\frac{1}{4}\left[\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\circ\bm{\phi_{+}}+(\bm{E_{1}N_{1}})^{*}\circ\bm{\phi_{+}}^{*}\right]+\frac{1}{4}(\bm{E_{1}}\circ\bm{\phi_{-}}+\bm{E_{1}}^{*}\circ\bm{\phi_{-}}^{*})+\frac{1}{4}(\bm{E_{1}}+\bm{E_{1}}^{*})\circ\bm{I}_{d}-\frac{1}{2}\bm{E_{1}}\circ\bm{I}_{d}-\frac{1}{4}\bm{I}_{d}

and

\displaystyle\bm{Y}=\frac{1}{4}\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\circ(\bm{E_{1}N_{1}})^{*}+\frac{1}{4}\bm{E_{1}}\circ\bm{E_{1}}^{*}-\frac{1}{4}\left[\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\circ\bm{\phi_{+}}+(\bm{E_{1}N_{1}})^{*}\circ\bm{\phi_{+}}^{*}\right]-\frac{1}{4}(\bm{E_{1}}\circ\bm{\phi_{-}}+\bm{E_{1}}^{*}\circ\bm{\phi_{-}}^{*})-\frac{1}{4}(\bm{E_{1}}+\bm{E_{1}}^{*})\circ\bm{I}_{d}+\frac{1}{2}\bm{I}_{d}\circ\bm{\phi_{-}}+\frac{1}{4}\bm{I}_{d}.

Finally, from Eq. (
70
), and using

\bm{h}_{i,j}=\bm{h}_{i+d,j+d}=\bm{X}_{ij}

and

\bm{h}_{i,j+d}=\bm{h}_{i+d,j}=\bm{Y}_{ij}
,
we find

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}}=

\displaystyle 2\bm{X}-2\bm{Y}=\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\circ\bm{\phi_{+}}^{*}+(\bm{E_{1}N_{1}})^{*}\circ\bm{\phi_{+}}^{*}+\bm{E_{1}}\circ\bm{\phi_{-}}+\bm{E_{1}}^{*}\circ\bm{\phi_{-}}^{*}-\bm{\phi_{-}}\circ\bm{I}_{d}+\bm{E_{1}}^{*}\circ\bm{I}_{d}-\bm{I}_{d}.

(80)

Furthermore, we use

\displaystyle(\bm{E_{1}N_{1}})_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\bm{A_{1}}^{\dagger}\bm{C_{1}D_{1}}\bm{A_{1}}^{*}\right)_{ij}=\sum_{kl}\left(\bm{U}^{\dagger}\right)_{ik}\left(e^{i\varphi}sc\right)\delta_{kl}\delta_{Dl}\left(\bm{U}^{*}\right)_{lj}=e^{i\varphi}sc\hskip 2.84544pt\left(\bm{U}^{\dagger}\right)_{iD}\left(\bm{U}^{*}\right)_{Dj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\varphi}sc\hskip 2.84544pt\left(\bm{U}^{*}\right)_{Di}\left(\bm{U}^{*}\right)_{Dj}

and

\displaystyle(\bm{E_{1}})_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\bm{A_{1}}^{\dagger}\bm{C_{1}}\bm{A_{1}}\right)_{ij}=\sum_{kl}\left(\bm{U}^{\dagger}\right)_{ik}\delta_{kl}\left[\left(1-\delta_{Dl}\right)+\delta_{Dl}c^{2}\right]\left(\bm{U}\right)_{lj}=\sum_{kl}\left(\bm{U}^{\dagger}\right)_{ik}\delta_{kl}\left(\bm{U}\right)_{lj}+\sum_{kl}\left(\bm{U}^{\dagger}\right)_{ik}(c^{2}-1)\delta_{kl}\delta_{Dl}\left(\bm{U}\right)_{lj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}+s^{2}\left(\bm{U}^{*}\right)_{Di}\left(\bm{U}\right)_{Dj},

derived on the basis of Eq. (
74
) and (
73
), respectively.
Also, since

(\bm{\phi_{+}})_{ij}=|\alpha_{i}||\alpha_{j}|e^{-i\phi_{i}}e^{-i\phi_{j}}
,
and

(\bm{\phi_{-}})_{ij}=|\alpha_{i}||\alpha_{j}|e^{-i\phi_{i}}e^{i\phi_{j}}
,
one has

(\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\circ\bm{\phi_{+}})_{ij}=e^{i\varphi}sc\hskip 2.84544pt\left(\bm{U}^{*}\right)_{Di}\left(\bm{U}^{*}\right)_{Dj}|\alpha_{i}||\alpha_{j}|e^{-i\phi_{i}}e^{-i\phi_{j}},

(81)

and

\displaystyle(\bm{E_{1}}\circ\bm{\phi_{-}})_{ij}=

\displaystyle\left[\delta_{ij}+s^{2}\left(\bm{U}^{*}\right)_{Di}\left(\bm{U}\right)_{Dj}\right]|\alpha_{i}||\alpha_{j}|e^{-i\phi_{i}}e^{i\phi_{j}}=|\alpha_{i}|^{2}\delta_{ij}+s^{2}\left(\bm{U}^{*}\right)_{Di}\left(\bm{U}\right)_{Dj}|\alpha_{i}||\alpha_{j}|e^{-i\phi_{i}}e^{i\phi_{j}}.

(82)

For the sake of a lighter notation, we set
(\bm{U})_{Di}\equiv u_{i}
; the same notation was used in the main text. Plugging Eqs. (
81
) and (
82
) into Eq. (
80

\displaystyle(\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}})_{ij}=

\displaystyle|\alpha_{i}||\alpha_{j}|\left[\left(e^{-i\varphi}u_{i}e^{i\phi_{i}}u_{j}e^{i\phi_{j}}+e^{i\varphi}u^{*}_{i}e^{-i\phi_{i}}u^{*}_{j}e^{-i\phi_{j}}\right)sc+2\delta_{ij}+\left(u^{*}_{i}e^{-i\phi_{i}}u_{j}e^{i\phi_{j}}+u_{i}e^{i\phi_{i}}u^{*}_{j}e^{-i\phi_{j}}\right)s^{2}-\delta_{ij}\right]+|u_{i}|^{2}\delta_{ij}s^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha_{i}||\alpha_{j}|\left[\Re\left(e^{i\chi_{i}}u_{i}\right)\Re\left(e^{i\chi_{j}}u_{j}\right)\left(e^{2r}-1\right)+\Im\left(e^{i\chi_{i}}u_{i}\right)\Im\left(e^{i\chi_{j}}u_{j}\right)\left(e^{-2r}-1\right)\right]+\left(|\alpha_{i}|^{2}+|u_{i}|^{2}\bar{n}_{s}\right)\delta_{ij},

(83)

where
\chi_{i}\equiv\phi_{i}-\varphi/2
and

. By imposing the condition
\Im(e^{i\chi_{j}}u_{j})=0
, for

, one finally recovers Eq. (
6
) of the main text.

VII.1.3
Inverse moment matrix, Eq. (
5
)

The so called
moment matrix
corresponds to the covariance matrix of a particular set of estimators of the unknown parameters
\theta_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,d
. It is defined as follows
GessnerNATCOMM2020
:

\bm{\mathcal{M}}=\bm{G}^{T}\bm{\Gamma}^{-1}\bm{G},

(84)

where

\bm{G}_{ij}=\frac{\partial\langle\hat{X}_{i}\rangle_{\rho(\bm{\theta})}}{\partial\theta_{j}}\hskip 14.22636pt(i=1,\dots,K;\hskip 2.84544ptj=1,\dots,d),

(85)

and

\bm{\Gamma}_{ij}=\langle\hat{X}_{i}\hat{X}_{j}\rangle_{\rho(\bm{\theta})}-\langle\hat{X}_{i}\rangle_{\rho(\bm{\theta})}\langle\hat{X}_{j}\rangle_{\rho(\bm{\theta})}\hskip 14.22636pt(i,j=1,\dots,K),

(86)

\rho(\bm{\theta})
being the output state of the whole sensor network. The
\hat{X}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,K
, are
K
hermitian operators which correspond to measurements performed on the output state
\rho(\bm{\theta})
. We choose
\hat{X}_{i}\equiv(\hat{J}_{z})_{i}=\left(\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{i}-\hat{b}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{i}\right)/2
, with
i=1,\dots,d
. In such case, the two matrices
\bm{G}
and
\bm{\Gamma}
can be evaluated through the
Q function
-based technique already illustrated in the previous section. This time, we need to take into account the complete transformation performed by the network on the input state, including the phase–encoding stage. Such transformation is described by the matrix

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{\dagger}=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\tilde{C}}&\bm{\tilde{S}}\\
-\bm{\tilde{S}}&\bm{\tilde{C}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{d}&0\\
0&\bm{A_{1}}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\tilde{C}}&\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{A_{1}}^{\dagger}\\
-\bm{\tilde{S}}&\bm{\tilde{C}}\bm{A_{1}}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix},

(87)

where
\bm{\tilde{C}}_{ij}=\cos{\left(\theta_{i}/2\right)}\delta_{ij}
and
\bm{\tilde{S}}_{ij}=\sin{\left(\theta_{i}/2\right)}\delta_{ij}
.
Let
|\Psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle
be the output state of our MZI sensor network. On the basis of Eq. (
86
) and having set
\hat{X}_{i}\equiv(\hat{J}_{z})_{i}
, we have

\bm{\Gamma}_{ij}=\langle\Psi(\bm{\theta})|(\hat{J}_{z})_{i}(\hat{J}_{z})_{j}|\Psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle-\langle\Psi(\bm{\theta})|(\hat{J}_{z})_{i}|\Psi(\bm{\theta}\rangle)\langle\Psi(\bm{\theta})|(\hat{J}_{z})_{j}|\Psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle\hskip 14.22636pt(i,j=1,\dots,d),

(88)

which can be rewritten as

\bm{\Gamma}_{ij}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\bm{h}_{i,j}+\bm{h}_{i+d,j+d}-\bm{h}_{i,j+d}-\bm{h}_{i+d,j}\right)\hskip 14.22636pt(i,j=1,\dots,d),

(89)

where
\bm{h}_{ij}=\langle\Psi(\bm{\theta})|\hat{n}_{i}\hat{n}_{j}|\Psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle-\langle\Psi(\bm{\theta})|\hat{n}_{i}|\Psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle\langle\Psi(\bm{\theta})|\hat{n}_{j}|\Psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle
. Notice that
\bm{h}_{ij}
can still be computed by means of Eq. (
68
), provided that the expression of matrix
\bm{A}^{\dagger}
is that given in Eq. (
87
). Matrices
\bm{C}
and
\bm{CD}
, on the other hand, being only related to the form of the input state, have the same expressions as in Eqs. from (
71
) to (
74
). So we get

\displaystyle\bm{E}=\bm{A}^{\dagger}\bm{C}\bm{A}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\tilde{C}}^{2}+\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{E_{1}}\bm{\tilde{S}}&-\left(\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{\tilde{C}}-\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{E_{1}}\bm{\tilde{C}}\right)\\
-\left(\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{\tilde{C}}-\bm{\tilde{C}}\bm{E_{1}}\bm{\tilde{S}}\right)&\bm{\tilde{S}}^{2}+\bm{\tilde{C}}\bm{E_{1}}\bm{\tilde{C}}\end{pmatrix}

(90)

and

\displaystyle\bm{EN}=

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{\dagger}\bm{CD}\bm{A}^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\bm{\tilde{S}}&\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\bm{\tilde{C}}\\
\bm{\tilde{C}}\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\bm{\tilde{S}}&\bm{\tilde{C}}\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\bm{\tilde{C}}\end{pmatrix}.

We also have

\displaystyle\bm{\gamma}=

\displaystyle\bm{b}^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\tilde{C}}\bm{\beta_{0}}^{*}\\
-\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{\beta_{0}}^{*}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\tilde{C}}\bm{\gamma_{0}}\\
-\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{\gamma_{0}}\end{pmatrix},

so that

\displaystyle\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{\dagger}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\tilde{C}}\bm{\phi_{-}}\bm{\tilde{C}}&-\bm{\tilde{C}}\bm{\phi_{-}}\bm{\tilde{S}}\\
-\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{\phi_{-}}\bm{\tilde{C}}&\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{\phi_{-}}\bm{\tilde{S}}\end{pmatrix},

(91)

and

\displaystyle\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\tilde{C}}\bm{\phi_{+}}\bm{\tilde{C}}&-\bm{\tilde{C}}\bm{\phi_{+}}\bm{\tilde{S}}\\
-\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{\phi_{+}}\bm{\tilde{C}}&\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{\phi_{+}}\bm{\tilde{S}}\end{pmatrix}.

At this point, we set
\theta_{i}=\pi/2
, a choice which is expected to lead to minimum estimation uncertainty [maximum slope condition, see Eq. (
93
) below].
We thus obtain the same matrices as in Eqs. (
75
)-(
79
), while, for what concerns Eq. (
76
), we find

\displaystyle-\bm{EN}\circ\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{T}=\frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix}-\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\circ\bm{\phi_{+}}&\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\circ\bm{\phi_{+}}\\
\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\circ\bm{\phi_{+}}&-\bm{E_{1}N_{1}}\circ\bm{\phi_{+}}\end{pmatrix},

which has opposite sign with respect to the corresponding matrix used in the QFIM case. As a consequence, we eventually obtain an expression for
\bm{\Gamma}
which is almost identical to the QFIM, the most notable difference being the swap of the real and imaginary part of the term
e^{i\chi_{i}}u_{i}
(compare with Eq. (
83
)):

\displaystyle\bm{\Gamma}_{ij}=\frac{1}{4}\left\{|\alpha_{i}||\alpha_{j}|\left[\Im\left(e^{i\chi_{i}}u_{i}\right)\Im\left(e^{i\chi_{j}}u_{j}\right)\left(e^{2r}-1\right)+\Re\left(e^{i\chi_{i}}u_{i}\right)\Re\left(e^{i\chi_{j}}u_{j}\right)\left(e^{-2r}-1\right)\right]+\left(|\alpha_{i}|^{2}+|u_{i}|^{2}\bar{n}_{s}\right)\delta_{ij}\right\}.

(92)

Consider now Eq. (
85
), rewritten for the specific choice
\hat{X}_{i}\equiv(\hat{J}_{z})_{i}
:

\displaystyle\bm{G}_{ij}=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\langle\Psi(\bm{\theta})|(\hat{J}_{z})_{i}|\Psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle}{\partial\theta_{j}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\langle\Psi(\bm{\theta})|(\hat{n}_{i}-\hat{n}_{i+d})|\Psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle}{\partial\theta_{i}}\delta_{ij},\hskip 14.22636pt(i=1,\dots,d).

(93)

A formula for the evaluation of
\langle\Psi(\bm{\theta})|\hat{n}_{i}|\Psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle
was derived in Eq. (
65
):

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(\bm{\theta})|\hat{n}_{i}|\Psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle=-1+\bm{E}_{ii}+\bm{\gamma}_{i}\bm{\gamma}_{i}^{*}\hskip 14.22636pt(i=1,\dots,2d).

The same information is conveniently condensed in the (diagonal) matrix

\displaystyle-\bm{I}_{d}+\left(\bm{E}+\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{\dagger}\right)\circ\bm{I}_{d},

whose diagonal elements are the
\langle\Psi(\bm{\theta})|\hat{n}_{i}|\Psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle
,
i=1,\dots,2d
.
Making use of Eqs. (
90
) and (
91
), one finds

\displaystyle-\bm{I}_{d}+\left(\bm{E}+\bm{\gamma}\bm{\gamma}^{\dagger}\right)\circ\bm{I}_{d}=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}-\bm{I}_{d}+\bm{\tilde{C}}^{2}+\left(\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{E_{1}}\bm{\tilde{S}}\right)\circ\bm{I}_{d}+\bm{\tilde{C}}\bm{\phi_{-}}\bm{\tilde{C}}&0\\
0&-\bm{I}_{d}+\bm{\tilde{S}}^{2}+\left(\bm{\tilde{C}}\bm{E_{1}}\bm{\tilde{C}}\right)\circ\bm{I}_{d}+\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{\phi_{-}}\bm{\tilde{S}}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}-\bm{I}_{d}+\bm{\tilde{C}}^{2}+\bm{\tilde{S}}^{2}\left(\bm{E_{1}}\circ\bm{I}_{d}\right)+\bm{\tilde{C}}\bm{\phi_{-}}\bm{\tilde{C}}&0\\
0&-\bm{I}_{d}+\bm{\tilde{S}}^{2}+\bm{\tilde{C}}^{2}\left(\bm{E_{1}}\circ\bm{I}_{d}\right)+\bm{\tilde{S}}\bm{\phi_{-}}\bm{\tilde{S}}\end{pmatrix},

where the general result
(\bm{Z}\bm{Y}\bm{Z})\circ\bm{I}_{d}=\bm{Z}^{2}(\bm{Y}\circ\bm{I}_{d})
has been used,
\bm{Z}
being a diagonal matrix. Note that, for
i=1,\dots,d
, the expectation value
\langle\Psi(\bm{\theta})|(\hat{n}_{i}-\hat{n}_{i+d})|\Psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle
corresponds to the difference between the two diagonal blocks of this matrix, so that, after simple algebraic manipulation, we get

\displaystyle\bm{G}_{ij}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\langle\Psi(\bm{\theta})|(\hat{n}_{i}-\hat{n}_{i+d})|\Psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle}{\partial\theta_{i}}\delta_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\sin\theta_{i}\left[\bm{E_{1}}\circ\bm{I}_{d}-\left(1+|\alpha_{i}|^{2}\right)\right]_{ii}\delta_{ij}.

We set again
\theta_{i}=\pi/2
, ending up with:

\displaystyle\bm{G}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left(|u_{i}|^{2}s^{2}-|\alpha_{i}|^{2}\right)\delta_{ij}.

(94)

At this point, if we aimed at obtaining the explicit expression of the moment matrix, we would still have to compute the inverse of matrix
\bm{\Gamma}
. On the other hand, we only need
\bm{G}^{-1}
to derive
\bm{M}^{-1}=(\bm{G}^{-1})^{T}\bm{\Gamma}\bm{G}^{-1}
, the advantage being that
\bm{G}
is diagonal and thus easily inverted. From Eqs. (
92
) and (
94
), one readily gets

\displaystyle\left(\bm{M}^{-1}\right)_{ij}=

\displaystyle|\alpha_{i}||\alpha_{j}|\frac{\Im{(e^{i\chi_{i}}u_{i})}}{|\alpha_{i}|^{2}-|u_{i}|^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}\frac{\Im{(e^{i\chi_{j}}u_{j})}}{|\alpha|_{j}^{2}-|u_{j}|^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}(e^{2r}-1)+|\alpha_{i}||\alpha_{j}|\frac{\Re{(e^{i\chi_{i}}u_{i})}}{|\alpha_{i}|^{2}-|u_{i}|^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}\frac{\Re{(e^{i\chi_{j}}u_{j})}}{|\alpha|_{j}^{2}-|u_{j}|^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}(e^{-2r}-1)+\frac{|\alpha_{i}|^{2}+|u_{i}|^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}{(|\alpha_{i}|^{2}-|u_{i}|^{2}\bar{n}_{s})^{2}}\delta_{ij},

(95)

where
\chi_{i}\equiv\phi_{i}-\varphi/2
and
s^{2}\equiv\sinh^{2}r=\bar{n}_{s}
.
Under the condition
\Im(e^{i\chi_{j}}u_{j})=0
, for
j=1,...,d
, one finally recovers Eq. (
5
).

VII.2
Demonstration of Eqs. (
11
) and (
12
)

First, let us recall the general expression of
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}
, obtained using Eq. (
5
):

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}=\bm{v}^{T}\bm{\mathcal{M}}(\tilde{\bm{u}},|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2},\bar{n}_{s})^{-1}\bm{v}

\displaystyle\quad=(e^{-2r}-1)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{|\alpha_{j}|\tilde{u}_{j}v_{j}}{|\alpha_{j}|^{2}-\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{|\alpha_{j}|^{2}+\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}{\left(|\alpha_{j}|^{2}-\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}\right)^{2}}v_{j}^{2}.

Also, as a general result, we notice that the minimization problem
\min_{\bm{U}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}
is characterized by a symmetry of a simple type: the value of the minimum is invariant under the transformation
v_{i}\to-v_{i}
performed on an arbitrary number of components of
\bm{v}
.
This can be proved as follows. Let
\bm{\tilde{u}}_{\bm{v}}
be the specific vector
\bm{\tilde{u}}
that minimizes
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}
. Inverting the sign of
v_{i}
for a certain
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
[
v_{i}^{\prime}=-v_{i}
] will leave the second term in the second line of Eq. (
VII.2
) unaltered, only the first term will change. However, it is easy to realize that the only effect of this transformation is to modify the minimum point of
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}
, which is now
\bm{\tilde{u}}_{\bm{v^{\prime}}}
with
(\bm{\tilde{u}}_{\bm{v^{\prime}}})_{i}=-(\bm{\tilde{u}}_{\bm{v}})_{i}
, while the minimum itself will remain the same as for
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}
. Also, this argument can be immediately extended to a transformation that inverts the sign of an arbitrary number of components of
\bm{v}
.
The same symmetry is easily seen to apply also to
\min_{r_{1}^{\prime},\dots,r_{d}^{\prime}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}
, the sequential strategy case.

The lower bound (
11
) is obtained by noticing that

\min_{\bm{U},|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}\geq\min_{\bm{U},|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}_{j}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}

where we have indicated
\bm{v}_{j}
as the vector with elements
v_{j}=1/\sqrt{d}
and
v_{i\neq j}=0
and corresponds to the estimation of a single phase shift
\bm{v}_{j}\cdot\bm{\theta}=\theta_{j}/\sqrt{d}
.
This inequality is supported by numerical evidence, see Fig.
3
.
Specifically, from Eq. (
VII.2
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}_{j}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(e^{-2r}-1)|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}}{d(|\alpha_{j}|^{2}-\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s})^{2}}+\frac{|\alpha_{j}|^{2}+\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}{d(|\alpha_{j}|^{2}-\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s})^{2}}

(97)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{(e^{-2r}-1)\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}+1}{d|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}+\frac{\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}{d|\alpha_{j}|^{4}},

where the approximate expression in the second line is obtained for
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\gg\bar{n}_{s}
.
Rewriting the numerator of Eq. (
97
) as
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}+[\bar{n}_{s}-(1-e^{-2r})|\alpha_{j}|^{2}]\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}
, we see that the minimum in the interval
0\leqslant\tilde{u}^{2}_{j}\leqslant 1
is clearly achieved for
\tilde{u}^{2}_{j}=1
if the term between square brackets is negative, namely
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}>\bar{n}_{s}/(1-e^{-2r})
, or simply
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}>\bar{n}_{s}
for
r\gg 1
.
Moreover, the minimum of Eq. (
97
) with respect to
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}
is clearly obtained by setting
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}
to its maximum value, which corresponds to
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}=d\bar{n}_{c}
. Thus, we have

\min_{\bm{U},|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}_{j}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}=\frac{e^{-2r}}{d^{2}\bar{n}_{c}}+\frac{\bar{n}_{s}}{d^{3}\bar{n}_{c}^{2}}.

We recall that the above equation has been derived under the condition
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}=d\bar{n}_{c}\gg\bar{n}_{s}
that implies
\bar{n}_{T}=d\bar{n}_{c}+\bar{n}_{s}\approx d\bar{n}_{c}
and this
\bar{n}_{T}\gg\bar{n}_{s}
.
We thus recover Eq. (
11
).

The upper bound (
12
) is obtained for the sub-optimal conditions i)
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}=\bar{n}_{c}
for all
j
, where
\bar{n}_{c}=(\bar{n}_{T}-\bar{n}_{s})/d
, and ii)
\bm{\tilde{u}}=\bm{v}\sqrt{d}
.
The bound reads

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{U},|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}\leq\bm{v}^{T}\bm{\mathcal{M}}(\bm{v}\sqrt{d},\bar{n}_{c},\bar{n}_{s})^{-1}\bm{v}

\displaystyle=d\bar{n}_{c}(e^{-2r}-1)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{\bar{n}_{c}-dv_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\bar{n}_{c}+dv_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}{(\bar{n}_{c}-dv_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s})^{2}}v_{j}^{2}.

Taking into account the normalization
\sum_{j=1}^{d}v_{j}^{2}=1/d
, we have
dv_{j}^{2}\leq 1
for all
j
.
Therefore,

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{\bar{n}_{c}-dv_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{d^{2}(\bar{n}_{c}-\bar{n}_{s})^{2}}

and

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\bar{n}_{c}+dv_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}{(\bar{n}_{c}-dv_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s})^{2}}v_{j}^{2}\leq\frac{d\bar{n}_{c}+\mathcal{W}\bar{n}_{s}}{d^{2}(\bar{n}_{c}-\bar{n}_{s})^{2}},

where
\mathcal{W}=d^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{d}v_{j}^{4}
.
Notice that
\mathcal{W}\geq 1
, with
\mathcal{W}=1
for
v_{j}=\pm 1/d
for all
j
.
Combining the above equations and using
d\bar{n}_{c}=\bar{n}_{T}-\bar{n}_{s}
,
we find

\min_{\bm{U},|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}\leq\frac{(\bar{n}_{T}-\bar{n}_{s})e^{-2r}+\bar{n}_{s}\mathcal{W}}{[\bar{n}_{T}-(d+1)\bar{n}_{s}]^{2}}.

Finally, we recover Eq. (
12
) under the condition
\bar{n}_{T}\gg(d+1)\bar{n}_{s}
.

VII.3
Demonstration of Eqs. (
16
) and (
17
)

The lower bound to
\min_{\bm{U},|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}
is obtained by first using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}=\bm{v}^{T}\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}}^{-1}\bm{v}\geq\frac{|\bm{v}|^{4}}{(\bm{v}^{T}\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}}\bm{v})}.

(98)

From Eq. (
6
) we then have

\bm{v}^{T}\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}}\bm{v}=(e^{2r}-1)\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j}|\tilde{u}_{j}v_{j}\bigg)^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}v_{j}^{2}+\bar{n}_{s}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}v_{j}^{2}.

(99)

We have

\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j}|\tilde{u}_{j}v_{j}\bigg)^{2}\leq\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}v_{j}^{2}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}\bigg)\leq\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}{d},

where the first inequality is due to Cauchy-Schwarz and the second is a consequence of
\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}\leq 1
and
\sum_{j=1}^{d}v_{j}^{2}=1/d
.
Using
v_{j}^{2}\leq 1/d
, we also have

\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}v_{j}^{2}\leq\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}{d},

and

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}v_{j}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{d}.

Combining Eq. (
99
) with the above inequalities gives

\bm{v}^{T}\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}}\bm{v}\leq\frac{e^{2r}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}+\bar{n}_{s}}{d}.

Taking into account that
\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}=\bar{n}_{T}-\bar{n}_{s}
and
|\bm{v}|^{4}=1/d^{2}
, from Eq. (
98
), we obtain

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}\geq\frac{1}{d[\bar{n}_{T}e^{2r}-\bar{n}_{s}(e^{2r}-1)]}.

The lower bound is valid for all
\bm{U}
and all
|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2}
and thus also for the optimal configuration,

\min_{\bm{U},\,|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}\geq\frac{1}{d[\bar{n}_{T}e^{2r}-\bar{n}_{s}(e^{2r}-1)]},

(100)

which corresponds to the lower bound (
16
).

To derive Eq. (
17
), we first use the Sherman-Morrison formula to invert Eq. (
6
):

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}}^{-1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\alpha_{i}|^{2}+\tilde{u}_{i}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(e^{2r}-1\right)}{1+\mathcal{K}\left(e^{2r}-1\right)}\frac{|\alpha_{i}||\alpha_{j}|\tilde{u}_{i}\tilde{u}_{j}}{\left(|\alpha_{i}|^{2}+\tilde{u}_{i}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}\right)\left(|\alpha_{j}|^{2}+\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}\right)},

(101)

where
\mathcal{K}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}}{|\alpha_{j}|^{2}+\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}
.
From Eq. (
VII.3
), it is possible to derive the general expression for
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}
:

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}=\bm{v}^{T}\bm{\mathcal{F}_{\rm Q}}(\bm{\tilde{u}},|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...,|\alpha_{d}|^{2},\bar{n}_{s})^{-1}\bm{v}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{|\alpha_{j}|^{2}+\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}-\frac{\left(e^{2r}-1\right)}{1+\mathcal{K}\left(e^{2r}-1\right)}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{|\alpha_{j}|\tilde{u}_{j}v_{j}}{|\alpha_{j}|^{2}+\tilde{u}_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}\right)^{2}.

Similar to the derivation of Eq. (
12
), the upper bound (
17
) is obtained by a specific configuration of the sensor network that simplifies the above equation: i)
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}=\bar{n}_{c}
for all
j
, where
\bar{n}_{c}=(\bar{n}_{T}-\bar{n}_{s})/d
, and ii)
\bm{\tilde{u}}=\bm{v}\sqrt{d}
.
In this case, we obtain

\min_{\bm{U},\,|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}\leq\frac{\mathcal{K}^{\prime}}{1+d\bar{n}_{c}(e^{2r}-1)\mathcal{K}^{\prime}}.

(103)

with
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{\bar{n}_{c}+dv_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}}
.
To derive an upper bound that is independent from
\bm{v}
, we notice that
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}/(1+x\mathcal{K}^{\prime})
is a monotonic growing function of
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
, implying that
f(\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime})<f(\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\prime})
if
\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime}<\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\prime}
.
We have
\bar{n}_{c}+dv_{j}^{2}\bar{n}_{s}\geq\bar{n}_{c}
and thus
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\leq 1/(d\bar{n}_{c})
.
Therefore,

\min_{\bm{U},\,|\alpha_{1}|^{2},...|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}\leq\frac{1}{d\bar{n}_{c}e^{2r}}.

(104)

We recover the upper bound (
17
) when noticing that
d\bar{n}_{c}=\bar{n}_{T}-\bar{n}_{s}
.

VII.4
Demonstration of Eqs. (
20
) and (
21
)

Let us consider Eq. (
VII.2
) for
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}=\bar{n}_{c}
, for all
j
.
In the regime
\bar{n}_{c}\gg\bar{n}_{s}
[or, equivalently,
\bar{n}_{T}=d\bar{n}_{c}+\bar{n}_{s}\gg(d+1)\bar{n}_{s}
], we find

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}=\frac{e^{-2r}-1}{\bar{n}_{c}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\tilde{u}_{i}v_{i}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{d\bar{n}_{c}}+\frac{\bar{n}_{s}}{\bar{n}_{c}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\tilde{u}_{i}^{2}v_{i}^{2}.

(105)

We now show that the choice
\bm{\tilde{u}}=\sqrt{d}\bm{v}
is optimal for the estimation of the generalized average phase
\bm{v}_{\rm ave}\cdot\bm{\theta}
, thus proving Eq. (
20
).

Let us denote Eq. (
105
) with
f(\bm{\tilde{u}})
, as a function of
\bm{\tilde{u}}
.
The goal here is to minimize
f(\bm{\tilde{u}})
with respect to
\bm{\tilde{u}}
under the normalization condition
g(\bm{\tilde{u}})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\tilde{u}_{i}^{2}-1=0
.
This problem can be solved through the method of Lagrange multipliers.
Let
\lambda
denote the Lagrange multiplier, the computation of the partial derivative with respect to
\tilde{u}_{i}
of the Lagrangian function
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\tilde{u}},\lambda)=f(\bm{\tilde{u}})-\lambda g(\bm{\tilde{u}})
gives

\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{u}_{i}}\mathcal{L}(\bm{\tilde{u}},\lambda)=\frac{e^{-2r}-1}{\bar{n}_{c}}2\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{u}_{j}v_{j}\right)v_{i}+2\frac{\bar{n}_{s}}{\bar{n}_{c}^{2}}\tilde{u}_{i}v_{i}^{2}-2\lambda\tilde{u}_{i}.

(106)

The constrained minimum problem is solved by the pair
(\bm{\tilde{u}},\lambda)
which satisfies
(\partial/\partial\tilde{u}_{i})\mathcal{L}(\bm{\tilde{u}},\lambda)=0
and
g(\bm{\tilde{u}})=0
at the same time. Note that
\bm{\tilde{u}}=\sqrt{d}\bm{v}
satisfies the constraint
g(\bm{\tilde{u}})=0
. Therefore, the choice
\bm{\tilde{u}}=\sqrt{d}\bm{v}
is optimal if and only if a value of
\lambda
can be found such that
(\partial/\partial\tilde{u}_{i})\mathcal{L}\left(\bm{\tilde{u}},\lambda\right)|_{\bm{\tilde{u}}=\sqrt{d}\bm{v}}=0
.
We thus search for the unique value of
\lambda
that simultaneously solves all the equations in the following set:

\frac{e^{-2r}-1}{\bar{n}_{c}}+2d\frac{\bar{n}_{s}}{\bar{n}_{c}^{2}}v_{i}^{2}-2d\lambda=0,

(107)

with
i
such that
v_{i}\neq 0
, since we have divided by
v_{i}\neq 0
– if
v_{i}=0
, the
i
-th equation is identically satisfied. If such
\lambda
actually exists, by subtracting consecutive pairs of equations contained in (
107
), we will end up with

2d\frac{\bar{n}_{s}}{\bar{n}_{c}^{2}}\left(v_{i}^{2}-v_{i+1}^{2}\right)=0,

(108)

where
v_{i},v_{i+1}\neq 0
.
On the other hand, if the above conditions are met, we can immediately determine the sought value of
\lambda
by solving any of the [linear] equations in (
107
). Therefore, Eq. (
108
) contains necessary and sufficient conditions for
\bm{\tilde{u}}=\sqrt{d}\bm{v}
to give the optimal QC, which can be fulfilled in two different ways:

\frac{{\bar{n}}_{s}}{\bar{n}_{c}^{2}}=0,\qquad{\rm or}\qquad v_{i}^{2}-v_{i+1}^{2}=0,

with
v_{i},v_{i+1}\neq 0
.
The first alternative may be interpreted as referring to the situation when the last term in Eq. (
105
) is so small with respect to the rest that it can be neglected.
If that is the case, the system in (
107
) admits one solution
\lambda
for every value of
\bm{v}
, implying that
\bm{\tilde{u}}=\sqrt{d}\bm{v}
must be optimal in the estimation of any linear combination of phases.
On the other hand, if such term cannot be neglected, we need that
v_{i}^{2}-v_{i+1}^{2}=0
for all
v_{i},v_{i+1}\neq 0
, namely, that all the non-vanishing components of
\bm{v}
are equal in modulus. As anticipated, this condition identifies the generalized average phase estimation problem, with an arbitrary number of modes between
1
and
d
. Evaluated at its minimum point and for
\bm{v}=\bm{v}_{\rm ave}
, Eq. (
105
) becomes

\min_{\bm{U},\,|\alpha_{1}|^{2},\dots,|\alpha_{d}|^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}_{\rm ave}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}=\frac{e^{-2r}\bar{n}_{c}+\bar{n}_{s}/d}{d\bar{n}_{c}^{2}}.

(109)

Finally, we prove Eq. (
21
) for
\bar{n}_{c}\gg\bar{n}_{s}
[equivalent to
\bar{n}_{T}\gg(d+1)\bar{n}_{s}
].
Setting
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}=\bar{n}_{c}
for all
j
in Eq. (
VII.3
) and taking the limit
\bar{n}_{c}\gg\bar{n}_{s}
, one gets

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}=\frac{e^{-2r}-1}{\bar{n}_{c}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\tilde{u}_{i}v_{i}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{d\bar{n}_{c}}.

(110)

This is just Eq. (
105
) lacking the last term. As already discussed above, the function in Eq. (
110
) is minimized by choosing
\bm{\tilde{u}}=\sqrt{d}\bm{v}
, which corresponds to the condition used to get the upper bound. The saturation of the bound is only realized for
\bm{v}=\bm{v}_{\rm ave}
since only in that case an even distribution of photons between the coherent modes,
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}=\bar{n}_{c}
for all
j
, can be used to attain the minimum of
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm eQCR}
.
The right hand side of Eq. (
21
) is obtained, for any value of
\bar{n}_{T}
, assuming that conditions
\bm{\tilde{u}}=\sqrt{d}\bm{v}_{\rm ave}
and
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}
for all
j
identify the optimal configuration of the sensor network, for any
\bar{n}_{T}
.
These assumptions are confirmed by the results of numerical simulations shown in Fig.
2

VII.5
Demonstration of Eq. (
26
)

To derive Eq. (
26
), we refer to Eq. (
25
) which gives the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{2}(\bm{v})
. We work in the regime
\bar{n}_{c}\gg e^{2r}\bar{n}_{s}
, which implies also
\bar{n}_{c}\gg e^{2r^{\prime}_{i}}(\bar{n}^{\prime}_{s})_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,d
since, under the constraint
\mathcal{C}_{2}
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\bar{n}^{\prime}_{s})_{i}=\bar{n}_{s}
. The sensitivity of the entangled strategy,
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}
, can be obtained from Eq. (
105
) by neglecting the last term, which needs not to be considered in such regime. The expression of
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}
, the sensitivity of the separable strategy, is derived from Eq. (
7
). In the limit considered, we only keep the first term both in the numerator and in the denominator of the inverse moment matrix, getting

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}=\bm{v}^{T}\bm{\mathcal{M}^{-1}}\bm{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{e^{-2r^{\prime}_{i}}}{\bar{n}_{c}}v_{i}^{2}.

(111)

We then optimize the sensitivities of both strategies. In the previous section we proved that, when the rightmost term in Eq. (
105
) is negligible, the entangled strategy is optimized by the choice
\bm{\tilde{u}}=\sqrt{d}\bm{v}
, with

\min_{\bm{U}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}=\frac{e^{-2r}}{d\bar{n}_{c}}.

(112)

In order to make the minimization of Eq. (
111
) as simple to perform, we need an extra condition that allows us to express
e^{2r^{\prime}_{i}}
as a simple function of
(\bar{n}^{\prime}_{s})_{i}=\sinh^{2}r^{\prime}_{i}
. As a first case, we assume
r^{\prime}_{i}\gg 1
, so that
e^{2r^{\prime}_{i}}\approx 4(\bar{n}^{\prime}_{s})_{i}
. The method of Lagrange multipliers is very easily applied to this case, predicting the minimum

\min_{r^{\prime}_{1},...,r^{\prime}_{d}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}=\frac{e^{-2r}}{\bar{n}_{c}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|v_{i}|\right)^{2},

(113)

which is achieved for
(\bar{n}^{\prime}_{s})_{i}=\bar{n}_{s}|v_{i}|/\sum_{i=1}^{d}|v_{i}|
. We also find
\lambda=-(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|v_{i}|)^{2}/4\bar{n}_{s}^{2}
,
\lambda
denoting the Lagrange multiplier associated with this optimization problem. It is easy to see that the ratio between the two sensitivities corresponds to Eq. (
26
). Notice that, for
v_{i}\to 0
, one has
(\bar{n}^{\prime}_{s})_{i}\to 0
too, so that the approximation
r^{\prime}_{i}\gg 1
cannot be valid in such limit. We thus consider a second case, the limit
r^{\prime}_{i}\ll 1
, which justifies the truncated series expansion
e^{-2r^{\prime}_{i}}\approx 1-2r^{\prime}_{i}\approx 1-2\sqrt{(\bar{n}^{\prime}_{s})_{i}}
. Applying again the method of Lagrange multipliers, this time we find

\min_{r^{\prime}_{1},...,r^{\prime}_{d}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}=\frac{1}{d\bar{n}_{c}}-2\frac{\sqrt{\bar{n}_{s}}}{\bar{n}_{c}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}v_{i}^{4}}

(114)

and
(\bar{n}^{\prime}_{s})_{i}=\bar{n}_{s}v_{i}^{4}/\sum_{i=1}^{d}v_{i}^{4}
, with
\lambda=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}v_{i}^{4}}/\sqrt{\bar{n}_{s}}
.
The expression of the gain is easily verified to be

\mathcal{G}_{2}(\bm{v})=\left(1-2d\sqrt{\bar{n}_{s}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}v_{i}^{4}}\right)e^{2r}.

(115)

In principle, both of the formulas for
\mathcal{G}_{2}(\bm{v})
, Eqs. (
26
) and (
115
), are not expected to provide accurate predictions for non-uniform vectors
\bm{v}
, when
r^{\prime}_{i}\gg 1
and
r^{\prime}_{i}\ll 1
occur simultaneously, for different components
v_{i}
of the same
\bm{v}
.
So, it is interesting to note that Eqs. (
26
) and (
115
) still give the correct value of the gain,
\mathcal{G}_{2}(\bm{v})=1
, when applied to the estimation of a single phase.
Equation (
26
) is found in good agreement with numerical results for all values of
\bm{v}
.

VII.6
Demonstration of Eqs. (
28
) and (
29
)

We demonstrate here Eq. (
29
), which refers to the gain
\mathcal{G}_{2}(\bm{v}_{\rm ave})
achieved in the estimation of the generalized average phase. Starting again from Eq. (
7
), with
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}=\bar{n}_{c}
, this time we can impose the less strict condition
\bar{n}_{c}\gg\bar{n}_{s},(\bar{n}_{s}^{\prime})_{i}
. As a consequence, we are only allowed to neglect the second term in the denominator of the inverse moment matrix, ending up with

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}=\bm{v}^{T}\bm{\mathcal{M}^{-1}}\bm{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\bar{n}_{c}e^{-2r^{\prime}_{i}}+(\bar{n}_{s}^{\prime})_{i}}{\bar{n}_{c}^{2}}v_{i}^{2}.

(116)

Making use of the general relation
e^{2r_{i}^{\prime}}=1+2(\bar{n}_{s}^{\prime})_{i}+\sqrt{[1+2(\bar{n}_{s}^{\prime})_{i}]^{2}-1}
, with
(\bar{n}_{s}^{\prime})_{i}=\sinh^{2}r_{i}^{\prime}
, which holds for any value of
r^{\prime}_{i}
, and setting
\bm{v}=\bm{v}_{\rm ave}
, or
v_{i}^{2}=1/d^{2}
, we get

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}_{\rm ave}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}=\frac{1}{d^{2}\bar{n}_{c}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\bar{n}_{c}}{X_{i}+\sqrt{X^{2}_{i}-1}}+\frac{X_{i}-1}{2}.

(117)

Here, we have set
X_{i}\equiv 1+2(\bar{n}_{s}^{\prime})_{i}
for convenience. The function above has to be minimized with respect to the set of variables
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,d}
under the constraint
\sum_{i=1}^{d}X_{i}=X
, which comes from the original constraint
\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\bar{n}^{\prime}_{s})_{i}=\bar{n}_{s}
. Notice that
X=d+2\bar{n}_{s}
. Relying again on the method of Lagrange multipliers, we are able to show that the minimum point is
X_{i}=X/d
, which corresponds, as expected, to splitting
\bar{n}_{s}
evenly between the
d
MZIs used in the separable strategy:
(\bar{n}_{s}^{\prime})_{i}=\bar{n}_{s}/d
. In this case, we find the following value for the Lagrange multiplier:
\lambda=1/(d^{2}\bar{n}_{c}^{2})[\bar{n}_{c}(1-X/\sqrt{X^{2}-d^{2}})+1/2]
. Evaluated at its minimum point and for
\bm{v}=\bm{v}_{\rm ave}
, Eq. (
116
) becomes

\min_{r^{\prime}_{1},...,r^{\prime}_{d}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}_{\rm ave}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm smom}=\frac{\bar{n}_{c}e^{-2r^{\prime}}+\bar{n}_{s}/d}{d\bar{n}_{c}^{2}},

(118)

with
r^{\prime}={\rm arcsinh}\sqrt{\bar{n}_{s}/d}
. Taking the ratio with Eq. (
27
), which expresses the optimal sensitivity of the entangled strategy, we find Eq. (
29
).

VII.8
Demonstration of Eqs. (
38
) and (
39
)

Let us demonstrate Eq. (
38
): the demonstration of Eq. (
39
) is analogous.
Equation (
38
) is a direct consequence of

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}=\bm{v}^{T}\bm{\mathcal{M}}^{-1}\bm{v}\geq|\bm{v}|^{4}/\bm{v}^{T}\bm{\mathcal{M}}\bm{v},

(121)

\bm{v}^{T}\bm{\mathcal{M}}\bm{v}\leq\bm{v}_{\mu_{\rm max}}^{T}\bm{\mathcal{M}}\bm{v}_{\mu_{\rm max}}=\mu_{\rm max}
and
|\bm{v}|^{2}=1/d
.
These imply the bound

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm emom}\geq 1/(\mu_{\rm max}d)
, which is saturable for
\bm{v}/|\bm{v}|=\bm{v}_{\mu_{\rm max}}
.
The inequality (
121
) follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
(\bm{f}^{T}\bm{f})(\bm{g}^{T}\bm{g})\geq(\bm{f}^{T}\bm{g})^{2}
with
\bm{f}=\bm{\mathcal{M}}^{1/2}\bm{v}
and

\bm{g}=\bm{\mathcal{M}}^{-1/2}\bm{v}
and is saturated if and only if
\bm{f}=\lambda\bm{g}
for some real number
\lambda
, namely
if and only if
\bm{v}
is an eigenvector of
\bm{\mathcal{M}}
.
Note that
\bm{\mathcal{M}}^{1/2}
is well definite since
\bm{\mathcal{M}}\geq 0
:
this follows from
\bm{\mathcal{M}}=\bm{C}^{T}\bm{\Gamma}^{-1}\bm{C}
,

\bm{\Gamma}\geq 0
being a covariance matrix, and
\bm{\Gamma}^{-1}\geq 0
being the inverse of a positive semidefinite matrix.

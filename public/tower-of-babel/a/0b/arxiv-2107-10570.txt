On the ranks and implicit constant fields of valuations induced by pseudo monotone sequences
Arpan Dutta

Abstract
. Given a valued field ( K , v ) (K,v) and a pseudo monotone sequence E E in ( K , v ) (K,v) , one has an induced valuation v E v_{E} extending v v to K ⁡ ( X ) K(X) . After fixing an extension of v E v_{E} to a fixed algebraic closure K ⁡ ( X ) ¯ \overline{K(X)} of K ⁡ ( X ) K(X) , we show that the implicit constant field of the extension ( K ⁡ ( X ) | K , v E ) (K(X)|K,v_{E}) is simply the henselization of ( K , v ) (K,v) . We consider the question: given a value transcendental extension w w of v v to K ⁡ ( X ) K(X) and a pseudo monotone sequence E E in ( K , v ) (K,v) , under which precise conditions is w w induced by E E ? The dual nature of pseudo convergent sequences of algebraic type and pseudo divergent sequences is also explored. Further, we provide a complete description of the various possibilities of the rank of the valuation v E v_{E} , provided that v v has finite rank.

1.
Introduction

A central problem in valuation theory is to study the set of all extensions of a valuation
v
from a field
K
to a rational function field
K(X)
. On the other hand, given such an extension
w
of
v
to
K(X)
, it is important to give a complete description of the valuation
w
. Several different objects have been introduced to tackle these interconnected problems. Minimal pairs of definition were introduced by Alexandru, Popescu and Zaharescu [
References
,
References
] to study residue transcendental extensions. The notion of key polynomials was introduced by MacLane [
References
] which was later generalized by Vaquié [
References
]. An alternative form of key polynomials have been recently developed by Spivakovsky and Novacoski [
References
,
References
]. Ostrowski [
References
] introduced pseudo convergent sequences which were succesfully used by Kaplansky [
References
] to answer maximality questions for immediate extensions. Recently, this notion has been generalized to that of pseudo monotone sequences which has found significant use in the works of Peruginelli and Spirito [
References
]. The notion of implicit constant fields has been introduced by Kuhlmann [
References
].

Our goal in this article is to study the extensions of a valued field
(K,v)
induced by pseudo monotone sequences in
(K,v)
. In particular, given an extension
v_{E}
of
v
to
K(X)
induced by a pseudo monotone sequence
E
in
(K,v)
and an extension of
v_{E}
to a fixed algebraic closure
\overline{K(X)}
of
K(X)
, we investigate the following:

•

the implicit constant field of the extension
(K(X)|K,v_{E})
,

•

the rank of the value group
v_{E}K(X)
.

It has recently been observed by Peruginelli and Spirito [
References
] that every extension of a valuation
v
from
K
to
K(X)
is induced by a pseudo monotone sequence in
(K,v)
if and only if the
v
-adic completion
\hat{K}
is algebraically closed. As a consequence, our results hold for all extensions
(K(X)|K,w)
whenever
\hat{K}
is algebraically closed.

Given a valued field extension
(K(X)|K,w)
and an extension of
w
to
\overline{K(X)}
, the
implicit constant field
of the extension is defined to be the relative algebraic closure of
K
in the henselization of
K(X)
and it is denoted by
IC(K(X)|K,w)
. In particular,
IC(K(X)|K,w)
is a separable-algebraic extension of the henselization
K^{h}
of
(K,v)
. It has been observed in [
References
, Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.3] that explicit computation of
IC(K(X)|K,w)
can be quite difficult in general. In this article we discover that

IC(K(X)|K,v_{E})=K^{h}

whenever
E
is a pseudo monotone sequence in
(K,v)
(see Theorem
6.2
and Theorem
7.1
).

The extension
(K(X)|K,v_{E})
is value transcendental if and only if
E
is either pseudo convergent of algebraic type or pseudo divergent. We consider the question:

Question 1.1
.

Given a value transcendental extension
(K(X)|K,w)
and a pseudo monotone sequence
E
in
(K,v)
, under which precise conditions is
w=v_{E}
?

Some related equivalences for this equality to hold are provided in Theorem
7.2
and Theorem
8.1
. In particular, we observe the strong connections between pairs of definition for
v_{E}
over
K
and the limits of
E
, which play a crucial role throughout this paper. The duality of pseudo convergent sequences of algebraic type and pseudo divergent sequences is made clear in Theorem
7.2
(iii)
and Theorem
8.1
(iii)
. In the corollary to Theorem
7.2
we observe that if
E
is a Cauchy sequence and
(K(X)|K,w)
is a value transcendental extension such that
X
is a limit of
E
, then
w=v_{E}
. Similarly, if
E
diverges to infinity and
X
is not a limit of
E
, then
w=v_{E}
.

We also provide a complete characterization of the rank of the value group
v_{E}K(X)
, provided that
vK
is of finite rank. It is well known that the rank either remains same or increases by one. We give a constructive description of the various possibilities (see Figures
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
). Since the value group remains unchanged when
E
is a pseudo convergent sequence of transcendental type or a pseudo constant sequence, we only focus on the cases when
E
is pseudo convergent of algebraic type or pseudo divergent. The duality observed in Theorem
7.2
(iii)
and Theorem
8.1
(iii)
comes to the fore at this juncture.

After fixing an extension
\overline{v}
of
v
to
\overline{K}
, we introduce the notion of supremum and infimum of a pseudo convergent sequence of algebraic type or a pseudo divergent sequence, when
(K,v)
has finite rank. Thereafter, we observe in Theorem
9.3
that whenever
E
is a Cauchy sequence or a pseudo convergent sequence of algebraic type such that the supremum of
E
is contained in
\overline{v}\overline{K}
, we obtain
\mbox{\rm rk}\,v_{E}K(X)=\mbox{\rm rk}\,vK+1
. The analogous result holds true when
E
diverges to infinity or
E
is pseudo divergent such that the infimum of
E
is contained in
\overline{v}\overline{K}
.

The paper is structured as follows: in Section
2
we give a quick tour of the theories of minimal pairs of definition and key polynomials. The definitions of pseudo monotone sequences and their limits are provided in Section
3
. In Section
4
we define valuations induced by pseudo monotone sequences and study some of their basic properties. Section
5
deals with the extensions of
v_{E}
to
\overline{K}(X)
. The definition of pure extensions is provided in Section
6
, where we also observe that
(K(X)|K,v_{E})
is a pure extension whenever
E
is not a pseudo convergent sequence without a limit in
K
. The remaining case is considered in Section
7
. Question
1.1
is also investigated in this section, under the assumption that
E
is pseudo convergent of algebraic type. The extension
(K(X)|K,v_{E})
when
E
is pseudo divergent is studied in greater detail in Section
8
. Finally, we define the valuation theoretic concept of rank and compute the rank of the valuation
v_{E}
in Section
9
.

Notations

A valued field
(K,v)
is a field
K
endowed with a valuation
v
. The value group of
K
is denoted by
vK
and the residue field by
Kv
. The value of an element
a\in K
is denoted by
va
. By an extension of valued fields
(L|K,v)
we will mean that
L|K
is an extension of fields,
v
is a valuation on
L
and
K
is equipped with the restricted valuation. If
L
and
K
are subfields of a larger valued field
(F,v)
, then we will also mean that both
L
and
K
are equipped with the restricted valuations.

2.
Minimal pairs and key polynomials

A valued field extension
(K(X)|K,v)
satisfies the famous Abhyankar inequality:

\mbox{\rm rat rk}\,\frac{vK(X)}{vK}+\mbox{\rm trdeg}\,[K(X)v:Kv]\leq 1,

where
\mbox{\rm rat rk}\,vK(X)/vK
is the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-dimension of the divisible hull
{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(vK(X)/vK)
. This inequality is a consequence of [
References
, Chapter VI, §10.3, Theorem 1]. The extension
(K(X)|K,v)
is said to be
valuation transcendental
if equality holds in the above inequality. The extension is said to be
value transcendental
if
\mbox{\rm rat rk}\,vK(X)/vK=1
and is said to be
residue transcendental
if
\mbox{\rm trdeg}\,[K(X)v:Kv]=1
.

Take a polynomial
f(X)\in K[X]
and
a\in K
. Write
f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}(X-a)^{i}
where
c_{i}\in K
. Take an element
\alpha
in some ordered abelian group extending
vK
. We define the map
v_{a,\alpha}:K[X]\longrightarrow vK+{\mathbb{Z}}\alpha
by setting
v_{a,\alpha}f:=\min\{vc_{i}+i\alpha\}
and extend it canonically to
K(X)
. Then
v_{a,\alpha}
is a valuation transcendental extension of
v
from
K
to
K(X)
[
References
, Lemma 3.10]. Further,
v_{a,\alpha}
is value transcendental if and only if
\alpha\notin{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}vK
[
References
, Lemma 3.10]. The valuation
v_{a,\alpha}
is said to be a
monomial valuation
. It is known that the extension
(K(X)|K,v)
is value (residue) transcendental if and only if
(\overline{K}(X)|\overline{K},\overline{v})
is also value (residue) transcendental [
References
, Lemma 3.3], where
\overline{v}
is some extension of
v
to
\overline{K}(X)
. Further,
(\overline{K}(X)|\overline{K},\overline{v})
is valuation transcendental if and only if the valuation
\overline{v}
on
\overline{K}(X)
is a monomial valuation [
References
, Theorem 3.11]. If we have
\overline{v}=\overline{v}_{a,\alpha}
on
\overline{K}(X)
for some
a\in\overline{K}
and
\alpha\in\overline{v}\overline{K}(X)
, then we say that
(a,\alpha)
is a
pair of definition for
v
over
K
. It can be easily checked that
(a,\alpha)
is a pair of definition for
v
over
K
if and only if

\alpha=\overline{v}(X-a)=\max\overline{v}(X-\overline{K}),

where
\overline{v}(X-\overline{K}):=\{\overline{v}(X-c)\mid c\in\overline{K}\}
(see [
References
, Section 2] for a proof). A valuation transcendental extension can have multiple pairs of definition. The following result, proved in [
References
, Proposition 3], gives us a relation between the different pairs of definition:

Lemma 2.1
.

Take
a,a^{\prime}
in
\overline{K}
and
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}
in some ordered abelian group containing
\overline{v}\overline{K}
. Then,

\overline{v}_{a,\alpha}=\overline{v}_{a^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}}\text{ if and only if }\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}\text{ and }\overline{v}(a-a^{\prime})\geq\alpha.

Definition 2.2
.

A pair
(a,\alpha)\in\overline{K}\times\overline{v}\overline{K}(X)
is said to be a
minimal pair of definition for
v
over
K
if the following conditions are satisifed:

(MP1)
\alpha=\overline{v}(X-a)=\max\overline{v}(X-\overline{K})
,

(MP2)
\overline{v}(a-b)\geq\alpha\Longrightarrow[K(b):K]\geq[K(a):K]
for all
b\in\overline{K}
.

Take a polynomial
f(X)\in K[X]
. We define,

\delta(f):=\max\{\overline{v}(X-c)\mid c\text{ is a root of }f\}.

It has been observed in [
References
] that
\delta(f)
is independent of the choice of the extension of
v
to
\overline{K}(X)
. A monic polynomial
Q(X)\in K[X]
is said to be a
key polynomial for
v
over
K
if
\delta(f)<\delta(Q)
for all
f(X)\in K[X]
such that
\deg f<\deg Q
. Observe that a monic linear polynomial is a key polynomial by definition.

Given a polynomial
f(X)\in K[X]
and a monic polynomial
Q(X)\in K[X]
, we have a unique expansion
f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}f_{i}Q^{i}
, where
f_{i}(X)\in K[X]
with
\deg f_{i}<\deg Q
. We define a map
v_{Q}:K[X]\longrightarrow vK(X)
by setting
v_{Q}(f):=\min\{vf_{i}+ivQ\}
, and extend it canonically to
K(X)
. It is known that
v_{Q}
is a valuation on
K(X)
whenever
Q
is a key polynomial for
v
over
K
[
References
, Proposition 2.6]. A family
\Lambda
of key polynomials for
v
over
K
is said to form a
complete sequence of key polynomials for
v
over
K
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(CSKP1)
\delta(Q)\neq\delta(Q^{\prime})
for
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\Lambda
with
Q\neq Q^{\prime}
,

(CSKP2)
\Lambda
is well ordered with respect to the ordering given by
Q<Q^{\prime}
if and only if
\delta(Q)<\delta(Q^{\prime})
,

(CSKP3) for any
f(X)\in K[X]
, there exists some
Q\in\Lambda
such that
vf=v_{Q}f
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(K(y)|K,v)
be an extension of valued fields. Assume that
\alpha\in v(y-K)
such that
\alpha\notin vK
. Then
\alpha=\max v(y-K)
.

Proof.

Take
a\in K
such that
\alpha:=v(y-a)\notin vK
. Suppose that there exists
b\in K
such that
v(y-b)>v(y-a)=\alpha
. It then follows from the triangle inequality that
\alpha=v(a-b)\in vK
, which contradicts our assumption.
∎

3.
Pseudo monotone sequences

For the remainder of this article, we will assume that
(K,v)
is a valued field and
E:=\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a sequence in
K
where
\lambda
is some limit ordinal.

Definition 3.1
.

The sequence
E
is said to be a
pseudo convergent sequence
(pcs) if

v(z_{\nu_{1}}-z_{\nu_{2}})<v(z_{\nu_{2}}-z_{\nu_{3}})\text{ for all }\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}<\nu_{3}<\lambda.

Observe that
v(z_{\nu}-z_{\mu})=v(z_{\nu}-z_{\nu+1})
for all
\mu>\nu
. We set
\delta_{\nu}:=v(z_{\nu}-z_{\nu+1})
. By definition,
\{\delta_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a strictly increasing sequence.

Definition 3.2
.

The sequence
E
is said to be
pseudo divergent sequence
(pds) if

v(z_{\nu_{1}}-z_{\nu_{2}})>v(z_{\nu_{2}}-z_{\nu_{3}})\text{ for all }\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}<\nu_{3}<\lambda.

Observe that
v(z_{\nu}-z_{\mu})=v(z_{\nu}-z_{\mu^{\prime}})
for all
\mu^{\prime},\mu<\nu
. We set
\delta_{\nu}:=v(z_{\nu}-z_{\mu})\text{ for all }\mu<\nu
. By definition,
\{\delta_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a strictly decreasing sequence.

Definition 3.3
.

The sequence
E
is said to be a
pseudo constant sequence
(pcts) if

v(z_{\nu}-z_{\mu})=v(z_{\nu^{\prime}}-z_{\mu^{\prime}})\text{ for all }\nu\neq\mu,\,\nu^{\prime}\neq\mu^{\prime}.

We define
\delta:=v(z_{\nu}-z_{\mu})\text{ for all }\nu\neq\mu
. If we are making general statements about the sequence
E
without differentiating between the cases, we will write
\delta_{\nu}
to mean
\delta
in the pcts case.

Definition 3.4
.

A pcs
E
is said to be a
Cauchy sequence
if
\{\delta_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is cofinal in
vK
. A pds
E
will be said to be
diverging to infinity
if
\{\delta_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is coinitial in
vK
.

Definition 3.5
.

The sequence
E
is said to be
pseudo monotone sequence
(pms) if is either a pcs, or pds, or pcts.

Definition 3.6
.

An element
y\in K
is said to be a
limit
of
E
if

v(y-z_{\nu})=\delta_{\nu}\text{ for all }\nu\text{ sufficiently large}.

Hence
y
is a limit if the sequence

\{v(y-z_{\nu})\}_{\nu<\lambda}\text{ is }\begin{cases}\text{ultimately strictly increasing,}&\quad\text{when }E\text{ is a pcs,}\\
\text{ultimately strictly decreasing,}&\quad\text{when }E\text{ is a pds,}\\
\text{ultimately constant,}&\quad\text{when }E\text{ is a pcts.}\end{cases}

Observe that a limit of
E
is not necessarily uniquely determined. Here are some straightforward observations.

Remark 3.7
.

Assume that
y
is a limit of
E
. If
E
is a pcs, then
\{v(y-z_{\nu})\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is strictly increasing. If
E
is a pcts, then
v(y-z_{\nu})=\delta
for all but at most element of
E
. Both of these are consequences of the triangle inequality. Further, every element of
E
is a limit of
E
when
E
is a pds or pcts. When
E
is a pcs, then no element of
E
is a limit of
E
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(L|K,v)
be an extension of valued fields and
E
a pms in
(K,v)
. Assume that
y
is a limit of
E
. Take any
b\in L
. Then
b
is also a limit of
E
if and only if
v(y-b)\geq\delta_{\nu}
for all
\nu
sufficiently large.

Proof.

The fact that
y
is a limit of
E
implies that
v(y-z_{\nu})=\delta_{\nu}
for all
\nu
sufficiently large. By definition,
b
is also a limit of
E
if and only if
v(b-z_{\nu})=\delta_{\nu}=v(y-z_{\nu})
for all
\nu
sufficiently large. The result now follows from the ultrametric inequality.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
E
be a pms in
(K,v)
and take
y\in K
. Then either
y
is a limit of
E
or the sequence
S:=\{v(y-z_{\nu})\}_{\mu<\lambda}
is ultimately constant.

Proof.

If
E
is a pcts, then the sequence
S
is ultimately constant either way.

We now assume that
E
is a pds and
y
is not a limit. Suppose that
S
is not ultimately constant. If
S
is ultimately increasing, then we can find
\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}<\nu_{3}
such that

v(y-z_{\nu_{1}})<v(y-z_{\nu_{2}})<v(y-z_{\nu_{3}}).

By the triangle inequality we obtain

\delta_{\nu_{2}}=v(y-z_{\nu_{1}})<v(y-z_{\nu_{2}})=\delta_{\nu_{3}}

which contradicts the fact that the sequence
\{\delta_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is strictly decreasing. If
S
is ultimately decreasing, then the assumption that
y
is not a limit implies that
S
is not ultimately strictly decreasing. So we obtain
\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}<\nu_{3}
such that

v(y-z_{\nu_{1}})>v(y-z_{\nu_{2}})=v(y-z_{\nu_{3}}).

It now follows from the triangle inequality that
\delta_{\nu_{2}}=\delta_{\nu_{3}}
which is again a contradiction. Thus
S
is neither ultimately increasing nor ultimately decreasing. Now take any
\nu<\lambda
. The fact that
S
is not ultimately increasing implies that there exists some
\mu_{1}>\nu
such that
v(y-z_{\nu})>v(y-z_{\mu_{1}})
. The fact that
S
is not ultimately decreasing implies that there exists
\mu_{2}>\mu_{1}
such that
v(y-z_{\mu_{1}})<v(y-z_{\mu_{2}})
. Hence,

v(y-z_{\nu})>v(y-z_{\mu_{1}})<v(y-z_{\mu_{2}}).

We now obtain from the triangle inequality that
\delta_{\mu_{1}}=v(y-z_{\mu_{1}})=\delta_{\mu_{2}}
which again yields a contradiction. Thus
S
is ultimately constant.

We now assume that
E
is a pcs and
y
is not a limit. Suppose that
S
is not ultimately constant. If
S
is ultimately increasing, then the assumption that
y
is not a limit implies that
S
is not ultimately strictly increasing. So we have a relation

v(y-z_{\nu_{1}})=v(y-z_{\nu_{2}})<v(y-z_{\nu_{3}}).

But this yields
\delta_{\nu_{1}}=\delta_{\nu_{2}}
which is a contradiction. If
S
is ultimately decreasing, then we have a relation

v(y-z_{\nu_{1}})>v(y-z_{\nu_{2}})>v(y-z_{\nu_{3}})

which yields
\delta_{\nu_{1}}=v(y-z_{\nu_{2}})>v(y-z_{\nu_{3}})=\delta_{\nu_{2}}
. But this contradicts the fact that
\{\delta_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is strictly increasing. Hence
S
is neither ultimately increasing nor ultimately decreasing. Thus we have a relation

v(y-z_{\nu})>v(y-z_{\mu_{1}})<v(y-z_{\mu_{2}})

where
\nu<\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}
. The triangle inequality again leads to the contradiction
\delta_{\nu}=v(y-z_{\mu_{1}})=\delta_{\mu_{1}}
. Hence
S
is ultimately constant.
∎

Fix an extension
\overline{v}
of
v
to
\overline{K}
. Take a polynomial
f(X)\in K[X]
. Write

f(X)=c(X-a_{1})\dotsc(X-a_{j})\dotsc(X-a_{n}),

where
c\in K
and
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{j}
are all the
root-limits
of
(f,E)
, that is, they are all the roots of
f
which are also limits of
E
. It follows from Lemma
3.9
that
\{\overline{v}(z_{\nu}-a_{i})\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is ultimately constant for all
i\geq j+1
. We denote the ultimate constant value of
\{\overline{v}(z_{\nu}-a_{i})\}_{\nu<\lambda}
by
\beta_{i}
. Then,

vf(z_{\nu})=j\delta_{\nu}+\beta\text{ for all }\nu\text{ sufficiently large},

where
\beta:=vc+\beta_{j+1}+\dotsc+\beta_{n}
. The fact that
vf(z_{\nu}),\delta_{\nu}\in vK
implies that

\beta\in vK.

The above observation naturally extends to rational functions. Take
\varphi(X)\in K(X)
. Then there exist
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\beta\in vK
such that

v\varphi(z_{\nu})=d\delta_{\nu}+\beta\text{ for all }\nu\text{ sufficiently large}.

The number
d
is referred to as the
dominating degree of
\varphi
with respect to
E
in [
References
] and is denoted by
\deg\mathrm{dom}\,_{E}(\varphi)
. Observe that if
\varphi=f/g
where
f,g\in K[X]
, then

\deg\mathrm{dom}\,_{E}(\varphi)=d=\#\{\text{root-limits of }(f,E)\}-\#\{\text{root-limits of }(g,E)\}.

Finally, observe that
v\varphi(z_{\nu})-v\varphi(z_{\nu+1})=d(\delta_{\nu}-\delta_{\nu+1})
for all
\nu
sufficiently large. Since
v\varphi(z_{\nu})-v\varphi(z_{\nu+1})
and
\delta_{\nu}-\delta_{\nu+1}
are solely dependent on
v
, we conclude that

\text{the dominating degree $d$ is independent of the choice of the extension of $v$ to }\overline{K}.

If
E
is a pcs or pds, then
\{\delta_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is either strictly increasing or strictly decreasing. The following result is now straightfoward.

Proposition 3.10
.

Take
\varphi(X)\in K(X)
. Then
\{v\varphi(z_{\nu})\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is ultimately constant if and only if either
E
is a pcts, or
E
is a pcs or pds such that
\deg\mathrm{dom}\,_{E}(\varphi)=0
.

It follows from our preceding discussions that when
E
is a pcs, the sequence
\{vf(z_{\nu})\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is either ultimately constant or ultimately strictly increasing, where
f(X)\in K[X]
. If the sequence is ultimately constant for all polynomials
f
over
K
, then we say that
E
is a
pcs of transcendental type
. In this case
E
does not admit any algebraic limits. Otherwise,
E
is said to be a
pcs of algebraic type
. In this case, we can find a polynomial
Q(X)\in K[X]
of minimal degree such that
\{vQ(z_{\nu})\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is ultimately strictly increasing. Without any loss of generality, we can choose
Q
to be monic. It can be observed that
Q(X)
is irreducible over
K
. The polynomial
Q(X)
is said to be an
associated minimal polynomial for
E
.

4.
Valuations induced by pseudo monotone sequences

Let
E:=\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
be a pms in
(K,v)
. Define,

\displaystyle V_{E}

\displaystyle:=\{\varphi(X)\in K(X)\mid v\varphi(z_{\nu})\geq 0\text{ for all }\nu\text{ sufficiently large}\},

\displaystyle M_{E}

\displaystyle:=\{\varphi(X)\in K(X)\mid v\varphi(z_{\nu})>0\text{ for all }\nu\text{ sufficiently large}\}.

Then

Proposition 4.1
.

Take
\varphi(X)\in K(X)
. Then
\{v\varphi(z_{\nu})\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is ultimately constant if and only if

. Further, in this case
v_{E}(\varphi)=\beta
where
v\varphi(z_{\nu})=\beta
for all
\nu
sufficiently large.

Proof.

We first assume that

. Take

such that

. Write

ultimately. So without any loss of generality we can assume that
\beta=0
, that is,
v\varphi(z_{\nu})=0
for all
\nu
sufficiently large. It now follows from the definition that
\varphi\in V_{E}\setminus M_{E}

v_{E}(\varphi)=0
.

We now assume that
v_{E}(\varphi)\in vK
. Take

. Write
\varphi^{\prime}(X):=\frac{1}{a}\varphi(X)
. Then
v_{E}(\varphi^{\prime})=0

\varphi^{\prime}\in V_{E}\setminus M_{E}

\nu
sufficiently large. Consequently,
v\varphi(z_{\nu})=va
for all
\nu
sufficiently large and hence
\{v\varphi(z_{\nu})\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is ultimately constant.
∎

Recall that
v\varphi(z_{\nu})=d\delta_{\nu}+\beta
for all
\nu
sufficiently large, where

for all

Conversely, take a polynomial

v_{E}(f)\notin vK\text{ if and only if $E$ is a pcs or pds and }(f,E)\text{ admits root-limits}.

Now assume that

is a limit of
E

v_{E}(f)\notin vK\text{ if and only if }v_{E}(f)\notin{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}vK.

Remark 4.2
.

(

is a pcs)

If

Remark 4.3
.

(

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

Proof.

We first assume that

is a limit of

\overline{v}_{E}(X-a)\notin\overline{v}\overline{K}

\overline{v}_{E}=\overline{v}_{a,\alpha}
.

We now assume that
E
is a pcts. Observe that the fact that
E
is a pcts implies that
\alpha\in\overline{v}\overline{K}
. It follows from Proposition
4.1
that
\overline{v}_{E}(X-a)=\overline{v}(z_{\nu}-a)
for all
\nu

Take any

\overline{v}_{E}(X-b)=\overline{v}_{a,\delta}(X-b)
. Now assume that
\overline{v}(a-b)=\delta

3.8
that

is also a limit of

. By definition,
\overline{v}(b-z_{\nu})=\delta
for all
\nu
sufficiently large. Consequently, it follows from Proposition

that
\overline{v}_{E}(X-b)=\delta
. Thus,

\overline{v}_{E}(X-b)=\overline{v}_{a,\delta}(X-b)\text{ for all }b\in\overline{K}.

Since any polynomial over
\overline{K}
splits into linear factors over

\overline{v}_{E}=\overline{v}_{a,\delta}=\overline{v}_{a,\alpha}
.
∎

Remark 5.3

Proposition 5.4

6.
Pure extensions

An extension
(K(X)|K,v)
is said to be a
pure extension
if it satisfies one of the following conditions:

(PE1)
v(X-a)
is not a torsion element modulo
vK
for some
a\in K
,

(PE2)
vb(X-a)=0
and
b(X-a)v
is transcendental over
Kv
for some
a,b\in K
,

(PE3)
X
is the limit of some pcs
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
of transcendental type in
(K,v)
.

Pure extensions are very well understood in terms of minimal pairs, key polynomials and implicit constant fields.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(\overline{K(X)}|K,v)
be an extension of valued fields such that
(K(X)|K,v)
is pure. Then the following statements hold true:

(1)
IC(K(X)|K,v)=K^{h}
,

(2) if the extension
(K(X)|K,v)
satisfies (PE1) or (PE2), then
(a,v(X-a))
is a minimal pair of definition for
v
over
K
and
\{X-a\}
forms a complete sequence of key polynomials for
v
over
K
,

(3) if the condition (PE3) is satisfied, then
(K(X)|K,v)

\{X-z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
forms a complete sequence of key polynomials for
v
over
K
.

Proof.

The first assertion is proved in [
References
, Lemma 3.7].

We now assume that
v(X-a)
is not a torsion element modulo
vK
, that is,
v(X-a)\notin v\overline{K}
. Take a polynomial

and write

where

. Observe that each of the monomials in this expression have distinct values. It follows that

, that is,
(a,v(X-a))
is a pair of definition for
v
over
K
. The fact that

implies that
(a,v(X-a))
is a minimal pair of definition for
v
over
K
. Further, the observation

for all

implies that

, where

. By definition,
\{X-a\}
forms a complete sequence of key polynomials for
v
over
K
.

We now assume that
vb(X-a)=0
and
b(X-a)v
is transcendental over
Kv
for some
a,b\in K
. It follows from [
References
, Lemma 2.10] that

. Hence

over

(a,v(X-a))

over

Finally, we assume that

over

9.

Let
\Gamma

\Delta

\Gamma

•

\Delta

\Gamma

•

\beta\in\Delta
for all
\beta\in\Gamma
with
-\alpha\leq\beta\leq\alpha

Thus the set of all isolated subgroups of

We now assume that

Radiation Hardness Studies of RPC Based on Diamond-Like Carbon Electrodes for MEG II Experiment Journal: Nuclear Instruments and Methods in Physics Research Section A
Masato Takahashi, Sei Ban, Weiyuan Li, Atsuhiko Ochi, Wataru Ootani, Atsushi Oya, Hiromu Suzuki, Kensuke Yamamoto

Abstract
A novel type of resistive plate chamber, based on diamond-like carbon (DLC) electrodes is under development for background identification in the MEG II experiment. The DLC-RPC is required to have a radiation hardness to mass irradiation since it is planned to be placed in a high-intensity and low-momentum muon beam. In this study, the aging test using a high-intensity X-ray beam was conducted to evaluate the radiation hardness of the DLC-RPC. The accumulated charge due to X-ray irradiation reached about 54 C/ cm 2 \mathrm{cm^{2}} , which is approximately half of the one-year irradiation dose expected in the MEG II experiment. As a result, the degradation of the gas gain was observed due to fluorine deposition and insulators formed on the DLC electrodes. In addition, discharges via spacers were also observed repeatedly and interrupted the DLC-RPC operation.

Introduction

The MEG II experiment

\mu\to e\gamma

The dominant background is an accidental coincidence between background
\gamma

Identification of the radiative muon decay (RMD;
\mu\to e\nu\bar{\nu}\gamma
), one of the background
\gamma
-ray sources, can further suppress background events.
A novel type of resistive plate chamber, based on diamond-like carbon (DLC) electrodes is under development and planned to be installed in front of the target for further background suppression.
It detects low-energy positrons of 1 – 5 MeV from the RMD with a high-energy
\gamma
-ray of
E_{\gamma}>
48 MeV. The detector should have an ultra-low mass (0.1 %
X_{0}
), high-rate capability (up to 3 MHz/
\mathrm{cm^{2}}
), and radiation hardness (irradiation dose of
\sim
100 C/
\mathrm{cm^{2}}
) since the high-intensity (
7\times 10^{7}~\mu/\mathrm{s}
) and low-momentum (
28~\mathrm{MeV}/c
) muon beam pass through there.
Furthermore, the detection efficiency of 90 % for minimum ionizing particles, the timing resolution of 1 ns, and the 20 cm diameter detector size are also required to identify the RMD efficiently.
The geometrical acceptance to the RMD positrons is expected to be about 100 % with a 20 cm in diameter detector.

2
Radiation hardness of the DLC-RPC

The DLC-RPC is required to have a radiation hardness for a continuous operation for 20 weeks, which corresponds to the duration of the one-year physics run in the MEG II experiment, under the muon beam.
Aging in gaseous detectors is expected to occur mainly due to the high-energy charge generated by avalanches.
Therefore, the amount of irradiation dose by the muon beam is estimated as the amount of accumulated charge.

Calculated with a beam muon rate of 3 MHz/
\mathrm{cm^{2}}
, an average avalanche charge of 3 pC, and an operation period of 20 weeks, the total irradiation dose for MEG II is expected to be an accumulated charge equivalent to about 100 C/
\mathrm{cm^{2}}
.
Here, the average avalanche charge is estimated in the previous test using the muon beam
[
2
]
.

Evaluation of aging phenomena

5.1
Degradation of the DLC-RPC gas gain

As shown in Fig.

This means the decrease in the DLC-RPC gas gain because the HV current was considered proportional to the gas gain.
The X-ray intensity did not change according to the output of the X-ray monitoring device.
Comparison of the pulse height spectra for
\beta

5.4
DLC peeling

Figure 9
:
DLC peeling. The orange area in the center has DLC peeling off, exposing the base material polyimide.

Although the adhesion between DLC and polyimide is known to be good, the DLC peeling was observed in the X-ray irradiation area as shown in Fig.
9
.
To explain the mechanism, it is supposed that the fluorine separates bonds between the carbons in the DLC. However, a clear mechanism is not known, and further investigation is needed.

5.5
Discharge problems

Twice discharges during the X-ray irradiation (Fig.
5
) occurred via the spacing pillars.
There is discoloration from discharge at the base of the pillar, and dust on the side of the pillar is presumed to have served as a discharge path as shown in Fig.
10
.
Such discharges occurred repeatedly at the same pillar.
In this test, no further discharge was observed because the relevant pillar was removed after the second discharge.

Discharge via the pillar is a significant problem for the stable operation of the DLC-RPC.
Therefore, it is necessary to establish a mechanism to suppress discharges and a system that enables remote recovery from outside the chamber in the event of a discharge.

6
Conclusion

A novel type of resistive plate chamber, based on diamond-like carbon electrodes is under development for background
\gamma
-ray identification in the MEG II experiment.
Using the small prototype electrode, the radiation hardness of the DLC-RPC was evaluated. As a result, the degradation of the DLC-RPC gas gain due to fluorine deposition and interruptions of operation due to discharges were observed.
To solve these problems, future development work should be done to suppress deposit formation, improve quality control, and develop a structure that suppresses discharges.

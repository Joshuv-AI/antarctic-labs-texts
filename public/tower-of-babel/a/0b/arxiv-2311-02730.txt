Spontaneous baryogenesis and generation of gravitational waves in a new model of quintessential 𝜶 \alpha -attractor
and Buddhadeb Ghosh

Abstract
We study the role of α \alpha -parameter of the newly proposed model of quintessential α \alpha -attractor inflation (arXiv: 2305.00230 [gr-qc]) to the case of quintessential spontaneous baryogenesis and generation of relic gravitational waves in presence of a rolling scalar field during kination. An effective 4-Fermi construct technique has been employed to compute the freeze-out temperature and the baryon-to-entropy ratio, of which the obtained results conform to the experimental requirements for 0.28 ≤ α ≤ 0.30 0.28\leq\alpha\leq 0.30 . This range of α \alpha is found to originate from the functional behaviour of the end-value expression of the potential concerned. We also find a blue-tilted gravitational wave spectrum during a transition from inflation to kination. The amplitudes of the gravitational waves during radiation domination satisfy the constraint for nucleosynthesis and the characteristic strain of the ongoing gravitational wave detectors. Thus, the most important observation emerged from the present study is that, increasingly small fractional values of α \alpha are favourable for unification of inflation, baryogenesis, quintessence and gravitational waves within a single model. This could have an interesting connection with the fundamental origin of α \alpha -attractor.

The post-inflationary kination period

In the standard formulation of inflationary quintessence
[
54
]
, the
\phi
field enters into a phase of kinetic energy domination, called
kination

[
55
]
, just after the end of inflation. This period of expansion can be characterized by the scale factor (
a

[
74
]

\rho_{\mathrm{kin}}(a)=\rho_{\mathrm{end}}\left(\frac{a_{\mathrm{end}}}{a}\right)^{6}

(2.1)

and Hubble rate

H_{\mathrm{kin}}(a)=\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{kin}}(a)}{3M_{p}^{2}}}=H_{\mathrm{end}}\left(\frac{a_{\mathrm{end}}}{a}\right)^{3}.

(2.2)

Here, the subscript ‘end’ denotes the respective parameters at the point where the inflation ends and
M_{p}(=1/\sqrt{8\pi G})
is the reduced Planck mass. The constants
\rho_{\mathrm{end}}
and
H_{\mathrm{end}}
are the inputs from the scale of inflation
V_{\mathrm{end}}
. Under the slow-roll approximation we can write

\rho_{\mathrm{end}}\approx V_{\mathrm{end}},

(2.3)

because during inflation potential energy of the inflaton field is much larger than the kinetic energy. At the end of inflation,
\phi
reaches
\phi_{\mathrm{end}}
and the corresponding value of the potential,
V_{\mathrm{end}}=V(\phi_{\mathrm{end}})
, is obtained from equating the first potential slow-roll parameter
\epsilon_{V}
to unity. The end-value of the potential is therefore induced in
\rho_{\mathrm{end}}
and
H_{\mathrm{end}}

H_{\mathrm{end}}=\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{end}}}{3M_{p}^{2}}}\approx\sqrt{\frac{V_{\mathrm{end}}}{3M_{p}^{2}}}.

(2.4)

Now, since in the period of kination, the kinetic energy of the
\phi
field dominates over the potential energy (
i.e.

\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}\gg V(\phi)
), we can write

\rho_{\mathrm{kin}}=\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}+V(\phi)\approx\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}.

(2.5)

From Eqs. (
2.1

2.3
) and (
2.5

\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}(a)}{2}\approx V_{\mathrm{end}}\left(\frac{a_{\mathrm{end}}}{a}\right)^{6},

(2.6)

which yields the field velocity of the form

\dot{\phi}(a)\approx\sqrt{2V_{\mathrm{end}}}\left(\frac{a_{\mathrm{end}}}{a}\right)^{3}.

(2.7)

Differentiating once again and identifying
H=\frac{\dot{a}}{a}
we obtain,

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}=0.

(2.8)

Eq. (
2.8

\rho_{\mathrm{kin}}(a_{\mathrm{end}})=\rho_{\mathrm{end}}\approx V_{\mathrm{end}}

2.1

2.3
) and (
2.7

2.3
) The dependency of

\dot{\phi}\propto\frac{1}{a^{3}}

2.7

\phi

Basic framework of spontaneous baryogenesis

The relevant Lagrangian here is,

\mathcal{L}_{\phi}=\left(-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-V(\phi)-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}\right),

(3.1)

where the quintessential inflaton field
\phi
is in the Friedmann metric background

of signature
(-,+,+,+)
. In addition,
\phi

by the following

[

4

51

53

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\partial_{\mu}\phi J^{\mu},

(3.2)

\lambda^{\prime}
being a dimensionless coupling constant and the mass scale

signifies a sub-Planckian cut-off of the effective theory. The associated energy-momentum tensor can be obtained from the equation

T^{\mu}_{\nu}=\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\phi}-2g^{\mu\alpha}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\phi}}{\partial g^{\alpha\nu}}.

(3.3)

The scalar field
\phi
is spatially homogenized by the cosmic expansion after inflation. All primordial quantum fluctuations (
\delta\phi
) buried in
\phi
within the Hubble sphere are transferred to density perturbations, whose imprints are detected in the anisotropies of the cosmic microwave background. The remaining
\phi

(3.4)

where
\delta\mathcal{E}=-\delta\bar{\mathcal{E}}=-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}\dot{\phi}}{M}
.
\rho_{0}
is the interaction-free part of the density
\rho
. The second term indicates that, if the field velocity
\dot{\phi}
is appreciably non-zero, then for each baryon (anti-baryon), an extra amount of energy density
\delta\mathcal{E}(\delta\bar{\mathcal{E}})
would appear in the total energy density budget of the universe. The extra positive and negative energy density terms originate from the interaction term
\partial_{\mu}\phi J^{\mu}
responsible for spontaneous
CPT
symmetry breaking at a scale below the Planck mass, as explained in Section
1
, which ultimately ensure the baryon-excess (
n>\bar{n}
) in the universe. If, on the other hand,
CPT
symmetry is not broken in thermal equilibrium, there would be no baryon asymmetry
i.e.

n=\bar{n}
. We shall calculate
n-\bar{n}
in the next paragraph and show that it is positive definite. At the thermal equilibrium, this additional energy per baryon (anti-baryon) is recognized as an effective chemical potential
\mu
(

)
[

. Alternative realizations of chemical potentials, in this context, can be seen in Refs.
[

We explicitly compute the baryon excess by considering the statistical phase space distribution function in thermal equilibrium,

f(\epsilon,\mu)=\frac{1}{e^{\left(\frac{\epsilon-\mu}{T}\right)}+1}=\frac{1}{e^{\left(\frac{|\vec{p}|-\mu}{T}\right)}+1},

(3.5)

of an ensemble of massless relativistic baryons of momentum
\vec{p}
at a particular energy level
\epsilon=|\vec{p}|
with degeneracy
\tilde{g}
as,

\Delta n=n-\bar{n}=\int\tilde{g}\frac{d^{3}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[f(\epsilon,\mu)-f(\epsilon,-\mu)\right],

(3.6)

with the convention
c=\hbar=k_{B}=1
. Under the high temperature approximation,
\mu/T\ll 1
, Eq. (
3.6
) reduces to (see
B
)

\Delta n\approx\frac{\tilde{g}\mu T^{2}}{6}=\frac{\tilde{g}\lambda^{\prime}\dot{\phi}T^{2}}{6M}>0.

(3.7)

The temporal behavior of
\phi
, determined by
\dot{\phi}
, is encoded in the solution of its equation of motion under the effective interaction of Eq. (
3.2
) during kination in expanding background. We derive that equation of motion, using Eq. (
3.1

\left[\partial_{\mu}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial_{\mu}\phi}\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\right]\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{\phi}=0,

(3.8)

as (see
C
)

\left[1-\frac{\tilde{g}}{6}\left(\frac{\lambda^{\prime}M_{p}}{M}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{T}{M_{p}}\right)^{2}\right]\left(\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}\right)+\frac{dV(\phi)}{d\phi}=0.

(3.9)

The second term in the third bracket signifies the back-reaction on
\phi
of the coupling described in Eq (
3.2
). According to the

[

,
11

\frac{\lambda^{\prime}M_{p}}{M}<8

the second term is very small. Then from Eq. (
3.9

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+\frac{dV(\phi)}{d\phi}\approx 0,

which is the well-known Klein-Gordon equation of the scalar field
\phi

Now, in the kination regime,
\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}\gg V(\phi)

\frac{dV(\phi)}{d\phi}

\dot{\phi}
and

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}\approx 0.

\frac{1}{a^{3}}\frac{d}{dt}\left(a^{3}\dot{\phi}\right)=0,

Density of
\phi

The Lagrangian of the
\phi
-field is given by

\mathcal{L}_{\phi}=-\frac{1}{2}g^{\beta\gamma}\partial_{\beta}\phi\partial_{\gamma}\phi-V(\phi)-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\partial_{\beta}\phi J^{\beta}

(A.1)

and the corresponding energy-momentum tensor can be calculated as

\begin{split}T_{\nu}^{\mu}&=\delta_{\nu}^{\mu}\mathcal{L}_{\phi}-2g^{\mu\alpha}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\phi}}{\partial g^{\alpha\nu}}\\
&=\delta_{\nu}^{\mu}\left(-\frac{1}{2}g^{\beta\gamma}\partial_{\beta}\phi\partial_{\gamma}\phi-V(\phi)-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\partial_{\beta}\phi J^{\beta}\right)+\left(g^{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi+\frac{2\lambda^{\prime}}{M}g^{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\phi J_{\nu}\right)\\
&=\delta_{\nu}^{\mu}\left(\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}-V(\phi)-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\dot{\phi}J^{0}\right)+\left(g^{\mu 0}\dot{\phi}\partial_{\nu}\phi+\frac{2\lambda^{\prime}}{M}g^{\mu 0}\dot{\phi}J_{\nu}\right).\end{split}

(A.2)

The time-time component of
T_{\nu}^{\mu}
is,

\begin{split}T_{0}^{0}&=\left(\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}-V(\phi)-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\dot{\phi}J^{0}\right)+\left(-\dot{\phi}^{2}+\frac{2\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\dot{\phi}J^{0}\right)\\
&=-\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}-V(\phi)+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\dot{\phi}J^{0},\end{split}

(A.3)

from which we obtain the density as

\begin{split}\rho&=-T_{0}^{0}=\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}+V(\phi)-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\dot{\phi}J^{0}\\
&=\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}+V(\phi)-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\dot{\phi}\left(n-\bar{n}\right).\end{split}

(A.4)

Appendix B
Derivation of baryon excess
\Delta n

The Fermi-Dirac distribution function for massless relativistic baryons having momentum
|\vec{p}|\equiv p
, energy
\epsilon=p
and chemical potential
\mu
is,

f(p,\mu)=\frac{1}{e^{\left(\frac{p-\mu}{T}\right)}+1}=\frac{e^{-p/T}}{e^{-\mu/T}+e^{-p/T}}.

(B.1)

Under high temperature approximation we have
\mu/T\ll 1
and terms
\mathcal{O}(\mu^{2}/T^{2})
are neglected. Therefore,

\begin{split}&f(p,\mu)\approx\frac{e^{-p/T}}{1-\left(\frac{\mu}{T}-e^{-p/T}\right)}\\
&=e^{-p/T}\left[1-\left(\frac{\mu}{T}-e^{-p/T}\right)\right]^{-1}\\
&=e^{-p/T}\left[1+\sum_{\mathbb{Z}\ni n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu}{T}-e^{-p/T}\right)^{n}\right]\\
&=e^{-p/T}\Bigl[1+\sum_{\mathbb{Z}\ni n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}{}^{n}C_{m}\left(\frac{\mu}{T}\right)^{n-m}\left(-e^{-p/T}\right)^{m}\Bigr]\\
&\approx e^{-p/T}\left[1+\sum_{\mathbb{Z}\ni n=1}^{\infty}{}^{n}C_{n-1}\left(\frac{\mu}{T}\right)\left(-e^{-p/T}\right)^{n-1}\right]\\
&=e^{-p/T}+\frac{\mu}{T}\sum_{\mathbb{Z}\ni n=1}^{\infty}n\left(-1\right)^{n-1}\left(e^{-np/T}\right).\end{split}

(B.2)

Similarly, the same distribution function for anti-baryons is

f(p,-\mu)=e^{-p/T}-\frac{\mu}{T}\sum_{\mathbb{Z}\ni n=1}^{\infty}n\left(-1\right)^{n-1}\left(e^{-np/T}\right).

(B.3)

Therefore,

f(p,\mu)-f(p,-\mu)=\frac{2\mu}{T}\sum_{\mathbb{Z}\ni n=1}^{\infty}n\left(-1\right)^{n-1}\left(e^{-np/T}\right).

(B.4)

Thus, the baryon excess,

\begin{split}&\Delta n=n-\bar{n}=\int\tilde{g}\frac{d^{3}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[f(p,\mu)-f(p,-\mu)\right]\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{g}\frac{4\pi p^{2}dp}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[f(p,\mu)-f(p,-\mu)\right]\\
&=\tilde{g}\frac{\mu}{\pi^{2}T}\sum_{\mathbb{Z}\ni n=1}^{\infty}n(-1)^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}p^{2}e^{-np/T}dp.\end{split}

(B.5)

Putting
x=\frac{np}{T}
,

\begin{split}&\Delta n=\tilde{g}\frac{\mu T^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{\mathbb{Z}\ni n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{2}e^{-x}dx\\
&=\tilde{g}\frac{\mu T^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\eta(2)\Gamma(3),\end{split}

(B.6)

where
\eta(2)
is the
Dirichlet eta function
, which is related to
Riemann zeta function
as
\eta(2)=\left(1-2^{1-2}\right)\zeta(2)
. Putting
\zeta(2)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}
and
\Gamma(3)=2!
we finally obtain

\Delta n=\frac{\tilde{g}\mu T^{2}}{6}=\frac{\tilde{g}\lambda^{\prime}\dot{\phi}T^{2}}{6M}.

(B.7)

Appendix C
Equation of motion of
\phi
with the effective interaction

We have the Lagrangian for the
\phi
field

\mathcal{L}_{\phi}=-\frac{1}{2}g^{\beta\gamma}\partial_{\beta}\phi\partial_{\gamma}\phi-V(\phi)-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\partial_{\beta}\phi J^{\beta},

(C.1)

where the metric is composed of usual Friedmann metric components
g_{00}=-1
and
g_{ii}=a^{2}
such that
\sqrt{-g}=a^{3}
. Now,

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\phi}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\phi)}=-\frac{1}{2}g^{\beta\gamma}\left(\delta_{\beta}^{\mu}\partial_{\gamma}\phi+\partial_{\beta}\phi\delta_{\gamma}^{\mu}\right)-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\delta_{\beta}^{\mu}J^{\beta}\\
&=-g^{\mu\beta}\partial_{\beta}\phi-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\delta_{\beta}^{\mu}J^{\beta}=-g^{\mu 0}\dot{\phi}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\delta_{0}^{\mu}J^{0},\end{split}

(C.2)

\begin{split}&\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\phi}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\phi)}\right)\\
&=-\frac{\partial_{\mu}g}{2\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\phi}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\phi)}+\sqrt{-g}\partial_{\mu}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\phi}}{\partial\left(\partial_{\mu}\phi\right)}\right)\\
&=\frac{6a^{5}\dot{a}}{2a^{3}}\left(\dot{\phi}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}J^{0}\right)+a^{3}\left(\ddot{\phi}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\partial_{0}J^{0}\right)\\
&=a^{3}\left[\left(\ddot{\phi}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}\partial_{0}J^{0}\right)+3H\left(\dot{\phi}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{M}J^{0}\right)\right]\\
&=a^{3}\left[\left(\ddot{\phi}-\frac{\tilde{g}\lambda^{\prime 2}T^{2}}{6M^{2}}\ddot{\phi}\right)+3H\left(\dot{\phi}-\frac{\tilde{g}\lambda^{\prime 2}T^{2}}{6M^{2}}\dot{\phi}\right)\right]\\
&=a^{3}\left[1-\frac{\tilde{g}}{6}\left(\frac{\lambda^{\prime}M_{p}}{M}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{T}{M_{p}}\right)^{2}\right]\left(\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}\right)\end{split}

(C.3)

and

\frac{\partial\left(\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{\phi}\right)}{\partial\phi}=-a^{3}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\phi}.

(C.4)

Therefore, the equation of motion becomes

\begin{split}&\left[\partial_{\mu}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\phi)}\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\right]\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{\phi}=0\\
\mathrm{or,}&\left[1-\frac{\tilde{g}}{6}\left(\frac{\lambda^{\prime}M_{p}}{M}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{T}{M_{p}}\right)^{2}\right]\left(\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}\right)+\frac{\partial V}{\partial\phi}=0.\end{split}

(C.5)

Queue Scheduling with Adversarial Bandit Learning
Jiatai Huang, Leana Golubchik, Longbo Huang

Abstract
In this paper, we study scheduling of a queueing system with zero knowledge of instantaneous network conditions. We consider a one-hop single-server queueing system consisting of K K queues, each with time-varying and non-stationary arrival and service rates. Our scheduling approach builds on an innovative combination of adversarial bandit learning and Lyapunov drift minimization, without knowledge of the instantaneous network state (the arrival and service rates) of each queue. We then present two novel algorithms SoftMW (SoftMaxWeight) and SSMW (Sliding-window SoftMaxWeight), both capable of stabilizing systems that can be stablized by some (possibly unknown) sequence of randomized policies whose time-variation satisfies a mild condition. We further generalize our results to the setting where arrivals and departures only have bounded moments instead of being deterministically bounded and propose SoftMW+ and SSMW+ that are capable of stabilizing the system. As a building block of our new algorithms, we also extend the classical EXP3.S [ 4 ] algorithm for multi-armed bandits to handle unboundedly large feedback signals, which can be of independent interest.

One basic requirement of most existing scheduling solutions is having knowledge of the instantaneous network state – i.e., the amount of arrival traffic and the amount of service under any feasible control action, e.g., the power allocation among all links – before taking a new scheduling action. Given this information,
there have been many successful network scheduling algorithms, with various aspects of theoretical performance guarantees, including queue stability

However, in many real-world scenarios, such network-state knowledge may not always be available if its measurement or estimation is too difficult or costly to obtain. Even when such knowledge is available, it can be biased and imperfect. For instance, in an IoT system, due to sensors’ temperature-drift or device malfunction, unexpected changes in traffic and channel patterns can occur at any time
[
12
]
. In an underwater communication system, it is extremely challenging to perform perfect channel state estimation
[
18
]
. Moreover, in applications where the communicating parties can move rapidly, e.g., self-driving vehicles
[

, or in an arbitrary manner, e.g., wireless AR/VR devices
[
9

, channel conditions can also change rapidly and thus difficult to estimate accurately.
Therefore, scheduling policies relying on precise network-state knowledge may not be applicable to many real-world tasks; relying on such policies can result in significant performance degradation due to inaccurate information.
Hence, network scheduling
without

instantaneous knowledge and accurate estimation of the network state is important both, in theory and in practice, i.e., it can significantly improve robustness and availability of large-scale networked systems while reducing operational and maintenance costs.

To this end, in this paper, we focus on a novel
scheduling without network-state knowledge
formulation. Specifically, we focus on a one-hop scheduling task, where a single-server serves
K
queues, each corresponding to a job type.
The server chooses a single queue to serve at each time slot. The network dynamics, i.e., arrival and service rates, evolve in an
oblivious adversarial
manner and are unknown before the scheduling decision. Moreover, the service outcome is only observed after the action with bandit feedback, i.e., only the served queue produces an observation. Our goal is to seek an efficient scheduling policy to stabilize the network.

To solve this problem, we introduce novel learning-augmented scheduling algorithms, inspired by the celebrated
MaxWeight
queue scheduling algorithm
[
29
]
and the success of the
EXP3
family of algorithms on non-stationary Multi-Armed Bandits (MAB) problems
[
4
]

system,
as long as the system can be stabilized by a randomized policy whose total variation of probabilities to serve each type of job is not too large. Perhaps surprisingly, our algorithms rely on neither knowing the network statistics before-hand, nor on complicated explicit real-time estimation of the system. As a result, compared to its network-state knowledge dependent counterparts, our algorithms are naturally more robust to jitter and unexpected traffic/service patterns in the system.

3
Problem Setting

We consider the problem of scheduling
K
job types on a single work-conserving server with a slotted time system. Each arriving job first joins a queue associated with its type
i
, which we denote by
Q_{i}
. Denote by
A_{t,i}
the amount of arriving jobs of type
i
in the
t
-th time slot, and by
S_{t,i}
the maximum amount of jobs of type
i
the server can serve in the
t
-th time slot. At the beginning of each time slot
t
, the server chooses
exactly one
type of a job
a_{t}\in[K]
to serve. Denote by
Q_{t,i}
the queue length of type
i
jobs at the end of time slot
t
. Then, each
Q_{t,i}
evolves according to the following equation:

Q_{t,i}=\max\left\{Q_{t-1,i}+A_{t,i}-S_{t,i}\mathbbm{1}[i=a_{t}],0\right\}

where
\mathbf{Q}_{0}=(Q_{0,1},...,Q_{0,K})=\mathbf{0}
.
At the beginning of each time slot
t
, the latest queue lengths
Q_{t-1,1},\ldots,Q_{t-1,K}
are available to the server for making new decisions. The maximum service amount of past actions
S_{0,a_{0}},\ldots,S_{t-1,a_{t}}
are also visible to the server.

We assume that there are two sequences of distributions
\{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\ldots\}
and
\{\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2},\ldots\}
, all fixed before the queue process starts, and their statistics are known to the scheduler before-hand. All distributions
\mathcal{A}_{t}
s and
\mathcal{S}_{t}
s are supported on
[0,M]^{K}
, where
M
is a constant known before-hand. We further assume that each
\mathbf{A}_{t}
is randomly sampled from
\mathcal{A}_{t}
, each
\mathbf{S}_{t}
is sampled from
\mathcal{S}_{t}
, and all
\mathbf{A}_{t}
s and
\mathbf{S}_{t}
s are independent random vectors.
We denote by
\vec{\lambda}_{t}
the mean of
\mathcal{A}_{t}
, and by
\vec{\sigma}_{t}
the mean of
\mathcal{S}_{t}
.

Our objective is to design a scheduling policy, under which we have the following upper-bound on the average expected queue lengths:

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}[Q_{t,i}]=\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}(1).

We say a scheduling policy
stabilizes
the system, or the system is
stable
under some scheduling policy, if the above bound holds.

Classical scheduling tasks on stationary systems (e.g.,
[
10
,
22
]
) correspond to the case where
\mathcal{A}_{t}=\mathcal{A}_{1}
(
\mathcal{S}_{t}=\mathcal{S}_{1}
), i.e., the distributions are time-invariant in our setting. In our problem, it is complicated to explore and estimate the time-varying service distributions subject to the queue stability.

5
Queue Scheduling with only Bandit Feedback

In contrast to the setting with perfect network state knowledge, in our case, there is no such accurate channel condition for the scheduler. Specifically, the server only receives a bandit feedback for each time step’s actual service, i.e., only
S_{t,a_{t}}
is known after the service decision
a_{t}
is made.

In this section, we present a novel algorithm, which is capable of stabilizing the system using only bandit feedback,
S_{t,a_{t}}
. Our core idea is to embed a suitable Multi-Armed Bandit algorithm into the
MaxWeight
scheduler
[
37
]
, so that the term
\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^{T}Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S_{t,a_{t}}]
, which is the key ingredient of
MaxWeight
, is guaranteed to be not too far from
\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle]
. Given access to
\vec{\sigma}_{t}
,
MaxWeight
achieves this by greedily choosing
a_{t}=\argmax_{i}Q_{t-1,i}\sigma_{t,i}
at each time step
t
. However, when
\vec{\sigma}_{t}
is unknown and time-varying, it is hard to guarantee that each summand
Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S_{t,a_{t}}
is large. Thus, we focus on optimizing the whole sum
\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^{T}Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S_{t,a_{t}}]
.

In the remainder of this section, we will first present
EXP3.S+
, an extended version of the
EXP3.S

[
4
]
algorithm for adversarial MAB (
Section

5.1
).
EXP3.S+
has adequate flexibility to serve as an important building block of our novel scheduling algorithm

(
Section

5.2

Finally, in
Section

5.3

8
Related Work

Recent literature includes learning-based scheduling policies that require little prior-knowledge and can gather channel statistics at run-time.

Learning-based approaches to scheduling queueing systems without perfect channel state knowledge require substantial exploration, to probe for more information of all the channels inside the system instead of merely exploiting the statistics at hand (e.g., via a
MaxWeight
style planning). Typical ways to introduce adequate exploration include epsilon-greedy, which explicitly allocates a small probability to serve each channel unconditionally
[
31
,
21
,
22
]
; here the exploration is blind to the queue sizes and historical channel statistics and thus almost decoupled from exploitation.
By contrast, optimistic exploration works by adding bonus terms to current channel statistics, so that exploration and exploitation are naturally coupled during scheduling
[
10
,
22
,
36
,
41
]
. Upper confidence bound (UCB)
[
3
]
is a classical method for designing a bonus term.

Existing works on scheduling in non-stationary queueing systems include
[
41
]
, which uses discounted UCB estimators for an up-to-date service rate of each link to replace the actual mean services rate in classical
MaxWeight
. The resulting policy can stabilize problem instances where the difference of each link’s arrival (and service) rates between any two time steps in any time window of length
W
is sufficiently small, and this window length
W
needs to match with the discounting factor
\gamma
used in discounted UCB estimators. Compared to
[
41
]
, our smoothness assumption is on the reference randomized policies rather than the true service rates.

9
Conclusions and Future Work

In this paper, we propose a novel approach to apply adversarial bandit learning techniques to schedule queueing systems with unknown, time-varying channel states. The presented new algorithms
SoftMW
and
SSMW
are capable of stabilizing the system whenever the system can be stabilized by some (possibly unknown) sequence of randomized policies, and their time-variation satisfies some mild condition. We further generalize our results to the setting where arrivals and departures only have bounded moments and develop two stablizing algorithms
SoftMW+
and
SSMW+
.

We believe our approach can be generalized to more complex stochastic networks (e.g., multi-hop networks), and to achieve other tasks such as utility optimization subject to queue stability. It is also an interesting future work to design distributed network scheduling algorithms using adversarial bandit learning techniques.

Lemma B.1

Below is a technical lemma relating the single-step regret to single-step OMD update.

Appendix C
On the Implementation of
EXP3.S+
(Algorithm 1)

The core operation when implementing our
EXP3.S+
(
Algorithm

1
) is the
\argmin
expression in
Algorithm

1
. In other words, we need to solve the following optimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}}\quad

\displaystyle-\langle\mathbf{g},\mathbf{y}\rangle+D_{\Psi}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x})

(28)

s.t.

\displaystyle y_{i}\geq\beta\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq K

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}y_{i}=1

where
\mathbf{g}\in\mathcal{R}_{+}^{K}
and
x\in\Delta^{[K]}
are two fixed vectors,
0\leq\beta<\frac{1}{K}
is a constant.
For this optimization problem, we can introduce a set of Lagrange multipliers
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{K},\mu
and the Lagrangian

L(\mathbf{y};\vec{\lambda},\mu)\triangleq-\sum_{i=1}^{K}g_{i}y_{i}+D_{\Psi}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x})+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\lambda_{i}(y_{i}-\beta)+\mu\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}y_{i}-1\right).

Let
\mathbf{y}^{*}\in\Delta^{[K],\beta}
be an optimizer for the optimization problem
Equation

28
, and let
\vec{\lambda}^{*},\mu^{*}
be the corresponding multipliers.
Then we can write down the KKT condition for
\mathbf{y}^{*}
:

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial L}{\partial y_{i}}\right\rvert_{\mathbf{y}^{*};\vec{\lambda}^{*},\mu^{*}}=-g_{i}+\ln(y^{*}_{i})-\ln(x_{i})+\lambda^{*}_{i}+\mu^{*}

\displaystyle=0\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq K,

\displaystyle y^{*}_{i}>\beta\Rightarrow\lambda^{*}_{i}

\displaystyle=0\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq K.

Denote by
\mathcal{I}\triangleq\left\{i\in[K]:y^{*}_{i}>\beta\right\}
, then the KKT conditions implies that

\ln(y^{*}_{i})-\ln(x_{i})-g_{i}=\ln(y^{*}_{j})-\ln(x_{j})-g_{j}

(29)

for all
i,j\in\mathcal{I}
. On the other hand, we know that

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}y^{*}_{i}=1-\beta\cdot(K-\left\lvert\mathcal{I}\right\rvert)

(30)

since we have assumed that
y^{*}_{i}=\beta
for all
i\in[K]\setminus\mathcal{I}
. In fact,
Equations

29
and
30
have an explicit “soft-max” solution

y^{*}_{i}\propto x_{i}\exp(g_{i})

and thus

y^{*}_{i}=\left[1-\beta\cdot(K-\left\lvert\mathcal{I}\right\rvert)\right]\cdot\frac{x_{i}\exp(g_{i})}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}}x_{j}\exp(g_{j})}

for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
.
Therefore, it remains to determine
\mathcal{I}
to complete depict
y^{*}
. Note that the optimization problem
Equation

28
satisfies Slater’s condition, hence its optimizers are completely determined by the KKT conditions. One can verify that the following problem

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}}\quad

\displaystyle D_{\Psi}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})

(31)

s.t.

\displaystyle y_{i}\geq\beta\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq K

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}y_{i}=1

where
x^{\prime}_{i}=x_{i}\exp(g_{i})
, has the exactly same KKT conditions as
Equation

28
. Furthermore, without loss of generality, we assume that
x^{\prime}_{1}\leq x^{\prime}_{2}\leq\cdots\leq x^{\prime}_{K}
in
Equation

31
(otherwise we can permute and rearrange the coordinates of
\mathbf{x}
since both
D_{\Psi}(\cdot,\cdot)
and the constraints are symmetric in all coordinates). Then, we can claim that
y^{*}_{1}\leq y^{*}_{2}\leq\cdots\leq y^{*}_{K}
, i.e., the coordinates of
y^{*}
are also in a sorted order. The reason is, we can expand the objective of
Equation

31
as:

\displaystyle OBJ

\displaystyle=\Psi(\mathbf{y})-\Psi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})-\langle\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}y_{i}\ln(y_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{K}x^{\prime}_{i}\ln(x^{\prime}_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{K}\ln(x^{\prime}_{i})(y_{i}-x^{\prime}_{i})-1+\lVert\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}y_{i}\cdot(-\ln(x^{\prime}_{i}))+\text{some permutation-invariant quantity of }\mathbf{y}

Then, one can see if
\mathbf{y}
has a pair of coordinates
i<j
but
y_{i}>y_{j}
, then swapping
y_{i}
and
y_{j}
makes the objective function smaller. Therefore, after reducing the optimization problem to
Equation

31
with ascending
x^{\prime}_{i}
’s, there exists
0\leq i\leq K
such that
\beta=y^{*}_{1}=\cdots=y^{*}_{i}=\beta<y^{*}_{i+1}\leq y^{*}_{i+2}\leq\cdots\leq y^{*}_{K}
. To solve
Equation

31
, it suffices to enumerate this boundary index
i=0\ldots K
, then calculate
y^{*}_{i+1},\ldots,y^{*}_{K}
by

y^{*}_{j}=(1-\beta\cdot i)\cdot\frac{x^{\prime}_{j}}{\sum_{k=i+1}^{K}x^{\prime}_{k}}.

If
y^{*}_{i+1}\geq\beta
, we can accept the current
i
as the optimal boundary index, and conclude that
(\beta,\ldots,\beta,y^{*}_{i+1},\ldots,y^{*}_{K})
is the optimizer of
Equation

31
.

Appendix G
High-Level Ideas to Handle Queue Length Increments with Bounded Moments

Here, we explain the intuition behind the design of
SoftMW+
and
SSMW+
. Recall that in the design and analysis of
SoftMW
and
SSMW
, the original assumption of bounded increments is mainly utilized in the following two steps:

•

(
EXP3.S+
feedback signal scale)
In order to apply
Theorem

5.1
, it must be guaranteed that each feedback value
g_{t,a_{t}}
to be fed into
EXP3.S+
is no more than
\eta_{t}^{-1}\gamma_{t}e_{t,a_{t}}
. This is the main reason for us to set
\mathbf{e}_{t}
to the normalized current queue length vector, and set
\gamma_{t}=\eta_{t}\cdot M\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t}\rVert_{1}
in the bounded increment case.

•

(Bounding
EXP3.S+
regret by
\sum\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t}\rVert_{1}
)
After successfully applying
Theorem

5.1
(and possibly summing over
EXP3.S+
instances), we obtain an upper-bound on the expected quadratic Lynapunov function value at the end of time
T
. Compared to the bound we can obtain in the stationary setting with
Max-Weight
via a standard Lyapunov analysis, our bound contains an additional term with a
\sqrt{\sum\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}}
factor. We then apply
Lemma

5.9
for
SoftMW
and
Lemma

6.3
for
SSMW
to upper-bound the
\sqrt{\sum\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}}
by expressions in
\sum\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t}\rVert_{1}
.

Therefore, if we want to handle potentially unbounded queue length increments with only bounded moments, we need to generalize the arguments of the above two steps. Below, we introduce the high-level ideas to overcome the challenge introduced by unbounded increments.

Skipping large feedback values from
EXP3.S+

Suppose we are now at time
t
and choose
\mathbf{e}_{t}=\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}/\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{1}
and
\gamma_{t}=C_{t}\cdot M\eta_{t}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{1}
in the next
EXP3.S+
step, where
C_{t}>0
is some constant to be determined at the end of time
t-1
. Then
EXP3.S+
can handle a new feedback signal no more than
C_{t}\cdot MQ_{t-1,a_{t}}
, where
a_{t}
is the index of the queue we finally choose to serve in this time step. In other words, we can feed the signal to
EXP3
only if
S_{t,a_{t}}\leq C_{t}M
. Since our goal of applying
EXP3.S+
is to make the sum of
\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle-Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S_{t,a_{t}}
small, we can write

\displaystyle\quad\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle-Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S_{t,a_{t}}\leq\mathbbm{1}[S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M]\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle+\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle-Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S^{\prime}_{t,a_{t}}

where

S^{\prime}_{t,i}=\begin{cases}S_{t,i}&\text{if }S_{t,i}\leq C_{t}M\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

is a truncation of
S_{t,i}
. Then,
\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle-Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S^{\prime}_{t,a_{t}}
is a quantity with an expected cumulative sum that can be regarded as the regret of a MAB problem, and thus can be controlled by
EXP3.S+
, if we take
Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S^{\prime}_{t,a_{t}}
as the feedback value. Now there will be a multiplicative factor
\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}(\max_{1\leq t\leq T}C_{t})
in the obtained regret upper-bound.
Also note that

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\mathbbm{1}[S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M]\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\mathbbm{1}[S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M]\sum_{i\neq a_{t}}Q_{t-1,i}S_{t,i}\theta_{t,i}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\mathbbm{1}[S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M]Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S_{t,a_{t}}\theta_{t,a_{t}}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\mathbb{P}\left[\left.S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1},a_{t}\right]\sum_{i\neq a_{t}}Q_{t-1,i}\sigma_{t,i}\theta_{t,i}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\mathbbm{1}[S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M]Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S_{t,a_{t}}\theta_{t,a_{t}}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\left.S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\vec{\sigma}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle+\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\mathbbm{1}[S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M]Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S_{t,a_{t}}\theta_{t,a_{t}}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\left.S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\vec{\sigma}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle+\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\mathbbm{1}[S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M]S_{t,a_{t}}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}C_{t}^{-\alpha}\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\vec{\sigma}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle+C_{t}^{-\alpha+1}M\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{t}^{-\alpha+1}M\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{\infty}.

Here in step
(a)
, we take the conditional expectation of
\mathbbm{1}[S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M]\sum_{i\neq a_{t}}Q_{t-1,i}S_{t,i}\theta_{t,i}
with respect to
a_{t}
before taking the conditional expectation with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
. In step
(b)
, we make use of the fact that
\mathbb{P}\left[\left.S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\leq C_{t}^{-\alpha}
and
\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\mathbbm{1}[S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M]S_{t,a_{t}}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}[S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M]S_{t,a_{t}}^{\alpha}/(C_{t}M)^{\alpha-1}\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\leq(C_{t}M)^{-\alpha+1}\mathbb{E}[S_{t,a_{t}}^{\alpha}\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\leq MC_{t}^{-\alpha+1}
. Thus, the expected cumulative sum of
\mathbbm{1}[S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M]\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle
can also be well-controlled as long as we choose sufficiently small
C_{t}
s.

By choosing appropriate
C_{t}
s, we can trade-off the clipping error
\sum\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\mathbbm{1}[S_{t,a_{t}}>C_{t}M]\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle\right.\right]
and the post-clipping MAB regret
\sum\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle-Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S^{\prime}_{t,a_{t}}\right]
well to make their sum, i.e., the pre-clipping MAB regret
\sum\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle-Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S_{t,a_{t}}\right]
, not too large.

General conversion lemmas between
\sum\lVert\mathbb{Q}_{t}\rVert_{1}
and
\sum\lVert\mathbb{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}

So far we have dealt with issues of unbounded feedback values when applying regret bounds of
EXP3.S+
. It remains to bound
\sum\lVert\mathbb{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}
by
\sum\lVert\mathbb{Q}_{t}\rVert_{1}
so that it can finally solve to an upper-bound of
\sum\lVert\mathbb{Q}_{t}\rVert_{1}
on the whole time-horizon.

In fact,
Lemma

5.9
and
Lemma

6.3
are both
sample-path
bounds for
\sum\lVert\mathbb{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}
which hold under
arbitrary
scheduling policies. Specifically,
Lemma

5.9
states that
\sum\lVert\mathbb{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}
is
\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}(\sum\lVert\mathbb{Q}_{t}\rVert_{1})^{\frac{3}{2}}
on the entire time-horizon, whereas
Lemma

6.3
describes how
\sum\lVert\mathbb{Q}_{t}\rVert_{1}
and
\sum\lVert\mathbb{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}
can be used to bound each other on any successive
m=\Theta(\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}}\rVert_{\infty}/M)
time steps (where
T_{0}
is the first time step of the
m
time steps considered). On the other hand, our analysis actually only needs a relationship between the expectations
\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum\lVert\mathbb{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]
and
\mathbb{E}[\sum\lVert\mathbb{Q}_{t}\rVert_{1}]
. Therefore, there may be room for generalizing the result for queue size increments with bounded moments.

Surprisingly, when one tries to develop expectation versions of
Lemma

5.9
and
Lemma

6.3
, they behave quite differently.
Lemma

6.3
has a direct expectation alternative but this does not seem to be the case for
Lemma

5.9
. Specifically, we can generalize
Lemma

6.3
to
Lemma

G.1
.

Lemma G.1
.

Under Assumption
4
, for any
T_{0}\geq 0
, let
m=\lceil\frac{\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}}\rVert_{\infty}}{2M}\rceil
; further, suppose that
\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}}\rVert_{\infty}\geq 8M
; then

\frac{M}{2}m^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\sum_{t=1}^{m}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}+t-1}\rVert_{1}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]\leq 3KMm^{2},

M^{2}m^{3}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\sum_{t=1}^{m}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}+t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]\leq 5KM^{2}m^{3}.

Thus, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}+t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}+t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{5K}}{\sqrt{m}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}+t-1}\rVert_{1}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right].

Proof.

See
Appendix

H
.
∎

However, we cannot expect to generalize
Lemma

5.9
in the same way. Formally speaking, now our goal would be to obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]\leq\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}\left(T^{a}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{1}}\right]\right)^{b}\right)

(41)

for some
a+b<1
. Recall that to obtain the last statement in
Lemma

G.1
, we simply apply Jensen’s inequality to get
\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\sum\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]}
. But now we want
Equation

41
to hold under any scheduling policy; if we similarly apply Jensen’s inequality, the task would reduce to bounding
\mathbb{E}\left[\sum\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]

unconditionally
, which is impossible since under trivial scheduling policies that cannot stabilize the system, we can have
\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}=\Theta(t)
and thus
\mathbb{E}\left[\sum\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]
is
\Theta(T^{2})
.

It seems quite difficult to directly obtain an expectation bound for
\sqrt{\sum\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}}
without interchanging the square root and the expectation. Nevertheless, we can still utilize
Lemma

5.9
in a somewhat naive way. Let

L\triangleq\max_{1\leq t\leq T,i\in[K]}\lvert Q_{t,i}-Q_{t-1,i}\rvert,

i.e.,
L
is the maximum queue length increment in this sample path; hence, it is a random variable. Then, we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[L>C\right]\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[L\leq C\right]\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[L>C\right]\sqrt{KT^{3}L^{2}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[L\leq C\right]\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{K}T^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[L>C\right]L\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[L\leq C\right]\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right].

For the
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[L>C\right]L\right]
term, recall that
L
is the maximum of
KT
random variables (and they are martingale differences) with bounded
\alpha
-th moments; it can be shown that
L
’s
\alpha
-th moment is no more than
2KTM^{\alpha}
, and
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[L>C\right]L\right]\leq 2KTM^{\alpha}C^{1-\alpha}
. As for the other term, we can apply
Lemma

5.9
to obtain a sample-path bound
\mathbbm{1}\left[L\leq C\right]\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\leq\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}(C^{\frac{1}{4}}(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{1})^{\frac{3}{4}})
. Therefore, when
\alpha
is sufficiently large, we can choose
C
as some power of
T
to conclude
\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]\leq\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}\left(T^{a}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{1}}\right]\right)^{b}\right)+T^{c}
where
0<a,b,c<1
and
a+b<1
.

Proof of
Lemma

G.1
.

From the bouned
\alpha
-th moment assumption for queue length increments, we can also get a bound for the queue length increment itself:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left\lvert Q_{t,i}-Q_{t-1,i}\right\rvert\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left(\left\lvert Q_{t,i}-Q_{t-1,i}\right\rvert^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left\lvert Q_{t,i}-Q_{t-1,i}\right\rvert^{\alpha}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(M^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=M,

where
(a)
is due to the concavity of
x\mapsto x^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
. Similarly, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left(Q_{t,i}-Q_{t-1,i}\right)^{2}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\leq M^{2}.

Then, for any
t\geq T_{0}
and
i\in[K]
, we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left.Q_{t,i}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq Q_{T_{0},i}+\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\sum_{s=T_{0}}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left\lvert Q_{s+1,i}-Q_{s,i}\right\rvert\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq Q_{T_{0},i}+(t-T_{0})M.

If
T_{0}\leq t\leq T_{0}+m
, then we have
\mathbb{E}\left[\left.Q_{t,i}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]\leq Q_{T_{0},i}+Mm
, hence

\displaystyle\sum_{t=T_{0}}^{T_{0}+m-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\lVert Q_{t}\rVert_{1}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=T_{0}}^{T_{0}+m-1}\sum_{i\in[K]}\left(Q_{T_{0},i}+Mm\right)

\displaystyle=m\lVert Q_{T_{0}}\rVert_{1}+KMm^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}m\cdot 2KMm+KMm^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3KMm^{2},

where step
(a)
is due to our choice
m=\left\lceil\frac{\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}}\rVert_{\infty}}{2M}\right\rceil
.

Similarly, for any
T_{0}\leq t\leq T_{0}+m
and
i\in[K]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left.Q_{t,i}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq Q_{T_{0},i}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\sum_{s=T_{0}}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left\lvert Q_{s+1,i}-Q_{s,i}\right\rvert\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq Q_{T_{0},i}-(t-T_{0})M

\displaystyle=Q_{T_{0},i}-Mm,

thus, denoting by
i^{*}=\argmax_{i\in[K]}Q_{T_{0},i}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=T_{0}}^{T_{0}+m-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\lVert Q_{t}\rVert_{1}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{t=T_{0}}^{T_{0}+m-1}Q_{t,i^{*}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{t=T_{0}+1}^{T_{0}+m}\left(Q_{T_{0},i^{*}}-Mm\right)

\displaystyle=m\lVert Q_{T_{0}}\rVert_{\infty}-Mm^{2}

\displaystyle\geq m\cdot(2Mm-2M)-Mm^{2}

\displaystyle=Mm^{2}-2Mm

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}Mm^{2},

where the last step uses the assumption that
\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}}\rVert_{\infty}\geq 8M
, which implies that
m\geq 4
. Therefore, we conclude that

\frac{M}{2}m^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\sum_{t=1}^{m}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}+t-1}\rVert_{1}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]\leq 3KMm^{2}.

For the bound for
\sum\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\left.Q_{t,i}^{2}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq Q_{T_{0},i}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\sum_{s=T_{0}}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left(Q_{s+1,i}-Q_{s,i}\right)^{2}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]+2\sum_{T_{0}\leq s<s^{\prime}<t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left\lvert Q_{s+1,i}-Q_{s,i}\right\rvert\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left\lvert Q_{s^{\prime}+1,i}-Q_{s^{\prime},i}\right\rvert\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{s^{\prime}}\right]\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq Q_{T_{0},i}^{2}+(t-T_{0})M^{2}+2\binom{t-T_{0}}{2}M^{2}

\displaystyle=Q_{T_{0},i}^{2}+(t-T_{0})^{2}M^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Q_{T_{0},i}^{2}+M^{2}m^{2}

for all
T_{0}\leq t\leq T_{0}+m
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{t=T_{0}}^{T_{0}+m-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\lVert Q_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=T_{0}}^{T_{0}+m-1}\sum_{i\in[K]}\left(Q_{T_{0},i}^{2}+M^{2}m^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=m\lVert Q_{T_{0}}\rVert_{2}^{2}+KM^{2}m^{3}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}m\cdot K\cdot(2Mm)^{2}+KM^{2}m^{3}

\displaystyle\leq 5KM^{2}m^{3},

where step
(a)
is due to our choice
m=\left\lceil\frac{\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}}\rVert_{\infty}}{2M}\right\rceil
. For the other direction, we have

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\left.Q_{t,i}^{2}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq Q_{T_{0},i}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\sum_{s=T_{0}}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left(Q_{s+1,i}-Q_{s,i}\right)^{2}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]-2\sum_{T_{0}\leq s<s^{\prime}<t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left\lvert Q_{s+1,i}-Q_{s,i}\right\rvert\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left\lvert Q_{s^{\prime}+1,i}-Q_{s^{\prime},i}\right\rvert\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{s^{\prime}}\right]\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq Q_{T_{0},i}^{2}-(t-T_{0})M^{2}-2\binom{t-T_{0}}{2}M^{2}

\displaystyle=Q_{T_{0},i}^{2}-(t-T_{0})^{2}M^{2}

\displaystyle\geq Q_{T_{0},i}^{2}-M^{2}m^{2}.

and thus denoting by
i^{*}=\argmax_{i\in[K]}Q_{T_{0},i}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=T_{0}}^{T_{0}+m-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\lVert Q_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{t=T_{0}}^{T_{0}+m-1}\left(Q_{T_{0},i^{*}}^{2}-M^{2}m^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=m\lVert Q_{T_{0}}\rVert_{\infty}^{2}-M^{2}m^{3}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\geq}}m\cdot(2Mm-2M)^{2}-M^{2}m^{3}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\geq}}m\cdot\left(\frac{3}{2}Mm\right)^{2}-M^{2}m^{3}

\displaystyle\geq M^{2}m^{3},

where step
(a)
is due to our choice
m=\left\lceil\frac{\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}}\rVert_{\infty}}{2M}\right\rceil
, step
(b)
uses the assumption that
m\geq 4
hence
2M\leq\frac{1}{2}Mm
. Therefore,

M^{2}m^{3}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\sum_{t=1}^{m}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}+t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]\leq 5KM^{2}m^{3}.

∎

.

Suppose Assumption
1
,
2
and
4
hold, then, running
Algorithm

4
guarantees

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle-Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S^{\prime}_{t,a_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[L_{T-1}(1+C_{V})T^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}(4\ln T+\ln K)\sqrt{86L_{T-1}^{2}K^{6}T^{\frac{3}{2}}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[8MT^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{4}}\sqrt{1+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}+4ML_{T-1}\right]

(42)

for any time horizon length
T\geq 1
. Here
S_{t,i}
is defined as

S^{\prime}_{t,i}\triangleq\begin{cases}S_{t,i}&\text{if }S_{t,i}\leq Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},

and
\{\vec{\theta}_{t}\}
is the reference policy in Assumption
1
and
2
.

Proof.

For each
\vec{\theta}_{t}
, we can find a
\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\in\Delta^{[K],\beta_{t}}
so that
\lVert\vec{\theta}_{t}-\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rVert_{1}\leq 2K\beta_{t}
and
\lVert\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{s}-\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rVert_{1}\leq\lVert\vec{\theta}_{s}-\vec{\theta}_{t}\rVert_{1}
. For example, we can choose

\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}=(1-\beta_{t})\vec{\theta}_{t}+\beta_{t}\mathbf{1}.

Then, we can write

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rangle+\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t},\vec{\theta}_{t}-\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rangle+\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t}\rVert_{\infty}\cdot\lVert\vec{\theta}_{t}-\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rangle+L_{t-1}t\cdot Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\cdot 2K\beta_{t}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rangle+2L_{t-1}Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}-3}

\displaystyle\leq\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rangle+2L_{t-1}Mt^{-2}.

Then, one can see the quantity

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rangle-Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S^{\prime}_{t,a_{t}}\right]=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t},\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rangle-(\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t})_{a_{t}}\right]

satisfies the condition to apply
Theorem

5.1
. On the other hand, the total difference of the regret to
\{\vec{\theta}_{t}\}
and the regret to
\{\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\}
is within

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}2L_{t-1}Mt^{-2}

\displaystyle\leq 2ML_{T-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{-2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}ML_{T-1}.

Theorem

5.1
asserts that

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rangle-Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S^{\prime}_{t,a_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\lVert\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t+1}-\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rVert_{1}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\eta_{T}^{-1}\ln\frac{1}{\beta_{T}}\right]+e\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma_{t}\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t},\frac{\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}}{\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{1}}\right\rangle\right].

(43)

Below, we bound each term in the RHS of
Equation

43
. Firstly, we have
\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\lVert\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t+1}-\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rVert_{1}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\lVert\vec{\theta}_{t+1}-\vec{\theta}_{t}\rVert_{1}\leq C_{V}T^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}
according to Assumption
2
, hence

\displaystyle\quad\left(1+\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\lVert\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t+1}-\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rVert_{1}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\eta_{T}^{-1}\ln\frac{1}{\beta_{T}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C_{V}T^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\eta_{T}^{-1}\ln\frac{1}{\beta_{T}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C_{V}T^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\right)\left(4\ln T+\ln K\right)\mathbb{E}\left[T^{-\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}L_{T-1}\sqrt{86L_{T-1}^{2}K^{6}T^{\frac{3}{2}}+\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{s}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C_{V}\right)T^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}\left(4\ln T+\ln K\right)L_{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{86L_{T-1}^{2}K^{6}T^{\frac{3}{2}}+\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{s}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right].

For the second term, we have

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\eta_{t}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t}\rVert_{2}^{2}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\sum_{i\in[K]}Q_{t-i,i}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.S_{t,i}^{\prime 2}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}M^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_{t}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=M\sum_{t=1}^{T}ML_{t-1}^{-1}t^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}\left(\sqrt{86L_{t-1}^{2}K^{6}t^{\frac{3}{2}}+\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{s}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right)^{-1}\cdot\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}M\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}\left(\sqrt{1+\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{s}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right)^{-1}\cdot\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq MT^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\sqrt{1+\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{s}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right)^{-1}\cdot\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\leq}}2MT^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}\sqrt{1+\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{s}\rVert_{2}^{2}},

where in step
(a)
, we apply the second-order moment bound for
S_{t,i}
instead of the clipping threshold
Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}
. Step
(b)
is due to
L_{t}\geq M
for all
t
. In step
(c)
, we use the fact that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}}{\sqrt{1+\sum_{j=1}^{i}x_{j}}}\leq 2\sqrt{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}

for non-negative
x_{1}\ldots,x_{n}
.

The third term can be bounded very similarly to the second term, to be specific, we have

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\gamma_{t}\left\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t},\frac{\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}}{\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{1}}\right\rangle\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}M\sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma_{t}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{1}^{-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq M\sum_{t=1}^{T}ML_{t-1}^{-1}t^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\cdot t^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}\left(\sqrt{1+\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{s}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right)^{-1}\cdot\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq M\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{4}}\left(\sqrt{1+\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{s}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right)^{-1}\cdot\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2MT^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{4}}\sqrt{1+\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{s}\rVert_{2}^{2}},

where in step
(a)
we apply the first-order moment bound for
S_{t,i}
.

Combining everything together then taking expectation, we can see that

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}^{\prime}_{t}\rangle-Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S^{\prime}_{t,a_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C_{V}\right)T^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}\left(4\ln T+\ln K\right)L_{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{86L_{T-1}^{2}K^{6}T^{\frac{3}{2}}+\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{s}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+2MT^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{1+\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{s}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]+2eMT^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{4}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{1+\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{s}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}M\mathbb{E}\left[L_{T-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C_{V}\right)T^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}\left(4\ln T+\ln K\right)L_{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{86L_{T-1}^{2}K^{6}T^{\frac{3}{2}}+\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{s}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+8MT^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{4}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{1+\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{s}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]+4M\mathbb{E}\left[L_{T-1}\right].

∎

Then, we can turn
Lemma

H.2
, the upper-bound for
(\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\cdot\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t})
-objective regret to a
(\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{t})
-objective one:

Lemma H.3
.

Suppose Assumption
1
,
2
and
4
hold, then, running
Algorithm

4
guarantees

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle-Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S_{t,a_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[L_{T-1}(1+C_{V})T^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}(4\ln T+\ln K)\sqrt{86L_{T-1}^{2}K^{6}T^{\frac{3}{2}}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[8MT^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{4}}\sqrt{1+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}+4ML_{T-1}\right]+o(T)

(44)

for any time horizon length
T\geq 1
. Here
\{\vec{\theta}_{t}\}
is the reference policy in Assumption
1
and
2
.

Proof.

Note that for each
t
, the single-step regret
\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle-Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S_{t,a_{t}}
can be upper-bounded by

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle-Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S_{t,a_{t}}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle-Q_{t-1,a_{t}}S^{\prime}_{t,a_{t}}}_{(\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\cdot\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{t})\text{-objective single step regret}}+\mathbbm{1}\left[S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle

where

S^{\prime}_{t,i}\triangleq\begin{cases}S_{t,i}&\text{if }S_{t,i}\leq Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Therefore, it suffices to upper-bound
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle\right]
. In fact,

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\mathbbm{1}\left[S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{\infty}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]\lVert\mathbf{S}_{t}\rVert_{1}\right]

\displaystyle=\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{\infty}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]\sum_{i\in[K]:i\neq a_{t}}S_{t,i}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]S_{t,a_{t}}\right]\right).

For the first term
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]\sum_{i\in[K]:i\neq a_{t}}S_{t,i}\right]
, we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]\sum_{i\in[K]:i\neq a_{t}}S_{t,i}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}\left[\left.S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right\rvert a_{t}\right]\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\sum_{i\in[K]:i\neq a_{t}}S_{t,i}\right\rvert a_{t}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}\left[\left.S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right\rvert a_{t}\right]\cdot\sum_{i\in[K]:i\neq a_{t}}\sigma_{t,i}\right]

\displaystyle\leq KM\cdot\mathbb{P}\left[S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq KM\cdot M^{\alpha}\cdot(Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}})^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle=KMt^{-\frac{\delta\alpha}{4}},

where the last inequality is due to the
\alpha
-th moment upper-bound for
S_{t,a_{t}}
and Chebyshev’s inequality. For the other term
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]S_{t,a_{t}}\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]S_{t,a_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[S_{t,a_{t}}^{\alpha}\cdot(Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}})^{-(\alpha-1)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq Mt^{-\frac{\delta(\alpha-1)}{4}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\mathbbm{1}\left[S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\leq KMt^{-\frac{\delta(\alpha-1)}{4}}\cdot\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{\infty},

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[S_{t,a_{t}}>Mt^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]\langle\mathbf{Q}_{t-1},\mathbf{S}_{t}\odot\vec{\theta}_{t}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\leq KM\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{-\frac{\delta(\alpha-1)}{4}}\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle\leq KM\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{1-\frac{\delta(\alpha-1)}{4}}\mathbb{E}\left[L_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\leq KM\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{1-\frac{\delta(\alpha-1)}{4}}\mathbb{E}\left[L_{t}^{\alpha}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq 2K^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}M^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{1-\frac{\delta(\alpha-1)}{4}}\cdot t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq o(T)

where the last step is due to the assumption of
\delta\cdot\alpha>7
.
∎

Compared to
Lemma

5.7
, the RHS of the bound in
Lemma

H.3
now involves
L_{T-1}
, the sample-path maximum queue length increment. Now we will do a further calculation to get rid of the
L_{T-1}
factors.

Recall that
L_{t}
is defined as

L_{t}\triangleq\max\left\{M,\max_{1\leq s\leq t,i\in[K]}\left\lvert Q_{s,i}-Q_{s-1,i}\right\rvert\right\}

in
Algorithm

4
, the bounded-moment-queue-length increment assumption gives an upper-bound for the
\alpha
-th moment of
L_{t}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[L_{t}^{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{M^{\alpha},\sum_{s=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert Q_{s,i}-Q_{s-1,i}\right\rvert^{\alpha}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{M^{\alpha},2KTM^{\alpha}\right\}

\displaystyle=2KTM^{\alpha}.

This moment bound for
L_{t}
enables us to deal with the
L_{T-1}
factor in
Lemma

H.3
. For example, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[L_{T-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[L_{T-1}^{\alpha}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\leq 2K^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}MT^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}=o(T).

To bound the
\mathbb{E}\left[L_{T-1}(1+C_{V})T^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}(4\ln T+\ln K)\sqrt{86L_{T-1}^{2}K^{6}T^{\frac{3}{2}}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]
term, we first simply write

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[L_{T-1}(1+C_{V})T^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}(4\ln T+\ln K)\sqrt{86L_{T-1}^{2}K^{6}T^{\frac{3}{2}}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[L_{T-1}(1+C_{V})T^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}(4\ln T+\ln K)\cdot\left(\sqrt{86L_{T-1}^{2}K^{6}T^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[K^{3}(1+C_{V})T^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}(4\ln T+\ln K)L_{T-1}^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[(1+C_{V})T^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}(4\ln T+\ln K)L_{T-1}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]

and then bound the two terms one by one. The
L_{T-1}^{2}
term is easy:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[K^{3}(1+C_{V})T^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}(4\ln T+\ln K)L_{T-1}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq K^{3}(1+C_{V})T^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}(4\ln T+\ln K)\mathbb{E}\left[L_{T-1}^{\alpha}\right]^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq K^{3}(1+C_{V})T^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}(4\ln T+\ln K)\cdot 2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}M^{2}T^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}(T^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{2}{\alpha}})

\displaystyle\leq o(T)

where the last step is due to the assumption that
\delta\cdot\alpha>7
. For the other term, we can write

\displaystyle\quad L_{T-1}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{1}\left[L_{T-1}\leq MT^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]L_{T-1}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}+\mathbbm{1}\left[L_{T-1}>MT^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]L_{T-1}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}.

(45)

For the term with factor
\mathbbm{1}\left[L_{T-1}\leq MT^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]
in
Equation

45
, we can apply
Lemma

5.9
to get

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}\left[L_{T-1}\leq MT^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]L_{T-1}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{1}\left[L_{T-1}\leq MT^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]2L_{T-1}^{\frac{5}{4}}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{1}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq 2M^{\frac{5}{4}}T^{\frac{5}{16}\delta}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{1}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}.

For the other term with factor
\mathbbm{1}\left[L_{T-1}>MT^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]
, we can write

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}\left[L_{T-1}>MT^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]L_{T-1}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{1}\left[L_{T-1}>MT^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]L_{T-1}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}K(L_{T-1}T)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{K}T^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathbbm{1}\left[L_{T-1}>MT^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]L_{T-1}^{2},

besides, we can bound its expectation by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[L_{T-1}>MT^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]L_{T-1}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[L_{T-1}^{\alpha}/(MT^{\frac{\delta}{4}})^{\alpha-2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2KTM^{\alpha}\cdot(MT^{\frac{\delta}{4}})^{-(\alpha-2)}

\displaystyle\leq 2KM^{2}T^{1-\frac{\alpha-2}{4}\delta},

hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\left[L_{T-1}>MT^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]L_{T-1}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{K}T^{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot 2KM^{2}T^{1-\frac{\alpha-2}{4}\delta}

\displaystyle=2K^{\frac{3}{2}}M^{2}T^{\frac{5}{2}-\frac{\alpha-2}{4}\delta}

\displaystyle=2K^{\frac{3}{2}}M^{2}T^{\frac{10+2\delta-\delta\alpha}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq 2K^{\frac{3}{2}}M^{2}T^{\frac{11-\delta\alpha}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq o(T)

where the last inequality is due to the assumption that
\delta\cdot\alpha>7
.

Combining bounds for all terms, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[(1+C_{V})T^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}(4\ln T+\ln K)L_{T-1}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq o(T)+\mathbb{E}\left[(1+C_{V})T^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}(4\ln T+\ln K)\cdot\mathbbm{1}\left[L_{T-1}\leq MT^{\frac{\delta}{4}}\right]L_{T-1}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{2}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq o(T)+(1+C_{V})T^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}}(4\ln T+\ln K)\cdot 2M^{\frac{5}{4}}T^{\frac{5}{16}\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{1}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}\right]

\displaystyle=o(T)+2(1+C_{V})M^{\frac{5}{4}}T^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{3}{16}\delta}(4\ln T+\ln K)\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{t-1}\rVert_{1}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}\right].

It remains to bound the

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left.Q_{T_{0}+\tau-1,a_{t}}^{2}S_{T_{0}+\tau,a_{t}}^{2}\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]\cdot\left(Mm^{\frac{\delta}{3}}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}}\rVert_{\infty}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=5M^{2}m^{1-\frac{\delta}{3}}.

Therefore, combining the different parts of bounds, we get

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\tau=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\mathbbm{1}\left[Q_{T_{0}+\tau-1,a_{T_{0}+\tau}}S_{T_{0}+\tau,a_{T_{0}+\tau}}>Mm^{\frac{\delta}{3}}\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}}\rVert_{\infty}\right]\langle\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}+\tau-1},\mathbf{S}_{T_{0}+\tau}\odot\vec{\theta}_{T_{0}+\tau}\rangle\right\rvert\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 5\sqrt{5}(K-1)M^{2}m^{2-\frac{2}{3}\delta}+5M^{2}m^{2-\frac{\delta}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq 12KM^{2}m^{2-\frac{\delta}{3}}.

Putting this regret difference bound with the
\mathbf{g}_{T_{0}+\tau}
-objective regret bound, we get

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{t=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\langle\mathbf{Q}_{T_{0}+t-1}\odot\mathbf{S}_{T_{0}+t},\vec{\theta}_{T_{0}+t}\rangle-Q_{T_{0}+t-1,a_{T_{0}+t}}S_{T_{0}+t,a_{T_{0}+t}}\right|\mathcal{F}_{T_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 9(1+C_{V})M^{3}Km^{2-\frac{\delta}{3}}\cdot\left(3\ln m+\ln K\right)+4M^{2}+12KM^{2}m^{2-\frac{\delta}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq 21(1+C_{V})M^{3}Km^{2-\frac{\delta}{3}}\cdot\left(3\ln m+\ln K\right)+4M^{2}.

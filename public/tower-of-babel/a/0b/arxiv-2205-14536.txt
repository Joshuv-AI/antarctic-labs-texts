Configurational microcanonical statistical mechanics from Riemannian geometry of equipotenital level sets
Matteo Gori

Abstract
In the present work, we present a detailed discussion of a Riemannian metric structure originally introduced in [Gori et al., J. Stat. Mech. , 9 093204 (2018)] on the configuration space and on phase space allowing to interpret the derivatives of the configurational microcanonical entropy and of the canonical entropy in terms of integrals of extrinsic geoemtrical quantities associated to the equipotential level sets.



I
Introduction

In the last decades, a field of research has been
established aiming at investigating how thermodynamic
properties of classical systems arise from
the geometrical properties of the equipotential level sets in configuration space
Pettini 2007
;
Franzosi 2011
. For instance, the so-called Topological Theory (TT) on the
origin of phase transitions (PT) in classical systems,
developed in the last three decades, represents one of the
conceptual frameworks that have contributed to
orienting the research in this field and have provided
a generalization of the statistical mechanical description
of phase transitions in small or mesoscopic systems.
According to TT, the singularities of thermodynamic
potentials - arising in the thermodynamic limit in the
canonical and grancanonical ensembles - would be induced by
suitable topological changes of some submanifolds of
configuration space.
These same topological changes can occur for any finite number of degrees of freedom. This theory, supported by
many pieces of evidence ranging from numerical simulations to exact
analytic computations carried on different statistical
models have been rigorously rooted in two
theorems that associate topological changes of the
equipotential level sets of configuration space with the
loss of analyticity of microcanonical configuration
entropy.
In particular, one of these theorems named
Necessity
theorem
, states that in its original formulation that if
all the equipotential level sets in a certain interval of
specific potential energy
\displaystyle[\bar{v}_{1},\bar{v}_{2}]
are
diffeomorphic among them at any finite, then the system
cannot undergo any phase transition in the corresponding
interval of temperatures for short-range regular potential.
According to Morse Theory, this statement can be rephrased
as follows: the absence of critical points of the potential
energy in the specific potential energy
\displaystyle[\bar{v}_{1},\bar{v}_{2}]
at any finite N implies the absence of phase
transition in the corresponding interval of temperatures.
In the last decade, a counterexample to the original
formulation of the
Necessity Theorem
has been
found: for the
\displaystyle\phi^{4}
model on a 2D lattice and nearest
neighbors interactions, there are no critical points of
the potential energy for the critical value of the specific potential energy
\displaystyle\bar{v}_{c}
corresponding to
the second-order phase transition observed at the critical
temperature
\displaystyle T_{c}
in the thermodynamic limit.
These findings oriented the research towards a refinement of the
topological theory leading to a new formulation of the

Necessity Theorem
including the hypothesis of asymptotic
diffeomorphicity of equipotential level sets in thermodynamic
limit. In the conceptualization process leading to such a
development of the Topological Theory (TT), it appears to be a key step
to search for a clear connection between the Riemannian geometric
properties of the regular equipotential level sets and the
derivatives of the configurational microcanonical entropy with
respect to specific potential energy. The natural kinetic energy metric in configuration space does not provide a suited framework for this purpose.

In the present work, we present a method to define a Riemannian
metric structure both in configuration space in the absence
of critical points of potential energy allowing to provide clear
identification of the derivatives of the configurational
microcanonical entropy with integrals of extrinsic geometric
curvature over equipotential (isoenergetic) level sets.

Definition II.1

(Equipotential level sets)
.

We recall that the equipotential level sets
\displaystyle\Sigma_{\bar{v}}^{\overline{V}_{N}}
are defined as

\Sigma_{\bar{v}}^{\overline{V}_{N}}=\left\{q\in\Lambda_{q}|\overline{V}_{N}(q)=\overline{v}\right\}

(4)

Remark 1

(Compactness of level sets)
.

As
\displaystyle\overline{V}_{N}\in C^{\infty}(\Lambda_{q})
is a continuous function then

\displaystyle\overline{V}_{N}^{-1}(\overline{v})\subseteq\Lambda_{q}
is a compact set.

Let the gradient vector field
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{grad}}_{g}\overline{V}_{N}\in\mathfrak{X}(\Lambda_{q})
of the function
\displaystyle\overline{V}_{N}
be

g(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{grad}}_{g}\overline{V}_{N},\mathbf{X})=\mathrm{d}\overline{V}_{N}(X)\qquad\forall\mathbf{X}\in\mathfrak{X}(\Lambda_{q})\,\,.

(5)

Remark 2

(Non critical level set)
.

If there are no critical points of
\displaystyle\overline{V}_{N}
on the level set
\displaystyle\Sigma_{\overline{v}}^{\overline{V}_{N}}
, i.e.:

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{grad}}_{g}\overline{V}_{N}\Bigr|_{\mathbf{q}}\neq\mathbf{0}\qquad\forall q\in\Sigma_{\overline{v}}

(6)

then
\displaystyle\Sigma_{\overline{v}}^{\overline{V}_{N}}
is a regular compact hypersurface.

Let consider a configuration space subset where there is no
critical point, i.e.

\mathcal{B}_{N}=\left\{q\in\Lambda_{q}|\ \boldsymbol{\mathrm{grad}}_{g}\overline{V}\Bigr|_{q}\neq\mathbf{0}\right\}.

(7)

and the set

M_{\left[\overline{v}_{0},\overline{v}_{1}\right]}^{\overline{V}_{N}}=\bigcup_{\bar{v}\in\left[\bar{v}_{0},\bar{v}_{1}\right]}\Sigma_{\bar{v}}^{\overline{V}_{N}}

(8)

and suppose that exist some
\displaystyle\overline{v}_{0},\overline{v}_{1}
such that
\displaystyle M_{\left[\bar{v}_{0},\bar{v}_{1}\right]}^{\overline{V}_{N}}\subset B
. This means that the one-form
\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\overline{V}_{N}
is non-degenerate over
\displaystyle M_{\left[\bar{v}_{0},\bar{v}_{1}\right]}^{\overline{V}_{N}}
.

It follows from Froebenius’ Theorem that a co-dimension one foliation can be defined
on
\displaystyle M_{\left[\bar{v}_{0},\bar{v}_{1}\right]}^{\overline{V}_{N}}
through regular level sets of
\displaystyle\overline{V}_{N}
. Hence, it is possible to define a unit normal vector field to the
equipotential hypersurfaces (the leaves of the foliation):

\boldsymbol{\nu}_{N}=\dfrac{\mathbf{grad}_{g}\overline{V}_{N}}{\|\mathbf{grad}_{g}\overline{V}_{N}\|_{g}}

(9)

We stress that the absence of critical points of
\displaystyle\overline{V}_{N}
over the manifold

\displaystyle M_{\left[\overline{v}_{0},\overline{v}_{1}\right]}^{\overline{V}_{N}}
has important consequences on the topology of the level sets therein: in particular, we will use the following well known result in differential topology:

Theorem II.1

(Regular interval theorem(
Hirsch 1997
, p.153))
.

Let
\displaystyle f:M\longrightarrow\left[a,b\right]
be a
\displaystyle C^{r+1}
map on a compact manifold
\displaystyle 1\leq r\leq\omega
(where
\displaystyle\omega
means analytical). Suppose
\displaystyle f
has no critical points and
\displaystyle f(\partial M)=\{a,b\}
. Then all level surfaces of
\displaystyle f
are diffeomorphic.

In the proof of the same theorem in
Hirsch 1997
, an explicit formulation is given for the vector field that generates the diffeomorphisms among the level sets and it is parametrized by the values taken by
\displaystyle f
on them.

If the function
\displaystyle f
is identified with
\displaystyle\overline{V}_{N}
(as already mentioned in the previous Section) the vector field that generates the diffeomorphisms among the level sets, parametrized by
\displaystyle\bar{v}
, is

\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{N}=\dfrac{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{grad}}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\overline{V}_{N}}{\|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{grad}}_{g_{\mathbb{E}^{N}}}\overline{V}_{N}\|^{2}_{g_{\mathbb{E}^{N}}}}=\overline{\chi}_{N}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{N}\qquad\boldsymbol{\xi}_{N}\in\mathfrak{X}(M_{[v_{0},v_{1}]}^{N})\,.

(10)

where we have introduced the symbol
\displaystyle\overline{\chi}_{N}
for the norm of vector field
\displaystyle\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{N}
in the ambient space (
\displaystyle\Lambda_{q}
,
\displaystyle g
), i.e.:

\overline{\chi}_{N}=\dfrac{1}{\|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{grad}}_{g}\overline{V}_{N}\|_{g}}=\|\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{N}\|_{g}\,\,.

(11)

This means that the diffeomorphic flow generated by
\displaystyle\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{N}
is normal to the level sets and is parametrized by the differences of
\displaystyle\overline{V}_{N}
along the flow lines

\mathrm{d}\overline{V}_{N}(\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{N})=1\,

(12)

More in explicit, this means that there exists a diffeomorphism flow

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fl}:\Sigma_{\bar{v}_{0}}^{\overline{V}_{N}}\times[0;\bar{v}_{1}-\bar{v}_{0}]\rightarrow M_{[\bar{v}_{0},\bar{v}_{1}]}^{\overline{V}_{N}}
among the level
sets, s.t.

\begin{cases}\mathrm{Fl}_{t}(p)\in\Sigma_{\bar{v}_{0}+t}^{\overline{V}_{N}}\\
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(f\circ\mathrm{Fl}_{t}\right)(p)\Biggr|_{t=(\bar{v}-\bar{v}_{0})}=\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{N}f\right)(\mathrm{Fl}_{(\bar{v}-\bar{v}_{0})}(p))\qquad\forall p\in\Sigma_{\bar{v}_{0}}^{\overline{V}_{N}}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\forall\bar{v}\in[\bar{v}_{0};\bar{v}_{1}]\\
\end{cases}

(13)

where
\displaystyle f
is an arbitrary function of class
\displaystyle\mathit{C}^{1}
defined over an open set of
\displaystyle\Lambda_{q}
containing
\displaystyle M_{[\bar{v}_{0},\bar{v}_{1}]}^{\overline{V}_{N}}
.

In this differential topological framework it is possible
to express the microcanonical entropy and its derivatives in terms of integral of quantities related only to the vector field
\displaystyle\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{N}

\displaystyle M_{[\overline{v}_{0},\overline{v}_{1}]}^{\overline{V}_{N}}

\displaystyle\bar{v}

Theorem II.3

(Derivation of integral over regular level sets)
.

Let
\displaystyle O
an open bounded set of a Riemannian manifold
\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}^{N},g)
with a connection
\displaystyle\nabla
. Let
\displaystyle\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{p+1}(\overline{O})
be constant on each connected component of the boundary
\displaystyle\partial\overline{O}
and
\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{C}^{p}(O)
. Define
\displaystyle M_{]t,t^{\prime}[}^{N}=\left\{x\in O|t<\psi(x)<t^{\prime}\right\}
and

F(v)=\int_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi}}\,f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi},g}

(19)

where
\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi},g}
is the Riemannian area
\displaystyle N-1
-form induced over
\displaystyle\Sigma_{v}^{\psi}
. If
\displaystyle C>0
exists such that for any
\displaystyle M_{]t,t^{\prime}[}^{\psi}
,
\displaystyle\|\mathbf{grad}_{g}\psi(x)\|_{g}\geq C
and the level sets
\displaystyle\Sigma_{v}^{\psi}
of
\displaystyle\psi
are without boundary, then for any
\displaystyle k
such that
\displaystyle 0\leq k\leq p
, for any
\displaystyle v\in]t,t^{\prime}[
, one has

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{k}F}{\mathrm{d}v^{k}}(v)=\int_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi}}\,A_{\psi,g}^{k}f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi},g}

(20)

with

A_{\psi,g}f=\mathrm{div}_{g}\left(\boldsymbol{\nu}f\right)\dfrac{1}{\|\mathbf{grad}_{g}\psi(x)\|_{g}}\qquad\boldsymbol{\nu}=\dfrac{\mathbf{grad}_{g}\psi(x)}{\|\mathbf{grad}_{g}\psi(x)\|_{g}}

(21)

proof
.

We prove the formula at the first order of derivation, namely for
\displaystyle k=1

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}F}{\mathrm{d}v}(v)=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}v}\int_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi}}\,A_{\psi,g}f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi},g}

(22)

as the case for
\displaystyle k>1
can be obtained by recursion.

The absence of critical points of
\displaystyle\psi
implies that the level sets
\displaystyle\Sigma_{v}^{\psi}
of
\displaystyle\psi
determine a foliation of the open manifold
\displaystyle M_{]t,t^{\prime}[}^{N}
. Moreover all the level sets are diffeomorphic by after
Theorem II.1
and a
vector field
\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
generating a family of one-parameter group of diffeomorphisms
\displaystyle\mathrm{Fl}_{t}
parametrized by differences of values of
\displaystyle\psi
can be found, i.e.

\boldsymbol{\xi}=\dfrac{\mathbf{grad}_{g}\psi(x)}{\|\mathbf{grad}_{g}\psi(x)\|_{g}^{2}}=\chi\boldsymbol{\nu}\qquad\chi=\dfrac{1}{\|\mathbf{grad}_{g}\psi(x)\|_{g}}.

(23)

In order to pass the derivative into the integral in eq.(
22
)
we use the transport property of integral under the action of the one-parameter group of diffeomorphisms:

\begin{split}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}F}{\mathrm{d}v}(v)&=\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{\displaystyle{\int_{\mathrm{Fl}_{s}(\Sigma_{v}^{\psi})}\,f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi},g}-\int_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi}}\,f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{f},g}}}{s}=\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi}}\,\dfrac{\mathrm{Fl}^{*}_{s}(f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi},g})-(f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi},g})}{s}=\\
&=\int_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi},g})=\int_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi}}\,\chi\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(f\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi},g})=\int_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi}}\,\chi\left[\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(f)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi},g}+f\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi},g}))\right]\end{split}

(24)

where we have used the definition of the Lie derivative
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
of forms with respect to the vector field
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\xi}
, and we used its linearity with respect to reparametrization of
the one-parameter group of diffeomorphisms.
As

\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi},g})=\mathrm{Tr}^{g}(\mathrm{II})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi},g}=\tau_{1,g}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi},g}\,\,,

(25)

where
\displaystyle\mathrm{II}_{g}
is the second fundamental form of the hypersurface
\displaystyle\Sigma_{v}^{\psi}

and
\displaystyle\tau_{1,g}
is the sum of principal curvatures (see

),
the last expression in eq.(

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}F}{\mathrm{d}v}(v)=\int_{\Sigma_{v}^{\psi}}\,\chi\left[\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(f)+f\tau_{1,g}\right]\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma_{v}^{N},g}\,.

(26)

In order to complete the proof it is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{g}(f\boldsymbol{\nu})
is equal to the expression in square brackets in eq.(
26
). Let us choos an adapted orthonormal frame

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\nu},\boldsymbol{e}_{1},...,\boldsymbol{e}_{N-1})
to the regular set
\displaystyle\Sigma_{v}^{\psi}

\begin{split}\mathrm{div}_{g}(f\boldsymbol{\nu})&=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}g(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{e}_{i}}(f\boldsymbol{\nu}),\boldsymbol{e}_{i})+g(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(f\boldsymbol{\nu}),\boldsymbol{\nu})=\\

Remark 6

IV
Conclusions

In the present work we have discussed how it is possible to encode
the full information on the thermodynamic behavior of a
given system into a riemannian geometric structure defined in
subspaces of configuration space where equipotential level sets define a regular foliation.

This result could in principle pave the way for new approaches to
study how phase transition in microcanonical ensemble are related
with the geometry and topolpogy of equipotential level sets. Some of
these new developments are presented in a recent paper

Di Cairano 2022
where it is studied in details the interplay
between the riemannian curvatures of the configuration space and the
rate expansion of the configurational microcanonical volume
\displaystyle W_{N}(\bar{v})
.

In order to give a geometrical interpretation of Riemann curvature, let us introduce the concept
geodesic curve
as the shortest smooth curve that connects two points on a Riemannian manifold; a variational formulation of this condition for a geodesic
\displaystyle\gamma=(x^{1}(t)\ldots x^{N}(t)))
is given by:

\Delta_{\dot{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}\dot{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\ddot{x}^{i}+\Gamma^{i}_{kj}\ddot{x}^{j}\ddot{x}^{k}\,.

(140)

Now let us consider a smooth one parameter family of geodesic
\displaystyle\gamma_{\tau}
with
\displaystyle\gamma_{0}(t)=\gamma(t)
than the
Jacobi vector field

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}
along the geodesic
\displaystyle\gamma(t)
is defined as

\mathbf{J}=\partial\gamma_{\tau}(t)\Bigr|_{\tau=0}

(141)

The Riemann curvature tensor gives a measure of the local geodesic spread. The evolution of the Jacobi field along a given geodesic is described by the
Jacobi-Levi-Civita equation
. Let us suppose that along the geodesic it is consider a orthonormal frame
\displaystyle\{\mathbf{e}_{i}\}_{i=1,...,N}
in
\displaystyle T_{\gamma(t)}\mathcal{M}
, parallel transported all along
\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{e}_{i}=0
and with
\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{1}=\dot{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}/\|\dot{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\|_{g}
than if the jacobi vector field is expressed
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{J}=y^{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}
the Jacobi Levi Civita for the geodesic spread reads

\ddot{y}^{k}+\|\dot{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\|^{2}_{g}\mathrm{R}^{k}_{1j1}y^{j}=0\,.

(142)

The contraction
\displaystyle C_{1,2}(R)
of the Riemann curvature tensor is the
Ricci curvature tensor

\mathrm{Ric}_{ij}=R^{k}_{ikj}=\partial_{l}\Gamma^{l}_{ji}-\partial_{j}\Gamma^{l}_{li}+\Gamma^{l}_{lk}\Gamma^{k}_{ji}-\Gamma^{l}_{jk}\Gamma^{k}_{il}\,.

(143)

For a given metric
\displaystyle g
the Ricci curvature gives a local measure of the difference between the volume form with respect to an euclidean metric
\displaystyle g_{\mathbb{E}}
, i.e. if in a certain point
\displaystyle g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+o\left(\|\mathbf{X}\|_{g}^{2}\right)
with
\displaystyle\mathbf{X}=X^{i}\boldsymbol{\partial}_{i}

\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol}_{g}=\mathrm{d}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{\mathbb{E}}}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{6}\mathrm{Ric}_{jk}X^{k}X^{j}+o\left(\|\mathbf{X}\|_{g}^{3}\right)\right)

(144)

so in the directions
\displaystyle\mathbf{X}
such that
\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}(\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{X})
is positive the volume is contracted with respect to the Euclidean volume.

The metric-dependent trace of the Ricci curvature tensor is the
Scalar curvature

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=\mathrm{Tr}^{g}\mathrm{Ric}=g^{ij}\mathrm{Ric}_{ij}
.

Appendix E
Riemmanian geometry of codimension one submanifolds (regular level sets)

Let
\displaystyle(\mathcal{M},\bar{g})
be a
\displaystyle N
-dimensional Riemannian manifold whose Levi-Civita commection is
\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}
. A
regular Submanifold
of dimension
\displaystyle K
is a subset
\displaystyle\Sigma
such that for every point
\displaystyle p\in\Sigma
such that for a certain chart
\displaystyle(U,x)
over
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}
it holds
\displaystyle x\left(U\cap Sigma\right)=x(U)\cup{\mathbb{R}^{K}\times\{c\}}
for some
\displaystyle c\in\mathbb{R}^{N-K}
, that means that exists a chart over
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}
such that for its restriction over
\displaystyle\Sigma
there are
\displaystyle N-K
fixed components. In particular,
the set defined as a locus that in a chart
\displaystyle(U,x)
reads
\displaystyle\Sigma=\left\{p\in\mathcal{M}|f_{A}(x^{1}(p),...,x_{N}(p))\right\}=0\qquad\text{for}\,\,A=1,...,L
it is a
\displaystyle K
-dimensional level sets (with
\displaystyle K\leq L
) if the rank of the Jacobian
\displaystyle\partial_{i}f_{A}
is
\displaystyle K
.

The equipotential level sets discussed in the first part of this manuscript are an example of regular submanifold without boundaries in absence of critical point of potential energy.
Moreover in this specific case, we have a local
regular foliation

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
of the configuration space, namely when the ambient manifolds can be regarded as the disjoint union of connected regular submanifolds
\displaystyle\Sigma_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-K}}
called
leaves
of dimension
\displaystyle K
(and co-dimension
\displaystyle N-K
) such that in a neighbourhood of any point of the ambient space exists a chart
\displaystyle(U,x)
such where the coordinate can be expressed in the form
\displaystyle(a^{1},...,a^{N-K},x^{1},...,x^{K})
. In what follows we consider level sets of one single function so that
\displaystyle K=N-1
.

As we supposed that the ambient space has a Riemannian structure, each leaf inherits
a metric structure, the so called
First Fundamental Form

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}:T_{p}\Sigma\times T_{p}\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined over a regular submanifold of co-dimension one

\mathrm{I}_{\Sigma}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}):\equiv\bar{g}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})\qquad\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}\in T_{p}\Sigma

(145)

and it coincides with the restriction of metric tensor
\displaystyle\bar{g}
on the immersed co-dimension one submanifold
\displaystyle\Sigma
. This allows to define a Levi-Civita connection
\displaystyle\nabla
over the regular submanifold. In order to introduce a more suitable notation for a fixed
\displaystyle\Sigma
, we will use in what follows
\displaystyle g=\mathrm{I}
.

Moreover, for a co-dimension one regular submanifold
\displaystyle\Sigma
in a ambient space
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}
it is possible to define a
normal vector field
as the vector
field
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nu}\neq\mathbf{0}
such that
\displaystyle\bar{g}(\boldsymbol{\nu},\mathbf{X})=0
for every
\displaystyle\mathbf{X}\in T_{p}\Sigma
.

The rate of (covariant) variation of the normal vector field in a direction tangent to the submanifolds defines is intuitively related with the concept of curvature for a
immersed submanifold. This is can be formalized as follows. First of all let us consider the
Weingarten operator or Shape Operator

\displaystyle W_{\boldsymbol{\nu}_{p}}:T_{p}\Sigma\rightarrow T_{p}\Sigma
:

W_{\boldsymbol{\nu}_{p}}(X):\equiv\overline{\nabla}_{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\nu}\bigr|_{p}\qquad\mathbf{X}\in T_{p}\Sigma\,\,\,\,\,.

(146)

This operator can be regarded as
\displaystyle(1,1)
-rank tensor field over he submanifold tangent space; the induced metric structure over the submanifold allows to construct a
\displaystyle(0,2)
-rank tensor field over this submanifold called
Second Fundamental Form

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}_{\Sigma}:T_{p}\Sigma\times T_{p}\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\mathrm{II}_{\Sigma}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})=\bar{g}(W_{\nu}(\mathbf{X}),\mathbf{Y})=\bar{g}(\nabla_{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\nu},\mathbf{Y})=-\bar{g}(\boldsymbol{\nu},\nabla_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{Y})\qquad\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}\in T_{p}\Sigma

(147)

The second fundamental form is symmetric in its arguments as:

\begin{split}\mathrm{II}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})&=\bar{g}(\overline{\nabla}_{\mathbf{X}}\boldsymbol{\nu},\mathbf{Y})=-\bar{g}(\boldsymbol{\nu},\overline{\nabla}_{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{Y})=-\bar{g}(\boldsymbol{\nu},\overline{\nabla}_{\mathbf{Y}}\mathbf{X})-\bar{g}(\boldsymbol{\nu},\left[\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}\right])=\\
&=\bar{g}(\overline{\nabla}_{\mathbf{Y}}\boldsymbol{\nu},\mathbf{X})=\mathrm{II}(\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X})\qquad\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}\in T_{p}\Sigma\end{split}

(148)

The eigenvalues
\displaystyle(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})
of the Weingarten operator are called
principal curvatures
and the metric-dependent trace of shape operator is called
mean curvature
:

h_{\Sigma,\bar{g}}=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\lambda_{i,\bar{g}}}{N-1}=\dfrac{\mathrm{Tr}^{g}(\mathrm{II})}{N-1}=\dfrac{\tau_{1,\bar{g}}}{N-1}

(149)

We derive a formula for the variation of mean curvature along the normal direction, in a coordinate system
\displaystyle(x^{0},x^{1},...,x^{N-1})
, where
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\partial}_{0}=\boldsymbol{\nu}
, for a co-dimension one regular foliation

\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(\tau_{1,\bar{g}})=\overline{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\left(g^{ij}\mathrm{II}_{ij}\right)=\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(g^{ij})\mathrm{II}_{ij}+g^{ij}\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\left(\mathrm{II}_{ij}\right)\qquad i,j=1,...,N-1

(150)

We note that

\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\left(g^{ij}g_{jk}\right)=0

(151)

from what follows

\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}g^{ij}=-g^{jk}g^{il}\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(g_{kl})\,.

(152)

The last term can be calculated considering that the action of Lie derivative and covariant differentiation coincide on functions

\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(g_{kl})=\overline{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\left(\bar{g}(\mathbf{e}_{k},\mathbf{e}_{l})\right)=2(\overline{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{\bar{g}}\mathbf{e}_{k},\mathbf{e}_{l})=2(\overline{\nabla}_{\mathbf{e}_{k}}\boldsymbol{\nu},\mathbf{e}_{l})=2\mathrm{II}(\mathbf{e}_{k},\mathbf{e}_{l})=2\mathrm{II}_{kl}

(153)

so that substituting the last expression in (
151
) we obtain

\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}g^{ij}=-2\mathrm{II}^{ij}

(154)

and the first term of right-side in eq. (
150
) results

\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(g^{ij})\mathrm{II}_{ij}=-2\mathrm{II}^{ij}\mathrm{II}_{ij}=-2(\mathrm{II}^{2})^{i}_{i}=-2\mathrm{Tr}^{g}\left(\mathrm{II}^{2}\right)\left(=-2\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\lambda_{1,\bar{g}}^{2}:=-2\tau_{2,\bar{g}}\right)

(155)

Let us now consider the Lie derivative of second fundamental form along the normal field.

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\mathrm{II}_{ij}&=\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\mathrm{II}(\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{j})=\overline{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\bar{g}(\nabla_{\mathbf{e}_{i}}^{\bar{g}}\boldsymbol{\nu},\mathbf{e}_{j})=\bar{g}(\overline{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{\bar{g}}\overline{\nabla}_{\mathbf{e}_{i}}\boldsymbol{\nu},\mathbf{e}_{j})+\bar{g}(\overline{\nabla}_{\mathbf{e}_{i}}\boldsymbol{\nu},\overline{\nabla}_{\mathbf{\nu}}\boldsymbol{e}_{j})=\\
&=\bar{g}\left(\bar{R}(\boldsymbol{\nu},\mathbf{e}_{i})\boldsymbol{\nu},\mathbf{e}_{j}\right)+\bar{g}(\overline{\nabla}_{\mathbf{e}_{i}}\overline{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\boldsymbol{\nu},\mathbf{e}_{j})+\bar{g}(\left[\boldsymbol{\nu},\mathbf{e}_{i}\right],\mathbf{e}_{j})+\bar{g}(\overline{\nabla}_{\mathbf{e}_{i}}\boldsymbol{\nu},\overline{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\mathbf{e}_{j})\\
\end{split}

(156)

where
\displaystyle R
is the Riemannian curvature tensor of the ambient space. Under the hypothesis that
\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\boldsymbol{\nu}=0
and using the antisymmetrical properties of the Riemann tensor we obtain:

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\mathrm{II}_{ij}=-\bar{g}\left(\bar{R}(\mathbf{e}_{i},\boldsymbol{\nu})\boldsymbol{\nu},\mathbf{e}_{j}\right)+\bar{g}(\overline{\nabla}_{\mathbf{e}_{i}}\boldsymbol{\nu},\overline{\nabla}_{\mathbf{e}_{j}}\boldsymbol{\nu})=-\bar{R}_{j0i0}+\mathrm{W}^{k}_{i}\mathrm{W}^{l}_{j}\bar{g}(\mathbf{e}_{k},\mathbf{e}_{l})=\\
&=-\bar{R}_{j0i0}+\mathrm{II}^{k}_{i}\mathrm{II}^{l}_{j}\bar{g}_{k,l}=-\bar{R}_{j0i0}+\mathrm{II}_{i}^{k}\mathrm{II}_{kj}\,.\end{split}

(157)

Putting together eqs. (
151
) and (
157
) in eq.(
150
) we obtain

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(\tau_{1,\bar{g}})=-2\mathrm{II}^{ij}\mathrm{II}_{ij}+g^{ij}\left(-\overline{R}_{j0i0}+\mathrm{II}_{i}^{k}\mathrm{II}_{kj}\right)=-\left(\mathrm{II}^{ij}\mathrm{II}_{ij}+\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}_{00}\right)=\\
&=-\mathrm{Tr}^{g}(\mathrm{II}^{2})-\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}(\boldsymbol{\nu},\boldsymbol{\nu})=-\tau_{2,\bar{g}}-\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}(\boldsymbol{\nu},\boldsymbol{\nu})\\
\end{split}

(158)

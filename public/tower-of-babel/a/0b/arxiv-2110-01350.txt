The Tate Conjecture for Motivic Endomorphisms of K3 Surfaces over Finite Fields
Ziquan Yang

Abstract
The Tate conjecture for squares of K3 surfaces over finite fields was recently proved by Ito–Ito–Koshikawa. We give a more geometric proof when the characteristic is at least 5 5 . The main idea is to use twisted derived equivalences between K3 surfaces to link the Tate conjecture to finiteness results over finite fields, in the spirit of Tate.

Introduction

In this note, we give a proof of the Tate conjecture for squares of K3 surfaces over finite fields. We will phrase and study the problem from the perspective of motives, so we first introduce some notation. Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p
with a chosen algebraic closure
\bar{k}
,
\mathscr{M}_{\mathrm{Chow}}(k)
be the category of Chow motives, which comes equipped with fiber functors
\omega_{\ell}
(resp.
\omega_{\mathrm{cris}}
) given by
\ell
-adic cohomology for every prime
\ell\neq p
(resp. crystalline cohomology with
W[1/p]
coefficients). Let
\mathscr{M}_{\mathrm{hom}}(k)
be the category
\mathscr{M}_{\mathrm{Chow}}(k)
but with
\mathrm{Hom}
groups taken modulo the kernel of
\omega:=\omega_{\ell}\times\omega_{\mathrm{cris}}
. Let
\mathfrak{h}(-)
denote the usual functor from the category of varieties over
k
to
\mathscr{M}_{\mathrm{hom}}(k)
given by
X\mapsto(X,\mathrm{id},0)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a K3 surface over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of charactersitic
p\geq 5
. Then natural morphism

\omega_{\ell}:\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{h}(X)){\otimes}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\to\mathrm{End}_{F}(\mathrm{H}^{*}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(X_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}))

is an isomorphism for every prime
\ell\neq p
, where
F
denotes the Frobenius action on
\mathrm{H}^{*}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(X_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
.

When
X
is a K3 surface over
k
,
\mathfrak{h}(X)
is known to admit a Chow-Künneth decomposition
\mathfrak{h}(X)=\mathfrak{h}^{0}(X)\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{4}(X)
. For two K3 surfaces
X
and
X^{\prime}
over
k
, an
isogeny
from
X^{\prime}
to
X
is an isomorphism
f:\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X^{\prime})
such that
\omega(f)
preserves the Poincaré pairing. We consider the
isogeny category of K3 surfaces

\mathsf{IK3}(k)
, whose objects are K3 surfaces over
k
and whose morphisms are isogenies. For a give K3 surface
X
, we define the
automorphism group functor of
X
in the isogeny category
by

\displaystyle I_{X}(R):=\{f\in(\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)){\otimes}R)^{\times}:\omega(f)\text{ preserves the Poincar\'{e} pairing}\}

for every
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
R
. Then we have a group-theoretic version of Thm
1.1
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be as in Thm
1.1
. Assume that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is big enough so that all line bundles on
X_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
F\in\mathrm{SO}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(X_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1)))
. Let
I_{X,\ell}
denote the centralizer of the geometric Frobenius action in
\mathrm{SO}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(X_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1)))
. Then
I_{X}
is representable by a reductive group of finite type over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\omega_{\ell}
induces an isomorphism
I_{X}{\otimes}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\,{\cong}\,I_{X,\ell}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a K3 surface over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
F
denote the geometric Frobenius action on
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\ell}(X)
or
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. For any isometric embedding
\iota:\Lambda_{\ell}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\ell}(X)
, such that
\iota(\Lambda_{\ell})
is
F
-invariant, there exists another K3 surface
X^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which is unique up to isomorphism and comes with a derived
\ell
-isogeny
f:\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X^{\prime})\to\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)
such that
f(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\ell}(X^{\prime})=\iota(\Lambda_{\ell})
.

Let
X,X^{\prime}
be two K3 surfaces over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Suppose there exists a derived
\ell
-isogeny
f:\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)
such that
f(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\ell}(X^{\prime}))=\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\ell}(X)
, then
X\cong X^{\prime}
.

We now explain the main ideas of proof. First, let
X
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be as in the above theorem and
\mathfrak{t}
be the transcendental part of
\mathfrak{h}(X)
, i.e., the orthogonal complement to the span of submotives. Similarly, we consider
I_{\mathfrak{t}(X)}
, which is defined just as
I_{X}
but with
\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)
replaced by
\mathfrak{t}
and the centralizer
I_{\mathfrak{t},\ell}
of the geometric Frobenius in
\mathrm{SO}(\omega_{\ell}(\mathfrak{t}(1)))
. It suffices to show that
\omega_{\ell}:\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{t}(1)){\otimes}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\to\mathrm{End}_{F}(\omega_{\ell}(\mathfrak{t}(1)))
and
\omega_{\ell}:I_{\mathfrak{t}}{\otimes}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\to I_{\mathfrak{t},\ell}
are isomorphisms.

Next, we geometrically construct lots of elements in
\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{t})
using
twisted derived equivalences
. A twisted K3 surface is a pair
(X,\alpha)
where
X
is a K3 surface and
\alpha
is a Brauer class. The order of
(X,\alpha)
is the order of
\alpha
. Up to choices of B-fields, a derived equivalence
\mathbb{D}(X,\alpha)\,{\cong}\,\mathbb{D}(X^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
between two twisted K3 surfaces of
\ell^{\infty}
-order gives rise to an isomorphism
f:\mathfrak{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
, where
\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
is the transcendental part of
\mathfrak{h}(X^{\prime})
. We call compositions of such isomorphisms
derived transcendental
\ell
-isogenies
. Let
S_{\mathfrak{t}}^{\mathrm{der},\ell}\subset\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{t})
denote the subgroup of derived transcendental
\ell
-isogenies from
X
to itself. After developing the structural theorems for derived transcendental
\ell
-isogenies, we use the key fact that there are only finitely many K3 surfaces up to isomorphism over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to show the quotient
S_{\mathfrak{t}}^{\mathrm{der},\ell}\backslash I_{\mathfrak{t}(X),\ell}
is
compact
(Thm
3.7
).

Note that this compactness statement already implies that
S_{\mathfrak{t}}^{\mathrm{der},\ell}
is big. Our next step is to find a reductive
\mathbb{Q}
-group
I^{\prime}
which is equipped with a morphism
\nu:I^{\prime}\to I_{\mathfrak{t}(X),\ell}
and maps
S_{\mathfrak{t}}^{\mathrm{der},\ell}\to I^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})\to\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{t})
compatible with
\nu
in the obvious sense. The compactness result and a theorem of Borel and Tis implies that
v(I^{\prime}_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}})\subseteq I_{\mathfrak{t},\ell}
is a parabolic subgroup (see Thm
3.6
). However,
v(I^{\prime}_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}})
is reductive and
I_{\mathfrak{t},\ell}
is connected, so
v(I^{\prime}_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}})=I_{\mathfrak{t},\ell}
. Then using the density of
I^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})
in
I^{\prime}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
we check that the image of
I^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})
in
\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{t})
spans
\mathrm{End}_{F}\omega_{\ell}(\mathfrak{t})
. This implies both Thm
1.1
and
1.2
.

It remains to explain how to construct
I^{\prime}
. Ideally, if
X\times X
satisfies some of the standard conjectures, then we can directly verify that
I_{\mathfrak{t}}
defines a reductive
\mathbb{Q}
-group and simply take
I^{\prime}=I_{\mathfrak{t}}
. In fact, a little refinement of the argument shows that
\mathrm{End}_{F}\omega_{\ell}(\mathfrak{t})
is spanned by
S^{\mathrm{der},\ell}_{\mathfrak{t}}
. We will carry out this strategy out for K3 surfaces which arise from the Kummer construction (Ex.
3.10
) and a slight variant of this strategy to ordinary K3 surfaces (Ex.
3.11
). To treat the general case, we choose a polarization
\xi
on
X
and consider the
Kuga-Satake
abelian variety
A
attached to
(X,\xi)
. The abelian variety
A
comes with a “CSpin-structure”. We consider the group scheme
\widetilde{I}
which parametrizes the automorphisms of
A
which preserve this CSpin-structure and define
I^{\prime}:=\widetilde{I}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
. The main point of considering
\widetilde{I}
is that it fixes a polarization on
A
up to scalar, so that
I^{\prime}
is reductive by the
positivity of Rosati involution
. The general mechinery of the Kuga-Satake construction gives us a morphism
I^{\prime}\to I_{\mathfrak{t},\ell}
and we construct maps
S_{\mathfrak{t}}^{\mathrm{der},\ell}\to I^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})\to\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{t})
by lifting-reduction arguments.

Remark 1.4
.

We remark that Thm
1.1
(including the case
p=2,3
) was previously proved in
[
10
]
by Ito–Ito–Koshikawa, whose method relies more heavily on the Kuga-Satake construction. The main idea there is to develop a refined CM lifting theory for K3 surfaces over finite fields, and then deduce Thm
1.1
using Kisin’s version of Tate’s theorem on the endomorphisms of abelian varieties, and the fact that squares of K3 surfaces with CM satisfies the Hodge conjecture, proved by Buskin. In comparison, our method relies more on the geometry of K3 surfaces themselves and Kuga-Satake abelian varieties are only used to bypass the standard conjectures. In particular, we do not make use of any CM liftings or Tate’s theorem

Derived
\ell
-Isogenies for K3 Surfaces

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field. Let
Y
be a K3 surface or a product of two K3 surface over
k
and let
\alpha
be a Brauer class in
\mathrm{Br}(Y)[\ell^{\infty}]
. The
\alpha
-twisted coherent sheaves form an abelian category, so we can talk about its bounded derived category
\mathbb{D}(Y,\alpha)
. To take Chern characters of
\alpha
-twisted sheaves, we need the notion of B-field lifts:

Definition 2.1
.

A
B
-field lift of
\alpha
is an element in
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(Y,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))
with the following property: Suppose
\mathrm{ord}(\alpha)=\ell^{m}
and
B=\beta/\ell^{m}
. Then
\beta
lies in
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(Y,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1))
and maps to
\alpha
under the composition
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(Y,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1))\to\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(Y,\mu_{\ell^{m}})\to\mathrm{Br}(Y)
, where the first map is the reduction map modulo
\ell^{m}
.

Given a complex of
\alpha
-twisted sheaf
\mathscr{E}
on
Y
, and a B-field lift
B
of
\alpha
as above, we can define its
twisted Chern character

\rm ch^{B}(\mathscr{E})
, which depends on the choice of
B
. The basic idea is that, given a choice of a gerbe
\mathscr{X}
representing
\beta
,
\mathscr{E}^{{\otimes}\ell^{m}}
becomes an untwisted sheaf on
X
, so we can take the usual Chern character
\rm ch(\mathscr{E}^{{\otimes}\ell^{m}})
and define
\rm ch^{B}(\mathscr{E})
as
\sqrt[\ell^{m}]{\rm ch(\mathscr{E}^{{\otimes}\ell^{m}})}
, which depends only on
B
and not on
\mathscr{X}
. For details, see
[
4
, App. A]
and
[
5
, §2]
. The
twisted Mukai vector
of
\mathscr{E}
is defined by
v^{B}(\mathscr{E}):=\rm ch^{B}(\mathscr{E})\sqrt{\mathrm{td}(Y)}
.

Now suppose we are given given two twisted K3 surfaces
(X,\alpha),(X^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
and a derived equialence
\Phi:\mathbb{D}(X,\alpha)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathbb{D}(X^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
. By Orlov’s theorem,
\Phi
is induced by a complex
\mathscr{E}\in D(X\times X^{\prime},\alpha^{-1}\boxtimes\alpha^{\prime})
, which is unique up to quasi-isomorphism. To emphasize this dependence we denote
\Phi
by
\Phi_{\mathscr{E}}
. Given B-field lifts
B,B^{\prime}
of
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}
, we have a twisted Mukai vector
v^{-B\boxtimes B^{\prime}}(\mathscr{E})
. As an element of
\mathrm{Ch}^{*}(X\times X^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}}
, it induces a map
\Phi^{-B\boxtimes B^{\prime}}(\mathscr{E}):\mathrm{H}^{*}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathrm{H}^{*}(X^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

The map
\Phi^{-B\boxtimes B^{\prime}}(\mathscr{E})
as above restricts to a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell^{\prime}}
-integral isomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{*}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell^{\prime}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathrm{H}^{*}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell^{\prime}})
for every prime
\ell^{\prime}\not\in\{\ell,\mathrm{char\,}k\}
. If
\mathrm{char\,}k=p
, then
\Phi^{-B\boxtimes B^{\prime}}(\mathscr{E})
in addition restricts to a
W
-integral isomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{*}_{\mathrm{cris}}(X/W)\to\mathrm{H}^{*}_{\mathrm{cris}}(X^{\prime}/W)
.

In general,
\Phi^{-B\boxtimes B^{\prime}}(\mathscr{E}):\mathrm{H}^{*}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathrm{H}^{*}(X^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}}
does not need to preserve the codimension filtration, or restricts to an isogeny
\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X^{\prime})
. Nonetheless, it always restricts to an isomorphism
\mathfrak{t}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{t}(X^{\prime})
which preserves the Poincaré pairing. One argues by Witt’s cancellation theorem that
\mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{NS}(X^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}}
as quadratic forms, so that given such
\mathfrak{t}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{t}(X^{\prime})
we can always complete to an isogeny
\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X^{\prime})
by adding an isomorphism
\mathfrak{a}^{2}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{a}^{2}(X^{\prime})
which preserves the Poincaré pairing.

Definition 2.3
.

(a)

A
primitive derived transcendental
\ell
-isogeny
is an isomorphism
\mathfrak{t}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{t}(X^{\prime})
is given by
v^{-B\boxtimes B^{\prime}}(\mathscr{E})
for some choices of
B,B^{\prime},\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}
and
\Phi_{\mathscr{E}}:\mathbb{D}(X,\alpha)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathbb{D}(X^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
.

(b)

A
primitive derived
\ell
-isogeny

f:\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X^{\prime})
is an isogeny whose restriction to the transcendental parts is a primitive derived transcendental
\ell
-isogeny.

(c)

A (transcendental)
derived
\ell
-isogeny
is a finite composition of the primitive ones.

Remark 2.4
.

We remark that isomorphisms between K3 surfaces and “reflection in
(-2)
-curves” on a single K3 (see
[

) give rise to primitive derived
\ell
-isogenies with
B,B^{\prime},\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}
all being zero.

2.1 Existence Theorems

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
X
be a K3 surface over
k
. Denote by
\Lambda_{\ell}
the quadratic lattice given by
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
. For every isometric embedding
\iota:\Lambda_{\ell}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
, there exists another K3 surface
X^{\prime}
together with a derived
\ell
-isogeny
f:\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X^{\prime})\to\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)
such that
f(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}))=\iota(\Lambda_{\ell})
.

Proof.

This is part of
[
5
, Thm 1.3]
. We explain the main steps for readers’ convenience: First, by Cartan-Dieudonné theorem, every element of
\mathrm{O}(\Lambda_{\ell}{\otimes}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
can be written as a composition of relfections, so it suffices to treat the case when
\iota(\Lambda_{\ell})=s_{b}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}))
, where
s_{b}
is the reflection in
b\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
. We may assume
b
is a primitive element in
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
.

Now set
n=b^{2}/2
and
B:=b/n
. Let
\alpha
be the Brauer class defined by
B
. Let
X^{\prime}
be the moduli space of stable
\alpha
-twisted sheaves with Mukai vector
(n,0,0)
. Then there is an equivalence
\Phi_{\mathscr{E}}:\mathbb{D}(X^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathbb{D}(X,\alpha)
for some
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Br}(X^{\prime})
. For a suitable B-field lift
B^{\prime}
of
\alpha^{\prime}
,
v^{-B^{\prime}\boxtimes B}(\mathscr{E})
restricts to the desired isogeny
f
. For details of this argument, see
[
5
, Lem. 6.2(1)]
.
∎

When
k=\mathbb{C}
, this gives an adelic proof of
[
9
, Thm 0.1]
, which refines
[
6
, Thm 1.1]
:

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
X,X^{\prime}
be algebraic K3 surfaces over
\mathbb{C}
. Every Hodge isometry
g:\mathrm{H}^{2}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{Q})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is given by a derived isogeny.

Proof.

By applying Thm
2.5
for different
\ell
’s, we find a derived isogeny
f:\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X^{\prime\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)
such that
f^{*}(\mathrm{H}^{2}(X^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{Z}))=g(\mathrm{H}^{2}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}))
for some other K3 surface
X^{\prime\prime}
. The composition
g\circ f^{*}
is an integral Hodge isometry
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{Z})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(X^{\prime},\mathbb{Z})
. By
[
18
, Lem. 6.2]
, up to precomposing
\pm f
by a composition of reflections in
(-2)
-curves, we may assume
g\circ f^{*}
preserves the ample cones. Therefore,
g\circ f^{*}
is induced by an isomorphism by the global Torelli theorem.
∎

2.2 Torelli Theorem

If a lifting
X_{W}
of
X
over
W
carries a lifting of all line bundles on
X
, i.e., the natural map
\mathrm{Pic}\,(X_{W})\to\mathrm{Pic}\,(X)
is an isomorphism, then we say that
X_{W}
is a
perfect
lifting. By
[
11
, Prop. 4.10]
, perfect liftings always exist for non-supersingular K3 surfaces.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
f:\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X^{\prime})
be a derived
\ell
-isogeny between non-supersingular K3 surfaces
X,X^{\prime}
. For every perfect lifting
X_{W}
of
X
over
W
, there exists a perfect lifting
X^{\prime}_{W}
such that
f
lifts to an isogeny between their generic fibers of
X_{W}
and
X_{W}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It suffices to treat the case when
f
is primitive, so that the restriction of
f
to the transcendental parts is given by a twisted Fourier-Mukai equivalence
\Phi_{\mathscr{E}}:\mathbb{D}(X,\alpha)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathbb{D}(X^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
for some Brauer classes
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}
of
\ell
-power order. Moreover, since we are considering perfect liftings, which in particular carry all liftings of
(-2)
-curves, using
[
18
, Lem. 6.2]
we reduce to the case when
f
is polarizable. Any lifting
X_{W}
carries a unique lift
\alpha_{W}
of the Brauer class
\alpha
, so we may now conclude by
[
5
, Thm 4.6]
, which states that for some lifting
X^{\prime}_{W}
of
X
,
\mathscr{E}
lifts up to quasi-isomorphism to a complex in
\mathbb{D}(X_{W}\times X_{W}^{\prime},-\alpha_{W}\boxtimes\alpha_{W}^{\prime})
, where
\alpha^{\prime}_{W}
is the lift of
\alpha^{\prime}
on
X_{W}^{\prime}
. Moreover, if
X
is ordinary and
X_{W}
is the canonical lifting, then
X^{\prime}
is also ordinary and
X^{\prime}_{W}
is the canonical lifting of
X^{\prime}
.

For the second statement, note that since
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{cris}}(X/W)\,{\cong}\,\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{cris}}(X^{\prime}/W)
,
X
and
X^{\prime}
have the same height. Moreover,
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(X_{\bar{K}},\mathbb{Z}_{p})\,{\cong}\,\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(X^{\prime}_{\bar{K}},\mathbb{Z}_{p})
as
\mathrm{Gal}_{K}
-modules. It follows from
[
16
, Thm C]
that if
X_{W}
is the canonical lifting, then so is
X^{\prime}_{W}
.
∎

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
f:\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X^{\prime})
be derived
\ell
-isogeny. If
f
is polarizable and
\omega(f)
restricts to an isomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(X^{\prime})
, then
f
is induced by a unique isomorphism
X^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}X
.

Remark 2.9
.

Thm
2.8
is proved in
[
5
]
in more generality, which also deals with derived equivlences between twisted K3 surfaces whose Brauer classes are not of prime-to-
p
order. The proof there also becomes significantly more involved because when the order of
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(X)
is not prime-to-
p
, it is not true that every lifting
X_{W}
of
X
over
W
carries a lifting of

, so the proof of Thm
2.7
breaks down.

2.3 Galois Descent

Using Thm
2.8
we give a Galois descent theorem for derived
\ell
-isogenies:

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
k
be a perfect field with algebraic closure
\bar{k}
. Let
f:\bar{X}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\bar{X}
be a derived
\ell
-isogeny bewteen two K3 surface
\bar{X},\bar{X}^{\prime}
over
\bar{k}
. Suppose
\bar{X}
admits a model
X
over
k
and the
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-lattice
f^{*}(\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(\bar{X}^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}))
in
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(\bar{X},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is invariant under the induced
\mathrm{Gal}_{k}
-action, then
\bar{X}^{\prime}
admits a model
X^{\prime}
over
k
such that
f
decends to a derived
\ell
-isogeny
\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{h}^{2}(X)
.

Proof.

For each
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}_{k}
, we use supscript
\sigma
to denote base change via
\sigma:\bar{k}\to\bar{k}
. Let us write
b_{\sigma}:\bar{\to}X
and
b^{\prime}_{\sigma}:(X^{\prime})^{\sigma}\to X^{\prime}

3.1 Linear Algebra

Let

be a
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
vector space of dimension
2r

on

\displaystyle q^{{\otimes}(1,1)}(\alpha,\beta)=\mathrm{tr}(\alpha\beta^{\dagger})

where

Let

denote the group scheme defined by units of

\mathbb{Q}

Lemma 3.2
.

If the natural map

is injecture, or equivalently,
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}E=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\langle E\rangle

\mathrm{U}(E){\otimes}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\to\mathrm{GL}(V_{\ell})
is injective.

Proof.

When evaluated on a
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-algebra

,
\mathrm{U}(E){\otimes}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\to\mathrm{GL}(V_{\ell})
becomes the map
(E{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Q}}R)^{\times}\to\mathrm{GL}(V_{\ell}{\otimes}R)
. This map is a restriction of the

which is obtained from

via base change. Then we use the fact that algebras over a field are flat.
∎

For a quick non-example, note that if

,

cannot inject into

as

.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

be any finite dimensional

Choose a section of the projection

. Then we obtain a finite dimensional subspace

such that the restriction of

to

is an isomorphism. In other words,

\mathbb{Q}
-linear structure on

. Note that the pairing on

On pointwise error estimates for Voronoï-based finite volume methods for the Poisson equation on the sphere
Leonardo A. Poveda, Pedro Peixoto

Abstract
In this paper, we give pointwise estimates of a Voronoï-based finite volume approximation of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on Voronoï-Delaunay decompositions of the sphere. These estimates are the basis for a local error analysis, in the maximum norm, of the approximate solution of the Poisson equation and its gradient. Here, we consider the Voronoï-based finite volume method as a perturbation of the finite element method. Finally, using regularized Green’s functions, we derive quasi-optimal convergence order in the maximum-norm with minimal regularity requirements. Numerical examples show that the convergence is at least as good as predicted.



Keywords:
Laplace-Beltrami operator, Poisson equation, spherical icosahedral grids, finite volume method,
a priori
error estimates, pointwise estimates, uniform error estimates

Introduction

The study of numerical solutions of Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) posed on spheres and other surfaces arise naturally in many applications. It is especially important in geophysical fluid dynamics, where a sphere is usually adopted as a domain. For instance, in weather forecasting and climate modeling, PDEs are used and discretized through finite differences, finite elements, and finite volume methods on a spherical domain
[
27
,
55
,
30
]
.

The efficiency and accuracy of the approximate solutions depend on certain characteristics of the discretization of the sphere and of the differential operators. In the present work, we start by looking at the approximations of solutions of the Poisson problem on the unit sphere using
spherical icosahedral geodesic grids

[
51
,
56
,
4
,
30
]
,
i.e.
, grids that come from an icosahedron inscribed on the sphere, with its vertices and faces projected onto the spherical surface. We obtain a spherical triangular grid whose edges are geodesic arcs and satisfy the so-called Delaunay criterion
i.e.
, maximizing the smallest angle
[
28
,
26
,
32
]
. Following
[
2
,
50
]
, the mentioned construction will allow us to define two types of grids on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
: a triangular Delaunay (primal) decomposition and a Voronoï (dual) decomposition. In what follows, we will consider such grids in a more general way, not necessarily restricted to those directly built from an icosahedron. Therefore, we will refer to them as general Voronoï-Delaunay decompositions of the sphere throughout this paper.

Voronoï-based finite volume methods are very popular and allow great flexibility. However, the finite volume scheme may not be formally consistent, yet can still lead to second-order convergence results, leading to what is sometimes called supra-convergence
[
3
,
14
,
6
,
48
,
44
,
37
,
15
]
. Error estimates of the first order of convergence for approximate solutions of planar Voronoï-based finite volume method in the
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
-norm have been reported by
[
45
,
25
,
24
,
19
,
23
]
. Second-order accuracy in the
L^{2}
-norm using planar dual Donald decompositions, which uses the triangle barycenters as vertices of the dual grid, were considered in
[
33
,
42
,
10
,
11
]
and for general surfaces in
[
34
,
35
]
. These latter works explicitly use properties of the barycentric dual cells,
i.e.
, the quadratic order is obtained by using the centroid of triangles, and therefore these constructions cannot be generally extended to the arbitrary Voronoï-Delaunay decompositions.

Previous work
[
42
,
10
,
8
,
57
]
also usually have an extra requirement in the regularity of the exact solution (belong to
H^{3}
or
W^{3,p}
), which is excessive compared to the requirements in finite element methods
[
7
,
12
,
49
]
. However,
[
22
]
has reported a sufficient condition to decrease the regularity of the exact solution (is in

) but imposes an added regularity requirement on the forcing source term, which is in

to get the optimal convergence order. The authors also highlighted that, except for one-dimensional domains or the solution domain has a boundary smooth enough, the
H^{1}
-regularity of the source term does not automatically imply the

-regularity of the exact solution.

On the sphere,
[
20
]
show a quadratic order estimate in the Voronoï-Delaunay decomposition on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
in the
L^{2}
-norm and a Spherical Centroidal Voronoï Tesselation (SCVT) optimized grid
[
17
,
18
]
is used as their Voronoï-Delaunay decomposition and the excessive regularity assumption in the exact solution. Based on this, as a first minor result of this work, we show more general error estimates than given in
[
20
]
applying the approach of
[
22
]
,
i.e.
, decreasing the regularity in the exact solution and imposing a minimum regularity in the source term. Thus we obtain the desired order of convergence. We conclude, as in
[
20
]
, that the proof cannot be extended to Voronoï-Delaunay decompositions in general due to the explicit use of the criteria of the SCVT. In general, determining a quadratic convergence order in the
L^{2}
-norm is an open issue for the Voronoï-based finite volume method. Several efforts have been made to answer this question and are limited to topological aspects; for example,
[
47
]
gives a partial answer and an important improvement in relation to what is known so far.

The topic of this paper is the convergence analysis for approximate solutions of the Voronoï-based finite volume method in the maximum-norm, extending existing results for the plane
[
22
,
58
,
9
]
to the sphere. We do not know that advances in this direction have been previously described in the literature, particularly for Voronoï-Delaunay decompositions on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. We opted to consider the Voronoï-based finite volume method as a perturbation of the finite element method, an approach widely described in the literature
[
42
,
22
,
43
]
.

The main idea is to use the standard error estimation procedures developed for the finite elements on surfaces such as
[
13
,
38
,
36
]
along with the use of the regularized Green’s functions on the sphere.

The main result of our work is the proof of the sub-linear convergence order of a classic finite volume method on general spherical Voronoï-Delaunay decompositions, for the Poisson equation, in the maximum norm. This result tightens the gap between theoretical convergence analysis and existing empirical evidence for the convergence of such schemes in the sphere. Empirical evidence indicated the possibility of linear convergence; however, here, our results contain a logarithmic factor caused by the use of linear functions in the primal Delaunay decomposition, which apparently cannot be avoided, as also was initially examined in Euclidean domains
via finite element methods by
[
54
,
49
,
53
]
and more recently described in
[
39
,
40
,
41
]
. Additionally, linear convergence order in the maximum norm is proved for SCVT grids.

2
Problem setting

In this section, we start defining the model problem, some notations, and function spaces that will be used throughout the paper. Let
\mathbb{S}^{2}:=\{\mathrm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\|\mathrm{x}\|=1\}
be the unit sphere, where
\|\cdot\|
represents the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Let
\nabla_{s}
denote the tangential gradient
[
21
]
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
defined by,

\nabla_{s}u(\mathrm{x})=\nabla u(\mathrm{x})-\left(\nabla u(\mathrm{x})\cdot\vec{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathbb{S}^{2},\mathrm{x}}\right)\vec{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathbb{S}^{2},\mathrm{x}},

where
\nabla
denotes the usual gradient in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\vec{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathbb{S}^{2},\mathrm{x}}
represents the unit outer normal vector to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
at
\mathrm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
. We shall adopt standard notation for Sobolev spaces on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
(see
e.g.
,
[
29
]
). Given
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
k
non-negative integer, we denote the Sobolev spaces by,

\begin{split}L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{2})&=\left\{u(\mathrm{x}):\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|u(\mathrm{x})|^{p}ds(\mathrm{x})<\infty\right\},\\
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{S}^{2})&=\left\{u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{2}):\nabla_{s}^{\alpha}u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{2}),\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{for }0\leq|\alpha|\leq k\right\},\end{split}

where
\nabla_{s}^{\alpha}=\nabla_{s,1}^{\alpha_{1}}\nabla_{s,2}^{\alpha_{2}}\nabla_{s,3}^{\alpha_{3}}
is the multi-index notation for the weak tangential derivatives up to order
k
, where
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
is a vector of non-negative integers with
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}
. The function space
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
is equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\begin{cases}\left(\sum_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq k}\|\nabla_{s}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{p}\right)^{1/p},&\mbox{for }1\leq p<\infty\\
\max_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq k}\|\nabla_{s}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})},&\mbox{for }p=\infty.\end{cases}

We set
H^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{2})=W^{k,2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
along with the standard inner product

(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u(\mathrm{x})v(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x}),\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{for all }u,v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}),

where
ds(\mathrm{x})
is the surface area measure. Additionally, we define the zero-averaged subspace of
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
as

H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}):=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}):\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x})=0\right\},

equipped with the
H^{1}
-norm. Throughout this paper, we use
C_{\ast}
as a generic positive real constant, which may vary with the context and depends on the problem data and other model parameters. Also, we will use the notation
a\preceq b
, which means that there is a positive constant
C
, independent of the mesh size, such that
a\leq Cb
.

We now introduce the Poisson equation to be considered. Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
be a given forcing (source) satisfying the compatibility condition,

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x})=0.

(2.1)

The model problem consists of finding a scalar function
u:\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

-\Delta_{s}u(\mathrm{x})=f(\mathrm{x}),\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{for each }\mathrm{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{2},

(2.2)

where
-\Delta_{s}=-\nabla_{s}\cdot\nabla_{s}
denotes the Laplacian on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. We impose
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x})=0
to ensure uniqueness of solution. For any
u,v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, we define the bilinear functional
\mathcal{A}:H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\times H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathcal{A}(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\nabla_{s}u(\mathrm{x})\cdot\nabla_{s}v(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x}).

(2.3)

The bilinear functional is well-defined on the space
H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\times H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
and is continuous and coercive, i.e.,
there are positive constants
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
such that

\begin{split}\left|\mathcal{A}(u,v)\right|&\leq C_{0}\|\nabla_{s}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\nabla_{s}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})},\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{for each }u,v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}),\\
\mathcal{A}(u,u)&\geq C_{1}\|\nabla_{s}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2},\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{for each }u\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}).\end{split}

The variational formulation of (
2.2
) reads: find
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
such that

\mathcal{A}(u,v)=(f,v),\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{for each }v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}),

(2.4)

in which
(f,v)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f(\mathrm{x})v(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x})
and the source data
f
satisfies (
2.1
).

As a consequence of the Lax-Milgram theorem
[
12
,
7
]
, problem (
2.4
) has a unique solution. This solution is such that for some positive constant
C_{S}
,

\|\nabla_{s}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq C_{S}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(2.5)

Moreover, we have the regularity property: for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
satisfying (
2.1
), the unique weak solution
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
of (
2.4
) satisfies, for some positive constant
C_{R}
,

\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq C_{R}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(2.6)

A detailed proof of (
2.6
) can be found in
[
21
, Theorem 3.3 pp. 304]
.

4
A Voronoï-based finite volume method

In this section, we seek an approximate solution of (
2.2
) via a finite difference/volume scheme. First, from Gauss theorem, we have that

-\int_{\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}}\Delta_{s}u(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x})=-\int_{\partial\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}}\nabla_{s}u(\mathrm{x})\cdot\vec{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathrm{x},\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}}d\gamma(\mathrm{x}),

where
\vec{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathrm{x},\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}}
denotes the co-normal unit vector on
\partial\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}
at
\mathrm{x}
, and
d\gamma(\mathrm{x})
is the geodesic length measure. Since
\partial\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}=\cup_{j\in\Lambda(i)}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij}
, we have that

-\sum_{j\in\Lambda(i)}\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij}}\nabla_{s}u(\mathrm{x})\cdot\vec{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathrm{x},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij}}d\gamma(\mathrm{x})=\int_{\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}}f(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x}).

We denote the continuous flux of
u
across the edge
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij}
by

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{ij}(u):=-\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij}}\nabla_{s}u(\mathrm{x})\cdot\vec{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathrm{x},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij}}d\gamma(\mathrm{x}),

(4.1)

and define its central difference approximation

\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{ij}(u_{h}):=-m_{l}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij})\frac{u_{h}(\mathrm{x}_{j})-u_{h}(\mathrm{x}_{i})}{\|\mathrm{x}_{j}-\mathrm{x}_{i}\|}.

(4.2)

Additionally, for each cell
\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}
, let
f_{i}
denote the mean value of the data
f
on
\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}
,
i.e.
,

f_{i}=\frac{1}{m_{a}(\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i})}\int_{\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}}f(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x}).

(4.3)

The Voronoï-based finite volume scheme is defined as

(\mathrm{L}_{s,h}(u_{h}))_{i}:=\frac{1}{m_{a}(\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i})}\sum_{j\in\Lambda(i)}\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{ij}(u_{h})=f_{i},\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{for }1\leq i\leq N,

(4.4)

where
\mathrm{L}_{s,h}
is a discretization of the Laplacian,
u_{h}
is an approximate solution and
f_{i}
is defined in (
4.3
). To emphasize the dependence of the grid, we always will use the subscript
h
. The finite volume scheme (
4.4
) is conservative, since we have that,

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j\in\Lambda(i)}\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{ij}(u_{h})=0,

which follows from
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{ij}=-\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{ji}
if the vertices
\mathrm{x}_{i}
and
\mathrm{x}_{j}
are neighbors with
m_{l}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij})>0
.

to get

\begin{split}I_{4}=\left|\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(\varphi_{h}))-\mathcal{A}(u_{h},\varphi_{h})\right|&\leq Ch^{2}\|\nabla_{s}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\nabla_{s}\varphi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\\
&\leq Ch^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\nabla_{s}\varphi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.\end{split}

(5.5)

Gathering (
5.2
) and inequalities (
5.3
)–(
5.5
), we have an expression as

\begin{split}\|\nabla_{s}(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}&\leq Ch\|\nabla_{s}(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+Ch\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\nabla_{s}\varphi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+Ch^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\nabla_{s}\varphi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.\end{split}

(5.6)

Using Young’s and triangle inequalities, and Proposition
4.1
, we find

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{s}(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\preceq\epsilon\left(\|\nabla_{s}(u-u_{h})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|\nabla_{s}\varphi_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right)+h^{2}\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+h^{4}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\preceq\epsilon\left(\|\nabla_{s}(u-u_{h})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+Ch^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right)+h^{2}\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+h^{4}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

Taking
\epsilon
small enough, we arrive to

\|\nabla_{s}(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}\preceq h^{2}\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right)+h^{4}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

Finally, for
h
small enough, we obtain (
5.1a
).

In order to prove (
5.1b
), we derive an error estimate following classical duality argument: for
u-u_{h}\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, from (
2.4
), there exists a unique weak solution
w\in H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
satisfying

\mathcal{A}(v,w)=(v,u-u_{h}),\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{for all }v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}).

Taking
v=u-u_{h}
and by using the regularity estimate (
2.6
), we have

\|w\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq C\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.7)

Now, assume that
w_{h}=\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(w)\in\widetilde{U}_{h}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}&=(u-u_{h},u-u_{h})\leq|\mathcal{A}(u-u_{h},w)|\\
&=|\mathcal{A}(u-u_{h},w-w_{h})+\mathcal{A}(u,w_{h})-\mathcal{A}(u_{h},w_{h})|\\
&\leq|\mathcal{A}(u-u_{h},w-w_{h})|+|\mathcal{A}(u,w_{h})-\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(w_{h}))|\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+|\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(w_{h}))-\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(w_{h}))|\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+|\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(w_{h}))-\mathcal{A}(u_{h},w_{h})|\\
&=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.\end{split}

About
I_{1}
, by equation (
5.1a
), Proposition
4.1
and inequality (
5.7
), we have

\begin{split}I_{1}=|\mathcal{A}(u-u_{h},w-w_{h})|&\leq C\|\nabla_{s}(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\nabla_{s}(w-w_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\\
&\leq Ch^{2}\left(\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right)\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.\end{split}

(5.8)

For
I_{2}
, by Lemma
4.10
and expression (
5.7
), we have

I_{2}=|\mathcal{A}(u,w_{h})-\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(w_{h}))|\leq Ch\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.9)

Analogously for
I_{3}
, using Lemma
4.7
, equations (
4.13
) and (
5.7
) follows that

I_{3}=\left|\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(w_{h}))-\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(w_{h}))\right|\leq Ch^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.10)

Finally, by Lemma
4.9
along with estimates (
4.13
) and (
5.7
) yields the expression

I_{4}=\left|\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(w_{h}))-\mathcal{A}(u_{h},w_{h})\right|\leq Ch^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.11)

Combining (
5.8
)–(
5.11
) with
h>0
small enough, we have

\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}\preceq(h\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+h^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+h^{2}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})})\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

Dividing by
\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}
encloses the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Notice that the convergence rate in the
L^{2}
-norm is lower than that obtained in FEM. As we will see in the numerical experiment, the errors of the finite volume solutions behave better than the estimates given above. Meanwhile, no known standard method exists to increase the convergence estimate in the
L^{2}
-norm by using Voronoï-Delaunay decomposition in general.

The dominant term in the bounds of Theorem
5.1
is given by Lemma
4.10
. One way to gain extra power in the convergence rate is to use the SCVT optimizations in the standard decomposition. A quadratic order estimate was reported by
[
20
]
. Still, we will illustrate below that a decrease in regularity in the exact solution is possible using the techniques investigated by
[
22
]
.

The following lemma is a simplified version of a result given by
[
20
, Lemma 1, pp. 1682]
, assuming that the density function
\rho
is equal to
1
.

Lemma 5.3

(
[
20
]
)
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{h}=\{\mathrm{x}_{i},\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be an almost uniform Spherical Centroidal Voronoï-Delaunay decomposition (SCVT) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Then, for any
w\in H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
h
, such that

\left|\int_{\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}}[w(\mathrm{x})-\widetilde{\Pi}^{\ast}_{h}(w)(\mathrm{x})]ds(\mathrm{x})\right|\leq Ch^{2}m_{a}(\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i})^{1/2}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i})}.

In light of the lemma above, we have the following result.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
f\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
be a function that satisfies the compatibility condition (
2.1
) and
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{h}=\{\mathrm{x}_{i},\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be an almost uniform Spherical Centroidal Voronoï-Delaunay decomposition (SCVT) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Then, for each
w\in H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
h
, such that

\left(f,\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(w)-\widetilde{\Pi}^{\ast}_{h}(w)\right)\leq Ch^{2}\|f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})},

where
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}
and
\widetilde{\Pi}^{\ast}_{h}
are the interpolation operator on spaces
\widetilde{U}_{h}
and
\widetilde{V}_{h}
respectively.

Proof.

By definition of the inner product we have

\begin{split}\left(f,\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(w)-\widetilde{\Pi}^{\ast}_{h}(w)\right)&=\sum_{\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\int_{\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}}f(\mathrm{x})\left[\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(w)(\mathrm{x})-w(\mathrm{x})\right]ds(\mathrm{x})\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}}\left[f(\mathrm{x})-\mathrm{P}_{h}(f)\right]\left[w(\mathrm{x})-\widetilde{\Pi}^{\ast}_{h}(w)(\mathrm{x})\right]ds(\mathrm{x})\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}}\mathrm{P}_{h}(f)\left[w(\mathrm{x})-\widetilde{\Pi}^{\ast}_{h}(w)(\mathrm{x})\right]ds(\mathrm{x})\\
&=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},\end{split}

where
\mathrm{P}_{h}(f)
denotes the
L^{2}
-projection of the function
f
on
\widetilde{V}_{h}
. For
I_{1}
, by using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Proposition
4.1
, we obtain

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq\sum_{\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\left(\int_{\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}}|f(\mathrm{x})|^{2}ds(\mathrm{x})\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}}|\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(w)(\mathrm{x})-w(\mathrm{x})|^{2}ds(\mathrm{x})\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leq Ch^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.\end{split}

(5.12)

Analogously, for
I_{2}
,

\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int_{\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}}|f(\mathrm{x})-\mathrm{P}_{h}(f)(\mathrm{x})|^{2}ds(\mathrm{x})\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}}|w(\mathrm{x})-\widetilde{\Pi}^{\ast}_{h}(w)(\mathrm{x})|^{2}ds(\mathrm{x})\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leq Ch\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla_{s}f\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i})}\|w-\widetilde{\Pi}^{\ast}_{h}(w)\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i})}\leq Ch^{2}\|f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.\end{split}

(5.13)

Finally, for
I_{3}
, using Lemma
5.3
and Proposition
4.4
, we have that

\begin{split}I_{3}&\leq Ch^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\mathrm{P}_{h}(f)\big|_{\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}}m_{a}(\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i})^{1/2}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i})}\\
&\leq Ch^{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{a}(\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i})\left|\mathrm{P}_{h}(f)\big|_{\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}}\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i})}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leq Ch^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.\end{split}

(5.14)

Combining (
5.12
)–(
5.14
) we obtain the result.
∎

We will show below a subtle modification of the proof given in
[
20
]
, assuming that the exact solution
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
and source term
f\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{h}=\{\mathrm{x}_{i},\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be an almost uniform Spherical Centroidal Voronoï-Delaunay decomposition (SCVT) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Assume that
f\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
and that
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
is the unique solution of (
2.2
). Let
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{ij}
be the discrete flux defined in (
4.2
), such that the discrete problem (
4.12
) has a unique solution
u_{h}\in\widetilde{U}_{h}
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of

, such that

\|\varepsilon_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq Ch^{2}\left(\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right),

where
\varepsilon_{h}=u-u_{h}
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to (
5.1b
) in Theorem
5.1
, now using Lemma
5.4
into equation (
5.9
) to guarantee an extra power in the term

,

,

where

5.2.1
Regularity properties of Green’s functions

The lemma below has fundamental properties of the regularized Green’s functions on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. The proof is detailed in
[
1
, Theorem 4.13, pp. 108]
.

Lemma 5.6

(
[
1
]
)
.

There exists
G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})
, a Green’s function for the Laplacian on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
satisfying, for each
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
and
\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
with
\mathrm{x}\neq\mathrm{y}
,

u(\mathrm{y})=\frac{1}{m_{a}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x})-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})\Delta_{s}u(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x}),

and there exist positive constants
C_{0},C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle|G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}(1+|\ln\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})|),

\displaystyle|\nabla_{s}G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\frac{1}{|\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})|},\hskip 9.24994pt|\Delta_{s}G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})|\leq C_{2}\frac{1}{|\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})|^{2}},

where
\nabla_{s}
and
\Delta_{s}
denote the tangential gradient and Laplacian acting on a function of
\mathrm{x}
. Finally,
G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})ds(\mathrm{x})=0.

(5.15)

From now on, we denote by
G^{\mathrm{y}}(\mathrm{x})
the Green’s function
G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})
. Further, we consider the notation
g^{\mathrm{y}}
to refer to the discrete Green’s function defined as

g^{\mathrm{y}}(\mathrm{x}):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}G^{\mathrm{x}}(\mathrm{z})\delta^{\mathrm{y}}(\mathrm{z})ds(\mathrm{z}),

(5.16)

where
\delta^{\mathrm{y}}(\mathrm{z})
represents the discrete Dirac delta function at the point
\mathrm{y}
.

We now present a proposition by Demlow
[
13
, Proposition 2.8, pp. 813]
, as it will be used further in the analysis.

Proposition 5.7

(
[
13
]
)
.

Consider
v_{h}\in\widetilde{U}_{h}
and fix
\mathrm{y}\in\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}\subset\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Let
\vec{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathrm{y},\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}}
be a unit vector on the tangent plane
\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{y},\mathbb{S}^{2}}
at
\mathrm{y}
. Then, there exist
\delta^{\mathrm{y}}
and
\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\mathrm{y},\Omega}
, both independent of
v_{h}
, such that for some positive constant
C
,

\|\delta^{\mathrm{y}}\|_{W^{m,p}(\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk})}+\|\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\mathrm{y},\Omega}\|_{W^{m,p}(\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk})}\leq Ch^{-m-2\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)},

for
m\in\{0,1\}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Further, there exists positive generic constants
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
, such that

\displaystyle|v_{h}(\mathrm{y})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left|\int_{\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}}\delta^{\mathrm{y}}(\mathrm{x})v_{h}(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x})\right|,

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{s}v_{h}(\mathrm{y})\cdot\vec{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathrm{y},\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left|\int_{\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}}v_{h}(\mathrm{x})\nabla_{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\mathrm{y},\Omega}(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x})\right|,

where
\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\mathrm{y},\Omega}=\|\mathrm{x}^{\ast}\|\delta^{\mathrm{y}^{\ast},\Omega}\vec{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathrm{y},\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}}
, with
\mathrm{y}={\mathcal{P}}(\mathrm{y}^{\ast})
.

Let us now introduce a variational formulation for the discrete Green’s functions. Fix
\mathrm{y}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, then we consider two kinds of regularized Green’s functions
g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
satisfying the following variational problems:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(v,g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0})

\displaystyle=(v,\delta^{\mathrm{y}}),\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{for each }v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}),

(5.17a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(v,g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1})

\displaystyle=(v,\nabla_{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\mathrm{y},\Omega}),\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{for each }v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}).

(5.17b)

Accordingly, we define
g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h}\in\widetilde{U}_{h}
as the solutions for the finite element approximate problems

\mathcal{A}(w_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h})=(w_{h},\delta^{\mathrm{y}}),\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{for each }w_{h}\in\widetilde{U}_{h},

(5.18)

and

\mathcal{A}(w_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h})=(w_{h},\nabla_{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\mathrm{y},\Omega}),\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{for each }w_{h}\in\widetilde{U}_{h}.

(5.19)

The finite element approximation
g^{\mathrm{y}}_{n,h}\in\widetilde{U}_{h}
with
n\in\{0,1\}
is taken to be the unique solution of problem

\mathcal{A}(w_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{n}-g^{\mathrm{y}}_{n,h})=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{for each }w_{h}\in\widetilde{U}_{h}.

(5.20)

Notice that
g^{\mathrm{y}}_{n,h}
satisfies (
5.15
) from the structure of the space
\widetilde{U}_{h}
for
n\in\{0,1\}
. On the other hand, the term
g^{\mathrm{y}}_{n}-g^{\mathrm{y}}_{n,h}
satisfies the error estimates in
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
-norm
[
13
]
. These discrete Green’s functions appear because we will need some additional
a priori
estimates, which are well studied in the literature by
[
13
,
38
,
49
,
52
]
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
g^{\mathrm{y}}_{n}
be a discrete Green’s function and
g^{\mathrm{y}}_{n,h}\in\widetilde{U}_{h}
its finite element approximation with
n\in\{0,1\}
. Then, we have:

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{s}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1}-g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C,

(5.21a)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{s}g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0}\|_{W^{2,1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C|\ln h|,

(5.21b)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{s}g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C|\ln h|^{1/2}.

(5.21c)

where
C
are positive generic constants independent of
h
. Here the factor
|\ln h|^{1/2}
has order
{\mathcal{O}}(h^{-\eta})
for
\eta\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

We omit the details of (
5.21a
) and (
5.21b
), but we highlight that these proofs are detailed in
[
13
, Lemma 3.3, pp. 819]
and
[
38
, Lemma 5.2, pp. 10]
. About (

), let
g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}\in\widetilde{U}_{h}
be the finite element approximation of
g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0}
. From equation (

) follows that

\|\nabla_{s}g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\mathcal{A}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h})=(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h},\delta^{\mathrm{y}}).

From discrete Sobolev inequality (see
e.g.
,
[
7
, Lemma 4.9.2, pp. 124]
or
[
36
, Lemma 3.12, pp. 527]
), there exists a positive constant
C
, such that

\left|g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}(\mathrm{y})\right|\leq C|\ln h|^{1/2}\|\nabla_{s}g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.22)

Therefore, we obtain (
5.21c
).
∎

) in the

Proposition 5.9
.

Assume that
u_{h}\in\widetilde{U}_{h}
is the unique solution of (

). Then, there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of

, such that

\|u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq C|\ln h|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

From Proposition
5.7
, there exists
\delta^{\mathrm{y}}
supported in
\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{h}}

Finally, we get the desired inequality by applying Propositions
4.6
and
4.4
.

We now state and show the main results of this section.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{h}=\{\mathrm{x}_{i},\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be an almost uniform Voronoï-Delaunay decomposition on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Assume that
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
satisfies (
2.1
) and the unique weak solution
u
of (
2.2
) belongs to
W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\cap H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
. Let
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{ij}
be the discrete flux defined in (
4.2
), such that the discrete problem (
4.12
) has a unique solution
u_{h}\in\widetilde{U}_{h}
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
h
, such that

\|\varepsilon_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq Ch|\ln h|^{1/2}\left(\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right)+Ch^{2}\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})},

where
\varepsilon_{h}=u-u_{h}
.

Proof.

We shall fix
\mathrm{y}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}

\delta^{\mathrm{y}}

\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
, we then have

\begin{split}\left|(u-u_{h})(\mathrm{y})\right|&\leq\left|(u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u))(\mathrm{y})\right|+\left|(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h})(\mathrm{y})\right|\\

&\preceq\|u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\left|\mathcal{A}\left(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0}-g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}\right)\right|\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\left|\mathcal{A}\left(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}\right)\right|\\

(5.23)

About

with

I_{1}=\|u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq Ch^{2}\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.24)

For
I_{2}
, from (
5.20
), we obtain

I_{2}=0.

(5.25)

Finally, for

, utilizing the continuity of
\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
and Proposition
4.1
, Theorem
5.1
and inequality (

) from Lemma

, we have

\begin{split}I_{3}&\leq C\|\nabla_{s}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\nabla_{s}g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\\
&\leq C\left(\|\nabla_{s}(u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|\nabla_{s}(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right)\|\nabla_{s}g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\\

(5.26)

Combining (

\left|(u-u_{h})(\mathrm{y})\right|\leq Ch|\ln h|^{1/2}\left(\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right)+Ch^{2}\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

In order to prove that, the following result provides a pointwise error estimate for the tangential gradient of the approximate solution.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{h}=\{\mathrm{x}_{i},\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be an almost uniform Voronoï-Delaunay decomposition on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Assume that
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
satisfies (
2.1
) and that the unique weak solution
u
of (
2.2
) belongs to
W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
. Let
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{ij}
be a discrete flux (
4.2
), such that the discrete problem (
4.12
) has a unique solution
u_{h}\in\widetilde{U}_{h}
. Then, there exist positive constants
C
and
h_{0}
independent of
u
, such that for
0<h\leq h_{0}<1
,

\|\nabla_{s}\varepsilon_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq Ch|\ln h|\left(\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right),

where
\varepsilon_{h}=u-u_{h}
.

Proof.

We will proceed via a duality argument: we fix
\mathrm{y}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
and consider
\vec{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathrm{y},\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}}\in\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{y},\mathbb{S}^{2}}
a tangent unit vector, and from Proposition
5.7
, there exists
\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\mathrm{y},\Omega}
supported in
\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}
. Let
g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1}
be a discrete Green’s function satisfying (
5.15
) and the variational problem (
5.17b
). Also, we consider the finite element approximation
g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h}
as the unique solution of (
5.20
). From the triangular inequality, we have

\begin{split}\left|\nabla_{s}(u-u_{h})(\mathrm{y})\cdot\vec{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathrm{y},\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}}\right|&\leq\left|\nabla_{s}(u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u))(\mathrm{y})\cdot\vec{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathrm{y},\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}}\right|\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\left|\nabla_{s}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h})(\mathrm{y})\cdot\vec{\mathrm{n}}_{\mathrm{y},\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}}\right|\\
&\preceq\|\nabla_{s}(u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h})(\mathrm{x})\nabla_{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\mathrm{y},\Omega}(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x})\right|\\
&\preceq\|\nabla_{s}(u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\left|\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1})\right|\\
&\preceq\|\nabla_{s}(u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\left|\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1}-g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h})\right|\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\left|\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u,g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h})\right|+\left|\mathcal{A}(u-u_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h})\right|\\
&=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.\end{split}

(5.27)

About
I_{1}
, applying Proposition
4.1
, we have

I_{1}=\|\nabla_{s}(u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq Ch\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.28)

For
I_{2}
, from (
5.20
), we obtain

I_{2}=\left|\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1}-g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h})\right|=0.

(5.29)

For
I_{3}
, using the continuity of
\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
, Proposition
4.1
, the triangular inequality and equations (
5.21b
) and (
5.21a
) from Lemma
5.8
, we have

\begin{split}I_{3}&\leq C\|\nabla_{s}(u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\nabla_{s}g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\\
&\leq C\|\nabla_{s}(u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\left(\|\nabla_{s}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1}-g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|\nabla_{s}g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right)\\
&\leq Ch(1+|\ln h|)\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.\end{split}

(5.30)

Now about
I_{4}
, by using the linearity of
\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
and adding up and subtracting the total fluxes
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
, we obtain

\begin{split}I_{4}&\leq\left|\mathcal{A}(u,g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h})-\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h}))\right|+\left|\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h}))-\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h}))\right|\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\left|\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h}))-\mathcal{A}(u_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h})\right|\\
&=I_{4,1}+I_{4,2}+I_{4,3}.\end{split}

(5.31)

For
I_{4,1}
, applying Lemmas
4.10
and
5.8
for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
and
g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h}\in\widetilde{U}_{h}
, we find that

\begin{split}I_{4,1}&=\left|(f,g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h}-\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h}))\right|\leq Ch\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\nabla_{s}g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\\
&\leq Ch(1+|\ln h|)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.\end{split}

(5.32)

For
I_{4,2}
, by using Lemma
4.7
and collecting the inequalities (
5.21a
) and (
5.21b
) from Lemma
5.8
, we arrive at

\begin{split}I_{4,2}\leq Ch^{2}\|\nabla_{s}u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\nabla_{s}g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq Ch^{2}(1+|\ln h|)\|\nabla_{s}u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.\end{split}

(5.33)

Similarly, for
I_{4,3}
, from Lemma
4.9
and Lemma
5.8
, we obtain

I_{4,3}\leq Ch^{2}\|\nabla_{s}u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\|\nabla_{s}g^{\mathrm{y}}_{1,h}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq Ch^{2}(1+|\ln h|)\|\nabla_{s}u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.34)

Notice that from triangle inequality, we obtain

\|\nabla_{s}u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\|\nabla_{s}(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|\nabla_{s}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.35)

Thus, gathering all the estimates (
5.32
)-(
5.35
) into (
5.31
), follows that

I_{4}\leq Ch(1+|\ln h|)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+Ch^{2}(1+|\ln h|)\left(\|\nabla_{s}(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|\nabla_{s}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right).

(5.36)

Finally, combining (
5.36
) with (
5.27
)–(
5.30
), for
h_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, such that
0<h\leq h_{0}<1
and by applying the maximum value, we find

\|\nabla_{s}(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq Ch|\ln h|\left(\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right),

which leads to the desired result.
∎

Now, we show error estimates for approximate solution in
\max

Theorem 5.12
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.11
. Then, there exist positive constants
C
and
h_{0}
independent of
u
, such that for
0<h\leq h_{0}<1
,

\|\varepsilon_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq Ch|\ln h|\left(\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right),

where
\varepsilon_{h}=u-u_{h}
.

Proof.

We proceed similarly to Theorem
5.11
. We fix
\mathrm{y}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, and from Proposition
5.7
, there exists a smooth function
\delta^{\mathrm{y}}
supported in
\widetilde{\mathrm{T}}_{ijk}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
. Let
g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0}
be a discrete Green’s function satisfying (
5.15
) and the variational problem (
5.17a
). We also consider the finite element approximation
g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}
as a unique solution of the problem (
5.20
). By using triangular inequality, we have

\begin{split}\left|(u-u_{h})(\mathrm{y})\right|&\leq\left|(u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u))(\mathrm{y})\right|+\left|(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h})(\mathrm{y})\right|\\
&\preceq\|u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h})(\mathrm{x})\delta^{\mathrm{y}}(\mathrm{x})ds(\mathrm{x})\right|\\
&\preceq\|u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\left|\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0})\right|\\
&\preceq\|u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\left|\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0}-g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h})\right|\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\left|\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u,g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h})\right|+\left|\mathcal{A}(u-u_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h})\right|\\
&=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.\end{split}

(5.37)

For
I_{1}
, from Proposition
4.1
, we have

I_{1}=\|u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq Ch^{2}\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.38)

For
I_{2}
, from (
5.20
), we have

I_{2}=\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0}-g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h})=0.

(5.39)

For
I_{3}
, using the continuity of
\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
, Proposition (
4.1
) and Lemma
5.8
with (
5.21c
), we obtain

I_{3}=\left|\mathcal{A}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u,g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h})\right|\leq Ch|\ln h|^{1/2}\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.40)

About
I_{4}
, analogously to Theorem
5.11
, by using the linearity of
\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
and the total fluxes
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
, we have

\begin{split}I_{4}&\leq\left|\mathcal{A}(u,g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h})-\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}))\right|+\left|\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}))-\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}))\right|\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\left|\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}))-\mathcal{A}(u_{h},g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h})\right|\\
&=I_{4,1}+I_{4,2}+I_{4,3}.\end{split}

(5.41)

For
I_{4,1}
, by using Lemmas
4.10
and
5.8
, we find

I_{4,1}=\left|\mathcal{A}(u,g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h})-\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}))\right|=\left|(f,g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}-\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}))\right|\leq Ch|\ln h|\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.42)

For

, Lemma

I_{4,2}=\left|\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}))-\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},\widetilde{\mathrm{I}}_{h}(g^{\mathrm{y}}_{0,h}))\right|\leq Ch^{2}|\ln h|\|\nabla_{s}u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

, there exists

such that for all

, we have

\begin{split}\|\nabla_{s}u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq Ch|\ln h|\left(\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right)+\|\nabla_{s}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.\end{split}

Then

I_{4,2}\leq Ch^{2}|\ln h|\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.43)

As for

, using Lemma

, we have

I_{4,3}\leq Ch^{2}|\ln h|\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.44)

Consequently, for

small enough and gathering (

) with (

)–(

), we have

I_{4}\leq Ch|\ln h|\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(5.45)

Finally, combining (

) with (

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{h}=\{\mathrm{x}_{i},\widetilde{\mathrm{V}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be an almost uniform Spherical Centroidal Voronoï-Delaunay decomposition (SCVT) on

f\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
and that

). Let
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{ij}
be the discrete flux defined in (
4.2
), such that the discrete problem (
4.12
) has a unique solution
u_{h}\in\widetilde{U}_{h}

C
and
h_{0}
, independent of

, such that for
0<h\leq h_{0}<1
,

\|\varepsilon_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq Ch^{2}\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+Ch\left(\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right),

where
\varepsilon_{h}=u-u_{h}
.

Proof.

From triangular inequality, Proposition
4.1
, Lemma
4.2
and Theorem
5.5
, we then obtain

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\|u\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+Ch^{-1}\|\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(u)-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

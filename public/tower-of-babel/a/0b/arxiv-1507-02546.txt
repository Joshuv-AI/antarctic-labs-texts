A Study on Edge-Set Graphs of Certain Graphs
Johan Kok, N. K. Sudev, K. P. Chithra

Abstract
Let G ⁡ ( V , E ) G(V,E) simple connected graph, with | E | = ϵ |E|=\epsilon . In this paper, we define an edge-set graph 𝒢 G \mathscr{G}_{G} constructed from the graph G G such that any vertex v s , i v_{s,i} of 𝒢 G \mathscr{G}_{G} corresponds to the i i -th s s -element subset of E ⁡ ( G ) E(G) and any two vertices v s , i , v k , m v_{s,i},v_{k,m} of 𝒢 G \mathscr{G}_{G} are adjacent if and only if there is at least one edge in the edge-subset corresponding to v s , i v_{s,i} which is adjacent to at least one edge in the edge-subset corresponding to v k , m v_{k,m} , where s , k s,k are positive integers. It can be noted that the edge-set graph 𝒢 G \mathscr{G}_{G} of a graph G G is dependent on both the structure of G G as well as the number of edges ϵ \epsilon . We also discuss the characteristics and properties of the edge-set graphs corresponding to certain standard graphs.

Edge-set graph, total edge-degree of a graph, edge-degree of vertex, connected edge dominating set, artificial edge-set element.

2
Edge-Set Graph of a Graph

Let
A
be a non-empty finite set. We denote
\mathcal{S}
be the collection of all
s
-element subsets of
A
(arranged in some order), where
1\leq s\leq|A|
, and the
i
-th element
\mathcal{S}
by
A_{i,s}
. In this paper, we study certain graphs whose vertices are labeled by distinct elements of the collection
\mathcal{P}(A)-\{\emptyset\}
in an injective manner. We denote the vertex of a graph corresponding to the set
A_{s,i}
by
v_{s,i}

Using these concepts, similar to the notion of a set-graph, let us first define the notion of an edge-set graph of a given graph
G
as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G(V,E)
be a non-empty finite graph with
|E|=\epsilon
and
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{P}(E)-\{\emptyset\}
, where
\mathcal{P}(E)
is the power set of the edge set
E(G)
. For
1\leq s\leq\epsilon
, let
\mathcal{S}
be the collection of all
s
-element subsets of
E(G)
and
E_{s,i}
be the
i
-th element of
\mathcal{S}
. Then, the
edge-set graph
corresponding to
G
, denoted by
G_{E^{(\epsilon)}}
or
\mathscr{G}_{G}
, is the graph with the following properties.

(i)

|V(\mathscr{G}_{G})|=2^{\epsilon}-1
so that there exists a one to one correspondence between
V(\mathscr{G}_{G})
and
\mathcal{E}
.

(ii)

Two vertices, say
v_{s,i}
and
v_{t,j}
, in
\mathscr{G}_{G}
are adjacent if some elements (edges of
G
) in
E_{s,i}
is adjacent to some elements of
E_{t,j}
in
G
.

From the above definition, it can be seen that the edge-set graph
\mathscr{G}_{G}
of a given graph
G
is dependent not only on the number of edges
\epsilon
, but the structure of
G
also. Therefore, we have distinct edge-set graphs for non-isomorphic graphs of the same size.

As in the case of a set-graph
G_{A^{(n)}}
, it can be observed that an edge-set graph
\mathscr{G}_{G}
has an odd number of vertices. It can also be noted that if
G
is a trivial graph, then
\mathscr{G}_{G}
is an empty graph (since
\epsilon=0
). Also, we have
\mathscr{G}_{P_{2}}=K_{1}
and
\mathscr{G}_{P_{3}}=C_{3}
.

In this paper, we use the following conventions.

(i)

If an edge
e_{j}
is incident with vertex
v_{k}
, then we write it as
(e_{j}\to v_{k})
.

(ii)

If the edges
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
of a graph
G
are adjacent, then we write it as
e_{i}\cong e_{j}
.

(iii)

The
n
vertices of the path
P_{n}
are positioned horizontally and the vertices and edges are labeled from left to right as
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},\ldots,v_{n}
and
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},\ldots,e_{n-1}
, respectively.

(iv)

The
n
vertices of the cycle
C_{n}
are seated on the circumference of a circle and the vertices and edges are labeled clockwise as
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},\ldots,v_{n}
and
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},\ldots,e_{n}
, respectively such that
e_{i}=v_{i}v_{i+1}
, in the sense that
v_{n+1}=v_{1}
.

Analogous to the degree of a vertex in a given graph
G
, we define the edge-degree of elements (vertices or edges) in
G
as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

For a graph
G
with edges
E(G)=\{e_{i}:1\leq i\leq\epsilon\}
, the
edge-degree of an edge

e_{i}
of
G
incident with vertex
v_{k}
is equal to the number of edges
e_{j}\to v_{k},e_{i}\neq e_{j}
and denoted by
d_{G(v_{k})}(e_{i})
. If the edge
e_{i}
is incident with vertices
v_{k},v_{l}
, then the
general edge-degree
of
e_{i}
is defined to be
d_{G}(e_{i})=d_{G(v_{k})}(e_{i})+d_{G(v_{l})}(e_{i})=d_{G}(v_{k})+d_{G}(v_{l})-2
. (Also see
[
1
]
).

Similarly, the edge-degree of a vertex of a given graph
G
is defined as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

For a graph
G
with edges
E(G)=\{e_{i}:1\leq i\leq\epsilon\}
the
edge-degree of a vertex

v_{i}
, denoted by
d_{G(e)}(v_{i})
, is defined as
d_{G(e)}(v_{i})=\sum\limits_{(e_{l}\to v_{i})}d_{G(v_{i})}(e_{l})
.

Using the above definitions, we introduce the notion of the total edge-degree of a graph as follows.

Definition 2.4
.

The
total edge-degree
of a graph G, denoted by
d^{t}_{G(e)}(G)
, is defined by
d^{t}_{G(e)}(G)=\sum\limits_{v_{i}\in V(G)}d_{G(e)}(v_{i})
.

Invoking these definitions given above, it can be noticed that both
d^{t}_{G(e)}(G)
and
d_{G(e)}(v_{i})
are single values, while
d_{G(v_{k})}(e_{j})\leq d_{G(v_{m})}(e_{j}),(e_{j}\to v_{k}),(e_{j}\to v_{m})
.

Proposition 2.5

The induced graph

Let

Analogous to the corresponding theorem on the relation between the minimum and maximum degree of vertices in a set-graph, we propose the following theorem.

Theorem 2.6
.

For the edge-set graph
\mathscr{G}_{G}
,
\epsilon\geq 1
corresponding to a given graph
G
, we have
\Delta(\mathscr{G}_{G})=2(2^{\epsilon-1}-1)
and
\delta(\mathscr{G}_{G})\geq 2(\epsilon-1)
.

Proof.

First part of the Theorem follows immediately by Theorem
1.4
, we have
\Delta(\mathscr{G}_{G})=2(2^{\epsilon-1}-1)
.

Next, note that the graph
G
with
\epsilon
edges, with
\min\{d^{t}_{G(e)}(G)\}=2(\epsilon-1)
is the path
P_{\epsilon+1}
. It can be verified that for
P_{n},2\leq n\leq 4
,
\delta(\mathscr{G}_{P_{\epsilon+1}})=2(\epsilon-1)
, where
\epsilon=1,2,3
. For
P_{5}
, we have
d^{t}_{P_{5}(e)}(P_{5})=2(4-1)=6<8=\delta(P_{5,E^{(4)}})
. In a similar way, by mathematical induction, the result
2(\epsilon-1)\leq\delta(P_{(\epsilon+1),E})\leq\delta(\mathscr{G}_{G})
,
\epsilon\geq 1
follows.
∎

The following theorem describes the condition required for an edge-set graph to be a complete graph.

Theorem 2.7
.

For
\epsilon\geq 4
, the edge-set graph of a given graph
G
is complete if and only if
G\cong K_{1,\epsilon}
. In other words, the edge-set graph of a graph
G
, with more than three edges, is a complete graph if and only if
G
is a star graph.

Proof.

First consider the graph
G=K_{1,4}
, whose central vertex is denoted by
u
and the other vertices are denoted by
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}
respectively. Let the edges of
G
be given by
e_{i}=uv_{i};~1\leq i\leq 4
. Clearly, the number of non-empty subsets of
E(K_{1,4})
is given by
2^{4}-1
and each subset has at least one edge
e_{i}\in E(K_{1,4})
adjacent to at least one edge
e_{j}\in E(K_{1,4})
in each of the other subsets. Hence, pairwise there exists an edge
v_{s,i}v_{t,j}
for all
s
-element and
t
-element subsets of
E(K_{1,4})
. Hence,
K_{(1,4),E^{(4)}}
is complete. Now, assume that the results holds for
G=K_{1,k}
,
k
being a positive integer greater than
4
. Now, consider the graph
G=K_{1,(k+1)}
with
u
as the central vertex and
v_{k+1}
the new vertex in
K_{1,(k+1)}
that is not in
K_{1,k}
. Then, the new edge
e_{k+1}=uv_{k+1}
is adjacent to all other edges in
G
and hence the vertices in the edge-set graph with the set-label containing the element
e_{k+1}
will also be adjacent to all other vertices of the edge-set graph
\mathscr{G}_{K_{1,k+1}}
. Therefore,
\mathscr{G}_{K_{1,k+1}}
is also a complete graph. Hence, by mathematical induction, the result holds for all
K_{1,\epsilon},\epsilon\geq 4
.

Conversely, assume the edge-set graph
\mathscr{G}_{G},~|V(\mathscr{G}_{G})|=2^{\epsilon}-1\geq 15
is complete. Therefore,
\mathscr{G}_{G}-v_{\epsilon,1}
on
2^{\epsilon}-2=\frac{1}{2}(2^{\epsilon+1}-1)
vertices is complete as well. Applying the inverse of Definition
2.1
, to
\mathscr{G}_{G}-v_{\epsilon,1}
results in a smallest graph
K_{1,\epsilon}
. Assume the edge-set graph
\mathscr{G}_{G}
also corresponds to
K_{1,\epsilon}+v_{i}v_{j}
with
v_{i}v_{j}
an arbitrary additional edge. Now, we must consider an edge-set graph on
2^{\epsilon+1}-1>\frac{1}{2}(2^{\epsilon+1}-1)+1
edge-subsets. The aforesaid edge-set graph is not isomorphic to the initial graph
\mathscr{G}_{G},|V(\mathscr{G}_{G})|=2^{\epsilon}-1\geq 15
. Hence,
K_{1,\epsilon}
is the unique graph corresponding to the complete edge-set graph,
\mathscr{G}_{G},|V(\mathscr{G}_{G})|=2^{\epsilon}-1\geq 15\Rightarrow\epsilon\geq 4
. This completes the proof.
∎

It can be followed that the edge-set graphs corresponding to
K_{1,1},K_{1,2}
and
K_{1,3}
are complete. Furthermore, for any edge
e_{i}
in the cycle
C_{n},n\geq 4
there is exactly
n-3
edges which are not adjacent to
e_{i}
. By Theorem 2.3,
\mathscr{G}_{C_{n}}
is not complete. Hence,
C_{3}
is the only cycle for which the corresponding edge-set graph is complete.

Theorem 2.8
.

For
\epsilon=n\geq 2
, we have
\sum\limits_{v_{i,j}\in V(\mathscr{G}_{G})}d_{\mathscr{G}_{G}}(v_{i,j})>\sum\limits_{v_{i,j}\in V(G_{A^{(n)}})}d_{G_{A^{(n)}}}(v_{i,j})
.

Proof.

Since paths have the minimally connectivity, we first prove the result for paths. It is to be noted that For
\epsilon=n=2
, we have
\mathscr{G}_{P_{3}}\cong K_{3}
and
G_{A^{(2)}}\cong P_{3}
. Hence,
\sum\limits_{v_{i,j}\in V(\mathscr{G}_{P_{3}})}d_{\mathscr{G}_{P_{3}}}(v_{i,j})\geq\sum\limits_{v_{i,j}\in V(\mathscr{G}_{P_{3}})}d_{\mathscr{G}_{P_{3}}}(v_{i,j})=6>4=\sum\limits_{v_{i,j}\in V(G_{A^{(2)}})}d_{G_{A^{(2)}}}(v_{i,j})
.

Assume the result holds for all paths
P_{k+1}
hence, by implication it holds for all graphs
G
on
\epsilon=n=k
edges.

Now consider
P_{k+2}=P_{k+1}+e_{k+1}
. Note that that in the set-graph
G_{A^{(k+1)}}
, singleton subsets are pairwise non-adjacent (see
[
6
]
) while in an edge-set graph some pairs of singleton edge-subsets are adjacent if and only if the corresponding edges are adjacent in
G
. Hence, it can be noted that
d_{\mathscr{G}_{P_{k+2}}}(v_{1,k+1})=d_{G_{A^{(k+1)}}}(v_{1,k+1})+1>d_{G_{A^{(k+1)}}}(v_{1,k+1})
. Hence,
\delta(\mathscr{G}_{P_{k+2}})>\delta(G_{A^{(k+1)}})
. Similarly, we have
d_{\mathscr{G}_{P_{k+2}}}(v_{s,i})\geq d_{G_{A^{(k+1)}}}(v_{s,i})
,
\forall s,i
. We also note that
G_{A^{(k+1)}}
has a unique vertex having degree equal to
\Delta(G_{A^{(k+1)}})
(see
[
6
]
) when the edge-set graph
\mathscr{G}_{P_{k+2}}
has four such vertices. Hence, we have
\sum\limits_{v_{i,j}\in V(\mathscr{G}_{P_{k+2}})}d_{\mathscr{G}_{P_{k+2}}}(v_{i,j})>\sum\limits_{v_{i,j}\in V(G_{A^{(k+1)}})}d_{G_{A^{(k+1)}}}(v_{i,j})
. Therefore, the result follows for all paths by mathematical induction.

Since all other connected graphs have higher connectivity than paths, the result,
\sum\limits_{v_{i,j}\in V(\mathscr{G}_{G})}d_{\mathscr{G}_{G}}(v_{i,j})>\sum\limits_{v_{i,j}\in V(G_{A^{(n)}})}d_{G_{A^{(n)}}}(v_{i,j})
, holds for any connected graphs.
∎

In
[
6
]
, it is proved that there exists a unique vertex
v_{n,1}
in a set-graph
G_{A^{(n)}}
having degree
\Delta(G_{A^{(n)}})
. In edge-set graphs, the analogous result does not hold. In order to discuss the existence of multiple vertices with the maximum degree in an edge-set graph,we need the following definition.

Definition 2.9
.

A
connected edge dominating set
of a graph
G
is an edge dominating set
X\subseteq E(G)
where the induced graph
\langle X\rangle
is a connected subgraph of
G
. The minimum number of elements in a connected edge dominating set of a graph
G
is called the
connected edge domination number
(CED-number) of
G
.

In view of this definition, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
X
be a connected edge dominating set of a graph
G
with
\epsilon\geq 2
and let
V_{X}(\mathscr{G}_{G})
be the the subset of vertex set of the edge-set graph
\mathscr{G}_{G}
defined by
V_{X}(\mathscr{G}_{G})=\{v_{s,i}:E_{s,i}\in\mathcal{S},X\subseteq E_{s,i}\}
. Then, we have
d_{\mathscr{G}_{G}}(v_{s,i})=\Delta(\mathscr{G}_{G}),\forall v_{s,i}\in V_{X}(\mathscr{G}_{G})
.

Proof.

By the choice of
X
, it is the set-labels of some element in
V_{X}(\mathscr{G}_{G})
. Consider any edge
e_{j}\in E(G)
. Then, we consider the following cases.

Case-1:
If
e_{j}\in X
, then there exists a vertex, say
v_{l,k}
, in
\mathscr{G}_{G}
corresponding to
X
, which is adjacent to all other vertices corresponding to the edge-subsets
E_{s,i}
containing the edge
e_{j}
. Since
X
is an edge dominating set onto itself, this argument holds for all edges in
X
.

Case-2:
Since
X
is an edge dominating set, all edges in
E(G)-X
are adjacent to some edges in
X
. If
e_{j}\in E(G)-X
, then a similar argument holds.

From the above two cases, we can see that any arbitrary vertex
v_{l,k}
of
V_{X}(\mathscr{G}_{G})
is adjacent to all vertices in
\mathscr{G}_{G}
. Hence, we have
d_{\mathscr{G}_{G}}(v_{s,i})=\Delta(\mathscr{G}_{G})
.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

The edge-set
E(G)
is the set-label of some vertex in
V(\mathscr{G}_{G})
and for any edge
e_{i}\in E(G),i\leq\epsilon
, the edge-subsets
E(G)-e_{i}
are connected edge dominating sets in
G
. Hence, we have
d_{\mathscr{G}_{G}}(v_{\epsilon,1})=\Delta(\mathscr{G}_{G})
and
d_{\mathscr{G}_{G}}(v_{\epsilon-1,j})=\Delta(\mathscr{G}_{G})
.

By Theorem
2.7
, we note that the edge-set graphs corresponding to paths, cycles and complete graphs are not complete graphs. Hence, determining the maximum and minimum degrees of the corresponding edge-set graphs arouses much interest in this context.

Let us now introduce the notion of connected edge domination index of a given graph as follows.

Definition 2.12
.

The
connected edge domination index
(CED-index) of a graph
G
is defined as the number of smallest connected edge dominating sets in
G
and is denoted by
\mathfrak{E}(G)
.

The CED-indices of paths, cycles and complete graphs are determined in the following results.

Proposition 2.13
.

The CED-index of a path
P_{n}
is
1
.

Proof.

The path
P_{n}
,
n\geq 3
has exactly three connected edge dominating sets, namely
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},\ldots,e_{n-2}\}
,
\{e_{2},e_{3},e_{4},\ldots,e_{n-1}\}
and
\{e_{2},e_{3},e_{4},\ldots,e_{n-2}\}
. Clearly, the third set is the smallest connected edge dominating set. That is, number of minimal connected edge dominating set
\mathfrak{E}(P_{n})=1
.
∎

Proposition 2.14
.

The CED-index of a cycle
C_{n}
is
n
.

Proof.

Consider the set of edge pairs
\mathfrak{S}=\{\{e_{i},e_{i+1}\}:1\leq i\leq n\}
of the cycle
C_{n}
, in the sense that
e_{n+1}=e_{1}
. Clearly,
E(C_{n})-\{e_{i},e_{j}\}
,
\forall\{e_{i},e_{j}\}\in\mathfrak{S}
are all smallest connected edge dominating sets of
C_{n}
. Hence, we have
\mathfrak{E}(C_{n})=n
.
∎

Proposition 2.15
.

The CED-index of a complete graph is
\frac{n}{2}(n-1)!
.

Proof.

It is known that
K_{n},n\geq 3
has
\frac{1}{2}(n-1)!
distinct Hamiltonian cycles (see
[
7
]
). Also, a minimal connected edge dominating set of a Hamiltonian cycle of
K_{n}
is also a smallest connected edge dominating set of
K_{n}
. By Proposition
2.14
, we have
\mathfrak{E}(K_{n})=\frac{n}{2}(n-1)!
.
∎

In the proof of Proposition
2.13
it was noted that
P_{n}
,
n\geq 3
has exactly three connected edge dominating sets hence,
\mathscr{G}_{P_{n}}
has exactly four vertices with maximum degree. Let
\mathfrak{M}(\mathscr{G}_{G})
denote the number of vertices of
\mathscr{G}_{G}
having the maximum degree
\Delta(\mathscr{G}_{G})
.

The number of maximum degree vertices in the edge-set graphs corresponding to the graphs
C_{n}
and
K_{n}
are determined in the following results.

Proposition 2.16
.

The number of maximum degree vertices in the edge-set graph of
C_{n}
is
3n+1
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, any smallest connected edge dominating set of
C_{n}
say,
\{e_{2},e_{3}.e_{4},\ldots,e_{n-1}\}
can be extended to a larger connected edge dominating set, say
\{e_{2},e_{3},e_{4},\ldots,e_{n-1},e_{n}\}
or
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},\ldots,e_{n-1}\}
. Hence, an additional
2n
such extended edge sets exists.
E(C_{n})
is also a connected edge dominating set as well so invoking Proposition
2.14
, implies
\mathfrak{M}(C_{n})=3n+1
.
∎

Proposition 2.17
.

The number of maximum degree vertices in the edge-set graph of
K_{n}
is
\frac{1}{2}(3n+1)(n-1)!
.

Proof.

Similar to the reasoning in the above proposition, each of the
\frac{1}{2}(n-1)!
Hamiltonian cycles in
K_{n}
is a connected edge dominating set of
K_{n}
. Also, each of the smallest connected edge dominating sets can be extended in the manner shown in Proposition
2.16
. Hence,
\mathfrak{M}(C_{n})=3\frac{n}{2}(n-1)!+\frac{1}{2}(n-1)!=(\frac{3n+1}{2})(n-1)!
.
∎

The next result is another obvious but important property of edge-set graphs, which leads to an interesting result in respect of regular graphs.

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
e
be an edge of a graph
G
having the minimum edge degree in
G
. Then, the vertex
v
in
\mathscr{G}_{G}
corresponding to the singleton edge-subset
\{e\}
has minimum degree in
\mathscr{G}_{G}
.

Proof.

Assume that the edge
e
has minimum edge-degree
d_{G}(e)=l
. Label the edges adjacent to
e
as
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},\ldots,f_{l}
. From the definition edge-set graphs, it follows that a vertex
v
is adjacent to
v_{s,i}
if and only if some of these edges
f_{i}
belongs to
E_{s,i}
. This number of edges that are adjacent to
e
in
G
is equal to the degree of
v
in
\mathscr{G}_{G}
.

Now, assume there exists a vertex
v_{t,j}
with
d_{\mathscr{G}_{G}}(v_{t,j})=\delta(\mathscr{G}_{G})<d_{\mathscr{G}_{G}}(v)
. It implies that by Definition
2.1
, the vertex
v_{t,j}
is adjacent to
v_{k,m}
if and only if some or all edges
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},\ldots,g_{s}
, which are in
E_{k,m}
and an edge
e^{\ast}\in E_{t,j}
is adjacent to an edge
g_{i}\in E_{k,m}
, with
s<l
and
d_{G}(g_{i})<s
. This contradicts the assumption that
d_{G}(e)
has minimum degree in
G
. Hence,
d_{\mathscr{G}_{G}}(v)=\delta(\mathscr{G}_{G})
.
∎

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.18
, we observe that the edge-set graph
\mathscr{G}_{G}
corresponding to an
r
-regular graph
G
has
\epsilon
vertices,
v_{1,1},v_{1,2},v_{1,3},\ldots,v_{1,\epsilon}
, with degree equal to
\delta(\mathscr{G}_{G})
. Also, we note that an edge
e
is adjacent to exactly
d_{G}(e)
edges in
G
. Therefore, there are exactly
\epsilon-d_{G}(e)
edges that are not adjacent to the edge
e
in
G
. This leads us to the following result.

Proposition 2.19
.

The degree of the vertex
v_{1,i}
corresponding to
\{e_{i}\}
in
\mathscr{G}_{G}
is given by
d_{\mathscr{G}_{G}}(v_{1,i})=2^{\epsilon}-2^{\epsilon-d_{G}(e_{i})}
.

Proof.

The edge-set graph
\mathscr{G}_{G}
has
2^{\epsilon}-1
vertices. Exactly
2^{\epsilon-d_{G}(e_{i})}-1
edge-subsets do not contain an edge adjacent to
e_{i}
as an element. Hence, exactly
2^{\epsilon}-2^{\epsilon-d_{G}(e_{i})}
edge-subsets do contain an edge adjacent to
e_{i}
as an element. Therefore, the degree of the vertex
v_{1,i}
in
\mathscr{G}_{G}
is
2^{\epsilon}-2^{\epsilon-d_{G}(e_{i})}
.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
2.19
, an
r
-regular graph
G
on
\nu
vertices has
\delta(\mathscr{G}_{G})=2^{\frac{1}{2}r\nu}(1-\frac{1}{2^{\frac{1}{2}(r-1)}})
.

Let us now recall the well-known theorem on Eulerian graphs.

Theorem 2.20
.

[
5
]
A connected graph
G
is Eulerian if and only if
G
has no odd degree vertices.

In view of Theorem
2.20
, we establish Eulerian nature of the edge-set graphs in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.21
.

The edge-set graph of any connected graph
G
is an Eulerian graph.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{G}_{G}
is the edge-set graph of a given graph
G
with
\epsilon
edges. Note that the number of subsets of
E(G)
containing the same
r
edges is
2^{\epsilon-r}
, where
r\leq\epsilon
, which is always an even integer.

Let
e_{i}
be an arbitrary edge of
G
. Since
G
is a connected graph, the edge
e_{i}
is adjacent to at least one edge in
G
. Without loss of generality, let the edge
e_{i}
is adjacent to
r
edges in
G
, where
r\geq 1
. Let
e_{j_{1}},e_{j_{2}},e_{j_{3}},\ldots,e_{j_{r}}
be the
r
edges in
E(G)
that are adjacent to
e_{i}
in
G
Then, every vertex
v
in
\mathscr{G}_{G}
, whose set-label contain the element
e_{i}
must be adjacent to the vertices of
\mathscr{G}_{G}
, whose set-labels contain at lease one element
e_{j_{l}}
, where
1\leq l\leq r
. Let
E_{l}
denotes the subset of
E(G)
containing the element
e_{j_{l}}
. Then, by the principle of inclusion and exclusion, we have the number of vertices in
G_{G}
that are adjacent to the vertex
v
is
|\bigcup\limits_{l=1}^{r}E_{j_{l}}|=\sum\limits_{L}(-1)^{|L|-1}|\bigcap\limits_{l\in L}E_{j_{l}}|
, where
\emptyset\neq L\subseteq\{1,2,3,\ldots,r\}
. Note that every term in the above series is an even integer and as a result
d_{\mathscr{G}_{G}}(v)
is always an even integer. Since
e_{i}
is an arbitrary edge of
G
,
v
is also an arbitrary vertex (corresponding to the choice of
e_{i}
) of
G_{G}
and hence every vertex of
G_{G}
is an even degree vertex. Then, by Theorem
2.20
,
G_{G}
is an Eulerian graph.
∎

3
Conclusion and Scope for Further Studies

So far we have discussed the properties and characteristics of a new types of graphs called edge-set graphs derived from the edge sets of given graphs. This study is only a preliminary study on edge-set graphs and several related areas still remain to be explored. In this paper, we have established certain results for paths, cycles and complete graphs only. Extending these results to more standard graphs are much interesting and challenging problems. More fruitful results are expected on certain edge-set graphs on the effective utilisation of Definitions
2.2
, Definitions
2.3
and Definitions
2.4
to more graph classes.

It seems to be worthy and promising for future studies on the relationship between
\mathscr{G}_{G}
,
\mathscr{G}_{G+e}
and, where
G+e
is the graph obtained by joining two non-adjacent vertices in
G
by the edge
e
. The results related to the edge-set graphs of the graphs obtained by different graph operations are also open. Another important area that offer much for further investigations is the study on the edges of the edge-set graphs and set-graphs of given graphs. More studies are possible on the comparison of the set-graphs and edge-set graphs derived from the edge sets of different graphs. All these facts highlight the wide scope for further research in this area.

Label Differential Privacy via Aggregation
Anand Brahmbhatt*, Rishi Saket*, Shreyas Havaldar*, Anshul Nasery*, Aravindan Raghuveer*

Abstract
In many real-world applications, due to recent developments in the privacy landscape, training data may be aggregated to preserve the privacy of sensitive training labels. In the learning from label proportions (LLP) framework, the dataset is partitioned into bags of feature-vectors which are available only with the sum of the labels per bag. A further restriction, which we call learning from bag aggregates (LBA) is where instead of individual feature-vectors, only the (possibly weighted) sum of the feature-vectors per bag is available. We study whether such aggregation techniques can provide privacy guarantees under the notion of label differential privacy (label-DP) previously studied in for e.g. [Chaudhuri-Hsu’11, Ghazi et al.’21, Esfandiari et al.’22]. It is easily seen that naive LBA and LLP do not provide label-DP. Our main result however, shows that weighted LBA using iid Gaussian weights with m m randomly sampled disjoint k k -sized bags is in fact ( ε , δ ) (\varepsilon,\delta) -label-DP for any ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 with δ ≈ exp ​ ( − Ω ​ ( k ) ) \delta\approx\textnormal{exp}(-\Omega(\sqrt{k})) assuming a lower bound on the linear-mse regression loss. Further, the ℓ 2 2 \ell_{2}^{2} -regressor which minimizes the loss on the aggregated dataset has a loss within ( 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) \left(1+o(1)\right) -factor of the optimum on the original dataset w.p. ≈ 1 − exp ​ ( − Ω ​ ( m ) ) \approx 1-\textnormal{exp}(-\Omega(m)) . We emphasize that no additive label noise is required. The analogous weighted-LLP does not however admit label-DP. Nevertheless, we show that if additive N ⁡ ( 0 , 1 ) N(0,1) noise can be added to any constant fraction of the instance labels, then the noisy weighted-LLP admits similar label-DP guarantees without assumptions on the dataset, while preserving the utility of Lipschitz-bounded neural mse-regression tasks. Our work is the first to demonstrate that label-DP can be achieved by randomly weighted aggregation for regression tasks, using no or little additive noise.

Introduction

The notion of
differential privacy
introduced
[
8
]
has become a widely studied measure of the privacy guarantee of a randomized
mechanism
i.e., a data processing algorithm. Roughly speaking, it states that the output distribution of the mechanism should not change significantly with
unit
changes to the input data. In other words, an adversary which is interested in some small (sensitive) part of the data cannot easily detect changes to it using the mechanism’s output. For e.g., in machine learning a differentially private (DP) model trainer would be robust to changes in a single training example i.e., a pair of feature-vector and its label.

In many cases, the sensitive information may entirely reside in the labels of the training feature-vectors, e.g. the votes of electors or the outcomes of medical tests. The corresponding mechanisms have been studied using the notion of
label differential privacy

[
2
]
in which the input data changes only on one training label.
In differentially private (DP) mechanisms the guaranteed bound on the distributional shift is typically achieved by adding independent noise to each element (or label in the label-DP case) of the dataset.

Recently, aggregation based frameworks such as
learning from label proportions
(LLP) have assumed importance due to privacy concerns
[
26
,
23
]
. In LLP, the training data is partitioned into
bags
, and while for each bag its constituent feature-vectors are available, only the sum of the labels in the bag can be observed. In this way, the labels of individual feature-vectors are obfuscated.
In some situations, due to instrumentation or efficiency considerations instead of a bag’s feature-vectors only their linear sum is made available
[
36
]
. We refer to this framework as
learning from bag aggregates
(LBA).

Unlike additive noise based DP mechanisms, LLP and LBA aggregation occur naturally in real-world applications due to either systemic or regulatory limitations, and we believe it is important to understand how much privacy they preserve. However, while LLP and (to a lesser extent) LBA have been studied in several previous works, there have been little formal privacy guarantees shown.

We study label differential privacy (label-DP) for LBA and LLP in the random bags model. In particular, we investigate whether such aggregation mechanisms can guarantee label-DP on their own or in conjunction with (more efficient) noise mechanisms, while at the same time preserving the utility of natural regression tasks.

It is easy to observe that the naive LBA and LLP are by themselves not label-DP when the dataset is randomly partitioned into bags. This is because changing a single label changes the average label of the dataset which is the sum of the bag label-sums. A similar argument rules out privacy of these mechanisms when a significant fraction of the dataset is aggregated into bags. So the question then becomes whether and in what scenarios can enhancements to LBA and LLP provide label-DP guarantees while providing the desired utility bounds.

Informal description of our results

We propose a generic bag sampling process with iid weights for the examples in each bag. Using this we define a
weighted
version of LBA as follows: the weighted-LBA algorithm outputs for each sampled bag the weighted sum of the feature-vectors as the aggregate feature-vector along with the weighted sum of the labels as the aggregate label. Note that the weighted-LBA mechanism does not add
additive
noise to the labels.

Our first result shows that label-DP can be achieved via the weighted-LBA mechanism using iid Gaussian weights under mild assumptions on the non-degeneracy of the dataset labels, while preserving the utility (optimum) of linear regression using bounded norm regressors with the
mean squared error
(mse) loss. The non-degeneracy is used to ensure that even after fixing the bag aggregate feature-vector, there is enough aggregate label variance to obtain the desired privacy bound.

However, using the same reasoning which rules out label-DP of naive LLP, it can also be seen that a weighted version of LLP – where the bag feature-vectors along with their weights are individually made available – is not label-DP.
Nevertheless, we show in our second result that this can be overcome by adding additive noise to (any) small fraction of the dataset’s labels. In particular, using iid additive Gaussian noise of
unit variance
only for a
fixed small fraction
of the labels followed by weighted-LLP with Gaussian weights can ensure label-DP. We call this the
noisy
weighted-LLP mechanism. Note that the additive label noise is on a small but constant fraction of the labels, and therefore it shifts the optimim of mse-linear regression. Nevertheless, we show that this mechanism preserves the utility w.r.t. the noisy dataset of neural mse-regression using neural networks with Lipschitz-bounded outputs and bounded weights, which includes the linear regression task mentioned above.

Next we provide an overview of the previous work on LLP and LBA. We then formally define the setup and state our results followed by a discussion on their importance and an overview of the techniques used to prove them.

For the utility bound, we first show that for any

4
Conclusions and Future Work

Our work shows that it is possible to achieve label-DP using enhancements of natural aggregation mechanisms with little or no additive label noise.
Under a mild non-degeneracy conditions on the underlying data, LBA with random aggregation weights is label-DP while preserving the utility (optimum) of the linear regression task. Similar guarantees are provided by weighted LLP applied with additive noise added to only a small fraction of the instance labels, while preserving the utility of general neural regression.

While we obtain strong inverse exponential (in either bag size or number of bags) error bounds for privacy and utility, they are asymptotic and yield unrealistically large bag sizes and number of bags for typical problem settings. In particular, the net based arguments and the deviation bounds on perturbed Gaussians cause significant blowup. In future work, these bounds may be tightened using either alternate analytical tools or more efficient aggregation mechanisms.

Appendix E
Neural Network Setup

We will show utility bound over a general neural network. The dataset setup is the same as that in Sec.
1.2
for
noisy-wtd-LLP
. We assume that for a given dataset
\mathbf{D}:=\{{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)},y^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{m}
,
y^{(i)}\in[-B_{1},B_{1}]
and
\|{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}\|_{2}\leq B_{2}
for all

. We consider the class
\mathcal{F}_{1}
of neural-networks
f
characterized by its
d^{\prime}
weights
\mathbf{s}_{f}
which satisfy:
\|\mathbf{s}_{f}\|_{2}\leq B_{4}
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
, and the following Lipshcitzness property:
\left|f({\mathbf{x}})-f^{\prime}({\mathbf{x}})\right|\leq L\|\mathbf{s}_{f}-\mathbf{s}_{f^{\prime}}\|_{2}
, for
f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
. We use
B_{\textnormal{loss}}
as an upper bound on squared loss on any point of
\mathbf{D}
. We will prove these properties for bounded neural networks. The output of the algorithm is an LLP dataset. Given access to it, the objective is to minimize
{\sf{val}}(\tilde{\mathbf{D}}_{\textnormal{LLP}},f)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\overline{y}^{(j)}-\sum_{i\in S_{j}}w_{ij}f({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}))^{2}
, where
{\sf{opt}}(\tilde{\mathbf{D}}_{\textnormal{LLP}},\mathcal{F}_{1}):=\inf\limits_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{1}}{\sf{val}}(\tilde{\mathbf{D}}_{\textnormal{LLP}},f)
.

E.1
Properties of bounded neural networks

Define a function
f_{l}
as follows

f_{l}({\mathbf{x}};{\mathbf{W}})=\begin{cases}{\mathbf{W}}_{l}[o(f_{l-1}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{W}}))\,\mid\,1]&\text{ if }l>0\\
{\mathbf{x}}&\text{ if }l=0\end{cases}

(43)

A neural network
f
of
l_{0}
layers can be defined as
f=f_{l_{0}}
. Here
[\cdot\mid 1]
represents the operation of appending
1
to a vector,
o(\cdot)
is the activation function which is
1
-lipschitz and
{\mathbf{W}}_{l}
is the weight matrix of the
l^{th}
layer. We say this neural network is bounded of the frobenius norm of every
{\mathbf{W}}_{l}
is bounded by some positive constant
K
. Let us call this class of neural networks
\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{NN}}(K,l_{0})
. WLOG let
K\geq B_{2}
(if that is not the case then take
K=\max(K,B_{2})
. Observe that if
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{NN}}(K,l_{0})
then,
\|{\mathbf{s}}_{f}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{l_{0}}K
.

Lemma E.1

The output of neural networks in
\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{NN}}(K,l_{0})
is bounded by
K\sqrt{\tfrac{K^{2l_{0}+2}-1}{K^{2}-1}}
. It follows that the mean squared error is bounded by
\left(B_{1}+K\sqrt{\tfrac{K^{2l_{0}+2}-1}{K^{2}-1}}\right)^{2}
.

Using Cautchy-Schwartz inequality and the fact that
\|o({\mathbf{x}})\|_{2}\leq\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}
, for all
l>0
,

\displaystyle\|[f_{l}({\mathbf{x}};{\mathbf{W}})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{W}}_{l}\|_{2}^{2}(\|o(f_{l-1}({\mathbf{x}};{\mathbf{W}}))\|_{2}^{2}+1)

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{W}}_{l}\|_{F}^{2}(\|f_{l-1}({\mathbf{x}};{\mathbf{W}})\|_{2}^{2}+1)

\displaystyle\leq K^{2}(\|f_{l-1}({\mathbf{x}};{\mathbf{W}})\|_{2}^{2}+1)

(44)

and
\|f_{0}({\mathbf{x}};{\mathbf{W}})\|_{2}^{2}=\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}\leq K^{2}
. Unrolling the recursion,
\|[f_{l_{0}}({\mathbf{x}};{\mathbf{W}})\|_{2}^{2}\leq K^{2}\tfrac{K^{2l_{0}+2}-1}{K^{2}-1}
.

\square

Lemma E.2

f\in\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{NN}}(K,l_{0})
are
\max\left\{\sqrt{l_{0}\tfrac{K^{2l+2}-1}{K^{2}-1}},l_{0}\right\}
-lipschitz w.r.t the weights.

For convenience, let

and
f^{\prime}=f_{l_{0}}(\cdot,{\mathbf{W}}^{\prime})
. Using the triangular inequality,
1
-lipschitzness of

for all

\displaystyle\|f_{l}({\mathbf{x}})-f_{l}^{\prime}({\mathbf{x}})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{W}}_{l}\|_{2}\|f_{l-1}({\mathbf{x}})-f^{\prime}_{l-1}({\mathbf{x}})\|_{2}+\|{\mathbf{W}}_{l}-{\mathbf{W}}^{\prime}_{l}\|_{2}\|[f^{\prime}_{l-1}({\mathbf{x}})|1]\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq K\|f_{l-1}({\mathbf{x}})-f^{\prime}_{l-1}({\mathbf{x}})\|_{2}+\|{\mathbf{W}}_{l}-{\mathbf{W}}^{\prime}_{l}\|_{2}\sqrt{\tfrac{K^{2l+2}-1}{K^{2}-1}}

and
\|f_{0}({\mathbf{x}})-f_{0}^{\prime}({\mathbf{x}})\|_{2}=0
. Unrolling the recursion, we get that

\displaystyle\|f_{l_{0}}({\mathbf{x}})-f_{l_{0}}^{\prime}({\mathbf{x}})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}\sqrt{\tfrac{K^{2l+2}-1}{K^{2}-1}}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{l_{0}}\|{\mathbf{W}}_{l}-{\mathbf{W}}^{\prime}_{l}\|_{2}\right)&\text{ if }K>1\\
\sum_{l=1}^{l_{0}}\|{\mathbf{W}}_{l}-{\mathbf{W}}^{\prime}_{l}\|_{2}&\text{ if }K\leq 1\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}\sqrt{\tfrac{K^{2l+2}-1}{K^{2}-1}}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{l_{0}}\|{\mathbf{W}}_{l}-{\mathbf{W}}^{\prime}_{l}\|_{F}\right)&\text{ if }K>1\\

\end{cases}

(45)

\square

This concludes the proof.

\square

Lemma H.5

Consider a finite sequence

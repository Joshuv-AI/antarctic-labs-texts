On lattice polarizable cubic fourfolds
Song Yang and Xun Yu

Abstract
. We extend non-emtpyness and irreducibility of Hassett divisors to the moduli spaces of M M -polarizable cubic fourfolds for higher rank lattices M M , which in turn provides a systematic approach for describing the irreducible components of intersection of Hassett divisors. We show that Fermat cubic fourfold is contained in every Hassett divisor, which yields a new proof of Hassett’s existence theorem of special cubic fourfolds. We obtain an algorithm to determine the irreducible components of the intersection of any two Hassett divisors and we give new examples of rational cubic fourfolds. Moreover, we derive a numerical criterion for the algebraic cohomology of a cubic fourfold having an associated K3 surface and answer a question of Laza by realizing infinitely many rank 11 11 lattices as the algebraic cohomologies of cubic fourfolds having no associated K3 surfaces.

Introduction

Cubic fourfolds have received considerable attention in the last few decades,

2.1.

Definition 2.1
.

A

is a finite rank free
\Z
-module
L
together with a symmetric and non-degenerate bilinear form

(-.-)_{L}:L\times L\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}.

Let
L
be a lattice and
m\in\Z

L(m)
denotes the same free
\Z
-module with the bilinear form
(-.-)_{L(m)}:=m(-.-)_{L}
.
Let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be two lattices.

L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
denotes the orthogonal direct sum of

and
L_{2}
,
namely, the bilinear form
(x+y.x^{\prime}+y^{\prime})_{L_{1}\oplus L_{2}}:=(x.x^{\prime})_{L_{1}}+(y.y^{\prime})_{L_{1}}
.
For the bilinear form, we will drop the subscript if without causing any confusion.

Let
(e_{1},e_{2},...,e_{r(L)})
be a basis of a lattice
L
of rank

.
We call
((e_{i}.e_{j}))_{1\leq i,j\leq r(L)}
the
Gram matrix
of

with respect to

.
The

of a lattice

is the determinant of the Gram matrix with respect to an arbitrary basis of

.
A lattice
L
is called

if its discriminant
\disc(L)=\pm 1
.
An element

if
(x.x)=2
.
A lattice

is called

otherwise, it is called

.
The bilinear form
(-.-)
on

The latter intersection matrix can be diagonalized with only

The

where

So the rank

A lattice

Definition 2.4
.

A

2.3.
Extensions

The glue group plays a fundamental role in classifying extensions of a lattice.
Let
L
be a lattice.
An
overlattice
of
L
is a lattice
M
such that
L\subset M\subset L^{\vee}
and
M/L
is a finite abelian group.
Note that the subgroup
H_{M}:=M/L\subset G(L)
is isotropic.
Moreover, the set of overlattices
L\subset M\subset L^{\vee}
is bijectively corresponding to the set of isotropic subgroups
0\subset H_{M}\subset G(L)
.
Notice that
[M:L]=|H_{M}|
and
[M:L]^{2}|\disc(M)|=|\disc(L)|=|G(L)|
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be two lattices.
A
gluing map
is an isomorphism
\varphi:H_{1}\longrightarrow H_{2}

between subgroups
H_{i}\subset G(L_{i})
such that

b_{G(L_{1})}(x,y)=-b_{G(L_{2})}(\varphi(x),\varphi(y)),

for
x,y\in H_{1}
.

Therefore, the subgroup
{H}_{M}:=\{(x,\varphi(x))\mid x\in H_{1}\}\subset G(L_{1}\oplus L_{2})
is isotropic,
and thus
\varphi
defines a lattice as

(2.1)

M=L_{1}\oplus_{\varphi}L_{2}:=\{(x,y)\in L_{1}^{\vee}\oplus L_{2}^{\vee}\mid\bar{x}\in H_{1},\bar{y}\in H_{2},\bar{y}=\varphi(\bar{x})\}.

Note that
\disc(L_{1})\disc(L_{2})=\disc(L_{1}\oplus_{\varphi}L_{2})|H_{M}|^{2}
.
Then
M
is a primitive extension of
L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
in the sense of that
M/L_{i}
is torsion-free.
Moreover, the set of primitive extensions
L_{1}\oplus L_{2}\subset M

is bijective to the set of gluing map
\phi:H_{1}\rightarrow H_{2}
of subgroups of
G(L_{1})
and
G(L_{2})
.

Finally, we conclude this section reviewing the extending isometries.
An isometry
f\in\mathrm{O}(L)
extends to an overlattice
M\supset L

if
\bar{f}(H_{M})=H_{M}
.
Also, there is a bijection between the set of extensions
f_{1}\oplus_{\varphi}f_{2}\in\mathrm{O}(M)
of
f_{1}\oplus f_{2}\in\mathrm{O}(L_{1}\oplus L_{2})
and the set of gluing maps
\varphi:H_{1}\rightarrow H_{2}
with
\varphi\circ f_{1}=f_{2}\circ\varphi
.

3.
Special cubic fourfolds

This section reviews some important aspects on lattice theory and Hodge theory of cubic fourfolds and mainly focuses on special cubic fourfolds;

Let

It is well-known that the singular cohomology

is torsion-free
and the Hodge diamond of

(3.1)

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\begin{matrix}&&&&&1\\
&&&&0&&0\\

&&0&&0&&0&&0\\
&0&&1&&21&&1&&0\end{matrix}\end{array}

Moreover, by Poincaré duality and Hodge-Riemann bilinear relations,

(21,2)

Note that

4.
M
-polarizable cubic fourfolds

In this section, we discuss some basic properties (Lemma

,
Corollary

,
a generalization of Hassett divisors,
of

-polarizable cubic fourfolds.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
M
be a positive definite lattice of rank
2\leq r(M)\leq 21
. We say a cubic fourfold

is

-polarizable

\iota:M\hookrightarrow A(X)\;\;\textrm{such that}\;\;h^{2}_{X}\in\iota(M)

Remark 4.2
.

(1) A cubic fourfold

is a special cubic fourfold with a labelling

of discriminant

if and only if

is a
K_{d}
-polarizable cubic fourfold. So lattice polarizable cubic fourfolds are generalization of special cubic fourfolds and
\mathcal{C}_{M}
is a generalization of Hassett divisors

toward generalizing Hassett’s results (Theorem
3.2
and Corollary

to lattices

of higher rank.

(2) Notice that the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{M}
in this paper is different from that in
[

]
(see also
[

]

together with a prescribed primitive embedding

such that

in
[

]
is the same as
\mathcal{C}_{(M,\mathfrak{o},\phi)}
defined in Definition
4.5
below, where

.

(3) The notion of
M
-polarized cubic fourfolds is analogous to lattice polarized K3 surfaces defined by Dolgachev

]
.
For study of

-polarized cubic fourfolds and their moduli for explicit

of higher rank
(see e.g.
[

Definition 4.3
.

An element
\mathfrak{o}
of a lattice

is called

if
(\mathfrak{o}.\mathfrak{o})=3
and the orthogonal complement
\langle\mathfrak{o}\rangle^{\bot}_{M}
is an even lattice.

For any cubic fourfold
X
, the lattice
\langle h_{X}^{2}\rangle^{\bot}_{A(X)}
is even. Thus, for a positive definite lattice
M
, if there exists a
M
-polarizable cubic fourfold, then
M
contains a distinguished element. By direct computation, we have the following

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{o}
be an element of a lattice
M
of rank
r(M)\geq 2
with
(\mathfrak{o}.\mathfrak{o})=3
. Suppose
(\mathfrak{o},e_{1},...,e_{r(M)-1})
is a basis of
M
.
Then
\mathfrak{o}
is a distinguished element of
M
if and only if the integer
(e_{i}.e_{i})-(\mathfrak{o}.e_{i})
is even for every
1\leq i\leq r(M)-1
.

A
marking
of a cubic fourfold
X
is an isometry

\varphi:H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\Lambda

such that
\varphi(h_{X}^{2})=h^{2}
. To study the structure of
\mathcal{C}_{M}
, we need the following

Definition 4.5
.

Let
M
be a positive definite lattice containing a distinguished element
\mathfrak{o}
.

(i)

We define

\CC_{(M,\mathfrak{o})}:=\{[X]\in\CC\mid\;(X,\iota)\textrm{ is an }M\text{-polarized cubic fourfold with }\iota(\mathfrak{o})=h_{X}^{2}\}.

(ii)

We set

\mathcal{E}_{(M,\mathfrak{o})}:=\{\phi\mid\,\phi:M\hookrightarrow\Lambda\text{ is a primitive embedding with }\phi(\mathfrak{o})=h^{2}\}.

(iii)

For any
\phi\in\mathcal{E}_{(M,\mathfrak{o})}
,

\mathcal{C}_{(M,\mathfrak{o},\phi)}:=\{[X]\in\mathcal{C}\mid\,\exists\text{ marking }\varphi\text{ of }X\text{ such that }\;h^{2}\in\phi(M)\subset\varphi(A(X))\subset\Lambda\}.

The following lemma is clear from Definition
4.5
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
M
be a positive definite lattice containing a distinguished element
\mathfrak{o}
. Then

(1)

\mathcal{C}_{(M,\mathfrak{o})}=\cup_{\phi\in\mathcal{E}_{(M,\mathfrak{o})}}\mathcal{C}_{(M,\mathfrak{o},\phi)}
.

(2)

For any

and any
\phi\in\mathcal{E}_{(M,\mathfrak{o})}
,
we have
\mathcal{C}_{(M,\mathfrak{o},\phi)}=\mathcal{C}_{(M,\mathfrak{o},g\circ\phi)}
.

For rank two positive definite lattices
M
with a distinguished element, the three notions
\mathcal{C}_{M}
,
\CC_{(M,\mathfrak{o})}
,
\mathcal{C}_{(M,\mathfrak{o},\phi)}
give the same set of cubic fourfolds.

Corollary 4.7

(Hassett
[
Has00
,
Has16
]
)
.

Let
M
be a positive definite rank-two lattice of discriminant

.
Suppose that

contains a distinguished element
\mathfrak{o}
.
Then one of the following two conditions must hold:

(i)

d>0,\,d\,\equiv\,2\,(\mathrm{mod}\;6)

and there exists

such that

and its Gram matrix is

\small{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}3&1\\

(ii)

d>0,\,d\,\equiv\,0\,(\mathrm{mod}\;6)
and there exists

such that

and its Gram matrix is

\small{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}3&0\\

In particular, if

is another positive definite lattice containing a distinguished element
\mathfrak{o}^{{}^{\prime}}

(M^{{}^{\prime}},\mathfrak{o}^{{}^{\prime}})

Similarly, for a higher-rank positive definite lattice
M
,
we have the following:

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
M
be a positive definite lattice containing a distinguished element
\mathfrak{o}_{1}

If
\mathfrak{o}_{2}
is another distinguished element of

, then there is an isometry

such that

\mathcal{C}_{M}=\CC_{(M,\mathfrak{o}_{1})}

Proof.

We consider the lattice
L:=\langle\mathfrak{o}_{1},\mathfrak{o}_{2}\rangle\subset M
.
Its Gram matrix as follows:

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}3&a\\
a&3\end{array}\right).

By definiteness,
a=\pm 2,\pm 1,0
.
Using Lemma
4.4
, we can rule out

.
Hence,
a=\pm 1
, and we have
L\cong K_{8}
. Replacing
\mathfrak{o}_{2}
by
-\mathfrak{o}_{2}
if necessary, we may and will assume

.

Let
N:=L^{\perp}_{M}
. Let
L^{\prime}:=N^{\perp}_{M}
. Then
L^{\prime}
is the primitive closure of
L
in
M
. Thus, either
[L^{\prime}:L]=2
or
L^{\prime}=L
. Note that
L^{\prime}\oplus_{\phi}N=M
for some gluing map
\phi:H_{1}\longrightarrow H_{2}
, where
H_{1}\subset G(L^{\prime})
and
H_{2}\subset G(N)
. Next, our goal is to find an isometry
f\in\mathrm{O}(M)
such that
f(\mathfrak{o}_{1})=\mathfrak{o}_{2}
by extending an isometry of
L^{\prime}\oplus N
to that of
L^{\prime}\oplus_{\phi}N=M
.
Since the possible orders of the subgroup
H_{i}
are
1,2,4,8
, the arguments can be divided into three cases:

(
Case 1
) If the order
|H_{1}|=8
, then
L^{\prime}=L
,
H_{1}=G(L)=\langle\overline{\frac{-3\mathfrak{o}_{1}+\mathfrak{o}_{2}}{8}}\rangle\cong\Z/8\Z
.
Thus, by (

), there exists an element
\alpha\in N
such that

\frac{\alpha}{8}\in N^{\vee}\;\textrm{and}\;\frac{-3\mathfrak{o}_{1}+\mathfrak{o}_{2}}{8}+\frac{\alpha}{8}\in M.

Set
\beta:=\frac{-3\mathfrak{o}_{1}+\mathfrak{o}_{2}+\alpha}{8}\in M
.
Then we have
(\mathfrak{o}_{1}.\beta)=-1
,
(\mathfrak{o}_{2}.\beta)=0
(i.e.,
\beta\in\langle\mathfrak{o}_{2}\rangle^{\perp}
) and
\beta+\mathfrak{o}_{2}\in\langle\mathfrak{o}_{1}\rangle^{\perp}_{M}
.
Therefore, we get

(\beta+\mathfrak{o}_{2}.\beta+\mathfrak{o}_{2})=(\beta.\beta)+2(\beta.\mathfrak{o}_{2})+(\mathfrak{o}_{2}.\mathfrak{o}_{2})=(\beta.\beta)+3\in 2\Z.

This contradicts

(
Case 2
) Suppose

and

. Then
|H_{1}|=1,2
or

We choose an isometry
f_{1}\in\mathrm{O}(L)
such that
f_{1}(\mathfrak{o}_{1})=\mathfrak{o}_{2}
and
f_{1}(\mathfrak{o}_{2})=\mathfrak{o}_{1}
.
Thus,
\bar{f}_{1}|{G(L)}=\Id_{G(L)}
.
Consider

.
Then the isometry

extends to an isometry

.

(
Case 3
) Assume

and
[L^{\prime}:L]=2
. Then
\disc(L^{\prime})=2
,
L^{\prime}=\langle\mathfrak{o}_{1},\frac{\mathfrak{o}_{1}+\mathfrak{o}_{2}}{2}\rangle

.
we have

H_{1}=\langle 0\rangle\;\textrm{or}\;\langle\overline{\frac{\mathfrak{o}_{1}+\mathfrak{o}_{2}}{4}}\rangle.

Agian, there exists an isometry
f_{1}\in\mathrm{O}(L^{\prime})
such that
f_{1}(\mathfrak{o}_{1})=\mathfrak{o}_{2}
and
f_{1}(\mathfrak{o}_{2})=\mathfrak{o}_{1}
. As in (Case 2), the isometry

Remark 4.9

For higher rank cases,

Next we recall the Torelli theorem for cubic fourfolds.

Proposition 4.13

Proposition 4.14
.

Let

\mathfrak{o}

Proof.

(1) By Proposition

This section is mainly dedicated to the proof of Theorem

For a rank-two positive definite lattice

(see Theorem
1.1
)
.

be a positive definite lattice of rank

\mathfrak{o}

; and

Proposition 5.3

Let

First of all, let us setup some notations for later use:

•

the lattice

(1,0)

•

the lattice

(0,2)

•

the lattice

For one thing,
we notice that the glue group

For another,

Lemma 7.1
.

Let

Then

if and only if there exists a primitive embedding
\varphi:M_{2}\hookrightarrow M_{1}

\varphi(\mathfrak{o}_{2})=\mathfrak{o}_{1}

Proof.

By Lemma

,

to

∎

r(A(X))=11
is achieved for some cubic fourfold
X
with nonadmissible
A(X)
.
We give the following affirmative answer:

Corollary 8.18
.

Let
n_{i}
(
i=1,2,...,10
) be positive integers with
n_{i}\geq 2
for all
i
. Let
M
be a rank
11
positive definite lattice with a basis
\mathbf{b}:=(\mathfrak{o},\mu_{1},\mu_{2},...,\mu_{10})
such that the Gram matrix is equal to the following diagonal matrix

{\rm diag}(3,2n_{1},2n_{2},...,2n_{10}).

Then there exists a cubic fourfold
X
and an isometry
\phi:M\longrightarrow A(X)
such that
\phi(\mathfrak{o})=h_{X}^{2}
.
Moreover, the lattice
M
is not admissible if one of the following three statements is true:

(1)

3\;|\;n_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq 10
.

(2)

2\;|\;n_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq 10
.

(3)

There exists an odd prime
p\equiv 2\;({\rm mod}\;3)
such that
p\;|\;n_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq 10
.

Proof.

Clearly
\mathfrak{o}
is a distinguished element and
M
has no roots.
Thus, by Corollary
5.6
, there exists a cubic fourfold
X
and an isometry

\phi:M\longrightarrow A(X)
such that
\phi(\mathfrak{o})=h_{X}^{2}
.
Note that the form

f_{(M,\mathbf{b})}(x_{1},...,x_{10})=6(n_{1}x_{1}^{2}+n_{2}x_{2}^{2}+...+n_{10}x_{10}^{2})=\lambda g(x_{1},...,x_{10}),

where
\lambda={\rm gcd}(6n_{1},6n_{2},...,6n_{10})
and
g(x_{1},...,x_{10})
is a primitive integral quadratic form.
As a result, it follows Theorem
8.9
that
M
is nonadmissible.
∎

Remark 8.19
.

(i) For
X
in Corollary
8.18
, if the statement (1) holds, then
\ell(A(X))=r(A(X))
.
On the other hand, if (1) does not hold but (2) or (3) holds, then
\ell(A(X))=r(A(X))-1
.

(ii) For other examples, we consider the positive definite lattice
M
of rank
3\leq n\leq 11
with the Gram matrix
(a_{ij})
such that
a_{ii}=3
and
a_{ij}=1
for
i\neq j
.
We observe that
M
contains a distinguished element and
M
has no roots.
According to Corollary
5.6
,
there exists an
M
-polarizable cubic fourfold
X
containing mutually intersecting
n-1
planes with
M\cong A(X)
.
By elementary computations, we get
\lambda=4
and
\ell(A(X))=r(A(X))-1
.
By Theorem
8.8
,

A(X)
is nonadmissible; see
[
DM19
, Remark 7.4]
for
n=11
.

(iii) In
[
Laz18
]
Laza introduced the
algebraic index

\kappa_{X}:=\frac{\rho_{X}}{d_{X}}
of a cubic fourfold
X
, where
\rho_{X}:=r(A(X))-1
and
d_{X}:=\disc(A(X))
. He showed that
\kappa_{X}\leq 1
for
potentially irrational
cubic fourfolds
X
(equivalently, cubic fourfolds
X
with nonadmissible
A(X)
). Moreover, based on
[
LPZ18
]
, he showed that
\kappa_{X_{0}}=1
for some potentially irrational cubic fourfolds with
\rho_{X_{0}}=6
and
G(A(X_{0}))\cong(\Z/2\Z)^{\oplus 6}
. Now we consider
M:=A(X_{0})\oplus D_{4}(2)
, where
D_{4}
denotes the root lattice given by the corresponding Dynkin diagram (in particular,
r(D_{4})=4
,
\disc(D_{4})=4
). Then by Corollary
5.6
and Theorem
8.8
, there exists a potentially irrational cubic fourfold
X_{1}
with
A(X_{1})\cong M
. Moreover,
\ell(A(X_{1}))=\rho_{X_{1}}=10
and
\kappa_{X_{1}}=\frac{2^{10}}{2^{6}\cdot 2^{6}}=\frac{1}{4}
.

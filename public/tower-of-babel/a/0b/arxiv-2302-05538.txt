Introduction

In
[
2
]
, Cianchi and Maz’ya considered the boundary value problems

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\operatorname{div}\left(a(\left|\nabla u\right|)\nabla u\right)=f(x)&\text{in}&\Omega\\
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0&\text{on}&\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\operatorname{div}\left(a(\left|\nabla u\right|)\nabla u\right)=f(x)&\text{in}&\Omega\\
u=0&\text{on}&\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N},

N\geq 3,

f\in L^{N,1}(\Omega),
and
a:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is a function of

satisfying

-1<i_{a}:=\inf_{t>0}\frac{ta^{\prime}(t)}{a(t)}\leq s_{a}:=\sup_{t>0}\frac{ta^{\prime}(t)}{a(t)}<\infty.

(1.3)

Assuming that
\partial\Omega\in W^{2}L^{N-1,1}
and
\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathrm{d}x=0,
Cianchi and Maz’ya proved the estimate

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}\leq Cb^{-1}(\left\|f\right\|_{N,1})

for a weak solution
u
to the Neumann problem (
1.1
), where

and

is an abstract constant
that depends on
\Omega,

i_{a}
and
s_{a}.
This result is stated in
Theorem 1.1 of
[
2
]
.

As remarked in
[
2
]
, the assumption
\partial\Omega\in W^{2}L^{N-1,1}

means that the boundary of
\Omega
is locally the subgraph of a function of

N-1
variables whose derivatives up to second order are in the Lorentz space

L^{N-1,1}.
Moreover, this is the weakest possible integrability assumption
on the second-order derivatives under which
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,0}
(see

[
4
]
).

By a small change in the proof of Theorem 1.1, Cianchi and Maz’ya obtained the
same estimate (
1.4
) for the weak solution of the Dirichlet problem
(
1.2
), with
\partial\Omega\in W^{2}L^{N-1,1}
and
f\in L^{N,1}(\Omega)

(see Theorem 1.3 of
[
2
]
). Further, they showed that (
1.4
) also
holds in both problems if the hypothesis
\partial\Omega\in W^{2}L^{N-1,1}
is
replaced with the hypothesis that
\Omega
is convex (see Theorems 1.2 and 1.4
of
[
2
]

Under additional regularity conditions on

f

\partial\Omega,

in a first step of Section 4 of
[
2

, supported by estimates established
in Section 2 of that paper. The extra regularity assumptions are then removed
in three more steps of Section 4 by approximation arguments.

When restricted to the
p
-Laplacian operator, that is,
a(t)=t^{p-2},

the estimate (

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{N,1}

\Omega

In this case,

Our main goal in the present paper is to exhibit an explicit expression (not

with respect to

for the
p
-Laplacian. The well-known regularization

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N},

such that
\partial\Omega\in W^{2}L^{\theta,1},
for some

\int_{\Omega}f(x)\mathrm{d}x=0.
Let

be a weak solution
to the Neumann problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\operatorname{div}(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u)=f(x)&\text{in}&\Omega\\
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0&\text{on}&\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

\left\|\nabla u_{p}\right\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\leq C\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}2^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(p-1)^{-\frac{\theta N}{\theta-(N-1)}}\left\|f\right\|_{N,1}&\text{if}&1<p<2\\
&&\\

(1.7)

Moreover, if the assumption

is replaced

\Omega
convex, then

\left\|\nabla u_{p}\right\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\leq C\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}2^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\left\|f\right\|_{N,1}&\text{if}&1<p<2\\
p^{\frac{5}{2}}\left\|f\right\|_{N,1}&\text{if}&p\geq 2\end{array}\right.

where

is a constant depending at most on

and
\Omega.

For
\epsilon>0

a_{\epsilon}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
as

a_{\epsilon}(t):=(t^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}

(2.1)

where
p>1.
Note that

a_{\epsilon}\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))\text{ \ and \ }a_{\epsilon}(0)>0.

(2.2)

As

\frac{ta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)}{a_{\epsilon}(t)}=(p-2)\frac{t^{2}}{t^{2}+\epsilon}\quad\forall\,t>0

one can readily verify that
a_{\epsilon}
satisfies (
1.12
). In fact,
one has

i_{a_{\epsilon}}:=\inf_{t>0}\frac{ta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)}{a_{\epsilon}(t)}=\min\left\{p-2,0\right\}>-1

(2.3)

and

s_{a_{\epsilon}}:=\sup_{t>0}\frac{ta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)}{a_{\epsilon}(t)}=\max\left\{p-2,0\right\}<\infty.

(2.4)

Moreover, as
i_{a}=i_{b}=p-2,
one has

\min\left\{i_{a},0\right\}=i_{a_{\epsilon}}\leq s_{a_{\epsilon}}=\max\left\{s_{a},0\right\}.

(2.5)

Let us define the strictly increasing functions
b_{\epsilon}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
and
B_{\epsilon}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty),
respectively as

b_{\epsilon}(t)=a_{\epsilon}(t)t

(2.6)

and

B_{\epsilon}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}b_{\epsilon}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau.

The monotonicity of
b_{\epsilon}
follows from (
2.3
) as

b_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)=a_{\epsilon}(t)+ta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)=a_{\epsilon}(t)\left(1+\frac{ta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)}{a_{\epsilon}(t)}\right)\geq a_{\epsilon}(t)\left(1+i_{a_{\epsilon}}\right)>0.

It is simple to check that

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}b_{\epsilon}(t)=t^{p-1}\text{\ uniformly in
}[0,M]\text{ for every }M>0

(2.7)

and

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}B_{\epsilon}(t)=\frac{t^{p}}{p}\text{\ uniformly in }[0,M]\text{ for every }M>0.

(2.8)

Moreover, it follows from (
2.7
) that

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}a_{\epsilon}(\left|x\right|)x=\left|x\right|^{p-2}x\text{ \ uniformly in }\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\left|x\right|\leq M\right\}\text{ for every
}M>0.

(2.9)

We remark from (
2.2
)-(
2.9
) that the conclusions of Lemma 3.3 of

[
2
]
hold for
a_{\epsilon},

b_{\epsilon}
and
B_{\epsilon}.

Lemma 2.1

Let
\psi_{\epsilon}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be the
function defined as

\psi_{\epsilon}(s):=sb_{\epsilon}^{-1}(s).

(2.10)

If

then

C\psi_{\epsilon}(s)\leq\psi_{\epsilon}(Cs)\quad\forall\text{ }s\geq 0.

(2.11)

Proof.
As
b^{-1}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is increasing and
C\geq 1
,
one has

C\psi_{\epsilon}(s)=Csb_{\epsilon}^{-1}(s)\leq Csb_{\epsilon}^{-1}(Cs)=\psi_{\epsilon}(Cs)\quad\forall\text{ }s\geq 0.

Lemma 2.2

Let
h:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a function of class

C^{1}
such that

\alpha\leq\frac{th^{\prime}(t)}{h(t)}\leq\beta\quad\forall\,t>0.

(2.12)

Then,

\min\left\{c^{\alpha},c^{\beta}\right\}\leq\frac{h(\rho t)}{h(t)}\leq\max\left\{c^{\alpha},c^{\beta}\right\}\quad\forall\,c,t>0.

(2.13)

Proof.
It follows from (
2.12
) that

\frac{d}{dt}\log(t^{\alpha})\leq\frac{d}{dt}\log(h(t))\leq\frac{d}{dt}\log(t^{\alpha}).

(2.14)

If
0<c\leq 1,
then we integrate (
2.14
) over the interval
[ct,t]
to
obtain the inequalities

\log(c^{-\alpha})\leq\log(h(t)/h(ct))\leq\log(c^{-\beta})

which leads to (
2.13
) after exponentiation. Analogously, the integration
of (
2.14
) over
[t,ct]
followed by exponentiation yields (
2.13
) if

c>1.

We note that

\frac{tb_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)}{b_{\epsilon}(t)}=t\frac{a_{\epsilon}(t)+ta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)}{ta_{\epsilon}(t)}=1+\frac{ta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)}{a_{\epsilon}(t)}

so that

\min\left\{1,p-1\right\}=1+i_{a_{\epsilon}}\leq\frac{tb_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)}{b_{\epsilon}(t)}\leq 1+s_{a_{\epsilon}}=\max\left\{1,p-1\right\}\quad\forall\,t>0.

(2.15)

Thus, it follows from Lemma

\min\left\{c,c^{p-1}\right\}\leq\frac{b_{\epsilon}(ct)}{b_{\epsilon}(t)}\leq\max\left\{c,c^{p-1}\right\}\quad\forall\,c,t>0.

In the sequel

m(c,p):=\min\left\{2c,2c^{p-1},pc,pc^{p-1}\right\}

and

Lemma 2.3

For each
c>0

m(c,p)\leq\frac{tb_{\epsilon}(ct)}{B_{\epsilon}(t)}\leq M(c,p)\quad\forall\,t>0.

(2.17)

Proof.
It also follows from (

Hence, as

we obtain the bounds

\min\left\{2,p\right\}\leq\frac{tb_{\epsilon}(t)}{B_{\epsilon}(t)}\leq\max\left\{2,p\right\}.

(2.18)

Noticing that

we gather (

) and (

\min\left\{c,c^{p-1}\right\}\min\left\{2,p\right\}\leq\frac{tb_{\epsilon}(ct)}{B_{\epsilon}(t)}\leq\max\left\{2,p\right\}\max\left\{c,c^{p-1}\right\}

Let
\widehat{B_{\epsilon}}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be function

\widehat{B_{\epsilon}}(0)=0\text{ \ and \ }\widehat{B_{\epsilon}}(t):=\frac{B_{\epsilon}(t)}{t}\quad\forall\,t>0,

Proposition 2.4

One has

\min\left\{2,p\right\}B_{\epsilon}(t)\leq tb_{\epsilon}(t)\leq\max\left\{2,p\right\}B_{\epsilon}(t)\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,

(2.20)

\widehat{B_{\epsilon}}^{-1}(s)\leq C_{p}b_{\epsilon}^{-1}(s)\quad\forall\,s>0,

(2.21)

F_{\epsilon}(t)\leq tb_{\epsilon}(t)^{2}\leq K_{p}F_{\epsilon}(t)\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,

p&\mathrm{if}&p\geq 2,\end{array}\right.

and

K_{p}:=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}3&\mathrm{if}&1<p<2\\
2p-1&\mathrm{if}&p\geq 2.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

2.17
) with

Inequality (

) is equivalent to

\frac{tb_{\epsilon}(t/C_{p})}{B_{\epsilon}(t)}\leq 1\quad\forall\,t>0,

(

Proofs

Let
\left(\mathcal{R},\mathfrak{m}\right)
be a finite, positive measure
space and let
l>1.
We recall that the Lorentz space
L^{l,1}(\mathcal{R})

consists of all measurable functions
v:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such
that

\int_{0}^{\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{R})}\tau^{-1/l^{\prime}}\left|v^{\ast}(\tau)\right|\mathrm{d}\tau<\infty.

Here,
l^{\prime}:=\frac{l}{l-1}
and
v^{\ast}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
stands for the decreasing rearrangement of
v
, which is

If

(p-1)^{-1}<2^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(p-1)^{-\frac{\theta N}{\theta-(N-1)}}

(
3.28
) yields

S_{3}\leq C2^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(p-1)^{-\frac{\theta N}{(\theta-(N-1))}}

(3.33)

which combined with (
3.32
) produces (
1.7
).

Similarly, if
p\geq 2
then (
3.20
) and (
3.29
) imply that

\frac{1}{s_{p}}\leq\overline{C}_{N,\Omega}(2(2p-1))^{\frac{\theta N}{\theta-(N-1)}}<4^{\frac{\theta N}{\theta-(N-1)}}\overline{C}_{N,\Omega}p^{\frac{\theta N}{\theta-(N-1)}}.

Hence, it follows from (
3.24
) and (
3.25
) that

S_{2}\leq Cp^{2+\frac{\theta N}{\theta-(N-1)}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{C(2p-1)}S_{2}+\frac{2\left(C(2p-1)+1\right)}{\min\left\{p-1,1\right\}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{C(2p-1)}S_{2}+2\left(C(2p-1)+1\right)\leq Cp^{\frac{1}{2}}S_{2},

that is,

S_{3}\leq Cp^{\left(2+\frac{\theta N}{\theta-(N-1)}\right)+\frac{1}{2}}=Cp^{\frac{5}{2}+\frac{\theta N}{\theta-(N-1)}}.

(3.34)

Therefore, (
1.7
) follows from (
3.32
) and (
3.34
).

According to Remark
3.7
in the case where
\Omega
is convex
(
1.8
) follows from the fact that (
3.33
) and (
3.34
) can be
respectively replaced with
S_{3}\leq C2^{\frac{p}{p-1}}
and
S_{3}\leq Cp^{\frac{5}{2}}.

3.2
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this subsection we consider
N=2,

\theta>1
and
q>2.
Again, we
initially assume the regularity assumptions (
3.5
) and (
3.6
).

The inequality (
3.13
) now writes as

\begin{array}[c]{lll}2\xi_{p}F_{\epsilon}(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty})&\leq&2\xi_{p}F_{\epsilon}(t_{0})+C_{\Omega}b_{\epsilon}(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty})\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}}r^{-1/2}\phi(r)\mathrm{d}r\\
&&+\dfrac{C_{\Omega}}{\xi_{p}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}}r^{-1}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}}f^{\ast}(\rho)^{2}\mathrm{d}\rho\mathrm{d}r\\
&&+K_{p}F_{\epsilon}(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}){\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\mu(t_{0})}}k^{\ast\ast}(c_{\Omega}r^{\frac{1}{2}})r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}r\end{array}

(3.35)

whenever
t_{0}\in\left[\left|\nabla u\right|^{\ast}(\Omega/2),\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}\right],
where
k
is a
nonnegative function in
L^{\theta,1}(\partial\Omega).

Let us derive the following estimates that respectively correspond to
(
3.15
) and (
3.16
):

\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}r^{-1/2}\phi(r)\mathrm{d}r\leq C_{q,\Omega}\left\|f\right\|_{q}

(3.36)

and

\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}r^{-1}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}}f^{\ast}(\rho)^{2}\mathrm{d}\rho\mathrm{d}r\leq\widetilde{C}_{q,\Omega}\left\|f\right\|_{q}^{2}

(3.37)

where
C_{q,\Omega}
and
\widetilde{C}_{q,\Omega}
are constants that depend
only on
q
and
\left|\Omega\right|.

First, we note from Hölder inequality that

\int_{0}^{r}f^{\ast}(\tau)^{2}d\tau\leq\left\|f\right\|_{q}^{2}r^{1-(2/q)},\text{ }r\geq 0.

(3.38)

Proceeding as in
[
2
, Lemma 3.5]
, we obtain the following inequalities

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}r^{-1/2}\phi(r)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}r^{-1/2}\phi^{\ast}(r)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}r^{-1/2}\left(\phi^{\ast}(r)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}r^{-1/2}\left(\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\phi^{\ast}(\tau)^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{1/2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}r^{-1}\left(\int_{0}^{r}\phi^{\ast}(\tau)^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{1/2}\mathrm{d}r.

Here, we have used the Hardy-Littlewood inequality and the fact that

\phi^{\ast}
is decreasing.

Hence, the estimate (
3.36
) follows after using (
3.14
) and
(
3.38
):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}r^{-1/2}\phi(r)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}r^{-1}\left(\int_{0}^{r}f^{\ast}(\tau)^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{1/2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\right\|_{q}\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}r^{-(1/2)-(1/q)}\mathrm{d}r=C_{q,\Omega}\left\|f\right\|_{q}.

where
C_{q,\Omega}:=\frac{\left|\Omega\right|^{(1/2)-(1/q)}}{(1/2)-(1/q)}.

Estimate (
3.37
) also stems from (
3.38
), since

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}r^{-1}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}}f^{\ast}(\rho)^{2}\mathrm{d}\rho\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\right\|_{q}^{2}\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}r^{-1}r^{1-(2/q)}\mathrm{d}\rho\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\left\|f\right\|_{q}^{2}\frac{q}{q-2}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{q-2}{q}}=\widetilde{C}_{q,\Omega}\left\|f\right\|_{q}^{2}

where
\widetilde{C}_{q,\Omega}:=\frac{q}{q-2}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{q-2}{q}}.

Now, using (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) in (
3.35
) we obtain

\begin{array}[c]{lll}2\xi_{p}F_{\epsilon}(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty})&\leq&2\xi_{p}F_{\epsilon}(t_{0})+C_{\Omega}C_{q,\Omega}b_{\epsilon}(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty})\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f\right\|_{q}\\
&&+\dfrac{C_{\Omega}\widetilde{C}_{q,\Omega}}{\xi_{p}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f\right\|_{q}^{2}\\
&&+K_{p}F_{\epsilon}(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}){\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\mu(t_{0})}}k^{\ast\ast}(c_{\Omega}r^{\frac{1}{2}})r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}r\end{array}

(3.39)

whenever
t_{0}\in\left[\left|\nabla u\right|^{\ast}(\Omega/2),\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}\right].

Let us define
G:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
as

G(s):={\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}}k^{\ast\ast}(c_{\Omega}r^{\frac{1}{2}})r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}r=\frac{2}{c_{\Omega}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{c_{\Omega}s^{1/2}}}k^{\ast\ast}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau,\text{ \ }s>0.

If
c_{\Omega}s^{1/2}\leq\left|\partial\Omega\right|,
then

G(s)\leq\frac{2}{c_{\Omega}}(c_{\Omega}s^{1/2})^{\frac{1}{\theta^{\prime}}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{c_{\Omega}s^{1/2}}}k^{\ast\ast}(\tau)\tau^{-\frac{1}{\theta^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}\tau\leq 2(c_{\Omega})^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}s^{\frac{1}{2\theta^{\prime}}}\left\|k\right\|_{\theta,1}.

Hence, we define
\overline{s}_{p}
by the equality

2(c_{\Omega})^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}(\overline{s}_{p})^{\frac{1}{2\theta^{\prime}}}\left\|k\right\|_{\theta,1}=\frac{\xi_{p}}{K_{p}},

so that

\overline{s}_{p}=\overline{C}\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}\left(\frac{p-1}{6}\right)^{\frac{2\theta}{\theta-1}}&\mathrm{if}&1<p<2\\
\left(\frac{1}{2(2p-1)}\right)^{\frac{2\theta}{\theta-1}}&\mathrm{if}&p\geq 2\end{array}\right.

(3.40)

where
\overline{C}:=(2\left\|k\right\|_{\theta,1}(c_{\Omega})^{-\frac{1}{\theta}})^{-\frac{2\theta}{\theta-1}}.

Let
t_{p}:=\left|\nabla u\right|^{\ast}(s_{p})
where

s_{p}:=\min\left\{C_{\Omega}\text{ },\text{ }\overline{s}_{p}\right\}.

(3.41)

and

C_{\Omega}:=\min\left\{\left(\frac{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}{c_{\Omega}}\right)^{2}\;,\;\frac{\left|\Omega\right|}{2}\;,\;C^{\prime\prime}\right\}

It follows that

\displaystyle K_{p}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\mu(t_{p})}}k^{\ast\ast}(c_{\Omega}r^{\frac{1}{2}})r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=K_{p}G(\mu(t_{p}))

\displaystyle\leq K_{p}G(s_{p})\leq K_{p}2(c_{\Omega})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}(\overline{s}_{p})^{1/(2\theta^{\prime})}\left\|k\right\|_{\theta,1}=\xi_{p}.

Taking

in (

2.22
), the latter estimate
and Lemma

as in

4
An explict estimate for more general operators

Suppose that
a_{\epsilon}
is an abstract regularization of
a
(not
necessarily related to the
p
-Laplacian) satisfying the structural
conditions

-1<i_{a_{\epsilon}}:=\inf_{t>0}\frac{ta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)}{a_{\epsilon}(t)}\leq s_{a_{\epsilon}}:=\sup_{t>0}\frac{ta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(t)}{a_{\epsilon}(t)}<\infty.

Inspecting the proofs given in Section
3
and taking into account
Remark
2.5
one can deduce that

b_{\epsilon}(\left\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\right\|_{\infty})\leq S_{3,_{\epsilon}}\left\|f\right\|_{N,1}

where

S_{3,_{\epsilon}}:=\sqrt{CK_{\epsilon}}S_{2,\epsilon}+\dfrac{2(CK_{\epsilon}+1)}{1+\min\left\{i_{a_{\epsilon}},0\right\}},

with

S_{2,_{\epsilon}}\leq CS_{1,\epsilon}\left(\dfrac{3+2s_{a_{\epsilon}}}{1+\min\left\{i_{a_{\epsilon}},0\right\}}\right)^{\frac{\theta N}{\theta-(N-1)}}

and

S_{1,\epsilon}:=(2+s_{a_{\epsilon}})C_{\epsilon}=(2+s_{a_{\epsilon}})^{\frac{2+i_{a_{\epsilon}}}{1+i_{a_{\epsilon}}}}>1.

These facts imply that

b_{\epsilon}(\left\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\right\|_{\infty})\leq C\Lambda_{\epsilon}\left\|f\right\|_{N,1}

where

\Lambda_{\epsilon}:=\sqrt{(3+2s_{a_{\epsilon}})}(2+s_{a_{\epsilon}})^{\frac{2+i_{a_{\epsilon}}}{1+i_{a_{\epsilon}}}}\left(\dfrac{3+2s_{a_{\epsilon}}}{1+\min\left\{i_{a_{\epsilon}},0\right\}}\right)^{\frac{\theta N}{\theta-(N-1)}}+\dfrac{4+2s_{a_{\epsilon}}}{1+\min\left\{i_{a_{\epsilon}},0\right\}}

and
C
is a constant depending at most on
N
and
\Omega.
If
\Omega
is
convex, then the factor involving the power
\frac{\theta N}{\theta-(N-1)}

must be replaced with
1.
Moreover, if

-1<\min\left\{i_{a},0\right\}\leq\min\left\{i_{a_{\epsilon}},0\right\}\leq i_{a_{\epsilon}}\leq s_{a_{\epsilon}}\leq\max\left\{s_{a},0\right\},

then

\Lambda_{\epsilon}\leq\Lambda(i_{a},s_{a})

where
\Lambda(i_{a},s_{a})
is the explicit function of
i_{a}
and
s_{a}

obtained from
\Lambda_{\epsilon}
by replacing
s_{a_{\epsilon}}
with

\max\left\{s_{a},0\right\}
and both
i_{a_{\epsilon}}
and
\min\left\{i_{a_{\epsilon}},0\right\}
with
\min\left\{i_{a},0\right\}.
For
example, if
\Omega
is convex then

\Lambda(i_{a},s_{a})=\sqrt{(3+2\max\left\{s_{a},0\right\})}(2+\max\left\{s_{a},0\right\})^{1+\frac{1}{1+\min\left\{i_{a},0\right\}}}+\dfrac{4+2s_{a_{\epsilon}}}{1+\min\left\{i_{a},0\right\}}.

Therefore, the results of Cianchi and Maz’ya lead to global estimate

b(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty})\leq C\Lambda(i_{a},s_{a})\left\|f\right\|_{N,1}

for the solutions to (
1.6
) and (
1.9
) under the hypotheses of
Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. A similar estimate, with
\left\|f\right\|_{q}
in the place of
\left\|f\right\|_{N,1},
also
holds under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
.

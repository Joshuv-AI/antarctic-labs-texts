Abstract
The shepherding problem refers to guiding a group of agents (called sheep) to a specific destination using an external agent with repulsive forces (called shepherd). Although various movement algorithms for the shepherd have been explored in the literature, there is a scarcity of methodologies for selective guidance , which is a key technology for precise swarm control. Therefore, this study investigates the problem of guiding a single target sheep within a swarm to a given destination using a shepherd. We first present our model of the dynamics of sheep agents and the interaction between sheep and shepherd agents. The model is shown to be well-defined with no collision if the interaction magnitude between sheep and shepherd is reasonably limited. Based on the analysis with Lyapunov stability principles, we design a shepherd control law to guide the target sheep to the origin while avoiding collisions among sheep agents. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in guiding the target sheep in both small and large scale swarms.

However, the shepherd control methods mentioned above

,
18

In this study, the control objective is to use a shepherd agent to guide a sheep agent (target) in a swarm to the origin (goal). First, the repulsion-bounded swarm model is described. We then carefully analyze and rigorously prove that under relatively mild conditions, sheep in the swarm do not collide, even under the influence of a shepherd. Based on this analysis, we use the Lyapunov stability principle to design a motion controller for the shepherd to guide the target to the endpoint (i.e., the origin). Problem statement

We consider a swarm of
N>2
individuals (
sheep
) and one herder (
shepherd
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The positions of sheep
i
and shepherd at time
t\geq 0
are denoted by
x_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
y(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, respectively. Let
\mathcal{N}=\{1,2,...,N\}
.

We assume that, for all
i\in\mathcal{N}
, the velocity
\dot{x}_{i}
of the
i
th sheep is specified by a linear combination of three components:

\dot{x}_{i}=f_{ai}+f_{bi}+f_{yi},

(1)

where
f_{ai}
and
f_{bi}
represent the attraction and repulsion functions from the other sheep to the
i
th sheep, respectively, and
f_{yi}
represents the function from the shepherd to the
i
th sheep.

Below, we describe how
f_{ai}
,
f_{bi}
, and
f_{yi}
are constructed. First, the attraction function of the
i
th sheep is given by:

f_{ai}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}{\backslash\{i\}}}\phi_{a}(x_{i}-x_{j}),

(2)

where the function
\phi_{a}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is defined as

\phi_{a}({x})=-m_{a}x

(3)

for a positive constant
m_{a}
. Then,
the bounded repulsion function of the
i
th sheep is given by

f_{bi}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}{\backslash\{i\}}}\phi_{b}(x_{i}-x_{j}),

(4)

where the function
\phi_{b}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is defined as

\phi_{b}({x})=\begin{cases}m_{b}\dfrac{{x}}{\lVert{x}\rVert^{2}},&\mbox{if }\lVert{x}\rVert>\ell_{b},\\
M_{b}\dfrac{{x}}{\lVert{x}\rVert},&\mbox{if }\lVert{x}\rVert\leq\ell_{b},\end{cases}

(5)

for positive constants
\ell_{b}
,
m_{b}
, and
M_{b}
satisfying

M_{b}=\frac{m_{b}}{\ell_{b}},\quad\ell_{b}<R.

(6)

Finally, we define
f_{yi}
as

f_{yi}=\begin{cases}\gamma_{1}\dfrac{x_{i}-y}{\lVert x_{i}-y\rVert^{2}},\vskip 5.69054pt&\mbox{if $\lVert x_{i}-y\rVert>\ell_{y}$,}\\
\displaystyle\gamma_{2}\dfrac{x_{i}-y}{\lVert x_{i}-y\rVert^{2}}g(\lVert x_{i}-y\rVert),&\mbox{if $\lVert x_{i}-y\rVert\leq\ell_{y}$,}\end{cases}

(7)

where the function
g\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is defined by:

g({d})=\begin{cases}0,&\mbox{if $d=0$,}\\
\exp\left({-{1}/{d}}\right),&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

(8)

and the parameters
\gamma_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}
, and
\ell_{y}
are positive constants that satisfy

\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{1}g^{-1}(\ell_{y}).

(9)

We can now state the problem studied in this paper.

Problem 1
.

Assume that the shepherd knows all sheep positions and can calculate the interaction force
f_{aT}+f_{bT}
of the target sheep
T\in\mathcal{N}
.
Design a controller for the velocity of the shepherd such that the target sheep is asymptotically guided to the origin.

III
Collision-Free Condition

In this section, we present a sufficient condition that guarantees that no pairs of sheep will collide within a swarm. We begin by presenting the main results of this section. Within the theorem, the maximum shepherd force for one sheep is defined as follows:

f_{y}^{\max}=\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\ell_{y}},

IV
Control Design

From Theorem
1
, we conclude that no collision occurs, the dynamics of the system are continuous, and we do not need to consider collisions when using the model (
1
) to design a controller for regulating the target to the origin. In this section, we describe the design of the controller for the shepherd to ensure that the target sheep is controlled to the origin. We specifically use the Lyapunov stability theorem to ensure that the shepherd has ability of locally regulating the target to the origin.

Let us denote the sum of the interaction forces of the target as

w_{T}=f_{aT}+f_{bT}.

(39)

We remark that this quantity is supposed to be available by the shepherd. Then, using (
7
), we can rewrite the velocity of the target as

\begin{multlined}\dot{x}_{T}=\\
\begin{cases}\gamma_{1}\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert^{-2}(x_{T}-y)+w_{T},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \!\mbox{if }\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert>\ell_{y},\\
\gamma_{2}\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert^{-2}g(\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert)(x_{T}-y)+w_{T},\ \mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}\end{multlined}

(40)

The system (
40
) is controllable when
f_{y}^{\max}\geq w_{T}
for
t>0
. The controller design should base on the controllability of the (
40
). Thus we select the gains that satisfy

f_{y}^{\max}<M_{b}\quad f_{y}^{\max}\geq N(M_{b}-m_{a})

(41)

To realize our control objective, we set the desired position of the shepherd, denoted by
y_{d}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, as

y_{d}=K_{1}x_{T},

(42)

where
K_{1}
is a positive constant. This choice is intuitive because if the shepherd is always at the desired position specified above, the target sheep will be regulated to the origin by the repulsive force of the shepherd. Then, we quantify
the mismatch between the actual position
y
and desired position of the shepherd as

e_{y}=y_{d}-y.

(43)

In this study, we consider the following first-order dynamics for the shepherd:

\dot{y}=u_{y}.

(44)

The following theorem shows that we can construct a feedback controller to locally achieve the control objective stated in Problem
1
.

Theorem 5
.

Assume that inequality (
12
) is satisfied.
Define
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\}
by

\begin{multlined}\gamma(x_{T},y)=\begin{cases}\gamma_{1},&\mbox{{if} }\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert>\ell_{y},\\
\gamma_{2},&\mbox{{if} }\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert\leq\ell_{y}.\end{cases}\end{multlined}

(45)

and construct a feedback controller as

\displaystyle u_{y}

\displaystyle=\gamma\frac{e_{y}}{\lVert e_{y}\rVert}(K_{1}+\lVert x_{T}\rVert)\lVert w_{T}\rVert

(46)

\displaystyle+\gamma\frac{e_{y}}{\lVert e_{y}\rVert}\left(\frac{\lVert x_{T}\rVert^{2}+\lVert x_{T}\rVert\lVert y\rVert+K_{1}\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert}{\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert^{2}}\right).

Define

V=\frac{1}{2}\lVert x_{T}\rVert^{2}+\lVert e_{y}\rVert.

(47)

Then, for any constant
c>0
, set
V\leq c

is forward invariant under feedback controller (
46
).

Proof.

By inequality (
12
) and Theorem
1
, the existence of the solution of the system is guaranteed. Therefore, it is sufficient to demonstrate that the time derivative

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle=x_{T}^{\top}\dot{x}_{T}+\frac{(y_{d}-y)^{\top}}{\lVert y_{d}-y\rVert}(\dot{y}_{d}-\dot{y})

(48)

is negative. First, if
\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert>\ell_{y}
, then

\begin{multlined}\dot{V}=\gamma_{1}x_{T}^{\top}\left(\frac{x_{T}-y}{\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert^{2}}+w_{T}\right)\\
+K_{1}\gamma_{1}\frac{e_{y}^{\top}}{\lVert e_{y}\rVert}\left(\frac{x_{T}-y}{\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert^{2}}+w_{T}\right)-\frac{e_{y}^{\top}}{\lVert e_{y}\rVert}\dot{y}.\end{multlined}

(49)

Therefore, using (
46
) and (
49
), we can easily show
\dot{V}<0
.
Let us consider the case where
\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert\leq\ell_{y}
. In this case, we show that

\displaystyle\dot{V}=\gamma_{2}x_{T}^{\top}\left(\frac{x_{T}-y}{\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert^{2}}g(\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert)+w_{T}\right)

(50)

\displaystyle+K_{1}\gamma_{2}\frac{e_{y}^{\top}}{\lVert e_{y}\rVert}\left(\frac{x_{T}-y}{\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert^{2}}g(\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert)+w_{T}\right)-\frac{e_{y}^{\top}}{\lVert e_{y}\rVert}\dot{y}.

Because

g(\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert)=\exp(-{1}/{\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert})<1
,
we can further evaluate the derivative as

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle<\begin{multlined}\gamma_{2}\left(\frac{\lVert x_{T}\rVert^{2}+\lVert x_{T}\rVert\lVert y\rVert+K_{1}\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert}{\lVert x_{T}-y\rVert^{2}}\right)\\
+\gamma_{2}(\lVert x_{T}\rVert+K_{1})w_{T}-\frac{e_{y}^{\top}}{\lVert e_{y}\rVert}\dot{y}=0\end{multlined}

(51)

as desired. This completes the proof of the theorem.

∎

VI
Conclusion and Future works

In this study, we investigated the problem of using a shepherd to accurately guide a target in a large swarm to its destination. We first analyzed the properties of the swarm and rigorously proved through mathematical analysis that under certain inequality constraints, no sheep in the swarm will collide. Subsequently, we used the Lyapunov stability principle to design a shepherd control law that precisely guides the target to the origin. We also compared our model with a baseline shepherding control algorithm. Our experimental results demonstrate that the shepherd can precisely guide the target to the goal in both small and large swarms.
Our future work includes finding necessary and sufficient conditions of the collision-free property of shepherding swarm.

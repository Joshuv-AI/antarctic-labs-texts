Abstract
. Let P P and Q Q be polynomials in one variable over an algebraically closed field k k of characteristic zero. Let f f and g g be elements of a function field 𝐊 {\bf K} over k k such that P ⁡ ( f ) = Q ⁡ ( g ) . P(f)=Q(g). We give conditions on P P and Q Q such that the height of f f and g g can be effectively bounded, and moreover, we give sufficient conditions on P P and Q Q under which f f and g g must be constant.



1.
Introduction

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, and
let
P
and
Q
be polynomials in
k[X].
Determining when
algebraic curves defined by equations of the form
P(X)-Q(Y)=0

have irreducible components of geometric genus zero or one is or interest
both in arithmetic and complex function theory because it is related
to when such curves may have infinitely many rational solutions in a number
field or non-constant meromorphic function solutions.
Genus zero components are also related to the study of what
are known as “uniqueness polynomials.” Special cases
of this problem have been considered, but to date there has been no
complete characterization of when an algebraic curve of the
form
P(x)-Q(y)=0
has no irreducible components of genus at most one. For example, in
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
, authors considered the problem for cases of genus zero over non-archimedean field.
Fujimoto in
[
15
]
gave some sufficient conditions for the problem over complex number field under assumption
Q=cP
for some constant
c
and the polynomial
P
satisfies
Hypothesis I,
that is
P

is injective on the set of distinct zeros of
P^{\prime}.

Let
C
be a smooth curve of genus
\mathfrak{g}
over
k,

and let
{\bf K}
be its function
field. Let

F_{1}(X,Y,Z)
be the homogenization of
[P(X)-P(Y)]/[X-Y]
and

F_{c}(X,Y,Z)
,
c\neq 0,1
, be the homogenization of

P(X)-cP(Y)
. If
f
and
g
are algebraic functions in
{\bf K}

such that
P(f)=cP(g)
for some nonzero constant
c
,
then the morphism

\Phi:=(f,g,1)
is a morphism from
C
into the curve
[F_{c}(X,Y,Z)=0]
.
By the Hurwitz theorem this cannot happen if the curves
[F_{c}(X,Y,Z)=0]

have no components of genus
\leq\mathfrak{g}
.
In
[
3
]
, An and Wang gave sufficient conditions on a polynomial

P
satisfying Hypothesis I such that the height of any solution

(f,g)
with
f,g\in{\bf K}
such that
P(f)=cP(g)
could be effectively
bounded above.
The purpose of this paper is to consider more general separated
variable equations of the form
P(x)-Q(y)
and to give
some conditions on the polynomials
P
and
Q
such that if
f
and
g
are
elements of
{\bf K}
satisfying the equation
P(f)=Q(g),
then the heights of
f

and
g
can be effectively bounded.

For each point
\mathbf{p}\in C
, we may choose a uniformizer
t_{\mathbf{p}}
to define a
normalized order function

v_{\mathbf{p}}:=\text{ord}_{\mathbf{p}}:{\bf K}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}

at

\mathbf{p}
. For a non-zero element
f\in{\bf K}
, the height
h(f)
counts
the number of poles of
f
with multiplicities, i.e.

h(f):=\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in C}-\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{0,\,v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)\}.

For
[f,g]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}({\bf K})
,
its height is defined by

h(f,g):=\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in C}-\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{v_{\mathbf{p}}(f),v_{\mathbf{p}}(g)\}.

Clearly,
h(f)=h(f,1)
.

From now we will let
P(X)
and
Q(X)
be nonconstant polynomials of degree

n
and
m
, respectively, in
k[X]
. Without loss of generality,
throughout the paper we will assume that
n\geq m
. We will denote by
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},...,\alpha_{l}
and

\beta_{1},\beta_{2},...,\beta_{h}
the distinct roots of
P^{\prime}(X)
and
Q^{\prime}(X)
, respectively. We will use
p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{l}

and
q_{1},q_{2},...,q_{h}
to denote the multiplicities of the roots in

P^{\prime}(X)
and
Q^{\prime}(X)
, respectively. Thus, for some
a
,
b
in
k
,

P^{\prime}(X)=a{(X-\alpha_{1})}^{p_{1}}{(X-\alpha_{2})}^{p_{2}}...{(X-\alpha_{l})}^{p_{l}}

Q^{\prime}(X)=b{(X-\beta_{1})}^{q_{1}}{(X-\beta_{2})}^{q_{2}}...{(X-\beta_{h})}^{q_{h}}.

Recall, the polynomial
P(X)
satisfies
Hypothesis I
if

P(\alpha_{i})\neq P(\alpha_{j})\ {\rm whenever}\ i\neq j,

or in other words
P
is injective on the roots of
P^{\prime}
.

If one of the polynomials
P
or
Q
is linear, say
P(X)=aX+b,
then
(\frac{1}{a}Q(f)-b,f)
is a solution of the equation
P(X)=Q(Y),
where
f
is some nonconstant element of
{\bf K}
. Hence, from now we always assume that both
P
and
Q
are not linear polynomials.
The main results are as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
f
and
g
are two distinct
non-constant rational functions in
{\bf K}
such that
P(f)=Q(g)
. Let

\displaystyle B_{0}=\{i\mid 1\leq i\leq l,\ P(\alpha_{i})\neq Q(\beta_{j})\text{ for all $j=1,...,h\}$\hskip 10.22217ptand }

\displaystyle B_{1}=\{i\mid 1\leq i\leq h,\ Q(\beta_{i})\neq P(\alpha_{j})\text{ for

Then

(a)

nh(f)={m}h(g);

(b)

\Big({\displaystyle\sum_{i\in B_{0}}p_{i}}-\frac{m+n}{m}\Big)h(f)\leq{2\mathfrak{g}-2};

(c)

\Big(\displaystyle\sum_{i\in B_{1}}q_{i}-\frac{2m}{n}\Big)\ h(g)\leq 2\mathfrak{g}-2.

As a consequence of Theorem
1
we have the following corollary.

Corollary 2
.

With the same hypothesis as in Theorem
1
.

(a)

If either
{\displaystyle\sum_{i\in B_{0}}p_{i}}-\frac{m+n}{m}>0
or
\displaystyle\sum_{i\in B_{1}}q_{i}-\frac{2m}{n}>0
,
then the heights of
f
and
g
are effectively bounded above;

(b)

If either
{\displaystyle\sum_{i\in B_{0}}p_{i}}-\frac{m+n}{m}>\max\{0,2\mathfrak{g}-2\}
or
\displaystyle\sum_{i\in B_{1}}q_{i}-\frac{2m}{n}>\max\{0,2\mathfrak{g}-2\}
, then

f
and
g
are constant.

3
clearly,
we need to introduce the following notation:

\displaystyle A_{0}:=\{(i,j)\mid 1\leq i\leq l,1\leq j\leq h,\ P(\alpha_{i})=Q(\beta_{j})\},

\displaystyle A_{1}:=\{(i,j)\mid(i,j)\in A_{0},\ p_{i}>q_{j}\},

\displaystyle A_{2}:=\{(i,j)\mid(i,j)\in A_{0},\ p_{i}<q_{j}\}.

and

We will see that Hypothesis I tells us

Especially, when the genus

Theorem 5

Let
\mathfrak{g}=0

(B)

2.
Some lemmas

For simplicity of notation, for
i\geq 1
,
t\in{\bf K}\setminus k
and

\eta\in{\bf K}
, we denote by

d_{t}^{i}\eta:=\frac{d^{i}\eta}{dt^{i}},\qquad d_{\mathbf{p}}^{i}\eta:=\frac{d^{i}\eta}{dt_{\mathbf{p}}^{i}}.

We first recall the following well-known properties, which follow from the
Riemann-Roch theorem and the sum formula.

Proposition 6
.

Let
\eta\neq 0\in{\bf K}
and

[f,g]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}({\bf K})
. We have

(i)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in C}v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}\eta)=2\mathfrak{g}-2
if
\eta
is not constant.

(ii)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in C}v_{\mathbf{p}}(\eta)=0
.

(iii)

h(\eta f,\eta g)=h(f,g).

In order to study some sufficient conditions ensuring that the equation
P(X)=Q(Y)

has no non-constant rational function solutions, the basic idea is as follows.
Suppose there are two distinct non-constant rational functions
f
and
g

in
{\bf K}
such that
P(f)=Q(g)
. We will study the height of
f
and
g

and give upper bounds for
h(f)
and
h(g)
. We first give an upper bound
for
h(P^{\prime}(f),Q^{\prime}(g))
thanks to the following lemma.

Lemma 7
.

Suppose that
f
and

g
are distinct non-constant rational functions in
{\bf K}
such that
P(f)=Q(g)
.
Then

(i)

nh(f)=mh(g);

(ii)

h({P^{\prime}(f)},{Q^{\prime}(g)})+\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in C}\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f),v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(d_{\mathbf{p}}g)\}\leq\frac{m+n}{m}h(f)+2\mathfrak{g}-2;

where
v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(\eta):=\max\{0,v_{\mathbf{p}}(\eta)\}
for
\eta\in{\bf K}^{*}.

Proof.

Since
P(f)=Q(g)
, for any
\mathbf{p}\in C
such that
v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)<0
, we have

nv_{\mathbf{p}}(f)=v_{\mathbf{p}}(P(f))=v_{\mathbf{p}}(Q(g))=mv_{\mathbf{p}}(g).

Hence,
nh(f)=mh(g)
. This also yields that

d_{t}fP^{\prime}(f)=d_{t}gQ^{\prime}(g),

for
t
in
{\bf K}\setminus{\bf k}
, and hence

h(P^{\prime}(f),Q^{\prime}(g))=h(\frac{P^{\prime}(f)}{Q^{\prime}(g)},1)=h(\frac{d_{t}g}{d_{t}f},1)=h(d_{t}f,d_{t}g).

Since

d_{\mathbf{p}}f=d_{t}fd_{\mathbf{p}}t,
it follows that
v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f)=v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{t}f)+v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}t),

and hence

\displaystyle v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{t}f)=v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f)-v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}t).

We have

\displaystyle h({P^{\prime}(f)},{Q^{\prime}(g)})=h(d_{t}f,d_{t}g)=\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in C}-\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{t}f),v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{t}g)\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in C}-\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f)-v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}t),v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}g)-v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}t)\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in C}v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}t)+\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in C}-\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f),v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}g)\}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in C}v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}t)+\displaystyle\sum_{v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)<0}-\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f),v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}g)\}-\displaystyle\sum_{v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)\geq 0}\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f),v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}g)\}.

If
v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)<0
, then
v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f)=v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)-1
and

v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}g)=v_{\mathbf{p}}(g)-1=\frac{n}{m}v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)-1.

If
v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)\geq 0
, then
v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f)\geq 0
hence

v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f)=\max\{0,v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f)\}=v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f)

and
v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(d_{\mathbf{p}}g)=v_{\mathbf{p}}(d_{\mathbf{p}}g).

All together, we have

\displaystyle h({P^{\prime}(f)},{Q^{\prime}(g)})

\displaystyle=2\mathfrak{g}-2+\displaystyle\sum_{v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)<0}-\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)-1,\frac{n}{m}v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)-1\}-\displaystyle\sum_{v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)\geq 0}\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f),v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(d_{\mathbf{p}}g)\}

\displaystyle=2\mathfrak{g}-2+\displaystyle\sum_{v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)<0}(-\frac{n}{m}v_{\mathbf{p}}f+1)-\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in C}\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f),v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(d_{\mathbf{p}}g)\}

\displaystyle=2\mathfrak{g}-2+\frac{n}{m}h(f)+\#\{\mathbf{p}\in C\mid v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)<0\}-\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in C}\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f),v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(d_{\mathbf{p}}g)\}.

Clearly,
\#\{\mathbf{p}\in C\mid v_{\mathbf{p}}(f)<0\}\leq h(f)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle h({P^{\prime}(f)},{Q^{\prime}(g)})\leq 2\mathfrak{g}-2+\frac{m+n}{m}h(f)-\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in C}\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f),v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(d_{\mathbf{p}}g)\}.

Hence

\displaystyle h({P^{\prime}(f)},{Q^{\prime}(g)})+\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in C}\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\{v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(d_{\mathbf{p}}f),v_{\mathbf{p}}^{0}(d_{\mathbf{p}}g)\}\leq\frac{m+n}{m}h(f)+2\mathfrak{g}-2.

∎

Suppose that

Quadratic perturbations of the Schwarzschild black hole: The algebraically special sector
Jibril Ben Achour, Hugo Roussille

Abstract
We investigate quadratic algebraically special perturbations (ASPs) of the Schwarzschild black hole. Their dynamics are derived from the expansion up to second order in perturbation of the most general algebraically special twisting vacuum solution of general relativity. Following this strategy, we present analytical expressions for the axial-axial, polar-polar and polar-axial source terms entering in the dynamical equations. We show that these complicated inhomogeneous equations can be solved analytically and we present explicit expressions for the profiles of the quadratic ASPs. As expected, they exhibit exponential growth both at the past and future horizons even in the non-linear regime. We further use this result to analyze the quadratic zero modes and their interpretation in terms of quadratic corrections to mass and spin of the Schwarzschild black hole. The present work provides a direct extension beyond the linear regime of the original work by Couch and Newman.

I
Review of algebraically special modes

Linear perturbations of the Schwarzschild black hole geometry are described by the well-known Regge-Wheeler and Zerilli equations. Using the spherical symmetry of the background, these two equations reduce to simple 2d second order differential equations dictating the time and radial profile of the polar (even parity) perturbation, denoted
Y_{\text{RW}}
, and the axial (odd-parity) perturbations denoted
Y_{\text{Z}}
. These decoupled equations read respectively

\displaystyle-\derivative[2]{Y_\mathrm{RW}}{t}+\derivative[2]{Y_\mathrm{RW}}{r_*}=V_{\mathrm{RW}}(r)Y_{\mathrm{RW}}\,,

(1)

\displaystyle-\derivative[2]{Y_\mathrm{Z}}{t}+\derivative[2]{Y_\mathrm{Z}}{r_*}=V_{\mathrm{Z}}(r)Y_{\mathrm{Z}}\,,

(2)

Here,
r_{\ast}
is the tortoise coordinate defined by

\derivative{r_*}{r}=\Big(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Big)^{-1}\quad\mbox{or}\quad r_{*}(r)=r+2M\log(r - 2M)\,,

(3)

such that
r_{\ast}\rightarrow-\infty
at the horizon, i.e. when
r=2M
and the RW and Zerilli potentials are given explicitly by

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{RW}}(r)

\displaystyle=\Big(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Big)\frac{2({\lambda}_{\mathrm{a}}+1)r-6M}{r^{3}}\,,

(4)

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{Z}}(r)

\displaystyle=\Big(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Big)\frac{72M^{3}+72M^{2}r{\lambda}_{\mathrm{p}}+24Mr^{2}{\lambda}_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}+8r^{3}{\lambda}_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}({\lambda}_{\mathrm{p}}+1)}{r^{3}(6M+2r{\lambda}_{\mathrm{p}})^{2}}\,.

(5)

We have written
{\lambda}_{\mathrm{a}}
and
{\lambda}_{\mathrm{p}}
the values of
\lambda
associated respectively to axial and polar perturbations.
In these expressions, we use the integer
\lambda
defined from the angular momentum
\ell
of perturbations by

2\lambda=\ell(\ell+1)-2\,.

(6)

Solving these two wave equations requires choosing some boundary conditions which lead to qualitatively different perturbations. The quasi-normal modes (QNM) are obtained by demanding that the wave is purely in-going at the horizon and purely out-going at
{\mathcal{I}}^{+}
. Another choice consists in imposing that the wave is purely in-going at the horizon and at
{\mathcal{I}}^{+}
which gives the so called totally transmitted from the right modes (TTM
R
). Demanding instead that the wave is purely out-going at both boundaries gives the so called totally transmitted from the left modes (TTM
L
).

Another way to solve for these equations is to introduce the ansätze

Y_{\mathrm{RW}}=Y_{\mathrm{RW}}^{\mathrm{ret}}=e^{{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}(t-r_{*})}{f}_{\mathrm{a}}(r)\quad\mbox{and}\quad Y_{\mathrm{Z}}=Y_{\mathrm{Z}}^{\mathrm{ret}}=e^{-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}(t-r_{*})}{f}_{\mathrm{p}}(r)\,.

(7)

where
{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}
and
{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}
are real constants.
Injecting these expressions into (
1
) and (
2
), one obtains the following exact solutions

\kappa=\frac{(\ell-1)\ell(\ell+1)(\ell+2)}{12M}\,,\quad{f}_{\mathrm{a}}=\frac{3M+{\lambda}_{\mathrm{a}}r}{r}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{f}_{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{r}{3M+{\lambda}_{\mathrm{p}}r}\,.

(8)

These solutions are dubbed
algebraically special modes
for reasons that will be clarified in the following. They correspond to non-oscillating modes which violate the boundary conditions satisfied by the QNMs and by the TTMs
L/R
, giving rise to a new family of modes. Indeed, introducing the retarded and advanced time coordinates

u=t-r_{\ast}\qquad v=t+r_{\ast}

(9)

we see from (
7
) that while
Y_{\mathrm{RW}}
diverges at
{\mathcal{I}}^{+}
, i.e. when
u\rightarrow+\infty
, the polar perturbation
Y_{\mathrm{Z}}
diverges at the horizon, i.e. when
u\rightarrow-\infty
. This couple of ASM comes with another set of solutions obtained by reversing the time coordinate
t
, leading to

Y_{\mathrm{RW}}=Y_{\mathrm{RW}}^{\mathrm{adv}}=e^{-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}(t+r_{*})}{f}_{\mathrm{a}}(r)\quad\mbox{and}\quad Y_{\mathrm{Z}}=Y_{\mathrm{Z}}^{\mathrm{adv}}=e^{{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}(t+r_{*})}{f}_{\mathrm{p}}(r)\,.

(10)

This second couple of solutions behaves exactly in the opposite way from the former.

The status of these ASM, which are well defined exact solutions of the RW and Zerilli equations (at least when considered in a finite region of spacetime corresponding to a finite range of
u
or
v
) has attracted considerable attention over the years. Because they blow up on the horizon, these solutions of the RW and Zerilli equations cannot be considered as perturbations per se, as their evolution prevent the geometry to return back to the Schwarzschild one. Nevertheless, it is known that among the well behaved perturbations of the Schwarzschild black hole, one can find QNMs and TTMs exhibiting a vanishing frequency and a decay time which is numerically close from the ASM
[
48
,
50
,
49
]
, giving rise to well-behaved highly damped modes (see
[
52
,
53
,
54
]
for Kerr). Despite these results, the exact spectral equivalence of these specific modes to the ASM remains an open question
[
4
]
.

More importantly for the purpose of this work, this set of ASM turn out to correspond to linearized algebraically special radiative solutions of general relativity, hence their name.
This result was shown by Couch and Newman in
[
43
]
and their relation to the Chandrasekhar algebraically special modes was stressed by Qi and Schultz in
[
44
]
. This alternative derivation of these ASM makes it explicit that contrary to QNM and TTMs, one can derive their expressions without imposing boundary conditions, but simply by imposing symmetry restriction (i.e. restricting the Petrov type of the perturbed geometry). Remarkably, this strategy can be generalized to higher order in perturbation theory, giving access to an analytic treatment of the non-linear ASMs. As we shall see, studying these ASM beyond the linear order allows one to contemplate the complicated mode-mixing in an analytical form as well as to discuss the fate of non-linear zero modes in black hole perturbation theory.

II
Twisting radiative spacetime and black hole perturbations

In this section, we present the exact family of twisting vacuum radiative solutions of general relativity which will be our starting point to derive analytic expression for the quadratic algebraically special perturbations (ASP). We follow closely
[
41
]
where this family of solutions is presented in detail. Let us introduce the tetrad
\omega

given by

\omega^{l}=\differential{u}+L\differential{z}+\bar{L}\differential{\bar{z}}\,,\quad\omega^{n}=\differential{r}+W\differential{z}+\bar{W}\differential{\bar{z}}+H\omega^{l}\,,\quad\omega^{m}=\frac{\differential{\bar{z}}}{P\rho}\,,

where the functions

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{r+i\Sigma}\,,

\displaystyle 2i\Sigma

\displaystyle=P^{2}(\bar{\partial}L-\partial\bar{L})\,,

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{u}L}{\rho}+\partial(i\Sigma)\,,

\displaystyle\partial

\displaystyle=\partial_{z}-L\partial_{u}\,,

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\frac{K}{2}-r\partial_{u}(\log(P))-\frac{Mr+N\Sigma}{r^{2}+\Sigma^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=2P^{2}\real(\partial(\bar{\partial}\log P-\partial_{u}\bar{L}))\,,

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=\Sigma K+P^{2}\real(\partial\bar{\partial}\Sigma-2\partial_{u}\bar{L}\partial\Sigma-\Sigma\partial_{u}\partial\bar{L})\,.

Consider the line element given by

\differential{s}^{2}=-2\omega^{l}\omega^{n}+2\omega^{m}\omega^{\bar{m}}\,,

(13)

In this setup,

and

are real functions and

is a complex function. Let us describe the main kinematical properties of the geometries belonging to this family. By construction, one has

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}

\displaystyle=(m+iN)\rho^{3}

(14)

\displaystyle\Psi_{3}

\displaystyle=-P^{3}\rho^{2}\partial I+{\mathcal{O}}(\rho^{3})

(15)

\displaystyle\Psi_{4}

\displaystyle=P^{2}\rho\partial_{u}I+{\mathcal{O}}(\rho^{2})

(16)

The Newman-Penrose spin coefficients (NPSC) satisfy

\displaystyle\kappa=\sigma=\tau=0

while the other NPSC are non-vanishing. In particular, one has
\text{Im}(\rho)\neq 0

It follows that the null tetrad describes a geodesic, non-shearing but twisting and expanding congruence of null rays. This family of solutions describes the twisting algebraically special vacuum radiative solutions of GR. When one further imposes that the twist vanishes, i.e, that

One can show that under the above assumptions (choice of tetrad), the Einstein field equations reduce to

\displaystyle i\partial N-3(M+iN)\partial_{u}L=0\,,

(18)

\displaystyle P\quantity[\D+ 2(\D\log P - \partial_u\bar{L})]\partial\quantity[\Db(\Db\log P - \partial_u\bar{L}) + (\Db\log P - \partial_u\bar{L})^2]-\partial_{u}\quantity[P^{-3}(M + i N)]=0\,,

(19)

which provides the dynamical equations relating the three functions
(M,P,L)
. When
L=0
, the twist vanishes and this set of solutions reduces to the Robinson-Trautman family of exact radiative solutions in which case the mass
M
can only depend on

.
How can this family of exact non-linear radiative solutions be useful to investigate black hole perturbations ?

When investigating black hole perturbations, one is interested in perturbations about a Petrov type D geometry (being either Kerr or Schwarzschild). Without further restriction, general perturbations will break the algebraically special character of the background geometry resulting in a Petrov type I geometry
[
46
]
. However, it is interesting to focus on the subset of these perturbations which, while spoiling the Petrov type D character of the background, still allows the perturbed background to remain algebraically special. Since the perturbed black hole geometrie remains at most of Petrov type II, the above family of exact solutions provides a framework to analyze these algebraically special perturbations without imposing any boundary conditions.

This strategy was used by Couch and Newman in
[
43
]
to derive the linear ASP of the Schwarzschild black hole and soon after, the same approach was generalized to the linear ASP of the Kerr black hole by Wald
[
45
]
. In the following, we shall focus on the Schwarzschild perturbations. For completeness, we will first review the analysis of Couch and Newman for the linear ASP. Then, we will generalize their result to second order in perturbation theory and present the exact solutions for the quadratic ASP.

III
Linear algebraically special perturbations

We start by reviewing the derivation of the linear ASP for the Schwarzschild background. Within this large family of twisting vacuum radiative solutions described by the two functions
(M,P,L)
, the Schwarzschild geometry corresponds

P=P_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1+z\bar{z})\quad\mbox{and}\quad L=0\,.

(20)

while

is a constant corresponding to the Schwarzschild mass.
The
“usual”

(u,r,z,\bar{z})
by the change of variables

u=t-r-2M\log(r - 2M)\quad\mbox{and}\quad z=\cot\frac{\theta}{2}e^{i\varphi}\,.

In the following, we will keep working with the coordinates
(u,r,z,\bar{z})
.

III.1
Dynamics

To describe the ASP of the Schwarzschild geometry, it is natural to introduce the perturbed
(P,L)

4

In principle, one could also consider a perturbation of the mass function

.

(38)

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\cot\frac{\theta}{2}e^{i\varphi}\,,

(39)

leads to the metric

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-f\left(1-\frac{K}{f}\right)\mathrm{d}t^{2}-\frac{2K}{f}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}r-f^{-1}\left(1-\frac{K}{f}\right)\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}(1+K)\mathrm{d}\Omega^{2}

(40)

where the constants
K
are given by

K=\frac{\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}{\sqrt{3}}\,.

(41)

To remove the off diagonal terms and bring this metric to the Schwarzschild form, one can perform the following change of time coordinate given by

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{K}{2}\right)\tilde{t}+\frac{\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\tilde{r}+4M\log{(\tilde{r}-2M)}-\frac{4M^{2}}{\tilde{r}-2M}\right)\,,

(42)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{K}{2}\right)\tilde{r}\,,

(43)

which brings the metric to the standard Schwarzschild form

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2M(1+\epsilon M_{1})}{r}\right)\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\left(1-\frac{2M(1+\epsilon M_{1})}{r}\right)^{-1}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\mathrm{d}\Omega^{2}\,,

(44)

where the shift in mass is given by

M_{1}=-\frac{3K}{\sqrt{3}}\,.

(45)

As expected, the
\ell=0
polar zero mode simply shifts the Schwarzschild mass.

Next, let us consider the zero mode corresponding to
{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}={\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}=1
. The resulting geometry is stationary and given by

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-f\mathrm{d}t^{2}-2\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{a}\sin^{2}{\theta}\left(\frac{M}{r}\mathrm{d}t-\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{2f}\right)\mathrm{d}\varphi+\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}{f}+r^{2}\left(1-\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{p}\cos{\theta}\right)\mathrm{d}\Omega^{2}\,.

(46)

Despite the non standard off diagonal terms and the presence of the three constants
(M,{\mathcal{E}}_{a},{\mathcal{E}}_{p})
, this line element describes the linearized Kerr black hole solution. To see that, one can apply the following change of the angular coordinates:

\displaystyle\theta=\tilde{\theta}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{p}\sin{\tilde{\theta}}\,,\qquad\qquad\varphi=\tilde{\varphi}-\frac{\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}{4M}\log{f}\,.

(47)

Within these new coordinates, the metric takes the more familiar form

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-f\mathrm{d}t^{2}-\frac{2\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{a}M}{r}\sin^{2}{\tilde{\theta}}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\tilde{\varphi}+\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}{f}+r^{2}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\Omega}^{2}\,,

(48)

which one recognizes as being the well-known Kerr black hole geometry linearized w.r.t its angular momentum. We see that at the linear level, the dipolar axial perturbation introduces a (small) angular momentum controlled by its amplitude
{\mathcal{E}}_{a}
. However, the dipolar polar perturbation can always be absorbed by the change of coordinates (
47
) and the additional constant
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
completely disappears, showing that it is a pure gauge, in agreement with the standard result
[
2
]
.

IV
Quadratic algebraically special perturbations

In this section, we generalize the results of Couch and Newman and we analyze the dynamics and present the analytic expression for the profiles of quadratic ASP of the Schwarzschild black hole.

IV.1
Dynamics

Following the same procedure as for the linear perturbations, we now expand the two functions
(P,L)
up to second order in perturbations

P(u,z,\bar{z})=P_{0}e^{\varepsilon F_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}F_{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad L(u,z,\bar{z})=\varepsilon L_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}L_{2}\,.

(49)

Furthermore, we introduce the function
f_{2}
such that

L_{2}=i\partial_{z}f_{2}+{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}\partial_{z}(f_{1}^{2})\,.

(50)

where we recall that
L_{1}
is related to
f_{1}
via
L_{1}=i\partial_{z}f_{1}
. Now, using the expressions for the linear perturbations
(F_{1},f_{1})
, we expand up to second order the dynamical equations (
18
) and (
19
) which encdes the dynamics of the second order perturbations
(F_{2},f_{2})
.

After a lengthy computation, the dynamics for the second order axial perturbation
f_{2}
splits into

\boxed{\Delta_{0}\Delta_{0}f_{2}+2\Delta_{0}f_{2}-12M\partial_{u}f_{2}=\mathcal{A}}\,,

(51)

where
{\mathcal{A}}
stands for the source term generated by the polar-axial mode interaction. Its expression reads

\mathcal{A}=-2F_{1}\Delta_{0}\Delta_{0}f_{1}-2\Delta_{0}F_{1}\Delta_{0}f_{1}-2\Delta_{0}(F_{1}\Delta_{0}f_{1})-8F_{1}\Delta_{0}f_{1}\,.

(52)

After expanding up to second order the equation (
19
), and having split its imaginary and real parts, the dynamical equation for the second order polar perturbation is given by

\boxed{\Delta_{0}\Delta_{0}F_{2}+2\Delta_{0}F_{2}+12M\partial_{u}F_{2}=\mathcal{P}+\mathcal{Q}+\mathcal{R}}\,,

(53)

where the source term is split into three different contributions given explicitly by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=-2\Delta_{0}(F_{1}\Delta_{0}F_{1})-2\Delta_{0}(F_{1}^{2})-2F_{1}\Delta_{0}\Delta_{0}F_{1}-4F_{1}\Delta_{0}F_{1}\,,

(54)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle={\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}^{2}\Big[\Delta_{0}(f_{1}^{2})-f_{1}\Delta_{0}\Delta_{0}f_{1}+4(\Delta_{0}f_{1})^{2}+6f_{1}\Delta_{0}f_{1}+\frac{3}{2}\Delta_{0}\Delta_{0}(f_{1}^{2})+2\Delta_{0}(f_{1}\Delta_{0}f_{1})\Big]\,,

(55)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=8{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}\imaginary[\partial_{\bar{z}}(P_{0}^{2}\partial_{\bar{z}}F_{1})\partial_{z}(P_{0}^{2}\partial_{z}f_{1})]-8({\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}})\imaginary[P_{0}^{2}\partial_{z}f_{1}(\partial_{\bar{z}}\Delta_{0}F_{1}+2\partial_{\bar{z}}F_{1})]\,.

(56)

It is direct to see that
\mathcal{P}
encodes the polar-polar interaction,
\mathcal{Q}
encodes the axial-axial interaction and finally
\mathcal{R}
encodes the polar-axial interaction generating the source term. Let us further point that the differential operator acting on the second order perturbations
(F_{2},f_{2})
is exactly the same as the one entering in the wave equation verified by the linear perturbations
(F_{1},f_{1})
. We now turn to the resolution of these equations.

IV.2
Exact solutions

Despite the rather complicated form of these equations, let us show how one can construct exact analytical solutions for the quadratic ASP. This is the first main result of this work.

IV.2.1
Axial perturbations

Consider first the quadratic axial ASP encoded in
f_{2}
. In order to solve Eqs. (
51
), one can follow a procedure close to the one used to solve the linear perturbation equations. Given that
f_{2}
is sourced only by axial-polar interactions, we can take the following ansatze:

f_{2}={E}_{\mathrm{p}}{E}_{\mathrm{a}}e^{({\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}})u}\sum_{\ell,m}a^{\ell}_{m}Y_{\ell}^{m}\,,

(57)

where the
a^{\ell}_{m}
are constant coefficients. These can be obtained from Eqs. (
51
) by making use of the decomposition of any product
Y_{\ell_{1}}Y_{\ell_{2}}
onto the basis of
Y_{\ell}
. This decomposition uses the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients
k_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}m}^{m_{1}m_{2}\ell}
, whose definition is given in App.
A.3
for convenience. Using this ansatze, the source term decomposes as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=-{E}_{\mathrm{p}}{E}_{\mathrm{a}}e^{({\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}})u}\Big[2Y_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}}^{{m}_{\mathrm{p}}}\Delta_{0}\Delta_{0}Y_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}}^{{m}_{\mathrm{a}}}+2\Delta_{0}Y_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}}^{{m}_{\mathrm{p}}}\Delta_{0}Y_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}}^{{m}_{\mathrm{a}}}+2\Delta_{0}(Y_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}}^{{m}_{\mathrm{p}}}\Delta_{0}Y_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}}^{{m}_{\mathrm{a}}})+8Y_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}}^{{m}_{\mathrm{p}}}\Delta_{0}Y_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}}^{{m}_{\mathrm{a}}}\Big]\,,

\displaystyle={E}_{\mathrm{p}}{E}_{\mathrm{a}}e^{({\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}})u}\sum_{\ell}\mathcal{A}_{m}^{\ell}Y_{\ell}^{m}\,,

(58)

where the coefficients
\mathcal{A}_{m}^{\ell}
are given explicitly by

\mathcal{A}_{m}^{\ell}=-8({\lambda}_{\mathrm{a}}+1)({\lambda}_{\mathrm{p}}+{\lambda}_{\mathrm{a}}+\lambda+1)k^{{m}_{\mathrm{a}}{m}_{\mathrm{p}}\ell}_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}m}\,,

(59)

In these expressions,
\lambda
is defined from
\ell
(and similarly,
{\lambda}_{\mathrm{a}}
and
{\lambda}_{\mathrm{p}}
from
{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}
and
{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}
respectively) using Eq. (
6
). Using these expressions and plugging them in the dynamical equation (
51
), one obtains the following constraint

\sum_{\ell,m}\Big[(\ell-1)\ell(\ell+1)(\ell+2)a^{\ell}_{m}-12M({\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}})a^{\ell}_{m}-\mathcal{A}^{\ell}_{m}\Big]Y_{\ell}^{m}=0\,.

(60)

which holds for all
\ell
.
Since the
Y_{\ell}^{m}
form a basis of the space of real functions on the 2-sphere, every coefficient must be put to zero, leading to

12M(\kappa-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}+{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}})a^{\ell}_{m}-\mathcal{A}^{\ell}_{m}=0\,.

(61)

If
{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}\neq{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}
, this equation can be solved by imposing

a^{\ell}_{m}=\frac{1}{12M}\frac{\mathcal{A}^{\ell}_{m}}{\kappa-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}+{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}}\,.

(62)

If one has
\kappa={\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}
then the only solution is
a^{\ell}_{m}=0
, except when
\ell=0
or
\ell=1
. The question oof the zero modes will be discussed at the end of the section.

IV.2.2
Polar perturbations

Let us now consider the quadratic polar perturbations encoded in
F_{2}
. In this case, the source term is much more involved. Nevertheless, a good ansatze to this equations
is given by

F_{2}={E}_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}e^{-2{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}u}\sum_{\ell,m}p^{\ell}_{m}Y_{\ell}^{m}+{E}_{\mathrm{a}}^{2}e^{2{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}u}\sum_{\ell,m}q^{\ell}_{m}Y_{\ell}^{m}+{E}_{\mathrm{p}}{E}_{\mathrm{a}}e^{({\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}})u}\sum_{\ell,m}r^{\ell}_{m}Y_{\ell}^{m}\,,

(63)

with
p^{\ell}_{m}
,
q^{\ell}_{m}
and
r^{\ell}_{m}
constant coefficients. Decomposing the source terms
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
, one obtains the following expression for
\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=-2{E}_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}e^{-2{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}u}\Big\{\Big[-{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}({\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}+1)+1\Big]\Delta_{0}(Y_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}}^{2})+\Big[{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}({\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}+1)^{2}-2{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}({\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}+1)\Big]Y_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}}^{2}\Big\}\,,

\displaystyle={E}_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}e^{-2{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}u}\sum_{\ell,m}\mathcal{P}^{\ell}_{m}Y_{\ell}^{m}\,.

(64)

while
\mathcal{Q}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle={\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}^{2}{E}_{\mathrm{a}}^{2}e^{2{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}u}\Big\{\frac{3}{2}\Delta_{0}\Delta_{0}(Y_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}}^{2})+\Big[-2{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}({\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}+1)+1\Big]\Delta_{0}(Y_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}}^{2})+\Big[3{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}^{2}({\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}+1)^{2}-6{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}({\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}+1)\Big]Y_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}}^{2}\Big\}\,,

\displaystyle={E}_{\mathrm{a}}^{2}e^{2{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}u}\sum_{\ell,m}\mathcal{Q}^{\ell}_{m}Y_{\ell}^{m}\,.

(65)

In both expressions, the coefficients
\mathcal{P}^{\ell}_{m}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\ell}_{m}
are defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\ell}_{m}

\displaystyle=-4\Big[1+\lambda+4{\lambda}_{\mathrm{p}}+2\lambda{\lambda}_{\mathrm{p}}+2{\lambda}_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\Big]k^{{m}_{\mathrm{p}}{m}_{\mathrm{p}}\ell}_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}m}\,,

(66)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{\ell}_{m}

\displaystyle=2{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}^{2}\Big[6+3\lambda(\lambda+3)+10{\lambda}_{\mathrm{a}}+4\lambda{\lambda}_{\mathrm{a}}+6{\lambda}_{\mathrm{a}}^{2}\Big]k^{{m}_{\mathrm{a}}{m}_{\mathrm{a}}\ell}_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}m}\,.

(67)

In order to decompose the source term
\mathcal{R}
, it is convenient to introduce the coefficients
h
and
l
defined in App.
A.3
. Using these definitions, one can write

\mathcal{R}={E}_{\mathrm{p}}{E}_{\mathrm{a}}e^{({\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}})u}\sum_{\ell,m}\mathcal{R}^{\ell}_{m}Y_{\ell}^{m}\,,

(68)

where the coefficients
\mathcal{R}^{\ell}_{m}
reads

\mathcal{R}^{\ell}_{m}=8{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}\imaginary(l_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}m}^{{m}_{\mathrm{a}}{m}_{\mathrm{p}}\ell}\big)+16{\lambda}_{\mathrm{p}}({\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}})\imaginary(h_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}m}^{{m}_{\mathrm{a}}{m}_{\mathrm{p}}\ell}\big)\,.

(69)

In the case
{m}_{\mathrm{a}}={m}_{\mathrm{p}}=0
, one has
\mathcal{R}=0
, because
h_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}m}^{00\ell}
and
l_{{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}m}^{00\ell}
are reals (see App.
A
). This means that for
{m}_{\mathrm{a}}={m}_{\mathrm{p}}=0
, quadratic polar perturbations cannot be sourced by axial-polar interaction; this is expected for parity conservation reasons.

Using the above decomposition of the source terms, the dynamical equation (
53
) for the quadratic polar perturbations splits into three conditions given by

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\Big[(\ell-1)\ell(\ell+1)(\ell+2)p^{\ell}_{m}-24M{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}p^{\ell}_{m}-\mathcal{P}^{\ell}_{m}\Big]Y_{\ell}=0\,,

(70)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\Big[(\ell-1)\ell(\ell+1)(\ell+2)q^{\ell}_{m}+24M{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}q^{\ell}_{m}-\mathcal{Q}^{\ell}_{m}\Big]Y_{\ell}=0\,,

(71)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\Big[(\ell-1)\ell(\ell+1)(\ell+2)r^{\ell}_{m}+12M({\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}})r^{\ell}_{m}-\mathcal{R}^{\ell}_{m}\Big]Y_{\ell}=0\,.

(72)

These equations are solved by taking

p^{\ell}_{m}=\frac{1}{12M}\frac{\mathcal{P}^{\ell}_{m}}{\kappa-2{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}}\,,\quad q^{\ell}_{m}=\frac{1}{12M}\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{\ell}_{m}}{\kappa+2{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}}\,,\quad r^{\ell}_{m}=\frac{1}{12M}\frac{\mathcal{R}^{\ell}_{m}}{\kappa+{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}}\,.

(73)

These expressions provide an analytic exact solutions for the quadratic ASP of the Schwarzschild black hole.

In order to demonstrate the usefulness of the exact analytical solutions presented here, we have computed the profiles of the quadratic perturbations
(F_{2},f_{2})
sourced by linear perturbations
(F_{1},f_{1})
with quantum numbers
{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}=2
,
{m}_{\mathrm{a}}=1
and
{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}=3
,
{m}_{\mathrm{p}}=2
. The detailed derivation is presented in appendix
C
. This solution describes the quadratic perturbation sourced by the interaction of a linear axial quadrupole with a linear polar hexapole which have a decay time given respectively by

{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}=\frac{2}{M}\,,\quad{\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{10}{M}

(74)

The analytic profile of the quadratic axial ASP sourced by the polar-axial interaction is given by

f_{2}={E}_{\mathrm{p}}{E}_{\mathrm{a}}\Big(-\sqrt{\frac{3}{7\pi}}Y_{1}^{1}-\frac{13}{18\sqrt{6\pi}}Y_{3}^{1}-\frac{13}{54}\sqrt{\frac{5}{2\pi}}Y_{3}^{3}+\frac{2}{39}\sqrt{\frac{55}{21\pi}}Y_{5}^{1}-\frac{2}{117}\sqrt{\frac{110}{\pi}}Y_{5}^{3}\Big)e^{-8u/M}\,.

(75)

while the quadratic polar ASP has additional contributions coming from the polar-polar, axial-axial and axial-polar interactions. It reads

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle={E}_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}Y_{0}^{0}+\frac{119}{66\sqrt{\pi}}Y_{4}^{0}-\frac{17}{66}\sqrt{\frac{35}{\pi}}Y_{4}^{4}-\frac{97}{264\sqrt{13\pi}}Y_{6}^{0}-\frac{97}{264}\sqrt{\frac{7}{13\pi}}Y_{6}^{4}\Big)e^{-20u/M}

(76)

\displaystyle+\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{a}}^{2}}{M^{2}}\Big(\frac{3}{\sqrt{\pi}}Y_{0}^{0}+\frac{16}{21}\sqrt{\frac{5}{\pi}}Y_{2}^{0}+\frac{16}{7}\sqrt{\frac{5}{3\pi}}Y_{2}^{2}-\frac{892}{357\sqrt{\pi}}Y_{4}^{0}+\frac{446}{357}\sqrt{\frac{5}{\pi}}Y_{4}^{2}\Big)e^{4u/M}

(77)

\displaystyle+\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{p}}{E}_{\mathrm{a}}}{M}\Big(\frac{25}{33}\sqrt{\frac{5}{2\pi}}Y_{4}^{-3}-\frac{25}{33}\sqrt{\frac{35}{2\pi}}Y_{4}^{-1}\Big)e^{-8u/M}\,.

(78)

Notice that the contribution coming from the axial-polar interaction is non-vanishing because we have chosen linear ASP with non-vanishing magnetic numbers.

IV.3
Quadratic zero modes

Now, we can use our exact solutions for both polar and axial quadratic ASP to analyze the quadratic zero modes. From (
57
) and (
63
), we see that in order to have time-independent quadratic perturbations, one has to fix

{\kappa}_{\mathrm{a}}={\kappa}_{\mathrm{p}}=0

(79)

which further select the time-independent linear perturbations.
They are of three types:

•

{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}={\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}=1

•

{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}=0
and
{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}=1

•

{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}=1
and
{\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}=0

For each of them, three subcases have to be distinguish depending on the values of the magnetic numbers
{m}_{\mathrm{a}}
and
{m}_{\mathrm{p}}
.

IV.3.1
Monopole-monopole zero mode

Consider the case where
{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}={\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}=0
. The associated metric is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-f\left(1-\frac{K_{+}}{f}\right)\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\frac{2K_{+}}{f}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}r-f^{-1}\left(1-\frac{K_{+}}{f}\right)\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}(1-K_{-})\mathrm{d}\Omega^{2}

(80)

where the constants
K_{\pm}
are given by

K_{\pm}=\frac{\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}{2\sqrt{3}}\pm 1\right)

(81)

To compare this to the Schwarzschild metric, we need to remove the off diagonal terms. This is achieved by performing the change

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{K_{-}}{2}\right)\tilde{t}+\frac{\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\tilde{r}+4M\log{(\tilde{r}-2M)}-\frac{4M^{2}}{\tilde{r}-2M}\right)+\frac{4\epsilon^{2}{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{p}M}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{2M(2\tilde{r}-3M)}{(\tilde{r}-2M)^{2}}-\log{(\tilde{r}-2M)}\right)

(82)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{K_{+}}{2}\right)\tilde{r}

(83)

which bring the metric to the standard Schwarzschild form

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2M(1+\epsilon M_{1}+\epsilon^{2}M_{2})}{r}\right)\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\left(1-\frac{2M(1+\epsilon M_{1}+\epsilon^{2}M_{2})}{r}\right)^{-1}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\mathrm{d}\Omega^{2}

(84)

The new constants
(M_{1},M_{2})
are given in term of the polar perturbation by

M_{1}=-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{3}}{\mathcal{E}}_{p}\quad\mbox{and}\quad M_{2}=\frac{3}{8}{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{p}\,.

(85)

As expected, it corresponds to a quadratic shift of the Schwarzschild mass.

IV.3.2
Dipole-dipole zero mode

Let us focus on the simplest one corresponding to

{\ell}_{\mathrm{a}}={\ell}_{\mathrm{p}}=1
and
{m}_{\mathrm{a}}={m}_{\mathrm{p}}=0
. This geometry does not depend on time nor on the angular coordinate
\varphi
since the magnetic numbers have been chosen to vanish. It is therefore axi-symmetric and the associated metric is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{K_{1}(r,\theta)}{r^{3}f}\right)\left(-f\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}{f}\right)+\frac{2K_{1}(r,\theta)}{r^{3}f}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}r+2K_{4}(r,\theta)\left(\frac{M}{r}\mathrm{d}t-\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{2f}\right)\mathrm{d}\varphi

(86)

\displaystyle\;\;\;+r^{2}\left(K_{2}(r,\theta)\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+K_{3}(r,\theta)\sin^{2}{\theta}\mathrm{d}\varphi^{2}\right)

(87)

where the four functions are given by

\displaystyle K_{1}(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\epsilon^{2}{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{a}M(1+\cos{2\theta})-\frac{2}{3}\epsilon^{2}{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{p}r^{3}\right)

(88)

\displaystyle K_{2}(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=1-\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{p}\cos{\theta}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{8}\left({\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{a}+\frac{7}{3}{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{p}r^{2}+({\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{a}+3{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{p}r^{2})\cos{2\theta}\right)

(89)

\displaystyle K_{3}(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=1-\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{p}\cos{\theta}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{8}\left(\frac{7}{3}{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{p}r^{3}+2{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{a}(M+r)+({\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{p}r^{3}-2{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{a}M)\cos{2\theta}\right)

(90)

\displaystyle K_{4}(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=-\left(1-\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{p}\cos{\theta}\right)\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{a}M\sin^{2}{\theta}

(91)

By virtue of the no-hair theorem, this metric is expected to be related to the slowly rotating Kerr metric up to quadratic order in the spin. Let us write down the explicit diffemorphism which allows to transform the above metric to the more familiar form. To proceed, we introduce the following change of coordinates

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{\epsilon^{2}{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{p}}{12}\right)\tilde{t}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{8}\left[\frac{2\tilde{r}}{f(\tilde{r})}\left(\frac{8}{3}{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{p}M^{2}-{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{a}\right)-\frac{{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{a}}{M}\log{f(\tilde{r})}-\frac{16}{3}{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{p}M\log{(\tilde{r}-2M)}-\frac{{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{p}\tilde{r}}{12}\right]\,,

(92)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{p}}{12}\right)\tilde{r}\,,

(93)

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle=\tilde{\theta}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{p}\sin{\tilde{\theta}}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{16}{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{p}\sin{2\tilde{\theta}}\,,

(94)

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle=\tilde{\varphi}-\frac{\epsilon{\mathcal{E}}_{a}}{4M}\log{f(\tilde{r})}\,.

(95)

Within these new coordinates, the off diagonal
g_{tr}
and
g_{r\varphi}
in (
86
) disappear and the metric takes the following form

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left[f(\tilde{r})+\epsilon^{2}\left(\frac{2M_{2}}{r}-\frac{2aM\cos^{2}{\tilde{\theta}}}{\tilde{r}^{3}}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}^{2}+\left[f^{-1}(\tilde{r})+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\tilde{r}f(\tilde{r})}\left(\frac{a^{2}-2\tilde{r}M_{2}}{f(\tilde{r})}+a^{2}\cos^{2}{\tilde{\theta}}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}\tilde{r}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{4\epsilon aM\sin^{2}{\tilde{\theta}}}{\tilde{r}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\varphi}+\left(\tilde{r}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}a^{2}\cos^{2}{\tilde{\theta}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tilde{\theta}^{2}+\left[1+\frac{\epsilon^{2}a^{2}(\tilde{r}+2M\sin^{2}{\tilde{\theta}})}{\tilde{r}^{3}}\right]\tilde{r}^{2}\sin^{2}{\tilde{\theta}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\varphi}^{2}\,,

(96)

where the constants
(a,{\mathcal{M}},M_{2})
are given in terms of the perturbations by

M_{2}=\frac{{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{2}M}{4}\quad\mbox{and}\quad a=\frac{{\mathcal{E}}_{a}}{2}\,.

(97)

and where
M
is the original Schwarzschild mass.
As expected, it corresponds to the slowly rotating Kerr metric up to quadratic order in the spin
a
. We see that the Schwarzschild mass is shifted by the polar perturbation, while the spin is generated by the axial perturbation.
This geometry admits a new Killing horizon located at

r_{+}=2M+\frac{\epsilon^{2}M}{3}\left({\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{p}-\frac{{\mathcal{E}}^{2}_{a}}{8M^{2}}\right)

(98)

Therefore, contrary to the linear regime where the horizon of the black hole perturbed by the dipolar zero mode is not shifted, in the non-linear regime, the Schwarzschild black hole geometry dressed with the quadratic zero modes admits a shifted horizon at
r_{+}\neq 2M
.

Discussion

In this work, we have revisited the description of the algebraically special perturbations (ASPs) of the Schwarzschild black hole and extended the analysis of Couch and Newman to the non-linear regime. The dynamic of the quadratic ASPs can be derived by expanding up to second order the reduced Einstein equations (
18
–
19
) of the most general vacuum twisting family of solutions of general relativity. Expanding these equations up to second order in the perturbations of the two fields
(P,L)
which label this family, we have obtained the master non-linear wave equations (
51
) and (
53
) which describe respectively the dynamic of the quadratic axial perturbations
f_{2}
and the dynamic of quadratic polar perturbations
F_{2}
. By construction, these solutions of these equations preserve the Petrov type II of the geometry.

A nice feature of these equations is that the sources can be written explicitly. From these expressions, we see that the quadratic axial perturbation
f_{2}
is sourced by the axial-polar interaction encoded in the source
{\mathcal{A}}
given by (
52
), while the quadratic polar perturbation
F_{2}
is sourced by three types of interactions: polar-polar (
54
), axial-axial (
55
) and axial-polar (
56
). This last contribution is only present when the involved linear perturbations exhibit non-zero magnetic numbers.

Then, we have shown that upon using the ansätze (
57
) and (
63
) together with a suitable decomposition of the sources in terms of spherical harmonics, one can construct analytic solutions to these inhomogeneous wave equations upon solving the condition (
61
) for the axial perturbations and the relations (
73
) for the polar perturbations. We have further presented an explicit example, given by (
74
) and (
75
), of an analytic solution corresponding to a quadratic ASP sourced by a linear axial quadrupole and a linear polar hexapole. This example demonstrates that i) the damping time of the quadratic ASPs is related as expected to the damping times of the linear perturbations and ii) that the known divergence at the horizon of the linear ASPs is not cured by their non-linear interactions.

Finally, we have treated the case of the quadratic zero modes, focusing on the
\ell=0
and
\ell=1
quadratic ASPs. We have shown that as expected, the quadratic zero mode sourced by the
\ell=0
axial and polar linear modes corresponds to a Schwarzschild black hole with quadratic corrections of its mass given by (
84
). These corrections are sourced solely by the polar mode through its amplitude
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
. Similarly, we have shown that the quadratic zero mode sourced by the
\ell=1
axial and polar zero linear modes (with
{m}_{\mathrm{a}}={m}_{\mathrm{p}}=0
) deform the Schwarzschild background to the Kerr geometry up to quadratic corrections in the spin and in the mass corresponding to the metric (
96
). As expected, the spin correction (
97
) is generated by the axial sector and related to the amplitude
{\mathcal{E}}_{a}
. While these results are expected, we provide here the explicit diffeomorphisms one has to implement to confirm these results. There are given by (
82
-
83
) and (
92
-
95
). To our knowledge, this has not been presented in that form elsewhere.

This study can serves as the basis to explore several open questions. First, it would be interesting to determine if, similarly to the linear case
[
50
]
, one can find well-behaved highly damped QQNMs of the Schwarzschild black hole which are spectrally close (i.e. numerically indistinguishable) from the quadratic ASPs described in this work. Second, one can use the expression for the quadratic ASP to search for possible hidden symmetry structures in the non-linear regime of black hole perturbations. Finally, we stress that while pathological, the ASPs provide one of the few examples corresponding to explicit solutions in the time-domain. Finding other exact solutions within a closed form in the time-domain but describing instead well-behaved perturbations remains an interesting goal, as it would surely allows one to better understand the different phases of the signal during the different stages of the relaxation process.

Appendix A
Spherical harmonics in stereographic coordinates

In this appendix, we present the relevant properties of the spherical harmonics which we use throughout this work.

A.1
Definition

Spherical harmonics are eigenfunctions of the Laplacian operator
\Delta_{0}
:

\Delta_{0}Y_{\ell}^{m}=-\ell(\ell+1)Y_{\ell}^{m}\,.

(99)

They are indexed by the two integers
\ell
and
m
, with
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\in\llbracket-\ell,\ell\rrbracket
. The most common way to construct them is the following:

\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{m}=(-1)^{m}\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}}P_{\ell}^{m}\Big(\frac{z\bar{z}-1}{z\bar{z}+1}\Big)\Big(\frac{z}{\bar{z}}\Big)^{m/2}\,.

(100)

Here,
P_{\ell}^{m}
represent an associated Legendre polynomial, and we have written the eigenfunctions
\underline{Y}
to emphasize the fact that they take their values in
\mathbb{C}
.

As defined in (
100
), spherical harmonics satisfy the following properties beyond the eigenvalue equation (
99
):

\displaystyle\Big(z\partialderivative{z}-\bar{z}\partialderivative{\bar{z}}\Big)\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{m}=m\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{m}\,,

(101)

\displaystyle\big(\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{m}\big)^{*}=(-1)^{m}\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{-m}\,,

(102)

\displaystyle(1+z\bar{z})\partial_{z}\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{m}=\frac{m}{z}\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{m}+\frac{\bar{z}}{z}\sqrt{(\ell-m)(\ell+m+1)}\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{m+1}\,,

(103)

\displaystyle(1+z\bar{z})\partial_{\bar{z}}\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{m}=-mz\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{m}+\sqrt{(\ell-m)(\ell+m+1)}\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{m+1}\,,

(104)

\displaystyle\int\frac{2\differential{z}\differential{\bar{z}}}{P_{0}^{2}}\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{m}\big(\underline{Y}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{m^{\prime}}\big)^{*}=\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\,.

(105)

A.2
Real spherical harmonics

In this work, we focus on
real
spherical harmonics. These are built from the complex harmonics
\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{m}
by

Y_{\ell}^{m}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big[\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{m}+(-1)^{m}\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{-m}\big]=\sqrt{2}\real(\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{m})&if $m>0$,\\
\underline{Y}_{\ell}^{0}&if $m=0$,\\

A.3
Decomposition coefficients

Several times throughout this paper, it is necessary to decompose a product of
Y_{\ell}^{m}
functions or their derivatives onto a basis of functions. Here, we define all the coefficients we use. They can be computed using the scalar product
\innerproduct{\cdot}{\cdot}
defined by

\innerproduct{f}{g}=\int\frac{2\differential{z}\differential{\bar{z}}}{P_{0}^{2}}fg\,.

(108)

This is possible because the real spherical harmonics form an orthonormal basis of functions on the 2-sphere.

•

The most important coefficients are the ones describing the decomposition of a product of two spherical harmonics onto the basis of spherical harmonics:

Y_{\ell_{1}}^{m_{1}}Y_{\ell_{2}}^{m_{2}}=\sum_{\ell,m}k^{m_{1}m_{2}\ell}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}m}Y_{\ell}^{m}\quad\mbox{with}\quad k^{m_{1}m_{2}\ell}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}m}=\innerproduct{Y_\ell^m}{Y_{\ell_1}^{m_1} Y_{\ell_2}^{m_2}}\,.

(109)

These coefficients are the well-known Clebsch-Gordan coefficients.

•

In order to compute the coefficients
\mathcal{R}_{m}^{\ell}
in (
68
), the metric expansions in App.
B.1
or the metric reconstruction in Sec.
B.3
, one needs to use the coefficients
h
defined by

P_{0}^{2}\partial_{z}Y_{\ell_{1}}^{m_{1}}\partial_{\bar{z}}Y_{\ell_{2}}^{m_{2}}=\sum_{\ell,m}h_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}m}^{m_{1}m_{2}\ell}Y_{\ell}^{m}\quad\mbox{or}\quad h_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}m}^{m_{1}m_{2}\ell}=\innerproduct{Y_\ell^m}{P_0^2 \partial_z Y_{\ell_1}^{m_1} \partial_{\bar{z}} Y_{\ell_2}^{m_2}}\,.

(110)

These coefficients are complex when
(\ell_{1},m_{1})\neq(\ell_{2},m_{2})
: in general, one has

\big(h_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}m}^{m_{1}m_{2}\ell}\big)^{*}=h_{\ell_{2}\ell_{1}m}^{m_{2}m_{1}\ell}\,.

(111)

One should note that in the case
m_{1}=m_{2}=0
, these coefficients are real.

•

The computation of the coefficients
\mathcal{R}^{\ell}_{m}
also requires the coefficients
l
such that

\partial_{z}(P_{0}^{2}\partial_{z}Y_{\ell_{1}}^{m_{1}})\partial_{\bar{z}}(P_{0}^{2}\partial_{\bar{z}}Y_{\ell_{2}}^{m_{2}})=\sum_{\ell,m}l_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}m}^{m_{1}m_{2}\ell}Y_{\ell}^{m}\,.

(112)

These coefficients can be computed using

l_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}m}^{m_{1}m_{2}\ell}=\innerproduct{Y_\ell^m}{\partial_{\bar{z}}(P_0^2 \partial_{\bar{z}} Y_{\ell_1}^{m_1}) \partial_z(P_0^2 \partial_z Y_{\ell_2}^{m_2})}\,.

(113)

One should note that in the case
m_{1}=m_{2}=0
, these coefficients are real.

•

Finally, the metric reconstruction also requires defining the coefficients
j
such that

\big(\partial_{z}Y_{\ell_{1}}^{m_{1}}\big)^{2}=\sum_{\ell,m}j_{\ell_{1}m}^{m_{1}\ell}(\partial_{z}^{2}+2\partial_{z}(\log P_{0})\partial_{z})Y_{\ell}^{m}\,,

(114)

and the coefficients
g
defined by

Y_{\ell_{1}}^{m_{1}}\partial_{z}Y_{\ell_{2}}^{m_{2}}=\sum_{\ell,m}g_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}m}^{m_{1}m_{2}\ell}\partial_{z}Y_{\ell}^{m}\,.

(115)

With these definitions, one can decompose the various appearance of product of spherical harmonics and their derivatives in a straigtforward way.

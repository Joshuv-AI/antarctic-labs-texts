Chow rings of matroids as permutation representations
Robert Angarone, Anastasia Nathanson, Victor Reiner

Abstract
. Given a matroid with a symmetry group, we study the induced group action on the Chow ring of the matroid with respect to symmetric building sets. This turns out to always be a permutation action. Work of Adiprasito, Huh and Katz showed that the Chow ring satisfies Poincaré duality and the Hard Lefschetz theorem. We lift these to statements about this permutation action, and suggest further conjectures in this vein.

Introduction

A

is a combinatorial abstraction of lists of vectors

in a vector space, recording only the information about which subsets of the vectors
are linearly independent or dependent, forgetting their coordinates– see

In groundbreaking work, Adiprasito, Huh and Katz
[

]
affirmed long-standing conjectures of Rota–Heron–Welsh and Mason about vectors and matroids via a new methodology. Their work employed
a certain graded

called the

for a matroid

of rank

, introduced by Feichtner and Yuzvinsky
[

]
as a generalization of the Chow ring of DeConcini and Procesi’s

for hyperplane arrangement complements
[

A remarkable integral Gröbner basis result proven by Feichtner and Yuzvinsky

A key step in

]
shows not only that

\displaystyle a_{k}=a_{r-k}\text{ for }k\leq r/2

enjoys a trio of properties referred to as the

2.1.
Lattices and Building Sets

Although our results concern matroids, their lattices
of flats, building sets, and Chow rings, Feichtner and Yuzvinsky
[
FY04
]
actually worked at a higher level of generality: building sets in
atomic lattices
and their
Chow rings. We start with this more general framework,
in order to present their integral Gröbner basis result.

Throughout,
\mathcal{L}
will be a finite lattice, that is a finite poset in which any pair of elements

has
a least upper bound and greatest lower bound, called their join

\vee A

\hat{1}=\vee\mathcal{L}

and bottom element
\hat{0}=\wedge\mathcal{L}
.
For elements

Definition 2.1
.

[

In a finite lattice
\mathcal{L}

in

\max(\mathcal{G}_{\leq F})=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{\ell}\}
denote the maximal elements of
\mathcal{G}_{\leq F}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}F^{\prime}\leq F\}
, one has a poset isomorphism

[\hat{0},F]\cong[\hat{0},F_{1}]\times\cdots\times[\hat{0},F_{\ell}].

On the right side, the Cartesian product of posets
inherits a componentwise ordering from its factors.

Example 2.2

The

Note that the

2.3.
Atomic and geometric lattices and matroids

So far these notions have played no role. However,
atomicity is important in Feichtner and Yuzvinsky’s Gröbner basis presentation for their Chow ring
A(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{G})
, while the geometric lattice/matroid assumption implies a crucial numerical identity, Lemma
2.13
below.

Definition 2.7
.

Recall the
atoms
of a finite lattice
\mathcal{L}
are
the elements that cover
\hat{0}
. One calls
\mathcal{L}

atomic
if every
F
in
L
is the join of the set of atoms lying weakly below
F
in
\mathcal{L}
.

When
\mathcal{L}
is a finite
atomic
lattice, the following quantity
is well-defined
for any
F,F^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{L}
with
F\leq F^{\prime}
:

(14)

d(F,F^{\prime})=\min\{d\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}F^{\prime}=F\vee a_{1}\vee\cdots\vee a_{d}\text{ for some atoms }a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\in\mathcal{L}\}.

Well-definedness follows from the fact that
it is a minimum over a (finite) set of positive integers which is nonempty, as
F^{\prime}=F\vee a_{1}\vee\cdots\vee a_{d}
where
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\}

is the set of
all
atoms of
L

with
a_{i}\leq F^{\prime}
.

This function
d(F,F^{\prime})
behaves more simply under extra
hypotheses on
\mathcal{L}
.

Definition 2.8
.

A finite lattice
\mathcal{L}
is
semimodular
if
F,F^{\prime}
covering
F\wedge F^{\prime}
implies

F\vee F^{\prime}
covers
F,F^{\prime}
.

It is well-known
[
Sta12
, Prop. 3.3.2]
that, for a finite lattice
\mathcal{L}
, this condition is equivalent to being
ranked
and having the rank function
\rk\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}L\rightarrow\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
obey the
semimodular inequality

(15)

\rk(F\vee F^{\prime})+\rk(F\wedge F^{\prime})\leq\rk(F)+\rk(F^{\prime}).

Definition 2.9
.

An atomic semimodular lattice
\mathcal{L}
is called a
geometric lattice
.

For geometric lattices
\mathcal{L}
, one can check that
the semimodular inequality (
15
) implies this rewriting of (
14
):

(16)

d(F,F^{\prime})=\rk(F^{\prime})-\rk(F).

Finite geometric lattices are known
[
Sta12
, §3.3]
to be the same as lattices of flats of finite
matroids
, briefly reviewed here.
On matroids, some excellent references include Oxley
[
Oxl92
]
and Ardila
[
Ard23
]
.

Definition 2.10
.

A matroid
\mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}})
consists of a (finite) ground set
E
and a collection of subsets
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}=\{F\}\subseteq 2^{E}
called
flats
,
satisfying these axioms:

(F1)

E\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
.

(F2)

If
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
, then
F\cap F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
.

(F3)

For any
F\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
, and any
i\in E\setminus F
, there is a unique
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
containing
i
which
covers
F
in this sense:

F\subsetneq F^{\prime}
, and no other flat
F^{\prime\prime}
has
F\subsetneq F^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq F^{\prime}
.

These axioms combinatorially abstract properties of a
realizable matroid

\mathcal{M}
coming from a list of vectors
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}

in a vector space over some field
\mathbb{F}
.
A realizable matroid
\mathcal{M}
has ground set
E=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, and
F\subseteq E
is a flat in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
when
the inclusion
F\subseteq\{i\in E\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}v_{i}\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{v_{j}\}_{j\in F}\}
is an equality.
Axioms (F1),(F2) imply that the inclusion poset
on
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
forms a lattice, with
meets

F\wedge F^{\prime}=F\cap F^{\prime}
and
joins

F\vee F^{\prime}=\bigcap_{F^{\prime\prime}\supseteq F,F^{\prime}}F^{\prime\prime}
.
Axiom (F3) further implies that the lattice
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
will be
geometric
.
The
rank
of the matroid
\mathcal{M}
itself is defined to be
\rk(E)
, which we assume throughout has
\rk(E)=r+1
.

Remark 2.11
.

In this paper, we will only consider
simple matroids
, that is,

•

\mathcal{M}
is
loopless
, so that
\hat{0}=\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}}F=\varnothing
is a flat, and

•

\mathcal{M}
has no
parallel elements
, so that the atoms of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
are all singleton flats
F=\{a\}
.

This means that
\mathcal{M}
is completely determined by
the geometric lattice
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
. This does not
impose a serious restriction, since the Chow ring
A(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{G})
defined in Section
2.5
below only uses information about the lattice strucutre of

\mathcal{L}
and the choice of
building set
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{L}\setminus\{\hat{0}\}
.

The matroid automorphisms
\Aut(\mathcal{M})
of
\mathcal{M}
are the permutations
g
of the ground set
E
preserving
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
setwise, that is, sending flats
F
to flats
g(F)
. These also give poset and
lattice automorphisms of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
. For a subgroup
G\subseteq\Aut(\mathcal{M})
, a building set
\mathcal{G}
is called
G
-stable
if each
g\in G
preserves
\mathcal{G}
setwise.

Example 2.12
.

For any
G\subseteq\Aut(\mathcal{M})
, the maximal and minimal building sets

\mathcal{G}_{\max},\mathcal{G}_{\min}
are both
G
-stable.
On the other hand, Theorem
1.1
imposes two extra technical conditions, the first being that the building set contains the ground set
E=\hat{1}
.
This always holds for
\mathcal{G}_{\max}
. It holds for
\mathcal{G}_{\min}
if and only if
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}
for a
connected
(simple) matroid
\mathcal{M}
, that is, one cannot express
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{2}
as a nontrivial direct sum.

The second technical condition is the
stabilizer condition (
10
), saying that a nested set which is setwise stabilized by some

g
in
G
must also be pointwise stabilized. Again this always holds for
\mathcal{G}_{\max}
. However for
\mathcal{G}_{\min}
, this condition can easily fail. One such example of this type is the Chow ring for the
moduli space
\overline{M}_{0,n+1}
of genus
0
curves with
n+1
marked points
; see, e.g., Dotsenko
[
Dot22
]
, Gibney and Maclagan
[
GM10
]
, Keel
[
Kee92
]
, Maestroni and McCullough
[
MM23
, Rmk. 2.4]
. This Chow ring turns out to be

A(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{G}_{\min})
where
\mathcal{M}
is the graphic matroid associated to a complete graph on
n
vertices and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}=\Pi_{n}
is the set partition lattice from Example
2.5
. In Remark
3.2
below, we explain how both the stabilizer condition (
10
) and the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
fail for this Chow ring when

.

2.4.
A surprisingly important identity

A certain quantity from
Feichtner and Yuzvinsky
[
FY04
, §3]
,
controls their
Gröbner bases: for
an atomic lattice
\mathcal{L}
, building set
\mathcal{G}
, and
\mathcal{G}
-nested set
N
with
F\in N
, define

(17)

m_{N}(F)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=d(\vee N_{<F},F)

where
N_{<F}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{F^{\prime}\in N\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}F^{\prime}<F\}
.
For geometric lattices, (
16
) lets one rewrite (
17
) as in the Introduction:

(18)

m_{N}(F)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\rk(F)-\rk\left(\vee N_{<F}\right).

The next identity involving
m_{N}(F)
for geometric lattices plays a surprisingly crucial role here.
It appears in the proof of Corollary
2.24
bounding the degrees occurring in a Chow ring.
It similarly appears in the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
showing certain subsets of FY-monomials, forming products of chains under divisibility, live in intervals of degrees
symmetrically placed
with respect to the grading on
the Chow ring
A(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{G})
.

Lemma 2.13
.

For any geometric lattice
\mathcal{L}
, any building set
\mathcal{G}
,
and any
\mathcal{G}
-nested set
N
, its join

\vee N
has its rank expressible as

\rk(\vee N)=\sum_{F\in N}m_{N}(F).

Proof.

Proceed by induction on
|N|
. The base case
N=\varnothing
has
\rk(\vee N)=\rk(\hat{0})=0
equal to the empty sum.
In the inductive step, let the set of inclusion-maximal elements

\max(N)=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{\ell}\}

have size
\ell
, and proceed by cases
\ell=1

versus
\ell\geq 2
.

Case 1.

\ell=1
.

Here
F_{1}=\vee N
.
Letting
\hat{N}
denote the
\mathcal{G}
-nested set
N\setminus\{F_{1}\}
, one has this calculation:

\sum_{F\in N}m_{N}(F)=m_{N}(F_{1})+\sum_{F\in\hat{N}}m_{N}(F)=m_{N}(F_{1})+\sum_{F\in\hat{N}}m_{\hat{N}}(F)\overset{(*)}{=}m_{N}(F_{1})+\rk(\vee\hat{N})=\rk(F_{1})=\rk(\vee N)

where equality (*) applied the inductive hypothesis to the nested set
\hat{N}
.

Case 2.

\ell\geq 2
.

From
[
FK04
, Prop 2.8]
, one has that

\max\mathcal{G}_{\leq\vee N}=\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{\ell}\},

and therefore

[\hat{0},\vee N]\cong\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}[\hat{0},F_{i}]
.
This gives the first equality in the following
calculation, whose remaining equalities are justified below:

\displaystyle\rk(\vee N)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\rk(F_{i})\overset{(**)}{=}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\sum_{F\in N_{\leq F_{i}}}m_{N_{\leq F_{i}}}(F)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\sum_{F\in N_{\leq F_{i}}}m_{N}(F)\overset{(***)}{=}\sum_{F\in N}m_{N}(F)

Equality (**) used the assumption
\ell\geq 2
, to apply the inductive hypothesis to each
\mathcal{G}
-nested set
N_{\leq F_{i}}
. Equality (***) used the fact that one has a disjoint decomposition

N=\sqcup_{i=1}^{\ell}N_{\leq F_{i}}

by Lemma
2.6
.
∎

2.5.
Chow Rings

The following definition was introduced by
Feichtner and Yuzvinsky
[
FY04
]
.

Definition 2.14
.

For a finite lattice
\mathcal{L}
and building set
\mathcal{G}
,
the
Chow ring

A(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{G})
is the quotient
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra

A(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{G})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=S/(I+J)

where
S=\mathbb{Z}[x_{F}]_{F\in\mathcal{G}}
is a polynomial algebra, and
I,J
are the following ideals of
S
:

•

I
is the
Stanley-Reisner ideal
for the simplicial complex
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{G})
, meaning it is
generated by products
x_{F_{1}}x_{F_{2}}\cdots x_{F_{t}}
for subsets
\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{t}\}\subset\mathcal{G}
which are
not

\mathcal{G}
-nested.

•

J
is generated by the linear elements

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in F\in\mathcal{G}}x_{F}
for each atom
a

in
\mathcal{L}
.

We wish to consider subgroups
G
of the group
\Aut(\mathcal{L})
of poset automorphisms of
\mathcal{L}
, and building sets
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{L}\setminus\{\hat{0}\}
setwise stabilized by
G
, meaning
g(F)\in\mathcal{G}
for all
F\in\mathcal{G}
and
g
in
G
. We claim that in this setting, any
g
in
G
also acts by a
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra automorphism on
A(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{G})
via
g(x_{F})=x_{g(F)}
; the key points are that poset automorphisms
g
of
\mathcal{L}

•

preserve order and carry atoms to atoms, thus sending generators of
J
to other generators of
J
, and

•

because they
are also lattice automorphisms, they carry
\mathcal{G}
-nested sets to
\mathcal{G}
-nested sets, thus sending generators of
I
to other generators of
I
.

We will also wish to consider
A(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{G})
as a
graded

\mathbb{Z}
-algebra, in which it inherits the standard grading on

S=\mathbb{Z}[x_{F}]_{F\in\mathcal{G}}
for which each variable
x_{F}
has

\deg(x_{F})=1
. This happens because the ideals
I,J\subset S
are both graded ideals, generated by homogeneous elements of
S
. Also, subgroups
G\subset\Aut(\mathcal{L})
act as above via graded
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra automorphisms, that is,

A(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{G})=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}A^{k}

and each
A^{k}
becomes a
\mathbb{Z}G
-module.

It is not yet clear that
A(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{G})
has only
finitely many non-vanishing components
A^{k}
.
For this, we next
consider Feichtner and Yuzvinsky’s remarkable Gröbner basis presentation
mentioned in the Introduction.

2.6.
In praise of the Feichtner–Yuzvinsky Gröbner basis

We recall here a version of Gröbner basis theory with respect to monomial orders on polynomial rings

\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{x}]\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

over
\mathbb{Z}
, used in
[
FY04
]
.

Definition 2.15
.

A linear order
\prec
on the set of all monomials
\{\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\}
in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{x}]
is called a
monomial ordering

if it is a
well-ordering
(every subset of monomials has a
\prec
-minimum element) and
\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}\prec\mathbf{x}^{\beta}
implies

\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{\gamma}\prec\mathbf{x}^{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{\gamma}

for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
in
\{0,1,2,\ldots\}^{n}
.

Example 2.16
.

After fixing a linear order on the variables
x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}
,
the associated
lexicographic order

\prec
has

\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}\prec\mathbf{x}^{\beta}
if there exists some
k=1,2,\ldots,n
with
\alpha_{1}=\beta_{1},\alpha_{2}=\beta_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{k-1}=\beta_{k-1}
,
but
\alpha_{k}<\beta_{k}
.

Definition 2.17
.

For
f=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}
in
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
,
let
\mathrm{in}_{\prec}(f)=\mathbf{x}^{\beta}
denote the
\prec
-largest monomial in
f
having
c_{\beta}\neq 0
. Say
f
is
\prec
-monic
if the initial monomial
\mathrm{in}_{\prec}(f)=\mathbf{x}^{\beta}
has its coefficient
c_{\beta}=\pm 1
.

Having fixed a monomial order
\prec
, for any ideal
I\subset\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{x}]
, one says that a subset
\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{t}\}\subset I
is a
monic Gröbner basis
for
I
(with respect to
\prec
) if each
g_{i}
is
\prec
-monic, and
every
f
in
I
has
\mathrm{in}_{\prec}(f)
divisible by at least one
of the intial monomials

\{\mathrm{in}_{\prec}(g_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{in}_{\prec}(g_{t})\}
.
One calls
\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}
a
standard monomial
for

\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{t}\}
with respect to
\prec
if

\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}
is divisible by none of
\{\mathrm{in}_{\prec}(g_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{in}_{\prec}(g_{t})\}
.

It should be noted that some ideals
I\subset\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{x}]

have
no
monic Gröbner basis, e.g.
I=(2)\subset\mathbb{Z}[x]
. However, whenever
I

does
have a monic Gröbner basis, it has the following strong consequence, proven exactly as for Gröbner bases over

\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{x}]
with field cofficients
\mathbb{F}
; see, e.g.,
Cox, Little, O’Shea
[
CLO07
, §2.5, 5.3]
.

Proposition 2.18
.

Given a monic Gröbner basis
\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{t}\}
for
I\subset S=\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{x}]
with respect to
\prec
, then

•

I=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{t})
, that is,
\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{t}\}
generate
I
as an ideal.

•

The quotient ring
S/I
is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module, with
a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis given by the standard monomials
\{x^{\alpha}\}
.

The following crucial result appears as
[
FY04
, Thm. 2]
. To state it, define an
FY-monomial order
on
S=\mathbb{Z}[x_{F}]_{F\in\mathcal{G}}
to be any monomial order
based on a linear order of the variables with
x_{F}>x_{F^{\prime}}
if
F<F^{\prime}
.

Theorem 2.19
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a finite atomic lattice, and
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{L}\setminus\{\hat{0}\}
a building set.
Then for any choice of an
\mathrm{FY}
-monomial order on the ring
S=\mathbb{Z}[x_{F}]_{F\in\mathcal{G}}
,
the ideal
I+J
presenting
A(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{G})=S/(I+J)

has a monic Gröbner basis consisting of the following
elements, along with their
\prec
-initial terms:

•

all
x_{F_{1}}x_{F_{2}}\cdots x_{F_{t}}
for sets
\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{t}\}\subset\mathcal{G}
which are not
\mathcal{G}
-nested, each its own
\prec
-initial term,

•

for each
\mathcal{G}
-nested set
N
with maximal elements

\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{\ell}\}
, and for each
F
in
\mathcal{G}
with
F>\vee N
,

\displaystyle\text{ the product }\quad x_{F_{1}}\cdots x_{F_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{F^{\prime}\geq F}x_{F^{\prime}}\right)^{d(\vee N,F)}

\displaystyle\text{ whose $\prec$-initial term is }\quad x_{F_{1}}\cdots x_{F_{\ell}}\cdot

\displaystyle x_{F}^{d(\vee N,F)}.

This has the following very strong consequence,
using the notation
m_{N}(F)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=d(\vee N_{<F},F)
defined in (
17
), where

d(F,F^{\prime})=\min\{d\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}F^{\prime}=F\vee a_{1}\vee\cdots\vee a_{d}\text{ for atoms }a_{i}\in\mathcal{L}\}
was
defined in (
14
).

Corollary 2.20
.

(
[
FY04
, Cor. 1]
)

Under the hypotheses of Theorem

,
the Chow ring
A(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{G})
is free as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module, with
the
\prec
-standard monomial
\mathbb{Z}
-basis given by the set of
\mathrm{FY}
-monomials

(19)

\mathrm{FY}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\left\{x_{F_{1}}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{F_{\ell}}^{m_{\ell}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}N=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{\ell}\}\text{ is }\mathcal{G}\text{-nested, and }0\leq m_{i}<m_{N}(F_{i})\right\}.

This has a particularly interesting further consequence for symmetries of
\mathcal{L}
,
inspired by work of H.-C. Liao on the Chow rings of Boolean lattices
\mathcal{L}

with their maximal building set
\mathcal{G}_{\max}
; see
[
Lia24a
, Thm. 3.6]
.

Corollary 2.21
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
2.19
,
if one has a subgroup
G\subseteq\Aut(\mathcal{L})
setwise stabilizing the

\mathcal{G}
, then

permutes the set
\mathrm{FY}
,
as well as its subset of degree

\mathrm{FY}^{k}\subset\mathrm{FY}
.

Consequently, the
\mathbb{Z}G

and each of its homogeneous components

\mathrm{FY}
and each

Proof.

It was noted that
g
in

\mathcal{G}

-nested sets

.
Thus

5.2.
Equivariant
\gamma
-positivity?

Hilbert functions
(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
for

A(\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{G}_{\max})
of rank
r+1
simple matroids are

\gamma
-positivity

nonnegativity
for all coefficients

\gamma=(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor})
appearing in the unique expansion

(31)

\sum_{i=0}^{r}a_{i}t^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor}\gamma_{i}\,\,t^{i}(1+t)^{r-2i}.

See Athanasiadis
[
Ath18
]
for a nice survey on
\gamma
-positivity.
It has been shown, independently
by Ferroni, Matherne, Stevens and Vecchi
[
Fer+24
, Thm. 3.25]

and by Wang (see
[
Fer+24
, p. 29]

\gamma
-positivity for Hilbert series of Chow rings of matroids
follows from results of Braden, Huh, Matherne, Proudfoot and Wang
[
Bra+22
]
on
semismall decompositions
.
The maximal building set is again important: the
same example of

-positivity

\gamma
-representations

Signed Real Hurwitz numbers
Thomas Guidoni

Abstract
In this paper, we enumerate with signs the holomorphic maps between Real Riemann surfaces. We relate the signs and the numbers obtained to the Real Gromov-Witten theory of the target. For a finite group G G with a non-trivial morphism to { ± 1 } \{\pm 1\} , we present a signed count of principal G G -bundles yielding a Klein Topological Quantum Field Theory.



1
Introduction

Classical Hurwitz theory is the enumerative study of ramified covers of a given Riemann surface with prescribed ramification profile. It is closely related to the representation theory of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
: degree
d
Hurwitz numbers admit closed formulas in terms of the irreducible characters of
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
. This connection extends to any finite group
G
if one counts principal
G
-bundles instead of covers. It yields a Topological Quantum Field Theory, or equivalently the structure of a Frobenius algebra on the center
Z\mathbb{C}G
of the group algebra of
G
.

From the perspective of Gromov-Witten theory, the Hurwitz numbers are the relative Gromov-Witten invariants of the target. In
[
3
]
, Georgieva and Ionel express the relative Real Gromov-Witten invariants of Real Riemann surfaces in terms of the irreducible characters of the symmetric groups. These numbers arise as
signed
counts of ramified Real covers of a given Real Riemann surface with prescribed ramification profile. Real means that both the source and the target carry an anti-holomorphic involution with which the covering map commutes. The signs come from orientation of the moduli spaces of stable Real maps and there is no explicit desciption of the sign of a given Real cover.

In this paper, we first define explicitly a sign of a Real ramified cover in terms of topological data of the target. In Theorem
3
, we prove that it coincides with the sign defined abstractly in
[
3
]
. Thus, it can be understood as the
1
-dimensional counterpart of Welschinger’s signs
[
12
]
.

The sign is defined using the monodromy representation and the sign of a permutation as follows. Let
(X,\sigma)
be a connected Real Riemann surface such that
\sigma
has no fixed point and
f:(X^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})\rightarrow(X,\sigma)
be a Real holomorphic map. A topological cover is obtained by taking the quotients by the involutions and removing the branch locus
B
. This yields a monodromy representation
\rho_{f}
from the fundamental group of
(X\setminus B)/\sigma
to a symmetric group. The sign of
f
is the sign of the image by
\rho_{f}
of a representative of the Poincaré dual of the first Stiefel-Whitney class of
(X\setminus B)/\sigma
. The
signed Real Hurwitz numbers
are defined as the signed automorphism-weighted count of Real ramified covers of
(X,\sigma)
with prescribed ramification profile.

More generally, let
G
be a finite group and

\varepsilon:G\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}

(1.1)

be a non-trivial group morphism. We define similarly a sign of a principal
G
-bundle over
(X\setminus B)/\sigma
using the monodromy representation morphism of principal
G
-bundles and the morphism
\varepsilon
. The
(G,\varepsilon)
-Real Hurwitz number is obtained as the signed automorphism-weighted count of principal
G
-bundles with prescribed restriction around the punctures
B
. For the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
with the only non-trivial morphism
\varepsilon
, the
(\mathfrak{S}_{d},\varepsilon)
-Real Hurwitz numbers and the signed Real Hurwitz numbers coincide.

When the irreducible characters of
G
are real-valued, the
(G,\varepsilon)
-Real Hurwitz numbers do not depend on the representative of the first Stiefel-Whitney class chosen. We derive a formula in terms of the irreducible characters of
G
. This expression involves the
signed Frobenius-Schur indicator

SFS_{G,\varepsilon}
studied in Section
3
and reads

\mathbb{R}H_{g,G,\varepsilon}^{\bullet}(\bm{c})=\sum_{\rho^{T}=\rho}\left(SFS_{G,\varepsilon}(\rho)\frac{dim(\rho)}{\#G}\right)^{1-g}\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{\bm{c}_{i}}(\rho)

(1.2)

where
g
is the genus of
X
,
\rho^{T}
is the representation obtained from
\rho
by tensoring with
\varepsilon
,
\bm{c}
is a sequence of conjugacy classes of
G
and
f_{c}(\rho)
is defined in (
2.5
). For the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
with the sign morphism, the irreducible representations are labelled by the set of integer partitions
\mu
of
d
, and we prove that the signed Frobenius-Schur indicator is given by

SFS_{\mathfrak{S}_{d},\varepsilon}(\rho_{\mu})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-1)^{\frac{d-r(\mu)}{2}}&\text{ if }\rho_{\mu}^{T}=\rho_{\mu},\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

where
r(\mu)
is the length of diagonal of the Young diagram of
\mu
, see Subsection
2.2
. Thus, the signed Real Hurwitz numbers are equal to the relative Real Gromov-Witten invariants studied in
[
3
]
.

These relative Gromov-Witten invariants arise as integrals over a moduli space of pseudo-holomorphic maps whose domain has no marked point. Allowing marked points with
stationnary insertions
corresponds on the Hurwitz side to consider the
Hurwitz numbers with completed cycles

[
9
]
. In Subsection
4.7
, we introduce the
signed Real Hurwitz numbers with completed cycles
. It will be proved in a subsequent publication that they are the relative Real Gromov-Witten invariants with stationnary insertions.

The
(G,\varepsilon)
-signed Real Hurwitz numbers satisfy degeneration formulas that arise as one degenerates simulaneously pairs of conjugated circles in the target, see Proposition
31
. They are equivalent to the fact that
(G,\varepsilon)
-Real Hurwitz numbers can be assembled to form a
Klein Topological Quantum Field Theory
. We describe this from the equivalent perspective of the structure of
extended Frobenius algebras

[
11
]
that they define on
Z\mathbb{C}G
, which extends the standard Frobenius algebra structure on
Z\mathbb{C}G
. Thus, we obtain a map

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{Non-trivial}\\
\varepsilon:G\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}\end{array}\right\}\rightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{ Extensions of the}\\
\text{standard Frobenius algebra}\\
\text{structure on }Z\mathbb{C}G\end{array}\right\}.

(1.4)

for groups
G
whose characters are real-valued. These extensions differ from the one obtained in
[
8
]
. The latter would correspond in our setting to the trivial group morphism
\varepsilon
.

The degeneration of a genus
1
Riemann surface into a pair of genus
0
Riemann surfaces exchanged by the involution leads to a surprising combinatorial formula :

\#\{\rho\ |\ \rho^{T}=\rho\}=\sum_{c}\varepsilon(c)

(1.5)

where the sum is over the conjugacy classes of
G
. For the symmetric groups, the lef-hand side is the number of symmetric Young diagrams of a given size.

In Section
6
, we consider targets
(X,\sigma)
for which the fixed locus of
\sigma
is not empty, and Real holomorphic maps without Real branch points. We define signs and the corresponding signed Real Hurwitz numbers, and prove that the signed Real Hurwitz numbers obtained do not depend on the involution
\sigma
of the target.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains preliminary results. In Section
3
, we study the signed Frobenius-Schur indicator which is needed to express the
(G,\varepsilon)
-Real Hurwitz numbers and signed Real Hurwitz numbers. The signs and the corresponding Real Hurwitz numbers are introduced in Section
4
for targets without fixed locus and we the formulas in terms of the irreducible characters are obtained. In Subsection
4.3
, we give an expression of the
doublet contributions
to the signed Real Hurwitz numbers in terms of Hurwitz numbers. Section
5
studies the degeneration formulas and its consequences. In Subsection
5.4
, we study the degeneration process at the more accurate level of the signs. It yields Theorem
3
that relates the signs of Section
4
to those defined abstractly in
[
3
]
. The case of targets with fixed points is discussed in Section
6
.

Preliminaries

2.1
Representation theory of finite groups

Let
G
be a finite group. We denote by
C(G)
the set of conjugacy classes of
G
and by
Irr(G)
the set of (isomorphism classes of) irreducible representations of
G
on a finite-dimensional complex vector space. They are finite sets of the same cardinality. The vector space

Z\mathbb{C}G=\bigoplus_{c\in C(G)}\mathbb{C}\cdot c

(2.1)

admits an algebra structure coming from the product in
G
, considering
c
as the sum of its elements. It is commutative, associative and unital, with unit the conjugacy class
\{e\}
of the identity element
e
of
G
. There exists moreover an idempotent basis
(v_{\rho})
labelled by the set
Irr(G)
. Given
\rho\in Irr(G)
, its character
\chi_{\rho}:C(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is defined by

\chi_{\rho}(c)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(g))\text{ for any }g\in c.

(2.2)

The idempotent basis consists of the elements

v_{\rho}=\sum_{c\in C(G)}\frac{dim(\rho)}{\#G}\overline{\chi_{\rho}(c)}\cdot c.

(2.3)

The elements of the standard basis read

c=\sum_{\rho\in Irr(G)}f_{c}(\rho)\cdot v_{\rho}.

(2.4)

where

f_{c}(\rho)=\frac{\#c}{dim(\rho)}\chi_{\rho}(c).

(2.5)

The vector

\mathfrak{K}=\sum_{c\in C(G)}z_{c}c\overline{c}=\sum_{\rho\in Irr(G)}\left(\frac{\#G}{dim(\rho)}\right)^{2}\cdot v_{\rho},

(2.6)

where
z_{c}=\tfrac{\#G}{\#c}
and
\overline{c}=\{g^{-1},\ g\in c\}
, plays a key role in the classical Hurwitz theory.

2.2
Young diagrams

Fix a non-negative integer
d
. In the case of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
of permutations of the set
\{1,\ldots,d\}
, the sets
C(\mathfrak{S}_{d})
and
Irr(\mathfrak{S}_{d})
are both canonically labeled by the set of
integer partitions
of
d
. Such a partition is a sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{l})
of positive integers such that
\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{l}=d
and
\lambda_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{l}\geq 1
. The
size
of the partition
\lambda
is
|\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{l}=d
and its
length
is
l(\lambda)=l
.

The identification between the of partitions of
d
and
C(\mathfrak{S}_{d})
is obtained by sending
\lambda
to the conjugacy class
c_{\lambda}
of permutations whose cycle type is
\lambda
. The identification of a partition
\mu
and an irreducible representation
\rho_{\mu}
is more complicated ; see
[
2
, Lecture 4]
. If the context is clear enough, we might denote by
\lambda
and
\mu
respectively
c_{\lambda}
and
\rho_{\mu}
. For instance,
\chi_{\mu}(\lambda)
refers to
\chi_{\rho_{\mu}}(c_{\lambda})
and
f_{\lambda}(\mu)
to
f_{c_{\lambda}}(\rho_{\mu})
.

The case
d=0
refers to the trivial group
\mathfrak{S}_{0}
. There is only one partition, the empty partition, which corresponds to the unit of the group and to the trivial representation.

The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
admits a unique non-trivial morphism

\varepsilon:\mathfrak{S}_{d}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}.

(2.7)

It associates a permutation to its sign. The notation
\varepsilon(\lambda)
refers to the sign of any permutation in the conjugacy class
c_{\lambda}
.

Partitions of
d
can be depicted as
Young diagrams
. A Young diagram is a sequence of rows of boxes, left-justified, such that the lengths are non-increasing, see Figure
1
. To a partition
\lambda
, we associate the Young diagram whose
i
-th row contains
\lambda_{i}
boxes. The
diagonal length

r(\lambda)
of a partition
\lambda
is the number
\{i\ |\lambda_{i}\geq i\}
of diagonal boxes in the associated Young diagram. The pictorial description of partitions allows for a natural involution
\mu\mapsto\mu^{T}
on the set of partitions of
d
. On the level of Young diagrams, it is obtained by sending a diagram to its symmetric with respect to the diagonal. A partition is said to be
symmetric
if
\mu=\mu^{T}
.

(4,2,1,1)

2.3
Completed cycles

In the context of Hurwitz theory, completed cycles were introduced by Okounkov and Pandharipande
[
9
]
to express the stationnary Gromov-Witten with descendents in terms of Hurwitz numbers.

Following
[
6
,
9
]
, we extend the functions
f_{\lambda}(\mu)
by

f_{\lambda}(\mu)=\binom{m_{1}(\lambda)+|\mu|-|\lambda|}{m_{1}(\lambda)}f_{(\lambda,1,\ldots,1)}(\mu)

(2.8)

where
\lambda,\mu
might not have the same size. The number
m_{1}(\lambda)
is the number of parts
\lambda_{i}
that equal
1
. The extended function
f_{\lambda}(\mu)
vanishes for
|\mu|<|\lambda|
. We introduce the functions

p_{k}^{*}(\mu)=k!c_{k+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\mu_{i}-i+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}-\left(-i+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}

(2.9)

for positive integers
k
, where the coeffcients
(c_{k})
appear in the expansion

\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}(z)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}z^{k}\text{ with }\mathcal{S}(z)=\frac{\sinh(z/2)}{z/2}

(2.10)

and
(k)
is the partition of
k
with one part. By
[
6
]
, there exists coefficients
(q_{k,\nu})
for
|\nu|<k
such that

\frac{1}{k}p_{k}^{*}=f_{(k)}+\sum_{|\nu|}q_{k,\nu}f_{\nu}.

(2.11)

For instance,
q_{k,\emptyset}=(k-1)!c_{k+1}
and we interpret the formula as
q_{k,(k)}=1
.

Definition 1

(
[
9
]
)
.

The
k
-th completed cycle
is the formal sum of integer partitions

\overline{(k)}=(k)+\sum_{|\nu|<k}q_{k,\nu}\nu.

(2.12)

Example 2

(
[
9
]
)
.

The first few completed cycles are

\begin{array}[]{l}\overline{(1)}=(1)-\frac{1}{24}(\emptyset)\\
\overline{(2)}=(2)\\
\overline{(3)}=(3)+(1,1)+\frac{1}{12}(1)+\frac{7}{2880}(\emptyset)\\

By

2.4
Complex Hurwitz numbers

In this subsection, we review the classical Hurwitz numbers. The terminology
Complex

Definition 3

((Complex) Hurwitz numbers)
.

Let

The ramification profile over

\bm{\lambda}_{i}

(3)

is unramified over

The weight of the isomorphism class

Definition 4
.

A

The axioms of a Frobenius algebra reflect the relations between the morphisms in the category of oriented

Representation theory of a pair

In this section, we study the
signed Frobenius-Schur indicator
of a finite group
G

\varepsilon:G\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
, see Definition
3.1
. Lemma
7
expresses the signed Frobenius-Schur indicator of
(G,\varepsilon)
in terms of the (non-signed) Frobenius-Schur indicators of
G
and
\ker(\varepsilon)
. This is used in Subsection

\mathfrak{S}_{d}
, which recovers the formula obtained in

3.1
Signed Frobenius-Schur indicator

Let
G
be a finite group and
\varepsilon:G\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
be a non-trivial group morphism. Denote by
H
the kernel of
\varepsilon
. An element
\tau\in G
, and by extension its conjugacy class, is said to be
even
or
odd
if it satisfies respectively
\varepsilon(\tau)=1
or
\varepsilon(\tau)=-1
.

Definition 6

(
[
3
]
)
.

The
signed Frobenius-Schur indicator
of the pair
(G,\varepsilon)
is the function
SFS_{G,\varepsilon}:\{C(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by

SFS_{G,\varepsilon}(\chi)=\frac{1}{\#G}\sum_{\tau\in G}\varepsilon(\tau)\chi(\tau^{2}).

(3.1)

In
[
3
]
, the signed Frobenius-Schur indicator has been computed explicitly for the symmetric groups
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
with the sign morphism
\varepsilon
using the Weyl formula. The purpose of the present subsection is to provide a similar result for any pair
(G,\varepsilon)
. Since the pair
(G,\varepsilon)
is fixed, we omit the subscript
(G,\varepsilon)
for the rest of the subsection. We will often denote by
SFS(\rho)
the number
SFS(\chi_{\rho})
for
\rho\in Irr(G)
.

In order to compute
SFS(\chi)
, let us recall the
Frobenius-Schur indicator

[
2
, §3.5]
of
G
. It is defined on any central function
\chi
by

FS_{G}(\chi)=\frac{1}{\#G}\sum_{\tau\in G}\chi(\tau^{2}).

(3.2)

A direct computation shows that

SFS(\chi)+FS_{G}(\chi)=FS_{H}(\chi).

(3.3)

Thus, the knowledge of
FS_{G}
and
FS_{H}
is enough to determine
SFS
. It turns out that the values of the Frobenius-Schur indicator on the characters of irreducible representations are known
[
2
, §3.5]
.

(a)

Suppose that the irreducible representation
\rho
can be defined over
\mathbb{R}
. Then,
FS(\chi_{\rho})=1
. We call
\rho
a
real irreducible representation
.

(b)

Suppose that the character
\chi_{\rho}
of the irreducible representation
\rho
is not real-valued. Then,
FS(\chi_{\rho})=0
. We call
\rho
a
complex irreducible representation
.

(c)

Suppose that the irreducible representation
\rho
does not satisfy (a) or (b). Then,
FS(\chi_{\rho})=-1
. We call
\rho
a
quaternionic irreducible representation
.

In order to compute
SFS(\chi_{\rho})
for
\rho\in Irr(G)
using (
3.3
) and this knowledge on the Frobenius-Schur indicator, we describe how the restriction of
\rho
to
H
splits as a sum of irreducible representations of
H
. The morphism
\varepsilon:G\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}\subseteq GL(\mathbb{C})
defines an irreducible representation on
\mathbb{C}
, that we also denote by
\varepsilon
. Its character is

\chi_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon.

(3.4)

Taking the tensor product with
\varepsilon
defines an involution
\rho\mapsto\rho^{T}
of
Irr(G)
. An irreducible representation is said to be
symmetric
if
\rho^{T}
and
\rho
are isomorphic. Since

\chi_{\rho^{T}}(\tau)=\varepsilon(\tau)\chi_{\rho}(\tau),

(3.5)

the irreducible representation
\rho
is symmetric if and only if
\chi_{\rho}(\tau)=0
any time
\varepsilon(\tau)=-1
. By
[
2
, §5.1]
:

•

In the case
\rho
is symmetric, its restriction to
H
splits as the sum of two irreducible representations which are both real, both complex or both quaternionic.

•

Otherwise, the restriction of
\rho
to
H
is irreducible.

Lemma 7
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
\varepsilon:G\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
a non-trivial morphism. The signed Frobenius-Schur indicator
SFS(\chi_{\rho})
for
\rho\in Irr(G)
is computed using exactly one of the following cases.

(1)

\rho
is a real representation.

(1a)

If
\rho
is symmetric and its restriction to
H
splits as a sum of two real representations, then
SFS(\chi_{\rho})=1
.

(1b)

If
\rho
is symmetric and its restriction to
H
splits as a sum of two complex representations, then
SFS(\chi_{\rho})=-1
.

(1c)

If
\rho
is not symmetric, then
SFS(\chi_{\rho})=0
.

(2)

\rho
is a complex representation.

(2a)

If
\rho
is symmetric, then
SFS(\chi_{\rho})=0
.

(2b)

If
\rho
is not symmetric, then
SFS(\chi_{\rho})
equals the Frobenius-Schur indicator of
\chi_{\rho|_{H}}
, which can take any value in
\{1,0,-1\}
.

(3)

\rho
is a quaternionic representation.

(3a)

If
\rho
is symmetric and its restriction to
H
splits as a sum of two quaternionic representations, then
SFS(\chi_{\rho})=-1
.

(3b)

If
\rho
is symmetric and its restriction to
H
splits as a sum of two complex representations, then
SFS(\chi_{\rho})=1
.

(3c)

If
\rho
is not symmetric, then
SFS(\chi_{\rho})=0
.

Proof.

First, we prove that
SFS(\chi_{\rho})
belongs to
\{1,0,-1\}
. Denote by
S
and
\Lambda
the symmetric and anti-symmetric squares of
\rho
, such that
S\oplus\Lambda
is isomorphic to
\rho\otimes\rho
. Denote by
a,b,c
the number of copies of the representation
\varepsilon
in the decomposition of
S,\Lambda,\rho\otimes\rho
as a sum of irreducible representations. By
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
, we have

\chi_{\rho}(\tau^{2})=\chi_{S}(\tau)-\chi_{\Lambda}(\tau)\text{ and }\chi_{\rho}(\tau)^{2}=\chi_{S}(\tau)+\chi_{\Lambda}(\tau)

(3.6)

for all
\tau
. Multiplying those identities with
\varepsilon(\tau)
and summing over
\tau
, we obtain

SFS(\chi_{\rho})=a-b\text{ and }c=a+b.

(3.7)

Since
c
can also be expressed as

c=\frac{1}{\#G}\sum_{\tau\in G}\chi_{\rho}(\tau)\chi_{\rho^{T}}(\tau)=\delta_{\rho^{*},\rho^{T}}\in\{0,1\},

(3.8)

we have
a,b\in\{1,0\}
and
SFS(\chi_{\rho})\in\{1,0,-1\}
.

The cases (1),(2),(3) are handled similarly. We only provide the proof of the case (1). Formula (
3.3
) then reads
SFS(\chi_{\rho})=FS_{H}(\chi_{\rho})-1
.

Suppose first that
\rho
is symmetric. Then,
FS_{H}(\chi_{\rho})\in\{2,0,-2\}
. It can not take the value
-2
, otherwise we would obtain
SFS(\chi_{\rho})=-3
. The two other values are the cases (1a),(1b).

Suppose that
\rho
is not symmetric. Its restriction to
H
is also defined over
\mathbb{R}
, and we know that it is irreducible. Thus,
FS_{H}(\chi_{\rho})=1
and
SFS(\chi_{\rho})=0
. This is the case (1c).
∎

Since the characters of irreducible representations form a basis of the space of functions
\{C(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\}
, Lemma
7
describes entirely the signed Frobenius-Schur indicator.

A key role for signed Real Hurwitz numbers is played by the element

\mathfrak{L}=\sum_{\tau\in G}\varepsilon(\tau)\tau^{2}\in Z\mathbb{C}G.

(3.9)

It is of similar importance as
\mathfrak{K}
for Hurwitz numbers. When all the irreducible characters of
G
are real-valued, the element
\mathfrak{L}
posseses crucial properties described in Corollary
8
.

Corollary 8
.

Let
G
be a finite group whose characters are real-valued and
\varepsilon:G\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
a non-trivial morphism. Then,

\mathfrak{L}c=0\text{ in }Z\mathbb{C}G

(3.10)

for every odd conjugacy class
c
.

Proof.

In the idempotent basis,

\mathfrak{L}=\sum_{\rho\in Irr(G)}\frac{\#G}{dim(\rho)}SFS_{G,\varepsilon}(\rho)\cdot v_{\rho}

(3.11)

so that

\mathfrak{L}c=\sum_{\rho\in Irr(G)}\frac{\#G\#c}{dim(\rho)^{2}}SFS_{G,\varepsilon}(\rho)\chi_{\rho}(c)\cdot v_{\rho}.

(3.12)

The product
SFS_{G,\varepsilon}(\rho)\chi_{\rho}(c)
vanishes whenever
c
is odd. Indeed, the representation
\rho
is real or quaternionic since its character is real-valued. Thus, according to Lemma
7
, either
SFS_{G,\varepsilon}(\rho)=0
or
\rho
is symmetric. In the later case,
\chi_{\rho}(c)=\varepsilon(c)\chi_{\rho}(c)=-\chi_{\rho}(c)
. Thus,
\mathfrak{L}c=0
.
∎

3.2
The case of the symmetric group

In this subsection, we consider the symmetric group
G=\mathfrak{S}_{d}
with the sign morphism
\varepsilon:\mathfrak{S}_{d}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
. Its kernel is the alternating group
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
. By
[
2
, §5.1]
, the involutions
\rho_{\mu}\mapsto\left(\rho_{\mu}\right)^{T}
and
\mu\mapsto\mu^{T}
are related by

\rho_{\mu^{T}}=\left(\rho_{\mu}\right)^{T}.

(3.13)

In particular, the representation
\rho_{\mu}
is symmetric if and only if the partition
\mu
is symmetric.

Proposition 9
.

The signed Frobenius-Schur indicator
SFS_{\mathfrak{S}_{d},\varepsilon}
satisfies

SFS_{\mathfrak{S}_{d},\varepsilon}(\mu)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-1)^{\frac{d-r(\mu)}{2}}&\text{ if }\mu^{T}=\mu,\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(3.14)

Proof.

All irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
can be defined over
\mathbb{R}
(even over
\mathbb{Q}
). Therefore, by Lemma
7
,
SFS_{\mathfrak{S}_{d},\varepsilon}(\mu)=\pm 1
if
\mu
is symmetric and
SFS_{\mathfrak{S}_{d},\varepsilon}(\mu)=0
otherwise.

Suppose that
\mu
is symmetric. The restriction of
\rho_{\mu}
to
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
splits as the sum of two irreducible representations
\rho_{+},\rho_{-}
. The values of the characters
\chi_{\rho_{+}},\chi_{\rho_{-}}
can be described as follows. Following
[
2
, §5.1]
, we associate to the symmetric partition
\mu
another partition
\lambda
of
d
, with distinct odd parts, by requiring that
\lambda_{i}
is the number of boxes below and to the right of the
i
-th diagonal box in the Young diagram of
\mu
. Explicitly,
\lambda_{i}=2(\mu_{i}-i)+1=2\mu_{i}-2i+1
. The conjugacy class
c_{\lambda}
splits in
\mathfrak{A}_{d}
as the union of two different conjugacy classes
c_{+},c_{-}
.

By
[
2
, §5.1]
, the values of the characters
\chi_{\rho_{+}},\chi_{\rho_{-}}
are the following.

•

On a conjugacy class
c\neq c_{+},c_{-}
,
\chi_{\rho_{+}}(c)=\chi_{\mu}(c)/2=\chi_{\rho_{-}}(c)
. In particular, both are real numbers.

•

On the conjugacy classes
c_{+},c_{-}
, we have

\chi_{\rho_{+}}(c_{+})=(-1)^{\frac{d-r(\mu)}{2}}+\left((-1)^{\frac{d-r(\mu)}{2}}\lambda_{1},\ldots\lambda_{l}\right)^{1/2}=\chi_{\rho_{-}}(c_{-})

(3.15)

and

\chi_{\rho_{+}}(c_{-})=(-1)^{\frac{d-r(\mu)}{2}}-\left((-1)^{\frac{d-r(\mu)}{2}}\lambda_{1},\ldots\lambda_{l}\right)^{1/2}=\chi_{\rho_{-}}(c_{+}).

(3.16)

The number
d-r(\mu)
is even for
\mu
symmetric so that
(-1)^{\frac{d-r(\mu)}{2}}=\pm 1
. Thus, the characters
\chi_{\rho_{\pm}}
are real-valued if and only if
\frac{d-r(\mu)}{2}
is even. Lemma
7
implies the statement.
∎

An independant proof of Proposition
3.14
is provided in
[
3
, Lemma 9.6]
. It uses the Weyl formula for
B
-type Lie algebras.

4
Construction of the signed Real Hurwitz numbers

4.1
Real Riemann surfaces

Definition 10
.

A
Real Riemann surface

(X,\sigma)
is a closed Riemann surface
X
together with an anti-holomorphic involution
\sigma
. The pair is
connected
if
X
is connected. It is a
doublet
if it is isomorphic to a pair
(Y\sqcup\overline{Y},id)
, where
Y
is a connected Riemann surface,
\overline{Y}
is the same Riemann surface with the opposite complex structure, and the involution switches the two connected components.

Any Real Riemann surface is the disjoint union of connected Real Riemann surfaces and doublets. The
real locus

X^{\sigma}=\{x\in X\ |\ \sigma(x)=x\}
of a Real Riemann surface is a closed
1
-dimensional smooth submanifold,
ie.
a disjoint union of embedded circles. Except in Section
6
, we assume

X^{\sigma}=\emptyset.

(4.1)

Let
(X,\sigma)
be a Riemann surface and
B\subseteq X
a finite
\sigma
-invariant subset. It can be written as
B=\{b_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,b_{n}^{\pm}\}
where
\sigma(b_{i}^{\pm})=b_{i}^{\mp}
. The triple
(X,B,\sigma)
, together with a specified ordere
b_{i}^{+},b_{i}^{-}
for each
i
, will be called a
marked
Real Riemann surface. Our counts will be independant of the order if the target is connected and it will differ from the Complex Hurwitz numbers by a global sign if it is a doublet, see Definition
14
and Definition
21
. We denote respectively by
X_{\sigma}
,
X^{o}_{\sigma}
and
B_{\sigma}
the quotient space of
X
,
X\setminus B
and
B
by
\sigma
. They are smooth manifolds.

In this paper, homology and cohomology are taken with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Let
(X,B,\sigma)
be a connected marked Riemann surface. The spaces
X_{\sigma}
and
X_{\sigma}^{o}
are both unorientable. We can describe the canonical map

H_{1}(X_{\sigma}^{o})\rightarrow H_{1}^{BM}(X_{\sigma}^{o})

(4.2)

from homology to Borel-Moore homology as follows. Its kernel is generated by small loops around the missing points
B_{\sigma}
. Its cokernel consists of open paths joining two points of
B_{\sigma}
,
modulo
the classes of closed loops. Moreover,
H_{1}(X_{\sigma}^{o})
can be described explictly as the abelianization of the fundamental group of
X_{\sigma}^{o}
. The latter admits the presentation (see Figure
2
)

\pi_{1}(X_{\sigma}^{o},p)\simeq\left<\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{h},\beta_{h}\\
\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k}\\
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n}\end{array}\right|\begin{array}[]{cc}[\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}]\ldots[\alpha_{h},\beta_{h}]\\
=\gamma_{0}^{2}\ldots\gamma_{k}^{2}\delta_{1}\ldots\delta_{n}\end{array}\right>

(4.3)

arising from a standard polygonal description of
X_{\sigma}^{o}
. The non-negative integers
h,k
are related to the genus
g
of
X
by
g=2h+k
. Thus,
H_{1}(X_{\sigma}^{o})
can be described as the quotient of the free
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-vector space generated by the classes
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta
by the relation
\delta_{1}+\ldots+\delta_{n}=0
, and
H_{1}^{BM}(X_{\sigma}^{o})
admits a subspace freely generated by the classes
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
.

Lemma 11
.

The Poincaré dual
\gamma(X_{\sigma}^{o})\in H_{1}^{BM}(X_{\sigma}^{o})
of the first Stiefel-Whitney class
w_{1}(X_{\sigma}^{o})\in H^{1}(X_{\sigma}^{o})
is given in the presentation (
4.3
) by

\gamma(X_{\sigma}^{o})=\gamma_{0}+\ldots+\gamma_{k}.

(4.4)

Proof.

The cohomology class
w_{1}(X_{\sigma}^{o})
takes value
0
on any orientation-preserving loop and
1
on any orientation-reversing loop. Thus, under the identification
H^{1}(X^{o}_{\sigma})=H_{1}(X_{\sigma}^{o})^{*}
,

\omega_{1}(X_{\sigma}^{o})=\gamma^{0}+\ldots+\gamma^{k}

(4.5)

where we raise the labels to denote the dual basis. By a standard argument on the polygonal description, the Poincaré dual of
\gamma^{i}
is the class
\gamma_{i}
in
H_{1}^{BM}(X_{\sigma}^{o})
. The lemma is proved.
∎

Definition 12

(Admissible class)
.

A homology class

is

if its image in

is
\gamma(X_{\sigma}^{o})
.

Having chosen a presentation (
4.3
) of
\pi_{1}(X_{\sigma}^{o},p)
, any admissible class reads

a=\gamma_{0}+\ldots+\gamma_{k}+x_{1}\delta_{1}+\ldots+x_{n}\delta_{n}

(4.6)

for some
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 13
.

In the presentation (

corresponds to a cross-cap, see Figure

\pi_{1}(X_{\sigma}^{o},p)\simeq\left<\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{h},\beta_{h}\\

\gamma_{0}^{2}\ldots\gamma_{k-2}^{2}\zeta\xi^{-1}\zeta\xi\delta_{1}\ldots\delta_{n}\end{array}\right>.

(4.7)

With these new generators, an admissible class reads

a=\zeta+\gamma_{0}+\ldots+\gamma_{k-2}+x_{1}\delta_{1}+\ldots x_{n}\delta_{n}

(4.8)

with
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. The relation between the classes
\zeta,\xi
and

can be understood by considering different polygonal descriptions of the quotient

. It is described in the case

with its involution, we think of two of the middle circles as being exchanged, see Figure

.

4.2
Definition of the signs and closed formulas : connected Real target

Let
(X,B,\sigma)
be a connected marked Real Riemann surface. Given a degree
d
Real ramified cover
f:(X^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})\rightarrow(X,\sigma)
unramified over
X\setminus B
, we obtain a degree
d
unramified cover of
X_{\sigma}^{o}
and therefore a monodromy representation morphism

\rho_{f}:\pi_{1}(X_{\sigma}^{o},p)\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{d}

(4.9)

well-defined up to conjugacy in
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
. Composing with the sign morphism
\varepsilon:\mathfrak{S}_{d}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
, it induces a well-defined map

\varepsilon\rho_{f}:H_{1}(X_{\sigma}^{o})\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}.

(4.10)

Definition 14

(Signed Real Hurwitz numbers)
.

Let

•

g,d,n
be non-negative integers,

•

(X,B,\sigma)
a connected genus
g
marked Real Riemann surface with
B=\{b_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,b_{n}^{\pm}\}
,

•

\bm{\lambda}=(\bm{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\lambda}_{n})
a sequence of partitions of the integer
d
and

•

a\in H_{1}(X_{\sigma}^{o})
an admissible class.

The
connected signed Real Hurwitz number

\mathbb{R}H_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
is the signed weighted-number of isomorphism classes of degree
d
Real holomorphic maps
f:(X^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})\rightarrow(X,\sigma)
such that

(a)

(X^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
is a connected Real Riemann surface or a doublet,

(b)

The ramification profile over
b_{i}^{\pm}
is given by the partition
\bm{\lambda}_{i}
and

(c)

f
is unramified over
X\setminus B
.

The weight of the isomorphism class
[f]
is
1/\#\mathrm{Aut}(f)
and its sign is
\varepsilon\rho_{f}(a)
,
ie.

\mathbb{R}H_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})=\sum_{[f]}\frac{\varepsilon\rho_{f}(a)}{\#Aut(f)}.

(4.11)

The
disconnected signed Real Hurwitz number

\mathbb{R}H_{g,d}^{\bullet}(\bm{\lambda})
is defined similarly by removing the connectedness assumption in (a).

The admissible class
a
does not appear in the notations
\mathbb{R}H_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
and
\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
. In fact, those numbers do not depend on
a
. It is proved in Lemma
15
. By Definition
12
, the sign
\varepsilon\rho_{f}(a)
does not depend on
a
if all the partitions are even. The independence result is equivalent to the fact that the numbers
\mathbb{R}H_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
and
\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
vanish whenever a partition
\bm{\lambda}_{i}
is odd.

Lemma 15
.

The number
\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
is the coefficient of the identity in the product

\frac{1}{d!}\mathfrak{K}^{h}\mathfrak{L}^{k+1}c_{\bm{\lambda}_{1}}\ldots c_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}\in Z\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{d}

(4.12)

where
g=2h+k
. The numbers
\mathbb{R}H_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
and
\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
do not depend on the admissible class chosen.

Proof.

The standard argument in Hurwitz theory using monodromy representation and a presentation (
4.3
) of the fundamental group of
X_{\sigma}^{o}
shows that
\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
is the signed weighted number of tuples of permutations
\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta},\bm{\gamma}\bm{\delta}
satisfying

[\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}]\ldots[\alpha_{h},\beta_{h}]=\gamma_{0}^{2}\ldots\gamma_{k}^{2}\delta_{1}\ldots,\delta_{n}

(4.13)

and such that
\delta_{i}
has cycle type
\bm{\lambda}_{i}
. The weight is constant equal to
1/d!
and the sign is

\varepsilon(\gamma_{0})\ldots\varepsilon(\gamma_{k})\varepsilon(\bm{\lambda}_{1})^{x_{1}}\ldots\varepsilon(\bm{\lambda}_{n})^{x_{n}}

(4.14)

for some coefficients
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\{0,1\}
defined by the admissible class
a
. This signed weighted sum is the coefficient of the identity in

\frac{\varepsilon(\bm{\lambda}_{1})^{x_{1}}\ldots\varepsilon(\bm{\lambda}_{n})^{x_{n}}}{d!}\mathfrak{K}^{h}\mathfrak{L}^{k+1}c_{\bm{\lambda}_{1}}\ldots c_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}\in Z\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{d}.

(4.15)

If every partition
\bm{\lambda}_{i}
is even, then it is the required formula. Otherwise, Corrolary
8
implies that this product vanishes so that the sign
\varepsilon(\bm{\lambda}_{1})^{x_{1}}\ldots\varepsilon(\bm{\lambda}_{n})^{x_{n}}
does not contribute. Thus,
\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
does not depend on the choice of the admissible class
a
.

In order to prove it for the connected numbers
\mathbb{R}H_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
, we use the relation between connected and disconnected Hurwitz numbers, see
[
1
, Chapter 10]
in the Complex case. Choose an admissible class
a
and introduce the generating series
\mathcal{H}
of the connected signed Real Hurwitz of genus
g
with
n
pairs of marked points. It depends on formal variables
p_{i,j}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
j\geq 1
. We define the formal series
\mathcal{H}
as follows. Given partitions
\bm{\lambda}=(\bm{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\lambda}_{n})
of an integer
d
,
\mathbb{R}H_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
is the coefficient of the monomial

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{k=1}^{l(\bm{\lambda}_{i})}p_{i,\bm{\lambda}_{i,k}}

(4.16)

in
\mathcal{H}
. We obtain similarly
\mathcal{H}^{\bullet}
by considering the disconnected numbers
\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
. The two generating series are easily seen to satisfy

\mathcal{H}^{\bullet}=\exp( \mathcal{H} ).

(4.17)

This relation can be inverted to obtain
\mathcal{H}=\log( \mathcal{H}^\bullet)
. Since the right-hand side does not depend on
a
, the left-hand side does not either.
∎

Remark 16
.

The term
connected
qualifying the numbers
\mathbb{R}H_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
in Definition
14
refers to the fact that it corresponds to the count of holomorphic maps for which the quotient of the domain by the involution is connected (orientable for doublet domains, non-orientable for connected domains). It is expressed by the usual relation (
4.17
) between connected and disconnected theories.

The connected signed Real Hurwitz number
\mathbb{R}H_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
can be written as the sum of the
connected contributions
and the
doublet contributions
. In Corollary
19
, we give a closed formula of the doublet contributions in terms of the Complex Hurwitz numbers. Since this formula does not depend on the choice of the admissible class, both the connected and doublet contributions are independent of this choice.

Theorem 1
.

The disconnected signed Real Hurwitz numbers are given explicitly by

\mathbb{R}H_{g,d}^{\bullet}(\bm{\lambda})=\sum_{\mu^{T}=\mu}\left((-1)^{\frac{d-r(\mu)}{2}}\frac{dim(\mu)}{d!}\right)^{1-g}\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{\bm{\lambda}_{i}}(\mu).

(4.18)

Proof.

It is a computation done by expressing
\mathfrak{K},\mathfrak{L},c_{\lambda}
in the idempotent basis, see (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), and using Lemma
15
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d!}\mathfrak{K}^{h}\mathfrak{L}^{k+1}c_{\bm{\lambda}_{1}}\ldots c_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{|\mu|=d}\left(\frac{dim(\mu)}{d!}\right)^{-2h-k-1}SFS(\mu)^{k+1}f_{\bm{\lambda}_{1}}(\mu)\ldots f_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\mu)v_{\mu}

(4.19)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d!}\sum_{\mu^{T}=\mu}\left(SFS(\mu)\frac{dim(\mu)}{d!}\right)^{-1-g}f_{\bm{\lambda}_{1}}(\mu)\ldots f_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\mu)v_{\mu}.

(4.20)

The coefficient of the identity in this expression is

\sum_{\mu^{T}=\mu}\left(SFS(\mu)\frac{dim(\mu)}{d!}\right)^{1-g}f_{\bm{\lambda}_{1}}(\mu)\ldots f_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\mu).

(4.21)

Finally, we use Proposition
3.14
to express
SFS(\mu)
.
∎

Theorem
4.18
recovers from a different perspective the closed formulas obtained in
[
3
]
using Real Gromov-Witten invariants.

4.3
Doublet contribution

The connected signed Real Hurwitz number
\mathbb{R}H_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda})
is the sum of the doublet contributions and the connected contributions, defined by replacing the condition (a) of Definition
14
by respectively

(a’)

(X^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
is a doublet

and

(a”)

(X^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
is a connected Real Riemann surface.

In the present subsection, we study the doublet contribution. It vanishes if the degree is odd. If
d=2d^{\prime}
, Corollary
19
expresses it in terms of a signed sum of Complex Hurwitz numbers
H_{g,d^{\prime}}(\bm{\eta})
with
2n
marked points. In light of Corollary
19
, the doublet contribution turns out to be independent of the admissible class chosen, thus so is the connected contribution.

Since modifying the admissible class by a loop around a puncture modifies the count by the sign
\varepsilon(\bm{\lambda}_{i})
of the partition corresponding to the ramification profile around the pucture, this independance property is equivalent to the vanishing of the doublet contribution and of the connected contribution whenever one of the partitions
\bm{\lambda}_{i}
is odd.

Let
(X,B,\sigma)
be a marked connected Real Riemann surface of genus
g
. Let

f:(D,\sigma^{\prime})\rightarrow(X,\sigma)

(4.22)

be a degree
d=2d^{\prime}
ramified Real cover by a doublet, corresponding to the ramification profile
\bm{\lambda}=(\bm{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\lambda}_{n})
. Choosing a connected component
X^{\prime}
of
D
, one obtains a degree
d^{\prime}
ramified cover

f^{\prime}=f|_{X^{\prime}}:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X.

(4.23)

Consider a cross-cap circle in

The combinatorial coefficient account for the different ways to choose the subsets
S_{j}^{\pm}
given the partitions
(\bm{\nu}_{j})
. Comparing with (

\sum_{\mu^{T}=\mu}\left((-1)^{\frac{d-r(\mu)}{2}}\frac{dim(\mu)}{d!}\right)^{1-g}\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{\bm{\lambda}_{i}}(\mu)\prod_{j=1}^{m}\underbrace{\sum_{\nu}q_{\bm{k}_{j},\nu}f_{\nu}(\mu)}_{\frac{p^{*}_{\bm{k}_{j}}(\mu)}{\bm{k}_{j}}}.

∎

The decorations

can be understood geometrically as follows, see

in the context of double Hurwitz numbers. For each

The notion of connectedness and doublets has to be changed to reflect this geometrical point of view on the decorations.

In this section, we fix a pair
(G,\varepsilon)
as in Subsection
4.6
and omit
(G,\varepsilon)
from the notations. We degenerate the targets by collapsing simultaneously a pair of conjugate circles in the target curve. Such a
degeneration process
is introduced in Subsection
5.1
. In Subsection
5.2
, we relate the
(G,\varepsilon)
-Real Hurwitz numbers corresponding to a connected Real target to the
(G,\varepsilon)
-Real and Complex Hurwitz numbers of the degenerated target. It is a direct consequence of the orthogonality of the characters. In Subsection
5.4
, we consider the more subtle problem of the relation between the sign of a given covering and the sign of the degenerated covering. It enables to relate our signs with those provided in

5.1
Degenerations

Let
(X,\sigma,B)
be a marked Real Riemann surface. Given a pair of disjoint embedded circles in
X\setminus B
exchanged by
\sigma
, we construct another marked Real Riemann surface
(Y,\sigma,C)
as follows :

(1)

Collapse the two circles into two nodes, conjugated by
\sigma
.

(2)

Resolve each of the two nodes. Label the two marked points obtained by resolving the same node by the same label
\pm
. The involution
\sigma
extends uniquely to the four marked points obtained.

At the level of the complex structures, the degeneration process happens when one varries the complex structure of
(X,\sigma)
in such a way that the length of the embbeded circles tend to
0
.

When
(X,\sigma)
is connected of genus
g
, the degeneration
(Y,\sigma,C)
can have exactly four different topological types :

(a)

(Y,\sigma)
is the union of two connected Real Riemann surfaces of genus
h,h^{\prime}
such that
g=h+h^{\prime}+1
.

(b)

(Y,\sigma)
is the union of a connected Real Riemann surface of genus
h
and the doublet of a genus
h^{\prime}
Riemann surface such that
g=h+2h^{\prime}
.

(c)

(Y,\sigma)
is a connected Real Riemann surface of genus
h
such that
g=h+2
.

(d)

(Y,\sigma)
is the doublet of a Riemann surface of genus
h
such that
g=2h+1
.

The cases (a),(b),(c),(d) are depicted in Figure
7
.

Lemma 30
.

If the degeneration is of type (d), then each connected component of
Y
carries one of the additionnal marked points labelled
+
and one of those labelled
-
.

Proof.

Each connected component carries two additionnal marked points. Suppose that one component carries the two marked points labelled
+
, and the other the two marked points labelled
-
. The original Riemann surface must be homeomorphic to the space obtained from
Y
by gluing the positive marked points together, smoothing the node, and proceeding similarly for the negative marked points. The later space has two connected components, while
X
is connected. This is a contradiction.
∎

5.2
Degeneration formulas

The signed Real Hurwitz numbers are topological. Indeed, they do not depend on the complex structure chosen in the target. In particular, they remain constant during a degeneration process. At the limit, it implies an identity with the numbers associated to the degenerated marked Real Riemann surface. This is the content of Proposition
31
. We have chosen to provide a short representation-theoretic proof, but a combinatorial one could also have been given using Lemma
40
. The formulas involve the combinatorial coefficients

z_{c}=\frac{\#G}{\#c}

(5.1)

where
c
is a conjugacy class of
G
.

Proposition 31
.

The following degeneration formulas hold.

(a)

Real-Real degeneration :

\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{g}(\bm{c},\bm{c^{\prime}})=\sum_{c\in C(G)}z_{c}\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{h}(\bm{c},c)\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{h^{\prime}}(c,\bm{c^{\prime}})

(5.2)

with
g=h+h^{\prime}+1
.

(b)

Real-Complex degeneration :

\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{g}(\bm{c},\bm{c^{\prime}})=\sum_{c\in C(G)}z_{c}\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{h}(\bm{c},c)H^{\bullet}_{h^{\prime}}(c,\bm{c^{\prime}})

(5.3)

with
g=h+2h^{\prime}
.

(c)

Real degeneration :

\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{g}(\bm{c})=\sum_{c\in C(G)}z_{c}\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{h}(\bm{c},c,c)

(5.4)

with
g=h+2
.

(d)

Complex degeneration :

\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{g}(\bm{c})=\sum_{c\in C(G)}\varepsilon(c)z_{c}H^{\bullet}_{h}(\bm{c},c,c)

(5.5)

with
g=2h+1
.

Proof.

The characters of
G
are real-valued, see the assumption made in Subsection
4.6
. In this case, the orthogonality of the characters reads

\sum_{c\in C(G)}z_{c}f_{c}(\rho)f_{c}(\rho^{\prime})=\left(\frac{dim(\rho)}{\#G}\right)^{-2}\delta_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}.

(5.6)

The degeneration formulas are a direct consequence of this relation. Consider for instance the formula (b). The right-hand side involves the sum over
c\in C(G)
and two sums over irreducible representations
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
. We perform the sum over
c
first. The orthogonality of the characters transforms the two sums over irreducible characters into a single one. Thus, the right-hand side equals

\sum_{\rho^{T}=\rho}\left(SFS_{G,\varepsilon}(\rho)\frac{dim(\rho)}{\#G}\right)^{1-h}\prod_{i}f_{\bm{c}_{i}}(\rho)\left(\frac{dim(\rho)}{\#G}\right)^{-2}\left(\frac{dim(\rho)}{\#G}\right)^{2-2h^{\prime}}\prod_{j}f_{\bm{c^{\prime}}_{j}}(\rho).

(5.7)

All the representations involved in the sum satisfy
SFS_{G,\varepsilon}(\rho)=\pm 1
, so that we can add it inside the powers
-2
and
2-2h
. The degeneration formula (b) is proved by noticing that
1-g=(1-h)-2+(2-2h^{\prime})
if
g=h+2h^{\prime}
.

The proof of the formulas (a) and (c) is similar. In order to prove (d), we use the following version of the orthogonality of the characters :

\sum_{c\in C(G)}\varepsilon(c)z_{c}f_{c}(\rho)f_{c}(\rho)=\sum_{c\in C(G)}z_{c}f_{c}(\rho)f_{c}(\rho^{T})=\left(\frac{dim(\rho)}{\#G}\right)^{-2}\delta_{\rho,\rho^{T}}.

(5.8)

The right-hand side of the formula (d) involves a sum over
c
and a sum over
\rho
. Performing the sum over
c
first restricts the sum over
\rho
to the symmetric representations, and the right-hand side becomes

\sum_{\rho^{T}=\rho}\left(\frac{dim(\rho)}{\#G}\right)^{2-2h}\prod_{i}f_{\bm{c}_{i}}(\rho)\left(\frac{dim(\rho)}{\#G}\right)^{-2}.

(5.9)

We incorporate
SFS_{G,\varepsilon}(\rho)
as before and notice that
1-2g=2-2h-2
. This proves the formula (d).
∎

The degeneration formulas (b) and (d) both involve the creation of a doublet component, whose corresponding
(G,\varepsilon)
-Doublet Hurwitz numbers is given by Definition
4.55
.

In formula (b), the summands corresponding to an odd conjugacy class
c
vanish, and the left-hand-side vanishes if
\bm{c}
or
\bm{c^{\prime}}
contain an odd conjugacy. Thus, one can replace
H^{\bullet}_{h^{\prime}}(c,\bm{c^{\prime}})
by
H^{\bullet}_{h^{\prime},a}(c,\bm{c^{\prime}})
for any chosen puncture class
a
in the right-hand side of (b).

However, it is necessary to keep the sign
\varepsilon(c)
in (d). The righ-hand side involves

H^{\bullet}_{h,a}(\bm{c},c,c)=\varepsilon(c)H^{\bullet}_{h}(\bm{c},c,c)

(5.10)

where
a
is the puncture class consiting of a simple loop around one of the marked points created. One can replace
a
by any puncture class defined from a marking consistent with the degeneration process. The latter are constrained by Lemma
30
.

Remark 32
.

Using degenerations of types (a) and (b), one is able to express any
(G,\varepsilon)
-Real Hurwitz number in terms of
\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{0,G}(c)
with
c\in C(G)
and the
G
-Complex Hurwitz numbers.

Corollary 33
.

If
G=\mathfrak{S}_{d}
is the symmetric group of order
d
, then the formula

\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{g,d}(\bm{\lambda};\bm{k})=\sum_{\nu_{0},\ldots,\nu_{g}}H^{\bullet}_{0,d}(\bm{\lambda},\nu_{0},\ldots,\nu_{g};\bm{k})\prod_{i=0}^{g}z_{\nu_{i}}\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{0,d}(\nu_{i})

(5.11)

holds with completed cycles.

Proof.

The formula holds without completed cycles due to the degeneration formulas (a) and (b). The disconnected signed Real Hurwitz numbers and disconnected Complex Hurwitz numbers with completed cycles are linear combinations of their analogs without completed cycles. Thus, the formula holds also with completed cycles.
∎

The last degeneration formula has a surprising combinatorial consequence.

Corollary 34
.

Given a finite group
G
whose characters are real-valued and
\varepsilon:G\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
a non-trivial morphism,

\#\{\rho\in Irr(G)\ |\ \rho^{T}=\rho\}=\sum_{c\in C(G)}\varepsilon(c).

(5.12)

In particular, the number of symmetric Young diagrams of size

\sum_{|\lambda|=d}\varepsilon(c_{\lambda}).

(5.13)

The number
\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{1}(\emptyset)
is given explicitly by

\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{1}(\emptyset)=\sum_{\rho^{T}=\rho}1=\#\{\rho\in Irr(G)\ |\ \rho^{T}=\rho\}

(5.14)

according to theorem
4.54
. Using a degeneration of type (d), it also reads

\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{1}(\emptyset)=\sum_{c\in C(G)}\varepsilon(c)z_{c}H^{\bullet}_{0}(c,c)=\sum_{c\in C(G)}\varepsilon(c).

(5.15)

∎

5.3

In this Subsection, we use
(G,\varepsilon)
-Real Hurwitz numbers to describe an extra structure on the Frobenius algebra
(Z\mathbb{C}G,\star,e,\eta)
of Subsection
2.5
. This structure is called an
extended Frobenius algebra
in
[
11
]
. The case of the symmetric groups with the sign morphism has been obtained in
[
3
]
using the Real Gromov-Witten invariants. Recall the vector

\Delta=\sum_{c\in C(G)}z_{c}c\otimes\overline{c}

(5.16)

introduced in Subsection
2.5
.

Definition 35

(Extended Frobenius algebra,
[
11
]
)
.

An

is a Frobenius algebra
(A,\star,e,\eta)
together with

•

an involutive automorphism
\Omega
of
(A,\star,e,\eta)
and

•

a vector
\mathcal{U}\in A
which satisfies
\Omega(x\star\mathcal{U})=x\star\mathcal{U}
for all
x
, and such that
\mathcal{U}\star\mathcal{U}
equals the product of the bivector
(\Omega\otimes id_{A})(\Delta)
.

We define a linear map

Z_{X,\sigma,B}:Z\mathbb{C}G^{\otimes m}\rightarrow Z\mathbb{C}G^{\otimes n}

(5.17)

for any marked Real Riemann surface
(X,\sigma,B)
with
m
pairs of conjugated marked points considered as inputs and
n
as outputs. In what follows, the fact that the characters of
G
are real-valued implies that the conjugacy classes
c
and
\overline{c}
are always equal. The map
Z_{X,\sigma,B}
is defined in the standard basis. If
(X,\sigma)
is a connected Real Riemann surface of genus
g
, we set the image of
\bm{c}_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\bm{c}_{m}
to be

\sum_{\bm{c^{\prime}}}z_{\bm{c^{\prime}}_{1}}\ldots z_{\bm{c^{\prime}}_{n}}\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{g,G,\varepsilon}(\bm{{c}},\bm{c^{\prime}})\bm{c^{\prime}}_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\bm{c^{\prime}}_{n}.

(5.18)

If
(X,\sigma)
is the doublet of a genus
g
Riemann surface, we set it to be

\sum_{\bm{c^{\prime}}}z_{\bm{c^{\prime}}_{1}}\ldots z_{\bm{c^{\prime}}_{n}}H^{\bullet}_{g,G,\varepsilon,a}(\bm{{c}},\bm{c^{\prime}})\bm{c^{\prime}}_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\bm{c^{\prime}}_{n}

where
a
is the puncture class associated to the marking. In particular, if the doublet has a canonical marking, then

coincides with the linear map associated in Subsection
2.5
to one of the connected components of

In the general case, we define
Z_{X,\sigma,B}
by declaring that
Z
transforms disjoint unions into tensor product of maps.

Introduce a linear map
\Omega
and a vector
\mathcal{U}
as
Z_{X,\sigma,B}
for the doublet of sphere with one input, one output and the non-canonical marking, and a connected sphere with one output respectively.

Lemma 36
.

The vector
\mathcal{U}
is the element

\mathfrak{L}=\frac{1}{\#G}\sum_{\gamma\in G}\varepsilon(\gamma)\gamma^{2}=\sum_{\rho^{T}=\rho}SFS(\rho)\frac{\#G}{dim(\rho)}v_{\rho}

of (

\Omega(c)=\varepsilon(c)c\text{ or equivalently }\Omega(v_{\rho})=v_{\rho^{T}}.

The first expression of

\mathbb{R}H^{\bullet}_{0,G}(c)=\frac{1}{\#G}\sum_{\gamma^{2}\in c}\varepsilon(\gamma)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle=\sum_{c\in C(G)}z_{c}\sum_{\rho^{T}=\rho}SFS(\rho)\frac{dim(\rho)}{\#G}f_{c}(\rho)c

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho^{T}=\rho}SFS(\rho)\frac{\#G}{dim(\rho)}\sum_{c\in C(G)}\frac{dim(\rho)}{\#G}\chi_{\rho}(c)c

\displaystyle=\sum_{c^{\prime}\in C(G)}\varepsilon(c^{\prime})z_{c^{\prime}}H^{\bullet}_{0}(c,c^{\prime})c^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(c)c.

(5.27)

.

In the idempotent basis, the linear maps

The involution
\Omega

Proposition 39
.

Let
(X,\sigma,B)
be a marked connected Real Riemann surface and
(Y,\sigma,C)
its degeneration along a pair conjugated circles. Then,

The four types of degenerations must be treated seperately. They are similar so that we focus on the degenerations of type (d). Let
(X,\sigma,B)
be a connected Real Riemann surface of genus

with
m

of type (d) is the doublet of a genus

Riemann surface with

v_{\rho_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{\rho_{m}}\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{dim(\rho)}{\#G}\right)^{2-2g-2(n+1)}v_{\rho}^{\otimes n}&\text{ if }\rho_{1}=\ldots=\rho_{n}=:\rho=\rho^{T},\\

(5.35)

Since the power is even, one can add the factor

in the parenthesis. Writting the exponent as

Decay of CP-even Higgs H → h ​ γ ​ γ H\rightarrow h\gamma\gamma in Two Higgs Doublet Model: one-loop analytic results, ward identity checks
Khiem Hong Phan, Dzung Tri Tran, Thanh Huy Nguyen

Abstract
The first analytic expressions for loop-induced contributions for the decay of CP-even Higgs H → h ​ γ ​ γ H\rightarrow h\gamma\gamma with h h being Standard-Model-like Higgs boson within the framework of Two Higgs Doublet Model are presented in this paper. The one-loop form factors for the decay processes are written in terms of the scalar one-loop Passarino-Veltman functions following the notations of the packages LoopTools and Collier . Subsequently, physical results for the decay processes can be generated numerically by using one of the above-mentioned packages. The analytic expressions shown in this paper are verified by several numerical checks, for examples, the ultraviolet and infrared finiteness of one-loop amplitude. Furthermore, the amplitude satisfies the Ward-Takahashi identity due to on-shell photons in final states. The identity is also verified numerically in this work. In phenomenological studies, the differential decay rates as functions of the invariant mass of two photons in final states of H → h ​ γ ​ γ H\rightarrow h\gamma\gamma are first studied in parameter space of the Two Higgs Doublet Models.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{H\rightarrow h\gamma\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[F_{00}g^{\mu\nu}+F_{11}q_{1}^{\mu}q_{1}^{\nu}+F_{12}q_{1}^{\mu}q_{2}^{\nu}+F_{13}q_{3}^{\mu}q_{1}^{\nu}+F_{23}q_{3}^{\mu}q_{2}^{\nu}\Big]\varepsilon^{*}_{\mu}(q_{2})\varepsilon^{*}_{\nu}(q_{3}).

\displaystyle q_{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q_{1}+q_{2})^{2}=M_{h}^{2}+2q_{1}\cdot q_{2},

(18)

\displaystyle q_{13}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q_{1}+q_{3})^{2}=M_{h}^{2}+2q_{1}\cdot q_{3},

(19)

\displaystyle q_{23}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q_{2}+q_{3})^{2}=2q_{2}\cdot q_{3}.

(20)

The above-mentioned

\displaystyle q_{12}+q_{13}+q_{23}=M_{H}^{2}+M_{h}^{2}.

(21)

\displaystyle F_{13}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{q_{12}-M_{h}^{2}}{q_{23}}F_{11},

(22)

\displaystyle F_{00}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{q_{12}-M_{h}^{2}}{2}F_{12}-\frac{q_{23}}{2}F_{23},

(23)

\displaystyle F_{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{q_{13}-M_{h}^{2}}{q_{23}}F_{11},

(24)

\displaystyle F_{00}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{q_{13}-M_{h}^{2}}{2}F_{13}-\frac{q_{23}}{2}F_{23}.

Using the above relations,

\displaystyle\Bigg\{\Bigg[\frac{(q_{12}-M_{h}^{2})(q_{13}-M_{h}^{2})}{2q_{23}}\cdot g^{\mu\nu}+q_{1}^{\mu}q_{1}^{\nu}+\frac{(M_{h}^{2}-q_{13})}{q_{23}}\cdot q_{1}^{\mu}q_{2}^{\nu}

Conclusions

We have presented
the first analytical results
for one-loop induced contributions
to the important decay channels
of CP-even Higgs
H\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
and
H\rightarrow h\gamma\gamma
with
h
being
standard model-like Higgs boson
within the framework of Two
Higgs Doublet Model. Analytical
expressions for one-loop form
factors are written in terms
of the basis PV-functions
following the standard notations
of the packages
LoopTools

and
Collier
.
Subsequently, physical results
can be evaluated numerically
by using one of the mentioned
packages. Analytical formulas
shown in this paper have verified
by the numerical checks such as
the UV and IR-finiteness of
one-loop amplitude. Furthermore,
due to on-shell photons in final
states, the corresponding one-loop
amplitude obeys the Ward-Takahashi
identity. The identity has also
tested numerically. In phenomenological
studies, the differential decay rates
as functions of the invariant mass
of two photons in final states
are examined in parameter space
of the Two Higgs Doublet Models.

Appendix A: Tensor reduction for one-loop integrals

In this work, we follow
tensor reduction method developed in
Ref.
[
97
]
.
The method is reviewed briefly in
this appendix. In general,
tensor one-loop integrals with

N
-external lines
can be decomposed
into the basis integrals such as
scalar one-loop one-, two-, three-
and four- point integrals
(they are noted hereafter as

A_{0},\penalty\ B_{0},\penalty\ C_{0}
and
D_{0}
),
respectively. The definition for
tensor one-loop integrals
with rank
P
are as follows:

\displaystyle\{A;B;C;D\}^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\cdots\mu_{P}}=(\mu^{2})^{2-d/2}\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\dfrac{k^{\mu_{1}}k^{\mu_{2}}\cdots k^{\mu_{P}}}{\{D_{1};D_{1}D_{2};D_{1}D_{2}D_{3};D_{1}D_{2}D_{3}D_{4}\}}.

(39)

Where the numerator is
expressed in terms of
the Lorentz structure of
the corresponding
tensor one-loop integrals
with rank
P
. While
D_{j}

(
j=1,\cdots,4
) are for
the inverse Feynman propagators
in the denominators.
We show explicitly
the Feynman propagators
as follows:

\displaystyle D_{j}^{-1}=\dfrac{1}{[(k+q_{j})^{2}-m_{j}^{2}+i\rho]}.

(40)

In this definition,

q_{j}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}p_{i}
,

p_{i}
are the external momenta
and
m_{j}
are internal masses
in the loops. Dimensional
regularization for one-loop integrals
are performed within the space-time
dimension
d=4-2\varepsilon
for

\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.
The parameter
\mu^{2}
in the
integrals play role of a
renormalization scale.
Several reduction
formulas for one-loop
one-, two-, three- and
four-point tensor
integrals up to rank

P=3

[
97
]

are shown explicitly,
for examples,

\displaystyle A^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(41)

\displaystyle A^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}A_{00},

(42)

\displaystyle A^{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(43)

\displaystyle B^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\mu}B_{1},

(44)

\displaystyle B^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}B_{00}+q^{\mu}q^{\nu}B_{11},

(45)

\displaystyle B^{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{g,q\}^{\mu\nu\rho}B_{001}+q^{\mu}q^{\nu}q^{\rho}B_{111},

(46)

\displaystyle C^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{1}^{\mu}C_{1}+q_{2}^{\mu}C_{2}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}q_{i}^{\mu}C_{i},

(47)

\displaystyle C^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}C_{00}+\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{2}q_{i}^{\mu}q_{j}^{\nu}C_{ij},

(48)

\displaystyle C^{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\{g,q_{i}\}^{\mu\nu\rho}C_{00i}+\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{2}q^{\mu}_{i}q^{\nu}_{j}q^{\rho}_{k}C_{ijk},

(49)

\displaystyle D^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{1}^{\mu}D_{1}+q_{2}^{\mu}D_{2}+q_{3}^{\mu}D_{3}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}q_{i}^{\mu}D_{i},

(50)

\displaystyle D^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}D_{00}+\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{3}q_{i}^{\mu}q_{j}^{\nu}D_{ij},

(51)

\displaystyle D^{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\{g,q_{i}\}^{\mu\nu\rho}D_{00i}+\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{3}q^{\mu}_{i}q^{\nu}_{j}q^{\rho}_{k}D_{ijk}.

(52)

In these expression, we have used the
notation

\{g,q_{i}\}^{\mu\nu\rho}
as in
[
97
]
.
The tensor is defined
as follows:

\{g,q_{i}\}^{\mu\nu\rho}=g^{\mu\nu}q^{\rho}_{i}+g^{\nu\rho}q^{\mu}_{i}+g^{\mu\rho}q^{\nu}_{i}
.
All scalar coefficients

The basic PV-functions are

LoopTools

[
93
]

In this calculation, analytic results

\displaystyle\{A;B;C;D\}_{ijk\cdots}^{[f_{1},f_{2},\cdots](\cdots)}[q_{i}\cdot q_{j}]=\{A;B;C;D\}_{ijk\cdots}^{(\cdots)}[q_{i}\cdot q_{j};m_{f_{1}}^{2},m_{f_{2}}^{2},\cdots].

(53)

We also expand scalar one-loop
three-point functions in terms of

\displaystyle C_{0}(0,0,M^{2},m^{2},m^{2},m^{2})

\displaystyle+\dfrac{e^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\cdot\dfrac{g}{M_{W}}\cdot\kappa_{\phi\,WW}\times F_{\phi\rightarrow\gamma\gamma}^{W}

for

.
In this equation,

given explicitly

\displaystyle F_{\phi\rightarrow\gamma\gamma}^{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{m_{f}^{2}}{M_{\phi}^{2}}\right)\Big[2-\big(M_{\phi}^{2}-4m_{f}^{2}\big)C_{0}(0,0,M_{\phi}^{2},m_{f}^{2},m_{f}^{2},m_{f}^{2})\Big],

(61)

\displaystyle F_{\phi\rightarrow\gamma\gamma}^{W}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2}{M_{\phi}^{2}}\right)\Big[M_{\phi}^{2}+6M_{W}^{2}-6M_{W}^{2}(M_{\phi}^{2}-2M_{W}^{2})C_{0}(0,0,M_{\phi}^{2},M_{W}^{2},M_{W}^{2},M_{W}^{2})\Big],

(62)

as in Eq. (

),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\alpha}{4\pi}\Bigg\{\sum\limits_{f}N^{C}_{f}\,Q_{f}^{2}\cdot g_{\phi ff}\cdot(4\tau_{f})\cdot\Big[1+\Big(1-\tau_{f}\Big)f(\tau_{f})\Big]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\left(\dfrac{4\;g_{\phi H^{\pm}H^{\mp}}}{M_{\phi}^{2}}\right)\cdot\Big[1-\tau_{H^{\pm}}f(\tau_{H^{\pm}})\Big]\Bigg\}.

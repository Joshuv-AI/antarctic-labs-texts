Introduction

Two curves in
\mathds{P}^{3}
are
geometrically directly linked
if their union is the complete intersection of two surfaces. This definition can be extended to define an equivalence relation,
linkage equivalence
(for more details see
[
14
]
). In the 1940’s Apery and Gaeta showed that a smooth curve
C
in
\mathds{P}^{3}
is in the linkage class of a complete intersection if and only if it is
arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay (ACM for short)
(cf.
[
2
]
and
[
7
]
). This result was extended to arbitrary codimension two subschemes of projective space by Peskine and Szpiro
[
17
]
, putting the theory of liaison into the framework of scheme theory. We are interested in studying families related by links, in particular, we ask ourselves:

How do the linkage classes behave in (flat) families?

We study this question, and exhibit families of curves in the linkage class of two disjoint lines as limits of ACM curves. We can phrase our first result as follows:

Theorem A

(Corollary
4.8
)
.

Let
\overline{\mathscr{C}_{r}}
be the closure of the family of curves in the linkage class of a line in the Hilbert scheme of curves of degree
d_{r}=\frac{r(r+1)}{2}
and genus
g_{r}=\frac{r(r+1)(2r-5)}{6}+1
, then
\overline{\mathscr{C}_{r}}
is irreducible and has two irreducible divisors, each parametrizes curves in the linkage class of two disjoint lines.

This is not obvious since the Hilbert scheme is reducible, which constitutes a major obstacle this paper had to overcome. Furthermore, it is the first result that uses Liaison Theory in describing effective divisors of the Hilbert scheme of curves in projective 3-space, as far as we know. This Theorem is an effective instance of a result by Ballico and Bolondi (cf.
[
3
]
*Corollary 2.3).

There is a further reason that attracted our attention to our previous theorem: its implications in birational geometry.

Indeed, consider the following two families of 1-dimensional sub schemes of degree 6 and arithmetic genus 3: the family of hyperelliptic curves of degree
6
, denoted by
\mathscr{C}^{h}
, and the family of curves that parameterize the union of a plane quartic with two incident skew lines, denoted by
\mathscr{A}
. The closure of these two families are the divisors of the theorem A on
\overline{\mathscr{C}_{3}}
. The component
\overline{\mathscr{C}_{3}}
may not be normal, thus, we have to take the normalzation of
\overline{\mathscr{C}_{3}}
, denoted by
\mathscr{B}
, that contains all the locally Cohen-Macaulay curves, in particular, the families
\overline{\mathscr{C}^{h}}
and
\overline{\mathscr{A}}
. In this case, if
N^{1}(\mathscr{B})
is the space of divisors of
\mathscr{B}
modulo numerical equivalence, then we prove:

Theorem B

(Theorem
2.4
)
.

The classes of
\overline{\mathscr{A}}
and
\overline{\mathscr{C}^{h}}
in
N^{1}(\mathscr{B})
are linearly independent and each of them spans an extremal ray of the cone of effective divisors:

\overline{\text{Eff}(\mathscr{B})}\subseteq N^{1}(\mathscr{B}).

As a corollary, we compute the dimension of the space
N^{1}(\mathscr{B})
:

Corollary

(Corollary
2.6
)
.

The dimension of the vector space
N^{1}(\mathscr{B})
is
3
.

In general, these divisors allow us to give a lower bound for the dimension of the vector space of divisors of
\overline{\mathscr{C}_{r}}
modulo numerical equivalence:

Corollary
.

For every
r\geq 4
we have that

dim\,N^{1}(\overline{\mathscr{C}_{r}})\geq 2.

Theorem A is proved using basic notions of liaison theory and the theory developed in
[
11
]
. On the other hand, to prove Theorem B we construct a birational map that contracts both families.

Structure of the paper.
The first section presents the preliminaries of Liaison Theory, the definitions of the objects that we study throughout and verify that there exists a unique component of ACM curves in
\mathscr{H}_{r}
. Section 2 is devoted to the case of curves of degree
6
and genus
3
and focuses on the main component. In this section, we prove Theorem B. The last two sections are dedicated to the study of the general case; in particular, in section 4 we prove Theorem A.

The algebraic structure of additive conjucyclic codes over 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} Journal: COAM
Jingjie Lv, Ruihu Li, Juan Li

Abstract
Conjucyclic codes are an important and special family of classical error-correcting codes, which have been used to construct binary quantum error-correcting codes (QECCs) Calderbank . However, at present, the research on the conjucyclic codes is extremely insufficient. This paper will explore the algebraic structure of additive conjucyclic codes over 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} for the first time. Mainly via the trace function from 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} down 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} , we will firstly build an isomorphic mapping between q 2 q^{2} -ary additive conjucyclic codes and q q -ary linear cyclic codes. Since the mapping preserves the weight and orthogonality, then the dual structure of these codes with respect to the alternating inner product will be described. Then a new construction of QECCs from conjucyclic codes can be obtained. Finally, the enumeration of q 2 q^{2} -ary additive conjucyclic codes of length n n and the explicit forms of their generator and parity-check matrices will be determined.



∎

Introduction

Additive codes
Delsarte
were first discovered by Delsarte and
Levenshtein
in 1973. In
Calderbank
, Calderbank
et
al.
built a direct relationship between quaternary additive codes
and binary quantum error-correcting codes (QECCs). The latter codes
can effectively reduce the influences of environmental and
operational noise (decoherence) of quantum information, which play
an important role in both quantum communication and quantum
computation. Thereafter, the study on additive codes has been a hot
topic
Benbelkacem
;
Ezerman
;
Huffman2
;
Kim1
;
Kim2
;
Mahmoudi
.

Additive cyclic codes are a special class of additive codes, whose
codewords are closed for the cyclic shifts (see Definition 1). So
they can effectively be encoded and decoded by the shift registers.
The algebraic structure of
quaternary additive cyclic codes was first explored by Huffman

Huffman1
, who found a canonical decomposition for those codes
of odd length. Later, the generalization of additive cyclic codes
over general fields was developed by Bierbrauer
Bierbrauer
.
In 2017, Güneri
et al.

Guneri
presented a
sufficient condition under which a special class of additive cyclic
codes are complementary dual. In
Shi
, Shi
et al.

showed the existence of asymptotically good additive cyclic codes.
In 2023, ref.
Dastbasteh
gave a polynomial representation for
additive cyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
and some new binary
quantum codes are also derived.

Similar to the additive cyclic codes, additive conjucyclic codes are
closed for the conjugated cyclic shift (see Definition 2), which
also can be utilized to construct QECCs
Calderbank
. However,
compared to the sufficient studies of additive cyclic codes, the
research on the additive conjucyclic codes now is extremely
insufficient. The major reason is that these codes can not be
represented canonically by polynomials, which leads to some
difficulties in the structural analysis of these codes.
Very recently, Abualrub

et al.
explored the algebraic descriptions of conjucyclic
codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
by a technical linear algebraic approach

Abualrub
;
Abualrub1
. However, this approach will cause some
difficulties when we intend to study the
q^{2}
-ary conjucyclic
codes.

In this paper, we will focus on the algebraic structure of additive
conjucyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Utilizing the trace
function from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
down
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we find
that there exists an isomorphic mapping between
q^{2}
-ary additive
conjucyclic codes and
q
-ary linear cyclic codes. By defining the
alternating inner product on vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
,
the dual-containing condition of these conjucyclic codes will be
obtained, and a constructed method of
q
-ary QECCs from conjucyclic
codes can be derived. In addition, the enumeration of
q^{2}
-ary
additive conjucyclic codes of length
n
as well as their generator
and parity-check matrices will be determined.

This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces some
definitions and preliminary properties of cyclic codes, conjucyclic
codes and some inner products. In Section 3, we will build a
canonical correspondence between
q^{2}
-ary additive conjucyclic
codes and
q
-ary linear cyclic codes mainly by virtue of the trace

2
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
be a finite field with
q^{2}
elements, where

q
is a power of prime
p
. We call
p
the characteristic of

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
denoted by
{\rm char}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
.
For
any element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, let
\bar{\alpha}
denote
its conjugation
\alpha^{q}
. Recall that a linear code with
parameters
[n,k]_{q^{2}}
is defined as a linear subspace of

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
with dimension
k
. An
(n,M)_{q^{2}}

additive code
\mathscr{C}
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}

with
M
elements for the additive operation. It implies that for
arbitrary element
k\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and codeword
\bm{c}\in\mathscr{C}
, the vector
k\bm{c}
may no longer belong to

\mathscr{C}
. In particular, if
k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and the
vector
k\bm{c}
must be in
\mathscr{C}
, then we call
\mathscr{C}

an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear additive code. In this paper, we use
additive code to indicate it unless otherwise stated.

For vector
\bm{u}=(u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
,
denote
w_{h}(\bm{u})={\rm card}\{i|u_{i}\neq 0\}
,
0\leq i\leq n-1
. Let
\mathscr{C}
be a
q
-ary linear or additive code of
length
n
, then the minimum Hamming weight of
\mathscr{C}
is
defined as

w_{h}(\mathscr{C})={\rm min}\{w_{h}(\bm{u})|\bm{u}\in\mathscr{C},\bm{u}\neq\bm{0}\}.

If
n=2m
, we write

w_{s}(\bm{u})={\rm card}\{j|(u_{j},u_{m+j})\neq(0,0)\}
for
0\leq j\leq m-1.
Then we denote the minimum symplectic weight of

\mathscr{C}

w_{s}(\mathscr{C})={\rm min}\{w_{s}(\bm{u})|\bm{u}\in\mathscr{C},\bm{u}\neq\bm{0}\}.

Now we introduce the definition of cyclic codes as follows:

Definition 1

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a linear or additive code of length
n
over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For any codeword

\bm{c}=(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in\mathscr{C}
, if vector

\sigma(\bm{c})=(c_{n-1},c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-2})
also belongs to

\mathscr{C}
, where
\sigma
is the right cyclic shift operator,
then the code
\mathscr{C}
is called a
q
-ary cyclic code of
length
n
.

Let
\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
be a quotient
ring. Define an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-module isomorphic mapping
\varphi

from vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}
as

\varphi(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})=c_{0}+c_{1}x+\cdots+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}.

It is easy to see that
\mathscr{C}
is a
q
-ary linear cyclic code
of length
n
if and only if
\varphi(\mathscr{C})
is an ideal of
the ring
\mathbb{R}
. Since
\mathbb{R}
is a principal ideal ring,
one can prove that there exists a one-to-one correspondence between
a monic divisor of
x^{n}-1
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and a
q
-ary linear
cyclic code of length
n
. Therefore, the factorization of
x^{n}-1

is crucial to classify linear cyclic codes of length
n
. If a
linear cyclic code
\mathscr{C}
is generated by a monic divisor

g(x)
of
x^{n}-1
,
g(x)
is called the generator polynomial of

\mathscr{C}
. For convenience, the cyclic code
\mathscr{C}
will
be denoted by

\langle g(x)\rangle
in the rest of the paper.

The definition of conjucyclic codes can be given similarly.

Definition 2

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a
q^{2}
-ary linear or additive code of length

n
. For each codeword
\bm{c}=(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in\mathscr{C}
, the code
\mathscr{C}
is said to be conjucyclic if

T(\bm{c})=(\bar{c}_{n-1},c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-2})\in\mathscr{C}
,
where
T
is the right conjucyclic shift operator.

Next, we propose some inner products and the corresponding dual
codes of
\mathscr{C}
, which will be used in this paper.

Given two vectors

\bm{u}=(u_{0},u_{1}\ldots,u_{n-1})
and

\bm{v}=(v_{0},v_{1}\ldots,v_{n-1})
in the vector space

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, define their Euclidean inner product as

\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\rangle_{e}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}u_{i}v_{i}.

If
n=2m
, their symplectic inner
product is defined as

\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\rangle_{s}=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}(u_{i}v_{m+i}-u_{m+i}v_{i}).

The Euclidean and
symplectic dual codes of code
\mathscr{C}
are respectively denoted
as

\mathscr{C}^{\perp_{e}}=\{\bm{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}|\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\rangle_{e}=0,~\forall\bm{u}\in\mathscr{C}\},~~\mathscr{C}^{\perp_{s}}=\{\bm{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2m}|\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\rangle_{s}=0,~\forall\bm{u}\in\mathscr{C}\}.

Since for any
q^{2}
-ary additive code
\mathscr{C}
, its dual codes

\mathscr{C}^{\perp_{e}}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\perp_{s}}
must be

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-linear. Hence, these usual inner products are not
the most useful to study the dual structure of additive codes
anymore.

In the following, we will give an alternating inner product on
the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
. Choose
\beta
as a
primitive element of field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.
For
\bm{u}=(u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n-1})
,

\bm{v}=(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
, the
alternating inner product is defined as

\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\rangle_{a}=(\bar{\beta}^{2}-\beta^{2})\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(u_{i}\bar{v}_{i}-\bar{u}_{i}v_{i}).

It is easy to check that the alternating inner product

\langle\cdot~,~\cdot\rangle_{a}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-bilinear. In
addition, since
\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\rangle_{a}^{q}=\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\rangle_{a}
, we have
\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\rangle_{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Particularly, when
q=2
, we
have

\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\rangle_{a}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(u_{i}\bar{v}_{i}+\bar{u}_{i}v_{i})={\rm Tr}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\bar{v}_{i}\right),

where
{\rm Tr}
is
the trace function of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
down
\mathbb{F}_{q}

that is defined as
{\rm Tr}(x)=x+\bar{x}
for all

x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Therefore, the alternating inner product
can be viewed as a generalized form of the trace-Hermitian inner
product appeared in
Calderbank
.

Similarly, the alternating dual codes of
\mathscr{C}
can be
presented as follows:

\mathscr{C}^{\perp_{a}}=\{\bm{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}|\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\rangle_{a}=0,~\forall\bm{u}\in\mathscr{C}\}.

3
The algebraic structure of additive conjucyclic codes

In this section, we will firstly show that there exists a bijective
correspondence between
q^{2}
-ary conjucyclic codes of length
n
and

q
-ary linear cyclic codes of length
2n
.

For any element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, we define a mapping
\phi
from

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
as follows:

\begin{split}\phi:~~\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}&\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\\
\alpha&\longmapsto\left({\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha)\right),\\
\end{split}

where
\beta
is the primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and
{\rm Tr}
is the trace function
from

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
down
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proposition 1

The map
\phi
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear isomorphism.

Proof

For
any
k_{1}
,
k_{2}

\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\alpha_{1}
,

\alpha_{2}

\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, we have

\begin{split}\phi(k_{1}\alpha_{1}+k_{2}\alpha_{2})&=\left({\rm Tr}(\beta(k_{1}\alpha_{1}+k_{2}\alpha_{2})\right),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}(k_{1}\alpha_{1}+k_{2}\alpha_{2})))\\
&=\left(k_{1}{\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{1})+k_{2}{\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{2}),k_{1}{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{1})+k_{2}{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{2})\right)\\
&=k_{1}({\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{1}),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{1}))+k_{2}({\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{2}),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{2}))\\
&=k_{1}\phi(\alpha_{1})+k_{2}\phi(\alpha_{2}),\end{split}

which implies that the map
\phi
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear.

Next, we prove that
\phi
is an injective mapping. Assume

\phi(\alpha_{1})=\phi(\alpha_{2})
, then
\left({\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{1}),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{1})\right)-\left({\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{2}),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{2})\right)=\left({\rm Tr}(\beta(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\right)=(0,0)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\beta(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})+\bar{\beta}(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})^{q}=0\\
\bar{\beta}(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})+\beta(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})^{q}=0.\end{cases}

(1)

If
{\rm char}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})=2
, by Equation (1), we have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\beta(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})=\bar{\beta}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{q}\\
\bar{\beta}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})=\beta(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{q},\end{cases}

which means that both
\beta(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})
and

\bar{\beta}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})
are in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since

\beta
is a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and

\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are arbitrary elements in

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, then
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}
can be derived
directly.

Now we suppose
{\rm char}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\neq 2
,
then Equation (1) gives

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\beta+\bar{\beta})\left((\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})+(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})^{q}\right)=0\\
(\beta-\bar{\beta})\left((\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})-(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})^{q}\right)=0.\end{cases}

Obviously,
(\beta+\bar{\beta})\neq 0
and

(\beta-\bar{\beta})\neq 0
. If not,
\beta^{2q-2}=1
or

\beta^{q-1}=1
. There exists a contradiction
because the order of

\beta
is
q^{2}-1
and
q-1<2q-2<q^{2}-1
. So the above equations can
be transformed into

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})+(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})^{q}=0\\
(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})-(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})^{q}=0\end{cases}\Rightarrow\begin{cases}2(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})=0\\
2(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})^{q}=0\end{cases}\Rightarrow\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}.

Hence,
\phi
is an injective map. Moreover, since

|\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}|=|\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}|=q^{2}
,
\phi
must be
surjective. In conclusion,
\phi
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear
isomorphism. ∎

Notice that the mapping
\phi
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear
isomorphic. Then any element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
is
determined by
({\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha))\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
. In fact,

\alpha=\frac{1}{\beta-\beta^{2q-1}}{\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha)-\frac{\beta^{q-1}}{\beta-\beta^{2q-1}}{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha).

(2)

Example 1

Let
q=3
and
\beta
be a primitive element of field

\mathbb{F}_{9}
.
Then the mapping
\phi
from

\mathbb{F}_{9}
to
\mathbb{F}_{3}
can be given as follows:

\begin{split}\phi:~~\mathbb{F}_{9}&\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{3}^{2}\\
0\longmapsto(0,0)&~~~~~~~\beta^{4}\longmapsto(2,2)\\
1\longmapsto(1,1)&~~~~~~~\beta^{5}\longmapsto(0,2)\\
\beta\longmapsto(0,1)&~~~~~~~\beta^{6}\longmapsto(2,1)\\
\beta^{2}\longmapsto(1,2)&~~~~~~~\beta^{7}\longmapsto(2,0)~.\\
\beta^{3}\longmapsto(1,0)&~~~~~~~\end{split}

One can check that the mapping
\phi
is an
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-linear
isomorphism. According to Equation (2), we have

\frac{1}{\beta-\beta^{5}}=\beta^{3}
,

\frac{\beta^{2}}{\beta-\beta^{5}}=\beta^{5}
and

\begin{split}0=\beta^{3}\times 0-\beta^{5}\times 0&~~~~~~~\beta^{4}=\beta^{3}\times 2-\beta^{5}\times 2\\
1=\beta^{3}\times 1-\beta^{5}\times 1&~~~~~~~\beta^{5}=\beta^{3}\times 0-\beta^{5}\times 2\\
\beta=\beta^{3}\times 0-\beta^{5}\times 1&~~~~~~~\beta^{6}=\beta^{3}\times 2-\beta^{5}\times 1\\

Naturally, the mapping
\phi
can be generalized to the vector space

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
. For vector

\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
, the extended mapping denoted by
\Phi
is

\begin{split}\Phi(\bm{\alpha})=\left({\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{0}),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{0}),\ldots,{\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{n-1}),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{n-1})\right).\\
\end{split}

If we define a permutation
\varsigma
on vector

\bm{v}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{2n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
as

\varsigma(\bm{v})=(v_{0},v_{2},\ldots,v_{2n-2},v_{1},v_{3},\ldots,v_{2n-1}),

then the following proposition can be gained directly.

Proposition 2

\Psi=\varsigma\cdot\Phi
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear
isomorphism, and for all vector

\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
,
we have

\Psi(\bm{\alpha})=\left({\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{0}),\ldots,{\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{n-1}),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{0}),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{n-1})\right).

Now we can build a relationship between the conjucyclic shift on
vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
and the cyclic shift on vectors in

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
.

Proposition 3

For any vector
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
, we have

\Psi(T(\bm{\alpha}))=\sigma(\Psi(\bm{\alpha}))
, where
\sigma
and

T
are cyclic and conjucyclic shift operators, respectively. To be
a more intuitive understanding, a commutative diagram is exhibited
as follows:

\begin{CD}\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}@>{T}>{}>\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}\\
@V{\Psi}V{}V@V{}V{\Psi}V\\
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}@>{\sigma}>{}>\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}.\end{CD}

Proof

For any

\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
,
we have

\begin{split}\Psi(T(\bm{\alpha}))=&\Psi(\bar{\alpha}_{n-1},\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{n-2})=({\rm Tr}(\beta\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}),{\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{0}),\ldots,{\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{n-2}),\\
&{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\bar{\alpha}_{n-1}),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{0}),\ldots,{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{n-2})),\end{split}

\begin{split}\sigma(\Psi(\bm{\alpha}))=&\sigma({\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{0}),\ldots,{\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{n-1}),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{0}),\ldots,{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{n-1}))=({\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{n-1}),\\
&{\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{0}),\ldots,{\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{n-2}),{\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{n-1}),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{0}),\ldots,{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{n-2})).\end{split}

Since
{\rm Tr}(\beta\bar{\alpha}_{n-1})={\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\alpha_{n-1})
,
{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}\bar{\alpha}_{n-1})={\rm Tr}(\beta\alpha_{n-1})
, the
proposition holds obviously. ∎

Then a bijective correspondence between additive conjucyclic codes
and linear cyclic codes can be obtained.

Theorem 3.1

\mathscr{C}
is an additive conjucyclic code over

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
of length
n
if and only if there exists a

q
-ary linear cyclic code
\mathscr{D}=\Psi(\mathscr{C})
of length

2n
. Moreover, the minimum Hamming weight of
\mathscr{C}
is equal
to the minimum symplectic weight of
\mathscr{C}
, i.e.,

w_{h}(\mathscr{C})=w_{s}(\mathscr{D})
.

Proof

If
\mathscr{C}
is an additive conjucyclic code over

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
of length
n
, the mapping
\Psi
gives that

\mathscr{D}
is a vector subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
. It is
equivalent to say,
\mathscr{D}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear code
of length
2n
. Via Proposition 3, we have

\sigma(\Psi(\mathscr{C}))=\Psi(T(\mathscr{C}))=\Psi(\mathscr{C})
.
So
\mathscr{D}
is a
q
-ary linear cyclic code of length
2n
.

Conversely, assume that
\mathscr{D}
is a
q
-ary linear cyclic
code of length
2n
. Since
\Psi
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear
isomorphism, then
\mathscr{C}=\Psi^{-1}(\mathscr{D})
is an additive subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
.
In addition, by

\Psi(T(\mathscr{C}))=\sigma(\Psi(\mathscr{C}))=\Psi(\mathscr{C})
,
we have
T(\mathscr{C})=\mathscr{C}
. Hence,
\mathscr{C}
is an
additive conjucyclic code.

For each codeword

\bm{c}=(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in\mathscr{C}
, according to
Equation (2), it is easy to see that
c_{i}=0
if and only if

\left({\rm Tr}(\beta c_{i}),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}c_{i})\right)=0
for

0\leq i\leq n-1
, which implies

w_{h}(\mathscr{C})=w_{s}(\mathscr{D})
.
∎

Remark 2

By the theory of cyclic codes
Huffman0
, these binary cyclic
codes derived from additive conjucyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}

in
Abualrub
must be repeated-root since
{\rm gcd}(2n,2)=2
.
However, ref.
Castagnoli
claimed that the repeated-root
cyclic codes can not be asymptotically better than the simple-root
cyclic codes. In this work, our cyclic codes derived from additive
conjucyclic codes will contain the both cases.

In the following result, we will exhibit that the mapping
\Psi

also preserves the orthogonality.

Proposition 4

Let
\bm{u}
and
\bm{v}
be two vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
.
Then

\langle\Psi(\bm{u}),\Psi(\bm{v})\rangle_{s}=\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\rangle_{a},

where
\langle\cdot~,~\cdot\rangle_{s}
and

\langle\cdot~,~\cdot\rangle_{a}
denote the symplectic and
alternating inner product, respectively.

Proof

Select
\bm{u}=(u_{0},\ldots,u_{n-1})
and

\bm{v}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
, we have

\Psi(\bm{u})=\left({\rm Tr}(\beta u_{0}),\ldots,{\rm Tr}(\beta u_{n-1}),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}u_{0}),\ldots,{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}u_{n-1})\right),

\Psi(\bm{v})=\left({\rm Tr}(\beta v_{0}),\ldots,{\rm Tr}(\beta v_{n-1}),{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}v_{0}),\ldots,{\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}v_{n-1})\right).

\begin{split}\text{Then}~\langle\Psi(\bm{u}),\Psi(\bm{v})\rangle_{s}=&\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left({\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}u_{i}){\rm Tr}(\beta v_{i})-{\rm Tr}(\beta u_{i}){\rm Tr}(\bar{\beta}v_{i})\right)\\
=&\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left((\bar{\beta}u_{i}+\beta\bar{u}_{i})(\beta v_{i}+\bar{\beta}\bar{v}_{i})-(\beta u_{i}+\bar{\beta}\bar{u}_{i})(\bar{\beta}v_{i}+\beta\bar{v}_{i})\right)\\
=&\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\beta^{q+1}u_{i}v_{i}+\bar{\beta}^{2}u_{i}\bar{v}_{i}+\beta^{2}\bar{u}_{i}v_{i}+\beta^{q+1}\bar{u}_{i}\bar{v}_{i}-\beta^{q+1}u_{i}v_{i}\\
&~~~~~-\beta^{2}u_{i}\bar{v}_{i}-\bar{\beta}^{2}\bar{u}_{i}v_{i}-\beta^{q+1}\bar{u}_{i}\bar{v}_{i})\\
=&\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\bar{\beta}^{2}u_{i}\bar{v}_{i}+\beta^{2}\bar{u}_{i}v_{i}-\beta^{2}u_{i}\bar{v}_{i}-\bar{\beta}^{2}\bar{u}_{i}v_{i})\\
=&\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\bar{\beta}^{2}(u_{i}\bar{v}_{i}-\bar{u}_{i}v_{i})+\beta^{2}(\bar{u}_{i}v_{i}-u_{i}\bar{v}_{i}))\\
=&(\bar{\beta}^{2}-\beta^{2})\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(u_{i}\bar{v}_{i}-\bar{u}_{i}v_{i})=\langle\bm{u},\bm{v}\rangle_{a}.\\
\end{split}

∎

Proof

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we have developed the algebraic theory of additive
conjucyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. At first, we have
illuminated that there exists a one-to-one correspondence between

q^{2}
-ary additive conjucyclic codes and
q
-ary linear cyclic
codes.
Then by defining an alternating inner product on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
, we
have given a necessary and sufficient condition under which these
conjucyclic codes are dual-containing.
A new constructed method of QECCs from additive conjucyclic
codes can be derived. In addition, the enumeration of
q^{2}
-ary
additive conjucyclic codes of length
n
as well as their generator
and parity-check matrices were also determined.

As far as we know, this may be the first attempt to provide
non-binary QECCs from additive conjucyclic codes. We hope that
applying conjucyclic codes to construct QECCs with good parameters
would attract more scholars.

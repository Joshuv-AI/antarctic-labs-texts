Spectral Turán Type Problems on Cancellative Hypergraphs
Zhenyu Ni, Lele Liu, Liying Kang

Abstract
Let G G be a cancellative 3 3 -uniform hypergraph in which the symmetric difference of any two edges is not contained in a third one. Equivalently, a 3 3 -uniform hypergraph G G is cancellative if and only if G G is { F 4 , F 5 } \{F_{4},F_{5}\} -free, where F 4 = { a ​ b ​ c , a ​ b ​ d , b ​ c ​ d } F_{4}=\{abc,abd,bcd\} and F 5 = { a ​ b ​ c , a ​ b ​ d , c ​ d ​ e } F_{5}=\{abc,abd,cde\} . A classical result in extremal combinatorics stated that the maximum size of a cancellative hypergraph is achieved by the balanced complete tripartite 3 3 -uniform hypergraph, which was firstly proved by Bollobás and later by Keevash and Mubayi. In this paper, we consider spectral extremal problems for cancellative hypergraphs. More precisely, we determine the maximum p p -spectral radius of cancellative 3 3 -uniform hypergraphs, and characterize the extremal hypergraph. As a by-product, we give an alternative proof of Bollobás’ result from spectral viewpoint. Keywords: Hypergraph; Spectral radius; Spectral Turán problem. AMS Classification: 05C35; 05C50; 05C65.

Consider an

little understanding of the hypergraph Turán number. We refer the reader to the surveys

[
6
,

,

In this paper we consider spectral analogues of Turán type problems for

[

[
10
]
, Keevash-Lenz-Mubayi

-spectral radius of any

-spectral version of the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem on

t
-intersecting

-graphs. Recently, Ellingham-Lu-Wang
[

show that the

It is well-known that the study of Turán numbers dates back to Mantel’s theorem, which states that

3.1
General properties on cancellative hypergraphs

We start this subsection with a basic fact.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

be a cancellative hypergraph, and

be adjacent vertices. Then
L(u)
and
L(v)
are edge-disjoint graphs.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
e\in E(L(u))\cap E(L(v))
. Since

and

are adjacent in

, we have

\{u,v\}\subset e_{1}\in E(G)
for some edge

. Hence,
e_{2}=e\cup\{u\}
,
e_{3}=e\cup\{v\}
and
e_{1}

are three edges of
G
such that
e_{2}\Delta e_{3}\subset e_{1}
, a contradiction.
∎

Let
G
be a
3
-graph and
v\in V(G)
. We denote by
E_{v}(G)
the collection of edges of

containing

E_{v}(G)=\{e:v\in e\in E(G)\}

and

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a cancellative
3

T_{v}^{u}(G)
is also cancellative for any

.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that there exist three edges

e_{1}\triangle e_{2}\subset e_{3}
.
Recalling the definition of
T_{v}^{u}(G)

u
,
v
are non-adjacent in

, and

(e\cup\{u\})\setminus\{v\}\in E(G)
for any

is cancellative,
we have
v\in e_{1}\cup e_{2}\cup e_{3}
. Denote by

,
e_{2}
,
e_{3}
containing

. It
suffices to consider the following three cases.

Case 1.

\alpha=3
. We have

. Hence,
e_{1}^{\prime}=\left(e_{1}\cup\{u\}\right)\setminus\{v\}
,

e_{2}^{\prime}=\left(e_{2}\cup\{u\}\right)\setminus\{v\}
and
e_{3}^{\prime}=\left(e_{3}\cup\{u\}\right)\setminus\{v\}
are three edges
in

with

. This contradicts the fact that

is cancellative.

Case 2.

\alpha=2

or

. If

e_{3}\in E(G)
. It follows that

e_{1}^{\prime}=(e_{1}\cup\{u\})\setminus\{v\}
,
e_{2}^{\prime}=(e_{2}\cup\{u\})\setminus\{v\}
and
e_{3}
are three edges of
G

with
e_{1}^{\prime}\triangle e_{2}^{\prime}\subset e_{3}
, which is a contradiction. If
v\in(e_{1}\cap e_{3})\setminus e_{2}
, then
e_{2}\in E(G)
.
It follows that
e_{1}^{\prime}=(e_{1}\cup\{u\})\setminus\{v\}
,
e_{2}
and
e_{3}^{\prime}=(e_{3}\cup\{u\})\setminus\{v\}
are
three edges of
G
with
e_{1}^{\prime}\triangle e_{2}\subset e_{3}^{\prime}
, a contradiction.

Case 3.

\alpha=1
. Without loss of generality, we assume
v\in e_{3}\setminus(e_{1}\cup e_{2})
. Then

e_{1}\in E(G)
and
e_{2}\in E(G)
. We immediately obtain that
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
and
e_{3}^{\prime}=(e_{3}\cup\{u\})\setminus\{v\}

are three edges of
G

e_{1}\triangle e_{2}\subset e_{3}^{\prime}

∎

Lemma 3.3

Let
p>1
and
G
be a complete
3

\lambda^{(p)}(G)=\frac{(27\cdot|E(G)|)^{1-1/p}}{9}.

Proof.

Assume that

,
V_{2}
and
V_{3}
are the vertex classes of

Let

, and set

for short. In light of eigenequation (

3.2
Extremal
p
-spectral radius of cancellative hypergraphs

Let
\Ex_{sp}(n,\{F_{4},F_{5}\})
be the set of all
3
-graphs attaining the maximum

-spectral radius among
cancellative hypergraphs on
n
vertices. Given a vector
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a set
S\subset[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
,
we write
\bm{x}(S):=\prod_{i\in S}x_{i}
for short. The
support set

S
of a vector

is the index
of non-zero elements in
\bm{x}
, i.e.,
S=\{i\in[n]:x_{i}\neq 0\}
. Also, we denote by
x_{\min}:=\min\{|x_{i}|:i\in[n]\}

and
x_{\max}:=\max\{|x_{i}|:i\in[n]\}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p>1
,
G\in\Ex_{sp}(n,\{F_{4},F_{5}\})
, and
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{S}_{p,+}^{n-1}
be an eigenvector corresponding
to
\lambda^{(p)}(G)
. If
u,v
are two non-adjacent vertices, then
x_{u}=x_{v}
.

Proof.

Assume
u
and
v
are two non-adjacent vertices in
G
. Since
G
is a cancellative
3
-graph, we have
T_{u}^{v}(G)

is also cancellative by Lemma
3.2
. It follows from (
2.1
) and
(
2.2
) that

\displaystyle\lambda^{(p)}(T_{u}^{v}(G))

\displaystyle\geq 3\sum_{e\in E(G)}\bm{x}(e)-3\sum_{e\in E_{u}(G)}\bm{x}(e)+3\sum_{e\in E_{v}(G)}\bm{x}(e\setminus\{v\})\cdot x_{u}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{(p)}(G)-3\lambda^{(p)}(G)x_{u}^{p}+3\lambda^{(p)}(G)x_{v}^{p-1}x_{u}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{(p)}(G)+3\lambda^{(p)}(G)(x_{v}^{p-1}-x_{u}^{p-1})\cdot x_{u},

which yields that
x_{u}\geq x_{v}
. Likewise, we also have
x_{v}\geq x_{u}
by considering
T_{v}^{u}(G)
.
Hence,
x_{u}=x_{v}
, completing the proof of Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
p>1
,
G\in\Ex_{sp}(n,\{F_{4},F_{5}\})
, and

be two non-adjacent vertices. Then there exists

Proof.

Assume that
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{S}_{p,+}^{n-1}
is an eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda^{(p)}(G)
. By

,
x_{u}=x_{v}
. Without loss of generality, we assume

. In view

) and (

), we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{(p)}(T_{u}^{v}(G))

\displaystyle\geq 3\sum_{e\in E(G)}\bm{x}(e)-3\sum_{e\in E_{u}(G)}\bm{x}(e)+3\sum_{e\in E_{v}(G)}\bm{x}(e\setminus\{v\})\cdot x_{u}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{(p)}(G)+3\lambda^{(p)}(G)(x_{v}^{p-1}-x_{u}^{p-1})\cdot x_{u}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{(p)}(G).

Observe that
T_{u}^{v}(G)
is a cancellative
3
-graph and

obtain that
\lambda^{(p)}(T_{u}^{v}(G))=\lambda^{(p)}(G)

∎

Next, we give an estimation on the entries of eigenvectors corresponding to
\lambda^{(p)}(G)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
G\in\Ex_{sp}(n,\{F_{4},F_{5}\})
and
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{S}_{p,+}^{n-1}
be an eigenvector corresponding
to

Suppose to the contrary that

. Let
u
and

v
be two vertices such that
x_{u}=x_{\min}
and

\left(1+\frac{x_{u}}{x_{v}}\right)\left(\frac{x_{u}}{x_{v}}\right)^{p-1}\leq\bigg(1+\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{2/(p-1)}\bigg)\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{7}{4}\cdot\frac{9}{16}<1,

which implies that

x_{v}^{p}-x_{u}^{p}>x_{u}^{p-1}x_{v}.

(3.2)

On the other hand, by eigenequations we have

\sum_{e\in E_{v}(G)\setminus E_{u}(G)}\bm{x}(e)\geq\lambda^{(p)}(G)(x_{v}^{p}-x_{u}^{p}).

(3.3)

Now, we consider the cancellative
3
-graph
T_{u}^{v}(G)
. In light of (

) and
(

), we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{(p)}(T_{u}^{v}(G))

\displaystyle\geq 3\sum_{e\in E(G)}\bm{x}(e)-3\sum_{e\in E_{u}(G)}\bm{x}(e)+3\sum_{e\in E_{v}(G)\setminus E_{u}(G)}\bm{x}(e\setminus\{v\})\cdot x_{u}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{(p)}(G)-3\lambda^{(p)}(G)x_{u}^{p}+3\lambda^{p}(G)(x_{v}^{p}-x_{u}^{p})\cdot\frac{x_{u}}{x_{v}}

\displaystyle>\lambda^{(p)}(G)+3\lambda^{(p)}(G)\Big(-x_{u}^{p}+x_{u}^{p-1}x_{v}\cdot\frac{x_{u}}{x_{v}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{(p)}(G),

where the third inequality is due to (

). This contradicts the fact that

has maximum

-spectral radius over all cancellative hypergraphs.
∎

The following statements hold:

(1)

(2)

d_{G^{*}}(u_{2})>n(n-1)/12

(3)

Proof of Claim

.
Since

is a cancellative
3

\lambda^{(3)}(G^{*})\geq\lambda^{(3)}(T_{3}(n))=\frac{\big(27\cdot t_{3}(n)\big)^{2/3}}{9}.

By simple algebra we see

\lambda^{(3)}(G^{*})\geq\frac{\big((n-2)(n+1)^{2}\big)^{2/3}}{9}>\frac{n(n-1)}{9}.

(3.4)

(1). By eigenequation with respect to

, we have

\lambda^{(3)}(G^{*})x_{u_{1}}^{2}=\sum_{\{u_{1},i,j\}\in E(G^{*})}x_{i}x_{j}\leq d_{G^{*}}(u_{1})x_{u_{1}}^{2}.

Combining with (
3.4
), we get

d_{G^{*}}(u_{1})\geq\lambda^{(3)}(G^{*})>\frac{n(n-1)}{9}.

(3.5)

(2). Observe that the definition of
U_{1}
, and
L_{G^{*}}(u)=L_{G^{*}}(v)
for any pair
u,v\in U_{1}
.
We immediately obtain that
|(e\setminus\{u_{2}\})\cap U_{1}|\leq 1
for each
e\in E_{u_{2}}(G^{*})
. It
follows from
x_{u_{2}}=\max\{x_{v}:v\in V(G^{*})\setminus U_{1}\}
that

\lambda^{(3)}(G^{*})x_{u_{2}}^{2}=\sum_{\{u_{2},i,j\}\in E(G^{*})}x_{i}x_{j}\leq d_{G^{*}}(u_{2})x_{u_{1}}x_{u_{2}},

which, together with Lemma
3.6
for

, gives

\displaystyle d_{G^{*}}(u_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{x_{u_{2}}}{x_{u_{1}}}\cdot\lambda^{(3)}(G^{*})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{4}\cdot\lambda^{(3)}(G^{*})

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{12}n(n-1).

The last inequality is due to (
3.4
).

(3). Let
v
be an arbitrary vertex in
V(G^{*})
. Then

\lambda^{(3)}(G^{*})x_{v}^{2}=\sum_{\{v,i,j\}\in E(G^{*})}x_{i}x_{j}\leq d_{G^{*}}(v)x_{u_{1}}^{2}.

Hence, by Lemma
3.6
and (

\Box

Next, we consider the graph

Claim 3.2

and

Corollary 3.1
.

Let

3
-graph on

Proof.

.
In view of (

), we deduce that

\lambda^{(3)}(G)\geq\frac{P_{G}(\bm{z})}{\|\bm{z}\|_{3}^{3}}=\frac{3|E(G)|}{n}.

On the other hand, by Theorem

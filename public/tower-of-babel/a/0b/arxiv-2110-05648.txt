Peak algebras in combinatorial Hopf algebras
Farid Aliniaeifard, Shu Xiao Li

Abstract
The peak algebra is originally introduced by Stembridge using enriched P P -partitions. Using the character theory by Aguiar-Bergeron-Sottile, the peak algebra is also the image of Θ \Theta , the universal morphism between certain combinatorial Hopf algebras. We extend the notion of peak algebras and theta maps to shuffle, tensor, and symmetric algebras. As examples, we study the peak algebras of symmetric functions in non-commuting variables and the graded associated Hopf algebra on permutations. We also introduce a new shuffle basis of quasi-symmetric functions that its elements are the eigenfunctions of Θ \Theta . Using this new basis, we show that the peak algebra is the space spanned by the set of shuffle basis elements indexed by compositions whose all parts are odd.

Introduction

A special family of symmetric functions, known as the Schur’s
Q

27

to study the projective representations of symmetric and alternating groups. Combinatorially, the Schur’s

28

. Moreover, the subspace spanned by Schur’s
Q

\Omega

[
20
]
.

Using the combinatorics of enriched
P
-partitions, the Schur’s
Q
functions are generalized to the peak algebra
\Pi
, indexed by odd compositions, in the fundamental paper
[
29
]
. The peak algebra and its graded dual have triggered a variety amount of studies. For instance, it is shown that the peak algebra corresponds to the representation of
0
-Hecke-Clifford algebra. Also, the dual peak algebra is a Hopf ideal in noncommutative symmetric functions with respect to the internal product. See
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
15
,
22
]
for a small portion.

A natural question arises: is it possible to define analogous peak algebra for other Hopf algebras?

There are already many trials to generalize peak algebra in the literature. Most of these generalizations are combinatorial through enriched
P
-partition and peak sets. Notably, in
[
17
]
the peak algebra is generalized to Poirier-Reutenauer Hopf algebra of standard Young tableaux, which is introduced in
[
23
]
. Other generalizations can be found in
[
2
,
5
,
16
]
.

In this paper, we try to give an answer to this question from an algebraic angle. As observed in
[
1
]
, there is another description of
\Omega
and
\Pi
, given by character theory of combinatorial Hopf algebras. The Hopf algebra of quasi-symmetric functions
\mathrm{QSym}
and the canonical character

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}:&\mathrm{QSym}&\to&\mathbb{C}\\
&f(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)&\mapsto&f(1,0,0,\dots)\end{array}

is the unique Hopf morphism that makes the following diagram commute.

\Theta_{\mathrm{Sym}}
.

For any combinatorial Hopf algebra
\mathsf{H}
with morphism
\Phi:\mathsf{H}\to\mathrm{QSym}

The image

1.

They give a shuffle-like Hopf structure i.e. shuffle product and deconcatenation coproduct,

Using this new basis and its dual basis, we are able to find explicit theta maps and peak algebras for shuffle algebras, tensor algebras and symmetric algebras. The symmetric functions in non-commuting variables,

which finds a set of free generators that are also primitive. Our construction results in a peak algebra indexed by set partitions whose parts all have odd size. It is worthy to mention that in a forthcoming paper, the authors also construct peak algebra of

There is another large family of shuffle and tensor algebras that our construction can be applied to. As shown in
[
3

, for any Hopf algebra
\mathsf{H}
that is graded as a coalgebra, the associated graded Hopf algebra,

is a tensor algebra. We use the graded associated Hopf algebra on permutations, also defined in

This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 gives necessary backgound information on the Hopf algebras symmetric functions and quasi-symmetric functions, together with character theory of combinatorial Hopf algebras and precise definitions of theta maps. The shuffle basis is introduced and studied in section 3. In section 4, we revisit the shuffle, tensor and symmetric algebra. We then define candidates of theta maps and give explicit formula. The Hopf subalgebras of odd elements are introduced as the peak algebras under theta maps. And lastly, section 5 gives examples of theta maps and peak algebras, including the symmetric functions in non-commuting variables, and the graded associated Hopf algebra on permutations.

Preliminaries

In this section we present the Hopf algebras
\mathrm{Sym},\mathrm{QSym},\mathrm{NSym}
, together with the character theory of combinatorial Hopf algebras. For more details about Hopf algebras we refer the readers to
[
13
]
. The base field is set to be
\mathbb{C}

2.1
The Hopf algebras
\mathrm{QSym},\mathrm{Sym}
and
\mathrm{NSym}

A
composition

\alpha
of a positive integer
n
, written
\alpha\models n
, is a finite ordered list of positive integers
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell})
where
\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{\ell}=n
. We let
\ell(\alpha)=\ell
be the number of parts of
\alpha
and it is called the
length
of
\alpha
.
When there is no confusion, we sometimes write
\alpha
as a word
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\cdots\alpha_{\ell(\alpha)}
. The
concatenation
of
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell(\alpha)})\models n
and
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{\ell(\beta)})\models m
is the composition
\alpha\beta=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell(\alpha)},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{\ell(\beta)})
.

For a positive integer
n
, let
[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. There is a one-to-one correspondence
\mathcal{I}
between the set of all compositions of
n
and the set of all subsets of
[n-1]
such that for
\alpha\models n
,

\mathcal{I}(\alpha)=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{\ell(\alpha)-1}\}.

Let
\alpha,\beta\models n
. We write
\beta\leq\alpha
, or
\alpha
refines
\beta
if
\mathcal{I}(\beta)\subseteq\mathcal{I}(\alpha)
.

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
denote the set of permutations of
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Let
{\rm Sh}_{n,m}
be the following subset of the permutation group
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+m}
,

{\rm Sh}_{n,m}=\{\sigma\in{S}_{n+m}:\sigma^{-1}(1)<\sigma^{-1}(2)<\cdots<\sigma^{-1}(n);\sigma^{-1}(n+1)<\cdots<\sigma^{-1}(n+m)\}.

For compositions
\alpha
and
\beta
with lengths
n
and
m
, respectively, we define
\alpha\shuffle\beta
to be the multi-set

\{(\gamma_{\sigma(1)},\gamma_{\sigma(2)},\dots,\gamma_{\sigma(n+m)}):\sigma\in{\rm Sh}_{n,m}\}

where
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{n+m})
is the concatenation
\alpha\beta
.

The quasi-shuffle
\alpha\overline{\shuffle}\beta
is the multi-set of compositions
(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{\ell})
such that

1.

(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{\ell})\leq(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n+m})
for some
(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n+m})\in\alpha\shuffle\beta
,

2.

either
\delta_{i}=\gamma_{j}
for some
j
, or
\delta_{i}=\gamma_{j}+\gamma_{j+1}
where
\gamma_{j}
is a part of
\alpha
and
\gamma_{j+1}
is a part of
\beta
.

For a composition
\alpha
of
n
, the
monomial quasisymmetric function

M_{\alpha}
is

M_{\alpha}:=\sum_{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{\ell(\alpha)}}x_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{\ell(\alpha)}}^{\alpha_{\ell(\alpha)}}

which is an element of the algebra of bounded-degree formal power series in commutative variables

\{x_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
. By convention,
M_{()}=1
where
()
is the unique composition of
0
.
The vector space of
quasisymmetric functions
is denoted by
\mathrm{QSym}
and is defined as

\mathrm{QSym}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{QSym}_{n}

where

\mathrm{QSym}_{n}=\mathbb{C}\text{-span}\{M_{\alpha}:\alpha\models n\}.

The space of quasisymmetric functions is indeed a Hopf algebra with product inherited from the ring of bounded-degree formal power series. Its product is given by quasi-shuffle

M_{\alpha}\cdot M_{\beta}=\sum_{\gamma\in\alpha\overline{\shuffle}\beta}M_{\gamma}.

Its coproduct is given by deconcatenation

\Delta(M_{\alpha})=\sum_{\beta\gamma=\alpha}M_{\beta}\otimes M_{\gamma}.

A special subspace of
\mathrm{QSym}
is the Hopf algebra of
symmetric functions
,
\mathrm{Sym}
. The space
\mathrm{Sym}
is (commutatively) freely generated by the
scaled power sum functions
defined as

p_{n}=\frac{1}{n}M_{n}.

The scaled power sum functions are primitive elements i.e. for
n\geq 1
,
\Delta(p_{n})=1\otimes p_{n}+p_{n}\otimes 1
. For a composition
\alpha
, we write
p_{\alpha}=p_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{\alpha_{\ell(\alpha)}}
. In particular,
\mathrm{Sym}
is both commutative and cocommutative.

The complete homogeneous functions of
\mathrm{Sym}
are defined as

h_{n}=\sum_{\alpha\models n}M_{\alpha}

and we write
h_{\alpha}=h_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots h_{\alpha_{\ell(\alpha)}}
.

There is a well-known expansion of
p_{n}
in terms of the complete homogeneous functions, cf.
[
13
, Ex. 5.43]
, given by

p_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\alpha\models n}(-1)^{\ell(\alpha)-1}\alpha_{\ell(\alpha)}h_{\alpha}.

The Hopf algebra of
non-commutative symmetric functions
,
\mathrm{NSym}
, originally defined in
[
12
]
, is the graded dual of
\mathrm{QSym}
. Let
\{H_{\alpha}\}
be the basis, indexed by compositions, of
\mathrm{NSym}
dual to the basis
\{M_{\alpha}\}
of
\mathrm{QSym}
. Thus, We have the Hopf pairing
\langle H_{\alpha},M_{\beta}\rangle=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
where
\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
is the Kronecker delta.

Equivalently,
\mathrm{NSym}=\langle H_{1},H_{2},\cdots\rangle
is the non-commutative algebra freely generated by the set
\{H_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
with coproduct defined, on generators and extended linearly and multiplicatively, as

\Delta(H_{n})=\sum_{i+j=n}H_{i}\otimes H_{j}.

For a composition
\alpha
, we write
H_{\alpha}=H_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots H_{\alpha_{\ell(\alpha)}}
. The Hopf algebra
\mathrm{NSym}
is graded with
\deg(H_{n})=n
. A linear basis for the homogeneous component
\mathrm{NSym}_{n}
of degree
n
is
\{H_{\alpha}:\alpha\models n\}
.

It is well-known that
\mathrm{Sym}
is self-dual via the isomorphism defined on generators as

\begin{array}[]{cccc}I:&\mathrm{Sym}^{*}&\rightarrow&\mathrm{Sym}\\
&m_{n}^{*}&\mapsto&h_{n}.\end{array}

It can be shown, via Cauchy identity, that
I(p_{n}^{*})=np_{n}
. Let
\iota:\mathrm{Sym}\to\mathrm{QSym}
be the natural embedding. There is a forgetful projection
\pi:\mathrm{NSym}\to\mathrm{Sym}
defined on generators as
H_{n}\mapsto h_{n}
. The map
\pi
is a graded Hopf morphism and is also the map composition
I^{-1}\circ\iota^{*}
where
\iota^{*}:\mathrm{NSym}\to\mathrm{Sym}^{*}
is the adjoint map of
\iota
i.e.
\pi=I\circ\iota^{*}=(\iota\circ I)^{*}
.

2.2
Combinatorial Hopf algebras

A combinatorial Hopf algebra is a graded connected Hopf algebra
\mathsf{H}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathsf{H}_{n}
with a linear and multiplicative map
\zeta:\mathsf{H}\to\mathbb{C}
. The map
\zeta
is called the character. The set of characters
\rm{Hom}(\mathsf{H},\mathbb{C})
forms a group under convolution product

\zeta\zeta^{\prime}=m\circ(\zeta\otimes\zeta^{\prime})\circ\Delta.

The identity element of the group
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathsf{H},\mathbb{C})
is the counit
\epsilon_{\mathsf{H}}
and inverse element is given by
\zeta^{-1}=\zeta\circ\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{H}}
where
\mathcal{S}_{\mathsf{H}}
is the antipode of
\mathsf{H}
. We define
\bar{\zeta}
to be the character such that
\bar{\zeta}|_{\mathsf{H}_{n}}=(-1)^{n}\zeta|_{\mathsf{H}_{n}}
.

A
morphism of combinatorial Hopf algebras

\Phi:(\mathsf{H},\zeta_{\mathsf{H}})\rightarrow(\mathsf{H}^{{}^{\prime}},\zeta_{\mathsf{H}^{{}^{\prime}}})
, is a graded Hopf morphism
\Phi:\mathsf{H}\to\mathsf{H}^{\prime}
such that
\zeta_{\mathsf{H}^{{}^{\prime}}}\circ\Phi=\zeta_{\mathsf{H}}
. The character theory of Hopf algebras has been studied by Aguiar, Bergeron, and Sottile in
[
1
]
.

Let
\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}
be the character of
\mathrm{QSym}
given by

\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}(f(x_{1},x_{2},\dots))=f(1,0,0,0,\dots).

Example 2.1
.

We have
\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}(p_{n})=\frac{1}{n}
and
\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}(M_{\alpha})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\text{if }\ell(\alpha)=0\text{ or }1\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

And let
\zeta_{\mathrm{Sym}}
be the restricted character
\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}|_{\mathrm{Sym}}
. The characters
\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}
and
\zeta_{\mathrm{Sym}}
are canonical characters in the following sense.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
1
, Theorem 4.1]

For any combinatorial Hopf algebra
(\mathsf{H},\zeta)
, there exists a unique morphism of combinatorial Hopf algebras

\Phi:(\mathsf{H},\zeta)\rightarrow(\mathrm{QSym},\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}).

Moreover, if
\mathsf{H}
is cocommutative, then
\Phi(\mathsf{H})\subseteq\mathrm{Sym}
.

The
odd and even Hopf subalgebras
of a combinatorial Hopf algebra
(\mathsf{H},\zeta_{\mathsf{H}})
, denoted by
\mathsf{S}_{-}(\mathsf{H},\zeta_{\mathsf{H}})
and
\mathsf{S}_{+}(\mathsf{H},\zeta_{\mathsf{H}})
respectively, are defined as the largest sub-coalgebra contained in
\ker\left(\overline{\zeta_{\mathsf{H}}^{-1}}-\zeta_{\mathsf{H}}\right)
and
\ker\left(\overline{\zeta_{\mathsf{H}}}-\zeta_{\mathsf{H}}\right)
respectively.

We will be using the following important properties of the odd and even Hopf subalgebras.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
1
, Theorem 5.3]

The coalgebras
\mathsf{S}_{-}(\mathsf{H},\zeta_{\mathsf{H}})
and
\mathsf{S}_{+}(\mathsf{H},\zeta_{\mathsf{H}})
are graded Hopf sub-algebras of
\mathsf{H}
. Moreover, a homogeneous element
h\in\mathsf{H}
belongs to
\mathsf{S}_{-}(\mathsf{H},\zeta_{\mathsf{H}})
(or
\mathsf{S}_{+}(\mathsf{H},\zeta_{\mathsf{H}})
) if and only if
(\rm{id}\otimes\left(\overline{\zeta_{\mathsf{H}}^{-1}}-\zeta_{\mathsf{H}}\right)\otimes\rm{id})\circ\Delta^{(2)}(h)=0
(or
(\rm{id}\otimes\left(\overline{\zeta_{\mathsf{H}}}-\zeta_{\mathsf{H}}\right)\otimes\rm{id})\circ\Delta^{(2)}(h)=0
respectively).

The shuffle basis of
\mathrm{QSym}

In this section, we define a new basis of

Definition 3.1
.

For a composition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell})
,

•

its odd-to-even set,
\mathrm{OtE}(\alpha)
is defined to be
\{i:\alpha_{i}\text{ is odd},\alpha_{i+1}\text{ is even}\}

•

\mathrm{EtO}(\alpha)

\{i:\alpha_{i}\text{ is even},\alpha_{i+1}\text{ is odd}\}

•

\mathrm{OtE}(\alpha)=\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}\}

•

given a composition

\beta_{i}
is a sum of consecutive parts of

Definition 3.3
.

The shuffle function

, indexed by a composition

S_{\alpha}=\sum_{m_{o}(\alpha)\leq\beta\leq\alpha}c_{\alpha}^{\beta}M_{\beta}.

Proposition 3.4
.

The set

Proof.

It is clear from definition that
c_{\alpha}^{\alpha}=1

\alpha

∎

Theorem 3.5
.

The product of shuffle basis is given by shuffle of compositions i.e.

S_{\alpha}\cdot S_{\beta}=\sum_{\gamma\in\alpha\shuffle\beta}S_{\gamma}.

Proof.

For each composition
\gamma\in\alpha\shuffle\beta
, and
m_{o}(\gamma)\leq\delta\leq\gamma
, we construct a pair of compositions
\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta}
and
\epsilon\in\hat{\alpha}\overline{\shuffle}\hat{\beta}
as follows. By definition,
\delta_{i}=\alpha_{p_{i}}+\alpha_{p_{i}+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{p_{i}+q}+\beta_{r_{i}}+\beta_{r_{i}+1}+\cdots+\beta_{r_{i}+t}
for some
p_{i},q,r_{i},t
such that the multi-set
\{\alpha_{p_{i}},\dots,\alpha_{p_{i}+q},\beta_{r_{i}},\beta_{r_{i}+t}\}
is equal to
\{\gamma_{s_{i}},\gamma_{s_{i}+1},\dots,\gamma_{s_{i}+q+t}\}
for some
s_{i}
and
\mathrm{OtE}(\gamma_{s_{i}},\dots,\gamma_{s_{i}+q+t})=\emptyset
.

We set
a_{i}=\alpha_{p_{i}}+\cdots+\alpha_{p_{i}+q}
and
b_{i}=\beta_{r_{i}}+\cdots+\beta_{r_{i}+t}
. Then,
\hat{\alpha}
and
\hat{\beta}
are the compositions obtained from

and
(b_{1},\dots,b_{\ell(\delta)})
respectively by removing zeros. Since
\mathrm{OtE}(\alpha_{p_{i}},\dots,\alpha_{p_{i}+q})=\mathrm{OtE}(\beta_{r_{i}},\dots,\beta_{r_{i}+t})=\emptyset

and
m_{o}(\beta)\leq\hat{\beta}\leq\beta
. Furthermore,

.

This process gives a map

For example, for

,

\gamma={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}12}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}113}

Theorem 3.6
.

The coproduct of shuffle basis is given by deconcatenation of composition i.e.

\Delta(S_{\alpha})=\sum_{\beta\gamma=\alpha}S_{\beta}\otimes S_{\gamma}.

Proof.

Fix a composition
\alpha\models n

For simplicity, we use

Fix an

Theorem 3.8
.

The map

\Theta_{\mathrm{QSym}}(S_{\alpha})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}2^{\ell(\alpha)}S_{\alpha}&\text{if }\alpha\text{ is odd}\\

It is easy to check that

is a Hopf algebra morphism.
Since

is the unique combinatorial Hopf morphism from

\overline{\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}^{-1}}\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}=\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}\circ\Phi

Recall that

It follows from the definition of shuffle functions that,

\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}(S_{\alpha})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\cfrac{1}{p!(\ell(\alpha)-p)!}&\text{if }\mathrm{EtO}(\alpha)=\{p\}\text{ and }\mathrm{OtE}(\alpha)=\emptyset\\
\cfrac{1}{\ell(\alpha)!}&\text{if }\mathrm{EtO}(\alpha)=\mathrm{OtE}(\alpha)=\emptyset\\

(3.1)

\overline{\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}^{-1}}(S_{\alpha})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(-1)^{|\alpha|+\ell(\alpha)}\cfrac{1}{q!(\ell(\alpha)-q)!}&\text{if }\mathrm{EtO}(\alpha)=\emptyset\text{ and }\mathrm{OtE}(\alpha)=\{q\}\\
(-1)^{|\alpha|+\ell(\alpha)}\cfrac{1}{\ell(\alpha)!}&\text{if }\mathrm{EtO}(\alpha)=\mathrm{OtE}(\alpha)=\emptyset\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right..

(3.2)

To sum up,

\displaystyle\overline{\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}^{-1}}\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}(S_{\alpha})=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell(\alpha)}\overline{\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}^{-1}}(S_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{i})})\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}(S_{(\alpha_{i+1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell(\alpha)})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle\sum_{i=q}^{p}(-1)^{\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{i}+i}\frac{1}{q!(i-q)!}\frac{1}{(p-i)!(\ell(\alpha)-p)!}&\text{if }\mathrm{EtO}(\alpha)=\{p\},\mathrm{OtE}(\alpha)=\{q\}\text{ and }q<p\\

\displaystyle\sum_{i=q}^{\ell(\alpha)}(-1)^{\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{i}+i}\frac{1}{q!(i-q)!}\frac{1}{(\ell(\alpha)-i)!}&\text{if }\mathrm{EtO}(\alpha)=\emptyset\text{ and }\mathrm{OtE}(\alpha)=\{q\}\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\ell(\alpha)}(-1)^{\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{i}+i}\frac{1}{i!}\frac{1}{(\ell(\alpha)-i)!}&\text{if }\mathrm{EtO}(\alpha)=\mathrm{OtE}(\alpha)=\emptyset\\

The same argument can be applied to all other cases except the sub-case of 4 that

4
The shuffle algebras, tensor algebras and symmetric algebras

In this section, we revisit shuffle algebras, tensor algebras and symmetric algebras and construct their theta maps.

4.1
The shuffle algebra

Let
V
be a vector space with a countable basis and

V^{\otimes n}=\underbrace{V\otimes V\otimes\dots\otimes V}_{\text{$n$ times}}.

As vector space, the shuffle algebra on
V
is

\mathrm{S}(V)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}V^{\otimes n}.

We fix a basis
A=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots\}
of
V
throughout this paper. For convenience, we write
v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}
as
v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{n}
.
Then a basis for
\mathrm{S}(V)
contains all elements of the form
v_{\alpha_{1}}v_{\alpha_{2}}\cdots v_{\alpha_{\ell}}
. For a composition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell})
, we write
v_{\alpha_{1}}v_{\alpha_{2}}\cdots v_{\alpha_{\ell}}
as
v_{\alpha}
. Essentially, the elements of
\mathrm{S}(V)
are linear combinations of the words on the alphabet
A
. Note that
V^{\otimes 0}
is generated by the empty word.

We now present the Hopf structure of
\mathrm{S}(V)
on the basis elements, and extended linearly.

The product of
\mathrm{S}(V)
is given by the shuffle of the words, that is,

v_{\alpha}\cdot v_{\beta}=\sum_{\gamma\in\alpha\shuffle\beta}v_{\gamma}.

The coproduct is given by deconcatenation of words, that is,

\Delta(v_{\alpha})=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell(\alpha)}v_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{i})}\otimes v_{(\alpha_{i+1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell(\alpha)})}.

It is well-known, e.g. in
[
11
]
, that the antipode formula for
S(V)
is given by

\mathcal{S}(v_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell}(\alpha))})=(-1)^{\ell(\alpha)}v_{(\alpha_{\ell(\alpha)},\dots,\alpha_{1})}.

When
V
is equipped with a grading such that the basis elements
v_{i}
are homogeneous, the degree of
v_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}
in
V^{\otimes n}
is

{\rm deg}(v_{\alpha_{1}})+{\rm deg}(v_{\alpha_{2}})+\dots+{\rm deg}(v_{\alpha_{n}}).

With such a grading,
\mathrm{S}(V)
becomes a graded connected Hopf algebra. In the rest of this paper, we always assume that
V
has such a grading and moreover, the set
\{\alpha:\deg(v_{\alpha})=n\}
is finite for all
n
.

Consider the morphism of graded Hopf algebras
\Phi_{\mathrm{S}(V)}:\mathrm{S}(V)\to\mathrm{QSym}
, that makes
\mathrm{S}(V)
a combinatorial Hopf algebra, defined as

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Phi_{\mathrm{S}(V)}:&\mathrm{S}(V)&\rightarrow&\mathrm{QSym}\\
&v_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell})}&\mapsto&S_{(\deg(v_{\alpha_{1}}),\dots,\deg(v_{\alpha_{\ell}}))}.\end{array}

The fact that
\Phi_{\mathrm{S}(V)}
is a graded Hopf morphism follows directly from the Hopf structure of the shuffle basis in Theorem
3.5
and
3.6
.

The
canonical
character of
\mathrm{S}(V)
, denoted by
\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}
is defined to be the map composition

\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}=\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}\circ\Phi_{\mathrm{S}(V)}.

More explicitly, let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell})
and
(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell})=(\deg(v_{\alpha_{1}}),\dots,\deg(v_{\alpha_{\ell}}))
, then

\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}(v_{\alpha})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\cfrac{1}{p!(\ell-p)!}&\text{if }\mathrm{EtO}(\beta)=\{p\}\text{ and }\mathrm{OtE}(\beta)=\emptyset\\
\cfrac{1}{\ell!}&\text{if }\mathrm{EtO}(\beta)=\mathrm{OtE}(\beta)=\emptyset\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right..

In particular,
\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}(v_{i})=1
for all
i
.

4.2
The tensor algebra

Fix a graded vector space
V
with a homogenous basis
\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots\}
. As a vector space, the tensor algebra
\mathrm{T}(V)
is the same as
\mathrm{S}(V)
. The Hopf structure of
\mathrm{T}(V)
can be expressed, on basis elements and extended linearly, as follows.

The product is given by concatenation of compositions, that is,

v_{\alpha}\cdot v_{\beta}=v_{\alpha\beta}.

The coproduct is given by deshuffle of composition, that is,

\Delta(v_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell})})=\sum_{J\subseteq[\ell]}v_{\alpha_{J}}\otimes v_{\alpha_{[\ell]\setminus J}}

where
[\ell]=\{1,2,\dots,\ell\}
, and if
J=\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}\}
with
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}
, then
\alpha_{J}=(\alpha_{i_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{i_{k}})
.

Equivalently,
v_{i}
’s are primitive elements that freely generate
\mathrm{T}(V)
.

The antipode formula is given by

\mathcal{S}(v_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell}(\alpha))})=(-1)^{\ell(\alpha)}v_{(\alpha_{\ell(\alpha)},\dots,\alpha_{1})}.

The tensor algebra and symmetric algebra are graded dual of each other under the Hopf pairing
\langle-,-\rangle:\mathrm{S}(V)\times\mathrm{T}(V)\to\mathbb{C}
given by
\langle v_{\alpha},v_{\beta}\rangle=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
where
\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
is the Kronecker delta.

There is a natural morphism of graded Hopf algebras
\Phi_{\mathrm{T}(V)}:\mathrm{T}(V)\to\mathrm{Sym}
, that makes
\mathrm{T}(V)
a combinatorial Hopf algebra, defined, on generators and extended linearly and multiplicatively, as

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Phi_{\mathrm{T}(V)}:&\mathrm{T}(V)&\rightarrow&\mathrm{Sym}\\
&v_{i}&\mapsto&p_{\deg(v_{i})}.\end{array}

where
p
is the scaled power sum symmetric function. The fact that
\Phi_{\mathrm{T}(V)}
is a graded Hopf morphism follows from the fact that the scaled power sum functions
p_{n}
are both multiplicative and primitive.

The
canonical
character of
\mathrm{T}(V)
, denoted by
\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}
is defined to be the map composition

\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}=\zeta_{\mathrm{Sym}}\circ\Phi_{\mathrm{T}(V)}.

Equivalently,
\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}
can be defined, on generators and extended linearly and multiplicatively, as

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}:&\mathrm{T}(V)&\rightarrow&\mathbb{C}\\
&v_{i}&\mapsto&\cfrac{1}{\deg(v_{i})}.\end{array}

4.3
The symmetric algebra

Let
{\rm I}(V)
be the commutator ideal of
\mathrm{T}(V)
i.e.
{\rm I}(V)=\langle v_{i}v_{j}-v_{j}v_{i}\rangle
for all pairs of basis elements. Then, the symmetric algebra on
V
is defined as

\mathrm{Sym}(V)=\frac{\mathrm{T}(V)}{{\rm I}(V)}=\mathbb{C}\text{-span}\{\overline{v_{\alpha}}=v_{\alpha}+{\rm I}(V)\}.

We will use
\overline{v_{\alpha}}
to denote the equivalence class of
v
throughout this paper.

The quotient space
\mathrm{Sym}(V)
is a commutative and cocommutative Hopf algebra. Its antipode
\mathcal{S}
maps each
\overline{v_{\alpha}}
to
(-1)^{\ell(\alpha)}\overline{v_{\alpha}}
. Since the ideal
{\rm I}(V)
is homogeneous,
\mathrm{Sym}(V)
inherits a grading from
\mathrm{T}(V)
given by
\deg(\overline{v_{\alpha}})=\deg(v_{\alpha})
.

It is easy to see that
{\rm I}(V)\subseteq\ker\Phi_{\mathrm{T}(V)}
. We have the induced Hopf morphism defined, on generators and extended linearly and multiplicatively, as

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Phi_{\mathrm{Sym}(V)}:&\mathrm{Sym}(V)&\rightarrow&\mathrm{Sym}\\
&\overline{v_{i}}&\mapsto&p_{\deg(v_{i})}.\end{array}

The
canonical
character of
\mathrm{Sym}(V)
, denoted by
\zeta_{\mathrm{Sym}(V)}
is defined to be the induced character

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\zeta_{\mathrm{Sym}(V)}:&\mathrm{Sym}(V)&\rightarrow&\mathbb{C}\\
&\overline{v_{i}}&\mapsto&\cfrac{1}{\deg(v_{i})}.\end{array}

For a composition
\alpha
, let
m_{i}
be the number of parts in
\alpha
that are equal to
i
i.e.
m_{i}=|\{k:\alpha_{k}=i\}|
, and let
z_{\alpha}=m_{1}!1^{m_{1}}m_{2}!2^{m_{2}}\cdots
.

Theorem 4.1
.

The symmetric algebra
\mathrm{Sym}(V)
is self-dual under the Hopf pairing

\left\langle\cfrac{\deg(v_{\alpha})!}{\sqrt{z_{\alpha}}}\overline{v_{\alpha}},\cfrac{\deg(v_{\beta})!}{\sqrt{z_{\beta}}}\overline{v_{\beta}}\right\rangle=\delta_{{\rm sort}(\alpha),{\rm sort}(\beta)}

where
\deg(v_{\alpha})!=\deg(\alpha_{1})!\deg(\alpha_{2})!\cdots\deg(\alpha_{\ell(\alpha)})!
,
\deg(v_{\beta})!=\deg(\beta_{1})!\deg(\beta_{2})!\cdots\deg(\beta_{\ell(\beta)})!
and sort
(\alpha)
is the composition obtained by rearranging parts of
\alpha
in decreasing order.

It is not hard to see that the Hopf pairing is well-defined i.e. does not depend on the choice of representative. The theorem above follows from the isomorphism between (ungraded) Hopf algebras

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathrm{Sym}(V)&\to&\mathrm{Sym}\\
\overline{v_{\alpha}}&\mapsto&p_{\alpha}\end{array}

and the well-known Hopf pairing on scaled power sum basis

\left\langle\cfrac{\prod_{i}\alpha_{i}}{\sqrt{z_{\alpha}}}p_{\alpha},\cfrac{\prod_{i}\beta_{i}}{\sqrt{z_{\beta}}}p_{\beta}\right\rangle=\delta_{{\rm sort}(\alpha),{\rm sort}(\beta)}.

4.4
The Hopf subalgebras of odd and even elements

We call an element

v_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}
of
V^{\otimes n}

odd
if the degree of each
v_{\alpha_{i}}
is odd, and
even
if the degree of each
v_{\alpha_{i}}
is even.

Let the subspace of odd elements of
V
be

\mathrm{O}(V)=\mathbb{C}\text{-span}\{v_{i}:\deg(v_{i})\text{~is odd}\},

and let the subspace of even elements be

\mathrm{E}(V)=\mathbb{C}\text{-span}\{v_{i}:\deg(v_{i})\text{~is even}\}.

Proposition 4.2
.

(1) The shuffle algebras

\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{O}(V))=\mathbb{C}\text{\rm-span}\{v_{\alpha}:v_{\alpha}\text{~is odd}\}

and

\mathrm{S}({\rm E}(V))=\mathbb{C}\text{\rm-span}\{v_{\alpha}:v_{\alpha}\text{~is even}\}

are Hopf subalgebras of
\mathrm{S}(V)
.

(2) The tensor algebras

\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{O}(V))=\mathbb{C}\text{\rm-span}\{v_{\alpha}:v_{\alpha}\text{~is odd}\}

and

\mathrm{T}({\rm E}(V))=\mathbb{C}\text{\rm-span}\{v_{\alpha}:v_{\alpha}\text{~is even}\}

are Hopf subalgebras of
\mathrm{T}(V)
.

(3) The symmetric algebras

\mathrm{Sym}(\mathrm{O}(V))=\mathbb{C}\text{\rm-span}\{\overline{v_{\alpha}}:v_{\alpha}\text{~is odd}\}

and

\mathrm{Sym}({\rm E}(V))=\mathbb{C}\text{\rm-span}\{\overline{v_{\alpha}}:v_{\alpha}\text{~is even}\}

are Hopf subalgebras of
\mathrm{Sym}(V)
.

Proof.

All these subspaces are closed under multiplication and comultiplication operations.
∎

We call
\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{O}(V))
,
\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{O}(V))
and
\mathrm{Sym}(\mathrm{O}(V))
the
Hopf subalgebras of odd elements
of
\mathrm{S}(V)
,
\mathrm{T}(V)
and
\mathrm{Sym}(V))
respectively. We call
\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{E}(V))
,
\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{E}(V))
and
\mathrm{Sym}(\mathrm{E}(V))
the
Hopf subalgebras of even elements
of
\mathrm{S}(V)
,
\mathrm{T}(V)
and
\mathrm{Sym}(V))
respectively. In this paper, we mostly study the Hopf subalgebra of odd elements since later we show that they are the images of cetain theta maps.

Corollary 4.3
.

The Hopf algebras
\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{O}(V))
and
\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{O}(V))
are graded dual of each other with Hopf pairing
\langle v_{\alpha},v_{\beta}\rangle=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
where
\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
is the Kronecker delta. Additionally,
\mathrm{Sym}(\mathrm{O}(V))
is a self-dual Hopf algebra under the Hopf pairing
\left\langle\cfrac{\deg(v_{\alpha})!}{\sqrt{z_{\alpha}}}\overline{v_{\alpha}},\cfrac{\deg(v_{\beta})!}{\sqrt{z_{\beta}}}\overline{v_{\beta}}\right\rangle=\delta_{{\rm sort}(\alpha),{\rm sort}(\beta)}.

Proof.

Both
\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{O}(V))
and
\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{O}(V))
are Hopf subalgebras, their basis have the same index set and the Hopf pairing follows from the duality between
\mathrm{S}(V)
and
\mathrm{T}(V)
. Similarly, the self-duality and Hopf pairing of
\mathrm{Sym}(O(V))
are inherited from
\mathrm{Sym}(V)
, as in Theorem
4.1
.
∎

We then present the connection between Hopf subalgebras of odd and even elements with the odd and even Hopf subalgebras.

Proposition 4.4
.

We have the following inclusions

1.

\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{O}(V))\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{-}(\mathrm{S}(V),\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)})
,
\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{E}(V))\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{+}(\mathrm{S}(V),\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)})
,

2.

\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{O}(V))\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{-}(\mathrm{T}(V),\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)})
,
\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{E}(V))\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{+}(\mathrm{T}(V),\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)})
,

3.

\mathrm{Sym}(\mathrm{O}(V))\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{-}(\mathrm{Sym}(V),\zeta_{\mathrm{Sym}(V)})
,
\mathrm{Sym}(\mathrm{E}(V))\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{+}(\mathrm{Sym}(V),\zeta_{\mathrm{Sym}(V)})
.

Proof.

Recall from Theorem
2.3
that
v_{\alpha}\in\mathsf{S}_{-}(\mathrm{S}(V),\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)})
if and only if

({\rm id}\otimes\overline{\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}^{-1}}-\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}\otimes{\rm id})\circ\Delta^{(2)}(v_{\alpha})=0.

Proof of 1. Fix a word
v_{\alpha}=v_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots v_{\alpha_{\ell}}
such that
\deg(v_{\alpha_{i}})=\beta_{i}
is odd for all
i
, let
\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell})
. Since the coproduct in
\mathrm{S}(V)
is given by deconcatenation, it suffices to show that
\left(\overline{\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}^{-1}}-\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}\right)(v_{\alpha})=0
.

By definition,
\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}=\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}\circ\Phi_{\mathrm{S}(V)}
and
\Phi_{\mathrm{S}(V)}(v_{\alpha})=S_{\beta}
, we have

\left(\overline{\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}^{-1}}-\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}\right)(v_{\alpha})=\overline{\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}^{-1}}(S_{\beta})-\zeta_{\mathrm{QSym}}(S_{\beta})=\frac{(-1)^{\deg(v_{\alpha})+\ell}}{\ell!}-\frac{1}{\ell!}=0

where the second to last equality follows from equation
3.1
and
3.2
. The even part can be proved using similar arguments.

Proof of 3. Since
\mathrm{T}(V)
is freely generated by
\{v_{i}\}
, and by Theorem
2.3
,
S_{-}(\mathrm{T}(V),\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)})
is an algebra, it suffices to show that
v_{i}\in S_{-}(\mathrm{T}(V),\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)})
for all
v_{i}
with odd degree. Since
v_{i}
is primitive in
\mathrm{T}(V)
, we have
S(v_{i})=-v_{i}
and hence
\overline{\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}^{-1}}(v_{i})=\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}(v_{i})
. Therefore,
\left(\overline{\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}^{-1}}-\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}\right)(v_{i})=0
and
v_{i}\in S_{-}(\mathrm{T}(V),\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)})
follows from

({\rm id}\otimes\overline{\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}^{-1}}-\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}\otimes{\rm id})\circ\Delta^{(2)}(v_{i})=0.

The even part can be proved using similar arguments. Since
\mathrm{Sym}(V)
is generated by
\overline{v_{i}}
with odd degree, the last inclusions can be proved using a similar arguments as well.
∎

4.5
The theta maps

Consider the linear maps defined on basis elements and extended linearly

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}:&\mathrm{S}(V)&\rightarrow&\mathrm{S}(V),\\
&v_{\alpha}&\mapsto&\begin{cases}2^{\ell(\alpha)}v_{\alpha}&\text{if $\alpha$ is odd}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Theta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}:&\mathrm{T}(V)&\rightarrow&\mathrm{T}(V),\\
&v_{\alpha}&\mapsto&\begin{cases}2^{\ell(\alpha)}v_{\alpha}&\text{if $\alpha$ is odd}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Theta_{\mathrm{Sym}(V)}:&\mathrm{Sym}(V)&\rightarrow&\mathrm{Sym}(V),\\
&\overline{v_{\alpha}}&\mapsto&\begin{cases}2^{\ell(\alpha)}\overline{v_{\alpha}}&\text{if $\alpha$ is odd}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\end{array}

It is easy to see that the images of
\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}
,
\Theta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}
and
\Theta_{\mathrm{Sym}(V)}
are
\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{O}(V))
,
\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{O}(V))
and
\mathrm{Sym}(\mathrm{O}(V))
respectively.

Next, we show that
\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}
,
\Theta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}
and
\Theta_{\mathrm{Sym}(V)}
are theta maps for
(\mathrm{S}(V),\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)})
,
(\mathrm{S}(V),\zeta_{\mathrm{T}(V)})
and
(\mathrm{S}(V),\zeta_{\mathrm{Sym}(V)})
respectively. As a consequence,
\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{O}(V))
,
\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{O}(V))
and
\mathrm{Sym}(\mathrm{O}(V))
are peak algebras of
\mathrm{S}(V)
,
\mathrm{T}(V)
and
\mathrm{Sym}(V)
respectively.

Theorem 4.5
.

The map
\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}
is a graded Hopf morphism and moreover, the following diagram commutes.

\mathrm{S}(V)
\mathrm{QSym}
\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{O}(V))
\Pi
\Phi_{\mathrm{S}(V)}
\Phi_{\mathrm{S}(V)}
\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}
\Theta_{\mathrm{QSym}}

So the map
\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}
is a theta map for
(\mathrm{S}(V),\zeta_{\mathrm{S}(V)})
.

Proof.

The map
\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}
is an algebra morphism since for odd compositions
\alpha
and
\beta
,

\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}(v_{\alpha})\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}(v_{\beta})=2^{\ell(\alpha)}v_{\alpha}\cdot 2^{\ell(\beta)}v_{\beta}=2^{\ell(\alpha)+\ell(\beta)}\sum_{\gamma\in\alpha\shuffle\beta}v_{\gamma}=\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}(v_{\alpha}\cdot v_{\beta})

and both sides vanish if at least one of
\alpha
and
\beta
is not odd.

The map
\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}
is a coalgebra morphism since for odd composition
\alpha
,

\Delta(\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}(v_{\alpha}))=\Delta\left(2^{\ell(\alpha)}v_{\alpha}\right)=2^{\ell(\alpha)}\sum_{\beta\gamma=\alpha}v_{\beta}\otimes v_{\gamma}=\left(\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}\otimes\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}\right)\circ\Delta(v_{\alpha})

and both sides vanish if
\alpha
is not odd.

By Theorem
3.8
, we have

\Theta_{\mathrm{QSym}}\circ\Phi_{\mathrm{S}(V)}(v_{\alpha})=\Theta_{\mathrm{QSym}}(S_{\alpha})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}2^{\ell(\alpha)}S_{\alpha}&\text{if }\alpha\text{ is odd}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

On the other hand,

\Phi_{\mathrm{S}(V)}\circ\Theta_{\mathrm{S}(V)}(v_{\alpha})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi_{\mathrm{S}(V)}\left(2^{\ell(\alpha)}v_{\alpha}\right)&\text{if }\alpha\text{ is odd}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}2^{\ell(\alpha)}S_{\alpha}&\text{if }\alpha\text{ is odd}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Hence, the diagram commutes.
∎

\Theta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}

The map

\alpha

,

\Theta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}(v_{\alpha})\Theta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}(v_{\beta})=2^{\ell(\alpha)}v_{\alpha}\cdot 2^{\ell(\beta)}v_{\beta}=2^{\ell(\alpha)+\ell(\beta)}v_{\alpha\beta}=\Theta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}(v_{\alpha}\cdot v_{\beta})

and both sides vanish if at least one of
\alpha
and
\beta
is not odd.

The map
\Theta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}
is a coalgebra morphism since for odd composition
\alpha

\Delta(\Theta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}(v_{\alpha}))=\Delta\left(2^{\ell(\alpha)}v_{\alpha}\right)=2^{\ell(\alpha)}\sum_{J\subseteq[\ell(\alpha)]}v_{\alpha_{J}}\otimes v_{\alpha_{[\ell(\alpha)]\setminus J}}=\left(\Theta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}\otimes\Theta_{\mathrm{T}(V)}\right)\circ\Delta(v_{\alpha})

5.2
The Hopf algebra
\mathcal{V}

As is shown in
[
3
]
, the associated graded Hopf algebra for any graded cofree coalgebra is a shuffle algebra. In this subsection we present a co-commutative Hopf structure,
\mathcal{V}
, on permutations that is the associated graded Hopf algebra to the Malvenuto–Reutenauer Hopf algebra. This Hopf algebra is isomorphic to Grossman-Larson Hopf algebra
[
14
]
and also appears in
[
19
,
30
]

Fix a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, its global descent set
\rm{GD}(\sigma)
is defined to be
\{i:\sigma(a)>\sigma(b)\text{ for all }a\leq i<b\}
. For example,
\rm{GD}(5763421)=\{3,5,6\}
. The shifted concatenation of two permutations
\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}
and
\tau_{1}\cdots\tau_{m}
is defined as
\sigma\odot\tau=(\sigma_{1}+m)\cdots(\sigma_{n}+m)\tau_{1}\cdots\tau_{m}
. For example,
132\odot 3421=5763421
. For each permutation
\sigma
, there is a unique decomposition

\sigma=\sigma^{1}\odot\sigma^{2}\odot\cdots\odot\sigma^{\ell}

such that
\sigma^{i}
are permutations and
\rm{GD}(\sigma^{i})=\emptyset
. For example,
5763421=132\odot 12\odot 1\odot 1
. In this subsection, we view a permutation as a word whose letters are permutations without global descent. The degree of a permutation
\sigma
, denoted by
\deg(\sigma)
, is
n
if
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

As a graded vector space,
\mathcal{V}=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{V}_{n}
where
\mathcal{V}_{n}=\mathbb{C}
-span
\{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\}
. For convenience,
\mathfrak{S}_{0}
contains the unique empty permutation
\emptyset
. The multiplication is given by shuffle of permutations

\sigma\cdot\tau=\sum_{\eta\in\sigma\shuffle\tau}\eta,

and the comultiplication is given by deconcatenation

\Delta(\sigma)=\sum_{\tau\odot\eta=\sigma}\tau\otimes\eta.

For example,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}132\odot 12}\cdot{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}12\odot 1}

\displaystyle={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}132}\odot{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}12}\odot{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}12}\odot{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}132}\odot{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}12}\odot{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}12}\odot{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}132}\odot{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}12}\odot{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1}\odot{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}12}

\displaystyle+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}12}\odot{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}132}\odot{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}12}\odot{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}12}\odot{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}132}\odot{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1}\odot{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}12}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}12}\odot{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1}\odot{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}132}\odot{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}12},

\Delta(132\odot 12\odot 1)=\emptyset\otimes 132\odot 12\odot 1+132\otimes 12\odot 1+132\odot 12\otimes 1+132\odot 12\odot 1\otimes\emptyset.

With this Hopf structure,
\mathcal{V}
is a shuffle algebra. The linear map defined on basis elements

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Phi_{\mathcal{V}}:&\mathcal{V}&\rightarrow&\mathrm{QSym}\\
&\sigma^{1}\odot\sigma^{2}\odot\cdots\odot\sigma^{\ell}&\mapsto&S_{(\deg(\sigma^{1}),\dots,\deg(\sigma^{\ell}))}\end{array}

makes
\mathcal{V}
a combinatorial Hopf algebra. Then, Theorem
4.5
describes a theta map for
\mathcal{V}
as follows.

Corollary 5.2
.

The linear map defined on basis elements as

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Theta_{\mathcal{V}}:&\mathcal{V}&\rightarrow&\mathcal{V},\\
&\sigma^{1}\odot\sigma^{2}\odot\cdots\odot\sigma^{\ell}&\mapsto&\begin{cases}2^{\ell}(\sigma^{1}\odot\sigma^{2}\odot\cdots\odot\sigma^{\ell})&\text{if $\deg(\sigma^{i})$ is odd for all $i$}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\end{array}

is a theta map for
\mathcal{V}
.

The peak algebra for
\mathcal{V}
is given by
\Theta_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{V})=\mathbb{C}
-span
\{\sigma^{1}\odot\cdots\odot\sigma^{\ell}:\rm{GD}(\sigma^{i})=\emptyset,\deg(\sigma^{i})\text{ are odd}\}
. Its Hilbert series is given by

\rm{Hilb}(\Theta_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{V}))=1+x+x^{2}+4x^{3}+7x^{4}+81x^{5}+164x^{6}+\cdots.

5.3
The Hopf algebra
\mathrm{NCSym}

The Hopf algebra of symmetric functions in non-commuting variables
{\rm NCSym}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}{\rm NCSym}_{n}
are originally defined in
[
31
]
. For more information about what algebraic structures of
{\rm NCSym}
has been revealed see
[
4

.

A set partition of
[n]
is a set of disjoint non-empty subsets of
[n]
whose union is
[n]
. A set partition of
[n]
is said to be atomic if there exists
i
such that for any
a\leq i<b
,
a
and
b
are not in the same subset. The bases of
{\rm NCSym}_{n}
are indexed by set partitions of
[n]
. It is known that
\mathrm{NCSym}
is free as an algebra, whose generators are indexed by atomic set partitions. Lauve and Mastnak
[
18
]
discovered that there is a set of primitive elements
\{\mathbf{p}_{\pi}:\pi\text{~atomic}\}
that freely generates
{\rm NCSym}
, in particular,
\mathrm{NCSym}
is a tensor algebra.

The degree of
\pi
is
n
if
\pi
is a set partition of
[n]
. As a consequence of Theorem
4.6
, the linear map defined on basis elements as

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Theta_{\mathrm{NCSym}}:&\mathrm{NCSym}&\rightarrow&{\rm NCPeak}\\

0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\end{array}

is a theta map for
\mathrm{NCSym}
.

The peak algebra for
\mathrm{NCSym}
is given by
\Theta_{\mathrm{NCSym}}(\mathrm{NCSym})=\mathbb{C}
-span
\{\mathbf{p}_{\pi_{1}}\cdots\mathbf{p}_{\pi_{\ell}}:\deg(\pi_{i})\text{ are odd}\}
. Its Hilbert series is given by

\rm{Hilb}(\Theta_{\mathrm{NCSym}}(\mathrm{NCSym}))=1+x+x^{2}+3x^{3}+5x^{4}+29x^{5}+57x^{6}+\cdots.

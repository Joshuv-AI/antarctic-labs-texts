Sudakov minoration for products of radial-type log concave measures
Witold Bednorz

Abstract
The first step to study lower bounds for a stochastic process is to prove a special property - Sudakov minoration. The property means that if a certain number of points from the index set are well separated then we can provide an optimal type lower bound for the mean value of the supremum of the process. Together with the generic chaining argument the property can be used to fully characterize the mean value of the supremum of the stochastic process. In this article we prove the property for canonical processes based on radial-type log concave measures.



1
Introduction

Consider a random vector
X
, which takes values in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Let
T
be a subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we can
define canonical process as

(X_{t})_{t\in T},\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;X_{t}=\sum^{d}_{i=1}t_{i}X_{i}=\langle t,X\rangle.

One of the basic questions in the analysis of stochastic processes is to characterize

S_{X}(T)=\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}
, where
(X_{t})_{t\in T}
is a family of
random variables. It is well known that usually
S_{X}(T)<\infty
is equivalent to
\mathbf{P}(\sup_{t\in T}|X_{t}|<\infty)=1
, i.e that paths are a.s. bounded.
In order to avoid measurablility questions formally

S_{X}(T)
should be defined
\sup_{F\subset T}\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in F}X_{t}
, where the supremum is over all finite subsets
F
of
T
.
The main tool invented to study the quantity is the generic chaining. Although, the idea works neatly whenever the upper bound is concerned, the lower bound is a much more subtle matter. What one can use towards establishing the lower bound for
\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}
is the growth condition which can be later used in the partition scheme
[
20
]
or construction of a special family of functionals
[
4
]
.
The core of the approach is the Sudakov minoration. Basically, the minoration means that we can answer the question about
understanding
\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}
in the simplest setting, where points in
T
are well separated. Before we state the condition, let us make a simple remark.

Remark 1

Let
p\geqslant 1
. Suppose that
|T|\leqslant e^{p}
, and
\|X_{t}\|_{p}\leqslant A
for each
t\in T
. Then
\mathbf{E}|\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}|\leqslant eA
.

Proof.

Indeed, we have

\mathbf{E}|\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}|\leqslant\mathbf{E}\left[\sum_{t\in T}|X_{t}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leqslant\left[\mathbf{E}\sum_{t\in T}|X_{t}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leqslant eA.

\blacksquare

The Sudakov minoration means that something opposite happen. Namely, we require that
|T|\geqslant e^{p}
,
p\geqslant 1

and for all
s,t\in T
,
s\neq t
,
\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}\geqslant A
. These assumptions should imply

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}\geqslant K^{-1}A,

(1)

where
K
is an absolute constant. The problem has a long history, which we outline now.

The property was first proved in 1969 for
X
-Gaussian, i.e.
X=G=(g_{i})^{d}_{i=1}
,
g_{i}
are independent standard normal variables.
The result is prescribed to Sudakov
[
16
,
17
]
, although some other researchers could be aware of the fact. In 1986 Pajor and Tomczak-Jaegermann
[
15
]
observed that the property for Guassian
X
can be established in the dual way. Then, Talagrand
[
18
]
proved in 1990, that the minoration works for
X=\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{i})^{d}_{i=1}
,
\varepsilon_{i}
are independent Rademachers. He invented the result, when studying properties of infinitely divisible processes. Later, in 1994 Talagrand
[
19
]
realized that the minoration holds also for exponential-type distributions. The result was improved by Latala
[
8
]
in 1997, so that it holds for
X
which has independent entries of log-concave tails. The problem to go beyond the class of independent entries occurred to be unexpectedly hard. First result towards this direction was due to Latala
[
9
]
, 2014 and concerned
X
of density
\exp(-U(\|x\|_{p}))
, where
U
is an increasing convex function and
\|x\|_{p}=\left(\sum^{d}_{i=1}|x_{i}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}
. Then, in 2015 Latala and Tkocz
[
10
]
proved that the minoration in the case of independent entries necessarily requires that entries are
\alpha
-regular, which basically means that they behaves like variables of log-concave distribution. Finally, it has to be mentioned that some new ideas appeared how a proof of the minoration could be established. For example the dimension reduction - the work of Mendelson, Milman and Paouris
[
13
]
. In this paper there covered some simple cases, though it is known that the program cannot succeed in full generality.

The aim of the paper is to prove that the minoration works for a certain class of log-concave distributions, that extends mentioned examples. More precisely, we show that the property holds for
X
of density

\displaystyle\mu_{X}(dx)=\exp(-\sum^{M}_{k=1}U_{k}(\|x_{k}\|^{p_{k}}_{p_{k}}))dx,

(2)

where
U_{k}
are increasing convex functions,
p_{k}>1
and
x_{k}=(x_{i})_{i\in J_{k}}
,
J_{k}
are pairwise disjoint and covers
[d]=\{1,2,\ldots,d\}
.
We use the notation
n_{k}=|J_{k}|
and
q_{k}
for
p_{k}^{\ast}
, i.e.
1/p_{k}+1/q_{k}=1
.
It will be clear from our proof that we can say something about the minoration also if
p_{k}\geqslant 1
, however
if we want the constant
K
not to depend on the problem setting, we need some cutoff level for
p_{k}
from
1
.

The distribution
\mu_{X}
has some properties that are important for the proof of minoration.
Namely:

1.

isotropic position;

2.

one unconditionality;

3.

log-concavity;

4.

structural condition.

Let us comment below on them:

1) Isotropic position. We say that
X
is in isotropic position, if

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}X=0,\;\;\mbox{i.e.}\mathbf{E}X_{i}=0,\;\;i\in[d],\;\;\mathbf{Cov}X=\mathrm{Id}_{d},\;\;\mbox{i.e.}\;\;\mathbf{Cov}(X_{i}X_{j})=\delta_{i,j}.

(3)

Note that by an affine transformation we can impose the property, keeping the structural assumption (
2
).
Note also that it is a very natural modification which makes the Eucledean distance
important, i.e.
\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{2}=d_{2}(s,t)=\|t-s\|_{2}
.

2) We say that
X
is one-unconditional if

\displaystyle(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}(\varepsilon_{1}|X_{1}|,\varepsilon_{2}|X_{2}|,\ldots,\varepsilon_{d}|X_{d}|),

(4)

where
|X_{i}|
,
i\in[d]
and independent random signs
\varepsilon_{i}
,
i\in[d]
that
are independent of all
|X_{i}|
,
i\in[d]
. Observe that the assumption
makes it possible to consider
(X_{t})_{t\in T}
in the following form

X_{t}=\sum^{d}_{i=1}t_{i}X_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\sum^{d}_{i=1}t_{i}\varepsilon_{i}|X_{i}|.

3) Log-concavity. We assume that
X
has the distribution

\displaystyle\mu(dx)=\exp(-U(x))dx,

(5)

where
U
is a convex function. Due to the result of
[
10
]
the assumption is almost necessary.
More formally, what we need is the comparison of moments. For log-concave and
1
unconditional
X
, we have
that for all
0<p<q
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(see
[
14
]
or
[
2
]
for the proof)

\displaystyle\|X_{t}\|_{q}\leqslant\frac{\Gamma(q+1)^{\frac{1}{q}}}{\Gamma(p+1)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|X_{t}\|_{p}\leqslant\frac{q}{p}\|X_{t}\|_{p}.

(6)

The necessary condition for the Sudakov minoration formulated in
[
10
]
states that
X
must be
\alpha
-regular, which means that there exists
\alpha\geqslant 1
such that, for all
2\leqslant p<q

\displaystyle\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{q}\leqslant\alpha\frac{q}{p}\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}.

(7)

Clearly, for log-concave and
1
unconditional
X
the inequality (
7
) works with
\alpha=1
.

4) Structural assumption. As we have explained in (
2
) we assume that the distribution of
X
has
a certain structure. More precisely, we require that

\displaystyle U(x)=\sum^{M}_{k=1}U_{k}(\|x_{k}\|^{p_{k}}_{p_{k}}),\;\;p_{k}>1.

(8)

Therefore we can treat
X
as
(X_{k})^{M}_{k=1}
, where vectors
X_{k}
with values in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{k}}
are independent.
The fact, which we use later is that each
X_{k}
has the same distribution as
R_{k}V_{K}
, where
R_{k}
and
V_{k}
are independent and

R_{k}
is distributed on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with the density
x^{n-1}\exp(-U_{k}(x^{p_{k}}))|\partial B^{n_{k}}_{p_{k}}|
and
V_{k}
is distributed on
\partial B^{n_{k}}_{p_{k}}

with respect to the (probabilistic) cone measure
\nu_{k}
- for details see e.g.
[
21
]
. The most important property
of the cone measure is that for any integrable
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{k}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{k}}}f(x)dx=|\partial B^{n_{k}}_{p_{k}}|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\int_{\partial B^{n_{k}}_{p_{k}}}f(r\theta)r^{n_{k}-1}\nu_{k}(d\theta)dr.

Note that here
B^{n_{k}}_{p_{k}}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{k}}:\;\|x\|_{p_{k}}\leqslant 1\}
and

\partial B^{n_{k}}_{p_{k}}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{k}}:\;\|x\|_{p_{k}}=1\}
.

2
Results

Following the previous section, we can formulate the main result of this paper

Theorem 1

Suppose that
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a random vector whose density is of the form

\mu_{X}=\mu_{1}\otimes\mu_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mu_{M}
and
\mu_{k}
is a log concave distribution on

{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{k}}
given by

\mu_{k}(dx_{k})=\exp(-U_{k}(\|x_{k}\|^{p_{k}}_{p_{k}}))dx_{k},\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;x_{k}=(x_{i})_{i\in J_{k}},

where
J_{k}
are disjoint sets such that
\bigcup^{M}_{k=1}J_{k}=[d]
and
p_{k}\geqslant 1+\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
.
Then, the Sudakov minoration holds, i.e. for every set
T\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}\geqslant A
,
s\neq t
,
s,t\in T

for some
p\geqslant 1
the following inequality holds

\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}\geqslant K^{-1}A,

where
K
depends on
\varepsilon
only.

The proof is quite complicated that is why it is good to give a sketch of our approach.

1.

Simplifications. We show that Theorem
1
has to be established for
A=p
,
X
in the isotropic position and sets
T
such that for each
t\in T
,
t=(t_{i})^{d}_{i=1}
,
t_{i}\in\{0,k_{i}\}
for some positive
k_{i}
,
i\in[d]
and
\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}\geqslant p
,
s\neq t
,
s,t\in T
. Moreover, for each
t\in T
,
\sum^{d}_{i=1}|t_{i}|
and
\sum^{d}_{i=1}1_{t_{i}=k_{i}}
are much smaller than
p
. In other words, minoration should be proved for cube-like sets with thin supports.

2.

Moments. The next step is a careful analysis of the condition
\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}\geqslant p
. In particular, we are going to use the structure assumption
X=(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{M})
, where entries
X_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{k}}
are independent. The main trick here is to define a random vector
Y=(Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots,Y_{M})
such that
Y
has all coordinates independent, i.e.
not only
Y_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{k}}
are independent but also coordinates of each
Y_{k}
are independent. Due to our structure
assumption it will be possible to represent
X_{k}=R_{k}V_{k}
,
Y_{k}=\tilde{R}_{k}V_{k}
, where
R_{k},\tilde{R}_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

are some
\alpha
regular variables and
V_{k}
are distributed with respect to cone measures on
\partial B^{n_{k}}_{p_{k}}
respectively.

3.

Split. We observe that we can split each point
t\in T
into
t^{\ast}
- small part and
t^{\dagger}
- large part.
More precisely, we decide whether
t^{\ast}_{k}
is
t_{k}
or
0
and respectively
t^{\dagger}_{k}
is
0
or
t_{k}
depending on
how large some norm of
t_{k}
is.
Moreover, we show that either there is a subset
S\subset T
,
|S|\geqslant e^{p/2}
such that

\|X_{t^{\ast}}-X_{s^{\ast}}\|_{p}\gtrsim p
for any
s\neq t

s,t\in S
or we can find

S\subset T
,
|S|\geqslant e^{p/2}
such that
\|X_{t^{\dagger}}-X_{s^{\dagger}}\|_{p}\gtrsim p
for any
s\neq t
,
s,t
in
S
.

4.

Small part. If the ’small part’ case holds then we show that one may forget about variables
R_{k},\tilde{R}_{k}
and
in this way deduce the minoration from such a result for the random vector
Y
.

5.

Large part. If the ’large part’ case holds, then we prove that variables
V_{k}
are not important, more precisely
we prove, following the approach from the ’simplifications’ step, that not only
t_{i}\in\{0,k_{i}\}
but we have such a property for

t^{\dagger}_{k}=(t^{\dagger}_{i})_{i\in J_{k}}
, namely
t^{\dagger}_{k}\in\{x_{k},0\}
for some
x_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{k}}
. Consequently, we
deduce the minoration from such a result for the random vector
(X_{x_{k}})^{M}_{k=1}
.

It is a standard argument that having the minoration one can prove the comparison between

\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}
and
\gamma_{X}(T)
. However, this approach requires some two additional
conditions. First, we need that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\|X_{t}\|_{2^{n+1}}\geqslant(1+\varepsilon)\|X_{t}\|_{2^{n}},\;\;n\geqslant 0.

(9)

The property works if there is a cutoff level for all
p_{k}
below
\infty
.
Moreover, there must hold a certain form of measure concentration.
What suffices is that there exist constants
K,L\geqslant 1

such that for any
p\geqslant 2
and set
T\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}-K\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}\right)_{+}\right\|_{p}\leqslant L\sup_{s,t\in T}\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}.

(10)

The result is known only in few cases. Fortunately, in our setting it can be derived from the infimum convolution
[
11
]
. Note that the problem can be easily reduced to the one where
T
is a gauge of some norm in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Moreover,
the main concentration inequality -
CI(\beta)
from
[
11
]
holds for radial log-concave densities and it can be tensorized, namely if
CI(\beta)
holds for measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{M}
then
CI(\beta^{\prime})
holds for

\mu_{1}\otimes\mu_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mu_{M}
with some dependence between
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
, i.e.
\beta^{\prime}
is some multiplication of
\beta
.

Theorem 2

Under the assumption of Theorem
1
and assuming additionally that
p_{k}\leqslant p_{\infty}<\infty

then

\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}\simeq\gamma_{X}(T).

The result is quite standard and goes through the idea of growth condition -
[
20
]
.

Before we start the proof of Theorem (
1
) we need a preliminary result which explains that the Sudakov minoration has to be established only for sets
T
that have cube-like structure.

3
Cube-like sets

First step concerns some basic simplifications of the problem. We first note that the minoration has to be proved only for certain cube-like sets
T
.
We use symbols
\lesssim,\gtrsim,\simeq
whenever we compare quantities up to a numerical constant comparable to
1
.

We have to start from the Bobkov Nazarov
[
3
]
inequality or rather from its
basic consequence:

\displaystyle\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}\lesssim\|{\cal E}_{t}-{\cal E}_{s}\|_{p},

(11)

where
({\cal E}_{i})^{d}_{i=1}
are independent symmetric standard exponential variables.
The Bobkov Nazarov inequality concerns one-unconditional and log concave distributions in the isotropic position.
However, the inequality (
11
) may hold in a bit more general setting. For example, it is also true
if entries of an isotropic
X
are independent and
\alpha
-regular.
That is why we simply refer to (
11
)
as the basic requirement. In the proof of the simplification, the condition is used together with the Kwapien-Gluskin result
[
6
]

\displaystyle\|{\cal E}_{t}\|_{p}\sim p\|t\|_{\infty}+\sqrt{p}\|t\|_{2}.

(12)

On the other hand, by the result of Hitczenko
[
5
]
, we have also the lower bound

\displaystyle\|X_{t}\|_{p}\gtrsim\|\varepsilon_{t}\|_{p}\sim\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant p}|t^{\ast}_{i}|+\sqrt{p}\left(\sum^{d}_{i>p}|t_{i}^{\ast}|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(13)

where
(t^{\ast}_{i})^{d}_{i=1}
is the rearrangement of
t
such that
|t^{\ast}_{1}|\geqslant|t^{\ast}_{2}|\geqslant\ldots\geqslant|t^{\ast}_{d}|
.
The last tool we need is that Sudakov minoration works for the random vector
(\varepsilon_{i})^{d}_{i=1}
. As we have already mentioned the result was first proved in
[
18
]
. We are ready to formulate the main simplification result

Proposition 1

Suppose that
X
is in the isotropic position and
Fix
p\geqslant 2
. Suppose that:

1.

X
is one unconditional;

2.

X
is log concave;

3.

X
satisfies (
11
).

Then to show the Sudakov minoration it suffices to prove
for all sets
T
such that:

1.

\exp(p)\leqslant|T|\leqslant 1+\exp(p)
and
0\in T
;

2.

for each
i\in[d]=\{1,2,\ldots,d\}

t_{i}\in\{0,k_{i}\},\;\;\mbox{where}\;k_{i}\geqslant\rho,

where
\rho\leqslant e^{-1}
and
\rho/\log\frac{1}{\rho}=4C\delta
;

3.

for each
t\in T

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I(t)}k_{i}\leqslant 2C\delta p,\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;I(t)=\{i\in[d]:\;t_{i}=k_{i}\};

(14)

4.

for all

,

\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}\geqslant p,

where

is suitably small and

that the following inequality holds

\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}\geqslant K^{-1}p,

where

is a universal constant.

Proof.

The proof is based on a number of straightforward steps.

Step 1
Obviously it suffices to show that the Sudakov minoration works for
A=p
.
Let
m_{p}(t,s)=\|\sum^{n}_{i=1}(t_{i}-s_{i})\varepsilon_{i}\|_{p}
.
We may assume that
p\geqslant 1
is suitably large. Moreover, we may consider
T
such that
0\in T
,
|T|\geqslant\exp(\frac{p}{2})+\exp(\frac{3p}{4})
and

m_{p}(t,0)\leqslant\delta p
for all
t\in T
, where
\delta\leqslant 1
can be suitably small.

By the Talagrand’s result
[
19
]
(see also
[
20
]
for the modern exposition)
if
N(T,m_{p},u)\geqslant\exp(\frac{p}{4})-1
then
\mathbf{E}\sup_{s,t\in T}\sum^{n}_{i=1}(t_{i}-s_{i})\varepsilon_{i}\geqslant L^{-1}u
, for
a universal
L
. Therefore either the result holds with
K=2^{-1}L^{-1}\delta
or

\mathbf{E}\sup_{s,t\in T}\sum^{n}_{i=1}(t_{i}-s_{i})\varepsilon_{i}<2^{-1}L^{-1}\delta p
and then

N(T,m_{p},\frac{1}{2}\delta p)\leqslant\exp(\frac{p}{4})-1\leqslant\frac{\exp(\frac{p}{2})}{1+\exp(\frac{1}{4}p)}.

It implies that there exists
t_{0}\in T
such that

\{t\in T:\;m_{p}(t,t_{0})\leqslant\delta p\}|\geqslant|T|\frac{1+\exp(\frac{1}{4}p)}{\exp(\frac{p}{2})}\geqslant\exp(\frac{p}{2})+\exp(\frac{3p}{4}).

Therefore, we may consider set
T^{\prime}=\{t-t_{0}:m_{p}(t,t_{0})\leqslant\delta p\}
, which satisfies
all the requirements we have promised.

Step 2

Let
\delta\leqslant e^{-1}
and
\rho/\log(1/\rho)=4C\delta
.
We may assume that
0\in T
,
|T|\geqslant 1+\exp(\frac{p}{4})
and additionally

t_{i}\in(k_{i}-\rho,k_{i}+\rho)\cup(-\rho,\rho)\;\;\mbox{for all}\;t\in T\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;i\in[d],

where
k_{i}
are given numbers such that
k_{i}\geqslant\rho
where
\rho\leqslant e^{-1}
and satisfies

\rho\log\frac{1}{\rho}=4\delta\leqslant e^{-1},\;\;\mbox{where}\;C\geqslant 1

is a universal constant which we choose later.

Indeed. consider measure
\mu=\otimes^{d}_{i=1}\mu_{i}
, where
\mu_{i}(dx)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-|x|}dx
for
all

. For any

,
x=(x_{i})^{d}_{i=1}
and

,

and

A_{t}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\;t_{i}\in(x_{i}-\rho,x_{i}+\rho)\cup(-\rho,\rho),\;i\in[d]\}.

Now there are two possibilities either

\displaystyle\mu_{i}(\{x_{i}:\;t_{i}\in(x_{i}-\rho,x_{i}+\rho)\cup(-\rho,\rho)\})\geqslant\rho e^{-|t_{i}|-\rho}

(15)

or

\displaystyle\mu_{i}(\{x_{i}:\;t_{i}\in(x_{i}-\rho,x_{i}+\rho)\cup(-\rho,\rho)\})=1,\;\;\mbox{if}\;|t_{i}|<\rho.

(16)

Applying (
13
) we get for some
C\geqslant 1

\displaystyle m_{p}(t,0)=\|\sum^{d}_{i=1}t_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\|_{p}\geqslant C^{-1}\left(\sum_{i\leqslant p}|t^{\ast}_{i}|+\sqrt{p}\left(\sum_{i>p}|t_{i}^{\ast}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(17)

where
|t^{\ast}_{i}|
is the non-decreasing rearrangement of
|t_{i}|
. Again we deduce from (

\displaystyle m_{p}(t,0)\leqslant C\left(\sum_{i\leqslant p}|t^{\ast}_{i}|+\sqrt{p}\left(\sum_{i>p}|t_{i}^{\ast}\right)^{2})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(18)

for some

. Therefore, using (

\displaystyle|\{i\in\{1,...,d\}:\;|t_{i}|\geqslant\rho\}|\leqslant\frac{p}{4\log\frac{1}{\rho}}\leqslant\frac{p}{4}.

(19)

Again using (

\displaystyle\sum^{d}_{i=1}|t_{i}|1_{|t_{i}|\geqslant\rho}\leqslant C\delta p.

(20)

Consequently, by (

16

\mu(A_{t})\geqslant\rho^{\frac{p}{4\log\frac{1}{\rho}}}\exp(-2C\delta p)\geqslant\exp(-\frac{p}{2}).

However, using that

Therefore we get that there exists

|T_{k}|\geqslant\exp(\frac{p}{4}).

It is obvious that

.
Combining (

\sum^{d}_{i=1}(|k_{i}|-\rho)\vee\rho 1_{|t_{i}|\geqslant\rho}\leqslant C\delta p.

Clearly

1=\mathbf{E}V_{ki}^{2}\mathbf{E}\tilde{R}_{k}^{2}=\frac{1}{b_{k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}}}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+2}{p_{k}}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}}\right)}\mathbf{E}V_{ki}^{2},

hence

\mathbf{E}V_{ki}^{2}=\frac{b_{k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+2}{p_{k}}\right)}.

Therefore, by Lemma
1
we get

\frac{1}{\mathbf{E}(V_{ki})^{2}}=\mathbf{E}R_{k}^{2}\leqslant e^{\frac{2}{n_{k}}\tilde{U}_{k}(0)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+2}{p_{k}}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}}\right)^{1+\frac{2}{n_{k}}}}.

It proves the following inequality

\displaystyle e^{\frac{1}{n_{k}}\tilde{U}_{k}(0)}\geqslant b_{k}^{-\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n_{k}}}.

(35)

In order to prove the upper bound we need a Hensley type inequality.
First observe that

\mathbf{P}(R_{k}\leqslant t)=\int^{t}_{0}x^{n_{k}-1}e^{-\tilde{U}_{k}(x^{p_{k}})}dx\leqslant\int^{t}_{0}x^{n_{k}-1}e^{-\tilde{U}_{k}(0)}dx=\frac{1}{n_{k}}t^{n_{k}}e^{-\tilde{U}_{k}(0)}.

Let
F_{k}(t)=\frac{1}{n_{k}}t^{n_{k}}e^{-\tilde{U}_{k}(0)}
for
0\leqslant t\leqslant t_{\ast}
,
where
t_{\ast}=\left(n_{k}e^{\tilde{U}_{k}(0)}\right)^{1/n_{k}}
.
We can calculate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathbf{E}(V_{k})^{2}_{i}}=\mathbf{E}R_{k}^{2}=2\int^{\infty}_{0}t\mathbf{P}(R_{k}>t)dt

\displaystyle\geqslant 2\int^{t_{\ast}}_{0}t(1-F_{k}(t))dt\geqslant 2\int^{t_{\ast}}_{0}t(1-t^{n_{k}})dt

\displaystyle=t_{\ast}^{2}-2\frac{1}{n_{k}+2}t_{\ast}^{n_{k}+2}e^{-\tilde{U}_{k}(0)}\frac{1}{n_{k}}

\displaystyle=t_{\ast}^{2}-\frac{2}{n_{k}+2}t_{\ast}^{2}=\frac{n_{k}}{n_{k}+2}t_{\ast}^{2}.

Therefore

\left(n_{k}e^{\tilde{U}_{k}(0)}\right)^{\frac{2}{n_{k}}}\leqslant\frac{n_{k}+2}{n_{k}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+2}{p_{k}}\right)}{b_{k}^{\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}}\right)}.

Which means

\displaystyle e^{\frac{1}{n_{k}}\tilde{U}_{k}(0)}\leqslant\frac{1}{n_{k}^{\frac{1}{n_{k}}}}\frac{(n_{k}+2)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{n_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+2}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{b_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

(36)

It finally proves

\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n_{k}}}}{b_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}}\leqslant e^{\frac{1}{n_{k}}\tilde{U}_{k}(0)}\leqslant\frac{1}{n_{k}^{\frac{1}{n_{k}}}}\frac{(n_{k}+2)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{n_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+2}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{b_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}

and hence

e^{\frac{1}{n_{k}}\tilde{U}_{k}(0)}\simeq\frac{1}{\|V_{ki}\|_{2}}\simeq n_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}.

Together with Corollary
1
it shows that

\displaystyle\|R_{k}\|_{r}\lesssim n_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+r}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n_{k}}+\frac{1}{r}}}.

(37)

Note that

\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+r}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n_{k}}+\frac{1}{r}}}\simeq\left(1+\frac{r}{n_{k}}\right)^{\frac{n_{k}+r}{p_{k}r}}.

That is why

\displaystyle\|R_{k}\|_{r}\lesssim(n_{k}+r)^{1/p_{k}}.

(38)

In particular
\|R_{k}\|_{r}\simeq n_{k}^{1/p_{k}}
for
1\leqslant r\leqslant n_{k}
.
We introduce variables
P_{k}=R_{k}/\|R_{k}\|_{2}
,
Q_{k}=\tilde{R}_{k}/\|\tilde{R}_{k}\|_{2}
,
W_{k}=\|R_{k}\|_{2}V_{k}
.
This helps since now vectors
(\varepsilon_{k}P_{k})^{M}_{k=1}
,
(\varepsilon_{k}Q_{k})^{M}_{k=1}

are in the isotropic position, where
\varepsilon_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,M

are independent Rademacher variables, independent of all
P_{k}
and
Q_{k}
.
Obviously, also
W_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,M
are isotropic, independent and independent of all
P_{k}
.
Thus in particular
\|P_{k}\|_{r},\|Q_{k}\|_{r}\simeq 1
, for
1\leqslant r\leqslant n_{k}
.
Once again due to Lemma
1

\displaystyle e^{-\tilde{U}_{k}(0)(\frac{n_{k}+q}{p_{k}}-1)}\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+p}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{n_{k}+q}{p_{k}}}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}x^{\frac{n_{k}+q}{p_{k}}-1}e^{-\tilde{U}_{k}(x)}dx\right]^{\frac{n_{k}+p}{p_{k}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{-\tilde{U}_{k}(0)(\frac{n_{k}+p}{p_{k}}-1)}\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+q}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{n_{k}+p}{p_{k}}}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}x^{\frac{n_{k}+p}{p_{k}}-1}e^{-\tilde{U}_{k}(x)}dx\right]^{\frac{n_{k}+q}{p_{k}}}.

Therefore,

\|R_{k}\|_{q}^{\frac{q(n_{k}+p)}{p_{k}}}\leqslant e^{\tilde{U}_{k}(0)\frac{(q-p)}{p_{k}}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+q}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{n_{k}+p}{p_{k}}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+p}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{n_{k}+q}{p_{k}}}}\|R_{k}\|_{p}^{\frac{p(n_{k}+q)}{p_{k}}}.

which is

\displaystyle\|R_{k}\|_{q}\leqslant e^{\tilde{U}_{k}(0)\frac{(q-p)}{q(n_{k}+p)}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+q}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+p}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{n_{k}+q}{q(n_{k}+p)}}}\|R_{k}\|_{p}^{\frac{p(n_{k}+q)}{q(n_{k}+p)}}.

(39)

Note that

\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+q}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}+p}{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{n_{k}+q}{q(n_{k}+p)}}}\simeq\frac{(n_{k}+q)^{\frac{n_{k}+q}{p_{k}q}}}{(n_{k}+p)^{\frac{n_{k}+q}{p_{k}q}}}\simeq\left(\frac{n_{k}+q}{n_{k}+p}\right)^{\frac{n_{k}+q}{qp_{k}}}.

Moreover, for
p\geqslant 1

e^{\tilde{U}_{k}(0)\frac{(q-p)}{q(n_{k}+p)}}\lesssim(n_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}})^{\frac{(q-p)n_{k}}{q(n_{k}+p)}}\lesssim\|R_{k}\|_{p}^{\frac{(q-p)n_{k}}{q(n_{k}+p)}}

and since

\frac{(q-p)n_{k}}{q(n_{k}+p)}+\frac{p(n_{k}+q)}{q(n_{k}+p)}=1

it proves that

\displaystyle\|R_{k}\|_{q}\lesssim\left(\frac{n_{k}+q}{n_{k}+p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\|R_{k}\|_{p},

(40)

which means that
R_{k}
is
\alpha
regular. In particular
\varepsilon_{k}R_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,M
are independent symmetric

\alpha
-regular variables. It also implies that
\tilde{R}_{k}
have the fastest growth among all the possible distributions of
R_{k}
.

We end this section with the characterization of
\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}
.
Recall our notation
P_{k}=R_{k}/\|R_{k}\|_{2}
and
W_{k}=\|R_{k}\|_{2}V_{k}
.
We show

Proposition 4

Under the assumptions as in Theorem
3
and assuming that all the properties mentioned in Preposition
1
are satisfied, the following result holds

\displaystyle\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}\simeq\sup\left\{\sum^{M}_{k=1}\|P_{k}\|_{r_{k}}\|\langle W_{k},t_{k}-s_{k}\rangle\|_{r_{k}}1_{r_{k}\geqslant|I_{k}(t)\triangle I_{k}(s)||}:\;\;\sum^{M}_{k=1}r_{k}=p\right\}

(41)

\displaystyle\|Y_{t}-Y_{s}\|_{p}\simeq\sup\left\{\sum^{M}_{k=1}\|Q_{k}\|_{r_{k}}\|\langle W_{k},t_{k}-s_{k}\rangle\|_{r_{k}}1_{r_{k}\geqslant|I_{k}(t)\triangle I_{k}(s)||}:\;\;\sum^{M}_{k=1}r_{k}=p\right\}.

(42)

Moreover, if
r_{k}\geqslant|I_{k}(t)\triangle I_{k}(s)||
then

\|Q_{k}\|_{r_{k}}\|\langle W_{k},t_{k}-s_{k}\rangle\|_{r_{k}}=\|Y_{t_{k}}\|_{r_{k}}\sim r_{k}^{1/p_{k}}\|t_{k}\|_{q_{k}}\lesssim\|P_{k}\|_{r_{k}}|\langle W_{k},t_{k}-s_{k}\rangle\|_{r_{k}}=\|X_{t_{k}}\|_{r_{k}}.

Proof.

We use that
X_{t}-X_{s}=\sum^{M}_{k=1}\langle X_{k},t_{k}-s_{k}\rangle
and
X_{k}
are independent, log-concave vectors. Observe that

X_{t_{k}-s_{k}}=\langle X_{k},t_{k}-s_{k}\rangle=P_{k}\langle W_{k},t_{k}-s_{k}\rangle.

Let us denote also
I_{k}(t)=J_{k}\cap I(t)
.
By Proposition
1
- see (
22
) - the number of
k\in M
for which
t_{k}-s_{k}
is non-zero is much smaller than
p
. We use Theorem
5
treating
X_{t}-X_{s}
as
\sum^{M}_{k=1}X_{t_{k}-s_{k}}
, it yields

\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}\simeq\sup\left\{\sum^{M}_{k=1}\|X_{t_{k}}\|_{r_{k}}:\;\;\sum^{M}_{k=1}r_{k}=p\right\}.

Since
\|X_{t_{k}-s_{k}}\|_{r_{k}}=\|P_{k}\|_{r_{k}}\|\langle W_{k},t_{k}-s_{k}\rangle\|_{r_{k}}
we get

\displaystyle\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}\simeq\sup\left\{\sum^{M}_{k=1}\|P_{k}\|_{r_{k}}\|\langle W_{k},t_{k}-s_{k}\rangle\|_{r_{k}}:\;\;\sum^{M}_{k=1}r_{k}=p\right\}.

(43)

Moreover,

\|P_{k}\|_{r}\simeq 1,\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leqslant n_{k},\;\;\|P_{k}\|_{r}\lesssim\frac{r_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}}{n_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}},\;\;r>n_{k}.

and

\|\langle W_{k},t_{k}-s_{k}\rangle\|_{r}\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}r^{1/p_{k}}\|t_{k}-s_{k}\|_{q_{k}}&\mbox{for}&|I_{k}(t)\triangle I_{k}(s)|\leqslant r\leqslant n_{k}\\
n_{k}^{1/p_{k}}\|t_{k}-s_{k}\|_{q_{k}}&\mbox{for}&r>n_{k}\end{array}\right..

We stress that we should not care about
r_{k}<|I_{k}(t)\triangle I_{k}(s)|

since by (

),
\sum^{M}_{k=1}|I_{k}(t)\triangle I_{k}(s)|
is much smaller than

. Thus

can be ignored when estimating

. Thus finally

\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}\simeq\left\{\sum^{M}_{k=1}\|P_{k}\|_{r_{k}}\|\langle W_{k},t_{k}-s_{k}\rangle\|_{r_{k}}1_{r_{k}\geqslant|I_{k}(t)\triangle I_{k}(s)||}:\;\;\sum^{M}_{k=1}r_{k}=p\right\}

).

\blacksquare

We will use the above result many times in the subsequent proofs, even not mentioning it.

5
Positive process

We are going to show that it suffices to prove that for all
p\geqslant 1

\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}\sum^{M}_{k=1}|X_{t_{k}}|\geqslant\frac{1}{K}p.

For simplicity we define
|X|_{t}=\sum^{M}_{k=1}|X_{t_{k}}|
.
We show that

Lemma 2

Suppose that
T
satisfies simplifications from Proposition
1
. Then

\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}|X|_{t}\geqslant\frac{1}{K}p
, implies that also

\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}\geqslant\frac{1}{4K}p
.

Proof.

Before we start, let introduce notation: writing
\mathbf{E}_{I}
for
I\subset[M]
we mean the integration over vectors
(X_{k})_{k\in I}
.
Note in particular that
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{E}_{I,I^{c}}
.

Let us observe that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}=\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}\sum^{M}_{k=1}X_{t_{k}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}\sum^{M}_{k=1}\varepsilon_{k}X_{t_{k}}\geqslant\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}\sum^{M}_{k=1}\varepsilon_{k}|X_{t_{k}}|,

where
(\varepsilon_{k})^{M}_{k=1}
are independent random signs. The last inequality is due to Bernoulli comparison
and the inequality

||X_{t_{k}}|-|X_{s_{k}}||\leqslant|X_{t_{k}}-X_{s_{k}}|.

Now the standard argument works, namely

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}\sum^{M}_{k=1}\varepsilon_{k}|X_{t_{k}}|=\sum_{I\subset[M]}\frac{1}{2^{M}}\mathbf{E}_{I,I^{c}}\sup_{t\in T}\sum_{k\in I}|X_{t_{k}}|-\sum_{k\in I^{c}}|X_{t_{k}}|

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{I\subset[M]}\frac{1}{2^{M}}\mathbf{E}_{I}\sup_{t\in T}\left(\sum_{k\in I}|X_{t_{k}}|-\sum_{k\in I^{c}}\mathbf{E}_{I^{c}}|X_{t_{k}}|\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{I\subset[M]}\frac{1}{2^{M}}\mathbf{E}\sum_{k\in I}|X_{t_{k}}|-\delta"p\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}\sum^{M}_{k=1}|X_{t_{k}}|-\delta"p,

where we have used Jensen’s inequality and (
22
) which implies

\sum_{k\in I^{c}}\mathbf{E}|X_{t_{k}}|\leqslant\sum_{i\in I(t)}k_{i}\mathbf{E}|X_{i}|\leqslant\sum_{i\in I(t)}k_{i}\leqslant\delta"p.

It suffices that
\delta"\leqslant 1/(4K)
.

\blacksquare

The above result enables us to split points
t_{k}
into small and large part. More precisely, let us split
t_{k}=t^{\ast}_{k}+t^{\dagger}_{k}
, where

\displaystyle{\bf small\;\;part:}\;\;t^{\ast}_{k}=t_{k}\;\;\mbox{if}\;\;D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|_{q_{k}}^{q_{k}}\leqslant A^{p_{k}}n_{k}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;0\;\;\mbox{otherwise},

(44)

\displaystyle{\bf large\;\;part:}\;\;t^{\dagger}_{k}=t_{k}\;\;\mbox{if}\;\;D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|_{q_{k}}^{q_{k}}>A^{p_{k}}n_{k}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;0\;\;\mbox{otherwise}.

(45)

We will need that
A,D\geqslant 1
and
A
is such that
A^{p_{k}}\geqslant D^{q_{k}}
for each
k\in[M]
.
This is the moment when we take advantage of the cutoff level
p_{k}\geqslant 1+\varepsilon
, since then we can find such
A
that does depend on
\varepsilon
only. In fact we could do better since our split is not important when
q_{k}>n_{k}
. Indeed, then

n_{k}^{1/q_{k}}\simeq 1
,
\|t_{k}\|_{q_{k}}\geqslant\|t_{k}\|_{\infty}
, and it is well known that we can require in the proof of minoration
that each
|t_{i}|
,
i\in I(t)
is large enough. That means, for
p_{k}
too close to
1
, the first case simply cannot happen.
In general, we do not want to bound
n_{k}
but fortunately, it is possible to reduce the dimension of
our problem by a simple trick. Note that
T
has about
e^{p}
points whose supports are thin - much smaller than
p
. Thus, there
are only
pe^{p}
important coordinates on which we can condition our basic vector
X
. Moreover,
due to the Prekopa-Leindler theorem, such a reduced problem is still of log-concave distribution
type. Since it does not affect computation of
\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}
moments
s,t
we end up in the question,
where
d\leqslant pe^{p}
, which together with Remark
3
show that we should care only for
p\leqslant d\leqslant pe^{p}
.
The trouble with this concept is that it introduces dependence of
\varepsilon
on p which we do not like in
our approach.

We have two possibilities:

•

either there exists
S\subset T
,
|S|\geqslant e^{p/2}
such that for any
s,t\in S
,
s\neq t
and

\displaystyle\sum^{M}_{k=1}D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}^{\ast}-s^{\ast}_{k}\|_{q_{k}}^{q_{k}}=\sum^{M}_{k=1}\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})\triangle I_{k}(s^{\ast})}D^{q_{k}}k_{i}^{q_{k}}\geqslant p;

(46)

•

or there is a point
t_{0}\in T
and a subset
S\subset T
,
|S|\geqslant e^{p/2}
such that for any
t\in S
we have

\displaystyle\sum^{M}_{k=1}D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}^{\ast}-t^{\ast}_{0k}\|_{q_{k}}^{q_{k}}=\sum^{M}_{k=1}\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})\triangle I_{k}(t_{0}^{\ast})}D^{q_{k}}k_{i}^{q_{k}}<p.

(47)

Let
\mathbf{E}_{P},\mathbf{E}_{W}
denote the integration with respect to
(P_{k})^{M}_{k=1}
and
(W_{k})^{M}_{k=1}
.
Note that we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}|X|_{t}\geqslant\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}\sum^{M}_{k=1}|P_{k}||\langle W_{k},t_{k}\rangle|

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbf{E}_{W}\sup_{t\in T}\sum^{M}_{k=1}\mathbf{E}_{P}|P_{k}||\langle W_{k},t_{k}\rangle|\simeq\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}\sum^{M}_{k=1}|\langle W_{k},t_{k}\rangle|.

(48)

For simplicity we denote

|\tilde{X}|_{t}=\sum^{M}_{k=1}|\langle W_{k},t_{k}\rangle|.

Obviously,
|\tilde{X}|_{t}\geqslant|\tilde{X}|_{t^{\ast}}
. We are going to prove that in the case of small coefficients (cf. (
44
)) necessarily

\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}|\tilde{X}|_{t^{\ast}}\geqslant K^{-1}p
.

6
Small coefficients

Recall that we work in the cube-like setting introduced in Proposition
1
.
We are going to prove the Sudakov minoration, in the setting where
there are a lot of well separated points in
T
, in the sense of (
46
).
Toward this goal, we need the process
(Y_{t})_{t\in T}
- see (
28
). We slightly modify the process, namely let

Z_{t}=\sum^{M}_{k=1}\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t)}\varepsilon_{i}\left(k_{i}|Y_{i}|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}k_{i}^{q_{k}},\;\;|Z|_{t}=\sum^{M}_{k=1}\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t)}\left(k_{i}|Y_{i}|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}k_{i}^{q_{k}},

where as usual
\varepsilon_{i}
,
i\in[d]
are independent random signs.
Since now the entrances are independent, in order to prove that
\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in S}Z_{t^{\ast}}\geqslant K^{-1}p
it suffices
to show that
\|Z_{t^{\ast}}-Z_{s^{\ast}}\|_{p}\gtrsim p/D
. We prove that this holds under the condition (
46
).

Lemma 3

Suppose that (
46
) holds. Then
\|Z_{t^{\ast}}-Z_{s^{\ast}}\|_{p}\gtrsim p/D
.

Proof.

Note that for some
\sum^{M}_{k=1}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}r_{ki}=p

\|Z_{t^{\ast}}-Z_{s^{\ast}}\|_{p}\simeq\sum^{M}_{k=1}\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})\triangle I_{k}(s^{\ast})}\|\left(k_{i}|Y_{i}|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}k_{i}^{q_{k}}\|_{r_{ki}}.

However, it is clear that
\|\left(k_{i}|Y_{i}|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}k_{i}^{q_{k}}\|_{r_{ki}}\simeq\|k_{i}Y_{i}\|_{r_{ki}}\wedge D^{q_{k}}k_{i}^{q_{k}}
.
Since
\|k_{i}Y_{i}\|_{r_{ki}}\simeq k_{i}r_{ki}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}
we get

\|Z_{t^{\ast}}-Z_{s^{\ast}}\|_{p}\simeq\sum^{M}_{k=1}\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})\triangle I_{k}(s^{\ast})}\left(k_{i}r_{ki}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}k_{i}^{q_{k}}.

Our assumption is that

\sum^{M}_{k=1}\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})\triangle I_{k}(s^{\ast})}D^{q_{k}}k_{i}^{q_{k}}\geqslant p,

which means that we can select sets
I_{k}\subset I_{k}(t^{\ast})\triangle I_{k}(s^{\ast})
such that
r_{ki}=D^{q_{k}}k_{i}^{q_{k}}
for each
i\in I_{k}

and
r_{ki}=0
for
i\in J_{k}\backslash I_{k}
, which satisfy
\sum^{M}_{k=1}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}D^{q_{k}}k_{i}^{q_{k}}\simeq p
. Then

\|Z_{t^{\ast}}-Z_{s^{\ast}}\|_{p}\gtrsim\sum^{M}_{k=1}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}D^{q_{k}-1}k_{i}^{q_{k}}\gtrsim p/D.

This proves the result.

\blacksquare

We have proved that
\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in S}Z_{t^{\ast}}\geqslant K^{-1}p
.
This implies that

K^{-1}p\leqslant\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in S}\sum^{M}_{k=1}\left(\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})}k_{i}\varepsilon_{i}|Y_{i}|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|_{q_{k}}^{q_{k}}.

The crucial thing is to establish a similar inequality replacing
\big|\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t)}k_{i}\varepsilon_{i}|Y_{i}|\big|
with

\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t)}k_{i}|Y_{i}|
. This can be done following the approach we have used in the proof of the positive process lemma - Lemma
2
. Namely, we have

Lemma 4

The following inequality holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in S}\sum^{M}_{k=1}\left(\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})}k_{i}\varepsilon_{i}|Y_{i}|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|_{q_{k}}^{q_{k}}\geqslant K^{-1}p.

(49)

Proof.

Let us observe that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in S}\sum^{M}_{k=1}|\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})}k_{i}Y_{i}\big|\wedge\|t_{k}\|_{q_{k}}^{q_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{I\subset[d]}\mathbf{E}_{I,I^{c}}\sup_{t\in S}\left(\sum^{M}_{k=1}\big|\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})\cap I}k_{i}|Y_{i}|-\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})\cap I^{c}}k_{i}|Y_{i}|\big|\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|_{q_{k}}^{q_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{I\subset[d]}\mathbf{E}_{I}\sup_{t\in S}\left(\sum^{M}_{k=1}\left(\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})\cap I}k_{i}|Y_{i}|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|_{q_{k}}^{q_{k}}-\mathbf{E}_{I^{c}}\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})\cap I^{c}}k_{i}|Y_{i}|\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{I\subset[d]}\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in S}\sum^{M}_{k=1}\left(\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})\cap I}k_{i}|Y_{i}|\right)\wedge D^{k_{q}}\|t_{k}\|_{q_{k}}^{q_{k}}-\delta^{\prime\prime}p

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in S}\sum^{M}_{k=1}\left(\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast}}k_{i}|Y_{i}|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|_{q_{k}}^{q_{k}}-\delta^{\prime\prime}p,

where we have used
\mathbf{E}|Y_{i}|\leqslant 1
and
\sum_{i\in I(t)}k_{i}\leqslant\delta^{\prime\prime}p
- Proposition
1
, see (
22
).
We have used also that for positive
a,b,c
, inequalities
|a-b|\wedge c\geqslant a\wedge c-b
and
a\wedge c+b\wedge c\geqslant(a+b)\wedge c

For suitably small

(4K)^{-1}p\leqslant\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in S}\sum^{M}_{k=1}\big|\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})}k_{i}\varepsilon_{i}|Y_{i}|\big|\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|^{q_{k}}_{q_{k}}.

\blacksquare

Let us recall our notation
Q_{k}=\tilde{R}_{k}/\|\tilde{R}_{k}\|_{2}

W_{k}=V_{k}\|\tilde{R}_{k}\|_{2}

Q_{k}\langle W_{k},t_{k}\rangle=Y_{t_{k}}

Consequently, the above result can be rewritten in the following form

\displaystyle(4K)^{-1}p\leqslant\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in S}\sum^{M}_{k=1}\left(Q_{k}|\langle W_{k},t^{\ast}_{k}\rangle|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|^{q_{k}}_{q_{k}}.

(50)

and finally

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in S}\sum^{M}_{k=1}\left(Q_{k}|\langle W_{k},t^{\ast}_{k}\rangle|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|^{q_{k}}_{q_{k}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in S}\sum^{M}_{k=1}\left(C|\langle W_{k},t^{\ast}_{k}\rangle|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|^{q_{k}}_{q_{k}}

\displaystyle+\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in S}\sum^{M}_{k=1}\left((Q_{k}-C)_{+}|\langle W_{k},t^{\ast}_{k}\rangle|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|^{q_{k}}_{q_{k}}.

\displaystyle\left\|\sum^{M}_{k=1}\left((Q_{k}-C)_{+}|\langle W_{k},t^{\ast}_{k}\rangle|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|^{q_{k}}_{q_{k}}\right\|_{p}\leqslant cp

(52)

for

This is the main estimate in this section.

Theorem 6

For suitably large
C
,
(

which can be suitably small.

Proof.

Clearly, for
u>\tilde{C}

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(Q_{k}>u+\tilde{C})=\frac{b_{k}^{\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}}}}{\frac{1}{p_{k}}\Gamma(\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}})}\int^{\infty}_{\|\tilde{R}_{k}\|_{2}(\tilde{C}+u)}x^{n_{k}-1}e^{-b_{k}x^{p_{k}}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{k}^{\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}}}}{\frac{1}{p_{k}}\Gamma(\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}})}\int^{\infty}_{\|\tilde{R}_{k}\|_{2}u}(\|\tilde{R}_{k}\|_{2}\tilde{C}+x)^{n_{k}-1}e^{-b_{k}(\|\tilde{R}_{k}\|_{2}\tilde{C}+x)^{p_{k}}}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{n_{k}-1}\exp(-b_{k}\|\tilde{R}_{k}\|^{p_{k}}_{2}C^{p_{k}})\frac{b_{k}^{\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}}}}{\frac{1}{p_{k}}\Gamma(\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}})}\int^{\infty}_{\|\tilde{R}_{k}\|_{2}u}x^{n_{k}-1}e^{-b_{k}x^{p_{k}}}dx

\displaystyle=2^{n_{k}-1}\exp(-b_{k}\|\tilde{R}_{k}\|_{2}^{p_{k}}\tilde{C}^{p_{k}})\mathbf{P}(Q_{k}>u).

Therefore, choosing

, we have

can be replaced by

, where

We denote by

integration with respect to variables

\displaystyle\|\sum^{M}_{k=1}\left((Q_{k}-C)_{+}|\langle W_{k},t^{\ast}_{k}\rangle|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|^{q_{k}}_{q_{k}}\|_{p}\leqslant\|\sum^{M}_{k=1}\delta_{k}\left(Q_{k}|\langle W_{k},t^{\ast}_{k}\rangle|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|^{q_{k}}_{q_{k}}\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left[\mathbf{E}_{\delta}\sum_{K\subset[M]}\prod_{k\in K}1_{\delta_{k}=1}\prod_{l\in K^{c}}1_{\delta_{l}=0}\mathbf{E}_{Q,W}\left[\sum_{k\in K}\left(Q_{k}|\langle W_{k},t_{k}\rangle|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|^{q_{k}}_{q_{k}}\right]^{p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(53)

We use Proposition
4

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{E}_{Q,W}\left[\sum_{k\in K}\left(Q_{k}|\langle W_{k},t^{\ast}_{k}\rangle|\right)\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|^{q_{k}}_{q_{k}}\right]^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\|\sum^{M}_{k=1}Q_{k}|\langle W_{k},t^{\ast}_{k}\rangle|\wedge D^{q_{k}}\|t_{k}\|^{q_{k}}_{q_{k}}\|_{p}

On the other hand, if for some

Consequently, by (

Large coefficients

We work in the cube-like setting formulated in Proposition

accompanied by the simplification from Proposition

.
Our goal is to prove the minoration for large coefficients - see (
45

(

t_{0}\in T
and a subset

\sum^{M}_{k=1}\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})\triangle I_{k}(t_{0}^{\ast})}D^{q_{k}}k_{i}^{q_{k}}<p.

We are going to show that in this case
\|X_{t^{\dagger}}-X_{s^{\dagger}}\|_{p}\gtrsim p
for all

.
It suffices to prove that
\|X_{t^{\ast}}-X_{t^{\ast}_{0}}\|_{p}

\|X_{s^{\ast}}-X_{t^{\ast}_{0}}\|_{p}

.
More precisely,

Lemma 5

If
D
is suitably large then
\|X_{t^{\ast}}-X_{t^{\ast}_{0}}\|_{p}\leqslant p/4
for any
t\in S
.

Proof.

Consider
\sum^{M}_{k=1}r_{k}=p
and

\|X_{t^{\ast}}-X_{t_{0}^{\ast}}\|_{p}\simeq\sum^{M}_{k=1}\|P_{k}\|_{r_{k}}\|\langle W_{k},t^{\ast}_{k}-t^{\ast}_{0k}\rangle\|_{r_{k}}.

Not that we have the inequality

\|P_{k}\|_{r_{k}}\\
|\langle W_{k},t^{\ast}_{k}-t^{\ast}_{0k}\rangle\|_{r_{k}}\lesssim r_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\|t^{\ast}_{k}-t^{\ast}_{0k}\|_{q_{k}}.

Using that
a^{1/p_{k}}b^{1/q_{k}}\leqslant a/p_{k}+b/q_{k}
we get

\|P_{k}\|_{r_{k}}\\
|\langle W_{k},t^{\ast}_{k}-t^{\ast}_{0k}\rangle\|_{r_{k}}\leqslant p_{k}^{-1}\frac{r_{k}}{D}+q_{k}^{-1}D^{\frac{q_{k}}{p_{k}}}\|t^{\ast}_{k}-t^{\ast}_{0k}\|^{q_{k}}_{q_{k}}.

Clearly
q_{k}/p_{k}=1/(p_{k}-1)
. Now we can benefit from the condition (
47
), namely we have

\sum^{M}_{k=1}\sum_{i\in I_{k}(t^{\ast})\triangle I_{k}(t_{0}^{\ast})}D^{\frac{1}{p_{k}-1}}k_{i}^{q_{k}}<p/D.

For
D
suitably small it proves that
\|X_{t^{\ast}}-X_{t_{0}^{\ast}}\|_{p}\leqslant p/4
.

\blacksquare

Consequently, since
\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}\geqslant p
for
s\neq t
,
s,t\in S
we obtain

\displaystyle\|X_{t^{\dagger}}-X_{s^{\dagger}}\|_{p}\geqslant\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{p}-\|X_{t^{\ast}}-X_{t_{0}^{\ast}}\|_{p}-\|X_{s^{\ast}}-X_{t_{0}^{\ast}}\|_{p}\geqslant p/2.

(54)

The last result we need
stems from the fact that
\|X_{t^{\dagger}}\|_{p}\leqslant\|X_{t}\|_{p}\lesssim p
, which is due to Bernoulli comparison and Proposition
2
.
Namely, we have

Lemma 6

For any
t\in T
, the following inequality holds

\sum^{M}_{k=1}n_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\|t^{\dagger}_{k}\|_{q_{k}}\lesssim p.

Moreover,
\sum^{M}_{k=1}n_{k}1_{t^{\dagger}_{k}\neq 0}\leqslant pc
, where
c
can be suitably small.

Proof.

Obviously
\|X_{t^{\dagger}}\|_{p}=\|\sum^{M}_{k=1}P_{k}\langle W_{k},t^{\dagger}_{k}\rangle\|_{p}
, so since
\|X_{t^{\dagger}}\|_{p}\lesssim p

there must exist
r_{k}
,
1\leqslant k\leqslant M
such that
\sum^{M}_{k=1}r_{k}=p
and

\sum^{M}_{k=1}\|P_{k}\|_{r_{k}}\|\langle W_{k},t^{\dagger}_{k}\rangle\|_{r_{k}}\lesssim p.

If
r_{k}\geqslant n_{k}
we can use

\|P_{k}\|_{r_{k}}\|\langle W_{k},t^{\dagger}_{k}\rangle\|_{r_{k}}\gtrsim n_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\|t^{\dagger}_{k}\|_{q_{k}}.

Our aim is to show that we can use
r_{k}\geqslant n_{k}
for all
k
. This possible if

\sum^{M}_{k=1}n_{k}1_{t^{\dagger_{k}\neq 0}}\leqslant p.

Suppose conversely that
\sum_{k\in J}n_{k}1_{t^{\dagger}_{k}\neq 0}\simeq p
, for some
J\subset[M]
then
the inequality
\|X_{t^{\dagger}}\|_{p}\lesssim p
gives

\sum_{k\in J}n_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\|t^{\dagger}_{k}\|_{q_{k}}\lesssim p.

However, if
t^{\dagger}_{k}\neq 0
, then
D\|t^{\dagger}_{k}\|_{q_{k}}>A^{p_{k}-1}n_{k}^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}
by (
45
).
This is exactly the moment, where we need our technical assumption that
p_{k}
are not too close to
1
.
Namely, if
A\gtrsim\max\{D^{\frac{1}{p_{k}-1}}\}
we get
\sum_{k\in J}\sum^{M}_{k=1}n_{k}1_{t^{\dagger}_{k}\ neq0}>p

which is a contradiction. Consequently, we can select
r_{k}\geqslant n_{k}
for any
k
such that
t^{\dagger}_{k}\neq 0
.
Thus finally,

\sum^{M}_{k=1}n_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\|t^{\dagger}_{k}\|_{q_{k}}\lesssim p.

Moreover,

\sum^{M}_{k=1}n_{k}\frac{A^{p_{k}-1}}{D}\lesssim p.

Since
A
can be much larger than
\max{D^{\frac{1}{p_{k}-1}}}
it completes the proof.

\blacksquare

We can start the main proof in the case of large coefficients - cf. (
45
).
We are going to use a similar trick to the approach presented in the ’simplification lemma’ - Proposition
1
.
The point is that on each
{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{k}}={\mathbb{R}}^{|J_{k}|}
we impose a radial-type distribution.
Namely, let
\mu_{k}
be the probability distribution on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{k}}
with the density

\mu_{k}(dx)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{B}n_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\|x\|_{q_{k}}\right)\frac{n_{k}^{\frac{n_{k}}{p_{k}q_{k}}}}{B^{\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}}\right)|\partial B^{n_{k}}_{q_{k}}|}dx.

The fundamental property of
\mu_{k}
is that

\mu_{k}(n^{-\frac{1}{p_{k}}}BuB^{n_{k}}_{q_{k}})=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}}\right)}\int^{u}_{0}s^{\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}}-1}e^{-s}ds.

The median value of
u_{0}
the distribution
\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}},1\right)
is comparable to
\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}}
and hence
for
u=\rho\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}}
, where
\rho
is smaller than
1
we have

\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}}\right)}\int^{u}_{0}s^{\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}}-1}e^{-s}ds\gtrsim\rho^{\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}}}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{u}{u_{0}}\right)^{\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}}}.

Since
u_{0}\leqslant n_{k}/q_{k}
it gives

\mu_{k}(\rho Bn_{k}^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}q_{k}^{-1})\geqslant\rho^{\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}}},

for any
\rho
which is bit smaller that
1
.
Furthermore, by the construction

\mu_{k}\left(t^{\dagger}_{k}+\rho Bn_{k}^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}q_{k}^{-1}B^{n_{k}}_{q_{k}}\right)\gtrsim\rho^{\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}}}e^{-B^{-1}n_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\|t^{\dagger}_{k}\|_{q_{k}}}=\exp\left(-\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}}\log\frac{1}{\rho}-B^{-1}n_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\|t^{\dagger}_{k}\|_{q_{k}}\right).

Due to Lemma
6
we may find constant
B\geqslant 1
in such a way that for any
t\in T
,

\displaystyle B^{-1}\sum^{M}_{k=1}n_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\|t^{\dagger}_{k}\|_{q_{k}}\leqslant p/8.

(55)

Moreover, by the same result we may require that

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\right)\cdot\sum^{M}_{k=1}n_{k}1_{t^{\dagger}_{k}\neq 0}\leqslant p/8

(56)

for some suitably small
\rho
.

Let
\mu
be a measure defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by
\mu=\mu_{1}\otimes\mu_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mu_{M}
.
We define also

A_{t}=\left(x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\;\;\|t^{\dagger}_{k}-x_{k}\|_{q_{k}}\leqslant\rho Bn_{k}^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}q_{k}^{-1},\;\;\mbox{or}\;\;\|t^{\dagger}_{k}\|_{q_{k}}\leqslant\rho Bn_{k}^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}q_{k}^{-1},\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;k\in[M]\right).

Using (
55
) and (
56
), we get

\mu(A_{t})\geqslant\exp\left(-\sum^{M}_{k=1}\left(\frac{n_{k}}{q_{k}}\log\frac{1}{\rho}1_{t^{\dagger}_{k}\neq 0}+n_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\|t^{\dagger}_{k}\|_{q_{k}}\right)\right)\geqslant e^{-p/4}.

However, there are at least
e^{p/2}
points in
S
and hence

\sum_{t\in S}\mu(A_{t})\geqslant e^{p/4}.

We define

A_{x}=\left(t\in T:\;\;\|t^{\dagger}_{k}-x_{k}\|_{q_{k}}\leqslant\rho Bn_{k}^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}q_{k}^{-1},\;\;\mbox{or}\;\;\|t^{\dagger}_{k}\|_{q_{k}}\leqslant\rho Bn_{k}^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}q_{k}^{-1},\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;k\in[M]\right)

and observe that

\displaystyle\int|A_{x}|\mu(dx)=\sum_{t\in T}\int 1_{t\in A_{x}}\mu(dx)=\sum_{t\in T}\int 1_{x\in A_{t}}\mu(dx)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in T}\mu(A_{t})\geqslant e^{p/4}.

Consequently, there must exists
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
|A_{x}|
counts at least
e^{p/4}

points. Note that we can require that
\|x_{k}\|_{q_{k}}>\rho Bn_{k}^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}q_{k}^{-1}
. Let us denote the improved set
S
by
\bar{S}
, in particular

|\bar{S}|>e^{p/4}
.

The final step is as follows.
We define the function

\varphi_{k}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}x_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{k}}&\mbox{if}&\|t_{k}\|_{q_{k}}>\rho Bn^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}q_{k}^{-1},\\
0\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{k}}&\mbox{if}&\|t_{k}\|_{q_{k}}\leqslant\rho Bn_{k}^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}q_{k}^{-1}\end{array}\right.

Let
\varphi=(\varphi_{k})^{M}_{k=1}
. Note that if
\varphi_{k}(t)=0
then

by (
45
).
Therefore, we are sure that
\|t^{\dagger}_{k}-\varphi_{k}(t)\|_{q_{k}}\leqslant\rho Bn_{k}^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}q_{k}^{-1}
.

Lemma 7

The following inequality holds

\|X_{t^{\dagger}}-X_{\varphi(t)}\|_{p}\lesssim cp,

where

is suitably small.

Proof.

Indeed it suffices to check for

\sum^{M}_{k=1}r_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\|t^{\dagger}_{k}-\varphi_{k}(t)\|_{q_{k}}\leqslant cp.

However,
\rho
can be suitably small, thus the result follows.

\blacksquare

Now if

is suitably small we get that

\|X_{\varphi(t)}-X_{\varphi(s)}\|_{p}\geqslant p/4.

Indeed this is the consequence of (

Moreover, by Remark
1
and
\bar{S}\subset S\subset T
,
0\in T
and
|T|\leqslant 1+e^{p}

\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in\bar{S}}(X_{\varphi(t)}-X_{t^{\dagger}})\leqslant ecp.

It proves that

\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in\bar{S}}X_{\varphi(t)}\leqslant\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in\bar{S}}X_{t^{\dagger}}+\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in\bar{S}}(X_{\varphi(t)}-X_{t^{\dagger}})\leqslant\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in\bar{S}}X_{t^{\dagger}}+ecp.

It remains to observe that

9
) reads as
(1+\varepsilon)d_{n}\leqslant d_{n+1}
. Let us recall that the property works if all
p_{k}
are smaller than
some
p_{\infty}<\infty
.
Note that this assumption is not easily removable, since
for Bernoulli canonical processes the theory which we describe below is does not work.

We follow the generic chaining approach for families of distances described in
[
20
]
.
Let
N_{n}=2^{2^{n}}
,
n\geqslant 1
,
N_{0}=1
.
The natural candidate for the family
F_{n,j}
is
F_{n,j}=F
, where

F(A)=K\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in A}X_{t}
,
A\subset T
for suitably large constant
K
. We have to prove the growth condition, namely
that for some
r=2^{\kappa-2}\geqslant 4
and fixed
n_{0}\geqslant 0
for any given
n\geqslant n_{0}

and
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
if we can find points
t_{1},t_{2},\ldots t_{N_{n}}\in B_{n}(t,2^{n}r^{-j})

which are
2^{n+1}r^{-j-1}
separated in
d_{n+1}
distance, then for any
sets
H_{i}\subset B_{n+\kappa}(t_{i},2^{n+\kappa}r^{-j-2})
the following inequality
holds

\displaystyle F\left(\bigcup^{N_{n}}_{i=1}H_{i}\right)\geqslant 2^{n}r^{-j-1}+\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N_{n}}F(H_{i}).

(57)

Note that in our setting

\displaystyle B_{n}(t,2^{n}r^{j-2})\subset B_{n+1}(t,2^{n+1}r^{j-2})\subset\ldots\subset B_{n+\kappa}(t,2^{n+\kappa}r^{-j-2})

(58)

and also

\displaystyle B_{n+\kappa}(t,2^{n+\kappa}r^{-j-2})\subset B_{n}\left(t,\frac{2^{n+\kappa}r^{-j-2}}{(1+\varepsilon)^{\kappa}}\right)\subset B_{n}\left(t,\frac{2^{n+2}r^{-j-1}}{(1+\varepsilon)^{\kappa}}\right).

(59)

In particular we get from (
59
) that

\displaystyle H_{i}\subset B_{n+\kappa}(t_{i},2^{n+\kappa}r^{-j-2})\subset B_{n}\left(t_{i},\frac{2^{n+2}r^{-j-2}}{(1+\varepsilon)^{\kappa}}\right)

(60)

and hence we have that
H_{i}
are small when compared with the separation level
r^{-j-1}
.
Clearly if
d_{n+1}(t_{i},t_{j})\geqslant 2^{n+1}r^{-j-1}
then

d_{n}(t_{i},t_{j})\geqslant 2^{-1}d_{n+1}(t_{i},t_{j})\geqslant 2^{n}r^{-j-1}.

The last property we need is a special form of concentration, i.e.

\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in A}X_{t}-\sup_{t\in A}X_{t})_{+}\|_{p}\leqslant L\sup_{t\in A}\|X_{t}\|_{p}.

(61)

Let us recall that due to the result of
[
11
]
and some straightforward observations fortunately the
inequality holds in our setting. We turn to prove the main result of this section.

Proposition 5

Suppose that
X_{t}
satisfies assumptions on the regularity of distances
as well as the concentration inequality (
61
) then

F_{n,j}=F
,
F(A)=K\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in A}X_{t}
satisfies the growth condition (
57
) for
some
r
and
n_{0}
.

Proof.

Let us define
A=\bigcup^{N_{n}}_{i=1}H_{i}
and
H_{i}\subset B_{n+1}(t_{i},2^{n+1}r^{-j-1})
.
We have

\displaystyle F(A)\geqslant K\mathbf{E}\sup_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N_{n}}X_{t_{i}}+\sup_{t\in H_{i}}(X_{t}-X_{t_{i}})

\displaystyle\geqslant K\mathbf{E}\sup_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N_{n}}X_{t_{i}}+\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N_{n}}F(H_{i}-t_{i})

\displaystyle-K(\mathbf{E}\sup_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N_{n}}(\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in H_{i}}(X_{t}-X_{t_{i}})-\sup_{t\in H_{i}}(X_{t}-X_{t_{i}}))

\displaystyle\geqslant 2^{n+1}r^{-j-1}+\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N_{n}}F(H_{i})-2KL\sup_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N_{n}}\|(\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in H_{i}}(X_{t}-X_{t_{i}})-\sup_{t\in H_{i}}(X_{t}-X_{t_{i}}))_{+}\|_{2^{n}}

\displaystyle\geqslant 2^{n+1}r^{-j-1}+\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N_{n}}F(H_{i})-2KL\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}\Delta_{n}(H_{i}),

where we have used here the Sudakov minoration.
Using (
60
), we get

\Delta_{n}(H_{i})\leqslant\frac{2^{n+3}r^{j-1}}{(1+\varepsilon)^{\kappa}}.

Therefore choosing
\kappa
sufficiently large, we can guarantee that

F(A)\geqslant 2^{n}r^{-j-1}

as required.

\blacksquare

The basic result of
[
20
]
is that having the growth condition for families of distances, it is true that

S_{X}(T)=\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}
is comparable with
\gamma_{X}(T)
, where

\gamma_{X}(T)=\inf_{{\cal A}}\sup_{t\in T}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\Delta_{n}(A_{n}(t)).

It completes the proof of Theorem
2
. Namely,
for some absolute constant
K
,

K^{-1}\gamma_{X}(T)\leqslant\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}\geqslant K\gamma_{X}(T).

In this way it establishes a geometric characterization of

S_{X}(T)
, assuming our list of conditions.

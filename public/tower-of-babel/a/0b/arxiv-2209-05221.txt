Local parameter selection in the C 0 interior penalty method for the biharmonic equation
Philipp Bringmann, Carsten Carstensen, Julian Streitberger

Abstract
The symmetric C 0 interior penalty method is one of the most popular discontinuous Galerkin methods for the biharmonic equation. This paper introduces an automatic local selection of the involved stability parameter in terms of the geometry of the underlying triangulation for arbitrary polynomial degrees. The proposed choice ensures a stable discretization with guaranteed discrete ellipticity constant. Numerical evidence for uniform and adaptive mesh-refinement and various polynomial degrees supports the reliability and efficiency of the local parameter selection and recommends this in practice. The approach is documented in 2D for triangles, but the methodology behind can be generalized to higher dimensions, to non-uniform polynomial degrees, and to rectangular discretizations. Two appendices present the realization of our proposed parameter selection in various established finite element software packages as well as a detailed documentation of a self-contained MATLAB program for the lowest-order C 0 interior penalty method.

the definition of the average,

|\nu_{E}|=1
,
and (
14
) verify

\displaystyle\|\langle(\operatorname{D}\mkern-1.0mu^{2}_{\textup{pw}}v_{\textup{IP}}\,\nu_{E})\cdot\nu_{E}\rangle_{E}\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(\operatorname{D}\mkern-1.0mu^{2}_{\textup{pw}}v_{\textup{IP}}\,\nu_{E})\cdot\nu_{E}\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}\leq\|\operatorname{D}\mkern-1.0mu^{2}_{\textup{pw}}v_{\textup{IP}}\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k(k-1)h_{E}}{2|T_{+}|}\|\operatorname{D}\mkern-1.0mu^{2}v_{\textup{IP}}\|_{L^{2}(T_{+})}^{2}.

Consequently,
the multiplication with

h_{E}/(\varepsilon\sigma_{{\textup{IP}},E})

and the definition of the penalty parameter
\sigma_{{\textup{IP}},E}

result in the analogous estimate (
15
)
for
E\in\mathcal{E}(\partial\Omega)
.

The sum of (
15
) over all edges
E\in\mathcal{E}

and the finite overlap of the edge patches

(\omega(E):E\in\mathcal{E})
lead to

\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}}\frac{h_{E}}{\varepsilon\sigma_{{\textup{IP}},E}}\|\langle(\operatorname{D}\mkern-1.0mu^{2}_{\textup{pw}}v_{\textup{IP}}\,\nu_{E})\cdot\nu_{E}\rangle_{E}\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{a\varepsilon}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|v_{\textup{IP}}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|_{\textup{pw}}^{2}.

(16)

The third step
concludes the proof with
a combination of the lower bound (
13
), and
the estimate (
16
) for

\begin{split}\left(1-\kappa-\frac{1}{a\varepsilon}\right)|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|v_{\textup{IP}}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|_{\textup{pw}}^{2}+(1-\kappa-\varepsilon)\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}}\frac{\sigma_{{\textup{IP}},E}}{h_{E}}&\|[\nabla v_{\textup{IP}}\cdot\nu_{E}]_{E}\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}\\
&\hphantom{{}={}}\leq A_{h}(v_{\textup{IP}},v_{\textup{IP}})-\kappa\|v_{\textup{IP}}\|_{h}^{2}.\end{split}

(17)

Every choice of

0<\kappa\leq\min\{1-1/(a\varepsilon),1-\varepsilon\}

leads to a nonnegative lower bound in (
17
), and so proves
the claim (
10
);

\varepsilon=1/\sqrt{a}

results in
\kappa=1-1/\sqrt{a}
.

Conclusions

The numerical experiments validate

The Figures

Appendix B
Direct realization in MATLAB

This subsection discusses the explicit implementation
of the lowest-order C0IP method.
The resulting software
is published online in

]

under GNU general public license
and has been implemented and tested

The self-contained program
comprises less than 230 lines of code and
does not rely on any additional library.
Although,
the generalization to higher polynomial degrees is possible,
its implementation is much more involved.
For the sake of the general accessibility,
the presentation restricts to the lowest-order case.

B.1

The implemented function solely requires
two key data structures that represent the underlying
conforming triangulation
\mathcal{T}
.
The rows of the array

Three vertices form a triangle and their numbers

in

All further geometric information
are computed and documented in the function

The function

of degrees of freedom.
This minimal set of input data structures
enables the application of the C0IP realization
to triangulations from arbitrary mesh generators

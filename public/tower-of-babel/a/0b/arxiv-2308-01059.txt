The Rhie-Chow stabilized Box Method for the Stokes problem
G. Negrini, N. Parolini, M. Verani

Abstract
The Finite Volume method (FVM) is widely adopted in many different applications because of its built-in conservation properties, its ability to deal with arbitrary mesh and its computational efficiency. In this work, we consider the Rhie-Chow stabilized Box Method (RCBM) for the approximation of the Stokes problem. The Box Method (BM) is a piecewise linear Petrov-Galerkin formulation on the Voronoi dual mesh of a Delaunay triangulation, whereas the Rhie-Chow (RC) stabilization is a well known stabilization technique for FVM. The first part of the paper provides a variational formulation of the RC stabilization and discusses the validity of crucial properties relevant for the well-posedeness and convergence of RCBM. Moreover, a numerical exploration of the convergence properties of the method on 2D and 3D test cases is presented. The last part of the paper considers the theoretically justification of the well-posedeness of RCBM and the experimentally observed convergence rates. This latter justification hinges upon suitable assumptions, whose validity is numerically explored.

Introduction

The FVM is a popular numerical strategy for the spatial discretization of partial differential equations widely used for the solution of industrial flow problems. One crucial property of FVM is that, by construction, physical conservation laws governing in a given application are naturally discretized preserving global and local conservation properties. This makes the method very attractive when dealing with problems where conservation plays an important role, such as fluid mechanics and heat and mass transfer. This property is a consequence of the formulation of FVM. In fact, the core procedure of FVM is the imposition of the conservation law on each cell, or control volume, of the mesh
[
14
,
15
]
, which is usually performed using the Gauss theorem and then numerically reconstructing fluxes through each face of the control volume. The conservation properties of the Finite Volume method give raise to robust numerical schemes that work on arbitrarily complex geometries
[
10
]
.

In the present work we consider a particular formulation of the FVM, namely the so-called Box Method (BM) (also known as Finite Volume Element method or piecewise linear FVM). This method has been the object of an intense study in the literature. It was first introduced for scalar elliptic problems in
[
2
,
13
]
and, later, in
[
9
,
22
]
. More recently, a stabilized version of BM has been applied to Stokes system in
[
18
]
.

Two attractive features of BM are the simplicity of the formulation and an elegant relationship with the Finite Element method (FEM) (in this respect, see, e.g.
[
2
,
16
]
), which hinges upon the fact that BM is the “dual method” of the finite element method,
i.e. it consists of a piecewise linear Petrov-Galerkin formulation on the Voronoi dual mesh of a Delaunay triangulation.

In this work, we introduce a variational formulation of the stabilized version of BM to approximate the Stokes problem. This methods turns out to be equivalent to the discretization of the Stokes problem using piecewise linear elements for both velocity and pressure also employing numerical discretization of fluxes, where a suitable stabilization term is added in order to make the discrete problem well-posed. Concerning this latter aspect, we introduce a stabilization term equivalent to the so-called Rhie-Chow interpolation
[
20
,
23
,
11
]
, a common stabilization technique in Finite Volume applications. In the paper, we refer to the resulting stabilized method as to the Rhie-Chow Box Method (RCBM). We first present 2D and 3D numerical results to empirically explore the convergence properties of RBCM, conjecturing that the convergence rate equals the one of the piecewise linear FEM. The solution of these test cases is performed with OpenFOAM, an open-source software widely employed in both industrial and academic CFD applications.
Then we provide a detailed study of the continuity, consistency, coercivity and
inf-sup
stability properties of the RC stabilization. Despite RC stabilization is widely used in the OpenFOAM community, our results seem to be novel. The aforementioned properties are then employed to theoretically study the well-posedeness and convergence properties of RCBM. Also this second aspect seems to be novel in the literature.

More specifically, the outline of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the physical problem in the continuous variational framework. In Section
3
we first introduce the discrete functional setting and the construction of a Voronoi-dual mesh and then we introduce the general form of stabilized Box Methods for the approximation of the Stokes problem. In Section
4
we introduce the Rhie-Chow stabilization term and discuss some crucial properties (consistency, continuity, coercivity and
inf-sup
stability), which will be important to prove the well-posedeness and the convergence of RCBM. In Section
5
we numerically explore the convergence properties of RCBM. Finally, in Section
6
we go through the theoretical analysis to prove the well-posedness and convergence of RCBM, which are obtained under suitable assumptions, whose validity is numerically explored.

The Stokes problem

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\,d=2,3
be a polyhedral bounded domain and let
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
be its boundary. We consider the steady incompressible Stokes problem for a Newtonia fluid:

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta{\textbf{\text{u}}}+\nabla p={\textbf{\text{f}}}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(1)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\textbf{\text{u}}}=0

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle{\textbf{\text{u}}}={\textbf{\text{g}}}

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma=\partial\Omega

where
{\textbf{\text{f}}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}
and
{\textbf{\text{g}}}\in[H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)]^{d}
. Let
\mathcal{V}=H^{1}(\Omega),\,\mathcal{V}_{g}=H^{1}_{\Gamma}=\{v\in\mathcal{V}:\,v=g\text{ on }\Gamma\}
and
\mathcal{Q}=L^{2}(\Omega)
where
g
is a sufficiently regular function. We will denote by a bold symbol the
d
-dimensional counterparts of those spaces, namely,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}=[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{{\textbf{\text{g}}}}=[H^{1}_{\Gamma}]^{d}
. Moreover, from now on, the standard norms for
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
spaces have to be intended on the whole domain
\Omega
where the domain of integration is not specified.

Then, we define the following bilinear forms:

\displaystyle a:\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:

\displaystyle a({\textbf{\text{u}}},{\textbf{\text{v}}})=\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla{\textbf{\text{u}}}:\nabla{\textbf{\text{v}}}\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\forall{\textbf{\text{u}}},{\textbf{\text{v}}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}},

(2)

\displaystyle b:\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}\times\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:

\displaystyle b({\textbf{\text{v}}},p)=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot{\textbf{\text{v}}}\,p\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\forall{\textbf{\text{v}}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}},\forall p\in\mathcal{Q}.

The weak formulation of the problem reads: find
({\textbf{\text{u}}},p)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}\times\mathcal{Q},\,{\textbf{\text{u}}}={\textbf{\text{g}}}\text{ on }\Gamma
, such that

\displaystyle a({\textbf{\text{u}}},{\textbf{\text{v}}})+b({\textbf{\text{v}}},p)

\displaystyle=({\textbf{\text{f}}},{\textbf{\text{v}}})_{\Omega},\quad\forall{\textbf{\text{v}}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{0},

(3)

\displaystyle b({\textbf{\text{u}}},q)

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall q\in\mathcal{Q},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Omega}
is the usual
L^{2}
scalar product on
\Omega
.

We also state the standard well-posedness result
[
1
]
for problem (
3
):

Theorem 2.1

(Well-posedness)
.

The saddle-point problem (
3
) is well-posed if

1.

the bilinear form
a
is continuous and coercive;

2.

the bilinear form
b
is continuous;

3.

the inf-sup condition holds:
\exists\beta>0
s.t.

\inf_{q\in\mathcal{Q}:q\neq 0}\sup_{{\textbf{\text{v}}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}:{\textbf{\text{v}}}\neq{\textbf{\text{0}}}}\cfrac{b({\textbf{\text{v}}},q)}{\left\lVert\nabla{\textbf{\text{v}}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}\geq\beta>0.

(4)

Moreover, the solution
({\textbf{\text{u}}},p)\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}\times\mathcal{Q}
satisfies the following stability estimate:

\left\lVert\nabla{\textbf{\text{u}}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert p\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\left\lVert{\textbf{\text{f}}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert{\textbf{\text{g}}}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\right).

(5)

.

The following observation will be useful in the sequel of the paper.

Property 3.1
.

Let us consider a function
p_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
and recall from Figure
2
the meaning of the segment
d_{ij}
, the face
F_{ij}
and the diamond
D_{ij}
. Moreover, we introduce the subset
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ij}=\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}:T\cap F_{ij}\neq\emptyset\}
of elements of the primal mesh.
By the fact that
p_{h}\in C^{0}(\Omega)
, we have

\left(\nabla p_{h}|_{T}\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\right)|_{d_{ij}}=c_{ij},

for every
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ij}
, where
c_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant value, whose value varies with
F_{ij}
. Now, observing that
{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}
is constant over the face
F_{ij}
and it is aligned with
d_{ij}
yields
(\nabla p_{h}|_{D_{ij}}\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij})|_{F_{ij}}=c_{ij}
. Thus, from now on,
{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}
or
\nabla p_{h}\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}
appearing in a volume integral on the diamond
D_{ij}
, are meant as constant extensions over
D_{ij}
.

In view of the above discussion, the following equalities, that will be repeatedly employed in the sequel of the paper, hold:

\displaystyle\int_{F_{ij}}\nabla p_{h}\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\mathrm{d}s=

\displaystyle\left|F_{ij}\right|\nabla p_{h}\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}=\cfrac{\left|F_{ij}\right|}{\left|D_{ij}\right|}\int_{D_{ij}}\nabla p_{h}\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{1}{\left|D_{ij}\right|}\int_{F_{ij}}\int_{D_{ij}}\nabla p_{h}\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s.

In the sequel we will work under the following assumption on the regularity of the computational mesh.

Assumption 3.1

(Mesh regularity)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{h}
be the Voronoi-type dual mesh of a Delaunay triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}
such that

\exists\delta>0:h_{m}=\delta h,\qquad h_{m}=\min_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}.

Moreover, we assume that mesh size does not change too much between neighbouring boxes. Hence
\forall B_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{h}
and
\forall F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
, for some
T:T\cap B_{i}\neq\emptyset
,

d_{ij}\simeq h_{T},\quad\left|F_{ij}\right|\simeq h_{T}^{d-1},\quad\left|B_{i}\right|\simeq h_{T}^{d},\quad.

Moreover,
\exists C_{1},C_{2}>0:\,C_{1}h\leq h_{T}\leq C_{2}h,\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

Remark 3.1
.

The primal mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is assumed to be a Delaunay triangulation, i.e. no vertex of the triangulation is inside the circumcircle of any triangle of
\mathcal{T}_{h}

Under these assumptions, the Box method and the classical FVM will present similar features.

\displaystyle=\left\{w_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega):w_{h}|_{B_{i}}\in\mathbb{P}^{0}(B_{i}),\,\forall B_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{h}\right\},

(6)

\varphi_{i}

\Pi_{h}\phi_{i}=\Pi_{h}\phi_{h}|_{B_{i}},\quad\forall\phi_{h}\in{\mathcal{V}_{h}}.

(7)

\displaystyle\left|q_{h}\right|_{*}\leq

\displaystyle C\left|q_{h}\right|_{H^{1}},

\displaystyle\left|q_{h}\right|_{H^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left|q_{h}\right|_{*},

\displaystyle\left\lVert q_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\left|q_{h}\right|_{*},

\displaystyle 2h_{m}^{-1}\left\lVert\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

In particular, the

(9)

\forall({\textbf{\text{v}}}_{h},q_{h})\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h}\times\mathcal{V}_{h}

Remark 3.2

Let

We then introduce the following discrete bilinear forms:

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{B}({\textbf{\text{v}}}_{h},{\textbf{\text{w}}}_{h}){:{\kern-2.15277pt=}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{B_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{h}}\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}\nu\cfrac{\left|F_{ij}\right|}{d_{ij}}\left[\Pi_{h}{\textbf{\text{v}}}_{j}-\Pi_{h}{\textbf{\text{v}}}_{i}\right]\cdot{\textbf{\text{w}}}_{h},

By the fact that the discrete functions are piecewise linear on the primal mesh and given the orthogonality of the dual mesh, we have

\displaystyle\forall{\textbf{\text{v}}}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h},

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{B}({\textbf{\text{u}}}_{B},\Pi_{h}q_{h})+\tilde{s}_{B}(p_{B},\Pi_{h}q_{h})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\forall q_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h},

.
Introducing the compact form

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{B}(({\textbf{\text{u}}}_{B},p_{B}),(\Pi_{h}{\textbf{\text{v}}}_{h},\Pi_{h}q_{h}))=a_{B}({\textbf{\text{u}}}_{B},\Pi_{h}{\textbf{\text{v}}}_{h})+\tilde{b}_{B}(\Pi_{h}{\textbf{\text{v}}}_{h},p_{B})+\tilde{c}_{B}({\textbf{\text{u}}}_{B},\Pi_{h}q_{h})+\tilde{s}_{B}(p_{B},\Pi_{h}q_{h}),

(19)

the problem above can be rewritten as

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{B}(({\textbf{\text{u}}}_{B},p_{B}),(\Pi_{h}{\textbf{\text{v}}}_{h},\Pi_{h}q_{h}))=({\textbf{\text{f}}},\Pi_{h}{\textbf{\text{v}}}_{h})

(20)

\forall({\textbf{\text{v}}}_{h},q_{h})\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h}\times\mathcal{V}_{h}
.

4
The Rhie-Chow stabilization

In this section we introduce the Rhie-Chow (RC) stabilization to be employed in the stabilized Box Method (
18
) giving rise to the Rhie-Chow stabilized Box Method (RCBM). Moreover, we study crucial properties of the RC stabilization (consistency, continuity, coercivity,
inf-sup
stability) that will be important for the well-posedeness and convergence of RCBM.

Let
N
be the number of vertices
p
,
N_{u}=dN
the number of d.o.f of velocity and
N_{p}=N
the number of d.o.f. of pressure,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h}:\displaystyle\left\{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{N_{u}},\qquad\mathcal{V}_{h}:\hfil\hfil\displaystyle\left\{\phi_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{N_{p}}.

(21)

For space
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}}_{h},\mathcal{W}_{h}
the basis are the projections on boxes of the ones of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h},\mathcal{V}_{h}
.

Then, we write
{\textbf{\text{u}}}_{B}
and
p_{B}
as linear combinations of the basis functions:

{\textbf{\text{u}}}_{B}({\textbf{\text{x}}})=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{u}}u_{j}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}({\textbf{\text{x}}}),\qquad p_{B}({\textbf{\text{x}}})=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{p}}p_{j}\phi_{j}({\textbf{\text{x}}}).

(22)

To find the expressions of the matrices of the problem, A, B and
{\text{B}}^{\intercal}
, we plug the expressions for
{\textbf{\text{u}}}_{B}
and
p_{B}
into the bilinear forms setting,
{\textbf{\text{w}}}_{h}=\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{i}
and
w_{h}=\Pi_{h}\phi_{i}
, where the operator
\Pi_{h}
acts component-wise.

\displaystyle(\text{A}{\textbf{\text{u}}})_{i}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} =&\sum_{j}\text{A}_{ij}{\textbf{\text{u}}}_{j}=\tilde{a}_{B}({\textbf{\text{u}}}_{B},{\textbf{\text{w}}}_{h})=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{u}}u_{j}\tilde{a}_{B}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j},\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{i})\\
=&-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{u}}u_{j}\sum_{B_{k}\in G_{j}}\nu\cfrac{\left|F_{jk}\right|}{d_{jk}}\left[\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{k}-\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}\right]\cdot\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{i}\\
=&u_{i}\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}\nu\cfrac{\left|F_{ij}\right|}{d_{ij}}-\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}u_{j}\nu\cfrac{\left|F_{ij}\right|}{d_{ij}},\end{aligned}

(23a)

\displaystyle(\text{B}^{\intercal}{\textbf{\text{p}}})_{i}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} =&\sum_{j}\text{B}^{\intercal}_{ij}{\textbf{\text{p}}}_{j}=\tilde{b}_{B}({\textbf{\text{w}}}_{h},p_{B})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}p_{j}\tilde{b}_{B}(\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{i},\phi_{j})\\
=&\sum_{j=1}^{N}p_{j}\sum_{B_{k}\in G_{j}}\left|F_{jk}\right|\left[w_{ij}\Pi_{h}\phi_{j}+(1-w_{ij})\Pi_{h}\phi_{k}\right]{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{jk}\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}_{i}\\
=&p_{i}\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}\left|F_{ij}\right|w_{ij}{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\cdot{\textbf{\text{e}}}_{i}+\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}p_{j}\left|F_{ij}\right|(1-w_{ij}){\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\cdot{\textbf{\text{e}}}_{i},\end{aligned}

(23b)

\displaystyle(\text{B}{\textbf{\text{u}}})_{i}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} =&\sum_{j}\text{B}_{ij}{\textbf{\text{u}}}_{j}=\tilde{c}_{B}({\textbf{\text{u}}}_{B},w_{h})=\sum_{j=1}^{3N}u_{j}\tilde{c}_{B}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j},\Pi_{h}\phi_{i})\\
=&\sum_{j=1}^{3N}u_{j}\sum_{B_{k}\in G_{j}}\left|F_{jk}\right|\left[w_{ij}\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}+(1-w_{ij})\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{k}\right]\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{jk}\Pi_{h}\phi_{i}\\
=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1+(l-1)N,\\
\,l=1,\dots,d\end{subarray}}^{lN}u_{i}\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}\left|F_{ij}\right|w_{ij}{\textbf{\text{e}}}_{l}\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}+\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}u_{j}\left|F_{ij}\right|(1-w_{ij}){\textbf{\text{e}}}_{l}\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}.\end{aligned}

(23c)

Using equations (
23
), the algebraic linear system associated to problem (
18
), considering
s_{B}=0
, reads:

\left[\begin{matrix}\text{A}&\text{B}^{\intercal}\\
\text{B}&0\\
\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}{\textbf{\text{u}}}\\
{\textbf{\text{p}}}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}{\textbf{\text{F}}}\\
{\textbf{\text{0}}}\end{matrix}\right]

(24)

where
F
is the discretization of the right-hand-side.

For the solution of system (
24
) we consider the SIMPLE (Semi-Implicit Pressure Linked Equation) method
[
17
]
, that is a splitting algorithm for the solution of Stokes and Navier-Stokes problems. We introduce the additive splitting A = D-H, where D is the diagonal of A and -H is the off-diagonal part. Inverting the system with respect to its diagonal, we obtain the following expression for
u
:

{\textbf{\text{u}}}=\text{D}^{-1}\left[\text{H}{\textbf{\text{u}}}-\text{B}^{\intercal}{\textbf{\text{p}}}+{\textbf{\text{F}}}\right]=\tilde{{\textbf{\text{u}}}}-\text{D}^{-1}\text{B}^{\intercal}{\textbf{\text{p}}},

where
\tilde{{\textbf{\text{u}}}}=\text{D}^{-1}\left[\text{H}{\textbf{\text{u}}}+{\textbf{\text{F}}}\right]
, and we substitute it in the second equation of system (
24
) to obtain

\text{B}\tilde{{\textbf{\text{u}}}}-\text{B}\text{D}^{-1}\text{B}^{\intercal}{\textbf{\text{p}}}={\textbf{\text{0}}}.

The term
\text{B}\text{D}^{-1}\text{B}^{\intercal}{\textbf{\text{p}}}
is the algebraic counterpart of a Laplacian problem for the pressure where the diffusivity coefficient is
\text{D}^{-1}
, piecewise constant on boxes. This co-located discretization procedure is known to generate spurious pressure modes
[
11
]
because the diagonal dominance of the algebraic system is no more ensured, due to the too large bandwidth of the matrix. To stabilize the problem we employ the so called
Rhie-Chow interpolation

[
11
,
23
,
15
]
, that basically substitutes the term
\text{B}\text{D}^{-1}\text{B}^{\intercal}{\textbf{\text{p}}}
with the discretization of a pressure Laplacian, using
\text{D}^{-1}
as a diffusion. Thus, the discrete form of Rhie-Chow stabilization reads

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{B}(p_{B},z_{h})=

\displaystyle\sum_{B_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{h}}\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}\left|F_{ij}\right|\Biggl[w_{ij}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\sum_{B_{k}\in G_{i}}\left|F_{ik}\right|\left(w_{ik}\Pi_{h}p_{i}+(1-w_{ik})\Pi_{h}p_{k}\right){\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ik}

(25)

\displaystyle+(1-w_{ij})\text{D}^{-1}_{j}\sum_{B_{k}\in G_{j}}\left|F_{jk}\right|\left(w_{jk}\Pi_{h}p_{j}+(1-w_{jk})\Pi_{h}p_{k}\right){\textbf{\text{n}}}_{jk}\Biggr]\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}z_{h}

\displaystyle-\sum_{B_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{h}}\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}\left|F_{ij}\right|\left[w_{ij}\left|B_{i}\right|\text{D}_{i}^{-1}+(1-w_{ij})\left|B_{j}\right|\text{D}^{-1}_{j}\right]\cfrac{\Pi_{h}p_{j}-\Pi_{h}p_{i}}{{d_{ij}}}z_{h}.

Upon introducing for any
q_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
the Gauss-Green gradient
\nabla_{h,i}q_{h}
of
q_{h}
evaluated on box
i
:

\left|B_{i}\right|\nabla_{h,i}q_{h}:=\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}\left|F_{ij}\right|{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left(w_{ij}\Pi_{h}q_{i}+(1-w_{ij})\Pi_{h}q_{j}\right)

we can rewrite the Rhie-Chow stabilization operator
\tilde{s}_{B}:\mathcal{V}_{h}\times\mathcal{W}_{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as follows

\tilde{s}_{B}(q_{h},z_{h})=\tilde{s}_{B}^{1}(q,z_{h})-\tilde{s}_{B}^{2}(q,z_{h}),

(26)

where
\tilde{s}_{B}^{1}
and
\tilde{s}_{B}^{2}
can be written explicitly as

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{B}^{1}(q_{h},z_{h})=

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}\left(w_{ij}\text{D}_{i}^{-1}\left|B_{i}\right|\nabla_{h,i}q_{h}+(1-w_{ij})\text{D}_{j}^{-1}\left|B_{i}\right|\nabla_{h,j}p_{h}\right)\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left[\!\left[z_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s,

(27)

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{B}^{2}(q_{h},z_{h})=

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}\left(w_{ij}\left|B_{i}\right|\text{D}^{-1}_{i}+(1-w_{ij})\left|B_{j}\right|\text{D}^{-1}_{j}\right)\cfrac{\Pi_{h}q_{j}-\Pi_{h}q_{i}}{d_{ij}}\left[\!\left[z_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s,

where
\left|B_{i}\right|
is the measure of box
B_{i}
,
\text{D}_{i}
are the diagonal coefficients of matrix A. We also define the Auxiliary Rhie-Chow stabilization operator, acting on continuous functions:

\displaystyle s_{B}:H^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{W}_{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:

\displaystyle s_{B}(q,z_{h})=s_{B}^{1}(q,z_{h})-s_{B}^{2}(q,z_{h}),

(28)

where
s_{B}^{1}
and
s_{B}^{2}
can be written explicitly as

\displaystyle s_{B}^{1}(q,z_{h})=

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}\left(w_{ij}\text{D}_{i}^{-1}\int_{B_{i}}\nabla q\mathrm{d}x+(1-w_{ij})\text{D}_{j}^{-1}\int_{B_{j}}\nabla q\mathrm{d}x\right)\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left[\!\left[z_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s,

(29)

\displaystyle s_{B}^{2}(q,z_{h})=

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}\left(w_{ij}\left|B_{i}\right|\text{D}^{-1}_{i}+(1-w_{ij})\left|B_{j}\right|\text{D}^{-1}_{j}\right)\frac{\partial q}{\partial{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}}\left[\!\left[z_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s.

Remark 4.1
.

The two forms
s_{B}^{1}
and
s_{B}^{2}
have a precise meaning, in particular, the algebraic representation of
s_{B}^{1}
is
\text{B}\text{D}^{-1}\text{B}^{\intercal}{\textbf{\text{p}}}
and
s^{2}
is the scalar counterpart of bilinear form
\tilde{a}_{B}
with
\text{D}^{-1}
as a diffusion.

Remark 4.2
.

In
s_{B}^{1}
, the gradient on a box is computed using the Gauss-Green theorem. Following the discretization rules used for equations (
17
), then for
q_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
we have:

\displaystyle\left|B_{i}\right|\nabla_{h,i}q_{h}\simeq\int_{\partial B_{i}}q_{h}{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{i}\mathrm{d}s=\int_{B_{i}}\nabla\cdot(q_{h}\text{I})\mathrm{d}x=\int_{B_{i}}\nabla q_{h}\mathrm{d}x,

(30)

where I is the identity matrix.

4.1
Properties of the Rhie-Chow stabilization

We now discuss some important properties of the Rhie-Chow stabilization that will be employed to prove well-posedness and convergence of problem (
18
). In particular we discuss
consistency, continuity, coercivity and
inf-sup
stability.

Consistency

The consistency of Rhie-Chow stabilization is given by:

(s_{B}-\tilde{s}_{B})(p_{B},\Pi_{h}q_{h})\lesssim h^{\frac{3}{2}}\left|p_{B}\right|_{H^{1}}\left|q_{h}\right|_{*}

(31)

where
p_{B},q_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
.

To prove it, let us observe that, in view of the definitions of
s_{B}
and
\tilde{s}_{B}
, we have

\displaystyle(s_{B}-\tilde{s}_{B})(p_{B},\Pi_{h}q_{h})=

\displaystyle(s^{1}_{B}-\tilde{s}^{1}_{B})(p_{B},\Pi_{h}q_{h})-(s^{2}_{B}-\tilde{s}^{2}_{B})(p_{B},\Pi_{h}q_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I)+(II).

The second term
(II)=0
by the fact that

\cfrac{\Pi_{h}p_{j}-\Pi_{h}p_{i}}{d_{ij}}=\frac{\partial p_{B}}{\partial{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}}.

On the other hand, we can write
(I)
as

\displaystyle(I)=

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}\left(w_{ij}\text{D}_{i}^{-1}\int_{B_{i}}(\nabla p_{B}-\nabla_{h,i}p_{B})\mathrm{d}x\right.

(32)

\displaystyle\left.+(1-w_{ij})\text{D}_{j}^{-1}\int_{B_{j}}(\nabla p_{B}-\nabla_{h,j}p_{B})\mathrm{d}x\right)\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left[\!\left[q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s.

Without loss of generality, we treat only the case of the first addendum, the second term being similar. Let us first desume that, under Assumption
3.1
,
\text{D}^{-1}_{i}
scales as
h_{T}^{2-d}
, indeed we have

\displaystyle\text{D}_{i}=\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}\cfrac{\left|F_{ij}\right|}{d_{ij}}\simeq\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}h_{T}^{d-2},

(33)

\displaystyle\cfrac{h_{T}^{2-d}}{N_{B_{i}}}\lesssim\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\lesssim\cfrac{h_{T}^{2-d}}{N_{B_{i}}}.

where
N_{B_{i}}
denotes the number of faces of box
B_{i}
(c.f. Section
3
).

Considering the face barycentres
{\textbf{\text{f}}}_{ij}
, since
p_{B}
is piecewise linear, the following identity holds:

w_{ij}\Pi_{h}p_{i}+(1-w_{ij})\Pi_{h}p_{j}=p_{B}({\textbf{\text{f}}}_{ij}),

(34)

where we employed notation (
7
). Let now
x
be a point on the face
F_{ij}
. As
p_{B}
is piecewise linear, using a Taylor expansion around
{\textbf{\text{f}}}_{ij}
we have:

\displaystyle p_{B}({\textbf{\text{x}}})=

\displaystyle p_{B}({\textbf{\text{f}}}_{ij})+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}:T\cap D_{ij}\neq\emptyset}({\textbf{\text{x}}}-{\textbf{\text{f}}}_{ij})\cdot\nabla p_{B}\mathcal{X}_{T\cap D_{ij}},

(35)

where
\mathcal{X}
is the indicator function and
\nabla p_{B}
is piecewise constant on each intersection between triangle
T
and diamond
D_{ij}
(c.f. Figure
2
).

Using equations (
34
) and (
35
), the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Assumption
3.1
, inequality (
33
), the Inverse Trace inequality (c.f. Lemma
A.4
) and the Hölder inequality, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}

\displaystyle w_{ij}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\left(\int_{B_{i}}\nabla p_{B}-\left|B_{i}\right|\nabla_{h,i}p_{B})\right)\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}w_{ij}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\left(\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}\int_{F_{ij}}{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left(p_{B}-w_{ij}\Pi_{h}p_{i}-(1-w_{ij})\Pi_{h}p_{j}\right)\mathrm{d}s\right)\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}w_{ij}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\left(\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}\int_{F_{ij}}{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}({\textbf{\text{x}}}-{\textbf{\text{f}}}_{ij})\cdot\nabla p_{B}\mathrm{d}s\right)\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(h_{T}^{d-1}\cfrac{h_{T}^{4-2d}}{N_{B_{i}}}\left(\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}\int_{F_{ij}}{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}({\textbf{\text{x}}}-{\textbf{\text{f}}}_{ij})\cdot\nabla p_{B}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{F_{ij}}\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\cfrac{h_{T}^{3-d}}{N_{B_{i}}}\left(\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}\int_{F_{ij}}\left|{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)\left(\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}\int_{F_{ij}}\left|{\textbf{\text{x}}}-{\textbf{\text{f}}}_{ij}\right|^{2}\left|\nabla p_{B}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{F_{ij}}\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(N_{B_{i}}\cfrac{h_{T}^{3-d}}{N_{B_{i}}}h_{T}^{d-1}h_{T}^{2}\left(\sum_{B_{j}\in G_{i}}\left\lVert\nabla p_{B}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(F_{ij})}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{F_{ij}}\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(C^{2}_{inv}N_{B_{i}}h_{T}^{4}h_{T}^{-2}\left\lVert\nabla p_{B}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{i})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}h_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(d_{ij}\int_{F_{ij}}\left|\frac{\partial q_{h}}{\partial{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}C_{inv}\sqrt{N_{B_{i}}}h_{T}^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\left\lVert\nabla p_{B}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{i})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(d_{ij}\int_{F_{ij}}\left|\frac{\partial q_{h}}{\partial{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{inv}\sqrt{N_{B}}h^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left\lVert\nabla p_{B}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{i})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}d_{ij}\int_{F_{ij}}\left|\frac{\partial q_{h}}{\partial{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle C_{inv}\sqrt{N_{B}}h^{\frac{3}{2}}\left|p_{B}\right|_{H^{1}}\left|q_{h}\right|_{*}.

Continuity

In the following, we discuss the continuity of the Auxiliary Rhie-Chow operator (
28
)
and the continuity of the Rhie-Chow operator (
26
).

For what concerns the continuity of the Auxiliary Rhie-Chow operator, we distinguish two cases, depending on the first entry of the bilinear form
s_{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
: (a) infinite dimensional, (b) finite dimensional.
Let us first consider the infinite dimensional case, namely we aim at proving the following

s_{B}(p,\Pi_{h}q_{h})\lesssim h^{\frac{3}{2}}\left|\nabla p\right|_{h,1}\left|q_{h}\right|_{T,*}

(36)

where
p\in\mathcal{Q}\cap H^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
and
q_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
.

To prove it, using equations (
28
) and (
29
), we have

\displaystyle s_{B}(p,\Pi_{h}q_{h})=

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}\left(w_{ij}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\int_{B_{i}}\nabla p\mathrm{d}x+(1-w_{ij})\text{D}^{-1}_{j}\int_{B_{j}}\nabla p\mathrm{d}x\right)\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}\left(w_{ij}\left|B_{i}\right|\text{D}^{-1}_{i}+(1-w_{ij})\left|B_{j}\right|\text{D}^{-1}_{j}\right)\frac{\partial p}{\partial{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}}\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}w_{ij}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\left(\int_{B_{i}}\nabla p\mathrm{d}x-\left|B_{i}\right|\nabla p\right)\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}(1-w_{ij})\text{D}^{-1}_{j}\left(\int_{B_{j}}\nabla p\mathrm{d}x-\left|B_{j}\right|\nabla p\right)\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s.

Without loss of generality, we treat only the case of the first addendum, the second term being similar. Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the Inverse trace inequality (c.f. Lemma
A.4
), Assumption
3.1
, the Poincarè inequality (c.f. Lemma
A.3
), the Hölder inequality and standard interpolation error estimates, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}

\displaystyle\cfrac{1}{2}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\left(\int_{B_{i}}\nabla p\mathrm{d}x-\left|B_{i}\right|\nabla p\right)\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\int_{F_{ij}}\cfrac{1}{4}\text{D}^{-2}_{i}\left(\int_{B_{i}}\nabla p\mathrm{d}x-\left|B_{i}\right|\nabla p\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{F_{ij}}d_{ij}^{2}\cfrac{\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}^{2}}{d_{ij}^{2}}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\left|B_{i}\right|\left\lVert\cfrac{1}{\left|B_{i}\right|}\int_{B_{i}}\nabla p\mathrm{d}x-\nabla p\right\rVert_{L^{2}(F_{ij})}\sqrt{d_{ij}}\left(d_{ij}\int_{F_{ij}}\cfrac{\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}^{2}}{d_{ij}^{2}}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}&h_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\left|B_{i}\right|\\
&C_{\text{inv}}\left[h_{T}^{-1}\left\lVert\cfrac{1}{\left|B_{i}\right|}\int_{B_{i}}\nabla p\mathrm{d}x-\nabla p\right\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{i})}+h_{T}\left|\cfrac{1}{\left|B_{i}\right|}\int_{B_{i}}\nabla p\mathrm{d}x-\nabla p\right|_{H^{1}(B_{i})}\right]\\
&\left(d_{ij}\int_{F_{ij}}\cfrac{\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}^{2}}{d_{ij}^{2}}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}C_{\text{inv}}h_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\left|B_{i}\right|\left[C_{d}h_{T}^{-1}h_{T}\left|\nabla p\right|_{H^{1}(B_{i})}+h_{T}\left|\nabla p\right|_{H^{1}(B_{i})}\right]\left(d_{ij}\int_{F_{ij}}\cfrac{\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}^{2}}{d_{ij}^{2}}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}C_{\text{inv}}C_{1}(C_{d}+h_{T})h_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}h_{T}^{2-d}h_{T}^{d}\left|\nabla p\right|_{H^{1}(B_{i})}\left(d_{ij}\int_{F_{ij}}\cfrac{\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}^{2}}{d_{ij}^{2}}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\text{inv}}C_{1}C_{d}h^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left|\nabla p\right|_{H^{1}(B_{i})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}d_{ij}\int_{F_{ij}}\cfrac{\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}^{2}}{d_{ij}^{2}}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle C_{\text{inv}}C_{1}C_{d}h^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}:T\cap B_{i}\neq\emptyset}\left|\nabla p\right|_{H^{1}(T)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|q_{h}\right|_{T,*}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle C_{\text{inv}}C_{1}C_{d}h^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(N_{B_{i}}\sum_{B_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{h}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}:T\cap B_{i}\neq\emptyset}\left|\nabla p\right|_{H^{1}(T)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|q_{h}\right|_{T,*}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle C_{\text{inv}}C_{1}C_{d}\sqrt{N_{B}}h^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left|\nabla p\right|_{H^{1}(T)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|q_{h}\right|_{T,*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle{\displaystyle C_{\text{inv}}C_{1}C_{d}\sqrt{N_{B}}h^{\frac{3}{2}}\left|\nabla p\right|_{h,1}\left|q_{h}\right|_{T,*}}

where we recall that
\left|\cdot\right|_{h,1}
denotes the broken
H^{1}
-norm on the elements of the primal mesh and where we employed the definition (
10
).

On the other hand, for the finite dimensional case, we prove the following continuity property for the Auxiliary Rhie-Chow operator:

s_{B}(p_{h},\Pi_{h}q_{h})\lesssim h^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|p_{h}\right|_{H^{1}}\left|q_{h}\right|_{T,*}

(37)

for any
p_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
.
From the above computations, we have:

\displaystyle s_{B}(p_{h},\Pi_{h}q_{h})=

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}w_{ij}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\left(\int_{B_{i}}\nabla p_{h}\mathrm{d}x-\left|B_{i}\right|\nabla p_{h}\right)\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s

(38)

\displaystyle+\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}(1-w_{ij})\text{D}^{-1}_{j}\left(\int_{B_{j}}\nabla p_{h}\mathrm{d}x-\left|B_{j}\right|\nabla p_{h}\right)\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s.

Also in this case, without loss of generality, we treat only the case of the first addendum, the second term being similar.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Property
3.1
, Assumption
3.1
and the Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}

\displaystyle w_{ij}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\left(\int_{B_{i}}\nabla p_{h}\mathrm{d}x-\left|B_{i}\right|\nabla p_{h}\right)\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s

(39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}w_{ij}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\left(\int_{B_{i}}\nabla p_{h}\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\mathrm{d}x-\cfrac{\left|B_{i}\right|}{\left|D_{ij}\right|}\int_{D_{ij}}\nabla p_{h}\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\mathrm{d}x\right)\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}w_{ij}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\int_{B_{i}}\left|\nabla p_{h}\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\right|\mathrm{d}x\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\sum_{F_{ij}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\int_{F_{ij}}w_{ij}\text{D}^{-1}_{i}\cfrac{\left|B_{i}\right|}{\left|D_{ij}\right|}\int_{D_{ij}}\left|\nabla p_{h}\cdot{\textbf{\text{n}}}_{ij}\right|\mathrm{d}x\left[\!\left[\Pi_{h}q_{h}\right]\!\right]_{ij}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle h^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|p_{h}\right|_{H^{1}}\left|q_{h}\right|_{T,*}.

6.2
Convergence analysis

The convergence of the RCBM is guaranteed by the following theorem.

Theorem 6.2

(Convergence)
.

Let
\Omega
be of class
C^{2}
and let
{\textbf{\text{f}}}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}
.
Let
({\textbf{\text{u}}},p)\in(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}\cap[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d})\times(\mathcal{Q}\cap H^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega))
the solution to problem (
3
). Then

\left\lVert{\textbf{\text{u}}}_{B}-{\textbf{\text{u}}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert p_{B}-p\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim h.

Proof.

Let us define
I{\textbf{\text{u}}},Ip\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}}_{h}\times\mathcal{V}_{h}
be the Lagrangian linear interpolations of the exact solutions
{\textbf{\text{u}}},p
, respectively. Define also
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}={\textbf{\text{u}}}-I{\textbf{\text{u}}},\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B}=I{\textbf{\text{u}}}-{\textbf{\text{u}}}_{B}
and
\eta=p-Ip,\,\eta_{B}=Ip-p_{B}
. Employing Lemma
6.4
we have

\displaystyle\left|\eta_{B}\right|_{T,*}^{2}+\nu\left|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B}\right|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{B}((\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B},\eta_{B}),(\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B},\Pi_{h}\eta_{B}))\pm b_{B}(\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B},p)

(61)

\displaystyle\pm c_{B}({\textbf{\text{u}}},\Pi_{h}\eta_{B})\pm s_{B}(p,\Pi_{h}\eta_{B})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{B}((\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\eta),(\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B},\Pi_{h}\eta_{B}))+(\tilde{b}_{B}-b_{B})(\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B},Ip)

\displaystyle+(\tilde{c}_{B}-c_{B})(I{\textbf{\text{u}}},\Pi_{h}\eta_{B})+(\tilde{s}_{B}-s_{B})(Ip,\Pi_{h}\eta_{B})

\displaystyle+\mathcal{C}_{B}(({\textbf{\text{u}}},p),(\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B},\Pi_{h}\eta_{B}))-\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{B}(({\textbf{\text{u}}}_{B},p_{B}),(\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B},\Pi_{h}\eta_{B})).

Now, using the continuity of
\mathcal{C}_{B}
(Lemma
6.2
) on the first term, equation (
79
) on second and third terms and consistency (Lemma
6.1
) on the fourth and fifth terms, we get the following:

\displaystyle\left|\eta_{B}\right|_{T,*}^{2}+\nu\left|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B}\right|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{B}((\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B},\eta_{B}),(\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B},\Pi_{h}\eta_{B}))

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\left|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\right|_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\eta\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+h\left|\nabla\eta\right|_{h,1}\right){{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B},\eta_{B}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}_{box}

\displaystyle+h\left|Ip\right|_{H^{1}}\left|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B}\right|_{H^{1}}+\left(h^{2}\left\lVert{\textbf{\text{f}}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+h\left|p_{B}\right|_{H^{1}}+h^{\frac{3}{2}}\left|\nabla p\right|_{h,1}\right){{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B},\eta_{B}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}_{box}.

is the broken Hessian operator, equation (

\displaystyle\left|\eta_{B}\right|_{T,*}^{2}+\nu\left|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B}\right|_{H^{1}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\left|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\right|_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\eta\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+h\right){{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B},\eta_{B}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}_{box}

(63)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}\,{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{B},\eta_{B}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}_{box}

and

Now employing equation (

7
Conclusions

In this work, we considered the Rhie-Chow stabilized Box Method (RCBM) for the numerical approximation of the Stokes problem. In particular, the Rhie-Chow stabilization, a well-known stabilization technique for FVM, has been employed to stabilize the classical Box Method.
In the first part of the paper we provided a variational formulation of the RC stabilization and discussed the validity of crucial properties relevant for the well-posedeness and convergence of RCBM. Then we numerically explored the convergence properties of the RCBM on 2D and 3D test cases. Finally, in the last part of the paper, we theoretically justified the well posedeness of RCBM and the experimentally observed convergence rates. To tackle this latter issue we built upon some assumptions, whose validity has been numerically explored.

The results contained in this work can be potentially extended to other relevant differential problems in the context of computational fluid dynamics, like the Navier-Stokes equations
[
21
]
and the non-Newtonian Navier-Stokes equations, where the main difficulty is represented by the nonlinear nature of the problem. Moreover, the study of upwind-based schemes, that are more suitable for advection dominated problems, can be potentially faced in the present framework. Ultimately, the analysis of the Box method contained in this work sets a base workflow to theoretically deal, through the lens of the variational framework, with the Finite Volume methods actually implemented in OpenFOAM, the leading open-source software for CFD in industrial applications.

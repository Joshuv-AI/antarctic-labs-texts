Topological Linear System Identification via Moderate Deviations Theory
Wouter Jongeneel, Tobias Sutter, Daniel Kuhn

Abstract
Two dynamical systems are topologically equivalent when their phase-portraits can be morphed into each other by a homeomorphic coordinate transformation on the state space. The induced equivalence classes capture qualitative properties such as stability or the oscillatory nature of the state trajectories, for example. In this paper we develop a method to learn the topological class of an unknown stable system from a single trajectory of finitely many state observations. Using a moderate deviations principle for the least squares estimator of the unknown system matrix θ \theta , we prove that the probability of misclassification decays exponentially with the number of observations at a rate that is proportional to the square of the smallest singular value of θ \theta .



I
Introduction

We consider the discrete-time linear time-invariant system

x_{t+1}=\theta x_{t}+w_{t},\quad x_{0}\sim\nu,

(1)

where
x_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

. Except for asymptotic stability we assume that nothing is known about

\theta

\widehat{\theta}_{T}=\left(\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widehat{x}_{t}\widehat{x}_{t-1}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)\left(\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widehat{x}_{t-1}\widehat{x}_{t-1}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)^{-1},

(2)

\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
.
It is therefore possible that
\widehat{\theta}_{T}
is unstable even though
\theta

[

2

3

4

, you predict a damper but get a spring.
Capturing the correct qualitative behaviour in the scalar case translates to enforcing stability and to the need of estimating the sign of

Contributions.

II-A

Topological equivalence of linear dynamical systems

This section is mainly inspired by the work of Kuiper and Robbin

Definition II.1

(Topological equivalence)
.

Two linear time-one maps
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
and

Recall that a homeomorphism is a continuous bijection with a continjous inverse. We say that two dynamical systems are

if their time-one maps are topologically equivalent in the sense of Definition
II.1
. If the two dynamical systems are noise-free (
i.e.
, the time-one maps alone determine the dynamics), then they are topologically equivalent if there is a homeomorphism mapping the trajectories of one system onto those of the other
[
19
]
,
[
20
, Chapter 2]
.
This means that the state trajectories of topologically equivalent dynamical systems are

identical. To see this, consider two time-one maps

and

and

and

are topologically equivalent. In this case

Definition II.2

(Orientation)
.

We call a linear invertible time-one map
f(x)=Fx
orientation-preserving if
\mathrm{det}(F)>0
, and we denote by
\mathrm{or}(f)
the sign of
\mathrm{det}(F)
.

Note that
\mathsf{GL}^{+}(n,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathsf{GL}^{-}(n,\mathbb{R})
represent the components of
\mathsf{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
that contain all invertible matrices
F
whose associated time-one maps
f(x)=Fx
satisfy
\mathrm{or}(f)=1
and
\mathrm{or}(f)=-1
, respectively. Note also that the sign of the (signed) volume of the unit hypercube is invariant under an orientation-preserving map. For additional information on the concept of orientation see for example
[
21
, Chapter 6]

The orientation of a map is also known to be a
topological invariant
, that is, if two invertible maps
f
and
g
are topologically equivalent, then
\mathrm{or}(f)=\mathrm{or}(g)

[
22

Lemma II.3

(Topological equivalence of asymptotically stable systems
[
19
, Theorem 9.2]
)
.

Assume that

and

are asymptotically stable linear isomorphisms on

. Then, we have

if and only if there exists a continuous path

in
\mathsf{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
parametrized in

with
X(0)=F
and
X(1)=G
.

As
\mathsf{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
can be decomposed into
\mathsf{GL}^{+}(n,\mathbb{R})

\mathsf{GL}^{-}(n,\mathbb{R})

F
and
G

\mathrm{or}(F)
of an invertible matrix

Moderate deviations theory

Throughout the paper we assume that all random objects are defined on a measurable space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
equipped with a probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
parametrized by the (asymptotically stable) system matrix

Assumption II.4

(Linear system)
.

The following hold.

(i)

The system (
1

\theta\in\Theta
.

(ii)

For each
\theta\in\Theta

are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) and independent of

under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[w_{t}]=0

Assumption

S_{\theta}=\theta S_{\theta}\theta^{\mathsf{T}}+S_{w},

(3)

[
24

I:\Theta^{\prime}\times\Theta\rightarrow[0,\infty]

(4)

to quantify the difference between an estimator realization

and the system matrix

; see
[
4
]
.
Here, the invariant state covariance matrix

is defined as in (

Definition II.5

(Rate function)
.

We call
I:\Theta^{\prime}\times\Theta\rightarrow[0,\infty]
a rate function if it is lower semi-continuous in
\theta^{\prime}
.

Definition II.6

(Moderate deviation principle)
.

A sequence of estimators
\{\widehat{\vartheta}_{T}\}_{T\in\mathbb{N}}
is said to satisfy a moderate deviation principle with rate function
I
if for every sequence
\{a_{T}\}_{T\in\mathbb{N}}
of real numbers with
1\ll a_{T}\ll T
, for every Borel set
\mathcal{D}\subset\Theta^{\prime}
and for every
\theta\in\Theta
we have

\displaystyle-\inf_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathsf{int}\,{\mathcal{D}}}\,I(\theta^{\prime},\theta)\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{T\to\infty}~\frac{1}{a_{T}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\left(\widehat{\vartheta}_{T}\in\mathcal{D}\right)

(5a)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}~\frac{1}{a_{T}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\left(\widehat{\vartheta}_{T}\in\mathcal{D}\right)

(5b)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\inf_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathsf{cl}\,{\mathcal{D}}}\,I(\theta^{\prime},\theta).

(5c)

If
I(\theta^{\prime},\theta)
is continuous (instead of merely lower semi-continuous) in
\theta^{\prime}
and if
\mathsf{int}\,\mathcal{D}
is dense in
\mathcal{D}
, then the infima in (

) and (

5

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(\widehat{\vartheta}_{T}\in\mathcal{D})=e^{-ra_{T}+o(a_{T})}

I

\theta
from the set

, see Figure
3

coincides with the decay rate of the probability that
\widehat{\vartheta}_{T}
materializes in
\mathcal{D}
, while

can be viewed as the speed of convergence. The condition
1\ll a_{T}\ll T
is satisfied, for example, if
a_{T}=\sqrt{T}
,
T\in\mathbb{N}
.

III
Main results

To aid the presentation we assume from now on without much loss of generality that
\theta
is invertible. We are now ready to demonstrate that the MDP of Proposition
II.8
allows us via the reverse
I
-projection
\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\theta}_{T})
to derive sharp bounds on the decay rate of the probability of the event
\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\theta}_{T})\not\overset{t}{\sim}\theta
. Recall from Lemma
II.3
that the two stable and (
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
-almost surely) invertible matrices
\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\theta}_{T})
and
\theta
are topologically equivalent if and only if they have the same orientation. Recall also that
\mathrm{or}(\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\theta}_{T}))=\mathrm{or}(\widehat{\theta}_{T})
because the reverse
I
-projection preserves orientation. Checking whether
\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\theta}_{T})
is topologically equivalent to
\theta
is thus tantamount to checking whether the determinants of
\widehat{\theta}_{T}
and
\theta
have the same signs.

Theorem III.1

(Probability of misclassification)
.

Assume that
\theta\in\Theta\cap\mathsf{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
,
\{\widehat{\theta}_{T}\}_{T\in\mathbb{N}}
are the least squares estimators (
2
) and
\{a_{T}\}_{T\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence with
1\ll a_{T}\ll T
. If
S_{\theta^{\circ}}=S_{w}^{-1/2}S_{\theta}S_{w}^{-1/2}
and
r=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(S_{\theta^{\circ}}-I_{n})
, then

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{T}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\big(\mathrm{or}(\widehat{\theta}_{T})\not=\mathrm{or}(\theta)\big)\leq-r,

(9a)

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{T}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\big(\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\theta}_{T})\not\overset{t}{\sim}\theta\big)\leq-r.

(9b)

Proof.

As for (
9a
), note that
\widehat{\theta}_{T}\in\mathsf{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
-almost surely for all sufficiently large
T
, and therefore we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\big(\mathrm{or}(\widehat{\theta}_{T})\not=\mathrm{or}(\theta)\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\big(\mathrm{or}(\widehat{\theta}_{T})=-\mathrm{or}(\theta)\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\big(\exists G\in\mathsf{GL}^{-}(n,\mathbb{R}):\widehat{\theta}_{T}=G\theta\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\big(\widehat{\vartheta}_{T}\in\mathcal{D}_{T}(\theta)\big)

where the second equality holds because any invertible matrices
\widehat{\theta}_{T}
and
\theta
whose determinants have opposite signs satisfy
\widehat{\theta}_{T}=G\theta
for some
G\in\mathsf{GL}^{-}(n,\mathbb{R})
. The third equality follows from the definition of the transformed least squares estimators
\{\widehat{\vartheta}_{T}\}_{T\in\mathbb{N}}
in (
6
) and the construction of the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T}(\theta)=\{

\displaystyle\sqrt{(T/a_{T})}(G-I_{n})\theta+\theta:G\in\mathsf{GL}^{-}(n,\mathbb{R})\}.

As this set is time-dependent, we cannot directly use it. That is, it is not admissible in the sense of Definition
II.6
. To sidestep this complication, we consider instead the larger set

\mathcal{D}(\theta)=\bigcup_{T\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{D}_{T}(\theta).

The above reasoning then implies that

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{T}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\big(\mathrm{or}(\widehat{\theta}_{T})\not=\mathrm{or}(\theta)\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{T}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\big(\widehat{\vartheta}_{T}\in\mathcal{D}(\theta)\big)\leq-\inf_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathsf{cl}\,\mathcal{D}(\theta)}I(\theta^{\prime},\theta),

where the first inquality holds because
\mathcal{D}_{T}(\theta)\subset\mathcal{D}(\theta)
for all
T\in\mathbb{N}
, while the second inequality follows from the MDP established in Proposition
II.8
. In the remainder of the proof we derive an analytical lower bound on the minimization problem on the right hand side of the above expression. To this end, assume first that
S_{w}=I_{n}
. Evaluating the rate function (
4
) at an arbitrary
\theta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}(\theta)
then yields

\displaystyle I(\theta^{\prime},\theta)=\frac{T}{2a_{T}}\mathsf{tr}\left((G-I_{n})\theta S_{\theta}\theta^{\mathsf{T}}(G-I_{n})^{\mathsf{T}}\right)

(10)

for some
G\in\mathsf{GL}^{-}(n,\mathbb{R})
and some
T\in\mathbb{N}
, and
the Lyapunov equation (
3
) implies that
\theta S_{\theta}\theta^{\mathsf{T}}=S_{\theta}-I_{n}
. In addition, our assumptions about the sequence
\{a_{T}\}_{T\in\mathbb{N}}
imply that
T/a_{T}\geq 1
. We may thus conclude that

\displaystyle\min_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathsf{cl}\,\mathcal{D}(\theta)}

\displaystyle I(\theta^{\prime},\theta)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\det(G)\leq 0}\tfrac{1}{2}\mathsf{tr}\left((G-I_{n})(S_{\theta}-I_{n})(G-I_{n})^{\mathsf{T}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(S_{\theta})-1)\inf_{\det(G)\leq 0}\|G-I_{n}\|_{\mathsf{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(S_{\theta})-1),

where the first inequality holds because
\det(G)\leq 0
for every
G\in\mathsf{cl}\,\mathsf{GL}^{-}(n,\mathbb{R})
, the second inequality uses the bound
\mathsf{tr}(AB)\geq\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(A)\mathsf{tr}(B)
for any
A,B\succeq 0
, and the third inequality follows from the Eckart-Young Theorem
[
26
, Theorem 2.4.8]
. One can actually show that the second inequality is tight, but this is not needed to prove the theorem.
This establishes (
9a
) for
S_{w}=I_{n}
. If
S_{w}\succ 0
is arbitrary, one may first apply a change of coordinates
x^{\circ}=S_{w}^{-1/2}x
, under which the noise covariance matrix simplifies to
I_{n}
, while the system matrix and the invariant state covariance matrix become
\theta^{\circ}=S_{w}^{-1/2}\theta S_{w}^{1/2}
and
S_{\theta^{\circ}}=S_{w}^{-1/2}S_{\theta}S_{w}^{-1/2}
, respectively. As
S_{w}\succ 0
, we further have
\theta^{\circ}\overset{t}{\sim}\theta
. Applying the results of the first part of the proof to the transformed system finally yields (
9a
). As for (
9b
), recall from Sections
II-A
and
II-B
that
\mathcal{P}(\theta^{\prime})
is asymptotically stable and that
\mathrm{or}(\theta^{\prime})=\mathrm{or}(\mathcal{P}(\theta^{\prime}))
for any
\theta^{\prime}\in\mathsf{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
. Therefore, we can focus on orientation solely,
i.e.,
if
\mathrm{or}(\theta^{\prime})=\mathrm{or}(\theta)
then
\mathcal{P}(\theta^{\prime})\overset{t}{\sim}\theta
. Then again, as
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}\big(\widehat{\theta}_{T}\not\in\mathsf{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})\big)=0
for sufficiently large
T
, the claim follows from (
9a
).
∎

The rate
r=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(S_{\theta^{\circ}}-I_{n})
established in Theorem
III.1
is non-trivial (strictly positive) for any
\theta\in\Theta\cap\mathsf{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
as

S_{\theta^{\circ}}=S_{w}^{-1/2}S_{\theta}S_{w}^{-1/2}\overset{\eqref{eq:Lyapunov}}{=}S_{w}^{-1/2}\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\theta^{k}S_{w}(\theta^{k})^{\mathsf{T}}S_{w}^{-1/2}+I_{n}.

III-A

Implications of Theorem
III.1

As the rate
r
derived in Theorem
III.1
is a function of
\theta
, the properties of the unknown system matrix
\theta
determine the likelihood of topological misclassification.
In high-performance applications where some eigenvalues of
\theta
are close to
0
, for example, the sign of the determinant of
\theta
and therefore the topological class of the underlying system are difficult to estimate.
As such applications are usually safety-critical, however, inferring the correct topological class is of utmost importance. In the context of Figure
1
, designing a controller tailored to a damper might be detrimental if the true system is a spring. Theorem
III.1
indicates that designing a system for high performance in the sense of constructing some eigenvalues of
\theta
to be close to
0
is in conflict with the reliable and fast identification of the system’s topological class.

Quantitative notions of stability and controllability, which strengthen the standard qualitative notions of stability and controllability, respectively, offer further insights into (
9b
).

Definition III.2

(Strong controllability
[
27
]
)
.

A pair
(A,B)
is called
(\ell,\nu)
-strongly controllable for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
\nu>0
if the matrix
C_{\ell}=\begin{pmatrix}B\,AB\,\cdots\,A^{\ell-1}B\end{pmatrix}
satisfies
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(C_{\ell})\geq\nu
.

If
A
and
B
parametrize the deterministic system
x_{t+1}=Ax_{t}+Bu_{t}
and
(A,B)
is
(\ell,\nu)
-strongly controllable,
then, a small
\nu
indicates that for two points
x_{a},x_{b}
being close, the inputs to reach them from
x_{0}
might be wildly different.

Definition III.3

(Strong stability
[
27
]
)
.

A square matrix
A
is
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strongly stable for
\kappa>0
and
\gamma\in(0,1]
if
A=HLH^{-1}
for
L
and
H
such that
\|L\|_{2}\leq 1-\gamma
and
\|H\|_{2}\|H^{-1}\|_{2}\leq\kappa
.

One readily verifies that any
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strongly stable matrix is asymptotically stable. Conversely, by Lyapunov stability theory, for any asymptotically stable matrix
A
there exists
P\succ 0
such that
P-A^{\mathsf{T}}PA\succ 0
. Thus, the transformed matrix
A^{\prime}=P^{1/2}AP^{-1/2}
satisfies
I_{n}-(A^{\prime})^{\mathsf{T}}A^{\prime}\succ 0
, which in turn implies that
\|A^{\prime}\|_{2}<1
. Hence,
A=HLH^{-1}
with
H=P^{-1/2}
and
L=A^{\prime}
, and
A
is
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strongly stable with
\gamma=1-\|A^{\prime}\|_{2}
and
\kappa
being proportional to the condition number of
P^{1/2}
. The next lemma relates the rate
r
of Theorem
III.1
to strong controllability and stability properties of the pair
(\theta^{\circ},I_{n})
.

Lemma III.4

(Bounding the rate of Theorem
III.1
)
.

If the pair
(\theta^{\circ},I_{n})
is
(\ell,\nu)
-strongly controllable and
\theta^{\circ}
is
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strongly stable, then

\nu^{2}\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(S_{\theta^{\circ}})\leq(\kappa^{2})/(2\gamma-\gamma^{2}).

(11)

Proof.

The matrix
C_{\ell}
introduced in Definition
III.2
with
A=\theta^{\circ}
and
B=I_{n}
satisfies
C_{\ell}C_{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}}=\sum^{\ell-1}_{i=0}(\theta^{\circ})^{i}((\theta^{\circ})^{i})^{\mathsf{T}}
. Thus, the controllability Gramian
W=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}C_{\ell}C_{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}}
coincides with
S_{\theta^{\circ}}
as defined in Theorem
III.1
. As
(\theta^{\circ},I_{n})
is
(\ell,\nu)
-strongly controllable, we may thus conclude that
\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(S_{\theta^{\circ}})\geq\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(C_{\ell}C_{\ell}^{\mathsf{T}})\geq\nu^{2}
. An upper bound on
\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(S_{\theta^{\circ}})
can be
obtained from the
(\kappa,\gamma)
-strong stability of
\theta^{\circ}
, which implies via
[
27
, Lemma 3.3]
that
\mathsf{tr}(S_{\theta^{\circ}})\leq(\kappa^{2}/\gamma)\mathsf{tr}(I_{n})
and hence
\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(S_{\theta^{\circ}})\leq(\kappa^{2}/\gamma)
. Looking at the proof of
[
27
, Lemma 3.3]
, this can be sharpened to (
11
).
∎

The bound (
11
) is in line with the folklore wisdom in system identification that a slow (
\gamma
small), yet well-excited (
\nu
large) system is desirable. Next, we highlight a few other properties of the problem parameters
\theta
and
S_{w}
one might be able to manipulate in order to increase the rate in Theorem
III.1
(even though this rate depends on
\theta
and is thus unobservable).

III-A
1
Tuning
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta^{\circ})
and the noise covariance matrix

Using the series representation of
S_{\theta^{\circ}}
one can show that

S_{\theta^{\circ}}\succeq I_{n}+\frac{1}{1-\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta^{\circ})^{2}}\theta^{\circ}(\theta^{\circ})^{\mathsf{T}},

see
[
28
]
, where
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta^{\circ})\leq\rho(\theta^{\circ})<1

\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(S_{\theta^{\circ}}-I_{n})\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta^{\circ})^{2}.

Hence, an increase in
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta^{\circ})
improves the rate
r
from Theorem
III.1
.
Next, assume that
\theta
is diagonalizable,
i.e.
,
\theta=V\Lambda V^{-1}
for some diagonal matrix
\Lambda
and invertible matrix
V
. As
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta^{\circ})\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta^{\circ})=\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta)
, the preferred noise covariance matrix for which
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta^{\circ})
matches the bound
\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta)
(which is independent of
S_{w}
) is given by
S_{w}=\alpha VV^{\mathsf{T}}
for any
\alpha>0
.
As already pointed out in Example
II.9
the magnitude of

is not important, its principal axes are.

III-A
2
Tuning
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta)

The rate
\mathcal{O}(-\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta)^{2}\sqrt{T})
from the introduction follows as
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta)\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta^{\circ})\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}(S_{w}^{1/2})/\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(S_{w}^{1/2})
. In a system-theoretic context, as

III-A
3
Minimizing interconnections

Consider a separable and an interconnected system with

\theta_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}Y&0\\
0&Y\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}Y&I_{n}\\

for some

One can show that
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta_{1})\geq\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\theta_{2})
, which aligns with intuition: if possible, identify subsystems individually.

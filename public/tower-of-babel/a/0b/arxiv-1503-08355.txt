Abstract
We classify the irreducible Hermitian real variations of Hodge structure admitting an infinitesimal normal function, and draw conclusions for cycle-class maps on families of abelian varieties with a given Mumford–Tate group.

2.1
Normal functions and infinitesimal invariants

Let
\mathcal{V}\to\mathscr{S}
be a polarized
\mathbb{Q}
-VHS over a complex manifold, pure of weight
-1
. By abuse of notation,
\mathcal{V}
will denote the underlying holomorphic vector bundle and its sheaf of sections, with Hodge filtration
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
;
\mathbb{V}
is the underlying
\mathbb{Q}
-local system,
Q\colon\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{Q}_{\mathscr{S}}
the polarization, and
\nabla

Define a filtered complex of sheaves

\displaystyle C^{\bullet}:=(\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathscr{S}},\nabla),\qquad F^{p}C^{\bullet}:=(\mathcal{F}^{p-\bullet}\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathscr{S}},\nabla)

on
\mathscr{S}
, so that the differential of each

\displaystyle{\rm Gr}_{F}^{p}C^{\bullet}=\big({\rm Gr}_{\mathcal{F}}^{p-\bullet}\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega_{\mathscr{S}}^{\bullet},\bar{\nabla}\big)

is
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{S}}
-linear. Consider the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to F^{0}C^{\bullet}\to C^{\bullet}/\mathbb{V}\to C^{\bullet}/\big(F^{0}C^{\bullet}+\mathbb{V}\big)\to 0;

(2.1)

the sheaf of (quasi-)horizontal sections of the Jacobian bundle
J(\mathcal{V})
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\rm hor}^{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\nabla}\big(C^{\bullet}/\big(F^{0}C^{\bullet}+\mathbb{V}\big)\big).

The space of normal functions associated to
\mathcal{V}
is

\displaystyle\operatorname{NF}_{\mathscr{S}}(\mathcal{V}):=\Gamma\big(\mathscr{S},\mathcal{J}_{\rm hor}^{\mathbb{Q}}\big),

with infinitesimal invariant

\displaystyle\delta\colon\ \operatorname{NF}_{\mathscr{S}}(\mathcal{V})=\mathbb{H}^{0}\left(\mathscr{S},\frac{C^{\bullet}}{F^{0}C^{\bullet}+\mathbb{V}}\right)\to\mathbb{H}^{1}\big(\mathscr{S},F^{0}C^{\bullet}\big)\to\Gamma\big(\mathscr{S},\mathcal{H}_{\nabla}^{1}\big(F^{0}C^{\bullet}\big)\big)

arising from (
2.1
).

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{H}_{\nabla}^{0}\big(F^{0}C^{\bullet}\big)=0
, then
\delta
is injective.

Proof.

The assumption implies that we only need to check injectivity into
\mathbb{H}^{1}\big(\mathscr{S},F^{0}C^{\bullet}\big)
. By the theorem of the fixed part
[
27
]
, the assumption also implies that
H^{0}(\mathscr{S},\mathbb{V})=\{0\}
. Since
\mathbb{H}^{0}(\mathscr{S},C^{\bullet}/\mathbb{V})=H^{0}(\mathscr{S},\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{C}}/\mathbb{V})=H^{0}(\mathscr{S},\mathbb{V})\otimes\tfrac{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{Q}}
,
the result now follows from the long-exact sequence of (
2.1
).∎

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\mathcal{H}_{\nabla}^{1}\big(F^{0}C^{\bullet}\big)
and
\mathcal{H}_{\nabla}^{0}\big(F^{0}C^{\bullet}\big)
vanish, then
\operatorname{NF}_{\mathscr{T}}(\jmath^{*}\mathcal{V})=\{0\}
for any étale neighborhood
\jmath\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{S}
.

Proof.

Vanishing of
\mathcal{H}^{0}\big(F^{0}C^{\bullet}\big)=\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{C}}\cap\mathcal{F}^{0}
implies (again by
[
27
]
) that
H^{0}(\mathscr{T},\jmath^{*}\mathbb{V})=\{0\}
; now apply the previous lemma.
∎

Now writing
\mathcal{H}^{k}(j):=\mathcal{H}_{\bar{\nabla}}^{k}\big({\rm Gr}_{F}^{j}C^{\bullet}\big)
, consider the spectral sequence

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}^{q,p}:=\begin{cases}\mathcal{H}^{p+q}(p),&p\geq 0,\\
0,&p<0\end{cases}

(2.2)

converging to
\mathcal{H}_{\nabla}^{*}\big(F^{0}C^{\bullet}\big)
. We recover at once the result of Green and Voisin
[
10
]
, in the form stated by Nori
[

]

Proposition 2.3
.

If all the
\mathcal{H}^{0}(j)
and
\mathcal{H}^{1}(j)
vanish for
j\geq 0
, then
\operatorname{NF}_{\mathscr{T}}(\jmath^{*}\mathcal{V})=\{0\}
for any étale neighborhood
\jmath\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{S}
.

Two obvious situations in which the vanishing conditions
fail
are those of VHS of level one, or level three and “CY type”. Recall that if

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{F}^{p_{\min}}\mathcal{V}\supsetneq\mathcal{F}^{p_{\min}+1}\mathcal{V}\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq\mathcal{F}^{p_{\max}}\mathcal{V}\supsetneq\mathcal{F}^{p_{\max}+1}\mathcal{V}=\{0\}

then the level

; for weight

\ell(\mathcal{V})=2p_{\max}+1=-2p_{\min}-1
. Write
h^{p}:=\dim({\rm Gr}_{\mathcal{F}}^{p}\mathcal{V})
for the Hodge numbers, and
\underline{h}=\big(h^{p_{\min}},\ldots,h^{p_{\max}}\big)
; put

. By naively computing ranks we have

Example 2.4

(level 1)
.

If
\underline{h}=(n,n)
and

, then
\mathcal{H}^{1}(0)\neq\{0\}
. If

then
\mathcal{H}^{1}(1)\neq\{0\}
.

Example 2.5

(level 3 CY)

If

This makes the broad vanishing results we obtain in this paper somewhat surprising. It also explains why we have to ignore the level-one cases below.

Finally, given a smooth family of varieties

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{AJ}}^{r}_{X_{s_{0}}}\colon\ \operatorname{Griff}^{r}(X_{s_{0}})\to\overline{J}^{r}(X_{s_{0}}):=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{{\rm MHS}}\big(\mathbb{Q}(0),\overline{H}^{2r-1}(X_{s_{0}},\mathbb{Q}(r))\big),

where

\displaystyle\nu_{\mathfrak{Z}}\in\operatorname{NF}_{\mathscr{T}}(\mathcal{V}),\qquad\mathcal{V}=\overline{R}^{2r-1}(\pi_{\mathscr{T}})_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{\mathscr{X}_{\mathscr{T}}}(r)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{T}},

Corollary 2.6
.

If all the
\mathcal{H}^{0}(j)
and
\mathcal{H}^{1}(j)
vanish for
j\geq 0
, then
\overline{\operatorname{AJ}}_{X_{s_{0}}}^{r}(Z)=0
in (

While there is no converse result, nonvanishing of

in particular seems to be a good predictor of the existence of interesting cycles. Note that its nonvanishing for level-one VHS

has a geometric “origin”. Namely, these VHS correspond to the

(the algebraic closure of the function field) yields nontrivial geometric normal functions in

) over some étale neighborhood

. This explains why we only consider the Griffiths group.

2.2
\mathfrak{n}
-cohomology of finite-dimensional representations

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra of rank
n
,
\mathfrak{b}\supset\mathfrak{t}
Borel and Cartan subalgebras,
\Delta=\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})\subset\mathfrak{t}^{*}
the corresponding roots. Denote by
\Delta^{+}=\Delta(\mathfrak{b})
the positive roots,
\Sigma=\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\}\subset\Delta^{+}
the simple roots,
\Omega=\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}\}\subset\mathfrak{t}^{*}
the fundamental weights, and
\Lambda
the (weight) lattice they generate. The Killing form
B(X,Y)=\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{ad}X\circ\operatorname{ad}Y)
on
\mathfrak{g}
induces a symmetric bilinear form
\langle\,,\,\rangle
on
\Lambda
, a particular orthonormal basis of which (as in
[
17
, Appendix C]
) will be denoted by
\{e_{i}\}
. We have in particular
\langle\omega_{i},\sigma_{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle\sigma_{j},\sigma_{j}\rangle\delta_{ij}
.

By the theorem of the highest weight, the irreducible representations
\big\{V^{\lambda}\big\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
(of finite dimension) are parametrized by their highest weight
\lambda
; there is a 1-to-1 correspondence between irreps and weights of the form
\lambda=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\omega_{i}
with
m_{i}\geq 0
. Fix an element
{\tt E}\in\mathfrak{t}
with all
\frac{1}{2}{\tt E}(\sigma_{i})
non-positive and integral, and let
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}^{j,-j}
be the decomposition into
\operatorname{ad}({\tt E})
-eigenspaces with eigenvalue
2j
. For any representation
(V,\rho)
, there is a corresponding decomposition into
\rho({\tt E)}
-eigenspaces, which can have odd or fractional eigenvalues. Write
\mathfrak{p}=\oplus_{j\geq 0}\mathfrak{g}^{j,-j}
,
\mathfrak{n}=\oplus_{j<0}\mathfrak{g}^{j,-j}
(so that
\Delta(\mathfrak{n})\subset\Delta^{+}
), and
\mathfrak{g}^{0}=\mathfrak{g}^{0,0}
. We also denote
\Delta_{0}=\Delta\big(\mathfrak{g}^{0},\mathfrak{t}\big)
,
\Delta_{0}^{+}=\Delta_{0}\cap\Delta^{+}
, and
\big\{V_{0}^{\xi}\big\}
for the highest-weight irreps of
\mathfrak{g}^{0}
.

Our main computational tool will be a result of Kostant (cf.
[
18
, Theorem 5.14]
). It computes the decomposition of the cohomologies

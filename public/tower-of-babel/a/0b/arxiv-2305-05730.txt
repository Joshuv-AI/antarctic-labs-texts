Partial Plateau’s Problem with H H -mass
Enrique Alvarado, Qinglan Xia

Abstract
Classically, Plateau’s problem asks to find a surface of least area with a given boundary B B . In this article, we investigate a version of Plateau’s problem, where the boundary of an admissible surface is only required to partially span B B . Our boundary data is given by a flat ( m − 1 ) (m-1) -chain B B and a smooth compactly supported differential ( m − 1 ) (m-1) -form Φ \Phi . We are interested in minimizing 𝐌 ⁡ ( T ) − ∫ ∂ T Φ \mathbf{M}(T)-\int_{\partial T}\Phi over all m m -dimensional rectifiable currents T T in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} such that ∂ T \partial T is a subcurrent of the given boundary B B . The existence of a rectifiable minimizer is proven with Federer and Fleming’s compactness theorem. We generalize this problem by replacing the mass 𝐌 \mathbf{M} with the H H -mass of rectifiable currents. By minimizing over a larger class of objects, called scans with boundary , and by defining their H H -mass as a type of lower-semicontinuous envelope over the H H -mass of rectifiable currents, we prove an existence result for this problem by using Hardt and De Pauw’s BV compactness theorem.



1
Introduction

A brief history of Plateau’s problem.

Loosely speaking, Plateau’s problem asks to find a
surface
of least
area
with a given
boundary
.
The various answers to this question depend on the precise notion of a surface, of its area, and of its boundary.
Although this problem was classically studied for surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with the use of mappings of surfaces
[
14
]
, geometric measure theory has now provided us with precise formulations and definitions of these italicized terms
[
7
,
17
,
13
,
2
,
16
]
.
Motivated by De Rham’s notion of currents, in the seminal work
[
8
]
, Federer and Fleming solved a version of Plateau’s problem with a class of generalized oriented submanifolds called
integral currents
, which come equipped with a natural notion of a boundary and surface area.
We refer the reader to
[
11
]
for a historical overview of Plateau’s problem and geometric measure theory.

The
H
-mass and scans.

The
mass

\mathbf{M}(T)
of a current
T
is used as a way to measure its area, and for a rectifiable current
T=\mathcal{H}^{m}\,\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.4pt,depth=0.0pt,width=6.0pt}\,M\wedge\theta\xi
, its mass is given by
\int_{M}\theta\,d\mathcal{H}^{m}
where
\theta:M\to\mathbb{N}
is its multiplicity function (see §
2
). For surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, a mass minimizing current
T
provides a good model for some but not all soap films.
For example, in
[
10
]
, Fleming showed that 2-dimensional mass minimizing currents in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
are smooth embedded surfaces away from their boundary, whereas general soap films have interior singularities.
To get a better model for soap films, Almgren
[
1
]
introduced the notion of
size
for rectifiable currents and investigated size minimizing currents.
However, in general, the existence of size minimizing currents is difficult to obtain.
In part, this is due to the fact that size minimizing sequences may have unbounded mass, as can be seen in examples provided by F. Morgan in
[
12
]
.
The lack of bounded mass prevents one from applying Federer and Fleming’s
compactness theorem

[
8
]
to size minimizing sequences.

Another important measurement of surface area is the
\alpha
-mass,

\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(T)=\int_{M}\theta^{\alpha}\,d\mathcal{H}^{m}

(1)

of a real rectifiable current
T=\mathcal{H}^{m}\,\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.4pt,depth=0.0pt,width=6.0pt}\,M\wedge\theta\xi
, where the parameter
\alpha\in[0,1]
.
The
\alpha
-mass acts as a bridge between the mass and the size of a real rectifiable current.
When
\alpha=0
, we get the size of
T
, and when
\alpha=1
we get its mass.
Given two probability measures
\mu_{+}
and
\mu_{-}
, any real rectifiable
1
-dimensional current
T
with
\partial T=\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}
is called a
transport path from
\mu_{+}
to
\mu_{-}
in the study of branched (ramified) optimal transport
[
18
,
3
,
19
]
.
The
\alpha
-mass of
T
is called the
\alpha
-cost of the transport path
T
.
A more general version of the
\alpha
-mass is the
H
-mass.
For a
concave integrand
(Definition
4.1
)
H:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, the
H
-mass of a real rectifiable current
T=\mathcal{H}^{m}\,\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.4pt,depth=0.0pt,width=6.0pt}\,M\wedge\theta\xi
is given by
\int_{M}H\circ\theta\,d\mathcal{H}^{m}
.

In article
[
5
]
, Hardt and De Pauw investigated a Plateau-type problem: minimizing the
H
-mass over all rectifiable currents with a fixed boundary.
Although
\mathbf{M}_{H}
minimizing sequences of rectifiable currents may not necessarily have bounded mass, they showed that for any fixed
\epsilon>0
, the minimizing sequences for the approximate problem:
\mathbf{M}_{H}+\epsilon\mathbf{M}
do.
They then considered a sequence of rectifiable currents, each of which is a minimizer to the approximate problem for a sequence
\epsilon_{i}\to 0
.
Using their general BV-compactness theorem on this sequence, they obtained an
\mathbf{M}_{H}
minimizing subsequence of rectifiable currents, that when viewed as scans, converges to a rectifiable scan.
An
m
-dimensional
scan
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a type of measurable function
\mathcal{T}
from the space of
(n-m)
planes, to the space of
0
dimensional currents in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
endowed with the
H
-flat distance
(Definition
4.3
).
For example, an
m
-dimensional rectifiable current in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be viewed as a scan by slicing it with
n-m
planes (see §
4.1
).

Partial Plateau’s problem.

Figure 1
:
The solution to the standard Plateau’s problem for the closed curve shown in
(A)
is the surface shown in
(B)
.
When one of the outer arcs is removed as shown in
(C)
, the soap film shown in
(D)
may still form.
Although it is no longer a solution to the standard Plateau’s problem, it is a solution to partial Plateau’s problem studied in the examples of §
3
.

In the above Plateau-type problems, the boundaries of the surfaces are prescribed.
In this article, we propose a new Plateau-type problem, where the boundary is not required to completely fulfill the prescribed one, but rather, is only required to satisfy a portion of it.
We call this
partial Plateau’s problem
.
To motivate it, let us consider the following scenario.
Suppose that we have an oriented wire that has the form of a sunflower as in Figure
1
(
A
).
The solution to the standard oriented Plateau’s problem with this boundary will be the union of all petals as in Figure
1
(
B
).
Now, assume that one petal accidentally falls off the flower and the boundary wire becomes the one shown in Figure
1
(
C
).
In this case, there is no solution to the corresponding Plateau’s problem anymore since the resulting wire is no longer a closed curve.
Nevertheless, the remaining wire still generates a soap film in the form of the remaining pedals as shown in Figure
1
(
D
).
This scenario (where the soap film only spans a portion of the given wire) motivates us to relax the boundary condition.
In Example
3.6
, we investigate this example in detail, and show that the solution shown in Figure
1
(
D
) is given by a solution to our partial Plateau’s problem.

Another motivation for this problem comes from the study of ramified optimal transportation as given in
[
20
]
.
More precisely, let
\mu
and
\nu
be two Radon measures on a convex compact subset
X
of the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}
be the
\alpha
-mass as defined in (
1
) for some
\alpha\in[0,1)
, and let
h
be a continuous function on the support of the signed measure
\nu-\mu
.
Then,
[
20
]
considered the following
resource allocation problem:
Minimize

\mathbf{E}_{\alpha}^{h}(T):=\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(T)-\int_{X}h\,d(\partial T)

(2)

among all 1 dimensional real rectifiable currents
T
with

\partial T\preceq\nu-\mu
, in the sense that its Jordan decomposition
\partial T=\tilde{\nu}-\tilde{\mu}
satisfies
\tilde{\mu}\leq\mu
and
\tilde{\nu}\leq\nu
.
In
[
20
]
, this problem was motivated by considering the following example: Consider a firm that produces and sells a product in
various regions.
The measures
\mu

and
\nu
represent, respectively, the distributions of production
capacities and market sizes.
The function
h
represents the sale price on the support of
\nu
, and the production cost on the support of
\mu
.
The firm aims to maximize its profit (the negative of
\mathbf{E}_{\alpha}^{h}(T)
) defined as sale revenue
\int_{X}h\,d\tilde{\nu}
minus the cost
involved in transportation
\mathbf{M}_{\alpha}(T)
and production
\int_{X}h\,d\tilde{\mu}
.
Note that the boundary
\partial T
is not required to equal
\nu-\mu
, but rather to be a portion of it.
Similar kinds of optimal partial mass transportation problems have been studied for instance by Caffarelli and McCann
[
4
]
and also Figalli
[
9
]
for the scenario of Monge-Kantorovich problems with particular attention to the quadratic cost.

Partial Plateau’s problem with
H
-mass.

The above Plateau-type problems either look to minimize functionals involving

•

the
mass
, or more generally, the
H
-mass
over
m
-dimensional
rectifiable
currents with a
fixed
boundary condition, or

•

the
\alpha
-mass
over
1 dimensional real rectifiable
currents with a
partial
boundary condition.

In this article, we investigate a Plateau type problem that minimizes a functional involving the
H
-mass
over
m
-dimensional rectifiable
currents with a
partial
boundary condition.
Precisely, let
H
be a concave integrand, let
B
be an
(m-1)
-dimensional rectifiable current with finite
H
-mass, and let
\Phi
be a smooth, compactly supported
(m-1)
-differential form in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We are interested in minimizing

\mathbf{E}_{H,\Phi}(T):=\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)-\int_{\partial T}\Phi

over all
m
-dimensional rectifiable currents
T
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\partial T\preceq B
.
By definition,
\partial T\preceq B
means that
\partial T
is a
subcurrent

[
15
]
of
B
, in the sense that
\mathbf{M}(B)=\mathbf{M}(B-\partial T)+\mathbf{M}(\partial T)
.
For the sake of intuition, it is worth mentioning that whenever
B
is a real rectifiable current with finite mass,
\partial T\preceq B
means that
\partial T
is supported within the support of
B
, has the same orientation as
B
, but only has a fraction of its density
[
15
,
Lemma 3.7
]
.

We have thus far formulated our Plateau type problems as minimization problems over rectifiable currents.
However, to obtain our main existence results (Theorem
4.18
, Theorem
5.1
, and Corollary
5.5
), we perform our minimization over a larger class of objects, called
m
-dimensional scans with boundary
(see Definitions
4.7
and
4.10
).
Loosely speaking, these are
scans
(introduced in
[
5
]
) with well-defined boundaries that are obtained as pointwise limits of rectifiable currents (when viewed as scans).
The reason that we minimize over scans with boundary is that when viewed as scans, the class of
m
-dimensional rectifiable currents is not closed under pointwise almost everywhere convergence.

Organization of the article.

In §
2
, we go over relevant definitions and notations that are by now standard in geometric measure theory literature.
Then in §
3
, we look at
partial Plateau’s problem with mass
: Given an
(m-1)
-dimensional flat chain
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with finite mass, and a smooth, compactly supported
(m-1)
-differential form
\Phi
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, consider

\mathcal{P}_{(\mathbf{E}_{\Phi},\,\preceq B,\,\mathbb{R}^{n})}\begin{cases}\text{minimize }\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}(T):=\mathbf{M}(T)-\partial T(\Phi)\\
\text{among }T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ such that }\partial T\preceq B.\end{cases}

(3)

In Theorem
3.5
, we prove the existence of this problem by using the standard compactness arguments of rectifiable currents.
Here, for any
\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}
minimizing sequence
\{T_{i}\}
, we have uniform bounds for
\{\mathbf{M}(T_{i})+\mathbf{M}(\partial T_{i})\}
.
Although this is a special case of partial Plateau’s problem with
H
-mass (studied in §
5
) when
H(\theta)=\theta
, we do not have to minimize over
m
-dimensional scans in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as in the following problems that involve the general
H
-mass.

In §
4
, we re-investigate
Plateau’s problem with
H
-mass
, which was first investigated by De Pauw and Hardt in their seminal work
[
5
]
.
Formulated over rectifiable currents, the problem is stated as follows: Given a rectifiable current
T_{0}\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\begin{cases}\text{minimize }\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)\\
\text{among }T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ such that }\partial T=\partial T_{0}.\end{cases}

(4)

To get an existence-type result
[
5
, Theorem 3.5.2.]
for problem (
4
), De Pauw and Hardt innovatively relax the notion of a rectifiable current to a rectifiable scan.
In their proof of the theorem, they construct a sequence of
\mathbf{M}_{H}
minimizing rectifiable currents that pointwise converge (as scans) to a rectifiable scan
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}
.
By Fateau’s lemma, it is clear that
\mathbf{M}_{H}(\mathcal{T}^{\ast})
is less than or equal to the infimum value of problem (
4
).
Nevertheless, since
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}
is not necessarily a rectifiable current, one cannot conclude that
\mathbf{M}_{H}(\mathcal{T}^{\ast})
achieves the infimum value of problem (
4
) as claimed in
[
5
, Theorem 3.5.2.]
.

To overcome this issue, in §
4.2
we amend their existence result
[
5
, Theorem 3.5.2.]
by reformulating problem (
4
) with a modified version of the
H
-mass, denoted by
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}
, and by minimizing
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}
over
Scan_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the class of
m
-dimensional scans with boundary
(Definitions
4.7
and
4.10
).
In particular, we provide the following reformulation of
Plateau’s problem with
H
-mass
: Given a scan
\mathcal{T}_{0}\in{\it Scan}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with boundary,

\mathcal{P}_{(\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H},\,\partial\mathcal{T}_{0},\,\mathbb{R}^{n})}\begin{cases}\text{minimize }\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T})\\
\text{among }\mathcal{T}\in Scan_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ such that }\partial\mathcal{T}=\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}.\end{cases}

(5)

It is worth mentioning the following two facts about
Scan_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
The class of
m
-dimensional scans with boundary is larger than the class of
m
-dimensional rectifiable currents, and any element in
Scan_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has a well-defined boundary, which is not necessarily the case for the general
m
-dimensional scans studied in
[
5
]
.
Under such modifications, in Theorem
4.18
we are able to get the existence of an
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}
minimizing
m
-dimensional scan with fixed boundary.

Given
B
,
\Phi
, and
H
as above, and the additional constraint that
B
is rectifiable and has finite
H
-mass, in §
5
we consider partial Plateau’s problem with
H
-mass over rectifiable currents:

\begin{cases}\text{minimize }\mathbf{E}_{\Phi,H}(T):=\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)-\partial T(\Phi)\\
\text{among }T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ such that }\partial T\preceq B.\end{cases}

(6)

Unfortunately, as in Plateau’s problem (
4
) with
H
-mass, this problem does not always have a solution among rectifiable currents.
To resolve this issue, we reformulate the problem by using our modified version
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}
of the
H
-mass on the class
Scan_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and investigate our main problem of interest, called
partial Plateau’s problem with
H
-mass
:

\mathcal{P}_{(\mathbf{E}_{\Phi,H},\,\preceq B,\,\mathbb{R}^{n})}\begin{cases}\text{minimize }\mathbf{E}_{\Phi,H}(\mathcal{T}):=\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T})-\partial\mathcal{T}(\Phi)\\
\text{among }\mathcal{T}\in Scan_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ such that }\partial\mathcal{T}\preceq\mathcal{S}(B).\end{cases}

(7)

Our main existence result, Theorem
5.1
, is proven within a slightly more general setting, and the existence of minimizer
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}
to problem (
7
) is then given in Corollary
5.5
.
Moreover, Corollary
5.5
also shows us that

\mathbf{E}_{\Phi,H}(\mathcal{T}^{*})=\inf\{\mathbf{E}_{\Phi,H}(T):T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\partial T\preceq B\}.

In other words, despite the fact that one can not expect to find an
\mathbf{E}_{H,\Phi}
minimizer to problem (
6
) among rectifiable currents, its infimum value is obtained by the corresponding
\mathbf{E}_{H,\Phi}
minimizer of problem (
7
), where we minimize over scans with boundary under the same partial boundary condition.

For the convenience of the reader, we will now give a brief review of the standard concepts and results from geometric measure theory that will be important for us in this article.

Let
0\leq m\leq n
.
We let
\mathscr{D}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the real vector space of smooth differential
m
-forms with compact support.
The class
\mathscr{D}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the vector space of all continuous real-valued linear functions on
\mathscr{D}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Elements of
\mathscr{D}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are called
m
-dimensional currents
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the subspace
\mathscr{E}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of
\mathscr{D}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
contains all
m
-dimensional currents with compact support.
The
mass
of a current
T\in\mathscr{D}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as

\mathbf{M}(T):=\sup\{T(\phi):\phi\in\mathscr{D}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\text{ with }\langle e_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{m},\phi(x)\rangle\leq 1,\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ and }e_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\}.

(8)

For
m\geq 1
, the
boundary
of a current
T\in\mathscr{D}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the current
\partial T\in\mathscr{D}_{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined by
\partial T(\phi):=T(d\phi)
for
\phi\in\mathscr{D}^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
\partial T
is defined to be the zero-current whenever
T\in\mathscr{D}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
For a sequence
\{T_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathscr{D}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of
m
-dimensional currents and a current
T\in\mathscr{D}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we say
T_{i}

converges
(weakly) to
T
, written as
T_{i}\rightharpoonup T
, if

T_{i}(\phi)\to T(\phi)
for all
\phi\in\mathscr{D}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The rectifiable currents of Federer and Fleming play an important role in this article.
With
\mathcal{H}^{m}
denoting the
m
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a subset
M
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
(\mathcal{H}^{m},m)
-rectifiable
if
\mathcal{H}^{m}(M)<\infty
and
\mathcal{H}^{m}(M\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}N_{i})=0
for some finite or countable family
\{N_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
m
-dimensional
C^{1}
-submanifolds of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The following is an important characterization of the class
\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of
m
-dimensional rectifiable currents.

Let
0\leq m\leq n
.
An
m
-dimensional rectifiable current

T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is given by the following three things:

1.

an
\mathcal{H}^{m}
-measurable and bounded
(\mathcal{H}^{m},m)
-rectifiable subset
M
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

2.

an
\mathcal{H}^{m}
-measurable
m
-vectorfield
\xi:M\to\wedge_{m}\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for
\mathcal{H}^{m}
-a.e.

,
\xi(x)=e_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{m}
for some orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
of the approximate tangent space
\mathrm{Tan}(M,x)
,

3.

and an
(\mathcal{H}^{m}\,\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.4pt,depth=0.0pt,width=6.0pt}\,M)
-summable function

We will be denoting such a rectifiable current

by
\mathcal{H}^{m}\,\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.4pt,depth=0.0pt,width=6.0pt}\,M\wedge\theta\xi

[

]
.

[

]
, or by

Its action on

Remark 2.1
.

Notice that the

3
Partial Plateau’s problem with mass

As in
[
15
]
, for any two
k
dimensional currents
A
and
B
of finite mass, we say that
A
is a
subcurrent
of
B
, denoted by
A\preceq B
, if

\mathbf{M}(B)=\mathbf{M}(B-A)+\mathbf{M}(A).

(15)

Notice that the zero-current
\boldsymbol{0}
is always a subcurrent of
B
and that
\mathbf{M}(A)\leq\mathbf{M}(B)
whenever
A\preceq B
. In particular,
A\preceq{\bf 0}
if and only if
A={\bf 0}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
B=\mathscr{H}^{m}\,\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.4pt,depth=0.0pt,width=6.0pt}\,M\wedge\theta\xi
be a real-rectifiable current.
If
A
is any
m
-dimensional subcurrent of
B
, Paolini and Stepanov
[
15
,
Lemma 3.7
]
showed that
A=\mathscr{H}^{m}\,\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.4pt,depth=0.0pt,width=6.0pt}\,M\wedge\lambda\theta\xi
for some Borel function
\lambda:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,1]
.
In particular,
A
is a real-rectifiable current with
\mathrm{spt}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{spt}(B)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
B
be a
k
dimensional current and
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of
k
dimensional currents such that
A_{i}\preceq B
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
A
is an
k
dimensional current such that
A_{i}\rightharpoonup A
, then
A\preceq B
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{M}
satisfies the triangle inequality, it is sufficient to show that
\mathbf{M}(B-A)+\mathbf{M}(A)\leq\mathbf{M}(B)
.
By lower-semicontinuity of
\mathbf{M}
with respect to weak convergence,

\mathbf{M}(B-A)\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}(B-A_{i})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbf{M}(A)\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}(A_{i}).

Therefore since
A_{i}\preceq B
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}(B-A)+\mathbf{M}(A)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}(B-A_{i})+\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}(A_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\left(\mathbf{M}(B-A_{i})+\mathbf{M}(A_{i})\right)\leq\mathbf{M}(B).

∎

Remark 3.3
.

We mention that Lemma
3.2
holds true if we replace
A_{i}\rightharpoonup A
by
\mathbf{F}_{K}(A_{i}-A)\rightarrow 0
for some compact subset
K
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
This is because by Remark
2.4
,
\mathbf{F}_{K}(A_{i}-A)\rightarrow 0
implies
A_{i}\rightharpoonup A
.

We are interested in what we call,
partial Plateau’s problem
:
Given a flat chain
B\in\mathbf{F}_{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with mass
\mathbf{M}(B)<\infty
, a smooth compactly supported form
\Phi\in\mathscr{D}^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and a rectifiable current
T_{0}\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, consider

\mathcal{P}_{(\mathbf{E}_{\Phi},\preceq B,T_{0},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\begin{cases}\text{minimize }\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}(T)\\
\text{among }T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ such that }\partial(T-T_{0})\preceq B,\end{cases}

where

\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}(T):=\mathbf{M}(T)-\partial T(\Phi).

(16)

Remark 3.4
.

Note the following:

•

When
B={\bf 0}
,
\partial(T-T_{0})\preceq\boldsymbol{0}
implies
\partial T=\partial T_{0}
.
Thus, up to a constant, this problem corresponds to the standard Plateau’s problem of rectifiable currents: Minimize
\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}(T):=\mathbf{M}(T)-\partial T_{0}(\Phi)
among all
T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\partial T=\partial T_{0}
.

•

The introduction of
T_{0}
makes this a slightly more general problem than
\mathcal{P}_{(\mathbf{E}_{\Phi},\,\preceq B,\,\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, given in (
3
).
When
T_{0}={\bf 0}
, the boundary of
T
is a subcurrent of
B
.

•

We require
\mathbf{M}(B)<\infty
but do not require
\mathbf{M}(\partial T_{0})<\infty
.
Thus for any admissible
T
, the mass of its boundary
\mathbf{M}(\partial T)
may be unbounded.

We now state the existence result of the partial Plateau’s problem.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathscr{D}^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
B\in\mathbf{F}_{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathbf{M}(B)<\infty
.
Given
T_{0}\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
there exists a rectifiable current
T^{\ast}\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\partial(T^{\ast}-T_{0})\preceq B
such that

\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}(T^{\ast})=\min\{\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}(T):T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\partial(T-T_{0})\preceq B\}.

Proof.

Notice that, since the zero-current
{\bf 0}\preceq B
, this class contains
T_{0}
.
By definition, both
T_{0}
and
B
have compact support.
Let
K
be a compact convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing
\mathrm{spt}(T_{0})\cup\mathrm{spt}(B)
and let
\pi_{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the nearest point projection onto
K

[
7
, 4.1.15]
.

For any
T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\partial(T-T_{0})\preceq B
, it holds that
\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}((\pi_{K})_{\#}T)\leq\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}(T)
.
Indeed, observe that
\mathrm{spt}(\partial T)\subseteq K
because
\partial T=\partial(T-T_{0})+\partial T_{0}
,
\mathrm{spt}(\partial(T-T_{0}))\subseteq\mathrm{spt}(B)
(see Remark
3.1
), and
\mathrm{spt}(T_{0})\cup\mathrm{spt}(B)\subseteq K
.
Thus,
\partial\left((\pi_{K})_{\#}T\right)=(\pi_{K})_{\#}\partial T=\partial T
. Since also

\mathbf{M}((\pi_{K})_{\#}T)\leq\mathbf{M}(T)
, it follows that

\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}((\pi_{K})_{\#}T)=\mathbf{M}((\pi_{K})_{\#}T)-\partial((\pi_{K})_{\#}T)(\Phi)\leq\mathbf{M}(T)-\partial T(\Phi)=\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}(T).

(17)

Let
\{T_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be an
\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}
-minimizing sequence with
\partial(T_{i}-T_{0})\preceq B
.
Thus, for each
i
,

\mathbf{M}(\partial(T_{i}-T_{0}))\leq\mathbf{M}(B)<\infty.

By (
17
), without loss of generality we may assume
\mathrm{spt}(T_{i})\subseteq K
and
\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}(T_{i})\leq\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}(T_{0})
. Then,

\mathbf{M}(T_{i})=\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}(T_{i})+\partial T_{i}(\Phi)\leq\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}(T_{0})+\partial T_{i}(\Phi)=\mathbf{M}(T_{0})+\partial(T_{i}-T_{0})(\Phi)\leq\mathbf{M}(T_{0})+\mathbf{M}(B)||\Phi||,

where
||\Phi||
is the
comass
norm of
\Phi
, and

\mathbf{M}(T_{i}-T_{0})\leq\mathbf{M}(T_{i})+\mathbf{M}(T_{0})\leq 2\mathbf{M}(T_{0})+\mathbf{M}(B)||\Phi||<\infty.

Thus,
\{T_{i}-T_{0}\}\subseteq\mathbf{I}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\{T:\mathbf{N}(T)<c\}
for some large enough
c>0
.
By the Federer and Fleming’s compactness theorem for integral currents
[
7
, 4.2.17(b)]
, a subsequence of
\{T_{i}-T_{0}\}
, still denoted by
\{T_{i}-T_{0}\}
,
\mathscr{F}_{K}
converges to some
R\in\mathbf{I}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Thus,
T_{i}-T_{0}\rightharpoonup R
and
T_{i}\rightharpoonup T^{\ast}:=R+T_{0}\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Since
\partial(T_{i}-T_{0})\preceq B
, and
\partial(T_{i}-T_{0})\rightharpoonup\partial R=\partial(T^{\ast}-T_{0})
, by Lemma
3.2
,
\partial(T^{\ast}-T_{0})\preceq B
as well.
Also, by the lower-semicontinuity of
\mathbf{M}
,

\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}(T^{\ast})=\mathbf{M}(T^{\ast})-\partial T^{\ast}(\Phi)\leq\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{M}(T_{i})-\partial T_{i}(\Phi)=\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}(T_{i}).

Since
\{T_{i}\}
is an
\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}
-minimizing sequence, we have
T^{\ast}
is the desired
\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}
-minimizer.
∎

Building upon the work of De Pauw and Hardt

4.1
The
H
-mass and the
H
-flat distance

We now list a few concepts and results from
[
5
]
that are particularly useful for the analysis in this article.
We start by defining the
H
-mass of real rectifiable currents with finite size and by defining the
H
-flat distance on integral flat chains.

Definition 4.1

(
[
5
, Definition 3.2.1.]
)
.

A function
H:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called a concave integrand if it satisfies the following conditions:

1.

H(0)=0
;

2.

H(1)=1
;

3.

H(\theta_{1})<H(\theta_{2})
whenever
0\leq\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}
;

4.

H(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})\leq H(\theta_{1})+H(\theta_{2})
whenever
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\geq 0
;

5.

\lim_{\theta\to\infty}H(\theta)=\infty
.

Definition 4.2

(
[
5
, Definition 3.2.2.]
)
.

For a concave integrand
H
and a real rectifiable current
T\in\mathscr{D}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathbf{S}(T)<\infty
, the
H
-mass of
T
is defined as

\mathbf{M}_{H}(T):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(\Theta^{m}(\|T\|,x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{m}(x).

(20)

Definition 4.3

(
[
5
, Definition 3.2.8.]
)
.

For a compact subset
K

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a concave integrand
H
, and an integral flat chain

\mathscr{F}^{H}_{K}(T):=\inf\{\mathbf{M}_{H}(T-\partial S)+\mathbf{M}_{H}(S):S\in\mathscr{R}_{m+1,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ T-\partial S\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}.

(21)

As in
[
7
, 1.7.4, 2.7.16]

\mathbf{O}(n)
be the orthogonal group of

and

In

and

measurable map

\mathcal{S}(T):\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,k)\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbf{F}_{m-k,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad\mathcal{S}(T)(p,y):=\langle T,p,y\rangle,

defined for every

and for

almost every

.
The

with

under the orthogonal projection map
p
for

For the remainder of the paper, the map

Let

For

almost every

.
In addition, as in

, (17)]
, the integral-geometric identity says that its mass can be given by integrating the mass of its

\mathbf{M}(T)=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}(m,n)}\int_{\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathbf{M}(\mathcal{S}(T)(p,y))\,d(\mathbf{\theta}_{n,m}^{\ast}\times\mathcal{L}^{m})(p,y),

[

, 2.7.16]
.
By

\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}(m,n)}\int_{\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathbf{M}_{H}(\mathcal{S}(T)(p,y))\,d(\mathbf{\theta}_{n,m}^{\ast}\times\mathcal{L}^{m})(p,y).

(23)

Motivated by (

[

defined the

They also introduced the concept of a

from the observation that a flat chain

to

in the following definition.

Definition 4.4
.

For any
\mathbf{\theta}_{n,m}^{\ast}\times\mathcal{L}^{m}

\mathbf{M}_{H}(f):=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}(m,n)}\int_{\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathbf{M}_{H}(f(p,y))\,d(\mathbf{\theta}_{n,m}^{\ast}\times\mathcal{L}^{m})(p,y)

(24)

and define the variation of

as

\mathbf{D}(f):=\sup\{\int_{\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}|D(\phi\circ f)|:\ \phi:\mathbf{I}_{0,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}\text{ with }\mathrm{Lip}(\phi)\leq 1\}.

(25)

Moreover we say that

is a cycle, and write

, if for

almost every

where

denotes the constant function

on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

From equations (
23
) and (

T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)=\mathbf{M}_{H}(\mathcal{S}(T)).

(26)

For any compact
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we will denote the class of measurable maps obtained by rectifiable currents in
\mathcal{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
\mathcal{S}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{\mathcal{S}(T):T\in\mathcal{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}
and let

\mathcal{S}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\bigcup_{K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ compact}}\mathcal{S}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(27)

We now recall one of the most important theorems from

]
, and then state a direct corollary that we will use in the proofs of Theorems

.

Theorem 4.5

(
[
5
, Theorem 3.4.1]
)
.

Suppose

is a

is sequentially compact in

.

and maps

with

, then some subsequence

converges pointwise
\mathcal{H}^{k}
a.e. to a function

with

\int_{X}M(f(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{k}x+\int_{X}|D(\phi\circ f)|\leq\Lambda

for all such
\phi
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let

be a sequence of

for all

.
Then there exists a subsequence
(f_{j_{k}})
of
(f_{j})
and an
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ast}_{n,m}\times\mathcal{L}^{m}
measurable map

such that

\mathbf{M}_{H}(f)+\mathbf{D}(f)\leq\Lambda,

and

(f_{j_{k}})
converges pointwise a.e. to

in the sense that for

almost every

,

\mathscr{F}_{K}^{H}[f_{j_{k}}(p,y)-f(p,y)]\to 0\text{ as }k\to\infty.

Proof.

This is a special case of
[

, Theorem 3.4.1]
with

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\mathrm{the\ Riemannian\ manifold}\ \mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\mathbf{I}_{0,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\ \mathrm{with}\ \mathrm{dist}_{Y}(T,\tilde{T})=\mathscr{F}_{K}^{H}(T-\tilde{T})

4.2
Existence of
H
-mass minimizing scans

In this subsection, we amend the seminal work
[
5
, Theorem 3.5.2.]
of De Pauw and Hardt, who consider the following Plateau-type problem: Given
T_{0}\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\begin{cases}\text{minimize }\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)\\
\text{among }T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ such that }\partial T=\partial T_{0},\end{cases}

where
\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)
denotes the
H
-mass of
T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as given in (
20
).
Let
\Gamma(\mathbf{M}_{H},T_{0},\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the infimum of that problem:

\Gamma(\mathbf{M}_{H},T_{0},\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\inf\{\mathbf{M}_{H}(T):T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ and }\partial T=\partial T_{0}\}.

(28)

For the convenience of the reader, we now state
[
5
, Theorem 3.5.2.]
of De Pauw and Hardt.

Theorem

(
[
5
, Theorem 3.5.2.]
)
.

Let
T_{0}\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathcal{I}_{1}^{m}(\mathrm{spt}(\partial T_{0}))=0
and let
H
be a concave integrand.
Then there exists an
m
-dimensional rectifiable scan
\mathcal{T}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\boldsymbol{\partial}(\mathcal{T}-\mathcal{S}(T_{0}))=0
and
\mathbf{M}_{H}(\mathcal{T})=\Gamma(\mathbf{M}_{H},T_{0},\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Moreover, if
\mathrm{spt}(\partial T_{0})
is an
m-1
dimensional compact propertly embedded
C^{1,1}
submanifold then there exists
T\in\mathbf{I}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\partial T=\partial T_{0}
and
\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)=\Gamma(\mathbf{M}_{H},T_{0},\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Before we discuss their proof, with the notation in (
27
) and by equation (
26
), observe that (
28
) has the following equivalent formulation:

\Gamma(\mathbf{M}_{H},T_{0},\mathbb{R}^{n})=\inf\{\mathbf{M}_{H}(\mathcal{R}):\mathcal{R}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ and }\boldsymbol{\partial}(\mathcal{R}-\mathcal{S}(T_{0}))=0\}.

In other words,
\mathcal{S}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the class of objects that
[
5
]
minimizes
\mathbf{M}_{H}
over.

Now, in the proof, De Pauw and Hardt construct a sequence of
\mathbf{M}_{H}
minimizing rectifiable currents, and use their BV compactness theorem
[
5
, Theorem 3.4.1.]
to obtain a subsequence
\{T_{j}\}
such that
\mathcal{S}(T_{j})
converges
pointwise almost everywhere
to a
rectifiable scan

\mathcal{T}
.
By the lower-semicontinuity of
\mathbf{M}_{H}
for
0
dimensional integral currents (under the
H
-flat distance), and by Fatou’s Lemma, it follows that

\mathbf{M}_{H}(\mathcal{T})\leq\Gamma(\mathbf{M}_{H},T_{0},\mathbb{R}^{n}).

However, since the rectifiable scan
\mathcal{T}
is not necessarily a rectifiable current, we cannot conclude that
\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and in turn, can neither conclude that

\mathbf{M}_{H}(\mathcal{T})=\Gamma(\mathbf{M}_{H},T_{0},\mathbb{R}^{n}).

To overcome this issue (the lack of closedness of
\mathcal{S}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under pointwise almost everywhere convergence), we introduce a modified class of objects that we will minimize over, denoted by
{\it Scan}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, as well as a modified version of the
H
-mass.

Definition 4.7
.

Whenever
K
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we say that an
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ast}_{n,m}\times\mathcal{L}^{m}
measurable map
\mathcal{T}:\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathscr{F}_{0,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an
m
-dimensional scan in
K
if
it is in the closure of
\mathcal{S}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
That is, if there exists a sequence of rectifiable currents
T_{1},T_{2},\dots\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\mathcal{S}(T_{i})\to\mathcal{T}
pointwise almost everywhere, in the sense that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{K}^{H}[\langle T_{i},p,y\rangle-\mathcal{T}(p,y)]=0\text{ for }\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ast}_{n,m}\times\mathcal{L}^{m}\text{ almost every }(p,y)\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(29)

We define

{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

to be the collection of all
m
-dimensional scans in
K
such that there exists a rectifiable current
T\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\boldsymbol{\partial}(\mathcal{T}-\mathcal{S}(T))=0
.
1
1

1

By Definition
4.4
,
\boldsymbol{\partial}(\mathcal{T}-\mathcal{S}(T))=0
means that
(\mathcal{T}-\mathcal{S}(T))(p,y)(\boldsymbol{1})=0
for
\theta^{\ast}_{n,m}\times\mathcal{L}^{m}
almost every
(p,y)\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Members of
{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are called
m
-dimensional scans in
K
with boundary.

Let us now investigate a couple of facts concerning the classes
{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and its members.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
K_{1},K_{2}
be two compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
If
K_{1}\subseteq K_{2}
, then
{\it Scan}_{m,K_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq{\it Scan}_{m,K_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

For any
\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by definition,
\mathcal{T}:\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathscr{F}_{0,K_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an
m
-dimensional scan in
K_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{\partial}(\mathcal{T}-\mathcal{S}(T))=0
for some
T\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Since
K_{1}\subseteq K_{2}
,
\mathcal{T}
is also an
m
-dimensional scan in
K_{2}
and
T\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
So
\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as well.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For any integral flat chain
T\in\mathscr{F}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ast}_{n,m}\times\mathcal{L}^{m}
measurable map

\mathcal{S}(T):\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathscr{F}_{0,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad\mathcal{S}(T)(p,y):=\langle T,p,y\rangle,

is an
m
-dimensional scan in
K
with boundary.

Proof.

We first show that
\mathcal{S}(T)
is an
m
-dimensional scan in
K
. Indeed, since
T\in\mathscr{F}_{m,K}
is an integral flat chain, there exists a sequence of rectifiable currents
T_{1},T_{2},\cdots\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{K}(T-T_{i})=0
.
By
[
5
, Remark 3.2.9.]
,
\mathcal{F}_{K}^{H}(T-T_{i})\leq H(2)\mathcal{F}_{K}(T-T_{i})
, and hence
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{K}^{H}(T-T_{i})=0
.
By
[
5
, Remark 3.2.11.]
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathcal{F}_{K}^{H}(\langle T-T_{i},p,y\rangle)\,d\mathcal{L}^{m}y\leq\mathcal{F}_{K}^{H}(T-T_{i})
for any
p\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)
.
Thus,

\int_{\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathcal{F}_{K}^{H}(\langle T-T_{i},p,y\rangle)\,d(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ast}_{n,m}\times\mathcal{L}^{m})(p,y)\leq\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}(n,m)\mathcal{F}_{K}^{H}(T-T_{i})\to 0\quad\text{ as }i\to\infty.

By
[
6
, Theorem 1.21]
, there exists a subsequence
(T_{ij})
of
(T_{i})
such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{K}^{H}(\langle T-T_{ij},p,y\rangle)=0

for
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ast}_{n,m}\times\mathcal{L}^{m}
almost every
(p,y)\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
As a result,
\mathcal{S}(T)
is an
m
-dimensional scan in
K
.

We now show that
\mathcal{S}(T)
is also in
{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Since
T\in\mathscr{F}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists
R\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
Q\in\mathscr{R}_{m+1,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
T=R+\partial Q
.
Thus,
by
[
5
, Proposition 3.1.6.]
,

\boldsymbol{\partial}(\mathcal{S}(T)-\mathcal{S}(R))=\boldsymbol{\partial}(\mathcal{S}(T-R))=\boldsymbol{\partial}(\mathcal{S}(\partial Q))=0.

(30)

Therefore,
\mathcal{S}(T)\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Definition 4.10
.

We define the class

{\it Scan}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\bigcup_{K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ compact}}{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

whose members are called
m
-dimensional scans in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with boundary.
For each
\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the boundary of
\mathcal{T}
is defined by

\partial\mathcal{T}:=\mathcal{S}(\partial T)

(31)

for any
T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying
\boldsymbol{\partial}(\mathcal{T}-\mathcal{S}(T))=0
.

With this definition we can now say that by (
30
),
\partial(\mathcal{S}(T))=\mathcal{S}(\partial T)
for any
T\in\mathscr{F}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Equation (
31
) defines a boundary operator
\partial:{\it Scan}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow{\it Scan}_{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\partial^{2}=0
, and
\partial:{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow{\it Scan}_{m-1,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
K
compact.

Proof.

For any
\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we first show that
\partial\mathcal{T}:=\mathcal{S}(\partial T)
is well-defined in the sense that
\mathcal{S}(\partial T)
is independent of the choices of
T\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that satisfies
\boldsymbol{\partial}(\mathcal{T}-\mathcal{S}(T))=0
and the choice of the compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Indeed, suppose there are two compact sets
K_{1},K_{2}
and an associated rectifiable current
T_{j}\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathscr{R}_{m,K_{1}\cup K_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\boldsymbol{\partial}(\mathcal{T}-\mathcal{S}(T_{j}))=0
for
j=1,2
.
Here,
\mathcal{T}-\mathcal{S}(T_{j}):\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathscr{F}_{0,K_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathscr{F}_{0,K_{1}\cup K_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
So,
\boldsymbol{\partial}(\mathcal{S}(T_{1}-T_{2}))=\boldsymbol{\partial}(\mathcal{S}(T_{1})-\mathcal{S}(T_{2}))=0
.
By
[
5
, Proposition 3.1.6.]
,
\partial(T_{1}-T_{2})=0
for
T_{1}-T_{2}\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K_{1}\cup K_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Therefore,
\partial T_{1}=\partial T_{2}
and hence
\mathcal{S}(\partial T_{1})=\mathcal{S}(\partial T_{2})
.
This shows that the definition
\partial\mathcal{T}:=\mathcal{S}(\partial T)
is well-defined.
By definition,
\partial T\in\mathscr{F}_{m-1,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Thus by Lemma
4.9
,
\partial\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{S}(\partial T)\in{\it Scan}_{m-1,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\partial^{2}\mathcal{T}=\partial(\mathcal{S}(\partial T))=\mathcal{S}(\partial^{2}T)=\mathcal{S}(0)=0
.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

Recall that the symbol
\boldsymbol{\partial}
is only used in the form of
\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathcal{T}=0
, denoting that
\mathcal{T}
is a cycle and that we cannot apply this symbol to other maps, while
\partial
is a well-defined boundary operator on
{\it Scan}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
In
{\it Scan}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the boundary operator
\partial
is equivalent to
\boldsymbol{\partial}
, in the sense that for any
\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\partial\mathcal{T}=0
if and only if
\boldsymbol{\partial}\mathcal{T}=0
.

For each compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the following functional, denoted by
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H,K}
, serves as a modification of the
H
-mass.
Lemma
4.15
shows that it agrees with
\mathbf{M}_{H}
on the class of scans of rectifiable currents.

Definition 4.13
.

For any compact
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the functional
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H,K}:{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}
by

\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H,K}(\mathcal{T}):=\inf\{\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}_{H}(T_{i}):T_{i}\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathcal{S}(T_{i})\to\mathcal{T},\text{ and }\partial(\mathcal{S}(T_{i}))=\partial\mathcal{T}\}.

(32)

We will simply write
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T})
instead of
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H,K}(\mathcal{T})
if
K
is clear from context.

Remark 4.14
.

In general, the value of
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H,K}(\mathcal{T})
depends on the compact set
K
.
Nevertheless, if
K_{1}\subseteq K_{2}
are nonempty compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap{\it Scan}_{m,K_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, since
\mathscr{R}_{m,K_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathscr{R}_{m,K_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathscr{F}_{K_{2}}^{H}\leq\mathscr{F}_{K_{1}}^{H}
, it follows that
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H,K_{2}}(\mathcal{T})\leq\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H,K_{1}}(\mathcal{T})
.

Lemma 4.15
.

For any
T\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{S}(T))=\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)=\mathbf{M}_{H}(\mathcal{S}(T))
.

Proof.

Taking the constant sequence
T_{i}=T
in (
32
) gives us that
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{S}(T))\leq\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)
.
We will now show that
\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)\leq\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{S}(T))
.
Let
T_{i}\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathcal{S}(T_{i})\to\mathcal{S}(T)
.
By definition, for
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ast}_{n,m}\times\mathcal{L}^{m}
almost every
(p,y)\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\mathscr{F}_{K}^{H}[\langle T_{i},p,y\rangle-\langle T,p,y\rangle]\to 0
as
i\to\infty
.
By lower semi-continuity of
\mathbf{M}_{H}
under
\mathscr{F}_{K}^{H}
convergence
[
5
, Lemma 3.2.14.]
,

\mathbf{M}_{H}(\langle T,p,y\rangle)\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}_{H}(\langle T_{i},p,y\rangle).

Integrating both sides and using Fatou’s lemma, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{H}(\mathcal{S}(T))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}(m,n)}\int_{\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathbf{M}_{H}(\langle T,p,y\rangle)\,d(\mathbf{\theta}_{n,m}^{\ast}\times\mathcal{L}^{m})(p,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}(m,n)}\int_{\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}_{H}(\langle T_{i},p,y\rangle)\,d(\mathbf{\theta}_{n,m}^{\ast}\times\mathcal{L}^{m})(p,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}(m,n)}\int_{\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathbf{M}_{H}(\langle T_{i},p,y\rangle)\,d(\mathbf{\theta}_{n,m}^{\ast}\times\mathcal{L}^{m})(p,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}_{H}(\mathcal{S}(T_{i})).

Thus,
\mathbf{M}_{H}(\mathcal{S}(T))\leq\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(T)
and hence the result follows from
\mathbf{M}_{H}(\mathcal{S}(T))=\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)
.
∎

The following proposition says that
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}
is subadditive on
{\it Scan}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proposition 4.16
.

For any nonempty compact subset
K
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2})\leq\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{1})+\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{2})
for any
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
K
be a nonempty compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that both
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
For each
k=1,2
, let
\{T^{k}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence in
\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\mathcal{S}(T_{i}^{k})\to\mathcal{T}_{k}
with
\partial(\mathcal{S}(T_{i}^{k}))=\partial\mathcal{T}_{k}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. By picking a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that
for each
k=1,2
,

\{\mathbf{M}_{H}(T^{k}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
converges.
Now consider the sequence
\{T_{i}^{1}+T_{i}^{2}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and notice that
\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\mathcal{S}(T_{i}^{1}+T_{i}^{2})\to(\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2})
, and
\partial(\mathcal{S}(T_{i}^{1}+T_{i}^{2}))=\partial(\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2})
.
Indeed, for
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ast}_{n,m}\times\mathcal{L}^{m}
almost every
(p,y)\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\mathscr{F}_{K}^{H}[(\mathcal{S}(T_{i}^{1}+T_{i}^{2})(p,y)-(\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2})(p,y)]\leq\mathscr{F}_{K}^{H}[(\mathcal{S}(T_{i}^{1})-\mathcal{T}_{1})(p,y)]+\mathscr{F}_{K}^{H}[(\mathcal{S}(T_{i}^{2})-\mathcal{T}_{2})(p,y)]\to 0

as
i\to\infty
, and

((\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2})-\mathcal{S}(T_{i}^{1}+T_{i}^{2}))(p,y)(\boldsymbol{1})=(\mathcal{T}_{1}-\mathcal{S}(T_{i}^{1})(p,y)(\boldsymbol{1})+(\mathcal{T}_{2}-\mathcal{S}(T_{i}^{2})(p,y)(\boldsymbol{1})=0

for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
By definition, this implies
\boldsymbol{\partial}((\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2})-\mathcal{S}(T_{i}^{1}+T_{i}^{2}))=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}_{H}(T^{1}_{i}+T^{2}_{i})\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}_{H}(T_{i}^{1})+\mathbf{M}_{H}(T_{i}^{2})

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}_{H}(T_{i}^{1})+\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}_{H}(T_{i}^{2})=\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}_{H}(T_{i}^{1})+\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}_{H}(T_{i}^{2}).

Since the sequences
\{T^{1}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
\{T_{i}^{2}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
were arbitrary, we have
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{1}+\mathcal{T}_{2})\leq\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{1})+\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{2})
.
∎

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
T_{i}\in\mathbf{I}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a sequence of
0
dimensional integral currents.
If
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{K}^{H}(T_{i})=0
, then
\lim_{i\to\infty}T_{i}(\boldsymbol{1})=0
.

Proof.

By definition of
\mathscr{F}_{K}^{H}
, there exists a sequence
\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathscr{R}_{1,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}_{H}(T_{i}-\partial S_{i})=0
.
It is sufficient to prove that
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{K}(T_{i}-\partial S_{i})=0
since this implies that

\lim_{i\to\infty}T_{i}(\boldsymbol{1})=\lim_{i\to\infty}T_{i}(\boldsymbol{1})-S_{i}(d(\boldsymbol{1}))=\lim_{i\to\infty}(T_{i}-\partial S_{i})(\boldsymbol{1})=0.

To prove that
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{K}(T_{i}-\partial S_{i})=0
we will apply
[
5
, Lemma 3.2.13.]
which says that any sequence of integral flat chains
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
with
\sup_{i}\{\mathbf{M}(Q_{i})+\mathbf{M}(\partial Q_{i})\}<\infty
and
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{K}^{H}(Q_{i})=0
will also have that
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{K}(Q_{i})=0
.
To apply this lemma to our sequence
\{T_{i}-\partial S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of
0
dimensional integral currents, we only need to show that
\sup_{i}\{\mathbf{M}(T_{i}-\partial S_{i})\}<\infty
and
\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{K}^{H}(T_{i}-\partial S_{i})=0
.
Our last claim is shown by

\mathscr{F}_{K}^{H}(T_{i}-\partial S_{i})\leq\mathbf{M}_{H}(T_{i}-\partial S_{i})\to 0.

We may also assume that

\sup_{i}\{\mathbf{M}_{H}(T_{i}-\partial S_{i})\}<\infty
.
Remark 3.2.7. in
[
5
]
says that
H(\mathbf{M}(T))\leq\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)
for all
T\in\mathbf{I}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and hence
\sup_{i}\{H(\mathbf{M}(T_{i}-\partial S_{i}))\}<\infty
.
Since
H
is strictly increasing, we lastly get that
\sup_{i}\{\mathbf{M}(T_{i}-\partial S_{i})\}<\infty
.
∎

Using this modified version of the
H
-mass
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}
on the class
{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of
m
-dimensional scans in
K
with boundary, we now show that the
Plateau’s problem with
H
-mass
(
5
) has a solution for each
K
.

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
K
be a nonempty compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For any
\mathcal{T}_{0}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a scan
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\partial\mathcal{T}^{\ast}=\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}
such that

\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}^{\ast})=\min\{\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}):\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \partial\mathcal{T}=\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}\}.

(33)

Moreover,
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}(p,y)\in\mathbf{I}_{0,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ast}_{n,m}\times\mathcal{L}^{m}
almost every
(p,y)\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Proof.

By assumption
\{\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\partial\mathcal{T}=\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}\}
contains
\mathcal{T}_{0}
, and hence is non-empty.
We may assume that
\inf\{\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}):\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \partial\mathcal{T}=\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}\}<\infty
as otherwise
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{0})=\infty
and we may simply pick
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}=\mathcal{T}_{0}
.
Let
\{\mathcal{T}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}
minimizing sequence in this collection.
That is,
\mathcal{T}^{i}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\partial\mathcal{T}^{i}=\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}
, and

\lim_{i\to\infty}\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}^{i})=\inf\{\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}):\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \partial\mathcal{T}=\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}\}.

(34)

Since
\{\mathcal{T}^{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is an
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}
minimizing sequence, without loss of generality, we may assume that

\sup_{i}\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}^{i})\leq C<\infty

(35)

for some
C>0
.
By definition of
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}
in (
32
), for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a sequence
\{T_{j}^{i}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathbf{M}_{H}(T_{j}^{i})=\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}^{i}),\quad\text{ and }\quad\partial(\mathcal{S}(T_{j}^{i}))=\partial\mathcal{T}^{i}=\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}\text{ for all }j\in\mathbb{N}.

(36)

Since
\mathcal{T}_{0}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a
T_{0}\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}=\partial(\mathcal{S}(T_{0}))
.
By the proof of Proposition
4.11
, the condition
\partial(\mathcal{S}(T_{j}^{i}))=\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}
imply that
\partial T_{j}^{i}=\partial T_{0}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
.
By (
36
), for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
there exists an
N_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

|\mathbf{M}_{H}(T^{i}_{N_{i}})-\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}^{i})|\leq\frac{1}{i}.

(37)

Hence by (
35
),
\sup_{i}\mathbf{M}_{H}(T^{i}_{N_{i}})\leq C+1<\infty
.
Thus,

\mathbf{M}_{H}(\mathcal{S}(T^{i}_{N_{i}}-T_{0}))=\mathbf{M}_{H}(T^{i}_{N_{i}}-T_{0})\leq C+1+\mathbf{M}_{H}(T_{0})\quad\text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{N}.

On the other hand, by
[
5
, Remark 3.3.3]
,
[
5
, Lemma 3.1.2]
, and the fact that
\partial T^{i}_{N_{i}}=\partial T_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{S}(T^{i}_{N_{i}}-T_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{c}_{1}[\mathbf{M}_{H}((T^{i}_{N_{i}}-T_{0})\times\llbracket\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\rrbracket)+\mathbf{M}_{H}(\partial((T^{i}_{N_{i}}-T_{0})\times\llbracket\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\rrbracket))]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{c}_{1}\mathbf{M}_{H}((T^{i}_{N_{i}}-T_{0})\times\llbracket\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\rrbracket)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{c}_{2}\mathbf{M}_{H}(T^{i}_{N_{i}}-T_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{c}_{2}[C+1+\mathbf{M}_{H}(T_{0})],

where the constants
\mathbf{c}_{1}
and
\mathbf{c}_{2}
depend only on
n
and
K
.
By Corollary
4.6
of the BV compactness theorem
[
5
, Theorem 3.4.1]
of De Pauw and Hardt, and replacing
\{T_{N_{i}}^{i}\}
by a subsequence if necessary, there exists a
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ast}_{n,m}\times\mathcal{L}^{m}
measurable map
\mathcal{R}:\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbf{I}_{0,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\mathscr{F}_{K}^{H}[\mathcal{S}(T_{N_{i}}^{i}-T_{0})(p,y)-\mathcal{R}(p,y)]=0

(38)

for
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ast}_{n,m}\times\mathcal{L}^{m}
almost every
(p,y)\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Now, let
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}=\mathcal{R}+\mathcal{S}(T_{0})
.
By definition, (
38
) implies
\mathcal{S}(T_{N_{i}}^{i})\to\mathcal{R}+\mathcal{S}(T_{0})=\mathcal{T}^{\ast}
.
By Lemma
4.17
, this implies

(\mathcal{T}^{\ast}-\mathcal{S}(T_{0}))(p,y)(\boldsymbol{1})=\lim_{i\to\infty}\langle T_{N_{i}}^{i}-T_{0},p,y\rangle(\boldsymbol{1})=0

\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ast}_{n,m}\times\mathcal{L}^{m}
almost every
(p,y)
, where the second equality is given by
[
5
, Proposition 3.1.6.]
since
\partial T_{N_{i}}^{i}=\partial T_{0}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
This gives us our boundary condition,
\boldsymbol{\partial}(\mathcal{T}^{\ast}-\mathcal{S}(T_{0}))=0
, i.e.,
\partial\mathcal{T}^{\ast}=\partial(\mathcal{S}(T_{0}))=\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}
.
As a result,
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\partial\mathcal{T}=\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}
.
Hence

\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}^{\ast})\geq\inf\{\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}):\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \partial\mathcal{T}=\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}\}.

Additionally, by (
32
), (
37
), and (
34
),

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}^{\ast})\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{M}_{H}(T_{N_{i}}^{i})=\liminf_{i\to\infty}\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}^{i})=\inf\{\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}):\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \partial\mathcal{T}=\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}\}.

Therefore,
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}
satisfies (
33
) as desired.
∎

Corollary 4.19
.

Let
K
be a nonempty compact and convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
H
be a concave integrand.
For any
T_{0}\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\in\mathrm{argmin}\{\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}):\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \partial\mathcal{T}=\partial\mathcal{T}_{0}\}

(39)

with
\mathcal{T}_{0}:=\mathcal{S}(T_{0})
,
we have that
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}^{\ast})=\Gamma(\mathbf{M}_{H},T_{0},\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

First, recall that
\Gamma(\mathbf{M}_{H},T_{0},\mathbb{R}^{n})=\inf\{\mathbf{M}_{H}(T):T\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \partial T=\partial T_{0}\}
since
\mathbf{M}_{H}({\pi_{K}}_{\#}(T))\leq\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)
for any
T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under the nearest point projection map
\pi_{K}
.

Since
\mathcal{S}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by Lemma
4.15
, we have
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}^{\ast})\leq\inf\{\mathbf{M}_{H}(T):T\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \partial T=\partial T_{0}\}=\Gamma(\mathbf{M}_{H},T_{0},\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
On the other hand, since each
\mathbf{M}_{H}(T^{i}_{j})\geq\inf\{\mathbf{M}_{H}(T):T\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \partial T=\partial T_{0}\}
, by (
36
), we have

\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}^{\ast})\geq\Gamma(\mathbf{M}_{H},T_{0},\mathbb{R}^{n})
as well.
Therefore,
\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}^{\ast})=\Gamma(\mathbf{M}_{H},T_{0},\mathbb{R}^{n})
as desired.
∎

5
Partial Plateau’s problem with
H
-mass

In this section, we assume that
\Phi\in\mathscr{D}^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
H
is a concave integrand, and that
B\in\mathscr{R}_{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathbf{M}_{H}(B)<\infty
.

We are interested in what we call,
partial Plateau’s problem with
H
-mass
:

\inf\{\mathbf{E}_{H,\Phi}(T):T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\partial T\preceq B\}\quad\mathrm{where}\quad\mathbf{E}_{H,\Phi}(T):=\mathbf{M}_{H}(T)-\partial T(\Phi).

(40)

To consider this problem, as in the previous section, we will consider an extension of
\mathbf{E}_{H,\Phi}
to elements in
{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for a fixed compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For any
\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by definition, there exists a
T\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\partial\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{S}(\partial T)
.
We simply write
\partial\mathcal{T}\preceq\mathcal{S}(B)
whenever
\partial T\preceq B
.
Also, we define
\partial\mathcal{T}(\Phi):=\partial T(\Phi)
and

\mathbf{E}_{H,\Phi}(\mathcal{T}):=\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T})-\partial\mathcal{T}(\Phi).

It extends the definition of
\mathbf{E}_{H,\Phi}
in (
40
) since by Lemma
4.15
, it holds that
\mathbf{E}_{H,\Phi}(\mathcal{S}(T))=\mathbf{E}_{H,\Phi}(T)
for any
T\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
K
be a nonempty, compact, and convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\Phi\in\mathscr{D}^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
H
a concave integrand, and let
B\in\mathscr{R}_{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\mathbf{M}_{H}(B)<\infty
.
For any
\mathcal{T}_{0}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a scan
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}:\mathbf{O}^{\ast}(n,m)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbf{I}_{0,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\partial(\mathcal{T}^{\ast}-\mathcal{T}_{0})\preceq\mathcal{S}(B)
, and

\mathbf{E}_{H,\Phi}(\mathcal{T}^{\ast})=\min\{\mathbf{E}_{H,\Phi}(\mathcal{T}):\mathcal{T}\in{\it Scan}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \partial(\mathcal{T}-\mathcal{T}_{0})\preceq\mathcal{S}(B)\}.

(41)

Moreover, if
\mathcal{T}_{0}=\mathcal{S}(T_{0})
for some
T_{0}\in\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

\mathbf{E}_{H,\Phi}(\mathcal{T}^{\ast})=\inf\{\mathbf{E}_{H,\Phi}(T):T\in\mathscr{R}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\partial(T-T_{0})\preceq B\}.

(42)

The proof of this theorem will make use of the following two lemmas.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
B\in\mathscr{R}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
k\geq 0
.
If
A\preceq B
, then
A
is a
k
dimensional real rectifiable current with
\mathbf{M}_{H}(A)\leq\mathbf{M}_{H}(B)
.

Proof.

Since
B\in\mathscr{R}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

B=\mathcal{H}^{k}\,\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.4pt,depth=0.0pt,width=6.0pt}\,M\wedge\theta\xi

such that
M
is an
(\mathcal{H}^{k},k)
-rectifiable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\theta:M\to\mathbb{Z}
is summable, and
\xi:M\to\wedge_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a simple
k
-vectorfield on
M
that coincides with the approximate tangent space of
M
.
By
[
15
,
Lemma 3.7
]
,
A\preceq B
implies that

A=\mathcal{H}^{m}\,\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.4pt,depth=0.0pt,width=6.0pt}\,M\wedge\sigma\theta\xi\text{ for some Borel function }\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,1].

Thus,

\mathbf{M}_{H}(A)=\int_{M}H(\sigma(x)\theta(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{m}(x)\leq\int_{M}H(\theta(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{m}(x)=\mathbf{M}_{H}(B)

(43)

since
0\leq\sigma\leq 1
and
H
is monotonically non-decreasing.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
K
be a nonempty, convex, compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
Z\in\mathscr{R}_{m-1,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\partial Z=0
, and let
\{Z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathscr{R}_{m-1,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\partial Z_{j}=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{K}(Z_{j}-Z)=0
, then there exists
C,C_{1},C_{2},\dots
in
\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\partial C=Z
,
\partial C_{j}=Z_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{K}(C_{j}-C)=0
.

Proof.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
C_{j}=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}Z_{j}
and
C=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}Z
be the cones over
Z_{j}
and
Z
respectively with the fixed vertex
v\in K

[
8
, 4.1.11]
.
Note that since
K
is convex, the cones
C_{j}
and
C
are in
\mathscr{R}_{m,K}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
By the homotopy formula,

\partial C_{j}=\partial(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}Z_{j})=Z_{j}-\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}\partial Z_{j}=Z_{j}\text{ and }\partial C=\partial(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}Z)=Z-\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}\partial Z=Z.

For any
\epsilon>0
, since
\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{K}(Z_{j}-Z)=0
, there exists an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that whenever
j\geq N
, it holds that

\mathscr{F}_{K}(Z_{j}-Z)<\epsilon
.
Thus, by the definition of the flat norm
\mathscr{F}_{K}
, there exists
R_{j}
and
S_{j}
such that

Z_{j}-Z=R_{j}+\partial S_{j},\text{ and }\mathbf{M}(R_{j})+\mathbf{M}(S_{j})<\epsilon.

Now,

\displaystyle C_{j}-C

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}Z_{j}-\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}Z=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}(Z_{j}-Z)=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}(R_{j}+\partial S_{j})

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}R_{j}+\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}\partial S_{j}=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}R_{j}+S_{j}-\partial(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}S_{j}),

where we used the homotopy formula in the last equality.
Assume that the compact set
K
is contained in the ball
B(0,\rho)
for some
\rho>0
.
Then,

\mathbf{M}(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}R_{j})\leq\rho\mathbf{M}(R_{j})\text{ and }\mathbf{M}(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}S_{j})\leq\rho\mathbf{M}(S_{j}),

and thus

\mathscr{F}_{K}(C_{j}-C)\leq\mathbf{M}(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}R_{j}+S_{j})+\mathbf{M}(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{v}\hbox{{$\times$}\kern-6.49994pt{$\times$}}S_{j})\leq\rho\mathbf{M}(R_{j})+\mathbf{M}(S_{j})+\rho\mathbf{M}(S_{j})\leq(1+\rho)\epsilon.

This shows that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathscr{F}_{K}(C_{j}-C)=0
as desired.
∎

(44)

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{M}}_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{i})

.

,

,

A note on quark and gluon energy-momentum tensors Preprint: P3H-22-088, TTK-22-28
Taushif Ahmed, Long Chen, Michał Czakon

Abstract
We discuss the constraints on quark and gluon energy-momentum tensors in QCD that follow from the requirement of Renormalisation-Group invariance of the traces of these operators. Our study covers the most general form of the latter traces, while the energy-momentum tensors themselves are only subjected to very mild constraints. We derive Renormalisation-Group equations for the two finite independent functions of the strong coupling constant and renormalisation scale of minimal subtraction which completely define the energy-momentum tensors. We demonstrate that previously proposed definitions of the renormalized quark and gluon energy-momentum tensors are special cases of our results assuming no explicit dependence on the renormalisation scale. Finally, we present MS ¯ \overline{\mathrm{MS}} -renormalised quark and gluon energy-momentum tensors at four-loop order.

Introduction

The classical rank-two symmetric energy-momentum tensor (EMT) describes the density and flux of energy and momentum of a physical system. For a closed system, its four-vector components are the conserved Noether currents associated with spacetime-translation invariance up to separately conserved terms that yield the symmetric form. The density and flux of energy and momentum are the sources of the gravitational field in Einstein’s field equations of General Relativity. The EMT is thus indispensable in order to describe the motion of gravitating systems.

The quantum EMT has attracted substantial attention after the discovery of the so-called trace anomaly
Crewther:1972kn
;
Chanowitz:1972vd
;
Chanowitz:1972da

Callan:1970yg
;

;

;

;

Since the first systematic decomposition of the EMT of QCD and the related decomposition of hadron masses

Our goal is to obtain well-defined finite quark and gluon EMTs that have Renormalisation-Group (RG) invariant traces. This last property is particularly useful in view of applications to the proton mass, since perturbation theory can barely be used at the relevant renormalisation scale. RG invariance removes the large scale dependence otherwise present in the expectation values of the traces of the quark and gluon EMTs. 2
Renormalisation of physical-basis operators

This Section follows Refs.
Hatta:2018sqd
;
Tanaka:2018nae
to a large extent, although we modify the notation and attempt to simplify the argumentation.

The physical symmetric EMT of QCD, obtained according to the Belinfante-Rosenfeld
belinfante1939spin
;
belinfante1940current
;
rosenfeld1940energy
method, is given by:

\Theta^{\mu\nu}\equiv-F^{a\,\mu}{}_{\rho}F^{a\,\nu\rho}+\frac{1}{4}g^{\mu\nu}F^{a}_{\rho\sigma}\,F^{a\,\rho\sigma}+\frac{i}{4}\sum_{q}\bar{q}\,\big(\gamma^{\mu}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\mu}\big)\,q\;,

(1)

with field-strength tensor:

F^{a}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}^{a}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}^{a}+g_{s}\,f^{abc}A_{\mu}^{b}A_{\nu}^{c}\;,

(2)

and covariant derivative:

\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}\equiv D_{\mu}-\overleftarrow{D}_{\mu}\;,\qquad D_{\mu}=\partial^{\mu}-ig_{s}A_{\mu}^{a}T^{a}\;.

(3)

A_{\mu}^{a}
is the gluon field,
q
is a quark field of mass
m_{q}
,
g_{s}
is the strong coupling constant,
T^{a}
are hermitian SU(
N_{c}
),
N_{c}=3
, generators, and
f^{abc}
are real structure constants normalised according to
\commutator{T^a}{T^b}=if^{abc}T^{c}
. Depending on the quantisation, the actual symmetric EMT derived from the quantised Lagrangian with gauge-fixing terms contains additional terms that do not contribute in matrix elements of physical on-shell states. In linear gauges, for instance, these additional terms amount to a BRS (Becchi-Rouet-Stora) variation of a local operator
Nielsen:1977sy
;
Collins:1976yq
(see also application at the three-loop order in Ref.
Zoller:2012qv
). In the present work, we will only be concerned with Eq. (
1
) which is necessary for physical applications.

The EMT Eq. (
1
) is conveniently expressed in a basis of local operators:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{\mu\nu}_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv-F^{a\,\mu}{}_{\rho}F^{a\,\nu\rho}\;,\qquad

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{\mu\nu}_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv g^{\mu\nu}F^{a}_{\rho\sigma}\,F^{a\,\rho\sigma}\equiv g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{O}_{F}\;,

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{\mu\nu}_{3}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{i}{4}\sum_{q}\bar{q}\,\big(\gamma^{\mu}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\mu}\big)\,q\;,\qquad

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{\mu\nu}_{4}

\displaystyle\equiv g^{\mu\nu}\sum_{q}m_{q}\bar{q}q\equiv g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{O}_{m}\;,

(5)

\Theta=\mathcal{O}_{1}+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{O}_{2}+\mathcal{O}_{3}\;,

(6)

where we have suppressed Lorentz indices in the last equation as we will also do below. The fourth operator,
\mathcal{O}_{4}
, has been introduced to accommodate the renormalisation of
\mathcal{O}_{2}
. Indeed, there is
Nielsen:1975ph
;
Collins:1976yq
;
Nielsen:1977sy
;
Tarrach:1981bi
:

\big[\mathcal{O}_{F}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}=Z_{FF}\big[\mathcal{O}_{F}\big]_{\mathrm{B}}+Z_{Fm}\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{B}}\;,\qquad\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}=\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{B}}\;.

(7)

where
\big[\cdot]_{\mathrm{R}}
denotes a renormalised operator with finite matrix elements, while
\big[\cdot]_{\mathrm{B}}
is defined by replacing fields, masses and coupling constants by their bare counterparts. In Eq. (
7
) and in the remainder of this publication, we neglect operators vanishing by the equations-of-motion and BRS-exact operators assuming Faddeev-Popov quantisation. The renormalisation constants
Z_{FF}
and
Z_{Fm}
are related in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme with spacetime dimension
D\equiv 4-2\epsilon
to the fundamental anomalous dimensions of the theory
Spiridonov:1984br
:

Z_{FF}=\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{\epsilon}\Big)^{-1}\;,\qquad Z_{Fm}=-\frac{4\gamma_{m}}{\epsilon}Z_{FF}\;,

(8)

where
\beta
and
\gamma_{m}
are defined through:

\derivative{\ln a_s}{\ln\mu^2}\equiv-\epsilon+\beta\;,\qquad a_{s}\equiv\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}=\frac{g_{s}^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\;,\qquad\derivative{\ln m}{\ln\mu^2}\equiv\gamma_{m}\;.

(9)

The four operators
\mathcal{O}_{1,\dots,4}
mix under renormalisation:

\big[\mathcal{O}_{i}]_{\mathrm{R}}\equiv Z_{ij}\big[\mathcal{O}_{j}\big]_{\mathrm{B}}\;.

(10)

Our main
assumption
that will influence the quark and gluon EMTs defined in the next Section can be stated as follows:

The renormalisation constants
Z_{ij}
are defined in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme for all operators but the mixing of
\mathcal{O}_{2,4}
into
\mathcal{O}_{1,3}
. The Laurent expansion of
Z_{ij}
,
i=1,3
,
j=2,4
may contain terms proportional to
\epsilon^{k}
,
k\geq 0
.

We will discuss this assumption further in Section
5
. The renormalisation-constant matrix has the structure:

Z=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix Z_{11} & Z_{12} & Z_{13} & Z_{14} \\[.2cm]
0 & Z_{FF} & 0 & Z_{Fm} \\[.2cm]
Z_{31} & Z_{32} & Z_{33} & Z_{34} \\[.2cm]
0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \endlx@physics@matrix)\;,

(11)

where we have taken into account:

1.

Z_{ij}=0
for
i=2,4
and
j=1,3
because
\mathcal{O}_{2,4}
are proportional to scalar operators, while
\mathcal{O}_{1,3}
are (reducible) rank-two tensor operators;

2.

Z_{4j}=0
for

because the canonical dimension of
\mathcal{O}_{4}
is 3, less than those of
\mathcal{O}_{1,2,3}
;

3.

Z_{22}=Z_{FF}
,

,

because
\mathcal{O}^{\mu\nu}_{2,4}=g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{O}_{F,m}

The renormalisation constants are subject to a constraint following from the non-renormalisation

;
Adler:1976zt
;
Collins:1976yq
;
Nielsen:1977sy
;
Collins:1994ee

assume
that this equality holds:

\big[\Theta]_{\mathrm{R}}\equiv\big[\Theta\big]_{\mathrm{B}}\;.

(12)

As a consequence, we obtain the constraints:

\displaystyle Z_{13}

\displaystyle=1-Z_{33}\;,\qquad

\displaystyle Z_{31}

\displaystyle=1-Z_{11}\;,

(13)

\displaystyle Z_{32}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4}Z_{FF}-Z_{12}\;,\qquad

\displaystyle Z_{34}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}Z_{Fm}-Z_{14}\;.

It turns out that the renormalisation constants that we have allowed to be defined in a non-

scheme,

,
i=1,3
,
j=2,4
, may be further constrained by considering a different operator basis containing traceless rank-two operators that correspond to an irreducible representation of the Lorentz group. With the following notation for the trace of a rank-two tensor operator:

\Tr(\mathcal{A})\equiv\mathcal{A}^{\rho}{}_{\rho}\;,

(14)

there is:

\Tr(\mathcal{O}_1)=-\mathcal{O}_{F}\;,\qquad\Tr(\mathcal{O}_2)=D\mathcal{O}_{F}\;,\qquad\Tr(\mathcal{O}_3)=\mathcal{O}_{m}\;,\qquad\Tr(\mathcal{O}_4)=D\mathcal{O}_{m}\;.

(15)

Let us define:

\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\mu\nu}_{1,3}\equiv\mathcal{O}^{\mu\nu}_{1,3}-\frac{g^{\mu\nu}}{D}\Tr(\mathcal{O}_{1,3})\;.

(16)

These operators are by construction traceless:

\Tr(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{1,3})=0\;.

(17)

We extend the set by rescaling the remaining oprators so that:

\Tr(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{2})=\mathcal{O}_{F}\;,\qquad\Tr(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{4})=\mathcal{O}_{m}\;.

(18)

The operators
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{1,\dots,4}
are related to
\mathcal{O}_{1,\dots,4}
by:

\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{i}\equiv M_{ij}\mathcal{O}_{j}\;,\qquad M\equiv\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 1 & 1/D & 0 & 0 \\[.2cm] 0 & 1/D & 0 & 0 \\[.2cm] 0 & 0 & 1 & -1/D \\[.2cm] 0 & 0 & 0 & 1/D \endlx@physics@matrix)\;,\qquad M^{-1}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix 1 & -1 & 0 & 0 \\[.2cm] 0 & D & 0 & 0 \\[.2cm] 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 \\[.2cm] 0 & 0 & 0 & D \endlx@physics@matrix)\;,

(19)

\big[\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{i}]_{\mathrm{R}}\equiv\tilde{Z}_{ij}\big[\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{j}\big]_{\mathrm{B}}\;,

\tilde{Z}=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix Z_{11} & 0 & Z_{13} & 0 \\[.2cm]

With the help of

The difference between the two bases is:

\big[\mathcal{O}_{i}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}-\big[\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{i}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}=\big(\mathbbm{1}-M^{-1}\tilde{Z}MZ^{-1}\big)_{ij}\big[\mathcal{O}_{j}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\;.

\big[\mathcal{O}_{1,3}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}-\big[\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{1,3}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}=\frac{1}{D}\Big(x_{1,3}\big[\mathcal{O}_{2}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}+y_{1,3}\big[\mathcal{O}_{4}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\Big)\;,

\displaystyle=\frac{-Z_{31}+DZ_{32}}{Z_{FF}}\;,\qquad

\displaystyle=-1-x_{3}Z_{Fm}+Z_{33}+DZ_{34}\;.

\overline{\mathrm{MS}}

The case of
Z_{ij}
,

Eqs. (

The same equations (

\beta\equiv-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}a_{s}^{l}\beta_{l-1}\;,\qquad\gamma_{m}\equiv\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}a_{s}^{l}\gamma_{m}^{(l)}\;,\qquad\gamma_{ij}\equiv\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}a_{s}^{l}\gamma_{ij}^{(l)}\;,

3
Quark and gluon EMTs with Renormalisation-Group-invariant traces

Hatta:2018sqd
;
Tanaka:2018nae

\displaystyle\big[\Theta]_{\mathrm{R}}=\Big[\mathcal{O}_{1}+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{O}_{2}\Big]_{\mathrm{R}}+\big[\mathcal{O}_{3}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\equiv\Big[\Theta_{g}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}+\big[\Theta_{q}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\;,

(35)

\displaystyle\big[\Theta_{g}]_{\mathrm{R}}=\Big[\mathcal{O}_{1}+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{O}_{2}\Big]_{\mathrm{R}}\;,\qquad\big[\Theta_{q}]_{\mathrm{R}}=\big[\mathcal{O}_{3}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\;,

\epsilon=0

In this Section, we work exclusively with renormalised operators. In consequence, we set
\epsilon=0

Our goal is to obtain a decomposition of the EMT for which both
\Theta_{g}

\Theta_{q}
have RG-invariant traces. This allows to attach a physical meaning to matrix elements of these traces. The RG invariance of the trace of

\Tr(\big[ \Theta\big]_\mathrm{R})=\Tr(\Theta)=\frac{\beta}{2}\big[\mathcal{O}_{F}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}+\big(1-2\gamma_{m}\big)\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\;.

(39)

The crucial observation is that the traces of the quark and gluon EMTs will also be expressed through
\big[\mathcal{O}_{F}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}
and
\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}
which may be combined linearly to yield two RG-invariant operators:

\derivative{\ln\mu^2}\Tr(\Theta)=0\;,\qquad\derivative{\ln\mu^2}\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}=0\;.

(40)

In terms of these invariant operators:

\begin{split}\Tr(\big[ \Theta_g \big]_\mathrm{R})&=x_{1}\big[\mathcal{O}_{F}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}+y_{1}\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\\[5.69046pt]
&=\frac{2x_{1}}{\beta}\Tr(\Theta)+\Big(y_{1}-\frac{2x_{1}}{\beta}\big(1-2\gamma_{m}\big)\Big)\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\;,\\[11.38092pt]
\Tr(\big[ \Theta_q \big]_\mathrm{R})&=x_{3}\big[\mathcal{O}_{F}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}+\big(1+y_{3}\big)\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\\[5.69046pt]
&=\frac{2x_{3}}{\beta}\Tr(\Theta)+\Big(1+y_{3}-\frac{2x_{3}}{\beta}\big(1-2\gamma_{m}\big)\Big)\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\;.\end{split}

(41)

Because the scale dependence of
a_{s}
is
\order{a_s^2}
, the scale dependence of the coefficients of the invariant operators on the r.h.s. is
\order{a_s^2}
independently of the particular choice of the constants
x_{1,3},y_{1,3}
. Hence, the scale dependence of the traces is always
\order{a_s^2}
. Let us now state the conditions that lead to RG-invariant traces:

if the constants
x_{1,3},y_{1,3}
that define the renormalised EMTs Eqs. (
36
) through the renormalisation constants Eqs. (
29
) satisfy:

\derivative{\ln\mu^2}\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{\beta}\Big)=0\;,\qquad\derivative{y_1}{\ln\mu^2}=-\frac{4x_{1}}{\beta}\derivative{\gamma_m}{\ln\mu^2}=-4x_{1}\,a_{s}\derivative{\gamma_m}{a_s}\;,

(42)

x_{3}=\frac{\beta}{2}-x_{1}\;,\qquad y_{3}=-2\gamma_{m}-y_{1}\;,

(43)

then the traces of the renormalised quark and gluon EMTs are RG invariant:

\derivative{\ln\mu^2}\Tr(\big[ \Theta_{g,q} \big]_\mathrm{R})=0\;.

(44)

The constants
x_{1,3},y_{1,3}
that yield RG-invariant quark and gluon EMT traces are thus some very general series in
a_{s}(\mu_{0})
starting from
\order{a_s(\mu_0)}
, with their dependence on the scale
\mu^{2}
determined by solving Eqs. (
42
). This introduces an explicit dependence on
\ln\mu^{2}/\mu_{0}^{2}
given that:

\derivative{\ln\mu^2}=\partialderivative{\ln\mu^2}+\beta a_{s}\partialderivative{a_s}\;.

(45)

The form of
x_{1,3},y_{1,3}
is substantially constrained, if we further assume that they do not explicitly depend on the scale
\mu
, but are at most functions of
a_{s}
. Indeed, in this case:

x_{1}=\frac{\beta}{2}\,k\;,\qquad x_{3}=\frac{\beta}{2}\big(1-k\big)\;,\qquad k\in\mathbb{R}\;.

(46)

The general solution to the second of Eqs. (
42
) is
y_{1}=-2\gamma_{m}\,k+c
,
c\in\mathbb{R}
. However, since
y_{1}
is
\order{a_s}
,
c
must vanish:

y_{1}=-2\gamma_{m}\,k\;,\qquad y_{3}=-2\gamma_{m}\,\big(1-k\big)\;.

(47)

This yields the traces:

\begin{split}\Tr(\big[ \Theta_g \big]_\mathrm{R})&=k\,\big(\Tr(\Theta)-\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\big)=k\,\Big(\frac{\beta}{2}\big[\mathcal{O}_{F}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}-2\gamma_{m}\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\Big)\;,\\[11.38092pt]
\Tr(\big[ \Theta_q \big]_\mathrm{R})&=\big(1-k\big)\big(\Tr(\Theta)-\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\big)+\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\;.\end{split}

(48)

Let us finally note that Eqs. (
48
) cover, in particular, various special cases proposed previously in the literature. For instance,
k=1
corresponds to the D2 scheme of Ref.
Metz:2020vxd
, while
k=0
corresponds to the D3 scheme of Ref.
Lorce:2021xku
.

3.1
Perturbative correction of non-RG-invariant traces

Suppose that we are given a decomposition of the EMT Eq. (
36
) characterised by some
x_{1,3},y_{1,3}
that are functions of
a_{s}
only but do not match at least one of Eqs. (
46
), (
47
). An example would be the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
constants Eqs. (
34
). In this case, the traces Eqs. (
41
) are not RG invariant:

\derivative{\ln\mu^2}\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix \Tr(\big[ \Theta_g \big]_\mathrm{R}) \\[.2cm] \Tr(\big[ \Theta_q \big]_\mathrm{R})\endlx@physics@matrix)=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix \gamma^{\mathrm{tr}}_{gg} & -\gamma^{\mathrm{tr}}_{qq} \\[.2cm] -\gamma^{\mathrm{tr}}_{gg} & \gamma^{\mathrm{tr}}_{qq} \endlx@physics@matrix)\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix \Tr(\big[ \Theta_g \big]_\mathrm{R}) \\[.2cm] \Tr(\big[ \Theta_q \big]_\mathrm{R})\endlx@physics@matrix)\equiv\gamma^{\mathrm{tr}}\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix \Tr(\big[ \Theta_g \big]_\mathrm{R}) \\[.2cm] \Tr(\big[ \Theta_q \big]_\mathrm{R})\endlx@physics@matrix)\;,\qquad\gamma^{\mathrm{tr}}\neq 0\;,

(49)

where we have used the invariance of the trace of the EMT, Eq. (
40
).
As noted above, the matrix elements of
\gamma^{\mathrm{tr}}
are always
\order{a_s^2}
, independently of the particular
x_{1,3},y_{1,3}
.
One can, nevertheless, perform a rotation of the traces to obtain RG-invariant expressions:

\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix \Tr(\big[ \Theta_g' \big]_\mathrm{R}) \\[.2cm] \Tr(\big[ \Theta_q' \big]_\mathrm{R})\endlx@physics@matrix)\equiv F\,\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix \Tr(\big[ \Theta_g \big]_\mathrm{R}) \\[.2cm] \Tr(\big[ \Theta_q \big]_\mathrm{R})\endlx@physics@matrix)\;,\qquad\derivative{F}{\ln\mu^2}=-F\,\gamma^{\mathrm{tr}}\;.

(50)

Assuming that
F
is perturbative, i.e.
F(a_{s}=0)=\mathbbm{1}
, it can be uniquely determined by solving the above RG equation for any given
x_{1,3},y_{1,3}
. Furthermore, the
result of the rotation must necessarily match the form of Eqs. (
48
) with:

k=\evaluated{\frac{2 x_1}{\beta}}_{a_s = 0}\;.

(51)

Indeed, there is for instance:

\Tr(\big[ \Theta_g \big]_\mathrm{R})-\Tr(\big[ \Theta_g' \big]_\mathrm{R})=\Big(\frac{2x_{1}}{\beta}-k\Big)\Tr(\Theta)+\Big(y_{1}-\frac{2x_{1}}{\beta}\big(1-2\gamma_{m}\big)+k\Big)\big[\mathcal{O}_{m}\big]_{\mathrm{R}}\;.

(52)

However,
F=\mathbbm{1}+\order{a_s}
implies:

\frac{2x_{1}}{\beta}-k=\order{a_s}\;,\qquad y_{1}-\frac{2x_{1}}{\beta}\big(1-2\gamma_{m}\big)+k=\order{a_s}\;,

(53)

which yields Eq. (
51
).

The above procedure applied to the D1 scheme of Ref.
Rodini:2020pis
yields the D2 scheme of Ref.
Metz:2020vxd
. In the present publication we make the following proposal:

Let the quark and gluon EMTs,
[\Theta_{q}]_{R}^{\mathrm{RGItr}}
and
[\Theta_{g}]_{R}^{\mathrm{RGItr}}
, with RG-invariant traces be defined by perturbatively correcting the respective tensors defined in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme. This corresponds to setting in Eqs. (
48
):

k=\frac{\beta_{0}\big|_{n_{f}=0}}{\beta_{0}}=\Big(1-\frac{2}{33}n_{f}\Big)^{-1}\;,\qquad\beta_{0}=\frac{11}{3}C_{A}-\frac{4}{3}T_{F}n_{f}\;,

(54)

where we have used Eq. (
34
), and
n_{f}
in
\beta_{0}
is the number of massless quarks and the color-trace normalization factor is denoted by
T_{F}
.

[\Theta_{g,q}]_{R}^{\mathrm{RGItr}}
are defined by Eqs. (
36
) with the renormalised operators defined in Eqs. (
10
), (
11
) with non-
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
-scheme renormalisation constants Eqs. (
29
) with
x_{1,3},y_{1,3}
from Eqs. (
46
) and (
47
).

5
Conclusions

In this publication, we have provided a general construction of quark and gluon EMTs with RG-invariant traces. The only two constraints that we have assumed are: 1)
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
-renormalisation of the occurring irreducible rank-two tensor operators, 2) equality of the renormalised and the bare total EMT. An alternative construction to the one presented here consists of renormalising all physical operators occurring in the definition of the quark and gluon EMTs, i.e. not only the irreducible rank-two tensor operators but also scalar operators, in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme, followed by the addition of finite contributions proportional to the two renormalised scalar operators. The two approaches are equivalent by Eq. (
37
).

A possible generalisation of our study would consist in allowing for finite contributions to the quark and gluon EMTs proportional to the two renormalised irreducible rank-two tensor operators. This would not change the analysis of the traces of the EMTs, since irreducible tensor operators are traceless. On the other hand, the additional freedom would allow constructing quark and gluon EMTs that are RG invariant themselves rather than only after taking the trace. While such a construction is certainly interesting, it is irrelevant to the physical motivation behind the study of separate quark and gluon EMTs, namely the understanding of the origin of mass of strongly interacting particles.

Our analysis has shown that the finite coefficients entering the renormalisation of the scalar operators are strongly constrained. Expressing the traces of the EMTs in terms of RG-invariant operators, we have obtained RG equations that these coefficients must satisfy. These equations still allow for rather general solutions. Hence, we have discussed the case where they do not explicitly depend on the renormalisation scale. This case covers the majority of definitions of quark and gluon EMTs presented in the literature and leaves the freedom to choose a single numeric parameter. We have proposed a value for this parameter that corresponds to a minimal modification of quark and gluon EMTs defined in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme.
The corresponding operator-renormalisation scheme removes the perturbative correction terms in Eq. (
57
), which are consequences of the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
renormalisation of the quark and gluon trace operators. It nevertheless coincides with
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
renormalisation at leading order. This feature is desirable in applications to mesons whose mass scales are in the non-perturbative regime of the strong coupling constant.
As one possible example where the physical analysis may benefit from our operator-renormalisation scheme, one may consider the so-called nucleon’s twist-four gravitational form factors in the forward limit, see e.g. Refs.
Ji:1996ek
;
Lorce:2017xzd
;
Polyakov:2018zvc
;
Tanaka:2018wea
;
Hatta:2018sqd
;
Tanaka:2018nae
;
Liu:2021gco
. It is straightforward to prove that the anomalous dimensions in the homogeneous part of the RG equation for these renormalised objects defined in our scheme will be simply those in Eq. (
32
). The latter are known to high orders in perturbation theory from the literature. We hope to return to this point in the future.

Finally, we have updated previous theoretical results for the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
-renormalised quark and gluon contributions to the proton and pion mass induced by the respective EMTs to achieve four-loop accuracy.

Note on the bispectrum and one-loop corrections in single-field inflation with primordial black hole formation Preprint: RESCEU-3/23
Jason Kristiano, Jun’ichi Yokoyama

Abstract
Primordial black holes can be formed from the collapse of large-amplitude perturbation on small scales in the early Universe. Such an enhanced spectrum can be realized by introducing a flat region in the potential of single-field inflation, which makes the inflaton go into a temporary ultraslow-roll period. In this paper, we calculate the bispectrum of curvature perturbation in such a scenario. We explicitly confirm that bispectrum satisfies Maldacena’s theorem. At the end of the ultraslow-roll period, the bispectrum is generated by bulk interaction and field redefinition. At the end of inflation, bispectrum is generated only by bulk interaction. We also calculate the one-loop correction to the power spectrum from the bispectrum, called the source method. We find it consistent with the calculation of the one-loop correction from the second-order expansion of in-in perturbation theory.



I
Introduction

Despite no observational evidence, primordial black holes (PBHs) have been a research interest
Zel’dovich and Novikov 1967
;
Hawking 1971
;
Carr and Hawking 1974
;
Hawking 1974
because they are a potential dark matter candidate
Green and Kavanagh 2021
;
Carr and Kuhnel 2020
and they can explain the origin of binary black holes found by gravitational wave events
Sasaki et al. 2016
. The most widely studied formation mechanism of a PBH is the collapse of large-amplitude quantum fluctuations on small scales generated in single-field inflation. On large scales, quantum fluctuations are tightly constrained by cosmic microwave background (CMB) observation
Aghanim et al. 2020
;
Akrami et al. 2020a
;
Akrami et al. 2020b
. Their power spectrum is almost scale invariant with the amplitude
2.1\times 10^{-9}
. On small scales, observational constraints are loose enough so it is possible to have a theory of large amplitude of the power spectrum
Nakama et al. 2014
;
Jeong et al. 2014
;
Inomata et al. 2016
;
Nakama et al. 2018
;
Kawasaki et al. 2022
;
Kimura et al. 2021
;
Wang et al. 2023
. Typically
\mathcal{O}(0.01)
amplitude of power spectrum is needed to produce a significant amount of PBHs
Carr et al. 2010
;
Carr et al. 2021
.

In our paper
Kristiano and Yokoyama
, we pointed out that such a large amplitude of small-scale perturbation can affect prediction on large scales. This is possible because cubic self-interaction between perturbation with long and short wavelengths induces the one-loop correction to the large-scale power spectrum. We considered a PBH formation from an extremely flat region in the potential that induces a temporary ultraslow-roll (USR) period
Kinney 1997
;
Yokoyama and Inoue 2002
;
Kinney 2005
;
Martin et al. 2013
;
Motohashi and Hu 2017
. In the end, we argued that our result could be generalized into any PBH formation model with a sharp transition of the second slow-roll (SR) parameter. We found that if the small-scale power spectrum reached
\mathcal{O}(0.01)
, the one-loop correction to the large-scale power spectrum would become comparable to its tree-level contribution breaking the perturbativity of the theory. Therefore, we have concluded that PBH formation in single-field inflation is ruled out.

In this paper, we explore the features of this cubic self-interaction. In Sec.
II
, we briefly review the power spectrum of curvature perturbation. In Sec.
III
, we calculate the bispectrum of curvature perturbation generated by cubic self-interaction. We explicitly confirm that it satisfies Maldacena’s theorem. In Sec.
IV
, we calculate the one-loop correction to the large-scale power spectrum by two different methods. We find the same result of one-loop correction in both methods. In Sec.
V
, we conclude our paper.

II
Two-point functions

In this section, we briefly review the analytical formula of curvature perturbation in PBH formation

Leach et al. 2001

;

;

;

;

. We consider a formation model from an extremely flat region in the potential
Ivanov et al. 1994

S=\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[M_{\mathrm{pl}}^{2}R-(\partial_{\mu}\phi)^{2}-2V(\phi)\right],

(1)

where
M_{\mathrm{pl}}
is reduced Planck scale,
g=\mathrm{det}~g_{\mu\nu}
,

and
R
are metric tensor and its Ricci scalar. Consider a spatially flat, homogeneous and isotropic background,

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+a^{2}(t)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}^{2}=a^{2}(\tau)(-\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}^{2}),

(2)

where
\tau
is conformal time. Equations of motion for the scale factor
a(t)
and the homogeneous part of the inflaton
\phi(t)
are the Friedmann equations,

H^{2}=\frac{1}{3M_{\mathrm{pl}}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}+V(\phi)\right),~\dot{H}=-\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2M_{\mathrm{pl}}^{2}},

(3)

with
H=\dot{a}/{a}
being the Hubble parameter, and the Klein-Gordon equation,

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+\frac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}\phi}=0.

(4)

Here, a dot denotes time derivative.

When CMB-scale fluctuations leave the horizon at around
\phi_{\mathrm{CMB}}
(see Fig.
1
), the potential is slightly tilted to realize slow-roll inflation, satisfying

\absolutevalue{\frac{\ddot{\phi}}{\dot{\phi} H}}\ll 1,~\epsilon\equiv-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}=\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2M_{\mathrm{pl}}^{2}H^{2}}\ll 1,

(5)

where
\epsilon
is a SR parameter. In the SR period,
\epsilon
is approximately constant. Then the inflaton goes through an extremely flat region of the potential, between time
t_{s}
to
t_{e}
, experiencing an USR period. When inflaton enters this region with
\mathrm{d}V/\mathrm{d}\phi\approx 0
, Eq. (
4

\ddot{\phi}\approx-3H\dot{\phi}
,
so
\dot{\phi}\propto a^{-3}
, which breaks SR approximation
Martin et al. 2013
. This makes
\epsilon
strongly time dependent and extremely small as

\epsilon=\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2M_{\mathrm{pl}}^{2}H^{2}}\propto a^{-6}.

(6)

We also define the second SR parameter,

\eta\equiv\frac{\dot{\epsilon}}{\epsilon H}=2\epsilon+2\frac{\ddot{\phi}}{\dot{\phi}H},

(7)

which is approximately constant and very small in SR period
\absolutevalue{\eta}\ll 1

Saito et al. 2008

3-\epsilon+\eta<0

After the USR period, the inflaton enters the SR period again until the end of inflation. In both SR and USR periods, because
\epsilon
is very small, the scale factor can be approximated as
a=-1/H\tau\propto e^{Ht}

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we consider a single-field inflation model with sharp transition of the second SR parameter from SR to USR to SR period, which leads to PBH formation. We have derived the bispectrum and one-loop correction in such a setup. We have shown explicitly that the bispectrum in squeezed limit satisfies Maldacena’s theorem. We have also clarified which terms in the cubic self-interaction of the curvature perturbation yield contribution to the squeezed bispectrum. At the end of the USR period, the bispectrum is generated by the bulk interaction and field redefinition. At the end of inflation, bispectrum is generated only by bulk interaction. We have also demonstrated that the calculation of the one-loop correction by source method and direct in-in formalism leads to the same result, confirming the conclusion of our paper
Kristiano and Yokoyama

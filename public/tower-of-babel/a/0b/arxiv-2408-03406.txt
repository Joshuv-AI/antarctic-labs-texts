Random Turán Problems for Hypergraph Expansions
Jiaxi Nie, Sam Spiro

Abstract
Given an r 0 r_{0} -uniform hypergraph F F , we define its r r -uniform expansion F ( r ) F^{(r)} to be the hypergraph obtained from F F by inserting r − r 0 r-r_{0} distinct vertices into each edge of F F , and we define ex ⁡ ( G n , p r , F ( r ) ) \mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F^{(r)}) to be the largest F ( r ) F^{(r)} -free subgraph of the random hypergraph G n , p r G_{n,p}^{r} . We initiate the first systematic study of ex ⁡ ( G n , p r , F ( r ) ) \mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F^{(r)}) for general hypergraphs F F . Our main result essentially resolves this problem for large r r by showing that ex ⁡ ( G n , p r , F ( r ) ) \mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F^{(r)}) goes through three predictable phases whenever F F is Sidorenko and r r is sufficiently large, with the behavior of ex ⁡ ( G n , p r , F ( r ) ) \mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F^{(r)}) being provably more complex whenever F F has no Sidorenko expansion. Moreover, our methods unify and generalize almost all previously known results for the random Turán problem for degenerate hypergraphs of uniformity at least 3.



1
Introduction

This paper involves the intersection of three different streams of research in extremal combinatorics: random Turán problems, Sidorenko hypergraphs, and hypergraph expansions. Throughout this paper we will work with
r
-uniform hypergraphs, or
r
-graphs for short. Given an
r
-graph
F
, we let
v(F)
denote the number of vertices of
F
and
|F|
the number of edges of
F
; and if
|F|\geq 2
, then we define its
r
-density

d_{r}(F)
by

d_{r}(F):=\max_{F^{\prime}\subseteq F,\ |F^{\prime}|\geq 2}\frac{|F^{\prime}|-1}{v(F^{\prime})-r}.

1.1
Random Turán Problems

The
Turán number

\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
of an
r
-graph
F
is defined to be the maximum number of edges that an
n
-vertex
F
-free
r
-graph can have. Let
G_{n,p}^{r}
denote the random
r
-graph on
n
vertices obtained by including each possible edge independently and with probability
p
, and when
r=2
we simply write
G_{n,p}
instead of
G_{n,p}^{2}
. We define the
random Turán number

\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F)
to be the maximum number of edges in an
F
-free subgraph of
G_{n,p}^{r}
. Note that when
p=1
we have
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,1}^{r},F)=\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
, so the random Turán number can be viewed as a probabilistic analog of the classical Turán number.

The asymptotics for
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F)
are essentially known if
F
is not an
r
-partite
r
-graph due to independent breakthrough work of Conlon and Gowers
[
4
]
and of Schacht
[
33
]
, and because of this, we focus only on the degenerate case when
F
is
r
-partite. This degenerate case seems to be very difficult, even in the graph setting
r=2
where tight bounds are known only for even cycles
[
22
,
13
]
, complete bipartite graphs
[
22
]
, and theta graphs
[
20
]
. All of the tight bounds from these examples agree with the following conjecture of McKinley and Spiro
[
20
]
, which gives a general prediction for how the function
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p},F)
should behave for all bipartite
F
. To state this conjecture, we say that a sequence of events
A_{n}
holds
asymptotically almost surely
or
a.a.s.
for short if
\Pr[A_{n}]\to 1
as
n\to\infty
, and we write
f(n)\ll g(n)
if
f(n)/g(n)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Conjecture 1

(
[
20
]
)
.

If
F
is a graph with
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)=\Theta(n^{\alpha})
for some
\alpha\in(1,2]
, then a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p},F)=\begin{cases}\max\{\Theta(p^{\alpha-1}n^{\alpha}),n^{2-1/d_{2}(F)}(\log n)^{\Theta(1)}\}&p\gg n^{-1/d_{2}(F)},\\
(1+o(1))p{n\choose 2}&n^{-2}\ll p\ll n^{-1/d_{2}(F)}.\end{cases}

In particular, this conjecture predicts that
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p},F)
should always have three ranges of behavior (i.e. it should be roughly equal to either
p^{\alpha-1}n^{\alpha},\ n^{2-1/d_{2}(F)}
, or
p{n\choose 2}
depending on the value of
p
), and moreover, it predicts that one of these ranges will be a “flat middle range,” i.e. a range where
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p},F)=n^{2-1/d_{2}(F)}(\log n)^{\Theta(1)}
is essentially independent of
p
for a sizable range of
p
close to
n^{-1/d_{2}(F)}
.

Very few results are known regarding
Conjecture

1
for general graphs. In particular, the only upper bounds known for
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p},F)
for
arbitrary
graphs
F
are due to Jiang and Longbrake
[
13
]
, with these general bounds matching
Conjecture

1
when
F
is an even cycle. However, it can be shown that in all cases other than
F=C_{2\ell}
, the bounds of
[
13
]
are weaker than what is predicted by
Conjecture

1
, so more work is needed to prove the bounds of
Conjecture

1
in every other case.

Turning now from graphs to hypergraphs, we note that not much is known about the random Turán problem for
r
-graphs with
r\geq 3
. Indeed, the only such
F
for which effective (though typically non-tight) bounds are known for are complete
r
-partite
r
-graphs
[
36
]
, loose even cycles
[
25
,
27
]
, Berge cycles
[
27
,
36
]
, expansions of hypergraph cliques and subgraphs of tight trees
[
30
,
26
]
, and non-Sidorenko hypergraphs
[
29
]
.

1.2
Sidorenko Hypergraphs

Part of the difficulty in proving results about random Turán numbers for hypergraphs is that no analog of
Conjecture

1
is known to hold for
r
-graphs, making it difficult to predict a priori what the expected behavior of
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F)
should be for a given
r
-graph
F
.
Moreover, this lack of a general conjecture for the random Turán problem of hypergraphs turns out to be closely related to the lack of an analog of Sidorenko’s conjecture for hypergraphs. To discuss this further, we need to formally define what it means for a hypergraph to be Sidorenko.

Definition 1
.

A
homomorphism
from an
r
-graph
F
to an
r
-graph
H
is a map
\phi:V(F)\to V(H)
such that
\phi(e)
is an edge of
H
whenever
e
is an edge of
F
. We let
\hom(F,H)
denote the number of homomorphisms from
F
to
H
and define the
homomorphism density

t_{F}(H)=\frac{\hom(F,H)}{v(H)^{v(F)}}.

We say that an
r
-graph
F
is
Sidorenko
if for every
r
-graph
H
we have

t_{F}(H)\geq t_{K_{r}^{r}}(H)^{|F|},

where
K_{r}^{r}
is the
r
-graph consisting of a single edge.

The study of Sidorenko hypergraphs is an extremely active area of research within extremal combinatorics, especially in the context of graphs
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
5
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
37
]
, with the most outstanding problem in this direction being the following.

Conjecture 2

(Sidorenko’s conjecture
[
35
,
34
]
)
.

Every bipartite graph is Sidorenko.

It is well known that Sidorenko’s conjecture does not extend to hypergraphs, in the sense that there exist
r
-partite
r
-graphs
F
which are not Sidorenko for all
r\geq 3
. For such hypergraphs, it was shown by Conlon, Lee, and Sidorenko
[
8
]
that
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
is always strictly larger than the trivial bound given by a simple deletion argument. This result was later extend to the random Turán setting in
[
29
]
, where in particular it was shown that essentially any counterexample to Sidorenko’s conjecture would provide a counterexample to
Conjecture

1
, and more generally that any non-Sidorenko
r
-partite
r
-graph would provide a counterexample to the hypergraph analog of
Conjecture

1
.

1.3
Hypergraph Expansions

The discussion above illustrates that getting a complete understanding of random Turán numbers of arbitrary hypergraphs is complicated by the existence of non-Sidorenko hypergraphs. To partially overcome this, we will restrict ourselves in this paper to the following class of hypergraphs which is known to behave well with respect to both random Turán numbers and being Sidorenko.

Definition 2
.

Given an
r_{0}
-graph
F
, we define its
r
-expansion

F^{(r)}
to be the
r
-graph obtained by inserting
r-r_{0}
distinct new vertices into each edge of
F
. When
F=C_{\ell}
is a graph cycle we will sometimes refer to
F^{(r)}=C_{\ell}^{(r)}
as a
loose cycle
.

Hypergraph expansions are a natural and well-studied class of hypergraphs, especially in the context of (classical) Turán numbers. For example, the famed Erdős matching conjecture
[
10
]
is equivalent to determining
\mathrm{ex}(n,M_{k}^{(r)})
when
M_{k}
is a graph matching of size
k
. There are many more results for Turán numbers of expansions
[
2
,
16
,
23
,
31
]
, and we refer the interested reader to the survey by Mubayi and Verstraëte
[
24
]
for much more on (classical) Turán problems for expansions.

One nice features about hypergraph expansions is that their Turán numbers are easy to compute for large
r
. In particular, it is straightforward to show for any
r_{0}
-graph
F
which does not contain a vertex contained in every edge of
F
that
\mathrm{ex}(n,F^{(r)})=\Theta_{r}(n^{r-1})
for all
r
sufficiently large in terms of
F
. In view of this, it is natural to ask if one can also easily establish bounds for random Turán numbers of expansions, at least in the case when
r
is large and
p
is relatively close to 1. And indeed, there have been a number of works studying special cases of this problem
[
25
,
30
,
26
,
27
]
, with perhaps the most well studied case being that of loose even cycles
C_{2\ell}^{(r)}
.

The particular problem of studying loose even cycles was initially studied by Mubayi and Yepremyan
[
25
]
, with later independent work also being done by Nie
[
27
]
.
In total, these two streams of research culminated in the following set of tight bounds for this problem whenever the uniformity is at least
4
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
27
,
25
]
)
.

For every
r\geq 4
and
\ell\geq 2
, we have a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G^{r}_{n,p},C^{(r)}_{2\ell})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\max\{\Theta(pn^{r-1}),n^{1+\frac{1}{2\ell-1}}(\log n)^{\Theta(1)}\},~~&\text{if}~p\gg n^{-r+1+\frac{1}{2\ell-1}}\\
&(1+o(1))p\binom{n}{r},~~&\text{if}~n^{-r}\ll p\ll n^{-r+1+\frac{1}{2\ell-1}}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

We emphasize here the peculiar situation that tight bounds for loose cycles are unknown when
r=3
, despite tight bounds being known for
r=2
due to
[
22
]
and for
r\geq 4
due to
Theorem

1.1
. We also note that very recently, Jiang and Longbrake
[
14
]
improved upon the bounds of
Theorem

1.1
by obtaining tight asymptotic bounds for
r\geq 5
and
p
sufficiently large through developing a supersaturation version of the celebrated delta-system method.

For general hypergraph expansions, the behavior of
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F^{(r)})
when both
r
and
p
are large was determined by Nie
[
26
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem 1.3,
[
26
]
)
.

If
F
is an
r_{0}
-graph, then for all
r\geq v(F)
and

p\geq n^{-\frac{r-v(F)}{r-v(F)+1}\cdot\frac{1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)})}}(\log n)^{\Omega(1)}
, we have a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G^{r}_{n,p},F^{(r)})=\Theta\left(pn^{r-1}\right).

In fact, a somewhat stronger result is proven in
[
26
]
where the
v(F)
term throughout
Theorem

1.2
can be replaced by a somewhat smaller integer
k
, but in either case the theorem fails to determine the behavior of
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F^{(r)})
for all ranges of
p
for any hypergraph
F
outside of certain classes of hypergraph trees.

This failure to determine the random Turán number for all values of
p
and nearly all hypergraphs is largely due to the fact that
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F^{(r)})
can exhibit wildly differing behaviors for different choices of
F
. For example, if
F
is an even cycle, then
Theorem

1.1
shows that
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F^{(r)})=\Theta(pn^{r-1})
for all
p\gg n^{\frac{-1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)})}}
. On the other hand, if
F=C_{3}
then it is known
[
30
,
26
]
that
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p},F^{(r)})\gg pn^{r-1}
for
n^{\frac{-1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)})}}\ll p\ll n^{-\frac{r-v(F)}{r-v(F)+1}\cdot\frac{1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)})}}
, which in particular shows that the range of
p
for which
Theorem

1.2
holds can not be increased for arbitrary
F
. More generally, it is known that
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F^{(r)})
will be substantially larger than
pn^{r-1}
for certain ranges of
p\gg n^{\frac{-1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)})}}
whenever
F^{(r)}
is not Sidorenko, and in these cases the behavior of
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F^{(r)})
is largely unknown even in the simple case of
F=C_{5}
.

2
Main Results

Our main result is a significant extension of
Theorem

1.2
, essentially classifying which hypergraphs
F
are such that the random Turán numbers
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F^{(r)})
for large
r
exhibits three simple ranges of behavior analogous to the ranges predicted in
Conjecture

1
for graphs. To state this classification, we say that an
r_{0}
-graph
F
with
|F|\geq 3
is
strictly
r_{0}
-balanced
if
d_{r_{0}}(F)=\frac{|F|-1}{v(F)-r_{0}}
and if
\frac{|F^{\prime}|-1}{v(F^{\prime})-r_{0}}<d_{r_{0}}(F)
for every proper subgraph
F^{\prime}\subseteq F
with at least two edges. We also let
\Delta(F)
denote the maximum degree of
F
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
F
be a strictly
r_{0}
-balanced
r_{0}
-graph with
2\leq\Delta(F)<|F|
. If
F
is Sidorenko, then for all
r>|F|^{2}v(F)r_{0}
, we have a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G^{r}_{n,p},F^{(r)})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\max\left\{\Theta(pn^{r-1}),n^{r_{0}-\frac{1}{d_{r_{0}}(F)}}(\log n)^{\Theta(1)}\right\},~~&\text{if}~p\gg n^{-r+r_{0}-\frac{1}{d_{r_{0}}(F)}}\\
&(1+o(1))p\binom{n}{r},~~&\text{if}~n^{-r}\ll p\ll n^{-r+r_{0}-\frac{1}{d_{r_{0}}(F)}}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, if there does not exist any
k
such that
F^{(k)}
is Sidorenko, then these bounds fail to hold for all
r
.

As an aside, it is unknown whether or not there exist hypergraphs
F
which are not Sidorenko and which have some expansion
F^{(k)}
that is Sidorenko
[
29
, Question 5.2]
, so the hypothesis in the“moreover” part of this statement may in fact be equivalent to saying that
F
is not Sidorenko.

While the bound
r>|F|^{2}v(F)r_{0}
from
Theorem

2.1
is not very large given the high level of generality of the theorem statement, it is natural to ask if these bounds can be improved for specific choices of
F
. In particular, the following problem is of interest.

Question 1
.

Which Sidorenko
r_{0}
-graphs
F
are such that
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)=\omega(n^{r_{0}-1})
and are such that the tight bounds of
Theorem

2.1
hold for every
r
with
\mathrm{ex}(n,F^{(r)})=\Theta(n^{r-1})
?

The hypothesis that
\mathrm{ex}(n,F^{(r)})=\Theta(n^{r-1})
is necessary for the bounds of
Theorem

2.1
to hold, as otherwise the stated bound for
p=1
would be incorrect. The condition
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)=\omega(n^{r_{0}-1})
is introduced primarily to avoid trivial solutions to this problem, since without this condition, every large expansion of a Sidorenko hypergraph would give a positive answer by
Theorem

2.1
.

As far as we are aware, there are no known examples for which either positive or negative answers to
Question

1
are known, and in particular, it may very well be the case that
Question

1
has a positive answer for
every
Sidorenko hypergraph. The closest the current literature comes to verifying any case of
Question

1
is
Theorem

1.1
for
F
an even cycle, giving tight bounds for all
r\geq 4
(c.f.
Question

1
which asks for
r\geq 3
in this case).

Although we remain unable to answer
Question

1
even for the simplest even cycle
C_{4}=K_{2,2}
, we can give a positive answer for every other
K_{2,t}
, giving the first (infinite number of) positive answers to
Question

1
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For all integers
r,t\geq 3
and all
0<p=p(n)\leq 1
we have a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G^{r}_{n,p},K_{2,t}^{(r)})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\max\{\Theta(pn^{r-1}),n^{\frac{3t-1}{2t-1}}(\log n)^{\Theta(1)}\},~~&\text{if}~p\gg n^{-r+\frac{3t-1}{2t-1}}\\
&(1+o(1))p\binom{n}{r},~~&\text{if}~n^{-r}\ll p\ll n^{-r+\frac{3t-1}{2t-1}}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

More generally, we can significantly improve upon the dependency on
r
from
Theorem

2.1
for all complete bipartite graphs
K_{s,t}
, though optimal bounds only hold for
s=2
.

Theorem 2.3
.

If
r,s,t\geq 2
are integers such that
t\geq s
and either
t\geq s^{2}-2s+3
and
r\geq s+1
or
r\geq s+2
, then for all
0<p=p(n)\leq 1
we have a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G^{r}_{n,p},K_{s,t}^{(r)})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\max\{\Theta(pn^{r-1}),n^{2-\frac{s+t-2}{st-1}}(\log n)^{\Theta(1)}\},~~&\text{if}~p\gg n^{-r+2-\frac{s+t-2}{st-1}}\\
&(1+o(1))p\binom{n}{r},~~&\text{if}~n^{-r}\ll p\ll n^{-r+2-\frac{s+t-2}{st-1}}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

It is possible that these tight bounds for
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},K_{s,t}^{(r)})
continue to hold for any
r
such that
{r\choose 2}\geq s
, as this is precisely the range where
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{s,t}^{(r)})=\Theta(n^{r-1})
for
t
sufficiently large
[
19
, Theorem 1.4]
, but
Theorem

2.3
is the best bound for
r
that can be obtained with our general methods and the known (tight) random Turán bounds for the graph
K_{s,t}

[
22
]
. This being said, in forthcoming work
[
28
]
, we use a significantly more technical argument tailored specifically towards
K_{s,t}
expansions in order to go beyond
Theorem

2.3
and prove the same result for
r
at least roughly
2s/3+1
, in particular giving another positive answer to
Question

1
for
K_{4,t}
and
t
sufficiently large.

Finally, we give a positive solution to
Question

1
for theta graphs with sufficiently many paths. Here the
theta graph

\theta_{a,b}
is defined to be the graph consisting of
a
internally disjoint paths of length
b
between a common pair of vertices
u,v
. For example,
\theta_{2,b}=C_{2b}
and
\theta_{a,2}=K_{2,a}
. Our general methods give good bounds on
\theta_{a,b}^{(r)}
whenever effective bounds for the random Turán number of
\theta_{a,b}
are known and
a\geq 3
(see
Proposition

6.7
), and in particular, recent results of McKinley and Spiro
[
20
]
give the following.

Theorem 2.4
.

If
r\geq 3
,
a\geq 100
, and
b\geq 3
are integers, then for all
0<p=p(n)\leq 1
, we have a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G^{r}_{n,p},\theta_{a,b}^{(r)})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\max\{\Theta(pn^{r-1}),n^{1+\frac{a-1}{ab-1}}(\log n)^{\Theta(1)}\},~~&\text{if}~p\gg n^{-r+1+\frac{a-1}{ab-1}}\\
&(1+o(1))p\binom{n}{r},~~&\text{if}~n^{-r}\ll p\ll n^{-r+1+\frac{a-1}{ab-1}}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

2.1
General Tools for Random Turán Problems

In the process of proving the results detailed above, we will establish a number of general tools for random Turán problems (listed out below) which we expect will be of use in future study into this area, especially for problems related to hypergraph expansions. In particular, we note that this collection of results are enough to fairly easily recover every previously known upper bound for random Turán problems of expansions
[
25
,
30
,
26
,
27
]
.

We begin with a very basic result that esatablishes effective bounds for arbitrary hypergraphs
F
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
F
be an
r
-graph with
\Delta(F)\geq 2
. If
n^{-r}\ll p\ll n^{-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}
, then a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F)=(1+o(1))p{n\choose r}.

If
p\gg n^{-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}
, then a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F)\geq\max\{\Omega(p\cdot\mathrm{ex}(n,F)),n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}(\log n)^{-1}\}.

With
Proposition

2.5
in hand, all that is needed to prove tight bounds for
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F)
are effective upper bounds on the problem whenever
p\gg n^{-1/d_{r}(F)}
, which is usually the most challenging component of random Turán problems. Establishing such bounds typically requires proving a “balanced supersaturation” result, which informally says that if an
r
-graph
H
has many edges, then one can find a collection
\mathcal{H}
of copies of
F
in
H
which is “spread out” in the sense that no set of edges in
H
lies in too many copies of
F
in
\mathcal{H}
.

More precisely, we will make use of the following definition, where here for a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
and
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
we define the degree
\deg_{\mathcal{H}}(S)
to be the number of edges of
\mathcal{H}
containing
S
, and we define
\Delta_{i}(\mathcal{H})=\max\{\deg_{\mathcal{H}}(S):S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H}),\ |S|=i\}
.

Definition 3
.

Given positive functions
M=M(n),\ \gamma=\gamma(n)
, and
\tau=\tau(n,m)
, we say that an
r
-graph
F
is
(M,\gamma,\tau)
-balanced
if for every
n
-vertex
m
-edge
r
-graph
H
with
n
sufficiently large and
m\geq M(n)
, there exists a non-empty collection
\mathcal{H}
of copies of
F
in
H
(which we view as an
|F|
-uniform hypergraph on
E(H)
) such that, for all integers
1\leq i\leq|F|
,

\Delta_{i}(\mathcal{H})\leq\frac{\gamma(n)|\mathcal{H}|}{m}\left(\frac{\tau(n,m)}{m}\right)^{i-1}.

(1)

In other words,
F
is
(M,\gamma,\tau)
-balanced if for every
r
-graph with sufficiently many vertices and a large number of edges (i.e.
m\geq M(n)
), we can find a collection of copies of
F
which are spread out in the sense of (
1
).

This definition together with a standard application of hypergraph containers yields the following result.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
F
be an
r
-graph with
r\geq 2
. If there exists a
C>0
and positive functions
M=M(n)
and
\tau=\tau(n,m)

Balanced Supersaturation for Expansions

In this section we prove our two general results Theorems

and

about lifting balanced supersaturation from

. For this, given an

-graph

we say that a set of vertices

is an

-shadow
of

3.1
Proof Sketch

To illustrate our methods, we informally consider the toy problem of finding copies of
C_{4}^{(3)}
in a 3-graph
H
where every pair of vertices in
H
has degree either 0 or
d
. In this setting we use one of two approaches to build our copies of
C_{4}^{(3)}
.

The first approach grows a
C_{4}^{(3)}
from a copy of
C_{4}
in the shadow graph
\partial H
, say a
C_{4}
which has vertex set
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}
. Because
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
has degree
d
in
H
, there exist at least
d-4
choices for a vertex
u_{1,2}\notin\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}
such that
\{v_{1},v_{2},u_{1,2}\}
is an edge in
H
. Similarly there are at least
d-8
choices for each of the vertices
u_{2,3},u_{3,4},u_{1,4}
such that
\{v_{i},v_{j},u_{i,j}\}
are edges in
H
and such that all of these vertices are distinct, and these vertices in total define a copy of
C_{4}^{(3)}
in
H
. With this procedure, each graph
C_{4}
in
\partial H
can be used to build roughly
d^{4}
copies of
C_{4}^{(3)}
in
H
. As such, if we can find a collection
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
of copies of
C_{4}
in
\partial H
, then we can use this to find a collection
\mathcal{H}
of copies of
C_{4}^{(3)}
in
H
of size roughly
d^{4}|\mathcal{H}^{\prime}|
, and moreover, it will turn out that
\Delta_{i}(\mathcal{H})
will be small provided

is, from which we will conclude that balanced supersaturation for
C_{4}
leads to balanced supersaturation for
C_{4}^{(3)}
.

The second approach involves growing a
C_{4}^{(3)}
from an arbitrary edge
h=\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}
in
H
. Because
v_{1},v_{3}
has degree
d
, we can choose in
d-3
ways some
v_{4}\notin\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}
which is in an edge with

, after which we can perform the exact same expansion procedure as above to build a total of roughly
d^{5}
copies of
C_{4}^{(3)}
from this edge

. In total this approach gives a collection of
C_{4}^{(3)}
’s of size about
d^{5}|H|
, which will do better than the previous approach depending on the relative sizes of

and

. We emphasize here that while our first approach works for essentially any

and

, this second approach will only work if the uniformity of

is sufficiently large for the tight tree condition to hold.

We informally refer to the first approach described here as “Shadow Expansion” and the second as “Greedy Expansion”. We will prove
Theorem

2.7
by using only shadow expansion, and we will prove
Theorem

2.8
by combining both of these methods.

|U|\geq\deg_{H^{\prime}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})^{\frac{1}{r-k}}\geq\left(\frac{\Delta_{[k]}}{(2r\log n)^{2^{r}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r-k}},

with this last step using our lower bound for

∎

Observe that if

. Hence by the claim together with (

\displaystyle\geq\partial_{[k-1]}\left(\frac{\Delta_{[k]}}{(2r\log n)^{2^{r}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r-k}}\geq(n^{\prime})^{\frac{r-k}{r-1}}|H^{\prime}|^{\frac{k-1}{r-1}}(2r\log n)^{-(k-1)2^{r}-\frac{1}{r-k}2^{r}}

where this last inequality holds with equality if

By the Pigeonhole Principle, we conclude that for some

.
Finally, by (

∎

We now prove our second result

Lemma 3.4
.

For

-partite

1\leq i\leq|F|
that

\Delta_{i}(\mathcal{H})\leq\frac{\gamma_{r}(n)|\mathcal{H}|}{m}\left(\frac{\tau_{r}(n,m)}{m}\right)^{i-1},

showing that
F^{(r)}
is indeed
(M_{r},\gamma_{r},\tau_{r})
-balanced.
∎

4
Optimal Balanced Supersaturation

Here we explore what can be said about (expansions)
F
which have “optimal” balanced supersaturation. More precisely, we observe the following limits for how balanced a hypergraph
F
can be.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
F
is an
r
-graph with
r,|F|\geq 2
which is
(M,\gamma,\tau)
-balanced, then for sufficiently large
n
we have
M(n)>\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
and

\tau(n,m)\geq r^{-\frac{r|F|}{|F|-1}}\gamma(n)^{-\frac{1}{|F|-1}}n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}.

Proof.

If
M(n)\leq\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
for infinitely many
n
, then one could consider an infinite sequence of
F
-free
r
-graphs
H
with
n
vertices and
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
edges, from which no collection
\mathcal{H}
of copies of
F
can be guaranteed, contradicting
F
being
(M,\gamma,\tau)
-balanced.

Finally, let
H
be the random
r
-uniform hypergraph
G^{r}_{n,p}
with
p=m/\binom{n}{r}
for some
m\geq M(n)
. With positive probability we have
|H|\geq m
and the number of copies of
F
in
H
is at most
p^{|F|}n^{v(F)}\leq r^{r|F|}m^{|F|}n^{v(F)-r|F|}
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a non-empty collection of copies of
F
in
H
satisfying (
1
). Then we have
|\mathcal{H}|\leq r^{r|F|}m^{|F|}n^{v(F)-r|F|}
. Note that
\Delta_{|F|}(\mathcal{H})=1
, so by (
1
) we have

\tau(n,m)\geq\left(\frac{m}{\gamma(n)|\mathcal{H}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{|F|-1}}m\geq r^{-\frac{r|F|}{|F|-1}}n^{r-\frac{v(F)-r}{|F|-1}}\gamma(n)^{-\frac{1}{|F|-1}}\geq r^{-\frac{r|F|}{|F|-1}}n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}\gamma(n)^{-\frac{1}{|F|-1}}.

∎

Our main goal of this section is to prove a result (
Proposition

4.4
) giving a sufficient condition for expansions to have optimal balanced supersaturation, which will be the main ingredient for proving
Theorem

2.9
. We will need two lemmas regarding expansions, the first of which shows that
r
-densities play nicely with expansions.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
r_{0}
-graph
F
with
r_{0},|F|\geq 2
and integer
r\geq r_{0}
, we have

\frac{1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)})}=r-r_{0}+\frac{1}{d_{r_{0}}(F)},

and in particular
d_{r}(F^{(r)})<\frac{1}{r-r_{0}}
.

Proof.

Let
F^{\prime}\subseteq F
be a subgraph satisfying
d_{r_{0}}(F)=\frac{|F^{\prime}|-1}{v(F^{\prime})-r_{0}}
. Then the expansion
F^{\prime\prime}:=(F^{\prime})^{(r)}\subseteq F^{(r)}
satisfies

\frac{1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)})}\leq\frac{v(F^{\prime\prime})-r}{|F^{\prime\prime}|-1}=\frac{v(F^{\prime})+(r-r_{0})|F^{\prime}|-r}{|F^{\prime}|-1}=r-r_{0}+\frac{1}{d_{r_{0}}(F)}.

Now let
F^{\prime}\subseteq F^{(r)}
be the subgraph satisfying
d_{r}(F^{(r)})=\frac{|F^{\prime}|-1}{v(F^{\prime})-r}
. Define
F^{\prime\prime}\subseteq F
to be the induced subraph on
V(F^{\prime})\cap V(F)
. Observe that
|F^{\prime\prime}|\geq|F^{\prime}|
and
v(F^{\prime\prime})\leq v(F^{\prime})-(r-r_{0})|F^{\prime}|
(since each edge of
F^{\prime}
contains
r-r_{0}
vertices not in
V(F)
). This implies that

\frac{1}{d_{r_{0}}(F)}\leq\frac{v(F^{\prime\prime})-r_{0}}{|F^{\prime\prime}|-1}\leq\frac{v(F^{\prime})-r_{0}-(r-r_{0})|F^{\prime}|}{|F^{\prime}|-1}=\frac{1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)})}-r+r_{0},

which together with the previous inequality gives the desired result.
∎

We next have a lemma which gives sufficient conditions for (expansions of)
F
to be a spanning subgraph of a tight tree. Here the
k
-shadow of an
r_{0}
-graph

is defined to be the
k
-graph on

consisting of the

-shadows of

Lemma 4.3
.

If

is a

-graph and

In particular, if

is a spanning subgraph of a tight

Proof.

The result is trivial if

is a single edge, so we will assume this is not the case. Given a pair of integers

and
\ell\geq k
, we will say that

(r,\ell)
-good if there exists some ordering

of the vertices of

and an ordered set of
r

such that (1)
h_{r}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\}

and there exists some

is contained in some

.

We first observe that

-good implies that the
r

with edges

is a tight

-tree whose

-shadow contains
F^{(\ell)}
as a spanning subgraph. In particular, the hypothesis implies that
F
is
(r_{0},k)
-good. We aim to show that
F
is
(r,r)
-good for any

Assume that we shown

to be
(r,\ell)
-good for some

and

denote any index such that

contains

(which exists by (3)). For each

, we let

be any

and any

which contains

(which exists since

(r,\ell+1)
-good, proving this claim.

Now assume we have shown

to be

be the ordered

Combining the two claims above together with the observation that

is
(r,r)
-good for all

, proving the result.
∎

We now prove our main result of the section, which roughly shows that if
F
has sufficiently high uniformity and optimal balanced supersaturation, then so does every expansion of
F
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
F
be an
r_{0}
-graph with
\Delta(F)\geq 2
which is the spanning subgraph of a tight
r_{0}
-tree such that there exists a constant
C_{r_{0}}
such that
F
is
(M_{r_{0}},\gamma_{r_{0}},\tau_{r_{0}})
-balanced with
M_{r_{0}}(n)=C_{r_{0}}n^{r_{0}-1}
,
\gamma_{r_{0}}(n)=(\log n)^{C_{r_{0}}}
and
\tau_{r_{0}}(n,m)=n^{r_{0}-\frac{1}{d_{r_{0}}(F)}}(\log n)^{C_{r_{0}}}
. Then for all
r\geq r_{0}
,
F^{(r)}
is the spanning subgraph of a tight tree and there exists a constant
C_{r}
such that
F^{(r)}
is
(M_{r},\gamma_{r},\tau_{r})
-balanced with
M_{r}(n)=C_{r}n^{r-1}
,
\gamma_{r}(n)=(\log n)^{C_{r}}
and
\tau_{r}(n,m)=n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}(\log n)^{C_{r}}
.

Proof.

We claim that it suffices to prove the result when
r_{0}=r-1
. Indeed, assuming this case we prove the general result by induction on
r
, the
r=r_{0}
case being trivial. Assume that we have proven the result up to some
r>r_{0}
. Because the result holds at
r-1
,
F^{(r-1)}
continues to satisfy the hypothesis of the lemma, so the assumed
r-1
result completes the induction. With this we may assume that
r_{0}=r-1
from now on.

It is straightforward to check that properties (a), (b), and (c) of
Theorem

2.8
are satisfied, and we have that
F^{(r)}
is a spanning subgraph of a tight tree due to
Lemma

4.3
applied with
k=r_{0}
. Let
C
be the constant guaranteed to exist from
Theorem

2.8
. Let
M_{r}(n)=Cn^{r-1}
, let
\gamma_{r}(n)=\gamma_{r-1}(n)\cdot C\log n=C(\log n)^{C_{r-1}+1}
, and let

A(n,m)=\max\{m^{d_{r}(F^{(r)})}n^{-rd_{r}(F^{(r)})+1},~mn^{1-r}\}.

We claim that property (d) of
Theorem

2.8
holds. Indeed, the lower bound
A(n,m)\geq mn^{1-r}
holds trivially since this is one of the terms in the maximum. The upper bound
A(n,m)\leq\frac{m}{M_{r-1}(n)C\log n}=\frac{m}{C_{r-1}n^{r-2}\cdot C\log n}
holds for the second term of
A
provided
{n\choose r-1}\geq C_{r-1}n^{r-2}
, which holds for
n
sufficiently large, and it holds for the first term provided

m^{d_{r}(F^{(r)})-1}n^{-rd_{r}(F^{(r)})+1}\leq\frac{1}{C_{r-1}\cdot C\log n}n^{2-r}.

Because
d_{r}(F^{(r)})<1
by
Lemma

4.2
, this inequality is hardest to satisfy at the smallest possible value of
m\geq Cn^{r-1}
, and one can verify this holds for
n
sufficiently large through the inequality
-d_{r}(F^{(r)})+2-r<2-r
.

Define

\displaystyle\tau_{r}(n,m)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{A(n,m)^{-\frac{1}{d_{r}\left(F^{(r)}\right)}}\cdot m,~\tau_{r-1}\left(n,~\frac{m}{A(n,m)\cdot C\log n}\right)\cdot C\log n\right\}

\displaystyle\leq n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}\left(F^{(r)}\right)}}\cdot C(\log n)^{C_{r-1}+1},

where this last step used
\tau_{r-1}=n^{r-1-\frac{1}{d_{r-1}(F)}}(\log n)^{C_{r-1}}=n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)}}}(\log n)^{C_{r-1}}
by
Lemma

4.2
. We conclude by
Theorem

2.8
that
F^{(r)}
is
(M_{r},\gamma_{r},\tau_{r})
-balanced.
∎

5
General Random Turán Results

Here we prove a set of general lower and upper bounds for the random Turán problem, special cases of which have appeared throughout the literature in previous papers. We begin with proving the simple bounds of
Proposition

2.5
, which we restate here for the reader’s convenience.

Proposition
2.5
.

Let
F
be an
r
-graph with
\Delta(F)\geq 2
. If
n^{-r}\ll p\ll n^{-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}
, then a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F)=(1+o(1))p{n\choose r}.

If
p\gg n^{-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}
, then a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F)\geq\max\{\Omega(p\cdot\mathrm{ex}(n,F)),n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}(\log n)^{-1}\}.

Proof.

For the upper bound, we observe that trivially
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F)\leq|G_{n,p}^{r}|
, and this is at most
(1+o(1))p{n\choose r}
a.a.s. provided
p\gg n^{-r}
by the Chernoff bound.

For the first lower bound, let
F^{\prime}\subseteq F
be such that
d_{r}(F)=\frac{|F^{\prime}|-1}{v(F^{\prime})-r}
, and let
X
be the number of copies of
F^{\prime}
in
G_{n,p}^{r}
. Note that
\mathbb{E}[X]=\Theta(p^{|F^{\prime}|}n^{v(F^{\prime})})=o(p{n\choose r})
by our range of
p
. As such we have

\omega:=\frac{p{n\choose r}}{\mathbb{E}[X]}\to\infty.

Choose
\epsilon=\epsilon(n)
any function such that
\epsilon\to 0
and
\epsilon\omega\to\infty
. By Markov’s inequality,

\Pr\left[X\geq\epsilon p{n\choose r}\right]\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon\omega}\to 0.

Thus a.a.s.
X=o(p{n\choose r})
. By the Chernoff bound,
|G_{n,p}^{r}|\geq(1-o(1))p{n\choose r}
a.a.s. for
p\gg n^{-r}
. Thus by deleting an edge from each of the
X
copies of
F
in
G_{n,p}^{r}
, we obtain an
F
-free subgraph of
G_{n,p}^{r}
with
(1-o(1))p{n\choose r}
edges a.a.s.

For the remaining lower bounds, we observe that
\Delta(F)\geq 2
implies (and is in fact equivalent to)
d_{r}(F)>1/r
by considering
F^{\prime}\subseteq F
consisting of two intersecting edges. The lower bound
n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}(\log n)^{-1}
then follows from the monotonicity of
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F)
with respect to
p
: more precisely, taking
q:=n^{-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}(\log\log n)^{-1}
, we see that
n^{-r}\ll q\ll n^{-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}
(because
d_{r}(F)>1/r
), and hence the bound above implies that a.a.s.
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,q}^{r},F)\geq\frac{1}{2}q{n\choose r}\geq n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}(\log n)^{-1}
, and hence this same lower bound continues to hold a.a.s. for
\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F)
for any
p\geq q
.

Finally, the subgraph
G\subseteq G_{n,p}^{r}
obtained by intersecting
G_{n,p}^{r}
with an
F
-free subgraph with
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
edges is a binomial random variable with mean
p\cdot\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
, so this will be at least
\Omega(p\cdot\mathrm{ex}(n,F))
a.a.s. provided
p\cdot\mathrm{ex}(n,F)\to\infty
. And indeed, due to the maximum in the lower bound we only need to show this in the case when
p\cdot\mathrm{ex}(n,F)\geq n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}(\log n)^{-1}\to\infty
since
d_{r}(F)>1/r
.
∎

We now move onto our upper bound for the random Turán problem

2.6
. Our proof uses the hypergaph container method, developed independently by Balogh–Morris–Samotij
[
1
]
and Saxton–Thomason

]
, which is fundamental in the study of random Turán problems. Here we make use of the following simplified version of Theorem 1.5 in

]

Theorem 5.2

(

]
)

For every integer

\mathcal{H}
a

-graph satisfying

Then there exists a collection

We now proving

2.6
, which we restate below for convenience.

\mathcal{C}_{0}=\{K^{r}_{n}\}

5.3
and linearity of expectation, we have

\mathbb{E}[X_{E}]\leq|\mathcal{C}|\binom{m}{E}p^{E}\leq\exp\left((\log n)^{C^{\prime\prime\prime}}\tau(n,m)+\log\left(\frac{emp}{E}\right)\cdot E\right)\leq\exp(-E).

Since
E\geq C_{2}pm\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, by Markov’s inequality, we have

\Pr[X_{E}\geq 1]\leq\mathbb{E}[X_{E}]\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, a.a.s.

\mathrm{ex}(G^{r}_{n,p},F)\leq E=\max\left\{(\log n)^{C_{2}}\tau(n,m),~C_{2}pm\right\}.

∎

Combining our results here gives a criteria to establish tight bounds for random Turán problems.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
F
be an
r
-graph with
\Delta(F)\geq 2
such that
F
is
(M,\gamma,\tau)
-balanced with
M(n)=\Theta(\mathrm{ex}(n,F))
,
\gamma=(\log n)^{\Theta(1)}
and
\tau=n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}(\log n)^{\Theta(1)}
. Then

\mathrm{ex}(G^{r}_{n,p},F)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\max\{\Theta(p\cdot\mathrm{ex}(n,F)),n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}(\log n)^{\Theta(1)}\},~~&\text{if}~p\gg n^{-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}\\
&(1+o(1))p\binom{n}{r},~~&\text{if}~n^{-r}\ll p\ll n^{-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

The upper bound comes from applying
Proposition

2.6
with
m=M(n)
, and the lower bound comes from
Proposition

2.5
.
∎

We now prove
Theorem

2.9
which we recall below.

Theorem
2.9
.

Let
F
be an
r_{0}
-graph with
2\leq\Delta(F)<|F|
which is the spanning subgraph of a tight
r_{0}
-tree.
If there exists a constant
C_{r_{0}}
such that
F
is
(M_{r_{0}},\gamma_{r_{0}},\tau_{r_{0}})
-balanced where
M_{r_{0}}(n)=C_{r_{0}}n^{r_{0}-1}
,
\gamma_{r_{0}}(n)=(\log n)^{C_{r_{0}}}
and
\tau_{r_{0}}(n,m)=n^{r_{0}-\frac{1}{d_{r_{0}}(F)}}(\log n)^{C_{r_{0}}}
; then for all

, we have

\mathrm{ex}(G^{r}_{n,p},F^{(r)})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\max\{\Theta(pn^{r-1}),n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)})}}(\log n)^{\Theta(1)}\},~~&\text{if}~p\gg n^{-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)})}}\\

\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

By

4.4
(whose hypothesis is exactly the same as that of

2.9
), we find for all

We claim that

6.1
Proof of
Theorem

2.1

The statement of
Theorem

2.1
involves Sidorenko hypergraphs, for which we make a related definition.

Definition 5
.

We say that a hypergraph is
(C,\alpha)
-Sidorenko if every
n
-vertex
r
-graph
H
with
|H|\geq Cn^{\alpha}
contains at least
C^{-1}(|H|n^{-r})^{|F|}n^{v(F)}
copies of
F
.

It is well known that being
(C,\alpha)
-Sidorenko for some
\alpha<r
is equivalent to being Sidorenko, though for our purposes, we will only need the following easy-to-prove direction of this relationship.

Lemma 6.1
.

If
F
is a non-empty
r
-graph which is Sidorenko, then there exists some
C>0
such that
F
is
(C,r-\frac{1}{|F|})
-Sidorenko.

Proof.

Let
C
be a sufficiently large constant and let
H
be an
r
-graph with
|H|\geq Cn^{r-1/|F|}
. Because
F
is Sidorenko, the number of homomorphisms from
F
to
H
is at least
(|H|n^{-r})^{|F|}n^{v(F)}\geq C^{|F|}n^{v(F)-1}
.
Since the number of non-injective homomorphisms from
F
to
H
is trivially at most
O(n^{v(F)-1})
, we conclude that, given
C
is sufficiently large, at least a half of the homomorphisms from
F
to
H
are injective, i.e. there are at least
\frac{1}{2}(|H|n^{-r})^{|F|}n^{v(F)}
genuine copies of
F
in
H
.
Thus taking
C
sufficiently large gives the result.
∎

We next state a hypergraph analog of a balanced supersaturation result for general graphs due to Jiang and Longbrake
[
13
, Theorem 3.7]
. For this, given an
r
-graph
F
with
|F|\geq 3
, we define its
proper
r
-density

d^{*}_{r}(F)
by

d^{*}_{r}(F):=\max_{F^{\prime}\subsetneq F,\ |F^{\prime}|\geq 2}\frac{|F^{\prime}|-1}{v(F^{\prime})-r}.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
F
be a non-empty
r
-partite
r
-graph with
|F|\geq 3
. If there exist positive reals
C
and
\alpha>r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F)}
such that
F
is
(C,\alpha)
-Sidorenko, then there exists a constant
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(F)>0
such that
F
is
(M,\gamma,\tau)
-balanced where
M(n)=C^{\prime}n^{r-\frac{1}{|F|}}
,
\gamma(n)=C^{\prime}
, and

\tau(n,m)=C^{\prime}\max\left\{n^{r-\frac{v(F)-r}{|F|-1}},~m^{1-\frac{1}{d_{r}^{*}(F)(r-\alpha)}}n^{\frac{\alpha}{d^{*}_{r}(F)(r-\alpha)}}\right\}.

The proof of
Theorem

6.2
is an entirely straightforward generalization of
[
13
, Theorem 3.7]
from graphs to hypergraphs, and as such we defer the exact details of this argument to an arXiv-only appendix.

The following lemma provides some inequalities that will be useful in the upcoming proofs. For this, we recall from the introduction that we say an
r
-graph
F
with
|F|\geq 3
is strictly
r
-balanced if
d_{r}(F)=\frac{|F|-1}{v(F)-r}
and if (in terms of the new notation above) we have
d_{r}(F)>d^{*}_{r}(F)
.

Lemma 6.3
.

If
F
is a strictly balanced
r_{0}
-graph with
\Delta(F)\geq 2
and
r\geq|F|^{2}v(F)r_{0}
, then

(i)

r_{0}-\frac{v(F)-r_{0}}{|F|-1}>0,

(ii)

\frac{r_{0}|F|-1}{d^{*}_{r_{0}}(F)}+\left(1-\frac{|F|}{d_{r_{0}}^{*}(F)}\right)\left(\frac{r-r_{0}}{r-1}\right)>0,

(iii)

\left(r_{0}-\frac{1}{|F|}\right)\frac{r-1}{r_{0}-1}-\frac{r-r_{0}}{r_{0}-1}<r-1,

(iv)

r_{0}-\frac{v(F)-r_{0}}{|F|-1}\geq\frac{r_{0}|F|-1}{d_{r_{0}}^{*}(F)}+\left(r_{0}-\frac{r_{0}-1}{r-1}\right)\left(1-\frac{|F|}{d_{r_{0}}^{*}(F)}\right).

Proof.

Part
(i)
is equivalent to saying
v(F)<r_{0}|F|
. And indeed, since
F
is balanced and has at least one edge,
F
has no isolated vertices, implying that
v(F)\leq r_{0}|F|
with equality only if
F
is a matching. However,
F
can not be a matching since
\Delta(F)\geq 2
, so this inequality must be strict.

For part
(ii)
, since
0<\frac{r-r_{0}}{r-1}<1
, the lefthand side of the inequality is larger than

\frac{r_{0}|F|-1}{d^{*}_{r_{0}}(F)}-\frac{|F|}{d^{*}_{r_{0}}(F)}\geq\frac{|F|-1}{d^{*}_{r_{0}}(F)}>0.

For part
(iii)
, moving all monomials with
r
to the righthand side gives

\frac{1}{|F|(r_{0}-1)}+1<\frac{r}{|F|(r_{0}-1)},

which is equivalent to
r>|F|(r_{0}-1)+1
. This clearly holds since
r\geq|F|^{2}v(F)r_{0}
.

For part
(iv)
, by moving all of the terms in the inequality depending on
r
to one side and using that
\frac{v(F)-r_{0}}{|F|-1}=\frac{1}{d_{r_{0}}(F)}
since
F
is balanced, we find that the stated inequality is equivalent to

\frac{1}{d_{r_{0}}^{*}(F)}-\frac{1}{d_{r_{0}}(F)}\geq\frac{r_{0}-1}{r-1}\left(\frac{|F|}{d^{*}_{r_{0}}(F)}-1\right),

which after multiplying by
d_{r_{0}}(F)d_{r_{0}}(F)
on both sides gives

d_{r_{0}}(F)-d_{r_{0}}^{*}(F)\geq\frac{r_{0}-1}{r-1}(|F|-d^{*}_{r_{0}}(F))d_{r_{0}}(F).

We claim that

d_{r_{0}}(F)-d^{*}_{r_{0}}(F)\geq\frac{1}{\left(v(F)-r_{0}\right)^{2}}.

Indeed, this follows from the elementary fact that if
a/b
and
c/d
are rationals with
a/b>c/d
and
1\leq b,d\leq t
, then

\frac{a}{b}-\frac{c}{d}=\frac{ad-bc}{bd}\geq\frac{1}{t^{2}}.

On the other hand,

\frac{r_{0}-1}{r-1}\cdot\left(|F|-d^{*}_{r_{0}}(F)\right)\cdot d_{r_{0}}(F)\leq\frac{r_{0}}{r}\cdot|F|\cdot\frac{|F|}{v(F)-r_{0}}.

Thus it suffices to check that

\frac{1}{\left(v(F)-r_{0}\right)^{2}}\geq\frac{r_{0}}{r}\cdot|F|\cdot\frac{|F|}{v(F)-r_{0}},

which is true since
r\geq|F|^{2}v(F)r_{0}
.

∎

Using the Lifting Theorems (
Theorem

2.7
and
Theorem

2.8
) together with
Lemma

6.1
and
Theorem

6.2
, we are now ready to show that
F^{(r)}
has optimal balanced supersaturation when
r
is sufficiently large.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
F
be a strictly
r_{0}
-balanced
r_{0}
-graph with
\Delta(F)\geq 2
. If
F
is Sidorenko, then for all
r>|F|^{2}v(F)r_{0}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that
F^{(r)}
is
(M,\gamma,\tau)
-balanced where
M(n)=Cn^{r-1}
,
\gamma=(\log n)^{C}
, and

\tau(n,m)=n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)})}}(\log n)^{C}.

Proof.

Since
F
is Sidorenko, we know that
F
is
r_{0}
-partite, and by
Lemma

6.1
, we know that there exists a constant
C_{1}
such that
F
is
(C_{1},r_{0}-\frac{1}{|F|})
-Sidorenko. Note that
d_{r_{0}}(F)=\frac{|F|-1}{v(F)-r_{0}}<|F|
, which implies
r_{0}-\frac{1}{|F|}>r_{0}-\frac{1}{d_{r_{0}}(F)}
. Thus by
Theorem

6.2
, there exists a constant
C_{r_{0}}>0
such that
F
is
(M_{r_{0}},\gamma_{r_{0}},\tau_{r_{0}})
-balanced where
M_{r_{0}}(n)=C_{r_{0}}n^{r_{0}-\frac{1}{|F|}}
,
\gamma_{r_{0}}(n)=C_{r_{0}}
, and

\tau_{r_{0}}(n,m)=C_{r_{0}}\max\left\{n^{r_{0}-\frac{v(F)-r_{0}}{|F|-1}},~m^{1-\frac{|F|}{d_{r_{0}}^{*}(F)}}n^{\frac{r_{0}|F|-1}{d^{*}_{r_{0}}(F)}}\right\}.

Let
r_{1}=|F|^{2}v(F)r_{0}
. We want to apply
Theorem

2.7
to “lift” the balanced supersaturation results for
F
to
F^{(r_{1})}
. To this end, we need to check that conditions (a), (b), (c) and (d) in
Theorem

2.7
hold. Conditions (a), (b) and (c) (that

is
(M_{r_{0}},\gamma_{r_{0}},\tau_{r_{0}})
-balanced, that
M_{r_{0}}(n)n^{-\frac{r_{1}-r_{0}}{r_{1}-1}}
is non-decreasing, and that

is non-decreasing) are straightforward to verify. To check condition (d) (that
\tau_{r_{0}}(n,m)
is non-increasing with

and that
\tau_{r_{0}}(nx,mx^{\frac{r_{1}-r_{0}}{r_{1}-1}})
is non-decreasing with
x
), note that the exponent of

in
\tau_{r_{0}}(n,m)
is either 0 or
1-\frac{|F|}{d^{*}_{r_{0}}(F)}<0
, and that the exponent of
x
in
\tau_{r_{0}}(nx,mx^{\frac{r_{1}-r_{0}}{r_{1}-1}})
is either

r_{0}-\frac{v(F)-r_{0}}{|F|-1}\text{~~or~~}\frac{r_{0}|F|-1}{d^{*}_{r_{0}}(F)}+\left(1-\frac{|F|}{d_{r_{0}}^{*}(F)}\right)\left(\frac{r_{1}-r_{0}}{r_{1}-1}\right),

which are both positive by
Lemma

6.3
. Thus condition (d) holds. By
Theorem

2.7
we know that there exists a sufficiently large constant
C_{r_{1}}
such that
F^{(r_{1})}
is
(M_{r_{1}},\gamma_{r_{1}},\tau_{r_{1}})
-balanced where

M_{r_{1}}(n)=\max\left\{M_{r_{0}}(n)^{\frac{r_{1}-1}{r_{0}-1}}n^{-\frac{r_{1}-r_{0}}{r_{0}-1}},~n^{r_{1}-1}\right\}(\log n)^{C_{r_{1}}}=n^{r_{1}-1}(\log n)^{C_{r_{1}}},

where here the second equality used
Lemma

6.3
(iii),

\gamma_{r_{1}}(n)=\gamma_{r_{0}}(n)\left(\log n\right)^{C^{\prime}}\leq\left(\log n\right)^{C_{r_{1}}},

and

\displaystyle\tau_{r_{1}}(n,m)

\displaystyle=\tau_{r_{0}}\left(n,n^{\frac{r_{1}-r_{0}}{r_{1}-1}}m^{\frac{r_{0}-1}{r_{1}-1}}(\log n)^{-C^{\prime}}\right)(\log n)^{C^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{n^{r_{0}-\frac{v(F)-r_{0}}{|F|-1}},~\left(n^{\frac{r_{1}-r_{0}}{r_{1}-1}}m^{\frac{r_{0}-1}{r_{1}-1}}\right)^{\left(1-\frac{|F|}{d_{r_{0}}^{*}(F)}\right)}n^{\frac{r_{0}|F|-1}{d^{*}_{r_{0}}(F)}}\right\}(\log n)^{C_{r_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{n^{r_{0}-\frac{v(F)-r_{0}}{|F|-1}},~n^{\left(\frac{r_{1}-r_{0}}{r_{1}-1}+r_{0}-1\right)\left(1-\frac{|F|}{d_{r_{0}}^{*}(F)}\right)}n^{\frac{r_{0}|F|-1}{d^{*}_{r_{0}}(F)}}\right\}(\log n)^{C_{r_{1}}}

\displaystyle=n^{r_{0}-\frac{v(F)-r_{0}}{|F|-1}}(\log n)^{C_{r_{1}}},

where here the second inequality uses the fact that
m\geq M_{r_{1}}(n)\geq n^{r_{1}-1}
and that
1-\frac{|F|}{d^{*}_{r_{0}}(F)}<0
, and the last equality is implied by
Lemma

6.3
(iv).

For
r>r_{1}
, we want to get rid of the logarithmic factor in
M(n)
by using
Theorem

2.8
on
F^{(r-1)}
. Note that
r>r_{1}>v(F)
, and thus
F^{(r)}
is a spanning subgraph of a tight
r
-tree by
Lemma

4.3
(since the
r_{0}
-shadow of a single

-edge contains
F
as a spanning subgraph). Suppose that we have inductively proven that there exists a constant
C_{r-1}
such that
F^{(r-1)}
is
(M_{r-1},\gamma_{r-1},\tau_{r-1})
-balanced where
M_{r-1}(n)=n^{r-2}(\log n)^{C_{r-1}}
,
\gamma_{r-1}(n)=(\log n)^{C_{r-1}}
, and

\tau_{r-1}(n,m)=n^{r_{0}-\frac{v(F)-r_{0}}{|F|-1}}(\log n)^{C_{r-1}},

noting that we have proved this for the case
r-1=r_{1}
. It is straightforward to check that conditions (a), (b) and (c) in
Theorem

2.8
hold. Let
C_{r}
be a sufficiently large constant and let
M_{r}(n)=C_{r}n^{r-1}
,
\gamma_{r}(n)=\gamma_{r-1}(n)\cdot C_{r}(\log n)
, and let

A=A(n,m)=\frac{m}{M_{r-1}(n)\cdot C_{r}\log n}=\frac{m}{n^{r-2}\cdot C_{r}(\log n)^{C_{r-1}+1}}.

Clearly, condition (d) in
Theorem

2.8
holds. Thus by
Theorem

2.8
,
F^{(r)}
is
(M_{r},\gamma_{r},\tau_{r})
-balanced where there exists a sufficiently large constant
C
such that

\tau_{r}(n,m)=\max\left\{A(n,m)^{-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)})}}\cdot m,~\tau_{r-1}\left(n,~\frac{m}{A(n,m)\cdot C_{r}\log n}\right)\cdot C_{r}\log n\right\}\leq n^{r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)})}}(\log n)^{C},

where here the last inequality comes from the definition of

together with the fact from

that

r_{0}-\frac{v(F)-r_{0}}{|F|-1}=r_{0}-\frac{1}{d_{r_{0}}(F)}=r-\frac{1}{d_{r}(F^{(r)})},

and that (using

)

∎

As an aside, we note that it is perhaps a little surprising that although the general result of Jiang and Longbrake

typically does not give optimal balanced supersaturation results for most

-graphs

, the proof of

Finally, we will need the following result from

in order to prove the “moreover” portion of

.

Theorem 6.5

(
[

]
)
.

If
F
is an

and
\frac{v(F)-r}{|F|-1}<r
which is not Sidorenko, then there exists some
\epsilon>0
such that for any

\mathrm{ex}(G_{n,p}^{r},F)\geq n^{r-\frac{v(F)-r}{|F|-1}-o(1)}(pn^{\frac{v(F)-r}{|F|-1}})^{\epsilon}.

The exact statement of

in fact replaces the

\frac{s(F)}{e(F)-1+s(F)}
where

measures how “far” from Sidorenko

is, but all that we need here is

.

Proof of
Theorem

2.1
.

The first part of the theorem regarding Sidorenko

follows immediately from

2.9
and

6.4
. For the moreover portion, let
F
be as in the hypothesis of the theorem such that

is not Sidorenko for all

. By the definition of the expansion

, there exists some

Before moving on, we emphasize that the bound of

obtained in

can be improved upon significantly provided more information about the hypergraph

is known. Notably, one can obtain better bounds here provided one has better bounds on either (a) the difference

, (b) the smallest

such that

such that

-Sidorenko for some

.

,

and

, then a more involved calculation shows that the tight bounds of

In particular, when

, we can use

,

,

, and

to obtain tight bounds for

, recovering the known bounds for even cycles as stated in

. However, we emphasize that one can often obtain better bounds for

than even this optimized version of our result whenever more precise balanced supersaturation results are known beyond the general results of Jiang and Longbrake. In particular, for the case

, this optimized result above gives tight bounds only for

, while (as we show below) the true answer is

for

.

6.2
Proofs of

2.2
and

Our proof for theta graphs relies on

, for which we need the following.

Lemma 6.6
.

For all

, the 3-graph

is a spanning subgraph of a tight

-tree.

Proof.

By

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we will work on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
. Let
\Omega
be
homogeneous of degree zero, integrable on
the unit sphere
{S}^{d-1}
and have mean value zero. Define the singular integral operator

{T}_{\Omega}
by

(1.1)

\displaystyle{T}_{\Omega}f(x)={\rm p.\,v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Omega(y^{\prime})}{|y|^{d}}f(x-y)dy,

where and in the following,
y^{\prime}=y/|y|
for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This operator was introduced by
Calderón and Zygmund
[
2
]
, and then studied by many authors
in the last sixty years. Calderón and Zygmund
[
3
]
proved that
if
\Omega\in L\log L({S}^{d-1})
, then
T_{\Omega}
is bounded on

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
p\in(1,\,\infty)
. Ricci
and Weiss
[
22
]
improved the result of Calderón and Zygmund, and
showed that
\Omega\in H^{1}(S^{d-1})
guarantees the

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
boundedness on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
p\in(1,\,\infty)
. Seeger
[
24
]
showed that
\Omega\in L\log L(S^{d-1})
is a sufficient condition such that
T_{\Omega}
is bounded
from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
L^{1,\,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, see also
[
4
]
.

Let
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(p\in[1,\,\infty))
be the weight function class of Muckenhoupt, that is,

A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{w:\,w\,\hbox{is\,\,nonnegative\,\,and\,\,locally\,\,integrable\,\,in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d},\,[w]_{A_{p}}<\infty\},

where and in what follows,

[w]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{Q}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)dx\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w^{1-p^{\prime}}(x)dx\Big)^{p-1},\,\,\,p\in(1,\,\infty),

and

[w]_{A_{1}}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{Mw(x)}{w(x)},

with
M
the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator. Given
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, the constant
[w]_{A_{p}}
just defined is known the
A_{p}
constant of
w
(see
[
10
, Chapter 9]
for the properties of
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
). For
w\in\cup_{p>1}A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

[w]_{A_{\infty}}
, the
A_{\infty}
constant of
w
, is defined by

[w]_{A_{\infty}}=\sup_{Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}M(w\chi_{Q})(x)dx,

see
[
8
]
or
[
26
]
. Duoandikoetxea and Rubio de Francia
[
6
]
considered the boundedness on weighted
L^{p}
spaces with
A_{p}
weights for
T_{\Omega}
when
\Omega\in L^{q}(S^{d-1})
for some
q\in(1,\,\infty)
, see also

[
25
]
.
In the last
decade, considerable attention has been paid to the quantitative weighted bounds for the operator
T_{\Omega}
. The first work in this area is the paper of Hytönen, Roncal and Tapiola

[
13
]
.
By a quantitative weighted estimate for the Calderón-Zygmund operators, approximation to the identity and interpolation, Hytönen et al.
(see Theorem 1.4 in
[
13
]
) proved that

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be homogeneous of degree zero, have mean value zero on
S^{d-1}
and
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
.
Then for
p\in(1,\,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|T_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\max\{[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},\,[w^{1-p^{\prime}}]_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\}

\displaystyle\times\max\{[w]_{A_{\infty}},\,[w^{1-p^{\prime}}]_{A_{\infty}}\}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,w)}.

For a family of cubes
\mathcal{S}
, we say that
\mathcal{S}
is
\eta
-sparse,
\eta\in(0,\,1)
, if for each fixed
Q\in\mathcal{S}
, there exists a measurable subset
E_{Q}\subset Q
, such that
|E_{Q}|\geq\eta|Q|
and
\{E_{Q}\}
are pairwise disjoint.

Let
\Phi_{1},\,\Phi_{2}
be two Young functions, and
\mathcal{S}
be a sparse family. Associated with
\Phi_{1},\,\Phi_{2}
and
\mathcal{S}
, the bilinear sparse operator
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},\,\Phi_{1},\,\Phi_{2}}
is defined by

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},\,\Phi_{1},\,\Phi_{2}}(f_{1},\,f_{2})=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\langle|f_{1}|\rangle_{\Phi_{1},Q}\langle|f_{2}|\rangle_{\Phi_{2},Q}|Q|.

where and in what follows,
\langle|f|\rangle_{\Phi_{1},\,Q}
is the Luxemburgh norm defined by

\langle|f|\rangle_{\Phi_{1},Q}=\inf\Big\{\lambda>0:\,\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\Phi_{1}\big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\big)dx\leq 1\Big\},

see
[
21
]
. For the case of
\Phi_{1}(t)=t^{p}
, we denote
\langle f\rangle_{\Phi_{1},\,Q}
by
\langle f\rangle_{p,Q}
for simplicity.

Conde-Alonso, Culiuc, Di Plinio and Ou
[
5
]
proved that for all
p\in(1,\,\infty)
, bounded functions
f
and
g
with compact supports,

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)T_{\Omega}f(x)dx\Big|\lesssim p^{\prime}\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}\sup_{\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},\,L^{1},L^{p}}(f,\,g),

where the supremum is take over sparse families.
Using this bilinear sparse domination and some new estimates for bilinear sparse operators, Li, Pérez, Rivera-Rios and Roncal
[
19
]
proved that

\displaystyle\|T_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\max\{[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},\,[w^{1-p^{\prime}}]_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\}

\displaystyle\times\min\{[w]_{A_{\infty}},\,[w^{1-p^{\prime}}]_{A_{\infty}}\}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,w)},

which improved (
1.2
). Moreover, Li et al.
[
19
]
proved that for any
w\in A_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\|T_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{1,\,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}[w]_{A_{1}}[w]_{A_{\infty}}\log({\rm e}+[w]_{A_{\infty}})\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,w)},

and established the weighted inequality of Coifman-Fefferman type that for
p\in[1,\,\infty)
and
w\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\|T_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,w)}\lesssim\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{2}\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,w)}.

Now let
T_{\Omega}^{*}
be the maximal singular integral operator associated to
T_{\Omega}
, that is,

T_{\Omega}^{*}f(x)=\sup_{\epsilon>0}\Big|\int_{|y|>\epsilon}\frac{\Omega(y)}{|y|^{d}}f(x-y)dy\Big|.

Also,
\Omega\in L\log L(S^{d-1})
is a sufficient condition such that
T_{\Omega}^{*}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
p\in(1,\,\infty)
(see
[
3
]
). Duoandikoetxea and Rudio de Francia
[
6
]
proved that if
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
, then
T_{\Omega}^{*}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,w)
for all
p\in(1,\,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For a long time, the weak type endpoint estimate for
T_{\Omega}^{*}
is an open problem even for
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
. Honzík
[
11
]
established a local weak type endpoint estimate for
T_{\Omega}^{*}
when
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
. Bhojak and Mohanty
[
1
]
improved the result of Honzík, and proved the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be homogeneous of degree zero, have mean value zero on
S^{d-1}
, and
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
. Then for all
\alpha>0
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,T_{\Omega}^{*}f(x)>\alpha\}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim[w]_{A_{1}}[w]_{A_{\infty}}\log({\rm e}+[w]_{A_{\infty}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi\big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\alpha}\big)dx.

Throughout this paper,
\Phi(t)=t\log\log({\rm e}^{2}+t)
.

Di Plinio, Hytönen and Li
[
7
]
established a bilinear sparse domination for
T_{\Omega}^{*}
when
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
. They proved that

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be homogeneous of degree zero, have mean value zero on
S^{d-1}
, and
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
. Then for each
p\in(1,\,\infty)
, bounded functions
f
and
g
with compact supports, there exists a sparse family
\mathcal{S}
, such that

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)T_{\Omega}f(x)dx\Big|\lesssim p^{\prime}\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}\sup_{\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},\,L^{p},L^{p}}(f,\,g).

Moreover, Di Plinio et al.
[
7
]
established the following weighted norm inequality of Coifman-Fefferman type from their sparse estimate (see
[
7
, Theorem B]
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Omega
be homogeneous of degree zero, have mean value zero on
S^{d-1}
and
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
. Let
r\in(1,\,\infty)
.
Then for
p\in[1,\,\infty)
and
w\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\|T^{*}_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,w)}\lesssim r^{\prime}\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{2}\|M_{r}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,w)}.

provided that
f
is a bounded function with compact support, where and in the following,
M_{r}f(x)=[M(|f|^{r})(x)]^{1/r}
.

The purpose of this paper is to establish a more refined bilinear sparse domination for
T_{\Omega}^{*}
. Our main result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Omega
be homogeneous of degree zero, have mean value zero on
S^{d-1}
, and
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
. Then for each
r\in(1,\,\infty)
and bounded function
f
, there exists a sparse family
\mathcal{S}
, such that for all bounded function
g
with compact support,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)T^{*}_{\Omega}f(x)dx\Big|\lesssim\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}\big(r^{\prime}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},\,L^{1},L^{r}}(f,\,g)+\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},L^{\Phi},L^{r}}(f,g)\big).

Theorem
1.5
leads to an alternative proof of Theorem
1.2
. We will show this in Section 3. Moreover, the sparse domination (
1.4
) is more refined than the sparse domination in Theorem
1.3
. This follows from the fact that for
r\in(1,\,\infty)
and cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\langle|f|\rangle_{\Phi,\,Q}\lesssim\log r^{\prime}\langle|f|\rangle_{Q}+\langle|f|\rangle_{r,\,Q}.

To prove this, we may assume that
\langle|f|\rangle_{r,\,Q}=1
. Write

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\Phi(|f(x)|)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Q(|f|>4{\rm e}^{2})}\Phi(|f(x)|)dx+\int_{Q(|f|\leq 4{\rm e}^{2})}\Phi(|f(x)|)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{Q(|f|>4{\rm e}^{2})}\Phi(|f(x)|)dx+2\int_{Q}|f(x)|dx.

Recall that when
t>4{\rm e}^{2}
,
\log t\leq\frac{t^{r-1}}{r-1}
and

\displaystyle\log\log({\rm e}^{2}+t)\leq\log\Big(\frac{(2t)^{r-1}}{r-1}\Big)\leq(r-1)\log 2+t^{r-1}+\log(\frac{1}{r-1}).

This implies that

\displaystyle\int_{Q(|f|>4{\rm e}^{2})}\Phi(|f(x)|)dx\lesssim\int_{Q}|f(x)|^{r}dx+\int_{Q}|f(x)|dx\log r^{\prime}.

Combining the estimate above leads to that

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\Phi(|f(x)|)dx\lesssim 1+\log(r^{\prime})\langle|f|\rangle_{Q},

which yields (
1.5
).

always denotes a

to denote that there exists a positive constant

C
such that
A\leq CB
. Constant with subscript such as
c_{1}
, does not change
in different occurrences. For a set

,

\chi_{E}
denotes its characteristic function. For a cube

, we use

to denote the side length of

, and
\lambda Q
denotes the cube with the same center as
Q
whose
side length is
\lambda

Q
. For a suitable function

, we denote
\widehat{f}
the Fourier transform of

.

2.
An endpoint estimate

Given a sublinear operator
T
, define the maximal operator
{M}_{\lambda,\,T}
by

{M}_{\lambda,\,T}f(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\big(T(f\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash 3Q})\chi_{Q}\big)^{*}(\lambda|Q|),\,\,(0<\lambda<1),

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
containing
x
, and
h^{*}
denotes the non-increasing rearrangement of
h
.
The operator
{M}_{\lambda,\,T}
was introduced by Lerner
[
16
]
, and is useful in the study of bilinear sparse dominations for the operator
T_{\Omega}
, see
[
16
,
12
]
. Also, for
p\in(0,\,\infty]
, define the maximal operator
\mathscr{M}_{p,T}
by

\mathscr{M}_{p,\,T}f(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T(f\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash 3Q})(y)|^{p}dy\Big)^{1/p}.

We denote
\mathscr{M}_{\infty,\,T}
by
\mathcal{M}_{T}
.
Obviously, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
p\in(0,\,\infty]
,

M_{\lambda,\,T}f(x)\leq\lambda^{-1/p}\mathscr{M}_{p,\,T}f(x).

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a radial function such that
{\rm supp}\,\eta\subset\{x:\frac{1}{2}\leq|x|\leq 2\}
and

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\eta_{j}(x)=1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
, where
\eta_{j}(x)=\eta(2^{-j}x)
.
For
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and a function
\Omega
on
S^{d-1}
, let
K_{j}(x)=\frac{\Omega(x)}{|x|^{d}}\eta_{j}(x)
. Then the singular integral operator
T_{\Omega}
can be written as
T_{\Omega}f(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}K_{j}*f(x)
. Let
T_{\Omega}^{**}
be the lacunary maximal operator associated with
T_{\Omega}
, defined by

T_{\Omega}^{**}f(x)=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\sum_{j\geq k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K_{j}(x-y)f(y)dy\Big|.

Our main purpose in this section is to establish the following weak type endpoint estimate for
M_{\lambda,\,T_{\Omega}^{**}}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be homogeneous of degree zero and have mean value zero.
Suppose that
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
with
\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}=1
. Then for
\lambda\in(0,\,1)
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:M_{\lambda,T_{\Omega}^{**}}f(x)>\alpha\}|\lesssim\big(1+\log\big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\big)\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(x)|}{\alpha}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi\big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\alpha}\big)dx.

To prove Theorem
2.1
, we will employ some lemmas.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
16
]

Let
T
be a sublinear operator which is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then for
\lambda\in(0,\,1)
,

\|M_{\lambda,\,T}f\|_{L^{2,\,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\lambda^{-1/2}\|T\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We recall some definitions from
[
17
]
. Given
a cube
Q_{0}
, we denote by
\mathcal{D}(Q_{0})
the set of dyadic cubes with respect to
Q_{0}
, that is, the cubes from
\mathcal{D}(Q_{0})
are formed by repeating subdivision of
Q_{0}
and each of descendants into
2^{n}
congruent sub-cubes.

Let
\mathscr{D}
be a collection of cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We say that
\mathscr{D}
is a dyadic lattice if

(i)

if
Q\in\mathscr{D}
, then
\mathcal{D}(Q)\subset\mathscr{D}
;

(ii)

every two cubes
Q^{\prime},\,Q^{\prime\prime}\in\mathscr{D}
have a common ancestor, that is, there
exists
Q\in\mathscr{D}
such that
Q^{\prime},\,Q^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{D}(Q)
;

(iii)

for every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists a cube
Q\in\mathscr{D}

containing
K
.

We also have the following three lattice theorem, see
[
16
, Theorem 2.4]
or
[
17
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every dyadic lattice

\mathscr{D}
, there exist
3^{d}
dyadic lattices
\mathscr{D}_{1}
, …,
\mathscr{D}_{3^{d}}
such that

\{3Q:Q\in\mathscr{D}\}=\cup_{j=1}^{3^{d}}\mathscr{D}_{j}

and for every cube
Q\in\mathscr{D}
and
j=1,\,\dots,\,3^{d}
, there exists a unique cube

R\in\mathscr{D}_{j}
of side length
\ell(R)=3\ell(Q)
containing
Q
.

Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a nonnegative radial function such that

{\rm supp}\,\psi\subset\{x:\,|x|\leq 1/4\}
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x)dx=1
.
For
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, set
\psi_{j}(x)=2^{-jd}\psi(2^{-j}x)
. For a positive integer
l
, define

K^{l}(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}K_{j}*\psi_{j-l}(x).

Let
T_{l}
,
W_{l}
,
T_{l}^{**}
be the operators defined by

T_{l}f(x)=K^{l}*f(x),

W_{l}f(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(K_{j}-K_{j}*\psi_{j-l}\big)*f(x),

and

T_{l}^{**}f(x)=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\sum_{j\geq k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K_{j}*\psi_{j-l}(x-y)f(y)dy\Big|

respectively.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Omega
be homogeneous of degree zero and have mean value zero, and let
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
. Then, for every
0<\kappa<1
, one has

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|T_{\Omega}f-T_{l}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{-\kappa l}\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|W^{**}_{l}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{-\kappa l}\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where and in the following,

\displaystyle W^{**}_{l}f(x)=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\sum_{j\geq k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(K_{j}*\psi_{j-l}(x-y)-K_{j}(x-y)\big)f(y)dy\Big|.

Proof.

The estimate (
2.2
) was proved in
[
25
, p. 396]
. To prove the estimate (
2.3
), we will employ the
ideas used in
[
6
]
, with appropriate modifications. Let
S_{l}^{j}f(x)=\big(K_{j}-K_{j}*\psi_{j-l}\big)*f(x)
. Let

\omega\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

{\rm supp}\,\omega\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,|x|\leq 1\},\,\,\omega(x)\equiv 1\,\,\hbox{if}\,\,|x|\leq 1/2.

For each integer
k
, let

\Psi_{k}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\widehat{\Psi}_{k}(\xi)=\omega(2^{k}\xi).
For each fixed
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, write

\displaystyle\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}S_{l}^{j}*f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}*\big(T_{\Omega}f-T_{l}f\big)(x)-\Psi_{k}*\Big(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k-1}S_{l}^{j}*f\Big)(x)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(\delta-\Psi_{k})*S_{l}^{j}*f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm I}_{l}^{k}f(x)+{\rm II}_{l}^{k}f(x)+{\rm III}_{l}^{k}f(x),

with
\delta
the Dirac distribution. It is obvious that

\big|{\rm I}_{l}^{k}f(x)\big|\lesssim M\big(T_{\Omega}f-T_{l}f\big)(x),

and so by (
2.2
),

\big\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|{\rm I}_{l}^{k}f|\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|T_{\Omega}f-T_{l}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{-\kappa l}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Now write

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|{\rm II}_{l}^{k}f(x)|\lesssim\Big(\sum_{u=-\infty}^{\infty}\Big|\Psi_{u}*\sum_{j=-\infty}^{u-1}S_{l}^{j}*f(x)\Big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Note that for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\Big|\omega(2^{u}\xi)\sum_{j=-\infty}^{u-1}\widehat{K_{j}}(\xi)\big(\widehat{\psi}(2^{j-l}\xi)-1\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big|\omega(2^{u}\xi)\sum_{j=-\infty}^{u-1}|2^{j-l}\xi|\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-l}\omega(2^{u}\xi)|2^{u}\xi|.

It then follows from Plancherel’s theorem that

\displaystyle\big\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|{\rm II}_{l}^{k}f|\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\sum_{u=-\infty}^{\infty}\Big\|\Psi_{u}*\sum_{j=-\infty}^{u-1}S_{l}^{j}*f\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{u=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\sum_{j=-\infty}^{u-1}\widehat{K_{j}}(\xi)\big(\widehat{\psi}(2^{j-l}\xi)-1\big)\Big|^{2}|\omega(2^{u}\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim 2^{-2l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{u=-\infty}^{\infty}|\omega(2^{u}\xi)|^{2}|2^{u}\xi|^{2}\big|\widehat{f}(\xi)\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi.

Recalling that
|\omega(x)|\lesssim\min\{1,\,|x|^{-2}\}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{u=-\infty}^{\infty}|\omega(2^{u}\xi)|^{2}|2^{u}\xi|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{u:\,|2^{u}\xi|\leq 1}|2^{u}\xi|^{2}+\sum_{u:\,|2^{u}\xi|>1}|2^{u}\xi|^{-2}\lesssim 1.

This, in turn, implies that

\displaystyle\big\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|{\rm II}_{l}^{k}f|\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{-l}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

It remains to estimate term

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|{\rm III}_{l}^{k}f|
. write

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|{\rm III}_{l}^{k}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|(\delta-\Psi_{k})*S_{l}^{j+k}*f(x)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{u=-\infty}^{\infty}\Big|(\delta-\Psi_{u-j})*S_{l}^{u}*f(x)\Big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

As it is well known (see
[
6
]
), there exists a constant
\kappa_{1}\in(0,\,1)
such that

|\widehat{K_{j}}(\xi)|\lesssim\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}|2^{j}\xi|^{-\kappa_{1}},

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{u=-\infty}^{\infty}\Big|(\delta-\Psi_{u-j})*S_{l}^{u}*f(x)\Big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{u=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|1-\omega(2^{u-l}\xi)\big|^{2}\Big|\widehat{K}_{u}(\xi)\big(\widehat{\psi}(2^{u-l}\xi)-1\big)\Big|^{2}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{u=-\infty}^{\infty}|1-\omega(2^{u-l}\xi)|^{2}|2^{u}\xi|^{-2\kappa_{1}}\big)\big|\widehat{f}(\xi)\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim 2^{-2l\kappa_{1}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},

since for each
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{u=-\infty}^{\infty}|1-\psi(2^{u-l}\xi)|^{2}|2^{u}\xi|^{-2\kappa_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{u:\,|2^{u}\xi|\geq 2^{l-1}}|2^{u}\xi|^{-2\kappa_{1}}\lesssim 2^{-2l\kappa_{1}}.

Thus,

\big\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|{\rm III}_{l}^{k}f|\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{-\kappa_{1}l}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Combining the estimates
for
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|{\rm I}_{l}^{k}f|
,
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|{\rm II}_{l}^{k}f|
and
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|{\rm III}_{l}^{k}f|
leads to our desired conclusion and then completes the proof of Lemma
2.4
.∎

m\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\displaystyle\|H^{**}_{m}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{-\kappa 2^{m}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

see Lemma 8 in

for another proof of (
2.4
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Omega
be homogeneous of degree zero and have mean value zero.
Suppose that
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
. Then the operators
T_{l}
and
T_{l}^{**}
are all bounded from

to

with bound no more than
Cl\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}
.

Proof.

The proof of Lemma
2.5
was essentially given in
[
16
, Section 2]
, with a different definition of
T^{l}
. We present the proof here for the sake of self-contained. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}=1
. Obviously,
K^{l}
satisfies the size condition that

(2.5)

\displaystyle|K^{l}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K_{j}(x-y)||\psi_{j-l}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{|x|^{d}}\chi_{\{2^{j-2}\leq|x|\leq 2^{j+2}\}}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\psi_{j-l}(y)|dy\lesssim|x|^{-d}.

On the other hand, a trivial computation leads to that

\displaystyle|\nabla K^{l}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K_{j}(y)||\nabla\psi_{j-l}(x-y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{l-j}\frac{1}{|x|^{d}}\chi_{\{2^{j-2}\leq|x|\leq 2^{j+2}\}}(|x|)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K_{j}(y)|dy\lesssim 2^{l}|x|^{-d-1}.

Therefore, for
x,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|y|\leq|x|/4
, we have that

(2.6)

\displaystyle|K^{l}(x-y)-K^{l}(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{d}}\omega\big(\frac{|y|}{x}\big),

with

\omega(t)=\min\{1,\,2^{l}t\}.

Obviously,

\int_{0}^{1}\omega(t)\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim l.

Lemma
2.4
tells us that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|T_{l}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Inequalities (
2.5
)-(
2.7
), via argument in
[
9
, Chap. 4.3]
, implies that

\|T_{l}^{**}f\|_{L^{1,\,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim l\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\Omega
be homogeneous of degree zero and have mean value zero.

and

The arguement is similar to the proof of Lemma 2.3 in
[
15
]
. Again we assume that
\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}=1

Let

be a cube,

, and

be the closed ball centered at

and having radius

. Then

. For

and
l\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle T_{l,j}f(x)=K_{j}*\psi_{j-l}*f(x).

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|T_{l}^{**}(f\chi_{B_{x}\backslash 3Q})(\xi)|+|T_{l}^{**}(f\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash B_{x}})(x)|

\displaystyle+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\sum_{j\geq k}\Big(T_{l,j}(f\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash B_{x}})(\xi)-T_{l,j}(f\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash B_{x}})(x)\Big)\Big|.

2.5
, we get the lemma.

,

(3.3)

\displaystyle|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,\mathscr{M}_{p,T_{\Omega}^{*}}f(x)>\alpha\}|\lesssim p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(x)|}{\alpha}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi\big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\alpha}\big)dx.

On the other hand, we know that

(3.4)

\displaystyle|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,T_{\Omega}^{*}f(x)>\alpha\}|\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi\big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\alpha}\big)dx,

see
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
. These two endpoint estimates, via the argument used in the proof of Theorem 4.4 in
[
12
]
(see also the proof of Theorem 3.1 in
[
16
]
), leads to the conclusion of Theorem
1.5
.
∎

At the end of this section, we give some applications of Theorem
1.5
.
At first, by mimicking the proof of Theorem 1.1 in
[
19
]
, we can verify that Theorem
1.5
implies the following weighted inequality of Coifman-Fefferman type.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Omega
be homogeneous of degree zero, have mean value zero on
S^{d-1}
and
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
.
Then for
p\in[1,\,\infty)
and
w\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\|T^{*}_{\Omega}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{2}\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,w)}

\displaystyle+\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}[w]_{A_{\infty}}\log({\rm e}+[w]_{A_{\infty}})\|M_{\Phi}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\,w)},

provided that
f
is a bounded function with compact support, where and in the following,
M_{\Phi}
is the maximal operator defined by

M_{\Phi}f(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\langle|f|\rangle_{\Phi,\,Q}.

From (
1.5
), we know that for
r\in(1,\,\infty)
,

M_{\Phi}f(x)\lesssim M_{r}f(x)+\log(r^{\prime})Mf(x).

This shows that Theorem
3.2
improves Theorem
1.4
substantially.

To give another application of Theorem
1.5
, we formulate the following weighted weak type endpoint
estimates.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\,\gamma_{2}\geq 0
be two constants,
U
be a sublinear operator,
\Psi
be a Young function such that
\Psi(t)\geq t
, and for all
p\in(1,\,\infty)
,

\Psi(t)\lesssim p^{\prime\gamma_{1}}t^{p-1}\,\,\hbox{when}\,\,t\geq 1.

Suppose that for bounded function
f
with compact support, and for each
r\in(1,\,\infty)
, there exists a sparse family
\mathcal{S}
, such that for all bounded function
g
with compact support,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)Uf(x)dx\Big|\lesssim r^{\prime\gamma_{2}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},\,L^{\Psi},\,L^{r}}(f,\,g).

Then for any bounded function
f
with compact support,
\alpha>0
and
w\in A_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|Uf(x)|>\alpha\})\lesssim[w]^{\gamma_{2}}_{A_{\infty}}\log^{1+\gamma_{1}}({\rm e}+[w]_{A_{\infty}})[w]_{A_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\alpha}\big)w(x)dx.

Theorem
3.3
is a generalization of Corollary 3.3 in
[
12
]
, and the proof is fairly similar to the proof of Corollary 3.3 in
[
12
]
, we omit the details for brevity.

Obviously,Theorem
1.5
, along with Theorem
3.3
, leads to Theorem
1.2
directly.

Finally, we consider the maximal operator associated with the commutator of the operator
T_{\Omega}
in (
1.1
). Let
b\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Define the commutator of
T_{\Omega}
and
b
by

[b,\,T_{\Omega}]f(x)=bT_{\Omega}f(x)-T_{\Omega}(bf)(x).

The quantitative weighted estimates for
[b,\,T_{\Omega}]
were considered by many authors, see
[
18
,
20
,
23
,
14
]
and related references therein.
[b,\,T_{\Omega}]^{*}
, the maximal operator associated with
[b,\,T_{\Omega}]
, is defined by

[b,\,T_{\Omega}]^{*}f(x)=\sup_{\epsilon>0}\Big|\int_{|x-y|>\epsilon}\frac{\Omega(x-y)}{|x-y|^{d}}(b(x)-b(y))f(y)dy\Big|.

Using estimate (
3.3
)-(
3.4
), and repeating the proof of Theorem 2.7 in
[
14
]
, we deduce that

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Omega
be homogeneous of degree zero, have mean value zero on
S^{d-1}
, and
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
. Then for each
r\in(1,\,\infty)
and bounded function
f
, there exists a sparse family
\mathcal{S}
, such that for all bounded function
g
with compact support,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)[b,\,T_{\Omega}]^{*}f(x)dx\Big|

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}\big(r^{\prime 2}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},\,L^{1},L^{r}}(f,\,g)+r^{\prime}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},L^{\Phi},L^{r}}(f,g)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})}\big(r^{\prime}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},\,L^{\Psi_{1}},L^{r}}(f,\,g)+\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S},L^{\Psi_{2}},L^{r}}(f,g)\big),

where and in the following,
\Psi_{1}(t)=t\log({\rm e}+t)
,
\Psi_{2}(t)=t\log({\rm e}+t)\log\log({\rm e}^{2}+t)
.

Theorem
3.4
, along with Theorem
3.3
, leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\Omega
be homogeneous of degree zero, have mean value zero on
S^{d-1}
, and
\Omega\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1})
. Let
b\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then for all
\alpha>0
,

\displaystyle w\big(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,[b,\,T_{\Omega}]^{*}f(x)>\alpha\}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim[w]_{A_{1}}[w]^{2}_{A_{\infty}}\log({\rm e}+[w]_{A_{\infty}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi_{2}\big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\alpha}\big)w(x)dx.

As far as we knon, Corollary
3.5
is new even for the case
w\equiv 1
.

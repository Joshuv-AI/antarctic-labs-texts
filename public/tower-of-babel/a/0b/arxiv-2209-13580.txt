Different types of weak amenability for Banach algebras
M. J. Mehdipour and A. Rejali

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we introduce and investigate the concepts of cyclically weakly amenable and point amenable. Then, we compare these concepts with the concepts of weakly amenable and cyclically amenable and find the relation between them. For example, we prove that a Banach algebra is weakly amenable if and only if it is both cyclically amenable and cyclically weakly amaneble. In the case where
A
is commutative, the weak amenability and cyclically weak amenability of
A
are equivalent. We also show that if
A
is a Banach algebra with
\Delta(A)\neq\emptyset
, then
A
is cyclically weakly amenable if and only if
A
is point amenable and essential. For a unital, commutative Banach algebra
A
, the notions of weakly amenable, cyclically weakly amenable and point amenable coincide. In this case, these are equivalent to the fact that every maximal ideal of
A
is essential.

Let

D(ab)=D(a)\cdot b+a\cdot D(b)

. Also,

such that for every

D(a)=\hbox{ad}_{F}(a):=F\cdot a-a\cdot F,

where for every
b\in A

\langle F\cdot a,b\rangle=\langle F,ab\rangle\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\langle a\cdot F,b\rangle=\langle F,ba\rangle.

If for every
a,b\in A

\langle D(a),b\rangle+\langle D(b),a\rangle=0,

then
D
is called
cyclic
.
The space of all continuous (inner, cyclic) derivations from

is denoted by (
{\cal I}_{nn}(A,A^{*}),{\cal Z}_{C}(A,A^{*})
)
{\cal Z}(A,A^{*})
, respectively. A Banach algebra

if

Gronbaek

]
introduced the concept of cyclically amenable and studied some study hereditary properties of it. In fact, he called
A

These definitions motivate us to introduce the notion of cyclically weakly amenable. We call

{\cal Z}(A,A^{*})={\cal Z}_{c}(A,A^{*}).

Note that the mapping
\delta:A\times A\rightarrow{C}
defined by

\delta(a,b)=\langle D(a),b\rangle+\langle D(b),a\rangle

Hence we may define the linear operator

is cyclically weakly amenable if

. Also,
A

\hbox{ker}T={\cal I}_{nn}(A,A^{*}).

For

\langle{D}_{F_{1},F_{2}}(a),b\rangle=F_{1}(a)F_{2}(b).

This paper is organized as follow. In Section 2, we study cyclically weakly amenable and point amenable Banach algebras. We show that every cyclically weakly amenable Banach algebra is essential. We also characterize continuous point derivations and prove that Banach algebra
A
is point amenable if and only if

Cyclically weakly amenable and point amenable

A continuous derivation
D:A\rightarrow A^{*}

vanishes on the diameter of

A
if

\langle D(a),a\rangle=0
for all
a\in A
.

Proposition 2.1

Let
A
be a Banach algebra and
D:A\rightarrow A^{*}
be a continuous derivation. Then the following assertion are equivalent.

(a)

D
is cyclic.

(b)

D
vanishes on the diameter of
A
.

If
A
is unital, then the statements
(a)
and
(b)
are equivalent to

(c)

\langle D(a),1\rangle=0
for all
a\in A
.

Proof.
It is clear that (a) implies (b). Let (b) hold. Hence for every
a,b\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\langle D(a),b\rangle+\langle D(b),a\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D(a),a\rangle+\langle D(a),b\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle D(b),a\rangle+\langle D(b),b\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D(a+b),a+b\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

This shows that
D
is cyclic. Thus (b) implies (a).

Now, let
A
be unital. If
D
is cyclic, then for every
a\in A

\langle D(a),1\rangle=\langle D(a),1\rangle+\langle D(1),a\rangle=0.

So (a) implies (c). Finally, let (c) hold. Then

\displaystyle 2\langle D(a),a\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle 2D(a)a,1\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D(a^{2}),1\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

for all
a\in A
. Therefore,
D
is cyclic. That is, (c) implies (b).

Proposition 2.3

Let

(i)
If

(ii)

Proof.

\displaystyle\langle{D}_{F,F}(b),ca\rangle+\langle{D}_{F,F}(a),bc\rangle

\displaystyle=

Relation between cyclically weakly amenable and point amenable

A quasi-additive functional

of

such that

p(a\otimes b)=F(ab-ba)

for all
a,b\in A

is called

if
p(a\otimes a)=0
for all
a\in A

In the following, we show that for commutative Banach algebras the weak amenability and weak cyclic amenability coincide.

Theorem 4.2

Let
A
be a commutative Banach algebra. Then

is weakly amenable if and only if

is cyclically weakly amenable.

Proof.

A^{*}

\langle F\cdot a,b\rangle=\langle F,ab\rangle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt(F\in A^{*},a,b\in A).

\displaystyle\langle D(ab)\cdot c,x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D(ab),cx\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D(a)\cdot b+a\cdot D(b),cx\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D(a),b(cx)\rangle+\langle D(b),(cx)a\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D(a),b(cx)\rangle+\langle D(b),(ca)x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D(a)\cdot(bc),x\rangle+\langle D(b)\cdot(ca),x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D(a)\cdot(bc)+D(b)\cdot(ca),x\rangle.

\displaystyle D(ab)\cdot c=D(a)\cdot(bc)+D(b)\cdot(ca).

\displaystyle U(a)=T(D(a))=\Phi\mathchar 13389\relax D(a).

\displaystyle\langle U(a)\cdot b+a\cdot U(b),c\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle U(a),bc\rangle+\langle U(b),ca\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Phi\mathchar 13389\relax D(a),bc\rangle+\langle\Phi\mathchar 13389\relax D(b),ca\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Phi,D(a)\cdot(bc)+D(b)\cdot(ca)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Phi,D(ab)\cdot c\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Phi\mathchar 13389\relax D(ab),c\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle U(ab),c\rangle.

\displaystyle\langle U(a_{0}),b_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle U(b_{0}),a_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Phi\mathchar 13389\relax D(a_{0}),b_{0}\rangle+\langle\Phi\mathchar 13389\relax D(b_{0}),a_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Phi,D(a_{0})\cdot b_{0}\rangle+\langle\Phi,D(b_{0})\cdot a_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Phi,D(a_{0})\cdot b_{0}+D(b_{0})\cdot a_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Phi,D(a_{0}b_{0})\rangle

(c)

(d)
For every continuous point derivation
d

\varphi\in\Delta(B)
of

.

The Banach algebra

Proof.
Since
B
is a unital, commutative Banach algebra, by Theorem

, the statements (a), (b) and (c) are equivalent. Clearly, (c) implies (d). Now, let
\varphi\in\Delta(B)

\varphi
of

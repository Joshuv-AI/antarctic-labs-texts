Optimal Gaussian Strategies for Vector-valued Witsenhausen Counterexample with Non-causal State Estimator
Mengyuan Zhao, Tobias J. Oechtering, Maël Le Treust

Abstract
In this study, we investigate a vector-valued Witsenhausen model where the second dm ( dm ) acquires a vector of observations before selecting a vector of estimations. Here, the first dm acts causally whereas the second dm estimates non-causally. When the vector length grows, we characterize, via a single-letter expression, the optimal trade-off between the power cost at the first dm and the estimation cost at the second dm . In this paper, we show that the best linear scheme is achieved by using the time-sharing method between two affine strategies, which coincides with the convex envelope of the solution of Witsenhausen in 1968. Here also, Witsenhausen’s two-point strategy and the scheme of Grover and Sahai in 2010 where both devices operate non-causally, outperform our best linear scheme. Therefore, gains obtained with block-coding schemes are only attainable if all dm s operate non-causally.

Remark II.4

The following Markov chains follow from the joint probability distribution (

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &X_{0}\text{ is independent of }W_{1},\\

Remark IV.1
.

Now, we discuss the optimal Gaussian cost for the case where both

•

The optimal Gaussian strategy discussed in

Remark IV.2
.

Our discussions above lead us to a remarkable conclusion: Even though the optimal achievable cost regions are different, the optimal Gaussian costs for the following five distinct configurations are the same:

1.

Causal encoding and causal decoding.

2.

Causal encoding and non-causal decoding.

3.

Causal encoding and non-causal decoding with channel feedback.

4.

Non-causal encoding and causal decoding.

5.

Non-causal encoding and causal decoding with source feedforward.

This equality is surprising since these settings all have different single-letter optimal regions, but these regions coincide if we restrict to Gaussian schemes. Therefore, block-coding gains can be obtained only if both
dm

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(X_{1}-\mathbb{E}\big[X_{1}\big|W_{1},W_{2},Y_{1}\big]\big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\Var\left(X_{1}|W_{1},W_{2},Y_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(b)}}}{{=}}\sigma_{X_{1}}^{2}-\Sigma_{X_{1}W}^{\top}\Sigma_{WW}^{-1}\Sigma_{X_{1}W}

\displaystyle=\frac{N\cdot f_{1}(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5})}{(1-\rho_{4}^{2})\cdot N+f_{1}(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5})},

where step (b) is obtained using the Schur complement. Here,
W=(W_{1},W_{2},Y_{1})^{\top}
,
\Sigma_{X_{1}W}=(\sigma_{X_{1},W_{1}},\sigma_{X_{1},W_{2}},\sigma_{X_{1},Y_{1}})^{\top}
, and
\Sigma_{WW}
is the covariance matrix of
(W_{1},W_{2},Y_{1})
.
∎

Thus, given the expression of (
20
), we can obtain a new expression of the original information constraint

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{T_{1}}{T_{1}-T_{2}}\right)\geq 0\Leftrightarrow T_{1}\geq T_{2}\geq 0\text{ or }T_{1}\leq T_{2}\leq 0.

If
1-\rho_{4}^{2}\ =0
, from (
21
), we can get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(X_{1}-\mathbb{E}\big[X_{1}\big|W_{1},W_{2},Y_{1}\big]\big)^{2}\Big]=N.

Next, we focus on the case if
1-\rho_{4}^{2}\ \neq 0
. In this case, (
21
) is of the form

\displaystyle\frac{N\cdot f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5})}{N+f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5})},

(22)

where
f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5})=f_{1}(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5})/(1-\rho_{4}^{2})=\frac{-P\rho_{2}^{2}\rho_{3}^{2}-(Q+2\rho_{3}\sqrt{PQ})(-1+\rho_{2}^{2}+\rho_{4}^{2})+P(1-\rho_{4}^{2})(1-\rho_{5}^{2})}{(1-\rho_{4}^{2})}
.

Note that, the function
x\mapsto\frac{N\cdot x}{N+x}
is nonnegative and strictly increasing over the region
(-\infty,-N]\cup[0,\infty)
. Therefore, our goal of minimizing (
22
) is now transformed to either minimizing the nonnegative object

\displaystyle f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5})\geq 0

or minimizing the negative object

\displaystyle f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5})\leq-N

subject to the following constraints:

\displaystyle\text{1. }\det(K)\geq 0\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad QV_{1}V_{2}P(-1+\rho_{2}^{2}+\rho_{4}^{2})(-1+\rho_{3}^{2}+\rho_{5}^{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(A)}}}{{\geq}}0,

\displaystyle\text{2. }\det(K_{2})\geq 0\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad QV_{1}V_{2}(-1+\rho_{2}^{2}+\rho_{4}^{2})(P(-1+\rho_{3}^{2}+\rho_{5}^{2})-N)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(B)}}}{{\geq}}0,

\displaystyle\text{3. }T_{1}\geq T_{2}\geq 0\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad(Q+P+N+2\rho_{3}\sqrt{QP})(-1+\rho_{2}^{2}+\rho_{4}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(C1)}}}{{\geq}}N\rho_{2}^{2}+P\rho_{2}^{2}(1-\rho_{3}^{2})-P\rho_{5}^{2}(1-\rho_{4}^{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(D1)}}}{{\geq}}0,

\displaystyle\quad\text{or, }T_{1}\leq T_{2}\leq 0\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad(Q+P+N+2\rho_{3}\sqrt{QP})(-1+\rho_{2}^{2}+\rho_{4}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(C2)}}}{{\leq}}N\rho_{2}^{2}+P\rho_{2}^{2}(1-\rho_{3}^{2})-P\rho_{5}^{2}(1-\rho_{4}^{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(D2)}}}{{\leq}}0.

To simplify the above constraints, we consider the following two distinct cases:

Case 1,
if
-1+\rho_{2}^{2}+\rho_{4}^{2}\geq 0
, constraints (A) and (B) together yield
-1+\rho_{3}^{2}+\rho_{5}^{2}\geq\frac{N}{P}
. Moreover, constraint (C1) gives us
f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5})\leq-N
. In this case, our optimization problem boils down to minimizing

\displaystyle f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5})\leq-N,

(23)

subject to

\displaystyle\text{1. }-1+\rho_{2}^{2}+\rho_{4}^{2}\geq 0,

(24)

\displaystyle\text{2. }-1+\rho_{3}^{2}+\rho_{5}^{2}\geq\frac{N}{P},

(25)

\displaystyle\text{3. }N\rho_{2}^{2}+P\rho_{2}^{2}(1-\rho_{3}^{2})-P\rho_{5}^{2}(1-\rho_{4}^{2})\geq 0.

(26)

Notice that
f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5})
is decreasing function of
\rho_{5}^{2}
. From (
26
), we get that
\rho_{5}^{2}\leq\frac{N\rho_{2}^{2}+P\rho_{2}^{2}(1-\rho_{3}^{2})}{P(1-\rho_{4}^{2})}
. Therefore, the optimizer is given by
(\rho_{5}^{*})^{2}=\frac{N\rho_{2}^{2}+P\rho_{2}^{2}(1-\rho_{3}^{2})}{P(1-\rho_{4}^{2})}
. By plugging
(\rho_{5}^{*})^{2}
into (
23
), we obtain that

\displaystyle f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5}^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{-(-1+\rho_{2}^{2}+\rho_{4}^{2})(Q+P+2\rho_{3}\sqrt{PQ})-N\rho_{2}^{2}}{1-\rho_{4}^{2}}.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5}^{*})}{\partial\rho_{4}^{2}}\leq 0,

we know that
f
decreases to
-\infty
as
\rho_{4}^{2}
approaches
1
. Moreover, since
\mathbb{E}\big[\big(X_{1}-\mathbb{E}\big[X_{1}\big|W_{1},W_{2},Y_{1}\big]\big)^{2}\big]
is continuous and converges to
N
when
\rho_{4}^{2}\rightarrow 1
, in this case, the minimal value of
N
is obtained at the boundary
\rho_{4}^{2}=1
.

Case 2,
If
-1+\rho_{2}^{2}+\rho_{4}^{2}\leq 0
, conditions (A) and (B) together give us
-1+\rho_{3}^{2}+\rho_{5}^{2}\leq 0
, and (C2) gives us
f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5})\geq-N
(but only
f\geq 0
contributes to a nonnegative estimation cost). Therefore, in this case, our optimization problem boils down to minimizing

\displaystyle f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5})\geq 0,

(27)

subject to

\displaystyle\text{1. }-1+\rho_{2}^{2}+\rho_{4}^{2}\leq 0,

(28)

\displaystyle\text{2. }-1+\rho_{3}^{2}+\rho_{5}^{2}\leq 0,

(29)

\displaystyle\text{3. }N\rho_{2}^{2}+P\rho_{2}^{2}(1-\rho_{3}^{2})-P\rho_{5}^{2}(1-\rho_{4}^{2})\leq 0.

(30)

Since
f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5})
is reduced especially when
\rho_{5}^{2}
is increased, therefore, from (
29
), we get that
(\rho_{5}^{*})^{2}=1-\rho_{3}^{2}
. By replacing
(\rho_{5}^{*})^{2}
into (
27
), we get that

\displaystyle f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5}^{*})=\frac{(1-\rho_{2}^{2}-\rho_{4}^{2})(\sqrt{P}\rho_{3}+\sqrt{Q})^{2}}{(1-\rho_{4}^{2})}.

(31)

Therefore, when
P\geq Q
, taking
\rho_{3}^{*}=-\sqrt{\frac{Q}{P}}
and any
\rho_{2}
,
\rho_{4}
satisfy the constraints results in the optimal value of
f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3}^{*},\rho_{4},\rho_{5}^{*})=0
. In this case,
\mathbb{E}\big[\big(X_{1}-\mathbb{E}\big[X_{1}\big|W_{1},W_{2},Y_{1}\big]\big)^{2}\big]=0
.

When
P<Q
,
f(\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5}^{*})
is a decreasing function of
\rho_{2}^{2}
. The constraint (
30
) gives us the optimal value of
\rho_{2}^{2}
:

(\rho_{2}^{*})^{2}=\frac{P(1-\rho_{3}^{2})(1-\rho_{4}^{2})}{N+P(1-\rho_{3}^{2})}.

(32)

Plugging the
(\rho_{2}^{*})^{2}
in (
32
) into
(
31
), we have

\displaystyle f(\rho_{2}^{*},\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5}^{*})=\frac{N(\sqrt{P}\rho_{3}+\sqrt{Q})^{2}}{N+P(1-\rho_{3}^{2})}.

Then, taking
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\rho_{3}}=0
gives us the optimum

\displaystyle\rho_{3}^{*}=-\frac{P+N}{\sqrt{QP}},

which is valid only if the following condition holds

\displaystyle(\rho_{3}^{*})^{2}=\frac{(P+N)^{2}}{QP}\leq 1\Longrightarrow\quad\left\{\begin{aligned} &Q>4N,\\
&P\in\left[P_{1},P_{2}\right],\end{aligned}\right.

(33)

where
P_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(Q-2N-\sqrt{Q^{2}-4QN}\right)
,
P_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(Q-2N+\sqrt{Q^{2}-4QN}\right)
.
In this case,
(\rho_{5}^{*})^{2}=\frac{QP-(P+N)^{2}}{QP}
, and
f(\rho_{2}^{*},\rho_{3}^{*},\rho_{4}^{*},\rho_{5}^{*})=\frac{N(Q-N-P)}{N+P}
, which results in the estimation cost of

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(X_{1}-\mathbb{E}\big[X_{1}\big|W_{1},W_{2},Y_{1}\big]\big)^{2}\Big]=\frac{N(Q-N-P)}{Q}.

In the case when the condition (
33
) is unmet, we always have
-\frac{P+N}{\sqrt{QP}}<-1
. Since
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\rho_{3}}>0
, function
f
increases when
\rho_{3}\in[-1,1]
increases. Therefore, the minimal value of
f
achieves at the left boundary
\rho_{3}^{*}=-1
, which gives us
\rho_{2}^{*}=\rho_{5}^{*}=0
and
f(\rho_{2}^{*},\rho_{3}^{*},\rho_{4}^{*},\rho_{5}^{*})=(\sqrt{Q}-\sqrt{P})^{2}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[(X_{1}-\mathbb{E}\big[X_{1}\big|W_{1},W_{2},Y_{1}\big]\big)^{2}\Big]=\frac{N\cdot(\sqrt{Q}-\sqrt{P})^{2}}{N+(\sqrt{Q}-\sqrt{P})^{2}}.

Obviously, the mimimal estimation cost value
N
from case 1 is always larger than the cost derived in case 2. Therefore, summarizing our above analysis, the optimal Gaussian cost
S_{\mathsf{G}}(P)
is given by (
12
).
∎

Proof of Lemma
III.5
.

From the Markov chain, we have that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=I(X;Z|Y)

\displaystyle=H(X,Y)+H(Y,Z)-H(Y)-H(X,Y,Z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{\det(\Sigma_{X,Y})\cdot\det(\Sigma_{Y,Z})}{\sigma_{Y}^{2}\cdot\det(\Sigma_{X,Y,Z})}\right),

where all the information of the last step can be obtained from the covariance matrix (
15
). Therefore,

\displaystyle 0=\det(\Sigma_{X,Y})\cdot\det(\Sigma_{Y,Z})-\sigma_{Y}^{2}\cdot\det(\Sigma_{X,Y,Z})

\displaystyle=PQ^{2}V(\rho_{1}\rho_{3}-\rho_{2})^{2},

which implies the result (
16
).
∎

Proof of Corollary
III.6
.

According to
[
24
]
, the information constraint for Witsenhausen counterexample with causal encoder and non-causal decoder with channel feedback
Y_{1}
is

I(W_{1};Y_{1})-I(U_{2};X_{0}|W_{1},Y_{1})\geq 0.

(34)

Moreover, in the Gaussian settings, the MMSE estimator of
X_{1}
can be represented as
U_{2}=\mathbb{E}\big[X_{1}\big|W_{1},W_{2},Y_{1}\big]=a\cdot W_{1}+b\cdot W_{2}+c\cdot Y_{1}
, with some constants
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence, the information constraint (
34
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle I(W_{1};Y_{1})-I(U_{2};X_{0}|W_{1},Y_{1})

\displaystyle=I(W_{1};Y_{1})-I(a\cdot W_{1}+b\cdot W_{2}+c\cdot Y_{1};X_{0}|W_{1},Y_{1})

\displaystyle=I(W_{1};Y_{1})-I(W_{2};X_{0}|W_{1},Y_{1}),

which recovers the information constraint for the framework without channel feedback (
19
). Thus, the optimization domain for this optimization problem is exactly the same as the one without channel feedback, i.e., (
8
).
∎

Introduction and preliminaries

In
1922
, Polish mathematician Stefan Banach
[
2
]
gave a fixed point theorem. It is also known as the Banach Contraction mapping theorem or principle (BCP). It is an important tool in the metric fixed point theory. It confirms the existence and uniqueness of fixed point of certain self maps of metric spaces and provides a constructive method to find fixed points. There are so many extension, generalizations of BCP in different settings and there applications. Among them, In 1976, Caristi
[
4
]
proved a fixed point theorem and applied to derive a generalization of the Contraction Mapping Principle in a complete metric space. Recently, In 2019, E. Karapinar et al.,
[
9
]
give a new fixed point theorem in
b
- metric space which is inspired from both Caristi and Banach.
b
- metric space introduced by Czerwik
[
5
]
to generalize the concept of metric space by introducing a real number
s\geq 1
in the triangle inequality of metric space.

Inspired by E. Karapinar et al.,
[
9
]
we introduce the notion of a Caristi-Banach type
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{R}}^{b}

Definition 1.1
.

[
5
]
Let
M
be a non-empty set and
s\geq 1
be a given real number. A function

is said to be a
b
-metric space if, for all
\sigma,\rho,w\in M

(i)
d(\sigma,\rho)=0

iff

\sigma=\rho
;

(ii)
d(\sigma,\rho)=d(\rho,\sigma)
;

(iii)
d(\sigma,w)\leq s[d(\sigma,\rho)+d(\rho,w)]
.

The pair
(M,d)
is called a
b
-metric space.

It should be noted that, every metric space is a
b
-metric space with
s=1
and hence the class of
b
-metric spaces is larger than the class of metric spaces. But a metric space need not be
b
-metric space (see example 1.4
[
14
]

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a self mapping on a
b
-metric space
(\mathcal{M},d)
equipped with a binary relation
\mathcal{R}
. If there exist
\zeta\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{R}}^{b}
and
\mathcal{\phi}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle d(\sigma,\mathcal{F}\sigma)>0\Rightarrow\zeta(sd(\mathcal{F}\sigma,\mathcal{F}\rho),(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma)-\mathcal{\phi}(\mathcal{F}\sigma)d(\sigma,\rho))\geq 0,

(2.1)

\forall

\sigma,\rho\in\mathcal{M}
,
(\sigma,\rho)\in\mathcal{R}
, then
\mathcal{F}
is called Caristi - Banach type
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{R}}^{b}
-contraction.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},d)
be a complete
b
- metric space equipped with a binary relation
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{F}
be a self mapping on
\mathcal{M}
. Let the following hypotheses holds

(i)
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F};\mathcal{R})
is non-empty;

(ii)
\mathcal{R}
is
\mathcal{F}
-closed and transitive;

(iii) either
\mathcal{F}
is
\mathcal{R}
- continuous or
\mathcal{R}
is
b
-
d
- self-closed;

(iv)
\mathcal{F}
is Caristi - Banach type
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{R}}^{b}
-contraction with respect to
\zeta\in\mathcal{Z}
.

Then
\mathcal{F}
has a fixed point.

Proof.

Let
\sigma_{0}
be an arbitrary point in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F};\mathcal{R})
. Put
\sigma_{n}=\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n-1}=\mathcal{F}^{n}\sigma_{0}

\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
C_{n+1}=d(\sigma_{n},\sigma_{n+1})
, if for some
n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\sigma_{n^{\prime}}=\sigma_{n^{\prime}+1}
, then
\sigma_{n^{\prime}}
is a fixed point of
\mathcal{F}
and so the proof is complete. Thus, we let
\sigma_{n}\neq\sigma_{n+1}

\forall

n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
i.e.,
C_{n+1}>0
. Since
(\sigma_{0},\mathcal{F}\sigma_{0})\in\mathcal{R}
, using the
\mathcal{F}
-closedness of
\mathcal{R}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}\sigma_{0},\mathcal{F}^{2}\sigma_{0}),(\mathcal{F}^{2}\sigma_{0},\mathcal{F}^{3}\sigma_{0}),...,(\mathcal{F}^{n}\sigma_{0},\mathcal{F}^{n+1}\sigma_{0}),...\in\mathcal{R}.

Thus

\displaystyle(\sigma_{n},\sigma_{n+1})\in\mathcal{R},

(2.2)

and the sequence
\{\sigma_{n}\}
is
\mathcal{R}
- preserving. Since
\mathcal{F}
is Caristi - Banach type
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{R}}^{b}
-contraction, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta\big(sd(\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n-1},\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n}),(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n-1})-\mathcal{\phi}(\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n-1}))(\sigma_{n-1},\sigma_{n})\big)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n-1})-\mathcal{\phi}(\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n-1}))(\sigma_{n-1},\sigma_{n})-sd(\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n-1},\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n}),

which yield

\displaystyle C_{n+1}=d(\sigma_{n},\sigma_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n-1},\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n})\leq sd(\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n-1},\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n-1})-\mathcal{\phi}(\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n-1}))(\sigma_{n-1},\sigma_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n-1})-\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n}))C_{n}.

So we have

0<\frac{C_{n+1}}{C_{n}}\leq(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n-1})-\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n}))
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Thus the sequence
\{\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n})\}
is necessarily non-negative and decreasing. Hence, it converges to some
\mathfrak{a}\geq 0
. On the other hand, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{C_{k+1}}{C_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{k-1})-\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{k}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{0})-\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{1}))+(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{1})-\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{2}))+...+(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n-1})-\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{0})-\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n}))\rightarrow\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{0})-\mathfrak{a}<\infty,~as~n\rightarrow\infty.

It means that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_{n+1}}{C_{n}}<\infty.

Accordingly, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{C_{n+1}}{C_{n}}=0.

(2.3)

On account of (
2.3
), for
\varrho\in(0,1)
, there exist
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{C_{n+1}}{C_{n}}\leq\varrho,~\forall~n\geq n_{0}.

(2.4)

It yield that

\displaystyle d(\sigma_{n},\sigma_{n+1})\leq\varrho d(\sigma_{n-1},\sigma_{n}),~\forall~n\geq n_{0}.

(2.5)

Now using Lemma 3.1
[
17
]
we obtain that the sequence
\{\sigma_{n}\}
is Cauchy. Thus from the completeness of
\mathcal{M}
, there exist
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\sigma_{n}\rightarrow\sigma
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. By (iii), if
\mathcal{F}
is
\mathcal{R}
- continuous then
\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}\sigma
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Alternately, let us assume that
\mathcal{R}
is
b
-
d
-self-closed. As
\{\sigma_{n}\}
is an
\mathcal{R}
preserving sequence and
\sigma_{n}\xrightarrow{d}\sigma
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. So there exist a subsequence
\{\sigma_{n_{k}}\}
of
\{\sigma_{n}\}
with
[\sigma_{n_{k}},\sigma]\in\mathcal{R}
,
\forall

k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Notice that
[\sigma_{n_{k}},\sigma]\in\mathcal{R}
,
\forall

k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
implies that either
(\sigma_{n_{k}},\sigma)\in\mathcal{R}
,
\forall

k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
or
(\sigma,\sigma_{n_{k}})\in\mathcal{R}
,
\forall

k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Applying condition (iv) to
(\sigma_{n_{k}},\sigma)\in\mathcal{R}
,
\forall

k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta\big(sd(\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n_{k}},\mathcal{F}\sigma),(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n_{k}})-\mathcal{\phi}(\mathcal{F}(\sigma_{n_{k}}))d(\sigma_{n_{k}},\sigma)\big)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n_{k}})-\mathcal{\phi}(\mathcal{F}(\sigma_{n_{k}}))d(\sigma_{n_{k}},\sigma)-sd(\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n_{k}},\mathcal{F}\sigma)

\Longrightarrow

sd(\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n_{k}},\mathcal{F}\sigma)<(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n_{k}})-\mathcal{\phi}(\mathcal{F}(\sigma_{n_{k}}))d(\sigma_{n_{k}},\sigma)
.

Using the triangle inequality together with the inequality above, we derive that

\displaystyle d(\sigma,\mathcal{F}\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s[d(\sigma,\sigma_{n_{k}+1})+d(\sigma_{n_{k}+1},\mathcal{F}\sigma)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s[d(\sigma,\sigma_{n_{k}+1})+d(\mathcal{F}\sigma_{n_{k}},\mathcal{F}\sigma)]

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle sd(\sigma,\sigma_{n_{k}+1})+(\mathcal{\phi}(\sigma_{n_{k}})-\mathcal{\phi}(\mathcal{F}(\sigma_{n_{k}}))d(\sigma_{n_{k}},\sigma),

putting
n\rightarrow\infty
and using
\sigma_{n_{k}}\xrightarrow{d}\sigma
, above inequality
\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Consequently, we obtain that
d(\sigma,\mathcal{F}\sigma)=0
, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}\sigma=\sigma
.

Similarly, if
(\sigma,\sigma_{n_{k}})\in\mathcal{R}
,
\forall

k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we obtain
d(\mathcal{F}\sigma,\sigma)=0
. So that
\mathcal{F}\sigma=\sigma
, i.e.,
\sigma
is a fixed point of
\mathcal{F}
. ∎

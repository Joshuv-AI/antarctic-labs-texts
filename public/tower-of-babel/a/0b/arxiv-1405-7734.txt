Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain of
{\rm I\mskip-3.5muR}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
,
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
the critical exponent for Sobolev embedding. Define
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
two disjoint domains such that
\bar{\Omega}=\bar{\Omega}_{1}\cup\bar{\Omega_{2}}
. Denote
\Gamma=\partial\Omega_{1}\cap\partial\Omega_{2}
. Define the set
V(\Omega)=\displaystyle\left\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\quad\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2^{*}}dx=1\right\}
and define the barycenter function, see
[
7
]
,
\beta:\,V(\Omega)\rightarrow{\rm I\mskip-3.5muR}^{N}
,
u\rightarrow\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}x\,|u|^{2^{*}}dx
.

We consider the minimizing problem

S(p)=\displaystyle\inf_{u\in V(\Omega),\,\,\beta(u)\in\Gamma}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p(x)|\nabla u|^{2}dx,

(1)

where
p
is a discontinuous function as the following

p(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\alpha_{1}&\textrm{if $x\in\Omega_{1}$}\\[6.0pt]
\alpha_{2}&\textrm{if $x\in{\bar{\Omega}}_{2}$},\end{array}\right.

(2)

where
\alpha_{i}
,
i=1,2
are some positive constants such that
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}
.
It’s important to remark that without the additional condition
\beta(u)\in\Gamma
we have
S(p)=\alpha_{1}S
, as one can verify concentrating an extremal function for the best Sobolev constant
S
near a point in the interior of the region
\Omega_{1}
. In this case, the infimum
S(p)
is not achieved

This problem is closely related to the best constant in Sobolev inequality in
{\rm I\mskip-3.5muR}^{N}
. It posses many interesting properties, see Talenti
[
15
]
, and arising in many areas of mathematics and in a geometric context namely for example in the Yamabe problem and the prescribed scalar
curvature problem see Aubin
[
1
]
. It’s invariance under dilations produces a lack of compactness.

The phenomenon of lack of compactness and the failure of the Palais Smale condition of this type of problem has been the subject of several studies and it was analyzed in minute detail by Struwe
[
14
]
.

In the case where
p
is a constant, it is well known that (
1
) is not achieved for a general domain
\Omega
. Nevertheless, Brezis and Nirenberg showed in
[
6
]
that (
1
) has minimizer under a linear perturbation, also, in the same sprit, Demyanov and Nazarov establish, in
[
9
]
, sufficient conditions for the existence of an extremal function in four embedding theorems for more general Sobolev spaces. Bahri and Coron in
[
4
]

proved that the Euler equation associated to this problem is solvable when some homology group of the domain with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is nontrivial, see also the work of Coron
[
7
]
.
In the case where
p
is a smooth positive function, Hadiji and Yazidi proved that the study of problem (
1
) depends on the behavior of the weight
p
near its minima, see
[
10
]
, see also Hadiji, Molle, Passaseo and Yazidi
[
11
]
) and Furtado and Souza
[
12
]
.

One may ask whether the lack of compactness of the variational functional associated to (
1
) can be made up by the discontinuity of the weight.

In this work, we investigate the effect of non smoothness of weight
p
on the existence of solution without linear perturbation or additional conditions on the domain
\Omega
.

The following proposition presents a strict lower bound for the minimizing problem

Proposition 2.1

The following inequality holds

\alpha_{1}\,S<S(p).

Proof.

We have

Since
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p(x)|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}dx\geq\alpha_{1}\,S

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}dx=S
. Therefore, there exists

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}p(x)|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}dx=p(z_{0})\,S>\alpha_{1}\,S

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{S_{\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2}}}\int_{\Omega}p(x)|\nabla v_{j}(x)|^{2}dx.

(18)

where

{\rm I\mskip-3.5muR}_{+,\,x_{0}}^{N}=\displaystyle\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in{\rm I\mskip-3.5muR}^{N},\,\,\,\setminus\,\,x^{\prime}\in{\rm I\mskip-3.5muR}^{N-1},\,\,x_{N}>x_{0N}\}
and

\displaystyle{\rm I\mskip-3.5muR}_{-,\,x_{0}}^{N}=\displaystyle\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in{\rm I\mskip-3.5muR}^{N},\,\,\,\setminus\,\,x^{\prime}\in{\rm I\mskip-3.5muR}^{N-1},\,\,x_{N}<x_{0N}\}
with

is such that
\displaystyle x_{0}=\displaystyle(x^{\prime},x_{0N})
.

We claim that

.

Indeed, suppose that
u\equiv 0
. From (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p(x)|\nabla v_{j}|^{2}dx=S(p)+o(1),

then

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}p(x)|\nabla v_{j}|^{2}dx=S(p).

From (
16
) we see that

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\|v_{j}\|_{L^{2^{*}}}=1.

Or (
18
) gives that

\displaystyle\|v_{j}\|_{L^{2^{*}}}^{2}S_{\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2}}\leq\int_{\Omega}p(x)|\nabla v_{j}|^{2}dx.

Passing to limit in the previous inequality we obtain
S_{\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2}}\leq S(p)
. This contradicts the hypothesis
S(p)<S_{\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2}}
. Consequently
u\not\equiv 0
.

Now, we deduce from (

) and (

) that

S(p)\leq\displaystyle S(p)\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}}}^{2}+\frac{S(p)}{S_{\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2}}}\int_{\Omega}p(x)|\nabla v_{j}(x)|^{2}dx+o(1).

(19)

Combining (

) and (

) we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p(x)|\nabla u(x)|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}p(x)|\nabla v_{j}(x)|^{2}dx\leq S(p)\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}}}^{2}+\displaystyle\frac{S(p)}{S_{\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2}}}\int_{\Omega}p(x)|\nabla v_{j}(x)|^{2}dx+o(1).\end{array}

Thus

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p(x)|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx\leq S(p)\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}}}^{2}+\displaystyle\left[\frac{S(p)}{S_{\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2}}}-1\right]\int_{\Omega}p(x)|\nabla v_{j}(x)|^{2}dx+o(1).\end{array}

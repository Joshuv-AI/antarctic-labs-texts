Tien-Cuong Dinh and Hao Wu

Abstract
. Let f f be a meromorphic correspondence on a compact Kähler manifold X X of dimension k k . Assume that its topological degree is larger than the dynamical degree of order k − 1 k-1 . We obtain a quantitative regularity of the equilibrium measure of f f in terms of its super-potentials.



Classification AMS 2020
: 32H, 37F05, 32U40.

Keywords:
meromorphic correspondence, equilibrium measure, dynamical degree, super-potential.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
k
endowed with a Kähler form
\omega
normalized so the the integral of
\omega^{k}
on
X
is 1.
A
meromorphic correspondence

f
on
X
is given by a finite holomorphic chain
\Gamma:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma_{j}
such that

(1)

for each
j
,
\Gamma_{j}
is an irreducible analytic subset (may not be distinct) of dimension
k
in
X\times X
;

(2)

the natural projections
\pi_{i}:X\times X\to X
,
i=1,2
, restricted to each
\Gamma_{j}
, are surjective and generically finite.

We call
\Gamma
the
graph
of
f
. The action of
f
is given by

f(A):=\pi_{2}\big(\pi_{1}^{-1}(A)\cap\Gamma\big)\quad\text{and}\quad f^{-1}(A):=\pi_{1}\big(\pi_{2}^{-1}(A)\cap\Gamma\big)\quad\text{for every set}\quad A\subset X.

We see that a meromorphic correspondence can be viewed as a meromorphic multi-valued map from
X
to itself. We call the two sets

I_{1}(f):=\{x\in X:\,\dim\pi_{1}^{-1}(x)\cap\Gamma>0\}\quad\text{and}\quad I_{2}(f):=\{x\in X:\,\dim\pi_{2}^{-1}(x)\cap\Gamma>0\}

the
first and second indeterminacy sets
of
f
. They are of codimension at least
2
in
X
. If
I_{1}(f)
is empty, then
f
is a
holomorphic correspondence
.
If for a generic point
x\in X
,
\pi_{1}^{-1}(x)
contains only one point and
\Gamma
is irreducible, then
f
is a dominant meromorphic map.

To define
f^{n}:=f\circ\cdots\circ f
(
n
times), we compose
f
with itself outside an analytic set and compactify the graph in order to get the graph of
f^{n}
. We can also define the push-forward and pull-back of smooth forms under the actions of
f^{n}
, see Section
2
.
For
0\leq p\leq k
, it is known that the limit

d_{p}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|(f^{n})^{*}(\omega^{p})\|^{1/n}

exists and is a bi-meromorphic invariant, see
[
8
]
. We call it the
dynamical degree of order
p
of
f
. We have that
d_{0}
is the number of points in a generic fiber of the map
\pi_{1}|_{\Gamma}
and
d_{k}
is the one of
\pi_{2}|_{\Gamma}
(counting multiplicity). We also call
d_{k}
the
topological degree
of
f
. For more details on dynamical degrees and Kähler geometry, the readers may refer to
[
4
,
6
,
15
,
17
]
.

Sibony and the first author
[
8
]
proved that if
f
is a meromorphic correspondence on
X
with a dominant topology degree, i.e.,
d_{k}>d_{k-1}
, then
f
admits an
equilibrium measure
. More precisely, there exists a unique probability measure
\mu
on
X
such that
d_{k}^{-n}(f^{n})^{*}(\omega^{k})
converge to
\mu
weakly and it satisfies
f^{*}(\mu)=d_{k}\mu
, see also
[
12
]
.

Recall that a
quasi-plurisubharmonic
(
quasi-p.s.h.
for short) function on
X
is locally the difference of a plurisubharmonic function and a smooth one. A subset of
X
is called
pluripolar
if it is contained in
\{\phi=-\infty\}
for some quasi-plurisubharmonic function
\phi
, see also
[
19
]
. The following two sets are compact in
L^{p}(X)
for
1\leq p<\infty
,

\{\phi\,\text{ quasi-p.s.h.}:\,dd^{c}\phi\geq-\omega,\quad\max\phi=0\}

\Big\{\phi\,\text{ quasi-p.s.h.}:\,dd^{c}\phi\geq-\omega,\quad\int_{X}\phi\,\omega=0\Big\}.

Let
{\rm DSH}(X)
denote the space of all d.s.h. functions on
X
, i.e., functions which are equal to a difference of two quasi-p.s.h. functions. We identify two d.s.h. functions if they are equal outside a pluripolar set. If
\phi
is d.s.h., there are positive closed
(1,1)
-currents
T^{\pm}
on
X
such that
dd^{c}\phi=T^{+}-T^{-}
.
Since these two currents are cohomologous, they have the same mass. We define a norm on
{\rm DSH}(X)
by

\|\phi\|_{\rm DSH}:=\Big|\int_{X}\phi\,\omega\Big|+\inf\|T^{\pm}\|,

where the infimum is taken over all
T^{\pm}
as above. This norm induces a distance on
{\rm DSH}(X)
. But in this article, we will use the
L^{1}
-distance on this space.

For every d.s.h. function
\phi
on
X
, there are quasi-p.s.h. functions
\phi^{\pm}
and a number
m
, such that
\phi=\phi^{+}-\phi^{-}+m
,
\max\phi^{\pm}=0
,
dd^{c}\phi^{\pm}\geq-c\lVert\phi\rVert_{{\rm DSH}}\omega
,
\lVert\phi^{\pm}\rVert_{{\rm DSH}}\leq c\lVert\phi\rVert_{{\rm DSH}}
and
|m|\leq c\lVert\phi\rVert_{{\rm DSH}}
, where
c>0
is a constant independent of
\phi
, see
[
8
]
.

The following is our main result, which is already new when
f
is a dominant meromorphic map or a correspondence on a Riemann surface.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
be a meromorphic correspondence on a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
of dimension
k
. Let
d_{p}
denote its dynamical degree of order
p
. Assume that
d_{k}>d_{k-1}
. Let
\mu
denote the equilibrium measure of
f
. Then there is a number
\gamma>0
such that if
\mathscr{F}\subset{\rm DSH}(X)
is a bounded subset of
{\rm DSH}(X)
we have for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathscr{F}
,

\Big|\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu-\int_{X}\psi\,d\mu\Big|\leq c\big(\log^{\star}\lVert\phi-\psi\rVert_{L^{1}}\big)^{-\gamma},

where
c=c(\mathscr{F})>0
is a constant independent of
\phi
and
\psi
and
\log^{\star}(t):=1+|\log t|
.

Recall that the functional
\phi\mapsto\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu
with
\phi
d.s.h. is called the
super-potential
of
\mu
. The above theorem says that the super-potential of the equilibrium measure is
\log^{\gamma}
-continuous in the above sense. It is known that
\log^{\gamma}
-continuity is useful in complex dynamics, see
[
1
,
5
]
. For
k=1
, the result says that
\mu
has locally
\log^{\gamma}
-continuous potentials.

In Section
2
, we will discuss the action of
f
on d.s.h. functions and positive closed currents. The proof of Theorem
1.1
will be given in Section
3
.

2.
Pull-back of d.s.h. functions and
(1,1)
-currents

We refer to
[
4
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
for properties discussed in this section.

Let
f
be a meromorphic correspondence on
X
which is given by a chain
\Gamma
as above.
Since
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are holomorphic proper submersions,
(\pi_{1})_{*}
and
\pi_{2}^{*}
are well-defined on the space of currents. So for a current
T
on
X
,
we define the pull-back
f^{*}(T)
by

f^{*}(T):=(\pi_{1})_{*}\big(\pi_{2}^{*}(T)\wedge[\Gamma]\big)

if
\pi_{2}^{*}(T)\wedge[\Gamma]
is well-defined. Here,
[\Gamma]
is the positive closed current of integration on
\Gamma
. In particular, if
T
is a smooth form, then
f^{*}(T)
is well-defined and it is an
L^{1}
-form.

Let
T
be a smooth
(p,q)
-form. It is not hard to see that
f^{*}(T)
is closed if
T
is closed, and
f^{*}(T)
is exact if
T
is exact. Therefore,
f^{*}
induces a linear map on Hodge cohomology

f^{*}:H^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow H^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C}).

In general, we do not have
(g^{n})^{*}\circ(g^{m})^{*}\neq(g^{n+m})^{*}
. When the equality holds for
p=q=1
,
f
is said to be
algebraically stable
in the sense of Fornaess-Sibony
[
11
,
16
]
.

For a positive
(p,p)
-current
T
on the compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
of dimension
k
, recall that the mass of
T
is defined by
\lVert T\rVert:=\int_{X}T\wedge\omega^{k-p}
. If
T
is closed, the mass of
T
only depends on the class of
T
in
H^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{C})
.

By the above definition, we have

\lVert f^{*}(\omega^{p})\rVert=\int_{X}f^{*}(\omega^{p})\wedge\omega^{k-p}=\int_{\Gamma}\pi_{2}^{*}(\omega^{p})\wedge\pi_{1}^{*}(\omega^{k-p})=\int_{X}f_{*}(\omega^{k-p})\wedge\omega^{p}=\lVert f_{*}(\omega^{k-p})\rVert.

Observe that if
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are two positive closed
(p,p)
-forms in the same cohomology class, then
\lVert f^{*}(S_{1})\rVert=\lVert f^{*}(S_{2})\rVert
. Thus, the norm of
f^{*}
acting on
H^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{C})
is related to the action
f^{*}
on positive closed currents.
Since
H^{0,0}(X,\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathbb{C}
and
H^{k,k}(X,\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathbb{C}
,
f^{*}
acting on
H^{0,0}(X,\mathbb{C})
is the multiplication by
d_{0}
and its action on
H^{k,k}(X,\mathbb{C})
is the multiplication by
d_{k}
. Here,
d_{0}
and
d_{k}
are the dynamic degrees defined in the introduction.

For simplicity, in what follows, we assume that
\Gamma
is irreducible. The general case can be deduced from this case.
Let
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma\subset X^{2}
be a desingularization of
\Gamma
, which can be obtained by a finite number of blow-ups of
X^{2}
over the singularities of
\Gamma
. By Blanchard
[
3
]
,
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is a compact Kähler manifold. Define
\Pi_{i}:=\pi_{i}|_{\Gamma}\circ\pi
.
Then
(\Pi_{1})_{*}:{\rm DSH}(\widetilde{\Gamma})\to{\rm DSH}(X)
and
\Pi_{2}^{*}:{\rm DSH}(X)\to{\rm DSH}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
are well-defined, as well as
f^{*}=(\Pi_{1})_{*}\circ\Pi_{2}^{*}
on
{\rm DSH}(X)
. In particular, we can define
\pi_{2}^{*}(\phi)\wedge[\Gamma]:=\pi_{*}(\Pi_{2}^{*}(\phi))
for
\phi\in{\rm DSH}(X)
. This definition is independent of the choice of
\pi
.

Now let
T
be a positive closed
(1,1)
-current. We can take a real smooth
(1,1)
-form
S
which is in the same cohomology class of
T
. Then there exists a d.s.h. function
\phi
such that
dd^{c}\phi=T-S
and we have

\pi_{2}^{*}(T)\wedge[\Gamma]=\pi_{2}^{*}(dd^{c}\phi+S)\wedge[\Gamma]=\pi_{2}^{*}(dd^{c}\phi)\wedge[\Gamma]+\pi_{2}^{*}(S)\wedge[\Gamma]=dd^{c}(\pi_{2}^{*}(\phi)[\Gamma])+\pi_{2}^{*}(S)\wedge[\Gamma].

In the last sum, the first term is well-defined from our discussion above, and the second one is also well-defined since
S
is smooth. Hence
\pi_{2}^{*}(T)\wedge[\Gamma]
is well-defined. So we can define

f^{*}(T):=(\pi_{1})_{*}\big(dd^{c}(\pi_{2}^{*}\phi\wedge[\Gamma])+\pi_{2}^{*}(S)\wedge[\Gamma]\big).

Clearly, this definition is independent of the choices of
\phi
and
S
. It depends continuously on
T
and we have
\lVert f^{*}(T)\rVert=\lVert f^{*}(S)\rVert
.

Similarly, we can define the push-forward
f_{*}
on d.s.h. functions and positive closed
(1,1)
-currents as above.

Since
\Pi_{1}
is holomorphic and proper, it is not hard to see that the push-forward operator
(\Pi_{1})_{*}:{\rm DSH}(X)\to{\rm DSH}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
is bounded with respect to the
{\rm DSH}
-norm.
The pull-back operator
\Pi_{2}^{*}:{\rm DSH}(\widetilde{\Gamma})\to{\rm DSH}(X)
is bounded as well by the next lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
g:X\to Y
be a surjective holomorphic map between two compact Kähler manifolds. Then the pull-back operator
g^{*}:{\rm DSH}(Y)\to{\rm DSH}(X)
is bounded with respect to the
{\rm DSH}
-norm.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in{\rm DSH}(Y)
with
\lVert\phi\rVert_{{\rm DSH}}=1
. It enough to show that
g^{*}(\phi)
is contained in a compact subset of
{\rm DSH}(X)
. We write
\phi=\phi^{+}-\phi^{-}+m
for quasi-p.s.h. functions
\phi^{\pm}
, where
\max\phi^{\pm}=0
,
dd^{c}\phi^{\pm}\geq-c\omega
,
\lVert\phi^{\pm}\rVert_{{\rm DSH}}\leq c
and
|m|\leq c
, for some constant
c>0
independent of
\phi
. We have
g^{*}(\phi)=g^{*}(\phi^{+})-g^{*}(\phi^{-})+m
. Moreover,
dd^{c}g^{*}(\phi^{\pm})\geq-cg^{*}(\omega)
and
\max g^{*}(\phi^{\pm})=0
.
Therefore,
g^{*}(\phi^{\pm})
are in a compact subset of
L^{1}(X)
. Combining with
|m|\leq c
, we deduce that
g^{*}(\phi)
is also in a compact subset of
{\rm DSH}(X)
.
∎

3.
Proof of the main theorem

We start the proof of Theorem
1.1
with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
B
be a metric space of finite diameter. Let
u:B\to\mathbb{R}
be a Lipschitz function with Lipschitz constant
c_{1}
, and let
\Lambda:B\to B
be a
\theta
-Hölder map (
0<\theta\leq 1
) with
\lVert\Lambda\rVert_{\mathscr{C}^{0,\theta}}=c_{2}
. Let
\delta>1
be a constant. Then the function

v(x):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta^{-n}u(\Lambda^{n}(x))

is
\log^{\gamma}
-continuous for some constant
\gamma>0
. That is, we have

|v(x)-v(y)|\leq c\big(\log^{\star}\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits(x,y)\big)^{-\gamma}\quad\text{for all}\quad x,y\in B,

where
c>0
is a constant depending on
c_{1},c_{2},\theta
and
\delta
.

Proof.

When
\theta=1
, by
[
10
, Lemma 1.19]
,
v
is Hölder continuous, which clearly implies the desired inequality. Now we assume
\theta<1
.
Define
\xi:=\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits(x,y)
. We only need to consider
\xi<1/4
because otherwise the desired inequality holds for
c
large enough. We have
1+|\log\xi|\leq 2|\log\xi|
. So it is enough to prove

(3.1)

|v(x)-v(y)|\leq c|\log\xi|^{-\gamma}\quad\text{for some}\quad c>0.

Fix a constant
c_{3}>1
larger than
c_{2}
. By induction,
we have for
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\big|\Lambda^{n}(x)-\Lambda^{n}(y)\big|\leq c_{2}^{1+\theta+\cdots+\theta^{n-1}}\xi^{\theta^{n}}\leq c_{3}^{1/(1-\theta)}\xi^{\theta^{n}}.

After multiplying
u
by a constant, one can assume
\max u-\min u\leq 1
and
c_{1}\leq 1
.
Therefore, using that
\xi^{\theta^{n}}\leq\xi^{\theta^{m}}
for
n\leq m
, we get

\displaystyle|v(x)-v(y)|\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta^{-n}\big|u(\Lambda^{n}(x))-u(\Lambda^{n}(y))\big|

\displaystyle\leq|u(x)-u(y)|+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\delta^{-n}\big|u(\Lambda^{n}(x))-u(\Lambda^{n}(y))\big|+\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\delta^{-n}

\displaystyle\leq\xi+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\delta^{-n}c_{3}^{1/(1-\theta)}\xi^{\theta^{n}}+{\delta^{-N}\over\delta-1}\leq C_{1}(\xi^{\theta^{N}}+\delta^{-N}).

Here,
N
is any positive integer, and
C_{1}
is some constant depending on
c_{3},\theta
and
\delta
.

Now we take

N:=\lfloor\log|\log\xi|/|2\log\theta|\rfloor\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma:=\log\delta/|2\log\theta|.

Recall that
\theta<1
and
\xi<1/4
. So

\displaystyle\xi^{\theta^{N}}\leq\xi^{\theta^{\log|\log\xi|/|2\log\theta|}}=\exp\Big[\theta^{\log|\log\xi|/|2\log\theta|}\log\xi\Big]=\exp\Big[|\log\xi|^{-1/2}\log\xi\Big]

\displaystyle=\exp\Big[-|\log\xi|^{1/2}\Big]\leq C_{2}\exp\Big[-\gamma\log|\log\xi|\Big]=C_{2}|\log\xi|^{-\gamma}

for some constant
C_{2}
depending on
\theta
and
\delta
. For the inequality above, we use that
-|\log\xi|^{1/2}\leq A-\gamma\log|\log\xi|
for some large constant
A
depending on
\theta
and
\delta
.

For the term
\delta^{-N}
, we have

\delta^{-N}\leq\delta^{-\log|\log\xi|/|2\log\theta|+1}=\delta\exp\Big[\big(-\log|\log\xi|/|2\log\theta|\big)\cdot\log\delta\Big]=\delta\exp\Big[-\gamma\log|\log\xi|\Big],

which is equal to
\delta|\log\xi|^{-\gamma}
.
So we conclude that

|v(x)-v(y)|\leq C_{1}(C_{2}|\log\xi|^{-\gamma}+\delta|\log\xi|^{-\gamma})=C_{1}(C_{2}+\delta)|\log\xi|^{-\gamma}.

This gives (
3.1
) for
c=C_{1}(C_{2}+\delta)
and finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

From the discussions in Section
2
,
f_{*}
is a Lipschitz map on the space of d.s.h. functions with respect to the DSH-distance. Moreover, we need that with the
L^{1}
-distance on
{\rm DSH}(X)
,
f_{*}
is Hölder on bounded subset
{\rm DSH}(X)
. This is the content of the next lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

There is a number
0<\theta\leq 1
such that
\phi\mapsto f_{*}(\phi)
is
\theta
-Hölder with respect to the
L^{1}
-norm on every bounded subset of
{\rm DSH}(X)
. That is, for every bounded subset
\mathscr{F}\subset{\rm DSH}(X)
, there exists a constant
c=c(\mathscr{F})
such that

\big\|f_{*}(\phi)-f_{*}(\psi)\big\|_{L^{1}}\leq c\|\phi-\psi\|_{L^{1}}^{\theta}\quad\text{for}\quad\phi,\psi\in\mathscr{F}.

Proof.

Observe that we can replace
\mathscr{F}
by
\mathscr{F}-\mathscr{F}
so that it is enough to prove that
\lVert f_{*}(\phi)\rVert_{L^{1}}\lesssim\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\theta}
for
\phi\in\mathscr{F}
. We can work with each component of
\Gamma
separately. So, we can assume that
\Gamma
is irreducible for simplicity.

Let
\pi:\widetilde{\Gamma}\to\Gamma
be a desingularization of
\Gamma
as in Section
2
and we fix a Kähler form
\widetilde{\omega}
on
\widetilde{\Gamma}
.
Define
\Pi_{i}:=\pi_{i}\circ\pi
.
We have
\Pi_{1}^{*}:{\rm DSH}(X)\to{\rm DSH}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
and
(\Pi_{2})_{*}:{\rm DSH}(\widetilde{\Gamma})\to{\rm DSH}(X)
and
f_{*}=(\Pi_{2})_{*}\circ\Pi_{1}^{*}
. From Section
2
, we know that all these maps are well-defined and bounded with respect to the
{\rm DSH}
-norm.

We first consider
(\Pi_{2})_{*}
. Since
\Pi_{2}
is holomorphic,
(\Pi_{2})^{*}(\omega^{j})
is smooth and hence
(\Pi_{2})^{*}(\omega^{j})\lesssim\widetilde{\omega}^{j}
. For any
\varphi\in{\rm DSH}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
, we have

\lVert(\Pi_{2})_{*}(\varphi)\rVert_{L^{1}}\leq\int_{X}(\Pi_{2})_{*}(|\varphi|)\,\omega^{k}=\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}|\varphi|\,(\Pi_{2})^{*}(\omega^{k})\lesssim\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}|\varphi|\,\widetilde{\omega}^{k}=\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{1}}.

It remains to prove
\|\Pi_{1}^{*}(\phi)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}^{\theta}
for
\phi\in\mathscr{F}
. Let
\Sigma
denote the set of critical values of
\Pi_{1}
and let
\widetilde{\Sigma}:=\Pi_{1}^{-1}(\Sigma)
. We also denote by
\widetilde{\Sigma}^{\prime}
the set of critical points of
\Pi_{1}
, which is the zero set
\{\mathrm{Jac}\Pi_{1}=0\}
of the Jacobian of
\Pi_{1}
in local holomorphic coordinates (we cover
\widetilde{\Gamma}
by a finite number of local holomorphic charts). Clearly,
\widetilde{\Sigma}^{\prime}\subset\widetilde{\Sigma}
. Using blow-ups, we can assume that
\widetilde{\Sigma}
is a finite union of smooth manifolds.
Let
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}
be the
\epsilon
-neighbourhood of
\widetilde{\Sigma}
. We separate
\|\Pi_{1}^{*}(\phi)\|_{L^{1}}
into two parts:

(3.2)

\|\Pi_{1}^{*}(\phi)\|_{L^{1}}\leq\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}\setminus\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}}\Pi_{1}^{*}(|\phi|)\,\widetilde{\omega}^{k}+\int_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}}\Pi_{1}^{*}(|\phi|)\,\widetilde{\omega}^{k}.

Claim 1.

We have for some
\alpha>0
, independent of
\phi
, that

\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}\setminus\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}}\Pi_{1}^{*}(|\phi|)\,\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\lesssim\epsilon^{-\alpha}\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{1}}\quad\text{for all}\quad\phi\in L^{1}(X).

Proof of Claim 1.

Since
\widetilde{\Sigma}^{\prime}
is an analytic subset of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
defined by
\mathrm{Jac}\Pi_{1}
, we can apply Lojasiewicz inequality
[
2
, Theorem 6.4]
to the function
\mathrm{Jac}\Pi_{1}
and get

\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits(x,\widetilde{\Sigma}^{\prime})\lesssim|\mathrm{Jac}\Pi_{1}(x)|^{A}\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\widetilde{\Gamma},

where

A>0
is a constant independent of
x
. Hence

\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits(x,\widetilde{\Sigma})\leq\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits(x,\widetilde{\Sigma}^{\prime})\lesssim|\mathrm{Jac}\Pi_{1}(x)|^{A}.

So for
x\in\widetilde{\Gamma}\setminus\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}
, one has

|\mathrm{Jac}\Pi_{1}(x)|^{-1}\lesssim\epsilon^{-1/A}.

For every
x\in\widetilde{\Gamma}\setminus\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}
, one can find a small neighborhood
D\subset\widetilde{\Gamma}
of
x
such that
\Pi_{1}
is injective on
D
and hence,
\Pi_{1}
is a biholomorphic map between
D
and
\Pi_{1}(D)
. Therefore, at the point
\Pi_{1}(x)
, we have

(\Pi_{1}|_{D})_{*}(\widetilde{\omega}^{k})\lesssim|\mathrm{Jac}\Pi_{1}|^{-2}\omega^{k}\lesssim\epsilon^{-2/A}\omega^{k}.

Notice that
\Pi_{1}
is a holomorphic covering of degree
d_{0}
outside
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}
. So

(\Pi_{1})_{*}(\widetilde{\omega}^{k})\lesssim\epsilon^{-2/A}\omega^{k}\quad\text{on}\quad\Pi_{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma}\setminus\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}).

Taking
\alpha:=2/A
, we have

\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}\setminus\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}}\Pi_{1}^{*}(|\phi|)\,\widetilde{\omega}^{k}=\int_{\Pi_{1}(\widetilde{\Gamma}\setminus\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon})}|\phi|\,(\Pi_{1})_{*}(\widetilde{\omega}^{k})\lesssim\int_{X}|\phi|\epsilon^{-\alpha}\,\omega^{k}=\epsilon^{-\alpha}\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{1}}.

The proof of Claim 1 is finished.
∎

Recall that a positive measure
\nu
on
X
is said to be
moderate
if
\nu
has no mass on pluripolar sets and for any bounded family
\mathscr{F}
of d.s.h. functions on
X
, there are constants
\alpha^{\prime}>0
and
c^{\prime}>0
such that

\int_{X}e^{\alpha^{\prime}|\psi|}d\nu\leq c^{\prime}\quad\text{for}\quad\psi\in\mathscr{F}.

Equivalently, there are constants
\beta^{\prime}>0
and
c^{\prime}>0
such that

\nu\{z\in X:|\psi(z)|>M\}\leq c^{\prime}e^{-\beta^{\prime}M}

for
M\geq 0
and
\psi\in\mathscr{F}
.
The probability measure
\omega^{k}
is moderate
[
13
]
. The Lebesgue measure on a generic real submanifold of
X
with dimension
\geq k
is also moderate
[
18
]
. See also
[
14
]
for a local situation.

Claim 2.

We have

\int_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}}\Pi_{1}^{*}(|\phi|)\,\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\lesssim\epsilon^{1/2}\quad\text{for}\quad\phi\in\mathscr{F}.

Proof of Claim 2.

It is enough to consider
\epsilon
small.
We first estimate the volume of
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}
. Recall that
\widetilde{\Sigma}
is a finite union of smooth manifolds. So the volume of
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}
is
\lesssim\epsilon
.
Moreover, since
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}
contains a ball of radius
\epsilon
, its volume is
\gtrsim\epsilon^{2k}
.

By Lemma
2.1
,
\Pi_{1}^{*}(|\phi|)
is in a compact subset of
{\rm DSH}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
for
\phi\in\mathscr{F}
.
Using the moderate property of
\widetilde{\omega}^{k}
again, for
\phi\in\mathscr{F}
, one has

\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}e^{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\Pi_{1}^{*}(|\phi|)}\,\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\leq c^{\prime\prime}

for some positive constants
\alpha^{\prime\prime}
and
c^{\prime\prime}
independent of
\phi
. In particular,

\int_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}}e^{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\Pi_{1}^{*}(|\phi|)}\,\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\leq c^{\prime\prime}\quad\text{for all}\quad\phi\in\mathscr{F}.

Applying Jensen’s inequality to the probability measure
\widetilde{\omega}^{k}/\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon})
on
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}
, yields

\exp\Big[\int_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}}\alpha^{\prime\prime}\Pi_{1}^{*}(|\phi|)\,{\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\over\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon})}\Big]\leq\int_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}}e^{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\Pi_{1}^{*}(|\phi|)}\,{\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\over\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon})}\leq{c^{\prime\prime}\over\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon})}.

Therefore,

\int_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}}\Pi_{1}^{*}(|\phi|)\,\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon})\over\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\log{c^{\prime\prime}\over\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\epsilon})}\lesssim\epsilon\cdot\log(\epsilon^{-2k})\lesssim\epsilon^{1/2}.

This finishes the proof of Claim 2.
∎

Using the above Claims and taking
\epsilon:=\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{1}}^{2/(2\alpha+1)}
, we get from (
3.2
) that
\|\Pi_{1}^{*}(\phi)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{1}}^{1/(2\alpha+1)}
and finish the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\epsilon>0
be any positive number. Then there is a constant
c=c(\epsilon)>0
such that for any
n\geq 0
and any positive closed
(1,1)
-current
S
of mass
1
we have

\|(f^{n})_{*}(S)\|\leq c(d_{k-1}+\epsilon)^{n}.

Proof.

We fix a norm on the cohomology group
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{C})
of
X
. Recall from introduction that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|(f^{n})^{*}:H^{k-1,k-1}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{k-1,k-1}(X,\mathbb{C})\|^{1/n}=d_{k-1}.

By Poincaré duality, we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|(f^{n})_{*}:H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{C})\|^{1/n}=d_{k-1}.

By
[
4
,
7
]
, there are positive closed smooth
(1,1)
-forms
S_{j}^{\pm}
on
X
such that
\|S_{j}^{\pm}\|
is bounded by a constant and

S_{j}^{+}-S_{j}^{-}\to S
in the sense of currents. Since
(f^{n})_{*}
acts continuously on positive closed
(1,1)
-currents, we can replace
S
by
S_{j}^{\pm}
in order to assume that
S
is smooth.

Since
\|S\|=1
, its cohomology class in
H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{C})
has a norm bounded by a constant
A
. Thus, the norm of the cohomology class of
(f^{n})_{*}(S)
in
H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{C})
is bounded by

A\|(f^{n})_{*}:H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{C})\|.

We easily deduce that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|(f^{n})_{*}(S)\|^{1/n}\leq d_{k-1}
using the fact that
the mass of a positive closed current only depends on its cohomology class. The lemma follows.
∎

We continue the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Recall that
d_{k-1}<d_{k}
. According to the last lemma, replacing
f
by an iterate (this doesn’t change the measure
\mu
) allows us to assume that

\kappa:=\sup\big\{\|f_{*}(S)\|:\,S\text{ positive closed $(1,1)$-current of mass 1}\big\}<d_{k}.

We fix a
\delta
with
1<\delta<d_{k}/\kappa
and define
\Lambda:=\delta d_{k}^{-1}f_{*}
acting on
{\rm DSH}(X)
.

Recall from
[
8
]
that
\phi\mapsto\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu
is continuous on
{\rm DSH}(X)
. So we can replace the
{\rm DSH}
-norm by the following equivalent norm

\|\phi\|_{\mu}:=\Big|\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu\Big|+\inf\|T^{\pm}\|,

where the infimum is taken over all positive closed
(1,1)
-currents
T^{\pm}
such that
dd^{c}\phi=T^{+}-T^{-}
.

Consider the hyperplane of
{\rm DSH}(X)
defined by

E:=\Big\{\phi\in{\rm DSH}(X):\,\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu=0\Big\}.

Lemma 3.4
.

The space
E
is invariant by
\Lambda
and we have
\|\Lambda\|_{\mu}\leq 1
. In particular, any ball of center
0
in
E
is invariant by
\Lambda
.

Proof.

Since
\mu
has no mass on pluripolar sets and
f^{*}(\mu)=d_{k}\mu
, we have for
\phi\in E
,

\int_{X}\Lambda(\phi)\,d\mu=\int_{X}\delta d_{k}^{-1}f_{*}(\phi)\,d\mu=\delta\int_{X}\phi\,d_{k}^{-1}\,df^{*}(\mu)=\delta\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu=0.

So
E
is invariant by
\Lambda
.

For the second assertion, it is enough to prove
\lVert\Lambda(T)\rVert\leq\lVert T\rVert
for all positive closed
(1,1)
-current
T
. This follows from the assumption that
\delta d_{k}^{-1}\kappa<1
.
∎

Now we can complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

End of the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For
\phi,\psi\in{\rm DSH}(X)
with the DSH-norm bounded by a fixed constant
M>1
, define
\phi^{\prime}:=d_{k}^{-1}f_{*}(\phi)-\phi
and
\psi^{\prime}:=d_{k}^{-1}f_{*}(\psi)-\psi
. Then both
\phi^{\prime}
and
\psi^{\prime}
are in
E
since
\mu
is invariant by
d_{k}^{-1}f^{*}
.

The definition of
\phi^{\prime}
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{X}d_{k}^{-n}(f^{n})_{*}(\phi^{\prime})\,\omega^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{X}d_{k}^{-n}(f^{n})_{*}(d_{k}^{-1}f_{*}(\phi)-\phi)\,\omega^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big[\int_{X}d_{k}^{-(n+1)}(f^{n+1})_{*}(\phi)\,\omega^{k}-\int_{X}d_{k}^{-n}(f^{n})_{*}(\phi)\,\omega^{k}\Big]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}d_{k}^{-n}(f^{n})_{*}(\phi)\,\omega^{k}-\int_{X}\phi\,\omega^{k}.

Recall that
d_{k}^{-n}(f^{n})^{*}(\omega^{k})
converges to
\mu
. So

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}d_{k}^{-n}(f^{n})_{*}(\phi)\,\omega^{k}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}\phi\,d_{k}^{-n}(f^{n})^{*}(\omega^{k})=\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu.

Therefore, by the definition of
\Lambda
, we have

\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu=\int_{X}\phi\,\omega^{k}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{X}d_{k}^{-n}(f^{n})_{*}(\phi^{\prime})\,\omega^{k}=\int_{X}\phi\,\omega^{k}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta^{-n}\int_{X}\Lambda^{n}(\phi^{\prime})\,\omega^{k}.

The same equality holds after replacing
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
by
\psi,\psi^{\prime}
.
Hence

\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu-\int_{X}\psi\,d\mu=\int_{X}(\phi-\psi)\,\omega^{k}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta^{-n}\int_{X}\Lambda^{n}(\phi^{\prime}-\psi^{\prime})\,\omega^{k}.

Notice that
\lVert\phi^{\prime}\rVert_{{\rm DSH}},\lVert\psi^{\prime}\rVert_{{\rm DSH}}
are both bounded by a fixed constant times
M
. So
\phi^{\prime}
and
\psi^{\prime}
belong to a ball
\mathbb{B}
(with respect to the
L^{1}
-norm) of center
0
in
E
with radius bounded by a constant times
M
. Moreover, by Lemmas
3.2
and
3.4
,
\Lambda
is an
\theta
-Hölder map from
\mathbb{B}
to
\mathbb{B}
with respect to the
L^{1}
-distance for some
0<\theta\leq 1
.
So we can apply Lemma
3.1
with
u(\phi):=\int_{X}\phi\,\omega^{k}
, getting

\Big|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta^{-n}\int_{X}\Lambda(\phi^{\prime}-\psi^{\prime})\,\omega^{k}\Big|\lesssim\big(\log^{\star}\lVert\phi^{\prime}-\psi^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{1}}\big)^{-\gamma}

for some
\gamma>0
.

By using Lemma
3.2
again, we have

\lVert\phi^{\prime}-\psi^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{1}}\leq\big\|d_{k}^{-1}f_{*}(\phi-\psi)\big\|_{L^{1}}+\lVert\phi-\psi\rVert_{L^{1}}\lesssim\lVert\phi-\psi\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\theta}+\lVert\phi-\psi\rVert_{L^{1}}\lesssim\lVert\phi-\psi\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\theta}.

Here, we use that
\lVert\phi-\psi\rVert_{L^{1}}\lesssim M
.

We conclude that

\Big|\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu-\int_{X}\psi\,d\mu\Big|\lesssim\lVert\phi-\psi\rVert_{L^{1}}+\big(\log^{\star}\lVert\phi-\psi\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\theta}\big)^{-\gamma}.

Since
\lVert\phi-\psi\rVert_{L^{1}}\lesssim M
, to prove the desired inequality, it is enough to consider the case where
t:=\lVert\phi-\psi\rVert_{L^{1}}\leq 1/2
. In this case, we have

t\lesssim(1-\log t)^{-\gamma}

and

(1-\theta\log t)^{-\gamma}=\theta^{-\gamma}(\theta^{-1}-\log t)^{-\gamma}\leq\theta^{-\gamma}(1-\log t)^{-\gamma}.

We deduce that

\Big|\int_{X}\phi\,d\mu-\int_{X}\psi\,d\mu\Big|\leq c(1-\log t)^{-\gamma}=c(\log^{\star}t)^{-\gamma}

for some constant
c>0
depending on
\theta
and
\gamma
.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
is now complete.
∎

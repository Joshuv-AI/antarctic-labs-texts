On Young regimes for locally monotone SPDEs
Florian Bechtold, Jörn Wichmann

Abstract
. We consider the following SPDE on a Gelfand-triple ( V , H , V ∗ ) (V,H,V^{*}) : d ​ u ​ ( t ) = A ⁡ ( t , u ⁡ ( t ) ) ​ d ​ t + d ​ I t ​ ( u ) , u ⁡ ( 0 ) = u 0 ∈ H . \begin{split}\,\text{\rm d}u(t)&=A(t,u(t))\,\text{\rm d}t+\,\text{\rm d}I_{t}(u),\\ u(0)&=u_{0}\in H.\end{split} Given certain local monotonicity, continuity, coercivity and growth conditions of the operator A : [ 0 , T ] × V → V ∗ A:[0,T]\times V\to V^{*} and a sufficiently regular operator I I we establish global existence of weak solutions. In analogy to the Young regime for SDEs, no probabilistic structure is required in our analysis, which is based on a careful combination of monotone operator theory and the recently developed Besov rough analysis in [ FSZK22 ] . Due to the abstract nature of our approach, it applies to various examples of monotone and locally monotone operators A A , such as the p p -Laplace operator, the porous medium operator, and an operator that arises in the context of shear-thickening fluids; and operators I I , including additive Young drivers I t ​ ( u ) = Z t − Z 0 I_{t}(u)=Z_{t}-Z_{0} , abstract Young integrals I t ​ ( u ) = ∫ 0 t σ ⁡ ( u s ) ​ d ​ X s I_{t}(u)=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{s})\,\text{\rm d}X_{s} , and translated integrals I t ​ ( u ) = ∫ 0 t b ⁡ ( u s − w s ) ​ d ​ s I_{t}(u)=\int_{0}^{t}b(u_{s}-w_{s})\,\text{\rm d}s that arise in the context of regularization by noise. In each of the latter cases, we identify corresponding noise regimes (i.e. Young regimes) that assure our abstract result to be applicable. In the case of additive drivers, we identify the Brownian setting as borderline, i.e. noises which enjoy slightly more temporal regularity are amenable to our completely pathwise analysis.



1.
Introduction

Going back to the works of Young
[
You36
]
and Kondurar
[
Kon37
]
, it has been a well established fact that differential equations of the form

Y_{t}=Y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(Y_{s})\,\text{\rm d}X_{s}

(1.1)

are well-posed for smooth
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to L(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{n})
provided
X\in C^{\alpha}_{t}\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
\alpha>1/2
. It is therefore common to call this regularity class the Young regime
1
1

1

For a discuss of Young regimes on different regularity scales, see section
1.1
associated to (
1.1
). In modern terms, this can be readily verified by an application of the sewing lemma, see for instance
[
FH20
, Section 8.3]
.

As is also well known, sample paths of
d
-dimensional Brownian motion are almost surely
C^{\alpha}_{t}\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
\alpha<1/2
, meaning their regularity is just shy of the Young regime. This means that additional probabilistic structure of Brownian motion needs to be exploited in order to obtain a well-posedness theory for (
1.1
). In the case of Itô calculus for example, one crucially makes use of the martingale property of Brownian motion. Let us stress that from a purely analytical point of view, one could argue that this additional probabilistic structure compensates for the lack of regularity of Brownian sample paths, which fall outside the Young regime for (
1.1
) almost surely.

Recall finally that Itô calculus allows for an infinite dimensional generalization. Indeed, if
H
,
U
are separable Hilbert spaces,
B
is an
U
-cylindrical Wiener process,
Z\in L_{2}(U,H)
and
W=ZB
, then the variational approach
2
2

2

refer to section
1.3
to SPDEs allows for the study of abstract problems of the form

\,\text{\rm d}u_{t}=A(t,u_{t})\,\text{\rm d}t+\,\text{\rm d}W_{t},\qquad u(0)=u_{0}\in H.

(1.2)

Here
A:[0,T]\times V\to V^{*}
is some (locally) monotone, hemicontinuous, coercive and bounded operator defined on a Gelfand triple
(V,H,V^{*})
. Besides semi-linear problems, this abstract framework provides a well-posedness theory for
A
being the
p
-Laplace or porous medium operator for example. Note that in this setting, the driving noise enjoys regularity
C^{\alpha}_{t}H
for any
\alpha<1/2
almost surely.

On the background of all of the above, one could wonder if it is possible to still obtain well-posedness for (
1.2
), provided we replace the infinite-dimensional stochastic process
W
by some generic path

, which posses higher temporal regularity. More precisely, we ask for a Young regime associated to (

Question 1.1
.

Given a Gelfand triple
(V,H,V^{*})
and an operator
A:[0,T]\times V\to V^{*}

Does there exists a regularity scale, i.e., a choice of parameters
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, such that for any

\,\text{\rm d}u_{t}=A(t,u_{t})\,\text{\rm d}t+\,\text{\rm d}X_{t},\qquad u(0)=u_{0}\in H,

is well-posed?

We give a full answer to Question
1.1
in Theorem
2.13
. A natural related question that we will address separately, concerns the multiplicative forcing setting, i.e.

Question 1.2
.

Given a Gelfand triple
(V,H,V^{*})
, an operator

1.1.
Young regimes for SDEs and semilinear SPDEs

Beyond the Hölder scale mentioned above, different regularity scales for driving noises

]

) on Besov scales, while also addressing the ’rough regime’. In the general case of nonlinear smooth

for
\alpha>1/2
and

. In
[
LPT21
, Theorem 3.8]
, the authors are able to cover

with
\alpha>1/2
and

. Finally, a completion of this program can be considered
[

, which obtains well-posedness for

provided either

,

and

or
\alpha=1/2
,

and

for

A first step towards non-linear SPDEs is the understanding of semilinear SPDEs. These equations typically take the form

\,\text{\rm d}u_{t}=Au_{t}\,\text{\rm d}t+f(u_{t})\,\text{\rm d}t+\sigma(u_{t})\,\text{\rm d}W_{t},\qquad u(0)=u_{0}\in H,

u_{t}=S_{t}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S_{t-s}f(u_{s})\,\text{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}S_{t-s}\sigma(u_{s})\,\text{\rm d}W_{s}.

(1.4)

This means that upon establishing adequate Schauder estimates and maximal inequalities for stochastic convolutions, (

[

,
GG94
,
BG99
,
Hof12
,

,

,
GH19
,
Bec21
]
. A classical reference for problems of this type is given by
[

]
.

In the case of

equally colored in space, (

) has been studied in
[
MN03

1.2

1.2.
Monotone operators and evolution equations

A different method to study evolution equations for a large class of non-linear differential operators consists in the monotone operator approach – a vastly studied field since its introduction by Browder
[
Bro63
]
and Minty
[
Min62
]
. Many authors contributed to the development of monotone and pseudo-monotone operator theory, e.g.,
[
Lio69
,
Bre73
,
Ken74
,
LM80
]
. More references can be found, for example, in the books
[
Zei90
,
Bar10
,
Sho13
,
Kal23
]
.

We briefly sketch some conceptual ideas: For a separable, reflexive Banach space
V
and an operator
A:V\to V^{*}
, the theorem of Browder and Minty asserts that the equation

A(u)=b\in V^{*},

will always have a solution
u\in V
, provided that
A:V\to V^{*}
is (i) monotone, (ii) hemicontinous and (iii) coercive. Typical examples of monotone operators include the
p
-Laplace and porous medium operators.

Its extension to evolution equations requires working on a Gelfand triple
(V,H,V^{*})
and imposing additionally (iv) a growth condition; in this situation, it can be shown (see for example
[
Lio69
, p. 319]
) that the associated initial value problem

\partial_{t}u=A(u),\qquad u(0)=u_{0}\in H,

is well-posed in
C([0,T];H)\cap L^{\alpha}((0,T);V)\cap W^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}((0,T);V^{*})
, where
\alpha>1
(
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha/(\alpha-1)
) quantifies the coercivity of
A
.

The theory canonically extends to additive perturbations
Z\in W^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}((0,T);V^{*})
and
Z\in L^{\alpha}((0,T);V)
by considering

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=A(u)+\partial_{t}Z,\hskip 30.00005pt\text{ and }\hskip 30.00005pt\partial_{t}v=A(v+Z),

respectively. In the latter case, the solution
u
is reconstructed by the transformation
u=v+Z
. Thus, additive perturbations are well understood for endpoint regularity assumptions, i.e., either the perturbation is temporally regular and spatially rough
Z\in W^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}((0,T);V^{*})
, or temporally rough and spatially regular
Z\in L^{\alpha}((0,T);V)
. The latter setting has been investigated extensively in
[
GLR11
]
for example. But an understanding on intermediate scales – below possessing a time derivative and taking values in
H
– is missing. This gap in the literature is addressed by Question
1.1
and its answer in Theorem
2.13
.

1.4.
Abstract formulation of the problem

Let us point out that a common feature of the results discussed in the last subsection is the usage of some martingale structure in
W
, allowing to first give meaning to the stochastic integral
\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{s})\,\text{\rm d}W_{s}
and secondly, to derive an Itô-formula for
\left\lVert u_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}
. This in turn provides crucial energy estimates similar to the ones encountered in the deterministic setting. Question
1.2
therefore implicitly asks two questions: (i) If we replace the stochastic process
W
with a generic path
X
, what regularity requirement on
u
and
X
do we need to impose in order for
I_{t}(u)=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{s})\,\text{\rm d}X_{s}
to be still well-defined? (ii) Provided this expression is well-defined, what further requirements on
I
ensure that we can solve the associated PDE? In order to decouple these two sub-questions, we isolate and abstract (ii) in the following way:

Question 1.3
.

Given a Gelfand triple
(V,H,V^{*})
and an operator
A:[0,T]\times V\to V^{*}
that is locally monotone, hemicontinuous, coercive and bounded.

Does there exist a regularity scale, i.e., a choice of parameters
\alpha_{1}\in(0,1)
,
\alpha_{2}\in(1/2,1)
and
p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\in[1,\infty]
, and class of operators

\mathcal{I}\subset\{I:B^{\alpha_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}((0,T);H)\to B^{\alpha_{2}}_{p_{2},q_{2}}((0,T);H)\}

such that for any
I\in\mathcal{I}
the problem

\,\text{\rm d}u_{t}=A(t,u_{t})\,\text{\rm d}t+\,\text{\rm d}I_{t}(u),\qquad u(0)=u_{0}\in H,

is well-posed?

In this article, we provide an affirmative answer to the existence of weak solutions raised in Question
1.3
for a specific class of abstract operators
I
– Theorem
2.8
. In short, weak solutions exist whenever the abstract operator

•

can be approximated by more regular integrals;

•

satisfies a non-standard stability bound;

•

and is continuous in a rather weak sense.

In a separate argument, we address (i) by providing conditions on
u,X,\sigma
, which ensure that
I_{t}(u)=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{s})\,\text{\rm d}X_{s}
is well-defined. We also show that under these conditions, Theorem
2.8
is applicable thus giving a partial answer to Question
1.2
.

Thanks to our decoupling approach, Theorem
2.8
can also be applied in some regularization by noise settings by choosing
I_{t}(u)=\int_{0}^{t}b(u_{s}-w_{s})\,\text{\rm d}s
. Here, we assume
w
to be a path with sufficiently regular local time
3
3

3

refer to the Appendix
8
. This allows us to extend a recent result
[
BW23
]
on regularisation by noise for the
p
-Laplace equation, Theorem
2.22
.

1.5.
Structure of the paper

In Section
2
, we establish the framework and introduce the main results of this article. We close the section by a discussion on possible extensions of the methods.

In Section

FSZK22

.

In Section

In Section

2.1.
Assumptions

Let

be a separable Hilbert space with inner product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{H}

Assumption 2.1

(Compact Gelfand triple)
.

We assume that

is compact.

Assumption 2.2

(Monotone operator)
.

We assume that

is

(H1)

(Hemicontinuity) For all

and

the map

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\ni\lambda\mapsto\langle A(t,u+\lambda v),w\rangle_{V^{*},V}

(H2)

(Local monotonicity) For all

and

2\langle A(t,u)-A(t,v),u-v\rangle_{V}\;\leqslant\;(h_{t}+\eta(u))\left\lVert u-v\right\rVert_{H}^{2},

where

is non-negative, and

is measurable and locally bounded.

(H3)

(Coercivity) There exist
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
,

,

(H4)

(Boundedness) There exist

.

and
t\in[0,T]

\left\lVert b^{n}(t,v)\right\rVert_{H}\;\leqslant\;C_{n}(1+\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H}),\qquad\left\lVert b^{n}(t,u)-b^{n}(t,v)\right\rVert_{H}\;\leqslant\;C_{n}\left\lVert u-v\right\rVert_{H}.

We set
I^{n}_{t}(v)=\int_{0}^{t}b^{n}(s,v(s))\,\text{\rm d}s
and further assume the following conditions to hold uniformly in
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

(H5)

(Time-local boundedness on
B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}(0,T;H)
) There exist
\lambda:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
\lambda(0)=0
and continuous in
0
, and
c_{4}\in[0,\infty)
such that for all
t>s\in[0,T]
and
u\in C([s,t];H)\cap B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(s,t;H)
,

\displaystyle\left[I^{n}(u)\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}(s,t;H)}^{2}\;\leqslant\;c_{4}\left(1+\lambda(\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert)\left(\left[u\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(s,t;H)}^{2}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(s,t;H)}^{2}\right)\right).

(H6)

(Continuity) There exists
\overline{\gamma}\in(1/2,\gamma]
such that for all
v_{n},v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(0,T;H)
with

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}+\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(0,T;H)}<\infty

and
v_{n}\rightarrow v\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
, implies
I^{n}(v_{n})\rightarrow I(v)\in B^{\overline{\gamma}}_{2,\infty}(0,T;H)
.

Before stating the main results, we present some examples of operators
I
satisfying Assumption
2.3
.

Remark 2.4
.

The operator
I
is a proxy for a generalized integral. By definition it is an extension of Bochner integration. Various choices are possible, e.g.:

•

(Young drivers)
I_{t}(u)\equiv Z_{t}-Z_{0}
;

•

(Young integrals)
I_{t}(u)=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{s})\,\text{\rm d}X_{s}
;

•

(Integrals of distributions)
I_{t}(u)=\int_{0}^{t}b(u_{s}-w_{s})\,\text{\rm d}s
.

For a verification of the above choices in concrete examples we refer to Sections
6
and
7
.

To increase our familiarity with Assumption
2.3
, let us discuss the regular situation at this stage:

Example 2.5
.

Let
I_{t}(u)=\int_{0}^{t}b(s,u_{s})\,\text{\rm d}s
, where
b
satisfies the conditions of
b^{n}
in Assumption
2.3
. In this situation, defining an approximate integral is trivial: set
b^{n}=b
and, thus,
I^{n}=I
. Fix
q\in(2,\infty]
and
\gamma\in(1/2+1/q,1)
.

Ad
(H5)
: The fundamental theorem and the growth condition on
b
imply

\displaystyle\left[I^{n}(u)\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}(s,t;H)}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert^{2(1-(\gamma-1/q))}\sup_{r\in[s,t]}\left\lVert b(r,u_{r})\right\rVert_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert^{2(1-(\gamma-1/q))}C^{2}(1+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(s,t;H)}^{2}).

Thus,
(H5)
holds with
\lambda(r)=r^{2(1-(\gamma-1/q))}
and
c_{4}=2C^{2}T^{2(1-(\gamma-1/q))}
.

Ad
(H6)
: Since
I^{n}=I
, the operator convergence
I^{n}\to I
is trivial. It remains to verify that strong convergence of
v_{n}\to v\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
with the additional information
v_{n},v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(0,T;H)
, implies strong convergence of
I(v_{n})\to I(v)\in B^{\overline{\gamma}}_{2,\infty}(0,T;H)
for some
\overline{\gamma}\in(1/2,\gamma]
. Fix
\ell\in(2,\infty)
and
\overline{\gamma}\in(1/2,1-1/\ell+1/q)
.

The embedding
W^{1,\ell}\hookrightarrow B^{\overline{\gamma}}_{q,\infty}
and the uniform Lipschitz-continuity of
b
show

\displaystyle\left[I(v_{n})-I(v)\right]_{B^{\overline{\gamma}}_{q,\infty}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;T^{1-1/\ell+1/q-\overline{\gamma}}\left[I(v_{n})-I(v)\right]_{W^{1,\ell}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle=T^{1-1/\ell+1/q-\overline{\gamma}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert b(s,v_{n}(s))-b(s,v(s))\right\rVert_{H}^{\ell}\,\text{\rm d}s\right)^{1/\ell}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;T^{1-1/\ell+1/q-\overline{\gamma}}C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert v_{n}(s)-v(s)\right\rVert_{H}^{\ell}\,\text{\rm d}s\right)^{1/\ell}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;T^{1-1/\ell+1/q-\overline{\gamma}}C\left(\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}\right)^{1-2/\ell}\left\lVert v_{n}-v\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}H}^{2/\ell}.

This verifies
(H6)
.

Remark 2.6
.

We want to emphasize that regular integral operators
I
, as presented in Example
2.5
, don’t need to incorporate additional information on the Besov scale
B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(0,T;H)
. This changes for more rough integrals such as Young integration.

2.2.
Main result: Existence of weak solutions

In this section we define our notion of solution and present our main result.

Definition 2.7
.

A function
u\in C([0,T];H)\cap B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{\alpha}(0,T;V)
is called weak solution to

\begin{cases}\,\text{\rm d}u_{t}+A(t,u_{t})\,\text{\rm d}t&=\,\text{\rm d}I_{t}(u),\\
\hfill u(0)&=u_{0},\end{cases}

(2.1)

if for all
t\in[0,T]
and
v\in V
the following evolution equation is satisfied:

\displaystyle\left(u_{t}-u_{0},v\right)_{H}=\int_{0}^{t}\langle A(s,u_{s}),v\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}s+\left(I_{t}(u),v\right)_{H}.

(2.2)

The following result addresses existence of weak solutions in an abstract framework. Due to its generality, it is a powerful tool for the analysis of various choices of operators.

Theorem 2.8

(Existence of weak solutions)
.

Let
T>0
and
u_{0}\in H
. Moreover, let

•

Assumption
2.1
be satisfied;

•

A
satisfy Assumption
2.2
;

•

and
I
satisfy Assumption
2.3
.

Then there exists a weak solution to
2.1
in the sense of Definition
2.7
. Moreover, this solution satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C([0,T];H)}^{2}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(0,T;H)}^{2}&+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}(0,T;V)}^{\alpha}\\
&\;\leqslant\;C\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T)}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha^{\prime}}(0,T)}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+1\right),\end{aligned}

(2.3)

for a constant
C=C(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4},\lambda,\alpha,q,\gamma,T)>0
.

We postpone the proof of Theorem
2.8
to Section
5
. However, we discuss the main steps at this point. This clarifies and motivates the new concepts needed for the existence theory of generic integral equations of the form (
2.2
).

Proof strategy and main ideas

The proof consists of
4
steps:

•

construction of approximate solutions by choosing more regular operators
I^{n}
;

•

derivation of uniform a priori estimates on
B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H\cap C_{t}H\cap L^{\alpha}V
;

•

extraction of limits using compactness;

•

identification of the limiting equation.

Replacing the abstract operator
I
by the more feasible and explicit integral
I^{n}
allows us to use standard monotone operator theory for the construction of an approximate solution
u^{n}
. A vital step towards a limit using compactness are uniform bounds on the approximate solution. In our situation, two regularity scales are of major importance:
C_{t}H\cap L^{\alpha}_{t}V
and
B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H
. Let us give the heuristic main ideas that go into two a priori bounds on these scales. In Assumption
2.2
, we will restrict ourselves here to
h=\eta=c_{2}=f=g=0
and
c_{1}=c_{3}=1
for the sake of readability.

The first regularity scale requires us to find an expansion of the squared
H
-norm, i.e. a classical energy inequality. Formally, provided
I(u)
is sufficiently regular – the precise formula is stated in Theorem
2.11
– any solution
u
to (
2.2
) satisfies

\,\text{\rm d}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H}^{2}=2\langle A(t,u),u\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}t+2\left(u,\,\text{\rm d}I_{t}(u)\right)_{H}.

(2.4)

Crucially, we need to make sense of the last term. If we assumed
I(u)\in C^{1}_{t}H
, this term would obviously make sense as

\int_{0}^{t}\left(u_{s},\,\text{\rm d}I_{s}(u)\,\text{\rm d}s\right)_{H}:=\int_{0}^{t}\left(u_{s},(\partial_{t}I(u))_{s}\right)_{H}\,\text{\rm d}s

However, we are interested in
I(u)
possessing only some weaker time regularity on
H
-scale. At this stage, the sewing lemma on Besov spaces distributes regularity requirements from the integrator
\,\text{\rm d}I(u)
to the integrand
u
; in other words,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(u_{s},\,\text{\rm d}I_{s}(u)\right)_{H}=\mathscr{S}_{t}(u,I(u))

can be constructed as a Young integral, whenever
I(u)\in C^{\gamma}H
for some
\gamma>1/2
provided we assume
u\in B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H
, cf. Theorem
4.1
. Accordingly however, the Young integral
\mathscr{S}
has to be controlled in these norms, meaning that the energy estimate formally becomes

\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{\alpha}_{L^{\alpha}_{t}V}\lesssim\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}\left\lVert I(u)\right\rVert_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H}.

(2.5)

The corresponding rigorous statement can be found in Lemma
5.3
. In order to control
I(u)
,
(H5)
appears as a natural condition. Under this condition, we are left with the task of controlling
u
on
B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H
-scale.

The Besov scale measures integrability of weighted time-differences. Locally, time-differences of the solution satisfy

\displaystyle\left\lVert\,\text{\rm d}u\right\rVert_{H}^{2}=\langle A(t,u),\,\text{\rm d}u\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}t+\left(\,\text{\rm d}u,\,\text{\rm d}I_{t}(u)\right)_{H}.

Let us for the moment assume that
A=0
. In this situation, Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality implies
\left\lVert\,\text{\rm d}u\right\rVert_{H}\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert\,\text{\rm d}I_{t}(u)\right\rVert_{H}
, which shows that
u
inherits any time-decay of
\,\text{\rm d}I(u)
. Closing the estimate requires the generalized integral operator
I
to be sufficiently regularizing, e.g., for some
\alpha>0

\displaystyle\left\lVert\,\text{\rm d}I_{t}(u)\right\rVert_{H}\;\leqslant\;\left\lvert\,\text{\rm d}t\right\rvert^{\alpha}(\left\lVert\,\text{\rm d}u\right\rVert_{H}+1).

The precise regularization condition is formulated in
(H5)
. On short time-intervals, this implies the a priori bound

\displaystyle\left\lVert\,\text{\rm d}u\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\;\leqslant\;\frac{\left\lvert\,\text{\rm d}t\right\rvert^{2\alpha}}{1-\left\lvert\,\text{\rm d}t\right\rvert^{2\alpha}}.

Therefore,
\alpha
-regularization of
I
transfers to
u
.

Going back to the general case, we need to cope with the additional difficulties imposed by the operator
A
. Cauchy–Schwarz’s and Young’s inequalities imply

\displaystyle\left\lVert\,\text{\rm d}u\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\;\leqslant\;2\langle A(t,u),\,\text{\rm d}u\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}t+\left\lVert\,\text{\rm d}I_{t}(u)\right\rVert_{H}^{2}.

Since we can’t expect any time decay of
\,\text{\rm d}u\in V
, the first term is of size
\,\text{\rm d}t
. Thus, even though
I
might be
\alpha
-regularizing for some
\alpha>1/2
,

u
will no longer inherit
\alpha
-regularity but is restricted to
\alpha\wedge 1/2
instead. Since we will always require
\alpha>1/2
in order to make sense of the chain rule in (
2.4
), the optimal regularity we might expect of
u
on
H
scale is
1/2
. More precisely, the bound we obtain in Lemma
5.2
reads in this formal setting

\displaystyle\left[u\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}_{t}V}^{\alpha}+\left[I(u)\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}.

(2.6)

A crucial step in the proof of Theorem
2.8
is the combination of estimates for
\left\lVert\,\text{\rm d}u\right\rVert_{H}^{2}
and
\,\text{\rm d}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H}^{2}
, i.e. (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) to derive stable a priori bounds, which couldn’t be closed individually. The precise statement is given in Lemma
5.4
.

Remark 2.9
.

We want to stress that the classical deterministic and stochastic situation, where
I(u)=\int b(u)\,\text{\rm d}s
and
I(u)=\int b(u)\,\text{\rm d}W(s)
for some Wiener process
W
, respectively, require the estimate on
\,\text{\rm d}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H}^{2}
only. In this situations, increased time-regularity on Besov scales can be verified a posteriori and is not part of the construction.

Obviously, the above considerations in deriving (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) are only formal and need to be made rigorous through an approximation procedure. Once a priori bounds on
u^{n}
, a solution to an approximate problem driven by
I^{n}(u^{n})
are derived uniformly in
n
, one proceeds with a compactness argument. In order to be able to pass to the limit in
I
, we naturally require some form of continuity expressed in
(H6)
. The limit identification for the remaining parts is more delicate; we need to re-weight the evolution of the squared
H
-norm in order to use the local monotonicity condition
(H2)
. This requires an application of the newly developed chain rule for Young integral equations, Theorem
4.4
, to find the evolution of (for some fixed
\phi
)

\displaystyle t\mapsto F(t,\left\lVert u_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2})=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}h_{s}+\eta(\phi_{s})\,\text{\rm d}s\right)\left\lVert u_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}.

Most importantly,
\partial_{y}F
is a sufficiently regular multiplier, so that
\mathscr{S}(\partial_{y}Fu^{n},\,\text{\rm d}I^{n}(u^{n}))
and
\mathscr{S}(\partial_{y}Fu,\,\text{\rm d}I(u))
are well-defined Young integrals.

Remark 2.10
.

The identification of the limit is considerably simpler if the local monotonicity is replaced by the stronger assumption:

(H2’)

(Monotonicity) For all
t\in[0,T]
and
u,v\in V
it holds

\displaystyle\langle A(t,u)-A(t,v),u-v\rangle_{V^{*},V}\;\leqslant\;0.

In this situation,
F(t,y)=y
and the chain rule (
4.10
) is trivial.

Sharpness of a chain rule

As mentioned above in the proof strategy for Theorem
2.8
, one crucial component consists in the establishment of a chain-rule, allowing to deduce an expression for the squared
H
-norm of any solution (
2.4
). Let us give the precise statement thereof and discuss in what sense this result is sharp when compared to the setting of cylindrical Brownian motion.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\gamma>1/2
, and
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
.
Suppose
X_{0}\in H
,
Y\in L^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*}
, and
I\in C^{\gamma}_{t}H
with
I_{0}=0
. Define the
V^{*}
-continuous element

\displaystyle X_{t}:=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}\,\text{\rm d}s+I_{t}.

(2.7)

Assume that
X\in B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H\cap L^{\infty}_{t}H\cap L^{\alpha}_{t}V
. Then
X
is a continuous
H
-valued element for which we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert X_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}=\left\lVert X_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle Y_{s},X_{s}\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}s+2\mathscr{S}_{t}(X,\,\text{\rm d}I),

(2.8)

where
\mathscr{S}_{t}(X,\,\text{\rm d}I)=\int_{0}^{t}(X_{s},\,\text{\rm d}I_{s})_{H}
denotes the Young integral constructed in Theorem
4.1
.

The proof is presented in Section
4.2
.

Remark 2.12
.

Theorem
2.11
is sharp in the following sense: Consider an
U
-cylindrical Brownian motion
B
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and
Z\in L_{2}(U,H)
. By Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem, we have
W=ZB\in C^{\gamma}_{t}H
almost surely for any
\gamma<1/2
; in particular,
W
doesn’t fullfill the conditions stated Theorem
2.11
. In this case for
X_{0}\in H
and
Y\in L^{\alpha^{\prime}}([0,T]\times\Omega,V^{*})
progressively measurable, we have for

X_{t}=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}\,\text{\rm d}s+W_{t}

by Itô’s formula (see for example
[
LR15
, Theorem 4.2.5]

\left\lVert X_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}=\left\lVert X_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle Y_{s},X_{s}\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}(X_{s},\,\text{\rm d}W_{s})_{H}+t\left\lVert Z\right\rVert_{L_{2}(U,H)}^{2}

(2.9)

where the integral
\int_{0}^{t}(X_{s},\,\text{\rm d}W_{s})_{H}
has to be understood as an infinite-dimensional Itô integral. Note in particular the second order Itô correction term not present in (
2.8
).

2.3.
Main result: Integral operators and Young regimes

Theorem
2.8
is a powerful tool towards answering Questions
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
. It identifies an abstract set of operators, i.e., operators that satisfy Assumption
2.3
, such that existence of solutions is guaranteed, partially answering Question
1.3
. In this section, we present various choices of integral operators that fall in our abstract framework:

•

additive Young drivers;

•

abstract Young integrals;

•

linear multiplicative spatially homogeneous noise;

•

averages over oscillating paths (regularization by noise).

We identify situations that give rise to Young regimes; that is, an identification of the data regularity such that both, existence and uniqueness of solutions is valid. Existence boils down to the verification of Assumption
2.3
. Uniqueness is more delicate and requires a case-by-case study. All proofs are postponed to Section
6
.

Additive Young regime

Additive drivers have one major advantage: differences of solutions to the same equation are no longer driven by the driver. This leads to the following result that answers Question
1.1
:

Theorem 2.13
.

Let Assumption
2.2
be satisfied,
\gamma>1/2
and
Z\in C^{\gamma}_{t}H
. Define
I_{t}(u)\equiv Z_{t}-Z_{0}
.

Then there exists a unique weak solution to (
2.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.7
. Moreover, any two weak solutions
u
and
v
of (
2.1
) started in
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
, respectively, satisfy for all
t\in[0,T]

\left\lVert u_{t}-v_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert u_{0}-v_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\exp{\left(\int_{0}^{t}h_{s}+\eta(u_{s})\,\text{\rm d}s\right)},

where
h
and
\eta
are given by
(H2)
.

Remark 2.14
.

If the additive driver
Z
is a stochastic process with sample paths almost surely in
C^{\gamma}_{t}H
, the above path-wise stability result establishes path-by-path uniqueness. An example is space-colored cylindrical
h
-fractional Brownian motion
W^{h}
with Hurst parameter
h>1/2
, i.e.

W^{h}_{t}=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}e_{k}\beta^{k,h}_{t}

where
(\lambda_{k})_{k}\in\ell^{2}
,
(e_{k})_{k}
forms an orthonormal basis in
H
and
(\beta^{k,h})_{k}
is a sequence of independent
h
-fractional Brownian motions. Since
W^{h}\in C^{h-}_{t}H
, Theorem
2.13
is applicable. Note that for
h=1/2
, our Theorem is not applicable. Indeed, in this case, the variational approach to SPDEs provides a well-posedness theory, see for example
[
LR15
, Theorem 4.2.4]
. Let us stress again however, that our result does not rely on any probabilistic structure of the driver
Z
.

Abstract Young integrals

An answer of Question
1.2
involves the rigorous construction of an abstract Young integral:

\displaystyle I_{t}(u)=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{s})\,\text{\rm d}X_{s}.

(2.10)

Both, time and space regularity of integrand and integrator need to be compatible for the construction of (
2.10
). We capture the spatial compatibility of the integrator by restricting to well-defined and stable pointwise multiplication:

Definition 2.15
.

A Banach space
E\subset H
is called multiplier in
H
if the bilinear multiplication

\displaystyle\cdot:H\times E

\displaystyle\to H,\hskip 30.00005pt(h,e)\mapsto h\cdot e,

is well-defined and there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
h\in H
and
e\in E
it holds
\left\lVert h\cdot e\right\rVert_{H}\;\leqslant\;C\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H}\left\lVert e\right\rVert_{E}
.

A multiplier
E
in
H
is called multiplicative ideal in
H
if
C=1
.

Example 2.16
.

An obvious example is given by
H=E=(\mathbb{R},\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert)
. Other than that, the most typical example to have in mind is
H=L^{2}(\Lambda)
and
E=L^{\infty}(\Lambda)
, where
\Lambda
is a bounded domain. Further examples can be given by means of Theorem
8.5
, for example,
H=W^{-1,2}(\Lambda)
and
E=C^{1+}(\Lambda)
.

The Banach space
E
quantifies the spatial compatibility of abstract Young integration. This, together with appropriate temporal compatibility allows us to rigorously define (
2.10
) as done in Lemma
6.1
. Again, it is a consequence of the remarkable sewing lemma on Besov spaces.

Towards an answer of Question
1.2
, we need further restrictions on the non-linearity
\sigma
and the path regularity of
X
:

Assumption 2.17
.

Let
E
be a multiplier in
H
,
\gamma>3/4
and
X\in C_{t}^{\gamma}E
. Moreover, let
\sigma:H\to H
satisfy the following:

(1)

(Linear growth) There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
u\in H

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sigma(u)\right\rVert_{H}\;\leqslant\;C(1+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H});

(2)

(Lipschitz continuity) There exists a constant
L>0
such that for all
u,v\in H

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sigma(u)-\sigma(v)\right\rVert_{H}\;\leqslant\;L\left\lVert u-v\right\rVert_{H}.

Assumption
2.17
guarantees that the Young integral is a valid integral operator in our abstract framework, which leads to a partial answer of Question
1.2
:

Theorem 2.18
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.1
,
2.2
and
2.17
are satisfied. Let
I_{t}(u)=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{s})\,\text{\rm d}X_{s}
be defined by Lemma
6.1
.

Then there exists a weak solution to (
2.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.7
.

It remains open whether Assumption
2.17
is also sufficient for uniqueness of weak solutions.

Linear multiplicative Young regime

Restricting to linear multiplicative and spatially homogeneous noise ensures uniqueness:

Theorem 2.19
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
are met and let
A
satisfy additionally
(H2’)
. Moreover, let
\gamma>3/4
,
\beta\in C^{\gamma}_{t}\mathbb{R}
and
I_{t}(u)=\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}\,\text{\rm d}\beta_{s}
be defined by Lemma
6.1
.

Then there exists a unique weak solution to (
2.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.7
. Moreover, any two weak solutions
u
and
v
of (
2.1
) started in
u_{0}
and

, respectively, satisfy for all

\left\lVert u_{t}-v_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert u_{0}-v_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\exp{(\beta_{t}-\beta_{0})}.

Remark 2.20
.

As in the previous case of additive noise, if
\beta
is a stochastic process with sample paths

for some

almost surely, then the above result implies path-by-path uniqueness. A particular example is given by an

-valued fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter

Regularization by noise

In our previous work
[

, Theorem 1.1]

) for

is a continuous path that admits a sufficiently regular local time (refer to the Appendix

Assumption 2.21
.

Let
b\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
be a Schwartz distribution,

and

be the local time of

. We assume the following to be true:

(1)

There exists

such that

(2)

For all

there exist

and constants

•

For all

•

For all

•

\left\lVert b^{n}*L^{w}-b*L^{w}\right\rVert_{C^{\gamma}(0,T;C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}))}\;\leqslant\;1/n

Theorem 2.22
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,

,

and let Assumption

be satisfied. Assume

.

Then there exists a weak solution

to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{p}u=b(u-w),\qquad u(0)=u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Lambda),

where we understand the right-hand side in the sense of Lemma

\int_{0}^{t}b(u_{r}-w_{r})=\mathscr{I}_{t}\big(b*L^{w}(\langle u\rangle)\big).

Remark 2.23
.

Compared with

we do not require a condition on

but only on

in the above assumptions. This is because in our previous work, we employed a strong formulation approach, allowing us to work on a regularity scale

Example 2.24
.

Let

2.4.
Outlook

A full identification of the Young regime for abstract Young integral equations involves establishing uniqueness of solutions. So far, we obtained uniqueness only in special cases: additive drivers and linear multiplicative spatial homogeneous noise. It remains open whether uniqueness of solutions holds in the general framework of Theorem
2.8
.

Theorem
2.18
shows that locally monotone, abstract Young integral equations are solvable if the driving path satisfies
X\in C^{3/4+}_{t}E
. In comparison, the Young regime for SDEs is
X\in C^{1/2+}
. We believe that this gap is due to technical restrictions of our method and can eventually be removed. In a similar fashion, we believe that
Z\in B^{1/2+}_{2,\infty}H
is sufficient for the well-posedness of additively driven, locally monotone equations, cf. Theorem
2.13
. More generally, we conjecture:

Conjecture 2.25
.

Let Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
be satisfied. Moreover, let
\theta\in(0,1)
and
Z\in B^{r_{\theta}+}_{\alpha_{\theta},\infty}V_{\theta}
with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{\theta}}:=\frac{1-\theta}{\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{\theta}{\alpha},\qquad r_{\theta}:=(1-\theta)\cdot 1+\theta\cdot 0,

where
\alpha
is given in
(H3)
and
V_{\theta}
is an interpolation space of
V
and
V^{*}
.

Then

\displaystyle\,\text{\rm d}u=A(t,u)\,\text{\rm d}t+\,\text{\rm d}Z,\qquad u(0)=u_{0}\in H,

is well-posed.

As already discussed in the introduction, classical monotone operator theory covers the endpoint cases
\theta\in\{0,1\}
, i.e.,
Z\in W^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*}
and
Z\in L^{\alpha}_{t}V
. Our results contribute towards the choice
\theta=1/2
.
The general case
\theta\in(0,1)\backslash\{1/2\}
requires an extension of the Besov sewing lemma to time and space; most importantly,
(u,\,\text{\rm d}Z)
needs to be well-defined and a new a priori bound on positive time regularity scale needs to be devised.

Another possible research direction would be going beyond the Young, and into the ’rough’ regime: Recall that in the SDE setting

Y_{t}=Y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(Y_{s})\,\text{\rm d}X_{s}

a pathwise solution theory can be established provided
X\in C^{\alpha}_{t}
with
\alpha>1/3
admits a so called rough path lift
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})
. Provided this lift is accordingly chosen, one is able to recover in this setting the classical Itô-theory of stochastic integration. A main advantage of this ’factorization procedure’ (lifting the path in a first probabilistic step to a rough path and performing then a purely pathwise analysis) lies in the fact that stability estimates and thus path-by-path uniqueness, the Wong-Zakai theorem and large deviations principles essentially come for free.
In the setting of locally monotone SPDEs, a natural question to address would be:

Question 2.26
.

Let Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
be satisfied. Let
Z
be a stochastic process such that almost surely
Z\in C^{\gamma}_{t}H
with
\gamma\in(1/3,1/2]
. Does there exists a rough path lift
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\mathbb{Z})
with
\mathbb{Z}\in C^{2\gamma}_{t}(H\otimes H)
, for which the problem

du=A(t,u)dt+\,\text{\rm d}\mathbf{Z},\qquad u(0)=u_{0}\in H,

is well-posed?

As in the previous conjecture, this question might be also asked for intermediate spatial regularity scales, i.e. replacing various
H
’s with some interpolation space
V_{\theta}
.

For the convenience of the reader, let us recall the Sewing Lemma in a Besov space setting due to

FSZK22

[

3.1.
The sewing lemma

Let
E
be a Banach space,
[0,T]
a given interval. Let
\Delta_{n}
denote the
n
-th simplex of
[0,T]
, i.e.
\Delta_{n}:\{(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})|0\;\leqslant\;t_{1}\dots\;\leqslant\;t_{n}\;\leqslant\;T\}
. For measurable functions
f:[0,T]\to E
and
A:\Delta_{2}\to E
we define
\delta f:\Delta_{2}\to E
and
\delta A:\Delta_{3}\to E
by

\displaystyle(\delta f)_{s,t}

\displaystyle:=f_{t}-f_{s}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ and }\hskip 20.00003pt(\delta A)_{s,u,t}:=A_{s,t}-A_{s,u}-A_{u,t},

respectively.

Given
A
and
\delta A
as above, we define for
p\in(0,\infty]
and
\tau\in(0,T]

\Omega_{p}(A,\tau):=\sup_{0\;\leqslant\;h\;\leqslant\;\tau}\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert A_{r,r+h}\right\rVert_{E}^{p}\,\text{\rm d}r\right)^{1/p},

as well as

\begin{split}\bar{\Omega}_{p}(\delta A,\tau):&=\sup_{\theta\in[0,1]}\sup_{0\;\leqslant\;h\;\leqslant\;\tau}\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert(\delta A)_{r,r+\theta h,r+h}\right\rVert_{E}^{p}\,\text{\rm d}r\right)^{1/p}.\end{split}

We further define for
\alpha>0

\displaystyle\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}([0,T],E)}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{0\;\leqslant\;\tau\;\leqslant\;T}\tau^{-\alpha}\Omega_{p}(A,\tau),

\displaystyle\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}([0,T],E)}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{0\;\leqslant\;\tau\;\leqslant\;T}\tau^{-\alpha}\bar{\Omega}_{p}(\delta A,\tau).

Notice that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}}\lesssim\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}},

(3.1)

since
\bar{\Omega}_{p}(\delta A,\tau)\;\leqslant\;3^{1/p\vee 1}\Omega_{p}(A,\tau)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}:=\{0=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\ldots<\tau_{N}=1\}
be a partition of
[0,1]
. For
(s,t)\in\Delta_{2}
we set

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{P}}A_{s,t}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{s+\tau_{i-1}(t-s),s+\tau_{i}(t-s)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{P}}A:=\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{P}}A-A.

(3.2)

Lemma 3.1

(Besov Sewing,
[
FSZK22
]
Theorem 3.1)
.

Assume that
p_{1},p_{2}\in(0,\infty]
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\gamma>1\vee 1/p_{2}
, and
A:\Delta_{2}\to E
be measurable such that

\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}([0,T],E)}+\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}([0,T],E)}<\infty.

Then there exist
\mathscr{I}A\in B^{\alpha}_{p_{1}\wedge p_{2},\infty}([0,T];E)
and
\mathscr{R}A\in\mathbb{B}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}([0,T];E)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\left\lVert\mathcal{P}\right\rVert\to 0}\left\lVert\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{P}}A-\delta\mathscr{I}A\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}}=\lim_{\left\lVert\mathcal{P}\right\rVert\to 0}\left\lVert\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{P}}A-\mathscr{R}A\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}}=0.

(3.3)

Moreover, we have the bounds

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathscr{R}A\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}([0,T],E)}\lesssim_{p_{2},\gamma}\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}([0,T],E)},

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle\left[\mathscr{I}A\right]_{B^{\alpha}_{p_{1}\wedge p_{2},\infty}([0,T];E)}\lesssim_{T,p_{1},p_{2},\gamma}\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}([0,T],E)}+T^{\gamma-\alpha}\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}([0,T],E)}.

(3.5)

3.2.
Dominated convergence

In order to perform our analysis, we need a result ensuring that the convergence
A^{n}\to A
implies (under certain additional assumptions) the convergence
\mathscr{I}A^{n}\to\mathscr{I}A
. The sewing Lemma implies that such an additional assumption could consist in the convergence
\delta A^{n}\to\delta A
. However, we can relax this condition to
(\delta A^{n})
being uniformly bounded in certain spaces (Lemma
3.3
). Towards this end, we need to first establish the following Lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose the setting of the previous Lemma
3.1
. Then, we have

\Omega_{p_{1}\wedge p_{2}}(\delta\mathscr{I}A,h)\lesssim h^{\alpha}\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}([0,T],E)}+h^{\gamma}\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}([0,T],E)}.

(3.6)

Moreover, we have for any
p\in(0,\infty]
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\Omega_{p}(\delta\mathscr{I}A,h)\;\leqslant\;m^{1\vee 1/p}\,\Omega_{p}\left(\delta\mathscr{I}A,\frac{h}{m}\right).

(3.7)

Proof.

Estimate (
3.6
) essentially follows from the proof of the Besov sewing Lemma, see
[
FSZK22
, p.190]
. Concerning (
3.7
), we have that

\begin{split}\Omega_{p}(\delta\mathscr{I}A,h)&=\sup_{0\;\leqslant\;\mu\;\leqslant\;h}\left(\int_{0}^{T-\mu}\left\lVert(\delta\mathscr{I}A)_{s,s+\mu}\right\rVert_{E}^{p}\,\text{\rm d}s\right)^{1/p}\\
&=\sup_{0\;\leqslant\;\tau\;\leqslant\;\frac{h}{m}}\left(\int_{0}^{T-m\tau}\left\lVert(\mathscr{I}A)_{s,s+m\tau}\right\rVert_{E}^{p}\,\text{\rm d}s\right)^{1/p}\end{split}

Now remark that since
(\mathscr{I}A)_{s,s+m\tau}=\mathscr{I}A_{s+m\tau}-\mathscr{I}A_{s}
, i.e. the sewing is an increment, we have

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T-m\tau}\left\lVert(\delta\mathscr{I}A)_{s,s+m\tau}\right\rVert_{E}^{p}\,\text{\rm d}s&=\int_{0}^{T-m\tau}\left\lVert\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(\mathscr{I}A)_{s+(k+1)\tau}-(\mathscr{I}A)_{s+k\tau}\right\rVert^{p}_{E}\,\text{\rm d}s\\
&\;\leqslant\;m^{(p-1)\vee 0}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\int_{0}^{T-m\tau}\left\lVert(\mathscr{I}A)_{r+(s+1)\tau}-(\mathscr{I}A)_{r+s\tau}\right\rVert^{p}_{E}\,\text{\rm d}s\\
&=m^{(p-1)\vee 0}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\int_{k\tau}^{T-(m-k)\tau}\left\lVert(\mathscr{I}A)_{s+\tau}-(\mathscr{I}A)_{s}\right\rVert^{p}_{E}\,\text{\rm d}s\\
&\;\leqslant\;m^{p\vee 1}\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left\lVert(\mathscr{I}A)_{s+\tau}-(\mathscr{I}A)_{s}\right\rVert^{p}_{E}\,\text{\rm d}s\end{split}

and thus

\begin{split}\Omega_{p}(\delta\mathscr{I}A,h)&\;\leqslant\;m^{1\vee 1/p}\sup_{0\;\leqslant\;\tau\;\leqslant\;\frac{h}{m}}\left(\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\left\lVert(\mathscr{I}A)_{s+\tau}-(\mathscr{I}A)_{s}\right\rVert^{p}_{E}\,\text{\rm d}s\right)^{1/p}\\
&=m^{1\vee 1/p}\,\Omega_{p}\left(\delta\mathscr{I}A,\frac{h}{m}\right).\end{split}

∎

Using Lemma
3.7
, we can now proceed to the following extension of Lemma A.2 in
[
Gal21
]
to the Besov setting we are concerned with.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p_{1},p_{2}\in(0,\infty]
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\gamma>1\vee 1/p_{2}
, and
A,A^{n}:\Delta_{2}\to E
be measurable and satisfy for some
R>0

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\lVert A^{n}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}E}+\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}E}+\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\lVert\delta A^{n}\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}}+\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}}\;\leqslant\;R.

Then
\left\lVert A^{n}-A\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}}\to 0
implies
\left[\mathscr{I}A-\mathscr{I}A^{n}\right]_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1}\wedge p_{2},\infty}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large, we have

\left[\mathscr{I}A-\mathscr{I}A^{n}\right]_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1}\wedge p_{2},\infty}}\lesssim(1+R)\left\lVert A^{n}-A\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma-\alpha}}.

Proof.

Since the sewing map
\mathscr{I}
is linear, assume without loss of generality that
A=0
. We will verify the assertion for
p=p_{1}\wedge p_{2}\in[1,\infty]
. The case
p\in(0,1)
follows analogously. Combining the two estimates in Lemma
3.7
, we obtain

\Omega_{p}(\delta\mathscr{I}A^{n},h)\lesssim m^{1-\alpha}h^{\alpha}\left\lVert A^{n}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}}+m^{1-\gamma}h^{\gamma}\left\lVert\delta A^{n}\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}}.

As we have by assumption

\frac{\left\lVert\delta A^{n}\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}}}{\left\lVert A^{n}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}}}\to\infty,

we (for
n
sufficiently big) choose
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m^{1-\alpha}\simeq(\left\lVert\delta A^{n}\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}}/\left\lVert A^{n}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}})^{\theta}
. By assumption, this yields the estimate

\Omega_{p}(\delta\mathscr{I}A^{n},h)\lesssim(1+R)h^{\alpha}\left\lVert A^{n}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}}^{1-\theta}+(1+R)h^{\gamma}\left\lVert A^{n}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}}^{\theta\frac{\gamma-1}{1-\alpha}}.

Choosing
\theta=(1-\alpha)/(\gamma-\alpha)
, deviding by
h^{\alpha}
and taking the supremum over
h\in[0,T]
then yields the claim.
∎

In the classical Hölder setting, it is well known that if two germs
A,A^{\prime}
give rise to two integrals
\mathscr{I}A
,
\mathscr{I}A^{\prime}
via the sewing lemma and satisfy
|A_{s,t}-A^{\prime}_{s,t}|\lesssim|t-s|^{\beta}
for
\beta>1
, then
\mathscr{I}A=\mathscr{I}A^{\prime}
. The following is a generalization of this to the Nikolskii setting. The lemma justifies the use of different local approximations that upon ’sewing’ all yield the same integral.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p_{1},p_{2}\in(0,\infty]
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\gamma>1\vee 1/p_{2}
, and
A,A^{\prime}:\Delta_{2}\to E
be measurable and satisfy

\displaystyle\left\lVert A^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}E}+\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{p_{1},\infty}E}+\left\lVert\delta A^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}}+\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}}<\infty.

Suppose furthermore that

\left\lVert A-A^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\beta}_{p_{1},\infty}([0,T],E)}<\infty

for some
\beta>1
. Then
\mathscr{I}A=\mathscr{I}A^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By (
3.6
) of Lemma
3.7
and the linearity of the sewing, we have that

\displaystyle\Omega_{p_{1}\wedge p_{2}}(\delta\mathscr{I}(A-A^{\prime}),h)

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{\beta}\left\lVert A-A^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\beta}_{p_{1},\infty}([0,T],E)}

\displaystyle+h^{\gamma}(\left\lVert\delta A\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}([0,T],E)}+\left\lVert\delta A^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma}_{p_{2},\infty}([0,T],E)}).

This readily implies

\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert\mathscr{I}(A-A^{\prime})_{r+h}-\mathscr{I}(A-A^{\prime})_{r}\right\rVert_{E}^{p_{1}\wedge p_{2}}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}\wedge p_{2}}}\lesssim h^{\beta}+h^{\gamma},

from which we conclude that the weak derivative
\partial_{t}(\mathscr{I}(A-A^{\prime}))\in L^{p_{1}\wedge p_{2}}([0,T],E)
exists and is equal to zero. As
\mathscr{I}(A-A^{\prime})_{0}=0
, this implies the claim.
∎

4.
Young integration

The sewing lemma is a powerful tool for the construction of a generalized integration theory. In this section, we define the Young integral
\mathscr{S}
that can be written heuristically as

\displaystyle(u,I)\mapsto\mathscr{S}_{t}(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)=\int_{0}^{t}\left(u_{s},\,\text{\rm d}I_{s}\right)_{H}.

(4.1)

It is a non-symmetric bi-linear operator that intertwines the integrand
u
and the integrator
I
by sewing function values of
u
and infinitesimal increments of
I
. We show that the Young integral generalises classical Bochner integration for sufficiently regular integrators and naturally occurs in the evolution equation for the squared
H
-norm, cf. Theorem
2.11
. Finally, we derive a chain rule for sufficiently smooth transformations of integral equations that involve the Young integral.

4.1.
Young integrals

The next theorem rigorously constructs (
4.1
) leveraging the sewing lemma on Besov spaces.

Theorem 4.1

(Young integral)
.

Let
\mu\in(0,\infty]
, and
p,q\in[\mu,\infty]
such that
1/\mu=1/p+1/q
.
Moreover, let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\beta>0
such that
\alpha+\beta>1\vee 1/\mu
.

There exists a bi-linear operator
\mathscr{S}:B^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}H\times B^{\beta}_{q,\infty}H\to B^{\beta}_{\mu,\infty}
called Young integral such that

\displaystyle\left[\mathscr{S}(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)\right]_{B^{\beta}_{\mu,\infty}}\lesssim_{T,\mu,\alpha,\beta}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{B^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}H}\left[I\right]_{B^{\beta}_{q,\infty}H}.

(4.2)

Proof.

Let
u\in B^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}H
and
I\in B^{\beta}_{q,\infty}H
. We construct
\mathscr{S}
as the sewing of the germ

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}\ni(s,t)\mapsto(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)_{s,t}:=\left(u_{s},I_{t}-I_{s}\right)_{H}.

(4.3)

Since we want to apply the Besov sewing lemma (Lemma
3.1
), we need to check that
(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)
is sew-able. We will show that

\displaystyle\left\lVert(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\beta}_{\mu,\infty}}+\left\lVert\delta(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\alpha+\beta}_{\mu,\infty}}<\infty.

(4.4)

By Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lvert(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)_{r,r+h}\right\rvert^{\mu}\,\text{\rm d}r\right)^{1/\mu}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{t}H}\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert I_{r+h}-I_{r}\right\rVert_{H}^{q}\,\text{\rm d}r\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{t}H}\left[I\right]_{B^{\beta}_{q,\infty}H}h^{\beta}.

This implies
\left\lVert(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\beta}_{\mu,\infty}}\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{t}H}\left[I\right]_{B^{\beta}_{q,\infty}H}
.

Similarly, using Hölder’s inequality and
\delta(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)_{s,r,t}=(u_{s}-u_{r},I_{t}-I_{r})_{H}
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}|\delta(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)_{r,r+\theta h,r+h}|^{\mu}\,\text{\rm d}r\right)^{1/\mu}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\;\leqslant\;\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert u_{r}-u_{r+\theta h}\right\rVert_{H}^{p}\,\text{\rm d}r\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert I_{r+h}-I_{r+\theta h}\right\rVert_{H}^{q}\,\text{\rm d}r\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\;\leqslant\;h^{\alpha+\beta}\left[u\right]_{B^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}H}\left[I\right]_{B^{\beta}_{q,\infty}H}.

It follows
\left\lVert\delta(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\alpha+\beta}_{\mu,\infty}}\;\leqslant\;\left[u\right]_{B^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}H}\left[I\right]_{B^{\beta}_{q,\infty}H}
.

We have verified (
4.4
) and, thus,
\mathscr{S}(u,\,\text{\rm d}I):=\mathscr{I}[(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)]\in B^{\gamma}_{\mu,\infty}
is well-defined by Lemma
3.1
. Estimate (
4.2
) follows from the stability of the sewing (
3.5
). Indeed,

\displaystyle\left[\mathscr{I}[(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)]\right]_{B^{\alpha}_{\mu,\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,\mu,\alpha,\beta}\left\lVert(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}_{\mu,\infty}}+T^{\beta}\left\lVert\delta(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\alpha+\beta}_{\mu,\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,\mu,\alpha,\beta}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{B^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}H}\left[I\right]_{B^{\beta}_{q,\infty}H}.

The bi-linearity of
\mathscr{S}
is inherited from linearity of the sewing
\mathscr{I}
, and bi-linearity of the germ
(u,I)\to(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)
.
∎

Next, we identify the Young integral as a classical Bochner integral provided the integrator
I
is sufficiently regular.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u\in L^{\infty}_{t}H\cap B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H
and
I\in W^{1,\infty}_{t}H
. Then for all
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{t}(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)=\int_{0}^{t}\left(u_{r},\partial_{t}I_{r}\right)_{H}\,\text{\rm d}r.

(4.5)

Proof.

Let
\mu\in(1,2]
and
q\in(2,\infty]
such that
1/\mu=1/2+1/q
.

Since
I\in W^{1,\infty}_{t}H\hookrightarrow B^{\gamma}_{r,\infty}H
for all
\gamma\in(0,1]
and
r\in[1,\infty]
,
u
and
I
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
with
\alpha=1/2
,
p=2
,
\beta>1/\mu
,
q=q
. Therefore, the left-hand side of (
4.5
) is well-defined in
B^{\beta}_{\mu,\infty}
. Similarly,
r\mapsto\left(u_{r},\partial_{t}I_{r}\right)_{H}\in L^{\infty}_{t}
, which makes the right-hand side of (
4.5
) well-defined. It remains to establish the identity (
4.5
).

Let
(s,t)\in\Delta_{2}
and
\mathcal{P}:=\{0=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\ldots<\tau_{N}=1\}
be a partition of
[0,1]
. The fundamental theorem implies, for
i=1,\ldots,N
,

\displaystyle\frac{I_{s+\tau_{i}(t-s)}-I_{s+\tau_{i-1}(t-s)}}{(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})(t-s)}=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{t}I_{s+\tau_{i}(t-s)+\theta((\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})(t-s))}\,\text{\rm d}\theta=:\partial_{t}I_{\mathcal{P}}^{i}(s,t).

This, (
4.3
) and (
3.2
) show

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{P}}[(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)]_{s,t}-\int_{s}^{t}\left(u_{r},\partial_{t}I_{r}\right)_{H}\,\text{\rm d}r

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{s+\tau_{i-1}(t-s)}^{s+\tau_{i}(t-s)}\left(\left(u_{s+\tau_{i-1}(t-s)},\frac{I_{s+\tau_{i}(t-s)}-I_{s+\tau_{i-1}(t-s)}}{(\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1})(t-s)}\right)_{H}-(u_{r},\partial_{t}I_{r})_{H}\right)\,\text{\rm d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\left(u_{\mathcal{P}}^{-}(r;s,t),\partial_{t}I_{\mathcal{P}}^{\mathrm{av}}(r;s,t)-\partial_{t}I_{r}\right)_{H}+\left(u_{\mathcal{P}}^{-}(r;s,t)-u_{r},\partial_{t}I_{r}\right)_{H}\,\text{\rm d}r,

where

\displaystyle u_{\mathcal{P}}^{-}(r;s,t)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}1_{(s+\tau_{i-1}(t-s),s+\tau_{i}(t-s)]}(r)u_{s+\tau_{i-1}(t-s)},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}I_{\mathcal{P}}^{\mathrm{av}}(r;s,t)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}1_{(s+\tau_{i-1}(t-s),s+\tau_{i}(t-s)]}(r)\partial_{t}I_{\mathcal{P}}^{i}(s,t),

denote the piece-wise constant interpolation of nodal values and averaged nodal values, respectively. Hölder’s inequality allows us to derive

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{P}_{l}}[(u,\,\text{\rm d}I)]_{s,t}-\int_{s}^{t}\left(u_{r},\partial_{t}I_{r}\right)_{H}\,\text{\rm d}r\right\rvert

\displaystyle\quad\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}\left\lVert\partial_{t}I_{\mathcal{P}_{l}}^{\mathrm{av}}(\cdot;s,t)-\partial_{t}I\right\rVert_{L^{1}([s,t];H)}+\left\lVert u_{\mathcal{P}_{l}}^{-}(\cdot;s,t)-u\right\rVert_{L^{1}([s,t];H)}\left\lVert\partial_{t}I\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}H}.

Similarly to
[
LR15
, Lemma 4.2.6]
there exists a sequence of partitions
\mathcal{P}_{l}
such that
\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{l}\right\rVert\to 0
and

\left\lVert u_{\mathcal{P}_{l}}^{-}(\cdot;s,t)-u\right\rVert_{L^{1}([s,t];H)}+\left\lVert\partial_{t}I_{\mathcal{P}_{l}}^{\mathrm{av}}(\cdot;s,t)-\partial_{t}I\right\rVert_{L^{1}([s,t];H)}\to 0
. This establishes the claim.
∎

Lemma
4.2
shows that Young integration is a consistent generalisation of classical Bochner integration. It relaxes the regularity conditions on the integrator, while strengthening the conditions on the integrand. Additionally, due to the identification (
4.5
) it is possible to derive new stability results for classical Bochner integrals by exploiting the stability of the Young integral.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
u\in L^{\infty}_{t}H\cap B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H
,
I\in W^{1,\infty}H
with
I_{0}=0
, and
\lambda\;\geqslant\;0
. Moreover, let
q\in(2,\infty]
,
1/\mu=1/2+1/q
and
\gamma\in(1/\mu,1)
.
Then

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda(t-s)}\left(\partial_{s}I_{s},u_{s}\right)_{H}\,\text{\rm d}s\lesssim_{\lambda,T,q,\gamma}\left(\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}+\left[u\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}\right)\left[I\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H}.

(4.6)

Proof.

Let
(s,t)\in\Delta_{2}
. First, notice that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda(t-s)}\left(\partial_{s}I_{s},u_{s}\right)_{H}\,\text{\rm d}s=e^{\lambda t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\partial_{s}J_{s},u_{s}\right)_{H}\,\text{\rm d}s+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\left(J_{s},u_{s}\right)_{H}\,\text{\rm d}s\right),

where
J_{s}:=e^{-\lambda s}I_{s}
.

Next, we show various bounds of
J
in terms of
I
; clearly,
\left\lVert J\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert I\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}
. Moreover,
J_{t}-J_{s}=e^{-\lambda s}\left((e^{-\lambda(t-s)}-1)I_{t}+I_{t}-I_{s}\right)
, which implies

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert J_{t+h}-J_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{q}\,\text{\rm d}t\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert(e^{-\lambda h}-1)I_{t+h}+I_{t+h}-I_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{q}\,\text{\rm d}t\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left\lvert e^{-\lambda h}-1\right\rvert\left\lVert I\right\rVert_{L^{q}_{t}H}+\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert I_{t+h}-I_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{q}\,\text{\rm d}t\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\lambda h\left\lVert I\right\rVert_{L^{q}_{t}H}+h^{\gamma}\left[I\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H}.

Therefore, using
I_{0}=0
and Lemma
8.6
,

\displaystyle\left[J\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H}\;\leqslant\;\left(\lambda TC_{\gamma,q}+1\right)\left[I\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H}.

(4.7)

Finally,
\partial_{s}J_{s}=-\lambda J_{s}+e^{-\lambda s}\partial_{s}I_{s}
, which shows
\left\lVert\partial_{s}J\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}H}\;\leqslant\;\lambda\left\lVert I\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}H}+\left\lVert\partial_{t}I\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}
.

We are ready to show (
4.6
); since
u\in L^{\infty}_{t}H\cap B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H
and
J\in W^{1,\infty}_{t}H
, it is possible to apply Lemma
4.2
. Notice that
\mathscr{S}_{0}(u,J)=0=J_{0}
. Moreover, recall that
B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H\hookrightarrow B^{\gamma}_{\mu,\infty}H\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}_{t}H
, cf.
[
Sim90
, Theorem 16]
. This and the stability of the Young integral (
4.2
) imply

\displaystyle e^{\lambda t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\partial_{s}J_{s},u_{s}\right)_{H}\,\text{\rm d}s+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\left(J_{s},u_{s}\right)_{H}\,\text{\rm d}s\right)

\displaystyle\quad=e^{\lambda t}\left(\mathscr{S}_{t}(u,\,\text{\rm d}J)+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\left(J_{s},u_{s}\right)_{H}\,\text{\rm d}s\right)

\displaystyle\quad\;\leqslant\;e^{\lambda T}\left(\left\lVert\mathscr{S}(u,\,\text{\rm d}J)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}}+\lambda T\left\lVert J\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\left[\mathscr{S}(u,\,\text{\rm d}J)\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{\mu,\infty}}+\left[J\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\left(\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}+\left[u\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}\right)\left[J\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H}.

Assertion (
4.6
) follows using (
4.7
), which completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Proof of an import formula

In this section we prove the formula for the squared
H
-norm of solutions to generic integral equations.

Proof of Theorem
2.11
.

The proof consists of three steps:

(1)

X
is uniformly continuous in time with respect to the weak topology in
H
;

(2)

X
satisfies the equation (
2.8
);

(3)

X
is continuous in time with respect to the strong topology in
H
.

Step
(1)
:
Let
\varepsilon>0
and
h\in H
. We need to show that there exists
\delta>0
such that
\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert\;\leqslant\;\delta
implies
\left\lvert\left(X_{t}-X_{s},h\right)_{H}\right\rvert\;\leqslant\;\varepsilon.

Let us start with an estimate of
(X_{t},v)
for
v\in V
. Since
V\hookrightarrow H
continuously and densely, and (
2.7
)

\displaystyle\left(X_{t}-X_{s},v\right)_{H}

\displaystyle=\langle X_{t}-X_{s},v\rangle_{V^{*},V}

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\langle Y_{r},v\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}r+\left(I_{t}-I_{s},v\right)_{H}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left(\int_{s}^{t}\left\lVert Y_{r}\right\rVert_{V^{*}}\,\text{\rm d}r+\left\lVert I_{t}-I_{s}\right\rVert_{H}\right)C\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{V}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert^{\beta}C\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{V},

where
\beta=1/\alpha\wedge\gamma
.

Using the density of
V\subset H
there exists
h_{n}\in V
such that
\left\lVert h_{n}\right\rVert_{V}\;\leqslant\;n
and
\left\lVert h-h_{n}\right\rVert_{H}\;\leqslant\;n^{-1}\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}H}^{-1}
. This allows us to conclude

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left(X_{t}-X_{s},h\right)_{H}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left\lvert\left(X_{t}-X_{s},h-h_{n}\right)_{H}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\left(X_{t}-X_{s},h_{n}\right)_{H}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2/n+\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert^{\beta}Cn.

Now, we first choose
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n\;\geqslant\;4/\varepsilon
; secondly, we choose
\delta>0
such that
\delta^{\beta}Cn\;\leqslant\;\varepsilon/2
. This implies the uniform continuity with respect to the weak topology in
H
.

Step
(2)
:

Let
\mathcal{J}=\{t\in[0,T]:\,X_{t}\in V\}
be the set of regular times of
X
. Since
X\in L^{\alpha}_{t}V
, the complement
\mathcal{N}=[0,T]\backslash\mathcal{J}
is a null set. Let
t,s\in\mathcal{J}
, consider increments of (
2.7
), and choose
v=X_{t}+X_{s}\in V
, yielding

\displaystyle\left\lVert X_{t}\right\rVert^{2}_{H}-\left\lVert X_{s}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=(X_{t}-X_{s},X_{t}+X_{s})_{H}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{s}^{t}\langle Y_{r},X_{t}+X_{s}\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}r}_{=:\mathrm{I}_{s,t}}+\underbrace{(I_{t}-I_{s},X_{t}+X_{s})_{H}}_{=:\mathrm{II}_{s,t}}.

Let
\mathcal{P}:=\{0=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\ldots<\tau_{N}=1\}
be a partition of
[0,1]
.

We start with
\mathrm{I}
: clearly,

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{s,t}-2\int_{s}^{t}\langle Y_{r},X_{r}\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}r=\underbrace{\int_{s}^{t}\langle Y_{r},X_{t}-X_{r}\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}r}_{\mathrm{R}^{1}_{s,t}}+\underbrace{\int_{s}^{t}\langle Y_{r},X_{s}-X_{r}\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}r}_{\mathrm{R}^{2}_{s,t}}.

An application of the sewing (
3.2
) implies

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{P}}\mathrm{R}^{1}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\langle Y_{r},X_{\mathcal{P}}^{+}(r;s,t)-X_{r}\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}r,

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{P}}\mathrm{R}^{2}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\langle Y_{r},X_{\mathcal{P}}^{-}(r;s,t)-X_{r}\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}r,

where
X_{\mathcal{P}}^{+}(r;s,t):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}1_{(s+\tau_{i-1}(t-s),s+\tau_{i}(t-s)]}(r)X_{s+\tau_{i}(t-s)}
, respectively,
X_{\mathcal{P}}^{-}(r;s,t):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}1_{(s+\tau_{i-1}(t-s),s+\tau_{i}(t-s)]}(r)X_{s+\tau_{i-1}(t-s)}
denote the piece-wise constant interpolation of the nodal values to the left, respectively, right interval. Similarly to
[
LR15
, Lemma 4.2.6]
there exists a sequence of partitions
\mathcal{P}_{l}
such that
\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{l}\right\rVert\to 0
and

\left\lVert X_{\mathcal{P}_{l}}^{+}(\cdot;s,t)-X\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}([s,t];V)}+\left\lVert X_{\mathcal{P}_{l}}^{-}(\cdot;s,t)-X\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}([s,t];V)}\to 0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\lvert(\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{P}_{l}}\mathrm{I})_{s,t}-2\int_{s}^{t}\langle Y_{r},X_{r}\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}r\right\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert Y\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*}}\left(\left\lVert X_{\mathcal{P}_{l}}^{+}(\cdot;s,t)-X\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}([s,t];V)}+\left\lVert X_{\mathcal{P}_{l}}^{-}(\cdot;s,t)-X\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}([s,t];V)}\right),

which implies
\lim_{\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{l}\right\rVert\to 0}(\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{P}_{l}}\mathrm{I})_{s,t}
exists and equals
2\int_{s}^{t}\langle Y_{r},X_{r}\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}r
.

Next, we investigate
\mathrm{II}
: a trivial expansion shows

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}_{s,t}-2(I_{t}-I_{s},X_{s})_{H}=(I_{t}-I_{s},X_{t}-X_{s})_{H}=:\mathrm{K}_{s,t}.

Due to the regularity assumption
I\in C^{\gamma}_{t}H
and
X\in B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H\cap L^{\infty}_{t}H
, we can apply Theorem
4.1
. This ensures the existence of
\lim_{\left\lVert\mathcal{P}\right\rVert\to 0}\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{P}}[(X,\,\text{\rm d}I)]=\mathscr{S}(X,\,\text{\rm d}I)
. It remains to show that
\mathrm{K}
is sew-able and decays sufficiently fast for
\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert\ll 1
, which implies that its sewing vanishes.

Let
h\in(0,T)
. Hölder’s inequality implies

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lvert\mathrm{K}_{t,t+h}\right\rvert^{2}\,\text{\rm d}t\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;h^{\gamma+1/2}\left[I\right]_{C^{\gamma}_{t}H}\left[X\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}.

This together with (
3.1
) ensure

\displaystyle\left\lVert\delta\mathrm{K}\right\rVert_{\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\gamma+1/2}_{2,\infty}}+\left\lVert\mathrm{K}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\gamma+1/2}_{2,\infty}}\lesssim\left[I\right]_{C^{\gamma}_{t}H}\left[X\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}.

Therefore, Lemma
3.4
implies
\lim_{\left\lVert\mathcal{P}\right\rVert\to 0}\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{P}}\mathrm{K}=0
.

In total, we have verified that (
2.8
) holds for all
t,s\in\mathcal{J}
. Next, we argue that (
2.8
) holds for all
[t,s]\in[0,T]
. Notice that

\displaystyle t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}\langle Y_{r},X_{r}\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}r\in W^{1,1}_{t}\hookrightarrow C_{t},

and

\displaystyle t\mapsto\mathscr{S}_{t}(X,\,\text{\rm d}I)\in B^{\gamma}_{2,\infty}\hookrightarrow C^{\gamma-1/2}_{t}.

Thus, the right-hand side of (
2.8
) is continuous in time, so must be its left-hand side.

Step
(3)
:
It remains to show the continuity of
X
in the strong topology of
H
. Let
t\in[0,T]
and
\varepsilon>0
. Due to (
2.8
), the map
t\mapsto\left\lVert X_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}
is continuous, i.e., there exists
\delta>0
such that
\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert\;\leqslant\;\delta
implies
\left\lvert\left\lVert X_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}-\left\lVert X_{s}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\right\rvert\;\leqslant\;\varepsilon
. Additionally, by Step
(1)
,
X
is uniformly continuous with respect to the weak topology, i.e., for all
h\in H
(in particular
h=X_{t}
) there exists
\widetilde{\delta}>0
such that
\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert\;\leqslant\;\widetilde{\delta}
implies
\left\lvert(X_{t}-X_{s},h)_{H}\right\rvert\;\leqslant\;\varepsilon
. Choosing
\overline{\delta}=\delta\wedge\widetilde{\delta}
and
s\in[0,T]
such that
\left\lvert s-t\right\rvert\;\leqslant\;\overline{\delta}
guarantee

\displaystyle\left\lVert X_{t}-X_{s}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}=\left\lVert X_{s}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}-\left\lVert X_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\left(X_{t}-X_{s},X_{t}\right)_{H}\;\leqslant\;2\varepsilon.

Thus, we have verified
(3)
.
∎

4.3.
Chain rule for Young integral equations

In order to cope with locally monotone operators, it is crucial to have access to a chain rule for Young integral equations.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
q\in(2,\infty]
,
\gamma>1/2+1/q
, and
1/\mu=1/2+1/q
. Moreover, let
b\in L^{1}_{t}
,
u\in L^{\infty}_{t}H\cap B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H
,
I\in B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H
, and

\displaystyle\forall t\in[0,T]:\quad y_{t}=y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b_{s}\,\text{\rm d}s+\mathscr{S}_{t}(u,\,\text{\rm d}I).

(4.8)

Let
F\in W^{1,1}\big([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\big)
satisfy the following: for all
R>0
there exists
C_{R}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{z\in B_{R}}\left\lvert\partial_{t}F(t,z)\right\rvert\,\text{\rm d}t\;\leqslant\;C_{R},

(4.9a)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{z\in B_{R}}\left\lvert\partial_{y}F(t,z)\right\rvert\;\leqslant\;C_{R},

(4.9b)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{z_{1},z_{2}\in B_{R}}\frac{\left\lvert\partial_{y}F(t,z_{1})-\partial_{y}F(t,z_{2})\right\rvert}{\left\lvert z_{1}-z_{2}\right\rvert}\;\leqslant\;C_{R},

(4.9c)

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in[0,T]}h^{-1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\sup_{z\in B_{R}}\left\lvert\partial_{y}F(t+h,z)-\partial_{y}F(t,z)\right\rvert^{2}\,\text{\rm d}t\right)^{1/2}\;\leqslant\;C_{R}.

(4.9d)

Then the following chain rule is satisfied

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \forall t\in[0,T]:\quad&F(t,y_{t})=F(0,y_{0})\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}F(s,y_{s})\,\text{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{y}F(s,y_{s})b_{s}\,\text{\rm d}s+\mathscr{S}_{t}(\partial_{y}F(y)u,\,\text{\rm d}I),\end{aligned}

(4.10)

where
t\mapsto\mathscr{S}_{t}(\partial_{y}F(y)u,\,\text{\rm d}I)\in B^{\gamma}_{\mu,\infty}
denotes the Young integral constructed in Theorem
4.1
.

Proof.

The proof consists of three steps:

(1)

the convex hull of
y([0,T])
is contained in a ball, which allows us to use the local assumption (
4.9
) on
F
;

(2)

increments of
t\mapsto F(t,y_{t})
poses an alternative representation using a finite partition
\mathcal{P}
;

(3)

terms in the new representation are identified for partitions with vanishing mesh-size.

Step
(1)
:
First, we notice that
t\mapsto y_{t}
is continuous as the right-hand side of (
4.8
) is continuous. Moreover, using
B^{\gamma}_{\mu,\infty}\hookrightarrow C^{\gamma-1/\mu}
and (
4.2

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lvert y_{t}\right\rvert

5.1
) in time, which leads us to the equivalent differential equation

\displaystyle\forall t\in(0,T)\backslash\mathcal{N},v\in V:\quad\langle\partial_{t}u_{t}-A(t,u_{t}),v\rangle_{V^{*},V}=\left(b^{n}(t,u_{t}),v\right)_{H},

(5.2)

where
\mathcal{N}\subset(0,T)
is a nullset.

The existence of weak solutions to monotone differential equations is a standard result and can be found e.g. in
[
Zei90
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists a unique weak solution
u^{n}\in W^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*}\cap C_{t}H\cap L^{\alpha}_{t}V
to (
5.2
). Moreover,
u^{n}
solves (
5.2
) if and only if
u^{n}
solves (
5.1
).

5.2.
New a priori bounds

Next, we will derive new a priori estimates leveraging the mapping properties of the approximate integral operators. Most importantly, since we don’t expect an asymptotically stable time derivative in
V^{*}
, we avoid using estimates that invoke the time derivative. Instead, we use estimates on the fractional Besov space
B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H
. This space can be thought of as a
1/2
-interpolation space in between
W^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*}
and
L^{\alpha}_{t}V
.

We start by deriving two estimates for approximate weak solutions: the first addresses fractional time-regularity on
H
, and the second provides an estimate on the natural energy space
C_{t}H\cap L^{\alpha}_{t}V
.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists a constant
C_{c_{3},\alpha}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}\;\leqslant\;C_{c_{3},\alpha}\left(\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}_{t}V}^{\alpha}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L_{t}^{\alpha^{\prime}}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\right)+\left[I^{n}(u^{n})\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}.

(5.3)

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{J}=\{t\in[0,T]:\,u^{n}(t)\in V\}
. Fix
h\in(0,T)\cap\mathcal{J}
and
t\in(0,T-h)\cap\mathcal{J}
, and subtract (
5.1
) for
t^{\prime}\in\{t+h,t\}
,

\displaystyle\left(u^{n}_{t+h}-u^{n}_{t},v\right)_{H}=\int_{t}^{t+h}\langle A(s,u^{n}_{s}),v\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}s+\left(I_{t+h}^{n}(u^{n})-I_{t}^{n}(u^{n}),v\right)_{H}.

Choosing
v=u^{n}_{t+h}-u^{n}_{t}\in V
, and using Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u^{n}_{t+h}-u^{n}_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\;\leqslant\;\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert I_{t+h}^{n}(u^{n})-I_{t}^{n}(u^{n})\right\rVert_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{t}^{t+h}\left\lVert A(s,u^{n}_{s})\right\rVert_{V^{*}}^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\,\text{\rm d}s+\frac{h}{\alpha}\left\lVert u^{n}_{t+h}-u^{n}_{t}\right\rVert_{V}^{\alpha}.

Next, we integrate over
t\in(0,T-h)\cap\mathcal{J}
and use Fubini’s theorem

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert u^{n}_{t+h}-u^{n}_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}t\;\leqslant\;\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert I_{t+h}^{n}(u^{n})-I_{t}^{n}(u^{n})\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+h\left(\frac{2}{\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert A(t,u^{n}_{t})\right\rVert_{V^{*}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\,\text{\rm d}t+\frac{2}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert u^{n}_{t+h}-u^{n}_{t}\right\rVert_{V}^{\alpha}\,\text{\rm d}t\right).

Re-scaling by
h^{-1}
, taking the supremum over
h\in(0,T)
, and using
(H4)
show

\displaystyle\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left[I^{n}(u^{n})\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}+\left(\frac{(2c_{3})^{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{2^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\right)\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert u^{n}_{t}\right\rVert^{\alpha}_{V}\,\text{\rm d}t+\frac{2^{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lvert g_{t}\right\rvert^{\alpha^{\prime}}\,\text{\rm d}t.

This establishes the estimate (
5.3
).
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

There exists a constant
C_{c_{1},c_{2},T,q,\gamma}>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}^{2}&+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}_{t}V}^{\alpha}\;\leqslant\;C_{c_{1},c_{2},T,q,\gamma}\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{t}}\right)\\
&+C_{c_{1},c_{2},T,q,\gamma}\left(\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}+\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}\right)\left[I^{n}(u^{n})\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H}.\end{aligned}

(5.4)

Proof.

Let
t\in[0,T]\backslash\mathcal{N}\cap\mathcal{J}
, where
\mathcal{J}=\{t\in[0,T]:\,u^{n}(t)\in V\}
and
\mathcal{N}
is defined in (
5.2
). Choose
v=u^{n}_{t}\in V
in (
5.2
) to find

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}u^{n}_{t},u^{n}_{t}\rangle_{V^{*},V}-\langle A(t,u^{n}_{t}),u^{n}_{t}\rangle_{V^{*},V}=\left(b^{n}(t,u^{n}_{t}),u^{n}_{t}\right)_{H}.

(5.5)

Since
V\hookrightarrow H
continuously and densely, it holds

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}u^{n}_{t},u^{n}_{t}\rangle_{V^{*},V}=\partial_{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u^{n}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}_{H}\right).

Invoking
(H3)
,

\displaystyle-\langle A(t,u^{n}_{t}),u^{n}_{t}\rangle_{V^{*},V}\;\geqslant\;c_{1}\left\lVert u^{n}_{t}\right\rVert_{V}^{\alpha}-c_{2}\left\lVert u^{n}_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}-f_{t}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\lVert u^{n}_{t}\right\rVert^{2}_{H}\;\leqslant\;2c_{2}\left\lVert u^{n}_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\left(\left(b^{n}(t,u^{n}_{t}),u^{n}_{t}\right)_{H}+f_{t}\right),

and Gronwall’s inequality implies for all
t^{\prime}\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{n}(t^{\prime})\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\;\leqslant\;e^{2c_{2}t^{\prime}}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}e^{2c_{2}(t^{\prime}-t)}\left(\left(b^{n}(t,u^{n}_{t}),u^{n}_{t}\right)_{H}+f_{t}\right)\,\text{\rm d}t.

(5.6)

Moreover, integrating (
5.5
) over
t\in[0,t^{\prime}]\backslash\mathcal{N}\cap\mathcal{J}
and using (
5.6
), allow us to show

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{n}(t^{\prime})\right\rVert_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle+2c_{1}\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\left\lVert u^{n}_{t}\right\rVert_{V}^{\alpha}\,\text{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\left(b^{n}(t,u^{n}_{t}),u^{n}_{t}\right)_{H}+c_{2}\left\lVert u^{n}_{t}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+f_{t}\,\text{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\left(b^{n}(t,u^{n}_{t}),u^{n}_{t}\right)_{H}+f_{t}\,\text{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\hskip-10.00002pt+\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}2c_{2}\left\{e^{2c_{2}t}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}e^{2c_{2}(t-s)}\left(\left(b^{n}(s,u^{n}_{s}),u^{n}_{s}\right)_{H}+f_{s}\right)\,\text{\rm d}s\right\}\,\text{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=e^{2c_{2}t^{\prime}}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}e^{2c_{2}(t^{\prime}-t)}\left(\left(b^{n}(t,u^{n}_{t}),u^{n}_{t}\right)_{H}+f_{t}\right)\,\text{\rm d}t,

where we used that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}2c_{2}e^{2c_{2}t}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}t=(e^{2c_{2}t^{\prime}}-1)\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}2c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2c_{2}(t-s)}\left(\left(b^{n}(s,u^{n}_{s}),u^{n}_{s}\right)_{H}+f_{s}\right)\,\text{\rm d}s\,\text{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\left(e^{2c_{2}(t^{\prime}-t)}-1\right)\left(\left(b^{n}(t,u^{n}_{t}),u^{n}_{t}\right)_{H}+f_{t}\right)\,\text{\rm d}t.

Recall that
b^{n}(s,u^{n}_{s})=\partial_{s}I^{n}_{s}(u^{n})\in L^{\infty}_{t}H
and
u^{n}\in W^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*}\cap L^{\alpha}_{t}V\hookrightarrow B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H
. Therefore, we can apply Corollary
4.3
with
\lambda=2c_{2}
to find a constant
C_{c_{2},T,q,\gamma}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t^{\prime}\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}e^{2c_{2}(t^{\prime}-t)}\left(b^{n}(t,u^{n}_{t}),u^{n}_{t}\right)_{H}\hskip-2.0pt\,\text{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt\;\leqslant\;C_{c_{2},T,q,\gamma}\left(\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}+\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}\right)\left[I^{n}(u^{n})\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H}.

We arrive at

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{n}(t^{\prime})\right\rVert_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle+2c_{1}\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\left\lVert u^{n}_{t}\right\rVert_{V}^{\alpha}\,\text{\rm d}t\;\leqslant\;e^{2c_{2}T}\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{t}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+2C_{c_{2},T,q,\gamma}\left(\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}+\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}\right)\left[I^{n}(u^{n})\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H}.

The assertion (
5.4
) follows by taking the supremum over
t^{\prime}\in[0,T]
.
∎

So far, neither (
5.3
) nor (
5.4
) are closed estimates for
u^{n}
as both sides of the inequalities depend on the approximate weak solution. Next, we will use the quantified mapping properties of the approximate integral operators
I^{n}
, cf.
(H5)
, to close the argument.

Lemma 5.4
.

There exists a constant
C=C(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4},\lambda,\alpha,q,\gamma,T)>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}^{2}+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}_{t}V}^{\alpha}+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}\;\leqslant\;C\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{t}}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L_{t}^{\alpha^{\prime}}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+1\right).

(5.7)

Proof.

The proof consists of six steps:

(1)

a preliminary estimate;

(2)

setup of induction;

(3)

start of induction;

(4)

induction hypothesis;

(5)

induction step;

(6)

gluing of local norms to global ones.

Step
(1)
:
First we use Lemma
5.2
and
5.3
to estimate

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}^{2}+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}_{t}V}^{\alpha}+\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}^{2}+(1+C_{c_{3},\alpha})\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}_{t}V}^{\alpha}+C_{c_{3},\alpha}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L_{t}^{\alpha^{\prime}}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+\left[I^{n}(u^{n})\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;(1+C_{c_{3},\alpha})\left(\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}^{2}+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}_{t}V}^{\alpha}\right)+C_{c_{3},\alpha}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L_{t}^{\alpha^{\prime}}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+\left[I^{n}(u^{n})\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;(1+C_{c_{3},\alpha})C_{c_{1},c_{2},T,q,\gamma}\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{t}}\right)+C_{c_{3},\alpha}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L_{t}^{\alpha^{\prime}}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+\left[I^{n}(u^{n})\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(1+C_{c_{3},\alpha})C_{c_{1},c_{2},T,q,\gamma}\left(\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}+\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}\right)\left[I^{n}(u^{n})\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H}.

Young’s inequality implies

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}^{2}&+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}_{t}V}^{\alpha}+\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}\\
&\;\leqslant\;2(1+C_{c_{3},\alpha})C_{c_{1},c_{2},T,q,\gamma}\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{t}}\right)+2C_{c_{3},\alpha}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L_{t}^{\alpha^{\prime}}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\\
&\quad+(1+C_{c_{3},\alpha})^{2}(1+C_{c_{1},c_{2},T,q,\gamma})^{2}\left[I^{n}(u^{n})\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H}^{2}.\end{aligned}

(5.8)

Finally, using
(H5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}^{2}&+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}_{t}V}^{\alpha}+\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}\\
&\;\leqslant\;\mathrm{K}_{1}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+\mathrm{K}_{2}+\mathrm{K}_{3}+\mathrm{K}_{3}\lambda(T)\left\{\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}^{2}\right\},\end{aligned}

(5.9)

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{1}

\displaystyle:=2(1+C_{c_{3},\alpha})C_{c_{1},c_{2},T,q,\gamma},

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{2}

\displaystyle:=4(1+C_{c_{3},\alpha})C_{c_{1},c_{2},T,q,\gamma}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{t}}+2C_{c_{3},\alpha}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L_{t}^{\alpha^{\prime}}}^{\alpha^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{3}

\displaystyle:=(1+C_{c_{3},\alpha})^{2}(1+C_{c_{1},c_{2},T,q,\gamma})^{2}c_{4}.

Step
(2)
– Induction setup:
Define

\displaystyle T^{*}=\inf\{t\in[0,T]:\lambda(t)\;\geqslant\;(2\mathrm{K}_{3})^{-1}\},

(5.10)

where we set
T^{*}=T
if
\lambda(t)<(2\mathrm{K}_{3})^{-1}
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Since
t\mapsto\lambda(t)
is continuous in
0
and
\lambda(0)=0
, it holds
T^{*}>0
. We define the ratio between
T
and
T^{*}
as
r=\lceil T/T^{*}\rceil
, the
k
-th interval as
J_{k}:=[(k-1)T^{*},kT^{*})\cap[0,T]
, and the partition of
[0,T)
with mesh-size
T^{*}
by
\mathcal{P}=\{J_{k}:k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\}
.

Step
(3)
– Induction start:
: Clearly,
u^{n}
solves (
5.1
) on
[0,T^{*})
. Thus, (
5.9
) holds for
T
replaced by
T^{*}
. In particular,
\mathrm{K}_{3}\lambda(T^{*})\;\leqslant\;1/2
by definition of
T^{*}
. We infer

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(J_{1};H)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}(J_{1};V)}^{\alpha}+\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(J_{1};H)}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathrm{K}_{1}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+\mathrm{K}_{2}+\mathrm{K}_{3}+1/2\left\{\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(J_{1};H)}^{2}+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(J_{1};H)}^{2}\right\},

which shows

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(J_{1};H)}^{2}+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}(J_{1};V)}^{\alpha}+\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(J_{1};H)}^{2}\;\leqslant\;2\mathrm{K}_{1}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}.

(5.11)

Step
(4)
– Induction hypothesis:

We assume that for all
\widetilde{k}\;\leqslant\;k-1
it holds

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(J_{\widetilde{k}};H)}^{2}&+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}(J_{\widetilde{k}};V)}^{\alpha}+\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(J_{\widetilde{k}};H)}^{2}\\
&\;\leqslant\;(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{\widetilde{k}}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+\left(2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}\right)\sum_{l=0}^{\widetilde{k}-1}(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{l}.\end{aligned}

(5.12)

Step
(5)
– Induction step:

Notice that
u^{n}
solves for
t\in J_{k}

\displaystyle\left(u_{t}^{n}-u_{(k-1)T^{*}}^{n},v\right)_{H}=\int_{(k-1)T^{*}}^{t}\langle A(s,u_{s}^{n}),v\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}s+\left(\overline{I}_{t}^{k-1,n}(u^{n}),v\right)_{H},

where
\overline{I}_{t}^{k-1,n}(u^{n}):=I_{t}^{n}(u^{n})-I_{(k-1)T^{*}}^{n}(u^{n})
. Similarly to Step
(1)
, we derive

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(J_{k};H)}^{2}&+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}(J_{k};V)}^{\alpha}+\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(J_{k};H)}^{2}\\
&\;\leqslant\;\mathrm{K}_{1}\left\lVert u_{(k-1)T^{*}}^{n}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+\mathrm{K}_{2}\\
&\quad+(1+C_{c_{3},\alpha})^{2}(1+C_{c_{1},c_{2},T^{*},q,\gamma})^{2}\left[\overline{I}^{k-1,n}(u^{n})\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}(J_{k};H)}^{2}.\end{aligned}

(5.13)

Observe that for fixed
h\;\leqslant\;T^{*}

\displaystyle\int_{(k-1)T^{*}}^{kT^{*}-h}\left\lVert\overline{I}_{t+h}^{k-1,n}(u^{n})-\overline{I}_{t}^{k-1,n}(u^{n})\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}t=\int_{(k-1)T^{*}}^{kT^{*}-h}\left\lVert I_{t+h}^{n}(u^{n})-I_{t}^{n}(u^{n})\right\rVert_{H}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}t,

which implies
\left[\overline{I}^{k-1,n}(u^{n})\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}(J_{k};H)}=\left[I^{n}(u^{n})\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}(J_{k};H)}
. Therefore, using
(H5)
and
\mathrm{K}_{3}\lambda(T^{*})\;\leqslant\;1/2
in (
5.13
) establish

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(J_{k};H)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}(J_{k};V)}^{\alpha}+\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(J_{k};H)}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathrm{K}_{1}\left\lVert u_{(k-1)T^{*}}^{n}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+\mathrm{K}_{2}+\mathrm{K}_{3}+1/2\left\{\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(J_{k};H)}^{2}+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(J_{k};H)}^{2}\right\}.

Reordering and using the induction hypothesis (
5.12
) provide

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(J_{k};H)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}(J_{k};V)}^{\alpha}+\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(J_{k};H)}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2\mathrm{K}_{1}\left\lVert u_{(k-1)T^{*}}^{n}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2\mathrm{K}_{1}\left((2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{k-1}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+\left(2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}\right)\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{l}\right)+2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}

\displaystyle=(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{k}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+\left(2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}\right)\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{l}.

Thus, we have proved that (
5.12
) remains valid for
\widetilde{k}=k
.

Step
(6)
– Gluing:
The induction verifies a uniform bound for localized norms. It remains to estimate the global norms in terms of local ones. We discuss each term on the left-hand side of (
5.7
) individually.

L^{\infty}([0,T];H)
: Clearly, using (
5.13
)

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H)}^{2}=\max_{k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}}\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(J_{k};H)}^{2}\;\leqslant\;(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{r}\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}\right).

L^{\alpha}([0,T];V)
: Similarly,

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}([0,T];V)}^{\alpha}=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}(J_{k};V)}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{r}(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{k}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{r+1}\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}\right).

B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}([0,T];H)
: Fractional Besov spaces can’t be localised easily, i.e.,

\displaystyle u^{n}\in\bigcap_{k=1}^{r}B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(J_{k};H)\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad u^{n}\in B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}([0,T];H).

However, we have the additional information
u^{n}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H)
. This is sufficient for a localisation of the Nikolskii norm; indeed, Lemma
8.7
and (
5.13
) imply

\displaystyle\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}([0,T];H)}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left[u^{n}\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(J_{k};H)}^{2}+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H)}^{2}2(2r-1)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left\{(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{k}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+\left(2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}\right)\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{l}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{r}\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}\right)2(2r-1)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{r}\left(2\mathrm{K}_{1}+2(2r-1)\right)\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}\right).

Observe that

\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}\;\leqslant\;T\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H)}^{2}\;\leqslant\;T(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{r}\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}([0,T];H)}^{2}\;\leqslant\;(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{r}\left(2\mathrm{K}_{1}+2(2r-1)+T\right)\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}\right).

All together, we find

\displaystyle\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}_{t}V}^{\alpha}+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;3(2\mathrm{K}_{1})^{r}\left(2\mathrm{K}_{1}+2(2r-1)+T\right)\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H}^{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{2}+2\mathrm{K}_{3}\right),

and the proof is complete.
∎

5.3.
A limit is born

The Banach–Alaoglu theorem states that

\displaystyle B_{X^{*}}:=\left\{f\in X^{*}:\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X^{*}}\;\leqslant\;R\right\}

is relatively compact with respect to weak-star convergence for arbitrary Banach spaces
X
and constants
R>0
. We aim to use this fact for the construction of limits.

Lemma 5.5
.

There exist
u\in L^{\infty}_{t}H\cap B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H\cap L^{\alpha}_{t}V
,
\overline{A}\in L^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*}
, and a sub-sequence (not relabeled) such that

\displaystyle u^{n}\rightharpoonup u

\displaystyle\in L^{\alpha}_{t}V,

(5.14a)

\displaystyle u^{n}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}u

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}_{t}H,

(5.14b)

\displaystyle u^{n}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}u

\displaystyle\in B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H,

(5.14c)

\displaystyle u^{n}\rightarrow u

\displaystyle\in L^{2}_{t}H,

(5.14d)

\displaystyle A(\cdot,u^{n})\rightharpoonup\overline{A}

\displaystyle\in L^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*}.

(5.14e)

Proof.

First, since
V
is a reflexive Banach space, we identify that
(L^{\alpha}_{t}V)^{*}=L^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*}
, and
(L^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*})^{*}=L^{\alpha}_{t}V^{**}=L^{\alpha}_{t}V
. Moreover,
L^{\infty}_{t}H=(L^{1}_{t}H)^{*}
and
B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H=(\widetilde{B}^{-1/2}_{2,1}H)^{*}
. The definition of Besov spaces with respect to integrability of difference quotients doesn’t immediately transfer to negative derivatives. However, for positive differentiability,
\gamma>0
, there exists an alternative spectral representation, i.e.,
B^{\gamma}_{2,\infty}=\widetilde{B}^{\gamma}_{2,\infty}
with equivalent norms. This spectral definition canonically extends to
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, one has the identification
\widetilde{B}^{\gamma}_{2,\infty}=(\widetilde{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,1})^{*}
. More details can be found in
[
Wic24
, Appendix A.1]
.

We start by verifying (
5.14a
): Choose
X=L^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*}
and define

\displaystyle f\mapsto\iota_{v}(f):=\int_{0}^{T}\langle v(t),f(t)\rangle_{V,V^{*}}\,\text{\rm d}t\in(L^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*})^{*},\quad v\in L^{\alpha}_{t}V.

(5.15)

Due to (
5.7
), it holds
\{\iota_{u^{n}}\}_{n}\subset B_{(L^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*})^{*}}
. The theorem of Banach–Alaoglu establishes the existence of
\overline{\iota}\in(L^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*})^{*}
and a sub-sequence (not relabeled) such that

\displaystyle\forall f\in L^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*}:\quad\iota_{u^{n}}(f)\rightarrow\overline{\iota}(f).

(5.16)

But since
(L^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{t}V^{*})^{*}=L^{\alpha}_{t}V^{**}=L^{\alpha}_{t}V
, there exists
u\in L^{\alpha}_{t}V
such that
\overline{\iota}(f)=\int\langle u(t),f(t)\rangle_{V,V^{*}}\,\text{\rm d}t=\iota_{u}(f)
. This and (
5.16
) imply
u^{n}\rightharpoonup u\in L^{\alpha}_{t}V
.

The assertions (
5.14b
), (
5.14c
), and (
5.14e
) follow analogously by choosing
X=L^{\alpha}_{t}V
,
X=L^{1}_{t}H
, and
X=\widetilde{B}^{-1/2}_{2,1}H
, respectively. In each step, we possibly restrict to another sub-sequence.

Lastly, we check (
5.14d
): Recall by Assumption
2.1
it holds
V\hookrightarrow H
compactly. Therefore, an application of Theorem
8.10
with
q=\alpha
,
X_{0}=V
,
\gamma=1/2
,
p=2
, and
X=H
shows that
\{u^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is relatively compact in
L^{2}_{t}H
.
∎

5.4.
The limit is a solution

The identification of the limit is a delicate task. We start by identifying the limiting equation; multiply (

) by a temporal test function

with

), (
5.14e
) respectively

\displaystyle\rightarrow\int_{0}^{T}\left(u_{t}-u_{0},v\right)_{H}\eta_{t}^{\prime}\,\text{\rm d}t,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\langle A(s,u^{n}_{s}),v\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}s\,\eta_{t}^{\prime}\,\text{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\rightarrow\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\overline{A}_{s},v\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}s\,\eta_{t}^{\prime}\,\text{\rm d}t,

Thus,
u
solves for almost all

and all

\displaystyle\left(u_{t}-u_{0},v\right)_{H}=\int_{0}^{t}\langle\overline{A}_{s},v\rangle_{V^{*},V}\,\text{\rm d}s+\left(I_{t}(u),v\right)_{H}.

(5.17)

Moreover, since the right-hand side of (

6.2.
Abstract Young integrals

Let

be such that

\left\lVert\sigma(u)\right\rVert_{H}\;\leqslant\;C(1+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H}),\qquad\left\lVert\sigma(u)-\sigma(v)\right\rVert_{H}\;\leqslant\;L\left\lVert u-v\right\rVert_{H}.

(6.1)

We want to construct the abstract Young integral

I_{t}(u):=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u_{s})\,\text{\rm d}X_{s}

for some given path
X
. Note that since locally,
I
should behave as
\sigma(u_{s})(X_{t}-X_{s})
, we need to first ensure that
X
is of such spatial regularity that
\sigma(u_{s})(X_{t}-X_{s})\in H
; in this context, we introduced in Definition
2.15
the concept of multiplier. A multiplier is a sufficient condition for the spatial compatibility of abstract Young integration.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and

a multiplier in

in the sense of Definition
2.15
. Suppose
\sigma:H\to H
satisfies (
6.1
). Let

Then for any

such that
\gamma+1/q>1
, the mapping

\begin{split}L^{\infty}_{t}H\cap B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H&\to B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H\\

(6.2)

is well-defined and satisfies

\displaystyle\left[I(u)\right]_{B^{\gamma}_{q,\infty}H}\lesssim(C+L)\left[X\right]_{C^{\gamma}_{t}E}(1+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H}+\left[u\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}).

(6.3)

In particular, for any sequence

such that

in

Remark 6.2
.

Note that we start with an element
u
on integrability scale
2
, yet the operator needs to enjoy integrability scale

, i.e., we need to gain integrability. This is possible, but at the price of imposing more regularity. Indeed, we use the embedding

, which ensures such a trade in order to gain integrability. The price we pay in regularity becomes apparant in the condition
\gamma+1/q>1
, in that it becomes more restrictive in

, the larger we intend to choose

.

Proof.

Let
u\in L^{\infty}_{t}H\cap B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H
and

.

We aim to apply the sewing lemma, Lemma
3.1

\displaystyle G_{s,t}=\langle\sigma(u)\rangle_{s,t}(X_{t}-X_{s}),

Estimating

from which we conclude that

\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{\mathbb{B}^{\gamma}_{\infty,\infty}H}\lesssim C(1+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H})\left[X\right]_{C^{\gamma}_{t}E}.

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert\left(\langle\sigma(u)\rangle_{s,s+h}-\sigma(u_{s})\right)(X_{s+h}-X_{s})\right\rVert_{H}^{q}\,\text{\rm d}s\right)^{1/q}\\

Bounding the remaining terms in a similar fashion, we obtain

\displaystyle\lesssim C(1+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H})\left[X\right]_{C^{\gamma}_{t}E}+T^{1/q}\left[X\right]_{C^{\gamma}_{t}E}L\left[u\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}.

Regularization by noise

It is well-known that randomness can lead to regularization; an effect nowadays called regularization by noise. In particular, the translation with respect to a rough process
w
is regularizing. One main tool behind the regularization effect is a formula that relates classical integration of differences and the sewing of a convolution with respect to the local time
L^{w}
of
w
(for brief overview of its basic definition and some properties, refer to the corresponding section in the Appendix
8
) evaluated at a time-averaged function value
\langle u\rangle
of
u
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}b(u_{s}-w_{s})\,\text{\rm d}s=\mathscr{I}_{t}\big(b*L^{w}(\langle u\rangle)\big).

(7.2)

Let us briefly motivate (
7.2
). Since we assume
w
to be a highly oscillating noise, we would expect that on small time scales, i.e.
|t-s|\ll 1
, we have

\int_{s}^{t}b(u_{r}-w_{r})\,\text{\rm d}r\simeq\int_{s}^{t}b(\langle u\rangle_{s,t}-w_{r})\,\text{\rm d}r=(b*L^{w}_{s,t})(\langle u\rangle_{s,t})

where in the last step, we used the occupation times formula. Sewing together these small scale approximation, one can recover (
7.2
), provided
L^{w}
is sufficiently regular with respect to
u
and
b
.
Note in particular that the right-hand-side can in principle still makes sense for irregular
b
; in other words,
w
can regularizes (
7.2
) through the regularity of its local time.

Our abstract framework captures this regularization effect by choosing

\displaystyle I_{t}(u):=\mathscr{I}_{t}\big(b*L^{w}(\langle u\rangle)\big).

It generalizes our results on regularization by noise for the
p
-Laplace equation
[
BW23
]
to a broader class of singularities
b
.

2.13
. It remains to verify their assumptions.

Ad Assumption
2.1
:
By the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem, it follows that the embedding
W^{1,p}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Lambda)\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{\mathrm{div}}(\Lambda)
is compact.

Closely related to the tensor
S
is the tensor

\displaystyle F(B):=\sqrt{\mu}(1+\left\lvert B\right\rvert)^{(p-2)/2}B.

The tensor
F
quantifies the monotonicity of the tensor
S
and is a convenient tool for the verification of Assumption
2.2
. More details on the relation between
F
and
S
can be found in e.g.
[
DFTW20
,
BDSW21
,
LW23
]
.

Keep in mind that by Korn’s and Poincaré’s inequalities the following norms on
W^{1,p}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Lambda)
are equivalent:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varepsilon u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}\eqsim\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}\eqsim\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{W^{1,p}(\Lambda)}.

Next, we verify Assumption
2.2
.

Ad
(H1)
:
Let
u,v,w\in W^{1,p}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Lambda)
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. We split

\displaystyle\langle A(u+\lambda v),w\rangle_{V^{*},V}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=-\int_{\Lambda}S(\varepsilon u+\lambda\varepsilon v):\varepsilon w\,\text{\rm d}x-\int_{\Lambda}\big((u+\lambda v)\cdot\nabla\big)(u+\lambda v)\cdot w\,\text{\rm d}x=:\mathrm{K}_{1}+\mathrm{K}_{2}.

We show that
\mathrm{K}_{1}
and
\mathrm{K}_{2}
are continuous functions in
\lambda
. Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Continuity of
\mathrm{K}_{1}
: Due to continuity of
S
it holds

\displaystyle\big(S(\varepsilon u+\lambda_{1}\varepsilon v)-S(\varepsilon u+\lambda_{2}\varepsilon v)\big):\varepsilon w\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ for }\quad\lambda_{1}\to\lambda_{2}.

Moreover, using Hölder’s inequality and
\left\lvert S(B)\right\rvert^{p^{\prime}}\lesssim\left\lvert B\right\rvert^{p}+1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda}\big(S(\varepsilon u+\lambda_{1}\varepsilon v)-S(\varepsilon u+\lambda_{2}\varepsilon v)\big):\varepsilon w\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\;\leqslant\;\big(\left\lVert S(\varepsilon u+\lambda_{1}\varepsilon v)\right\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda)}+\left\lVert S(\varepsilon u+\lambda_{2}\varepsilon v)\right\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda)}\big)\left\lVert\varepsilon w\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\lesssim\big(\left\lVert\varepsilon u+\lambda_{1}\varepsilon v\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}^{p}+\left\lVert\varepsilon u+\lambda_{2}\varepsilon v\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}^{p}+\left\lvert\Lambda\right\rvert\big)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left\lVert\varepsilon w\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}<\infty.

Therefore, dominated convergence ensures that

\displaystyle\lambda\mapsto\int_{\Lambda}S(\varepsilon u+\lambda\varepsilon v):\varepsilon w\,\text{\rm d}x

is continuous.

Continuity of
\mathrm{K}_{2}
: Hölder’s inequality implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda}\big[\big((u+\lambda_{1}v)\cdot\nabla\big)(u+\lambda_{1}v)-\big((u+\lambda_{2}v)\cdot\nabla\big)(u+\lambda_{2}v)\big]\cdot w\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\int_{\Lambda}\big(v\cdot\nabla)(u+\lambda_{1}v)+\big((u+\lambda_{2}v)\cdot\nabla)v\cdot w\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\;\leqslant\;\left\lvert\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\right\rvert\big(\left\lVert\nabla u+\lambda_{1}\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}\left\lVert v\otimes w\right\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle\hskip 90.00014pt+\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}\left\lVert(u+\lambda_{2}v)\otimes w\right\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda)}\big).

Notice that, since
p\;\geqslant\;3d/(d+2)
it holds
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Lambda)\hookrightarrow L^{2p^{\prime}}(\Lambda)
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert v\otimes w\right\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda)}\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2p^{\prime}}(\Lambda)}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{L^{2p^{\prime}}(\Lambda)}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}\left\lVert\nabla w\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla u+\lambda_{1}\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}\left\lVert v\otimes w\right\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda)}+\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}\left\lVert(u+\lambda_{2}v)\otimes w\right\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla u+\lambda_{1}\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}\left\lVert\nabla w\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle\hskip 40.00006pt+\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}\left\lVert\nabla u+\lambda_{2}\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}\left\lVert\nabla w\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}<\infty.

This implies the continuity of

\displaystyle\lambda\to\int_{\Lambda}\big((u+\lambda v)\cdot\nabla\big)(u+\lambda v)\cdot w\,\text{\rm d}x.

Thus, Condition
(H1)
holds.

Ad
(H2)

into strongly monotone and lower-order parts.

\displaystyle\langle A(u),u\rangle_{V^{*},V}

\displaystyle\left\lVert A(u)\right\rVert_{V^{*}}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\left\lVert S(\varepsilon u)\right\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda)}+C\left\lVert(u\cdot\nabla)u\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varepsilon u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}^{2}\;\leqslant\;(1+\left\lVert\varepsilon u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)})^{2}\;\leqslant\;(1+\left\lVert\varepsilon u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)})^{p-1}\;\leqslant\;2^{p-2}(1+\left\lVert\varepsilon u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Lambda)}^{p-1}).

\displaystyle\left\lVert A(u)\right\rVert_{V^{*}}

By assumption, Assumption
2.3

8.1.
Local time and occupation times formula

We recall for the reader the basic concepts of occupation measures, local times and the occupation times formula. A comprehensive review paper on these topics is
[
GH80
]
.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
w:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a measurable path. Then the occupation measure at time
t\in[0,T]
, written
\mu^{w}_{t}
is the Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

\mu^{w}_{t}(A):=\lambda(\{s\in[0,t]:\ w_{s}\in A\}),\quad A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
\lambda
denotes the standard Lebesgue measure.

The occupation measure thus measures how much time the process
w
spends in certain Borel sets. Provided for any
t\in[0,T]
, the measure is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we call the corresponding Radon-Nikodym derivative local time of the process
w
:

Definition 8.2
.

Let
w:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a measurable path. Assume that there exists a measurable function
L^{w}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\mu^{w}_{t}(A)=\int_{A}L^{w}_{t}(z)\,\text{\rm d}z,

for any
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
t\in[0,T]
. Then we call
L^{w}
local time of
w
.

Note that by the definition of the occupation measure, we have for any bounded measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
that

\int_{0}^{t}f(w_{s})ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(z)\mu^{w}_{t}(\,\text{\rm d}z).

(8.1)

The above equation (
8.1
) is called occupation times formula. Remark that in particular, provided
w
admits a local time, we also have for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

\int_{0}^{t}f(u-w_{s})\,\text{\rm d}s=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u-z)\mu^{w}_{t}(\,\text{\rm d}z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u-z)L^{w}_{t}(z)\,\text{\rm d}z=(f*L^{w}_{t})(u).

(8.2)

Lemma 8.3
.

Suppose
\sigma:H\to H
is Lipschitz-continuous, i.e., there exists a constant
L>0
such that for all

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)\right\rVert_{H}\;\leqslant\;L\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert_{H}.

Using Jensen’s inequality, (

), and Hölder’s inequalities,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert\langle\sigma(u)\rangle_{r,r+h}-\sigma(u_{r})\right\rVert_{H}^{q}\,\text{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\int_{0}^{h}s\,\text{\rm d}s\left[u\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}\;\leqslant\;h^{2}\left[u\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}H}^{2}.

Lemma 8.7

(Localisation of Nikolskii norm)
.

Let
J=[a,b]
,
a<b
be an interval,
\mathcal{P}=\{[t_{k-1},t_{k}]:\,t_{0}=a<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{N}=b\}
be a finite partition of
J
, and
E
be a Banach space. Then

\displaystyle u\in\bigcap_{k=1}^{N}B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}([t_{k-1},t_{k}];E)\cap L^{\infty}(J;E)\quad\Rightarrow\quad u\in B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(J;E).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left[u\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}(J;E)}^{2}\;\leqslant\;\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[u\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}([t_{k-1},t_{k}];E)}^{2}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(J;E)}^{2}2(N-1+h_{\mathrm{min}}^{-1}\left\lvert J\right\rvert),

(8.7)

where
h_{\mathrm{min}}=\min_{k=1,\ldots,N}\left\lvert t_{k}-t_{k-1}\right\rvert
.

Proof.

We distinguish two cases: differences for small and large times. Here small and large times should be understood with respect to the minimal partition mesh-size
h_{\mathrm{min}}
.

h\;\leqslant\;h_{\mathrm{min}}
:
For short times we can use the control provided by the local Nikolskii norm and an
L^{\infty}
-estimate. Notice that each
[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
splits into
[t_{k-1},t_{k}-h]\cup[t_{k}-h,t_{k}]
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b-h}\left\lVert u_{t+h}-u_{t}\right\rVert_{E}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}-h}\left\lVert u_{t+h}-u_{t}\right\rVert_{E}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\int_{t_{k}-h}^{t_{k}}\left\lVert u_{t+h}-u_{t}\right\rVert_{E}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;h\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[u\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}([t_{k-1},t_{k}];E)}^{2}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(J;E)}^{2}2(N-1)\right).

It follows

\displaystyle\sup_{h\;\leqslant\;h_{\mathrm{min}}}h^{-1}\int_{a}^{b-h}\left\lVert u_{t+h}-u_{t}\right\rVert_{E}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}t\;\leqslant\;\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[u\right]_{B^{1/2}_{2,\infty}([t_{k-1},t_{k}];E)}^{2}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(J;E)}^{2}2(N-1).

(8.8)

h>h_{\mathrm{min}}
:
For large times we no longer need to guarantee a decay of increments. Thus, it is sufficient to estimate

\displaystyle h^{-1}\int_{a}^{b-h}\left\lVert u_{t+h}-u_{t}\right\rVert_{E}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}t\;\leqslant\;2h_{\mathrm{min}}^{-1}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(J;E)}^{2}\left\lvert J\right\rvert,

which implies

\displaystyle\sup_{h>h_{\mathrm{min}}}h^{-1}\int_{a}^{b-h}\left\lVert u_{t+h}-u_{t}\right\rVert_{E}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}t\;\leqslant\;2h_{\mathrm{min}}^{-1}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(J;E)}^{2}\left\lvert J\right\rvert.

(8.9)

Combining (
8.8
) and (
8.9
) yields (
8.7
).

Lemma 8.8
.

B^{n}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\exp{(-\beta^{n}_{r})}\,\text{\rm d}B^{n}_{r}=\int_{0}^{t}\exp{(-\beta^{n}_{r})}Z^{n}_{r}\,\text{\rm d}\beta^{n}_{r}-\int_{0}^{t}\exp{(-\beta^{n}_{r})}\,\text{\rm d}Z^{n}_{r}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\,\text{\rm d}(Z^{n}_{r}\exp{(-\beta^{n}_{r})})=-Z^{n}_{t}\exp{(-\beta^{n}_{t})}+Z^{n}_{0}\exp{(-\beta^{n}_{0})}.

Using the stability of sewings under mollification as expressed in Lemma
3.3
, we are able to pass to the limit in the above, yielding the claim.
∎

8.3.
An Aubin Lions type Lemma

In an abstract sense, upon establishing suitable a priori bounds on approximate problems, one is confronted with the problem of the passage to the limit. However, as only weak or weak-
*
convergences are available from a priori bounds, the passage to the limit in non-linear terms typically poses an issue as this requires some form of strongly convergent subsequences. Such strong convergence is classically provided by the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem in combination with the Aubin-Lions Lemma. Remark however that due to the irregularity of
I(u)
, no
L^{q}([0,T],X)
estimates on
\partial_{t}u^{n}
will be available. We therefore establish an Aubin-Lions type Lemma suited for our context.

Theorem 8.9

(
[
Gut82
]
Theorem A.1)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and let
T\in(0,\infty)
. Let
X
be a Banach space. A set
F\subset L^{p}([0,T],X)
is relatively compact in
L^{p}([0,T],X)
if and only if

(1)

\sup_{f\in F}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p}([0,T],X)}<\infty

(2)

\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert f_{r+h}-f_{r}\right\rVert_{X}^{p}dr=0
uniformly in
f\in F

(3)

For every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a compact set
Q_{\epsilon}\subset X
such that for every
f\in F
there exists a set
A_{f,\epsilon}\subset[0,T]
with
\lambda([0,T]\backslash A_{f,\epsilon})\;\leqslant\;\epsilon
and

f_{t}\in Q_{\epsilon},\quad\forall t\in A_{f,\epsilon}

Given the above result, we may conclude the following.

Theorem 8.10
.

Let
X_{0},X
be Banach spaces, such that the embedding
X_{0}\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow X
is compact. Let
p,q\in[1,\infty)
and
\gamma>0
. Then the space

\mathcal{Y}:=L^{q}_{t}X_{0}\cap B^{\gamma}_{p,\infty}X

embeds compactly into
L^{p}_{t}X
.

Proof.

We apply Theorem
8.9
. Points
(1)
and
(2)
thereof follow immediately as

\int_{0}^{T-h}\left\lVert f_{r+h}-f_{r}\right\rVert_{X}^{p}dr\;\leqslant\;h^{\gamma p}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B^{\gamma}_{p,\infty}X}\to 0.

Let
(f^{n})_{n}
be a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{Y}
. Given
\epsilon>0
, we choose
R(\epsilon)
sufficiently large such that

\frac{1}{R(\epsilon)^{q}}\sup_{n}\left\lVert f^{n}\right\rVert_{L^{q}_{t}X_{0}}^{q}<\epsilon

Further set
A_{n,\epsilon}:=\{t\in[0,T]\ |\ \left\lVert f_{n}\right\rVert_{X_{0}}\;\geqslant\;R(\epsilon)\}
and for the canonical imbedding
i:X_{0}\to X
, set
Q_{\epsilon}:=\overline{i(B_{X_{0}}(0,R(\epsilon)))}
, which is a compact set in
X
such that
f^{n}_{t}\in Q_{\epsilon}
for all
t\in A_{n,\epsilon}
. Finally, by Markov’s inequality we have

\lambda([0,T]\backslash A_{n,\epsilon})\;\leqslant\;\frac{1}{R(\epsilon)^{q}}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert f^{n}_{r}\right\rVert_{X_{0}}^{q}dr<\epsilon.

This establishes
(3)
, thus allowing us to apply Theorem
8.9
concluding the proof.
∎

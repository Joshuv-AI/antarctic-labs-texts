Minimal twist of almost commutative geometries
Manuele Filaci † , Pierre Martinetti *†

Abstract
. We classify the twists of almost commutative spectral triples that keep the Hilbert space and the Dirac operator untouched. The involved twisting operator is shown to be the product of the grading of a manifold by a finite dimensional operator, which is not necessarily a grading of the internal space. Necessary and sufficient constraints on this operator are listed.

1.
Introduction

The Higgs field might be a probe of the internal structure of spacetime. This idea - pioneered in

[
17
]
- has been fully implemented in the
framework of noncommutative geometry
[
10
]
. It yields a
description of the Standard Model of fundamental interactions (including massive neutrinos
[
6
]
) as a
pure gravity theory on an
almost commutative geometry

[
11
]
, that is on
a space which is the product of a usual continuous manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
by some
internal matricial structure.

This
product is mathematically well defined in terms of spectral
triple. The latter consists in an involutive algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
acting
faithfully on some Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, together with a selfadjoint
operator
D
with compact resolvent, such that the commutator

[D,a]

is bounded for any
a
in
{\mathcal{A}}
. With additional axioms, spectral
triples furnish a purely algebraic characterization of
riemannian (spin) manifolds
[
12
]
, as well as their generalisation to the
noncommutative setting
[
11
]
.

A twisted spectral triple is defined similarly, except that the
commutator is no longer required to be bounded. Instead, one
asks for an automorphism
\rho
of
{\mathcal{A}}
such that the twisted commutator

(1)

[D,a]_{\rho}:=Da-\rho(a)D

is bounded for any
a
in
{\mathcal{A}}
. Such twists have been introduced in
[
13
]
with some mathematical
motivations. Later, they show to be useful for physical
applications as well, for they offer a way to build models beyond the
Standard Model
[
15
,
16
]
. In particular, by twisting the spectral triple of the
Standard Model in a minimal way, that is keeping the Hilbert
space and Dirac operator untouched (only the algebra is modified), one produces extra bosonic fields without
altering the fermionic content of the theory.

There exists a general procedure to obtain such minimal twist, recalled in
section
2
, which uses a
grading of the spectral triple
[
19
]
. Recall that the later is a selfadjoint operator

\Gamma
on
{\mathcal{H}}
, squaring to the identity
\mathbb{I}
and anticommuting with

D
, such that
[\Gamma,a]=0
for any
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
.
The aim of this note is to understand which of these properties
are necessary:
is the twist doable using a
twisting operator

that is not a grading ?

We first list in section

3
some basic properties
expected from the twisting operator. Then we proceed at the light of three conditions that must be satisfied by a real
twisted spectral triple: the boundedness of the twisted commutator
(
1
) in section
4
, the order zero condition
in section
5
and the twisted first-order
condition in section
6
.
The resulting constraints are listed in propositions

5.1
and

6.1
. The grading
operator is not the only solution.

For
almost-commutative geometries, assuming the
twisting operator is the product of an
operator
\mathcal{T}
acting on the spinor space of
{\mathcal{M}}
with
an operator
T_{F}
acting on the internal space, these constraints are
shown to be equivalent to their reduction to the internal space
(corollaries
5.1.1
and
6.1.1
)
. Although the boundedness of the commutator forces

\mathcal{T}
to be the grading of the manifold,

T_{F}
is not necessarily
a grading of the internal space.

2.
Minimal twist

A
minimal twist
of a (real, graded) spectral triple
({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)
is
[
19
, Def. 3.2]

a (real, graded) twisted spectral triple
({\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{H}},D)_{\rho}
where
\mathcal{B}
is a involutive algebra with unit
1_{\mathcal{B}}
,

\rho
an automorphism of
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}
and the
representation
\pi
of the latter on
{\mathcal{H}}
is such that

(2)

\pi(a\otimes 1_{\mathcal{B}})=\pi_{0}(a)\quad\forall a\in{\mathcal{A}},

where
\pi_{0}
is the representation of
{\mathcal{A}}
on
{\mathcal{H}}
from the initial spectral
triple.

If this initial spectral triple is graded, then
there always exists a
minimal twist with
1
1

1

To fix
notation we assume that
{\mathcal{A}}
is a complex algebra, but the results
also hold for real algebras.

{\mathcal{B}}={\mathbb{C}}^{2}

and
\rho
the automorphism of
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\simeq{\mathcal{A}}\oplus{\mathcal{A}}
given by the flip

(3)

\rho((a,a^{\prime})):=(a^{\prime},a)\quad\forall a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}.

The construction of this minimal
twist-by-grading
,

(4)

({\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2},{\mathcal{H}},D)_{\rho},

starts with the following
observation: by definition the grading

\Gamma
commutes with the algebra, so the projections
\frac{\mathbb{I}\pm\Gamma}{2}
on its
eigenspaces
{\mathcal{H}}_{\pm}
define two independent involutive
representations

(5)

\pi_{\pm}(a):=\left(\frac{\mathbb{I}\pm\Gamma}{2}\pi_{0}(a)\right)_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\pm}}

of
{\mathcal{A}}
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\pm}
. Their direct sum

(6)

\pi(a,a^{\prime}):=\frac{\mathbb{I}+\Gamma}{2}\pi_{0}(a)+\frac{\mathbb{I}-\Gamma}{2}\pi_{0}(a^{\prime})\qquad\forall a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}

is a representation of
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
that satisfies the properties
of a twisted spectral triple
[
19
, Prop.3.8]
as well as
condition (
2
).

This twist-by-grading is the only
possible minimal twist for the spectral triple naturally associated to
an (even dimensional) closed riemannian spin manifold
{\mathcal{M}}

[
19
, Prop.4.2]
, namely

(7)

{C^{\infty}({\mathcal{M}})},\quad L^{2}({\mathcal{M}},S),\quad{\not{\partial}}=-i\sum_{\mu=1}^{\text{dim}{\mathcal{M}}}\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}

where the unital algebra
{C^{\infty}({\mathcal{M}})}
of smooth functions on
{\mathcal{M}}
acts by
multiplication on the
Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathcal{M}},S)
of square integrable spinors,

(8)

(\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(f)\psi)(x):=f(x)\psi(x)\quad\forall\psi\in L^{2}({\mathcal{M}},S),x\in{\mathcal{M}},

and
{\not{\partial}}
is the Dirac operator
associated with the spin structure. This spectral triple is graded
with grading the product
\gamma_{{\mathcal{M}}}
of the gamma matrices.

The unicity of this twist no longer holds true for an almost commutative geometry

(9)

{\mathcal{A}}={C^{\infty}({\mathcal{M}})}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{F},\quad{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}({\mathcal{M}},S)\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{F},\quad D={\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F}+\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes D_{F},

that is the product of (
7
) with a finite dimensional graded
spectral
triple
({\mathcal{A}}_{F},{\mathcal{H}}_{F},D_{F})
with grading
\Gamma_{F}
(in the equation
above
\mathbb{I}_{F}
is the identity operator on
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
). The
representation

(10)

\pi_{0}=\pi_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes\pi_{F}

of
{\mathcal{A}}
on
{\mathcal{H}}
is the product of
\pi_{\mathcal{M}}
with the
representation
\pi_{F}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{F}
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
given by the finite dimensional
spectral triple. If
\pi_{F}
is irreducible, then any minimal twist is
necessarily by
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathbb{C}}^{2}
but the representation
\pi
is not necessarily
the one given in
(
6
), as explained below. If
\pi_{F}
is not irreducible, there exists minimal twists with
{\mathcal{B}}
different from

{\mathbb{C}}^{2}

[
19
, Corr.4.5]
.

Remark 2.1
.

The dimension of
{\mathcal{M}}
has to be even so that the spectral triple
(
7
) admits a grading
\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}
. The odd dimensional case should be
investigated elsewhere.

3.
Twisting operator

The point of this note is to investigate which properties of
the grading
\Gamma
are necessary to build a minimally twisted partner
to a usual spectral triple. The commutativity with the
initial representation of
{\mathcal{A}}
is important to get two
independent representations
\pi_{\pm}
, but to what extend are the commutation properties of
\Gamma

with
D
and (in case of a real spectraal triple) with the real structure
J
relevant ?

Given a spectral triple
({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)
, we thus consider
an operator in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
which shares all the properties of a grading but the commutation
properties with
D
and
J
. Namely
T
is selfadjoint,
T^{2}=\mathbb{I}
, the degeneracy of both its eigenvalues

\pm 1
are non-zero and
T
commutes with the
representation
\pi_{0}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
on
{\mathcal{H}}
.
The latter is thus
the direct sum
\pi_{+}\oplus\pi_{-}
of the two involutive representations of
{\mathcal{A}}
on the eigenspaces
{\mathcal{H}}_{\pm}
of
T
given by

(11)

\pi_{\pm}(a)=\left(\frac{\mathbb{I}\pm T}{2}\pi_{0}(a)\right)_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\pm}}.

As in (
6
), the operator
T
allows to define a
representation of
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
on
{\mathcal{H}}

(12)

\pi(a,a^{\prime}):=\pi_{+}(a)\oplus\pi_{-}(a^{\prime})=\frac{\mathbb{I}+T}{2}\pi_{0}(a)+\frac{\mathbb{I}-T}{2}\pi_{0}(a^{\prime}).

To avoid domain issues, we assume that
T{\mathcal{H}}\subset\text{Dom }D
. We call
T
a
twisting operator
.

For an almost commutative geometry (
9
), we further assume that

(13)

T={\mathcal{T}}\otimes T_{F}

where
{\mathcal{T}}\in L^{2}({\mathcal{M}},S)
and
T_{F}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{F})
. A bounded operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
is not necessarily of this form, but could be (the closure of) a sum of such
operators. However, we restrict to operators (
13
), for they already pave the way to interesting physical
applications beyond the Standard Model.
The selfadjointness of
T
is to guarantee that the representations

\pi_{\pm}
are involutive. This does not imply that
{\mathcal{T}}
and
T_{F}

are selfadjoint. However one may
always
restrict to this case.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T={\mathcal{T}}\otimes T_{F}
be a selfadjoint operator on
L^{2}({\mathcal{M}},S)\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
that squares to
\mathbb{I}
. Then there exist two selfadjoint operators
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
on
L^{2}({\mathcal{M}},S)
and

\tilde{T}_{F}
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
, squaring to the identity, such that

T=\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\otimes\tilde{T}_{F}
.

Proof.

The matrix
T_{F}^{\dagger}T_{F}
being non-zero (otherwise
T
does not
square to
\mathbb{I}
) is positive, thus it admits at least one real eigenvalue
\lambda>0
, with associated
eigenvectors
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{F}
, and all the other non-zero eigenvalues are
also strictly positive. For any
\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathcal{M}},S)
, one has

(14)

T^{\dagger}T(\varphi\otimes\psi)={\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}{\mathcal{T}}\varphi\otimes T_{F}^{\dagger}T_{F}\psi=\lambda{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}{\mathcal{T}}\varphi\otimes\psi.

On the other side, by hypothesis
T^{\dagger}T=\mathbb{I}
, that is
T^{\dagger}T(\varphi\otimes\psi)=\varphi\otimes\psi
. Therefore

(15)

(\lambda{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}{\mathcal{T}}-\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{M}})\varphi\otimes\psi=0\qquad\forall\varphi\in L^{2}(M,S)\,

meaning that
{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}{\mathcal{T}}
coincides with the operator of
multiplication of spinors by
\lambda^{-1}
. Repeating the analysis for another
non-zero eigenvalue
\lambda^{\prime}
shows that
{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}{\mathcal{T}}
coincides
with the multiplication by
{\lambda^{\prime}}^{-1}
, so

\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda
. This allows to define

(16)

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{T}},\quad\tilde{T}_{F}=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{F},

such that
T=\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\otimes\tilde{T}_{F}

with

(17)

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{T}}=\lambda^{-1}{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}{\mathcal{T}}=\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{M}}.

From
T^{\dagger}T=TT^{\dagger}=\mathbb{I}
then follows that
\tilde{T}_{F}^{\dagger}\tilde{T}_{F}=\tilde{T}_{F}\tilde{T}_{F}^{\dagger}=\mathbb{I}_{F}
, that is
\tilde{T}_{F}
is unitary,
and so is
T_{F}
.

To show that
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
and
\tilde{T}_{F}
are selfadjoint, let us
apply
T=T^{\dagger}
on
\varphi\otimes\Psi
where
\Psi
is an
eigenvector of
\tilde{T}_{F}
, with eigenvalue
\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}
,

|\tau|=1
. Using
\tilde{T}_{F}^{\dagger}\Psi=\tau^{-1}\Psi
, one obtains

(18)

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\varphi\otimes\tau\Psi=\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}\varphi\otimes\tau^{-1}\Psi\quad\forall\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathcal{M}},S),

meaning that

\tau^{-1}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}=\tau\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
.
Redefining
\tau\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
(that is

\tau^{-1}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\dagger}
), the
previous equation
shows that
\tilde{T}
is selfadjoint. The selfadjointness of
\tilde{T}_{F}

then follows from the one of
T
.
∎

4.
Boundedness of the commutator

We investigate the conditions
imposed, on the twisting operator (
13
) of an almost commutative
geometry, by the boundedness of the twisted commutator

(19)

[{\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F}+\gamma^{5}\otimes D_{F},\pi(a,a^{\prime})]_{\rho},

for
\pi
the representation (
12
) and
\rho
the flip
(
3
). For simplicity, we restrict to the case
\pi_{F}
is irreducible, that is

{\mathcal{B}}={\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For an almost commutative geometry, the twisted
commutator
[{\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F},\pi(a,a^{\prime})]_{\rho}
is bounded for any
(a,a^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
if and only if
{\mathcal{T}}
anticommutes with
{\not{\partial}}
.

Proof.

Using

(20)

\displaystyle({\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F})(\mathbb{I}\pm T)=(\mathbb{I}\mp T)({\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F})\pm\left\{{\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F},T\right\},

one obtains, omitting the symbol
\pi_{0}
,

(21)

\displaystyle[{\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F},\pi(a,a^{\prime})]_{\rho}

\displaystyle=({\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F})\pi(a,a^{\prime})-\pi(a^{\prime},a)({\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F}),

(22)

\displaystyle=({\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F})\left(\frac{\mathbb{I}+T}{2}a+\frac{\mathbb{I}-T}{2}a^{\prime}\right)-\left(\frac{\mathbb{I}+T}{2}a^{\prime}+\frac{\mathbb{I}-T}{2}a\right)({\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F}),

(23)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{I}-T}{2}[{\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F},a]+\frac{\mathbb{I}+T}{2}[{\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F},a^{\prime}]+\frac{1}{2}\left\{{\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F},T\right\}(a-a^{\prime}).

For
a=f\otimes m
, then
[{\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F},a]=[{\not{\partial}},f]\otimes m

is bounded, being
({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)
a spectral triple. The same is true for an arbitrary
a
in

{\mathcal{A}}
, and also for
[{\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F},a^{\prime}]
. So the first two terms in
(
23
) are bounded.

If
T
anticommutes with

{\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F}
, the last term in (
23
) is zero, so that (
21
) is
bounded.

Conversely, assume (
21
) is bounded for any
(a,a^{\prime})
in

{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. This means that the last term in (
23
) is
bounded. For
a-a^{\prime}=1\otimes m
with
1
the constant function

f(x)=1
on
{\mathcal{M}}
, then this last term is (up to a factor
\frac{1}{2}
)

(24)

\left\{{\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F},T\right\}(a-a^{\prime})=\left\{{\not{\partial}},{\mathcal{T}}\right\}\otimes T_{F}m.

This is bounded if and only if
\left\{{\not{\partial}},{\mathcal{T}}\right\}
is
bounded. For
\psi
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{+}
(
+1
eigenspace of
{\mathcal{T}}
), one has

(25)

\left\{{\not{\partial}},{\mathcal{T}}\right\}\psi={\not{\partial}}\psi+{\mathcal{T}}{\not{\partial}}\psi=(\mathbb{I}+{\mathcal{T}}){\not{\partial}}\psi

meaning
\left\{{\not{\partial}},{\mathcal{T}}\right\}
coincides with
(\mathbb{I}+{\mathcal{T}}){\not{\partial}}
which is
an unbounded operator, unless it is zero. So the restriction of

\left\{{\not{\partial}},{\mathcal{T}}\right\}
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{+}
is zero. A similar argument holds
for the restriction to
{\mathcal{H}}_{-}
. Hence the result.
∎

The finite part of an almost commutative geometry only involves
bounded operator. Therefore the boundedness of the twisted commutator (
19
) only
depends on the property of
{\mathcal{T}}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The twisted commutator (
19
) is bounded if, and only if,

{\mathcal{T}}=\pm\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proof.

The twisted commutator
[\gamma^{5}\otimes D_{F},\pi(a,a^{\prime}]_{\rho}
is
bounded, whether or not
T
anticommutes with

\gamma^{5}\otimes D_{F}
. So (
19
) is bounded iff

[{\not{\partial}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{2},\pi(a,a^{\prime})]_{\rho}
is bounded, that is by
Prop.
4.1
iff
{\mathcal{T}}

anticommutes with
{\not{\partial}}
. Explicitly, with
\nabla_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+\omega_{\mu}
where
\omega_{\mu}
is the spin connection,
this means

(26)

\left\{-i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu},{\mathcal{T}}\right\}+\left\{-i\gamma^{\mu}\omega_{\mu},{\mathcal{T}}\right\}=0.

The second term is bounded, being
\omega_{\mu}
bounded. By the Leibniz rule
satisfied by
{\not{\partial}}
, one has

(27)

\displaystyle\left\{-i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu},{\mathcal{T}}\right\}

\displaystyle=-i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}{\mathcal{T}}-i{\mathcal{T}}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}=-i\gamma^{\mu}(\partial_{\mu}{\mathcal{T}})-i\gamma^{\mu}{\mathcal{T}}\partial_{\mu}-i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}{\mathcal{T}},

(28)

\displaystyle=-i\gamma^{\mu}(\partial_{\mu}{\mathcal{T}})-i\left\{\gamma^{\mu},{\mathcal{T}}\right\}\partial_{\mu}.

The first term is bounded, the second one unbounded. For (
26
)
to hold, both the bounded part and the unbounded parts must be
zero. The latter condition is equivalent to
\left\{\gamma^{\mu},{\mathcal{T}}\right\}=0
, so
{\mathcal{T}}=\lambda\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}
, for the only operator that
anti-commutes with all the
\gamma
’s matrices are the multiple of

\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}
.
By lemma
3.1

{\mathcal{T}}
is selfadjoint - which forces

\lambda
to be real - and

{\mathcal{T}}^{2}=\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{M}}
, which reduces the choice to
\lambda=\pm 1
.
∎

5.
order-zero condition

Not all the axioms of noncommutative geometry have been adapted to the
twisted context. However, those most relevant for physics
(i.e. regarding gauge transformations) do make sense for a twisted
spectral triple
[
19
,
20
]
. Especially,
a real structure for a twisted spectral triple is defined as in the
non-twisted case, that is an antilinear operator
J
such that

(29)

J^{2}=\epsilon\mathbb{I},\;JD=\epsilon^{\prime}DJ,\;J\Gamma=\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\Gamma J

for some
\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\in\left\{-1,1\right\}
(those
three signs defines the
KO
-dimension of the triple), which implements a representation
\pi^{\circ}
of the
opposite algebra
{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ}
,

(30)

\pi_{0}^{\circ}(a^{\circ})=J\pi_{0}(a^{*})J^{-1}

asking to
commutes with the one of
{\mathcal{A}}
,

(31)

[\pi_{0}(a),J\pi_{0}(b^{*})J^{-1}]=0\qquad\forall a,b\in{\mathcal{A}}.

This is the order zero condition (the first-order
condition, discussed in the next section).

The
twist-by-grading of a real twisted spectral triple
({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)
automatically
satisfies the order zero condition, with the same real structure. Namely, if (
31
) holds for
({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)
, then
for
\pi
the representation (
6
) of
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
defined
by the grading one has

(32)

[\pi(a,a^{\prime}),J\pi(b^{*},{b^{\prime}}^{*})J^{-1}]=0\qquad\forall\,(a,a^{\prime}),(b,b^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}.

We work out below the
conditions such that the same holds true for the representation
\pi

(
12
) induced by the twisting operator.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)
be a real spectral triple with real structure

J
. If
{\mathcal{A}}
is a unital algebra, then the order zero condition (
32
) for the
representation
\pi
in (
12
) holds true if and only if

(33)

[T,JTJ^{-1}]=0\quad\text{ and }\quad[\pi_{0}(a),JTJ^{-1}]=0\qquad\forall a\in{\mathcal{A}}.

Proof.

By easy manipulations, one has

\displaystyle[\pi(a,a^{\prime}),J\pi(b,b^{\prime})J^{-1}]

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{I}+T\right)\pi_{0}(a)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{I}-T\right)\pi_{0}(a^{\prime}),J\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{I}+T\right)\pi_{0}(b)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{I}-T\right)\pi_{0}(b^{\prime})\right)J^{-1}\right],

(34)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\pi_{0}(\alpha)+T\pi_{0}(\alpha^{\prime}),J\left(\pi_{0}(\beta)+T\pi_{0}(\beta^{\prime})\right)J^{-1}\right],

where we write

(35)

\alpha:=a+a^{\prime},\;\alpha^{\prime}=a-a^{\prime},\;\beta:=b+b^{\prime},\;\beta^{\prime}=b-b^{\prime}.

The order zero condition is equivalent to (
34
) being
zero for any
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}
, that is (omitting the
symbol of representation and denoting with an hat the adjoint action
of
J
, e.g.
\hat{T}=JTJ^{-1}
)

(36)

\displaystyle\left[\alpha,\hat{\beta}\right]=0,\quad\;\,\,\left[\alpha,JT\beta J^{-1}\right]=0,

(37)

\displaystyle\left[T\alpha,\hat{\beta}\right]=0,\quad\left[T\alpha,JT\beta J^{-1}\right]=0\qquad\forall\alpha,\beta\in{\mathcal{A}}.

The first condition (
36
) is the order zero condition for

({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},D)
, so it is always true by hypothesis. The second
condition (
36
) writes

(38)

0=\left[\alpha,\hat{T}\,\hat{\beta}\right]=\hat{T}\left[\alpha,\hat{\beta}\right]+\left[\alpha,\hat{T}\right]\hat{\beta}.

The first term is zero by the order zero condition. The second term is zero for any
\alpha,\beta
if and only
if (consider the case
\beta
is the unit of
{\mathcal{A}}
)

(39)

\left[\alpha,\hat{T}\right]\quad\forall\alpha\in{\mathcal{A}}.

For the same reasons, the first equation (
37
) written as

(40)

0=T[\alpha,\hat{\beta}]+[T,\hat{\beta}]\alpha

is equivalent to
[T,\hat{\beta}]=0
that is, multiplying by

J^{-1}
on the left and
J

on the right,

(41)

0=J^{-1}TJ\beta-\beta J^{-1}TJ=[J^{-1}TJ,\beta].

Remembering that
J^{-1}=\epsilon J
, this is equivalent to
(
39
). Remains the second condition (
37
), which
is equivalent to

(42)

\hat{T}[T\alpha,\hat{\beta}]+[T\alpha,\hat{T}]\hat{\beta}=0,

It implies (consider
\alpha=\beta=1_{\mathcal{A}}
)

(43)

[T,\hat{T}]=0.

Therefore the order zero condition implies (
39
)
and (
43
). To show that these two conditions are sufficient, the only points that remains to show is that they
imply the second condition (
37
), that is (
42
). The
first term of this equation is zero as soon as (
39
) holds
(as shown studying the first term of (
37
) ). The second term
is obviously zero as soon as both
T
and
\alpha
commute with
\hat{T}
.∎

For an almost commutative geometry the conditions (
33
) are equivalent to their
restrictions to the finite dimensional spectral triple.
To show this, let us absorb the sign ambiguity of proposition
4.2

redefining
T_{F}\to\pm T_{F}
. The twisting operator
(
13
) of an almost commutative geometry is thus

(44)

T=\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes T_{F}.

Since
\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}
commutes with the representation
\pi_{\mathcal{M}}
(
8
),
requiring
T
to commute with the representation
\pi_{0}
(
10
)
implies

(45)

[T_{F},\pi_{F}(m)]=0\quad\forall m\in{\mathcal{A}}_{F}.

Corollary 5.1.1
.

The minimal twist of a real, almost commutative, geometry by
T
as in
(
44
) satisfies the order zero condition
if and only if

(46)

[T_{F},J_{F}T_{F}J_{F}^{-1}]=0\quad\text{ and }\quad[T_{F},J_{F}\,\pi_{F}(m)\,J_{F}^{-1}]=0\quad\forall m\in{\mathcal{A}}_{F}.

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{J}}\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}=\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{J}}
, then

(47)

JTJ^{-1}={\mathcal{J}}\gamma_{M}{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}\otimes J_{F}T_{F}J_{F}^{-1}=\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes J_{F}T_{F}J_{F}^{-1}.

The conditions (
33
) then become

(48)

\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\mathbb{I}\otimes[T_{F},J_{F}T_{F}J_{F}^{-1}]=0,\quad\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\gamma_{M}f\otimes[m,J_{F}T_{F}J_{F}^{-1}]=0\quad\forall f\otimes m\in{\mathcal{A}}.

This is equivalent to (
46
) using that

[m,J_{F}T_{F}J_{F}^{-1}]=0
is equivalent to

[T_{F},J_{F}mJ_{F}^{-1}]~=~0
.
∎

Since
T_{F}
commutes with the representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{F}
by (
45
), the two
conditions (
46
) are automatically satisfied if
T_{F}
commutes or
anticommutes with
J_{F}
, as required for a grading operator. However, by the
order zero condition of the initial triple, these
conditions together with (
45
) are also satisfied if
T_{F}
is the representation of any
element in the center of
{\mathcal{A}}_{F}
, not necessarily a grading. Furthermore these are not the only
possibilities: think for instance of a spectral triple obtained by
taking a subalgebra
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{F}
of a spectral triple
({\mathcal{A}}_{F},{\mathcal{H}}_{F},D_{F})
. Then any elements in the center of
{\mathcal{A}}_{F}
can be taken as

T_{F}
, even if it is not in
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{F}
.

6.
Twisted first order condition

The operator
T_{F}
is further constrained if one takes into
account the twisted version of the first order condition:

(49)

[[D,\pi((a,a^{\prime}))]_{\rho},\,J\pi_{0}((b^{*},{b^{\prime}}^{*}))J^{-1}]_{\rho^{\circ}}=0\quad\forall(a,a^{\prime}),\,(b,b^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}

where
\rho^{\circ}
is the automorphism of the opposite algebra

({\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{\circ}\simeq{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ}\oplus{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ}
induced by
\rho
:

(50)

\rho^{\circ}((a^{\circ},{a^{\prime}}^{\circ})):=(\rho^{-1}((a,a^{\prime}))^{\circ}=(a^{\prime},a)^{\circ}=({a^{\prime}}^{\circ},a^{\circ}).

Proposition 6.1
.

Consider the minimal twist by
T
of a real spectral triple, such that the order zero condition holds. Then the twisted first-order condition
holds if and only if

(51)

\left\{\left\{D,T\right\},JTJ^{-1}\right\}=0\quad\text{ and }\quad[\left\{D,T\right\},J\,\pi_{0}(a)\,J^{-1}]=0\quad\forall a\in{\mathcal{A}}.

Proof.

For the representation (
12
) and omitting the symbol
of representation
\pi_{0}
, one has

[D,\pi(a,a^{\prime})]_{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}D\left((\mathbb{I}+T)a+(\mathbb{I}-T)a^{\prime}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left((\mathbb{I}+T)a^{\prime}+(\mathbb{I}-T)a\right)D=\frac{1}{2}[D,a+a^{\prime}]+\frac{1}{2}\left\{D,T(a-a^{\prime})\right\},

while, denoting
\hat{b}:=JbJ^{-1}
the conjugation by
J
,

(52)

J\pi(b,b^{\prime})J^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}J\left((\mathbb{I}+T)b+(\mathbb{I}-T)b^{\prime}\right)J^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{b}+\widehat{b^{\prime}})+JT(b-b^{\prime})J^{-1}.

So the twisted commutator (
49
) (with
b,b^{\prime}
instead of
b^{*},{b^{\prime}}^{*})
is the sum of a term of order

0
in
T
,

(53)

\frac{1}{4}\left[[D,a+a^{\prime}],\hat{b}+\widehat{b^{\prime}}\right]

a term of order
1
,

(54)

\frac{1}{4}\left\{[D,a+a^{\prime}],JT(b-b^{\prime})J^{-1}\right\}+\frac{1}{4}\left[\left\{D,T(a-a^{\prime})\right\},\hat{b}+\hat{b}^{\prime}\right],

and a term of order
2
,

(55)

\frac{1}{4}\left\{\left\{D,T(a-a^{\prime})\right\},JT(b-b^{\prime})J^{-1}\right\}.

The term of order
0
is always zero by the first order condition of the
initial triple. The first component of the term of order
1
must
vanish independently: indeed, for
a=a^{\prime}
, the second term in (
54
) as well as the term of order
2
are zero, so the twisted
first-order condition reduces to

(56)

\left\{[D,\alpha],\hat{T}\hat{\beta}^{\prime}\right\}=0\quad\forall\alpha=a+a^{\prime},\;\beta^{\prime}=b-b^{\prime}.

By the twisted
first-order condition of the initial triple, one has

(57)

\displaystyle\left\{[D,\alpha],\hat{T}\hat{\beta}^{\prime}\right\}

\displaystyle=[D,\alpha]\hat{T}\hat{\beta}^{\prime}+\hat{T}\hat{\beta}^{\prime}[D,\alpha]=[D,\alpha]\hat{T}\hat{\beta}^{\prime}+\hat{T}[D,\alpha]\hat{\beta}^{\prime},

(58)

\displaystyle=\left\{D,\hat{T}\right\}\alpha\hat{\beta}^{\prime}-\alpha\left\{D,\hat{T}\right\}\hat{\beta}^{\prime}

where the second equation follows developing the commutators, and
using that
\hat{T}
commutes with
\alpha
by proposition

5.1
. In particular, for
\beta=1_{\mathcal{A}}
one gets that (
56
)
- hence the twisted first-order condition - implies

(59)

\left[\left\{D,\hat{T}\right\},\alpha\right]=0\quad\forall\alpha\in{\mathcal{A}}.

As well, the term of order
2
must vanish independently: for
a=-a^{\prime}
,

b^{\prime}=-1_{\mathcal{A}}
,
b=-2b^{\prime}
then the term of order
1
vanishes, so the
twisted first-order condition reduces to

(60)

\left\{\left\{D,T\alpha^{\prime}\right\},\hat{T}\right\}=0\quad\forall\alpha^{\prime}=a-a^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}.

In particular, for
\alpha^{\prime}=1_{\mathcal{A}}
, one gets that the twisted
first-order condition implies

(61)

\left\{\left\{D,T\right\},\hat{T}\right\}=0.

Therefore (
59
) and (
61
) are necessary to
get the twisted first-order condition. Let us show they are
sufficient conditions. If (
59
) holds, then (
58
)
vanishes for any
\alpha,\hat{\beta}^{\prime}
, meaning the first component of the
term of order
1
vanishes for any
a,a^{\prime},b,b^{\prime}
. The same is true for
the second component since, with
\beta=\hat{b}+\hat{b}^{\prime}
, the later writes

(62)

\displaystyle\left[\left\{D,T\alpha^{\prime}\right\},\hat{\beta}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[[D,\alpha^{\prime}]T,\hat{\beta}\right]+\left[\alpha^{\prime}\left\{D,T\right\},\hat{\beta}\right]

where we use

(63)

\left\{D,T\alpha^{\prime}\right\}=[D,\alpha^{\prime}]T+\alpha^{\prime}\left\{D,T\right\}

obtained by direct computation, with
T
commuting with

\alpha^{\prime}
by definition of twisting operator. The first
commutator in (
62
) vanishes because
\hat{\beta}
commutes with
both
[D,\alpha^{\prime}]
(by the first order condition of the initial
triple) and with
T
(by the second equation (
33
) rewritten as
[T,\hat{a}]=0
for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
). The second commutator in (
62
)
vanishes as well since
\bar{\beta}
commutes with both
\alpha^{\prime}
(by
the order zero condition of the initial triple) and with

\left\{D,T\right\}
by (
59
) rewritten as

(64)

\left[\left\{D,T\right\},\hat{\alpha}\right]=0\quad\forall a\in{\mathcal{A}}.

Finally, by (
63
) the term of order
2
writes

(65)

\left\{\left\{D,T\alpha^{\prime}\right\},\hat{T}\hat{\beta}^{\prime}\right\}=\left\{[D,\alpha^{\prime}]T,\hat{T}\hat{\beta}^{\prime}\right\}+\left\{\alpha^{\prime}\left\{D,T\right\},\hat{T}\hat{\beta}^{\prime}\right\}.

The second anti-commutator vanishes, for
\hat{T}\hat{\beta}^{\prime}

anticommutes with
\left\{D,T\right\}
(by (
61
) and
(
64
)) but commutes with
\alpha
(by (
33
) and the
order zero condition). The first anti-commutator vanishes as well, for

\hat{T}\hat{\beta}^{\prime}
commutes with
T
(by the first equation
(
33
) and the order zero condition) while it anticommutes with

[D,\alpha^{\prime}]T
. The latter assertion follows from the observation that

\hat{T}\hat{\beta}^{\prime}
anticommutes with
[D,\alpha^{\prime}]
, since
\hat{\beta}^{\prime}

commutes with it (by the first order condition of the initial triple)
while
T
anti-commutes with
[D,\alpha^{\prime}]
, as can be seen
from (
64
) noticing that

(66)

\displaystyle\left[\left\{D,T\right\},\hat{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle=DT\hat{\alpha}+TD\hat{\alpha}-\hat{\alpha}DT-\hat{\alpha}TD,

(67)

\displaystyle=[D,\hat{\alpha}]T+T[D,\hat{\alpha}]=\left\{[D,\hat{\alpha}],T\right\}.

So (
61
) and (
59
) (or equivalently (
64
))
are equivalent with the twisted first-order condition. Hence the result. ∎

For an almost commutative geometry, these conditions are equivalent to
their restriction of the finite dimensional space.

Corollary 6.1.1
.

Consider the minimal twist of a real, almost commutative, geometry by

T=\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes T_{F}
such that the order zero condition
holds. Then the twisted first-order condition holds if and only if

(68)

\left\{\left\{D_{F},T_{F}\right\},J_{F}T_{F}J_{F}^{-1}\right\}=0\quad\text{ and }\quad[\left\{D_{F},T_{F}\right\},J_{F}\,\pi_{F}(m)\,J_{F}^{-1}]=0\quad\forall m\in{\mathcal{A}}_{F}.

Proof.

For
D
as in (

, one has

(69)

\left\{D,T\right\}=\left\{\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes D_{F},\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes T_{F}\right\}=\mathbb{I}\otimes\left\{D_{F},T_{F}\right\},

so the second equation (

\left\{\left\{D_{F},T_{F}\right\},J_{F}T_{F}J_{F}^{-1}\right\}=0
. The
first equation (

(70)

0=\left\{\mathbb{I}\otimes\left\{D_{F},T_{F}\right\},{\mathcal{J}}\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}\otimes J_{F}T_{F}J_{F}^{-1}\right\}={\mathcal{J}}\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{J}}^{-1}\otimes\left\{\left\{D_{F},T_{F}\right\},J_{F}T_{F}J_{F}^{-1}\right\}

which is equivalent to the first equation (

These conditions are automatically satisfied if

this may not be the only possibility.

Conclusion

In recent time several modifiations of the framework of

beyond the Standard Model: removing the first order condition

, twisting the real
structure
[

Dabrowski:2019ab

[

,working in the

geometric background

[

]

(see
[

some of these procedures with the minimal twist presented here have
been investigated in
[

regarding the removal of the first order condition
[

and in
[
5

regarding the twisting of the real

[

).

The main result of this note is that the twist-by-grading is not the
only possibility for minimally twisting the Standard Model. It is true
that the minimal twist of an almost commutative geometry by a twisting
operator of the form
{\mathcal{T}}\otimes T_{F}
is a twisted spectral triple if
and only if
{\mathcal{T}}=\gamma_{\mathcal{M}}
. However,
T_{F}
does not need to be a grading of
the finite dimensional space. If one requires the order zero
condition, then

(71)

[T_{F},J_{F}T_{F}J_{F}^{-1}]=0\quad\text{ and }\quad[T_{F},J_{F}\,\pi_{F}(m)\,J_{F}^{-1}]=0\quad\forall m\in{\mathcal{A}}_{F},

and if one requires the twisted first-order condition, then

(72)

\left\{\left\{D_{F},T_{F}\right\},J_{F}T_{F}J_{F}^{-1}\right\}=0\quad\text{ and }\quad[\left\{D_{F},T_{F}\right\},J_{F}\,\pi_{F}(m)\,J_{F}^{-1}]=0\quad\forall m\in{\mathcal{A}}_{F}.

These conditions hold in more generality, for the minimal twist of an
arbitrary (real) spectral triple, as shown in Propositions

5.1
and
6.1
.
Classifying all the solutions of
these constraints for the spectral triple of the Standard Model will be
the object of a future work.

Oligomorphic groups, categories of partial bijections,
and ultrahomogeneous cubic spaces over finite fields

Abstract




We show that for a certain class of oligomorphic groups there is a version of multiplication of double cosets in the Ismagilov–Olshanski sense. Categories of (reduced) double cosets are realized as certain categories of partial bijections.
As an example, we consider the ultrahomogeneous linear space
\mathcal{Z}
of countable dimension over a
finite field equipped with a cubic form.
The group of automorphisms of
\mathcal{Z}
is an oligomorphic group, we
describe its open subgroups.
According Tsankov, this gives
a classification of its unitary representations. The category of reduced double cosets
in this case is the category of partial linear bijections of finite-dimensional cubic spaces preserving cubic forms.

1.1. Multiplication of double cosets.

Let

g\sim h_{1}gh_{2},\quad\text{where $h_{1}\in K_{1}$, $h_{2}\in K_{2}$.}

We denote classes of equivalence (points of the quotient) by

.

Let
\rho
be a unitary representation of a

in a

by
\mathcal{L}^{K}\subset\mathcal{L}
the subspace of
K

we denote the orthogonal projection to

. It is easy to see that an operator

\widehat{\rho}(g):=P^{K}\rho(g)\,P^{K}

depends only on the double coset
\mathfrak{g}=KgK
containing

. Indeed, let
k_{1}
,
k_{2}\in K
,
\xi
,
\eta\in\mathcal{L}
.
Then matrix elements of operators
P^{K}\rho(k_{1}gk_{2})P^{K}
and
P^{K}\rho(g)P^{K}
coincide. Indeed,

\bigl\langle P^{K}\rho(k_{1}gk_{2})P^{K}\xi,\,\eta\bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{L}}=\bigl\langle P^{K}\rho(k_{1})\rho(g)\rho(k_{2})P^{K}\xi,\,\eta\bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{L}}=\\
=\bigl\langle P^{K}\rho(k_{1})\rho(g)P^{K}\xi,\,\eta\bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{L}}=\bigl\langle\rho(k_{1})\rho(g)P^{K}\xi,\,P^{K}\eta\bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{L}}=\\
=\bigl\langle\rho(g)P^{K}\xi,\,\rho(k_{1}^{-1})P^{K}\eta\bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{L}}=\bigl\langle\rho(g)P^{K}\xi,\,P^{K}\eta\bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{L}}=\bigl\langle P^{K}\rho(g)P^{K}\xi,\,\eta\bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{L}}.

Remark.

Decompose

\widetilde{\rho}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathfrak{p})\,\widetilde{\rho}_{\beta,\gamma}(\mathfrak{q})=\widetilde{\rho}_{\alpha,\gamma}(\mathfrak{p}\circ\mathfrak{q})

3.3. Existence of the universal cubic space.

A. Consider a linear space
Z
over
\mathbb{F}
of countable dimension, fix a basis
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
, …. For each non-ordered triple of vectors we assign
a random value

\zeta(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k})\in\mathbb{F}
uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{F}
. We assume that all these values are independent.
We get a random cubic form
\zeta

on

V
. We also can say that we consider a countable direct product of copies of the field
\mathbb{F}

equipped with a uniform measure, factors are enumerated by non-ordered triples of basis elements
(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k})
, which can coincide.

Equivalently, we take an arbitrary exhaustive sequence

X_{1}\subset X_{2}\subset\dots
of finite dimensional subspaces such that
each
X_{j}
is equipped with a uniformly distributed cubic form.
A restriction of forms from
X_{j}
to
X_{j-1}
sends uniform distributions to
uniform distributions; we take the inverse limit.

Consider another exhaustive sequence
Y_{1}\subset Y_{2}\subset\dots
.
Then each
Y_{i}
is contained in some
X_{\alpha(i)}
and each

X_{j}
is contained in some
Y_{\beta(j)}
. So it produces the same
distribution of cubic forms on
Z
.

In particular, the distribution of forms does not depend on a choice of a basis.

B. We claim that
with probability 1 a form
\zeta
satisfies the Urysohn extension property
. Indeed, consider a finite dimensional cubic space
W

and its subspace
V
of codimension 1. Fix an isocubic embedding

\iota:V\to(Z,\zeta)
. Fix a basis

e_{1}
, …,
e_{k}
in
\iota(V)
and complete it to a basis
\{e_{\alpha}\}
in
Z
.
For each
\alpha>k
we consider the subspace
W_{\alpha}
generated by
\iota(V)

and
e_{\alpha}
.
The cubic form on
W_{\alpha}
is determined by a collection of

k(k+1)/2+k+1

numbers

(3.1)

\zeta(e_{i},e_{j},e_{\alpha}),\quad\zeta(e_{i},e_{\alpha},e_{\alpha}),\quad\zeta(e_{\alpha},e_{\alpha},e_{\alpha})\in\mathbb{F},

where
i
,
j\leqslant k
. With probability

q^{-(k+1)(k+2)/2}
this space is isocubic to
W
. Since for different
\alpha
collections
(
3.1
) are independent, with probability
1
we meet a subspace
isocubic to
W
.

The set of possible pairs of subspaces

is countable, the set of embeddings
V\to Z
is countable, so with probability 1 the extension of an embedding

V\to\mathcal{Z}
is possible for any pair
V\subset W
. The Urysohn extension property
is proved.

C. Now the
universality
is obvious – we take a basis
f_{1}
, …,
f_{k}
in a cubic space
Y
, denote by
Y_{j}\subset Y
the linear span of
f_{1}
, …,
f_{j}
. By the Urysohn property we can extend an
isocubic embedding
Y_{j}\to\mathcal{Z}
to an isocubic embedding

Y_{j+1}\to\mathcal{Z}
. We start with
Y_{0}=0
and come to
Y_{k}=Y
.

D. Let us verify the
ultrahomogeneity
.
Let
P_{k}
,
Q_{k}
be two
k
-dimensional isocubic subspaces in a space

\mathcal{Z}
satisfying
the Urysohn property.
Consider a sequence
a_{j}
containing once all elements of

\mathcal{Z}\setminus P_{k}

and a sequence
b_{j}
containing once all elements of

\mathcal{Z}\setminus Q_{k}
.

Denote
P_{k+1}:=P_{k}\dotplus\mathbb{F}a_{1}
.
We extend the map
\iota_{k}:P_{k}\to Q_{k}
to an isocubic embedding

\iota_{k+1}:P_{k+1}\to\mathcal{Z}
. Denote the image by
Q_{k+1}
.
Remove from the sequence
a_{j}
all elements that are contained
in
P_{k+1}
and shift numeration of remaining elements
preserving their order. Perform the same procedure with
Q_{k+1}

and the sequence
b_{k}
.

Now we denote

Q_{k+2}=Q_{k+1}\dotplus\mathbb{F}b_{1}
(with new
b_{1}
), take an isocubic embedding

\iota_{k+2}^{-1}:Q_{k+2}\to\mathcal{Z}
, and denote the image by
P_{k+2}
.
We repeat the same operation a countable number of times, interlacing
P_{m}
and
Q_{m}
, and come to an isocubic bijection
\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
sending
P_{k}\to Q_{k}
.

E. It remains to verify the
uniqueness
of
\mathcal{Z}
. We take two cubic spaces
\mathcal{Z}_{1}
,
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
satisfying the Urysohn property and repeat the arguments from the proof of the ultrahomogeneity starting
from
P_{0}=0
in
\mathcal{Z}_{1}
and
Q_{0}=0
in
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
.

\square

let
V:=\operatorname{dom}\theta
. Consider a sequence of
finite-dimensional subspaces

V_{1}\subset V_{2}\subset V_{3}\subset\dots
such that
V=\cup V_{j}
.
Consider a sequence
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
, …consisting of all elements
of the set
\mathcal{Z}\setminus V
. We choose a sequence
g_{k}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Isoc}}\nolimits(\mathcal{Z})

such that for each
k
:

—
g_{k}\Bigr|_{V_{k}}=\theta\Bigr|_{V_{k}}
;

— for each
j<k
,
i<k
we have
g_{k}x_{i}\neq g_{j}x_{i}
.

This is possible due to Neumann Lemma
2.4
. So, for each
x_{m}\in\mathcal{Z}\setminus V

the sequence
g_{j}x_{m}
consists of pairwise different elements.
On the other hand, each
v\in V
is contained in
V_{k}
with sufficiently
large
k
, so
g_{k}v=\theta v
. Therefore,
g_{k}
converges to

\theta
.

c) It is sufficient to verify this for irreducible representations.
We use the model of irreducible representations as Subsect.
2
.

A\subset\mathcal{Z}
be a finite-dimensional subspace,
\sigma

an irreducible representation of
\mathop{\mathrm{Isoc}}\nolimits(A)
. The space

2
.
Operators

are defined

0,\quad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

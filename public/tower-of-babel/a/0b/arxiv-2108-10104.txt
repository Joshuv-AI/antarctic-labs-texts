Representations of the orthosymplectic Yangian
A. I. Molev

Abstract
We give a complete description of the finite-dimensional irreducible representations of the Yangian associated with the orthosymplectic Lie superalgebra 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 1 | 2 \mathfrak{osp}_{1|2} . The representations are parameterized by monic polynomials in one variable, they are classified in terms of highest weights. We give explicit constructions of a family of elementary modules of the Yangian and show that a wide class of irreducible representations can be produced by taking tensor products of the elementary modules.



1
Introduction

It is well-known that the classification of finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
the orthosymplectic Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{osp}_{M|2n}
is more complicated than that of its general linear
counterpart
\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}
; see e.g. books
[
6
]
and
[
15
]
. It is therefore
not surprising that
such a disparity extends to the respective Yangians.

The Yangian for
\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}
was introduced by Nazarov
[
16
]
and its finite-dimensional
irreducible representations were classified by Zhang
[
19
]
. The orthosymplectic Yangians

{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|2n})
were
introduced by Arnaudon
et al.

[
1
]
, and the general
classification problem still remains open. Our goal in this paper is to describe
finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
.
One can expect that this simplest case will be instrumental
in solving the general classification problem. In particular,
an extension to
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n}
appears to be straightforward.

According to
[
1
]
, the
R
-matrix originated
in
[
18
]
leads to the
RTT
-type definition
of the extended Yangian
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|2n})
. The Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|2n})
can be regarded
as the quotient of the extended Yangian by the ideal generated by elements of the center, implying that
the finite-dimensional irreducible representations of these two algebras are essentially the same.
An explicit description of the center and the
Hopf algebra structure were given in
[
1
]
.
In the subsequent work
[
2
]
, a Drinfeld-type presentation of the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})

was produced, the double Yangian was constructed and its universal
R
-matrix
was calculated in an explicit form. Applications of the orthosymplectic Yangians to spin chain models
were discussed in
[
3
]
. More recently, linear and quadratic evaluations
in the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|2n})

were investigated in
[
8
]
and
[
10
]
.

By analogy with the
R
-matrix form of its orthogonal and symplectic counterparts,
every finite-dimensional irreducible representation of the
orthosymplectic Yangian is a highest weight representation; cf.
[
4
]
.
For the extended Yangian

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
, this means that
the representations are determined by the triples
(\lambda_{1}(u),\lambda_{2}(u),\lambda_{3}(u))
of formal series

\lambda_{i}(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt[[u^{-1}]]
which should satisfy the consistency condition

\lambda_{1}(u)\lambda_{3}(u+1/2)=\lambda_{2}(u)\lambda_{2}(u+1/2).

(1.1)

The key step in the classification is to find
the conditions on such triples for
the corresponding representation
to be finite-dimensional. Here we need a super-extension of the approach
originally used by Tarasov
[
17
]
and
which had already been adapted and applied to the twisted Yangians associated with the classical
Lie algebras; see
[
14
, Ch. 3 & 4]
. This leads to the following result.

Main Theorem
.

The finite-dimensional irreducible representation of the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})

associated with the highest weight
(\lambda_{1}(u),\lambda_{2}(u),\lambda_{3}(u))

is finite-dimensional if and only if

\frac{\lambda_{2}(u)}{\lambda_{1}(u)}=\frac{P(u+1)}{P(u)}

(1.2)

for some monic polynomial
P(u)
in
u
. Hence, the finite-dimensional irreducible
representations of the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
are parameterized by
monic polynomials
P(u)
.

The parametrization turns out to be the same as for the representations of the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})

given in
[
7
]
, and we will call
P(u)
the
Drinfeld polynomial
of the representation.
The appearance of the consistency conditions (
1.1
)
is best explained by the Gauss decomposition
of the generator matrix for
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
and can be compared
with the similar conditions for the Yangian

{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{o}_{3})
; see
[
12
]
,
[
13
, Sec. 5.3]
, which were
derived in a different way in
[
4
]
. However, unlike the case of
the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
(or
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{o}_{3})
),
there is no epimorphism
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})\to{\rm U}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
, so that in general, representations of

\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}

do not extend to the Yangian.

An essential step in the proof of the Main Theorem,
which will be completed in Sec.
4.4
,
is the analysis of the
elementary modules

L(\alpha,\beta)
over
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
associated with the highest weights of the form

\lambda_{1}(u)=\frac{u+\alpha}{u+\beta},\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda_{2}(u)=1,\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda_{3}(u)=\frac{u+\beta-1/2}{u+\alpha-1/2}.

(1.3)

The corresponding
small Verma module

M(\alpha,\beta)
turns out to be irreducible
if and only if
\beta-\alpha
and
\beta-\alpha+1/2
are not nonnegative integers.
The elementary modules
L(\alpha,\beta)

are the irreducible quotients of
M(\alpha,\beta)
and so they split into three families,
according to these conditions.
The module
L(\alpha,\beta)
is finite-dimensional if and only if
\beta-\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
.
In this case, when regarded as an
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}
-module,
L(\alpha,\beta)

decomposes into the direct sum

L(\alpha,\beta)\cong\bigoplus_{p=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\rfloor}V(\beta-\alpha-2\hskip 1.0ptp),

where
V(\mu)
denotes the
2\mu+1
-dimensional
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}
-module
with the highest weight
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
.
In particular,

\dim L(\alpha,\beta)=\binom{\beta-\alpha+2}{2}.

We construct a basis of each small Verma module
M(\alpha,\beta)
and give explicit formulas for
the action of the generators of
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
. This leads to a corresponding description of all
elementary modules.
We show that, up to twisting by a multiplication automorphism of
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
, every
finite-dimensional irreducible representation of this algebra is isomorphic to the quotient
of the submodule of the tensor product module of the form

L(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes L(\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}),

(1.4)

generated by the tensor product of the highest vectors. The final step is to investigate
irreducibility conditions for such tensor products.

In the case of the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
, an irreducibility criterion for tensor products of
evaluation modules
was given by Chari and Pressley
[
5
]
; see also
[
14
, Ch. 3]
.
Such tensor products exhaust all finite-dimensional irreducible
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-modules.
This property turns out not to extend to representations of the Yangian for
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}
;
see Example
4.21
below. A wide class of irreducible modules can still be constructed
explicitly via tensor products of the form (
1.4
); see Theorem
4.17
.

Sign conventions, definitions and preliminaries

Consider the three-dimensional space
\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2}
over
\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt
with basis elements
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}

where we assume a
\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{2}
-gradation defined by setting that the vector
e_{2}
is even and
the vectors
e_{1}
and
e_{3}
are odd. It will be convenient to use the involution
on the set
\{1,2,3\}
defined by
i\mapsto i^{\prime}=4-i
.
The endomorphism algebra
{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2}
then gets a
\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{2}
-gradation with
the parity of the matrix unit
e_{ij}
found by

i+j\mod 2
.

We will consider
3\times 3
matrices with entries in superalgebras. All such
matrices will be even so that the
(i,j)
entry will have the parity
i+j\mod 2
.
We want to posit that the product of two matrices with entries in a superalgebra
\mathcal{A}

is calculated in the standard way (without any additional signs). Accordingly, the algebra of
even matrices over
\mathcal{A}
will be identified with the tensor product algebra

{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2}\otimes\mathcal{A}
. A matrix
A=[a_{ij}]
will be regarded as the element

A=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}e_{ij}\otimes a_{ij}(-1)^{i\hskip 1.0ptj+j}\in{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2}\otimes\mathcal{A}.

We will use the involutive matrix
super-transposition

t
defined by

(A^{t})_{ij}=A_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}(-1)^{ij+j}\hskip 1.0pt\theta_{i}\theta_{j}
,
where we set

\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}=1\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\theta_{3}=-1.

This super-transposition is associated with the bilinear form on the space
\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2}

defined by the anti-diagonal matrix
G=[\delta_{ij^{\prime}}\hskip 1.0pt\theta_{i}]
.
We will also regard
t
as the linear map

t:{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2}\to{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2},\hskip 20.00003pte_{ij}\mapsto e_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}(-1)^{ij+i}\hskip 1.0pt\theta_{i}\theta_{j}.

(2.1)

In the case of multiple tensor products of the endomorphism algebras,
we will indicate by
t_{a}
the map (
2.1
)
acting on the
a
-th copy of
{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2}
.

A standard basis of the general linear Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{1|2}
is formed by elements
E_{ij}

of the parity
i+j\mod 2
for
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant 3
with the commutation relations

[E_{ij},E_{kl}]=\delta_{kj}\,E_{i\hskip 1.0ptl}-\delta_{i\hskip 1.0ptl}\,E_{kj}(-1)^{(i+j)(k+l)}.

We will regard the orthosymplectic Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}

associated with the bilinear from defined by
G
as the subalgebra
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{1|2}
spanned by the elements

F_{ij}=E_{ij}-E_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}(-1)^{i\hskip 1.0ptj+i}\,\theta_{i}\theta_{j}.

For any given
\mu\in\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt
we will denote by
V(\mu)
the irreducible highest weight module over

\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}
generated by a nonzero vector
\xi
such that

F_{11}\xi=\mu\,\xi\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003ptF_{12}\hskip 1.0pt\xi=0.

The module
V(\mu)
is finite-dimensional if and only if
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
. In that case,

\dim V(\mu)=2\mu+1
.

Introduce the permutation operator
P
as the element

P=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}e_{ij}\otimes e_{ji}(-1)^{j}\in{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2}\otimes{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2}

and set

Q=P^{\hskip 1.0ptt_{1}}=P^{\hskip 1.0ptt_{2}}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}e_{ij}\otimes e_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(-1)^{ij}\,\theta_{i}\theta_{j}\in{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2}\otimes{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2}.

The rational function in
u
given by

R(u)=1-\frac{P}{u}+\frac{Q}{u-\kappa},\hskip 20.00003pt\kappa=-3/2,

is an
R
-
matrix
, it satisfies
the
Yang–Baxter equation
as originally found in
[
18
]
. The
R
-matrices produced
in that paper are known to extend to the Brauer algebra so that
the Yang–Baxter equation can be verified
by taking a suitable Brauer algebra representation
in tensor products of the
\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{2}
-graded spaces; cf.
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
.

Following
[
1
]
,
define the
extended Yangian

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})

as an associative superalgebra with generators

t_{ij}^{(r)}
of parity
i+j\mod 2
, where
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant 3
and
r=1,2,\dots
,
satisfying certain quadratic relations. In order to write them down,
introduce the formal series

t_{ij}(u)=\delta_{ij}+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}t_{ij}^{(r)}\,u^{-r}\in{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})[[u^{-1}]]

(2.2)

and combine them into the
3\times 3
matrix
T(u)=[t_{ij}(u)]
so that

T(u)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}e_{ij}\otimes t_{ij}(u)(-1)^{i\hskip 1.0ptj+j}\in{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2}\otimes{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})[[u^{-1}]].

Consider the algebra

{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2}\otimes{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2}\otimes{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})[[u^{-1}]]

and introduce its elements
T_{1}(u)
and
T_{2}(u)
by

T_{1}(u)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}e_{ij}\otimes 1\otimes t_{ij}(u)(-1)^{i\hskip 1.0ptj+j},\hskip 20.00003ptT_{2}(u)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}1\otimes e_{ij}\otimes t_{ij}(u)(-1)^{i\hskip 1.0ptj+j}.

The defining relations for the superalgebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
can then
be written in the form of the
RTT
-

R(u-v)\,T_{1}(u)\,T_{2}(v)=T_{2}(v)\,T_{1}(u)\,R(u-v).

(2.3)

As shown in
[
1
]
, the product
T(u)\,T^{\hskip 1.0ptt}(u-\kappa)
is a scalar matrix with

T(u-\kappa)\,T^{\hskip 1.0ptt}(u)=c(u)1,

(2.4)

where
c(u)
is a series in

. All its coefficients belong to
the center
{\rm ZX}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
of
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})

The
Yangian

{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})

is defined as the subalgebra of

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
which
consists of the elements stable under
the automorphisms

t_{ij}(u)\mapsto f(u)\,t_{ij}(u)

(2.5)

for all series

f(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt[[u^{-1}]]
.
We have the tensor product decomposition

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})={\rm ZX}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})\otimes{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}).

(2.6)

The Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
can be equivalently defined as the quotient
of
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})

by the relation

T(u-\kappa)\,T^{\hskip 1.0ptt}(u)=1.

We will also use a more explicit form of the defining relations (
2.3
)
written in terms of the series (
2.2
) as follows:

\displaystyle[\hskip 1.0ptt_{ij}(u),t_{kl}(v)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u-v}\big(t_{kj}(u)\,t_{il}(v)-t_{kj}(v)\,t_{il}(u)\big)(-1)^{i\hskip 1.0ptj+i\hskip 1.0ptk+j\hskip 1.0ptk}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{u-v-\kappa}\Big(\delta_{ki^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}}\sum_{p=1}^{3}\,t_{pj}(u)\,t_{p^{\prime}l}(v)(-1)^{i+i\hskip 1.0ptj+j\hskip 1.0ptp}\,\theta_{i}\theta_{p}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt{}-\delta_{lj^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}}\sum_{p=1}^{3}\,t_{k\hskip 1.0ptp^{\prime}}(v)\,t_{ip}(u)(-1)^{j+p+i\hskip 1.0ptk+j\hskip 1.0ptk+i\hskip 1.0ptp}\,\theta_{j}\theta_{p}\Big).

The coefficients of the series
t_{11}(u),t_{12}(u),t_{21}(u)
and
c(u)

generate the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
.
The mapping

t_{ij}(u)\mapsto t_{ij}(-u)

defines an anti-automorphism of
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
, while each of the mappings

t_{ij}(u)\mapsto t_{ij}(u+a),\hskip 10.00002pta\in\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt,

(2.8)

and

t_{ij}(u)\mapsto t_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(u)\,\theta_{i}\theta_{j}

defines an automorphism. Consider their composition to define the anti-automorphism

\omega:t_{ij}(u)\mapsto t_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(-u+1/2)\,\theta_{i}\theta_{j}.

(2.9)

The universal enveloping algebra
{\rm U}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
can be regarded as a subalgebra of

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
via the embedding

F_{ij}\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\big(t_{ij}^{(1)}-t_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}^{(1)}(-1)^{j+ij}\,\theta_{i}\theta_{j}\big)(-1)^{i}.

(2.10)

This fact relies on the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem for the orthosymplectic Yangian

[
1
]
and
[
2
]
, as
the associated graded algebra
for
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
is isomorphic to
{\rm U}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}[u])
.
It states that given any total ordering on the set of generators

with
i+j\leqslant 4
and

,
the ordered monomials
in the generators with the powers of odd generators not exceeding

,
form a basis of
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
.
A detailed proof can be carried over by
adapting the arguments of
[
4
, Sec. 3]
to the super case in a straightforward way

).

The extended Yangian
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
is a Hopf algebra with the coproduct
defined by

\Delta:t_{ij}(u)\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{3}t_{ik}(u)\otimes t_{kj}(u).

For the image of the series

we have

Gaussian generators

A Drinfeld-type presentation of the Yangian for
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}
was given in
[
2
]

with the use of the Gauss decomposition of the matrix

some calculations
produced therein and derive consistency relations for the Gaussian generators.

Apply the Gauss decomposition
to the generator matrix

.

The following relations for the Gaussian generators hold:

e_{12}(u)=-e_{23}(u+1/2),\hskip 20.00003ptf_{21}(u)=f_{32}(u+1/2),

(3.2)

Proof.

\displaystyle h_{2}(u)+f_{21}(u)\hskip 1.0pt

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
.
Together with the coefficients of the series
h(u)=h_{1}(u)^{-1}h_{2}(u)

they generate the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
, and the defining relations for these generators
are given in
[
2
]
in a slightly different setting.

4
Yangian representations

4.1
Highest weight representations

A representation
V
of the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})

is called a
highest weight representation

if there exists a nonzero vector

\xi\in V
such that
V
is generated by
\xi
,

\displaystyle t_{ij}(u)\,\xi

\displaystyle=0\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{for}\hskip 10.00002pt1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 3,\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}

\displaystyle t_{ii}(u)\,\xi

\displaystyle=\lambda_{i}(u)\,\xi\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{for}\hskip 10.00002pti=1,2,3,

(4.1)

for some formal series

\lambda_{i}(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt[[u^{-1}]].

(4.2)

The vector
\xi
is called the
highest vector

of
V
and the triple
\lambda(u)=(\lambda_{1}(u),\lambda_{2}(u),\lambda_{3}(u))

is the
highest weight
of
V
.

The quasideterminant formulas for the Gaussian generators
h_{i}(u)
given in Sec.
3

imply that the conditions (
4.1
) in the above definition can be replaced with

h_{i}(u)\,\xi=\lambda_{i}(u)\,\xi\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002pti=1,2,3.

Hence, relation (
3.3
) of
Proposition
3.1
implies the consistency condition (
1.1
)
for the components
\lambda_{i}(u)
of the highest weight.

Given any triple
\lambda(u)=(\lambda_{1}(u),\lambda_{2}(u),\lambda_{3}(u))

of formal series of the form (
4.2
) safisfying the consistency
condition (
1.1
), define
the
Verma module

M(\lambda(u))
as the quotient of
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
by
the left ideal generated by all coefficients of the series
t_{ij}(u)

with
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 3
, and
t_{ii}(u)-\lambda_{i}(u)
for

i=1,2,3
. The Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem implies that the Verma module
M(\lambda(u))

is nonzero and we denote by
L(\lambda(u))
its irreducible quotient.

Remark 4.1
.

In terms of the Drinfeld presentation
of the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
given in
[
2
]
, the highest vector conditions
take the form

e_{12}(u)\,\xi=0\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pth(u)\,\xi=\mu(u)\,\xi

for a series
\mu(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt[[u^{-1}]]
.
The Main Theorem implies that the irreducible highest weight representation of
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})

associated with
\mu(u)
is finite-dimensional if and only if

\mu(u)=\frac{P(u+1)}{P(u)}

for some monic polynomial
P(u)
in
u
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Every finite-dimensional irreducible representation of the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})

is isomorphic to
L(\lambda(u))
for a certain highest weight
\lambda(u)
satisfying (
1.1
).

Proof.

The argument is a straightforward adaptation of the proof of
[
4
, Thm 5.1]
to the super case,
which amounts to taking care of additional signs in the calculations.
∎

To prove the Main Theorem, we only
need to determine which of the modules
L(\lambda(u))
are finite-dimensional.
The first step is to establish some necessary conditions.

Proposition 4.3
.

If the module
L(\lambda(u))
is finite-dimensional, then

\frac{\lambda_{1}(u)}{\lambda_{2}(u)}=\frac{(u+\alpha_{1})\dots(u+\alpha_{k})}{(u+\beta_{1})\dots(u+\beta_{k})}

for

and certain complex numbers
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}

Proof.

We follow the proof of a similar property for the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2})
; see
[

.
Note that due to (
1.1
), the series
\lambda_{3}(u)
is uniquely
determined by
\lambda_{1}(u)
and
\lambda_{2}(u)
, and so we can parameterize
the highest weights by arbitrary pairs of series
\lambda(u)=(\lambda_{1}(u),\lambda_{2}(u))
,
omitting
\lambda_{3}(u)
.
By twisting the action of the extended Yangian
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
on
L(\lambda(u))

by the automorphism (
2.5
)
with
f(u)=\lambda_{2}(u)^{-1}
,
we get an
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
-module isomorphic to

L(\mu(u),1)
with
\mu(u)=\lambda_{1}(u)/\lambda_{2}(u)
.
Let
\xi
denote the highest vector of
L(\mu(u),1)
.
Since this representation is finite-dimensional,
the vectors
t_{21}^{(i)}\xi\in L(\mu(u),1)
with
i\geqslant 1
are
linearly dependent,

\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\,t_{21}^{(i)}\xi=0

with
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt
, assuming
c_{m}\neq 0
. Apply the operators

t_{12}^{(r)}
for all
r\geqslant 1
to the linear combination on the left hand side
and take the coefficient of
\xi
. Since
t_{12}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\xi=0
,
we get from the defining relations (
2.7
) that

t_{12}(u)\hskip 1.0ptt_{21}(v)\hskip 1.0pt\xi=\frac{1}{u-v}\big(t_{22}(u)\,t_{11}(v)-t_{22}(v)\,t_{11}(u)\big)\hskip 1.0pt\xi=-\frac{\mu(u)-\mu(v)}{u-v}\hskip 1.0pt\xi.

Hence, writing

\mu(u)=1+\mu^{(1)}u^{-1}+\mu^{(2)}u^{-2}+\dots,\hskip 20.00003pt\mu^{(i)}\in\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt,

we derive

t_{12}^{(r)}\,t_{21}^{(i)}\xi=\mu^{(r+i-1)}\hskip 1.0pt\xi.

Therefore, for all

we have the relations

\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\hskip 1.0pt\mu^{(r+i-1)}=0.

They imply

\mu(u)\hskip 1.0pt(c_{1}+c_{2}\hskip 1.0ptu+\dots+c_{m}\hskip 1.0ptu^{m-1})=(b_{1}+b_{2}\hskip 1.0ptu+\dots+b_{m}\hskip 1.0ptu^{m-1})

so that

can be written as a rational function in

∎

We will use the name
elementary module
for the module

with

\lambda_{1}(u)=\frac{u+\alpha}{u+\beta}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda_{2}(u)=1

and denote it by

.

The Hopf algebra structure on the extended Yangian

allows us to regard

as
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
-modules. Let
\xi^{(i)}
denote the highest vector of

Proposition 4.4
.

The
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
-module
L(\lambda(u))
with

\lambda_{1}(u)=\frac{(u+\alpha_{1})\dots(u+\alpha_{k})}{(u+\beta_{1})\dots(u+\beta_{k})}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda_{2}(u)=1

The coproduct formula (

4.2

Note that by twisting the action of the extended Yangian

4.3

Let

Proposition 4.5
.

The module

is spanned by the vectors

t_{31}^{(2)\hskip 1.0ptr}\hskip 1.0ptt_{21}^{(1)\hskip 1.0pts}\,\xi,\hskip 20.00003ptr,s\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}.

Proof.

By the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem for the extended Yangian, the Verma module

has the basis

t_{31}^{(k_{1})}\dots t_{31}^{(k_{p})}\hskip 1.0ptt_{21}^{(l_{1})}\dots t_{21}^{(l_{q})}\hskip 1.0pt\xi,

(4.9)

where

and

on the length of the monomial in (

of the property that

and

The defining relations (

Furthermore, since

∎

We will regard

as an

We get the weight space decomposition

(4.11)

For all values

Proposition 4.6
.

All operators

on the small Verma module

Proof.

Calculating modulo

t_{21}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\xi=u^{-1}\hskip 1.0ptt_{21}^{(1)}\hskip 1.0pt\xi\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003ptt_{31}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\xi=\big(u^{-1}\hskip 1.0ptt_{31}^{(1)}+\frac{1}{u(u+\alpha-1/2)}t_{31}^{(2)}\big)\hskip 1.0pt\xi

on

By acting on the vectors (

and
t_{21}^{(1)}

for the operator

As a next step, consider the relations for the series

Lemma 4.8

In the module

. That is,

We will now use the results of the previous

and
equating the coefficient in question to zero, we get

b\hskip 1.0pt(u+\alpha_{1}+r_{0}-1/2)\hskip 1.0pt\prod_{i=2}^{k}(u+\alpha_{i}-1/2)(u+\beta_{i})\\
+(u+\alpha_{1}+r_{0}-1/2)(u+\alpha_{1}+s_{0}-1)\sum_{l=2}^{k}b_{l}\,\prod_{i=2}^{l-1}(u+\alpha_{i}-1/2)(u+\alpha_{i})\\
{}\times(u+\alpha_{l}-1/2)\prod_{i=l+1}^{k}(u+\alpha_{i}-1/2)(u+\beta_{i})=0,

(4.22)

where
b_{l}
are some constants, while
b
is a nonzero constant because of the condition

s_{0}\leqslant\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}
in the case
\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
implied by
Proposition
4.12
.
By cancelling the common factors and setting
u=-\alpha_{1}-s_{0}+1
we get

\prod_{i=2}^{k}(\beta_{i}-\alpha_{1}-s_{0}+1)=0.

It follows from this relation that the multiset

\{\beta_{i}-\alpha_{1}\ |\ i=1,\dots,k\}_{+}
is not empty,
because
\beta_{i}-\alpha_{1}=s_{0}-1\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
for some
i\in\{2,\dots,k\}
.
By assumption (1) of the theorem, we have

\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
and
\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}\leqslant\beta_{i}-\alpha_{1}
.
However, this makes a contradiction, as
by Proposition
4.12
we must have
s_{0}\leqslant\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}
.

Excluding the condition
r_{0}<s_{0}
in (
4.21
), we show next that
the condition
r_{0}=s_{0}\geqslant 1
is impossible either.
If this condition holds, consider the coefficient of
the vector
\xi_{r_{0}-1,r_{0}}^{(1)}\otimes\xi^{\prime}
in the relation
T_{12}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\zeta=0
.
This coefficient can only arise from the terms

T_{12}(u)\,\xi_{r_{0},r_{0}}^{(1)}\otimes T_{22}(u)\,\xi^{\prime}\pm T_{11}(u)\,\xi_{r_{0}-1,r_{0}}^{(1)}\otimes T_{12}(u)\,\zeta_{r_{0}-1,r_{0}}.

By the
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}
-weight condition,

\zeta_{r_{0}-1,r_{0}}=\sum_{l=2}^{k}c_{l}\,\xi^{(2)}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi_{01}^{(l)}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi^{(k)}

for some constants
c_{l}\in\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt
. Calculating as in the previous case,
we now come to the relation

b\hskip 1.0pt(u+\alpha_{1}+r_{0})\hskip 1.0pt\prod_{i=2}^{k}(u+\alpha_{i}-1/2)(u+\beta_{i})\\
+(u+\alpha_{1}+r_{0}-3/2)(u+\alpha_{1}+r_{0})\sum_{l=2}^{k}b_{l}\,\prod_{i=2}^{l-1}(u+\alpha_{i}-1/2)(u+\alpha_{i})\\
{}\times(u+\alpha_{l}-1/2)\prod_{i=l+1}^{k}(u+\alpha_{i}-1/2)(u+\beta_{i})=0,

(4.23)

where
b_{l}
are some constants, while
b
is a nonzero constant. The latter property
holds
because of the condition

r_{0}\leqslant\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}+1/2
in the case
\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}+1/2\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
implied by
Proposition
4.13
.
Cancel the common factors and set
u=-\alpha_{1}-r_{0}+3/2
to get

\prod_{i=2}^{k}(\beta_{i}-\alpha_{1}-r_{0}+3/2)=0.

This means that for some
i\in\{2,\dots,k\}
we have
\beta_{i}-\alpha_{1}+1/2=r_{0}-1\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
.
If the multiset
\{\beta_{1}-\alpha_{j},\ \beta_{j}-\alpha_{1}\ |\ j=1,\dots,k\}_{+}

is not empty,
then by assumption (1) of the theorem, we have

\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
and
\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}\leqslant\beta_{i}-\alpha_{1}+1/2
.
This is impossible because
by Proposition
4.12
we must have
r_{0}\leqslant\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}
.
Hence assumption (2) of the theorem for
h=1
should apply,
and we have
\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}+1/2\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
together with the inequality

\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}+1/2\leqslant\beta_{i}-\alpha_{1}+1/2.

This makes a contradiction, as
by Proposition
4.13
we must have
r_{0}\leqslant\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}+1/2
.

We have thus showed that any vector
\zeta\in L

with
T_{12}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\zeta=0
is proportional to
\xi^{(1)}\otimes\xi^{\prime}
.
By looking at the set of
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}
-weights
of any nonzero submodule of
L
we derive that such a submodule
must contain a nonzero vector
\zeta
with
T_{12}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\zeta=0
,
and so contain the vector
\xi^{(1)}\otimes\xi^{\prime}
.
It remains to prove this vector is cyclic in
L
.

Consider the vector space
L^{*}
dual to
L
which is
spanned by all linear maps
\sigma:L\to\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt

satisfying the condition that the linear span of the vectors
\eta\in L

such that
\sigma(\eta)\neq 0
, is finite-dimensional.
Equip
L^{*}
with an
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
-module structure
by setting

(x\,\sigma)(\eta)=\sigma(\omega(x)\,\eta)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002ptx\in{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma\in L^{*},\ \eta\in L,

(4.24)

where
\omega
is the anti-automorphism of the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})

defined in (
2.9
).
It is easy to verify that
L^{*}
is isomorphic to
the tensor
product module

L(-\beta_{1},-\alpha_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes L(-\beta_{k},-\alpha_{k}).

(4.25)

Moreover, the highest vector of the module
L(-\beta_{i},-\alpha_{i})

can be identified with the dual basis vector
\xi^{(i)\hskip 1.0pt*}
.
Suppose now that the submodule
N={\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})(\xi^{(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi^{(k)})

of
L
is proper and consider
its annihilator

{\rm{Ann}\,}N=\{\rho\in L^{*}\ |\ \rho(\eta)=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all}\hskip 10.00002pt\eta\in N\}.

(4.26)

Then
{\rm{Ann}\,}N

is a nonzero submodule of
L^{*}
, which does not
contain the vector
\xi^{(1)\hskip 1.0pt*}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi^{(k)\hskip 1.0pt*}
.
However, this contradicts the claim verified in the first part of the proof,
because the conditions on the parameters
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}

stated in the theorem
will remain satisfied after we replace each
\alpha_{i}
by
-\beta_{i}
and each

\beta_{i}
by
-\alpha_{i}
.
∎

Proposition 4.18
.

Suppose that the
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
-module
L(\lambda(u))
with
the highest weight (
4.5
) is finite-dimensional.
Then for any nonnegative integers
l_{1},\dots,l_{k}
and
m_{1},\dots,m_{k}

the module
L(\lambda^{+}(u))
with
the highest weight

\lambda^{+}_{1}(u)=\frac{(u+\alpha_{1}-l_{1})\dots(u+\alpha_{k}-l_{k})}{(u+\beta_{1}+m_{1})\dots(u+\beta_{k}+m_{k})}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda^{+}_{2}(u)=1

(4.27)

is also finite-dimensional.

Proof.

The highest weight module
L(\lambda^{+}(u))
is isomorphic to an irreducible
subquotient of the finite-dimensional
module

L(\lambda(u))\otimes L(\alpha_{1}-l_{1},\alpha_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes L(\alpha_{k}-l_{k},\alpha_{k})\otimes L(\beta_{1},\beta_{1}+m_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes L(\beta_{k},\beta_{k}+m_{k})

and hence is finite-dimensional.
∎

We now return to proving the Main Theorem. Let
the irreducible highest weight module
L(\lambda(u))
with
the highest weight (
4.5
) be finite-dimensional.
To argue by contradiction, suppose that it is impossible to renumber
the parameters
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}

in such a way that all differences
\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}
with

i=1,\dots,k
belong to
\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
. By Proposition
4.18
,
all modules
L(\lambda^{+}(u))
with
the highest weight of the form
(
4.27
) are also finite-dimensional. It is possible to
choose nonnegative integers
l_{i}
and
m_{i}

to ensure that the assumptions of Theorem
4.17
are satisfied by
the shifted parameters
\alpha^{\prime}_{i}=\alpha_{i}-l_{i}
and
\beta^{\prime}_{i}=\beta_{i}+m_{i}
, after a possible
renumbering. This can be done by induction, beginning
with the multiset

\{\beta_{1}-\alpha_{i},\ \beta_{i}-\alpha_{1}\ |\ i=1,\dots,k\}

and renumbering
the parameters
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
, if necessary, to ensure that
\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}

is a minimal element of the multiset

\{\beta_{1}-\alpha_{i},\ \beta_{i}-\alpha_{1}\ |\ i=1,\dots,k\}_{+}

(4.28)

if it is nonempty. Then assumption (1) of the theorem for
h=1
is achieved by suitable shifts

\alpha_{i}\mapsto\alpha_{i}-l_{i}
and
\beta_{i}\mapsto\beta_{i}+m_{i}
for
i=2,\dots,k
.
If the multiset (
4.28
) is empty, then
assumption (2) for
h=1
is achieved by a suitable renumbering
of the parameters
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
. Then we continue in the same way
to consider the multisets for
h=2
, etc. As a result,
by Theorem
4.17
, the module
L(\lambda^{+}(u))
is
isomorphic
to the tensor product of the corresponding elementary modules.
Since it is finite-dimensional, all new differences
\beta^{\prime}_{i}-\alpha^{\prime}_{i}

must be nonnegative integers due to Corollary
4.14
(2).

This argument implies, that
all the differences
\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}
of the original parameters
may be assumed to be integers. Moreover,
we can apply some shifts as given in Proposition
4.18
, to further suppose
that
\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
for
i=1,\dots,k-1
,
while
\alpha_{k}-\beta_{k}\in 1+\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
, and that it is impossible to renumber
the parameters to make all the differences
\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}
nonnegative integers.

Now consider all the parameters
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
which belong to the

\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt
-coset in
\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt
containing
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
. Renumbering them, if necessary, suppose that
they correspond to
i=d+1,\dots,k
for some
d\in\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}
.
After a further renumbering to satisfy the assumptions of
Theorem
4.17
, we obtain
that the
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
-module

L^{(2)}=L(\alpha_{d+1},\beta_{d+1})\otimes\dots\otimes L(\alpha_{k},\beta_{k})

is irreducible. Similarly, by applying suitable shifts of Proposition
4.18

to the remaining parameters
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}

with
i=1,\dots,d
, and possible relabelling,
we may assume that they satisfy the assumptions of
Theorem
4.17
and so
the
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
-module

L^{(1)}=L(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes L(\alpha_{d},\beta_{d})

is also irreducible. If the tensor product
L=L^{(1)}\otimes L^{(2)}
turns out to be
irreducible, then we arrive at a contradiction, because the module
L(\alpha_{k},\beta_{k})

is infinite-dimensional. So we will suppose that
L
is not irreducible and denote by
\mu

the
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}
-weight of the vector
\xi^{(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi^{(k)}
.
Consider the multiset

\{\beta_{i}-\alpha_{j}+1/2\ |\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant d,\hskip 10.00002ptd+1\leqslant j\leqslant k\}_{+}

(4.29)

and let
p_{0}
denote its minimal element, if the multiset is nonempty, or set
p_{0}=+\infty
otherwise.

Lemma 4.19
.

The
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}
-weight component
N_{\mu-p}

of the cyclic span

N={\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})(\xi^{(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi^{(k)})

coincides with

L_{\mu-p}
for all
0\leqslant p\leqslant 2\hskip 1.0ptp_{0}
.

Proof.

Equip
L^{*}
with an
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
-module structure
by using (
4.24
).
By considering the annihilator
{\rm{Ann}\,}N
, as defined by (
4.26
), it will be
sufficient to show that any vector
\zeta\in L^{*}
of the
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}
-weight
\mu-p

with the property
t_{12}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\zeta=0

is proportional to the vector
\xi^{(1)\hskip 1.0pt*}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi^{(k)\hskip 1.0pt*}
.
As before, we will identify
L^{*}
with the tensor product
module (
4.25
) and denote
by
\check{\xi}^{(i)}
the highest vector of the elementary module
L(-\beta_{i},-\alpha_{i})
.
We will now follow the first part of the proof of Theorem
4.17

to derive by a reverse induction on
l\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, beginning with
l=k
, that any vector

\zeta^{(l)}\in L(-\beta_{l},-\alpha_{l})\otimes\dots\otimes L(-\beta_{k},-\alpha_{k})

of the
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}
-weight
\mu-p

with the property
t_{12}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\zeta^{(l)}=0

is proportional to
\check{\xi}^{(l)}\otimes\dots\otimes\check{\xi}^{(k)}
.
This is clear for the values
l=d+1,\dots,k
, because the assumptions of
Theorem
4.17
are satisfied by the corresponding parameters.

Now suppose that
l\in\{1,\dots,d\}
and repeat the argument
of the first part of the proof of Theorem
4.17
to come to the
expression

\zeta^{(l)}=\check{\xi}_{r_{0}s_{0}}^{(l)}\otimes\xi^{\prime}+\sum_{r+s<p}\check{\xi}_{rs}^{(l)}\otimes\zeta_{rs},

analogous to (
4.21
), where
r_{0}+s_{0}=p
and
\xi^{\prime}=\check{\xi}^{(l+1)}\otimes\dots\otimes\check{\xi}^{(k)}
.
Arguing as in that proof, we find that
the condition
r_{0}<s_{0}
is impossible, leading to the only possibility
that
r_{0}=s_{0}\geqslant 1
. In this case, with our conditions of the parameters,
we must have
p=2r_{0}
and

\beta_{l}-\alpha_{j}+1/2=r_{0}-1

(4.30)

for some
d+1\leqslant j\leqslant k
. Since
r_{0}-1\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
,
relation (
4.30
) implies that
p_{0}
has a finite value and

r_{0}>p_{0}
.
This makes a contradiction, because
p=2r_{0}\leqslant 2p_{0}
by the
assumption, thus completing the proof of the lemma.
∎

For any
s\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
set
\eta_{s}=\xi^{(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes\xi^{(k-1)}\otimes\xi^{(k)}_{0\hskip 1.0pts}\in L(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes L(\alpha_{k},\beta_{k})
.

Lemma 4.20
.

In the tensor product module, for any
s\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
we have

\displaystyle T_{21}(-\alpha_{k}-s)\hskip 1.0pt\eta_{s}{}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(\beta_{i}-\alpha_{k}-s)(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{k}-s-1/2)\,\eta_{s+1},

(4.31)

\displaystyle T_{12}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\eta_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{2}\,(\beta_{k}-\alpha_{k}-s+1)\,\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(u+\alpha_{i}-1/2)(u+\alpha_{i})\,\eta_{s-1},

and

\displaystyle T_{11}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\eta_{s}

\displaystyle=(u+\alpha_{k}-1/2)(u+\alpha_{k}+s)\,\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(u+\alpha_{i}-1/2)(u+\alpha_{i})\,\eta_{s}.

Proof.

All relations are immediate from the coproduct rule (
2.11
)
and the formulas for the action of the generators
of the extended Yangian
in the basis
\xi_{rs}
of the elementary module
L(\alpha,\beta)
, which were
recalled in
the beginning of this section. In particular, for (
4.31
) we take into account
the relations
T_{11}(-\alpha_{k}-s)\,\xi^{(k)}_{0\hskip 1.0pts}=0
and

T_{21}(-\alpha_{k}-s)\,\xi^{(k)}_{0\hskip 1.0pts}=\xi^{(k)}_{0,s+1}
in
L(\alpha_{k},\beta_{k})
.
∎

Observe that the numerical coefficient on the right hand side of (
4.31
)
is nonzero for any values of
s
outside the multisets

\{\beta_{i}-\alpha_{k}\ |\ i=d+1,\dots,k-1\}_{+}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\{\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{k}-1/2\ |\ i=1,\dots,d\}_{+}.

(4.32)

On the other hand, recalling that
p_{0}
is the minimal element of the multiset (
4.29
)
when it is nonempty,
note that we can use the shifts of the parameters
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}
with
i=1,\dots,d

as in Proposition
4.18
to keep the assumptions of Theorem
4.17

satisfied. The module
L^{(1)}

with the shifted parameters remains irreducible, while we can make the value of
p_{0}

arbitrarily large. It will be sufficient to make
p_{0}
large enough for the elements
of both multisets in (
4.32
) not to exceed
2p_{0}
, noting that
the elements of the second multiset can only decrease after
the shifts

\alpha_{i}\mapsto\alpha_{i}-l_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,d
.

The
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2}
-weight of the vector
\eta_{s}
equals
\mu-s
, and hence, by
Lemma
4.19
, all vectors
\eta_{s}
with
s\leqslant 2p_{0}

belong to the cyclic span
N={\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})\hskip 1.0pt\eta_{0}
. This property
extends to all values
s\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
by relation (
4.31
) of Lemma
4.20
,
because the numerical coefficient of
\eta_{s+1}
does not vanish for
s>2p_{0}
.
The remaining two relations of Lemma
4.20
imply that
the images of the vectors
\eta_{s}
in the irreducible quotient
L(\lambda(u))
of
N

are linearly independent. Hence,
L(\lambda(u))
is infinite-dimensional, as it contains
an infinite family of linearly independent
vectors. This contradiction completes the proof of the first part of the Main Theorem.
The second part concerning representations of the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})

is immediate from the decomposition (
2.6
); cf.
[
4
, Sec. 5.3]
.

Comparing the irreducibility conditions with those for the evaluation modules over
the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2})
(see e.g.
[
14
, Sec. 3.3]
), note that unlike that case,
it is not possible, in general, to renumber the parameters of the given highest weight (
4.5
)
to satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
4.17
. In fact, not every module

L(\lambda(u))
is isomorphic to a tensor product module of the form (
4.4
),
as illustrated by the following example.

Example 4.21
.

To describe the
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
-module
L(\lambda(u))
with

\lambda_{1}(u)=\frac{(u-1)(u-5/2)}{u\hskip 1.0pt(u-3/2)},\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda_{2}(u)=1,

consider the tensor product
L=L(-1,0)\otimes L(-5/2,-3/2)

of two three-dimensional modules.
Note that its parameters do not satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
4.17
.
The module
L
turns out to have a proper submodule
K
which is generated by the vector

\zeta=\xi_{11}^{(1)}\otimes\xi^{(2)}+3\,\xi_{01}^{(1)}\otimes\xi_{01}^{(2)}-\xi^{(1)}\otimes\xi_{11}^{(2)}.

The submodule
K
is one-dimensional, isomorphic to a highest weight module
L(\mu(u))

with the components

\mu_{1}(u)=\mu_{2}(u)=\frac{(u-1/2)(u-5/2)}{(u-3/2)^{2}}.

The module
L(\lambda(u))
is isomorphic to the quotient
L/K
with
\dim L(\lambda(u))=8
and so does not
admit a tensor product decomposition of the form (
4.4
).
∎

To conclude, we note that by analysing submodules of reducible modules
M(\alpha,\beta)
, we can obtain
explicit constructions of some modules
L(\lambda(u))
beyond the elementary modules. In particular,
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{+}
the submodule of
M(-k)
generated by the vector
\xi_{0,k+1}

is isomorphic to the highest weight module
L(\lambda(u))
with

\lambda_{1}(u)=\frac{u+1}{u}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda_{2}(u)=\frac{(u+1/2)(u-k-1)}{u(u-k-1/2)}.

The vectors
\xi_{rs}
with

r\leqslant s
and
s>k
form its basis, and
the action of the generators is
described in Sec.
4.2
. The character of
L(\lambda(u))
,
as defined in Sec.
4.3
, is found by

{\rm{ch}\,}L(\lambda(u))=\frac{q+q^{2}-q^{k+3}}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})}.

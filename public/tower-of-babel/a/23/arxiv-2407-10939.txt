Teukolsky equations, twistor functions, and conformally self-dual spaces
Bernardo Araneda

Abstract
We prove a correspondence, for Riemannian manifolds with self-dual Weyl tensor, between twistor functions and solutions to the Teukolsky equations for any conformal and spin weights. In particular, we give a contour integral formula for solutions to the Teukolsky equations, and we find a recursion operator that generates an infinite family of solutions and leads to the construction of Čech representatives and sheaf cohomology classes on twistor space. Apart from the general conformally self-dual case, examples include self-dual black holes, scalar-flat Kähler surfaces, and quaternionic-Kähler metrics, where we map the Teukolsky equation to the conformal wave equation, establish new relations to the linearised Przanowski equation, and find new classes of quaternionic deformations.

Preliminaries

2.1
Complex structures and connections

Let
(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})
be a four-dimensional, orientable Riemannian manifold, with Levi-Civita connection
\nabla_{a}
. Let
J^{a}{}_{b}
be a compatible almost-complex structure. The 2-form
g_{ac}J^{c}{}_{b}
is necessarily self-dual or anti-self-dual. Choosing the latter for concreteness,
J^{a}{}_{b}
can be written in terms of its principal spinors as

\displaystyle J^{a}{}_{b}={\rm i}(o^{A}\iota_{B}+\iota^{A}o_{B})\delta^{A^{\prime}}_{B^{\prime}},

(2.1)

where we have chosen the normalisation
o_{A}\iota^{A}=1
. Notice that
o_{A},\iota_{A}
are only defined up to scale: we have the “GHP” freedom

\displaystyle o_{A}\to zo_{A},\qquad\iota_{A}\to z^{-1}\iota_{A},\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}.

(2.2)

In Euclidean signature we have
\iota_{A}=o^{\dagger}_{A}
, and
z\in U(1)
.

The
+{\rm i}
and
-{\rm i}
eigenspaces of
J^{a}{}_{b}
are respectively
T^{+}={\rm span}\{\iota^{A}\partial_{AA^{\prime}}\}
and
T^{-}={\rm span}\{o^{A}\partial_{AA^{\prime}}\}
. The almost-complex structure is integrable iff these eigenspaces are involutive, which can be shown to be equivalent to the “shear-free” equations

\displaystyle\iota^{A}\iota^{B}\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\iota_{B}=0,\qquad o^{A}o^{B}\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}o_{B}=0.

(2.3)

In this case
T^{+}
and
T^{-}
are tangent to (complex) surfaces, which are called twistor surfaces. Conversely, given two linearly independent spinor fields satisfying (
2.3
) and

, the tensor field (
2.1
) is an integrable almost-complex structure.

The triple

is integrable. The tensor field

is conformally invariant: if

\displaystyle\hat{o}_{A}=\Omega^{1/2}o_{A},\quad\hat{\iota}_{A}=\Omega^{1/2}\iota_{A},\qquad\hat{o}^{A}=\Omega^{-1/2}o^{A},\quad\hat{\iota}^{A}=\Omega^{-1/2}\iota^{A}.

We note that, in a complexified picture, one can choose
\hat{o}^{A}=\Omega^{w^{0}}o^{A}
and
\hat{\iota}^{A}=\Omega^{w^{1}}\iota^{A}

are any real numbers satisfying

(see
[
2

2.3
The twistor distribution

For any Riemannian 4-manifold

to be the projective (unprimed) spin bundle, so that locally

is identified with

A choice of compatible almost-complex structure

\displaystyle\lambda^{A}=\lambda_{1}o^{A}-\lambda_{0}\iota^{A},

\displaystyle\lambda^{A}L_{AA^{\prime}}=\lambda^{A}\partial_{AA^{\prime}}-\Gamma_{AA^{\prime}B}{}^{C}\lambda^{A}\lambda^{B}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{C}},

for some
\phi
. The interesting point now is that, by using the recursion operator
\mathcal{R}
, we can map the Teukolsky equation with any weights
(w,p)
to the conformal Laplacian, as long as
p
is even.

Proposition 12
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})
be conformal to a scalar-flat Kähler surface. Let
\chi_{(w,p)}
be an arbitrary weighted scalar field with weights
(w,p)
, where
p
is even. Then we have the identity

\displaystyle\Box^{\mathscr{C}}\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{p}{2}}\chi_{(w,p)}=\phi^{-(w+1)}\left(\Box+\tfrac{R}{6}\right)\left[\phi^{w+1}\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{p}{2}}\chi_{(w,p)}\right].

(3.19)

Proof.

Recall that if
\chi_{(w,p)}
has weights
(w,p)
, then
\mathcal{R}^{m}\chi_{(w,p)}
has weights
(w,p+2m)
, with
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
p
is even, it follows that
\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{p}{2}}\chi_{(w,p)}
has weights
(w,0)
. Then the identity (
2.16
) for the Teukolsky operator
\Box^{\mathscr{C}}
acting on scalar fields with weights
(w,0)
applies to
\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{p}{2}}\chi_{(w,p)}
. But since
\Psi_{2}\equiv 0
, we see that the operator simply reduces to the conformal Laplacian composed with conjugation by
\phi^{w+1}
.
∎

If
\chi_{(w,p)}
is now a solution to the Teukolsky equation for weights
(w,p)
, then
\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{p}{2}}\chi_{(w,p)}
is a solution to the corresponding equation for weights
(w,0)
, since
\mathcal{R}
maps solutions to solutions. Then the scalar field
\phi^{w+1}\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{p}{2}}\chi_{(w,p)}
has weights
(-1,0)
and, in view of (
3.19
), is a solution to the conformal wave equation. Conversely, suppose that
\Phi
has weights
(-1,0)
and solves the conformal wave equation
(\Box+\frac{R}{6})\Phi=0
. For
p
even, the field
\chi_{(w,p)}:=\phi^{-(w+1)}\mathcal{R}^{\frac{p}{2}}\Phi
has weights
(w,p)
and, again as a consequence of (
3.19
), solves
\Box^{\mathscr{C}}\chi_{(w,p)}=0
, which is the Teukolsky equation for weights
(w,p)
.

4
Quaternionic-Kähler Metrics

Here we specialise to the case in which the geometry
(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})
satisfies not only (
1.3
) but is also Einstein, with non-zero cosmological constant
\lambda
. In four dimensions, these manifolds are called quaternionic-Kähler (or self-dual Einstein)
6
6

6

Despite the name, these manifolds are generically not Kähler.
. The curvature satisfies

\displaystyle\Psi_{ABCD}=0,\qquad R_{ab}=\lambda g_{ab},\qquad\lambda\neq 0.

(4.1)

4.1
Gravitational perturbations

We will focus on metric deformations, that is, we perturb the metric
g_{ab}\to g_{ab}+\varepsilon h_{ab}
, and we wish to study the linearised Einstein equations
\mathcal{E}_{ab}[h]=0
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{ab}[h]:=-\tfrac{1}{2}\Box h_{ab}-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}{\rm tr}(h)+\nabla^{c}\nabla_{(a}h_{b)c}-\tfrac{1}{2}g_{ab}[\nabla^{c}\nabla^{d}h_{cd}-\Box{\rm tr}(h)]+\lambda h_{ab}=0

(4.2)

and
\Box=g^{ab}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}
,
{\rm tr}(h)=g^{ab}h_{ab}
.

Proposition 13
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})
be quaternionic-Kähler (
4.1
). Fix an integrable complex structure, with principal spinors
o_{A},\iota_{A}
, Lee form
f_{a}
, and connection
\mathscr{C}_{a}
as in section
2
. Then for an arbitrary symmetric tensor field
h_{ab}
, the following identities hold:

\displaystyle-\mathring{\Omega}^{-1}W^{abcd}_{\bf i}(\nabla_{a}-4f_{a})\nabla_{d}\mathcal{E}_{bc}[h]=\tfrac{1}{4}\Box^{\mathscr{C}}\left(\mathring{\Omega}^{-1}W^{abcd}_{\bf i}\dot{C}_{abcd}[h]\right),\qquad{\bf i}=0,...,4

(4.3)

where
\mathring{\Omega}
is an auxiliary constant conformal factor,
\mathcal{E}_{ab}[h]
is the linearised Einstein operator (
4.2
),
\dot{C}_{abcd}[h]
is the linearised Weyl tensor thought of as a linear functional of
h_{ab}
, and

\displaystyle W_{\bf i}^{abcd}:=S_{\bf i}^{ABCD}\epsilon^{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\epsilon^{C^{\prime}D^{\prime}},

(4.4)

with
S_{0}^{ABCD}=o^{(A}o^{B}o^{C}o^{D)}
,
S_{1}^{ABCD}=o^{(A}o^{B}o^{C}\iota^{D)}
, …,
S_{4}^{ABCD}=\iota^{(A}\iota^{B}\iota^{C}\iota^{D)}
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
(\mathcal{M},g_{ab},J^{a}{}_{b})
is an arbitrary almost-Hermitian manifold, with ASD Weyl spinor
\Psi_{ABCD}
. Recalling that
\Psi_{ABCD}
has conformal weight zero, let
\mathring{\Omega}
be an (auxiliary) constant conformal factor,
\nabla_{a}\mathring{\Omega}=0
, and define

\displaystyle\varphi_{ABCD}:=\mathring{\Omega}^{-1}\Psi_{ABCD},

(4.5)

which has conformal weight
w=-1
(and
p=0
). Using (
2.6b
), we have:

\displaystyle S_{\bf i}^{ABCD}\mathscr{C}_{AA^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}^{A^{\prime}E}\varphi_{BCDE}

\displaystyle=\mathring{\Omega}^{-1}S_{\bf i}^{ABCD}(\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}-4f_{AA^{\prime}})\nabla^{A^{\prime}E}\Psi_{BCDE}

\displaystyle=\mathring{\Omega}^{-1}S_{\bf i}^{ABCD}(\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}-4f_{AA^{\prime}})\nabla_{B}^{B^{\prime}}\Phi_{CDB^{\prime}}{}^{A^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathring{\Omega}^{-1}S_{\bf i}^{ACBD}\epsilon^{A^{\prime}C^{\prime}}\epsilon^{B^{\prime}D^{\prime}}(\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}-4f_{AA^{\prime}})\nabla_{DD^{\prime}}\Phi_{BCB^{\prime}C^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathring{\Omega}^{-1}W^{abcd}_{\bf i}(\nabla_{a}-4f_{a})\nabla_{d}(G_{bc}+\tfrac{R}{4}g_{bc}),

where in the second line we used Bianchi identities
[
2
, Eq. (4.10.7)]
, and in the last line we used the definition (
4.4
) and the relation between the Ricci spinor
\Phi_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
and the Einstein tensor
G_{ab}

[
2
, Eq. (4.6.25)]
. We now linearise the above equation around a space that satisfies (
4.1
). The ASD linearised Weyl tensor has a spinor decomposition
\dot{C}^{-}_{abcd}\equiv\dot{\Psi}_{ABCD}\epsilon_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\epsilon_{C^{\prime}D^{\prime}}
, and correspondingly we have a field
\dot{\varphi}_{ABCD}
as in (
4.5
). The fields
S_{\bf i}^{ABCD}
and the operator
\mathscr{C}_{AA^{\prime}}
can now be taken to be the ones of the background, so we have

\displaystyle S_{\bf i}^{ABCD}\mathscr{C}_{AA^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}^{A^{\prime}E}\dot{\varphi}_{BCDE}=\tfrac{1}{2}\Box^{\mathscr{C}}(S_{\bf i}^{ABCD}\dot{\varphi}_{ABCD}),

thus (
4.3
) follows.
∎

We see that the role of the auxiliary constant conformal factor
\mathring{\Omega}
is simply to allow us to apply the operator
\mathscr{C}_{AA^{\prime}}
to fields with the appropriate conformal weights; once the final formulae have been obtained,
\mathring{\Omega}
can simply be set to one.

The point of the identity (
4.3
) is the following. Suppose first that
h_{ab}
is a solution to the linearised Einstein equations,
\mathcal{E}_{ab}[h]=0
. Define the weighted scalar fields

\displaystyle\chi_{\bf i}:=\tfrac{1}{4}\mathring{\Omega}^{-1}W^{abcd}_{\bf i}\dot{C}_{abcd}[h],

(4.6)

whose weights are
w=-3
,
p=4-2{\bf i}
. Then (
4.3
) tells us that each
\chi_{\bf i}
,
{\bf i}=0,...,4
, is a solution to the Teukolsky equation for weights
(-3,4-2{\bf i})
. From proposition
11
, since the weights satisfy (
3.15
), each
\chi_{\bf i}
then generates a cohomology class in
\check{H}^{1}(\mathcal{PT},\mathcal{O}(-6))
. In addition, the linearised Bianchi identity
\nabla^{AA^{\prime}}\dot{\Psi}_{ABCD}=0
implies
\mathscr{C}^{AA^{\prime}}\dot{\varphi}_{ABCD}=0
, which gives
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{A^{\prime}}(\iota^{A}\dot{\varphi}_{ABCD})=\mathscr{C}_{A^{\prime}}(o^{A}\dot{\varphi}_{ABCD})
. Contracting with
o^{A}
’s and
\iota^{A}
’s, we see that the
\chi_{\bf i}
’s satisfy the recursion relations, therefore, from prop.
10
, they generate the same class. In summary, a solution
h_{ab}
to the linearised Einstein equations generates a cohomology class in
\check{H}^{1}(\mathcal{PT},\mathcal{O}(-6))
via the perturbed curvature.

In the opposite direction, from prop.
7
we can use a representative in
\check{H}^{1}(\mathcal{PT},\mathcal{O}(-6))
to generate solutions to the Teukolsky equations; however, how do we reconstruct a perturbed metric from them? The identity (
4.3
) gives a way to do this, but it also suggests that it is actually the group
\check{H}^{1}(\mathcal{PT},\mathcal{O}(+2))
which is naturally involved here:

Proposition 14
.

Let
\Phi^{\bf i}
be a solution to the Teukolsky equation with weights
w=1
,
p=2{\bf i}-4
, for any
{\bf i}
in
\{0,...,4\}
. Then the tensor field

\displaystyle h_{ab}^{\bf i}:=-\nabla^{d}[(\nabla^{c}+4f^{c})W^{\bf i}_{c(ab)d}\Phi^{\bf i}]

(4.7)

(no sum over
\bf i
) is a solution to the linearised Einstein equations,
\mathcal{E}_{ab}[h^{\bf i}]=0
.

Proof.

We use Wald’s adjoint operators method
[
19
]
and write (
4.3
) as the operator identity
\mathcal{S}_{\bf i}\mathcal{E}[h_{ab}]=\mathcal{O}\mathcal{T}_{\bf i}[h_{ab}]
valid for any symmetric
h_{ab}
, where:
\mathcal{E}
is defined in (
4.2
),
\mathcal{O}(\chi):=\Box^{\mathscr{C}}\chi
,
\mathcal{T}_{\bf i}[h_{ab}]:=\chi_{\bf i}
(with
\chi_{\bf i}
as in (
4.6
)), and
\mathcal{S}_{\bf i}
is the operator that is acting on
\mathcal{E}_{bc}[h]
in the left hand side of (
4.3
). The adjoint identity is
\mathcal{E}\mathcal{S}^{\dagger}_{\bf i}[\Phi]=\mathcal{T}^{\dagger}_{\bf i}\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}[\Phi]
, valid for any scalar field
\Phi
with the appropriate weights, where we used that
\mathcal{E}
is self-adjoint
[
19
]
. A straightforward calculation shows that
\mathcal{S}^{\dagger}_{{\bf i}\,ab}\equiv h^{\bf i}_{ab}
, with
h^{\bf i}_{ab}
given by (
4.7
). It follows that if
\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}[\Phi^{\bf i}]=0
, then (
4.7
) is a solution to the linearised Einstein equations. So it remains to understand the operator
\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}
. To this end, we recall that the adjoint of
\mathcal{O}
is the unique operator
\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}
defined by

\displaystyle\Phi^{\bf i}\mathcal{O}[\chi_{\bf i}]=\chi_{\bf i}\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}[\Phi^{\bf i}]+\nabla_{a}v^{a}_{\bf i}\qquad\text{(no sum over $\bf i$)}

for some vector field
v^{a}_{\bf i}
, cf.
[
19
, Eq. (9)]
. Since
\chi_{\bf i}
has weights
(-3,4-2{\bf i})
, we have that
\mathcal{O}[\chi_{\bf i}]=g^{ab}\mathscr{C}_{a}\mathscr{C}_{b}\chi_{\bf i}
has weights
(-5,4-2{\bf i})
. Multiplying by
\Phi^{\bf i}
, we get
\Phi^{\bf i}\mathcal{O}[\chi_{\bf i}]=\mathscr{C}_{a}v^{a}_{\bf i}+(\Box^{\mathscr{C}}\Phi^{\bf i})\chi_{\bf i}
, where
v^{a}_{\bf i}=\Phi^{\bf i}\mathscr{C}^{a}\chi_{\bf i}-\chi_{\bf i}\mathscr{C}^{a}\Phi^{\bf i}
. Using (
2.6c
), it is straightforward to show that in order to have
\mathscr{C}_{a}v^{a}_{\bf i}=\nabla_{a}v^{a}_{\bf i}
, the weights of
\Phi^{\bf i}
must be
w=+1
,
p=2{\bf i}-4
. So we see that for
\mathcal{O}=\Box^{\mathscr{C}}
as defined by (
4.3
) (that is, acting on scalars with weights
(-3,4-2{\bf i})
), the adjoint is
\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}=\Box^{\mathscr{C}}
but acting on scalars with weights
(1,2{\bf i}-4)
.
∎

Since in the above proposition
\Phi^{\bf i}
is a solution to the Teukolsky equation for weights
(1,2{\bf i}-4)
, applying prop.
9
we see that
\Phi^{\bf i}
is generated by a representative, say
\Psi^{\bf i}
, of a cohomology class in
\check{H}^{1}(\mathcal{PT},\mathcal{O}(2))
:

\displaystyle\Phi^{\bf i}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{\Psi^{\bf i}(x,\lambda)\lambda_{A}{\rm d}\lambda^{A}}{(o_{B}\lambda^{B})^{4-{\bf i}}(\iota_{B}\lambda^{B})^{\bf i}}.

(4.8)

Now, notice that, on restriction to any twistor line
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, the group
\check{H}^{1}(\mathbb{CP}^{1},\mathcal{O}(2))
is trivial (recall (
3.18
)).
This turns out to be manifested in the second item of the following proposition:

Proposition 15
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})
be quaternionic-Kähler (
4.1
).

1.

Let
\Pi_{A^{\prime}ABC}=\Pi_{A^{\prime}(ABC)}
be a spinor field satisfying

\displaystyle\nabla^{A^{\prime}}_{(A}\Pi_{BCD)A^{\prime}}=0,

(4.9)

and let
\gamma_{CDA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}:=\nabla_{(A^{\prime}}^{A}\Pi_{B^{\prime})ACD}
. Then the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\nabla_{(A}^{A^{\prime}}\nabla_{B}^{B^{\prime}}\gamma_{CD)A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=0.

(4.10)

2.

The linearised ASD Weyl spinor of the metric perturbation (
4.7
) identically vanishes.

Proof.

For the first item, the calculation is tedious but straightforward: one has to use repeatedly the spinor form of the commutator
[\nabla_{AA^{\prime}},\nabla_{BB^{\prime}}]
and related identities as in
[
2
, Section 4.9]
, together with the assumptions (
4.1
) and (
4.9
).

For the second item, we use (
4.4
) to write (
4.7
) in spinor form:

\displaystyle h^{\bf i}_{CDA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{(A^{\prime}}^{A}\Pi^{\bf i}_{B^{\prime})ACD},\qquad\text{where}\qquad\Pi^{\bf i}_{B^{\prime}ACD}:=(\nabla_{B^{\prime}}^{B}+4f_{B^{\prime}}^{B})S^{\bf i}_{ABCD}\Phi^{(i)}.

Using that the weights of
S^{\bf i}_{ABCD}\Phi^{\bf i}
are
w=3
,
p=0
, a short calculation gives
\Pi^{\bf i}_{B^{\prime}ACD}=\mathscr{C}_{B^{\prime}}^{B}[S^{\bf i}_{ABCD}\Phi^{\bf i}]
. We then get

\displaystyle\nabla_{(A}^{A^{\prime}}\Pi^{\bf i}_{BCD)A^{\prime}}=\mathscr{C}_{(A}^{A^{\prime}}\Pi^{\bf i}_{BCD)A^{\prime}}=S^{\bf i}_{(ABC}{}^{E}\mathscr{C}_{D)A^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}^{A^{\prime}}_{E}\Phi^{\bf i}=-\tfrac{1}{2}S^{\bf i}_{ABCD}\Box^{\mathscr{C}}\Phi^{\bf i},

where in the first equality we used that
\Pi^{\bf i}_{B^{\prime}ACD}
has weights
w=2
,
p=0
, and in the last equality we used (
2.17
). Since
\Phi^{\bf i}
is a solution to
\Box^{\mathscr{C}}\Phi^{\bf i}=0
, then
\Pi^{\bf i}_{B^{\prime}BCD}
satisfies (
4.9
). Setting
\gamma_{CDA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\equiv h^{\bf i}_{CDA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
, we see that eq. (
4.10
) is satisfied for
h^{\bf i}_{ab}
. But the left hand side of (
4.10
) is precisely the formula for the linearised ASD Weyl spinor of a metric perturbation

, see
[

∎

4.2
Quaternionic deformations

Since the metric perturbations (
4.7
) have vanishing linearised Ricci and ASD Weyl, they preserve the conditions (
4.1
) to linear order. We then say that the perturbations are
quaternionic deformations
. This fact allows us to connect the Teukolsky equations and the approach in the current work with the treatments of this kind of deformations in
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
. The latter are based on Przanowski’s characterisation of any geometry satisfying (
4.1
): in
[
20
]
, Przanowski showed that any solution to (
4.1
) admits local complex coordinates
z^{\alpha}=(z^{0},z^{1})
and a real scalar field
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}(z^{\alpha},\bar{z}^{\alpha})
such that the metric can be written as

\displaystyle{\rm d}{s}^{2}=\frac{6}{\lambda}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}{\rm d}{z}^{\alpha}{\rm d}\bar{z}^{\beta}+\frac{12}{\lambda}e^{\mathcal{K}}{\rm d}{z}^{1}{\rm d}\bar{z}^{1}

(4.11)

where
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}=\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\bar{\beta}}\mathcal{K}
. The self-dual Einstein condition (
4.1
) is satisfied iff
\mathcal{K}
solves Przanowski’s equation
{\rm Prz}(\mathcal{K})=0
, where

\displaystyle{\rm Prz}(\mathcal{K}):=\mathcal{K}_{0\bar{0}}\mathcal{K}_{1\bar{1}}-\mathcal{K}_{0\bar{1}}\mathcal{K}_{1\bar{0}}+(2\mathcal{K}_{0\bar{0}}-\mathcal{K}_{0}\mathcal{K}_{\bar{0}})e^{\mathcal{K}}.

(4.12)

A class of quaternionic deformations is obtained by simply perturbing Przanowski’s potential,
\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{K}+\varepsilon\delta\mathcal{K}
, where
\delta\mathcal{K}
is required to satisfy
{\rm dPrz}(\delta\mathcal{K})=0
, with
{\rm dPrz}
the linearised Przanowski operator:

\displaystyle{\rm dPrz}(\delta\mathcal{K})={}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}_{1\bar{1}}+2e^{\mathcal{K}})\delta\mathcal{K}_{0\bar{0}}+\mathcal{K}_{0\bar{0}}\delta\mathcal{K}_{1\bar{1}}-\mathcal{K}_{1\bar{0}}\delta\mathcal{K}_{0\bar{1}}-\mathcal{K}_{0\bar{1}}\delta\mathcal{K}_{1\bar{0}}

(4.13)

\displaystyle+e^{\mathcal{K}}[(2\mathcal{K}_{0\bar{0}}-\mathcal{K}_{0}\mathcal{K}_{\bar{0}})\delta\mathcal{K}-\mathcal{K}_{0}\delta\mathcal{K}_{\bar{0}}-\mathcal{K}_{\bar{0}}\delta\mathcal{K}_{0}]

where again
\delta\mathcal{K}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}=\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\bar{\beta}}\delta\mathcal{K}
.
In
[
7
]
, Högner constructed a conformally invariant differential operator on quaternionic-Kähler 4-manifolds, and showed that, when acting on scalar fields with conformal weight
w=1
, it is proportional to
{\rm dPrz}
. It turns out that Högner’s operator is essentially the quaternionic-Kähler specialisation of the Teukolsky operator of section
2.2
. Using (
2.6a
) and (
2.6c
), one can indeed check that on scalars with weights
(w,0)
,

\displaystyle\Box^{\mathscr{C}}=\Box+(w+1)(2f^{a}\nabla_{a}+wf^{a}f_{a})-w\tfrac{R}{6}.

In terms of the quantities in (
4.11
), the Lee form is
f_{a}{\rm d}{x}^{a}=-(\partial\log\mathcal{K}_{\bar{0}}+\bar{\partial}\log\mathcal{K}_{0})
. Then on any scalar with weights
w=1
,
p=0
, after a computation (and using
R=4\lambda
) we find

\displaystyle\Box^{\mathscr{C}}=\frac{6}{\lambda\det g_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}}{\rm dPrz}

(4.14)

in agreement with the pioneering work
[
7
]
. Furthermore, Högner also gave a contour integral formula for perturbations of Przanowski’s potential, see
[
7
, Eq. (5)]
(cf. also
[
8
]
). This coincides with the case
{\bf i}=2
in formula (
4.8
) (which corresponds to
w=1
,
p=0
).

While perturbations of Przanowski’s potential give one class of quaternionic deformations, the results of section
4.1
show that there are other such deformations described by the tensors (
4.7
) with
{\bf i}\neq 2
. In these cases, the scalar equation involved is the Teukolsky equation with
p\neq 0
(instead of (
4.13
)), and (
4.8
) gives a contour integral formula for its solutions. Note, however, that in Euclidean signature the tensor fields (
4.7
) with
{\bf i}\neq 2
are not real.

4.3
The case with an isometry

Finally, we comment very briefly on the case in which
(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})
satisfies (
4.1
) and has also a Killing vector. In this case, Tod showed in
[
21
]
that the geometry must be (locally) conformally Kähler, so the construction of section
3.4
applies. In the notation of that section, and using the expression for the Lee form given before, we deduce
\phi^{-1}=\mathcal{K}_{0}=\mathcal{K}_{\bar{0}}
. In particular, using (
3.19
) and (
4.14
), for
w=1
we recover
[
8
, Eq. (6.5)]
. More generally, we can map back and forth between the conformal wave equation and the Teukolsky equation for any weights
(w,p)
with
p
even, as described in section
3.4
.

5
Final Remarks

We constructed a correspondence between twistor functions and solutions to Teukolsky-like equations in conformally self-dual spaces, and we gave a contour integral formula for the latter in terms of essentially free holomorphic twistor data. Apart from the conformal self-duality assumption (
1.3
), the geometries we considered are generic (except in section
4
where we specialised to the self-dual Einstein case). To obtain more explicit coordinate descriptions of the space-time solutions, one must specialise to a specific geometry with its twistor space and use the corresponding incidence relations that express twistor coordinates in terms of space-time coordinates. For instance, in
[
7
, Section 5]
one can find examples of twistor coordinates for a number of geometries.

Following the twistor literature
[
13
,
6
]
, there are other structures of interest that one can explore using the results in this work, such as the existence of hierarchies of commuting flows, Hamiltonian formulations, hidden symmetry algebras, the construction of special coordinates for twistor space
\mathcal{PT}
, the linear deformations of
\mathcal{PT}
induced by gravitational perturbations, etc. A point of major interest for us would be to analyse whether the construction in this work can be used to study perturbations of
non-self-dual
black holes: in this case the background space also has an integrable complex structure and the connection
\mathscr{C}_{a}
and many of its properties can be used (cf.
[
22
]
), but the twistor distribution has trivial kernel so ordinary twistor functions do not exist. We leave these questions for future work.

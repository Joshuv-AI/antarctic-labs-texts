A key ingredient in the description of double parton distributions is their scale dependence. If the colour of each individual parton is summed over, the distributions evolve with the same DGLAP kernels as ordinary parton distributions. This is no longer true if the two partons are colour correlated. We compute the relevant kernels for this case at next-to-leading order in the strong coupling, for unpolarised or longitudinally polarised partons and for transversely polarised quarks.

To compute double parton scattering cross sections, one needs to know double parton distributions (DPDs), which quantify the joint probability for extracting two specified partons from a proton. Beyond their practical importance, they are of interest for studying hadron structure, since they describe correlations between partons that are not accessible in single-parton distributions.

The scale dependence of DPDs has long been a point of theoretical interest, mainly because of an inhomogeneous term that appears in the evolution equation when the distributions are integrated over the transverse distance
y
between the two partons
[
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
. As shown in
[
13
,
19
,
32
]
, this term is however absent if
y
is kept fixed. We concentrate on DPDs at fixed

Most theory investigations consider the case in which the colour of each individual parton is summed over; we will call the corresponding distributions “colour singlet DPDs” for reasons explained later. In this case, the evolution equations involve the same kernels that appear in the DGLAP equations for ordinary parton distributions (PDFs). These kernels are known to high perturbative orders
[
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
]

In general, however, two partons inside a proton are correlated in their colour as well as in their polarisation
[
39
]
. Colour correlations between the partons are suppressed by Sudakov logarithms
[
40
,
14
]
, but it is not obvious that this suppression is so strong that the correlations can be neglected altogether. A recent study suggests that their effect may be large enough to be observable
[
24
]
. Moreover, it is clear that the Sudakov suppression becomes weaker as the scale of the hard scattering decreases. Colour correlations in double parton scattering could hence play a role in the production of additional jets with moderate transverse momentum, which contribute to the underlying event if they are not explicitly identified.

These considerations motivate us to investigate the scale evolution for DPDs with general colour structure. At leading order (LO) in
\alpha_{s}
the corresponding kernels were already calculated in
[
13
,
32
]

We use two independent methods. One of them modifies the calculation of the ordinary DGLAP kernels that is reported in

42

43

44

46

2
Evolution and renormalisation of colour dependent DPDs

A collinear (i.e. transverse-momentum integrated) DPD
{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\zeta)
depends on the momentum fractions
x_{1},x_{2}
of the two observed partons, on their distance
y
in the transverse plane, and on three scales. These are the renormalisation scales
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
associated with each parton and a rapidity parameter
\zeta
with the dimension of a squared mass. The labels
a_{1},a_{2}
specify both the type and the polarisation of the two partons. We write
q,\bar{q},g
for an unpolarised parton,
\Delta q,\Delta\bar{q},\Delta g
for a longitudinally polarised one, and
\delta q,\delta\bar{q}
for transversely polarised quarks. A complete description of the gluon sector also requires linear gluon polarisation, which is not considered in the present work.

Finally,
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are irreducible representations of the colour group and specify the state of the pair of fields associated with each parton in the definition of a DPD (see below). The possible values for a quark or antiquark are
1
for the colour singlet and
8
for the octet. For gluons we have the colour singlet (
1
), the antisymmetric octet (
A
), the symmetric octet (
S
), the decuplet and antidecuplet (
10
and
\overline{10}
), and the representation of dimension
27
. We write
\overline{R}
for the conjugate of a representation
R
, bearing in mind that for the cases just enumerated
\overline{R}
differs from
R
only for the decuplet and antidecuplet.

Following earlier work in
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
, the evolution equations for DPDs in general colour representations have been derived in
[
32
]
. The dependence on the scale
\mu_{1}
is given by a DGLAP equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ln\mu_{1}}{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\zeta)

\displaystyle\qquad=2\sum_{b,R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}{}^{R_{1}\overline{R}{}^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muP_{a_{1}b}(x_{1}^{\prime},\mu_{1},x_{1}^{2}\zeta)\underset{x_{1}}{\otimes}{}^{R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{ba_{2}}(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2},y,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\zeta)\,,

(2.1)

and there is an analogous equation for the dependence on
\mu_{2}
. The kernel
{}^{R\overline{R}{}^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muP_{ab}
describes transitions between representations
R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu
and
R
of equal dimension, and the colour singlet kernels
{}^{11}\mskip-1.5muP_{ab}
are identical to the DGLAP kernels for ordinary PDFs. We use the term “colour dependent DGLAP kernels” to denote the set of all
{}^{R\overline{R}{}^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muP_{ab}
.

In physical terms, the parameter
\zeta
specifies a rapidity cutoff and is defined with respect to a reference momentum
k
. Technically, this can be written as
\zeta\sim(k^{+})^{2}
for a particle moving fast in the positive
z
direction.

\displaystyle{}^{\scalebox{0.7}{$10\phantom{\overline{1}}\hskip-3.01389pt$}\mskip 1.5mu\scalebox{0.7}{$\overline{10}$}}\mskip-1.5muP_{gg}

The rapidity dependence of the DPDs is given by a Collins-Soper equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\ln\zeta}{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,{}^{R_{1}}\mskip-1.5muJ(y,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\,{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\zeta)

\displaystyle{}^{1}\mskip-1.5muJ

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle{}^{8}\mskip-1.5muJ

\displaystyle={}^{A}\mskip-1.5muJ={}^{S}\mskip-1.5muJ\,,

\displaystyle{}^{\scalebox{0.7}{$10\phantom{\overline{1}}\hskip-3.01389pt$}}\mskip-1.5muJ

\displaystyle={}^{\scalebox{0.7}{$\overline{10}$}}\mskip-1.5muJ\,.

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ln\mu_{1}}{}^{R_{1}}\mskip-1.5muJ(y,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})

\displaystyle={}-{}^{R}\gamma_{J}(\mu_{1})

\displaystyle{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muP_{ab}(x,\mu,\zeta)

\displaystyle={}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5mu\widehat{P}_{ab}(x,\mu)-\frac{1}{4}\delta_{R\overline{R}{}^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5mu\delta_{ab}\delta(1-x)\,{}^{R}\gamma_{J}(\mu)\ln\frac{\zeta}{\mu^{2}}\,.

2.1
Renormalisation of DPDs

The equations just given follow from the renormalisation and the handling of rapidity divergences in the definition of DPDs. A detailed derivation was given in
[
32
]
and is quite lengthy. In the following, we will only sketch those aspects that are important for understanding the methods of calculation used in the present work.

Operators and colour structure.

DPDs with nontrivial colour structure are defined from a generalisation of the familiar twist-two operators in which colour indices are left open. For unpolarised quarks, the generalised operator reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{q}^{ii^{\prime}}(x,\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z})=2p^{+}\int\frac{dz^{-}}{2\pi}\,dy^{-}\;e^{ixp^{+}z^{-}}

\displaystyle\bar{q}_{B\mskip-1.5mu,\mskip 1.5muj^{\prime}}\bigl(y-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}z\bigr)\,W_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\bigl(y-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}z,v_{L}\bigr)\,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\gamma^{+}\,

\displaystyle\times W_{ij}\bigl(y+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}z,v_{L}\bigr)\,q_{B\mskip-1.5mu,\mskip 1.5muj}\bigl(y+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}z\bigr)\Big|_{z^{+}=y^{+}=0}\,,

(2.11)

where
i
and
i^{\prime}
are indices in the fundamental representation of the colour group.
Given the integrations over the minus-components of the field positions, this operator corresponds to a parton with plus-momentum
xp^{+}
. The Wilson line

\displaystyle W_{ij}(\xi,v)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{P}\exp\biggl[\mskip 1.5muig_{B}\mskip 1.5mut^{a}_{ij}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\!\!ds\;vA_{B}^{a}(\xi+sv)\mskip 1.5mu\biggr]\,,

(2.12)

is in the fundamental representation and runs along a path specified by the vector
v
, which is set to
v_{L}=(0,1,\boldsymbol{0})
in the matrix element. At this point, we use bare (i.e. unrenormalised) field operators
q_{B}
and
A_{B}
and the bare coupling
g_{B}
. Analogous definitions for polarised quarks and for gluons are given in section 3.1 of
[
32
]
.

To define an ordinary PDF for parton
a
, one takes the matrix element of

\mathcal{O}_{a}^{rr^{\prime}}(x,\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{0})
in a proton state with momentum
p
and contracts the open colour indices with
\delta_{rr^{\prime}}
(with indices
r

in the fundamental or adjoint representation as appropriate). Single-parton TMDs are defined from the same matrix element with finite
\boldsymbol{z}
.

Distributions of two partons are defined from the operator product

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{a_{1}}^{r_{1}r_{1}^{\prime}}(x_{1},\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z}_{1})\,\mathcal{O}_{a_{2}}^{r_{2}r_{2}^{\prime}}(x_{2},\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{z}_{2})\,.

(2.13)

Specifically, a bare and rapidity-unsubtracted DPD
(F_{B\mskip 1.5mu\text{us},a_{1}a_{2}})^{r_{1}r_{1}^{\prime}\mskip 1.5mur_{2}r_{2}^{\prime}}
with open colour indices is obtained from the proton matrix element of this operator product, as specified in equation (3.11) of
[
32
]

For collinear DPDs, the transverse distances
\boldsymbol{z}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{z}_{2}
between parton fields are set to zero, as in the PDF case. The result is then projected onto a definite colour combination by coupling the index pairs
(r_{1}r_{1}^{\prime})
and
(r_{2}\mskip 1.5mur_{2}^{\prime})
to irreducible representations
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
of the colour group, such that one obtains an overall colour singlet. The appropriate normalisation factors are given in equation (I.11) of
[
32
]
.

The second element in the construction is a soft factor, defined in terms of the operator

\displaystyle\bigl[\mathcal{O}_{S,a}(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z})\bigr]^{rr^{\prime}\!,\mskip 1.5muss^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\bigl[W(\boldsymbol{y}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{z},v_{L})\,W^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{y}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{z},v_{R})\bigr]_{rs}\,\bigl[W(\boldsymbol{y}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{z},v_{R})\,W^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{y}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{z},v_{L})\bigr]_{s^{\prime}r^{\prime}}\;,

(2.14)

where

The vector

points in the direction opposite to

, and the Wilson lines are in the fundamental (adjoint) representation for quarks (gluons).
As shown in section 4.4 of
[
32
]
, important simplifications arise if one sets
\boldsymbol{z}=\boldsymbol{0}
in this operator:
(i)
the projection of the index pairs

and

The bare soft factor

for DPDs is obtained from the vacuum expectation value of the product

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{S,a_{1}}^{r_{1}r_{1}^{\prime},\,s_{1}s_{1}^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z}_{1})\,\mathcal{O}_{S,a_{2}}^{r_{2}r_{2}^{\prime},\,s_{2}s_{2}^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{z}_{2})

For collinear DPDs one sets

Renormalisation.

For
\boldsymbol{z}=\boldsymbol{0}
, the operators in (
2.1
) and (
2.1
) have ultraviolet divergences beyond those associated with the bare fields, because they involve products of fields separated by a light-like distance. The projections of the bare parton operators
\mathcal{O}^{rr^{\prime}}_{a}(x,\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{0})
on definite colour representations are renormalised by convolution with functions
{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muZ_{\text{us},ab}(x)
. Their index pairs
(RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu)
and
(ab)
are needed to describe the mixing between quark and gluon operators under renormalisation. The bare soft operators
\mathcal{O}_{\smash{S}\vphantom{s}}^{rr^{\prime}\!,\mskip 1.5muss^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{0})

(rr^{\prime})
projected on
R
and
(ss^{\prime})
projected on
\overline{R}
are renormalised by multiplication with a factor
{}^{R}\mskip-1.5muZ_{S}
.
The renormalisation factor
{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muZ_{ab}(x)
for the rapidity-subtracted DPD is then obtained from
{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muZ_{\text{us},ab}(x)/\sqrt{{}^{R}\mskip-1.5muZ_{S}}
by removing the rapidity regulator in the same way as for the DPD. The renormalised DPD is obtained as

\displaystyle{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b_{1},R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}{}^{R_{1}\overline{R}{}^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muZ_{a_{1}b_{1}}(x_{1}^{\prime},\mu_{1},x_{1}^{2}\zeta)\,\underset{x_{1}}{\otimes}\,\sum_{b_{2},R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}{}^{R_{2}\overline{R}{}^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muZ_{a_{2}b_{2}}(x_{2}^{\prime},\mu_{2},x_{2}^{2}\zeta)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\underset{x_{2}}{\otimes}{}^{R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5muR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muF_{B\mskip-1.5mu,\mskip 1.5mub_{1}b_{2}}(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},y,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\zeta)\,,

(2.16)

where it is understood that after convolution with the

\epsilon=0
in dimensional regularisation.
Since the bare distributions are
\mu

2
) schematically from

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ln\mu_{1}}F(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d\ln\mu_{1}}\bigl[Z(\mu_{1})\underset{x_{1}}{\otimes}Z(\mu_{2})\underset{x_{2}}{\otimes}F_{B}\bigr]=\biggl[\frac{d}{d\ln\mu_{1}}Z(\mu_{1})\biggr]\underset{x_{1}}{\otimes}Z(\mu_{2})\underset{x_{2}}{\otimes}F_{B}

\displaystyle\underset{\text{def}}{=}\,\bigl[P(\mu_{1})\underset{x_{1}^{\prime}}{\otimes}Z(\mu_{1})\bigr]\underset{x_{1}}{\otimes}Z(\mu_{2})\underset{x_{2}}{\otimes}F_{B}=P(\mu_{1})\underset{x_{1}}{\otimes}\bigl[Z(\mu_{1})\underset{x_{1}^{\prime}}{\otimes}Z(\mu_{2})\underset{x_{2}}{\otimes}F_{B}\bigr]

\displaystyle=P(\mu_{1})\underset{x_{1}}{\otimes}F(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\,,

(2.17)

where we omitted all indices and arguments that are not essential for the manipulations. Whilst this is a standard argument when manipulating a renormalisation group equation (RGE), we need to be careful about the rescaling of
\zeta
. The renormalisation factors in (
2.16
) are defined such that they refer to the momentum of the first or second parton in the DPD, which explains the rescaling by

or

of

(given that the argument
\zeta
of the DPD refers to the proton rapidity). In the second line of (
2.1
) we need to use the modified law of associativity

\displaystyle\Bigl[A(x^{\prime\prime},x^{2}\zeta)\underset{x^{\prime}}{\otimes}B(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime\prime\,2\mskip 1.5mu}x^{2}\zeta/x^{\prime\,2})\Bigr]\underset{x}{\otimes}C(x^{\prime},\zeta)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=A(x^{\prime},x^{2}\zeta)\underset{x}{\otimes}\Bigl[B(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime\,2}\zeta)\underset{x^{\prime}}{\otimes}C(x^{\prime\prime},\zeta)\Bigr]\,,

4
Second method: modified TMD matching calculation

In general, the DGLAP kernels can be extracted from any quantity that obeys an evolution equation in which these kernels appear. One such quantity is the matching kernel that connects TMDs at short distances with ordinary PDFs.
This method can be extended to the colour dependent case, and in this section we show how to extract the colour dependent NLO kernels by modifying the two-loop calculation of the TMD matching kernels in
[
45
,
46
]
. That calculation was done in Feynman gauge with the
\delta
regulator for rapidity divergences. This allows us to compute also the terms going with
\delta(1-x)
, along with providing an independent cross check for the
x

To begin with, we recall that two-parton (or double) TMDs can be defined from
the operator products in (
2.13
) and
(
2.15
) at finite distances
\boldsymbol{z}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{z}_{2}
, as discussed at length in
[
32
]
. If both distances are small, one can use the operator product expansion to match the double TMDs onto collinear ones. This was done at LO in
[
32
]
, where it was observed that the colour dependent DGLAP kernels for DPDs can be extracted from the corresponding matching kernels of the same order in
a_{s}
. We follow a modified version of that strategy at NLO in the present work.

The matching kernels can be computed from matrix elements of
single

\mathcal{O}_{a}(x,\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z})
and
\mathcal{O}_{S,a}(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z})
rather than their products (

(

\mathcal{O}_{a}
between parton states and the vacuum expectation value of

[

for DPDs, the rapidity regulator can be removed. One thus obtains “TMD matrix elements”

Polarisation dependence and
\delta(1-x)
terms.

The TMD matching kernels of order
a_{s}^{2}
were computed in
[
45
]
for unpolarised partons and in
[
46
]
for transversely polarised quarks. The kernels for longitudinal polarisation have not been evaluated using these methods. We can hence not directly calculate the
\delta(1-x)
terms of the colour dependent DGLAP kernels. This is, however, not necessary because these terms are polarisation independent.

To understand this, let us see which graphs can provide a
\delta(1-x)
contribution to the bare matrix elements. There are purely virtual graphs, which cannot depend on the parton polarisation since the loop corrections do not connect the two partons that enter the operator vertex. In addition, there are contributions from mixed real-virtual or purely real graphs in the limit where the partons going across the final-state cut have zero plus-momentum. More specifically, one has either one or two cut gluons, or a cut quark-antiquark pair that merges into a gluon on at least one side of the cut. The connection between the two partons that enter the operator vertex — which is the potential source of polarisation dependence — thus always involves one or two gluons whose plus-momentum goes to zero. In this limit, one can use the eikonal approximation for their couplings to parton lines with finite plus-momentum. Since the eikonal coupling is spin independent, the
\delta(1-x)
terms in the bare matrix elements cannot depend on the polarisation of the twist-two operator. The same must therefore be true for the associated ultraviolet renormalisation factors, and hence for the full DGLAP kernels.

We explicitly verified this argument by direct computation of the
\delta(1-x)
terms for unpolarised and transversely polarised quarks, which come out to be equal as it must be.

5
Evolution channels and finite renormalisation

5.1
Linear combinations of DPDs and evolution kernels

Before presenting our results, we need to discuss in some detail the structure of the DGLAP kernels regarding the parton labels. This is well known in the colour singlet sector (see e.g.
[
33
,
34
]
), but there are some important differences in colour non-singlet channels that we need to review. For definiteness, we consider evolution in the scale
\mu_{1}
of the first parton in the DPD.

It is useful to form linear combinations of distributions, in order to decouple the DGLAP equations between different parton channels as much as possible.
Combinations with definite charge conjugation for the first parton are

\displaystyle{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{\smash{q_{i}^{\pm}}a_{2}}

\displaystyle={}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{q_{i}a_{2}}\pm{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{\bar{q}_{i}a_{2}}\,.

(5.1)

The DGLAP kernels for different parton transitions are related by charge conjugation invariance as follows:

\displaystyle{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{q_{i}q_{k}}

\displaystyle={}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{\mskip 1.5mu\bar{q}_{i}\bar{q}_{k}}\,,

(5.2)

\displaystyle{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{q_{i}\bar{q}_{k}}

\displaystyle={}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{\mskip 1.5mu\bar{q}_{i}q_{k}}\,,

(5.3)

\displaystyle{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muP_{q_{i}g}

\displaystyle=\eta(R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu\mskip 1.5mu)\,{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muP_{\mskip 1.5mu\bar{q}_{i}g}\,,

(5.4)

\displaystyle{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muP_{g\mskip 1.5muq_{i}}

\displaystyle=\eta(R)\,{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muP_{g\mskip 1.5mu\bar{q}_{i}}\,,

(5.5)

where
\eta(A)=-1
and
\eta(R)=+1
for all other representations. Due to this factor, the mixing pattern for gluons in the asymmetric octet differs from the well-known one in the colour singlet sector, as we shall see shortly. Notice that in (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) we simplified the representation indices, using that for quarks the only allowed representation pairs are
RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu=11
and
88
.

The flavour dependence for quark and antiquark kernels can be split into valence and pure singlet parts,

\displaystyle{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{a_{i}b_{k}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ik}{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{ab}^{V}+{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{ab}^{S}

\displaystyle\text{ with }a,b=q,\bar{q},

(5.6)

where
i
and
k
denote the quark flavour.
As a result of the above relations, flavour non-singlet combinations of DPDs decouple from gluons and evolve as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ln\mu_{1}}{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{\smash{(q_{i}^{\pm}-q_{k}^{\pm})}\mskip 1.5mua_{2}}

\displaystyle=2\;{}^{R_{1}R_{1}}\!P^{\pm}\underset{x_{1}}{\otimes}{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{\smash{(q_{i}^{\pm}-q_{k}^{\pm})}\mskip 1.5mua_{2}}

(5.7)

with kernels

\displaystyle{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP^{\pm}

\displaystyle={}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP^{V}_{qq}\pm{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP^{V}_{q\bar{q}}\,,

(5.8)

where we abbreviated

{F}_{\smash{(q_{i}^{\pm}-q_{k}^{\pm})}\mskip 1.5mua_{2}}={F}_{\smash{q_{i}^{\pm}}a_{2}}-{F}_{\smash{q_{k}^{\pm}}a_{2}}\mskip 1.5mu
.

The flavour singlet combinations for the first parton are defined as

\displaystyle{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{\Sigma^{\pm}a_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{\smash{q_{i}^{\pm}}a_{2}}

(5.9)

and mix with gluons. In the charge conjugation even sector, this results in the coupled system

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ln\mu_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}{}^{RR_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{\Sigma^{+}a_{2}}\\[1.00006pt]
{}^{R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{g\mskip 1.5mua_{2}}\ \end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=2\begin{pmatrix}{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{\Sigma^{+}\Sigma^{+}}&{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muP_{\Sigma^{+}g}\\[1.00006pt]
{}^{R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5muR}\mskip-1.5muP_{g\mskip 1.5mu\Sigma^{+}}&{}^{R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5muR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muP_{gg}\end{pmatrix}\underset{x_{1}}{\otimes}\begin{pmatrix}{}^{RR_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{\Sigma^{+}a_{2}}\\[1.00006pt]
{}^{R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{g\mskip 1.5mua_{2}}\ \end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\hskip-60.00009pt\text{ for }RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu=11,8S.

(5.10)

Due to the factors
\eta(R)
in the relations (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), there is also quark-gluon mixing in the charge conjugation odd sector:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ln\mu_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}{}^{8R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{\Sigma^{-}a_{2}}\\[1.00006pt]
{}^{AR_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{g\mskip 1.5mua_{2}}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=2\begin{pmatrix}{}^{88}\mskip-1.5muP_{\Sigma^{-}\Sigma^{-}}&{}^{8A}\mskip-1.5muP_{\Sigma^{-}g}\\[1.00006pt]
{}^{A8}\mskip-1.5muP_{g\mskip 1.5mu\Sigma^{-}}&{}^{AA}\mskip-1.5muP_{gg}\end{pmatrix}\underset{x_{1}}{\otimes}\begin{pmatrix}{}^{8R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{\Sigma^{-}a_{2}}\\[1.00006pt]
{}^{AR_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{g\mskip 1.5mua_{2}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(5.11)

The kernels appearing in these equations are given by

\displaystyle{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{\Sigma^{\pm}\Sigma^{\pm}}

\displaystyle={}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP^{\pm}+n_{f}\mskip 1.5mu\Bigl[{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{qq}^{S}\pm{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{q\bar{q}}^{S}\mskip 1.5mu\Bigr]

\displaystyle\text{for }R=1,8

(5.12)

and

\displaystyle{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muP_{\Sigma^{+}g}

\displaystyle=2n_{f}\,{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muP_{q_{i}g}\,,

\displaystyle{}^{R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5muR}\mskip-1.5muP_{g\Sigma^{+}}

\displaystyle={}^{R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5muR}\mskip-1.5muP_{g\mskip 1.5muq_{i}}

\displaystyle\text{for }RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu=11,8S,

(5.13)

\displaystyle{}^{8A}\mskip-1.5muP_{\Sigma^{-}g}

\displaystyle=2n_{f}\,{}^{8A}\mskip-1.5muP_{q_{i}g}\,,

\displaystyle{}^{A8}\mskip-1.5muP_{g\Sigma^{-}}

\displaystyle={}^{A8}\mskip-1.5muP_{g\mskip 1.5muq_{i}}\,,

(5.14)

where
n_{f}
is the number of active quark flavours.
Quark-gluon mixing kernels for the remaining combinations of
RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu
and
\Sigma^{\pm}
are zero.

In the colour singlet channel, the flavour singlet combination with odd charge conjugation parity evolves by itself:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ln\mu_{1}}{}^{1R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{\Sigma^{-}a_{2}}

\displaystyle=2\;{}^{11}\mskip-1.5muP_{\Sigma^{-}\Sigma^{-}}\otimes{}^{1R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{\Sigma^{-}a_{2}}\,.

(5.15)

The gluon distributions for higher colour representations,
{}^{\scalebox{0.7}{$10\phantom{\overline{1}}\hskip-3.01389pt$}\mskip 1.5mu\scalebox{0.7}{$\overline{10}$}}\mskip-1.5muF_{gg}
,
{}^{\scalebox{0.7}{$\overline{10}$}\,\scalebox{0.7}{$10\phantom{\overline{1}}\hskip-3.01389pt$}}\mskip-1.5muF_{gg}
, and
{}^{27\,27}\mskip-1.5muF_{gg}

The preceding discussion applies to polarised channels in complete analogy. For the polarised valence kernels, we use the notation

\displaystyle{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP^{\pm}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle={}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP^{V}_{\Delta q\Delta q}\pm{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP^{V}_{\Delta q\Delta\bar{q}}\,,

\displaystyle{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP^{\pm}_{\delta}

\displaystyle={}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP^{V}_{\delta q\delta q}\pm{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP^{V}_{\delta q\delta\bar{q}}\,.

(5.16)

Transversely polarised quarks do not mix with gluons under evolution, so that the corresponding kernels are all zero. The same holds for the pure singlet combinations,

\displaystyle{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP^{S}_{\delta q\delta q}

\displaystyle={}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP^{S}_{\delta q\delta\bar{q}}=0\,.

(5.17)

5.2
Finite renormalisation for longitudinal polarisation

It is known that
\overline{\text{MS}}
renormalisation of spin dependent quantities computed with the HVBM scheme for
\gamma_{5}
and the
\varepsilon
tensor have a number of undesirable features. These can be removed by an additional finite renormalisation in
4
space-time dimensions, after one has performed
\overline{\text{MS}}
subtraction and removed the dimensional regulator.

The corresponding scheme change for longitudinally polarised PDFs is analysed at length in section 3.1 of
[
43
]
, see also section 2 of
[
35
]
. It restores the relation

\displaystyle{}^{11}\mskip-1.5muP^{V}_{\Delta q\Delta q}

\displaystyle={}^{11}\mskip-1.5muP^{V}_{qq}\,,

(5.18)

which corresponds to helicity conservation in the valence sector and is broken at order
a_{s}^{2}
in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme. Restoration of the equality (
5.18
) also ensures the scale independence of the flavour non-singlet axial current, which was for instance discussed in
[
55
]
.

We shall review the necessary scheme transformation here, because we need to extend it to the colour octet sector. The finite renormalisation can be implemented at the level of the twist-two operators
{}^{R}\mathcal{O}_{a}^{rr^{\prime}}
that are projected on a definite colour representation. It reads

\displaystyle{}^{R}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta q_{i}}

\displaystyle={}^{R}\mskip-1.5mu\widetilde{Z}\underset{x}{\otimes}{}^{R}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta q_{i}}^{\,\scalebox{0.6}{$\overline{\text{MS}}$}}\,,

(5.19)

\displaystyle{}^{R}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta\bar{q}_{i}}

\displaystyle={}^{R}\mskip-1.5mu\widetilde{Z}\underset{x}{\otimes}{}^{R}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta\bar{q}_{i}}^{\,\scalebox{0.6}{$\overline{\text{MS}}$}}\,,

(5.20)

where for brevity we have omitted the colour indices of the operators.
One obtains the same transformation for the operator combinations associated with the distributions
F_{\smash{\Delta q_{i}^{\pm}}a_{2}}
and
F_{\Delta\Sigma^{\pm}\mskip 1.5mua_{2}}
. The
\overline{\text{MS}}
renormalised gluon operators are left untouched.
Notice that the renormalisation factor
{}^{R}\mskip-1.5mu\smash{\widetilde{Z}}(x,\mu)
does not depend on
\zeta
, so that the Collins-Soper equation for DPDs remains unchanged and polarisation independent.

Let us (re)derive the change of the evolution kernels induced by this finite renormalisation.
For a flavour non-singlet combination, the change in operator renormalisation specified by (
5.19
) and (
5.20
) implies

\displaystyle{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{\smash{(\Delta q_{i}^{\pm}-\Delta q_{k}^{\pm})}\mskip 1.5mua_{2}}

\displaystyle={}^{R_{1}}\mskip-1.5mu\widetilde{Z}\underset{x_{1}}{\otimes}{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{\smash{(\Delta q_{i}^{\pm}-\Delta q_{k}^{\pm})}\mskip 1.5mua_{2}}^{\,\scalebox{0.6}{$\overline{\text{MS}}$}}\,,

(5.21)

where it is understood that the scheme transformation is only performed for the first parton in the DPD.
Taking the RGE derivative in
\mu_{1}
on both sides gives

\displaystyle 2\,{}^{R_{1}R_{1}}\mskip-1.5muP^{\pm}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle\,\underset{x_{1}}{\otimes}\,\Bigl[{}^{R_{1}}\mskip-1.5mu\widetilde{Z}\underset{x^{\prime}_{1}}{\otimes}{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{(\Delta q_{i}^{\pm}-\Delta q_{k}^{\pm})\mskip 1.5mua_{2}}^{\,\scalebox{0.6}{$\overline{\text{MS}}$}}\Bigr]=\Bigl(\frac{d}{d\ln\mu_{1}}{}^{R_{1}}\mskip-1.5mu\widetilde{Z}\Bigr)\,\underset{x_{1}}{\otimes}\,{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{(\Delta q_{i}^{\pm}-\Delta q_{k}^{\pm})\mskip 1.5mua_{2}}^{\,\scalebox{0.6}{$\overline{\text{MS}}$}}

\displaystyle+2\,{}^{R_{1}}\mskip-1.5mu\widetilde{Z}\underset{x_{1}}{\otimes}\Bigl[{}^{R_{1}R_{1}}\mskip-1.5muP^{\pm}_{\Delta,\scalebox{0.6}{$\overline{\text{MS}}$}}\,\underset{x^{\prime}_{1}}{\otimes}\,{}^{R_{1}R_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{(\Delta q_{i}^{\pm}-\Delta q_{k}^{\pm})\mskip 1.5mua_{2}}^{\,\scalebox{0.6}{$\overline{\text{MS}}$}}\Bigl]\,.

(5.22)

With the modified law of associativity (
2.1
) for triple convolution products, this implies

\displaystyle{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP^{\pm}_{\Delta}(x^{\prime},\zeta)\underset{x}{\otimes}{}^{R}\mskip-1.5mu\widetilde{Z}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\ln\mu}{}^{R}\mskip-1.5mu\widetilde{Z}(x)+{}^{R}\mskip-1.5mu\widetilde{Z}(x^{\prime})\underset{x}{\otimes}{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP^{\pm}_{\Delta,\scalebox{0.6}{$\overline{\text{MS}}$}}\bigl(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\mskip 1.5mu2}\zeta/x^{2}\bigr)\,.

(5.23)

Taking the expansion

\displaystyle{}^{R}\mskip-1.5mu\widetilde{Z}(x,\mu)

\displaystyle=\delta(1-x)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{s}^{n}(\mu)\,{}^{R}\mskip-1.5mu\widetilde{Z}^{(n)}(x)

(5.24)

7
Summary

We have computed the DGLAP kernels for colour dependent DPDs at two-loop order, for unpolarised or longitudinally polarised partons and for transversely polarised quarks. We use two independent methods, adapting existing calculations for the ordinary two-loop DGLAP kernels
[
41
,
42
,
43
,
44
]
or for the matching of TMDs on PDFs
[
45
,
46
]
. The first calculation is done in light-cone gauge with a principal value regulator, whereas the second one uses Feynman gauge and the
\delta
regulator for rapidity divergences.
With the first method, we obtain the
x
dependent part of the kernels for all polarisations specified above, and with the second method we compute the full kernels for unpolarised partons and for transverse quark polarisation. Combining the methods and using that the
\delta(1-x)
terms are polarisation independent, we obtain the full kernels for all polarisations.

We find full agreement between the results that can be obtained with both methods. We also confirm the polarisation independence of the terms going with
\delta(1-x)
by explicit calculation for unpolarised or transversely polarised quarks. Further cross checks validate our calculations:
(i)
rapidity divergences of individual graphs cancel in the final results for both methods,
(ii)
in the calculation with the second method, double and triple poles in
\epsilon
have the structure required by an RGE analysis, and
(iii)
our result for the
\zeta
dependent part satisfies the all-order relation (
6.12
) between the cusp anomalous dimensions for colour octet DPDs and single-gluon TMDs.

For the terms proportional to
\delta(1-x)
in the splitting kernels, we find the simple structure in equation (
6
) up to two loops. In addition to the terms
d_{a}
already present in the colour singlet case (which are spin independent and differ only between quarks and gluons), there are terms
{}^{R}c-{}^{R}\gamma_{J}\,\log\sqrt{\zeta}/\mu
with coefficients that depend only on the dimension of the colour representation
R
. They are zero for the singlet, and their values for the higher representations
10
and
27
obey Casimir scaling relative to the octet.

For the complete kernels
{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip-1.5muP_{ab}(x)
, we find a number of noteworthy features.

1.

At LO, the
x
dependent part of each kernel factorises into a colour dependent prefactor and an
x
dependent function. This no longer holds at NLO, except for
{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{q\bar{q}}^{V}
,
{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{qq}^{S}
, and
{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{q\bar{q}}^{S}\mskip 1.5mu
. These kernels receive contributions only from one graph, or from one graph and a subtraction term with identical colour structure.

2.

The pure singlet kernels
{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{qq}^{S}
and
{}^{RR}\mskip-1.5muP_{q\bar{q}}^{S}
differ already at two-loop order for
R=8
, whereas for
R=1
their difference starts only at three loops.

3.

The colour octet kernels
{}^{AA}\mskip-1.5muP_{gg}
and
{}^{SS}\mskip-1.5muP_{gg}
are equal at LO but differ at NLO.

4.

In the colour octet sector, the kernels describing quark-gluon mixing satisfy the simple relation
{}^{8A}\mskip-1.5muP_{\Sigma^{-}g}:{}^{8S}\mskip-1.5muP_{\Sigma^{+}g}={}^{A8}\mskip-1.5muP_{g\Sigma^{-}}:{}^{S8}\mskip-1.5muP_{g\Sigma^{+}}=N:\sqrt{N^{2}-4}
, both at LO and at NLO accuracy.

5.

The
x
dependent part of the kernels in the decuplet sector is zero, and the corresponding two-gluon DPD evolves like a single-gluon TMD, as specified in equation (
6.16
).

6.

Both for
x\to 1
and for
x\to 0
, the NLO kernels in colour non-singlet channels become proportional to their colour singlet counterparts. For large
x
, the factor of proportionality is the same as at LO, whereas for small
x
it depends on both
N
and
n_{f}
and does not exhibit any simple pattern.

All statements just made also apply to the kernels for polarised partons. We furthermore find that with a suitably chosen renormalisation scheme transformation, one can achieve quark helicity conservation for the NLO valence kernels not only in the colour singlet sector (which is well known) but also in the colour octet channel.

The evolution kernels we have computed result from the renormalisation of the soft operator (
2.1
) combined with the twist-two operator (
2.1
) or with its analogues for gluons or polarised partons. In this sense, the kernels are not specific to the evolution of colour dependent DPDs and can possibly be used in other contexts. An example are the twist-three operators for TMDs discussed in
[
58
]
. We leave it to future work to investigate this in more detail.

Finally, it will be interesting to study the quantitative impact of the NLO kernels on the scale dependence of DPDs with colour correlations. This is left to future work.

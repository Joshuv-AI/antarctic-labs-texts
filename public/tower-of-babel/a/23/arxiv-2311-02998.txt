One-loop contributions for h → ℓ ​ ℓ ¯ ​ γ h\rightarrow\ell\bar{\ell}\gamma and e − ​ e + → h ​ γ e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow h\gamma in U ​ ( 1 ) B − L U(1)_{B-L} extension of the standard model
Dzung Tri Tran, Thanh Huy Nguyen, Khiem Hong Phan

Abstract
We present one-loop contributing for h → ℓ ​ ℓ ¯ ​ γ h\rightarrow\ell\bar{\ell}\gamma with ℓ = ν e , μ , τ , e , μ \ell=\nu_{e,\mu,\tau},e,\mu and e − ​ e + → h ​ γ e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow h\gamma in U ​ ( 1 ) B − L U(1)_{B-L} extension of the standard models. In phenomenological results, the signal strengths for h → ℓ ​ ℓ ¯ ​ γ h\rightarrow\ell\bar{\ell}\gamma at Large Hadron Collider and for e − ​ e + → h ​ γ e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow h\gamma at future Lepton Colliders are analyzed in physical parameter space for both vector and chiral B − L B-L models. We find that the contributions from neutral gauge boson Z ′ Z^{\prime} to the signal strengths are rather small. Consequently, the effects are hard to probe at future colliders. While the impacts of charged Higgs, CP-odd Higgs in the chiral B − L B-L model on the signal strengths are significant and can be measured with the help of the initial polarization beams at future lepton colliders.

\displaystyle F_{1,\text{Non-pole}\,H^{\pm}}^{L}\,\{q_{13}\leftrightarrow q_{23}\},

\displaystyle F_{i,\text{Non-pole}\,H^{\pm}}^{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

It is important to
note the following arguments.
When we take only

Z,W
in the non-
V_{0}
pole
diagrams and replace
overall couplings respectively to the
HESM, we then reprodure the corresponding
results in
[
32
]
. Furthermore,
including all contributions
from
Z,Z^{\prime},W
and charged scalar Higgs
in the non
V_{0}
-pole diagrams, we derive
alternative results from our previous
reference
[
31
]
.

Having all one-loop form factors,
we are going
to check the calculations by
verifying the
UV
finiteness
and
IR
finiteness of the form factors.
We verify that all form factors presented in
the calculations are
UV
-finite and
IR
-finite.
Since there is no
virtual photon exchanging
in the loop, we don’t have the
IR
-divergent
in the processes. We have confirmed
the
UV
finiteness for the form factors
analytically
in
[
30
,
32
]
.

After confirming the correctness
of the results, differential decay
rate for

h\rightarrow\ell\bar{\ell}\gamma
with including one-loop
contributions can be computed as
follows

\displaystyle\dfrac{d\Gamma_{h\rightarrow\ell\bar{\ell}\gamma}}{dq_{12}\,dq_{13}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{q_{12}}{512\pi^{3}M_{h}^{2}}\Big[q_{13}^{2}\Big(\big|F^{L}_{1}\big|^{2}+\big|F^{R}_{1}\big|^{2}\Big)+q_{23}^{2}\Big(\big|F^{L}_{2}\big|^{2}+\big|F^{R}_{2}\big|^{2}\Big)\Big].

(120)

Integration over

(m_{\ell\bar{\ell}}^{\text{cut}})^{2}\leq q_{12}\leq M_{h}^{2}
and
0\leq q_{13}\leq M_{h}^{2}-q_{12}
, one then obtains
the total decay rates. At the LHC, signal
strengths for
h\rightarrow\ell\bar{\ell}\gamma
can be derived as follows:

\displaystyle\mu_{B-L}=\dfrac{\sigma^{B-L}(pp\rightarrow h)}{\sigma^{SM}(pp\rightarrow h)}\dfrac{\Gamma_{h\rightarrow\ell\bar{\ell}\gamma}^{B-L}}{\Gamma_{h\rightarrow\ell\bar{\ell}\gamma}^{SM}}\dfrac{\Gamma_{h\rightarrow\textrm{all}}^{SM}}{\Gamma_{h\rightarrow\textrm{all}}^{B-L}}.

(121)

Where
\Gamma_{h\rightarrow\textrm{all}}^{B-L}=\Gamma_{h\rightarrow\textrm{all}}^{SM}+\Gamma^{B-L}[h\rightarrow Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}]+\Gamma^{B-L}[h\rightarrow\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}N_{i}\bar{N}_{i}]
.
In the scenario of heavy neutral
gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
and
heavy neutrinos
N_{i}
(
M_{N_{i}}\sim 1
TeV),
we then confirm that

\Gamma_{h\rightarrow\textrm{all}}^{B-L}\simeq\Gamma_{h\rightarrow\textrm{all}}^{SM}
.
Furthermore,
we verify that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\sigma^{B-L}(pp\rightarrow h)}{\sigma^{SM}(pp\rightarrow h)}\simeq\kappa_{hff}^{2}.

(122)

Where
\kappa_{hff}
is
defined as in Eq. (
106
).
Therefore, the signal strengths
can be evaluated as

\displaystyle\mu_{B-L}\simeq\kappa_{hff}^{2}\times\dfrac{\Gamma_{h\rightarrow\ell\bar{\ell}\gamma}^{B-L}}{\Gamma_{h\rightarrow\ell\bar{\ell}\gamma}^{SM}}.

(123)

In the scope of this paper, we are
interested in this limit and take
the signal strengths in
Eq. (
123
) for phenomenological
analysis at the LHC.

3.2
Form factors for
e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow h\gamma

We turn our attention to evaluate
cross-sections for

e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow h\gamma
at future lepton
colliders. Applying the same method for

h\rightarrow\ell\bar{\ell}\gamma
,
one-loop amplitude
for the production process can be written
in terms of the following Lorentz structure
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow h\gamma}_{1-\text{loop}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=\{1,2\}}\sum\limits_{P=\{L,R\}}\Big\{\bar{v}(q_{2})\Big(\gamma_{\nu}\mathcal{O}_{P}\Big)K_{i}^{P}\Big[q_{i}^{\mu}q_{3}^{\nu}-\Big(q_{i}\cdot q_{3}\Big)g^{\mu\nu}\Big]u(q_{1})\Big\}\varepsilon^{*}_{\mu}(q_{3}).

(124)

Where one-loop form factors
K_{i}^{P}

are collected as coefficient of

q_{i}^{\mu}q_{3}^{\nu}
. By comparing
with one-loop form factors
F_{i}^{P}
,
we find that
the form factors
K_{i}^{P}
can be derived
from
F_{i}^{P}
by applying
the following relations:

\displaystyle K_{i,\text{$V_{0}^{*}$-pole}}^{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{i,\text{$V_{0}^{*}$-pole}}^{P};

(125)

\displaystyle K_{i,\text{Non-pole}}^{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{i,\text{Non-pole}}^{P}\;|_{q_{13}\leftrightarrow q_{23}}\quad\textrm{for}\;P=L,R.

(126)

The above relations can be explained by
applying cross-symmetry for converting
the decay process

h\rightarrow e^{-}e^{+}\gamma
to the
production scattering
e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow h\gamma
, see
[
63
]

for more detail.
Differential cross section for the
production process including initial beam
polarizations can be expressed in terms of
one-loop form factors as follows:

\displaystyle\dfrac{d\sigma_{\text{pol}}}{d\cos\theta_{e^{-}\gamma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{12}-M_{h}^{2}}{256\pi\,q_{12}}\Big\{\Big(1+\lambda_{+}\Big)\Big(1-\lambda_{-}\Big)\Big[q_{13}^{2}\big|F^{L}_{1}\big|^{2}+q_{23}^{2}\big|F^{L}_{2}\big|^{2}\Big]

(127)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\Big(1-\lambda_{+}\Big)\Big(1+\lambda_{-}\Big)\Big[q_{13}^{2}\big|F^{R}_{1}\big|^{2}+q_{23}^{2}\big|F^{R}_{2}\big|^{2}\Big]\Big\}

where
\lambda_{-}
and
\lambda_{+}
are
the polarized degrees of the
initial
e^{-}
and
e^{+}
beams, respectively.
In the above expression,
q_{23},q_{13}

are given by

\displaystyle q_{23},q_{13}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{q_{12}-M_{h}^{2}}{2}\Big(1\mp\cos\theta_{e^{-}\gamma}\Big).

(128)

Here,
\theta_{e^{-}\gamma}
is the angle
between initial electron and external photon.

The signal strengths
for the Higgs production associated
with a photon for the models
at future lepton colliders can be estimated
as follows

\displaystyle\mu_{B-L}(\sqrt{s},\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-})=\dfrac{\sigma_{B-L}}{\sigma_{SM}}(\sqrt{s},\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-}).

(129)

Where
\sqrt{s}
is center-of-mass energy.

phenomenological results for the calculations.

5
Conclusions

In this work, we have presented
one-loop form factors
for
h\rightarrow\ell\bar{\ell}\gamma

in
U(1)_{B-L}

extension of the standard model.
The computations have performed
in ’t Hooft-Feynman gauge.
We have shown that
production cross-sections for

e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow h\gamma

at future lepton colliders
can be derived
by using
one-loop form factors
in the decay

h\rightarrow\ell\bar{\ell}\gamma
.
The form factors are expressed
in terms of one-loop scalar
PV-functions in the standard notation
of
LoopTools
. As a result,
one-loop decay rates, cross-sections
as well as the signal strengths can
be evaluated numerically by using

LoopTools
. In phenomenological
results, the signal strengths for

h\rightarrow\ell\bar{\ell}\gamma
at
Large Hadron Collider and
for
e^{-}e^{+}\rightarrow h\gamma

at future Linear Colliders
have investigated in physical
parameter space for both
vector and chiral
B-L
models.
We find that the contributions
from neutral gauge boson
Z^{\prime}

to the signal strengths are
rather small.
Consequence, the effects from
Z^{\prime}

in the models are
hard to probe at future colliders.
While the impacts
of charged Higgs in the
chiral
B-L
model on
the signal strengths
are significant and
can be measured with the help
of the initial polarization
beams at
future Lepton Colliders.

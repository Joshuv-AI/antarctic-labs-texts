The gradient discretisation method for the chemical reactions of biochemical systems
Yahya Alnashri, Hasan Alzubaidi

Abstract
. We consider a biochemical model that consists of a system of partial differential equations based on reaction terms and subject to non–homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. The model is discretised using the gradient discretisation method (GDM) which is a framework covering a large class of conforming and non conforming schemes. Under classical regularity assumptions on the exact solutions, the GDM enables us to establish the existence of the model solutions in a weak sense, and strong convergence for the approximate solution and its approximate gradient. Numerical test employing a finite volume method is presented to demonstrate the behaviour of the solutions to the model.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in studding reaction diffusion equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{x},t)-\mu_{1}\mathop{\rm div}(\nabla\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{x},t))

\displaystyle=F(\bar{u},\bar{v}),\quad(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{v}(\boldsymbol{x},t)-\mu_{2}\mathop{\rm div}(\nabla\bar{v}(\boldsymbol{x},t))

\displaystyle=G(\bar{u},\bar{v}),\quad(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.1b)

where
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are the diffusion coefficients corresponding to the chemical concentrations
\bar{u}
and
\bar{v}
respectively. The functions
F
and
G
describe the governing kinetics of the chemical reactions and some biochemical phenomena have been expressed in literature based on the choice of these reaction terms. For an example, in 2014, Yung–Rong Lee and Sy-Sang Lia
[
16
]
proposed a reaction-diffusion model that stimulated the relationship between concentrations of oxygen and lactic acid to simulate oxidative phosphorylation and glycolysis reactions in tissues. They concluded that a reaction-diffusion model can generate and maintain the ideal micro-environment for stem cells.

Another example is the Gray-Scott model, a very well-known reaction-diffusion system. The model describes the chemical reaction between two substances, an activator
\bar{v}
and substrate
\bar{u}
where both of which diffuse over time and so represents what is called cubic autocatalysis
[
15
]
.

The Brusselator model is also an example of such chemical reaction systems which is used to describe mechanism of chemical reaction – diffusion with non-linear oscillations
[
17
]
. The model occurs in a large number of physical problems such as the formation of ozone by atomic oxygen, in enzymatic reactions, and arises in laser and plasma physics from multiple coupling between modes
[
19
,
4
]
.

The gradient discretisation method (GDM) is a generic framework to design numerical schemes together with their convergence analysis for different models which are based on partial differential equations. It covers a variety of numerical schemes such as finite volumes, finite elements, discontinuous Galerkin, etc. We refer the reader to
[

,

,

The main purpose of this paper is the proof of convergence, in the GDM setting, of the approximate solutions to the weak solution of a system of reaction diffusion equations with non–homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. The convergence is established without non–physical regularity assumptions on the solutions since it is based on discrete compactness techniques detailed in

2.
Continuous model

We consider the following biochemical system of partial differential equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{x},t)-\mu_{1}\mathop{\rm div}(\nabla\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{x},t))

\displaystyle=F(\bar{u},\bar{v}),\quad(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),

(2.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{v}(\boldsymbol{x},t)-\mu_{2}\mathop{\rm div}(\nabla\bar{v}(\boldsymbol{x},t))

\displaystyle=G(\bar{u},\bar{v}),\quad(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{x},t)

\displaystyle=g,\quad(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{x},t)

\displaystyle=h,\quad(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{x},0)

\displaystyle=u_{\rm ini}(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\bar{v}(\boldsymbol{x},0)

\displaystyle=v_{\rm ini}(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega.

(2.6)

Our analysis focuses on the weak formulation of the above reaction diffusion model. Let us assume the following properties on the data of the model.

Assumptions 2.1
.

The assumptions on the data in Problem (
2.1
)–(
2.6
) are the following:

•

\Omega
is an open bounded connected subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\;(d>1)
,
T>0
,
\mu_{1},\;\mu_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
,

•

(u_{\rm ini},v_{\rm ini})
are in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

•

g
and
h
are traces of functions in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
whose time derivatives are in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))
,

•

the functions
F,\;G:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
are Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constants
L_{F}
and
L_{G}
, respectively.

Under Assumptions
2.1
, the weak solution of (
2.1
)–(
2.6
) is seeking

\displaystyle\bar{u}\in\{w\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\;:\;\gamma(\bar{u})=g\},\bar{v}\in\{w\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\;:\;\gamma(\bar{v})=h\}\mbox{ such that }

\displaystyle\bar{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)),\;\bar{v}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\forall\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)),\;\partial_{t}\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\;\varphi(\cdot,T)=0,

\displaystyle\forall\psi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)),\;\partial_{t}\psi\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\;\psi(\cdot,T)=0,

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{x},t)\partial_{t}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\mu_{1}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{x},t)\cdot\nabla\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t,

(2.7a)

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{\rm ini}(\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},0)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F(\bar{u},\bar{v})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bar{v}(\boldsymbol{x},t)\partial_{t}\psi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\mu_{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\bar{v}(\boldsymbol{x},t)\cdot\nabla\psi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

(2.7b)

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v_{\rm ini}(\boldsymbol{x})\psi(\boldsymbol{x},0)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}G(\bar{u},\bar{v})\psi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t.

3.
Discrete Problem

The analysis of numerical schemes for the approximation of solutions to our model is performed using the GDM. The first step to reach this analysis is the reconstruction of a set of discrete spaces and operators, which is called gradient discretisation.

Definition 3.1

(Gradient discretisation for time–dependent problems with non–homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions)
.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(with
d>1
) and
T>0
. A gradient discretisation for time–dependent problems with non–homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions is
{\mathcal{D}}=(X_{{\mathcal{D}}},\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{D}},\partial},\Pi_{\mathcal{D}},\nabla_{\mathcal{D}},J_{\mathcal{D}},(t^{(n)})_{n=0,...,N}))
, where

•

the set of discrete unknowns
X_{{\mathcal{D}}}=X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}\oplus X_{{\mathcal{D}},\partial\Omega}
is the direct sum of two finite dimensional spaces on
{\mathbb{R}}
, corresponding respectively to the interior unknowns and to the boundary unknowns,

•

the linear mapping
\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{D}},\partial}:H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)\to X_{{\mathcal{D}},\partial\Omega}
is an interpolation operator for the trace,

•

the function reconstruction
\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}:X_{{\mathcal{D}}}\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is a linear,

•

the gradient reconstruction
\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}:X_{{\mathcal{D}}}\to L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
is a linear and must be defined so that
\|\cdot||_{\mathcal{D}}:=||\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\cdot||_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}
defines a norm on
X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}
,

•

J_{\mathcal{D}}:L^{\infty}(\Omega)\to X_{\mathcal{D}}
is a linear and continuous interpolation operators for the
initial conditions,

•

t^{(0)}=0<t^{(1)}<....<t^{(N)}=T
are discrete times.

Let us introduce some notations to define the space–time reconstructions
\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi:\Omega\times[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
, and
\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi:\Omega\times[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and the discrete time derivative
\delta_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi:(0,T)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
, for
\varphi=(\varphi^{(n)})_{n=0,...,N}\in X_{\mathcal{D}}^{N}
.

For a.e
\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega
, for all
n\in\{0,...,N-1\}
and for all
t\in(t^{(n)},t^{(n+1)}]
, let

\begin{split}&\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},0)=\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi^{(0)}(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x}),\\
&\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x}).\end{split}

Set
\delta t^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}=t^{(n+1)}-t^{(n)}
and
\delta t_{\mathcal{D}}=\max_{n=0,...,N-1}\delta t^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}
, to define

\delta_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(t)=\delta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}\varphi:=\frac{\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi^{(n+1)}-\varphi^{(n)})}{\delta t^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}}.

Setting the gradient discretisation defined previously in the place of the continuous space and operators in the weak formulation of the model leads to a numerical scheme, called a gradient scheme.

Definition 3.2

(Gradient scheme)
.

The gradient scheme for the continuous problem (
2.7
) is to find families of pair
(u^{(n)},v^{(n)})_{n=0,...,N}\in({\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}},\partial}g+X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}^{N+1})\times({\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}},\partial}h+X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}^{N+1})
such that
(u^{(0)},v^{(0)})=(J_{\mathcal{D}}u_{\rm ini},J_{\mathcal{D}}v_{\rm ini})
, and for all
n=0,...,N-1
,
u^{(n+1)}
and
v^{(n+1)}
satisfy the following equalities:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}u(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})+\mu_{1}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}u^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

(3.1a)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F(\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}u^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x}),\Pi_{{\mathcal{D}}}v^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x}))\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},\quad\forall\varphi\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0},\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}v(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\psi(\boldsymbol{x})+\mu_{2}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}v^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\psi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

(3.1b)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}G(\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}u^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x}),\Pi_{{\mathcal{D}}}v^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x}))\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\psi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},\quad\forall\psi\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}.

In order to establish the stability and convergence of the above gradient scheme, sequences of gradient discretisations
({\mathcal{D}}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
described in Definition
3
are required to satisfy four properties; coercivity, consistency, limit–conformity and compactness.

Definition 3.3

(Coercivity)
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a gradient discretisation and let
C_{\mathcal{D}}
be defined by

C_{\mathcal{D}}=\max_{\varphi\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}-\{0\}}\displaystyle\frac{\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}}.

(3.2)

A sequence
({\mathcal{D}}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of a gradient discretisation is coercive if
C_{\mathcal{D}}
is bounded.

Definition 3.4

(Consistency)
.

If
{\mathcal{D}}
is a gradient discretisation, define the function

S_{\mathcal{D}}:H^{1}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty)
by, for
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi)=\min\Big\{\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}w-\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}w-\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}\;:\;w\in X_{\mathcal{D}},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mbox{ such that }w-\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{D}},\partial}\gamma\varphi\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}\Big\},

A sequence
(\mathcal{D}_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
of gradient discretisations is
consistent
if, as
m\to\infty

•

for all
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,
S_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}(\varphi)\to 0
,

•

for all
w\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\Pi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}J_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}w\to w
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
,

•

\delta t_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\to 0
.

Definition 3.5

(Limit-conformity)
.

If
{\mathcal{D}}
is a gradient discretisation, and the space
H_{\rm div}=\{{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\;:\;{\rm div}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}
, define the function
W_{\mathcal{D}}:H_{\rm div}\to[0,+\infty)
by, for all
{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\in H_{\rm div}
,

W_{\mathcal{D}}({\boldsymbol{\psi}})=\sup_{w\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\Big|\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}w\cdot{\boldsymbol{\psi}}+\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}w\mathop{\rm div}({\boldsymbol{\psi}}))\,\mathrm{d}x\Big|}{\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}}.

(3.4)

A sequence
({\mathcal{D}}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of gradient discretisations is
limit-conforming
if for all
{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\in H_{\rm div}
,
W_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}({\boldsymbol{\psi}})\to 0
, as
m\to\infty
.

Definition 3.6

(Compactness)
.

A sequence of gradient discretisation
({\mathcal{D}}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in the sense of Definition
3.1
is
compact
if for any sequence
(\varphi_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in X_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m},0}
, such that
(\|\nabla_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\varphi_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is bounded, the sequence
(\Pi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\varphi_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is relatively compact in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Definition 3.7

(Dual norm on
\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(X_{\mathcal{D}})
)
.

If
{\mathcal{D}}
be a gradient discretisation, the dual norm
\|\cdot\|_{\star,{\mathcal{D}}}
on
\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(X_{{\mathcal{D}},0})
is defined by, for all
w\in\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(X_{{\mathcal{D}},0})
,

\|w\|_{\star,{\mathcal{D}}}=\displaystyle\sup\Big\{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\;:\;\varphi\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0},\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}=1\Big\}.

(3.5)

4.
Convergence results

Our convergence results are stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1

(Convergence of the gradient scheme)
.

Assume (
2.1
) and let
({\mathcal{D}}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of gradient discretisations, that is coercive, consistent, limit-conforming and compact. For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
(u_{m},v_{m})\in({\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m},\partial}g+X_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m},0}^{N+1})\times({\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m},\partial}h+X_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m},0}^{N+1})
be a solution to the gradient scheme (
3.1
) with
{\mathcal{D}}={\mathcal{D}}_{m}
. Then there exists a weak solution
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
of (
2.7
) and a subsequence of gradient discretisations, still denoted by
({\mathcal{D}}_{m})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, such that, as
m\to\infty
,

(1)

\Pi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}u_{m}
converges strongly to
\bar{u}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
,

(2)

\Pi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}v_{m}
converges strongly to
\bar{v}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
,

(3)

\nabla_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}u_{m}
converges strongly to
\nabla\bar{u}
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))^{d}
,

(4)

\nabla_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}v_{m}
converges strongly to
\nabla\bar{v}
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))^{d}
.

Proof.

The proof relies on the compactness arguments as in
[
9
]
, and is divided into four stages.

Step 1:

Take liftings
\bar{g}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
of
g
and
\bar{h}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
of
h
, such that
\gamma\bar{g}=g
and
\gamma\bar{h}=h
. Thanks to the density of space–time tensorial functions in the space
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
established in
[
6
, Corollary 1.3.1]
, we can express the liftings
\bar{g}
and
\bar{h}
in the following way: let
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
(\phi)_{i=1,...,\ell},\;(\xi)_{i=1,...,\ell}\subset C^{\infty}([0,T])
, and
(p_{i})_{i=1,...,\ell},\;(q_{i})_{i=1,...,\ell}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\bar{g}(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\phi_{i}(t)p_{i}(\boldsymbol{x})\mbox{ and a.e. $\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega$ and for all $t\in(0,T)$},

\bar{h}(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\xi_{i}(t)q_{i}(\boldsymbol{x})\mbox{ and a.e. $\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega$ and for all $t\in(0,T)$}.

Let
g_{\mathcal{D}},\;h_{\mathcal{D}}\in X_{{\mathcal{D}}}^{N_{m}+1}
be defined by
g_{\mathcal{D}}^{(n)}=\phi(t^{(n)})I_{\mathcal{D}}p
and
h_{\mathcal{D}}^{(n)}=\xi(t^{(n)})I_{\mathcal{D}}q
for
0,...,N_{m}
, where

I_{\mathcal{D}}p=\argminB_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}},\partial g}+X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}}\left(\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi-p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi-\nabla p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}\right),

I_{\mathcal{D}}q=\argminB_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}},\partial h}+X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}}\left(\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi-q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi-\nabla q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}\right).

From the consistency property, as
m\to\infty
, we have

(1)

\Pi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}g_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\to\bar{g}
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))
and
\Pi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}h_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\to\bar{h}
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))
,

(2)

\nabla_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}g_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\to\nabla\bar{g}
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))^{d}
and
\nabla_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}h_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\to\nabla\bar{h}
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))^{d}
,

(3)

\delta_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}g_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\to\partial_{t}\bar{g}
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))
and
\delta_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}h_{{\mathcal{D}}_{m}}\to\partial_{t}\bar{h}
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))
.

For any solution
(u,v)
to the gradient scheme (
3.1
), writing
w_{1}=u-g_{\mathcal{D}}\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}
and
w_{2}=v-h_{\mathcal{D}}\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}
, we have for all
\varphi,\;\psi\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}w_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})+\mu_{1}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}w_{1}^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

(4.1a)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F(\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(w_{1}+g_{\mathcal{D}}),\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(w_{2}+h_{\mathcal{D}}))\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}g_{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})-\mu_{1}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}g_{\mathcal{D}}^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}w_{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\psi(\boldsymbol{x})+\mu_{2}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}w_{2}^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\psi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

(4.1b)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}G(\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(w_{1}+g_{\mathcal{D}}),\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(w_{2}+h_{\mathcal{D}}))\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\psi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}h_{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\psi(\boldsymbol{x})-\mu_{2}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}h_{\mathcal{D}}^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\psi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}.

Step 2:
We need to have estimates on the quantities
\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}w_{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}
,
\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}w_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}
,
\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}w_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))^{d}}
, and
\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}w_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T))^{d}}
.

Let
n\in\{0,...,N-1\}
and put
\varphi:=\delta t^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}w_{1}^{(n+1)}
in (
4.1a
) and
\psi:=\delta t^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}w_{2}^{(n+1)}
in (
4.1b
). We have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}w_{1}^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})-\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}w_{1}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{x})\Big)\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}w_{1}^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle+\mu_{1}\delta t^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}w_{1}^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}w_{1}^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\delta t^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F(\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(w_{1}^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})+g_{\mathcal{D}}),\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}(w_{2}^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})+h_{\mathcal{D}}))\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}w_{1}^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle-\delta t^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}g_{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{x})\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}w_{1}^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

To measure the efficiency of the gradient scheme (
3.1
) for the continuous problem (
2.1
)–(
2.6
), we consider a particular choice of the gradient discrtisation method known as the Hybrid Mimetic Mixed (HMM) method, which is a kind of finite volume scheme and can be written in three different formats; the hybrid finite volume method
[
13
]
, the (mixed–hybrid) mimetic finite differences methods
[
5
]
, and the mixed finite volume methods
[
18
]
. For the sake of completeness we briefly recall the definition of this gradient discretisation. Let
\mathcal{T}=({\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{V})
be the polytopal mesh of the spatial domain
\Omega
used in the previous section and described in
[
9
, Definition 7.2]
. The elements of the gradient discretisation are:

•

The discrete spaces are

X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}=\{v=((\varphi_{K})_{K\in\mathcal{M}},(\varphi_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}}})\;:\;\varphi_{K},\,\varphi_{\sigma}\in{\mathbb{R}},\;\varphi_{\sigma}=0,\;\forall\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}}\cap\partial\Omega\},

\displaystyle X_{{\mathcal{D}},\partial\Omega}=\{

\displaystyle v=((v_{K})_{K\in\mathcal{M}},(v_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}}})\;:\;v_{K}\in{\mathbb{R}},\,v_{\sigma}\in{\mathbb{R}},

\displaystyle v_{K}=0\mbox{ for all }K\in{\mathcal{M}},\,v_{\sigma}=0\mbox{ for all }\sigma\in{{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\rm int}}\}.

•

The non conforming a piecewise affine reconstruction
\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\forall\varphi\in X_{{\mathcal{D}}},\forall K\in{\mathcal{M}},\mbox{ for a.e. }\boldsymbol{x}\in K,

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi=\varphi_{K}\mbox{ on $K$}.

•

The reconstructed gradients is piecewise constant on the cells (broken gradient), defined by

\displaystyle\forall\varphi\in X_{{\mathcal{D}}},\;\forall K\in\mathcal{M},\,\forall\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}}_{K},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi=\nabla_{K}\varphi+\frac{\sqrt{d}}{d_{K,\sigma}}R_{K}(\varphi)\mathbf{n}_{K,\sigma}\mbox{ on }D_{K,\sigma},

where a cell–wise constant gradient
\nabla_{K}\varphi
and a stabilisation term
R_{K}(\varphi)
are respectively defined by:

\nabla_{K}\varphi=\displaystyle\frac{1}{|K|}\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}}_{K}}|\sigma|\varphi_{\sigma}\mathbf{n}_{K,\sigma}\mbox{ and }R_{K}(\varphi)=(\varphi_{\sigma}-\varphi_{K}-\nabla_{K}\varphi\cdot(x_{\sigma}-x_{K}))_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}}_{K}},

in which
x_{\sigma}
is centre of mass of
\sigma
,
x_{K}
is the gravity centre of cell
K
,
d_{K,\sigma}
is the orthogonal distance between
x_{K}
and
\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}}_{K}
,
{\bf n}_{K,\sigma}
is the unit vector normal to
\sigma
outward to
K
and
D_{K,\sigma}
is the convex hull of
\sigma\cup\{x_{K}\}
.

•

The interpolant
J_{\mathcal{D}}:L^{\infty}(\Omega)\to X_{{\mathcal{D}}}
is defined by:

\displaystyle\forall w\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\;:\;J_{\mathcal{D}}w=((w_{K})_{K\in{\mathcal{M}}},(w_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}}}),

\displaystyle\forall K\in{\mathcal{M}},\;w_{K}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{|K|}\displaystyle\int_{K}w(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\mbox{ and }\forall\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}},\;w_{\sigma}=0.

•

the interpolant
{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}},\partial}:H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)\to X_{{\mathcal{D}},\partial\Omega}
is defined by

\displaystyle\forall g\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)\,:{}

\displaystyle({\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}},\partial}g)_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int_{\sigma}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}s(x),

\displaystyle\mbox{ for all }\sigma\in{{{\mathcal{F}}}_{\rm ext}}\mbox{ such that }\sigma\subset\partial\Omega.

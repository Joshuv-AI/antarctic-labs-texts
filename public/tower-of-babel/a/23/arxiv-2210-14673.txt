Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the L p L^{p} -boundedness of the Riesz means and the L p 1 × L p 2 → L p L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p} boundedness of the bilinear Riesz means on Métivier groups. Métivier groups are generalization of Heisenberg groups and general H-type groups. Because general Métivier groups only satisfy the non-degeneracy condition and have high-dimensional centre, we have to use different methods and techniques from those on Heisenberg groups and H-type groups.

1.
Introduction

Métivier groups, introduced by Métivier
[
9
]
in his study
of analytic hypoellipticity, are two-step nilpotent Lie groups satisfying a
non-degeneracy condition. They are also characterized by the property that
the quotients with respect to the hyperplanes contained in the centre are
general Heisenberg groups. H-type groups, introduced by Kaplan
[
5
]
, are typical examples of Métivier groups, but there are many Métivier groups which are not isomorphic to H-type groups, for example, see

[
12
]
. Heisenberg groups are the simplest Métivier
groups. Casarino and Ciatti
[
2
]
investigated the spectral resolution
of the sub-Laplacian on Métivier groups, and proved a Stein-Tomas
restriction theorem in terms of the mixed norms. So, we can use the the
spectral decomposition of the sub-Laplacian to define the Riesz means and
the bilinear Riesz means on Métivier groups. Mauceri
[
8
]
and Müller
[
11
]
obtained the same results on the
L^{p}
-boundedness
of the Riesz means on Heisenberg groups by using different methods. We
proved the
L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}
boundedness of the
bilinear Riesz means on Heisenberg groups
[
6
]
. To extend these
results to general Métivier groups, we notice that there are two
essential difficulties: one is that Heisenberg groups have one dimensional
centre but the centre dimension of Métivier groups is in general bigger
than one; the other is that on H-type groups, the non-degeneracy condition
becomes a better orthogonality condition, which is not true on general Métivier groups. Therefore, in this paper, we shall introduce some different
techniques from those used in
[
8
]
or
[
11
]
and
[
6
]
to
obtain the
L^{p}
- boundedness of the Riesz means and
L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}
boundedness of the bilinear Riesz means on Métivier groups.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall the spectral
decomposition of the sub-Laplacian on Metivier groups and define the Riesz
means and bilinear Riesz means. In Section 3, we give the pointwise
estimates for the kernel of the Riesz means and the kerner of the bilinear
Riesz means. In Section 4, we present the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the Riesz
means. In the rest of this paper, we study the
L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}
boundedness of the bilinear Riesz means, which
for the case of
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq 2
in Section 5 and for some
particular cases in Section 6. In Section 7, we outline the bilinear
interpolation method and obtain the results in other cases.

2.
Preliminaries

We first recall Métivier groups. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a real finite
dimensional two step nilpotent Lie algebra, equipped with an inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
. Then

\mathfrak{g}\,=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},

where
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]=\mathfrak{g}_{2}
,
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}]=[\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{2}]=0
and
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{1}=d
,
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2}=m
. Let
\{X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{d}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
,
\{U_{1},U_{2},\cdots,U_{m}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
.

Define for
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}^{*}
the skew symmetric form
\omega_{\mu}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
by

\omega_{\mu}(X,Y)=\mu([X,Y])

and the corrsponding metrix

(J_{\mu})_{jk}=\omega_{\mu}(X_{j},X_{k}).

We say that the corresponding connected, simply connected Lie group
\mathbb{G}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a Métivier group (M-type group) if
\omega_{\mu}
is non-degenerate for all
\mu\neq 0
. Under this non-degenerate
hypothesis,
J_{\mu}
is a
d\times d
skew symmetric, invertible metrix
for all
\mu\neq 0
. It follows that
d=2n
is even. Since the exponential
mapping is a bijection, we shall parametrise the element
g=\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2n}x_{i}X_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}U_{j}\right)\in\mathbb{G}
by

(x,u)
where
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2n})\in\mathfrak{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
,
u=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{m})\in\mathfrak{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. By the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula, the group multiplication is
given by

(x,u)\cdot(y,v)=(x+y,u+v+\frac{1}{2}[x,y]),

where
[x,y]=(\left\langle x,J_{1}y\right\rangle,\left\langle x,J_{2}y\right\rangle,\cdots,\left\langle x,J_{m}y\right\rangle)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
J_{k}
is
2n\times 2n
skew-symmetric, non-degenerate metrix for
any
k=1,\cdots,m
. The identity element of
\mathbb{G}
is
(0,0)
and the
inversion of
(x,u)
is denoted by
(x,u)^{-1}=(-x,-u)
.

A homogeneous structure on
\mathbb{G}
is obtained by defining the
dilations
\delta_{t}(x,u)=(tx,t^{2}u)
,
t>0
. The homogeneous dimension
of
\mathbb{G}
is

Q=2n+2m.

The Haar measure on
\mathbb{G}
coincides with the Lebesgue measure on
\mathfrak{g}
denoted by
dxdu
. It is easy to verify that the Jacobian
determinant of the dilations
\delta_{t}
,
t>0
is constant, equal to
t^{Q}
. Define a homogeneous norm of degree one under the dilations
\{\delta_{t}
,
t>0\}
on
\mathbb{G}
by

\left|\omega\right|=\left|(x,u)\right|=\left(\frac{1}{16}\left|x\right|^{4}+\left|u\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}},\text{ }\omega=(x,u)\in\mathbb{G}\text{.}

This norm satisfies the triangle inequality
\left|\omega\cdot\omega^{\prime}\right|\leq\left|\omega\right|+\left|\omega^{\prime}\right|
and leads to a left-invariant distance
d(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=\left|\omega^{-1}\cdot\omega^{\prime}\right|
.

For any
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
, their convolution is defined by

f\ast g(x,u)=\int_{\mathbb{G}}f((x,u)\cdot(y,v)^{-1})g(y,v)dydv.

The
\mu
-twisted convolution of two suitable functions or distributions on

\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is defined by

\phi\times_{\mu}\psi(x)=\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\phi(x-y)\psi(y)e^{\frac{i}{2}\mu([x,y])}d_{\mu}y,

where
d_{\mu}y=\sqrt{\det J_{\mu}}dy
. Given
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
, we
define the Fourier transform with respect to the central variables by

f^{\mu}(x)=\int_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}f(x,u)e^{i\mu(u)}du.

It is easy to verify that

(f\ast g)^{\mu}=f^{\mu}\times_{\mu}g^{\mu}.

For any
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, we obtain a left-invariant vector field
X

on
\mathbb{G}
defined by

Xf(g)=\frac{d}{dt}f(g\cdot(tX))|_{t=0\text{, \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }}f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\text{, }g\in\mathbb{G}.

Then, the orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
,
\{X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{2n}\}
, is associated to the left-variant vector field:

X_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\langle x,J_{k}e_{j}\right\rangle\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}},\text{ \ \ }e_{j}=(0,\cdots,1_{j},0,\cdots,0_{2n})^{T},\ \ j=1,2,\cdots,2n.

The sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{G}
is the left-invariant hypoelliptic
operator defined by

\mathcal{L=-}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}X_{j}^{2}=-\Delta_{x}-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\langle x^{T}J_{k},\nabla_{x}\right\rangle\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}}-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\left\langle x^{T}J_{k},x^{T}J_{l}\right\rangle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial u_{p}\partial u_{q}},

where

\Delta_{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}},\quad\quad\nabla_{x}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}},\cdots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2n}}\right)^{T}.

For any
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{G})
and

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{i}{2}\left\langle x,J_{\mu}e_{j}\right\rangle\right)f^{\mu}(x).

we have that

\left(X_{j}f\right)^{\mu}=X_{j}^{\mu}f^{\mu}.

Define

\Delta^{\mu}=-\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\left(X_{j}^{\mu}\right)^{2}.

It follows that

\left(\mathcal{L}f\right)^{\mu}=\Delta^{\mu}f^{\mu}.

The new coordinates of the element in

More explicitly,

It follows that

\displaystyle L^{\lambda\eta}

\displaystyle=

S_{R}^{\delta}(x,u)=R^{\frac{Q}{2}}S_{1}^{\delta}\left(\sqrt{R}x,Ru\right),

we see that the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
S_{R}^{\delta}
can be deduced from
the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
S_{1}^{\delta}
. We write
S^{\delta}=S_{1}^{\delta}
and it is suffices to consider the bounds of
S^{\delta}
.

The bilinear Riesz means associated to
\mathcal{L}
for
f,g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{G})
is defined by

S_{R}^{\alpha}(f,g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}{R}\right)_{+}^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{1}}fP_{\lambda_{2}}gd\lambda_{1}d\lambda_{2}.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle S_{R}^{\alpha}(f,g)(x,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}f((x,u)\cdot(x_{1},u_{1})^{-1})g((x,u)\cdot(x_{2},u_{2})^{-1})

\displaystyle\times S_{R}^{\delta}((x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}))dx_{1}du_{1}dx_{2}du_{2},

where the kernel is given by

\displaystyle S_{R}^{\alpha}((x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{Q}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{(2k+n)\lambda_{1}+(2l+n)\lambda_{2}}{R}\right)_{+}^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}e_{k}^{\lambda_{1}\eta_{1}}(x_{1},u_{1})d\sigma(\eta_{1})

\displaystyle\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\times\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}e_{k}^{\lambda_{2}\eta_{2}}(x_{2},u_{2})d\sigma(\eta_{2})\lambda_{1}^{n+m-1}\lambda_{2}^{n+m-1}d\lambda_{1}d\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{Q}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left(1-\frac{(2k+n)\left|\mu_{1}\right|+(2l+n)\left|\mu_{2}\right|}{R}\right)_{+}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\times e_{k}^{\mu_{1}}(x_{1},u_{1})e_{k}^{\mu_{2}}(x_{2},u_{2})\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{n}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{n}d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}.

Note that

S_{R}^{\alpha}((x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}))=R^{Q}S_{1}^{\alpha}\left((\sqrt{R}x_{1},Ru_{1}),(\sqrt{R}x_{2},Ru_{2})\right).

By a dilation argument, the
L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}

boundedness of
S_{R}^{\alpha}
is deduced from the
L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}
boundedness of
S_{1}^{\alpha}
as
1/p=1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}
. Thus, we will concentrate on the operator
S_{1}^{\alpha}
and write
S^{\alpha}=S_{1}^{\alpha}
.

3.
Pointwise estimate for the kernel

In this section, we investigate the pointwise estimates for the kernel of
the Riesz means
S^{\delta}
and the bilinear Riesz means
S^{\alpha}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
S^{\delta}(x,u)
be the kernel of the Riesz means
S^{\delta}
. Assume that
N
is a positive integer. If
\delta>2N-1
,
then,

\left|S^{\delta}(x,u)\right|\leq C_{N}\left(1+\left|\left(A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}x,u\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}.

for any
\omega=(x,u)\in\mathbb{G}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\backslash\{0\}
.

Proof.

Since that

e_{k}^{\mu}(x,u)=e^{-i\mu(u)}\left(\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}\circ A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right)(x)\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|,

we have

\displaystyle S^{\delta}(x,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left(1-(2k+n)\left|\mu\right|\right)_{+}^{\delta}e_{k}^{\mu}(x,u)\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-i\mu(u)}\left(1-(2k+n)\left|\mu\right|\right)_{+}^{\delta}\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}\left(A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}x\right)\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu.

Let
z=A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}x
and

(3.1)

F(z,u)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-i\mu(u)}\left(1-(2k+n)\left|\mu\right|\right)_{+}^{\delta}\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}\left(z\right)\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu.

F(z,u)
is a radial fuction with respect to
z
. We set
r=\left|z\right|
and define

(3.2)

R_{k}(\mu,F)=\frac{2^{(1-n)}k!}{(k+n-1)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}F^{\mu}(r)\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}(r)r^{2n-1}dr,

where

F^{\mu}(r)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}F(r,u)e^{i\mu(u)}du.

Using the inversion formula of the Fourier transform and the Laguerre
expension of the radial function (see
[
15
]
), we have that

(3.3)

F(z,u)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-i\mu(u)}R_{k}(\mu,F)\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}(z)\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu.

Notice that

\left\|\varphi_{k}\right\|_{\infty}=c_{n}\frac{(k+n-1)!}{k!}.

So, if we can show that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|R_{k}\left(\mu,F\right)\right|\frac{\left(k+n-1\right)!}{k!}\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu<\infty

then
F
is bounded. Similarly, if we can show that for any
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, there exists a constant
C_{N}>0
such that for each
j=1,2,\cdots,m,

(3.4)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|R_{k}\left(\mu,\left(iu_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}\frac{1}{4}\left|z\right|^{2}\right)^{N}F\right)\right|\frac{\left(k+n-1\right)!}{k!}\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu<C_{N}\text{,}

then

\left|(z,u)\right|^{4N}\left|F\right|^{2}=\left(\sup_{j}\left|u_{j}\right|^{2}+\sup_{j}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{\left|\mu\right|^{2}}\frac{1}{16}\left|z\right|^{4}\right)^{N}\left|F\right|^{2}=\sup_{j}\left|\left(iu_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}\frac{1}{4}\left|z\right|^{2}\right)^{N}F\right|^{2}\leq C_{N}^{2},

namely,

\left|F(z,u)\right|\leq C_{N}\left(1+\left|(z,u)\right|\right)^{-2N}.

This yields that for any
\omega=(x,u)\in\mathbb{G}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\backslash\{0\}
,

\left|S^{\delta}(x,u)\right|=\left|F\left(A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}x,u\right)\right|\leq C_{N}\left(1+\left|\left(A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}x,u\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}.

Thus, to obtain Theorem
1
, it suffices to prove (
3.4
).
Fixing
j=1,\cdots,m
. We first let
N=1
and calculate
R_{k}\left(\mu,\left(iu_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}\frac{1}{4}\left|z\right|^{2}\right)F\right)
. From (
3.2
), we know
that

R_{k}(\mu,iu_{j}F)=\frac{2^{(1-n)}k!}{(k+n-1)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(iu_{j}F\right)^{\mu}(r)\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}(r)r^{2n-1}dr.

Since that

\left(iu_{j}F\right)^{\mu}(r)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_{j}}F^{\mu}(r),

then

R_{k}(\mu,iu_{j}F)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_{j}}R_{k}(\mu,F)-\frac{2^{(1-n)}k!}{(k+n-1)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}F^{\mu}(r)\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_{j}}\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}(r)r^{2n-1}dr.

Noticing

\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_{j}}\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}(r)=\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}\left(L_{k}^{n-1}\right)^{\prime}(\frac{1}{2}\left|\mu\right|r^{2})e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left|\mu\right|r^{2}}-\frac{1}{4}r^{2}\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}L_{k}^{n-1}(\frac{1}{2}\left|\mu\right|r^{2})e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left|\mu\right|r^{2}},

and using the recursion formula (see
[
15
]
)

r\frac{d}{dr}L_{k}^{n-1}(r)=kL_{k}^{n-1}(r)-(k+n-1)L_{k-1}^{n-1}(r),

we have that

(3.5)

\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_{j}}\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}(r)=\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}\left(\left|\mu\right|^{-1}k\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}(r)-\left|\mu\right|^{-1}(k+n-1)\varphi_{k-1}^{\left|\mu\right|}(r)-\frac{1}{4}r^{2}\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}(r)\right).

Then,

R_{k}\left(\mu,\left(iu_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}\frac{1}{4}r^{2}\right)F\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_{j}}R_{k}(\mu,F)-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}\left(\frac{k}{\left|\mu\right|}R_{k}(\mu,F)-\frac{k}{\left|\mu\right|}R_{k-1}(\mu,F)\right).

(
3.1
) and (
3.3
) yield that

R_{k}(\mu,F)=(1-(2k+n)\left|\mu\right|)_{+}^{\delta}\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|.

We set
\sigma=(2k+n)\left|\mu\right|
and
\psi(\sigma)=(1-\sigma)_{+}^{\delta}
. Then,
R_{k}\left(\mu,\left(iu_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}\frac{1}{4}r^{2}\right)F\right)
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle R_{k}\left(\mu,\left(iu_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}\frac{1}{4}r^{2}\right)F\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_{j}}\left(\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|\psi(\sigma)\right)-\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}\left(\frac{k}{\left|\mu\right|}\psi(\sigma)-\frac{k}{\left|\mu\right|}\psi(\sigma-2\left|\mu\right|)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_{j}}\left(\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|\psi(\sigma)\right)-\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|k\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial k}\psi\left((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|\right)

\displaystyle+\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|k\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial k}\psi\left((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|\right)-\psi((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|)+\psi(\left(2k-2+n\right)\left|\mu\right|)\right).

Using Taylor expansion, we get that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial k}\psi((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|)-\psi((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|)+\psi((2k-2+n)\left|\mu\right|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\left|\mu\right|^{2}\int_{k-1}^{k}(s+1-k)\psi^{\prime\prime}((2s+n)\left|\mu\right|)ds.

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}k\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|}{\left|\mu\right|^{2}}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial k}\psi((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|)-k\psi((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|)+k\psi((2k-2+n)\left|\mu\right|)\right|\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4k\int_{k-1}^{k}(s+1-k)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|\mu_{j}\right|(1-(2s+n)\left|\mu\right|)^{\delta-2}\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{k-1}^{k}(2s+n)^{-n-m-1}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(2k+n)^{-n-m}

if
(1-\left|\mu\right|)_{+}^{\delta-2}
is integrable on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. At the same time, we see that

\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_{j}}\left(\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|\psi(\sigma)\right)-\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|k\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial k}\psi\left((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|\right)=\psi(\sigma)\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_{j}}\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|.

So,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_{j}}\left(\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|\psi(\sigma)\right)-\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|k\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial k}\psi\left((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|\right)\right|\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\psi(\sigma)\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_{j}}\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|\right|\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi((2k+n)\lambda)\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta_{j}}\left|\det A_{\eta}\right|\right|d\eta\right)\lambda^{n+m-1}d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2k+n)^{-n-m}.

An iteration of the process shows that
R_{k}\left(\mu,\left(iu_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}\frac{1}{4}r^{2}\right)^{l}F\right)

also satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|R_{k}\left(\mu,\left(iu_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}\frac{1}{4}r^{2}\right)^{l}F\right)\right|\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu\leq C_{l}(2k+n)^{-n-m}

provided that
(1-\left|\mu\right|)_{+}^{\delta-2}
is
integrable on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Thus, when
l=N
and
\delta>2N-1
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|R_{k}\left(\mu,\left(iu_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}\frac{1}{4}r^{2}\right)^{N}F\right)\right|\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu\leq C_{N}(2k+n)^{-n-m},

which implies that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|R_{k}\left(\mu,\left(iu_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{\left|\mu\right|}\frac{1}{4}\left|z\right|^{2}\right)^{N}F\right)\right|\frac{\left(k+n-1\right)!}{k!}\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu<\infty.

The proof of Theorem
1
is completed.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. If
\delta>Q+1
, then
S^{\delta}

is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{G})
into
L^{p}(\mathbb{G})
.

Proof.

We take
N=\frac{Q}{2}+1
. If
\delta>Q+1
, we have
\delta>2N-1
. Then,
Theorem
1
is available. By Hölder’s inequality and Young’s
inequality, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|S^{\delta}f\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{p}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(1+\left|\left(A_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}x,u\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}dxdu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{p}\left|\det A_{\eta}\right|^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(1+\left|\left(x,u\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}dxdu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|f\right\|_{p}\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-2N+Q-1}du\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{p}\text{.}

The proof is completed.
∎

Next, we show the pointwise estimate of the kernel of the bilinear Riesz
means
S^{\alpha}
.

Theorem 2
.

For any
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, if
\alpha>4N-1
, then for any
\omega_{1}=(x_{1},u_{1}),\omega_{2}=(x_{2},u_{2})\in\mathbb{G}
and any
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\backslash\{0\},

\left|S^{\alpha}\left(\left(x_{1},u_{1}\right),\left(x_{2},u_{2}\right)\right)\right|\leq C_{N}\left(1+\left|\left(A_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}x_{1},u_{1}\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}\left(1+\left|\left(A_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}x_{2},u_{2}\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}.

Proof.

Choose a nonnegative function
\varphi

\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\frac{1}{2},2)

satisfying
\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi\left(2^{j}s\right)=1
,
s>0
. For each
j\geq 0
, we set function

\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left(s,t\right)=(1-s-t)_{+}^{\alpha}\varphi(2^{j}\left(1-s-t\right)),

and define bilinear operator

T_{j}^{\alpha}(f,g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)P_{\lambda_{1}}fP_{\lambda_{2}}gd\lambda_{1}d\lambda_{2}\text{.}

It is easy to see that

T_{j}^{\alpha}(f,g)(\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}f(\omega\omega_{1}^{-1})g(\omega\omega_{2}^{-1})K_{j}^{\alpha}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2},

where the kernel
K_{j}^{\alpha}
is given by

\displaystyle K_{j}^{\alpha}((x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{Q}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}((2k+n)\left|\mu_{1}\right|,(2l+n)\left|\mu_{2}\right|)e_{k}^{\mu_{1}}(x_{1},u_{1})e_{k}^{\mu_{2}}(x_{2},u_{2})\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{n}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{n}d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{Q}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-i\mu_{1}(u_{1})}e^{-i\mu_{2}(u_{2})}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}((2k+n)\left|\mu_{1}\right|,(2l+n)\left|\mu_{2}\right|)

\displaystyle\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\times\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}\left(A_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}x_{1}\right)\varphi_{l}^{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}\left(A_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}x_{2}\right)\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}\right|\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}\right|\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{n}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{n}d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}.

Clearly,

S^{\alpha}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}K_{j}^{\alpha}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\text{.}

Fixing
j\geq 0
. We first estimate
K_{j}^{\alpha}
. Let
z_{1}=A_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}x_{1}
,
z_{2}=A_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}x_{2}
and

\displaystyle F((z_{1},u_{1}),(z_{2},u_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{Q}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-i\mu_{1}(u_{1})}e^{-i\mu_{2}(u_{2})}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}((2k+n)\left|\mu_{1}\right|,(2l+n)\left|\mu_{2}\right|)

\displaystyle\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\times\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}\left(z_{1}\right)\varphi_{l}^{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}\left(z_{2}\right)\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}\right|\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}\right|\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{n}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{n}d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}.

Then,
F((z_{1},u_{1}),(z_{2},u_{2}))
is bi-radial with respect to
z_{1}

and
z_{2}
. We set
r_{1}=\left|z_{1}\right|
,
r_{2}=\left|z_{2}\right|
and define

(3.6)

\displaystyle R_{k,l}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{(1-n)}k!}{(k+n-1)!}\frac{2^{(1-n)}l!}{(l+n-1)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}F^{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(r_{1},r_{2})\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}(r_{1})\varphi_{l}^{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}(r_{2})r_{1}^{2n-1}r_{2}^{2n-1}dr_{1}dr_{2},

where

F^{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(r_{1},r_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{i\mu_{1}(u_{1})}e^{i\mu_{2}(u_{2})}F((r_{1},u_{1}),(r_{2},u_{2}))du_{1}du_{2}.

Using the inversion formula of the Fourier transform and the Laguerre
expension of the radial function (see
[
15
]
), the function
F
can be
written as

\displaystyle F((z_{1},u_{1}),(z_{2},u_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{Q}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-i\left(\mu_{1}(u_{1})+\mu_{2}(u_{2})\right)}F^{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(z_{1},z_{2})\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{n}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{n}d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{Q}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-i\left(\mu_{1}(u_{1})+\mu_{2}(u_{2})\right)}R_{k,l}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},F)\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}(z_{1})\varphi_{l}^{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}(z_{2})\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{n}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{n}d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}.

Hence, if we can show that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|R_{k,l}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},F)\right|\frac{\left(k+n-1\right)!}{k!}\frac{\left(l+n-1\right)!}{l!}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{n}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{n}d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}<\infty,

then
F
is bounded. Let
u_{1}
be the
p
-component of
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
be
the
q
-component of
u_{2}
. Similarly, if we can show that for any fixed
j\geq 0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, there exists a constant
C_{j,N}
such that for any
p,q=1,2,\cdots,m
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|R_{k,l}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\left(iu_{1}^{(p)}-\frac{\mu_{1}^{(p)}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}\frac{1}{4}\left|z_{1}\right|^{2}\right)^{N}\left(iu_{2}^{(q)}-\frac{\mu_{2}^{(q)}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}\frac{1}{4}\left|z_{2}\right|^{2}\right)^{N}F\right|

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a M-type group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\,=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
where
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is the

\mathfrak{g}

\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2}=m

Proof.

We take a partition of unity
\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi(2^{j}s)=1

where

\varphi_{j}^{\delta}(s)=(1-s)_{+}^{\delta}\varphi(2^{j}(1-s))

It is obvious that

S^{\delta}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}T_{j}^{\delta}.

Theorem
4
will be proved once we show that when

\varepsilon>0

(4.1)

\left\|T_{j}^{\delta}\right\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq 2^{-\varepsilon j}.

In order to prove (

and split the

of

into two parts

s_{j}^{\delta}(\omega)=s_{j}^{1}(\omega)+s_{j}^{2}(\omega),

where

,
s_{j}^{2}(\omega)=s_{j}^{\delta}\chi_{B_{j}^{c}}(\omega)

stands for the characteristic function of set
A

\gamma>0
is

\left\|f\ast s_{j}^{l}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq C2^{-\varepsilon j}\left\|f\right\|_{p},\text{ \ }l=1,2\text{.}

We first consider the convolution with

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|s_{j}^{2}(\omega)\right|d\omega

\displaystyle\int_{\left|\omega\right|>2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\left|s_{j}^{\delta}(\omega)\right|d\omega

4.5
), it follows that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{B_{j}(\xi\,,\frac{5}{4})}|f_{2}\ast s_{j}^{1}(\omega)|^{p}d\omega d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C2^{-\varepsilon j}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{B_{j}(\xi,\frac{5}{4})}|f(\omega)|^{p}d\omega d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Changing the variable and exchanging the order of integration, the left side

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{B_{j}(\xi\,,\frac{5}{4})}|f_{2}\ast s_{j}^{1}(\omega)|^{p}d\omega d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\left|\omega\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\left|f_{2}\ast s_{j}^{1}(\xi\omega)\right|^{p}d\omega d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\left|\omega\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|f_{2}\ast s_{j}^{1}(\xi\omega)\right|^{p}d\xi d\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{4}2^{j(1+\gamma)}\right)^{\frac{Q}{p}}\left\|f_{2}\ast s_{j}^{1}\right\|_{p}.

In the same way, the right side

C2^{-\varepsilon j}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{B_{j}(\xi,\frac{5}{4})}|f(\omega)|^{p}d\omega d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=C2^{-\varepsilon j}\left(\frac{5}{4}2^{j(1+\gamma)}\right)^{\frac{Q}{p}}\left\|f\right\|_{p}.

Hence, we have that

(4.6)

\left\|f_{2}\ast s_{j}^{1}\right\|_{p}\leq C2^{-\varepsilon j}\left\|f\right\|_{p}.

Now it remains to estimate
f_{1}\ast s_{j}^{1}
. Since that
f_{1}\neq 0

implies

\left|\xi^{-1}\cdot\omega\omega^{\prime-1}\right|\leq\frac{3}{4}2^{j(1+\mathbb{\gamma})}\text{,}

it follows that

\left|\omega^{\prime}\right|\leq 2^{j(1+\mathbb{\gamma})}\text{.}

So, we have

(4.7)

f_{1}\ast s_{j}^{1}(\omega)=f_{1}\ast s_{j}^{\delta}(\omega)\quad\text{for any}\quad\omega\in B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4}).

Note that

(4.8)

f\ast s_{j}^{\delta}=T_{j}^{\delta}f=\int_{1-2^{-j+1}}^{1-2^{-j-1}}\varphi_{j}^{\delta}(\lambda)P_{\lambda}fd\lambda.

Then,

\displaystyle\left\|f\ast s_{j}^{\delta}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{j}^{\delta}f,T_{j}^{\delta}f\right\rangle=\left\langle\left(T_{j}^{\delta}\right)^{\ast}T_{j}^{\delta}f,f\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(T_{j}^{\delta}\right)^{2}f,f\right\rangle\leq\left\|\left(T_{j}^{\delta}\right)^{2}f\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\left\|f\right\|_{p}\text{.}

Since

\left(T_{j}^{\delta}\right)^{2}f=\int_{1-2^{-j+1}}^{1-2^{-j-1}}\left(\varphi_{j}^{\delta}(\lambda)\right)^{2}P_{\lambda}f\,d\lambda\,,

using Theorem
3
, we get that

\displaystyle\|\left(T_{j}^{\delta}\right)^{2}f\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{1-2^{-j+1}}^{1-2^{-j-1}}\left(\varphi_{j}^{\delta}(\lambda)\right)^{2}\|P_{\lambda}f\|_{p^{\prime}}d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{1-2^{-j+1}}^{1-2^{-j-1}}\left(\varphi_{j}^{\delta}(\lambda)\right)^{2}\lambda^{Q(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})-1}\|f\|_{p}d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-2j\delta-j}\|f\|_{p}.

So,

\|f\ast s_{j}^{\delta}\|_{2}^{2}\leq C2^{-2j\delta-j}\|f\|_{p}^{2}\text{.}

This estimate, together with (
4.7
) and Hölder’s inequality yield
that

\displaystyle\left\|f_{1}\ast s_{j}^{1}\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|f_{1}\ast s_{j}^{\delta}\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{jQ(1+\gamma)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|f_{1}\ast s_{j}^{\delta}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-j\delta}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}2^{jQ(1+\gamma)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-j\delta}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}2^{jQ(1+\gamma)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{3}{4}))}.

Taking the
L^{p}
norm with respect to
\xi
, we have

\left\|f_{1}\ast s_{j}^{1}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq C2^{-j\delta}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}2^{jQ(1+\gamma)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

Thus, when
\delta>Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}
, we
can choose
\gamma>0
such that

\delta>Q(1+\gamma)\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2},

which implies that there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that

\left\|f_{1}\ast s_{j}^{1}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq C2^{-\varepsilon j}\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

The proof of Theorem
4
is completed.
∎

5.
Boundedness of
S^{\alpha}
for
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq 2

From this section, we begin to investigate the
L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}
boundedness of the bilinear Riesz means
S^{\alpha}
. We first consider the case of
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq 2
.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose
m\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Define operator
T_{m}f=\int_{a}^{b}m(\lambda)P_{\lambda}f\,d\lambda
with
0\leq a<b
.
Then, for any
1\leq p\leq 2
, we have

\left\|T_{m}f\right\|_{2}\leq C\left\|m\right\|_{\infty}\left((b-a)b^{n+m-1}\right)^{\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left\|f\right\|_{p}.

Proof.

Since that

P_{\lambda}f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n+m-1}}{\left(2\pi(2k+n)\right)^{n+m}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}f\ast\widetilde{e}_{k}^{\lambda\eta}(x,u)d\sigma(\eta),

the operator
T_{m}
can be written as

T_{m}f=\int_{a}^{b}m(\lambda)P_{\lambda}f\,d\lambda=f\ast G_{m},

where the kernel
G_{m}
is given by

\displaystyle G_{m}(x,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}m(\lambda)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n+m-1}}{\left(2\pi(2k+n)\right)^{n+m}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}\widetilde{e}_{k}^{\lambda\eta}(x,u)d\sigma(\eta)d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\frac{a}{2k+n}}^{\frac{b}{2k+n}}m((2k+n)\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}e_{k}^{\lambda\eta}(x,u)d\sigma(\eta)\lambda^{n+m-1}d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\frac{a}{2k+n}}^{\frac{b}{2k+n}}m((2k+n)\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}e^{-i\lambda\eta(u)}\left(\varphi_{k}^{\lambda}\circ A_{\eta}\right)(x)\left|\det A_{\eta}\right|d\sigma(\eta)\lambda^{n+m-1}d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\chi_{[\frac{a}{2k+n},\frac{b}{2k+n}]}(\left|\mu\right|)m((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|)e^{-i\mu(u)}\left(\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}\circ A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right)(x)\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu.

Applying the Plancherel theorem in the variable
u
and the orthogonality of

\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|G_{m}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-i\mu(u)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\chi_{[\frac{a}{2k+n},\frac{b}{2k+n}]}(\left|\mu\right|)m((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|)\left(\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}\circ A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right)(x)\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu\right|^{2}\,dudx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\chi_{[\frac{a}{2k+n},\frac{b}{2k+n}]}(\left|\mu\right|)m((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|)\left(\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}\circ A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right)(x)\right|^{2}\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|^{2}\,\left|\mu\right|^{2n}d\mu dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|\chi_{[\frac{a}{2k+n},\frac{b}{2k+n}]}(\left|\mu\right|)m((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|)\right|^{2}\left\|\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}\circ A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|^{2}|\mu|^{2n}d\mu.

Changing variables in the integral and using the estimate

(5.1)

\left\|\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}\right\|_{2}\leq C\left|\mu\right|^{-\frac{n}{2}}k^{\frac{n-1}{2}},

we get that

\left\|\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}\circ A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\left|\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}\left(A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}x\right)\right|^{2}dx=\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|^{-1}\left\|\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq C\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|^{-1}\left|\mu\right|^{-n}k^{n-1}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|G_{m}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|\chi_{[\frac{a}{2k+n},\frac{b}{2k+n}]}(\left|\mu\right|)m((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|)\right|^{2}\left\|\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu\right|}\circ A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|^{2}|\mu|^{2n}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{n-1}\left|\chi_{[\frac{a}{2k+n},\frac{b}{2k+n}]}(\left|\mu\right|)m((2k+n)\left|\mu\right|)\right|^{2}\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2k+n)^{-n-m}k^{n-1}\right)\int_{a\leq\left|\mu\right|\leq b}\left|m(\left|\mu\right|)\right|^{2}\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu}{\left|\mu\right|}}\right|\left|\mu\right|^{n}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(b-a)b^{n+m-1}\left\|m\right\|_{\infty}.

Using Young’s inequality, it follows that

\left\|T_{m}f\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|G_{m}\right\|_{2}\left\|f\right\|_{1}\leq C\left((b-a)b^{n+m-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|m\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f\right\|_{1}\text{.}

By interpolation with the trivial estimate

\left\|T_{m}f\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|m\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f\right\|_{2},

we conclude that

\left\|T_{m}f\right\|_{2}\leq C\left((b-a)b^{n+m-1}\right)^{\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left\|m\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f\right\|_{p}.

The proof is completed.
∎

Theorem 5
.

Suppose that
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq 2
and
1/p=1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}
. If
\alpha>Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)
, then
S^{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{G})\times L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{G})
into
L^{p}(\mathbb{G})
.

Proof.

In the proof of Theorem
2
, we have defined the operator

T_{j}^{\alpha}(f,g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)P_{\lambda_{1}}fP_{\lambda_{2}}gd\lambda_{1}d\lambda_{2},

and showed that

S^{\alpha}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}T_{j}^{\alpha}.

So, Theorem
5
would follow if we can show that when
\alpha>Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)
, there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that
for each
j\geq 0
,

(5.2)

\left\|T_{j}^{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq 2^{-\varepsilon j}.

Fixing
j\geq 0
. To prove (
5.2
), we define
B_{j}=\{\omega:\left|\omega\right|\leq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}\}\subseteq\mathbb{G}

and split the kernel of
T_{j}^{\alpha}
, denoted by
K_{j}^{\alpha}
,
into four parts:

K_{j}^{\alpha}=K_{j}^{1}+K_{j}^{2}+K_{j}^{3}+K_{j}^{4},

where

\displaystyle K_{j}^{1}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{j}^{\alpha}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\chi_{B_{j}}(\omega_{1})\chi_{B_{j}}(\omega_{2}),

\displaystyle K_{j}^{2}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{j}^{\alpha}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\chi_{B_{j}}(\omega_{1})\chi_{B_{j}^{c}}(\omega_{2}),

\displaystyle K_{j}^{3}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{j}^{\alpha}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\chi_{B_{j}^{c}}(\omega_{1})\chi_{B_{j}}(\omega_{2}),

\displaystyle K_{j}^{4}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{j}^{\alpha}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\chi_{B_{j}^{c}}(\omega_{1})\chi_{B_{j}^{c}}(\omega_{2}).

Let
T_{j}^{l}
be the bilinear operator with kernel
K_{j}^{l}
,
l=1,2,3,4
. Then, (
5.2
) would be the consequence of the estimates

\left\|T_{j}^{l}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq 2^{-\varepsilon j},\text{ \ \ \ }l=1,2,3,4.

First consider
T_{j}^{4}
. (
3.13
) tells that for any
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and
\omega_{1}=(x_{1},u_{1})
,
\omega_{2}=(x_{2},u_{2})\in\mathbb{G}
,

\left|K_{j}^{\alpha}(\left(x_{1},u_{1}\right),(x_{2},u_{2}))\right|\leq C_{N}2^{-j(\alpha-4N+1)}\left(1+\left|\left(A_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}x_{1},u_{1}\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}\left(1+\left|\left(A_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}x_{2},u_{2}\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality, Young’s inequality and changing variables,
we can easily get that

\displaystyle\left\|T_{j}^{4}(f,g)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{N}2^{j\cdot 4N}\left\|f\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g\right\|_{p_{2}}\left(\int_{\left|(x_{1},u_{1})\right|\geq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\left(1+\left|\left(A_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}x_{1},u_{1}\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}dx_{1}du_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{\left|(x_{2},u_{2})\right|\geq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\left(1+\left|\left(A_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}x_{2},u_{2}\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}dx_{2}du_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{N}2^{j\cdot 4N}\left\|f\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g\right\|_{p_{2}}\left(\int_{\left|\left(A_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{-1}z_{1},u_{1}\right)\right|\geq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\left(1+\left|\left(z_{1},u_{1}\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}dz_{1}du_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{\left|\left(A_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}^{-1}z_{2},u_{2}\right)\right|\geq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\left(1+\left|\left(z_{2},u_{2}\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}dz_{2}du_{2}\right)\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}\right|^{-1}\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}\right|^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{N}2^{j\cdot 4N}\left\|f\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g\right\|_{p_{2}}\int_{\left|\left(z_{1},u_{1}\right)\right|\geq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\left(1+\left|\left(z_{1},u_{1}\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}dz_{1}du_{1}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\left|\left(z_{2},u_{2}\right)\right|\geq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\left(1+\left|\left(z_{2},u_{2}\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}dz_{2}du_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{N}2^{j\cdot 4N}\left\|f\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g\right\|_{p_{2}}\left(\int_{\left|\omega_{1}\right|\geq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\left(1+\left|\omega_{1}\right|\right)^{-2N}d\omega_{1}\right)\left(\int_{\left|\omega_{2}\right|\geq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\left(1+\left|\omega_{2}\right|\right)^{-2N}d\omega_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{N}2^{j\cdot 4N}\left\|f\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g\right\|_{p_{2}}\left(\int_{2^{j(1+\gamma)}}^{\infty}t^{-2N+Q-1}dt\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{N}2^{j\cdot 4N}2^{j(1+\gamma)(-4N+2Q)}\left\|f\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g\right\|_{p_{2}}.

Choosing
N
large enough such that

4N\mathbb{\gamma}>2Q(1+\mathbb{\gamma}),

we have

(5.3)

\left\|T_{j}^{4}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq 2^{-\varepsilon j}

for some
\varepsilon>0
.

Consider the estimate of
T_{j}^{3}
. As the proof of Theorem
4
, the
kernel
K_{j}^{\alpha}
can be rewritten as

K_{j}^{\alpha}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=c_{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{1}}R_{\lambda_{1}}^{0}(\omega_{1})G_{\lambda_{2}}(\omega_{2})d\lambda_{1}d\lambda_{2},

where

G_{\lambda_{2}}(\omega_{2})=G_{\lambda_{2}}(x,u)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{2}^{n+m-1}}{(2\pi(2k+n))^{n+m}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}\widetilde{e}_{k}^{\lambda_{2}\eta_{2}}(x,u)\,d\sigma(\eta_{2}).

Intergration by parts and using the identity (
4.2
), we get that

K_{j}^{\alpha}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=c_{N}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\partial_{\lambda_{1}}^{2N+2}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\right)\lambda_{1}^{2N+1}R_{\lambda_{1}}^{2N+1}(\omega_{1})G_{\lambda_{2}}(\omega_{2})d\lambda_{1}d\lambda_{2}.

So,

\displaystyle\left|K_{j}^{3}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|K_{j}^{\alpha}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\chi_{B_{j}^{c}}(\omega_{1})\chi_{B_{j}}(\omega_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{1}\left|\lambda_{1}^{2N+1}R_{\lambda_{1}}^{2N+1}(\omega_{1})\chi_{B_{j}^{c}}(\omega_{1})\right|\left|\int_{0}^{1}\left(\partial_{\lambda_{2}}^{2N+2}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\right)G_{\lambda_{2}}(\omega_{2})\chi_{B_{j}}(\omega_{2})\,d\lambda_{2}\right|d\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sup_{\lambda_{1}\in[0,1]}\left|\lambda_{1}^{2N+1}R_{\lambda_{1}}^{2N+1}(\omega_{1})\chi_{B_{j}^{c}}(\omega_{1})\right|\int_{0}^{1}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\left(\partial_{\lambda_{2}}^{2N+2}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\right)G_{\lambda_{2}}(\omega_{2})\chi_{B_{j}}(\omega_{2})\,d\lambda_{2}\right|d\lambda_{1}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left\|T_{j}^{3}(f,g)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|f\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g\right\|_{p_{2}}\int_{\left|\omega_{1}\right|\geq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\sup_{\lambda_{1}\in[0,1]}\left|\lambda_{1}^{2N+1}R_{\lambda_{1}}^{2m+1}(\omega_{1})\right|\,d\omega_{1}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\left|\omega_{2}\right|\leq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\left(\partial_{\lambda_{2}}^{2N+2}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\right)G_{\lambda_{2}}(\omega_{2})\,d\lambda_{2}\right|d\lambda_{1}d\omega_{2}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwartz’s inequality and Lemma
2
, we
get that

\displaystyle\int_{\left|\omega_{2}\right|\leq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\left(\partial_{\lambda_{2}}^{2N+2}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\right)G_{\lambda_{2}}(\omega_{2})\,d\lambda_{2}\right|d\lambda_{1}d\omega_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{j(1+\gamma)\frac{Q}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\left(\partial_{\lambda_{2}}^{2N+2}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\right)G_{\lambda_{2}}(\omega_{2})\,d\lambda_{2}\right|^{2}d\omega_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{j(1+\gamma)\frac{Q}{2}}\sup_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in[0,1]}\left|\partial_{\lambda_{2}}^{2N+2}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{j(1+\gamma)\frac{Q}{2}}2^{j(2N+2)}.

On the other hand, (
4.3
) implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\left|\omega_{1}\right|\geq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\sup_{\lambda_{1}\in[0,1]}\left|\lambda_{1}^{2N+1}R_{\lambda_{1}}^{2N+1}(\omega_{1})\right|d\omega_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\left|(x_{1},u_{1})\right|\geq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\left(1+\left|\left(A_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}x_{1},u_{1}\right)\right|\right)^{-2N}dx_{1}du_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\left|\omega_{1}\right|\geq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}}\left(1+\left|\omega_{1}\right|\right)^{-2N}d\omega_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{j(1+\gamma)(-2N+Q)}.

Thus,

\left\|T_{j}^{3}(f,g)\right\|_{p}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g\right\|_{p_{2}}2^{j\frac{(1+\gamma)Q}{2}}2^{j(2N+2)}2^{j(1+\gamma)(-2N+Q)}\text{.}

Choose
N
large enough such that

2N\gamma\geq\frac{3}{2}Q(1+\gamma)+2,

we have

(5.4)

\left\|T_{j}^{3}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq C2^{-\varepsilon j}

for some
\varepsilon>0
. Obviously, (
5.4
) also holds for
T_{j}^{2}
.

Now, it remains to estimate
T_{j}^{1}
.
T_{j}^{1}
is denoted by

T_{j}^{1}(f,g)\left(\omega\right)=\int_{\mathbb{G}}\int_{\mathbb{G}}f(\omega\omega_{1}^{-1})g(\omega\omega_{2}^{-1})K_{j}^{1}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}.

We assume that
\omega\in B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4})
and slipt the functions
f,g
into three parts respectively:
f=f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}
,
g=g_{1}+g_{2}+g_{3}
, where

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\chi_{B_{j}(\xi,\frac{3}{4})},\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad g_{1}=g\chi_{B_{j}(\xi,\frac{3}{4})},

\displaystyle f_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\chi_{B_{j}(\xi,\frac{5}{4})\backslash B_{j}(\xi,\frac{3}{4})},\quad\quad g_{2}=g\chi_{B_{j}(\xi,\frac{5}{4})\backslash B_{j}(\xi,\frac{3}{4})},

\displaystyle f_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\chi_{\mathbb{G}\backslash B_{j}(\xi,\frac{5}{4})},\quad\quad\quad\quad g_{3}=g\chi_{\mathbb{G}\backslash B_{j}(\xi,\frac{5}{4})}.

Based on this decomposition, we notice that if
f_{3}\neq 0
or
g_{3}\neq 0
,

\left|\xi^{-1}\cdot\omega\omega_{1}^{-1}\right|\geq\frac{5}{4}2^{j(1+\gamma)}\text{ or }\left|\xi^{-1}\cdot\omega\omega_{2}^{-1}\right|\geq\frac{5}{4}2^{j(1+\gamma)}.

Since
\left|\xi^{-1}\omega\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}2^{j(1+\gamma)}
, by the triangle
inequality, we get that

\left|\omega_{1}\right|\geq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}\text{ or }\left|\omega_{2}\right|\geq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}.

Because the kernel
K_{j}^{1}
is supported on
B_{j}\times B_{j}
, we have
T_{j}^{1}(f_{3},g)=0
and
T_{j}^{1}(f,g_{3})=0
. Similarly,
f_{2}\neq 0

and
g_{2}\neq 0
yield that

\left|\omega_{1}\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}2^{j(1+\gamma)}\text{ and
}\left|\omega_{2}\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}2^{j(1+\gamma)}.

So, we can repeat the proof of (
5.3
) to obtain that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\left\|T_{j}^{1}(f_{2},g_{2})\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-\varepsilon j}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g_{2}\right\|_{p_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-\varepsilon j}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{5}{4}))}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{5}{4}))}.

Taking the
L^{p}
norm with respect to
\xi
, as the proof of Theorem
4
, we get that

(5.6)

\left\|T_{j}^{1}(f_{2},g_{2})\right\|_{p}\leq C2^{-\varepsilon j}\left\|f\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g\right\|_{p_{2}}.

If
f_{1}\neq 0
and
g_{2}\neq 0
, we have

\left|\omega_{1}\right|\leq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}\text{ and }\left|\omega_{2}\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}2^{j(1+\gamma)}.

Repeating the proof of (
5.4
), we can conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|T_{j}^{1}(f_{1},g_{2})\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-\varepsilon j}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g_{2}\right\|_{p_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-\varepsilon j}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{3}{4}))}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{5}{4}))}.

It follows that

(5.7)

\left\|T_{j}^{1}(f_{1},g_{2})\right\|_{p}\leq C2^{-\varepsilon j}\left\|f\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g\right\|_{p_{2}}.

Obviously, (
5.7
) also holds for
T_{j}^{1}(f_{2},g_{1})
. Finally, we
consider
T_{j}^{1}(f_{1},g_{1})
. Because
f_{1},g_{1}\neq 0
implies that

\left|\omega_{1}\right|\leq 2^{j(1+\gamma)}\text{ and }\left|\omega_{1}\right|\leq 2^{j(1+\gamma)},

so

(5.8)

T_{j}^{1}(f_{1},g_{1})(\omega)=T_{j}^{\alpha}(f_{1},g_{1})(\omega)

for any
\omega\in B_{j}\left(\xi,\frac{1}{4}\right)
. Notice that
T_{j}^{\alpha}
can be written as

\displaystyle T_{j}^{\alpha}(f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)P_{\lambda_{1}}fP_{\lambda_{2}}g\,d\lambda_{1}d\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{[-1,1]^{2}}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left(\left|\lambda_{1}\right|,\left|\lambda_{2}\right|\right)P_{\left|\lambda_{1}\right|}fP_{\left|\lambda_{2}\right|}g\,d\lambda_{1}d\lambda_{2}\text{.}

Since that for any fixed
s\in[-1,1]
, the function

t\rightarrow\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left(\left|s\right|,\left|t\right|\right)

is supported in
[-1,1]
and vanishes at endpoints
\pm 1
, we can expand
this function in Fourier series by considering a periodic extension on
\mathbb{R}
of period
2
, i.e.

\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}(\left|s\right|,\left|t\right|)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\gamma_{j,k}^{\alpha}(s)e^{i\pi kt},

where the Fourier coefficients are given by

\gamma_{j,k}^{\alpha}(s)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}(\left|s\right|,\left|t\right|)e^{-i\pi kt}\,dt\text{.}

It is easy to see that for any
0<\delta<\alpha
,

\sup_{s\in[-1,1]}\left|\gamma_{j,k}^{\alpha}(s)\right|\left(1+\left|k\right|\right)^{1+\delta}\leq C2^{-j(\alpha-\delta)}.

Then,
T_{j}^{\alpha}
can be expressed by

\displaystyle T_{j}^{\alpha}(f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{[-1,1]^{2}}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left(\left|\lambda_{1}\right|,\left|\lambda_{2}\right|\right)P_{\left|\lambda_{1}\right|}fP_{\left|\lambda_{2}\right|}g\,d\lambda_{1}d\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{[-1,1]^{2}}\gamma_{j,k}^{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})e^{i\pi k\lambda_{2}}P_{\left|\lambda_{1}\right|}fP_{\left|\lambda_{2}\right|}g\,d\lambda_{1}d\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-1}^{1}\gamma_{j,k}^{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})P_{\left|\lambda_{1}\right|}f\,d\lambda_{1}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{i\pi k\lambda_{2}}P_{\left|\lambda_{2}\right|}g\,d\lambda_{2}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwartz’s inequality and Lemma
2
, we
have

(5.9)

\displaystyle\left\|T_{j}^{\alpha}(f,g)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\|\int_{-1}^{1}\gamma_{j,k}^{\alpha}(\lambda_{1})P_{\left|\lambda_{1}\right|}f\,d\lambda_{1}\right\|_{2}\left\|\int_{-1}^{1}e^{i\pi k\lambda_{2}}P_{\left|\lambda_{2}\right|}g\,d\lambda_{2}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+\left|k\right|)^{-1-\delta}\left(\sup_{s\in[-1,1]}\left|\gamma_{j,k}^{\alpha}(s)\right|\left(1+\left|k\right|\right)^{1+\delta}\right)\left\|f\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g\right\|_{p_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-j(\alpha-\delta)}\left\|f\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g\right\|_{p_{2}}.

Using the Hölder’s inequality and (
5.8
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|T_{j}^{1}(f_{1},g_{1})\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{j(1+\gamma)Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)}\left\|T_{j}^{1}(f_{1},g_{1})\right\|_{L^{1}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{j(1+\gamma)Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)}\left\|T_{j}^{\alpha}(f_{1},g_{1})\right\|_{L^{1}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-j(\alpha-\delta)}2^{j(1+\gamma)Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g_{1}\right\|_{p_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-j(\alpha-\delta)}2^{j(1+\gamma)Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{3}{4}))}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{3}{4}))}.

Taking the
L^{p}
norm with respect to
\xi
, it follows that

(5.10)

\left\|T_{j}^{1}(f_{1},g_{1})\right\|_{p}\leq C2^{-j(\alpha-\delta)}2^{j(1+\gamma)Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)}\left\|f\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g\right\|_{p_{2}}.

Combining (
5.6
), (
5.7
) and (
5.10
), we can conclude that

\left\|T_{j}^{1}(f,g)\right\|_{p}\leq C2^{-j(\alpha-\delta)}2^{j(1+\gamma)Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)}\left\|f\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|g\right\|_{p_{2}}.

Thus, whenever
\alpha>Q\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)
, we can choose
suitable
\gamma,\delta>0
such that

\alpha>Q(1+\gamma)\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)+\delta,

which means that there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that

\left\|T_{j}^{1}\right\|_{L^{1}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq 2^{-\varepsilon j}.

The proof of Theorem
5
is completed.
∎

6.1.
The point
(1,\infty,1)

Theorem 6
.

If
\alpha>\frac{Q}{2}
, then
S^{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb{G})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
to
L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
.

Proof.

We keep the notations in Section 5. Note that (
5.3
), (
5.4
), (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) hold for any
\alpha>0
. So, the proof of Theorem
5
is valid apart from the estimate of
T_{j}^{1}(f_{1},g_{1})
.
According to (
5.9
), we know that for any
0<\delta<\alpha
,

\left\|T_{j}^{\alpha}(f,g)\right\|_{1}\leq C2^{-j(\alpha-\delta)}\left\|f\right\|_{1}\left\|g\right\|_{2}.

Using the Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|T_{j}^{1}(f_{1},g_{1})\right\|_{L^{1}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|T_{j}^{\alpha}(f_{1},g_{1})\right\|_{L^{1}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-j(\alpha-\delta)}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{3}{4}))}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{3}{4}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-j(\alpha-\delta)}2^{j(1+\gamma)\frac{Q}{2}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{3}{4}))}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4}))}.

Taking the
L^{p}
norm with respect to
\xi
yields that

\left\|T_{j}^{1}(f_{1},g_{1})\right\|_{1}\leq C2^{-j(\alpha-\delta)}2^{j(1+\gamma)\frac{Q}{2}}\left\|f\right\|_{1}\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}.

Thus, whenever
\alpha>\frac{Q}{2}
, we can choose
\gamma,\delta>0
such
that
\alpha>\frac{(1+\gamma)}{2}Q+\delta
, which means that there exists

\varepsilon>0
such that

\left\|T_{j}^{1}(f_{1},g_{1})\right\|_{1}\leq C2^{-\varepsilon j}\left\|f\right\|_{1}\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}.

∎

6.2.
The point
(\infty,\infty,\infty)

Theorem 7
.

If
\alpha>Q-\frac{1}{2}
, then

is bounded from

into

Proof.

We still keep the notation in last Section. To proof this Theorem, it
suffices to estimate

. Notice that

P_{\lambda}f(x,u)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n+m-1}}{\left(2\pi(2k+n)\right)^{n+m}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}f\ast\widetilde{e}_{k}^{\lambda\eta}(x,u)d\sigma(\eta),

Then,
T_{j}^{\alpha}(f,g)
can be written as

\displaystyle T_{j}^{\alpha}(f,g)(x,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{Q}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}((2k+n)\lambda_{1},(2l+n)\lambda_{2})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}f\ast e_{k}^{\lambda_{1}\eta_{1}}(x,u)d\sigma(\eta_{1})

\displaystyle\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\times\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}f\ast e_{l}^{\lambda_{2}\eta_{2}}(x,u)d\sigma(\eta_{2})\lambda_{1}^{n+m-1}\lambda_{2}^{n+m-1}d\lambda_{1}d\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{Q}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left((2k+n)\lambda_{1},(2l+n)\lambda_{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}e^{-i\lambda_{1}\eta_{1}(u)}\left(\left(f_{\eta_{1}}^{\lambda_{1}\eta_{1}}\times_{\lambda_{1}}\varphi_{k}^{\lambda_{1}}\right)\circ A_{\eta_{1}}\right)(x)d\sigma(\eta_{1})

\displaystyle\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\times\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}e^{-i\lambda_{2}\eta_{2}(u)}\left(\left(f_{\eta_{2}}^{\lambda_{2}\eta_{2}}\times_{\lambda_{2}}\varphi_{l}^{\lambda_{2}}\right)\circ A_{\eta_{2}}\right)(x)d\sigma(\eta_{2})\,\lambda_{1}^{n+m-1}\lambda_{2}^{n+m-1}d\lambda_{1}d\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{Q}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}e^{-i(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})(u)}\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}((2k+n)\left|\mu_{1}\right|,(2l+n)\left|\mu_{2}\right|)

\displaystyle\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\times\left(\left(f_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\times_{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}\right)\circ A_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}\right)(x)\left(\left(f_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}^{\mu_{2}}\times_{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}\varphi_{l}^{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}\right)\circ A_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}\right)(x)\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{n}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{n}d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}.

Using (
5.1
), we get that for any

,

\displaystyle\left\|T_{j}^{\alpha}(f,g)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left|\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}((2k+n)\left|\mu_{1}\right|,(2l+n)\left|\mu_{2}\right|)\right|

\displaystyle\times\left\|\left(f_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\times_{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}\right)\circ A_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\left(g_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}^{\mu_{2}}\times_{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}\varphi_{l}^{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}\right)\circ A_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}\right\|_{\infty}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{n}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{n}d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left|\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left((2k+n)\left|\mu_{1}\right|,(2l+n)\left|\mu_{2}\right|\right)\right|

\displaystyle\times\left\|f_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\times_{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|g_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}^{\mu_{2}}\times_{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}\varphi_{l}^{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}\right\|_{\infty}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{n}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{n}d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left|\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left((2k+n)\left|\mu_{1}\right|,(2l+n)\left|\mu_{2}\right|\right)\right|

\displaystyle\times\left\|f_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\right\|_{2}\left\|\varphi_{k}^{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}\right\|_{2}\left\|g_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}^{\mu_{2}}\right\|_{2}\left\|\varphi_{l}^{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}\right\|_{2}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{n}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{n}d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left|\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left((2k+n)\left|\mu_{1}\right|,(2l+n)\left|\mu_{2}\right|\right)\right|\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{\frac{n}{2}}k^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{\frac{n}{2}}l^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left\|f_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\right\|_{2}\left\|g_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}^{\mu_{2}}\right\|_{2}\,d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left|\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left((2k+n)\left|\mu_{1}\right|,(2l+n)\left|\mu_{2}\right|\right)\right|\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{\frac{n+\delta}{2}}k^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{\frac{n+\delta}{2}}l^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\right)^{2}d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{\left|\mu_{1}\right|\leq 1}\int_{\left|\mu_{2}\right|\leq 1}\left\|f_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|g_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}^{\mu_{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{-\delta}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}k^{\frac{n-1}{2}}l^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left((2k+n)\left|\mu_{1}\right|,(2l+n)\left|\mu_{2}\right|\right)\right|^{2}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{n+\delta}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{n+\delta}\,d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{\left|\mu_{1}\right|\leq 1}\left\|f_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\left|\mu_{2}\right|\leq 1}\left\|g_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}^{\mu_{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}k^{\frac{n-1}{2}}l^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(2k+n)^{-\frac{n+m+\delta}{2}}(2l+n)^{-\frac{n+m+\delta}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left(\left|\mu_{1}\right|,\left|\mu_{2}\right|\right)\right|^{2}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{n+\delta}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{n+\delta}\,d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{\left|\mu_{1}\right|\leq 1}\left\|f_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\left|\mu_{2}\right|\leq 1}\left\|g_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}^{\mu_{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}k^{-\frac{m+\delta+1}{2}}\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}l^{-\frac{m+\delta+1}{2}}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|\varphi_{j}^{\alpha}\left(\left|\mu_{1}\right|,\left|\mu_{2}\right|\right)\right|^{2}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{n+\delta}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{n+\delta}\,d\mu_{1}d\mu_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{\left|\mu_{1}\right|\leq 1}\left\|f_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\left|\mu_{2}\right|\leq 1}\left\|g_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}^{\mu_{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-j(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}\left(\int_{\left|\mu_{1}\right|\leq 1}\left\|f_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\left|\mu_{2}\right|\leq 1}\left\|g_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}^{\mu_{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Because

T_{j}^{1}(f_{1},g_{1})(\omega)=T_{j}^{\alpha}(f_{1},g_{1})(\omega)\quad\text{for any }\omega\in B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4}),

we have

(6.1)

\displaystyle\left\|T_{j}^{1}(f_{1},g_{1})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|T_{j}^{\alpha}(f_{1},g_{1})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{1}{4}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-j(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}\left(\int_{\left|\mu_{1}\right|\leq 1}\left\|(f_{1})_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\left|\mu_{2}\right|\leq 1}\left\|(g_{1})_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}^{\mu_{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\mu_{2}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Considering the integral about
\mu_{1}
, we notice that

\left\|(f_{1})_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\right\|_{2}=\left|\det A_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|f_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\right\|_{2}\leq C2^{j(1+\gamma)n}\left\|f_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq C2^{j(1+\gamma)\left(\frac{Q}{2}+m\right)}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{3}{4}))}.

So, we can get that

\displaystyle\int_{\left|\mu_{1}\right|\leq 1}\left\|(f_{1})_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{2^{-2j(1+\gamma)}\leq\left|\mu_{1}\right|\leq 1}\left\|(f_{1})_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{1}+\int_{\left|\mu_{1}\right|\leq 2^{-2j(1+\gamma)}}\left\|(f_{1})_{\frac{\mu_{1}}{\left|\mu_{1}\right|}}^{\mu_{1}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{2j\delta(1+\gamma)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left\|(f_{1})_{\frac{\mu_{2}}{\left|\mu_{2}\right|}}^{\mu_{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\,d\mu_{1}+C2^{j(1+\gamma)\left(Q+2m\right)}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{3}{4}))}^{2}\int_{\left|\mu_{1}\right|\leq 2^{-2j(1+\gamma)}}\left|\mu_{1}\right|^{-\delta}\,d\mu_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{2j\delta(1+\gamma)}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{2}^{2}+C2^{j(1+\gamma)\left(Q+2m\right)}2^{-2j(1+\gamma)(-\delta+m)}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{j}(\xi,\frac{3}{4}))}^{2}

7.
bilinear interpolation

Because
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are symmetric, we have obtained, in two sections
above, the boundedness of the bilinear Riesz means
S^{\alpha}
at some
specific triples of points
(p_{1},p_{2},p)
like

(1,1,\frac{1}{2}),(2,2,1),(\infty,\infty,\infty),(1,2,\frac{2}{3}),(2,1,\frac{2}{3}),(1,\infty,1),(\infty,1,1),(2,\infty,2),(\infty,2,2)\text{.}

We can obtain the intermediate boundedness of
S^{\alpha}
by using of the
bilinear interpolation via complex method adapted to the setting of analytic
families or real method in
[
3
]
. Bernicot et al.
[
1
]

described how to make use of real method. We outline this argument for reader’s convenience.

Consider a spherical decomposition of
S^{\alpha}
as

S^{\alpha}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j\alpha}T_{j,\alpha},

where

T_{j,\alpha}(f,g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{j,\alpha}\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)P_{\lambda_{1}}fP_{\lambda_{2}}gd\mu(\lambda_{1})d\mu(\lambda_{2})

and

\varphi_{j,\alpha}\left(s,t\right)=2^{j\alpha}(1-s-t)_{+}^{\alpha}\varphi(2^{j}\left(1-s-t\right)).

In the preceding sections, we have actually obtained the estimates of the
form

(7.1)

\left\|T_{j,\alpha}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq C2^{j\alpha(p_{1},p_{2})}

at some triples of points
(p_{1},p_{2},p)
. Since
\alpha(p_{1,}p_{2})

only depends on the point
(p_{1,}p_{2},p)
, (
7.1
) also holds for any
other
T_{j,\alpha^{\prime}}
, i.e.,

\left\|T_{j,\alpha^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq C2^{j\alpha(p_{1},p_{2})}.

So, fixing
j
and
\alpha^{\prime}
and applying bilinear real
interpolation theorem on
T_{j,\alpha^{\prime}}
, we can conclude that if
the point
(p_{1},p_{2},p)
satisfies

\left(\frac{1}{p_{1}},\frac{1}{p_{2}},\frac{1}{p}\right)=(1-\theta)\left(\frac{1}{p_{1}^{0}},\frac{1}{p_{2}^{0}},\frac{1}{p^{0}}\right)+\theta\left(\frac{1}{p_{1}^{1}},\frac{1}{p_{2}^{1}},\frac{1}{p^{1}}\right)

for some
\theta\in(0,1)
and
(p_{1}^{0},p_{2}^{0},p^{0}),(p_{1}^{1},p_{2}^{1},p^{1})
,  we have
that

\left\|T_{j,\alpha^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq C2^{j((1-\theta)\alpha(p_{1}^{0},p_{2}^{0})+\theta\alpha(p_{1}^{1},p_{2}^{1}))}.

Define
\alpha(p_{1,}p_{2})=(1-\theta)\alpha(p_{1}^{0},p_{2}^{0})+\theta\alpha(p_{1}^{1},p_{2}^{1})
and let
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha
. It
follows that

\left\|S^{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j\alpha}\left\|T_{j,\alpha}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j\alpha}2^{j\alpha(p_{1,}p_{2})}.

Thus, when
\alpha>\alpha(p_{1,}p_{2})
, we have
\left\|S^{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}}\leq C
, i.e.,
S^{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{G})\times L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{G})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{G})
.

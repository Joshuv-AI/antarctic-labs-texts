Quasi-homomorphisms of quantum cluster algebras
Wen Chang, Min Huang, Jian-Rong Li

Abstract
. In this paper, we study quasi-homomorphisms of quantum cluster algebras, which are quantum analogy of quasi-homomorphisms of cluster algebras introduced by Fraser. For a quantum Grassmannian cluster algebra ℂ q ​ [ Gr ⁡ ( k , n ) ] {\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)] , we show that there is an associated braid group and each generator σ i \sigma_{i} of the braid group preserves the quasi-commutative relations of quantum Plücker coordinates and exchange relations of the quantum Grassmannian cluster algebra. We conjecture that σ i \sigma_{i} also preserves r r -term ( r ≥ 4 r\geq 4 ) quantum Plücker relations of ℂ q ​ [ Gr ⁡ ( k , n ) ] {\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)] and other relations which cannot be derived from quantum quantum Plücker relations (if any). Up to this conjecture, we show that σ i \sigma_{i} is a quasi-automorphism of ℂ q ​ [ Gr ⁡ ( k , n ) ] {\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)] and the braid group acts on ℂ q ​ [ Gr ⁡ ( k , n ) ] {\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)] .



1.
Introduction

Cluster algebras were introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky in
[
FZ02
]
.
To study the symmetries of the cluster algebras, Assem, Schiffler, and Shramchenko introduced cluster automorphisms in
[
ASS12
]
.
The concept was generalized to quasi-homomorphisms of cluster algebras by Fraser in
[
Fra16
]
. More relations between cluster automorphisms and quasi-automorphisms were discovered in
[
CS19
]
. Both the automorphisms and quasi-homomorphisms of cluster algebras are essential tools in the study of symmetries of cluster algebras.

It is natural to consider quantum version of “quasi-homomorphisms of cluster algebras”. In
[
KQW22
]
, Kimura, Qin, and Wei introduced twist automorphisms for upper cluster algebras and cluster Poisson algebras with coefficients. In Section 8 of
[
KQW22
]
, they introduced quantization of twist automorphisms for quantum upper cluster algebras.

The goal of this paper is to study quasi-homomorphisms of quantum cluster algebras and to study braid group action on the quantum Grassmannian cluster algebra
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
.

We write a definition of a quasi-homomorphism
f
from a quantum cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}_{q}
to another quantum cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}_{q}^{\prime}
, see Definition
2.1
and Remark
2.2
, which is more similar to the non-quantum version of quasi-homomorphisms given by Fraser
[
Fra16
]
. We require that
f:\mathcal{A}_{q}\to\mathcal{A}_{q}^{\prime}
is an algebra homomorphism, sends quantum clusters in
\mathcal{A}_{q}
to quantum clusters in
\mathcal{A}_{q}^{\prime}
(up to frozen variables), preserves the mutable part of exchange matrix, and sends quantum cluster
\mathcal{X}
-variables (denoted by
\widehat{y}_{i}
) to quantum cluster
\mathcal{X}
-variables. The quantum quasi-homomorphism in Definition
2.1
is slightly different from the quantum twist automorphism in
[
KQW22
]
. The quantum quasi-homomorphism in Definition
2.1
is defined for quantum cluster algebras, while quantum twist automorphism in
[
KQW22
]
is defined for quantum upper cluster algebras. We prove some properties of quantum quasi-homomorphisms which will be used in the study of braid group actions on quantum Grassmannian cluster algebras, see Proposition
2.8
.

For
k\leq n
, the Grassmannian
\Gr(k,n)
is the set of
k
-dimensional subspaces in an
n
-dimensional vector space. Denote by
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
its coordinate ring. Scott proved that there is a cluster algebra structure on
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]

[
Sco06
]
. The coordinate ring
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
is called a
Grassmannian cluster algebra
. Fraser
[
Fra17
]
has studied braid group action on
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
. He introduced regular automorphisms
\sigma_{i},1\leq i\leq d-1,
on
\Gr(k,n)
, which induce quasi-automorphisms
\sigma^{*}_{i}
(for simplicity, we also write
\sigma_{i}^{*}
as
\sigma_{i}
) on the coordinate ring
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
, where
d={\rm gcd}(k,n)
, and he proved that these automorphisms give a braid group action on
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
.

In this paper we take
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}={\mathbb{C}}(q^{\pm 1/2})
. The quantized coordinate ring
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
is the subalgebra of the quantum matrix algebra
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[M(k,n)]
generated by the quantum Plücker coordinates, see
[
TT91
,
GL14
,
Lau06
,
Lau10
]
and Section
3.1
. Grabowski and Launois
[
GL11
,
GL14
]
proved that the quantum deformation
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
of
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
has a quantum algebra structure.

Jensen, King and Su showed (see the paragraphs before Theorem 1.3 in
[
JKS22
]
) that there is a quantum cluster algebra containing cluster variables
X_{J}
for all
k
-subsets
J
of
[n]
, whose quasi-commutation rules are the same as the quasi-commutation rules of the quantum minors
\Delta_{q}^{J}
of
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
. They showed that the quantum cluster algebra is isomorphic to the quantum coordinate ring
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
by identifying
X_{J}
with
\Delta_{q}^{J}
for all
J
, see Theorem 1.3 in
[
JKS22
]
(note that we take
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}={\mathbb{C}}(q^{\pm 1/2})
).

There is a bijection
\varphi
between cluster variables in
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
and cluster variables in
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
, see Section
3.3
. It is shown in
[
Fra17
]
that in
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
, for every cluster variable
x
,
\sigma_{i}(x)=y_{1}y_{2}\cdots y_{r}\tilde{x}
, where
r
is a postive integer,
y_{i}
is a frozen variable or the inverse of a frozen variable, and
\tilde{x}
is a cluster variable. In
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
, we define
\sigma_{i}(\varphi(x))
to be the quantum cluster Laurent monomial
[\varphi(y_{1})\varphi(y_{2})\cdots\varphi(y_{r})\varphi(\tilde{x})]
, see Sections
3.2
,
3.5
, and
3.2
for the definition of the notation
[x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{k_{m}}]
.

We prove that every map
\sigma_{i}
extends to a map (also denoted by
\sigma_{i}
) that preserves the quasi-commutation relations of quantum Plücker coordinates and exchange relations of the quantum Grassmannian cluster algebra
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
. We conjecture that
\sigma_{i}
also preserves
r
-term (
r\geq 4
) quantum Plücker relations of
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
and other relations which cannot be derived from quantum Plücker relations (if any), see Conjecture
3.11
. Up to this conjecture, we show that
\sigma_{i}
is a quasi-automorphism of
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
and the braid group acts on
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
, see Theorem
3.15
. This braid group action gives a way to discover many quantum cluster variables and quantum clusters of the quantum Grassmannian cluster algerba.

Symmetries of quantum Grassmannians have been studied in
[
LL11
]
,
[
AG12
]
,
[
LL23
]
. Launois and Lenagan proved that a certain cycling map
\rho
defines a
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}
-algebra isomorphism from
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
to its twisted algebra
T({\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)])
and they showed that the cycling map
\rho
is not a
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}
-algebra automorphism of
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
. For example, in
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(2,4)]
, the map
\sigma_{1}
is similar to but different from the cycling map
\rho
. Unlike
\rho
, the map
\sigma_{1}
is a
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}
-algebra automorphism on
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(2,4)]
(see Section
3.6
). The braid group action gives a large family of
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}
-algebra automorphisms on the quantum cluster algebra
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
, see Lemma
3.14
.

Marsh and Scott
[
MS16
]
defined a twist automorphism on
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
which sends cluster variables to cluster variables up to multiplying frozen variables. This twist automorphism is closed related to Berenstein–Fomin–Zelevinsky twist automorphisms on unipotent cells
[
BFZ96
]
. We expect that there is a quantum version of Marsh-Scott’s twist map on
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
which should be closely related to the quantum twist map studied in
[
KO19
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the concept of quasi-homomorphisms of quantum cluster algebras. In Section
3
, we study braid group actions on quantum Grassmannian cluster algebras. In Section
4
, we prove our main result: Theorem
3.15
.

2.
Quasi-homomorphisms of quantum cluster algebras

We will introduce the notion of quasi-homomorphisms of quantum cluster algebras in this section.
We start with briefly recalling some background which will be used in the following, see details in
[
BZ05
]
.

2.1.
Background on quantum cluster algebras

2.1.1.
Quantum seeds

Let
m
and
n
be two positive integers. A quantum seed is a triple
\Sigma=({\bf{x}},\widetilde{B},\Lambda)
, where

•

{\bf{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
is an ordered set with
m
elements;

•

\widetilde{B}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}B\\
C\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Mat_{m\times n}({\mathbb{Z}})
is an extended skew-symmetrizable matrix, that is, there exists a diagonal matrix
D
with positive integer entries such that
DB
is skew-symmetric, where
B
is the
n\times n
submatrix of
\widetilde{B}
consisting of the first
n
rows;

•

\Lambda\in\Mat_{m\times m}({\mathbb{Z}})
is a skew-symmetric matrix such that
(\widetilde{B},\Lambda)
is a compatible pair, that is
{\widetilde{B}}^{t}\Lambda=(D,0_{n\times(m-n)})
.

The set
{\bf{x}}
is the
cluster
of the quantum seed
\Sigma
. The elements in
{\bf{p}}=\{x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{m}\}\subset{\bf{x}}
are called the
coefficient variables
and the elements in
{\bf{x}}\setminus{\bf{p}}
are called the
cluster variables
of
\Sigma
. The
n\times n
matrix
B
is called the
exchange matrix
of
\Sigma
; its rows are called
exchangeable rows
, and the remaining rows of
\widetilde{B}
are called
frozen rows
. We always assume that both
\widetilde{B}
and
B
are indecomposable matrices, and we also assume that
n>1
for convenience.

2.1.2.
Quantum torus

Denote
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}={\mathbb{C}}(q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}})
, where
q
is an indeterminate. A quantum torus
\mathbb{T}
of rank
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
is a
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}
-algebra which has basis
\{X^{a}\mid a\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}\}
with multiplication:

(2.1)

X^{a}X^{b}=q^{\frac{1}{2}\Lambda(a,b)}X^{a+b},

where
\Lambda(-,-)
is the bilinear form given by a skew-symmetric matrix
\Lambda
.

Let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
be the standard basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
, then we have

(2.2)

X^{e_{i}}X^{e_{j}}=q^{\frac{1}{2}\Lambda(e_{i},e_{j})}X^{e_{i}+e_{j}},X^{e_{i}}X^{e_{j}}=q^{\Lambda(e_{i},e_{j})}X^{e_{j}}X^{e_{i}}.

We associate with
\mathbb{T}
the
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear
bar-involution
by setting

\overline{q^{\frac{1}{2}}X^{a}}=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}X^{a}

on each basis element.
An element
X
in
\mathbb{T}
is a
bar-invariant
if it is maintained by the bar-involution, that is,
\overline{X}=X
.

Given a quantum seed
\Sigma=({\bf{x}},\widetilde{B},\Lambda)
with notations as above, we associate to it a quantum torus
\mathbb{T}(\Sigma)
, where
X^{e_{i}}=x_{i}
and
\Lambda(-,-)
is the bilinear form given by
\Lambda
.
We denote by
\mathbb{P}
the monoid (the multiplication is inherited from
\mathbb{T}(\Sigma)
) consisting of elements
X^{(0,a)}
with
0\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m-n}
.

2.1.3.
Quantum mutations

Denote by
b_{ij}
the entry in
\widetilde{B}
located at position
(i,j)
for
1\leq i\leq m
,
1\leq j\leq n
and by
b_{k}
the
k
-th column of
\widetilde{B}
for
1\leq k\leq n
. Similarly we use notations
\lambda_{ij}
and
\lambda_{k}
for
\Lambda
with
1\leq i,j,k\leq m
.
The quantum seed
\mu_{k}(\Sigma)=(\mu_{k}({\bf{x}}),\mu_{k}(\widetilde{B}),\mu_{k}(\Lambda))
obtained by the
mutation
of
\Sigma
in direction
k
is given by:

•

\mu_{k}({\bf{x}})=({\bf{x}}\setminus\{x_{k}\})\sqcup\{\mu_{k}(x_{k})\}
where
\mu_{k}(x_{k})
is an element in
\mathbb{T}(\Sigma)
defined by

(2.3)

\mu_{k}(x_{k})=X^{-e_{k}+[b_{k}]_{+}}+X^{-e_{k}+[-b_{k}]_{+}}.

•

\mu_{k}(\widetilde{B})=(b^{\prime}_{ji})_{m\times n}\in M_{m\times n}({\mathbb{Z}})
is given by

(2.4)

b^{\prime}_{ji}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-b_{ji}&\textrm{ if }i=k\textrm{ or }j=k~;\\
b_{ji}+[-b_{jk}]_{+}b_{ki}+b_{jk}[b_{ki}]_{+}&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

•

\mu_{k}(\Lambda)=(\lambda^{\prime}_{ji})_{m\times m}\in M_{m\times m}({\mathbb{Z}})
is given by

(2.5)

\lambda^{\prime}_{ji}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda_{jk}+\lambda^{t}_{j}[b_{k}]_{+}&\textrm{ if }i=k~\vskip 2.84526pt;\\
\lambda_{ki}-\lambda^{t}_{i}[b_{k}]_{+}&\textrm{ if }j=k~;\\
\lambda_{ji}&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

It can be check that
(\mu_{k}(\widetilde{B}),\mu_{k}(\Lambda))
is still a compatible pair and
\mu(\Sigma)
is a quantum seed.

2.1.4.
Quantum cluster algebra

Let
\mathcal{X}
be the set of cluster variables in the quantum seeds obtained by iterated mutations from a quantum seed
\Sigma
. Then the algebra

\mathcal{A}_{q}=\mathcal{A}_{q}(\Sigma):={\mathbb{C}}_{q}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{X}]

algebraically generated by elements in
\mathcal{X}
over
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}\mathbb{P}
is called the
quantum cluster algebra
of
rank

n
defined by
\Sigma
, which is a
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}\mathbb{P}
-subalgebra of the quantum torus
\mathbb{T}(\Sigma)
. The elements in
\mathcal{X}
are called
cluster variables
, and the elements in
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}\mathbb{P}
are called
coefficients
of
\mathcal{A}_{q}
.

2.2.
Quasi-homomorphisms of quantum cluster algebras

Let
\mathcal{A}_{q}
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{q}
be two quantum cluster algebras of rank
n
.
Assume that
\Sigma=\{{\bf{x}}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\},\widetilde{B}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}B\\
C\end{smallmatrix}\right),\Lambda\}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}=\{{\bf{x}}^{\prime}=\{x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}\},\widetilde{B^{\prime}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}B^{\prime}\\
C^{\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\Lambda^{\prime}\}
be the initial seeds of them respectively. Correspondingly, we have quantum torus
\mathbb{T}
and
\mathbb{T}^{\prime}
.

Definition 2.1
.

We call a
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism
f:\mathcal{A}_{q}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{q}
a quantum quasi-homomorphism (with respect to
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
) if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

B=B^{\prime}
;

(2)

f(x_{i})=X^{\prime e^{\prime}_{i}+\widetilde{h}_{i}}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, and
f(x_{j})=X^{\prime\widetilde{l}_{j}}
for all
n+1\leq i\leq m
, where
\widetilde{h}_{i}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
h_{i}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
\widetilde{l}_{j}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
l_{j}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
with
0\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},h_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{{m^{\prime}}-n}
and
l_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{{m^{\prime}}-n}
such that

f(\hat{y}_{i})=\hat{y}^{\prime}_{i}

for all
1\leq i\leq n
, where
\hat{y}_{i}=X^{b_{i}}
and
\hat{y}^{\prime}_{i}=X^{\prime b^{\prime}_{i}}
.

Remark 2.2
.

In Definition 3.1.3 of
[
KQW22
]
, the variation map between Laurent polynomial rings preserving the Poisson structure was defined. It commutes with mutation sequences by Proposition 3.3.2 in
[
KQW22
]
. It restricts to a map between upper cluster algebras by Lemma 3.3.4 in
[
KQW22
]
. Since the Poisson structure is preserved, the map has a natural quantization (see the first paragraph in Section 8.2 in
[
KQW22
]
) and the authors introduced quantum twist automorphisms for quantum upper cluster algebras.
We would like to thank Fan Qin for telling us these after our paper was uploaded to Arxiv.

The quantum quasi-homomorphism in Definition
2.1
is slightly different from the quantum twist automorphism in
[
KQW22
]
. The quantum quasi-homomorphism in Definition
2.1
is defined for quantum cluster algebras, while quantum twist automorphism in
[
KQW22
]
is defined for quantum upper cluster algebras. Definition
2.1
is more similar to the non-quantum version of quasi-homomorphisms introduced by Fraser in
[
Fra16
]
.

Let

\displaystyle L=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{m-n}),\quad H=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}),\quad R=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n\times n}&0\\
H&L\end{array}\right)\in\Mat_{{m^{\prime}}\times m}({\mathbb{Z}}).

Denote by
r_{i}
the
i
-th column of
R
. Then for any
1\leq i\leq m
,

(2.6)

f(X^{e_{i}})=X^{\prime r_{i}}.

Lemma 2.3
.

For a quantum quasi-homomorphism
f:\mathcal{A}_{q}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{q}
, we have that

R^{t}\Lambda^{\prime}R=\Lambda.

Proof.

For any
i,j\in[m]
, we have
X^{e_{i}}X^{e_{j}}=q^{\Lambda(e_{i},e_{j})}X^{e_{j}}X^{e_{i}}.
As
f
is a
\mathbb{C}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism, we obtain

f(X^{e_{i}})f(X^{e_{j}})=q^{\Lambda(e_{i},e_{j})}f(X^{e_{j}})f(X^{e_{i}}).

Then by equalities (
2.2
) and (
2.6
), we have
\frac{1}{2}\Lambda^{\prime}(r_{i},r_{j})=\Lambda(e_{i},e_{j})+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda^{\prime}(r_{j},r_{i})
. Consequently,
r_{i}^{t}\Lambda^{\prime}r_{j}=\Lambda^{\prime}(r_{i},r_{j})=\Lambda(e_{i},e_{j})=\lambda_{ij}
for
i,j\in[1,m]
.
∎

For any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, denote by
R^{\prime}\in{\rm Mat}_{m^{\prime}\times m}(\mathbb{Z})
the matrix with the
k
-th column
r^{\prime}_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,m
, where

r^{\prime}_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e^{\prime}_{i}+R[b_{i}]_{+}-[b^{\prime}_{i}]_{+}&\textrm{ if }k=i~\vskip 2.84526pt;\\
r_{k}&\textrm{ if }k\neq i.\end{array}\right.

Note that if we set
q
to be one, then the definition degenerates back to the non-quantum case introduced by
[
Fra16
]
.
In particular, the following lemma follows from the commutative case.

Lemma 2.4
.

For a quantum quasi-homomorphism
f:\mathcal{A}_{q}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{q}
, we have that

(1)

R\widetilde{B}=\widetilde{B^{\prime}}
,

(2)

R^{\prime}\mu_{i}\widetilde{B}=\mu_{i}\widetilde{B^{\prime}}.

For any quasi-homomorphism
f:\mathcal{A}_{q}(\Sigma)\to\mathcal{A}_{q}(\Sigma^{\prime})
, it is clear that
f
induces a ring homomorphism
f:\mathbb{T}(\Sigma)\to\mathbb{T}(\Sigma^{\prime})
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f:\mathcal{A}_{q}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{q}
be a quasi-homomorphism. Then for any
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, we have that

\displaystyle f(X^{a})=X^{\prime Ra}.

In particular,
f(X^{a})
is bar-invariant.

Proof.

By direct calculation, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}f(X^{a})&=&f(q^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}a_{i}a_{j}\lambda_{ij}}x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{a_{m}})=q^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}a_{i}a_{j}\lambda_{ij}}X^{\prime a_{1}r_{1}}\cdots X^{\prime a_{m}r_{m}}\\
&=&q^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}a_{i}a_{j}\lambda_{ij}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}a_{i}a_{j}\Lambda^{\prime}(r_{i},r_{j})}X^{\prime a_{1}r_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}r_{m}}=X^{\prime a_{1}r_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}r_{m}}\\
&=&X^{\prime Ra},\end{array}

where the last but one equality follows by Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
f:\mathcal{A}_{q}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{q}
be a quasi-homomorphism. Then
f
maps a bar-invariant element in
\mathcal{A}_{q}
to a bar-invariant element in
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{q}
, that is,
\overline{f(X)}=f(X)
if
\overline{X}=X\in\mathcal{A}_{q}
.

Proof.

It follows from the second condition in Definition
2.1
that
f(x_{i}),i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
are bar-invariant. For any
X\in\mathcal{A}_{q}
such that
\overline{X}=X
, by the quantum Laurent phenomenon, we have
X=\sum_{a}\lambda(a)X^{a}
for some
\lambda(a)\in\mathbb{{\mathbb{C}}}_{q}
.
Then we have
f(X)=\sum_{a}\lambda(a)f(X^{a})
and
\overline{f(X)}=\sum_{a}\overline{\lambda(a)}f(X^{a})
. As
\overline{X}=X
, we have
\overline{\lambda(a)}=\lambda(a)
for each
a
. Then by Lemma
2.5
, we see that
\overline{f(X)}=f(X)
, that is,
f(X)
is bar-invariant.
∎

The following proposition shows that the definition of quasi-homomorphism of quantum cluster algebras is compatible with the mutations.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
f:\mathcal{A}_{q}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{q}
be a quantum quasi-homomorphism with respect to seeds
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
. Then for any sequence
(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{s})
,
i_{j}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we have that
f:\mathcal{A}_{q}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{q}
is a quantum quasi-homomorphism with respect to the seeds
\mu_{i_{s}}\cdots\mu_{i_{1}}\Sigma
and
\mu_{i_{s}}\cdots\mu_{i_{1}}\Sigma^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By induction, it suffices to prove the case that
s=1
. Denote
i=i_{1}
. Then by Lemma
2.5
we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}f(\mu_{i}x_{i})&=&f(X^{-e_{i}+[b_{i}]_{+}}+X^{-e_{i}+[-b_{i}]_{+}})=X^{\prime R(-e_{i}+[b_{i}]_{+})}+X^{\prime R(-e_{i}+[-b_{i}]_{+})}\\
&=&X^{\prime-e^{\prime}_{i}+R[b_{i}]_{+}}+X^{\prime-e^{\prime}_{i}+R[-b_{i}]_{+}}.\end{array}

By Lemma
2.4
(1), we have
[b_{i}^{\prime}]_{+}-[-b^{\prime}_{i}]_{+}=b^{\prime}_{i}=Rb_{i}=R[b_{i}]_{+}-R[-b_{i}]_{+}

\mu_{i}(x_{i}^{\prime})=X^{\prime-e_{i}^{\prime}+[b^{\prime}_{i}]_{+}}+X^{\prime-e_{i}^{\prime}+[-b^{\prime}_{i}]_{+}}

f(\mu_{i}x_{i})=(\mu_{i}(X^{\prime}))^{e^{\prime}_{i}+R[b_{i}]_{+}-[b^{\prime}_{i}]_{+}}.

As

and

, we see that the first

.

For any

n

are
0
,
we have

For any

, as the first
n

Next, we show that

\displaystyle=(\mu_{i}(X^{\prime}))^{d_{ij}(e^{\prime}_{i}+R[b_{i}]_{+}-[b^{\prime}_{i}]_{+})+\sum_{1\leq k(\neq i)\leq n}d_{kj}(e^{\prime}_{k}+\widetilde{h}_{k})+\sum_{n+1\leq k\leq m}d_{kj}\widetilde{l}_{j}}

∎

So roughly speaking, a quasi-homomorphism of quantum cluster algebras is an algebra homomorphism between quantum cluster algebras of the same type which sends a cluster variable to a cluster variable possibly multiplying a Laurent monomial in frozen variables, sends a frozen variable to a Laurent monomial in frozen variables, in a way that keeps the hatted
y
-variables and commutes with the mutations.
On the other hand, from a view point of linear algebra, the following proposition says that a quasi-homomorphism between quantum cluster algebras is equivalent to two matrix equations.

To introduce a concept of quasi-isomorphism of quantum cluster algebras, we need the following proportional relation, which is a quantum analogy of the one introduced by Fraser
[
Fra16
]
.
For elements
x,y
in a quantum cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}_{q}
, we say that
x

is proportional to

y
, writing
x\propto y
, if
x=q^{\frac{\ell}{2}}py
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
and some coefficient monomial
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Likewise, let
\mathcal{A}_{q}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{q}
be a pair of quantum cluster algebras, and let
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
be two quasi-homomorphisms from
\mathcal{A}_{q}
to
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{q}
, we say that
f_{1}

is proportional to

f_{2}
, and write
f_{1}\propto f_{2}
, if
f_{1}(x)\propto f_{2}(x)
for every cluster variable
x\in\mathcal{A}_{q}
.

A quasi-homomorphism
f\colon\mathcal{A}_{q}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{q}
is called a
quasi-isomorphism
if there is a quasi-homomorphism
g\colon\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{q}\to\mathcal{A}_{q}
such that the composite
g\circ f
is proportional to the identity map on
\mathcal{A}_{q}
and the composite
f\circ g
is proportional to the identity map on
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{q}
. A
quasi-automorphism
is a quasi-isomorphism from a quantum cluster algebra to itself.
Then we are able to consider the
quasi-automorphism group

{\rm QAut}(\mathcal{A}_{q})
of a quantum cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}_{q}
, which consists of the equivalence classes of quasi-automorphisms on
\mathcal{A}_{q}
up to proportionality.

Note that for a classical (non-quantum) cluster algebra, Assem, Schiffler, and Shramchenko introduced cluster automorphisms in
[
ASS12
]
. Similarly, one may consider the quantum version, a
quantum cluster automorphism
, that is, a
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}
-algebra automorphism of a quantum cluster algebra which sends a cluster to a cluster, sends a cluster variable to a cluster variable (without multiplying frozen variables), and sends a frozen variable to a frozen variable. Then it is not hard to see that a quantum cluster automorphism is a special quantum quasi-automorphism, and a quantum quasi-homomorphism
f
is a quantum cluster automorphism if and only if
(\widetilde{B},\Lambda)=(\widetilde{B^{\prime}},\Lambda^{\prime})
.

It would be interesting to compute the
automorphism group

{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{A}_{q})
of a quantum cluster algebra, which consists of all the quantum cluster automorphisms, and compare it with the group
{\rm QAut}(\mathcal{A}_{q})
.
This is in some sense trivial for the quantum cluster algebras arising from the surfaces.
More precisely, let
\mathcal{A}
be the (classical non-quantum) cluster algebra arising from a unpunctured surface
(S,M)
with coefficients arising from the boundary segments, and let
\mathcal{A}_{\rm triv}
be the associated cluster algebra with trivial coefficients. Since
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{A})\cong{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{A}_{\rm triv})\cong{\rm MCG}(S,M)

[
BS15
,
CZ16
]
, where
{\rm MCG}(S,M)
is the mapping class group of the surface, triangulations
T
and
T^{\prime}
are homotopic if and only if the associated matrices
\widetilde{B}
and
\widetilde{B^{\prime}}
coincide. Moreover, the Lambda matrix of the associated quantum surface cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}_{q}
comes from the triangulation in the sense of Muller
[
M16
]
, thus the matrices
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
arising from
T
and
T^{\prime}
coincide:
\Lambda=\Lambda^{\prime}
. Therefore we have an isomorphism
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{A}_{q})\cong{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{A})
.
On the other hand, it is proved in
[
CS19
]
that
{\rm QAut}(\mathcal{A})\cong{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{A})
.
By using the fact that the Lambda matrix is arising from the triangulation, the method used in
[
CS19
]
can be extended to the quantum case, and we have an isomorphism of groups:
{\rm QAut}(\mathcal{A}_{q})\cong{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{A}_{q})
. To sum up, for (almost all) the surface cluster algebras, both for the quantum case and the non-quantum case, the groups we mentioned above are all isomorphic.

We collect some background in the following,

3.1.
Background on the quantum Grassmannian coordinate ring
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]

The quantum matrix algebra
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[M(k,n)]
is a
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}
-algebra generated by
\{x_{ij}\mid 1\leq i\leq k,1\leq j\leq n\}
subject to the following relations.

\displaystyle x_{ij}x_{il}=qx_{il}x_{ij},

\displaystyle j<l,

\displaystyle x_{ij}x_{rj}=qx_{rj}x_{ij},

\displaystyle i<r,

\displaystyle x_{ij}x_{rl}=x_{rl}x_{ij},

\displaystyle i<r,j>l,

\displaystyle x_{ij}x_{rl}=x_{rl}x_{ij}+(q-q^{-1})x_{il}x_{rj},\quad

\displaystyle i<r,j<l.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. For any
I\subset[1,m]
,
J\subset[1,n]
with
|I|=|J|=l>0
, a quantum minor
\Delta_{I,J}
is defined by

\displaystyle\Delta_{q}^{I,J}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{l}}(-q)^{\ell(\sigma)}x_{i_{1},j_{\sigma(1)}}\cdots x_{i_{l},j_{\sigma(l)}},

where
\{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{l}\}=I
,
\{j_{1}<\cdots<j_{l}\}=J
, and
\ell(\sigma)
is the length of the permutation
\sigma
.

For the Grassmannian
\Gr(k,n)
, its quantized coordinate ring
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]

is the subalgebra of the quantum matrix algebra
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[M(k,n)]
generated by the quantum Plücker coordinates

\Delta_{q}^{J}:=\Delta_{q}^{I,J},

where
I=\{1,\ldots,k\}
and
J\subset[1,n]
with
|J|=k
, see
[
TT91
,
GL14
]
. We assume that the frozen quantum Plücker coordinates
\Delta_{q}^{J}
are invertible, where
J
consists of
k
consecutive integers (up to cyclic shift).

It is shown in
[
LZ98
]
that two quantum Plücker coordinates
\Delta_{q}^{I}
,
\Delta_{q}^{J}
quasi-commute if and only if
I,J
are weakly separated. We recall the definition of “weakly separated” in the following.

Definition 3.1
.

Given two
k
-subsets
I
and
J
of
[1,n]
, denote by
\min(J)
the minimal element in
J
and by
\max(I)
the maximal element in
I
, we write
I\prec J
if
\max(I)<\min(J)
. The sets
I
and
J
are called weakly separated if at least one of the
following two conditions holds:

(1)

J-I
can be partitioned into a disjoint union
J-I=J^{\prime}\sqcup J^{\prime\prime}
so that
J^{\prime}\prec I-J\prec J^{\prime\prime}
;

(2)

I-J
can be partitioned into a disjoint union
I-J=I^{\prime}\sqcup I^{\prime\prime}
so that
I^{\prime}\prec J-I\prec I^{\prime\prime}
.

In
[
Sco05
, Theorem 2]
, Scott computed quasi-commutation relations on weakly separated subsets.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
Sco05
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Suppose

and

are weakly separated
k
-subsets of
[1,n]
. Then we have
\Delta_{q}^{I}\Delta_{q}^{J}=q^{\Lambda(\Delta_{q}^{I},\Delta_{q}^{J})}\Delta_{q}^{J}\Delta_{q}^{I}
, where

\displaystyle\Lambda(\Delta_{q}^{I},\Delta_{q}^{J})=\begin{cases}|J^{\prime\prime}|-|J^{\prime}|,&\text{ if (1) in Definition \ref{def:weakly separted k-subsets} is satisfied},\\
|I^{\prime}|-|I^{\prime\prime}|,&\text{ if (2) in Definition \ref{def:weakly separted k-subsets} is satisfied}.\end{cases}

Every maximal weakly separated collection of
k
-subsets of

determines a seed in the cluster algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
, where the cluster in the seed is a set of Plücker coordinates parameterized by the subsets in the collection, see
[

,
Pos06
,
Sco06
,
OPS15
]
. According to Theorem 6.1 in
[
BZ05
]
and Theorem 7.6 in
[
GL14
]
, the exchange graphs of

and
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
are isomorphic. Together with the results in
[

Pos06
,
Sco05
,
Sco06
,

]

, where the Lambda matrix is given by the quasi-commutation relations of the associated Plücker coordinates in Theorem

3.3.
A bijection between cluster Laurent monomials in Grassmannian cluster algebra and the corresponding quantum Grassmannian cluster algebra

We define a map
\varphi

{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
to the set of cluster variables in
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
as follows. First for
\Delta^{J}
(
\Delta^{J}
can be a frozen variable) in the initial cluster, we define
\varphi(\Delta^{J})=\Delta_{q}^{J}
. We use induction to define
\varphi(x)
for other cluster variables in
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
. We apply the same mutation sequence to
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
and
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
. At each step of mutation at some vertex with cluster variable
x
, we define
\varphi(x^{\prime})=(\varphi(x))^{\prime}
, where
x^{\prime}
is the cluster variable of
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
obtained by mutation at
x
and
(\varphi(x))^{\prime}
is the cluster variable obtained by mutation at
\varphi(x)
in
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
. For a cluster variable or frozen variable
x
, we define
\varphi(x^{-1})=(\varphi(x))^{-1}
. According to Theorem 6.1 in
[
BZ05
]
and Theorem 7.6 in
[

]
, the exchange graph of the cluster algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
and the exchange graph of the quantum cluster algebra
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
are isomorphic.
The map
\varphi
is clearly a bijection between the set of cluster variables in
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
and the set of cluster variables in
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
. Therefore every cluster variable or frozen variable in
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
is of the form
\varphi(x)
for some cluster variable
x
in
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
.

For a cluster monomial
x_{1}\cdots x_{r}
in
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
and for

, we define

(3.1)

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{a_{r}})=[\varphi(x_{1})^{a_{1}}\cdots\varphi(x_{r})^{a_{r}}].

In

,

]

has a quantum cluster algebra structure. The exchange relations in

are of the form

.
Moreover, the extended exchange matrices of

and

3.4.
Fraser’s braid group action on Grassmannian cluster algebras

We now recall Fraser’s braid group action on
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
. Let
V
be a
k
-dimensional complex vector space. An
n
-tuple of vectors
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
is called consecutively generic if every cyclically consecutive
k
-tuple of vectors is linearly independent, i.e.,
\det(v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{i+k})\neq 0
for
i=1,\ldots,n
where the indices are treated modulo
n
. Denote by
(V^{n})^{\circ}\subset V^{n}
the quasi-affine variety consisting of consecutively generic
n
-tuples.

Let
d={\rm gcd}(k,n)
. For
i\in[1,d-1]
, the map
\sigma_{i}:(V^{n})^{\circ}\to(V^{n})^{\circ}
is defined as follows, see Definition 5.2 and Equation (18) in Remark 5.6 of
[
Fra17
]
. Divide an element
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
in
(V^{n})^{\circ}
into
\frac{n}{d}
windows:
[v_{1+jd},\ldots,v_{(j+1)d}]
,

. The map
\sigma_{i}
sends the first window to
[v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1},v_{i+1},w_{1},v_{i+2},\ldots,v_{d}]
, where

\displaystyle w_{1}=\frac{\det(v_{i},v_{i+2},\ldots,v_{i+k})}{\det(v_{i+1},v_{i+2},\ldots,v_{i+k})}v_{i+1}-v_{i}.

The
\ell
th window is defined by the same recipe by
d
-periodically augmenting indices. The map
\sigma_{i}^{-1}
us defined similarly. In Theorem 5.3 of
[
Fra17
]
, Fraser proved that the maps
\sigma_{i}
,
\sigma_{i}^{-1}
are inverse regular automorphisms on
(V^{n})^{\circ}
. The pullbacks
\sigma_{i}^{*}
,
(\sigma_{i}^{-1})^{*}
are inverse quasi-automorphisms on
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
(our notation

in this paper has the same meaning as

in
[
Fra17
]
. It is the affine cone over the Grassmannian of

-dimensional subspaces in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. With slight abuse of notation, we write the Zariski-open subset

The maps

We now define a map
\sigma_{i}:{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]\to{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]

It is shown in Theorem 5.3 of

and Theorem 7.6 in
[

, the exchange graph of the quantum Grassmannian cluster algebra

. Therefore there is one to one correspondence between cluster monomials in
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
and
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
. Thus every cluster monomial

in

has a unique image

.

We will prove in Lemma

that the map
\sigma_{i}
preserve the quasi-commutative relations between quantum Plücker coordinates which are in the same cluster, and prove in Lemma

\sigma_{i}
preserves exchange relations. Up to Conjecture

is well-defined.

We define

to

\sigma_{i}^{-1}

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
i\in[d-1]
and let
x_{1},x_{2}
be any pair of cluster variables (including frozen variables) in a cluster. Then for
\widetilde{x}_{1}\in\{x_{1},x_{1}^{-1}\}
,
\widetilde{x}_{2}\in\{x_{2},x_{2}^{-1}\}
, we have that
\widetilde{x}_{1}\widetilde{x}_{2}=q^{c}\widetilde{x}_{2}\widetilde{x}_{1}
for some
c
implies that
\sigma_{i}(\widetilde{x}_{1})\sigma_{i}(\widetilde{x}_{2})=q^{c}\sigma_{i}(\widetilde{x}_{2})\sigma_{i}(\widetilde{x}_{1})
, and
\sigma_{i}^{-1}(\widetilde{x}_{1})\sigma_{i}^{-1}(\widetilde{x}_{2})=q^{c}\sigma_{i}^{-1}(\widetilde{x}_{2})\sigma_{i}^{-1}(\widetilde{x}_{1})
.

Proof.

We will prove the statement for
\sigma_{i}
. The proof of the result for
\sigma_{i}^{-1}
is the similar.

Fraser
[
Fra17
]
has proved that in non-quantum case,
\sigma_{i}
sends a cluster to a cluster up to frozen variables. Therefore by the definition of
\sigma_{i}
in quantum case in the beginning of this subsection, we have that for two cluster variables
x_{1},x_{2}
in the same cluster,
\sigma_{i}(x_{1})
,
\sigma_{i}(x_{2})
are cluster variables (possibly multiply by Laurent monomials of frozen variables).

It suffices to prove the result for
x_{1},x_{2}
in one particular cluster. Indeed, suppose that the result is true for the cluster
{\bf{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
. Let
x_{r}^{\prime}
be the quantum cluster variable obtained by mutating
x_{r}
and let
{\bf{x}}^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m})
. Then
x_{r}^{\prime}=[x_{r}^{-1}x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{t}}]+[x_{r}^{-1}x_{e_{1}}\cdots x_{e_{s}}]
, where

s,t,j_{l},e_{l}
are some integers. Therefore
x_{r}^{\prime}=q^{a}x_{r}^{-1}x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{t}}+q^{b}x_{r}^{-1}x_{e_{1}}\cdots x_{e_{s}}
for some
a,b
. Suppose that
x_{f}x_{r}^{\prime}=q^{c}x_{r}^{\prime}x_{f}
for some
c
and some
x_{f}\in{\bf{x}}
. We want to show that
\sigma_{i}(x_{f})\sigma_{i}(x_{r}^{\prime})=q^{c}\sigma_{i}(x_{r}^{\prime})\sigma_{i}(x_{f})
. We have

\displaystyle x_{f}x_{r}^{\prime}=x_{f}(q^{a}x_{r}^{-1}x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{t}}+q^{b}x_{r}^{-1}x_{e_{1}}\cdots x_{e_{s}}).

Since
x_{f},x_{r},x_{j_{l}},x_{e_{l}}
are in the same cluster, we have

\displaystyle x_{f}x_{r}^{\prime}=(q^{a+d}x_{r}^{-1}x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{t}}+q^{b+d^{\prime}}x_{r}^{-1}x_{e_{1}}\cdots x_{e_{s}})x_{f},

for some
d,d^{\prime}
. Since we have assumed that
x_{f}x_{r}^{\prime}=q^{c}x_{r}^{\prime}x_{f}
, we have
d=d^{\prime}=c
. Since we have assumed that the result is true for the cluster
{\bf{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
, we have that

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}(x_{f})\sigma_{i}(x_{r}^{\prime})=\sigma_{i}(x_{f})\sigma_{i}(q^{a}x_{r}^{-1}x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{t}}+q^{b}x_{r}^{-1}x_{e_{1}}\cdots x_{e_{s}})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i}(x_{f})(q^{a}\sigma_{i}(x_{r}^{-1})\sigma_{i}(x_{j_{1}})\cdots\sigma_{i}(x_{j_{t}})+q^{b}\sigma_{i}(x_{r}^{-1})\sigma_{i}(x_{e_{1}})\cdots\sigma_{i}(x_{e_{s}}))

\displaystyle=(q^{a+c}\sigma_{i}(x_{r}^{-1})\sigma_{i}(x_{j_{1}})\cdots\sigma_{i}(x_{j_{t}})+q^{b+c}\sigma_{i}(x_{r}^{-1})\sigma_{i}(x_{e_{1}})\cdots\sigma_{i}(x_{e_{s}}))\sigma_{i}(x_{f}).

Therefore
\sigma_{i}(x_{f})\sigma_{i}(x_{r}^{\prime})=q^{c}\sigma_{i}(x_{r}^{\prime})\sigma_{i}(x_{f})
. It follows that
x_{f}^{\pm 1}{x_{r}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1}=q^{c}{x_{r}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1}x_{f}^{\pm 1}
for some
c
implies that
\sigma_{i}(x_{f}^{\pm 1})\sigma_{i}({x_{r}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1})=q^{c}\sigma_{i}({x_{r}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1})\sigma_{i}(x_{f}^{\pm 1})
.

We now prove the following: for cluster variables (including frozen variables)
\Delta^{J}_{q}
,
\Delta^{K}_{q}
in
{\bf{x}}(i)
(see Equation (
3.4
)), we have that
\Delta^{J}_{q}\Delta^{K}_{q}=q^{\lambda_{J,K}}\Delta^{K}_{q}\Delta^{J}_{q}
implies that
\sigma_{i}(\Delta^{J}_{q})\sigma_{i}(\Delta^{K}_{q})=q^{\lambda_{J,K}}\sigma_{i}(\Delta^{K}_{q})\sigma_{i}(\Delta^{J}_{q})
. From this, it follows that
(\Delta^{J}_{q})^{\pm 1}(\Delta^{K}_{q})^{\pm 1}=q^{\lambda_{J,K}}(\Delta^{K}_{q})^{\pm 1}(\Delta^{J}_{q})^{\pm 1}
implies that
\sigma_{i}((\Delta^{J}_{q})^{\pm 1})\sigma_{i}((\Delta^{K}_{q})^{\pm 1})=q^{\lambda_{J,K}}\sigma_{i}((\Delta^{K}_{q})^{\pm 1})\sigma_{i}((\Delta^{J}_{q})^{\pm 1})
.

The proof is straightforward by checking all cases in (
3.5
). Note that the cluster
{\bf{x}}(i)
is a very special cluster.

Case 1

,

is not changed, and

is changed to

,

for some

\displaystyle\Delta^{J}_{q}(\Delta^{K}_{q})^{-1}=q^{-\lambda_{J,K}}(\Delta^{K}_{q})^{-1}\Delta^{J}_{q},\quad\Delta^{J}_{q}\Delta^{K^{\prime}}_{q}=q^{\lambda_{J,K}-1}\Delta^{K^{\prime}}_{q}\Delta^{J}_{q},

\displaystyle\Delta^{J}_{q}[\Delta^{K^{\prime}}_{q}(\Delta^{K}_{q})^{-1}\Delta^{K^{\prime\prime}}_{q}]=q^{\lambda_{J,K}}[\Delta^{K^{\prime}}_{q}(\Delta^{K}_{q})^{-1}\Delta^{K^{\prime\prime}}_{q}]\Delta^{J}_{q}.

\sigma_{2}(\Delta_{q}^{123})\sigma_{2}(\Delta_{q}^{345})=q^{2}\sigma_{2}(\Delta_{q}^{345})\sigma_{2}(\Delta_{q}^{123})
.

for some

\displaystyle(\Delta^{J}_{q})^{-1}[\Delta^{K^{\prime}}_{q}(\Delta^{K}_{q})^{-1}\Delta^{K^{\prime\prime}}_{q}]=q^{-\lambda_{J,K}}[\Delta^{K^{\prime}}_{q}(\Delta^{K}_{q})^{-1}\Delta^{K^{\prime\prime}}_{q}](\Delta^{J}_{q})^{-1}.

\displaystyle[\Delta^{J^{\prime}}_{q}(\Delta^{J}_{q})^{-1}\Delta^{J^{\prime\prime}}_{q}][\Delta^{K^{\prime}}_{q}(\Delta^{K}_{q})^{-1}\Delta^{K^{\prime\prime}}_{q}]=q^{\lambda_{J,K}}[\Delta^{K^{\prime}}_{q}(\Delta^{K}_{q})^{-1}\Delta^{K^{\prime\prime}}_{q}][\Delta^{J^{\prime}}_{q}(\Delta^{J}_{q})^{-1}\Delta^{J^{\prime\prime}}_{q}].

is a frozen variable and

is a cluster variable. Here we only write the case that

Recall that
\overline{\sigma}_{i}
is the product
(i,i+1)(d+i,d+i+1)\cdots(n-d+i,n-d+i+1)
of simple reflections. After applying
\sigma_{i}
, we have either

,
\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K_{1})\prec J-\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K)\prec\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K_{2})
or
J-\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K)=J_{1}\sqcup J_{2}
,
J_{1}\prec\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K)-J\prec J_{2}
. Since
|\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K_{j})|=|K_{j}|
,
j=1,2
, we have
|\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K_{1})|-|\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K_{2})|=|K_{1}|-|K_{2}|
and hence the quasi-commutative relation does not change.

For example, in
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(3,6)]
we have that
\Delta_{q}^{123}\Delta_{q}^{146}=q^{2}\Delta_{q}^{146}\Delta_{q}^{123}
. We have that

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{123})=\Delta_{q}^{123},\quad\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{146})=\Delta_{q}^{256},

and
\Delta_{q}^{123}\Delta_{q}^{256}=q^{2}\Delta_{q}^{256}\Delta_{q}^{123}
. Therefore
\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{123})\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{256})=q^{2}\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{256})\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{123})
.

Case 3
. Both of
\Delta^{J}_{q}
and
\Delta^{K}_{q}
are cluster variables. In this case,
\Delta^{J}_{q}
is sent to
\Delta^{\overline{\sigma}_{i}(J)}_{q}
and
\Delta^{K}_{q}
is sent to
\Delta^{\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K)}_{q}
. This case is very similar to Case 2.

By Theorem
3.2
, we either have
\lambda_{J,K}=|K_{1}|-|K_{2}|
, where
K-J=K_{1}\sqcup K_{2}
,
K_{1}\prec J-K\prec K_{2}
, or
\lambda_{J,K}=|J_{2}|-|J_{1}|
, where
J-K=J_{1}\sqcup J_{2}
,
J_{1}\prec K-J\prec J_{2}
.

After applying
\sigma_{i}
, we have either
\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K)-\overline{\sigma}_{i}(J)=\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K_{1})\sqcup\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K_{2})
,
\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K_{1})\prec\overline{\sigma}_{i}(J)-\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K)\prec\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K_{2})
or
\overline{\sigma}_{i}(J)-\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K)=\overline{\sigma}_{i}(J_{1})\sqcup\overline{\sigma}_{i}(J_{2})
,
\overline{\sigma}_{i}(J_{1})\prec\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K)-\overline{\sigma}_{i}(J)\prec\overline{\sigma}_{i}(J_{2})
. Since
|\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K_{j})|=|K_{j}|
and
|\overline{\sigma}_{i}(J_{j})|=|J_{j}|
,
j=1,2
, we have
|\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K_{1})|-|\overline{\sigma}_{i}(K_{2})|=|K_{1}|-|K_{2}|
and
|\overline{\sigma}_{i}(J_{2})|-|\overline{\sigma}_{i}(J_{1})|=|J_{2}|-|J_{1}|
, and hence the quasi-commutative relation does not change.

For example, in
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(3,6)]
we have that
\Delta_{q}^{124}\Delta_{q}^{134}=q\Delta_{q}^{134}\Delta_{q}^{124}
. We have that

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{124})=\Delta_{q}^{125},\quad\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{134})=\Delta_{q}^{235},

and

\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{125})\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{235})=q\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{235})\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{125})
.

\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{14})\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{23})=\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{23})\sigma_{1}(\Delta_{q}^{14})

4.
Proof of Theorem
3.15

In this section, we prove our main result Theorem
3.15
.

4.1.
Proof of (i) of Theorem
3.15

By Lemma
3.14
, the maps
\sigma_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}^{-1}
are algebra automorphisms of the quantum cluster algebra
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
.

By the definition of
\sigma_{i}
, the matrix associated to
\sigma_{i}
is
R=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n\times n}&0\\
0&L\\
\end{array}\right)
, where
L=(l_{jk})
is an
n\times n
matrix with entries

(4.1)

l_{jk}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $j=l\not\equiv i+1,j+1=l\equiv i+1,~or~j-1=l\equiv i+1,$}\\
-1,&\text{if $j=l\equiv i+1,$}\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

It is not hard to see that
L^{-1}=L
and
R^{-1}=R
. Here we place the frozen variables, i.e., consecutive quantum Plücker coordinates, in the order of
[1,k],[2,k+1],\ldots,[n-k+1,n],[n-k+2,n+1],\ldots,[n,n+k-1]
, where the addition is taken in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

It is clear that by the definition, the maps
\sigma_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}^{-1}
satisfies the condition
(2)
in Proposition
2.8
. On the other hand, the conditions
(1)
and
(3)
in Proposition
2.8
follow from
[
Fra17
, Lemma 5.11]
. So to prove Theorem
3.15
(i) we only need to check the condition (4) in Proposition
2.8
, that is,
R^{t}\overline{\Lambda}R=\Lambda
. This follows from
Lemma
3.6
.

4.2.
Proof of (ii) of Theorem
3.15

Recall that in Section
3.3
, we defined a map
\varphi
sending a cluster Laurent monomial in
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
to a quantum cluster Laurent monomial in
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
. Every quantum cluster variable in
{\mathbb{C}}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
is of the form
\varphi(x)
, where
x
is a cluster variable in
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
. By Lemma
3.8
, we have that for every
i
and every cluster Laurent monomial
x
(
x
can contain frozen variables or inverses of frozen variables) in
{\mathbb{C}}[\Gr(k,n)]
,
\sigma_{i}(\varphi(x))=\varphi(\sigma_{i}(x))
. In particular, for any Plücker coordinate
P
, we have that
\sigma_{i}(\varphi(P))=\varphi(\sigma_{i}(P))
. It is shown in
[
Fra17
]
that for
|i-j|=1
,
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}(P)=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}(P)
. Therefore

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}(\varphi(P))=\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}(\varphi(\sigma_{i}(P)))=\varphi(\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}(P))=\varphi(\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}(P))=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}(\varphi(P)).

To show that
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}
on
\mathbb{C}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
, it suffices to shown that
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}(x_{1}\cdots x_{r})=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}(x_{1}\cdots x_{r})
, where
x_{i}
’s are quantum Plücker coordinates. This follows from the fact that
\sigma_{i}
,
\sigma_{j}
are algebra automorphisms on
\mathbb{C}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
and
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}(x_{i})=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}(x_{i})
for quantum Plücker coordinates
x_{i}
.

The proof of
\sigma_{i}(\sigma_{j}(x))=\sigma_{j}(\sigma_{i}(x))
for every
x\in\mathbb{C}_{q}[\Gr(k,n)]
,
|i-j|>1
, is similar.

Global well-posedness of slightly supercritical SQG equations and gradient estimate
Hyungjun Choi

Abstract
. We prove the global regularity of smooth solutions for a dissipative surface quasi-geostrophic equation with both velocity and dissipation logarithmically supercritical compared to the critical equation. By this, we mean that a symbol defined as a power of logarithm is added to both velocity and dissipation terms to penalize the equation’s criticality. Our primary tool is the nonlinear maximum principle which provides transparent proofs of global regularity for nonlinear dissipative equations. Combining the nonlinear maximum principle with a modulus of continuity, we prove a uniform-in-time gradient estimate for the critical and slightly supercritical surface quasi-geostrophic equation. It improves the previous double exponential bound by Kiselev-Nazarov-Volberg to the single exponential. In addition, we prove eventual exponential decay of the solutions.

Introduction

The global well-posedness of the dissipative surface quasi-geostrophic (SQG) equation (
1.1
) has been widely studied in recent literature.

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}\theta+(u\cdot\nabla)\theta+\Lambda^{\alpha}\theta&=0,\\
u&=\nabla^{\perp}\Lambda^{-1}\theta,\end{array}\right.

where
\Lambda=(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. The SQG equation first appeared in the mathematical literature in
[
3
]
. Due to the positivity of the operator
\Lambda^{\alpha}
, it can be easily seen that the
L^{p}
norms of
\theta
are nonincreasing in time under smooth evolution. Indeed, the equation has
L^{\infty}
-maximum principle
[
9
]
. The situation differs by whether the order of the diffusion term is higher/identical/lower than the nonlinear transport term under
L^{\infty}
-rescaling of a solution. The case
\alpha=1
is termed “critical” since the order is identical. The weak formulation and the global well-posedness for the subcritical case (
\alpha>1
) were understood in the usual sense
[
4
,
23
]
. The global well-posedness in the critical case was first proved in
[
2
]
(in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) and
[
17
,
18
]
(in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
). In particular, Kiselev-Nazarov-Volberg
[
17
]
analyzed a breakthrough scenario of a modulus of continuity, which they called the nonlocal maximum principle and is studied further in
[
19
]
. Shortly after that, Constantin-Vicol
[
8
]
presented another proof. They introduced so-called nonlinear maximum principle, which can be applied to well-posedness proofs for various active scalar equations.

In the supercritical case (
\alpha<1
), the global well-posedness for generic initial remains an open problem. There have been several results regarding conditional regularity
[
6
,
14
]
(a Hölder continuous solution is smooth) and eventual regularization of solutions
[
24
,
10
,
19
]
. The global regularity has been studied if velocity or dissipation is slightly supercritical. In particular, the slightly supercritical velocity was studied in
[
11
]
, where the veloctiy is obtained from
\theta
by a Fourier multiplier with symbol
i\zeta^{\perp}\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert^{-1}m(\zeta)
. Here,
m(\zeta)
grows slower than
\log\log\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert
as
\zeta\to\infty
. Moreover, the slightly supercritical dissipation for several active scalar equations was studied in
[
12
]
, where the dissipation term is given by multiplier with behavior
P(\zeta)\sim\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert(\log\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert)^{-\alpha}
(
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
) for large
\zeta
. These proofs were based on the nonlocal maximum principle introduced in
[
17
,
19
]
.

In this paper, we consider the SQG equation which is slightly supercritical in both velocity and dissipation:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}\theta+(u\cdot\nabla)\theta+\mathcal{L}\theta&=0,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\,t>0,\\
u&=\nabla^{\perp}\Lambda^{-1}m(\Lambda)\theta,\\
\theta(t=0)&=\theta_{0}.\end{array}\right.

The nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}
is defined by a convolutional kernel as follows:

\mathcal{L}\theta(x)=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\theta(x)-\theta(y))\frac{k(x-y)}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}\,dy.

Note that
\mathcal{L}=\Lambda
when
k(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi\left\lvert x\right\rvert}
. The main example we have in mind is

(1.3)

k(r)=\frac{1}{r(\log(10+r^{-1}))^{\alpha_{1}}},\quad m(\zeta)=(\log(10+\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert))^{\alpha_{2}},

for some
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\geq 0
. Throughout the paper, we assume on
k
and
m
as follows:

•

The kernel
k(x)=k(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)
is radially symmetric, nonnegative, and nonincreasing. In addition,
r^{2}k(r)
is nondecreasing for
r>0
.

•

(Slightly supercitical dissipation) For any
\epsilon>0
,

r^{1-\epsilon}k(r)

is nonincreasing for
0<r<r_{\epsilon}
and tends to
\infty
as
r\to 0
.

•

There exists a constant
c\geq 1
such that

k\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)\leq c\,k(r),\quad r>0.

•

The Fourier multiplier
m(\zeta)=m(\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert)\geq 1
is radially symmetric, nondecreasing, and satisfies the Hörmander-Mikhlin condition:

\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert^{k}|\nabla^{k}m(\zeta)|\leq C_{k}m(\zeta),\quad\zeta\neq 0,

for some constant
C_{k}>0
.

•

(Slightly supercritical velocity) For any
\epsilon>0
,

\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert^{\epsilon}m(\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert^{-1})

is nondecreasing in
0<\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert<r_{\epsilon}
and tends to
0
as
\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert\to 0
.

Especially, we call (
1.2
) slightly supercritical if
k
and
m
satisfy the slightly supercritical-ness conditions (the second and last assumptions above).

We prove the global regularity for a solution of (
1.2
) with help of the nonlinear maximum principle by Constantin-Vicol
[
8
]
. We show a conditional regularity result in the first step, then we prove that required regularity is conserved by evolution in the second step. The main difference between our proof and
[
8
]
is that we provide a modulus of continuity while
[
8
]
argues only with uniform continuity (the only small shock condition). We say a function
f
admits a modulus of continuity
\omega
if
|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\omega(\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert)
for all
x\neq y
. A modulus of continuity function
\omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
should be non-decreasing,
\omega(0)=0
, and continuous at
0
. An explicit modulus of continuity for solution allows us to construct an exponential gradient estimate; Main Theorem 2.

In section
2
, we list some estimates regarding the relationship between the kernel and the Fourier multiplier. We provide suitable conditions for

and

to behave similarly as the main example (

Preliminaries

3.
Conditional regularity

In this section, we prove that some modulus of continuity for a solution of (
1.2
) implies global regularity.

Theorem 8
.

Suppose
\theta
is a bounded weak solution, i.e.,
\theta\in L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{p}\cap L^{\infty})
where
1\leq p<\infty
, of the slightly supercritical SQG equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}\theta+(u\cdot\nabla)\theta+\mathcal{L}\theta&=0,\\
u&=\nabla^{\perp}\Lambda^{-1}m(\Lambda)\theta,\\
\theta(t=0)&=\theta_{0}.\end{array}\right.

with
k
and
m
satisfying the prescribed conditions. Assume that
\Omega
satisfies (
1.6
) with
\omega
replaced with
\Omega
. Also assume that
k
,
m
, and
\Omega
satisfy

\lim_{r\to 0+}\frac{m(r^{-1})\Omega(r)}{rk(r)}=0.

If
\theta
admits a modulus of continuity
\Omega
for
[0,T]
, then there is a uniform-in-time estimate for
\lVert\nabla\theta(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
and
\theta
is a smooth solution on
(0,T]
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the assertion for a smooth solution
\theta
. One may consider regularized equation with the term
-\epsilon\Delta\theta
, get a uniform estimate independent of
\epsilon
, and then take the inviscid limit
\epsilon\to 0+
to get the desired result. Due to Proposition
4
, it suffices to show that
\lVert\nabla\theta(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
stays uniformly bounded in time.

Step 1
. Evolution of
\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}

We take gradient on both sides of the equation to get:

\partial_{t}\nabla\theta+u\cdot\nabla^{2}\theta+\nabla u\cdot\nabla\theta+\mathcal{L}\nabla\theta=0.

Multiply
\nabla\theta
to both sides:

\nabla\theta\cdot\partial_{t}\nabla\theta+\nabla\theta\cdot u\cdot\nabla^{2}\theta+\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla u\cdot\nabla\theta+\nabla\theta\cdot\mathcal{L}\nabla\theta=0.

Considering singular integral formulation for the nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}
gives:

(3.1)

\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\mathcal{L})\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)-\nabla\theta(y,t)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}k(x-y)\,dy}_{=:D(x,t)}=-\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla u\cdot\nabla\theta.

Due to the maximum principle for
\mathcal{L}
, it suffices to show that

(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\mathcal{L})\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}(x,t)<0

whenever
\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert
is sufficiently large in terms of
\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}},k,m
, and
\Omega
.

Step 2
. Pointwise lower bound of
D(x,t)

A smooth function
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a non-decreasing cutoff function such that

\varphi(x)=0\text{ on }x\leq\frac{1}{2},\quad\varphi(x)=1\text{ on }x\geq 1,\quad 0\leq\varphi^{\prime}\leq 4.

For some sufficiently small
R=R(x,t)>0
which will be determined later, we have the following estimate on
D(x,t)
:

\displaystyle\frac{D(x,t)}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)-\nabla\theta(y,t)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}k(x-y)\varphi\Big(\frac{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}{R}\Big)\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq R}\frac{k(x-y)}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}\,dy-2\nabla\theta(x,t)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla\theta(y,t)\frac{k(x-y)}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}\varphi\Big(\frac{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}{R}\Big)\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}\int_{R}^{\infty}\frac{k(\rho)}{\rho}\,d\rho-2\nabla\theta(x,t)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\theta(y,t)-\theta(x,t))\left\{\nabla_{v}\left(\frac{k(\left\lvert v\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{2}}\varphi\Big(\frac{\left\lvert v\right\rvert}{R}\Big)\right)\right\}_{v=x-y}\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}k(R)-2\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\Omega(\rho)\left\lvert\frac{d}{d\rho}\Big(\frac{k(\rho)\varphi(\rho/R)}{\rho^{2}}\Big)\right\rvert\,d\rho.

By Lemma
7
(ii) and (iii),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\Omega(\rho)\left\lvert\frac{d}{d\rho}\Big(\frac{k(\rho)\varphi(\rho/R)}{\rho^{2}}\Big)\right\rvert\,d\rho

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{4}{R}\int_{R/2}^{R}\frac{\Omega(\rho)k(\rho)}{\rho}\,d\rho+\int_{R/2}^{\infty}\Omega(\rho)\left(-\frac{k^{\prime}(\rho)}{\rho}+\frac{2k(\rho)}{\rho^{2}}\right)\,d\rho

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{c_{1}}{8}\frac{\Omega(R)k(R)}{R}.

Therefore,

\frac{D(x,t)}{2\pi}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}k(R)-\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert\frac{c_{1}\Omega(R)k(R)}{4R}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}k(R)\left(1-\frac{c_{1}}{2\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert}\frac{\Omega(R)}{R}\right).

Set
R=R(x,t)>0
to satisfy

(3.2)

\frac{\Omega(R)}{R}=\frac{\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert}{c_{1}}.

Then

(3.3)

D(x,t)\geq 4c_{2}\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}k(R).

Note that
R(x,t)
is arbitrarily small when
\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert
is large enough.

Step 3
. Estimate of
\nabla u

\nabla u(x,t)=\nabla^{\perp}\Lambda^{-1}m(\Lambda)\nabla\theta(x,t)=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K(x-y)(\nabla\theta(x,t)-\nabla\theta(y,t))\,dy.

We estimate
\nabla u
by splitting
\mathbb{R}^{2}
into two pieces; an inner piece
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq r
and an outer piece
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert>r
for some
r=r(x,t)>0
.

For the inner piece, we use the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla u_{\text{in}}(x,t)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\bigg(\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq r}\frac{\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)-\nabla\theta(y,t)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}k(x-y)\,dy\bigg)\bigg(\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq r}K(x-y)^{2}\frac{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}{k(x-y)}\,dy\bigg)}

(Lemma
5
and
7
(iv))

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{D(x,t)\int_{0}^{r}\frac{m(\rho^{-1})^{2}}{\rho k(\rho)}\,d\rho}\leq c_{3}\sqrt{D(x,t)\frac{m(r^{-1})^{2}}{k(r)}}.

For the outer piece, we apply integration by parts and use the modulus of continuity of
\theta
:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla u_{\text{out}}(x,t)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert>r}\big(\theta(x,t)-\theta(y,t)\big)\nabla K(x-y)\,dy\right\rvert

\displaystyle\quad+\left\lvert\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert=r}\big(\theta(x,t)-\theta(y,t)\big)K(x-y)\nu(y)\,d\sigma(y)\right\rvert

(Lemma
5
)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{\Omega(\rho)m(\rho^{-1})}{\rho^{2}}\,d\rho+C\frac{\Omega(r)m(r^{-1})}{r}

(Lemma
7
(v))

\displaystyle\leq c_{4}\frac{\Omega(r)m(r^{-1})}{r}.

Therefore,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}&\leq\left\lvert\nabla u_{\text{in}}\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert\nabla u_{\text{out}}\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{D}{4}+c_{3}^{2}\frac{m(r^{-1})^{2}}{k(r)}\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{4}+c_{4}\frac{\Omega(r)m(r^{-1})}{r}\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}.\end{aligned}

Step 4
. Maximum principle

Combining (
3.1
), (
3.3
), and (
3.4
),

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\mathcal{L})\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{D}{2}+2c_{2}k(R)\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\mathcal{L})\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}+D

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,2\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}\leq\frac{D}{2}+2c_{3}^{2}\frac{m(r^{-1})^{2}}{k(r)}\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{4}+2c_{4}\frac{\Omega(r)m(r^{-1})}{r}\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}.

Hence

(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\mathcal{L})\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}+2c_{2}k(R)\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}\leq 2c_{3}^{2}\frac{m(r^{-1})^{2}}{k(r)}\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{4}+2c_{4}\frac{\Omega(r)m(r^{-1})}{r}\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}.

What we want to show are

(3.5)

2c_{3}^{2}\frac{m(r^{-1})^{2}}{k(r)}\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}<c_{2}k(R),

and

(3.6)

2c_{4}\frac{\Omega(r)m(r^{-1})}{r}<c_{2}k(R),

whenever
\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert
is sufficiently large. Recall (
3.2
) that

\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert=c_{1}\frac{\Omega(R)}{R}.

Hence (
3.5
) is equivalent to

\frac{m(r^{-1})^{2}\Omega(R)^{2}}{R^{2}k(r)k(R)}<\frac{c_{2}}{2c_{1}^{2}c_{3}^{2}}.

And (
3.6
) is equivalent to

\frac{m(r^{-1})\Omega(r)}{rk(R)}<\frac{c_{2}}{2c_{4}}.

Set
r=R=R(x,t)>0
and then the above two inequalities hold for sufficiently small
R
, since we assumed

\lim_{r\to 0+}\frac{m(r^{-1})\Omega(r)}{rk(r)}=0.\qed

4.
Conservation of modulus of continuity

In this section, we prove that some modulus of continuity of
\theta(\cdot,t)
is conserved by (
1.2
).

Theorem 9
.

Suppose
\theta
is a bounded weak solution of the slightly supercritical SQG equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}\theta+(u\cdot\nabla)\theta+\mathcal{L}\theta&=0,\\
u&=\nabla^{\perp}\Lambda^{-1}m(\Lambda)\theta,\\
\theta(t=0)&=\theta_{0}.\end{array}\right.

with
k
and
m
satisfying the prescribed conditions. Suppose a
C^{1}
-function
\omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
\omega(0)=0
is nondecreasing, concave, and satisfies (
1.6
) and

\lim_{r\to 0+}\frac{m(r^{-1})}{k(r)}\frac{\omega^{\prime}(r)}{\omega(r)}=0.

Then there exists a constant
M>0
depending on
\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
,
k
,
m
, and
\omega
such that if
\theta_{0}\in L^{p}\cap L^{\infty}
satisfies the modulus of continuity
\frac{M}{2}\omega
, then
\theta
satisfies the modulus of continuity
M\omega
as long as the solution is defined.

Proof.

For notational convenience, define
\delta_{h}f(x)=f(x+h)-f(x)
for a function
f
. We consider the maximum principle for

v(x,h;t):=\left(\frac{\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)}{\omega(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)}\right)^{2}F(h).

The function
F(h)=\exp(-G(|h|))
is just for the decay of
v
in
h
, so that
v\in L^{p}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
for some
1\leq p<\infty
. We set
G
being smooth, nonnegative, nondecreasing, and
G(r)=0
for
0\leq r\leq 1
.

Step 1
. Evolution of
v

Since

\partial_{t}\theta(x)+u(x)\cdot\nabla\theta(x)+\mathcal{L}\theta(x)=0,

and

\partial_{t}\theta(x+h)+u(x+h)\cdot\nabla\theta(x+h)+\mathcal{L}\theta(x+h)=0.

Subtracting two formulas gives:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\theta(x+h)-\theta(x))

\displaystyle\qquad+u(x)\cdot\nabla(\theta(x+h)-\theta(x))+(u(x+h)-u(x))\cdot\nabla\theta(x+h)+(\mathcal{L}\theta(x+h)-\mathcal{L}\theta(x))=0,

and thus the evolution of
\delta_{h}\theta
is given by

\big(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla_{x}+(\delta_{h}u)\cdot\nabla_{h}+\mathcal{L}_{x}\big)\delta_{h}\theta=0.

Multiply
\delta_{h}\theta
to both sides:

\frac{1}{2}\big(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla_{x}+(\delta_{h}u)\cdot\nabla_{h}+\mathcal{L}_{x}\big)(\delta_{h}\theta)^{2}(x,h,t)+\frac{1}{2}\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)-\delta_{h}\theta(y,t)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}k(x-y)\,dy}_{=:D_{h}(x,t)}=0.

Therefore

(4.1)

\big(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla_{x}+(\delta_{h}u)\cdot\nabla_{h}+\mathcal{L}_{x}\big)v(x,h,t)+D_{h}(x,t)\frac{F(h)}{\omega(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)^{2}}=-(\delta_{h}u)\cdot\frac{h}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert}\left(G^{\prime}(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)+\frac{2\omega^{\prime}(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)}{\omega(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)}\right)v.

Step 2
. Breakthrough moment and maximum principle

Fix
M>0
, which will be determined later. Suppose
\theta_{0}
satisfies a modulus of continuity
\frac{M}{2}\omega
and
\theta(t)
admits a modulus of continuity
M\omega
for
0\leq t\leq t^{\ast}
. However, at the time
t=t^{\ast}
,

(4.2)

\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t^{\ast})\right\rvert\geq M\omega(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)

for some
x,h
. Suppose we prove that

(4.3)

(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla_{x}+(\delta_{h}u)\cdot\nabla_{h}+\mathcal{L}_{x})v(x,h,t^{\ast})<0,

whenever the such event occurs, then we get contraction from the maximum principle of
\mathcal{L}_{x}
. Hence such an event is impossible, and the modulus of continuity
M\omega(\cdot)
is conserved.

Therefore it suffices to show that

(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla_{x}+(\delta_{h}u)\cdot\nabla_{h}+\mathcal{L}_{x})v(x,h,t^{*})<0,

whenever

M\omega(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)\leq\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t^{*})\right\rvert\leq 2M\omega(\left\lvert h\right\rvert).

If (
4.2
) is satisfed,

M\omega(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)\leq\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t^{*})\right\rvert\leq 2\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

Hence, we only need to consider
h
with
M\omega(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)\leq 2\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
. Let
r_{0}>0
be
M\omega(r_{0})=2\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
. We choose
M
large so that
r_{0}<1
and thus
F(h)=1
,
G^{\prime}(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)=0
for
\left\lvert h\right\rvert\leq r_{0}
.
Due to (
4.1
), it suffices to show that if
M
is large enough, then

(4.4)

D_{h}(x,t^{*})\geq 2\frac{\omega^{\prime}(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)}{\omega(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)}\left\lvert\delta_{h}u(x,t^{*})\right\rvert\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t^{*})\right\rvert^{2},

whenever
M\omega(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)\leq\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t^{*})\right\rvert\leq 2M\omega(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)
. Let us denote
t^{*}
by simply
t
from here.

Step 3
. Pointwise lower bound of
D_{h}

A smooth function
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a non-decreasing cutoff function such that

\varphi(r)=0\text{ on }r\leq\frac{1}{2},\quad\varphi(r)=1\text{ on }r\geq 1,\quad 0\leq\varphi^{\prime}\leq 4.

For some sufficiently small
R=R(x,h,t)\geq 6\left\lvert h\right\rvert
which will be determined later, we have the following estimate on
D_{h}(x,t)
:

\displaystyle\frac{D_{h}(x,t)}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)-\delta_{h}\theta(y,t)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}k(x-y)\varphi\Big(\frac{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}{R}\Big)\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq R}\frac{k(x-y)}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}\,dy-2\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\delta_{h}\theta(y,t)\frac{k(x-y)}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}}\varphi\Big(\frac{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}{R}\Big)\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}\int_{R}^{\infty}\frac{k(\rho)}{\rho}\,d\rho

\displaystyle\qquad-2\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\theta(y,t)-\theta(x,t))\bigg\{\delta_{h}\left(\frac{k(\left\lvert v\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{2}}\varphi\Big(\frac{\left\lvert v\right\rvert}{R}\Big)\right)\bigg\}_{v=x-y}\,dy.

By the mean value theorem,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\delta_{h}\left(\frac{k(\left\lvert v\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{2}}\varphi\Big(\frac{\left\lvert v\right\rvert}{R}\Big)\right)\right\rvert_{v=x-y}\leq\left\lvert h\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla_{v}\left(\frac{k(\left\lvert v\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{2}}\varphi\Big(\frac{\left\lvert v\right\rvert}{R}\Big)\right)\right\rvert_{v=x-y+h^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\left\lvert h\right\rvert\left\{\frac{4}{R}\frac{k(\rho^{\ast})}{(\rho^{\ast})^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{R\geq\rho^{\ast}\geq\frac{R}{2}}+\left(-\frac{k^{\prime}(\rho^{\ast})}{(\rho^{\ast})^{2}}+\frac{2k(\rho^{\ast})}{(\rho^{\ast})^{3}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\rho^{\ast}\geq\frac{R}{2}}\right\}_{\rho^{\ast}=\left\lvert x-y+h^{\ast}\right\rvert}

Since
\rho^{\ast}=\left\lvert x-y+h^{\ast}\right\rvert\geq\frac{R}{2}
and
\left\lvert h^{\ast}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert h\right\rvert\leq\frac{R}{6}
, it follows
\rho=\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq\frac{R}{3}
and
\rho^{\ast}\geq\rho-\frac{R}{6}\geq\frac{\rho}{2}
. Hence the above formula is less than equal to the same formula where
\rho^{\ast}
replaced by
\rho/2
,
R\geq\rho^{*}\geq\frac{R}{2}
replaced by
\frac{7R}{6}\geq\rho\geq\frac{R}{3}
, and
\rho^{\ast}\geq\frac{R}{2}
replaced by
\rho\geq\frac{R}{3}
.
In addition, regarding the modulus of continuity of
\theta(\cdot,t)
, we have

\left\lvert\theta(y,t)-\theta(x,t)\right\rvert\leq M\omega(\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert).

Therefore, wrapping up the estimates with Lemma
7
, we have the following estimate for
D_{h}
with a constant
c_{5}(>2)
.

\displaystyle\frac{D_{h}(x,t)}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}k(R)-2\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)\right\rvert\left\lvert h\right\rvert M\cdot c_{5}\frac{\omega(R)k(R)}{R}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}k(R)\left(1-\frac{4c_{5}\left\lvert h\right\rvert M}{\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)\right\rvert}\frac{\omega(R)}{R}\right).

Set
R=R(x,h,t)>0
to satisfy

\frac{\omega(R)}{R}=\frac{1}{8c_{5}M}\frac{\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)\right\rvert}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert}.

Since

\frac{\omega(R)}{R}=\frac{1}{8c_{5}M}\frac{\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)\right\rvert}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert}\leq\frac{1}{4c_{5}}\frac{\omega(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert}<\frac{\omega(6\left\lvert h\right\rvert)}{6\left\lvert h\right\rvert},

we get
R\geq 6\left\lvert h\right\rvert
as assumed. On the other hand,

\frac{\omega(R)}{R}=\frac{1}{8c_{5}M}\frac{\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)\right\rvert}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert}\geq\frac{1}{8c_{5}}\frac{\omega(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert},

and thus

8c_{5}\frac{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{1-\epsilon}}{R^{1-\epsilon}}\geq\frac{\omega(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{-\epsilon}}{\omega(R)R^{-\epsilon}}\geq 1.

Hence
R\leq(8c_{5})^{1/(1-\epsilon)}\left\lvert h\right\rvert
. Summing up,

(4.5)

D_{h}(x,t)\geq ck(R)\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}\geq 8c_{6}k(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)\left\lvert\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}.

Step 4
. Estimate of
\delta_{h}u

\delta_{h}u(x,t)=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K(x-y)(\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)-\delta_{h}\theta(y,t))\,dy.

We estimate
\nabla u
by splitting
\mathbb{R}^{2}
into two pieces; an inner piece
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq 3\left\lvert h\right\rvert
and an outer piece
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert>3\left\lvert h\right\rvert
.

For the inner piece, we use the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality:

(4.6)

\left\lvert\delta_{h}u_{\text{in}}(x,t)\right\rvert\leq c_{7}\sqrt{D_{h}(x,t)\frac{m(\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{-1})^{2}}{k(\left\lvert h\right\rvert)}}.

For the outer piece:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\delta_{h}u_{\text{out}}(x,t)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert>3\left\lvert h\right\rvert}K(x-y)(\theta(y,t)-\theta(y+h,t))\,dy\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert>3\left\lvert h\right\rvert}(K(x-y)-K(x-y+h))\theta(y,t)\,dy\right\rvert

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\lvert\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{y:\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert>3\left\lvert h\right\rvert\}\triangle\\
\{y:\left\lvert x-y+h\right\rvert>3\left\lvert h\right\rvert\}\end{subarray}}K(x-y+h)\theta(y,t)\,dy\right\rvert

The first term is estimated as follows:

(
\left\lvert h^{\ast}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert h\right\rvert<\frac{1}{3}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert
)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert>3\left\lvert h\right\rvert}\left\lvert h\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla K(x-y+h^{\ast})\right\rvert\,dy

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lvert h\right\rvert\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert>3\left\lvert h\right\rvert}\left\lvert\frac{2}{3}(x-y)\right\rvert^{-3}m\big(\left\lvert{\textstyle\frac{2}{3}}(x-y)\right\rvert^{-1}\big)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lvert h\right\rvert\int_{\left\lvert h\right\rvert}^{\infty}\frac{m\big(\rho^{-1}\big)}{\rho^{2}}\,d\rho\leq c_{3}\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}m\big(\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{-1}\big).

And the second term is estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{2\left\lvert h\right\rvert<\left\lvert x-y+h\right\rvert\leq 4\left\lvert h\right\rvert}\hskip-42.67912pt\left\lvert K(x-y+h)\right\rvert\,dy\leq C\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{2\left\lvert h\right\rvert}^{4\left\lvert h\right\rvert}\frac{m(\rho^{-1})}{\rho}\,d\rho\leq C\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}m(\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{-1}).

Therefore,

(4.7)

\left\lvert\delta_{h}u_{\text{out}}(x,t)\right\rvert\leq c_{8}\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}m(\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{-1}).

Step 5
. Proof of (
4.4
)

By the estimates (
4.5
), (
4.6
), and (
4.7
), it is enough to show that

(4.8)

gives:

(5.13)

\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda)\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\underbrace{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)-\nabla\theta(y,t)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{3}}\,dy}_{=:D(x,t)}=-\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla u\cdot\nabla\theta.

Our goal here is to prove

(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda)\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}(x,t)<0

whenever
\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert
is sufficiently large in terms of
M
.

Step 2
. Pointwise lower bound of
D(x,t)

A smooth function
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a non-decreasing cutoff function such that

\varphi(x)=0\text{ on }x\leq\frac{1}{2},\quad\varphi(x)=1\text{ on }x\geq 1,\quad 0\leq\varphi^{\prime}\leq 4.

For some sufficiently small
R=R(x,t)>0
which will be determined later, we have the following estimate on
D(x,t)
:

\displaystyle D(x,t)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)-\nabla\theta(y,t)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{3}}\varphi\Big(\frac{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}{R}\Big)\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\geq R}\frac{1}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{3}}\,dy-2\nabla\theta(x,t)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla\theta(y,t)\frac{1}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{3}}\varphi\Big(\frac{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}{R}\Big)\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}\int_{R}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\,d\rho-2\nabla\theta(x,t)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\theta(y,t)-\theta(x,t))\left\{\nabla_{v}\left(\frac{1}{\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{3}}\varphi\Big(\frac{\left\lvert v\right\rvert}{R}\Big)\right)\right\}_{v=x-y}\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}}{\rho}-2\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\Omega(\rho)\left\lvert\frac{d}{d\rho}\Big(\frac{\varphi(\rho/R)}{\rho^{3}}\Big)\right\rvert\,d\rho.

The second term:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\Omega(\rho)\left\lvert\frac{d}{d\rho}\Big(\frac{\varphi(\rho/R)}{\rho^{3}}\Big)\right\rvert\,d\rho\leq\frac{4}{R}\int_{R/2}^{R}\frac{\Omega(\rho)}{\rho^{2}}\,d\rho+2\int_{R/2}^{\infty}\frac{\Omega(\rho)}{\rho^{3}}\,d\rho\leq\frac{c_{1}}{4}\frac{\Omega(R)}{R^{2}}.

Therefore,

D(x,t)\geq\frac{\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}}{R}-\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert\frac{c_{1}\Omega(R)}{2R^{2}}=\frac{\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}}{R}\left(1-\frac{c_{1}}{2\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert}\frac{\Omega(R)}{R}\right).

Set
R=R(x,t)>0
to satisfy

(5.14)

\frac{\Omega(R)}{R}=\frac{\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert}{c_{1}}.

Then

(5.15)

D(x,t)\geq\frac{\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}}{2R}.

Note that
R(x,t)
is arbitrarily small when
\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)\right\rvert
is large enough.

Step 3
. Estimate of
\nabla u

\nabla u(x,t)=\nabla^{\perp}\Lambda^{-1}\nabla\theta(x,t)=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K(x-y)(\nabla\theta(x,t)-\nabla\theta(y,t))\,dy,

where
K(z)=z^{\perp}/\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{3}
.
We estimate
\nabla u
by splitting
\mathbb{R}^{2}
into two pieces; an inner piece
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq R
and an outer piece
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert>R
.

For the inner piece, we use the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla u_{\text{in}}(x,t)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\bigg(\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq R}\frac{\left\lvert\nabla\theta(x,t)-\nabla\theta(y,t)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{3}}\,dy\bigg)\bigg(\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq R}\frac{1}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}\,dy\bigg)}

\displaystyle=c_{3}\sqrt{D(x,t)R}.

For the outer piece, we apply integration by parts and use the modulus of continuity of
\theta
:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\nabla u_{\text{out}}(x,t)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert>R}\big(\theta(x,t)-\theta(y,t)\big)\nabla K(x-y)\,dy\right\rvert

\displaystyle\quad+\left\lvert\int_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert=R}\big(\theta(x,t)-\theta(y,t)\big)K(x-y)\nu(y)\,d\sigma(y)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{R}^{\infty}\frac{\Omega(\rho)}{\rho^{2}}\,d\rho+C\frac{\Omega(R)}{R}\leq c_{4}\frac{\Omega(R)}{R}.

Therefore,

(5.16)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}&\leq\left\lvert\nabla u_{\text{in}}\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert\nabla u_{\text{out}}\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{D}{4}+c_{3}^{2}R\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{4}+c_{4}\frac{\Omega(R)}{R}\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}.\end{aligned}

Step 4
. Maximum principle

Combining (
5.13
), (
5.15
), and (
5.16
),

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda)\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{D}{2}+\frac{\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}}{2R}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda)\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}+D

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,2\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}\leq\frac{D}{2}+2c_{3}^{2}R\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{4}+2c_{4}\frac{\Omega(R)}{R}\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}.

Hence

(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda)\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}}{2R}\leq 2c_{3}^{2}R\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{4}+2c_{4}\frac{\Omega(R)}{R}\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}.

Recall that (
5.14
) that

\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert=c_{1}\frac{\Omega(R)}{R}.

And let
r_{1}\in(0,1)
be

M(-\log r_{1})^{-\beta}=\Omega(r_{1})=\min\Big\{\frac{1}{8^{1/2}c_{1}c_{3}},\frac{1}{8c_{4}},1\Big\}=:c^{\prime}.

If
\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert>c_{1}\frac{\Omega(r_{1})}{r_{1}}
, then
R<r_{1}
,

2c_{3}^{2}R\bigg(c_{1}\frac{\Omega(R)}{R}\bigg)^{2}<\frac{1}{4R},\quad\text{and}\quad 2c_{4}\frac{\Omega(R)}{R}<\frac{1}{4R}.

Hence,

(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda)\left\lvert\nabla\theta\right\rvert^{2}(x,t)<0.

Due to the maximum principle,

\lVert\nabla\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq\max\Big\{c_{1}\frac{\Omega(r_{1})}{r_{1}},1\Big\}\leq\frac{C}{r_{1}}\leq C\exp\big((c^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}M^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\big).\qed

Proof of Main Theorem 2.

By setting
M=C\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{1+\beta}
for some large constant
C=C_{\beta}>0
, we get (
5.2
) due to Proposition
10
and
11
:

\lVert\nabla\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq C\lVert\nabla\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\exp(C\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{1+\frac{1}{\beta}}).\qed

, we do not know how to improve it to

6.
Asymptotic Decay of Solutions

In this section, we prove Main Theorem 3, i.e., decay of Sobolev norms of solutions to slightly supercritical SQG equation (
1.8
). Recall that
L^{\infty}
-norm of smooth solutions to drift-diffusion equations decay as follows:

Proposition 13

(Decay of
L^{\infty}
-norm, Theorem 4.1 in
[
9
]
)
.

Suppose
\theta
and
u
are smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,T)
(or
\mathbb{T}^{2}\times[0,T)
) satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta+\Lambda^{\alpha}\theta&=0,\\
\theta(t=0)&=\theta_{0}.\end{array}\right.

with
0<\alpha\leq 2
. Also assume that
\nabla\cdot u=0
and
\theta\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s})
with
s>1
. Then

\lVert\theta(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}{(1+C\alpha t\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}},\quad 0\leq t<T.

The constant
C
depends only on
\theta_{0}
.

Main Theorem 3.
Consider a slightly supercritical SQG equation (
1.2
) satisfying conditions of Main Theorem 1. Assume that the Fourier multiplier
P(\zeta)=P(\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert)
corresponding to the dissipation operator
\mathcal{L}
satisfies

P(\zeta)\geq Ck(\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert^{-1})\geq C_{\epsilon}\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert^{\beta},

for any
0\leq\beta<1
. Suppose
\theta\in C([0,T];H^{s})
for
s>1
is a global smooth solution to the equation, then

\lVert\theta(\cdot,t)\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}}\leq\exp(-c(t-t_{0}))\lVert\theta(\cdot,t_{0})\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}},\quad t>t_{0},

where
t_{0}>0
depends only on
\theta_{0}
and
c>0
depends only on
\theta_{0}
and
s
.

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
to be
0<\epsilon_{2}<\epsilon_{1}<\frac{1}{2}
. Set
\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon_{1}}{s+\frac{1}{2}-3\epsilon_{1}},\alpha_{2}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon_{2}}{s+\frac{1}{2}-3\epsilon_{2}}
and choose
\alpha\in(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
. Note that
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2s+1}
. Let
p,q\in(2,\infty)
be
p=\frac{2}{1-\alpha}
and
q=\frac{1}{\alpha}
. Set
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}>0
to be

\alpha\delta_{1}=\left(s+\frac{1}{2}-3\epsilon_{1}\right)(\alpha-\alpha_{1}),\quad(1-2\alpha)\delta_{2}=\left(s+\frac{1}{2}-3\epsilon_{2}\right)(\alpha_{2}-\alpha).

If we choose
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}
sufficiently close, then
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
can be arbitrary small.

Next, we perform the energy estimate by applying
\Lambda^{s}
to the equation and multiplying by
\Lambda^{s}\theta
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\Lambda^{s}P^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Lambda)\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\int\Lambda^{s}\big((u\cdot\nabla)\theta\big)\Lambda^{s}\theta\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int\Big(\Lambda^{s}\big((u\cdot\nabla)\theta\big)-(u\cdot\nabla)\Lambda^{s}\theta\Big)\Lambda^{s}\theta\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int(u\cdot\nabla)\left\lvert\Lambda^{s}\theta\right\rvert^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\lVert\Lambda^{s}\big((u\cdot\nabla)\theta\big)-(u\cdot\nabla)\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{p^{*}}}\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{p}}.

The second term at the second line vanishes since div
u=0
. Since
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
implies
\frac{1}{p^{*}}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
, Kato-Ponce type commutator estimate gives the following. (See e.g.
[
16
]
or Lemma 7.2 of
[
15
]
)

\displaystyle\lVert\Lambda^{s}\big((u\cdot\nabla)\theta\big)-(u\cdot\nabla)\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{p^{*}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{q}}\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{p}}+\lVert\nabla\theta\rVert_{L^{q}}\lVert\Lambda^{s}u\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\Lambda^{1+\delta_{1}}\theta\rVert_{L^{q}}\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{p}}+\lVert\Lambda\theta\rVert_{L^{q}}\lVert\Lambda^{s+\delta_{2}}\theta\rVert_{L^{p}}.

Recall that
u=\mathcal{R}^{\perp}m(\Lambda)\theta
where
m(\zeta)\leq C\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert^{\epsilon}
for any
\epsilon>0
. The last inequalities are applications of Hörmander-Mikhlin type multiplier theorem. (See e.g.
[
27
]
) The Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequalities

\displaystyle\lVert\Lambda^{1+\delta_{1}}\theta\rVert_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\Lambda^{2\epsilon_{1}+\delta_{1}}\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{1-2\alpha}\lVert\Lambda^{s+\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon_{1}}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2\alpha},

\displaystyle\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\Lambda^{2\epsilon_{1}+\delta_{1}}\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{\alpha}\lVert\Lambda^{s+\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon_{1}}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1-\alpha},

\displaystyle\lVert\Lambda^{1}\theta\rVert_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\Lambda^{2\epsilon_{2}+2\delta_{2}}\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{1-2\alpha}\lVert\Lambda^{s+\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon_{2}}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2\alpha},

\displaystyle\lVert\Lambda^{s+\delta_{2}}\theta\rVert_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\Lambda^{2\epsilon_{2}+2\delta_{2}}\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{\alpha}\lVert\Lambda^{s+\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon_{2}}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1-\alpha},

follow from
[
21
]
. Summing up the estimates, we get

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\Lambda^{s}P^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Lambda)\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\big(\lVert\Lambda^{2\epsilon_{1}+\delta_{1}}\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\lVert\Lambda^{2\epsilon_{2}+2\delta_{2}}\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\big)\lVert\Lambda^{s}P^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Lambda)\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Repeating the exact same argument Proposition
13
was proved in
[
9
]
, we get

\lim_{t\to\infty}\lVert\theta(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}=0.

Since
|\nabla\theta|
is uniformly bounded, the interpolation give that
\lVert\Lambda^{\gamma}\theta(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
for
0<\gamma<1
. Hence, there exists
t_{0}>0
so that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-c^{\prime}\lVert\Lambda^{s}P^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Lambda)\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-c\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad\text{for all }t>t_{0}.

Therefore
\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\exp(-c(t-t_{0}))\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta(\cdot,t_{0})\rVert_{L^{2}}.

∎

Remark
.

The Fourier multiplier
P
considered in the statement of Main Theorem 3 do not contain our benchmark case
P(\zeta)=\zeta(\log(10+\left\lvert\zeta\right\rvert))^{-\alpha_{1}}
,
\alpha_{1}>0
. In particular, the assumption that
P(\zeta)\geq c>0
was crucially used in the last line. The main issue is that we cannot prove exponential decay of low frequency part. We suggest two ways to overcome such problem.

(i)

If the spatial domain is a bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
or periodic
\mathbb{T}^{2}
(with mean zero assumption
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\theta\,dx=0
), then Poincaré inequality is applicable for the last line of the proof.

(ii)

It is possible to prove polynomial decay on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as follows:

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-c^{\prime}\lVert\Lambda^{s}P^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Lambda)\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-c^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1+\beta}}{\lVert\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\beta}}\right)^{2},\quad\text{for all }t>t_{0},

for
0<\beta<\frac{1}{2s}
. Since
\lVert\theta(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\lVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}
,

\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq-c\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1+2\beta},\quad\text{for all }t>t_{0}.

Therefore

\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta(t_{0})\rVert_{L^{2}}}{\big(1+2c\beta(t-t_{0})\lVert\Lambda^{s}\theta(t_{0})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2\beta}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2\beta}}},\quad\text{for all }t>t_{0}.

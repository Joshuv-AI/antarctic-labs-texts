Comparison of the Hitchin metric and the semi-flat metric in the rank two case
Takuro Mochizuki

Abstract
Let ( E , θ ) (E,\theta) be a Higgs bundle of rank 2 2 and degree 0 0 on a compact Riemann surface X X whose spectral curve is smooth. The tangent space of the moduli space of Higgs bundles at ( E , θ ) (E,\theta) is equipped with two natural metrics called the Hitchin metric and the semi-flat metric. It is known that the difference between two metrics along the curve ( E , t ​ θ ) (E,t\theta) ( t ≥ 1 ) (t\geq 1) decays in an exponential way. In this paper, we shall study how the exponential rate is improved. MSC: 53C07, 58E15, 14D21, 81T13. Keywords: harmonic bundle, moduli space of Higgs bundles, Hitchin metric, semi-flat metric

Introduction

1.1
Hitchin metric and semi-flat metric
of the moduli space of Higgs bundles

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{H}
denote the moduli space of
stable Higgs bundles of rank
2
and degree
0
on
X
.
According to Hitchin
[
10
]
,

\mathcal{M}_{H}
is equipped with the hyperkähler metric
g_{H,{\mathbb{R}}}

called the Hitchin metric.

There also exists the Hitchin fibration

\Phi_{H}:\mathcal{M}_{H}\to\mathcal{A}_{H}=H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{\otimes 2})
,
which admits a section
\mathcal{A}_{H}\to\mathcal{M}_{H}

called a Hitchin section
depending only on the choice of
K_{X}^{1/2}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}_{H}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{A}_{H}
denote the set of regular values.
We set
\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}=\Phi_{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{A}_{H}^{\prime})
.
The restriction of the Hitchin fibration

\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{A}_{H}^{\prime}
is an algebraic integrable system,
and in this situation

\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}
is equipped with the canonically defined hyperkähler metric

g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits,{\mathbb{R}}}

[
7
]
, called the semi-flat metric.

Let
(E,\theta)\in\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}
.
We obtain the curve
(E,t\theta)

(t\geq 1)
in
\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}
.
Let
g_{H}
and
g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits}
denote the Kähler metrics
of
\mathcal{M}_{H}
with the natural complex structure
induced by
g_{H,{\mathbb{R}}}
and
g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits,{\mathbb{R}}}
.
It was predicted in
[
8
,
9
]

and proved in
[
4
]
that
there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

g_{H|(E,t\theta)}-g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits|(E,t\theta)}=O(e^{-\epsilon t})

(1)

as
t\to\infty
.
Such an estimate was obtained in
[
4
]

under a mild assumption on
(E,\theta)
,
and in
[
18
]

without the additional assumption
by a different method.
However, the original prediction in

[
8
,
9
]

suggests an explicit range of
\epsilon
.
In this paper,
we shall partially verify it in the rank two case
by using the method in
[
18
]
.

Before explaining our main result more explicitly,
let us recall more detail about the related objects.

1.1.1
Hitchin metric

Let
(E,\theta)
be a Higgs bundle
on a compact Riemann surface
X

of degree
0
and
\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits 2
.
Assume that
\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits\theta=0
and

,

We also assume

,
which is equivalent to the condition that

\Sigma_{\theta}

According to the fundamental theorem of Hitchin and Simpson,
under the condition,

(E,\theta)
has a unique harmonic metric

the Hitchin equation

R(h)+[\theta,\theta^{\dagger}_{h}]=0

denotes the curvature
of the Chern connection of
(E,h)

and
\theta^{\dagger}_{h}
denotes the adjoint of
\theta

with respect to

.
We remark that the induced harmonic metric
on
(\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E),\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)
is also denoted by

.

We consider the Deformation complex

\mathop{\rm Def}\nolimits(E,\theta)

given by

\begin{CD}\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E)@>{\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta}>{}>\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E)\otimes K_{X},\end{CD}

where the first term sits at the degree
0
.
It is well known that

T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}\simeq H^{1}(X,\mathop{\rm Def}\nolimits(E,\theta)).

(2)

We recall that
an

-valued
1
-form

(\overline{\partial}_{E}+\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)\sigma=(\overline{\partial}_{E}+\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)^{\ast}_{h,g_{X}}\sigma=0

\overline{\partial}_{E}
denotes the holomorphic structure of
E
,
and
g_{X}
denotes a compact Kähler metric
g_{X}
of
X
.
It is equivalent to the condition that

(\overline{\partial}_{E}+\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)\sigma=(\partial_{E,h}+\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta^{\dagger}_{h})\sigma=0,

where
\partial_{E,h}
denotes the
(1,0)
-part of
the Chern connection of
(E,h)
.
Then, (
2
)
is also isomorphic to the space

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E),\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta,h)

of harmonic
1
-forms of
(\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E),\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta,h)
,

We consider the standard
L^{2}
-Hermitian product
of
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E)
-valued
1
-forms
\sigma_{i}

(i=1,2)

(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})_{L^{2},h}=2\sqrt{-1}\int_{X}\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits\Bigl(\sigma_{1}^{1,0}\cdot(\sigma_{2}^{1,0})^{\dagger}_{h}-\sigma_{1}^{0,1}\cdot(\sigma_{2}^{0,1})^{\dagger}_{h}\Bigr).

Then, the Hitchin metric

is obtained as the restriction of

to
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E),\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta,h)\simeq T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}

1.1.2
Semi-flat metric

Note that
\Phi_{H}:\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{A}_{H}^{\prime}

is a locally principal torus bundle.
It is a Lagrangian fibration,
and the fiber

\Phi_{H}^{-1}(\Phi_{H}(E,\theta))

is isomorphic to

\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits_{2g(X)-2}(\Sigma_{\theta})
.
There exists a Hitchin section
\mathcal{A}_{H}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}
.
There uniquely exists an integrable connection of

\Phi_{H}

such that
any Hitchin section is horizontal.
We obtain the decomposition
of
T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}

into the vertical part

(T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime})^{\mathop{\rm ver}\nolimits}

and the horizontal part

(T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime})^{\mathop{\rm hor}\nolimits}
.
Because the fiber is identified with
a component of the Picard variety of
\Sigma_{\theta}
,
there exists an isomorphism

(T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime})^{\mathop{\rm ver}\nolimits}\simeq H^{1}(\Sigma_{\theta},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{\theta}}).

Because the vertical part is a Lagrangian subspace,

(T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime})^{\mathop{\rm hor}\nolimits}

is the dual space of

(T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime})^{\mathop{\rm ver}\nolimits}
,
and hence

(T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime})^{\mathop{\rm hor}\nolimits}\simeq H^{0}(\Sigma_{\theta},K_{\Sigma_{\theta}}).

Therefore, we obtain

T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}\simeq H^{1}(\Sigma_{\theta},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{\theta}})\oplus H^{0}(\Sigma_{\theta},K_{\Sigma_{\theta}}).

It is also identified with
the space

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Sigma_{\theta})

of the harmonic
1
-forms of
\Sigma_{\theta}
.

We consider the standard
L^{2}
-Hermitian product
of
1
-forms
\mu_{i}

(i=1,2)
on
\Sigma_{\theta}
:

(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})_{L^{2},\Sigma_{\theta}}=2\sqrt{-1}\int_{\Sigma_{\theta}}\Bigl(\mu_{1}^{1,0}\wedge\overline{\mu_{2}^{1,0}}-\mu_{1}^{0,1}\wedge\overline{\mu_{2}^{0,1}}\Bigr).

Then, the semi-flat metric

g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits|(E,\theta)}

is obtained as the restriction of

(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2},\Sigma_{\theta}}

to
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Sigma)\simeq T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}
.

1.3
Main result

We set
\phi=-\det(\theta)\in H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{\otimes 2})
,
which induces the distance
d_{\phi}
on
X
.
According to
[
3
, §3.2]
,
a saddle connection of
\phi

is a geodesic segment on
(X,d_{\phi})

such that
(i) the starting point and the ending point are
contained in
D_{\phi}
,
(ii) any other point is contained in
X\setminus D_{\phi}
.
Note that the starting point and the ending point
are not necessarily distinct.
Let
M(\phi)
denote the minimum length
of the saddle connections of
\phi
,
which is called the threshold.

Proposition 1.2
(Corollary

3.34
)

g^{\mathop{\rm aux}\nolimits}_{|(E,t\theta)}=O(e^{-2at})

with respect to
g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits|(E,t\theta)}

for any
0<a<M(\phi)
.
As a result,
there exists
t_{0}>0
such that

g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits|(E,t\theta)}+g^{\mathop{\rm aux}\nolimits}_{|(E,t\theta)}

are positive definite Hermitian products
on
T_{(E,t\theta)}\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}

for any
t\geq t_{0}
.

The following theorem is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.3
(Theorem

3.35
)

For any
(E,\theta)\in\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}
,
we obtain

g_{H|(E,t\theta)}-\bigl(g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits|(E,t\theta)}+g^{\mathop{\rm aux}\nolimits}_{|(E,t\theta)}\bigr)=O\bigl(e^{-4at}\bigr)\quad(0<a<M(\phi)).

(11)

g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits}(\iota_{t}^{\mathop{\rm hor}\nolimits}(\nu),\iota_{t}^{\mathop{\rm ver}\nolimits}(\tau))=0.

(12)

,

.

1.4.4
Canonically decoupled harmonic metric

Let
Q\in X\setminus D_{\theta}
.
Let
(X_{Q},z_{Q})
denote
a holomorphic coordinate neighbourhood around
Q

such that
\phi=dz_{Q}\,dz_{Q}
.
There exists the decomposition,
which is orthogonal with respect to
C
:

(E,\theta)_{|X_{Q}}=(E_{Q,1},dz_{Q})\oplus(E_{Q,2},-dz_{Q}).

Let
v_{i}

(i=1,2)
be frames of
E_{Q,i}

such that
C(v_{i},v_{i})=1
.
We define
h_{\infty,Q}
by

h_{\infty,Q}(v_{i},v_{i})=1

and

h_{\infty,Q}(v_{1},v_{2})=0
,
which is independent of
v_{i}
.
By varying
Q\in X\setminus D_{\theta}
,
we obtain the canonical decoupled harmonic metric

h_{\infty}
of
(E,\theta)_{|X\setminus D_{\theta}}

compatible with
C
.
It equals the limiting configuration in
[
13
]
.

Let
s_{t}
be the automorphism of
E_{|X\setminus D_{\theta}}

determined by

h_{t}=h_{\infty}s_{t}
.
We have
\det(s_{t})=1
.

We set
g_{\phi}=|\phi|
as
the Kähler metric of
X\setminus D_{\theta}
,
which induces the distance
d_{\phi}
on
X
.
We obtain the connection
\nabla_{\infty}
of

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E)\otimes T^{\ast}(X\setminus D_{\theta})^{\otimes m}

for any
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}

induced by
h_{\infty}
and
g_{\phi}
.

For any
r>0
and
P\in X
,
we set
X_{P}(r)=\{Q\in X\,|\,d_{\phi}(Q,P)<r\}
.
We set
\kappa_{0}=M(\phi)/2
.
We have

X_{P}(\kappa_{0})\cap X_{P^{\prime}}(\kappa_{0})=\emptyset

if
P,P^{\prime}
are two distinct points in
D_{\theta}
.

For
0<\kappa<\kappa_{0}
,
we set

\delta(\kappa):=\min\{(\kappa-\kappa_{0})/10,\kappa/10\}
.
For each
P\in D_{\theta}
,
we set

X^{(1)}_{P,\kappa}=X_{P}(\kappa_{0}-\delta(\kappa)),\quad X^{(2)}_{P,\kappa}=X_{P}(\kappa_{0}-2\delta(\kappa)).

For any non-negative integer
j
,
there exist
C_{j}>0
such that the following holds
on
X\setminus\bigcup X^{(2)}_{P,\kappa}

for any
t\geq 1

(see Corollary
2.5
):

\bigl|\nabla_{\infty}^{j}(s_{t}-\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits)\bigr|_{h_{\infty},g_{\phi}}\leq C_{j}e^{-4(\kappa+7\delta(\kappa))t}.

(18)

This is essentially contained in
[
3
]
.

The multiplication of

\nu\in H^{0}(\Sigma_{\theta},K_{\Sigma_{\theta}})
on
L
induces
a section

F_{\nu}
of
(\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E)\otimes\Omega^{1,0})_{|X\setminus D_{\theta}}

such that
(\overline{\partial}_{E}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)F_{\nu}=0
.
Similarly,
\tau
induces
a section

F_{\tau}
of
(\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E)\otimes\Omega^{0,1})_{|X\setminus D_{\theta}}

such that
(\overline{\partial}_{E}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)F_{\tau}=0
.
We can regard

F_{\nu}
and
F_{\tau}
as approximations of

{\tt H}_{t}(\nu)
and
{\tt V}_{t}(\tau)

on
X\setminus\bigcup_{P}X_{P,\kappa}^{(2)}
.

1.4.5
Approximation of
{\tt H}_{t}(\nu)
around
P\in D_{\theta}

For any
W\subset X
,
we set
\Sigma_{\theta|W}=\Sigma_{\theta}\times_{X}W
.
There exists a holomorphic function
\alpha_{P}

on
\Sigma_{\theta|X_{P}(\kappa_{0})}

such that
\nu=d\alpha_{P}

and that
\alpha_{P}=0
at
\pi^{-1}(P)
.
The multiplication of
\alpha_{P}
on

induces
a holomorphic endomorphism

F_{\alpha_{P}}
on
E_{|X_{P}(\kappa_{0})}

such that

[\theta,F_{\alpha_{P}}]=0
.
We obtain the following
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E)
-valued

C^{\infty}

1
-forms for
t\geq 1
:

{\tt H}_{P,t}(\nu)=(\partial_{E,h_{t}}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta^{\dagger}_{h_{t}})F_{\alpha_{P}}.

They are harmonic
1
-forms of

,

on

.

-section

\rho_{P}

1.4.6
Approximation of
{\tt H}_{t}(\nu)
on
X

By patching
F_{\nu}
and
{\tt H}_{P,t}(\nu)

(P\in D_{\theta})
,
we construct
{\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu)
as follows.

•

On
X\setminus\bigcup X_{P,\kappa}^{(1)}
,
we set

{\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu)=F_{\nu}
.

•

For
P\in D_{\theta}
,
let
\chi_{P,\kappa}:X\to[0,1]
be
a
C^{\infty}
-function
such that
\chi_{P,\kappa}=1
on
X_{P,\kappa}^{(2)}

and
\chi_{P,\kappa}=0
on
X\setminus X_{P,\kappa}^{(1)}
.
Then, on
X^{(1)}_{P,\kappa}\setminus X^{(2)}_{P,\kappa}
,
we set

{\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu)=F_{\nu}-(\overline{\partial}_{E}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)

(\chi_{P,\kappa}\rho_{P})
.

•

On
X^{(2)}_{P,\kappa}
,
we set

{\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu)={\tt H}_{P,t}(\nu)
.

By the construction,
we obtain

(\overline{\partial}_{E}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta){\tt H}_{\kappa,t}^{\prime}(\nu)=0
.
We can observe that

{\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu)
is a section of
the Dolbeault resolution of

\mathop{\rm Def}\nolimits(E,\theta,C)
,
and that
{\tt H}_{\kappa,t}^{\prime}(\nu)^{\circ}=F_{\nu}
.
As explained in
[
18
]
,
the cohomology class of

{\tt H}_{\kappa,t}^{\prime}(\nu)

is
\iota_{t}^{\mathop{\rm hor}\nolimits}(\nu)
.

There exist
C_{j}>0

(j\geq 0)
such that
the following holds
on
X^{(1)}_{P,\kappa}\setminus X^{(2)}_{P,\kappa}

by (
18
):

\Bigl|\nabla_{\infty}^{j}({\tt H}_{P,t}(\nu)-F_{\nu})\Bigr|_{h_{\infty},g_{\phi}}\leq C_{j}e^{-4(\kappa+6\delta(\kappa))t}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}}.

(19)

There exist
C_{j}>0

(j\geq 0)
such that
the following holds
on
X^{(1)}_{P,\kappa}\setminus X^{(2)}_{P,\kappa}
:

\bigl|\nabla^{j}_{\infty}\rho_{P}\bigr|_{h_{\infty},g_{\phi}}\leq C_{j}e^{-4(\kappa+6\delta(\kappa))t}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence, there exist
C_{j}>0

(j\geq 0)
such that
on
X^{(1)}_{P,\kappa}\setminus X^{(2)}_{P,\kappa}

\bigl|\nabla^{j}_{\infty}({\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu)-F_{\nu})\bigr|_{h_{\infty},g_{\phi}}\leq C_{j}e^{-4(\kappa+6\delta(\kappa))t}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}}.

Let
g_{X}
be a Kähler metric.
Note that

(\partial_{E,h_{t}}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta^{\dagger}_{h_{t}}){\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu)=0

on
X\setminus\bigcup_{P}X^{(1)}_{P,\kappa}

and
\bigcup_{P}X_{P,\kappa}^{(2)}
.
There exists
C>0
such that
the following holds on
X
:

\bigl|(\partial_{E,h_{t}}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta^{\dagger}_{h_{t}}){\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu)\bigr|_{h_{t},g_{X}}\leq Ce^{-4(\kappa+6\delta(\kappa))t}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}}.

There exist unique
C^{\infty}
-sections
\gamma_{\kappa,t}(\nu)

of
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E)^{\mathop{\rm asym}\nolimits}
such that

(\partial_{E,h_{t}}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta^{\dagger}_{h_{t}})(\overline{\partial}_{E}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)\gamma_{\kappa,t}(\nu)=(\partial_{E,h_{t}}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta^{\dagger}_{h_{t}}){\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu).

By
[
18

,
there exists
C>0
such that

\|\gamma_{\kappa,t}(\nu)\|_{L^{2},h_{t},g_{X}}+\|(\overline{\partial}_{E}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)\gamma_{\kappa,t}(\nu)\|_{L^{2},h_{t},g_{X}}\leq Ce^{-4(\kappa+5\delta(\kappa))t}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}}.

We have

{\tt H}_{t}(\nu)={\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu)-\gamma_{\kappa,t}(\nu).

1.4.7
Estimate of the pairings of
{\tt H}_{t}(\nu)

First, we observe

({\tt H}_{t}(\nu),{\tt H}_{t}(\nu))_{L^{2},h_{t}}=({\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu),{\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu))_{L^{2},h_{t}}+O\bigl(e^{-8\kappa t}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr).

(20)

Indeed,
because

(\overline{\partial}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)^{\ast}_{h_{t},g_{X}}{\tt H}_{t}(\nu)=0
,
we obtain

({\tt H}_{t}(\nu),{\tt H}_{t}(\nu))_{L^{2},h_{t}}=({\tt H}_{t}(\nu),{\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu))_{L^{2},h_{t}}+\bigl({\tt H}_{t}(\nu),(\overline{\partial}_{E}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)\gamma_{\kappa,t}(\nu)\bigr)_{L^{2},h_{t}}=\\
({\tt H}_{t}(\nu),{\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu))_{L^{2},h_{t}}+\bigl((\overline{\partial}_{E}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)^{\ast}_{h_{t},g_{X}}{\tt H}_{t}(\nu),\gamma_{\kappa,t}(\nu)\bigr)_{L^{2},h_{t},g_{X}}=({\tt H}_{t}(\nu),{\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu))_{L^{2},h_{t}}.

(21)

Because

(\overline{\partial}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)^{\ast}_{h_{t},g_{X}}{\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu)=O\bigl(e^{-4\kappa t}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}}\bigr)

and

\gamma_{\kappa,t}(\nu)=O(e^{-4\kappa t}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}})
,
we obtain

\bigl({\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu),(\overline{\partial}_{E}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)\gamma_{\kappa,t}(\nu)\bigr)_{L^{2},h_{t}}=\bigl((\overline{\partial}_{E}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)^{\ast}_{h_{t},g_{X}}{\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu),\gamma_{\kappa,t}(\nu)\bigr)_{L^{2},h_{t},g_{X}}=O\bigl(e^{-8\kappa t}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr).

Hence, we obtain (
20
).

We have the following estimates on

W_{\kappa}=X\setminus\bigcup X_{P,\kappa}^{(2)}
:

{\tt H}_{\kappa,t}^{\prime}(\nu)^{\circ}=F_{\nu},\quad\quad\Bigl|\bigl({\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu)\bigr)^{\bot}\Bigr|_{h_{t},g_{X}}=O\bigl(e^{-4\kappa t}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}}\bigr),\quad\quad\bigl|s_{t}-\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits\bigr|_{h_{t}}=O(e^{-4\kappa t}).

(22)

We can prove the following
by using (
22
)
with an elementary consideration
as in §
3.6.1
:

({\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu),{\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu))_{L^{2},h_{t},W_{\kappa}}=2\sqrt{-1}\int_{W_{\kappa}}\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits\bigl(F_{\nu}\cdot(F_{\nu})^{\dagger}_{h_{\infty}}\bigr)+O\bigl(e^{-8\kappa t}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr)\\
=2\sqrt{-1}\int_{\Sigma_{\theta|W_{\kappa}}}\nu\wedge\overline{\nu}+O\bigl(e^{-8\kappa t}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr).

(23)

On
X^{(2)}_{P,\kappa}
,
we have

{\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu)={\tt H}_{P,t}(\nu)
.
Because

(\partial_{E,h_{t}}+t\theta^{\dagger}_{h_{t}})^{\ast}_{h_{t},g_{X}}{\tt H}_{P,t}(\nu)=0
,
we obtain the following
by using the Stokes formula (Lemma
3.55
):

\bigl({\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu),{\tt H}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\nu)\bigr)_{L^{2},h_{t},X^{(2)}_{P,\kappa}}=2\sqrt{-1}\int_{\partial X_{P,\kappa}^{(2)}}\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits\Bigl(F_{\alpha_{P}}\cdot({\tt H}_{P,t}(\nu)^{1,0})^{\dagger}_{h_{t}}\Bigr).

By using the estimates (
18
)
and (
19
)
with the elementary consideration
in §
3.6.1
,
we obtain

2\sqrt{-1}\int_{\partial X_{P,\kappa}^{(2)}}\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits\Bigl(F_{\alpha_{P}}\cdot({\tt H}_{P,t}(\nu)^{1,0})^{\dagger}_{h_{t}}\Bigr)=2\sqrt{-1}\int_{\partial X_{P,\kappa}^{(2)}}\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits\Bigl(F_{\alpha_{P}}\cdot(F_{\nu})^{\dagger}_{h_{\infty}}\Bigr)+O\bigl(e^{-8\kappa t}\|\nu\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\bigr)\\
=2\sqrt{-1}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta|X_{P,\kappa}^{(2)}}}\alpha_{P}\cdot\overline{\nu}+O\bigl(e^{-8\kappa t}\|\nu\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\bigr)=2\sqrt{-1}\int_{\Sigma_{\theta|X_{P,\kappa}^{(2)}}}\nu\overline{\nu}+O\bigl(e^{-8\kappa t}\|\nu\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\bigr).

(24)

In all, we obtain

({\tt H}_{t}(\nu),{\tt H}_{t}(\nu))_{L^{2},h_{t}}=(\nu,\nu)_{L^{2}}+O\bigl(e^{-8\kappa t}\|\nu\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr)\quad(0<\kappa<M(\phi)/2).

1.4.8

on

For each

\iota_{t}^{\mathop{\rm ver}\nolimits}(\tau)

By (

\Bigl|(\partial_{E,h_{t}}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta^{\dagger}_{h_{t}}){\tt V}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\tau)\Bigr|_{h_{t},g_{X}}\leq Ce^{-4(\kappa+7\delta(\kappa))t}\|\tau\|_{L^{2}}.

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E)

\|\gamma_{\kappa,t}(\tau)\|_{L^{2},h_{t},g_{X}}+\|(\overline{\partial}_{E}+t\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits\theta)\gamma_{\kappa,t}(\tau)\|_{L^{2},h_{t},g_{X}}\leq Ce^{-4(\kappa+5\delta(\kappa))t}\|\tau\|_{L^{2}}.

As in the case of (

\bigl({\tt V}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\tau),{\tt V}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\tau)\bigr)_{L^{2},h_{t},W_{\kappa}}=-2\sqrt{-1}\int_{W_{\kappa}}\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits\bigl(F_{\tau}\cdot(F_{\tau})^{\dagger}_{h_{\infty}}\bigr)+O\bigl(e^{-8\kappa t}\|\tau\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr)\\

(Lemma

\bigl({\tt V}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\tau),{\tt V}^{\prime}_{\kappa,t}(\tau)\bigr)_{L^{2},h_{t},X^{(2)}_{P,\kappa}}=-2\sqrt{-1}\int_{\partial X^{(2)}_{P,\kappa}}\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits\Bigl(F^{\dagger}_{\beta_{P}}\cdot\bigl({\tt V}^{\prime}_{t}(\tau)^{0,1}\bigr)^{\dagger}_{h_{t}}\Bigr)\\

Let

Lemma 2.7

Let

be an endomorphism of

Let

be the adjoint of

with respect to
h

Let

g^{\dagger}_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}={\boldsymbol{v}}G^{\dagger}_{H}

Lemma 2.8

We have

\bigl|[f^{\dagger}_{h},\pi_{1}]\bigr|_{h}^{2}=8(1+|b|^{2})|b|^{2}
.

Proof
Let
F
and
F^{\dagger}_{H}

be the matrix valued functions determined by

f{\boldsymbol{v}}={\boldsymbol{v}}F

and

f^{\dagger}_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}={\boldsymbol{v}}\cdot F^{\dagger}_{H}
.
More explicitly,

F=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right),\quad F^{\dagger}_{H}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}c&-\overline{b}\\
-b&a\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&\overline{b}\\
b&c\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1+2|b|^{2}&2\overline{b}c\\
-2ab&-1-2|b|^{2}\end{array}\right).

Let
\Pi_{1}
be the matrix valued function determined by

\pi_{1}{\boldsymbol{v}}={\boldsymbol{v}}\cdot\Pi_{1}

\Pi_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\

[F^{\dagger}_{H},\Pi_{1}]=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-2\overline{b}c\\
-2ab&0\end{array}\right)=[F^{\dagger}_{H},\Pi_{1}]^{\dagger}_{H}.

We obtain

[F^{\dagger}_{H},\Pi_{1}]\cdot[F^{\dagger}_{H},\Pi_{1}]^{\dagger}_{H}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}4(1+|b|^{2})|b|^{2}&0\\
0&4(1+|b|^{2})|b|^{2}\end{array}\right).

Hence, we obtain

\bigl|[f^{\dagger}_{h},\pi_{1}]\bigr|_{h}^{2}=\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits\bigl([F^{\dagger}_{H},\Pi_{1}]^{\dagger}_{H}\cdot[F^{\dagger}_{H},\Pi_{1}]\bigr)=8(1+|b|^{2})|b|^{2}.

Thus, we obtain Lemma
2.8
.

Corollary 2.9

We obtain

-\partial_{z}\partial_{\overline{z}}|b|_{h}^{2}\leq-8|b|_{h}^{2}

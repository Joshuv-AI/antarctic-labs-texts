Abstract
. We denote by ℋ d , g , r \mathcal{H}_{d,g,r} the Hilbert scheme of smooth curves, which is the union of components whose general point corresponds to a smooth, irreducible and non-degenerate curve of degree d d and genus g g in ℙ r . \mathbb{P}^{r}. In this article, we study ℋ 15 , g , 5 \mathcal{H}_{15,g,5} for every possible genus g g and determine when it is irreducible. We also study the moduli map ℋ 15 , g , 5 → ℳ g \mathcal{H}_{15,g,5}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{g} and several key properties such as gonality of a general element as well as characterizing smooth elements of each component.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the Hilbert scheme of smooth and non-degenerate curves
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
of degree
15
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
denote the Hilbert scheme of smooth curves of degree
d
and genus
g
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
.
We determine the number of irreducible components, their dimensions and study the properties of
\mathcal{H}_{15,g,5}
such as the gonality of a general element in each component
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{H}_{15,g,5}
. We also study the natural functorial map
\mu:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

Severi
[
33
]
asserted that
the Hilbert scheme
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
is irreducible for
triples
(d,g,r)
in the range

(i)
d\geq g+r~~~~

or in the much wider Brill-Noether range

(ii)
\rho(d,g,r):=g-(r+1)(g-d+r)\geq 0
.

In general, determining the irreducibility of a Hilbert scheme is a non-trivial task.
The assertion of Severi turns out to be true for
r=3,4
under the condition (i); cf.
[
16
,
17
]
.
However our overall knowledge on
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,5}
is not as extensive as those on
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,3}
or
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,4}
.

In this paper, we would like to concentrate on
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,5}
when the degree
d
of the curve in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
is relatively low. Specifically, we focus our attention on curves in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
of degree
d=15
and determine when
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{15,g,5}
is irreducible for every possible genus
g\leq\pi(15,5)=18
. Our results include:

Main Results
.

(i)

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{15,g,5}
is irreducible unless

; Propositions
3.2
,
3.4
,
3.5
,
3.6
,

, Theorems

,

and
[

, Theorem 1.1]
.

(ii)

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{15,g,5}=\emptyset
for
g=17
; Proposition

.

(iii)

An estimate of the dimension of the image of the forgetful map
\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{g}

and the gonality of curves in each component
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{15,g,5}
.

(iv)

A general
X\in\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{15,15,5}
has exactly one

, one base-point-free

The reducibility of
\cal{H}_{15,14,5}
is a result of another paper
[

, which we listed in Main Results for a more comprehensive list of results concerning
\cal{H}_{15,g,5}
.
Our main reason for aiming at an extensive study of curves of degree
d=15
in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
can be addressed as follows. The case
g=14
, which we studied in
[
6
]
, was hard enough requiring several new techniques. If we shift our attention to curves of higher genus e.g.
15\leq g\leq 18=\pi(15,5)
, there appear more interesting cases and components containing fruitful information on the geometry of projective algebraic curves.

Besides using classical methods in analyzing curves on smooth and singular rational surfaces, we use
the irreducibility of Severi varieties of nodal curves on Hirzebruch surfaces
[
34
]
in a key step in Theorem
4.1
.
We also use and prove seemingly well-known facts stating that curves on singular surfaces of low degrees such as singular del Pezzo or cone over an elliptic curve are flat limits of curves on smooth surfaces when we need to rule out the possibility for a family of curves on such singular surfaces constituting a full component; Proposition
2.1
, Lemma
5.12
. We also use the notion of residual schemes in one of our main results; cf. Remark
4.3
and Theorem
4.5

Unless the genus

of a smooth curve
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
is fairly high with respect to
\deg X

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}
with the very ample

is by no means
contained in a surface of low degrees.
However the residual series

may induce possibly singular curves of lower degrees which may sit on surfaces of small degrees with better classifications in lower dimensional projective spaces. If this occurs, studying (extrinsic) projective curves defined by the residual series may become easier than directly handling the original curve
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
. We use this simple idea several times throughout the paper.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In the next section, we prepare some preliminaries. In section 3, we treat curves of genus

15
,
16

1.1.
Notation and conventions

For notation and conventions, we follow those in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
; e.g.

d
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}

\mathbb{P}^{r}
not lying on a surface of minimal degree
r-1
; cf.
[
21
, p. 99]
,
[
3
, p. 123]
.

Following classical terminology, a linear series of degree
d
and dimension
r
on a smooth curve
X
is denoted by
g^{r}_{d}
.
A base-point-free linear series
g^{r}_{d}
(
r\geq 2
) on
X
is called
birationally very ample
when the morphism

X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{r}
induced by the
g^{r}_{d}
is generically one-to-one onto (or is birational to) its image curve. A base-point-free linear series

on
X
is said to be compounded of an involution (

for short) if the morphism induced by the linear series gives rise to a non-trivial covering map
X\rightarrow C^{\prime}
of degree
k\geq 2
.

We also recall the following standard set up and notation; cf.
[
4
, Ch. 21, §3, 5, 6, 11, 12]
or
[
2
, §1 and §2]
.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{g}
be the moduli space of smooth curves of genus
g
. Given an isomorphism class
[C]\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
corresponding to a smooth irreducible curve
C
, there exist a neighborhood
U\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
of
[C]
and a smooth connected variety
\mathcal{M}
which is a finite ramified covering
h:\mathcal{M}\xrightarrow{\ \ }U
, as well as varieties
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
proper over
\mathcal{M}
with the following properties:

(1)

\xi:\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathcal{M}
is a universal curve, i.e. for every
p\in\mathcal{M}
,
\xi^{-1}(p)
is a smooth curve of genus
g
whose isomorphism class is
h(p)
,

(2)

\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
parametrizes the pairs
(p,\mathcal{D})
, where
\mathcal{D}
is possibly an incomplete linear series of degree
d
and dimension
r
on

.

(3)

For any component
\cal{G}\subset\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
,

\dim\cal{G}\geq\lambda(d,g,r):=3g-3+\rho(d,g,r)

and for any component

\dim\cal{H}\geq\mathcal{X}(d,g,r):=\lambda(d,g,r)+\dim\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{r}).

Throughout, we work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero.

4.2.
Moduli map
\mu:\mathcal{H}_{15,15,5}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{M}_{15}

In this subsection, we show that two smooth curves in

are isomorphic as abstract curves if and only if
they are projectively equivalent. In order to prove this seemingly plausible assertion, we need several preparatory results which occupy a major part of this subsection.

Let

projectively equivalent to

is an irreducible quasi-projective variety isomorphic to
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{r})/G
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

denote the moduli map. Then we have

Remark 4.3

(9)

•

\Res_{D}(Z)=\emptyset

.

•

If

•

•

\diamond

.

\diamond

•

\diamond

\Lambda_{2}\cap\Lambda_{0}=\emptyset
. However we want to prove something stronger: we want to prove that no element of
\Lambda_{0}
is isomorphic to an element of
\Lambda_{2}
.
We will use the following lemma which asserts that the first scrollar invariant of the unique
g^{1}_{4}
on
X\in\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{15,18,5}
detects the integer
e\in\{0,2\}
such that
X\in\Lambda_{e}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Take
e\in\{0,2\}
and
X\in\Lambda_{e}
. Let
R
be the only degree
4
line bundle on
X
such that
h^{0}(X,R)=2
. Let
c
be the minimal integer such that
h^{0}(R^{\otimes c})\geq c+2
, the first scrollar invariant of
|R|
. Then we have the following two cases for the integer
c
:

\begin{cases}~~e=0,~c=7,~h^{0}(R^{\otimes 7})=11\\
~e=2,~c=5,~h^{0}(R^{\otimes 5})=7.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
e=0
we have
X\in|\cal{O}_{Q}(4,7)|
with
R=\cal{O}_{X}(0,1)
. Fix an integer
t\geq 1
. From the cohomology sequence of the exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{Q}(-4,t-7)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{Q}(0,t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{X}(0,t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

we have
h^{0}(R^{\otimes t})=h^{0}(Q,\cal{O}_{Q}(0,t))+h^{1}(Q,\cal{O}_{Q}(-4,t-7))
and hence

h^{0}(R^{\otimes 7})=11
and
c=7
.
For
e=2
we have
X\in|4H-L|=|4h+11f|
with
R=\cal{O}_{X}(f)
. We use the exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(-4h+(t-11)f)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(tf)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{X}(tf)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

to get
h^{0}(R^{\otimes 5})=7
and
c=5
. We omit routine computation.
∎

The following is an immediate consequence of Lemma
6.3
.

Proposition 6.4
.

No smooth element of
\Lambda_{2}
is isomorphic to a smooth element of
\Lambda_{0}
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Take
X\in\Lambda_{0}
. Then:

(a)

X
is
4
-gonal with a unique
g^{1}_{4}
, no base-point-free
g^{1}_{c}
for
5\leq c\leq 6
and a unique
g^{1}_{7}
.

(b)

\mu^{-1}(\mu(X))=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{5})X
.

(c)

\dim\mu(\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{15,18,5})=\dim\mu(\Lambda_{0})=33
.

Proof.

(a) follows from Proposition
2.3
.
For (b), we fix any
\tilde{X}\in\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{15,18,5}
isomorphic to
X
. Lemma
6.3
gives
\tilde{X}\in\Lambda_{0}
. Let
\tilde{S}
(resp.
S
) be the minimal degree surface containing
\tilde{X}
(resp.
X
).
Thus there is
u\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{5})
such that
u(\tilde{S})=S
; cf. Remark
2.5
. Call
\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(S)\cong\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\times\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\subset\operatorname{Aut}(S)
the connected component of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
containing the identity. By Corollary
2.4
there is
v\in\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(S)

such that
v(u(\tilde{X}))=X
. Remark
2.5
provides the existence of
w\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{5})
such that
w(S)=S
and
w_{|S}=v
. Thus
w\circ u\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{5})
satisfies
w\circ u(\tilde{S})=w(S)=S
and hence

w\circ u(\tilde{X})=w(u(\tilde{X}))=v(u(\tilde{X}))=X.

(c) follows from

\displaystyle\dim\mu(\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{15,18,5})

\displaystyle=\dim\mu(\Lambda_{0})=\dim\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{15,18,5}-\dim\mu^{-1}(\mu(X))

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{15,18,5}-\dim\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{5})=68-35=33.

∎

6.2.
An epilogue, a remark on the larger-than-expected components

Throughout this paper we dealt with several Hilbert schemes with larger-than-expected components.
Regarding the existence (or non-existence) of such components in general, the following conjecture is stated in
[
20
, p. 142]
:

Conjecture:
(i) If
\mathcal{H}
is any component of the Hilbert scheme
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}
such that
the image of the rational map
\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{g}
has codimention
g-4
or less, then

\dim\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{X}{(d,g.r)}.

(ii) Right after that, it is also remarked in
[
20
, p. 143]
that
“to be honest, the available evidence suggests that the existence of a number
\beta(g)
tending linearly to
\infty
with
g
, such that any such component
\mathcal{H}
whose image in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
has codimension
\beta\leq\beta(g)
has the expected dimension; we use the function
g-4
just for simplicity”
.

Our results discussed in this paper suggest that the function
\beta(g)=g-4
may need to be replaced with smaller
\beta(g)
.

Remark 6.6
.

(i) For
(d,g,r)=(15,15,5)
,
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}=\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
is irreducible and
\dim\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}=64>\mathcal{X}(d,g,r)=62
by Theorem
4.1
. By Theorem
4.1
,
\operatorname{codim}\mu(\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r})=3\cdot g-3-(\dim\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}-\dim\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{r}))=13>g-4
and hence the family
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}
is
special
with big codimension in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. Also, this does not give an evidence to disprove the conjecture.

(ii) For
(d,g,r)=(15,16,5)
the three components
\Gamma_{i}
(
i=1,2,3
) of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}=\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
have dimension larger than
\mathcal{X}(d,g,r)=60
by Theorem
5.1
. By Proposition
5.18
,
\operatorname{codim}\mu(\Gamma_{1})=12=g-4
, hence providing an evidence to the contrary to the conjecture if
\beta(g)=g-4
.

(iii) For
(d,g,r)=(15,13,5)
,
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{}}_{d,g,r}
is reducible with two components, one with the expected dimension and the other one with more than expected dimension whose image under the moduli map
\mu
has codimension
g-4=\operatorname{codim}\mathcal{M}^{1}_{g,3}=3g-3-(2g+1)
; Proposition
3.2
. Hence the literal statement of the above conjecture turns out to be untrue if one puts
\beta(g)=g-4
. However, elements of this component are not linearly normal. There are other examples of this kind suggesting that
\beta(g)=g-4
is rather too large to ensure the validity of the above conjecture. By
[
11
, Proposition 3.5]
, there exists a component
of the Hilbert scheme with
(d,g,r)=(2g-2-2k,g,r)
– subject to several crude and technical numerical conditions such as
2\leq\frac{g-4}{k-1},\frac{2g+2-2k}{3}-1<r\leq g-2-2k,g>(k-1)^{2}
– dominating
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{g,k}
, whereas
\operatorname{codim}\mathcal{M}^{1}_{g,k}=g+2-2k\leq\beta(g)=g-4
. Again, curves in this component are not linearly normal.

(iv) If one focus on components consisting of linearly normal curves,
\beta(g)=g-5
would be a
choice suggested by the example in (ii). The authors do not know of any example of a component with dimension greater-than-expected
such that the image under the moduli map
\mu
has codimension at most
g-5
.

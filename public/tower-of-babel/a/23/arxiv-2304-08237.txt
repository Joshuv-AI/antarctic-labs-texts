Abstract
We study the existence of normalized solutions to the following logarithmic Schrödinger equation − Δ ​ u + λ ​ u = α ​ u ​ log ⁡ u 2 + μ ​ | u | p − 2 ​ u , x ∈ ℝ N , -\Delta u+\lambda u=\alpha u\log u^{2}+\mu|u|^{p-2}u,\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}, under the mass constraint ∫ ℝ N u 2 ​ 𝑑 x = c 2 , \int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x=c^{2}, where α , μ ∈ ℝ \alpha,\mu\in{\mathbb{R}} , N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 , p > 2 p>2 , c > 0 c>0 is a constant, and λ ∈ ℝ \lambda\!\in\!{\mathbb{R}} appears as Lagrange multiplier. Under different assumptions on α , μ , p \alpha,\mu,p and c c , we prove the existence of ground state solution and excited state solution. The asymptotic behavior of the ground state solution as μ → 0 \mu\to 0 is also investigated. Our results including the case α < 0 \alpha<0 or μ < 0 \mu<0 , which is less studied in the literature.

Introduction

In this paper, for prescribed
c>0
, we look for solutions
(\lambda,u)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
satisfying

-\Delta u+\lambda u=\alpha u\log u^{2}+\mu|u|^{p-2}u,\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},

(1.1)

and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x=c^{2},

(1.2)

where
\alpha,\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
2<p\leq 2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
(
2^{*}=\infty
if
N=1,2
).
Equation (
1.1
) is closely related to the following
time-dependent Schrödinger equation

-i\partial_{t}\Phi-\Delta\Phi-\alpha\Phi\log|\Phi|^{2}-\mu|\Phi|^{p-2}\Phi=0\quad\ \text{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{N}.\\

(1.3)

Equation (
1.3
) admit plenty of applications related to quantum mechanics,
quantum optics, nuclear physics, transport and diffusion phenomena, open quantum
systems, effective quantum gravity, theory of superfluidity and Bose-Einstein condensation,
see
[

,

,

,

,

and the references therein.

Normalized solutions for equation (

) seem to be particularly meaningful from the physical
viewpoint, as pointed out in
[
32
,
33
]
, these solutions often offer a good insight
of the dynamical properties of the stationary solutions for the nonlinear Schrödinger
equation (
1.3
), such as stability and instability
[
8
,
15
]
.

In the past three decades, the existence and multiplicity of normalized solutions for Schrödinger equation has been investigated by many authors, see

-\Delta u+\lambda u=|u|^{q-2}u+\mu|u|^{p-2}u,\ \ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},

(1.4)

where

. If

In the case

Inspired by the above results, an interesting question is that whether one can find normalized solutions for equation (
1.1
)–(
1.2
). The goal of the present paper
is to give an affirmative answer. Although equation (
1.1
) has, at least formally, a variational structure related to the energy functional

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}+(\lambda+\alpha)u^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u^{2}\log u^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\mu}{p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x,

this energy functional is not well defined on the natural Sobolev space
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. In
[
13
]
, the idea is to work on the Banach space

W=\Big\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})~|~\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u^{2}|\log u^{2}|\mathrm{d}x<\infty\Big\},

(1.5)

which is equipped the norm given by

\|u\|_{W}=\|u\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+\inf\Big\{k>0~|~\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}A(k^{-1}|u|)\mathrm{d}x\leq 1\Big\},

where
A(s)=-s^{2}\log s^{2}
on
[0,e^{-3}]
and
A(s)=3s^{2}+4e^{-3}s-e^{-6}
on
[e^{-3},\infty)
. In fact, by
[
13
, Proposition 2.7]
,

I_{\lambda}:W\to{\mathbb{R}}
is well defined and
\mathcal{C}^{1}
smooth.

Solutions to equations (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) can be obtained by finding critical points of the energy functional

I(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\alpha u^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u^{2}\log u^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\mu}{p}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x

under the constraint

S(c):=\Big\{u\in W~\big|~\|u\|_{2}=c\Big\}.

Before introducing our main results, we give some definitions (see also
[
7
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
u_{0}
is a ground state solution for equation (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) on
S(c)
if

dI|_{S(c)}(u_{0})=0\quad\hbox{ and }\quad I(u_{0})=\inf\Big\{I(u)\hbox{ s.t. }dI|_{S(c)}(u)=0\ \hbox{and}\ u\in S(c)\Big\}.

We say that
v_{0}
is an excited state solution for (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) on
S(c)
if

dI|_{S(c)}(v_{0})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad I(v_{0})>\inf\Big\{I(u)\hbox{ s.t. }dI|_{S(c)}(u)=0\ \text{and}\ u\in S(c)\Big\}.

For
2<p<2^{*}
, the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (see
[
40
]
) is

\|u\|_{p}\leq C(N,p)\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\gamma_{p}}\|u\|_{2}^{1-\gamma_{p}},\quad\text{for each}\ \ u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),

(1.6)

where
C(N,p)
is the best constant in the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality,
\gamma_{p}=\frac{N(p-2)}{2p}
.

We shall mainly concentrate here on the cases
\alpha=\pm 1
and
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
. For
\alpha=0
, we refer the reader to
[
32
,
33
]
.
Our main results of the paper can be stated as follows. We first consider the existence of global minima on
S(c)
.
Define

m(c):=\inf_{u\in S(c)}I(u).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha=1
. Suppose
N\geq 2
,
c>0
and that one of the following three conditions holds

\displaystyle(i)~\mu\leq 0\ \text{and}\ p>2;\qquad(ii)~\mu>0\ \text{and}\ 2<p<2+\frac{4}{N};

\displaystyle(iii)~\mu>0,~p=\bar{p}:=2+\frac{4}{N},\ \ \text{and}\ \ c<\Big(\frac{N+2}{\mu N}\Big)^{\frac{N}{4}}\Big(\frac{1}{C(N,\bar{p})}\Big)^{\frac{N+2}{2}};

where
C(N,\bar{p})
is the best constant of (
1.6
) with
p=\bar{p}
, then
the infimum
m(c)
admits a minimizer
\tilde{u}\in S(c)
,
which is positive, radially symmetric and decreasing in
r=|x|
.
Moreover,
\tilde{u}
is a ground state of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

If
2+\frac{4}{N}<p\leq 2^{*}
, we can easily verify
\inf\limits_{u\in S(c)}I(u)=-\infty
. In spirit of
[
6
]
, we introduce the following Pohozaev manifold

\mathcal{P}_{c}=\big\{u\in S(c)~|~P(u)=0\big\},

where

\displaystyle P(u)=:\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}-\mu\gamma_{p}\|u\|^{p}_{p}-\frac{N}{2}\alpha c^{2},

(1.7)

with
\gamma_{p}=\frac{N(p-2)}{2p}
.
If
u\in W
is a weak solution of (
1.1
), then the Pohozaev identity
P(u)=0
holds.
For
u\in S(c)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

s\star u(x):=e^{\frac{N}{2}s}u\left(e^{s}x\right),~~~~\mbox{for}~\mbox{a.e.}~x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The Pohozaev manifold
\mathcal{P}_{c}
is quite closely related to the fiber map

\displaystyle\Psi_{u}(s):=I(s\star u)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2s}}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\alpha\frac{Ns}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u^{2}\log u^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{e^{p\gamma_{p}s}}{p}\mu\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x.

For
u\in S(c)
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

\Psi^{\prime}_{u}(s)=P\big(s\star u\big),

where
P
is defined by (
1.7
).
We shall see that the critical points of
\Psi_{u}(s)
allow us to project a function on
\mathcal{P}_{c}
. Thus, the monotonicity and convexity properties of
\Psi_{u}(s)
strongly affect the structure of
\mathcal{P}_{c}
.
Generally,
\mathcal{P}_{c}
can be divided into the disjoint union
\mathcal{P}_{c}=\mathcal{P}_{c}^{+}\cup\mathcal{P}_{c}^{0}\cup\mathcal{P}_{c}^{-}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{c}^{+}

\displaystyle:=\big\{u\in S(c)~|~\Psi_{u}^{\prime}(0)=0,\ \Psi_{u}^{\prime\prime}(0)>0\big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{c}^{0}

\displaystyle:=\big\{u\in S(c)~|~\Psi_{u}^{\prime}(0)=0,\ \Psi_{u}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0\big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{c}^{-}

\displaystyle:=\big\{u\in S(c)~|~\Psi_{u}^{\prime}(0)=0,\ \Psi_{u}^{\prime\prime}(0)<0\big\}.

Define

m^{+}(c):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{P}^{+}_{c}}I(u),\qquad\ m^{-}(c):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{P}^{-}_{c}}I(u)

and

c_{0}:=\Big[\frac{p2^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}}}{\mu\big(p\gamma_{p}\big)^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}+2}{2}}}\Big(\frac{p\gamma_{p}-2}{N}\Big)^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}-2}{2}}C^{-p}(N,p)\Big]^{\frac{1}{p-2}}.

If
\alpha=1
,
\mu>0
,
2+\frac{4}{N}<p<2^{*}
, then the functional

I|_{S(c)}
present a convex-concave geometry structure for
c>0
small.
We thus show that (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) admits two solutions

and

restricted to

(2.1)

Denote

Lemma 2.1
.

[

(i)

Lemma 2.2
.

[

The following facts holds

(i)

m_{c}:=\inf_{u\in V_{k_{0}}}I(u)=m^{+}(c),

where

is defined in (

Proof.

From the definition of

\mathcal{P}^{+}_{c}\subset V_{k_{0}}
, then

On the other hand, if

it follows that

∎

Now, we define

\mathcal{P}^{+}_{r,c}:=\mathcal{P}^{+}_{c}\bigcap H^{1}_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathcal{P}^{-}_{r,c}:=\mathcal{P}^{-}_{c}\bigcap H^{1}_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}).

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mu>0
and
2+\frac{4}{N}<p<2^{*}
. If
0<c<c_{0}
, then

Moreover,
m^{+}(c)
,
m^{-}(c)
are achieved by some Schwarz symmetric functions.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{P}^{+}_{r,c}\subset\mathcal{P}^{+}_{c}

\mathcal{P}^{-}_{r,c}\subset\mathcal{P}^{-}_{c}

\inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{P}^{+}_{r,c}}I(u)\geq\inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{P}^{+}_{c}}I(u)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{P}^{-}_{r,c}}I(u)\geq\inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{P}^{-}_{c}}I(u).

On the other hand, by Lemma
4.3
, for each

For

|u|

\inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{P}^{-}_{r,c}}I(u)\leq\inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{P}^{-}_{c}}I(u)

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in\mathcal{P}^{+}_{c}}I(u)

Similarly, we can prove that there exists

(5.2)

5.1
Existence of a local minima on
S(c)

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that
\mu>0
,
c>0
and
p=2^{*}
. If

and
\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}=k_{0}
, then

. Moreover, if

and

\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}\neq k_{0}

Proof.

Since

By the Sobolev inequality (
5.1
), it is easy to verify that

\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}\leq\mu\mathcal{S}^{-\frac{2^{*}}{2}}\|\nabla u\|^{2^{*}}_{2}+\frac{N}{2}c^{2}.

By using the fact

that

it follows that

.
Therefore, we deduce that if
P(u)\leq 0
and

, then

.
∎

Define

(5.3)

where

is defined by (

(5.4)

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mu>0
and

,

. Suppose that

Then

is achieved by some

Proof.

Let

, i.e.

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}I(u_{n})=m_{c}\ \ \text{and}\ \ dI|_{S(c)}(u_{n})\to 0\ \ \text{as}\ \ n\to\infty.

Since

\displaystyle I(u_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{n}\|^{2}_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u^{2}_{n}\log u^{2}_{n}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\mu}{2^{*}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|u_{n}|^{2^{*}}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}c^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{n}\|^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}A(|u_{n}|)\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}B(|u_{n}|)\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\mu}{2^{*}}\mathcal{S}^{-\frac{2^{*}}{2}}k^{\frac{2^{*}}{2}}_{0},

4.7
, we can show that

is bounded in
W_{r}
, then
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
W_{r}

Therefore,

.
According to the proof in Lemma

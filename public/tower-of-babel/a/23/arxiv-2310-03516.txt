The discrete horospherical p p -Minkowski problem in hyperbolic space
Haizhong Li, Yao Wan, Botong Xu

Abstract
. In [ 19 ] , the first author and the third author introduced and studied the horospherical p p -Minkowski problem for smooth horospherically convex domains in hyperbolic space. In this paper, we introduce and solve the discrete horospherical p p -Minkowski problem in hyperbolic space for all p ∈ ( − ∞ , + ∞ ) p\in(-\infty,+\infty) when the given measure is even on the unit sphere.

1.
Introduction

A central problem in the Brunn-Minkowski theory in Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is the Minkowski problem which asks if a given Borel measure
\mu
on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
arises as the surface area measure of a convex body. The existence of the solution to this problem for Borel measures was given by Alexandrov
[
1
]
and independently by Fenchel and Jessen
[
12
]
. The solution is unique up to translation, and its regularity was studied by Lewy
[
17
]
, Nirenberg
[
26
]
, Cheng-Yau
[
9
]
, Pogorelov
[
27
]
, and Caffarelli
[
7
]
. When the given measure
\mu
is discrete, the following discrete Minkowski problem can be understood as prescribing the surface areas of facets of a polytope, and the problem was solved by Minkowski
[
24
,
25
]
himself.

Discrete Minkowski problem in Euclidean space.

Let
\mu
be a discrete measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Find necessary and sufficient conditions on
\mu
so that there exists a convex polytope
P
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
whose surface area measure with respect to the Gauss map is the given measure
\mu
.

It is a natural question to ask how to propose the extension of the above prescribed discrete surface area measure problem in hyperbolic space. In this paper, we substitute the convexity in Euclidean space by the horospherical convexity (or h-convexity for short) in hyperbolic space, which is widely studied in hyperbolic geometry. The polytopes in Euclidean space are given by the intersections of finite half spaces. Similarly, we call a closed domain in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
an
h-convex polytope
if it is given by the intersection of finite closed horo-balls (see section
2
for more details). Now we ask the following problem, which can be understood as prescribing the surface areas of facets of an h-convex polytope.

(
Prescribed discrete horospherical surface area measure problem in hyperbolic space

\mu

\mathbb{S}^{n}

P
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
whose horospherical surface area measure with respect to the horospherical Gauss map is a multiple of the given measure

The
L_{p}
Minkowski problem in Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
was posted by Lutwak
[
23
]
, where the case
p=1
is the classical Minkowski problem. The
L_{p}
Minkowski problem asks to characterize the
L_{p}
surface area measure of a convex body, and it contains the logarithmic Minkowski problem (
p=0
) and the centro-affine Minkowski problem (
p=-n-1
) as special cases. The
L_{p}
Minkowski problem has been extensively studied in recent decades, see e.g.
[
23
,
22
,
8
,
21
,
10
,
4
,
5
,
15
]
; see also a recent survey by Böröczky
[
6
]
.

The
L_{p}
Minkowski problem for polytopes are of great importance. One reason is that the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem (
p\geq 1
) for Borel measures can be solved by an approximation argument by first solving the polytopal case, see e.g.
[
14
,
28
]
. Note that if a polytope
P
contains the origin in its interior with
N
facets whose outward unit normals are
\{\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and if the facet with outward unit normal
\textbf{e}_{i}
has area
a_{i}
and distance from the origin
u(P,\textbf{e}_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,N
. Then the
L_{p}
surface area measure of
P
is defined by

S_{p}(P,\cdot)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}u(P,\textbf{e}_{i})^{1-p}a_{i}\delta_{\textbf{e}_{i}}(\cdot),

where
\delta_{\textbf{e}_{i}}
denotes the delta measure concentrated at the point
\textbf{e}_{i}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. For a given discrete measure
\mu
, the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem can be stated in the following way:

Discrete
L_{p}
Minkowski problem in Euclidean space.

Let
\mu
be a discrete measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Find necessary and sufficient conditions on
\mu
so that there exists a convex polytope
P
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
whose
L_{p}
surface area measure
S_{p}(P,\cdot)
is the given measure
\mu
.

The discrete
L_{p}
Minkowski problem in Euclidean space was treated by, e.g., Hug, Lutwak, Yang and Zhang
[
14
]
for
p>1
, Böröczky, Hegedűs and Zhu
[
3
]
for
p=0
, and Zhu
[
31
,
32
,
30
,
33
]
for
p<1
.

In
[
19
]
, the first author and the third author introduced the horospherical
p
-surface area measures of smooth uniformly h-convex bounded domains in hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and have solved the corresponding horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem when the given measure is even on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Here a Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is said to be
even
if
\mu(\omega)=\mu(-\omega)
for any Borel subset
\omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
. In
[
18
]
, the first author and the second author investigated the Christoffel problem in the hyperbolic plane (i.e. the horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem in the case
n=1
and
p=-1
) and presented existence results without the evenness assumption on
\mu
. In the smooth category, the horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem in hyperbolic space can be stated in the following way:

Horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem in hyperbolic space.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Find necessary and sufficient conditions on
\mu
so that there exists a h-convex body
K
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
whose horospherical
p
-surface area measure
S_{p}(K,\cdot)
with respect to the horospherical Gauss map is a multiple of the given measure
\mu
.

The hyperboloid model of the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
in the Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
is given by
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}=\{X=(\textbf{x},x_{n+2})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}:\ X\cdot X=-1,\ x_{n+2}>0,\ \textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\}
. If a h-convex polytope
P
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
contains the origin
O=(\textbf{0},1)
in its interior and has
N
facets with horospherical normals
\{\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where each facet with horospherical normal
\textbf{e}_{i}
has area
a_{i}
and horospherical support function
u(P,\textbf{e}_{i})
, then we define the horospherical
p
-surface area measure of
P
by

S_{p}(P,\cdot)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}e^{-pu(P,\textbf{e}_{i})}a_{i}\delta_{\textbf{e}_{i}}(\cdot).

For a given discrete measure
\mu
, the horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem can be stated as follows:

Problem 1.2

(
Discrete horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem in hyperbolic space
)
.

Let
\mu
be a discrete measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Find necessary and sufficient conditions on
\mu
so that there exists a h-convex polytope
P
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
whose horospherical
p
-surface area measure
S_{p}(P,\cdot)
is a multiple of the given measure
\mu
.

When
p=0
, Problem
1.2
is reduced to Problem
1.1
. When
p=-n
, we call it the
discrete horospherical Minkowski problem
.

In this paper, we consider Problem
1.2
in the case that
\mu
is even on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Our main result is the following Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu
be an even discrete measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then there exists an origin-symmetric h-convex polytope
P
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, such that the measure
\mu
is a multiple of its horospherical
p
-surface area measure
S_{p}(P,\cdot)
.

The approach to prove Theorem
1.1
can be outlined as follows. First, we prove a selection theorem for h-convex polytopes. Additionally, we derive a variational formula for the volume functional of a h-convex polytope. Next, we divide Problem
1.2
into two cases:
p\geq 0
and
p<0
, and construct constrained optimization problems for each case such that their critical points are the desired solutions. Finally, by using the Lagrange multiplier method, the selection theorem and the variational formula proved in the first step, we prove the existence of solutions for the above two optimization problems.

The paper is organized as follows.
In section
2
, we provide some basic definitions and properties about h-convex bodies in hyperbolic space.
In section
3
, we study the Hausdorff metric on h-convex bodies and prove a selection theorem for h-convex polytopes. In section
4
, we study horospherical Wulff shape and derive a variational formula of volume functional of h-convex polytopes. In section
5
, we prove a boundedness lemma for h-convex bodies. In section
6
, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
by studying two constrained optimization problems.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some definitions and properties of horospherically convex bodies in hyperbolic space. We refer to
[
2
,
11
,
19
]
for details.

Consider the Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}
with canonical coordinates
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+2})
and the Lorentzian metric

X\cdot Y=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}y_{i}-x_{n+2}y_{n+2}.

The hyperbolic space is then realized in the Minkowski space as the hyperboloid

\mathbb{H}^{n+1}=\{X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+2})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}:\ X\cdot X=-1,\ x_{n+2}>0\}.

Denote by
\hat{B}_{r}(X)
the geodesic ball of radius
r
centered at
X
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, and denote by
d(X,Y)
the geodesic distance between
X
and
Y
on
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. We call
O=(\textbf{0},1)
the origin of the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
.

An important class of totally umbilical hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is the so-called
horosphere
. Specifically, the horospheres in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
are the hypersurfaces with principal curvatures equal to 1 everywhere.

Now we begin to parameterize the set of horospheres. Let
\Sigma
be a horosphere in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, denote by
\nu
the outward unit normal of
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, and let
c=X-\nu
be a nonzero vector-valued function defined on
\Sigma
. For any tangent vector
v\in T_{X}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, we then have

\nabla_{v}c=\nabla_{v}(X-\nu)=0,

which implies that
c
is constant along
\Sigma
. Moreover, by

X\cdot c=X\cdot(X-\nu)=-1,

we can describe the horosphere
\Sigma
as the intersection of the null hyperplane
\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1,1}:\ X\cdot c=-1\}
and the hyperboloid
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Since
c\cdot c=0
, we can assume
c=\lambda(\textbf{e},1)
for some
\textbf{e}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, then

-1=X\cdot c=\lambda X\cdot(\textbf{e},1).

On the other hand, since

X\cdot(\textbf{e},1)=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})\cdot\textbf{e}-x_{n+2}\leq\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-x_{n+2}<0,

we have
\lambda>0
, and hence we can define
s=-\log\lambda
. Consequently, the set of horospheres in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
can be parameterized by
(\textbf{e},s)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Denote by
H_{\textbf{e}}(s)
the horosphere

(2.1)

H_{\textbf{e}}(s)=\{X\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}:\ X\cdot(\textbf{e},1)=-e^{s}\},

where
e
is called the center of
H_{\textbf{e}}(s)
.
Denote by
B_{\textbf{e}}(s)
the
horo-ball
enclosed by
H_{\textbf{e}}(s)
, i.e.

(2.2)

B_{\textbf{e}}(s)=\{X\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}:\ 0>X\cdot(\textbf{e},1)>-e^{s}\}.

Now we show that the parameter
s
in
H_{\textbf{e}}(s)
represents the signed geodesic distance from
O
to
H_{\textbf{e}}(s)
. In fact, for any
X\in H_{\textbf{e}}(s)
, there exist
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
r\geq 0
such that
X=(\sinh r\theta,\cosh r)
. Here
r
represents the geodesic distance between
X
and
O
. Then

-e^{r}\leq-e^{s}=X\cdot(\textbf{e},1)=\sinh r\theta\cdot\textbf{e}-\cosh r\leq-e^{-r},

thus the geodesic distance between
H_{\textbf{e}}(s)
and
O
is
|s|
. The sign of
s
determines whether the origin
O
is inside
B_{\textbf{e}}(s)
.

In addition to the hyperboloid model
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, we will also use the Poincaré ball model
(\mathbb{B}^{n+1},g_{B})
and the upper half-space model
(\mathbb{U}^{n+1},g_{U})
of the hyperbolic space in the context.

The stereographic projection
\pi
with respect to
(\textbf{0},-1)
maps the hyperboloid model
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
to the Poincaré ball model
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
, which is given by

\pi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1},x_{n+2})=\frac{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})}{1+x_{n+2}}.

Conversely, for
Y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n+1})\in\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
, we have

\pi^{-1}(Y)=\left(\frac{2Y}{1-|Y|^{2}},\frac{1+|Y|^{2}}{1-|Y|^{2}}\right).

This together with (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) then gives

\displaystyle\pi\left(H_{\textbf{e}}(s)\right)=

\displaystyle\left\{Y\in\mathbb{B}^{n+1}:\ \left|Y-\frac{\textbf{e}}{1+e^{s}}\right|=\frac{e^{s}}{1+e^{s}}\right\},

\displaystyle\pi\left(B_{\textbf{e}}(s)\right)=

\displaystyle\left\{Y\in\mathbb{B}^{n+1}:\ \left|Y-\frac{\textbf{e}}{1+e^{s}}\right|<\frac{e^{s}}{1+e^{s}}\right\}.

Thus, in the Poincaré ball model
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
, the horosphere
H_{\textbf{e}}(s)
is a sphere tangent to
\textbf{e}\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
. So the horospheres can be viewed as ‘spheres’ centered at the infinity
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
of the hyperbolic space.

The upper half-space model
(\mathbb{U}^{n+1},g_{U})
of the hyperbolic space is given by

\mathbb{U}^{n+1}=\{Y=(\textbf{y},y_{n+1}):\ \textbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ y_{n+1}>0\},\quad g_{U}=\frac{1}{y_{n+1}^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}dy_{i}^{2}.

Note that
\partial\mathbb{U}^{n+1}=(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\})\cup\{\infty\}
and
\mathbb{S}^{n}
can be viewed as identical through an isometry between
(\mathbb{U}^{n+1},g_{U})
and
(\mathbb{B}^{n+1},g_{B})
. If
\textbf{e}=\infty\in\partial\mathbb{U}^{n+1}
, then it follows from
g_{U}
that

H_{\textbf{e}}(s)=\{Y\in\mathbb{U}^{n+1}:y_{n+1}=e^{-s}\},\quad B_{\textbf{e}}(s)=\{Y\in\mathbb{U}^{n+1}:y_{n+1}>e^{-s}\}.

If
\textbf{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}
, then the expression for
H_{\textbf{e}}(s)
can be obtained by the Euclidean inversion of
H_{\infty}(s)
with respect to the hemisphere centered at
e
passing through
(\textbf{0},1)
, see e.g. Lopez’s book
[
20
, p.193]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A closed domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is called
horospherically convex
(or
h-convex
for short) if each boundary point
X
of
\partial\Omega
has a supporting horo-ball, i.e. a horo-ball
B
such that
\Omega\subset\overline{B}
and
X\in\partial B
.

The above definition is equivalent to the following condition on
\Omega
: for each pair of points in
\Omega
, all the entire horocycle arcs joining them are also contained in
\Omega
, see e.g.
[
13
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
h-convex body
is a compact h-convex subset of
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
with nonempty interior. Denote by
\mathcal{K}^{h}
the class of h-convex bodies.

Let us introduce notations for two subsets of
\mathcal{K}^{h}
.

•

Denote by
\mathcal{K}_{o}^{h}
the class of h-convex bodies containing the origin
O
in their interiors.

•

Denote by
\mathcal{K}_{e}^{h}
the class of origin-symmetric h-convex bodies.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}^{h}
be a h-convex body, and let
\textbf{e}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Define the
horospherical support function
of
K
in direction
e
by

(2.3)

u(K,\textbf{e})=\inf\{s\in\mathbb{R}:\ K\subset\overline{B}_{\textbf{e}}(s)\}.

Then by (
2.2
),

(2.4)

u(K,\textbf{e})=\sup\{f_{\textbf{e}}(X):\ X\in K\},

where
f_{\textbf{e}}(X)=\log(-X\cdot(\textbf{e},1))
.
Moreover,
B_{\textbf{e}}(u(K,\textbf{e}))
is called the
supporting horo-ball
of
K
in direction
e
.

When
\partial K
is smooth, we define its
horospherical Gauss map

G:\partial K\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
by setting
G(X)=\textbf{e}
for
X\in\partial K
, where
e
satisfies
X-\nu=\lambda(\textbf{e},1)
for some
\lambda>0
. When
\partial K
is not smooth, we call
\textbf{e}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
its
horospherical normal
at
X\in\partial K
if
-(X-X^{\prime})\cdot(\textbf{e},1)\geq 0
for all
X^{\prime}\in K
. Equivalently,
X\in H_{\textbf{e}}(u(K,\textbf{e}))\cap\partial K
and
X^{\prime}\in B_{\textbf{e}}(u(K,\textbf{e}))
for all
X^{\prime}\in K
.

The main objects studied in this paper are the following h-convex polytopes.

Definition 2.3
.

A h-convex body
P
is called a
h-convex polytope
if it can be represented as the intersection of finite closed horo-balls, i.e., there exist
\{\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{m}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
(
m\geq 2
) and
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.5)

P=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{m}\overline{B}_{\textbf{e}_{i}}(u_{i}).

Denote by
\mathcal{P}^{h}
the set of h-convex polytopes.
If
F(P,\textbf{e}_{i}):=P\cap H_{\textbf{e}_{i}}(u_{i})
is
n
-dimensional, then
F(P,\textbf{e}_{i})
is called the
facet of
P
with horospherical normal
\textbf{e}_{i}
.

For convenience, let us introduce some notations for various subsets of
\mathcal{P}^{h}
. Let
\{\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{m}\}
be unit vectors in
\mathbb{S}^{n}\ (m\geq 2)
.

•

Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{o}^{h}
the class of h-convex polytopes containing the origin
O
in their interiors, i.e.
\mathcal{P}_{o}^{h}=\mathcal{P}^{h}\cap\mathcal{K}_{o}^{h}
.

•

Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{e}^{h}
the class of origin-symmetric h-convex polytopes, i.e.
\mathcal{P}_{e}^{h}=\mathcal{P}^{h}\cap\mathcal{K}_{e}^{h}
.

•

Denote by
\mathcal{P}^{h}(\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{m})
the subset of
\mathcal{P}^{h}
such that a h-convex polytope
P\in\mathcal{P}^{h}(\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{m})
if
P
can be represented as

P=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{m}\overline{B}_{\textbf{e}_{i}}(u(P,\textbf{e}_{i})).

Obviously, if
P\in\mathcal{P}^{h}(\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{m})
, then
P
has at most
m
facets, and its horospherical normals are in
\{\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{m}\}
.

•

Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{m}^{h}(\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{m})
the subset of
\mathcal{P}^{h}(\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{m})
such that a h-convex polytope
P\in\mathcal{P}_{m}^{h}(\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{m})
if
P
has exactly
m
facets.

3.
Hausdorff metric on h-convex bodies and a selection theorem

For any
K\in\mathcal{K}^{h}
, denote by
K^{\epsilon}
the outer parallel set of
K
with distance
\epsilon
, i.e.

K^{\epsilon}=\{X\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}:\ d(K,X)\leq\epsilon\}.

The following Proposition
3.1
gives the relationship between the horospherical support function of
K^{\epsilon}
and that of
K
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}^{h}
and
\epsilon>0
. Then the horospherical support function of
K^{\epsilon}
in direction
e
is given by

(3.1)

u(K^{\epsilon},\textbf{e})=u(K,\textbf{e})+\epsilon.

Proof.

We work in the upper half-space model
(\mathbb{U}^{n+1},g_{U})
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that

H_{\textbf{e}}(s)=\{Y\in\mathbb{U}^{n+1}:y_{n+1}=e^{-s}\}.

Taking
s=u(K,\textbf{e})
and
s=u(K,e)+\epsilon
respectively, we have

\displaystyle H_{\textbf{e}}(u(K,\textbf{e}))=

\displaystyle\{Y\in\mathbb{U}^{n+1}:y_{n+1}=e^{-u(K,\textbf{e})}\},

\displaystyle H_{\textbf{e}}(u(K,\textbf{e})+\epsilon)=

\displaystyle\{Y\in\mathbb{U}^{n+1}:y_{n+1}=e^{-u(K,\textbf{e})-\epsilon}\}.

Clearly, the geodesic distance between
H_{\textbf{e}}(u(K,\textbf{e}))
and
H_{\textbf{e}}(u(K,\textbf{e})+\epsilon)
is
\epsilon
. Thus

B_{\textbf{e}}(u(K,\textbf{e}))^{\epsilon}=B_{\textbf{e}}(u(K,\textbf{e})+\epsilon).

Note that the fact
K\subset B_{\textbf{e}}(u(K,\textbf{e}))
induces
K^{\epsilon}\subset B_{\textbf{e}}(u(K,\textbf{e}))^{\epsilon}
.
Then we have

(3.2)

u(K^{\epsilon},\textbf{e})\leq u(K,\textbf{e})+\epsilon.

On the other hand, the definition of
u(K,\textbf{e})
in (
2.3
) implies
\partial K\cap H_{\textbf{e}}(u(K,\textbf{e}))\neq\emptyset
. Then there exists

Recall that the Hausdorff metric between two sets

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\{K_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{K}_{o}^{h}
be a sequence of h-convex bodies, and let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{h}
. The following statements are equivalent,

(i)

K_{i}
converges to
K
in Hausdorff metric as
i\to\infty

(ii)

u(K_{i},\cdot)
converges uniformly on

as
i\to\infty

(iii)

\rho(K_{i},\cdot)
converges uniformly on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
to
\rho(K,\cdot)
as
i\to\infty
.

Proof.

The equivalence between the statements (i) and (ii) follows from (
3.5
). It suffices to show that statements (i) and (iii) are equivalent.

We first prove that statement (i) implies statement (iii). Let
\{K_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{K}_{o}^{h}
be a sequence of h-convex bodies that converges to
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}^{h}
in Hausdorff metric as
i\to\infty
. For any given
0<\epsilon<\min\left\{1,\frac{r(K)}{2}\right\}
, we have
d_{\mathcal{H}}(K_{i},K)<\epsilon
, i.e.
K_{i}\subset K^{\epsilon}
and
K\subset K_{i}^{\epsilon}
for large
i
. This together with (
3.8
) gives

(3.15)

\displaystyle R(K_{i})\leq R(K^{\epsilon})=R(K)+\epsilon\leq R(K)+1,

\displaystyle r(K_{i})=r(K_{i}^{\epsilon})-\epsilon\geq r(K)-\epsilon\geq\frac{r(K)}{2}.

Define the function
C:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

C(r,R)=\frac{2e^{2R}}{re^{r}}.

Clearly, the function
C(r,R)
is monotone increasing in
R
and decreasing in
r
. Using (
3.9
) and (
3.15
), for any
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
we have

\displaystyle\rho(K_{i},\theta)\leq\rho(K^{\epsilon},\theta)\leq\rho(K,\theta)+C(r(K),R(K))\epsilon,

and

\displaystyle\rho(K,\theta)\leq\rho(K_{i}^{\epsilon},\theta)\leq\rho(K_{i},\theta)+C\left(r(K_{i}),R(K_{i})\right)\epsilon\leq\rho(K_{i},\theta)+C\left(\frac{r(K)}{2},R(K)+1\right)\epsilon.

Taking
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
in the above two inequalities, we have that
|\rho(K_{i},\cdot)-\rho(K,\cdot)|
converges uniformly to
0
as
i\to\infty
, which is the statement (iii). Thus we obtain that statement (i) implies statement (iii).

Next, we prove that statement (iii) implies statement (i). Let
\{K_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{K}_{o}^{h}
be a sequence of h-convex bodies such that
\rho(K_{i},\cdot)
converges uniformly to
\rho(K,\cdot)
as
i\to\infty
. Given any
\epsilon>0
, we have
|\rho(K_{i},\theta)-\rho(K,\theta)|<\epsilon
for each
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
by choosing
i
large enough. Denote by
X=(\sinh\rho(K,\theta)\theta,\cosh\rho(K,\theta))
and
X_{i}=(\sinh\rho(K_{i},\theta)\theta,\cosh\rho(K_{i},\theta))
the boundary points in direction
\theta
of
K
and
K_{i}
, respectively.
Then

\displaystyle d(X,X_{i})=|\rho(K_{i},\theta)-\rho(K,\theta)|<\epsilon.

Hence we have
\partial K_{i}\subset K^{\epsilon}
and
\partial K\subset K_{i}^{\epsilon}
, which induces
d_{\mathcal{H}}(K_{i},K)<\epsilon
by (
3.4
). Therefore,
K_{i}
converges to
K
in Hausdorff metric as
i\to\infty
, which is the statement (i). Thus we obtain that statement (iii) implies statement (i).

We complete the proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Definition 3.7
.

We say that a sequence of h-convex bodies
\{K_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is
bounded
if they are all contained in a geodesic ball of fixed radius, i.e., there exists a constant
M>0
such that

\max\limits_{X\in K_{i}}d(O,X)\leq M

holds for all
i\geq 1
.

In Euclidean space, the Blaschke selection theorem says that every bounded sequence of compact convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
has a subsequence that converges to a compact convex set.
Now we prove a selection theorem in hyperbolic space.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\{K_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a bounded sequence of h-convex bodies in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Then there exists a subsequence
\{K_{i_{k}}\}
such that
u(K_{i_{k}},\cdot)
converges uniformly to a continuous function.

Moreover, suppose that
\{K_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence of h-convex polytopes in
\mathcal{P}_{o}^{h}(\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{m})
given by
K_{i}=\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{m}\overline{B}_{\textbf{e}_{j}}(x_{i,j})
, where
\{x_{i,j}\}
has a positive uniform lower bound for all
i,j
. Then there exists a subsequence
\{K_{i_{k}}\}
that converges to a h-convex polytope
K\in\mathcal{P}_{o}^{h}(\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{m})
in Hausdorff metric.

Proof.

Since the sequence
\{K_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is bounded, there exists a constant
M>0
such that
\max\limits_{X\in K_{i}}d(O,X)\leq M
for all
i\geq 1
.

First, we show that
\{u(K_{i},\cdot)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly bounded. Fix some
i\geq 1
.
Note that each
X\in K_{i}
can be represented as
X=(\sinh r_{X}\theta_{X},\cosh r_{X})
, where
r_{X}=d(O,X)\leq M
and
\theta_{X}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. By (
2.4
), for any
\textbf{e}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
we have

\displaystyle u(K_{i},\textbf{e})=\sup\limits_{X\in K_{i}}\log(-X\cdot(\textbf{e},1))=\sup\limits_{X\in K_{i}}\log(\cosh r_{X}-\sinh r_{X}\theta_{X}\cdot\textbf{e}).

Then

\displaystyle u(K_{i},\textbf{e})\leq\sup\limits_{X\in K_{i}}\log(\cosh r_{X}+\sinh r_{X})=\sup\limits_{X\in K_{i}}r_{X}\leq M,

and

\displaystyle u(K_{i},\textbf{e})\geq\sup\limits_{X\in K_{i}}\log(\cosh r_{X}-\sinh r_{X})=-\inf\limits_{X\in K_{i}}r_{X}\geq-M.

This implies that
\{u(K_{i},\cdot)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{S}^{n}

Next, we show that
\{u(K_{i},\cdot)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is equicontinuous. For any integer

and

\textbf{e}_{1}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
,

For any

It follows from (

) and (

\displaystyle u(K_{i},\textbf{e}_{1})-u(K_{i},\textbf{e}_{2})\leq

\displaystyle\log(-X_{i}\cdot(\textbf{e}_{1},1))-\log(-X_{i}\cdot(\textbf{e}_{2},1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\frac{\cosh r_{i}-\sinh r_{i}\theta_{i}\cdot\textbf{e}_{1}}{\cosh r_{i}-\sinh r_{i}\theta_{i}\cdot\textbf{e}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{0,\ \frac{\sinh r_{i}\theta_{i}\cdot(\textbf{e}_{2}-\textbf{e}_{1})}{\cosh r_{i}-\sinh r_{i}\theta_{i}\cdot\textbf{e}_{2}}\right\}

In addition, if
K_{i}

\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{m}\overline{B}_{\textbf{e}_{j}}(x_{i,j})

Definition 3.9
.

The
h-convex hull
of a set
A
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is the intersection of all h-convex sets containing
A
.

It is clear that the h-convex hull of
A
contains all horocycle arcs joining points in
A
, and the h-convex hull of finite points in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
is a h-convex polytope. However, the h-convex hull of finite points in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is not a h-convex polytope when
n\geq 2
, since there exist exactly two horocycle arcs joining two points in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, and there exist infinitely many horocycle arcs joining two points in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
for
n\geq 2
.

Using a similar argument as the case in Euclidean space, we note that a h-convex body in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
can be approximated by a sequence of h-convex polytopes.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
K
be a h-convex body in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Then there exists a sequence of h-convex polytopes
{P_{i}}
such that
d_{\mathcal{H}}(K,P_{i})\to 0
as
i\to\infty
.

Proof.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}\in K
such that the geodesic balls
\{\hat{B}_{\epsilon}(X_{i})\}
cover
K
. Denote by
P_{\epsilon}
the h-convex hull of
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, and by
(P_{\epsilon})^{\epsilon}
the outer parallel set of
P_{\epsilon}
with distance
\epsilon
. Since
(P_{\epsilon})^{\epsilon}
is a h-convex body containing all
\hat{B}_{\epsilon}(X_{i})
, then
(P_{\epsilon})^{\epsilon}
contains
K
. Therefore, the h-convex polytope
P_{\epsilon}
satisfies
P_{\epsilon}\subset K\subset(P_{\epsilon})^{\epsilon}
and
d_{\mathcal{H}}(K,P_{\epsilon})\leq\epsilon
. Then Theorem
3.10
follows by taking
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
.

N\geq 2
), and let
\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{N}
be unit vectors on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. For convenience, we define

\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}=\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\ x_{i}\geq 0,\ 1\leq i\leq m\}.

From now on, we always denote
x_{m+i}=x_{i}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}
and
1\leq i\leq m
.

Recall that a discrete measure
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\delta_{\textbf{e}_{i}}
defined on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is called even if
\mu(\omega)=\mu(-\omega)
for any Borel set
\omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
, which is equivalent to (up to a permutation)

N=2m,\ \textbf{e}_{i}=-\textbf{e}_{m+i},\ a_{i}=a_{m+i},\quad\forall\ 1\leq i\leq m.

Given any
x\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}
and unit vectors
\{\textbf{e}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2m}
satisfying
\textbf{e}_{i}=-\textbf{e}_{m+i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
, we can construct an origin-symmetric h-convex polytope
P(x)
in
\mathcal{P}^{h}(\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{2m})
by

(6.1)

P(x):=P(\{\textbf{e}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2m},(x,x))=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{2m}\overline{B}_{\textbf{e}_{i}}(x_{i})=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left(\overline{B}_{\textbf{e}_{i}}(x_{i})\cap\overline{B}_{\textbf{e}_{m+i}}(x_{i})\right).

Note that when
x_{i}=0
for some
1\leq i\leq m
,
P(x)
is exactly the origin point
O
. We define a function
\Phi_{p}:\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
as follows:

(6.2)

\Phi_{p}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2m}a_{i}(e^{px_{i}}-1),\quad\text{if}\ p\neq 0,\\
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2m}a_{i}x_{i},\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{if}\ p=0.\end{array}\right.

6.1.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
for
p\geq 0

In this subsection, we study an optimization problem with natural constraints. Its solution solves the discrete horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem for
p\geq 0
in the even case.

Let
p\geq 0
and
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{2m}a_{i}\delta_{\textbf{e}_{i}}
be an even discrete measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. We consider the following optimization problem

(6.3)

\displaystyle\sup\left\{V(P(x)):\ x\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}\ \text{and}\ \Phi_{p}(x)=1\right\}.

Since
M=\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}:\ \Phi_{p}(x)=1\}
is compact, and the function
V(P(x))
is continuous on
M
, there exists a point
z\in M
such that

(6.4)

V(P(z))=\max\limits_{x\in M}V(P(x)).

It follows from
V(P(z))>0
and the origin-symmetry of
P(z)
that
z
lies the interior of
\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}
. Using the Lagrange multiplier method and the variational formula in Lemma
4.6
, there exists a constant
\lambda
such that

(6.5)

\begin{split}0&=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right|_{x=z}\left[V(P(x))-\lambda\left(\Phi_{p}(x)-1\right)\right]\\
&=S({P(z)},\textbf{e}_{i})+S({P(z)},\textbf{e}_{m+i})-\lambda a_{i}e^{pz_{i}}-\lambda a_{m+i}e^{pz_{m+i}},\quad i=1,\ldots,m.\end{split}

Using
a_{i}=a_{m+i},\ z_{i}=z_{m+i}
and
S({P(z)},\textbf{e}_{i})=S({P(z)},\textbf{e}_{m+i})
, we obtain

(6.6)

S({P(z)},\textbf{e}_{i})=\lambda a_{i}e^{pz_{i}},\quad i=1,\ldots,m,

and

(6.7)

\lambda=\frac{\sum S({P(z)},\textbf{e}_{i})}{\sum a_{i}e^{pz_{i}}}=\frac{S(P(z))}{\sum a_{i}e^{pz_{i}}}>0.

We can conclude that
S({P(z)},\textbf{e}_{i})>0
and then
u(P(z),\textbf{e}_{i})=z_{i}
. Consequently,

(6.8)

\begin{split}\mu&=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2m}a_{i}\delta_{\textbf{e}_{i}}(\cdot)\\
&=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2m}e^{-pu(P(z),\textbf{e}_{i})}S({P(z)},\textbf{e}_{i})\delta_{\textbf{e}_{i}}(\cdot)\\
&=\frac{1}{\lambda}S_{p}(P(z),\cdot).\end{split}

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
for
p\geq 0
.

Remark 6.1
.

In particular, the case
p=0
of Theorem
1.1
corresponds to the prescribed discrete horospherical surface area measure problem. Namely, there exists an origin-symmetric h-convex polytope
P
that has precisely
\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{2m}
as its horospherical normals, and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{2m}
are multiples of corresponding areas of the facets of
P
.

Remark 6.2
.

For the case
p<0
, the corresponding optimization problem might be

(6.9)

\sup\left\{V(P(x)):\ x\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}\ \text{and}\ \Phi_{p}(x)=1\right\}

or

(6.10)

\inf\left\{V(P(x)):\ x\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}\ \text{and}\ \Phi_{p}(x)=1\right\}.

However, these problems are not suitable for obtaining solutions to the discrete horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem for
p<0
in the even case for the following reasons:

(i)

Since the set
M=\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}:\ \Phi_{p}(x)=1\}
is not bounded when
p<0
, the elements in
M
may not attain the supremum in the problem (
6.9
).

(ii)

For the infimum in the problem (
6.10
), we can choose some values
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{2m}\}
such that there exists
z\in M
with
z_{1}=0
, then
\inf_{x\in M}V(P(x))=V(P(z))=0
. In this case,
z
is on the boundary of
\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}
and the variational argument fails.

6.2.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
for
p<0

In this subsection, we study the discrete horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem for
p<0
in the even case.

Definition 6.3
.

A vector
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{2m})\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2m}
is called
admissible
if the horospherical Wulff shape associated with
(\{\textbf{e}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2m},u)
satisfies

(6.11)

\displaystyle u(P(\{\textbf{e}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2m},u),\textbf{e}_{i})=u_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq 2m.

We need the following lemma in the study of the optimization problem.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
p<0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}
satisfying
V(P(x))=V_{0}
. Then there exists an admissible
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}
such that
V(P(\tilde{x}))=V_{0}
and
\Phi_{p}(\tilde{x})\leq\Phi_{p}(x)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}
, and denote

\tilde{x}_{i}=u(P(x),\textbf{e}_{i}),\quad\tilde{x}=(\tilde{x}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}.

Using (
4.3
), we obtain

(6.12)

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{i}=u(P(x),\textbf{e}_{i})\leq x_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq m.

It follows that

\Phi_{p}(\tilde{x})=\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2m}a_{i}(e^{p\tilde{x}_{i}}-1)\leq\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2m}a_{i}(e^{px_{i}}-1)=\Phi_{p}(x).

By (
2.3
), we have
P(x)\subset\overline{B}_{\textbf{e}_{i}}(\tilde{x}_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Then

(6.13)

\displaystyle P(x)\subset\bigcap_{i=1}^{2m}\overline{B}_{\textbf{e}_{i}}(\tilde{x}_{i})=P(\tilde{x}).

On the other hand, it follows from (
6.12
) that
P(\tilde{x})\subset P(x)
. Combining this with (
6.13
), we have

\displaystyle P(\tilde{x})=P(x),\quad V(P(\tilde{x}))=V_{0}.

It is clear that

\displaystyle u(P(\tilde{x}),\textbf{e}_{i})=\tilde{x}_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq m.

Hence
\tilde{x}
is admissible. This completes the proof of Lemma
6.4
.
∎

Let
p<0
and
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{2m}a_{i}\delta_{\textbf{e}_{i}}
be an even discrete measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Let us consider the following optimization problem

(6.14)

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\Phi_{p}(x):\ x\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}\ \text{and}\ V(P(x))=V_{0}\right\},

where
V_{0}
is a given positive constant.

Note that the function
\Phi_{p}(x)
defined in (
6.2
) is non-negative. Let us take a minimizing sequence
\{x^{(k)}\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}
for the optimization problem (
6.14
), i.e.

(6.15)

V(P(x^{(k)}))=V_{0},

and

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\Phi_{p}(x^{(k)})=\inf\left\{\Phi_{p}(x):\ x\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}\ \text{and}\ V(P(x))=V_{0}\right\}.

By Lemma
6.4
, we may assume that each vector in the sequence
\{x^{(k)}\}
is admissible. Combining this with Lemma
5.1
, we conclude that
\{x^{(k)}\}
is bounded. On the other hand, it follows from (
6.15
) and the origin-symmetry of
P(x^{(k)})
that
\{x_{i}^{(k)}\}
has a positive uniform lower bound for all
i,k
.

Therefore, by use of Theorem
3.8
and Corollary
3.6
, there exists a subsequence of
P(x^{(k)})
(still denoted by
P(x^{(k)})
) that converges to an origin-symmetric h-convex polytope
P_{0}\in\mathcal{P}^{h}(\textbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{2m})
satisfying
V(P_{0})=V_{0}
.
Let
x^{(0)}=(u(P_{0},\textbf{e}_{i}))_{1\leq i\leq m}\in\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}
. Then
P(x^{(0)})=P_{0}
and
V(P(x^{(0)}))=V_{0}
. Since
P(x^{(k)})
converges to
P_{0}
in Hausdorff metric, we get

(6.16)

x^{(k)}\to x^{(0)}\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{p}(x^{(0)})=\inf\left\{\Phi_{p}(x):\ V(P(x))=V_{0}\right\}.

Due to the origin-symmetry of
P(x^{(0)})
and
V(P(x^{(0)}))=V_{0}>0
, we have that
x^{(0)}
is in the interior of
\mathbb{R}_{*}^{m}
. By the Lagrange multiplier method and the variational formula in Lemma
4.6
, there exists a constant
\lambda
such that

\begin{split}0&=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right|_{x=x^{(0)}}\left[\Phi_{p}(x)-\lambda(V(P(x))-V_{0})\right]\\
&=a_{i}e^{px_{i}^{(0)}}+a_{m+i}e^{px_{m+i}^{(0)}}-\lambda S({P(x^{(0)})},\textbf{e}_{i})-\lambda S({P(x^{(0)})},\textbf{e}_{m+i}),\quad i=1,\ldots,m.\end{split}

Using
a_{i}=a_{m+i},\ x_{i}^{(0)}=x_{m+i}^{(0)}
and
S({P(x^{(0)})},\textbf{e}_{i})=S({P(x^{(0)})},\textbf{e}_{m+i})
, we have

(6.17)

a_{i}e^{px_{i}^{(0)}}=\lambda S({P(x^{(0)})},\textbf{e}_{i}),\quad i=1,\ldots,m,

and

\lambda=\frac{\sum a_{i}e^{px_{i}^{(0)}}}{S(P(x^{(0)}))}>0.

Therefore,
S({P(x^{(0)})},\textbf{e}_{i})>0
and then
u(P(x^{(0)}),\textbf{e}_{i})=x_{i}^{(0)}
. This together with (
6.17
) implies

\displaystyle\mu=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2m}a_{i}\delta_{\textbf{e}_{i}}(\cdot)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2m}e^{-pu(P(x^{(0)}),\textbf{e}_{i})}S(P(x^{(0)}),\textbf{e}_{i})\delta_{\textbf{e}_{i}}(\cdot)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda S_{p}(P(x^{(0)}),\cdot).

Then
P(x^{(0)})
is the desired solution. We complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
for
p<0
.

6.3.
Further discussion

In Theorem
1.1
, we assumed that the prescribed measure
\mu
is even. However, if
\mu
is not even, then there may exist necessary conditions on
\mu
for the existence of solutions to Problem
1.2
. For the smooth case, one can refer to the Kazdan-Warner type obstruction of the horosphercial Minkowski problem in
[
19
]
. For the discrete case, we provide the following example.

Example 1

(Problem
1.1
when
N=2
)
.

For any unit vectors
\textbf{e}_{1},\textbf{e}_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and positive numbers
x_{1},x_{2}
, denote by
P
the horospherical Wulff shape associated with
(\{\textbf{e}_{1},\textbf{e}_{2}\},(x_{1},x_{2}))
, i.e.,
P=\bar{B}_{\textbf{e}_{1}}(x_{1})\cap\bar{B}_{\textbf{e}_{2}}(x_{2})
. A direct calculation shows that

(6.18)

\displaystyle S(P,\cdot)=\omega_{n}\left(e^{x_{1}+x_{2}}-1\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\delta_{\textbf{e}_{1}}(\cdot)+\delta_{\textbf{e}_{2}}(\cdot)\right).

Therefore, given a discrete measure
\mu=a_{1}\delta_{\textbf{e}_{1}}+a_{2}\delta_{\textbf{e}_{2}}
, there exists a solution to Problem
1.1
for
\mu
if and only if
a_{1}=a_{2}
. Moreover, when
a_{1}=a_{2}
, it follows from (
6.18
) that this problem has infinitely many solutions.

Finally, we restate Problem
1.2
in the case that
\mu
is not even. This problem is of great interest in the discrete horospherical
p
-Brunn-Minkowski theory.

Problem 6.1
.

Suppose that a discrete measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is not even. Find necessary and sufficient conditions on
\mu
so that there exists a h-convex polytope
P
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
whose horospherical
p
-surface area measure
S_{p}(P,\cdot)
is a multiple of the given measure
\mu
.

Euler sums of generalized harmonic numbers and connected extensions
Mümün CAN, Levent KARGIN, Ayhan DİL, Gültekin SOYLU

Abstract
This paper presents the evaluation of the Euler sums of generalized hyperharmonic numbers H n ( p , q ) H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)} ζ H ( p , q ) ​ ( r ) = ∑ n = 1 ∞ H n ( p , q ) n r \zeta_{H^{\left(p,q\right)}}\left(r\right)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}}{n^{r}} in terms of the famous Euler sums of generalized harmonic numbers. Moreover, several infinite series, whose terms consist of certain harmonic numbers and reciprocal binomial coefficients, are evaluated in terms of Riemann zeta values.

\displaystyle\zeta_{H^{\left(p,2\right)}}\left(r\right)

\displaystyle 2\zeta_{H^{\left(p,3\right)}}\left(r\right)

Later, Göral and Sertbaş

\left(q-1\right)H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}=\left(n+q-1\right)H_{n}^{\left(p,q-1\right)}-H_{n}^{\left(p-1,q-1\right)}

\zeta_{H^{\left(p,q+1\right)}}\left(r\right)=\frac{1}{q!}\sum_{m=0}^{q}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{q+1}{m+1}\binom{m}{k}\zeta_{H^{\left(p-k\right)}}\left(r+k-m\right).

\displaystyle 2\zeta_{H^{\left(6,4+1\right)}}\left(6\right)

\displaystyle=-1925\zeta\left(11\right)+\left(175\pi^{2}-\frac{905}{4}-\frac{3937\pi^{8}}{544\,320}\right)\zeta\left(9\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{245\pi^{2}}{12}+\frac{35\pi^{4}}{18}+\frac{31\pi^{10}}{46\,656}\right)\zeta\left(7\right)+\left(\frac{\pi^{4}}{4}+\frac{5\pi^{6}}{1134}+\frac{31\pi^{12}}{6123\,600}\right)\zeta\left(5\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{35}{12}\zeta^{2}\left(5\right)-\frac{1}{3}\zeta\left(3\right)\zeta\left(5\right)+\frac{\pi^{6}}{1134}\zeta\left(3\right)+\frac{\pi^{10}}{29\,160}+\frac{1406\pi^{12}}{638\,512\,875}.

In the last section we further extend our results. In this direction we

2
Euler sums of generalized hyperharmonic numbers

In this section we present an evaluation formula for Euler sums
\zeta_{H^{\left(p,q\right)}}\left(r\right)
under certain conditions. To
state and prove our result we need some preliminaries.

Firstly, recall the polylogarithm defined by

Li_{p}\left(t\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}}{k^{p}},\text{
}(\left|t\right|\leq 1\text{ if }p>1,\text{ and }\left|t\right|<1\text{ if }p\leq 1)\text{.}

The generating function of the numbers
H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}
in terms
of the polylogarithm is
[
21
]

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}t^{n}=\frac{Li_{p}\left(t\right)}{\left(1-t\right)^{q}},\text{ }\left|t\right|<1,\text{ }p,q\in\mathbb{Z}.

(9)

Our first result presents the following reduction formula for
H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}
.

Lemma 1

Let
p
and
q
be integers with
q\geq 1.
Reduction relation for

H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}
in the index
q
is

\left(q-1\right)H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}=\left(n+q-1\right)H_{n}^{\left(p,q-1\right)}-H_{n}^{\left(p-1,q-1\right)}.

(10)

Proof.
We define the polynomial
H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}\left(z\right)
as

H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}\left(z\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{k}^{\left(p,q\right)}z^{k}.

Considering (
9
), we obtain the ordinary generating function of

H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}\left(z\right)
as

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}\left(z\right)t^{n}=\frac{Li_{p}\left(zt\right)}{\left(1-t\right)\left(1-zt\right)^{q}}.

(11)

From (
11
), it can be seen that

z\frac{d}{dz}H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}\left(z\right)=H_{n}^{\left(p-1,q\right)}\left(z\right)+qzH_{n-1}^{\left(p,q+1\right)}\left(z\right).

(12)

On the other hand, we utilize (
8
) twice to find that

\displaystyle H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{k}^{\left(p,q-1\right)}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}H_{j}^{\left(p,q-2\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(n+1\right)H_{n}^{\left(p,q-1\right)}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}jH_{j}^{\left(p,q-2\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(n+1\right)H_{n}^{\left(p,q-1\right)}-\left.\frac{d}{dz}H_{n}^{\left(p,q-2\right)}\left(z\right)\right|_{z=1},

or equivalently

\left.\frac{d}{dz}H_{n}^{\left(p,q-2\right)}\left(z\right)\right|_{z=1}=\left(n+1\right)H_{n}^{\left(p,q-1\right)}-H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}\overset{\text{(\ref{0})}}{=}nH_{n}^{\left(p,q-1\right)}-H_{n-1}^{\left(p,q\right)}.

(13)

Therefore, (
12
) and (
13
) yield the desired formula.

The objective here is to express
H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}
in terms of

H_{n}^{\left(p\right)}.
In
[
21
]
this relation is listed for at
most
q=4
due to the complexity of the process. However, the next result
provides a general solution to this problem where the numbers
H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}
are expressed in terms of the numbers
H_{n}^{\left(p\right)}
and
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{q}{j}_{r}
. Here
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{q}{j}_{r}
denotes the
r
-Stirling number of the first kind defined by the
”horizontal” generating function
[
11
,
12
,
28
]

\left(x+r\right)\left(x+r+1\right)\cdots\left(x+r+q-1\right)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{q}{j}_{r}x^{j}.

(14)

The essence of the theorem’s proof is based on the relationship between

r
-Stirling numbers and symmetric polynomials. The
k
th elementary symmetric
polynomial
e_{k}\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q}\right)
in variables

X_{1},\ldots,X_{q}
is defined by (see for example
[
27
]
)

\displaystyle e_{0}\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q}\right)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle e_{k}\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{k}\leq q}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}X_{j_{i}},\text{ }1\leq k\leq q,

\displaystyle e_{k}\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q}\right)

\displaystyle=0,\text{ }k>q,

and possess the identity

\prod\limits_{j=1}^{q}\left(x-X_{j}\right)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q}\left(-1\right)^{r-j}e_{q-j}\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q}\right)x^{j}.

(15)

The comparison of (
14
) with (
15
) obviously leads to the following
relationship
[
28
, Theorem 4.1]

e_{q-j}\left(n+1,n+2,\ldots,n+q\right)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{q}{j}_{n+1}.

(16)

Theorem 2

Let
p
and
q
be integers with
q\geq 0.
Then,

q!H_{n}^{\left(p,q+1\right)}=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\left(-1\right)^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{q}{k}_{n+1}H_{n}^{\left(p-k\right)}.

(17)

Proof.
We employ (
10
) on the right-hand side of

\left(q-1\right)qH_{n}^{\left(p,q+1\right)}=\left(n+q\right)\left(q-1\right)H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}-\left(q-1\right)H_{n}^{\left(p-1,q\right)},

and see that

\displaystyle\left(q-1\right)qH_{n}^{\left(p,q+1\right)}

\displaystyle=H_{n}^{\left(p,q-1\right)}\left\{\left(n+q\right)\left(n+q-1\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\quad-H_{n}^{\left(p-1,q-1\right)}\left\{\left(n+q\right)+\left(n+q-1\right)\right\}+H_{n}^{\left(p-2,q-1\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\left(-1\right)^{k}e_{2-k}\left(n+q-1,n+q\right)H_{n}^{\left(p-k,q+1-2\right)}.

These initial steps suggest that the following equality should hold:

\displaystyle\left(q+1-r\right)\left(q+1-\left(r-1\right)\right)\cdots\left(q-1\right)qH_{n}^{\left(p,q+1\right)}

(18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left(-1\right)^{k}e_{r-k}\left(n+q-\left(r-1\right),n+q-\left(r-2\right),\ldots,n+q\right)H_{n}^{\left(p-k,q+1-r\right)}.

To prove this by induction we show that it is also true for
r+1\leq q.
We
multiply (
18
) by
\left(q-r\right)
and then use (
10
). Hence
we find that

\displaystyle\left(q-r\right)\left(q+1-r\right)\left(q+1-\left(r-1\right)\right)\cdots\left(q-1\right)qH_{n}^{\left(p,q+1\right)}

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left(-1\right)^{k}e_{r-k}\left(n+q-\left(r-1\right),\ldots,n+q\right)\left(n+q-r\right)H_{n}^{\left(p-k,q-r\right)}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{r}\left(-1\right)^{k+1}e_{r-k}\left(n+q-\left(r-1\right),\ldots,n+q\right)H_{n}^{\left(p-k-1,q-r\right)}

\displaystyle\ =e_{r}\left(n+q-\left(r-1\right),\ldots,n+q\right)\left(n+q-r\right)H_{n}^{\left(p,q-r\right)}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left(-1\right)^{k}H_{n}^{\left(p-k,q-r\right)}\left\{\left(n+q-r\right)e_{r-k}\left(n+q-\left(r-1\right),\ldots,n+q\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+e_{r+1-k}\left(n+q-\left(r-1\right),\ldots,n+q\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(-1\right)^{r+1}e_{0}\left(n+q-r,\ldots,n+q\right)H_{n}^{\left(p-\left(r+1\right),q-r\right)}

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{k=0}^{r+1}\left(-1\right)^{k}e_{r+1-k}\left(n+q-r,\ldots,n+q\right)H_{n}^{\left(p-k,q+1-\left(r+1\right)\right)}.

The case
r=q
in (
18
) gives

q!H_{n}^{\left(p,q+1\right)}=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\left(-1\right)^{k}e_{q-k}\left(n+1,\ldots,n+q\right)H_{n}^{\left(p-k\right)},

which combines with (
16
) to give the statement.

Now, we are ready to state and prove our evaluation formula for
\zeta_{H^{\left(p,q\right)}}\left(r\right).
Thanks to this formula the
evaluation of Euler sums of generalized hyperharmonic numbers reduces to the
evaluation of Euler sums of generalized harmonic numbers.

Theorem 3

For
p,q\geq 1
and
r>q+1,
we have

\zeta_{H^{\left(p,q+1\right)}}\left(r\right)=\frac{1}{q!}\sum_{m=0}^{q}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{q+1}{m+1}\binom{m}{k}\zeta_{H^{\left(p-k\right)}}\left(r+k-m\right).

Proof.
From (
17
) and the following identity
[
31
, p. 1661]

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{r+1}=\sum_{m=k}^{n}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n+1}{m+1}\binom{m}{k}r^{m-k},

we have

\displaystyle H_{n}^{\left(p,q+1\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q!}\sum_{k=0}^{q}\left(-1\right)^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{q}{k}_{n+1}H_{n}^{\left(p-k\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q!}\sum_{k=0}^{q}\sum_{m=k}^{q}\left(-1\right)^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{q+1}{m+1}\binom{m}{k}n^{m-k}H_{n}^{\left(p-k\right)}.

Multiplying both sides with
n^{-r}
and summing over
n
from
1
to
\infty
, we deduce the desired result.

The following corollary gives the reflection formula for Euler sums of
generalized hyperharmonic numbers. Combined with (
3
), this corollary
shows that
\zeta_{H^{\left(p,q+1\right)}}\left(r\right)+\zeta_{H^{\left(r,q+1\right)}}\left(p\right)
can be written as a
combination of Riemann zeta values. In this way, particular Euler sums of type

\zeta_{H^{\left(p,q\right)}}\left(p\right)
can be evaluated with
less computation.

Corollary 4

Let
p>q+1,

r>q+1
and
p+r
be even. Then

\displaystyle\zeta_{H^{\left(p,q+1\right)}}\left(r\right)+\zeta_{H^{\left(r,q+1\right)}}\left(p\right)

\displaystyle=\zeta\left(p+r\right)+\frac{2}{q!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=0\\
m\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{q}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{q+1}{m+1}\binom{m}{k}\zeta_{H^{\left(p-k\right)}}\left(r+k-m\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{q!}\sum_{m=0}^{q}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{m+k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{q+1}{m+1}\binom{m}{k}\zeta\left(p-k\right)\zeta\left(r+k-m\right).

Proof.
Let
\left(p+r\right)
be even. It is obvious from Theorem
3

that

\displaystyle\zeta_{H^{\left(p,q+1\right)}}\left(r\right)+\zeta_{H^{\left(r,q+1\right)}}\left(p\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q!}\sum_{m=0}^{q}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{q+1}{m+1}\binom{m}{k}\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{\left(p-k\right)}}{n^{r+k-m}}+\left(-1\right)^{m}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{\left(r+k-m\right)}}{n^{p-k}}\right\}.

We write the right-hand side as

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=0\\
m\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{q}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{q+1}{m+1}\binom{m}{k}\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{\left(p-k\right)}}{n^{r+k-m}}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{\left(r+k-m\right)}}{n^{p-k}}\right\}

\displaystyle+\sum_{0\leq m\leq q/2}\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\left(-1\right)^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{q+1}{2m+1}\binom{2m}{k}\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{\left(p-k\right)}}{n^{r+k-2m}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{\left(r+k-2m\right)}}{n^{p-k}}\right\}.

By the reflection formula (
4
) we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{\left(p-k\right)}}{n^{r+k-2m}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{\left(r+k-2m\right)}}{n^{p-k}}=\zeta\left(p+r-2m\right)+\zeta\left(p-k\right)\zeta\left(r+k-2m\right).

Moreover, for odd
m,
it can be seen from (
3
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{\left(p-k\right)}}{n^{r+k-m}}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{\left(r+k-m\right)}}{n^{p-k}}

\displaystyle=2\zeta_{H^{\left(p-k\right)}}\left(r+k-m\right)-\zeta\left(p+r-m\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\zeta\left(p-k\right)\zeta\left(r+k-m\right).

Hence, we obtain the desired equation.

Theorem 6

Let
p\geq 1
and
q,

l\geq 0
be integers with
l\geq q
. For

,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}}{\left(n+m\right)\binom{n+m+l}{l}}

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{j=0}^{l-q}\binom{l-q}{j}\left\{\frac{\left(-1\right)^{j+p-1}H_{m+j}}{\left(m+j\right)^{p}}+\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{j+k-1}}{\left(m+j\right)^{k}}\zeta\left(p+1-k\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}}{n\binom{n+l}{l}}

(20)

\displaystyle\ =\zeta\left(p+1\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{l-q}\binom{l-q}{j}\left\{\frac{\left(-1\right)^{j+p}H_{j}}{j^{p}}+\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{j+k}}{j^{k}}\zeta\left(p+1-k\right)\right\}.

Proof.
Using the formula (see
[

)

{\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}}t^{n+m-1}\left(1-t\right)^{l}dt=\frac{1}{\left(n+m\right)\binom{n+m+l}{l}},

\frac{H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}}{\left(n+m\right)\binom{n+m+l}{l}}={\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}}H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}t^{n+m-1}\left(1-t\right)^{l}dt.

With the help of (

), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}}{\left(n+m\right)\binom{n+m+l}{l}}

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}}t^{m-1}\left(1-t\right)^{l-q}Li_{p}\left(t\right)dt

7

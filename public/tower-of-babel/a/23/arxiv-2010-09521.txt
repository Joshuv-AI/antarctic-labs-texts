Capillary-Gravity Water Waves: Modified Flow Force Formulation Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author
Calin Iulian Martin, Biswajit Basu ∗

Abstract
. The classical irrotational capillary-gravity water wave problem described by the Euler equations with a nonlinear free surface boundary condition over a flat bed is considered. A modified flow force has been defined and a new formulation of capillary-gravity waves in the framework of the modified flow force function has been developed. Using bifurcation theory, the local existence of waves of small amplitude is proved. Keywords : Capillary-gravity, water waves, flow force, free surface flows, finite depth, local bifurcation. Mathematics Subject Classification : 35Q35, 35J15

The contribution of this paper is to develop a flow force based formulation of the irrotational capillary-gravity water waves with free surface flow over a flat bed. In this context, a “modified flow force” function is proposed to handle the capillary-gravity case. While we assume the free surface to be a graph, we do not need to require a priori the absence of stagnation points in the water flow. However, we consider irrotational flows in this paper and irrotational flows do not have stagnation points.
The original water wave problem is reformulated as a quasilinear pseudodifferential equation for a function of one variable, giving the elevation of free surface when the fluid domain is the conformal image of a half-plane. Instrumental in achieving our goals is the
Dirichlet-Neumann operator
.
The method of Crandall-Rabinowitz of bifurcation from simple eigenvalues is used to prove the existence of small amplitude capillary-gravity waves.

2.
Presentation of the water wave problem

2.1.
Stream function formulation of the water wave problem

Let us consider a water wave profile oscillating about a flat surface. The problem of two-dimensional periodic steady waves traveling at speed
c
, has a
space-time dependence of the free surface, of the velocity field and of the pressure of the form (
\widetilde{X}-ct
) and is periodic with period
L>0
. The flat bed is given
by
\widetilde{Y}=0
and the oscillating wave profile is represented by
\widetilde{Y}=\eta(\widetilde{X}-ct)
, where
\widetilde{Y}
is the vertical axis. The free boundary problem can be simplified by
a change of the reference frame from a moving one to a stationary one. Indeed, the transformation
(\widetilde{X}-ct,\widetilde{Y})\mapsto(X,Y)
allows us to supress the time variable from the equations of motion. To introduce the latter we denote the water domain by
\Omega
which is assumed to be bounded below by the impermeable flat bed

\mathcal{B}=\{(X,0);X\in\mathbb{R}\},

and above by an a priori unknown curve

\mathcal{S}=\{(X,\eta(X));X\in\mathbb{R}\},

(2.1)

satisfying

\eta(X+L)=\eta(X)\quad{\rm for}\,\,{\rm all}\quad X\in\mathbb{R},

where
L>0
is a constant that denotes the period of the motion.
The motion of the water flow obeys the Euler equations

\begin{split}(u_{1}-c)u_{1X}+u_{2}u_{1Y}&=-P_{X},\\
(u_{1}-c)u_{2X}+u_{2}u_{2Y}&=-P_{Y}-g,\end{split}

(2.2)

and the equation of mass conservation

u_{1X}+u_{2Y}=0,

(2.3)

where
(u_{1},u_{2})
and
P
represent the velocity field and the pressure in the flow, respectively, and are sought to be periodic of period
L
. The description of the water wave problem is complete together with the specification of the boundary conditions.
Of them we start with the kinematic boundary conditions that ensure that the two boundaries are impermeable. More precisely, the top boundary is defined by the
condition

u_{2}(X,\eta(X))=(u_{1}(X,\eta(X))-c)\eta_{X}

(2.4)

while, on the bottom, we require no motion in the vertical direction, that is

u_{2}(X,0)=0\quad{\rm for}\,\,{\rm all}\,\,X\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.5)

We close the system by the dynamic boundary condition

P(X,\eta(X))=P_{atm}-\sigma\frac{\eta_{XX}}{(1+\eta_{X}^{2})^{3/2}}~~\text{on}~~Y=\eta(X),

(2.6)

where
P_{atm}
is the constant atmospheric pressure and
\sigma>0
denotes the coefficient of surface tension.

Aiming at reducing the number of unknowns, we introduce the stream function
(X,Y)\rightarrow\psi(X,Y)
which
is defined (up to an additive constant) by the relations

\psi_{X}=-u_{2},\quad\psi_{Y}=u_{1}-c\ .

(2.7)

We assume throughout the paper that the flow is irrotational, that is

u_{1_{Y}}-u_{2_{X}}\equiv 0,

(2.8)

equation that readily implies via (
2.7
) that
\psi
is a harmonic function.

Another characteristic of the flow is the mass flux (relative to the uniform flow
c
) which is defined as

p_{0}=\int_{0}^{\eta(X)}(u_{1}(X,Y)-c)dY.

(2.9)

Availing of the equation of mass conservation (
2.3
) and of (
2.4
) we infer that
p_{0}
is indeed a constant.

From the Euler equation, we get Bernoulli’s law which states that the quantity

E:=\frac{(c-u_{1})^{2}+v^{2}}{2}+gY+P

(2.10)

is a constant (called hydraulic head) throughout the flow.
The dynamic boundary condition at the surface can be restated as

\psi^{2}_{X}+\psi_{Y}^{2}+2gY-2\sigma\frac{\eta_{XX}}{(1+\eta_{X}^{2})^{3/2}}=Q~~\text{on}~~Y=\eta(X)

(2.11)

where
Q=2(E-P_{atm})
is a constant for any given flow.

We can formulate the free surface irrotational capillary-gravity water wave problem as the system
[
8
]

\begin{split}\bigtriangleup\psi=0~~\text{in}~~0<Y<\eta(X),\\
|\nabla\psi|^{2}+2gY-2\sigma\frac{\eta_{XX}}{(1+\eta_{X}^{2})^{3/2}}=Q~~\text{on}~~Y=\eta(X),\\
\psi=0~~\text{on}~~Y=\eta(X),\\
\psi=-p_{0}~~\text{on}~~Y=0,\end{split}

(2.12)

which is to be solved for functions which are
L
-periodic in the
X-
variable within the fluid domain
\Omega
.

2.2.
Modified flow force reformulation

We now reformulate the governing equations in Section 2 using a different and novel approach
based on the modified flow force function.
We recall that the flow force function (in the absence of surface tension) is given by the expression

\overline{S}(X,Y)=\int_{0}^{Y}[P(X,r)+(u_{1}(X,r)-c)^{2}]{\rm d}r\ \

(2.13)

where,
the pressure at
(x,y)
is denoted by the function
P(x,y)

and
P_{atm}
is the atmospheric pressure. For our purposes (which pertain to a problem involving the combined effect of gravity and surface tension) we will need a modified flow force function. The latter is defined as

S(X,Y)=\overline{S}(X,Y)+e(X,Y)\ \

(2.14)

where

e(X,Y)=e_{0}(X)\frac{Y}{\eta(X)}\ ,

(2.15)

and

e_{0}(X)=-P_{atm}\eta(X)-\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{{1+\eta_{X}^{2}}}}+\sigma

(2.16)

Remark 2.1
.

An aspect of vital importance in the following considerations is
that the (modified) flow force function
S
is constant on the two boundaries of the fluid domain. Indeed, it is immediate from (
2.13
) and (
2.15
) that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}S=0&\mbox{ on the bottom }\mathcal{B}.\\
\end{array}

We will show in the following that
S
is constant on the free surface.
To this end we note that the gradient of
\overline{S}
satisfies

\overline{S}_{X}=-(u_{1}-c)u_{2},\qquad\overline{S}_{Y}=P(X,Y)+(u_{1}(X,Y)-c)^{2},

(2.17)

where the first equation above follows from the equality

P_{X}+2(u_{1}-c){u_{1}}_{X}=-(u_{1}-c){u_{2}}_{Y}-u_{2}{u_{1}}_{Y}=-\{(u_{1}-c)u_{2}\}_{Y},

(2.18)

which is a consequence of the Euler- and mass conservation equation.

Note also that on the free surface
\mathcal{S}
we have

e(X,\eta(X))=e_{0}(X).

(2.19)

Setting now
S_{0}(X):=S(X,\eta(X))
we find that

\begin{split}\frac{dS_{0}}{dX}=&\eta_{X}\Big(P(X,\eta(X))+(u_{1}(X,\eta(X))-c)^{2}\Big)\\
&-u_{2}(X,\eta(X))\Big(u_{1}(X,\eta(X))-c\Big)\\
&-\eta_{X}\left(P_{atm}-\sigma\frac{\eta_{XX}}{(1+\eta_{X}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\\
=&(u_{1}-c)\big((u_{1}-c)\eta_{X}-u_{2}\big)\\
=&0,\end{split}

(2.20)

where, to obtain the last equalities above, we have also used the dynamic and surface kinematic conditions. Thus,
S_{0}
is, in fact, a constant.

Recalling that

\begin{split}&S_{X}=(c-u_{1})u_{2}+e_{X},\\
&S_{Y}=P+(u_{1}-c)^{2}+e_{Y},\end{split}

(2.21)

we differentiate the first equation with respect to
X
and the second one with respect to
Y
, obtaining that

S_{XX}+S_{YY}=-g-2(c-u_{1})({u_{1}}_{Y}-{u_{2}}_{X})+e_{XX}

(2.22)

which, in the absence of any vorticity, leads to

\Delta S=-g+e_{XX}

(2.23)

with
S=0
on the flat bed
\mathcal{B}
and
S=S_{0}
on the free surface
\mathcal{S}
. The nonlinear dynamic boundary condition on the free surface may be expressed as

\left(\frac{\big(S_{X}-e_{X})^{2}}{(S_{Y}-P-e_{Y})}+(S_{Y}-P-e_{Y})\right)\Big|_{(X,\eta(X))}+2gY-2\sigma\frac{\eta_{XX}}{(1+\eta_{X}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}=Q.

(2.24)

Summing up, we have proved that the water wave problem (
2.12
) can be written in terms of the modified flow force function
S
as

\begin{split}\Delta S=-g+e_{XX},\\
S=S_{0}\,\,{\rm on}\,\,\mathcal{S},\\
S=0\,\,{\rm on}\,\,\mathcal{B},\\
\left(\frac{\big(S_{X}-e_{X})^{2}}{(S_{Y}-P-e_{Y})}+(S_{Y}-P-e_{Y})\right)\Big|_{(X,\eta(X))}+2gY-2\sigma\frac{\eta_{XX}}{(1+\eta_{X}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}=Q.\end{split}

(2.25)

2.3.
Formulation of the water wave problem as a quasilinear equation for the free surface

We will aim to further reformulate the problem (
2.25
) as a single equation for the free surface
\mathcal{S}
. To this end, we need a few notations and some preliminary results. For the proof of these results we refer the
reader to
[
19
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
we denote by
C^{p,\alpha}
the space of Hölder continuous functions.
The space of functions of class
C^{p,\alpha}
over any compact subset of their domain of definition will be denoted with
C_{\textrm{loc}}^{p,\alpha}
. Moreover, by
C_{2\pi}^{p,\alpha}
we denote the space of functions of one real variable which are
2\pi
periodic and of class
C_{\textrm{loc}}^{p,\alpha}
in
\mathbb{R}
, while
by
C_{2\pi,o}^{p,\alpha}
we denote the functions that are in
C_{2\pi}^{p,\alpha}
and have zero mean over one period. Finally, by
L_{2\pi}^{2}
we denote the space of
2\pi
-periodic locally square integrable functions of one real variable. By
L_{2\pi,\textrm{o}}^{2}
we denote the subspace of
L_{2\pi}^{2}
whose elements have zero mean over
one period.

A key ingredient for completing our tasks is played by the
Dirichlet-Neumann operator
associated to a horizontal strip in the plane. Before we introduce the Dirichlet-Neumann operator let us set

\mathcal{R}_{d}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:-d<y<0\},

where
d>0
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given
w\in C_{2\pi}^{p,\alpha}
we let
W\in C^{p,\alpha}(\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{d})
be the unique solution of

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Delta W&=&0\,\,\textrm{in}\,\,\mathcal{R}_{d},\\
W(x,-d)&=&0,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
W(x,0)&=&w(x),\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}

(2.26)

The function
(x,y)\rightarrow W(x,y)
is
2\pi
-periodic in
x
throughout
\mathcal{R}_{d}
. For
p\in\mathbb{Z},p\geq 1
, and
\alpha\in(0,1)
we define the periodic
Dirichlet-Neumann operator for a strip
\mathcal{G}_{d}
by

\mathcal{G}_{d}(w)(x):=W_{y}(x,0),\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

The Dirichlet-Neumann operator possesses the following properties.

Lemma 2.4
.

(i)

The operator
\mathcal{G}_{d}:C_{2\pi}^{p,\alpha}\rightarrow C_{2\pi}^{p-1,\alpha}
is a bounded linear operator.

(ii)

If the function
w
takes the constant value
c
then

\mathcal{G}_{d}(c)=\frac{c}{d}.

(2.27)

Closely related to the Dirichlet-Neumann operator is the periodic Hilbert transform for the strip
\mathcal{R}_{d}
. More precisely, having the previous notation in mind and setting
Z
to be the unique (up to a constant) harmonic function such that
Z+iW
is holomorphic in
\mathcal{R}_{d}
, we define for
w\in C_{2\pi,o}^{p,\alpha}

\mathcal{C}_{d}(w)(x):=Z(x,0)\,\,{\rm for}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

The map
\mathcal{C}_{d}
is called the
periodic Hilbert transform
for the strip
\mathcal{R}_{d}
. Some useful properties of the Hilbert transform are listed below.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that

w=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\cos(nx)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\sin(nx),

is the Fourier series expansion of
w\in L_{2\pi,\textrm{o}}^{2}
.
Then

\mathcal{C}_{d}(w)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\coth(nd)\sin(nx)-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\coth(nd)\cos(nx)

(2.28)

Lemma 2.6
.

(i)

For all
d>0,\,p\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
it holds that
\mathcal{C}_{d}:C_{2\pi,o}^{p,\alpha}\rightarrow C_{2\pi,o}^{p,\alpha}
is a bounded linear operator. Moreover,
\mathcal{C}_{d}^{-1}=-\mathcal{C}_{d}:C_{2\pi,\textrm{o}}^{p,\alpha}\rightarrow C_{2\pi,\textrm{o}}^{p,\alpha}
is also a bounded linear operator.

(ii)

\mathcal{G}_{d}(w)=(\mathcal{C}_{d}(w))^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}_{d}(w^{\prime})

for all

w\in C_{2\pi,o}^{p,\alpha}
mit
p\geq 1
.

(iii)

If
w\in C_{2\pi}^{p,\alpha}
then
\mathcal{G}_{d}(w)=\frac{\left[w\right]}{d}+\mathcal{C}_{d}(w^{\prime})
, where
[w]
denotes the average of
w
over one period.

The following two definitions specify the type of solutions we will be proving to exist for the free boundary value problem (
2.25
).

Definition 2.7
.

We say that a solution
(\Omega,S)
of the water wave problem (
2.25
) is of class
C^{2,\alpha}
if the free surface belongs to
C^{2,\alpha}_{2\pi}
and
S\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
.

Definition 2.8
.

•

We say that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is an L-periodic strip like domain if it is contained in the upper half
(X,Y)
-plane and if its boudary consists of the real
axis
\mathcal{B}
and of a parametric curve
\mathcal{S}
defined by
(
2.1
)
which is
L
-periodic.

•

For any such domain, the conformal mean depth is defined to be unique positive number
h
such that there exists an onto conformal mapping

\tilde{U}+i\tilde{V}:\mathcal{R}_{h}\rightarrow\Omega
which admits an extension between the closures of these domains, with onto mappings

\{(x,0):x\in\mathbb{R}\}\rightarrow\mathcal{S},

and

\{(x,-h):x\in\mathbb{R}\}\rightarrow\mathcal{B},

and such that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\tilde{U}(x+L,y)&=&\tilde{U}(x,y)+L\\
\tilde{V}(x+L,y)&=&\tilde{V}(x,y)\end{array}\,\,\,\,{\rm for}\,\,{\rm all}\,\,(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}_{h}.

(2.29)

The existence and uniqueness of such an
h
was proved in Appendix A of the paper
[
19
]
.

We are now ready to state the main result of the current section, namely the reformulation of the water wave problem as
a quasilinear equation for a periodic function (representing the elevation of the free surface) of one variable in a fixed domain.

Theorem 2.9
.

If
(\Omega,S)
of class
C^{2,\alpha}
is a solution of (
2.25
) then there exists a positive number h, a function
v\in C^{2,\alpha}_{2\pi}
and a constant a
\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{(A\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)-Bv^{\prime})^{2}}{(\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)^{2}+v^{\prime 2})\left(Av^{\prime}+B\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)-\left(P_{atm}-\sigma\frac{\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)v^{\prime\prime}-\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v^{\prime})v^{\prime}}{(\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)^{2}+v^{\prime 2})^{3/2}}\right)(\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)^{2}+v^{\prime 2})\right)}

(2.30)

\displaystyle+\frac{Av^{\prime}+B\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)}{\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)^{2}+v^{\prime 2}}-P_{atm}+\sigma\frac{\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)v^{\prime\prime}-\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v^{\prime})(v^{\prime})}{(\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)^{2}+v^{\prime 2})^{3/2}}

\displaystyle=\left(Q+2\sigma\frac{\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)v^{\prime\prime}-\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v^{\prime})v^{\prime}}{(v^{\prime 2}+\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)^{2})^{3/2}}-2gv\right)

where

\begin{split}&A=P_{atm}v^{\prime}+\sigma\frac{v^{\prime}(\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v^{\prime})v^{\prime}-\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)v^{\prime\prime})}{(\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)^{2}+v^{\prime 2})^{3/2}}\\
&B=\frac{S_{0}-\sigma}{kh}+P_{atm}\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)+g(\mathcal{G}_{kh}(\frac{v^{2}}{2})-v\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v))+\sigma\mathcal{G}_{kh}\left(\frac{\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)}{(\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)^{2}+v^{\prime 2})^{1/2}}\right)\\
&[v]=h\\
&v(x)>0\ for\ all\ x\ \in\ \mathbb{R},\\
&the\ mapping\ x\rightarrow\left(\frac{x}{k}+\mathcal{C}_{kh}(v-h)(x),v(x)\right)\ is\ injective\ on\ \mathbb{R},\\
&v^{\prime}(x)^{2}+\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)(x)^{2}\neq 0\ for\ all\ x\ \in\ \mathbb{R},\end{split}

(2.31)

where
k=\frac{2\pi}{L}
. Moreover

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\left\{\left(a+\frac{x}{k}+\mathcal{C}_{kh}(v-h)(x),v(x)\right):x\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

(2.32)

Conversely, let
h>0
and
v\in C^{2,\alpha}_{2\pi}
be such that (
2.30
) holds. Assume also that
\mathcal{S}
is defined by (
2.32
),
let
\Omega
be the domain whose boundary consists of
\mathcal{S}
and of the real axis
\mathcal{B}
and let
a\in\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary. Then there exists a function
S
in
\Omega
such that
(\Omega,S)

is a solution of (
2.25
) of class
C^{2,\alpha}
.

Proof.

We first prove the necessity. Let
(\Omega,S)
be a solution of class
C^{2,\alpha}
of (
2.25
). Then we denote by
h
the conformal mean depth of
\Omega
and by
\tilde{U}+i\tilde{V}
the conformal mapping associated to
\Omega
. If we consider the mapping
U+iV:\mathcal{R}_{kh}\rightarrow\Omega
given by

\begin{aligned} U(x,y)=\tilde{U}\left(\frac{x}{k},\frac{y}{k}\right)\\
V(x,y)=\tilde{V}\left(\frac{x}{k},\frac{y}{k}\right)\end{aligned},\quad(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}_{kh},

(2.33)

where
k=\frac{2\pi}{L}
, then following the proof of Theorem 2.2 in
[
19
]
we see that
U,V\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{R_{h}})
and
U+iV
is a conformal mapping from
\mathcal{R}_{kh}
onto
\Omega
which extends homeomorphically to the closures of these domains, with onto mappings

\{(x,0):x\in\mathbb{R}\}\rightarrow\mathcal{S},

and

\{(x,-kh):x\in\mathbb{R}\}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}.

Moreover,

U_{x}^{2}(x,0)+V_{x}^{2}(x,0)\neq 0\,\,{\rm for}\,\,{\rm all}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
we set

v(x):=V(x,0),\quad u(x):=U(x,0).

Let us remark that the set
\{(u(x),v(x)):\,x\in\mathbb{R}\}
constitutes a reparametrization of the free surface
Y=\eta(X)
. Consequently, we have for all
x\in\mathbb{R}

\begin{split}v^{\prime}(x)&=u^{\prime}(x)\eta_{X}(u(x)),\\
v^{\prime\prime}(x)&=u^{\prime\prime}(x)\eta_{X}(u(x))+(u^{\prime}(x))^{2}\eta_{XX}(u(x)).\end{split}

(2.34)

Moreover, it holds that

u=\mathcal{C}_{kh}(v)

and

u^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)\ and\ u^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v^{\prime}).

(2.35)

It also follows
[
19
]
that
v\in C_{2\pi}^{2,\alpha}
and

[v]=h,\ \ \ \

v(x)>0\,\,{\rm for}\,\,{\rm all}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R},

{\rm the}\,\,{\rm map}\,\,x\rightarrow\left(\frac{x}{k}+\mathcal{C}_{kh}(v-h)(x),v(x)\right)\,\,{\rm is}\,\,{\rm injective}\,\,{\rm on}\,\,\mathbb{R},

\mathcal{S}=\left\{\left(a+\frac{x}{k}+\mathcal{C}_{kh}(v-h)(x),v(x)\right):x\in\mathbb{R}\right\},

(2.36)

for some
a\in\mathbb{R}
, whose presence in formula (
2.36
) is due to the invariance of problem (
2.25
) to horizontal translations. From (
2.35
) and the Cauchy-Riemann equations it follows that

v^{\prime}(x)^{2}+\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)(x)^{2}\neq 0\,\,{\rm for}\,\,{\rm all}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

Now let
\xi:\mathcal{R}_{kh}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined by

\xi(x,y)=S(U(x,y),V(x,y))-e(U(x,y),V(x,y)),\ (x,y)\in\mathcal{R}_{kh}.

(2.37)

The harmonicity in
\Omega
of the function
(X,Y)\rightarrow S(X,Y)+\frac{g}{2}Y^{2}-e(X,Y)
and the invariance of harmonic functions under conformal mappings imply that

\xi+\frac{g}{2}V^{2}\ \text{is \ harmonic \ in}\ \mathcal{R}_{kh}.

The chain rule and the Cauchy-Riemann equations imply that

\begin{pmatrix}S_{X}-e_{X}\\
S_{Y}-e_{Y}\end{pmatrix}\Big|_{(U(x,y),V(x,y))}=\frac{1}{v^{\prime 2}+\mathcal{G}_{kh}(v)^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}V_{y}&-V_{x}\\
V_{x}&V_{y}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{x}\\
\xi_{y}\end{pmatrix}\ in\ \overline{\mathcal{R}_{kh}}

(2.38)

Define
\zeta:\mathcal{R}_{kh}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
through

\zeta(x,y)=\xi(x,y)+\frac{g}{2}V^{2}(x,y).

(2.39)

From the local bifurcation theorem we obtain the bifurcation values

\lambda=\frac{k^{2}\sigma+g}{k}\tanh{(kh)}

(3.18)

Note that if we set
\sigma=0
in (
3.18
), we recover the well-known dispersion relation for irrotational gravity water waves over flows of finite depth, cf.
[
34
]
.
Moreover,
g=0
in the previous formula retrieves the dispersion relation for irrotational capillary waves, cf.
[
35
]
.

We obtain the corresponding values for the flow force
S_{0}
as

S_{0}=\frac{k^{2}\sigma h+gh}{k}\tanh(kh)+\frac{gh^{2}}{2}.

(3.19)

In the case of the gravity waves
(g=0)
formula (
3.19
) recovers formula (5.14) in
[
23
]
.

We can now formulate the results concerning the existence of small amplitude periodic capillary-gravity irrotational water waves.

Theorem 3.6
.

For any
h>0,\,k>0
and
S_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
k>\pi\cdot 10^{-2}
there exists laminar flows with a flat free surface in water of depth h and modified flow force at the surface
S_{0}
. The laminar flows with modified flow force
S_{0}
at the surface are exactly those with horizontal speeds at the flat free surface equal to
\sqrt{\lambda}
given by (
3.18
). The values of
S_{0}
of the flow force given by (
3.19
) trigger the appearance of periodic steady waves of small amplitude, with period
\frac{2\pi}{k}
and conformal mean depth h, which have a smooth profile with one crest and one trough per period, monotone between consecutive crests and troughs and symmetric about any crest line.

Proof.

Using the argument from the proof of Theorem 3.2 in [22] we see that
w=0
gives rise to laminar flows in the fluid domain bounded below by the rigid bed
\mathcal{B}
and above by the flat free surface
Y=h
.
These laminar flows are given by

S(X,Y)=-\frac{g}{2}Y^{2}+\left(\frac{S_{0}}{h}+\frac{gh}{2}\right)Y,\quad X\in\mathbb{R},\,0\leq Y\leq h.

Observe that
S_{Y|Y=h}=(c-u_{1})^{2}|_{Y=h}=\frac{S_{0}}{h}-\frac{gh}{2}
which shows that for laminar flows the horizontal velocity at the free surface coincides with
\sqrt{\lambda}
given in (
3.5
). Formula (
3.18
), detailing the speed
\sqrt{\lambda}
at the free surface in terms of the depth
h
and period
2\pi/k
, is called the
dispersion relation
.

Concerning the existence of waves of small amplitude with the properties mentioned in the statement of the theorem we apply the Crandall-Rabinowitz Theorem which asserts the existence of the local bifurcation curve

\{(\lambda(s),(0+o(s),s\cos{x}+o(s))):|s|<\varepsilon\}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{X}

consisting of the solutions of (
3.8
) with
\lambda
given by (
3.18
).

Choosing
\varepsilon
sufficiently small and using Lemma
2.6
we can ensure that

w(x)>-h\,\,{\rm for}\,\,{\rm all}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R},

and

\frac{1}{k}+\mathcal{C}_{kh}(w^{\prime})(x)>0\,\,{\rm for}\,\,{\rm all}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

The above inequality implies that the corresponding non-flat free surface
\mathcal{S}
given by (
2.36
) with
v=w+h
is the graph of a smooth function,
symmetric with respect to the points obtained for the values
x=n\pi,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. From

w(x;s)=s\cos{x}+o(s)\,\,{\rm in}\,\,C_{2\pi}^{p+1,\alpha},

we have that

sw^{\prime}(x;s)<0
for all
x\in(0,\pi),0<|s|<\varepsilon,

for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small and
p\geq 1.
Using the evenness of
x\to w(x;s)
we conclude the proof of the assertion about the free surface
\mathcal{S}
, i.e.
\mathcal{S}
has one crest and one trough per minimal period and is monotone between consecutive crests and troughs.
∎

Positivity of the moduli part
Florin Ambro, Paolo Cascini, Vyacheslav Shokurov, Calum Spicer

Abstract
. We prove the Cone Theorem for algebraically integrable foliations. As a consequence, we show that termination of flips implies the b b -nefness of the moduli part of a log canonical pair with respect to a contraction, generalising the case of lc trivial fibrations.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
14E30, 37F75

1.
Introduction

Starting from the work of Kodaira for elliptic surfaces, the canonical bundle formula has played an important role in birational geometry. Indeed, given any fibration between normal complex varieties and with trivial relative canonical divisor, this formula allows one to
compare the canonical divisor of the domain with the canonical divisor of the target. More specifically,
let
(X,B)
be a log canonical pair and let
f\colon X\to Z
be a contraction between normal varieties over
\mathbb{C}
, i.e. a proper surjective morphism with connected fibres.
If
f
is lc trivial, i.e. if
K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{R}}f^{*}D
for some
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on
Y
, then we may write
D=K_{Z}+B_{Z}+M_{Z}
, where
B_{Z}
is the discriminant of the pair
(X/Z,B)
and
M_{Z}
is its moduli part. Roughly speaking,
B_{Z}
measures the singularities of the fibres of
f
, whilst
M_{Z}
measures how far the fibration is from being isotrivial.
In particular, it is known that, after possibly replacing the fibration
f
by taking a base change through a birational morphism
\alpha\colon Z^{\prime}\to Z
, the moduli part
M_{Z}
of
(X/Z,B)
is nef and for any further birational base change the moduli part of the induced pair coincides with the pull back of
M_{Z}
(e.g. see
[
Kaw98
,
Amb04
,
Kol07
,
PS09
]
).

Building on the work of the third author (cf.
[
Sho13
,
Sho20
]
),
the aim of this paper is to generalise the above results to fibrations which are not necessarily lc trivial.
Indeed, given a log canonical pair
(X,B)
and a contraction
f\colon X\to Z
between normal varieties, we can easily generalise the notion of discriminant and moduli part of the pair
(X/Z,B)
(cf. Section
2.2
). In contrast to the case of lc trivial fibrations,
the moduli part of
(X/Z,B)
is no longer a divisor on
Z
but a divisor on
X
. On the other hand, as in the lc trivial case,
if
B
has rational coefficients, then after possibly replacing the contraction
f\colon X\to Z
by taking a base change through a birational morphism,
it follows that the moduli part of
(X/Z,B)
is compatible with pull-backs
(cf. Theorem
2.8
).
Moreover, if
K_{X}+B
is relatively pseudo-effective over
Z
, and assuming termination of log canonical flips, we can run a Minimal Model Program
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
of
K_{X}+B
over
Z
so that
K_{Y}+\phi_{*}B
is relatively nef. Under suitable conditions, we prove that this implies the nefness of the moduli part of
(Y/Z,\phi_{*}B)
(cf. Theorem
4.4
). As a consequence we have:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume termination of log canonical flips in dimension
n
.

Let
(X,B)
be a log canonical pair of dimension
n
and let
f\colon X\to Z
be a projective contraction such that
K_{X}+B
is relatively nef and
B\geq 0
.

Then there exist a log canonical pair
(Y,C)
and a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

where
\alpha\colon Z^{\prime}\to Z
is a projective birational morphism and
\beta\colon Y\dashrightarrow X
is a birational map such that

(1)

(X,B)
and
(Y,C)
are crepant over the generic point of
Z
(cf. Definition
2.3
);

(2)

the moduli part
M_{Y}
of
(Y/Z^{\prime},C)
is nef;

(3)

(Y/Z^{\prime},C)
is BP stable and log stable (cf. Definition
2.5
and Definition
2.10
); and

(4)

(Y/Z^{\prime},C)
is maximal (cf. Definition
2.20
).

We remark that in contrast to the lc trivial situation, it is not the case in general that the moduli
part
M_{X^{\prime}}
can be taken to be semi-ample (cf. Section
5.4
).

The main ingredient of the proof of the above Theorem is the study of the birational geometry of algebraically integrable foliations, i.e. foliations induced by a dominant map
X\dashrightarrow Z
. Indeed, given a contraction
f\colon X\to Z
, by taking a weakly semi-stable reduction (cf. Theorem
2.2
) and by adding a
vertical
\mathbb{R}
-divisor to
B
, we may assume that
f
is toroidal and equidimensional and that the moduli part of
(X/Z,B)
coincides with

K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta
where
K_{\mathcal{F}}
is the canonical divisor of the induced foliation

\mathcal{F}
and
\Delta
is the horizontal part of
B
(cf. Proposition
3.6
).
Moreover, this property is preserved after running a relative Minimal Model Program over
Z
(cf. Proposition
2.18
).
It now remains to show that if the moduli part of the induced pair is not nef, then there exists a rational curve which is vertical with respect to the fibration.
To this end, we prove a stronger result: the Cone Theorem (cf. Theorem
3.9
) holds for algebraically integrable foliations (see also
[
BM16
,
Spi20
,
CS21
]
for other versions of the Cone Theorem for foliations).
This implies that if
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
is a foliated log pair (cf. Section
3.2
)
then any
(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)
-negative extremal ray is spanned by a rational curve which is contained in a fibre of the induced morphism.

Elsewhere in the literature results like Theorem
1.1
are proven by analysing the semi-positivity properties
of
f_{*}\omega_{X/Y}
, which in turn is based on the study of variations of Hodge structures. By using foliations,
our proofs avoid such techniques.

We also provide several other applications of Theorem
3.9
.
For instance, we prove a version of Theorem
1.1
where “nef” is replaced by “pseudo-effective”,
(cf. Theorem
5.2
).
An important consequence of the main results of
[
CP19
]
is that if a projective manifold admits a foliation
\mathcal{F}

where
K_{\mathcal{F}}
is not pseudo-effective, then
K_{X}
is not pseudo-effective
[
CP19
, Theorem 1.2]
.
We prove the existence of the
K_{\mathcal{F}}
-MMP under some natural and mild conditions which allows
us to prove a version of
[
CP19
, Theorem 1.2]
on singular varieties.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a klt projective
variety and let
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
be a foliated pair such that
K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta
is not pseudo-effective
and such that
(X,\Delta)
is log canonical.

Then
K_{X}+\Delta
is not pseudo-effective. Moreover,
X
is covered by
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-negative curves
tangent to
\mathcal{F}
.

This is a direct consequence of Theorem
5.3
where we prove a
slightly more general statement.

Finally, the following theorem is a slight generalisation of a theorem by Ambro

[
Amb04
, Theorem 0.2]
in the klt case and Kollár
[
Kol07
, Proposition 8.4.9]
in the log canonical case.
Note that in contrast to the existing proofs, our proof does not make use of variation of Hodge structures, but it relies mainly on methods from the Minimal Model Program (see Section
5.3
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a quasi projective normal variety of dimension
n
and let

(X/Z,B)
be a GLC pair such that
B\geq 0
and such that the induced morphism
f\colon X\to Z
is a projective contraction. Assume that

(1)

(X/Z,B)
is BP stable over
Z
(cf. Definition
2.5
); and

(2)

K_{X}+B\sim_{f,\mathbb{R}}0
.

Then the moduli part
M_{X}
of
(X/Z,B)
is nef.

2.
Preliminaries

We work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Let
X
be a normal variety and let
D
be a
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
. For any prime divisor
S
in
X
, we define
m_{S}D
to be the coefficient of
D
along
S
.
If
f\colon X\dashrightarrow Z
is a dominant map between normal varieties, we say that a subvariety
W\subset X
is
horizontal
with respect to
f
if
W
dominates
Z
, otherwise we say that
W
is
vertical
with respect to
f
.
A
contraction

f\colon X\to Z
is a surjective proper morphism between normal varieties such that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{Z}
. Given a reduced divisor
P
on
Z
, we denote by
f^{-1}(P)
its preimage as a reduced divisor on
X
.

Two surjective morphisms
f\colon X\to Z
and
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}
between normal varieties are said to be
birationally equivalent
if there exists a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are birational maps.

A log pair
(X,B)
consists of a normal variety
X
and a
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B
on
X
such that
K_{X}+B
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier. We refer to
[
KM98
, Section 2.3]
for the classical definitions of singularities
(e.g. klt, log canonical, etc.. ) appearing in the Minimal Model Program.

2.1.
Toroidal pairs

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,\Sigma_{X})
be a log pair, where
\Sigma_{X}\geq 0
is a reduced divisor. We say that
(X,\Sigma_{X})
is a
toroidal pair
if for every closed point
x\in X
, there exists a toric variety
X_{\sigma}
, a closed point
t\in X_{\sigma}
and an isomorphism of complete local algebras

\phi_{x}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}\simeq\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\sigma},t}

such that the ideal of
\Sigma_{X}
maps to the invariant ideal of
X_{\sigma}\setminus T_{\sigma}
where
T_{\sigma}\subset X_{\sigma}
is the maximal torus. The pair
(X_{\sigma},t)
is called a
local model
at
x\in X
.

Let
(X,\Sigma_{X})
and
(Z,\Sigma_{Z})
be toroidal log pairs. A dominant morphism
f\colon X\to Z
is a
toroidal morphism
if for every closed point
x\in X
there exist local models
(X_{\sigma},t)
at
x
and
(Z_{\tau},s)
at
f(x)
, and a toric morphism
g\colon X_{\sigma}\to Z_{\tau}
so that the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\sigma},t}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Z,f(z)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{\tau},s}}

where the vertical maps are the algebra homomorphisms induced by
f
and
g
respectively.

2.2.
Discriminant and Moduli Part

Throughout this paper, a pair
(X/Z,B)
consists of a surjective proper morphism
f\colon X\to Z
between normal varieties and a log pair
(X,B)
. We say that the pair
(X/Z,B)
is
generically log canonical
, in short GLC,
if
Z
is irreducible and
the pair
(X,B)
is log canonical over the generic point of
Z
.

Let
(X/Z,B)
be a GLC pair. Consider a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

where
f^{\prime}
is a surjective proper morphism between normal varieties,
Z^{\prime}
is irreducible and
\beta
and
\alpha
are generically finite surjective projective morphisms.
We may write

K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}=\beta^{*}(K_{X}+B).

Then the
induced pair

(X^{\prime}/Z^{\prime},B^{\prime})
is GLC (cf.
[
KM98
, Proposition 5.20]
).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(X/Z,B)
and
(X^{\prime}/Z^{\prime},B^{\prime})
be GLC pairs such that the induced morphisms
f\colon X\to Z
and
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}
are birational equivalent contractions. We say that
(X/Z,B)
and
(X^{\prime}/Z^{\prime},B^{\prime})
are
crepant
over the generic point of
Z
if, given birational morphisms
p\colon W\to X
and
q\colon W\to X^{\prime}
which resolve the indeterminacy of the induced map
X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
, we have that

p^{*}(K_{X}+B)-q^{*}(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime})

is vertical with respect to the induced morphism
W\to Z
.

Let
(X/Z,B)
be a GLC pair. For any prime divisor
P\subset Y
, we define

\gamma_{P}=\sup\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid(X,B+tf^{*}P)\text{ is log canonical over the generic point of }P\}.

The
discriminant
of
(X/Z,B)
is the
\mathbb{R}
-divisor

B_{Z}:=\sum_{P}(1-\gamma_{P})P.

We now define the moduli part of a GLC pair
(X/Z,B)
such that the induced morphism
f\colon X\to Z
is a contraction.
We first assume that
f
is equidimensional and

K_{Z}+B_{Z}
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier. In this case, we define the moduli part
M_{X}
of
(X/Z,B)
as

M_{X}\coloneqq K_{X}+B-f^{*}(K_{Z}+B_{Z}).

Note that
M_{X}
is only defined up to linear equivalence, as it depends on the choice of the canonical divisors
K_{X}
and
K_{Z}
.

Consider now any GLC pair
(X/Z,B)
, induced by a morphism
f\colon X\to Z
. By Theorem
2.2
, there exists an equidimensional contraction
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}
which is birational equivalent to
f
, such that
Z^{\prime}
is smooth and the induced maps
\alpha\colon Z^{\prime}\to Z
and
\beta\colon X^{\prime}\to X
are birational morphisms. Let
M_{X^{\prime}}
be the moduli part of the induced pair
(X^{\prime}/Z^{\prime},B^{\prime})
. We then define the
moduli part
of
(X/Z,B)
as

M_{X}\coloneqq\alpha_{*}M_{X^{\prime}}.

As above,
M_{X}
is only defined up to linear equivalence. It is easy to check that
M_{X}
does not depend on the choice of
f^{\prime}
.

.

Let
(X/Z,B)
be a GLC pair and let

2.3.
Boundary property

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(X/Z,B)
be a GLC pair induced by a morphism
f\colon X\to Z
. The pair
(X/Z,B)
is said to be
BP stable
(resp.
BP semi-stable
) over
Z
if
K_{Z}+B_{Z}
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier and
for any contraction
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}
which is birational equivalent to
f
and such that the induced maps
\alpha\colon Z^{\prime}\to Z
and
\beta\colon X^{\prime}\to X
are projective birational morphisms, if

B^{\prime}_{Z^{\prime}}
is the discriminant of the induced pair
(X^{\prime}/Z^{\prime},B^{\prime})
and if we write

K_{Z^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}_{Z}=\alpha^{*}(K_{Z}+B_{Z})

then
B^{\prime}_{Z}=B^{\prime}_{Z^{\prime}}
(resp.
B^{\prime}_{Z}\geq B^{\prime}_{Z^{\prime}}
).

We say that a GLC pair
(X/Z,B)
induced by a morphism
f\colon X\to Z
satisfies the
boundary property
if there exist a surjective projective morphism
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}
between normal varieties, projective birational morphisms
\alpha\colon Z^{\prime}\to Z
and
\beta\colon X^{\prime}\to X
, and a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

such that the induced pair
(X^{\prime}/Z^{\prime},B^{\prime})
is BP stable over
Z^{\prime}
.

Remark 2.6
.

(1)

Let
(X/Z,B)
be a GLC pair and let
\beta\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a birational morphism between normal varieties. Let
(X^{\prime}/Z,B^{\prime})
be the induced GLC pair.
Then the discriminant
B_{Z}
of
(X/Z,B)
coincides with the discriminant
B^{\prime}_{Z}
of
(X^{\prime}/Z,B^{\prime})
. In particular,
(X/Z,B)
is BP stable over
Z
if and only if
(X^{\prime}/Z,B^{\prime})
is BP stable over
Z
.

(2)

Let
(X/Z,B)
be a GLC pair induced by a contraction
f\colon X\to Z
and which is BP stable over
Z
. Then, it is easy to check that

M_{X}=K_{X}+B-f^{*}(K_{Z}+B_{Z}).

Example 2.7
.

Let
(X/Z,B)
be a GLC pair induced by a birational morphism
f\colon X\to Z
. Then
B_{Z}=f_{*}B
and
M_{X}=0
. It follows that
(X/Z,B)
is BP stable over
Z
if and only if
K_{Z}+B_{Z}
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier and
f
is
(K_{X}+B)
-crepant, i.e.

K_{X}+B=f^{*}(K_{Z}+B_{Z}).

2.4.
Log stable pairs

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
F
be a valuation over
X
.

Then there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
B
such that
(X,B)
is log canonical and
F
is the only log canonical centre of
(X,B)
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor such that
(X,\Delta)
is a log pair. We may assume that

\lfloor{\Delta}\rfloor\leq 0
. Let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be a log resolution of
(X,\Delta)
such that
F
is a divisor on
Y
. We may write

K_{Y}+\Gamma=f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta).

Let
t\in\mathbb{Q}
be such that
m_{F}(\Gamma+tF)=1
and
let
A_{1},A_{2}\geq 0
sufficiently general ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors on
Y
such that

\Gamma+tF+A_{1}-A_{2}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0,

(Y,\Gamma+tF+A_{1}-A_{2})
is log canonical and
F
is its only log canonical place.
We may then define
B\coloneqq\Delta+\pi_{*}(A_{1}-A_{2})
, to obtain the desired
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor.
∎

Definition 2.10
.

Let
(X/Z,B)
be a GLC pair induced by a contraction
f\colon X\to Z
and let
B_{Z}
be its discriminant. Then
(X/Z,B)
is said to be
log stable
if for any
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor
H
on
Z
we have that

(X,B+f^{*}H)
is log canonical if and only if
(Z,B_{Z}+H)
is log canonical.

Theorem 2.11
.

[
Sho20
, Proposition 4]

Let
(X/Z,B)
be a GLC pair induced by a contraction
f\colon X\to Z
.

Then
(X/Z,B)
is log stable if and only if
(X/Z,B)
is BP stable over
Z
.

Proof.

Let
B_{Z}
be the discriminant of
(X/Z,B)
.

Assume first that
(X/Z,B)
is BP stable over
Z
. Let
H
be a
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
Z
and note that
B_{Z}+H
is the discriminant of
(X/Z,B+f^{*}H)
. Assume that
(X,B+f^{*}H)
is not log canonical. Then, by Remark
2.6
, we may assume that
B+f^{*}H
has a component
S
of coefficient greater than one. Since
(X/Z,B+f^{*}H)
is BP stable over
Z
, after possibly replacing
X
and
Z
by higher models, we may assume that
f(S)
is a divisor on
Z
and the coefficient of
B_{Z}+H
along such a divisor is greater than one. Thus,
(Z,B_{Z}+H)
is not log canonical. Vice versa, assume that
(Z,B_{Z}+H)
is not log canonical. Then, since
(X/Z,B+f^{*}H)
is BP stable over
Z
, after possibly replacing
X
and
Z
by higher models, we may assume that a component of
B_{Z}+H
has coefficient greater than one and, therefore,
(X,B+f^{*}H)
is not log canonical. Thus,
(X/Z,B)
is log stable.

Assume now that
(X/Z,B)
is log stable. Let
F
be a valuation over
Z
. By Lemma
2.9
, there exists a
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor
H
on
Z
such that
(Z,B_{Z}+H)
is log canonical and
F
is the unique log canonical place for
(Z,B_{Z}+H)
.

B^{\prime}_{Y}
is the discriminant of
(W/Y,B^{\prime}_{W})
.
Moreover, since
(Z,B_{Z})
is log canonical, so is
(Y,B_{Y})
. Note though that
(Y,B_{Y})
is not necessarily the discriminant of
(W/Y,B_{W})
.

We have

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=(K_{W}+B_{W}-g^{*}(K_{Y}+B_{Y}))-(K_{W}+B^{\prime}_{W}-g^{*}(K_{Y}+B^{\prime}_{Y}))

\displaystyle=B_{W}-g^{*}B_{Y}-B^{\prime}_{W}+g^{*}B^{\prime}_{Y}.

Let
E
be a prime divisor on
X
which is vertical with respect to
f
and let
E^{\prime}
be its strict transform on
W
.
We claim that
m_{E^{\prime}}A\geq 0
.
Note that, by the negativity lemma and since
M_{X^{\prime}}
is nef, the claim immediately implies the Proposition.

Let
P\coloneqq f(E)
. We distinguish two cases. If
P
is contained in
\Sigma_{Z}
then, since
(X/Z,B)
satisfies Property
(*)
, it follows that
m_{E}B=1
and, therefore,

m_{E^{\prime}}B_{W}=1.

Since
(X^{\prime}/Z^{\prime},B^{\prime})
is BP stable over
Z^{\prime}
, it follows that
(W/Y,B^{\prime}_{W})
is BP stable over
Y
and,
Theorem
2.11
implies that it is log stable.
Thus, since
(Y,B_{Y})
is log canonical and
B^{\prime}_{Y}
is the discriminant of
(W/Y,B^{\prime}_{W})
, it follows that
(W,B^{\prime}_{W}+g^{*}(B_{Y}-B^{\prime}_{Y}))
is log canonical and, in particular,

m_{E^{\prime}}(B^{\prime}_{W}+g^{*}(B_{Y}-B^{\prime}_{Y}))\leq 1.

Thus,
m_{E^{\prime}}A\geq 0
, as claimed.

Assume now that
P
is not contained in
\Sigma_{Z}
. Lemma
2.14
implies that
f
is equidimensional outside
\Sigma_{Z}
and, therefore, it follows that
P
is a divisor in
Z
. Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{Z}+P
. Then, since
(X/Z,B)
satisfies Property
(*)
, we have that

m_{E}(B+f^{*}(\Sigma-\Sigma_{Z}))=1
.
Thus, if
P^{\prime}
is the strict transform of
P
in
Y
, we have that

m_{E^{\prime}}(B_{W}+g^{*}P^{\prime})=1.

Since
m_{P^{\prime}}B^{\prime}_{Y}=1-\gamma_{P^{\prime}}
where

\gamma_{P^{\prime}}=\sup\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid(W,B_{W}+tg^{*}P^{\prime})\text{ is log canonical over the generic point of }P^{\prime}\},

and since
m_{P^{\prime}}B_{Y}=0
, it follows that

\displaystyle m_{E^{\prime}}A

\displaystyle=m_{E^{\prime}}(B_{W}+g^{*}P^{\prime}-B^{\prime}_{W}-g^{*}(P^{\prime}-B^{\prime}_{Y}))

\displaystyle=1-m_{E^{\prime}}(B^{\prime}_{W}+\gamma_{P^{\prime}}g^{*}P^{\prime})\geq 0.

Thus, our claim follows again.
∎

Note that, using the same notation as Proposition

, the same proof as above shows that the same result holds if if we replace (1) by

(1)’

(X/Z,B)
satisfies Property

and

3.
Cone theorem for algebraically integrable foliations

The goal here is to explain a proof of the cone theorem for algebraically integrable foliations. This is the main ingredient in the proof of Theorem
4.4
.

Some of these ideas were first considered in
[
KMM92
]
and
[
Miy93
]

in the case of a smooth morphism using the notion of a relative deformation.
By adopting the foliated viewpoint
we are able to work on singular varieties as well as working with rational dominant maps.

3.1.
Definitions

A
foliation of rank
r
on a normal variety
X
is a coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset T_{X}
of rank
r
such that

(1)

T_{\mathcal{F}}
is saturated, i.e.
T_{X}/T_{\mathcal{F}}
is torsion free; and

(2)

T_{\mathcal{F}}
is closed under Lie bracket.

The
co-rank
of
\mathcal{F}
is its co-rank as a subsheaf of
T_{X}
. We define the canonical divisor
K_{\mathcal{F}}
to be a divisor on
X
so that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{\mathcal{F}})\cong(\operatorname{det}T_{\mathcal{F}})^{*}

and we define the conormal divisor
K_{[X/\mathcal{F}]}
to be a divisor on
X
so that

\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{[X/\mathcal{F}]})\cong(\operatorname{det}(T_{X}/T_{\mathcal{F}}))^{*}
.
For any positive integer
d
, we denote
\Omega^{[d]}_{X}\coloneqq(\wedge^{d}\Omega^{1}_{X})^{**}
.
Then, the foliation
\mathcal{F}
induces a map

\phi\colon(\Omega^{[r]}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{\mathcal{F}}))^{**}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}.

The
singular locus
of
\mathcal{F}
, denoted by
\operatorname{Sing}\mathcal{F}
, is the cosupport of the image of
\phi
.

Let
\sigma\colon Y\dashrightarrow X
be a dominant map between normal varieties and let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation of rank
r
on
X
. We denote by
\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{F}
the
induced foliation
on
Y
(e.g. see
[
Dru21
, Section 3.2]
).
If
T_{\mathcal{F}}=0
, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}
is the foliation by points on
X
,
then we refer to
\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{F}
as the
foliation induced by
\sigma
.
In this case, the foliation
\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{F}
is called
algebraically integrable
.

Let
f\colon X\to Z
be a morphism between normal varieties and let
\mathcal{F}
be the induced foliation on
X
.
If
f
is equidimensional and
Z
is smooth, then we define the
ramification divisor

R(f)
of
f
as

R(f)=\sum_{D}(\phi^{*}D-\phi^{-1}(D))

where the sum runs through all the prime divisors of
Z
. In this case, we have

K_{\mathcal{F}}\sim K_{X/Z}-R(f)

(e.g. see
[
Dru17
, Notation 2.7 and §2.9]
).

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
\mathcal{F}
be a rank
r
foliation on
X
.
Let
S\subset X
be a subvariety. Then
S
is said to be
\mathcal{F}
-invariant
, or
invariant by
\mathcal{F}
, if for any open subset
U\subset X
and any section
\partial\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{F})
, we have that

\partial(\mathcal{I}_{S\cap U})\subset\mathcal{I}_{S\cap U},

where
\mathcal{I}_{S\cap U}
denotes the ideal sheaf of
S\cap U
.
If
D\subset X
is a prime divisor then we define
\epsilon(D)=1
if
D
is not
\mathcal{F}
-invariant and
\epsilon(D)=0
if it is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant. Note that if
\mathcal{F}
is the foliation induced by a dominant map
\sigma\colon Y\dashrightarrow Z
, then a divisor
D
is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant if and only if it is vertical with respect to
\sigma
.

3.2.
Log canonical foliated pairs

Let
X
be a normal variety.
A
foliated pair

(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
on
X
consists of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
X
and a
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
divisor
\Delta

such that
K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.

Let
X
be a smooth variety, let
\mathcal{F}
be an algebraically integrable foliation on
X
and let
\Sigma_{X}
be a reduced divisor.
We say that the foliated pair
(\mathcal{F},\Sigma_{X})
is
foliated log smooth
if

(1)

(X,\Sigma_{X})
is a toroidal pair; and

(2)

there exists a log smooth toroidal pair
(Z,\Sigma_{Z})
and a toroidal contraction
f\colon X\to Z
which
induces
\mathcal{F}
.

Let
(X,B)
be a log pair,
f\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
a dominant map between normal varieties and let
\mathcal{F}
be the foliation induced by
f
. Then, by Theorem
2.2
,
there exists a birational morphism
m\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X

so that
(m^{-1}\mathcal{F},\operatorname{Supp}m_{*}^{-1}B+\operatorname{Exc}m)
is foliated log smooth.
We call such a modification a
foliated log resolution
provided
\operatorname{Exc}m
is of pure codimension one.

Given a birational morphism
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X

and a foliated pair
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
on
X
,
let
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
be the pulled back foliation on
\tilde{X}

and
\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta
be the strict transform of
\Delta
in
\widetilde{X}
.
We may write

K_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)+\sum a(E,\mathcal{F},\Delta)E.

where
\pi_{*}K_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}}=K_{\mathcal{F}}
, the sum runs over all the prime
\pi
-exceptional divisors on
\widetilde{X}
and the rational number
a(E,\mathcal{F},\Delta)
is called the
discrepancy
of
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
with respect to
E
.
We say that the foliated pair
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
is
log canonical

if

a(E,\mathcal{F},\Delta)\geq-\epsilon(E)
,
for any birational morphism
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
and for any prime
\pi
-exceptional divisor
E
on
\widetilde{X}
.
We say that
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
is
F-dlt
if there exists a
foliated log resolution which only extracts divisors
E

of discrepancy
a(E,\mathcal{F},\Delta)>-\epsilon(E)
.

Let
W\subset X
be a subvariety. We say that
W
is a
log canonical centre
of
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
if
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
is log canonical at the generic point of
W
and there exists a birational morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
and a prime divisor
E
on
Y
of discrepancy
a(E,\mathcal{F},\Delta)=-\epsilon(E)
and whose centre in
X
is
W
. The divisor
E
is called a
log canonical place
of
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
.
We say that
W
is a
non log canonical centre
of
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
if there exists a birational morphism
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\to X
and a prime divisor
E
on
\tilde{X}
of discrepancy
a(E,\mathcal{F},\Delta)<-\epsilon(E)
and whose centre in
X
is
W
.
We denote by
\operatorname{nlc}\,(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
the set of all the non log canonical centres of
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(X,\Sigma_{X})
and
(Z,\Sigma_{Z})
be toroidal pairs, let
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
be a toroidal contraction
and let
\mathcal{F}
be the induced foliation.
Let
\Delta
be the horizontal part of
\Sigma_{X}
.

Then
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
is log canonical.

Proof.

The statement is local about a point
x\in X
. Thus, we may freely assume that
(X,\Sigma_{X})
is a toric pair,
x
is the unique closed orbit
and
\mathcal{F}
is a torus invariant foliation.

If
\mathcal{F}
is of co-rank
q
then
\mathcal{F}

can be defined by a logarithmic torus invariant
q
-form
\omega=\sum_{|I|=q}\alpha_{I}\frac{dx_{I}}{x_{I}}
, for some
\alpha_{I}\in\mathbb{C}
.
Let
D
denote the polar locus of
\omega
, and observe that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{[X/\mathcal{F}]})=\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)
. Note that
D
is

\mathcal{F}
-invariant and torus invariant. In particular, since no component of
\Delta
is invariant,
it follows that
D
and
\Delta
have no components in common.

Let
b\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
be a birational morphism, set
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=b^{-1}\mathcal{F}
and let
\omega^{\prime}=b^{*}\omega
.
Note that

\omega^{\prime}=b^{*}\omega
is a logarithmic
q
-form with polar locus equal to

b_{*}^{-1}D+\sum\delta_{i}E_{i}
where
E_{i}
are the
b
-exceptional divisors
and
\delta_{i}\in\{0,1\}
. In fact, observe that
\delta_{i}=1-\epsilon(E_{i})
.
So we have
K_{[X^{\prime}/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}]}=b_{*}^{-1}K_{[X/\mathcal{F}]}+\sum(\epsilon(E_{i})-1)E_{i}
.

Thus, if we write

K_{X^{\prime}}+b_{*}^{-1}(D+\Delta)=b^{*}(K_{X}+D+\Delta)+F

and

K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+b_{*}^{-1}\Delta=b^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)+G

then we see that
G=F+\sum(1-\epsilon(E_{i}))E_{i}
.
Our claim then follows by observing that
(X,D+\Delta)
is log canonical.
∎

3.3.
Adjunction results

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial variety and let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation induced
by a contraction
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
. Let
T
be a prime divisor on
X

with normalisation
\nu\colon S\rightarrow T
.
Let
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
be a foliated pair on
X
where
\Delta\geq 0
is such that
m_{T}\Delta=\epsilon(T)
.

Then

(1)

there exists a foliated pair
(\mathcal{F}_{S},\Delta_{S})
on
S

such that
\Delta_{S}\geq 0
and

\nu^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)=K_{\mathcal{F}_{S}}+\Delta_{S};

(2)

if
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
is log canonical, then
(\mathcal{F}_{S},\Delta_{S})
is also log canonical; and

(3)

if
\epsilon(T)=1
and
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
is F-dlt then
(\mathcal{F}_{S},\Delta_{S})
is F-dlt.

The induced foliation
\mathcal{F}_{S}
on
S
is called the
restricted foliation
.
Observe that if
S
is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant then
\mathcal{F}_{S}
is not necessarily the foliation induced by

S\rightarrow Y
where
Y
is the normalisation of
f(T)
. For example, consider the blow up at a point of a smooth foliation induced by a smooth morphism from a threefold to a surface and let
S
be the exceptional divisor. However, it is still the case that
\mathcal{F}_{S}
is induced by a rational dominant map.

Proof.

We argue in cases based on
\epsilon(T)
.

Case I:
\epsilon(T)=0
.

Let
U\subset X
be an open subset containing the general point of
T
and not intersecting

\operatorname{Sing}\mathcal{F}\cup\operatorname{Sing}X\cup\operatorname{Sing}T
. Let
T^{\prime}=T\cap U
. Then, since
T
is invariant, we have that, over
U
, the inclusion of sheaves

T_{\mathcal{F}}|_{T^{\prime}}\to{T_{X}}|_{T^{\prime}}
factors through
T_{T^{\prime}}
and, in particular, it defines a foliation on
T^{\prime}
which yields a foliation
\mathcal{F}_{S}
on
S
. The existence of
\Delta_{S}
follows easily from
[
AD14
, Lemma 3.7]
. Thus, (1) follows.

We now prove (2).
Assume that
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
is log canonical.
Let
m\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
be a log resolution of
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
and let
S^{\prime}
be the strict transform of
T
. We may write

K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}+F_{0}+F_{1}=m^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta)

where
\Delta^{\prime}=m_{*}^{-1}\Delta
and
F_{0},F_{1}
are
\mathbb{R}
-divisors on
X^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{Supp}F_{0}
is
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
-invariant and
\operatorname{Supp}F_{1}
is not
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
-invariant. Since
(\mathcal{F},\Delta)
is log canonical, we have that
F_{0}\leq 0
and the coefficients of
F_{1}
are at most one.
We define

K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}|_{S^{\prime}}=K_{\mathcal{F}_{S^{\prime}}}+\Theta_{S^{\prime}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta_{S^{\prime}}\coloneqq\Theta_{S^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}|_{S^{\prime}}.

We may replace
m
by a higher model so that
(\mathcal{F}_{S^{\prime}},\Delta_{S^{\prime}})
is also log smooth. Let
n\colon S^{\prime}\to S
be the induced morphism. We have

K_{\mathcal{F}_{S^{\prime}}}+\Theta_{S^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}|_{S^{\prime}}+F_{0}|_{S^{\prime}}+F_{1}|_{S^{\prime}}=(K_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}+F_{0}+F_{1})|_{S^{\prime}}=n^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}_{S}}+\Delta_{S}).

We now make note of some easy consequences of log smoothness of
(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}+\operatorname{Exc}m)
. Indeed, each of these claims
is local, and so we may assume in fact that
(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}+\operatorname{Exc}m)
is induced by a toric morphism in which case these claims are easy to verify.
First, we note that
\Theta_{S^{\prime}}
is a reduced divisor supported
exactly on the codimension one components of
\operatorname{Sing}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\cap S^{\prime}
.
Next,
\Delta^{\prime}+F_{1}
contains no codimension
2
components of
\operatorname{Sing}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and so

\Theta_{S^{\prime}}
and
(\Delta^{\prime}+F_{1})|_{S^{\prime}}
have
no components in common.
Finally, no component of

F_{1}|_{S^{\prime}}
is
\mathcal{F}_{S^{\prime}}
-invariant
and no component of
\operatorname{Sing}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\cap S^{\prime}
is
\mathcal{F}_{S^{\prime}}
invariant.
From these three claims we see that
(\mathcal{F}_{S^{\prime}},\Theta_{S^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}|_{S^{\prime}}+F_{0}|_{S^{\prime}}+F_{1}|_{S^{\prime}})
is log canonical
and so
(\mathcal{F}_{S},\Delta_{S})
is log canonical from which (2) follows.

Case II:
\epsilon(T)=1
.

Note that in this case
T
dominates
Z
, let
g\colon S\to Z
be the induced morphism and let
\mathcal{F}_{S}
be the induced foliation.
Let
H
be a general ample divisor on
X
, let
h\colon H\rightarrow Z
be the induced morphism and let
\mathcal{F}_{H}

be the induced foliation.

We now show that
\Delta_{S}\geq 0
by induction on
\operatorname{dim}(X/Z)
.
If
\operatorname{dim}(X/Z)=1
then
we may apply
[
CS20
, Proposition 2.19]
to see that
\nu^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+T)=D\geq 0

(note that the threefold hypothesis there is not necessary) from which we may conclude.
Observe that if
H
is sufficiently general and if we write
(K_{\mathcal{F}}+H))|_{H}=D_{H}\geq 0

then
D_{H}
is supported on the locus where
H
is tangent to
\mathcal{F}
. In particular, if
H
is sufficiently general
and
E\subset H
is a divisor then
m_{E}D_{H}=0
if
h_{*}E=0
and
m_{E}D_{H}=r_{E}-1
if
h_{*}E\neq 0

where
r_{E}
is the ramification index of
h
along
E
.

Suppose now that
\operatorname{dim}(X/Z)>1
.
By
[
Dru21
, Proposition 3.6]
we see that
(K_{\mathcal{F}}+H)|_{H}=K_{\mathcal{F}_{H}}
.
Let
S_{H}\rightarrow T\cap H
be the normalisation morphism.
Write
(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta+H)|_{H}=K_{\mathcal{F}_{H}}+\Delta|_{H}
and observe that

\epsilon(T\cap H)=1
.
By induction if we write
(K_{\mathcal{F}_{H}}+\Delta|_{H})|_{S_{H}}=K_{\mathcal{F}_{S_{H}}}+\Delta_{S_{H}}

then
\Delta_{S_{H}}\geq 0
. On the other hand, we have
(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta+H)|_{S}=K_{\mathcal{F}_{S}}+\Delta_{S}+H_{S}

where
H_{S}\cong S_{H}
. If
\operatorname{dim}(S/Z)>1
then again by
[
Dru21
, Proposition 3.6]
we have
\Delta_{S_{H}}=\Delta_{S}|_{H_{S}}

and so
\Delta_{S}\geq 0
. If
\operatorname{dim}(S/Z)=1
then we have

(K_{\mathcal{F}_{S}}+\Delta_{S}+H_{S})|_{H_{S}}=K_{\mathcal{F}_{S_{H}}}+\Delta_{S}|_{H_{S}}+D

where
D\geq 0
is supported on the ramification divisor of
H_{S}\rightarrow Z
. In this case

0\leq\Delta_{S_{H}}=\Delta_{S}|_{H_{S}}+D
.
By choosing
H
to be sufficiently general we may arrange it so that
D
and
\Delta_{S}|_{H_{S}}
have no components in common
from which we may conclude that
\Delta_{S}\geq 0
.

(2) and (3) follow by similar arguments as in the previous case.
∎

3.4.
Tangent subvarieties

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation induced by a rational map
X\dashrightarrow Z
.
We say a subvariety
V\subset X
is
tangent
to
\mathcal{F}
if there exist

(1)

a birational modification
\mu\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
and an equidimensional contraction

X^{\prime}\rightarrow Z^{\prime}
which induces
\mu^{-1}\mathcal{F}
; and

(2)

a subvariety
V^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}
contained in a fibre of
X^{\prime}\rightarrow Z^{\prime}
such that
V=\mu(V^{\prime})
.

We remark that when
X
and
\mathcal{F}
are smooth this agrees with the usual definition of tangency.
We collect some basic observations about tangent subvarieties below.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation induced by a rational map
X\dashrightarrow Z
.

(1)

Let
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
be a birational morphism and let
W\subset Y
be a subvariety
tangent to
\pi^{-1}\mathcal{F}
. Then
\pi(W)
is tangent to
\mathcal{F}
.

(2)

Let
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow X
be a birational morphism and let
V\subset X
be tangent to
\mathcal{F}
.
Then there exists a subvariety
W\subset Y
tangent to
\pi^{-1}\mathcal{F}
and such that
\pi(W)=V
.

(3)

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is induced by a morphism
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
and suppose that
V
is tangent to
\mathcal{F}
.
Then
V
is contained in a fibre of
X\rightarrow Z
.

(4)

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is induced by a morphism
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
and let
\nu\colon S\rightarrow T\subset X

be the normalisation of a divisor with restricted foliation
\mathcal{F}_{S}
. If
V\subset S
is tangent to
\mathcal{F}_{S}
then
\nu(V)

is tangent to
\mathcal{F}
.

Remark 3.4
.

As above,
consider the blow up at a point of a smooth threefold which admits a smooth morphism to a surface, let
\mathcal{F}
be the induced foliation and let
S
be the exceptional divisor.
Then the restricted foliation
\mathcal{F}_{S}
is the radial foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. A curve in
S
which is not
\mathcal{F}_{S}
-invariant is also not tangent to
\mathcal{F}
, even though it is contained in a fibre of the morphism inducing
\mathcal{F}
. Indeed, the induced morphism is not equidimensional.

Proof.

(1) is a straightforward consequence of the definition of tangency.

Now, let
\mu\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
be a modification
so that
\mu^{-1}\mathcal{F}
is induced by an equidimensional contraction
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow Z^{\prime}
and there exists a subvariety
V^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}

contained in a fibre of
f^{\prime}
so that
\mu(V^{\prime})=V
.

We now prove (2). Let
\nu\colon X^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow X^{\prime}
be a birational morphism so that
\nu^{-1}\mu^{-1}\mathcal{F}
is induced by an equidimensional
contraction
f^{\prime\prime}\colon X^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow Z^{\prime\prime}
and so that we have a morphism
p\colon X^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow Y
.
Observe that we may find a subvariety
V^{\prime\prime}\subset X^{\prime\prime}
contained in a fibre of
f^{\prime\prime}
so that
\nu(V^{\prime\prime})=V^{\prime}
and so we may take

W=p(V^{\prime\prime})
, as required.

We now prove (3).
We may assume that there exists a birational morphism

.

is contained in a fibre of

is contained in a fibre of

.

We finally prove (4).
Let

be a modification
so that

is induced by an equidimensional contraction

.
By replacing

with a higher model, we may freely assume
that we have a morphism

By (2) there exists

which is tangent to

and such that

.
By (3) we know that

is contained in a fibre of

is contained in a fibre

5.4.
A counterexample to the semi-ampleness of the moduli part

In contrast to the
f
-trivial case, in general we cannot hope for the semi-ampleness
of the moduli part, as the following examples show. These examples also show that the
abundance conjecture does not hold for algebraically integrable foliations.

Both of the following examples are adapted from
[
Kee99
, Section 3]
.

Example 5.4
.

Let
C
be a curve of genus
g\geq 2
, let
X=C\times C
, let
\Delta
denote the diagonal and let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow C

be the projection onto the first coordinate.
Then
(X/Z,\Delta)
satisfies Property
(*)
with discriminant
B_{C}=0
. Thus, the moduli part of
(X/Z,\Delta)
is
M_{X}=K_{X/C}+\Delta
.
But
[
Kee99
, Theorem 3]
implies that
M_{X}
is big and nef, but not semi-ample. Clearly, in this case, the pair
(X,\Delta)
is log canonical but not klt.

Example 5.5
.

We continue to use the same notation as in the above example, Let
E
be an elliptic curve and let
P\in E
be a closed point. Let
Y=C\times E
and let
S
be the family of curves obtained
by gluing
Y
to
X
along the curves
C\times\{P\}
and
\Delta
. This gives a family of stable curves
S\rightarrow C

which corresponds
to a morphism
C\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. Let
\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
denote the universal family.

We may find a projective surface
T
and a generically finite morphism
f\colon T\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
so that
f(T)
contains the image
\overline{C}
of
C
.
Let
Z=T\times_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}\mathcal{U}
and observe that
Z
is a projective threefold.
Let
p\colon Z\rightarrow T
be the induced morphism. Observe that
p
is semi-stable and that
K_{Z}
is relatively nef over
Z
. Thus, if
B=0
then
(Z/T,B)
satisfies the Property
(*)
with discriminant
B_{T}=0
and, by Theorem
4.3
, it is BP stable over
Z
.
It follows that
K_{Z/T}
is the moduli part of

(Z/T,B)
.
Let
C^{\prime}
be an irreducible component of
f^{-1}(\overline{C})
and let
S^{\prime}=p^{-1}(C^{\prime})
. Observe that
S^{\prime}=X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime}
where

\sigma\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
is
finite and
ramified only along fibres of
X^{\prime}\rightarrow C^{\prime}

It follows that
K_{Z/T}|_{X^{\prime}}=K_{X^{\prime}/C^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}=\sigma^{*}(K_{X/C}+\Delta)
where
\Delta^{\prime}
is the graph of
C^{\prime}\rightarrow C
.
In particular,
K_{Z/T}
is not semi-ample.

One might instead ask if something weaker holds true, namely, if
\nu(K_{\mathcal{F}})=\kappa(K_{\mathcal{F}})

for any algebraically integrable foliation
\mathcal{F}
with canonical singularities and such that
K_{\mathcal{F}}
is nef. In light of Section
5.3

any such counter example must have
1\leq\nu(K_{\mathcal{F}})\leq\operatorname{dim}X-1
.

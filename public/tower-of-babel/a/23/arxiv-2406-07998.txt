Scaling properties of (2+1) directed polymers in the low temperature limit
Victor Dotsenko

Abstract
In terms of the replica method we consider the low temperature limit of (2+1) directed polymers in a random potential. The proposed approach allows to compute the scaling exponent θ \theta of the free energy fluctuations as well as the left tail of its probability distribution function. It is argued that θ = 1 / 4 \theta=1/4 which is slightly different from the zero-temperature numerical value which is close to 0.241 0.241 .

I
Introduction

In contrast to the achievements in the studies of one-dimensional directed polymers
as well as the other systems belonging to the so called KPZ universality class

[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
, not so much is achieved in understanding of the scaling properties
of the so called (2+1) model of directed polymers which describes the fluctuations
of an elastic string directed along the time axes and which passes through
a random medium in the three-dimensional space

[
6
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
7
]
.
Extensive numerical simulations as well as experimental studies rather convincingly demonstrate that in the
limit of large times
t
the free energy fluctuations
of such polymers scale as
t^{\theta}
with the scaling exponent

\theta\simeq 0.241
which is very close but not equal to
1/4

[
12
,
13
,
14
]
. On the other hand,
in the recent analytical study
[
15
]
it has been argued that
in the
high-temperature limit
this scaling exponent is equal to
\theta=0.5
.
If this statement is correct, it would mean that the scaling exponent of the (2+1) system
is
not universal
being temperature dependent in the drastic difference with the
corresponding (1+1) model where this exponent is universal and equal to
1/3
at all
temperatures.

In this paper in terms of the replica technique we study the scaling properties
of the two-dimensional directed polymers in the low-temperature limit.
The idea is to estimate the replica partition function
Z(N,t)

(which is the disorder average of the

N
-th power of the partition function) in the limit of
N\gg 1
which makes possible
to reconstruct the
left tail
asymptotic
of the free energy distribution function
P(F\to-\infty)
. Assuming that this
(unknown) distribution function is defined by the only energy scale
we find that the time scaling exponent of the free energy fluctuations is
\theta=1/4
.
We see that this value is somewhat different from the
zero temperature numerical results. To explain this discrepancy I would suggest that
in the numerical simulations the exact value of the scaling exponent
\theta=1/4

is distorted by the presence of a logarithmic prefactor which is common situation
for the two-dimensional systems.

In Section II we define the model and describe the general ideas
of the considered replica approach.
In Section III we present the main lines of the ”energy optimization method”
used for estimation of the replica partition function in the large
N
limit.
In Section IV we demonstrate how this method works in the case of (1+1) directed polymers
and show that here it perfectly reproduces well known exact results.
In section V we concentrate on (2+1) directed polymer model for which up to now
no exact results are known. Here we demonstrate that in the low temperature limit
and at
N\gg 1
the replica partition function

Z(N,t)\sim\exp\bigl\{u\,(\beta\,\epsilon)^{-2}\,(\beta N)^{4}\,t\bigr\}
,
where
\beta
is the inverse temperature,
u
is the strength of the random potential
and
\epsilon
is its spatial correlation length (see eqs.(
3
)-(
4
)).
Correspondingly,
for the left tail of the free energy distribution function
one obtains

P(F\to-\infty)\;\sim\;\exp\biggl\{-\biggl(\frac{|F|}{\bigl(\sqrt{u}/\beta\epsilon\bigr)^{1/2}\;t^{1/4}}\biggr)^{4/3}\biggr\}

(1)

so that the free energy fluctuations scales as
|F|\,\sim\,t^{1/4}
.
Finally, Section VI is devoted to the discussion of the obtained results.

II
The model and the replicas approach

We consider the model of directed polymers defined in terms
of an elastic string described by the two-dimensional vector

\boldsymbol{\phi}(\tau)\equiv\bigl(\phi_{x}(\tau),\,\phi_{y}(\tau)\bigr)

directed along the
\tau
-axes within an interval
[0,t]

which passes through a random medium
described by a random potential
V(\boldsymbol{\phi},\tau)
.
The energy of a given polymer’s trajectory

\boldsymbol{\phi}(\tau)
is

H[\boldsymbol{\phi}(\tau);V]=\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\biggl[\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\partial_{\tau}\boldsymbol{\phi}(\tau)\Bigr)^{2}+V\bigl(\boldsymbol{\phi}(\tau),\tau\bigr)\biggr];

(2)

Here the disorder potential
V[\boldsymbol{\phi},\tau]

is supposed to be Gaussian distributed with a zero mean

\overline{V(\boldsymbol{\phi},\tau)}=0

and the correlation function

\overline{V(\boldsymbol{\phi},\tau)V(\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})}=u\,\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon}(\boldsymbol{\phi}-\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\prime})

(3)

The parameter
u
is the strength of the disorder and
\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon}(\boldsymbol{\phi})

is the two-dimensional ”finite-size
\delta
-function”:

\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon}(\boldsymbol{\phi})\;=\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{\pi\epsilon^{2}}\;,\;\;\;\;\mbox{for}\;|\boldsymbol{\phi}|\,\leq\,\epsilon\\
\\
0\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{for}\;|\boldsymbol{\phi}|\,>\,\epsilon\end{array}\right.

(4)

We do not introduce here ”true” (zero-size) two-dimensional
\delta
-function for the reasons which will be
explained later.

For a given realization of the random potential
V(\boldsymbol{\phi},\tau)

the partition function of the considered system (with fixed boundary conditions) is

Z({\bf r},t)=\int_{\boldsymbol{\phi}(0)=\bf{0}}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}(t)={\bf r}}{\cal D}\boldsymbol{\phi}(\tau)\exp\bigl\{-\beta H[\boldsymbol{\phi}(\tau),V]\bigr\}\;=\;\exp\bigl\{-\beta F({\bf r},t)\bigr\}

(5)

where
\beta
is the inverse temperature,

F({\bf r},t)
is the free energy which is a random quantity
and the integration is taken over all
trajectories
\boldsymbol{\phi}(\tau)

starting at
\bf{0}
(at
\tau=0
) and ending at a point
{\bf r}

(at
\tau=t
).

For simplicity, in what follows we are going to consider the problem with
the zero boundary conditions:
\boldsymbol{\phi}(\tau=0)=\boldsymbol{\phi}(\tau=t)={\bf 0}
.
The free energy probability distribution function

P(F)
of this system can be studied in terms of the integer moments
of the above partition function, eq.(
5
):

\overline{Z^{N}}\equiv Z(N,t)\;=\;\prod_{a=1}^{N}\int_{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{a}(0)=0}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{a}(t)=0}{\cal D}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{a}(\tau)\;\overline{\Biggl(\exp\Bigl\{-\beta\sum_{a=1}^{N}H[\boldsymbol{\phi}_{a}(\tau),V]\Bigr\}\Biggr)}\;=\;\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dF\,P(F)\,\exp\bigl\{-\beta NF\bigr\}

(6)

where
\overline{(...)}
denotes the averaging over
the random potentials
V[\boldsymbol{\phi},\tau]

Performing this simple Gaussian averaging we get

Z(N,t)\;=\;\prod_{a=1}^{N}\int_{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{a}(0)=0}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{a}(t)=0}{\cal D}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{a}(\tau)\;\exp\Bigl\{-\beta H_{N}[\boldsymbol{\phi}_{1}(\tau),\,\boldsymbol{\phi}_{2}(\tau),\,...\,,\boldsymbol{\phi}_{N}(\tau)]\Bigr\}

(7)

where

\beta H_{N}[\boldsymbol{\phi}_{1}(\tau),\,\boldsymbol{\phi}_{2}(\tau),\,...\,,\boldsymbol{\phi}_{N}(\tau)]=\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\Biggl[\frac{1}{2}\beta\sum_{a=1}^{N}\Bigl(\partial_{\tau}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{a}(\tau)\Bigr)^{2}\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}\,u\,\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon}\bigl(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{a}(\tau)-\boldsymbol{\phi}_{b}(\tau)\bigr)\Biggr];

(8)

is the replica Hamiltonian which describes
N
elastic strings

\bigl\{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{1}(\tau),\,\boldsymbol{\phi}_{2}(\tau),\,...\,,\boldsymbol{\phi}_{N}(\tau)\bigr\}

with the attractive interactions

\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon}\bigl(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{a}-\boldsymbol{\phi}_{b}\bigr)
, eq.(
4
).
To compute the replica partition function
Z(N,t)
, eqs.(
7
)-(
8
), one introduces
the function:

\Psi({\bf r}_{1},\,{\bf r}_{2},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N};\;t)\;=\;\prod_{a=1}^{N}\int_{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{a}(0)=\bf{0}}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{a}(t)={\bf r}_{a}}{\cal D}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{a}(\tau)\;\exp\Bigl\{-\beta H_{N}[\boldsymbol{\phi}_{1}(\tau),\,\boldsymbol{\phi}_{2}(\tau),\,...\,,\boldsymbol{\phi}_{N}(\tau)]\Bigr\}

(9)

such that

Z(N,t)\,=\,\Psi({\bf r}_{1},\,{\bf r}_{2},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N};\,t)\big|_{{\bf r}_{a}=0}

(10)

One can easily show that
\Psi({\bf r}_{1},\,{\bf r}_{2},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N};\,t)

is the wave function of N quantum bosons defined by
the imaginary time Schrödinger equation

\beta\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Psi({\bf r}_{1},\,{\bf r}_{2},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N};\;t)\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{N}\,\Delta_{a}\Psi({\bf r}_{1},\,{\bf r}_{2},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N};\;t)\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\,\beta^{3}u\,\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon}({\bf r}_{a}-{\bf r}_{b})\,\Psi({\bf r}_{1},\,{\bf r}_{2},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N};\;t)

(11)

where
\Delta_{a}
is the two-dimensional Laplacian
with respect to the coordinate
{\bf r}_{a}
. The corresponding eigenvalue equation
for the eigenfunctions
\psi({\bf r}_{1},\,{\bf r}_{2},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})
,
defined by the relation

\Psi({\bf r}_{1},\,{\bf r}_{2},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N};\;t)\;=\;\psi({\bf r}_{1},\,{\bf r}_{2},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})\,\exp\bigl\{-t\,E_{N}\bigr\}

(12)

reads:

-2\beta\,E_{N}\,\psi({\bf r}_{1},\,{\bf r}_{2},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})\;=\;\sum_{a=1}^{N}\,\Delta_{a}\psi({\bf r}_{1},\,{\bf r}_{2},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})\;+\;\kappa\,\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon}({\bf r}_{a}-{\bf r}_{b})\,\psi({\bf r}_{1},\,{\bf r}_{2},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})

(13)

where
\kappa=\beta^{3}u
.

It is at this stage that we are facing the crucial difference of the considered problem
with the corresponding (1+1) one (see Section III). The general solution
of the one-dimensional counterpart of eq.(
13
) is given by the Bethe ansatz
wave function
[
16
,
17
,
18
]

which is valid only for
U(x)\,=\,\delta(x)
and which is based on the
exact two-particle wave functions (
N=2
) solution
exhibiting finite value energy
E_{N=2}
. It is this fundamental property
of (1+1) problem which eventually allows to derive the Tracy-Widom distribution
for the free energy fluctuation

[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
.
The situation in the (2+1) case, eq.(
13
), is much more complicated.
First of all, in two dimensions there exists no
finite
two-particle solution
for the true
\delta
-function potential

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon}({\bf r})
, eq(
4
).
Indeed, for
N=2
eq.(
13
) (where for simplicity we take
\beta=1
and
u=\pi
) reduces to

\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)\,+\,\frac{1}{r}\psi^{\prime}(r)\,+\,\bigl(\pi\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon}({\bf r})\,+\,2E_{2}\bigr)\,\psi(r)\;=\;0

(14)

where
r=|{\bf r}_{1}-{\bf r}_{2}|
.
It can be easily shown that at
\epsilon\ll 1
the ground state solution of this equation
(with
E_{2}<0
) is

\psi(r)\;\simeq\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\psi_{0}\,J_{0}\bigl(\sqrt{1-2\epsilon^{2}|E_{2}|}\;r/\epsilon\bigr)\;,\;\;\;\;\mbox{for}\;r\,\leq\,\epsilon\\
\\
\psi_{1}\,K_{0}\bigl(\sqrt{2\epsilon^{2}|E_{2}|}\;r/\epsilon\bigr)\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{for}\;r\,>\,\epsilon\end{array}\right.

(15)

where
J_{0}(x)
and
K_{0}(x)
are Bessel and Macdonald functions correspondingly, and
(irrelevant) constants
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{1}
are related by the condition

\psi_{0}\,J_{0}\bigl(\sqrt{1-2\epsilon^{2}|E_{2}|}\bigr)\;=\;\psi_{1}\,K_{0}\bigl(\sqrt{2\epsilon^{2}|E_{2}|}\bigr)
. The ground state energy
of this two-particle state is

E_{2}(\epsilon)\;\simeq\;-\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}\,x_{*}

(16)

where the number
x_{*}\simeq 0.04
is the solution of the equation

\frac{J_{0}^{\prime}\bigl(\sqrt{1-x}\bigr)\;\sqrt{1-x}}{J_{0}\bigl(\sqrt{1-x}\bigr)}\;=\;\frac{K_{0}^{\prime}\bigl(\sqrt{x}\bigr)\;\sqrt{x}}{K_{0}\bigl(\sqrt{x}\bigr)}

(17)

We see that in the limit
\epsilon\to 0
(where
\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon}({\bf r})

turns into the ”true”
\delta
-function) the ground state energy of this state

E_{2}(\epsilon\to 0)\,\to\,-\infty
.
This simple exercise clearly demonstrate that first, consideration of the

N
-particle problem, eq.(
13
) with the true (zero-size) two-dimensional

\delta
-function
makes no sense and second, the ground state energy of this
N
-particle problem must
explicitly depend on the (non-zero) size
\epsilon

of the interaction potential
\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon}({\bf r})
, eq.(
4
).

II.1
Free energy probability distribution function in the replica approach

Free energy probability distribution function (PDF)
P(F)
and the replica partition function
are related according to eq.(
6
):

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dF\,P(F)\,\exp\bigl\{-\beta NF\bigr\}\;=\;Z(N,t)

(18)

The problem is that to recover
P(F)
from the above equation (e.g. by inverse Laplace
transform) one has to derive the exact solution for the replica partition function

Z(N,t)
which is hardly possible for the model under consideration.
However if we do not pretend to obtain an entire PDF
P(F)
, but we are interested to
know only its
left tail
,
P(F\to-\infty)
, then the situation somewhat simplifies.
The point is that the asymptotics
P(F\to-\infty)
is defined by the behavior
of the replica partition function
Z(N,t)
in the limit
N\to\infty
only.

For example, let us suppose that in the limit
N\to\infty
the replica partition
function, eqs.(
10
), (
12
) has the form

Z(N,t)\;\sim\;\exp\Bigl\{g\,(\beta N)^{k}\,t\Bigr\}

(19)

where the factor
g
depends on the parameters of the model.
In this case it can be easily shown that the left tail of the function

P(F)
has stretched-exponential form,

P(F\to-\infty)\;\sim\;\exp\Bigl\{-\gamma\,|F|^{\alpha}\Bigr\}

(20)

where it is supposed that
\alpha>1
. Indeed,
substituting eqs.(
19
) and (
20
) into eq.(
18
) we get

\int_{-\infty}^{0}dF\,P(F)\,\exp\bigl\{\beta N|F|\bigr\}\;+\;\int_{0}^{+\infty}dF\,P(F)\,\exp\bigl\{-\beta NF\bigr\}\;\sim\;\exp\Bigl\{g\,(\beta N)^{k}\,t\Bigr\}

(21)

It is clear that due to the factor
\exp\bigl\{-\beta NF\bigr\}
the second
integral in the l.h.s of the above relation vanishes in the limit
N\to+\infty
,
while the first integral (over negative values of
F
) is dominated by the
saddle-point contribution of left tail of the function
P(F)
, namely

\int_{-\infty}^{0}dF\,\exp\bigl\{-\gamma\,|F|^{\alpha}\,+\,\beta N|F|\bigr\}\;\sim\;\exp\Bigl\{g\,(\beta N)^{k}\,t\Bigr\}

(22)

Formally the asymptotic form, eq.(
20
), is valid only for large (negative)
values of
F
so that the integration here can not be extended up to zero.
Nevertheless, since the saddle-point value of the free energy,

|F_{*}|\;=\;\biggl(\frac{\beta N}{\alpha\,\gamma}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}

(23)

is big in the limit
N\gg 1
, the contribution of the integration over
finite (negative) values of
F
(where PDF
P(F)
is not known) can be neglected
compared to the saddle-point contribution in the asymptotic region
F\to-\infty
.
Thus, substituting this saddle-point value into the exponential in
the l.h.s of eq.(
22
) we get

\exp\Bigl\{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\bigl(\alpha\gamma\bigr)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}\,\bigl(\beta N\bigr)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\Bigr\}\;\sim\;\exp\Bigl\{g\,(\beta N)^{k}\,t\Bigr\}

(24)

From this relation we immediately obtain

\alpha\;=\;\frac{k}{k-1}

(25)

and

\gamma\;=\;\frac{k-1}{k}\,\bigl(k\,g\,t\bigr)^{-\frac{1}{k-1}}

(26)

Substituting these values of
\alpha
and
\gamma
into eq.(
20
)
we get the following
left
asymptotics of the free energy probability distribution
function

P(F\to-\infty)\;\sim\;\exp\biggl\{-\frac{k-1}{k}\biggl(\frac{|F|}{\bigl(k\,g\,t\bigr)^{1/k}}\biggr)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\biggr\}

(27)

Now, if we admit that the free energy fluctuations of the considered system
are described by a
universal
probability distribution function, then
we can conclude that the time scaling of these fluctuations can be represented as

F\;\sim\;f\cdot t^{1/k}

(28)

where the random quantity
f\sim 1
is described by a probability distribution function

{\cal P}(f)
whose left tail is

{\cal P}(f\to-\infty)\;\sim\;\exp\Bigl\{-\mbox{(const)}\,|f|^{k/(k-1)}\Bigr\}

(29)

while its entire form remains unknown.

III
Replica energy balance method

In view of the absence of the exact solution for the
N
-particle
two-dimensional wave function
\psi({\bf r}_{1},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})

we propose the following
”heuristic” strategy of calculations of the replica energy
E_{N}
defined by eq.(
13
)
in the limit
N\gg 1
. Let us multiply eq.(
13
) by

\psi({\bf r}_{1},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})\;\delta\bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf r}_{a}\bigr)

and integrate over all particle’s positions:

\displaystyle-2\beta\,E_{N}\,\int\,d^{2}r_{1}...d^{2}r_{N}\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf r}_{a}\Bigr)\psi^{2}({\bf r}_{1},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\,\int\,d^{2}r_{1}...d^{2}r_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf r}_{a}\Bigr)\psi({\bf r}_{1},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})\Delta_{a}\psi({\bf r}_{1},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})

\displaystyle+\kappa\,\sum_{a\not=b}^{N}\int\,d^{2}r_{1}...d^{2}r_{N}\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf r}_{a}\Bigr)\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon}({\bf r}_{a}-{\bf r}_{b})\,\psi^{2}({\bf r}_{1},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})+\frac{\kappa}{\pi\epsilon^{2}}\,N\int\,d^{2}r_{1}...d^{2}r_{N}\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf r}_{a}\Bigr)\psi^{2}({\bf r}_{1},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})

(30)

Taking into account the symmetry of the wave function with respect to the particles
permutations the above equation can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle 2\beta\,\tilde{E}_{N}\,\int\,d^{2}r_{1}...d^{2}r_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf r}_{a}\Bigr)\psi^{2}({\bf r}_{1},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})=N\,\int\,d^{2}r_{1}...d^{2}r_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf r}_{a}\Bigr)\Bigl(\nabla_{1}\psi({\bf r}_{1},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle-\;\kappa\,N(N-1)\int\,d^{2}r_{1}...d^{2}r_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf r}_{a}\Bigr)\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon}({\bf r}_{1}-{\bf r}_{2})\,\psi^{2}({\bf r}_{1},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})

(31)

where

\tilde{E}_{N}\;=\;E_{N}\;+\;\frac{\kappa N}{2\beta\,\epsilon^{2}}

(32)

Let us suppose that the wave function of the present system is characterized by the only
spatial scale
R
(which will be defined later by minimizing the energy
\tilde{E}_{N}
),

\psi({\bf r}_{1},\,...\,{\bf r}_{N})\;\to\;\psi({\bf r}_{1}/R,\,...\,{\bf r}_{N}/R)
. Taking into account the properties of
wave function of the two-particle system, eqs.(
15
), where
the Macdonald function

K_{0}(x)\;\sim\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\ln(1/x)\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{at}\;x\ll 1\\
\\
\exp\{-x\}\;,\;\;\;\;\mbox{at}\;x\gtrsim 1\end{array}\right.

(33)

we will suppose that the
N
-particle wave function

\psi({\bf r}_{1}/R,\,...\,{\bf r}_{N}/R)

exhibits two principal properties:

(1) for any two particles
a
and
b
, such that
\epsilon<|{\bf r}_{a}-{\bf r}_{b}|\ll R
,

\psi\bigl({\bf r}_{1}/R,\,...,\,{\bf r}_{a}/R,\,...,\,{\bf r}_{b}/R,\,...,\,{\bf r}_{N}/R\bigr)\;\sim\;\ln\Bigl(\frac{R}{|{\bf r}_{a}-{\bf r}_{b}|}\Bigr)\times\psi\bigl({\bf r}_{1}/R,\,...,\,{\bf r}_{b}/R,\,...,\,{\bf r}_{b}/R,\,...,\,{\bf r}_{N}/R\bigr)

(34)

(2) at scales
|{\bf r}_{a}-{\bf r}_{b}|\gtrsim R
,

\psi\bigl({\bf r}_{1}/R,\,...,\,{\bf r}_{N}/R\bigr)\;\sim\;\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{1}{R}\sum_{1\leq a<b}^{N}\,|{\bf r}_{a}-{\bf r}_{b}|\Bigr\}

(35)

After rescaling
{\bf r}_{a}\to R\,{\bf x}_{a}
the above wave function

\psi\bigl({\bf r}_{1}/R,\,...,\,{\bf r}_{N}/R\bigr)\;\to\;\psi({\bf x}_{1},\,...\,{\bf x}_{N})
, and at scales
|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\gtrsim 1

instead of eq.(
35
) we get

\psi({\bf x}_{1},\,...\,{\bf x}_{N})\;\sim\;\exp\Bigl\{-\sum_{1\leq a<b}^{N}\,|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\Bigr\}

(36)

Now the relation (
31
) takes the form

2\beta\tilde{E}_{N}(R)\;I_{0}(N)\;=\;N\,R^{-2}\,I_{\small{\nabla}}(N,R)\;-\;N(N-1)\,\kappa\,R^{-2}\,I_{\delta}(N,R)

(37)

where

I_{0}(N)\;=\;\int\,d^{2}x_{1}...d^{2}x_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}\Bigr)\psi^{2}({\bf x}_{1},\,...\,{\bf x}_{N})

(38)

I_{\nabla}(N,R)\;=\;\int\,d^{2}x_{1}...d^{2}x_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}\Bigr)\Bigl(\nabla_{1}\psi({\bf x}_{1},\,...\,{\bf x}_{N})\Bigr)^{2}

(39)

I_{\delta}(N,R)\;=\;\int\,d^{2}x_{1}...d^{2}x_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}\Bigr)\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon/R}({\bf x}_{1}-{\bf x}_{2})\,\psi^{2}({\bf x}_{1},\,...\,{\bf x}_{N})

(40)

According to eq.(
34
), the leading (most divergent)
contribution to the function
I_{\nabla}(N,R)
, eq.(
39
),
comes from the integrations over
{\bf x}_{1}
in the vicinities of every point

{\bf x}_{a}
(
a=2,...N
):

I_{\nabla}(N,R)\;\sim\;\int\,d^{2}x_{2}...d^{2}x_{N}\;\sum_{a=2}^{N}\;\int_{\frac{\epsilon}{R}<|{\bf x}_{1}-{\bf x}_{a}|\ll 1}d^{2}x_{1}\Bigl[\nabla_{1}\ln\Bigl(\frac{1}{|{\bf x}_{1}-{\bf x}_{a}|}\Bigr)\Bigr]^{2}\delta\Bigl({\bf x_{a}}+\sum_{b=2}^{N}{\bf x}_{b}\Bigr)\psi^{2}\bigl({\bf x}_{a},\,{\bf x}_{2},\,...,\,{\bf x}_{a},\,...,\,{\bf x}_{N}\bigr)

(41)

Using the symmetry of the wave function
\psi({\bf x}_{1},\,...\,{\bf x}_{N})

and redefining
{\bf x}_{1}={\bf x}_{a}+{\boldsymbol{\eta}}

we get

I_{\nabla}(N,R)\;\sim\;(N-1)\,\int\,d^{2}x_{2}...d^{2}x_{N}\;\int_{\epsilon/R}^{1}d\eta\,\eta\,\Bigl[\frac{d}{d\eta}\ln\Bigl(\frac{1}{\eta}\Bigr)\Bigr]^{2}\delta\Bigl({\bf x_{2}}+\sum_{b=2}^{N}{\bf x}_{b}\Bigr)\psi^{2}\bigl({\bf x}_{2},\,{\bf x}_{2},\,{\bf x}_{3},\,...,\,{\bf x}_{N}\bigr)

(42)

or

I_{\nabla}(N,R)\;\sim\;(N-1)\,\ln\Bigl(\frac{R}{\epsilon}\Bigr)\int\,d^{2}x_{2}...d^{2}x_{N}\;\delta\Bigl({\bf x_{2}}+\sum_{b=2}^{N}{\bf x}_{b}\Bigr)\,\psi^{2}\bigl({\bf x}_{2},\,{\bf x}_{2},\,{\bf x}_{3},\,...,\,{\bf x}_{N}\bigr)

(43)

In a similar way we can estimate the value of the function
I_{\delta}(N,R)
, eq.(
40
).
Taking into account that according to eq.(
34
)

\lim_{|{\bf x}_{1}-{\bf x}_{2}|\to\epsilon/R}\;\psi^{2}\bigl({\bf x}_{1},\,{\bf x}_{2},\,{\bf x}_{3},\,...,\,{\bf x}_{N}\bigr)\;\sim\;\ln^{2}\Bigl(\frac{R}{\epsilon}\Bigr)\;\psi^{2}\bigl({\bf x}_{2},\,{\bf x}_{2},\,{\bf x}_{3},\,...,\,{\bf x}_{N}\bigr)

(44)

and that
\int d^{2}x\,\delta^{(2d)}_{\epsilon/R}({\bf x})\,=\,1
, we obtain

I_{\delta}(N,R)\;\sim\;\ln^{2}\Bigl(\frac{R}{\epsilon}\Bigr)\int\,d^{2}x_{2}...d^{2}x_{N}\;\delta\Bigl({\bf x_{2}}+\sum_{b=2}^{N}{\bf x}_{b}\Bigr)\,\psi^{2}\bigl({\bf x}_{2},\,{\bf x}_{2},\,{\bf x}_{3},\,...,\,{\bf x}_{N}\bigr)

(45)

Substituting eqs.(
43
) and (
45
) into eq.(
37
) we find

2\beta\tilde{E}_{N}(R)\;I_{0}(N)\;\sim\;N(N-1)\,\Bigl[R^{-2}\,\ln\Bigl(\frac{R}{\epsilon}\Bigr)\;-\;\kappa\,R^{-2}\,\ln^{2}\Bigl(\frac{R}{\epsilon}\Bigr)\Bigr]\;I_{1}(N)

(46)

or

\tilde{E}_{N}(R)\;\sim\;\frac{N(N-1)}{2\beta}\,\Bigl[R^{-2}\,\ln\Bigl(\frac{R}{\epsilon}\Bigr)\;-\;\kappa\,R^{-2}\,\ln^{2}\Bigl(\frac{R}{\epsilon}\Bigr)\Bigr]\;\frac{I_{1}(N)}{I_{0}(N)}

(47)

where

I_{1}(N)\;=\;\int\,d^{2}x_{2}...d^{2}x_{N}\;\delta\Bigl({\bf x_{2}}+\sum_{b=2}^{N}{\bf x}_{b}\Bigr)\,\psi^{2}\bigl({\bf x}_{2},\,{\bf x}_{2},\,{\bf x}_{3},\,...,\,{\bf x}_{N}\bigr)

(48)

and
I_{0}(N)
is given in eq.(
38
).

Further strategy consist in two (independent) steps: we have to find optimal
parameter
R_{*}
which minimizes
\tilde{E}_{N}(R)
, and we have to calculate
the ratio
I_{1}(N)/I_{0}(N)
as a function of
N

(in the limit
N\to\infty
). In this way we will find the
energy
E_{*}(N)\equiv\tilde{E}_{N}(R_{*})

of considered
N
-particle system as a function of
N
which defines
the replica partition function
Z(N,t)\sim\exp\{-t\,E_{*}(N)\}
.
This would be sufficient (as it was shown in Section II) to get the scaling exponent
of the free energy fluctuations as well as the form of the left tail of its probability
distribution function. But first, let us demonstrate how the proposed method
works in the case of
(1+1)
system where we can compare obtained results with
well known exact ones.

IV
(1+1) directed polymers

In one dimension instead of eq.(
13
) we have
the Schrödinger equation for the
N
-particle wave function

\psi(r_{1},\,...\,r_{N})
which depends on
N
one-dimensional
particles coordinates

\{-\infty\,<\,r_{a}\,<\,+\infty\}\;(a=1,...,N)
:

-2\beta\,E_{N}\,\psi(r_{1},\,...\,r_{N})\;=\;\sum_{a=1}^{N}\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r_{a}^{2}}\,\psi(r_{1},\,...\,r_{N})\;+\;\kappa\,\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}\delta_{\epsilon}(r_{a}-r_{b})\,\psi(r_{1},\,...\,r_{N})

(49)

where, as before,
\kappa=\beta^{3}u
and
\delta_{\epsilon}(r)
is
the ”finite-size
\delta
-function”

\delta_{\epsilon}(r)\;=\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\;,\;\;\;\;\mbox{for}\;|r|\,\leq\,\epsilon\\
\\
0\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{for}\;|r|\,>\,\epsilon\end{array}\right.

(50)

Multiplying eq.(
49
) by

\psi(r_{1},\,...\,r_{N})\;\delta\bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}r_{a}\bigr)

and integrating over all particle’s positions instead of eq.(
30
)
we get:

\displaystyle 2\beta\,\tilde{E}_{N}\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\,dr_{1}...dr_{N}\,\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}\,r_{a}\Bigr)\,\psi^{2}(r_{1},\,...\,r_{N})=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\,dr_{1}...dr_{N}\,\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}\,r_{a}\Bigr)\,\Bigl(\frac{\partial}{\partial r_{a}}\,\psi(r_{1},\,...\,r_{N})\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle-\kappa\,\sum_{a\not=b}^{N}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\,dr_{1}...dr_{N}\,\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}\,r_{a}\Bigr)\,\delta_{\epsilon}(r_{a}-r_{b})\,\psi^{2}(r_{1},\,...\,r_{N})

(51)

where

\tilde{E}_{N}\;=\;E_{N}\;+\;\frac{\kappa N}{4\beta\,\epsilon}

(52)

Supposing, as before, that the wave function of the present system is characterized
by the only spatial scale
R
and taking into account the properties of the
two-particle solution we admit that the
N
-particle wave function has the
Bethe anzats structure,

\psi(r_{1},\,...\,r_{N})\;=\;\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{1}{R}\sum_{1\leq a<b}^{N}\,|r_{a}-r_{b}|\Bigr\}

(53)

After rescaling
r_{a}\to R\,x_{a}
the relation (
51
) takes the form

2\beta\tilde{E}_{N}(R)\;I_{0}(N)\;=\;R^{-2}\,I_{\nabla}(N)\;-\;\kappa\,R^{-1}\,I_{\delta}(N)

(54)

where

I_{0}(N)\;=\;\int\,dx_{1}...dx_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}x_{a}\Bigr)\,\psi^{2}(x_{1},\,...\,x_{N})

(55)

I_{\nabla}(N)\;=\;\int\,dx_{1}...dx_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}x_{a}\Bigr)\,\sum_{b=1}^{N}\,\Bigl(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{a}}\psi(x_{1},\,...\,x_{N})\Bigr)^{2}

(56)

I_{\delta}(N)\;=\;\int\,dx_{1}...dx_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}x_{a}\Bigr)\,\sum_{b\not=c}^{N}\,\delta(x_{b}-x_{c})\,\psi^{2}(x_{1},\,...\,x_{N})

(57)

and

\psi(x_{1},\,...\,x_{N})\;=\;\exp\Bigl\{-\sum_{1\leq a<b}^{N}\,|x_{a}-x_{b}|\Bigr\}

(58)

As the wave function
\psi(x_{1},\,...\,x_{N})
, eq.(
58
), exhibits no
divergences at
|x_{a}-x_{b}|\to 0
,
the expression for
I_{\delta}(N)
, eq.(
57
), is given in the limit
\epsilon\to 0
.

Detailed calculations of the above three factors are given in Appendix A.
The results are:

\displaystyle I_{0}(N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-(N-1)}\,\frac{1}{(N-1)!}

(59)

\displaystyle I_{\nabla}(N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\,N(N^{2}-1)\;I_{0}(N)

(60)

\displaystyle I_{\delta}(N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\,N(N^{2}-1)\;I_{0}(N)

(61)

Substituting these expressions into eq.(
54
) we get

\tilde{E}_{N}(R)\;=\;\frac{1}{6\beta}\,N(N^{2}-1)\,\bigl(R^{-2}\;-\;\kappa\,R^{-1}\bigr)

(62)

The minimum of
\tilde{E}_{N}(R)
is achieved at
R_{*}\,=\,2/\kappa

such that

\tilde{E}_{N}(R_{*})\;\equiv\;E_{*}(N)\;=\;-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{24\beta}\,(N^{3}\,-\,N)

(63)

Substituting here
\kappa\,=\,\beta^{3}\,u
we get

E_{*}(N)\;=\;-\frac{1}{24}\,\beta^{5}\,u^{2}\,(N^{3}\,-\,N)

(64)

which perfectly coincides with the exact result for the ground state
energy of
N
-particle boson system with paired attractive
\delta
interactions
(see e.g.
[
26
]
). In the limit
N\to\infty
we obtain

Z(N,t)\;\sim\;\exp\bigl\{-E_{*}(N)\,t\bigr\}\;\sim\;\exp\Bigl\{\frac{1}{24}(\beta u)^{2}\,(\beta N)^{3}\Bigr\}

(65)

so that according to the discussion in Section II, eqs.(
19
) and
(
27
)-(
29
), the free energy fluctuations scale with time as

F\;\sim\;(\beta u)^{2/3}\,t^{1/3}\cdot f

(66)

where the random quantity
f\sim 1
is described by a probability distribution function
with the left tail

{\cal P}(f\to-\infty)\;\sim\;\exp\Bigl\{-(\mbox{const})\,|f|^{3/2}\Bigr\}

(67)

which also perfectly fits with well known exact results
(see e.g.
[
24
,
25
]
).

V
(2+1) Directed polymers: free energy scaling

According to eq.(
47
), to derive the scaling exponent of the free energy
fluctuations of (2+1) directed polymers we have to find the minimum of
\tilde{E}_{N}(R)

with respect to
R
(which is easy), but more important, we hate to derive the asymptotics
of the ratio
I_{1}(N)/I_{0}(N)
in the limit
N\to\infty
(which is not easy).
We start with the treatment of the factor
I_{1}(N)
, eq.(
48
):

I_{1}(N)\;=\;\int\,d^{2}x_{2}...d^{2}x_{N}\;\delta\Bigl({\bf x_{2}}+\sum_{b=2}^{N}{\bf x}_{b}\Bigr)\,\exp\Bigl\{-2\sum_{a=3}^{N}|{\bf x}_{2}-{\bf x}_{a}|\;-\;2\sum_{2\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\Bigr\}

(68)

Here and in what follows we approximate the wave function

\psi\bigl({\bf x}_{1},\,...,\,{\bf x}_{N}\bigr)

by its form at scales
|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\gtrsim 1
, eq.(
36
).
Note that as the integration in the vicinities of the logarithmic singularities at

|x_{a}-x_{b}|\to\epsilon\ll 1
is
converging its contribution is negligible compared to the one at scales

|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\gtrsim 1
.

It can be easily shown (see Appendix B) that at
N\gg 1
both
the typical (average) value of the distances between the particles

\langle|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\rangle\;\sim\;1/N

and the typical value of the lengths of the vectors

\langle|{\bf x}_{a}|\rangle\;\sim\;1/N
.
It makes possible to compare the orders of magnitudes
of the two factors in the exponential in the r.h.s. of eq.(
68
):

\displaystyle\sum_{a=3}^{N}|{\bf x}_{2}-{\bf x}_{a}|\;\sim\;\frac{1}{N}\,(N-2)\;\sim\;1

(69)

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\;\sim\;\frac{1}{N}\,(N-1)(N-2)\;\sim\;N

which shows that we can neglect the first one compared to the second one.
Similarly, for the terms under the
\delta
-function of eq.(
68
)
we have:

\displaystyle{\bf x}_{2}\;\sim\;\frac{1}{N}

(70)

\displaystyle\sum_{b=2}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}\;\sim\;\frac{1}{N}\,(N-1)\;\sim\;1

which makes possible to neglect the first term compared to the second one.

Thus, we conclude that

I_{1}(N)\;\sim\;\int\,d^{2}x_{2}...d^{2}x_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{b=2}^{N}{\bf x}_{b}\Bigr)\,\exp\Bigl\{-2\sum_{2\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\Bigr\}\;=\;I_{0}(N-1)

(71)

The factor
I_{0}(N)
, eq.(
38
), can be represented as follows:

\displaystyle I_{0}(N)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\int\,d^{2}x_{1}\,d^{2}x_{2}\,...\,d^{2}x_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}\Bigr)\,\exp\Bigl\{-2\sum_{1\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\Bigr\}

(72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}x_{2}...d^{2}x_{N}\;\int d^{2}x_{1}\;\delta\Bigl({\bf x}_{1}+\sum_{a=2}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}\Bigr)\,\exp\Bigl\{-2\sum_{a=2}^{N}|{\bf x}_{1}-{\bf x}_{a}|\;-\;2\sum_{2\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\Bigr\}

At this stage we are going to integrate over one degree of freedom (over
{\bf x}_{1}
)
which would make possible to relate the factors
I_{0}(N)
and
I_{0}(N-1)
.

Let us redefine the integration variables:

\{{\bf x}_{2},\,...\,,{\bf x}_{N}\}\;\to\;\{{\bf z},\,{\bf y}_{2},\,...\,,{\bf y}_{N-1}\}
:

\displaystyle{\bf x}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf z}\;+\;{\bf y}_{a}\;\;\;\;\;(a\,=\,2,...,N-1)

\displaystyle{\bf x}_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf z}\;-\;\sum_{a=2}^{N-1}\,{\bf y}_{a}

(73)

Denoting
{\bf y}_{N}\equiv-\sum_{a=2}^{N-1}\,{\bf y}_{a}
,
instead of eq.(
72
) we get

I_{0}(N)\;\sim\;\int d^{2}z\int d^{2}y_{2}...d^{2}y_{N}\,\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=2}^{N}{\bf y}_{a}\Bigr)\;D(N)\;\int d^{2}x_{1}\,\delta\bigl({\bf x}_{1}\,+\,(N-1){\bf z}\bigr)\exp\Bigl\{-2\sum_{a=2}^{N}|{\bf x}_{1}-{\bf z}-{\bf y}_{a}|\;-\;2\sum_{2\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf y}_{a}-{\bf y}_{b}|\Bigr\}

(74)

where
D(N)
is the Jacobian

D(N)\;=\;\det\bigg|\frac{\partial\bigl({\bf x}_{2},...,{\bf x}_{N}\bigr)}{\partial\bigl({\bf z},{\bf y}_{2},...,{\bf y}_{N-1}\bigr)}\bigg|

(75)

Simple calculations yield (see Appendix C)

D(N)\;=\;(N-1)^{2}\,\Big|_{N\gg 1}\;\simeq\;N^{2}

(76)

Thus, integrating over
{\bf x}_{1}
in eq.(
74
), we get

I_{0}(N)\;\sim\;N^{2}\int d^{2}y_{2}...d^{2}y_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=2}^{N}{\bf y}_{a}\Bigr)\;\exp\Bigl\{-2\sum_{2\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf y}_{a}-{\bf y}_{b}|\Bigr\}\,\int d^{2}z\;\exp\Bigl\{-2\sum_{a=2}^{N}|N{\bf z}+{\bf y}_{a}|\Bigr\}

(77)

The above expression can be represented as follows:

I_{0}(N)\;\sim\;N^{2}\,I_{0}(N-1)\,\int d^{2}z\,\frac{\int d^{2}y_{2}...d^{2}y_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=2}^{N}{\bf y}_{a}\Bigr)\;\exp\Bigl\{-2\sum_{2\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf y}_{a}-{\bf y}_{b}|\Bigr\}\,\exp\Bigl\{-2\sum_{a=2}^{N}|N{\bf z}+{\bf y}_{a}|\Bigr\}}{\int d^{2}y_{2}...d^{2}y_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=2}^{N}{\bf y}_{a}\Bigr)\;\exp\Bigl\{-2\sum_{2\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf y}_{a}-{\bf y}_{b}|\Bigr\}}

(78)

or

I_{0}(N)\;\sim\;N^{2}\,I_{0}(N-1)\,\int d^{2}z\;\bigg\langle\exp\Bigl\{-2\sum_{a=2}^{N}|N{\bf z}+{\bf y}_{a}|\Bigr\}\bigg\rangle_{\{{\bf y}\}}

(79)

where the averaging is done over
\{{\bf y}_{2},...,{\bf y_{N}}\}

with the wight

\exp\bigl\{-2\sum_{2\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf y}_{a}-{\bf y}_{b}|\bigr\}
.
Redefining
{\bf z}=\frac{1}{2N^{2}}{\bf u}
and

{\bf y}_{a}=\frac{1}{2N}{\bf v}_{a}
we get

I_{0}(N)\;\sim\;\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\,I_{0}(N-1)\,\int d^{2}u\;\bigg\langle\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{a=2}^{N}|{\bf u}+{\bf v}_{a}|\Bigr\}\bigg\rangle_{\{{\bf v}\}}

(80)

where the averaging is done over
\{{\bf v}_{2},...,{\bf v_{N}}\}

with the wight

\exp\bigl\{-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{2\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf v}_{a}-{\bf v}_{b}|\bigr\}
.
As the typical value of
{\bf y}_{a}

(defined by the distribution

\exp\bigl\{-2\sum_{2\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf y}_{a}-{\bf y}_{b}|\bigr\}
)
is
\sim N^{-1}
(see Appendix B), the typical value of the vectors
{\bf v}_{a}

is
\sim 1
. Thus, we can estimate

\Phi({\bf u})\equiv\bigg\langle\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{a=2}^{N}|{\bf u}+{\bf v}_{a}|\Bigr\}\bigg\rangle_{\{{\bf v}\}}\;\sim\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bigg\langle\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{a=2}^{N}|{\bf v}_{a}|\Bigr\}\bigg\rangle_{\{{\bf v}\}}\,=\,C_{0},\sim 1\;,\;\;\;\;\mbox{at}\;|{\bf u}|\,\ll 1\\
\\
\bigg\langle\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{a=2}^{N}|{\bf u}|\Bigr\}\bigg\rangle_{\{{\bf v}\}}\,\simeq\exp\bigl\{-|{\bf u}|\bigr\},\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{at}\;|{\bf u}|\,\gg 1\end{array}\right.

(81)

which means, according to eq.(
80
), that

I_{0}(N)\;\sim\;\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\,I_{0}(N-1)\,\int d^{2}u\;\Phi({\bf u})\;\ \sim\;N^{-2}\,I_{0}(N-1)

(82)

or, according to eq.(
71
)

I_{0}(N-1)\;\sim\;I_{1}(N)\;\sim\;N^{2}\,I_{0}(N)

(83)

Substituting this into eq.(
47
), we get

\tilde{E}_{N}(R)\;\sim\;\frac{1}{\beta}\,N^{4}\,\,\Bigl[R^{-2}\,\ln\Bigl(\frac{R}{\epsilon}\Bigr)\;-\;\kappa\,R^{-2}\,\ln^{2}\Bigl(\frac{R}{\epsilon}\Bigr)\Bigr]

(84)

This function of
R
has the minimum at

\ln\Bigl(\frac{R_{*}}{\epsilon}\Bigr)\;=\;\frac{1}{2\kappa}\,\bigl(1\,+\,\kappa\,+\,\sqrt{1\,+\,\kappa^{2}}\bigr)\,\Big|_{\kappa\gg 1}\;\simeq\;1

(85)

and its value at the minimum

\tilde{E}_{N}(R_{*})\,\equiv\,E_{*}(N)\,\sim\,-\frac{\kappa}{\beta\epsilon^{2}}\,N^{4}\;=\;-\frac{u}{({\beta\,\epsilon})^{2}}\,\bigl(\beta\,N\bigr)^{4}

(86)

Correspondingly, for the replica partition function we get

Z(N,t)\;\sim\;\exp\bigl\{-E_{*}(N)\,t\bigr\}\;\sim\;\exp\Bigl\{\frac{u}{({\beta\,\epsilon})^{2}}\,\bigl(\beta\,N\bigr)^{4}\,t\Bigr\}

(87)

Thus, according to eqs.(
19
) and (
27
)-(
29
), the free energy fluctuations
in the considered system scale with time as

F\;\sim\;\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{u}}{\beta\,\epsilon}\Bigr)^{1/2}\,t^{1/4}\cdot f

(88)

where the random quantity
f\sim 1
is described by a probability distribution function
with the left tail

{\cal P}(f\to-\infty)\;\sim\;\exp\Bigl\{-(\mbox{const})\,|f|^{4/3}\Bigr\}

(89)

The above eqs.(
88
) and (
89
) constitute the main result of the present research.

VI
Discussion

In this paper we argue that at least in the low temperature limit the scaling exponent
of the free energy fluctuations of
(2+1)
directed polymers is equal to
\theta=1/4
which
close but not equal to the value
0.241
obtained in numerical simulations.
I would suggest that this discrepancy could be due to the presence of
a logarithmic prefactor which in numerical simulations may distort the ”pure”
scaling behavior with
\theta=1/4
and which is out of reach of the present
analytical method.

It is worthing to stress that according to eq.(
88
), the prefactor in the time scaling of
the free energy fluctuation explicitly depend on the size
\epsilon
of the spatial
correlations of the random potential, eq.(
4
). Moreover, we see that the limit

\epsilon\to 0
(when these correlations turn into the true
\delta
-function)
do not exist here (the prefactor is divergent).
In other words, one can not consider the (2+1) directed polymers
with
\delta
-correlated random potential because such system is pathological.
This is in drastic difference with (1+1) directed polymers where it is just for the

\delta
-correlated random potential that the exact solution has been derived.

Of course, present research is based on the calculations which are far from being rigorous,
and correspondingly it leaves many open questions. The main problem is that the ground state
wave function for the considered two-dimensional
N
-particle boson system with
short-range attractive interactions (which should be the solution of eq.(
13
))
is not known. The key assumption of the present approach is that this wave function
can be approximated by the heuristic structure given in eqs.(
34
)-(
35
),
which is a straightforward generalization of the two-particle solution for the

N
-particle system and which contains one (spatial scale) adjustment parameter.
It is evident that such structure of the wave function is not correct for an arbitrary
(finite) value of
N
, but on the other hand it seems to be a good approximation
in the limit
N\to\infty
. To what extend the use of such approximation is essential
for the declared result
\theta=1/4
is not clear.

Another key point of the present approach is the
hypothesis
that the free energy
fluctuations of the considered system are described by a
universal

probability distribution function. This is known to take place in (1+1) directed polymers
where the exact solution is available, but it is not necessary guaranteed for the considered system.
If this hypothesis is correct then for obtaining the value of the scaling exponent
it is sufficient to derive the left tail of the free energy distribution function
(that is what has been done in this paper). If not, then the knowledge of this left tail
tells us just nothing.

One more problem is the evident dependence of the scaling properties of the considered system
on the temperature. The present study was devoted to the low temperature limit when
dimensionless parameter
\kappa=\beta^{3}\,u\;\gg\;1
(see eqs.(
85
)-(
86
))
and in this case we get
\theta=1/4
.
Unfortunately in the framework of the present approach one can not study the high temperature
limit (when
\kappa=\beta^{3}\,u\;\ll\;1
) as in this case the minimum of the function

\tilde{E}_{N}(R)
, eq.(
84
), becomes almost ”flat” and correspondingly the
value of
R_{*}
becomes ill defined. On the other hand, previous analytical study
of the high temperature limit (using completely different method)
[
15
]

yields the result
\theta=1/2
. If these two results
are both correct then we would have to conclude that unlike (1+1) directed polymers the value of the
scaling exponent in the present system is non-universal being temperature dependent.

Finally, like in the case of (1+1) directed polymers the
zero temperature
limit
(
\beta\to\infty
) in the considered (2+1) system remains non accessible in the framework
of the usual continuous-space and
replica symmetric
approach.
Either we have to consider the lattice version of this model or we have to introduce
some kind of a replica symmetry breaking ansatz
[
27
]
.

In any case further systematic study (both theoretical an numerical)
of this system would be highly welcomed.

Appendix A

For the calculations of the integrals in eqs.(
55
)-(
57
)
due to the symmetry of the wave function
\psi(x_{1},\,...\,x_{N})

we can order the particle’s positions, e.g. as follows:

\{x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{N}\}\;\to\;\{-\infty<x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq...\leq x_{N}<+\infty\}

(A.1)

In this case

\int\,dx_{1}\,...\,dx_{N}\;\to\;N!\underbrace{\int dx_{1}\,...\,dx_{N}}_{-\infty<x_{1}\leq...\leq x_{N}<+\infty}

(A.2)

and

\sum_{1\leq a<b}^{N}\,|x_{a}-x_{b}|\;=\;-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bigl(N-2k+1\bigr)\,x_{k}

(A.3)

so that the wave function takes the form

\psi(x_{1},\,...\,x_{N})\;=\;\exp\Bigl\{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\,\mu_{k}\,x_{k}\Bigr\}

(A.4)

where

\mu_{k}\;=\;\bigl(N-2k+1\bigr)

(A.5)

For the factor
I_{0}(N)
, eq.(
55
), we get the following integral representation

I_{0}(N)\;=\;2\,N!\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dp}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx_{N}\int_{-\infty}^{x_{N}}dx_{N-1}\;...\;\int_{-\infty}^{x_{3}}dx_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{x_{2}}dx_{1}\exp\Bigl\{2\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bigl(\mu_{k}+ip\bigr)x_{k}\Bigr\}

(A.6)

Integrating one after another over
x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{N-1}
we obtain

I_{0}(N)\;=\;2^{-(N-1)}\,N!\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dp}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx_{N}\frac{\exp\Bigl\{\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mu_{k}\;+\;iNp\Bigr)\,x_{N}\Bigr\}}{(\mu_{1}+ip)(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+2ip)\,...\,(\mu_{1}+...+\mu_{N-1}+i(N-1)p)}

(A.7)

Taking into account that
\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mu_{k}\,=\,0
we eventually find

\displaystyle I_{0}(N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-(N-1)}\,N!\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp\frac{\delta(Np)}{(\mu_{1}+ip)(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+2ip)\,...\,(\mu_{1}+...+\mu_{N-1}+i(N-1)p)}

(A.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-(N-1)}\,N!\,\frac{1}{N}\,\frac{1}{\mu_{1}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})\,...\,(\mu_{1}+...+\mu_{N-1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-(N-1)}\,(N-1)!\,\frac{1}{(N-1)(2N-4)(3N-9)\,...\,\bigl[(N-1)-(N-1)(N-1)\bigr]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-(N-1)}\,(N-1)!\,\frac{1}{\bigl[(N-1)!\bigr]^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{-(N-1)}}{(N-1)!}

In a similar way for the factor
I_{\nabla}(N)
, eq.(
56
),
we obtain

\displaystyle I_{\nabla}(N)

\displaystyle=

Appendix B

The average distance between particles can be computed as follows

\langle|\Delta{\bf x}|\rangle\;=\;\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\sum_{a<b}^{N}\langle|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\rangle\;=\;\frac{2\int d^{2}x_{1}\,...\,d^{2}x_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}\Bigr)\,\sum_{a<b}^{N}|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\;\exp\Bigl\{-2\sum_{1\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\Bigr\}}{N(N-1)\;\int d^{2}x_{1}\,...\,d^{2}x_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}\Bigr)\,\exp\Bigl\{-2\sum_{1\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\Bigr\}}

(B.1)

or

\langle|\Delta{\bf x}|\rangle\;=\;-\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\;\ln\biggl[\int d^{2}x_{1}\,...\,d^{2}x_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}\Bigr)\,\exp\Bigl\{-\lambda\sum_{1\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\Bigr\}\biggr]\,\bigg|_{\lambda=2}

(B.2)

After rescaling
{\bf x}_{a}\to\lambda^{-1}\,{\bf x}_{a}
we get

\displaystyle\langle|\Delta{\bf x}|\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\;\ln\biggl[\lambda^{-2(N-1)}\int d^{2}x_{1}\,...\,d^{2}x_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}\Bigr)\,\exp\Bigl\{-\sum_{1\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\Bigr\}\biggr]\,\bigg|_{\lambda=2}

(B.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\;\biggl\{-2(N-1)\ln(\lambda)\;+\;\ln\biggl[\int d^{2}x_{1}\,...\,d^{2}x_{N}\;\delta\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}\Bigr)\,\exp\Bigl\{-\sum_{1\leq a<b}^{N}|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\Bigr\}\biggr]\biggr\}\,\bigg|_{\lambda=2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\,2(N-1)\,\frac{1}{\lambda}\,\Big|_{\lambda=2}\;=\;\frac{2}{N}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{N}

As for any two particles
|{\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}|\,\sim\,N^{-1}
,
the square of this difference
\bigl({\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}\bigr)^{2}\,\sim\,N^{-2}
,
or

\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\sum_{a,b}^{N}\bigl({\bf x}_{a}-{\bf x}_{b}\bigr)^{2}\;=\;\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\Bigl[2N\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}^{2}\;-\;2\,\Bigl(\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]\;\sim\;N^{-2}

(B.4)

Taking into account that
\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}\;=\;0
we obtain

\frac{2}{(N-1)}\,\sum_{a=1}^{N}{\bf x}_{a}^{2}\;\sim\;N^{-2}

(B.5)

which means that
{\bf x}_{a}^{2}\,\sim\,N^{-2}
and
|{\bf x}_{a}|\,\sim\,N^{-1}
.

Appendix C

The Jacobian of the linear transformation

\displaystyle{\bf x}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf z}\;+\;{\bf y}_{a}\;\;\;\;\;(a\,=\,2,...,N-1)

\displaystyle{\bf x}_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf z}\;-\;\sum_{a=2}^{N-1}\,{\bf y}_{a}

(C.1)

is

D(N)\;=\;\det\bigg|\frac{\partial\bigl({\bf x}_{2},...,{\bf x}_{N}\bigr)}{\partial\bigl({\bf z},{\bf y}_{2},...,{\bf y}_{N-1}\bigr)}\bigg|

(C.2)

In the Cartesian vector representation
{\bf x}_{a}=(p_{a},q_{a})\,;\;\;{\bf y}_{a}=(s_{a},t_{a})\,;\;\;{\bf z}_{a}=(u,v)

the above linear transformation reads

\displaystyle p_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u\;+\;s_{a}\;\;\;\;\;(a\,=\,2,...,N-1)

\displaystyle p_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u\;-\;\sum_{a=2}^{N-1}\,s_{a}

(C.3)

\displaystyle q_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v\;+\;t_{a}\;\;\;\;\;(a\,=\,2,...,N-1)

\displaystyle q_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v\;-\;\sum_{a=2}^{N-1}\,t_{a}

(C.4)

so that

D(N)\;=\;\det\bigg|\frac{\partial\bigl(p_{2},...,p_{N}\bigr)}{\partial\bigl(u,s_{2},...,s_{N-1}\bigr)}\bigg|\,\times\,\det\bigg|\frac{\partial\bigl(q_{2},...,q_{N}\bigr)}{\partial\bigl(v,t_{2},...,t_{N-1}\bigr)}\bigg|

(C.5)

Explicitly,

\det\bigg|\frac{\partial\bigl(p_{2},...,p_{N}\bigr)}{\partial\bigl(u,s_{2},...,s_{N-1}\bigr)}\bigg|\;=\;\det\,\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr$\frac{\partial p_{2}}{\partial u}$&$\frac{\partial p_{3}}{\partial u}$&$\frac{\partial p_{4}}{\partial u}$&...&$\frac{\partial p_{N}}{\partial u}$\\
\hline\cr$\frac{\partial p_{2}}{\partial s_{2}}$&$\frac{\partial p_{3}}{\partial s_{2}}$&$\frac{\partial p_{4}}{\partial s_{2}}$&\; ...&$\frac{\partial p_{N}}{\partial s_{2}}$\\
\hline\cr$\frac{\partial p_{2}}{\partial s_{3}}$&$\frac{\partial p_{3}}{\partial s_{3}}$&$\frac{\partial p_{4}}{\partial s_{3}}$&...&$\frac{\partial p_{N}}{\partial s_{3}}$\\
\hline\cr\vrule\lx@intercol\hfil. . . . . . . . . . . .\hfil\lx@intercol\vrule\lx@intercol \\
\hline\cr$\frac{\partial p_{2}}{\partial s_{N-1}}$&$\frac{\partial p_{3}}{\partial s_{N-1}}$&$\frac{\partial p_{4}}{\partial s_{N-1}}$&...&$\frac{\partial p_{N}}{\partial s_{N-1}}$\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}

(C.6)

Simple calculations yield

\det\bigg|\frac{\partial\bigl(p_{2},...,p_{N}\bigr)}{\partial\bigl(u,s_{2},...,s_{N-1}\bigr)}\bigg|\;=\;\det\,\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr$\;\;1\;\;$&$\;\;1\;\;$&$\;\;1\;\;$&...&$1$\\
\hline\cr$1$&$0$&$0$&\; ...&$-1$\\
\hline\cr$0$&$1$&$0$&...&$-1$\\
\hline\cr\vrule\lx@intercol\hfil. . . . . . . . . . . .\hfil\lx@intercol\vrule\lx@intercol \\

(C.7)

Evidently, one gets the same result for the second determinant in the r.h.s of eq.(
C.5
).
Thus, we obtain

D(N)\;=\;(N-1)^{2}\Big|_{N\gg 1}\;\simeq\;N^{2}

(C.8)

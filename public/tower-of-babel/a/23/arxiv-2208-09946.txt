Tense logic based on finite orthomodular posets
Ivan Chajda, Helmut Länger

Abstract
It is widely accepted that the logic of quantum mechanics is based on orthomodular posets. However, such a logic is not dynamic in the sense that it does not incorporate time dimension. To fill this gap, we introduce certain tense operators on such a logic in an inexact way, but still satisfying requirements asked on tense operators in the classical logic based on Boolean algebras or in various non-classical logics. Our construction of tense operators works perfectly when the orthomodular poset in question is finite. We investigate the behaviour of these tense operators, e.g. we show that some of them form a dynamic pair. Moreover, we prove that if the tense operators preserve one of the inexact connectives conjunction or implication as defined by the authors recently in another paper, then they also preserve the other one. Finally, we show how to construct the binary relation of time preference on a given time set provided the tense operators are given, up to equivalence induced by natural quasiorders.

Introduction

At the beginning of the twentieth century it was recognized that the logic of quantum mechanics differs essentially from classical propositional calculus based algebraically on Boolean algebras. K. Husimi
[
16
]
and G. Birkhoff together with J. von Neumann
[
3
]
introduced orthomodular lattices in order to serve as an algebraic base for the logic of quantum mechanics, see
[
1
]
. These lattices incorporate many aspects of this logic with one exception. Namely, within the logic of quantum mechanics the disjunction of two propositions need not exist in the case when these propositions are neither orthogonal nor comparable. This fact motivated a number of researchers to consider orthomodular posets instead of orthomodular lattices within their corresponding investigations, see e.g.
[
18
]
and references therein.

A propositional logic, either classical or non-classical, usually does not incorporate the dimension of time. In order to organize this logic as a so-called
tense logic
(or

in another terminology, see
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
19
]
and
[

]
) we usually construct the so-called

,

,
H
and
G

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle\ldots\text{``It has at some time been the case that''},

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\ldots\text{``It will at some time be the case that''},

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle\ldots\text{``It has always been the case that''},

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle\ldots\text{``It will always be the case that''}.

As the reader may guess, we need a certain time scale. For this reason a
time frame

(T,R)
was introduced. By
T\neq\emptyset
is meant a set of time, either finite or infinite, and
R\subseteq T^{2}
is the relation of time preference, i.e. for

means

is “before”

, or

is “after”

. For our purposes in this paper we will consider only so-called

(see
[
8
]
), i.e. relations

such that for each

there exist some

with

and
s\mathrel{R}t
. Of course, if

is reflexive then it is serial. Usually,

is considered to be a partial order relation or a quasiorder relation, see e.g.
[
5
]
,
[

]
and
[

.

Another important task in tense logics is to construct for given tense operators a relation

coincide with the given tense operators.

If the logic in question is based on a complete lattice
(L,\vee,\wedge)
and the time frame
(T,R)
is given then the tense operators
P,F,H,G\colon L^{T}\rightarrow L^{T}
can be defined as follows:

\displaystyle P(q)(s)

\displaystyle:=\bigvee\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\},

\displaystyle F(q)(s)

\displaystyle:=\bigvee\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\},

\displaystyle H(q)(s)

\displaystyle:=\bigwedge\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\},

\displaystyle G(q)(s)

\displaystyle:=\bigwedge\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\}

for all
q\in A^{T}
and all

(see e.g.
[
4
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
and
[
20
]
).

The problem arises when our logic is based on a poset
(A,\leq)
where joins and meets need not exist. In
[
7
]
such a situation has been solved by embedding of
A^{T}
into a complete lattice. Then we can use the aforementioned definitions of tense operators
P
,
F
,
H
and
G
, but the disadvantage is that the results of these operators need not belong to
A^{T}
again. This motivated us to try another approach, see Section 3.

Moreover, if
\odot
denotes conjunction and
\rightarrow
implication within the given propositional logic, we usually ask our tense operators to satisfy the following inequalities and equalities:

(1)

P(x)\odot H(y)\leq P(x\odot y)
,

(2)

H(x)\odot P(y)\leq P(x\odot y)
,

(3)

H(x)\odot H(y)=H(x\odot y)
,

(4)

F(x)\odot G(y)\leq F(x\odot y)
,

(5)

G(x)\odot F(y)\leq F(x\odot y)
,

(6)

G(x)\odot G(y)=G(x\odot y)
,

(7)

P(x\rightarrow y)\leq H(x)\rightarrow P(y)
,

(8)

H(x\rightarrow y)\leq P(x)\rightarrow P(y)
,

(9)

H(x\rightarrow y)\leq H(x)\rightarrow H(y)
,

(10)

F(x\rightarrow y)\leq G(x)\rightarrow F(y)
,

(11)

G(x\rightarrow y)\leq F(x)\rightarrow F(y)
,

(12)

G(x\rightarrow y)\leq G(x)\rightarrow G(y)
.

For our considerations we need the connectives
\odot
and
\rightarrow
introduced in an orthomodular poset. In general this task is ambiguous, see
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
13
]
and
[
17
]
, but for finite orthomodular posets (in fact for orthomodular posets of finite height) it was solved by the authors in
[
6
]
where these connectives are introduced in such a way that one gets an adjoint pair. This is the reason why we will restrict ourselves to finite orthomodular posets only.

The connectives introduced in
[
6
]
are everywhere defined, but their results need not be elements of the given orthomodular poset, but may be subsets of this poset containing (incomparable) elements of the same (maximal) truth value. This may cause a problem for the composition of tense operators. Namely, we want to show that our couple
(P,G)
of tense operators forms a
dynamic pair
on the given orthomodular poset
\mathbf{A}=(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
, i.e. the following axioms (P1) – (P3) should hold (see

]
):

(P1)

G(1)=1
and
P(0)=0
,

(P2)

and

,

(P3)

q\leq(G\circ P)(q)
and
(P\circ G)(q)\leq q
.

If
G,P\colon A^{T}\rightarrow(2^{A}\setminus\{\emptyset\})^{T}
for some time frame
(T,R)
then we must solve the problem how to define the compositions
G\circ P
and
P\circ G
in (P3).

A unary operation

and if
a^{\prime\prime}=a

A

is a unary operation
′
on

and

. For a bounded poset
(A,\leq,0,1)
with an antitone involution
′

(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
.

Definition 2.1
.

An orthomodular poset is a bounded poset
(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
with an antitone involution
′
that is a complementation satisfying the following two conditions:

(i)

If
x,y\in A
and

then
x\vee y
is defined,

(ii)

if
x,y\in A
and
x\leq y
then
y=x\vee(y\wedge x^{\prime})
.

Condition
(ii)
is called the orthomodular law. Two elements
a,b
of
A
are called orthogonal to each other if
a\leq b^{\prime}

(
which is equivalent to
b\leq a^{\prime}
)
.

Let us note that due to De Morgan’s laws, (ii) of Definition
2.1
is equivalent to the condition

(ii’)

If
x,y\in A
and
x\leq y
then
x=y\wedge(x\vee y^{\prime})
.

\displaystyle:\Leftrightarrow p\leq q\text{ for all }p\in B\text{ and all }q\in B,

\displaystyle B\leq_{1}C

\displaystyle B\leq_{2}C

\displaystyle B\sqsubseteq C

Hereby, for

\displaystyle\{a\}\text{ with }a,

\displaystyle\text{the mapping assigning to each }t\in T\text{ the set }\{a_{t}\}\text{ with the mapping assigning to each}

\displaystyle\quad t\in T\text{ the element }a_{t},

\displaystyle\{p\}\text{ with }p.

Now for

\displaystyle L(B)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in A\mid x\leq B\},

\displaystyle:=\{x\in A\mid B\leq x\},

The following result was proved in

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be an orthomodular poset,
a,b\in A

. Then the following hold:

(i)

If
a\leq B

B=a\vee(B\wedge a^{\prime})
,

(ii)

(iii)

\Min U(a,b^{\prime})\wedge b

Let us note that both

\displaystyle B\odot C

\displaystyle:=\bigcup\{b\odot c\mid b\in B,c\in C\},

\displaystyle B\rightarrow C

\displaystyle:=\bigcup\{b\rightarrow c\mid b\in B,c\in C\}

Moreover, we define

\displaystyle(x\odot y)(t)

\displaystyle:=x(t)\odot y(t),

\displaystyle(x\rightarrow y)(t)

\displaystyle:=x(t)\rightarrow y(t)

(x\rightarrow y)\odot x\approx\Max L(x,y).

Moreover,

x\odot x\approx x
and
x\odot 1\approx 1\odot x\approx x
. In the following we will investigate tense operators on finite orthomodular posets equipped with the operators

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a finite orthomodular poset and
p,q\in A^{T}
. Then

(i)

p\leq q\rightarrow(p\odot q)
,

(ii)

(p\rightarrow q)\odot p\leq q
.

Proof.

(i)

Since
r\in\Min U(p,q^{\prime})\wedge q
implies
r\leq q
and hence
\Max L(q,r)=r
and since
q^{\prime}\leq\Min U(p,q^{\prime})
we have

\displaystyle q\rightarrow(p\odot q)

\displaystyle=q\rightarrow\big(\Min U(p,q^{\prime})\wedge q\big)=\bigcup\{q\rightarrow r\mid r\in\Min U(p,q^{\prime})\wedge q\}=

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{q^{\prime}\vee\Max L(q,r)\mid r\in\Min U(p,q^{\prime})\wedge q\}=

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{q^{\prime}\vee r\mid r\in\Min U(p,q^{\prime})\wedge q\}=q^{\prime}\vee\big(\Min U(p,q^{\prime})\wedge q\big)=

\displaystyle=\Min U(p,q^{\prime})\geq p

according to Proposition
2.2
.

(ii)

Since
r\in p^{\prime}\vee\Max L(p,q)\wedge q
implies
p^{\prime}\leq r
and hence
\Min U(r,p^{\prime})=r
and since
\Max L(p,q)\leq p
we have

\displaystyle(p\rightarrow q)\odot p

\displaystyle=\big(p^{\prime}\vee\Max L(p,q)\big)\odot p=\bigcup\{r\odot p\mid r\in p^{\prime}\vee\Max L(p,q)\}=

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{\Min U(r,p^{\prime})\wedge p\mid r\in p^{\prime}\vee\Max L(p,q)\}=

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{r\wedge p\mid r\in p^{\prime}\vee\Max L(p,q)\}=\big(p^{\prime}\vee\Max L(p,q)\big)\wedge p=\Max L(p,q)\leq

\displaystyle\leq q

according to Proposition
2.2
.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a finite orthomodular poset,
x\in(2^{A}\setminus\{\emptyset\})^{T}
and
p\in A^{T}
. Then

(i)

x\leq_{1}p\rightarrow(x\odot p)
and
x\leq_{2}p\rightarrow(x\odot p)
,

(ii)

(p\rightarrow x)\odot p\leq_{1}x
and
(p\rightarrow x)\odot p\leq_{2}x
.

Proof.

(i)

We have

\displaystyle p\rightarrow(x\odot p)

\displaystyle=p\rightarrow\bigcup\{q\odot p\mid q\in x\}=p\rightarrow\bigcup\{\Min U(q,p^{\prime})\wedge p\mid q\in x\}=

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{p\rightarrow r\mid r\in\bigcup\{\Min U(q,p^{\prime})\wedge p\mid q\in x\}\}=

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{p^{\prime}\vee\Max L(p,r)\mid r\in\bigcup\{\Min U(q,p^{\prime})\wedge p\mid q\in x\}\}=

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{p^{\prime}\vee r\mid r\in\bigcup\{\Min U(q,p^{\prime})\wedge p\mid q\in x\}\}=

\displaystyle=p^{\prime}\vee\bigcup\{\Min U(q,p^{\prime})\wedge p\mid q\in x\}=

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{p^{\prime}\vee\big(\Min U(q,p^{\prime})\wedge p\big)\mid q\in x\}=\bigcup\{\Min U(q,p^{\prime})\mid q\in x\}.

(ii)

We have

\displaystyle(p\rightarrow x)\odot p

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{p\rightarrow q\mid q\in x\}\odot p=\bigcup\{p^{\prime}\vee\Max L(p,q)\mid q\in x\}\odot p=

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{r\odot p\mid r\in\bigcup\{p^{\prime}\vee\Max L(p,q)\mid q\in x\}\}=

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{\Min U(r,p^{\prime})\wedge p\mid r\in\bigcup\{p^{\prime}\vee\Max L(p,q)\mid q\in x\}\}=

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{r\wedge p\mid r\in\bigcup\{p^{\prime}\vee\Max L(p,q)\mid q\in x\}\}=

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{p^{\prime}\vee\Max L(p,q)\mid q\in x\}\wedge p=

3
Dynamic pairs

As mentioned in the introduction, by a
dynamic pair
is meant a couple
(P,G)
of tense operators satisfying axioms (P1) – (P3). We firstly define tense operators on a finite orthomodular poset
(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
as follows: Let a time frame
(T,R)
be given. Define
P,F,H,G\colon A^{T}\rightarrow(2^{A}\setminus\{\emptyset\})^{T}
by

\displaystyle P(q)(s)

\displaystyle:=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\}),

\displaystyle F(q)(s)

\displaystyle:=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\}),

\displaystyle H(q)(s)

\displaystyle:=\Max L(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\}),

\displaystyle G(q)(s)

\displaystyle:=\Max L(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\})

for all
q\in A^{T}
and all
s\in T
.

It is elementary to show that if the orthomodular poset
(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
is a complete lattice then these tense operators coincide with those mentioned in the introduction. However, in general
P(q)
for
q\in A^{T}
need not be a single element of
A^{T}
(i.e. a singleton), but may be a non-empty subset of
A^{T}
. Analogously for the remaining tense operators. Hence, these operators are again “inexact” in the sense that they reach maximal (for
H
,
G
) or minimal (for
P
,
F
) incomparable values and we cannot distinguished among them. On the other hand, the results of these operators belong to
A
contrary to the case when the poset
(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
is embedded into a complete lattice, the method used in
[
7
]
.

Since we want to compose our tense operators we need to solve two essential tasks:

(i)

Extend
P,F,H,G
from
A^{T}
to
2^{(A^{T})}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
(i.e. lifting of the operators).

(ii)

Define how to compose them.

Concerning the first task we define for
B\in 2^{(A^{T})}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
and all
s\in T

\displaystyle P(B)(s)

\displaystyle:=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid q\in B\text{ and }t\mathrel{R}s\}),

\displaystyle F(B)(s)

\displaystyle:=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid q\in B\text{ and }s\mathrel{R}t\}),

\displaystyle H(B)(s)

\displaystyle:=\Max L(\{q(t)\mid q\in B\text{ and }t\mathrel{R}s\}),

\displaystyle G(B)(s)

\displaystyle:=\Max L(\{q(t)\mid q\in B\text{ and }s\mathrel{R}t\}).

In order to solve the second task, we should mention that our extended tense operators are not mappings from
2^{(A^{T})}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
to
2^{(A^{T})}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
, but from
2^{(A^{T})}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
to
(2^{A}\setminus\{\emptyset\})^{T}
. Hence we introduce the so-called
transformation function

\varphi\colon(2^{A}\setminus\{\emptyset\})^{T}\rightarrow 2^{(A^{T})}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
as follows:

\varphi(x):=\{q\in A^{T}\mid q(t)\in x(t)\text{ for all }t\in T\}\text{ for all }x\in(2^{A}\setminus\{\emptyset\})^{T}.

By means of the transformation function we can define the
composition

G*P
of the tense operators
G
and
P
by

G*P:=G\circ\varphi\circ P.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(A,\leq)
be a poset,
T\neq\emptyset
,
p\in A^{T}
and
x,y\in(2^{A}\setminus\{\emptyset\})^{T}
. Then

(i)

\varphi
is injective,

(ii)

if
z(t):=\{p(t)\}
for all

then
\varphi(z)=\{p\}
,

(iii)

if and only if
\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(y)

Proof.

(i)

Assume
x\neq y
. Then there exists some
t\in T
with
x(t)\neq y(t)
. Without loss of generality, assume
x(t)\setminus\big(y(t)\big)\neq\emptyset
. Let

and

with

and

for all

. Then

and hence
\varphi(x)\neq\varphi(y)
.

(ii)

If
z(t):=\{p(t)\}
for all
t\in T
then
\varphi(z)=\{q\in A^{T}\mid q(t)\in z(t)\text{ for all }t\in T\}=\{p\}
.

(iii)

Assume
x\leq y
. Let
p\in\varphi(x)
and
q\in\varphi(y)
. Then
p(s)\in x(s)
and
q(s)\in y(s)
for all
s\in T
. Hence
p(s)\leq q(s)
for all
s\in T
, i.e.
p\leq q
. This shows
\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(y)
. Conversely, assume
\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(y)
. Let
t\in T
,
a\in x(t)
and
b\in y(t)
. Take
p,q\in A^{T}
with
p(t)=a
,
p(s)\in x(s)
for all
s\in T\setminus\{t\}
,
q(t)=b
and
q(s)\in y(s)
for all
s\in T\setminus\{t\}
. Then
p(s)\in x(s)
and
q(s)\in y(s)
for all
s\in T
. Hence
p\in\varphi(x)
and
q\in\varphi(y)
which implies
p\leq q
whence
a=p(t)\leq q(t)\leq b
. This shows
x(t)\leq y(t)
. Since
t
was an arbitrary element of
T
we conclude
x\leq y
.

∎

Now we are ready to prove our result. But first we make the following more or less evident observation.

Observation 3.2
.

Let
(A,\leq)
be a finite poset and
B,C
subsets of
A
with
C\neq\emptyset
. Then
B\subseteq C
implies
B\leq_{1}\Max C
and
\Min C\leq_{2}B
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{A}=(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a finite orthomodular poset,
(T,R)
a time frame and
P
and
G
tense operators as defined above. Then the couple
(P,G)
forms a dynamic pair, i.e.

(P1)

G(1)=1
and
P(0)=0
,

(P2)

if
p,q\in A^{T}
and
p\leq q
then
G(p)\leq_{1}G(q)
and
P(p)\leq_{2}P(q)
,

(P3)

if
q\in A^{T}
then
q\leq_{1}(G*P)(q)
and
(P*G)(q)\leq_{2}q
.

Proof.

Let
p,q\in A^{T}
and
s\in T
.

(P1)

G(1)(s)=\Max L(1)=\Max A=1
and
P(0)(s)=\Min U(0)=\Min A=0

(P2)

Assume
p\leq q
. Then

\Max L(\{p(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\})\subseteq L(\{p(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\})\subseteq L(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\})

and hence

G(p)(s)=\Max L(\{p(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\})\leq_{1}\Max L(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\})=G(q)(s)

according to Observation
3.2
. Similarly,

\Min U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\})\subseteq U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\})\subseteq U(\{p(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\})

and hence

P(p)(s)=\Min U(\{p(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\})\leq_{2}\Min U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\})=P(q)(s)

according to Observation
3.2
.

(P3)

We have

\displaystyle P(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\}),

\displaystyle\varphi\big(P(q)\big)

\displaystyle=\{r\in A^{T}\mid r(u)\in P(q)(u)\text{ for all }u\in T\}=

\displaystyle=\{r\in A^{T}\mid r(u)\in\Min U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}u\})\text{ for all }u\in T\},

\displaystyle(G*P)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=G\Big(\varphi\big(P(q)\big)\Big)(s)=\Max L(\{r(v)\mid r\in\varphi\big(P(q)\big)\text{ and }s\mathrel{R}v\})=

\displaystyle=\Max L\big(\bigcup\{\Min U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}v\})\mid s\mathrel{R}v\}\big),

\displaystyle q(s)

\displaystyle\in L\big(\bigcup\{U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}v\})\mid s\mathrel{R}v\}\big)\subseteq

\displaystyle\subseteq L\big(\bigcup\{\Min U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}v\})\mid s\mathrel{R}v\}\big)

and hence
q(s)\leq_{1}(G*P)(q)(s)
according to Observation
3.2
. Analogously, we have

\displaystyle G(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Max L(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\}),

\displaystyle\varphi\big(G(q)\big)

\displaystyle=\{r\in A^{T}\mid r(u)\in G(q)(u)\text{ for all }u\in T\}=

\displaystyle=\{r\in A^{T}\mid r(u)\in\Max L(\{q(t)\mid u\mathrel{R}t\})\text{ for all }u\in T\},

\displaystyle(P*G)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=P\Big(\varphi\big(G(q)\big)\Big)(s)=\Min U(\{r(v)\mid r\in\varphi\big(G(q)\big)\text{ and }v\mathrel{R}s\})=

\displaystyle=\Min U\big(\bigcup\{\Max L\big(\{q(t)\mid v\mathrel{R}t\})\mid v\mathrel{R}s\}\big),

\displaystyle q(s)

\displaystyle\in U\big(\bigcup\{L(\{q(t)\mid v\mathrel{R}t\})\mid v\mathrel{R}s\}\big)\subseteq

\displaystyle\subseteq U\big(\bigcup\{\Max L(\{q(t)\mid v\mathrel{R}t\})\mid v\mathrel{R}s\}\big)

and hence
(P*G)(q)(s)\leq_{2}q(s)
according to Observation
3.2
.

∎

Similarly one can show that also the couple
(F,H)
forms a dynamic pair.

4
Properties of tense operators

As mentioned in our previous section, our logic based on a finite orthomodular poset is equipped with logical connectives
\odot
and
\rightarrow
forming an adjoint pair. The aim of this section is to show that our tense operators satisfy properties considered in classical or non-classical tense logics as collected in

Before formulating of our results, let us illuminate our concepts by the following example.

When combining operators, both tense ones and logical connectives, we must use the transformation function
\varphi
because of the reasons explained in the previous section. We can state and prove the following propositions.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(A,\leq,0,1)
be a finite bounded poset,
(T,R)
a time frame with reflexive
R
and
q\in A^{T}
. Then

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle\leq(\varphi\circ P)(q),

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle\leq(\varphi\circ F)(q),

\displaystyle(\varphi\circ H)(q)

\displaystyle\leq q,

\displaystyle(\varphi\circ G)(q)

\displaystyle\leq q.

Proof.

Let
s\in T
. We have

\displaystyle P(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\}),

\displaystyle(\varphi\circ P)(q)

\displaystyle=\{p\in A^{T}\mid p(t)\in\Min U(\{q(u)\mid u\mathrel{R}t\})\text{ for all }t\in T\},

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle\leq p\text{ for all }p\in(\varphi\circ P)(q),

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle\leq(\varphi\circ P)(q).

The result for
F
is analogous. Now

\displaystyle H(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Max L(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\}),

\displaystyle(\varphi\circ H)(q)

\displaystyle=\{p\in A^{T}\mid p(t)\in\Max L(\{q(u)\mid u\mathrel{R}t\})\text{ for all }t\in T\},

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\leq q\text{ for all }p\in(\varphi\circ H)(q),

\displaystyle(\varphi\circ H)(q)

\displaystyle\leq q.

The result for
G
is analogous.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a finite orthomodular poset,
(T,R)
a time frame,
x\in(2^{A}\setminus\{\emptyset\})^{T}
,
B,C\in 2^{(A^{T})}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
with
B\leq C
and
s\in T
and denote by
\varphi
the transformation function. Then the following hold:

(i)

\displaystyle P\big(\varphi(x)\big)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U\big(\bigcup\{x(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\}\big),

\displaystyle F\big(\varphi(x)\big)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U\big(\bigcup\{x(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\}\big),

\displaystyle H\big(\varphi(x)\big)(s)

\displaystyle=\Max L\big(\bigcup\{x(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\}\big),

\displaystyle G\big(\varphi(x)\big)(s)

\displaystyle=\Max L\big(\bigcup\{x(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\}\big),

(ii)

H(B)=P(B^{\prime})^{\prime}
and
G(B)=F(B^{\prime})^{\prime}
,

(iii)

P(B)\leq_{2}P(C)
,
F(B)\leq_{2}F(C)
,
H(B)\leq_{1}H(C)
and
G(B)\leq_{1}G(C)
,

(iv)

H(B)\leq P(B)
and
G(B)\leq F(B)
.

Proof.

(i)

We have

P\big(\varphi(x)\big)(s)=\Min L(\{p(t)\mid p\in\varphi(x)\text{ and }s\mathrel{R}t\})=\Min U\big(\bigcup\{x(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\}\big).

The proof for
F
,
H
and
G
is analogous.

(ii)

We have

\displaystyle P(B)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid q\in B\text{ and }t\mathrel{R}s\})=\Min U(\{q^{\prime}(t)\mid q\in B^{\prime}\text{ and }t\mathrel{R}s\})=

\displaystyle=\Min U(\{\big(q(t)\big)^{\prime}\mid q\in B^{\prime}\text{ and }t\mathrel{R}s\})=

\displaystyle=\big(\Max L(\{q(t)\mid q\in B^{\prime}\text{ and }t\mathrel{R}S\}\big)^{\prime}=H(B^{\prime})^{\prime}(s).

The second assertion can be proved in an analogous way.

(iii)

\displaystyle P(C)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid q\in C\text{ and }t\mathrel{R}s\})\subseteq U(\{q(t)\mid q\in C\text{ and }t\mathrel{R}s\})\subseteq

\displaystyle\subseteq U(\{p(t)\mid p\in B\text{ and }t\mathrel{R}s\})

P(B)(s)=\Min U(\{p(t)\mid p\in B\text{ and }t\mathrel{R}s\})\leq_{2}P(C)(s).

\displaystyle H(B)(s)

\displaystyle=\Max L(\{p(t)\mid p\in B\text{ and }t\mathrel{R}s\})\subseteq L(\{p(t)\mid p\in B\text{ and }t\mathrel{R}s\})\subseteq

.

is serial there exists some

,

]

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(A,\leq,0,1)
be a finite bounded poset and
(T,R)
a time frame with reflexive

. Then

\begin{array}[]{llll}P\leq_{2}P*F,&F\leq_{2}F*P,&H\leq_{1}H*P,&G\leq_{1}G*P,\\

Let

. According to Proposition

However, in some cases we can prove results which are stronger than those of Theorem

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(A,\leq,0,1)
be a finite bounded poset,
(T,R)
a time frame with reflexive

and

If
q\in A^{T}
and

then

and hence

\displaystyle(P*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\})\geq X(q)(s),

\displaystyle(F*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\})\geq X(q)(s),

\displaystyle(H*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Max L(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\})\leq X(q)(s),

\displaystyle(G*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Max L(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\})\leq X(q)(s).

∎

\hfill\Box

In the following we show that also preserving of the connective
\rightarrow
with respect to
H
or
G
can be derived by the corresponding property for
\odot
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a finite orthomodular poset,
\odot
and
\rightarrow
defined by
(13)
and
B,C,D\in 2^{A}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
. Then
B\odot C\sqsubseteq D
is equivalent to
B\sqsubseteq C\rightarrow D
.

Proof.

First assume
B\odot C\sqsubseteq D
. Then there exist
b\in B
,
c\in C
,
d\in D
and
a\in b\odot c
with
a\leq d
. Hence
b\odot c\sqsubseteq d
. By operator left adjointness this implies
b\sqsubseteq c\rightarrow d
, i.e. there exists some
e\in c\rightarrow d
with
b\leq e
. Since
b\in B
and
e\in C\rightarrow D
we conclude
B\sqsubseteq C\rightarrow D
. Conversely, assume
B\sqsubseteq C\rightarrow D
. Then there exist
b\in B
,
c\in C
,
d\in D
and
e\in c\rightarrow d
with
b\leq e
. Hence
b\sqsubseteq c\rightarrow d
. By operator left adjointness this implies
b\odot c\sqsubseteq d
, i.e. there exists some
f\in b\odot c
with
f\leq d
. Since
f\in B\odot C
and
d\in D
we conclude
B\odot C\sqsubseteq D
.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a finite orthomodular poset,
(T,R)
a time frame and
X,Y,Z\in\{P,F,H,G\}
. Then the following hold:

(i)

Put
i:=1
if
Z\in\{H,G\}
and
i:=2
otherwise and assume

X\big(\varphi(x)\big)\odot Y(q)\leq_{i}Z\big(\varphi(x\odot q)\big)\text{ for all }x\in(2^{A}\setminus\{\emptyset\})^{T}\text{ and all }q\in A^{T}.

Then

X\big(\varphi(p\rightarrow q)\big)\sqsubseteq Y(p)\rightarrow Z(q)\text{ for all }p,q\in A^{T}.

(ii)

Put
i:=1
if
X\in\{H,G\}
and
i:=2
otherwise and assume

X\big(\varphi(p\rightarrow x)\big)\leq_{i}Y(p)\rightarrow Z\big(\varphi(x)\big)\text{ for all }p\in A^{T}\text{ and all }x\in(2^{A}\setminus\emptyset\})^{T}.

Then

X(p)\odot Y(q)\sqsubseteq Z\big(\varphi(p\odot q)\big)\text{ for all }p,q\in A^{T}.

Proof.

Let
p,q\in A
and
x\in 2^{A}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
.

(i)

Because of divisibility of the operator residuated structure
(A,\leq,\odot,\rightarrow,0,1)
we have

X\big(\varphi(p\rightarrow q)\big)\odot Y(p)\leq_{i}Z\Big(\varphi\big((p\rightarrow q)\odot p\big)\Big)=Z\Big(\varphi\big(\Max L(p,q)\big)\Big).

Now
\Max L(p,q)\leq q
implies
\varphi\big(\Max L(p,q)\big)\leq q
according to Lemma
3.1
whence

X\big(\varphi(p\rightarrow q)\big)\odot Y(p)\leq_{i}Z\Big(\varphi\big(\Max L(p,q)\big)\Big)\leq_{i}Z(q)

according to (iii) of Proposition
4.3
. Hence
X\big(\varphi(p\rightarrow q)\big)\odot Y(p)\sqsubseteq Z(q)
. Operator left adjointness (cf.
[
6
]
) and Lemma
4.7
yields

X\big(\varphi(p\rightarrow q)\big)\sqsubseteq Y(p)\rightarrow Z(q).

(ii)

Because of (i) of Lemma
2.3
and (iii) of Proposition
4.3
we obtain

X(p)\leq_{i}X\Big(\varphi\big(q\rightarrow(p\odot q)\big)\Big)\leq_{i}Y(q)\rightarrow Z\big(\varphi(p\odot q)\big)

and therefore

X(p)\sqsubseteq Y(q)\rightarrow Z\big(\varphi(p\odot q)\big).

Operator left adjointness (cf.
[
6
]
) and Lemma
4.7
yields

X(p)\odot Y(q)\sqsubseteq Z\big(\varphi(p\odot q)\big).

∎

5
How to construct a preference relation

In this section we will present a construction of a binary relation
R
on
T
for a given time set
T
and
\mathbf{A}
equipped with the tense operators
P
,
F
,
H
and
G
.

Let
\mathbf{A}=(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a finite orthomodular poset,
T
a time set,
(T,R)
the time frame. We say that the tense operators
P
,
F
,
H
and
G
are
constructed by
(T,R)
if for each
q\in A^{T}
and each
s\in T

\displaystyle P(q)(s)

\displaystyle:=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\}),

\displaystyle F(q)(s)

\displaystyle:=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\}),

\displaystyle H(q)(s)

\displaystyle:=\Max L(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\}),

\displaystyle G(q)(s)

\displaystyle:=\Max L(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\}).

Since
(P,G)
as well as
(F,H)
form a dynamic pair, the quintuple
(\mathbf{A},P,F,H,G)
is called a
dynamic orthomodular poset
.

Conversely, let
(\mathbf{A},P,F,H,G)
be a dynamic orthomodular poset and
T
a given time set. The question is how to construct a binary relation
R^{*}
on
T
such that
P
,
F
,
H
and
G
are constructed by
(T,R^{*})
.

Let
(P,\leq)
be a poset. On
2^{P}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
we define two binary relations
\approx_{1}
and
\approx_{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle A\approx_{1}B

\displaystyle\text{ if }A\leq_{1}B\text{ and }B\leq_{1}A,

\displaystyle A\approx_{2}B

\displaystyle\text{ if }A\leq_{2}B\text{ and }B\leq_{2}A

(
A,B\subseteq P
). Since
\leq_{1}
and
\leq_{2}
are quasiorder relations on
2^{P}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
,
\approx_{1}
and
\approx_{2}
are the corresponding equivalence relations on
2^{P}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
.

Define a binary relation
R^{*}
on
T
as follows:

(14)

s\mathrel{R^{*}}t
if both
G(q)(s)\leq q(t)\leq F(q)(s)
and
H(q)(t)\leq q(s)\leq P(q)(t)

hold for each
q\in A^{T}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a finite orthomodular poset,
(T,R)
a time frame and
P
,
F
,
H
and
G
the tense operators constructed by
(T,R)
. Let
R^{*}
be the relation defined by
(14)
. Then
R\subseteq R^{*}
. Let
X\in\{P,F,H,G\}
,
q\in A^{T}
and
s\in T
, let
X^{*}
denote the tense operator constructed by
(T,R^{*})
corresponding to
X
and put
i:=1
if
X\in\{H,G\}
and
i:=2
otherwise. Then

X(q)(s)\approx_{i}X^{*}(q)(s).

Proof.

Let
t\in T
. If
s\mathrel{R}t
then

\displaystyle P(q)(t)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\{q(u)\mid u\mathrel{R}t\})\geq q(s),

\displaystyle F(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\{q(u)\mid s\mathrel{R}u\})\geq q(t),

\displaystyle H(q)(t)

\displaystyle=\Max L(\{q(u)\mid u\mathrel{R}t\})\leq q(s),

\displaystyle G(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Max L(\{q(u)\mid s\mathrel{R}u\})\leq q(t).

This shows
R\subseteq R^{*}
. Now

\displaystyle P^{*}(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R^{*}}s\})\subseteq U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R^{*}}s\})\subseteq U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\}),

\displaystyle F^{*}(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R^{*}}t\})\subseteq U(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R^{*}}t\})\subseteq U(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\}),

\displaystyle H^{*}(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Max L(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R^{*}}s\})\subseteq L(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R^{*}}s\})\subseteq L(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\}),

\displaystyle G^{*}(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Max L(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R^{*}}t\})\subseteq L(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R^{*}}t\})\subseteq L(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\})

and hence

\displaystyle P(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\})\leq_{2}P^{*}(q)(s),

\displaystyle F(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\})\leq_{2}F^{*}(q)(s),

\displaystyle H^{*}(q)(s)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}\Max L(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\})=H(q)(s),

\displaystyle G^{*}(q)(s)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}\Max L(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\})=G(q)(s)

according to Observation
3.2
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle q(t)

\displaystyle\leq P(q)(s)\text{ for all }t\text{ with }t\mathrel{R^{*}}s,

\displaystyle q(t)

\displaystyle\leq F(q)(s)\text{ for all }t\text{ with }s\mathrel{R^{*}}t,

\displaystyle H(q)(s)

\displaystyle\leq q(t)\text{ for all }t\text{ with }t\mathrel{R^{*}}s,

\displaystyle G(q)(s)

\displaystyle\leq q(t)\text{ for all }t\text{ with }s\mathrel{R^{*}}t

and hence

\displaystyle P(q)(s)

\displaystyle\in U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R^{*}}s\}),

\displaystyle F(q)(s)

\displaystyle\in U(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R^{*}}t\}),

\displaystyle H(q)(s)

\displaystyle\in L(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R^{*}}s\}),

\displaystyle G(q)(s)

\displaystyle\in L(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R^{*}}t\})

whence

\displaystyle P^{*}(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R^{*}}s\})\leq_{2}P(q)(s),

\displaystyle F^{*}(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R^{*}}t\})\leq_{2}F(q)(s),

\displaystyle H(q)(s)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}\Max L(\{q(t)\mid t\mathrel{R^{*}}s\})=H^{*}(q)(s),

\displaystyle G(q)(s)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}\Max L(\{q(t)\mid s\mathrel{R^{*}}t\})=G^{*}(q)(s)

according to Observation
3.2
.
∎

Now we present another construction of a time preference relation on the time set.

Define a binary relation
\bar{R}
on
T
as follows:

(15)

s\mathrel{\bar{R}}t
if both
(G*X)(q)(s)\leq X(q)(t)\leq(F*X)(q)(s)
and

(H*X)(q)(t)\leq X(q)(s)\leq(P*X)(q)(t)

hold for each
X\in\{P,F,H,G\}
and each
q\in A^{T}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(A,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a finite orthomodular poset,
(T,R)
a time frame and
P
,
F
,
H
and
G
the tense operators constructed by
(T,R)
. Let
\bar{R}
be the relation defined by
(15)
. Then
R\subseteq\bar{R}
and for each
X,Y\in\{P,F,H,G\}
, each
q\in A^{T}
and each
s\in T
we have

(Y*X)(q)(s)\approx_{i}(\bar{Y}*X)(q)(s)

where
i=1
if
Y\in\{H,G\}
and
i=2
otherwise, and where
\bar{Y}
denotes the corresponding tense operator constructed by
(T,\bar{R})
.

Proof.

Let
X\in\{P,F,H,G\}
,
q\in A^{T}
and
s,t\in T
. If
s\mathrel{R}t
then

\displaystyle(P*X)(q)(t)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\bigcup\{X(q)(u)\mid u\mathrel{R}t\})\geq X(q)(s),

\displaystyle(F*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\bigcup\{X(q)(u)\mid s\mathrel{R}u\})\geq X(q)(t),

\displaystyle(H*X)(q)(t)

\displaystyle=\Max L(\bigcup\{X(q)(u)\mid u\mathrel{R}t\})\leq X(q)(s),

\displaystyle(G*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Max L(\bigcup\{X(q)(u)\mid s\mathrel{R}u\})\leq X(q)(t).

This shows
R\subseteq\bar{R}
. Now

\displaystyle(\bar{P}*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid t\mathrel{\bar{R}}s\})\subseteq U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid t\mathrel{\bar{R}}s\})\subseteq

\displaystyle\subseteq U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\}),

\displaystyle(\bar{F}*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid s\mathrel{\bar{R}}t\})\subseteq U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid s\mathrel{\bar{R}}t\})\subseteq

\displaystyle\subseteq U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\}),

\displaystyle(\bar{H}*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Max L(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid t\mathrel{\bar{R}}s\})\subseteq L(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid t\mathrel{\bar{R}}s\})\subseteq

\displaystyle\subseteq L(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\}),

\displaystyle(\bar{G}*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Max L(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid s\mathrel{\bar{R}}t\})\subseteq U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid s\mathrel{\bar{R}}t\})\subseteq

\displaystyle\subseteq U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\})

and hence

\displaystyle(P*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\})\leq_{2}(\bar{P}*X)(q)(s),

\displaystyle(F*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\})\leq_{2}(\bar{F}*X)(q)(s),

\displaystyle(\bar{H}*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}\Max L(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid t\mathrel{R}s\})=(H*X)(q)(s),

\displaystyle(\bar{G}*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}\Max L(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid s\mathrel{R}t\})=(G*X)(q)(s)

according to Observation
3.2
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle X(q)(t)

\displaystyle\leq(P*X)(q)(s)\text{ for all }t\text{ with }t\mathrel{\bar{R}}s,

\displaystyle X(q)(t)

\displaystyle\leq(F*X)(q)(s)\text{ for all }t\text{ with }s\mathrel{\bar{R}}t,

\displaystyle(H*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle\leq X(q)(t)\text{ for all }t\text{ with }t\mathrel{\bar{R}}s,

\displaystyle(G*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle\leq X(q)(t)\text{ for all }t\text{ with }s\mathrel{\bar{R}}t

and hence

\displaystyle(P*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle\in U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid t\mathrel{\bar{R}}s\}),

\displaystyle(F*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle\in U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid s\mathrel{\bar{R}}t\}),

\displaystyle(H*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle\in L(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid t\mathrel{\bar{R}}s\}),

\displaystyle(G*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle\in L(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid s\mathrel{\bar{R}}t\})

whence

\displaystyle(\bar{P}*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid t\mathrel{\bar{R}}s\})\leq_{2}(P*X)(q)(s),

\displaystyle(\bar{F}*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle=\Min U(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid s\mathrel{\bar{R}}t\})\leq_{2}(F*X)(q)(s),

\displaystyle(\bar{H}*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}\Max L(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid t\mathrel{\bar{R}}s\})=(\bar{H}*X)(q)(s),

\displaystyle(\bar{G}*X)(q)(s)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}\Max L(\bigcup\{X(q)(t)\mid s\mathrel{\bar{R}}t\})=(\bar{G}*X)(q)(s)

according to Observation
3.2
.
∎

Abstract
. We develop the notion of a Kleinian Sphere Packing, a generalization of “crystallographic” (Apollonian-like) sphere packings defined by Kontorovich-Nakamura [ KN19 ] . Unlike crystallographic packings, Kleinian packings exist in all dimensions, as do “superintegral” such. We extend the Arithmeticity Theorem to Kleinian packings, that is, the superintegral ones come from ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} -arithmetic lattices of simplest type. The same holds for more general objects we call Kleinian Bugs, in which the spheres need not be disjoint but can meet with dihedral angles π / m \pi/m for finitely many m m . We settle two questions from [ KN19 ] : ( i ) (i) that the Arithmeticity Theorem is in general false over number fields, and ( i ​ i ) (ii) that integral packings only arise from non-uniform lattices.

1.2.
Packings over Number Fields

To complete the discussion of Kleinian packings (before we turn to Kleinian “bugs”), we show that the theory breaks down over number fields, answering another question posed in
[
KN19
]
. For a totally real number field
k
with ring of integers
\mathfrak{o}
, a packing is
\mathfrak{o}
-integral (resp.
\mathfrak{o}
-superintegral) if every bend in its packing (resp. superpacking) lies in
\mathfrak{o}
.

Proposition 14
.

The Subarithmeticity Theorem does not hold in number fields.

Indeed, already for the simplest case of the golden ring
k={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{5})
, there exist
\mathfrak{o}
-superintegral crystallographic packings whose supergroups are non-arithmetic. (See
Example
71
.)
But this is not the whole story, as we also have the following.

Theorem 15
.

For any
k
-arithmetic hyperbolic lattice of simplest type
O_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{o})
,
there is a totally real, quadratic extension
k^{\prime}\supset k
, with ring of integers
\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}
, such that

O_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{o})
is commensurable to the supergroup of
an
\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}
-superintegral Kleinian packing.

Note that there is no condition on being non-uniform here, unlike
Theorem
9
. Thus, even the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-anisotropic form
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}-7x_{4}^{2}
has
\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}
-superintegral Kleinian packings, for certain quadratic rings
\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}
.
This leaves open the problem of giving a proper formulation for how to characterize which
\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}
-superintegral packings come from arithmetic groups.

\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}\ :=\ \Gamma_{\mathscr{B}}\cdot\mathscr{B}.

A bug is

The Subarithmeticity
Theorem

applies just as well to bugs, giving the following:

Theorem 26
.

•

If a bug (with no further structure imposed) is superintegral, then its reflection group

is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-subarithmetic.

•

If a bug is Kleinian and integral, then its symmetry group

is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-subarithmetic.

•

If a bug is Kleinian and superintegral, then its supergroup
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-arithmetic.

In the opposite direction, we already have from the Classification

that every non-uniform
{\mathbb{Q}}
-arithmetic lattice (of simplest type) is commensurable to the supergroup of a superintegral Kleinian packing (and hence bug). Similarly, we have from

Remark 27
.

When a Kleinian bug (or just packing) is integral but not superintegral, we have the following curious situation: its symmetry group
\Gamma_{S}
is a subgroup of some integer orthogonal group
O_{{\mathcal{F}}}({\mathbb{Z}})
, but
it is also contained in a (non-arithmetic) lattice, the supergroup
\widetilde{\Gamma}
.

Note further that there are essentially only three ways that a lattice
\widetilde{\Gamma}<\mathrm{Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n+1})
(with

) can be non-arithmetic. By Selberg’s Rigidity
[
Sel60
]
, after conjugation,
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is contained in some

where

is a
number field with a given embedding to

. For
\widetilde{\Gamma}
to be non-arithmetic, either:

(i)

k
is not totally real, or

(ii)

k
is totally real but for at least one non-identity embedding

, the orthogonal group
O_{n+1,1}(\iota(k))
is non-compact (that is, the quadratic from is not hyperbolic), or

(iii)

is totally real and the quadratic form is hyperbolic,
but the entries of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
as elements of

have unbounded denominators.

Lattices satisfying the last condition

are called

by Vinberg.
The only currently known integral but non-superintegral bugs (e.g. the crystallographic packings constructed in
[
KN19
, Lemma 20]
)
all come from this last situation (with

).
It is interesting to investigate whether this is the only non-arithmeticity type possible for integral but not superintegral bugs.
(Note that for
\mathfrak{o}
-superintegrality, the non-arithmeticity in

is of type

Example

.)

Lemma 32

Suppose that

3.1.

Before embarking on the proofs,

For convenience, we work here with the standard hyperbolic quadratic form

To a cooriented round sphere

Here

When

is half the distance from the hyperplane to the origin, and

Rewriting (

The Möbius group

Lemma 50

The map

and

Proof.

The proof is essentially contained in the proof of

to the

We next discuss a relation between the Hausdorff dimension of the limit set, the critical exponent and the bottom of the spectrum of the Laplacian.

For a complete connected Riemannian manifold

\lambda=\inf\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}}

Theorem 73

M_{i}\to M\Rightarrow\lim\inf_{i\to\infty}\lambda(M_{i})\leq\lambda(M).

This is clear using the Rayleigh quotient definition: Every test-function on

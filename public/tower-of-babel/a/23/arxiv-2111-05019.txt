Abstract
. We first define the trace on a domain Ω \Omega which is definable in an o-minimal structure. We then show that every function u ∈ W 1 , p ​ ( Ω ) u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega) vanishing on the boundary in the trace sense satisfies Poincaré inequality. We finally show, given a definable family of domains ( Ω t ) t ∈ ℝ k (\Omega_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}} , that the constant of this inequality remains bounded, if so does the volume of Ω t \Omega_{t} .

1.
Introduction

Poincaré type inequalities are very valuable tools in the theory of PDE as well as in variational or numerical analysis. There are mainly two directions in the contemporary research: characterizing domains on which Poincaré type inequalities holds, see for instance
[
11
]
or
[
8
]
and the literature therein, or focus on the constant and find uniform bounds for certain classes of domains. For the latter issue, parametric version of Poincaré inequalities are very useful
[
3
,
9
,
12
,
13
]
.

The aim of this note is to provide a proof of a parametric version of Poincaré-Friedrichs inequality in the o-minimal setting, see Theorem
3.4
.
Definable sets can be considered as generalizations of semialgebraic or subanalytic sets. The theory of o-minimal structures, which sits at the intersections of model theory, geometry, and analysis, is very adequate to perform analysis on singular sets. Thanks to their "tameness" the o-minimal structures provide a sufficiently large playground for applicable finite dimensional variational and numerical analysis (especially in semialgebraic setting).

This is a part of our project to carry out the theory of Sobolev spaces on definable domains, with possibly singular boundary. In foregoing papers, we proved a version of Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality on subanalytic sets
[
14
]
, studied the trace operator on
W^{1,p}(M)
in the case where
p
is large and
M
is a bounded subanalytic submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
15
]
, and showed density of compactly supported functions in the kernel of the trace operator for such Sobolev spaces. Such a manifold
M
may of course admit singularities in its closure which are not metrically conical. Some other interesting inequalities on singular subanalytic domains were investigated in
[
1
,
2
,
6
]
. The advantage of working with o-minimal structures is that no extra ad hoc assumption on the metric geometry of the domains or on their boundaries is needed.

Some generalizations of Poincaré-Friedrichs inequality were given for example in
[
19
,
10
]
. Although the boundary of a domain which is definable in an o-minimal structure may admit singularities,
it must be an at least
\mathscr{C}^{1}
manifold almost everywhere. We rely on it to define a trace operator which is continuous in the
L^{p}_{loc}
topology on the complement of a negligible subset of the boundary of the domain. This enables us to show a version of Poincaré-Friedrichs inequality which is valid on every definable domain
\Omega
which is bounded in one direction for every element
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
that vanishes on the boundary in the trace sense.

It is a very interesting problem to study the extent to which the best constant of Poincaré inequality is related to the geometry of the domain, especially when singularities arise in its boundary. We show that there exists a uniform constant for every definable family of domains (i.e., definable with respect to parameters) of bounded volume.

We briefly recall that an
o-minimal structure
expanding the real field
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot)
is the data for every
n
of a Boolean algebra

\mathcal{S}_{n}
of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing all the algebraic subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and satisfying
the following axioms:

(1)

If
A\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
,
B\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
, then
A\times B\in\mathcal{S}_{m+n}
;

(2)

If

is the natural projection and
A\in\mathcal{S}_{n+1}
, then
\pi(A)\in\mathcal{S}_{n}

(3)

\mathcal{S}_{1}
is nothing else but all the finite unions of points and intervals.

The elements of

are called the

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Given a function

will stand for the graph of

. If

(\xi,\xi^{\prime}):=\{(x,y)\in A\times\mathbb{R}:\xi(x)<y<\xi^{\prime}(x)\}.

From now, we fix an o-minimal structure.

Let us recall the inductive definition of cells of

. Every subset of

is a cell of

such that one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

with

.

(2)

C=(\xi,\xi^{\prime})
, where
\xi
is either equal to
-\infty
or a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
definable function on

, and
\xi^{\prime}
is either equal

or a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
definable function on

.

A cell decomposition of

is a finite partition of

5

2.
Trace operator

In this section we define a trace operator on the boundary of a given open definable set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. As definable sets are piecewise
\mathscr{C}^{1}
submanifolds of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, this actually follows from the classical theory.

For this purpose, we shall make use of the set
W^{1,p}_{loc}(\Omega\cup\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega)
, which is defined as the set constituted by all the distributions
u
on
\Omega
such that every
x\in\Omega\cup\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega
has a neighborhood
U
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the restriction
u_{|U\cap\Omega}
belongs to
W^{1,p}(U\cap\Omega)
. We then say that
(u_{i})
converges to
u\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(\Omega\cup\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega)
if every
x\in\Omega\cup\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega
has a neighborhood
U
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the restriction
u_{i|U\cap\Omega}
converges to
u_{|U\cap\Omega}
in
W^{1,p}(U\cap\Omega)
. This defines the
W^{1,p}_{loc}
-topology.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
\Omega
is
connected at
x\in\partial\Omega
if
{\bf B}(x,\varepsilon)\cap\Omega
is connected for all
\varepsilon>0
small enough.
We say that
\Omega
is
normal
if it is connected at each
x\in\partial\Omega
.

Observation 2.2
.

If
\Omega
is normal then

\mathscr{C}^{1}(\Omega\cup\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega)
is dense in
W^{1,p}_{loc}(\Omega\cup\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega)
.

Proof.

Up to a partition of unity and a coordinate system of
\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega
, we are reduced to finding a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
approximation of
a given function
u\in W^{1,p}(H_{+})
, where
H_{+}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>0\}
is the positive half-space, for which the result is well-known
[
4
, Section III.2.5]
.∎

Observation 2.3
.

Let
(f_{i})\subset\mathscr{C}^{1}(\Omega\cup\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega)
be a sequence.

(i)

If
(f_{i})
is converging in the
W^{1,p}_{loc}
-topology, then
({f_{i}}_{|\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega})
is also
L^{p}_{loc}
-converging.

(ii)

If
(f_{i})
converges to
0
in the
W^{1,p}_{loc}
-topology, then
{f_{i}}_{|\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega}\overset{L^{p}_{loc}}{\longrightarrow}0
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega
. There are a relatively compact neighborhood
U
of
x
in
\Omega\cup\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega
and a constant
C>0
such that for all
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\Omega\cup\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega)
the following inequality holds (for instance by
[
4
, Proposition III.2.18]
)

(2.1)

||\varphi||_{L^{p}(U\cap\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{n-1})}\leq C||\varphi||^{1-1/p}_{L^{p}(U\cap\Omega)}\;||\varphi||^{1/p}_{W^{1,p}(U\cap\Omega)}.

Since the set
U
is relatively compact in
{\Omega}\cup\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega
, we know that if
(f_{i})
is convergent in
W^{1,p}_{loc}(\Omega\cup\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega)
then
(f_{i|U\cap\Omega})
is convergent in
W^{1,p}(U\cap\Omega)
.
We conclude from (
2.1
) that the sequence
({f_{i}}_{|U\cap\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega})
is Cauchy in
L^{p}(U\cap\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{n-1})
,
which establishes
(i)
. This also yields that if
(f_{i})
converges to
0
in the
W^{1,p}_{loc}
-topology then
{f_{i}}_{|\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega}\overset{L^{p}_{loc}}{\longrightarrow}0
.
∎

To define our trace operator in the case where
\Omega
is normal
for
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\Omega\cup\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega)
, let now
\mathbf{tr}(\varphi):=\varphi_{|\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega}
. By Observations
2.2
and
2.3
, this mapping extends to a mapping

\mathbf{tr}_{\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega}:{W}^{1,p}(\Omega)\to L^{p}_{loc}(\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}),

which is continuous in the
W^{1,p}_{loc}(\Omega\cup\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega)
-topology.

Let us now define the trace in the case where
\Omega
is not necessarily normal. There is a finite partition
\mathcal{P}
of
\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega
into definable sets such that every
(n-1)
-dimensional element
S
of
\mathcal{P}
is open in
\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega
and the number of connected components of
{\bf B}(x,\varepsilon)\cap\Omega
(for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small) is the same (
1
or
2
) for all
x\in S
. For instance, such a partition is given by the images of the open simplices of a
\mathscr{C}^{0}
definable triangulation
[
5
]
.
We start by defining
\mathbf{tr}_{S}
, where
S
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional element of
\mathcal{P}
.

First case:

\Omega
is connected at the points of
S
.
In this case, there is a neighborhood
V
of
S
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
V\cap\Omega
is normal and we can define:

\mathbf{tr}_{S}:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to L^{p}_{loc}(S,\mathcal{H}^{n-1})^{2},\quad u\mapsto(\mathbf{tr}_{S}\,u_{|V\cap\Omega},0).

Here, we add the zero function as second component since there is only one connected component. There will be a possibly nonzero component in the second case.

Second case:

\Omega
fails to be connected at the points of
S
.
Let
V
be a neighborhood of
S
such that
V\cap\partial\Omega=S
. If
V
is sufficiently small, the set
V\cap\Omega
has two connected components
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
such that
\Omega_{1}\cup S
and
\Omega_{2}\cup S
are both
\mathscr{C}^{1}
manifolds with boundary.

Let us denote by
\mathbf{tr}^{1}_{S}:W^{1,p}(\Omega_{1})\to L^{p}_{loc}(S,\mathcal{H}^{n-1})
and
\mathbf{tr}_{S}^{2}:W^{1,p}(\Omega_{2})\to L^{p}_{loc}(S,\mathcal{H}^{n-1})
the respective trace operators resulting from the above Observations. We then define

\mathbf{tr}_{S}:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to L^{p}_{loc}(S,\mathcal{H}^{n-1})^{2},\quad u\mapsto(\mathbf{tr}^{1}_{S}\,u_{|\Omega_{1}},\mathbf{tr}^{2}_{S}\,u_{|\Omega_{2}}).

Of course, this mapping depends on the way we have enumerated the connected components of
V\cap\Omega
, but, up to a possible permutation of the components, it is independent of any choice. In particular, the kernel of this mapping is independent of our choices.

Finally, we define
\mathbf{tr}\,u
by
\mathbf{tr}\,u(x)=\mathbf{tr}_{S}u(x)
, for
x\in S
, for each
S\in\mathcal{P}
such that
\dim S=n-1
. Since it is induced by the respective trace operators of the connected components of the germ of
\Omega
at every point of
x\in\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega
, it is an
L^{p}_{loc}
mapping on this set.

We let

W^{1,p}(\Omega,\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega):=\ker\mathbf{tr}.

Remark 2.4
.

It is easy to produce examples of definable domains
\Omega
admitting a function
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
\mathbf{tr}\,u
is not
L^{p}
on the boundary. Let for
k>2
,

\Omega_{k}:=\{(x,y)\in(0,1)^{2}:y<x^{k}\},

and let
u(x,y):=\frac{1}{x}.

Clearly,

u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{k})
, for
p\in[1,\frac{k}{2}]
, while
\mathbf{tr}\,u
is not
L^{p}
on
\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega_{k}
. The results of
[
15
]
however yield that there is
p_{0}
such that for
p\geq p_{0}
and
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
\mathbf{tr}\,u
is always
L^{p}
, if
\Omega
is a subanalytic bounded domain (the proof actually applies to any bounded domain which is definable in a polynomially bounded o-minimal structure expanding the real field). The real number
p_{0}
depends on the Lipschitz geometry of
\partial\Omega
.

3.
Uniform bound for the Poincaré constants of definable families

We say that
(A_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}
is a
definable family
if the set

A:=\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}A_{t}\times\{t\}

is a definable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
We will sometimes regard a definable subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
as a definable family,
setting

A_{t}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:(x,t)\in A\}.

Given two definable families
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we say that
F_{t}:A_{t}\to B_{t}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, is a
definable family of mappings
if the family of the graphs
(\Gamma_{F_{t}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}
, is a definable family of sets of
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
.

A definable family of mappings
F_{t}:A_{t}\to B_{t}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
is
uniformly Lipschitz (resp. bi-Lipschitz)
if there exists a constant
L
such that

F_{t}
is
L
-Lipschitz
1
1

1

i.e.
|F_{t}(x)-F_{t}(y)|\leq L|x-y|
for all
x,y\in A_{t}
(resp.
F_{t}
is
L
-bi-Lipschitz) for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Here, we wish to emphasize that a definable family of sets (resp. mappings) is not only a family of definable sets (resp. mappings): all the fibers must glue together into a definable set (resp. mapping). This confers to such families many uniform finiteness properties that are essential for our purpose (see
[
5
,
18
]
).

Given
X\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
, we denote by
(X)_{reg}
the set of the points
x\in X
at which
X
is a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(of any dimension) and by
T_{x}(X)_{reg}
the tangent space to
(X)_{reg}
at the point
x
.
We say that
\lambda\in{\bf S}^{n-1}
is
regular for
X
if there exists
\alpha>0
such that for all
x\in(X)_{reg}
:

(3.2)

dist(\lambda,T_{x}(X)_{reg})\geq\alpha.

We say that
\lambda
is
regular for a family
(A_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}
, if there is
\alpha>0
such that (
3.2
) holds for
X=A_{t}
, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and all
x\in(A_{t})_{reg}
.

If
\lambda\in{\bf S}^{n-1}
is regular for
A\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n+k}
, it is of course regular for
A_{t}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, but it is indeed even stronger since
the angle between the vector
\lambda
and the tangent spaces to the fibers must then be bounded below away from zero by a positive constant
independent of the parameter
t
.

Regular vectors do not always exist, even if the considered set has empty interior, as it is shown by the simple example of a circle. Nevertheless, when the considered sets have empty interior, up to a definable family of bi-Lipschitz maps, we can find such a vector:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(A_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}
be a definable family of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
A_{t}
has empty interior for each
t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. There exists a uniformly bi-Lipschitz definable family of homeomorphisms
h_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the vector
e_{n}
is regular for the family
(h_{t}(A_{t}))_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to apply Theorem
3.1.3
of
[
16
]
to the generic fibers of the family
A
(see
[
5
, section 5]
for the definition of the generic fibers) and the compactness of the Stone space of the Boolean algebra of the definable sets. A more elementary explicit proof of this theorem, avoiding the abstract material of the Stone space, is provided in
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
(see also
[
18
, Theorem 3.1.2]
for more details). ∎

For
\lambda\in{\bf S}^{n-1}
, the
thickness of a set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the direction
\lambda
is defined as

|\Omega|_{\lambda}:=\sup\limits_{x\in\Omega}\;\sup\{s\geq 0\,:\,x+s\lambda\in\Omega\}.

We say that
\Omega
is
bounded in the direction

\lambda
if
|\Omega|_{\lambda}<+\infty
.

We need the following Poincaré type inequality on definable domains.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open definable subset bounded in the direction
\lambda\in{\bf S}^{n-1}
and let
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega)
,
p\in[1,\infty)
.
We have

(3.3)

||u||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq 2^{1/p}\;|\Omega|_{\lambda}||\partial u||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Changing the coordinate system if necessary, we can assume that
\lambda=e_{n}
.
Take a cell decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
compatible with
\Omega
and
\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega
, and let
E
be an open cell of this decomposition which is included in
\Omega
. This cell is of the form
\{(\tilde{x},x_{n}),\,\tilde{x}\in D,\,\xi_{1}(\tilde{x})<x_{n}<\xi_{2}(\tilde{x})\}
, where
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
are
\mathscr{C}^{1}
definable functions on an open cell
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
such that
\xi_{1}<\xi_{2}
.
If the graph
\Gamma_{\xi_{1}}
(resp.
\Gamma_{\xi_{2}}
) is not in
\partial\Omega
then the cell which lies below (resp. above)
E
is also included in
\Omega
. As we can make a bigger cell by gluing these cells to
E
, we may assume that
\Gamma_{\xi_{1}}
and
\Gamma_{\xi_{2}}
are included in
\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega
.

Define now for
\varepsilon>0

D_{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in D\;:\;dist(x,\partial D)>\varepsilon\},

F_{\varepsilon}:=\pi^{-1}(D_{\varepsilon})\cap(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),

where
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is the projection onto the
(n-1)
first coordinates.
Since for each
\varepsilon>0
the set
\overline{F_{\varepsilon}}
is a compact subset of the manifold with boundary
E\cup\Gamma_{\xi_{1}}\cup\Gamma_{\xi_{2}}
and the function
u
has trace zero on the boundary, we can approximate
u
by smooth functions, i.e. for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a sequence
(u_{i})\subset\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{0}(E)
satisfying

(3.4)

||u-u_{i}||_{W^{1,p}(F_{\varepsilon})}\to 0.

To construct such a sequence, we take local
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{0}
approximations at the points of
\overline{F_{\varepsilon}}
(using for instance the mollyfying operators constructed in
[
4
, III.2.2]
) that we then glue together by means of a partition of unity subordinated to a finite covering of
\overline{F_{\varepsilon}}
.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
and set for simplicity
N:=|\Omega|_{e_{n}}
. By the Main Calculus Theorem, we have for
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in E
:

(3.5)

u_{i}(x)=\int_{\xi_{2}(\tilde{x})-x_{n}}^{0}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{n}}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}+s)ds,

and hence

(3.6)

|u_{i}(x)|\leq\int_{0}^{N}\left|\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{n}}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}+s)\right|ds.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\tilde{x}\in D

we put

(3.7)

v_{i,t}(\tilde{x},s):=\begin{cases}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{n}}(\tilde{x},s+t),&(\tilde{x},s+t)\in F_{\varepsilon}\\
0,&(\tilde{x},s+t)\notin F_{\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

Then, by Minkowski’s inequality

(3.8)

||u_{i}||_{L^{p}(F_{\varepsilon})}=\left|\left|\int_{0}^{N}\left|v_{i,s}(\tilde{x},x_{n})\right|ds\right|\right|_{L^{p}(F_{\varepsilon})}\leq\int_{0}^{N}\left|\left|v_{i,s}\right|\right|_{L^{p}(F_{\varepsilon})}ds.

By Hölder’s inequality we get

(3.9)

||u_{i}||_{L^{p}(F_{\varepsilon})}\leq(N)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left(\int_{0}^{N}\left|\left|v_{i,s}\right|\right|_{L^{p}(F_{\varepsilon})}^{p}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
,
which reads, since
v_{i,s}(x_{n})=v_{i,x_{n}}(s)
,

||u_{i}||_{L^{p}(F_{\varepsilon})}\leq(N)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left(\int_{0}^{N}\left(\int_{-N}^{N}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\left|v_{i,x_{n}}(\tilde{x},s)\right|^{p}d\tilde{x}\ dx_{n}\right)ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

=(N)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left(\int_{-N}^{N}\left(\int_{0}^{N}\int_{D_{\varepsilon}}\left|v_{i,x_{n}}(\tilde{x},s)\right|^{p}d\tilde{x}\ ds\right)dx_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\leq(N)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left(\int_{-N}^{N}\left|\left|v_{i,x_{n}}\right|\right|^{p}_{L^{p}(\pi^{-1}(D_{\varepsilon}))}dx_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}{=}2^{1/p}N\left|\left|\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{n}}\right|\right|_{L^{p}(F_{\varepsilon})},

since for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have
||v_{i,t}||_{L^{p}(\pi^{-1}(D_{\varepsilon}))}\overset{(\ref{eq_vs})}{=}||\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{n}}||_{L^{p}(F_{\varepsilon})}
.
Passing to the limit as
i\to\infty
we get, thanks to (
3.4
), that the above inequality holds for
u
as well.
Making
\varepsilon\to 0
we finally obtain

||u||_{L^{p}(E)}\leq 2^{1/p}N||\partial u||_{L^{p}(E)},

for every cell
E\subset\Omega
.
∎

Our uniform Poincaré inequality for definable families will require the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a definable family of open sets. There exist
K>0
and a uniformly bi-Lipschitz definable family of homeomorphisms
h_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
:

(3.10)

|h_{t}(\Omega_{t})|_{e_{n}}\leq K\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Omega_{t})^{1/n}.

Proof.

It suffices to focus on the elements
t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
for which
\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Omega_{t})
is finite since (
3.10
) is trivial for the other parameters. As
\partial\Omega_{t}
is a definable family of sets of empty interiors, by Theorem
3.1
, there is a uniformly bi-Lipschitz definable family of homeomorphisms
h_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
e_{n}
is regular for the family
(\partial\Omega_{t}^{\prime})_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}
, where
\Omega^{\prime}_{t}:=h_{t}(\Omega_{t})
, which entails that
\partial\Omega^{\prime}_{t}
is comprised in the union of the respective graphs of some uniformly Lipschitz definable families of functions
\xi_{i,t}:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\dots,m
(see for instance
[
17
, Proposition
3.6
]
or
[
18
, Chap.
3
]
). Using the
\min
operator, we can transform these families into families that satisfy
\xi_{i,t}\leq\xi_{i+1,t}
, for all
i<m
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
(see
[
17
, Proposition
3.14
]
).

Fix
t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Since
\partial\Omega_{t}^{\prime}
is included in the graphs of the functions
\xi_{i,t}
, the set
\Omega_{t}^{\prime}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma_{\xi_{i,t}}
is the union of some connected components (which are finitely many) of the sets
(\xi_{i,t},\xi_{i+1,t})

0\leq i\leq m
, with
\xi_{0}\equiv-\infty
and
\xi_{m+1}\equiv+\infty
. As
\Omega^{\prime}_{t}
has finite volume, we therefore see that it actually must be included in
(\xi_{1,t},\xi_{m,t})
.

Let us thus fix a connected component
E
of
(\xi_{j,t},\xi_{j+1,t})\cap\Omega_{t}^{\prime}
, for some
0<j<m
, and observe that, since the number of such connected components is bounded independently of
t
, it is enough to prove that we have

|E|_{e_{n}}\leq K\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Omega_{t}^{\prime})^{1/n},

for some constant
K
independent of
t
.

We are going to show that this inequality is satisfied by any
K>4L^{1-1/n}
, where
L
is the Lipschitz constant of the function
(\xi_{j,t}-\xi_{j+1,t})
.
Fix for this purpose a point
a
in
\pi(E)
, where
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is the projection onto the
(n-1)
first coordinates, and
suppose by contradiction that

\xi_{j+1,t}(a)-\xi_{j,t}(a)>K\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Omega_{t}^{\prime})^{1/n}.

Then, for any
x\in Z:={\bf B}(a,\frac{K\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Omega_{t}^{\prime})^{1/n}}{2L})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
we have

\xi_{j+1,t}(x)-\xi_{j,t}(x)>\frac{K}{2}\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Omega_{t}^{\prime})^{1/n},

and hence, integrating over
Z
, we get (since
(\xi_{j,t|Z},\xi_{j+1,t|Z})\subset E\subset\Omega^{\prime}_{t}
)

\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Omega_{t}^{\prime})\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left({\bf B}(a,\frac{K}{2L}\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Omega^{\prime}_{t})^{1/n})\right)\cdot\frac{K}{2}\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Omega_{t}^{\prime})^{1/n}=\frac{K^{n}\,b_{n}}{2^{n}L^{n-1}}\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Omega_{t}^{\prime}),

where
b_{n}:=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}({\bf B}(0_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}},1))
.
If
K>4L^{1-1/n}
, we therefore have a contradiction (as
b_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2^{n}}
).
∎

We conclude, directly from the above lemma and Theorem
3.2
:

Theorem 3.4
.

For every definable family
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
of open sets of finite volume there is a constant
C

such that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and all
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{t},\partial^{1}_{reg}\Omega_{t})
,
p\in[1,\infty)
, we have:

(3.11)

||u||_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}\leq C\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Omega_{t})^{1/n}||\partial u||_{L^{p}(\Omega_{t})}.

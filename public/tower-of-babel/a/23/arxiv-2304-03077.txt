Flach system of Jacquet–Langlands type and quaternionic period
Haining Wang

Abstract
. In this article, we study the adjoint Selmer group of a modular form that admits a Jacquet–Langlands transfer to a modular form on a definite quaternion algebra. We show that this Selmer group has length given by the valuation of the Petersson norm of the quaternionic modular form. Our proof is based on an Euler system argument first used by Flach in his study for the adjoint Selmer group of an elliptic curve by producing elements in the motivic cohomology of the product of two Shimura curves. However our construction does not involve Siegel modular units which make it more adaptable to study adjoint motives coming from products of higher dimensional Shimura varieties.

1.
Introduction

In a seminal work of Flach
[
Flach1
]
, the author proves the finiteness of the adjoint Selmer group of an elliptic curve using an Euler system type argument following Kolyvagin. The key point is to construct suitable elements in the motivic cohomology of product of two modular curves with tight control of the local ramification behaviour when mapped to the Galois cohomology via certain Abel–Jacobi map. In fact, in
[
Flach1
]
, such element consists of a Hecke correspondence at a suitable auxiliary prime and a well-chosen Siegel modular unit. The local ramification behaviour of this element at the auxiliary prime reflects geometrically the Eichler–Shimura congruence relation. This work has been extended in various directions to the adjoint Selmer group of modular forms in
[
Flach2
]
and
[
Weston1
]
following similar strategies in Flach’s original work. Recently, Flach’s construction has been vastly extended to many automorphic motives and has been used to study Iwasawa theoretic question associated to these automorphic motives. We will refer to
[
BDR1
]
,
[
BDR2
]
,
[
LZ
]
,
[
LLZ
]
,
[
LSZ
]
for some of these examples.

In
[
Flach1
]
, the natural bound comes out of his Euler system argument is the degree of the modular parametrization for the elliptic curve. It is known also that the degree of modular parametrization is closely related to the congruence module of the modular form associated to the elliptic curve, see
[
ARS
]
and
[
Zag
]
. In this article, we will revisit the construction of
[
Flach1
]
in the setting when the modular form admits Jacquet–Langlands transfer to a modular form on a definite quaternion algebra. In this case, we can incorporate techniques from arithmetic level raising to bound the Selmer group which manifest the Jacquet–Langlands correspondence at a geometric level. In particular, Siegel modular unit does not appear in our construction which makes it more adaptable to the setting of product of higher dimensional Shimura varieties and we refer to this Flach type system as a Flach system of Jacquet–Langlands type to distinguish it from the original Flach system which is of Eichler–Shimura type under the terminology of
[
Weston1
]
. It turns out that in our setting the natural bound for the adjoint Selmer group is given by the Petersson norm of the quaternionic modular form obtained by a normalized Jacquet–Langlands transfer. We will also refer to this Petersson norm as the quaternionic period of the modular form and hence explains the title of this article. This bound is also reasonable as it is known this quaternionic period also measures congruences between modular forms, see in particular
[
PW11
]
,
[
CH1
]
and
[
KO
]
for such connections.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose

is a newform in
\mathrm{S}^{\mathrm{new}}_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathrm{N}))
which admits a normailized Jacquet–Langlands transfer to a quaternionic modular form
f^{\dagger}
in
\mathrm{S}^{\overline{\mathrm{B}}}(\mathrm{N}^{+})
. Let
\eta=\varpi^{\nu}
and
\nu=\mathrm{ord}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{P}(f^{\dagger}))
for the quaternionic period
\mathcal{P}(f^{\dagger})
of

. We assume that

(1)

the residual Galois representation
\overline{\rho}
satisfies Assumption
1
;

(2)

the Galois cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q}(\mathrm{M}_{n})/\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{M}_{n})=0

\mathbf{Q}(\mathrm{M}_{n})

Then the Selmer group
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{Ad}^{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\rho}))
is annihilated by
\eta
and in fact has length equal to
\nu=\mathrm{ord}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{P}(f^{\dagger}))
.

We remark that the first assumption in the statement of the theorem is mainly used to prove results concerning arithmetic level raising theorems and certain
\mathrm{R}=\mathrm{T}
theorem which we think can be weakened. The second assumption is inherited from the Euler system argument used in
[

]
and
[
Weston1
]
and this assumption is verified when the Galois representation

is surjective onto its image.

1.2.
Strategy of our proof

Next we describe the strategy for proving this theorem. To bound the Selmer group of
\mathrm{Ad}^{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\rho})
, we will bound the Selmer group of
\mathrm{M}_{n}
for each
n\geq 1
. We are naturally led to construct elements in the Galois cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{N}_{n})
where
\mathrm{N}_{n}=\Sym^{2}\mathrm{T}_{n}
which is the Cartier dual of
\mathrm{M}_{n}
.

In light of the arithmetic level raising theorems, we are led to consider the Shimura curve
\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})
where
\mathrm{B}=\mathrm{B}_{p\mathrm{N}^{-}}
is the indefinite quaternion algebra of discriminant
p\mathrm{N}^{-}
and where
p
is the so-called
n
-admissible prime for
f
. These primes are level raising primes for
f
such that the Galois representation
\mathrm{T}_{n}
naturally appear in the first cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbf{Q}},\mathcal{O}_{n}(1))
of
\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})
. Let
\mathbb{T}^{[p]}=\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{N}^{+},p\mathrm{N}^{-}}
be the
\lambda
-adic Hecke algebra corresponding to the cusp forms of level
\mathrm{N}p=\mathrm{N}^{+}\mathrm{N}^{-}p
, which are new at primes dividing
\mathrm{N}^{-}p
. Since
p
is
n
-admissible and hence level raising for
f
, the morphism
\phi_{f}
modulo
\lambda
gives rise to a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}
of
\mathbb{T}^{[p]}
. Then
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbf{Q}},\mathcal{O}_{n}(1))_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}
is in fact isomorphic to some copies of
\mathrm{T}_{n}
. Therefore
\mathrm{N}_{n}
will appear naturally in

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbf{Q}},\mathcal{O}_{n}(1))^{\otimes 2}_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}

and we need to construct elements in

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbf{Q}},\mathcal{O}_{n}(1))^{\otimes 2}_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}).

For this purpose, we consider the motivic cohomology group

\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})^{2},\mathbf{Z}(2))

for the surface
\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})^{2}
whose elements consist of pairs
(\mathrm{Z},f)
where
\mathrm{Z}
is a curve on
\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})^{2}
and
f
is rational function on
\mathrm{Z}
whose Weil divisor is trivial.

Let
\theta:\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\rightarrow\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})^{2}
be the diagonal embedding, then we will define the
Flach element

\Theta^{[p]}(\mathrm{B})
to be the class in
\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})^{2},\mathbf{Z}(2))
represented by the pair

\Theta^{[p]}(\mathrm{B}):=(\theta_{\ast}\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B}),p).

There is an Abel–Jacobi map

\mathrm{AJ}_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}:\mathrm{H}^{3}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})^{2},\mathbf{Z}(2))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbf{Q}},\mathcal{O}(1))^{\otimes 2}_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}})

induced by the Chern character map from the coniveau spectral sequence in
K
-theory to that in étale cohomology.
We let

\kappa^{[p]}:=\mathrm{AJ}_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}(\Theta^{[p]}(\mathrm{B}))\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbf{Q}},\mathcal{O}(1))^{\otimes 2}_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}})

and we will refer to this element as the
Flach class
for
\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})^{2}
.
Composing the map
\mathrm{AJ}_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}}
with the natural projection map from
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{X}(\mathrm{B})\otimes\overline{\mathbf{Q}},\mathcal{O}(1))^{\otimes 2}_{\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}})
to

\kappa^{[p]}_{n}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{N}_{n})

To apply the Euler system argument, we need to analyze the restriction of the element

in the local Galois cohomology

v

\mathbf{Q}
. Under our assumption, the most interesting place is

be the normalized Jacquet–Langlands transfer of

of
\mathcal{O}
-valued functions on the Shimura set

. There is a natural pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\Gamma(\mathrm{Z}(\overline{\mathrm{B}}),\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}\times\Gamma(\mathrm{Z}(\overline{\mathrm{B}}),\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}

1

(1)

Then we have an isomorphism

Let

(\partial^{(i)}_{p}(\kappa^{[p]}),f^{\dagger}\otimes f^{\dagger})=\mathcal{P}(f^{\dagger}).

\mathrm{S}_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(\mathrm{N}))
which are new at primes dividing
\mathrm{N}^{-}
. On the other hand, as a consequence of the freeness of
\Gamma(\mathrm{Z}(\overline{\mathrm{B}}),\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}
over a suitable localized Hecke algebra. The congruence number

has length equal to

.

From the above sketch, it follows that the elements

with

varying and

being an

-admissible prime form a partial geometric Euler system of depth

in the sense of Mazur–Weston

. It is also clear that arithmetic level raising and hence Jacquet–Langlands correspondence plays a prominent role in the construction and therefore we say the elements

form a Flach system of Jacquet–Langlands type. In a very analogues setting, we will show that such Flach system exists for certain quaternionic Hilbert modular surfaces
[

]
. In these two cases, the cycles are both given by Shimura curves which admit Drinfeld uniformization at an auxiliary prime and this makes the proof much easier as the rational function cuts out the entire special fiber which agree with the supersingular locus of the Shimura curve at this auxiliary prime. However we believe this is not a serious constraint and that Flach system of Jacquet–Langlands type should exist for adjoint motives appearing in the cohomology of product of Shimura varieties whenever the principle of Jacquet–Langlands transfer can be realized geometrically. For example, we will treat the case of product of unitary Shimura varieties studied in
[

]
and the case of product of quaternionic unitary Shimura varieties
[

]
studied by the author himself in a future work.

Remark 3.3

This element is the analogue of the Flach element used in

3.4.

Recall the setting we are in. We have a modular form

n
-admissible prime for

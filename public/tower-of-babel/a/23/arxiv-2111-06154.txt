On a two-species cross attraction system in higher dimensions
Eric A. Carlen, Suleyman Ulusoy

Abstract
. We consider a degenerate chemotaxis model with two-species and two-stimuli in dimension d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 . Under the hypothesis of integrable initial data with finite second moment and energy, we show local-in-time existence for any mass of free-energy solutions, namely weak solutions with some free energy estimates. We exhibit that the qualitative behavior of solutions is decided by a set of critical values: there is a critical value of a parameter pair in the system of equations for which there is a global-in-time energy solution and there exist blowing-up free-energy solutions under a criticality condition is violated for the parameter pair.

1.
Introduction

Inspired by
[
7
]
(see also
[
9
,
12
]
), for modeling the interaction and motion of two cell populations in breast cancer cell invasion models in
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d},\,d\geq 3
we propose the following chemotaxis kind of system with two chemicals and a nonlinear diffusion term:

(1.1)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u&=\alpha_{1}\left(\frac{d-2}{d}\right)||u||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{4/(d+2)}\Delta(u^{\frac{2d}{d+2}})-\nabla\cdot\left(u\nabla v\right),\quad x\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d},t>0,\\
-\Delta v&=w,\quad x\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d},t>0,\\
\partial_{t}w&=\alpha_{2}\left(\frac{d-2}{d}\right)||w||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{4/(d+2)}\Delta(w^{\frac{2d}{d+2}})-\nabla\cdot\left(w\nabla z\right),\quad x\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d},t>0,\\
-\Delta z&=u,\quad x\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d},t>0,\end{split}

with initial data satisfying

(1.2)

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x);\quad w(x,0)=w_{0}(x),\,x\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}.

In (
1.1
)
u(x,t)
and
w(x,t)
denote the macrophages and the tumor cells;
v(x,t)
and
z(x,t)
denote the concentration of the chemicals produced by
w(x,t)
and
u(x,t)
, respectively and
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(0,1]
. For simplicity the initial data are assumed to satisfy

(1.3)

u_{0}\in L^{1}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d};(1+|x|^{2})dx)\cap L^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}),\,\nabla u_{0}^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\in L^{2}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}),\,\,u_{0}\geq 0,

(1.4)

w_{0}\in L^{1}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d};(1+|x|^{2})dx)\cap L^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}),\,\nabla w_{0}^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\in L^{2}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}),\,\,w_{0}\geq 0.

We note that
v(x,t)
and
z(x,t)
are given by

(1.5)

v(x,t)=\mathcal{K}\ast w=c_{d}\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{w(y,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\,dy,

(1.6)

z(x,t)=\mathcal{K}\ast u=c_{d}\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{u(y,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\,dy,

with
\mathcal{K}(x)=\frac{c_{d}}{|x|^{d-2}}
and
c_{d}
is the surface area of the sphere
S^{d-1}
in
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
. We can rewrite the system (
1.1
) as follows:

(1.7)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u&=\alpha_{1}\left(\frac{d-2}{d}\right)||u||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\Delta(u^{\frac{2d}{d+2}})-\nabla\cdot\left(u\nabla\mathcal{K}\ast w\right),\\
\partial_{t}w&=\alpha_{2}\left(\frac{d-2}{d}\right)||w||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\Delta(w^{\frac{2d}{d+2}})-\nabla\cdot\left(w\nabla\mathcal{K}\ast u\right),\end{split}

with initial data (
1.2
). Note that mass is conserved for (
1.1
)(or (
1.7
)):

(1.8)

M_{1}:=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u(x,t)\,dx=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{0}(x)\,dx;\quad M_{2}:=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w(x,t)\,dx=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w_{0}(x)\,dx.

There is a free energy associated with equation (
1.1
):

(1.9)

E_{\alpha}[u(t),w(t)]:=\alpha_{1}||u||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{2}+\alpha_{2}||w||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{2}-c_{d}H[u,w],

where

(1.10)

H[u,w]:=\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{u(x,t)w(y,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\,dx\,dy.

The energy functional can be rewritten as

(1.11)

E_{\alpha}[u(t),w(t)]:=(\sqrt{\alpha_{1}}||u||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}-\sqrt{\alpha_{2}}||w||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}})^{2}+\left(2\sqrt{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}||u||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}||w||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}-c_{d}H[u,w]\right)\ .

By the HLS inequality, (see (
2.3
) below), the second term is positive for all
u,w
in case

(1.12)

\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}}{c_{d}}\geq C_{HLS}\ ,

the precise value of which was determined by Lieb for the exponents considered here. Suppose however, that (
1.12
) is violated. Let

\delta:=C_{HLS}-\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}}{c_{d}}\ .

Then if we choose
u
and
w
to be HLS optimizers such that

\sqrt{\alpha_{1}}||u||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}=\sqrt{\alpha_{2}}||w||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\ ,

E_{\alpha}[u(t),w(t)]=-\delta||u||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}||w||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\ .

Thus, (
1.12
) is the necessary and sufficient condition for non-negativity of
E_{\alpha}[u(t),w(t)]
.

The connection between the free energy (
1.9
) and the equation (
1.1
) is that the latter can be written as a gradient flow with respect to Wasserstein metric, and then formally one has the dissipation of this free energy

(1.13)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}E_{\alpha}[u(t),w(t)]&=-\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u\left|\nabla\left(\alpha_{1}2||u||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{\frac{4}{d+2}}u^{\frac{d-2}{d+2}}\right)-\nabla v\right|^{2}\,dx\\
&\quad-\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w\left|\nabla\left(\alpha_{2}2||w||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{\frac{4}{d+2}}w^{\frac{d-2}{d+2}}\right)-\nabla z\right|^{2}\,dx.\end{split}

We now define the weak and free energy solutions for (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
T>0
. Suppose
(u_{0},w_{0})
satisfies (
1.3
)-(
1.4
). Then
(u,w)
of nonnegative functions defined in
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times(0,T)
is called a weak solution if

(i)

(u,w)\in\left(C([0,T);L^{1}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}))\cap L^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times(0,T))\right)^{2}
and

\left(u^{\frac{2d}{d+2}},w^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\right)\in\left(L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}))\right)^{2}
.

(ii)

(u,w)
satisfies

(1.14)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u\phi_{1t}\,dx\,dt+\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{0}(x)\phi_{1}(x,0)\,dx\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\left(\alpha_{1}2||u||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{\frac{4}{d+2}}\nabla(u^{\frac{2d}{d+2}})-u\nabla v\right)\cdot\nabla\phi_{1}\,dx\,dt.\\
&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w\phi_{2t}\,dx\,dt+\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w_{0}(x)\phi_{2}(x,0)\,dx\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\left(\alpha_{2}2||w||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{\frac{4}{d+2}}\nabla(w^{\frac{2d}{d+2}})-w\nabla z\right)\cdot\nabla\phi_{2}\,dx\,dt,\end{split}

for any test functions
\phi_{i}\in\mathcal{D}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times[0,T));i=1,2
with
v=\mathcal{K}\ast w
and
z=\mathcal{K}\ast u.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
T>0
. Then
(u,w)
is called a free energy solution with initial data
(u_{0},w_{0})
on
(0,T)
if
(u,w)
is a weak solution and moreover satisfies

(1.15)

\left(u^{\frac{3d-2}{2(d+2)}},w^{\frac{3d-2}{2(d+2)}}\right)\in\left(L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}))\right)^{2},

and

(1.16)

\begin{split}&E_{\alpha}[u(t),w(t)]+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u\left|\nabla\left(\alpha_{1}2||u||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{\frac{4}{d+2}}u^{\frac{d-2}{d+2}}\right)-\nabla v\right|^{2}\,dx\,ds\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w\left|\nabla\left(\alpha_{2}2||w||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{\frac{4}{d+2}}w^{\frac{d-2}{d+2}}\right)-\nabla z\right|^{2}\,dx\,ds\leq E_{\alpha}[u_{0},w_{0}],\end{split}

for all
t\in(0,T)
with
v=\mathcal{K}\ast w
and
z=\mathcal{K}\ast u
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that the initial data
(u_{0},w_{0})
with
||u_{0}||_{1}=M_{1},||w_{0}||_{1}=M_{2}
satisfies (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).

(i)

If
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
satisfy (
1.12
), then there exists a global free energy solutions.

(ii)

If
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
do not satisfy (
1.12
), then it is possible to construct large initial data ensuring blow up in finite time.

2.
Approximate System

For the existence of solutions as usual we first consider a regularized system

(2.1)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}&=\alpha_{1}\left(\frac{d-2}{d}\right)||u_{\varepsilon}||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{4/(d+2)}\Delta((u_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{2d}{d+2}})-\nabla\cdot\left(u_{\varepsilon}\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\right),\,\,x\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d},t>0,\\
v_{\varepsilon}&=\mathcal{K}\ast w_{\varepsilon},\quad x\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d},t>0,\\
\partial_{t}w_{\varepsilon}&=\alpha_{2}\left(\frac{d-2}{d}\right)||w_{\varepsilon}||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{4/(d+2)}\Delta((w_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{2d}{d+2}})-\nabla\cdot\left(w_{\varepsilon}\nabla z_{\varepsilon}\right),\,\,x\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d},t>0,\\
z_{\varepsilon}&=\mathcal{K}\ast u_{\varepsilon},\quad x\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d},t>0,\end{split}

with initial data satisfying

(2.2)

u_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=u_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)\geq 0;\quad w_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=w_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)\geq 0,\,x\in{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}.

with
u_{0}^{\varepsilon}
and
w_{0}^{\varepsilon}
being the convolution of
u_{0}
and
w_{0}
with a sequence of mollifiers and
||u_{0}^{\varepsilon}||_{1}=||u||_{1}=M_{1}
and
||w_{0}^{\varepsilon}||_{1}=||w||_{1}=M_{2}
. As usual we will obtain a priori estimates for the regularized system (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) to ensure the existence of weak or free energy solution as
\varepsilon
tends to 0.

By following the general procedure in the single population chemotaxis systems
[
14
,
17
,
6
]
one can obtain the following lemma for which no proof is provided here.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
. There exists
T_{max}^{\varepsilon}\in(0,\infty]
denoting the maximal existence time such that the regularized system (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) has a unique nonnegative solution
(u_{\varepsilon},w_{\varepsilon})\in\left(W_{p}^{2,1}(Q_{T})\right)^{2}
with some
p>1
, where
Q_{T}={{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times(0,T)
with
T\in(0,T_{max}^{\varepsilon}
and

W_{p}^{2,1}(Q_{T}):=\{u\in L^{p}(0,T;W^{2,p}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})\cap W^{1,p}(0,T;L^{p}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})))\}.

Moreover, if
T_{max}^{\varepsilon}<\infty
then

\lim_{t\to T_{max}^{\varepsilon}}\left(||u_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)||_{\infty}+||w_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)||_{\infty}\right)=\infty.

We also recall here a version of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality(HLS) that if

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1+\frac{\lambda}{d}

and
h_{1}\in L^{p}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
,
h_{2}\in L^{q}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
with

then there exists a constant
C_{HLS}=C_{HLS}(d,\lambda,p)>0
such that

(2.3)

\left|\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{h_{1}(x)h_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-\lambda}}\,dy\,dx\right|\leq C_{HLS}||h_{1}||_{p}||h_{2}||_{q}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
T\in(0,T_{max}^{\varepsilon}]
. Suppose that there exists a constant
C>0
such that solution
(u_{\varepsilon},w_{\varepsilon})
of the system (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) with initial data
(u_{0}^{\varepsilon},w_{0}^{\varepsilon})
being the convolution of
(u_{0},w_{0})
satisfies
||u_{\varepsilon}(t)||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\leq C
and
||w_{\varepsilon}(t)||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\leq C
for
t\in(0,T)
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(d,u_{0}^{\varepsilon},w_{0}^{\varepsilon})>0
such that

(2.4)

||\left(u_{\varepsilon}(t),w_{\varepsilon}(t)\right)||_{r}\leq C,

and

(2.5)

||\left(v_{\varepsilon}(t),z_{\varepsilon}(t)\right)||_{r}+||\left(\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(t),\nabla z_{\varepsilon}(t)\right)||_{r}\leq C,

for
r\in[1,\infty)
and
t\in(0,T)
.

Proof.

For
p>1
, we test the first equation in (
2.1
) by
u_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}
and integrate to find that

(2.6)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{p}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{p}\,dx&=\underbrace{-\alpha_{1}(\frac{d-2}{d})||u_{\varepsilon}||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{\frac{4}{d+2}}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\nabla(u_{\varepsilon}^{p-1})\cdot\nabla(u_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\,dx}_{I}\\
&\quad+\underbrace{\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}\nabla(u_{\varepsilon}^{p-1})\cdot\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\,dx}_{II}.\end{split}

It is not difficult to see that

(2.7)

\begin{split}I&=-2\alpha_{1}\frac{d-2}{d+2}(p-1)||u_{\varepsilon}||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{\frac{4}{d+2}}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{p-2}(u_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{2d}{d+2}-1}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\\
&\leq-2\alpha_{1}\frac{d-2}{d+2}(p-1)||u_{\varepsilon}||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{\frac{4}{d+2}}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{p+\frac{2d}{d+2}-3}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\\
&=-4\alpha_{1}\frac{d-2}{d+2}(p-1)\frac{1}{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\left|\nabla\left(u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+(\frac{d-2}{d+2})}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}\,dx.\end{split}

(2.8)

II=\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{p}w_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

Similarly, by multiplying the third equation in (
2.1
) by
w_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}
and using
-\Delta z_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}

(2.9)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{p}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w_{\varepsilon}^{p}\,dx&\leq-4\alpha_{2}\frac{d-2}{d+2}(p-1)\frac{1}{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\left|\nabla\left(w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+(\frac{d-2}{d+2})}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}\,dx\\
&\qquad+\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}w_{\varepsilon}^{p}\,dx.\end{split}

Combining together

(2.10)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{p}\,dx+\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w_{\varepsilon}^{p}\,dx\right\}\right)\\
&+4\alpha_{1}\frac{d-2}{d+2}(p-1)\frac{1}{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\left|\nabla\left(u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+(\frac{d-2}{d+2})}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}\,dx\\
&+4\alpha_{2}\frac{d-2}{d+2}(p-1)\frac{1}{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\left|\nabla\left(w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+(\frac{d-2}{d+2})}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{p-1}{p}\left\{\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{p}w_{\varepsilon}\,dx+\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}w_{\varepsilon}^{p}\,dx\right\}.\end{split}

Now,

(2.11)

\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{p}w_{\varepsilon}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{pr_{1}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w_{\varepsilon}^{r_{1}^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}^{\prime}}}

and

(2.12)

\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}w_{\varepsilon}^{p}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{r_{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r_{2}}}\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w_{\varepsilon}^{pr_{2}^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r_{2}^{\prime}}}

by Hölder inequality with
r_{1},r_{2}>1,r_{1}^{\prime}=\frac{r_{1}}{r_{1}-1}
and
r_{2}^{\prime}=\frac{r_{2}}{r_{2}-1}
. We have

(2.13)

\frac{2d}{d+2}<pr_{1}<\frac{\left(p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}\right)d}{d-2}

and

(2.14)

\frac{1}{r_{1}}>\max\left\{\frac{d-2}{2d},\frac{d-2}{d}\frac{p}{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}\right\}.

We now recall a variant of Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (see Lemma 6 in
[
16
]
)

(2.15)

||\Phi||_{k_{2}}\leq C^{2/{r+m-1}}||\Phi||_{k_{1}}^{1-\sigma}||\nabla\Phi^{\frac{r+m-1}{2}}||_{2}^{\frac{2\sigma}{r+m-1}}

with
m\geq 1,\,k_{1}\in[1,r+m-1]
and
1\leq k_{1}\leq k_{2}\leq\frac{(r+m-1)d}{d-2}
with
d\geq 3
and
\sigma=\frac{r+m-1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}-\frac{1}{k_{2}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{r+m-1}{2k_{1}}\right)^{-1}
. We pick

and use the above inequalities to deduce

||u_{\varepsilon}||_{pr_{1}}^{p}\leq C||u_{\varepsilon}||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{p(1-\sigma)}||\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}}||_{2}^{p\frac{2\sigma}{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}},

with

\sigma=\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}\frac{\frac{d+2}{2d}-\frac{1}{pr_{1}}}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{\frac{4d}{d+2}}}\in(0,1).

Using the bound
||u_{\varepsilon}(t)||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\leq C
for
t\in(0,T)
we deduce that

(2.17)

\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{pr_{1}}\,dx\right)^{1/{r_{1}}}\leq C||\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}}||_{2}^{\frac{\frac{p(d+2)}{2d}-\frac{1}{r_{1}}}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{\frac{4d}{d+2}}}}.

Likewise, using

\frac{2d}{d+2}<r_{1}^{\prime}<\frac{(p+\frac{d-2}{d+2})d}{d-2}

and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality with
||w_{\varepsilon}(t)||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\leq C

(2.18)

||w_{\varepsilon}||_{r_{1}^{\prime}}\leq C||\nabla w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}}||_{2}^{\frac{\frac{d+2}{2d}-\frac{1}{r_{1}^{\prime}}}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{4d/{d+2}}}}.

Then

(2.19)

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{pr_{1}}\,dx\right)^{1/{r_{1}}}\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w_{\varepsilon}^{r_{1}^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{1/{r_{1}^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq C||\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}}||_{2}^{\frac{p(\frac{d+2}{2d})-\frac{1}{r_{1}}}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{4d/{d+2}}}}||\nabla w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}}||_{2}^{\frac{p(\frac{d+2}{2d})-1+\frac{1}{r_{1}}}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{4d/{d+2}}}}.\end{split}

We have

\frac{2d}{d+2}<r_{2}<\frac{(p+\frac{d-2}{d+2})d}{d-2}.

Then by Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

(2.20)

\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{r_{2}}\,dx\right)^{1/{r_{2}}}\leq C||\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}}||_{2}^{\frac{\frac{d+2}{2d}-\frac{1}{r_{2}}}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{4d/{d+2}}}}

as

and

.
Now,

\frac{2d}{d+2}<pr_{2}^{\prime}<\frac{(p+\frac{d-2}{d+2})d}{d-2}.

One has

(2.21)

\begin{split}||w_{\varepsilon}||_{pr_{2}^{\prime}}^{p}&\leq C||w_{\varepsilon}||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{p(1-\sigma)}||\nabla w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}}||_{2}^{p\frac{2\sigma}{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}}\\
&\leq C\left|\left|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{\frac{\left(\frac{p(d+2)}{2d}-\frac{1}{r_{2}^{\prime}}\right)}{\left(\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{4d/{d+2}}\right)}}\end{split}

with

\sigma=\frac{(d+2)\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{4d}-\frac{1}{pr_{2}^{\prime}}}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+(d+2)\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{4d}}.

Then,

(2.22)

\begin{split}\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{r_{2}}\,dx\right)^{1/{r_{2}}}\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w_{\varepsilon}^{pr_{2}^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{1/{r_{2}^{\prime}}}&\leq C\left|\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{\frac{\frac{d+2}{2d}-\frac{1}{r_{2}}}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+(d+2)\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{4d}}}\\
&\quad\times\left|\left|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}^{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{\frac{\frac{d+2}{2d}-\frac{1}{r_{2}}-1}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+(d+2)\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{4d}}}\end{split}

Combining, one gets

(2.23)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{p}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{p}+w_{\varepsilon}^{p}\,dx\right)+\delta_{1}(d,\alpha_{1})\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}}|^{2}\,dx\\
+\delta_{2}(d,\alpha_{2})\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}}|^{2}\,dx\\

+C||\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}}||_{2}^{\frac{\frac{d+2}{2d}-\frac{1}{r_{2}}}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(d+2)(p+\frac{d-2}{d+2})}{4d}}}||\nabla w_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}}||_{2}^{\frac{\frac{p(d+2)}{2d}-1+\frac{1}{r_{2}}}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(d+2)(p+\frac{d-2}{d+2})}{4d}}},\end{split}

where
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}>0
and can be calculated explicitly.

We now deal with the boundedness of
u_{\varepsilon}
and
w_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{p}-
space: Let

be such that

. For

\alpha^{\gamma_{1}}\beta^{\gamma_{2}}\leq\varepsilon(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2})+C.

From the above calculations, there exists some

with some

such that

\frac{\frac{p(d+2)}{2d}-\frac{1}{r_{1}}}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p+\frac{2d}{d+2}-1}{4d/(d+2)}}+\frac{\frac{d+2}{2d}-1+\frac{1}{r_{1}}}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p+\frac{2d}{d+2}-1}{4d/(d+2)}}<2.

\frac{\frac{d+2}{2d}-\frac{1}{r_{2}}}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p+\frac{2d}{d+2}-1}{4d/(d+2)}}+\frac{\frac{(d+2)p}{2d}-1+\frac{1}{r_{2}}}{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p+\frac{2d}{d+2}-1}{4d/(d+2)}}<2.

The above two inequalities can be simplified further(left to the reader). Now,

(2.24)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{p}\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}^{p}\,dx+\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w_{\varepsilon}^{p}\,dx\right)+\delta_{1}(d,\alpha_{1})\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}}\right|^{2}\,dx\\

with
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}>0
. Using Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality with

and

&\quad\leq\frac{\frac{4d}{d+2}(p-1)}{(p+\frac{d-2}{d+2})^{2}}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p+\frac{d-2}{d+2}}{2}}|^{2}\,dx+C\end{split}

and

by

3.
BLOW-UP

In this section we deal with the finite-time blow-up phenomenon. We begin with a simple lemma stating the time-evolution of second moment of solutions.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(u_{0},w_{0})
satisfy (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) and let
(u,w)
be a free energy solution of (
1.1
) on
[0,T_{max})
with
T_{max}\in(0,\infty]
. Then,

\frac{d}{dt}I(t)=G(t),\quad\forall t\in(0,T_{max}),

where

I(t):=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}|x|^{2}\left(u(x,t)+w(x,t)\right)\,dx

and

\begin{split}G(t)&:=C(\alpha_{1},u)\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u(x,t)^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\,dx+C(\alpha_{2},w)\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w(x,t)^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\,dx\\
&-2c_{d}(d-2)\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{u(x,t)w(y,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\,dy\,dx.\end{split}

with

\begin{split}&C(\alpha_{1},u):=2(d-2)\alpha_{1}||u||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{4/{d+2}},\\
&C(\alpha_{2},w):=2(d-2)\alpha_{2}||w||_{\frac{2d}{d+2}}^{4/{d+2}}.\end{split}

Notice that with the definitions,

(3.1)

G(t)=2(d-2)E_{\alpha}[u(t),v(t)]\ .

Proof.

Straightforward calculations lead to

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}I(t)=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}|x|^{2}\left(u(x,t)+w(x,t)\right)\,dx\\
&=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}|x|^{2}\Big[C(\alpha_{1},u)\Delta\left(u^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\right)-\nabla\cdot\left(u\nabla v\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+C(\alpha_{2},w)\Delta\left(w^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\right)-\nabla\cdot\left(w\nabla z\right)\Big]\,dx\\
&=2dC(\alpha_{1},u)\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u(x,t)^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\,dx+C(\alpha_{2},w)\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w(x,t)^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\,dx\\
&\quad+2\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\left[x\cdot\nabla\mathcal{K}(x-y)\right]u(x,t)w(y,t)\,dy\,dx\\
&\qquad+2\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\left[x\cdot\nabla\mathcal{K}(x-y)\right]u(y,t)w(x,t)\,dy\,dx,\end{split}

with
\mathcal{K}(x)=\frac{c_{d}}{|x|^{d-2}}
and
c_{d}
is the surface area of the sphere
S^{d-1}
in
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
.
Now,

\begin{split}&2\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\left[x\cdot\nabla\mathcal{K}(x-y)\right]u(x,t)w(y,t)\,dy\,dx\\
&=-2c_{d}(d-2)\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{(x-y)\cdot x}{|x-y|^{d}}u(x,t)w(y,t)\,dy\,dx\\
&=-2c_{d}(d-2)\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{|x|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d}}u(x,t)w(y,t)\,dy\,dx\\
&\quad+2c_{d}(d-2)\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{y\cdot x}{|x-y|^{d}}u(x,t)w(y,t)\,dy\,dx\\
&=-c_{d}(d-2)\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{|x|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d}}u(x,t)w(y,t)\,dy\,dx\\
&\quad-c_{d}(d-2)\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{|y|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d}}u(x,t)w(y,t)\,dy\,dx\\
&\qquad+2c_{d}(d-2)\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{y\cdot x}{|x-y|^{d}}u(x,t)w(y,t)\,dy\,dx.\end{split}

Similarly,

\begin{split}&2\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\left[x\cdot\nabla\mathcal{K}(x-y)\right]u(y,t)w(x,t)\,dy\,dx\\
&=-c_{d}(d-2)\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{|x|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d}}u(y,t)w(x,t)\,dy\,dx\\
&\quad-c_{d}(d-2)\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{|y|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d}}u(x,t)w(y,t)\,dy\,dx\\
&\qquad+2c_{d}(d-2)\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{y\cdot x}{|x-y|^{d}}u(x,t)w(y,t)\,dy\,dx.\end{split}

Combining, we deduce that

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}|x|^{2}\left(u(x,t)+w(y,t)\right)\,dx=2dC(\alpha_{1},u)\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+2dC(\alpha_{2},w)\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\,dx\\
&-c_{d}(d-2)\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d}}u(x,t)w(y,t)\,dy\,dx\\
&-c_{d}(d-2)\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d}}u(y,t)w(x,t)\,dy\,dx\\
&+4c_{d}(d-2)\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{y\cdot x}{|x-y|^{d}}u(x,t)w(y,t)\,dy\,dx\\
&=2dC(\alpha_{1},u)\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}u^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\,dx+2dC(\alpha_{2},w)\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}w^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\,dx\\
&\qquad-2c_{d}(d-2)\iint_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\frac{u(x,t)w(y,t)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\,dy\,dx.\end{split}

∎

We now observe that

(3.2)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}I(t)=G(t)&=2(d-2)E_{\alpha}[u(t),w(t)]\\
&\leq 2(d-2)E_{\alpha}[u_{0},w_{0}]=G(0).\end{split}

As we have seen above,
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
are such that (
1.12
) is violated, then we may take the initial data

(u_{0},w_{0})
to consist of appropriate multiples of
HLS
optimizers, and then we shall have
E_{\alpha}[u_{0},w_{0}]<0
, and hence
G(0)<0
. By the montonicity if the energy, we will then have

G(t)\leq G(0)<0
for all
t
such that a regular solution exists. But then (
3.2
) will imply that the second moment will be negative after some time which is impossible by the non-negativity of
u
and
w
. Hence blow-up occurs in a finite time.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
be such that (
1.12
) is violated. Then one can find some initial data
(u_{0},w_{0})
satisfying (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) such that free energy solution
(u,w)
of (
1.1
) with
(u,w)|_{t=0}=(u_{0},w_{0})
blows up in finite time.

Proof.

We consider an initial data
(u_{0},w_{0})
with
G(0)<0.
By a continuity argument there exists a
T^{*}>0
such that

G(t)<\frac{G(0)}{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad t\in[0,T^{*}].

By Lemma
3.1

(3.3)

\frac{d}{dt}I(t)=G(t)<\frac{G(0)}{2},\quad\text{for all}\quad t\in[0,T^{*}].

Integrating (
3.3
), it follows that

(3.4)

I(T^{*})<I(0)+\frac{G(0)}{2}T^{*}.

We may choose the initial data in such a way that the right hand side of (
3.4
) is negative. But this leads to a contradiction since it implies that
I(T^{*})<0
but
I(t)
is always non-negative for all
t>0.
Hence the solution
(u(t),w(t))
blows-up in finite time.

∎

On Gradings Modulo 2 of Simple Lie Algebras in Characteristic 2
Andrey KRUTOV †‡ and Alexei LEBEDEV §

Abstract
The ground field in the text is of characteristic 2. The classification of modulo 2 gradings of simple Lie algebras is vital for the classification of simple finite-dimensional Lie superalgebras: with each grading, a simple Lie superalgebra is associated, see arXiv:1407.1695. No classification of gradings was known for any type of simple Lie algebras, bar restricted Jacobson–Witt algebras (i.e., the first derived of the Lie algebras of vector fields with truncated polynomials as coefficients) on not less than 3 indeterminates. Here we completely describe gradings modulo 2 for several series of Lie algebras and their simple relatives: of special linear series, its projectivizations, and projectivizations of the derived Lie algebras of two inequivalent orthogonal series (except for 𝔬 Π ​ ( 8 ) {\mathfrak{o}}_{\Pi}(8) ). The classification of gradings is new, but all of the corresponding superizations are known. For the simple derived Zassenhaus algebras of height n > 1 n>1 , there is an ( n − 2 ) (n-2) -parametric family of modulo 2 gradings; all but one of the corresponding simple Lie superalgebras are new. Our classification also proves non-triviality of a deformation of a simple 3 | 2 3|2 -dimensional Lie superalgebra (new result).

Lie superlagebras in characteristic 2.
A

is a superspace

such that the even part

is a Lie algebra, the odd part

\displaystyle x\mapsto x^{2}\quad\text{such that $(ax)^{2}=a^{2}x^{2}$ for any $x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}$ and $a\in{\mathbb{K}}$, and}

(x+y)^{2}-x^{2}-y^{2}
is a bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
with values in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}

Then the bracket of odd elements is defined to be

\displaystyle[x,y]:=(x+y)^{2}-x^{2}-y^{2}.

The Jacobi identity involving odd elements takes the following form:

\displaystyle\big[x^{2},y\big]=[x,[x,y]]\qquad\text{for any}\ x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}},\ y\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}},\qquad\big[x^{2},x\big]=0\qquad\text{for any}\ x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}.

Divided powers.
There are two natural integer bases of the commutative algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[x]
of polynomials in
m
indeterminates

: the monomial one and the basis of divided powers, constructed as follows. For any multi-index

\displaystyle u_{i}^{(r_{i})}:=\frac{x_{i}^{r_{i}}}{r_{i}!}\qquad\text{and}\qquad u^{({\underline{r}})}:=\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}u_{i}^{(r_{i})}.

These

form an integer basis of

. Their multiplication relations are

\displaystyle u^{({\underline{r}})}\cdot u^{(\underline{s})}=\binom{{\underline{r}}+\underline{s}}{{\underline{r}}}u^{({\underline{r}}+\underline{s})},\qquad\text{where}\quad\binom{{\underline{r}}+\underline{s}}{{\underline{r}}}=\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}\binom{r_{i}+s_{i}}{r_{i}}.

The algebra

Solution was found long ago: one has to introduce

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\big(u_{j}^{(k)}\big):=\delta_{ij}u_{j}^{(k-1)}\ \ \text{ for all $k$, i.e., }\partial_{i}=\sum_{j\geq 1}(-1)^{j-1}y_{i,1}^{p-1}\cdots y_{i,j-1}^{p-1}\partial_{y_{i,j}}.

\boldsymbol{p}
-structure.
If

is a Lie algebra, then for every

\displaystyle\big[x^{[p]},y\big]=(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{x})^{p}(y)\qquad\text{for any $x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$},

\displaystyle(ax)^{[p]}=a^{p}x^{[p]}\qquad\text{for any~}a\in{\mathbb{K}},~x\in{\mathfrak{g}},

\displaystyle(x+y)^{[p]}=x^{[p]}+y^{[p]}+\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq p-1}s_{i}(x,y)\qquad\text{for any~}x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}},

If the Lie algebra

is without center, then the last two conditions of (

\displaystyle\big[g_{i}^{[p]},y\big]=(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{g_{i}})^{p}(y)\qquad\text{for any}\quad y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

1.3
How we seek
\boldsymbol{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}
-gradings

Let

\displaystyle([x,y])_{{\bar{0}}}=[x_{{\bar{0}}},y_{{\bar{0}}}]+[x_{{\bar{1}}},y_{{\bar{1}}}]\qquad\text{and}\qquad([x,y])_{{\bar{1}}}=[x_{{\bar{0}}},y_{{\bar{1}}}]+[x_{{\bar{1}}},y_{{\bar{0}}}].

\displaystyle U[x,y]=[Ux,(I-U)y]+[(I-U)x,Uy]=-2[Ux,Uy]+[Ux,y]+[x,Uy].

By definition, an operator

belongs to
{\mathfrak{o}}_{B}(V)
, where

is a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form, if and only if

\displaystyle B(Ax,y)+B(x,Ay)=0\qquad\text{for all $x,y\in V$.}

\mathop{\text{\rm Im}}\nolimits A
and

Conjecture 6.8

Appendix A
Necessary proofs from
[
2
]

Statement A.1

(
[
2
, Statement 3.8.1.a]
)
.

For
n=3
or
n\geq 5
, the algebra
{\mathfrak{der}}\,{\mathfrak{o}}^{(1)}_{I}(n)
can be identified with
{\mathfrak{o}}_{I}(n)/{\mathfrak{c}}
in the sense that for any
D\in{\mathfrak{der}}\,{\mathfrak{o}}^{(1)}_{I}(n)
, there is
A_{D}\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{I}(n)
such that
D
coincides with the restriction of
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{A_{D}}
to
{\mathfrak{o}}^{(1)}_{I}(n)
; for a given
D
, the element
A_{D}
is uniquely defined up to adding a scalar matrix.

Proof.

In this proof,
i
,
j
,
k
,
l
,
m
are always indices from
1
to
n
.

The algebra
{\mathfrak{o}}^{(1)}_{I}(n)
consists of zero-diagonal symmetric
n\times n
matrices, which means that the elements
o^{\{i,j\}}:=E^{i,j}+E^{j,i}
, where
\{i,j\}
are all two-element subsets of
\{1,\dots,n\}
, form a basis of
{\mathfrak{o}}^{(1)}_{I}(n)
. Their commutation relations are (we assume that
i\neq j
and
k\neq l
):

\displaystyle{}[o^{\{i,j\}},o^{\{k,l\}}]=\begin{cases}0&\text{if~}\{k,l\}=\{i,j\}\text{~or~}\{k,l\}\cap\{i,j\}=\varnothing,\\
o^{\{j,k\}}&\text{for~}l=i\text{~and~}k\neq i,j.\end{cases}

Alternatively, we can say that for an arbitrary matrix
M\in{\mathfrak{o}}^{(1)}_{I}(n)
and
i\neq j
,

\displaystyle\big[M,o^{\{i,j\}}\big]_{kl}=0\qquad\text{if}\ \{k,l\}=\{i,j\}\text{~or~}\{k,l\}\cap\{i,j\}=\varnothing,

\displaystyle\big[M,o^{\{i,j\}}\big]_{ik}=\big[M,o^{\{i,j\}}\big]_{ki}=M_{jk}\qquad\text{for~}k\neq i,j,

\displaystyle\big[M,o^{\{i,j\}}\big]_{kk}=0\qquad\text{for an arbitrary~}k.

Let
D
be a derivation of
{\mathfrak{o}}^{(1)}_{I}(n)
. Let us prove that for arbitrary three pairwise distinct indices
i
,
j
,
k
,

\displaystyle\big(Do^{\{i,j\}}\big)_{ij}+\big(Do^{\{i,k\}}\big)_{ik}+\big(Do^{\{j,k\}}\big)_{jk}=0,

(A.1)

and that for arbitrary four pairwise distinct indices
i
,
j
,
k
,
l
,

\displaystyle\big(Do^{\{i,j\}}\big)_{kl}=0,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\big(Do^{\{i,k\}}\big)_{il}=\big(Do^{\{j,k\}}\big)_{jl}=\big(Do^{\{i,l\}}\big)_{ik}.

(A.3)

Since
o^{\{i,k\}}=\big[o^{\{i,j\}},o^{\{j,k\}}\big]
, we have

\displaystyle\big(Do^{\{i,k\}}\big)_{ik}=\big(D[o^{\{i,j\}},o^{\{j,k\}}]\big)_{ik}=\big[Do^{\{i,j\}},o^{\{j,k\}}\big]_{ik}+\big[o^{\{i,j\}},Do^{\{j,k\}}\big]_{ik}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\big(Do^{\lx@text@lbrace i,k\lx@text@rbrace}\big)_{ik}}{}=\big(Do^{\{i,j\}}\big)_{ij}+\big(Do^{\{k,j\}}\big)_{jk},

which proves (
A.1
).

Note that (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) are vacuously true for
n=3
, since in this case, there exist no four pairwise distinct indices. In case of
n\geq 5
, let
m
be an index different from all of
i
,
j
,
k
,
l
. Then
\big[o^{\{i,j\}},o^{\{l,m\}}\big]=0
, which means that

\displaystyle 0=\big(D\big[o^{\{i,j\}},o^{\{l,m\}}\big]\big)_{km}=\big[Do^{\{i,j\}},o^{\{l,m\}}\big]_{km}+\big[o^{\{i,j\}},Do^{\{l,m\}}\big]_{km}=\big(Do^{\{i,j\}}\big)_{kl}+0,

which proves (
A.2
). Since
o^{\{i,k\}}=\big[o^{\{i,j\}},o^{\{j,k\}}\big]
, we have

\displaystyle\big(Do^{\{i,k\}}\big)_{il}=\big(D\big[o^{\{i,j\}},o^{\{j,k\}}\big]\big)_{il}=\big[Do^{\{i,j\}},o^{\{j,k\}}\big]_{il}+\big[o^{\{i,j\}},Do^{\{j,k\}}\big]_{il}=0+\big(Do^{\{j,k\}}\big)_{jl},

which proves the first equality in (
A.3
). And since
o^{\{i,k\}}=\big[o^{\{i,l\}},o^{\{k,l\}}\big]
, we have

\displaystyle\big(Do^{\{i,k\}}\big)_{il}=\big(D\big[o^{\{i,l\}},o^{\{k,l\}}\big]\big)_{il}=\big[Do^{\{i,l\}},o^{\{k,l\}}\big]_{il}+\big[o^{\{i,l\}},Do^{\{k,l\}}\big]_{il}=\big(Do^{\{i,l\}}\big)_{ik}+0,

which proves the second equality in (
A.3
).

Now consider matrix
A_{D}
whose entries are as follows:

\displaystyle(A_{D})_{11}=0,\qquad(A_{D})_{ii}=\big(Do^{\{1,i\}}\big)_{1i}\qquad\text{for}\ i\neq 1,

\displaystyle(A_{D})_{ij}=\big(Do^{\{k,i\}}\big)_{kj}\qquad\text{for}\ i\neq j,\text{~and some~}k\neq i,j.

Note that (
A.3
) shows that
(A_{D})_{ij}
does not depend on the choice of
k
and that the matrix is symmetric, i.e.,
(A_{D})_{ij}=(A_{D})_{ji}
.

Then it follows from (
A.1
), (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) that
Do^{\{i,j\}}=\big[A_{D},o^{\{i,j\}}\big]
for an arbitrary
o^{\{i,j\}}
. Since the
o^{\{i,j\}}
form a basis of
{\mathfrak{o}}^{(1)}_{I}(n)
, it means that
D
coincides with the restriction of
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{A_{D}}
to
{\mathfrak{o}}^{(1)}_{I}(n)
. On the other hand, it is easy to check that for any matrix
A\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{I}(n)
, the restriction of
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{A}
to
{\mathfrak{o}}^{(1)}_{I}(n)
is a derivation of
{\mathfrak{o}}^{(1)}_{I}(n)
, and that two matrices
A,A^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{I}(n)
determine the same derivation of
{\mathfrak{o}}^{(1)}_{I}(n)
if and only if
A-A^{\prime}=c1_{n}
for some
c\in{\mathbb{K}}
. This proves our claim.
∎

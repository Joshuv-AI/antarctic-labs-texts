A local study of the fiber-full scheme
Yairon Cid-Ruiz, Ritvik Ramkumar

Abstract
. We study some of the local properties of the fiber-full scheme, which is a fine moduli space that generalizes the Hilbert scheme by parametrizing closed subschemes with prescribed cohomological data. As a consequence, we provide sufficient conditions for cohomology to remain constant under Gröbner degenerations. We also describe a tangent-obstruction theory for the fiber-full scheme in analogy with the one for the Hilbert scheme.

1.
Introduction

In previous recent work
[
5
]
, we introduced the
fiber-full scheme
which can be seen as the parameter space that generalizes the Hilbert scheme by controlling the entire cohomological data.
Here we continue the study of this scheme from a local point of view.
Before discussing our findings regarding the fiber-full scheme, we choose to address the following fundamental questions that are among the main motivations of our work.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
R
be a standard graded polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
,
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})\subset R
be the graded irrelevant ideal and
S=R[t]\cong R\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathbb{k}[t]
.

Question 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}\subset S
be a one-parameter flat family of homogeneous ideals with special fiber
I_{0}\subset R
and general fiber
I_{1}\subset R
.
Under which conditions imposed
only
on the special fiber
I_{0}\subset R
do we have that

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})\right]_{\nu}\right)=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{1})\right]_{\nu}\right)

for all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
?

We say that a one-parameter flat family
\mathcal{I}\subset S

has constant cohomology
if it satisfies the conclusion of the above question.
Recall that if
\mathcal{I}\subset S
has constant cohomology then important invariants are equal for the special fiber
I_{0}\subset R
and the general fiber
I_{1}\subset R
(for instance, we obtain equalities in Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity and in depth, i.e.,
\text{reg}(R/I_{0})=\text{reg}(R/I_{1})
and
0pt(R/I_{0})=0pt(R/I_{1})
).
By upper semicontinuity, we always have that
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})\right]_{\nu}\right)\geq\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{1})\right]_{\nu}\right)
.
A particular case of major interest in the following sub-question about
Gröbner degenerations
.

Question 1.2
.

Let
I\subset R
be a homogeneous ideal and
\text{in}_{>}(I)\subset R
be the initial ideal with respect to a monomial order
>
on
R
.
Under which conditions imposed
only
on the initial ideal
\text{in}_{>}(I)\subset R
do we have that

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I)\right]_{\nu}\right)=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/\text{in}_{>}(I))\right]_{\nu}\right)

for all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
?

Important progress was made recently by Conca and Varbaro in
[
7
]
as they showed that the conclusion of
Section 1
is satisfied when
\text{in}_{>}(I)
is squarefree.
Our main contribution towards the above questions is the following theorem.

Theorem A

(
Theorem 5.3
)
.

Under the above notation, let
\mathcal{I}\subset S
be a one-parameter flat family of homogeneous ideals with special fiber
I_{0}\subset R
and general fiber
I_{1}\subset R
.
If the zero map is the only zero-degree homogeneous
R
-linear map from
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i-1}(R/I_{0})
to
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})
for all
1\leq i\leq r
, i.e.,

\left[{\text{Hom}}_{R}\Big(\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i-1}(R/I_{0}),\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})\Big)\right]_{0}=0\quad\text{for all $1\leq i\leq r$},

then the family
\mathcal{I}
has constant cohomology.

This result provides an abundance of new families of ideals such that flat degenerations to them preserve cohomology. Notice that the condition of
Theorem A
is trivially satisfied when the special fiber
I_{0}\subset R
is Cohen-Macaulay. As pointed out by the following examples, there are many non-squarefree monomial ideals that satisfy the criterion of
Theorem A
.

Example 1.3

(
Section 7
,
Section 7
)
.

Consider the (non-squarefree) monomial ideals

I_{1}=(x^{2},y^{2},xy,xz,yz),\quad I_{2}=(x^{2}y,xz^{2},y^{2}z,z^{3})\quad\text{and}\quad I_{3}=(xy^{2},xz)

in
R=\mathbb{k}[x,y,z]
, which are not Cohen-Macaulay.
These three ideals satisfy the criterion of
Theorem A
.
Thus, for any homogeneous ideal
I\subset R
such that
\text{in}_{>}(I)
equals
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
or
I_{3}
, we have that
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I)\right]_{\nu}\right)=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/\text{in}_{>}(I))\right]_{\nu}\right)

for all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The result of
Theorem A
does not cover all squarefree monomial ideals;
in
Section 7
we give a squarefree monomial ideal that does not satisfy the condition of
Theorem A
.

From another point of view, a typical example of a non-Cohen Macaulay ideal of dimension at least
2
, is one where the local cohomology is only non-vanishing at the the depth and dimension, both of which are different. Even more significantly, any ideal whose local cohomology is vanishing in alternate degrees also satisfies the hypothesis of
Theorem A
. In both of these situations, the ideals satisfy the criterion of
Theorem A
. For instance, generic residual intersections
Section 7
and many semigroup rings
[
27
]
.

Our proof of
Theorem A
follows directly from the local study of the fiber-full scheme that we make in this paper.
This is hardly a surprise because, by definition, the fiber-full scheme is the parameter space controlling the entire cohomological data.
The fiber-full scheme
{\text{Fib}}_{\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}/A}^{\mathbf{h}}
is a fine moduli space parametrizing all closed subschemes
Z\subset\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}
such that
\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{Z}(\nu))
is a locally free
A
-module of rank equal to
h_{i}(\nu)
, where
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
is a fixed tuple of functions (see
[
5
]
).

Since we are interested in arbitrary ideals and not just the saturated ones, we introduce a version of the fiber-full scheme that works for homogeneous ideals.
Accordingly, we obtain a cohomological stratification of the version of the Hilbert scheme introduced by Haiman and Sturmfels
[
12
]
.
Let
A
be a Noetherian ring,
T=A[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})\subset T
.
For a given function
g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
, the Hilbert scheme
{\text{HS}}_{T/A}^{g}
of Haiman and Sturmfels parametrizes all homogeneous ideals
I\subset T
such that
\left[T/I\right]_{\nu}
is a locally free
A
-module of rank equal to
g(\nu)
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
.
We stratify the scheme
{\text{HS}}_{T/A}^{g}
as follows.
Given a function
g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
and a tuple of functions
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r+1}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+2}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+2}
, we develop the fiber-full scheme
{\text{Fib}}_{T/A}^{g,\mathbf{h}}
parametrizing all homogeneous ideals
I\subset T
that satisfy the two conditions:

(a)

\left[T/I\right]_{\nu}
is a locally free
A
-module of rank equal to
g(\nu)
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, and

(b)

\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(T/I)\right]_{\nu}
is a locally free
A
-module of rank equal to
h_{i}(\nu)
for all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

In
Theorem 3.1
, by using the techniques developed in
[
5
]
, we show the existence of the parameter space
{\text{Fib}}_{T/A}^{g,\mathbf{h}}
.
Note that

{\text{Fib}}_{T/A}\,=\,\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\\
\mathbf{h}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+2}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+2}\end{subarray}}{\text{Fib}}_{T/A}^{g,\mathbf{h}}

is the fiber-full scheme that parametrizes all homogeneous ideals giving a flat quotient with flat local cohomology modules.

Our first main result towards a local study of the fiber-full scheme is determining its tangent space.

Theorem B

(
Theorem 4.7
)
.

Under the above notation, let
I_{0}\subset R
be a homogeneous ideal.
The tangent space of
{\text{Fib}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
at the corresponding point
[I_{0}]
is given by

T_{[I_{0}]}{\text{Fib}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}=\big\{\varphi\in\big[{\text{Hom}}(I_{0},R/I_{0})\big]_{0}\,\mid\,\text{H}^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(\varphi)=0\text{ for all $i\geq 0$}\big\},

where
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\varphi)
denotes the natural map
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\varphi):\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I_{0})\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})
induced in cohomology.

Since
T_{[I_{0}]}{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}=\left[{\text{Hom}}_{R}(I_{0},R/I_{0})\right]_{0}
, one could rephrase the above theorem by saying that the tangent space of
{\text{Fib}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
is given by the tangent vectors of
{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
that are trivial from a cohomological point of view.
This seems quite intuitive since by definition cohomologies remain constant over the entire fiber-full scheme
{\text{Fib}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}^{g,\mathbf{h}}
.
In
Theorem 4.14
, we give a similar computation for the tangent space of the fiber-full scheme
{\text{Fib}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}/\mathbb{k}}
.

We also provide a description of a tangent-obstruction theory for the fiber-full scheme in analogy with the one for the Hilbert scheme
(see, e.g.,
[
13
, Theorem 6.2]
).
Let
(C^{\prime},{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime},\mathbb{k})
and
(C,{\mathfrak{n}},\mathbb{k})
be Artinian local rings with residue field
\mathbb{k}
, and suppose that

0\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}\rightarrow C^{\prime}\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0

is a short exact sequence with
{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}\mathfrak{a}=0
.
Let
T=C[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
and
T^{\prime}=C^{\prime}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
be standard graded polynomial rings.
Let
I\subset T
be a homogeneous ideal such that
[I]\in{\text{Fib}}_{T/C}
, and denote by
I_{0}\subset R
the homogeneous ideal
I_{0}=I\otimes_{C}\mathbb{k}
(i.e.,
I\subset T
is a fiber-full extension of
I_{0}\subset R
over
C
).
We seek all homogeneous ideals
I^{\prime}\subset T^{\prime}
such that
[I^{\prime}]\in{\text{Fib}}_{T^{\prime}/C^{\prime}}
and
I\cong I^{\prime}\otimes_{C^{\prime}}C
.
The following result describes the fiber-full extensions of
I
over
C^{\prime}
.

Theorem C

(
Theorem 4.8
)
.

Under the notations above, the set of fiber-full extensions of
[I]\in{\text{Fib}}_{T/C}
over
C^{\prime}

\left\{[I^{\prime}]\in{\text{Fib}}_{T^{\prime}/C^{\prime}}\;\text{ such that }\;I^{\prime}\otimes_{C^{\prime}}C\cong I\right\}

is a pseudotorsor under the action of the subgroup of
\big[{\text{Hom}}_{R}\left(I_{0},R/I_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathfrak{a}\right)\big]_{0}
given by

\big\{\varphi\in\big[{\text{Hom}}_{R}\left(I_{0},R/I_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathfrak{a}\right)\big]_{0}\;\mid\;\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\varphi)=0\text{ for all $i\geq 0$}\big\},

where
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\varphi)
denotes the natural map
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\varphi):\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I_{0})\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathfrak{a})
induced in cohomology.

In
Theorem 4.9
, we determine sufficient conditions for the existence of obstructions in the fiber-full scheme
{\text{Fib}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
.
Furthermore, in
§4.2
we provide a similar study for a tangent-obstruction theory for the fiber-full scheme
{\text{Fib}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}/\mathbb{k}}
parametrizing closed subschemes.

The organization of this paper is as follows.
In

, we set up the notation used throughout the document and we recall some required results.
In

We study tangent-obstruction theories for the fiber-full schemes

Our results regarding one-parameter flat families are given in

2.
Preliminaries

In this short section, we fix the notation and recall some necessary results that were obtained in the previous papers
[
6
,
5
]
.
Here we review some basic results regarding
fiber-full modules and sheaves
, and the corresponding parameter space that we introduced:
the fiber-full scheme
.
Throughout this section the following setup is fixed.

Setup 2.1
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian ring,
R=A[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring, and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})\subset R
be the graded irrelevant ideal.

The main interest in fiber-full modules and sheaves comes from the fact that all their cohomologies are flat over the base ring.

Definition 2.2
.

A finitely generated graded
R
-module is said to be
fiber-full over
A
if
M
is flat over
A
and
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)
is flat over
A
for all
i\geq 0
.

Definition 2.3
.

A coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}=\text{Proj}(R)
is said to be
fiber-full over
A
if
\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r},\mathcal{F}(\nu))
is flat over
A
for all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The following result is a fundamental criterion to detect fiber-full modules and sheaves. It is also the primary reason for choosing the term
fiber-full
(see also
[
24
, Chapter 3]
).

Theorem 2.4

(
[
6
, Theorem A]
)
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module.
Then, the following results are equivalent:

(i)

M
is fiber-full over
A
.

(ii)

M
is flat over
A
and
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\left(M\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}^{q}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)
is
(A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}^{q}A_{\mathfrak{p}})
-flat for all
i\geq 0
,
q\geq 1
and
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.

(iii)

M
is flat over
A
and the natural map
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\left(M\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}^{q}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\left(M\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)
is surjective for all
i\geq 0
,
q\geq 1
and
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
5
, Theorem 4.2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}
.
Then, the following results are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{F}
is fiber-full over
A
.

(ii)

\mathcal{F}
is flat over
A
and
\text{H}^{i}\left(\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r},\mathcal{F}(\nu)\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}^{q}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)
is
A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}^{q}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
-flat for all
i\geq 0
,
q\geq 1
,
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.

(iii)

\mathcal{F}
is flat over
A
and the natural map
\text{H}^{i}\left(\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r},\mathcal{F}(\nu)\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}^{q}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\rightarrow\text{H}^{i}\left(\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r},\mathcal{F}(\nu)\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)
is surjective for all
i\geq 0
,
q\geq 1
,
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.

Quite importantly, these modules and sheaves commute with arbitrary base change in cohomology.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
6
, Theorem A]
,
[
5
, Proposition 3.1]
)
.

For any
A
-algebra
B
, the following statements hold:

(i)

If
M
is a finitely generated graded
R
-module that is fiber-full over
A
, then one has a natural base change isomorphism

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}B\;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\;\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M\otimes_{A}B)

for all
i\geq 0
.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{F}
is a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}}
-module that is fiber-full over
A
, then one has a natural base change isomorphism

\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r},\mathcal{F}(\nu))\otimes_{A}B\;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{B}^{r},\mathcal{F}(\nu)\otimes_{A}B)

for all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

In
[
5
]
we introduced the natural parameter space for fiber-full sheaves.
This new object provided a generalization of the classical Hilbert and Quot schemes.
Here we only consider a special case, and we refer the reader to
[
5
]
for more details.

We consider the following covariant functor
\fFib_{\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}/A}:(A\text{-alg})\rightarrow(\text{Sets})
from the category of Noetherian
A
-algebras to the category of sets

\fFib_{\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}/A}(B)\,:=\,\Big\{\text{closed subschemes $Z\subset\mathbb{P}_{B}^{r}$}\,\mid\,\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{B}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{Z}(\nu))\text{ is $B$-flat for all $i\geq 0$, $\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}$}\Big\}.

We stratify this functor in terms of “Hilbert functions” for all the cohomologies.
For a tuple of functions
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
, we define the subfunctor

\fFib_{\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}/A}^{\mathbf{h}}(B)\,:=\,\left\{Z\in\fFib_{\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}/A}(B)\;\begin{array}[]{|l}\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\big(\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{Z_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\nu))\big)=h_{i}(\nu)\\
\text{for all $0\leq i\leq r$, $\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}$, $\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(B)$}\end{array}\right\},

where
\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):=B_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}B_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the residue field of
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(B)
and
Z_{\mathfrak{p}}:=Z\times_{{\text{Spec}}(B)}{\text{Spec}}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))
is the corresponding fiber.
The following theorem is the main result of
[
5
]
and yields the existence of the
fiber-full scheme
.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
5
, Theorem A]
)
.

Assume
Section 2
.
Let
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
be a tuple of functions and suppose that
P_{\mathbf{h}}:=\sum_{i=0}^{r}{(-1)}^{r}h_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}[m]
is a numerical polynomial.
Then, there is a quasi-projective
A
-scheme
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}/A}
that represents the functor
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}/A}
and that is a locally closed subscheme of the Hilbert scheme
\text{Hilb}^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}/A}
.

A direct consequence of this theorem is that the functor
\fFib_{\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}/A}
is represented by a scheme given as the disjoint union
{\text{Fib}}_{\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}/A}:=\bigsqcup_{\mathbf{h}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}}{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}/A}
.
We refer to both
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}/A}
and
{\text{Fib}}_{\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}/A}
as fiber-full schemes.

3.
The fiber-full scheme for homogeneous ideals

In this section, we introduce a version of the fiber-full scheme that works for homogeneous ideals.
This scheme provides a cohomological stratification of the
multigraded Hilbert scheme

[
12
]
of Haiman and Sturmfels.
The multigraded Hilbert scheme was also constructed in
[
1
]
and called the
homogeneous Hilbert scheme
.
Here we continue using
Section 2
.
To simplify notation, for any
A
-algebra
B
, let
R_{B}
be the standard graded polynomial ring
R_{B}:=R\otimes_{A}B
.

Following
[
12
]
, given a function
g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
, we consider the covariant functor
\fHS_{R/A}^{g}:(A\text{-alg})\rightarrow(\text{Sets})
given by

\fHS_{R/A}^{g}(B)\,:=\,\left\{I\subset R_{B}\text{ homogeneous ideal}\;\begin{array}[]{|l}\left[R_{B}/I\right]_{\nu}\text{ is locally free over $B$}\\
\text{of constant rank $g(\nu)$ for all $\nu\geq 0$}\end{array}\right\}.

By
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
(also, see
[
1
]
), there is a projective
A
-scheme
{\text{HS}}_{R/A}^{g}
that represents the functor
\fHS_{R/A}^{g}
.
The multigraded Hilbert scheme
{\text{HS}}_{R/A}^{g}
is, by construction, the parameter space for homogeneous ideals with Hilbert function
g
.
We denote by
{\text{HS}}_{R/A}:=\bigsqcup_{g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}}{\text{HS}}^{g}_{R/A}
the parameter space of homogeneous ideals that give a flat quotient.

We now stratify the multigraded Hilbert scheme in cohomological terms.
Given a function
g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
and a tuple of functions
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r+1}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+2}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+2}
, we define a subfunctor of
\fHS_{R/A}^{g}
given by

\fFib_{R/A}^{g,\mathbf{h}}(B)\,:=\,\left\{I\in\fHS_{R/A}^{g}(B)\;\begin{array}[]{|l}\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\left(R_{B}/I\right)\right]_{\nu}\text{ is locally free over $B$ of}\\
\text{constant rank $h_{i}(\nu)$ for all $0\leq i\leq r+1$, $\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}$}\end{array}\right\}.

Thus,
\fFib_{R/A}^{g,\mathbf{h}}
provides a cohomological stratification of
\fHS_{R/A}^{g}
.
By using the same techniques developed in
[
5
]
, we can show that the functor
\fFib_{R/A}^{g,\mathbf{h}}
is representable.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume
Section 2
.
Let
g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be a function and
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r+1}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+2}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+2}
be a tuple of functions.
Then, there is a quasi-projective
A
-scheme
{\text{Fib}}^{g,\mathbf{h}}_{R/A}
that represents the functor
\fFib^{g,\mathbf{h}}_{R/A}
and that is a locally closed subscheme of the multigraded Hilbert scheme
{\text{HS}}^{g}_{R/A}
.

Proof.

The proof follows essentially along the same lines of
[
5
, Theorem 5.4]
.
By the existence of the multigraded Hilbert scheme
[
12
]
, the functor
\fHS_{R/A}^{g}
is represented by the projective
A
-scheme
{\text{HS}}_{R/A}^{g}
.
Since both functors
\fHS_{R/A}^{g}
and
\fFib^{g,\mathbf{h}}_{R/A}
are Zariski sheaves, we may substitute
{\text{HS}}_{R/A}^{g}
by an affine open subscheme
{\text{Spec}}(L)\subset{\text{HS}}_{R/A}^{g}
.
Consider the universal ideal

representing the subfunctor
\mathcal{F}_{L}:=h_{{\text{Spec}}(L)}
of
\fHS_{R/A}^{g}=h_{{\text{HS}}_{R/A}^{g}}
.
In other words, for any

-algebra

and any

, there is a classifying

-homomorphism

such that

.
Finally, the representability is achieved by invoking
[
5
, Theorem 2.16]
and restricting to the locally closed subscheme
{\text{FLoc}}_{R_{L}/I_{L}}^{\mathbf{h}}\subset{\text{Spec}}(L)
.
Indeed,
{\text{FLoc}}_{R_{L}/I_{L}}^{\mathbf{h}}
satisfies the condition that, for any homomorphism

, each
\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R_{L}/I_{L}\otimes_{L}B)\right]_{\nu}
is locally free over

if and only if

can be factored through
{\text{FLoc}}_{R_{L}/I_{L}}^{\mathbf{h}}
.
∎

We say that
{\text{Fib}}_{R/A}^{g,\mathbf{h}}
is the
fiber-full scheme
parametrizing homogeneous ideals with Hilbert function

and cohomological data
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r+1})
.
Then

{\text{Fib}}_{R/A}\,:=\,\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\\
\mathbf{h}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+2}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+2}\end{subarray}}{\text{Fib}}_{R/A}^{g,\mathbf{h}}

is the
fiber-full scheme
that parametrizes all homogeneous ideals giving a flat quotient with flat local cohomology modules.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
I\subset R
be a homogeneous ideal such that

is
A
-flat and
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I)
is
A
-flat for all

.
Let

and
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r+1}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+2}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+2}
, and suppose that

It then follows that

has a Hilbert polynomial equal to

(see

4.
Tangent-obstruction theory for the fiber-full scheme

In this section, we provide a tangent-obstruction theory for the fiber-full scheme.
We compute the tangent space and determine some sufficient conditions for the existence of obstructions.
There is a little bit of overlap as we treat both fiber-full schemes
{\text{Fib}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}/\mathbb{k}}
(parametrizing closed subschemes) and
{\text{Fib}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
(parametrizing homogeneous ideals), and the techniques to study both are fairly similar.
For organizational purposes, we divide the section into two subsections.
Throughout the entire section, we use the following setup.

Setup 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring,
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}=\text{Proj}(R)
, and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})\subset R
be the graded irrelevant ideal.
Consider the parameters spaces
{\text{Fib}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}/\mathbb{k}}
and
{\text{Fib}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
introduced in
Section 2
and
Section 3
, respectively.

Here are a couple of basic remarks that we will need.

Remark 4.2
.

Given a closed subscheme
Z\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
, the tangent space of
\text{Hilb}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}/\mathbb{k}}
at the corresponding point
[Z]
is given by
T_{[Z]}\text{Hilb}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}/\mathbb{k}}={\text{Hom}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}(\mathcal{I}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
.
Since
{\text{Fib}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}/\mathbb{k}}
is a locally closed subscheme of
\text{Hilb}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}/\mathbb{k}}
, the tangent space
T_{[Z]}{\text{Fib}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}/\mathbb{k}}
of
{\text{Fib}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}/\mathbb{k}}
at
[Z]
is a
\mathbb{k}
-vector subspace of
{\text{Hom}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}(\mathcal{I}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
.

Remark 4.3
.

Given a homogeneous ideal
I\subset R
, the tangent space of
{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
at the corresponding point
[I]
is given by
T_{[I]}{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}=\left[{\text{Hom}}_{R}(I,R/I)\right]_{0}
.
Since
{\text{Fib}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
is a locally closed subscheme of
{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
, the tangent space
T_{[I]}{\text{Fib}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
of
{\text{Fib}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
at
[I]
is a
\mathbb{k}
-vector subspace of
\left[{\text{Hom}}_{R}(I,R/I)\right]_{0}
.

Remark 4.4

(Tangent-obstruction theory of a functor)
.

We recall from
[
11
, Chapter 6]
the definition of a tangent-obstruction theory for a deformation functor.
Let
\mathcal{F}:({\text{Art}}_{\mathbb{k}})\rightarrow(\text{Sets})
be a deformation functor from the category of Artinian local
\mathbb{k}
-algebras with residue field
\mathbb{k}
to the category of sets.
A
tangent-obstruction theory
for
\mathcal{F}
is a pair of
\mathbb{k}
-vector spaces
(\text{T},\text{Ob})
such that for every small extension
0\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}\rightarrow C^{\prime}\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0
in
{\text{Art}}_{\mathbb{k}}
and every
u\in\mathcal{F}(C)
we have:

(1)

There is an element
\text{ob}(u,C^{\prime})\in\text{Ob}\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathfrak{a}
such that
\text{ob}(u,C^{\prime})=0
if and only if there is some lifting
u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}(C^{\prime})
of
u
.

(2)

If
\text{ob}(u,C^{\prime})=0
, then
T\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathfrak{a}
acts transitively on the set of liftings of
u
into
C^{\prime}
.

4.1.
The fiber-full scheme
{\text{Fib}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
for homogeneous ideals

Given a homogeneous ideal
I_{0}\subset R
, we consider the
local fiber-full functor

\fFib_{I_{0}}:({\text{Art}}_{\mathbb{k}})\rightarrow(\text{Sets})
given by

\fFib_{I_{0}}(A)\,:=\,\big\{I\in\fFib_{R/\mathbb{k}}(A)\mid I\otimes_{A}\mathbb{k}\cong I_{0}\big\}.

We start by giving some preparatory results.
We provide a base-change lemma that will be useful, and that is part of the folklore (cf.
[
2
, Exercise 6.1.10, Proposition 6.1.11]
).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
B
be a Noetherian ring and
S=B[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring. Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module that is flat over
B
.
Let
c=\max\big\{i\mid\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)\neq 0\big\}
.
Then, we have a base change isomorphism

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)\otimes_{B}N\,\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M\otimes_{B}N)

for any
B
-module
N
and for all
i\geq c
.

Proof.

Let
c^{\prime}
be an integer such that
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M\otimes_{B}N)=0
for all
i\geq c^{\prime}+1
and any
B
-module
N
.
Fix one such
B
-module
N
.
Pick a free
B
-presentation
F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0
of
N
, and let
K=\text{Im}(F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0})
be the corresponding module of syzygies.
The
B
-flatness of
M
yields a short exact sequence
0\rightarrow M\otimes_{B}K\rightarrow M\otimes_{B}F_{0}\rightarrow M\otimes_{B}N\rightarrow 0
, and so we get an exact sequence in cohomology

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{c^{\prime}}(M\otimes_{B}K)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{c^{\prime}}(M\otimes_{B}F_{0})\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{c^{\prime}}(M\otimes_{B}N)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{c^{\prime}+1}(M\otimes_{B}K)=0.

This gives the following exact sequence

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{c^{\prime}}(M)\otimes_{B}F_{1}\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{c^{\prime}}(M)\otimes_{B}F_{0}\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{c^{\prime}}(M\otimes_{B}N)\rightarrow 0,

and as a consequence, the isomorphism
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{c^{\prime}}(M)\otimes_{B}N\xrightarrow{\cong}\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{c^{\prime}}(M\otimes_{B}N)
.

It is clear that
c\leq c^{\prime}
.
On the other hand, the already proved isomorphism
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{c^{\prime}}(M)\otimes_{B}N\xrightarrow{\cong}\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{c^{\prime}}(M\otimes_{B}N)
shows that
c\geq\min\big\{c^{\prime}\mid\text{$\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M\otimes_{B}N)=0$ for all $i\geq c^{\prime}+1$ and any $B$-module $N$}\big\}
.
Therefore, the result of the lemma holds.
∎

The following lemma gives another equivalent characterization of fiber-fullness.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(B,\mathfrak{b},\mathbb{k})
be a Noetherian local ring and
S=B[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring.
Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal such that
S/I
is flat over
B
, and
I_{0}=I\otimes_{B}\mathbb{k}\subset R
be the homogeneous ideal corresponding with the closed fiber.
Then,
S/I
is fiber-full over
B
if and only if the natural map

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\left(I\right)\,\longrightarrow\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\left(I_{0}\right)

is surjective for all
i\geq 0
.

Proof.

By the local criterion for fiber-fullness (see
Theorem 2.4
),
S/I
is fiber-full over
B
if and only if
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/I\otimes_{B}B/\mathfrak{b}^{q})\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})
is surjective for all
i\geq 0,q\geq 1
.
The base change property of fiber-full modules (see
Section 2
) implies that the latter condition is equivalent to the surjectivity of the map
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/I)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})
for all
i\geq 0
.

First, suppose that
I_{0}=0
.
Since
S/I
is
B
-flat,
I=0
and the fiber-fullness condition holds.

Next, we assume that
I_{0}\neq 0
.
By
§4.1
,
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r+1}(S/I)\otimes_{B}\mathbb{k}\xrightarrow{\cong}\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r+1}(R/I_{0})=0
, and then Nakayama’s lemma implies
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r+1}(S/I)=0
.
Thus,
§4.1
gives the surjectivity of the map
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r}(S/I)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r}(R/I_{0})
.
From the short exact sequences
0\rightarrow I\rightarrow S\rightarrow S/I\rightarrow 0
and
0\rightarrow I_{0}\rightarrow R\rightarrow R/I_{0}\rightarrow 0
, we obtain the short exact sequences
0\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r}(S/I)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r+1}(I)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r+1}(S)\rightarrow 0
and
0\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r}(R/I_{0})\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r+1}(I_{0})\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r+1}(R)\rightarrow 0
and the isomorphisms
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I_{0})\cong\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i-1}(R/I_{0})
and
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I)\cong\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i-1}(S/I)
for all
i\leq r
.
Therefore,
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/I)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})
is surjective for all
i\geq 0
if and only if
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I_{0})
is surjective for all
i\geq 0
.
This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

5.
One-parameter flat families with constant cohomology

In this section, we study one-parameter flat families, and we determine an interesting class of families that keep the cohomologies constant.
These results seem of particular importance for the case of Gröbner degenerations. We begin by fixing the following setup for the rest of the section.

Setup 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
R
be a standard graded polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})\subset R
be the graded irrelevant ideal.
Let
A=\mathbb{k}[t]
be a polynomial ring, and
B=A_{(t)}
be the local ring at the prime ideal
(t)\subset A
.
Let
S=A[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]\cong A\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}R
be a polynomial ring with grading induced by
R
.
For each
q\geq 1
, set
B_{q}:=A/t^{q}A
and
S_{q}:=S/t^{q}S\cong B_{q}\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}R
.

We say that
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{k}}\cong\text{Proj}(S)
is a one-parameter flat family of closed subschemes with special fiber
Z_{0}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
and general fiber
Z_{1}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{k}}
is a closed subscheme flat over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{k}}
.

(2)

Z_{0}\cong\mathcal{Z}\times_{{\text{Spec}}(A)}{\text{Spec}}(A/tA)
and
Z_{1}\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\text{Spec}}(\mathbb{k}[t,t^{-1}])\cong\mathcal{Z}\times_{{\text{Spec}}(A)}{\text{Spec}}(\mathbb{k}[t,t^{-1}])
.

In particular, this implies that for any
0\neq\alpha\in\mathbb{k}
, the fiber
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}:=\mathcal{Z}\times_{{\text{Spec}}(A)}{\text{Spec}}(\mathbb{k}[t]/(t-\alpha))\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
coincides with
Z_{1}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
.
We say that the family
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\times\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1}
has constant cohomology
if the equality

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{Z_{0}}(\nu))\right)\,=\,\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{Z_{1}}(\nu))\right)

holds for all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

In more algebraic terms, we say that
\mathcal{I}\subset S
is a one-parameter flat family of homogeneous ideals with special fiber
I_{0}\subset R
and general fiber
I_{1}\subset R
if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\mathcal{I}\subset S
is a homogeneous ideal such that
S/\mathcal{I}
is flat over
A
.

(2)

R/I_{0}\cong S/\mathcal{I}\otimes_{A}A/tA
and
R/I_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathbb{k}[t,t^{-1}]\cong S/\mathcal{I}\otimes_{A}\mathbb{k}[t,t^{-1}]
.

This implies that for any
0\neq\alpha\in\mathbb{k}
, the fiber
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}:=\mathcal{I}\otimes_{A}\mathbb{k}[t]/(t-\alpha)\subset R
coincides with
I_{1}\subset R
.
We say that the family
\mathcal{I}\subset S
has constant cohomology
if the equality

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})\right]_{\nu}\right)\,=\,\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{1})\right]_{\nu}\right)

holds for all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The prime example of one-parameter flat families is that of
Gröbner degenerations
(see
[
10
, §15.7]

The following proposition deals with the flat extensions to

R

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

The main result of this section is the following sufficient condition for a one-parameter flat family of homogeneous ideals to have constant cohomology.

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume
Section 5
.
Let
\mathcal{I}\subset S
be a one-parameter flat family of homogeneous ideals with special fiber
I_{0}\subset R
and general fiber
I_{1}\subset R
.
If the zero map is the only zero-degree homogeneous
R
-linear map from
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i-1}(R/I_{0})
to
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})
for all
1\leq i\leq r
, i.e.,

\left[{\text{Hom}}_{R}\Big(\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i-1}(R/I_{0}),\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})\Big)\right]_{0}=0\quad\text{for all $1\leq i\leq r$},

then the family
\mathcal{I}
has constant cohomology.

Proof.

First, note that we get
\left[{\text{Hom}}_{R}\left(\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I_{0}),\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})\right)\right]_{0}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq r
, because there is an isomorphism
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i-1}(R/I_{0})\cong\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I_{0})
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.

We prove by induction that
J_{q}:=\mathcal{I}\otimes_{A}B_{q}\subset S_{q}
is a homogeneous ideal such that

S_{q}/J_{q}
is fiber-full over
B_{q}
for all
q\geq 1
.
The case
q=1
is clear.
Suppose that
S_{q}/J_{q}
is fiber-full over
B_{q}
.
Thus,
Section 5
gives an
R
-linear map
\Phi_{J_{q}}:I_{0}\rightarrow S_{q-1}/J_{q-1}\otimes_{B_{q-1}}(t)B_{q}\cong S_{q-1}/J_{q-1}
such that
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\Phi_{J_{q}})=0
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.
On the other hand, since
S_{q+1}/J_{q+1}
is
B_{q+1}
-flat, due to
Section 5
, there is an
R
-linear map
\Phi_{J_{q+1}}:I_{0}\rightarrow S_{q}/J_{q}\otimes_{B_{q}}(t)B_{q+1}\cong S_{q}/J_{q}
such that
S_{q+1}/J_{q+1}
is fiber-full over
B_{q+1}
if and only if
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\Phi_{J_{q+1}})=0
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.
Note that by construction the
R
-linear maps
\Phi_{J_{q}}
and
\Phi_{J_{q+1}}
are
R
-linear homogeneous of degree zero, and that they fit into the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R/I_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}(t^{q-1})B_{q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{q}/J_{q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{q-1}/J_{q-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\Phi_{J_{q+1}}
\Phi_{J_{q}}

Since by assumption
S_{q}/J_{q}
is fiber-full over
B_{q}
, the natural map
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S_{q}/J_{q})\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S_{q-1}/J_{q-1})
is surjective for all
i\geq 0
, and so we obtain the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I_{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}(t^{q-1})B_{q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S_{q}/J_{q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S_{q-1}/J_{q-1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\Phi_{J_{q+1}})
\qquad\;\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\Phi_{J_{q}})

As we already know that
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\Phi_{J_{q}})=0
, it follows that the map
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\Phi_{J_{q+1}})
factors as a map

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\Phi_{J_{q+1}})\,:\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I_{0})\longrightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}(t^{q-1})B_{q})\cong\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0}).

This latter map is
R
-linear homogeneous of degree zero from
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I_{0})
to
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})
, and so it holds that
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\Phi_{J_{q+1}})=0
.
Thus,
Section 5
implies that

S_{q+1}/J_{q+1}
is fiber-full over
B_{q+1}
.
So, the induction step is complete.

Since we have shown that
S_{q}/J_{q}
is fiber-full over
B_{q}
for all
q\geq 1
, the local criterion for fiber-fullness (see
Theorem 2.4
) implies that
S/\mathcal{I}\otimes_{A}B
is fiber-full over
B
.
Due to the flatness of cohomologies and the arbitrary base change under the fiber-full condition (see
Section 2
), we obtain the equalities

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})\right]_{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\left(S/\mathcal{I}\otimes_{A}B\right)\right]_{\nu}\otimes_{B}B/tB\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}(t)}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\left(S/\mathcal{I}\otimes_{A}B\right)\right]_{\nu}\otimes_{B}\mathbb{k}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{1})\right]_{\nu}\right)

for all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
This finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

The following corollary provides an effective method to discover flat families with constant cohomology.

Corollary 5.4
.

Assume
Section 5
.

This is a consequence of

6.
A local comparison of the fiber-full scheme and the Hilbert scheme

The goal of this section is to compare local deformation functors of
{\text{Fib}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
and
{\text{HS}}_{R/\mathbb{k}}
.
The results of this section are inspired by our study of one-parameter flat families in
Section 5
.
The setup below is used throughout this section.

Setup 6.1
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring, and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})\subset R
be the graded irrelevant ideal.

Let
I_{0}\subset R
be a homogeneous ideal.
We consider the deformation functors
\fFib_{I_{0}}:({\text{Art}}_{\mathbb{k}})\rightarrow(\text{Sets})
and
\fHS_{I_{0}}:({\text{Art}}_{\mathbb{k}})\rightarrow(\text{Sets})
given by

\fFib_{I_{0}}(A)\,=\,\big\{I\in\fFib_{R/\mathbb{k}}(A)\mid I\otimes_{A}\mathbb{k}\cong I_{0}\big\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\fHS_{I_{0}}(A)\,=\,\big\{I\in\fHS_{R/\mathbb{k}}(A)\mid I\otimes_{A}\mathbb{k}\cong I_{0}\big\},

respectively.
By construction
\fFib_{I_{0}}
is a subfunctor of
\fHS_{I_{0}}
.
The following theorem gives a family of ideals for which
\fFib_{I_{0}}
and
\fHS_{I_{0}}
coincide.

Theorem 6.2
.

Assume
Section 6
.
Let
I_{0}\subset R
be a homogeneous ideal such that

\left[{\text{Hom}}_{R}\Big(\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i-1}(R/I_{0}),\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})\Big)\right]_{0}=0\quad\text{for all $1\leq i\leq r$}.

Then the local deformation functors
\fFib_{I_{0}}
and
\fHS_{I_{0}}
coincide.

Proof.

The case
I_{0}=0
is clear.
Thus we assume that
I_{0}\neq 0
, and so
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r+1}(R/I_{0})=0
.
Therefore, we can actually assume that
\left[{\text{Hom}}_{R}\Big(\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i-1}(R/I_{0}),\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})\Big)\right]_{0}=0
for all
i\geq 1
.

We need to prove that
\fFib_{I_{0}}(A)=\fHS_{I_{0}}(A)
for any
A\in{\text{Art}}_{\mathbb{k}}
.
We proceed by induction on
\text{length}(A)
.
The base case
\text{length}(A)=1
is clear because we necessarily have
A=\mathbb{k}
.
Thus, we assume that
\text{length}(A)>1
.
We may choose an ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset A
generated by a socle element of
A
to obtain a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}\rightarrow A\rightarrow A^{\prime}\rightarrow 0

with
\mathfrak{a}\cong\mathbb{k}
and
\text{length}(A^{\prime})=\text{length}(A)-1
.
Let
S=A[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
and
S^{\prime}=A^{\prime}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
.
Choose
I\in\fHS_{I_{0}}(A)
and set
I^{\prime}=I\otimes_{A}A^{\prime}\in\in\fHS_{I_{0}}(A^{\prime})
.
By tensoring with
-\otimes_{A}S/I
, since
S/I
is flat over
A
, we obtain the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow R/I_{0}\rightarrow S/I\rightarrow S^{\prime}/I^{\prime}\rightarrow 0.

Then we get an induced long exact sequence in cohomology

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i-1}(S^{\prime}/I^{\prime})\,\xrightarrow{\delta_{i-1}}\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})\,\rightarrow\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/I)\,\rightarrow\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S^{\prime}/I^{\prime})\,\xrightarrow{\delta_{i}}\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i+1}(R/I_{0}).

The inductive hypothesis and
Section 2
yield the isomorphism
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S^{\prime}/I^{\prime})\otimes_{A^{\prime}}\mathbb{k}\xrightarrow{\cong}\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})
.
Then the map
\delta_{i}
gets the following factorization

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S^{\prime}/I^{\prime})\;\twoheadrightarrow\;\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S^{\prime}/I^{\prime})\otimes_{A^{\prime}}\mathbb{k}\cong\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})\;\xrightarrow{\beta_{i}}\;\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i+1}(R/I_{0})

where
\beta_{i}
is an
R
-linear map of degree
0
.
The condition
\left[{\text{Hom}}_{R}\left(\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0}),\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i+1}(R/I_{0})\right)\right]_{0}=0
implies that
\beta_{i}=0
, hence
\delta_{i}=0
.
We have proved that the natural map
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/I)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S^{\prime}/I^{\prime})
is surjective, and the natural map
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S^{\prime}/I^{\prime})\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})
is surjective by induction.
Therefore, it follows that the natural map

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/I)\,\rightarrow\,\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R/I_{0})

is also surjective for all
i\geq 0
.
Finally, by applying
Theorem 2.4
, we obtain that
S/I
is fiber-full over
S
.
This shows that
I\in\fFib_{I_{0}}(A)
, as required.
∎

7.
Examples

In this section, we give several examples to illustrate the main results in this paper.
We start by providing a couple of examples that indicate the broad applicability of
Theorem 5.3
.
We are particularly interested in examples of non-squarefree monomial ideals that satisfy the criterion given in
Theorem 5.3
.
These families of ideals are a further advance in the context of Gröbner degenerations (that preserve cohomology), as they are not covered by the result of Conca-Varbaro
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
.
By
[
7
, Proposition 2.3]
,
squarefree monomial ideals are a particular class of ideals whose quotients give a
cohomologically full ring
(
[
8
,
16
]
).
It should be mentioned that the same aforementioned result of Conca-Varbaro also holds for homogeneous ideals whose quotient is a cohomologically full ring (see also
[
7
, Remark 2.9]
).

Remark 7.1
.

Assume
Section 5
.
Let
\mathcal{I}\subset S
be a one-parameter flat family of homogeneous ideals with special fiber
I_{0}\subset R
and general fiber
I_{1}\subset R
.
If
R/I_{0}
is cohomologically full, then one can show that
\mathcal{I}
has constant cohomology by using
Theorem 2.4
.

We begin with an example on residual intersections.

Example 7.2

(Residual intersections)
.

Consider the
2\times n
generic matrix

M=\begin{pmatrix}x_{11}&x_{12}&\cdots&x_{1n}\\
x_{21}&x_{22}&\cdots&x_{2n}\end{pmatrix}

over
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{11},\dots,x_{2n}]
and let
I=I_{2}(M)
. Let
g:=n-1
denote the codimension and
\ell=2n-3
be the analytic spread. Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}
be quadrics in
I
that generate a minimal reduction. Let
J_{i}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{i})\subseteq I
and assume that
J_{i}:I
is a geometric
i
-residual intersection
i\leq\ell-1
. For
n\leq i\leq\ell-1
, the ideal
J_{i}:I
is not Cohen-Macaulay and
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
shows that

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{j}(R/(J_{i}:I))\neq 0\text{ if and only if }j\in\{2n-i-3,2n-i\}.

Thus the hypothesis of
Theorem 5.3
is satisfied and it follows that, for each
n\leq i\leq\ell-1
, any one-parameter degeneration to
J_{i}:I
has constant cohomology.

The following example gives two simple ideals whose quotients are not cohomologically full rings, but do satisfy the criterion of
Theorem 5.3
.

Example 7.3
.

We will show that the cohomology is constant in one-parameter flat families that have the following ideals
R=\mathbb{k}[x,y,z]
as the special fiber

I_{1}=(x^{2},y^{2},xy,xz,yz)\,\,\text{ and }\,\,I_{2}=(x^{2}y,xz^{2},y^{2}z,z^{3}).

Both ideals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are
1
-dimensional and not Cohen-Macaulay, and so both
R/I_{1}
and
R/I_{2}
are not cohomologically full rings (see
[
8
, Corollary 2.6]
).
Consider the Čech complex

\small C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet}:\hskip 9.24994pt0\to R\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\\
1\end{pmatrix}}R_{x}\oplus R_{y}\oplus R_{z}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&0\\
-1&0&1\\
0&-1&1\end{pmatrix}}R_{xy}\oplus R_{xz}\oplus R_{yz}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-1\\
1\end{pmatrix}^{T}}R_{xyz}\to 0.

(1)

Let
\overline{R}=R/I_{1}
and notice that the Čech complex
C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet}\otimes_{R}\overline{R}
collapses to

0\to\overline{R}\to\overline{R}_{z}\to 0.

It follows that the non-zero cohomology groups are
\text{H}^{0}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(\overline{R})=(x,y)\overline{R}\cong\left(\mathbb{k}(-1)\right)^{2}
and

\text{H}^{1}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(\overline{R})=\overline{R}_{z}/\overline{R}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{k}(i)
.
Thus
\left[{\text{Hom}}_{R}(\text{H}^{0}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(\overline{R}),\text{H}^{1}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(\overline{R}))\right]_{0}=0
and by
Theorem 5.3
cohomology is preserved under flat degenerations to the ideal
I_{1}\subset R
.

(2)

After setting
\overline{R}=R/I_{2}
, the Čech complex
C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet}
collapses to

0\to\overline{R}\xrightarrow{\varphi=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\end{pmatrix}^{T}}\overline{R}_{x}\oplus\overline{R}_{y}\to 0.

The non-zero cohomology groups are in positions
0
and
1
, with

\text{H}^{0}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(\overline{R})=(z^{2},yz)\overline{R}\cong\left(\mathbb{k}(-2)\right)^{2}\oplus\left(\mathbb{k}(-3)\right)^{2}
.
Moreover, one can check that
{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}\left(\overline{R}_{x}\oplus\overline{R}_{y}\right)
lies in the image of
\varphi
. It follows that
\text{H}^{1}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(\overline{R})
is supported in degrees
\leq 1
and thus
\left[{\text{Hom}}_{R}(\text{H}^{0}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(\overline{R}),\text{H}^{1}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(\overline{R}))\right]_{0}=0
as required.
Due to
Theorem 5.3
cohomology is preserved under flat degenerations to the ideal
I_{2}\subset R
.

We now test the applicability of
Section 5
with the following simple example of a non-squarefree monomial ideal.

Example 7.4
.

Consider the ideal
I=(xy^{2},xz)\subseteq R=\mathbb{k}[x,y,z]
.
By using the Taylor resolution, we can compute that
\text{Ext}_{R}^{1}(R/I,R)\cong\frac{R}{(x)}(1)
,
\text{Ext}_{R}^{2}(R/I,R)\cong\frac{R}{(y^{2},z)}(4)
and that all the other modules
\text{Ext}_{R}^{i}(R/I,R)
vanish.
In this case, we even have that
{\text{Hom}}_{R}\left(\frac{R}{(x)},\frac{R}{(y^{2},z)}\right)=0
.
Therefore,
Section 5
implies that cohomology is preserved under flat degenerations to the ideal
I\subset R
.
Notice that
\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{1}(R/I)\right]_{\nu}\neq 0
for
\nu\leq 1
and
\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{2}(R/I)\right]_{\nu}\neq 0
for
\nu\leq-2
, but we have
{\text{Hom}}_{R}\left(\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{1}(R/I),\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{2}(R/I)\right)=0
.

Example 7.5
.

Here are two more examples for which degenerations to them preserve cohomology:
I_{1}=(x^{3},xy,xz+y^{2})\subseteq\mathbb{k}[x,y,z]
and
I_{2}=(x_{1}^{2},x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}x_{4},x_{2}^{2},x_{3}^{2},x_{3}x_{4})\subseteq\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{4}]
. The verification is analogous to the previous examples.

Example 7.6
.

Let
I\subseteq R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a squarefree monomial ideal such that
\dim(R/I)\leq 2
.
Then the Stanley-Reisner complex,
\Delta
, associated to
I
is at most one dimensional.
Thus for any subcomplex
H\subseteq\Delta
we have the reduced cohomology
\widetilde{\text{H}}^{j}(H;\mathbb{k})=0
for all
j\geq 1
. We want to show that there are no non-zero maps
\left[\text{H}^{i-1}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R/I)\right]_{\mathbf{b}}\to\left[\text{H}^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R/I)\right]_{\mathbf{b}}
for any
i\geq 1
and
\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
.
By Hochster’s formula
[
19
, Theorem 13.13]
this correspond to a map

\widetilde{\text{H}}^{i-\lvert\sigma\rvert-2}(\text{lk}_{\Delta}\sigma;\mathbb{k})\longrightarrow\widetilde{\text{H}}^{i-\lvert\sigma\rvert-1}(\text{lk}_{\Delta}\sigma;\mathbb{k})

where
\sigma=\text{supp}(\mathbf{b})
. However, as we just mentioned, since
\Delta
has at most dimension
1
, either the domain or codomain of the map is
0
. Thus the hypothesis of
Theorem 5.3
is satisfied and we obtain the constancy of cohomology for flat degenerations to
I\subset R
.
This is a simple illustrative instance of the result of Conca-Varbaro
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
.

It is also possible that
Theorem 5.3
fails while the ideal is monomial squarefree.

Example 7.7
.

Consider the squarefree monomial ideal in
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1}\dots x_{6}]
given by

I=(x_{1}x_{4},x_{2}x_{4},x_{3}x_{4},x_{1}x_{5},x_{2}x_{5},x_{3}x_{5},x_{1}x_{6},x_{2}x_{6},x_{3}x_{6},x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}).

This is the Stanley-Reisner ideal associated the simplicial complex
\Delta
which is a disjoint union of a 3-cycle and a 2-simplex:

By
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
, any one-parameter flat degeneration to
I
keeps cohomology constant.
On the other hand, we will show that there is a non-zero homogeneous
R
-module homomorphism of degree zero
\left[\text{H}^{1}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R/I)\right]_{0}\to\left[\text{H}^{2}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R/I)\right]_{0}
.
By using Hochster’s formula
[
19
, Theorem 13.13]
, we obtain

\text{H}^{1}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R/I)=\left[\text{H}^{1}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R/I)\right]_{0}=\left[\text{H}^{1}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R/I)\right]_{(0,\dots,0)}\cong\widetilde{\text{H}}^{0}(\text{link}_{\Delta}(\emptyset);\mathbb{k})=\mathbb{k}

and

\left[\text{H}^{2}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R/I)\right]_{\geq 0}=\left[\text{H}^{2}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R/I)\right]_{(0,\dots,0)}\cong\widetilde{\text{H}}^{1}(\text{link}_{\Delta}(\emptyset);\mathbb{k})=\mathbb{k}.

Thus the map on
\mathbb{k}
-vector spaces
\mathbb{k}\cong\text{H}^{1}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R/I)\to\left[\text{H}^{2}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R/I)\right]_{0}\cong\mathbb{k}
given by
1\mapsto 1
extends to a homogeneous
R
-module homomorphism of degree zero.
This invalidates the hypothesis of
Theorem 5.3
.

Our final example involves the Hilbert scheme of curves of degree

that every

induces an exact sequence

where

is a locally Cohen-Macaulay curve of degree
4
and
X^{\prime}
has arithmetic genus
0
,
1
or
3
. If the genus of

is
0
then

is either an extremal or a subextremal curve

. By

the curve

is an extremal curve if and only if

h^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{X}(v))=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }v\leq-1\\
1&\text{ if }0\leq v\leq 2\\
0&\text{ if }v\geq 3\end{cases}\quad\text{ and }\quad h^{2}(\mathcal{I}_{X}(v))=\begin{cases}-1-4\nu&\text{ if }v\leq-1\\
1&\text{ if }v=0\\

Using the ideal sheaf long exact sequence we deduce that

, the corresponding fiber-full scheme, namely

Example 7.9
.

Let

be a subscheme parameterized by
{\text{Fib}}^{(\mathbf{h},0,0)}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}

,

is a disjoint union of a line and a plane cubic or

are forms in
x_{2},x_{3}
of degrees

and

the fiber-full scheme
{\text{Fib}}^{(\mathbf{h},0,0)}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}

It is easy to see that a disjoint union of a line and a plane cubic is smooth inside the Hilbert scheme and thus also in the fiber-full scheme. We may assume that

is expressed in the form of Equation

Since

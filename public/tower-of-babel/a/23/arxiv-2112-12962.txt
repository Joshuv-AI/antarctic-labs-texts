Minimum Steps to Reach a Smaller Number in Collatz Process
Daohang Sha

Abstract
. Through computation and analyses, we show that a non-trivial cycle number must be infinite as the cycle length goes to infinity and bounded low and above (10 times low) dependent on the cycle length. A cycle number must be m 1 ≥ 978 m_{1}\geq 978 at the cycle length s = 485 s=485 ; as the cycle length goes up to s = 17087915 s=17087915 , the cycle number must be m 1 ≥ 46449769 821013301 m_{1}\geq 46449769^{821013301} , which is approximately equal to 2 2 × 10 10 2^{2\times 10^{10}} , or 10 6 × 10 9 10^{6\times 10^{9}} .

Lemma 3.1
.

If a certain odd number that can reach to a smaller number than itself after Collatz process, i.e.
F_{q}(m)<m
, then
3^{r}<2^{s}
.

Proof.

Because
m
is an odd number,
\sum_{i=1}^{r}3^{r-i}2^{1(i)-1}>0.

Thus,

\dfrac{3^{r}m}{2^{s}}<\dfrac{3^{r}m+\sum_{i=1}^{r}3^{r-i}2^{1(i)-1}}{2^{s}}=F_{q}(m)<m,

which indicates
3^{r}<2^{s}
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

For infinite numbers
n=2^{s}(3j+k)+m;k=0,1,2,j=0,1,2,3,...,
usually
m<2^{s}
, if
F_{q}(m)<m
, then the process will be in the same way as given
q
to reach a smaller number than
n
, i.e.

(3.3)

F_{q}(n)=\dfrac{3^{r}n+\sum_{i=1}^{r}3^{r-i}2^{1(i)-1}}{2^{s}}<n.

Proof.

Replace
m
in the equation of
3.2
with
n=2^{s}(3j+k)+m
, one can get

\displaystyle F_{q}(n)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{3^{r}n+\sum_{i=1}^{r}3^{r-i}2^{1(i)-1}}{2^{s}}

\displaystyle=3^{r}(3j+k)+\dfrac{3^{r}m+\sum_{i=1}^{r}3^{r-i}2^{1(i)-1}}{2^{s}}

\displaystyle<3^{r}(3j+k)+m

\displaystyle<2^{s}(3j+k)+m

\displaystyle=n.

which completes the proof. Here, we used
F_{q}(m)<m
and
3^{r}<2^{s}
in the above chain of inequalities.

Generic root counts of tropically transverse systems
Isaac Holt and Yue Ren

Abstract
The main mathematical focus of this paper is a class of parametrised polynomial systems that we refer to as being tropically transverse. We show how their generic number of solutions can be expressed as the mixed volume of a modified system. We then provide an alternate proof of a recent result by Borovik et al, on the number of equilibria of coupled nonlinear oscillators using elementary tropical geometry. The proof draws upon a wide range of concepts across tropical geometry, which we will use as an opportunity to give a first overview over various tropical features in OSCAR .

Introduction

Tropical geometry is often described as a piecewise linear analogue of algebraic geometry. Its concepts arise naturally in many applications both inside and outside mathematics. Due to its explicit nature, algorithms and computation have always played a central role in tropical geometry. Prominent examples include disproving strong polynomiality of certain barrier interior points methods in linear optimisation by computing tropicalisations of central paths
ABGJ 18
;
AGV 22
, proving finiteness of certain central configurations in the
N
-body problem by computing tropical prevarieties of their algebraic equations
HM 06
;
HJ 11
, or breakthroughs in the geometry of moduli spaces by computing tropicalisations of linear series
FJP 20
.

This paper addresses the problem of computing the generic number of solutions of a certain class of parametrized polynomial systems, which we call
tropically transverse
. These are a specialisation of the parametrised polynomial systems considered by Kaveh and Khovanskii in their work on Newton-Okounkov bodies
KK 12
, and examples include the polynomials describing the stationary motion of coupled nonlinear oscillators studied by Borovik, Breiding, Del Pino, Michalek and Zilberberg
BBPMZ 23
. One question of particular interest about parametrised polynomial system is that of their generic root count. It may be interesting in its own right, such as the Euclidean distance degree
DHOST 14
or the maximum likelihood degree
CHKS 06
, but it is also a crucial piece of information for solving polynomial systems via numerical algebraic geometry
Bat+ 23
.
We show that the generic root count of tropically transverse systems can be expressed as the mixed volume of a modified system in
Theorem

4.1
, and provide an alternate proof of
BBPMZ 23
, Theorem 5.1
using elementary tropical geometry in
Theorem

5.1
.

Before tackling tropically transverse systems, this paper serves as an introduction and invitation to the tropical package in
OSCAR

OSCAR
and
julia

Definition 1

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{\max}

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\mathbb{Q}\cup\{-\infty\},\oplus,\odot)\text{ with }a\oplus b\coloneqq\max(a,b),a\odot b\coloneqq a+b\text{, or}

2.2
Valued fields

Next, we introduce some tropical notions over valued fields, which are central to many applications of tropical geometry, in particular in and around algebraic geometry.

Convention 1

For the remainder of the paper, let
K
denote a field with a valuation, i.e., a field with a map
\val\colon K^{\ast}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying

1.

\val(a\cdot b)=\val(a)+\val(b)
,

2.

\val(a+b)\geq\min(\val(a),\val(b))
.

This valuation naturally induces a
ring of integers

R\coloneqq\{c\in K\mid\val(c)\geq 0\}
, a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\coloneqq\{c\in R\mid\val(c)>0\}\unlhd R
and a
residue field

\mathfrak{K}\coloneqq R/\mathfrak{m}
.

We further assume that there is a fixed splitting homomorphism
\psi\colon(\val(K^{\ast}),+)\rightarrow(K^{\ast},\cdot)
such that
\val(\psi(r))=r
. Such splitting homomorphism exists for all algebraically closed fields
MS 15
, Lemma 2.1.15
, but also many others. For example:

•

If
\val
is trivial, i.e.,
\val(c)=0
for all
c\in K^{\ast}
, then
\{0\}\rightarrow K^{\ast},0\mapsto 1
is a viable splitting homomorphism.

•

If
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is the field of
p
-adic numbers and
\val
is the usual
p
-adic valuation, then
\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\ast},r\mapsto p^{r}
is a viable splitting homomorphism.

•

If
K=\mathbb{Q}(t)
is the field of rational functions in
t
and
\val
is the
t
-adic valuation, then
\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}(t)^{\ast},r\mapsto t^{r}
is a viable splitting homomorphism.

Following the notation of
MS 15
, we abbreviate
t^{r}\coloneqq\psi(r)
.

Definition 2

To each polynomial
f\in K[x]\coloneqq K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, say
f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
with
c_{\alpha}\in K
, we can assign a tropical polynomial
\trop(f)\in\mathbb{T}[x]
defined as follows:

\trop(f)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bigoplus_{c_{\alpha}\neq 0}\val(c_{\alpha})\odot x^{\odot\alpha}&\text{if }\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{T}_{\min},\\[19.91692pt]
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{c_{\alpha}\neq 0}-\val(c_{\alpha})\odot x^{\odot\alpha}&\text{if }\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{T}_{\max}.\end{cases}

The
initial form
of
f
with respect to a weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is then defined as

\initial_{w}(f)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\trop(c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha})(w)\\
=\trop(f)(w)\end{subarray}}\overline{t^{-\val(c_{\alpha})}c_{\alpha}}\cdot x^{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{K}[x],

where
\overline{(\cdot)}\colon R\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{K}
denotes the quotient map.

The
initial ideal
of an ideal
I\unlhd K[x]
with respect to a weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by

\initial_{w}(I)\coloneqq\langle\initial_{w}(f)\mid f\in I\rangle\unlhd\mathfrak{K}[x].

A
Gröbner basis
of an ideal
I\unlhd K[x]
with respect to a weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a finite set
G\subseteq I
such that
\initial_{w}(I)=\langle\initial_{w}(g)\mid g\in G\rangle
. Note that, any Gröbner basis of
I
is a generating set provided
I
is homogeneous
MS 15
, Remark 2.4.4
.

2.3
Tropical varieties

Next, we recall the notion of tropical varieties. While
MS 15
considers tropicalizations of very affine varieties
V(I)
, we consider tropicalizations of (Laurent) polynomial ideal
I
. Consequently, our tropicalizations are weighted polyhedral complexes instead of supports thereof. The reason for this is two-fold:

1.

From
Section

3
onwards, we are interested in the number of points in
V(I)
counted with multiplicity, so the tropical multiplicities matter.

2.

For
OSCAR
, it is much easier to work with concrete polyhedral complexes rather than supports thereof.

Essentially, our definition of tropical varieties entails part of
MS 15
, Structure Theorem 3.3.5
.

Definition 3

Let
I\unlhd K[x]
be a homogeneous ideal. Then the
Gröbner polyhedron
of
I
around a weight vector
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined to be

C_{w}(I)\coloneqq\overline{\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\initial_{v}(I)=\initial_{w}(I)\}},

and the set of all Gröbner polyhedra is called the
Gröbner complex

\Sigma(I)\coloneqq\{C_{w}(I)\mid w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
. As the name suggest, Gröbner polyhedra are convex polyhedra and the Gröbner complex is a finite polyhedral complex
MS 15
, Theorem 2.5.3
.

Definition 4

The
tropical variety
of an homogeneous ideal
I\unlhd K[x]
is defined to be the following polyhedral complex consisting of all Gröbner cones whose initial ideals are monomial free:

\Trop(I)\coloneqq\{C_{w}(I)\mid\initial_{w}(I)\text{ monomial-free}\}

The
tropical variety
of an arbitrary ideal
I\unlhd K[x]
is defined to be

\Trop(I)\coloneqq\Big\{C_{w}(I^{h})\cap\{e_{0}=0\}\mid C_{w}(I^{h})\in\Trop(I^{h})\Big\},

where
I^{h}\unlhd K[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
denotes the homogenization of
I
in
x_{0}
, whose Gröbner polyhedra
C_{w}(I^{h})
lie in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with standard basis vectors
e_{0},\dots,e_{n}
, and we naturally identify the subspace
\{e_{0}=0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that both definitions coincide for homogeneous ideals.

Moreover, there is a canonical way to assign multiplicities to the maximal polyhedra of
\Trop(I)
via the initial ideals
MS 15
, Definition 3.4.3
that make
\Trop(I)
a balanced polyhedral complex if
I
is prime
MS 15
, Theorem 3.3.5
. For the sake of brevity, we omit their general definition and instead introduce them only for the special classes of tropical varieties that are of immediate interest to us below.

Remark 1

The umbrella term tropical variety extends well beyond the tropicalization of ideals covered in
Definition

4
. Most notably, there is an incredibly rich theory on the moduli of tropical curves, abstract graphs without a fixed embedding into some
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, whose significance extends well beyond the area of tropical geometry itself
Mar 20
.

OSCAR
uses parameters to distinguish between embedded and abstract tropical varieties, as well as min- and max-tropical varieties. Moreover, there are four distinct types of tropical varieties, see Examples
5
to
4

This is because some functions only make sense for some types (e.g., tropical Pluecker vectors for tropical linear spaces), while other functions can be aggressively optimised depending on the type (e.g., stable intersection of tropical hypersurfaces). Embedded tropical varieties are always weighted polyhedral complexes. Abstract tropical varieties depend on the type and may not be defined for all four types.

The significance of stable intersections is the fact that they are the “expected” tropicalisations when taking the sum of two ideals. This is for example captured in the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1

Let
I_{1},I_{2}\unlhd\mathbb{C}[x]
be complete intersections. Suppose that
\Trop(I_{1})
and
\Trop(I_{2})
intersect transversely. Then

\Trop(I_{1}+I_{2})=\Trop(I_{1})\wedge\Trop(I_{2}),

where the equality only holds up to refinement of weighted polyhedral complexes.

Proof

The statement is a consequence of
OP 13
, Corollary 5.1.2
and
OP 13
, Corollary 5.1.3
by Osserman and Payne. The first result shows the equality set-theoretically, while the second result shows that the multiplicities coincide. Both results require
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
to be Cohen-Macaulay, which is implied from
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
being complete intersections.

Theorem

2.1
is a generalisation of the Transverse Intersection Theorem
MS 15
, Theorem 3.4.12
which relies on
OP 13
, Corollary 5.1.2
. From now onward, we will generally consider tropical varieties up to refinement, which does not change the tropical intersection number in
Definition

Tropical geometry of generic root counts

In this section, we recall some basic notions and a result of
HR 22
.

Convention 2

In addition to Convention
1
, we further fix an affine space
K^{m}
, which we refer to as the
parameter space
. Let
K[a]\coloneqq K[a_{1},\dots,a_{m}]
be its coordinate ring and
K(a)\coloneqq K(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
be the field of fractions thereof.

We will refer to points
P\in K^{m}
as
choices of parameters
, elements
f\in K[a][x^{\pm}]
as
parametrised (Laurent) polynomials
, ideals
I\subseteq K[a][x^{\pm}]
as
parametrised (Laurent) polynomial ideals
, and finite generating sets
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}\subseteq I
as
parametrised (Laurent) polynomial systems
.

Definition 6

Let
P\in K^{m}
be a choice of parameters. For any parametrised polynomial
f\in K[a][x^{\pm}]
, say
f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
with
c_{\alpha}\in K[a]
, we define the
specialisation
of
f
at
P
to be

f_{P}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}c_{\alpha}(P)x^{\alpha}\in K[x^{\pm}].

For any parametrised polynomial ideal
I\unlhd K[a][x^{\pm}]
, we define the
specialisation
of
I
at
P
to be

I_{P}=\langle f_{P}\mid f\in I\rangle\unlhd K[x^{\pm}].

The
root count
of
I
at
P
is the vector space dimension

\ell_{I,P}\coloneqq\dim_{K}(K[x^{\pm}]/I_{P})\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}.

Definition 7

The
generic specialisation
of a parametrised polynomial ideal
I\unlhd K[a][x^{\pm}]
is the ideal
I_{K(a)}\unlhd K(a)[x^{\pm}]
generated by
I
under the inclusion
K[a][x^{\pm}]\subseteq K(a)[x^{\pm}]
.
The
generic root count
of
I
is the vector space dimension

\ell_{I}\coloneqq\dim_{K(a)}(K(a)[x^{\pm}]/I_{K(a)})\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}.

We say
I_{1},\dots,I_{r}\unlhd K[a][x^{\pm}]
are
generically of complementary dimension
, if

\codim(I_{1,K(a)})+\dots+\codim(I_{r,K(a)})=n,

where
\codim(\cdot)
denotes the Krull-codimension.

Definition 8

Recall that the torus
(K^{\ast})^{n}
is an algebraic group with group multiplication

(K^{\ast})^{n}\times(K^{\ast})^{n}\rightarrow(K^{\ast})^{n},\quad(\underbrace{(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})}_{\eqqcolon t},\underbrace{(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})}_{\eqqcolon s})\mapsto\underbrace{(t_{1}s_{1},\dots,t_{n}s_{n})}_{\eqqcolon t\cdot s}.

A parametrised polynomial ideal
I\unlhd K[a][x^{\pm}]
is
torus-equivariant
, if there is a matching torus action on the parameter space

(K^{\ast})^{n}\times K^{m}\rightarrow K^{m},\quad(t,P)\mapsto t\cdot P\quad\text{such that }V(I_{t\cdot P})=t\cdot V(I_{P}).

Moreover, several parametrised polynomial ideals
I_{1},\dots,I_{r}\unlhd K[a][x^{\pm}]
are
parametrically independent
, if their generators have distinct parameters, i.e.,
I_{i}=\langle F_{i}\rangle
with
F_{i}\subseteq K[a_{j}\mid j\in A_{i}][x^{\pm}]
for some partition
[m]=\coprod_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}
.

Example 9

The principal ideal
I\unlhd K[a_{1},\dots,a_{5}][x_{1}^{\pm},x_{2}^{\pm}]
generated by

f(a;x)\coloneqq a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+a_{2}x_{1}x_{2}+a_{3}x_{2}^{2}+a_{4}x_{1}+a_{5}x_{2}+1

is torus equivariant under the torus action

\displaystyle(K^{\ast})^{2}\times K^{5}

\displaystyle\rightarrow K^{5},

\displaystyle((t_{1},t_{2}),(a_{1},\dots,a_{5}))

\displaystyle\mapsto(t_{1}^{-2}a_{1},t_{1}^{-1}t_{2}^{-1}a_{2},t_{2}^{-2}a_{3},t_{1}^{-1}a_{4},t_{2}^{-1}a_{5}),

since

t\cdot V(f(a;x))=V(f(a;t^{-1}\cdot x))=V(f(t\cdot a;x))
.
Moreover,
I
is parametrically independent to the principal ideal generated by

g(a;x)\coloneqq x_{1}^{2}+x_{1}x_{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{1}+x_{2}+1,

as
f
and
g
share no parameters.

One central result for this paper is the following proposition that expresses the generic root count as a tropical intersection number:

Proposition 1

HR 22
, Proposition 4.19

Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{r}\unlhd K[a][x^{\pm}]
be parametrized polynomial ideals generically of complementary dimension, i.e.,
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\codim(I_{i,K(a)})=n

I_{1},\dots,I_{r-1}

I_{1},\dots,I_{r}
are parametrically independent. Then the generic root count of their sum
I\coloneqq I_{1}+\dots+I_{r}

\ell_{I}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Trop(I_{i,P})\quad\text{for }P\in K^{m}\text{ generic.}

Lemma 1

Let

be a tropically transverse system, and let

be defined as in

To prove this, consider an intersection point

\spanoperator(\sigma_{\ell})=\begin{cases}(e_{y_{\ell}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\cdot e_{x_{i}})^{\perp}&\text{for a monomial }x^{\alpha}\text{ in }b_{\ell}\text{ (Type Y), or}\\

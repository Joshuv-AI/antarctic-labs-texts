On the homotopy invariance of the twisted Lie algebroid cohomology. Thanks: This project was partially funded by the RTG 2491 in Göttingen. Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author


Abstract
. Twisted Lie algebroid cohomologies, i.e. with values in representations, are shown to be Lie algebroid homotopy-invariant. Several important classes of examples are discussed. As an application, a generalized version of the Poincaré lemma is given in the transitive case. Together with the Mayer-Vietoris theorem, which holds in this more general context as well, this leads to a Künneth formula for the cohomologies of Lie algebroids with values in representations. In particular, this comprehensive paper gives a systematic way to compute explicitly the twisted Lie algebroid cohomologies of some transitive and almost transitive Lie algebroids.

Introduction

The extensively studied de Rham, Chevalley-Eilenberg, BRST, Poisson, Foliated and Equivariant de Rham cohomologies can be treated at once as special cases of Lie algebroid cohomology, which was introduced by Pradines in 1967
[
Pra67
]

Lie algebroid cohomology has been studied for a few decades now – the latest references on the subject include

Lie algebroid cohomology contains obstructions for the existence of geometric objects such as flat connection and infinitesimal ideal systems (see e.g.
[
Jot22
]
). The more general
twisted
Lie algebroid cohomologies provide further geometric information. For instance, the second cohomology vector space
\text{H}^{2}_{\nabla}(A,E)
is in natural bijection with the set of equivalence classes of extensions of the Lie algebroid
A
by a vector bundle
E
whose induced representation is
\nabla
(see
[
Mac05
, Proposition 7.1.13]
and also
[
CE56

for the original case of Lie algebras).

The main goal of this paper is a systematic study of homotopy of Lie algebroids
and of its relations with further classical tools of differential topology adapted to twisted Lie algebroid cohomology.

A Lie algebroid is a smooth vector bundle
A\to M
with a Lie bracket on its space of sections, that is
anchored
by the geometry of the basis manifold
M
, in the sense that a vector bundle homomorphism
\rho\colon A\to TM
over the identity on
M
allows for differentiation of smooth functions on
M

along
sections of
A
; an operation that is compatible with the Lie bracket on
\Gamma(A)
(see Definition
2.1
).

The tangent bundles of smooth manifolds on the one hand, and Lie algebras on the other hand, are prototypes of Lie algebroids.
The de Rham cohomology of a smooth manifold is defined by the geometry of its tangent bundle. Precisely, given a smooth manifold
M
, its de Rham differential is defined on the exterior algebra of sections of
T^{*}M
. Similarly, the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential associated to a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
lives on
\bigwedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
In general, the Lie algebroid differential of a Lie algebroid
A\to M
is a differential operator on
\Gamma(\bigwedge^{\bullet}A^{*})
, that is defined
in the same manner as the de Rham differential, but using the anchor
\rho\colon A\to TM
in order to differentiate smooth functions on
M
along sections of
A
.
The resulting cohomology is called the
Lie algebroid cohomology
and encompasses all types of cohomologies listed above.
More generally, a Lie algebroid representation of
A\to M
on a vector bundle
E\to M
defines the
Lie algebroid cohomology of
A
with values in the representation
E
. It is also called
the
twisted
Lie algebroid cohomology of
A
with coefficients in
E
.

This paper studies LA-homotopies, i.e. homotopies in the category of Lie algebroids.
Two Lie algebroid morphisms
(\Phi_{0},\varphi_{0})
,
(\Phi_{1},\varphi_{1})
from a Lie algebroid
A\to M
to a Lie algebroid

are

if there exists an LA-morphism
(\Phi,\varphi)
of the form

\textstyle{TI\times A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 8.19447pt\Phi}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{I\times M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 8.19447pt\varphi}

The following extension to twisted Lie algebroid cohomology is proved here, and a study of examples and further applications for computing the cohomology itself is made.

Main Theorem

(see
3.8

.

The twisted Lie algebroid cohomology is LA-homotopy-invariant.
More precisely, consider two LA-homotopic LA-morphisms

(\Phi_{0},\varphi_{0}),(\Phi_{1},\varphi_{1})\colon A\to B

via an LA-homotopy

\Phi\colon TI\times A\to B

over

\nabla\colon\Gamma(B)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)

Then the maps in twisted cohomology induced by the Lie algebroid inclusions
\mathcal{I}_{t}\colon A\to TI\times A

\overline{\mathcal{I}_{t}^{*}}\colon\text{H}_{\Phi^{*}\nabla}^{\bullet}(TI\times A,\varphi^{!}E)\to\text{H}_{\Phi_{t}^{*}\nabla}^{\bullet}(A,\varphi_{t}^{!}E)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{\nabla}(B,E)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{\Phi_{0}^{*}\nabla}(A,\varphi_{0}^{!}E)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{\Phi_{1}^{*}\nabla}(A,\varphi_{1}^{!}E).}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\Phi_{0}^{*}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\Phi_{1}^{*}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\overline{\mathcal{I}_{1}^{*}}\circ(\overline{\mathcal{I}_{0}^{*}})}^{-1}}

Theorem

(see
5.1
)
.

The twisted cohomology of a transitive Lie algebroid is isomorphic to the twisted cohomology of its pullback Lie algebroid under a homotopy equivalence of the base manifolds. Under suitable hypotheses, the same result holds also for non-transitive Lie algebroids.

A direct consequence of this is a generalized Poincaré lemma for transitive Lie algebroids.

Lemma

(see
5.4
)
.

The twisted Lie algebroid cohomology of a transitive Lie algebroid over a contractible manifold is isomorphic to the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of its isotropy Lie algebra, with values in the restricted representation.

Concerning computational tools for Lie algebroid cohomology, it is relevant to mention that a new spectral sequence for Lie algebroid cohomology is studied in the recent work

Outline of the paper

Section
2
introduces necessary concepts and results for the rest of the paper. Particular attention is dedicated here to the notion of pullback under Lie algebroid morphisms of forms with values in representations. This is a natural definition and likely to be folklore knowledge, although hard to find in the literature. A detailed treatment is provided in Subsection
2.4
for future reference. The other parts of Section
2
are intended for readers who are not familiar with Lie algebroids and their cohomology, so very detailed as well.

Section
3
defines the notion of homotopy of Lie algebroids, called LA-homotopy, and carefully justifies it.
The main theorem on the LA-homotopy invariance of the twisted LA-cohomology is given in Section
3.1
. Its proof is technical and therefore splits between this section and Section
7.3
. It uses a study in Section
7
of flows of special linear vector fields on Lie algebroids.
Section
3.2
studies the homotopy-invariance of pullback Lie algebroids. As a consequence, a large class of examples of LA-homotopies is constructed in
3.3
.

The special case of Lie algebras is investigated in Section
4.1
and
4.2
. In particular Section
4.2
explains how LA-homotopy turns out to be compatible with the deformation theory of morphisms of Lie algebras.

Results on the twisted cohomology of pullback Lie algebroids via homotopy equivalences are studied in Section
5.1
.
A relevant application is a generalized Poincaré-Lemma for transitive Lie algebroids, which is explained in Section
5.2
. Relations with the existing literature on Lie algebroid cohomology of Morita equivalent Lie algebroids are investigated in Section
5.3
.

Section
6.1
observes that the Mayer-Vietoris principle holds for twisted Lie algebroid cohomology. This result provides in particular an alternative proof of the finite dimensionality of the twisted LA-cohomology of transitive Lie algebroids over a compact base manifold. By combining Mayer-Vietoris with the generalized Poincaré-Lemma, a Künneth formula is then proved in Section
6.2
.

Section
7
contains a detailed treatment of the invariance under LA-homotopies of representations of Lie algebroids. This specializes to the invariance of flat connections under classical homotopies.

2.
Preliminaries

This section collects necessary background on Lie algebroids, their representations and their cohomology, as well as on vector-valued forms and their pullbacks under Lie algebroid morphisms.

2.1.
The category of Lie algebroids

The definitions of Lie algebroid and of Lie algebroid morphisms are recalled here for the convenience of the reader. See e.g.
[
Mac05
]
for more detail.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Lie algebroid
is a triple
(A\to M,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho_{A})
, where
A\to M
is a vector bundle,
[\cdot,\cdot]
is a
\mathbb{R}
-Lie algebra bracket on
\Gamma(A)
, that is
anchored
by a vector bundle morphism
\rho_{A}\colon A\to TM
, called the
anchor
:

[a_{1},f\cdot a_{2}]=f\cdot[a_{1},a_{2}]+{{\pounds}}_{\rho_{A}(a_{1})}(f)\cdot a_{2}

for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in\Gamma(A)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.
{{\pounds}}_{\rho_{A}(a_{1})}(f)
denotes the Lie derivative of
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
with respect to
\rho_{A}(a_{1})\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, it is often written
\rho_{A}(a_{1})(f)
.

It follows that the anchor is compatible with the bracket in the following sense:

[\rho_{A}(a_{1}),\rho_{A}(a_{2})]=\rho_{A}[a_{1},a_{2}]

for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in\Gamma(A)
.

A Lie algebroid
A\to M
is called
regular
if
\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{A})
is a subbundle of
TM
.
Transitive Lie algebroids
, i.e. Lie algebroids with surjective anchor, are special cases of regular Lie algebroids.

Examples 2.2
.

•

For
M=\{*\}
, the anchor map vanishes and
A
is a real vector space with a Lie bracket, i.e. a real Lie algebra.

•

The tangent bundle
TM\to M
is a Lie algebroid with the identity as anchor and the Lie bracket of vector fields. Involutive subbundles are exactly the infinitesimal description of foliations (by Frobenius’ Theorem,
[
Dea40
,
Cle66
]
).
Therefore, involutive subbundles are often simply called
foliations
. An involutive subbundle
F\subseteq TM
of the tangent bundle of a smooth manifold
M
is a Lie algebroid with the inclusion
\iota\colon F\to TM
as anchor.

•

Let
G
be a Lie group and consider a principal
G
-bundle
\pi\colon P\to M
. That is,
P
is equipped with a free and proper smooth right action of the Lie group
G
,
M\simeq P/G
and
\pi\colon P\to P/G=M
is the orbit projection, a surjective submersion. The associated algebroid, called
Atiyah Lie algebroid
, has

A(P):=TP/G\to M

as underlying vector bundle.
The space of sections is isomorphic to the space of
G
-equivariant vector fields on
P
:
\Gamma(TP/G)\cong\mathfrak{X}^{G}(P)
and the Lie bracket of
G
-equivariant vector fields on
P
is again
G
-equivariant.
Hence the
C^{\infty}(M)
-module of
G
-equivariant vector fields in
P
is equipped with the Lie bracket

\left[\overline{X},\overline{Y}\right]_{TP/G}(m):=\overline{[X,Y]}(m)

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}^{G}(P)
and all
p\in\pi^{-1}(m)
.
Here,
\overline{X}\in\Gamma(TP/G)
is the section defined by
X
:

\overline{X}(m)=[X(p)]\in TP/G

for all
m\in M
and
p\in\pi^{-1}(m)
.
Since the projection
\pi
is
G
-invariant, its tangent map factors to

\rho:=\overline{T\pi}\colon TP/G\to TM,\qquad\rho[v_{p}]=T_{p}\pi v_{p}

for all
v_{p}\in T_{p}P
. This induced vector bundle morphism
\rho
over the identity on
M
anchors the Lie bracket on
A(P)=TP/G
and

(A(P)\to M,[\cdot\,,\cdot]_{TP/G},\rho)
is a Lie algebroid.

Morphisms of Lie algebroids are vector bundle morphisms with an addition compatibility with the anchors and the Lie brackets.
If
A,B\to M
are Lie algebroids over a same base, then the definition is straightforward: A vector bundle morphism
\Phi\colon A\to B
over the identity on
M
is a Lie algebroid morphism if
\rho_{B}\circ\Phi=\rho_{A}
and

\Phi[a_{1},a_{2}]_{A}=[\Phi(a_{1}),\Phi(a_{2})]_{B}

for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in\Gamma(A)
.
In general, the bracket condition of a morphism of Lie algebroids over different bases is more difficult to formulate, since the composition of sections of a vector bundle
A\to M
with a vector bundle morphism
\Phi\colon A\to B
are generally not sections of the codomain vector bundle
B\to N
. The bracket condition is written using an appropriate pullback construction of the codomain vector bundle via the base map
\varphi\colon M\to N
, as in the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

A vector bundle map
(\Phi,\varphi)
between Lie algebroids
A\to M
and
B\to N

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{N}

is a
Lie algebroid morphism
if the anchor maps satisfy

\rho_{B}\circ\Phi=T\varphi\circ\rho_{A}

and if the Lie brackets satisfy the condition described in the following.
Let

\Phi^{!}\colon A\to\varphi^{!}B,\qquad a\mapsto(q_{A}(a),\Phi(a))

be vector bundle morphism (over the identity on
M
) induced by
\Phi
to the pullback of
B\to N
through
\varphi\colon M\to N
. If
a_{1},a_{2}\in\Gamma(A)
are such that
\Phi^{!}(a_{1})=\sum_{i}f^{1}_{i}\varphi^{!}b^{1}_{i}
and
\Phi^{!}(a_{2})=\sum_{j}f^{2}_{j}\varphi^{!}b^{2}_{j}
, for
b^{1}_{i},b^{2}_{j}\in\Gamma(B)
and
f^{1}_{i},f^{2}_{j}\in C^{\infty}(M)
, then the bracket condition is

\Phi^{!}([a_{1},a_{2}]_{A})=\sum_{ij}f^{1}_{i}\,f^{2}_{j}\varphi^{!}[b^{1}_{i},b^{2}_{j}]_{B}+\sum_{j}\rho_{A}(a_{1})(f^{2}_{j})\varphi^{!}b^{2}_{j}-\sum_{i}\rho_{A}(a_{2})(f^{1}_{i})\varphi^{!}b^{1}_{i}.

The space of Lie algebroid morphisms from
A
to
B
is denoted by
\text{Hom}_{LA}(A,B)
.

Examples 2.4
.

•

If
A
and
B
are Lie algebras, i.e. if

and
N
are just a point, then a Lie algebroid morphism

is a Lie algebra morphism.

•

Let
M
and

be smooth manifolds and let

Then

.

Let

and

be principal

over

The remainder of this section collects some further useful notions and constructions around Lie algebroids.

Definition 2.5
.

Given two Lie algebroids
A\to M
and
B\to N
, the product
A\times B\to M\times N
of vector bundles comes naturally equipped with the
direct product Lie algebroid
structure. Note that if
\pr_{M}\colon M\times N\to M
and
\pr_{N}\colon M\times N\to N
are the projections, then
A\times B
is isomorphic to the Whitney sum
\pr_{M}^{!}A\oplus\pr_{N}^{!}B
, as vector bundles over
M\times N
.
The anchor
\rho_{A\times B}\colon A\times B\to TM\times TN
is the product
\rho_{A}\times\rho_{B}\colon(a_{m},b_{n})\mapsto(\rho_{A}(a_{m}),\rho_{B}(b_{n}))\in T_{m}M\times T_{n}N
.
Since the
C^{\infty}(M\times N)
-module of sections of
A\times B
is generated by pullback sections of the form
\pr_{M}^{!}a\colon M\times N\to A\times B
,
(m,n)\mapsto(a(m),0^{B}_{n})
and
\pr_{N}^{!}b
with
a\in\Gamma(A)
and
b\in\Gamma(B)
, the bracket is uniquely (well-)defined by the Leibniz rule and

\left[\pr_{M}^{!}a_{1},\pr_{M}^{!}a_{2}\right]_{A\times B}=\pr_{M}^{!}\left[a_{1},a_{2}\right]_{A},\qquad\left[\pr_{N}^{!}b_{1},\pr_{N}^{!}b_{2}\right]_{A\times B}=\pr_{N}^{!}\left[b_{1},b_{2}\right]_{B}

\left[\pr_{M}^{!}a,\pr_{N}^{!}b\right]_{A\times B}=0=\left[\pr_{N}^{!}b,\pr_{M}^{!}a\right]_{A\times B}

for
a,a_{1},a_{2}\in\Gamma(A)
and
b,b_{1},b_{2}\in\Gamma(B)
.

As already mentioned in the introduction, the key idea of the research on Lie algebroid cohomology and of this paper is to understand standard de Rham cohomology
as the Lie algebroid cohomology of the tangent bundles of manifolds, and then attempt to adapt known results in this special case to the general setting of Lie algebroids. It is easy to see that the pullback of a tangent bundle under a smooth map is not a tangent bundle anymore.

2.2.
The LA-cohomology

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A},\rho_{A})
be a Lie algebroid over a base manifold
M
, with
\text{rank}(A)=n
. Define the space of
A
-forms of degree
k\in\mathbb{N}
as
\Omega^{k}(A):=\Gamma(\bigwedge^{k}(A^{*}))
and the differential
\mathbf{d}_{A}\colon\Omega^{\bullet}(A)\to\Omega^{\bullet+1}(A)
by

\begin{split}(\mathbf{d}_{A}\omega)(a_{1}\dots,a_{k+1})&:=\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{j+1}\,\rho_{A}(a_{j})(\omega(a_{1},\dots,\widehat{a_{j}},\dots,a_{k+1}))\\
&\qquad+\sum_{j<l}(-1)^{j+l}\omega([a_{j},a_{l}],a_{1},\dots,\widehat{a}_{j},\dots,\widehat{a}_{l},\dots,a_{k+1})\end{split}

for
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(A)
and
a_{j}\in\Gamma(A)
,
j=1,\dots,k+1
. Here,
\rho_{A}(a_{j})(f):={{\pounds}}_{\rho_{A}(a_{j})}(f)
is the Lie derivative of the function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
with respect to
\rho_{A}(a_{j})\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
\widehat{a_{j}}
means that the section
a_{j}
is omitted. It is immediate by the definition of
\mathbf{d}_{A}
and by the Jacoby identity for
[\cdot,\cdot]_{A}
that
\mathbf{d}_{A}^{2}=0
, so the cochain complex

0\rightarrow\Omega^{0}(A)=C^{\infty}(M)\xrightarrow{\mathbf{d}_{A}}\Omega^{1}(A)\xrightarrow{\mathbf{d}_{A}}\cdots\Omega^{n}(A)\to 0

induces the
Lie algebroid cohomology vector spaces
(or
LA-cohomology vector spaces
) of the Lie algebroid
A\to M
:

\displaystyle\text{H}^{k}(A):=\text{ker}(\mathbf{d}_{k})/\text{im}(\mathbf{d}_{k-1})

with the notation
\mathbf{d}_{k}:=\mathbf{d}_{A}\arrowvert_{\Omega^{k}(A)}\colon\Omega^{k}(A)\to\Omega^{k+1}(A)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
k
-forms in
\text{ker}(\mathbf{d}_{k})=:\Omega^{k}_{cl}(A)
are called
closed
. The graded algebra of closed forms is written
\Omega^{\bullet}_{cl}(A):=\bigoplus_{k}\Omega^{k}_{cl}(A)
.

Example 2.9
.

The LA-cohomology of a given Lie algebroid can be extremely “large”.
For instance, if
A\to M
is a trivial Lie algebroid, i.e. a Lie algebroid with zero anchor and zero bracket, then the differential
\mathbf{d}_{A}
vanishes by definition. Hence

\displaystyle\text{H}^{0}(A)=C^{\infty}(M)\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{H}^{k}(A)=\Omega^{k}(A)

for all
k>0
.

Remark 2.11
.

Let
A\to M
be a Lie algebroid. Then

\text{H}^{\bullet}(A):=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\text{H}^{k}(A)
is a graded algebra,
with cup product induced by the wedge product of

[

A Lie algebroid morphism induces a morphism of rings in cohomology. In particular, the map

2.3.
LA-cohomology with values in representations

A more general Lie algebroid cohomology can be defined using ‘twists’ with respect to linear flat Lie algebroid connections. All necessary basic definitions are recalled before introducing the
twisted LA-cohomology
, also called
LA-cohomology with values on representations
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
A\to M
be a Lie algebroid. A
linear
A
-connection
on the vector bundle
E\to M
is an
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear map

\nabla\colon\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)

such that

\nabla_{a}(f\cdot e)=f\cdot\nabla_{a}e+\rho_{A}(a)(f)\cdot e

and

\nabla_{f\cdot a}e=f\cdot\nabla_{a}e

for
a\in\Gamma(A)
,
e\in\Gamma(E)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

Let
A\to M
be a Lie algebroid and let
E\to M
be a vector bundle.
Define

\Omega^{\bullet}(A,E):=\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}A^{*}\otimes E).

A linear
A
-connection
\nabla\colon\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
is equivalent to an operator

\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}\colon\Omega^{\bullet}(A,E)\to\Omega^{\bullet+1}(A,E)

defined by the conditions

\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}e=\nabla_{\cdot}e\in\Omega^{1}(A,E)

\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}(\omega\wedge\eta)=(\mathbf{d}_{A}\omega)\wedge\eta+(-1)^{k}\omega\wedge\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}\eta,

for
e\in\Gamma(E)=\Omega^{0}(A,E)
,
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(A)
and
\eta\in\Omega^{\bullet}(A,E)
.

Remark 2.13
.

If
E=\mathbb{R}\times M\to M
with the canonical connection
\nabla_{a}f=\mathcal{L}_{\rho_{A}(a)}(f)
, then
\Omega^{\bullet}(A,E)=\Omega^{\bullet}(A)
and
\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}=\mathbf{d}_{A}
.

The linear connection
\nabla
is called
flat
if the corresponding curvature
R_{\nabla}\in\Omega^{2}(A,\operatorname{End}(E))
defined by

R_{\nabla}(a_{1},a_{2}):=\nabla_{a_{1}}\circ\nabla_{a_{2}}-\nabla_{a_{2}}\circ\nabla_{a_{1}}-\nabla_{[a_{1},a_{2}]}

for every
a_{1},a_{2}\in\Gamma(A)
vanishes identically. This is equivalent (see for instance
[
Jot22
]
) to

0\equiv\mathbf{d}^{2}_{\nabla}\colon\Omega^{\bullet}(A,E)\to\Omega^{\bullet+2}(A,E).

The cohomology induced by this differential is the
LA-cohomology with values in the representation
\nabla
(or LA-cohomology with values in the representation
E
, or simply
twisted LA-cohomology
), since flat Lie algebroid connections are the
representations
of Lie algebroids.
For a Lie algebroid
A\to M
, a vector bundle
E\to M
and a flat connection
\nabla\colon\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
, the corresponding cohomology is denoted by

\operatorname{H}_{\nabla}^{k}(A,E):=\text{ker}(\mathbf{d}_{k})/\text{im}(\mathbf{d}_{k-1})

with
\mathbf{d}_{k}:=(\mathbf{d}_{\nabla})_{|_{\Omega^{k}(A,E)}}\colon\Omega^{k}(A,E)\to\Omega^{k+1}(A,E)
. A form
\omega\in\text{ker}(\mathbf{d}_{k})=:\Omega^{k}_{cl}(A)
is called a
closed form
.

Lemma 2.14
.

\operatorname{H}_{\nabla}^{\bullet}(A,E)
is an

-module.

Proof.

Define the module structure via

(1)

\overline{\omega}\cup\overline{\eta}:=\overline{\omega\wedge\eta}

with

,

It remains to prove that this is well-defined. For
\omega\in\Omega_{\text{cl}}^{k}(A)
,

\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}(\omega\wedge\eta)=\mathbf{d}_{A}\omega\wedge\eta+(-1)^{k}\omega\wedge\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}\eta=0.

That is, if

and
\eta\in\Omega^{\bullet}_{\text{cl}}(A,E)

and
\overline{\omega\wedge\eta}
exists.
Moreover, for

and

\begin{split}(\omega\,+\,\mathbf{d}_{A}\theta)\wedge(\eta\,+\,\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}\xi)&=\omega\wedge\eta\,+\,\omega\wedge\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}\xi\,+\,\mathbf{d}_{A}\theta\wedge\eta\,+\,\mathbf{d}_{A}\theta\wedge\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}\xi\\

where the last passage holds because the forms

3.8
consists in showing that taking its direct product with the tangent bundle of an open interval does not affect the twisted Lie algebroid cohomologies of a Lie algebroid. In other words, the map
\overline{\mathcal{I}_{t}^{*}}
in (
9
) is an isomorphism for
t=0,1
. The precise statement is formalized in Theorem
3.11
below. Note that for twists given by the standard representation on the trivial line bundle, i.e. for the usual Lie algebroid cohomology Theorem
3.11
is sufficient to conclude using Example
2.22
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
A\to M
be a Lie algebroid and let
F\to M
be a vector bundle with an
A
-representation
\nabla
. Then

\overline{\operatorname{Pr}_{A}^{*}}\colon\operatorname{H}_{\nabla}^{\bullet}(A,F)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{\operatorname{Pr}_{A}^{*}\nabla}(TI\times A,\operatorname{pr}_{M}^{!}F)

is a module isomorphism over the ring homomorphism
\overline{\operatorname{Pr}_{A}^{*}}\colon\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(A)\to\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(TI\times A)
, where
\operatorname{Pr}_{A}\colon TI\times A\to A
and
\operatorname{pr}_{M}\colon I\times M\to M
are the projection maps to the second components.
The inverse of
\overline{\Ppr_{A}^{*}}
is given by

\overline{\mathcal{I}_{t}^{*}}\colon\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{\operatorname{Pr}_{A}^{*}\nabla}(TI\times A,\operatorname{pr}_{M}^{!}F)\to\operatorname{H}_{\mathcal{I}_{t}^{*}\operatorname{Pr}_{A}^{*}\nabla}^{\bullet}(A,\iota_{t}^{!}\pr_{M}^{!}F)=\operatorname{H}_{\nabla}^{\bullet}(A,F)

for any
t\in I
, with
\mathcal{I}_{t}
defined as in (
7
) and right-inverse to
\Ppr_{A}
.

The proof of this theorem relies on the following lemma, which is standard.

Lemma 3.12
.

The space
\Omega^{k}(TI\times A,\operatorname{pr}^{!}_{M}F)
is (locally) generated as a
C^{\infty}(I\times M)
-module by forms of the following type:

(i)

\operatorname{Pr}_{A}^{*}\omega

(ii)

\mathbf{d}t\wedge\operatorname{Pr}_{A}^{*}\eta

with
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(A,F)
and
\eta\in\Omega^{k-1}(A,F)
.

Remark 3.13
.

Note that the cohomology maps
\overline{\mathcal{I}_{t}^{*}}
in Theorem
3.8
and in Theorem
3.11
are not the same maps, although both induced by the Lie algebroid morphism
\mathcal{I}_{t}
in (
7
). The first one is the cohomology map defined by pulling back
\Phi^{*}\nabla\colon\Gamma(TI\times A)\times\Gamma(\varphi^{!}E)\to\Gamma(\varphi^{!}E)
along
\mathcal{I}_{t}
, with
\nabla\colon\Gamma(B)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
a general Lie algebroid representation. The second one is defined by pulling back
\Ppr_{A}^{*}\nabla\colon\Gamma(TI\times A)\times\Gamma(\pr_{M}^{!}F)\to\Gamma(\pr_{M}^{!}F)
, for
\nabla\colon\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(F)\to\Gamma(F)
a Lie algebroid representation. However which version of
\overline{\mathcal{I}_{t}^{*}}
is meant is clear from the context, so the notation is taken to be the same for simplicity.

Proof of Theorem
3.11
.

For simplicity, use the notation

\nabla^{TI\times A}:=\operatorname{Pr}_{A}^{*}\nabla\colon\Gamma(TI\times A)\times\Gamma(\pr_{M}^{!}F)\to\Gamma(\pr_{M}^{!}F).

Recall the Lie algebroid morphisms (inclusions)
\mathcal{I}_{t}\colon A\to TI\times A
,
\mathcal{I}_{t}(a):=(0_{t},a)
over
\iota_{t}\colon M\to I\times M,\,\iota_{t}(m):=(t,m)
for all
t\in I
.

The map
\overline{\operatorname{Pr}^{*}_{A}}
is injective because the identities
\pr_{A}\circ\iota_{t}=\text{id}_{M}
and
\operatorname{Pr}_{A}\circ\mathcal{I}_{t}=\text{id}_{A}
imply
\iota_{t}^{!}(\pr_{A}^{!}F)=(\pr_{A}\circ\iota_{t})^{!}F=F
and

\overline{\mathcal{I}_{t}^{*}}\circ\overline{\operatorname{Pr}^{*}_{A}}=\text{id}_{H^{\bullet}_{\nabla}(A,F)}
in cohomology, with

\operatorname{H}_{\nabla}^{\bullet}(A,F)\xrightarrow{\overline{\operatorname{Pr}^{*}_{A}}}\operatorname{H}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}}^{\bullet}(TI\times A,\pr_{M}^{!}F)\xrightarrow{\overline{\mathcal{I}_{t}^{*}}}\operatorname{H}_{\nabla}^{\bullet}(A,F).

To show the surjectivity of
\overline{\operatorname{Pr}^{*}_{A}}
, define as follows an explicit homotopy operator.
Inspired by
[
BT82
]
and using Lemma (
3.12
), define the operator

K\colon\Omega^{k}(TI\times A,\pr_{M}^{!}F)\to\Omega^{k-1}(TI\times A,\pr_{M}^{!}F)

by

K(h\cdot\Ppr_{A}^{*}\omega):=0

and

K(g\cdot\mathbf{d}t\wedge\Ppr_{A}^{*}\eta)\arrowvert_{(t,m)}:=(-1)^{k-1}\,\int_{0}^{t}g(s,m)ds\cdot(\Ppr_{A}^{*}\eta)\arrowvert_{(t,m)},

for all
t\in I
and
m\in M
,
where
h,g\in C^{\infty}(I\times M)
,
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(A,F)
,
\eta\in\Omega^{k-1}(A,F)
.

Now check as follows that
K
is indeed a homotopy operator.
On a form
\xi=h\cdot\text{Pr}_{A}^{*}\omega
of type (
i
),

((\text{id}-\operatorname{Pr}_{A}^{*}\circ\mathcal{I}^{*}_{0})(\xi))\arrowvert_{(t,m)}=(h(t,m)-h(0,m))\cdot(\text{Pr}^{*}_{A}\omega)\arrowvert_{(t,m)}.

Let
k
be the degree of
\omega
. Compute using
K(\xi)=0

\begin{split}&(\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}}K-K\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}})(\xi)=-K\,\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}}(\xi)\\
&\,=-K(\mathbf{d}_{TI\times A}h\wedge\Ppr_{A}^{*}\omega+(-1)^{k}h\cdot\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}}(\Ppr_{A}^{*}\omega))\\
&\,=-K(\mathbf{d}_{TI\times A}h\wedge\Ppr_{A}^{*}\omega+(-1)^{k}h\cdot\Ppr_{A}^{*}(\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}\omega))\\
&\,=-K(\mathbf{d}_{TI\times A}h\wedge\Ppr_{A}^{*}\omega).\end{split}

Choose a local frame
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
of
A
and the dual frame
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})
of
A^{*}
.
Then

\mathbf{d}_{TI\times A}h=\partial_{t}h\cdot\mathbf{d}t+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\rho(a_{j})(h)\cdot\Ppr_{A}^{*}\left(\alpha_{j}\right).

Hence

(\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}}K-K\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}})(\xi)=-K(\partial_{t}h\cdot\mathbf{d}t\wedge\Ppr_{A}^{*}\omega).

which is

\begin{split}&-(-1)^{k}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{s}h(s,m)ds\cdot(\Ppr^{*}_{A}\omega)\arrowvert_{(t,m)}=-(-1)^{k}(h(t,m)-h(0,m))(\Ppr^{*}_{A}\omega)\arrowvert_{(t,m)}\end{split}

when evaluated at
(t,m)\in I\times M
. This shows that

(\text{id}-\operatorname{Pr}_{A}^{*}\circ\mathcal{I}^{*}_{0})(\xi)=(-1)^{|\xi|-1}\cdot(\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}}K-K\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}})(\xi)

for
\xi
of type (
i
).

On a form of type
\xi=g\cdot\mathbf{d}t\wedge\text{Pr}_{A}^{*}\eta
of type (
ii
) with
\eta\in\Omega^{k-1}(A,F)
, compute

(\text{id}-\text{Pr}_{A}^{*}\circ\mathcal{I}^{*}_{0})(\xi)=\xi

because
\mathcal{I}^{*}_{0}(\mathbf{d}t)=\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}(\iota^{*}_{0}t)=\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}(0)=0
. The identity

\mathbf{d}_{TI\times A}(g\mathbf{d}t)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\rho(a_{j})(g)\cdot\Ppr_{A}^{*}(\alpha_{j})\wedge\mathbf{d}t

yields

\begin{split}\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}}\xi=&-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\rho(a_{j})(g)\mathbf{d}t\wedge\Ppr_{A}^{*}(\alpha_{j})\wedge\Ppr_{A}^{*}(\eta)-g\mathbf{d}t\wedge\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}}(\Ppr_{A}^{*}\eta)\\
=&-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\rho(a_{j})(g)\mathbf{d}t\wedge\Ppr_{A}^{*}(\alpha_{j}\wedge\eta)-g\mathbf{d}t\wedge\Ppr_{A}^{*}(\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}\eta)\end{split}

and so

\begin{split}K(\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}}\xi)\arrowvert_{(t,m)}&=-(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\int_{0}^{t}\rho(a_{j})(g)(s,m)ds\cdot\Ppr_{A}^{*}(\alpha_{j}\wedge\eta)\arrowvert_{(t,m)}\\
&\qquad-(-1)^{k}\int_{0}^{t}g(s,m)ds\cdot(\Ppr_{A}^{*}(\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}\eta))\arrowvert_{(t,m)}.\end{split}

On the other hand,

K(\xi)\arrowvert_{(t,m)}=(-1)^{k-1}\,\int_{0}^{t}g(s,m)ds\cdot(\Ppr_{A}^{*}\eta)\arrowvert_{(t,m)},

for all
t\in I
and
m\in M
,
and the fact that
g
is smooth yields as above in local coordinates

\begin{split}(\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}}(K(\xi)))\arrowvert_{(t,m)}=&\,(-1)^{k-1}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{r}\int_{0}^{t}\rho(a_{j})g(s,m)ds\cdot\Ppr_{A}^{*}(\alpha_{j}\wedge\eta)\arrowvert_{(t,m)}\\
&+(-1)^{k-1}\cdot g(t,m)\cdot\mathbf{d}t\wedge\Ppr_{A}^{*}(\eta)\arrowvert_{(t,m)}\\
&+(-1)^{k-1}\int_{0}^{t}g(s,m)ds\cdot\Ppr_{A}^{*}(\mathbf{d}_{\nabla}\eta)\arrowvert_{(t,m)}\\
=&\,K(\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}}\xi)\arrowvert_{(t,m)}+(-1)^{k-1}\xi\arrowvert_{(t,m)}.\end{split}

This shows

\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}}(K(\xi))-K(\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}}(\xi))=(-1)^{|\xi|-1}\cdot\xi,

and so

(\text{id}-\operatorname{Pr}_{A}^{*}\circ\mathcal{I}^{*}_{0})(\xi)=(-1)^{|\xi|-1}\cdot(\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}}K-K\mathbf{d}_{\nabla^{TI\times A}})(\xi)

for
\xi
of type (
ii
).
∎

Example 3.14
.

Let
P\to M
and
Q\to N
be
G
-principal bundles and let
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\colon P\to Q
be
G
-equivariant maps. Suppose that there exists a smooth
G
-equivariant homotopy
\varphi\colon I\times P\to Q
, such that
\varphi(0,\cdot):=\varphi_{0}
and
\varphi(1,\cdot):=\varphi_{1}
, where the
G
-action is extended to
I\times P
trivially on the first factor, then it is known that
\varphi_{0}
and
\varphi_{1}
induce the same map in equivariant de Rham cohomology (see e.g.
[
GZ19
]
). Theorem
3.8
generalizes this result to the equivariant de Rham cohomology with coefficients. The proof goes as follows. The above mentioned
G
-equivariant maps factor to maps
\bar{\varphi}\colon I\times M\to N
and
\bar{\varphi}_{0},\bar{\varphi_{1}}\colon M\to N
. They induce LA-morphisms between the corresponding Atiyah Lie algebroids
\overline{T\varphi}\colon A(I\times P)\cong TI\times A(P)\to A(Q)
and
\overline{T\varphi_{0}},\overline{T\varphi_{1}}\colon A(P)\to A(Q)
(see Example
2.4
). By Theorem
3.8
conclude that
\overline{T\varphi_{0}^{*}}\equiv\overline{T\varphi_{1}^{*}}
in the twisted Lie algebroid cohomology, which is isomorphic to the twisted equivariant de Rham cohomology (see Example
2.8
).

Example 3.15
.

It is well known that the foliated (de Rham) cohomology is not homotopy invariant (see
[
Hae71
]
).
For instance, the bundle of rank zero
0_{U}\to U
over a contractible manifold has zero differential, because both its bracket and its anchor map are zero. Its foliated de Rham cohomology therefore coincides with the space of forms. In particular,
\operatorname{H}^{0}(0_{U})=C^{\infty}(U)
, and so the Poincaré Lemma cannot hold here.

However, Theorem
3.8
shows that the foliated de Rham cohomology is LA-homotopy invariant, since foliations are special cases of Lie algebroids. The notion of LA-homotopy in the category of foliations reads as follows.

Let
(M,F_{M})
and
(N,F_{N})
be foliated manifolds, i.e.
F_{M}\subseteq TM
and
F_{N}\subseteq TN
are involutive subbundles. Consider smooth maps
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\colon M\to N
such that

T\varphi_{j}(F_{M})\subseteq F_{N}
for
j=0,1
, i.e. Lie algebroid morphisms
T\varphi_{j}\colon F_{M}\to F_{N}
over
\varphi_{j}\colon M\to N
.
Equivalently, for each leaf
L
of
F_{M}
there exists leaves
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
of
F_{N}
such that
\varphi_{j}(L)\subseteq K_{j}
, for
j=0,1
.
Note that in the following,
a leaf of
F_{M}
is short for
a maximal integral leaf of
F_{M}
.

Then if
\varphi_{0}
and
\varphi_{1}
are LA-homotopic, then there exists a Lie algebroid morphism
\Phi\colon TI\times F_{M}\to F_{N}

over a smooth map
\varphi\colon I\times M\to N
. But then the anchor condition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{TI\times TM}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces TN}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{TI\times F_{M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F_{N}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{I\times M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

shows that
\Phi=T\varphi\arrowvert_{TI\times F_{M}}
and that
T\varphi(TI\times F_{M})\subseteq F_{N}
.
A leaf of
TI\times F_{M}\subseteq TI\times TM
is a product
I\times L
with
L
a leaf of
F_{M}
. The inclusion

T\varphi(TI\times F_{M})\subseteq F_{N}
implies that for each leaf
L
of
F_{M}
, there exists a leaf
K\subseteq N
such that

\varphi(I\times L)\subseteq K.

In particular,

\varphi_{j}(L)=\varphi(\{j\}\times L)\subseteq K

for
j=0,1
, i.e.
\varphi_{0}
and
\varphi_{1}
map each leaf of
F_{M}
in the same leaf of
F_{N}
.

This shows that a LA-homotopy between morphisms
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\colon(M,\mathcal{F}_{M})\to(N,\mathcal{F}_{N})
of foliated manifolds
is a
homotopy along the foliations
, i.e. a smooth map
\varphi\colon I\times M\to N
such that
\varphi\circ\iota_{j}=\varphi_{j}
for
j=0,1
and for each leaf
L
of
\mathcal{F}_{M}
there exists a leaf
K
of
\mathcal{F}_{N}
such that
\varphi(I\times L)\subseteq K
. By Theorem
3.8
, if such a smooth homotopy exist, then the maps induced by
T\varphi_{0}\arrowvert{F_{M}}
and
T\varphi_{1}\arrowvert{F_{M}}
coincide in foliated cohomology.

LA-homotopy in the case of foliations coincides with the notion of integrable homotopy, introduced by Haefliger in
[
Hae71
]
. The name integrable homotopy is first used in
[
Azi83
]
. Foliated cohomology is known there to be invariant under integrable homotopy, see
[
Sch21
]
for more details.

3.2.
Pullback of Lie algebroids via homotopies

Pullback Lie algebroids are useful to systematically construct examples of LA-morphisms, and LA-homotopies (see Corollary
3.21
). In the transitive case, similar constructions are done in
[
Mei21
]
.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
B\to N
be a Lie algebroid and let
M
be a smooth manifold. Consider a smooth homotopy

\begin{split}\varphi\colon I\times M&\to N,\qquad(t,m)\mapsto\varphi_{t}(m)\end{split}

such that
\varphi^{!!}B
exists.
If there exists a smooth section
\tilde{y}
of
\varphi^{!!}B
of the form
(s,m)\mapsto(\partial_{t}\arrowvert_{s},0_{m},y(s,m))\in(T_{s}I\times T_{m}M)\times_{TN}B_{\varphi(t,m)}
, for some
y\colon I\times M\to B
, then the induced pullback Lie algebroids

\varphi^{!!}_{t}B\to M
are all isomorphic.

Note that in the proposition above, the existence of the section
\tilde{y}
implies that
TI\times 0\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{\varphi^{!!}B})
. As a consequence, the inclusions
\iota_{t}\colon M\to I\times M
,
m\mapsto(t,m)
, are transversal to the anchor map
\rho_{\varphi^{!!}B}
, and so the pullback Lie algebroids
\iota_{t}^{!!}\varphi^{!!}B\cong\varphi_{t}^{!!}B
exist for all
t\in I
.

Remark 3.17
.

In Proposition
3.16
, a necessary condition for the existence of the section
\tilde{y}
is
TI\times 0\subseteq\operatorname{Im}\rho_{\varphi^{!!}B}
.
This is equivalent to
\operatorname{Im}(T\varphi\arrowvert_{(TI\times 0)})\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{B})
, i.e. to the homotopy
\varphi
deforming along the, possibly singular, foliation defined by the Lie algebroid. Indeed, recall that

\varphi^{!!}B:=\{((r,v),b)\in T_{t}I\times T_{m}M\times B_{\varphi(t,m)}\mid T_{(t,m)}\varphi(r,v)=\rho_{B}(b),(t,m)\in I\times M\}

with anchor map
\rho_{\varphi^{!!}B}((r,v),b):=(r,v)
. The condition
\text{Im}(T\varphi\arrowvert_{(TI\times 0)})\subseteq\text{Im}(\rho_{B})
means that for each vector
r\in T_{t}I
and each
m\in M
there exists
b\in B_{\varphi(t,m)}
such that
T\varphi(r,0_{m})=\rho_{B}(b)
. Then
(r,0_{m})=\rho_{\varphi^{!!}B}((r,0_{m}),b)\in\text{Im}(\rho_{\varphi^{!!}B})
.
This shows the inclusion
TI\times 0\subseteq\text{Im}(\rho_{\varphi^{!!}B})
. The inverse implication follows from the definition of the anchor map of the pullback Lie algebroid. For each vector
r\in T_{t}I
and each
m\in M
the condition
(r,0_{m})\in\operatorname{Im}(\varphi^{!!}B)
implies that there exists an element
b\in B_{\varphi(t,m)}
such that
T\varphi(r,0_{m})=\rho_{B}(b)
.

Proof of Proposition
3.16
.

Consider the derivation
D:=[\tilde{y},\cdot]
of the Lie algebroid
\varphi^{!!}B
, with symbol
X:=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\in\mathfrak{X}(I\times M)
. Then
\widehat{D}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(\varphi^{!!}B)
is linear over
X
.
Set
\Omega:=\{(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}\times I\mid t+s\in I\}
. Since
X
has the flow

\psi\colon\Omega\times M\ni(t,s,m)\mapsto(s+t,m)\in I\times M,

the flow
\Psi
of
\widehat{D}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(\varphi^{!!}B)
is
a map

\Psi\colon\Omega\times\varphi^{!!}B\to\varphi^{!!}B

over
\psi
by Lemma
7.1
.
By Proposition
7.2
, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
the map

\Psi_{t}\colon\varphi^{!!}B\arrowvert_{(I\cap(I-t))\times M}\to\varphi^{!!}B\arrowvert_{(I\cap(I+t))\times M}

is further an isomorphism of Lie algebroids over

\psi_{t}\colon(I\cap(I-t))\times M\to(I\cap(I+t))\times M,\qquad(s,m)\mapsto(s+t,m).

Take
s\in I
and consider the Lie algebroid
\varphi_{s}^{!!}B\to M
. The underlying vector bundle is given by

\varphi_{s}^{!!}B:=\{(v,b)\in T_{m}M\times B_{\varphi_{s}(m)}\mid T_{m}\varphi_{s}(v)=\rho_{B}(b),m\in M\}.

Since
T_{m}\varphi_{s}(v)=T_{(s,m)}\varphi(0_{s},v)
for all
m\in M
and
v\in T_{m}M
, the vector bundle
\varphi_{s}^{!!}B
comes with the embedding
\varphi_{s}^{!!}B\hookrightarrow\varphi^{!!}B

(v,b)\mapsto(0,v,b)

over
M\hookrightarrow I\times M
,
m\mapsto(s,m)
. This embedding is a morphism of Lie algebroids by definition. Take
t\in I
. The image of
\varphi_{s}^{!!}B
under
\Psi_{t-s}
equals
\varphi_{t}^{!!}B
: for all
(v,b)\in\varphi_{s}^{!!}B

\rho_{\varphi^{!!}B}(\Psi_{t-s}(0_{s},v,b))=T\psi_{t-s}(\rho_{\varphi^{!!}B}(0_{s},v,b))=T\psi_{t-s}(0_{s},v)=(0_{t},v).

Hence
\Psi_{t-s}(0_{s},v,b)=(0_{t},v,c)
for some
c\in B_{\varphi(t,m)}
, i.e.
\Psi_{t-s}(0_{s},v,b)\in\varphi_{t}^{!!}B\subseteq\varphi^{!!}B
. Since
\Psi_{t-s}\colon\varphi^{!!}B\to\varphi^{!!}B
is an isomorphism of Lie algebroids, it thus restricts to an isomorphism of the Lie algebroids

\Psi_{t-s}\arrowvert_{\varphi_{s}^{!!}B}\colon\varphi_{s}^{!!}B\to\varphi_{t}^{!!}B

over
\psi_{t-s}\colon\{s\}\times M\simeq M\to\{t\}\times M\simeq M
.
∎

Remark 3.19
.

The condition
\text{Im}(T\varphi\arrowvert_{(TI\times 0)})\subseteq\text{Im}(\rho_{B})
cannot be dropped. Choose a non-abelian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and define the regular Lie algebroid with underlying vector bundle

B:=I\times\mathfrak{g}\to I,

the zero anchor and the (pointwise) Lie bracket
[(t,v),(t,w)]:=(t,t[v,w]_{\mathfrak{g}})
.
Choose the smooth homotopy
\varphi=\text{Id}_{I}\colon I\to I
.
Then it is easy to see that
\varphi^{!!}B=0_{I}\times_{I}B\simeq B
, and that
\varphi_{t}^{!!}B=0_{\{t\}}\times B_{t}\simeq\{t\}\times\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{g}
for all
t\in I
, with the Lie bracket

[v,w]_{t}=t[v,w]

for all
v,w\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Then
\varphi^{!!}_{0}B
is an abelian algebra and
\varphi^{!!}_{1}B
is not, since it is the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Therefore,
\varphi^{!!}_{0}B
and
\varphi^{!!}_{1}B
cannot be isomorphic.

If

4.
The Lie algebra case

Lie algebras are prototypical examples of Lie algebroids. They are probably the most “rigid” class of Lie algebroids, i.e. where examples of LA-homotopies are the rarest. Nevertheless, it is interesting to measure how restrictive this notion is and how it relates to the classical deformation theory of morphisms of Lie algebras.This section shows that the study of LA-homotopy in the subcategory of Lie algebras provides an equivalent characterization expressed in classical Lie theory terms, so accessible to a reader with no interest in general Lie algebroids.

4.1.
LA-homotopy for Lie algebras

This section proves that LA-homotopies between Lie algebra morphisms are smooth curves of Lie algebra morphisms, the variations of which are controlled by the adjoint representation of the target Lie algebras.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be Lie algebras. Consider a vector bundle morphism

\textstyle{TI\times\mathfrak{g}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{h}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{I\times\{*\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{\{*\}}

Then
(\Phi,\varphi)
is a LA-morphism, i.e. an LA-homotopy between
\Phi_{0}:=\Phi(0\arrowvert_{0},\cdot)
and
\Phi_{1}:=\Phi(0\arrowvert_{1},\cdot)\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
, if and only if
it can be rewritten by means of smooth curves

and
\psi\colon I\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
, as

(10)

\Phi(r\partial_{t}{{}_{|_{s}}},x):=rc(s)+\psi_{s}(x)

with
r\in\mathbb{R}
,

and

,
satisfying the partial differential equation

(11)

(\partial_{t}\psi)_{s}(x)=-\operatorname{ad}_{c(s)}(\psi_{s}(x)):=-[c(s),\psi_{s}(x)]

for each
s\in I
and each

.

Remark 4.2
.

(1)

The curve
\psi\colon I\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is automatically a smooth curve of morphisms of Lie algebras. In order to see this, use the differential equation (
11
) and the Jacobi identity and compute

(12)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\left(\left[\psi_{t}(x),\psi_{t}(y)\right]-\psi_{t}[x,y]\right)=\left[c(t),\psi_{t}[x,y]-\left[\psi_{t}(x),\psi_{t}(y)\right]\right]\end{split}

for

\left[\psi_{0}(x),\psi_{0}(y)\right]-\psi_{0}[x,y]=0

since

is a morphism of Lie algebras.
The constant curve
I\to\mathfrak{h}
,

is a solution of (

) for each

, and so it is its unique solution.

(2)

By definition,
\Phi_{j}=\psi_{j}
for
j=0,1
.

Proof.

\Phi
is a Lie algebroid morphism if and only if the vector bundle morphism

\Phi^{!!}\colon TI\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow TI\times\mathfrak{h}=\varphi^{!!}\mathfrak{h}

(r{{\partial_{t}}_{|}}_{s},x)\mapsto(r{{\partial_{t}}_{|}}_{s},r\,c(s)+\psi_{s}(x))

for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
s\in I
and all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, is a Lie algebroid morphism (see e.g.
[

]
).

A pair of a smooth function
f\in C^{\infty}(I)
and
x\in C^{\infty}(I,\mathfrak{g})
defines a smooth section

(f\partial_{t},x)\colon s\to(f(s)\partial_{t}\arrowvert_{s},x(s))
of
TI\times\mathfrak{g}\to I\times\{*\}
. This smooth section is sent by
\Phi^{!!}
to
the smooth section
s\mapsto(f(s)\partial_{t}\arrowvert_{s},f(s)c(s)+\psi_{s}(x(s)))
of
TI\times\mathfrak{h}\to I\times\{*\}
.

In the following calculations, use the usual simplified notation
f^{\prime}:=\partial_{t}f
,
x^{\prime}:=\partial_{t}x
and
\psi^{\prime}:=\partial_{t}\psi
. Then
for

and

\begin{split}\Phi^{!!}[(f\partial_{t},x),(g\partial_{t},y)]&=\Phi^{!!}((fg^{\prime}-gf^{\prime})\partial_{t},[x,y]+fy^{\prime}-gx^{\prime})\\
&=((fg^{\prime}-gf^{\prime})\partial_{t},(fg^{\prime}-gf^{\prime})\,c+\psi([x,y]+fy^{\prime}-gx^{\prime}))\end{split}

Moreover,

\begin{split}&[\Phi^{!!}(f\partial_{t},x),\Phi^{!!}(g\partial_{t},y)]=[(f\partial_{t},fc+\psi(x)),(g\partial_{t},gc+\psi(y))]\\

5.

and Lemma

have a direct application in the computation of the twisted Lie algebroid cohomology of some Lie algebroids.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
f\colon M\to N
and
g\colon N\to M
define a smooth homotopy equivalence between the manifolds
M
and
N
, with homotopies
\eta\colon I\times M\to M
and
\varphi\colon I\times N\to N
. Let
B\to N
be a Lie algebroid. Assume that
f^{!!}B
exists and the pairs
B
and
\varphi
, and
f^{!!}B
and
\eta
satisfy the hypotheses of Proposition
3.16
.

Then for each flat
B
-connection
\nabla\colon\Gamma(B)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
on a vector bundle
E\to N
, the morphism

\operatorname{H}_{\nabla}^{\bullet}(B,E)\xrightarrow{\overline{p^{*}_{B,f}}}\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{p_{B,f}^{*}\nabla}(f^{!!}B,f^{!}E)

is an isomorphism of modules over the ring isomorphism
\overline{p_{B,f}^{*}}\colon\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(B)\to\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(f^{!!}B)
.

Remark 5.2
.

Note that Theorem
5.1
holds in particular in the following two situations.

(1)

B\to N
is a regular Lie algebroid such that
f^{!!}B
,
\varphi^{!!}B
and
\eta^{!!}f^{!!}B
exist, and such that
T\eta(TI\times 0)\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{f^{!!}B})
and
T\varphi(TI\times 0)\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{B})
, i.e. the homotopies deform along the leaves of the target manifold.

(2)

B\to N
is transitive.

The transitivity of
B\to N
implies the condition of the theorem, because the hypotheses of Proposition
3.16
are satisfied for a transitive Lie algebroid and the pullback of a transitive Lie algebroid exists and is transitive. Similarly, (1) implies the condition of the theorem by Remark
3.18
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Consider the canonical LA-morphisms

\textstyle{f^{!!}B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 8.19447ptp_{B,f}\hskip 8.19447pt}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{f^{!!}B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces g^{!!}(f^{!!}B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 8.19447ptp_{f^{!!}B,g}\hskip 8.19447pt}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{N}
\textstyle{M}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces N}
\scriptstyle{g}

Let the smooth homotopy
\varphi\colon I\times N\to N
be such that
\varphi_{0}=\text{id}_{N}
and
\varphi_{1}=f\circ g
.
By Lemma
3.22
there exists an LA-morphism

\tilde{\Phi}\colon TI\times B\to B

\tilde{\Phi}\colon TI\times B\xrightarrow{\tilde{\Pi}}\varphi^{!!}B\xrightarrow{p_{B,\varphi}}B,

which is an LA-homotopy between

\tilde{\Phi}\circ\mathcal{I}_{0}=p_{B,\varphi}\circ\tilde{\Pi}\circ\mathcal{I}_{0}=\Phi\circ\mathcal{I}_{0}\circ(\Phi\circ\mathcal{I}_{0})^{-1}=\operatorname{Id}_{B}

and

\tilde{\Phi}\circ\mathcal{I}_{1}=p_{B,\varphi}\circ\tilde{\Pi}\circ\mathcal{I}_{1}=p_{B,\varphi}\arrowvert_{\varphi^{!!}_{1}B}\circ\tilde{\Pi}\circ\mathcal{I}_{1}=p_{B,f}\circ p_{f^{!!}B,g}\circ\tilde{\Pi}\circ\mathcal{I}_{1}.

The above uses that the image of
\tilde{\Pi}\circ\mathcal{I}_{1}
is contained in
\varphi^{!!}_{1}B\subseteq\varphi^{!!}B
, and that

p_{B,\varphi}
coincides with
p_{B,\varphi_{1}}=p_{B,f\circ g}=p_{B,f}\circ p_{f^{!!}B,g}
on this subbundle.
Recall that by Proposition
3.16
(and Remark
3.17
),
\varphi_{1}^{!!}B\simeq\varphi_{0}^{!!}B=\text{id}_{N}^{!!}B=B
. In addition,
\tilde{\Pi}\colon TI\times B\to\varphi^{!!}B
is an LA-isomorphism over the identity on
I\times N
. As a consequence,
the map
\tilde{\Pi}\circ\mathcal{I}_{1}\colon B\to\varphi_{1}^{!!}B
(with restricted codomain) is injective, and since
B
and
\varphi_{1}^{!!}B
have the same rank, it is an isomorphism. It hence defines an isomorphism

\overline{\left(\tilde{\Pi}\circ\mathcal{I}_{1}\right)^{*}}\colon\operatorname{H}_{p_{B,f\circ g}^{*}\nabla}\left((f\circ g)^{!!}B,(f\circ g)^{!}E\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{\nabla}(B,E)

in cohomology.

Set

F:=p_{B,f}\colon f^{!!}B\to B,\qquad F(v_{m},b_{f(m)}):=b_{f(m)}

and

G:=p_{f^{!!}B,g}\circ\tilde{\Pi}\circ\mathcal{I}_{1}\colon B\to f^{!!}B

The composition

is by definition
\tilde{\Phi}\circ\mathcal{I}_{1}
. Thus it is LA-homotopic to
\operatorname{Id}_{B}
via the LA-homotopy
\tilde{\Phi}\colon TI\times B\to B
, over
\varphi\colon I\times N\to N
. As a consequence, the induced map
\overline{G^{*}}
in cohomology is surjective and
\overline{F^{*}}
is injective.
Since
\overline{\left(\tilde{\Pi}\circ\mathcal{I}_{1}\right)^{*}}
is an isomorphism, the surjectivity of
\overline{p_{f^{!!}B,g}^{*}}
then follows from the surjectivity of
\overline{G^{*}}
.

With an analogous construction, given the homotopy
\eta\colon I\times M\to M
between
\eta_{0}=\operatorname{Id}_{M}
and
\eta_{1}=g\circ f
, apply Lemma
3.22
to get an isomorphism
\tilde{\Theta}\colon TI\times f^{!!}B\to\eta^{!!}f^{!!}B
, over the identity map, and an LA-homotopy

\tilde{\Psi}\colon TI\times f^{!!}B\xrightarrow{\tilde{\Theta}}\eta^{!!}f^{!!}B\xrightarrow{p_{f^{!!}{B},\eta}}f^{!!}B

such that

\tilde{\Psi}\circ\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{0}=\operatorname{Id}_{f^{!!}B},

for
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{0}\colon f^{!!}B\to TI\times f^{!!}B
the inclusion LA-morphism at time

At time

\begin{split}\tilde{\Psi}\circ\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{1}&=p_{f^{!!}B,\eta}\circ\tilde{\Theta}\circ\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{1}=p_{f^{!!}B,\eta}\arrowvert_{\eta_{1}^{!!}f^{!!}B}\circ\tilde{\Theta}\circ\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{1}=p_{f^{!!}B,g\circ f}\circ\tilde{\Theta}\circ\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{1}\\

Define

\tilde{F}:=p_{g^{!!}(f^{!!}B),f}\circ\tilde{\Theta}\circ\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{1}\colon f^{!!}B\to g^{!!}(f^{!!}B)

and

Since Atiyah Lie algebroids are transitive, Theorem

has the following corollary.

.

Let

be a principal

a Lie group, and

a homotopy equivalence. For each flat
A(P)
-connection
\nabla\colon\Gamma(A(P))\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
with values on the vector bundle

, the map

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{\nabla}(A(P),E)\xrightarrow{\overline{p_{A(P),f}^{*}}}\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{p_{A(P),f}^{*}\nabla}(A(f^{*}P),f^{!}E)

It is an immediate consequence of the well known fact that

(see for instance

) and Theorem

.
∎

5.2.
A generalized Poincaré lemma

A crucial property of de Rham cohomology is its local triviality, also known as Poincaré Lemma. In general, Lie algebroid cohomology is not locally trivial. However, in the transitive case, the Lie algebroid cohomology can be more easily computed using the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of the isotropy Lie algebra.

Lemma 5.4

(Generalized Poincaré-Lemma)
.

Let
B\to U
be a transitive Lie algebroid over a contractible base manifold. Let
\nabla
be a
B
-representation on
E\to U
. For
x\in U
, denote as usual by
\mathfrak{g}_{x}:=\operatorname{ker}(\rho_{B})\arrowvert_{x}
the
isotropy Lie algebra
of
B
at
x
. Define
f_{x}\colon\{*\}\to U
,
f_{x}(*):=x
. Then

B
and
\mathfrak{g}_{x}
are LA-homotopy equivalent for each
x
in
U
. In particular,

(13)

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{\nabla}(B,E)\xrightarrow{\overline{p_{B,f_{x}}^{*}}}\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{\nabla\arrowvert_{x}}(\mathfrak{g}_{x},E\arrowvert_{x})\qquad\forall x\in U,

is a module isomorphism over the ring isomorphism
\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(B)\xrightarrow{\overline{p_{B,f_{x}}^{*}}}\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}_{x})
.

\nabla\arrowvert_{x}
denotes the pullback of
\nabla
via the map
f_{x}
.
\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{\nabla\arrowvert_{x}}(\mathfrak{g}_{x},E\arrowvert_{x})
is the twisted Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology with coefficients in
E\arrowvert_{x}
of the isotropy Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{x}
.

Proof.

Since
U
is contractible, each map
f_{x}\colon\{*\}\to U
,
f(*):=x
, for
x\in U
, defines a homotopy equivalence between
U
and the point
\{*\}
. Thus
f_{x}^{!!}B
and
B
have isomorphic cohomology modules, by Theorem
5.1
,
for each choice of
x\in U
. Conclude by observing that

f_{x}^{!!}B=\{(0,b)\in T\{*\}\times B\mid Tf_{x}(0)=\rho_{B}(b)\}\cong\operatorname{ker}(\rho_{B}(b))\arrowvert_{x}=\mathfrak{g}_{x}.

∎

Remark 5.5
.

When the Lie algebroid
B
is the tangent bundle
TU
of a contractible manifold, Lemma
5.4
reduces to the classical Poincaré Lemma of de Rham cohomology.

In general it is not possible to reduce to the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of a Lie algebra for non-transitive Lie algebroids. For instance, for Lie algebroids with zero anchor and trivial brackets the equality
\operatorname{H}^{0}(A)=C^{\infty}(U)
holds.
C^{\infty}(U)
is an infinite dimensional vector space, whether the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of a Lie algebra is finite dimensional.

5.3.
Relation with the existing literature on Morita equivalence

In
[
Cra03
]
Crainic proves the following result.

Theorem
.

Let
\pi\colon P\to M
be a surjective submersion with homologically
n
-connected fibers, let
B\to M
be a Lie algebroid. Then
\overline{p_{B,\pi}^{*}}\colon\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(B)\to\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(\pi^{!!}B)
is an isomorphism in all degrees
k\leq n
. The same holds with general coefficients.

This section compares Theorem
5.1
with this result for weakly connected fibers and for
B
a transitive Lie algebroid.
The following special case of Crainic’s result is a corollary of Theorem
5.1
.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
\pi\colon P\to M
be a surjective submersion with weakly connected fibers, let
B\to M
be a transitive Lie algebroid and let
\nabla
be a flat
E\to M
connection.
\overline{p_{B,\pi}^{*}}\colon\operatorname{H}_{\nabla}^{\bullet}(B,E)\to\operatorname{H}_{p_{B,\pi}^{*}\nabla}^{\bullet}({\pi}^{!!}B,\pi^{!}E)

is a module isomorphism over the ring isomorphism
\overline{p_{B,\pi}^{*}}\colon\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(B)\to\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(\pi^{!!}B)
.

Proof.

Fibers of surjective submersions are smooth manifolds, in particular CW-complexes. By Whitehead’s theorem,
weakly connected (i.e. with all homotopy group trivial) fibers are contractible fibers. It is proved in
[
Mei02
]
(see Corollary
13
combined with an observation after Lemma
6
) that any surjective submersion with contractible fibers is a fibration and a homotopy equivalence. As a consequence of Theorem
5.1
, conclude that
\overline{p_{B,\pi}^{*}}\colon\operatorname{H}_{\nabla}^{\bullet}(B,E)\to\operatorname{H}_{p_{B,\pi}^{*}\nabla}^{\bullet}({\pi}^{!!}B,\pi^{!}E)

is a module isomorphism over the ring isomorphism
\overline{p_{B,\pi}^{*}}\colon\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(B)\to\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(\pi^{!!}B)
.
∎

On the other hand,

the generalized Poincaré Lemma (Theorem
5.4
) is a corollary of Crainic’s theorem
:
If
U
is a contractible manifold,
c\colon U\to\{*\}
is a surjective submersion with contractible fibers. Let
B\to U
be a transitive Lie algebroid and
\mathfrak{g}
its isotropy Lie algebra, then there is a surjective submersion

p_{\mathfrak{g},c}\colon c^{!!}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}

with
c^{!!}\mathfrak{g}\cong TU\times\mathfrak{g}
, which is isomorphic to
B
by Theorem
3.20
. This implies that

\overline{p_{\mathfrak{g},c}^{*}}\colon\operatorname{H}_{\nabla}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},V)\to\operatorname{H}_{p_{\mathfrak{g},c}^{*}\nabla}^{\bullet}(c^{!!}\mathfrak{g},c^{!}V)
is a module isomorphism, using Theorem
5.1
.
All representations of
B
are of the form
p_{\mathfrak{g},c}^{*}\nabla
, see
[
Cra03
]
.
Then the Poincaré Lemma follows.

6.
Mayer-Vietoris principle and a Künneth formula

Given the Poincaré lemma, the usual tool for computing the cohomology of more general objects is a Mayer-Vietoris principle.

6.1.
Mayer-Vietoris

A Mayer-Vietoris principle seems to be folklore knowledge; it is used for instance in
[
Cra03
]
and
[
Fre19
]
, it is stated in
[
Vai90
]
and
[
Vai94
]
for Poisson manifolds. In this section, an explicit expression for the coboundary operator in the long exact sequence is deduced by a generalization of standard techniques to twisted Lie algebroid cohomology (see
[
BT82
]
).

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and suppose that
M=U\cup V
, with
U,V
open. Denote with
j_{0}\colon U\cap V\hookrightarrow U
,
j_{1}\colon U\cap V\hookrightarrow V
,
i_{0}\colon U\hookrightarrow M
,
i_{1}\colon V\hookrightarrow M
and
i\colon U\sqcup V\hookrightarrow M
the inclusions. Then the two sequences
of smooth maps

U\cap V\xrightarrow{j_{k}}U\sqcup V\xrightarrow{i}M

for
k=0,1
, give the same composition
i\circ j_{0}=i\circ j_{1}\colon U\cap V\to M
.

Let
A\to M
be a Lie algebroid and
\nabla
an
A
-representation on
E\to M
.
Recall that
A\arrowvert_{U}:=i^{!!}_{0}A
denotes the restriction Lie algebroid and
E\arrowvert_{U}
the restricted vector bundle.
The notation
\nabla\arrowvert_{U}\colon\Gamma(A\arrowvert_{U})\times\Gamma(E\arrowvert_{U})\to\Gamma(E\arrowvert_{U})
stands for the restriction to
U
of the connection
\nabla
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
A\to M
be a Lie algebroid and
\nabla
an
A
-representation on
E\to M
.
Then the Mayer-Vietoris sequence

0\rightarrow\Omega^{\bullet}(A,E)\xrightarrow{i^{*}}\Omega^{\bullet}(A_{|_{U}},E_{|_{U}})\oplus\Omega^{\bullet}(A_{|_{V}},E_{|_{V}})\xrightarrow{j_{1}^{*}-j_{0}^{*}}\Omega^{\bullet}(A_{|_{U\cap V}},E_{|_{U\cap V}})\rightarrow 0,

is exact.

Let
\{\mu_{U},\mu_{V}\}
be a partition of unity subordinated to the cover
\{U,V\}
. The Mayer-Vietoris sequence induces a long exact sequence in LA-cohomology with values in representations

\cdots\to\operatorname{H}^{q}_{\nabla\arrowvert_{U\cap V}}(A\arrowvert_{U\cap V},E\arrowvert_{U\cap V})\xrightarrow{\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}^{*}}\operatorname{H}_{\nabla}^{q+1}(A,E)\xrightarrow{i^{*}}\operatorname{H}_{\nabla\arrowvert_{U}}^{q+1}(A\arrowvert_{U},E\arrowvert_{U})\oplus\operatorname{H}_{\nabla\arrowvert_{V}}^{q+1}(A\arrowvert_{V},E\arrowvert_{V})

\xrightarrow{j_{1}^{*}-j_{0}^{*}}\operatorname{H}^{q+1}_{\nabla\arrowvert_{U\cap V}}(A\arrowvert_{U\cap V},E\arrowvert_{U\cap V})\xrightarrow{\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}^{*}}\operatorname{H}_{\nabla}^{q+2}(A,E)\to\cdots

The coboundary operator
\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}^{*}
is a morphism of vector spaces explicitly defined as

\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}^{*}([\omega]):=\begin{cases}[-\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}(\mu_{V}\omega)]&\operatorname{on}\,U\\
[\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}(\mu_{U}\omega)]&\operatorname{on}\,V\end{cases}

for
\omega\in\Omega^{\bullet}(A\arrowvert_{U\cap V},E\arrowvert_{U\cap V})
.

Proof.

The injectivity of the first map and the condition
\operatorname{ker}(j^{*}_{1}-j^{*}_{0})=\operatorname{im}(i^{*})
follows because Lie algebroid forms define a sheaf, see Remark
2.21
. Each form
\omega\in\Omega^{\bullet}(A\arrowvert_{U\cap V},E\arrowvert_{U\cap V})
can be rewritten as

\omega=(\mu_{U})_{|_{U\cap V}}\,\omega+(\mu_{V})_{|_{U\cap V}}\,\omega.

Then a preimage of
\omega
via
j_{1}^{*}-j_{0}^{*}
is
\eta:=(-(\mu_{V})_{|_{U}}\,\omega,(\mu_{U})_{|_{V}}\,\omega)\in\Omega^{\bullet}(A\arrowvert_{U},E\arrowvert_{U})\oplus\Omega^{\bullet}(A\arrowvert_{V},E\arrowvert_{V})
. The Mayer-Vietoris sequence therefore is exact. The existence of the induced long exact sequence is a consequence of standard results of Homological Algebra (see e.g.
[
BT82
]
). The idea of the proof is sketched to deduce the explicit expression of the coboundary operator. Let
\omega\in\Omega^{\bullet}(A\arrowvert_{U\cap V},E\arrowvert_{U\cap V})
be a closed form. Since
(j_{1}^{*}-j_{0}^{*})
intertwines the differentials, it is true that
(j_{1}^{*}-j_{0}^{*})\circ\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}(\eta)=\operatorname{d}_{\nabla\arrowvert_{U\cap V}}(\omega)=0
,
for a closed form
\omega\in\Omega^{\bullet}(A\arrowvert_{U\cap V},E\arrowvert_{U\cap V})
and
\eta:=(-(\mu_{V})_{|_{U}}\,\omega,(\mu_{U})_{|_{V}}\,\omega)\in\Omega^{\bullet}(A\arrowvert_{U},E\arrowvert_{U})\oplus\Omega^{\bullet}(A\arrowvert_{V},E\arrowvert_{V})
as above. In other words,
-\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}((\mu_{V})\arrowvert_{U\cap V}\omega)
and
\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}((\mu_{U})\arrowvert_{U\cap V}\omega)
agree on
U\cap V
. By exactness of the Mayer-Vietoris sequence, there exists
\alpha\in\Omega^{q+1}(A,E)
such that
i^{*}(\alpha)=\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}\eta
.
\alpha
is a closed form because

i^{*}\circ\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}\alpha=\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}\circ i^{*}(\alpha)=\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}\circ\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}\eta=0
and
i^{*}
is injective. Define
\operatorname{d}_{\nabla}^{*}([\omega]):=[\alpha]
. It is a standard exercise to prove that
\operatorname{d}^{*}_{\nabla}
is well defined. By definition of
i^{*}
, the coboundary operator is explicitly defined as in the theorem.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
A\to M
be a Lie algebroid over
M
admitting a finite good open cover (i.e. all open subsets and their intersections are contractible)
(U_{j})_{j\in J}
such that

\operatorname{H}^{k}_{\nabla\arrowvert_{U_{j}}}(A\arrowvert_{U_{j}},E\arrowvert_{U_{j}})
is finite-dimensional for each
j\in J
. Then

\operatorname{H}_{\nabla}^{k}(A,E)
is finite-dimensional.

Proof.

See the proof of Proposition 5.3.1 in
[
BT82
]
.
∎

More concretely, this yields an alternative basic proof for the following well known result.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
A\to M
be a transitive Lie algebroid over a compact
M
. Then each twisted Lie algebroid cohomology of
A
is finite-dimensional.

Proof.

The compact manifold
M
admits a finite good cover
(U_{j})_{j\in J}
. Moreover the generalized Poincaré Lemma
5.4
implies that
\operatorname{H}^{k}_{\nabla\arrowvert_{U_{j}}}(A\arrowvert_{U_{j}},E\arrowvert_{U_{j}})
is isomorphic to the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of the isotropy Lie algebra with values in a representation, which is finite dimensional by definition (the vector spaces of the chain complex are finite dimensional). Conclude using Corollary
6.2
.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

Corollary
6.3
is usually proven by observing that the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of transitive Lie algebroids is elliptic (see
[
Kř10
]
). The cohomology therefore is finite dimensional if the base manifold is compact (see
[
AB67
]
).
This arguments is used for instance in
[
Wal23
]
.

6.2.
A Künneth formula.

A Künneth formula is proved as an application of the generalised Poincaré lemma
5.4
and the Mayer-Vietoris principle
6.1
.

Let
A\to M
and
B\to N
be Lie algebroids. Set the notation
\operatorname{Pr}_{A}\colon A\times B\to A
,
(a,b)\mapsto a
, and accordingly
\operatorname{Pr}_{B}\colon A\times B\to B
,
\operatorname{pr}_{M}\colon M\times N\to M
and
\operatorname{pr}_{N}\colon M\times N\to N
for the projection morphisms in the respective categories.
Let
\nabla^{A}
be an
A
-representation on the vector bundle
E\to M
and let
\nabla^{B}
be a
B
-representation on
F\to N
. The vector bundle
\operatorname{pr}_{M}^{!}E\otimes\operatorname{pr}_{N}^{!}F
is the tensor product of the pullback vector bundles on
M\times N
. It is naturally equipped with the connection
\nabla
defined by the Leibniz rule and

(14)

\displaystyle\nabla_{(a,b)}(\operatorname{pr}_{M}^{!}e\otimes\operatorname{pr}_{N}^{!}f)

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{Pr}^{*}_{A}\nabla^{A})_{(a,b)}(\operatorname{pr}_{M}^{!}e)\otimes\operatorname{pr}_{N}^{!}f+\operatorname{pr_{M}}^{!}e\otimes(\operatorname{Pr}^{*}_{B}\nabla^{B})_{(a,b)}(\operatorname{pr}_{N}^{!}f)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{pr}_{M}^{!}(\nabla^{A}_{a}e)\otimes\operatorname{pr}_{N}^{!}f+\operatorname{pr}^{!}_{M}e\otimes\operatorname{pr}_{N}^{!}(\nabla^{B}_{b}f)

for
a\in\Gamma(A)
,
b\in\Gamma(B)
,
(a,b):=\operatorname{pr}^{!}_{M}a+\operatorname{pr}^{!}_{N}b\in\Gamma(A\times B)
,
e\in\Gamma(E)
and
f\in\Gamma(F)
.

Lemma 6.5
.

In the situation above, the connection
\nabla
is well defined and flat.

Proof.

\nabla
is linear in its first entry by definition. Moreover (
14
)
is compatible with the Leibniz rule on pullbacks of functions since

(15)

\begin{split}&\nabla_{(a,b)}(\operatorname{pr}_{M}^{!}(g\cdot e)\otimes\operatorname{pr}_{N}^{!}f)\\
&=\operatorname{pr}^{*}_{M}g\cdot\operatorname{pr}^{!}_{M}(\nabla_{a}e)\otimes\operatorname{pr}_{N}^{!}f+\operatorname{pr}_{M}^{!}(\rho_{A}(a)(g)\cdot e)\otimes\operatorname{pr}^{!}_{N}f+\operatorname{pr}^{*}_{M}g\cdot\operatorname{pr}_{M}^{!}e\otimes\operatorname{pr}_{N}^{!}(\nabla_{b}f)\\
&=\operatorname{pr}^{*}_{M}g\cdot\nabla_{(a,b)}(e\otimes f)+\rho(a,b)(\operatorname{pr}^{*}_{M}g)\cdot e\otimes\operatorname{pr}_{N}^{!}f,\end{split}

for
g\in C^{\infty}(M)
,
(a,b)\in\Gamma(A\times B)
,
e\in\Gamma(E)
and
f\in\Gamma(F)
. In the same manner,

\nabla_{(a,b)}(\operatorname{pr}_{M}^{!}e\otimes\operatorname{pr}_{N}^{!}(g\cdot f))

for
g\in C^{\infty}(N)
,
(a,b)\in\Gamma(A\times B)
,
e\in\Gamma(E)
and
f\in\Gamma(F)
. Hence the Leibniz rule can be used in order to extend (
14

The construction uses three linear flows satisfying the hypothesis of Corollary
7.5
: the flow
\Psi
on
A
of
\widehat{[\partial_{t}\times 0,\cdot]}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(TI\times A)
, the flow
\Xi
of
\widehat{\nabla_{X}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(F)
on
F
and the smooth flow
\xi
of
\partial_{t}\times 0\in\mathfrak{X}(I\times M)
on
I\times M
.

First consider the section
X=\partial_{t}\times 0
of
TI\times A\to I\times M
.
Its anchor is
\partial_{t}\times 0\in\mathfrak{X}(I\times M)
with the flow

\xi\colon\Omega:=\{(\lambda,s,m)\in\mathbb{R}\times I\times M\mid\lambda+s\in I\}\to I\times M,

(\lambda,(t,m))\mapsto(t+\lambda,m).

By construction, and using Lemma
7.3
, the flow

\Psi\colon\Omega^{A}:=\{(\lambda,v_{s},a_{m})\in\mathbb{R}\times TI\times A\mid\lambda+s\in I\}\to TI\times A

of
\widehat{[X,\cdot]}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(TI\times A)

satisfies

(23)

\Psi_{\lambda}^{\star}(0,a)=(0,a)

on
((I-\lambda)\cap I)\times M
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and all
a\in\Gamma(A)
,
since
[X,(0,a)]=0
for all
a\in\Gamma(A)
, where
(0,a)
is the section of
TI\times A\to I\times M
given by
(s,m)\mapsto(0_{s},a(m))
.
Hence
\Psi
is given by

\Psi(\lambda,r\partial_{t}\arrowvert_{s},a_{m})=(r\partial_{t}\arrowvert_{s+\lambda},a_{m})

for
(\lambda,r\partial_{t}\arrowvert_{s},a_{m})\in\Omega^{A}
.

Consider the flow

\Xi\colon\tilde{\Omega}=\{(\lambda,e_{(s,m)})\in\mathbb{R}\times F\mid\lambda+s\in I\}\to F

of
\widehat{\nabla_{X}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(F)
.
For all
s,t\in I
, set

\theta_{t,s}:=\Xi_{s-t}\arrowvert_{\iota_{t}^{!}F}\colon\iota_{t}^{!}F\to\iota_{s}^{!}F.

Then the map
\theta_{t,s}
is an isomorphism of vector bundles over
M
, since
\Xi_{s-t}
is an isomorphism of vector bundles over
\xi_{s-t}
.

Choose an arbitrary time
t\in I
. For each section
e\in\Gamma_{M}(\iota_{t}^{!}F)
define

\tilde{e}\in\Gamma(F)
by

\tilde{e}(s,m)=\Xi(s-t,e(m))=\theta_{t,s}(e(m)).

Then for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
s\in(I-\lambda)\cap I
and
m\in M

(\Xi_{\lambda}^{\star}\tilde{e})(s,m)=\Xi_{-\lambda}(\tilde{e}(s+\lambda,m))=\Xi(s+\lambda-t-\lambda,e(m))=\Xi(s-t,e(m))=\tilde{e}(s,m).

This shows that

(24)

\tilde{e}=\Xi_{\lambda}^{\star}\tilde{e}

on
(I\cap(I-\lambda))\times M
.

In addition, for all
s\in I
and
m\in M
, compute

(25)

(\iota_{s}^{!}\tilde{e})(m)=\tilde{e}(s,m)=\Xi(s-t,e(m))=\theta_{t,s}(e(m)).

Hence
\iota_{s}^{!}\tilde{e}=\theta_{t,s}\circ e
for all
s\in I
. In particular,

(26)

\iota_{t}^{!}\tilde{e}=e.

As a consequence of the considerations above, according to Corollary
7.5
, for all
a\in\Gamma(A)
, all
e\in\Gamma(\iota_{t}^{!}F)
and all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

\nabla_{(0,a)}\tilde{e}=\nabla_{\Psi_{\lambda}^{\star}(0,a)}\Xi^{\star}_{\lambda}\tilde{e}=\Xi^{\star}_{\lambda}(\nabla_{(0,a)}\tilde{e})

on
(I\cap(I-\lambda))\times M
.
This implies for
(s,m)\in I\times M

\begin{split}(\mathcal{I}_{s}^{*}\nabla)_{a(m)}(\theta_{t,s}\circ e)&=(\mathcal{I}_{s}^{*}\nabla)_{a(m)}(\iota_{s}^{!}\tilde{e})=\nabla_{(0_{s},a(m))}\tilde{e}\\
&=\Xi^{\star}_{t-s}(\nabla_{(0,a)}\tilde{e})(s,m)=\Xi_{s-t}\left(\nabla_{(0_{t},a(m))}\tilde{e}\right)\\
&=\Xi_{s-t}\left((\mathcal{I}_{t}^{*}\nabla)_{a(m)}\iota^{!}_{t}\tilde{e}\right)=\Xi_{s-t}\left((\mathcal{I}_{t}^{*}\nabla)_{a(m)}e\right),\end{split}

for all
a\in\Gamma(A)
and
e\in\Gamma(\iota_{t}^{!}F)
.
This is equivalent to

(27)

\theta_{s,t}\circ(\mathcal{I}_{s}^{*}\nabla)\circ\theta_{t,s}=\mathcal{I}_{t}^{*}\nabla.

Hence
\theta_{t,s}
is a gauge equivalence from
\mathcal{I}_{t}^{*}\nabla
to
\mathcal{I}_{s}^{*}\nabla
.

7.3.2.

One key idea is the fact that

is gauge equivalent to flat connections

to construct the isomorphism

Consider for each

is an isomorphism
of vector bundles
over the identity on

As a consequence for

\begin{split}\Theta^{s}\circ(\Theta^{t})^{-1}(r,e)&=\Theta^{s}(\Xi(r-t,e))=(r,\Xi(s-r,\Xi(r-t,e)))\\
&=(r,\Xi(s-t,e))=(r,\theta_{t,s}(e))\end{split}

for all

and

. Denote this map by
\Theta_{t,s}\colon I\times\iota_{t}^{!}F\to I\times\iota_{s}^{!}F
,

\Theta_{t,s}=\Theta^{s}\circ(\Theta^{t})^{-1}\colon(r,e)\mapsto(r,\theta_{t,s}e).

For each

the flat connection
\nabla
is hence gauge equivalent to the flat connection

defined by

\nabla^{t}:=\Theta^{t}\circ\nabla\circ(\Theta^{t})^{-1}

and the cohomologies defined by

and
\nabla^{t}
are isomorphic via

By definition for
s,t\in I

\nabla^{t}=\Theta_{s,t}\circ\nabla^{s}\circ\Theta_{t,s}.

Hence the cohomologies

and
H^{\bullet}_{\nabla^{s}}(TI\times A,I\times\iota_{s}^{!}F)
are isomorphic via

This step has hence constructed the commutative diagrams of isomorphisms

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{\nabla}(TI\times A,F)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{\nabla^{t}}(TI\times A,I\times\iota_{t}^{!}F)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{\nabla^{s}}(TI\times A,I\times\iota_{s}^{!}F)}}

for all

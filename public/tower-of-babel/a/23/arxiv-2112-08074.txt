Thin knots and the Cabling Conjecture
Robert DeYeso III

Abstract
. The Cabling Conjecture of González-Acuña and Short holds that only cable knots admit Dehn surgery to a manifold containing an essential sphere. We approach this conjecture for thin knots using Heegaard Floer homology, primarily via immersed curves techniques inspired by Hanselman’s work on the Cosmetic Surgery Conjecture. We show that almost all thin knots satisfy the Cabling Conjecture, with possible exception coming from a (conjecturally non-existent) collection of thin, hyperbolic, L-space knots. This result serves as a reproof that the Cabling Conjecture is satisfied by alternating knots, and also a new proof that thin, slice knots satisfy the Cabling Conjecture.

Introduction

]
. Additionally, satellite

]

]

with coefficients in

]

]

.

If a thin, hyperbolic knot

It is conjectured that the only thin, L-space knots are the torus knots

. Provided this is true, there would not exist thin, hyperbolic, L-space knots and so Theorem
1.2

Corollary 1.3
.

Alternating knots satisfy the Cabling Conjecture.

The next corollary follows because the only thin, slice,
L
-space knot is the unknot.

Corollary 1.4
.

Thin, slice knots satisfy the Cabling Conjecture.

Corollary 1.5
.

Thin knots cannot admit two reducible surgeries.

[

Corollary 1.6

If
K

When
K
is a non-trivial knot in
S^{3}
with reducible surgery

, the surgery decomposes as a connected sum and the reducing slope satisfies
r\neq 0

[
Gab87
]
. We saw from the cabled knot example that the reducing slope is an integer and one of the connected summands is a lens space. The former and latter conditions occur for all reducible surgeries due to
[
GL87
]
and
[
GL89
]
, respectively. A reducible surgery can admit at most three connected summands due to the combined efforts of
[
Say98
]
[
VS99
]
[
How02
]
, in which case two summands are lens spaces and the remaining summand is an integer homology sphere. Since
S^{3}_{r}(K)
must have a non-trivial lens space summand, the integral reducing slope

satisifes
r\neq-1,0,1
. In
[
MS03
]
, Matignon and Sayari provide the following genus bound if

is non-cabled:

1<|r|\leq 2g(K)-1.

Heegaard Floer homology satisfies a Künneth formula for connected sums, and has proved very useful in general for studying Dehn surgery. If surgery along
K
produces precisely a connected sum of two lens spaces, then
K
must be a cabled knot due to
[
Gre15
]
. Further,
[
Gre15
]
together with
[
BZ98
]
shows that a hyperbolic knot in
S^{3}
cannot admit both a lens space surgery and a reducible surgery. Hom, Lidman, and Zufelt showed that a hyperbolic,
L
-space knot can admit at most one reducing slope, and the slope must be
2g(K)-1
after mirroring the knot to make the slope positive
[
HLZ15
]
. They also established a periodicity structure to the Heegaard Floer homology of a reducible surgery, which is invaluable to the proof strategy of Theorem
1.2
. We will involve these constraints via bordered invariants in the form of immersed curves.

Lipshitz, Ozsváth, and Thurston introduced bordered Heegaard Floer invariants for manifolds with torus boundary in
[
LOT18
]
. With
M_{0}\cup_{h}M_{1}
denoting a gluing of two manifolds along their torus boundaries, they prove a pairing theorem involving the two bordered invariants that recovers the Heegaard Floer homology of such a gluing. Hanselman, Rasmussen, and Watson reinterpreted these bordered invariants as collections of immersed curves in the punctured torus, and proved an analogous pairing theorem. In
[
Han19
]
, Hanselman used this package to obtain obstructions for cosmetic surgeries along knots in
S^{3}
, and our approach in this paper is largely inspired by this work.

Organization

We only consider surgeries with positive slopes, and mirror knots to achieve this whenever necessary. All manifolds are assumed to be compact, connected, oriented 3-manifolds, unless stated otherwise, and the coefficients in Floer homology are taken to belong to
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
. We will denote closed manifolds by
X
or
Y
, and manifolds with (typically torus) boundary by
M
. Figures containing immersed curves invariants will have the curves for
S^{3}\setminus\nu K
in blue and the curves for the filling solid torus in red/purple.

Section 2 summarizes the relevant background from knot Floer homology and Heegaard Floer homology. It also contains an overview of immersed curves invariants, their general properties and form for thin knots, as well as their associated pairing theorem and how to compute Maslov grading differences.

Section 3 expands on the relative Maslov grading for immersed curves invariants of complements of thin knots. Along the way we set up formulas for components of the grading difference formula in terms of
\tau(K)
.

Section 4 uses these relations to generate obstructions to periodicity for various cases of
r
in relation to
\tau(K)
and
g(K)
. It hosts a sizable collection of lemmas for the cases with
|\tau(K)|<g(K)
.
Section 5 resolves the remaining cases where
|\tau(K)|=g(K)
, including some that use absolute grading information. Afterward, all lemmas are collected to handle the proof of the theorem.

(and possibly
[r/2]
) are symmetric in this way by default.

Recall that the smallest element of
MR^{[s]}
is the relative grading of an intersection belonging to the
0
th column of
\widetilde{T}
, which is either the reference intersection
a^{s}
or a nearby right/left intersection. This means that we can capture
\text{Width}(MR^{[s]})
by considering non-negative intersections associated to both
[s]
and
[-s]
. Note that since
\text{parity}([s])=\text{parity}([-s])
, the need to translate a multiset by 1 is consistent if it arises.

Definition 4.1
.

The multiset
MR^{[s]}_{+}
consists of the relative gradings of intersections between
\widehat{\textit{HF}}(M)
and
l^{s}_{r}
that belong to non-negative columns of
\widetilde{T}
. We define
MR^{[s]}_{-}
analogously, and notice that
\text{Width}(MR^{[s]})=\text{max}\left\{\text{Width}(MR^{[s]}_{+}),\text{Width}(MR^{[s]}_{-})\right\}
.

Due to how genus detection is expressed by
\widehat{\textit{HF}}(M)
, either
\overline{\gamma}
achieves height
g(K)
(equivalent to
|\tau(K)|=g(K)
), or only a simple figure-eight at height
g(K)-1
achieves this desired height (equivalent to
|\tau(K)|<g(K))
. We will divide the problem among these two cases, starting with the latter.

Case A:
\mathbf{|\tau(K)|<g(K)}
. Since
|\tau(K)|<g(K)
, there exists a simple figure-eight component at height
g(K)-1
. Let
[s^{\prime}]
be the
\text{spin}^{c}
structure for which
l^{s^{\prime}}_{r}
intersects this simple figure-eight, which means
w_{s^{\prime}}=\frac{g(K)-1-s^{\prime}}{r}
. Our potential reducing slopes divide this case into two subcases. We see that
[s^{\prime}]=g(K)-1
when
r\geq 2(g(K)-1)
, which implies
w_{s^{\prime}}=0
. Otherwise
w_{s^{\prime}}>0
, which is the easier starting point.

Case A1:
\mathbf{w_{s^{\prime}}>0}
. In this situation, we will show that
\text{Width}(MR^{[s^{\prime}]})
is maximal.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
K
is thin,
|\tau(K)|<g(K)
, and
1<r<2(g(K)-1)
. Then there exists an
[s^{\prime}]\in\text{Spin}^{c}(S^{3}_{r}(K))
for which every
[s]\neq[\pm s^{\prime}]
satisfies
MR^{[s]}\not\cong MR^{[s^{\prime}]}
up to translation.

Proof.

For some
[s]\neq[\pm s^{\prime}]
, the largest possible relative grading that
MR^{[s]}_{+}
can achieve is associated to an intersection of some hypothetical simple figure-eight at largest height. Looking at the terms in the grading difference formula for a bigon from
a^{s}
to such a generator, we see that the
2\text{Wind}(P)
term satisifes
2\text{Wind}(P)\geq 2n
while the other term is
-1-\tau(K)-n
. For this reason, we will suppose that
\widehat{\textit{HF}}(M)
has a simple figure-eight at height
n
, taken to be the largest integer satisfying both
n<g(K)-1
and
n\equiv s\,\,(\text{mod}\,\,r)
. Let
P^{\prime}
be the bigon between
a^{s^{\prime}}
and
y^{g-1}
, and
P
the bigon between
a^{s}
and
y^{n}
. Because the choice of
a^{s^{\prime}}
depends on
\tau(K)
, we will handle the
\tau(K)\geq 0
subcase first before handling the
\tau(K)<0
subcase.

Subcase A1a:
\mathbf{\tau(K)\geq 0}
. Due to Lemma
3.2
, Proposition
3.3
, and Proposition
3.4
,
\text{Width}(MR^{[s]}_{+})
is nearly determined by
M_{rel}(y^{n})
. We have
M_{rel}(y^{n})\leq\text{Width}(MR^{[s]}_{+})\leq M_{rel}(y^{n})+1
, with either equality depending on whether
a^{s}
is the smallest relatively graded intersection. To compare widths, we compute

M_{rel}(y^{g-1})=2\left(\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}+\sum_{i=1}^{w_{s^{\prime}}}(s^{\prime}+ir)\right)-1-(s^{\prime}+w_{s^{\prime}}r),

and likewise

M_{rel}(y^{n})=2\left(\widetilde{H}_{s}+\sum_{i=1}^{w_{s}}(s+ir)\right)-1-(s+w_{s}r).

Their difference is then

\displaystyle M_{rel}(y^{g-1})-M_{rel}(y^{n})

\displaystyle=2\left(\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}+\sum_{i=1}^{w_{s^{\prime}}}(s^{\prime}+ir)-\left(\widetilde{H}_{s}+\sum_{i=1}^{w_{s}}(s+ir)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,-(s^{\prime}+w_{s^{\prime}}r-(s+w_{s}r))

\displaystyle=2\left((\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}-\widetilde{H}_{s})+\sum_{i=1}^{w_{s^{\prime}}}(s^{\prime}+ir)-\sum_{i=1}^{w_{s}}(s+ir)\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,-(s^{\prime}-s)-r(w_{s^{\prime}}-w_{s}).

If
s<s^{\prime}
so that
w_{s}=w_{s^{\prime}}
, then

\displaystyle M_{rel}(y^{g-1})-M_{rel}(y^{n})

\displaystyle=2((\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}-\widetilde{H}_{s})+w_{s^{\prime}}(s^{\prime}-s))-(s^{\prime}-s)

\displaystyle=2(\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}-\widetilde{H}_{s})+(2w_{s^{\prime}}-1)(s^{\prime}-s)

\displaystyle\geq 1,

since
w_{s^{\prime}}>0
and
s^{\prime}>s
implies that
\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}\geq\widetilde{H}_{s}
.

If
s>s^{\prime}
so that
w_{s}=w_{s^{\prime}}-1
, then shifting
P
one column to the right in
\widetilde{T}
(see Figure
11
) provides

\displaystyle M_{rel}(y^{g-1})-M_{rel}(y^{n})

\displaystyle=2\left((\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}-\widetilde{H}_{s})+\sum_{i=1}^{w_{s^{\prime}}}(s^{\prime}+ir)-\sum_{i=1}^{w_{s}}(s+ir)\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,-(s^{\prime}-s)-r(w_{s^{\prime}}-w_{s})

\displaystyle(\text{column shift})\,\,\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle=2\left((\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}-\widetilde{H}_{s})+\sum_{i=1}^{w_{s^{\prime}}}(s^{\prime}+ir)-\sum_{i=2}^{w_{s^{\prime}}}(s+(i-1)r)\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,-(s^{\prime}+r-s)

\displaystyle=2\left((\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}-\widetilde{H}_{s})+(s^{\prime}+r)+\sum_{i=2}^{w_{s}^{\prime}}(s^{\prime}+ir)-\sum_{i=2}^{w_{s^{\prime}}}(s+(i-1)r)\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,-(s^{\prime}+r-s)

\displaystyle=2\left((\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}+s-\widetilde{H}_{s})+(s^{\prime}+r-s)+(w_{s^{\prime}}-1)(s^{\prime}+r-s)\right)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,-(s^{\prime}+r-s)

\displaystyle=2(\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}+(s-\widetilde{H}_{s}))+(2w_{s^{\prime}}-1)(s^{\prime}+r-s)

\displaystyle=2(\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}-\widetilde{V}_{s})+(2w_{s^{\prime}}-1)(s^{\prime}+r-s)

\displaystyle=2(\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}-\widetilde{H}_{-s})+(2w_{s^{\prime}}-1)(s^{\prime}+r-s).

Notice that
s^{\prime}+s-1\leq 2(\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}-\widetilde{H}_{-s})\leq s^{\prime}+s
depending on the parities of
s
and
s^{\prime}
together with
s>s^{\prime}
. Then we have

\displaystyle M_{rel}(y^{g-1})-M_{rel}(y^{n})

\displaystyle=2(\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}-\widetilde{H}_{-s})+(2w_{s^{\prime}}-1)(s^{\prime}+r-s)

\displaystyle\geq s^{\prime}+s-1+(2w_{s^{\prime}}-1)(s^{\prime}+r-s)

\displaystyle\geq s^{\prime}+s-1+s^{\prime}+r-s

\displaystyle=2s^{\prime}-1+r

\displaystyle>1,

since
w_{s^{\prime}}>0
and
s^{\prime}<\frac{r-1}{2}
if there exists an
s>s^{\prime}
.

Case A2:
\mathbf{w_{s^{\prime}}=0}
. Recall that in this case we have

, so let us consider

\tau(K)\geq 0
, the surgery slope is large enough so that every intersection lies in the
0
th column of

. Width alone as an invariant won’t be enough, so we will also need to appeal to the multiplicities of the elements of the relative grading multisets. They will be used to show that only

\tau(K)<0
, we need far less sublety.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
K
is thin,
0\leq\tau(K)<g(K)
, and
r=2g(K)-1
. Then there exists an
[s^{\prime}]\in\text{Spin}^{c}(S^{3}_{r}(K))
for which
[s]\neq[\pm s^{\prime}]
satisfies
MR^{[s]}\cong MR^{[s^{\prime}]}
up to translation only if
\text{parity}([s])=\text{parity}([s^{\prime}])
.

Proof.

The
\text{spin}^{c}
structure
[s^{\prime}]
we want to consider has
[s^{\prime}]=g(K)-1
. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that some
[s]\neq[\pm s^{\prime}]
satisfies
MR^{[s]}\cong MR^{[s^{\prime}]}
up to translation and
\text{parity}(s)\neq\text{parity}(s^{\prime})
. We know that
r>1
forces
g(K)>1
, and also that each
l^{s}_{r}
intersects
\widehat{\textit{HF}}(M)
exactly once due to this large surgery slope. Because the choice of reference generator
a^{s^{\prime}}
depends on
\tau(K)
, let us split into two cases:
\tau(K)\geq 0
and
\tau(K)<0
.

Assume
\tau(K)\geq 0
. Because all intersections lie within the 0th column of
\widetilde{T}
, we will instead use the hyperelliptic involution invariance of
\widehat{\textit{HF}}(M)
to only consider
s\geq 0
. If
\widehat{\textit{HF}}(M)
has no simple figure-eight at height
s
, then
\text{Width}(MR^{[s]})=0
immediately does not match
\text{Width}(MR^{[s^{\prime}]})\geq 1
, so we may as well assume that there is a simple figure-eight at height
s
. We have
M_{rel}(y^{s^{\prime}})=2\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}-1-\tau(K)-s^{\prime}=s^{\prime}-1-\tau(K)
by Lemma
3.2
and Proposition
3.4
, since
\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}=s^{\prime}
when
\tau(K)\leq g(K)-1=s^{\prime}
. Further,

\displaystyle M_{rel}(y^{s^{\prime}})-M_{rel}(y^{s})

\displaystyle=2\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}-1-\tau(K)-s^{\prime}-(2\widetilde{H}_{s}-1-\tau(K)-s)

\displaystyle=2(\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}-\widetilde{H}_{s})-(s^{\prime}-s).

If
s>\tau(K)
, then
\widetilde{H}_{s}=s
implies that
M_{rel}(y^{s^{\prime}})-M_{rel}(y^{s})=s^{\prime}-s\geq 1
. But then

\text{Width}(MR^{[s^{\prime}]}=M_{rel}(y^{s^{\prime}})+1>M_{rel}(y^{s})+1\geq\text{Width}(MR^{[s]}),

so we must have
s\leq\tau(K)
together with
\text{Width}(MR^{[s]})=1
. Notice that
\text{Width}(MR^{[s^{\prime}]})=M_{rel}(y^{s^{\prime}})+1=s^{\prime}-\tau(K)>1
if
\tau(K)<s^{\prime}-1
, and so we are also forced to have either
\tau(K)=s^{\prime}-1
or
\tau(K)=s^{\prime}
. In both cases we have
\text{Width}(MR^{[s^{\prime}]})=1
. Since using width as an invariant has been exhausted, let us count multiplicities of elements of the
MR^{[s]}
’s next.

Recall that
e_{n}
denotes the number of simple figure-eights at height
n
of
\widehat{\textit{HF}}(M)
. Further, we need
e_{s}=e_{s^{\prime}}
in order to have
|MR^{[s]}|=|MR^{[s^{\prime}]}|
. We have assumed that
\text{parity}([s])\neq\text{parity}([s^{\prime}])
, so one of these two multisets contains

and must be translated by 1 to make 0 the smallest element. This translated multiset will then contain
0

0
with multiplicity
e_{s^{\prime}}+1
. This is the desired contradiction.
∎

5.

With the case analysis for

Case B:
\mathbf{|\tau(K)|=g(K)}

\overline{\gamma}

[s^{\prime}]
so that
g-1\equiv s^{\prime}\,\,(\text{mod}\,\,r)

w_{s}
, except now modifying it to just be the largest multiple of
r

\tau(K)=-g(K)

Case B1:
\mathbf{\tau(K)=-g(K)}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
K
is thin with
\tau(K)=-g(K)
, and let
1<r\leq 2g(K)-1
. Then there exists an
[s^{\prime}]\in\text{Spin}^{c}(S^{3}_{r}(K))
for which every
[s]\neq[\pm s^{\prime}]
satisfies
MR^{[s]}\not\cong MR^{[s^{\prime}]}
up to translation.

Proof.

Recall the labeling scheme from Figure
5
. The reference intersection
a^{s}
is immediately to the left of the
0th
column if
s\geq 0
, and is similarly immediately to the right of the
0th
column if
s>0
. In general we will label these generators
x_{s}^{l}
and
x_{s}^{r}
, respectively. Let us dispense with the
w_{s^{\prime}}=0
case first.

Notice that each
MR^{[s]}
contains two elements whose difference is precisely
2|s|
. These arise from
M_{rel}(x_{s}^{r})-M_{rel}(x_{s}^{l})=2\widetilde{H}_{s}-1-(2\widetilde{V}_{s}-1)=2(\widetilde{H}_{s}-\widetilde{V}_{s})=2s
. We also see that
2\widetilde{H}_{s}-1\leq\text{Width}(MR^{[s]})\leq 2\widetilde{H}_{s}
if
s\leq 0
and
2\widetilde{V}_{s}-1\leq\text{Width}(MR^{[s]})\leq 2\widetilde{V}_{s}
if
s>0
, with the even equalities achieved if an appropriate generator from a simple figure-eight exists at height
s
. So if some
[s]\neq[\pm s^{\prime}]
is to achieve
MR^{[s]}\cong MR^{[s^{\prime}]}
up to translation, we should see that the widths of these multisets agree and that there exist pairs with grading differences
2|s|
and
2|s^{\prime}|
. These are only possibly simultaneously true if
\widetilde{V}_{s}=\widetilde{H}_{s^{\prime}}
and
\widetilde{H}_{s}=\widetilde{V}_{s^{\prime}}
, which forces
s=-s^{\prime}
with
K
thin. Thus,
w_{s^{\prime}}>0
.

If

, it follows that
d(L(r,1),[s])=\tfrac{s^{2}}{r}-s+\tfrac{r-1}{4}
. Summing the prior two equations and using Proposition
2.3
(with
V_{\frac{r-k}{2}}=\text{max}\{V_{\frac{r+k}{2}},V_{r-\frac{r+k}{2}}\}
) yields

\displaystyle 2\left(V_{0}-V_{\frac{r}{2}}+V_{\frac{k}{2}}-V_{\frac{r-k}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=d(L(r,1),[0])-d(L(r,1),[\tfrac{r}{2}])+d(L(r,1),[\tfrac{k}{2}])

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,-d(L(r,1),[\tfrac{r+k}{2}])

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{r^{2}}{4r}-\frac{r}{2}\right)+\left(\frac{k^{2}}{4r}-\frac{k}{2}\right)-\left(\frac{(r+k)^{2}}{4r}-\frac{r+k}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{r-k}{2},

Therefore, we have the following relationship between
r
,
k
, and the
V
’s associated to
K
:

(1)

\frac{r-k}{4}=(V_{0}-V_{\frac{r}{2}})+(V_{\frac{k}{2}}-V_{\frac{r-k}{2}}).

Notice that when
K
is thin and
\tau(K)\geq 0
, we have that

\displaystyle V_{0}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l c}\dfrac{\tau(K)+1}{2}&\text{if parity}(\tau(K))=1\\
\dfrac{\tau(K)}{2}&\text{if parity}(\tau(K))=0\\
\end{array}\right.

We will use this to generate contradictions, and break into cases since the values of
V_{\frac{r}{2}}
and
V_{\frac{r-k}{2}}
depend on
\tau(K)
. It will also be useful to use the fact that
k\leq\frac{r}{3}
.

Case C1:
\mathbf{\tau(K)=g(K)}
. Here we have
V_{\frac{r}{2}}=1
and
V_{\frac{r-k}{2}}>0
. We see that
V_{\frac{k}{2}}-V_{\frac{r-k}{2}}
is given by half the distance between
\frac{r-k}{2}-\frac{k}{2}
since
K
is thin. Thus,

V_{\frac{k}{2}}-V_{\frac{r-k}{2}}=\frac{r}{4}-\frac{k}{2}.

This together with Equation
1
above then yields

V_{0}=\frac{k}{4}+1.

If
\text{parity}(\tau(K))=1
, then we have

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\frac{\tau(K)+1}{2}=\frac{k}{4}+1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{\tau(K)-1}{2}=\frac{k}{4}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{\tau(K)-1}{2}\leq\frac{r}{12}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 6(\tau(K)-1)\leq 2(g(K)-1)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 6(\tau(K)-1)\leq 2(\tau(K)-1)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow 4\tau(K)\leq 4.

However this would imply
g(K)=\tau(K)=1\Rightarrow r=0
, a clear contradiction. If
\text{parity}(\tau(K))=0
, then similar reasoning yields
\tau(K)\leq 2
, which forces
r=\tau(K)=g(K)=2
. We return to immersed curves techniques to rule out this case by comparing the multiplicity of elements of
MR^{[0]}
and
MR^{[1]}
. Since
\text{parity}(\tau(K))=0
, we must translate
MR^{[0]}
by one so that
0
is its smallest element. The multiplicity of
0
in the translated
MR^{[0]}
is
e_{0}
, and the multiplicity of
0
in
MR^{[1]}
is
2e_{1}+2
. However if
S^{3}_{2}(K)
is reducible then Lemma
2.1
forces
e_{0}=2e_{1}+1
in order for dim
\widehat{\textit{HF}}(S^{3}_{2}(K),[1])=
dim
\widehat{\textit{HF}}(S^{3}_{2}(K),[0])
, generating the desired contradiction.

Case C2:
\mathbf{\tau(K)=g(K)-1}
. Once again
V_{\frac{r-k}{2}}>0
, and in this case we obtain
V_{0}=\frac{k}{4}
since
V_{\frac{r}{2}}=0
. Together with
r=2\tau(K)
, the argument of the previous case yields the contradiction
4\tau(K)\leq-4
when
\text{parity}(\tau(K))=1
or
4\tau(K)\leq 2
when
\text{parity}(\tau(K))=0
.

Case C3:
\mathbf{\tau(K)=g(K)-2}
. We still have
V_{\frac{r}{2}}=0
, and things are more interesting here since it is possible for
V_{\frac{r-k}{2}}=0
. This happens only if
k=2
, in which case Equation
1
becomes

\frac{r-2}{4}=V_{0}+V_{1}.

Curiously enough
\tau(K)=V_{0}+V_{1}
for a thin knot, and so this would force
\tau(K)=\frac{r-2}{4}=\frac{2(\tau(K)+1)-2}{4}\Leftrightarrow 4\tau(K)=2\tau(K)\Rightarrow\tau(K)=0
. However this forces
r=k
, a contradiction. Then we cannot have
k=2
, and so
V_{\frac{r-k}{2}}>0
and we once again have
V_{0}=\frac{k}{4}
. As with the previous cases, having
r=2(\tau(K)+1)
would yield the contradictions
6(\tau(K)+1)\leq 2(\tau(K)+1)
if
\text{parity}(\tau(K))=1
or
4\tau(K)\leq 2
if
\text{parity}(\tau(K))=0
. This completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Proof.

Suppose
S^{3}_{r}(K)
is reducible for
K
thin and hyperbolic. The Matignon-Sayari bound implies that
1<r\leq 2g(K)-1
, after mirroring the knot if necessary to make the surgery slope positive. Reducibility also gives
S^{3}_{r}(K)\cong Y\#Z
for some lens space
Y
and some
Z
with
|H^{2}(Z)|=k<\infty
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have
\widehat{\textit{HF}}(S^{3}_{r}(K),[s+\alpha k])\cong\widehat{\textit{HF}}(S^{3}_{r}(K),[s])
for arbitrary
[s],\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
. When
r<2(g(K)-1)
, Lemmas
4.2
,
5.1
, and
5.2
apply to show that there exists an
[s^{\prime}]\in\text{Spin}^{c}(S^{3}_{r}(K))
such that either
\widehat{\textit{HF}}(S^{3}_{r}(K),[s^{\prime}])
is relatively-graded isomorphic only to
\widehat{\textit{HF}}(S^{3}_{r}(K),[-s^{\prime}])
, or
\tau(K)=g(K)=r=3
. The latter is prevented by Lemma
5.4
, so we proceed with the former. Since
Y\not\cong S^{3}
, we see that
[s^{\prime}]
cannot be self-conjugate and also that
|H_{1}(Y)|=2
. This implies
Y=\mathbb{R}P^{3}
, as well as
k=|[s^{\prime}]-[-s^{\prime}]|=2|[s^{\prime}]|
. However, together this means 4 divides
r
, which is impossible when
S^{3}_{r}(K)
admits an
\mathbb{R}P^{3}
summand with
H_{1}(S^{3}_{r}(K))
cyclic.

Therefore, we must have
r\geq 2(g(K)-1)
. Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
cover
-g(K)<\tau(K)<g(K)-2
and Lemma
5.5
covers
\tau(K)\geq g(K)-2
for the possibility that
r=2(g(K)-1)
, and so we must have
r=2g(K)-1
. In this situation
k
is odd since
r
is odd, which means periodicity will cycle through
\text{spin}^{c}
structures with different parities. Then Lemmas
4.3
,
4.5
, and
5.1
apply to fully obstruct reducibility via the above argument if
\tau(K)\neq g(K)
. If
\tau(K)=g(K)
, our techniques have been exhausted and leave just the conclusion of Lemma
5.3
, showing that
K
must be an
L
-space knot.
∎

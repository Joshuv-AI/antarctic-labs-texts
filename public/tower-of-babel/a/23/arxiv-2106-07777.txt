Abstract

1.
Introduction

Let
(B,\mathfrak{b})
be a Noetherian local ring,
R
be a finitely generated positively graded
B
-algebra, and
{\mathfrak{m}}=[R]_{+}
be the graded irrelevant ideal of
R
.
This paper is motivated by the following two lines of research:

(a)

Recently there has been a lot of interest in the closely related notions of
algebras having liftable local cohomology
introduced by Kollár and Kovács
[
12
]
, and
cohomologically full rings
introduced by Dao, De Stefani and Ma
[
7
]
.
The study of these concepts has produced a number of important results, see, e.g.,
[
12
,
7
,
6
]
.
Of great interest to us is the important work of Kollár and Kovács
[
12
]
on the flatness and base change of the cohomologies of relative dualizing complexes.

For the case of modules, the correct extension of these notions seems to be the notion of
fiber-full modules
that was recently coined by Varbaro in
[
16
]
(also, see
[
20
]
).
Here we introduce and study a notion of fiber full-modules which is relative over the base ring
B
.
We say that a finitely generated graded
R
-module
M
is
fiber-full over
B
if
M
is a free
B
-module and the natural map

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\big(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M\big)\;\rightarrow\;\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\big(M/\mathfrak{b}M\big)

is surjective for all
i\geq 0
and
q\geq 1
(see
Section 2
; cf.
[
16
, Definition 3.8]
).

(b)

An important question is to characterize when the local cohomology modules
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)
are free
B
-modules for a finitely generated graded
R
-module
M
.
This question has been addressed in various contexts by Hochster and Roberts
[
10
, Theorem 3.4]
, by Kollár
[
13
, Theorem 78]
, by Smith
[
18
]
, and by Chardin and Simis in joint work
[
4
]
with the author of this paper.

Our main result is the following theorem that gives a local criterion characterizing the
B
-freeness of all the local cohomology modules
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)
.
This result shows that fiber-full modules are
exactly
the ones that enjoy the property that
M
is a free
B
-module and
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)
is a free
B
-module for all
i\geq 0
.
So, in a sense, it paves a bridge between
(a)
and
(b)
.

Theorem A

(
Theorem 2.15
)
.

Let
(B,\mathfrak{b})
be a Noetherian local ring,
R
be a finitely generated positively graded
B
-algebra, and
{\mathfrak{m}}=[R]_{+}
.
Let
T=B[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a positively graded polynomial ring over
B
such that we have a homogeneous surjection
T\twoheadrightarrow R
.
Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module and suppose that
M
is a free
B
-module.
Then, the following six conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)
is a free
B
-module for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

(2)

\text{Ext}_{T}^{i}(M,T)
is a free
B
-module for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

(3)

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M)
is a free
B/\mathfrak{b}^{q}
-module for all
0\leq i\leq r
and
q\geq 1
.

(4)

\text{Ext}_{T/\mathfrak{b}^{q}T}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M,T/\mathfrak{b}^{q}T)
is a free
B/\mathfrak{b}^{q}
-module for all
0\leq i\leq r
and
q\geq 1
.

(5)

The natural map

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\big(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M\big)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\big(M/\mathfrak{b}M\big)

is surjective for all
0\leq i\leq r
and
q\geq 1
.

(6)

The natural map

\text{Ext}_{T/\mathfrak{b}^{q}T}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}M,\omega_{T/\mathfrak{b}^{q}T})\rightarrow\text{Ext}_{T/\mathfrak{b}^{q}T}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M,\omega_{T/\mathfrak{b}^{q}T})

is injective for all
0\leq i\leq r
and
q\geq 1
, where
\omega_{T/\mathfrak{b}^{q}T}
denotes the graded canonical module of
T/\mathfrak{b}^{q}T
.

Moreover, when any of the above equivalent conditions is satisfied, we have the following isomorphisms

(i)

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)\;\cong\;\big(\text{Ext}_{T}^{r-i}(M,T(-\delta))\big)^{*_{B}}
where
\delta=\deg(x_{1})+\cdots+\deg(x_{r})
,

(ii)

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)\otimes_{B}C\;\xrightarrow{\cong}\;\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M\otimes_{B}C)
, and

(iii)

\text{Ext}_{T}^{i}(M,T)\otimes_{B}C\;\xrightarrow{\cong}\;\text{Ext}_{T\otimes_{B}C}^{i}(M\otimes_{B}C,T\otimes_{B}C)

for all integers
i
and any
B
-algebra
C
.

A couple of words regarding
Theorem A
are in place.
Because of the above theorem, fiber-full modules get six equivalent desirable definitions, and this makes them quite a versatile class of modules.
For fiber-full modules, we obtain a graded local duality theorem relative to the base ring
B
(see
Section 2
) and base change isomorphisms for all
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)
and
\text{Ext}_{T}^{i}(M,T)
.
Consider the morphism
f:X={\text{Spec}}(R)\rightarrow Y={\text{Spec}}(B)
and the relative dualizing complex
\omega_{X/Y}^{\bullet}
, and suppose that
f
is flat.
Since
f
is embeddable into the smooth morphism
\mathbb{A}_{B}^{r}={\text{Spec}}(T)\rightarrow{\text{Spec}}(B)
, it follows that the
-i
-th cohomology
h^{-i}(\omega_{X/Y}^{\bullet})
of
\omega_{X/Y}^{\bullet}
coincides with the sheafification of
\text{Ext}_{T}^{r-i}(R,T)
.
Therefore, in our current setting when we consider the special case
M=R
, parts
(2)
and
(iii)
of
Theorem A
recover the result of Kollár and Kovács
[
12
]
on the flatness and base change of
h^{-i}(\omega_{X/Y}^{\bullet})
.

Next, we concentrate on some applications of the theorem above.

We now work over an arbitrary Noetherian ring, and we are interested in studying the locus of fiber-fullness of a module.
Let
A
be a Noetherian ring,
R
be a finitely generated positively graded
A
-algebra, and
{\mathfrak{m}}=[R]_{+}
.
For any
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, let
k(\mathfrak{p})=A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the residue field of
\mathfrak{p}
.
For a finitely generated graded
R
-module
M
, we define the
fiber-full locus
of
M
by

\mathfrak{Fib}(M)\;=\;\big\{\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)\mid M\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ is fiber-full over }A_{\mathfrak{p}}\big\}.

Interestingly, it turns out that the fiber-full locus is very well-behaved:
\mathfrak{Fib}(M)
is always an open subset, and if
A
is generically reduced then
\mathfrak{Fib}(M)
is also dense.
The following theorem gives some results regarding the fiber-full locus of a module.

Theorem B

(
Theorem 3.2
)
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian ring,
R
be a finitely generated positively graded
A
-algebra, and
{\mathfrak{m}}=[R]_{+}
.
Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module.
Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

\mathfrak{Fib}(M)
is an open subset of
{\text{Spec}}(A)
.

(ii)

If
A
is generically reduced, then there is an element
a\in A
avoiding the minimal primes of
A
such that
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a projective
A_{a}
-module for all
i\geq 0
, and so
\mathfrak{Fib}(M)
is a dense subset of
{\text{Spec}}(A)
.

(iii)

For all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
the function

{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow\mathbb{N},\quad\mathfrak{p}\;\mapsto\;\dim_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M\otimes_{A}k(\mathfrak{p}))\right]_{\nu}\right)

is locally constant on
\mathfrak{Fib}(M)
.

Finally, as a consequence of our methods, in
Theorem 4.1
we give an alternative proof of an important result of Conca and Varbaro
[
6
]
, where they showed that for a given homogeneous ideal
I\subset S=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]\;({\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}))
the Hilbert functions of
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/I)
and
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/\text{in}_{<}(I))
coincide provided the initial ideal
\text{in}_{<}(I)
is square-free.
This result settled a previous conjecture of Herzog.

The basic outline of this paper is as follows.
In
Section 2
, we prove our main result, that is, we give the proof of
Theorem A
.
In
Section 3
, we obtain
Theorem B
.
Finally, in
Section 4
, we study square-free Gröbner degenerations.

2.
A local freeness criterion for local cohomology modules

In this section, we provide a local criterion for the freeness of local cohomology modules.
Throughout this section, the following setup is set in place.

Setup 2.1
.

Let
(B,\mathfrak{b})
be a Noetherian local ring.
Let
R
be a finitely generated positively graded
B
-algebra.
Let
T=B[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a positively graded polynomial ring over
B
(that is,
[T]_{0}=B
and
\deg(x_{i})>0
), such that we have a homogeneous surjection
T\twoheadrightarrow R
.
Let
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})\subset T
be the graded irrelevant ideal of
T
.
By abuse of notation, interchangeably,
{\mathfrak{m}}
will also denote the graded irrelevant ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}R=[R]_{+}=\bigoplus_{\nu\geq 1}[R]_{\nu}
of
R
.
For each
q\geq 1
, let
B_{q}:=B/\mathfrak{b}^{q}
,
R_{q}:=R\otimes_{B}B_{q}
and
T_{q}:=T\otimes_{B}B_{q}\cong B_{q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
.
Let
\delta:=\deg(x_{1})+\cdots+\deg(x_{r})\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 2.2
.

We begin by recalling the following basic facts:

(i)

For any
R
-module
M
, by the Independence Theorem (see
[
2
, Theorem 4.2.1]
) we can compute
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)
by considering
M
as a
T
-module.

(ii)

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r}(T)\cong\frac{1}{x_{1}\cdots x_{r}}B[x_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1}]
and
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(T)=0
for all
i\neq r
.

(iii)

Since
B
is a Noetherian local ring, there is no distinction between the notions of
B
-flat,
B
-projective and
B
-free for a finitely generated
B
-module (see, e.g.,
[
15
, §7]
).
It then follows that the properties of
B
-flat,
B
-projective and
B
-free are equivalent for a finitely generated graded
R
-module.

(iv)

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)=0
for all
i\geq r+1
and any
R
-module
M
.

(v)

If
M
is a finitely generated graded
R
-module, then for each
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
the graded component
{[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)]}_{\nu}
is a finitely generated
B
-module (see, e.g.,
[
9
, Theorem III.5.2]
,
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
).
So the conditions
B
-flat,
B
-projective and
B
-free are equivalent for
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)
.

Our main object of study is the following interesting class of modules (cf.
[
16
, Definition 3.8]
).
We introduce a notion which is relative over the base ring
B
.

Definition 2.3
.

A finitely generated graded
R
-module
M
is
fiber-full over
B
if
M
is a free
B
-module and the natural map

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}M)

is surjective for all
i\geq 0
and
q\geq 1
.

An equivalent definition for the notion of fiber-full is given by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module.
Then, the natural map

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M)\rightarrow\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}M)

is surjective if and only if the natural map

\text{Ext}_{T_{q}}^{r-i}(M/\mathfrak{b}M,\omega_{T_{q}})\rightarrow\text{Ext}_{T_{q}}^{r-i}(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M,\omega_{T_{q}})

is injective, where
\omega_{T_{q}}
denotes the graded canonical module of
T_{q}
.

Proof.

Note that
T_{q}
is a Cohen-Macaulay
∗
complete
∗
local ring with
∗
maximal ideal
(\mathfrak{b}+{\mathfrak{m}})T_{q}
of
∗
dimension
r
(under the notations of
[
3
]
).
Thus, the graded local duality theorem
[
3
, Theorem 3.6.19]
implies that the natural map
\text{H}_{\mathfrak{b}+{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M)\rightarrow\text{H}_{\mathfrak{b}+{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}M)
is surjective if and only if the natural map
\text{Ext}_{T_{q}}^{r-i}(M/\mathfrak{b}M,\omega_{T_{q}})\rightarrow\text{Ext}_{T_{q}}^{r-i}(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M,\omega_{T_{q}})
is injective.
Since
M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M
is
\mathfrak{b}
-torsion, the spectral sequence
E_{2}^{i,j}=\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\big(\text{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{j}(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M)\big)\Rightarrow\text{H}_{\mathfrak{b}+{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i+j}(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M)
yields the isomorphism
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M)\cong\text{H}_{\mathfrak{b}+{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M)
.
So, the result is clear.
∎

An explicit computation of the graded canonical module
\omega_{T_{q}}
is given in the remark below.

Remark 2.5
.

As in the proof of
Section 2
, we have
\text{H}_{\mathfrak{b}+{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r}(T_{q})\cong\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r}(T_{q})\cong\frac{1}{x_{1}\cdots x_{r}}B_{q}[x_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1}]

Consequently,
[
3
, Theorem 3.6.19]
gives the following isomorphism

\omega_{T_{q}}\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\text{Hom}_{B_{q}}\left(\left[\frac{1}{x_{1}\cdots x_{r}}B_{q}[x_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1}]\right]_{-\nu},\omega_{B_{q}}\right)\;\cong\;T_{q}(-\delta)\otimes_{B_{q}}\omega_{B_{q}}

where
\omega_{B_{q}}
denotes the canonical module of the Artinian local ring
B_{q}
.

For completeness, we show below that
Section 2
agrees with
[
16
, Definition 3.8]
under a general common setting.

Remark 2.6
.

Let
C=\mathbb{k}[t]
and

.
In

a finitely generated graded

is

Definition 2.7
.

For a graded
T
-module
M
, we denote the
B
-relative graded Matlis dual

\left(M\right)^{*_{B}}={}^{*}\text{Hom}_{B}(M,B):=\bigoplus_{\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\text{Hom}_{B}\left({\left[M\right]}_{-\nu},B\right).

Note that
\left(M\right)^{*_{B}}
has a natural structure of graded
T

From the canonical perfect pairing of free

{\left[T\right]}_{\nu}\otimes_{B}{\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r}(T)\right]}_{-\delta-\nu}\rightarrow{\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r}(T)\right]}_{-\delta}\cong B

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r}(T)\cong{\left(T(-\delta)\right)}^{*_{B}}={}^{*}\text{Hom}_{B}\left(T(-\delta),B\right).

Then, for a graded complex
F_{\bullet}
of finitely generated free
T

(1)

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r}(F_{\bullet})\;\cong\;{\Big(\text{Hom}_{T}(F_{\bullet},T(-\delta))\Big)}^{*_{B}}.

The lemma below is an important tool for us.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

be a finitely generated graded

Let
F_{\bullet}
be a graded free
T
-resolution of
M
by modules of finite rank.
If
M
is a free

-module, then

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M\otimes_{B}C)\cong\text{H}_{r-i}\big(\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r}(F_{\bullet})\otimes_{B}C\big)

for any
B
-algebra
C
and all integers

.

Proof.

See
[
4

.
∎

The next lemma provides a known base change isomorphism for local cohomology.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module.
Suppose that
M
is a free
B
-module and
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)
is a free
B
-module for all
i\geq 0
.
Then

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)\otimes_{B}C\;\xrightarrow{\cong}\;\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M\otimes_{B}C)

for all
i\geq 0
and any
B
-algebra
C
.

Proof.

Let
F_{\bullet}
be a graded free
T
-resolution of
M
by modules of finite rank, and set
G_{\bullet}:=\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r}(F_{\bullet})
.
By
Section 2
,
\text{H}_{i}(G_{\bullet})
is a free
B
-module for all
i\geq 0
.
Then, it follows that
\text{H}_{i}(G_{\bullet})\otimes_{B}C\xrightarrow{\cong}\text{H}_{i}(G_{\bullet}\otimes_{B}C)
for all
i\geq 0
(see, e.g.,
[
4
, Lemma 2.8]
).
By using
Section 2
again we obtain

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)\otimes_{B}C\;\cong\;\text{H}_{r-i}(G_{\bullet})\otimes_{B}C\;\xrightarrow{\cong}\;\text{H}_{r-i}(G_{\bullet}\otimes_{B}C)\;\cong\;\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M\otimes_{B}C),

and so the result follows.
∎

By using the property of exchange for local Ext’s (see
[
1
, Theorem 1.9]
,
[
14
, Theorem A.5]
), we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module and suppose that
M
is a free
B
-module.
Let
F_{\bullet}:\>\cdots\rightarrow F_{i}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}
be a graded free
T
-resolution of
M
by modules of finite rank.
Let

D_{M}^{i}\;:=\;\text{Coker}\big(\text{Hom}_{T}(F_{i-1},T)\rightarrow\text{Hom}_{T}(F_{i},T)\big)

for each
i\geq 0
.
Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

\text{Ext}_{T}^{i}(M,T)=0
for all
i\geq r+1
.

(ii)

D_{M}^{i}
is a free
B
-module for all
i\geq r+1
.

(iii)

If
\text{Ext}_{T}^{i}(M,T)
is a free
B
-module for all
0\leq i\leq r
, then

\text{Ext}_{T}^{i}(M,T)\otimes_{B}C\;\xrightarrow{\cong}\;\text{Ext}_{T\otimes_{B}C}^{i}(M\otimes_{B}C,T\otimes_{B}C)

for all
i\geq 0
any
B
-algebra
C
.

Proof.

(i)
Since
M
is a free
B
-module,
F_{\bullet}\otimes_{B}B/\mathfrak{b}
is a free
T_{1}
-resolution of
M/\mathfrak{b}M
.
By using Hilbert’s syzygy theorem on the polynomial ring
T_{1}\cong B/\mathfrak{b}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
, we get that
\text{Ext}_{T_{1}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}M,T_{1})=0
for all
i\geq r+1
.
In particular, the natural map

\text{Ext}_{T}^{i}(M,T)\otimes_{B}B/\mathfrak{b}\rightarrow\text{Ext}_{T_{1}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}M,T_{1})=0

is surjective for all
i\geq r+1
, however,
[
1
, Theorem 1.9]
or
[
14
, Theorem A.5]
imply that the map above is actually bijective.
As
\text{Ext}_{T}^{i}(M,T)
is a finitely generated graded
T
-module, each graded component
\left[\text{Ext}_{T}^{i}(M,T)\right]_{\nu}
is a finitely generated
B
-module.
Note that the condition
\text{Ext}_{T}^{i}(M,T)\otimes_{B}B/\mathfrak{b}=0
implies that

\left[\text{Ext}_{T}^{i}(M,T)\right]_{\nu}\otimes_{B}B/\mathfrak{b}=0

for all
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then, for all
i\geq r+1
, Nakayama’s lemma yields that
\left[\text{Ext}_{T}^{i}(M,T)\right]_{\nu}=0
for all
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and so
\text{Ext}_{T}^{i}(M,T)=0
, as required.

(ii)
Since
M
is
B
-free, by considering the complex
\text{Hom}_{T}(F_{\bullet},T)\otimes_{B}C
with
C
any
B
-algebra, we obtain the four-term exact sequence

(2)

0\rightarrow\text{Ext}_{T\otimes_{B}C}^{i}(M\otimes_{B}C,T\otimes_{B}C)\rightarrow D_{M}^{i}\otimes_{B}C\rightarrow\text{Hom}_{T}(F_{i+1},T)\otimes_{B}C\rightarrow D_{M}^{i+1}\otimes_{B}C\rightarrow 0

for all
i\geq 0
.
For all
i\geq r+1
, due to the computations of part (i), when we substitute
C=B
and
C=B/\mathfrak{b}
in
(2)
, we get the short exact sequences

(3)

0\rightarrow D_{M}^{i}\rightarrow\text{Hom}_{T}(F_{i+1},T)\rightarrow D_{M}^{i+1}\rightarrow 0

and

(4)

Section 2
, we have that

{[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)]}_{\nu}\,{\widehat{}}\;\xrightarrow{\cong}\;\underset{\leftarrow}{\lim}\,{[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M/\mathfrak{b}^{q}M)]}_{\nu}

is a flat
B
-module.
Finally, this implies that
{[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)]}_{\nu}
is a free
B
-module for each
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and so the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

3.
Fiber-full locus of a module

In this section, we provide some applications that follow from
Theorem 2.15
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian ring,
R
be a finitely generated positively graded
A
-algebra, and
{\mathfrak{m}}=[R]_{+}
be the graded irrelevant ideal of
R
. For any
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, let
k(\mathfrak{p}):=A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the residue field of
\mathfrak{p}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module.
The
fiber-full locus
of
M
is defined as

\mathfrak{Fib}(M)\;:=\;\big\{\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)\mid M\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ is fiber-full over }A_{\mathfrak{p}}\big\}

(see
Section 2
).

The following theorem gives some properties of the fiber-full locus of a module.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module.
Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

\mathfrak{Fib}(M)
is an open subset of
{\text{Spec}}(A)
.

(ii)

If
A
is generically reduced, then there is an element
a\in A
avoiding the minimal primes of
A
such that
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a projective
A_{a}
-module for all
i\geq 0
, and so
\mathfrak{Fib}(M)
is a dense subset of
{\text{Spec}}(A)
.

(iii)

For all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
the function

{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow\mathbb{N},\quad\mathfrak{p}\;\mapsto\;\dim_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M\otimes_{A}k(\mathfrak{p}))\right]_{\nu}\right)

is locally constant on
\mathfrak{Fib}(M)
.

Proof.

(i)
It is a known result that for a finitely generated graded
R
-module
L
, the following locus

U_{L}\;:=\;\big\{\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)\mid L\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ is a free }A_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{-module}\big\}

is an open subset of
{\text{Spec}}(A)
(see, e.g.,
[
4
, Lemma 2.5, Notation 2.6]
).
Similarly to
Section 2
, we choose a positively graded polynomial ring
T=A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
over
A
such that we have a homogeneous surjection
T\twoheadrightarrow R
.
Then by
Theorem 2.15
we obtain the equality

\mathfrak{Fib}(M)\;=\;U_{M}\,\cap\,U_{\text{Hom}_{T}(M,T)}\,\cap\,U_{\text{Ext}_{T}^{1}(M,T)}\,\cap\,\cdots\,\cap\,U_{\text{Ext}_{T}^{r}(M,T)},

and so the result follows.

(ii)
Suppose that
A
is generically reduced.
Let
\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{k}\}
be the minimal primes of
A
.
By part (i), we choose an ideal
I\subset A
such that
\mathfrak{Fib}(M)={\text{Spec}}(A)\setminus V(I)
.
As
A
is generically reduced,
A_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
is a field for any minimal prime
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, and so it is clear that
M\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
is fiber-full over
A_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}
.
This implies that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\not\supseteq I
.
Finally, the prime avoidance lemma gives an element
a\in A
such that
a\in I\setminus(\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathfrak{p}_{k})
.

(iii)
Fix any
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{Fib}(M)
.
Since
\mathfrak{Fib}(M)\subset{\text{Spec}}(A)
is an open subset by part (i), we can choose
0\neq a\in A
such that
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A_{a})\subset\mathfrak{Fib}(M)
.
Then, by using
Theorem 2.15
, we obtain that
[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)]_{\nu}\otimes_{A}A_{a}
is a finitely generated locally free
A_{a}
-module, and the base change isomorphism

\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M\otimes_{A}k(\mathfrak{q}))\;\cong\;\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)\otimes_{A}k(\mathfrak{q})

for all
\mathfrak{q}\in{\text{Spec}}(A_{a})
.
By invoking
[
19
,
Tag 00NX
]
there exists an open subset
V\subset{\text{Spec}}(A_{a})
containing
\mathfrak{p}
such that the function

{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow\mathbb{N},\quad\mathfrak{q}\;\mapsto\;\dim_{k(\mathfrak{q})}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)\right]_{\nu}\otimes_{A}k(\mathfrak{q})\right)

is constant on
V
.
Therefore, the proof is complete.
∎

4.
Square-free Gröbner degenerations à la Conca-Varbaro

In this section, we give our alternative proof of the main result of
[
6
]
.
Another alternative proof of this result was given in
[
16
]
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a positively graded polynomial ring and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\subset S
.
Let
A=\mathbb{k}[t]
be a polynomial ring.
Let
R=A\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}S\cong S[t]
be a polynomial with grading induced from
S
; i.e.,
x_{i}\in R
maintains the degree as an element of
S
and
\deg(t)=0
.
Let
<
be a term order on
S
.

Our proof of the following result is a simple consequence of our previous developments.

Theorem 4.1

(Conca-Varbaro)
.

Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal.
If the initial ideal
\text{in}_{<}(I)
is square-free, then

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/I)\right]_{\nu}\right)\;=\;\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/\text{in}_{<}(I))\right]_{\nu}\right)

for all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

We can choose a weight vector
\omega\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
such that the
\omega
-homogenization
J=\hom_{\omega}(I)\subset R
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

J
is an
R
-homogeneous ideal,

(ii)

R/J
is a free
A
-module,

(iii)

R/J\otimes_{A}A/(t)\cong S/\text{in}_{<}(I)
and,

(iv)

R/J\otimes_{A}\mathbb{k}(t)\cong S/I\otimes_{A}\mathbb{k}(t)

(see, e.g.,
[
8
, §15.8]
).
The residue fields of the prime ideals
(0)\subset A
and
(t)\subset A
are given by
k\big((0)\big)=\mathbb{k}(t)
and
k\big((t)\big)=\mathbb{k}
, respectively.
We have that
R/J\otimes_{A}A_{(t)}
is fiber-full over
A_{(t)}
by
[
6
, Proposition 2.3]
or
[
16
, Corollary 3.4]
.
Finally,
Theorem 3.2
implies that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/I)\right]_{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;\dim_{\mathbb{k}(t)}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\big(R/J\otimes_{A}\mathbb{k}(t)\big)\right]_{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}\big(R/J\otimes_{A}A/(t)\big)\right]_{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/\text{in}_{<}(I))\right]_{\nu}\right)

for all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
∎

For a finitely generated graded
S
-module
M
, we denote the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity and the depth of
M
by
\text{reg}(M)
and
0pt(M)
, respectively, and a non-zero Betti number
\beta_{i,i+j}(M):=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\big(\left[\text{Tor}_{i}^{S}(\mathbb{k},M)\right]_{i+j}\big)
is called extremal if
\beta_{h,h+k}(M)=0
for every
h\geq i,k\geq j
with
(h,k)\neq(i,j)
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal.
If
\text{in}_{<}(I)
is square-free, then
S/I
and
S/\text{in}_{<}(I)
have the same extremal Betti numbers, and, in particular,

0pt(S/I)=0pt(S/\text{in}_{<}(I))\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{reg}(S/I)=\text{reg}(S/\text{in}_{<}(I)).

Proof.

It follows from
Theorem 4.1
and
[
17
, Theorem 3.11]
.
∎

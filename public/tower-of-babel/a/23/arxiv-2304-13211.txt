Explaining enhanced UV divergence cancellations
Renata Kallosh

Abstract
We study supergravities with “enhanced UV divergence cancellations”. We show that all these cancellations are explained by a simple dimensional analysis of nonlinear local supersymmetry (NLS). We also show that in all cases where E7-type duality was used in the past via vanishing single scalar limit (SSL) to explain/predict UV cancellations one could have used dimensional analysis of NLS. The SSL constraints in d = 4 d=4 predict L ≤ 𝒩 − 2 L\leq\mathcal{N}-2 for UV finiteness, dimensional analysis of NLS predicts L ≤ 𝒩 − 1 L\leq\mathcal{N}-1 for UV finiteness, including enhanced cases like 𝒩 = 5 , L = 4 \mathcal{N}=5,L=4 .

III
Dimensional analysis of NLS

Consider first the case of pure gravity.
In
Kallosh:1974yh
in pure (no matter) gravity the analysis of
gauge-independent
perturbative UV divergences was performed based on a dimensional analysis of the
on shell
Riemannian space geometry where

R=R_{\mu\nu}=0\qquad R_{\mu\nu\lambda\delta}\neq 0

(15)

A single 2-loop UV divergence was predicted in
Kallosh:1974yh
based on the fact that in
d=4
the action

S^{2-loop}=\kappa^{2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\,R_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho\sigma}R_{\rho\sigma}{}^{\alpha\beta}

(16)

is dimensionless: -2 (from
\kappa^{2}
) -4 (from
d^{4}x
) +6 (from
R^{3}
) =0. The absence of other candidate CT’s at 2-loop order in
Kallosh:1974yh
was due to an algebraic fact that
only one of all independent scalar invariants in the four-dimensional Riemannian space can give a non-zero contribution on the mass shell when
R=R_{\mu\nu}=0
.

At higher loops, candidate CT’s depend on higher powers of the Riemann-Christoffel 4-tensor with insertion of covariant derivatives. In a symbolic form, the Lagrangian is a scalar, depending on various contraction of Riemann-Christoffel tensors with covariant derivatives. For the
L
-loop the CT is

S^{L}=\kappa^{2(L-1)}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}\,{\mathcal{L}}\Big((D_{\lambda})^{m},(R_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\mu\nu})^{n}\Big),

(17)

and

-2L-2+m+2n=0\,,\qquad L={m\over 2}+n-1\geq 2\ .

We assumed here that for the S-matrix
n\geq 3
.
At
L=2,m=0,n=3
we recover the 2-loop case.

Now we look at pure supergravity in the same spirit: the main difference is that we have bosonic and fermionic coordinates, but the on shell geometry is known.
In pure supergravity the dimensional analysis of candidate CT’s
Kallosh:1980fi
;
Howe:1980th
is based on the one shell
d=4
superspace
Brink:1979nt
;
Howe:1981gz
. The analog of the
L
-loop candidate CT’s
(
17
) respecting a nonlinear superspace geometry is given by

CT^{L\geq\mathcal{N}}=\kappa^{2(L-1)}\int d^{4}x\,d^{4\mathcal{N}}\theta\det E\,\mathcal{L}(x,\theta)\ ,

(18)

\mathcal{L}(x,\theta)=\,\chi_{\alpha\,ijk}\ \chi_{mnl}^{\alpha}\,D^{2(L-\mathcal{N})}\ \bar{\chi}_{\dot{\alpha}}^{ijk}\ \bar{\chi}^{\dot{\alpha}\,mnl}\ .

(19)

Here spinorial superfield
\chi_{\alpha\,ijk}(x,\theta)
is associated with the superspace torsion, whereas its derivatives define superspace curvatures.
The term
D^{2(L-\mathcal{N})}
in (
19
) symbolically denotes multiples of either spinor or spacetime covariant and
\mathcal{H}
-covariant derivatives with total dimension
2(L-\mathcal{N})
.
These expressions require that the classical equations of motion are valid since the superspace in
\mathcal{N}\geq 5
is available only on shell
Brink:1979nt
;
Howe:1981gz
. The dimensional analysis requires that

{\rm dim}\,\mathcal{L}(x,\theta)=2+2(L-\mathcal{N}),\ L\geq\mathcal{N},

(20)

and
the superspace Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(x,\theta)
has minimal dimension 2, the smallest possible dimension for a geometric Lagrangian. A dimension of the volume of superspace is

{\rm dim}\,[d^{4}x\,d^{4\mathcal{N}}\theta]=-4+2\mathcal{N}
. We conclude that in
d=4
candidate CT’s with nonlinear local supersymmetry are available at

L\geq\mathcal{N}\ ,

as was shown in
Kallosh:1980fi
;
Howe:1980th
. This is a dimensional analysis of NLS in

d=4
. An analogous dimensional analysis of NLS is available in other dimensions
Kallosh:2023css
.

\mathcal{N}=5,L=4,d=4

(21)

Investigation of this case revealed a cancellation of UV divergences in a sum of 82 diagrams found in
Bern:2014sna

From our perspective, this cancellation is explained by the simple dimensional analysis of nonlinear local supersymmetry. Indeed, according to
Kallosh:1980fi

L\geq L_{cr}=\mathcal{N}

1

\mathcal{N}=5,L=4

One may only wonder why this simple fact was not noticed long ago, and for the last 9 years this cancellation was considered as unexplained

Kallosh:2023asd

The second case in

\mathcal{N}=4,L=3,d=4

There are subtleties in this case since there are 1-loop

L=4

The third case in

\mathcal{N}=4,L=2,d=5

This case has UV divergence cancellation for

There are also subtleties in this case since and at the next loop order, namely, at

VI
Discussion of 12 predictions of dimensional analysis of NLS

Here we stress that dimensional analysis of NLS predictions is based on a
local
supersymmetry, there is no need to look into a more complicated analysis of global
\mathcal{G}
symmetry, for example
E_{7(7)}
in
\mathcal{N}=8
. And because it is a local symmetry, it is just a standard requirement of a fundamental consistency of the theory.

The analysis of 12 ultraviolet loop cancellations in supergravities predicted by dimensional analysis of NLS includes

all 3 known as enhanced ultraviolet cancellations
and another 9. These are 11 cases in
d=4
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=8,\,L=3,4,5,6,7\ ,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=6,\,L=3,4,5\ ,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=5,\,L=3,4\ ,

\displaystyle cN=4,\,L=3

(30)

and there is one in
d=5
,

\mathcal{N}=4,\,L=2\ .\\

All of these have the property that

d=4:~L<L_{cr}=\mathcal{N}\,,\quad d=5:~\mathcal{N}=4\,,~L<L_{cr}=3\ .\\

We have an interesting statistics here, since many of these are already confirmed by loop computations and support the case for unbroken dimensional analysis of NLS.

The existence of these cases points out towards significance of the difference between linearized candidate CT’s and nonlinear geometric CT’s. The nonlinear ones start at
L_{cr}
and exists at
L\geq L_{cr}
. The linearized ones exist at
L<L_{cr}
, but when NLS is unbroken, they do not appear as UV divergences.

The fact that in all these 12 cases there is the same explanation, via dimensional analysis of NLS, is satisfactory. We have argued in
Kallosh:2023asd
that in amplitudes only linear supersymmetry is manifest, the nonlinear features of supergravities are only partially explored via single scalar soft limits, see for example
Beisert:2010jx
;
Freedman:2018mrv
.
But SSL are less powerful than the constraints from the full E7 type
\mathcal{G}
-symmetry and local
\mathcal{H}
-symmetry which are both linearly realized and independent: shift symmetry SSL analysis is useful but incomplete, it predicts UV cancellation for
L<\mathcal{N}-2
but missing the case of
L=\mathcal{N}-1
, see Appendix.

It is important to stress here that in all cases of maximal supergravities in integer dimensions
d>4
there are known UV divergences at
L<L_{cr}
which break NLS
Kallosh:2023css
. So far,
d=4
looks special for maximal supergravities
Kallosh:2023asd
;
Kallosh:2023css
.

The half-maximal
d=4,L=3
and
d=5,L=2
cases of enhanced cancellation are easily explained by the fact that
L<L_{cr}
in both cases. Both of these enhanced cases have the following features: after enhanced cancellation at the next loop there is a UV divergence, namely
d=4,L=4
and
d=5,L=3
are UV divergent, and moreover, the NLS is broken by these UV divergences. In
d=4
this is known, see
Carrasco:2013ypa
,
Bern:2013uka
, in
d=5
we discuss it in
Kallosh:2023css
, based on the computations in
Bern:2014lha
.

In conclusion, 2 out of 3 cases of enhanced UV cancellations are not long lasting. Both cases of half-maximal supergravity in
d=4,5
already at the next loop order have UV divergences breaking nonlinear local supersymmetry. In case of
\mathcal{N}=5,L=4,d=4
we still have to learn what is the fate of the next loop order.

The dimensional analysis of NLS does not constrain the candidate CT’s for
L\geq L_{cr}
in the on shell Lorentz covariant superspace
Brink:1979nt
;
Howe:1981gz
, as shown in
Kallosh:1980fi
;
Howe:1980th
. The corresponding CT’s at
L\geq L_{cr}
are available at the level of a simple dimensional analysis. The next step in the analysis of NLS was performed in
Kallosh:2023asd
, where restriction on candidate CT’s for
L\geq L_{cr}
were studied. The conclusion there was, based on investigation of the action deformed by candidate CT, required by the preservation of E7-symmetry. It was found that the deformation breaks NSL.

Therefore unbroken full nonlinear local supersymmetry and E7-symmetry were found in
Kallosh:2023asd
to protect
\mathcal{N}\geq 5
supergravities in
d=4
from UV divergences even at
L\geq L_{cr}
. The computation of
\mathcal{N}=5,L=5
case will be the first to test this prediction.
Hopefully, more new loop computations will be performed and help to understand the theoretical analysis based on symmetries of the theory.

Appendix A
Why SSL predicts UV finiteness for
L\leq\mathcal{N}-2
whereas dimensional analysis of NLS predicts it for
L\leq\mathcal{N}-1

SSL was used in
Beisert:2010jx
;
Freedman:2018mrv
to study the restrictions on UV finiteness in
d=4
. The result was that for
L\leq\mathcal{N}-2
one can predict UV finiteness. This was one loop order below the most interesting case of enhanced cancellation in
\mathcal{N}=5,L=4,d=4
.

Meanwhile here we stated that dimensional analysis of NLS leads to much stronger results. It predicts the UV finiteness for
L\leq\mathcal{N}-1
, which include the most interesting case of enhanced cancellation in
\mathcal{N}=5,L=4,d=4
.

The difference can be explained by the fact that SSL is using only part of a full E7-type duality symmetry, and also a linear approximation of it. Meanwhile NLS is using a total group
\mathcal{G}
.

The vanishing soft single scalar limit is a requirement on amplitudes originating from the shift symmetry on scalars, which is part of the duality symmetry.

Consider for example,
E_{7(7)}
duality in
\mathcal{N}=8
supergravity and compare it with the shift symmetry on scalars.
The classical action
Cremmer:1979up
;
deWit:1982bul

with a local
SU(8)
symmetry before it is gauge fixed depends on 133 scalars represented by a 56-bein

\displaystyle{\cal V}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{ij}{}^{IJ}&v_{ijKL}\\
v^{klIJ}&u^{kl}{}_{KL}\\
\end{array}\right)\ .

The capital indices
I,J
refer to
E_{7(7)}
and small ones
ij
refer to
SU(8)
. The 56-bein transforms under a local
SU(8)
symmetry
U(x)
and a global
E_{7(7)}
symmetry
E
as follows

{\cal V}(x)\rightarrow U(x){\cal V}(x)E^{-1}\ .

(34)

These two symmetries are linearly realized and independent. Here
E\in E_{7(7)}
is in the fundamental 56-dimensional representation where

\displaystyle E=\exp\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda_{IJ}{}^{KL}&\Sigma_{IJPQ}\\
\Sigma^{MNKL}&\Lambda^{MN}{}_{PQ}\\
\end{array}\right)\ .

Duality symmetry in (
A
) consists of a diagonal transformation
\Lambda_{IJ}{}^{KL}=\delta_{[I}{}^{K}\Lambda_{J]}{}^{L}
where
\Lambda_{J}{}^{L}
are the generators of the
SU(8)
maximal subgroup of
E_{7(7)}
with 63 parameters. The off-diagonal part is self-dual
\Sigma_{IJPQ}=\pm{1\over 4!}\epsilon_{IJPQMNKL}\Sigma^{MNKL}
and has 70 real parameters.

In
\mathcal{N}=8
supergravity in unitary gauge where there are 70 scalars, the local
SU(8)
symmetry is gauge fixed in the unitary gauge

\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}^{\dagger}\ .

(38)

In the unitary gauge there is no distinction anymore between the capital
I,J
indices of
E_{7(7)}
and original local
SU(8)
indices
i,j
.
There are 70 independent scalars in

\displaystyle{\cal V}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{ij}{}^{IJ}&v_{ijKL}\\
v^{klIJ}&u^{kl}{}_{KL}\\
\end{array}\right)|_{\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}^{\dagger}}\,\,\Rightarrow\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}P^{-1/2}&\,\,-P^{-1/2}y\\
-\bar{P}^{-1/2}\bar{y}&\bar{P}^{-1/2}\\
\end{array}\right),

where

P=1-y\bar{y}\,,\quad y_{ij,kl}=\phi_{ijmn}\left({\tanh\sqrt{{1\over 8}\bar{\phi}\phi}\over\sqrt{\bar{\phi}\phi}}\right)^{mn}{}_{kl}\ ,

(44)

Here
\phi_{ijkl}
and
\bar{\phi}^{ijkl}=\pm{1\over 24}\epsilon^{ijklmnpq}\phi_{mnpq}
transform in 35-dimensional representation of
SU(8)
. These are 70 physical scalars in the unitary gauge.

The explicit action of duality symmetry in the unitary gauge was presented in
Kallosh:2008ic
. It is a combination of
E^{-1}
in (
34
) and the field dependent compensating
U(x)
transformation preserving the gauge. The infinitesimal
E_{7(7)}
transformation takes the form

\delta y\equiv y^{\prime}-y=\Sigma+y\bar{\Lambda}-\Lambda\bar{y}-y\bar{\Sigma}y\ .

(45)

In the linear approximation
y={1\over\sqrt{8}}\phi

\delta\phi_{ijkl}=\Sigma_{ijkl}+(\phi\bar{\Lambda}-\Lambda\bar{\phi})_{ijkl}\ ,

(46)

we are left with a shift of scalars,
\delta^{shift}\phi_{ijkl}=\Sigma_{ijkl}
, which allows to remove a constant part of the scalar, as well as an
SU(8)
rotation
\delta^{SU(8)}\phi_{ijkl}=(\phi\bar{\Lambda}-\Lambda\bar{\phi})_{ijkl}
.

But in general the
E_{7(7)}
symmetry depends on 133 parameters
\Sigma,\Lambda
in the unitary gauge, which acts on scalars as in (

) and also acts on vectors and spinors, as shown in details in
Kallosh:2008ic

On Markovian semigroups of Lévy driven SDEs, symbols and pseudo–differential operators
Pani W. Fernando, E. Hausenblas, Fahim Kistosil

Abstract
. We analyse analytic properties of nonlocal transition semigroups associated with a class of stochastic differential equations (SDEs) in ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} driven by pure jump–type Lévy processes. First, we will show under which conditions the semigroup will be analytic on the Besov space B p , q m ​ ( ℝ d ) B_{p,q}^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}) with 1 ≤ p , q < ∞ 1\leq p,q<\infty and m ∈ ℝ m\in{\mathbb{R}} . Secondly, we present some applications by proving the strong Feller property and give weak error estimates for approximating schemes of the SDEs over the Besov space B ∞ , ∞ m ​ ( ℝ d ) B_{\infty,\infty}^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}) .

4.
Analyticity of the Markovian semigroup in general Besov spaces

Given a function space
\mathbb{X}
over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
we would be interested under which conditions on the coefficients
\sigma
,
b
and the symbol
\psi
,
the Markovian semigroup
({\mathcal{P}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
generates an analytic semigroup on
\mathbb{X}
.
Here, one has first to verify that
({\mathcal{P}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup. The necessary and sufficient conditions to fulfill a given semigroup is strongly continuous semigroup are given by Hille–Yosida Theorem.
Let us assume that
\mathbb{X}
is a Banach space. For an operator
A
, let
\rho(A)
represent the resolvent set, i.e.
\rho(A)=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:(\lambda I-A)
is invertible
\}
and
\sigma(A)={\mathbb{C}}\setminus\rho(A)
. Now, if
(A,D(A))
is closed, densely defined, and for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re\lambda>0
one has
\lambda\in\rho(A)
(compare
[
14
, Theorem 3.5, p. 73]
, or
[
40
, Theorem 1.5.2]
)
and

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|R(\lambda,A)\|_{L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})}\leq\frac{1}{\Re\lambda},

then
A
generates a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathbb{X}
.
Secondly, to show that this strongly continuous semigroup is analytic, one has to show either that
(compare
[
14
, Theorem 4.6, p. 101]
)

(4.2)

\displaystyle M:=\sup_{t>0}\|tA{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\|_{L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})}<\infty,

or

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|R(\vartheta+i\tau:A)\|_{L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(A)}{|\tau|},\quad\vartheta>0,\vartheta,\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Let
S(A)=\{\langle x^{\ast},Ax\rangle:x\in D(A),x\in\mathbb{X}^{\ast},\|x\|=1,\|x^{\ast}\|=1,\langle x^{\ast},x\rangle=1\}

be the numerical range of an operator
A
. If
\mathbb{X}
is a Hilbert space and
\sigma
constant,
S(A)
can be characterized by the

\mathfrak{Rg}(\psi):=\overline{\{a(x,\xi)\in{\mathbb{C}}:x,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}}
, where
a=(x,\xi):=\psi(\sigma^{T}\xi)
.
Since the range of
\psi
contains the numerical range
S(A)

of
A
, we have (see Remark
2.4
),

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|R(\lambda,A)\|_{L(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X})}\leq\frac{1}{\mbox{dist}(\lambda,S(A))}.

Hence, for
\mathbb{X}=H^{m}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\sigma(x)=\sigma_{0}
, one can show by analyzing the numerical range, which is here given by

S(a(x,D))=\left\{{\langle}x,a(x,D)x{\rangle}:x\in{\;\mbox{dom}}(a(x,D)),|x|_{H^{m}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=1,{\langle}x,x{\rangle}_{H^{m}_{2}}=1\right\},

and some purely geometric considerations, the analyticity of the semigroup
({\mathcal{P}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{X}
.
Here
{\langle}\,\,,\,\,{\rangle}
represent the inner product in
H^{m}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. In fact, choosing a complex number
\lambda=\vartheta+i\tau
with
\vartheta>0
and
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
using that the symbol
\psi
is of type
(0,\theta)
, we obtain by the following series of computations (see Theorem 3.9
[
40
, Chapter I]
),

\displaystyle\left\|R(\lambda,a(x,D))\right\|_{L(H^{m}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),H^{m}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|R(\vartheta+i\tau,a(x,D))\right\|_{L(H^{m}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),H^{m}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mbox{dist}(\lambda,\bar{S}(a(x,D)))}\leq\frac{1}{\mbox{dist}(\lambda,\rho(a(x,D)))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mbox{dist}(\lambda,\rho(a(x,D)))}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mbox{dist}(\vartheta+i\tau,\rho(a(x,D)))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\cos\theta}{|\tau|}=\frac{C}{|\tau|},

where
C=\cos\theta
. These calculation implies that
({\mathcal{P}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{X}
is an analytic semigroup in
\mathbb{X}
.

This result can be generalized to arbitrary Besov spaces, for an introduction to Besov spaces we refer to
[
52
]
.
The motivation to analyse the analyticity of the Markovian semigroup in Besov spaces comes from the aim to investigate the strong Feller property of the Markovian semigroup.
Since one has the embedding
C^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subset B_{\infty,\infty}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(

), it is nearby switching to Besov spaces.
If one abandon the Hilbert space setting, the numerical range gets more complicated and it is better to use other methods.

5.
The first application: the strong Feller property

Let
L=\{L(t):t\geq 0\}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}dX^{x}(t)&=&b(X^{x}(t-))\,dt+\sigma(X^{x}(t-))dL(t)\\

By

A Markovian semigroup is Feller, iff

Definition A.1

Let

]
as well.∎

One can easily see under which conditions
a(x,D)
is also bounded from
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
into
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
To see it, first, observe that the operator can also be represented by a kernel of the form

a(x,D)f(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}k(x,x-y)f(y)\,dy,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where the kernel is given by the inverse Fourier transform

k(x,z)={\mathcal{F}}_{\xi\to z}\left[a(x,\xi)\right](z).

Differentiation gives the following estimate

\left|k(x,z)\right|\leq C\,\left|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}p(x,\xi)\right|\,|z|^{-\alpha}.

By this estimate and the Young inequality for convolutions one can calculate bounds of the operator between Lebesgue spaces, like

|a(x,D)f|_{L^{q}}\leq\left\|a\right\|_{{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\gamma,0;1,0}}|f|_{L^{q}},

for
\gamma\geq{d+1}
. In case, we have additional regularity of the functions, or the function is a distribution, it is not that obvious.
The next Theorem gives characterize the action of a pseudo–differential operator on Besov spaces.

Theorem A.1
.

(compare
[
1
, Theorem 6.19, p. 164]
) Let
\kappa,m\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
a(x,\xi)\in S^{\kappa}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
. Then,
a(x,D):B^{\kappa+m}_{p,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to B^{m}_{p,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a linear and bounded operator.

Remark A.2
.

Tracing step by step of the proof of Theorem 6.19 in
[
1
, p. 164]
, one can see that for all

\kappa,m\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
a(x,\xi)\in S^{\kappa}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
and any
k\geq d+1

the following inequality holds

\left|a(x,D)f\right|_{B^{m}_{p,r}}\leq\left\|a\right\|_{{\mathcal{A}}^{\kappa}_{k,0;\delta,0}}\,\left|f\right|_{B^{\kappa+m}_{p,r}}.

To analyze the composition of two operators of given symbols, one has to evaluate
a so-called oscillatory integral. In particular, for any
\chi\in\textsc{css}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\chi(0,0)=1

and
a\in\textsc{css}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
we define the oscillatory integral by

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Os--}\!\!\iint e^{-iy\eta}a(y,\eta)\,dy\,d\eta:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\chi(\varepsilon y,\varepsilon\eta)\,e^{-i{\langle}y,\eta{\rangle}}a(y,\eta)\,dy\,d\eta.

To calculate the oscillatory integral, the following Theorem is essential.

Theorem A.2
.

(compare
[
1
, Theorem 3.9, p. 46]
)
Let
m\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
a\in{\mathcal{A}}_{(d+1+m)\wedge 0,d+1;1,0}^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and let
\chi\in\textsc{css}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\chi(0,0)=1
. Then the oscillatory integral

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Os--}\!\!\iint e^{-i{\langle}y,\eta{\rangle}}a(y,\eta)\,dy\,d\eta

exists and

\displaystyle\left|\mbox{\rm Os--}\!\!\iint e^{-i{\langle}y,\eta{\rangle}}a(y,\eta)\,dy\,d\eta\right|\leq C_{m,d}\left\|a\right\|_{{\mathcal{A}}^{m}_{(d+1+m)\wedge 0,d+1;1,0}}.

Corollary A.2
.

(compare
[
1
, Corollary 3.10, p. 48]
) Let
a_{j}\in S^{m}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be a bounded sequence in

{\mathcal{A}}_{d+1+m,d+1;\rho,\delta}^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that there exists some
a\in{\mathcal{A}}_{d+1+m,d+1;\rho,\delta}^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})

\lim_{j\to\infty}\partial^{\alpha}_{\eta}\partial^{\beta}_{y}a_{j}(y,\eta)=\partial_{\eta}^{\alpha}\partial_{y}^{\beta}a(y,\eta),

for any
|\alpha|\leq d+m+1
,
|\beta|\leq d+1
,
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\mbox{\rm Os--}\!\!\iint e^{-i{\langle}y,\eta{\rangle}}a_{j}(y,\eta)\,dy\,d\eta=\mbox{\rm Os--}\!\!\iint e^{-i{\langle}y,\eta{\rangle}}a(y,\eta)\,dy\,d\eta.

With the help of the oscillatory integral, one can show that the composition of two pseudo-differential operators
is
again a pseudo-differential operator.
Using formal calculations, an application of the Taylor formula leads to the
following characterization.

Theorem A.3
.

(compare
[
1
, Theorem 3.16, p. 55]
)
Let
a_{1}(x,\xi)\in S^{m_{1}}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
a_{2}(x,\xi)\in S^{m_{2}}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Then the composition
a_{1}(x,D)\,a_{2}(x,D)

is again a pseudo–differential operator, whose symbol we denote by
[a_{1}\,\circ\,a_{2}](x,\xi)
, and we have

[a_{1}\,\circ\,a_{2}](x,\xi)\in S^{m_{1}+m_{2}}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

Moreover, it can be expanded asymptotically as follows

(A.1)

\displaystyle[a_{1}\,\circ\,a_{2}](x,\xi)\sim\sum_{\alpha}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\left(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a_{1}(x,\xi)\right)\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}a_{2}(x,\xi)\right).

To be more precise, equation (
A.1
) means that

(A.2)

\displaystyle[a_{1}\,\circ\,a_{2}](x,\xi)-\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}{1\over\alpha!}\left(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a_{1}(x,\xi)\right)\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}a_{2}(x,\xi)\right)

belongs to
S^{m_{1}+m_{2}-N}_{1,0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for every positive integer
N
.

Remark A.3
.

Following the proof of Theorem 3.16
[
1
, p. 53]
,
one observes that

(A.3)

\displaystyle[a_{1}\,\circ\,a_{2}](x,\xi)-\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}{1\over\alpha!}\left(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a_{1}(x,\xi)\right)\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}a_{2}(x,\xi)\right)

(A.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N+1)\sum_{|\alpha|=N+1}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\mbox{\rm Os--}\!\!\iint e^{-i{\langle}y,\eta{\rangle}}\eta^{\alpha}r_{\alpha}(x,\xi,y,\eta)\,dy\,d\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(N+1)\sum_{|\alpha|=N+1}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\mbox{\rm Os--}\!\!\iint e^{-i{\langle}y,\eta{\rangle}}D_{y}^{\alpha}r_{\alpha}(x,\xi,y,\eta)\,dy\,d\eta

with

r_{\alpha}(x,\xi,y,\eta)=\int_{0}^{1}\left[\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi^{\prime}}p_{1}(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})\mid_{\xi^{\prime}=\xi+\theta\eta\atop x^{\prime}=x}\partial^{\alpha}_{x^{\prime}}p_{2}(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})\mid_{\xi^{\prime}=\xi\atop x^{\prime}=x+y}(1-\theta)^{N}\right]\,d\theta.

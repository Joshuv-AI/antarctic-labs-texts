Forward-Backward algorithms for weakly convex problems
Ewa Bednarczuk, Giovanni Bruccola, Gabriele Scrivanti, The Hung Tran

Abstract
We investigate the convergence properties of exact and inexact forward-backward algorithms to minimise the sum of two weakly convex functions defined on a Hilbert space, where one has a Lipschitz-continuous gradient. We show that the exact forward-backward algorithm converges strongly to a global solution, provided that the objective function satisfies a sharpness condition. For the inexact forward-backward algorithm, the same condition ensures that the distance from the iterates to the solution set approaches a positive threshold depending on the accuracy level of the proximal computations. As an application of the considered setting, we provide numerical experiments related to discrete tomography.



Keywords:

weakly convex functions, sharpness condition, forward-backward algorithm, inexact forward-backward algorithm,
\rho
-criticality, proximal subgradient, proximal operator

Given a Hilbert space

We highlight that assuming convexity of

The main scope of this paper is to analyse the convergence properties of the following inexact forward-backward splitting when applied to problem (

The main contribution of this work can be summarised in the following points.

1.

We exploit the proximal subdifferentials – which are defined globally for the class of weakly convex functions – to analyse the convergence of (

\varepsilon=0

Related Works

The FB Algorithm

The FB algorithm (or proximal gradient algorithm) belongs to the class of splitting methods
[
30
,
40
]
whose aim is to minimise the sum of a smooth function and a non-smooth one.
This kind of algorithm has been well studied and understood in Hilbert spaces for the convex case
[
30
,
23
]
.
Many variants of FB have been proposed recently
e.g.
inertial FB
[
10
,
44
,
16
]
, to accelerate the algorithm, variable metric FB
[
22
]
to improve convergence, or FB in Banach spaces,
[
29
]
.

When the convexity hypothesis is relaxed, two main drawbacks arise. Firstly, the convergence to global minimiser cannot be easily guaranteed any longer. Secondly, the lack of convexity of the non-smooth function invalidates the fact that the corresponding proximal operator is single-valued.
In particular, in
[
15
]
, a line search-based inexact proximal gradient scheme is designed for problems where function
g
is not necessarily convex, whereas
f
is convex and its proximal map can be approximated using the strategy proposed in
[
50
]
. Therefore, we highlight that in

Algorithm

1
we take the proximal step on the weakly convex function
f
, while the function
g
is Fréchet differentiable with a
L_{g}
-Lipschitz continuous gradient and also convex, by the manipulation in (
2
).

Convergence under error bound conditions

In the general non-convex case, the seminal works
[
2
,
41
]
illustrate that the convergence to a local minimum can be ensured provided that the objective function satisfies the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) inequality
[
35
,
42
,
43
]
.
The attractiveness of the KL inequality comes from the fact that, in finite-dimensional spaces, it is satisfied by, among others, subanalytic and semialgebraic functions, which are wide classes covering both convex and non-convex functions appearing in most applications.
Given its considerable impact on several fields of applied mathematics, the authors in
[
13
]
proposed a characterisation of the KL property in a non-smooth and infinite dimensional setting.

The KL inequality is a sufficient condition for non-linear error bounds conditions, in the sense of
sufficient (and necessary) conditions for error bounds are discussed in many papers, see e.g.
[
24
]
, or

[
5
]
in the finite-dimensional case.

In a recent work
[
1
]
, the authors assume the subdifferential error bound to obtain the convergence of a FB scheme for the sum of a convex and a smooth functions to a stationary point.
Meanwhile, in our setting of a general Hilbert space, we consider the sharpness condition with respect to the set of global minimisers of the sum of a weakly convex and a smooth function. Many innovative contributions of forward-backward splitting methods in non-convex settings, such as
[
2
,
54
,
36
]
, study global convergence to a critical point of
f+g
by using limiting subdifferentials. However, it has already been observed that proximal subgradient is a suitably powerful tool for analysing non-differentiable weakly convex functions
[
21
,
47
,
32
]
.

Proximal Subdifferential

Focusing on the class of weakly convex functions allows us to adopt a specific form of the general Fréchet subdifferential, namely

proximal subdifferential
, see
e.g.
in the finite-dimensional case, the monograph by Rockafellar and Wets
[
47
]
, in Hilbert spaces the work by Bernard and Thibault
[
11
]
and the book by Clarke
et al.

[
21
]
.
In particular, we make use of the fact that weakly convex functions
enjoy a so-called
globalisation property

[

,
49

, which states that the proximal subgradient inequality holds globally (see

1
and

Definition 1

(
\rho
-weak convexity
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Hilbert space. A function

(\forall(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}^{2})\quad h(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\leq\lambda h(x)+(1-\lambda)h(y)+\lambda(1-\lambda)\frac{\rho}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}.

(4)

According to

.

The

For every

Function

Proximal Operators

We take into account a possibly inexact proximal step for the weakly convex function in algorithm (
3
). The exact proximal step is considered in
[
12

to infer a complexity bound for the exact forward-backward algorithm. We use the concept of

-proximal operator introduced in
[

]
, see also
Definition

5
below. Its properties, expressed in terms of global proximal
{\varepsilon\text{-subdifferential}}
s, are investigated in
[
9
, Proposition 4, Proposition 5]
, where a comparison with other concepts of
\varepsilon
-proximal operator in the convex case is provided. The concept of an inexact proximal operator and its computational tractability lie at the core of many proximal methods referring to fully convex case, where both
f
and

are convex
[

,
45
,
50
]

Proximal operators for nonconvex functions are investigated, e.g. in

Definition 3

(Proximal Map)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Hilbert space and
h:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper
\rho
-weakly convex function on
\mathcal{X}
. For any
\alpha>0
, the set-valued mapping

\text{prox}_{\alpha h}:\mathcal{X}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{X}

defined as

\left(\forall y\in\mathcal{X}\right)\quad\text{prox}_{\alpha h}(y):=\underset{x\in\mathcal{X}}{\argmin}\left\{h(x)+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|x-y\|^{2}\right\}

(8)

is called
proximal map
of
h
at
x
with respect to parameter
\alpha
.
In general,
\text{dom}\,\text{prox}_{\alpha h}
could be empty.
When
1/\alpha>\rho
, or when
h
is bounded from below,
\text{dom}\,\text{prox}_{\alpha h}:=\{x\in\mathcal{X}\,|\,\text{prox}_{\alpha h}(x)\neq\emptyset\}=\mathcal{X}
. And, if
1/\alpha>\rho
,
\text{prox}_{\alpha h}
is single-valued.

The proximal point mapping is locally Lipschitz continuous
and its set of fixed points coincides with the critical points of the original function.
It is not always possible to rely on a closed-form expression for the proximity operator of a function (we refer to the web page
[
20
]
for a list of examples where these explicit forms are available).
In general, the computation of the proximal map is an independent optimisation problem.
It follows that the proximal map might be known up to a certain accuracy only, and in our convergence analysis, we take this fact into account.

To achieve convergence for the forward-backward algorithm

In this section, we discuss the concept of sharpness, which is one of the main ingredients of the convergence analysis in Section

Consider the problem

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{x\in\mathcal{X}}\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{minimize}}\;\;h(x)

where the objective function

Definition 7

(Sharpness,
[

,

]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Hilbert space and

be a function with a nonempty solution set,
{S:=\argmin_{x\in\mathcal{X}}h(x)}\neq\emptyset
. Function

satisfies the sharpness condition locally if there
exist
\mu>0
and
\delta>0
such that

\left(\forall x\in B(S,\delta)\right)\quad h(x)-\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}h(x)\geq\mu\operatorname{dist}(x,S).

(16)

If (

) is satisfied for every
\delta>0
, then the sharpness condition holds globally and it reads as

\left(\forall x\in\mathcal{X}\right)\qquad h(x)-\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}h(x)\geq\mu\operatorname{dist}(x,S).

(17)

The sharpness defined above appears in the literature under different names,

weak sharp solutions, error bound and was investigated on different levels of generality. For the detailed discussion see

[
24

and the references therein. The following elementary example shows that weakly convex globally sharp functions exist.

The function

from

{\delta=\left|\frac{a-b}{2}\right|}

{\overline{B}(a,\delta)}\cap{\overline{B}(b,\delta)}=\{\frac{a+b}{2}\}

,
then

{f(x)=|-\left(\frac{a-b}{2}\right)\left(\frac{a-b}{2}\right)|=|\frac{a-b}{2}|\operatorname{dist}(x,S).}

If

then
{f(x)\geq\delta|x-a|=\delta\operatorname{dist}(x,S)}
. If

, then

{f(x)\geq\delta|x-b|=\delta\operatorname{dist}(x,S)}

and

Conclusions

We investigate the convergence properties of the exact and the inexact forward-backward algorithms for the minimisation of a function that is expressed as the sum of a weakly convex function and a smooth function with Lipschitz-continuous gradient. In the inexact case, we inferred a convergence result that relies on the hypothesis that the accuracy level
\varepsilon>0
for the inexact proximal computation is kept constant throughout all the iterations.
We successfully applied the analysed method to binary tomography.
It will be interesting, in future work, to extend our results so as to take into account non-constant accuracy levels
\varepsilon_{t}
.

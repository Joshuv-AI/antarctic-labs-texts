A proof for Ando’s theorem on norm-coherent coordinates via the Coleman norm operator
Hongxiang Zhao

Abstract
Ando established an algebraic criterion for when a complex orientation for a Morava E-theory is an H ∞ H_{\infty} -map. The criterion relates such an orientation to a specific property of the formal group associated to the E-theory, namely, a norm coherence condition on its coordinate. On the other hand, Coleman constructed a norm operator for interpolating division values in local fields, which depends on a Lubin–Tate formal group law. These formal group laws are important tools in explicit local class field theory. In this article, we give a conceptual proof for Ando’s theorem using the Coleman norm operator via the bridge of formal group laws between topology and arithmetic.



1
Introduction

The purpose of this article is to supply a number-theoretic, conceptual proof for an old, topological theorem of Matthew Ando’s from his study of structured ring spectra and their multiplicative operations. We follow a suggestion by Charles Rezk and Yifei Zhu (cf.
[
Zhu20
, Remark 1.3]
).

•

We identify Ando’s algebraic criterion for
H_{\infty}
complex orientations of Morava E-theories as a coherence condition for certain division values in local fields with respect to a Coleman norm operator.

•

We then coherently construct these division values by an infinite iteration of the operator.

In a concrete way, the ingredients going into the proof hint towards a higher-algebraic theory for class fields (c.f., e.g.,
[
BSY22
]
).

Throughout,
k
denotes a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
,
R
denotes a complete local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and whose residue field
R/\mathfrak{m}
contains
k
. Given a formal group law
F\in R\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket
, we will write
X+_{F}Y:=F(X,Y)
.

Morava E-theories

are complex oriented cohomology theories in stable homotopy theory, whose coefficient ring
(E_{n})_{*}
in degree
0
classifies deformations of a
1
-dimensional formal group law
G
of height
n
over
k
to
R
. Each E-theory is represented by an
E_{\infty}
-spectrum
[
GH04
, Corollary 7.6]
and carries power operations associated to this multiplicative structure. Let
{\rm MU}
be the complex cobordism theory. It is well-known that
{\rm MU}
also admits power operations (
[
tD68
]
and
[
May77
, §IV.2]
). A complex orientation on
E_{n}
is a map
{\rm MU}\to E_{n}
of homotopy commutative ring spectra. Upon taking the connective cover
{\rm MU}\langle 0\rangle
, this is equivalent to a coordinate (i.e., local uniformizer) of the universal deformation formal group associated with
E_{n}
, which in turn determines a formal group law
[
Zhu20
, Section 2]
. In the case that
G
is a Honda formal group law over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, Ando gave a criterion for when power operations on
{\rm MU}
and
E_{n}
are compatible along the map
{\rm MU}\to E_{n}
, i.e., when this map is
H_{\infty}

[
And95
, Theorem 5]
. The criterion is formulated in terms of the formal group law
F
of the universal deformation associated to the coordinate.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
And95
, Theorem 4]
)
.

Suppose
k=\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
G
is the Honda formal group law of height
n
over
k
, so that
[p]_{G}(T)=T^{p^{n}}
, where
[p]_{G}
is the
p
-series of
G
. Then in each
\star
-isomorphism class of lifts of
G
to
\pi_{0}(E_{n})\cong W(k)\llbracket u_{1},\cdots,u_{n-1}\rrbracket
, there is a unique lift
F
such that

[p]_{F}(T)=\prod_{\lambda}(T+_{F}\lambda)

where the product runs over all roots
\lambda
of
[p]_{F}
.

Such a coordinate is said to be
norm-coherent
, in that the right-hand side has the form of a norm map. The condition says that the canonical lift of Frobenius, which is just the map of multiplication by
p
corresponding to the
p
-series in this case, coincides with the norm map. For a detailed discussion about the condition, see Section
4
and
[
Zhu20
, Section 6]
.

On the number-theoretic side, an important tool invented in Lubin and Tate’s explicit construction of the local Artin map in local class field theory is the family of Lubin–Tate formal group laws. Suppose a prime number
p
is a uniformizer of a local field
K
, i.e.,
K
is an unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Then a Lubin–Tate formal group law reduces to a Honda formal group law over the residue field. In 1979, Coleman proved an interpolation theorem on division values in local fields, with applications to
p
-adic
L
-functions and modular units
[
Col79
, Theorem A]
. For that, he constructed a norm operator
\mathscr{N}_{F}
depending on a Lubin–Tate formal group law
F
such that

\mathscr{N}_{F}(g)\circ[p]_{F}(T)=\prod_{\{\lambda\colon[p]_{F}(\lambda)=0\}}g(T+_{F}\lambda)

Rezk conjectured that the Coleman norm and Ando’s algebraic criteria were closely related. Here, we prove Theorem
1.1
via the norm operators
\mathscr{N}_{F}
.

In more detail, the original definition of a norm operator restricts to the special case when
R
is a complete discrete valuation ring with uniformizer
p
. We shall first give a description for Coleman’s norm operators and prove Ando’s theorem in this special case. Our proof will only depend on several properties of the norm operators and do not require
G
to be a Honda formal group law. In view of this, we will generalize the definition of a norm operator to complete local domains in which
p\neq 0
, and show that the generalized norm operator satisfies the desired properties. In particular,
\pi_{0}(E_{n})
is a complete local domain with
p\neq 0
. The main result of this article is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
G
is a formal group law of finite height over
k
and
R
is a complete local domain with
p\neq 0
whose residue field contains
k
. Then in each
\star
-isomorphism class of lifts of
G
to
R
, there is a unique lift whose corresponding coordinate is norm-coherent.

A more precise and explicit formulation of Theorem
1.2
will be given as Theorem
4.8
.

Remark 1.3
.

Zhu generalized the above theorem to apply to arbitrary complete local rings
R
following the original proof by Ando
[
Zhu20
, Theorem 1.2]
. It would be interesting to have an alternative approach from number-theoretic constructions in this generality.

In this section, suppose that

is an unramified extension of

of degree

of height

In (

is a lift of

to

Definition 3.1

(

-isomorphisms)

Two formal group laws

over

are said to be

such that

Theorem 3.2
.

With notations as above and in (

), in each
\star
-isomorphism class of lifts of
G
to
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, there is a unique formal group law

, necessarily a Lubin–Tate formal group law for some
\alpha\in\mathscr{F}_{\pi}
, satisfying

[p]_{F_{\alpha}}(T)=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\alpha,1}}(T+_{F_{\alpha}}\lambda)

(4)

To prove this special case of Ando’s theorem, we proceed as follows. In terms of the norm operator, we see that a Lubin–Tate formal group law satisfies (
4
) if and only if

[p]_{F_{\alpha}}(T)=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\alpha,1}}(T+_{F_{\alpha}}\lambda)=\bigl(\mathscr{N}_{F_{\alpha}}(T)\circ[p]_{F_{\alpha}}\bigr)(T)

(5)

Since
p
is invertible in

,
[p]_{F_{\alpha}}
has a composition inverse in

. We can thus cancel the term from both sides above, so that condition (
4

\mathscr{N}_{F_{\alpha}}(T)=T

(6)

Begin with any lift
F_{\alpha}
of

to
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Let
u\in T+\pi T\mathcal{O}_{K}\llbracket T\rrbracket=T+T\mathfrak{m}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. Then there is an element

. Indeed, since

and
\alpha_{u}=[p]_{F_{\alpha_{u}}}
, we have
\alpha_{u}=u\circ\alpha\circ u^{-1}
. Clearly

and
F_{\alpha_{u}}
are
\star
-isomorphic. In order to show that there is a unique
u\in T+T\mathfrak{m}\llbracket T\rrbracket
such that

satisfies (
4
), we are reduced to show that there is a unique
u\in T+T\mathfrak{m}\llbracket T\rrbracket
such that

\mathscr{N}_{F_{\alpha_{u}}}(T)=T

(7)

Note that
u
induces a bijection from
\Lambda_{\alpha,1}
to
\Lambda_{\alpha_{u},1}
. By definition,

\bigl(\mathscr{N}_{F_{\alpha_{u}}}(T)\circ[p]_{F_{\alpha_{u}}}\bigr)(T)=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\alpha_{u},1}}(T+_{F_{\alpha_{u}}}\lambda)

We rewrite this identity as follows:

\displaystyle\bigl(\mathscr{N}_{F_{\alpha_{u}}}(T)\circ u\circ[p]_{F_{\alpha}}\circ u^{-1}\bigr)(T)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\alpha,1}}\bigl(T+_{F_{\alpha_{u}}}u(\lambda)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\alpha,1}}F_{\alpha_{u}}\biggl(u\bigl(u^{-1}(T)\bigr),u(\lambda)\biggr)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\alpha,1}}u\circ F_{\alpha}\bigl(u^{-1}(T),\lambda\bigr)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\alpha,1}}u\circ\bigl(u^{-1}(T)+_{F_{\alpha}}\lambda\bigr)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\mathscr{N}_{F_{\alpha}}(u)\circ[p]_{F_{\alpha}}\bigr)\bigl(u^{-1}(T)\bigr)

By canceling

from both sides, we obtain
\bigl(\mathscr{N}_{F_{\alpha_{u}}}(T)\circ u\bigr)(T)=\mathscr{N}_{F_{\alpha}}(u)(T)
. Therefore, (
7
) is equivalent to

\mathscr{N}_{F_{\alpha}}(u)=u

Consequently, it remains to show the following.

Proposition 3.3
.

Given any
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{\pi}
, there is a unique

, such that

.

Existence: By Lemma

(b), let

. Then we have
\mathscr{N}_{F_{\alpha}}^{\infty}(T)=Th_{1}h_{2}\cdots
. It is easy to see that

, so

Uniqueness: If
\mathscr{N}_{F_{\alpha}}(u)=u
, then
\mathscr{N}_{F_{\alpha}}^{i}(u)=u
for each

\mathscr{N}_{F_{\alpha}}^{\infty}(u)=u
after taking the limit. Since

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta^{\prime}}

Two deformations of Frobenius
(F_{1},i_{1},\eta_{1})\to(F_{1}^{\prime},i_{1}^{\prime},\eta_{1}^{\prime})
and
(F_{2},i_{2},\eta_{2})\to(F_{2}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime},\eta_{2}^{\prime})
are said to be
isomorphic
if
(F_{1},i_{1},\eta_{1}),(F_{2},i_{2},\eta_{2})
are
\star
-isomorphic and
(F_{1}^{\prime},i_{1}^{\prime},\eta_{1}^{\prime}),(F_{2}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime},\eta_{2}^{\prime})
are
\star
-isomorphic.

The following theorem classifies deformations of Frobenius.

Theorem 4.4

(cf.
[
Str97
, Theorem 42]
)
.

There is a universal deformation
(F_{\rm univ},{\rm id},{\rm id})
of
G
to the Lubin–Tate ring
W(k)\llbracket u_{1},\cdots,u_{n-1}\rrbracket
, in the following sense. For each
r\geqslant 0
, there is a complete local ring
A^{r}
such that

\{\mbox{deformations }(F,i,\eta)\to(F^{\prime},i^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\mbox{ of }\Phi^{r}\mbox{ to }T\}/\mbox{isomorphisms}\cong{\rm Hom}(A^{r},T)

Moreover, we have
A^{0}=W(k)\llbracket u_{1},\cdots,u_{n-1}\rrbracket
and
A^{r}
is a bimodule over
A^{0}
with structure maps
s^{r},t^{r}\colon A^{0}\to A^{r}
, which are local homomorphisms.

The isomorphism is given as follows: for any deformation
(F,i,\eta)\to(F^{\prime},i^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})
of
\Phi^{r}
to
T
, there is a unique local homomorphism
\rho^{r}\colon A^{r}\to T
such that
\rho^{r}\circ s^{r}
and
\rho^{r}\circ t^{r}
restrict to
i
and
i^{\prime}
on the residue fields respectively and that there are unique
\star
-isomorphisms
(F,i,\eta)\to({\rho^{r}}^{*}{s^{r}}^{*}F_{\rm univ},i,\rm{id})
and
(F^{\prime},i^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\to({\rho^{r}}^{*}{t^{r}}^{*}F_{\rm univ},i^{\prime},{\rm id})
.

Remark 4.5
.

According to
[
Zhu20
, Remark 6.2]
, given any deformation
(F,i,\eta)
of
G
to
R
, there exists a unique deformation
(\tilde{F},i,\rm{id})
given by
[
LT66
, Theorem 3.1]
such that the two deformations are
\star
-isomorphic. Thus, we will assume
\eta=\rm{id}
in the following.

Suppose from now on that
(F,i,\rm{id})
is a deformation of
G
to
R
, where
R
is a complete local domain with
p\neq 0
.

Since
[p]_{F}\equiv[p]_{G}\mod{\mathfrak{m}}
and
G
has height
n<\infty
, not all coefficients of
[p]_{F}
are in
\mathfrak{m}
. By the Weierstrass preparation theorem, there is a unit
v
in
R\llbracket T\rrbracket
and a monic polynomial
\beta
of degree
p^{n}
, such that
[p]_{F}=v\cdot\beta
. Note that roots of
[p]_{F}
are the same as the roots of
\beta
. Let

\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}:=\{\mbox{roots of $[p]_{F}$ in a larger ring $S$ obtained from $R$ by adjoining roots of $\beta$}\}

(10)

Since
p\neq 0
in
R
,
0
is a simple root of
[p]_{F}
. For any
\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}
,
[p]_{F}\bigl(T-_{F}\lambda\bigr)=[p]_{F}(T)
. Therefore,
\lambda
is also a simple root of
[p]_{F}
. We conclude that the set
\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}
has exactly
p^{n}
elements.

Let
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{F}[p]
be the subgroup of
\mathcal{F}
of
p
-torsions defined over
S
. Then the quotient isogeny
f_{p}\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}[p]
is given by

f_{p}(T)=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}}\bigl(T+_{F}\lambda\bigr)

Note that
f_{p}(T)
is invariant under the action of
\mbox{Aut}(S/R)
, so
f_{p}(T)\in R\llbracket T\rrbracket
. As a consequence,
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}[p]
can be defined over
R
. Thus,
f_{p}
is a deformation of Frobenius between
(F,i,\rm{id})
and
(F/F[p],i\circ\sigma^{n},\rm{id})
in view of (
9
). By Theorem
4.4
, there exists a unique local homomorphism
\rho^{n}\colon A^{n}\to R
with a unique
\star
-isomorphism
g_{p}\colon F/F[p]\to{\rho^{n}}^{*}{t^{n}}^{*}F_{\rm univ}
.

Definition 4.6
.

Define

l_{p}:=g_{p}\circ f_{p}\colon F\to{\rho^{n}}^{*}{t^{n}}^{*}F_{\rm univ}

Remark 4.7
.

According to
[
Zhu20
, Remark 6.7]
, the isogeny
l_{p}
is an isogeny of formal group laws over
R
characterized by the following properties.

(a)

It is an isogeny from
F
to
{\rho^{n}}^{*}{t^{n}}^{*}F_{\rm univ}
.

(b)

The kernel of

is the same as that of
[p]_{F}
.

(c)

The reduction of
l_{p}
to the residue field is the
p^{n}
-power relative Frobenius

, i.e.,
l_{p}(T)\equiv T^{p^{n}}\mod{\mathfrak{m}}
.

To proceed to the general case, we formulate the norm coherence condition of Theorem
1.2
precisely as follows.

Theorem 4.8
.

Suppose
R
is a complete local domain with

and residue field containing

. In each
\star
-isomorphism class of deformations of

to

, there is a unique element

such that

(11)

i.e.,

.

Remark 4.9
.

According to the proof for

is norm-coherent with respect to any finite subgroup

.

Remark 4.10
.

Back to the topological side, an orientation on the Morava E-theory
E_{n}
corresponds to a coordinate on the formal group
{\rm Spf}\bigl(\pi_{0}\bigl(E_{n}^{\mathbb{CP}_{+}^{\infty}}\bigr)\bigr)

[

, so it induces a deformation

of

to

5
Generalization of the norm operators

In this section, we aim to prove Theorem
4.8
(and hence Theorem
1.2
) following the proof of the special case in Section
3
. Observe that our earlier proof actually only requires
\mathcal{O}_{K}
to be a complete local domain such that
[p]_{F_{\alpha}}
is right-cancellative and the definition and properties of the Coleman norm operator over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
in Section
2
. Therefore, we need only show that
l_{p}
is right-cancellative and generalize Theorem
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
to the case of a complete local domain
R
such that
p\neq 0
and residue field contains
k
substituting
[p]_{F}
by
l_{p}
. The proof then applies mutatis mutandis.

Remark 5.1
.

In the general case, we do not require
G
to be a Honda formal group law and
F
to be a Lubin-Tate formal group law as in Section
3
, since the property that
[p]_{F}\equiv T^{p^{n}}\mod{\mathfrak{m}}
has been replaced by Remark
4.7
(c).

Lemma 5.2
.

The power series
l_{p}
is right-cancellative, i.e., if there are
g,h\in R\llbracket T\rrbracket
such that
g\circ l_{p}=h\circ l_{p}
, we have
g=h
.

Proof.

We may assume
h=0
, so that
g\bigl(l_{p}(T)\bigr)=0
. Since
R
is complete with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
, we need only prove by induction on
i
that
g\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{m}^{i}}
for each
i\geqslant 0
. The statement is vacuous for
i=0
. Suppose we have proven that
g\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{m}^{i}}
. Since
l_{p}(T)\equiv T^{p^{n}}\mod{\mathfrak{m}}
by Remark
4.7
(c),
g(T^{p^{n}})\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}}
, and hence
g\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}}
.
∎

The following proofs essentially follow those in
[
Col79
]
.

Lemma 5.3

(cf.
[
Col79
, Lemma 3]
)
.

If
g\in R\llbracket T\rrbracket
and
g(T+_{F}\lambda)=g(T)
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}
, then there is a unique
h\in R\llbracket T\rrbracket
such that
h\circ l_{p}=g
.

Proof.

The uniqueness follows from Lemma
5.2
.

For the existence, we inductively construct formal power series
g_{m}
for each
m\geqslant 0
. Let
g_{0}=g
. Suppose that we have constructed
a_{i}\in R
for
0\leqslant i\leqslant m-1
such that

g-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}l_{p}^{i}=l_{p}^{m}\cdot g_{m}

for some
g_{m}\in R\llbracket T\rrbracket
. Note that by assumption
g(T+_{F}\lambda)=g(T)
and by Remark
4.7
(b)
l_{p}(T+_{F}\lambda)=l_{p}(T)
. We thus have
g_{m}(T+_{F}\lambda)=g_{m}(T)
. In particular,
\bigl(g_{m}-g_{m}(0)\bigr)(\lambda)=0
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}
. By the Euclidean algorithm for power series, there exists elements
g_{m+1}\in R\llbracket T\rrbracket
with
r_{m}\in R[T]
such that
g_{m}-g_{m}(0)=l_{p}\cdot g_{m+1}+r_{m}
and
\deg(r_{m})<p^{n}
. Then
r_{m}
vanishes on
\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}
. Since we have deduced in the paragraph after (
10
) that
\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}
has
p^{n}
elements, we have
r_{m}=0
. Let
a_{m}=g_{m}(0)
. This finishes the inductive step. Thus, we obtain

g-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}l_{p}^{i}\in\bigcap_{i=0}^{\infty}l_{p}^{i}R\llbracket T\rrbracket=0

Then
h(T):=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}T^{i}
is the desired element.
∎

Now we also endow
R\llbracket T\rrbracket
with the compact–open topology similar to that on
\mathcal{O}_{K}\llbracket T\rrbracket
as in Section
2
. Here
R
has the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic topology.

Theorem 5.4

(cf. Theorem
2.3
)
.

There is a unique operator
\mathscr{N}_{F}\colon R\llbracket T\rrbracket\to R\llbracket T\rrbracket
such that for any
g\in R\llbracket T\rrbracket
,

\mathscr{N}_{F}(g)\circ l_{p}(T)=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}}g(T+_{F}\lambda)

Moreover,
\mathscr{N}_{F}
is multiplicative and continuous.

Proof.

Note that the right-hand side of the above identity satisfies the condition of
\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}
-invariance from Lemma
5.3
. Thus, there is a unique
\mathscr{N}_{F}
satisfying the formula.

Given any
g,h\in R\llbracket T\rrbracket
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathscr{N}_{F}(gh)\circ l_{p}(T)&=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}}gh(T+_{F}\lambda)\\
&=\bigl(\mathscr{N}_{F}(g)\circ l_{p}(T)\bigr)\cdot\bigl(\mathscr{N}_{F}(h)\circ l_{p}(T)\bigr)\\
&=\bigl(\mathscr{N}_{F}(g)\cdot\mathscr{N}_{F}(h)\bigr)\circ l_{p}(T)\end{split}

By Lemma
5.2
, we then obtain
\mathscr{N}_{F}(gh)=\mathscr{N}_{F}(g)\cdot\mathscr{N}_{F}(h)
.

Suppose
\{g_{n}\}
converges to
g
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigl(\lim\mathscr{N}_{F}(g_{n})\bigr)\circ l_{p}(T)&=\lim\bigl(\mathscr{N}_{F}(g_{n})\circ l_{p}\bigr)(T)=\lim\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}}g_{n}(T+_{F}\lambda)\\
&=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}}g(T+_{F}\lambda)=\mathscr{N}_{F}(g)\circ l_{p}(T)\end{split}

Again, it follows from Lemma
5.2
that
\lim\mathscr{N}_{F}(g_{n})=\mathscr{N}_{F}(g)
.
∎

Note that in the proof in Section
3
we only took limits of
\mathscr{N}_{F_{\alpha}}
applied to elements in
1+\mathfrak{m}\llbracket T\rrbracket
and to
T
. Thus, it remains to show the following for
\mathscr{N}_{F}
.

Lemma 5.5

(cf. Lemma
2.4
)
.

Let
g\in 1+\mathfrak{m}^{i}\llbracket T\rrbracket
and
i\geqslant 1
. Then

(a)

\mathscr{N}_{F}(g)\in 1+\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}\llbracket T\rrbracket
and

(b)

\mathscr{N}_{F}^{i}(T)/\mathscr{N}_{F}^{i-1}(T)\in 1+\mathfrak{m}^{i}\llbracket T\rrbracket
.

Proof.

(a)

By definition,
\mathscr{N}_{F}(g)\circ l_{p}(T)=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}}g(T+_{F}\lambda)
. Suppose that
g(T)=1+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b_{j}T^{j}
, where
b_{j}\in\mathfrak{m}^{i}
. Since
i\geqslant 1
, terms divided by
b_{j_{1}}b_{j_{2}}
for some
j_{1},j_{2}
must lie in
\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}
. Therefore, modulo
\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathscr{N}_{F}(g)\circ l_{p}(T)&\equiv 1+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b_{j}(T+_{F}\lambda)^{j}\\
&=1+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}}b_{j}(T+_{F}\lambda)^{j}\\
&=1+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b_{j}\bigl(p^{n}T^{j}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{k}(\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1})T^{k}\bigr)\end{split}

where each
p_{k}(\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1})
is a symmetric function on
\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}
. Recall from Section
4
that
\beta
is a polynomial of degree
p^{n}
dividing
[p]_{F}
from the Weierstrass preparation theorem, and
\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}
is the set of roots of
\beta
. Thus,
\beta(T)\equiv T^{p^{n}}\mod{\mathfrak{m}}
. It follows that
p_{k}(\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1})\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{m}}
, since
p_{k}(\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1})
is a polynomial in the non-leading coefficients of
\beta
(i.e., those elementary symmetric functions). Therefore,

\mathscr{N}_{F}(g)\circ l_{p}(T)\equiv 1\mod{\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}}

Next we prove by induction on
j
that if
h\in R\llbracket T\rrbracket
and
h\circ l_{p}\in\mathfrak{m}^{j}\llbracket T\rrbracket
, then
h\in\mathfrak{m}^{j}\llbracket T\rrbracket
(here
j\geqslant 0
). Setting
h=\mathscr{N}_{F}(g)-1
completes the proof of part (a). The case is vacuous when
j=0
. Suppose
j\geqslant 1
and the statement holds for
j-1
. By the induction hypothesis,
h\in\mathfrak{m}^{j-1}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. Since
l_{p}(T)\equiv T^{p^{n}}\mod{\mathfrak{m}}
, we have

h\circ l_{p}(T)\equiv h(T^{p^{n}})\mod{\mathfrak{m}^{j}}

Since
h\circ l_{p}\in\mathfrak{m}^{j}\llbracket T\rrbracket
, we must have
h\in\mathfrak{m}^{j}\llbracket T\rrbracket
.

(b)

By part (a) and the multiplicativity of
\mathscr{N}_{F}
from Theorem
5.4
, we need only show the case when
i=1
. Since
l_{p}(T)\equiv T^{p^{n}}\mod{\mathfrak{m}}
,

\mathscr{N}_{F}(T)(T^{p^{n}})\equiv\mathscr{N}_{F}(T)\circ l_{p}(T)=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}}(T+_{F}\lambda)\mod{\mathfrak{m}}

By an argument with symmetric functions similar to that for part (a), we obtain that
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}}(T+_{F}\lambda)\equiv T^{p^{n}}\mod{\mathfrak{m}}
. Hence,
\mathscr{N}_{F}(T)\equiv T\mod{\mathfrak{m}}
. Therefore, we have that
\mathscr{N}_{F}(T)/T\equiv 1\mod{T^{-1}\mathfrak{m}\llbracket T\rrbracket}
. It remains to show that
T
divides
\mathscr{N}_{F}(T)
in
R\llbracket T\rrbracket
, or
\mathscr{N}_{F}(T)(0)=0
. Since
0\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}
,

\mathscr{N}_{F}(T)(0)=\mathscr{N}_{F}(T)\circ l_{p}(0)=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{[p]_{F},1}}\lambda=0

∎

Remark 5.6
.

Comparing (
5
) from the proof in Section
3
, we see that
f_{p}
as defined in Section
4
now factors as
\mathscr{N}_{F}(T)\circ l_{p}
. Recall that
l_{p}=g_{p}\circ f_{p}
by definition. By Lemma
5.2
,
l_{p}
is right-cancellative with respect to composition, and hence so does
f_{p}
. Therefore,
\mathscr{N}_{F}(T)
turns out to be the inverse to
g_{p}
, i.e., it is a (necessarily unique)
\star
-isomorphism
{\rho^{n}}^{*}{t^{n}}^{*}F_{\rm univ}\to F/F[p]
. This agrees with the construction of the coordinate associated with
F/F[p]
in Definition
4.1
.

In view of this, the proof in Section
3
is actually similar to the one for
[
And95
, Theorem 2.6.4]
. In
[
And95
, Theorem 2.6.4]
, Ando constructed a series of
\star
-isomorphisms to modify the coordinates such that
g_{p}(T)
can approximate
T
inductively modulo
\mathfrak{m}^{r}
. On the other hand, our proof constructed a
\star
-isomorphism
u=\mathscr{N}_{F}^{\infty}(T)
directly so that
g_{p}^{-1}(T)=\mathscr{N}_{F}(T)(T)=T
after a change of coordinate by
u
. Indeed, properties of this limit of operators (i.e., Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
5.5
) are deduced inductively on
r
modulo
\mathfrak{m}^{r}
similar to the one in Ando’s construction. However, our proof cannot proceed by constructing a series of
\star
-isomorphisms, since
\mathscr{N}_{F}^{\infty}(T)
is not the infinite composite of
\mathscr{N}_{F}(T)
.

Remark 5.7
.

Walker has also observed the relationship between the Coleman norm operator and Ando’s criterion
[
Wal08
, Chapter 5]
. In particular, he has deduced that Ando’s criterion is equivalent to (
6
) in
[
Wal08
, Lemma 5.0.5 and (5.0.10)]
. However, he did not prove Theorem
1.1
via the Coleman norm operator.

Remark 5.8
.

As mentioned in Remark
1.3
, Zhu generalized Theorem
4.8
to apply to arbitrary complete local rings. To apply our proof to arbitrary complete local rings, we need to generalize the Coleman norm operator to such cases. However, recall in the argument around (
10
), we need
R
to be a domain so that we can count the number of roots of
\beta
, and we need
p\neq 0
in
R
so that
\beta
is separable. Thus, there is a question whether the Coleman norm operator can be defined over arbitrary complete local rings.

Inhomogeneous Diophantine approximation for generic homogeneous functions Thanks: The co-author Kleinbock has been supported by NSF grant DMS-1900560
Dmitry Kleinbock, Mishel Skenderi

Abstract
. The present paper is a sequel to [Monatsh. Math. 194 (2021), 523–554] in which results of that paper are generalized so that they hold in the setting of inhomogeneous Diophantine approximation. Given any integers n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 and ℓ ≥ 1 \ell\geq 1 , any 𝝃 = ( ξ 1 , … , ξ ℓ ) ∈ ℝ ℓ {\boldsymbol{\xi}}=\left(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{\ell}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell} , and any homogeneous function f = ( f 1 , … , f ℓ ) : ℝ n → ℝ ℓ f=\left(f_{1},\dots,f_{\ell}\right):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{\ell} that satisfies a certain nonsingularity assumption, we obtain a biconditional criterion on the approximating function ψ = ( ψ 1 , … , ψ ℓ ) : ℝ ≥ 0 → ( ℝ > 0 ) ℓ \psi=\left(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{\ell}\right):\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\left(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\right)^{\ell} for a generic element f ∘ g f\circ g in the SL n ⁡ ( ℝ ) \operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R}) -orbit of f f to be (respectively, not to be) ψ \psi -approximable at 𝝃 = ( ξ 1 , … , ξ n ) {\boldsymbol{\xi}}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}) : that is, for there to exist infinitely many (respectively, only finitely many) 𝐯 ∈ ℤ n \mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n} such that | ξ j − ( f j ∘ g ) ​ ( 𝐯 ) | ≤ ψ j ​ ( ‖ 𝐯 ‖ ) \left|\xi_{j}-\left(f_{j}\circ g\right)(\mathbf{v})\right|\leq\psi_{j}(\|\mathbf{v}\|) for each j ∈ { 1 , … , ℓ } j\in\left\{1,\dots,\ell\right\} . In this setting, we also obtain a sufficient condition for uniform approximation. We also consider some examples of f f that do not satisfy our nonsingularity assumptions and prove similar results for these examples. Moreover, one can replace SL n ⁡ ( ℝ ) \operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R}) above by any closed subgroup of ASL n ⁡ ( ℝ ) \operatorname{ASL}_{n}(\mathbb{R}) that satisfies certain integrability axioms (being of Siegel and Rogers type) introduced by the authors in the aforementioned previous paper.

Introduction

Let
f
be an indefinite nondegenerate quadratic form in
n\geq 3
real variables that is not a real multiple of a quadratic form with rational coefficients. The Oppenheim–Davenport Conjecture, resolved affirmatively by Margulis
[
17
]
, states that every real number is an accumulation point of
f(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
:

(1.1)

{\text{For any }\xi\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and any }\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},\text{ there exist infinitely many }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\text{ for which }|f(\mathbf{v})-\xi|\leq\varepsilon.}

The rich history of the Oppenheim–Davenport conjecture and its seminal resolution by Margulis, among various other related topics, are extensively discussed in Margulis’s survey
[
18
]
. The influence of Margulis’s theorem and related problems continues unabated to this day. As of a few years ago, there has been a great increase of activity in proving
effective
variants of Margulis’s theorem for
generic
quadratic forms and other homogeneous polynomials: for instance, one often considers the
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-orbit (under the natural action) of a real homogeneous polynomial in
n
real variables; one then has a natural notion of measure class (and thus measure-theoretic genericity) for this orbit. Let us briefly recall some recent results that exemplify this circle of problems. Throughout this paper, we write
\mathbb{N}:=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
: that is to say, we do not consider
0\in\mathbb{Z}
to be a natural number.

For any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
and any
(p,q)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}

2.
General results

We proceed by laying the groundwork necessary to state and prove our general results. We follow the presentation given by the authors in
[
14
]
.

Let
\operatorname{ASL}_{n}(\mathbb{R}):=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{n}.
We assume that
\operatorname{ASL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and each subgroup thereof act on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the usual manner: for any
\langle h,\mathbf{z}\rangle\in\operatorname{ASL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
we have

(

Set

Let

and

This proves the first assertion.

Using
[

in which the subhomogeneous function

therein is replaced by a function of the form

where

is some sufficiently well-behaved Borel measurable function and

(
[

)
.

Let

be subhomogeneous, and let

as in Definition

. Let
\eta
and
\nu
be arbitrary norms on

. The following then hold.

(i)

Let

. Then

if and only if
m(A_{f,\psi,\nu})<\infty
.

(ii)

Let

and

Then
\displaystyle{t}B_{f,{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}},\nu,T}\subseteq B_{f,{t}^{{\delta}}{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}},\nu,{t}T}

is sufficient to ensure that the volumes of the sets

and

.

Let

and

Lemma 2.13
.

Let

and

(i)

Suppose that

is regular and nonincreasing. Suppose that there exists a norm
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for any

we have

Then the pair
(f,\psi)
is asymptotically acceptable.

(ii)

Suppose that there exists a norm
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for any

we have

is uniformly acceptable.

Proof.

Let
\eta
be an arbitrary norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(i)

Let
a=a(\psi)\in\mathbb{R}_{>1}
and

. Let

For every

We begin with the following elementary observation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\left(M,g_{M}\right)
be an oriented
\mathscr{C}^{1}
Riemannian manifold that is Hausdorff, second-countable, and without boundary. Let
\sigma
denote the Borel measure on
M
induced by the natural Riemannian volume form on
M
. Let
h:M\to\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
be a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
map, and suppose that
h^{-1}\left(\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\right)\neq\varnothing.
Let
z\in h^{-1}\left(\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\right)
, and suppose that
z
is a regular point of
h.
Then there exist
C=C_{z}\in\mathbb{R}_{>1}
, an open subset
V=V_{z}
of
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
with
\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\in V
, and an open subset
W=W_{z}
of
M
with
z\in W
such that for any Borel subset
E
of
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
with
E\subseteq V,
we have

C^{-1}\,m(E)\leq\sigma\big(W\cap h^{-1}(E)\big)\leq C\,m(E).

In particular,
m|_{V}
and the restriction to
V
of the pushforward of
\sigma|_{W}
by
h|_{W}
are equivalent Borel measures.

Proof.

For the sake of clarity, we note that
m
denotes Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
. Set
k:=\dim M.
Note that
k\geq\ell.
By the Constant Rank Theorem
[
4
, Theorem 7.1]

there exist
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and maps
\phi:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)^{k}\to M
and
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
such that:

•

the set
\phi\left((-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)^{k}\right)
is an open subset of
M
, and
\phi
is a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
diffeomorphism onto
\phi\left((-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)^{k}\right)
;

•

the set
\Phi\left(\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\right)
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
, and
\Phi
is a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
diffeomorphism onto
\Phi\left(\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\right)
;

•

\phi\left(\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\right)=z
; and

•

\Phi\circ h\circ\phi={\pi_{\ell}}_{|(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)^{k}}
, where
\pi_{\ell}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
is given by
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\mapsto(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})
.

Set
\displaystyle W=W_{z}:=\phi\left(\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{k}\right)\subseteq M.
Then
W
is an open subset of
M
for which

z\in W\subset\overline{W}=\phi\left(\left[-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]^{k}\right)\subset\phi\left((-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)^{k}\right)\subseteq M.

Note that
\Phi\left(\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\right)=\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}.
Set
\displaystyle V=V_{z}:=\Phi^{-1}\left(\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{\ell}\right)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\ell}.
Then
V
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
for which

\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\in V\subset\overline{V}=\Phi^{-1}\left(\left[-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]^{\ell}\right)\subset\Phi^{-1}\left(\left(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon\right)^{\ell}\right)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\ell}.

Let
E
be an arbitrary Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
with
E\subseteq V.
Then

W\cap h^{-1}(E)=\phi\left(\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{k}\,\cap\,\pi_{\ell}^{-1}\big(\Phi(E)\big)\right).

Since each of
\overline{V}
and
\overline{W}
is compact, each of
h
and
\pi_{\ell}
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{1}
, and each of
\phi
and
\Phi
is a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
diffeomorphism from its domain onto its image, it follows that

\sigma\big(W\cap h^{-1}(E)\big)\asymp_{{g_{M},\,z}}\,m\left(\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{k}\,\cap\,\pi_{\ell}^{-1}\big(\Phi(E)\big)\right)=\varepsilon^{k-\ell}\,m\big(\Phi(E)\big)\asymp_{z}\,m(E).

∎

Let us now use the preceding lemma to derive a global statement.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\left(M,g_{M}\right)
be an oriented
\mathscr{C}^{1}
Riemannian manifold that is compact, Hausdorff, second-countable, and without boundary. Let
\sigma
denote the Borel measure on
M
induced by the natural Riemannian volume form on
M
. Let
h:M\to\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
be a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
map; suppose that
h^{-1}\left(\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\right)\neq\varnothing
and that every element of
h^{-1}\left(\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\right)
is a regular point of
h.
Then there exists an open subset
V
of
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
with
\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\in V
such that for any Borel subset
E
of
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
with
E\subseteq V,
we have

{\sigma\big(h^{-1}(E)\big)}\asymp_{M,{g_{M}},h}\,{m(E)}.

Proof.

Set
Z:=h^{-1}\left(\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\right)\neq\varnothing.
For every
z\in Z,
let
C_{z}\in\mathbb{R}_{>1},

V_{z}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
with
\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\in V_{z},
and
W_{z}\subseteq M
with
z\in W_{z}
be as in Lemma
3.1
. Since
Z
is compact, there exist finitely many
z_{1},\dots,z_{N}\in Z
such that
\displaystyle Z\subseteq W:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}W_{z_{i}}.
Set
\displaystyle U:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}V_{z_{i}}
and
\displaystyle C:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}C_{z_{i}}.
Let
V
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
such that
\displaystyle\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\in V\subseteq U
and
h^{-1}(V)\subseteq W.
(We defer the proof of the existence of
V
until the end of this theorem’s proof.) If
E
is any Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
with
E\subseteq V,
then

C^{-1}\,m(E)\leq\min\big\{\sigma\big(W_{z_{i}}\cap h^{-1}(E)\big):1\leq i\leq N\big\}\leq\sigma\big(h^{-1}(E)\big)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma\big(W_{z_{i}}\cap h^{-1}(E)\big)\leq C\,m(E).

We now prove the existence of such a set
V
; suppose by way of contradiction that such a set did not exist. This would imply that for every open subset
U^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
with
\displaystyle\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\in U^{\prime}\subseteq U
there exists
\mathbf{y}\in(M\smallsetminus W)
for which

. Let
\|\cdot\|
denote the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}.
Then for each
r\in\mathbb{N}
there exists some
\mathbf{x}_{r}\in(M\smallsetminus W)
for which
h(\mathbf{x}_{r})\in U
and
\|h(\mathbf{x}_{r})\|<r^{-1}
. Since
M
is compact, the sequence
\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{x}_{r}\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
has a convergent subsequence whose limit we denote by
\mathbf{x}\in M.
Since
W
is an open subset of
M
, we have
\mathbf{x}\in(M\smallsetminus W)
; this implies
\mathbf{x}\notin Z
, so that
h(\mathbf{x})\neq\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}.
On the other hand, the sequence
\displaystyle\left(h(\mathbf{x}_{r})\right)_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
clearly converges to
\mathbf{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}
. The continuity of

then implies

This is a contradiction. ∎

(

(

We begin by attending to some preliminary matters.

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\neq 0}\quad\quad\text{given by}\quad\quad(t,\mathbf{u})\mapsto t\mathbf{u}

(i)

Let

We shall show that the pair

{m\left(A_{{}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}f,s\psi,\|\cdot\|}\right)<\infty\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{n-(d+1)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\psi_{j}(t)\right)\mathrm{d}t<\infty.}

\left\{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}:g_{t}\big({\boldsymbol{\xi}}-s\psi({t})\big)\preccurlyeq\mathbf{w}\preccurlyeq g_{t}\big({\boldsymbol{\xi}}+s\psi({t})\big)\right\}\subseteq V.

Theorem
3.2
then implies that for each
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq M}
we have

(3.5)

\sigma_{n}\left(\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:g_{t}\big({\boldsymbol{\xi}}-s\psi({t})\big)\preccurlyeq h(\mathbf{u})\preccurlyeq g_{t}\big({\boldsymbol{\xi}}+s\psi({t})\big)\right\}\right)\asymp_{n,\ell,f}\ t^{-d}\,\left(\left(2s\right)^{\ell}\,\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\psi_{j}\left(t\right)\right).

It then follows from (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) that

\displaystyle m\left(\{\mathbf{x}\in A_{{}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}f,s\psi,\|\cdot\|}:\|\mathbf{x}\|\geq M\}\right)

\displaystyle\asymp_{n}\ \int_{M}^{{\infty}}\,t^{n-1}\ \sigma_{n}\left(\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:g_{t}\big({\boldsymbol{\xi}}-s\psi({t})\big)\preccurlyeq h(\mathbf{u})\preccurlyeq g_{t}\big({\boldsymbol{\xi}}+s\psi({t})\big)\right\}\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\asymp_{n,\ell,f}\ \left(2s\right)^{\ell}\int_{M}^{{\infty}}\,t^{n-(d+1)}\,\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\psi_{j}\left(t\right)\right)\mathrm{d}t.

Since
\psi
is bounded, this proves (
3.3
). Lemma
2.13
(i) then implies that the pair
\left({{}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}f},\psi\right)
is asymptotically acceptable. The desired result now follows from Theorem
2.14
.

(ii)

Let us prove that the pair
\left({{}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}f},\psi\right)
is uniformly acceptable. Let
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be given. Arguing as in part (i), we infer that there exists
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>2}
such that for each
T\in\mathbb{R}
with
T>M/s_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle m\left(\left\{\mathbf{x}\in B_{{{}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}f},s_{1}\psi(T),\|\cdot\|,{s_{2}T}}:\|\mathbf{x}\|\geq M\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\asymp_{n}\int_{M}^{s_{2}T}\,t^{n-1}\ \sigma_{n}\left(\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:g_{t}\left({\boldsymbol{\xi}}-s_{1}\psi(T)\right)\preccurlyeq f(\mathbf{u})\preccurlyeq g_{t}\left({\boldsymbol{\xi}}+s_{1}\psi(T)\right)\right\}\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\asymp_{n,\ell,f}\ \int_{M}^{s_{2}T}\,t^{n-1}\,t^{-d}\,\left(\left(2s_{1}\right)^{\ell}\,\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\psi_{j}(T)\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\asymp_{\ell,s_{1}}\ \int_{M}^{s_{2}T}\,t^{n-(d+1)}\,\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\psi_{j}(T)\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\left[\left(s_{2}T\right)^{n-d}-M^{n-d}\right]\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\psi_{j}(T)\right)

\displaystyle\asymp_{n,d,s_{2},M}\ T^{n-d}\,\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\psi_{j}(T).

It follows that

0<\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{T^{n-d}\,\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\psi_{j}\left(T\right)}{m\left(B_{{{}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}f},s_{1}\psi\left(T\right),\|\cdot\|,{s_{2}T}}\right)}\leq\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{T^{n-d}\,\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\psi_{j}\left(T\right)}{m\left(B_{{{}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}f},s_{1}\psi\left(T\right),\|\cdot\|,{s_{2}T}}\right)}<\infty.

Lemma
2.13
(ii) now implies that the pair
\left({{}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}f},\psi\right)
is uniformly acceptable. It is clear from the preceding work that

\inf_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}m\left(B_{{{}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}f},\psi\left(2^{k}\right),\|\cdot\|,{2^{k}}}\right)^{1-r}<\infty\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[2^{k(n-d)}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\psi_{j}\left(2^{k}\right)\right]^{1-r}<\infty.

The desired result now follows from Theorem
2.17
.

∎

We shall now consider examples of
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
that do not satisfy the nonsingularity hypotheses of Theorem
3.3
. Since the measure estimates furnished by Theorem
3.2
are no longer available in this setting, we shall instead use some
ad hoc
measure calculations that were performed in the authors’ previous paper: see
[
14
, Corollaries 4.2 and 4.3]
. In what follows, for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we write
\log^{i}
to denote the function
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
t\mapsto\left(\log t\right)^{i}
; in particular,
\log^{0}
denotes the constant function that is equal to
1
everywhere on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}.
Our first example expands upon
[
14
, Corollary 4.2]
; in that corollary, we considered the function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(3.6)

{f(\mathbf{x}):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left|x_{i}\right|}

and essentially proved the following result.

Lemma 3.4

(
[
14
, Corollary 4.2(i)]
)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be as in (
3.6
), let
{\overline{\psi}}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be bounded and Borel measurable, and let
\eta
denote the maximum norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Then there exists
R=R\left({\overline{\psi}},n\right)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
such that for every Borel measurable function
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with
\psi\leq{\overline{\psi}}
on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and any real numbers
S
and
T
with
R\leq S\leq T,
we have

(3.7)

m\left(A_{f,\psi,\eta}\cap\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:{S}\leq\eta(\mathbf{x})\leq{T}\}\right)=2^{n}\,n\int_{S}^{T}\frac{\psi({t})}{{t}}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\frac{1}{i!}\log^{i}\left(\frac{{t}^{n}}{\psi({t})}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}t.

We remark that, strictly speaking, an application of
[
14
, Corollary 4.2(i)]
would require the function
\psi
to be nonincreasing (see
[
14
, Remark 4.4(iii)]
) and would provide a value of
R
dependent on
\psi.
That being said, an inspection of said corollary’s proof shows that only the boundedness and Borel measurability of
\psi
are required and not its monotonicity; this inspection furthermore shows that one may choose

R=R\left({\overline{\psi}},n\right):=1+\left(\sup\left\{{\overline{\psi}}(t):t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\right\}\right)^{1/n}.

We now consider a generalized version of the function in (
3.6
), given by raising that function to an arbitrary power
\omega\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be regular and nonincreasing. Let
\omega\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be arbitrary. Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be given by
f(\mathbf{x}):=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left|x_{i}\right|\right)^{\omega}.
Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.
Then the following hold.

(i)

If

\begin{cases}{\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi(t)^{1/\omega}}{t}\log^{n-2}\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}&\text{ if }\xi=0\\
{\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi(t)}{t}\log^{n-2}\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}&\text{ if }\xi>0\end{cases}

is finite
(
respectively, infinite
)
,
then
\left({{}_{\xi}}f\right)\circ g
is
\left(\psi,\nu,\mathcal{P}\right)
-approximable for Haar-almost no
(
respectively, almost every
)

g\in G
.

(ii)

If the infinite series

\begin{cases}{\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[k^{n-1}\psi\left(2^{k}\right)^{1/\omega}\right]^{1-r}}&\text{ if }\xi=0\\
{\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[k^{n-1}\psi\left(2^{k}\right)\right]^{1-r}}&\text{ if }\xi>0\end{cases}

converges, then
\left({{}_{\xi}}f\right)\circ g
is
uniformly

\left(\psi,\nu,\mathcal{P}\right)
-approximable for Haar-almost every
g\in G
.

Proof.

If
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\psi(t)>0
, then note that we can easily construct a function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
that is regular, nonincreasing, satisfies
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\varphi(t)=0
, and for which the following hold: when
\psi
is replaced by
\varphi
in Theorem
3.5
, the integral in (i) diverges and the infinite series in (ii) converges. The conclusions of (i) and (ii) will then follow for
\varphi
; we then infer that the conclusions of (i) and (ii) follow for
\psi
as well. We therefore assume without loss of generality that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\psi(t)=0.
In concert with the regularity and monotonicity of
\psi
, this implies that there exists some
\lambda=\lambda(\psi)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that for every sufficiently large
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
, we have

(3.8)

1\leq-\log\big(\psi(t)\big)\leq\lambda\log(t).

Let us first discuss the case
\xi=0
, which was the subject of
[
14
, Corollary 4.2]
. Let us assume that
\omega=1
, since the general case
\xi=0
reduces to this particular sub-case. Even though the integral and summatory conditions in that corollary and those in the
\xi=0
case of this theorem look different from one another, they are actually equivalent. Indeed, (
3.8
) implies that the integral

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi({t})}{{t}}\log^{n-2}\left(\frac{{t}^{n}}{\psi({t})}\right)\mathrm{d}{t}

in
[
14
, Corollary 4.2(ii)]
converges if and only if the integral
\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi(t)}{t}\log^{n-2}\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t
converges. Likewise, the infinite series in
[
14
, Corollary 4.2(iii)]
, converges if and only if the infinite series
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[k^{n-1}\psi\left(2^{k}\right)\right]^{1-r}
converges. The
\xi=0
case of this theorem then follows from the preceding work and
[
14
, Corollary 4.2]
.

Suppose now that
\xi>0.
Let
\eta
denote the maximum norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(i)

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be given. Define
\overline{\psi}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
by
\overline{\psi}(t):=\left(\xi+s\psi\right)^{1/\omega}
. Let
R=R(\overline{\psi},n)
be as in Lemma
3.4
. Fix
M\in\mathbb{R}
for which
M>R
and
\displaystyle s\psi(M)<\xi/2.
Then for each
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq M}
, we have
\displaystyle s\psi(t)<\xi/2.
Define
\underline{\psi}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
by

\underline{\psi}(t):=\begin{cases}{\displaystyle\overline{\psi}(t)}&\text{ if }t\in[0,M)\\
{\displaystyle\left(\xi-s\psi(t)\right)^{1/\omega}}&\text{ if }t\in[M,\infty)\end{cases}.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be given by
h(\mathbf{x}):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left|x_{i}\right|.
Note that

(3.9)

\left(A_{{{h}},\overline{\psi},\eta}\smallsetminus A_{{{h}},\underline{\psi},\eta}\right)\cap\eta^{-1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{\geq M}\right)\subseteq A_{{{}_{\xi}{f}},s\psi,\eta}\cap\eta^{-1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{\geq M}\right)

and

(3.10)

m\Bigg(\bigg(A_{{{}_{\xi}{f}},s\psi,\eta}\cap\eta^{-1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{\geq M}\right)\bigg)\smallsetminus\bigg(\left(A_{{{h}},\overline{\psi},\eta}\smallsetminus A_{{{h}},\underline{\psi},\eta}\right)\cap\eta^{-1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{\geq M}\right)\bigg)\Bigg)=0.

Now fix any real numbers
S
and
T
with
M\leq S\leq T.
Using Lemma
3.4
, it follows that

(3.11)

m\left(A_{{{h}},\overline{\psi},\eta}\cap\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:S\leq\eta(\mathbf{x})\leq T\right\}\right)=2^{n}\,n\int_{S}^{T}\frac{\big(\xi+s\psi(t)\big)^{1/\omega}}{{t}}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\frac{1}{i!}\log^{i}\left(\frac{{t}^{n}}{\big(\xi+s\psi(t)\big)^{1/\omega}}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}t

and

(3.12)

m\left(A_{{{h}},\underline{\psi},\eta}\cap\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:S\leq\eta(\mathbf{x})\leq T\right\}\right)=2^{n}\,n\int_{S}^{T}\frac{\big(\xi-s\psi(t)\big)^{1/\omega}}{{t}}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\frac{1}{i!}\log^{i}\left(\frac{{t}^{n}}{\big(\xi-s\psi(t)\big)^{1/\omega}}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}t.

It is easy to see that

(3.13)

m\left(A_{{{}_{\xi}f},s\psi,\eta}\right)=\infty\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\quad\quad\quad\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi(t)}{t}\log^{n-2}\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t=\infty.

This follows from (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), subtracting the right-hand side of (
3.12
) from that of (
3.11
), using (
3.8
) and the dominance of
\log^{n-2}
, and then performing two first-order Taylor approximations. Note that the integral criterion in (
3.13
) is independent of
s\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.
Lemma
2.13
(i) then implies that the pair
\left({{}_{\xi}f},\psi\right)
is asymptotically acceptable. The desired result now follows from the foregoing work and Theorem
2.14
.

(ii)

Let
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be given. Set
J:=1+\psi(0).
Arguing as in part (i), we infer that there exists
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>2}
such that for each
T\in\mathbb{R}
with
T>M/s_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle m\left(\left\{\mathbf{x}\in B_{{{}_{\xi}f},{s_{1}\psi(T)},\eta,{s_{2}T}}:\eta(\mathbf{x})\geq M\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\asymp_{n,J,M,s_{1},s_{2}}\,\int_{M}^{s_{2}T}\psi(T)\ t^{-1}\,\log^{n-2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\psi(T)\,\left[\log^{n-1}\left(s_{2}T\right)-\log^{n-1}(M)\right]

\displaystyle\asymp_{n,M,s_{2}}\,\psi(T)\,\log^{n-1}(T).

It follows that

0<\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{\psi(T)\,\log^{n-1}(T)}{m\left(B_{{{}_{\xi}f},s_{1}\psi(T),\eta,{s_{2}T}}\right)}\leq\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{\psi(T)\,\log^{n-1}(T)}{m\left(B_{{{}_{\xi}f},s_{1}\psi(T),\eta,{s_{2}T}}\right)}<\infty.

Lemma
2.13
(ii) then implies that the pair
\left({{}_{\xi}f},\psi\right)
is uniformly acceptable. It is clear from the foregoing work that

\inf_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}m\left(B_{{{}_{\xi}f},\psi\left(2^{k}\right),\eta,{2^{k}}}\right)^{1-r}<\infty\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[k^{n-1}\psi\left(2^{k}\right)\right]^{1-r}<\infty.

An application of Theorem
2.17
then yields the desired result.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

It is easy to see that one may modify the preceding proof to obtain a similar result when
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a function of the form
f(\mathbf{x}):=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)^{q_{1}/q_{2}},
where
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are arbitrary odd natural numbers.

Our next and final example expands upon
[
14
, Corollary 4.3]
and is of interest because of its relation to the Khintchine–Groshev
Theorem.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be regular and nonincreasing. Let
p\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{p})\in\left(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\right)^{p}
be given. Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be given by

f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=\max\left\{|x_{i}|^{z_{i}}:1\leq i\leq p\right\}.

Set
z:=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(z_{i}\right)^{-1}.
Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.
Then the following hold.

(i)

If

\begin{cases}{\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(t)^{z}{t}^{n-(p+1)}\ \mathrm{d}t}&\text{ if }\xi=0\\
{\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(t)\,{t}^{n-(p+1)}\ \mathrm{d}t}&\text{ if }\xi>0\end{cases}

is finite
(
respectively, infinite
)
,
then
\left({{}_{\xi}}f\right)\circ g
is
\left(\psi,\nu,\mathcal{P}\right)
-approximable for Haar-almost no
(
respectively, almost every
)

g\in G.

(ii)

If the infinite series

\begin{cases}{\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[2^{(n-p)k}\psi\left(2^{k}\right)^{z}\right]^{1-r}}&\text{ if }\xi=0\\
{\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[2^{(n-p)k}\,\psi\left(2^{k}\right)\right]^{1-r}}&\text{ if }\xi>0\end{cases}

converges, then
\left({{}_{\xi}}f\right)\circ g
is
uniformly

\left(\psi,\nu,\mathcal{P}\right)
-approximable for Haar-almost every
g\in G.

The proof of this theorem makes use of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.8

(
[
14
, Corollary 4.3(i)]
)
.

Let
p\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{p})\in\left(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\right)^{p}
be given. Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be given by

f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=\max\left\{|x_{i}|^{z_{i}}:1\leq i\leq p\right\}.

Set
z:=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(z_{i}\right)^{-1}.
Let
{\overline{\psi}}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be bounded and Borel measurable. Let
\eta
denote the maximum norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Then there exists some
R=R\left({\overline{\psi}},\mathbf{z}\right)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
such that for every Borel measurable function
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with
\psi\leq{\overline{\psi}}
on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and any real numbers
S
and
T
with
R\leq S\leq T,
we have

m\left(A_{f,\psi,\eta}\cap\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:{S}\leq\eta(\mathbf{x})\leq{T}\}\right)=2^{n}(n-p)\int_{S}^{T}\psi({t})^{z}\,t^{n-(p+1)}\,\mathrm{d}t.

The relationship between Lemma
3.8
and
[
14
, Corollary 4.3(i)]
is analogous to that between Lemma
3.4
and
[
14
, Corollary 4.2(i)]
, and similar remarks to those made earlier apply here. In particular, inspecting the proof of
[
14
, Corollary 4.3(i)]
shows that one may choose

R=R\left({\overline{\psi}},\mathbf{z}\right):=1+\max_{1\leq i\leq p}\left(\sup\left\{{\overline{\psi}}(t):t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\right\}\right)^{1/{z_{i}}}

in Lemma
3.8
. The
\xi=0
case of Theorem
3.7
is already known: see
[
14
, Corollary 4.3]
. The proof of the
\xi>0
case of this theorem is similar to, and simpler than, that of the
\xi>0
case of Theorem
3.5
: it is therefore omitted.

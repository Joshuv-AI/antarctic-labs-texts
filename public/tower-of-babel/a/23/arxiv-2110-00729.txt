Nonexistence of global positive solutions for p-Laplacian equations with non-linear memory
Mokhtar Kirane, Ahmad Z. Fino, Sebti Kerbal

Abstract




Abstract

The Cauchy problem in
\mathbb{R}^{d},

d\geq 1,
for a non-local in time p-Laplacian equations is considered. The nonexistence of nontrivial global weak solutions by using the test function method is obtained.

Keywords:

Cauchy problem; p-Laplacian equation; Global nonexistence

1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\,\,\displaystyle{u_{t}-\mbox{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=\mathcal{I},}&\displaystyle{t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},}\\
\displaystyle u\geq 0,&\displaystyle{t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},}\\
\displaystyle{u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\qquad\qquad}&\displaystyle{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},}\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\mathcal{I}:=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\gamma}u(s)^{q}\,ds,

p,q>1
,
d\geq 1
,
0<\gamma<1
, and
u_{0}\in L_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We are interested in the nonexistence of nontrivial global weak solutions.

The study of nonexistence of global solutions for nonlinear parabolic equations started by Fujita
[
5
]
; he studied the Cauchy
problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\,\,\displaystyle{u_{t}-\Delta u=u^{p},}&\displaystyle{p>1,t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},}\\
\\
\displaystyle{u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,\qquad\qquad}&\displaystyle{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}};}\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

and obtained that

a)
there is no nontrivial global solution of (
1.2
) whenever
p<1+2/d
.

b)
whereas (
1.2
) admits a global solution whenever
p>1+2/d
and
u_{0}(x)\leq\delta e^{-|x|^{2}}
, (
0<\delta\ll 1
),.

For the limiting case
p=1+2/d
, it is shown by Hayakawa
[
6
]
for
d=1,2
and by Kobayashi, Sirao and Tanaka
[
8
]
for
d\geq 1
that (
1.2
)
has no global solution
u(x,t)
satisfying
\|u(\cdotp,t)\|_{\infty}<\infty
for
t\geq 0
. Weissler in
[
13
]
proved that if (
1.2
)
has no global solution
u(x,t)
satisfying
\|u(\cdotp,t)\|_{q}<\infty
for
t>0
and some
q\in[1,+\infty)
whenever if
p=1+2/d
. Therefore, the value

p_{F}=1+2/d

(1.3)

is the limiting exponent of (
1.2
); it is called Fujita’s exponent.

Since Fujita’s paper, a sizeable number of extensions in many directions have been published. Recently, Cazenave, Dickstein and Weissler
[
3
]
extended the results of Fujita
to the non-local in time heat equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\,\,\displaystyle{w_{t}-\Delta w=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\vartheta}|w(s)|^{p-1}w(s)\,ds,}&\displaystyle{t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\;p>1,}\\
\\
\displaystyle{w(0,x)=w_{0}(x),\qquad\qquad}&\displaystyle{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},}\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

where
w_{0}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\vartheta\in(0,1)
. In this case, the value of the critical Fujita exponent is

p_{c}=\max\left\{\frac{1}{\vartheta},1+\frac{2(2-\vartheta)}{(d-2(1-\vartheta))_{+}}\right\}.

(1.5)

For the p-Laplacian equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{w_{t}-\mbox{div}\left(|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w\right)=w^{q},}&\displaystyle{t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},p>2,q>1,}\\
\displaystyle w\geq 0,&\displaystyle{t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},}\\
\displaystyle{w(0,x)=w_{0}(x)\geq 0,\qquad\qquad}&\displaystyle{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},}\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

Zhao
[
15
]
and Mitidieri and Pohozaev
[
11
]
obtained the critical exponent

q^{*}=p-1+p/d;

(1.7)

in fact, Zhao
[
15
]
proved that if
p-1<q<q^{*}
, then the Cauchy problem (
1.6
) has no nontrivial global solution, however if
q>q^{*}
and
w_{0}(x)
is small enough, then (
1.6
) admits a global solution. Mitidieri and Pohozaev
[
11
]
completed the study by proving the nonexistence of nontrivial global solution in the case
q\leq q^{*}
and for all
p>2d/(d+1)
. Andreucci and Tedeev
[
1
,
10
]
obtained similar results by considering doubly singular parabolic equations.

The test function method was used to prove the nonexistence of global solutions. This method was introduced by Baras and Kersner in
[
2
]
and developed by Zhang in
[
14
]
and Pohozaev and Mitidieri in
[
11
]
, it was also used by Kirane et al. in
[
9
]
.

Here, we are concerned with the non-existence of nontrivial global solutions of (
1.1
); inspired by
[
11
]
, we choose a suitable test function in the weak formulation of the problem. Our main result is

Theorem 1.1

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

0<\gamma<1
,
p>1
,
d\geq 1
,
q>\max\{1,p-1\}
. Suppose that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{q\leq\max\left\{\frac{p-1}{\gamma};q_{c}\right\},}&\displaystyle{\hbox{if}\,\,p<\frac{d}{1-\gamma},}\\
\displaystyle{q<\infty,}&\displaystyle{\hbox{if}\,\,p\geq\frac{d}{1-\gamma},}\\
\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

where

q_{c}:=p-1+\frac{(d+p)(1-\gamma)(p-1)+p-d(1-\gamma)}{d-(1-\gamma)p},

then no nontrivial global weak solutions exist for problem
(\ref{eq1})
.

Remark 1.2

Note that (
1.8
) can be seen as follows

q\leq\max\left\{\frac{p-1}{\gamma};p-1+\frac{(d+p)(1-\gamma)(p-1)+p-d(1-\gamma)}{(d-(1-\gamma)p)_{+}}\right\},

whence

\bullet
when
p=2
and
\gamma\rightarrow 1
, then
q_{c}\rightarrow p_{F}
where
p_{F}
is defined in (
1.3
).

\bullet
when
p=2
, then
q_{c}=p_{c}
where
p_{c}
is defined in (
1.5
).

\bullet
when
\gamma\rightarrow 1
, and
p>2d/(d+1)
, then
q_{c}\rightarrow q^{*}
where
q^{*}
is defined in (
1.7
).

Remark 1.3

The same result can be obtained for the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{u_{t}-\mbox{div}A(t,x,u,\nabla u)=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\gamma}u(s)^{q}\,ds,}&\displaystyle{t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},}\\
\displaystyle u\geq 0,&\displaystyle{t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},}\\
\displaystyle{u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\qquad\qquad}&\displaystyle{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},}\end{array}\right.

where
q>1,0<\gamma<1,d\geq 1,u_{0}\in L_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
A:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Carathéodory function, where one assumes the existence of
c_{1},c_{2}>0
and
p>1
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle(A(t,x,u,w),w)\geq c_{1}\,|w|^{p},\\
\\
\displaystyle|A(t,x,u,w)|\leq c_{2}\,|w|^{p-1},\end{array}\right.

(1.9)

for all
(t,x,u,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For the study of the non-existence of global solution, the following condition is needed

(A(t,x,u,w),w)\geq c_{3}\,|A(t,x,u,w)|^{\frac{p}{p-1}},\quad\mbox{for all}\,(t,x,u,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.10)

which is a general case of (
1.9
).

Some words on the structure of the paper: Some definitions and properties on the
fractional integrals and derivatives are recalled in Section
2
. Section
3
, is reserved to the proof of the main result (Theorem
1.1
).

2
Preliminaries

Definition 2.1

A function
\mathcal{A}:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
-\infty<a<b<\infty
, is said to be absolutely continuous if and only if there exists
\psi\in L^{1}(a,b)
such that

\mathcal{A}(t)=\mathcal{A}(a)+\int_{a}^{t}\psi(s)\,ds.

AC[a,b]
denotes the space of these functions. Moreover,

AC^{2}[a,b]:=\left\{\varphi:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\hbox{/}\;\varphi^{\prime}\in AC[a,b]\right\}.

Definition 2.2

Let
f\in L^{1}(c,d)
,
-\infty<c<d<\infty
. The Riemann-Liouville left-and right-sided fractional integrals are defined by

I^{\alpha}_{c|t}f(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{c}^{t}(t-s)^{-(1-\alpha)}f(s)\,ds,\quad t>c,\;\alpha\in(0,1),

(2.1)

and

I^{\alpha}_{t|d}f(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{t}^{d}(s-t)^{-(1-\alpha)}f(s)\,ds,\quad t<d,\;\alpha\in(0,1)

(2.2)

where
\Gamma
is the Euler gamma function.

Definition 2.3

Let
f\in AC[c,d]
,
-\infty<c<d<\infty
. The Riemann-Liouville left-and right-sided fractional derivatives are defined by

D^{\alpha}_{c|t}f(t):=\frac{d}{dt}I^{1-\alpha}_{c|t}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{c}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}f(s)\,ds,\quad t>c,\;\alpha\in(0,1),

(2.3)

and

D^{\alpha}_{t|d}f(t):=-\frac{d}{dt}I^{1-\alpha}_{t|d}f(t)=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{t}^{d}(s-t)^{-\alpha}f(s)\,ds,\quad t<d,\;\alpha\in(0,1).

(2.4)

Proposition 2.4

[
12
, (2.64), p. 46]

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
-\infty<c<d<\infty
. The fractional integration by parts formula

\int_{c}^{d}f(t)D^{\alpha}_{c|t}g(t)\,dt\;=\;\int_{c}^{d}g(t)D^{\alpha}_{t|d}f(t)\,dt,

(2.5)

is satisfied for every
f\in I^{\alpha}_{t|d}(L^{p}(c,d))
,
g\in I^{\alpha}_{c|t}(L^{q}(c,d))
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\leq 1+\alpha
,
p,q>1
, where

I^{\alpha}_{c|t}(L^{q}(0,T)):=\left\{f=I^{\alpha}_{c|t}h,\,\,h\in L^{q}(c,d)\right\},

and

I^{\alpha}_{t|d}(L^{p}(c,d)):=\left\{f=I^{\alpha}_{t|d}h,\,\,h\in L^{p}(c,d)\right\}.

Remark 2.5

A simple sufficient condition for functions
f
and
g
to satisfy (
2.5
) is that
f,g\in C[c,d],
such that

D^{\alpha}_{t|d}f(t),D^{\alpha}_{c|t}g(t)
exist at every point
t\in[c,d]
and are continuous.

Proposition 2.6

[
12
, Chapter 1]

For
0<\alpha<1
,
-\infty<c<d<\infty
, we have the following identities

D^{\alpha}_{c|t}I^{\alpha}_{c|t}\varphi(t)=\varphi(t),\,\hbox{a.e. $t\in(c,d)$},\quad\hbox{for all}\,\varphi\in L^{r}(c,d),1\leq r\leq\infty,

(2.6)

and

-\frac{d}{dt}\,D^{\alpha}_{t|d}\varphi=D^{1+\alpha}_{t|d}\varphi,\quad\varphi\in AC^{2}[c,d],

(2.7)

where
D:=\frac{d}{dt}
.

Given
T>0
, let us define the function
w_{1}
by

\displaystyle w_{1}(t)=\left(1-t/T\right)^{\sigma},\quad t\in[0,T],\;\sigma\gg 1

(2.8)

The following properties concerning the functions
w_{1}
will be used later on.

Lemma 2.7

[
12
, (2.45), p. 40]

Let
T>0
,
0<\alpha<1
. For all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

D_{t|T}^{\alpha}w_{1}(t)=\frac{\Gamma(\sigma+1)}{\Gamma(\sigma+1-\alpha)}T^{-\alpha}(1-t/T)^{\sigma-\alpha},

(2.9)

and

D_{t|T}^{1+\alpha}w_{1}(t)=\frac{\Gamma(\sigma+1)}{\Gamma(\sigma-\alpha)}T^{-(1+\alpha)}(1-t/T)^{\sigma-\alpha-1}.

(2.10)

Any constant will be denoted by
C
.

3
Global nonexistence

In this section, the proof of Theorem
1.1
will be presented.

We set

Q_{T}=(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad\mathcal{B}_{T,B}=(0,T)\times\Omega(B),

where
\Omega(B):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\;|x|<2B\}
, for
T,B>0
.

Definition 3.1

(Weak solution)

Let
0<\gamma<1
, and
T>0.
A function

u\in C([0,T],L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{2q}((0,T),L^{2q}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{p}((0,T),W^{1,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap W^{1,2}((0,T),L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),

is said to be a weak solution of (
1.1
) if
u\geq 0
,
u_{|_{t=0}}=u_{0}
and the following formulation

\displaystyle\Gamma(\delta)\int_{Q_{T}}I^{\delta}_{0|t}(u^{q})\varphi\,dx\,dt=\int_{Q_{T}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,dx\,dt+\int_{Q_{T}}u_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt,

(3.1)

holds for all
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{p}(0;\infty,W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
such that supp
\varphi\subset[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
is compact, where
\delta=1-\gamma
. We denote the lifespan for the weak solution by

T_{w}(u_{0}):=\sup\{T\in(0,\infty];\,\,\hbox{there exists a unique weak solution u to (\ref{eq1})}\}.

Moreover, if
T>0
can be arbitrary chosen, i.e.
T_{w}(u_{0})=\infty
, then
u
is called a global weak solution of (
1.1
).

Proof.
Let
u\geq 0
be a global weak solution of
(\ref{eq1})
, then

\Gamma(\delta)\int_{Q_{T}}I^{\delta}_{0|t}(u^{q})\varphi\,dx\,dt=\int_{Q_{T}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,dx\,dt+\int_{Q_{T}}u_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt,

for all
T>0
and all
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{p}(0;\infty,W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
such that supp
\varphi\subset[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
is compact, where
\delta=1-\gamma
. Let

\displaystyle\varphi(x,t)=D^{\delta}_{t|T}\left(\tilde{\varphi}(x,t)\right):=D^{\delta}_{t|T}\left(\varphi^{\ell}_{1}(x)\varphi_{2}(t)\right)\;

\displaystyle\mbox{with}\quad\varphi_{1}(x):=\Phi\left({|x|}/{B}\right),\;\varphi_{2}(t):=\left(1-\frac{t}{T}\right)^{\eta}_{+},

(3.2)

where
\ell,\eta\gg 1
and
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be

\Phi(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{1},&\displaystyle{\quad 0\leq s\leq 1,}\\
\displaystyle{\searrow}&\displaystyle{\quad 1\leq s\leq 2,}\\
\displaystyle{0},&\displaystyle{\quad s\geq 2.}\end{array}\right.

Then, using the integration-by-parts formula, we get

\displaystyle\Gamma(\delta)\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}I^{\delta}_{0|t}(u^{q})D^{\delta}_{t|T}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega(B)}u_{0}(x)D^{\delta}_{t|T}\tilde{\varphi}(0,x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,dx\,dt-\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u\;\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt.

From
(\ref{IP})
and Lemma
2.7
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}D^{\delta}_{0|t}I^{\delta}_{0|t}(u^{q})\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\;T^{-\delta}\int_{\Omega(B)}u_{0}(x)\varphi^{\ell}_{1}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,dx\,dt-C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u\;\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt.

Moreover, using (
2.6
),
u_{0}\geq 0
and
u\geq 0
, it follows

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt\leq C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p-1}|\nabla\varphi|\,dx\,dt+C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u|\partial_{t}\varphi|\,dx\,dt=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

(3.3)

Next, by introducing
\tilde{\varphi}^{1/q}\tilde{\varphi}^{-1/q}
in
I_{2}
and applying the following Young’s inequality

AB\leq\frac{1}{8}A^{q}+C(q)B^{q^{\prime}},\quad A\geq 0,\;B\geq 0,\;q+q^{\prime}=qq^{\prime},

we get

I_{2}\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt+C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}\left(D^{1+\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{q^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt.

(3.4)

In order to obtain a similar estimation on
I_{1}
, let
\alpha<0
be an auxiliary constant such that
\alpha>\max\{-1,1-p,1-\frac{q}{p-1}\}
and let

u_{\epsilon}(x,t)=u(x,t)+\epsilon,\quad\epsilon>0.

By taking
\varphi_{\epsilon}(x,t)=u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}(x,t)\varphi(x,t)
as a test function where
\varphi
is given in (
3
), and using the fact that
u
is a weak solution, we obtain

\Gamma(\delta)\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}I^{\delta}_{0|t}(u^{q})u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\varphi\,dx\,dt=\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\varphi)\,dx\,dt+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{t}\,u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\varphi\,dx\,dt.

Using the integration-by-parts formula, we get

\displaystyle\Gamma(\delta)\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}I^{\delta}_{0|t}(u^{q})u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\varphi)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u\partial_{t}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\varphi)\,dx\,dt-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega(B)}u_{0}(x)u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}(x,0)\varphi(x,0)\,dx.

Then, as

\nabla(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\varphi)=\alpha u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1}\varphi\nabla u+u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi\quad\mbox{and}\quad\partial_{t}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\varphi)=\alpha u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1}\varphi\partial_{t}u+u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\varphi,

it comes

\displaystyle\Gamma(\delta)\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}I^{\delta}_{0|t}(u^{q})u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\alpha\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1}\varphi\,dx\,dt+\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad-\;\alpha\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}uu_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1}\varphi\partial_{t}u\,dx\,dt-\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}uu_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt-\int_{\Omega(B)}u_{0}(x)u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}(x,0)\varphi(x,0)\,dx.\quad\qquad

(3.5)

Whereupon,

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}uu_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1}\varphi\partial_{t}u\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}(u_{\epsilon}-\epsilon)u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1}\varphi\partial_{t}u\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\varphi\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon}\,dx\,dt-\epsilon\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1}\varphi\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\partial_{t}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha+1})\varphi\,dx\,dt-\frac{\epsilon}{\alpha}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\partial_{t}(u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha})\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha+1}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt-\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int_{\Omega(B)}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha+1}(x,0)\varphi(x,0)\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{\epsilon}{\alpha}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt+\frac{\epsilon}{\alpha}\int_{\Omega(B)}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}(x,0)\varphi(x,0)\,dx.

Similarly,

\displaystyle J_{2}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}uu_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}(u_{\epsilon}-\epsilon)u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha+1}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt-\epsilon\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt,

and

\displaystyle J_{3}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega(B)}u_{0}(x)u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}(x,0)\varphi(x,0)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega(B)}(u_{\epsilon}(x,0)-\epsilon)u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}(x,0)\varphi(x,0)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega(B)}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha+1}(x,0)\varphi(x,0)\,dx-\epsilon\int_{\Omega(B)}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}(x,0)\varphi(x,0)\,dx.

By
J_{1}
,
J_{2}
, and
J_{3}
, it follows from (
3
) that

\displaystyle\Gamma(\delta)\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}I^{\delta}_{0|t}(u^{q})u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\varphi\,dx\,dt=\alpha\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1}\varphi\,dx\,dt+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\,dx\,dt-\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha+1}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int_{\Omega(B)}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha+1}(x,0)\varphi(x,0)\,dx,

then

\displaystyle\Gamma(\delta)\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}I^{\delta}_{0|t}(u^{q})u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\varphi\,dx\,dt+|\alpha|\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1}\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\,dx\,dt-\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha+1}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int_{\Omega(B)}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha+1}(x,0)\varphi(x,0)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p-1}|\nabla\varphi|u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha+1}|\partial_{t}\varphi|\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}(|\nabla u|^{p-1}u_{\epsilon}^{\frac{(\alpha-1)(p-1)}{p}}\varphi^{\frac{p-1}{p}})(|\nabla\varphi|u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-\frac{(\alpha-1)(p-1)}{p}}\varphi^{-\frac{p-1}{p}})\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha+1}|\partial_{t}\varphi|\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\alpha|}{2}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1}\varphi\,dx\,dt+C(\alpha)\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{p-1+\alpha}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\varphi^{1-p}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha+1}|\partial_{t}\varphi|\,dx\,dt,

where Young’s inequality

AB\leq\frac{|\alpha|}{2}\;A^{\frac{p}{p-1}}+C(\alpha)\;B^{p},\qquad A\geq 0,\;B\geq 0

(3.6)

has been used.
Consequently, as
u\geq 0
, we conclude that

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1}\varphi\,dx\,dt\leq C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{p-1+\alpha}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\varphi^{1-p}\,dx\,dt+C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha+1}|\partial_{t}\varphi|\,dx\,dt.

Young’s inequality and the last inequality, allow to get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p-1}|\nabla\varphi|\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-1}u_{\epsilon}^{\frac{(\alpha-1)(p-1)}{p}}\varphi^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\right)\left(u_{\epsilon}^{\frac{(1-\alpha)(p-1)}{p}}\varphi^{\frac{1-p}{p}}|\nabla\varphi|\right)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|\nabla u|^{p}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha-1}\varphi\,dx\,dt+C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{(1-\alpha)(p-1)}\varphi^{1-p}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{p-1+\alpha}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\varphi^{1-p}\,dx\,dt+C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{\alpha+1}|\partial_{t}\varphi|\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\;C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u_{\epsilon}^{(1-\alpha)(p-1)}\varphi^{1-p}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\,dx\,dt.

Applying Fatou’s and Lebesgue’s theorems, as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u^{p-1+\alpha}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\varphi^{1-p}\,dx\,dt+C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u^{\alpha+1}|\partial_{t}\varphi|\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\,C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u^{(1-\alpha)(p-1)}\varphi^{1-p}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\,dx\,dt=:K_{1}+K_{2}+K_{3}.

Now, we use Young’s inequality,
\nabla(\varphi_{1}^{\ell})=\ell\varphi_{1}^{\ell-1}\nabla\varphi_{1}
, and the conditions that
q>\max\{1,p-1\}
,
\alpha<0
to get estimations of
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
and
K_{3}
; we have

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}(u^{p-1+\alpha}\tilde{\varphi}^{(p-1+\alpha)/q})(C\;\tilde{\varphi}^{-(p-1+\alpha)/q}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\varphi^{1-p})\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\,C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell-\frac{pq}{q-p+1-\alpha}}|\nabla\varphi_{1}|^{\frac{pq}{q-p+1-\alpha}}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{p-1+\alpha}{q-p+1-\alpha}}\left(D^{\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p+1-\alpha}}\,dx\,dt,

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}(u^{\alpha+1}\tilde{\varphi}^{(\alpha+1)/q})(C\;\tilde{\varphi}^{-(\alpha+1)/q}|\partial_{t}\varphi|)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt+C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{q-1-\alpha}}\left(D^{1+\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-1-\alpha}}\,dx\,dt,

and

\displaystyle K_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}(u^{(1-\alpha)(p-1)}\tilde{\varphi}^{(1-\alpha)(p-1)/q})(C\;\tilde{\varphi}^{-(1-\alpha)(p-1)/q}\varphi^{1-p}|\nabla\varphi|^{p})\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\,C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell-\frac{pq}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}|\nabla\varphi_{1}|^{\frac{pq}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{(1-\alpha)(p-1)}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}\left(D^{\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}\,dx\,dt.

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{8}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell-\frac{pq}{q-p+1-\alpha}}|\nabla\varphi_{1}|^{\frac{pq}{q-p+1-\alpha}}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{p-1+\alpha}{q-p+1-\alpha}}\left(D^{\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p+1-\alpha}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\;C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{q-1-\alpha}}\left(D^{1+\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-1-\alpha}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\;C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell-\frac{pq}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}|\nabla\varphi_{1}|^{\frac{pq}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{(1-\alpha)(p-1)}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}\left(D^{\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}\,dx\,dt.

Using the estimates of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
into (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}\left(D^{1+\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\,C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell-\frac{pq}{q-p+1-\alpha}}|\nabla\varphi_{1}|^{\frac{pq}{q-p+1-\alpha}}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{p-1+\alpha}{q-p+1-\alpha}}\left(D^{\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p+1-\alpha}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\,C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{q-1-\alpha}}\left(D^{1+\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-1-\alpha}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\,C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell-\frac{pq}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}|\nabla\varphi_{1}|^{\frac{pq}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{(1-\alpha)(p-1)}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}\left(D^{\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}\,dx\,dt.\qquad

(3.7)

At this stage, we choose
B=T^{\theta}
,
\theta>0
. Taking
\alpha
small enough and passing to
s=T^{-1}t,

y=T^{-\theta}x
, we get from (
3
) that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T^{\theta})}|u|^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt\leq C\,T^{-\frac{(\delta+1)q}{q-1}+d\theta+1}+C\,T^{-\frac{(\delta+\theta p)q}{p-p+1}+d\theta+1}.

(3.8)

If all exponents of
T
are negative, by taking
T\longrightarrow+\infty
and using the dominated convergence theorem, we conclude that
u=0
. In order to ensure the negativity of the exponents of
T
, it is sufficient to require

q<\frac{1+d\theta}{(d\theta-\delta)_{+}}=:q_{1}(\theta)\quad\hbox{and}\quad q<\frac{(p-1)(1+d\theta)}{(d\theta-\theta p+1-\delta)_{+}}=:q_{2}(\theta).

which is equivalent to

q<\max_{\theta>0}\min\{q_{1}(\theta),q_{2}(\theta)\}.

(3.9)

To take into consideration
q_{1}(\theta)
and
q_{2}(\theta)
, we first look at
(d\theta-\delta)_{+}
and
(d\theta-\theta p+1-\delta)_{+}
and try to compare them in terms of
\theta
, i.e, to compare between
\delta/d
and
(1-\delta)/((p-d)_{+})
; this requires to study two cases:
p\geq d/\delta
and
p<d/\delta
.

a)
If
p\geq d/\delta
:
In this case we have
(1-\delta)/(p-d)\leq\delta/d
. As
q_{1}(\theta)
is non-increasing and
q_{2}(\theta)
is non-decreasing as a function of
\theta
, (
3.9
) can be read as

q<\max\left\{\max_{\theta\in[\frac{\delta}{d},+\infty[}q_{1}(\theta),\max_{\theta\in]0,\frac{1-\delta}{p-d}]}q_{2}(\theta)\right\}=+\infty.

b)
If
p<d/\delta
:
Technical calculations lead us to to distinguish
3
cases.

i)
Case
p>d
.
In this case we have
\delta/d<(1-\delta)/(p-d)
. So, (
3.9
) can be read as

q<\max\left\{\max_{\theta\in[\theta_{0},+\infty[}q_{1}(\theta),\max_{\theta\in]0,\theta_{0}]}q_{2}(\theta)\right\}=q_{1}(\theta_{0})=q_{2}(\theta_{0})=q_{c}=\max\left\{\frac{p-1}{1-\delta},q_{c}\right\},

with

\theta_{0}:=\frac{\delta p+1-2\delta}{dp+p-2d}>0,

using that
q_{1}(\theta)
is non-increasing and
q_{2}(\theta)
is non-decreasing.

ii)
Case
2d/(d+1)<p\leq d
.
In this case, (
3.9
) can be read as

q<\max\left\{\max_{\theta\in[\theta_{0},+\infty[}q_{1}(\theta),\max_{\theta\in]0,\theta_{0}]}q_{2}(\theta)\right\}.

(3.10)

As
p_{2}(\theta)
is non-decreasing when
p\geq d\delta
, and non-increasing when
p\leq d\delta
, we can see that (
3.10
) is equivalent to

q<\max\left\{\frac{p-1}{1-\delta},q_{c}\right\}.

iii)
Case of
1<p\leq 2d/(d+1)
:
In this case, (
3.9
) can be read as

q<\max\left\{\max_{\theta\in]0,+\infty[}q_{2}(\theta)\right\}=q_{2}(0)=\frac{p-1}{1-\delta}=\max\left\{\frac{p-1}{1-\delta},q_{c}\right\}.

Finally, to get similar results in the critical case

q=\max\left\{\frac{p-1}{1-\delta},q_{c}\right\},

we choose
B=R^{-\theta}T^{\theta},
where
1\ll R<T
is such that
T
and
R
do not go simultaneously to infinity. Moreover, due to the calculation made above, a positive constant
D
independent of
T
exists such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{q}\,dx\,dt\leq D,

leading to

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Delta(B)}|u|^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\;\;T\rightarrow\infty,

(3.11)

where
\Delta(B):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d};\;B<|x|<2B\}
. Repeating a similar calculation as in the subcritical case,
q<\max\{\frac{p-1}{1-\delta},q_{c}\}
, and using Hölder’s inequality instead of Young’s one in
K_{1}
and
K_{3}
, we get

I_{2}\leq\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt+C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell}\varphi_{2}^{-1/(q-1)}\left(D^{1+\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{q^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt,

\displaystyle K_{1}\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Delta(B)}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{p-1+\alpha}{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\frac{\ell(q-p+1-\alpha)-pq}{q-p+1-\alpha}}|\nabla\varphi_{1}|^{\frac{pq}{q-p+1-\alpha}}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{p-1+\alpha}{q-p+1-\alpha}}\left(D^{\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p+1-\alpha}}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{q-p+1-\alpha}{q}},

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt+C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{q-1-\alpha}}\left(D^{1+\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-1-\alpha}}\,dx\,dt,

and

\displaystyle K_{3}\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Delta(B)}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{(1-\alpha)(p-1)}{q}}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\frac{\ell(q-(1-\alpha)(p-1))-pq}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}|\nabla\varphi_{1}|^{\frac{pq}{q-p+1-\alpha}}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{(1-\alpha)(p-1)}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}\left(D^{\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{q-(1-\alpha)(p-1)}{q}}.

Whereupon

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\;C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell}\varphi_{2}^{-1/(q-1)}\left(D^{1+\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Delta(B)}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{p-1}{q}}

\displaystyle\qquad\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\frac{\ell(q-p+1)-pq}{q-p+1}}|\nabla\varphi_{1}|^{\frac{pq}{q-p+1}}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{p-1}{q-p+1}}\left(D^{\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}

\displaystyle+C\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\ell}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}\left(D^{1+\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Delta(B)}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{p-1}{q}}

\displaystyle\qquad\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}\varphi_{1}^{\frac{\ell(q-p+1)-pq}{q-p+1}}|\nabla\varphi_{1}|^{\frac{pq}{q-p+1}}\varphi_{2}^{-\frac{p-1}{q-p+1}}\left(D^{\delta}_{t|T}\varphi_{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}.

(3.12)

Taking into account that
q=\max\{\frac{p-1}{1-\delta},q_{c}\}
and
s=T^{-1}t,\;y=R^{\theta}T^{-\theta}x
, we get

\int_{\mathcal{B}_{T,B}}|u|^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt\leq C\,R^{-d\theta}+CR^{-d\theta+\frac{pq\theta}{q-p+1}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Delta(B)}u^{q}\tilde{\varphi}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\frac{p-1}{q}}

Taking the limit when
T\rightarrow\infty
, and using
(\ref{conditioninfini})
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{q}\,dx\,dt\leq C\,R^{-d\theta}.

Finally, letting
R\rightarrow\infty
, it comes that
u=0
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is complete.

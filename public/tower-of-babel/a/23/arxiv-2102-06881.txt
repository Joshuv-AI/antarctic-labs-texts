Ordered graphs of bounded twin-width
Pierre Simon, and, Szymon Toruńczyk

Abstract
We consider hereditary classes of graphs equipped with a total order. We provide multiple equivalent characterisations of those classes which have bounded twin-width. In particular, we prove that those are exactly the classes which avoid certain large grid-like structures and induced substructures. From this we derive that the model-checking problem for first-order logic is fixed-parameter tractable over a hereditary class of ordered graphs if, and – under common complexity-theoretic assumptions – only if the class has bounded twin-width. We also show that bounded twin-width is equivalent to the NIP property from model theory, as well as the smallness condition from enumerative combinatorics. We prove the existence of a gap in the growth of hereditary classes of ordered graphs. Furthermore, we prove a grid theorem which applies to all monadically NIP classes of structures (ordered or unordered), or equivalently, classes which do not transduce the class of all finite graphs.

Introduction

The recently introduced notion of twin-width
[
BKTW20a
,
BGK+
,
BGK+20
]

is a graph width parameter with remarkable properties.
It measures how well a given graph can be recursively decomposed into parts which have simple interactions with each other (see Sec.
2
for a definition).
The notion generalizes to arbitrary relational structures equipped with unary and binary relations.

Many well-studied classes have bounded twin-width: the class of planar graphs, and more generally, any class of graphs excluding a fixed minor; the class of cographs and more generally, any class of bounded cliquewidth; posets of bounded width; and classes of permutations (viewed as sets with two total orders) omitting a fixed permutation as an induced substructure.
Moreover, classes of bounded twin-width enjoy good properties of combinatorial, algorithmic, and logical nature.
For instance, classes of bounded twin-width are closed under first-order transductions, are small
(contain
n!\cdot 2^{\mathcal{O}(n)}
distinct labelled graphs on
n
vertices), and are
\chi
-bounded (the chromatic number can be bounded in terms of the clique number)
[
BGK+
]
.
Furthermore, it is shown that model-checking first-order logic is fixed-parameter tractable on classes of bounded twin-width, assuming the input graph
G
is given
together with a certificate
of having bounded twin-width (a certain sequence of operations).
More precisely, given a first-order sentence
\varphi
, a graph
G
, and a certificate that
G
has twin-width at most

G
satisfies
\varphi

for some computable function

and fixed constant

.

For each of the classes
\mathscr{C}
mentioned above there is an algorithm which, given a graph
G\in\mathscr{C}
, computes
some certificate that
G
has twin-width bounded by a constant, in polynomial time
[

]

Hence, for each of those classes

, model-checking first-order logic is fixed-parameter tractable, generalizing many previous results.

The appropriate certificate of having bounded twin-width is usually obtained from a suitable ordering of the vertices.
The adjacency matrix of the graph with respect to this order should be simple in a certain sense. The existence of such an order is essential in proving results about classes of bounded twin-width, as well as obtaining efficient algorithms.
This suggests that
ordered graphs
of bounded twin-width are the more fundamental object.

Main result

We solve a number of problems which are open for graphs of bounded twin-width,
in the case of ordered graphs of bounded twin-width. Among other things, we show that if a class

Related work

Enumerative combinatorics.

Enumerative combinatorics is involved in counting the number of labelled
or unlabelled structures in a given class of structures.
Marcus and Tardos proved
[
MT04
]
the Stanley-Wilf conjecture, that every class of permutations (viewed as sets equipped with two linear orders) avoiding a fixed permutation as a substructure has growth
2^{\mathcal{O}(n)}
, that is, contains at most
2^{\mathcal{O}(n)}
unlabelled structures with
n
elements (up to isomorphism). In particular, a hereditary class of permutations either has growth
2^{\mathcal{O}(n)}
or has factorial growth
n!
.
The Marcus-Tardos theorem is fundamental in twin-width theory,
and underlies many of the arguments used in
[
BKTW20a
,
BGK+
,
BGK+20
]
, and also in the current paper. It is also an immediate consequence of the equivalence (
6
)
\leftrightarrow
(
2
) in Theorem
1.1
,
as every permutation is a substructure of any sufficiently large regular semigrid (defined suitably for structures with two linear orders).

Our result proves the existence of a gap in the growth of hereditary classes of ordered graphs: either the class has exponential growth
2^{\mathcal{O}(n)}
or, or otherwise it has at least factorial growth
\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\rfloor!
. This generalizes previous results and answers a problem posed by Bolagh, Bollobás and Morris
[
BBM06
, Sec. 8]
.

In
[
BGK+
]
it is shown that every class of bounded twin-width is small,
that is, contains at most
n!2^{\mathcal{O}(n)}

labelled
structures with
n
elements (that is, with domain
\{1,\ldots,n
}).
It has been also conjectured that the converse holds for every hereditary class of graphs. Our equivalence (
1

\leftrightarrow
(
6

confirms this conjecture in the case of ordered graphs.
Note that a class of ordered graphs is small if and only if it contains at most
2^{\mathcal{O}(n)}

unlabelled structures of size
n
(that is, up to isomorphism)
as any ordered structure yields exactly
n!
distinct labelled structures.

Transductions and interpretations.

The study of transductions in theoretical computer science
originates from the study of word-like and tree-like structures,
such as graphs of bounded treewidth
[
ALS88
]
or graphs of bounded cliquewidth
[
Cou94
]
.

By results of
[
BKTW20a
]
, classes of bounded twin-width are closed under transductions; in particular, no class of bounded twin-width transduces (nor interprets) the class of all graphs.

The equivalence (
1
)
\leftrightarrow
(
4
), characterising hereditary classes of ordered graphs of bounded twin-width
as precisely those which do not transduce the class of all graphs,
is not unlike a result
[
CiO07
]

characterizing classes of bounded cliquewidth as precisely those which do not transduce the class of all graphs via some transduction
of counting monadic second-order logic (CMSO,
an extension of first-order logic).

Note that in our result we require the graphs to be ordered for the implication (
4
)
\rightarrow
(
1

the class
\mathscr{C}
of graphs of maximum degree
3
has unbounded twin-width
[
BGK+
]
, but does not transduce the class of all graphs as it is nowhere dense (see below).

Both the above result for cliquewidth and our results are obtained using appropriate grid theorems.

Grid theorems.

Grid theorems are dichotomy results in structural graph theory which state that
either a structure has a small width with respect to a considered width parameter, or otherwise, a grid-like obstruction can be found in the structure.
For example, this applies to the treewidth parameter and planar grids occurring as minors
[
RS86
]
. It also applies to cliquewidth and planar grids being definable in CMSO
[
CiO07
]
.
As CMSO formulas can define
the relations of being in the same row/column in a planar grid, this allows to encode any graph in a subgraph of a sufficiently large grid, implying the result mentioned earlier about classes of bounded cliquewidth.

Our main result also proves an appropriate grid theorem for classes of ordered graphs of bounded twin-width,
as made precise by the notion of regular semigrids.
For such semigrids there is a first-order formula which defines the relation
of being in the same interval
I_{1},\ldots,I_{n}
(‘same row’), and the relation of having the same distance from the beginning of an interval (‘same column’).
From this it follows that if a hereditary class has unbounded twin-width then it interprets the class of all graphs.

There are other known grid theorems. The Marcus-Tardos result itself proves a result of this kind, stating that if a square
0-1
matrix has sufficiently many
1
’s then it must contain a large grid-like pattern formed by
1
’s.
Finally,
[
BKTW20a
]
use the Marcus-Tardos result to exhibit a grid-like structure in classes of unbounded twin-width
(as a subdivision of the adjacency matrix).
However, the grids obtained this way are not sufficiently structured to allow to define the ‘same row’ and ‘same column’ relations.
The large semigrids that we exhibit provide the ultimate grid theorem for classes of unbounded twin-width.

Monadic NIP.

Model theory classifies typically infinite structures according to the combinatorial complexity of families of definable sets. This is usually done through the introduction of tameness properties. The most important such notion is that of stability. A structure is stable if no formula
\varphi(x,y)
encodes arbitrary large half-graphs (
\leqslant
-graphs as in Fig.
1
),
which roughly means that there is no definable order on large subsets of the structure.
Stability captures the tameness properties of families of algebraic sets.

A related, weaker, notion is that of NIP: a structure is NIP if every definable family of sets has finite VC-dimension. This captures the tameness properties of families of sets arising from geometric settings (for instance families of semi-algebraic sets of bounded complexity have finite VC-dimension).

The notion of monadically NIP is a much stronger requirement which says that the structure is NIP even if every subset of the domain can be used as a unary predicate.

This notion is closely related to notions which are studied in theoretical computer science and structural graph theory.

As mentioned, a class of structures
\mathscr{C}
is monadically NIP if and only if it does not transduce the class of all graphs.
Hence, Theorem
1.1
proves that a class of ordered graphs is monadically NIP if, and only if it has bounded twin-width.

Examples of monadically NIP graph classes include all nowhere dense classes.
A class of graphs
\mathscr{C}
is
nowhere dense

if for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
there is some
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that the
r
-subdivision of the
t
-clique is not a subgraph of any graph in
\mathscr{C}
.
Examples include the class of graphs with maximum degree bounded by a constant (those classes have unbounded twin-width
[
BGK+
]
),
as well as every proper minor-closed graph class (here the twin-width is bounded).
A subgraph-closed class of graphs is nowhere dense if and only if it is monadically NIP
[
AA14
]
.

Monadically NIP classes are closed under transductions, so any transduction of a nowhere dense graph class is also monadically NIP,
but not necessarily nowhere dense.

Theorem
1.2
provides a grid theorem for any monadically NIP class of structures – not necessarily ordered, binary, nor finite.

\{i\}\times[n+1]
in increasing order
(that is, the smallest element in
I_{i}
is identified with
(i,0)
, the second smallest with
(i,1)
, etc.).
In other words, the intervals
I_{0},\ldots,I_{m}
are
arranged in an
(m+1)\times(n+1)
matrix by putting
I_{0}
in the first row,

I_{1}
in the next one, and so on.
Note that the order
\leqslant
on
I_{1}\cup\cdots\cup I_{m}
(with the interval
I_{0}
omitted) agrees with the lexicographic order on
\{1,\ldots,m\}\times\{0,\ldots,n\}
.
For
p,q\in I_{1}\cup\cdots\cup I_{m}
, with
p=(i,j)

in the
i
th row and
j
th column and
q=(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
in the
i^{\prime}
th row and
j^{\prime}
th column,
and
p<q
lexicographically, let

\mathrm{dir}(p,q)\in\{\rightarrow,\downarrow,\searrow,\swarrow\}
be equal to:

\rightarrow
if
i=i^{\prime}
,
j<j^{\prime}
,
\downarrow
if
j=j^{\prime}
,
i<i^{\prime}
,

\searrow
if
i<i^{\prime}
,
j>j^{\prime}
,
\swarrow
if
i<i^{\prime}
,
j^{\prime}<j^{\prime}
.

An
m\times n
-semigrid is
regular
if:

1.

I_{0}<I_{1}<\ldots<I_{m}
or
I_{1}<\ldots<I_{m}<I_{0}
,

2.

I_{0}
forms a clique or an independent set, and

3.

for
p,q\in I_{1}\cup\cdots\cup I_{n}
with
p<q
, the adjacency between
p
and
q

depends only
\mathrm{dir}(p,q)
.

A
scheme
of a semigrid consists of: the type
R\in\{\leqslant,\geqslant,\neq,=\}
, a parameter
S\in\{<,>\}

corresponding to the choice (1) above,
a parameter
T\in\{\text{clique},\text{independent}\}
corresponding to the choice (2) above,
and a subset
X\subseteq\{\rightarrow,\downarrow,\swarrow,\searrow\}
corresponding to the choices (3) above, where a pair
p,q
with
p<q
is adjacent if and only if
\mathrm{dir}(p,q)\in X
.
Denote by

\mathrm{Schemes}=\{\leqslant,\geqslant,\neq,=\}\times\{<,>\}\times\{\text{clique},\text{independent}\}\times 2^{\{\rightarrow,\downarrow,\swarrow,\searrow\}}

the set of all possible schemes, so
|\mathrm{Schemes}|=2^{8}
.
For each
\sigma\in\mathrm{Schemes}
, let
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}
denote the class of all regular
m\times n
semigrids conforming to
\sigma
, for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Note that a regular
m\times n
-semigrid
G\in\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}

contains as induced substructures
every regular
m^{\prime}\times n^{\prime}
-semigird
G^{\prime}\in\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}
, for all
m^{\prime}\leqslant m
and
n^{\prime}\leqslant n
(by removing some rows and columns of
G
),
so if a hereditary class
\mathscr{C}
contains arbitrarily large regular
n\times n
-semigrids then
\mathscr{C}\supseteq\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}
for some
\sigma\in\mathrm{Schemes}
.

The definition of regular semigrids is generalized to arbitrary binary structures in Section
5
.

Proposition
3.4
is restated below.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a hereditary class of ordered graphs.
If
\mathscr{C}
contains arbitrarily large regular semigrids then:

1.

\mathscr{C}
interprets the class of all graphs,

2.

model-checking first-order logic on
\mathscr{C}
is AW[
\ast
]-hard,

3.

\mathscr{C}
contains at least
\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\rfloor!
non-isomorphic structures with
n
elements.

Proof.

Fix any
\sigma\in\mathrm{Schemes}
such that
\mathscr{C}
contains
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}
.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
\sigma
imposes an independent set on
I_{0}
(otherwise we replace edges by non-edges in all graphs in
\mathscr{C}
and replacing ‘
\mathrm{clique}
’ by ‘
\mathrm{independent}
’ in
\sigma
).
Also without loss of generality we may assume that
\sigma
imposes
I_{0}<I_{1}
(otherwise reverse the order in all structures in
\mathscr{C}
and replace
<
by
>
in
\sigma
).

Fix a semigrid
G\in\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}
; recall that its
elements are identified with
[m+1]\times[n+1]
.
Let
\ast=(0,0)
denote the smallest element in
G
. This is also the smallest element of
I_{0}
, by the assumption above.
Let
\mathcal{R}=\{\min I_{1},\ldots,\min I_{m}\}=\{(1,0),(2,0),\ldots,(m,0)\}
be the set of smallest elements of each of the intervals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}
(
I_{0}
is omitted). Those elements represent the rows of
M=\{1,\ldots,m\}\times\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Let
\mathcal{C}=\{(0,1),\ldots,(0,n)\}=I_{0}-\{\ast\}
; those elements represent the columns of
M
.

For a given
S\subseteq M
, denote by
G^{S}
the ordered subgraph of
G
induced by
\{\ast\}\cup\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{R}\cup S
.
The following is immediate.

Claim 4.1
.

There is a polynomial-time algorithm which inputs numbers
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, a relation
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\times\{1,\ldots,n\}
and in polynomial time outputs the ordered graph
G^{S}\in\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}
.

This will be the basis of our reduction from the model-checking problem over the class of arbitrary binary relations to that over
\mathscr{C}
.

We now show how to interpret the binary relation
S
in
G^{S}
by a first-order formula.
The following lemma is proved by a simple case analysis, depending on
\sigma\in\mathrm{Schemes}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

There are first-order formulas
\varphi_{\mathcal{C}}(x),\varphi_{\mathcal{R}}(x),\pi_{1}(x,y),\pi_{2}(x,y),\rho(x,y)

such that for every
m\times n
-semigrid
G\in\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}
and
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\times\{1,\ldots,n\}
:

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathcal{C}}(G^{S})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}\subseteq G^{S},

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathcal{R}}(G^{S})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}\subseteq G^{S},

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(G^{S})

\displaystyle=\{((i,j),(i,0))\mid(i,j)\in S\}\subseteq S\times\mathcal{R}\subseteq G^{S}\times G^{S}

\displaystyle\pi_{2}(G^{S})

\displaystyle=\{((i,j),(0,j))\mid(i,j)\in S\}\subseteq S\times\mathcal{C}\subseteq G^{S}\times G^{S}

\displaystyle\rho(G^{S})

\displaystyle=\{((i,0),(0,j))\mid(i,j)\in S\}\subseteq\mathcal{R}\times\mathcal{C}\subseteq G^{S}\times G^{S}.

In words,
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
define the graphs of the two projections from
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\times\{1,\ldots,n\}
onto

\mathcal{R}\simeq\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
\mathcal{C}\simeq\{1,\ldots,n\}
, while

\rho
defines the relation
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\times\{1,\ldots,n\}
as a relation on
\mathcal{R}\times\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

If
\sigma
specifies a type among
\leqslant,\geqslant,=
, then
\mathcal{C}
is the open interval between
\ast
and the smallest neighbor of
\ast
.
If
\sigma
specifies the type
\neq
, then
\mathcal{C}
is
the open interval between
\ast
and the smallest neighbor of the successor of
\ast
. In either case,
\mathcal{C}
can be described by a first-order formula
\varphi_{\mathcal{C}}(x)
not depending on
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
S
.

If
\sigma
specifies the type
=
or
\geqslant
, then
\mathcal{R}
is the set of neighbors of
\ast
.
If
\sigma
specifies the type
\leqslant
then
\mathcal{R}
is the neighborhood of
\ast
minus the neighborhood of the successor of
\ast
. Finally, if
\sigma
specifies the type
\neq
then
\mathcal{R}
is the complement of
\mathcal{C}\cup\{\ast\}\cup N(\ast)
(the set of neighbors of
\ast
). In all cases,
\mathcal{R}
can be described by a first-order formula
\psi_{\mathcal{R}}(x)
not depending on
m,n
and
S
.

Note that an element
p\in S
lies in the
i
th row if and only if
(i,0)<p<(i+1,0)
, which can be expressed by a first-order formula. More precisely, consider the first-order formula
\pi_{1}(x,y)
which holds if and only if
y\in\mathcal{R}
, and
y<x<y^{\prime}
where
y^{\prime}
is the least
y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
with
y>y^{\prime}
(unless no such
y^{\prime}
exists, then just take
y<x
). Then
\pi_{1}(p,q)
holds if and only if
p
and
q
are in the same row and

, defining the required relation.

Dually, we can define that an element in
S
is in a column
c\in\mathcal{C}
. Namely, let
\pi_{2}(x,y)
be the first-order formula expressing that
y\in\mathcal{C}
,
and:

(case
=
)

x
is a neighbor of
y
,

(case
\neq
)

x
is a non-neighbor of
y
,

(case
\leqslant
)

y
is a neighbor of
x
but not of the predecessor of
x
,

(case
\geqslant
)

x
is a neighbor
y
but not of the predecessor of
y
.

Finally, the formula
\rho(x,y)
expresses that
x\in\mathcal{R},y\in\mathcal{C}
and there exists
z
such that
\pi_{1}(z,x)
and
\pi_{2}(z,y)
hold.
∎

Let
\mathscr{B}
be the class of all finite, ordered bipartite graphs
H=(V_{1}\cup V_{2},E,\leqslant_{1},\leqslant_{2})
with parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, where

is totally ordered by
\leqslant_{1}
and
V_{2}
is totally ordered by
\leqslant_{2}
, and

.

Lemma 4.3
.

There is an interpretation
I_{\sigma}
such that
{I_{\sigma}(\mathscr{C})=\mathscr{B}}
for every hereditary class of ordered graphs
\mathscr{C}
containing
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}
.
More precisely, for
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\times\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
the structure
I_{\sigma}(G^{S})
is the bipartite graph with parts
\mathcal{R}=\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
\mathcal{C}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, each ordered by
\leqslant
, where
i\in\mathcal{R}
and

are adjacent if and only if
(i,j)\in S
.

Proof.

I_{\sigma}
consists of:

–

the the domain formula
\delta(x)\equiv\varphi_{\mathcal{R}}(x)\lor\varphi_{\mathcal{C}}(x)
defining the domain
\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{C}
,

–

the formula
\varphi_{E}(x,y)\equiv\rho(x,y)
defining the edge relation

,

–

the formula
\varphi_{\mathcal{R}}(x)\land\varphi_{\mathcal{R}}(y)\land x\leqslant y
defining the order
\leqslant_{1}
on
\mathcal{R}
, and

–

the formula

\varphi_{\mathcal{C}}(x)\land\varphi_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\land x\leqslant y
defining the order
\leqslant_{2}
on
\mathcal{C}
.

The statement follows from Lemma
4.2
.
∎

As the class of all graphs interprets in the class of all bipartite graphs via some first-order interpretation
T
,
the interpretation
I_{\sigma}
can be further composed with
T
obtaining an interpretation
T\circ T_{\sigma}
such that
(T\circ T_{\sigma})(\mathscr{C})
is the class of all finite graphs. This proves (
1
).

This also gives a polynomial-time reduction of the model-checking problem on the class of bipartite graphs to the model-checking problem on
\mathscr{C}
:
a sentence
\psi
holds in a bipartite graph
H=(V_{1},V_{2},E)
with
V_{1}=\{1,\ldots,m\},V_{2}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
if and only if the sentence obtained from
\psi
by replacing each atom

by
\rho(x,y)
holds in the structure
G^{S}
, where
G\in\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}

is the
m\times n
-semigrid and
S=E\cap(V_{1}\times V_{2})
.
As
G^{S}
can be computed in polynomial time given
H
(cf. Claim
4.1
),
this yields the required polynomial-time reduction. Since model-checking first-order logic on the class of all bipartite graphs is AW[
\ast
]-hard
[
FG06
]
, this yields the same lower bound for
\mathscr{C}
, proving (
2
).

We now prove (
3
).
For a
k\times l
matrix
M\in\{0,1\}^{k\times l}
with 0-1 entries
let
|M|
denote
k+l
plus the number of nonzero entries in
M
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
define

f(n)=\max\{|M|\mid k,l\in\mathbb{N},M\in\{0,1\}^{k\times l}\}.

It is easy to see that

.
Indeed: for

consider a

k\times k
permutation matrix

,
having exactly one nonzero entry in each row and each column. Then
|M|=n
, and there are exactly
k!
such matrices.

This bound can be easily improved:
instead of permutation matrices we can consider all

matrices with exactly one nonzero entry in each column,
yielding

for

.
Further, taking all

matrices with exactly

for

.
We expect those lower bounds can be improved further.
We use the first lower bound
as it yields the simplest expression among the three.

Lemma 4.4
.

\mathscr{C}
contains
at least
f(n)
non-isomorphic structures with
n
vertices, for each

.

Proof.

For all
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
and matrix
M\in\{0,1\}^{k,l}
, consider the
k\times l
-regular semigrid
G\in\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}
and denote by
F(k,l,M)
the ordered graph
G^{S}\in\mathscr{C}
as constructed above for

being the set of non-zero entries in

:

S=\{(i,j)\mid 1\leqslant i\leqslant k,1\leqslant j\leqslant l,M_{ij}=1\}.

It follows from the above that

maps distinct matrices to non-isomorphic structures in
\mathscr{C}
. Indeed: for the transduction

defined above,

uniquely determines

and

.
This proves the lemma.
∎

This completes the proof of Proposition
4.1
, and hence Proposition
3.4
.
(See Corollary

below for a generalization to arbitrary binary signatures).
∎

.

Proof.

If

has bounded twin-width then it does not interpret the class of all graphs

]
and is small

]

5
From quantifier-free grids to regular semigrids

In this section we prove
Theorem
3.3
,
which we recall below, generalized to arbitrary binary signatures
\Sigma
:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a hereditary class of ordered binary structures graphs.
If
\mathscr{C}
defines large grids using a quantifier-free formula, then
\mathscr{C}
contains arbitrarily large regular
n\times n
-semigrids.

In particular, we need to define the notion of a regular semigrid for an arbitrary binary signature
\Sigma
.

Before doing this, we introduce the relevant result from Ramsey theory.
Let an
ordered
m\times n
grid

be the relational structure with domain

[m]\times[n]
and two quasi-orders
\leqslant_{1}
and
\leqslant_{2}
, where two points
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})

are related by
\leqslant_{1}

if and only if
i\leqslant i^{\prime}
and are related by
\leqslant_{2}
if and only if
j\leqslant j^{\prime}
.
A
pair coloring
of such an ordered
m\times n
grid is a function
c\colon([m]\times[n])^{2}\to\Gamma
to a fixed finite set
\Gamma
of
colors
.
Such a coloring is
homogeneous

if the color
c(p,q)
depends only on the atomic type of the pair
(p,q)
. In other words, the color of a pair
(p,q)
depends only on whether
p=q
, and if not, on the quadrant or principle semi-axis in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
to which the vector
p-q
belongs to.
There are
9
possible types, corresponding to the pairs in
\{<,=,>\}^{2}
describing the relations between
p
and
q
in each of the two dimensions.

The following result is a special case of the so-called
product Ramsey theorem

(see e.g. Proposition 3 in
[
Bod15
]

in the special case of the full product of two copies of
(\mathbb{Q},\leqslant)
. See also the historical comment following it).

Lemma 5.2
.

Fix a finite set of colors
\Gamma
.
For every
m,n
there are
m^{\prime},n^{\prime}
such that
for every coloring
c
of the ordered
m^{\prime}\times n^{\prime}
there is an induced substructure isomorphic to the
m\times n
grid for which the coloring induced by
c
is homogeneous.

Fix a formula
\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y},z)
,
an ordered structure
\mathrm{S}
and an
m\times n
grid
(A,B,C)
defined by
\varphi
in
\mathrm{S}
.
The sets
A\subseteq\mathrm{S}^{\bar{x}}
and
B\subseteq\mathrm{S}^{\bar{y}}
are ordered lexicographically with respect to the order on
\mathrm{S}
, and some fixed enumeration of the tuples
\bar{x}
and
\bar{y}
.
Those two orders induce two quasi-orders
\leqslant_{1},\leqslant_{2}
on
C
, via the bijection
\alpha\colon A\times B\to C
defined by the formula
\varphi
. Namely,
\alpha(\bar{a},\bar{b})\leqslant_{1}\alpha(\bar{a}^{\prime},\bar{b}^{\prime})
if and only if
\bar{a}\leqslant_{\textrm{lex}}\bar{a}^{\prime}

and
\alpha(\bar{a},\bar{b})\leqslant_{2}\alpha(\bar{a}^{\prime},\bar{b}^{\prime})
if and only if
\bar{b}\leqslant_{\textrm{lex}}\bar{b}^{\prime}
.

Say that the grid
(A,B,C)
is
homogeneous
if for every
(\bar{a},\bar{b}),(\bar{a}^{\prime},\bar{b}^{\prime})\in A\times B\subseteq\mathrm{S}^{\bar{x}\bar{y}}
the atomic type of
(\bar{a},\bar{b},\bar{a}^{\prime},\bar{b}^{\prime},\alpha(\bar{a},\bar{b}),\alpha(\bar{a}^{\prime},\bar{b}^{\prime}))
in
\mathrm{S}
depends only on the atomic type of
(\alpha(\bar{a},\bar{b}),\alpha(\bar{a}^{\prime},\bar{b}^{\prime}))

in
(C,\leqslant_{1},\leqslant_{2})
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Fix a finite signature
\Sigma
containing
\leqslant
and a
\Sigma
-formula
\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y},z)
.
For every
m,n
there are
m^{\prime},n^{\prime}
such that
if
\varphi
defines an
m^{\prime}\times n^{\prime}
grid in an ordered
\Sigma
-structure
\mathrm{S}
then
\varphi
also defines an
m\times n
homogeneous grid in
\mathrm{S}
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
be the set of atomic
\Sigma
-types of tuples of length
2(|\bar{x}|+|\bar{y}|+1)
.
Apply Lemma
5.2
yielding numbers
m^{\prime},n^{\prime}
.

Suppose
\varphi
defines an
m^{\prime}\times n^{\prime}
grid
(A,B,C)
in
\mathrm{S}
, and let
\alpha\colon A\times B\to C
be the bijection defined by
\varphi
.
Consider
C
with the two orders
\leqslant_{1}
and
\leqslant_{2}
as described earlier, then
(C,\leqslant_{1},\leqslant_{2})
is isomorphic to the ordered
m^{\prime}\times n^{\prime}
grid.
Color each pair
(\alpha(\bar{a},\bar{b}),\alpha(\bar{a}^{\prime},\bar{b}^{\prime}))\in C^{2}
by the atomic type of the tuple
(\bar{a},\bar{b},\bar{a}^{\prime},\bar{b}^{\prime},\alpha(\bar{a},\bar{b}),\alpha(\bar{a}^{\prime},\bar{b}^{\prime}))
in
\mathrm{S}
.

By Lemma
5.2
,
(C,\leqslant_{1},\leqslant_{2})
contains a copy
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
of a homogeneous ordered
m\times n
-grid.
Then
\alpha^{-1}(C^{\prime})\subseteq A\times B
and
C^{\prime}
form a homogeneous
m\times n
grid defined by
\varphi
.
∎

We now generalize the notion of a regular semigrid from Section
4
to
an arbitrary relational binary signature
\Sigma

containing the order symbol
\leqslant
.

Fix
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the ordered grid
(m+1)\times(n+1)
-grid
(C,\leqslant_{1},\leqslant_{2})
with domain
C=[m+1]\times[n+1]
as described above.
Let
B=[0]\times[n+1]\subseteq C
,
and let
\pi\colon C\to B
be the projection
mapping
(i,j)\in C
to
(0,j)
.

A
regular
m\times n
-semigrid
is an ordered
\Sigma
-structure
\mathrm{S}
with domain
C
such that the order
\leqslant
of
\mathrm{S}
agrees with the natural lexicographic order on
C=[m+1]\times[n+1]
or its inverse,
and for all
a,b\in B
and
p,q\in C-B
:

1.

the atomic type of
(a,b)
in
\mathrm{S}
depends only on the atomic type of
(a,b)
in
(B,\leqslant)
;

2.

the atomic type of
(p,q)
in
\mathrm{S}
depends only on the atomic type of
(p,q)
in
(C,\leqslant_{1},\leqslant_{2})
;

3.

the atomic type of
(p,b)
in
\mathrm{S}
depends only on the atomic type of
(\pi(p),b)
in
(B,\leqslant)
, and is non-constant.

A regular semigrid is completely specified by the dimensions
m\times n
,
the information (
<
or
>
) about whether or not to reverse the lexicographic order,
as well as a tuple of functions describing the dependencies as above.
For example, the dependencies (1) and (3) above
are each specified by a function from
\{<,=,>\}
to the set
T
of atomic
\Sigma
-types of pairs, whereas the dependency (2) is specified by a function from
\{<,=,>\}^{2}
to
T
. In total, all the dependencies are described by a tuple in
T^{3+3+9}=T^{15}
(with some tuples being excluded due to the non-constant requirement, and some being unsatisfiable in an ordered structure).
Let
\mathrm{Schemes}=\{<,>\}\times T^{15}
denote the set of possible choices, called
schemes
.

As
|T|\leqslant 4^{|\Sigma|}
, we get the following.

Lemma 5.4
.

For every
n\geqslant 1
there are at most
2^{\mathcal{O}(|\Sigma|)}
distinct regular
n\times n
-semigrids, and they can be enumerated in time
2^{\mathcal{O}(|\Sigma|)}\cdot\textit{poly}(n)
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume
\Sigma=\{E,\leqslant\}
consists only of the edge symbol
E
and order symbol
\leqslant
.
Then an ordered graph is a regular semigrid
in the sense of Section
4
if and only if it is a regular semigrid as defined above.

Proof sketch.

In one direction, suppose
G
is a regular semigrid in the sense of Section
4
with intervals
I_{0},\ldots,I_{m}
of size
n+1
,
and assume
I_{0}<I_{1}<\ldots<I_{m}
(in the other case reverse the order of

).
Arrange
I_{0},\ldots,I_{m}
in a grid
C=[m+1]\times[n+1]

Approximating twin-width

In this section we assume that

of binary structures of unbounded twin-width contains arbitrarily large regular semigrids.
This will be proved in the following sections using entirely different tools, but in this section we focus only on the algorithmic application of this fact. We prove:

In other words, model-checking first-order logic is fixed-parameter tractable on ordered, binary structures, with the parameter being the formula

Proof.

Follows from Theorem

∎

Fix an ordered binary structure

Lemma 7.3

We have:

Proof.

The first inequality is by Theorem

8
Model-theoretic characterisations

In this section, we present our model-theoretic characterisations of classes of bounded twin-width,
as well as prove more general results concerning arbitrary classes of structures.
Among others, this will prove Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
1.2
.

We start with introducing the relevant notions from model theory.

8.1
Monadically NIP classes

Monadically NIP classes

A formula
\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
defines in a structure
\mathrm{S}
a bipartite graph with parts
\mathrm{S}^{\bar{x}}
and
\mathrm{S}^{\bar{y}}
,
in which two tuples
\bar{a}\in\mathrm{S}^{\bar{x}}
and
\bar{b}\in\mathrm{S}^{\bar{y}}
are adjacent if
\varphi(\bar{a},\bar{b})
holds in
\mathrm{S}
.

Definition 7.

A class of structures
\mathscr{C}
is
NIP

if for every formula
\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
there is a finite bipartite graph
H
which does not occur as an induced subgraph of
\varphi(\mathrm{S})
, for all
\mathrm{S}\in\mathscr{C}
.

For example, the class of all cliques is NIP, whereas the class
\mathscr{C}
of all finite graphs is not, since the edge formula
E(x,y)
may define arbitrary bipartite graphs in
\mathscr{C}
.
As another example, consider the class of all cliques with each edge subdivided: an additional vertex placed in the middle of each edge. This class is NIP – intuitively, one cannot define much more in such a graph than in a usual clique.
However, this changes if one can remove some of the newly inserted vertices and the edges incident to them – this way, we can obtain any graph
G
with each edge subdivided, and hence, very complex graphs can be obtained.
The same is true if instead of removing the vertices, we can color some of the vertices by adding unary predicates, and use those predicates in the formulas.

Definition 8 (Monadic NIP).

A class of structures
\mathscr{C}
is
monadically NIP
if every expansion
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
of
\mathscr{C}
by unary predicates is NIP (here
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
is such that every structure from
\mathrm{S}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
is obtained from some structure in
\mathrm{S}\in\mathscr{C}
by equipping
\mathrm{S}
with arbitrarily many unary predicates).

In particular, if
\mathscr{C}
is monadically NIP then the hereditary closure of
\mathscr{C}
is also monadically NIP, since removing vertices can be simulated by coloring them.
The following fact essentially says that it is enough to consider formulas
\varphi(x,y)
with just two variables when considering monadic NIP.

Definition 9 (Finitely monadically NIP).

A class of structures
\mathscr{C}
is
finitely monadically NIP

(
fmNIP
)
if every expansion of
\mathscr{C}
by finitely many unary predicates that are interpreted in structures from
\mathscr{C}
as
finite sets
is NIP.

The following lemma is immediate.

Lemma 8.1
.

1.

The class of all finite graphs is not NIP.

2.

A class of finite structures is monadically NIP if and only if it is fmNIP.

The following proposition is a restatement of a result of the first author
[
Sim21
]
,
see Appendix
A.5
for more details.

Proposition 8.2

(
[
Sim21
]
)
.

The following conditions are equivalent for a class of structures
\mathscr{D}
:

–

\mathscr{D}
is not NIP,

–

there is a formula
\varphi(x,y;\bar{z})

such that for every
n
there is a structure
\mathrm{M}\in\mathscr{D}
and a tuple
\bar{c}\in\mathrm{M}^{\bar{z}}
such that
\varphi(\mathrm{M};\bar{c})\subseteq\mathrm{M}^{2}
defines a binary relation of VC-dimension at least
n
;

Corollary 8.3
.

The following conditions are equivalent for
a class of structures
\mathscr{C}
:

1.

\mathscr{C}
does not transduce the class of all finite graphs;

2.

\mathscr{C}
is not monadically NIP.

Corollary
8.3
also follows from results of Baldwin and Shelah
[
BS85
]
.

Together with Fact
2
this gives:

Corollary 8.4
.

Every class of bounded twin-width is monadically NIP.

Finitely monadically NIP classes do not define large grids (cf. Def.
4
):

Lemma 8.5
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is fmNIP then
\mathscr{C}
does not define large grids.

Proof.

Observe that if
\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y},z)
defines a grid
(A,B,C)
in a structure
\mathrm{S}
then for every binary relation
R\subseteq A\times B
there is a unary predicate
U\subseteq C
such that the formula

\psi(\bar{x},\bar{y}):=\exists z.\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y},c)\land U(z)

defines

and
\bar{b}\in B

(\bar{a},\bar{b})\in R\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\mathrm{S}\models\psi(\bar{a},\bar{b}).

In particular,
if
|A|=n
and
|B|=2^{n}
then for a suitably chosen

,
the formula
\psi
defines a relation of VC-dimension
n
between
A
and
B
.

Consider the expansion of
\mathscr{C}
by a unary predicate

interpreted as finite sets.
Then the formula

above has unbounded VC-dimension on

∎

8.2

We now introduce a wholly model-theoretic notion characterizing bounded twin-width.
For this, we first recall some basic notions from model theory.

By a

we mean a structure which is typically infinite, as opposed to the structures considered earlier, which were typically finite.
We give a brief account of basic notions from model theory in Appendix

The
elementary closure
of a class of structures

\mathscr{C}
is the class of all models
\mathrm{M}
that satisfy every sentence
\varphi
that holds in all structures
\mathrm{S}\in\mathscr{C}
.
In particular, if
\mathscr{C}
does not define large grids,
then neither does its elementary closure. This is because for any fixed

the existence of an

-grid defined by a fixed formula
\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y},z)
can be expressed by a first-order sentence
\varphi^{\prime}

which existentially quantifies
(|\bar{x}|+|\bar{y}|)\cdot n+n^{2}

of
\bar{x}
-tuples,
\bar{y}
-tuples and single vertices,
and then checks that
\varphi
defines a bijection between
A\times B
and
C
.

By the compactness theorem (cf. Thm.
A.1
), if
\mathscr{C}
defines large grids, then its elementary closure contains a structure that defines a grid
(A,B,C)

with

and
B
of arbitrarily large infinite cardinalities.

Definition 10 (Elementary extension).

Let

Lemma 9.1
.

Fix a formula

Suppose

\tp^{\theta}(\bar{b}/A)

\tp^{\theta}(\bar{b}/\mathrm{M})

there is a function

\tp^{\theta}(\bar{b}/A)=f(\tp^{\theta}(\bar{b}/\mathrm{M})).

Proof.

We show that if
\tp^{\theta}(\bar{b}/A)\neq\tp^{\theta}(\bar{b}^{\prime}/A)

In particular,

, proving the claim.

Claim 9.2

is

-dimensional and let

Appendix A
Model theoretic preliminaries

A.1
Basic notions from model theory

Models and theories.

In model theory, structures are called
models
,
and we will therefore denote them
\mathrm{M},\mathrm{N}
, etc.
They will typically be infinite.

A (first-order)
theory
is a set
T
of sentences over a fixed signature. A
model of a theory

T
is a model
\mathrm{M}
(finite or not) which satisfies all the sentences in
T
, which is denoted
\mathrm{M}\models T
. We say that
T

has a model
if there is some model
\mathrm{M}
of
T
.

The theory
of a class of structures
\mathscr{C}

is the set
T
of all sentences
\varphi
such that
\mathrm{S}\models\varphi
for all
\mathrm{S}\in\mathscr{C}
. Trivially, every structure in
\mathscr{C}
is a model of
T
, but typically,
T
has also other models.
Those can be constructed using the compactness theorem:

Theorem A.1

(Compactness of first-order logic)
.

Let
T
be a theory such that every finite subset
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
has a model. Then
T
has a model.

For example, let
\mathscr{C}
be a class of structures over a signature
\Sigma
, and assume that

\mathscr{C}
contains structures of arbitrarily large finite size.
Then the models of the theory of
\mathscr{C}
also include infinite models of arbitrarily large cardinality.
To see this, consider the theory
T
of
\mathscr{C}

and let
\Sigma^{\prime}
extend the signature of
\mathscr{C}

by an arbitrary set
C
of constant symbols. For
c,d\in C
,
let
\varphi_{cd}
be the
\Sigma^{\prime}
-sentence
c\neq d
.
Then
T\cup\{\varphi_{cd}\mid c,d\in C,c\neq d\}
satisfies the assumption of the compactness theorem, so it has a model
\mathrm{M}
, and this model has at least the cardinality of
C
.

Elementary extensions.

Let
\mathrm{M},\mathrm{N}
be two models such that the domain of
\mathrm{M}
is contained in the domain of
\mathrm{N}
.
Then
\mathrm{N}
is an
elementary extension
of
\mathrm{M}
,
written
\mathrm{M}\prec\mathrm{N}
,
if for every
formula
\varphi(\bar{x})
and tuple
\bar{a}\in\mathrm{M}^{\bar{x}}
of elements of
\mathrm{M}
,

\mathrm{M}\models\varphi(\bar{a})\text{\quad if and only if \quad}\mathrm{N}\models\varphi(\bar{a}).

In other words, it doesn’t matter if we evaluate formulas in
\mathrm{M}
or in
\mathrm{N}
.

A typical way of constructing an elementary extension of
\mathrm{M}

is by considering the following theory, called the
elementary diagram
of
\mathrm{M}
. Let
\Sigma
be the signature of
\mathrm{M}
,
and let
\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma\cup\mathrm{M}
, where the elements of
\mathrm{M}
are viewed as constant symbols.

For a
\Sigma
-formula
\varphi(\bar{y})
and tuple
\bar{a}\in\mathrm{M}^{\bar{y}}
write
\varphi(\bar{a})
for the
\Sigma^{\prime}
-sentence
obtained by replacing the variables in
\bar{y}
by constants in
\mathrm{M}
, according to
\bar{a}
.
Let
T
be the
\Sigma^{\prime}
-theory consisting of all sentences
\varphi(\bar{a})
, for all
\Sigma
-formulas
\varphi(\bar{x})
and tuples
\bar{a}
such that
\mathrm{M}\models\varphi(\bar{a})
.

Pick a model
\mathrm{N}^{\prime}
of
T
, and
let
\mathrm{N}
denote the
\Sigma
-structure obtained from
\mathrm{N}^{\prime}
by forgetting the constants in
\mathrm{M}\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}
.
The interpretation of the constants
m\in\mathrm{M}
of
\Sigma^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{N}^{\prime}
yields a function
i\colon\mathrm{M}\to\mathrm{N}
.
By the definition of
T
,
for any formula
\varphi(\bar{y})
and tuple
\bar{a}\in\mathrm{M}^{\bar{y}}
,

\mathrm{M}\models\varphi(\bar{a})\text{ if and only if }\mathrm{N}\models\varphi(i(\bar{a})).

Therefore, we may view (identyfing each
m\in\mathrm{M}
with
i(m)\in\mathrm{N}
) the
\Sigma
-structure
\mathrm{N}
as an elementary extension of
\mathrm{M}
.

Reassuming, models of the elementary diagram of
\mathrm{M}
correspond precisely to elementary extensions of
\mathrm{M}
. In particular, by extending the elementary diagram of
\mathrm{M}
by an arbitrary set of constants, from compactness we get that
\mathrm{M}
has elementary extensions of arbitrarily large cardinality (unless
\mathrm{M}
is finite). More generally, we have the following.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\mathrm{M}
be a model and let
\alpha(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime})
be a formula with
|\bar{x}|=|\bar{x}^{\prime}|
defining an equivalence relation in
\mathrm{M}
with infinitely many classes. Then for every cardinality
\mathfrak{n}

there is an elementary extension
\mathrm{N}\succ\mathrm{M}

in which

\alpha
defines an equivalence relation with at least
\mathfrak{n}
equivalence classes.

Proof.

To simplify notation, assume that
|\bar{x}|=|\bar{x}^{\prime}|=1
.
The case of
|\bar{x}|=|\bar{x}^{\prime}|=k>1
proceeds similarly, or can be deduced from the case
k=1
by extending the domain of
\mathrm{M}
by
\mathrm{M}^{k}
and the
k
projection functions.

Let
\alpha(x,x^{\prime})
be formula defining an equivalence relation
{\sim}
in
\mathrm{M}
with infinitely many classes.
Let
\Sigma
be the signature of
\mathrm{M}
.
Fix any set of constants
C
and let
\Sigma^{\prime}
extend
\Sigma
by
C\cup\mathrm{M}
,
where all the added elements are constant symbols. For any
c,d\in C
consider the
\Sigma^{\prime}
-sentence
\varphi_{cd}=\neg\alpha(c,d)
.
Let
T
be the
\Sigma^{\prime}
-theory consisting of:

–

the sentences
\varphi_{cd}
, for all
c\neq d
in
C
,

–

the elementary diagram of
\mathrm{M}
.

We show that every
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
containing finitely many sentences of the form
\varphi_{cd}
has a model. Let
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
be the finite set of constants appearing in the sentences
\varphi_{cd}\in T^{\prime}
. Let
\mathrm{M}^{\prime}
be the model
\mathrm{M}
together with
each constant
c
in
\mathrm{M}\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}
interpreted as the corresponding element
c\in\mathrm{M}
,
and
constants in
C^{\prime}
interpreted as pairwise
\sim
-inequivalent elements of
\mathrm{M}
, and constants in
C-C^{\prime}
interpreted as arbitrary elements of
\mathrm{M}
. This can be done, since there are infinitely many pairwise
\sim
-inequivalent elements in
\mathrm{M}
. This shows that
T^{\prime}
has a model.

By compactness,
T
has a model
\mathrm{N}^{\prime}
.
This model can be seen as an elementary extension of
T
together with
a set of
|C|
elements which are pairwise inequivalent with respect to the equivalence relation defined by
\alpha
in
\mathrm{N}
. Since
C
was taken arbitrary, this proves the lemma.
∎

Parameters.

Let
\mathrm{M}
be a model over a signature
\Sigma
and let
A\subseteq\mathrm{M}
be a set of elements. We may view
\mathrm{M}

as a model over a signature
\Sigma\cup A
, where the elements of
A
are seen as constant symbols, interpreted in
\mathrm{M}
in the expected way: a constant
a\in A
is interpreted as the element
a\in\mathrm{M}
. We call the elements of
A

parameters
in this context.
A
\Sigma
-formula
with parameters
from
A
is a formula over the signature
\Sigma\cup A
.

Types.

A
type
with variables
\bar{x}
and parameters from
A
,
or a
type over

A

is a set
p
of formulas
\varphi(\bar{x})
with parameters from
A
. We may write
p(\bar{x})
to indicate that
p
has variables
\bar{x}
.

If
p(\bar{x})
is a type over
A
and
B\subseteq A
then
p|B
denotes the subset of
p
consisting of all formulas with parameters from
B
.
If
\bar{b}\in\mathrm{M}^{\bar{x}}
is a tuple of elements of
\mathrm{M}
then
the type
of
\bar{b}

over

A
in
\mathrm{M}

is the set of formulas
\varphi(\bar{x})
with parameters from
A
that are satisfied by
\bar{b}
in
\mathrm{M}
.
This type is denoted
\tp(\bar{b}/A)
or
\tp_{\bar{x}}(\bar{b}/A)
. Note that

\tp(\bar{b}/A)
is related to the notion of
\theta
-types
as follows, for every formula
\theta(\bar{x};\bar{y})
and tuple
\bar{a}\in A^{\bar{y}}
:

\theta(\bar{x};\bar{a})\in\tp(\bar{b}/A)\iff\bar{a}\in\tp^{\theta}(\bar{b}/A).

In particular,
\tp(\bar{b}/A)
is uniquely determined by
\{\tp^{\theta}(\bar{b}/A)\mid\theta(\bar{x};\bar{y})\text{ is a formula}\}
.

A type
p(\bar{x})
is
satisfiable in a set

C
if there is some tuple
\bar{c}\in C^{\bar{x}}
which satisfies all the formulas in
p
.
A type
p(\bar{x})
with parameters from
A\subseteq\mathrm{M}
is
satisfiable
if it is satisfiable
in some elementary extension
\mathrm{N}
of
\mathrm{M}
.
By compactness, this is equivalent to saying that for any finite conjunction
\varphi(\bar{x})
of formulas in
p(\bar{x})
we have
\mathrm{M}\models\exists\bar{x}.\varphi(\bar{x})
.

A type
p(\bar{x})
with parameters from
A
is

complete
if it is satisfiable and for every formula

\varphi(\bar{x})
with parameters from
A
, either
\varphi
or
\neg\varphi
belongs to
p
. Equivalently,
p(\bar{x})
is the type over
A
of some tuple
\bar{b}\in\mathrm{N}^{\bar{x}}
, for some elementary extension
\mathrm{N}
of
\mathrm{M}
.
We sometimes say that a type is
partial
to emphasise that it may not be complete.
We denote the set of complete types with variables
\bar{x}
and parameters from
A
by
\mathrm{S}^{\bar{x}}(A)
or simply
\mathrm{S}(A)
, if
\bar{x}
is understood from the context.
Note that we have ommitted the model
\mathrm{M}
from the notation. Indeed,
if
\mathrm{M}^{\prime}
is a model containing the parameters
A

and satisfying the same sentences with parameters from
A

as
\mathrm{M}
, then
\mathrm{M}
and
\mathrm{M}^{\prime}
have
identical sets of complete types
p(\bar{x})
with parameters from
A
.
Hence,
\mathrm{S}^{\bar{x}}(A)
does not depend on
\mathrm{M}
, but only on the set of sentences satisfied by
A
in
\mathrm{M}
.

A.2
Finite satisfiability

A (partial) type
p(\bar{x})
with parameters from
A
is
finitely satisfiable
in
C
if every finite subset
p^{\prime}\subseteq p
is satisfiable in
C
.
Note that
\bar{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-6.45831pt\cr$|$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{\mathrm{M}}}B
(cf. Def.
11
) if and only if
\tp(\bar{a}/\mathrm{M}B)
is finitely satisfiable in
\mathrm{M}
.

Lemma A.3
.

A type
p(\bar{x})
with parameters from
\mathrm{M}

is finitely satisfiable in
\mathrm{M}
if and only if it is satisfiable. Consequently,
\bar{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-6.45831pt\cr$|$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{\mathrm{M}}}\mathrm{M}
for all
\bar{a}
in an elementary extension of
\mathrm{M}
.

Proof.

For the right-to-left implication, assume that
p
is satisfied by some tuple
\bar{c}\in\mathrm{N}^{\bar{x}}
for some elementary extension
\mathrm{N}
of
\mathrm{M}
. Pick a finite
p^{\prime}\subseteq p
, and suppose
p^{\prime}=\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k}\}
.
Consider the formula
\psi:=\varphi_{1}\land\cdots\land\varphi_{k}
.
Note that
\psi
may use some parameters from
\mathrm{M}
.
So we may write
\psi
as
\psi=\psi^{\prime}(\bar{x},\bar{a})

where
\psi^{\prime}(\bar{x},\bar{z})
is a formula and
\bar{a}\in\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{z}}
.

The formula
\exists_{\bar{x}}\psi^{\prime}(\bar{x},\bar{a})
holds in
\mathrm{N}
, as witnessed by
\bar{c}
. As
\mathrm{N}
is an elementary extension of
\mathrm{M}
, this formula also holds in
\mathrm{M}
.
So there is some
\bar{m}\in\mathrm{M}
satisfying
\psi^{\prime}(\bar{b},\bar{a})
. Therefore,
p^{\prime}
is satisfied by
\bar{m}
in
\mathrm{M}
, proving that
p
is finitely satisfiable in
\mathrm{M}
.

The left-to-right implicaiton is a basic application of the compactness theorem.

Consider the signature
\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma\cup\mathrm{M}\cup\bar{x}

extending
\Sigma
by constant symbols for each element of
\mathrm{M}
and each variable in
\bar{x}
.
Let
T
be the theory over
\Sigma^{\prime}
consisting of:

–

For every formula
\varphi(\bar{x})\in p
,
the
\Sigma^{\prime}
-sentence obtained from
\varphi(\bar{x})
by viewing each parameter
a\in\mathrm{M}
as the constant
a\in\mathrm{M}\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}
, and each variable
x\in\bar{x}
as the constant
x\in\bar{x}\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}
.

–

the elementary diagram of
\mathrm{M}
.

Then every finite subset
T^{\prime}
of
T
has a model.
Indeed, let
p^{\prime}
be the set of formulas
\varphi(\bar{x})
which occur (as
\Sigma^{\prime}
-sentences) in
T^{\prime}
.
Since
p(\bar{x})
is finitely satisfiable in
\mathrm{M}
,

p^{\prime}(\bar{x})
is satisfied by some tuple
\bar{m}\in\mathrm{M}^{\bar{x}}
. The pair
(\mathrm{M},\bar{m})
may be seen as a
\Sigma^{\prime}
-structure, where a constant
m\in\mathrm{M}
is interpreted by the corresponding element of
\mathrm{M}
,
and a constant
x\in\bar{x}
is interpreted as
\bar{m}(x)
.
Then
(\mathrm{M},\bar{m})
is a model of
T^{\prime}
.

By compactness,
T
has a model
\mathrm{N}^{\prime}
.
This model can be seen as an elementary extension
\mathrm{N}
of
\mathrm{M}
together with a tuple
\bar{c}\in\mathrm{N}^{\bar{x}}
of elements (obtained by the interpretation of the constants
\bar{x}
in
\mathrm{N}^{\prime}
), such that

\mathrm{N}\models\varphi(\bar{c})
for every formula
\varphi(\bar{x})\in p
.
Hence,
\bar{c}
satisfies
p(\bar{x})
in
\mathrm{N}.

∎

Finite satisfiability and filters.

Recall that a
filter
on a set
U
is a nonempty set
F\subseteq P(U)
that is closed under taking supersets (if
A\subseteq B
then
A\in F
implies
B\in F
), under binary intersections, and does not contain the empty set. A filter is an
ultrafilter
if for every
A\subseteq U
, either
A\in F
or
U-A\in F
. Every filter is contained in some ultrafilter, by the Kuratowski-Zorn lemma.

Let
\mathrm{N}
be a model,
A\subseteq\mathrm{N}
be a set and
\bar{x}
be a set of variables. Fix a filter
F
on
A^{\bar{x}}
.
The
average (partial) type
over
\mathrm{N}
is the partial type denoted
\Av_{F}(\bar{x})
such that for every formula
\varphi(\bar{x})
with parameters from
\mathrm{N}
,

\varphi(\bar{x})\in\Av_{F}(\bar{x})\iff\{\bar{a}\in A^{\bar{x}}:\mathrm{N}\models\varphi(\bar{a})\}\in F.

This is a consistent partial type: if say
\varphi_{1}(\bar{x}),\ldots,\varphi_{n}(\bar{x})\in\pi(\bar{x})
, then since any finitely many elements of
F
have non-empty intersection, there is
\bar{a}\in A^{\bar{x}}
which satisfies the conjunction
\varphi_{1}(\bar{x})\wedge\cdots\wedge\varphi_{n}(\bar{x})
. Hence this conjunction is consistent, indeed we have shown that
\Av_{F}(\bar{x})
is finitely satisfiable in
A
.

If
F
is an ultrafilter on
A^{\bar{x}}
, then
\Av_{F}(\bar{x})
is a complete type: for every formula
\varphi(\bar{x})
, either
\varphi(\bar{x})\in\Av_{F}(\bar{x})
or
\neg\varphi(\bar{x})\in\Av_{F}(\bar{x})
.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\pi(\bar{x})
be a partial type, then
\pi(\bar{x})
is finitely satisfiable in
A
if and only if there is a filter
F
on
A^{\bar{x}}
such that
\pi(\bar{x})\subseteq\Av_{F}(\bar{x})
.

Proof.

We have already observed that
\Av_{F}(\bar{x})
is finitely satisfiable in
A
. Conversely, assume that
\pi(\bar{x})
is finitely satisfiable in
A
, then define
F_{0}\subseteq P(A^{\bar{x}})
by:
F_{0}=\{\varphi(A):\varphi(\bar{x})\in\pi(\bar{x})\}
. The fact that
\pi(\bar{x})
is finitely satisfiable in
A
implies that any finitely many elements of
F_{0}
have non-empty intersection. Let
F
be the filter generated by
F_{0}
. Then we have
\pi(\bar{x})\subseteq\Av_{F}(\bar{x})
.
∎

Lemma A.5
.

Let
p(\bar{x})\in\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{N})
be a complete type, then
p(\bar{x})
is finitely satisfiable in
A
if and only if there is an ultrafilter
F
on
A^{\bar{x}}
such that
p(\bar{x})=\Av_{F}(\bar{x})
.

Proof.

We have already seen that if
F
is an ultrafilter on
A^{\bar{x}}
, then
\Av_{F}(\bar{x})
is a complete type over
\mathrm{N}
, which is finitely satisfiable in
A
. Conversely, if
p(\bar{x})\in\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{N})
is finitely satisfiable in
A
, then by the previous lemma, there is a filter
F_{0}
on
A^{\bar{x}}
such that
p(\bar{x})\subseteq\Av_{F_{0}}(\bar{x})
. Extend
F_{0}
to an ultrafilter
F
on
A^{\bar{x}}
. Then
p(\bar{x})\subseteq\Av_{F_{0}}(\bar{x})\subseteq\Av_{F}(\bar{x})
. But since
p(\bar{x})
is a complete type, one cannot add any formulas to it without making it inconsistent. Since
\Av_{F}(\bar{x})
is consistent, we must have
p(\bar{x})=\Av_{F}(\bar{x})
.
∎

Lemma A.6
.

Let
\pi(\bar{x})
be a partial type finitely satisfiable in
A
. Then there is a complete type
p(\bar{x})\in\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{N})
finitely satisfiable in

which extends
\pi(\bar{x})
.

Proof.

Let
F
be a filter on
A^{\bar{x}}
such that
\pi(\bar{x})\subseteq\Av_{F}(\bar{x})
. Let
F^{\prime}
be an ultrafilter extending

and let

. Then

is finitely satisfiable in

and extends

.
∎

Lemma A.7
.

Let
p(\bar{x})\in\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{N})
be finitely satisfiable in
A
. Then
p
is
A
-invariant
, that is: for any formula
\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
and tuples
\bar{b},\bar{b}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{N}^{\bar{y}}
, we have:

\tp(\bar{b}/A)=\tp(\bar{b}^{\prime}/A)\Longrightarrow\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{b})\in p\leftrightarrow\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{b}^{\prime})\in p.

Proof.

If
\tp(\bar{b}/A)=\tp(\bar{b}^{\prime}/A)
, then the formula
\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{b})\triangle\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{b}^{\prime})
has no solution in

. Since

A.3
Indiscernible sequences

Definition 14.

Let
\mathrm{M}
be a structure and
A\subseteq\mathrm{M}
. Let
I
be a linear order. A sequence
(\bar{a}_{i}:i\in I)
of tuples of
\mathrm{M}
is
indiscernible over
A
if for any
n<\omega
and indices

i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\qquad i^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<i^{\prime}_{n}

in
I
, we have

\tp(a_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{i_{n}}/A)=\tp(a_{i^{\prime}_{1}},\ldots,a_{i^{\prime}_{n}}/A).

Another way to state this is that the sequence
(\bar{a}_{i}:i\in I)
is indiscernible over
A
if for any
n<\omega
, indices

i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\qquad i^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<i^{\prime}_{n}

in
I
and formula
\theta(\bar{x}_{1},\ldots,\bar{x}_{n})
with parameters in
A
, we have

(1)\qquad\mathrm{M}\models\theta(\bar{a}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{i_{n}})\leftrightarrow\theta(\bar{a}_{i^{\prime}_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{i^{\prime}_{n}}).

If
\Delta
is a set of formulas with parameters in
A
, we will say that the sequence
(\bar{a}_{i}:i\in I)
is
\Delta
-indiscernible
if
(1)
holds for each
\theta
in
\Delta
. If
\Delta
and
I
are both finite, then this is expressible by a single first order formula.

Definition 15.

Two sequences
(\bar{a}_{i}:i\in I)
and
(\bar{b}_{j}:j\in J)
are
mutually indiscernible
over
A
if
(\bar{a}_{i}:i\in I)
is indiscernible over
A\cup\{\bar{b}_{j}:j\in J\}
and
(\bar{b}_{j}:j\in J)
is indiscernible over
A\cup\{\bar{a}_{i}:i\in I\}
.

An equivalent definition is that the sequences
(\bar{a}_{i}:i\in I)
and
(\bar{b}_{j}:j\in J)
are
mutually indiscernible
over
A
if for any
n<\omega
, indices

i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\qquad i^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<i^{\prime}_{n}

and

j_{1}<\cdots<j_{n}\qquad j^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<j^{\prime}_{n}

in
I
and any formula
\theta(\bar{x}_{1},\ldots,\bar{x}_{n};\bar{y}_{1},\ldots,\bar{y}_{n})
with parameters in
A
, we have

(2)\qquad\mathrm{M}\models\theta(\bar{a}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{i_{n}};\bar{b}_{j_{1}},\ldots,\bar{b}_{j_{n}})\leftrightarrow\theta(\bar{a}_{i^{\prime}_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{i^{\prime}_{n}};\bar{b}_{j^{\prime}_{1}},\ldots,\bar{b}_{j^{\prime}_{n}}).

If
\Delta
is a set of formulas with parameters in
A
, we will say that the sequences
(\bar{a}_{i}:i\in I)
and
(\bar{b}_{j}:j<\omega)
are
mutually
\Delta
-indiscernible
if
(2)
holds for each
\theta
in
\Delta
. If
\Delta
,
I
and
J
are finite, then this is again expressible by a single first-order formula.

In the following lemma, we use the notation
\Av_{F}|C
to mean the restriction of the type
\Av_{F}
to
C
. We also use the notation
\bar{a}_{<i}
to mean
\{\bar{a}_{j}:j<i\}
.

Lemma A.8
.

Let
A\subseteq B\subseteq\mathrm{M}
. Let
F
be an ultrafilter on
A^{\bar{x}}
. Let
I
be a linear order and let
(\bar{a}_{i}:i\in I)
be a sequence of tuples of
\mathrm{M}
such that:

\bar{a}_{i}\models\Av_{F}|B\bar{a}_{<i}.

Then the sequence
(\bar{a}_{i}:i\in I)
is indiscernible over
B
.

Proof.

Write
p=\Av_{F}
. Note that
p
is finitely satisfiable in
A
and a fortiori finitely satisfiable in
B
.

We prove by induction on
n
that if
n<\omega
and
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}
,
j_{1}<\cdots<j_{n}
are in
I
, then
\tp(\bar{a}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{i_{n}}/A)=\tp(\bar{a}_{j_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{j_{n}}/A)
. For
n=1
this follows from the fact that all
\bar{a}_{i}
realize
\Av_{F}|B
, which is a complete type over
B
. Assume we know it for
n
and take
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}<i_{n+1}
,
j_{1}<\cdots<j_{n}<j_{n+1}
in
I
. By induction hypothesis, we have

\tp(\bar{a}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{i_{n}}/B)=\tp(\bar{a}_{j_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{j_{n}}/B).

By Lemma
A.7
, for any formula
\theta(\bar{x};\bar{y}_{1},\ldots,\bar{y}_{n})
with parameters in
B
, we have:

\theta(\bar{x};\bar{a}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{i_{n}})\in p\iff\theta(\bar{x};\bar{a}_{j_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{j_{n}})\in p.

Now since
\bar{a}_{i_{n+1}}\models p|Ba_{i_{1}}\ldots a_{i_{n}}
, we have

\theta(\bar{x};\bar{a}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{i_{n}})\in p\iff\mathrm{N}\models\theta(\bar{a}_{i_{n+1}};\bar{a}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{i_{n}}),

and similarly since
\bar{a}_{j_{n+1}}\models p|Ba_{j_{1}}\ldots a_{j_{n}}
, we have

\theta(\bar{x};\bar{a}_{j_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{j_{n}})\in p\iff\mathrm{N}\models\theta(\bar{a}_{j_{n+1}};\bar{a}_{j_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{j_{n}}).

Putting all of this together, we get

\mathrm{N}\models\theta(\bar{a}_{i_{n+1}};\bar{a}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{i_{n}})\iff\mathrm{N}\models\theta(\bar{a}_{j_{n+1}};\bar{a}_{j_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{j_{n}}).

Since the formula
\theta
was an arbitrary formula with parameters in
B
, we deduce

\tp(\bar{a}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{i_{n+1}}/B)=\tp(\bar{a}_{j_{1}},\ldots,\bar{a}_{j_{n+1}}/B)

as required.
∎

Definition 16.

Let the type
p(\bar{x})\in\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{M})
be finitely satisfiable in

and let

of

By the previous lemma, a Morley sequence of

.

A.4

Indiscernible sequences are easy to find thanks to Ramsey’s theorem.

Definition 17.

.

Let
\mathrm{N}
be a model and

Appendix C
Proof of Proposition
8.6

Before proving Proposition
8.6
,
we prove some lemmas.

The first lemma
is a characterisation of theories that exclude large grids
using mutually indiscernible sequences.

Lemma C.1
.

Assume that in some model
\mathrm{M}
there are two mutually indiscernible sequences
(\bar{a}_{i}:i<\omega)
and
(\bar{b}_{j}:j<\omega)
and a singleton
c
and for some formulas
\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y};z)
with parameters in
\mathrm{M}
such that:

–

\varphi(\bar{a}_{0},\bar{b}_{0};c)
holds,

–

\neg\varphi(\bar{a}_{i},\bar{b}_{0};c)
holds for all
i>0
,

–

\neg\varphi(\bar{a}_{0},\bar{b}_{j},c)
holds for all
j>0
.

Then the formula

\psi(\bar{x}\bar{x}^{\prime},\bar{y}\bar{y}^{\prime};z)\equiv\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y};z)\wedge\neg\varphi(\bar{x}^{\prime},\bar{y};z)\wedge\neg\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y}^{\prime};z)

defines large grids in
\mathrm{M}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
A.11
, there are two sequences
(\bar{a}^{\prime}_{i}:i\in\mathbb{Z})
and
(\bar{b}^{\prime}_{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z})
in some elementary extension of
\mathrm{M}
which are mutually indiscernible and based on
(\bar{a}_{i}:i<\omega)
and
(\bar{b}_{j}:j<\omega)
. Since those latter sequences are already mutually indiscernible, we have
\tp((\bar{a}^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(\bar{b}^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}})=\tp((\bar{a}_{i})_{i<\omega}(\bar{b}_{i})_{i<\omega})
. It follows that we can find some
\bar{a}_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{-}
, and
\bar{b}_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{-}
so that

\tp((\bar{a}^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(\bar{b}^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}})=\tp((\bar{a}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(\bar{b}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}).

In particular, the two sequences
(\bar{a}_{i}:i\in\mathbb{Z})
and
(\bar{b}_{i}:i\in\mathbb{Z})
are mutually indiscernible. By removing some points with negative indices, we may assume that either for all
i<0
,
\varphi(\bar{a}_{i},\bar{b}_{0};c)
holds for for all
i<0
,
\neg\varphi(\bar{a}_{i},\bar{b}_{0};c)
holds and similarly for
\varphi(\bar{a}_{0},\bar{b}_{j};c)
.

By mutual indiscernibility of the two sequences, for any
(k,l)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, we can find some
c_{k,l}
in an elementary extension
\mathrm{N}
of
\mathrm{M}
such that:

–

\varphi(\bar{a}_{k},\bar{b}_{l};c_{k,l})

–

\neg\varphi(\bar{a}_{i},\bar{b}_{l};c_{k,l})
holds for
i>k
, and

–

\varphi(\bar{a}_{k},\bar{b}_{j};c_{k,l})
holds for
j>l
.

Note that the points
c_{k,l}
are necessarily pairwise distinct.
Consider the formula
\psi
as in the statement.
Then
\psi(\bar{x}\bar{x}^{\prime},\bar{y}\bar{y}^{\prime};z)
holds of a tuple
(\bar{a}_{i}\bar{a}_{i+1},\bar{b}_{j}\bar{b}_{j+1},c_{k,l})
if and only if
(i,j)=(k,l)
. Hence,
\psi
defines an infinite grid in
\mathrm{N}
. As
\mathrm{N}
is an elementary extension of
\mathrm{M}
, the formula
\psi
also defines arbitrarily large grids in
\mathrm{M}
.
∎

Lemma C.2
.

Let
\mathrm{M}\prec\mathrm{N}
be models. Let
p(\bar{x})
be a type with parameters in
\mathrm{N}
, finitely satisfiable in

\bar{a}\models p|\mathrm{M}
. Let

\mathrm{M}
extending

Proof.

By Lemma
A.6
, it is enough to show that the partial type
\pi(\bar{x};\bar{y})=p(\bar{x})\cup\tp_{\bar{x}\bar{y}}(\bar{a}\bar{c}/\mathrm{M})
is finitely satisfiable in
\mathrm{M}
. Take
\theta(\bar{x})\in p(\bar{x})
and
\psi(\bar{x};\bar{y})\in\tp(\bar{a}\bar{c}/\mathrm{M})

\theta(\bar{x})\wedge\psi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
in
\mathrm{M}
. Consider the formula
\theta^{\prime}(\bar{x})=\theta(\bar{x})\wedge(\exists\bar{y})\psi(\bar{x};\bar{y})

\mathrm{M}

\bar{a}_{0}

\bar{c}_{0}
in

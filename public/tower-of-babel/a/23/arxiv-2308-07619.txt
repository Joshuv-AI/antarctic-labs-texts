Baxter operators in Ruijsenaars hyperbolic system IV. Coupling constant reflection symmetry


Abstract
We introduce and study a new family of commuting Baxter operators in the Ruijsenaars hyperbolic system, different from that considered by us earlier. Using a degeneration of Rains integral identity we verify the commutativity between the two families of Baxter operators and explore this fact for the proof of the coupling constant symmetry of the wave function. We also establish a connection between new Baxter operators and Noumi-Sano difference operators.

Introduction

1.1
Ruijsenaars system and
Q
-operator

Denote by
T^{a}_{x_{i}}
the shift operator

T^{a}_{x_{i}}:=e^{a\partial_{x_{i}}},\qquad\left(T^{a}_{x_{i}}\,f\right)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},\ldots,x_{n})=f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}+a,\ldots,x_{n})

(1.1)

and define its products for any subset
I\subset[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}

T^{a}_{I,x}=\prod_{i\in I}T_{x_{i}}^{a}.

(1.2)

The Ruijsenaars hyperbolic system
[
R1
]
is governed by commuting symmetric difference operators

H_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}-\imath g\right)}{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)}\cdot T^{-\imath\omega_{1}}_{I,x}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}+\imath g\right)}{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)}

(1.3)

where
r=1,\dots,n
.
Here and in what follows we denote tuples of
n
variables as

\bm{x}_{n}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}).

(1.4)

We also consider gauge equivalent Macdonald operators

M_{r}:=M_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\sh\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}-\imath g\right)}{\sh\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)}\cdot T^{-\imath\omega_{1}}_{I,x}.

(1.5)

Both families of operators are parametrized by three constants: periods
\bm{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
and a coupling constant
g
,
which originally are supposed to be real positive. The equivalence is established by means of the measure function

\mu(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\mu(x_{i}-x_{j}),\qquad\mu(x):=\mu_{g}(x|\bm{\omega})=S_{2}(\imath x|\bm{\omega})S_{2}^{-1}(\imath x+g|\bm{\omega}).

(1.6)

Here
S_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})
is the double sine function, see Appendix
A
.
Namely,

\sqrt{\mu(\bm{x}_{n})}\,M_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu(\bm{x}_{n})}}=H_{r}(\bm{x}_{n},g|\bm{\omega}).

(1.7)

In this paper, as well as in
[
BDKK
,
BDKK2
,
BDKK3
]
and unlike the original Ruijsenaars setting, we consider periods
\bm{\omega}
and coupling constant
g
to be complex valued, assuming that

\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{1}>0,\qquad\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{2}>0,\qquad 0<\mathrm{Re}\,g<\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{1}+\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{2}

(1.8)

and

\nu_{g}=\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{g}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}>0.

(1.9)

Denote the dual coupling constant by reflection

g\rightarrow g^{\ast}=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-g

(1.10)

and introduce the function
{K}(x)

{K}(x):={K}_{g}(x|\bm{\omega})=S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(\imath x+\frac{g^{\ast}}{2}\Big|\bm{\omega}\Bigr)S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(-\imath x+\frac{g^{\ast}}{2}\Big|\bm{\omega}\Bigr).

(1.11)

We also frequently use the products of this function

{K}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{m})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}{K}(x_{i}-y_{j}).

(1.12)

Note that in the notation (
1.10
) the measure function (
1.6
) can be rewritten as

\mu(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}S_{2}(\imath x_{i}-\imath x_{j}|\bm{\omega})\,S_{2}(\imath x_{i}-\imath x_{j}+g^{\ast}|\bm{\omega})

(1.13)

due to the reflection formula (
A.3
). In
[
BDKK
,
BDKK2
]
we studied a family of operators
Q_{n}(\lambda)
parameterized by
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and called Baxter
Q
-operators. These are integral operators

\left(Q_{n}(\lambda)f\right)(\bm{x}_{n})=d_{n}(g|\bm{\omega})\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\,Q(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n};\lambda)f(\bm{y}_{n})

(1.14)

with the kernel

Q(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n};\lambda)=e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n})}{K}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})\mu(\bm{y}_{n})

(1.15)

and normalizing constant

d_{n}(g|\bm{\omega})=\frac{1}{n!}\left[\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}S_{2}(g|\bm{\omega})\right]^{-n}.

(1.16)

Here and below for a tuple
\bm{x}_{n}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
we use the notation
\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}
for the sum of components

\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}=x_{1}+\ldots+x_{n}.

(1.17)

In
[
BDKK
]
we established the commutativity of Baxter operators with Macdonald operators and of Baxter operators themselves

\displaystyle Q_{n}(\lambda)\,M_{r}

\displaystyle=M_{r}\,Q_{n}(\lambda),

(1.18)

\displaystyle Q_{n}(\lambda)\,Q_{n}(\rho)

\displaystyle=Q_{n}(\rho)\,Q_{n}(\lambda),

(1.19)

where
r=1,\dots,n
. The kernels of the operators in both sides of (
1.19
) are analytic functions of
\lambda,\rho
in the strip

|\operatorname{Im}(\lambda-\rho)|<\nu_{g}.

(1.20)

The commutativity (
1.19
) holds under assumptions (
1.8
), (
1.9
). For (
1.18
) we assume in addition

\mathrm{Re}\,g<\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{2}.

(1.21)

As it was shown for other integrable systems, kernels of Baxter operators in the classical limit give generating functions of Backlund transformations
[
KS
,
Skl
]
. Classical transformation corresponding to the present
Q
-operators was considered in the paper
[
HR1
]
. We also note that for the trigonometric Ruijsenaars and Calogero models Baxter operators of similar type were studied in the works
[
KMS
,
GLO
]
.

1.2
Reflection
g\rightarrow g^{*}
and
Q^{*}
-operator

Commuting the shift operators to the right in Ruijsenaars operators (
1.3
) we rewrite them in the form

H_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}-\imath g\right)}{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)}\frac{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}-\imath g^{*}\right)}{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}-\imath\omega_{1}-\imath\omega_{2}\right)}\cdot T^{-\imath\omega_{1}}_{I,x}.

(1.22)

Here we omit the dependence on periods
\bm{\omega}
. From (
1.22
) it is clear that these operators are invariant under reflection
g\rightarrow g^{*}=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-g

H_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g)=H_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g^{*}).

(1.23)

Since the measure function (
1.6
) depends explicitly on
g
, the Macdonald operators (
1.5
) lack this symmetry. Instead, due to the connection formula (
1.7
), they satisfy

M_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g)=\eta^{-1}(\bm{x}_{n})\,M_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g^{*})\,\eta(\bm{x}_{n})

(1.24)

with

\eta(\bm{x}_{n})=\sqrt{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{\mu_{g}(x_{i}-x_{j})}{\mu_{g^{*}}(x_{i}-x_{j})}}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}S^{-1}_{2}(\imath x_{i}-\imath x_{j}+g),

(1.25)

where we used reflection formula (
A.3
) for the double sine function. The function
\eta(\bm{x}_{n})
represents
g
-dependent part of the measure function
\mu(\bm{x}_{n})
(
1.6
)

\mu(\bm{x}_{n})=\eta(\bm{x}_{n})\Delta(\bm{x}_{n})

(1.26)

with the rest part
\Delta(\bm{x}_{n})
being hyperbolic Vandermonde determinant

\Delta(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}S_{2}(\imath x_{i}-\imath x_{j})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i<j\end{subarray}}^{n}4\sh\frac{\pi(x_{i}-x_{j})}{\omega_{1}}\sh\frac{\pi(x_{i}-x_{j})}{\omega_{2}},

(1.27)

see the reflection formula (
A.2
).

As well as Macdonald operators, Baxter operators (
1.14
) depend explicitly on
g
. For a moment we emphasize it in notation writing
Q_{n}(\lambda;g)
. The symmetry (
1.24
) suggests to look at another family of integral operators. Namely, introduce a family of operators
Q^{*}_{n}(\lambda)
parameterized by
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}

Q^{*}_{n}(\lambda)=\eta^{-1}(\bm{x}_{n})\,Q_{n}(\lambda;g^{*})\,\eta(\bm{x}_{n}).

(1.28)

It is given by integral operator

\bigl(Q^{*}_{n}(\lambda)f\bigr)(\bm{x}_{n})=d_{n}(g^{*})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\,Q^{*}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n};\lambda)f(\bm{y}_{n})

(1.29)

with the kernel obtained from (
1.15
) using (
1.26
)

Q^{*}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n};\lambda)=\eta^{-1}(\bm{x}_{n})\,e^{2\pi\imath\lambda(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n})}K^{*}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})\Delta(\bm{y}_{n}).

(1.30)

Here we denoted

K^{*}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})=\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}K^{*}(x_{i}-y_{j})

(1.31)

with
K^{*}(x)
being the counterpart of
K(x)
(
1.11
) with respect to the reflection
g\rightarrow g^{*}

K^{*}(x):=K_{g^{*}}(x|\bm{\omega})=S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(\imath x+\frac{g}{2}\,\Big|\,\bm{\omega}\Bigr)S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(-\imath x+\frac{g}{2}\,\Big|\,\bm{\omega}\Bigr).

(1.32)

In the light of the formulas (
1.24
) and (
1.28
) the commutativity relations for the first
Q
-operator and Macdonald operators (
1.18
), (
1.19
) imply the same relations between Macdonald operators and the second
Q
-operator, as well as for the second
Q
-operators themselves

\displaystyle Q^{*}_{n}(\lambda)\,M_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g)

\displaystyle=M_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g)\,Q^{*}_{n}(\lambda),

(1.33)

\displaystyle Q^{*}_{n}(\lambda)\,Q^{*}_{n}(\rho)

\displaystyle=Q^{*}_{n}(\rho)\,Q^{*}_{n}(\lambda).

(1.34)

For the second relation we need assumption analogous to (
1.9
), that is we assume

\nu_{g^{*}}=\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{g^{*}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}>0.

(1.35)

For the first commutativity we in addition need the condition analogous to (
1.21
)

\mathrm{Re}\,g^{*}<\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{2}.

(1.36)

Note that in the case of real constants
\bm{\omega},g
both assumptions (
1.9
), (
1.35
) follow from (
1.8
). Looking at the mentioned above commutativity relations it is natural to expect that the first and the second
Q
-operators also commute with each other.

Theorem 1
.

Under conditions (
1.8
), (
1.9
), (
1.35
) the two families of Baxter
Q
-operators commute

Q_{n}^{*}(\lambda)\,Q_{n}(\rho)=Q_{n}(\rho)\,Q_{n}^{*}(\lambda).

(1.37)

The kernels of the operators in both sides are analytic functions of
\lambda,\rho
in the
strip

|\operatorname{Im}(\lambda-\rho)|<\frac{\nu_{g}+\nu_{g^{*}}}{2}=\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}.

(1.38)

The proof of this theorem is given in Section
2.1
. The main ingredient of the proof is the degeneration of the remarkable elliptic integral identity proved by E. Rains
[
Ra1
, Theorem 4.1]
. A derivation of this degeneration is given in Appendix
B
.

1.3
Wave function and local relations

Commutativity relations between both
Q
-operators and Macdonald operators (
1.33
), (
1.34
), (
1.37
) suggest that they should have common eigenfunctions. Eigenfunctions of the Macdonald operators were constructed by M. Hallnäs and S. Ruijsenaars
[
HR2
]
. In
[
BDKK2
]
we proved that these eigenfunctions diagonalize the operator
Q_{n}(\lambda)
. In the present work we show that they also diagonalize the operator
Q_{n}^{*}(\lambda)
. It is done using certain local relations between Baxter operators and their degenerations.

Denote by
\Lambda_{n}(\lambda)
the integral operator

\begin{split}\left(\Lambda_{n}(\lambda)f\right)(\bm{x}_{n})=d_{n-1}(g)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}d\bm{y}_{n-1}\,\Lambda(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1};\lambda)f(\bm{y}_{n-1})\end{split}

(1.39)

with the kernel

\Lambda(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1};\lambda)=e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1})}{K}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1})\mu(\bm{y}_{n-1})

(1.40)

and normalizing constant
d_{n-1}(g)
given by the formula (
1.16
). We call it raising operator. The wave function is given by the multiple integral

\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}):=\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})=\Lambda_{n}(\lambda_{n})\,\Lambda_{n-1}(\lambda_{n-1})\cdots\Lambda_{2}(\lambda_{2})\,e^{{2\pi\imath}\lambda_{1}x_{1}}

(1.41)

or recursively

\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\Lambda_{n}(\lambda_{n})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n-1}}(\bm{x}_{n-1}),\qquad\Psi_{\lambda_{1}}(x_{1})=e^{{2\pi\imath}{}\lambda_{1}x_{1}}.

(1.42)

M. Hallnäs and S. Ruijsenaars proved
[
HR2
]
for real periods
\bm{\omega}
and complex valued constant
g
that the function (
1.41
)
is a joint eigenfunction of Macdonald operators. In
[
BDKK2
]
by similar arguments we extended this result to the case of complex
\bm{\omega}
and proved that it is also an eigenfunction of the first
Q
-operator (
1.14
). Let us describe the corresponding eigenvalues.
For any
a\in\mathbb{C}
denote

\hat{a}=\frac{a}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}},

(1.43)

so that

\hat{\bm{\omega}}=(\omega_{2}^{-1},\omega_{1}^{-1}),\qquad\hat{g}=\frac{g}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}},

(1.44)

and analogously to
g^{*}=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-g
we define

\hat{g}^{*}=\hat{\omega}_{1}+\hat{\omega}_{2}-\hat{g}=\frac{g^{*}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}.

(1.45)

Also introduce the function

\hat{K}(\lambda):=K_{\hat{g}^{*}}(\lambda|\hat{\bm{\omega}})=S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(\imath\lambda+\frac{\hat{g}}{2}\,\Big|\,\hat{\bm{\omega}}\Bigr)\,S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(-\imath\lambda+\frac{\hat{g}}{2}\,\Big|\,\hat{\bm{\omega}}\Bigr).

(1.46)

Then the spectral description of the first
Q
-operator has the following form

Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\hat{K}(\lambda-\lambda_{j})\,\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}),

(1.47)

see
[
BDKK2
, Theorem 4]
. For the proof we use the iterative structure of the wave function (
1.41
) and the local exchange relation between
Q
-operator and raising operator
[
BDKK2
, Theorem 3]

Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\Lambda_{n}(\rho)=\hat{K}(\lambda-\rho)\,\Lambda_{n}(\rho)\,Q_{n-1}(\lambda).

(1.48)

In the course of proof one also needs to justify convergence of various multiple integrals appearing on the way.

The
\Lambda
-operator (
1.39
) can be obtained in the certain limit from the first Baxter
Q
-operator, due to the similarity of their kernels (

), (

) and asymptotic behavior of the function

In present work we investigate properties of the second

-operator (
1.28
) precisely in the same way. First, taking certain limit of the commutativity relation (

) we obtain an analogous to (

) exchange relation, but for the second

\hat{K}^{*}(\lambda):=K_{\hat{g}}(\lambda|\hat{\bm{\omega}})=S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(\imath\lambda+\frac{\hat{g}^{*}}{2}\,\Big|\,\hat{\bm{\omega}}\Bigr)\,S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(-\imath\lambda+\frac{\hat{g}^{*}}{2}\,\Big|\,\hat{\bm{\omega}}\Bigr).

It is a counterpart of the function
\hat{K}(\lambda)
with respect to the reflection
\hat{g}\rightarrow\hat{g}^{*}

.

The operator identity

\Lambda^{*}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1};\lambda)=\eta^{-1}(\bm{x}_{n})\,e^{2\pi\imath\lambda(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1})}\,K^{*}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1})\,\Delta(\bm{y}_{n-1})

Theorem 3
.

The operator identity

\Lambda_{n}^{*}(\lambda)\,\Lambda_{n-1}(\rho)=K_{2\hat{g}}(\lambda-\rho|\hat{\bm{\omega}})\,\Lambda_{n}(\rho)\,\Lambda^{*}_{n-1}(\lambda)

holds true for

(1.56)

\displaystyle Q^{*}_{n}(\lambda)\,Q_{n}(\rho)=Q_{n}(\rho)\,Q_{n}^{*}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\hskip 64.01869pt\downarrow

\displaystyle Q_{n}(\lambda)\,Q_{n}(\rho)=Q_{n}(\rho)\,Q_{n}(\lambda)

Theorem 4
.

The wave function
\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})
is a joint eigenfunction of the commuting family of operators
Q^{*}_{n}(\lambda)

Q^{*}_{n}(\lambda)\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\hat{K}^{*}(\lambda-\lambda_{j})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(1.58)

The integrals in both sides of (
1.58
) converge if

|\operatorname{Im}(\lambda-\lambda_{n})|<\frac{1}{2}(\nu_{g^{*}}-\varepsilon\nu_{g}),\qquad|\operatorname{Im}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})|\leq\theta(\varepsilon),\qquad k,j=1,\dots,n

(1.59)

for any
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
and

\theta(\varepsilon)=\frac{\nu_{g}}{2(n-1)!e}\varepsilon.

(1.60)

On the other hand,
the exchange relation (
1.55

, which is suggested by the formula (

\hat{\eta}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n}):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}S_{2}^{-1}(\imath\lambda_{i}-\imath\lambda_{j}+\hat{g}^{*}|\hat{\bm{\omega}}).

(1.61)

Theorem 5
.

The wave function satisfies the relation

\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})=\hat{\eta}^{-1}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n})\,\eta^{-1}(\bm{x}_{n})\,\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n};g^{*}|\bm{\omega}).

(1.62)

The proof of Theorem
5
based on the local relation (
1.55
) is given in Section
3.2
. The symmetry (
1.62
) was conjectured by M. Hallnäs and S. Ruijsenaars in
[
HR2
]
and was proven by them for

]

The relation (

conjectured by S. Ruijsenaars and proven in

: symmetry over spatial variables and over spectral variables, and the space-spectral duality

\Psi_{\bm{\lambda}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})=\Psi_{\bm{x}_{n}}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n};\hat{g}^{*}|\hat{\bm{\omega}}),

(1.63)

see

Relation to Noumi-Sano operators

Denote by
[x|\omega_{1}]_{m}
and
[x|\omega_{2}]_{m}
the following trigonometric Pochhammer symbols

\begin{split}[x|\omega_{1}]_{m}=&\frac{S_{2}(x)}{S_{2}(x+m\omega_{1})}=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}2\sin\frac{\pi(x+j\omega_{1})}{\omega_{2}},\\

(1.76)

Note that

[x+\omega_{1}|\omega_{2}]_{m}=(-1)^{m}[x|\omega_{2}]_{m}.

(1.77)

In
[
NS
]
M. Noumi and A. Sano introduced an infinite family of difference operators, that in the case of hyperbolic Ruijsenaars model have the following form

N_{r}^{(1)}(\bm{x}_{n})=(-1)^{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{m}_{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}\\[2.0pt]

(1.78)

Here
\bm{m}_{n}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})
is a sequence of non-negative integers and

|\bm{m}_{n}|=m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}.

(1.79)

Let us collect them into the generating series

N^{(1)}(\bm{x}_{n};\lambda)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{r}e^{-2\pi\lambda r\omega_{1}}\,N_{r}^{(1)}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(1.80)

Alternatively, we can consider Noumi-Sano operators with shifts by period
\omega_{2}

N_{r}^{(2)}(\bm{x}_{n})=(-1)^{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{m}_{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}\\[2.0pt]
|\bm{m}_{n}|=r\end{subarray}}\,\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{[\imath x_{i}-\imath x_{j}+g|\omega_{2}]_{m_{i}}}{[\imath x_{i}-\imath x_{j}-m_{j}\omega_{2}|\omega_{2}]_{m_{i}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}T^{-\imath m_{i}\omega_{2}}_{x_{i}}

(1.81)

collected into the generating series

N^{(2)}(\bm{x}_{n};\lambda)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{r}e^{-2\pi\lambda r\omega_{2}}\,N_{r}^{(2)}(\bm{x}_{n}).

(1.82)

As opposed to Macdonald operators, Noumi-Sano operators contain shifts by multiple periods
\omega_{i}
.
In the work
[
NS
]
it is proven that these operators commute with Macdonald operators and between themselves

\begin{split}M_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})\,N_{s}^{(i)}(\bm{x}_{n})&=N_{s}^{(i)}(\bm{x}_{n})\,M_{n}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega}),\\[8.0pt]
N_{r}^{(i)}(\bm{x}_{n})N_{s}^{(i)}(\bm{x}_{n})&=N_{s}^{(i)}(\bm{x}_{n})N_{r}^{(i)}(\bm{x}_{n})\end{split}

(1.83)

for any
r,s
and
i=1,2
.
Due to (

) Noumi-Sano operators of different kind also commute between themselves. Moreover, since they can be expressed via Macdonald operators by means of certain determinant formulas
[

, Proposition 1.3]

-operators.

\frac{S_{2}(z)}{S_{2}(z+\omega_{1})}=2\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{2}},\qquad\frac{S_{2}(z)}{S_{2}(z+\omega_{2})}=2\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{1}}

and inversion relation

S_{2}(z)S_{2}(-z)=-4\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{1}}\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{2}},

(A.2)

or equivalently

S_{2}(z)S_{2}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-z)=1.

(A.3)

The factorization formula

S_{2}(z+m\omega_{1}+k\omega_{2})=(-1)^{mk}\,\frac{S_{2}(z+m\omega_{1})\,S_{2}(z+k\omega_{2})}{S_{2}(z)}

(A.4)

follows from (
A.1
).
The function
S_{2}(z)
is a meromorphic function of

with poles at

z_{m,k}=m\omega_{1}+k\omega_{2},\qquad m,k\geq 1

(A.5)

and zeros at

(A.6)

For

all poles and zeros are simple. The residues of
S_{2}(z)
and
S^{-1}_{2}(z)
at these points are

\displaystyle\underset{z=z_{m,k}}{\operatorname{Res}}\,S_{2}(z)=\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}}{2\pi}\frac{(-1)^{mk}}{\prod\limits_{s=1}^{m-1}2\sin\dfrac{\pi s\omega_{1}}{\omega_{2}}\prod\limits_{l=1}^{k-1}2\sin\dfrac{\pi l\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}}},

(A.7)

\displaystyle\underset{z=z_{-m,-k}}{\operatorname{Res}}\,S^{-1}_{2}(z)=\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}}{2\pi}\frac{(-1)^{mk+m+k}}{\prod\limits_{s=1}^{m}2\sin\dfrac{\pi s\omega_{1}}{\omega_{2}}\prod\limits_{l=1}^{k}2\sin\dfrac{\pi l\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}}}.

(A.8)

In the analytic region
\mathrm{Re}\,z\in(0,\mathrm{Re}\,(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}))
we have the following integral representation for the logarithm of
S_{2}(z)

\ln S_{2}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{2t}\left(\frac{\sh\left[(2z-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})t\right]}{\sh(\omega_{1}t)\sh(\omega_{2}t)}-\frac{2z-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}t}\right).

(A.9)

It is clear from this representation that the double sine function is homogeneous

S_{2}(\gamma z|\gamma\omega_{1},\gamma\omega_{2})=S_{2}(z|\omega_{1},\omega_{2}),\qquad\gamma\in(0,\infty)

(A.10)

and invariant under permutation of periods

S_{2}(z|\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=S_{2}(z|\omega_{2},\omega_{1}).

(A.11)

The double sine function can be expressed through the Barnes double Gamma function

It is also connected to the Ruijsenaars hyperbolic Gamma function

Lemma 2
.

For any
\varepsilon\in[0,2]
we have

L_{n}\leq(n-1)\varepsilon\|\bm{x}_{n}\|-\varepsilon\|\bm{y}_{n-1}\|.

(C.3)

Proof.

Both sides of the stated inequality are symmetric with respect to components of
\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1}
. Therefore, without loss of generality we assume the ordering

x_{1}\geq\ldots\geq x_{n},\qquad y_{1}\geq\ldots\geq y_{n-1}.

(C.4)

For the vector
\bm{x}_{n}
with ordered components we write

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i<j\end{subarray}}^{n}|x_{i}-x_{j}|=\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(n-2m+1)|x_{m}-x_{n-m+1}|.

(C.5)

Similarly for
\bm{y}_{n-1}
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle L_{n}=\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(n-2m+1)|x_{m}-x_{n-m+1}|

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}(n-2m)|y_{m}-y_{n-m}|

(C.6)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}|x_{i}-y_{j}|.

Next step it to regroup terms. Consider term with
m=1
from the first sum and terms with
i=1,n
from the third double sum and write the estimate

\displaystyle(n-1)|x_{1}-x_{n}|

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(|x_{1}-y_{j}|+|x_{n}-y_{j}|\right)

(C.7)

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)\,\varepsilon\left(|x_{1}|+|x_{n}|\right)-\varepsilon\,\|\bm{y}_{n-1}\|,

where we used inequality (
C.1
) multiple times. Similarly let us estimate the term with
m>1
from the first sum together with the corresponding terms from the third double sum

(n-2m+1)|x_{m}-x_{n-m+1}|-\sum_{j=m}^{n-m}\left(|x_{m}-y_{j}|+|x_{n-m+1}-y_{j}|\right)\leq 0,

(C.8)

where we used triangle inequality multiple times. Remaining from the third double sum terms can be grouped with terms from the second sum

(n-2m)|y_{m}-y_{n-m}|-\sum_{i=m+1}^{n-m}\left(|x_{i}-y_{m}|+|x_{i}-y_{n-m}|\right)\leq 0,

(C.9)

where we again used triangle inequalities. Collecting everything together we have

L_{n}\leq(n-1)\,\varepsilon\left(|x_{1}|+|x_{n}|\right)-\varepsilon\,\|\bm{y}_{n-1}\|\leq(n-1)\,\varepsilon\|\bm{x}_{n}\|-\varepsilon\,\|\bm{y}_{n-1}\|.

(C.10)

∎

Classical gravitational anomalies of Liouville theory
Pavel Haman, Alfredo Iorio

Abstract
We show that for classical Liouville field theory, diffeomorphism invariance, Weyl invariance and locality cannot hold together. This is due to a genuine Virasoro center, present in the theory, that leads to an energy-momentum tensor with non-tensorial conformal transformations, in flat space, and with a non-vanishing trace, in curved space. Our focus is on a field-independent term, proportional to the square of the Weyl gauge field, W μ ​ W μ W_{\mu}W^{\mu} , that makes the action Weyl-invariant and was disregarded in previous investigations of Weyl and conformal symmetry. We show this term to be related to the classical center of the Virasoro algebra. The mechanism uncovered here is a classical version of the quantum anomalous phenomenon: the generalization to curved space only allows to keep one of the two symmetries enjoyed by the flat space theory, either Lorentz (diffeomorphism) or conformal invariance.

with
\mu,\nu\in\{-,+\}
. Thus, the scalar product is
x^{2}=2x^{+}x^{-}
. Raising and lowering indices change the
+
to
-
sign and vice versa, e.g.
\partial_{\pm}=\partial^{\mp}
,
\Theta_{\mp\mp}=\Theta^{\pm\pm}
. The Levi-Civita symbol is
\varepsilon^{-+}=+1
.

Appendix B
Improvement of the Energy-momentum Tensor

Here we compute the extra improvement term of the EMT

T^{\mu\nu}_{\text{extra}}\equiv-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\functionalderivative{g_{\mu\nu}}\int\differential[2]{x}\frac{2}{\beta^{2}}\sqrt{-g}W^{\mu}W_{\mu}\equiv-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\functionalderivative{\Delta A}{g_{\mu\nu}}\,.

To facilitate calculations it is easier to introduce the “conformal” metric

\gamma_{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{\sqrt{-g}},\qquad\gamma^{\mu\nu}\equiv\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu},\qquad\sqrt{-g}\equiv\rho\,,

(9)

which, in light-cone coordinates, can be parametrized as

\gamma_{++}=e^{\gamma}\sinh\sigma,\quad\gamma_{+-}=\cosh\sigma,\quad\gamma_{--}=e^{-\gamma}\sinh\sigma\,.

(10)

In this parametrization a new quantity
R^{\mu}
can be defined

R^{\mu}\equiv 2\sqrt{-g}W^{\mu}\,.

(11)

Using the following identity

\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}=\gamma^{\mu\beta}\gamma^{\nu\alpha}-\gamma^{\mu\alpha}\gamma^{\nu\beta}\,,

(12)

it can be seen that

R^{\mu}=-\gamma^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\rho-\partial_{\nu}\gamma^{\mu\nu}+\epsilon^{\mu\nu}(\cosh\sigma-1)\partial_{\nu}\gamma+\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}r\,.

(13)

The natural way to compute the EMT for
\Delta A
is by varying the latter with respect to
\gamma^{\mu\nu}

T_{\mu\nu}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta\Delta A}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}=2\frac{\delta\Delta A}{\delta\gamma^{\mu\nu}}-\gamma_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta\Delta A}{\delta\gamma^{\alpha\beta}}\,.

(14)

To begin, we see that

\delta\Delta A=\frac{1}{2\beta^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}x\,\delta\gamma_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu}R^{\nu}+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}x\,\gamma_{\mu\nu}R^{\nu}\delta R^{\mu}\,,

(15)

where

\delta R^{\mu}=-\delta\gamma^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\sigma-\gamma^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\delta\sigma-\partial_{\nu}\delta\gamma^{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\nu}[\epsilon^{\mu\nu}(\cosh\sigma-1)\delta\gamma]-\bar{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}\delta\gamma^{\alpha\beta}+\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\delta r\,.

(16)

The last two terms are the result of the following computation
[
Deser and Jackiw 1996
]

\delta\quantity[\epsilon^{\mu\nu}(\cosh\sigma- 1)\partial_\nu\gamma]-\partial_{\nu}\quantity[\epsilon^{\mu\nu}(\cosh\sigma- 1)\delta\gamma]=-\bar{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}\delta\gamma^{\alpha\beta}\delta\gamma^{\alpha\beta}\,,

(17)

where

\bar{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\left(\partial_{\alpha}\gamma_{\nu\beta}+\partial_{\beta}\gamma_{\nu\alpha}-\partial_{\nu}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\right)\,.

(18)

Let us now split
\delta\Delta A
into four terms

\delta\Delta A=\delta\Delta A^{1}+\delta\Delta A^{2}+\delta\Delta A^{3}+\delta\Delta A^{4}\,,

(19)

where

\displaystyle\delta\Delta A^{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\beta^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}x\,\delta\gamma_{\mu\nu}R^{\nu}R^{\mu}\,,

\displaystyle\delta\Delta A^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}x\,\gamma_{\mu\nu}R^{\nu}(-\delta\gamma^{\mu\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}\rho-\partial_{\lambda}\delta\gamma^{\mu\lambda}-\bar{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}\delta\gamma^{\alpha\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\beta^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}x\,\delta\gamma^{\alpha\beta}\left[g_{\beta\lambda}\nabla_{\alpha}\left(\frac{R^{\lambda}}{\sqrt{-g}}\right)+g_{\alpha\lambda}\nabla_{\beta}\left(\frac{R^{\lambda}}{\sqrt{-g}}\right)\right]\,,

\displaystyle\delta\Delta A^{3}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}x\,R^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\delta\rho=-\frac{1}{2\beta^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}x\,\sqrt{-g}Rg_{\alpha\beta}\delta g^{\alpha\beta}\,,

\displaystyle\delta\Delta A^{4}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}x\,R^{\mu}\gamma_{\mu\nu}\{\partial_{\lambda}[\epsilon^{\nu\lambda}(\cosh\omega-1)\delta\gamma]+\epsilon^{\nu\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}\delta r\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}x\,\partial_{\lambda}(R^{\mu}\gamma_{\mu\nu})\epsilon^{\nu\lambda}\delta g_{\alpha\beta}[(\cosh\omega-1)\Gamma^{\alpha\beta}+r^{\alpha\beta}]\,.

TO derive the second expression for
\delta\Delta A^{2}
we used

\begin{split}\partial_{\mu}T^{\alpha\ldots}_{\beta\ldots}+\partial_{\mu}\rho T^{\alpha\ldots}_{\beta\ldots}=&~\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{-g}T^{\alpha\ldots}_{\beta\ldots})\,,\\
g_{\alpha\lambda}\nabla_{\beta}V^{\lambda}+g_{\beta\lambda}\nabla_{\alpha}V^{\lambda}=&~g_{\alpha\lambda}\partial_{\beta}V^{\lambda}+g_{\beta\lambda}\partial_{\alpha}V^{\lambda}+V^{\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}g_{\alpha\beta}\,,\\
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\delta\gamma^{\alpha\beta}=&~0\,.\end{split}

To derive
\delta\Delta A^{3}
we used the fact that
R^{\mu}
is a solution of

\sqrt{-g}R=\partial_{\mu}R^{\mu}\,.

Finally, rewriting
\delta\Delta A^{4}
, we defined

\displaystyle\delta\gamma=

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu}\,,

\displaystyle\delta r=

\displaystyle r^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu}\,.

By a direct calculations it follows that

\Gamma^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{g_{--}g_{++}}}\left(\begin{matrix}-\sinh\gamma&\cosh\gamma\\
\cosh\gamma&-\sinh\gamma\end{matrix}\right)\,.

(20)

With this preliminaries, we are now ready for the computation of the EMT in four steps

T^{i}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta\Delta A^{i}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}\,,

with
i=1,2,3,4
, and the results are

\begin{split}T^{1}_{\mu\nu}=&~\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}R^{\alpha}R^{\beta}-\gamma_{\mu\alpha}\gamma_{\nu\beta}R^{\alpha}R^{\beta}\right)\,,\\
T^{2}_{\mu\nu}=&~\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\left(g_{\mu\lambda}\nabla_{\nu}\left(\frac{R^{\lambda}}{\sqrt{-g}}\right)+g_{\nu\lambda}\nabla_{\mu}\left(\frac{R^{\lambda}}{\sqrt{-g}}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}Rg_{\mu\nu}\,,\\
T^{3}_{\mu\nu}=&~-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}Rg_{\mu\nu}\,,\\
T^{4}_{\mu\nu}=&~\frac{2}{\beta^{2}\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\beta}\left(\frac{R^{\lambda}g_{\alpha\lambda}}{\sqrt{-g}}\right)\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}[(\cosh\sigma-1)\Gamma_{\mu\nu}+r_{\mu\nu}]\,.\end{split}

(21)

Adding these together we have

\begin{split}\beta^{2}T^{\mu\nu}_{\text{extra}}=&~\frac{1}{g}\left(R^{\mu}R^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}R\cdot R\right)-2Rg^{\mu\nu}\\
&~+g^{\mu\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\left(\frac{R^{\nu}}{\sqrt{-g}}\right)+g^{\nu\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\left(\frac{R^{\mu}}{\sqrt{-g}}\right)\\
&~+\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\beta}\left(\frac{R^{\lambda}g_{\alpha\lambda}}{\sqrt{-g}}\right)\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}[(\cosh\sigma-1)\Gamma^{\mu\nu}+r^{\mu\nu}]\\
=&~2g^{\mu\nu}W^{\alpha}W^{\beta}g_{\alpha\beta}-4W^{\mu}W^{\nu}-2Rg^{\mu\nu}\\
&~+2g^{\mu\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}W^{\nu}+2g^{\nu\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}W^{\mu}\\
&~+\frac{4}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\beta}\left(W^{\lambda}g_{\alpha\lambda}\right)\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}[(\cosh\sigma-1)\Gamma^{\mu\nu}+r^{\mu\nu}]\,.\end{split}

(22)

Since this improvement term should cancel the trace of the EMT, let us compute its trace

\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}T_{\text{extra}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}=-R+2\frac{\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\beta}\left(W^{\lambda}g_{\alpha\lambda}\right)[(\cosh\sigma-1)\Gamma^{\mu\nu}+r^{\mu\nu}]g_{\mu\nu}\,.

(23)

Recalling that

\Gamma^{\mu\nu}=\frac{2}{(g_{00}+g_{11})^{2}-4g_{01}^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{0}^{\mu}\delta_{1}^{\nu}+\delta_{1}^{\mu}\delta_{0}^{\nu})(g_{00}+g_{11})-g_{01}(\delta_{0}^{\mu}\delta_{0}^{\nu}+\delta_{1}^{\nu}\delta_{1}^{\mu})\right]\,,

we see that

g_{\mu\nu}\Gamma^{\mu\nu}=0\,,

(24)

therefore

T_{\text{extra}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}=-\frac{2}{\beta^{2}}R+\frac{4}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\beta}\left(W^{\lambda}g_{\alpha\lambda}\right)\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}r^{\mu\nu}g_{\mu\nu}\,.

(25)

From here we see another condition for
r

g_{\mu\nu}r^{\mu\nu}=0\,,

(26)

with which

T_{\text{extra}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}=-\frac{2}{\beta^{2}}R\,.

(27)

Hence the trace of the improvement cancels the anomalous trace of the canonical EMT

T^{\mu}_{\mu}=\frac{2}{\beta^{2}}R\,.

This proves the Weyl invariance of the improved Liouville action.

Local Dirac energy decay in the 5D Myers-Perry geometry using an integral spectral representation for the Dirac propagator
Qiu Shi Wang 1

Abstract
We consider the massive Dirac equation in the exterior region of the 5-dimensional Myers-Perry black hole. Using the resulting ODEs obtained from the separation of variables of the Dirac equation, we construct an integral spectral representation for the solution of the Cauchy problem with compactly supported smooth initial data. We then prove that the probability of presence of a Dirac particle to be in any compact region of space decays to zero as t → ∞ t\to\infty , in analogy with the case of the Dirac operator in the Kerr-Newman geometry [ 9 ] .



1
Introduction

Chandrasekhar’s separation of variables for the Dirac equation in the Kerr geometry
[
2
]
provides an avenue for the study of the long-term behaviour of solutions of the massive Dirac equation in 4-dimensional black hole geometries. In
[
9
]
, Finster, Kamran, Smoller and Yau obtain decay estimates for spinor fields in the exterior region of the non-extreme Kerr-Newman geometry using an integral spectral representation that they established for solutions to the Cauchy problem for the Dirac equation. In
[
5
,
4
]
, Daudé studies energy decay and scattering of Dirac spinors in a class of spacetimes that includes the Kerr-Newman geometry. In the latter geometry, Batic
[
1
]
develops a time-dependent scattering theory of Dirac particles using an integral representation for the Dirac propagator.

Similar questions may be asked concerning the Dirac equation in higher-dimensional black hole geometries; the present paper is concerned with the Myers-Perry metrics. Recall that the latter metrics, derived by Myers and Perry
[
12
]
, are the
(d>4)
-dimensional generalizations of the Kerr metric, with rotations along
\left\lfloor{(d-1)/2}\right\rfloor
independent axes. In particular, the 5D Myers-Perry black hole has two independent angular momenta.

The separation of variables for the Dirac equation in the Kerr-Newman geometry can be done equivalently using the Newman-Penrose formalism or the orthonormal local Lorentz frame formalism. In the absence of a suitable generalization of the Newman-Penrose formalism to 5 dimensions, Wu
[
18
]
chooses an orthonormal local Lorentz frame (pentad) to formulate the Dirac equation in the 5D Myers-Perry geometry and separate it into radial and angular ODEs. In the same geometry, Daudé and Kamran
[
6
]
use Wu’s pentad and a “locally non-rotating” pentad that they construct to prove local energy decay for Dirac spinors using Mourre theory, thus generalizing the decay result of
[
9
]
to 5 dimensions. The objective of the present work is to obtain a similar result to that of
[
6
]
, namely the local decay of Dirac spinors in the 5D Myers-Perry geometry, by constructing an integral spectral representation for the solution to the Cauchy problem with compactly supported smooth initial data, in analogy with what was done in the Kerr-Newman geometry in
[
9
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the 5D Myers-Perry metric and the Dirac equation in the orthonormal pentad formalism. In Section
3
, we perform separation of variables on the form of the Dirac equation obtained by
[
18
,
6
]
and derive systems of radial and angular ODEs analogous to those obtained in
[
9
]
for the Kerr-Newman metric. In Section
4
, we construct a suitable Hilbert space structure on the space of Dirac spinors, then derive in Section
5
an integral spectral representation (
72
) for the Dirac propagator (Theorem
1
). The decay of Dirac spinors in any compact region (Theorem
2
) follows from Theorem
1
and the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma.

Throughout the text, we will highlight similarities and differences between the results obtained for the Kerr and 5D Myers-Perry geometries.

2
The 5D Myers-Perry metric and the Dirac equation

In this section, we introduce the 5D Myers-Perry geometry and formulate the Dirac equation on it using the orthonormal pentad formalism.

In Boyer-Lindquist coordinates
(t,r,\theta,\varphi,\psi)
, a 5D Myers-Perry black hole is represented by the manifold

\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}_{t}\times(0,\infty)_{r}\times(0,\frac{\pi}{2})_{\theta}\times(0,2\pi)_{\varphi}\times(0,2\pi)_{\psi}

(1)

equipped with the Lorentzian metric

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+\frac{\Sigma r^{2}}{\Delta}dr^{2}+\Sigma d\theta^{2}+(r^{2}+a^{2})\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2}+(r^{2}+b^{2})\cos^{2}\theta d\psi^{2}+\frac{\mu}{\Sigma}(dt-a\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi-b\cos^{2}\theta d\psi)^{2},

(2)

where
\mu/2
is the mass of the black hole,
a
and
b
its two independent angular momenta, and

\Delta=(r^{2}+a^{2})(r^{2}+b^{2})-\mu r^{2},\qquad\Sigma=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+b^{2}\sin^{2}\theta.

(3)

Furthermore, the 5D Myers-Perry metric has three commuting Killing vector fields
\partial_{t}
,
\partial_{\varphi}
and
\partial_{\psi}
. We restrict our attention to the non-extreme case
\mu>a^{2}+b^{2}+2\lvert ab\rvert
, for which
\Delta
has two distinct positive roots

r_{\pm}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\big(\mu-a^{2}-b^{2}\pm\sqrt{(\mu-a^{2}-b^{2})^{2}-4a^{2}b^{2}}\big).

(4)

The radii
r_{-},r_{+}
are the Cauchy and event horizons respectively. We consider only the exterior region
r>r_{+}
.

We choose the gamma matrices
\gamma^{A}
,
A=0,1,2,3,5
, as

\gamma^{0}=i\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
I&0\end{pmatrix},\gamma^{1}=i\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^{3}\\
-\sigma^{3}&0\end{pmatrix},\gamma^{2}=i\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^{1}\\
-\sigma^{1}&0\end{pmatrix},\gamma^{3}=i\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^{2}\\
-\sigma^{2}&0\end{pmatrix},\gamma^{5}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&-I\end{pmatrix},

(5)

where the
\sigma^{j}
are the Pauli matrices. They satisfy the Clifford algebra anticommutation relations

\{\gamma^{A},\gamma^{B}\}=2\eta^{AB},

(6)

where
\eta^{AB}=\diag\{-1,1,1,1,1\}
is the five-dimensional Minkowski metric in our chosen signature. We define the matrices
\Gamma^{A}
by
\Gamma^{0}=i\gamma^{0}
and
\Gamma^{j}=-\gamma^{0}\gamma^{j}
for
j\neq 0
; they satisfy the anticommutation relations

\{\Gamma^{A},\Gamma^{B}\}=2\delta^{AB},

(7)

where
\delta^{AB}=\diag\{1,1,1,1,1\}
is the Kronecker delta.

The massive Dirac equation takes the form

(\gamma^{A}(\partial_{A}+\Gamma_{A})-m)\phi=0,

(8)

where
\Gamma_{A}
are the components of the spinor connection
\Gamma=\Gamma_{A}e^{A}=\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{A}\gamma^{B}\omega_{AB}
in the orthonormal pentad frame
e^{A}={e^{A}}_{\mu}dx^{\mu}
, and
\omega_{AB}
is the connection 1-form in the same frame.

3
Separation of variables

The Dirac equation on the 5D Myers-Perry metric is put into a separable form
(\mathcal{R}+\mathcal{A})\Psi=0
using a local Lorentz frame in
[
18
,
6
]
. In this section, we summarize this procedure, then perform separation of variables using an ansatz (
18
) analogous to the one used in
[
9
]
. The resulting angular and radial ODEs are similar to the corresponding results in the Kerr geometry
[
9
]
, but differ slightly due to the additional spatial dimension affecting the form of the horizon-defining function
\Delta
and the
\theta
-singularities of the angular ODE.

Using the local Lorentz frame
\partial_{A}={e_{A}}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{0}&=\frac{(r^{2}+a^{2})(r^{2}+b^{2})}{r\sqrt{\Delta\Sigma}}\Big(\partial_{t}+\frac{a}{r^{2}+a^{2}}\partial_{\varphi}+\frac{b}{r^{2}+b^{2}}\partial_{\psi}\Big),\\
\partial_{1}&=\sqrt{\frac{\Delta}{r^{2}\Sigma}}\partial_{r},\\
\partial_{2}&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Sigma}}\partial_{\theta},\\
\partial_{3}&=\frac{\sin\theta\cos\theta}{p\sqrt{\Sigma}}\Big((a^{2}-b^{2})\partial_{t}+\frac{a}{\sin^{2}\theta}\partial_{\varphi}-\frac{b}{\cos^{2}\theta}\partial_{\psi}\Big),\\
\partial_{5}&=\frac{1}{rp}\Big(ab\partial_{t}+b\partial_{\varphi}+a\partial_{\psi}\Big),\end{split}

(9)

where
p^{2}=a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+b^{2}\sin^{2}\theta
, making the substitution

\Psi=\Delta^{1/4}\sqrt{r+ip\gamma^{5}}\phi=\left(\sqrt{\frac{r+\sqrt{\Sigma}}{2}}I+i\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{\Sigma}-r}{2}}\gamma^{5}\right)\phi

(10)

and introducing in the exterior region the radial variable
x
defined by

\frac{dx}{dr}=\frac{(r^{2}+a^{2})(r^{2}+b^{2})}{\Delta},

(11)

one obtains the Hamiltonian form for the Dirac equation

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\Psi

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}\Psi,

(12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle=N\mathcal{D}_{0},

(13)

where, writing
D_{\alpha}=-i\partial_{\alpha}
,

N^{-1}=I+\frac{r\sqrt{\Delta}}{(r^{2}+a^{2})(r^{2}+b^{2})}\Big(\frac{(a^{2}-b^{2})}{p}\sin\theta\cos\theta\Gamma^{3}+\frac{ab}{p}\Gamma^{5}+\frac{ab}{r}\Gamma^{0}\Big),

(14)

\mathcal{D}_{0}=\mathbb{D}_{0}+\frac{\sqrt{\Delta}}{(r^{2}+a^{2})(r^{2}+b^{2})}\left(b\Gamma^{0}D_{\varphi}+a\Gamma^{0}D_{\psi}+\frac{ab}{r}\gamma^{1}+mpr\Gamma^{5}\right),

(15)

\mathbb{D}_{0}=\Gamma^{1}D_{x}+\frac{r\sqrt{\Delta}}{(r^{2}+a^{2})(r^{2}+b^{2})}\mathbb{D}_{S^{3}}-m\frac{r^{2}\sqrt{\Delta}}{(r^{2}+a^{2})(r^{2}+b^{2})}\Gamma^{0}+\frac{a}{r^{2}+a^{2}}D_{\varphi}+\frac{b}{r^{2}+b^{2}}D_{\psi},

(16)

and

\mathbb{D}_{S^{3}}=i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{2}\Big(\partial_{\theta}+\frac{\cot\theta}{2}-\frac{\tan\theta}{2}\Big)+i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{3}\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\varphi}+i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{5}\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\partial_{\psi}.

(17)

It is shown in
[
6
, Appendix A]
that the matrix
N^{-1}
is positive definite.

We use a separation ansatz analogous to the one used in
[
9
]
, but with two spinor angular momentum parameters
k_{a},k_{b}\in\mathbb{N}
; it is of the form

\Psi(t,x,\theta,\varphi,\psi)=e^{-i\omega t}e^{-i((k_{a}+\frac{1}{2})\varphi+(k_{b}+\frac{1}{2})\psi)}\begin{pmatrix}X_{+}(x)Y_{+}(\theta)\\
X_{-}(x)Y_{-}(\theta)\\
X_{-}(x)Y_{+}(\theta)\\
X_{+}(x)Y_{-}(\theta)\end{pmatrix}.

(18)

The Dirac equation can thus be written in the form

(\mathcal{R}+\mathcal{A})\Psi=0,

(19)

where

\mathcal{R}=\frac{(r^{2}+a^{2})(r^{2}+b^{2})}{r\sqrt{\Delta}}\Gamma^{1}D_{x}-mr\Gamma^{0}+\Big(\frac{a(r^{2}+b^{2})}{r\sqrt{\Delta}}+\frac{a}{r}\Gamma^{0}\Big)D_{\varphi}\\
+\Big(\frac{b(r^{2}+a^{2})}{r\sqrt{\Delta}}+\frac{b}{r}\Gamma^{0}\Big)D_{\psi}+\frac{ab}{r^{2}}\gamma^{1}-\omega\Big(\frac{(r^{2}+a^{2})(r^{2}+b^{2})}{r\sqrt{\Delta}}+\frac{ab}{r}\Gamma^{0}\Big)

(20)

and

\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{D}_{S^{3}}+mp\Gamma^{5}-\omega\Big(\frac{(a^{2}-b^{2})\sin\theta\cos\theta}{p}\Gamma^{3}+\frac{ab}{p}\Gamma^{5}\Big)

(21)

are purely radial and angular operators respectively, with
\mathbb{D}_{S^{3}}
as in (
17
).

The eigenvalue relations
D_{\varphi}\Psi=-(k_{a}+\frac{1}{2})\Psi
and
D_{\psi}\Psi=-(k_{b}+\frac{1}{2})\Psi
follow from the ansatz (
18
), under which the Dirac equation (
19
) separates into ODEs
-\mathcal{R}\Psi=\mathcal{A}\Psi=\lambda\gamma^{1}\Psi
for a separation constant
\lambda
. In terms of
X=(X_{+},X_{-})
, the radial ODE is

\left[\frac{d}{dx}+i\Omega(x)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\right]X=\frac{\sqrt{\Delta}}{(r^{2}+a^{2})(r^{2}+b^{2})}\begin{pmatrix}0&-ab/r-\lambda r+iB(x)\\
-ab/r-\lambda r-iB(x)&0\end{pmatrix}X,

(22)

where

\displaystyle\Omega(x)

\displaystyle=\omega+\frac{a(k_{a}+\frac{1}{2})}{r^{2}+a^{2}}+\frac{b(k_{b}+\frac{1}{2})}{r^{2}+b^{2}},

(23)

\displaystyle B(x)

\displaystyle=mr^{2}+a\left(k_{a}+\frac{1}{2}\right)+b\left(k_{b}+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\omega ab,

(24)

recalling that
r
is defined as function of
x
by (
11
). In terms of
Y=(Y_{+},Y_{-})
, the angular ODE is

AY\equiv\begin{pmatrix}C_{b}(\theta)&L_{\theta}+C_{a}(\theta)\\
-L_{\theta}+C_{a}(\theta)&-C_{b}(\theta)\end{pmatrix}Y=\lambda Y,

(25)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}L_{\theta}&=\partial_{\theta}+\frac{\cot\theta}{2}-\frac{\tan\theta}{2},\\
C_{a}(\theta)&=-\frac{(k_{a}+\frac{1}{2})}{\sin\theta}-\frac{\omega(a^{2}-b^{2})\sin\theta\cos\theta}{p},\\
C_{b}(\theta)&=-\frac{(k_{b}+\frac{1}{2})}{\cos\theta}+mp-\frac{\omega ab}{p}.\end{split}

(26)

The radial and angular ODEs resemble closely those obtained from the separation of variables for the Dirac equation in the Kerr metric
[
9
]
, which, in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates
(t,r,\theta,\varphi)
with a change of radial coordinate
du/dr=(r^{2}+a^{2})/\Delta_{\mathrm{Kerr}}
, are of the form

\displaystyle\left[\frac{d}{du}+i\Omega_{\mathrm{Kerr}}(u)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\right]X_{\mathrm{Kerr}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{Kerr}}}{r^{2}+a^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&imr-\lambda\\
-imr-\lambda&0\end{pmatrix}X_{\mathrm{Kerr}},

(27)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}-am\cos\theta&\mathcal{L}_{-}\\
-\mathcal{L}_{+}&am\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}Y_{\mathrm{Kerr}}

\displaystyle=\lambda Y_{\mathrm{Kerr}}.

(28)

where
\Delta_{\mathrm{Kerr}}=r^{2}-\mu r-a^{2}
is the horizon-defining function for the Kerr metric,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{Kerr}}(u)

\displaystyle=\omega+\frac{a(k+\frac{1}{2})}{r^{2}+a^{2}},

(29)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\theta}+\frac{\cot\theta}{2}\mp\left(a\omega\sin\theta+\frac{k+\frac{1}{2}}{\sin\theta}\right),

(30)

and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
the azimuthal quantum number. In the limiting case of a 5D Myers-Perry black hole with zero angular momentum about one of the two independent axes of rotation, say the
\partial_{\psi}
direction so that
b=0
, (
22
) and (
25
) simplify to

\left[\frac{d}{dx}+i\Omega_{\mathrm{Kerr}}(x)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\right]X=\\
\frac{\sqrt{r^{2}+a^{2}-\mu}}{r^{2}+a^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&imr-\lambda+i(a/r)(k_{a}+\frac{1}{2})\\
-imr-\lambda-i(a/r)(k_{a}+\frac{1}{2})&0\end{pmatrix}X,

(31)

\begin{pmatrix}am\cos\theta-\frac{k_{b}+1/2}{\cos\theta}&L_{\theta}-a\omega\sin\theta-\frac{k_{a}+1/2}{\sin\theta}\\
-L_{\theta}-a\omega\sin\theta-\frac{k_{a}+1/2}{\sin\theta}&-am\cos\theta+\frac{k_{b}+1/2}{\cos\theta}\end{pmatrix}Y=\lambda Y.

(32)

The
b=0
radial equation (
31
) differs from the Kerr result (
27
) only by the different
\mu
-dependance and the addition of a
1/r
-damped angular term. Up to some signs, the
b=0
angular equation (
32
) differs from (
28
) only by the addition of
\sim\sec\theta
terms singular at
\theta=\pi/2
arising from the Hopf coordinates
(\theta,\varphi,\psi)
on the
S^{3}
geometry of constant
t,r
hypersurfaces of the 5D Myers-Perry black hole.

4
Hilbert space structure

In this section, we construct suitable scalar products
(\cdot|\cdot)
and
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle
on the Dirac spinors with respect to which
\gamma^{1}\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{D}
are respectively essentially self-adjoint, then describe suitable radial boundary conditions to impose to preserve self-adjointness of the Hamiltonian on a radial interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}_{x}
bounded on one or both sides.

Let
\Sigma_{t}
be a constant-
t
hypersurface
\mathbb{R}_{x}\times S^{3}_{\theta,\varphi,\psi}
. Let
\Psi,\Phi\in L^{2}(\Sigma_{t},d\nu)
, where
d\nu=dxd\Omega
is the product of Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}_{x}
and the usual measure
d\Omega=\sin\theta\cos\theta d\theta d\varphi d\psi
on
S^{3}
. Designating by
\overline{\Psi}
the conjugate transpose spinor (not the adjoint spinor
\Psi^{\dagger}=\overline{\Psi}\gamma^{0}
), we define the scalar product

\big(\Psi|\Phi\big)=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\overline{\Psi}\Phi\>d\nu.

(33)

If we define
Z^{k_{a}k_{b}\omega\lambda}=\exp\left(-i\left((k_{a}+\frac{1}{2})\varphi+(k_{b}+\frac{1}{2})\psi\right)\right)Y^{k_{a}k_{b}\omega\lambda}
and the scalar products

\displaystyle\big(X^{k_{a}k_{b}\omega\lambda}|X^{k_{a}^{\prime}k_{b}^{\prime}\omega^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\overline{X}^{k_{a}k_{b}\omega\lambda}(x)X^{k_{a}^{\prime}k_{b}^{\prime}\omega^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}(x)\>dx,

(34)

\displaystyle\big(Y^{k_{a}k_{b}\omega\lambda}|Y^{k_{a}^{\prime}k_{b}^{\prime}\omega^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{3}}\overline{Z}^{k_{a}k_{b}\omega\lambda}Z^{k_{a}^{\prime}k_{b}^{\prime}\omega^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}\>d\Omega,

(35)

then (
33
) factors in the sense that

\big(\Psi^{k_{a}k_{b}\omega\lambda}|\Psi^{k_{a}^{\prime}k_{b}^{\prime}\omega^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}\big)=\big(X^{k_{a}k_{b}\omega\lambda}|X^{k_{a}^{\prime}k_{b}^{\prime}\omega^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}\big)\big(Y^{k_{a}k_{b}\omega\lambda}|Y^{k_{a}^{\prime}k_{b}^{\prime}\omega^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}\big).

(36)

\mathcal{G}
be the Hilbert space obtained by completion of the space of spinors of the form (
18
) with respect to the scalar product (
33
). We have that
\mathcal{D}_{0}
is essentially self-adjoint on
\mathcal{G}
, but not
\mathcal{D}=N\mathcal{D}_{0}
. To compensate for the factor

\langle\Psi|\Phi\rangle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\overline{\Psi}N^{-1}\Phi\>d\nu,

and let
\mathcal{H}
be the Hilbert space of spinors in

equipped with scalar product (
37
). In order to construct an integral spectral representation for

, it is important that
\mathcal{D}
be essentially self-adjoint with respect to (
37
). This is precisely what was shown in Lemma 19 of
[
6
, Appendix A]
, namely

Lemma 1

(
[

]
)
.

The Hamiltonian
\mathcal{D}
is essentially self-adjoint on
\mathcal{H}

D(\mathcal{D})=\{\psi\in\mathcal{G},\lVert\mathcal{D}_{0}\psi\rVert^{2}<\infty\}.

Note that the space of smooth, compactly supported test spinors

. Another possible method to prove the essential self-adjointness of

The scalar product (

) does not factor into a product. More precisely, we have

\langle\Psi|\Psi^{\prime}\rangle=\big(\Psi|\Psi^{\prime}\big)-(a^{2}-b^{2})\big(X|\frac{r\sqrt{\Delta}}{(r^{2}+a^{2})(r^{2}+b^{2})}\sigma^{2}|X^{\prime}\big)\big(Y|\frac{\sin\theta\cos\theta}{p}\sigma^{1}|Y^{\prime}\big)\\

5
Integral spectral representation and decay estimates

In this section, we use the radial ODE (
22
) and the Hilbert space structure obtained above to obtain an integral representation formula for the Dirac propagator and thus conclude

decay of the probability of the Dirac particle to be in any compact region of space. As the construction follows the Kerr-Newman case closely, we will only describe the ways in which the proofs differ from those in
[
9
]
, and omit or provide sketches of the identical arguments.

First, we construct a suitable basis for
\mathcal{H}_{x_{1},x_{2}}
of eigenvectors of
\mathcal{D}_{x_{1},x_{2}}
. We choose the basis vectors to be eigenvectors of
D_{\varphi}
and

with eigenvalues

and

\mathcal{D}_{x_{1},x_{2}}
to a fixed

\mathcal{D}^{k_{a}k_{b}}_{x_{1},x_{2}}

\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{H}_{x_{1}x_{2}}^{k_{a}k_{b}}
by
\mathcal{A}^{k_{a}k_{b}}
.

Then, we choose them to be solutions of the angular ODE

, or equivalently we require them to be eigensolutions of
\gamma^{1}\mathcal{A}^{k_{a}k_{b}}\Psi=\lambda\Psi
of the form (

as shown in the Appendix, we may write the angular eigenvalues as smooth functions
\lambda_{n}(\omega)

\lambda_{n}<\lambda_{n+1}
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. For fixed

for which the radial equation (

\{\Psi^{k_{a}k_{b}\omega n}_{x_{1},x_{2}}|k_{a},k_{b}\in\mathbb{Z},\omega\in\sigma(\mathcal{D}^{k_{a}k_{b}}_{x_{1},x_{2}}),n\in N(k_{a},k_{b},\omega)\},

(41)

normalized such that
(X^{k_{a}k_{b}\omega n}_{x_{1},x_{2}}|X^{k_{a}k_{b}\omega n}_{x_{1},x_{2}})=1
and
(Y^{k_{a}k_{b}\omega n}|Y^{k_{a}k_{b}\omega n})=1
.

Lemma 2

\displaystyle|f_{0}^{+}|^{2}+|f_{0}^{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle\neq 0,

(43)

\displaystyle\Omega_{0}

\displaystyle=\omega+\frac{a(k_{a}+\frac{1}{2})}{r_{+}^{2}+a^{2}}+\frac{b(k_{b}+\frac{1}{2})}{r_{+}^{2}+b^{2}},

(44)

\displaystyle|R_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq ce^{dx},

Proof.

Writing

6
Perspectives and future work

The decay result of
[
9
]
does not hold in the case of the extreme Kerr geometry, in which the mass of the black hole is equal to the magnitude of its angular momentum and the two horizons coincide. In fact, in the exterior region of such a black hole, Schmid
[
16
]
shows the existence of time-periodic
L^{2}
-normalizable bound state solutions to the Dirac equation. It would be of interest to determine whether
L^{2}
bound state solutions exist in the extreme 5D Myers-Perry geometry.

The Boyer–Lindquist-type coordinates used in the present paper and in
[
9
]
are only well-defined outside the event horizon, as they have metric coefficients that are singular at the event and Cauchy horizons. In
[
15
]
, Röken derives Eddington–Finkelstein-type coordinates on the Kerr black hole, an analytic extension of the Boyer-Lindquist-type coordinates through the horizons. In
[
10
]
, Finster and Röken construct a self-adjoint extension of the Dirac Hamiltonian in a class of spacetimes which include the case of spacetimes containing horizons, such as black holes in Eddington–Finkelstein-type coordinates, where the Dirac Hamiltonian is not elliptic. This allows them to derive in
[
11
]
an integral spectral representation for the Dirac propagator in the Kerr geometry using Stone’s formula. Since the self-adjoint extension of
[
10
]
includes the case of the 5D Myers-Perry geometry in Eddington–Finkelstein-type coordinates, it would be relevant to derive an integral spectral representation for the Dirac propagator through the horizons in 5 dimensions, analogously to what was done in
[
11
]
in the Kerr geometry.

Using the integral spectral representation they obtained in
[
9
]
as main technical tool, Finster, Kamran, Smoller and Yau
[
8
]
derive pointwise decay rates and probability estimates for Dirac spinors in the Kerr-Newman geometry. In particular, they show that for generic initial data, Dirac spinors decay at a rate of
t^{-5/6}
, which is slower than the decay rate of
t^{-3/2}
in Minkowski space. Using the integral representation (
72
) as a starting point, it would be of interest to determine whether analogous results hold in the 5D Myers-Perry geometry.

The results of
[
9
]
and the present work illustrate considerable similarities between the behaviour of Dirac spinors in 4 and 5-dimensional rotating black hole metrics. It is a promising question to ask whether the radial asymptotic behaviour and local decay hold in higher dimensions. However, separation of variables for the Dirac equation in Myers-Perry metrics of dimension
\geq 6
has not yet been done; it thus offers an interesting direction for future work.

\theta=0
. We perform the transformation
\tilde{\alpha}=U\alpha
with

U(\theta,\varphi)=\exp\Big(-i\frac{\varphi}{2}\sigma^{3}\Big)\exp\Big(-i\frac{\theta}{2}\sigma^{2}\Big).

(94)

As in
[
7
, Appendix A]
, the equation
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\tilde{\alpha}=\lambda\tilde{\alpha}
thus obtained can be seen as an eigenvalue problem on
S^{2}
. However, due to the singularities of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
at
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, it is only smooth in some neighbourhood
V\subset S^{2}
of
\theta=0
. In fact
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is first order and elliptic in
V
, and thus by the elliptic regularity theorem
[
17
]
, its eigensolutions
\tilde{\alpha}
are in the Sobolev space
H^{1}(V)\subset L^{2}(V,d\Omega)
. However, choosing
\epsilon>0
such that
V_{\epsilon}=\{(\theta,\varphi)\in S^{2}|\theta<\epsilon\}\subset V
, we notice that for all
l\geq 1
,

\left\|\frac{1}{\theta^{l}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(V,d\Omega)}=\int_{V}\frac{1}{\theta^{2l}}d\Omega\geq\int_{V_{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\theta^{2l}}d\Omega=2\pi\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta^{2l}}d\theta=\infty.

(95)

We can therefore rule out one of the two fundamental solutions (
87
) and thus conclude that the spectrum of
A
is nondegenerate.

As
A=A(\omega)
is an analytic family of operators with nondegenerate spectra, by the Kato-Rellich theorem
[
14
]
its eigenvalues
\lambda(\omega)
and eigenvectors
Y^{k_{a}k_{b}}(\omega)
depend smoothly on
\omega
.

∎

A gap between holographic and quantum mechanical extreme rays of the subadditivity cone Preprint: APS/123-QED
Temple He, Veronika E. Hubeny, Massimiliano Rota

Abstract
We show via explicit construction that for six or more parties, there exist extreme rays of the subadditivity cone that can be realized by quantum states, but not by holographic states. This is a counterexample to a conjecture first formulated in Hernández-Cuenca et al. 2022 , and implies the existence of deep holographic constraints that restrict the allowed patterns of independence among various subsystems beyond the universal quantum mechanical restrictions.

Introduction

A central question in the context of the gauge/gravity duality
Maldacena 1998
;
Gubser et al. 1998
;
Witten 1998

holographic entropy cone
(HEC)

;

;

;

;

;
Hernández Cuenca 2019
;
Czech and Dong 2019
;
He et al. 2019
;
He et al. 2020
;

;
Czech and Wang 2023
.

As the number of parties
{\sf N}
increases, the search for new inequalities quickly becomes computationally unfeasible due to the fact that the combinatorics governing the number of inequalities typically grows doubly exponentially as a function of
{\sf N}

{\sf N}
is immaterial in QFT, since one can always imagine further partitioning the

The HMIP is the restriction of the more general

{\sf N}
-party system and a complete specification of the presence of correlation (or conversely the lack thereof) among the various subsystems, is there a density matrix that satisfies these constraints? This problem can be conveniently formalized using the polyhedral cone in entropy space carved out by all instances of

If this reconstruction is indeed possible, the characterization of the HEC would reduce to the characterization of the set of extreme rays of the SAC that can be realized by geometric states. A natural guess suggested in
Hernández-Cuenca et al. 2022

II
The holographic entropy cone from marginal independence

II.1
Definition of the holographic entropy cone

The HEC was introduced in
Bao et al. 2015
, following the analogous program for arbitrary quantum states
Pippenger 2003
, as a convenient framework to analyze the set of inequalities implied by the Ryu–Takayanagi (RT) formula
Ryu and Takayanagi 2006
. For our purposes, it will be sufficient to view the HEC as the convex cone of entropy vectors which are realized by
graph models of holographic entanglement
, which we now briefly review. For the original definition of the HEC, and for a detailed explanation of how a graph model is related to the RT surfaces that compute the entropies of a collection of boundary spatial subsystems, we refer the reader to
Bao et al. 2015
(see also
Marolf et al. 2017
;
Hubeny et al. 2018
;
Hubeny et al. 2019
for additional subtleties).

An
{\sf N}
-party graph model is a simple weighted graph
G=(V,E)
with positive weights, a specification of a subset
\partial V\subseteq V
of vertices called
boundary vertices
, and a surjective (but not necessarily injective) map
\xi:\partial V\rightarrow[{\sf N}+1]=\{1,2,\ldots,{\sf N}+1\}
, where
1,\ldots,{\sf N}
label the parties and
{\sf N}+1
labels the purifier. Given a graph model, one associates to it an
entropy vector

\vec{\sf S}\in\mathbb{R}^{\sf{D}}
, with
\sf{D}=2^{\sf N}-1
, as follows. For a non-empty subset
\mathscr{I}\subseteq[{\sf N}]
, a cut “homologous to
\mathscr{I}
” (
\mathscr{I}
-cut) is a subset
V_{\mathscr{I}}\subset V
such that
\partial V\cap V_{\mathscr{I}}=\xi^{-1}(\mathscr{I})
, where
\xi^{-1}
denotes the pre-image of
\xi
. The
cost
of any such cut is the sum of the weights of the edges that connect a vertex in
V_{\mathscr{I}}
to one in
V_{\mathscr{I}}^{c}
, the complement of
V_{\mathscr{I}}
in
V
. The entropy
{\sf S}_{\mathscr{I}}
is then defined as the cost of the
\mathscr{I}
-cut with
minimal cost
.

It is straightforward to see that the set of entropy vectors obtained from all such graph models at any fixed
{\sf N}
is a convex cone. This follows from the fact that given any two graph models
G_{1},G_{2}
with entropy vectors
\vec{\sf S}_{1},\vec{\sf S}_{2}
, the conical combination
\vec{\sf S}=\alpha\vec{\sf S}_{1}+\beta\vec{\sf S}_{2}
, with
\alpha,\beta>0
, is realized by the graph
G=\alpha G_{1}\oplus\beta G_{2}
, where
\alpha G_{1}
is a graph model obtained from
G_{1}
by rescaling the weights with the coefficient
\alpha
(and similarly for
\beta G_{2}
), and
\oplus
denotes the disjoint union. As shown in
Bao et al. 2015
, this cone is identical to the HEC, and it is polyhedral for any
{\sf N}
, i.e., it is specified by a finite number of inequalities, or equivalently, by a finite number of extreme rays. It was further shown in
Hayden et al. 2016
that, for any
{\sf N}
, the cone of graph models, or equivalently the HEC, is contained in the QEC
4
4

4

This is not a priori obvious because the original definition of the HEC in
Bao et al. 2015
is a purely geometric one, and it does not assume that the bulk geometry corresponds to a CFT state
Marolf et al. 2017
.
.

II.2
Formalization of the HMIP and its extreme version

An obvious outer bound to the QEC, and therefore also the HEC, is the SAC, i.e., the polyhedral cone carved out by all instances of SA:

{\sf S}_{\underline{\mathscr{I}}}+{\sf S}_{\underline{\mathscr{K}}}-{\sf S}_{\underline{\mathscr{I}}\underline{\mathscr{K}}}\geq 0\quad\forall\,\underline{\mathscr{I}},\underline{\mathscr{K}}\subset[{\sf N}+1],\quad\underline{\mathscr{I}}\cap\underline{\mathscr{K}}=\varnothing,

(1)

where underlined indices indicate subsystems that can include the purifier
{\sf N}+1
(as opposed to
\mathscr{I}\subseteq[{\sf N}]
). Thus, the Araki-Lieb inequality conveniently takes the form of an SA involving the purifier.

Consider now a face
\mathscr{F}
of the SAC and a vector
\vec{\sf S}\in\text{int}(\mathscr{F})
. Notice that the collection of SA instances which are saturated by
\vec{\sf S}
is independent from the specific choice of this vector. Furthermore, the saturation of SA is equivalent to the vanishing of the
mutual information
(MI)
{\sf I}(\underline{\mathscr{I}}:\underline{\mathscr{K}})
, which in quantum mechanics is attained if and only if the density matrix factorizes, i.e., if the subsystems
\underline{\mathscr{I}}
and
\underline{\mathscr{K}}
are independent. We can then interpret the linear subspace spanned by
\mathscr{F}
as corresponding to a specification of which subsystems are independent and which manifest some correlation, while remaining agnostic about the specific values of the entropies. This subspace is in fact a PMI as defined in
Hernández-Cuenca et al. 2022
.

The QMIP and HMIP then ask which PMIs can be realized by quantum states and graph models, respectively. More specifically, they ask which PMIs correspond to faces of the SAC such that there exist in the interior at least one entropy vector realized by a quantum state or, respectively, a graph model. In the following sections we will be interested in the extremal version of these problems, which we denote by EQMIP and EHMIP, and only focus on the 1-dimensional PMIs. These correspond to the 1-dimensional faces of the SAC, i.e., its extreme rays.

II.3
Reconstructing the HEC from the solution to the EHMIP

The intuition that the solution to the HMIP could provide sufficient information for the derivation of the HEC was first suggested in
Hubeny et al. 2018
;
Hubeny et al. 2019
, based on the observation that the PMI of a choice of boundary regions in a geometric state is captured by the connectivity of the RT surfaces, while the specific value of the entropy is immaterial in QFT because it depends on the choice of cut-offs. This intuition was then further developed in
Hernández-Cuenca et al. 2022
, which formulated the following conjecture and checked that it holds for
{\sf N}\leq 5

III
Deriving extreme rays of the SAC which satisfy SSA

As we mentioned,
2

{\sf N}\leq 5

{\sf N}=6

IV
Quantum state realization

To show that (
5

hypergraph models

Bao et al. 2020

Hypergraph models are defined analogously to the graph models presented above, with the only difference being that in addition to edges one also allows for hyperedges connecting three or more vertices. Therefore we only need to clarify under what circumstances the weight of a hyperedge contributes to the cost of an
\mathscr{I}
-cut. As for standard edges, given an
\mathscr{I}
-cut
V_{\mathscr{I}}

h
(thought of as a collection of vertices), the weight of
h
contributes to the cost of the cut if and only if
h
contains at least one vertex in both
V_{\mathscr{I}}
and
V_{\mathscr{I}}^{c}

\mathscr{I}
is then given by the cost of the
\mathscr{I}
-cut with minimal cost.

V
Discussion

We conclude with a few comments about the implications of the failure of
2
for the reconstruction and the physical interpretation of the HEC. If
1
is true, the HEC can be fully reconstructed from the solution to the EHMIP. In that case, if
2
were also true, the HEC would be the largest possible polyhedral cone compatible with this reconstruction procedure and quantum mechanics. One should then question the actual physical meaning of holographic entropy inequalities, since they would just follow from the bound on
{\sf N}
, which is artificial in QFT. The failure of
2
proves that this is not the case, even if the reconstruction procedure of
1
can indeed be achieved. In that case, one should then try to understand what distinguishes the extreme rays of the SAC that can be realized by graph models from the larger set of quantum mechanical ones.

A first step in this direction is the derivation of all extreme rays which are compatible with SSA for
{\sf N}=6

He et al.
. Since this is the smallest value of
{\sf N}
where the solutions to the EQMIP and EHMIP differ, it is a useful testing ground to develop intuition. Next, one should determine which of these rays can be realized in quantum mechanics, and as demonstrated here, the hypergraph construction could be a useful tool in this direction. Ultimately, one may then attempt to use the construction suggested in
Bao et al. 2020
, or the techniques from
Hayden et al. 2016
;
Walter and Witteveen 2021
to explicitly construct the corresponding quantum states and find a new characterization from the perspective of quantum information theory. We leave these questions for future investigation.

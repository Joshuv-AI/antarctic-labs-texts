Parikh Automata on Infinite Words Journal: arXiv
Mario Grobler, Leif Sabellek, Sebastian Siebertz

Abstract
Parikh automata on finite words were first introduced by Klaedtke and Rueß [Automata, Languages and Programming, 2003]. In this paper, we introduce several variants of Parikh automata on infinite words and study their expressiveness. We show that one of our new models is equivalent to synchronous blind counter machines introduced by Fernau and Stiebe [Fundamenta Informaticae, 2008]. All our models admit ε \varepsilon -elimination, which to the best of our knowledge is an open question for blind counter automata. We then study the classical decision problems of the new automata models.



1
Introduction

Parikh automata on finite words (PA), originally introduced by Klaedtke and Rueß in
[
9
]
, are finite automata enriched with counters. A PA is a non-deterministic finite automaton that is additionally equipped with a semi-linear set
C
. Furthermore, every transition is equipped with a
d
-tuple of non-negative integers and every time a transition is used, the counters are incremented by the values in the tuple accordingly. An input word is accepted if the PA ends in a final state and additionally, the resulting
d
-tuple in the counters lies in
C
.
The class of languages recognized by PA contains all regular languages, but also many more, even languages that are not context-free, e. g., the language
\{a^{n}b^{n}c^{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. On the other hand, the language of palindromes is context-free, but cannot be recognized by a PA.

On finite words, Parikh automata have been investigated extensively. As shown in
[
9
]
, the class of languages recognized by PA is captured precisely by weak existential monadic second-order logic of one successor extended with linear cardinality constraints. Cadilhac, Finkel and McKenzie introduced the variant of affine Parikh automata
[
2
]
and used Parikh automata to characterize bounded languages
[
3
]
. Two-way PA were studied in
[
4
]
and PA with a visibly pushdown stack were studied in
[
7
]
.

In this paper, we initiate the research on Parikh automata on infinite words. This research direction was proposed by Klaedtke and Rueß in the conclusion of their work
[
9
]
. Automata on infinite words play an important role in logics and formal verification, see e.g. the textbook
[
11
]
.
For example, a non-terminating system that should distribute resources among three consumers equally could be modelled using the language
\{a^{n}b^{n}c^{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}^{\omega}
, which is not
\omega
-regular and not even
\omega
-context-free. By Büchi’s theorem every regular
\omega
-language can be characterized as the finite union of
U_{i}V_{i}^{\omega}
, where
U_{i},V_{i}
are regular languages.
A natural generalization is the class
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA}}^{\omega}
of languages that can be characterized as the union of
U_{i}V_{i}^{\omega}
, where
U_{i},V_{i}
are Parikh-recognizable languages. This class was also mentioned by Fernau and Stiebe under the name
\mathcal{K}_{*}
in
[
5
]
.

We suggest three different definitions for extending Büchi automata
[
1
]
with a Parikh condition, which we call
Parikh-Büchi-automata
(PBA).
All models are syntactically equal to PA, and they only differ in their semantics.
We consider
PBA with prefix-acceptance condition
(PPBA),
PBA with strong reset-acceptance condition
(SPBA), and
PBA with weak reset-acceptance condition
(WPBA). Furthermore, we consider their variants with
\varepsilon
-transitions and variants where every accepting state is equipped with its own semi-linear set.

A PPBA accepts its input if there is a run that satisfies the acceptance condition for infinitely many prefixes of the word, i. e., it happens infinitely often that the automaton is in an accepting state and the current sum of the vectors lies in the semi-linear set of the automaton.
This model was proposed by Klaedtke and Rueß
[
9
]
. We write
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{Prefix}}
for the class of PPBA-recognizable languages.
We prove that PPBA are equivalent to synchronous blind counter machines introduced by Fernau and Stiebe
[
5
]
.
The main difficulty in this result is to eliminate
\varepsilon
-transitions in PPBAs, which turns out to be surprisingly challenging.
By this equivalence, PPBA-recognizable languages are closed under union, but not under intersection or complement.
Furthermore, if
L
is a Parikh language, then
L^{\omega}
is not necessarily PPBA-recognizable, e. g., the language
\{a^{n}b^{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}^{\omega}
is not PPBA-recognizable.

This last weakness motivates the definition of PBA-acceptance with the ability to reset the values of all counters, which leads to the definitions of SPBA and WPBA.
An SPBA is defined like a PPBA, but the current sum of the vectors
must
lie in the semi-linear set whenever an accepting state is visited.
After the visit of an accepting state all counters are reset to zero. Similarly, a WPBA is not forced to, but
may
reset in an accepting state. Both automata accept if they reset infinitely often. We prove that both models have the same expressiveness and we
write
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{Reset}}
for the class of SPBA/WPBA-recongizable
\omega
-languages.
One of our main results is that PBA with a reset condition are strictly more expressive than PPBA by showing that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA}}^{\omega}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{Reset}}
. Together with the known results for blind counter automata we get

\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{Prefix}}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA}}^{\omega}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{Reset}}.

If
L
is a Parikh language, then
L^{\omega}
is SPBA/WPBA-recognizable, hence, they do not have the weakness of PPBA mentioned above.

Finally, we study the classical decision problems for PPBA and SPBA/WPBA. We show that for all models, emptiness is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete and universality (and hence, inclusion and equivalence) are undecidable.

. In

, we study the decision problems for PPBA and SPBA/WPBA. We conclude in
Section

8
.

Preliminaries

2.1
Finite and infinite words

We write
\mathbb{N}
for the set of non-negative integers including
0
, and
\mathbb{Z}
for the set of all integers. Let
\Sigma
be an alphabet, i. e., a finite non-empty set and let
\Sigma^{*}
be the set of all finite words over
\Sigma
.
For a word
w\in\Sigma^{*}
, we denote by
|w|
the length of
w
, and by
|w|_{a}
the number of occurrences of the letter
a\in\Sigma
in
w
.
We write
\varepsilon
for the empty word of length
0
and
\Sigma^{+}=\Sigma^{*}\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
for the set of all non-empty finite words over
\Sigma
.

An
infinite word
over an alphabet
\Sigma
is a function
\alpha:\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\Sigma
. We often write
\alpha_{i}
instead of
\alpha(i)
.
Thus, we can understand an infinite word as an infinite sequence of symbols
\alpha=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\ldots
For
m\leq n
, we abbreviate the finite infix
\alpha_{m}\ldots\alpha_{n}
by
\alpha[m,n]
.
We denote by
\Sigma^{\omega}
the set of all infinite words over
\Sigma
.
We call a subset
L\subseteq\Sigma^{\omega}
an
\omega
-language
.
Moreover, for
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
, we define
L^{\omega}=\{w_{1}w_{2}\dots\mid w_{i}\in L\setminus\{\varepsilon\}\}\subseteq\Sigma^{\omega}
.

2.2
Regular and
\omega
-regular languages

A
Nondeterministic Finite Automaton
(NFA) is a tuple
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,F)
, where
Q
is the finite set of states,
\Sigma
is the input alphabet,
q_{0}\in Q
is the initial state,
\Delta\subseteq Q\times\Sigma\times Q
is the set of transitions and
F\subseteq Q
is the set of accepting states.
A
run
of
\mathcal{A}
on a word
w=w_{1}\cdots w_{n}\in\Sigma^{*}
is a (possibly empty) sequence of transitions
r=r_{1}\dots r_{n}
with
r_{i}=(p_{i-1},w_{i},p_{i})\in\Delta
such that
p_{0}=q_{0}
.
We say
r
is
accepting
if
p_{n}\in F
. The empty run on
\varepsilon
is accepting if
q_{0}\in F
. We define the
language recognized by
\mathcal{A}

as
L(\mathcal{A})=\{w\in\Sigma^{*}\mid\text{there is an accepting run of $\mathcal{A}$ on $w$}\}
. If a language
L
is recognized by some NFA
\mathcal{A}
, we call
L

regular
.

A
Büchi Automaton
(BA) is an NFA
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,F)
that takes infinite words as input.
A
run
of
\mathcal{A}
on an infinite word
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\dots
is an infinite sequence of transitions
r=r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\dots
with
r_{i}=(p_{i-1},\alpha_{i},p_{i})\in\Delta
such that
p_{0}=q_{0}
.
We say
r
is
accepting
if there are infinitely many
i
such that
p_{i}\in F
.
We define the
\omega
-language recognized by
\mathcal{A}
as
L_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})=\{\alpha\in\Sigma^{\omega}\mid\text{there is an accepting run of $\mathcal{A}$ on $\alpha$}\}
.
If an
\omega
-language
L
is recognized by some BA
\mathcal{A}
, we call
L

\omega
-regular
. Büchi’s theorem establishes an important connection between regular and
\omega
-regular languages:

Theorem 2.1

(Büchi)
.

A language
L\subseteq\Sigma^{\omega}
is
\omega
-regular if and only if there are regular languages
U_{1},V_{1},\dots,U_{n},V_{n}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
for some
n\geq 1
such that
L=U_{1}V_{1}^{\omega}\cup\dots\cup U_{n}V_{n}^{\omega}
.

2.3
Semi-linear sets

A
linear set
of dimension
d
for
d\geq 1
is a set of the form
\{b_{0}+b_{1}z_{1}+\dots+b_{\ell}z_{\ell}\mid z_{1},\dots,z_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
for
b_{0},\ldots,b_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
.
A
semi-linear set
is the finite union of linear sets.
For vectors
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{c})\in\mathbb{N}^{c},\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we denote by
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{c},v_{1},\dots,v_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{c+d}
the
concatenation of
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
.
We extend this definition to sets of vectors. Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{c}
and
V\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Then
U\cdot V=\{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}\mid\mathbf{u}\in U,\mathbf{v}\in V\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{c+d}
.
We denote by
\mathbf{0}^{d}
(or simply
\mathbf{0}
if
d
is clear from the context) the all-zero vector, and by
\mathbf{e}^{d}_{i}
(or simply
\mathbf{e}_{i})
the
d
-dimensional vector where the
i
th entry is
1
and all other entries are 0.

2.4
Parikh-recognizable languages

A
Parikh Automaton
(PA) is a tuple
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,F,C)
where
Q
,
\Sigma
,
q_{0}
, and
F
are defined as for NFA,
\Delta\subseteq Q\times\Sigma\times\mathbb{N}^{d}\times Q
is the set of
labeled transitions
, and
C\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
is a semi-linear set.
We call
d
the
dimension
of
\mathcal{A}
and refer to the entries of a vector
\mathbf{v}
in a transition
(p,a,\mathbf{v},q)
as
counters
.
Similar to NFA, a
run
of
\mathcal{A}
on a word
w=x_{1}\dots x_{n}
is a (possibly empty) sequence of labeled transitions
r=r_{1}\dots r_{n}
with
r_{i}=(p_{i-1},x_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i},p_{i})\in\Delta
such that
p_{0}=q_{0}
. We define the
extended Parikh image
of a run
r
as
\rho(r)=\sum_{i\leq n}\mathbf{v}_{i}
(with the convention that the empty sum equals
\mathbf{0}
).
We say
r
is accepting if
p_{n}\in F
and
\rho(r)\in C
,
referring to the latter condition as the
Parikh condition
.
We define the
language recognized by
\mathcal{A}

as
L(\mathcal{A})=\{w\in\Sigma^{*}\mid\text{there is an accepting run of $\mathcal{A}$ on $w$}\}
.
If a language
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
is recognized by some PA, then we call
L

Parikh-recognizable
. We write
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA}}
to denote the class of all Parikh-recognizable languages.

3
Parikh automata on infinite words

In this section we introduce our models of Parikh automata on infinite words and make some simple observations.

3.1
Definitions of Parikh-Büchi Automata

We begin with the definition of Parikh automata on infinite words.

Definition 3.1
.

A
Parikh-Büchi Automaton
(PBA) is a PA
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,F,C)
. A run of
\mathcal{A}
on an infinite word
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\dots
is an infinite sequence of labeled transitions
r=r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\dots
with
r_{i}=(p_{i-1},\alpha_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i},p_{i})\in\Delta
such that
p_{0}=q_{0}
.
We consider three acceptance conditions.

1.

PBA with prefix-acceptance condition
(PPBA): We say that
r

satisfies the prefix-acceptance condition
(
is accepting
for short) if there are infinitely many
i\geq 1
such that
p_{i}\in F
and
\rho(r_{1}\dots r_{i})\in C
. For these positions
i
we say that there is an
accepting hit in
r_{i}
. We define the
\omega
-language recognized by a PPBA
\mathcal{A}
as
P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})=\{\alpha\in\Sigma^{\omega}\mid\mathcal{A}\text{ accepts }\alpha\}
.

2.

PBA with weak reset-acceptance condition
(WPBA): We say that
r

satisfies the weak reset-acceptance condition
(
is accepting
for short) if there are infinitely many
reset positions

0=k_{0}<k_{1}<k_{2}\dots
such that
p_{k_{i}}\in F
and
\rho(r_{k_{i-1}+1}\dots r_{k_{i}})\in C
for all
i\geq 1
. We define the
\omega
-language recognized by a WPBA
\mathcal{A}
as
W_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})=\{\alpha\in\Sigma^{\omega}\mid\mathcal{A}\text{ accepts }\alpha\}
.

3.

PBA with strong reset-acceptance condition
(SPBA): Let
k_{0}=0
and denote by
k_{1},k_{2},\dots
the positions of all accepting states in
r
, i. e.,
p_{k_{i}}\in F
for all
i\geq 1
. We say that
r

satisfies the strong reset-acceptance condition
(
is accepting
for short) if
k_{1},k_{2},\dots
is an infinite sequence and
\rho(r_{k_{i-1}+1}\dots r_{k_{i}})\in C
for all
i\geq 1
. We define the
\omega
-language recognized by an SPBA
\mathcal{A}
as
S_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})=\{\alpha\in\Sigma^{\omega}\mid\mathcal{A}\text{ accepts }\alpha\}
.

Intuitively, an SPBA
\mathcal{A}
accepts all infinite words
\alpha
in such a way, that whenever a run of
\mathcal{A}
on
\alpha
visits an accepting state, the Parikh condition
must
be satisfied. After that, the counters are reset.
Compared to that, a WPBA
may
reset the counters when visiting an accepting state but does not have to do so.
Both accept if they reset their counters infinitely often. A PPBA does not reset its counters at all. It accepts if there exists a run that has infinitely many accepting (finite) prefixes that satisfy the Parikh condition.
Since all automata are syntactically equal objects we write
P_{\omega}
,
W_{\omega}
and
S_{\omega}
for the accepted
\omega
-languages to make the different acceptance conditions explicit. For the same reason we will often speak about resetting states instead of accepting states in the context of SPBA/WPBA.

Example 1
.

q_{0}
q_{1}
a,\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}
b,\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}
b,\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}
a,\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}

Figure 1:
The PBA
\mathcal{A}
with
C=\{(z,z)\mid z\in\mathbb{N}\})
from
Example

.

Let
\mathcal{A}
with
C=\{(z,z)\mid z\in\mathbb{N}\}
be the PBA depicted in Figure
1
. Then
W_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})=\{w\in\{a,b\}^{*}\cdot\{b\}\mid|w|_{a}=|w|_{b}\}^{\omega}
.
Note that this language is not
\omega
-regular. While the first counter tracks the number of already read
a
s, the second counter tracks the number of already read
b
s.
The semi-linear set
C
essentially states that both values need to be the same (eventually).
Since a WPBA may or may not reset its counters when visiting an accepting state,
\mathcal{A}
can "move freely" between both of its states. Note that
P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})=W_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})
.

Considering the strong reset-acceptance condition, we have
S_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})=\{ab\}^{\omega}
, only a single infinite word is accepted by this automaton. Assume
\mathcal{A}
tries to accept an infinite word
\alpha
that has an infix
a^{n}
where
n>1
.
That is,
\mathcal{A}
loops
n
times in
q_{0}
, hence the first counter has a value of
n
and the second counter a value of 0.
The only chance to increase the second counter is by moving into the accepting state
q_{1}
.
Since both counters do not have the same values by then,
\alpha
will be rejected.
If
\mathcal{A}
tries to accept an infinite word that starts with a
b
, or has an infix
b^{n}
where
n>1
, the input will be rejected for similar reasons.

We now introduce PBAs that allow different semi-linear sets on all accepting states, which we call Multi-PBA.

Definition 3.2
.

A
Multi-PBA
is a tuple
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,F,C)
where
C:F\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}^{d})
is a function that assigns a semi-linear set to each
q\in F
.
We define Multi-PPBA (MPBA), MWPBA and MSPBA as SPBA, WPBA and SPBA, respectively, where the condition
\rho(r_{i},\ldots,r_{j})\in C
in the respective definitions is replaced by
\rho(r_{i},\ldots,r_{j})\in C(p_{j})
.

We finally introduce definitions of PBA that allow
\varepsilon
-transitions.

Definition 3.3
.

An
\varepsilon
-PBA is a tuple
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,\mathcal{E},F,C)
where
\mathcal{E}\subseteq Q\times\{\varepsilon\}\times\mathbb{N}^{d}\times Q
is the set of
labeled
\varepsilon
-transitions
, and all other entries are defined as for PBA.
A run of
\mathcal{A}
on an infinite word
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\dots
is an infinite sequence of transitions
r\in(\mathcal{E}^{*}\Delta)^{\omega}
, say
r=r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\dots
with
r_{i}=(p_{i-1},\gamma_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i},p_{i})
such that
p_{0}=q_{0}
, and
\gamma_{i}=\varepsilon
if
r_{i}\in\mathcal{E}
, and
\gamma_{i}=\alpha_{j}
if
r_{i}\in\Delta
is the
j
-th occurrence of a (non-
\varepsilon
) transition in
r
.
The definitions of prefix-acceptance condition, weak reset-acceptance condition and strong reset-condition are the same as in Definition
3.1
and extend to Multi-PBA in the natural way.
We use the terms
\varepsilon
-PPBA,
\varepsilon
-WPBA,
\varepsilon
-SPBA,
\varepsilon
-MPPBA,
\varepsilon
-MWPBA, and
\varepsilon
-MSPBA, respectively. We call a transition of the form
(p,\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},p)\in\mathcal{E}
an
\varepsilon
-loop
.

Note that we can treat every PBA as an
\varepsilon
-PBA, that is, a PBA
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,F,C)
is equivalent to the
\varepsilon
-PBA
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,\varnothing,F,C)
in the sense that
P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})=P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
,
W_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})=W_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
, and
S_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})=S_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
.

3.2
Simple Observations.

We begin with a few simple observations. We first show that the accepting states
F
and the linear sets of
C
of an
\varepsilon
-PPBA
\mathcal{A}
are in a sense independent, as formalized by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,\mathcal{E},F,C)
be an
\varepsilon
-PPBA, where
C=C_{1}\cup\dots\cup C_{\ell}
for linear sets
C_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq\ell
. Then
P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})=\bigcup_{f\in F}\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq\ell}P_{\omega}(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,\mathcal{E},\{f\},C_{j})
.

Proof.

The right-to-left direction is obvious, thus we show the left-to-right direction.
Let
\alpha\in P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})
with an accepting run
r=r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\dots
where
r_{i}=(p_{i-1},\gamma_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i},p_{i})
, i. e., there are infinitely many
i\geq 1
such that
p_{i}\in F
and
\rho(r_{1}\dots r_{i})\in C
.
By the infinite pigeonhole principle, there is a state
f\in F
such that
p_{i^{\prime}}=f
and
j
such that
\rho(r_{1}\dots r_{i^{\prime}})\in C_{j}
for infinitely many
i^{\prime}\geq 1
.
Thus,
\alpha\in P_{\omega}(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,\mathcal{E},\{f\},C_{j})
.
∎

Note that this lemma does in general not hold for
\varepsilon
-WPBA and
\varepsilon
-SPBA.

The following lemma illustrates the simple and yet important combinatorial method to use additional counters and an adapted semi-linear set to store information about runs.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
\varepsilon
-MPPBA
\mathcal{A}
there exists an
\varepsilon
-PPBA
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
with a single accepting state such that
P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})=P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
. If
\mathcal{A}
has no non-loop
\varepsilon
-transitions, then
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
has no non-loop
\varepsilon
-transitions and if
\mathcal{A}
is an MPPBA, then
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is a PPBA.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,\mathcal{E},F,C)
be an
\varepsilon
-MPPBA of dimension
d
and let
F=\{q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\}
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is an
\varepsilon
-MPPBA,
C\colon F\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}^{d})
is a function assigning a semi-linear set to each
q\in F
.
We construct an
\varepsilon
-PPBA
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
of dimension
d+k
with a single accepting state.
We introduce a new accepting state
q_{f}
.
A naive approach would be to connect all
q_{i}
via
\varepsilon
-transitions to
q_{f}
and vice versa. However, this approach fails, as
q_{f}
has no information from which of the accepting states a run enters and thus does not know which outgoing transitions are valid such that no invalid shortcuts are created. Hence, we use non-determinism to guess one accepting state (say
q_{i}
) that is visited infinitely often to satisfy the prefix-acceptance condition. Additionally, we introduce one new counter per accepting state to ensure that we only use transitions related to
q_{i}
.

Formally, let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=(Q\cup\{q_{f}\},\Sigma,\Delta^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\{q_{f}\},C)
where

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\ \{(q,a,\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{0}^{k},q^{\prime})\mid(q,a,\mathbf{v},q^{\prime})\in\Delta\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(q,a,\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{i}^{k},q_{f})\mid(q,a,\mathbf{v},q_{i})\in\Delta,i\leq k\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(q_{f},a,\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{i}^{k},q)\mid(q_{i},a,\mathbf{v},q)\in\Delta,i\leq k\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(q_{f},a,\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{i}^{k},q_{f})\mid(q_{i},a,\mathbf{v},q_{i})\in\Delta,i\leq k\}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\ \{(q,\varepsilon,\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{0}^{k},q^{\prime})\mid(q,\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},q^{\prime})\in\mathcal{E}\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(q,\varepsilon,\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{i}^{k},q_{f})\mid(q,\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},q_{i})\in\mathcal{E},i\leq k,q\neq q_{i}\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(q_{f},\varepsilon,\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{i}^{k},q)\mid(q_{i},\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},q)\in\mathcal{E},i\leq k,q\neq q_{i}\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(q_{f},\varepsilon,\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{i}^{k},q_{f})\mid(q_{i},\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},q_{i})\in\mathcal{E},i\leq k\}.

Define
C_{i}=C(q_{i})\cdot\{z\mathbf{e}_{i}^{k}\mid z\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
C=\bigcup_{i\leq k}C_{i}
. We claim that

.

To show
P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})\subseteq P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
, let
\alpha\in P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})
with accepting run

of
\mathcal{A}
, where
r_{j}=(p_{j-1},\gamma_{j},\mathbf{v}_{j},p_{j})
.
By the infinite pigeonhole principle, there exists an accepting state
q_{i}
satisfying the prefix-acceptance condition infinitely often, i. e., there are infinitely many

such that
p_{j}=q_{i}
and
\rho(r_{1}\dots r_{j})\in C(q_{i})
.
From

we construct an accepting run

of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
by replacing every transition after reading
\alpha_{1}
of the form
(p_{j-1},\gamma_{j},\mathbf{v}_{j},q_{i})

(p_{j-1},\gamma_{j},\mathbf{v}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{e}^{k}_{i},q_{f})
, every
(q_{i},\gamma_{j},\mathbf{v}_{j},q_{i})

(q_{f},\gamma_{j},\mathbf{v}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{e}^{k}_{i},q_{f})
, and every
(q_{i},\gamma_{j},\mathbf{v}_{j},p_{j})

(q_{f},\gamma_{j},\mathbf{v}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{e}^{k}_{i},p_{j})
.
Finally, every other transition
(p_{j-1},\gamma_{j},\mathbf{v}_{j},p_{j})
is replaced by
(p_{j-1},\gamma_{j},\mathbf{v}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{0}^{k},p_{j})
.
By the choice of
\Delta^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
the run
r^{\prime}
is indeed a run of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
on
\alpha
.
Furthermore,
r^{\prime}
is accepting, as every accepting hit in
q_{i}
in

after reading
\alpha_{1}
(before reading the first symbol of
\alpha
it might be the case that we could not shortcut into

) translates into an accepting hit in

: the first

-th component of the appended

-dimensional vector in

.
Thus, we have
\rho(r^{\prime}_{1}\dots r^{\prime}_{j})\in C_{i}\subseteq C
whenever we have
\rho(r_{1}\dots r_{j})\in C(q_{i})
.

To show
P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})\supseteq P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
, let
\alpha\in P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
with accepting run
r^{\prime}=r^{\prime}_{1}r^{\prime}_{2}r^{\prime}_{3}\dots
of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
where
r^{\prime}_{j}=(p_{j-1},\gamma_{j},\mathbf{v}_{j},p_{j})
.
By construction of
C
there is a unique
C_{i}
such that
\rho(r^{\prime}_{1}\dots r^{\prime}_{j})\in C_{i}
for infinitely many
j
.
Similar as above, we can replace every occurrence of
q_{f}
in
r^{\prime}
by
q_{i}
and forget the additional counters to obtain a valid run
r
of
\mathcal{A}
on
\alpha
, which is accepting as every accepting hit on
q_{f}
in
r^{\prime}
then corresponds to an accepting hit on
q_{i}
in
r
.

Finally, observe that
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
has only non-loop
\varepsilon
-transitions if
\mathcal{A}
has only non-loop
\varepsilon
-transitions
, as in this case the first and fourth line of the definition of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
introduce only
\varepsilon
-loops and the sets defined in the second and third line are empty. If
\mathcal{A}
was a MPPBA, we may consider it as an
\varepsilon
-MPPBA with
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\varnothing
, hence,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
can be seen as a PPBA in this case.
∎

Observe that we can use a similar trick to convert (
\varepsilon
-)MSPBA and (
\varepsilon
-)MWPBA into equivalent (
\varepsilon
-)SPBA and (
\varepsilon
-)WPBA. However, in general a single accepting state is not sufficient anymore.

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
\varepsilon
-MSPBA (
\varepsilon
-MWPBA)
\mathcal{A}
there exists an
\varepsilon
-SPBA (
\varepsilon
-WPBA)
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
such that
S_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})=S_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
(
W_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})=W_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
). If
\mathcal{A}
has no non-loop
\varepsilon
-transitions, then
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
has no non-loop
\varepsilon
-transitions and if
\mathcal{A}
is an MSPBA (MWPBA), then
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is an SPBA (WPBA).

Proof sketch.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,\mathcal{E},F,C)
be an (
\varepsilon
-)MSPBA of dimension
d
and let
F=\{q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\}
. Again,
C\colon F\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}^{d})
is a function assigning a semi-linear set to each
q\in F
.
We construct an equivalent
(\varepsilon
-)SPBA
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
of dimension
d+k
with a single semi-linear set
C^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d+k}
.

The idea is to use the additional counters to mark into which resetting state we enter, that is, every transition that does not lead to a resetting state is simply padded with
0
, while every transition entering the resetting state
q_{i}
has a
1
exactly at the
d+i
th position. The semi-linear set
C^{\prime}
is defined as
\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k}C(q_{i})\cdot\{\mathbf{e}^{d}_{i}\}
.

When
\mathcal{A}
is an
(\varepsilon)
-MWPBA, then additionally, for every transition entering a resetting state we add the transition padded with
0
. Then, the resulting automaton can non-deterministically decide if it wants to reset or not.

Finally, observe that the resulting automaton
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
has only non-loop
\varepsilon
-transitions if
\mathcal{A}
has only non-loop
\varepsilon
-transitions
. If
\mathcal{A}
was a MPPBA, we may consider it as an
\varepsilon
-MPPBA with
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\varnothing
, hence,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
can be seen as a PPBA in this case.
∎

3.3
Equivalence of
\varepsilon
-WPBA and
\varepsilon
-SPBA

We now prove that the reset models
\varepsilon
-SPBA and
\varepsilon
-WPBA define the same class of
\omega
-languages. Furthermore, we can efficiently convert an
\varepsilon
-SPBA into an
\varepsilon
-WPBA and vice versa.

Lemma 3.4
.

Every
\varepsilon
-SPBA
\mathcal{A}
is equivalent to an
\varepsilon
-WPBA
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
that has the same number of states and uses one additional counter. If
\mathcal{A}
is an SPBA, then
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is a WPBA.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,\mathcal{E},F,C)
be an
\varepsilon
-SPBA. We construct an equivalent
\varepsilon
-WPBA
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
that simulates
\mathcal{A}
, ensuring that no run visits an accepting state without resetting.
To achieve that, we add an additional counter that tracks the number of visits of an accepting state (without resetting).
Moreover, we modify
C
such that this new counter must be set to 1 when visiting an accepting state, thus disallowing to pass such a state without resetting.

We choose
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta^{\prime},
\mathcal{E}
’
F,C^{\prime})
where

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\ \{(q,x,\mathbf{v}\cdot 0,q^{\prime})\mid(q,x,\mathbf{v},q^{\prime})\in\Delta,q^{\prime}\notin F\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(q,x,\mathbf{v}\cdot 1,q_{f})\mid(q,x,\mathbf{v},q_{f})\in\Delta,q_{f}\in F\},

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\ \{(q,\varepsilon,\mathbf{v}\cdot 0,q^{\prime})\mid(q,\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},q^{\prime})\in\mathcal{E},q^{\prime}\notin F\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(q,\varepsilon,\mathbf{v}\cdot 1,q_{f})\mid(q,\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},q_{f})\in\mathcal{E},q_{f}\in F\}.

Finally, let
C^{\prime}=C\cdot\{1\}
. We claim that
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is an
\varepsilon
-WPBA equivalent to
\mathcal{A}
.

Let
\alpha\in S_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})
. Since
\mathcal{A}
resets every time when visiting an accepting state,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
can simulate an accepting run using the same states and reset positions.
In particular, the new counter will be 1 on every visit of an accepting state.
Thus, the choice of
C^{\prime}
implies that
\alpha\in W_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
.

Now, let
\alpha\in W_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
. Due to the choice of
C^{\prime}
, it is indispensable for
\mathcal{A}
to reset the counters every time an accepting state is visited.
Otherwise, the new counter tracking the number of visits of accepting states would be greater than 1, thus violating the weak reset-acceptance condition.
Hence
\alpha\in S_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Every
\varepsilon
-WPBA
\mathcal{A}
is equivalent to an
\varepsilon
-SPBA
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
with at most twice the number of states and the same number of counters. If
\mathcal{A}
is an SPBA, then
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is a WPBA.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,\mathcal{E},F,C)
be an
\varepsilon
-WPBA
. We construct an equivalent
\varepsilon
-SPBA
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
that simulates
\mathcal{A}
by having the option to “avoid" accepting states arbitrarily long. For this purpose, we create a non-accepting copy of
F
. Consequently,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
can decide to continue or reset a partial run using non-determinism.

We choose
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=(Q\cup\{\hat{q}_{f}\mid q_{f}\in F\},\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},F,C)
, where

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta\ \cup

\displaystyle\ \{(q,x,\mathbf{v},\hat{q}_{f})\mid(q,x,\mathbf{v},q_{f})\in\Delta,q_{f}\in F\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(\hat{q}_{f},x,\mathbf{v},q)\mid(q_{f},x,\mathbf{v},q)\in\Delta,q_{f}\in F\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(\hat{q}_{f},x,\mathbf{v},\hat{q}_{f}^{\prime})\mid(q_{f},x,\mathbf{v},q_{f}^{\prime})\in\Delta,q_{f},q_{f}^{\prime}\in F\},

and similarly,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\mathcal{E}\ \cup

\displaystyle\ \{(q,\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},\hat{q}_{f})\mid(q,\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},q_{f})\in\mathcal{E},q_{f}\in F\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(\hat{q}_{f},\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},q)\mid(q_{f},\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},q)\in\mathcal{E},q_{f}\in F\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(\hat{q}_{f},\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},\hat{q}_{f}^{\prime})\mid(q_{f},\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},q_{f}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{E},q_{f},q_{f}^{\prime}\in F\}.

We claim that
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is an
\varepsilon
-SPBA equivalent to
\mathcal{A}
.

\Rightarrow
We first show
W_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})\subseteq S_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
. Let
\alpha\in W_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})
and
r=r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\dots
be an accepting run of
\mathcal{A}
on
\alpha
with reset positions
k_{0}<k_{1}<k_{2}\dots
. Now
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is able to simulate
r
by choosing the state
\hat{q}_{i}
for every state
q_{i}\in F
where
i\neq k_{j},j\geq 1
. Hence,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
visits an accepting state if and only if
\mathcal{A}
resets its counters at the same position. Thus,
\alpha\in S_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
.

\Leftarrow
To see that
S_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\subseteq W_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})
, let
\alpha\in S_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
and
r^{\prime}=r_{1}^{\prime}r_{2}^{\prime}r_{3}^{\prime}\dots
, with
r_{i}^{\prime}=(p_{i-1}^{\prime},\gamma_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i},p_{i}^{\prime})
, where
p_{i}^{\prime}\in\{p_{i},\hat{p}_{i}\}
for all

, be a run of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
on
\alpha
satisfying the strong reset-acceptance condition.
Then
r=r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\dots
with
r_{i}=(p_{i-1},\gamma_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i},p_{i})
is a run of
\mathcal{A}
on
\alpha
.
Furthermore,
r
satisfies the weak reset-acceptance condition: let
k_{1},k_{2},\dots
denote all positions in
r^{\prime}
where
p^{\prime}_{f_{i}}
is accepting. In particular,
k_{1},k_{2},\dots
are an infinite number of (possible) reset positions, thus satisfying the weak reset-acceptance condition. Therefore,
\alpha\in W_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})
.
∎

As a result, we call an
\omega
-language
L

Reset-recognizable
if there is an SPBA
\mathcal{A}
such that

. We write
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{Reset}}
to denote the class of all Reset-recognizable languages. Similarly, we call
L

Prefix-recognizable
if there is a PPBA
\mathcal{A}
, such that

and denote the class of Prefix-recognizable languages by

.

4
Schnepsilon-elimination

4.1
\varepsilon
-elimination for PPBA

We now show that
\varepsilon
-transitions in
\varepsilon
-PPBA can be eliminated, that is, every
\varepsilon
-PPBA
\mathcal{A}
is equivalent to a PPBA
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.
We proceed in two steps. In the first step, we show that we can convert every
\varepsilon
-PPBA into an equivalent
\varepsilon
-PPBA where all occurring
\varepsilon
-transitions are
\varepsilon
-loops.
In the second step we show how to remove all
\varepsilon
-loops, thus obtaining a PPBA without
\varepsilon
-transitions.

Let
\Sigma=\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k}\}
. The
Parikh image
of a (finite) word
w\in\Sigma^{*}
is the vector
p(w)=(|w|_{\sigma_{1}},\dots,|w|_{\sigma_{k}})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
. The definition extends to languages
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
in the natural way:
p(L)=\{p(w)\mid w\in L\}
.
An important ingredient of our proof is (the first statement of) Parikh’s Theorem
[
10
]
, stating the following.

Theorem 4.1

(Parikh)
.

For every regular language
L
the set
p(L)
is semi-linear.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{k}
be finite. For a word
w=w_{1}\ldots w_{\ell}\in\Gamma^{*}
we write
\sum w
for
\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}w_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
.
For a language
L\subseteq\Gamma^{*}
we define
\sum L=\{\sum w\mid w\in L\}
. Let
\Sigma=\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}\}
be an alphabet.
Let
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}=\{\mathbf{e}^{k}_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq k\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{k}
be the set of
k
-dimensional unit vectors.
Then Parikh’s Theorem implies that for every regular language
L
over
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
the set
\sum L
is semi-linear.
Klaedtke and Ruess showed that this statement is true for arbitrary finite alphabets
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{k}
.

Lemma 4.1

(Klaedkte, Ruess, Lemma 5 of
[
9
]
, rephrased)
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{k}
be a finite alphabet. Then for every regular language
L
over
\Gamma
the set
\sum L
is semi-linear.

For an
\varepsilon
-PPBA
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,\mathcal{E},F,C)
we define
\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}
to be the defined as
\mathcal{A}
where all non-
\varepsilon
-transitions are removed, that is,
\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\varnothing,\mathcal{E},F,C)
.
Furthermore, for
p,q\in Q
let
\mathcal{A}_{p,q}
be defined as
\mathcal{A}
with initial state
p
and a single accepting state
q
, i. e.,
\mathcal{A}_{p,q}=(Q,\Sigma,p,\Delta,\mathcal{E},\{q\},C)
. Thus
\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}_{p,q}=(Q,\Sigma,p,\varnothing,\mathcal{E},\{q\},C)
.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{p,q}
the NFA over the alphabet
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{k}
obtained from
\mathcal{A}_{p,q}^{\varepsilon}
by replacing every labeled
\varepsilon
-transition with a transition where only the vector remains, that is,
\mathcal{B}_{p,q}=(Q,\Gamma,\{p\},\Delta^{\prime},\{q\})
where
\Delta^{\prime}=\{(q_{1},\mathbf{v},q_{2})\mid(q_{1},\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},q_{2})\in\mathcal{E}\}
.
Then, the following is immediate from
Lemma

4.1
.

Corollary 4.1
.

For every
\varepsilon
-PPBA
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,\mathcal{E},F,C)
and all
p,q\in Q
, the set
\sum L(\mathcal{B}_{p,q})
is semi-linear.

We first show that we may assume that the initial state has no non-loop
\varepsilon
-transitions.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every
\varepsilon
-PPBA
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,\mathcal{E},F,C)
there is an equivalent
\varepsilon
-PPBA
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=(Q\cup\{q_{0}^{\prime}\},\Sigma,q_{0}^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},F,C^{\prime})
such that all outgoing
\varepsilon
-transitions of
q_{0}^{\prime}
are
\varepsilon
-loops.

Proof.

We introduce a fresh initial state
q_{0}^{\prime}
.
The idea of the proof is as follows. For every
q\in Q
reachable from
q_{0}
by a sequence of
\varepsilon
-transitions followed by a single transition labeled with a symbol
a
, we introduce a shortcut labeled with
a
. To account for the missing
\varepsilon
-transitions we introduce
\varepsilon
-loops on
q_{0}^{\prime}
, so that the run up to
q
can be simulated by the
\varepsilon
-transitions on
q_{0}^{\prime}
followed by the

-shortcut to
q
. The labels of the new
\varepsilon
-transitions on
q_{0}^{\prime}
are given by the automata
\mathcal{B}_{q_{0},q}
. Furthermore, we will use additional counters to make sure that we do not mix the transitions for different states
q,q^{\prime}
. We come to the formal details.

For every state
q\in Q
we consider the set
\sum L(\mathcal{B}_{q_{0},q})
, which is semi-linear by
Corollary

4.1
.
Note that
\sum L(\mathcal{B}_{q_{0},q})
is empty if
q
is not reachable from
q_{0}
via
\varepsilon
-transitions.
Otherwise
\sum L(\mathcal{B}_{q_{0},q})
can be written as the finite union of linear sets, say
C^{(q)}_{1}\cup\dots\cup C^{(q)}_{\ell_{q}}
where
C^{(q)}_{i}=\{b_{i,0}^{(q)}+b_{i,1}^{(q)}z_{1}+\dots+b^{(q)}_{i,k_{i,q}}z_{k_{i,q}}\mid z_{1},\dots,z_{k_{i,q}}\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
We denote by
k_{i,q}
the number of period vectors of
C^{(q)}_{i}
, by
\ell_{q}
the number of linear sets of the semi-linear set
\sum L(\mathcal{B}_{q_{0},q})
, and define
M=\sum_{q\in Q}\ell_{q}
.
We fix an arbitrary bijection
\pi
between the
C^{(q)}_{i}
and
\{1,\dots,M\}
.

We now introduce the new shortcuts and
\varepsilon
-loops on
q_{0}^{\prime}
. Informally, whenever a run of
\mathcal{A}
starts with a sequence of
\varepsilon
-transitions
r_{1}\dots r_{m}r_{m+1}
, where
r_{1}\dots r_{m}\in\mathcal{E}^{*}
and

, the automaton
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
guesses the last state

of the sequence of
\varepsilon
-transitions and take a shortcut from

to
p_{m+1}
.
As
\rho(r_{1}\dots r_{m})\in C^{(p_{m})}_{i}
for some

, the automaton
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
also guesses the set
C^{(p_{m})}_{i}
and use the
\varepsilon
-loops on
q_{0}^{\prime}
labeled with the period vectors of the
C^{(q)}_{i}
concatenated with a
0
-

-vector of dimension

with a single

These loops replace all sequences of
\varepsilon
-transitions leaving
q_{0}
and the new counters ensure that we do not mix the period vectors of different linear sets.

Formally, we define
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
as follows.

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\ \{(p,a,\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{0}^{M},q)\mid(p,a,\mathbf{v},q)\in\Delta\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(q_{0}^{\prime},a,\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{0}^{M},q)\mid(q_{0},a,\mathbf{v},q)\in\Delta\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(q_{0}^{\prime},a,(\mathbf{v}+b^{(p)}_{i,0})\cdot\mathbf{e}^{M}_{\pi(C^{(p)}_{i})},q)\mid(p,a,\mathbf{v},q)\in\Delta,1\leq i\leq\ell_{p}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\ \{(p,\varepsilon,\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{0}^{M},q)\mid(p,\varepsilon,\mathbf{v},q)\in\mathcal{E},p\neq q_{0}\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\ \{(q^{\prime}_{0},\varepsilon,b^{(p)}_{i,j}\cdot\mathbf{e}^{M}_{\pi(C^{(p)}_{i})},q^{\prime}_{0})\mid p\in Q,1\leq i\leq\ell_{p},1\leq j\leq k_{i,q}\},

and

\displaystyle C^{\prime}=C\cdot\{z\mathbf{e}_{i}^{M}\mid z\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq M\}.

We prove that
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{A}
.

\Rightarrow

To show that
P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})\subseteq P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
, let
\alpha\in P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})
with accepting run
r=r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\dots
where
r_{i}=(p_{i-1},\gamma_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i},p_{i})
. We distinguish two cases.

In the first case assume that
r_{1}=(q_{0},\alpha_{1},\mathbf{v},p_{1})\in\Delta
. In this case we can simply replace
r_{1}
by
r_{1}^{\prime}=(q_{0}^{\prime},\alpha_{1},\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{0},p_{1})
and continue the run as in
\mathcal{A}
, padding all vectors with zeros.
That is, for
i\geq 2
let
r_{i}^{\prime}=(p_{i-1},\gamma_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{0}^{M},p_{i})
. Then
r^{\prime}=r_{1}^{\prime}r_{2}^{\prime}r_{3}^{\prime}
is a run of

on

. We show that

is also accepting. As

.
As

is accepting.

For the second case assume that

, i. e., the first transition of
\mathcal{A}
is an
\varepsilon
-transition
.
Let

be the last occurrence of the initial
\varepsilon
-sequence, that is,

on

-loops,

It remains to show that

. Thus, all (infinitely many) accepting hits of

\lrcorner

\Leftarrow
To show vice versa that

, let
\alpha\in P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
with accepting run

.

.
Hence, we can replace

,

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,\mathcal{E},F,C)
be an
\varepsilon
-PPBA of dimension
d
. There is an equivalent
\varepsilon
-PPBA
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
where all
\varepsilon
-transitions are
\varepsilon
-loops.

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.2
we may assume that
\mathcal{A}
has only a single accepting state
f
, that is,
F=\{f\}
, and by
Lemma

4.2
we may assume that all outgoing
\varepsilon
-transitions of the initial state are
\varepsilon
-loops.

In the beginning,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=(Q^{\prime},\Sigma,q_{0}^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},F^{\prime},C^{\prime})
is a copy of
\mathcal{A}
, which will be modified step-by-step. First, we remove all non-loop
\varepsilon
-transitions from
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.

The intuition for the construction is as follows.
We introduce new states such that in every run of
\mathcal{A}
on an infinite word we can replace every maximal partial run
r_{i}\dots r_{j}
of
\varepsilon
-transitions
, where
r_{i-1}=(p_{i-2},\alpha_{z-1},\mathbf{v}_{i-1},p_{i-1}),r_{i}=(p_{i-1},\varepsilon,\mathbf{v}_{i},p_{i}),r_{j}=(p_{j-1},\varepsilon,\mathbf{v}_{j},p_{j})
, and
r_{j+1}=(p_{j},\alpha_{z},\mathbf{v}_{j+1},p_{j+1})
, by a sequence of
\varepsilon
-loops on a new state depending on
p_{i-1},p_{j}
, and
\sum L(\mathcal{B}_{p_{i-1},p_{j}})
.
The (finite) partial run
r_{i}\dots r_{j}
translates into an accepting run in
\mathcal{B}_{p_{i-1},p_{j}}
, thus
\rho(r_{i}\dots r_{j})\in\sum L(\mathcal{B}_{p_{i-1},p_{j}})
.
By
Corollary

4.1
the set
\sum L(\mathcal{B}_{p_{i-1},p_{j}})
is semi-linear and hence a finite union of linear sets, each defined by a base vector and a set of period vectors, we can encode the period vectors as
\varepsilon
-loops and shortcut the base vector.
Some care must be taken if we visit the accepting state
f
in the partial run
r_{i}\dots r_{j}
.

Let us continue with the formal construction.
We iterate through all pairs
p,q
of states. If
L(\mathcal{B}_{p,q})=\varnothing
, we do nothing and continue with the next pair of states.
Otherwise, the state
q
is reachable from
p
in
\mathcal{B}_{p,q}
and
\sum L(\mathcal{B}_{p,q})
is non-empty and can be written as the finite union of linear sets.
For the sake of readability we assume that
\sum L(\mathcal{B}_{p,q})=C_{p,q}
is a single linear set.
For the general case we apply the following construction independently for each of the linear sets in the union. In the following, for any pair of states
s,t
we denote by
b_{s,t}
the base vector of
C_{s,t}
.

We add a new state
(p,q)
to the new state set
Q^{\prime}
(the new state is the tuple of states
p
and
q
).
If
C_{p,q}=\{b_{p,q}+b_{1}z_{1}+\dots+b_{k}z_{k}\mid z_{1},\dots,z_{k}\in\mathbb{N}\}
we add
\varepsilon
-loops labeled with the period vectors
b_{1},\dots,b_{k}
of
C_{p,q}
to the state
(p,q)
.
Finally, we add
shortcuts
to
(p,q)
, that is, for every transition of the form
(s,a,\mathbf{v},p)\in\Delta
we add a transition
(s,a,\mathbf{v}+b_{p,q},(p,q))
to
\Delta^{\prime}
(note that we add the base vector
b_{p,q}
of
C_{p,q}
to
\mathbf{v}
).
Likewise, for every transition of the form
(q,a,\mathbf{v},s)\in\Delta
we add a transition
((p,q),a,\mathbf{v},s)
to
\Delta^{\prime}
.
Finally, we connect the new states by further adding transitions

((p,q),a,\mathbf{v}+b_{s,t},(s,t))
to
\Delta^{\prime}
for every transition
(q,a,\mathbf{v},s)\in\Delta
and
t\in Q
such that
(s,t)\in Q^{\prime}
(again assuming that
C_{s,t}
is linear; otherwise, we would have multiple copies of
(s,t)
, each of which gets connected by a transition as above.).

After the exhaustive application of this procedure
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
can simulate every run of
\mathcal{A}
in the sense that all
\varepsilon
-transitions that appear in
\mathcal{A}
have been reduced to a number of new states equipped with
\varepsilon
-loops.
However, if the accepting state
f
is reachable from
p
in
\mathcal{B}_{p,q}
we must take into account that the procedure above might have shortcut the accepting state, which leads to missing accepting hits.
To prevent this, we need to add additional new states.

We iterate over all pairs of states
p,q\in Q
again.
However, this time we consider the automata
\mathcal{A}_{p,f}^{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{A}_{f,q}^{\varepsilon}
.
As above, let
\mathcal{B}_{p,f}
and
\mathcal{B}_{f,q}
be the NFA whose alphabets are subsets of
\mathbb{N}^{d}
obtained from
\mathcal{A}_{p,f}^{\varepsilon}
, resp.
\mathcal{A}_{f,q}^{\varepsilon}
by keeping only the vectors of the
\varepsilon
-transitions.
By
Corollary

4.1
the sets
\sum L(\mathcal{B}_{p,f})
and
\sum L(\mathcal{B}_{f,q})
are semi-linear.

If both of these sets are non-empty, we can write each of them as a finite union of linear sets.
Again, for the sake of readability, we assume that
\sum L(\mathcal{B}_{p,f})=C_{p,f}
and
\sum L(\mathcal{B}_{f,q})=C_{f,q}
are linear sets. For the general case, we apply the following construction for every combination of a linear set of
L(\mathcal{B}_{p,f})
with a linear set of
L(\mathcal{B}_{f,q})
independently.

Let
C_{p,f}=\{b_{p,f}+b_{1}z_{1}+\dots+b_{k}z_{k}\mid z_{1},\dots,z_{k}\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
C_{f,q}=\{b_{f,q}+c_{1}z_{1}+\dots+c_{\ell}z_{\ell}\mid z_{1},\dots,z_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
We add a new accepting state
(p,f,q)
to
Q^{\prime}
and equip it with
\varepsilon
-loops labeled with the period vectors
b_{1},\dots,b_{k}
of
C_{p,f}
.
Furthermore, for each transition of the form
(r,a,\mathbf{v},p)\in\Delta
we add a shortcut
(r,a,\mathbf{v}+b_{p,f},(p,f,q))
to
\Delta^{\prime}
.

Let us now connect the
(p,q)
-states to the states just introduced.
For every transition
(q,a,\mathbf{v},s)\in\Delta
we add the ingoing transitions
((p,q),a,\mathbf{v}+b_{s,f},(s,f,t))
for all
t\in Q
to
\Delta^{\prime}
.

In the next step we introduce the outgoing transitions of the
(p,f,q)
-states. Unfortunately, this situation is more complicated and we have to introduce yet more states
(p,f,q,s)
and
(p,f,q,s,t)
.
The idea is that these states act like a copy of
s
resp.
(s,t)
, but are additionally equipped with the
\varepsilon
-loops labeled with the period vectors of
C_{f,q}
.
We cannot simply add these loops to
(p,f,q)
, as this might lead to accepting hits in
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
that are not possible in
\mathcal{A}
by using these loops (which are for vectors of
\varepsilon
-sequences
leaving

f
).
We cannot simply ignore them either, as they are necessary to simulate the runs of
\mathcal{A}
appropriately.
Hence the copies, which allow us to use the loops without generating false accepting hits.

Formally, we insert a new state
(p,f,q,s)
for all
p,q,s\in Q
and a new state
(p,f,q,s,t)
for all
p,q\in Q
and
(s,t)\in Q^{\prime}
. For every transition
(q,a,\mathbf{v},s)\in\Delta
we add the following transitions to
\Delta^{\prime}
:

((p,f,q),a,\mathbf{v}+b_{f,q},(p,f,q,s))
, and

((p,f,q),a,\mathbf{v}+b_{f,q}+b_{s,t},(p,f,q,s,t))
for all
t\in Q
such that there exists
(s,t)\in Q^{\prime}
.

Finally, we connect the new states
(p,f,q,s)
and
(p,f,q,s,t)
as follows. First,
(p,f,q,s)
and
(p,f,q,s,t)
are equipped with
\varepsilon
-loops labeled with the period vectors of
C_{f,q}
.
Furthermore,
(p,f,q,s)
has all outgoing transitions of
s
, that is, for every transition
(s,a,\mathbf{v},t)\in\Delta^{\prime}
we also add a transition
((p,f,q,s),a,\mathbf{v},t)
to
\Delta^{\prime}
.
Similarly,
(p,f,q,s,t)
gets all outgoing transitions of
(s,t)
in
\Delta^{\prime}
. Additionally,
(p,f,q,s,t)
gets all
\varepsilon
-loops of
(s,t)
.

Now, all remaining
\varepsilon
-transitions of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
are loops and we have finished the construction of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.
\hfill\lrcorner

We prove that
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{A}
. In the following, by an
\varepsilon
-sequence we mean a maximal sequence of
\varepsilon
-transitions containing at least one non-loop
\varepsilon
-transition.

\Rightarrow
To show that
P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})\subseteq P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
, let
\alpha\in P_{\omega}(\mathcal{A})
with accepting run
r=r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\dots
. If there are no
\varepsilon
-sequences in
r
we are done (as
r
is also a run of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
on
\alpha
).

Otherwise, we construct an accepting run

of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
on
\alpha
by replacing
\varepsilon
-sequences step-by-step.
Let
i
be minimal such that
r_{i}\dots r_{j}
is an
\varepsilon
-sequence.
Note that because
q_{0}
has no non-loop
\varepsilon
-transitions we have
i>1
. Let

,

, and

be minimal such that

\varepsilon
-sequence, that is,

\varepsilon

By construction, there are
\varepsilon

.
Hence, we can replace

.

We now continue with the next

.

\bullet
Case (F1F). We do the exact same replacement as in (F1N). We thereby lose an accepting hit in the second sequence, however, this is not a problem, as we still have an accepting hit in the first sequence and we have infinitely many accepting hits to come.

\bullet
Case (F2N). This is similar to (F1N) but we chose
\hat{r}_{m+1}=((p_{i-1},f,p_{j}),\alpha_{z},\mathbf{v}_{j+1}+b_{f,p_{j}},(p_{i-1},f,p_{j},p_{j+1}))

\bullet
Case (F2F) is the same as (F2N).

\bullet
Case (N1F). Here we replace

.

has already been added to
\hat{r}_{i-1}

\bullet
The remaining cases (N1N), (N2F) and (N2N) are the same as (N1F).

All accepting hits outside of

Lemma 4.4
.

[Theorem 22 of Klaedtke and Ruess
[
9
]
, reformulated]
For every PA
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,F,C)
of dimension
d

on finite words
(with
\varepsilon
-transitions) there exists an equivalent
\varepsilon
-free PA
\hat{}\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\hat{\Delta},F,\hat{C})
on the same state set of dimension
d+|Q|-1
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,\mathcal{E},F,C)
be an
\varepsilon
-PPBA of dimension
d
where all occuring
\varepsilon
-transitions are
\varepsilon
-loops. There is an equivalent PPBA
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let us first sketch the proof idea. Intuitively, we will split the automaton into two parts. We will guess the set
S
of states that will be seen infinitely often. In the first part of the automaton we will deal with the set of states that are seen only finitely often. For this, we apply the construction of

Lemma

4.4
and make all accepting states non-accepting to obtain the automaton
\hat{}\mathcal{A}
.
We non-deterministically switch to the second part, where we will verify that for some set
S\subseteq Q
exactly the states of
S
will be seen infinitely often.
We follow the idea of Klaedtke and Ruess
[
9
]
for finite words:
Since it does not matter when and in what order vectors are added, we can simulate
\varepsilon
-loops by an appropriate modification of the semi-linear set.
Instead of
\varepsilon
-looping on a state we can intuitively “substract” the semi-linear set corresponding to the loop from
C
.
Formally, we will construct an automaton
\mathcal{A}_{S}
for each possible guess of
S
and shortcut appropriately from
\hat{}\mathcal{A}
. We will work with one semi-linear set for each
\mathcal{A}_{S}
, so that we formally construct an MPPBA.
We conclude by applying
Lemma

3.2
to translate this automaton finally to an equivalent PPBA. Let us come to the formal proof.

By
lemma

3.2
we may assume that
\mathcal{A}
has only a single accepting state, say
F=\{f\}
. We first interpret
\mathcal{A}
as a PA on finite words and denote by
\hat{}\mathcal{A}
the
\varepsilon
-free automaton obtained from
Lemma

4.4
by
padding every vector with
d
-many zeros (that is
\hat{}\mathcal{A}
is of dimension
2d+|Q|-1
instead of
d+|Q|-1
). We denote the semi-linear set of
\hat{}\mathcal{A}
by
\hat{C}
.

Now, for every non-empty subset of states
S\subseteq Q
containing at least
f
, we construct an MPPBA
\mathcal{A}_{S}
of dimension
d
, as follows.
Let the states of
S
be ordered arbitrarily, say
S=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{m}\}
.
S
is a candidate set for the set consisting exactly of those states that will be visited infinitely often.
We connect
\hat{}\mathcal{A}
to all
\mathcal{A}_{S}

\mathcal{A}_{S}

The automaton

consists of

In the following, we call the

By allowing to switch from the

From the last layer we have shortcuts into the new state

, allowing us to "wait" for the point where the next transition would bring us to
f
and the counters to a value in the semi-linear set.
Additionally,
q_{S}
has basically the same outgoing transitions as the accepting state
f
, but they lead into the layer for

.

Formally, let
\mathcal{A}_{S}=((S\times\{1,\dots,m+1\})\cup\{q_{S}\},\Sigma,q_{S},\Delta_{S},\{q_{S}\},C_{S})
, where

\displaystyle\Delta_{S}=\

\displaystyle\{((s_{i},k),a,\mathbf{0}^{d+|Q|-1}\cdot\mathbf{v},(s_{j},k))\mid(s_{i},a,\mathbf{v},s_{j})\in\Delta,k\leq m+1\}

\displaystyle\cup\

\displaystyle\{((s_{i},i),a,\mathbf{0}^{d+|Q|-1}\cdot\mathbf{v},(s_{j},i+1)\mid(s_{i},a,\mathbf{v},s_{j})\in\Delta,i\leq m\}

\displaystyle\cup\

\displaystyle\{((s_{i},m+1),a,\mathbf{0}^{d+|Q|-1}\cdot\mathbf{v},q_{S})\mid(s_{i},a,\mathbf{v},f)\in\Delta\}

\displaystyle\cup\

\displaystyle\{(q_{S},a,\mathbf{0}^{d+|Q|-1}\cdot\mathbf{v},(s_{i},1)\mid(f,a,\mathbf{v},s_{i})\in\Delta\}.

For two semi-linear sets
C_{1},C_{2}
of dimension

Finally, let

A

A configuration

\mathcal{M}

and for all

for some

That is, a word is accepted if we infinitely often visit an accepting state when the counters are
\mathbf{0}
, and every symbol of
\alpha
is read at some point.
We define the
\omega
-language recognized by
\mathcal{M}
as

.

We show that we can effectively convert every CM into an equivalent
\varepsilon
-PPBA, which is equivalent to a PPBA by

.
For the other direction we will make use of
\varepsilon
-transitions, i. e., our results do not yield an

\varepsilon
-transitions are as powerful as CM with

Lemma 5.1
.

For every CM
\mathcal{M}
there is an equivalent
\varepsilon
-PPBA

.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,F)
be a

-counter machine. For a vector

∎

From the previous two lemmas follows the equivalence of PPBA and CM.

Corollary 5.1
.

The classes of PPBA-recognizable
\omega
-languages and CM-recognizable

-languages coincide.

At this point we briefly mention that the class of SPBA-recognizable
\omega
-languages is also closed under union and intersection with

-regular languages, and not closed under intersection and complement (which can be shown using very similar arguments).

Observation 5.1
.

The classes of PPBA-recognizable
\omega
-languages and SPBA-recognizable
\omega
-languages
are closed under union, and closed under intersection with
\omega
-regular languages, but not under intersection and complement.

We conclude this section by showing that the classes of PPBA-recognizable and SPBA-recognizable

-languages are also closed under left-concatenation with Parikh-recognizable languages.
We prove the following auxiliary lemma, which simplifies the proof of this statement, as well as some proofs in the following sections.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,F,C)
be a PA (on finite words) with

. Then we can construct in polynomial time a PA

with the following properties.

1.

L(\mathcal{A}_{N})=L\setminus\{\varepsilon\}
.

2.

\mathcal{A}_{N}
has a single accepting state
f
, and
f
has no outgoing transitions.

We say that

is

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{N}=(Q\cup\{f\},\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta_{N},\{f\},C)
, where

,

, that is,

guesses the position of the last symbol of the input word and moves to the new accepting state (which has no outgoing transitions) if the last symbol is read. As we need to read at least one symbol to reach

, we can never accept the empty word.
It is obvious that
\mathcal{A}_{N}
as constructed above satisfies the conditions of being normalized and can be computed in polynomial time.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let

We start with the argument for SPBA.

\displaystyle\Delta=

\displaystyle\cup

for

.

In this section, we show that the class of PPBA-recognizable
\omega
-languages is a strict subset of the class of SPBA-recognizable
\omega
-languages.
It will be convenient to consider the class

We show that

Let

7
Decision problems

In this section, we show that the (un)decidability results of the common decision problems for PA on finite words in
[
9
,
6
]
can be transferred to PBA. These problems include the following:

1.

Emptiness.
Does
\mathcal{A}
recognize the empty language?

2.

Universality.
Does
\mathcal{A}
accept every word?

3.

Equivalence.
Do
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
recognize the same language?

4.

Inclusion.
Does
\mathcal{A}_{2}
recognize every word that
\mathcal{A}_{1}
recognizes?

In the following we assume that all semi-linear sets are given as a collection of lists of base vector
b_{0}
and period vectors
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
(one list for each linear set in the finite union). All numbers are encoded in binary.

Theorem 7.1
.

Emptiness for SPBA, WPBA, and PPBA (and their equivalent models) are
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete. Universality, inclusion and equivalence for SPBA, WPBA, and PPBA (and their equivalent models) are undecidable.

Since every SPBA can be converted efficiently into an equivalent WPBA and vice versa by
Lemma

3.4
and
Lemma

3.5
, the decidability results for SPBA hold for WPBA, too.
Furthermore, we show how to convert any PPBA into an equivalent WPBA (and hence SPBA) in polynomial time (note that this does not directly follow from our results, as the intermediate step of converting a PPBA into an equivalent CM introduces
\varepsilon
-transitions whose elimination requires super-polynomial time).

Thus, for the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-completeness of emptiness it is sufficent to show that emptiness for
\varepsilon
-MSPBA is in
{\mathsf{coNP}}
(yielding the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-membership for all of our models), and
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-hardness for (even 1-dimensional) PPBA (yielding hardness for all our models).
Similarly, it is sufficient to show the undecidability results for PPBA.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,F,C)
be a PPBA of dimension
d
. Then there is an equivalent WPBA that can be constructed in polynomial time.

Proof.

The idea is as follows. We have a copy of
\mathcal{A}
and additionally, for each linear set
C_{i}
in the semi-linear set

we introduce a copy
\mathcal{A}_{i}
of
\mathcal{A}
.
All states of the copy of
\mathcal{A}
are non-resetting and all states of

are resetting in the
\mathcal{A}_{i}

At any point while a run is still in
\mathcal{A}
and would enter a state

\mathcal{A}_{i}
and to reset the counters. For this, we first go to a special resetting copy of

and then transition to
\mathcal{A}_{i}
.
We use additional counters to mark in the semi-linear set in which

we are moving and modify the semi-linear set so that we do not count the base-vector again when continuing the run in
\mathcal{A}_{i}
.

Formally, assume

.
Let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=(Q^{\prime},\Sigma,(q_{0},0),\Delta^{\prime},F^{\prime},C^{\prime})

where

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\ Q\times\{0,1,\ldots,k\}\cup F\times\{1,\ldots,k\}\times\{R\},

\displaystyle F^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\ F\times\{1,\ldots,k\}\times\{R\}\cup F\times\{1,\ldots,k\},

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\ \{((p,0),a,\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{0}^{k+1},(q,0)),((p,i),a,\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{i}^{k+1},(q,i))\mid(p,a,\mathbf{v},q)\in\Delta,1\leq i\leq k\}

\displaystyle\cup

Now we show that universality for PPBA (and thus for all our models) is undecidable.

Lemma 7.5
.

Universality for PPBA is undecidable.

Proof.

We reduce from the universality problem for PA, which is known to be undecidable
[
9
]
. Let
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\Sigma,q_{0},\Delta,F,C)
be an arbitrary PA. We construct a PPBA

The idea is the following. The PPBA

accepts all words that are accepted by

, followed by an infinite sequence of dummy symbols

Observe that

.
Let
\mathcal{A}_{2}
with

and let
\mathcal{A}_{3}
be a PPBA that accepts all infinite words that contain a dummy symbol followed by a non-dummy symbol, i. e.,

. According to
5.1
, we can construct a PPBA that recognizes

. Let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
be this PPBA. Then

if and only if

.

\Rightarrow
Let
\mathcal{A}
be universal, i. e.,
\mathcal{A}
accepts all words

, that is, according to the construction of

, all infinite words of the form

will be accepted by

. Since

\Leftarrow

8
Conclusion and Outlook

We have introduced Parikh-Büchi automata with different acceptance conditions.
We have shown that PBA with strong and weak reset acceptance condition describe the same class of
\omega
-languages, which is a strict superclass of the class PPBA-recognizable
\omega
-languages.
The latter class is equivalent to the class of blind
k
-counter machines introduced by Fernau and Stiebe. As a side-product we show that all our models admit
\varepsilon
-elimination and are equivalent to their multi-counterparts. Finally, we have studied common decision problems.

An interesting open question remains the search for an (intuitive) model that captures exactly
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{PA}}^{\omega}
. Vice versa, characterizations of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{Prefix}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{Reset}}
in the spirit of Büchi’s theorem are yet to be found. Furthermore, it is interesting to study deterministic PBA, as deterministic Büchi automata are already weaker than (non-deterministic) Büchi automata. Finally, Klaedtke and Ruess proposed to study a variant of PBA where the semi-linear sets are augmented with a symbol
\infty
for infinity, and the extended Parikh image of an infinite word is computed accordingly using transfinite induction.

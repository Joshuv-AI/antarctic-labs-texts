On the Existence of Shimura curves in the Prym locus of abelian covers of projective line
Abolfazl Mohajer

Abstract
. Using the theory of Higgs bundles and their stabitlity properties associated to fibered surfaces and the Viehweg-Zuo characterization of Shimura curves in the moduli space of abelian varieties in terms of Higgs bundles, we prove that there does not exist any non-compact Shimura curves in the Prym locus of totally ramified ℤ 2 ​ p {\mathbb{Z}}_{2p} - or ℤ 2 ​ p × ( ℤ p ) m − 1 {\mathbb{Z}}_{2p}\times({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{m-1} -covers of the projective line in A g A_{g} for g ≥ 8 g\geq 8 , where p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 is a prime number.

An abelian Galois cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is determined by a collection of equations as follows:

Fix inetegers
m
and
s
and let
A=(r_{ij})
be an
m\times s
matrix whose entries
r_{ij}
are in
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
for some
N\geq 2
. Let
\overline{\mathbb{C}(z)}
be the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{C}(z)
. For each
i=1,...,m,
choose a function
w_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{C}(z)}
with

(2.0.1)

w_{i}^{N}=\prod_{j=1}^{s}(z-z_{j})^{\widetilde{r}_{ij}}\text{ for }i=1,\dots,m,

in
\mathbb{C}(z)[w_{1},\dots,w_{m}]
. We have denoted the lift of
r_{ij}
to
{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[0,N)
by
\widetilde{r}_{ij}
and
z_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}
for
j=1,2,\dots,s
. The equations
2.0.1
define only a singular affine curve in general and we consider a smooth projective model associated to this affine curve. The matrix
A
should have the following property: the sum of the columns of
A
is zero (when considered as a vector in
(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{m}
). This condition ensures that the curve defined by
2.0.1
is
not
branched over the infinity. The matrix
A
is called the matrix of the covering.

The local monodromy around the branch point
z_{j}
is given by the column vector
(r_{1j},\dots,r_{mj})^{t}
and therefore the order of ramification over
z_{j}
is equal to
\frac{N}{\gcd(N,\widetilde{r}_{1j},\dots,\widetilde{r}_{mj})}
. This together with the Riemann-Hurwitz formula yield the following theorem to compute the genus
g
of the cover:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\tilde{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be an abelian
\tilde{G}
-cover as above. Then the group
\tilde{G}
acts on the space
H^{0}(\tilde{C},K_{\tilde{C}})
and the dimension of the eigenspace
H^{0}(\tilde{C},K_{\tilde{C}})_{n}
related to
n\in\tilde{G}
is given by

(2.1.1)

d_{n}=\dim H^{0}(\tilde{C},K_{\tilde{C}})_{n}=-1+\sum_{1}^{s}\langle-\frac{\alpha_{j}}{N}\rangle

Also the genus of the cover is given by

(2.1.2)

\tilde{g}=1+d(\frac{s-2}{2}-\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\gcd(N,\widetilde{r}_{1j},\dots,\widetilde{r}_{mj})),

Here
d
is the degree of the covering which is equal, as pointed out above, to the column span (equivalently row span) of the matrix
A
. In this way, the Galois group
\tilde{G}
of the covering will be a subgroup of
(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{m}
. Note also that this group is isomorphic to the column span of the above matrix.

The following corollary is useful when we consider cyclic covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and in particular the totally ramified ones.

Corollary 2.2
.

For a cyclic cover
\tilde{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
with Galois group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
, we have

(2.2.1)

d_{n}=\dim H^{0}(\tilde{C},K_{\tilde{C}})_{n}=-1+\sum_{1}^{s}\langle-\frac{nr_{j}}{N}\rangle

In particular, if the cyclic cover is totally ramified, then

(2.2.2)

d_{n}=-1+s(1-\frac{n}{N})

and also

(2.2.3)

\tilde{g}=(-1+\frac{s}{2})(N-1)

In what follows we describe the construction of families of abelian covers of the projective line.

Let
T\subset(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{1})^{s}
be the complement of the big diagonals, i.e.,

(2.2.4)

T=\mathcal{P}_{s}=\{(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})\in(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{1})^{s}\mid z_{i}\neq z_{j}\forall i\neq j\}

Let

We begin this subsection by the following remark

Remark 2.4
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group. We call the group
\mu_{G}={\rm Hom}(G,\mathbb{C}^{*})

of

\mu_{G}

Let

but the

are considered as integers in

.

Let

be the eigenspace of

-action with respect to

. This is a vector space over

. It is shown in

. Here

The action of

for some subset of

(and also as identity on

By the above description of the space

H^{0}(\tilde{C},K_{\tilde{C}})_{n}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

and in general we have:

3.
Shimura curves and Higgs bundles

3.1.
Shimura varieties

In this subsection, we will breifly introduce and review some facts about Shimura varieties and special subvarieties. In this note we only consider connected Shimura varieties which are complex algebraic varieties of the form
\Gamma\backslash X^{+}
, where
X^{+}
is a Hermitian symmetric domain and
\Gamma
is a congruence subgroup of a semisimple algebraic group
G^{der}({\mathbb{Q}})
acting on
X^{+}
. Inside
\Gamma\backslash X^{+}
there are Shimura or special subvarieties associated with Shimura subdata, see
[
13
]
, §5. In simple terms, they are subvarieties of the form
\Gamma\backslash{X^{\prime}}^{+}\subseteq\Gamma\backslash X^{+}
, where
{X^{\prime}}^{+}\subseteq X^{+}
are equivariant embeddings of Hermitian symmetric subdomains of
X^{+}
. Equivariantly embedded here means that it is defined by some semi-simple Lie subgroup
G^{\prime}
of
G^{der}({\mathbb{R}})
and the inclusion
{X^{\prime}}^{+}\hookrightarrow X^{+}
is equivariant with respect to
G^{\prime}\hookrightarrow G^{der}({\mathbb{R}})
. The zero-dimensional special subvarieties are CM points. In this paper, we are interested about higher dimensional special subvarieties. The most important Shimura variety for us is the moduli space of complex
g
-dimensional principally polarized abelian varieties
A_{g}
. Here
A_{g}=A_{g,l}=\Gamma_{g}(l)\backslash{\mathbb{H}}_{g}
, where
{\mathbb{H}}_{g}
is the Siegel uper half-space of genus
g
and
\Gamma_{g}(l)
is the principal congruence subgroup of level-
l
in
Sp_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})
which is the kernel of the natural map
Sp_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})\to Sp_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}}/l)
, where
l\geq 3
is an odd integer. In this case
\Gamma_{g}(l)
is torsion-free and the quotient
\Gamma_{g}(l)\backslash{\mathbb{H}}_{g}
is a smooth complex submanifold. Special subvarities of dimension one are called
Shimura curves
.

In this paper we investigate Shimura curves in
A_{g}
inside the Prym locus and cuting the locus
{\mathcal{P}}^{\circ}_{g}
non-trivially. As indicated in the introduction, the Coleman-Oort conjecture asserts that for
g
large enough, there are no special subvarieties inside the Prym locus and cuttng the locus
{\mathcal{P}}^{\circ}_{g}
non-trivially. In this note we investigate this conjecture for subvarieties coming from families of Galois covers of curves. Indeed we consider families whose fibers are
\tilde{C_{t}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
which give rise to subvarieties in
M_{g}
and, by the Prym map, in
A_{g}
. Any such variety naturally lies in the Prym locus and intersects
{\mathcal{P}}^{\circ}_{g}
. The families of
\tilde{G}
-coverings of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
are
semi-stable
in the sense that the resulting family of Prym varieties are semistable abelian varieties. This can always be achieved by a finite base change.

Lemma 4.2

Let

Proof.

This is a generalization of
[
6
]
, Lemma 4.9. In the first step, one shows that for any non-zero unitary subbundle

4.1
,

11

\displaystyle\tau\circ\rho_{i_{1}}\wedge\tau\circ\rho_{i_{2}}:\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}{\mathcal{U}}\otimes\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}F^{1,0}_{\overline{B},-,i_{2}}\to\wedge^{2}\Omega^{1}_{\widetilde{Y}}(\widetilde{R})_{-}\otimes({\mathcal{L}^{(i_{1})}}^{-1}\otimes{\mathcal{L}^{(i_{2})}}^{-1})\to\omega_{\widetilde{Y}}(\widetilde{R})\otimes({\mathcal{L}^{(i_{1})}}^{-1}\otimes{\mathcal{L}^{(i_{2})}}^{-1})

is semi-positive in the sense that if

\widetilde{Y}

[
7
]

Introduction and statement of the main results

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space with inner product and norm respectively
denoted by
\langle.,.\rangle
and
\left|.\right|.

V
is an other
Hilbert space continuously and densely embedded in
H.
Let
V^{\prime}
be the
dual space of
V.
By identifying
H
with its dual space, we have

V\hookrightarrow H\hookrightarrow V^{\prime}.
We recall the following
important relation that will be used repeatedly in the sequel:

(1.1)

\langle v,w\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}=\langle v,w\rangle~\forall(v,w)\in H\times V.

In this paper, we investigate the long time behavior as
t\rightarrow+\infty

of solutions
u(t)
to the following second order semi-linear hyperbolic
equation:

(E
α
)

u^{\prime\prime}(t)+\gamma(t)u^{\prime}(t)+Au(t)+f(u(t))=g(t),~t\geq 0,

where the damping term
\gamma
is a function in
W_{loc}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{R}^{+})
which behaviors like
\frac{K}{t^{\alpha}}
for some

K>0,~\alpha\in[0,1[,
and
t
large enough. Precisely, we assume that
there exist
K>0,~t_{0}\geq 0
and
\alpha\in[0,1[
such that:

(1.2)

\gamma(t)\geq\frac{K}{(1+t)^{\alpha}}~\forall t\geq t_{0},

(1.3)

((1+t)^{\alpha}\gamma(t))^{\prime}\leq 0~\text{a.e. }t\geq t_{0}.

The operator
A:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
is linear and continuous. We suppose
that the associated bilinear form
a:V\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined
by

a(v,w)=\langle Av,w\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}

is symmetric, positive and satisfies the semi-coercivity property:

\exists\lambda\geq 0,\mu>0:~a(v,v)+\lambda\left|v\right|^{2}\geq\mu\left\|v\right\|_{V}^{2}~\forall v\in V.

We assume that the function
f:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
is a continuous
function that derives from a
C^{1}
convex function
F:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
in the sense:

(1.4)

\forall u,v\in V,F^{\prime}(u)(v)=\langle f(u),v\rangle_{V^{\prime},V},

which is equivalent to

(1.5)

\forall u\in V,\nabla F(u)=f(u).

Let us consider the function
\Phi:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by:

\Phi(v)=\frac{1}{2}a(v,v)+F(v).

It is clear that
\Phi
is a
C^{1}
convex function and for all
v\in V,

\nabla\Phi(v)=Av+f(v).
We assume that the set

\arg\min\Phi=\{v\in V:\Phi(v)=\min_{V}\Phi:=\Phi^{\ast}\},

which coincides with the set
\{v\in V:Av+f(v)=0\},
is nonempty.

Last, we suppose that function
g:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow H
belongs to the space

L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H).

In this paper, we assume the existence of a global solution
u
to Eq.
(E
α
) in the class

(1.6)

W_{loc}^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H)\cap W_{loc}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},V),

and we focus our attention on the study of the asymptotic behavior of
u(t)
as
t
goes to infinity.
Before setting our main theorems, let us first recall some previous results related to this subject.
In the pioneer paper
[
1
]
, Alvarez considered the case where
V=H,
the
damping term
\gamma
is a non negative constant and the source
g
is equal
to
0.
He proved that
u(t)
converges weakly to a minimizer
of the function
\Phi.
Moreover, he showed that the convergence is strong if
the function
\Phi
is even or the interior of
\arg\min\Phi
is not empty. In

[
6
]
, Haraux and Jendoubi extended the weak convergence result of Alvarez
to the case where the source term is in the space
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H).
Cabot and Frankel
[
5
]
studied Eq. (E
α
) where
g=0

and
\gamma(t)
behaviors at infinity like
\frac{K}{t^{\alpha}}
with
K>0

and
\alpha\in]0,1[.
They proved that every
bounded
solution
converges weakly toward a critical point of
\Phi.
In the paper

[
8
]
, the second author of the present paper improved the result of Cabot
and Frankel by getting rid of the supplementary hypothesis on the boundedness
of the solution. In
[
7
]
, it was proved that the main convergence result
of Cabot and Frankel remains true if the source term
g
satisfies the
condition
\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1+t)\left|g(t)\right|dt<\infty
. The
first purpose of the present paper is to improve this last result. In fact, we
prove that the convergence holds under the weaker and optimal condition

(1.7)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1+t)^{\alpha}\left|g(t)\right|dt<\infty.

Precisely, we establish the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1+t)^{\alpha}\left|g(t)\right|dt<\infty.
Let
u
be a solution to Eq. (E
α
) in the
class (
1.6
). If
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H),
then
u(t)
converges

\arg\min\Phi.

Moreover, the energy function

satisfies
\mathcal{E}(t)=\circ(t^{-2\alpha})
as

In the next theorem, we prove that we can get rid of the hypothesis on the
boundedness of the solution by adding a second condition on the source term.
This theorem generalizes the main result of
[
8
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1+t)^{\alpha}\left|g(t)\right|dt<\infty

the class (
1.6

and, therefore,

Our two last main results concern the strong convergence of the solution when

\Phi
is even or the interior of the set

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that the function
\Phi
is even,
\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1+t)^{\alpha}\left|g(t)\right|dt<\infty,

Let

be a

) in the class (
1.6

.

Assume that the interior of the set

Remark 1.2

A typical example of Eq. (E
{}_{\alpha})
is the following nonlinear damped wave

in
\mathcal{H}
, then
x\in S.

(ii)

For every
z\in S,

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\left\|x(t)-z\right\|
exists.

Then there exists
z_{\infty}\in S
such that
x(t)\rightharpoonup z_{\infty}
weakly in
\mathcal{H}
as
t\rightarrow+\infty.

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists
\tau_{0}\geq 0
such that for every
\tau\geq\tau_{0}

\int_{\tau}^{+\infty}e^{-\Gamma(t,\tau)}dt\leq\frac{2}{K}(1+\tau)^{\alpha}

where
\Gamma(t,\tau)=\int_{\tau}^{t}\gamma(s)ds.

Proof.

Let
\tau\geq t_{0}.
In view of (
1.2
),

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{+\infty}e^{-\Gamma(t,\tau)}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K}\int_{\tau}^{+\infty}(1+t)^{\alpha}\gamma(t)e^{-\Gamma(t,\tau)}dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{K}\int_{\tau}^{+\infty}(1+t)^{\alpha}\left(e^{-\Gamma(t,\tau)}\right)^{\prime}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}(1+\tau)^{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{K}\int_{\tau}^{+\infty}(1+t)^{\alpha-1}e^{-\Gamma(t,\tau)}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K}(1+\tau)^{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{K(1+\tau)^{1-\alpha}}\int_{\tau}^{+\infty}e^{-\Gamma(t,\tau)}dt.

Hence to conclude we just have to choose
\tau_{0}
large enough such that

\frac{\alpha}{K(1+\tau_{0})^{1-\alpha}}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

∎

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Let us first prove that
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},V).
Let
\bar{u}\in\arg\min\Phi
and define, as in the proof
of Proposition
2.1
, the function
p:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
by
p(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left|u(t)-\bar{u}\right|^{2}.

This function belongs to the space
W_{loc}^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{R}^{+})
and satisfies almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{+},

\displaystyle p^{\prime\prime}+\gamma p^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left|u^{\prime}\right|^{2}-\langle\nabla\Phi(u),u-\bar{u}\rangle+\langle g,u-\bar{u}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left|u^{\prime}\right|^{2}-\langle\nabla\Phi(u)-\nabla\Phi(\bar{u}),u-\bar{u}\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}+\langle g,u-\bar{u}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left|u^{\prime}\right|^{2}+\left|g\right|\sqrt{2p},

where we have used the monotonicity of the operator
\nabla\Phi.

Therefore, for almost every
t\geq\tau_{0},

p^{\prime}(t)\leq e^{-\Gamma(t,\tau_{0})}p^{\prime}(\tau_{0})+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}e^{-\Gamma(t,s)}\rho(s)ds

where
\rho:=\left|u^{\prime}\right|^{2}+\left|g\right|\sqrt{2p}.

Thus, by using the previous lemma and Fubini’s theorem, we get for
every
t\geq\tau_{0}

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}[p^{\prime}(\tau)]_{+}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\left(1+\tau_{0}\right)^{\alpha}}{K}\left|p^{\prime}(\tau_{0})\right|+\frac{2}{K}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}(1+s)^{\alpha}\rho(s)ds

(2.13)

\displaystyle\leq c_{0}+\frac{2}{K}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}(1+s)^{\alpha}\left|g(s)\right|\sqrt{2p(s)}ds

where
c_{0}=\frac{2\left(1+\tau_{0}\right)^{\alpha}}{K}\left|p^{\prime}(\tau_{0})\right|+\frac{2}{K}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1+s)^{\alpha}\left|u^{\prime}(s)\right|^{2}ds
and
[p^{\prime}(\tau)]_{+}
is
the positive part of
p^{\prime}(\tau).

Using now the inequalities
\sqrt{2p}\leq 1+2p
and
p(t)\leq p(\tau_{0})+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}[p^{\prime}(\tau)]_{+}d\tau,
we obtain

p(t)\leq c_{1}+\frac{4}{K}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}(1+s)^{\alpha}\left|g(s)\right|p(s)ds,~\forall t\geq\tau_{0},

with
c_{1}=c_{0}+p(\tau_{0})+\frac{2}{K}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1+s)^{\alpha}\left|g(s)\right|ds.
Hence, by applying Gronwall’s inequality, we
deduce that the function
p
is bounded which is equivalent to
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H).
Using now
[
5
, Remark 3.4]
, we obtain that

u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},V).
Coming back to the estimate (
2.13
),
we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{0}}^{+\infty}[p^{\prime}(\tau)]_{+}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq c_{0}+\frac{2}{K}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1+s)^{\alpha}\left|g(s)\right|ds\sqrt{\sup_{t\geq 0}2p(t)}

\displaystyle<+\infty

which implies that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}p(t)
and therefore

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\left|u(t)-\bar{u}\right|
exist. Now,
let
\bar{x}\in H
such that there exists a sequence
(t_{n})_{n}
of positive
real numbers tending to
+\infty
such that
u(t_{n})
converges weakly in
H

to
\bar{x}.
Since
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},V),

u(t_{n})
converges
weakly also in the space
V
to the same element
\bar{x}.
Using now the weak
lower semi-continuity of the continuous and convex function
\Phi,
we deduce
that
\Phi^{\ast}=\lim\inf\Phi(u(t_{n}))\leq\Phi(\bar{x}).
Thus
\bar{x}\in\arg\min\Phi.
Therefore, applying Opial’s lemma with
S=\arg\min\Phi

ensures that
u(t)
converges weakly in
H
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
to some
element of
\arg\min\Phi.
Recalling that
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},V),

we conclude that this weak convergence holds also in the space
V.

∎

.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
v\in V,

(2.14)

\left|v\right|\leq\left\|v\right\|_{V^{\prime}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|v\right\|_{V}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Let

3.
Proof of the main results

This section is devoted to the proof of our main theorems. Let us first notice
that Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
follow immediately from
Proposition
2.1
(with
\nu=2\alpha
) and Proposition
2.2
. Hence it remains to prove Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The proof is based on the adaptation of a method
introduced by Bruck
[
4
]
for the steepest descent method and used by
Alvarez
[
1
]
for the heavy ball with friction system.

Since
2\alpha+1\geq 3\alpha,
then in view of Theorem
1.2
,
Proposition
2.1
, and Proposition
2.2
,
u(t)
converges weakly in

V
to some
u_{\infty}\in\arg\min\Phi,
and
\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1+t)^{\alpha}\left|u^{\prime}(t)\right|^{2}dt<\infty.
Let
\tau\geq\tau_{0}

where
\tau_{0}
is the real defined in Lemma
2.2
. We define the
function
q
on the interval
[\tau_{0},\tau]
by:

q(t)=\left|u(t)\right|^{2}-\left|u(\tau)\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left|u(t)-u(\tau)\right|^{2}.

The function
q
belongs to the space
W^{2,1}([\tau_{0},\tau],\mathbb{R})

and satisfies almost everywhere

(3.1)

q^{\prime}(t)=\langle u^{\prime}(t),u(t)+u(\tau)\rangle

(3.2)

q^{\prime\prime}(t)=\left|u^{\prime}(t)\right|^{2}+\langle u^{\prime\prime}(t),u(t)+u(\tau)\rangle.

Combining this two equalities, we obtain

\displaystyle q^{\prime\prime}(t)+\gamma(t)q^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\left|u^{\prime}(t)\right|^{2}+\langle\nabla\Phi(u),-u(\tau)-u(t)\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}+\langle g(t),u(t)+u(\tau)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left|u^{\prime}(t)\right|^{2}+\Phi(-u(\tau))-\Phi(u(t))+2M\left|g(t)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|u^{\prime}(t)\right|^{2}+\Phi(u(\tau))-\Phi(u(t))+2M\left|g(t)\right|

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}\left|u^{\prime}(t)\right|^{2}+\mathcal{\tilde{E}}(\tau)-\mathcal{\tilde{E}}(t)+2M\left|g(t)\right|+\int_{t}^{\tau}\frac{\left|g(s)\right|^{2}}{4\gamma(s)}ds

where
M=\sup_{t\geq 0}\left|u(t)\right|
and
\mathcal{\tilde{E}}

is the modified energy function defined by:

\mathcal{\tilde{E}}(t)=\mathcal{E}(t)+\int_{t}^{+\infty}\frac{\left|g(s)\right|^{2}}{4\gamma(s)}ds,

where
\mathcal{E}
is the energy function given by (
1.8
). Using
(
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{\tilde{E}}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=-\gamma(t)\left|u^{\prime}(t)\right|^{2}+\langle g(t),u^{\prime}(t)\rangle-\frac{\left|g(t)\right|^{2}}{4\gamma(t)}

\displaystyle\leq-\left(\sqrt{\gamma(t)}\left|u^{\prime}(t)\right|-\frac{\left|g(t)\right|}{2\sqrt{\gamma(t)}}\right)^{2}.

Therefore the function
\mathcal{\tilde{E}}
is non increasing. Hence
(
3.3
) and (
1.2
) yield

q^{\prime\prime}(t)+\gamma(t)q^{\prime}(t)\leq\omega(t),

where

\omega(t)=\frac{3}{2}\left|u^{\prime}(t)\right|^{2}+2M\left|g(t)\right|+\frac{1}{4K}\int_{t}^{+\infty}(1+s)^{\alpha}\left|g(s)\right|^{2}ds.

Therefore, for almost every
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau],

(3.4)

q^{\prime}(t)\leq e^{-\Gamma(t,\tau_{0})}\left|q^{\prime}(\tau_{0})\right|+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}e^{-\Gamma(t,s)}\omega(s)ds\equiv\kappa(t).

A simple calculation, using Fubini’s theorem and Lemma
2.2
, gives

\int_{\tau_{0}}^{+\infty}\kappa(t)dt\leq c_{0}+\frac{2}{K}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{+\infty}(1+s)^{\alpha}\omega(s)ds

where
c_{0}=\frac{2}{K}(1+\tau_{0})^{\alpha}\left|q^{\prime}(\tau_{0})\right|.

Using once again Fubini’s theorem, we get

\int_{\tau_{0}}^{+\infty}(1+t)^{\alpha}\int_{t}^{+\infty}(1+s)^{\alpha}\left|g(s)\right|^{2}dsdt\leq\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{+\infty}(1+s)^{2\alpha+1}\left|g(s)\right|^{2}ds.

Then we deduce that the integral
\int_{\tau_{0}}^{+\infty}(1+s)^{\alpha}\omega(s)ds
is finite which implies

(3.5)

\int_{\tau_{0}}^{+\infty}\kappa(t)dt<+\infty.

Integrating now (
3.4
) between
t
and
\tau,
with
\tau_{0}\leq t\leq\tau,
we get

(3.6)

\frac{1}{2}\left|u(t)-u(\tau)\right|^{2}\leq\left|u(t)\right|^{2}-\left|u(\tau)\right|^{2}+\int_{t}^{\tau}\kappa(s)ds.

In the proof of Proposition
2.2
, we showed that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\left|u(t)-\bar{u}\right|^{2}
exists for all
\bar{u}
in

\arg\min\Phi.
But
0\in\arg\min\Phi
since
\Phi
is convex and even, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\left|u(t)\right|^{2}
exists. Therefore,
(
3.6
) and (
3.5
) imply

\left|u(\tau)-u(t)\right|\rightarrow 0\text{ as }t,\tau\rightarrow+\infty.

Thus, in view of Cauchy criteria,
u(t)
converges strongly in
H
as

t\rightarrow+\infty
. Therefore, by using
[
5
, Corollary 3.6]
, we
deduce that
u(t)
converges strongly in
V
as
t\rightarrow+\infty.

Finally, since
u(t)\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}
weakly in
V,
we conclude
that
u(t)\rightarrow u_{\infty}
strongly in
V.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By assumption, there exists
x^{\ast}\in\arg\min\Phi
and
r>0
such
that for all
v
in the unit Ball
B_{V}(0,1)
of
V
we have
\nabla\Phi(x^{\ast}+rv)=0.
Therefore the monotonicity of
\nabla\Phi
implies that
for every
x\in V,~\langle\nabla\Phi(x),x-x^{\ast}-rv\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}\geq 0
which yields that
\langle\nabla\Phi(x),v\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}\leq\frac{1}{r}\langle\nabla\Phi(x),x-x^{\ast}\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}.
Hence
by taking the supremum on
v\in B_{V}(0,1),
we get

(3.7)

\left\|\nabla\Phi(x)\right\|_{V^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{r}\langle\nabla\Phi(x),x-x^{\ast}\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}.

Let us now define the function
p(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left|u(t)-x^{\ast}\right|^{2}.
We already know that
p
satisfies the differential
inequality

p^{\prime\prime}(t)+\gamma(t)p^{\prime}(t)\leq\left|u^{\prime}(t)\right|^{2}-\langle\nabla\Phi(u(t)),u(t)-x^{\ast}\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}+\langle g(t),u(t)-x^{\ast}\rangle.

Hence by using (
3.7
), we obtain

(3.8)

r\left\|\nabla\Phi(u(t))\right\|_{V^{\prime}}\leq-p^{\prime\prime}(t)-\gamma(t)p^{\prime}(t)+\sigma(t),

where
\sigma(t)=\left|u^{\prime}(t)\right|^{2}+\left|g(t)\right|\sup_{t\geq 0}\left|u(t)-x^{\ast}\right|.

Recalling that in view Proposition
2.1
,
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda_{\alpha}(t)\sigma(t)dt<\infty
where
\lambda_{\alpha}(t)=(1+t)^{\alpha}.
Hence, by multiplying (
3.8
) by
\lambda_{\alpha}(t)

and integrating between
t_{0}
and
\tau\geq t_{0},
we get after
integrations by parts and simplification

\displaystyle r\int_{t_{0}}^{\tau}\lambda_{\alpha}(t)\left\|\nabla\Phi(u(t))\right\|_{V^{\prime}}dt

\displaystyle\leq C-\lambda_{\alpha}(\tau)p^{\prime}(\tau)+\lambda_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\tau)p(\tau)

\displaystyle-\underset{\geq 0}{\underbrace{(\lambda_{\alpha}\gamma)}}(\tau)p(\tau)+\int_{t_{0}}^{\tau}[\underset{\leq 0}{\underbrace{(\lambda_{\alpha}\gamma)^{\prime}}}-\lambda_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}](t)p(t)dt

where
C
is a constant independent of
\tau.
Since
\alpha<1
and
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H),
the integral
\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\left|\lambda_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}(t)\right|p(t)dt
and the supremum

\sup_{\tau\geq t_{0}}\lambda_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\tau)p(\tau)
are finite.
Moreover, from Proposition
2.1
,
\left|u^{\prime}(\tau)\right|=\circ(\tau^{-\alpha})
as
\tau\rightarrow+\infty,
then

\sup_{\tau\geq t_{0}}\lambda_{\alpha}(\tau)\left|p^{\prime}(\tau)\right|\leq\sup_{\tau\geq t_{0}}\lambda_{\alpha}(\tau)\left|u^{\prime}(\tau)\right|\left|u(\tau)-x^{\ast}\right|<\infty.

Therefore, we conclude that

(3.9)

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\lambda_{\alpha}(t)\left\|\nabla\Phi(u(t))\right\|_{V^{\prime}}dt<+\infty.

From Eq. (E
α
), we have

u^{\prime\prime}(t)+\gamma(t)u^{\prime}(t)=g(t)-\nabla\Phi(u(t))

Hence, by integrating this equation we get

(3.10)

u^{\prime}(t)=e^{-\Gamma(t,\tau_{0})}u^{\prime}(\tau_{0})+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}e^{-\Gamma(t,s)}[g(s)-\nabla\Phi(u(s))]ds,

for almost every
t\geq\tau_{0}
where
\tau_{0}
is the real defined by Lemma

2.2
. Up to replace
\tau_{0}
by
\tau_{0}^{\prime}>\tau_{0},
we can
assume that
u^{\prime}(\tau_{0})\in H.
Thus by applying Lemma
2.2
and
Fubini’s theorem to the equality (
3.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{0}}^{+\infty}\left\|u^{\prime}(t)\right\|_{V^{\prime}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{K}(1+\tau_{0})^{\alpha}\left\|u^{\prime}(\tau_{0})\right\|_{V^{\prime}}+\frac{2}{K}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{+\infty}(1+s)^{\alpha}\left\|g(s)\right\|_{V^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{K}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{+\infty}(1+s)^{\alpha}\left\|\nabla\Phi(u(s))\right\|_{V^{\prime}}ds.

Hence
\int_{\tau_{0}}^{+\infty}\left\|u^{\prime}(t)\right\|_{V^{\prime}}dt<+\infty
thanks to the continuous injection
H\hookrightarrow V^{\prime},
the hypothesis on
g,
and the estimate (
3.9
). Thus we deduce
that
u(t)
converges strongly in
V^{\prime}
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
to
some
u_{\infty}.
Recalling that, in view of Theorem
1.1
,
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},V)
and applying Lemma
2.3
, we infer
that
u(t)\rightarrow u_{\infty}
strongly in

which in view of

[
5
, Corollary 3.6]
implies that

converges strongly to
u_{\infty}
in

Finally, Theorem
1.1
ensures that

The proof is completed.

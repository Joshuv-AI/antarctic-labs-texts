Abstract
. Conformal dimension is a fundamental invariant of metric spaces, particularly suited to the study of self-similar spaces, such as spaces with an expanding self-covering (e.g. Julia sets of complex rational functions). The dynamics of these systems are encoded by the associated iterated monodromy groups, which are examples of contracting self-similar groups. Their amenability is a well-known open question. We show that if G G is an iterated monodromy group, and if the (Alfhors-regular) conformal dimension of the underlying space is strictly less than 2, then every symmetric random walk with finite second moment on G G has the Liouville property. As a corollary, every such group is amenable. This criterion applies to all examples of contracting groups previously known to be amenable, and to many new ones. In particular, it implies that for every sub-hyperbolic complex rational function f f whose Julia set is not the whole sphere, the iterated monodromy group of f f is amenable.



1.
Introduction

Conformal dimension was introduced in the late 1980s by P. Pansu in the study of quasi-isometries of hyperbolic spaces and related constructions (see, for example,
[
45
]
). The conformal dimension of a metric space is the infimum of the Hausdorff dimensions of spaces quasi-symmetric to it. Since quasi-isometries of hyperbolic spaces correspond to quasi-symmetries of their boundaries, the conformal dimension is a natural invariant of hyperbolic groups and their boundaries.
Since its definition, the conformal (and related Ahlfors-regular conformal) dimension has become an important invariant in geometric group theory and dynamical systems. See
[
34
]
for a survey of its properties and applications.

Ahlfors-regular conformal dimension is especially useful and natural for self-similar metric spaces. Boundaries of hyperbolic groups are examples of such self-similar spaces. Another natural class of examples is metric spaces with an expanding (branched) covering map
f\colon\mathcal{J}\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}
(for example Julia sets of hyperbolic complex rational functions).
Their quasi-conformal geometry is the subject of the works
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
by P. Haïssinsky and K. Pilgrim.

Expanding self-coverings are encoded by the associated
iterated monodromy groups
, introduced in the early 2000s, see
[
39
,
43
]
. They are examples of
contracting self-similar groups
. The self-covering is reconstructed from the iterated monodromy group as their
limit dynamical system
. The potential importance of the conformal dimension to the study of contracting self-similar groups was observed in
[
22
]
.

The amenability of iterated monodromy groups has been a well-known open question since their introduction. In fact, amenability of the iterated monodromy group of the complex polynomial
z^{2}-1
(also known as the
Basilica group
) was asked even before the iterated monodromy groups were defined (see
[
20
]
).

Amenability of the Basilica group group was proved by L. Bartholdi and B. Virág in
[
8
]
. Their proof was later generalized to include iterated monodromy groups of all post-critically finite complex polynomials in
[
6
]
(and more generally, all groups generated by
bounded
automata). Their methods were further extended in
[
2
]
to all groups generated by automata of
linear activity
(in the sense of S. Sidki
[
49
]
).

Amenability was proved in these papers by constructing a self-similar random walk step distribution
\mu
on a special family of “mother groups”, and using the self-similarity to prove that the asymptotic entropy (equivalently, speed) of the
\mu
-random walk is zero (see also
[
30
]
). By the results of V. Kaimanovich and A. Vershik
[
28
]
and Y. Derriennic
[
14
]
this implies the triviality of the Poisson boundary of the corresponding random walk, also known as the
Liouville property
that all bounded
\mu
-harmonic functions are constant, which in turn implies amenability of the group.

A different approach to prove the amenability of groups is based on the notion of extensively amenable actions, first introduced by K. Juschenko and N. Monod
[
26
]
. This method was used in
[
27
]
to show amenability of a class of iterated monodromy groups. This approach allows one to prove the amenability of iterated monodromy groups of complex rational functions with “crochet” Julia sets, and of all contracting self-simlar groups generated by automata of
polynomial activity
, see
[
37
]
. The framework of extensive amenability is not based on the study of the Liouville property for random walks, but random walks are implicitly present in it, as one of the main conditions used to prove amenability is the recurrence of the induced random walk on an orbit of some action of the group.

We show in our paper how conformal geometry can be used to prove the Liouville property for symmetric random walks under suitable moment conditions on many contracting groups (equivalently, iterated monodromy groups of expanding covering maps).

Namely, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a finitely generated contracting self-similar group, and suppose that its limit space has Ahlfors-regular conformal dimension
\alpha
, with
\alpha<2
. Then for every symmetric measure
\mu
on
G
having finite
\beta
-moment for some
\beta>\alpha
, the
\mu
-random walk is Liouville. In particular, any such group is amenable.

This implies the following (the relevant terminology on rational functions is recalled in §
2.4
).

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
f
be a sub-hyperbolic complex rational function. If the Julia set of
f
is not the whole sphere (in particular, if
f
is hyperbolic), then its iterated monodromy group is amenable.

Our results cover many new examples of amenable self-similar groups. Namely, all previously known contracting amenable groups had limit spaces of conformal dimension equal to 1 (this is true for any contracting group which can be generated by an automaton of polynomial activity, see Theorem
6.1
). In this case, Theorem
1.1
implies that any random walk on them generated by symmetric measures of finite
(1+\varepsilon)
-moment is Liouville, encompassing various previous results for some more restricted classes of groups and measures.
Beyond this case, Theorem
1.1
can be applied to contracting groups whose amenability was an open question until now, in particular to groups whose limit space is homeomorphic to the Sierpiński carpet. As shown in
[
23
]
, there exist hyperbolic rational maps with Sierpiński carpet Julia sets whose conformal dimensions are arbitrarily close to 2 (the corresponding groups must therefore be generated by automata of exponential activity).

Critical exponent and moment conditions

The moment condition on the measure in Theorem
1.1
is tightly related to another numeric invariant of a contracting self-similar group: its
critical exponent
. For every
p\in(0,\infty]
we define the
\ell^{p}
-contraction coefficient

\eta_{p}
of a finitely generated contracting group
(G,\mathfrak{B})
. It is the exponential rate of decay of the
\ell^{p}
-norm of the vector of lengths of the sections
g|_{v}
for the vertices
v
of the
n
th level of the tree. The condition for a self-similar group to be contracting is equivalent to the condition
\eta_{\infty}<1
. The function
p\mapsto\eta_{p}
is continuous and non-increasing. The critical exponent
p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
is the infimum of the values
p
for which
\eta_{p}<1
. It is a measure of the complexity of the self-similar group. For example, for any
\varepsilon>0
, the action on the tree of every element
g\in G
of length
n
can be explicitly described with
O(n^{p_{c}+\epsilon})
bits of information (more precisely, the
portrait
of
g
has size
O(n^{p_{c}+\epsilon})
, see Proposition
4.5
). In particular,
p_{c}<1
implies sub-exponential volume growth. In our setting, such complexity bounds can be used to control the contribution to entropy from long jumps of the random walk.
In analogy to critical constant for recurrence introduced in
[
17
]
, for a finitely generated group
G
equipped with a word metric, we define the (symmetric)
critical constant for the Liouville property
, denoted by
\rm{Cr}_{Liouv}(G)
, to be the infimum of
p
, such that all symmetric random walks with finite
p
-moment are Liouville. For a contracting group
(G,\mathfrak{B})
, we denote by
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}(G,\mathfrak{B})
the Ahlfors-regular conformal dimension of its limit space. Between these critical quantities and conformal dimension, we show the following inequality.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a finitely generated contracting self-similar group such that
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}(G,\mathfrak{B})<2
. Then

\rm{Cr}_{Liouv}(G)\leq p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})\leq\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}(G,\mathfrak{B}).

Then Theorem
1.1
above is a consequence of this inequality. The second inequality is not sharp in general, for example for the Grigorchuk group we have
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}(G,\mathfrak{B})=1
and
\rm{Cr}_{Liouv}(G)=p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})\simeq 0.7674
; see the discussion after Theorem
4.6
. The first inequality is strict for some examples where the group
G
is virtually nilpotent: for instance if
(G,\mathfrak{B})
is the
adding machine
(so that
G\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}
) then
\rm{Cr}_{Liouv}(G)=0
and
p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})=1
. We do not know whether this is essentially the only source of examples.

Further background and comparison with previous work

Let us give some further insight on previous results on amenability and the Liouville property for self-similar groups, and compare them with Theorem
1.1
. Recall that a contracting group
G
is generated by a finite state-automata over a finite alphabet
\mathsf{X}
, and has a natural action on the associated rooted tree
\mathsf{X}^{*}
whose vertex set consists of finite words. For every integer
n
, the action of an element
g\in G
on
\mathsf{X}^{*}
is determined by two pieces of information: the permutation induced on the
n
-th level of the tree, and associated collection of sections
(g|_{v})_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}
, which describe the action of
g
on the subtrees rooted at level
n
.

In order to show vanishing of the asymptotic entropy of the random walk associated to a measure
\mu
, one is immediately led to study the sections
\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v}
of a sample
\mathbf{g}_{t}=\mathbf{h}_{t}\cdots\mathbf{h}_{1}
of the
\mu
-random walk. More precisely, it is enough to show that the rate of growth of the sum of the entropies (or alternatively, the word lengths) of all sections
\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v}
at level
n
is bounded by some constant which tends to 0 as
n
tends to infinity (Proposition
5.5
). The sequence of sections
(\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v})_{t\geq 1}
satisfies the cocycle rule
\mathbf{g}_{t+1}|_{v}=(\mathbf{h}_{t+1}|_{\mathbf{g}_{t}(v)})\cdot(\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v})
, so in order to study it, one is also led to study the behaviour of the process
(\mathbf{g}_{t}(v))_{t\geq 1}
, which performs a random walk on the finite Schreier graph associated with the
n
-th level of the tree.

These observations (in various related formulations) have already been the starting point of previous results establishing the Liouville property for random walk on groups generated by automata of bounded or linear activity
[
8
,
6
,
2
,
1
]
. This means that for every element
s
in a generating set of the group, the number of vertices
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
such that the sections
s|_{v}
is non-trivial is bounded
[
8
,
6
,
1
]
, or grows linearly with
n

[
2
]
. This feature was used in
[
8
,
6
,
2
]
to construct special classes of self-similar random walks, for which the distribution of the process
\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v}
can be analysed by explicit recursive computations based on the form of the automaton. Self-similarity is a very restrictive condition in this case, and it is not clear how to construct such self-similar random walks in general (it seems that a finitely supported self-similar step distribution does not exist in most cases), so in
[
6
,
2
]
the corresponding groups have to be embedded into special “mother groups” for which such a self-similar random walk can be constructed.

For groups generated by bounded automata, this self-similarity restriction on the random walk was removed in
[
1
]
, by showing that every symmetric finitely supported measure generates a Liouville random walk. This was done by analysing explicitly the Schreier graphs of the action of the group on finite levels of the tree. In fact in this case, to understand the behaviour of the sections
\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v}
, it is enough to understand the visits of
(\mathbf{g}_{t}(v))
to some uniformly bounded subsets of “singular points” on the graphs (namely the vertices
w
such that the section
h|_{w}
is non-trivial for some
h
in the support of the measure). The length of sections
\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v}
is controlled by the number of
traverses
of the random walk between pairs of singular points lying far away on the Schreier graph, which in turn can be bounded by estimating the
\ell^{2}
-
capacity
(or
effective conductance
).

The existence of such special finite sets of “singular points” for each group element can be interpreted in terms of germs of the action on the boundary of the tree
\mathsf{X}^{\omega}
, and is also crucial for the application of the method of extensive amenability
[
26
,
27
,
3
,
37
]
. The idea of this method is to identify a large amenable subgroup
H
of the group whose action has no singular points, and then exploit recurrence of the Schreier graphs to control the expected number of singular points along the random walk, which allows to deduce amenability from the amenability of
H
.

For a typical group as in Theorem
1.1
, the approaches outlined above meet two related difficulties. First, in many cases (e.g. for the iterated mondromy group of a sufficiently general hyperbolic rational function), any explicit automaton generating the group will necessarily have a exponential activity and might be quite complicated; considerations based on the form of such an automaton would quickly become intractable. Second, there is no natural “finite set of singular points” associated to each group element (the set of singularities will rather correspond to a Cantor subset of the boundary of the tree). For a contracting self-similar group, the finiteness of the singular points seems to be related to the existence of countable separating subsets in the limit space. This is a restrictive condition limited to examples with limit space of conformal dimension 1, see
[
37
]
.

Outline of the proof

Our proof of Theorem
1.3
also proceeds by reducing the problem to the analysis of random walk on Schreier graphs, but this is achieved by establishing direct relationships between the properties of a contracting self-similar group, and those of its limit space.
We crucially use the fact that the finite Schreier graphs can be seen as approximations of the limit space. There are three main ingredients in our proof.

The first ingredient, purely geometric, is Theorem
2.16
, which estimates the total length of
n
-th level sections of a word
g=s_{t}\cdots s_{1}
in terms of the paths that the word describes on the corresponding Schreier graph. It says that in order for the total length of sections to be large, these paths must traverse many times between certain regions which approximate some disjoint parts of the limit space (which roughly speaking correspond to different
tiles
). The precise identification of these regions requires the framework of
contracting models
developed in
[
42
,
44
]
.

The second ingredient is Theorem
5.7
, where the assumption that the conformal dimension of the limit space is less than 2 is used to show that the
\ell^{2}
-capacity between such disjoint regions must tend to 0 as
n\to\infty
. This is reminiscent of the results of M. Carrasco
[
11
]
and J. Kigami
[
31
]
, which relate the conformal dimension of a metric space to potential theory on graphs that approximate the space (although our proof of Theorem
5.7
is self-contained).
These two ingredients put us in a position to apply a traverse-counting argument to show the vanishing of random walk asymptotic entropy, at least for symmetric finitely supported measures.

The third ingredient is the study of critical exponent (as described above), which allows us to go beyond the case of finitely supported measures.

On return probability

Let us also mention that it is known that for symmetric random walks with finite second moment, sufficiently slow decay of return probability, namely
\mu^{(2n)}(id)
decaying slower than
\exp(-n^{1/2})
, implies that the
\mu
-random walk is Liouville, see
[
46
]
. However this result is not applicable in the study of contracting self-similar groups: first, it is not clear for what subclass of groups one can expect such a slow decay, secondly, even in the case of bounded automata groups, it is challenging to prove lower estimates for return probability. Indeed, to the best our knowledge, for all contracting groups whose amenability has been proven so far and those covered by Theorem
1.1
, the best known lower bound for
\mu^{(2n)}(id)
comes from entropy, that is,
\mu^{(2n)}(id)\geq\exp(-H(X_{2n}))
. It is an open problem to find methods to establish sharper lower estimates for return probability, even in examples such as the Basilica group, and a tightly related problem is to find contracting groups that are amenable but not Liouville for symmetric simple random walks (see §
6.4
for an example of non-Liouville contracting group).

Organisation of the article

Section 2 is devoted to the theory of self-similar contracting groups and their limit spaces. After recalling various preliminaries from this theory, we prove Theorem
2.16
, which is at the core of the first ingredient described above.

In Section 3 we describe the quasi-conformal structure of the limit space of a contracting self-similar group and introduce its Ahlfors-regular conformal dimension. The limit space can be seen as the boundary of a natural Gromov-hyperbolic graph (the
self-similarity complex
of the group), and therefore can be endowed with the associated visual metrics. Similarly to the case of boundaries of hyperbolic groups, its Ahlfors-regular conformal dimension is defined as the infimum of the Hausdorff dimensions of Ahlfors-regular metrics quasisymmetric to a visual metric.

Section 4 discusses the
critical exponent

p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
for contracting self-similar groups as mentioned above. We prove that the critical exponent
p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
of a contracting group is not larger than the conformal dimension
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}(G,\mathfrak{B})
, see Theorem
4.6
.
Lower estimates for
p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
, and hence also for
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}(G,\mathfrak{B})
, can be obtained from exhibiting a carefully chosen sequence of group elements. In Theorem
4.10
, we show a relation between
\ell^{p}
-contraction and the spectral radius of the linear operator
T_{p}
, where
T_{p}
is the Thurston linear transform adapted to
\ell^{p}
calculations.

Section 5 is devoted to random walk, and contains the proof of the main result on the Liouville property. It starts with a brief review of preliminaries on random walk and electric network theory, bounded harmonic functions, and the entropy criterion. We then show a direct bound on
\ell^{q}
-capacity on the Schreier graphs of the action of a contracting group
(G,\mathfrak{B})
on the levels of the tree, which is valid for
q>\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}(G,\mathfrak{B})
and for all symmetric measures which admit a finite moment of order
p>p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
, see Proposition
5.6
, from which the estimates on capacity stated in Theorem
5.7
follow. We then give a proof of the Liouville property for the finite support case based on traverse counting and
\ell^{2}
-capacity estimates. For the general case under finite
p
-moment,
p>p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
, we first apply a geometric argument similar to the proof of Theorem
2.16
, then random walk entropy is bounded by contributions from traverses and long jumps separately. The former is controlled by
\ell^{2}
-capacity estimates in the same way as in the finite supported case; and the latter is controlled by
\ell^{p}
-contraction.

Section 6 contains several examples of applications of the main theorem. The first class of examples are groups generated by automata of
polynomial activity growth
, as defined by S. Sidki in
[
49
]
. It was shown in
[
37
]
that limit spaces of all contracting self-similar groups in this class have Ahlfors-regular conformal dimension 1. Amenability of such groups also follows (as explained in
[
37
]
) from the theory of extensively amenable actions, developed in
[
27
,
25
]
(the Liouville property for all groups in this class seems to be new even for finitely supported symmetric measures).

The second class of examples is iterated monodromy groups of sub-hyperbolic complex rational functions. If
f
is a sub-hyperbolic rational function, then the limit space of its iterated monodromy group
\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f\right)}
is canonically homeomorphic to the Julia set of
f
. Moreover, the spherical metric restricted to the Julia set, seen as a metric on the limit space of the group, is quasi-conformal and Ahlfors-regular. In particular, the Hausdorff dimension of the Julia set is an upper bound of the conformal dimension of the limit space of the iterated monodromy group.
It follows that if the Julia set of the function is not the whole sphere, then the iterated monodromy group is Liouville (for every symmetric measure with finite second moment) and thus amenable.

We provide an explicit example of the iterated monodromy group of a rational function whose Julia set is homeomorphic to the Sierpiński carpet (and thus can not be generated by an automaton of polynomial activity growth).

We also describe explicitly a contracting self-similar group whose limit space coincides with the classical Sierpiński carpet (i.e., quasi-symmetric to it).

Finally, we give an example of a finitely generated self-similar contracting group, which is not Liouville for any finitely supported non-degenerate measure.

2.
Self-similar groups and their limit spaces

2.1.
Basic definitions

Let
\mathsf{X}
be a finite alphabet. The set of finite words
\mathsf{X}^{\ast}
over
\mathsf{X}
is naturally a rooted tree, with root the empty word
\varnothing
, where each
w\in\mathsf{X}^{\ast}
is connected by an edge to
wx
for every
x\in\mathsf{X}
. We say that
w\in\mathsf{X}^{*}
is
below

v\in\mathsf{X}^{*}
(or a
descendant
of
v
) if
w=vu
for some
u\in\mathsf{X}^{\ast}
.

Definition 2.1
.

An action of a group
G
on
\mathsf{X}^{*}
by is said to be
self-similar
if for every
g\in G
and
v\in\mathsf{X}^{*}
, there exists an element
g|_{v}\in G
such that

g(vw)=g(v)g|_{v}(w)

for all
w\in\mathsf{X}^{*}
.

Note that a self-similar action is in particular an action by automorphisms on the rooted tree
\mathsf{X}^{\ast}
.

Consider a faithful self-similar action of
G
on
\mathsf{X}^{*}
. The
biset of the action
is the set
\mathfrak{B}
of transformations of
\mathsf{X}^{*}
of the form

x\cdot g\colon v\mapsto xg(v)

for
x\in\mathsf{X}
and
g\in G
. Since the action is faithful, the set
\mathfrak{B}
is in a natural bijection with the set
\mathsf{X}\times G
.

The set
\mathfrak{B}
is closed under pre- and post-compositions with elements of
G
, hence it is a
biset
, i.e., a set with commuting left and right actions of
G
. They are given by the rules

(x\cdot g)\cdot h=x\cdot gh,\qquad h\cdot(x\cdot g)=h(x)\cdot h|_{x}g.

The right action of
G
on the biset
\mathfrak{B}
is free and has
|\mathsf{X}|
orbits, where
\mathsf{X}\cdot\{1\}\subset\mathfrak{B}
(naturally identified with
\mathsf{X}
) intersects each orbit exactly once.

In general, a
self-similar group

(G,\mathfrak{B})
is a group
G
and a biset
\mathfrak{B}
such that the right action is free and has finitely many orbits. A subset
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathfrak{B}
is called a
basis
of the biset if it intersects each orbit exactly once.

If
\mathfrak{B}_{1},\mathfrak{B}_{2}
are two
G
-bisets, then the biset
\mathfrak{B}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{B}_{2}
is the quotient of
\mathfrak{B}_{1}\times\mathfrak{B}_{2}
by the identification
(x\cdot g,y)\sim(x,g\cdot y)
and with the actions on the quotient induced by the actions
g\cdot(x,y)\cdot h=(g\cdot x,y\cdot h)
. We denote the image of the element
(x,y)\in\mathfrak{B}_{1}\times\mathfrak{B}_{2}
in
\mathfrak{B}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{B}_{2}
by
x\otimes y
or just by
xy
.

This operation is associative up to isomorphisms of bisets. In particular, for every biset
\mathfrak{B}
we get bisets
\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}
for all
n\geq 0
. Here
\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes 0}
is defined as
G
with the natural left and right
G
-actions.

If
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathfrak{B}
is a basis of a biset, then every element of
\mathfrak{B}
is written in a unique way as
x\cdot g
for
x\in\mathsf{X}
and
g\in G
. For every
g\in G
and every
x\in G
there exist unique
y\in\mathsf{X}
and
h\in G
such that

g\cdot x=y\cdot h.

It is easy to see that the map
x\mapsto y
is a permutation of
\mathsf{X}
(since the left
G
-action permutes the right
G
-orbits). We get the
associated action
of
G
on
\mathsf{X}
, and denote
y=g(x)
,
h=g|_{x}
. The map
g\mapsto\sigma_{g}(g|_{x})_{x\in\mathsf{X}}\in\mathop{Symm}(\mathsf{X})\ltimes G^{\mathsf{X}}
, where
\sigma_{g}\in\mathop{Symm}(\mathsf{X})
is the image of
g
under the associated action on
\mathsf{X}
, is called the
wreath recursion
. It is a homomorphism from
G
to the wreath product
\mathop{Symm}(\mathsf{X})\ltimes G^{\mathsf{X}}
.

Changing the basis
\mathsf{X}
corresponds to composing the wreath recursion with an inner automorphism of the wreath product.

If
\mathsf{X}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{B}
, then
\mathsf{X}^{\otimes n}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}
. We identify the set
\mathsf{X}^{\otimes n}
with
\mathsf{X}^{n}
in the natural way. Every element of
\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}
is written in a unique way as
v\cdot g
for
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
and
g\in G
.

In particular, for every
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}\subset\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}
and for every
g\in G
there exist unique
u\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
and
g|_{v}\in G
such that

g\cdot v=u\cdot g|_{v}.

The map
v\mapsto u
is a permutation of
\mathsf{X}^{n}
(conjugate to the permutation induced by
g
on the set of right orbits of
\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}
), so we denote
u=g(v)
.

We get hence the
associated action
of
G
on
\mathsf{X}^{*}
.
The associated action on
\mathsf{X}^{*}
does not depend, up to conjugacy, on the choice of the basis
\mathsf{X}
(since it is conjugate to the left action of
G
on
\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}/G
). The
faithful quotient
of a self-similar group
(G,\mathfrak{B})
is the quotient of
G
by the kernel of the associated action.

Note that if
(G,\mathfrak{B})
is a self-similar group and
m\geq 1
, then
(G,\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes m})
is a self-similar group with the same faithful quotient. Indeed the associated action of
G
on the tree
(\mathsf{X}^{m})^{*}
identifies with the restriction of the original action of
G
on the levels of
\mathsf{X}^{\ast}
that are multiple of
m
. For many arguments, there is no loss of generality in replacing
(G,\mathfrak{B})
by some iterate
(G,\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes m})
, as we shall often do.

2.2.
Contracting groups and limit space

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a self-similar group. Let
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathfrak{B}
be a basis. The self-similar group is said to be
contracting
if there exists a finite set
\mathcal{N}\subset G
such that for every
g\in G
there exists
n
such that

g|_{v}\in\mathcal{N}

for all
v\in\mathsf{X}^{m}
for all
m\geq n
. The smallest set
\mathcal{N}
satisfying this condition is called the
nucleus
of the group. It is shown in
[
39
, Corollary 2.11.7]
that the property to be contracting does not depend on the choice of the basis
\mathsf{X}
. The nucleus, however, depends on
\mathsf{X}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a contracting group. Choose a basis
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathfrak{B}
, and let
\mathsf{X}^{-\omega}
be the space of left-infinite sequences
\ldots x_{2}x_{1}
with the direct product topology (where
\mathsf{X}
is discrete). The
limit space

\mathcal{J}_{G}
is the quotient of
\mathsf{X}^{-\omega}
by the
asymptotic equivalence relation
, identifying two sequences
\ldots x_{2}x_{1},\ldots y_{2}y_{1}
if and only if there exists a sequence
g_{n}
of elements of the nucleus
\mathcal{N}
such that
g_{n}(x_{n}\ldots x_{2}x_{1})=y_{n}\ldots y_{2}y_{1}
.

It is shown in
[
39
]
that the asymptotic equivalence relation is transitive, and that the limit space is a metrizable compact space of finite topological dimension.

It follows from the definition that the asymptotic equivalence relation is invariant under the shift
\ldots x_{2}x_{1}\mapsto\ldots x_{3}x_{2}
. Consequently, the shift induces a continuous map
\mathsf{s}\colon\mathcal{J}_{G}\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}_{G}
, which we call the
limit dynamical system
. It is shown in
[
39
]
that the limit dynamical system does not depend, up to topological conjugacy, on the choice of the basis
\mathsf{X}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
and
\mathsf{X}
be as above. The
limit
G
-space

\mathcal{X}_{G}
is the quotient of the direct product
\mathsf{X}^{-\omega}\times G
(where
G
is discrete) by the equivalence relation identifying
\ldots x_{2}x_{1}\cdot g
and
\ldots y_{2}y_{1}\cdot h
if and only if there exists a sequence
g_{n}
of elements of the nucleus such that

g_{n}\cdot x_{n}\ldots x_{2}x_{1}\cdot g=y_{n}\ldots y_{2}y_{1}\cdot h

in
\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}
for all
n
.

The equality in the definition of
\mathcal{X}_{G}
is equivalent to the equalities

g_{n}(x_{n}\ldots x_{2}x_{1})=y_{n}\ldots y_{2}y_{1},\qquad g_{n}|_{x_{n}\ldots x_{2}x_{1}}g=h.

We have a natural right action of
G
on
\mathcal{X}_{G}
induced by the action
(\ldots x_{2}x_{1}\cdot g)\cdot h=\ldots x_{2}x_{1}\cdot gh
on
\mathsf{X}^{-\omega}\times G
. One can show that the space
\mathcal{X}_{G}
and the action do not depend (up to topological conjugacy) on the choice of the basis
\mathsf{X}
.

Definition 2.4
.

We denote by
{\mathcal{T}}
the image of
\mathsf{X}^{-\omega}
in
\mathcal{X}_{G}
.

The action of
G
on
\mathcal{X}_{G}
is proper and co-compact with the quotient
\mathcal{X}_{G}/G
naturally homeomorphic to the limit space
\mathcal{J}_{G}
. The set
{\mathcal{T}}
is compact and intersects every
G
-orbit.

Definition 2.5
.

For
v\in\mathsf{X}^{*}
, we denote by
{\mathcal{T}}_{v}
the image of the set
\mathsf{X}^{-\omega}v
of sequences ending with
v
in the limit space
\mathcal{J}_{G}
. We call it a
tile of
n
th level
if
n=|v|
.

For every point
\xi\in\mathcal{J}_{G}
and every

, the union of the tiles of the

th level containing

is a neighborhood of
\xi
. The following is
[
39
, Proposition 3.5.8]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Two tiles
{\mathcal{T}}_{v},{\mathcal{T}}_{u}
of the same level intersect if and only if there exists an element
g
of the nucleus such that
g(v)=u
.

2.3.
Contracting models

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a contracting self-similar group, and let
\mathcal{X}
be a proper metric space on which
G
acts properly and co-compactly by isometries from the right.

Then
\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathfrak{B}
is defined as the quotient of
\mathcal{X}\times\mathfrak{B}
(where
\mathfrak{B}
is discrete) by the action
(\xi,x)\mapsto(\xi\cdot g^{-1},g\cdot x)
. We denote the orbit of
(\xi,x)
by
\xi\otimes x
. We have then
\xi\otimes g\cdot x=\xi\cdot g\otimes x
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{X},x\in\mathfrak{B}
, and
g\in G
.

Let
\mathsf{X}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{B}
. Assume that
I\colon\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathfrak{B}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
is a
G
-equivariant map. Then
I
is uniquely determined by the maps
I_{x}(\xi)=I(\xi\otimes x)
, which satisfy the following condition:

(1)

I_{x}(\xi\cdot g)=I_{g(x)}(\xi)\cdot g|_{x},

for all
x\in\mathsf{X}
,
g\in G
, and
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
, where
g(x)\in\mathsf{X}
and
g|_{x}\in G
, as usual, are given by
g\cdot x=g(x)\cdot g|_{x}
.

Conversely, every collection of maps
I_{x}\colon\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
satisfying (
1
) defines an equivariant map
I\colon\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathfrak{B}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
by the formula
I(\xi\otimes x\cdot g)=I_{x}(\xi)\cdot g
.

Every equivariant map
I\colon\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathfrak{B}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
induces an equivariant map
I_{n}\colon\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes(n+1)}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}
by the rule

I_{n}(\xi\otimes x\otimes v)=I(\xi\otimes x)\otimes v

for
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
,
x\in\mathfrak{B}
,
v\in\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}
.
According to this, we will sometimes denote
I
by
I_{0}
.

We also denote by
I^{n}\colon\xi\otimes\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
the composition
I_{n-1}\circ I_{n-2}\circ\cdots\circ I_{1}\circ I
.

Definition 2.7
.

A
G
-equivariant map
I\colon\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathfrak{B}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
is said to be
contracting
if there exist
n\geq 1
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that
d(I^{n}(\xi_{1}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{2}\otimes v))\leq\lambda d(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
and
v\in\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}
.

A
G
-equivariant map
I\colon\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathfrak{B}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
is contracting if and only if there exist
C>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that

d(I^{n}(\xi_{1}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{2}\otimes v))\leq C\lambda^{n}d(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})

for all
n
,
v\in\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}
,
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
.

The natural map
\mathcal{X}_{G}\otimes\mathfrak{B}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{G}
mapping
\xi\otimes(x\cdot h)
, where
x\in\mathsf{X},h\in G
, and
\xi
is represented by
\ldots x_{2}x_{1}\cdot g\in\mathsf{X}^{-\omega}\times G
to the point of
\mathcal{X}_{G}
represented by
\ldots x_{2}x_{1}g(x)\cdot g|_{x}h
, is a
G
-equivariant
homeomorphism

\mathcal{X}_{G}\otimes\mathfrak{B}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{G}
. We will denote the image of
\xi\otimes x
just by
\xi\otimes x
, thus identifying
\mathcal{X}_{G}\otimes\mathfrak{B}
with
\mathcal{X}_{G}
(this slight abuse of notation is justified by the fact that
x_{1}\cdot g\otimes x\cdot h\mapsto x_{1}\otimes g(x)\cdot g|_{x}h
is the identical map on
\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes 2}
).

It is proved in
[
44
, Theorem 2.10]
that the natural homeomorphism
\mathcal{X}_{G}\otimes\mathfrak{B}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{G}
is contracting in the sense of Definition
2.7
(with respect to a natural distance on
\mathcal{X}_{G}
) provided
G
is finitely generated.

The following theorem is proved in
[
42
]
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
I\colon\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathfrak{B}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
be a contracting
G
-equivariant map. Then the inverse limit of the sequence of
G
-spaces
\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}
and maps
I_{n}
is
G
-equivariantly homeomorphic to the limit
G
-space
\mathcal{X}_{G}
. The map on the limit induced by the maps
I_{n}
is conjugated by the homeomorphism with the natural homeomorphism
\mathcal{X}_{G}\otimes\mathfrak{B}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{G}
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a contracting self-similar group. A
contracting model
of
(G,\mathfrak{B})
is a proper co-compact action of
G
on a metric space
\mathcal{X}
by isometries together with a contracting equivariant map
I\colon\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathfrak{B}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
.

It is proved in
[
42
]
(see also
[
44
, Theorem 3.1]
) that for every finitely generated contracting group
(G,\mathfrak{B})
there exists
n_{0}
such that
(G,\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n_{0}})
has a contracting model
I\colon\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n_{0}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
where
\mathcal{X}
is a connected simplicial complex and the maps

are piecewise affine.

We shall say that a metric space
(\mathcal{X},d)
is
almost geodesic
if there exists
C>0
such that for every
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{X}
, there exists a sequence of points
x_{0}=\xi,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}=\eta
such that
d(x_{i},x_{i+1})\leq C
and

\lvert d(\xi,\eta)-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}d(x_{i},x_{i+1})\rvert\leq C.

It is easy to see (and it follows e.g. from Theorem 4.5.C and Corollary 4.B.11 in
[
12
]
) that a proper almost geodesic space
\mathcal{X}
satisfies the Schwarz--Milnor lemma

1

In the most common formulation of the Schwarz–Milnor lemma,
\mathcal{X}
is supposed to be a length space in the usual sense. Note also that an almost geodesic space is in particular

in the sense of

: if a group
G
acts properly, isometrically and co-compactly on

is finitely generated and for any word metric on

We will need the following simple lemma.

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose that
G
is finitely generated, and let
l(g)
denote the length of an element
g\in G
with respect to a fixed finite generating set of
G
. Choose a basis
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathfrak{B}
. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a proper metric space on which
G
acts by isometries from the right so that the action is co-compact and proper, and let
I\colon\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathfrak{B}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
be a
G
-equivariant map.

(i)

If
I
is contracting, then for every compact subset
\mathcal{T}_{0}\subset\mathcal{X}
, there exists
\mathcal{T}\supset\mathcal{T}_{0}
compact such that
I^{n}(\xi\otimes v)\in\mathcal{T}
for every
n\geq 1,\xi\in\mathcal{T}
and
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
.

(ii)

Suppose that
\mathcal{X}
is an almost geodesic space, and let
\mathcal{T}
be any compact set satisfying the conclusion of point
(i)
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

l(g|_{v})\leq Cd(I^{n}(\xi\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi\cdot g\otimes v))+C

for every
n\geq 1
,
g\in G
,
\xi\in{\mathcal{T}}
, and
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
.

Proof.

(i)
We take
\mathcal{T}
to be the closure of the set of points of the form
I^{|v|}(\xi\otimes v)
for
\xi\in\mathcal{T}_{0}
and
v\in\mathsf{X}^{*}
(compare with the proof of
[
42
, Theorem 5.9]
). Let us check that it has finite diameter (and hence it is compact). Fix
m\geq 1
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that
d(I^{m}(\xi_{1}\otimes v),I^{m}(\xi_{2}\otimes v))\leq\lambda d(\xi_{1},x_{2})
for all
v\in\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes m}
. For
k\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{T}_{k}
be the set of points of the form
I^{|v|}(\xi\otimes v)
for
\xi\in\mathcal{T}_{0}
and
\lvert v\rvert\leq mk
, and note that
\mathcal{T}_{k}\subset\mathcal{T}_{k+1}
and that
\mathcal{T}
is the closure of
\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{T}_{k}
. Pick a basepoint
\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{T}_{0}
, and let
C>0
be an upper bound of the diameter of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
. For
k\geq 1
, if
\xi\in\mathcal{T}_{k+1}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{k}
, then
\xi=I(\xi^{\prime}\otimes v)
for some

, and we have

d(\xi,\xi_{0})\leq d(\xi_{0},I(\xi_{0}\otimes v))+d(I(\xi^{\prime}\otimes v),I(\xi_{0}\otimes x))\leq C+\lambda d(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{0}),

where we have used that
\xi_{0},I(\xi_{0}\otimes v)\in\mathcal{T}_{1}

is contained in the ball centered at

, and hence

has diameter bounded by

.

(ii)
Since
\mathcal{X}
is an almost geodesic space, the Schwarz–Milnor lemma and compactness of

It follows that
l(g|_{v})\leq Cd(I^{n}(\xi\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi\otimes g(v))\cdot g|_{v})+C=Cd(I^{n}(\xi\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi\cdot g\otimes v)+C

2.4.
Iterated monodromy groups

Definition 2.11
.

A
virtual endomorphism
of a topological space
\mathcal{J}
is a finite degree covering map
f\colon\mathcal{J}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}
together with a continuous map
\phi\colon\mathcal{J}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}
.

Let
f,\phi\colon\mathcal{J}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}
be a virtual endomorphism. Suppose that
\mathcal{J}
is path connected. Consider the fundamental group
G=\pi_{1}(\mathcal{J},t)
. The
associated biset
is the set
\mathfrak{B}
of pairs
(z,[\delta])
, where
z\in f^{-1}(t)
, and
[\delta]
is the homotopy class of a path
\delta
in
\mathcal{J}
connecting
t
to
\phi(z)
. The action of
G
on
\mathfrak{B}
is given by the formulas

(z,[\delta])\cdot[\gamma]=(z,[\delta\gamma]),\qquad[\gamma](z,[\delta])=(y,[\phi(\gamma_{z})\delta]),

where
\gamma
is a loop based at
t
,
\gamma_{z}
is the lift of
\gamma
by
f
starting in
z
,
y
is the end of
\gamma_{z}
. We multiply paths as functions: in a product
\alpha\beta
the path
\beta
is passed before
\alpha
.

The
iterated monodromy group
of the virtual endomorphism is the faithful quotient of the self-similar group
(G,\mathfrak{B})
.

By the definition of the associated biset, a basis of
\mathfrak{B}
is a collection

\{(z_{1},[\ell_{1}]),(z_{2},[\ell_{2}]),\ldots,(z_{n},[\ell_{n}]\},

where
\{z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}\}=f^{-1}(t)
, and
\ell_{i}
is a path from
t
to
\phi(z_{i})
. Let us denote
x_{i}=(z_{i},[\ell_{i}])
. Then, as shown in
[
42
, Theorem 4.5]
, the action of the iterated monodromy group on the tree
\mathsf{X}^{*}
for
\mathsf{X}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
is given by the recurrent formula

(2)

[\gamma](x_{i}w)=x_{j}[\ell_{j}^{-1}\phi(\gamma_{i})\ell_{i}](w),

where
\gamma_{i}
is the unique lift of
\gamma
by
f
starting in
z_{i}
, and
j
is such that
z_{j}
is the end of
\gamma_{i}
.

A particular case of this situation is a
partial self-covering
, i.e., a finite degree covering map
f\colon\mathcal{J}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}
where
\mathcal{J}_{1}\subset\mathcal{J}
. It is a virtual endomorphism with the map
\phi\colon\mathcal{J}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}
equal to the identical embedding. Then the
iterated monodromy group
of
f
is the iterated monodromy group of the virtual endomorphism
f,\phi\colon\mathcal{J}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}
. In this case, it is natural to omit
\phi
(as it is the identity map) in the formula (
2
).

An important class of examples consists of
sub-hyperbolic complex rational functions
.

Let
f(z)\in\mathbb{C}(z)
be a rational function seen as a self-map of the Riemannian sphere
\mathbb{PC}^{1}
. Denote by
P_{f}
the closure of the union of the forward orbits of the
critical values
of
f
(i.e., values at the critical points of the map
f\colon\mathbb{PC}^{1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{PC}^{1}
).

The function
f
is said to be
sub-hyperbolic
if it is expanding with respect to some orbifold metric on a neighborhood of its Julia set, see
[
36
, §19]
. If such a metric can be chosen to be a usual Riemannian metric (i.e. with no non-trivial orbifold singularities), then
f
is said to be
hyperbolic
. By a theorem of A. Douady and J.H. Hubbard (see
[
36
, Theorem 19.6]
) a rational function
f
is sub-hyperbolic if and only if the orbit of every critical point is either finite or converges to an
attracting cycle
, i.e. a periodic orbit
f\colon x_{1}\mapsto\cdots\mapsto x_{d}\mapsto x_{1}
such that
\prod_{i}|f^{\prime}(x_{i})|<1
(it is hyperbolic if and only if all critical points converge to an attracting cycle). In particular,
f
is sub-hyperbolic provided
P_{f}
is finite (such functions are called
post-critically finite
).
A sub-hyperbolic function induces a partial self-covering

f\colon\mathbb{PC}^{1}\setminus f^{-1}(P_{f})\to\mathbb{PC}^{1}\setminus P_{f}.

(Note that
P_{f}
and
f^{-1}(P_{f})
are countable closed sets with finitely many accumulation points, in particular their complements are connected manifolds). By definition, the iterated monodromy group
\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f\right)}
is the iterated monodromy group of this partial self-covering.

The following result is a consequence of the orbifold version of Theorem
2.8
(see
[
39
, Theorem 5.5.3]
and
[
43
, Theorem 4.5.36]
).

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
f
be a sub-hyperbolic (e.g., post-critically finite) complex rational function. Then its iterated monodromy group
G=\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f\right)}
is contracting and its limit dynamical system
\mathsf{s}\colon\mathcal{J}_{G}\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}_{G}
is topologically conjugate to the restriction of
f
to its Julia set.

Proof sketch.

If the function
f
is post-critically finite, this is exactly
[
39
, Theorem 6.4.4]
. We briefly explain how to apply
[
39
, Theorem 5.5.3]
to a general sub-hyperbolic function, since that result assumes that the fundamental group of the base space is finitely generated, and this is not the case of
\mathbb{PC}^{1}\setminus P_{f}
when
P_{f}
is infinite. This issue can be bypassed as follows.
Fix finitely many small open discs
D_{1},\cdots,D_{r}\subset\mathbb{PC}^{1}
, with each
\overline{D}_{i}
contained in the basin of attraction some attracting cycle, whose union
D_{1}\cup\cdots\cup D_{k}
contains all points in the attracting cycles and all post-critical points converging to them, and is forward
f
-invariant. One can find such disks
D_{i}
, for example, using the
Böttcher coordinate
uniformizing the Fatou components
[
36
, §9]
, see for example the proof of
[
40
, Theorem 6.6]
. Then the complement
\mathcal{M}
of
D_{1}\cup\ldots\cup D_{r}
is a compact neighbourhood of the Julia set such that
f^{-1}(\mathcal{M})\subset\mathcal{M}
. There is a natural structure of
Thurston orbifold
and an orbifold metric on
\mathcal{M}
with respect to which
f\colon f^{-1}(\mathcal{M})\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}
is a uniformly expanding partial self-covering, see
[
36
, §19]
and
[
43
, § 4.7.1]
. Let us denote by
\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f,\mathcal{M}\right)}
its iterated monodromy group. Theorem
[
39
, Theorem 5.5.3]
(or alternatively
[
42
, Theorem 5.10]
) can be applied to this new self-covering, and implies that the group
\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f,\mathcal{M}\right)}
is contracting and its limit dynamical system is conjugate to the restriction of
f
to its Julia set.

If we choose the basepoint and the connecting paths
\ell_{x}
inside
\mathcal{M}
and compute the iterated monodromy groups using (
2
), then
the group
\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f,\mathcal{M}\right)}
will be realized as a subgroup of
\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f\right)}
, acting on the same rooted tree
\mathsf{X}^{\ast}
. For every compact set
C\subset\mathbb{PC}^{1}\setminus P_{f}
there exists

such that

(since each
f^{n}(\overline{D}_{i})
is disjoint from
C
for
n
sufficiently large). Consequently (
2
) implies that for every element
g\in\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f\right)}
there exists

such that
g|_{v}\in\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f,\mathcal{M}\right)}
for all

. In particular, the self-similar group
\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f\right)}
is contracting with the same nucleus as
\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f,\mathcal{M}\right)}
, and hence it also has the same limit dynamical system. ∎

The following theorem is proved in

Theorem 2.13

Suppose that the limit space

n_{0}
and a contracting model
I\colon\Delta_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n_{0}}\longrightarrow\Delta_{0}
, where
\Delta_{0}
is a locally finite connected graph on which
G
acts properly and co-compactly by automorphisms.

Below we suppose to be in the situation of Theorem
2.13
. After replacing
\mathfrak{B}
by
\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n_{0}}
, we assume that
n_{0}=1
.

Denote
\Delta_{n}=\Delta_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}
. Since the action of
G
on
\Delta_{0}
is by automorphisms of graphs, the spaces
\Delta_{n}
are also graphs.

Denote by
\Gamma_{n}
the quotient
\Delta_{n}/G
and by
\pi_{n}\colon\Delta_{n}\to\Gamma_{n}
the quotient projection. Then
\Gamma_{n}
is a finite graph. The maps
I_{n}\colon\Delta_{n+1}\longrightarrow\Delta_{n}
induce continuous maps
\phi_{n}\colon\Gamma_{n+1}\longrightarrow\Gamma_{n}
. We may assume that in the original action of
G
on
\Delta_{0}
, no pair of neighbouring vertices of
\Delta_{0}
belong to the same
G
-orbit (otherwise pass to the barycentric subdivision of
\Delta_{0}
). In particular,
\Gamma_{0}
has no loops.

Notation 2.14
.

Denote by
U_{z}
the ball of radius 1/3 with center in a vertex
z
of the graph
\Gamma_{0}=\Delta_{0}/G
. It is a bouquet of segments of length 1/3. (We use the combinatorial distance in the graphs, identifying each edge with a real segment of length 1.)

Fix a basepoint
\xi_{0}\in\Delta_{0}
.
Denote by
U_{z,n}
the set of elements
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
such that
\pi_{0}(I^{n}(\xi_{0}\otimes v))\in U_{z}
.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
s_{t}s_{t-1}\ldots s_{1}
be a word in elements
s_{i}\in G
(formally, an element of the free group with basis
G
). Let
z_{1},z_{2}
be vertices of
\Gamma_{0}
. A
traverse of level
n
from
z_{1}
to
z_{2}
of the word
s_{t}s_{t-1}\ldots s_{1}
is a quadruple
(i,j,v_{1},v_{2})
, where
1\leq i\leq j\leq t
,
v_{1}\in U_{z_{1},n}
,
v_{2}\in U_{z_{2},n}
for
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
are such that
s_{k}\cdots s_{i+1}s_{i}(v_{1})\notin U_{z_{1},n}\cup U_{z_{2},n}
for all
i<k<j
, and
s_{j}s_{j-1}\cdots s_{i}(v_{1})=v_{2}
.

In other words, a traverse is a segment of the word
s_{t}s_{t-1}\ldots s_{1}
corresponding to a travel from a point of
U_{z_{1},n}
to a point of
U_{z_{2},n}
such that the sets
U_{z_{1},n}
and
U_{z_{2},n}
are not touched during the travel.

We denote by
\tau_{n}(s_{t}s_{t-1}\ldots s_{1})
the total number of traverses of level
n
for the word
s_{t}s_{t-1}\ldots s_{1}
.

Theorem 2.16
.

Retain all previous assumptions and notation. Let
S
be a finite generating set for
G
.
There exists
C>1
such that for all
n
big enough and every element
g=s_{t}s_{t-1}\cdots s_{1}\in G
, where
s_{i}\in S
, we have

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}l(g|_{v})\leq C\tau_{n}(s_{t}s_{t-1}\cdots s_{1})+C|\mathsf{X}^{n}|.

Proof.

By item
(i)
in Lemma
2.10
, we may choose a compact set
\mathcal{T}\ni\xi_{0}
such that
I^{n}(\xi\otimes v)\in\mathcal{T}
for every
\xi\in\mathcal{T}
and every
n,v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
. Then item
(ii)
in the same lemma (which can be applied because a connected graph is an almost geodesic space) implies that there exists
C_{1}>0
such that for every
g\in G
and
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
, the length of
g|_{v}
is bounded from above by
C_{1}d(I^{n}(\xi_{0}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{0}\cdot g\otimes v))+C_{1}
.
Since the
G
action is isometric, the quantity
d(\xi_{0}\cdot g,\xi_{0}\cdot sg)
is uniformly bounded for
s\in S
and
g\in G
. Hence there exists
n_{0}
such that
d(I^{n}(\xi_{0}\cdot g\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{0}\cdot sg\otimes v))<1/3
for every
g\in G,s\in S
and
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n},n\geq n_{0}
. Fix
v
. Consider
g=s_{t}\cdots s_{1}
as in the statement of the theorem, and set
\xi_{i}=\xi_{0}\cdot s_{i}\cdots s_{1}
. Then the distance between two consecutive points
I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v)
is at most 1/3, and

l(g|_{v})\leq C_{1}d(I^{n}(\xi_{0}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{t}\otimes v))+C_{1}.

Let
\delta_{i}
be a geodesic curve joining
I^{n}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v)
to
I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v)
and
\gamma=\delta_{t}\cdots\delta_{1}
be their concatenation (where the rightmost curve is passed first). Consider an edge
e
which is crossed by
\gamma
, meaning that there is a subcurve
\gamma|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}
such that
\gamma(t_{1}),\gamma(t_{2})
are the extreme points of the edge and
\gamma(s)
is in the interior of
e
for
s\in(t_{1},t_{2})
. For every such edge crossing we can find
i<j
such that
I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v)
and
I^{n}(\xi_{j}\otimes v)
are at distance at most 1/3 from the two extreme points of
e
, and every
I^{n}(\xi_{k}\otimes v)
with
i<k<j
is in the interior of
e
at distance at least 1/3 from the extreme points. The distance between
d(I^{n}(\xi_{0}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{t}\otimes v))
is not larger than the total number of edge crossings of
\gamma
plus 2.

Now recall that the quotient projection
\pi_{0}\colon\Delta_{0}\to\Gamma_{0}
maps edges homeomorphically to edges, and moreover

\pi_{0}(I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v))=\pi_{0}(I^{n}(\xi_{0}\otimes s_{i}\cdots s_{1}(v))\cdot s_{i}\cdots s_{1}|_{v})=\pi_{0}(I^{n}(\xi_{0}\otimes s_{i}\cdots s_{1}(v))).

We deduce that to every edge-crossing of
\gamma
we can associate (injectively) a traverse. Thus there is a constant
C>0
such that

l(g|_{v})\leq C\tau_{n,v}(s_{t}\cdots s_{1})+C,

where
\tau_{n,v}
denotes the number traverses at level
n
of the form
(i,j,v_{1},v_{2})
with
v_{1}=s_{i}\cdots s_{1}(v)
and
v_{2}=s_{j}\cdots s_{1}(v)
. Summing over
v
we obtain the statement of the theorem. ∎

3.
Conformal dimension of contracting groups

3.1.
Visual metric

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a contracting group. Choose a basis
\mathsf{X}
of
\mathfrak{B}
and let
\mathcal{N}
be the corresponding nucleus of
G
. The
self-similarity complex
is the graph with set of vertices
\mathsf{X}^{*}
in which two vertices are connected by an edge in one of the following two situations: either they are of the form
v,xv
for
v\in\mathsf{X}^{*}
and
x\in\mathsf{X}
(
vertical edges
), or they are of the form
v,g(v)
for
v\in\mathsf{X}^{*}
and
g\in\mathcal{N}
(
horizontal edges
).

Notation 3.1
.

Given two vertices
x_{n}\ldots x_{2}x_{1}
and
y_{m}\ldots y_{2}y_{1}
, we denote by
\ell(x_{n}\ldots x_{2}x_{1},y_{m}\ldots y_{2}y_{1})
the largest
k\leq\min(n,m)
such that there exists
g\in\mathcal{N}
such that
g(x_{k}\ldots x_{2}x_{1})=y_{k}\ldots y_{2}y_{1}
.

Below we use the notation
(x|y)_{o}
for the Gromov product in a metric space (with respect to a basepoint
o
), namely

(x|y)_{o}=\frac{1}{2}(d(x,o)+d(y,o)-d(x,y)).

The following theorem is proved in
[
38
]
(see also
[
39
, Theorem 3.8.8]
). The statement about the Gromov product follows directly from the proof.

Theorem 3.2
.

The self-similarity complex is Gromov hyperbolic. Its boundary is homeomorphic to the limit space
\mathcal{J}_{G}
, where a point of
\mathcal{J}_{G}
represented by
\ldots x_{2}x_{1}\in\mathsf{X}^{-\omega}
corresponds to the limit of the geodesic path
(x_{1},x_{2}x_{1},x_{3}x_{2}x_{1},\ldots)
in the self-similarity complex.

For every basepoint
o
, there exists a constant
C
such that the Gromov product any two vertices
v=x_{n}\ldots x_{2}x_{1}
and
w=y_{m}\ldots y_{2}y_{1}
with respect to
\varnothing
satisfies

\lvert(v|w)_{\varnothing}-\ell(v,w)\rvert\leq C.

For
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{J}_{G}
, define
\ell(\xi,\eta)
to be the the largest
n
such that there exist representatives
\xi=\cdots x_{2}x_{1}
and
\eta=\cdots y_{2}y_{1}
such that
g(x_{n}\ldots x_{2}x_{1})=y_{n}\ldots y_{2}y_{1}
for some
g\in\mathcal{N}
. It follows from Theorem
3.2
that
\ell(\cdot,\cdot)
coincides (up to an additive constant) with the natural extension of the Gromov product to
\mathcal{J}_{G}
. Note that we define
\ell(\xi,\xi)=\infty
for
\xi\in\mathcal{J}_{G}
.

The following is a classical result of the theory of Gromov-hyperbolic spaces, see
[
18
, §3]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

There exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that for every
\alpha<\alpha_{0}
there exists a metric
d_{\alpha}
on
\mathcal{J}_{G}
and a constant
C>1
such that

(3)

C^{-1}e^{-\alpha\ell(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}\leq d_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\leq Ce^{-\alpha\ell(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}

for all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathcal{J}_{G}
.

Note that (for a fixed value of
\alpha
) the metric
d_{\alpha}
is unique up to bi-Lipschitz equivalence. We call it the
visual metric of exponent
\alpha
.

If
f,g
are real-valued functions with the same domain of definition, we write
f\asymp g
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
C^{-1}f\leq g\leq Cf
.

It follows from Proposition
2.6
that for every
\zeta\in\mathcal{J}_{G}
the set

T(\zeta,n)=\{\xi\in\mathcal{J}_{G}:\ell(\xi,\zeta)\geq n\}

is equal to the union of the tiles
{\mathcal{T}}_{v}
of the
n
th level intersecting tiles of the
n
th level that contain
\zeta
. Note that
T(\zeta,n)
is a union of not more than
|\mathcal{N}|^{2}
tiles of the
n
th level, by Proposition
2.6
. For later use, we also record the following tightly related observation.

Lemma 3.4
.

Fix
\zeta\in\mathcal{J}_{G}
. Then for every
n\geq 1
, we have

|\{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}\colon\exists\xi\in\mathcal{T}_{v}\text{ such that }\zeta\in T(\xi,n)\}|\leq|\mathcal{N}|^{3}.

Proof.

Fix
u\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
such that
\zeta\in\mathcal{T}_{u}
.
If
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
belongs to the set in the statement of the lemma, then there exists
\xi\in\mathcal{T}_{v}
and
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
such that
\xi\in\mathcal{T}_{v}\cap\mathcal{T}_{v_{1}},\mathcal{T}_{v_{1}}\cap\mathcal{T}_{v}\neq\varnothing
and
\zeta\in\mathcal{T}_{v_{2}}\cap\mathcal{T}_{u}
. It follows from Proposition
2.6
that
v=h(u)
for some
h\in\mathcal{N}^{3}
.
∎

Note also that if
d_{\alpha}
is a visual metric, then we have

(4)

B_{d_{\alpha}}(\zeta,C^{-1}e^{-\alpha n})\subset T(\zeta,n)\subset B_{d_{\alpha}}(\zeta,Ce^{-\alpha n}),

where
B_{d_{\alpha}}(x,R)
denotes the ball of radius
R
with center in
x
, and
C
satisfies (
3
).

Let
\mu
be the push-forward of the uniform Bernoulli measure on
\mathsf{X}^{-\omega}
by the quotient map
\mathsf{X}^{-\omega}\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}_{G}
. Since the quotient map is at most
|\mathcal{N}|
-to-one, we have, for every
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}

|\mathsf{X}|^{-n}\leq\mu({\mathcal{T}}_{v})\leq|\mathcal{N}|\cdot|\mathsf{X}|^{-n}.

It follows that
\mu(T(\zeta,n))\asymp|\mathsf{X}|^{-n}
, so

\mu(B_{d_{\alpha}}(\zeta,R))\asymp|\mathsf{X}|^{-\frac{-\ln R}{\alpha}}=\exp\left(\frac{\ln R\ln|\mathsf{X}|}{\alpha}\right)=R^{\frac{\ln|\mathsf{X}|}{\alpha}}.

3.2.
Conformal dimension

A homeomorphism
f\colon\mathcal{X}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{2}
between metric spaces
(\mathcal{X}_{1},d_{1})
and
(\mathcal{X}_{2},d_{2})
is said to be
quasisymmetric
if there exists a homeomorphism
\eta\colon[0,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
such that

\frac{d_{2}(f(x),f(y))}{d_{2}(f(x),f(z))}\leq\eta\left(\frac{d_{1}(x,y)}{d_{1}(x,z)}\right)

for all
x,y,z\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
such that
x\neq z
.

We refer to
[
24
, Chapter 10]
for general properties of quasisymmetric homeomorphism. It is easy to see that the inverse of every quasisymmetric homeomorphism is also quasisymmetric
[
24
, Proposition 10.6]
.

Two metrics on the same underlying topological space are said to be quasisymmetric to each other if the identity map is quasisymmetric.

Definition 3.5
.

A metric
d
on a space
\mathcal{X}
is said to be
Ahlfors-regular
if there exists
\beta>0
and a measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{X}
such that

\mu(B_{d}(x,R))\asymp R^{\beta}

for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
R<\diam(\mathcal{X})
. The exponent
\beta
is equal to the Hausdorff dimension of
d
.

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a contracting self-similar group. It follows directly from the definitions that any two visual metrics
d_{\alpha_{1}},d_{\alpha_{2}}
on
\mathcal{J}_{G}
are quasisymmetric to each other.
We have seen that a visual metric
d_{\alpha}
is Ahlfors-regular of Hausdorff dimension
\frac{\ln|\mathsf{X}|}{\alpha}
.

Definition 3.6
.

We say that a metric
d
on
\mathcal{J}_{G}
is
quasi-conformal
if it is quasi-symmetric to a visual metric.

The infimum of the set of Hausdorff dimensions of Ahlfors-regular quasi-conformal metrics on
\mathcal{J}_{G}
is called the
Ahlfors-regular conformal dimension
of
\mathcal{J}_{G}
and is denoted
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}\mathcal{J}_{G}
or
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}(G,\mathfrak{B})
.

Remark 3.7
.

It is a general fact that
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}\mathcal{J}_{G}
is always greater or equal to the topological dimension of
\mathcal{J}_{G}
(see
[
34
, Theorem 1.4.12]
). On the other hand, the topological dimension of
\mathcal{J}_{G}
is zero if and only if
G
is locally finite
[
44
, Theorem 4.1]
. Consequently, for every finitely generated infinite contracting group
(G,\mathfrak{B})
we have
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}\mathcal{J}_{G}\geq 1
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
d
be an Ahlfors–regular quasi-conformal metric on
\mathcal{J}_{G}
.

(i)

There exists
C_{1}>0
and
n_{0}
such that for every
\xi\in\mathcal{J}_{G}
and every
n\geq n_{0}
, there exists
r
such that
B_{d}(\xi,r)\subset T(\xi,n)\subset B_{d}(\xi,C_{1}r)
.

(ii)

There exist
C_{2}>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that

\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{J}_{G}}\diam_{d}(T(\xi,n))\leq C_{2}\lambda^{n}

for all
n
.

Proof.

Let
\eta\colon[0,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
be a homeomorphism such that
\frac{d(x,y)}{d(x,z)}\leq\eta\left(\frac{d_{\alpha}(x,y)}{d_{\alpha}(x,z)}\right)
for all
x,y,z\in\mathcal{J}_{G}
with
x\neq z
, where
d_{\alpha}
is a visual metric on
\mathcal{J}_{G}
. Recall (see (
4
)) that there exists a constant
C>1
such that

B_{d_{\alpha}}(\xi,C^{-1}e^{-n\alpha})\subset T(\xi,n)\subset B_{d_{\alpha}}(\xi,Ce^{-n\alpha}).

In particular, the statement in
(i)
is true for the metric
d_{\alpha}
, and it is easy to see that the same conclusion must hold for any quasisymmetric metric. Let us give a proof for completeness. Fix
\xi
and
n
, and set
R\colon=2\sup\{d(\xi,x)\colon x\in T(\xi,n)\}
.
Then
T(\xi,n)\subset B_{d}(\xi,R)
, and we can choose
y\in T(\xi,n)
such that
d(\xi,y)>\frac{1}{4}R
.
Suppose that
z\notin T(\xi,n)
. Then
d_{\alpha}(\xi,y)\leq Ce^{-n\alpha}
and
d_{\alpha}(\xi,z)\geq C^{-1}e^{-n\alpha}
, so

\frac{d(\xi,y)}{d(\xi,z)}\leq\eta\left(\frac{d_{\alpha}(\xi,y)}{d_{\alpha}(\xi,z)}\right)\leq\eta(C^{2}),

and therefore

d(\xi,z)\geq\eta(C^{2})^{-1}d(\xi,y)\geq(4\eta(C^{2}))^{-1}R.

Since this is true for every
z\notin T(\xi,n)
, it follows that

B_{d}(\xi,C_{1}^{-1}R)\subset T(\xi,n)\subset B_{d}(\xi,R)

with
C_{1}=4\eta(C^{2})
, which implies
(i)
.

We now prove
(ii)
. Let
\xi\in\mathcal{J}_{G}
and
n,m\geq 0
. Then for every
x\in T(\xi,n+m)\subset{\mathcal{T}}(\xi,n)
there exists
y\in T(\xi,n+m)
such that
2d(x,y)\geq\diam_{d}(T(\xi,n+m))
. Similarly, there exists
z\in T(\xi,n)
such that
d_{\alpha}(x,z)\geq\diam_{d_{\alpha}}(T(\xi,n))/2
. Then

\frac{\diam_{d}(T(\xi,n+m))}{\diam_{d}(T(\xi,n))}\leq\frac{2d(x,y)}{d(x,z)}\leq 2\eta\left(\frac{d_{\alpha}(x,y)}{d_{\alpha}(x,z)}\right)\leq 2\eta\left(\frac{\diam_{d_{\alpha}}(T(\xi,n+m))}{\diam_{d_{\alpha}}(T(\xi,n))/2}\right).

Now note that
\diam_{d_{\alpha}}(T(\xi,n))\asymp e^{-n\alpha}
(where the implicit multiplicative constant does not depend on
\xi
and
n
).
It follows that there exists
C_{3}>0
such that

\frac{\diam_{d_{\alpha}}(T(\xi,n+m))}{\diam_{d_{\alpha}}(T(\xi,n))}\leq C_{3}e^{-m\alpha}

for all
\xi\in\mathcal{J}_{G}
and
n,m\geq 0
. Since
\eta\colon[0,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
is a homeomorphism, this implies that there exist
m_{0}
and
\lambda_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
\frac{\diam_{d}(T(\xi,n+m_{0}))}{\diam_{d}(T(\xi,n))}\leq\lambda_{0}
for all
\xi
and
n
. This implies the statement in
(ii)
.
∎

4.
Contraction coefficients

4.1.
\ell^{p}
-contraction

In this section we study
\ell^{p}
-contraction coefficients, first introduced in the second author’s book
[
43
, §4.3.5]
.

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a contracting finitely generated group.
Fix some finite generating set of
G
, and let
l(g)
be the corresponding word length of
g\in G
.

Fix
p\in(0,\infty)
. The
\ell^{p}
-contraction coefficient
of the group is

\eta_{p}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\limsup_{l(g)\to\infty}\frac{\left(\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}l(g|_{v})^{p}\right)^{1/p}}{l(g)}}.

The
\ell^{\infty}
-contraction coefficient
\eta_{\infty}
is defined in the same way, replacing the
\ell^{p}
norm of the vector
(l(g|_{v}))_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}
by its
\ell^{\infty}
norm
\max_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}l(g|_{v})
. The existence of the first limit in the formula defining
\eta_{p}
follows from the lemma below.

Lemma 4.1
.

Denote

\eta_{p,n}=\limsup_{l(g)\to\infty}\frac{\left(\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}l(g|_{v})^{p}\right)^{1/p}}{l(g)}.

Then
\eta_{p,n_{1}+n_{2}}\leq\eta_{p,n_{1}}\eta_{p,n_{2}}
.

Proof.

For every
\lambda_{i}>\eta_{p,n_{i}}
, there exists
l_{i}>0
such that for every
g
with
l(g)\geq l_{i}
we have

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{i}}}l(g|_{v})^{p}\leq\lambda_{i}^{p}l(g)^{p}.

It follows that there exists a constant
C
not depending on
g
(but possibly depending on
n_{1},n_{2}
) such that

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{i}}}l(g|_{v})^{p}\leq\lambda_{i}^{p}(l(g)+C)^{p}

for all
g\in G
.

Suppose at first that
p\geq 1
. We have then, using the triangle inequality for the
p
-norm:

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}l(g|_{v})^{p}=\sum_{v_{1}\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{1}}}\sum_{v_{2}\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{2}}}l(g|_{v_{1}v_{2}})^{p}\leq\sum_{v_{1}\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{1}}}\lambda_{2}^{p}(l(g|_{v_{1}})+C)^{p}\leq\\
\left(\lambda_{2}C|\mathsf{X}^{n_{1}}|^{\frac{1}{p}}+\lambda_{2}\left(\sum_{v_{1}\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{1}}}l(g|_{v_{1}})^{p}\right)^{1/p}\right)^{p}\leq\\
\left(\lambda_{2}C|\mathsf{X}^{n_{1}}|^{\frac{1}{p}}+\lambda_{2}\lambda_{1}(l(g)+C)\right)^{p}

which implies that

\eta_{p,n_{1}+n_{2}}\leq\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2},

for all
\lambda_{1}>\eta_{p,n_{1}}
and
\lambda_{2}>\eta_{p,n_{2}}
.

The case
p\in(0,1)
is analogous, but using the triangle inequality for the norm
(x_{i})\mapsto\sum|x_{i}|^{p}
.
∎

It is shown in
[
43
, Proposition 4.3.12]
that
\eta_{p}
does not depend on the choice of the finite generating set or the choice of the basis
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathfrak{B}
. (This is true for all
p\in(0,\infty]
.)

It is shown in
[
43
, Proposition 4.3.15]
that for all
0<p<q\leq\infty
we have

(5)

\eta_{p}\geq\eta_{q}\geq|\mathsf{X}|^{-1},\quad|\mathsf{X}|^{-1/p}\eta_{p}\leq|\mathsf{X}|^{-1/q}\eta_{q}.

In particular, the function
p\to\eta_{p}
is non-increasing and continuous.

Definition 4.2
.

The
critical exponent

p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
of a contracting group is the infimum of the set of values
p
such that
\eta_{p}<1
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
(G,\mathfrak{B})
is a finitely generated contracting group, and
l(\cdot)
is a word metric on
G
(associated to some finite symmetric generating set). Fix a basis
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathfrak{B}
and let
\mathcal{N}
be the corresponding nucleus. Let
p>0
be such that
\eta_{p}<1
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
and
\eta\in(0,1)
such that for every
g\in G
and
n\geq 0
we have

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n},g|_{v}\notin\mathcal{N}}l(g|_{v})^{p}\leq C\eta^{n}l(g)^{p}.

In particular

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{\ast},g|_{v}\notin\mathcal{N}}l(g|_{v})^{p}\leq C_{1}l(g)^{p},

for
C_{1}=C/(1-\eta)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality (since the statement that we want to prove is invariant under changing the word metric to a bi-Lipschitz equivalent one), we may suppose that the generating set
S
used to define the word metric contains the nucleus and is such that
s|_{v}\in S
for every
s\in S
(note that every finite generating set of
G
is contained in a finite generating set satisfying these conditions). Then the cocycle rule
gh|_{v}=g|_{h(v)}h|_{v}
implies that
l(g|_{v})\leq l(g)
for every
g\in G
and
v\in\mathsf{X}^{\ast}
.

We first claim that it is enough to find some
l_{0}\geq 2
, and constants
C
and
\eta
(possibly depending on
l_{0}
) such that

(6)

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n},l(g|_{v})\geq l_{0}}l(g|_{v})^{p}\leq C\eta^{n}(g)^{p}.

Indeed since
G
is contracting, for every
l_{0}\geq 2
there exists
k
(depending only on
l_{0}
) such that if
l(g)<l_{0}
, then
g|_{v}\in\mathcal{N}
for all
|v|\leq k
.
For
n\geq k
and for every
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
, we have that
l(g|_{v})
is not greater than
l(g|_{w})
, where
w
is the unique prefix of
v
in
\mathsf{X}^{n-k}
. Since the projection map from
\mathsf{X}^{n}
to
\mathsf{X}^{n-k}
is
|\mathsf{X}|^{k}
-to-1, this implies that

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n},g|_{v}\notin\mathcal{N}}l(g|_{v})^{p}\leq|\mathsf{X}|^{k}\cdot\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n-k},l(g|_{v})\geq l_{0}}l(g|_{v})^{p}.

Note also that if
n\geq k
, we have
\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}l(g|_{v})^{p}\leq|\mathsf{X}|^{k}l(g)^{p}
.
It follows that if we prove (
6
) for some
l_{0}\geq 0
and some constants
C,\eta
and for every
n\geq 1
, we will also prove the conclusion in the statement (for some larger constants
C^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}
).

It is also easy to see (arguing similarly) that we can replace
\mathsf{X}
by any power
\mathsf{X}^{n_{0}}
, i.e., prove (
6
) only for
n
divisible by
n_{0}
.
Indeed this follows from the same reasoning as above, that implies that for any
n=kn_{0}+r
, with
0<r<n_{0}
, we have

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n},l(g|_{v})\geq l_{0}}l(g|_{v})^{p}\leq|\mathsf{X}|^{n_{0}}\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{kn_{0}},l(g|_{v})\geq l_{0}}l(g|_{v})^{p}.

If
\eta_{p}<1
, then there exist
n_{0}
,
l_{0}
, and
\eta<1
such that

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{0}}}l(g|_{v})^{p}\leq\eta l(g)^{p}

for all
g\in G
such that
l(g)\geq l_{0}
. Redefine the length by setting

\overline{l}(g)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}l(g)&\text{if $l(g)\geq l_{0}$}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

If
\overline{l}(g)=0
, then
\overline{l}(g|_{v})=0
for all
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{0}}
, since
l(g|_{v})\leq l(g)
. If
\overline{l}(g)\neq 0
, then

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{0}}}\overline{l}(g|_{v})^{p}\leq\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{0}}}l(g|_{v})^{p}\leq\eta l(g)^{p}=\eta\overline{l}(g)^{p},

so that in either case we have
\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{0}}}\overline{l}(g|_{v})^{p}\leq\eta\overline{l}(g)^{p}
,
for all
g\in G
. Consequently,

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{kn_{0}},l(g|_{v})\geq l_{0}}l(g|_{v})^{p}=\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{kn_{0}}}\overline{l}(g|_{v})^{p}\leq\eta^{k}\overline{l}(g)^{p}\leq\eta^{k}l(g)^{p},

which finishes the proof. ∎

Definition 4.4
.

The
portrait
of an element
g\in G
is the set
P(g)
of finite words
v\in\mathsf{X}^{*}
such that
g|_{u}
does not belong to the nucleus
\mathcal{N}
of
G
for any proper prefix
u
of
v
. If
g\in\mathcal{N}
, then we set
P(g)=\{\emptyset\}
.

The portrait is prefix-closed, i.e., a rooted subtree of
\mathsf{X}^{*}
. Let us denote by
L(g)
the set of leaves of this subtree, i.e., the set of words
v\in\mathsf{X}^{*}
such that
g|_{v}\in\mathcal{N}
but
g|_{u}\notin\mathcal{N}
for any proper prefix
u
of
v
.

The following bound on the size of the portrait follows immediatly from Proposition
4.3
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a contracting group, and choose a basis
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathfrak{B}
. Let
p\geq 1
be such that
\eta_{p}<1
. Then there exist
\eta\in(0,1)
and a constant
C>1
such that

|P(g)\cap\mathsf{X}^{n}|\leq C\eta^{n}l(g)^{p}

and

|P(g)|\leq C_{1}l(g)^{p}

for every
g\in G
and
n\geq 0
, with
C_{1}=C/(1-\eta)

The portrait
P(g)
of an element
g
together with the labeling of the elements
v\in P(g)
by the permutations the sections
g|_{v}
induce on the first level
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathsf{X}^{*}
and the labeling of the elements of
L(g)
by the sections
g|_{v}\in\mathcal{N}
uniquely determine
g
. This, together with Proposition
4.5
, implies that the growth rate of the group
G
is bounded from above by
e^{Cn^{\alpha}}
for every
\alpha>p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
.

4.2.
Critical exponent and conformal dimension

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a finitely generated contracting group. Its critical exponent
p_{c}
is not greater than
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}\mathcal{J}_{G}
.

Proof.

We can assume that
G
is infinite (else
p_{c}=0
). Hence
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}\mathcal{J}_{G}\geq 1
(see Remark
3.7
). Let
d
be an Ahlfors-regular quasiconformal metric on
\mathcal{J}_{G}
. Let
\nu
be the corresponding Hausdorff measure, and let
\alpha
be its Hausdorff dimension. Take
\beta>\alpha
. It is enough to prove that
p_{c}<\beta
, i.e., that
\eta_{\beta}<1
.

Let
S
be a finite symmetric generating set of
G
.
Consider a product
g=s_{m}\ldots s_{2}s_{1}
of of elements of
S
. Let
n_{0}
be such that all sections
s|_{v}
, for
s\in S
and
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{0}}
, belong to the nucleus. Then for every
u\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
and
v_{0}\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{0}}
consider the sequence

v_{0}u,\quad s_{1}(v_{0}u),\quad s_{2}s_{1}(v_{0}u),\ldots,\quad s_{m}\ldots s_{2}s_{1}(v_{0}u).

Denote by
u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
the corresponding suffixes of length
n
. Then
u_{i}=s_{i}|_{s_{i-1}\cdots s_{1}(v_{0})}(u_{i-1})
, so the tiles
{\mathcal{T}}_{u_{i}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{u_{i-1}}
intersect in
\mathcal{J}_{G}
, and the tiles
{\mathcal{T}}\otimes u_{i}
and
{\mathcal{T}}\otimes u_{i-1}
intersect in
\mathcal{X}_{G}
.

Choose
\xi_{i,v_{0}u}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{u_{i}}\cap{\mathcal{T}}_{u_{i-1}}
for every
i=1,2,\ldots,m
. Let
R_{i,v_{0}u}
and
r_{i,v_{0}u}
be the smallest and the largest radii such that

B_{d}(\xi_{i,v_{0}u},r_{i,v_{0}u})\subseteq T(\xi_{i,v_{0}u},n)\subseteq B_{d}(\xi_{i,v_{0}u},\frac{1}{2}R_{i,v_{0}u}).

By Proposition
3.8

(i)
and by Ahlfors–regularity, we can fix a constant
C>0
(depending only on the chosen metric and measure on
\mathcal{J}_{G}
) such that
R_{i,v_{0}u}/r_{i,v_{0}u}\in[1,C]
and such that
\nu(T(\xi_{i,v_{0}u},n))\geq C^{-1}r_{i,v_{0}u}^{\alpha}\geq C^{-1-\alpha}R_{i,v_{0}u}^{\alpha}
.

Denote by
M_{n}
the maximum over
\xi\in\mathcal{J}_{G}
of the minimal radius
R
such that
T(\xi,n)\subset B_{d}(\xi,\frac{1}{2}R)
. It follows from Proposition
3.8
that
M_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Consequently (using
\beta>1
),

(R_{1,v_{0}u}+R_{2,v_{0}u}+\cdots+R_{m,v_{0}u})^{\beta}\leq m^{\beta-1}(R_{1,v_{0}u}^{\beta}+R_{2,v_{0}u}^{\beta}+\cdots+R_{m,v_{0}u}^{\beta})\leq\\
m^{\beta-1}M_{n}^{\beta-\alpha}(R_{1,v_{0}u}^{\alpha}+R_{2,v_{0}u}^{\alpha}+\cdots+R_{m,v_{0}u}^{\alpha})\leq\\
m^{\beta-1}M_{n}^{\beta-\alpha}C^{1+\alpha}(\nu(T(\xi_{1,v_{0}u},n))+\nu(T(\xi_{2,v_{0}u},n))+\cdots+\nu(T(\xi_{m,v_{0}u},n))).

Since
\xi_{i,v_{0}u}\in\mathcal{T}_{u}
, Lemma
3.4
tells us that for every
v_{0}
and
i
we have

\sup_{\zeta\in\mathcal{J}_{G}}\sum_{u\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{T(\xi_{i,v_{0}u},n)}(\zeta)\leq|\mathcal{N}|^{3},

where we denote by
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
the indicator function of a subset
A\subset\mathcal{J}_{G}
. It follows that

\sum_{v_{0}\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{0}},u\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}\nu(T(\xi_{i,v_{0}u},n))=\sum_{v_{0}\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{0}}}\int_{\mathcal{J}_{G}}\left(\sum_{u\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}\mathbbm{1}_{T(\xi_{i,v_{0}u},n)}\right)d\nu\leq|\mathsf{X}|^{n_{0}}\cdot|\mathcal{N}|^{3}\cdot\nu(\mathcal{J}_{G}).

Hence, adding all the inequalities over all
v_{0}\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{0}},u\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
, we will get

\sum_{v_{0}\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{0}},u\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}(R_{1,v_{0}u}+R_{2,v_{0}u}+\cdots+R_{m,v_{0}u})^{\beta}\leq\\
|\mathsf{X}|^{n_{0}}\cdot|\mathcal{N}|^{3}\cdot\nu(\mathcal{J}_{G})\cdot C^{1+\alpha}\cdot M_{n}^{\beta-\alpha}\cdot m^{\beta-1}\leq KM_{n}^{\beta-\alpha}m^{\beta}

for some constant
K>0
.

Lemma 4.7
.

There exists a constant
C_{1}>0
, depending only on
G
,
\mathsf{X}
, and
d
, such that

l(g|_{v_{0}u})\leq C_{1}(R_{1,v_{0}u}+R_{2,v_{0}u}+\cdots+R_{m,v_{0}u})+C_{1}
.

Proof.

Let us define a metric on
\mathcal{X}_{G}
. Let
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathcal{X}_{G}
.
Take all
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n}
for all
n\geq 0
such that
\zeta_{i}\in({\mathcal{T}}S)\otimes v_{i}
and
({\mathcal{T}}S)\otimes v_{1}\cap({\mathcal{T}}S)\otimes v_{2}\neq\emptyset
, and then take the infimum
d_{0}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})
of
\diam(({\mathcal{T}}S)\otimes v_{1}/G)+\diam(({\mathcal{T}}S)\otimes v_{2}/G)
. (Here
\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes 0}=G
.) Denote the infimum by
d_{0}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})
(it may be infinite).

We obviously have that
d_{0}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})
is not smaller than the distance between the images of
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
in
\mathcal{J}_{G}
.

Define then
d_{1}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})
as the infimum of
d_{0}(\xi_{0},\xi_{1})+d_{0}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})+\cdots+d_{0}(\xi_{n-1},\xi_{n})
over all sequences
\xi_{i}
such that
\xi_{0}=\zeta_{1}
and
\xi_{n}=\zeta_{2}
. Then
d_{1}
is a finite metric on
\mathcal{X}_{G}
. It is obviously
G
-invariant and, by construction,
(\mathcal{X},d_{1})
is an almost geodesic space. For every
\xi\in\mathcal{X}_{G}
, the diameter of the union of the tiles of the
n
th level of
\mathcal{X}_{G}
containing
\xi
is not larger than the diameter of the union of the tiles of the
n
th level of
\mathcal{J}_{G}
containing the image of
\xi
. Consequently, the metric
d_{1}
is compatible with the topology on
\mathcal{X}_{G}
.

Choose a point
\xi_{0}\in{\mathcal{T}}
, and consider the sequence

\xi_{0}\otimes v_{0}u,\quad\xi_{0}\cdot s_{1}\otimes v_{0}u,\quad\xi_{0}\cdot s_{2}s_{1}\otimes v_{0}u,\ldots,\quad\xi_{0}\cdot s_{m}\cdots s_{2}s_{1}\otimes v_{0}u.

Then
R_{1,v_{0}u}+R_{2,v_{0}u}+\cdots+R_{m,v_{0}u}
is an upper bound on
d_{1}(\xi_{0}\otimes v_{0}u,\xi_{0}\cdot g\otimes v_{0}u)
. The lemma follows then from Lemma
2.10
.
∎

Consequently, using the triangle inequality for the
\ell^{\beta}
-norm (since
\beta>1
),

\left(\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n+n_{0}}}l(g|_{v})^{\beta}\right)^{1/\beta}\leq\\
C_{1}\left(\sum_{v_{0}\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{0}},u\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}(R_{1,v_{0}u}+R_{2,v_{0}u}+\cdots+R_{m,v_{0}u}+1)^{\beta}\right)^{1/\beta}\leq\\
C_{1}\left(\sum_{v_{0}\in\mathsf{X}^{n_{0}},u\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}(R_{1,v_{0}u}+R_{2,v_{0}u}+\cdots+R_{m,v_{0}u})^{\beta}\right)^{1/\beta}+C_{1}|\mathsf{X}^{n+n_{0}}|^{1/\beta}\leq\\
K_{1}M_{n}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\cdot l(g)+C_{1}|\mathsf{X}^{n+n_{0}}|^{1/\beta},

for some
K_{1}>0
, which implies that
\limsup_{l(g)\to\infty}\frac{\left(\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n+n_{0}}}l(g|_{v})^{\beta}\right)^{1/\beta}}{l(g)}\leq K_{1}M_{n}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}
. Since
M_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, Lemma
4.1
implies that
\eta_{\beta}<1
.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

The inequality in Theorem
4.6
is not sharp. For example, it is known that
\eta_{1}
for the first Grigorchuk group
G
is strictly less than 1 (this is the classical sum-contraction of Grigorchuk
[
19
]
), which implies by the inequality (
5
) that its critical exponent is strictly less than 1. On the other hand, the limit space of the Grigorchuk group is a segment, hence we have
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}\mathcal{J}_{G}=1
. In fact, it follows from the norm contracting inequality in
[
9
]
and the explicit sequence of group elements which satisfies the reverse inequality (see
[
5
, Prop 4.7]
) that its critical exponent is equal to
\alpha_{0}=\log 2/\log\lambda_{0}\simeq 0.7674
, where
\lambda_{0}
is the positive real root of the polynomial
X^{3}-X^{2}-2X-4
. Recall the definition of critical constant for Liouville property in the introduction. From the random walk with nontrivial Poisson boundary constructed in
[
16
]
for the purpose of volume lower estimate, we have that
\rm{Cr}_{Liouv}(G)
is also equal to
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{0}
is the growth exponent of
G
.

4.3.
Thurston
p
-maps

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a self-similar group. In this subsection we present another bound on
p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
, related to Thurston’s theorem (see
[
15
]
) characterizing post-critically finite branched self-coverings of the sphere that are realizable as complex rational function. (Note that this result will not be used in the proof of our main theorems in the introduction.)

Consider the linear span
V
of the conjugacy classes of infinite order elements of
G
. We denote by
[g]
the conjugacy class of an element
g\in G
seen as an element of
V
. If
g
is of finite order, then we define
[g]
to be equal to zero. Choose a basis
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathfrak{B}
and the associated self-similar action on
\mathsf{X}^{*}
. Let
g\in G
. Consider the action of
g
on
\mathsf{X}
, and for every cycle
x_{1}\mapsto x_{2}\mapsto\ldots\mapsto x_{k}\mapsto x_{1}
of the action, consider the conjugacy class
[g|_{x_{k}}\cdots g|_{x_{2}}g|_{x_{1}}]=[(g^{k})|_{x_{1}}]
. Note that it does not depend on the choice of the initial element
x_{1}
of the cycle. Choose
p\geq 1
, and denote by
T_{p}([g])
the sum of the elements
k^{1-p}[(g^{k})|_{x_{1}}]\in V
taken over all cycles of the action of
g
on
\mathsf{X}
. Since a change of the basis
\mathsf{X}
corresponds to conjugation of the wreath recursion by an element of the wreath product, the value of
T_{p}([g])
does not depend on the choice of
\mathsf{X}
.

For example, if
a=\sigma(1,a)
is the binary odometer, then we have
T_{p}([a])=2^{1-p}[a]
.

We call the linear operator
T_{p}
the
Thurston’s
p
-map
.

Lemma 4.9
.

If
T_{p}
is the Thurston’s
p
-map for
(G,\mathfrak{B})
, then the Thurston’s
p
-map for
(G,\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n})
is
T_{p}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
x_{1}\mapsto x_{2}\mapsto\cdots\mapsto x_{k}\mapsto x_{1}
be a cycle of the action of
g
on
\mathsf{X}
. Denote
h=(g^{k})|_{x_{1}}
. For every cycle
v_{1}\mapsto v_{2}\mapsto\cdots\mapsto v_{m}\mapsto v_{1}
of the action of
h
on
\mathsf{X}^{n}
we get the cycle

x_{1}v_{1}\mapsto x_{2}g|_{x_{1}}(v_{1})\mapsto\cdots\mapsto x_{k}(g^{k-1})_{x_{1}}(v_{1})\mapsto\\
x_{1}v_{2}\mapsto x_{2}g|_{x_{1}}(v_{2})\mapsto\cdots\mapsto x_{k}(g^{k-1})_{x_{1}}(v_{2})\mapsto\\
\ldots\\
x_{1}v_{m}\mapsto x_{2}g|_{x_{1}}(v_{m})\mapsto\cdots\mapsto x_{k}(g^{k-1})_{x_{1}}(v_{m})\mapsto x_{1}v_{1}

of length
km
of the action of
g
on
\mathsf{X}^{n+1}
. We have

(g^{km})|_{x_{1}v_{1}}=h^{m}|_{v_{1}}
. A proof of the lemma now follows by induction.
∎

Theorem 4.10
.

Suppose that
U<V
is a
T_{p}
-invariant subspace spanned by a finite set of conjugacy classes. If the
\ell^{p}
-contraction coefficient
\eta_{p}
of
(G,\mathfrak{B})
is less than 1, then the spectral radius of
T_{p}|_{U}
is also less than 1.

In particular, we can use this theorem to find lower estimates for
p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
, and hence for
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}(G,\mathfrak{B})
.

A particular case of this theorem is related to one direction of Thurston’s theorem (see
[
15
]
). In Thurston’s theorem one considers subspaces spanned by conjugacy classes defined by disjoint simple closed curves of the punctured sphere (with some additional conditions). Such collections of curves are called
multicurves
.

Proof.

Let
\{[g_{1}],[g_{2}],\ldots,[g_{m}]\}
be a finite spanning set of
U
. Let
N
be a natural number such that the action of each element
g_{i}^{N}
on
\mathsf{X}
is identical. Denote by
L_{i,kN}
the length of
g_{i}^{kN}
(with respect to some fixed finite generating set of the group). We have then
L_{i,k_{1}k_{2}N}\leq k_{1}L_{i,k_{2}N}
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
,
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1
. Equivalently, we have

L_{i,k_{2}N}\geq k_{1}^{-1}L_{i,k_{1}k_{2}N}.

Note also that
L_{i,kN}\to\infty
as
kN\to\infty
, since we assume that the elements
g_{i}
are of infinite order.

Assume that
\eta_{p}<1
. Let
\eta
be such that
\eta_{p}<\eta<1
. After replacing
\mathsf{X}
by
\mathsf{X}^{n}
for a large
n
(using Lemma
4.9
) and assuming that
N
is large enough, we will have

\sum_{x\in\mathsf{X}}l(g_{i}^{Nk}|_{x})^{p}\leq\eta^{p}l(g_{i}^{Nk})^{p}

for all
i
and
k
.

For a cycle
x_{1}\mapsto x_{2}\mapsto\cdots\mapsto x_{r}\mapsto x_{1}
of
g_{i}
, the corresponding sections
g_{i}^{Nk}|_{x_{t}}
are conjugate to
\left(g_{i}^{r}|_{x_{1}}\right)^{\frac{Nk}{r}}
. If
g_{i}^{r}|_{x_{1}}
has infinite order, then it is conjugate to one of the elements
g_{j}
. Since the number of corresponding conjugators is finite, we get

l(g_{i}^{Nk}|_{x_{t}})\geq L_{j,Nk/r}-C

for some constant
C
that depends only on the spanning set
\{[g_{1}],[g_{2}],\ldots,[g_{m}]\}
.

Choose an arbitrary
\rho\in(\eta^{p},1)
, so that
\eta^{-p}\rho>1
. Assuming that
N
is large enough, we will get
l(g_{i}^{Nk}|_{x_{t}})\geq\rho L_{j,Nk/r}
for all
k\geq 1
and all
1\leq t\leq r
. Consequently,

\sum_{t=1}^{r}l(g_{i}^{Nk}|_{x_{t}})^{p}\geq\rho rL_{j,Nk/r}^{p}\geq\rho r\left(r^{-1}L_{j,Nk}\right)^{p}=\rho r^{1-p}L_{j,Nk}^{p}.

It follows that

L_{i,Nk}^{p}=l(g_{i}^{Nk})^{p}\geq\eta^{-p}\sum_{x\in\mathsf{X}}l(g_{i}^{Nk}|_{x})^{p}\geq\eta^{-p}\rho\cdot\phi(T_{p}([g_{i}])),

where
\phi
is the linear functional defined by

\phi([g_{j}])=L_{j,Nk}^{p}.

Consider the matrix of the dual operator
T_{p}^{*}
in the basis of
U^{*}
dual to the basis
([g_{1}],[g_{2}],\ldots,[g_{m}])
. The above inequality means that the
i
th coordinate
T_{p}^{*}(\phi)([g_{i}])=\phi(T_{p}([g_{i}])
of
T_{p}^{*}(\phi)\in U^{*}
is less than or equal to
\eta^{p}\rho^{-1}
times the
i
th coordinate
\phi([g_{i}])
of
\phi
. Since all coordinates of
\phi
are positive, the Perron-Frobenius theorem implies that the spectral radius of
T_{p}^{*}
is strictly less than 1. Consequently, the spectral radius of
T_{p}
is also strictly less than 1.
∎

As an example of an application of Theorem
4.10
(and Thurston’s theorem), consider a mating of two cubic polynomials, found by M. Shishikura and L. Tan, see
[
48
]
.

The iterated monodromy group is generated by

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=(12)(1,1,a_{3}),

\displaystyle\quad b_{1}

\displaystyle=(1,b_{3},1),

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=(a_{1},1,1),

\displaystyle\quad b_{2}

\displaystyle=(b_{1},1,1),

\displaystyle a_{3}

\displaystyle=(02)(a_{3}^{-1},1,a_{2}a_{3}),

\displaystyle\quad b_{3}

\displaystyle=(012)(b_{3}^{-1},1,b_{2}b_{3}).

Note that
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}=b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}=\tau
, where
\tau=(012)(1,1,\tau)
. The groups
\langle a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\rangle
and
\langle b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\rangle
are the iterated monodromy groups of the mated cubic polynomials.

It is checked directly that the group is contracting with the nucleus consisting of the elements
a_{3},a_{2}a_{3},b_{3},b_{2}b_{3},a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}=b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}
, their inverses, and the identity element. Moreover, the wreath recursion is contracting for the free group (of rank 5), hence the limit space has topological dimension 1, see
[
44
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Post-conjugating the wreath recursion by
(1,b_{3}^{-1},b_{3}^{-1})
, and passing to the generating set
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},x=b_{1}^{-1}a_{1}
,
y=b_{3}a_{3}^{-1}
(we have
b_{2}^{-1}a_{2}=yx^{-1}
in the faithful quotient), we get an equivalent wreath recursion:

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=(1,b_{3},1),

\displaystyle\quad x

\displaystyle=(12)(1,1,y^{-1}),

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=(b_{1},1,1),

\displaystyle\quad y

\displaystyle=(12)(x,1,y^{-1}),

\displaystyle b_{3}

\displaystyle=(012)(1,1,b_{2}).

Let
U
be the formal linear span of the conjugacy classes

[x],[x^{-1}],[y],[y^{-1}]
.
Thurston map
T_{2}
acts by the rule

\displaystyle[x]

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{1}{2}[y^{-1}],

\displaystyle\quad[x^{-1}]

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{1}{2}[y],

\displaystyle[y]

\displaystyle\mapsto[x]+\frac{1}{2}[y^{-1}],

\displaystyle\quad[y^{-1}]

\displaystyle\mapsto[x^{-1}]+\frac{1}{2}[y].

It is easy to see that the spectral radius of
T_{2}|_{U}
is equal to 1. In particular, this means, by Thurston’s theorem, that the mating is
obstructed
, i.e., is not equivalent to a rational function. Theorem
4.10
implies that the conformal dimension of the limit space of the group is at least 2.

5.
Random walk and Liouville property

5.1.
Random walk preliminaries

5.1.1.
Notation

Let
G
be a group endowed with a symmetric probability measure
\mu
. The measure
\mu
is said to be
non-degenerate
if its support
\supp\mu
generates
G
as a semi-group. If
G
is finitely generated, the measure
\mu
is said to have
finite
p
-moment
for
p>0
if
\sum\mu(g)l(g)^{p}<+\infty
, where
l(\cdot)
is the word metric on
G
associated to some finite symmetric generating set. We denote by
(\mathbf{g}_{t})
the (left) random walk associated to
\mu
, namely
\mathbf{g}_{t}=\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}
, where
(\mathbf{s}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is a sequence of independent random variables with distribution
\mu
.

5.1.2.
Capacities and random walk on Schreier graphs

Assume that
G
acts on a countable set
\Omega
. Then for each starting point
\omega\in\Omega
, the process
(\mathbf{g}_{t}\cdot\omega)
is a Markov chain on
\Omega
with transition probabilities
p(v,w)=\sum_{g\cdot v=w}\mu(g)
. The assumption that
\mu
is symmetric implies that the induced Markov chain is reversible with respect to the counting measure on
\Omega
, which we use as the reference measure in the Dirichlet forms below.
For
p\geq 1
, the associated
p
-Dirichlet form
on
\ell^{p}(\Omega)
is

\mathcal{E}_{p,\mu}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{g\in G,v\in\Omega}\mu(g)|f(v)-f(g\cdot v)|^{p},\quad\quad f\in\ell^{p}(\Omega).

Let
A,B
be two disjoint finite subsets of
\Omega
. For
p\geq 1
, the (effective)
p
-capacity
between
A
and
B
is defined as

\Capc_{p,\mu}(A,B)=\inf\left\{\mathcal{E}_{p,v}(f)\colon f\in\ell^{p}(\Omega),f|_{A}=1,f|_{B}=0\right\}.

For
p=2
this has the following well-known probabilistic interpretation, which is
[
33
, Exercise 2.47]
. We provide a proof for completeness.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(G,\mu)
be a group endowed with a probability measure, and
(\mathbf{g}_{n})
the associated random walk. Assume that
G
acts on a finite set
\Omega
. For
\omega\in\Omega
and
B\subset\Omega
, consider the stopping time:

\mathbf{T}_{\omega\to B}=\min\{t\geq 1\colon\mathbf{g}_{t}\cdot\omega\in B\}.

Then for
A,B
disjoint finite subsets of
\Omega
,

\Capc_{2,\mu}(A,B)=\sum_{\omega\in A}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{T}_{\omega\to B}<\mathbf{T}_{\omega\to A}).

Proof.

We use the same notation and terminology on electric networks as in
[
33
, Chapter 2]
. (Note that many statements about capacity are proven there assuming that
A=\{a\}
is a singleton, leaving the generalization to more general subsets as a (usually straightforward) exercise.)
Since
\mu
is symmetric, we may regard the induced random walk transition
operator
P_{\mu}
on
\Omega
as coming from an electric network
with conductance
c(x,y)=\sum_{g\in G}{\bf 1}_{\{y=g\cdot x\}}\mu(g)
,
and reversing measure
\pi(x)=1
,
x,y\in\Omega
. Denote by
\tau_{\omega\to A}=\min\{t\geq 0\colon\mathbf{g}_{t}\omega\in A\}

the hitting time of
A
. Let
v(x)=\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x\to A}<\tau_{x\to B})
.
Then
v
is a voltage function which is
1
on the source set
A
,
and
0
on the sink set
B
. Considering the first step of the random
walk, we have

\sum_{\omega\in A}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{T}_{\omega\to B}<\mathbf{T}_{\omega\to A})=\sum_{\omega\in A}\sum_{x\in\Omega}P_{\mu}(\omega,x)\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x\to B}<\tau_{x\to A})\\
=\sum_{\omega\in A}\sum_{x\in\Omega}c(\omega,x)(v(\omega)-v(x)).

Regard
i(\omega,x)=c(\omega,x)(v(\omega)-v(x))
as the current on
the edge
(\omega,x)
. The capacity
{\rm Cap}_{2,\mu}(A,B)
is
the effective conductance between
A
and
B
, thus it is equal to the
total amount of current flowing out of
A
under the voltage function

v
, which is
\sum_{\omega\in A}\sum_{x\in\Omega}i(\omega,x)
. (This is essentially the definition of capacity between subsets given in
[
33
]
, see
[
33
, Exercise 2.5]
, the fact that it coincides with our definition above is a consequence of Thomson’s principle
[
33
, p.35]
, see
[
33
, Exercise 2.13]
).∎

5.1.3.
Liouville property and entropy

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
G
. A function
f\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
is
\mu
-harmonic
if
f(x)=\sum_{g\in G}f(xg)\mu(g)
for all
x\in G
. By definition the
\mu
-random walk has the
Liouville propery
if
\mu
has trivial Poisson boundary, namely if every bounded
\mu
-harmonic function is constant on the subgroup
\langle\supp\mu\rangle
.
Furstenberg observed that admitting a non-degenerate measure
\mu
with the Liouville property implies amenability of the group
G
(see
[
28
, Theorem 4.2]
). Conversely, every amenable group admits a symmetric non-degenerate measure with trivial Poisson boundary, by a result of Kaimanovich and Vershik
[
28
, Theorem 4.4]
and Rosenblatt
[
47
]
.

Recall that given a probability measure
\nu
supported on a discrete set
\Omega
, the
(Shannon) entropy
of
\nu
is defined as

H(\nu)=-\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\nu(\omega)\log\nu(\omega).

If
\mathbf{X}
is a discrete random variable taking values in a set
\Omega
, the entropy
H(\mathbf{X})
is defined as the entropy of the distribution of
\mathbf{X}
.

Let
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}
be discrete random variables, and for any value
y
taken by
\mathbf{Y}
with positive probability, denote by
\mathbf{X}^{(y)}
the random variable with the conditional distribution of
\mathbf{X}
given
\{\mathbf{Y}=y\}
. Then the
conditional entropy
of
\mathbf{X}
given
\mathbf{Y}
is defined as the expected value of
H(\mathbf{X}^{(y)})
, namely

H(\mathbf{X}|\mathbf{Y})=\sum_{y}H(\mathbf{X}^{(y)})\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}=y).

We resume below some basic properties of Shannon entropy (for proofs, see e.g.
[
13
, Chapter 2]
).

Lemma 5.2
.

(i)

If
\mathbf{X}
is a random variable taking values in a finite set
\Omega
, then
H(\mathbf{X})\leq\log|\Omega|
.

(ii)

The entropy of the joint distribution of two discrete random variables
(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
satisfies

H(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})=H(\mathbf{X}|\mathbf{Y})+H(\mathbf{Y}).

(iii)

Assume that a random variable
\mathbf{Y}
can be expressed as
\mathbf{Y}=f(\mathbf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{k})
for some discrete random variables
\mathbf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{k}
and some measurable function
f
. Then

H(\mathbf{Y})\leq H(\mathbf{X}_{1})+\cdots+H(\mathbf{X}_{k}).

We also recall the following well-known fact (see e.g. the proof of
[
29
, Lemma 12.2]
)

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group and
l(\cdot)
be a word metric on
G
associated to a finite symmetric generating set
S
containing the identity. Then for every
G
-valued random variable
\mathbf{g}
we have
H(\mathbf{g})\leq(\log|S|+1)\mathbb{E}[l(\mathbf{g})]+1
.

Assume that
\mu
is a probability measure on a countable group
G
. Then the limit

h_{\mu}=\lim\frac{1}{n}H(\mu^{*n})

exists and is called the (Avez)
asymptotic entropy
of
\mu
.
We recall the fundamental:

Theorem 5.4

(Entropy criterion, Kaimanovich–Vershik
[
28
]
, Derriennic
[
14
]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a probability mesaure on a countable group
G
. If
H(\mu)<+\infty
, then the
\mu
-random walk has the Liouville property if and only if
h_{\mu}=0
.

For groups acting on rooted trees, the entropy criterion combined with the previous properties of entropy gives the following sufficient condition for boundary triviality. Some tightly related criteria appeared in
[
8
,
30
]
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group of automorphisms of a rooted tree
\mathsf{X}^{*}
endowed with a word metric, and
\mu
be a probability measure on
G
with finite entropy. Then there is a constant
C>0
such that for every
n,t>0
we have

H(\mu^{*t})\leq C\mathbb{E}[\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}l(\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v})]+C|\mathsf{X}|^{n}.

In particular, if there exists a sequence
(\varepsilon_{n})
tending to
0
such that for every
n
we have

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}l(\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v})\right]\leq\varepsilon_{n},

then
h_{\mu}=0
.

(In fact, it is easy to check that the limsup in the statement is a limit, by subadditivity).

Proof.

For every
n
and
t
, the random variable
\mathbf{g}_{t}
is completely determined by the collection of sections
(\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v})_{v\in X^{n}}
and by the permutation
\sigma
describing the action on level
n
. Thus, by Lemma
5.2

(iii)
,

H(\mathbf{g}_{t})\leq\sum_{v\in X^{n}}H(\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v})+H(\sigma).

By Lemma
5.3
, the first term is bounded by
C_{1}\mathbb{E}[\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}l(\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v})]+|\mathsf{X}|^{n}
, where
C_{1}
depends only on the generating set defining
l(\cdot)
. The permutation
\sigma
is completely determined by the first-level permutation of all sections
\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{w}
where
|w|\leq n-1
. Thus the total number of possibilities for
\sigma
is bounded by
\exp(C_{2}|\mathsf{X}|^{n})
(where
C_{2}
depends only on
|\mathsf{X}|
), and
H(\sigma)\leq C_{2}|\mathsf{X}|^{n}
by Lemma
5.2

(i)
. The proposition follows. ∎

5.2.
Critical contraction exponent and
p
-capacity

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a finitely generated contracting group with a basis
\mathsf{X}
, and
\mu
be a symmetric probability measure on
G
. In this subsection, we will relate the
p
-capacities associated to the action of
G
on the levels
\mathsf{X}^{n}
with
p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
and
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}\mathcal{J}_{G}
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Suppose that
(G,\mathfrak{B})
is a contracting finitely generated group. Denote by
l(g)
the word length with respect to some fixed finite generating set on
G
.

Let
d
be an Ahlfors-regular metric on
\mathcal{J}_{G}
of Hausdorff dimension
\alpha
. Let
\beta>\alpha
and let

. Pick a basis
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathfrak{B}
.

There exist
C>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that for every
g\in G
and every
\ldots x_{2}x_{1}\in\mathsf{X}^{-\omega}
we have

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}d(\ldots x_{2}x_{1}g(v),\ldots x_{2}x_{1}v)^{\beta}\leq C\lambda^{n}l(g)^{p}.

Proof.

Let
\nu
be the Hausdorff measure of the metric
d
.
Take an element
g\in G
. For every

, if

, let

be the shortest prefix of

such that

(note that

can well be empty or equal to

, then define

. Let
v^{\prime\prime}\in\mathsf{X}^{*}
be such that

.

We have
\ldots x_{2}x_{1}g(v)\in T(\ldots x_{2}x_{1}v,|v^{\prime\prime}|)
for every

. It follows that

d(\ldots x_{2}x_{1}g(v),\ldots x_{2}x_{1}v)^{\alpha}\leq C_{1}\nu(T(\ldots x_{2}x_{1}v,|v^{\prime\prime}|)

for some constant
C_{1}
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
to be specified shortly. There exists
C_{2}>0
such that we have

(7)

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}d(\ldots x_{2}x_{1}g(v),\ldots x_{2}x_{1}v)^{\beta}\leq C_{2}\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}\nu(T(\ldots x_{2}x_{1}v,|v^{\prime\prime}|)^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\leq\\
C_{2}\nu(\mathcal{J}_{G})^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\cdot|\{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}\colon|v^{\prime\prime}|\leq\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor\}|+C_{2}\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n},|v^{\prime\prime}|>\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor}\nu(T(\ldots x_{2}x_{1}v,|v^{\prime\prime}|)^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}.

Note that if
|v^{\prime\prime}|\leq\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor
, then the left-prefix of
v
of length
{n-\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor}
belongs to the portrait
P(g)
of
g
, so that

|\{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}\colon|v^{\prime\prime}|\leq\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor\}|\leq|\mathsf{X}^{\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor}|\cdot|P(g)\cap\mathsf{X}^{n-\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor}|.

By Proposition
4.5
, there exists
C_{3}>0
and

such that
|P(g)\cap\mathsf{X}^{n-\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor}|\leq C_{3}\eta^{(1-\varepsilon)n}l(g)^{p}
. Thus, if
\varepsilon
is small enough so that
\lambda_{1}\colon=|\mathsf{X}|^{\varepsilon}\eta^{(1-\varepsilon)}<1
, we obtain

(8)

|\{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}\colon|v^{\prime\prime}|\leq\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor\}|\leq C_{4}\lambda_{1}^{n}l(g)^{p},

for some
C_{4}>0
and
\lambda_{1}\in(0,1)
.
To bound the second summand in (
7
), first note that for every
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
such that
|v^{\prime\prime}|>0
, we have
v^{\prime}\in P(g)
. Moreover, for any
\xi\in\mathcal{J}_{G}
, any

and any fixed of

implies that there are at most

.

implies that the last quantity is bounded from above by

for some

and

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}d(\ldots x_{2}x_{1}g(v),\ldots x_{2}x_{1}v)^{\beta}\leq C\lambda^{n}l(g)^{p}

for some

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a contracting finitely generated self-similar group, and let
\mu
be a symmetric probability measure on
G
with finite
p
-moment for some
p>p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
.

Let
A
and
B
be disjoint closed subsets of
\mathcal{J}_{G}
. Fix
\xi_{0}=\ldots x_{2}x_{1}\in\mathsf{X}^{-\omega}
, and denote by
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
the sets of words
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
such that the point of
\mathcal{J}_{G}
represented by the sequence
\ldots x_{2}x_{1}v
belongs to
A
and
B
, respectively. If
\beta>\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}\mathcal{J}_{G}
then there exist
C>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that

\Capc_{\beta,\mu}(A_{n},B_{n})\leq C\lambda^{n}.

Proof.

Let
d
be an Ahlfors-regular metric on
\mathcal{J}_{G}
of Hausdorff dimension
\alpha<\beta
.
The algebra of Lipschitz functions
\mathcal{J}_{G}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
(with respect to
d
and to the usual metric on
{\mathbb{R}}
) is dense in the algebra of all continuous functions, by the Stone-Weierstrass theorem. Therefore, there exists a Lipschitz function
f_{0}\colon\mathcal{J}_{G}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
such that
|f_{0}(a)-1|<1/3
and
|f_{0}(b)|<1/3
for all
a\in A
and
b\in B
. Let
\chi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a Lipschitz function such that
\chi(x)=0
for all
x\in(-1/3,1/3)
and
\chi(x)=1
for all
x\in(2/3,5/3)
. Then the function
f=\chi\circ f_{0}\colon\mathcal{J}_{G}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a Lipschitz function such that
f(a)=0
and
f(b)=1
for all
a\in A
and
b\in B
. Let
K>0
be a Lipschitz constant for
f
. Then Proposition
5.6
implies that there exist
C>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that for every
g\in G

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}|f(\cdots x_{2}x_{1}g(v))-f(\cdots x_{2}x_{1}v)|^{\beta}\\
\leq K^{\beta}\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}d(\ldots x_{2}x_{1}g(v),\ldots x_{2}x_{1}v)^{\beta}\leq K^{\beta}C\lambda^{n}l(g)^{p}.

To conclude, note that the function
f
induces a function
f_{n}
on
\mathsf{X}^{n}
, given by
f_{n}(v)=f(\cdots x_{2}x_{1}v)
, such that
f_{n}|_{A_{n}}\equiv 0
and
f_{n}|_{B_{n}}\equiv 1
.
The previous computation shows that

\Capc_{\beta,\mu}(A_{n},B_{n})\leq\mathcal{E}_{\beta,\mu}(f)\leq\frac{1}{2}K^{\beta}C\left(\sum_{g}l(g)^{p}\mu(p)\right)\lambda^{n},

and since the
p
-moment of
\mu
is finite, this finishes the proof.
∎

2.13

.

Let

and

for any two distinct vertices

of

.

Proof.

Note first that since

is a contracting model, if we replace the basepoint

by a different basepoint
\xi_{0}^{\prime}
, then the corresponding sets

satisfy

for all

large enough. Therefore, without loss of generality, we can suppose that
\xi_{0}
is the image of a point
\widehat{\xi}_{0}\in\mathcal{X}_{G}
under the natural map
\mathcal{X}_{G}\to\Delta_{0}
given by Theorem

. We will apply Theorem

given by the pre-images of

in the statement of Theorem

5.3.
Proof of Liouville property under conformal dimension
<2

In this subsection, we complete the proof of the following statement on Liouville property.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be finitely generated contracting group such that
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}\mathcal{J}_{G}<2
. Let
p>p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
. Then every symmetric probability measure
\mu
on
G
with finite
p
-moment has trivial Poisson boundary.

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B})
be a finitely generated contracting group. If
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}\mathcal{J}_{G}<2
, then
G
is amenable.

In the proofs below we take terminology and notation from Subsection
2.5
. In particular, we suppose that
I\colon\Delta_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{B}^{\otimes n_{0}}\longrightarrow\Delta_{0}
is a contracting model, where
\Delta_{0}
is a locally finite connected graph, and after replacing
\mathfrak{B}
by
\mathfrak{B}^{n_{0}}
(which does not change the group
G
as an abstract group) we suppose that
n_{0}=1
. As in Subsection
2.5
, we also assume (upon passing to a barycentric subdivision) that no pair of neighbouring vertices of
\Delta_{0}
belong to the same
G
-orbit. Recall that then
\Gamma_{0}\colon=\Delta_{0}/G
is a finite graph with no loops. For a vertex
z
of
\Gamma_{0}
we define the sets
U_{z,n}
and the number of traverses
\tau_{n}
as in Notation
2.14
and Definition
2.15
, and are in position to apply Theorem
2.16
.

For clarity we first prove the theorem under the simplified assumption that
\mu
is symmetric and finitely supported.

Proof of Theorem
5.9
(finite support case).

Let
\mu
be a symmetric probability measure supported on the finite set
S
, and
\mathbf{g}_{t}=\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}
be the associated random walk. We wish to estimate the expected number of traverses
\mathbb{E}[\tau_{n}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1})]
. By Corollary
5.8
we have

\max_{z_{1},z_{2}}\Capc_{2,\mu}(U_{z_{1},n},U_{z_{2},n})\leq C\lambda^{n}
for some
C>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
, where the maximum is taken over all distinct vertices
z_{1},z_{2}
of
\Gamma_{0}
.

Say that a level
n
traverse from
z_{1}
to
z_{2}
starts at time
i
at a vertex
v\in U_{z_{1},n}
if there exists
w\in U_{z_{2},n}
and
j\geq i
so that
(i,j,v,w)
is a traverse from
z_{1}
to
z_{2}
. Denote this event by
A(i,v,z_{1},z_{2})
. Let
N_{i}
be the total number of traverses that start at time
i
. Then
N_{i}=\sum_{z_{1},z_{2}}\sum_{v\in U_{z_{1},n}}\mathbbm{1}_{A(i,v,z_{1},z_{2})}
. It follows from Lemma
5.1
that

\mathbb{E}[N_{i}]=\sum_{z_{1},z_{2}}\sum_{v\in U_{z_{1},n}}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{T}_{v\to U_{z_{2},n}}<\mathbf{T}_{v\to U_{z_{1},n}})\leq|\Gamma_{0}|^{2}C\lambda^{n},

where
|\Gamma_{0}|
stands for the size of the vertex set of
\Gamma_{0}
. Therefore, we have

\mathbb{E}[\tau_{n}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1})]\leq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[N_{i}]\leq|\Gamma_{0}|^{2}C\lambda^{n}t.

By Theorem
2.16
we deduce that there is
C_{1}>0
(not depending on
n
) such that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}[\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}l(\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v})]\leq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}[\tau_{n}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1})]\leq C_{1}\lambda^{n}.

Proposition
5.5
concludes the proof. ∎

We now move to the general case.

Proof of Theorem
5.9
.

First note that if
p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})<1
, then for every
p\in(p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B}),1)
the volume growth of
G
is bounded above by
\exp(Cn^{p})
for some
C>0
(see Proposition
4.5
and the discussion after it). It follows from a general argument that any measure on
G
(not necessarily symmetric) of finite
p
-moment,
p>p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
, has finite entropy and trivial Poisson boundary, see Corollary 2.3 in
[
16
]
.

For the rest of the proof we assume that
p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})\geq 1
. Let
\mu
be a measure on
G
of finite
p
-moment,
p>p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})
. In particular,
\mu
has finite first moment, and thus expectations of the length of sections
l(\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v})
are finite. By Corollary
5.8
, we have the capacity estimate

(9)

\max_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\Gamma_{0}}\Capc_{2,\mu}(U_{z_{1},n},U_{z_{2},n})\leq C\lambda^{n}.

The main difference from the finite-support case is that we can no longer apply directly Theorem
2.16
to a word
\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}
consisting of samples of
\mu
because the support of
\mu
is infinite.

Let us revisit the argument in the proof of Theorem
2.16
, using the same notation as there. Fix
\xi_{0}\in\Delta_{0}
, and let
\mathcal{T}\subset\Delta_{0}
be a compact subset containing
\xi_{0}
and such that
I^{|v|}(\xi\otimes v)\in\mathcal{T}
for every
\xi\in\mathcal{T}
and
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
(see Lemma
2.10

(i)
). By the Schwarz–Milnor lemma (and compactness of
\mathcal{T})
, we can also fix a constant
L>0
such that

(10)

L^{-1}l(g)-L\leq d(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\cdot g)\leq Ll(g)+L

for every
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
and every
g\in G
.

Let
\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{2}\mathbf{s}_{1}
be a sample of the random walk, and set
\mathbf{g}_{i}=\mathbf{s}_{i}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{2}\mathbf{s}_{1}
, with
\mathbf{g}_{0}=1
, and
\xi_{i}=\xi_{0}\cdot\mathbf{g}_{i}
. Let
n
be large enough and fix
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
. Choose a geodesic path
\delta_{i}
from
I^{n}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v)
to
I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v)
and let
\gamma=\delta_{t}\cdots\delta_{1}
be their concatenation. By the same arguments as in Theorem
2.16
, the length of
\mathbf{g}|_{v}
is bounded by an affine function of the total number of edges crossed by
\gamma
. Consider a crossing of an edge
e
corresponding to a subcurve
\gamma|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}
, and let
\delta_{i},\delta_{j}
be the subcurves containing
\gamma(t_{1})
and
\gamma(t_{2})
. If both
\delta_{i}
and
\delta_{j}
have length less than 1/3, then we can associate to the crossing of
e
a traverse of the word
\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{2}\mathbf{s}_{1}
. The number of edges crossed by
\gamma
whose beginning or end is inside a path
\delta_{i}
of length
\geq 1/3
is not larger than the length of
\delta_{i}
plus
2=6\times({1}/{3})
, and hence less than 7 times the length of
\delta_{i}
. We obtain the estimate

\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}l(\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v})\leq C|\mathsf{X}|^{n}+C\tau_{n}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdots\mathbf{s_{2}}\mathbf{s}_{1})\\
+C\sum_{i=1}^{t}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}\\
d(I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v))\geq 1/3\end{subarray}}d(I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v)),

for some
C>0
. By (
9
) and by the same argument as for finitely supported measures, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\tau_{n}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdots\mathbf{s_{2}}\mathbf{s}_{1})\right]\leq C_{1}\lambda^{n}\cdot t

for some
C_{1}>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
.
To conclude the proof, we need to bound the last sum in the estimate above.
First observe that

I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v)=I^{n}(\xi_{0}\otimes\mathbf{s}_{i}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}(v))\cdot\mathbf{s}_{i}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}|_{v}

and since the the action of
G
on
\Delta_{0}
is isometric and
\mathbf{s}_{i}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}|_{v}=(\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{\mathbf{s}_{i-1}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}(v)})\cdot(\mathbf{s}_{i-1}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}|_{v})
, we have

d\left(I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v)\right)=d\left(I^{n}(\xi_{0}\otimes\mathbf{s}_{i}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}(v))\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{\mathbf{s}_{i-1}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}(v)},I^{n}(\xi_{0}\otimes\mathbf{s}_{i-1}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}(v))\right).

Thus, by (
10
) we have

L^{-1}l(\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{\mathbf{s}_{i-1}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}(v)})-L\leq d\left(I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v)\right)\leq Ll(\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{\mathbf{s}_{i-1}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}(v)})+L

for every
i
and
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
. Note that in particular, if
d\left(I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v)\right)\geq 2L
, then

(2L)^{-1}l(\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{\mathbf{s}_{i-1}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}(v)})\leq d\left(I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v)\right)\leq 2Ll(\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{\mathbf{s}_{i-1}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}(v)}).

For
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
with
n\geq k
, let us denote by
\pi_{n-k}(v)
the left prefix of
v
of length
n-k
. Since the map
I
is contracting, there exists
C_{2}>0
and
\eta\in(0,1)
such that for every
n\geq k
we have

d(I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v))\leq C_{2}\eta^{k}d(I^{n-k}(\xi_{i}\otimes\pi_{n-k}(v))),I^{n-k}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes\pi_{n-k}(v))).

Hence there exists
k>0
(depending only
C_{2},\eta
) such that for every
n\geq k
, every
v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}
and every
i
such that
d(I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v))\geq 1/3
, we have

d((I^{n-k}(\xi_{i}\otimes\pi_{n-k}(v)),I^{n-k}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes\pi_{n-k}(v))\geq 4L.

The last inequality implies that

d((I^{n-k}(\xi_{i}\otimes\pi_{n-k}(v)),I^{n-k}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes\pi_{n-k}(v))\leq 2Ll(\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{\mathbf{s}_{i-1}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}(v)})

and that
l(\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{\mathbf{s}_{i-1}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}(v)})\geq 2
. Without loss of generality, we suppose that the generating set used to define
l(\cdot)
contains the nucleus
\mathcal{N}
, so, in particular, the last condition implies that
\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{\mathbf{s}_{i-1}\cdots\mathbf{s}_{1}(v)}\notin\mathcal{N}
. We can also suppose that
k
is large enough so that
d(I^{n-k}(\xi_{i}\otimes v),I^{n-k}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v))\leq d((I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes\pi_{n-k}(v)),I^{n}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes\pi_{n-k}(v))
. Since the map
\pi_{n-k}\colon\mathsf{X}^{n}\to\mathsf{X}^{n-k}
is
|\mathsf{X}|^{k}
-to-1, we deduce that for every
i
and
n\geq k
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}\\
d(I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v))\geq 1/3\end{subarray}}d(I^{n}(\xi_{i}\otimes v),I^{n}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v))\\
\leq|\mathsf{X}|^{k}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathsf{X}^{n-k}\\
d(I^{n-k}(\xi_{i}\otimes v),I^{n-k}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v))\geq 4L\end{subarray}}d(I^{n-k}(\xi_{i}\otimes v),I^{n-k}(\xi_{i-1}\otimes v))\\
\leq 2L|\mathsf{X}|^{k}\sum_{{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n-k},\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{v}\notin\mathcal{N},}}l(\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{v})^{p}.

In the last line we used that
l(\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{v})\leq l(\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{v})^{p}
since
p>1
and
l(\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{v})
is a non-negative integer. By Proposition
4.3
, there exist
C_{3}>0
and
\eta_{1}\in(0,1)
such that

\sum_{{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{v}\notin\mathcal{N}}}l(\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{v})^{p}\leq C_{3}\eta_{1}^{n}l(\mathbf{s}_{i})^{p}.

Since
\mu
has finite
p
-moment, taking the expectation on both sides we find

\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n},\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{v}\notin\mathcal{N}}}l(\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{v})^{p}\right)\leq C_{4}\eta_{1}^{n},\quad\quad C_{4}=C_{3}\mathbb{E}\left(l(\mathbf{s}_{1})^{p}\right).

Therefore there is a constant
C_{5}
(depending only on the previous constants
C,C_{1},\ldots
) such that for every
n\geq k
and every
t
we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}}l(\mathbf{g}_{t}|_{v})\right)\leq C|\mathsf{X}|^{n}+C\mathbb{E}\left(\tau_{n}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdots\mathbf{s_{2}}\mathbf{s}_{1})\right)+C\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n-k},\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{v}\notin\mathcal{N}}l(\mathbf{s}_{i}|_{v})^{p}\right)\\
\leq C|\mathsf{X}|^{n}+C_{5}(\lambda^{n}+\eta_{1}^{n-k})\cdot t.

Hence Proposition
5.5
concludes the proof. ∎

6.
Examples

6.1.
Automata of polynomial activity growth

Let
G
be a faithful self-similar group acting on
\mathsf{X}^{*}
. Consider, for
g\in G
, the function

n\mapsto\alpha_{g}(n)\colon=|\{v\in\mathsf{X}^{n}\colon g|_{v}\neq 1\}|

counting the number of non-trivial sections on the
n
th level. Since the total set of sections
\{g|_{v}\colon v\in\mathsf{X}^{*}\}
is finite,
\alpha_{g}(n)
is the number of paths in a finite directed graph (the
Moore diagram
with the trivial state removed) starting in a vertex. Therefore, the function
\alpha_{g}(n)
is either exponentially growing, or is bounded by a polynomial
[
49
, Theorem A]
. It is not hard to show that the subset of elements
g\in G
for which
\alpha_{g}(n)
is bounded from above by a polynomial of degree
d
is a subgroup of
G
. We say that
G
is
generated by an automaton of polynomial activity growth
if
\alpha_{g}(n)
is bounded by a polynomial for every
g\in G
.

Groups generated by automata of polynomial activity growth were introduced by S. Sidki in
[
49
]
(in greater generality than we described here). In particular, he showed that such groups can not have free subgroups.

A special sub-class of such groups are groups generated by
bounded automata
, i.e., consisting of elements
g
such that
\alpha_{g}(n)
is bounded. It includes iterated monodromy groups of all sub-hyperbolic polynomials (see
[
7
,
41
]
).

L. Bartholdi and B. Virág proved amenability of
\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(z^{2}-1\right)}
in
[
8
]
using random walks. Their methods were extended in
[
6
]
to prove that all groups generated by bounded automata are amenable. Later, this result was extended to groups for which
\alpha_{g}(n)
is bounded by polynomials of degree 1 (in
[
2
]
) and 2 (in
[
3
]
).

The following theorem is proved in
[
37
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

If
(G,\mathfrak{B})
is a self-similar contracting group such that its action on
\mathsf{X}^{*}
for some basis
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathfrak{B}
is a subgroup of the group of automata of polynomial activity growth, then
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}\mathcal{J}_{G}\leq 1
.

Corollary 6.2
.

If
(G,\mathfrak{B})
satisfies the conditions of the above theorem, and if
\mu
is a symmetric probability measure on
G
with a finite
p
-moment for some
p>1
, then the
\mu
-random walk is Liouville. In particular
G
is amenable.

Note that groups generated by bounded automata are always contracting, but this is no longer true for groups generated by automata of polynomial activity of degree at least 1. For example, the group generated by the wreath recursion
a=\sigma(1,a)
and
b=(b,a)
is generated by an automaton of linear activity growth and is not contracting. As shown by G. Amir and B. Virág in
[
4
]
, there are automata groups of polynomial activity that do not have the Liouville property for any finitely supported symmetric non-degenerate measure so that the contracting assumption is crucial in Corollary
6.2
.

It follows from the results of
[
27
]
that a sufficient condition for a group generated by an automaton of polynomial activity growth to be amenable is recurrence of the orbital graphs of its action on the boundary of the tree
\mathsf{X}^{*}
(see the proof of
[
27
, Theorem 5.1]
). This gives another way to conclude amenability of groups satisfying the conditions of Theorem
6.1
, see
[
37
]
. In contrast, the statement on the Liouville property in Corollary
6.2
is new (even for finitely supported measures when the activity degree is larger than 1). For bounded automata groups, it extends the results of
[
1
]
to measures with a moment condition (in that particular case the proof of Theorem
5.9
can actually be modified to include measures with finite first moment, by showing that Proposition
4.5
holds for
p=1
).

6.2.
Iterated monodromy groups of rational functions

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}(z)
be a sub-hyperbolic (e.g., post-critically finite) rational function. Then the canonical homeomorphism of the limit space of
\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f\right)}
with the Julia set of
f
is a quasi-symmetry with respect to the visual metric on the limit space and the spherical metric on the Julia set.

Proof.

This is a result of P. Haïssinksy and K. Pilgrim. Namely,
it is proved in
[
22
, Theorem 6.15]
that
the limit dynamical system
\mathsf{s}\colon\mathcal{J}_{G}\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}_{G}
of a contracting self-similar group is
coarse expanding conformal
with respect to a visual metric (see the definition in
[
21
]
).

It is also shown in
[
22
, Theorem 3.3]
that for every sub-hyperbolic rational function
f
the spherical metric restricted to a neighborhood of the Julia set of
f
is also coarse expanding conformal. By
[
21
, Theorem 2.8.2]
any topological conjugacy between coarse expanding conformal dynamical systems is a quasi-symmetry.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}(z)
be a sub-hyperbolic rational function such that its Julia set is not the whole sphere (equivalently, having at least one attracting cycle). Then
\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f\right)}
is amenable. Moreover, if
H\leq\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f\right)}
is any finitely generated subgroup, and if
\mu
is any symmetric probability measure on
H
with finite second moment, then the random walk generated by
\mu
is Liouville.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the second part of the statement, since a group is amenable if and only if all its finitely generated subgroups are. Upon replacing
H
by a larger finitely generated subgroup, we can assume that it is self-similar and contains the nucleus of
\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f\right)}
(with respect to some basis of the associated biset). Then
H
is itself a contracting self-similar group with the same limit space as
\mathop{\mathrm{IMG}\left(f\right)}
. By Theorems
6.3
and
5.9
, and
4.6
, it is enough to show that the Julia set of
f
has Hausdorff dimension strictly less than 2. This is shown in
[
35
, Corollary 6.2]
.
∎

th one, replace each piece with the corresponding one on the right part of the picture.

The same substitution rules from Figure
3
are also valid for the infinite orbital Schreier graphs of the action on the boundary
\mathsf{X}^{\omega}
of the tree
\mathsf{X}^{*}
. If we modify the Schreier graphs (of the action of either
\mathsf{X}^{*}
of
\mathsf{X}^{\omega}
) by imposing that the edges corresponding to
a
have length 0 (i.e. contracting them to a single vertex), and that the edges corresponding to

is finite). It follows then from the recurrent rules that the shift map
xv\mapsto v
contracts the distances twice. Since this map is four-to-one, it follows that the orbital Schreier graphs have quadratic growth.

6.3.
Sierpiński carpet group

Let
X=\{1,\cdots,8\}
be an alphabet. Consider the group
G
acting on
X^{*}
generated by the set
S=\{a,b,c,d\}
, given by the wreath recursion

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=(12)(67)(1,1,a,1,a,1,1,a)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=(46)(58)(b,b,b,1,1,1,1,1)

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=(23)(78)(c,1,1,c,1,c,1,1)

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=(14)(35)(1,1,1,1,1,d,d,d)

Its dual Moore diagram is shown in Figure
5
. All four generators are of order 2.

The limit space
\mathcal{J}_{G}
can be identified (up to quasisymmetry) with the classical Sierpiński carpet, obtained by successively subdividing a Euclidean square into nine equal squares and removing the middle one. The limit dynamical system
s\colon\mathcal{J}_{G}\to\mathcal{J}_{G}
can be described by first dilating the carpet by a factor of nine, and then folding it in the natural way, so that each of the eight tiles of the dilated carpet is mapped isometrically onto the original carpet (preserving the orientation for the four corner tiles, and reversing it for the four remaining ones).

Figure 5.
The Sierpiński carpet dual automaton

The products
ab,bc,cd
, and
ad
are of order 6. For example,

ab=(12)(476)(58)(b,b,ba,1,a,1,1,a),

so

(ab)^{2}=(476)(1,1,(ba)^{2},1,1,1,1,1),

and

(ab)^{6}=(1,1,(ba)^{6},1,1,1,1,1),

which implies that
(ab)^{6}=1
.

We have

ac=(123)(678)(c,a,1,c,a,c,a,1),

and

(ac)^{3}=(ac,ca,ac,c,a,ac,ca,ac),

hence
ac
has infinite order.

Looking at the cycles
(123)
and
(678)
of the action of
ac
on
\mathsf{X}
and the corresponding sections, we see that the Thurston’s
p
-map
T_{p}
satisfies

T_{p}([ac])=2\cdot 3^{1-p}[ac].

It is contracting only when
1-p<\frac{\log 2}{\log 3}
, i.e., for
p>\frac{\log 6}{\log 3}
. Consequently,
p_{c}(G,\mathfrak{B})\geq\frac{\log 6}{\log 3}
, by Theorem
4.10
. In particular, Theorem
4.6
provides the same lower bound for
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}(G,\mathfrak{B})
.

On the other hand, the usual metric on Sierpiński carpet gives the bound
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}(G,\mathfrak{B})\leq\frac{\log 8}{\log 3}
. The exact value of
\mathop{\mathrm{ARdim}}(G,\mathfrak{B})
is still unknown, see some
estimates in

]
.

6.4.
A non-Liouville contracting group

Not all contracting self-similar groups are Liouville even for finitely supported generating measures. Let us describe one counter-example.

Consider the group
H
of affine transformations of the space
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{3}
(where
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
is the ring of dyadic integers) of the form
\vec{x}\mapsto(-1)^{k}\vec{x}+\vec{v}
, where
\vec{v}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
and
k=0,1
. Note that
H
has a subgroup of index 2 isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
whose action is free, in particular, it is finitely generated and the orbital Schreier graphs of its action on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{3}
are quasi-isometric to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
.

We will denote the points of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{3}
as infinite matrices
\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}i_{11}&i_{12}&\ldots\\
i_{21}&i_{22}&\ldots\\
i_{31}&i_{32}&\ldots\end{array}\right)
in such a way that
i_{mn}\in\{0,1\}
, and if
\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}i_{11}&i_{12}&\ldots\\
i_{21}&i_{22}&\ldots\\
i_{31}&i_{32}&\ldots\end{array}\right)
represents
\vec{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{3}
, and the shifted matrix
\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}i_{12}&i_{13}&\ldots\\
i_{22}&i_{23}&\ldots\\
i_{32}&i_{33}&\ldots\end{array}\right)
represents
\vec{y}
, then

\vec{x}=-\left(\begin{array}[]{c}i_{1}\\
i_{2}\\
i_{3}\end{array}\right)+2\vec{y}.

Essentially, we are using the binary numeration system with digits
0
and
-1
, but write
1
instead of
-1
.

Denote, for
(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})\in\{0,1\}^{3}
, by
a_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}
the transformation
\vec{x}\mapsto-\vec{x}+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}i_{1}\\
i_{2}\\
i_{3}\end{array}\right)
. The elements
a_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}
generate the group
H
.

We have

a_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\left(-\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j_{1}\\
j_{2}\\
j_{3}\end{array}\right)+2\vec{y}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j_{1}\\
j_{2}\\
j_{3}\end{array}\right)-2\vec{y}+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}i_{1}\\
i_{2}\\
i_{3}\end{array}\right)=-\left(\begin{array}[]{c}k_{1}\\
k_{2}\\
k_{3}\end{array}\right)+2\left(-\vec{y}+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}l_{1}\\
l_{2}\\
l_{3}\end{array}\right)\right)

if
i_{t}+j_{t}=2l_{t}-k_{t}
. Equivalently,
l_{t}=\max(i_{t},j_{t})
and
k_{t}
is
i_{t}+l_{t}
modulo 2.

To shorten notation, let us denote the columns
\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\\
0\end{array}\right)
,
\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\\
1\end{array}\right)
,
\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
1\\
0\end{array}\right)
,

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
1\\
1\end{array}\right)
,

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\\
0\end{array}\right)
,

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\\
1\end{array}\right)
,

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\\
0\end{array}\right)
,

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\\
1\end{array}\right)
by

0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7
, respectively. Similarly, we will denote the generators
a_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}
by
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{7}
.
We have then

\displaystyle a_{0}

\displaystyle=(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{5},a_{6},a_{7}),

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=(01)(23)(45)(67)(a_{1},a_{1},a_{3},a_{3},a_{5},a_{5},a_{7},a_{7}),

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=(02)(13)(46)(57)(a_{2},a_{3},a_{2},a_{3},a_{6},a_{7},a_{6},a_{7}),

\displaystyle a_{3}

\displaystyle=(03)(12)(47)(56)(a_{3},a_{3},a_{3},a_{3},a_{7},a_{7},a_{7},a_{7}),

\displaystyle a_{4}

\displaystyle=(04)(15)(26)(37)(a_{4},a_{5},a_{6},a_{7},a_{4},a_{5},a_{6},a_{7}),

\displaystyle a_{5}

\displaystyle=(05)(14)(27)(36)(a_{5},a_{5},a_{7},a_{7},a_{5},a_{5},a_{7},a_{7}),

\displaystyle a_{6}

\displaystyle=(06)(17)(24)(35)(a_{6},a_{7},a_{6},a_{7},a_{6},a_{7},a_{6},a_{7}),

\displaystyle a_{7}

\displaystyle=(07)(16)(25)(34)(a_{7},a_{7},a_{7},a_{7},a_{7},a_{7},a_{7},a_{7}).

Note that
a_{0}
is not a section of any of the other generators
a_{i}
.
Let us “fragment”
a_{0}
into a product of two commuting elements
b
and
c
given by

b=(b,a_{1},1,1,1,1,1,1),\qquad c=(c,1,a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{5},a_{6},a_{7}).

Note that
b,c\notin G
and
bc=a_{0}
.

Let
G
be the group generated by
H
and
b,c
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\mu
be the uniform probability measure on the generating set
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots 7}\cup\{b,c\}
of
G
. Then the
\mu
-random walk is not Liouville.

Proof.

It follows directly from the recursion that
if not all letters of
v\in\mathsf{X}^{*}
are 0 and the first non-zero letter of
v
is
1
, then
b(v)=a_{0}(v)
and
b|_{v}=a_{0}|_{v}
. If not all letters of
v
are
0
and the first non-zero letter is not
1
, then
b(v)=v
and
b|_{v}=1
. An analogous statement (but the other way around) is true for
c
.

Define the map
\chi\colon G\longrightarrow\{0,1\}
by the condition
that
\chi(g)=0
if
g^{-1}|_{\underbrace{00\ldots 0}_{\text{$n$ times}}}\in H
for all
n
large enough, and
\chi(g)=1
otherwise.

Then, according to the first paragraph of the proof, if
g\in G
and
s\in\{a_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,7}\cup\{b,c\}
is a generator, then
\chi(g)\neq\chi(sg)
only if
g(00\ldots)=00\ldots
and
s\in\{b,c\}
.

Consider the
\mu
-random walk on
G
. The induced random walk on the
G
-orbit of
000\ldots
coincides with a random walk generated by a finitely supported measure on the generating set
\{a_{i}\}_{i=0,1,\ldots,7}\cup\{1\}
of
H
. Since the Schreier graph is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, and simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
is transient by Pólya’s theorem, it follows from the comparison theorem (see e.g.,
[
33
, Theorem 2.17]
) that this random walk is transient. Consequently, it will visit
000\ldots
finitely many times almost surely, and so the value of
\chi
along a trajectory of the walk will be eventually constant. The eventual value of
\chi
will be a non-trivial event invariant under the tail equivalence relation, hence the Poisson boundary of the random walk is non-trivial.
∎

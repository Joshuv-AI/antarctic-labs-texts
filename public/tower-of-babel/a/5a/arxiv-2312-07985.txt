Inverse problems for third–order nonlinear perturbations of biharmonic operators
Sombuddha Bhattacharyya, Katya Krupchyk, Suman Kumar Sahoo, Gunther Uhlmann

Abstract
. We study inverse boundary problems for third–order nonlinear tensorial perturbations of biharmonic operators on a bounded domain in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , where n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . By imposing appropriate assumptions on the nonlinearity, we demonstrate that the Dirichlet–to–Neumann map, known on the boundary of the domain, uniquely determines the genuinely nonlinear tensorial third-order perturbations of the biharmonic operator. The proof relies on the inversion of certain generalized momentum ray transforms on symmetric tensor fields. Notably, the corresponding inverse boundary problem for linear tensorial third-order perturbations of the biharmonic operator remains an open question.

2.1.
Symmetric tensor fields

Let
n\geq 2
, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Let
T^{m}:=T^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the complex vector space of functions
\underbrace{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\dots\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}_{\text{m times}}\to\mathbb{C}
which are
\mathbb{R}
–linear in each of its argument, and let
S^{m}:=S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be its subspace which consists of functions symmetric in all arguments. The elements of
T^{m}
are called tensors of degree
m
while the elements of
S^{m}
are said to be symmetric tensors of degree
m
. In particular,
S^{0}=\mathbb{C}
and
S^{1}=\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
\sigma:T^{m}\to S^{m}
be the canonical projection (symmetrization) defined by

(2.1)

\sigma u(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{m}}u(x_{\pi(1)},\dots,x_{\pi(m)}),

where the summation is taken over the group
\Pi_{m}
of all permutations of the set
\{1,\dots,m\}
.

Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
be a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the numbers
u_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}=u(e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{m}})
are said to be the covariant coordinates or components of
u\in T^{m}
. A tensor
u\in T^{m}
belongs to
S^{m}
if and only of its covariant components
u_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}
are symmetric with respect to all indices. Together with covariant coordinates
u_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}
of the tensor
u
, one can define the contravariant coordinates
u^{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}
, see
[
55
, page 23]
. In the case of the Euclidean metric tensor
g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}
, we have
u^{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}=u_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}
.

The scalar product on
T^{m}
is defined by

(2.2)

\langle u,v\rangle=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}=1}^{n}u^{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}\bar{v}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}},\quad u,v\in T^{m}.

For
u\in T^{k}
and
v\in T^{m}
, the tensor product
u\otimes v\in T^{k+m}
is given by

u\otimes v(x_{1},\dots,x_{k+m})=u(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})v(x_{k+1},\dots,x_{k+m}).

Together with the full symmetrization (
2.1
), given indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{p}
, the operator of partial symmetrization of a tensor
u\in T^{m}
with respect to
i_{1},\dots,i_{p}
,
p\leq m
, is defined by

(2.3)

\sigma(i_{1},\dots,i_{p})u_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}=\frac{1}{p!}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{p}}u_{i_{\pi(1)}\dots i_{\pi(p)}i_{p+1}\dots i_{m}}.

We define the operator
i_{\delta}
of symmetric multiplication by the Euclidean metric tensor
\delta
as follows,

(2.4)

i_{\delta}:S^{m}\to S^{m+2},\quad(i_{\delta}u)_{i_{1}\dots i_{m+2}}=\sigma(i_{1},\dots,i_{m+2})(u_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}\delta_{i_{m+1}i_{m+2}}).

We also define the operator
j_{\delta}
, dual to
i_{\delta}
, as follows,

(2.5)

j_{\delta}:S^{m}\to S^{m-2},\quad(j_{\delta}u)_{i_{1}\dots i_{m-2}}=\sum_{i_{m-1},i_{m}=1}^{n}u_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}\delta^{i_{m-1}i_{m}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}u_{i_{1}\dots i_{m-2}kk}.

We also set
j_{\delta}f=0
for
f\in S^{0}
and
f\in S^{1}
. If for
f\in S^{m}
,
j_{\delta}f=0
, we say that
f
is a trace free tensor.

For
m\geq 0
, the following commutation formula holds on
S^{m}
,

(2.6)

j_{\delta}i_{\delta}=\frac{2(n+2m)}{(m+1)(m+2)}E+\frac{m(m-1)}{(m+1)(m+2)}i_{\delta}j_{\delta},

where
E
is the identity operator on
S^{m}
, see
[
18
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. We denote by
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega;S^{m})
the space of smooth compactly supported symmetric
m
–tensor fields on
\Omega
,
and by
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\Omega;S^{m})
the space of compactly supported
m
–tensor field-distributions.
We let
L^{p}(\Omega;S^{m})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, be the space of
L^{p}
integrable
m
–tensor fields on
\Omega
, and let
L^{\infty}(\Omega;S^{m})
be the spaces of bounded almost everywhere
m
–tensor fields on
\Omega
. We define the scalar product on
L^{2}(\Omega;S^{m})
by

(u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega;S^{m})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle u(x),v(x)\rangle dx,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the scalar product on
S^{m}
given by (
2.2
).

2.2.
Momentum ray transforms and their injectivity

Let

T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}=\{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|=1,\ x\cdot\xi=0\}

be the tangent bundle of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Following
[
54
]
, see also

[
55
,
29
,
30
,
14
,
51
]
, for
f^{(m)}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})

we define the
k

\widetilde{I}^{m,k}
by

(2.7)

\widetilde{I}^{m,k}f^{(m)}(x,\xi)=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{k}f^{(m)}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x+t\xi)\xi_{i_{1}}\dots\xi_{i_{m}}dt,

where
(x,\xi)\in T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. One can easily see that

(2.8)

\widetilde{I}^{m,k}:C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})\to C_{0}(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}),\quad\widetilde{I}^{m,k}:C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})\to C^{\infty}_{0}(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}),

where
C_{0}(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
(
C^{\infty}_{0}(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
) is the spaces of continuous (smooth) functions on
T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
that have compact support in the first variable.

When
k=0
,
\widetilde{I}^{m,0}
is the classical ray transform. When
m=0
, the transform
\widetilde{I}^{0,0}
is the main mathematical tool of Computer Tomography. When
m=1
, the operator
\widetilde{I}^{1,0}
is known as the Doppler transform and is the key mathematical tool of Doppler Tomography. When
k>0
and
m=0
, the operator
\widetilde{I}^{0,k}
appears in the study of the inversion of the cone transform and conical Radon transform whereas the latter transform has an application in Compton cameras, see
[
39
]
.

We observe that (
2.7
) also makes sense when
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
and
f^{(m)}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})
. We thus set

(2.9)

I^{m,k}f^{(m)}(x,\xi)=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{k}f^{(m)}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x+t\xi)\xi_{i_{1}}\dots\xi_{i_{m}}dt,

where
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. A direct computation shows that the function
I^{m,k}f^{(m)}(x,\xi)
has the following homogeneity property in the second variable,

(2.10)

(I^{m,k}f^{(m)})(x,\tau\xi)=\frac{\tau^{m-k}}{|\tau|}(I^{m,k}f^{(m)})(x,\xi),\quad\tau\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},

and the following property in the first variable,

(2.11)

(I^{m,k}f^{(m)})(x+\tau\xi,\xi)=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\begin{pmatrix}k\\
l\end{pmatrix}(-\tau)^{k-l}(I^{m,l}f^{(m)})(x,\xi),\quad\tau\in\mathbb{R},

see
[
29

. It follows from (

) and (

(2.12)

\displaystyle(I^{m,k}f^{(m)})(x,\xi)=

\displaystyle|\xi|^{m-2k-1}\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-l}\begin{pmatrix}k\\
l\end{pmatrix}|\xi|^{l}(\xi\cdot x)^{k-l}

\displaystyle\times(I^{m,l}f^{(m)})\bigg(x-\frac{\xi\cdot x}{|\xi|^{2}}\xi,\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\bigg),

see
[
29
, formula (2.6)]
. Note that here
(x-\frac{\xi\cdot x}{|\xi|^{2}}\xi,\frac{\xi}{|\xi|})\in T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Therefore, using (

), we see from (
2.12
) that

(2.13)

\displaystyle I^{m,k}:C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})\to C(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})),

\displaystyle I^{m,k}:C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})).

We can also consider the
k
th momentum ray transform, defined by (

), for
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
f^{(m)}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})
. This transform will be denoted as

, i.e

(2.14)

J^{m,k}f^{(m)}(x,\xi)=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{k}f^{(m)}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x+t\xi)\xi_{i_{1}}\dots\xi_{i_{m}}dt,

where
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. We have
J^{m,k}f^{(m)}=I^{m,k}f^{(m)}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, and therefore,

(2.15)

J^{m,k}:C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})\to C(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}),\ J^{m,k}:C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}).

When
f^{(m)}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})
, (

(2.16)

I^{m,k}f^{(m)}(x,\xi)=|\xi|^{m-k-1}J^{m,k}f^{(m)}\bigg(x,\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\bigg),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

Hence, the operators
I^{m,k}
and

, defined on

, provide equivalent information.

In what follows, we shall only consider the momentum ray transforms
I^{m,k}
and
J^{m,k}
. We shall next extend the definition (

) of

to
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})
. To that end, we let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}))
be the spaces of distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
.
The
k
th momentum ray transform

on

(2.17)

\displaystyle I^{m,k}:\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})),\quad\langle I^{m,k}f^{(m)},\psi\rangle=\langle f^{(m)},(I^{m,k})^{*}\psi\rangle,

and

given by

(2.18)

\displaystyle(I^{m,k})^{*}:C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}))\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}),

\displaystyle((I^{m,k})^{*}\psi)_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{k}\xi_{i_{1}}\dots\xi_{i_{m}}\psi(x-t\xi,\xi)dtd\xi,\quad 1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\leq n,

[

Let

Let

where

is given by (
2.9
) with

. Let
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathcal{S}^{m}):=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{0})\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{1})\dots\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})

k

can be extended to
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathcal{S}^{m})

\displaystyle I^{m,k}:\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathcal{S}^{m})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})),\quad\langle I^{m,k}F,\psi\rangle=\sum_{l=0}^{m}\langle f^{(l)},(I^{l,k})^{*}\psi\rangle,

Next, we state the injectivity results for the momentum ray transforms. Let us first recall that when

, the ray transform

always has a nontrivial kernel. Indeed, any symmetric tensor field can be decomposed uniquely into its solenoidal and potential parts, see
[

Theorem 2.1
.

[
14
, Theorem 4.5]

Let
F\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathcal{S}^{m})
. If
I^{m,k}F=0
for all

, then
F=0
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
f^{(m)}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})
. If
I^{m,k}f^{(m)}=0
for all
k=0,1,\dots,m
, then
f^{(m)}=0
.

The following stronger injectivity result holds.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
14
, Theorem 4.9]

Let
F\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathcal{S}^{m})
. If

then
F=0
.

We extend the operator
J^{m,k}
to
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})

(2.20)

\displaystyle J^{m,k}:\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}),\quad\langle J^{m,k}f^{(m)},\psi\rangle=\langle f^{(m)},(J^{m,k})^{*}\psi\rangle.

Here
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and
(J^{m,k})^{*}
is the adjoint of
J^{m,k}
given by

(2.21)

\displaystyle(J^{m,k})^{*}:C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}),

\displaystyle((J^{m,k})^{*}\psi)_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{k}\xi_{i_{1}}\dots\xi_{i_{m}}\psi(x-t\xi,\xi)dtdS(\xi),\ 1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\leq n,

where

, see
[

. One can easily see that when

, the
k
th momentum ray transform

defined by (

2.20
).

Let

). The

) with

. Furthermore,

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathcal{S}^{m})

\displaystyle J^{m,k}:\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathcal{S}^{m})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}),\quad\langle J^{m,k}F,\psi\rangle=\sum_{l=0}^{m}\langle f^{(l)},(J^{l,k})^{*}\psi\rangle,

In what follows, we shall need some properties of the

(2.22)

\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}

(2.24)

see
[

We shall need the following result established in

[

Let

(ii)

(iii)

, when
m=1

(iv)

, when

.

Remark 2.5
.

It follows from Theorem

and Theorem

To prove Theorem

, we need to utilize a result related to the injectivity of the generalized momentum ray transform, as defined in (

Lemma 2.6
.

[
14
, Lemma 3.7]

Let
n\geq 3
,
e_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let

with some

Assume that

(2.25)

\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{m}f^{(m)}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(y_{0}^{\prime}+t\eta^{\prime})(e_{1}+i\eta)_{i_{1}}\dots(e_{1}+i\eta)_{i_{m}}dt=0,

for all

and all

such that

and

f^{(m)}=\begin{cases}i_{\delta}v^{(m-2)},&m=2,3,\\
0,&m=1,\end{cases}

where

We can derive the following immediate consequence of Lemma
2.6
.

.

For

or
3

2.6
are met, and if, additionally,

, it follows that

.

In the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we will also require the following result, which addresses the injectivity of the generalized momentum ray transforms applied to a sum of tensor fields of order one and two, see

for a similar result. The proof of this result will be presented in Appendix

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

,

Assume that

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{2+k}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{1+k}f^{(1)}_{i_{1}}(y_{0}^{\prime}+t\eta^{\prime})(e_{1}+i\eta)_{i_{1}}dt=0,

for all

such that

and

, and

. Then

f^{(2)}=i_{\delta}v^{(0)},\quad f^{(1)}=0,

where

.

We have the following immediate consequence of Lemma

.

.

Assuming that all the conditions of Lemma

and

.

3.
Construction of biharmonic functions

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, be a bounded open set with
C^{\infty}
boundary. In this section, we aim to construct complex geometric optics (CGO) solutions to the biharmonic equation

(3.1)

\Delta^{2}u=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

which are necessary for proving Theorem
1.4
. Although the construction follows a standard approach as outlined in
[
31
,
32
,
14
]
, we present it here to address the need for solutions with enhanced regularity. Specifically, the constructed CGO solutions should belong to
C^{4,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
rather than the commonly used
H^{4}(\Omega)
, see the integral identity (
1.8
) as well as (
5.5
) and (
5.14
) below. Additionally, since the integral identity (
1.8
) in Theorem
1.4
involves products of three biharmonic functions and their derivatives up to the third order, it is advantageous to estimate the norms of the derivatives of the remainders up to the third order in the
L^{\infty}
-norm. Therefore, we need to construct CGO solutions that exhibit small remainders measured in the
C^{3}(\overline{\Omega})
-norm instead of the commonly used
H^{4}_{\text{scl}}(\Omega)
-norm in existing constructions. Consequently, we proceed with the construction of CGO solutions to (
3.1
) that possess
C^{5}(\overline{\Omega})
regularity, accompanied by small remainders in
C^{5}(\overline{\Omega})
.

Our starting point is the following Carleman estimate for the semiclassical Laplacian, which is established in
[
27
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and let
\varphi(x)=\alpha\cdot x
. Then given any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have for all
h>0
small enough and all
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
,

(3.2)

h\|u\|_{H^{s}_{\emph{\text{scl}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|e^{\frac{\varphi}{h}}(-h^{2}\Delta)e^{-\frac{\varphi}{h}}u\|_{H^{s}_{\emph{\textrm{scl}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad C>0.

Here
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, is the standard Sobolev space, equipped with the semiclassical norm
\|u\|_{H^{s}_{\text{scl}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\langle hD\rangle^{s}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, where
\langle\xi\rangle=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}
.

Iterating the Carleman estimate (
3.2
) twice, we obtain the following Carleman estimate for the biharmonic operator,

(3.3)

h^{2}\|u\|_{H^{s}_{\text{scl}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|e^{\frac{\varphi}{h}}(-h^{2}\Delta)^{2}e^{-\frac{\varphi}{h}}u\|_{H^{s}_{\textrm{scl}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad C>0,

for all
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
and
h>0
small enough.

Using a standard argument, we convert the Carleman estimate (
3.3
) into the following solvability result, see

[
33
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, let
\varphi(x)=\alpha\cdot x
, and let
s\in\mathbb{R}
. If
h>0
is small enough, then for any
v\in H^{s}(\Omega)
, there is a solution
u\in H^{s}(\Omega)
of the equation

e^{\frac{\varphi}{h}}(-h^{2}\Delta)^{2}e^{-\frac{\varphi}{h}}u=v\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

which satisfies the bound

\|u\|_{H^{s}_{\emph{\text{scl}}}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C}{h^{2}}\|v\|_{H^{s}_{\emph{\text{scl}}}(\Omega)}.

Here

H^{s}(\Omega)=\{V|_{\Omega}:V\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\},\quad s\in\mathbb{R},

equipped with the norm

\|v\|_{H^{s}_{\text{scl}}(\Omega)}=\inf_{V\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),v=V|_{\Omega}}\|V\|_{H^{s}_{\text{scl}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
. We shall construct CGO solutions to the equation (
3.1
) in the form

(3.4)

u(x;h)=e^{\frac{x\cdot\zeta}{h}}(a(x;h)+r(x;h)),

where
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\zeta\cdot\zeta=0
,
|\text{Re}\,\zeta|=|\text{Im}\,\zeta|\sim 1
, the amplitude
a\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, and the remainder satisfies
\|r\|_{H^{l}_{\text{scl}}(\Omega)}\to 0
as
h\to 0
.

Now
u
given by (
3.4
) is a solution to (
3.1
) provided that

(3.5)

e^{-\frac{x\cdot\zeta}{h}}(-h^{2}\Delta)^{2}e^{\frac{x\cdot\zeta}{h}}r=-(-h^{2}\Delta-2\zeta\cdot h\nabla)^{2}a\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega.

Letting

T=\zeta\cdot\nabla,

(
3.5
) can be rewritten as follows,

(3.6)

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{x\cdot\text{Re}\,\zeta}{h}}(-h^{2}\Delta)^{2}e^{\frac{x\cdot\zeta}{h}}r

\displaystyle=-e^{\frac{ix\cdot\text{Im}\,\zeta}{h}}(h^{2}\Delta+2hT)^{2}a

\displaystyle=-e^{\frac{ix\cdot\text{Im}\,\zeta}{h}}(h^{4}\Delta^{2}+4h^{3}T\Delta+4h^{2}T^{2})a\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega.

We shall look for the amplitude in the form,

a(x;h)=a_{0}(x)+ha_{1}(x)+\dots+h^{k}a_{k}(x),

for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where
a_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies the first transport equation,

(3.7)

T^{2}a_{0}=0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n},

and
a_{j}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
solves the
j
th transport equation,

(3.8)

T^{2}a_{j}=-T\Delta a_{j-1}-\frac{1}{4}\Delta^{2}a_{j-2}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

j=1,2,\dots,k
. Here we set
a_{-1}=0
.

In the proof of Theorem
1.4
we shall need CGO solutions (
3.1
) of the form (
3.4
) with
\zeta=\mu(e_{1}+i\eta_{2})
, where
0\neq\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. Here
e_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\eta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is such that
e_{1}\cdot\eta_{2}=0
and
|\eta_{2}|=1
. In this case, the first transport equation (
3.7
) becomes

(3.9)

((e_{1}+i\eta_{2})\cdot\nabla)^{2}a_{0}=0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We shall proceed to solve (
3.9
). To that end, we complete
e_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
to an orthonormal basis in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and denote it by
\{e_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3},\dots,\eta_{n}\}
. We denote by
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})
,
y_{1}=x_{1}
, the coordinates with respect to this basis. In these coordinates (
3.9
) becomes

(3.10)

(\partial_{y_{1}}+i\partial_{y_{2}})^{2}a_{0}=0.

Letting
z=y_{1}+iy_{2}
be a complex variable,
\partial_{\bar{z}}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{y_{1}}+i\partial_{y_{2}})
, and making a change of coordinates
y\mapsto(z,y^{\prime\prime})
,
y^{\prime\prime}=(y_{3},\dots,y_{n})
, the equation (
3.10
) reduces to

(3.11)

\partial_{\bar{z}}^{2}a_{0}=0.

Functions
a_{0}(z)
that satisfy (
3.11
) are called polyanalytic of order two, see

[
10
]
. One can easily see that any solution of (
3.11

We shall now proceed to solve the

Note that (

Appendix A
Well-posedness of the Dirichlet problem for a third-order nonlinear perturbation of a biharmonic operator

In this appendix, we shall show the well-posedness of the Dirichlet problem for a third-order nonlinear perturbation of a biharmonic operator. The argument is standard, see
[
35
]
,

[
42
]
, and is presented here for completeness and convenience of the reader.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, be a bounded open set with
C^{\infty}
boundary. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
0<\alpha<1
, and let
C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
be the standard Hölder space, see
[
23
]
. Note that
C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
is an algebra under pointwise multiplication, with

(A.1)

\|uv\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\big(\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}\big),\quad u,v\in C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}),

see
[
23
, Theorem A.7]
.

Consider the Dirichlet problem for the operator
L_{A^{(1)},A^{(2)},A^{(3)},q}
, given by (
1.1
),

(A.2)

\begin{cases}L_{A^{(1)},A^{(2)},A^{(3)},q}u=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
u|_{\partial\Omega}=f,\\
\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega}=g,\end{cases}

where
\nu
is the unit outer normal to the boundary. Here

A^{(j)}:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{C}\to S^{j}
,
j=1,2,3
, and
q:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfy the conditions:

(i)

the map
\mathbb{C}\ni z\mapsto A^{(j)}(\cdot,z)
is holomorphic with values in
C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega};S^{j})
for some
0<\alpha<1
, the space of Hölder continuous symmetric
j
–tensor fields,

(ii)

the map
\mathbb{C}\ni z\mapsto q(\cdot,z)
is holomorphic with values in
C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
,

(iii)

A^{(j)}(x,0)=0
,
q(x,0)=0
, and
\partial_{z}q(x,0)=0
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,
j=1,2,3
.

It follows from (i), (ii), (iii) that
A^{(j)}
,
j=1,2,3
, and
q
can be expanded into power series

(A.3)

A^{(j)}(x,z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A^{(j),k}(x)\frac{z^{k}}{k!},\quad A^{(j),k}(x):=\partial_{z}^{k}A^{(j)}(x,0)\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega};S^{j}),

converging in the
C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega};S^{j})

(A.4)

q(x,z)=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}q^{k}(x)\frac{z^{k}}{k!},\quad q^{k}(x):=\partial_{z}^{k}q(x,0)\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}),

converging in the

In what follows, when
X,Y
are Banach spaces, we equip

with the norm
\|(x,y)\|_{X\times Y}=\|x\|_{X}+\|y\|_{Y}
. Then
X\times Y
becomes a Banach space. We have the following result.

Theorem A.1
.

Assume that
A^{(j)}:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{C}\to S^{j}
,
j=1,2,3
, and
q:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfy the conditions (i), (ii), and (iii). Then there exist
\delta>0
and

such that for any
(f,g)\in B_{\delta}(\partial\Omega):=\{(f,g)\in C^{4,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)\times C^{3,\alpha}(\partial\Omega):\|f\|_{C^{4,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)}+\|g\|_{C^{3,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)}<\delta\}

A.2
) has a solution

satisfying

The solution is unique within the class
\{u\in C^{4,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}):\|u\|_{C^{4,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}<C\delta\}
, and it depends holomorphically on
(f,g)\in B_{\delta}(\partial\Omega)
. Furthermore, the map

B_{\delta}(\partial\Omega)\to C^{2,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)\times C^{1,\alpha}(\partial\Omega),\quad(f,g)\mapsto(\partial_{\nu}^{2}u|_{\partial\Omega},\partial_{\nu}^{3}u|_{\partial\Omega})

is holomorphic.

Proof.

We shall follow
[
35
, Appendix A]
, see also
[
42
]
, and rely on the implicit function theorem for holomorphic maps between complex Banach spaces, see
[
50
, p. 144]
. In doing so, we consider the map

(A.5)

\displaystyle F:C^{4,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)\times C^{3,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)\times C^{4,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\to C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\times C^{4,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)\times C^{3,\alpha}(\partial\Omega),

\displaystyle F(f,g,u)=\big(L_{A^{(1)},A^{(2)},A^{(3)},q}u,u|_{\partial\Omega}-f,\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega}-g\big).

Let us check that

enjoys the mapping properties (
A.5
). To that end let

and first note that
(-\Delta)^{2}u\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
. Let
j=1,2,3
and let us now check that

. As in
[
35
]
, using Cauchy’s estimates, we see that the coefficients

in (

) satisfy

\|A^{(j),k}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega};S^{j})}\leq\frac{k!}{R^{k}}\sup_{|z|=R}\|A^{(j)}(\cdot,z)\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega};S^{j})},

where

and
k=1,2,\dots
. It follows from (
A.6
) and (

) that

(A.7)

\bigg\|A^{(j),k}\frac{u^{k}}{k!}\bigg\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega};S^{j})}\leq\frac{C^{k}}{R^{k}}\|u\|^{k}_{C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}\sup_{|z|=R}\|A^{(j)}(\cdot,z)\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega};S^{j})}.

Choosing

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A^{(j),k}(x)\frac{u^{k}}{k!}

Let us next show that

, given by (

), is holomorphic. First,

is locally bounded as it is continuous in

. Therefore, it suffices to check that

is weakly holomorphic, see
[
50
, p. 133]

, locally uniformly in

, see (

).

Now we have

and the partial differential

\partial_{u}F(0,0,0):C^{4,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\to C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\times C^{4,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)\times C^{3,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)

, see also

An application of the implicit function theorem, see
[
50
, p. 144]
, gives that there exists
\delta>0

such that

for all

. Setting

and noting that

is Lipschitz continuous with

, we get

\|u\|_{C^{4,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C(\|f\|_{C^{4,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)}+\|g\|_{C^{3,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)}).

∎

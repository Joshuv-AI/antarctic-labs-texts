Long-time asymptotic behavior for the complex short pulse equation
Jian Xu, Engui Fan*

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the initial value problem for the complex short pulse equation with a Wadati-Konno-Ichikawa type Lax pair. We show that the solution to the initial value problem has a parametric expression in terms of the solution of 2 × 2 2\times 2 -matrix Riemann-Hilbert problem, from which an implicit one-soliton solution is obtained on the discrete spectrum. While on the continuous spectrum we further establish the explicit long-time asymptotic behavior of the non-soliton solution by using Deift-Zhou nonlinear steepest descent method. Key words: Complex short pulse equation; Initial value problem; Riemann-Hilbert problem; Nonlinear steepest descent method; Long-time asymptotics.

[

The CSP equation can be viewed as an analogue of the NLS equation

It is founded that the CSP equation also admitted a Wadati-Konno-Ichikawa (WKI)-type Lax pair like the SP equation

The soliton solutions were obtained by using Hirota method

Recently, in

Organization of this paper is as follows. In section 2, since the associated Lax pair of short pulse (

3.
The Riemann-Hilbert problem for CSP equation

Let us define

M(x,t,k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}[\mu_{2}]_{1}&\displaystyle{\frac{[\mu_{1}]_{2}}{a(k)}}\end{array}\right),&k\in D_{1},\\[9.0pt]
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle{\frac{[\mu_{2}]_{1}}{\overline{a(\bar{k})}}}&[\mu_{1}]_{2}\end{array}\right),&k\in D_{2}.\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

From the definition (
3.1
) and (
2.28
), we can deduce that
M(x,t,k)
admits the symmetry

\overline{M(x,t,\bar{k})}=\sigma_{2}M(x,t,k)\sigma_{2}.

(3.2)

and satisfies the following Riemann-Hilbert problem:

•

Jump condition: The two limiting values

M_{\pm}(x,t,k)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}M_{\pm}(x,t,k\pm i\varepsilon),\quad k\in{\mathbb{R}},

(3.3)

are related by

M_{+}(x,t,k)=M_{-}(x,t,k)J(x,t,k),\quad k\in{\mathbb{R}},

(3.4)

where

J(x,t,k)=e^{ikp(x,t,k)\hat{\sigma}_{3}}J_{0}(k),

(3.5)

J_{0}(k)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&r(k)\\
\overline{r(k)}&1+|r(k)|^{2}\end{array}\right),\ \ r(k)=\frac{b(k)}{a(k)}.

(3.6)

•

Normalize condition as
k\rightarrow\infty

M(x,t,k)=\mathbb{I}+O(\frac{1}{k}).

(3.7)

In order to get the information of the solution
u(x,t)
, we should consider the asymptotic behavior of
M(x,t,k)
as
k\rightarrow 0
, that is,

M(x,t,k)=e^{-d_{-}\sigma_{3}}G^{-1}(x,t)\left[\mathbb{I}+k(ic_{+}\sigma_{3}+i\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&u\\
\bar{u}&0\end{array}\right))+O(k^{2})\right]e^{d\sigma_{3}},

(3.8)

where

c_{+}=\int_{x}^{+\infty}(\sqrt{m(x^{\prime},t)}-1)dx^{\prime}.

(3.9)

Equations (
3.8
) show that the matrix-valued function
M(x,t,k)
contains all necessary information for reconstructing the solution of the initial value problem of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in terms of the solution of a matrix-valued Riemann-Hilbert problem.

However, the jump relation (
3.5
) cannot
be used immediately for recovering the solution of CSP equation (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). Since, in the representation of the jump matrix

e^{ikp(x,t,k)\hat{\sigma}_{3}}J_{0}(k)
the factor
J_{0}(k)
is indeed given in terms of the known initial
data
u_{0}(x)
but
p(x,t,k)
is not, it involves
m(x,t)
which is unknown.

To overcome this, we introduce the new (time-dependent) scale

y(x,t)=x-\int_{x}^{+\infty}(\sqrt{m(x^{\prime},t)}-1)dx^{\prime}=x-c_{+}(x,t),

(3.10)

which make the jump matrix explicit, however, the solution of the initial problem
can be given only in implicit form: it will be given in terms of
functions in the new scale, whereas the original scale will also be given in terms
of functions in the new scale.

By the definition of the new scale
y(x,t)
, we define

\tilde{M}(y,t,k)=M(x(y,t),t,k),

(3.11)

then we can obtain the Riemann-Hilbert problem of
\tilde{M}(y,t,k)
as follows:

•

Analyticity:
\tilde{M}(y,t,k)
is analytic in the two open half-planes
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, and continuous up to the boundary
k\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

•

Jump condition: The two limiting values

\tilde{M}_{+}(y,t,k)=\tilde{M}_{-}(y,t,k)\tilde{J}(y,t,k),\quad k\in{\mathbb{R}},

(3.12a)

where the jump matrix is

\tilde{J}(y,t,k)=e^{i(ky+\frac{t}{4k})\hat{\sigma}_{3}}J_{0}(k)

(3.12b)

with

J_{0}(k)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&r(k)\\
\overline{r(k)}&1+|r(k)|^{2}\end{array}\right)

(3.12c)

•

Normalization:

\tilde{M}(y,t,k)\rightarrow\mathbb{I},\quad k\rightarrow\infty.

(3.13)

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\tilde{M}(y,t,k)
satisfies the above conditions, then this Riemann-Hilbert problem has a unique solution. And the solution
u(x,t)
of the initial value problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) can be expressed, in parametric form, in terms of the solution of this Rieamnn-Hilbert problem:

u(x,t)=u(y(x,t),t),

(3.14a)

where

x(y,t)=y+\lim_{k\rightarrow 0}\frac{\left((\tilde{M}(y,t,0))^{-1}\tilde{M}(y,t,k)\right)_{11}-1}{ik}

(3.14b)

e^{-2d}u(y,t)=\lim_{k\rightarrow 0}\frac{\left((\tilde{M}(y,t,0))^{-1}\tilde{M}(y,t,k)\right)_{12}}{ik}

(3.14c)

Proof.

Since the jump matrix
\tilde{J}(y,t,k)
is a Hermitian matrix, then the Riemann-Hilbert problem of
\tilde{M}(y,t,k)
indeed has a solution. Furthermore, the Riemann-Hilbert problem has only one solution because of the normalize condition.

The statements of the solution
u(x,t)
is following from the asymptotic formula (
3.8
).

∎

iu(y,t)e^{-2d}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k_{0}t}}\left[\tilde{M}^{(k_{0})1}_{12}+\tilde{M}^{(-k_{0})1}_{12}\right].

(5.60)

A direct calculation shows that the leading order asymptotic behavior of the solution
u(y,t)
is

iu(y,t)e^{-2d}=\delta_{0}^{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\left[\sqrt{\frac{-\nu(-k_{0})}{k_{0}}}e^{i\phi_{1}}-\sqrt{\frac{-\nu(k_{0})}{k_{0}}}e^{-i\phi_{2}}\right],

(5.61)

where

\begin{array}[]{l}\phi_{1}=\frac{i\pi}{4}+\mbox{arg}(r(-k_{0}))+\mbox{arg}(\Gamma(i\nu(-k_{0})))+\nu(-k_{0})\ln(\frac{k_{0}^{3}}{t})\\
{}-\nu(k_{0})\ln(4k_{0}^{2})-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-k_{0}}^{k_{0}}\ln(s+k_{0})d\ln(1+|r(s)|^{2})-\frac{t}{k_{0}},\end{array}

(5.62a)

\begin{array}[]{l}\phi_{2}=\frac{i\pi}{4}-\mbox{arg}(r(-k_{0}))-\mbox{arg}(\Gamma(-i\nu(k_{0})))+\nu(k_{0})\ln(\frac{k_{0}^{3}}{t})\\
{}-\nu(-k_{0})\ln(4k_{0}^{2})+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-k_{0}}^{k_{0}}\ln(k_{0}-s)d\ln(1+|r(s)|^{2})-\frac{t}{k_{0}}.\end{array}

(5.62b)

The relation
c_{+}
lies between the new scale
y
and the original scale
x
,

ic_{+}=-\delta_{1}+o(1),\quad t\rightarrow\infty.

(5.63)

Hence, the solution
u(x,t)

5.1

Semi device independence of the BB84 protocol
Erik Woodhead

Abstract
The BB84 quantum key distribution protocol is semi device independent in the sense that it can be shown to be secure if just one of the users’ devices is restricted to a qubit Hilbert space. Here, we derive an analytic lower bound on the asymptotic secret key rate for the entanglement-based version of BB84 assuming only that one of the users performs unknown qubit POVMs. The result holds against the class of collective attacks and reduces to the well known Shor-Preskill key rate for correlations corresponding to the ideal BB84 correlations mixed with any amount of random noise.



I
BB84 and device independence

Quantum key distribution (QKD)
[
1
,
2
]
protocols allow
cooperating users to generate cryptographic keys in such a way that
unauthorised eavesdropping can be detected. This is achieved by exploiting
features of quantum physics, such as the general inability to measure a
quantum state without disturbing it, in a way that guarantees that any
attempt at eavesdropping on the protocol will introduce detectable errors.

One of a QKD protocol’s differentiating features is the degree to which it is

device independent

[
3
,
4
,
5
]
, i.e., the
extent to which the protocol can be proved secure independently of
assumptions about the internal functioning of the devices in the physical
setup. This is of practical interest as device-independent protocols are
intrinsically more robust, ensuring that both unintended and maliciously
introduced implementation faults are detected automatically. Protocols can
range from fully characterised (the exact quantum state preparations and/or
measurements must be known) to fully device independent (security is
established based only on the detection of Bell-nonlocal

[
6
,
7
]
correlations, independently of the mechanism that
produced them). Between these extremes, partially device-independent
protocols have also been proposed in which only some of the devices are fully
characterised
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
and in which only a
Hilbert space dimension bound is assumed for the source of quantum states

[
11
,
12
]
.

The BB84 protocol
[
13
]
was originally introduced as a fully
characterised protocol. A commonly considered
prepare-and-measure

version runs as follows. One user (“Alice”) generates a string of random
bits that she wishes to transmit to another distant user (“Bob”). Alice
sequentially encodes each bit onto one of two corresponding orthogonal
\sigma_{\mathrm{z}}

eigenstates
\lvert 0\rangle
and
\lvert 1\rangle
which she transmits to Bob. In order to
be able to detect eavesdropping, Alice inserts instances of the
\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}

eigenstates
\lvert+\rangle
and
\lvert-\rangle
, with
\lvert\pm\rangle=(\lvert 0\rangle\pm\lvert 1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
, at some random locations in the sequence of quantum states to be
transmitted to Bob. Bob measures most of the states he receives from Alice in
the
\sigma_{\mathrm{z}}=\lvert 0\rangle\langle 0\rvert-\lvert 1\rangle\langle 1\rvert
basis and the remaining minority of cases in
the
\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}=\lvert+\rangle\langle+\rvert-\lvert-\rangle\langle-\rvert
basis. Afterwards, the record of cases where
Alice and Bob used mismatched bases (Alice prepared a
\sigma_{\mathrm{z}}
state and Bob
measured
\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}
or vice versa) are discarded. The cases where Alice and Bob
both used the
\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}
basis and a randomly chosen subset of cases where they
both used the
\sigma_{\mathrm{z}}
basis are used to estimate the
\mathrm{x}
- and
\mathrm{z}
-basis
error rates
\delta_{\mathrm{x}}
and
\delta_{\mathrm{z}}
and then likewise
discarded. Finally, if the error rates are not too high, classical
postprocessing allows a (generally shorter) secret key to be generated with
the relative errors between Alice’s and Bob’s versions corrected and with any
knowledge of the key by an adversary effectively erased.

There is also an
entanglement-based
version of BB84, in which a
central source prepares and distributes entangled states which Alice, as well
as Bob, measures in the
\sigma_{\mathrm{z}}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}
bases. In this case, the initial
bitstring is obtained from the measurement results rather than from a
separate randomness generation procedure. Since Alice’s
\sigma_{\mathrm{z}}
or
\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}

measurement can be thought of as effectively preparing a state for Bob

[
14
]
, there is some equivalence between the two versions of the
protocol. In particular, in both versions, one-way classical postprocessing
allows a secret key to be extracted at an asymptotic rate given by the
Shor-Preskill key rate
[
15
]
,

r\geq 1-h(\delta_{\mathrm{x}})-h(\delta_{\mathrm{z}})\,,

(1)

where
h(x)=-x\log_{2}(x)-(1-x)\log_{2}(1-x)
is the binary
entropy function, depending on the error rates
\delta_{\mathrm{x}}
and

\delta_{\mathrm{z}}
.

Since its original proposal, it has become apparent that the BB84 protocol
exhibits a significant degree of device independence. BB84 was first found to
be
one-sided device independent
, i.e., the explicit characterisation
of one of the devices can be dropped. This was already indicated by some
early security results
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
for the
prepare-and-measure version of BB84 which do not explicitly depend on Bob’s
measurements, and later analyses
[
19
,
20
]
found that the
Shor-Preskill key-rate bound (
1
) still holds at the
one-sided-device-independent level if Alice’s source prepares the
\sigma_{\mathrm{z}}
and

\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}
eigenstates (in the prepare-and-measure version) or just one of the
users measures in the
\sigma_{\mathrm{z}}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{x}}
bases (in the entanglement-based
version).

Recent analyses have started to exploit results from the mismatched bases
cases, which are usually discarded, in order to improve the security
certification
[
21
,
22
]
, and some authors have further
pointed out that this can reduce the level of characterisation required to
just a dimension bound for one of the devices. In Ref.
[
23
]
, it
was first shown that the Shor-Preskill rate still holds if no correlations
are observed in the mismatched bases cases assuming that Alice performs
unknown projective qubit measurements. A similar result was recovered
numerically in Ref.
[
24
]
for general qubit POVMs on Alice’s
side, assuming that Bob also performs qubit measurements. The
prepare-and-measure version of BB84 was also studied numerically in

[
25
]
at a similar level of device independence, where Alice’s
source prepares unknown pure qubit states and Bob performs unknown projective
qubit measurements.

Here, we study the BB84 protocol in this
semi-device-independent

scenario (borrowing the name from
[
11
]
), where we assume only
that Alice’s device acts on a two-dimensional Hilbert space. The main result
will be an analytic lower bound on the asymptotic secret key rate for the
entanglement-based version of BB84 where we allow Alice’s measurements to be
arbitrary qubit POVMs and Bob’s measurements are left uncharacterised. The
result holds against the class of collective attacks
[
17
]
(i.e.,
assuming that Alice’s and Bob’s measurements are always performed on the same
entangled state), which is known to imply unconditional security at least if
the measurements are memoryless and if the Hilbert-space dimension is bounded

[
26
]
.

The qubit device assumption is taken here to mean that Alice’s result depends

only
on the measurement of a qubit state. In particular, similar to

[
27
,
28
]
, we assume that Alice’s measurement result does
not depend on additional classical information that could also be available
to Bob’s device (so-called “shared randomness”
[
11
]
). This is
necessary as the ideal (entanglement-based) BB84 correlations can be
simulated with two shared classical random bits—a special case of what an
adversary could prepare with a shared classical bit and an entangled qubit
which is completely insecure from a cryptographic perspective. A consequence
is that, unusually for a QKD security result, any (nontrivial) lower bound on
the key rate cannot be a convex function of the probabilities
P(ab\mid uv)

at this level of device independence.

II
Scenario and main result

In the entanglement-based version of the BB84 protocol, Alice and Bob share a
state
\rho_{\mathrm{AB}}
on some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{A}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{B}}
,
on which they can perform POVMs
\{M^{(u)}_{0},M^{(u)}_{1}\}
and

\{N^{(v)}_{0},N^{(v)}_{1}\}
indexed by measurement choices

u,v\in\{\mathrm{z},\mathrm{x}\}
and yielding results
a,b\in\{0,1\}
with
probability

P(ab\mid uv)=\Tr\bigl[(M^{(u)}_{a}\otimes N^{(v)}_{b})\rho_{\mathrm{AB}}\bigr]\,.

(2)

In the semi-device-independent level of security that we consider, we assume
that
\dim\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{A}}=2
. The state
\rho_{\mathrm{AB}}
and measurements are
otherwise treated as unknown. Setting
\hat{A}_{u}=M^{(u)}_{0}-M^{(u)}_{1}
and
\hat{B}_{v}=N^{(v)}_{0}-N^{(v)}_{1}
, a convenient
summary of the probabilities
P(ab\mid uv)
that we will use is given by the
eight parameters

\displaystyle A_{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\hat{A}_{u}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{B}}\rangle\,,

(3)

\displaystyle B_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{A}}\otimes\hat{B}_{v}\rangle\,,

(4)

\displaystyle E_{uv}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\hat{A}_{u}\otimes\hat{B}_{v}\rangle\,,

(5)

with
\langle\;\cdot\;\rangle=\Tr[\;\cdot\;\rho_{\mathrm{AB}}]
. Note that
E_{\mathrm{zz}}
and

E_{\mathrm{xx}}
here are related to the more conventional
\mathrm{z}
- and
\mathrm{x}
-basis
error rates
\delta_{\mathrm{z}}
and
\delta_{\mathrm{x}}
by
E_{uu}=1-2\delta_{u}
.

The full security analysis of the protocol will be undertaken in the next
section, but it is worth already sketching a result for the special case
where Alice performs rank-one projective measurements since one can be
derived directly from the Shor-Preskill rate. In this scenario, where Alice’s

\mathrm{z}
and
\mathrm{x}
measurements simply project into orthogonal bases

\{\lvert 0_{\mathrm{z}}\rangle,\lvert 1_{\mathrm{z}}\rangle\}
and
\{\lvert 0_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle,\lvert 1_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\}
,
essentially the only relevant parameter differentiating the measurements is
the Bloch-sphere angle between them. For some suitable basis

\{\lvert 0_{\mathrm{w}}\rangle,\lvert 1_{\mathrm{w}}\rangle\}
conjugate to

\{\lvert 0_{\mathrm{z}}\rangle,\lvert 1_{\mathrm{z}}\rangle\}
, we may write

\hat{A}_{\mathrm{x}}=\cos(\varphi)\hat{A}_{\mathrm{z}}+\sin(\varphi)\hat{A}_{\mathrm{w}}\,,

(6)

where
\hat{A}_{\mathrm{w}}=\lvert 0_{\mathrm{w}}\rangle\langle 0_{\mathrm{w}}\rvert-\lvert 1_{\mathrm{w}}\rangle\langle 1_{\mathrm{w}}\rvert
and
\varphi
is the
(unknown) Bloch-sphere angle between
\hat{A}_{\mathrm{z}}
and

\hat{A}_{\mathrm{x}}
. Setting

E_{\mathrm{wx}}=\langle\hat{A}_{\mathrm{w}}\otimes\hat{B}_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle
, linearity of the
quantum expectation value implies the relation

E_{\mathrm{xx}}=\cos(\varphi)E_{\mathrm{zx}}+\sin(\varphi)E_{\mathrm{wx}}\,.

(7)

The conjugate “
\mathrm{w}
basis” introduced here is useful because the
(one-sided-device-independent) Shor-Preskill key rate applies to
it. Introducing, for convenience, the function

\phi(x)=1-\tfrac{1}{2}(1+x)\log_{2}(1+x)-\tfrac{1}{2}(1-x)\log_{2}(1-x)

(8)

(related to the binary entropy by

\phi(x)=h(\tfrac{1}{2}\pm\tfrac{1}{2}x)
), the Shor-Preskill rate can
be expressed as

r\geq 1-\phi(E_{\mathrm{wx}})-\phi(E_{\mathrm{zz}})\,.

(9)

From here, it is a simple matter to obtain a key-rate bound depending only on
the observed correlations. From the relation (
7
)
between the correlators, we obtain

\displaystyle\lvert E_{\mathrm{xx}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lvert\cos(\varphi)\rvert\lvert E_{\mathrm{zx}}\rvert+\lvert\sin(\varphi)\rvert\lvert E_{\mathrm{wx}}\rvert

(10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{zx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{zx}}2}+E_{\mathrm{wx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{wx}}2}}\,,

which rearranges to

E_{\mathrm{wx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{wx}}2}\geq E_{\mathrm{xx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{xx}}2}-E_{\mathrm{zx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{zx}}2}\,.

(11)

As long as
\lvert E_{\mathrm{xx}}\rvert\geq\lvert E_{\mathrm{zx}}\rvert
, this implies the lower bound

r\geq 1-\phi\bigl(\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{xx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{xx}}2}-E_{\mathrm{zx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{zx}}2}}\bigr)-\phi(E_{\mathrm{zz}})

(12)

for the key rate.

More generally, it is clear that the key-rate bound
(
12
) cannot hold against arbitrary POVMs on
Alice’s side. A simple counterexample is that if we allow Alice to perform
the degenerate projective measurement

\{M^{(\mathrm{z})}_{0},M^{(\mathrm{z})}_{1}\}=\{\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{A}},\mathbb{0}_{\mathrm{A}}\}
, it is
possible for Alice and Bob to obtain the result
a=b=0
deterministically
(which is completely insecure) while observing the correlations

E_{\mathrm{xx}}=E_{\mathrm{zz}}=1
and
E_{\mathrm{zx}}=0
(for which
(
12
) would imply
r=1
). Of course, this
particular pathological case is easily detected since Alice and Bob could
notice that they keep getting the same measurement results. In terms of the
parameterisation given above, we thus do not expect
(
12
) to still apply if
A_{\mathrm{z}}=1
.

There is a significant parameter range in which the rate
(
12
) still holds, though. The main result of
this article is that the asymptotic rate (
12
)
still applies, at least against collective attacks, if the correlations
satisfy
\lvert E_{\mathrm{xx}}\rvert>\lvert B_{\mathrm{x}}\rvert
and

E_{\mathrm{xx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{xx}}2}+E_{\mathrm{zx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{zx}}2}\leq 1-2\lvert A_{\mathrm{z}}-E_{\mathrm{zx}}B_{\mathrm{x}}\rvert+A_{\mathrm{z}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{z}}2}\,.

(13)

This is proved in the next section. As a special case, we recover the
Shor-Preskill rate

r\geq 1-\phi(E_{\mathrm{xx}})-\phi(E_{\mathrm{zz}})

(14)

if there are no correlations in the mismatched bases cases (so that

E_{\mathrm{zx}}=0
) and if

\lvert B_{\mathrm{x}}\rvert<\lvert E_{\mathrm{xx}}\rvert\leq 1-\lvert A_{\mathrm{z}}\rvert
; the latter
constraint reduces to
\lvert E_{\mathrm{xx}}\rvert>0
(which is necessary to certify a
nonzero key rate anyway) if Alice’s and Bob’s marginal results are
equiprobable (so that
A_{\mathrm{z}}=B_{\mathrm{x}}=0
).

In principle, the derivation given in the next section could be pursued
further in order to derive a lower bound for the key rate in the case that
the condition (
13
) is not satisfied. There is an easier way
of getting a result for this case, though. Since the condition
(
13
) and key rate (
12
) are
device independent on Bob’s side, we can simply apply the result they

would
imply if Bob’s measurement operator
\hat{B}_{\mathrm{x}}
were scaled
down to
\lambda\hat{B}_{\mathrm{x}}
for some scaling factor
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. This way, we can use the modified bound

r\geq 1-\phi\bigl(\lambda\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{xx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{xx}}2}-E_{\mathrm{zx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{zx}}2}}\bigr)-\phi(E_{\mathrm{zz}})\,,

(15)

taking for
\lambda
the highest number between zero and one satisfying

\lambda^{2}\bigl(E_{\mathrm{xx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{xx}}2}+E_{\mathrm{zx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{zx}}2}\bigr)=1-2\lvert A_{\mathrm{z}}-\lambda^{2}E_{\mathrm{zx}}B_{\mathrm{x}}\rvert+A_{\mathrm{z}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{z}}2}\,.

(16)

III
Proof of main result

Problem definition

In the worst-case scenario, Alice, Bob, and the adversary Eve share a
purification
\lvert\Psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{A}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{B}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{E}}
, prepared by Eve, of the state
\rho_{\mathrm{AB}}
responsible for the
observed correlations according to (
2
). When Alice
measures
u=\mathrm{z}
, the system in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{B}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{E}}
is
projected to the (unnormalised) state

\rho=\Tr_{\mathrm{A}}\bigl[(M^{(\mathrm{z})}_{0}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{BE}})\Psi\bigr]

(17)

or

\rho^{\prime}=\Tr_{\mathrm{A}}\bigl[(M^{(\mathrm{z})}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{BE}})\Psi\bigr]\,,

(18)

depending, respectively, on whether Alice gets the result
a=0
or
a=1
. (We will in general write, e.g.,
\Psi
as a shorthand for the density
operator
\lvert\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi\rvert
associated to some pure state
\lvert\Psi\rangle
.) The
normalisations of these states are related to the probabilities with which
they are prepared according to
\Tr[\rho]=P_{\mathrm{A}}(0\mid\mathrm{z})
and

\Tr[\rho^{\prime}]=P_{\mathrm{A}}(1\mid\mathrm{z})
. The correlation between Alice’s result

a
and the state available to Eve is summarised by the classical-quantum
state

\tau_{A\mathrm{E}}=\lvert 0\rangle\langle 0\rvert\otimes\rho^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{E}}+\lvert 1\rangle\langle 1\rvert\otimes\rho^{\prime}_{\mathrm{E}}\,,

(19)

in terms of Eve’s parts
\rho^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{E}}=\Tr_{\mathrm{B}}[\rho]
and
\rho^{\prime}_{\mathrm{E}}=\Tr_{\mathrm{B}}[\rho^{\prime}]
of the possible density operators
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
.

We consider the case where the key is extracted from the
u=v=\mathrm{z}

measurement results. In this case, the one-way asymptotic key rate secure
against collective attacks is lower bounded by the Devetak-Winter rate

[
29
]
, which can be expressed as the difference of two entropies

r=H(A\mid\mathrm{E})-H(A\mid B)\,.

(20)

In (
20
),
H(A\mid B)
is the Shannon entropy of Alice’s
outcome conditioned on Bob’s and can either be computed directly or
approximated by
H(A\mid B)\leq h(\delta_{\mathrm{z}})=\phi(E_{\mathrm{zz}})
. The
main problem, and the main goal of this section, is to derive a lower bound
for the conditional von Neumann entropy
H(A\mid\mathrm{E})
, which is given by

\displaystyle H(A\mid\mathrm{E})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(\tau_{A\mathrm{E}})-S(\tau_{\mathrm{E}})

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(\rho^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{E}})+S(\rho^{\prime}_{\mathrm{E}})-S(\rho^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{E}}+\rho^{\prime}_{\mathrm{E}})\,,

where
S(\rho)=-\Tr[\rho\log_{2}(\rho)]
, when computed on the
classical-quantum state (
19
).

The derivation followed in the remainder of this section uses a few
mathematical tools (two of which are minor restatements of results in

[
30
]
) which are presented here as lemmas. Proofs for these are
supplied as appendices to this article.

General proof outline

The starting point is the following relation for the conditional von Neumann
entropy, which simplifies the problem to that of lower bounding the fidelity
between the marginal states available to Eve.

Lemma 1
.

The conditional von Neumann entropy, computed on the classical-quantum
state
\lvert 0\rangle\langle 0\rvert\otimes\rho^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{E}}+\lvert 1\rangle\langle 1\rvert\otimes\rho^{\prime}_{\mathrm{E}}
, is
lower bounded by

H(A\mid\mathrm{E})\geq\phi(A_{\mathrm{z}})-\phi\Bigl(\sqrt{A_{\mathrm{z}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{z}}2}+4F(\rho^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{E}},\rho^{\prime}_{\mathrm{E}})^{2}}\Bigr)

(22)

in terms of the fidelity
F(\rho^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{E}},\rho^{\prime}_{\mathrm{E}})
between

\rho^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{E}}
and
\rho^{\prime}_{\mathrm{E}}
. Furthermore, for fixed
F(\rho^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{E}},\rho^{\prime}_{\mathrm{E}})
, the right-hand side of (
22
) is
convex in
A_{\mathrm{z}}
and is minimised with
A_{\mathrm{z}}=0
.

Here, we take the fidelity to be defined by

F(\rho,\sigma)=\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\sigma}\rVert_{1}
, where

\lVert A\rVert_{1}=\Tr[\lvert A\rvert]=\Tr[\sqrt{A^{\dagger}A}]
denotes the trace
norm of an operator
A
, for (generally unnormalised) density operators

\rho
and
\sigma
. Note that the minimisation of
(
22
) at
A_{\mathrm{z}}=0
allows the bound for the von
Neumann entropy to be simplified to

H(A\mid\mathrm{E})\geq 1-\phi\bigl(2F(\rho^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{E}},\rho^{\prime}_{\mathrm{E}})\bigr)\,,

(23)

though this step is optional, since
A_{\mathrm{z}}
is an observed parameter.

The approach we follow involves reducing the problem to considering pure
states. To this end, we introduce orthonormal bases

\{\lvert 0_{u}\rangle,\lvert 1_{u}\rangle\}
,
u\in\{\mathrm{z},\mathrm{x}\}
, in which Alice’s (qubit
Hermitian) POVM elements
M^{(u)}_{a}
are diagonal. In these bases, Alice’s
POVMs can be expressed as convex sums

\displaystyle\{M^{(u)}_{0},M^{(u)}_{1}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{(u)}_{1}\{0_{u},1_{u}\}+m^{(u)}_{2}\{1_{u},0_{u}\}

(24)

\displaystyle+\>m^{(u)}_{3}\{\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{A}},\mathbb{0}_{\mathrm{A}}\}+m^{(u)}_{4}\{\mathbb{0}_{\mathrm{A}},\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{A}}\}

of the four projective measurements
\{0_{u},1_{u}\}
,
\{1_{u},0_{u}\}
,

\{\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{A}},\mathbb{0}_{\mathrm{A}}\}
, and
\{\mathbb{0}_{\mathrm{A}},\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{A}}\}
for convex
coefficients satisfying
m^{(u)}_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i}m^{(u)}_{i}=1
. (Here,
0_{u}
and
1_{u}
are shorthand for
\lvert 0_{u}\rangle\langle 0_{u}\rvert
and

\lvert 1_{u}\rangle\langle 1_{u}\rvert
, and
\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{A}}
and
\mathbb{0}_{\mathrm{A}}
denote the identity and null
operators on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{A}}
.)

Concentrating on the
\mathrm{z}
measurement, we can express the entangled state as

\lvert\Psi\rangle=\lvert 0_{\mathrm{z}}\rangle\lvert\alpha\rangle+\lvert 1_{\mathrm{z}}\rangle\lvert\alpha^{\prime}\rangle

(25)

for (unnormalised and not necessarily orthogonal) states
\lvert\alpha\rangle,\lvert\alpha^{\prime}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{B}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{E}}
. The fidelity between
Eve’s parts
\alpha^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{E}}
and
\alpha^{\prime}_{\mathrm{E}}
of the states

\lvert\alpha\rangle
and
\lvert\alpha^{\prime}\rangle
introduced this way can, according to the
following relation, be bounded in terms of an operator
W_{\mathrm{B}}
on Bob’s
Hilbert space.

Lemma 2
.

The fidelity between Eve’s partial traces
\alpha^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{E}}
and

\alpha^{\prime}_{\mathrm{E}}
of the pure states
\lvert\alpha\rangle
and
\lvert\alpha^{\prime}\rangle

satisfies

2F(\alpha^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{E}},\alpha^{\prime}_{\mathrm{E}})\geq\lVert W_{\mathrm{B}}\rVert_{1}\,,

(26)

where
W_{\mathrm{B}}=\Tr_{\mathrm{E}}[W]
and

W=\lvert\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha^{\prime}\rvert+\lvert\alpha^{\prime}\rangle\langle\alpha\rvert
.

We approach the problem of lower bounding
\lVert W_{\mathrm{B}}\rVert_{1}
in the following
way. Similar to (
25
), we express the entangled state as

\lvert\Psi\rangle=\lvert 0_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\lvert\beta\rangle+\lvert 1_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\lvert\beta^{\prime}\rangle

(27)

for the
u=\mathrm{x}
measurement. In an appropriate phase convention, the
diagonalising bases are related by

\displaystyle\lvert 0_{\mathrm{z}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos(\tfrac{\varphi}{2})\lvert 0_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle-\sin(\tfrac{\varphi}{2})\lvert 1_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle\,,

(28)

\displaystyle\lvert 1_{\mathrm{z}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin(\tfrac{\varphi}{2})\lvert 0_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle+\cos(\tfrac{\varphi}{2})\lvert 1_{\mathrm{x}}\rangle

(29)

for some angle
\varphi
. From this and requiring that
(
25
) and (
27
) are the same state, we
extract

\displaystyle\lvert\beta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos(\tfrac{\varphi}{2})\lvert\alpha\rangle+\sin(\tfrac{\varphi}{2})\lvert\alpha^{\prime}\rangle\,,

(30)

\displaystyle\lvert\beta^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sin(\tfrac{\varphi}{2})\lvert\alpha\rangle+\cos(\tfrac{\varphi}{2})\lvert\alpha^{\prime}\rangle\,.

(31)

Introducing the correlators

\displaystyle\bar{\bar{E}}_{\mathrm{zx}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Tr\bigl[\hat{B}_{\mathrm{x}}(\alpha^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{B}}-\alpha^{\prime}_{\mathrm{B}})\bigr]\,,

(32)

\displaystyle\bar{\bar{E}}_{\mathrm{xx}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Tr\bigl[\hat{B}_{\mathrm{x}}(\beta^{\vphantom{\prime}}_{\mathrm{B}}-\beta^{\prime}_{\mathrm{B}})\bigr]

(33)

for the pure states and

\bar{\bar{E}}_{\mathrm{wx}}=\Tr\bigl[\hat{B}_{\mathrm{x}}W_{\mathrm{B}}\bigr]

(34)

for the operator
W
appearing in Lemma
2
, the
relations (
30
) and (
31
) imply

\bar{\bar{E}}_{\mathrm{xx}}=\cos(\varphi)\bar{\bar{E}}_{\mathrm{zx}}+\sin(\varphi)\bar{\bar{E}}_{\mathrm{wx}}\,,

(35)

and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and rearranging, we obtain

\bar{\bar{E}}_{\mathrm{wx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{wx}}2}\geq\bar{\bar{E}}_{\mathrm{xx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{xx}}2}-\bar{\bar{E}}_{\mathrm{zx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{zx}}2}\,,

(36)

similar to the outline of the previous section. Finally, since

\hat{B}_{\mathrm{x}}
is the difference of two POVM elements, it satisfies the
operator inequalities
-\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{B}}\leq\hat{B}_{\mathrm{x}}\leq\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{B}}
; this
allows
\bar{\bar{E}}_{\mathrm{wx}}
to be used as a lower bound on the trace norm

\lVert W_{\mathrm{B}}\rVert_{1}

IV
Conclusion

The preceding section proves that the key rate asymptotically secure against
collective attacks for BB84 is lower bounded by

r\geq\phi(A_{\mathrm{z}})-\phi\bigl(\sqrt{A_{\mathrm{z}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{z}}2}+E_{\mathrm{xx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{xx}}2}-E_{\mathrm{zx}}^{\phantom{\mathrm{zx}}2}}\bigr)-\phi(E_{\mathrm{zz}})

(74)

if
\lvert E_{\mathrm{xx}}\rvert>\lvert B_{\mathrm{x}}\rvert
and if the condition
(
72
) is satisfied. This is never less than the
simpler bound (
12
) claimed in
section
II
. If (
72
) is not satisfied,
device independence on Bob’s side still allows the main result to be used
with the replacements
E_{\mathrm{xx}}\to\lambda E_{\mathrm{xx}}
and

E_{\mathrm{zx}}\to\lambda E_{\mathrm{zx}}
, with the scaling factor
\lambda
determined
by (
16
) above. Together, these give a general
semi-device-independent security result for the BB84 protocol against
collective (and possibly
[
26
]
more general) attacks. The
traditional set of assumptions used to prove the security of the BB84
protocol can thus be relaxed to a significant degree. It is still necessary
to trust that one of the users’ measurements are restricted to a
two-dimensional Hilbert space, but exact knowledge of the measurements beyond
this is not required.

In the scenario considered, aside from the qubit restriction on Alice’s side,
Alice’s and Bob’s measurements were allowed to be arbitrary POVMs. One could
go further, similar to
[
27
,
28
]
, and imagine that Eve
may have more detailed knowledge of the measurements. Specifically, the
approach followed in this article could probably be modified to allow Eve to
know the indices
i
and
j
in decompositions of the form

M^{(u)}_{a}=\sum_{i}p_{i}M^{(u)}_{a;i}
and

N^{(v)}_{b}=\sum_{j}q_{j}N^{(v)}_{b;j}
for the POVM elements, although
the resulting key rate will probably not include the Shor-Preskill rate as a
special case if the adversary is granted this extra power.

Finally, the main result was derived for the entanglement-based version of
BB84. It is likely that a similar result should hold for the
prepare-and-measure BB84 variant assuming a source which is restricted to
emitting qubit states, which was tested in a recent implementation

[
31
]
. Adapting the approach followed here for the
prepare-and-measure scenario is thus an obvious problem for future work.

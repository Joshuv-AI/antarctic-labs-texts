Homomorphisms of distributive lattices as restrictions of congruences. III. Rectangular lattices and two convex sublattices
George Grätzer, Harry Lakser

Abstract
. Let L L be a finite lattice and let I I be an ideal of L L . Then the restriction map is a bounded lattice homomorphism of the congruence lattice of L L into the congruence lattice of I I . In a 2009 paper, the authors proved the converse. In a 2012 paper, G. Czédli proved an analogous result for rectangular lattices. In this paper, we prove a stronger form of Czédli’s result and provide a short, elementary, and direct proof.

2.2.
BRT

The Birkhoff Representation Theorem (BRT, for short)
relates bounded homomorphisms of finite distributive lattices
and isotone maps of finite ordered sets.

For a finite distributive lattice
D
,
let
\textup{J}(D)
denote the ordered sets of join-irreducible elements of
D
;
for
a\in D
, let
{\textup{spec}(a)}
denote the set of join-irreducible elements
\leq a
.

Theorem 4

(BRT)
.

Let
D
and
E
be finite distributive lattices and set
P=\textup{J}(D)
and
Q=\textup{J}(E)
.
Then the following five statements hold.

(i)

With every bounded homomorphism
\varphi\colon D\to E
, we can associate an isotone map
\textup{J}(\varphi)\colon Q\to P
defined by

\textup{J}(\varphi)x=\bigwedge(\,e\in D\mid x\leq\varphi e\,)

for
x\in Q
.

(ii)

With every isotone map
\psi\colon Q\to P
, we can associate a
bounded homomorphism
\Down(\psi)\colon D\to E
defined by

\Down(\psi)(e)=\bigvee\psi^{-1}({\textup{spec}(e)})

for
e\in D
.

(iii)

The constructions of
(i)
and
(ii)
are
inverses to one another, and so yield together a bijection between
bounded homomorphisms
\varphi\colon D\to E

and isotone maps
\textup{J}(\varphi)\colon Q\to P
.

(iv)

\varphi
is one-to-one iff
\textup{J}(\varphi)
is onto.

(v)

\varphi
is onto iff
\textup{J}(\varphi)
is an order-embedding.

The two main formulas in this result are easy to visualize.
For the formula in (i),
for
x\in Q
, take the set
X
of all elements of
D
mapped by
\varphi
onto
x
or above.
Since
\varphi
is a homomorphism,
it follows that the meet of all these elements, let us denote it by
x^{\dagger}
,
is still mapped by
\varphi
onto
x
or above.
The map
\textup{J}(\varphi)
maps
x
into
x^{\dagger}
.

With every isotone map
\psi\colon Q\to P
, we can associate a
bounded homomorphism
\Down(\psi)\colon D\to E
defined by

\Down(\psi)(e)=\bigvee\psi^{-1}({\textup{spec}(e)})

for
e\in D
.

Now for the formula in (ii), take an element
e\in D
.
We form the set
Y
of join-irreducible elements of
E

mapped by
\psi
to an element
\leq e
.
The join of
Y
denoted by
e^{\ddagger}
, is an element of
E
.
The map
\Down(\psi)
maps
e
into
e^{\ddagger}
.

The constructions of (i) and (ii) are
inverses to one another, and so yield together a bijection between
bounded homomorphisms
\varphi\colon D\to E
and isotone maps
\psi\colon Q\to P
.

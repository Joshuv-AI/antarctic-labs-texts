Spin evolution of massive fermion in QED plasma
Ziyue Wang

Abstract
The dynamical evolution of spin of a massive probe fermion in a massless hot QED plasma at local equilibrium is investigated through the quantum kinetic theory. We consider the massive probe fermion undergoing 2-by-2 Coulomb scattering with the massless fermions in the medium. The axial kinetic equation is derived including the collision terms to the first order of gradients and leading logarithmic order of the coupling. The collision terms are vanishing at global equilibrium, around which the relaxation time can be extracted as an operator. We further decompose the axial kinetic equation into kinetic equations of axial-charge density as well as the transverse magnetic dipole moment, and illustrate the diffusion and polarization effect through preliminary numerical analysis.

in the derivation. For abbreviation, we denote

in (

defined in (

into
\partial^{\mu}f_{P}
. After straightforward but tedious algebra, the collision terms of axial kinetic equation can be casted into
P\cdot\partial n_{\mu}=C_{\mu}
,

\displaystyle C_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4e^{4}(2\pi)^{3}\int_{Q,K}\int_{K^{\prime},P^{\prime}}\Big\{M^{A1}_{\mu\nu}(\bar{f}_{K}{f}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{f}_{K^{\prime}}+{f}_{K}\bar{f}_{P^{\prime}}{f}_{K^{\prime}})n^{\nu}(P)+M^{A2}_{\mu\nu}(f_{P}{f}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{f}_{K^{\prime}}+\bar{f}_{P}\bar{f}_{P^{\prime}}{f}_{K^{\prime}}){n}^{\nu}(K)

(14)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\;\;\;+M^{A3}_{\mu\nu}(\bar{f}_{K}\bar{f}_{K^{\prime}}{f}_{P^{\prime}}+{f}_{K}{f}_{K^{\prime}}\bar{f}_{P^{\prime}})\partial^{\nu}f_{P}\;+M^{A4}_{\mu\nu}(f_{P}{f}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{f}_{K^{\prime}}+\bar{f}_{P}\bar{f}_{P^{\prime}}{f}_{K^{\prime}})\partial^{\nu}f_{K}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\;\;\;+M^{A5}_{\mu\nu}(\bar{f}_{K^{\prime}}f_{P}\bar{f}_{K}+{f}_{K^{\prime}}\bar{f}_{P}{f}_{K})n^{\nu}_{\text{leq}}(P^{\prime})+M^{A6}_{\mu\nu}({f}_{P^{\prime}}f_{P}\bar{f}_{K}+\bar{f}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{f}_{P}{f}_{K}){n}^{\nu}_{\text{leq}}(K^{\prime})\Big\},

where
\int_{Q,K}\int_{K^{\prime},P^{\prime}}=\int(2\pi)^{-16}d^{4}Qd^{4}Kd^{4}K^{\prime}d^{4}P^{\prime}(2\pi)^{8}\delta(P-K-Q)\delta(Q+P^{\prime}-K^{\prime})\epsilon(K\cdot u)\epsilon(P^{\prime}\cdot u)\epsilon(K^{\prime}\cdot u)\delta(K^{2}-m^{2})\delta(P^{\prime 2})\delta(K^{\prime 2})

\displaystyle M^{A1}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(Q^{2})^{2}}g_{\mu\nu}\big(-m^{2}P^{\prime}\cdot K^{\prime}+2P\cdot P^{\prime}K\cdot K^{\prime}\big)+\{P^{\prime}\leftrightarrow K^{\prime}\},

\displaystyle M^{A2}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(Q^{2})^{2}}\Big(g_{\mu\nu}\big(K\cdot PP^{\prime}\cdot K^{\prime}-2K\cdot K^{\prime}P\cdot P^{\prime}\big)-K^{\prime}\cdot P^{\prime}K_{\mu}P_{\nu}-2P\cdot P^{\prime}Q_{\mu}K^{\prime}_{\nu}+2P\cdot QP^{\prime}_{\mu}K^{\prime}_{\nu}+2K\cdot K^{\prime}P^{\prime}_{\mu}P_{\nu}\Big)

\displaystyle+\{P^{\prime}\leftrightarrow K^{\prime}\},

\displaystyle M^{A3}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(Q^{2})^{2}}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}K\cdot K^{\prime}P^{\beta}P^{\prime\alpha}+\{P^{\prime}\leftrightarrow K^{\prime}\},

\displaystyle M^{A4}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(Q^{2})^{2}}\Big(\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\big(\frac{1}{2}P^{\prime}\cdot K^{\prime}P^{\beta}K^{\alpha}+P\cdot P^{\prime}K^{\beta}K^{\prime\alpha}\big)+\epsilon_{\lambda\nu\alpha\beta}K^{\prime}_{\mu}P^{\alpha}K^{\beta}P^{\prime\lambda}\Big)+\{P^{\prime}\leftrightarrow K^{\prime}\},

\displaystyle M^{A5}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(Q^{2})^{2}}\big(m^{2}Q\cdot K^{\prime}g_{\mu\nu}-m^{2}K^{\prime}_{\mu}Q_{\nu}+K\cdot K^{\prime}P_{\mu}P_{\nu}-P\cdot K^{\prime}P_{\mu}K_{\nu}\big),

\displaystyle M^{A6}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(Q^{2})^{2}}\big(m^{2}Q\cdot P^{\prime}g_{\mu\nu}-m^{2}P^{\prime}_{\mu}Q_{\nu}+K\cdot P^{\prime}P_{\mu}P_{\nu}-P\cdot P^{\prime}P_{\mu}K_{\nu}\big),

(15)

where
\{P^{\prime}\leftrightarrow K^{\prime}\}
denotes exchanging the two momentum in the terms before, namely
f(P^{\prime},K^{\prime})+\{P^{\prime}\leftrightarrow K^{\prime}\}
means

The first line in collision term (

) corresponds to the spin diffusion of the probe fermion, which are similar to classical spin relaxation processes

;
Yang:2020hri
. The last two lines are polarization of probe fermion contributing from the collection motion of the medium as well as spin of the medium fermion. The third line in (

f_{V}
of the probe fermion. The last line is the contribution from spin of the medium fermion. As
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
is
\mathcal{O}(\hbar)
in the power counting, in order to investigate its evolution, it is of key necessity to evaluate all the first order gradient in the collision term. Before going on to present the collision term after momentum integral, we first discuss each part of the collision terms.

Diffusion

Terms with
M^{A1}_{\mu\nu}
and
M^{A2}_{\mu\nu}
are the spin diffusion terms, these two terms have the same physical meaning as discussed in
Li:2019qkf
;
Yang:2020hri

\displaystyle C_{A\mu}^{\text{diff}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4e^{4}\int_{q_{0},q,k^{\prime}}\big\{M^{A1}_{\mu\nu}(\bar{f}_{K}{f}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{f}_{K^{\prime}}+{f}_{K}\bar{f}_{P^{\prime}}{f}_{K^{\prime}})n^{\nu}(P)+M^{A2}_{\mu\nu}(f_{P}{f}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{f}_{K^{\prime}}+\bar{f}_{P}\bar{f}_{P^{\prime}}{f}_{K^{\prime}}){n}^{\nu}(K)\big\},

(16)

where
\int_{q_{0},q,k^{\prime}}
is the abbreviation for
\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{5}}\int dq_{0}d^{3}qd^{3}k^{\prime}\frac{1}{2p^{\prime}_{0}2k^{\prime}_{0}2k_{0}}\delta(p_{0}-k_{0}-q_{0})\delta(p^{\prime}_{0}-k^{\prime}_{0}+q_{0})
.
Instead of photon propagator in the HTL approximation, we use the photon propagator with one-loop correction (
III
). This leads to the same structure in the collision term, while the coefficients will be different by a constant factor compared with
Yang:2020hri
, coming
SU(N)
of the symmetry of color field in
Yang:2020hri
. In the HTL approximation, the probe fermion and medium fermion are hard fermions
p,k,p^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sim T
, while the momentum transfer is soft
eT\ll q_{0},q\ll T
. In order to complete the momentum integral and keep the result to leading logarithmic order, the axial-vector components of the out-going probe fermion is expanded in terms of soft momentum
Q
as

\displaystyle n_{\mu}(K)=n_{\mu}(P-Q)\simeq n_{\mu}(P)-Q^{\nu}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}n_{\mu}(P)+\frac{1}{2}Q^{\rho}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\rho}}Q^{\nu}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}n_{\mu}(P)+\mathcal{O}(Q^{3}).

(17)

With restriction of the on-shell condition
\delta(P^{2}-m^{2})

n_{\mu}(P)
is only function of the three-momentum
P_{\perp}^{\mu}
, hence the derivative
\partial_{p_{0}}n_{\mu}(P)
can be dropped. And likewise in the expansion of
f_{K}
and
f_{P^{\prime}}
by soft momentum
Q
. The basic strategy is to expand the integrand to
\mathcal{O}(Q^{-2})
, together with the integral measure which is
\mathcal{O}(Q^{2})
, then the momentum integral gives leading logarithmic result. Further details of momentum integral is presented in Appendix.
A.2

polarization

The second and third lines in (
14
) contain first order gradients through derivatives of number distribution of the probe
\partial^{\nu}f_{P},\;\partial^{\nu}f_{K}
as well as the axial-vector component of the medium fermion
n^{\nu}_{\text{leq}}(P^{\prime})
and
n^{\nu}_{\text{leq}}(K^{\prime})
. We take the assumption that the number distribution of the probe has reached local equilibrium with the medium, in this scenario, we can decompose
\partial^{\nu}f_{P},\;\partial^{\nu}f_{K}
in terms of gradients of the fluid. Similar to
Hidaka:2017auj
, derivative of the fluid velocity
u
can be decomposed into anti-symmetric and symmetric part
\partial^{\mu}u^{\nu}=\omega^{\mu\nu}+\sigma^{\mu\nu}
, where
\omega^{\mu\nu}=(\partial^{\mu}u^{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}u^{\mu})/2
and
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=(\partial^{\mu}u^{\nu}+\partial^{\nu}u^{\mu})/2
. With the vorticity defined as
\omega^{\mu}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}u_{\nu}(\partial_{\alpha}u_{\beta})
, the symmetric part is casted into
\omega_{\alpha\beta}=-\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu}u^{\nu}+\kappa_{\alpha\beta}
, with
\kappa_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{2}(u_{\alpha}Du_{\beta}-u_{\beta}Du_{\alpha})
. Defining
\tilde{E}^{\sigma}(P)=P_{\lambda}(\frac{1}{T}u^{\lambda}\partial^{\sigma}T-\sigma^{\sigma\lambda}-\kappa^{\sigma\lambda})
, we arrive at the following decomposition,

\displaystyle\partial^{\nu}f_{P}=-\big(\epsilon^{\nu\rho\alpha\beta}P_{\rho}\omega_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+\tilde{E}^{\nu}(P)\big)f^{\prime}_{P},

(18)

where
f^{\prime}_{P}=\partial_{u\cdot P}f_{P}=(-\frac{1}{T})f_{P}\bar{f}_{P}
.
\tilde{E}^{\sigma}(P)
can be further casted in combination of shear tensor, acceleration and gradient of temperature

\displaystyle\tilde{E}^{\sigma}(P)=-P_{\lambda}(\partial^{\langle\sigma}u^{\lambda\rangle}+\frac{1}{3}\Delta^{\sigma\lambda}\theta+u^{\sigma}Du^{\lambda})+P\cdot u[\partial^{\sigma}\ln T].

(19)

The spin evolution of the massive probe fermion in a massless QED at local equilibrium also involves the exchanging of spin with the massless medium fermion, which are at local equilibrium. For the medium fermion, we take the local equilibrium distribution of spin
Hidaka:2017auj
, namely

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mu}^{\text{leq}}(P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\epsilon(P\cdot u)\delta(P^{2})\Big(\frac{P\cdot\omega\,u_{\mu}}{2}-\frac{P\cdot u\omega_{\mu}}{2}-S^{(u)}_{\mu\sigma}(P)\tilde{E}^{\sigma}(P)\Big)f^{\prime}_{V}(P),

(20)

where

\displaystyle S^{(u)}_{\mu\nu}(P)=\frac{\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}P^{\alpha}u^{\beta}}{2P\cdot u}.

(21)

The polarization effect contains contribution from vorticity, shear tensor, acceleration and the gradient of temperature. With the decomposition (
18
) and (
20
), the vorticity related terms in the collision term can be collected into,

\displaystyle C_{A\mu}^{\text{vor}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4e^{4}\int_{q_{0},q,k^{\prime}}C_{\mu}^{\text{vor}}(-\beta)\bar{f}_{K}\bar{f}_{K^{\prime}}{f}_{P^{\prime}}f_{P},

(22)

with
\beta(x)=T(x)^{-1}
, and

\displaystyle C_{\mu}^{\text{vor}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(M^{A3}_{\mu\nu}P_{\rho}+M^{A4}_{\mu\nu}K_{\rho})\epsilon^{\nu\rho\alpha\beta}\omega_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}(M^{A5}_{\mu\nu}P^{\prime}_{\rho}+M^{A6}_{\mu\nu}K^{\prime}_{\rho})\omega^{[\rho}u^{\nu]}.

(23)

The effective amplitudes
M^{Ai}
defined in (
III
). (
22
) characterizes the polarization effect due the the vorticity in the medium. Using the decomposition (
18
) and (
20
), the shear tensor related terms in the collision term can be casted into,

\displaystyle C_{A\mu}^{\text{shear}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4e^{4}\int_{q_{0},q,k^{\prime}}C_{\mu\alpha\beta}^{\text{shear}}\sigma^{\langle\alpha\beta\rangle}(-\beta)\bar{f}_{K}\bar{f}_{K^{\prime}}{f}_{P^{\prime}}f_{P}

(24)

with
C_{\mu\alpha\beta}^{\text{shear}}
defined through

\displaystyle C_{\mu\alpha\beta}^{\text{shear}}=M^{A3}_{\mu\alpha}P_{\beta}+M^{A4}_{\mu\alpha}K_{\beta}+\frac{1}{2P^{\prime}\cdot u}(M^{A5})_{\mu}^{~\nu}\epsilon_{\nu\alpha\rho\lambda}P^{\prime\rho}u^{\lambda}P^{\prime}_{\beta}+\frac{1}{2K^{\prime}\cdot u}(M^{A6})_{\mu}^{~\nu}\epsilon_{\nu\alpha\rho\lambda}K^{\prime\rho}u^{\lambda}K^{\prime}_{\beta}.

(25)

The shear tensor
\sigma^{\langle\alpha\beta\rangle}
is the symmetric and traceless part of
\sigma^{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\perp}^{\alpha}u^{\beta}+\partial_{\perp}^{\beta}u^{\alpha})-\frac{1}{3}\Delta^{\alpha\beta}\theta
. In the local rest frame of the fluid, the shear tensor only spatial components
\sigma_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}u_{j}+\partial_{j}u_{i})-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\vec{\partial}\cdot\vec{u}
. The efficient amplitudes are presented in (
III
). The remaining first order gradients are the temperature gradient and acceleration, the corresponding collision term is collected into

\displaystyle C_{A\mu}^{\text{Tgra+acc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4e^{4}\int_{q_{0},q,k^{\prime}}\big(C_{\mu\lambda}^{\text{Tgra}}\partial^{\lambda}\ln T+C_{\mu\lambda}^{\text{acc}}Du^{\lambda}\big)(-\beta)\bar{f}_{K}\bar{f}_{K^{\prime}}{f}_{P^{\prime}}f_{P},

(26)

with coefficients
C_{\mu\lambda}^{\text{Tgra}}
and
C_{\mu\lambda}^{\text{acc}}

\displaystyle C_{\mu\lambda}^{\text{Tgra}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-M^{A3}_{\mu\lambda}P\cdot u-M^{A4}_{\mu\lambda}K\cdot u-\frac{1}{2}(M^{A5})_{\mu}^{\;\,\nu}\epsilon_{\nu\lambda\alpha\beta}P^{\prime\alpha}u^{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}(M^{A6})_{\mu}^{\;\,\nu}\epsilon_{\nu\lambda\alpha\beta}K^{\prime\alpha}u^{\beta},

\displaystyle C_{\mu\lambda}^{\text{acc}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M^{A3}_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}P_{\lambda}+M^{A4}_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}K_{\lambda}.

(27)

The final result of the collision term will be the sum of the all the parts above, namely
C_{A\mu}=C_{A\mu}^{\text{diff}}+C_{A\mu}^{\text{vor}}+C_{A\mu}^{\text{shear}}+C_{A\mu}^{\text{Tgra+acc}}
. The further calculation of the collision terms is presented in Appendix.

IV
result

In this section, we show explicitly the result of the collision term. The leading logarithmic contribution comes from the soft
eT\ll q_{0},q\ll T
regime, the basic strategy to obtain the leading logarithmic contribution is to collecte all the terms up to
\mathcal{O}(Q^{-2})
in the integrand. Combined with the measure which is
\mathcal{O}(Q^{2})
, both combined will give the leading logarithmic results. With the assumption that mass of the probe fermion is much larger than thermal mass
m\gg m_{D}\sim eT
, Compton scattering is sub-leading and only Coulomb scattering is considered. In the calculation, there is no more restriction for the mass of the probe fermion. In the following, we present the collision term after the momentum integral for arbitrary mass of the probe fermion, and also take massless and non-relativistic limit for a comparison.

IV.1
arbitrary mass

For arbitrary nonzero mass of the probe fermion, the kinetic equation of the axial-vector component becomes

\displaystyle P\cdot\partial n^{\mu}(P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\kappa_{LL}\frac{T}{mv}\Big\{C^{(1)}n^{\mu}(P)+C^{(2)}u^{\mu}+C^{(3)}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\mu}+C^{(4)}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}\partial_{P_{\perp\mu}}n_{\nu}(P)

(28)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+C^{(5)}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}n^{\mu}(P)+C^{(6)}g^{\nu\rho}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\rho}}n^{\mu}(P)+C^{(7)}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\rho}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\rho}}n^{\mu}(P)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+C^{(8)}(\omega^{\mu}+\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\mu}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}\omega_{\nu})+C^{(9)}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\alpha}u_{\nu}\hat{P}_{\perp\rho}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\beta}+\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\beta}u_{\nu}\hat{P}_{\perp\rho}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\alpha}\Big)\sigma_{\langle\alpha\beta\rangle}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+C^{(10)}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha}\hat{P}_{\perp\beta}Du_{\nu}+C^{(11)}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha}\hat{P}_{\perp\beta}\partial_{\nu}\ln T\Big\},

note that both sides of the kinetic equation are on the mass shell
\delta(P^{2}-m^{2})
.
\kappa_{LL}
is the leading logarithmic coefficient defined by

\displaystyle\kappa_{LL}=e^{4}\ln\frac{1}{e}\frac{T^{2}}{8\pi}.

(29)

Similar to
Yang:2020hri
, we also introduce the four velocity
v^{\mu}\equiv P^{\mu}/m
for simplicity. Which has the normalization
v^{\mu}v_{\mu}=1
, and
v_{0}=p_{0}/m
with
v=|\vec{p}|/m
. The rapidity is
\eta_{p}\equiv\text{arctanh}(p/p_{0})\equiv 2^{-1}\ln[(p_{0}+p)/(p_{0}-p)]
, also define for simplicity
\theta_{n}\equiv v-v_{0}^{n}\eta_{p}
. The coefficients in (
28
) are

\displaystyle C^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v}{3v_{0}}-\frac{m^{2}v_{0}\theta_{-1}}{3T^{2}}(1-f_{p})f_{p}-\frac{mv^{3}}{3Tv_{0}^{2}}(1-2f_{p})

\displaystyle C^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{\theta_{-1}}{3v}-\frac{mv_{0}\theta_{-1}}{6Tv}(1-2f_{p})\Big)\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}n_{\nu}(P)+\frac{m(2v^{3}-v_{0}^{2}\theta_{-1})}{6v^{2}}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}n^{\nu}(P)+\frac{m(2v^{3}-3v_{0}^{2}\theta_{-1})}{6v^{2}}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\rho}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\rho}}n_{\nu}(P),

\displaystyle C^{(3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{v}{3v_{0}^{3}}+\frac{\theta_{-1}}{3v_{0}}-\frac{m\theta_{-1}}{6T}(1-2f_{p})\Big)\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}n_{\nu}(P)-\frac{mv_{0}\theta_{-1}}{6v}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}n^{\nu}(P)+\frac{m(2v^{3}-3v_{0}^{2}\theta_{-1})}{6vv_{0}}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\rho}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\rho}}n_{\nu}(P),

\displaystyle C^{(4)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{mv^{2}}{3v_{0}},

\displaystyle C^{(5)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m}{3v_{0}}-\frac{m^{2}v_{0}^{2}\theta_{-1}}{6Tv}(1-2f_{p}),

\displaystyle C^{(6)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m^{2}(3v^{3}v_{0}-v_{0}^{3}\theta_{-3})}{12v^{2}},

\displaystyle C^{(7)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{m^{2}v_{0}(2\theta_{1}+\theta_{-1})}{12v^{2}},

\displaystyle C^{(8)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(-\frac{mv}{3v_{0}^{2}}-\frac{m^{2}v_{0}\theta_{-1}}{6T}(1-2f_{p})\Big)\frac{(1-f_{p})f_{p}}{2T},

\displaystyle C^{(9)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{m(v^{5}+3v_{0}^{2}\theta_{1})}{3v^{2}v_{0}^{2}}+\frac{m^{2}v_{0}(2v^{3}-(v^{2}+3v_{0}^{2})\theta_{-1})}{6Tv^{2}}(1-2f_{p})\Big)\frac{(1-f_{p})f_{p}}{2T},

\displaystyle C^{(10)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{mv_{0}\theta_{-1}}{2v}+\frac{m^{2}(2v^{3}-3\theta_{-1}v_{0}^{2})}{12Tv}(1-2f_{p})\Big)\frac{(1-f_{p})f_{p}}{2T},

\displaystyle C^{(11)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{m(2v-3v_{0}^{2}\theta_{-1})}{6vv_{0}}+\frac{m^{2}(3v^{3}+5\theta_{1})}{12Tv}(1-2f_{p})\Big)\frac{(1-f_{p})f_{p}}{2T}.

(30)

f_{p}
is the local equilibrium number distribution of the probe, which is the Fermi Dirac distribution
f_{p}=1/(e^{E_{p}/T}+1)
, and
E_{p}=\sqrt{p^{2}+m^{2}}
.

The first two lines in (
28
) are the spin-diffusion terms. These terms has same structure as
Yang:2020hri
, while the explicit coefficients different by a
SU(N)
group constant. The last two lines in (
28

If the probe fermion is not polarized in the initial state, there will be polarization effects during the evolution, until the polarization effect balance the diffusion effect, then the spin of the probe fermion will achieve local equilibrium of the medium. It is worth emphasizing that, we have taken the assumption that the number distribution of the probe fermion has achieved local equilibrium with the medium. In general, the relaxation of
f_{V}
of the probe is coupled with the relaxation of spin
n^{\mu}
and should also be incorporated. These will be presented in further studies.

m\ll eT
is not satisfied, the Compton scattering with polarized photon is no more suppressed and contributes also at leading logarithmic order. \displaystyle C^{(8)}_{\text{chi}}

IV.3
non-relativistic limit

To investigate the spin evolution of heavy quark in the quark gluon plasma, the non-relativistic limit of the kinetic equation is analyzed. In the non-relativistic limit, it is assumed that
m\gg p,T
. We keep the collision term to
\mathcal{O}(m^{-2})
for later convenience when checking the eliminating of the collision term in the global equilibrium.

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{m}P_{\perp}^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\Big)n^{\mu}(P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\kappa_{LL}\Big\{C^{(1)}_{\text{non}}n^{\mu}(P)+C^{(2)}_{\text{non}}u^{\mu}+C^{(3)}_{\text{non}}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\mu}+C^{(4)}_{\text{non}}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}\partial_{P_{\perp\mu}}n_{\nu}(P)

(32)

\displaystyle\qquad\;+C^{(5)}_{\text{non}}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}n^{\mu}(P)+C^{(6)}_{\text{non}}g^{\nu\rho}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\rho}}n^{\mu}(P)

\displaystyle\qquad\;+C^{(8)}_{\text{non}}(\omega^{\mu}+\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\mu}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}\omega_{\nu})+C^{(9)}_{\text{non}}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\alpha}u_{\nu}\hat{P}_{\perp\rho}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\beta}+\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\beta}u_{\nu}\hat{P}_{\perp\rho}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\alpha}\Big)\sigma_{\langle\alpha\beta\rangle}

\displaystyle\qquad\;+C^{(10)}_{\text{non}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha}\hat{P}_{\perp\beta}Du_{\nu}+C^{(11)}_{\text{non}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha}\hat{P}_{\perp\beta}\partial_{\nu}\ln T\Big\},

with the coefficients are

\displaystyle C^{(1)}_{\text{non}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m^{2}}\big(\frac{T}{3}-\frac{2p^{2}}{9T}f_{p}(1-f_{p})\big),\qquad\qquad\quad\;\;C^{(2)}_{\text{non}}~=~\big(\frac{T}{3mp}-\frac{p}{9m^{2}}(1-2f_{p})\big)\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}n_{\nu}(P)+\frac{2T}{9m}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}n^{\nu}(P),

\displaystyle C^{(3)}_{\text{non}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{T}{3m^{2}}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}n_{\nu}(P)-\frac{Tp}{9m^{2}}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}n^{\nu}(P),\qquad\;\;\,C^{(4)}_{\text{non}}~=~\frac{Tp}{3m^{2}},

\displaystyle C^{(5)}_{\text{non}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{T}{3p}-\frac{p}{9m}(1-2f_{p})-\frac{p\,T}{6m^{2}},\qquad\qquad\quad C^{(6)}_{\text{non}}~=~\frac{T}{9}-\frac{p^{2}T}{12m^{2}},

\displaystyle C^{(8)}_{\text{non}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(-\frac{T}{3m}-\frac{p^{2}}{9m^{2}}\big)\frac{f_{p}(1-f_{p})}{2T},\qquad\qquad\;\;\,C^{(9)}_{\text{non}}~=~\big(-\frac{T}{3m}-\frac{4p^{2}(1-2f_{p})}{9m^{2}}\big)\frac{f_{p}(1-f_{p})}{2T},

\displaystyle C^{(10)}_{\text{non}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{pT}{3m^{2}}\frac{f_{p}(1-f_{p})}{2T},\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\;\;C^{(11)}_{\text{non}}~=~\big(\frac{T}{3p}+\frac{p(1-2f_{p})}{9m}-\frac{pT}{2m^{2}}\big)\frac{f_{p}(1-f_{p})}{2T}.

(33)

Both diffusion and polarization get suppressed, guarantees that in the heavy quark limit
m\rightarrow\infty
, the orientation of spin is fixed while the spin density still experience the diffusion process. The different behavior in spin orientation and spin density can be observed more clearly when further decomposing kinetic equation of
n^{\mu}
to its three degrees of freedom, as presented in Sec.
V
.

IV.4
Relaxation near global equilibrium

For quantum kinetic theory, the elimination of collision term in global equilibrium for massive fermion has been proved in
Wang:2020pej
;
Weickgenannt:2020aaf
. The collision terms in chiral kinetic theory is also shown to be vanishing in local equilibrium
Hidaka:2017auj
;
Fang:2022ttm
. We here check the vanishing of collision term in global equilibrium as a guarantee of the correctness the above calculation, and also as a prerequisite for extracting the relaxation rate. In global equilibrium,
n_{\mu}(P)
in a purely rotating fluid could be defined frame-independently
Wang:2020pej
;
Hidaka:2017auj
,

\displaystyle n_{\mu}^{\text{geq}}(P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{P\cdot\omega\,u_{\mu}}{2}-\frac{P\cdot u\omega_{\mu}}{2}\Big)f^{\prime}_{P}.

(34)

Using the following derivatives of the equilibrium number distribution function,
f_{p}=n_{F}(E_{p})
,

\displaystyle\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\mu}}f_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{p}(1-f_{p})\frac{P_{\perp\mu}}{p_{0}T},

\displaystyle\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\mu}}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}f_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{p}(1-f_{p})(1-2f_{p})\frac{P_{\perp\mu}P_{\perp\nu}}{p_{0}^{2}T^{2}}+f_{p}(1-f_{p})\frac{1}{p_{0}T}\Big(\frac{P_{\perp\mu}P_{\perp\nu}}{p_{0}^{2}}+\Delta_{\mu\nu}\Big),

(35)

together with the following tensors for the momentum derivatives of
n_{\mu}^{\text{geq}}(P)
,

\displaystyle\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}n_{\mu}^{\text{geq}}(P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{f_{p}(1-f_{p})}{2T}\Big(\omega_{\nu}u_{\mu}+\frac{P_{\perp\nu}}{p_{0}}\omega_{\mu}\Big)-\frac{f_{p}(1-f_{p})(1-2f_{p})}{2T}\frac{P_{\perp\nu}}{p_{0}T}\big(P_{\perp}^{\sigma}\omega_{\sigma}u_{\mu}-p_{0}\omega_{\mu}\big),

\displaystyle\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\rho}}n_{\mu}^{\text{geq}}(P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{f_{p}(1-f_{p})}{2T}\frac{1}{p_{0}}\Big(\Delta_{\nu\rho}+\frac{P_{\perp\nu}P_{\perp\rho}}{p_{0}^{2}}\Big)\omega_{\mu}

(36)

\displaystyle-\frac{f_{p}(1-f_{p})(1-2f_{p})}{2T}\frac{1}{p_{0}T}\Big(\big(\Delta_{\nu\rho}+\frac{P_{\perp\nu}P_{\perp\rho}}{p_{0}^{2}}\big)\big(P_{\perp}^{\sigma}\omega_{\sigma}u_{\mu}-p_{0}\omega_{\mu}\big)+\big(P_{\perp(\rho}\omega_{\nu)}u_{\mu}+\frac{2P_{\perp\nu}P_{\perp\rho}}{p_{0}}\omega_{\mu}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{f_{p}(1-f_{p})\big((1-2f_{p})^{2}-2f_{p}(1-f_{p})\big)}{2T}\frac{P_{\perp\nu}P_{\perp\rho}}{p_{0}^{2}T^{2}}\big(P_{\perp}^{\sigma}\omega_{\sigma}u_{\mu}-p_{0}\omega_{\mu}\big),

one can easily get all the derivatives in the collision terms. Substituting these derivatives back into the diffusion part, one can explicitly find that the diffusion part balances the vorticity part, and thus the collision terms are eliminated in global equilibrium. The vanishing of collision terms is also be proved for the massless limit and non-relativistic limit when inserting the derivatives in corresponding limits.

Near the global equilibrium, the relaxation of the spin is dominated by the diffusion terms, leading to the relaxation rate near global equilibrium

\displaystyle P\cdot\partial n^{\mu}(P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\hat{\tau}^{-1})^{\mu\nu}\delta n_{\nu}(P),

(37)

where the relaxation time is now an operator,

\displaystyle(\hat{\tau}^{-1})^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa_{LL}\frac{T}{mv}\bigg\{g^{\mu\nu}\bigg(\frac{v}{3v_{0}}-\frac{m^{2}v_{0}\theta_{-1}}{3T^{2}}(1-f_{p})f_{p}-\frac{mv^{3}}{3Tv_{0}^{2}}(1-2f_{p})+\Big(\frac{m}{3v_{0}}-\frac{m^{2}v_{0}^{2}\theta_{-1}}{6Tv}(1-2f_{p})\Big)\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\alpha}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\alpha}}

(38)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{m^{2}(3v^{3}v_{0}-v_{0}^{3}\theta_{-3})}{12v^{2}}g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\alpha}}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\beta}}-\frac{m^{2}v_{0}(2\theta_{1}+\theta_{-1})}{12v^{2}}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\alpha}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\beta}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\alpha}}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\beta}}\bigg)+\frac{mv^{2}}{3v_{0}}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}\partial_{P_{\perp\mu}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\Big(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{\theta_{-1}}{3v}-\frac{mv_{0}\theta_{-1}}{6Tv}(1-2f_{p})\Big)u^{\mu}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}+\frac{m(2v^{3}-v_{0}^{2}\theta_{-1})}{6v^{2}}u^{\mu}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}+\frac{m(2v^{3}-3v_{0}^{2}\theta_{-1})}{6v^{2}}u^{\mu}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\rho}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\rho}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\Big(\frac{v}{3v_{0}^{3}}+\frac{\theta_{-1}}{3v_{0}}-\frac{m\theta_{-1}}{6T}(1-2f_{p})\Big)\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\mu}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}-\frac{mv_{0}\theta_{-1}}{6v}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\mu}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}+\frac{m(2v^{3}-3v_{0}^{2}\theta_{-1})}{6vv_{0}}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\mu}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\rho}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\rho}}\bigg\}.

The assumption that the number distribution of the probe fermion has reached local equilibrium with the medium leads to the disappearance of gradient terms in the relaxation time. Otherwise, the kinetic equation of both spin and number distribution will couple with each other, the relaxation of the charge also contributes to spin evolution
Wang:2021qnt
;
Fang:2022ttm
. The full set of kinetic equation of both spin and charge to the first order of gradient will be included in an upcoming paper.

Numerical Analysis

V.1
Decomposition of kinetic equation

In the end, we give some preliminary attempts to solve the transport equation. So far, the axial component
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
has been treated as an independent variable in the transport equation, only constrained by the perpendicular relation that
P^{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}=0
. The decomposition of
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
was not considered for the simplicity when deriving the transport equation. Yet, the decomposition is necessary to obtain components with definite physical meanings, and to compare with the chiral limit. Limited by
P^{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}=0
,
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
has three independent degrees of freedom in the massive case, while in the chiral limit, the orientation of spin is locked to the momentum, so that
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
has only one degree of freedom. To get the transport equation for each of the three degrees of freedom of
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
and meanwhile keep the correct massless limit, we adopt the following decomposition of
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}=2\pi\epsilon(P\cdot u)\delta(P^{2}-m^{2})n_{\mu}

Sheng:2020oqs
,

\displaystyle n^{\mu}=P^{\mu}f_{A}+\frac{P^{2}}{(u\cdot P)^{2}-P^{2}}{P}_{\perp}^{\mu}f_{A}+\frac{P^{2}}{u\cdot P}\mathcal{M}_{\perp}^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2(u\cdot P)}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}P_{\nu}u_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}f_{V}.

(39)

f_{A}=u\cdot n/u\cdot P
is identified as the axial-charge density,
\mathcal{M}_{\perp}^{\mu}
is the transverse magnetic dipole-moment
\mathcal{M}_{\perp}^{\mu}=\Xi^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{M}_{\nu}
, where
\mathcal{M}^{\mu}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}u_{\nu}\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}
and
\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}
is the dipole moment tensor defined in last equation in (
II
). With such decomposition, the various components of
n^{\mu}
have clear physical meanings and smooth massless limit. See
Sheng:2020oqs
;
Guo:2020zpa
for more details of the decomposition.

Consider that the charge distribution has achieved local equilibrium, the general
f_{V}
in (

and transverse magnetic dipole-moment
\mathcal{M}_{\perp}^{\mu}(P)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+C^{(5)}_{A}\partial_{P_{\perp}^{\nu}}\mathcal{M}_{\perp}^{\nu}+C^{(6)}_{A}\hat{P}_{\perp}^{\nu}\omega_{\nu}\Big\},

both equations are coupled, with the coefficient

(41)

with

VI
Conclusion and Outlook

In this paper, we investigate the evolution of spin of a hard massive probe fermion in a massless hot QED plasma at local equilibrium in the framework of quantum kinetic equation. We adopt the assumption that mass of the probe fermion is much larger than the thermal mass
m\gg m_{D}\sim eT
. In this case, Coulomb scattering contributes to the leading logarithmic collision terms in the HTL approximation, while the Compton scattering is sub-leading and thus excluded. Besides, as the first step, we have assumed that
f_{V}
of the probe fermion has reached local equilibrium with the QED plasma, which means only the axial kinetic equation is required. As the axial-charge density and spin polarization in heavy ion collision are aroused by quantum effects or collective motion of the medium characterized by gradients of the fluid velocity, it is physically motivated to count
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
as
\mathcal{O}(\hbar)
. The diffusion and polarization effect in the spin evolution are hence both of
\mathcal{O}(\hbar)
and should be treated on the same basis. It is thus essential to derive the collision term of the axial kinetic equation to include all first order gradients, or to
\mathcal{O}(\hbar)
order.

Under such assumptions, we derive the collision term of axial-kinetic equation to the leading logarithmic order and to the first order of gradients. The diffusion and polarization effects coexist in the collision term, where the former drives the spin fluctuation to damp out, while the later characterizes the spin getting polarized by the vorticity, shear tensor, acceleration and temperature gradients of the fluid. Massless limit and non-relativistic limit of the axial kinetic equation are also presented. The effect of diffusion and polarization balance with each other, leading to the elimination of collision terms in the global equilibrium. Near the global equilibrium, the relaxation rate for the fluctuation is extracted. So as to illustrate the difference among the three dof of
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
, the axial kinetic equation is further decomposed into transport equation of axial charge and transverse magnetic dipole moment considering the purely rotational medium. Preliminary numerical analysis is carried out for the transverse dipole moment, the evolution of spin is observed for different mass and magnitude of vorticity. Without vorticity, spin of the probe experiences purely diffusion process, when vorticity is turned on, spin get polarized in direction of the vorticity. Modes with small momentum and small mass get polarized easier, inconsistent with the result of non-relativistic limit.

The physical settings in this paper can be viewed as a toy model for the evolution of spin of s-quark in the quark gluon plasma. In a more self-consistent scenario, we will consider the scattering of massive quark with a QCD plasma, without assuming the local equilibrium of number distribution for the probe quark, the full set of vector kinetic equation and axial kinetic equation will be derived. Besides, elimination of collision term of axial kinetic equation in global equation is considered in this paper, local equilibrium for the massive fermion is still under discussion. These will be included in an forthcoming paper.

\hat{p}
as
\hat{z}
for now. And introduce
\Omega
as the angle between
\vec{k}^{\prime}
and
\vec{q}
, namely
\cos\Omega=\cos\theta_{k}\cos\theta_{q}-\sin\theta_{k}\sin\theta_{q}\cos\Delta\varphi
, with
\Delta\varphi=\varphi_{q}-\varphi_{k}
. The measure can be parametrized as

\displaystyle\int d^{3}qd^{3}k^{\prime}=\int q^{2}dqd\cos\theta_{q}d\varphi_{q}k^{\prime 2}dk^{\prime}d\cos\theta_{k}d\Delta\varphi.

(47)

Considering that loop fermion are light fermions, which can be treated as massless.
Using
\vec{p}^{\prime}=\vec{k}^{\prime}-\vec{q}
and
\vec{k}=\vec{p}-\vec{q}
, we can use the on-shell condition to cast the
\delta
-function into

\displaystyle\delta(p_{0}-k_{0}-q_{0})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\delta(q\frac{p}{p_{0}}\cos\theta_{q}-q^{2}\frac{(p^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{q}+m^{2})}{2p_{0}^{3}}-q_{0}),

\displaystyle\delta(p^{\prime}_{0}-k^{\prime}_{0}+q_{0})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\delta(q\cos\Omega-q^{2}\frac{\sin^{2}\Omega}{2k^{\prime}}-q_{0}),

(48)

with
p_{0}=(p^{2}+m^{2})^{1/2}
. The angular integral over
\varphi_{q}
and
\varphi_{k}
can be performed to obtain,

\displaystyle\int d\varphi_{q}d\varphi_{k}\delta(p^{\prime}_{0}-k^{\prime}_{0}+q_{0})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 4\pi\frac{1}{q(1+\frac{q_{0}}{k^{\prime}})}\frac{1}{[\sin^{2}\theta_{q}\sin^{2}\theta_{k}-(\cos\Omega-\cos\theta_{q}\cos\theta_{k})^{2}]^{1/2}}.

(49)

Note that the above
\delta-
function constrain the unique solution of
\cos\Delta\varphi
, yet
\sin\Delta\varphi
can take both solutions
\pm(1-\cos^{2}\Delta\varphi)^{1/2}
. Thus integrals containing odd number of
\sin\Delta\varphi
will be vanishing under the angular integral. The square root constrains the domain of
\cos\theta_{k}
as
\cos(\theta_{q}-\Omega)<\cos\theta_{k}<\cos(\theta_{q}+\Omega)
. The other
\delta
-function gives

\displaystyle\int d\cos\theta_{q}\delta(p_{0}-k_{0}-q_{0})\simeq\frac{1}{\frac{pq}{p_{0}}(1+\frac{q_{0}}{p_{0}})}.

(50)

From the
\delta
-function, one can solve

\displaystyle\cos\Omega\simeq\frac{q_{0}}{q}+\frac{q}{2k^{\prime}}\Big(1-\frac{q_{0}^{2}}{q^{2}}\Big)+\mathcal{O}(q^{2}),\qquad\qquad\quad\sin\Omega\simeq\Big(1-\frac{q_{0}^{2}}{q^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(1-\frac{q_{0}}{2{k^{\prime}}}\Big),

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{q}\simeq\frac{p_{0}q_{0}}{pq}+\frac{q}{2p}\Big(1-\frac{q_{0}^{2}}{q^{2}}\Big)+\mathcal{O}(q^{2}),\qquad\qquad\sin\theta_{q}\simeq\Big(1-\frac{p_{0}^{2}}{p^{2}}\frac{q_{0}^{2}}{q^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(1-\frac{q^{2}-q_{0}^{2}}{p^{2}-p_{0}^{2}q_{0}^{2}}\frac{q_{0}p_{0}}{2}\Big),

(51)

in obtaining the leading-log order result, it is enough to keep the above solution to the first order of
q
. Note that
-1\leq\cos\Omega,\cos\theta_{q}\leq 1
also set a limit to
x=q_{0}/q
that
-\frac{p}{p_{0}}\leq\frac{q_{0}}{q}\leq\frac{p}{p_{0}}
. So that
\int dxdk^{\prime}
has the integration domain
\int dxdk^{\prime}\rightarrow\int_{0}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}\int_{-p/p_{0}}^{+p/p_{0}}dx
. With the above approximation of small momentum transfer, the collision term at leading logarithmic order can be explicitly calculated. The basic process is to collect all terms of integrad to
Q^{-2}
, after combining the measure, and integrate
q
ranges from
eT\ll q\ll T
, the log thus arises from
\int_{eT}^{T}dq/q=\ln(1/e)
.

To finish the remaining integral over
k^{\prime}
, the following expression are often utilized,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}n_{F}(k^{\prime})(-1+n_{F}(k^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-T^{2}\ln 2,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime 2}n_{F}(k^{\prime})(-1+n_{F}(k^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}\pi^{2}T^{3},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime 2}n_{F}^{2}(k^{\prime})(-1+n_{F}(k^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}\pi^{2}T^{3}+T^{3}\ln 2,

(52)

where

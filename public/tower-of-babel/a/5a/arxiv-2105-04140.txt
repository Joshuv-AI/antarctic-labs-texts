Abstract
. We study the existence of the stochastic flow associated to a linear stochastic evolution equation d ​ X = A ​ X ​ d ​ t + ∑ k B k ​ X ​ d ​ W k , {\rm d}X=AX{\rm d}t+\sum_{k}B_{k}X{\rm d}W_{k}, on a Hilbert space. Our first result covers the case where A A is the generator of a C 0 C_{0} -semigroup, and ( B k ) (B_{k}) is a sequence of bounded linear operators such that ∑ k ‖ B k ‖ < + ∞ \sum_{k}\|B_{k}\|<+\infty . We also provide sufficient conditions for the existence of stochastic flows in the Schatten classes beyond the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators. Some new results and examples concerning the so-called commutative case are presented as well.

1.
Introduction

Consider the following linear evolution equation

(1.1)

{\rm d}X=AX{\rm d}t+\sum_{k}B_{k}X{\rm d}W_{k},\qquad t\geq s,\,\,X(s)=x,

where
(W_{k})
is a sequence of independent standard real valued Wiener processes defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},\mathbb{P})
,
A
is the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left({\rm e}^{tA}\right)
on a Hilbert space
(H,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H})
and
B_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots
, are possibly unbounded linear operators on
H
. By the solution to
(
1.1
)
we understand the so-called mild solution defined as an adapted to the filtration generated by
(W_{k})
and having continuous trajectories in
H
process
X^{x}_{s}(t)
,
t\geq s
, satisfying the integral equation

X^{x}_{s}(t)={\rm e}^{(t-s)A}x+\sum_{k}\int_{s}^{t}{\rm e}^{(t-r)A}B_{k}X^{x}_{s}(r){\rm d}W_{k}(r),\qquad t\geq s.

For more details see the books of Da Prato and Zabczyk
[
9
]
and Flandoli
[
10
]
.

Let us denote by
L(H,H)
the space of all bounded linear operators on
H
. We denote by
\|\cdot\|
the operator norm on
L(H,H)
and by
|\cdot|_{H}
the norm on
H
. Let
\Delta:=\{(s,t)\colon 0\leq s\leq t<+\infty\}
.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that (
1.1
) defines a
stochastic flow
if there exists a mapping
\mathcal{X}\colon\Delta\times\Omega\mapsto L(H,H)
such that:

(i)

for every
s\geq 0
and
x\in H
, the process
\mathcal{X}(s,t;\cdot)(x)
,
t\geq s
, has continuous trajectories in
H
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

(ii)

for every
s\geq 0
and
x\in H
, we have
\mathcal{X}(s,t)(x)=X^{x}_{s}(t)
, for all
t\geq s
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

(iii)

for all
0\leq s\leq t\leq r
and
\omega\in\Omega
,
\mathcal{X}(t,r;\omega)\circ\mathcal{X}(s,t;\omega)=\mathcal{X}(s,r;\omega)
.

Remark 1.2
.

Property
(i)
means that for each
s
and
x
, the solution
X^{x}_{s}(t)=\mathcal{X}(s,t;\cdot)(x)
has continuous trajectories. This holds in the most of interesting cases by means of the so-called Da Prato-Kwapień-Zabczyk factorization, see e.g.
[
9
]
. In particular, the solution has the required property if
(B_{k})
is a finite sequence of bounded operators on
H
. Note, that property
(iii)
of the stochastic flow follows from the existence and uniqueness of the solution. Finally, note that for all
0\leq s\leq t\leq r\leq h
and
x,y\in H
, the random variables
\mathcal{X}(s,t)(x)
and
\mathcal{X}(r,h)(y)
are independent. Stochastic flows with this property are known as Brownian flows.

The theory of stochastic flows for linear and nonlinear stochastic differential equations in finite dimensional spaces is well established, see for example
[
18
]
. In particular, it is known that the nonlinear SDE

(1.2)

{\rm d}X=b(X){\rm d}t+\sigma(X){\rm d}W,\qquad X(s)=x,

defines a (nonlinear) stochastic flow if
b
and
\sigma
are
C^{1}
with bounded derivatives. The proof of this result requires a Sobolev embedding theorem. The case of additive noise, where
\sigma
does not depend on
X
, is easier. Namely, let
Y^{x}_{s}(t)
,
t\geq s
, be the solution to the ordinary differential equation with random coefficients

(1.3)

{\rm d}Y(t)=b\left(Y(t)+\sigma(W(t)-W(s))\right){\rm d}t,\qquad Y(s)=x.

Then the solution
X^{x}_{s}
to (
1.2
) is given by
X^{x}_{s}(t)=Y^{x}_{s}(t)+\sigma(W(t)-W(s))
, and consequently (
1.2
) defines a stochastic flow if (
1.3
) defines a flow.

It is natural to ask a question about the existence of stochastic flow associated to a stochastic evolution equation

(1.4)

{\rm d}X=(AX+F(X)){\rm d}t+\sum_{k}\sigma_{k}(X){\rm d}W_{k},\quad X(0)=x\,,

where
H
is an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space and
A
is the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
H
. If
\sigma
does not depend on
X
, then one can apply the same argument as in in the finite dimensional case. In this paper we focus on a linear equation (
1.1
), where
F=0
and
\sigma_{k}(x)=B_{k}x
. Skorokhod showed in his famous example
[
23
]
that the question of the existence of the flow for equation (
1.1
) is much more intricate than in finite dimensions. In Section 4 we consider a more general version of the Skorokhod example to obtain new and interesting phenomena.

In general, it is not possible to prove the existence of the flow in infinite dimensions using the Sobolev embedding theorems that are not available in infinite dimensions. However, for many nonlinear equations of the form (
1.4
) the corresponding flows can be obtained as continuous transformations of the flows corresponding to ordinary or partial differential equations with random coefficients (see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
12
,
20
]
). In fact, we will use a similar method in the proof of our main result concerning linear equations. A very general approach to the question of existence of stochastic flows for non-linear stochastic partial differential equations can be found in
[
11
]
.

It is tempting to obtain the existence of the flow
\mathcal{X}
corresponding to linear equation (
1.1
) by solving a linear equation

{\rm d}\mathcal{X}=A\mathcal{X}{\rm d}t+\sum_{k}B_{k}\mathcal{X}\,{\rm d}W_{k},\qquad t\geq s,\,\,\mathcal{X}(s)=I\,,

on the space of bounded operators. Unfortunately, the theory of stochastic integration on the space of all bounded linear operators on infinite dimensional Hilbert space does not exist. It is possible however to integrate in some smaller spaces, such as the Schatten classes. This approach has been applied by Flandoli
[
10
]
) in the case of Hilbert-Schmidt operators. In Section
5
of this paper we use the theory of stochastic integration on
M
-type 2 Banach spaces to extend the Flandoli result to the scale of Schatten classes
\mathbb{S}^{p}
for all
p\in[2,\infty)
. We will show that in the diagonal case solutions in the Schatten classes can exist
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. but not in the sense of expected value. In particular, in Proposition 4.3.3 we give conditions under which
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{X}(t))<+\infty
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. while
\mathbb{E}\,\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{X}(t))=+\infty
. It would be interesting to find more general conditions for such a behaviour of stochastic flows.

The paper is organized as follows: in the next section we prove a theorem dealing with equation (
1.1
) with a sequence of bounded operators
B_{k}
. We do not assume that the operators commute, therefore there are no explicit solutions. Then in Sections
4
we examine the case of commuting
B_{k}
. In this case the solution is given in an explicit form, and this allows us to construct interesting examples that lead to new questions that remain open. In Section
5
we study the existence of stochastic flows in Schatten classes.

2.
Main results

In this section we consider a linear stochastic equation on a separable Hilbert space
H
:

(2.1)

{\rm d}Y=B_{0}Y{\rm d}t+\sum_{k}B_{k}Y{\rm d}W_{k}

driven by a sequence of independent Wiener processes
W_{k}
. In theorems below we assume that
B_{0},B_{1},\ldots
is a sequence of bounded linear operators on
H
such that

(2.2)

M:=\sum_{k}\|B_{k}\|<+\infty.

We start with a result on the existence and regularity of the flow.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume (
2.2
). Then equation (
2.1
)
defines a stochastic flow
\mathcal{Y}
on
H
. Moreover, for any
T\in(0,+\infty)
, and
q\geq 1
,

(2.3)

\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{Y}(s,t)\right\|^{q}<+\infty.

Moreover, for every
T\in(0,+\infty)
, and
L=2,3,\ldots
, there exists a constant
C
such that for all for
0\leq s\leq t\leq u\leq T
we have

(2.4)

\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{Y}(s,t)-\mathcal{Y}(s,u)\right\|^{2L}\leq C\left|t-u\right|^{L-1}.

Consequently, by the Kolmogorov test, for any
s\geq 0
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\mathcal{Y}(s,t)
is a Hölder continuous
L(H,H)
-valued mapping of
t\geq s
, with exponent
\gamma<1/2
. Finally, for any
T\in(0,+\infty)
, and
L=2,3,\ldots
, there exists a constant
C
such that for all for
0\leq s\leq t\leq u\leq T
we have

(2.5)

\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{Y}(s,t)-\mathcal{Y}(u,v)\right\|^{2L}\leq C\left(\left|u-s\right|+\left|t-v\right|\right)^{L-1}.

Consequently, by the Kolmogorov test, for any
s\geq 0
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\mathcal{Y}(s,t)
is a Hölder continuous
L(H,H)
-valued mapping of
(t,s)\in\Delta
, with exponent
\gamma<1/2
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume (
2.2
). Let
\tilde{B}=\sum_{k\geq 1}B_{k}^{2}
, and let
\mathcal{Y}
be the flow defined by (
2.1
). Then for all
0\leq s\leq t
,
\mathcal{Y}(s,t)
is an invertible operator
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. and
\mathcal{Z}(s,t)=\left(\mathcal{Y}^{-1}(s,t)\right)^{\star}
is the flow defined by the equation

(2.6)

{\rm d}Z=\left(-B_{0}^{\star}+\tilde{B}^{\star}\right)Z{\rm d}t-\sum_{k}B_{k}^{\star}Z{\rm d}W_{k},\quad Z(s)=z\,.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume (
2.2
) Then for any generator
A
of a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
H
, equation (
1.1
) defines stochastic flow on
H
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that
B_{0},B_{1},\ldots,B_{N}
is a finite sequence of commuting generators of
C_{0}
-groups on
H
. Then the equation

{\rm d}X=B_{0}X{\rm d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{N}B_{k}X{\rm d}_{S}W_{k},\quad X(s)=x

considered in the Stratonovich sense defines stochastic flow on
H
. Moreover,

\mathcal{X}(s,t)=\exp\left\{(t-s)B_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(W_{k}(t)-W_{k}(s)\right)B_{k}\right\}.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

Write
W_{0}(t)\equiv t
. Then (
2.1
) can be written as follows

{\rm d}Y=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}B_{k}Y{\rm d}W_{k},\qquad Y(s)=x.

Let

\Theta:=\bigcup_{n=1}^{+\infty}\{0,1,\ldots\}^{n}.

Given
\alpha\in\{0,1,\ldots\}^{n}
we set
|\alpha|=n
and
B^{\alpha}:=B_{\alpha_{1}}B_{\alpha_{2}}\ldots B_{\alpha_{n}}
. Let us fix an
s\geq 0
. We define by induction the iterated stochastic integrals. If
|\alpha|=1
and
\alpha=(k):=k
,
k=0,1,\ldots
, then
I_{k}(t)=W_{k}(t)-W_{k}(s)
. For
\alpha\in\{0,1,\ldots\}^{n}
we set

I_{\alpha}(s,t):=\int_{s}^{t}I_{\widehat{\alpha}}(s,r){\rm d}W_{\alpha_{1}}(r),

where
{\widehat{\alpha}}:=(\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
. Then the solution
Y^{x}_{s}
to (
2.1
) is given by

Y^{x}_{s}(t)=x+\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|=n}^{+\infty}I_{\alpha}(s,t)B^{\alpha}x.

Therefore the corresponding flow
\mathcal{Y}
exists and

\mathcal{Y}(s,t;\omega)=I+\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|=n}^{+\infty}I_{\alpha}(s,t;\omega)B^{\alpha}

provided the series

\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|=n}^{+\infty}I_{\alpha}(s,t;\omega)B^{\alpha}

converges
\mathbb{P}
-a.s in
L(H,H)
. We have

\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|=n}^{+\infty}\|I_{\alpha}(s,t;\omega)B^{\alpha}\|\leq\xi(s,t;\omega),

where

\xi(s,t;\omega):=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=n}^{+\infty}\left|I_{\alpha}(s,t;\omega)\right|\,\|B^{\alpha}\|.

Therefore the stochastic flow exists if
\xi(s,t)<+\infty
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. Moreover,
\left\|\mathcal{Y}(s,t;\omega)\right\|\leq 1+\xi(s,t;\omega)
. In fact we will show that for any
T\in(0,+\infty)
and
L=1,2,3,\ldots
, we have

(2.7)

\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\,\xi^{2L}(s,t)<+\infty,

which obviously guarantees (
2.3
). To this end note that for
\alpha\colon|\alpha|=n\geq 1
we have

\mathbb{E}\left|I_{\alpha}(s,t)\right|^{2L}\leq C_{L}^{2Ln}\frac{\max\{(1,(t-s)^{2Ln}\}}{n!}(t-s)^{L-1}=:\eta_{n,L}(s,t),

where
C_{L}:=\frac{2L}{2L-1}
.

Let
\alpha^{1},\alpha^{2},\ldots,\alpha^{2L}\in\Theta
. By the Schwarz inequality we have

\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=1}^{2L}\left|I_{\alpha^{i}}(s,t)\right|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{2L}\left(\mathbb{E}\left|I_{\alpha^{i}}(s,t)\right|^{2L}\right)^{1/(2L)}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\xi^{2L}(s,t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|=n}^{+\infty}\left|I_{\alpha}(s,t)\right|\|B^{\alpha}\|\right)^{2L}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n_{1},\ldots n_{2L}=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{\alpha^{i}\colon|\alpha^{i}|=n_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{2L}\|B^{\alpha^{i}}\|\,\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=1}^{2L}\left|I_{\alpha^{i}}(s,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|=n}^{+\infty}\|B^{\alpha}\|\left(\mathbb{E}\left|I_{\alpha}(s,t)\right|^{2L}\right)^{1/(2L)}\right)^{2L}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\left(\eta_{n,L}(s,t)\right)^{1/(2L)}\sum_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|=n}^{+\infty}\|B^{\alpha}\|\right)^{2L}

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|=n}^{+\infty}\|B^{\alpha}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|=n}^{+\infty}\|B_{\alpha_{1}}\|\|B_{\alpha_{2}}\|\ldots\|B_{\alpha_{n}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\|B_{k}\|\right)^{n}=:M^{n},

we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\,\xi^{2L}(s,t)\right)^{1/(2L)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}M^{n}\left(\eta_{n,L}(s,t)\right)^{1/(2L)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}M^{n}C_{L}^{n}\left(\frac{\max\{(1,(t-s)^{2Ln}\}}{n!}\right)^{1/(2L)}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2L}}<+\infty,

which gives (
2.7
). We will show (
2.4
) using calculations from the proof of (
2.7
). Namely, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{Y}(s,t)-\mathcal{Y}(s,t+h)\right\|^{2L}=\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|=n}^{+\infty}\left[I_{\alpha}(s,t)-I_{\alpha}(s,t+h)\right]B^{\alpha}\right\|^{2L}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{\alpha\colon|\alpha|=n}^{+\infty}\|B^{\alpha}\|\left(\mathbb{E}\left|I_{\alpha}(s,t)-I_{\alpha}(s,t+h)\right|^{2L}\right)^{1/(2L)}\right)^{2L}.

Since

\mathbb{E}\left|I_{\alpha}(s,t)-I_{\alpha}(s,t+h)\right|^{2L}=\mathbb{E}\left|I_{\alpha}(0,h)\right|^{2L},

and (
2.7
) holds, for
0\leq s\leq t\leq u\leq T
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{Y}(s,t)-\mathcal{Y}(s,u)\right\|^{2L}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{Y}(0,0)-\mathcal{Y}(0,u-t)\right\|^{2L}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,\xi^{2L}(0,u-t)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\left(MC_{L}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{\max\{1,T^{2Ln}\}}{n!}\right)^{1/(2L)}\right)^{2L}(u-t)^{L-1}.\qquad\square

We are showing now (
2.5
). We can assume that
0\leq s\leq u
. Then there are three cases
(i)\colon s\leq t\leq u\leq v
,
(ii)\colon s\leq u\leq t\leq v
and
(iii)\colon s\leq u\leq v\leq t
. The first case follows from (
2.4
) by elementary calculations. For, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\|\mathcal{Y}(s,t)-\mathcal{Y}(u,v)\|^{2L}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2L}\left(\mathbb{E}\,\|\mathcal{Y}(s,t)\|^{2L}+\mathbb{E}\,\|\mathcal{Y}(u,v)\|^{2L}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|t-s|^{L-1}+|v-u|^{L-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|t-s|+|v-u|\right)^{L-1}=C\left(t-s+v-u\right)^{L-1}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(v-t+u-s\right)^{L-1}=C\left(|t-v|+|s-u|\right)^{L-1}.

The cases
(ii)
and
(iii)
follow also from (
2.3
), (
2.4
), and the flow property of
\mathcal{Y}
. Indeed consider
(ii)
. Then

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Y}(s,t)-\mathcal{Y}(u,v)\|

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{Y}(u,t)\circ\mathcal{Y}(s,u)-\mathcal{Y}(t,v)\circ\mathcal{Y}(u,t)\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\mathcal{Y}(u,t)\circ(\mathcal{Y}(s,u)-I\right)-\left(\mathcal{Y}(t,v)-I\right)\circ\mathcal{Y}(u,t)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathcal{Y}(u,t)\right\|\left\|\mathcal{Y}(s,u)-\mathcal{Y}(s,s)\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{Y}(t,v)-\mathcal{Y}(t,t)\right\|\left\|\mathcal{Y}(u,t)\right\|.

Therefore applying the Schwarz inequality, and (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) for
4L
, we can find a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\|\mathcal{Y}(s,t)-\mathcal{Y}(u,v)\|^{2L}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|s-u|^{2L-1}+|t-v|^{2L-1}\right)^{1/2}\leq C^{\prime}\left(|s-u|+|t-v|\right)^{L-1}.

Finally in the last case (case
(iii)
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Y}(s,t)-\mathcal{Y}(u,v)\|

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{Y}(v,t)\circ\mathcal{Y}(u,v)\circ\mathcal{Y}(s,u)-\mathcal{Y}(u,v)\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{Y}(v,t)\circ\mathcal{Y}(u,v)\circ\left(\mathcal{Y}(s,u)-I\right)+\left(\mathcal{Y}(v,t)-I\right)\circ\mathcal{Y}(u,v)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathcal{Y}(v,t)\right\|\left\|\mathcal{Y}(u,v)\right\|\left\|\mathcal{Y}(s,u)-\mathcal{Y}(s,s)\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{Y}(v,t)-\mathcal{Y}(v,v)\right\|\left\|\mathcal{Y}(u,v)\right\|.

Therefore applying the Schwarz inequality, and (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) for
4L
and
8L
, respectively, we can find a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\|\mathcal{Y}(s,t)-\mathcal{Y}(u,v)\|^{2L}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|s-u|^{2L-1}+|t-v|^{2L-1}\right)^{1/2}\leq C^{\prime}\left(|s-u|+|t-v|\right)^{L-1}.

\square
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2

From the first part we can easily deduce the invertibility of
\mathcal{Y}(s,t)
. For, let us fix
0\leq s\leq t<+\infty
. Consider, the partition
s=t^{n}_{0}<t^{n}_{1}<\ldots t_{n}^{n}=t
such that
t^{n}_{k+1}-t^{n}_{k}=\frac{t-s}{n}
. From the Hölder continuity of
\mathcal{Y}
it follows that with probability
1
, for any
\omega\in\Omega
, there is an
n(\omega)
such that

\left\|\mathcal{Y}(t^{n}_{k},t^{n}_{k+1};\omega)-\mathcal{Y}(t^{n}_{k},t^{n}_{k};\omega)\right\|\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Hence, since
\mathcal{Y}(t^{n}_{k},t^{n}_{k};\omega)=I
, each
\mathcal{Y}(t^{n}_{k},t^{n}_{k+1};\omega)

\mathcal{Y}(s,t;\omega)=\mathcal{Y}(t^{n}_{n-1},t^{n}_{n};\omega)\circ\mathcal{Y}(t^{n}_{n-2},t^{n}_{n-1};\omega)\circ\ldots\circ\mathcal{Y}(t^{n}_{0},t^{n}_{1};\omega),

\mathcal{Y}(s,t;\omega)

From the first part it also follows that (
2.6
) defines stochastic flow
\mathcal{Z}
. We will first show that
\mathcal{Z}^{\star}(s,t)\mathcal{Y}(s,t)=I
. To this end note that for all
z,y\in H
, and
0\leq s\leq t
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{Z}^{\star}(s,t)\mathcal{Y}(s,t)y,z\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{Y}(s,t)y,\mathcal{Z}(s,t)z\rangle_{H}=\langle Y^{y}_{s}(t),Z^{z}_{s}(t)\rangle_{H}.

Next

\displaystyle{\rm d}\langle Y^{y}_{s}(t),Z^{z}_{s}(t)\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\langle B_{0}Y^{y}_{s}(t),Z^{z}_{s}(t)\rangle_{H}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\langle Y^{y}_{s}(t),(-B_{0}^{\star}+\tilde{B}^{\star})Z^{z}_{s}(t)\rangle_{H}{\rm d}t-\sum_{k}\langle B_{k}Y^{y}_{s}(t),B_{k}^{\star}Z^{z}_{s}(t)\rangle_{H}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k}\langle B_{k}Y^{y}_{s}(t),Z^{z}_{s}(t)\rangle_{H}{\rm d}W_{k}(t)-\sum_{k}\langle Y^{y}_{s}(t),B^{\star}_{k}Z^{z}_{s}(t)\rangle_{H}{\rm d}W_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=0.

Hence for all

,

\langle\mathcal{Z}^{\star}(s,t)\mathcal{Y}(s,t)y,z\rangle_{H}=\langle y,z\rangle_{H},

and therefore
\mathcal{Z}^{\star}(s,t)\mathcal{Y}(s,t)=I
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. Since
\mathcal{Y}(s,t)
is invertible,
\mathcal{Z}^{\star}(s,t)=\left(\mathcal{Y}(s,t)\right)^{-1}
as required.
\square

Proof of Theorem

(A,D(A))

{\rm d}X=A_{\lambda}X{\rm d}t+\sum_{k}B_{k}X{\rm d}W_{k},\quad X(s)=x\in H.

Let
X^{x,\lambda}_{s}

X^{x}_{s}
is the solution to (
1.1
). It is easy to show that

(2.9)

\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left|X^{x,\lambda}_{s}(t)-X^{x}_{s}(t)\right|^{2}_{H}=0,\qquad\forall\,t\geq s.

Define
B_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}B_{k}^{2}
. Consider the following linear stochastic differential equation.

(2.10)

{\rm d}Y=B_{0}Y{\rm d}t+\sum_{k}B_{k}Y{\rm d}W_{k},\qquad Y(s)=x\in H.

From the first part of the theorem we know that (
2.8
) and (
2.10
) define stochastic flows,
\mathcal{X}_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, respectively. Consider the following equation with random coefficients

(2.11)

{\rm d}\mathcal{G}(t)=\mathcal{Y}(s,t)^{-1}\left(A_{\lambda}-B_{0}\right)\mathcal{Y}(s,t)\mathcal{G}(t){\rm d}t,\quad\mathcal{G}(s)=I.

Taking into account Hölder continuity of
\mathcal{Y}
one can see that the solution
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
exists and

\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(t,s)=\exp\left\{\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{Y}(s,r)^{-1}\left(A_{\lambda}-B_{0}\right)\mathcal{Y}(s,r){\rm d}r\right\}.

Moreover, see
[
24
]
, there is a strongly continuous two parameter evolution system
\mathcal{G}(s,t)
of bounded linear operators on
H
, such that for any

(2.12)

\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(s,t)x=\mathcal{G}(s,t)x,\qquad\mathbb{P}-a.s.

We will show that (
1.1
) defines stochastic flow and
\mathcal{X}(s,t)=\mathcal{Y}(s,t)\mathcal{G}(s,t)
. Taking into account (
2.9
) and (
2.12
) it is enough to show that
X^{x,\lambda}_{s}(t)=\mathcal{Y}(s,t)\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(s,t)x
,
t\geq s
, that is that
\mathcal{Y}(s,t)\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(s,t)x
,
t\geq s
, solves (
2.8
). Clearly
\mathcal{Y}(s,s)\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(s,s)x=x
. Next, for any

we have

{\rm d}\mathcal{Y}^{\star}(s,t)y=\mathcal{Y}^{\star}(s,t)B_{0}^{\star}y{\rm d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathcal{Y}^{\star}(s,t)B_{k}^{\star}y{\rm d}W_{k}(t).

Therefore

\displaystyle{\rm d}\left\langle\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(s,t)x,\mathcal{Y}^{\star}(s,t)y\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{Y}(s,t)^{-1}\left(A_{\lambda}-B_{0}\right)\mathcal{Y}(s,t)\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(s,t)x,\mathcal{Y}^{\star}(s,t)y\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(s,t)x,\mathcal{Y}^{\star}(s,t)B_{0}^{\star}y\right\rangle_{H}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(s,t)x,\mathcal{Y}^{\star}(s,t)B_{k}^{\star}y\right\rangle_{H}{\rm d}W_{k}(t),

and consequently we have the desired conclusion

{\rm d}\mathcal{Y}(s,t)\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(s,t)x=A_{\lambda}\mathcal{Y}(s,t)\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(s,t)x{\rm d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}B_{k}\mathcal{Y}(s,t)\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(s,t)x{\rm d}W_{k}(t).\qquad\square

Remark 2.5
.

The trick used in the proof of Theorem
2.3
is well-known in finite dimensional case and in some infinite dimensional cases and is known as the Doss–Sussman transformation.

There are important and interesting questions:
(i)
whether the flow
\mathcal{X}
defined by (
1.1
) is Hölder continuous
L(H,H)
-valued mapping of
(s,t)\in\Delta
,
(ii)
if the flow is invertible. Clearly, see the proof of Theorem
2.1
(ii),
(i)
implies
(ii)
. Unfortunately, if
A
is unbounded it is probably impossible that the flow is continuous in the operator norm. Let us recall that the semigroup
{\rm e}^{tA}
generated by
A
is continuous in the operator norm if and only if
A
is bounded. In the case when
{\rm e}^{tA}
,
t>0
, are compact it is however possible that
{\rm e}^{tA}
is continuous in the operator norm for
t>0
. Therefore, we can expect that in some cases the flow is continuous in the operator norm on the open set
0<s<t<+\infty
, see Section

. Finally note that if the flow
\mathcal{X}
is invertible, than formally
\mathcal{Z}=\left(\mathcal{X}^{-1}\right)^{*}
is the flow for the equation

{\rm d}Z=\left(-A^{\star}+\sum_{k}\left(B_{k}^{2}\right)^{\star}\right)Z{\rm d}t-\sum_{k}B_{k}^{\star}Z{\rm d}W_{k}.

Unfortunately, only in the case when
A
generates a group,
-A^{\star}
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup, and therefore the equation is often ill posed.

Example 2.6
.

This example is an extension of a model that is important in the theory of random evolution of spins in ferromagnetic materials, see

.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

B_{k}\colon L^{2}\left(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)\to L^{2}\left(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{3}\right),\quad B_{k}x(\xi)=x(\xi)\times g_{k}\left(\xi\right),\quad\xi\in\mathcal{O}\,,

where

.
Assume that

, hence

. Then, by Theorem

, the stochastic differential equation

3.
Nonlinear case

The method based on the Doss–Susmann transformation can be generalized to some non-linear equations, see
[
11
,
16
,
17
]
. Assume that
B_{0},\ldots B_{N}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is a finite sequence of functions with bounded derivatives. Let
\mathcal{O}
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
(A,D(A))
be the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
H=L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
. Consider the following SPDE and SDE in the Stratonovich sense

(3.1)

{\rm d}X=\left[AX+B_{0}(X)\right]{\rm d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{N}B_{k}(X){\rm d}_{S}W_{k},\qquad X(s)=x\in L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),

(3.2)

{\rm d}y=B_{0}(y){\rm d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{N}B_{k}(y){\rm d}_{S}W_{k}(t),\qquad y(s)=\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

By the classical theory of SDEs, see e.g.
[
15
,
18
]
, (
3.2
) defines stochastic flow
\eta(s,t)
,
0\leq s\leq t
, of diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}
. Consider now the following stochastic evolution equation on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
,

(3.3)

{\rm d}Y=B_{0}(Y){\rm d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{N}B_{k}(Y){\rm d}_{S}W_{k},\qquad Y(s)=x\in L^{2}(\mathcal{O}).

Then (
3.3
) defines stochastic flow
\mathcal{Y}
on
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
and

\mathcal{Y}(s,t)(x)(\xi)=\eta(s,t)(x(\xi)),\qquad x\in L^{2}(\mathcal{O}),\quad\xi\in\mathcal{O}.

Let
G_{s}^{x}(t)
be the solution to the evolution equation with random coefficients

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}G_{s}^{x}(t)=\mathcal{U}(s,t,G_{s}^{x}(t)),\qquad G_{s}^{x}(s)=x,

where

\mathcal{U}(s,t,y)=\left[D\mathcal{Y}(s,t)(y)\right]^{-1}A\mathcal{Y}(s,t)(y).

Above,
D\mathcal{Y}(s,t)(y)
is the derivative with respect to initial condition
y
. Clearly we need to assume that
\mathcal{Y}(s,t)(G_{s}^{x}(t))
is in the domain of
A
, and
D\mathcal{Y}(s,t)(G_{s}^{x}(t))
is an invertible operator.
Then by the Itô–Vencel formula

X^{x}_{s}(t)=\mathcal{Y}(s,t)(G^{x}_{s}(t)).

In
[
11
]
, this method was applied to equations of the type

{\rm d}X=\mathcal{A}(X){\rm d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{N}B_{k}X{\rm d}_{S}W_{k},

where
\mathcal{A}
is a monotone operator, and
(B_{k})
are first order differential linear operators.

4.

Proposition 4.1
.

Consider (
1.1
) with

(i)
For each initial value

) has a square integrable solution

\sup_{k}\left(2\alpha_{k}+\sigma_{k}^{2}\right)<+\infty.

(ii)
Assume (

(4.2)

\rho(s,t):=\sup_{k}\left\{\left[\alpha_{k}-\frac{\sigma_{k}^{2}}{2}\right]\sqrt{t-s}+\sigma_{k}\sqrt{2\log k}\right)<+\infty,\quad\forall\,0\leq s<t.

Define

\zeta_{k}(s,t;\omega):=\exp\left\{\sigma_{k}\left(W_{k}(t;\omega)-W_{k}(s;\omega)\right)+\left(\alpha_{k}-\frac{\sigma_{k}^{2}}{2}\right)(t-s)\right\}.

Clearly random variable
\zeta_{k}(s,t)
,

are independent and

(4.3)

\mathbb{E}\,\zeta_{k}(s,t)={\rm e}^{\alpha_{k}(t-s)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{E}\,\zeta^{2}_{k}(s,t)={\rm e}^{(2\alpha_{k}+\sigma_{k}^{2})(t-s)}.

Consider a finite dimensional subspace

of

, the solution exists and

(4.4)

X^{x}_{s}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\zeta_{k}(s,t)\langle x,e_{k}\rangle_{H}e_{k}.

Next, note that for any
x\in H
, if

is a solution, then it has to be of form (

). For, if

\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{|x|_{H}\leq 1}\left|X^{x}_{s}(t)\right|_{H}<+\infty\right\}=1.

Clearly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{|x|_{H}\leq 1}\left|X^{x}_{s}(t)\right|_{H}<+\infty\right\}=\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{k}\zeta(s,t)<+\infty\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{k}\left[\sigma_{k}\left(W_{k}(t)-W_{k}(s)\right)+\left(\alpha_{k}-\frac{\sigma_{k}^{2}}{2}\right)(t-s)\right]<+\infty\right\}.

Therefore the desired conclusion follows from the fact that if
(Z_{k})
is a sequence of independent

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{Z_{k}}{\sqrt{2\log k}}=1,\qquad\mathbb{P}-a.s.

∎

Remark 4.2

4.1.
Beyond integrability

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume (
4.5

(i)

If

or

(ii)

If
\sigma_{k}\to 0

(iii)

\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sqrt{\log k}}\to+\infty

Statement

. The first part of

\sup_{k}\sigma_{k}\sqrt{\log k}<+\infty

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\frac{W_{k}(t)-W_{k}(s)}{\sqrt{2\log k}}=-\sqrt{t-s},\qquad\mathbb{P}-a.s.

we have

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\sigma_{k}\left(W_{k}(t)-W_{k}(s)\right)=-\sqrt{t-s}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\sigma_{k}\sqrt{2\log k}>-\infty,\quad\mathbb{P}-a.s.

\sup_{k}\sigma_{k}^{2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2\log k}}{\sigma_{k}}-\frac{\sqrt{t-s}}{2}\right)<+\infty,\qquad\forall\,0\leq s<t.

We will use the Kolmogorov three series theorem to show that

\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{X}(s,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\zeta_{k}(s,t)<+\infty,\qquad\mathbb{P}-a.s.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\zeta_{k}(s,t)>1\right)

\displaystyle<+\infty,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathbb{E}\,Y_{k}

\displaystyle<+\infty,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Var}\,Y_{k}

\displaystyle<+\infty

.

Proof.

The first part follows from Proposition

Example 4.7

5.
Equations in Schatten classes

The problem of the existence of the stochastic flow in the finite dimensional case
H=\mathbb{R}^{d}
is simple. The flow
\mathcal{X}(s,t)
takes valued in the space of bounded linear operators
L(H,H)
that can be identified with the space of
d\times d
matrices
M(d\times d)
. We have the following SDE for
\mathcal{X}
in the space
M(d\times d)
;

(5.1)

{\rm d}\mathcal{X}=A\mathcal{X}{\rm d}t+({\rm d}\mathcal{W})\mathcal{X},\qquad\mathcal{X}(s,s)=I,

where
\mathcal{W}:=\sum_{k=1}^{d}B_{k}W_{k}
and
I
is the identity matrix. By a standard fixed point argument (
5.1
) has a unique global solution.

In infinite dimensional case, even if
(B_{k})
is a finite sequence of bounded linear operators, the situation is different. There is no proper theory of stochastic integration in the space
L(H,H)
if
H
is infinite-dimensional. One can overcome this difficulty by replacing
L(H,H)
with a smaller space of operators, such as the Hilbert–Schmidt or, more generally, the Schatten classes of operators.

5.1.
Main result

Let us recall that for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
the Schatten class
\mathbb{S}^{p}
of compact operators
K\colon H\to H
is a Banach space endowed with the norm

\|K\|_{p}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\lambda_{k}\left(K^{\star}K\right)^{p/2}\right)^{1/p}<+\infty\,,

where
\lambda_{k}(K^{\star}K)
stands for the
k
-th eigenvalue of
K^{\star}K
. For every
p\in[1,+\infty)
the space
\mathbb{S}^{p}
is a separable Banach space.

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
p\geq 2
the space
\mathbb{S}^{p}
is an M-type 2 Banach space.

Proof.

By Propositions 5.4.2 in
[
13
]
the space
\mathbb{S}^{p}
is a UMD space for every
p\in(1,+\infty)
. By Proposition 7.1.11 in
[
14
]
,
\mathbb{S}^{p}
has type 2 for
p\in[2,+\infty)
, and by Proposition 4.3.13 in
[
13
]
, M-type 2 property follows.
∎

Lemma
5.1
ensures that if
p\geq 2
, then
\mathbb{S}^{p}
is a right space for stochastic integration, for more details see e.g.
[
5
]
. We only recall here that if
W_{k}
are independent Wiener processes, ad
\psi_{k}
are
\mathbb{S}^{p}
-valued progressively measurable processes, such that

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|\psi_{k}(s)\|^{2}_{p}{\rm d}s<+\infty,\qquad k=1,2,\ldots,

then for each
k
, the integral

\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{k}(s){\rm d}W_{k}(s),

is well-defined, has continuous trajectories in
\mathbb{S}^{p}
and there exists a universal constant
C
such that

\mathbb{E}\left\|\int_{0}^{T}\psi_{k}(s){\rm d}W_{k}(s)\right\|^{2}_{p}\leq C\,\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|\psi_{k}(s)\|^{2}_{p}{\rm d}s<+\infty\,.

Moreover,

\mathbb{E}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{k}(s){\rm d}W_{k}(s)\right\|^{2}_{p}\leq C^{\prime}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|\psi_{k}(s)\|^{2}_{p}{\rm d}s.

The definition of the Wiener process given below uses the fact that
L(H,H)=\left(\mathbb{S}^{1}\right)^{\star}
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
B_{k}\in L(H,H)
for
k\geq 1
. We call
\mathcal{W}=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}W_{k}B_{k}
a
cylindrical Wiener process
on
L(H,H)
if for every
K\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
the process

W^{K}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Tr}\left(KB_{k}\right)W_{k}(t)\,,

is a real-valued Wiener process, and there is a constant
C
independent of
K
such that

(5.2)

\mathbb{E}\left|W^{K}(t)\right|^{2}=t\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left(KB_{k}\right)\right)^{2}\leq Ct\|K\|_{1}^{2}<+\infty\,.

Note that condition (
5.2
) holds if
\sum_{k}\left\|B_{k}\right\|^{2}_{L(H,H)}<\infty
. Indeed, we have (see Theorem 3.1 in
[
22
]
)

\left|\mathrm{Tr}\left(KB_{k}\right)\right|\leq\left\|KB_{k}\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|B_{k}\right\|_{L(H,H)}\|K\|_{1}

and the claim follows.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(A,D(A))
be the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
({\rm e}^{tA})
on
H
. For
T\in L(H,H)
we define

(5.3)

\mathcal{S}(t)T={\rm e}^{tA}\circ T,\quad t\geq 0\,,

and denote
\mathcal{S}=\{\mathcal{S}(t);\,t\geq 0\}
. Then:

(a)
\mathcal{S}=\left(\mathcal{S}(t)\right)
defines a semigroup (but in general not a
C_{0}
-semigroup) of bounded operators on
L(H,H)
.

(b) For every
p\in[1,+\infty)
we have
\mathcal{S}(t)\mathbb{S}^{p}\subset\mathbb{S}^{p}
and
\mathcal{S}
defines a

C_{0}
-semigroup on
\mathbb{S}^{p}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{S}^{p}
is an ideal, we have
\mathcal{S}(t)\mathbb{S}^{p}\subset\mathbb{S}^{p}
and the operator
\mathcal{S}(t)\colon\mathbb{S}^{p}\to\mathbb{S}^{p}
is bounded. To prove strong continuity of
\mathcal{S}
on
\mathbb{S}^{p}
, let us recall that for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
the space
\mathbb{S}^{p}
is the closure of the space of finite rank operators in the
\mathbb{S}^{p}
-norm. Let
t>0
and
T\in\mathbb{S}^{p}
be fixed. There exists a sequence
\left(T_{n}\right)
of finite rank operators, such that
\left\|T_{n}-T\right\|_{p}\to 0
. Choose
n
such that for
t\leq 1
,

\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{p}+\left\|{\rm e}^{tA}\left(T-T_{n}\right)\right\|_{p}<\varepsilon\,.

Since

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}(t)T-T\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathcal{S}(t)\left(T-T_{n}\right)\right\|_{p}+\left\|\mathcal{S}(t)T_{n}-T_{n}\right\|_{p}+\left\|T-T_{n}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\left\|\mathcal{S}(t)\left(T-T_{n}\right)\right\|_{p}

and

\lim_{t\to 0}\left\|\mathcal{S}(t)T_{n}-T_{n}\right\|_{p}=0\,,

the strong continuity follows.
∎

Let
A
be the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
({\rm e}^{tA})
on
H
and let
\mathcal{A}
be the generator of the semigroup
\mathcal{S}
defined in Lemma
5.3
. Let
W
be a cylindrical Wiener process on

. Consider the following stochastic equation on
\mathbb{S}^{p}
, where

,

(5.4)

{\rm d}\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{A}\mathcal{X}{\rm d}t+({\rm d}\mathcal{W})\mathcal{X},\qquad\mathcal{X}(s)=I.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
p\geq 1
. We will say that a process
\mathcal{X}(s,\cdot;\cdot)\colon[s,+\infty)\times\Omega\mapsto L(H,H)
with continuous paths in

is an

-valued solution to (

\mathcal{X}(s,\cdot;\cdot)\colon(s,+\infty)\times\mapsto\mathbb{S}^{p}
is measurable and adapted,

\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{T}\|\mathcal{X}(t)\|_{p}^{2}{\rm d}t<+\infty,\qquad\text{for any $T\in(s,+\infty)$},

and

\mathcal{X}(t)=\mathcal{S}(t-s)I+\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-r)({\rm d}\mathcal{W}(r))\mathcal{X}(r)\qquad\text{for $t\geq s$, $\mathbb{P}$-a.s.}.

In the equation above

\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-r)({\rm d}\mathcal{W}(r))\mathcal{X}(r)=\sum_{k}\mathcal{S}(t-r)B_{k}\mathcal{X}(r){\rm d}W_{k}(r)\,.

We have the following result.

Proposition 5.5
.

If
\mathcal{X}
is a solution to (
5.4

\mathcal{X}
is the flow corresponding to (
1.1
).

Theorem 5.6
.

Assume that there exist constants
\gamma<1/2
and
C>0
such that

(5.5)

\|S(t)\|_{p}\leq\frac{C}{t^{\gamma}},\quad t\leq 1.

Assume that
\mathcal{W}=\sum_{k}B_{k}W_{k}
is a cylindrical Wiener process on
L(H,H)
such that
\sum_{k}\|B_{k}\|^{2}<+\infty
.
Then for any
s\geq 0
, (
5.4
) has a unique solution in
\mathbb{S}^{p}
. Moreover,
(s,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto\mathcal{X}(s,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{p}
is continuous
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

Proof.

Let us fix
0\leq s<T<+\infty
. Let
\Psi
be the space of all adapted measurable processes
\psi\colon(s,T]\times\Omega\mapsto\mathbb{S}^{p}
such that

\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{T}\|\psi(t)\|^{2}_{p}{\rm d}t<+\infty.

On
\Psi
consider the family of equivalent norms

|||\psi|||_{\beta}:=\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{T}{\rm e}^{-\beta t}\|\psi(t)\|^{2}_{p}{\rm d}t\right)^{1/2},\qquad\beta\geq 0.

Note that, as
({\rm e}^{tA})
is
C_{0}
on
H
there is a constant
C_{1}<+\infty
such that for
t\in(s,T]
, and
\psi\in\Psi
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{t}\|\mathcal{S}(t-r)B_{k}\psi(r)\|_{p}^{2}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{t}\|{\rm e}^{(t-r)A}\|^{2}\|B_{k}\|^{2}\|\psi(r)\|_{p}^{2}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\sum_{k}\|B_{k}\|^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{t}\|\psi(r)\|_{p}^{2}{\rm d}r<+\infty.

Therefore, by assumption (
5.5
), the mapping

\mathcal{I}\psi(t):=\mathcal{S}(t-s)I+\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-r)({\rm d}\mathcal{W}(r))\psi(r),\qquad\psi\in\Psi,\quad t\in(s,T],

is well-defined from
\Psi
into
\Psi
. For
\beta
large enough
\mathcal{I}
is contraction on
(\Psi,|\|\cdot\||_{\beta})
. For we have

\displaystyle|||\mathcal{I}(\psi)-\mathcal{I}(\phi)|||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\sum_{k}\|B_{k}\|^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{T}{\rm e}^{-\beta t}\int_{s}^{t}\|\psi(r)-\phi(r)\|^{2}_{p}{\rm d}r{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{T}{\rm e}^{-\beta r}\|\psi(r)-\phi(r)\|^{2}_{p}\int_{r}^{T}{\rm e}^{-\beta(t-r)}{\rm d}t{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\frac{1}{\beta}|||\psi-\phi|||^{2}_{\beta}.

Thus by the Banach fixed point theorem there is an
\mathcal{X}(s,\cdot)\in\Psi
such that
\mathcal{I}\left(\mathcal{X}(s,\cdot)\right)=\mathcal{X}(s,\cdot)
. What is left is to show that for any
\psi\in\Psi
, the stochastic integral

\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-r)({\rm d}\mathcal{W}(r))\psi(r),\qquad t\geq s,

has continuous paths in
\mathbb{S}^{p}
. Since there exists an
\alpha>0
such that

\int_{s}^{T}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\|\mathcal{S}(t-s)I\|^{2}_{p}{\rm d}t<+\infty,

the desired continuity follows from Burkholder inequality by a standard modification of the Da Prato–Kwapien–Zabczyk factorization method.
∎

Example 5.7
.

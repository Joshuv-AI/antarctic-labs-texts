The Connection Between Nesterov’s Accelerated Methods and Halpern Fixed-Point Iterations
Quoc Tran-Dinh

Abstract
We derive a direct connection between Nesterov’s accelerated first-order algorithm and the Halpern fixed-point iteration scheme for approximating a solution of a co-coercive equation. We show that one method is equivalent to another via a simple transformation, leading to a simple convergence proof for Nesterov’s accelerated scheme. Alternatively, we directly establish convergence rates of Nesterov’s accelerated variant, and as a consequence, we obtain a new convergence rate of the Halpern fixed-point iteration. Next, we apply our results to different methods such as proximal-point, forward-backward splitting, and three-operator splitting methods for solving monotone inclusions, where our convergence guarantees are applicable. Since the forward scheme requires the co-coerciveness of the underlying operator, we derive new Nesterov’s accelerated variants for both recent extra-anchored gradient and past-extra anchored gradient methods which can avoid the co-coerciveness assumption. For the extra-anchored gradient method, we consider two variants studied in [ 43 ] for the monotone case and in [ 21 ] for the co-monotone case. The latter covers the former as a special case and has a tighter convergence rate. For the past-extra anchored gradient method (an accelerated variant of the optimistic gradient method) [ 41 ] , we obtain a new Nesterov’s accelerated variant with two past-iterate correction terms that may help to develop new Nesterov’s accelerated methods for minimax problems and their continuous views. We test our theoretical results on two numerical examples, where the actual convergence rates match well the theoretical rates up to a constant factor.

Motivation and related work.

In the last decades, accelerated first-order methods have become an extremely popular and attractive research topic in optimization and related fields due to their applications to large-scale optimization problems in machine learning and signal and image processing

Among this research theme, Nesterov’s accelerated approach

This well-known technique has been extended to different directions, including minimax problems, variational inequalities, and monotone inclusions

Convergence rates of these methods have been intensively studied, which show significant improvements from

The latter rate matches the lower bound rates in different settings using different criteria, see, e.g.,

In recent years, many researchers, including

They have proved
\mathcal{O}\left(1/k^{2}\right)

Note that the problem of approximating a solution of a maximally monotone inclusion can be reformulated equivalently to approximating a fixed-point of a non-expansive operator

Therefore, theory and solution methods from one field can be applied to the other and vice versa.

Alternatively, the Halpern fixed-point iteration is a classical method in fixed-point theory rooted from
[

to approximate a fixed-point of a nonexpansive operator.
This method has recently attracted a great attention due to its ability to accelerate convergence rate in terms of operator residual norm.
F. Lieder appears to be the first who proved an

Unlike Nesterov’s accelerated method which was originally developed for solving convex optimization problems and its convergence rate is given in terms of the objective residual in general convex case, the Halpern fixed-point iteration was proposed to approximate a fixed-point of a nonexpansive operator, which is much more general than convex optimization, and hence, very convenient to extend to maximally monotone inclusions, and, in particular, game theory, robust optimization, and minimax problems

Such a type of questions was mentioned in

Since both schemes come from different roots, at first glance, it is unclear to see a clear connection between Nesterov’s accelerated and the Halpern fixed-point schemes.
Nesterov’s accelerated method is perhaps rooted from the gradient descent scheme in convex optimization with an additional momentum term.

Contribution.

In this paper, we show that the Halpern fixed-point method can be transformed into Nesterov’s accelerated scheme and vice versa.
We first present our results on approximating a solution of a co-coercive equation, and then we extend them to other schemes.
In the first case, we establish that the iterate sequences generated by both schemes are identical, but the choice of parameters in these schemes could be different, leading to different convergence guarantees.
We show that one can obtain the convergence of one scheme from another and vice versa.
Then, by utilizing our analysis, we prove that a number of methods, including proximal-point, forward-backward splitting, Douglas-Rachford splitting, and three-operator splitting schemes can be easily accelerated and achieve fast convergence rates compared to their classical schemes.

Our next contribution is to show that the recent extra-anchored gradient (EAG) proposed in
[
21
,
43
]
can be transformed into Nesterov’s accelerated interpretation.
We provide an alternative analysis using a slightly different Lyapunov function and still obtain the same convergence rate on the operator norm as in
[
21
,
43
]
.
One important fact is that this accelerated method works with monotone and Lipschitz continuous operator instead of a co-coercive one as in
[
1
,
18
,
25
]
.
This may provide some initial steps toward understanding Nesterov’s accelerated behavior on non-co-coercive operators, and possibly their continuous views.

Finally, we derive Nesterov’s accelerated variant of the past-extra anchored gradient method in
[
41
]
and provide a different convergence rate analysis than that of
[
41
]
by using two different stepsizes for the extra-gradient step.
Interestingly, such a new scheme is very different from existing ones, e.g.,
[
1
,
18
,
25
]
, due to the use of two consecutive past iterates in the momentum/correction terms.
This algorithm illustrates that one can build Nesterov’s accelerated scheme for monotone equations without co-coerciveness.

As a caveat, while our analysis in the co-coercive case hints a different choice of parameters for Halpern fixed-point iteration schemes and leads to different convergence guarantees, the analysis in the extra anchored gradient-type schemes does not provide such an opportunity to achieve
o\left(1/k^{2}\right)
-rates, and therefore, we plan to investigate it in our future work.
In addition, we have not tried to optimize the choice of parameters in order to close the gap between the upper and lower bound rates.
While we are working on this paper, a recent manuscript
[
32
]
comes to our attention, which closely relates to Subsection
3.2
of this paper.
However,
[
32
]
mainly focuses on establishing exact optimal rates of accelerated schemes for maximally monotone inclusions, whereas we study the connection between the two accelerated approaches and their convergence guarantees for different methods, including extra-anchored gradient.

2.1
Basic concepts

We work with finite dimensional spaces
\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the standard inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|
.
For a set-valued mapping
G:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightrightarrows 2^{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
,
\mathrm{dom}(G)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:Gx\not=\emptyset\right\}
denotes its domain,
\mathrm{graph}(G)=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}:y\in Gx\right\}
denotes its graph, where
2^{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
is the set of all subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
The inverse of
G
is defined as
G^{-1}y:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:y\in Gx\}
.
Throughout this paper, we work with finite dimensional spaces; however, we believe that our result can be easily extended to Hilbert spaces.

Monotonicity.

For a set-valued mapping
G:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightrightarrows 2^{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
, we say that
G
is monotone if
\langle u-v,x-y\rangle\geq 0
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
,
u\in Gx
, and
v\in Gy
.

G
is said to be
\mu_{G}
-strongly monotone (or sometimes called coercive) if
\langle u-v,x-y\rangle\geq\mu_{G}\|x-y\|^{2}
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
,
u\in Gx
, and
v\in Gy
, where
\mu_{G}>0
is called a strong monotonicity parameter.
If
\mu_{G}<0
, then we say that
G
is weakly monotone (also known as
-\mu_{G}
-hypomonotone).
If
G
is single-valued, then these conditions reduce to
\langle Gx-Gy,x-y\rangle\geq 0
and
\langle Gx-Gy,x-y\rangle\geq\mu_{G}\|x-y\|^{2}
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
, respectively.
We say that
G
is maximally monotone if
\mathrm{graph}(G)
is not properly contained in the graph of any other monotone operator.
Note that
G
is maximally monotone, then
\alpha G
is also maximally monotone for any
\alpha>0
, and if
G
and
H
are maximally monotone, and
\mathrm{dom}(F)\cap\mathrm{int}\left(\mathrm{dom}(H)\right)\not=\emptyset
, then
G+H
is maximally monotone.

Lipschitz continuity and co-coerciveness.

A single-valued operator
G
is said to be
L
-Lipschitz continuous if
\|Gx-Gy\|\leq L\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
, where
L\geq 0
is a Lipschitz constant.
If
L=1
, then we say that
G
is nonexpansive, while if
L\in[0,1)
, then we say that
G
is
L
-contractive, and
L
is its contraction factor.
We say that
G
is
\frac{1}{L}
-co-coercive if
\langle Gx-Gy,x-y\rangle\geq\frac{1}{L}\|Gx-Gy\|^{2}
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
.
If
L=1
, then we say that
G
is firmly nonexpansive.
Note that if
G
is
\frac{1}{L}
-cocoercive, then it is also monotone and
L
-Lipschitz continuous (by using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality), but the reverse statement is not true in general.
If
L<0
, then we say that
G
is
\frac{1}{L}
-co-monotone
[
6
]
(also known as
-\frac{1}{L}
-cohypomonotone).

Resolvent operator.

The operator
J_{G}x:=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:x\in y+Gy\right\}
is called the resolvent of
G
, often denoted by
J_{G}x=(\mathbb{I}+G)^{-1}x
, where
\mathbb{I}
is the identity mapping.
Clearly, evaluating
J_{G}
requires solving a strongly monotone inclusion
0\in y-x+Gy
.
If
G
is monotone, then
J_{G}
is singled-valued, and if
G
is maximally monotone then
J_{G}
is singled-valued and
\mathrm{dom}(J_{G})=\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
If
G
is monotone, then
J_{G}
is firmly nonexpansive
[
5
, Proposition 23.10]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
and
B
in (
1
) be maximally monotone,
B
be single-valued, and
C=0
.
Let
G_{\lambda Q}
be the forward-backward residual mapping defined by (
2
).
Then, we have

\langle G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y,x-y+\lambda(Bx-By)\rangle\geq\lambda\|G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y\|^{2}+\langle Bx-By,x-y\rangle.

(3)

Moreover,
G_{\lambda Q}y^{\star}=0
iff
y^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(A+B)
.
If, additionally,
B
is
\frac{1}{L}
-co-coercive, then
G_{\lambda Q}
is
\frac{\lambda(4-\lambda L)}{4}
-co-coercive provided that
0<\lambda<\frac{4}{L}
.

Proof.

The inequality (
3
) and the statement:
G_{\lambda Q}y^{\star}=0
iff
y^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(A+B)
were proved in
[
41
]
.
From the definition of
G_{\lambda Q}
, we have
J_{\lambda A}(y-\lambda By)=y-\lambda G_{\lambda Q}y
.
By the firm non-expansiveness of
J_{\lambda A}
, we have
\langle J_{\lambda A}(x-\lambda Bx)-J_{\lambda A}(y-\lambda By),x-y-\lambda(Bx-By)\rangle\geq\|J_{\lambda A}(x-\lambda Bx)-J_{\lambda A}(y-\lambda By)\|^{2}
, leading to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle x-y-\lambda(G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y),x-y-\lambda(Bx-By)\rangle\geq\|x-y-\lambda(G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y)\|^{2}.\end{array}

Expanding this inequality, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|x-y\|^{2}&-&\lambda\langle x-y,Bx-By\rangle-\lambda\langle x-y,G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y\rangle+\lambda^{2}\langle Bx-By,G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y\rangle\\
&\geq&\|x-y\|^{2}-2\lambda\langle x-y,G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y\rangle+\lambda^{2}\|G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y\|^{2}\end{array}

Simplifying this expression and utilizing the
\frac{1}{L}
-co-coerciveness of
C
, we arrive at

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle x-y,G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y\rangle&\geq&\lambda\|G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y\|^{2}+\langle x-y,Bx-By\rangle-\lambda\langle Bx-By,G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y\rangle\\
&\geq&\lambda\|G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y\|^{2}+\frac{1}{L}\|Bx-By\|^{2}-\lambda\|Bx-By\|\|G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y\|\\
&\geq&\lambda\|G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y\|^{2}+\frac{1}{L}\|Bx-By\|^{2}-\frac{1}{L}\|Bx-By\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{\lambda^{2}L}{4}\|G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y\|^{2}\\
&=&\lambda\left(1-\frac{\lambda L}{4}\right)\|G_{\lambda Q}x-G_{\lambda Q}y\|^{2}.\end{array}

This shows that
G_{\lambda Q}
is
\frac{\lambda(4-\lambda L)}{4}
-co-coercive provided that
0<\lambda<\frac{4}{L}
.
∎

The second operator is the following residual mapping of a three-operator splitting scheme:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}E_{\lambda Q}y&:=&\frac{1}{\lambda}(J_{\lambda B}y-J_{\lambda A}(2J_{\lambda B}y-y-\lambda C\circ J_{\lambda B}y)),\\

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A
and
B
in (
1
) be maximally monotone, and
C
be
\frac{1}{L}
-co-coercive.
Let
E_{\lambda Q}
be the residual mapping defined by (
4
).
Then,
E_{\lambda Q}u^{\star}=0
iff
y^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(A+B+C)
, where
y^{\star}=J_{\lambda B}u^{\star}
.
Moreover, we have
E_{\lambda Q}
satisfies the following property for all

and

\langle E_{\lambda Q}u-E_{\lambda Q}v,u-v\rangle\geq\tfrac{\lambda(4-L\lambda)}{4}\|E_{\lambda Q}u-E_{\lambda Q}v\|^{2}.

(5)

If
B
is single-valued and
C=0
, and
G_{\lambda Q}
is defined by (
2
), then we have
E_{\lambda Q}u=G_{\gamma Q}y
, where

or equivalently,
u=y+\lambda By
.

Proof.

The property
E_{\lambda Q}u^{\star}=0
iff

]

The property (

Let us introduce

Then, by the

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle z_{u}-z_{v},u-v\rangle&\geq&\|z_{u}-z_{v}\|^{2},\\
\langle 2(z_{u}-z_{v})-(u-v)-\lambda(Cz_{u}-Cz_{v}),w_{u}-w_{v}\rangle&\geq&\|w_{u}-w_{v}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Next, using these inequalities and noting that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\lambda\langle E_{\lambda Q}u-E_{\lambda Q}v,u-v\rangle&=&\langle z_{u}-z_{v},u-v\rangle-\langle w_{u}-w_{v},u-v\rangle\\
&\geq&\|z_{u}-z_{v}\|^{2}+\|w_{u}-w_{v}\|^{2}-2\langle z_{u}-z_{v},w_{u}-w_{v}\rangle\\
&&+{~}\lambda\langle Cz_{u}-Cz_{v},w_{u}-w_{v}\rangle\\
&=&\lambda^{2}\|E_{\lambda Q}u-E_{\lambda Q}v\|^{2}+\lambda\langle Cz_{u}-Cz_{v},w_{u}-w_{v}\rangle.\end{array}

Now, by the

-co-coerciveness of

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle Cz_{u}-Cz_{v},w_{u}-w_{v}\rangle&=&\langle Cz_{u}-Cz_{v},z_{u}-z_{v}\rangle-\lambda\langle Cz_{u}-Cz_{v},E_{\lambda G}u-E_{\lambda G}v\rangle\\
&\geq&\frac{1}{L}\|Cz_{u}-Cz_{v}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{L}\|Cz_{u}-Cz_{v}\|^{2}-\frac{L\lambda^{2}}{4}\|E_{\lambda G}u-E_{\lambda G}v\|^{2}.\end{array}

Combining both inequalities, we obtain (

Finally, assume that

3
Equivalence Between Halpern and Nesterov Acceleration

Both Nesterov’s accelerated and Halpern iteration schemes show significant improvement on convergence rates over classical methods for solving (
1
).
However, they are derived from different perspectives, and it is unclear if they are closely related to each other.
In this section, we show that these schemes are actually equivalent, though they may use different sets of parameters.

To present our analysis framework, we consider the following co-coercive equation:

\text{Find}~y^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}~\text{such that:}~Gy^{\star}=0,

(6)

where
G:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
is a single-valued and
\frac{1}{L}
-co-coercive operator.
We denote by
\mathrm{zer}(G):=G^{-1}(0)=\left\{y^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:Gy^{\star}=0\right\}
the solution set of (
6
), and assume that
\mathrm{zer}(G)
is nonempty.
Equation (
6
) is a special case,
Case 3
, of (
1
).
However, we study it separately to provide a generic framework for developing new variants to solve the remaining cases of (
1
).

3.1
The Halpern Fixed-Point Scheme and Its Convergence

The Halpern fixed-point scheme for solving (
6
) can be written as follows (see
[
13
,
16
,
22
]
):

y_{k+1}:=\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\eta_{k}Gy_{k},

(7)

where
\beta_{k}\in(0,1)
and
\eta_{k}>0
are appropriately chosen.

The convergence rate of (
7
) has been established in
[
13
,
22
]
using different tools.
While
[
22
]
provides a direct proof and uses a performance estimation problem approach to establish convergence of (
7
),
[
13
]
exploits a Lyapunov technique to analyze convergence rate. Let us summarize the result in
[
13
]
in our context.

The standard Lyapunov function to study (
7
) is

\mathcal{L}_{k}:=\frac{p_{k}}{L}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}+q_{k}\langle Gy_{k},y_{k}-y_{0}\rangle,

(8)

where
p_{k}:=q_{0}k(k+1)
and
q_{k}:=q_{0}(k+1)
(for some
q_{0}>0
) are given parameters.
The following theorem states the convergence of (
7
).

Theorem 3.1

(
[
13
,
22
]
)
.

Assume that
G
in (
6
) is
\frac{1}{L}
-co-coercive with
L\in(0,+\infty)
, and
y^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(G)
.
Let
\{y_{k}\}
be generated by (
7
) using
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
and
\eta_{k}:=\frac{2(1-\beta_{k})}{L}=\frac{2(k+1)}{(k+2)L}
.
Then

\|Gy_{k}\|\leq\frac{L\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|}{k+1}.

(9)

If we choose
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
and
\eta_{k}:=\frac{(1-\beta_{k})}{L}
, then we have
\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4L^{2}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}}{(k+1)(k+3)}
and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)(k+2)\|Gy_{k+1}-Gy_{k}\|^{2}\leq 2L^{2}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}
.

The last statement of Theorem
3.1
was not proved in
[
13
,
22
]
, but requires a few elementary justification, and hence we omit it here.
Note that if
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
, then we can rewrite (
7
) into the Halpern fixed-point iteration as in
[
22
]
:

y_{k+1}:=\frac{1}{(k+2)}y_{0}+\left(1-\frac{1}{(k+2)}\right)Ty_{k},\quad\text{where}\quad Ty_{k}:=y_{k}-\frac{2}{L}Gy_{k}.

(10)

Since
G
is
\frac{1}{L}
-co-coercive,
T=\mathbb{I}-\frac{2}{L}G
is nonexpansive, see
[
5
, Proposition 4.11]
.
Therefore, (
7
) is equivalent to the scheme studied in
[
22
]
, and Theorem
3.1
can be obtained from the results in
[
22
]
.
The choice of
\beta_{k}
and
\eta_{k}
are tight and the bound (
9
) is unimprovable since there exists an example that achieves this rate as the lower bound, see, e.g.,
[
22
]
for such an example.

3.2
The Relation Between Halpern’s and Nesterov’s Accelerations

Our next step is to show that the Halpern fixed-point iteration (
7
) can be transformed into a Nesterov’s accelerated interpretation and vice versa.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}
and
\{y_{k}\}
be generated by the following scheme starting from
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
x_{0}=x_{-1}=y_{-1}:=y_{0}
and
\beta_{-1}=\eta_{-1}=0
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&y_{k}-\gamma_{k}Gy_{k},\\
y_{k+1}&:=&x_{k+1}+\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\nu_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k+1})+\kappa_{k}(y_{k-1}-x_{k}),\end{array}\right.

(11)

where
\theta_{k}:=\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}
,
\nu_{k}:=\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}+1-\beta_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\gamma_{k}}
, and
\kappa_{k}:=\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}\left(\frac{\eta_{k-1}}{\gamma_{k-1}}-1+\beta_{k-1}\right)
.
Then, the sequence
\{y_{k}\}
is identical to the one generated by (
7
) for solving (
6
).

In particular, if we choose
\gamma_{k}:=\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}
, then
\nu_{k}=\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}
,
\kappa_{k}=0
, and (
11
) reduces to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&y_{k}-\gamma_{k}Gy_{k},\\
y_{k+1}&:=&x_{k+1}+\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\nu_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k+1}).\end{array}\right.

(12)

Before proving Theorem
3.2
, let us make the following remarks.
Both (
11
) and (
12
) can be viewed as Nesterov’s accelerated variants with correction terms.
While (
11
) has two correction terms
\nu_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k+1})
and
\kappa_{k}(y_{k-1}-x_{k})
, (
12
) has only one term
\nu_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k+1})
.
In fact, (
12
) covers the proximal-point scheme in
[
24
]
as a special case.
As discussed in
[
2
]
, (
12
) can be viewed as Ravine’s method if convergence is given in
y_{k}
instead of
x_{k}
.

Proof.

[
\eqref{eq:NAG_scheme00_2corr}\Rightarrow\eqref{eq:HP_scheme00}
]

Substituting
\theta_{k}
,
\nu_{k}
, and
\kappa_{k}
into (
11
), and simplifying the result, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}y_{k+1}&=&\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}-\beta_{k}+1\right)x_{k+1}-\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}x_{k}+\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}+1-\beta_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\gamma_{k}}\right)(y_{k}-x_{k+1})\\
&&+{~}\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}\left(\frac{\eta_{k-1}}{\gamma_{k-1}}-1+\beta_{k-1}\right)(y_{k-1}-x_{k}).\end{array}

Now, using the first line of (
11
) into this expression, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}y_{k+1}&=&\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}-\beta_{k}+1\right)(y_{k}-\gamma_{k}Gy_{k})-\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}(y_{k-1}-\gamma_{k-1}Gy_{k-1})\\
&&+{~}\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}+1-\beta_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\gamma_{k}}\right)\gamma_{k}Gy_{k}+\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}\left(\frac{\eta_{k-1}}{\gamma_{k-1}}-1+\beta_{k-1}\right)\gamma_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}.\end{array}

Rearranging this expression, we arrive at

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}y_{k+1}-\left(\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}-1\right)y_{k}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}}Gy_{k}&=&\frac{1}{\beta_{k-1}}y_{k}-\left(\frac{1}{\beta_{k-1}}-1\right)y_{k-1}-\frac{\eta_{k-1}}{\beta_{k-1}}Gy_{k-1}.\end{array}

By induction, and noticing that
y_{-1}=y_{0}
, and
\eta_{-1}=0
, this expression leads to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}y_{k+1}-\left(\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}-1\right)y_{k}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{\beta_{k}}Gy_{k}&=&y_{0}.\end{array}

This is indeed equivalent to (
7
).

[(
7
)
\Rightarrow
(
11
)]

Firstly, shifting the index from
k
to
k-1
in (
7
), we have
y_{k}=\beta_{k-1}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k-1})y_{k-1}-\eta_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}
.
Here, we assume that
y_{-1}=y_{0}
.
Multiplying this expression by
\beta_{k}
and (
7
) by
-\beta_{k-1}
and adding the results, we obtain

\beta_{k-1}y_{k+1}-\beta_{k}y_{k}=\beta_{k-1}(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})y_{k-1}-\beta_{k-1}\eta_{k}Gy_{k}+\beta_{k}\eta_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}.

This expression leads to

y_{k+1}=\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}+1-\beta_{k}\right)y_{k}-\eta_{k}Gy_{k}-\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}y_{k-1}+\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k-1}}{\beta_{k-1}}Gy_{k-1}.

(13)

Next, let us introduce
x_{k+1}:=y_{k}-\gamma_{k}Gy_{k}
for some
\gamma_{k}>0
.
Then, we have
Gy_{k}=\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}(y_{k}-x_{k+1})
.
Substituting this relation into (
13
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}y_{k+1}&=&\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}+1-\beta_{k}\right)y_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\gamma_{k}}(y_{k}-x_{k+1})-\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}y_{k-1}+\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k-1}}{\beta_{k-1}\gamma_{k-1}}(y_{k-1}-x_{k})\\
&=&x_{k+1}+\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}+1-\beta_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\gamma_{k}}\right)(y_{k}-x_{k+1})\\
&&+{~}\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}\left(\frac{\eta_{k-1}}{\gamma_{k-1}}-1+\beta_{k-1}\right)(y_{k-1}-x_{k}).\end{array}

If we define
\theta_{k}:=\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}
,
\nu_{k}:=\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}+1-\beta_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\gamma_{k}}
and
\kappa_{k}:=\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}\left(\frac{\eta_{k-1}}{\gamma_{k-1}}-1+\beta_{k-1}\right)
, then this expression can be rewritten as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}y_{k+1}&=&x_{k+1}+\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\nu_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k+1})+\kappa_{k}(y_{k-1}-x_{k}).\end{array}

Combining this line and
x_{k+1}=y_{k}-\gamma_{k}Gy_{k}
, we get (

).

Finally, if we choose
\gamma_{k}:=\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}
, then it is obvious that
\kappa_{k}=0
, and
\nu_{k}=\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}
.
Hence, (

) reduces to (

).
∎

In particular, if we choose
\gamma_{k}:=\frac{1}{L}
,
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
, and
\eta_{k}:=\frac{(1-\beta_{k})}{L}=\frac{(k+1)}{L(k+2)}

) reduces to the following one:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&y_{k}-\frac{1}{L}Gy_{k},\\
y_{k+1}&:=&x_{k+1}+\frac{k}{k+2}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\frac{k+1}{k+2}(y_{k}-x_{k+1}).\end{array}\right.

(14)

Alternatively, if we choose
\gamma_{k}:=\frac{1}{L}
,
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
, and
\eta_{k}:=\frac{2(1-\beta_{k})}{L}
, then (
11
) reduces to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&y_{k}-\frac{1}{L}Gy_{k},\\
y_{k+1}&:=&x_{k+1}+\frac{k}{k+2}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\frac{k}{k+2}(y_{k-1}-x_{k}).\end{array}\right.

(15)

The convergence on
\|Gy_{k}\|
of both (

) and (

) is guaranteed by Theorem

.
The scheme (
15
) covers
[

]
as a special case when

, the resolvent of a maximally monotone operator
\lambda A
.
We state this result in the following corollary.
However, it is not clear how to derive convergence of
\|Gx_{k}\|
as well as
o\left(1/k^{2}\right)
-rates as in Theorem
3.1
.
We summarize this result in the following lemma.

Corollary 3.1
.

Assume that
G
in (
6
) is
\frac{1}{L}
-co-coercive and
\mathrm{zer}(G)\neq\emptyset
.
Let
\left\{(x_{k},y_{k})\right\}
be generated by (
11
) using
\gamma_{k}:=\frac{1}{L}
,
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
, and
\eta_{k}:=\frac{(1-\beta_{k})}{L}
.
Then, we obtain
\theta_{k}:=\frac{k}{k+2}
,
\nu_{k}:=\frac{k+1}{k+2}
, and
\kappa_{k}:=0
.
Moreover, (
11
) reduces to (
12
)
(
or equivalently (
14
)
)
, and the following guarantee holds:

\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4L^{2}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}}{(k+1)(k+3)},\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)(k+2)\|Gy_{k+1}-Gy_{k}\|^{2}\leq 2L^{2}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}.

(16)

If we use
\gamma_{k}:=\frac{1}{L}
,
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
, and
\eta_{k}:=\frac{2(1-\beta_{k})}{L}
, then we obtain
\theta_{k}:=\frac{k}{k+2}
,
\nu_{k}:=0
, and
\kappa_{k}:=\frac{k}{k+2}
, and (

) reduces to (

).

\|Gy_{k}\|\leq\frac{L\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|}{(k+1)}
.

The constant factor in the bound (

) is slightly worse than the one in
\|Gy_{k}\|\leq\frac{L\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|}{(k+1)}
.
In fact, the latter one is exactly optimal since there exists an example showing it matches the lower bound complexity, see, e.g.
[

,
22
]
.

3.3
Convergence Analysis of Nesterov’s Accelerated Scheme (
12
)

In this subsection, we provide a direct convergence analysis of (
12
) without using Theorem
3.1
.
For simplicity, we will analyze the convergence of (
12
) with only one correction term.
However, our analysis can be easily extended to (
11
) when
\kappa_{k}\neq 0
with some simple modifications.

Our analysis relies on the following Lyapunov function:

\mathcal{V}_{k}:=a_{k}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle+\|x_{k}+t_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k})-y^{\star}\|^{2}+\mu\|x_{k}-y^{\star}\|^{2},

(17)

where
a_{k}
,
b_{k}
, and
t_{k}>0
are given parameters, which will be determined later, and
\mu\geq 0
is an optimal parameter.
This Lyapunov is slightly different from
\mathcal{L}_{k}
defined by (
8
), but it is closely related to standard Nesterov’s potential function (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
).
To see the connection between
\mathcal{V}_{k}
and
\mathcal{L}_{k}
, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{k}
be defined by (
8
) and
\mathcal{V}_{k}
be defined by (
17
).
Assume that
a_{k+1}:=\frac{4p_{k}^{2}}{Lq_{k}^{2}}+\frac{4p_{k}\eta_{k}}{Lq_{k}(1-\beta_{k})}
,
b_{k+1}:=\frac{4p_{k}}{Lq_{k}\beta_{k}}
,
t_{k+1}:=\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}
, and
\gamma_{k}:=\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}
.
Then, we have

\mathcal{V}_{k+1}=\frac{4p_{k}}{Lq_{k}^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{k}+\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}+\mu\|x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\|^{2}.

(18)

Before proving this lemma, we make the following remarks.

•

For
p_{k}=q_{0}k(k+1)
and
q_{k}=q_{0}(k+1)
, we have
\frac{4p_{k}}{Lq_{k}^{2}}=\frac{4k}{Lq_{0}(k+1)}\approx\frac{4}{Lq_{0}}
.
Hence, we have
\mathcal{V}_{k+1}=\frac{4k}{Lq_{0}(k+1)}\mathcal{L}_{k}+\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}+\mu\|x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\|^{2}
.

•

If we choose
p_{k}=cq_{k}^{2}
for some
c>0
, then
\mathcal{V}_{k+1}=\frac{4c}{L}\mathcal{L}_{k}+\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}+\mu\|x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\|^{2}
.
Clearly, if
\mu=0
, then
\mathcal{V}_{k+1}=\frac{4c}{L}\mathcal{L}_{k}+\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}
.

•

The term
\mu\|x_{k}-y^{\star}\|^{2}
allows us to get the tail
\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}
in (
27
) of Lemma
3.3
, which is a key to prove convergence in Theorem
3.3
, especially
o\left(1/k^{2}\right)
-convergence rates.
It remains unclear to us how to prove such a convergence rate without the term
\mu\|x_{k}-y^{\star}\|^{2}
.

Proof.

From
\mathcal{L}_{k}=\frac{p_{k}}{L}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}+q_{k}\langle Gy_{k},y_{k}-y_{0}\rangle
in (

), we can write it as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{L}_{k}&=&\frac{p_{k}}{L}\|Gy_{k}-\frac{q_{k}}{2p_{k}}(y_{0}-y^{\star})\|^{2}+q_{k}\langle Gy_{k},y_{k}-y^{\star}\rangle-\frac{q_{k}^{2}}{4p_{k}}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}.\end{array}

From (

.
Using
x_{k+1}=y_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}Gy_{k}
, we have
y_{k}=x_{k+1}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}Gy_{k}
.
Combining these lines, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}Gy_{k}-\frac{Lq_{k}}{2p_{k}}(y_{0}-y^{\star})=Gy_{k}-\frac{Lq_{k}}{2p_{k}}\big[x_{k+1}+\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}(y_{k+1}-x_{k+1})-y^{\star}\big].\end{array}

Then, taking again

&=&\frac{Lq_{k}^{2}}{4p_{k}}\left[\left(\frac{4p_{k}^{2}}{L^{2}q_{k}^{2}}+\frac{4p_{k}\eta_{k}}{Lq_{k}(1-\beta_{k})}\right)\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{4p_{k}}{Lq_{k}\beta_{k}}\langle Gy_{k},x_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\rangle+\|z_{k+1}-y^{\star}\|^{2}-\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}\right],\end{array}

where we have used
x_{k+1}-z_{k+1}=\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}(x_{k+1}-y_{k+1})
in the last line.
This expression together with (

) imply (

).
∎

Now, we prove the following key lemma for our convergence analysis.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\{(x_{k},y_{k})\}
be generated by (

) using
\gamma_{k}:=\gamma>0
, and
\mathcal{V}_{k}
be defined by (

).
Then, if
b_{k+1}\theta_{k}+2\gamma t_{k}(t_{k}-1)-2\gamma\nu_{k}\theta_{k}t_{k+1}^{2}\geq 0

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}&\geq&\left(\gamma b_{k+1}\nu_{k}+\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}-\gamma^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}\nu_{k}^{2}-a_{k+1}-\frac{\gamma^{2}b_{k}^{2}}{4a_{k}}\right)\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\\

Proof.

Our goal is to lower bound the difference
\mathcal{V}_{k}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}

Firstly, from (

), we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}&=&a_{k}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle-b_{k+1}\langle Gy_{k},x_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\rangle\\

&&-{~}\|x_{k+1}-y^{\star}+t_{k+1}(y_{k+1}-x_{k+1})\|^{2}.\end{array}

(20)

Next, since
y_{k}=x_{k+1}+\gamma Gy_{k}
from (

), it is easy to show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle&=&-\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle-\gamma\langle Gy_{k-1},Gy_{k}\rangle.\end{array}

Alternatively, from (

), we have
x_{k+1}-y_{k+1}=-\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})-\gamma\nu_{k}Gy_{k}
.
Therefore, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle Gy_{k},x_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\rangle&=&-\theta_{k}\langle Gy_{k},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle-\gamma\nu_{k}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(22)

Then, using again
y_{k}=x_{k+1}+\gamma Gy_{k}
from (
12
), we can derive

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|x_{k}-y^{\star}+t_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k})\|^{2}&=&\|x_{k+1}-y^{\star}+(t_{k}-1)(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\gamma t_{k}Gy_{k}\|^{2}\\
&=&\|x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\|^{2}+(t_{k}-1)^{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}+\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2(t_{k}-1)\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\rangle+2\gamma t_{k}\langle Gy_{k},x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2\gamma t_{k}(t_{k}-1)\langle Gy_{k},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle.\end{array}

(23)

Similarly, using
y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}=\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\gamma\nu_{k}Gy_{k}
, we can show that

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|x_{k+1}-y^{\star}+t_{k+1}(y_{k+1}-x_{k+1})\|^{2}&=&\|x_{k+1}-y^{\star}+t_{k+1}\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\gamma t_{k+1}\nu_{k}Gy_{k}\|^{2}\\
&=&\|x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\|^{2}+t_{k+1}^{2}\theta_{k}^{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}+\gamma^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}\nu_{k}^{2}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2\gamma\nu_{k}\theta_{k}t_{k+1}^{2}\langle Gy_{k},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2\gamma t_{k+1}\nu_{k}\langle Gy_{k},x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2t_{k+1}\theta_{k}\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\rangle.\end{array}\hskip-34.44434pt

(24)

Substituting (
21
), (
22
), (
23
), and (
24
) into (
20
), and using the identity
\mu\|x_{k}-y^{\star}\|^{2}-\mu\|x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\|^{2}=\mu\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}-2\mu\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\rangle
, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}&=&a_{k}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+\left[\gamma b_{k+1}\nu_{k}+\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}-\gamma^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}\nu_{k}^{2}-a_{k+1}\right]\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\gamma b_{k}\langle Gy_{k-1},Gy_{k}\rangle-b_{k}\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle\\
&&+{~}\left[b_{k+1}\theta_{k}+2\gamma t_{k}(t_{k}-1)-2\gamma\nu_{k}\theta_{k}t_{k+1}^{2}\right]\langle Gy_{k},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle\\
&&+{~}\left[\mu+(t_{k}-1)^{2}-t_{k+1}^{2}\theta_{k}^{2}\right]\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2\left(t_{k}-t_{k+1}\theta_{k}-1-\mu\right)\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2\gamma\big(t_{k}-t_{k+1}\nu_{k}\big)\langle Gy_{k},x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\rangle.\end{array}

(25)

By the
\frac{1}{L}
-co-coerciveness of
G
and
x_{k+1}=y_{k}-\gamma Gy_{k}
from (
12
), we can derive

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle&=&\langle Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1},y_{k}-y_{k-1}\rangle-\gamma\|Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&\left(\frac{1}{L}-\gamma\right)\|Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

This inequality implies that
\langle Gy_{k},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle\geq\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle+\left(\frac{1}{L}-\gamma\right)\|Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}
.
Finally, if we assume that
b_{k+1}\theta_{k}+2\gamma t_{k}(t_{k}-1)-2\gamma\nu_{k}\theta_{k}t_{k+1}^{2}\geq 0
, then by substituting the last inequality into (
25
) and noting that
a_{k}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}-\gamma b_{k}\langle Gy_{k},Gy_{k-1}\rangle=a_{k}\|Gy_{k-1}-\frac{\gamma b_{k}}{2a_{k}}Gy_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma^{2}b_{k}^{2}}{4a_{k}}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}
, we obtain (
19
).
∎

Our next lemma is to provide a particular choice of parameters such that
\mathcal{V}_{k}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}\geq 0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
0<\gamma\leq\frac{1}{L}
,
\mu\geq 0
, and
\omega\geq 1
be given.
Let
\{(x_{k},y_{k})\}
be generated by (
12
) and
\mathcal{V}_{k}
be defined by (
17
).
Assume that
t_{k}
,
\theta_{k}
,
\nu_{k}
,
a_{k}
, and
b_{k}
in (
12
) and (
17
) are chosen as follows:

\begin{array}[]{ll}&t_{k}:=\tfrac{k+2\omega+1}{\omega},\quad\theta_{k}:=\tfrac{t_{k}-1-\mu}{t_{k+1}},\quad\nu_{k}:=1-\tfrac{1}{t_{k+1}},\\
&b_{k}:=2\gamma t_{k}(t_{k}-1),\quad\text{and}\quad a_{k}:=\gamma^{2}t_{k}(t_{k}-1).\end{array}

(26)

Then, it holds that

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}&\geq&\mu(2t_{k}-1-\mu)\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}+\gamma^{2}t_{k}(t_{k}-1)\|Gy_{k}-\nu_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2\gamma\left(\frac{1}{L}-\gamma\right)t_{k}(t_{k}-1)\|Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma(\omega-1)}{L\omega}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\geq 0.\end{array}\hskip-17.22217pt

(27)

Moreover, we have
\mathcal{V}_{k}\geq\mu\|x_{k}-y^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}}{t_{k}}\left(\frac{1}{L}-\gamma\right)\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}\geq 0
.
Consequently, we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu(2t_{k}-1-\mu)\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}&\leq&\mathcal{V}_{0},\\
\frac{\gamma(\omega-1)}{L\omega}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}&\leq&\mathcal{V}_{0},\\
\frac{2\gamma(1-L\gamma)}{L}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}(t_{k}-1)\|Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}&\leq&\mathcal{V}_{0},\\
\gamma^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}(t_{k}-1)\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}-\theta_{k-1}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\|^{2}&\leq&\mathcal{V}_{0}.\end{array}\right.

(28)

Proof.

Let us show that
t_{k}
,
\theta_{k}
,
\nu_{k}
, and
b_{k}
chosen by (
26
) satisfy

\begin{array}[]{lcl}t_{k}-t_{k+1}\theta_{k}-1-\mu=0\quad\text{and}\quad b_{k+1}\theta_{k}+2\gamma t_{k}(t_{k}-1)-2\gamma\nu_{k}\theta_{k}t_{k+1}^{2}-b_{k}=0.\end{array}

(29)

First, since
\theta_{k}=\frac{t_{k}-1-\mu}{t_{k+1}}
, the first condition of (
29
) automatically holds.
Next, using
b_{k}=2\gamma t_{k}(t_{k}-\mu)
and
\nu_{k}=1-\frac{1}{t_{k+1}}
, one can easily verify the second condition of (
29
).

Now, using (
26
), we can directly compute the following coefficients of (
19
):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}t_{k}^{2}-2t_{k}+1+\mu-t_{k+1}^{2}\theta_{k}^{2}&=&\mu(2t_{k}-1-\mu),\\
t_{k}-t_{k+1}\nu_{k}&=&\frac{\omega-1}{\omega},\\
b_{k+1}\theta_{k}+2\gamma t_{k}(t_{k}-1)-2\gamma\nu_{k}\theta_{k}t_{k+1}^{2}&=&2\gamma t_{k}(t_{k}-1),\\
\gamma b_{k+1}\nu_{k}+\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}-\gamma^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}\nu_{k}^{2}-a_{k+1}-\frac{\gamma^{2}b_{k}^{2}}{4a_{k}}&=&\frac{\gamma^{2}(\omega-1)}{\omega}.\end{array}\right.

Substituting these coefficients and the two conditions (
29
) into (
19
), we get (
27
).

Finally, since
Gy^{\star}=0
, using the
\frac{1}{L}
-co-coerciveness of
G
, we get
\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k}-y^{\star}\rangle=\langle Gy_{k-1}-Gy^{\star},y_{k-1}-y^{\star}-\gamma(Gy_{k-1}-Gy^{\star})\rangle\geq\left(\frac{1}{L}-\gamma\right)\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}
.
Therefore, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}b_{k}\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle&=&-\frac{b_{k}}{t_{k}}\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k}-y^{\star}+t_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k})\rangle+\frac{b_{k}}{t_{k}}\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k}-y^{\star}\rangle\\
&\geq&-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{4t_{k}^{2}}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}-\|x_{k}-y^{\star}+t_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}}{t_{k}}\left(\frac{1}{L}-\gamma\right)\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Substituting this bound into the definition (
17
) of
\mathcal{V}_{k}
and noticing that
a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{4t_{k}^{2}}=\gamma^{2}(t_{k}-1)
, we get
\mathcal{V}_{k}\geq\left(a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{4t_{k}^{2}}\right)\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}}{t_{k}}\left(\frac{1}{L}-\gamma\right)\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+\mu\|x_{k}-y^{\star}\|^{2}=\frac{\gamma(2-\gamma L)(t_{k}-1)}{L}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+\mu\|x_{k}-y^{\star}\|^{2}
, which proves the nonnegativity of
\mathcal{V}_{k}
.
Clearly, (
28
) is a direct consequence of (
27
) and the fact that
x_{k+1}-x_{k}-\theta_{k-1}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})=\gamma(Gy_{k}-\nu_{k-1}Gy_{k-1})
.
∎

The following theorem proves convergence of Nesterov’s accelerated scheme (
12
), but using a different set of parameters compared to Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that
G
in (
6
) is
\frac{1}{L}
-co-coercive and
\mathrm{zer}(G)\neq\emptyset
.
Let
\left\{(x_{k},y_{k})\right\}
be generated by (
12
) to solve (
6
) using
\gamma_{k}:=\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L}\right)
,
\theta_{k}:=\frac{k+1}{k+2\omega+2}
, and
\nu_{k}:=\frac{k+\omega+2}{k+2\omega+2}\in(0,1)
for a given constant
\omega>2
.
Then, the following estimates hold:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+\omega+1)\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right),\\
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+2\omega+1)\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right),\\
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+\omega+1)\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right),\\
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+\omega+1)\|Gx_{k}\|^{2}<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\|Gx_{k}\|^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right),\\
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+\omega)\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right).\end{array}\right.

(30)

Consequently, both
\left\{x_{k}\right\}
and
\left\{y_{k}\right\}
converge to
y^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(G)
.

Before proving Theorem
3.3
, we make the following remarks.
First, if we choose
\gamma=\frac{1}{L}
, then we only obtain the first result of (
30
) and
\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)
.
This rate is slightly better than the
\mathcal{O}\left(1/k^{2}\right)
rate in
[
18
]
when
k
is sufficiently large.
Second, if
\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L}\right)
, then we can prove
o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)
convergence rates of
\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}
,
\|Gx_{k}\|^{2}
,
\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}
, and
\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}
.
Note that we can simply choose
\omega=3
to further simplify the results.
In this case, we obtain
\theta_{k}=\frac{k+1}{k+8}
, which is different from
\theta_{k}=\frac{k}{k+2}
in (
14
) obtained by Theorem
3.1
.
We emphasize that
o\left(\cdot\right)
convergence rates have recently studied in a number of works such as
[
1
,
3
,
24
,
25
]
.

Proof.

The first claim in the first line of (
30
) is directly from (
28
) by noticing that
t_{k}-1=\frac{k+\omega+1}{\omega}
.
Now, we prove the second line of (
30
).
We first choose
\mu=1
in (
17
).
Then, from (
12
), we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}&=&\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\gamma\nu_{k}Gy_{k}=\nu_{k}(x_{k+1}+\gamma Gy_{k}-x_{k})+(\theta_{k}-\nu_{k})(x_{k+1}-x_{k})\\
&=&\nu_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k})+(\theta_{k}-\nu_{k})(x_{k+1}-x_{k}).\end{array}

Hence, by Young’s inequality,
\nu_{k}\in(0,1)
, and this expression, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}t_{k+1}^{2}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|&=&t_{k+1}^{2}\|\nu_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k})+(\theta_{k}-\nu_{k})(x_{k+1}-x_{k})\|^{2}\\
&\leq&t_{k+1}^{2}\nu_{k}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{t_{k+1}^{2}(\theta_{k}-\nu_{k})^{2}}{1-\nu_{k}}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}\\
\end{array}

Notice from (
26
) that
t_{k+1}^{2}\nu_{k}=t_{k}^{2}-\frac{\omega(\omega-2)t_{k}+\omega-1}{\omega^{2}}

and
\frac{t_{k+1}^{2}(\theta_{k}-\nu_{k})^{2}}{1-\nu_{k}}=\frac{(\omega-1)^{2}(k+2\omega+2)}{\omega^{3}}
.
Utilizing these expressions into the last inequality, we obtain

\hskip-4.30554pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{\omega(\omega-2)t_{k}+\omega-1}{\omega^{2}}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}\leq t_{k}^{2}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}-t_{k+1}^{2}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}+\frac{(\omega-1)^{2}t_{k+1}}{\omega^{2}}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-12.91663pt

(31)

Summing up this estimate from
k=0
to
k=K
, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{\omega(\omega-2)t_{k}+\omega-1}{\omega^{2}}\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}\leq t_{0}^{2}\|y_{0}-x_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{(\omega-1)^{2}}{\omega^{3}}\sum_{k=0}^{K}(k+2\omega+2)\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Using the first line of (
30
) into this inequality and
\omega>2
, we obtain
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big[(\omega-2)(k+2\omega+1)+\omega-1\big]\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}<+\infty
, which implies the first claim in the second line of (
30
).
Moreover, (
31
) also shows that
\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}^{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}
exists.
Combining this fact and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+2\omega+1)\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}<+\infty
, we obtain
\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}^{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}=0
, which shows that
\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}=o\left(1/k^{2}\right)
.

To prove the third line of (
30
), we note that
\gamma\nu_{k}Gy_{k}=(y_{k+1}-x_{k+1})-\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k+1})
.
Hence,
\gamma^{2}\nu_{k}^{2}(t_{k}-1)\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\leq 2(t_{k}-1)\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}+2\theta_{k}^{2}(t_{k}-1)\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}
.
Exploiting the finite sum of the last two terms from (
30
), we obtain
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+\omega+1)\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}<+\infty
.

To prove the second part in the first line of (
30
), utilizing (
12
), we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[1]}&:=&\theta_{k-1}^{2}t_{k}^{2}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}-\theta_{k}^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}\\
&=&t_{k}^{2}\|y_{k}-x_{k}-\gamma\nu_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}-t_{k}^{2}\|y_{k}-x_{k}-\gamma Gy_{k}\|^{2}+(t_{k}^{2}-\theta_{k}^{2}t_{k+1}^{2})\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}\\
&=&\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}\|Gy_{k}-\nu_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+2\gamma t_{k}^{2}\langle Gy_{k}-\nu_{k-1}Gy_{k-1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle\\
&&+{~}(t_{k}^{2}-\theta_{k}^{2}t_{k+1}^{2})\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}\\
&=&\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}\|Gy_{k}-\nu_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+2\gamma t_{k}^{2}\langle Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2\gamma t_{k}^{2}(1-\nu_{k})\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle+(t_{k}^{2}-\theta_{k}^{2}t_{k+1}^{2})\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&\gamma^{2}t_{k}^{2}\|Gy_{k}-\nu_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+2\gamma t_{k}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{L}-\gamma\right)\|Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2\gamma t_{k}^{2}(1-\nu_{k})\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle+(t_{k}^{2}-\theta_{k}^{2}t_{k+1}^{2})\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Employing the update rule (
26
) and Young’s inequality, this inequality leads to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\theta_{k-1}^{2}t_{k}^{2}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}-\theta_{k}^{2}t_{k+1}^{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}&\geq&2\gamma t_{k}^{2}(1-\nu_{k})\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle\\
&\geq&-\frac{\gamma t_{k}^{2}}{t_{k+1}}\left[\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}\right].\end{array}

Following the same argument as in the proof of
\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}
, we can show that
\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}^{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|=0
, and hence,
\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}=o\left(1/k^{2}\right)
, which proves the second part in the first line of (
30
).
Since
\gamma^{2}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}=\|x_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}\leq 2\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}+2\|y_{k}-x_{k}\|^{2}
, we also obtain
\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}=o\left(1/k^{2}\right)
.

Since
\|Gx_{k}\|^{2}\leq 2\|Gx_{k}-Gy_{k}\|+2\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\leq 2L^{2}\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}+2\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}
, we obtain the fourth line of (
30
) from the previous lines.

Now, to prove the last line of (
30
).
Since
y_{k+1}-y_{k}=x_{k+1}-x_{k}+\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})-\theta_{k-1}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})-\gamma\left(\nu_{k}Gy_{k}-\nu_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}\right]
, we can bound

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}&\leq&4(1+\theta_{k})^{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}+4\theta_{k-1}^{2}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}4\gamma^{2}\nu_{k}^{2}\|Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+4\gamma(\nu_{k}-\nu_{k-1})^{2}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&16\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}+4\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}+4\gamma^{2}\|Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+4\gamma^{2}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Here, we have used the facts that
\theta_{k},\theta_{k-1},\nu_{k},\nu_{k-1}\in(0,1)
and
(\nu_{k}-\nu_{k-1})^{2}<1
.
Combining this estimate and the first and second lines of (
30
), we obtain the third line of (
30
).

Finally, to prove the convergence of
\{x_{k}\}
and
\{y_{k}\}
, we note that
\|x_{k}-y^{\star}\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{V}_{k}\leq\mathcal{V}_{0}
.
Hence
\{x_{k}\}
is bounded, which has a limit point.
Moreover, since
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|=0
,
\{x_{k}\}
is convergent to
y^{\star}
.
We have
\|Gx_{k}\|\leq\|Gy_{k}\|+\|Gy_{k}-Gx_{k}\|\leq\|Gy_{k}\|+L\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.
Since
G
is
\frac{1}{L}
-co-cocercive, it is
L
-Lipschitz continuous.
Passing the limit through
G
and using the continuity of
G
, we obtain
Gy^{\star}=0
.
Since
\|x_{k}-y_{k}\|\to\infty
, we also have
\lim_{k\to\infty}y^{k}=y^{\star}
.
∎

From the result of Theorem
3.3
, we can derive the convergence of the Halpern fixed-point iteration (
7
), but under different choice of parameters.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\{y_{k}\}
be generated by the Halpern fixed-point iteration (
7
) using
\beta_{k}:=\frac{\omega+1}{k+2\omega+2}
and
\eta_{k}:=\gamma(1-\beta_{k})
for a fixed
\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L}\right)
and
\omega>2
.
Then, the following statements hold:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+\omega+1)\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right),\\
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+\omega)\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\|^{2}=o\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right).\end{array}\right.

(32)

Consequently,
\left\{y_{k}\right\}
converges to
y^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(G)
.

Proof.

As proved in Theorem
3.2
, (
7
) is equivalent to (
12
) provided that
\theta_{k}=\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}
,
\nu_{k}=\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}
, and
\gamma_{k}:=\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}
.
Using the choice of
\beta_{k}
,
\nu_{k}
, and
\gamma_{k}
in Theorem
3.3
, we can show that
\theta_{k}=\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}=\frac{k+1}{k+2\omega+2}
and
\nu_{k}=\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}=\frac{k+\omega+2}{k+2\omega+2}
.
These relations lead to
\beta_{k}=\frac{\omega+1}{k+2\omega+2}
.
Moreover, since
\gamma_{k}=\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}=\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L}\right)
, we have
\eta_{k}=\gamma(1-\beta_{k})
.
Consequently, (
32
) follows from (
30
).
∎

If we set
\omega=0
, then we obtain
\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{k+2}
as in Theorem
3.1
.
In this case, we have to set
\mu=0
in
\mathcal{V}_{k}
from (
17
), and hence only obtain
\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}\left(1/k^{2}\right)
convergence rate.
Note that other choices of parameters in Theorem
3.3
are possible, e.g., by changing
\mu
and
\omega
.
Here, we have not tried to optimize the choice of these parameters.
As shown in
[
5
, Proposition 4.11]
that
T
is a non-expansive mapping if and only if
G:=\mathbb{I}-T
is
\frac{1}{2}
-co-coercive.
Therefore, we can obtain new convergence results on the residual norm
\|y_{k}-Ty_{k}\|
from Corollary
3.2
for a Halpern fixed-point iteration scheme to approximate a fixed-point
y^{\star}
of
T
.

4
Application to Monotone Inclusions

In this section, we present some applications of the results in Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
to proximal-point, forward-backward splitting, and three-operator splitting methods.
The main idea is to transform (
1
) and its special cases to a co-coercive equation of the form (
6
), and then apply the results from Section
3
to this equation.

4.1
Application to Proximal-Point Method

We consider
Case 2
where (
1
) reduces to finding
y^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that
0\in Ay^{\star}
.
Let
J_{\lambda A}y:=(\mathbb{I}+\lambda A)y
be the resolvent of
\lambda A
for any
\lambda>0
and
G_{\lambda A}y=\frac{1}{\lambda}(\mathbb{I}-J_{\lambda A})y=\frac{1}{\lambda}(y-J_{\lambda A}y)
be the Yosida approximation of
A
with index
\lambda>0
.
Then, it is well-known that
G_{\lambda A}
is
\lambda
-co-coercive
[
5
, Corollary 23.11]
.
Moreover,
y^{\star}
solves
0\in Ay^{\star}
if and only if
G_{\lambda A}y^{\star}=0
.
Hence, solving
0\in Ay^{\star}
is equivalent to solving the co-coercive equation
G_{\lambda A}y^{\star}=0
with
\lambda
-co-coercive
G_{\lambda A}
.

In this case, the Halpern-type fixed-point scheme (
7
) applying to
G_{\lambda A}y^{\star}=0
, or equivalently, to solving
0\in Ay^{\star}
, can be written as

y_{k+1}:=\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\eta_{k}G_{\lambda A}y_{k}=\beta_{k}y_{0}+\left(1-\beta_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\lambda}\right)y_{k}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{\lambda}J_{\lambda A}y_{k},

(33)

where
\beta_{k}
and
\eta_{k}
can be chosen either in Theorem
3.1
or Corollary
3.2
to guarantee convergence of (
33
).
If
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
and
\eta_{k}:=2\lambda(1-\beta_{k})
as in Theorem
3.1
, then we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}y_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-2(1-\beta_{k})(y_{k}-J_{\lambda A}y_{k})\\
&=&\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})(2J_{\lambda A}y_{k}-y_{k})\\
&=&\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})R_{\lambda A}y_{k},\end{array}

where
R_{\lambda A}:=2J_{\lambda A}-\mathbb{I}
is the reflected resolvent of
\lambda A
.
Moreover, under this choice of parameters, we have the following result from Theorem
3.1
:

\|G_{\lambda A}y_{k}\|\leq\frac{\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|}{\lambda(k+1)}.

If we choose
\beta_{k}:=\frac{\omega+1}{k+2\omega+2}
and
\eta_{k}:=\gamma(1-\beta_{k})
as in Corollary
3.2
, then (
33
) becomes

y_{k+1}:=\frac{\omega+1}{k+2\omega+2}\cdot y_{0}+\frac{k+\omega+1}{k+2\omega+2}\cdot\left[\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\right)y_{k}+\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}J_{\lambda A}y_{k}\right].

This expression can be viewed as a new variant of the Halpern fixed-point iteration applied to the averaged mapping
P_{\rho A}y=(1-\rho)y+\rho J_{\lambda A}y
with
\rho:=\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}
provided that
\gamma\in(0,\lambda]
.
In this case, we obtain a convergence result as in (
32
).

Alternatively, if we apply (
12
) to solve
G_{\lambda A}y^{\star}=0
, then we obtain a Nesterov’s accelerated interpretation of (
33
) as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&y_{k}-\gamma_{k}G_{\lambda A}y_{k}=\left(1-\rho_{k}\right)y_{k}+\rho_{k}J_{\lambda A}y_{k}\quad\text{with}\quad\rho_{k}:=\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\lambda},\\
y_{k+1}&:=&x_{k+1}+\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\nu_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k+1}).\end{array}\right.

(34)

This method was studied in
[
24
]
.
Nevertheless, our analysis in Theorem
3.3
is simpler than that of
[
24
]
when it applies to (
34
).
In particular, if we choose
\gamma_{k}:=\lambda
, then the first line of (
34
) reduces to
x_{k+1}=J_{\lambda A}y_{k}
.
The convergence rate guarantees of (
34
) can be obtained as results of Corollary
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
, respectively.

Finally, if we apply (
11
) to solve
G_{\lambda A}y^{\star}=0
and choose
\gamma_{k}:=\lambda
,
\eta_{k}:=\lambda\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}+1-\beta_{k}\right)
such that
\nu_{k}=0
and
\kappa_{k}=\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-2}}
, then (
11
) reduces to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&J_{\lambda A}y_{k},\\
y_{k+1}&:=&x_{k+1}+\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\kappa_{k}(y_{k-1}-x_{k}).\end{array}\right.

Clearly, if we choose
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
, then
\theta_{k}=\frac{k}{k+2}
and
\kappa_{k}=\frac{k}{k+2}
.
In this case, the last scheme reduces to the accelerated proximal-point algorithm studied in
[
18
]
.
In addition, we have
\eta_{k}=\frac{2\lambda(k+1)}{k+2}
as in Theorem
3.1
.
Hence, the result of Corollary
3.1
is still applicable to this scheme to obtain a convergence rate guarantee
\|G_{\lambda Q}y_{k}\|\leq\frac{\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|}{\lambda(k+1)}
as in
[
18
, Theorem 4.1]
.

4.2
Application to Forward-Backward Splitting Method

Let us consider
Case 3
when (
1
) reduces to finding
y^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that
0\in Ay^{\star}+By^{\star}
.
By Lemma
2.1
,
y^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(A+B)
if and only if
G_{\lambda Q}y^{\star}=0
, where
Q:=A+B
and
G_{\lambda Q}
is defined by (
2
).
Moreover,
G_{\lambda Q}
is
\frac{\lambda(4-\lambda L)}{4}
-co-coercive, provided that
0<\lambda<\frac{4}{L}
.

If we apply (
7
) to solve
G_{\lambda Q}y^{\star}=0
, then its iterate can be written explicitly as

y_{k+1}:=\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\eta_{k}G_{\lambda Q}y_{k}=\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})\big[(1-\rho)y_{k}+\rho J_{\lambda A}(y_{k}-\lambda By_{k})\big],

(35)

where we have set
\rho:=\frac{4-\lambda L}{2}
.
In particular, if we choose
\lambda:=\frac{2}{L}
, then
\rho=1
and (
35
) reduces to
y_{k+1}:=\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})J_{\lambda A}(y_{k}-\lambda By_{k})
, which can be viewed as the Halpern fixed-point iteration applied to approximate a fixed-point of
J_{\lambda A}(y_{k}-\lambda By_{k})
.

Depending on the choice of
\beta_{k}
and
\rho
as in Theorem
3.1
or Corollary
3.2
, we obtain

\|G_{\lambda Q}y_{k}\|\leq\frac{4\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|}{\lambda(4-\lambda L)(k+1)},\quad\text{or}\quad\|G_{\lambda Q}y_{k}\|=o\left(1/k\right),

respectively, provided that
0<\lambda<\frac{4}{L}
.

Now, we consider Nesterov’s accelerated variant of (
35
) by applying (
12
) to
G_{\lambda Q}y^{\star}=0
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&(1-\rho_{k})y_{k}+\rho_{k}J_{\lambda A}(y_{k}-\lambda By_{k}),\\
y_{k+1}&:=&x_{k+1}+\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\nu_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k+1}),\end{array}\right.

(36)

where
\rho_{k}:=\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\lambda}
,
\theta_{k}:=\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}
, and
\nu_{k}:=\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}
.
This scheme is similar to the one studied in
[
25
]
.
Again, the convergence of (
36
) can be guaranteed by either Corollary
3.1
or Theorem
3.3
depending on the choice of
\gamma_{k}
,
\theta_{k}
, and
\nu_{k}
.
However, we omit the details here.

4.3
Application to Three-Operator Splitting Method

Finally, we consider the general case,
Case 4
.
As stated in Lemma
2.2
,
y^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(A+B+C)
if and only if
E_{\lambda Q}y^{\star}=0
, where
Q:=A+B+C
and
E_{\lambda Q}
is defined by (
4
).
Let us apply (
7
) to
E_{\lambda Q}y^{\star}=0
and arrive at the following scheme:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}y_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\eta_{k}E_{\lambda Q}y_{k}\\
&=&\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\lambda}(J_{\lambda B}y_{k}-J_{\lambda A}(2J_{\lambda B}y_{k}-y_{k}-\lambda C\circ J_{\lambda B}y_{k})).\end{array}

Unfolding this scheme by using intermediate variables
z_{k}
and
w_{k}
, we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}z_{k}&:=&J_{\lambda B}y_{k},\\
w_{k}&:=&J_{\lambda A}(2z_{k}-y_{k}-\lambda Cz_{k}),\\
y_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\lambda}(z_{k}-w_{k}).\end{array}\right.

(37)

This is called a Halpern-type three-operator splitting scheme for solving (
1
).
If
C=0
, then it reduces to Halpern-type Douglas-Rachford splitting scheme for solving
Case 2
of (
1
) derived from (
7
).
The latter case was proposed in
[
41
]
with a direct convergence proof for both dynamic and constant stepsizes, but the convergence is given on
G_{\lambda Q}
instead of
E_{\lambda Q}
.
Note that the convergence results of Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
can be applied to (
37
) to obtain convergence rates on
\|E_{\lambda Q}y_{k}\|
.
Such rates can be transformed to the ones on
\|G_{\lambda Q}z_{k}\|
when
C=0
and
B
is single valued.

Next, we can also derive Nesterov’s accelerated variant of (
37
) by applying (
12
) to solve
E_{\lambda Q}y^{\star}=0
.
In this case, (
12
) becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}z_{k}&:=&J_{\lambda B}y_{k},\\
w_{k}&:=&J_{\lambda A}(2z_{k}-y_{k}-\lambda Cz_{k}),\\
x_{k+1}&:=&y_{k}+\frac{1}{\lambda}(w_{k}-z_{k}),\\
y_{k+1}&:=&x_{k+1}+\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\nu_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k+1}).\end{array}\right.

(38)

Here, the parameters
\theta_{k}
and
\nu_{k}
can be chosen as in either Corollary
3.1
or Theorem
3.3
.
This scheme essentially has the same per-iteration complexity as the standard three-operator splitting scheme in the literature
[
12
]
.
However, its convergence rate is much faster than the standard one by applying either Corollary
3.1
or Theorem
3.3
.
If
C=0
, then (
38
) reduces to accelerated Douglas-Rachford splitting scheme, where its fast convergence rate guarantee can be obtained as a special case of either Corollary
3.1
or Theorem
3.3
.

5
Extra-Anchored Gradient Method and Its Variants

Motivation.

While the gradient of a convex and
L
-smooth function is co-coercive, monotone and Lipschitz continuous operators are not co-coercive in general.
As a simple example, one can take
Gx=(Av,-A^{\top}u)
as the gradient of the saddle objective function in a bilinear game, where
A
in
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is given and
x=(u,v)
.
In order to solve (
6
) when
G
is only monotone and
L
-Lipschitz continuous, the extragradient method (EG) appears to be one of the most suitable candidates
[
20
]
.
This method has recently been extended to weak Minty variational inequality, i.e.
\langle Gy,y-y^{\star}\rangle\geq-\rho\|Gy\|^{2}
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
y^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(G)
in our context, see, e.g.
[
14
]
.
In
[
43
]
, Yoon and Ryu have applied the Halpern fixed-point iteration to EG and obtained a new algorithm called extra-anchored gradient method (EAG).
This algorithm achieves optimal convergence rate on
\|Gy_{k}\|
.
Recently,
[
21
]
has extended EAG to a co-monotone setting of (
6
) and still achieved the same rate
\|Gy_{k}\|=\mathcal{O}\left(1/k\right)
as in
[
43
]
.
This is perhaps surprised since
G
is nonmonotone.
An extension to Popov’s scheme (also called past-extra-gradient, or reflected forward methods) can be be found in
[
41
]
.
Our goal in this section is to derive a corresponding Nesterov’s accelerated interpretation of these schemes and possibly provide an alternative convergence rate analysis for existing results in
[
21
,
41
,
43
]
.

5.1
The Extra-Anchored Gradient Method and Its Convergence

The extra-anchored gradient method (EAG) was proposed in
[
43
]
to solve (
6
) under the monotonicity and
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
G
, which can be written as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}z_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\eta_{k}Gy_{k},\\
y_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\hat{\eta}_{k}Gz_{k+1},\end{array}\right.

(39)

where
\beta_{k}\in(0,1)
,
y_{0}
is an initial point, and
\eta_{k}
and
\hat{\eta}_{k}
are two given step-sizes.
Here, we use two different step-sizes
\eta_{k}
and
\hat{\eta}_{k}
compared to the original EAG in
[
43
]
by adopting the idea of EG+ from
[
14
]
, see also
[
21
]
.

As proved in
[
43
]
, if we update
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
,
\eta_{k+1}:=\left(1-\frac{L^{2}\eta_{k}^{2}}{(1-L^{2}\eta_{k}^{2})(k+1)(k+3)}\right)\eta_{k}
, and
\hat{\eta}_{k+1}:=\eta_{k+1}
with some
0<\eta_{0}<\frac{1}{L}
, then the following convergence rate guarantee is obtained:

\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{*}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}}{(k+1)(k+2)},\quad\text{where}\quad\eta_{*}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\eta_{k}>0\quad\text{and}\quad C_{*}:=\frac{4(1+\eta_{0}\eta_{*}L^{2})}{\eta_{*}^{2}}.

(40)

Alternatively, one can also fix the step-size
\hat{\eta}_{k}=\eta_{k}=\eta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{8L}\right]
, and the following convergence guarantee is established:

\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{*}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}}{(k+1)^{2}},\quad\text{where}\quad C_{*}:=\frac{4(1+\eta L+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\eta^{2}(1+\eta L)}.

(41)

In particular, if
\eta:=\frac{1}{8L}
, then
C_{*}=260
.
Both guarantees (
40
) and (
41
) were proven in
[
43
]
.

5.2
Nesterov’s Accelerated Interpretation of EAG

Now, let us derive Nesterov’s accelerated interpretation of (
39
) by proving the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\{(x_{k},y_{k},z_{k})\}
be generated by the following scheme:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&y_{k}-\gamma_{k}Gy_{k},\\
z_{k+1}&:=&x_{k+1}+\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\nu_{k}(z_{k}-x_{k+1}),\\
y_{k+1}&:=&z_{k+1}-\hat{\eta}_{k}Gz_{k+1}+\eta_{k}Gy_{k},\end{array}\right.

(42)

starting from
z_{0}=x_{0}:=y_{0}
, where
\gamma_{k}:=\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}
,
\theta_{k}:=\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}
, and
\nu_{k}:=\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}
.
Then,
\{(y_{k},z_{k})\}
is identical to the one generated by the EAG scheme (
39
).

Clearly, (
42
) does not look like any Nesterov’s accelerated scheme in the literature.
To see the relation, let us rewrite (
42
) equivalently to the following scheme:

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&z_{k}-\hat{\eta}_{k-1}\hat{G}z_{k},\\
z_{k+1}&:=&x_{k+1}+\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\nu_{k}(z_{k}-x_{k+1}),\\
\end{array}\right.\\
\text{where}&\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{G}z_{k}&:=&Gz_{k}+\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\hat{\eta}_{k-1}}Gy_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k-1}}{\hat{\eta}_{k-1}}Gy_{k-1},\\
y_{k+1}&:=&z_{k+1}-\hat{\eta}_{k}Gz_{k+1}+\eta_{k}Gy_{k}.\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(43)

Clearly, the first two lines of (
43
) are similar to (
12
), but using an approximate operator
\hat{G}z_{k}
instead of the exact evaluation
Gz_{k}
as in (
12
).
Therefore, (
43
) can be viewed as an inexact variant of the Nesterov’s accelerated method (
12
).

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We only prove that (
39
) leads to (
42
).
The opposite direction from (
42
) to (
39
) is obtained by reverting back the derivations below, and hence omitted.

Firstly, multiplying the first line of (
39
) by
\beta_{k-1}
, we have
\beta_{k-1}z_{k+1}=\beta_{k}\beta_{k-1}y_{0}+\beta_{k-1}(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\beta_{k-1}\eta_{k}Gy_{k}
.
Shifting the index from
k
to
k-1
of the first line of (
39
), and then multiplying the result by
-\beta_{k}
, we get
-\beta_{k}z_{k}=-\beta_{k}\beta_{k-1}y_{0}-\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})y_{k-1}+\beta_{k}\eta_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}
.
Summing up both expressions, we arrive at
\beta_{k-1}z_{k+1}-\beta_{k}z_{k}=\beta_{k-1}(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\beta_{k-1}\eta_{k}Gy_{k}-\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})y_{k-1}+\beta_{k}\eta_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}
.
The last expression leads to

z_{k+1}=\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}z_{k}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\eta_{k}Gy_{k}-\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}y_{k-1}+\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k-1}}{\beta_{k-1}}Gy_{k-1}.

Next, subtracting the first line from the second one of (
39
), we have
y_{k+1}-z_{k+1}=-\hat{\eta}_{k}Gz_{k+1}+\eta_{k}Gy_{k}
, leading to
y_{k+1}=z_{k+1}-\hat{\eta}_{k}Gz_{k+1}+\eta_{k}Gy_{k}
.
Combining both expressions, we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}z_{k+1}&=&\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}z_{k}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\eta_{k}Gy_{k}-\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}y_{k-1}+\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k-1}}{\beta_{k-1}}Gy_{k-1},\\
y_{k+1}&=&z_{k+1}-\hat{\eta}_{k}Gz_{k+1}+\eta_{k}Gy_{k}.\end{array}\right.

(44)

Let us introduce
x_{k+1}:=y_{k}-\gamma_{k}Gy_{k}
.
Then, we have
Gy_{k}=\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}(y_{k}-x_{k+1})
.
Substituting these expressions into the first line of (
44
), we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}z_{k+1}&=&\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}z_{k}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\gamma_{k}}(y_{k}-x_{k+1})-\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}y_{k-1}+\frac{\beta_{k}\eta_{k-1}}{\beta_{k-1}\gamma_{k-1}}(y_{k-1}-x_{k})\\
&=&\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}z_{k}+\left(1-\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}\right)x_{k+1}+\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\left(1-\beta_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\gamma_{k}}\right)(y_{k}-x_{k+1})\\
&&-{~}\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}\big(1-\beta_{k-1}-\frac{\eta_{k-1}}{\gamma_{k-1}}\big)(y_{k-1}-x_{k}).\end{array}

If we choose
\gamma_{k}
such that
1-\beta_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{\gamma_{k}}=0
(or equivalently,
\gamma_{k}=\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}
), then we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}z_{k+1}&=&\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}z_{k}+\left(1-\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}\right)x_{k+1}+\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}(x_{k+1}-x_{k}).\end{array}

Finally, putting the above derivation together, we eventually get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&y_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}Gy_{k},\\
z_{k+1}&:=&\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}z_{k}+\left(1-\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}\right)x_{k+1}+\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}(x_{k+1}-x_{k}),\\
y_{k+1}&:=&z_{k+1}-\hat{\eta}_{k}Gz_{k+1}+\eta_{k}Gy_{k},\end{array}\right.

which is exactly (
42
).
∎

In order to analyze the convergence of (
42
), following the same approach as in Section
3
, we consider the following Lyapunov function:

\mathcal{Q}_{k}:=a_{k}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k}-z_{k}\rangle+\|x_{k}+t_{k}(z_{k}-x_{k})-y^{\star}\|^{2}+\mu\|x_{k}-y^{\star}\|^{2},

(45)

where
a_{k}>0
,
b_{k}>0
, and
t_{k}>0
are given parameters, which will be determined later, and
\mu\geq 0
.

Recall that the Lyapunov function used in
[
43
]
to establish convergence of (
39
) is
\mathcal{L}_{k}:=p_{k}\|Gy_{k}\|+q_{k}\langle Gy_{k},y_{k}-y_{0}\rangle
, where
p_{k}
and
q_{k}
are given parameters.
Similar to Lemma
3.1
, we can show that if
\mu=0
,
a_{k}:=\frac{4p_{k}(p_{k}+\gamma_{k}q_{k})}{q_{k}^{2}}
, and
b_{k}:=\frac{4p_{k}}{\beta_{k}q_{k}}
, then
\mathcal{L}_{k}=\frac{q_{k}^{2}}{4p_{k}}\left[\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}-\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}\right]
.
This relation allows one to adopt the analysis in
[
43
]
to prove convergence of (
42
).
However, the analysis using
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
remains different from
[
43
]
since
\mathcal{L}_{k-1}-\mathcal{L}_{k}
is different from
\mathcal{Q}_{k}-\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}
provided that
\frac{q_{k}^{2}}{4p_{k}}
is not a constant.
The following lemma proves a monotone property of
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
, which plays a key role for our analysis.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that
G
in (
6
) is monotone and
L
-Lipschitz continuous.
Let
\{(x_{k},y_{k},z_{k})\}
be generated by (
42
) and
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
be defined by (
45
) with
\mu:=0
.
Assume that the parameters
t_{k}
,
\gamma_{k}
,
\hat{\eta}_{k}
,
\eta_{k}
,
\theta_{k}
,
\nu_{k}
,
a_{k}
, and
b_{k}
are updated by

\begin{array}[]{ll}&t_{k}:=k+1,\quad\gamma_{k}:=\frac{1}{L},\quad\hat{\eta}_{k}:=\frac{1}{L},\quad\eta_{k}:=\frac{(k+1)}{L(k+2)},\quad\theta_{k}:=\frac{k}{k+2},\quad\nu_{k}:=\frac{k+1}{k+2},\\
&a_{k}:=\frac{b_{k}(k+2)}{2(k+1)},\quad\text{and}\quad b_{k+1}:=\frac{b_{k}(k+2)}{k}.\end{array}

(46)

Then, for
k\geq 0
, the following estimates hold:

\mathcal{Q}_{k}-\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}\geq 0,\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}\geq\frac{b_{1}(4-Lb_{1})(k+1)^{2}}{16L}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}.

(47)

Moreover, we also have
b_{k}=\frac{b_{1}k(k+1)}{2}
and hence
a_{k}=\frac{b_{1}k(k+2)}{4L}
.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of (
25
) in Lemma
3.2
, using (
42
) and (
45
), we can prove that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{Q}_{k}-\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}&=&a_{k}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}-b_{k}\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle\\
&&+{~}b_{k+1}\theta_{k}\langle Gy_{k},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle-b_{k}\langle Gy_{k-1},z_{k}-x_{k+1}\rangle\\
&&+{~}b_{k+1}\nu_{k}\langle Gy_{k},z_{k}-x_{k+1}\rangle+(t_{k}^{2}-\nu_{k}^{2}t_{k+1}^{2})\|z_{k}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[(t_{k}-1)^{2}-t_{k+1}^{2}\theta_{k}^{2}+\mu\right]\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2(t_{k}-1-t_{k+1}\theta_{k}-\mu)\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2(t_{k}-t_{k+1}\nu_{k})\langle z_{k}-x_{k+1},x_{k+1}-y^{\star}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2\left[t_{k}(t_{k}-1)-\nu_{k}\theta_{k}t_{k+1}^{2}\right]\langle z_{k}-x_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle.\end{array}

(48)

Now, let us choose the parameters
t_{k}
,
b_{k}
,
\theta_{k}
, and
\nu_{k}
such that

t_{k}-t_{k+1}\nu_{k}=0,\quad t_{k}(t_{k}-1)-\nu_{k}\theta_{k}t_{k+1}^{2}=0,\quad t_{k}-1-t_{k+1}\theta_{k}-\mu=0,\quad\text{and}\quad b_{k}=b_{k+1}\theta_{k}.

(49)

The third condition leads to
\theta_{k}:=\frac{t_{k}-1-\mu}{t_{k+1}}
, while the first one gives us
\nu_{k}:=\frac{t_{k}}{t_{k+1}}
.
The second condition becomes
t_{k}(t_{k}-1)=t_{k}(t_{k-1}-1-\mu)
, which is satisfied if
\mu=0
.
The last condition holds if we choose
b_{k+1}:=\frac{b_{k}}{\theta_{k}}
.
These updates are exactly given in (
46
).

Noticing from (
42
) that
z_{k}-x_{k+1}=\gamma_{k}Gy_{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k-1}Gz_{k}-\eta_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}
.
Using this relation and our choice
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k}}{\theta_{k}}
, we can easily show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[2]}&:=&b_{k+1}\nu_{k}\langle Gy_{k},z_{k}-x_{k+1}\rangle-b_{k}\langle Gy_{k-1},z_{k}-x_{k+1}\rangle\\
&=&b_{k}\langle\frac{\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1},\gamma_{k}Gy_{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k-1}Gz_{k}-\eta_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}\rangle\\
&=&\frac{b_{k}\gamma_{k}\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}-b_{k}\left(\gamma_{k}+\frac{\eta_{k-1}\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}\right)\langle Gy_{k},Gy_{k-1}\rangle+\frac{b_{k}\hat{\eta}_{k-1}\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}\langle Gy_{k},Gz_{k}\rangle\\
&&-{~}b_{k}\hat{\eta}_{k-1}\langle Gy_{k-1},Gz_{k}\rangle+b_{k}\eta_{k-1}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Alternatively, using the monotononcity of
G
,
b_{k}=b_{k+1}\theta_{k}
, and
x_{k+1}=y_{k}-\gamma_{k}Gy_{k}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[3]}&:=&b_{k+1}\theta_{k}\langle Gy_{k},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle-b_{k}\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle\\
&=&b_{k}\langle Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1},y_{k}-y_{k-1}-(\gamma_{k}Gy_{k}-\gamma_{k-1}Gy_{k-1})\rangle\\
&=&b_{k}\langle Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1},y_{k}-y_{k-1}\rangle-b_{k}\gamma_{k}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}+b_{k}(\gamma_{k}+\gamma_{k-1})\langle Gy_{k},Gy_{k-1}\rangle-b_{k}\gamma_{k-1}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&-b_{k}\gamma_{k}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}+b_{k}(\gamma_{k}+\gamma_{k-1})\langle Gy_{k},Gy_{k-1}\rangle-b_{k}\gamma_{k-1}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Substituting (
49
),
\mathcal{T}_{[2]}
, and
\mathcal{T}_{[3]}
into (
48
), we can further lower bound

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{Q}_{k}-\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}&\geq&\left[a_{k}+b_{k}(\eta_{k-1}-\gamma_{k-1})\right]\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+\left[b_{k}\gamma_{k}\big(\frac{\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-1\big)-a_{k+1}\right]\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}b_{k}\left(\frac{\eta_{k-1}\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-\gamma_{k-1}\right)\langle Gy_{k},Gy_{k-1}\rangle+b_{k}\hat{\eta}_{k-1}\left(\frac{\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-1\right)\langle Gy_{k},Gz_{k}\rangle\\
&&+{~}b_{k}\hat{\eta}_{k-1}\langle Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1},Gz_{k}\rangle.\end{array}

(50)

Now, using the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
G
, we have
\|Gz_{k}-Gy_{k}\|^{2}\leq L^{2}\|z_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}=L^{2}\|\hat{\eta}_{k-1}Gz_{k}-\eta_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}
, which leads to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}&+&(1-L^{2}\hat{\eta}_{k-1}^{2})\|Gz_{k}\|^{2}-2\left(1-L^{2}\eta_{k-1}\hat{\eta}_{k-1}\right)\langle Gy_{k},Gz_{k}\rangle\\
&&-{~}2L^{2}\eta_{k-1}\hat{\eta}_{k-1}\langle Gz_{k},Gy_{k}-Gy_{k-1}\rangle-L^{2}\eta_{k-1}^{2}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}\leq 0.\end{array}

Multiplying this inequality by
\frac{b_{k}}{2L^{2}\eta_{k-1}}
and adding the result to (
50
), we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{Q}_{k}-\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}&\geq&\left[a_{k}+\frac{b_{k}}{2}(\eta_{k-1}-2\gamma_{k-1})\right]\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}(1-L^{2}\hat{\eta}_{k-1}^{2})}{2L^{2}\eta_{k-1}}\|Gz_{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[b_{k}\gamma_{k}\big(\frac{\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-1\big)+\frac{b_{k}}{2L^{2}\eta_{k-1}}-a_{k+1}\right]\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}b_{k}\left(\frac{\hat{\eta}_{k-1}\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-\frac{1}{L^{2}\eta_{k-1}}\right)\langle Gy_{k},Gz_{k}\rangle-b_{k}\left(\frac{\eta_{k-1}\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-\gamma_{k-1}\right)\langle Gy_{k},Gy_{k-1}\rangle.\end{array}

(51)

Let us choose
\gamma_{k-1}
,
\hat{\eta}_{k-1}
,
\eta_{k-1}
, and
a_{k}
as follows:

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\gamma_{k-1}=\hat{\eta}_{k-1}:=\frac{1}{L},\quad\eta_{k-1}:=\frac{\theta_{k}}{L^{2}\nu_{k}\hat{\eta}_{k-1}}=\frac{(t_{k}-1)}{Lt_{k}},\quad\text{and}\quad a_{k}:=\frac{b_{k}(2\gamma_{k-1}-\eta_{k-1})}{2}=\frac{b_{k}(t_{k}+1)}{2Lt_{k}}.\end{array}\hskip-12.91663pt

(52)

Then, noting from (
49
) that
b_{k}=b_{k+1}\theta_{k}=\frac{b_{k+1}(t_{k}-1)}{t_{k+1}}
, we can easily check that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}a_{k}+\frac{b_{k}}{2}(\eta_{k-1}-2\gamma_{k-1})&=&0,\\
\frac{b_{k}(1-L^{2}\hat{\eta}_{k-1}^{2})}{2L^{2}\eta_{k-1}}&=&0,\\
b_{k}\left(\frac{\hat{\eta}_{k-1}\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-\frac{1}{L^{2}\eta_{k-1}}\right)&=&0,\\
b_{k}\left(\frac{\eta_{k-1}\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-\gamma_{k-1}\right)&=&0,\\
b_{k}\gamma_{k}\big(\frac{\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-1\big)+\frac{b_{k}}{2L^{2}\eta_{k-1}}-a_{k+1}&=&\frac{b_{k+1}(t_{k}-t_{k+1}+1)}{2Lt_{k+1}}.\end{array}\right.

If we choose
t_{k}:=k+1
, then both (
49
) and (
52
) lead to the updates in (
46
).
Moreover, the last term of the above expression becomes
b_{k}\gamma_{k}\big(\frac{\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-1\big)+\frac{b_{k}}{2L^{2}\eta_{k-1}}-a_{k+1}=0
.
Hence, (
51
) leads to
\mathcal{Q}_{k}-\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}\geq 0
, which proves the first estimate of (
47
).
Note that since
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k}(k+2)}{k}
, by induction, we get
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_{1}(k+1)(k+2)}{2}
, leading to
b_{k}=\frac{b_{1}k(k+1)}{2}
, and hence
a_{k}=\frac{b_{k}(t_{k}+1)}{2Lt_{k}}=\frac{b_{1}k(k+2)}{4L}
.
Clearly, with
b_{k}=\frac{b_{1}k(k+1)}{2}
, we have
b_{0}=0
and
a_{0}=0
.

Finally, using the definition (
45
) of
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
,
x_{k}=y_{k-1}-\gamma_{k-1}Gy_{k-1}
, and the monotonicity of
G
, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{Q}_{k}&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:NAG_scheme10_Lyapunov}}{=}&a_{k}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}-\frac{b_{k}}{t_{k}}\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k}+t_{k}(x_{k}-z_{k})-y^{\star}\rangle\\
&&+{~}\|x_{k}+t_{k}(z_{k}-x_{k})-y^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}}{t_{k}}\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k}-y^{\star}\rangle\\
&=&\|x_{k}+t_{k}(z_{k}-x_{k})-y^{\star}-\frac{b_{k}}{2t_{k}}Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+\left(a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{4t_{k}^{2}}\right)\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}}{t_{k}}\langle Gy_{k-1},x_{k}-y^{\star}\rangle\\
&\geq&\left(a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{4t_{k}^{2}}-\frac{b_{k}\gamma_{k-1}}{t_{k}}\right)\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Utilizing (
46
) and
b_{k}=\frac{b_{1}k(k+1)}{2}
, we obtain
\mathcal{Q}_{k}\geq\frac{b_{1}k^{2}(4-Lb_{1})}{16L}\|Gy_{k-1}\|^{2}
, which proves the second estimate of (
47
).
∎

Now, we can state the main convergence result of (
42
) in the following theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume that
G
in (
6
) is monotone and
L
-Lipschitz continuous.
Let
\{(x_{k},y_{k},z_{k})\}
be generated by (
42
) using the update rules (
46
).
Then, for all
k\geq 0
, we have

\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4L^{2}}{(k+1)^{2}}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}.

(53)

Proof.

From (
47
), if we choose
b_{1}:=\frac{2}{L}
, then we have
\mathcal{Q}_{k}-\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}\geq 0
and
\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}\geq\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{4L^{2}}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}
.
By induction, we get
\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}\leq\mathcal{Q}_{0}
.
Noting that
a_{0}=b_{0}=0
and
z_{0}=x_{0}
, we obtain
\mathcal{Q}_{0}=\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}
.
Combining three last statements, we arrive at
\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{4L^{2}}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}\leq\mathcal{Q}_{0}\leq\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}
, which leads to the first line of (
53
).
∎

Unfortunately, with the proof technique in Theorem
5.2
, we were unable to achieve faster
o\left(1/k^{2}\right)
-convergence rates as in Theorem
3.3
.
We believe that such rates require to choose
\mu>0
in the Lyapunov function
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
of (
45
).
We leave this analysis as our future research.

5.3
EAG for Co-Monotone Case and Its Nesterov’s Acceleration

We consider the variant of EAG in
[
21
]
for the co-monotone operator
G
.
Recall that the operator
G
in (
6
) is said to be
\rho
-comonotone if
\langle Gx-Gy,x-y\rangle\geq\rho\|Gx-Gy\|^{2}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, where
\rho<0
.
In this subsection, we consider the case that
G
is also
L
-Lipschitz continuous and
\rho
is chosen such that
-\frac{1}{2L}<\rho\leq\frac{1}{L}
.
Hence, it covers three cases: co-coerciveness when
\rho>0
, monotonicity when
\rho=0
, and co-monotonicity when
\rho<0
.
The second case has been studied in Subsection
5.2
.

More specifically,
[
21
]
proposes a variant of (
39
) to solve (
6
), which can be written as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}z_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-(1-\beta_{k})\left(2\rho+\eta_{k}\right)Gy_{k},\\
y_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-2\rho(1-\beta_{k})Gy_{k}-\eta_{k}Gz_{k+1},\end{array}\right.

(54)

where
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+1}
,
\eta_{k}:=\frac{1}{L}
.
If
\rho:=0
, then (
54
) reduces to (
42
).

As proved in
[
21
]
, the following convergence guarantee is obtained for (
54
):

\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4L^{2}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}}{(1+2\rho L)k^{2}},\quad\forall k\geq 1.

(55)

Here, the key condition is that
\rho>-\frac{1}{2L}
, which allows one to handle a non-monotone
G
in (
6
).

Let us rewrite (
54
) in a different form.
First, we have
y_{k+1}-z_{k+1}=-\eta_{k}Gz_{k+1}+\eta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})Gy_{k}
.
Hence, we get
2\rho(1-\beta_{k})Gy_{k}=\frac{2\rho}{\eta_{k}}(y_{k+1}-z_{k+1})+2\rho Gz_{k+1}
.
Then, we have
y_{k+1}=\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\eta_{k}Gz_{k+1}-\frac{2\rho}{\eta_{k}}(y_{k+1}-z_{k+1})-2\rho Gz_{k+1}
.
This expression implies that
(\eta_{k}+2\rho)y_{k+1}=2\rho z_{k+1}+\eta_{k}[\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}]-\eta_{k}(\eta_{k}+2\rho)Gz_{k+1}
.
Therefore, we eventually arrive at

y_{k+1}=(1-\tau_{k})x_{k+1}+\tau_{k}\big[\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\tfrac{\eta_{k}^{2}}{\tau_{k}}Gz_{k+1}\big],\quad\text{where}\quad\tau_{k}:=\tfrac{\eta_{k}}{\eta_{k}+2\rho}>0.

Overall, the scheme (
54
) can be rewritten as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}z_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\frac{(1-\beta_{k})\eta_{k}}{\tau_{k}}Gy_{k},\\
w_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}}{\tau_{k}}Gz_{k+1},\\
y_{k+1}&:=&(1-\tau_{k})z_{k+1}+\tau_{k}w_{k+1}.\end{array}\right.

(56)

The last line is a convex combination of the first two lines of the accelerated variant of EG+ from
[
14
]
.
If
\tau_{k}=1
(i.e.
\rho=0
or
G
is monotone), then (
56
) reduces to EAG in (
39
).

Now, we derive Nesterov’s accelerated interpretation of (
54
).
Following the same derivation as of (
42
), we can show that (
54
) is equivalent to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&y_{k}-(\eta_{k}+2\rho)Gy_{k},\\
z_{k+1}&:=&x_{k+1}+\theta_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+\nu_{k}(z_{k}-x_{k+1}),\\
y_{k+1}&:=&z_{k+1}-\eta_{k}(Gz_{k+1}-(1-\beta_{k})Gy_{k}).\end{array}\right.

(57)

This Nesterov’s accelerated interpretation reduces to (
42
) when
\rho=0
.
Moreover, it still achieves
\mathcal{O}\left(1/k^{2}\right)
rate even when
G
is co-monotone (i.e.
-\frac{1}{2L}<\rho<0
), which is non-monotone.
The analysis in Theorem
5.2
can be applied to (
57
).
However, we omit the details here.

5.4
Nesterov’s Accelerated Interpretation of Halpern-Type PEG Method

Our final step is to consider the Halpern-type past-extragradient scheme for solving (
6
) studied in
[
41
]
, which can be written as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}z_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\eta_{k}Gz_{k},\\
y_{k+1}&:=&\beta_{k}y_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})y_{k}-\hat{\eta}_{k}Gz_{k+1}.\end{array}\right.

(58)

Here,
z_{0}:=y_{0}
,
\beta_{k}\in(0,1)
, and
\eta_{k},\hat{\eta}_{k}>0
are given parameters, which will be determined in Theorem
5.3
.
The convergence of (
58
) has been proved in
[
41
]
for
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
,
\eta_{k+1}:=\frac{(1-\beta_{k}^{2}-2L^{2}\eta_{k}^{2})\beta_{k+1}\eta_{k}}{(1-2L^{2}\eta_{k}^{2})(1-\beta_{k})\beta_{k}}
, and
\hat{\eta}_{k}:=\eta_{k}
with some
0<\eta_{0}<\frac{1}{2L}
.
However, we provide a slightly different variant in Theorem
5.3
with
\eta_{k}\neq\hat{\eta}_{k}
, whose proof is deferred to Appendix
A
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume that
G
in (
6
) is monotone and
L
-Lipschitz continuous and
\mathrm{zer}(G)\neq\emptyset
.
For a given
\sigma>0
, let
M:=L^{2}(1+\sigma)
.
Let
\{(y_{k},z_{k})\}
be generated by (
58
) to solve (
6
) using
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
,
\eta_{k}:=\frac{1-\beta_{k}}{\sqrt{2M}}
, and
\hat{\eta}_{k}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2M}}
.
Then, the following convergence guarantees hold:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}+2L^{2}\|z_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}&\leq&\dfrac{2(1+4M)\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}}{(k+1)^{2}},\\
\|Gz_{k}\|^{2}&\leq&\dfrac{3(1+4M)\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}}{(k+1)^{2}},\\
(\sigma-1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k(k+1)\|z_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}&\leq&\frac{(1+4M)}{L^{2}}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}.\end{array}\right.

(59)

The last statement of (
59
) holds if
\sigma>1
.
In particular, if we choose
\sigma:=\frac{19}{100}
, then
\hat{\eta}_{k}:=\frac{0.9}{L}
.
This stepsize is smaller than the one in (
39
).
However, (
58
) only requires one evaluation of
G
per iteration compared to two evaluations as in (
39
).

By following the same way as (
42
), we can derive Nesterov’s accelerated interpretation of (
58
).
This result is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\beta_{k}\in(0,1)
and
\eta_{k},\hat{\eta}_{k}>0
be given.
Let
\{(\hat{x}_{k},y_{k},z_{k})\}
be generated by the following scheme:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{x}_{k+1}&:=&z_{k}-\hat{\gamma}_{k}Gz_{k},\\
z_{k+1}&:=&\hat{x}_{k+1}+\theta_{k}(\hat{x}_{k+1}-\hat{x}_{k})+\nu_{k}(z_{k}-\hat{x}_{k+1})+\kappa_{k}(z_{k-1}-\hat{x}_{k})-\zeta_{k}(z_{k-2}-\hat{x}_{k-1}),\end{array}\right.

(60)

starting from
z_{-2}=z_{-1}=z_{0}=\hat{x}_{-1}=\hat{x}_{0}:=y_{0}
, and using
\theta_{k}:=\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k-1})}{\beta_{k-1}}
,
\nu_{k}:=\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{k-1}}

\hat{\gamma}_{k}:=\hat{\eta}_{k-1}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}

Then,
\{(y_{k},z_{k})\}

.

Proof.

Similar to the derivation of (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&y_{k}-\gamma_{k}Gz_{k},\\

(61)

where

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{k+1}&:=&z_{k}-(\hat{\eta}_{k-1}+\gamma_{k})Gz_{k}+\eta_{k-1}Gz_{k-1},\\

(62)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{x}_{k+1}&:=&z_{k}-\hat{\gamma}_{k}Gz_{k},\\

if

K

\|K\|

Appendix A
The proof of Theorem
5.3
: Convergence Analysis of PEAG

The proof of Theorem
5.3
.

Let us consider the function
\mathcal{L}_{k}:=a_{k}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle Gy_{k},y_{k}-y_{0}\rangle
.
Then, as proved in
[
43
]
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{k}\geq\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{2a_{k}}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}
.
Following the same proof as in
[
41
]
, by choosing
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}
and using (
58
), we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{L}_{k}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}&\geq&a_{k}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|Gy_{k+1}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}\hat{\eta}_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\langle Gy_{k+1}-Gy_{k},Gz_{k+1}\rangle\\
&&+{~}b_{k+1}\hat{\eta}_{k}\langle Gy_{k+1},Gz_{k+1}\rangle.\end{array}

(65)

Now, using again (
58
), we have
z_{k+1}-y_{k+1}=\hat{\eta}_{k}Gz_{k+1}-\eta_{k}Gz_{k}
.
This expression leads to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|z_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\|^{2}&\leq&2\|\hat{\eta}_{k}Gz_{k+1}-\eta_{k}Gy_{k}\|^{2}+2\eta_{k}^{2}\|Gy_{k}-Gz_{k}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&2\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}\|Gz_{k+1}\|^{2}-4\hat{\eta}_{k}\eta_{k}\langle Gz_{k+1},Gy_{k}\rangle+2\eta_{k}^{2}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}+2L^{2}\eta_{k}^{2}\|y_{k}-z_{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

On the other hand, by the Lipschitz continuity of
G
, we have
\|Gz_{k+1}\|^{2}-2\langle Gz_{k+1},Gy_{k+1}\rangle+\|Gy_{k+1}\|^{2}=\|Gz_{k+1}-Gy_{k+1}\|^{2}\leq L^{2}\|z_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\|^{2}
.
Combining the last two inequalities, for any
\sigma>0
, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[4]}&:=&\|Gz_{k+1}\|^{2}-2\langle Gz_{k+1},Gy_{k+1}\rangle+\|Gy_{k+1}\|^{2}+\sigma L^{2}\|z_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&2(1+\sigma)L^{2}\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}\|Gz_{k+1}\|^{2}-4(1+\sigma)L^{2}\hat{\eta}_{k}\eta_{k}\langle Gz_{k+1},Gy_{k}\rangle+2(1+\sigma)L^{2}\eta_{k}^{2}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2(1+\sigma)L^{4}\eta_{k}^{2}\|z_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Let
M:=(1+\sigma)L^{2}
.
Then, the last inequality leads to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{[4]}&:=&\|Gy_{k+1}\|^{2}+(1-2M\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2})\|Gz_{k+1}\|^{2}-2M\eta_{k}^{2}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}-2\left(1-2M\hat{\eta}_{k}\eta_{k}\right)\langle Gz_{k+1},Gy_{k+1}\rangle\\
&&-{~}4M\hat{\eta}_{k}\eta_{k}\langle Gy_{k+1}-Gy_{k},Gz_{k+1}\rangle+\sigma L^{2}\|z_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\|^{2}-2ML^{2}\eta_{k}^{2}\|z_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&0.\end{array}

Multiplying this inequality by
\frac{b_{k}}{4M\eta_{k}\beta_{k}}
and adding the result to (
65
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{L}_{k}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}&\geq&\left(a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}\right)\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}+\left(\frac{b_{k}}{4M\eta_{k}\beta_{k}}-a_{k+1}\right)\|Gy_{k+1}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}(1-2M\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2})}{4M\eta_{k}\beta_{k}}\|Gz_{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{b_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\left(\frac{\hat{\eta}_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}-\frac{1}{2M\eta_{k}}\right)\langle Gy_{k+1},Gz_{k+1}\rangle+\frac{\sigma L^{2}b_{k}}{4M\eta_{k}\beta_{k}}\|z_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{L^{2}b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}\|z_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(66)

Here, we have used
b_{k+1}(1-\beta_{k})=b_{k}
.
Let us choose
\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
,
a_{k}:=\frac{b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}
,
\hat{\eta}_{k}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2M}}
, and
\eta_{k}:=\frac{(1-\beta_{k})}{2M\hat{\eta}_{k}}=\frac{(1-\beta_{k})}{\sqrt{2M}}
.
Then,
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k}}{1-\beta_{k}}=\frac{b_{k}(k+2)}{k+1}=b_{0}(k+2)
and
a_{k}=\frac{b_{0}(k+1)^{2}}{2\sqrt{2M}}
.
Moreover, we have
\frac{b_{k}}{4M\eta_{k}\beta_{k}}-a_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k}}{4M\eta_{k}\beta_{k}}-\frac{b_{k+1}\eta_{k+1}}{2\beta_{k+1}}=\frac{b_{k}}{2\sqrt{2M}\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}(1-\beta_{k+1})}{2\sqrt{2M}\beta_{k+1}(1-\beta_{k})}=\frac{b_{k}}{2\sqrt{2M}(1-\beta_{k})}\left(\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}-\frac{1}{\beta_{k+1}}+1\right)=0
.
Using the choice of these parameters, we obtain from (
66
) that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{L}_{k}+\frac{L^{2}b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}\|z_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}&\geq&\mathcal{L}_{k+1}+\frac{\sigma L^{2}b_{k}}{4M\eta_{k}\beta_{k}}\|z_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(67)

Note that
\frac{\sigma b_{k}}{2M\eta_{k}\beta_{k}}-\frac{b_{k+1}\eta_{k+1}}{\beta_{k+1}}=\frac{\sigma b_{k}}{\sqrt{2M}\beta_{k}(1-\beta_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}(1-\beta_{k+1})}{\sqrt{2M}(1-\beta_{k})\beta_{k+1}}=\frac{b_{k}}{\sqrt{2M}(1-\beta_{k})}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\beta_{k}}-\frac{1}{\beta_{k+1}}+1\right)=\frac{(\sigma-1)b_{k}(k+1)}{\sqrt{2M}(1-\beta_{k})}=\frac{(\sigma-1)b_{k}(k+2)}{\sqrt{2M}}
.
If we define
\mathcal{E}_{k}:=\mathcal{L}_{k}+\frac{L^{2}b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}\|z_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}+b_{0}\sqrt{2M}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}
, then by using the last relation, (
67
) becomes

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{E}_{k}&\geq&\mathcal{E}_{k+1}+\frac{L^{2}(\sigma-1)b_{k}(k+2)}{2\sqrt{2M}}\|z_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(68)

Moreover, since
\mathcal{L}_{k}\geq\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{2a_{k}}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}
, we have
\mathcal{E}_{k}\geq\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{L^{2}b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}\|z_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}\geq 0
.
For
\sigma\geq 1
and noting that
b_{k}=b_{0}(k+1)
, summing up (
68
) from
k=0
to
K
, and passing the limit as
K\to\infty
, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{L^{2}(\sigma-1)b_{0}}{2\sqrt{2M}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)(k+2)\|z_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}.\end{array}

(69)

Moreover, (
68
) also implies that
\frac{a_{k}}{2}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{L^{2}b_{k}\eta_{k}}{2\beta_{k}}\|z_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{E}_{k}\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}
, leading to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}+2L^{2}\|z_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4\sqrt{2M}\mathcal{E}_{0}}{b_{0}(k+1)^{2}}.\end{array}

Since
\mathcal{E}_{0}=\frac{b_{0}}{2\sqrt{2M}}\|Gy_{0}\|^{2}+b_{0}\sqrt{2M}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}\leq b_{0}\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2M}}+\sqrt{2M}\right)\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}
, we obtain the first line of (

) from the last inequality and this one.

Using the Lipschitz continuity of

, we have
\|Gz_{k}\|^{2}\leq 3\|Gz_{k}-Gy_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}\left[2L^{2}\|z_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}+\|Gy_{k}\|^{2}\right]
.
Combining this inequality and the first line of (
59
), we get the second line of (
59
).
Finally, combining the upper bound of
\mathcal{E}_{0}\leq\frac{b_{0}(1+4M)}{2\sqrt{M}}\|y_{0}-y^{\star}\|^{2}
and (
69
), and noting that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k(k+1)\|z_{k}-y_{k}\|^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)(k+2)\|z_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\|^{2}
, we obtain the third line of (

).
∎

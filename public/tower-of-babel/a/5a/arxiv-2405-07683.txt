Integral means spectrum functionals on Teichmüller spaces Thanks: The author was supported by National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant Nos. 11501157)
Jianjun Jin

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce and study the integral means spectrum (IMS) functionals on Teichmüller spaces. We show that the IMS functionals on the closure of the universal Teichmüller space and the universal asymptotic Teichmüller space are both continuous. During the proof, we consider the Pre-Schwarzian derivative model of universal asymptotic Teichmüller space and establish some new results for it. We also show that the integral means spectrum of any univalent function admitting a quasiconformal extension to the extended complex plane is strictly less than the universal integral means spectrum.

Introduction

Let
\Delta=\{z:|z|<1\}
denote the unit disk in the
complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. We denote the extended complex plane by
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
. For two positive numbers
A,B
, we write
A\asymp B
if there are two constants
C_{1}>0,C_{2}>0
which are independent of the arguments and such that
C_{1}B\leq A\leq C_{2}B

We denote the class of all univalent functions
f
in
\Delta
by
\mathcal{U}
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be the class of all univalent functions
f
in
\Delta
with
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0
. We let
\mathcal{S}_{b}
be the subclass of
\mathcal{S}
, which consists of all bounded univalent functions.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The
integral means spectrum

\beta_{f}(t)
for
f\in\mathcal{U}
is defined as

\beta_{f}(t)=\limsup_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\frac{\log\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|^{t}d\theta}{|\log(1-r)|}.

The famous Koebe function
\kappa
is defined as

\kappa(z):=\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}},\,z\in\Delta.

It is well known, see for example
[
32
]
, that, as
r\rightarrow 1^{-}
,

(1.1)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{|1-re^{i\theta}|^{\gamma}}\asymp\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(1-r)^{\gamma-1}},\;\text{if}\;\gamma>1,\\
\log\frac{1}{1-r},\;\;\;\text{if}\;\gamma=1,\\
1,\;\quad\,\,\,\,\quad\;\;\text{if}\;\gamma<1.\end{cases}

Then we see from (
1.1
) that

\beta{\kappa}(t)=\begin{cases}3t-1,\;\text{if}\;t\geq\frac{1}{3},\\
0,\;\quad\;\;\;\;\text{if}\;t\in[-1,\frac{1}{3}),\\
|t|-1,\;\text{if}\;t<-1.\end{cases}

The
universal integral mean spectrum

B(t)
and
B_{b}(t)
are defined as

B(t)=\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{S}}\beta_{f}(t),\,\,B_{b}(t)=\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{S}_{b}}\beta_{f}(t).

It is an important problem in the theory of univalent functions(conformal mappings) to determine the exact values of the universal integral mean spectra
B(t)
and
B_{b}(t)
. We review some known results and open problems on this topic. It was proved by Makarov in
[
29
]
that

Theorem 1.1
.

B(t)=\max\{B_{b}(t),3t-1\},\,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Remark 1.2
.

It is easy to see from Makarov’s result that
B(t)=B_{b}(t)
for
t\leq\frac{1}{3}
.

For large
t
, Feng and MacGregor showed in
[
15
]
that

Theorem 1.3
.

B(t)=3t-1,\,\,t\geq\frac{2}{5}.

Definition 1.4
.

We say a function

is an

for

(or

) if
\beta_{f}(t)=B_{b}(t)
(or
\beta_{f}(t)=B(t)
).

Remark 1.5
.

It should be pointed out that the extremal function for
B_{b}(t)
is not asked to be contained in
\mathcal{S}_{b}
in our definition.

Remark 1.6
.

Theorem
1.3
tells us that the Koebe function is an extremal function for

.

Kayumov proved in

Also, see
[

Theorem 1.8
.

B_{b}(t)=t-1,\,\,t\geq 2.

Remark 1.9
.

When
t\geq 2
, by using (
1.1
), we can check that

is an extremal function for
B_{b}(t)
.

Carleson and Makarov obtained in
[

that

.

There is a constant

such that

B_{b}(t)=B(t)=|t|-1,\,\,t\leq t_{0}.

An interesting open problem is to find out the optimal

. It is only known that

.
It is conjectured that

, or equivalently that

, which is usually referred to as Brennan’s conjecture. By the experimental work, Kraetzer has conjectured in

B_{b}(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{t^{2}}{4},\;\;\;\;\;\;\;{\textrm{if}}\;\,|t|\leq 2,\\
|t|-1,\;{\textrm{if}}\;\,|t|>2.\end{cases}

For more results on the integral means spectrum and related topics, see the monograph

\Delta^{*}=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}-\overline{\Delta}

\mathbb{T}=\partial\Delta=\partial\Delta^{*}
be the unit circle. We use the notation
\Delta(z,r)

Here

For an analytic function

2.1.
Univalent functions (conformal mappings)

First, we have

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{U}
. Then, for any
z\in\Delta
,

\frac{1}{4}(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq{\textup{dist}}(f(z),\partial f(\Delta))\leq(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
f
be a univalent function in
\Delta
and
g
be a univalent function in
f(\Delta)
. It follows from Proposition
2.1
that

{\textup{dist}}(g\circ f(z),\partial g(f(\Delta)))\leq(1-|z|^{2})|[g\circ f(z)]^{\prime}|=(1-|z|^{2})|g^{\prime}\circ f(z)|\cdot|f^{\prime}(z)|,

and

(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq 4{\textup{dist}}(f(z),\partial f(\Delta)).

Consequently, we have

{\textup{dist}}(g(\zeta),\partial g(f(\Delta)))\leq 4|g^{\prime}(\zeta)|{\textup{dist}}(\zeta,\partial f(\Delta)).

Here
z\in\Delta,\zeta=f(z)\in f(\Delta).

Remark 2.3
.

Let
f
be a univalent function from
\Delta
to a bounded Jordan domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}
. Then we know that
f
can be extended to a mapping which is a homeomorphism from
\overline{\Delta}
to
\overline{\Omega}
.
The extended mapping is still denoted by
f
. Then we have

{\textup{dist}}(f(z),f(\mathbb{T}))={\textup{dist}}(0,\mathbf{K}_{f}(\mathbb{T}))(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|,\,\,z\in\Delta.

Here
\mathbf{K}_{f}
is the Koebe transform of
f
(with respect to
z\in\Delta
), defined as

\mathbf{K}_{f}(w):=\frac{f(\sigma_{z}(w))-f(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|},\,\,\,\sigma_{z}(w)=\frac{z+w}{1+\overline{z}w},\,\,\,w\in\Delta.

On the other hand, by Proposition
2.1
, we have

{\textup{dist}}(0,\mathbf{K}_{f}(\mathbb{T}))\asymp|\mathbf{K}_{f}(e^{i\arg z})-\mathbf{K}_{f}(0)|=\frac{|f(z)-f(e^{i\arg z})|}{(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|}.

Then we obtain that

{\textup{dist}}(f(z),f(\mathbb{T}))\asymp|f(z)-f(e^{i\arg z})|,\,z\in\Delta.

Let
\mathcal{A}(\Delta)
denote the class of all analytic functions in
\Delta
. We let
{E}_{j}
be the Banach space of functions
\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\Delta)
with the norm

\|\phi\|_{E_{j}}:=\sup_{z\in\Delta}|\phi(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{j}<\infty,\,\,\,\,j=1,2.

Let
f
be a locally univalent function in an open domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{C}
. The
Pre-Schwarzian derivative

N_{f}
of
f
, and the
Schwarzian derivative

S_{f}
of
f
are defined as

N_{f}(z):=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{{f^{\prime}(z)}},\,z\in\Omega,

and

S_{f}(z):=[N_{f}(z)]^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}[N_{f}(z)]^{2}=\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-\frac{3}{2}\left[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right]^{2},\,z\in\Omega.

Let
g
be another locally univalent function in
f(\Omega)
. Then we have

(2.1)

N_{g\circ f}(z)=N_{g}(f(z))[f^{\prime}(z)]+N_{f}(z),\,\,z\in\Omega,

and

(2.2)

S_{g\circ f}(z)=S_{g}(f(z))[f^{\prime}(z)]^{2}+S_{f}(z),\,\,z\in\Omega.

It is well known that

|N_{f}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})\leq 6,\,\,{\text{and}}\,\,|S_{f}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\leq 6,

for all
f\in\mathcal{U}
. This means that

\|N_{f}\|_{E_{1}}\leq 6\,\,{\text{and}}\,\,\|S_{f}\|_{E_{2}}\leq 6
for any
f\in\mathcal{U}
.

We define the classes
\mathbf{N}
and
\mathbf{S}
as

\mathbf{N}=\{\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\Delta):\,\phi=N_{f}(z),f\in\mathcal{U}\},\,\,\,\mathbf{S}=\{\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\Delta):\,\phi=S_{f}(z),f\in\mathcal{U}\}.

Then we see that
\mathbf{N}\subset E_{1}
and
\mathbf{S}\subset E_{2}
. Moreover, we have

Proposition 2.4
.

\mathbf{N}
and
\mathbf{S}
are closed in
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, respectively.

Remark 2.5
.

It has been proved in
[
27
, Page 115]
that
\mathbf{S}
is closed in
E_{2}
. The statement that
\mathbf{N}
is closed in
E_{1}
is also known in the literature. For the
completeness of the paper, we will present a direct proof for this statement and some arguments of the proof will be used later. First, we have

Claim 2.6
.

For two locally univalent functions
f_{1},f_{2}
in
\Delta
, we have

N_{f_{1}}(z)=N_{f_{2}}(z)
for all
z\in\Delta
if and only if there are two numbers
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
such that
f_{1}(z)=af_{2}(z)+b
for all
z\in\Delta
.

The if part of this claim is trivial. We only prove the only if part. We let
N_{f_{j}}(z):=\phi(z),j=1,2
. Then

f_{j}(z):=\int_{0}^{z}e^{\int_{0}^{\zeta}\phi(w)dw+\log f_{j}^{\prime}(0)}d\zeta+f_{j}(0),\,j=1,2.

It follows that

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{z}e^{\int_{0}^{\zeta}\phi(w)dw+\log f_{1}^{\prime}(0)}d\zeta+f_{1}(0).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{[\log f^{\prime}_{1}(0)-\log f^{\prime}_{2}(0)]}[\int_{0}^{z}e^{\int_{0}^{\zeta}\phi(w)dw+\log f_{2}^{\prime}(0)}d\zeta+f_{2}(0)]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+f_{1}(0)-e^{[\log f^{\prime}_{1}(0)-\log f^{\prime}_{2}(0)]}f_{2}(0).

This proves the only if part of the claim and the proof of Claim
2.6
is done.

We proceed to prove the statement. We suppose that there is a sequence
\{{\bf f}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
,
{\bf f}_{n}\in\mathcal{U}
and
{\Phi}\in E_{1}
such that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|N_{{\bf f}_{n}}-\Phi\|_{E_{1}}=0.

First we have

\Phi(z)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}N_{{\bf f}_{n}}(z),

for each
z\in\Delta
. We will show that there is an
f_{\Phi}\in\mathcal{U}
such that
N_{f_{\Phi}}=\Phi
. Without loss of generality, since the Pre-Schwarzian derivative is affine invariant, we assume that
{\bf f}_{n}\in\mathcal{S}
for all
n
.
Noting that
\{{\bf f}_{n}\}
is a normal family, then
\{{\bf f}_{n}\}
contains a subsequence (still denoted by
\{{\bf f}_{n}\}
) which is locally uniformly convergent in
\Delta
. We denote by
g
the limit of the subsequence, i.e.,

g(z)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\bf f}_{n}(z),

and we know that
g\in\mathcal{S}
. Then at every point
z\in\Delta
we have

N_{g}(z)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}N_{{\bf f}_{n}}(z).

On the other hand, for
\Phi\in E_{1}
, there is a locally univalent function
f_{\Phi}
in
\Delta
such that

\Phi(z)=N_{f_{\Phi}}(z),\,\,z\in\Delta.

For example, we can take

f_{\Phi}(z):=\int_{0}^{z}e^{\int_{0}^{\zeta}\Phi(w)dw}d\zeta,\,\,\,z\in\Delta.

Consequently, we obtain that
N_{g}(z)=N_{f_{\Phi}}(z).

Then we know from the above claim that
f_{\Phi}
is univalent in
\Delta
. This means that
\mathbf{N}
is closed in
E_{1}
.

2.2.
Quasiconformal mappings

We say a sense-preserving homeomorphism
f
, from one open domain
\Omega
in

\mathbb{C}
to another is a quasiconformal mapping if it has locally square
integral distributional derivatives and satisfies the Beltrami equation
\bar{\partial}f=\mu_{f}\partial{f}
with

\|\mu_{f}\|_{\infty}=\mathop{\text{ess sup}}\limits_{z\in\Omega}|\mu_{f}(z)|<1.

Here the function
\mu_{f}(z)
is called the
Beltrami coefficient
of
f
and

\bar{\partial}f=f_{\bar{z}}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)f,\,\,\,\partial f=f_{z}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)f.

Let

D_{f}(z):=\frac{1+|\mu_{f}(z)|}{1-|\mu_{f}(z)|},\,\,K_{f}:=\frac{1+\|\mu_{f}\|_{\infty}}{1-\|\mu_{f}\|_{\infty}}.

We call
D_{f}(z)
the
dilatation function
of
f
in
\Omega
and
K_{f}
is called the
dilatation
of
f
.

Let
f
be a quasiconformal mapping from one open domain
\Omega_{1}
to another domain
\Omega_{2}
. If
g
is another quasiconformal mapping from
\Omega_{1}
to
\Omega_{3}
. Then the Beltrami coefficients of
f,g
and
g\circ f^{-1}
satisfy the following chain rule.

(2.3)

\mu_{g\circ f^{-1}}\circ f(z)=\frac{1}{\tau}\frac{\mu_{g}(z)-\mu_{f}(z)}{1-\overline{\mu_{f}(z)}\mu_{g}(z)},\,\,\tau=\frac{\overline{\partial f}}{\partial f},\,\,\,z\in\Omega_{1}.

Let
f
be a bounded univalent function in a Jordan domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{C}
admitting a quasiconformal mapping (still denoted by
f
) to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. The
boundary dilatation
of
f
, denoted by
b(f)
, is defined as

b(f):=\inf\{\|\mu_{f}|_{\Omega^{*}-E}\|_{\infty}:\,E{\text{ is a compact set in}}\,\,\Omega^{*}\}.

Here
\Omega^{*}=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}-\overline{\Omega}
is seen as an open set in the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
under the spherical distance and
b(f)
is the infimum of
\|\mu_{f}|_{\Omega^{*}-E}\|_{\infty}
over all compact subsets
E
contained in
\Omega^{*}
.

We say a domain
D
in
\mathbb{C}
is a
ring domain
if it can be conformally mapped into an annulus
\{z:0<r_{1}<|z|<r_{2}<\infty\}
, and the module
{\textup{Mod}}(D)
of such ring domain
D
is defined as
{\textup{Mod}}(D)=\log\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
D_{1}
be a ring domain in
\mathbb{C}
. Let
D_{2}
be another ring domain such that
{D_{2}}
is contained in
D_{1}
and
D_{1}-{D_{2}}
is not connected. Then we have

{\textup{Mod}}(D_{2})\leq{\textup{Mod}}(D_{1}).

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
f
be a quasiconformal mapping from one open domain
\Omega_{1}
of
\mathbb{C}
to another domain
\Omega_{2}
in
\mathbb{C}
. Let
D
be a ring domain with
\overline{D}\subset\Omega_{1}
. Then we have

{\textup{Mod}}(D)/K_{f}\leq{\textup{Mod}}(f(D))\leq K_{f}{\textup{Mod}}(D).

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
f
be a quasiconformal mapping from
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
to itself with
f(\infty)=\infty
. Then, for any
r>0,z\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\frac{\max\limits_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}|f(z+re^{i\theta})-f(z)|}{\min\limits_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}|f(z+re^{i\theta})-f(z)|}\leq C(K_{f}).

Then we obtain that

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
f
be a quasiconformal mapping from
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
to itself with
f(\infty)=\infty
. Assume that
f
maps one domain
\Omega_{1}
of
\mathbb{C}
to another domain
\Omega_{2}
in
\mathbb{C}
. For
z\in\Omega_{1}
, let
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
be such that
\Delta(z,r_{2})\subset\Omega_{1}
. Then we have

(2.4)

\frac{\max\limits_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}|f(z+r_{2}e^{i\theta})-f(z)|}{\min\limits_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}|f(z+r_{1}e^{i\theta})-f(z)|}\leq C(K_{f})\Big(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\Big)^{K_{f}}.

Proof.

We set

\mathbf{M}_{2}=\max\limits_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}|f(z+r_{2}e^{i\theta})-f(z)|,\,\,\mathbf{m}_{2}=\min\limits_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}|f(z+r_{2}e^{i\theta})-f(z)|,

\mathbf{M}_{1}=\max\limits_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}|f(z+r_{1}e^{i\theta})-f(z)|,\,\,\mathbf{m}_{1}=\min\limits_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}|f(z+r_{1}e^{i\theta})-f(z)|.

From Lemma
2.9
, we know that there is a positive constant
C_{1}(K_{f})
such that

(2.5)

\mathbf{M}_{1}\leq C_{1}(K_{f})\mathbf{m}_{1},\,\,\mathbf{M}_{2}\leq C_{1}(K_{f})\mathbf{m}_{2}.

If
\mathbf{m}_{2}\leq C_{1}(K_{f})\mathbf{m}_{1}
, then it is easy to see from (
2.5
) that (
2.4
) holds in this case.
On the other hand, if
\mathbf{m}_{2}>C_{1}(K_{f})\mathbf{m}_{1}\geq\mathbf{M}_{1}
, then we see that the annulus
\mathbf{A}:=\Delta(f(z),\mathbf{m}_{2}))-\Delta(f(z),\mathbf{M}_{1})
is contained in the ring domain
\mathbf{R}:=f(\Delta(z,r_{2}))-f(\Delta(z,r_{1}))
. Consequently, we obtain from Lemma
2.7
that

{\textup{Mod}}(\mathbf{A})\leq{\textup{Mod}}(\mathbf{R}).

It follows from (
2.5
) again and Lemma
2.8

\displaystyle\log\frac{\mathbf{M}_{2}}{\mathbf{m}_{1}}

We use
\mathcal{M}(\Delta^{*})

\displaystyle d_{T}([\mu]_{T},[\nu]_{T})=\frac{1}{2}\inf\bigg\{\log\frac{1+\|(\mu_{1}-\nu_{1})/(1-\overline{\nu_{1}}\mu_{1})\|_{\infty}}{1-\|(\mu_{1}-\nu_{1})/(1-\overline{\nu_{1}}\mu_{1})\|_{\infty}},

\displaystyle\quad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[\mu_{1}]_{T}=[\mu]_{T},[\nu_{1}]_{T}=[\nu]_{T}\bigg\}.

\mu

and

\displaystyle\quad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[\mu_{1}]_{AT}=[\mu]_{AT},[\nu_{1}]_{AT}=[\nu]_{AT)}\bigg\}.

H[\mu]=\inf\{\|\mu|_{\Delta^{*}-E}\|_{\infty}:\,E{\text{ is a compact set in}}\,\,\Delta^{*}\}.

\|\mu_{\mathbf{f}}\|_{\infty}>0

[

Lemma 4.2

5.1.
A final main theorem

Let
A
be a subset of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. A
holomorphic motion
of
A
is a map
\mathbf{H}:\Delta\times A\rightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
such that:

\bullet
for each fixed
z\in A
, the map
\lambda\mapsto\mathbf{H}(\lambda,z)
is holomorphic in
\Delta
;

\bullet
for each fixed
\lambda\in\Delta
, the map
z\mapsto\mathbf{H}(\lambda,z)
is injective in
\Delta
;

\bullet
for all
z\in A
, we have
\mathbf{H}(0,z)=z
.

Holomorphic motions were introduced in
[
30
]
by Mañé, Sad and Sullivan, who proved the
\lambda
-lemma. Slodkowski later established in
[
38
]
the extended
\lambda
-lemma, which confirmed a conjecture of Sullivan and Thurston
[
39
]
. The theory of holomorphic motions have many applications in complex analysis and holomorphic dynamics, see
[
4
]
. Holomorphic motions are closely related to quasiconformal mappings. It is known that

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Delta^{*})
and let
k=\|\mu\|_{\infty}
. Then there exists a (canonical) holomorphic motion
\mathbf{H}:\Delta\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\mathbf{H}(k,z)=f_{\mu}(z)
. Moreover, when
k=\|\mu\|_{\infty}>0
, for each fixed
\lambda\in\Delta
,
\mathbf{H}(\lambda,z)
is a quasiconformal mapping from
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
to itself with

\mu_{\mathbf{H}(\lambda,z)|_{\Delta}}=0,\,\,z\in\Delta
and
\mu_{\mathbf{H}(\lambda,z)|_{\Delta^{*}}}=\lambda\cdot\frac{\mu(z)}{\|\mu\|_{\infty}},\,z\in\Delta^{*}.

Remark 5.2
.

For any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Delta^{*})
with
|\mu\|_{\infty}=k
. We see from Proposition
5.1
that there is a holomorphic motion
\mathbf{H}:\Delta\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\mathbf{H}(k,z)=f_{\mu}(z)
. We denote
\mathbf{H}|_{\Delta\times\Delta}:={\mathbf{H}}_{\lambda}(z)
. For fixed
\lambda\in\Delta
,
{\mathbf{H}}_{\lambda}(z)
is univalent in
\Delta
and
{\mathbf{H}}_{k}(z)=f_{\mu}(z)
for any
z\in\Delta
. Then we know that, for fixed
z\in\Delta
,

\lambda\mapsto[{\mathbf{H}}^{\prime}_{\lambda}(z)]^{t}

is holomorphic in
\Delta
. For fixed
r\in(0,1)
, by
[
37
, Theorem 2.4.8]
, we have

\lambda\mapsto\int_{0}^{2\pi}|{\mathbf{H}}^{\prime}_{\lambda}(re^{i\theta})|^{t}d\theta

is subharmonic in
\Delta
. Note that

\beta_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\lambda}}(t)=\lim\limits_{\gamma\rightarrow 1^{-}}\sup\limits_{r\in(\gamma,1)}\frac{\log\int_{0}^{2\pi}|{\mathbf{H}}^{\prime}_{\lambda}(re^{i\theta})|^{t}d\theta}{|\log(1-r)|}.

Then, by the potential theory, it is reasonable to guess that
\lambda\mapsto\beta_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\lambda}}(t)
may satisfy the maximum modulus principle and so that
\beta_{f_{\mu}}(t)<B_{b}(t)

for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Delta^{*})
when
t\neq 0
. We will prove that this guess is true.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
t\neq 0
. For any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Delta^{*})
, we have

(5.1)

\beta_{f_{\mu}}(t)<B_{b}(t).

To prove Theorem
5.3
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Delta^{*})
.
(1)
If
\beta_{f_{\mu}}(t_{1})>0
for some
t_{1}>0
, then
\beta_{f_{\mu}}(t)
is strictly increasing on
[t_{1},+\infty)
.

(2)
If
\beta_{f_{\mu}}(t_{1})>0
for some
t_{1}<0
, then
\beta_{f_{\mu}}(t)
is strictly decreasing on
(-\infty,t_{1}]
.

Proof.

When
t_{1}>0
, let
\beta=\beta_{f_{\mu}}(t_{1})>0
. Let
\varepsilon<\beta
be a positive number, which will be fixed later. Then we see from the definition of integral means spectrum of
f_{\mu}
that
there is a sequence
\{r_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with
r_{n}<1
and
r_{n}\rightarrow 1
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and such that

\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}}d\theta>\frac{1}{(1-r_{n})^{\beta-\varepsilon}}.

We let

\mathcal{A}_{n}:=\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}}d\theta,\,\,\mathcal{D}_{n}:=\frac{1}{(1-r_{n})^{\beta-\varepsilon}},\,\,n\in\mathbb{N},

and define

{\mathcal{E}}_{n}:=\{\theta:|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}}>\frac{\mathcal{D}_{n}}{2\pi},\,\theta\in[0,2\pi)\},

\mathcal{F}_{n}:=\{\theta:|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}}\leq\frac{\mathcal{D}_{n}}{2\pi},\,\theta\in[0,2\pi)\}.

It is obvious that
\mathcal{E}_{n}\bigcup\mathcal{F}_{n}=[0,2\pi)
and
\mathcal{E}_{n}\bigcap\mathcal{F}_{n}=\emptyset.
We denote

I_{\mathcal{E}}=\int_{\mathcal{E}_{n}}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}}d\theta,\,\,I_{\mathcal{F}}=\int_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}}d\theta.

Case 1. If
I_{\mathcal{E}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}_{n}
, then for
\Delta t>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}+\Delta t}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{E}_{n}}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}+\Delta t}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\mathcal{D}_{n}}{2\pi}\Big)^{\frac{\Delta t}{t_{1}}}\int_{\mathcal{E}_{n}}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{\mathcal{D}_{n}}{2\pi}\Big)^{\frac{\Delta t}{t_{1}}}\mathcal{A}_{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[2\pi]^{-\frac{\Delta t}{t_{1}}}[\mathcal{D}_{n}]^{1+\frac{\Delta t}{t_{1}}}=:C_{1}(t_{1},\Delta t)[\mathcal{D}_{n}]^{1+\frac{\Delta t}{t_{1}}}.

Consequently, we obtain that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}+\Delta t}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}(t_{1},\Delta t)}{(1-r_{n})^{(\beta-\varepsilon)(1+\frac{\Delta t}{t_{1}})}}.

Case 2. If
I_{\mathcal{F}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}_{n}
, we set

\mathcal{G}_{n}:=\{\theta:|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}}\leq\frac{1}{16}\frac{\mathcal{D}_{n}}{2\pi},\,\theta\in[0,2\pi)\},

{\mathcal{H}_{n}}:=\{\theta:\frac{1}{16}\frac{\mathcal{D}_{n}}{2\pi}<|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}}\leq\frac{\mathcal{D}_{n}}{2\pi},\,\theta\in[0,2\pi)\}.

We easily see that
\mathcal{G}_{n}\bigcup\mathcal{H}_{n}=\mathcal{F}_{n}
and
\mathcal{G}_{n}\bigcap\mathcal{H}_{n}=\emptyset.
Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{n}}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}}d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}}d\theta-\int_{\mathcal{G}_{n}}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}_{n}-2\pi\frac{1}{16}\frac{\mathcal{D}_{n}}{2\pi}\geq\frac{7}{16}\mathcal{D}_{n}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}+\Delta t}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{n}}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}+\Delta t}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\mathcal{D}_{n}}{32\pi}\Big)^{\frac{\Delta t}{t_{1}}}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{n}}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{7}{16}[32\pi]^{-\frac{\Delta t}{t_{1}}}[\mathcal{D}_{n}]^{1+\frac{\Delta t}{t_{1}}}:=C_{2}(t_{1},\Delta t)[\mathcal{D}_{n}]^{1+\frac{\Delta t}{t_{1}}}.

Consequently, we obtain that

(5.3)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}+\Delta t}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}(t_{1},\Delta t)}{(1-r_{n})^{(\beta-\varepsilon)(1+\frac{\Delta t}{t_{1}})}}.

Now, for any
\Delta t>0
, taking

\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\beta\Delta t}{t_{1}+\Delta t}\in(0,\beta),

we see that

(\beta-\varepsilon)(1+\frac{\Delta t}{t_{1}})=\beta+\frac{\beta\Delta t}{2t_{1}}.

Thus, it follows from (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f_{\mu}^{\prime}(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|^{t_{1}+\Delta t}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}(t_{1},\Delta t)}{(1-r_{n})^{\beta+\frac{\beta\Delta t}{2t_{1}}}},

since
C_{2}(t_{1},\Delta t)\leq C_{1}(t_{1},\Delta t).
This implies that

\beta_{f_{\mu}}(t_{1}+\Delta t)\geq\beta+\frac{\beta\Delta t}{3t_{1}}>\beta=\beta_{f_{\mu}}(t_{1}).

This proves the part
(1)
of the lemma.

When
t_{1}<0,\beta_{f_{\mu}}(t_{1})>0
, replacing
f^{\prime}_{\mu}
by
\frac{1}{f^{\prime}_{\mu}}
in the above arguments, we can similarly show that the part
(2)
also holds. The proof of Lemma
5.4
is finished.
∎

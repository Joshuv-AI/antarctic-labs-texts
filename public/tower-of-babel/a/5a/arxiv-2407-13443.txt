Monodromy of the Prym map and semicanonical pencils in genus 6
Martí Lahoz, Juan Carlos Naranjo, Andrés Rojas, Irene Spelta

Abstract
. The Prym map 𝒫 6 \mathcal{P}_{6} in genus 6 is dominant and generically finite of degree 27. When restricted to the divisor of curves with an odd semicanonical pencil 𝒯 6 o \mathcal{T}_{6}^{o} , it is still generically finite, but of degree strictly smaller. In this paper, we prove that 𝒫 6 \mathcal{P}_{6} restricted to 𝒯 6 o \mathcal{T}_{6}^{o} is birational and that the monodromy group over the image of 𝒯 6 o \mathcal{T}_{6}^{o} is the Weyl group W ​ D 5 WD_{5} . Thus, there are two other irreducible divisors in the moduli space of Prym curves ℛ 6 \mathcal{R}_{6} and the degree of 𝒫 6 \mathcal{P}_{6} restricted to them is 10 and 16. Moreover, we study the geometry of the divisor where 𝒫 6 \mathcal{P}_{6} has degree 10.

1.
Introduction

It is a classical fact, dating back to Wirtinger, that a general principally polarized abelian variety (ppav) of dimension
\leq 5
is a Prym variety. In other words, the
Prym map

\mathcal{P}_{g}:\mathcal{R}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g-1}
between the moduli spaces
\mathcal{R}_{g}
(of étale double covers of smooth genus
g
curves) and
\mathcal{A}_{g-1}
(of
(g-1)
-dimensional ppavs) is dominant for
g\leq 6
. This allows to understand ppavs of dimension
\leq 5
in terms of geometry of curves, with applications ranging from unirationality of their moduli space (
[
Don84
,
Ver84
]
) to the geometry of theta divisors (
[
IvS95
,
ITW17
,
FGSMV14
]
).

In the case
g=6
, the Prym map is generically finite of degree 27, as proved by Donagi-Smith
[
DS81
]
. Furthermore its monodromy group equals the Weyl group
WE_{6}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{27}
, and Donagi’s tetragonal construction endows its general fiber with the incidence structure of the 27 lines on a smooth cubic surface (
[
Don81
,
Don92
]
).
It turns out that the rich geometry of the map
\mathcal{P}_{6}
can be used to describe effective divisors on both moduli spaces
\mathcal{R}_{6}
and
\mathcal{A}_{5}
, and the monodromy group of
\mathcal{P}_{6}
over such divisors becomes an interesting question.

The purpose of this note is to study this problem in a concrete geometric example. More precisely, let
\mathcal{T}_{g}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
denote the divisor of (isomorphism classes of) curves
C
with a
semicanonical pencil
1
1

1

In the literature, this is frequently called a
vanishing theta-null
.
(i.e., with a theta-characteristic
L\in\Pic^{g-1}C
such that
h^{0}(C,L)
is even and positive).
The preimage of
\mathcal{T}_{g}
in
\mathcal{R}_{g}
(via the forgetful map
\pi:\mathcal{R}_{g}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
) decomposes as the union of two divisors, according to a parity condition.
We denote these two divisors by
\mathcal{T}^{e}_{g}
and
\mathcal{T}^{o}_{g}
(for
even
and
odd
Prym semicanonical pencils, respectively), which are known to be irreducible (
[
MPR23
]
).

Recent work by the first three authors
[
LNR23
]
addresses the study of the Prym map restricted to
\mathcal{T}^{e}_{g}
and
\mathcal{T}^{o}_{g}
. Whereas the case of
\mathcal{T}^{e}_{g}
is relatively elementary, the analysis for
\mathcal{T}^{o}_{g}
is substantially harder and is often related to rich geometry. For instance, the study of
\mathcal{P}_{5}|_{\mathcal{T}^{o}_{5}}
reveals enumerative properties of lines on cubic threefolds (
[
LNR23
, Section 6]
).

In the case
g=6
, it is proved that
\mathcal{P}_{6}|_{\mathcal{T}_{6}^{o}}
is generically finite onto its image, of degree strictly less than
27
(in contrast to
\mathcal{P}_{6}|_{\mathcal{T}^{e}_{6}}
). This opens the way to consider other effective divisors on
\mathcal{R}_{6}
, namely the irreducible components of
\mathcal{P}_{6}^{-1}\,(\overline{\mathcal{P}_{6}(\mathcal{T}_{6}^{o})})
. The degree of
\mathcal{P}_{6}
restricted to each component, as well as the monodromy group of
\mathcal{P}_{6}
over
\overline{\mathcal{P}_{6}(\mathcal{T}_{6}^{o})}
, arise as natural questions in this context.

Our main result is:

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathcal{A}_{5}
denote the divisor obtained as the closure of
\mathcal{P}_{6}(\mathcal{T}^{o}_{6})
. Then the preimage
\mathcal{P}_{6}^{-1}(\mathcal{Z})
consists of three irreducible components, namely:

(1)

\mathcal{T}^{o}_{6}
, which is birational to
\mathcal{Z}
:
\deg\left(\mathcal{P}_{6}|_{\mathcal{T}^{o}_{6}}\right)=1
.

(2)

\pi^{-1}\mathcal{D}
, where

\;\;\;\mathcal{D}=\left\{[C]\in\mathcal{M}_{6}\;|\;\exists M\in W^{1}_{4}(C)\text{ and }x,y,z\in C\text{ with }\omega_{C}\otimes M^{-1}\cong\mathcal{O}_{C}(2x+2y+2z)\right\}

is the divisor whose general point is a genus 6 curve admitting a plane sextic model with a tritangent line. The equality
\deg\left(\mathcal{P}_{6}|_{\pi^{-1}\mathcal{D}}\right)=10
holds.

(3)

A third component
\mathcal{L}
, which satisfies
\deg\left(\mathcal{P}_{6}|_{\mathcal{L}}\right)=16
.

Furthermore, the monodromy group of
\mathcal{P}_{6}^{-1}(\mathcal{Z})\to\mathcal{Z}
equals
WD_{5}
.

Let us sketch briefly the argument.
Since
\mathcal{Z}
is not the branch divisor of
\mathcal{P}_{6}
, the general fiber of
\mathcal{P}_{6}^{-1}(\mathcal{Z})
can be identified with the set of 27 lines on a smooth cubic surface: two elements in the fiber are tetragonally related if, and only if, the corresponding lines intersect.

In a first part, we apply the tetragonal construction to a general element
(C,\eta)\in\mathcal{T}^{o}_{6}
; independently of the chosen
g^{1}_{4}
on
C
, one always recovers two elements in
\pi^{-1}\mathcal{D}
(with their
tritangent

g^{1}_{4}
).
Conversely, the tetragonal construction applied to a general element in
\pi^{-1}\mathcal{D}
(after choosing a tritangent
g^{1}_{4}
) returns an element in
\mathcal{T}^{o}_{6}
and another element in
\pi^{-1}\mathcal{D}
(again with a tritangent
g^{1}_{4}
).

Since the general curve in
\mathcal{D}
has a unique tritangent
g^{1}_{4}
, this implies
\deg\left(\mathcal{P}_{6}|_{\mathcal{T}^{o}_{6}}\right)=1
,
\pi^{-1}\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{P}_{6}^{-1}(\mathcal{Z})
and
\deg\left(\mathcal{P}_{6}|_{\pi^{-1}\mathcal{D}}\right)=10
. In particular, the monodromy group of
\mathcal{P}_{6}^{-1}(\mathcal{Z})\to\mathcal{Z}
must be contained in
WD_{5}
(the stabilizer in
WE_{6}
of a line).

In a second part, we prove that the monodromy is the entire
WD_{5}
. We exploit the fact that the Jacobian locus is contained in
\mathcal{Z}
to consider an appropriate blown-up map
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{6}^{-1}(\mathcal{Z})}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}
, for which we can determine
WD_{5}
as its monodromy group. The idea is inspired by
[
Don92
, Theorem 4.4]
, but does not follow from the results there; some other arguments, involving degree 4 del Pezzo surfaces and transversality of
\mathcal{T}^{o}_{6}
with the locus of coverings of trigonal curves, must be employed.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we review some basics that we will need later. Through the paper, we work over the field of the complex numbers.

2.1.
The Prym map and the divisors of Prym semicanonical pencils

Consider the moduli space of
smooth Prym curves
of genus
g

\mathcal{R}_{g}=\{(C,\eta)\mid[C]\in\mathcal{M}_{g},\;\eta\in JC[2]\setminus\{\mathcal{O}_{C}\}\}/\cong

parametrizing étale, irreducible double covers of smooth genus
g
curves.
Given
(C,\eta)\in\mathcal{R}_{g}
and
f:{\widetilde{C}}\to C
its associated cover, one can define the
Prym variety

P:=P(C,\eta)
as the connected component of the kernel of the induced norm map
\Nm_{f}:J{\widetilde{C}}\to JC
that contains
0_{J{\widetilde{C}}}
.
The principal polarization on
J{\widetilde{C}}
restricts to twice a principal polarization on
P
(
[
Mum74
, Section 3]
), thus we obtain the
Prym map

\mathcal{P}_{g}:\mathcal{R}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g-1}.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{g}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
be the irreducible divisor given by curves admitting a
semicanonical pencil
, i.e. a theta-characteristic
L\in\Pic^{g-1}C
such that
h^{0}(C,L)
is even and positive. In the literature our semicanonical pencils are also called
vanishing theta-nulls
, but later we will use the theta-null divisor in the context of abelian varieties, so we prefer to use this name to avoid any confusion.

By using the forgetful map
\pi:\mathcal{R}_{g}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
, we consider the following irreducible divisors:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{e}_{g}=\left\{(C,\eta)\in\mathcal{R}_{g}\mid C\text{ has a semicanonical pencil $L$ with $h^{0}(C,L\otimes\eta)$ even}\right\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{o}_{g}=\left\{(C,\eta)\in\mathcal{R}_{g}\mid C\text{ has a semicanonical pencil $L$ with $h^{0}(C,L\otimes\eta)$ odd}\right\}.

Thanks to the description of the singularities of the theta divisor of a Prym variety provided in
[
Mum74
, Section 7]
, it is known that
\mathcal{P}_{g}
maps
\mathcal{T}_{g}^{e}
to the divisor
\theta_{null}\subset\mathcal{A}_{g-1}
of principally polarized abelian varieties whose (symmetric) theta divisor contains a singular 2-torsion point of even multiplicity.
More precisely, let

P^{+}:=\left\{M\in\Pic^{2g-2}{\widetilde{C}}\mid\Nm_{f}(M)=\omega_{C}\ \text{and }h^{0}({\widetilde{C}},M)\text{ is even}\right\}

be a presentation of the Prym variety in
\Pic^{2g-2}{\widetilde{C}}
.
Then
\Sigma^{+}=\{M\in P^{+}\mid h^{0}({\widetilde{C}},M)\geq 2\}
is a “canonical” presentation of the theta divisor of
P
.
This shows that an even semicanonical pencil provides an easy example of a singular (exceptional) point in
\Sigma^{+}
.
The 2-torsion property follows from the fact that 2-torsion points of
P
in
\Pic^{0}{\widetilde{C}}
are, in
P^{+}
, the theta characteristics of
{\widetilde{C}}
lying in
P^{+}
.

In
[
LNR23
, Theorems A-B]
, the first three authors showed that
\mathcal{T}_{g}^{e}=\mathcal{P}_{g}^{-1}(\theta_{null})
for
g\geq 3
and that
\mathcal{T}^{o}_{g}
dominates
\mathcal{A}_{g-1}
as long as
\dim\mathcal{T}^{o}_{g}\geq\mathcal{A}_{g-1}
.
Furthermore, they proved that if
g\geq 6
then the restrictions
\mathcal{P}_{6}|_{\mathcal{T}^{e}_{6}}
and
\mathcal{P}_{6}|_{\mathcal{T}^{o}_{6}}
are generically finite onto their image.
In particular, when
g=6
, the Prym map
\mathcal{P}_{6}|_{\mathcal{T}^{e}_{6}}
has generically degree 27, whereas
\mathcal{P}_{6}|_{\mathcal{T}^{o}_{6}}
has generically degree strictly smaller than 27.

2.2.
Brill-Noether loci on Prym varieties

Given an étale double cover
(C,\eta)\in\mathcal{R}_{g}
, one can consider a Brill-Noether theory for the Prym variety
P
.
This is due to Welters (
[
Wel85
]
), who defined the
Prym-Brill-Noether loci
as follows:

V^{r}(C,\eta):=\left\{M\in\Pic^{2g-2}{\widetilde{C}}\mid\Nm_{f}(M)=\omega_{C},\,h^{0}({\widetilde{C}},M)\geq r+1,\,h^{0}({\widetilde{C}},M)\equiv r+1\text{(mod }2)\right\},

with the scheme structure defined by
P^{+}\cap W^{r}_{2g-2}({\widetilde{C}})
when
r
is odd, and by
P^{-}\cap W^{r}_{2g-2}({\widetilde{C}})
when
r
is even (here
P^{-}
is defined analogously to
P^{+}
, by requiring an odd number of sections).

In particular, we have
V^{0}(C,\eta)=P^{-}
and
V^{1}(C,\eta)=\Sigma^{+}
. Moreover, being
T({\widetilde{C}})
the theta-dual of the Abel-Prym curve
{\widetilde{C}}
inside
P
(which parametrizes the translates of
{\widetilde{C}}\subset P
contained in the theta divisor), we have
V^{2}(C,\eta)=T({\widetilde{C}})
if
g\geq 4
and
C
is not hyperelliptic (
[
LN13
]
).

If
(C,\eta)\in\mathcal{T}^{o}_{g}
and
L
is a semicanonical pencil on
C
with
h^{0}(L\otimes\eta)
odd, then
f^{*}L\in V^{2}(C,\eta)
.
Moreover
f^{*}L
is a singular point of
V^{2}(C,\eta)
(see for instance
[
LNR23
, Lemma 2.2]
); if
P^{-}
is identified with
P(C,\eta)
by using a theta-characteristic of
{\widetilde{C}}
lying in
P^{-}
, then
V^{2}(C,\eta)
becomes a symmetric subvariety of
P(C,\eta)
which is singular at a 2-torsion point.

2.3.
Trigonal and tetragonal constructions, and the Prym map in genus 6

The classical
trigonal construction
due to Recillas
[
Rec74
]
provides a bijection between étale double covers of genus
g
trigonal curves and tetragonal curves of genus
g-1
.
More precisely, the Prym variety attached to a cover
{\widetilde{T}}\to T
of a trigonal curve is isomorphic, as a principally polarized abelian variety, to the Jacobian of a curve
X
equipped with a
g^{1}_{4}

M
.
Moreover, one can recover
{\widetilde{T}}
(with its natural involution) from
JX
as the locus of elements
p+q\in X^{(2)}
such that
h^{0}(X,M(-p-q))>0
.

Similarly, Donagi
[
Don81
]
found the so-called
tetragonal construction
, which produces from an étale double cover
{\widetilde{C}}_{1}\to C_{1}
of a genus
g
curve
C_{1}
with a fixed
g^{1}_{4}

M_{1}
, the data of two more such covers of tetragonal curves
({\widetilde{C}}_{i}\to C_{i},M_{i})
,
i=2,3
, such that the three Prym varieties
P({\widetilde{C}}_{i}\to C_{i})
are isomorphic.
He reformulated in
[
Don92
, Lemma 5.5]
this construction in the following way, which is more convenient for our purposes:

Lemma 2.1
.

There is a bijection between the following two sets of data:

(1)

Triples
(T,N,W)
, where
T\in\mathcal{M}_{g+1}
is a trigonal curve,
N
is a
g^{1}_{3}
on
T
and
W=\{0,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}\}\subset JT[2]
is a totally isotropic subgroup with respect to the Weil pairing.

(2)

A tetragonally related triple
\{(C_{i},\eta_{i},M_{i})\}_{i=1,2,3}
with
(C_{i},\eta_{i})\in\mathcal{R}_{g}
and
M_{i}
a
g^{1}_{4}
on
C_{i}
.

In this bijection, the triplet
(T,N,\mu_{i})
corresponds to
(C_{i},M_{i})
via Recillas’ construction, whereas
\mu_{j}\in\langle\mu_{i}\rangle^{\perp}
(
j\neq i
) corresponds to
\eta_{i}\in JC_{i}[2]
via Mumford’s exact sequence describing the 2-torsion subgroup of a Prym variety (
[
Mum74
, Section 3]
).

Finally, let us recall the main properties of the Prym map in genus
6
, as discussed in
[
DS81
]
and
[
Don92
]
:

Theorem 2.2
.

The Prym map
\mathcal{P}_{6}:\mathcal{R}_{6}\to\mathcal{A}_{5}
is dominant and generically finite, of degree
27
. Moreover:

(1)

The general fiber of
\mathcal{P}_{6}
can be identified with the set of 27 lines on a smooth cubic surface, and two elements in the fiber are tetragonally related if, and only if, the corresponding lines intersect.

(2)

The monodromy group of
\mathcal{R}_{6}
over
\mathcal{A}_{5}
is the Weyl group
WE_{6}
, the symmetry group of the incidence of the
27
lines on a smooth cubic surface.

2.4.
Compactifications

In order to compute complete fibres of the Prym map, it is convenient to compactify the involved moduli spaces. Abusing notation,
\mathcal{T}_{6}^{o}
will also denote the closure of this divisor in
\overline{\mathcal{R}_{6}}
(the Deligne-Mumford compactification) or in
\mathcal{R}_{6}^{\prime}
(Beauville’s partial compactification by admissible covers).
We will denote by
\mathcal{P}_{6}^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{R}_{6}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{A}_{5}
Beauville’s proper extension of the Prym map (
[
Bea77
]
).

Following
[
FGSMV14
]
, we consider the rational Prym map
\overline{\mathcal{P}}:\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{6}\dasharrow\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{5}
obtained by extending the Prym map to the open subset of
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{6}
lying over the locus of stable curves in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{6}
with at most one node.
Here
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{5}
stands for the perfect cone compactification of
\mathcal{A}_{5}
, whose rational Picard group
\Pic(\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{5})_{\mathbb{Q}}
is generated by the Hodge class
L
and the class
D
of the irreducible boundary divisor. In
[
LNR23
, Proposition 7.3]
, it was proved that the class of the divisor
\mathcal{Z}=\overline{\mathcal{P}_{6}(\mathcal{T}^{o}_{6})}
appearing in
Theorem A
is proportional to
10584L-1320D
.

Arguing as in loc. cit., it is easy to conclude that the fiber of
\mathcal{P}_{6}
at a general point of
\mathcal{Z}
is contained in
\mathcal{R}_{6}
and consists of
27
coverings, all different to each other.

3.
Projective geometry of trigonal curves of genus 6

In this section, we present some results on genus
6
curves, all of them related with trigonal curves.
First, we consider the locus
\mathcal{D}
in
\mathcal{M}_{6}
of curves which can be represented as plane sextics (with four nodes) with a tritangent line. We will easily prove that the trigonal locus
\mathcal{M}_{6}^{trig}\subset\mathcal{M}_{6}
is contained in
\mathcal{D}
. As we will see in the next section, these sextics appear naturally when we apply the tetragonal construction to elements in
\mathcal{T}^{o}_{6}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
be the moduli space of pairs
(C,M)
with
C\in\mathcal{M}_{6}
and
M\in\Pic^{4}C
, such that
h^{0}(C,M)=2
and there exist three points
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in C
satisfying
\omega_{C}\otimes M^{-1}\cong\mathcal{O}_{C}(2x_{1}+2x_{2}+2x_{3})
. This is a locus in the moduli space of genus
6
curves with a marked
g^{1}_{4}
; we define
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{M}_{6}
as the closure of the image of the forgetful map restricted to
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
.

We have the following inclusion:

Lemma 3.2
.

The trigonal locus is contained in
\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Let
T\in\mathcal{M}_{6}
be a general trigonal curve, and let
N
denote the
g^{1}_{3}
on
T
.
On the one hand,
h^{0}(T,\omega_{T}\otimes N^{-2})=h^{0}(T,N^{2})-1
by Riemann-Roch.
On the other hand, since
T
is not hyperelliptic we have
h^{0}(T,N^{2})<4
, whereas the natural inclusion
\Sym^{2}H^{0}(T,N)\subset H^{0}(T,N^{2})
yields
h^{0}(T,N^{2})\geq 3
. It follows that
|\omega_{T}\otimes N^{-2}|
is a
g^{1}_{4}
on
T
, which proves the result.
∎

Observe that, geometrically, a non-trigonal
(C,M)
lies in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
if the image of the curve by the morphism attached to
\omega_{C}\otimes M^{-1}
(a plane sextic with four nodes) has a tritangent line.
There are four additional tetragonal series on
C
, by considering the pencils of lines through each of the nodes.
Recall that generically, a curve of genus
6
has five
g^{1}_{4}
. We fix one of the nodes
p
and denote by
M_{p}
the line bundle attached to the pencil.
Since the canonical series corresponds to the cubics through the four nodes, the linear series
|\omega_{C}\otimes M_{p}^{-1}|
is cut out by conics through the other three nodes. Then
(C,M_{p})\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
if, and only if, one of these conics is tangent to
C
at three points.
The next proposition asserts that this is not possible generically.

Proposition 3.3
.

For a general plane sextic with four nodes and one tritangent line (at three smooth points of the sextic), there are no conics passing through three of the nodes and tangent to the sextic in three smooth points. In other words, the forgetful map
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{D}
has degree
1
.

Proof.

Since the existence of a tritangent line and a tritangent conic (passing through three of the four nodes) are closed properties, it is enough to exhibit a sextic with (at least) four nodes and one tritangent line for which such a conic does not exist.

We consider a smooth plane quartic
D\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
with a fixed bitangent line
l
. Set
P\in l\setminus(l\cap D)
and choose two different lines
r_{1},r_{2}
, with
P\in r_{1}\cap r_{2}
and
l\neq r_{1},r_{2}
.
Our sextic is
S=D\cup r_{1}\cup r_{2}
, and the four nodes are two points in
D\cap r_{1}
and two points in
D\cap r_{2}
.
The line
l
is, by construction, tangent to
S
at three points different from the nodes.
A conic through three of the nodes will be tritangent to
S
(at three points different from the nodes) if passes through some other point in
r_{i}\cap D
and it is bitangent to
D
.
We want to see that for a general choice of
P
and
r_{1},r_{2}
, this conic does not exist for
S
.

To this end, we define two natural sets in the symmetric product
D^{(4)}
of the quartic. First we consider
I_{l}^{0}
to be the closure of

\left\{(x_{1}+x_{2},x_{3}+x_{4})\in D^{(2)}\times D^{(2)}\mid x_{1}x_{2},x_{3}x_{4}\text{ are different lines and }x_{1}x_{2}\cap x_{3}x_{4}\in l\right\},

and let
I_{l}
be the image of
I_{l}^{0}
under the addition map
D^{(2)}\times D^{(2)}\to D^{(4)}
.
This closed set has dimension
3
, and parametrizes sets of four nodes in a sextic of the form
D\cup r_{1}\cup r_{2}
.

The second set
W
, is defined as the preimage of the surface
2D^{(2)}\subset\Pic^{4}D
by the surjective map (with
1
-dimensional general fiber)

\psi:D^{(4)}\to\Pic^{4}D;\quad E\mapsto\mathcal{O}_{D}(2H-E),

where
H
is the divisor obtained by intersecting
D
with a line.
Notice that
W
parametrizes sets of
4
points in
D
such that there is a bitangent conic through the points.

To conclude the proof, we only need to show that
I_{l}
and
W
are different threefolds inside
D^{(4)}
.
More precisely, it is enough to see that
I_{l}
is not contained in
W
. This is an immediate consequence of the following claim.

Claim
.

The map
\psi
restricted to
I_{l}
is dominant.

Indeed, let
E=p_{1}+\ldots+p_{4}\in D^{(4)}
a general effective divisor of degree
4
in the quartic
D
.
That is,
\mathcal{O}_{D}(E)
is a general element in

Proposition 3.5
.

The set
\mathcal{C}_{b}^{(3,4)}
(and its preimage in
\mathcal{M}_{6}^{trig}
) is a generically reduced divisor.

Proof.

We use coordinates
([x:y],[u:v])
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We consider the following
1
-dimensional family of
(3,4)
-curves

\mathcal{V}=\left\{P_{\alpha}(x,y,u,v)=0\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\right\}\subset|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}(3,4)|
, where:

P_{\alpha}(x,y,u,v)=(x^{3}+y^{3})u^{4}-2x^{3}u^{3}v+(1-\alpha)x^{3}u^{2}v^{2}+2\alpha x^{3}uv^{3}+(-\alpha x^{3}+x^{2}y+y^{3})v^{4}.

By abuse of notation, we still denote by
P_{\alpha}
the curve with equation
P_{\alpha}(x,y,u,v)=0
.
Notice that the class of
P_{0}
determines a general point inside
\mathcal{C}_{b}^{(3,4)}
since it is smooth and
P_{0}(1,0,u,v)
has two double roots.

It is a well known fact from polynomial algebra
that an equation of the form
Au^{4}+Bu^{3}v+Cu^{2}v^{2}+Duv^{3}+Ev^{4}=0
is biquadratic if, and only if, the following two equations (in terms of the coefficients) hold:

\displaystyle\Delta(A,B,C,D,E)

\displaystyle=256A^{3}E^{3}-192A^{2}BDE^{2}-128A^{2}C^{2}E^{2}+144A^{2}CD^{2}E-27A^{2}D^{4}

\displaystyle+144AB^{2}CE^{2}-6AB^{2}D^{2}E-80ABC^{2}DE+18ABCD^{3}+16AC^{4}E

\displaystyle-4AC^{3}D^{2}-27B^{4}C^{2}+18B^{3}CDE-4B^{3}D^{3}-4B^{2}C^{3}E+B^{2}C^{2}D^{2}=0,

\displaystyle d(A,B,C,D,E)

\displaystyle=64A^{3}E-16A^{2}C^{2}+16AB^{2}C-16A^{2}BD-3B^{4}=0.

By replacing
A=x^{3}+y^{3}
,
B=-2x^{3}
,
C=(1-\alpha)x^{3}
,
D=2\alpha x^{3}
,
E=-\alpha x^{3}+x^{2}y+y^{3}
, we obtain the family of equations
\Delta_{\alpha}(x,y)=0,d_{\alpha}(x,y)=0
, depending on the parameter
\alpha
.
By using resultants it is not hard to see that there is no
\alpha\neq 0
and near
0
, such that
\Delta_{\alpha}
and
d_{\alpha}
share a root.
Therefore, for these
\alpha
,
P_{\alpha}
has not a vertical bitangent line, in particular
P_{0}(x,y,u,v)
and
P_{\alpha}(x,y,u,v)
are not projectively equivalent.
Thus,
\mathcal{V}
defines an actual curve around the class of
P_{0}
in
\mathcal{C}^{(3,4)}
.

Consider the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle

\mathcal{W}=\left\{(P_{\alpha},D_{\alpha})\mid D_{\alpha}\in g_{4}^{1}=|K_{p_{\alpha}}-2g^{1}_{3}|\right\}\to\mathcal{V}.

Around
P_{0}\in\mathcal{V}
we have a section of this
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle given by the fiber at the point
[x:y]=[1:0]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, which is given by the zeroes of the polynomial:

(u-v)^{2}(u^{2}-\alpha v^{2}).

On this section we always have a double root, hence the intersection with the preimage of
\mathcal{C}_{b}^{(3,4)}
corresponds to the locus where the corresponding homogeneous degree
4
equation has a second double root. Due to previous discussion we have to impose the equation

, which in our case is:

-16\alpha^{2}-32\alpha.

Since the linear term of this polynomial in
\alpha
is not zero, we conclude that the locus
\mathcal{C}_{b}^{(3,4)}
is reduced.
∎

4.
The tetragonal construction on

Given the structure of the Prym map in genus 6 (see

[

, where it was proved:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(C_{i},\eta_{i},M_{i})
(
i=1,2,3
) be a tetragonally related triple of smooth Prym curves
(C_{i},\eta_{i})\in\mathcal{R}_{6}
with a
g^{1}_{4}

M_{i}
on
C_{i}

(C_{1},\eta_{1})\in\mathcal{T}^{o}_{6}
is general, then
JC_{2},JC_{3}\in\mathcal{P}_{7}(\mathcal{T}^{o}_{7})

More precisely the pairs
(C_{2},M_{2}),(C_{3},M_{3})
correspond, under Recillas’ trigonal construction, to two covers

of the same trigonal curve

of genus 7. It is thus important to understand Prym varieties of covers of trigonal curves endowed with an odd semicanonical pencil. This is the content of the next proposition:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(T,\mu)
be a trigonal, non-hyperelliptic Prym curve of genus

, and let
C
be the curve of genus
g-1
(endowed with a
g^{1}_{4}

M
) obtained by trigonal construction. If
(T,\mu)\in\mathcal{T}^{o}_{g}

\omega_{C}\otimes M^{-1}\cong\mathcal{O}_{C}\left(2(x_{1}+\ldots+x_{g-4})\right)

for some

Proof.

We will exploit the fact that (a suitable translate of) the Prym-Brill-Noether locus
V^{2}(T,\mu)
is symmetric with a singular 2-torsion point, since
(T,\mu)\in\mathcal{T}^{o}_{g}
. We have

V^{2}(T,\mu)\cong W_{g-4}(C)\cup\left(-W_{g-4}(C)\right)

(see
[
Hoe12
, Section 4.C]
). More precisely, recall that the double cover
{\widetilde{T}}\to T
can be canonically embedded in

as

{\widetilde{T}}=\left\{p+q\in C^{(2)}\mid h^{0}(L(-p-q))>0\right\}\subset C^{(2)}\to\Pic^{2}C.

Thus, for a fixed
p_{0}+q_{0}\in{\widetilde{T}}
, we can consider the (non-canonical) Abel-Prym curve

{\widetilde{T}}\hookrightarrow\Pic^{0}C,\qquad p+q\mapsto\mathcal{O}_{C}(p+q-p_{0}-q_{0}).

Using this embedding, we have a model of

in

as the theta-dual of

V^{2}(T,\mu)=\left\{\xi\in\Pic^{g-2}C\mid{\widetilde{T}}+\xi\subset\Theta_{C}\right\}

(where

denotes the Riemann theta divisor), which consists of two components:

•

W_{g-4}(C)+p_{0}+q_{0}
.

•

K_{C}-r_{0}-s_{0}-W_{g-4}(C)
, where
r_{0},s_{0}\in C
satisfy

.

Note that if
C
is non-hyperelliptic, then
W_{g-4}(C)
does not admit a symmetric translate.
Indeed, suppose that
W_{g-4}(C)-\alpha=-W_{g-4}(C)+\alpha
, that is, there exists
\alpha
such that for all
x_{1}+\ldots+x_{g-4}
there exists

such that
2\alpha=x_{1}+\ldots+x_{g-4}+y_{1}+\ldots+y_{g-4}
.
Then

, and since
\deg\alpha=2g-8
, this implies that the Clifford index of

is 0, hence hyperelliptic.

Therefore,
W_{g-4}(C)
does not admit symmetric translates and

becomes symmetric exactly when its two components are exchanged by the involution
L\mapsto\omega_{C}\otimes L^{-1}
on
\Pic^{g-2}C
(recall that in the torsor
\Pic^{g-2}C
of
JC
, the inversion map
-1
corresponds to taking the adjoint line bundle).
This is equivalent to translate by
\alpha\in\Pic^{0}C
such that

W_{g-4}(C)+p_{0}+q_{0}+\alpha=W_{g-4}(C)+r_{0}+s_{0}-\alpha,

namely
\alpha^{\otimes 2}=\mathcal{O}_{C}(r_{0}+s_{0}-p_{0}-q_{0})
.

Note that any theta-characteristic on
C
(which now plays the role of a 2-torsion point in
\Pic^{g-2}C
) lying in this symmetric model of

is automatically a singular point of
V^{2}(T,\mu)
(since it lies in both components).
The condition of
W_{g-4}(C)+p_{0}+q_{0}+\alpha
containing a theta-characteristic is easily seen to be equivalent to the existence of

such that
2(x_{1}+\ldots+x_{g-4})\in|\omega_{C}\otimes M^{-1}|
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.4

The intersection

Proof.

Indeed, let

We want to prove that

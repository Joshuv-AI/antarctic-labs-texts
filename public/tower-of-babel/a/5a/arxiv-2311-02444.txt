Inequalities on the generalized and the joint spectral radius of bounded sets of positive operators on Banach function and sequence spaces
Katarina Bogdanović ∗

Abstract
. In this article we prove new inequalities for the generalized and the joint spectral radius of bounded sets of positive operators on Banach function and sequence spaces, in particular some inequalities for positive kernel operators on L 2 ​ ( X , μ ) L^{2}(X,\mu) that generalize, extend and refine several of results obtained recently.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

Let
(X,\mathfrak{M},\mu)
be a non -empty space
X
with a
\sigma
-finite positive measure
\mu
on a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathfrak{M}
, i. e.
X
is the union of at most countably numbers of sets of finite measure.We denote by
M(\mu)
the collection of all measurable complex valued functions such that functions being equal almost everywhere will be identified.
A Banach space
L\subseteq M(\mu)
is called a
Banach function space
on
(X,\mathfrak{M},\mu)
if for any
f\in L
and
g\in M(\mu)
the following implication holds
|g|\leq|f|\Rightarrow g\in L
and
\|g\|\leq\|f\|
. We assume that
X
is the carrier of
L
. Carrier is defined to be the complement in
X
of a maximal set
Y\in\mathfrak{M}
such that
f\chi_{Y}=0
a.e., for all
f\in L,
where
\chi_{Y}
stands for the characteristic function of the set
Y\in\mathfrak{M}
(see
[
20
]
for more details). In the special case of
(X,\mathfrak{M},\mu)
, where
X=\mathbb{N}
,
\mathfrak{M}
is a
\sigma
-algebra of all subsets of
\mathbb{N}
and
\mu
is a counting measure on
\mathbb{N}
, the Banach function space will be called
Banach sequence space
. Clearly the possibility that
X=\{1,\ldots,N\}
for some finite
N\in\mathbb{N}
is also allowed.

In the sequel we will refer to a family
\mathcal{L}
as the collection of all Banach sequence spaces

L
satisfying the condition that
e_{n}=\chi_{\{n\}}\in L
and

\|e_{n}\|_{L}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Examples of Banach function spaces are the classical Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(\mu)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, Orlicz spaces (
[
8
]
), Lorentz and Marcinkiewicz spaces (
[
1
]
,
[
9
]
) while examples of Banach sequence spaces are well known
l^{p}
spaces for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

The norm on a Banach function space
L
is called
order continuous norm
if
\|f_{n}\|_{L}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
for every positive, decreasing sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset M(\mu)
such that
\inf\{f_{n}\in M(\mu):n\in\mathbb{N}\}=0
, which is denoted by
f_{n}\downarrow 0
. For
1\leq p<\infty

L^{p}(\mu)
spaces are classical examples of spaces with an order continuous norm.
Moreover, every reflexive Banach function space has an order continuous norm. We will be particularly interested in Banach function spaces
L
such that
L
and its dual space
L^{*}
have order continuous norm. Specially well known among them are spaces
L^{p}(\mu)
,
1<p<\infty
. An example of a such non-reflexive Banach function space is
L=c_{0}\in\mathcal{L}
, where
L^{*}=(c_{0})^{*}=l^{1}\in\mathcal{L}
.

By an
operator
on a Banach function space
L
we always mean a linear operator on
L
. An operator
A
on
L
is said to be
positive
if it maps nonnegative functions to nonnegative ones, that is,
AL_{+}\subset L_{+}
, where
L_{+}
denotes the positive cone
L_{+}=\{f\in L:f\geq 0\;\mathrm{a.e}\}
. Given operators
A
and
B
on
L
, we write
A\geq B
if the operator
A-B
is positive. Recall that a positive operator on
L
is always bounded , i. e., its operator norm

\|A\|=\sup\{\|Ax\|_{L}:x\in L,\|x\|_{L}\leq 1\}=\sup\{\|Ax\|_{L}:x\in L_{+},\|x\|_{L}\leq 1\}

(1.1)

is finite (the second equality in (
1.1
) follows from
|Af|\leq A|f|
for
f\in L
). (see e. g.
[
14
, Theorem 1.7]
).

An operator
A
on a Banach function space
L
is called a
kernel operator
if there exists
\mu\times\mu
-measurable function
a(x,y)
on
X\times X
such that, for all
f\in L
and for almost all
x\in X

\int_{X}|a(x,y)f(y)|\,d\mu(y)<\infty\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ (Af)(x)=\int_{X}a(x,y)f(y)\,d\mu(y).

Recall that a kernel operator
A
is positive iff its kernel
a
is non-negative function almost everywhere.

Let
L
be a Banach function space such that
L
and
L^{*}
have order continuous norms and let
A
and
B
be positive kernel operators on
L
. Then the Hausdorff measure of non-compactness of
A
, denoted by
\gamma(A)
, is defined as follows

\gamma(A)=\inf\left\{\delta>0:\;\;\mathrm{there}\;\;\mathrm{is}\;\;\mathrm{a}\;\;\mathrm{finite}\;\;M\subset L\;\;\mathrm{such}\;\;\mathrm{that}\;\;A(D_{L})\subset M+\delta D_{L}\right\},

where
D_{L}=\{f\in L:\|f\|_{L}\leq 1\}
. The basic properties of the Hausdorff measure of non-compactness are
\gamma(A)\leq\|A\|
,
\gamma(A+B)\leq\gamma(A)+\gamma(B)
,
\gamma(AB)\leq\gamma(A)\gamma(B)
, and
\gamma(\alpha A)=\alpha\gamma(A)
for
\alpha\geq 0
. Moreover,
0\leq A\leq B
implies
\gamma(A)\leq\gamma(B)
(see e.g.
[
10
, Corollary 4.3.7 and Corollary 3.7.3]
).

The essential spectral radius of
A
, denoted by
\rho_{ess}(A)
is defined as follows

\rho_{ess}(A)=max\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\sigma_{ess}(A)\}

where
\sigma_{ess}(A)
is the essential spectral radius of
A
(see
[
10
]
for more details). Then

\rho_{ess}(A)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\gamma(A^{j})^{1/j}=\inf_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\gamma(A^{j})^{1/j}

(1.2)

Recall that if
L=L^{2}(X,\mu)

\gamma(A^{*})=\gamma(A)
and
\rho_{ess}(A^{*})=\rho_{ess}(A)
, where
A^{*}
denotes the adjoint of
A
. Note that the equalities (
1.2
) and

\rho_{ess}(A^{*})=\rho_{ess}(A)
are valid for any bounded operator
A
on a given complex Banach space
L
(see e.g.
[
10
, Theorem 4.3.13 and Proposition 4.3.11]
).

Let
A
and
B
be positive kernel operators on a Banach function space
L
with kernels
a
and
b
respectively, and
\alpha\geq 0
.
The
Hadamard or (Schur) product

A\circ B
of
A
and
B
is the kernel operator with kernel equal to
a(x,y)b(x,y)
at point
(x,y)\in X\times X
, i. e.
A\circ B

is the positive kernel operator given by

(A\circ B)f(x)=\int_{X}a(x,y)b(x,y)f(y)\,d\mu(y),

which can be defined, in general, only on some order ideal of
L
. Similarly, the
Hadamard or (Schur) power

A^{(\alpha)}
of
A
is the kernel operator with kernel equal to
(a(x,y))^{\alpha}
at point
(x,y)\in X\times X
,
which can be defined only on some order ideal of
L
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
be positive kernel operators on a Banach function space
L
, and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}
positive numbers such that
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}=1
. Then the
Hadamard weighted geometric mean

A=A_{1}^{(\alpha_{1})}\circ A_{2}^{(\alpha_{2})}\circ\cdots\circ A_{m}^{(\alpha_{m})}
of the operators

is a positive kernel operator defined on the whole space

, since
A\leq\alpha_{1}A_{1}+\alpha_{2}A_{2}+\ldots+\alpha_{m}A_{m}
by the inequality between weighted arithmetic and geometric means.
A matrix
A=[a_{ij}]_{i,j\in R}
is called
non-negative
if
a_{ij}\geq 0
for all
i,j\in R
.
For matrices

and
B
, we write

if the matrix
A-B
is non-negative.

By an
operator
on a Banach sequence space
L
we always
mean a linear operator on
L
. We say that a non-negative matrix

defines an operator on

(Ax)_{i}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{ij}x_{j}

and

operator on

\rho(\Sigma)=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\;[\sup_{A\in\Sigma^{m}}\rho(A)]^{1/m}

\hat{\rho}(\Sigma)=\lim_{m\to\infty}[\sup_{A\in\Sigma^{m}}\|A\|]^{1/m}.

The generalized essential spectral radius of

\rho_{ess}(\Sigma)=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\;[\sup_{A\in\Sigma^{m}}\rho_{ess}(A)]^{1/m}

and is equal to

\rho_{ess}(\Sigma)=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\;[\sup_{A\in\Sigma^{m}}\rho_{ess}(A)]^{1/m}.

The joint essential spectral radius of

\hat{\rho}_{ess}(\Sigma)=\lim_{m\to\infty}[\sup_{A\in\Sigma^{m}}\gamma(A)]^{1/m}.

for
j=1,\ldots,\frac{m}{2}
. Let
\Omega_{i}=\Sigma_{\nu(i)}\cdots\Sigma_{\nu(m)}\Sigma_{\nu(1)}\cdots\Sigma_{\nu(i-1)}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
and
r\in\{\rho,\hat{\rho}\}

(i)
Then

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{1}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Psi_{m}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\|\leq r^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{1}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Sigma_{m}^{(\frac{1}{m})})\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\leq r^{\frac{1}{2m}}(\Omega_{1}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Omega_{m}^{(\frac{1}{m})})\leq r^{\frac{1}{2m}}(\Sigma_{\nu(1)}\cdots\Sigma_{\nu(m)})

(3.11)

(ii)
Let
\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{m}
be bounded sets of nonnegative matrices that define operators on
l^{2}(R)
. If
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{m}
then

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{1}^{(\alpha)}\circ\cdots\circ\Psi_{m}^{(\alpha)}\|\leq r^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{1}^{(\alpha)}\circ\cdots\circ\Sigma_{m}^{(\alpha)})\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\leq r^{\frac{1}{2m}}(\Omega_{1}^{(\alpha)}\circ\cdots\circ\Omega_{m}^{(\alpha)})\leq r(\Sigma_{\nu(1)}\cdots\Sigma_{\nu(m)})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}

(3.12)

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
and using commutativity of Hadamard product we have

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{1}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Psi_{m}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\|=r((\Psi_{1}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Psi_{m}^{(\frac{1}{m})})^{*}(\Psi_{1}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Psi_{m}^{(\frac{1}{m})}))^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle=r(((\Psi_{\tau(1)}^{*})^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ(\Psi_{\tau(m-1)}^{*})^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ(\Psi_{\tau(2)}^{*})^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ(\Psi_{\tau(m)}^{*})^{(\frac{1}{m})})

\displaystyle\cdot(\Psi_{\tau(2)}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Psi_{\tau(m)}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\Psi_{\tau(1)}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Psi_{\tau(m-1)}^{(\frac{1}{m})}))^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\leq r((\Psi_{\tau(1)}^{*}\Psi_{\tau(2)})^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ(\Psi_{\tau(m-1)}^{*}\Psi_{\tau(m)})^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ(\Psi_{\tau(2)}^{*}\Psi_{\tau(1)})^{(\frac{1}{m})}\;\;\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle\circ\cdots\circ(\Psi_{\tau(m)}^{*}\Psi_{\tau(m-1)})^{(\frac{1}{m})})^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle=r(\Sigma_{1}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Sigma_{m}^{(\frac{1}{m})})^{\frac{1}{2}}=r(\Sigma_{\nu(1)}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Sigma_{\nu(m)}^{(\frac{1}{m})})^{\frac{1}{2}},\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;

which proves the first inequality in (
3.11
). To prove the second and the third inequality in (
3.11
) observe that

\displaystyle(\Sigma_{\nu(1)}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Sigma_{\nu(m)}^{(\frac{1}{m})})^{m}=(\Sigma_{\nu(1)}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\Sigma_{\nu(2)}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Sigma_{\nu(m)}^{(\frac{1}{m})})(\Sigma_{\nu(2)}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ(\Sigma_{\nu(3)}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Sigma_{\nu(1)}^{(\frac{1}{m})})

\displaystyle\cdots(\Sigma_{\nu(m)}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\Sigma_{\nu(1)}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Sigma_{\nu(m-1)}^{(\frac{1}{m})})\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;

and

\displaystyle r(\Sigma_{\nu(1)}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Sigma_{\nu(m)}^{(\frac{1}{m})})\leq r((\Sigma_{\nu(1)}\Sigma_{\nu(2)}\cdots\Sigma_{\nu(m)})^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ(\Sigma_{\nu(2)}\Sigma_{\nu(3)}\cdots\Sigma_{\nu(1)})^{(\frac{1}{m})}

\displaystyle\circ\cdots\circ(\Sigma_{\nu(m)}\Sigma_{\nu(1)}\cdots\Sigma_{\nu(m-1)})^{(\frac{1}{m})})^{\frac{1}{m}}=r(\Omega_{1}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Omega_{m}^{(\frac{1}{m})})^{\frac{1}{m}}

since
r(\Sigma^{m})=r(\Sigma)^{m}
and by
Theorem
1.3
(i). It follows that

\displaystyle r(\Sigma_{\nu(1)}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Sigma_{\nu(m)}^{(\frac{1}{m})})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq r(\Omega_{1}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Omega_{m}^{(\frac{1}{m})})^{\frac{1}{2m}}\leq(r(\Omega_{1})\cdots r(\Omega_{m}))^{\frac{1}{2m^{2}}}

\displaystyle=r(\Sigma_{\nu(1)}\cdots\Sigma_{\nu(m)})^{\frac{1}{2m}}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;

according to
Theorem
1.3
(i) and using the fact that
r(\Omega_{1})=\ldots=r(\Omega_{m})\!=r(\Sigma_{\nu(1)}\cdots\Sigma_{\nu(m)})
which completes the proof of (
3.11
). Inequalities (
3.12
) are proved in a similar way by applying
Theorem
1.3
(ii).
∎

Theorem 3.11
.

,

then

\|\Psi_{1}^{(\alpha)}\circ\cdots\circ\Psi_{m}^{(\alpha)}\|\leq r(\Sigma_{1}^{(\alpha)}\circ\cdots\circ\Sigma_{m}^{(\alpha)})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq r(\Sigma_{1}^{(\alpha)}\circ\cdots\circ\Sigma_{\frac{m}{2}}^{(\alpha)})

=r((\Psi_{\tau(1)}^{*}\Psi_{\tau(2)})^{(\alpha)}\circ(\Psi_{\tau(3)}^{*}\Psi_{\tau(4)})^{(\alpha)}\circ\cdots\circ(\Psi_{\tau(m-1)}^{*}\Psi_{\tau(m)})^{(\alpha)})

\leq r(\Theta_{1}^{(\alpha)}\circ\Theta_{2}^{(\alpha)}\circ\cdots\circ\Theta_{\frac{m}{2}}^{(\alpha)})^{\frac{2}{m}}\leq r(\Psi_{\tau(1)}^{*}\Psi_{\tau(2)}\Psi_{\tau(3)}^{*}\Psi_{\tau(4)}\cdots\Psi_{\tau(m-1)}^{*}\Psi_{\tau(m)})^{\alpha}

(3.13)

Proof.

By the first inequality in (
3.12
) and using

1.3
(ii) we have

Theorem 3.13
.

\leq r((\Omega_{1})^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ(\Omega_{m})^{(\frac{1}{m})})^{\frac{1}{2m}}\leq r(\Psi_{\tau(1)}^{*}\Psi_{\nu(1)}\cdots\Psi_{\tau(m)}^{*}\Psi_{\nu(m)})^{\frac{1}{2m}}

(3.15)

Proof.

We prove (
3.14
). By Lemma
3.1
, by commutativity of Hadamard product and by

1.3
(i) we have

\|\Psi_{1}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Psi_{m}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\|=r((\Psi_{1}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Psi_{m}^{(\frac{1}{m})})^{*}(\Psi_{1}^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ\Psi_{m}^{(\frac{1}{m})}))^{\frac{1}{2}}=

((\Psi_{\tau(1)}^{*}\Psi_{\nu(1)})^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ\cdots\circ(\Psi_{\tau(m)}^{*}\Psi_{\nu(m)})^{(\frac{1}{m})})^{m}=((\Psi_{\tau(1)}^{*}\Psi_{\nu(1)})^{(\frac{1}{m})}\circ

\cdots\circ(\Psi_{\tau(m)}^{*}\Psi_{\nu(m)})^{(\frac{1}{m})})\cdot

Constructing reflection-symmetric flexible realisations of graphs
Sean Dewar, Georg Grasegger, Jan Legerský

Abstract
We study reflection-symmetric realisations of symmetric graphs in the plane that allow a continuous symmetry and edge-length preserving deformation. To do so, we identify a necessary combinatorial condition on graphs with reflection-symmetric flexible realisations. This condition is based on a specific type of edge colouring, where edges are assigned one of three colours in a symmetric way. From some of these colourings we also construct concrete reflection-symmetric realisations with their corresponding symmetry preserving motion. We study also a specific class of reflection-symmetric realisations consisting of triangles and parallelograms. Keywords: flexible framework, rigidity, reflection symmetry, edge coloring MSC2020: 52C25, 70B15, 05C70

Introduction

A
realisation
of a graph
G=(V_{G},E_{G})
is the placement of the vertices in the plane, i.e. a map
p:V_{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}

We define a realisation to be
rigid
if any continuous edge-length preserving deformation corresponds to a rigid body motion.
However, graphs with rigid realisations may also have realisations that can be continuously deformed into non-congruent realisations while the edge lengths are preserved.
For some graphs these so-called
flexible realisations
are difficult to find,
and their study has a long history.
For instance, Dixon
[
6
]
studied flexible realisations of the complete bipartite graph
K_{3,3}
.

Symmetric properties of graphs and realisation in relation to rigidity properties have been widely studied.
See for instance
[
1
,
7
,
15
,
20
,
23
]
,
or
[
21
,
24
]
for an overview.
In this paper we consider reflection symmetry in particular.
Rigidity under this particular assumption has been previously investigated
in
[
22
,
23
]
and recently in
[
18
]
.
Flexibility and motions of symmetric graphs have also previously been
considered in
[
16
,
17
,
19
]
.
However, each of them either use different symmetries, a different metric, or different classes of graphs than we do here.

Recently the existence of flexible realisations in the plane has been analysed
more deeply
[
10
,
11
]
.
The main idea therein is to relate the flexibility to a special type of edge
colouring by two colours, so-called
NAC-colourings
(“No Almost Cycles”, see

[
10
]
).
NAC-colourings characterise the existence of a flexible realisation in the plane
and give a construction of one such realisation with its flexible motion.
The
SageMath
package
FlexRiLoG

[
9
,
12
]
offers functionality for
finding such colourings and constructing their corresponding motions.
The method of NAC-colourings has since been used for specific classes of graphs,
such as periodic
[
2
]
and infinite graphs, including Penrose patterns
[
5
]
.
Also, the study of rigid and flexible frameworks consisting of parallelograms
[
13
]
and additional triangles
[
14
]
was recently developed using properties of NAC-colourings.
In
[
3
,
4
]
, these methods were extended for constructing flexible realisations for rotation-symmetric graphs.

The aim of this paper is to analyse reflection-symmetric realisations of symmetric graphs.
In the spirit of NAC-colourings, we define a new type of edge colouring that allows us
to give some necessary and sufficient conditions on graphs having a reflection-symmetric flex.
These new colourings are tailored for reflection symmetry but are based on NAC-colourings (which are recalled therefore in
Section

2
).
In
Section

3
, we recall notions from symmetry and define our edge-colouring.
We obtain a necessary condition for graphs having reflection-symmetric motions, which is found in
Section

4

We also construct reflection-symmetric flexes for some of the graphs satisfying the necessary condition in
Section

Section

In

to the general or rotation-symmetric case.

Reflection-symmetric flex implies RS-colouring

, since the norm of the sum is constant.
Since also the angle of the sum is constant,
the angle between
\beta_{t}s
and
\beta^{\prime}_{t}s^{\prime}
must be independent of
t
,
particularly,
\beta_{t}
and
\beta^{\prime}_{t}
are independent of
t
.

But that implies that all blue or all red edges keep their angle
along the flex with the gold edge
\bar{u}\bar{v}
,
which contradicts that they have the same value under the valuation
\nu
used to construct
\delta
:
if edges

keep their mutual angle
\varphi
,
then
\nu(W_{u_{1},v_{1}})=\nu(e^{i\varphi}W_{u_{2},v_{2}})=\nu(e^{i\varphi})+\nu(W_{u_{2},v_{2}})=\nu(W_{u_{2},v_{2}})
.
Therefore, there are two (w.l.o.g.) blue edges

of
C
whose
mutual angle changes along the flex, so by
Lemma

4.4
we can construct
a valuation that distinguishes them and yields an active pseudo-RS-colouring
\delta^{\prime}
with
\delta^{\prime}(e_{1})\neq\delta^{\prime}(e_{2})
.
∎

Combining the lemmas, we can show that a reflection-symmetric flex implies the existence of an RS-colouring.

Theorem 4.7
.

If
(G,p)
is reflection-symmetric flexible,
then
G
has an RS-colouring.

Proof.

Since there must be a pair of edges whose angle is not constant along a reflection-symmetric flex,
there exists an algebraic motion
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{V}_{s}(G,p)

and valuation
\nu
on
\mathbb{C}(\mathcal{M})
by
Lemma

4.4
.
Additionally,
the set
S:=\{0\}\cup\{\left|\nu(W_{u,v})\right|\colon uv\in E_{G}\}

has at least two elements.
We fix
\alpha\in S\setminus\{\max S\}
to obtain
an active pseudo-RS-colouring
\delta
by
Lemma

4.5
.
By
Lemma

4.6
, it is an RS-colouring.
∎

We note that the necessary condition on the existence of a reflection-symmetric flex is not strong enough
in the sense that there are examples of RS-colourings that cannot be active for any motion,

In order to strengthen the necessary condition, we need the following concept,

Definition 4.8
.

Let

be reflection-symmetric graph with reflection
\sigma
.
For a reflection-symmetric graph

such that
G
is a spanning subgraph of
H
,
let

be the set of all pairs
\{u,v\}
such that
u
is an invariant vertex,

v(\sigma v)\in E_{G}
and there is
a path from
u
to
v
that is gold in all RS-colourings of
H
.
If

is a sequence of reflection-symmetric graphs with reflection
\sigma
such that

(i)

G=G_{0}
,

(ii)

G_{i}=(V_{G},E_{G_{i-1}}\cup\operatorname{gp}_{G}(G_{i-1}))
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and

(iii)

\operatorname{gp}_{G}(G_{n})=\emptyset
,

then
G_{n}
is called the gold-closure of
G
, denoted by
\operatorname{cl}_{\text{gold}}(G)
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Any reflection-symmetric algebraic motion
\mathcal{M}
of a reflection-symmetric framework

is a reflection-symmetric algebraic motion of
(\operatorname{cl}_{\text{gold}}(G),p)
.

w.r.t.

are exactly the restrictions of
the active RS-colourings of
\operatorname{cl}_{\text{gold}}(G)
and every edge in

is gold
in all RS-colourings of
\operatorname{cl}_{\text{gold}}(G)
.

Proof.

In order to prove that a reflection-symmetric algebraic motion
\mathcal{M}
of
(G,p)

is also a reflection-symmetric algebraic motion of
(\operatorname{cl}_{\text{gold}}(G),p)
,
it suffices to show that
\|p^{\prime}(u)-p^{\prime}(v)\|=\|p(u)-p(v)\|

for all
uv\in\operatorname{gp}_{G}(G)
and
p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}
,
then the statement follows by induction.
Let
uv\in\operatorname{gp}_{G}(G)
be such that
u=\sigma u
and
v\sigma v\in E_{G}
.
Let

be a path from
u
to

that is gold for all RS-colourings of
G
.
All edges in

keep their mutual angles along
\mathcal{M}
,
otherwise, by
Item

(i)
of
Lemma

4.4
, and

Lemmas

4.5
and
4.6
,
there would be an active RS-coloring distinguishing them, which is not possible by the assumption.
Therefore, the distance between

and

is constant along the motion

.
This distance is non-zero since

is on the

while

is not on the

is an edge.
Since

is gold in all RS-colourings of

\operatorname{cl}_{\text{gold}}(G)
.
Hence, the edge

is also gold in all RS-colourings of
\operatorname{cl}_{\text{gold}}(G)
.
The fact the active RS-colourings of

\mathcal{M}
are exactly the restrictions of
the active RS-colourings of
\operatorname{cl}_{\text{gold}}(G)
w.r.t.
\mathcal{M}
holds since algebraic motion is the same for both graphs.
∎

As a direct consequence, we obtain the following necessary condition on the existence of a reflection-symmetric flex.

Corollary 4.10

If
(G,p)
is reflection-symmetric flexible,
then
\operatorname{cl}_{\text{gold}}(G)

Definition 6.1

Let
G

\triangle

An equivalence class of the reflexive-transitive closure
of the union

For a walk

\sum_{(u,v)\in r\cap W}
means
we sum up over all edges
(u_{i},u_{i+1})
with
1\leq i<k

u_{i}u_{i+1}\in r
.

Definition 6.2
.

7
Conclusion

We have shown that the existence of an RS-colouring is a necessary condition
for the existence of a reflection-symmetric flex.
While in the general or rotation-symmetric case,
the existence of a (rotation-symmetric) NAC-colouring is also a sufficient condition for the existence of a (rotation-symmetric) flex,
the situation in the reflection-symmetric case is significantly more difficult.
The reason is that for a NAC-colouring
\delta
, there exists an algebraic motion
such that its active NAC-colourings are exactly
\delta
and its conjugate.
On the other hand, the graph in
Figure

3
has no RS-colouring without an almost red-blue cycle.
Hence, each of its motions (for instance the one constructed using
Theorem

5.2
) has at least two non-conjugated active RS-colourings.

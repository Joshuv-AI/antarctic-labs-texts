Expansions in completions of global function fields
Chunlin Wang

Abstract
. It is well known that any power series over a finite field represents a rational function if and only if its sequence of coefficients is ultimately periodic. The famous Christol’s Theorem states that a power series over a finite field is algebraic if and only if its sequence of coefficients is p p -automatic. In this paper, we extend these two results to expansions of elements in the completion of a global function field under a nontrivial valuation. As application of our generalization of Christol’s theorem, we answer some questions about β \beta -expansions of formal Laurent series over finite fields.

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number and
q
be a power of
p
. Denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q}
the finite field of
q
elements. The polynomial ring, power series ring and rational function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are denoted by
\mathbb{F}_{q}[z]
,
\mathbb{F}_{q}[[z]]
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
respectively. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}((z))
be the quotient field of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[[z]]
. For

f(z):=\sum_{n=m}^{+\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}((z)),

we say that
f
is
rational
(over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
) if
f(z)=P(z)/Q(z)
for some polynomials
P,Q
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and say
f
is
algebraic
(over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
) if there exists a polynomial
R(z,w)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[z,w]
such that
R(z,f(z))=0
.

Let
S:=\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
be the sequence of coefficients of
f
. The sequence
S
is called
ultimately periodic
if there exist integers
N\geq m
and
L>0
such that
a_{n}=a_{n+L}
for all
n\geq N
. For integers
i,j,k
with
k>0,i\geq 0
, and
0\leq j<k^{i}
, denote by
S_{i,j}
the subsequence
\{a_{nk^{i}+j-m}\}_{n\geq 0}
of
S
. We call
S

k
-automatic
if its
k
-kernel
, i.e. the set of subsequences

\{S_{i,j}:i\geq 0,0\leq j<k^{i}\},

is finite. It is worth mentioning that this definition is just one of the equivalent definitions of automatic sequences. For more on automatic sequences, we refer the reader to Allouche and Shallit
[
3
]
, and references there.

For power series over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have the following two well known facts about
f
and its sequence of coefficients.

Fact A
:
f
is rational if and only if
S
is ultimately periodic.

Fact B
:
f
is algebraic if and only if
S
is
p
-automatic.

Fact A is elementary but useful. Fact B, known as Christol’s Theorem (see
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
), builds a deep connection between computer science and arithmetic in global function fields.

Generalizations and analogues of Christol’s theorem are considered in literature. Salon
[
15
,
16
]
generalized Christol’s Theorem to multivariable power series over a finite field. Independently, Sharif and Woodcock
[
20
]
, and Harase
[
8
]
generalized Christol’s Theorem to the case of multivariable power series over an infinite field of positive characteristic. An extension of Christol’s theorem to generalized algebraic power series is given by Kedlaya
[
12
]
. Quantitative version of Christol’s theorem is studied in
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
1
,
4
]
and
[
2
]
. The automaticity of
\beta
-expansion of formal Laurent series is considered in
[
11
]
and
[
18
]
.

Seen form the viewpoint of valuation theory, the power series ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[[z]]
is just the completion of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[z]
under the valuation associated to the prime
z\mathbb{F}_{q}[z]
, and the power series expansion of a rational function
P/Q
, where
P,Q\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[z]
, is just a special case of expansions of elements in the completion of a global function field under a nontrivial valuation. This inspires us to consider generalizations of Fact A and B for expansions of elements in the completion of a global function field.

Let
K
be a
global function field
of characteristic
p
, i.e.,
K
is a field containing
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and an element
x
which is transcendental over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
[K:\mathbb{F}_{p}(x)]<\infty
. The algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
in
K
is called the constant field of
K
. A valuation on
K
is a function
v:K^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

(1).
v(a+b)\geq\inf\{v(a),v(b)\}
,

(2).
v(ab)=v(a)+v(b)
.

We say
v
is nontrivial if
v(x)\neq 0
for at least one
x\in K^{*}
, and say
v
is normalized if
v(K^{*})=\mathbb{Z}
. For convenience we define
v(0):=+\infty
. Let
A:=\{x\in K:v(x)\geq 0\}
be the valuation ring of
v
and
M:=\{x\in K:v(x)>0\}
be the maximal ideal of
A
. The valuation
v
induces a topological structure on
K
, under which
K
has a completion, denoted by
K_{v}
. Respectively denote by
A_{v}
and
M_{v}
the completions of
A
and
M
. Hereafter we always suppose
v
is normalized unless otherwise stated. For any
\pi\in K_{v}
with
v(\pi)>0
, denote by
r:=|A_{v}/\pi A_{v}|
. Let

\Gamma:=\{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{r}\}\subset A_{v}

be a
complete set of representatives
of
A_{v}/\pi A_{v}
, that is,
\{\bar{\gamma_{i}}:1\leq i\leq n\}=A_{v}/\pi A_{v}
, where
\bar{\gamma_{i}}
denotes the reduction of
\gamma_{i}
modulo
\pi A_{v}
. Then every element
x\in K_{v}
can be uniquely expressed as an expansion

(1.1)

x=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\pi^{n},

where
m
is an integer depending only on
x
, and
a_{n}\in\Gamma
for all
n\geq m
. We call (
1.1
) the expansion of
x
with respect to
(\Gamma,\pi)
, or simply expansion of
x
if
(\Gamma,\pi)
is clear from the context. The sequence of coefficients of (
1.1
) is
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
. An element
x\in K_{v}
is called rational if
x\in K
, and is called algebraic if
x
is algebraic over
K
.
We say
(\Gamma,\pi)
has
property A
(respectively,
property B
) if Fact A (respectively, Fact B) holds for expansions with respect to
(\Gamma,\pi)
.
Our main purpose is to find equivalent description for
(\Gamma,\pi)
that has property A or B. This goal is achieved for
(\Gamma,\pi)
having property A.

Theorem 1.1
.

(\Gamma,\pi)
has property A if and only if the following two are true:

(i)

\pi
and elements of
\Gamma
are rational.

(ii)

[K:\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)]=[K_{v}:\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))]
.

For
(\Gamma,\pi)
with property B, we have a conjectural result.

Conjecture 1.2
.

(\Gamma,\pi)
has property
B
if and only if
\pi
and elements of
\Gamma
are algebraic over
K
.

It is easy to show
\pi
and elements of
\Gamma
are algebraic if
(\Gamma,\pi)
has property B (see Lemma
3.1
). An equivalent but more concrete statement of Conjecture
1.2
is given at the end of Section 3. Though we could not verify Conjecture
1.2
for its full generality, we do confirm it when
\Gamma
is additively closed (see Theorem
3.5
), where we say
\Gamma
is additively closed if
\mu+\nu\in\Gamma
for all
\mu,\nu\in\Gamma
.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we study expansions of rational elements and prove Theorem
1.1
. In section 3, we study expansions of algebraic elements and prove Conjecture
1.2
for additively closed
\Gamma
, which gives a generalization of Christol’s theorem. In section 4, we use our generalization of Christol’s theorem to prove a result on
\beta
-expansions of formal Laurent series (see Theorem
4.1
), which answers questions posed in
[
11
]
and
[
18
]
.

2.
Expansions of rational elements

Denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
the field generated by
\pi
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and by
\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))
the completion of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
in
K_{v}
. Then
[K_{v}:\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))]=ef
(cf.
[
5
, Theorem 4.14]
). Moreover,
\pi A_{v}=M_{v}^{e}
, which implies
|A_{v}/\pi A_{v}|=|A_{v}/M_{v}|^{e}=q^{ef}
. Thus
A_{v}/\pi A_{v}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear space of dimension
ef
. Furthermore if
\pi\in A
, then
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
is a subfield of
K
and
A/\pi A\cong A_{v}/\pi A_{v}
. Choose
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{ef}\in A
whose reductions modulo
\pi
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent, i.e., the reductions of
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{ef}
modulo
\pi
consist of an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-basis of
A/\pi A
. Then
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{ef}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))
-linearly independent, hence consist of an
\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))
-basis of
K_{v}
. On the other hand,
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{ef}
are also
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
-linearly independent, which implies
[K:\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)]\geq[K_{v}:\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))]
.

This section is mainly devoted to proving Theorem
1.1
. First we need some lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
\pi\in A
and
\Gamma\subset A
. If
[K:\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)]>[K_{v}:\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))]
, then
(\Gamma,\pi)
can not have property A.

Proof.

Write
h:=[K:\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)]
. Choose
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{ef}\subset A
such that their reductions modulo
\pi A
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{ef}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
. Extend
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{ef}
to an
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
-basis of
K
, say
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{ef},\alpha_{ef+1},\ldots,\alpha_{h}\in A
. Then
x\in K
if and only if there exist uniquely determined elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{h}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
such that
x=x_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+x_{h}\alpha_{h}
.
In particular, for
\gamma\in\Gamma\subset A
, write

\gamma:=\gamma^{(1)}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\gamma^{(h)}\alpha_{h},

where
\gamma^{(i)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
.
Note that though
v(\gamma)\geq 0
, it is possible
v(\gamma^{(i)})<0
. For any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, define

m(\gamma):=\frac{1}{e}\min_{1\leq i\leq h}\{v(\gamma^{(i)})\}.

Then each
\gamma^{(i)}
is of the form

\gamma^{(i)}=\sum_{n\geq m(\gamma)}b_{n,\gamma}^{(i)}\pi^{n},

where
b_{n,\gamma}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
b_{m(\gamma),\gamma}^{(i)}\neq 0
for at least one
i
. Let

m(\Gamma):=\min_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\{m(\gamma)\}.

Then obviously
m(\Gamma)\leq 0
. Choose
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
such that
m(\gamma^{\prime})=m(\Gamma)=:m
. Set

\Lambda_{\gamma^{\prime}}:=\{(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{h})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{h}:\lambda_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{h}\alpha_{h}\equiv\gamma^{\prime}\mod\pi A\}.

Since the reductions of
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{ef}
consist of an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-basis of
A/\pi A
, for any
\lambda_{ef+1},\dots,\lambda_{h}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, there exists a unique
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{ef})
such that

(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{h})\in\Lambda_{\gamma^{\prime}}
. So
|\Lambda_{\gamma^{\prime}}|=q^{h-ef}
. Therefore whether
(b_{m,\gamma^{\prime}}^{(1)},\ldots,b_{m,\gamma^{\prime}}^{(h)})
belongs to
\Lambda_{\gamma^{\prime}}
or not, we may choose
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{h})\in\Lambda_{\gamma^{\prime}}
that is different from
(b_{m,\gamma^{\prime}}^{(1)},\ldots,b_{m,\gamma^{\prime}}^{(h)})
. Take

(2.1)

x=x_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+x_{h}\alpha_{h}.

Then
x\equiv\gamma\mod\pi A
. Let

x=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\pi^{n}

be the expansion of
x
with respect to
(\Gamma,\pi)
. Then
a_{0}=\gamma^{\prime}
. Write each
a_{n}
as

a_{n}=a_{n}^{(1)}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}^{(h)}\alpha_{h},

where
a_{n}^{(i)}=\gamma^{(i)}
for
1\leq i\leq h
if
a_{n}=\gamma\in\Gamma
. Then

(2.2)

x=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{i=1}^{h}a_{n}^{(i)}\alpha_{i}\pi^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}^{(i)}\pi^{n}.

Suppose
(\Gamma,\pi)
has property A. Then
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is ultimately periodic. Since
a_{n}^{(1)},\ldots,a_{n}^{(h)}
are uniquely determined by
a_{n}
,
\{a_{n}^{(i)}\}_{n\geq 0}
is also ultimately periodic for
1\leq i\leq h
. This implies
(1-\pi^{L})\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}^{(i)}\pi^{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
for some positive integer
L
, and hence
\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}^{(i)}\pi^{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
. Hence both (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are linear expressions of
x
with respect to the basis
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{h}
. Thus we have

x_{i}=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}^{(i)}\pi^{n}{\rm~for~}1\leq i\leq h.

Recall
a_{0}=\gamma^{\prime}
and
m=m(\gamma^{\prime})=m(\Gamma)\leq 0
. We deduce

\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}^{(i)}\pi^{n}-b_{m,\gamma^{\prime}}^{(i)}\pi^{m}=x_{i}-b_{m,\gamma^{\prime}}^{(i)}\pi^{m}\in\pi^{m+1}\mathbb{F}_{q}[[\pi]],

which could not happen if
m<0
. So we must have
m=0
. It then implies
m=0
and
x_{i}=b_{m,\gamma^{\prime}}^{(i)}
as both
x_{i}
and
b_{m,\gamma^{\prime}}^{(i)}
are in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This contradicts to
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{h})\neq(b_{m,\gamma^{\prime}}^{(1)},\ldots,b_{m,\gamma^{\prime}}^{(h)})
. Thus
(\Gamma,\pi)
can not have property A. the proof of Lemma
2.1
is finished.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
\pi\in A
and
[K:\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)]=[K_{v}:\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))]
. For
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{ef}\in A
such that their reductions modulo
\pi A
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
\Gamma=\mathbb{F}_{q}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\mathbb{F}_{q}\alpha_{s}
.
Then
(\Gamma,\pi)
has property A.

Proof.

Since
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{ef}\in A
consist of an
\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))
-basis of
K_{v}
, there exist
x_{1},\ldots,x_{ef}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))
such that
x=x_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+x_{ef}\alpha_{ef}
. Write each
x_{i}
as

x_{i}:=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}^{(i)}\pi^{n},a_{n}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

By letting
a_{n}:=a_{n}^{(1)}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}^{(ef)}\alpha_{ef}\in\Gamma
, we derive

x=\sum_{n\geq m}(a_{n}^{(1)}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}^{(ef)}\alpha_{ef})\pi^{n}=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\pi^{n}.

So
\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\pi^{n}
is the expansion of
x
with respect to
(\Gamma,\pi)
.

Note that under the hypothesis
[K:\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)]=[K_{v}:\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))]
,
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{ef}
also consist of an
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
-basis of
K
. We deduce that
x\in K
if and only if
x_{1},\ldots,x_{ef}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
, if and only if each sequence
\{a_{n}^{(i)}\}_{n\geq m}
is ultimately periodic, if and only if
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
is ultimately periodic. Hence
(\Gamma,\pi)
has property A. This shows Lemma
2.2
.

\pi\in A
,
\Gamma\subset A
, and
[K:\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)]=[K_{v}:\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))]
. Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{ef}
be elements of
A
whose reductions modulo

are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{ef}
consist of both
\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))
-basis of
K_{v}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
-basis of

\gamma\in\Gamma
can be write as

\gamma=\gamma^{(1)}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\gamma^{(ef)}\alpha_{ef}

with
\gamma^{(i)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)

(\Gamma,\pi)
has property

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\pi\in A
,
\Gamma\subset A
, and
[K:\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)]=[K_{v}:\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))]
. Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{ef}
be elements of
A
whose reductions modulo
\pi A
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. If
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}[\pi]\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\mathbb{F}_{q}[\pi]\alpha_{ef}
, then
(\Gamma,\pi)
has property A.

Proof.

For
x\in K_{v}
, let
x=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\pi^{n}
be the expansion of
x
with respect to
(\Gamma,\pi)
. If
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
is ultimately periodic, then it is easy to see
x
is rational.

Conversely let
x
be rational. We need to show
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
is ultimately periodic. For
\gamma\in\Gamma
, write
\gamma=\gamma^{(1)}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\gamma^{(ef)}\alpha_{ef}
, where
\gamma^{(i)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)
for
1\leq i\leq ef
. Since
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}[\pi]\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\mathbb{F}_{q}[\pi]\alpha_{ef}
,
each
\gamma^{(i)}
is a polynomial in
\pi
. Set

d:=\max_{\gamma\in\Gamma,1\leq i\leq ef}\deg\gamma^{(i)},

where
\deg\gamma^{(i)}
is the degree of
\gamma^{(i)}
as a polynomial in
\pi
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then we can write each
a_{n}\in\Gamma
as
a_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{ef}a_{n}^{(i)}\alpha_{i}
, where
a_{n}^{(i)}
is of the form

a_{n}^{(i)}=\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{n,k}^{(i)}\pi^{k},\ a_{n,k}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

Thus

x=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\pi^{n}=\sum_{n\geq m}\pi^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{ef}a_{n}^{(i)}\alpha_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{ef}\alpha_{i}\sum_{n\geq m}(\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{n,k}^{(i)}\pi^{k})\pi^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{ef}\alpha_{i}\sum_{n\geq m}\pi^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{n-k,k}^{(i)},

where we set
a_{n-k,k}^{(i)}=0
if
n-k<m
. Let
\Gamma^{\prime}:=\mathbb{F}_{q}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\mathbb{F}_{q}\alpha_{ef}
. Denote by

b_{n}^{(i)}:=\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{n-k,k}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q},\quad{\rm and}\quad b_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{ef}b_{n}^{(i)}\alpha_{i}\in\Gamma^{\prime}.

Then

x=\sum_{i=1}^{ef}\alpha_{i}\sum_{n\geq m}b_{n}^{(i)}\pi^{n}=\sum_{n\geq m}b_{n}\pi^{n}

is the expansion of
x
with respect to
(\Gamma^{\prime},\pi)
. Therefore by Lemma
2.2
,
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
is ultimately periodic. Hence there are integers
N\geq m+d
and
L>0
such that

(2.3)

b_{n}=b_{n+L}\ {\rm for\ all}\ n\geq N.

Fix any
n\geq N
. Consider
(d+1)
-tuples

(a_{n-d+iL},a_{n-d+1+iL},\ldots,a_{n+iL})\in\Gamma^{d+1}

for
0\leq i\leq r^{d+1}
. Since
|\Gamma^{d+1}|=r^{d+1}
, there must exist
0\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq r^{d+1}
such that

(a_{n-d+i_{1}L},\ldots,a_{n+i_{1}L})
and
(a_{n-d+i_{2}L},\ldots,a_{n+i_{2}L})
are equal. Take
n_{0}=n+i_{1}L
and
j=i_{2}-i_{1}
. Then

(2.4)

(a_{n_{0}-d},\ldots,a_{n_{0}})=(a_{n_{0}-d+jL},\ldots,a_{n_{0}+jL}).

Now we show that (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) imply
a_{n_{0}+1}=a_{n_{0}+1+jL}
. By (
2.3
), we have

b_{n_{0}+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{n_{0}+1-k,k}^{(i)}=\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{n_{0}+1-k+jL,k}^{(i)}=b_{n_{0}+1+jL}.

Meanwhile by applying (
2.4
) we may get

a_{n_{0}+1-k,k}^{(i)}=a_{n_{0}+1-k+jL,k}^{(i)}

for
1\leq k\leq d
and
1\leq i\leq{ef}
. It then follows that

a_{n_{0}+1,0}^{(i)}=a_{n_{0}+1+jL,0}^{(i)}

for
1\leq i\leq{ef}
. Since there exists exactly one
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that

\gamma\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{ef}a_{n_{0}+1,0}^{(i)}\alpha_{i}\mod M,

we derive
a_{n_{0}+1}=\gamma=a_{n_{0}+1+jL}
. Finally an easy induction argument gives us
a_{n}=a_{n+jL}
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. That is,
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
is ultimately periodic. So we conclude that
(\Gamma,\pi)
has property A. Lemma
2.3
is proved.
∎

Now we are ready to give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Assume
(\Gamma,\pi)
has property A. For
\gamma\in\Gamma
, if

\gamma=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\pi^{n}

is the expansion of
\gamma
with respect to
(\Gamma,\pi)
, then
m=0
,
a_{0}=\gamma
, and
a_{n}=0
for all
n>0
. So its sequence of coefficients is ultimately periodic. Thus
\gamma
is rational. By the same argument we can show that
\gamma\pi
is rational for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, which implies
\pi
is rational. The equation
[K:\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)]=[K_{v}:\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))]
follows from Lemma
2.1
. This shows necessity.

Conversely suppose
\pi\in A
,
\Gamma\subset A
, and
[K:\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)]=[K_{v}:\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))]
. We show
(\Gamma,\pi)
has property A. Let
x\in K_{v}
and
x=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\pi^{n}
be the expansion of
x
with respect to
(\Gamma,\pi)
. If the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
is ultimately periodic, then
x
is rational. It remains to prove
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
is ultimately periodic if
x
is rational.

Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{ef}\in A
such that their reductions modulo
\pi A
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{ef}
consist of an
B:=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)\cap\mathbb{F}_{q}[[\pi]]
-basis of
A
. For
\gamma\in\Gamma
, write

\gamma=\gamma^{(1)}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\gamma^{(ef)}\alpha_{ef},\gamma^{(i)}\in B.

Then there exists a positive integer
L
, depending only on
\Gamma
, such that
(1-\pi^{L})\gamma^{(i)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[\pi]
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
1\leq i\leq{ef}
. Let

\Gamma^{\prime}=(1-\pi^{L})\Gamma=\{(1-\pi^{L})\gamma:\gamma\in\Gamma\}.

Since
(1-\pi^{L})\gamma\equiv\gamma\mod\pi A
and
\Gamma
is a complete set of representatives modulo
\pi A
,
\Gamma^{\prime}
is also a complete set of representatives modulo
\pi A
. Note that
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}[\pi]\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\mathbb{F}_{q}[\pi]\alpha_{ef}
. Then by Lemma
2.3
,
(\Gamma^{\prime},\pi)
has property A.

Denote by
b_{n}:=(1-\pi^{L})a_{n}
. Then
b_{n}\in(1-\pi^{L})\Gamma=\Gamma^{\prime}
. Since
x=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\pi^{n}
is the expansion of
x
with respect to
(\Gamma,\pi)
,

(1-\pi^{L})x=\sum_{n\geq m}(1-\pi^{L})a_{n}\pi^{n}=\sum_{n\geq m}b_{n}\pi^{n}

is the expansion of
(1-\pi^{L})x
with respect to
(\Gamma^{\prime},\pi)
. Hence
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq m}=\{(1-\pi^{L})a_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
is ultimately periodic, implying that
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
is ultimately periodic. Now we conclude that
(\Gamma,\pi)
has property A. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is ended.
\Box

Remark 2.4
.

For given field
K
, valuation
v
and positive integer
e
, there does not necessarily exists
\pi\in K
such that
[K:\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)]=[K_{v}:\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))]
. An interesting question is to decide for which
K,v
and

that such a

exists.

3.
Expansions of algebraic elements

In this section we consider expansions of algebraic elements. For a nonnegative integer
k
and any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, if
\gamma\pi^{k}=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\pi^{n}
is the expansion of
\gamma\pi^{k}
with respect to
(\Gamma,\pi)
, then
m=k
,
a_{k}=\gamma
, and
a_{n}=0
for
n>k
. So
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
is ultimately periodic. Hence
(\Gamma,\pi)
has property B yields
\pi
and all
\gamma\in\Gamma
are algebraic. We conclude that

Lemma 3.1
.

If
(\Gamma,\pi)
has property B, then
\pi
and elements of
\Gamma
are algebraic.

This confirms the only if part of Conjecture
1.2
. Moreover, by using property of automatic sequences, one direction of the if part of Conjecture
1.2
is easy to verify.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
\pi
and elements of
\Gamma
are algebraic. For
x\in K_{v}
, if its expansion with respect to
(\Gamma,\pi)
is
p
-automatic, then
x
is algebraic.

Proof.

Let
x=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\pi^{n}
be the expansion of
x
with respect to
(\Gamma,\pi)
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
x\in A_{v}
, i.e.,
m=0
. If
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is
p
-automatic, then for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
,

x_{\gamma}:=\sum_{n\geq 0,a_{n}=\gamma}x^{n}

is algebraic. Hence
x=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma x_{\gamma}
is algebraic. This shows Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Now by Lemma
3.1
and
3.2
, to verify Conjecture
1.2
it remains to verify the following statement:
if
x
is algebraic, then its expansion with respect to
(\Gamma,\pi)
is
p
-automatic, provided that
\pi
and elements of
\Gamma
are algebraic.

We do not prove this remaining part in present paper. But we will verify it under the condition that
\Gamma
is additively closed. Recall that
\Gamma
is additively closed if
\mu+\nu\in\Gamma
for any
\mu,\nu\in\Gamma
. To proceed, we need a equivalent description for additively closed set.

Lemma 3.3
.

A set
S\subset K_{v}
is additively closed if and only if
S
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear space.

Proof.

If
S
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear space, then
S
is obviously additively closed. On the other hand,
K_{v}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear space. If
S
is an additively closed subset of
K_{v}
, then it is easy to see
S
is closed for addition and scalar multiplication, hence is a
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear subspace of
K_{v}
.
∎

Let
L/K
be a finite extension of global function fields of characteristic
p
. Let
w
be any valuation of
L
which extends the valuation
v
of
K
, that is
w|_{K}=v
. Let
L_{w}
be the completion of
L
under
w
. Denote by
L^{a}
the algebraic closure of
L
in
L_{w}
and
K^{a}
the algebraic closure of
K
in
K_{v}
. Obviously
L^{a}
is also the algebraic closure of
K
in
L_{w}
. Hence
K^{a}
is a subfield of
L^{a}
. In the process of proving the additively closed case of Conjecture
1.2
, we come across the following result.

Theorem 3.4
.

We have
[L^{a}:K^{a}]=[L_{w}:K_{v}]
.

Before delivering the proof of Theorem
3.4
, we show how it is used to prove the additively closed case of Conjecture
1.2
.

Theorem 3.5
.

.
Let
\Gamma
be additively closed. Then
(\Gamma,\pi)
has property B if and only if
\pi
and elements of
\Gamma
are algebraic.

Proof.

By the discussion right below Lemma
3.2
, we need only to show that each algebraic element
x
has
p
-automatic expansion with respect to
(\Gamma,\pi)
, provided that
\pi
and elements of
\Gamma
are algebraic. We do this in the following.

Since
\Gamma
is additively closed, by Lemma
3.3
,
\Gamma
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear space. Let
u:=\log_{p}|\Gamma|
, and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{u}
consist of an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-basis of
\Gamma
. Then the reductions of
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{u}
modulo
\pi
consist of an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-basis of
A/\pi A
. It follows that
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{u}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{p}((\pi))
, and hence are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\pi)^{a}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\pi)^{a}
denotes the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\pi)
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}((\pi))
. Applying Theorem
3.4
to the extension
K/\mathbb{F}_{p}(\pi)
, we deduce that

u=\log_{p}q^{ef}=[K_{v}:\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))][\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi)):\mathbb{F}_{p}((\pi))]=[K_{v}:\mathbb{F}_{p}((\pi))]=[K^{a}:\mathbb{F}_{p}(\pi)^{a}].

It follows that
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{u}
consist of an
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\pi)^{a}
-basis of
K^{a}
. Hence for
x\in K^{a}
there exist uniquely determined
x_{1},\ldots,x_{u}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}(\pi)^{a}
such that

x=x_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+x_{u}\alpha_{u}.

For
1\leq i\leq u
, write each
x_{i}
as

x_{i}=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}^{(i)}\pi^{n},a_{n}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\ {\rm for\ all\ }n\geq m.

By Christol’s theorem, each sequence
\{a_{n}^{(i)}\}_{n\geq m}
is
p
-automatic. Letting
a_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{u}a_{n}^{(i)}\alpha_{i}
, we have

x=\sum_{i=1}^{u}\alpha_{i}\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}^{(i)}x^{n}=\sum_{n\geq m}\pi^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{u}a_{n}^{(i)}\alpha_{i}=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\pi^{n}.

It is easy to see
a_{n}\in\Gamma
for all
\Gamma
, therefore
\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\pi^{n}
is the expansion of
x
with respect to
(\Gamma,\pi)
. Since
\{a_{n}^{(i)}\}_{n\geq m}
are
p
-automatic for
1\leq i\leq u
, we deduce that
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
is
p
-automatic. This finishes the proof of Theorem
3.5
.
∎

Now let us get back to the proof of Theorem
3.4
. We show in advance two lemmas which are special cases of Theorem
3.4
.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
L/K
is finite separable. Then
[L^{a}:K^{a}]=[L_{w}:K_{v}]
.

Proof.

Since
L/K
is finite separate, there exists a primitive element
\alpha
such that
L=K(\alpha)
. Furthermore, we see
L_{w}=K_{v}(\alpha)
since both fields are complete, and
L\subset K_{v}(\alpha)\subset L_{w}
. As
\alpha
is a separable element, we have
L_{w}/K_{v}
is separable. For any
\beta\in L^{a}
, obviously the minimal polynomial of
\beta
over
K_{v}
has all its coefficients from
K^{a}
, hence is also the minimal polynomial of
\beta
over
K^{a}
. So
\beta
is separable over
K^{a}
as it is separable over
K_{v}
. As a consequence,
L^{a}/K^{a}
is separable. In addition the coincidence of the minimal polynomials of
\alpha
over both
K_{v}
and
K^{a}
gives us
[L_{w}:K_{v}]=[K^{a}(\alpha):K^{a}]
. Hence to show
[L^{a}:K^{a}]=[L_{w}:K_{v}]
is equivalent to show
[L^{a}:K^{a}]=[K^{a}(\alpha):K^{a}]
. Assume to the contrary that
[L^{a}:K^{a}]
is strictly larger than
[K^{a}(\alpha):K]
. Then by
L^{a}/K^{a}
is separable, there exists some
\beta\in L^{a}
such that its minimal polynomial
g(x)
over
K^{a}
is of degree greater than
[K^{a}(\alpha):K^{a}]
. But
g(x)
is also the minimal polynomial of
\beta
over
K_{v}
, and hence is of degree no more than
[L_{w}:K_{v}]=[K^{a}(\alpha):K^{a}]
, a contradiction. Therefore we have
[L^{a}:K^{a}]=[L_{w}:K_{v}]
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

If
L/K
is purely inseparable of degree
p
, then
[L^{a}:K^{a}]=[L_{w}:K_{v}]=p
.

Proof.

If
L/K
is purely inseparable of degree
p
, then
L^{p}=K
. Taking completions on both side gives us
L_{w}^{p}=K_{v}
. Let
F_{s}
be the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
in
L_{w}
. Then
\mathbb{F}_{s}
is isomorphic to the residue field of
L_{w}
. Taking any uniformizer
\lambda
of
L_{w}
, we have
L_{w}=\mathbb{F}_{s}((\lambda))
(cf.
[
19
, Theorem 5.10]
). Then
K_{v}=\mathbb{F}_{s}((\lambda^{p}))
. Now by Christol’s theorem,
L^{a}
is the collection of Laurent series
\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\lambda^{n}
with
p
-automatic sequences of coefficients, while
K^{a}
is the collection of Laurent series
\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\lambda^{pn}
with
p
-automatic sequences of coefficients. Write

\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\lambda^{n}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq p-1}\lambda^{i}\sum_{n\geq m,n\equiv i\mod p}a_{n}\lambda^{pn}.

By
[
3
, Theorem 6.8.1]
, the subsequences
\{a_{n}:n\geq m,n\equiv i\mod p\}
are automatic, hence by Christol’ theorem
\sum_{n\geq m,n\equiv i\mod p}a_{n}\lambda^{pn}
are elements of
K^{a}
for
0\leq i\leq p-1
. So we drive that
1,\lambda,\dots,\lambda^{p-1}
is an
K^{a}
-basis of
L^{a}
, which induces
[L^{a}:K^{a}]=[L_{w}:K_{v}]=p
.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
3.4
.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Let
L_{0}
be the separable closure of
K
in
L
. Then
L/L_{0}
is purely inseparable. There exists a chain of proper extensions

K\subset L_{0}\subset\cdots\subset L_{n}=L,

where
L_{i}/L_{i-1}
are purely inseparable of degree
p
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Denote by
\widehat{L_{i}}
be the completion of
L_{i}
, and
L_{i}^{a}
the algebraic closure of
L_{i}
in
\widehat{L_{i}}
. Then we have chains of extensions

K_{v}\subset\widehat{L_{0}}\subset\cdots\subset\widehat{L_{n}}=L_{w}

and

K^{a}\subset L_{0}^{a}\subset\cdots\subset L_{n}^{a}=L^{a}.

Applying Lemma
3.6
and
3.7
to the above two chains gives us the desired equation.
\Box

. Let

. For all

k

divides

but

does not divide

. We define

and extend

by

Then

is a valuation on
\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
. We call
v_{P}
the

-adic valuation of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
. The completion of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\pi)

is called the

-adic completion of

.
Take

and let

be the collection of polynomials over

Then
\Gamma
is a complete set of representatives of
A/\pi A
. Additionally
\Gamma
is additively closed. For an element
x
of the

-adic completion of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)

x=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}P^{n}

be the expansion of

with respect to
(\Gamma,P(z))
. Then
x
is algebraic over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
if and only if

is

-automatic. Specially, take

, then

, we get the classical Christol’s theorem.

By the following example we illustrate that Conjecture
1.2
may be true when
\Gamma
is not additively closed. Suppose
\pi
and elements of
\Gamma
are algebraic. Let
\xi
be an algebraic element in

. If

is additively closed, then

is a complete set of representatives of

that is not additively closed. For any

, let

be its expansion with respect to

. We write
a_{n}=b_{n}-\xi\in\Gamma
for each
\geq m
. Then

x=\sum_{n\geq m}(a_{n}+\xi)\pi^{n}=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\pi^{n}+\frac{\pi^{m}\xi}{1-\pi}.

Hence
\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}\pi^{n}
is the expansion of

with respect to

. So

is algebraic if and only if
x-\pi^{m}\xi/(1-\pi)
is algebraic, if and only if
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
is
p
-automatic by Theorem

, if and only if
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
is
p
-automatic.

An equivalent statement of Conjecture
1.2

In the end of this section, we exhibit an equivalent but more concrete statement of Conjecture
1.2
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{s}
be the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in

. Then
\mathbb{F}_{s}

of

of

from

.

′

, and
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{F}_{s}[[z]]

As evidence to the conjecture we prove that

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\Gamma=\{f_{1},f_{2}\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}[[z]]
be a complete residue system of
\mathbb{F}_{2}[[z]]
modulo
z
. Let
x=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}z^{n}
be the expansion of
x
with respect to
(\Gamma,z)
. Then
(\Gamma,z)
has property B if and only if
f_{1},f_{2}
are algebraic.

Proof.

As it is similar to the proof of Theorem
3.5
, we need only show that if
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are algebraic, then the algebraicity of
x
implies the 2-automaticity of
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
.

Assume
f_{1},f_{2}
are algebraic. Suppose the constant terms of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are
1
and

respectively. Denote by
a_{n}^{(0)}
the constant term of

. Since

, we have

if

, and
a_{n}=f_{2}
if
a_{n}^{(0)}=0
. Then we deduce that

a_{n}=a_{n}^{(0)}f_{1}+(1-a_{n}^{(0)})f_{2}.

Thus

x=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}z^{n}=\sum_{n\geq m}(a_{n}^{(0)}f_{1}+(1-a_{n}^{(0)})f_{2})z^{n}=(f_{1}-f_{2})\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}^{(0)}z^{n}+f_{2}\sum_{n\geq m}z^{n}.

Note that

is

-automatic. Therefore

is

-automatic. This finishes the proof of Theorem

.

4.
\beta
-expansions of formal Laurent series

In this section, we apply Theorem
3.5
to study
\beta
-expansions of formal Laurent series. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}((1/z))
be the field of formal Laurent series of the form

x=\sum_{n\geq-m}\frac{a_{n}}{z^{n}},a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q},

where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
is called the degree of
x
. We say a formal Laurent series is algebraic if it is algebraic over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(1/z)
. The set of Laurent series of degree no more than zero is
\mathbb{F}_{q}[[1/z]]
. The integer part
[x]
and the fraction part
\{x\}
of
x
are respectively defined as

[x]:=\sum_{-m\leq n\leq 0}\frac{a_{n}}{z^{n}}~~{\rm and~~}\{x\}:=\sum_{n>0}\frac{a_{n}}{z^{n}}.

Let
\beta
be a series in
\mathbb{F}_{q}((1/z))
of positive degree. Define

T(x):=\beta x-[\beta x],\forall x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[[1/z]].

Denote by
T^{n}
the
n
-th iteration of
T
for positive integers
n
, and set
T^{0}(x)=x
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[[1/z]]
. Then we have the following expansion

x=\frac{a_{1}}{\beta}+\frac{a_{2}}{\beta^{2}}+\cdots,

where
a_{n}=[\beta T^{n}x]
are elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[z]
. The
\beta
-expansion of
x
, denoted by
d_{\beta}(x)
, is defined to be the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
. It is an analogue of
\beta
-expansions of real numbers introduced by Rényi
[
13
]
. Many properties about
\beta
-expansions are studied in
[
11
,
17
,
18
]
, we concentrate on those related to automatic sequences. Hbaib and Mkaouar
[
11
, Theorem 5.4]
proved that if
d_{\beta}(1)
is automatic, then
\beta
is algebraic. Assuming
\deg\beta=1
, they
[
11
, Theorem 5.6]
further proved that
d_{\beta}(1)
is
p
-automatic if and only if
\beta
is algebraic. Scheicher and Sirvent
[
18
, Theorem 6.1]
showed that if
\beta
is isolated of positive degree, then
d_{\beta}(1)
is
p
-automatic. They
[
18
, Theorem 6.6]
also showed that if
\beta
is a Pisot or Salem series, then
d_{\beta}(\alpha)
is
p
-automatic if and only if
\alpha
is algebraic. Based on above results, the following questions on
\beta
-expansions appear in
[
11
]
and
[
18
]
.

Question 1
[
18
, Remark 6.7]
: Is either the converse of
[
11
, Theorem 5.4]
or the converse of
[
18
, Theorem 6.1]
true. More precisely, whether one of the following statements is true: (i)
d_{\beta}(1)

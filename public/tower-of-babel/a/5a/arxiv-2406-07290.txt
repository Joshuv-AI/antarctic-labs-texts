Generalized semiclassical orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle: A Riemann–Hilbert perspective
Amílcar Branquinho 1, Ana Foulquié-Moreno 2, Karina Rampazzi 3

Abstract
. In this work we show how to get advantage from the Riemann–Hilbert analysis in order to obtain first and second order differential equations for the orthogonal polynomials and associated functions with a weight on the unit circle. We deduce properties for the recurrence relation coefficients from differential properties of the weight. We take the so called generalized modified Jacobi and Bessel weights as a case study.

42C05, 33C45, 33C47, 47A56, 47A75

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this work is to explore the connection between the theories of the Riemann–Hilbert problem and of the orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle defined in terms of Hölder type weights. Furthermore, we show how Riemann–Hilbert analysis could help in deriving analytical properties for sequences of orthogonal polynomials associated with specific weight functions.

A weight function,
w
, on the unit circle,

\mdmathbb{T}=\{z\in\mdmathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
, is called semiclassical if it satisfies the equation

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091pt}{\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091pt\theta}\left(A(\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\theta})w(\theta)\right)=B(\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\theta})w(\theta),

\displaystyle\theta\in[0,2\pi],

where
A
and
B
are Laurent polynomials and
A(\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\theta})=0
at the singular points of
1/w
. This definition is due to Magnus
[
24
]
in the case of the unit circle.
For the case of the real line, we refer to
[
23
]
and the references therein.
The equation (
1
) will be called a Pearson-type differential equation. In
[
6
]
the Laguerre–Hahn families on the unit circle are treated, which include the semiclassical weight functions as particular cases.

If we allow in equation (
1
) that the
B
can be taken as an entire function, we call
w
a
generalized semiclassical weight
.

For orthogonality on the unit circle, the classification problem of semiclassical orthogonal polynomials was studied and divided into classes in
[
8
]
, considering linear functionals that satisfy a Pearson-type equation.
This approach can be adapted to the equation (
1
) considering that, if
\deg A=p
and
\max\{p-1,\deg((p-1)A(z)+\operatorname{i}B(z))\}=q
, then
w
belongs to the class
(p,q)
of semiclassical weight functions.

A well known example of a semiclassical weight function on the unit circle is

\displaystyle w(\theta)=\operatorname{e}^{\ell\cos(\theta)},

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\theta\leqslant 2\pi,

with

\displaystyle\ell>0,

see
[
21
]
.
The associated sequence of orthogonal monic polynomials,
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
, on the unit circle satisfies the structure relation

(2)

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(z)=n\Phi_{n-1}(z)+\frac{\ell\kappa_{n-2}^{2}}{2\ \kappa_{n}^{2}}\Phi_{n-2}(z),

\displaystyle n\geqslant 2.

From this structure relation, it is possible to show the nonlinear difference equation

(3)

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}(\ell)+\alpha_{n-2}(\ell)=-\frac{2n}{\ell}\frac{\alpha_{n-1}(\ell)}{1-\alpha_{n-1}^{2}(\ell)},

\displaystyle n\geqslant 2.

where
\alpha_{n-1}=-\overline{\Phi_{n}(0)}
are known as Verblunsky coefficients.
This equation was presented by Periwal and Shevitz
[
26
]
. It corresponds to a discrete Painlevé
{\rm dP_{II}}
equation, see
[
29
]
. Studies on semiclassical weight functions on the unit circle can also be found in
[
7
,
24
]
as well as in
[
19
,
22
]
.
In the last two papers the authors derive raising and lowering operators and find second order differential equations
for the orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle associated with semiclassical weights.
In
[
19
]
it is stated a Riemann–Hilbert problem which is different to the one we present here,
but the authors did not take advantage of it to derive differential properties for the polynomials and
functions of second kind
related with semiclassical weights.

The Riemann–Hilbert method is of great importance in the theory of boundary value problems for holomorphic functions. The main objective is to find a function that is holomorphic in a certain region, taking into account certain jump relations between the values of its limits over the points of a given contour. This approach to orthogonal polynomials was formulated by Fokas, Its, and Kitaev in
[
18
]
, and a method for calculating asymptotics with Riemann–Hilbert problems was presented by Deift and Zhou in
[
17
]
, first in the context of integrable systems. The most comprehensive application to orthogonal polynomials occurred in 1999, thanks to the work of Deift, Kriecherbauer, McLaughlin, Venakides, and Zhou, see
[
12
,
15
,
16
]
. These discoveries were strongly influenced by the relationship between orthogonal polynomials and the theory of random matrices
[
11
,
14
]
.

This technique has been widely used in the theory of orthogonal polynomials to obtain asymptotic and differential properties. In this work, we derive first and second order differential equations for generalized semiclassical orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle, using the Riemann–Hilbert techniques. We also show that some of the first-order differential equations are equivalent to structure relations found in
[
3
]
.
More details about the Riemann–Hilbert Problem can be found in
[
1
,
2
,
13
]
.
Here we follow the Riemann–Hilbert Problem stated in
[
13
]
(see also
[
25
]
) where there appears two systems of associated functions that, so far as we know, have not been interpreted in terms of theirs differential properties.
For more recent results on Riemann–Hilbert theory related with matrix orthogonality, see
[
4
,
5
,
9
,
10
]
.

This work is structured as follows.
In Section
2
,
we start with the definition of orthogonality on the unit circle and present the Szegő recurrence satisfied by these polynomials.
Next we state the Riemann–Hilbert problem and a technical lemma necessary for the development of the work.
In Section
3
we study the analytic properties of the structure matrices associated with the generalized semiclassical weights.
We construct first and second order differential operators for the generalized semiclassical orthogonal polynomials in the unit circle and
associated function as well.

Furthermore, we determine, for the generalized semiclassical weights, the zero curvature formulas and find difference equations satisfied by Verblunsky coefficients.
We end this work, with Section
4
, where by using the Riemann–Hilbert approach, we determine the structure matrices associated with the generalized modified, Bessel and Jacobi, weight functions,
and construct the first and second order differential operators for orthogonal polynomials and
associated functions
with these two considered weight functions on the unit circle.

2.
Riemann–Hilbert problem

Let
\mu
be a positive measure on the unit circle
\mdmathbb{T}
,
absolutely continuous, with respect to the Lebesgue measure,
i.e.

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091pt\mu(z)=\frac{\nu(z)}{\operatorname{i}z}\,\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptz,

with

\displaystyle w(\theta)=\nu(\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\theta}),

\displaystyle\theta\in[0,2\pi].

The corresponding sequence of monic orthogonal polynomials,

\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
,
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}\Phi_{n}(z)\overline{\Phi_{m}(z)}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091pt\mu(z)=\kappa_{n}^{-2}\delta_{n,m},

\displaystyle n,m\in\mdmathbb N\cup\{0\},

with
\kappa_{n}>0
, or equivalently,

(4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}\Phi_{n}(z)\overline{\Phi_{m}(z)}\frac{\nu(z)}{\operatorname{i}z}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptz=\kappa_{n}^{-2}\delta_{n,m},

\displaystyle n,m\in\mdmathbb N\cup\{0\}.

Studies about orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle can be found in
[
21
,
28
]
.
These polynomials satisfy the following relation, known as Szegő recurrence, see
[
28
]
,

(5)

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}(z)=z\Phi_{n-1}(z)-\overline{\alpha}_{n-1}\Phi_{n-1}^{*}(z),

\displaystyle n\in\mdmathbb N,

where
\Phi_{0}(z)=1
,
\Phi_{n}^{*}(z)=z^{n}\overline{\Phi_{n}(1/\overline{z})}
is a reciprocal polynomial and
\alpha_{n-1}=-\overline{\Phi_{n}(0)}
, are the Verblunsky coefficients. Note that if the Verblunsky coefficients are known, then the relation (
5
) generates the sequence of polynomials
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
.

In terms of the powers of
z
, and the sequence of polynomials
\{\Phi_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
and
\{\Phi_{n}^{*}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
, the orthogonality relations (
4
)
read as

\displaystyle\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}z^{-k}\Phi_{n}(z)\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091pt\mu(z)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle k=0,1,\ldots,n-1

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}z^{-n}\Phi_{n}(z)\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091pt\mu(z)=\kappa^{-2}_{n},

\displaystyle\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}z^{-k}\Phi_{n}^{*}(z)\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091pt\mu(z)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle k=1,2,\ldots,n

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}\Phi_{n}^{*}(z)\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091pt\mu(z)=\kappa^{-2}_{n},

or, equivalently, as

(6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}\Phi_{n}(z)\frac{\nu(z)}{z^{j}}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptz

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}\Phi_{n}^{*}(z)\frac{\nu(z)}{z^{j+1}}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptz=0,

\displaystyle j=1,\ldots,n,

(7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}\Phi_{n}(z)\frac{\nu(z)}{\operatorname{i}z^{n+1}}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptz

\displaystyle=\kappa_{n}^{-2},

\displaystyle\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}\Phi_{n}^{*}(z)\frac{\nu(z)}{\operatorname{i}z}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptz=\kappa_{n}^{-2},

\displaystyle n\in\mdmathbb N.

From Szegő recurrence, we have the following equations

(8)

\displaystyle\Phi_{n+1}^{*}(z)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{n}^{*}(z)-\alpha_{n}z\Phi_{n}(z),

\displaystyle n\geqslant 0,

\displaystyle\Phi_{n+1}^{*}(z)

\displaystyle=(1-|\alpha_{n}|^{2})\Phi_{n}^{*}(z)-\alpha_{n}\Phi_{n+1}(z),

\displaystyle n\geqslant 0,

and
\kappa_{n}^{2}/\kappa_{n+1}^{2}=1-|\alpha_{n}|^{2}
, see
[
28
]
.
We denote the monic orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle as

(9)

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}(z)=z^{n}+\Phi_{1}^{n}z^{n-1}+\Phi_{2}^{n}z^{n-2}+\cdots+\Phi_{n-1}^{n}z+\Phi_{n}^{n},

\displaystyle n\geqslant 0.

Applying the Szegő recurrence (
5
), it is easy to see that the coefficients
\Phi_{1}^{n}
in (
9
) are given in terms of the Verblunsky coefficients as

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}^{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\overline{\alpha}_{j}\alpha_{j-1},

\displaystyle n\geqslant 1

with

\displaystyle\alpha_{-1}=-1.

Let
\nu
be a Hölder type weight on the unit circle
\mdmathbb T
, oriented in the positive direction.
Then, it can be seen (cf.
[
13
,
25
]
),
that the unique solution of the Riemann–Hilbert problem, which consists in the determination of a
2\times 2
matrix complex function such that:

(RH1)

Y_{n}
is holomorphic in
\mdmathbb{C}\setminus\mdmathbb{T}
;

(RH2)

Y_{n}
satisfies the jump condition for all
t\in\mdmathbb{T}
,

\displaystyle{(Y_{n}})_{+}(t)={(Y_{n})}_{-}(t)\begin{bmatrix}1&\nu(t)/t^{n}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},

where

Y_{n}
has continuous boundary values
Y_{+}(z)
as
z

approaches the unit circle from the inside, and
Y_{-}(z)
from the outside;

(RH3)

Y_{n}
has the following asymptotic behavior at infinity

\displaystyle Y_{n}(z)=\Big(\mathbf{\operatorname{I}}_{2}+\operatorname{O}(z^{-1})\Big)\begin{bmatrix}z^{n}&0\\
0&z^{-n}\end{bmatrix}

as

\displaystyle z\to\infty

(the
\mathbf{\operatorname{I}}_{2}
is the order
2
identity matrix) is given by

\displaystyle Y_{n}(z)=\begin{bmatrix}\Phi_{n}(z)&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int{T}\frac{\Phi_{n}(t)}{t-z}\frac{\nu(t)}{\operatorname{i}t^{n}}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt\\[10.66977pt]
-2\pi\kappa_{n-1}^{2}\Phi^{*}_{n-1}(z)&\displaystyle-\kappa_{n-1}^{2}\int{T}\frac{\Phi^{*}_{n-1}(t)}{t-z}\frac{\nu(t)}{\operatorname{i}t^{n}}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle n\in\mdmathbb N.

Moreover, it is also proved that
\det Y_{n}=1
,
n\in\mdmathbb N
.

We will write,
Y_{n}
,
in the form

(10)

\displaystyle Y_{n}(z)=\begin{bmatrix}\Phi_{n}(z)&G_{n}(z)\\[2.13387pt]
-b_{n-1}\Phi^{*}_{n-1}(z)&-b_{n-1}G^{*}_{n-1}(z)\end{bmatrix},

where

(11)

\displaystyle b_{n-1}=2\pi\kappa_{n-1}^{2},

\displaystyle n\in\mdmathbb N,

and

(12)

\displaystyle G_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\int{T}\frac{\Phi_{n}(t)}{t-z}\frac{\nu(t)}{t^{n}}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt,

\displaystyle G^{*}_{n-1}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\int{T}\frac{\Phi^{*}_{n-1}(t)}{t-z}\frac{\nu(t)}{t^{n}}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt,

will be called
associated functions with the orthogonal polynomials,
\Phi_{n}
and weight function
\nu
on the unit circle.
Again, since
\det Y_{n}=1
,
we can write

(13)

\displaystyle Y_{n}^{-1}(z)=\begin{bmatrix}-b_{n-1}G^{*}_{n-1}(z)&-G_{n}(z)\\[2.13387pt]
b_{n-1}\Phi^{*}_{n-1}(z)&\Phi_{n}(z)\end{bmatrix}.

Now, we will work on the
associated functions

G_{n}
and
G_{n-1}^{*}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091pt\mu(z)=\frac{\nu(z)}{\operatorname{i}z}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptz
be a measure defined on the unit circle. Then their sequences of
associated functions
defined by (
12
)
satisfies the following recurrence relations,

(14)

\displaystyle G_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=G_{n-1}(z)-\overline{\alpha}_{n-1}G^{*}_{n-1}(z),

\displaystyle n\in\mdmathbb N,

(15)

\displaystyle zG_{n}^{*}(z)

\displaystyle=G_{n-1}^{*}(z)-\alpha_{n-1}G_{n-1}(z),

\displaystyle n\in\mdmathbb N.

Proof.

To show the equation (
14
) we multiply (
5
)
in
t
variable by
\frac{\nu(t)}{2\pi\operatorname{i}t^{n+1}(t-z)}
and then take integral in
\mdmathbb T
with respect to the variable
t
, i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\int{T}\frac{\Phi_{n+1}(t)\nu(t)}{t^{n+1}(t-z)}{\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt}=\frac{1}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\int{T}\frac{t\Phi_{n}(t)\nu(t)}{t^{n+1}(t-z)}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt-\overline{\alpha}_{n}\frac{1}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\int{T}\frac{\Phi^{*}_{n}(t)\nu(t)}{t^{n+1}(t-z)}{\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt}

which is equivalent to (
14
).
To show (
15
), we proceed in the same way as before, now using the relation (
8
) in
t
variable and multiplying by
\frac{\nu(t)}{2\pi\operatorname{i}t^{n}(t-z)}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\int{T}\frac{\Phi^{*}_{n}(t)\nu(t)}{t^{n}(t-z)}{\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt}=\frac{1}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\int{T}\frac{t\Phi_{n-1}(t)\nu(t)}{t^{n}(t-z)}{\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt}-\alpha_{n-1}\frac{1}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\int{T}\frac{t\Phi_{n-1}(t)\nu(t)}{t^{n}(t-z)}{\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt}.

Now, taking into account that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\int{T}\frac{\Phi^{*}_{n}(t)\nu(t)}{t^{n}(t-z)}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt=\frac{1}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\int{T}\frac{t-z+z}{t-z}\frac{\Phi^{*}_{n}(t)\nu(t)}{t^{n+1}}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt=\frac{1}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\int{T}\frac{\Phi^{*}_{n}(t)\nu(t)}{t^{n+1}}{\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt}+\frac{z}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\int{T}\frac{\Phi^{*}_{n}(t)\nu(t)}{t^{n+1}(t-z)}{\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt}

and by (
6
)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt=\frac{z}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}\int{T}\frac{\Phi^{*}_{n}(t)\nu(t)}{t^{n+1}(t-z)}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt.

So, we obtain (
15
).
∎

and

about

Lemma 2.1

.

Now, proceeding as before departing from

\displaystyle\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}t^{2}\Phi_{n}(t)\frac{\nu(t)}{\operatorname{i}t}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}t\Phi_{n+1}(t)\frac{\nu(t)}{\operatorname{i}t}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt+\overline{\alpha}_{n}\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}t\Phi_{n}^{*}(t)\frac{\nu(t)}{\operatorname{i}t}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt,

and using the previous calculations, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}t^{2}\Phi_{n}(t)\frac{\nu(t)}{\operatorname{i}t}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt

\displaystyle=\frac{\overline{\alpha}_{n+1}}{\kappa_{n+1}^{2}}-\Phi_{1}^{n+1}\frac{\overline{\alpha}_{n}}{\kappa_{n}^{2}}.

Therefore, in general, we have the relation

\displaystyle\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}t^{j+1}\Phi_{n}(t)\frac{\nu(t)}{\operatorname{i}t}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt

\displaystyle=\frac{\overline{\alpha}_{n+j}}{\kappa_{n+j}^{2}}-\overline{\alpha}_{n}\int_{\mdmathbb{T}}t^{n+j}\overline{\Phi_{n}(t)}\frac{\nu(t)}{\operatorname{i}t}\operatorname{d}\hskip-1.2091ptt.

In this way, we get the asymptotic expansion for
G_{n}
at infinity (
17
).
∎

Now, we apply the Riemann–Hilbert problem to derive recurrence relations for orthogonal polynomials and
associated functions.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
Y_{n}
be the solution of the Riemann–Hilbert problem, then for all
n\in\mdmathbb N
,

(20)

\displaystyle Y_{n+1}(z)\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&z\end{bmatrix}=T_{n}(z)Y_{n}(z),

where

\displaystyle T_{n}(z)=\begin{bmatrix}z+\overline{\alpha}_{n}\alpha_{n-1}&\displaystyle{\overline{\alpha}_{n}}/{b_{n}}\\
\alpha_{n-1}b_{n}&1\end{bmatrix}

which is called the transfer matrix.

Proof.

The matrices
Y_{n}
and
Y_{n}^{-1}
are holomorphic at
\mdmathbb{C}\setminus\mdmathbb{T}
, then
the matrix function

\displaystyle T_{n}(z)=Y_{n+1}(z)\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&z\end{bmatrix}Y_{n}^{-1}(z)

is holomorphic at
\mdmathbb{C}\setminus\mdmathbb{T}
.
However,
T_{n}
has no jump on the unit circle
\mdmathbb{T}
. In fact, we have

\displaystyle(T_{n})_{+}(z)

\displaystyle=(Y_{n+1})_{+}(z)\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&z\end{bmatrix}(Y_{n}^{-1})_{+}(z)

\displaystyle={(Y_{n+1})}_{-}(z)\begin{bmatrix}1&\nu(z)/z^{n+1}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&z\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&-\nu(z)/z^{n}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}{(Y_{n}^{-1})}_{-}(z)

\displaystyle={(Y_{n+1})}_{-}(z)\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&z\end{bmatrix}{(Y_{n}^{-1})}_{-}(z)=(T_{n})_{-}(z),\quad z\in\mdmathbb{T}.

Therefore,
T_{n}
is holomorphic in
\mdmathbb{C}
and it is an entire function.
Moreover, from the representation of
Y_{n}
and
Y_{n}^{-1}
given by (
10
) and (
13
), respectively,
for
z\in\mdmathbb C\setminus\mdmathbb{T}
, we obtain

\displaystyle T_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\Phi_{n+1}(z)&G_{n+1}(z)\\[1.42271pt]
-b_{n}\Phi^{*}_{n}(z)&-b_{n}G^{*}_{n}(z)\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\[1.42271pt]
0&z\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-b_{n-1}G^{*}_{n-1}(z)&-G_{n}(z)\\[1.42271pt]
b_{n-1}\Phi^{*}_{n-1}(z)&\Phi_{n}(z)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}b_{n-1}(z\Phi_{n-1}^{*}(z)G_{n+1}(z)-\Phi_{n+1}(z)G^{*}_{n-1}(z))&-\Phi_{n+1}(z)G_{n}(z)+z\Phi_{n}(z)G_{n+1}(z)\\[2.84544pt]
b_{n}b_{n-1}(\Phi_{n}^{*}(z)G_{n-1}^{*}(z)-z\Phi^{*}_{n-1}(z)G_{n}^{*}(z))&b_{n}(\Phi_{n}^{*}(z)G_{n}(z)-z\Phi_{n}(z)G_{n}^{*}(z))\end{bmatrix}.

Now, using (
17
) and (
18
), and taking into account the behavior at infinity, and
using Liouville’s Theorem, we get
the explicit expression for
T_{n}
in (
20
).
∎

As a direct consequence from equation (
20
) we obtain the recurrence relations for the entries in
Y_{n}
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
Y_{n}
be the solution of the Riemann–Hilbert problem,
then for all
n\in\mdmathbb N
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{n+1}(z)

\displaystyle=(z+\overline{\alpha}_{n}\alpha_{n-1})\Phi_{n}(z)-\frac{b_{n-1}}{b_{n}}\overline{\alpha}_{n}\Phi_{n-1}^{*}(z),

\displaystyle-b_{n}\Phi_{n}^{*}(z)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n-1}b_{n}\Phi_{n}(z)-b_{n-1}\Phi_{n-1}^{*}(z),

\displaystyle zG_{n+1}(z)

\displaystyle=(z+\overline{\alpha}_{n}\alpha_{n-1})G_{n}(z)-\frac{b_{n-1}}{b_{n}}\overline{\alpha}_{n}G_{n-1}^{*}(z),

\displaystyle-b_{n}zG_{n}^{*}(z)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n-1}b_{n}G_{n}(z)-b_{n-1}G_{n-1}^{*}(z).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\nu(z)
be a

definined by (

Since

ii)

For

\displaystyle{(Z_{n}})_{+}(t)

\displaystyle={(Y_{n}})_{+}(t){\begin{bmatrix}\frac{t^{-n/2}}{\nu^{-1/2}}&0\\

at infinity.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\nu(z)
be a
generalized
semiclassical weight.
Then, the corresponding structure matrix
M_{n}
,
n\in\mdmathbb{N}
, given in (
22
), is a holomorphic function in
\mdmathbb{C}\setminus\mathcal{Z}_{A}
.

Proof.

Since
Z_{n}
is holomorphic in
\mdmathbb{C}\setminus(\mdmathbb{T}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{A})
, then

is also holomorphic in
\mdmathbb{C}\setminus(\mdmathbb{T}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{A})
.
Due to the fact that

has a constant jump on
\mdmathbb{T}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, the matrix function

has the same constant jump on
\mdmathbb{T}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, so the matrix

has no jump on
\mdmathbb{T}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, and it follows that in each element of

, the functions

have an isolated singularity.
∎

The next proposition is very important for finding nonlinear difference equations satisfied by the Verblunsky coefficients.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\nu(z)
be a
generalized
semiclassical weight.
Then, the corresponding structure matrix
M_{n}
,
n\in\mdmathbb{N}
, given in (
22
)
satisfies

(24)

\displaystyle T_{n}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2z}T_{n}={M}_{n+1}T_{n}-T_{n}{M}_{n},

\displaystyle n\in\mdmathbb N,

which is a zero curvature formula.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.1
we know that
\sqrt{z}\,Z_{n+1}=T_{n}Z_{n}
, then taking derivative
and again using
Proposition
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\{T_{n}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2z}T_{n}+T_{n}{M}_{n}-{M}_{n+1}T_{n}\Big\}Z_{n}=\mathbf{0}_{2}.

Therefore, since
Z_{n}
is invertible,
the result follows.
∎

We also have a kind of reciprocal of Proposition

.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\nu
be a Hölder type weight defined on
\mdmathbb T
, with sequences of transfer matrices
\{T_{n}\}_{n\in\mdmathbb N}
and constant jump fundamental matrices

,

\displaystyle\sqrt{z}\left(Z^{\prime}_{n+1}-M_{n+1}Z_{n+1}\right)=T_{n}\left(Z^{\prime}_{n}-M_{n}Z_{n}\right),

Multiplying by

and iterating the procedure we arrive to

\displaystyle(\sqrt{z})^{n+1}\left(Z^{\prime}_{n+1}-M_{n+1}Z_{n+1}\right)=T_{n}T_{n-1}\cdots T_{0}\left(Z^{\prime}_{0}-M_{0}Z_{0}\right),

and taking into account our hypothesis we get that (

) takes place.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\nu
be a

\mdmathbb T
.
Then

Now, we will split (

From the representation of

given in (

), we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{n}^{2}=\big(z|\alpha_{n-1}|^{2}(n+b)(\overline{b}+n)+\frac{1}{4}(\overline{b}-z(n+b)+n)^{2}\big)\,\mathbf{\operatorname{I}}_{2},

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{n}^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{n+b}{2}&0\\

\end{bmatrix},

and by definition of
C_{n}
in (

), we have

\displaystyle 2z(1-z)C_{n}^{\prime}C_{n}^{-1}+(1-2z)\mathbf{\operatorname{I}}_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}1-n(1-z)-(2+b)z-\overline{b}&0\\
0&1+n+(b-2-n)z+\overline{b}\end{bmatrix},

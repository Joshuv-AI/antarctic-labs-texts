Sharp well-posedness for the Cauchy problem of the two dimensional quadratic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with angular regularity
Hiroyuki Hirayama, Shinya Kinoshita, Mamoru Okamoto

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the Cauchy problem of the quadratic nonlinear Schrödinger equation in ℝ × ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2} with the nonlinearity η ​ | u | 2 \eta|u|^{2} where η ∈ ℂ ∖ { 0 } \eta\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\} and low regularity initial data. If s < − 1 / 4 s<-1/4 , the ill-posedness result in the Sobolev space H s ​ ( ℝ 2 ) H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}) is known. We will prove the well-posedness in H s ​ ( ℝ 2 ) H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}) for − 1 / 2 < s < − 1 / 4 -1/2<s<-1/4 by assuming some angular regularity on initial data. The key tools are the modified Fourier restriction norm and the convolution estimate on thickened hypersurfaces.

1.
Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS):

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u(t,x)-\Delta u(t,x)=\eta|u(t,x)|^{2},\quad(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\eta\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
,
\Delta
is the Laplacian in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and the solution
u
is complex-valued. The following scaling transformation

u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{2}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),\quad\lambda>0

implies that (
1.1
) has a scaling invariance in the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with the scaling critical index
s_{c}=d/2-2
.
Our aim is to establish the local in time well-posedness of (
1.1
) with
d=2
in a low regularity Sobolev space
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
with an additional angular regularity.

There are a lot of works on the Cauchy problem of NLS with quadratic nonlinearities.
It is known that a necessary regularity for the well-posedness depends heavily
on the structure of the nonlinear term. To see this, let us first consider the Cauchy problem of NLS with the nonlinear term
\eta u^{2}
where
\eta\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
:

i\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=\eta u^{2}\quad\textnormal{in}\ {\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

(1.2)

In the one dimensional case, Kenig, Ponce, and Vega
[
30
]
proved that the Cauchy problem of (
1.2
) is locally well-posed in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
if
s>-3/4
by exploiting the Fourier restriction norm method.
Bejenaru and Tao
[
6
]
relaxed the regularity condition by showing the local well-posedness of (
1.2
) in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
for
s\geq-1
and, in addition, they proved ill-posedness if
s
is below
-1
.
In the two dimensional case, the local well-posedness for (
1.2
) in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
with
s>-3/4
was obtained in
[
13
]
. This range was extended to
s>-1
in
[
5
]
. On the other hand, Iwabuchi and Ogawa
[
26
]
found that (
1.2
) is ill-posed in the scaling critical space
H^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
by showing so-called “norm inflation”.

Note that the same well-posedness result for the Cauchy problems of NLS with the nonlinear term
\eta_{1}u^{2}+\eta_{2}\overline{u}^{2}
where
\eta_{1}
,
\eta_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
was proved in
[
33
]
.

Now we turn to (
1.1
). For
d=1
, Kenig, Ponce, and Vega
[
30
]
proved the local well-posedness of (
1.1
) in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
for
s>-1/4
. This result was improved by Kishimoto and Tsugawa
[
34
]
who showed the well-posedness in
H^{-\frac{1}{4}}({\mathbb{R}})
.
In the two dimensional case, Colliander et al.
[
13
]
proved that (
1.1
) is locally well-posed if
s>-1/4
. This range was extended to
s\geq-1/4
by Kishimoto
[
32
]
.
In the three dimensional case, Tao
[
42
]
established the local well-posedness for
s>-1/4
. Later, it was observed by Iwabuchi and Uriya
[
27
]
that the regularity
s=-1/4
is critical for the well-posedness. They proved that the norm inflation occurs in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
when
d=1
,
2
,
3
if
s<-1/4
. Especially, (
1.1
) is ill-posed in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
when
d=1
,
2
,
3
, and
s<-1/4
.

Our goal in this paper is to prove the local well-posedness of (
1.1
) with
d=2
in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
for
s>-1/2
under some angular regularity assumption on initial data. As mentioned above, the necessary regularity is completely determined, that is,
s=-1/4
is the threshold for the well-posedness of (
1.1
) in the
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
framework with no extra angular regularity. We will push down the threshold to
s>-1/2
by exploiting angular regularity of initial data.

In the study of the Cauchy problem of NLS with general
p
-power type nonlinearities, e.g.

\eta|u|^{p-1}u
,
\eta|u|^{p}
,
\eta u^{p}
, radially symmetry and angular regularity assumptions have played an important role to push down the necessary regularity threshold for the well-posedness. Hidano
[
19
]
considered the mass-subcritical pure-power NLS:

i\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=\eta|u|^{p-1}u\quad\textnormal{in}\ {\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

(1.3)

where
\eta\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
,
d\geq 3
,
4/(d+1)<p-1<4/d
, and established the global well-posedness of (
1.3
) in the scaling invariant space
\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{2}{p-1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for small and radial initial data. Since the scaling critical index is negative, radially symmetry assumption on initial data is essential for his result. Some negative results in this direction can be found in
[
12
]
.
We refer to
[
18
]
for the analogous global well-posedness results of
(
1.3
), and
[
15
]
,
[
11
]
for the generalized results to the non-radial setting by assuming some angular regularity on data.

To state the main theorem, let us define the angular derivative and the function spaces.
Let
x=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and
\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}=(x^{(1)}\partial_{x^{(2)}}-x^{(2)}\partial_{x^{(1)}})^{2}
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. For
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we define the angular derivative
\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}
by
(1-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}
. We give the precise definition of
\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}
in Section 2.
Define the function space

H^{s,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\,|\,\|f\|_{H^{s,\sigma}}:=\|\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}f\|_{H^{s}}<\infty\}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d=2
,
-1/2<s\leq-1/4
, and
\sigma\geq-2s-1/2
. Then (
1.1
) is locally well-posed in
H^{s,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

Remark 1.2
.

We note that the local well-posedness of (
1.1
) in
H^{-\frac{1}{4}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})(=H^{-\frac{1}{4},0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
was proved by Kishimoto
[
32
]
.

We introduce the rough strategy and the key ingredients for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
We will prove Theorem
1.1
by the iteration argument which is a standard method to obtain the well-posedness.
The candidate of solution spaces would be the Fourier restriction norm space
X^{s,b}
which has been applied to the number of the studies of the dispersive and wave equations and provided numerous results.
The point is that, however, if
\sigma=-2s-1/2
, the
X^{s,b}
space will not yield Theorem
1.1
. The same difficulty arises in the one dimensional case. Kishimoto and Tsugawa
[
34
]
employed the modified
X^{s,b}
space to overcome the difficulty. We follow their approach and modify the
X^{s,b}
space to handle the nonlinear interaction efficiently. We explain the difficulty in further details in Section 2.

The second key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the convolution estimate of the functions restricted to thickened hypersurfaces (see Assumption
2
and Theorem
3.4
below) which is the main contribution of our study. Angular regularity can be exploited effectively by using this estimate.
Note that our approach for the proof of this estimate is inspired by the geometric observation by Bejenaru
[
1
]
. In Section 3, we introduce and discuss the estimate in more details.

We show that Theorem
1.1
is in fact optimal.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d=2
,
s<-1/4
, and
0\leq\sigma<\min(-2s-1/2,1/2)
. Then, for any
T>0
, the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto u

of (
1.1
), as a map from the unit ball in

H^{s,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
to

C([0,T];H^{s,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
fails to be continuous at the origin.

Remark 1.4
.

The condition
-1/2<s
implies
-2s-1/2<1/2
. Hence, Theorem
1.3
shows that Theorem
1.1
is an optimal result.

In addition, we will see that even if an initial datum is radially symmetric, we cannot prove the well-posedness of (
1.1
) if
s\leq-1/2
as long as we utilize the iteration argument. Let
H^{s}_{\mathrm{rad}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
be the collection of radially symmetric functions belong to
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
d=2
and
s\leq-1/2
. Then for any
T>0
, the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto u

of (
1.1
), as a map from the unit ball in

H^{s}_{\mathrm{rad}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
to

C([0,T];H^{s}_{\mathrm{rad}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
fails to be
C^{2}
at the origin.

To see Theorem
1.5
, we will follow Bourgain’s argument which is introduced in
[
9
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce notations, explicit definition and some properties of
\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}
. We introduce and discuss the function space where we will find the solution of (
1.1
). In Section 3, we introduce the two key estimates which play an essential role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In Section 4, by using the estimates obtained in Section 3, we will establish the bilinear estimate which immediately produce Theorem
1.1
. The last section, Section 5, is devoted to the proof of the negative results, Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
.
The failure of the standard
X^{s,b}
type bilinear estimate is also studied.

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

We will use
A\lesssim B
to denote an estimate of the form
A\leq CB
for some constant
C
and write
A\sim B
to mean
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.
We will use the convention that capital letters denote dyadic numbers, e.g.
N=2^{n}
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:={\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
and for a dyadic summation we write
\sum_{N\geq M}a_{N}:=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},2^{n}\geq M}a_{2^{n}}
for brevity.
Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((-2,2))
be an even, non-negative function such that
\chi(t)=1
for
|t|\leq 1
.
We define
\psi(t):=\chi(t)-\chi(2t)
,

\psi_{1}(t):=\chi(t)
, and
\psi_{N}(t):=\psi(N^{-1}t)
for
N\geq 2
.
Then,
\sum_{N\geq 1}\psi_{N}(t)=1
.

Let
\mathbf{1}_{A}
denotes the characteristic function of the set
A
.

We denote
\widehat{f}
by the spatial Fourier transform of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
\mathcal{F}_{t,x}u
by the space and time Fourier transform of
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{t,x}^{-1}
be the space and time Fourier inverse transform.
We define frequency Littlwood-Paley projections by
\widehat{P_{N}f}(\xi):=\psi_{N}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)

and
e^{\pm it\Delta}
by the propagators for the free Schrödinger equations
i\partial_{t}u(t,x)\pm\Delta u(t,x)=0
.

We collect the fundamental properties of the angular derivative operator
\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}
.
In this paper, we only consider the two dimensional case. For the higher dimensional case, we refer to
[
40
,
41
,
10
]
.

First, for
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, we recall that the Fourier series expansion implies

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=f(|x|\cos\theta,|x|\sin\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\langle f(|x|\cos\theta,|x|\sin\theta),e^{i\ell\theta}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\theta}}e^{i\ell\theta}\ \ \textnormal{in $L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})$},

where
\langle\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\rangle_{L_{\theta}^{2}}=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\omega_{1}(\theta)\overline{\omega_{2}}(\theta)d\theta
.
The operator
\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}
is formulated as

\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}f(x)=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\langle\ell\rangle^{\sigma}\langle f(|x|\cos\theta,|x|\sin\theta),e^{i\ell\theta}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\theta}}e^{i\ell\theta}.

Let
M\in 2^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
. We define the spherical Littilewood-Paley projections

\begin{split}(H_{M}f)(x)&=(H_{M}f)(|x|\cos\theta,|x|\sin\theta)\\
&=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\psi_{M}(\ell)\langle f(|x|\cos\theta,|x|\sin\theta),e^{i\ell\theta}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\theta}}e^{i\ell\theta}.\end{split}

(2.1)

Then, for
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, it holds that

f(x)=\sum_{M\geq 1}H_{M}f(x)\ \ \textnormal{in $L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})$},\quad\|\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}(H_{M}f)\|_{L^{2}}\sim M^{\sigma}\|H_{M}f\|_{L^{2}},

and thus, for
s
,
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we may write

\|f\|_{H^{s,\sigma}}\sim\bigl(\sum_{N\geq 1,M\geq 1}N^{2s}M^{2\sigma}\|H_{M}P_{N}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It is well-known that if
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
can be described as
f(|x|\cos\theta,|x|\sin\theta)=f_{0}(|x|)e^{i\ell\theta}
then there exists
F_{0}
such that

\widehat{f}(|\xi|\cos\phi,|\xi|\sin\phi)=F_{0}(|\xi|)e^{i\ell\phi}
(see e.g.
[
39
, Chapter 4]
).
This implies that

\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}\mathcal{F}_{x}=\mathcal{F}_{x}\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}

and, in particular, it holds that
\|\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}\widehat{H_{M}f}\|_{L^{2}}\sim M^{\sigma}\|H_{M}f\|_{L^{2}}
.
We will frequently use this relation throughout the paper.

2.1.
Function Spaces

We introduce the function spaces.

Definition 2.1
.

For
L\in 2^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
and
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, we define the operator
Q_{L}
by
\mathcal{F}_{t,x}{Q_{L}u}(\tau,\xi):=\psi_{L}(\tau-|\xi|^{2})\mathcal{F}_{t,x}{u}(\tau,\xi)
.
Let
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
\sigma\geq 0
. Define

\displaystyle X^{s,\sigma;b}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}):=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})\,|\,\|u\|_{X^{s,\sigma;b}}<\infty\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{s,\sigma;b}}:=\Bigl(\sum_{N\geq 1}\sum_{M\geq 1}\sum_{L\geq 1}N^{2s}M^{2\sigma}L^{2b}\|Q_{L}H_{M}P_{N}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle Y^{s,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}):=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})\,|\,\|u\|_{Y^{s,\sigma}}<\infty\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{Y^{s,\sigma}}:=\begin{cases}\Bigl(\sum_{N\geq 1}\sum_{M\geq 1}N^{2s}M^{2\sigma}\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x}H_{M}P_{N}u\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}L_{\tau}^{1}}^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ &(\sigma>0),\\
\Bigl(\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s}\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x}P_{N}u\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}L_{\tau}^{1}}^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ &(\sigma=0).\end{cases}

Remark 2.2
.

We give a comment about Definition
2.1
in the case
\sigma=0
. It is easy to see

\|u\|_{X^{s,0;b}}=\Bigl(\sum_{N\geq 1}\sum_{L\geq 1}N^{2s}L^{2b}\|Q_{L}H_{M}P_{N}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

While, since technical issues arise in the proof of the nonlinear estimate, e.g. in the proof of (
4.33
) below, the definition of the
Y^{s,0}
-norm is given in a slight different way from
Y^{s,\sigma}
with
\sigma>0
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\sigma\geq 0
, and
\kappa>0
. Define

\displaystyle Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}=X^{s,\sigma;\frac{1}{2}+\kappa}+(X^{s+4\kappa,\sigma;\frac{1}{2}-\kappa}\cap Y^{s,\sigma}),

\displaystyle\|u\|_{Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}}=\inf_{u=u_{1}+u_{2}}\bigl(\|u_{1}\|_{X^{s,\sigma;\frac{1}{2}+\kappa}}+\|u_{2}\|_{X^{s+4\kappa,\sigma;\frac{1}{2}-\kappa}}+\|u_{2}\|_{Y^{s,\sigma}}\bigr).

For a time interval
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, we define the time restricted space
Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}(I)
as follows:

\displaystyle Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}(I):=\{u\in C(I;H^{s,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))\,|\,\|u\|_{Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}(I)}<\infty\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}(I)}:=\inf\{\|v\|_{Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}}\,|\,u(t)=v(t)\ \mathrm{if}\ t\in I\}.

Remark 2.4
.

(1)

It is observed that
\|u\|_{Y^{s,\sigma}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{s,\sigma;\frac{1}{2}+\kappa}}
, and if
L\sim N^{2}
then
\|Q_{L}P_{N}u\|_{X^{s,\sigma;\frac{1}{2}+\kappa}}\sim\|Q_{L}P_{N}u\|_{X^{s+4\kappa,\sigma;\frac{1}{2}-\kappa}}
.
Thus, it holds that

\begin{split}&\|P_{N}u\|_{Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}}\\
&\sim\|Q_{L\ll N^{2}}P_{N}u\|_{X^{s,\sigma;\frac{1}{2}+\kappa}}+\|Q_{L\gtrsim N^{2}}P_{N}u\|_{X^{s+4\kappa,\sigma;\frac{1}{2}-\kappa}}+\|Q_{L\gtrsim N^{2}}P_{N}u\|_{Y^{s,\sigma}}.\end{split}

(2.2)

(2)

For
u\in C({\mathbb{R}};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
such that
\mathcal{F}_{t,x}u\in L_{\xi}^{2}L_{\tau}^{1}
, it is observed that

\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}=\Bigl\|\int e^{it\tau}(\mathcal{F}_{t,x}u)(\tau,\xi)d\tau\Bigl\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\leq\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x}u\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}L_{\tau}^{1}}.

This and
\|u\|_{Y^{s,\sigma}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{s,\sigma;\frac{1}{2}+\kappa}}
imply that, for
u\in Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}(I)
, it holds that

\|u\|_{C(I;H^{s,\sigma})}\lesssim\|u\|_{Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}(I)}.

(2.3)

Hence,
Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}(I)
is a Banach space.

We comment on why we need to employ the function space
Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}
instead of simply using
X^{s,\sigma;b}
.
In
[
37
]
, Nakanishi, Takaoka, and Tsutsumi proved that if
b\geq 1/2
, the following bilinear estimate fails to hold:

\|u\overline{v}\|_{X^{-\frac{1}{4};b-1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{-\frac{1}{4};b}}\|v\|_{X^{-\frac{1}{4};b}},

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle X^{s;b}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}):=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}})\,|\,\|u\|_{X^{s;b}}<\infty\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{s;b}}:=\Bigl(\sum_{N\geq 1}\sum_{L\geq 1}N^{2s}L^{2b}\|Q_{L}P_{N}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We notice that the estimate (
2.4
) is required to show the well-posedness of (
1.1
) with
d=1
in
H^{-\frac{1}{4}}({\mathbb{R}})
by using the contraction mapping argument in
X^{-\frac{1}{4};b}
with
b>1/2
.
Hence, the failure of (
2.4
) means that the standard
X^{s;b}
space is insufficient for the well-posedness of (
1.1
) in
H^{-\frac{1}{4}}({\mathbb{R}})
.
To overcome this, Kishimoto and Tsugawa
[
34
]
employed the modified Fourier restriction norm and established the well-posedness result in
H^{-\frac{1}{4}}({\mathbb{R}})
. Note that their strategy, modifying the Fourier restriction norm to handle dangerous nonlinear interactions, was inspired by the work by Bejenaru and Tao
[
6
]
.
Additionally, in the two dimensional case, Kishimoto
[
32
]
employed the modified Fourier restriction norm to prove the well-posedness of (
1.1
) in
H^{-\frac{1}{4}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

By following the proof of Theorem 1 (iii) in
[
37
]
by Nakanishi, Takaoka, and Tsutsumi, the failure of (
2.4
) can be extended to the
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
case, that is, if
b\geq 1/2
, the following estimate fails to hold:

\|u\overline{v}\|_{X^{-\frac{1}{4},0;b-1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{-\frac{1}{4},0;b}}\|v\|_{X^{-\frac{1}{4},0;b}}.

Here, the
X^{-\frac{1}{4},0;b}
-norm is the same as in Definition
2.1
.
Furthermore, we may generalize this fact as follows:

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
-1/2<s\leq-1/4
, and
b\geq 1/2
. Then, the following estimate fails to hold:

\|u\overline{v}\|_{X^{s,-2s-\frac{1}{2};b-1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{s,-2s-\frac{1}{2};b}}\|v\|_{X^{s,-2s-\frac{1}{2};b}}.

We will give the proof of Proposition
2.5
in Section
5
.

Remark 2.6
.

Proposition
2.5
suggests that we cannot show the well-posedness of (
1.1
) in
H^{s,-2s-\frac{1}{2}}
, which is the critical case in Theorem
1.1
, by just using the
X^{s,-2s-1/2;b}
space.
Thus, to prove Theorem
1.1
, we follow the approach by Kishimoto and Tsugawa
[
34
]
and employ the modified Fourier restriction norm
Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}
.
While, in the case
\sigma>-2s-1/2
, the modified space
Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}
is not required and the standard
X^{s,\sigma;b}
space yields Theorem
1.1
. That is, if
-1/2<s\leq-1/4
and
\sigma>-2s-1/2
then there exists
b>1/2
such that the following holds:

\|u\overline{v}\|_{X^{s,\sigma;b-1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{s,\sigma;b}}\|v\|_{X^{s,\sigma;b}}.

2.2.
A Key bilinear estimate and proof of Theorem
1.1

We introduce a key bilinear estimate which immediately establishes Theorem
1.1
by the iteration argument.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
-1/2<s\leq-1/4
, and
\sigma\geq-2s-1/2
. Then there exists small
\kappa=\kappa(s)>0
such that

\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x}^{-1}\bigl(\langle\tau-|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{t,x}({u}_{1}{\overline{u_{2}}})\bigr)\|_{Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}}\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}}\|u_{2}\|_{Z^{s,\sigma}_{\kappa}}.

Lastly in this section, we see that Proposition
2.7
yields Theorem
1.1
. Since the proof is standard, we give only a rough sketch of the proof. For the complete proof of Theorem
1.1
, we refer to
[
17
]
,

3.
Key convolution estimates

In this section, we introduce convolution estimates which play a central role in the proof of Proposition
2.7
.
The fist estimate is the convolution estimate on the hypersurfaces which can be seen as a nonlinear version of the Loomis-Whitney inequality. First, we define
C^{1,\beta}
-hypersurfaces given as graphs of
C^{1,\beta}
-functions following
[
3
, Assumption 1.1]
and
[
31
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

For
0<\beta\leq 1
, we say that
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is a
C^{1,\beta}
hypersurface if there exist an open and convex set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and a
C^{1,\beta}
function
\varphi:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

S=\{(\varphi(\xi),\xi)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\;|\;\xi\in\Omega\}.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
S
be a
C^{1,\beta}
hypersurface and
\mathfrak{n}
denote the unit normal vector field on
S
.
Then,
\mathfrak{n}
satisfies the Hölder condition

\sup_{\sigma,\tilde{\sigma}\in S}\biggl(\frac{|\mathfrak{n}(\sigma)-\mathfrak{n}(\tilde{\sigma})|}{|\sigma-\tilde{\sigma}|^{\beta}}+\frac{|\mathfrak{n}(\sigma)\cdot(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})|}{|\sigma-\tilde{\sigma}|^{1+\beta}}\biggr)<\infty.

Assumption 1
.

Let
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
,
S_{3}
be
C^{1,\beta}
hypersurfaces. For
i=1,2,3
, let
\mathfrak{n}_{i}
denotes the unit normal vector field on
S_{i}
.
There exists
A\geq 1
such that the matrix
N(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})=(\mathfrak{n}_{1}(\sigma_{1}),\mathfrak{n}_{2}(\sigma_{2}),\mathfrak{n}_{3}(\sigma_{3}))
satisfies the transversality condition

A^{-1}\leq|\det N(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})|\leq 1

(3.1)

for all
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})\in S_{1}\times S_{2}\times S_{3}
.

If hypersurfaces
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
,
S_{3}
satisfy Assumption
1
, the following convolution estimate of functions restricted to such hypersurfaces holds.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
,
S_{3}
satisfy Assumption
1
. Then, for each
f\in L^{2}(S_{1})
and
g\in L^{2}(S_{2})
, we have

\|f*g\|_{L^{2}(S_{3})}\lesssim A^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(S_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(S_{2})},

(3.2)

where the implicit constant is independent of
\beta
.

For the proof of Theorem
3.3
, we refer to Theorem 4.1 in
[
31
]
.
The convolution estimate (
3.2
), for functions restricted to
C^{3}
-regularity localized hypersurfaces, was introduced by Bennett, Carbery, and Wright in
[
8
]
as a perturbed version of the classical three dimensional Loomis-Whitney inequality
[
36
]
. After that, the regularity assumption on hypersurfaces was relaxed, and (
3.2
) for
C^{1,\beta}
(
\beta>0
) localized hypersurfaces was obtained in
[
2
,
4
]
. See also
[
3
]
.
Lastly, the locality assumption was removed in
[
35
,
31
]
.

The estimate (
3.2
) has a connection with the trilinear restriction estimates (see e.g.
[
8
,
7
]
) and has been applied to the study of the Cauchy problem of nonlinear dispersive equations.
For instance, (
3.2
) was utilized in
[
2
]
to push down the necessary regularity threshold for the well-posedness of the Zakharov system in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

For
\varepsilon>0
and a
C^{1,\beta}
hypersurface
S
given by

, the thickened hypersurface
S(\varepsilon)
is defined by

S(\varepsilon)=\{(\tau,\xi)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\,|\,\xi\in\Omega,\ |\tau-\varphi(\xi)|\leq\varepsilon\}.

(3.3)

We note that in the proof of the key bilinear estimate (Proposition
2.7
), instead of Theorem
3.3
, we use the following convolution estimate on thickened hypersurfaces:

\|f*g\|_{L^{2}(S_{3}(\varepsilon))}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}A^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(S_{1}(\varepsilon))}\|g\|_{L^{2}(S_{2}(\varepsilon))},

(3.4)

where
C^{1,\beta}
hypersurfaces
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
,
S_{3}
satisfy
Assumption

1
.
It is straightforward to see that Theorem
3.3
, Fubini’s theorem, and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality give (

), and in fact, we may see that the two estimates (

) and (

[
31
]
.

Next, we state the second key estimate which is similar in form to (
3.4
). For
2\leq p<\infty
, we define

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{\tau,r}^{2}L_{\theta}^{p}(S_{i}(\varepsilon))}=\Bigl(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathbf{1}_{S_{i}(\varepsilon)}|f(\tau,r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)|^{p}d\theta\Bigr)^{\frac{2}{p}}rdrd\tau\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{\tau,r}^{2}L_{\theta}^{\infty}(S_{i}(\varepsilon))}=\Bigl(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\mathbf{1}_{S_{i}(\varepsilon)}|f(\tau,r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)|^{2}rdrd\tau\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Let
B_{2}=\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,|\,|\xi|<2\}

Assumption 2
.

Suppose that the

hypersurfaces

,
S_{2}

(ii)

|\nabla\varphi_{i}(\xi)|\sim 1\quad\textnormal{if}\ \ |\xi|\sim 1
,

(iii)

If
\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}=0
,
\max_{i=1,2,3}|\xi_{i}|\sim 1
, and

|\varphi_{1}(\xi_{1})+\varphi_{2}(\xi_{2})+\varphi_{3}(\xi_{3})|\ll 1

|\nabla\varphi_{1}(\xi_{1})-\nabla\varphi_{2}(\xi_{2})|+|\nabla\varphi_{2}(\xi_{2})-\nabla\varphi_{3}(\xi_{3})|\sim 1,

.

For
i=1,2,3

0<\varepsilon
,

(3.5)

where

\langle\log\varepsilon\rangle\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\ &(p=\infty).\end{cases}

(3.6)

Remark 3.5

In contrast to Theorem

The following proposition shows that Theorem

is almost optimal.

Proposition 3.6

In the estimate (

,
0\leq\sigma<\min(-2s-1/2,1/2)
, and put
s_{\sigma}=-\sigma/2-1/4
which is equivalent to
\sigma=-2s_{\sigma}-1/2
. Note that
\sigma<\min(-2s-1/2,1/2)
means
\max(s,-1/2)<s_{\sigma}
. Let
N\gg 1
. We define the set
C_{N}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by

C_{N}=\{\xi=(|\xi|\cos\theta,|\xi|\sin\theta)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,|\,N\leq|\xi|\leq N+N^{-1},\ |\theta|\leq N^{-1}\}.

Let
0<\delta\ll 1
. We define the function
f_{N,\delta}
by

\mathcal{F}_{x}f_{N,\delta}=\delta N^{\frac{1}{2}-s_{\sigma}-\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{C_{N}}.

For
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
r>0
, and
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, we define

(f)_{\ell}(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)e^{-i\ell\theta}d\theta.

Then, if
N\leq r\leq N+N^{-1}
, it is easy to see

|(\mathbf{1}_{C_{N}})_{\ell}(r)|\sim N^{-1},\ (\mathrm{if}\ |\ell|\leq 2^{-3}N),\quad|(\mathbf{1}_{C_{N}})_{\ell}(r)|\lesssim|\ell|^{-1},\ (\mathrm{if}\ |\ell|\gtrsim N).

Since
0\leq\sigma<1/2
, these inequalities imply

\displaystyle\|f_{N,\delta}\|_{H^{s,\sigma}}

\displaystyle\sim N^{s}\|\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}\mathcal{F}_{x}f_{N,\delta}\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\delta N^{s-s_{\sigma}+\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{C_{N}}(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\|_{L_{\theta}^{2}}^{2}rdr\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\sim\delta N^{s-s_{\sigma}+\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}\Bigl(\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\langle\ell\rangle^{2\sigma}|(\mathbf{1}_{C_{N}})_{\ell}(N)|^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim\delta N^{s-s_{\sigma}}.

(5.1)

In particular, it holds that
\|f_{N,\delta}\|_{H^{s_{\sigma},\sigma}}\sim\delta
.

Next, we will see that, for
t\in[0,2^{-5}]
, it holds that

\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}|e^{-it^{\prime}\Delta}f_{N,\delta}|^{2}dt^{\prime}\Bigr\|_{H^{s,\sigma}}\gtrsim t\delta^{2}.

(5.2)

For
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
\widetilde{\xi}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we define

\Phi(\xi,\widetilde{\xi})=|\xi|^{2}-|\xi-\widetilde{\xi}|^{2}+|\widetilde{\xi}|^{2}.

If
|\xi|<1
,
\widetilde{\xi}\in C_{N}
, and
\xi-\widetilde{\xi}\in C_{N}
, we compute that

|\Phi(\xi,\widetilde{\xi})|\leq|\xi|^{2}+\bigl|\bigl(|\widetilde{\xi}|+|\xi-\widetilde{\xi}|\bigr)\bigl(|\widetilde{\xi}|-|\xi-\widetilde{\xi}|\bigr)\bigr|\leq 2^{3}.

(5.3)

Hence, if
t\in[0,2^{-5}]
, letting
B_{1}=\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,|\,|\xi|<1\}
, we have

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}|e^{-it^{\prime}\Delta}f_{N,\delta}|^{2}dt^{\prime}\Bigr\|_{H^{s,\sigma}}\gtrsim\Bigl\|\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}\mathcal{F}_{x}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}|e^{-it^{\prime}\Delta}f_{N,\delta}|^{2}dt^{\prime}\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(B_{1})}

\displaystyle=\delta^{2}N^{1-2s_{\sigma}-2\sigma}\Bigl\|\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}\Bigl(\int^{t}_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{-it^{\prime}\Phi(\xi,\widetilde{\xi})}{\bf 1}_{C_{N}}(\xi-\widetilde{\xi}){\bf 1}_{C_{N}}(-\widetilde{\xi})d\widetilde{\xi}dt^{\prime}\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\sim t\delta^{2}N^{1-2s_{\sigma}-2\sigma}\Bigl\|\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}\Bigl(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{\bf 1}_{C_{N}}(\xi-\widetilde{\xi}){\bf 1}_{C_{N}}(-\widetilde{\xi})d\widetilde{\xi}\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(B_{1})}.

Thus, (
5.2
) follows from

\Bigl\|\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}\Bigl(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{\bf 1}_{C_{N}}(\xi-\widetilde{\xi}){\bf 1}_{C_{N}}(-\widetilde{\xi})d\widetilde{\xi}\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(B_{1})}\gtrsim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+\sigma}.

(5.4)

Let us write
\xi=(\xi^{(1)},\xi^{(2)})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. We define

T_{N}=\Bigl\{\xi=(\xi^{(1)},\xi^{(2)})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,\Bigl|\,|\xi^{(1)}|\leq\frac{1}{4N},\quad|\xi^{(2)}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\Bigr\}.

Put
F(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{\bf 1}_{C_{N}}(\xi-\widetilde{\xi}){\bf 1}_{C_{N}}(-\widetilde{\xi})d\widetilde{\xi}
. Clearly,
F
is a real and positive-valued function which satisfies
F(\xi^{(1)},\xi^{(2)})=F(\xi^{(1)},-\xi^{(2)})
.
Let us first observe that

F(\xi)\gtrsim N^{-1}\quad\mathrm{if}\ \ \xi\in T_{N}.

(5.5)

For
\xi_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we define the set
T_{N}+\xi_{0}
by the collection of
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that
\xi-\xi_{0}\in T_{N}
.
It is easy to see that the following inclusion holds:

T_{N}+\Bigl(N+\frac{1}{4N},0\Bigr)\subset C_{N},

which implies that if
\xi\in T_{N}
, we have

\displaystyle F(\xi)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{\bf 1}_{T_{N}+(N+\frac{1}{4N},0)}(\xi-\widetilde{\xi}){\bf 1}_{T_{N}+(N+\frac{1}{4N},0)}(-\widetilde{\xi})d\widetilde{\xi}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{\bf 1}_{T_{N}}(\xi-\widetilde{\xi}){\bf 1}_{T_{N}}(-\widetilde{\xi})d\widetilde{\xi}=|T_{N}\cap(T_{N}+\xi)|\sim N^{-1}.

This completes the proof of (
5.5
).

It is straightforward to check that if
|\xi^{(1)}|\geq 3/N
then
F(\xi)=F(\xi^{(1)},\xi^{(2)})=0
.
This and (
5.5
) suggest that if
1/4\leq r\leq 1/2
then we have

\displaystyle F(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\gtrsim N^{-1}\quad\mathrm{if}\ \ \Bigl|\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr|\leq N^{-1},

\displaystyle F(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)=0\qquad\ \,\mathrm{if}\ \ \Bigl|\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr|\geq 2^{3}N^{-1}\ \mathrm{and}\ \theta\in[0,\pi].

Note that, if
|\theta-\pi/2|\leq 2^{3}N^{-1}
and
\ell\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}=\{0,\pm 2,\pm 4,\ldots\}
satisfies
|\ell|\leq 2^{-10}N
, then the inequality
\cos(\ell\theta)>1/2
holds.
Therefore, if
1/4\leq r\leq 1/2
, for
\ell\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
|\ell|\leq 2^{-10}N
, it holds that

\displaystyle|(F)_{\ell}(r)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Bigl|\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)e^{-i\ell\theta}d\theta\Bigr|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\pi}\Bigl|\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\cos(\ell\theta)d\theta\Bigr|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\Bigl|\int_{0}^{\pi}F(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\cos(\ell\theta)d\theta\Bigr|\gtrsim N^{-2}.

Here we used
F(\xi^{(1)},\xi^{(2)})=F(\xi^{(1)},-\xi^{(2)})
and
\cos(\ell\theta)=\cos(-\ell\theta)
.
Consequently, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}F\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\Bigl(\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\langle\Omega\rangle^{\sigma}F(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)|^{2}d\theta rdr\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\inf_{r\in[\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2}]}\bigl(\sum_{\ell\in 2{\mathbb{Z}},|\ell|\leq 2^{-10}N}\langle\ell\rangle^{2\sigma}|(F)_{\ell}(r)|^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\sim N^{-\frac{3}{2}+\sigma},

which implies (
5.4
).

Now we prove Theorem
1.3
. For
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, let us use

\mathcal{Q}(f)(t)=-i\eta\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}|e^{-it^{\prime}\Delta}f|^{2}dt^{\prime}.

Since
s_{\sigma}>-1/2
,
\sigma=-2s_{\sigma}-1/2
,
\|f_{N,\delta}\|_{H^{s_{\sigma},\sigma}}\sim\delta
, and
\delta
is small, it follows from Theorem
1.1
that we may find the solution of (
1.1
) with the initial datum
f_{N,\delta}
which satisfies

u_{N,\delta}(t)=e^{-it\Delta}f_{N,\delta}-i\eta\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}|u_{N,\delta}(t^{\prime})|^{2}dt^{\prime},\quad t\in[0,1].

(5.6)

We define
v_{N,\delta}
as

v_{N,\delta}(t)=u_{N,\delta}(t)-e^{-it\Delta}f_{N,\delta}-\mathcal{Q}(f_{N,\delta})(t),\quad t\in[0,1].

Then, it follows from (
5.6
) that

\displaystyle v_{N,\delta}(t)

\displaystyle=-i\eta\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}|u_{N,\delta}(t^{\prime})|^{2}dt^{\prime}-\mathcal{Q}(f_{N,\delta})(t)

\displaystyle=-i\eta\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}\Bigl(\bigl|e^{-it^{\prime}\Delta}f_{N,\delta}+\mathcal{Q}(f_{N,\delta})(t^{\prime})+v_{N,\delta}(t^{\prime})\bigr|^{2}-|e^{-it^{\prime}\Delta}f_{N,\delta}|^{2}\Bigr)dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-i\eta\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}\Bigl(|v_{N,\delta}(t^{\prime})|^{2}+2\Re\bigl((e^{-it^{\prime}\Delta}f_{N,\delta})\overline{v_{N,\delta}}(t^{\prime})\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+2\Re\bigl(\overline{\mathcal{Q}(f_{N,\delta})}(t^{\prime})(e^{-it^{\prime}\Delta}f_{N,\delta}+v_{N,\delta}(t^{\prime}))\bigr)\Bigr)dt^{\prime}.

Since
s_{\sigma}>-1/2
,
\sigma=-2s_{\sigma}-1/2
, as in the proof of (
2.6
), we may choose
0<\kappa<1
so that the estimate

\|\mathcal{N}(u,v)\|_{Z^{s_{\sigma},\sigma}_{\kappa}([0,1])}\lesssim\|u\|_{Z^{s_{\sigma},\sigma}_{\kappa}([0,1])}\|v\|_{Z^{s_{\sigma},\sigma}_{\kappa}([0,1])}

(5.7)

holds. Here the bilinear operator
\mathcal{N}(u,v)
is defined in (
2.5
).
Let
V(\delta)=\|v_{N,\delta}\|_{Z^{s_{\sigma},\sigma}_{\kappa}([0,1])}
where
Z^{s_{\sigma},\sigma}_{\kappa}([0,1])
is defined in Subsection
2.1
.
Then, by employing Lemma
2.8
, (
5.7
) and (
5.1
) to estimate the integral form of

, we get

V(\delta)\lesssim V(\delta)^{2}+(\delta+\delta^{2})V(\delta)+(\delta^{3}+\delta^{4}).

We recall the embedding (
2.3
). Because
V(\delta)
is continuous and
V(0)=0
, we have

\|v_{N,\delta}(t)\|_{H^{s_{\sigma},\sigma}}\leq V(\delta)\lesssim\delta^{3},

(5.8)

for any
t\in[0,1]
and sufficiently small
\delta>0
. Then, by utilizing the estimates (
5.2
) and (
5.8
), for any
t\in[0,2^{-5}]
there exists sufficiently small

such that

\displaystyle\|u_{N,\delta}(t)\|_{H^{s,\sigma}}

\displaystyle\geq\|\mathcal{Q}(f_{N,\delta})(t)\|_{H^{s,\sigma}}-\|v_{N,\delta}(t)\|_{H^{s_{\sigma},\sigma}}-\|f_{N,\delta}\|_{H^{s,\sigma}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim t\delta^{2}.

Because
\|f_{N,\delta}\|_{H^{s,\sigma}}\to 0
as
N\to\infty

which is a consequence of (
5.1
) and
s-s_{\sigma}<0
, this inequality implies the discontinuity of the flow map.
∎

Proof of Theorem

We will prove that, for arbitrarily large

(5.9)

Clearly, for any

which implies Theorem
1.5
. For the details of the lack of the twice differentiability of the flow map, we refer to Section 6 of
[
9
]

Let
N
,
M
be dyadic, and
0\leq j\leq N-1
. We define the set

A_{N}=\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,|\,N\leq|\xi|\leq N+N^{-1}\},

and the radial function
f_{N}
as

\mathcal{F}_{x}{f_{N}}\,=\,N^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf{1}}_{A_{N}}.

Clearly,
\|f_{N}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\sim 1
.
For
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
\widetilde{\xi}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we define

\Phi(\xi,\widetilde{\xi})=|\xi|^{2}-|\xi-\widetilde{\xi}|^{2}+|\widetilde{\xi}|^{2}.

As in the proof of (
5.3
), we can observe that
|\Phi(\xi,\widetilde{\xi})|\leq 2^{3}
if
|\xi|\leq 1
,
\widetilde{\xi}\in A_{N}
, and
\widetilde{\xi}-\xi\in A_{N}
.
We will show that if

, it holds that

\Bigl\|{\bf 1}_{B_{1}}(\xi)\mathcal{F}_{x}\Bigl(\int^{t}_{0}e^{-i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}|e^{-it^{\prime}\Delta}f_{N}|^{2}dt^{\prime}\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\gtrsim t(\log N)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(5.10)

For arbitrarily large
C\gg 1
, (
5.9
) follows from (
5.10
) by taking
N
large.
Applying the Fourier transform, (
5.10
) is given by

\Bigl\|{\bf 1}_{B_{1}}(\xi)\int^{t}_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{-it^{\prime}\Phi(\xi,\widetilde{\xi})}{\bf 1}_{A_{N}}(\xi-\widetilde{\xi}){\bf 1}_{A_{N}}(-\widetilde{\xi})d\widetilde{\xi}dt^{\prime}\Bigr\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\gtrsim t(\log N)^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{-1}.

(5.11)

Since

and

, we see

for any

. Hence, (
5.11
) is equivalent to

\Bigl\|{\bf 1}_{B_{1}}(\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{\bf 1}_{A_{N}}(\xi-\widetilde{\xi}){\bf 1}_{A_{N}}(-\widetilde{\xi})d\widetilde{\xi}\Bigr\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\gtrsim(\log N)^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{-1}.

(5.12)

Let
j\in 2^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
satisfy
0\leq j\leq N-1
. We define

{\mathfrak{D}}_{N,j}=\Bigl\{\xi=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\in A_{N}\,\Bigl|\,\theta\in\Bigl[\frac{2\pi}{N}j,\,\frac{2\pi}{N}\,(j+1)\Bigr]\Bigr\}.

Then, it follows from
A_{N}=\bigcup_{0\leq j\leq N-1}{\mathfrak{D}}_{N,j}
that

\begin{split}&\Bigl\|{\bf 1}_{B_{1}}(\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{\bf 1}_{A_{N}}(\xi-\widetilde{\xi}){\bf 1}_{A_{N}}(-\widetilde{\xi})d\widetilde{\xi}\Bigr\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\\
\geq&\Bigl\|\sum_{0\leq j\leq N-1}{\bf 1}_{B_{1}}(\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{\bf 1}_{{\mathfrak{D}}_{N,j}}(\xi-\widetilde{\xi}){\bf 1}_{{\mathfrak{D}}_{N,j}}(-\widetilde{\xi})d\widetilde{\xi}\Bigr\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}.\end{split}

(5.13)

We define the line segments
\{L_{j}\}
and the tube sets
\{T_{N,j}\}
by

\displaystyle L_{j}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\{\Bigl(-r\sin\frac{2\pi}{N}j,\,r\cos\frac{2\pi}{N}j\Bigr)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,\Bigl|\,|r|\leq 2^{-5}\Bigr\},

\displaystyle T_{N,j}

\displaystyle=\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,|\,\inf_{\eta\in L_{j}}|\xi-\eta|\leq 2^{-5}N^{-1}\}.

By the same argument as in the proof of (
5.5
), we may obtain

{\bf 1}_{B_{1}}(\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}{\bf 1}_{{\mathfrak{D}}_{N,j}}(\xi-\widetilde{\xi}){\bf 1}_{{\mathfrak{D}}_{N,j}}(-\widetilde{\xi})d\widetilde{\xi}\gtrsim N^{-1}{\bf 1}_{T_{N,j}}(\xi).

Therefore, combined with (
5.13
), the estimate (
5.12
) is verified by

\Bigl\|\sum_{0\leq j\leq N-1}{\bf 1}_{T_{N,j}}\Bigr\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\sim(\log N)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(5.14)

For

By utilizing this, we have

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{0\leq j\leq N-1}{\bf 1}_{T_{N,j}}\Bigr\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\sum_{0\leq j,k\leq N-1}{\bf 1}_{T_{N,j}}(\xi){\bf 1}_{T_{N,k}}(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{0\leq j,k\leq N-1}\frac{1}{N(1+|j-k|)}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq N-1}\frac{1}{(1+\ell)}\sim\log N.

This completes the proof of (
5.14
).

\displaystyle A_{2}(\varepsilon)=\{(\tau,\xi)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\,|\,|\tau+|\xi|^{2}+2\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}|\leq\varepsilon,\ 1-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq|\xi|\leq 1-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+\varepsilon\},

\displaystyle A_{3,N}(\varepsilon)=\{(\tau,\xi)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\,|\,|\tau-|\xi|^{2}|\leq 2^{-5}\varepsilon,\ \varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+N\varepsilon\leq|\xi|\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+2N\varepsilon\}.

Let
S_{1}=S_{3}=\{(|\xi|^{2},\xi)\,|\,\xi\in B_{2}\}
and
\widetilde{S}_{2}=\{(-|\xi|^{2}-2\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},\xi)\,|\,\xi\in B_{2}\}

S_{1}
,
\widetilde{S}_{2}
,

Assumption

2
and
A_{1}(\varepsilon)\subset S_{1}(\varepsilon)
,
A_{2}(\varepsilon)\subset\widetilde{S}_{2}(\varepsilon)

\bigcup_{1\leq N\leq 2^{-5}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}}A_{3,N}(\varepsilon)\subset S_{3}(\varepsilon)

|{\bf 1}_{A_{1}(\varepsilon)}*{\bf 1}_{A_{2}(\varepsilon)}*{\bf 1}_{A_{3,N}(\varepsilon)}(0)|\sim N^{\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{19}{4}}.

For fixed

, we will show

(5.19)

This readily gives (
5.18
). We consider (

The proof is analogous to the geometric observation in

.
For

We need to prove that the measure of

is comparable to
N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{9}{4}}

and ignore the time component. It suffices to show that the measure of the set

\biggl\{(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,\biggl|\,\begin{aligned} &1\leq r\leq 1+\varepsilon,\\
&1-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq|(r\cos\theta-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}-N\varepsilon,r\sin\theta)|\leq 1-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+\varepsilon\end{aligned}\biggr\}

is bounded from below by

.
By symmetry, we may assume
\theta\in[0,\pi]
.

For fixed
r
satisfies

, we define the function

by

F_{r}(\theta)=|(r\cos\theta-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}-N\varepsilon,r\sin\theta)|.

Clearly, the claim follows once we find

such that for any
\theta

1-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq F_{r}(\theta)\leq 1-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+\varepsilon.

is a smooth and monotonically increasing function and it holds that

\displaystyle F_{r}\bigl(\frac{\pi}{4}\bigr)=\Bigl|\Bigl(r\cos\frac{\pi}{4}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}-N\varepsilon,r\sin\frac{\pi}{4}\Bigr)\Bigr|\geq 1-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+\varepsilon.

Hence, we may find

Next, let us observe

From

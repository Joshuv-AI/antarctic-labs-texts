Hierarchical Bayes Approach to Personalized Federated Unsupervised Learning
Kaan Ozkara, Bruce Huang, Ruida Zhou, Suhas Diggavi

Abstract
Statistical heterogeneity of clients’ local data is an important characteristic in federated learning, motivating personalized algorithms tailored to the local data statistics. Though there has been a plethora of algorithms proposed for personalized supervised learning, discovering the structure of local data through personalized unsupervised learning is less explored. We initiate a systematic study of such personalized unsupervised learning by developing algorithms based on optimization criteria inspired by a hierarchical Bayesian statistical framework. We develop adaptive algorithms that discover the balance between using limited local data and collaborative information. We do this in the context of two unsupervised learning tasks: personalized dimensionality reduction and personalized diffusion models. We develop convergence analyses for our adaptive algorithms which illustrate the dependence on problem parameters ( e.g., heterogeneity, local sample size). We also develop a theoretical framework for personalized diffusion models, which shows the benefits of collaboration even under heterogeneity. We finally evaluate our proposed algorithms using synthetic and real data, demonstrating the effective sample amplification for personalized tasks, induced through collaboration, despite data heterogeneity.

Introduction

One of the goals of unsupervised learning is to discover the underlying structure in data and use this for tasks such as dimensionality reduction and generating new samples from the data distribution. We might want to perform this task on local data of a client,
e.g.,
data collected from personal sensors or other devices; such data could have heterogeneous statistics across clients. The desired
personalized
task should be tailored to the particular distribution of the local data, and hence to discover this structure one might need a significant amount of local data. There might be insufficient local samples for the task, motivating collaboration between clients. Moreover, as argued in the federated learning (FL) paradigm, we would like to leverage data across clients without explicit data sharing
[
26
,
16
]
. In this paper, we initiate a systematic study of personalized federated unsupervised learning, where clients collaborate to discover personalized structure in their (heterogeneous) local data.

There has been a plethora of personalized learning models proposed in the literature, mostly for

\displaystyle d\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\leftarrow}=\nabla\ln p_{\boldsymbol{x}_{T-t}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\leftarrow})dt+d\boldsymbol{w}^{\leftarrow}_{t},\boldsymbol{x}_{0}^{\leftarrow}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\theta},(\sigma_{0}^{2}+T)\boldsymbol{I}_{d}).

(14)

Without the knowledge of
\boldsymbol{\theta}
, we approximate the score function by a neural network of
\boldsymbol{\phi}(\boldsymbol{x};\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}},t)=-\frac{\boldsymbol{x}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+t}
and approximate the initial distribution of the time-reversed process
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\theta},(\sigma_{0}^{2}+T)\boldsymbol{I}_{d})
by
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},(\sigma_{0}^{2}+T)\boldsymbol{I}_{d})
. The learned/approximated time-reversed process is then

\displaystyle d\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\leftarrow}=-\frac{\boldsymbol{x}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+t}dt+d\boldsymbol{w}^{\leftarrow}_{t},\boldsymbol{x}_{0}^{\leftarrow}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},(\sigma_{0}^{2}+T)\boldsymbol{I}_{d}).

(15)

The following lemma characterizes the difference between the generation distribution and the target distribution.

Lemma 4.2
.

The output distribution
p_{\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{T}|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
of the learned reversed-time process (
15
) satisfies,

\displaystyle D_{KL}(p_{\boldsymbol{x}|\boldsymbol{\theta}}||p_{\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{T}|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}})=\left\|\boldsymbol{\theta}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}+\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right\|^{2},

(16)

where
D_{KL}
is the Kullback-Leibler divergence and
p_{\boldsymbol{x}|\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\sigma_{0}^{2}\boldsymbol{I}_{d})
is the target distribution.

The lemma shows that the KL-divergence between target distribution and the learned distribution is measured by the difference between
\boldsymbol{\theta}
and
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
a bias term
\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
due to the initial distribution mismatch of the time-reversed process.

It is straightforward to verify that for
\boldsymbol{\phi}(\boldsymbol{x};\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}},t)=-\frac{\boldsymbol{x}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+t}
, training with dataset
\boldsymbol{X}=(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{x}_{n})
by maximizing ELBO (
9
) has a closed-form sample-mean solution, i.e.,
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\argmax_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}ELBO_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{X})=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{j}}{n}
. Since the data are i.i.d. sampled from
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\sigma_{0}^{2}\boldsymbol{I}_{d})
, we have
\mathbb{E}[D_{KL}(p_{\boldsymbol{x}|\boldsymbol{\theta}}||p_{\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{T}|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}})]=\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}
, when omitting the initial distribution bias by
T\rightarrow\infty
. It can be viewed as personalized training without collaboration and in the following, we show that the proposed personalized training with collaboration can improve over this.

Personalized denoising diffusion model with Gaussian targets:

The local dataset
\boldsymbol{X}_{i}
of client-
i
sampled i.i.d. from a target Gaussian distribution
P(\boldsymbol{x}|\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i})=\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\theta}_{i},\sigma_{0}^{2}\boldsymbol{I}_{d})
, and the population distribution is also Gaussian with
P(\boldsymbol{\theta}|{\Gamma_{*}})=\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\theta};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{*},\sigma_{*}^{2}\boldsymbol{I}_{d})
. Note that we conduct analysis for a fixed unknown population distribution parameterized by
\Gamma_{*}=(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{*},\sigma_{*})
, though the proposed loss function and algorithm follows a Hierarchical Bayesian model as in Section
2.3
.

Lemma 4.3

(Personalized estimation)
.

For the parameterized score function
\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\boldsymbol{x};\boldsymbol{\theta},t)=-\frac{\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{\theta}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+t}
, the optimal solution to (
10
) is

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{ij}}{mn}

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{n\alpha\hat{\sigma}^{2}}{n\alpha\hat{\sigma}^{2}+1}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{ij}}{n}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\hat{\mu}}}{n\alpha\hat{\sigma}^{2}+1},

where
\alpha=\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+T}
and
\hat{\sigma}^{2}
satisfies
\hat{\sigma}^{2}=\frac{2\xi}{d}+s^{2}(\frac{n\alpha\hat{\sigma}^{2}}{n\alpha\hat{\sigma}^{2}+1})^{2}
with
s^{2}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{ij}\right\|^{2}}{md}
.

Remark 4.4
.

We note that the global optimum model is the average of average samples of each client. The personalized optimum model is interpolation of local estimate and the true global model. The interpolation coefficient depends on the heterogeneity (large
\sigma
skews the result towards the local estimate and low
\sigma
skews it towards true global model). A large amount of local samples
n
decreases the reliance on
\boldsymbol{\mu}
These observations are in parallel with findings in
[
28
]
on mean estimation.

Theorem 4.5

(Condition for performance improvement)
.

Consider an asymptotic regime that
m\rightarrow\infty
and
T\rightarrow\infty
. Compared to training without collaboration, the solution of
(\{\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}\},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\hat{\sigma}^{2})
in Lemma
4.3
improves the averaged KL-divergence
\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}D_{KL}(p_{\boldsymbol{x}|\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}}||p_{\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{T}|\hat{\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}_{i}})
by a factor of
\left(\frac{2\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n-\sigma_{*}^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n}\right)\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}/n}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n}\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}
, when
\hat{\sigma}^{2}>\frac{\sigma_{*}^{2}}{2}-\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{2n}
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Under the same setting as in Theorem
4.5
, choosing
\xi\geq\frac{3d\sigma_{0}^{2}}{2n}
guarantees strict improvement of collaboration for any population distribution
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{*},\sigma_{*}\boldsymbol{I}_{d})
.

Remark 4.7
.

Note that if our estimate
\hat{\sigma}^{2}
of the population variance is accurate,
i.e.,

\hat{\sigma}^{2}=\sigma_{*}^{2}
, then collaboration always improves over only using local data for personalized generation; and in fact the collaboration gain is the largest. In this case, the gain is larger when the number of local samples is relatively small. However, if the estimate is inaccurate, one could, in principle be better off without collaboration. However, by setting the hyperparameter
\xi\geq\frac{3d\sigma_{0}^{2}}{2n}
, we can ensure that our estimate
\hat{\sigma}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\sigma_{*}^{2}-\sigma_{0}^{2}/n\right)
, ensuring that collaboration is useful.

4.1
Proofs

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Let
\beta_{t}=\frac{1}{T-t+\sigma_{0}^{2}}
. For stochastic differential equation

\displaystyle d\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{t}+\beta_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{t}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})dt=d\boldsymbol{w}_{t},\quad\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,(\sigma_{0}^{2}+T)\boldsymbol{I}_{d}),

we have

\displaystyle d(e^{\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{s}ds}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{t}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}))=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{s}ds}d\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{t}+e^{\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{s}ds}\beta_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{t}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})dt

\displaystyle=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{s}ds}\left(d\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{t}+\beta_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{t}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})dt\right)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{s}ds}d\boldsymbol{w}_{t}.

Note that

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{s}ds}=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{T-s+\sigma_{0}^{2}}ds}=e^{\ln(T+\sigma_{0}^{2})-\ln(T-t+\sigma_{0}^{2})}=\frac{T+\sigma_{0}^{2}}{T-t+\sigma_{0}^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{s}ds}dt=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{(T+\sigma_{0}^{2})^{2}}{(T+\sigma_{0}^{2}-t)^{2}}dt=(T+\sigma_{0}^{2})^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{T+\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right)=\left(1+\frac{T}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right)T.

It then follows that

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{T}\beta_{s}ds}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{T}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})-(\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{0}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{s}ds}dt\right)=\mathcal{N}\left(0,\left(1+\frac{T}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right)T\right),

and equivalently

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{T}=\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}+\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+T}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{0}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})+\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}T}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+T}}\boldsymbol{\epsilon},

where
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\boldsymbol{I}_{d})
.

Since
\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,(\sigma_{0}^{2}+T)\boldsymbol{I}_{d})
, we have

p_{\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{T}|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}=\mathcal{N}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}-\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}},\sigma_{0}^{2}\boldsymbol{I}_{d}\right)
.
The KL-divergence between the target distribution
p_{\boldsymbol{x}|\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\sigma_{0}^{2}\boldsymbol{I}_{d})
and
p_{\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{T}|\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
can then be calculated as
\left\|\boldsymbol{\theta}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}+\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right\|^{2}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

The parameterized score function is
\boldsymbol{\phi}(x;\boldsymbol{\theta},t)=-\frac{\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{\theta}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+t}
. Note that
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}_{t}}\ln p_{\boldsymbol{x}_{t}|\boldsymbol{x}_{0}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t}|\boldsymbol{x}_{0})=-\frac{\boldsymbol{x}_{t}-\boldsymbol{x}_{0}}{t}
since
\boldsymbol{x}_{t}|\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{x}_{0},t\boldsymbol{I}_{d})
. The training loss of (
10
) can then be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\boldsymbol{I}_{d})}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{\phi}(\boldsymbol{x}_{ij}+\sqrt{t}\boldsymbol{\epsilon};\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j},t)+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}/\sqrt{t}\right\|^{2}\right]dt+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{2\xi+\|\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\mu}\|^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}+\frac{d}{2}\ln\sigma^{2}.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\boldsymbol{I}_{d})}[\|\boldsymbol{\phi}(\boldsymbol{x}_{ij}+\sqrt{t}\boldsymbol{\epsilon};\boldsymbol{\theta},t)+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}/\sqrt{t}\|^{2}]dt=\int_{t=0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\boldsymbol{I}_{d})}\left[\left\|-\frac{\boldsymbol{x}_{ij}+\sqrt{t}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}-\boldsymbol{\theta}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+t}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{t}}\right\|^{2}\right]dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\boldsymbol{I}_{d})}\left[\left\|\frac{\boldsymbol{\theta}-\boldsymbol{x}_{ij}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+t}+\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{\sqrt{t}(\sigma_{0}^{2}+t)}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}\right]dt=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{(\sigma_{0}^{2}+t)^{2}}dt\right)\|\boldsymbol{\theta}-\boldsymbol{x}_{ij}\|^{2}+\text{const}.

Since
\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{(\sigma_{0}^{2}+t)^{2}}dt=\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+T}
, the optimization of minimizing the training loss is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1:m},\boldsymbol{\theta},\sigma^{2}}\quad\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{j}-\boldsymbol{x}_{ij}\|^{2}+\frac{2\xi+\|\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\|^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)+\frac{d}{2}\ln\sigma^{2},

where
\alpha=\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+T}
.
By the KKT condition that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha(\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}-\boldsymbol{x}_{ij})+\frac{\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\mu}}}{\sigma^{2}}=0,\quad\forall i=1,2,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i},

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{2\xi+\|\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\|^{2}}{2\sigma^{4}}+\frac{d}{2\sigma^{2}}=0,

We thus have

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{ij}}{mn}

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{n\alpha\hat{\sigma}^{2}}{n\alpha\hat{\sigma}^{2}+1}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{ij}}{n}+\frac{\boldsymbol{\hat{\mu}}}{n\alpha\hat{\sigma}^{2}+1},

where
\hat{\sigma}^{2}
satisfies
\hat{\sigma}^{2}=\frac{2\xi}{d}+s^{2}(\frac{n\alpha\hat{\sigma}^{2}}{n\alpha\hat{\sigma}^{2}+1})^{2}
with
s^{2}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{ij}\right\|^{2}}{md}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

When
m\rightarrow\infty
,
s^{2}\rightarrow\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}+\sigma_{*}^{2}
and
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\mu}_{*}
, a.s..

\hat{\sigma}^{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}^{2}=\frac{2\xi}{d}+(\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}+\sigma_{*}^{2})(\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}+1/(n\alpha)})^{2}.

Since
\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}
are sampled i.i.d. from a population distribution
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{*},\sigma_{*}^{2}\boldsymbol{I}_{d})
.
\alpha=1/\sigma_{0}^{2}
since
T\rightarrow\infty
. Let
\boldsymbol{x}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}},\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}\boldsymbol{I}_{d})
,
\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\sigma^{2}\boldsymbol{I}_{d})
, by Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}D_{KL}(p_{\boldsymbol{x}|\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}}||p_{\boldsymbol{x}^{\leftarrow}_{T}|\hat{\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}_{i}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}_{i}+\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}+T}\hat{\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}_{i}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}-\boldsymbol{x}-\frac{1}{n\alpha\hat{\sigma}^{2}+1}(\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\mu}}-\boldsymbol{x})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=(\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}/n}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n})^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}-\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\right\|^{2}\right]+(\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n})^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}-\boldsymbol{x}\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=(\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}/n}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n})^{2}\sigma_{*}^{2}+(\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n})^{2}\sigma_{0}^{2}/n

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}+\frac{(\sigma_{*}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n)\sigma_{0}^{2}/n}{(\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n)^{2}}\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}-2\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}/n}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n}\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}-\left(\frac{2\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n-\sigma_{*}^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n}\right)\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}/n}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n}\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}

where the expectation is taken w.r.t.
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
.

Since without collaboration, the training of maximizing
ELBO_{i}
for client-
i
leads to parameter
\hat{\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}_{i}=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{x}_{ij}}{n}
and the KL-divergence between the target distribution and the output distribution is
\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}
, it follows that collaboration improves the performance as long as
\hat{\sigma}^{2}>\frac{\sigma_{*}^{2}}{2}-\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{2n}
.

The improvement is
\left(\frac{2\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n-\sigma_{*}^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n}\right)\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}/n}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n}\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}
and achieves the maximum when
\hat{\sigma}^{2}=\sigma_{*}^{2}
, i.e., the learned
\hat{\sigma}^{2}=\sigma_{*}^{2}

∎

Proof of Corollary
4.6
.

Under the same setting as in Theorem
4.5
, by Lemma
4.3
, we have
\hat{\sigma}^{2}=\frac{2\xi}{d}+(\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}+\sigma_{*}^{2})(\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n})^{2}
. Taking
\xi>\frac{2\xi}{d}=\frac{3d\sigma_{0}^{2}}{2n}
gives that
\hat{\sigma}^{2}\geq\frac{3\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}
, and thus
\hat{\sigma}^{2}>(\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}+\sigma_{*}^{2})(\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}/n})^{2}\geq\frac{9}{16}(\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n}+\sigma_{*}^{2})>\frac{\sigma_{*}^{2}}{2}-\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{2n}
, which guarantees strictly improvement by Theorem
4.5
.
∎

\displaystyle\;\frac{\eta_{3}}{2}\Bigg[\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\frac{\partial}{\partial{\sigma_{t-1}}}}f_{i}^{\mathrm{pca}}(\boldsymbol{U}_{i,t},\boldsymbol{V}_{t},\sigma_{t-1})-{\frac{\partial}{\partial{\sigma_{t-1}}}}f_{i}^{\mathrm{pca}}(\boldsymbol{U}_{i,t-1},\boldsymbol{V}_{t},\sigma_{t-1})\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+{\frac{\partial}{\partial{\sigma_{t-1}}}}f^{\mathrm{pca}}(\{\boldsymbol{U}_{i,t-1}\}_{i},\boldsymbol{V}_{t},\sigma_{t-1})-{\frac{\partial}{\partial{\sigma_{t-1}}}}f^{\mathrm{pca}}(\{\boldsymbol{U}_{i,t-1}\}_{i},\boldsymbol{V}_{t-1},\sigma_{t-1})\Bigg]^{2}

\displaystyle\;\left(-\eta_{1}+C_{\eta_{1}}\eta_{1}^{2}+\eta_{3}(L_{U}^{(\sigma)})^{2}\right)\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lVert P_{\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{U}_{i,t-1}}}\left(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{U}_{i,t-1}}f_{i}^{\mathrm{pca}}(\boldsymbol{U}_{i,t-1},\boldsymbol{V}_{t-1},,\sigma_{t-1})\right)\rVert_{F}^{2}

, and

\displaystyle\frac{-\eta_{3}+\eta_{3}^{2}L_{\sigma}}{2}=\eta_{3}\left(L_{\sigma}\eta_{3}-\frac{1}{6}\right)-\frac{\eta_{3}}{3}\leq-\frac{\eta_{3}}{3}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle f^{\mathrm{pca}}(\{\boldsymbol{U}_{i,t}\}_{i},\boldsymbol{V}_{t},\sigma_{t})-

\displaystyle f^{\mathrm{pca}}(\{\boldsymbol{U}_{i,t-1}\}_{i},\boldsymbol{V}_{t-1},\sigma_{t-1})\leq-\frac{\eta_{1}}{3}\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lVert P_{\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{U}_{i,t-1}}}\left(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{U}_{i,t-1}}f_{i}^{\mathrm{pca}}(\boldsymbol{U}_{i,t-1},\boldsymbol{V}_{t-1},,\sigma_{t-1})\right)\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\eta_{2}}{3}\lVert P_{\mathcal{T}_{\boldsymbol{V}_{t-1}}}\left(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{V}_{t-1}}f^{\mathrm{pca}}(\{\boldsymbol{U}_{i,t}\}_{i},\boldsymbol{V}_{t-1},\sigma_{t-1})\right)\rVert_{F}^{2}-\frac{\eta_{3}}{3}\left[{\frac{\partial}{\partial{\sigma_{t-1}}}}f^{\mathrm{pca}}(\{\boldsymbol{U}_{i,t-1}\}_{i},\boldsymbol{V}_{t-1},\sigma_{t-1})\right]^{2}.

Proof.

The gradient w.r.t.

is

\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}f_{i}^{\mathrm{ae}}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\mu},\sigma)=\frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\theta}}{2\sigma^{2}}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\theta}}{2\sigma^{2}}-\frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}-\boldsymbol{\theta}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right\|=\left\|\frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right\|\leq\frac{d_{\theta}}{2\xi\omega^{2}}\|\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}\|\leq L_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\|\boldsymbol{\mu}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}\|.

For
L_{\sigma}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\sigma^{2}}f_{i}^{\mathrm{ae}}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\mu},\sigma)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{6\xi+3\|\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\theta}\|^{2}}{\sigma^{4}}-\frac{d_{\theta}}{\sigma^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{6\xi+3\|\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\theta}\|^{2}}{\sigma^{4}}\right|+\left|\frac{d_{\theta}}{\sigma^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6\xi+3(2B)^{2}}{4\xi^{2}\omega^{4}/d_{\theta}^{2}}+\frac{d_{\theta}^{2}}{2\xi\omega^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{3\xi d_{\theta}^{2}}{2\xi^{2}\omega^{4}}+\frac{3d_{\theta}^{2}B^{2}}{\xi^{2}\omega^{4}}+\frac{d_{\theta}^{2}}{2\xi\omega^{2}}

\displaystyle=L_{\sigma}.

For
L_{\sigma}^{(\boldsymbol{\mu})}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}f_{i}^{\mathrm{ae}}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\mu},\sigma_{1})-\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}f_{i}^{\mathrm{ae}}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\mu},\sigma_{2})\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\theta}}{2\sigma_{1}^{2}}-\frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\theta}}{2\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right|\frac{\|\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\theta}\|}{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}\right|\left|\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}^{2}\sigma_{2}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right|\frac{\|\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\theta}\|}{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\omega\sqrt{\frac{2\xi}{d_{\theta}}}\right)^{-3}B|\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}|

\displaystyle=\frac{B\sqrt{d_{\theta}^{3}}}{\omega^{3}\sqrt{2\xi^{3}}}|\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}|

\displaystyle=L_{\sigma}^{(\boldsymbol{\mu})}|\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}|.

Population dynamics under demographic and environmental stochasticity
[

Abstract
The present paper is devoted to the study of the long term dynamics of diffusion processes modelling a single species that experiences both demographic and environmental stochasticity. In our setting, the long term dynamics of the diffusion process in the absence of demographic stochasticity is determined by the sign of Λ 0 \Lambda_{0} , the external Lyapunov exponent, as follows: Λ 0 < 0 \Lambda_{0}<0 implies (asymptotic) extinction and Λ 0 > 0 \Lambda_{0}>0 implies convergence to a unique positive stationary distribution μ 0 \mu_{0} . If the system is of size 1 ϵ 2 \frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}} for small ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 (the intensity of demographic stochasticity), demographic effects will make the extinction time finite almost surely. This suggests that to understand the dynamics one should analyze the quasi-stationary distribution (QSD) μ ϵ \mu_{\epsilon} of the system. The existence and uniqueness of the QSD is well-known under mild assumptions. We look at what happens when the population size is sent to infinity, i.e., when ϵ → 0 \epsilon\to 0 . We show that the external Lyapunov exponent still plays a key role: 1) If Λ 0 < 0 \Lambda_{0}<0 , then μ ϵ → δ 0 \mu_{\epsilon}\to\delta_{0} , the mean extinction time is of order | ln ⁡ ϵ | |\ln\epsilon| and the extinction rate associated with the QSD μ ϵ \mu_{\epsilon} has a lower bound of order 1 | ln ⁡ ϵ | \frac{1}{|\ln\epsilon|} ; 2) If Λ 0 > 0 \Lambda_{0}>0 , then μ ϵ → μ 0 \mu_{\epsilon}\to\mu_{0} , the mean extinction time is polynomial in 1 ϵ 2 \frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}} and the extinction rate is polynomial in ϵ 2 \epsilon^{2} . Furthermore, when Λ 0 > 0 \Lambda_{0}>0 we are able to show that the system exhibits multiscale dynamics: at first the process quickly approaches the QSD μ ϵ \mu_{\epsilon} and then, after spending a polynomially long time there, it relaxes to the extinction state. We give sharp asymptotics in ϵ \epsilon for the time spent close to μ ϵ \mu_{\epsilon} . In contrast to models that only take into account demographic stochasticity, our results demonstrate the significant effect of environmental stochasticity – it turns an exponentially long mean extinction time to a sub-exponential one.

Introduction

One of the most important questions from population dynamics is figuring out when a species persists or goes extinct. For deterministic models, persistence is usually quantified via the existence of an attractor that is bounded away from zero (the extinction state). In this setting extinction can only happen asymptotically as time goes to infinity. However, any realistic ecological model has to take into account various intrinsic and extrinsic random environmental fluctuations. Usually there are either ecological models that take into account environmental stochasticity that arises due to fluctuations of the environment, or models from population genetics that focus on demographic stochasticity, which arises because of the randomness due to reproduction in a finite population. There are few analytic models which account for the effects of both types of stochasticity.

If the system is of size
\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}
for some small
\epsilon>0
(intensity of the demographic noise), the presence of demographic effects will make the extinction time finite almost surely. As a result, in order to gain some information about the behavior of the process before extinction, it is natural and useful to look at quasi-stationary distributions (QSDs)
[
13
,
46
]
, i.e., stationary distributions of the process conditioned on not going extinct. A key problem is to study scaling limits of systems that have QSDs and see what happens with the family of QSDs as the intensity of the demographic noise is sent to zero.

The main goal of this paper is to analyze the dynamics of systems that have both types of stochasticity and can be modelled by stochastic differential equations (SDEs). We focus on the QSD and the extinction time as well as related quantities such as the extinction rate and the exponential convergence rate to the QSD, and investigate their asymptotic properties as the intensity of the demographic noise vanishes – a particular emphasis is put on the connections to properties of the limit system. Models with both types of stochasticity are more realistic as natural systems usually experience both types of randomness. The sharp criteria we find for the persistence and extinction of species are therefore more relevant to the modelling of natural ecosystems – see
[
26
,
19
]
.

Systems perturbed by either the environmental or demographic stochasticity have been attracting a lot of attention. If one looks at models that only have environmental stochasticity, there already exist many sharp results in the literature. In the one-dimensional setting, a full classification is possible by the well-known scale function and speed measure description of diffusions
[
4
]
. In the multi-dimensional setting things are more complicated. Some general theory for the existence and uniqueness of stationary distributions can be found in
[
38
]
, while the most up to date results for Kolmogorov systems are in
[

,

,

.

For models with only demographic stochasticity, asymptotic properties of QSDs and related quantities as the intensity of the stochasticity vanishes are often the focus of studies. They have been investigated for randomly perturbed dynamical systems and rescaled Markov jump processes. The first work seemingly dates back to
[
31
]
, where the author studied the stochastic Ricker model. This work was generalized in
[
42
,
52
]
to randomly perturbed interval maps that apply to density-dependent branching processes. Further generalizations were considered in
[
35

21

These works illuminate two fundamental properties when the unperturbed deterministic system has a global attractor which is bounded away from extinction: 1) QSDs tend to concentrate on the deterministic attractor as the noise intensity vanishes. 2) The extinction rate associated with a QSD is exponentially small with respect to the system size (i.e., the reciprocal of the noise intensity squared), and therefore, the extinction time grows exponentially with the system size if the initial distribution is given by the QSD. Concentration properties of QSDs as in 1) are in line with that of stationary distributions for randomly perturbed dynamical systems (see e.g.

Rescaled absorbed birth-and-death processes whose mean-field ODEs have a global asymptotically stable equilibrium have been investigated in

This paper is a first step towards generalizing the theory of randomly perturbed dynamical systems without absorbing states and randomly perturbed dynamical systems with absorbing states and only demographic noises to a theory of randomly perturbed dynamical systems with absorbing states and multiple types of noise. Inspired by the aforementioned theories of noise-vanishing asymptotics of stationary distributions, QSDs, and related quantities, and motivated by the fact that real systems are subject to both intrinsic and extrinsic stochastic perturbations, we intend to establish an analogous theory for dynamical systems under both environmental and demographic noise perturbations, and study the effects of both types of noises.

In the present paper, we consider one-dimensional SDEs with both environmental and demographic stochasticity:

dX^{\epsilon}_{t}=b(X^{\epsilon}_{t})dt+\sigma(X^{\epsilon}_{t})dB_{t}+\epsilon\sqrt{a(X^{\epsilon}_{t})}dW_{t}\quad\text{in}\quad[0,\infty),

where the coefficients
b
,
\sigma
and
a
satisfy natural assumptions. Let
T^{\epsilon}_{0}=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:X^{\epsilon}_{t}=0\right\}
be the extinction time of
X^{\epsilon}_{t}
. It is finite almost surely. Denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
the self-adjoint extension in
L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G}):=L^{2}((0,\infty),u_{\epsilon}^{G}dx)
of the generator of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
, where
u_{\epsilon}^{G}
is the non-integrable Gibbs density of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
as it grows like
\frac{1}{x}
as
x\to 0^{+}
. The spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
is purely discrete. Depending on the dynamics of the limiting SDE, which only has an environmental stochasticity term:

dX^{0}_{t}=b(X^{0}_{t})dt+\sigma(X^{0}_{t})dB_{t}\quad\text{in}\quad[0,\infty),

we are able to prove the following results (with rigorous statements given in Section
2
):

(I)
Suppose
\Lambda_{0}:=b^{\prime}(0)-\frac{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{2}>0
so that
X_{t}^{0}
has a unique stationary distribution
\mu_{0}
that does not put mass on the extinction state

•

The unique QSD

as the intensity of the demographic noise goes to zero, that is,
\epsilon\to 0

\lambda_{\epsilon,1}\sim\epsilon^{\frac{4b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}-2}
.

•

The normalized extinction time

\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\bullet}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]\sim\frac{1}{\lambda_{\epsilon,1}}\sim\epsilon^{2-\frac{4b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}}=\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\right)^{\frac{2b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}-1}.

The polynomial asymptotics of the extinction rate

The fact that the dependence changes from exponential to polynomial in the presence of environmental stochasticity has been recently showcased empirically and numerically in simple ecological models

,
19
]
.

•

The eigenfunction

The distribution of

If

The estimate (

(II)
Suppose
\Lambda_{0}<0
so that

goes extinct as

•

As
\epsilon\to 0

Denote by

Proving that

Given the fact that eigenfunctions of

The asymptotic of the extinction rate

2
Mathematical setup and main results

We consider the following family of SDEs:

dX^{\epsilon}_{t}=b(X^{\epsilon}_{t})dt+\sigma(X^{\epsilon}_{t})dB_{t}+\epsilon\sqrt{a(X^{\epsilon}_{t})}dW_{t}\quad\text{in}\quad[0,\infty),

(2.1)

where
0<\epsilon\ll 1
is a small parameter,
b,\sigma:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
,
a:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
and
B_{t},W_{t}
are two independent standard one-dimensional Brownian motions on some probability space. Here,
\sigma dB_{t}
models environmental stochasticity and
\epsilon\sqrt{a}dW_{t}
represents demographic stochasticity. Hence,
\epsilon
stands for the intensity of the demographic stochasticity. We point out that
\epsilon^{2}
is inversely proportional to the population size, and hence, tends to
0
as the population size goes to infinity.

Throughout this paper, we make the following assumptions on the coefficients
b
,
\sigma
and
a
.

(H)

The functions
b:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
,
\sigma:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
and
a:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
are assumed to satisfy the following conditions:

(1)

b\in C^{1}([0,\infty))
,

b(0)=0
,
b^{\prime}(0)>0
, and
\limsup_{x\to\infty}b(x)<0
;

(2)

\sigma\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
,
\sigma(0)=0
and
\sigma^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
;

(3)

a\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
,
a(0)=0
,
a^{\prime}(0)>0
, and
a>0
on
(0,\infty)
;

(4)

there holds

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{a(x)}{\sigma^{2}(x)}<\infty,\quad\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{b(x)}{|\sigma(x)|}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{xb(x)}{\sigma^{2}(x)}=-\infty,

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sigma^{2}(x)}{|b(x)|}\max\left\{\frac{a^{\prime}(x)}{a(x)},\frac{|\sigma^{\prime}(x)|}{|\sigma(x)|}\right\}=0,\quad\,\,\text{and}\,\,\quad

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sigma^{2}(x)}{b^{2}(x)}\max\left\{a^{\prime\prime}(x),(\sigma^{2}(x))^{\prime\prime},|b^{\prime}(x)|\right\}=0.

(H)

(H)
(2) is satisfied if

and often appears in modeling environmental stochasticity. We note that in most applications one has
\sigma(x)=\sigma x
for some

.
(H)

and behaves like

). The condition

(1) and the growth conditions on

) forms a dissipative system. Other conditions in
(H)
(4) restricting the derivatives of
a
,
b
and
\sigma^{2}
near
\infty
are mild technical assumptions. The assumption
(H)
applies in particular to the logistic diffusion:

dX_{t}=X_{t}(\mu-\kappa X_{t})dt+\sigma X_{t}dB_{t}+\epsilon\sqrt{\gamma X_{t}}dW_{t}\quad\text{in}\quad[0,\infty)

and the stochastic theta logistic model (with
\theta>0
):

dX_{t}=X_{t}(\mu-\kappa X_{t}^{\theta})dt+\sigma X_{t}dB_{t}+\epsilon\sqrt{\gamma X_{t}}dW_{t}\quad\text{in}\quad[0,\infty).

Denote by
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
the diffusion process on
[0,\infty)
generated by solutions of (
2.1
). For singular diffusion processes like (
2.1
), the strong uniqueness is ensured by the well-known Yamada-Watanabe theory
[
58
,
57
]
. Moreover,
X^{\epsilon}_{t}
gets absorbed by the absorbing state
0
in finite time almost surely (see e.g.
[
6
,
34
]
), leading eventually to extinction dynamics. However,
X^{\epsilon}_{t}
can display long interesting dynamics before hitting
0
. To capture such dynamics, we use
quasi-stationary distributions
of
X^{\epsilon}_{t}
– these are initial distributions of
X^{\epsilon}_{t}
on
(0,\infty)
such that the distribution of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
conditioned on not reaching
0
up to time
t
is independent of
t\geq 0
.

Let
T^{\epsilon}_{0}
be the first time that
X^{\epsilon}_{t}
hits
0
(often called the
extinction time
), that is,

T^{\epsilon}_{0}=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:X^{\epsilon}_{t}=0\right\}.

Then,
\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}<\infty]=1
as mentioned above (see also
[
34
, Chapter VI, Section 3]
), where
\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}
the law of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
with initial distribution
\mu
. The associated expectation is denoted by
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}
. If
\mu=\delta_{x}
, we simply write
\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{x}=\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\delta_{x}}
and
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x}=\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\delta_{x}}
.

Definition 2.1
(Quasi-stationary distribution).

A Borel probability measure
\mu_{\epsilon}
on
(0,\infty)
is
called a
quasi-stationary distribution (QSD)
of

X^{\epsilon}_{t}
if

\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu_{\epsilon}}\left[X_{t}^{\epsilon}\in B|T_{0}^{\epsilon}>t\right]=\mu_{\epsilon}(B),\quad\forall t\geq 0,\quad B\in\mathcal{B}((0,\infty)),

where
\mathcal{B}((0,\infty))
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
(0,\infty)
.

The general theory of QSDs (see e.g.
[
46
,
13
]
) says that if
\mu_{\epsilon}
is a QSD of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
, then there is a unique number
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}>0
such that
T_{0}^{\epsilon}
is exponentially distributed with rate
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
provided
X_{0}^{\epsilon}\sim\mu_{\epsilon}
, that is,

\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu_{\epsilon}}[T_{0}^{\epsilon}>t]=e^{-\lambda_{\epsilon,1}t},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

(2.2)

For this reason,
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
is often referred to as the
extinction rate
.

Following
[
6
]
, we check that under
(H)
,
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
admits a unique QSD
\mu_{\epsilon}
with a positive
C^{2}
density
u_{\epsilon}
(see Lemma
3.4
for details). Moreover, the associated extinction rate
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
is given by the principal (or the first) eigenvalue of
-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
is an appropriate extension of the generator of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
and acts on functions in
C^{2}((0,\infty))
as:

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}\phi=\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon^{2}a+\sigma^{2})\phi^{\prime\prime}+b\phi^{\prime},\quad\forall\phi\in C^{2}((0,\infty)).

(2.3)

The rigorous definition of
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
is given in Subsection
3.1
. In addition, the spectral gap between the first and second eigenvalues,
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
and
\lambda_{\epsilon,2}
, of the operator
-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
characterizes the exponential convergence rate of
\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}\left[X_{t}^{\epsilon}\in\bullet|t<T_{0}^{\epsilon}\right]
to the QSD
\mu_{\epsilon}
as
t\to\infty
whenever the initial distribution
\mu
is compactly supported in
(0,\infty)
.

The main goal of this paper is to analyze the combined effects of environmental and demographic noises on population persistence and extinction. In order to achieve this, it is of paramount importance to investigate the asymptotic properties of
\mu_{\epsilon}
,
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
and
\lambda_{\epsilon,2}
as
\epsilon\to 0
. We are able to provide detailed information about the diffusion process
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
governed by the QSD
\mu_{\epsilon}
, and to characterize the extinction time
T_{0}^{\epsilon}
(especially, the mean extinction time
\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}[T_{0}^{\epsilon}]
) as well as the global multiscale dynamics of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
.

Investigating the asymptotic properties of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
and related quantities (i.e.,
\mu_{\epsilon}
,
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
,
\lambda_{\epsilon,2}
and
T_{0}^{\epsilon}
) as
\epsilon\to 0
leads naturally to the limiting equation of (
2.1
), namely,

dX^{0}_{t}=b(X^{0}_{t})dt+\sigma(X^{0}_{t})dB_{t}\quad\text{in}\quad[0,\infty).

(2.4)

Intuitively, the first step towards a good understanding of these asymptotic properties is to acquire relevant information about the diffusion process
X_{t}^{0}
on
[0,\infty)
generated by solutions of (
2.4
). It is worthwhile to point out that
X_{t}^{0}
behaves fundamentally different from
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
over large time scales. For instance, if
X_{0}^{0}=x\in(0,\infty)
, then
X_{t}^{0}
does not reach the absorbing state
0
in finite time almost surely, that is,
X_{t}^{0}>0

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-a.e. for all
t>0
(see Proposition
3.1
). Moreover, the spectral structure of the generator of (
2.4
) differs very much from that of the generator of (
2.1
), i.e.,
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
. More precisely, the latter is purely discrete (see Lemma
3.1
), while the former is not (see Remark
3.1
).

The dynamics of
X_{t}^{0}
is very well understood. Following for example
[
37
,
20
,
28
]
, we define the
stochastic growth rate
(also called
invasion rate
or
external Lyapunov exponent
)

\Lambda_{0}:=b^{\prime}(0)-\frac{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{2}.

(2.5)

In population dynamics, the condition
\Lambda_{0}>0
implies that a species tends to increase when it is at a low density, and therefore, persists in the long run (see
[
37
,
20
,
28
]
). The following sharp threshold result is part of Proposition
3.1
:

•

if
\Lambda_{0}<0
, then
\delta_{0}
is the only stationary distribution of
X_{t}^{0}
;

•

if
\Lambda_{0}>0
, then
X_{t}^{0}
admits a unique positive stationary distribution
\mu_{0}
with a positive density
u_{0}\in C^{2}((0,\infty))
given by the normalized Gibbs density, namely,
u_{0}=\frac{u_{0}^{G}}{\|u_{0}^{G}\|_{L^{1}((0,\infty))}}
, where

u_{0}^{G}:=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}e^{2\int_{1}^{\bullet}\frac{b}{\sigma^{2}}ds}\quad\text{in}\quad(0,\infty).

(2.6)

More detailed information is given in Subsection
3.4
. Our main focus is on the case
\Lambda_{0}>0
– in this setting the persistence of a species whose dynamics is modelled by (
2.4
) becomes a transient property when the model (
2.1
) is used. This is how things usually behave in nature where a population persists for a long time after which it eventually goes extinct. Our purpose is to give quantitative and qualitative characterizations of this phenomenon. We are also able to establish interesting results in the case
\Lambda_{0}<0
, demonstrating significant changes as
\Lambda_{0}
crosses
0
, where a bifurcation occurs.

Our first result addresses the limiting behaviors of
\mu_{\epsilon}
as
\epsilon\to 0
. The space
C^{2}((0,\infty))
is equipped with the topology of locally uniform convergence up to the second derivative.

Theorem A
.

Assume
(H)
.

(1)

If
\Lambda_{0}<0
, then
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi d\mu_{\epsilon}=0
for any
\phi\in C_{b}([0,\infty))
with
\phi(0)=0
.

(2)

If
\Lambda_{0}>0
, then
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mu_{\epsilon}=\mu_{0}
weakly, and
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}u_{\epsilon}=u_{0}
in
C^{2}((0,\infty))
.

Given the aforementioned sharp threshold result of
X_{t}^{0}
and the fact that
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
is a small random perturbation of
X_{t}^{0}
(or, (
2.1
) is a small random perturbation of (
2.4
)), the conclusions from Theorem
A
are expected and look pretty straightforward. This, however, is completely deceptive from a technical perspective, especially in the case
\Lambda_{0}>0
. Indeed, when
\Lambda_{0}>0
, it is not hard to show that any limiting measure of
\{\mu_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon}
must be
\mu_{0}
(up to multiplication by a constant), and hence, the weak convergence
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mu_{\epsilon}=\mu_{0}
follows if
\{\mu_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon}
is tight. The tightness of
\{\mu_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon}
comes from studying their concentration near
0
and
\infty
. The concentration near
\infty
follows mainly from the dissipativity and is obtained by means of the usual technique on the basis of Lyapunov-type functions (see Proposition
4.1
). Establishing the concentration near
0
is however troublesome due to the following technical problems: (i) both the vector field
b
and the noise terms
\sigma
and
\epsilon\sqrt{a}
vanish at
0
; such degeneracies are known to cause difficulties in the analysis and are often avoided in the literature when treating noise-vanishing problems; (ii) techniques based on Lyapunov-type functions do not apply because of the demographic noise term which causes the finite time extinction of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
; otherwise, a unique non-trivial stationary distribution would exist, instead of the QSD. These issues are circumvented by a two-step approach: an
\epsilon
-dependent upper bound of
u_{\epsilon}
is first established (see Lemma
3.5
); it is followed by an argument of maximum principle type (see the proof of Proposition
4.2
). As a result, we establish in Proposition
4.2
the following concentration estimate of the densities
\{u_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon}
:

\sup_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}(x)\leq\frac{C}{x^{k}},\quad\forall x\in(0,x_{*})

for some
k\in(0,1)
,
x_{*}>0
and
C>0
. Such an upper bound is more or less inspired by the expectation
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}u_{\epsilon}=u_{0}
and the behavior of
u_{0}
near
0
, i.e.,
u_{0}(x)\sim C_{0}x^{\frac{2b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}-2}
as
x\to 0
for some
C_{0}>0
. Note that under the assumption
\Lambda_{0}>0
one has
2-\frac{2b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}<1
.

Our second result establishes asymptotic bounds for
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
and
\lambda_{\epsilon,2}
, the first two eigenvalues of
-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
. Throughout this paper, for positive numbers
A_{\epsilon}
and
B_{\epsilon}
indexed by
\epsilon
, we write

A_{\epsilon}\approx_{\epsilon}B_{\epsilon},\quad A_{\epsilon}\lesssim_{\epsilon}B_{\epsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad A_{\epsilon}\gtrsim_{\epsilon}B_{\epsilon}

if
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{A_{\epsilon}}{B_{\epsilon}}=1
,
\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{A_{\epsilon}}{B_{\epsilon}}\leq 1
and
\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{A_{\epsilon}}{B_{\epsilon}}\geq 1
, respectively.

Theorem B
.

Assume
(H)
. Then,
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,1}=0
. Moreover, the following hold.

(1)

If
\Lambda_{0}<0
, then there is
C>0
such that
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}\gtrsim_{\epsilon}\frac{C}{|\ln\epsilon|}
.

(2)

If
\Lambda_{0}>0
, then

•

for each
0<\gamma\ll 1
, there holds

\epsilon^{(1+\gamma)\frac{4b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}-2}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\lambda_{\epsilon,1}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\epsilon^{(1-\gamma)\frac{2b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}-1};

•

0<\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,2}\leq\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,2}<\infty
.

Remark 2.1
.

We offer some comments regarding Theorem
B
.

(1)

We first exhibit the significant effects the environmental noise
\sigma(X^{\epsilon}_{t})d{B}_{t}
has by comparing (
2.1
) with

d\tilde{X}^{\epsilon}_{t}=b(\tilde{X}^{\epsilon}_{t})dt+\epsilon\sqrt{a(\tilde{X}^{\epsilon}_{t})}dW_{t}\quad\text{in}\quad[0,\infty).

(2.7)

Just like
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
, the diffusion process
\tilde{X}^{\epsilon}_{t}
reaches the extinction state
0
in finite time almost surely and admits a unique QSD with extinction rate
\tilde{\lambda}_{\epsilon,1}
with
-\tilde{\lambda}_{\epsilon,1}
being the first eigenvalue of the (appropriately extended) generator of (
2.7
). It is shown in
[
36
, Theorem A]
that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\ln\tilde{\lambda}_{\epsilon,1}=-d
for some
d>0
that can be computed in terms of
a
and
b
. In particular,
\tilde{\lambda}_{\epsilon,1}
is exponentially small in
\epsilon
. Hence, the asymptotic of
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
is fundamentally different from that of
\tilde{\lambda}_{\epsilon,1}
, manifesting the significance of the environmental noise. More importantly, turning an exponentially small extinction rate into a sub-exponentially small one, greatly improves the observability of the extinction of a species, making (
2.1
) a much better model than (
2.7
).

The effects of the environmental noise extend to the extinction time, especially, the mean extinction time, thanks to the relationship between the extinction time
\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}^{\epsilon}[T_{0}^{\epsilon}]
and the extinction rate
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
. See (
2.2
) and Corollary
A
.

(2)

When
\Lambda_{0}<0
, we believe that the lower bound of order
\frac{1}{|\ln\epsilon|}
for
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
is sharp in the sense that there is
\tilde{C}>0
such that
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\frac{\tilde{C}}{|\ln\epsilon|}
. We offer some further explanation in Remark
2.2
after Corollary
B
.

(3)

When
\Lambda_{0}>0
, the upper bound of
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
is improved to
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\epsilon^{(1-\gamma)\frac{4b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}-2}
for each
0<\gamma\ll 1
in Corollary
B
. This says that the leading order of
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
is
\epsilon^{\frac{4b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}-2}
. The reason why we still include this as a main result is that its proof, relying only on the classical variational formula, is elementary, while the proof of Corollary
B
uses heavy machinery (see comments after Corollary
B
for details).

(4)

When
\Lambda_{0}>0
, the asymptotic bounds of
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
and
\lambda_{\epsilon,2}
imply that
\inf_{\epsilon}(\lambda_{\epsilon,2}-\lambda_{\epsilon,1})>0
. As mentioned earlier (or see Lemma
3.4
),
\lambda_{\epsilon,2}-\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
is the exponential convergence rate of
\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}\left[X_{t}^{\epsilon}\in\bullet|t<T_{0}^{\epsilon}\right]
to
\mu_{\epsilon}
as
t\to\infty
whenever the initial distribution
\mu
is compactly supported in
(0,\infty)
. These facts tell us that the distribution of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
quickly approaches
\mu_{\epsilon}
, then stays close to
\mu_{\epsilon}
until the time scale
\frac{1}{\lambda_{\epsilon,1}}
, after which it finally relaxes to the extinction state. The multiscale dynamics is precisely characterized in Theorem
C
.

In our third result, we characterize the multiscale dynamics of the distribution of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
in the case
\Lambda_{0}>0
as described in Remark
2.1
(4). Denote by
\phi_{\epsilon,1}
the positive eigenfunction of
-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
associated with
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
subject to the normalization
\|\phi_{\epsilon,1}\|_{L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})}=1
(see Lemma
3.1
). Let
\mathcal{P}((0,\infty))
be the set of Borel probability measures on
(0,\infty)
. For
\mu\in\mathcal{P}((0,\infty))
and for any measurable function
f:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
we write
\langle\mu,f\rangle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}fd\mu
.

Theorem C
.

Assume
(H)
and
\Lambda_{0}>0
. For any
\mathcal{K}\subset\subset(0,\infty)
, there is
C=C(\mathcal{K})>0
such that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\mathcal{P}((0,\infty))\\
\text{\rm supp}(\mu)\subset\mathcal{K}\end{subarray}}\left\|\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[X^{\epsilon}_{t}\in\bullet]-\left[e^{-\lambda_{\epsilon,1}t}\langle\mu,\alpha_{\epsilon,1}\rangle\mu_{\epsilon}+\left(1-e^{-\lambda_{\epsilon,1}t}\langle\mu,\alpha_{\epsilon,1}\rangle\right)\delta_{0}\right]\right\|_{TV}\leq Ce^{-\lambda_{\epsilon,2}t}

holds for all
t\geq 0
and
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, where
\alpha_{\epsilon,1}:=\|\phi_{\epsilon,1}\|_{L^{1}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})}\phi_{\epsilon,1}
satisfies

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\alpha_{\epsilon,1}=1\quad\text{locally uniformly in}\,\,(0,\infty).

Built on the eigenfunction expansion of the Markov semigroup associated with
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
before hitting
0
, Theorem
C
establishes a sharp estimate quantifying the total variation distance between the distribution of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
and the convex combination of the QSD
\mu_{\epsilon}
and the extinction state
\delta_{0}
. The locally uniform limit
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\alpha_{\epsilon,1}=1
and the fact that the constant
C
is independent of
\epsilon
are what make this estimate powerful. Together with the asymptotic bounds of
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
and
\lambda_{\epsilon,2}
in Theorem
B
(2), Theorem
C
has the following important dynamical implications:

•

if
t_{\epsilon}^{(1)}<t_{\epsilon}^{(2)}
are such that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}t_{\epsilon}^{(1)}=\infty
and
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,1}t_{\epsilon}^{(2)}=0
, then

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{t\in\left[t_{\epsilon}^{(1)},t_{\epsilon}^{(2)}\right]}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\mathcal{P}((0,\infty))\\
\text{\rm supp}(\mu)\subset\mathcal{K}\end{subarray}}\left\|\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[X^{\epsilon}_{t}\in\bullet]-\mu_{\epsilon}\right\|_{TV}=0;

•

if
t_{\epsilon}^{(3)}
is such that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,1}t_{\epsilon}^{(3)}=\infty
, then

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{t\in\left[t_{\epsilon}^{(3)},\infty\right)}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\mathcal{P}((0,\infty))\\
\text{\rm supp}(\mu)\subset\mathcal{K}\end{subarray}}\left\|\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[X^{\epsilon}_{t}\in\bullet]-\delta_{0}\right\|_{TV}=0.

Theorems
B
(2) and
C
have as immediate consequences the expected but far-reaching asymptotic reciprocal relationship between the extinction time
T_{0}^{\epsilon}
and the extinction rate
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
, and the asymptotic distribution of the normalized extinction time
\frac{T^{\epsilon}_{0}}{\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}^{\epsilon}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]}
.

Corollary A
.

Assume
(H)
and
\Lambda_{0}>0
. For each
\mu\in\mathcal{P}((0,\infty))
having compact support in
(0,\infty)
, there exists
C=C(\mu)>0
such that

\left|\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}>t]-e^{-\lambda_{\epsilon,1}t}\langle\mu,\alpha_{\epsilon,1}\rangle\right|\leq Ce^{-\lambda_{\epsilon,2}t},\quad\forall t>0\,\,\text{and}\,\,0<\epsilon\ll 1,

or equivalently,

\left|\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[\lambda_{\epsilon,1}T^{\epsilon}_{0}>t]-e^{-t}\langle\mu,\alpha_{\epsilon,1}\rangle\right|\leq Ce^{-\frac{\lambda_{\epsilon,2}}{\lambda_{\epsilon,1}}t},\quad\forall t>0\,\,\text{and}\,\,0<\epsilon\ll 1.

In particular,

•

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[\lambda_{\epsilon,1}T^{\epsilon}_{0}>t]=e^{-t}
locally uniformly in
t\in(0,\infty)
;

•

\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]\approx_{\epsilon}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\epsilon,1}}
;

•

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}\left[\frac{T^{\epsilon}_{0}}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}^{\epsilon}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]}>t\right]=e^{-t}
locally uniformly in
t\in(0,\infty)
.

Let
\mu
be as in the statement. Since
\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[X^{\epsilon}_{t}\in(0,\infty)]=\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}>t]
,
\mu_{\epsilon}((0,\infty))=1
and
\delta_{0}((0,\infty))=0
, we apply Theorem
C
and the definition of the total variation distance to find some
C=C(\mu)>0
such that
\left|\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}>t]-e^{-\lambda_{\epsilon,1}t}\langle\mu,\alpha_{\epsilon,1}\rangle\right|\leq Ce^{-\lambda_{\epsilon,2}t}
for all
t>0
. Replacing
t
by
\frac{t}{\lambda_{\epsilon,1}}
leads to

\left|\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[\lambda_{\epsilon,1}T^{\epsilon}_{0}>t]-e^{-t}\langle\mu,\alpha_{\epsilon,1}\rangle\right|\leq Ce^{-\frac{\lambda_{\epsilon,2}}{\lambda_{\epsilon,1}}t},\quad\forall t>0.

In particular,
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[\lambda_{\epsilon,1}T^{\epsilon}_{0}>t]=e^{-t}
locally uniformly in
t\in(0,\infty)
.

Integrating the above inequality with respect to
t
over
(0,\infty)
yields

\left|\lambda_{\epsilon,1}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]-\langle\mu,\alpha_{\epsilon,1}\rangle\right|\leq C\frac{\lambda_{\epsilon,1}}{\lambda_{\epsilon,2}}.

This together with Theorem
B
(2) and
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\langle\mu,\alpha_{\epsilon,1}\rangle=1
(by Theorem
C
) implies that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,1}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]=1
. The remaining result follows immediately.
∎

Corollary
A
says in particular that the normalized extinction time
\frac{T^{\epsilon}_{0}}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}^{\epsilon}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]}
weakly converges to an exponential random variable of mean
1
as
\epsilon\to 0
. The asymptotic reciprocal relationship
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]\approx_{\epsilon}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\epsilon,1}}
is a fundamental principle connecting the asymptotics of
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}^{\epsilon}[T_{0}^{\epsilon}]
and
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
– this allows using information about one of these quantities to analyze the other one. In particular, (non-sharp) asymptotic bounds of the mean extinction time
\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}^{\epsilon}[T_{0}^{\epsilon}]
in the case
\Lambda_{0}>0
can be obtained from the asymptotic bounds of
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
in Theorem
B
(2). However, we wanted to improve this and get sharp bounds.

Our last result is devoted to the investigation of the sharp asymptotic bounds of the mean extinction time
\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}^{\epsilon}[T_{0}^{\epsilon}]
.

Theorem D
.

Assume
(H)
. The following hold for each
\mu\in\mathcal{P}((0,\infty))
having compact support in
(0,\infty)
.

1.

If
\Lambda_{0}<0
, then there exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0

C_{1}|\ln\epsilon|\lesssim_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]\lesssim_{\epsilon}C_{2}|\ln\epsilon|.

2.

If
\Lambda_{0}>0
, then for each
0<\gamma\ll 1
,

\epsilon^{2-(1-\gamma)\frac{4b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]\lesssim_{\epsilon}\epsilon^{2-(1+\gamma)\frac{4b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}}.

Theorem
D
is established by adopting a probabilistic approach focusing on analyzing the behaviours of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
near
0
. It is independent of Theorems
A
-
C
.

As an immediate consequence of Corollary
A
and Theorem
D
(2), we get the following sharp asymptotic of
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
when
\Lambda_{0}>0
, improving the one given in Theorem
B
(2).

Corollary B
.

Assume
(H)
and
\Lambda_{0}>0
. Then, for each
0<\gamma\ll 1
,

\epsilon^{(1+\gamma)\frac{4b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}-2}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\lambda_{\epsilon,1}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\epsilon^{(1-\gamma)\frac{4b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}-2}.

Remark 2.2
.

When
\Lambda_{0}<0
, we are unable to establish the relationship
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}^{\epsilon}[T_{0}^{\epsilon}]\approx_{\epsilon}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\epsilon,1}}
for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}((0,\infty))
having compact support in
(0,\infty)
, and hence, can not apply Theorem
D
(1) to conclude
\frac{C_{1}}{|\ln\epsilon|}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\lambda_{\epsilon,1}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\frac{C_{2}}{|\ln\epsilon|}
. Nonetheless, we believe that the lower bound for
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
obtained in Theorem
B
(1) is sharp.

3.1
Generator, spectral theory and dynamics

In this subsection, we present some general results about the spectral theory of the generator of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
and the dynamics of the corresponding semigroup.

We start with the rigorous formalism of the generator of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
. Set

\alpha_{\epsilon}:=\epsilon^{2}a+\sigma^{2}\quad\text{on}\quad[0,\infty),\qquad V_{\epsilon}:=-\int_{1}^{\bullet}\frac{b}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}ds\quad\text{on}\quad(0,\infty).

Thanks to
(H)
, we have

\begin{split}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\alpha_{\epsilon}=\sigma^{2}\quad\text{in}\quad C^{2}([0,\infty)),\qquad\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}V_{\epsilon}=-\int_{1}^{\bullet}\frac{b}{\sigma^{2}}ds\quad\text{in}\quad C^{2}((0,\infty)).\end{split}

(3.1)

Consider the symmetric quadratic form
\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}:C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))\times C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\phi,\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha_{\epsilon}\phi^{\prime}\psi^{\prime}u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx,\quad\forall\phi,\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((0,\infty)),

where

u_{\epsilon}^{G}:=\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}e^{-2V_{\epsilon}}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}e^{2\int_{1}^{\bullet}\frac{b}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}ds}\quad\text{in}\quad(0,\infty)

is the non-integrable Gibbs density. The non-integrability of
u_{\epsilon}^{G}
comes from the singularity of order
\frac{1}{x}
near
0
. Recall
u_{0}^{G}
from (
2.6
) and note that clearly one has

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}u_{\epsilon}^{G}=u_{0}^{G}\quad\text{in}\quad C^{2}((0,\infty)).

(3.2)

This fact is frequently used in the sequel. Under
(H)
, it is not hard to check that the form
\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}
is Markovian and closable (see e.g.
[
23
]
). Its smallest closed extension, still denoted by
\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}
, is a Dirichlet form with domain
D(\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon})
being the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))
under the norm
\|\phi\|^{2}_{D(\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon})}:=\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})}+\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\phi,\phi)
, where
L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G}):=L^{2}((0,\infty),u_{\epsilon}^{G}dx)
. Denote by
(\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon},D(\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}))
the non-positive self-adjoint operator associated with
(\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon},D(\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}))
, that is,

\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\phi,\psi)=\langle-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}\phi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})},\quad\forall\phi\in D(\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}),\,\,\psi\in D(\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}),

where

D(\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}):=\left\{u\in D(\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}):\exists f\in L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})\,\,\text{s.t.}\,\,\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(u,\phi)=\langle f,\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})},\forall\phi\in D(\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon})\right\}.

It is informative to mention that

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}\phi=\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon^{2}a+\sigma^{2})\phi^{\prime\prime}+b\phi^{\prime},\quad\forall\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty)).

The operator
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
is a self-adjoint extension in
L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})
of the generator of (
2.1
).

In the next result, we collect basic properties about the spectrum of
-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
and the semigroup generated by
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
.

Lemma 3.1
(
[
6
,
36
]
).

Assume
(H)
. For each
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, the following hold.

(1)

-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
has purely discrete spectrum contained in
(0,\infty)
and listed as follows:
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}<\lambda_{\epsilon,2}<\lambda_{\epsilon,3}<\cdots\to\infty
.

(2)

Each
\lambda_{\epsilon,i}
is associated with a unique eigenfunction
\phi_{\epsilon,i}\in D(\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon})\cap L^{1}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})\cap C^{2}((0,\infty))
subject to the normalization
\|\phi_{\epsilon,i}\|_{L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})}=1
. Moreover,
\phi_{\epsilon,1}>0
.

(3)

The set
\{\phi_{\epsilon,i},i\in\mathbb{N}\}
forms an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})
.

(4)

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
generates a positive analytic semigroup
(P^{\epsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of contractions on
L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})
having the stochastic representation (or Feynman–Kac formula):

P^{\epsilon}_{t}f=\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\bullet}[f(X_{t}^{\epsilon})\mathbbm{1}_{t<T^{\epsilon}_{0}}],\quad\forall f\in L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})\cap C_{b}((0,\infty))\,\,\text{and}\,\,t\geq 0.

(5)

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
f\in L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})
and
t>0
,

\begin{split}P^{\epsilon}_{t}f=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}e^{-\lambda_{\epsilon,i}t}\langle f,\phi_{\epsilon,i}\rangle_{L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})}\phi_{\epsilon,i}+P^{\epsilon}_{t}Q^{\epsilon}_{k}f,\end{split}

(3.3)

where
Q^{\epsilon}_{k}
is the spectral projection of
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
corresponding to
\{-\lambda_{\epsilon,j}\}_{j\geq k}
. Moreover,

\|P^{\epsilon}_{t}Q^{\epsilon}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})\to L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})}\leq e^{-\lambda_{\epsilon,k}t},\quad t\geq 0.

(6)

For each
f\in C_{b}((0,\infty))
, the stochastic representation in
(4)
and (
3.3
) hold pointwisely.

The following result addressing the uniform-in-
\epsilon
boundedness of the
i
-th eigenvalue of
-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
is useful.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
(H)
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, there holds
\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,i}<\infty
.

Let
\{\phi_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}((0,\infty))
satisfy
\text{\rm supp}(\phi_{\ell})\subset({\ell},{\ell}+1)
and
\|\phi_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})}=1
. We find from (
3.1
) that the limit
\gamma_{\ell}:=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\phi_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})>0
exists for each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.

Fix
i\in\mathbb{N}
and set
S_{i}:=\text{span}\{\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{i}\}
. Since
-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
is self-adjoint in
L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})
, the Min-Max principle says in particular

\lambda_{\epsilon,i}\leq\max_{\phi\in S_{i}}\frac{\langle-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}\phi,\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})}}{\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})}}=\max_{\phi\in S_{i}}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\phi,\phi)}{\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})}}.

Note that each element
\phi\in S_{i}
can be written as
\phi:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}c_{\ell}\phi_{\ell}
for some
c_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\ell=1,\dots,i
. As the supports of
\{\phi_{\ell}\}_{\ell}
are disjoint, we calculate
\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}c_{\ell}^{2}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\phi,\phi)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}c_{\ell}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\phi_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})
. It follows that

\begin{split}\lambda_{\epsilon,i}\leq\max_{c_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R},\ell=1,\dots,i}\frac{\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}c_{\ell}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\phi_{\ell},\phi_{\ell})}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}c_{\ell}^{2}}&\leq\max_{\ell=1,\dots,i}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}(\phi_{\ell},\phi_{\ell}),\end{split}

leading to
\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,i}\leq\max_{\ell=1,\dots,i}\gamma_{\ell}
.
∎

3.2
Schrödinger operators

In this subsection, we follow the canonical procedure (see e.g.
[
6
]
) to derive the Schrödinger operator that is unitarily equivalent to
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
and establish some properties of its potential. These results will play a significant technical role in the sequel.

Note that
X^{\epsilon}_{t}
has the same distribution as the solution process of

d\tilde{X}^{\epsilon}_{t}=b(\tilde{X}^{\epsilon}_{t})dt+\sqrt{\alpha_{\epsilon}(\tilde{X}^{\epsilon}_{t})}d\tilde{W}_{t}\quad\text{in}\quad[0,\infty),

(3.4)

where
\tilde{W}_{t}
is a standard one-dimensional Brownian motion. Consider the change of variable

y=\xi_{\epsilon}(x):=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{\epsilon}}}ds=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}a+\sigma^{2}}}ds,\quad x\in(0,\infty).

Clearly,
\xi_{\epsilon}
is increasing and satisfies
\xi_{\epsilon}(0+)=0
.
Set
y_{\epsilon,\infty}:=\xi_{\epsilon}(\infty)
. Then,
\xi_{\epsilon}:(0,\infty)\to(0,y_{\epsilon,\infty})
is invertible. Its inverse is denoted by
\xi_{\epsilon}^{-1}:(0,y_{\epsilon,\infty})\to(0,\infty)
. This is the canonical transform converting the SDE (
3.4
) into the one with the simplest noise coefficient. More precisely, applying Itô’s formula, we find that
Y^{\epsilon}_{t}:=\xi_{\epsilon}(\tilde{X}^{\epsilon}_{t})
solves

dY^{\epsilon}_{t}=q_{\epsilon}(Y^{\epsilon}_{t})dt+d\tilde{W}_{t},

(3.5)

where
q_{\epsilon}:=\left(\frac{b}{\sqrt{\alpha_{\epsilon}}}-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}_{\epsilon}}{4\sqrt{\alpha_{\epsilon}}}\right)\circ\xi^{-1}_{\epsilon}
.

Set
v_{\epsilon}^{G}:=(u^{G}_{\epsilon}\sqrt{\alpha_{\epsilon}})\circ\xi^{-1}_{\epsilon}
and
L^{2}(v_{\epsilon}^{G}):=L^{2}((0,y_{\epsilon,\infty}),v_{\epsilon}^{G}dy)
. The generator of (
3.5
) is given by

\mathcal{L}^{Y}_{\epsilon}:=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}+q_{\epsilon}(y)\frac{d}{dy}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(v_{\epsilon}^{G}).

It is straightforward to check that
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{Y}
is unitarily equivalent to
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
. More precisely, there holds
U_{\epsilon}\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}=\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{Y}U_{\epsilon}
, where
U_{\epsilon}:L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})\to L^{2}(v_{\epsilon}^{G})
,
f\mapsto f\circ\xi^{-1}_{\epsilon}
is unitary.

Now, consider the Schrödinger operator

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{S}:=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\left(q^{2}_{\epsilon}(y)+q^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(y)\right)\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}((0,y_{\epsilon,\infty})).

(3.6)

It is not hard to check that
\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{Y}=\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{S}\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}
, where
\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}:L^{2}(v_{\epsilon}^{G})\to L^{2}((0,y_{\epsilon,\infty}))
,
f\mapsto f\sqrt{v_{\epsilon}^{G}}
is unitary.

We include the following commutative diagram for readers’ convenience:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\quad L^{2}((0,y_{\epsilon,\infty}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{\epsilon}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{Y}}

We point out that rigorous definitions of
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{Y}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{S}
can be done using quadratic forms as done in Subsection
3.1
for
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
. By the unitary transforms, the domains of
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{Y}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{S}
are respectively given by
U_{\epsilon}D(\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon})
and
\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}U_{\epsilon}D(\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon})
, and the domains of quadratic forms associated with
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{Y}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{S}
are respectively given by
U_{\epsilon}D(\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon})
and
\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}U_{\epsilon}D(\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon})
.

The potential of the Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}^{S}_{\epsilon}
is
denoted by

W_{\epsilon}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(q^{2}_{\epsilon}+q^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\right)\quad\text{on}\quad(0,y_{\epsilon,\infty}).

Some elementary properties of
W_{\epsilon}
are collected in the following result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume
(H)
. The following hold.

(1)

2W_{\epsilon}\circ\xi_{\epsilon}=\frac{3|\alpha^{\prime}_{\epsilon}|^{2}}{16\alpha_{\epsilon}}-\frac{\alpha_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}}{4}+b^{\prime}-\frac{b\alpha_{\epsilon}^{\prime}}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}+\frac{b^{2}}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}
.

(2)

There exist
y_{1}\in(0,\infty)
and
C>0
such that
\inf_{\epsilon}W_{\epsilon}(y)\geq\frac{C}{y^{2}}
for all
y\in(0,y_{1}]
.

(3)

There exists
x_{*}\in(0,\infty)
such that

W_{\epsilon}(y)\geq\frac{b^{2}(\xi^{-1}_{\epsilon}(y))}{4\sigma^{2}(\xi^{-1}_{\epsilon}(y))},\quad\forall y\in[\xi_{\epsilon}(x_{*}),y_{\epsilon,\infty})\,\,\text{and}\,\,0<\epsilon\ll 1.

(4)

The family
\{W_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon}
is uniformly lower bounded, that is,
\inf_{\epsilon}\min W_{\epsilon}>-\infty
.

(1) Straightforward calculations yield

\begin{split}q^{2}_{\epsilon}\circ\xi_{\epsilon}=\frac{b^{2}}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}-\frac{b\alpha^{\prime}_{\epsilon}}{2\alpha_{\epsilon}}+\frac{|\alpha^{\prime}_{\epsilon}|^{2}}{16\alpha_{\epsilon}},\quad q^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\circ\xi_{\epsilon}=b^{\prime}-\frac{b\alpha^{\prime}_{\epsilon}}{2\alpha_{\epsilon}}-\frac{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}}{4}+\frac{|\alpha^{\prime}_{\epsilon}|^{2}}{8\alpha_{\epsilon}}.\end{split}

The expression for
W_{\epsilon}
follows immediately.

(2) Thanks to
(H)
(1)-(3) and Taylor’s expansion at
x=0
,

\begin{split}\alpha_{\epsilon}(x)&=\epsilon^{2}a(x)+\sigma^{2}(x)=\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)x+\left(\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}a^{\prime\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}\right)x^{2}+o(x^{2}),\\
\alpha^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(x)&=\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(x)+2\sigma(x)\sigma^{\prime}(x)=\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+\left(\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime\prime}(0)+2|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}\right)x+o(x),\\
\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}(x)&=\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime\prime}(x)+2(|\sigma^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+\sigma(x)\sigma^{\prime\prime}(x))=\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime\prime}(0)+2|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}+o(1),\quad\,\,\text{and}\,\,\\
b(x)&=b^{\prime}(0)x+o(1).\end{split}

For fixed
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
(to be specified), we find
0<\kappa\ll 1
such that

\displaystyle 1-\delta\leq\frac{\alpha_{\epsilon}(x)}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)x}\leq 1+\delta,\quad 1-\delta\leq\frac{\alpha^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(x)}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)}\leq 1+\delta,

\displaystyle|\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}(x)|\leq 3|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2},\quad b^{\prime}(x)>0\quad\,\,\text{and}\,\,\quad 0<b(x)<2b^{\prime}(0)x,\quad\forall x\in(0,\kappa\epsilon^{2}).

Hence, with
y=\xi_{\epsilon}(x)
,

\begin{split}2W_{\epsilon}(y)&\geq\frac{3|\alpha^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(x)|^{2}}{16\alpha_{\epsilon}(x)}-\frac{\alpha_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}(x)}{4}-\frac{b(x)\alpha_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(x)}{\alpha_{\epsilon}(x)}\\
&\geq\frac{3(1-\delta)^{2}}{16(1+\delta)}\frac{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)}{x}-\frac{3}{4}|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}-\frac{2b^{\prime}(0)(1+\delta)}{(1-\delta)},\quad\forall x\in(0,\kappa\epsilon^{2}).\end{split}

Since

\frac{3}{4}|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}+\frac{2b^{\prime}(0)(1+\delta)}{(1-\delta)}<\frac{a^{\prime}(0)}{18\kappa}<\frac{1}{18}\frac{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)}{x},\quad\forall x\in(0,\kappa\epsilon^{2}),

where the first inequality is due to the smallness of
\kappa
, we arrive at

2W_{\epsilon}(y)\geq\frac{3(1-\delta)^{2}}{16(1+\delta)}\frac{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)}{x}-\frac{1}{18}\frac{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)}{x}=\frac{1}{9}\frac{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)}{x},\quad\forall x\in(0,\kappa\epsilon^{2}),

where we fixed
\delta
so that
\frac{(1-\delta)^{2}}{(1+\delta)}=\frac{8}{9}
in the equality.

Note that

y=\xi_{\epsilon}(x)\geq\int_{0}^{x}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{(1+\delta)\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)s}}=\frac{2\sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{(1+\delta)\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)}},\quad\forall x\in(0,\kappa\epsilon^{2}),

leading to

2W_{\epsilon}(y)\geq\frac{1}{9}\frac{4\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)}{(1+\delta)\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)y^{2}}=\frac{4}{9(1+\delta)}\frac{1}{y^{2}},\quad\forall y\in(0,y_{1}),

where
y_{1}:=\frac{2\sqrt{\kappa}}{\sqrt{(1+\delta)a^{\prime}(0)}}\leq\xi_{\epsilon}(\kappa\epsilon^{2})
.
This proves (2).

(3) Obviously,
2W_{\epsilon}\circ\xi_{\epsilon}\geq\frac{b^{2}}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}-\frac{b\alpha^{\prime}_{\epsilon}}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}-\frac{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}}{4}+b^{\prime}
. By
(H)
(4), there is
x_{*}>0
such that

\frac{b^{2}}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}\geq\frac{3b^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}},\quad\frac{\alpha^{\prime}_{\epsilon}}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}\leq\frac{|b|}{8\sigma^{2}}\quad\,\,\text{and}\,\,\quad\frac{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}}{4}-b^{\prime}\leq\frac{b^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\quad\text{in}\quad(x_{*},\infty).

Then,
2W_{\epsilon}\geq\frac{b^{2}\circ\xi^{-1}_{\epsilon}}{2\sigma^{2}\circ\xi^{-1}_{\epsilon}}
in
(\xi^{-1}_{\epsilon}(x_{*}),\infty)
, verifying (3).

(4) Let
x_{*}
be as in (3). The assumption
(H)
implies
\sup_{[0,x_{*}]}\max\left\{|\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}|,|b^{\prime}|,\frac{|b\alpha^{\prime}_{\epsilon}|}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}\right\}<\infty
. The conclusion then follows from (1) and (3).
∎

Lemma
3.3
says that the potential
W_{\epsilon}
is lower bounded and satisfies
W_{\epsilon}(y)\to\infty
as
y\to 0^{+}
and
y_{\epsilon,\infty}^{-}
. Classical spectral theory of Schrödinger operators then ensures that the spectrum of
-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{S}
is purely discrete, and so is that of
-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
by the unitary equivalence. This is the idea in
[
6
]
of obtaining the spectral structure of
-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
.

3.3
Quasi-stationary distributions

The existence and uniqueness of QSDs of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
and their properties are investigated in
[
6
]
(see also
[
55
,
44
,
46
,
43
,
13
,
48
,
10
,
27
,
30
]

\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\epsilon}
the Fokker-Planck operator associated with

\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\epsilon}\phi=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{\epsilon}\phi)^{\prime\prime}-(b\phi)^{\prime},\quad\forall\phi\in C^{2}((0,\infty)),

where we recall
\alpha_{\epsilon}:=\epsilon^{2}a+\sigma^{2}
. Recall from Lemma
3.1
that
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
and
\lambda_{\epsilon,2}
are the first two eigenvalues of
-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}

\phi_{\epsilon,1}
and
\phi_{\epsilon,2}
with
\phi_{\epsilon,1}>0

Lemma 3.4
(
[
6
]
).

Assume
(H)
. For each
0<\epsilon\ll 1
,
X^{\epsilon}_{t}
admits a unique QSD
\mu_{\epsilon}
with the extinction rate
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
. Moreover,
\mu_{\epsilon}
admits a positive density
u_{\epsilon}\in C^{2}((0,\infty))
satisfying
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}=-\lambda_{\epsilon,1}u_{\epsilon}
and given by
u_{\epsilon}=\frac{\phi_{\epsilon,1}u_{\epsilon}^{G}}{\|\phi_{\epsilon,1}\|_{L^{1}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})}}
. In addition, if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}((0,\infty))
has compact support in
(0,\infty)
, then for any
B\in\mathcal{B}((0,\infty))
,

\begin{split}&\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{(\lambda_{\epsilon,2}-\lambda_{\epsilon,1})t}\left(\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{\mu}\left[X_{t}^{\epsilon}\in B|t<T_{0}^{\epsilon}\right]-\mu_{\epsilon}(B)\right)\\
&\qquad=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{\epsilon,2}d\mu}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{\epsilon,1}d\mu}\left(\frac{\langle\mathbbm{1}_{B},\phi_{\epsilon,2}\rangle_{L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})}}{\|\phi_{\epsilon,1}\|_{L^{1}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})}}-\frac{\langle\mathbbm{1}_{B},\phi_{\epsilon,1}\rangle_{L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})}\langle\mathbbm{1},\phi_{\epsilon,2}\rangle_{L^{2}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})}}{\|\phi_{\epsilon,1}\|^{2}_{L^{1}(u_{\epsilon}^{G})}}\right).\end{split}

We point out that

being a QSD of

is a direct consequence of Lemma

. Verifying the uniqueness is however much more challenging. In

under the topology of weak convergence as

if

is compactly supported in

as stated in Lemma
3.4
.

The next result is a stepping stone to obtaining finer results of the QSD
\mu_{\epsilon}
or its density

near
0
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume

It is actually a special case of

d\tilde{X}_{t}=\tilde{b}(\tilde{X}_{t})dt+\epsilon\sqrt{\tilde{a}(\tilde{X}_{t})}dW_{t},

where

(1)

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume

. Then,

.

Set

Thanks to
(H)
(4), we find some

such that

(H)
, there is

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}V\leq-\frac{b^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\quad\text{in}\quad\{n_{0}\leq V\}.

(4.1)

Let

be a sequence of smooth and non-decreasing functions on
(0,\infty)
satisfying

\zeta_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x\in(0,n_{0}),\\
x,&x\in(n_{0}+1,n),\\
n+1,&x\in(n+2,\infty),\end{cases}\quad\,\,\text{and}\,\,\quad\zeta^{\prime\prime}_{n}\leq 0\text{ on }[n,n+2].

In addition, we let
\{\zeta_{n}\}_{n}
coincide on
[0,n_{0}+1]

Due to
V(\infty)=\infty
and
(H)

\zeta_{n}(V)-(n+1)

\begin{split}0&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}\left[\zeta_{n}(V)-(n+1)\right]u_{\epsilon}dx+\lambda_{\epsilon,1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\zeta_{n}(V)-(n+1)\right]u_{\epsilon}dx\\

&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\zeta^{\prime}_{n}(V)\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}V+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\epsilon}\zeta^{\prime\prime}_{n}(V)|V^{\prime}|^{2}\right]u_{\epsilon}dx\\
&=\int_{\{n_{0}\leq V\leq n+2\}}\left[\zeta^{\prime}_{n}(V)\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}V+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\epsilon}\zeta^{\prime\prime}_{n}(V)|V^{\prime}|^{2}\right]u_{\epsilon}dx\end{split}

and
\zeta_{n}-(n+1)\leq 0

\begin{split}&\int_{\{n_{0}\leq V\leq n+2\}}\zeta^{\prime}_{n}(V)\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}Vu_{\epsilon}dx\\

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{|X^{\epsilon}_{t}-X^{0}_{t}|\leq\delta}|f(X^{\epsilon}_{t})-f(X^{0}_{t})|d\mathbb{P}_{x}=0\quad\text{locally uniformly in }x\in[0,\infty).

Hence, we arrive at
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x}[f(X^{\epsilon}_{t})]=\mathbb{E}_{x}[f(X^{0}_{t})]
locally uniformly in

. It follows that

\begin{split}&\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\bullet}[f(X^{\epsilon}_{t})]-\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}[f(X^{0}_{t})]\right|d\mu_{\epsilon}\\
&\qquad\leq\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{A}\left|\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\bullet}[f(X^{\epsilon}_{t})]-\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}[f(X^{0}_{t})]\right|d\mu_{\epsilon}+2\|f\|_{\infty}\times\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\mu_{\epsilon}((A,\infty))\\
&\qquad\leq 2\|f\|_{\infty}\sup_{\epsilon}\mu_{\epsilon}((A,\infty)),\quad\forall A>0.\end{split}

Thanks to Proposition
4.1
, we pass to the limit

to find

\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\bullet}[f(X^{\epsilon}_{t})]-\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}[f(X^{0}_{t})]\right|d\mu_{\epsilon}=0.

The regularity of
b
and
\sigma
ensures that
\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}[f(X^{0}_{t})]\in C_{b}([0,\infty))
. Hence, the weak limit
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mu_{\epsilon}=\mu_{*}
implies that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}[f(X^{0}_{t})]d\mu_{\epsilon}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}[f(X^{0}_{t})]d\mu_{*}
. As a result,

\begin{split}&\left|\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\mu_{\epsilon}}[f(X^{\epsilon}_{t})]-\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{*}}[f(X^{0}_{t})]\right|\\
&\qquad\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\bullet}[f(X^{\epsilon}_{t})]-\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}[f(X^{0}_{t})]\right|d\mu_{\epsilon}\\
&\qquad\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}[f(X^{0}_{t})]d\mu_{\epsilon}-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}[f(X^{0}_{t})]d\mu_{*}\right|\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad\epsilon\to 0.\end{split}

Considering the facts
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\mu_{\epsilon}}[f(X^{\epsilon}_{t})]=e^{-\lambda_{\epsilon,1}t}\int_{0}^{\infty}fd\mu_{\epsilon}
,
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}fd\mu_{\epsilon}=\int_{0}^{\infty}fd\mu_{*}
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{\epsilon_{j}}=\lambda_{*}
, we deduce

\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{*}}[f(X^{0}_{t})]=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\mu_{\epsilon}}[f(X^{\epsilon}_{t})]=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}e^{-\lambda_{\epsilon,1}t}\int_{0}^{\infty}fd\mu_{\epsilon}=e^{-\lambda_{*}t}\int_{0}^{\infty}fd\mu_{*},

leading to (
4.2
). This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume

and

Let

. Straightforward calculations yield

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\epsilon}x^{-k}&=\left[\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}a^{\prime\prime}+\frac{(\sigma^{2})^{\prime\prime}}{2}-k\frac{(\sigma^{2})^{\prime}}{x}+\frac{k(k+1)\sigma^{2}}{2x^{2}}-b^{\prime}+k\frac{b}{x}\right]x^{-k}\\

\begin{split}&\frac{(\sigma^{2})^{\prime\prime}}{2}-k\frac{(\sigma^{2})^{\prime}}{x}+\frac{k(k+1)\sigma^{2}}{2x^{2}}=\frac{k^{2}-3k+2}{2}|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}+o(1),\\

By Theorem

Proof of Theorem

Lemma 5.1

(5.1)

where the inequality is a result of the assumption. Moreover,
-\mathcal{L}^{S}_{\epsilon}\psi_{\epsilon,i}=\lambda_{\epsilon,i}\psi_{\epsilon,i}

(5.2)

\frac{2}{n},&x\in[n,2n].\end{cases}

In addition, we require

(0,n_{0}]

converges (uniformly in

Set

\tilde{\eta}_{n,\epsilon}\in C_{0}^{2}((0,y_{\epsilon,\infty}))
with
\text{\rm supp}(\tilde{\eta}_{n,\epsilon})\subset(\xi_{\epsilon}(n_{0}-1),y_{\epsilon,\infty})

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}\tilde{\eta}^{2}_{n,\epsilon}|\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon,i}|^{2}dy+\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}\tilde{\eta}_{n,\epsilon}\tilde{\eta}^{\prime}_{n,\epsilon}\psi_{\epsilon,i}\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon,i}dy+\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}\tilde{\eta}^{2}_{n,\epsilon}W_{\epsilon}|\psi_{\epsilon,i}|^{2}dy\\
&\qquad=\lambda_{\epsilon,i}\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}\tilde{\eta}^{2}_{n,\epsilon}|\psi_{\epsilon,i}|^{2}dy\leq\lambda_{\epsilon,i}\int_{\xi_{\epsilon}(n_{0}-1)}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}|\psi_{\epsilon,i}|^{2}dy\leq\lambda_{\epsilon,i}.\end{split}

An application of Hölder’s inequality yields

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}\tilde{\eta}_{n,\epsilon}\tilde{\eta}^{\prime}_{n,\epsilon}\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon,i}\psi_{\epsilon,i}dy\right|&\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}|\tilde{\eta}_{n,\epsilon}\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon,i}|^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(2\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}|\tilde{\eta}^{\prime}_{n,\epsilon}\psi_{\epsilon,i}|^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\

Since

5.1

\begin{split}\frac{1}{8}\int_{n_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{b^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}|^{2}u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx=\frac{1}{8}\int_{\xi_{\epsilon}(n_{0})}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}\frac{b^{2}\circ\xi^{-1}_{\epsilon}}{\sigma^{2}\circ\xi^{-1}_{\epsilon}}|\psi_{\epsilon,i}|^{2}dy\leq 2M_{1},\end{split}

giving

for any

. The conclusion follows immediately from the fact

(4). This completes the proof.

5.1
Asymptotic bounds of the first eigenvalue

Note that the limit
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,1}=0
has been established in Theorem
4.1
. In the rest of this subsection, we prove finer asymptotic bounds of the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
of

stated in Theorem

The asymptotic bounds of
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
under the condition
\Lambda_{0}>0
stated in Theorem
B
(2) is restated in the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume
(H)
and
\Lambda_{0}>0
. For each
0<\gamma\ll 1
, there holds

\epsilon^{(1+\gamma)\frac{4b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}-2}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\lambda_{\epsilon,1}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\epsilon^{(1-\gamma)\frac{2b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}-1}.

The upper bound and lower bound are treated separately.

Upper bound.
As the first eigenvalue of the self-adjoint operator
-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
,
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
admits the variational formula:

\lambda_{\epsilon,1}=\inf_{\phi\in D(\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon})}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha_{\epsilon}|\phi^{\prime}|^{2}u_{\epsilon}^{G}dx}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi|^{2}u_{\epsilon}^{G}dx}\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}|^{2}e^{-2V_{\epsilon}}dx}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi_{\epsilon}|^{2}\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}e^{-2V_{\epsilon}}dx},

(5.4)

where
\phi_{\epsilon}\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))
is non-decreasing and satisfies

\phi_{\epsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&0<x<\epsilon,\\
1,&x>2\epsilon,\end{cases}\quad\,\,\text{and}\,\,\quad 0<\phi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\leq\frac{2}{\epsilon}\text{ on }\left(\epsilon,2\epsilon\right).

By
(H)
(1),
b>0
in
(0,x_{*})
for some
x_{*}>0
. Split

-V_{\epsilon}=\int_{1}^{x_{*}}\frac{b}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}ds+\int_{x_{*}}^{\bullet}\frac{b}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}ds=:A_{\epsilon}+B_{\epsilon}
. Clearly,
B_{\epsilon}
is increasing in

. Hence,

\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}|^{2}e^{-2V_{\epsilon}}dx=\int_{\epsilon}^{2\epsilon}|\phi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}|^{2}e^{-2V_{\epsilon}}dx\leq\frac{4}{\epsilon^{2}}e^{2A_{\epsilon}}\int_{\epsilon}^{2\epsilon}e^{2B_{\epsilon}(x)}dx\leq\frac{4}{\epsilon}e^{2A_{\epsilon}+2B_{\epsilon}(2\epsilon)},

and

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\phi_{\epsilon}|^{2}\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}e^{-2V_{\epsilon}}dx\geq e^{2A_{\epsilon}}\int_{2\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}e^{2B_{\epsilon}}dx=e^{2A_{\epsilon}+2B_{\epsilon}(2\epsilon)}\int_{2\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}(x)}e^{2B_{\epsilon}(x)-2B_{\epsilon}(2\epsilon)}dx.\end{split}

(5.6)

As
\Lambda_{0}>0

such that

for all

As a consequence, we find

\begin{split}2B_{\epsilon}(x)-2B_{\epsilon}(2\epsilon)&\geq 2\int_{2\epsilon}^{x}\frac{(1-\gamma)b^{\prime}(0)}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}s}ds\\
&=\kappa\ln\frac{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}x}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+2\epsilon|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}},\quad\forall x\in(2\epsilon,x_{*}),\end{split}

leading to

\begin{split}&\int_{2\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}(x)}e^{2B_{\epsilon}(x)-2B_{\epsilon}(2\epsilon)}dx\\
&\qquad\geq\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\int_{\frac{x_{*}}{2}}^{x_{*}}\frac{\left(\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}x\right)^{\kappa-1}}{\left(\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+2\epsilon|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}\right)^{\kappa}}\frac{1}{x}dx\\
&\qquad\geq\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+2\epsilon|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}\right]^{\kappa}\min_{[\frac{x_{*}}{2},x_{*}]}\left(\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}x\right)^{\kappa-1}\int_{\frac{x_{*}}{2}}^{x_{*}}\frac{1}{x}dx\\
&\qquad\geq\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+2\epsilon|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}\right]^{\kappa}\left[\frac{x_{*}|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{2}\right]^{\kappa-1}\ln 2\\
&\qquad\geq\frac{\ln 2}{1+\gamma}\left[\frac{x_{*}|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{2}\right]^{\kappa-1}\left[3\epsilon|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}\right]^{-\kappa}=:\frac{C^{\prime}_{\gamma}}{\epsilon^{\kappa}}.\end{split}

Hence, we see from (

Lower bound.

associated with

3.5

x_{*}=x_{*}(\delta)>0

We first treat

\begin{split}V_{\epsilon}(y)-V_{\epsilon}(x)\leq\frac{b^{\prime}(0)(1+\delta)}{(1-\delta)}\int_{y}^{x}\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}s}ds=\frac{\kappa}{2}\ln\frac{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}x}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}y},\end{split}

where
C_{1}:=[a^{\prime}(0)]^{1-\kappa-\gamma}|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2(\gamma-1)}(\kappa-1)^{\gamma-1}

\begin{split}\textup{I}_{\epsilon}&\leq\frac{C_{1}}{1-\delta}\epsilon^{2-2\kappa-2\gamma}\int_{0}^{x_{*}}x^{\gamma-1}[\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}x]^{\kappa-1}dx\\

for

\begin{split}\textup{II}_{\epsilon}&=\int_{x_{*}}^{x^{*}}\left(\int_{0}^{x_{*}}+\int_{x_{*}}^{x}\right)\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}(x)}e^{2[V_{\epsilon}(y)-V_{\epsilon}(x)]}dydx\\
&\leq\int_{x_{*}}^{x^{*}}\int_{0}^{x_{*}}\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}(x)}\left[\frac{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}x_{*}}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}y}\right]^{\kappa}e^{2\int_{x_{*}}^{x^{*}}\frac{|b|}{\sigma^{2}}ds}dydx\\

&=[\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}x_{*}]^{\kappa}e^{2\int_{x_{*}}^{x^{*}}\frac{|b|}{\sigma^{2}}ds}\int_{x_{*}}^{x^{*}}\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}(x)}\int_{0}^{x_{*}}[\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}y]^{-\kappa}dydx\\

&\quad+x^{*}e^{2\int_{x_{*}}^{x^{*}}\frac{|b|}{\sigma^{2}}ds}\int_{x_{*}}^{x^{*}}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}(x)}dx\\

\end{split}

(5.15)

Applying (
5.13

\kappa:=\frac{2(1+\delta)b^{\prime}(0)}{(1-\delta)|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}<1

(5.16)

Lemma 5.2

(0,y_{\epsilon,\infty})
, that is,

-\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon,2}+W_{\epsilon}\psi_{\epsilon,2}=\lambda_{\epsilon,2}\psi_{\epsilon,2}\quad\text{in}\quad(0,y_{\epsilon,\infty}),

(5.22)

where we recall that
y_{\epsilon,\infty}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}a+|\sigma|^{2}}}ds
. By
(H)
(2)-(3),
y_{\epsilon,\infty}
increases to
\infty
as
\epsilon\to 0
.

Fix
\eta_{*}\in(0,1)
(whose exactly value is to be determined) and
0<\delta_{*}\ll 1
. Let
\{\eta_{\delta}\}_{0<\delta<\delta_{*}}
be a family of functions in
C^{\infty}_{0}((0,1))
, take values in
[0,\eta_{*}]
and satisfy

\eta_{\delta}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&y\in(0,\delta),\\
\eta_{*},&y\in(2\delta,\frac{1}{2}),\end{cases}\quad 0\leq\eta^{\prime}_{\delta}\leq\frac{2\eta_{*}}{\delta}\text{ on }[\delta,2\delta]\quad\,\,\text{and}\,\,\quad|\eta^{\prime}_{\delta}|\leq 4\eta_{*}\text{ on }\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right).

(5.23)

Multiplying (
5.22
) by
\eta^{2}_{\delta}\psi_{\epsilon,2}
and integrating by parts yield

\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\eta^{2}_{\delta}|\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy+\int_{0}^{1}\eta_{\delta}\psi_{\epsilon,2}\eta^{\prime}_{\delta}\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon,2}dy+\int_{0}^{1}W_{\epsilon}\eta^{2}_{\delta}|\psi_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy=\lambda_{\epsilon,2}\int_{0}^{1}\eta_{\delta}^{2}|\psi_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy.

(5.24)

An application of Hölder’s inequality and (
5.23
) yields

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\eta_{\delta}\psi_{\epsilon,2}\eta^{\prime}_{\delta}\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon,2}dy\right|&\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\eta^{2}_{\delta}|\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy+\int_{0}^{1}|\eta^{\prime}_{\delta}|^{2}|\psi_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy\\
&\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\eta^{2}_{\delta}|\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy+\frac{4\eta_{*}^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\int_{\delta}^{2\delta}|\psi_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy+16\eta_{*}^{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}|\psi_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy.\end{split}

Thanks to Lemma
3.3
(2) and (4), we can find
C>0
and
M>1
, and make
\delta_{*}
smaller if necessary (all independent of
\epsilon
) such that
W_{\epsilon}(y)\geq\frac{C}{y^{2}}\geq\frac{C}{4\delta^{2}}
for
y\in(\delta,2\delta)
and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{*})
and
W_{\epsilon}+M\geq 1
. Setting
\eta_{*}:=\min\left\{\frac{\sqrt{C}}{4\sqrt{2}},\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}}\right\}
, we derive

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\eta_{\delta}\psi_{\epsilon,2}\eta^{\prime}_{\delta}\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon,2}dy\right|&\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\eta^{2}_{\delta}|\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy+\frac{C}{8\delta^{2}}\int_{\delta}^{2\delta}|\psi_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}|\psi_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy\\
&\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\eta^{2}_{\delta}|\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(W_{\epsilon}+M)\eta^{2}_{\delta}|\psi_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy.\end{split}

It then follows from (
5.24
) that

\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\eta^{2}_{\delta}|\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(W_{\epsilon}+M)\eta^{2}_{\delta}|\psi_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy\leq\left(\lambda_{\epsilon,2}+\frac{M}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{1}\eta^{2}_{\delta}|\psi_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy,

and thus,

\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\eta^{2}_{\delta}|\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\eta^{2}_{\delta}|\psi_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy\leq\left(\lambda_{\epsilon,2}+\frac{M}{2}\right)\eta_{*}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\psi_{\epsilon,2}|^{2}dy.

Note that it suffices to prove the result for each
0<\gamma\ll 1
. Let
\gamma
be such a number. As
\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,2}<\infty
(by Lemma
3.2
) and
\|\psi_{\epsilon,2}\|_{L^{2}((0,y_{\epsilon,\infty}))}=\|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}\|_{L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})}<\infty
, the Sobolev embedding theorem gives
\|\eta_{\delta}\psi_{\epsilon,2}\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}([0,1])}\leq C_{1}\|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}\|_{L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})}
for some
C_{1}=C_{1}(\gamma)>0
(independent of
\delta
).
In particular,

|\eta_{\delta}(y)\psi_{\epsilon,2}(y)|\leq C_{1}\|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}\|_{L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})}y^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma},\quad\forall y\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right).

As
\eta_{\delta}
converges to
\eta_{*}
on
(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we let
\delta\to 0^{+}
in the above inequality to find

|\psi_{\epsilon,2}(y)|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\eta_{*}}\|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}\|_{L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})}y^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}=:C_{2}y^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma},\quad\forall y\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right),

where
C_{2}=C_{2}(\epsilon,\gamma):=\frac{C_{1}}{\eta_{*}}\|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}\|_{L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})}
. Setting
x^{\epsilon}_{*}:=\xi^{-1}_{\epsilon}(\frac{1}{2})
, we see from
u_{\epsilon,2}=\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}u^{G}_{\epsilon}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}=U_{\epsilon}^{-1}\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\psi_{\epsilon,2}
that

\begin{split}|u_{\epsilon,2}|=|\psi_{\epsilon,2}\circ\xi_{\epsilon}|\alpha_{\epsilon}^{-\frac{3}{4}}e^{-V_{\epsilon}}\leq C_{2}\xi_{\epsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}(\epsilon^{2}a)^{-\frac{3}{4}}e^{-V_{\epsilon}}\quad\text{in}\quad(0,x^{\epsilon}_{*}).\end{split}

(5.25)

Since
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\xi_{\epsilon}(x)=\infty
for any
x>0
, there must hold
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}x^{\epsilon}_{*}=0
. Hence,
a(x)\geq\frac{1}{2}a^{\prime}(0)x
for
x\in(0,x^{\epsilon}_{*})
. Then,

\xi_{\epsilon}(x)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\epsilon\sqrt{a^{\prime}(0)}}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}ds=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\epsilon}\sqrt{\frac{x}{a^{\prime}(0)}},\quad\forall x\in(0,x^{\epsilon}_{*}).

As
V^{\prime}_{\epsilon}=-\frac{b}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}\leq 0
near
0
,
V_{\epsilon}
is non-increasing in
(0,x^{\epsilon}_{*})
. It follows from (
5.25
) that

\begin{split}|u_{\epsilon,2}(x)|&\leq C_{2}\left(\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\epsilon}\sqrt{\frac{x}{a^{\prime}(0)}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\left[\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)x\right]^{-\frac{3}{4}}e^{-V_{\epsilon}(x^{\epsilon}_{*})}=:\frac{C}{x^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}}},\quad\forall x\in(0,x_{*}^{\epsilon}).\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

The following result is in preparation for the contradiction arguments for
\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}
to be used in the proof of Theorem
5.3
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume
(H)
and
\Lambda_{0}>0
.

(1)

\lim_{z\to 0}\sup_{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{z}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx=0
.

(2)

If
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,2}=0
, then
\lim_{z\to 0}\sup_{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{z}|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}|u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx=0
.

As
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,1}=0
by Theorem
4.1
, the proof is done if we show that for
i=1,2
, the condition
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,i}=0
implies
\lim_{z\to 0}\sup_{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{z}|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}|u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx=0
. As
u_{\epsilon,i}=\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}u^{G}_{\epsilon}
, it is the same as showing

\lim_{z\to 0}\sup_{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{z}|u_{\epsilon,i}|dx=0.

(5.26)

We proceed as in the proof of Proposition
4.2
. Given
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,i}=0
, we apply Lemma
4.1
to find for fixed
k\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
the existence of
x_{1}\in(0,1)
such that
(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\epsilon}+\lambda_{\epsilon,i})x^{-k}<0
in
(0,x_{1})
. Setting
v_{\epsilon,i}:=\frac{u_{\epsilon,i}}{x^{-k}}
, we compute using (
5.21
)

\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\epsilon}v^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon,i}+\left(\alpha^{\prime}_{\epsilon}-b-\frac{k}{x}\alpha_{\epsilon}\right)v^{\prime}_{\epsilon,i}+\frac{(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\epsilon}+\lambda_{\epsilon,i})x^{-k}}{x^{-k}}v_{\epsilon,i}=0\quad\text{in}\quad(0,\infty).

(5.27)

Note that
\lim_{x\to 0}|v_{\epsilon,i}(x)|=0
. Indeed, Lemma
5.2
implies that there are
C_{\epsilon}>0
and
x_{\epsilon}>0
such that
|u_{\epsilon,i}(x)|\leq C_{\epsilon}x^{-\frac{1}{2}(k+\frac{1}{2})}
for
x\in(0,x_{\epsilon})
. Hence,

\lim_{x\to 0}|v_{\epsilon,i}(x)|\leq C_{\epsilon}\lim_{x\to 0}x^{k-\frac{1}{2}(k+\frac{1}{2})}=0
.

Let
x_{2}\in(0,x_{1})
. Note that the equation (
5.21
) can be written as

\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha_{\epsilon}u^{\prime}_{\epsilon,i}\right)^{\prime}+\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{\prime}_{\epsilon}-b\right)u_{\epsilon,i}\right]^{\prime}+\lambda_{\epsilon,i}u_{\epsilon,i}=0.

(5.28)

Due to the first limit in (
3.1
) and
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,i}=0
(by assumption), we apply the classical interior De Giorgi-Nash-Moser estimates (see e.g.
[
11
,
24
]
) in
(\frac{x_{2}}{2},\frac{3x_{2}}{2})
to find
C>0
(independent of
\epsilon
) such that

\sup_{(\frac{3x_{2}}{4},\frac{5x_{2}}{4})}|u_{\epsilon,i}|\leq\frac{C}{x_{2}}\int_{\frac{x_{2}}{2}}^{\frac{3x_{2}}{2}}|u_{\epsilon,i}|dx=\frac{C}{x_{2}}\int_{\frac{x_{2}}{2}}^{\frac{3x_{2}}{2}}|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}|u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx\leq\frac{C}{x_{2}},

where we used the normalization (
5.20
) in the last inequality. Hence,
|v_{\epsilon,i}(x_{2})|\leq Cx_{2}^{k-1}
.

Since
v_{\epsilon,i}
satisfies (
5.27
),
\lim_{x\to 0}v_{\epsilon,i}(x)=0
and
(\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\epsilon}+\lambda_{\epsilon,i})x^{-k}<0
, we apply the maximum principle to
v_{\epsilon,i}
in
(0,x_{2})
and conclude that
\sup_{x\in(0,x_{2})}|v_{\epsilon,i}(x)|=|v_{\epsilon,i}(x_{2})|\leq Cx_{2}^{k-1}
, leading to
|u_{\epsilon,i}(x)|\leq Cx_{2}^{k-1}x^{-k}
for
x\in(0,x_{2})
. Thus,

\int_{0}^{x^{2}_{2}}|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}|u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx=\int_{0}^{x^{2}_{2}}|u_{\epsilon,i}|dx\leq Cx_{2}^{k-1}\int_{0}^{x^{2}_{2}}x^{-k}dx=\frac{C}{1-k}x_{2}^{1-k}.

Since the above estimate holds for any
x_{2}\in(0,x_{1})
and is uniform in
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, we arrive at (
5.26
), and hence, proves the lemma.
∎

The monotonicity of
\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}
is addressed in the next result.

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume
(H)
. There holds
\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}^{\prime}>0
.

Note that
\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}
satisfies
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}=-\lambda_{\epsilon,1}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}
, or
\left(e^{-2V_{\epsilon}}\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}_{\epsilon,1}\right)^{\prime}=-\frac{2}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}\lambda_{\epsilon,1}e^{-2V_{\epsilon}}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}
. Since
\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}>0
,
e^{-2V_{\epsilon}}\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}_{\epsilon,1}
is strictly decreasing.

Suppose on the contrary that
\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}_{\epsilon,1}(x_{0})\leq 0
for some
x_{0}\in(0,\infty)
. Then, there is
x_{1}>x_{0}
such that

e^{-2V_{\epsilon}(x)}\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}_{\epsilon,1}(x)<e^{-2V_{\epsilon}(x_{1})}\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}_{\epsilon,1}(x_{1})<0,\quad\forall x>x_{1},

yielding
\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}_{\epsilon,1}(x)<e^{2(V_{\epsilon}(x)-V_{\epsilon}(x_{1}))}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}^{\prime}(x_{1})
for
x>x_{1}
. As
V_{\epsilon}(x)-V_{\epsilon}(x_{1})=-\int_{x_{1}}^{x}\frac{b}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}ds\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
, we find
\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}_{\epsilon,1}(x)\to-\infty
as
x\to\infty
, and hence,
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}(x)=-\infty
, contradicting
\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}>0
. Hence,
\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}_{\epsilon,1}>0
.
∎

The last lemma is elementary.

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume
(H)
. Then,
\sup_{\epsilon}\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx<\infty
.

By
(H)
(1)(4), there exist
x_{1}>0
and
\epsilon_{*}>0
such that
b<0
and
\epsilon^{2}a+\sigma^{2}\leq 2\sigma^{2}
in
(x_{1},\infty)
for all
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{*}
. Therefore,

\begin{split}u^{G}_{\epsilon}(x)&=\exp\left\{2\int_{1}^{x}\frac{b}{\epsilon^{2}a+\sigma^{2}}ds-\ln(\epsilon^{2}a(x)+\sigma^{2}(x))\right\}\\
&\leq\exp\left\{2\int_{1}^{x_{1}}\frac{b}{\epsilon^{2}a+\sigma^{2}}ds\right\}\times\exp\left\{\int_{x_{1}}^{x}\frac{b}{2\sigma^{2}}ds-\ln\sigma^{2}(x)\right\},\quad\forall x>x_{1}.\end{split}

Obviously, the conclusion follows if we show

\int_{x_{1}}^{\infty}\exp\left\{\int_{x_{1}}^{x}\frac{b}{2\sigma^{2}}ds-\ln\sigma^{2}(x)\right\}dx<\infty
, which can be verified by arguments as in the proof of Lemma
3.6
.
∎

We are ready to prove Theorem
5.3
.

Suppose on the contrary that
\inf_{\epsilon}\lambda_{\epsilon,2}=0
. Up to a subsequence, we may assume, without loss of generality, that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,2}=0
. We derive a contradiction within four steps.

Step 1.
We show for
i=1,2
the existence of
u_{i}\in C^{2}((0,\infty))\cap L^{1}((0,\infty))
satisfying
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{0}u_{i}=0
such that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}u_{\epsilon,i}=u_{i}
in
C^{2}((0,\infty))
.

Recall that
u_{\epsilon,i}=\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}u^{G}_{\epsilon}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}
satisfies (
5.20
). We apply Hölder’s inequality to find

\begin{split}\sup_{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{\infty}|u_{\epsilon,i}|dx&\leq 1+\sup_{\epsilon}\int_{2}^{\infty}|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}|u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx\\
&\leq 1+\sup_{\epsilon}\left(\int_{2}^{\infty}|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}|^{2}u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{2}^{\infty}u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq 1+\sup_{\epsilon}\left(\int_{2}^{\infty}u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty,\end{split}

(5.29)

where we used the normalization (
5.20
) in the first and last inequalities, and Lemma
5.5
to derive the final uniform boundedness. Considering the positive and negative parts of
\{u_{\epsilon,i}\}_{\epsilon}
separately, we apply Helly’s selection principle (see e.g.
[
12
, Theorem 4.3.3 and 4.4.1]
) to find a signed Borel measure
\mu_{i}
on
(0,\infty)
such that, up to a subsequence,
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi u_{\epsilon,i}dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi d\mu_{i}
for any
\phi\in C_{c}((0,\infty))
.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0
in (
5.21
), we find
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{0}\mu_{i}=0
in the weak sense from the first limit in (
3.1
) and
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\epsilon,i}=0
(by Theorem
4.1
if
i=1
and assumption if
i=2
). Moreover, we apply the classical interior De Giorgi-Nash-Moser estimates (see e.g.
[
11
,
24
]
) to find that for any open intervals
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{I}\subset\subset\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subset\subset(0,\infty)
, there exists
C=C(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\prime})>0
(independent of
\epsilon
) such that

\sup_{\mathcal{I}}|u_{\epsilon,i}|\leq C\|u_{\epsilon,i}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})}
. Then, we can follow the arguments as in the proof of Theorem
A
(2) to conclude that
\mu_{i}
admits a density
u_{i}\in C^{2}((0,\infty))
and
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}u_{\epsilon,i}=u_{i}
in
C^{2}((0,\infty))
. The estimate (
5.29
) and Fatou’s lemma guarantee
u_{i}\in L^{1}((0,\infty))
.

Step 2.
We show the existence of
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}\neq 0
such that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}=C_{i}
in
C^{2}((0,\infty))
for
i=1,2
.

By
Step 1
and
\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}>0
, we apply Lemma
3.7
to find
C_{1}\geq 0
and
C_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
u_{i}=C_{i}u^{G}_{0}
for
i=1,2
. Recall
\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}=\frac{u_{\epsilon,i}}{u^{G}_{\epsilon}}
. Thanks to
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}u_{\epsilon,i}=u_{i}
in
C^{2}((0,\infty))
(by
Step 1
) and (
3.2
), the limit
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}=C_{i}
holds in
C^{2}((0,\infty))
.

By Lemmas
5.1
and
5.3
, the normalization (
5.20
) ensures the existence of some
\kappa\gg 1
such that
\int_{\frac{1}{\kappa}}^{2}|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}|u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx+\int_{1}^{\kappa}|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,i}|^{2}u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
Letting
\epsilon\to 0
yields
|C_{i}|\int_{\frac{1}{\kappa}}^{2}u^{G}_{0}dx+C_{i}^{2}\int_{1}^{\kappa}u^{G}_{0}dx\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Hence,
C_{i}\neq 0
. In particular,
C_{1}>0
.

Step 3.
We show that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx=C_{1}C_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{G}_{0}dx\neq 0
.

Obviously, for any
\kappa>1
, there holds

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx-C_{1}C_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{G}_{0}dx\right|\\
&\qquad\leq\left|\int^{\kappa}_{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx-C_{1}C_{2}\int^{\kappa}_{\frac{1}{\kappa}}u^{G}_{0}dx\right|+\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}+\int_{\kappa}^{\infty}\right)\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}|u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx\\
&\qquad\quad+C_{1}|C_{2}|\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}+\int_{\kappa}^{\infty}\right)u^{G}_{0}dx=:\textup{I}_{\epsilon}(\kappa)+\textup{II}_{\epsilon}(\kappa)+\textup{III}(\kappa).\end{split}

(5.30)

We claim that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\textup{I}_{\epsilon}(\kappa)=0,\quad\forall\kappa>1,\quad\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\sup_{\epsilon}\textup{II}_{\epsilon}(\kappa)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\textup{III}(\kappa)=0.

(5.31)

Given (
5.31
), the conclusion follows from taking the limit
\epsilon\to 0
and then
\kappa\to\infty
in (
5.30
).

We prove (
5.31
). Clearly,
\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{0}^{G}dx<\infty
(by Lemma
3.6
) yields
\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\textup{III}(\kappa)=0
. Thanks to
Step 2
and (
3.2
), we see
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\textup{I}_{\epsilon}(\kappa)=0
for any
\kappa>1
.

For
\textup{II}_{\epsilon}(\kappa)
, Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
5.1
yield
\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\sup_{\epsilon}\int_{\kappa}^{\infty}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}|u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx=0
. Note that Lemma
5.4
and
Step 2
imply

\sup_{\epsilon}\sup_{(0,x)}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}\leq\sup_{\epsilon}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}(x)<\infty,\quad\forall x>0,

(5.32)

which together with Lemma
5.3
(2) leads to
\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\sup_{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}|\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}|u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx=0
. It follows that
\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\sup_{\epsilon}\textup{II}_{\epsilon}(\kappa)=0
. The claim (
5.31
), and hence, the conclusion is proven.

Step 4.
Recall that
-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
is self-adjoint in
L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})
. Since
\tilde{\phi}_{1,\epsilon}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{2,\epsilon}
are eigenfunctions associated with
\lambda_{\epsilon,1}
and
\lambda_{\epsilon,2}
, respectively, they are orthogonal in
L^{2}(u^{G}_{\epsilon})
, namely,
\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,1}\tilde{\phi}_{\epsilon,2}u^{G}_{\epsilon}dx=0
, which is in contradictory to
Step 3
.

In conclusion,
\inf_{\epsilon}\lambda_{\epsilon,2}>0
and the theorem is proven.
∎

6
Multiscale dynamics

In this section, we study the multiscale dynamics of the distribution of
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
and prove Theorem
C
. Recall from Lemma
3.1
the semigroup
(P_{t}^{\epsilon})_{t\geq 0}
and for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
the spectral projection
Q_{k}^{\epsilon}
of
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
corresponding to the eigenvalues
\{-\lambda_{\epsilon,j}\}_{j\geq k}
. The following lemma plays a crucial role in the proof of Theorem
C
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume
(H)
and
\Lambda_{0}>0
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there is
C_{k}>0
such that for
0<\epsilon\ll 1
,

|P^{\epsilon}_{t}Q^{\epsilon}_{k}f|\leq C_{k}\alpha_{\epsilon}^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{V_{\epsilon}}e^{-\lambda_{\epsilon,k}t}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\quad\text{in}\quad(0,\infty),\quad\forall t>2\,\,\text{and}\,\,f\in C_{b}((0,\infty)).

Set
\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t}:=\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}U_{\epsilon}P^{\epsilon}_{t}U_{\epsilon}^{-1}\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}^{-1}
, where
U_{\epsilon}
and
\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}
are unitary transforms specified in Subsection
3.2
. Then,
(\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is an analytic semigroup of contractions on
L^{2}((0,y_{\epsilon,\infty}))
generated by
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{S}
. The spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{S}
, being the same as that of
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
, consists of simple eigenvalues
\{-\lambda_{\epsilon,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
(see Lemma
3.1
). We finish the proof within four steps.

Step 1.
We show for each
p\in(2,\infty]
, there is
D_{1}(p)>0
such that

.

According to Lemma

(6.1)

As

generates the analytic semigroup

) is independent of

(independent of

(6.2)

Let

be the domain of

. Since

\begin{split}\langle-(\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{S}-M)u,u\rangle_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}|u^{\prime}|dy+\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}(W_{\epsilon}+M)|u|^{2}dy,\quad\forall u\in D(\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{S}),\end{split}

we derive from

and (

) that for

and

,

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}|\partial_{y}\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t}\tilde{f}|^{2}dy+\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}|\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t}\tilde{f}|^{2}dy\\
&\qquad\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}|\partial_{y}\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t}\tilde{f}|^{2}dy+\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}(W_{\epsilon}+M)|\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t}\tilde{f}|^{2}dy\\
&\qquad=\langle-(\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}^{S}-M)\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t}\tilde{f},\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t}\tilde{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
&\qquad\leq\frac{C_{1}e^{Mt}}{t}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}}\|\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C^{2}_{1}e^{2Mt}}{2t^{2}}\|\tilde{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}|\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t}\tilde{f}|^{2}dy,\end{split}

leading to

\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}|\partial_{y}\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t}\tilde{f}|^{2}dy+\int_{0}^{y_{\epsilon,\infty}}|\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t}\tilde{f}|^{2}dy\leq\frac{C^{2}_{1}e^{2Mt}}{t^{2}}\|\tilde{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Since Lemma

(2)-(3) ensures

blows up at

and

, we see that

belongs to

such that

\begin{split}\|\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}}&\leq C_{2}(p)\left(\|\partial_{y}\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\tilde{P}^{\epsilon}_{t}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}C_{1}C_{2}(p)}{t}e^{Mt}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Setting

yields the result with

.

Step 2.
We prove that for each

) for some

(6.4)

\begin{split}-V_{\epsilon}(x)&=\int_{x_{1}}^{x}\frac{b}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}ds+\int_{1}^{x_{1}}\frac{b}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}ds\\

where

As

Asymptotic bounds of the mean extinction time

(7.1)

s_{\epsilon}(0+)\in(-\infty,0)
and
r_{\epsilon}(0+)\in(0,\infty)
.

Replacing
\alpha_{\epsilon}
by
\sigma^{2}
in the definition of
s_{\epsilon}
and
r_{\epsilon}
, we define
s_{0}
and
r_{0}
. It is straightforward to check that
s_{0}(0+)\in(-\infty,0)
when
\Lambda_{0}<0
, and
s_{0}(0+)=-\infty
when
\Lambda_{0}>0
. Moreover,
r_{0}(0+)=\infty
.

We establish three lemmas before proving Theorem
D
. The first one concerns the asymptotic of
s_{\epsilon}(0+)
and
\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{x}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}}=\beta]
for
x\in(0,\beta)
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Assume
(H)
. Then,
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}s_{\epsilon}=s_{0}
. Moreover,

(1)

if
\Lambda_{0}<0
, then
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}s_{\epsilon}(0+)=s_{0}(0+)>-\infty
and

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{x}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}}=\beta]=\frac{s_{0}(x)-s_{0}(0+)}{s_{0}(\beta)-s_{0}(0+)}\in(0,1),\quad\forall x\in(0,\beta);

(2)

if
\Lambda_{0}>0
, then there are
C_{1},C_{2}>0
(depending on
\delta
) such that

C_{1}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{-}-1)}\lesssim_{\epsilon}-s_{\epsilon}(0+)\lesssim_{\epsilon}C_{2}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{+}-1)},

and for each
x\in(0,\beta)
, there are
C_{3},C_{4}>0
such that

1-C_{3}\epsilon^{2(\kappa_{-}-1)}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{x}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}}=\beta]\lesssim_{\epsilon}1-C_{4}\epsilon^{2(\kappa_{+}-1)}.

Since
\alpha_{\epsilon}\downarrow\sigma^{2}
on
(0,\beta)
as
\epsilon\to 0
, we apply the monotone convergence theorem to find
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}s_{\epsilon}=s_{0}
and
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}s_{\epsilon}(0+)=s_{0}(0+)
.

It is well known (see e.g.
[
34
, Theorem 6.3.1]
) that

\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{x}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}}=\beta]=\frac{s_{\epsilon}(x)-s_{\epsilon}(0+)}{s_{\epsilon}(\beta)-s_{\epsilon}(0+)},\quad\forall x\in(0,\beta).

(7.3)

(1) It is easy to see that
-s_{0}(0+)<\infty
. The limiting equality follows by letting
\epsilon\to 0
in (
7.3
).

(2) Using (
7.1
), we find

\begin{split}-s_{\epsilon}(0+)\leq\int_{0}^{x_{*}}e^{\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}\int_{y}^{x_{*}}\frac{2b^{\prime}(0)}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}s}ds}dy=\int_{0}^{x_{*}}\left[\frac{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}x_{*}}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}y}\right]^{\kappa_{+}}dy.\end{split}

Note that
\kappa_{+}>\kappa_{-}>1
in this case. Calculating the last integral leads to

\begin{split}-s_{\epsilon}(0+)&\leq 2[|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}x_{*}]^{\kappa_{+}}\frac{1}{(-\kappa_{+}+1)|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}\left[\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}y\right]^{-\kappa_{+}+1}\Big|_{y=0}^{x_{*}}\\
&\leq\frac{2x_{*}^{\kappa_{+}}|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2(\kappa_{+}-1)}}{(\kappa_{+}-1)[\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)]^{\kappa_{+}-1}}\left\{1-\frac{[\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)]^{\kappa_{+}-1}}{[\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}x_{*}]^{\kappa_{+}-1}}\right\}\\
&\approx_{\epsilon}\frac{2x_{*}^{\kappa_{+}}|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2(\kappa_{+}-1)}}{(\kappa_{+}-1)[a^{\prime}(0)]^{\kappa_{+}-1}}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{+}-1)}=:C_{1}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{+}-1)},\end{split}

which together with (
7.3
) leads to

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{x}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau}=\beta]&=1-\frac{s_{\epsilon}(\beta)-s_{\epsilon}(x)}{s_{\epsilon}(\beta)-s_{\epsilon}(0+)}\\
&\approx_{\epsilon}1-\frac{s_{0}(\beta)-s_{0}(x)}{s_{0}(\beta)-s_{\epsilon}(0+)}\\
&\lesssim_{\epsilon}1-[s_{0}(\beta)-s_{0}(x)]C_{1}^{-1}\epsilon^{2(\kappa_{+}-1)}=:1-C_{2}\epsilon^{2(\kappa_{+}-1)}.\end{split}

Similarly, there exist
C_{3},C_{4}>0
such that
-s_{\epsilon}(0)\gtrsim_{\epsilon}C_{3}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{-}-1)}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{x}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau}=\beta]\gtrsim_{\epsilon}1-C_{4}\epsilon^{2(\kappa_{-}-1)}
. This proves (2).
∎

In the second lemma, we study the asymptotic bounds of the mean exit time
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}]
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Assume
(H)
.

1.

If
\Lambda_{0}<0
, then there are
C_{1},C_{2}>0
(depending on
\delta
) such that

C_{1}|\ln\epsilon|\lesssim_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}]\lesssim_{\epsilon}C_{2}|\ln\epsilon|.

2.

If
\Lambda_{0}>0
, then
\inf_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}]>0
and
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}]\lesssim_{\epsilon}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{+}-\kappa_{-}+\delta)}
.

We first show that

\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}]=\frac{2\left[r_{\epsilon}(0+)s_{\epsilon}(\beta)-r_{\epsilon}(\beta)s_{\epsilon}(0+)\right]}{s_{\epsilon}(\beta)-s_{\epsilon}(0+)}.

(7.4)

It is well known that
u_{\epsilon}:=\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\bullet}[\tau^{\epsilon}]
solves

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\epsilon}u^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}+bu^{\prime}_{\epsilon}=-1\quad\text{in}\quad(0,\beta),\\
u_{\epsilon}(0)=0=u_{\epsilon}(\beta).\end{cases}

Direct calculations yield

\begin{split}u_{\epsilon}(x)&=-2r_{\epsilon}(x)+\frac{2(s_{\epsilon}(x)-s_{\epsilon}(0+))}{s_{\epsilon}(\beta)-s_{\epsilon}(0+)}r_{\epsilon}(\beta)+\frac{2(s_{\epsilon}(\beta)-s_{\epsilon}(x))}{s_{\epsilon}(\beta)-s_{\epsilon}(0+)}r_{\epsilon}(0+).\end{split}

Setting
x=x_{*}
, we derive (
7.4
) from
s_{\epsilon}(x_{*})=0
and
r_{\epsilon}(x_{*})=0
.

(1) Note
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}r_{\epsilon}(\beta)=r_{0}(\beta)
. Since
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}s_{\epsilon}(\beta)=s_{0}(\beta)
and
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}s_{\epsilon}(0+)=s_{0}(0+)
by Lemma
7.1
, we find from (
7.4
) that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}]&\approx_{\epsilon}\frac{2s_{0}(\beta)r_{\epsilon}(0+)}{s_{0}(\beta)-s_{0}(0+)}-\frac{2r_{0}(\beta)s_{0}(0+)}{s_{0}(\beta)-s_{0}(0+)}.\end{split}

(7.5)

If there are
C_{1},C_{2}>0
(depending on
\delta
) such that

C_{1}|\ln\epsilon|\lesssim_{\epsilon}r_{\epsilon}(0+)\lesssim_{\epsilon}C_{2}|\ln\epsilon|,

(7.6)

we deduce from (
7.5
) that

\frac{2C_{1}s_{0}(\beta)}{s_{0}(\beta)-s_{0}(0+)}|\ln\epsilon|\lesssim_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}]\lesssim_{\epsilon}\frac{2C_{2}s_{0}(\beta)}{s_{0}(\beta)-s_{0}(0+)}|\ln\epsilon|
, leading to the conclusion.

It remains to show (
7.6
). Thanks to (
7.1
), we compute

\begin{split}r_{\epsilon}(0+)&=\int_{0}^{x_{*}}\int_{y}^{x_{*}}\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}(z)}e^{2\int_{y}^{z}\frac{b}{\alpha_{\epsilon}}ds}dzdy\\
&\geq\int_{0}^{x_{*}}\int_{y}^{x_{*}}\frac{1}{(1+\delta)z[\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}z]}e^{\frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta}\int_{y}^{z}\frac{2b^{\prime}(0)}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}s}ds}dzdy\\
&=\frac{1}{1+\delta}\int_{0}^{x_{*}}\int_{y}^{x_{*}}\frac{1}{z}[\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|z]^{\kappa_{-}-1}[\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|y]^{-\kappa_{-}}dzdy\\
&\geq\frac{1}{1+\delta}\int_{0}^{\frac{x_{*}}{2}}\int_{y}^{2y}[\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|y]^{-1}\frac{1}{z}\left[\frac{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|z}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|y}\right]^{\kappa_{-}-1}dzdy.\end{split}

Noting that

\frac{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|z}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|y}\leq\frac{2\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|2y}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|y}=2,\quad\forall y\in\left(0,\frac{x_{*}}{2}\right)\,\,\text{and}\,\,z\in(y,2y),

we deduce from the fact
\kappa_{-}<1
that

\begin{split}r_{\epsilon}(0+)&\geq\frac{1}{1+\delta}\int_{0}^{\frac{x_{*}}{2}}[\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|y]^{-1}\int_{y}^{2y}\frac{1}{z}2^{\kappa_{-}-1}dzdy\\
&=\frac{2^{\kappa_{-}-1}\ln 2}{(1+\delta)|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}\ln\frac{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)+|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}\frac{x_{*}}{2}}{\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)}\\
&=\frac{2^{\kappa_{-}-1}\ln 2}{(1+\delta)|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}\ln\left(1+\frac{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}x_{*}}{2\epsilon^{2}a^{\prime}(0)}\right)\approx_{\epsilon}C_{1}|\ln\epsilon|,\end{split}

where
C_{1}:=\frac{2^{\kappa_{-}}\ln 2}{(1+\delta)|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}
, and the equality follows from direct calculations of the double integral.

To derive an upper bound, we change the order of integration to rewrite
r_{\epsilon}(0+)
as

r_{\epsilon}(0+)=\int_{0}^{x_{*}}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}(z)}e^{2(V_{\epsilon}(y)-V_{\epsilon}(z))}dydz,

(7.7)

which is just
\textup{I}_{\epsilon}
in (
5.17
). By (
5.19
),
r_{\epsilon}(0+)\lesssim_{\epsilon}C_{2}|\ln\epsilon|
for some
C_{2}>0
. Hence, (
7.6
) follows.

(2) Let
\hat{x}\in(0,x_{*})
. Obviously,
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}]\geq\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}]
, where
\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:X^{\epsilon}_{t}=\hat{x}\text{ or }\beta\}
. Note that
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\bullet}[\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}]
solves

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\epsilon}u^{\prime\prime}+bu^{\prime}=-1\quad\text{in}\quad(\hat{x},\beta),\\
u(\hat{x})=0=u(\beta).\end{cases}

As
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\alpha_{\epsilon}=\sigma^{2}
uniformly in
[\hat{x},\beta]
, the classical PDE theory ensures that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\bullet}[\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}]=u_{0}
uniformly in
[\hat{x},\beta]
, where
u_{0}
is the unique solution of

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}u_{0}^{\prime\prime}+bu_{0}^{\prime}=-1\quad\text{in}\quad(\hat{x},\beta),\\
u_{0}(\hat{x})=0=u_{0}(\beta).\end{cases}

Since
u_{0}(x_{*})>0
by the maximum principle, we conclude
\inf_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}]>0
.

It remains to derive the upper bound for
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}]
. Note that
\kappa_{+}>\kappa_{-}>1
. Using (
7.7
),
we apply (
5.14
) to find
r_{\epsilon}(0+)\lesssim_{\epsilon}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{+}-1+\delta)}
. Since
-s_{\epsilon}(0+)\gtrsim_{\epsilon}C_{1}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{-}-1)}
due to Lemma
7.1
, we deduce from (
7.4
) that

\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}]\approx_{\epsilon}\frac{2s_{0}(\beta)r_{\epsilon}(0+)}{s_{0}(\beta)-s_{\epsilon}(0+)}+2r_{0}(\beta)\lesssim_{\epsilon}\frac{2s_{0}(\beta)\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{+}-1+\delta)}}{C_{1}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{-}-1)}}=\frac{2s_{0}(\beta)}{C_{1}}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{+}-\kappa_{-}+\delta)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

The third lemma addresses the uniform-in-
\epsilon
finiteness of the mean hitting time
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\bullet}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}]
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Assume
(H)
. Then,
\sup_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}]<\infty
for each
x>x_{*}
.

Fix
x>x_{*}
. As in the proof of Proposition
4.1
, we can find a function
V\in C^{2}(0,\infty)
and a number
N_{0}\in(x,\infty)
such that
V(N_{0})>0
and
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}V\leq-\frac{b^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}
in
(N_{0},\infty)
. Since
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{b}{|\sigma|}=-\infty
by
(H4)
, we may assume
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}V\leq-1
in
(N_{0},\infty)
. Set
\tau^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:X^{\epsilon}_{t}=N_{0}\}
. An application of Itô-Dynkin’s formula yields
0\leq\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}+1}[V(\tau^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}})]\leq V(N_{0}+1)-\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}+1}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}]
,
leading to

\sup_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}+1}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}]\leq V(N_{0}+1).

(7.8)

Set
\tau^{\epsilon}_{(x_{*},N_{0}+1)}:=\{t\geq 0:X^{\epsilon}_{t}=x_{*}\text{ or }N_{0}+1\}
. Then,
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\bullet}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{(x_{*},N_{0}+1)}]
on
[x_{*},N_{0}+1]
solves

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}u=-1\quad\text{in}\quad(x_{*},N_{0}+1),\\
u(x_{*})=0=u(N_{0}+1).\end{cases}

Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
7.2
(2), we find

\sup_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{(x_{*},N_{0}+1)}]<\infty.

(7.9)

Let
X^{\epsilon}_{0}=N_{0}
,
\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{0}=0
and define recursively the following sequences of stopping times: before the first time
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
reaches
x_{*}
(i.e.,
\tau_{x_{*}}^{\epsilon}
), for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}
be the first time after
\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n-1}
at which
X^{\epsilon}_{t}
reaches
N_{0}+1
, and
\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n}
be the first time after
\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}
at which
X^{\epsilon}_{t}
reaches
N_{0}
; since
\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\tau_{x_{*}}^{\epsilon}<\infty]=1
,
\tau_{n}^{\epsilon}
and
\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n}
are defined up to some random index
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
; let
\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}=\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n}=\tau^{\epsilon}_{x^{*}}
for all
n\geq n_{0}+1
. To be more specific, we recursively define for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\begin{split}\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}:=\inf\left\{t\geq\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n-1}:X^{\epsilon}_{t}=N_{0}+1\right\}\wedge\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}},\quad\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n}:=\inf\left\{t\geq\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}:X^{\epsilon}_{t}=N_{0}\right\}\wedge\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}.\end{split}

Clearly,
\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}=\inf\{t\geq\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n-1}:X^{\epsilon}_{t}=x_{*}\text{ or }N_{0}+1\}
and
\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}\uparrow\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}
as
n\to\infty
for
\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}
-a.e. Hence,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}]&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}]=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}-\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n-1}]+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n}-\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}].\end{split}

(7.10)

Thanks to
[
34
, Theorem 6.3.1]
and Lemma
7.1
,
p_{\epsilon}:=\mathbb{P}_{N_{0}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}}=N_{0}+1]
satisfies

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}p_{\epsilon}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{s_{\epsilon}(N_{0})-s_{\epsilon}(x_{*})}{s_{\epsilon}(N_{0}+1)-s_{\epsilon}(x_{*})}=\frac{s_{0}(N_{0})-s_{0}(x_{*})}{s_{0}(N_{0}+1)-s_{0}(x_{*})}\in(0,1).

(7.11)

For
n\geq 1
, we show

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}}=\beta]&=p^{n}_{\epsilon},\\
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}-\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n-1}]&=p^{n-1}_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}],\\
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n}-\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}]&=p^{n}_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}+1}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}].\end{split}

(7.12)

The first two equalities for
n=1
are obvious. Thanks to the strong Markov property and time-homogeneity of
X^{\epsilon}_{t}
, we find

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{1}-\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}]&=\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{1}-\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}|X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}}=x_{*}]\times\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}}=x_{*}]\\
&\quad+\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{1}-\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}|X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}}=N_{0}+1]\times\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}}=N_{0}+1]\\
&=\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{1}-\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}|X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}}=N_{0}+1]\times\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}}=N_{0}+1]=\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}+1}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}]p_{\epsilon}.\end{split}

Hence, (
7.12
) holds for
n=1
. Suppose it is true for
n=k-1
with
k\geq 2
. By the strong Markov property and time-homogeneity of
X^{\epsilon}_{t}
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{k}}=N_{0}+1]&=\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{k}}=N_{0}+1|X^{\epsilon}_{\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{k-1}}=N_{0}]\\
&\quad\times\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{k-1}}=N_{0}|X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{k-1}}=N_{0}+1]\times\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{k-1}}=N_{0}+1]=p_{\epsilon}^{k},\\
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{k}-\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{k-1}]&=\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}\left[\tau^{\epsilon}_{k}-\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{k-1}|X^{\epsilon}_{\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{k-1}}=N_{0}\right]\\
&\quad\times\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{k-1}}=N_{0}|X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{k-1}}=N_{0}+1]\times\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{k-1}}=N_{0}+1]\\
&=\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}\left[\tau^{\epsilon}_{k}-\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{k-1}|X^{\epsilon}_{\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{k-1}}=N_{0}\right]\times\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{k-1}}=N_{0}+1]=\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}]p_{\epsilon}^{k-1},\\
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{k}-\tau^{\epsilon}_{k}]&=\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}\left[\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{k}-\tau^{\epsilon}_{k}|X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{k}}=N_{0}+1\right]\times\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{k}}=N_{0}+1]=\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}+1}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}]p_{\epsilon}^{k}.\end{split}

Consequently, (
7.12
) holds for
n=k
and thus, holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
by induction.

Given (
7.12
), we see from (
7.10
) that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}]&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(p^{n-1}_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}]+p^{n}_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}+1}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}]\right)=\frac{1}{1-p_{\epsilon}}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}]+\frac{p_{\epsilon}}{1-p_{\epsilon}}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}+1}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}],\end{split}

which together with (
7.8
), (
7.9
) and (
7.11
) yields

\sup_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}]\leq\sup_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{N_{0}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}]<\infty
.
∎

We are ready to prove Theorem
D
.

Clearly, it suffices to prove the result for
\mathbb{E}_{x}^{\epsilon}[T_{0}^{\epsilon}]
for each
x\in(0,\infty)
.

Fix
x\in(0,\infty)
. Let
0<\delta\ll 1
(depending on
x
) and then take
\beta=\beta(\delta)
and
x_{*}=x_{*}(\delta)\in(0,\beta)
so that

. They are introduced at the beginning of this section. The strong Markov property and time-homogeneity of

then imply that

\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]=\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x}\left[\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x}\left[(T^{\epsilon}_{0}-\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}+\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}})\big|X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}}\right]\right]=\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]+\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}].

(7.13)

Since
\sup_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}]<\infty
by Lemma
7.3
, it suffices to study the asymptotic bounds of
\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]
.

We follow the same idea as in the proof of Lemma
7.3
. Let
X^{\epsilon}_{0}=x_{*}
,
\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{0}=0
and define recursively the following sequences of stopping times: before the first time
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
reaches
0
(i.e.,
T_{0}^{\epsilon}
), for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}
be the first time after
\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n-1}
at which
X^{\epsilon}_{t}
reaches
\beta
, and
\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n}
be the first time after
\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}
at which
X^{\epsilon}_{t}
reaches
x_{*}
; since
\mathbb{P}_{x_{*}}[T_{0}^{\epsilon}<\infty]=1
,
\tau_{n}^{\epsilon}
and
\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n}
are defined up to some random index
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
; let
\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}=\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n}=T^{\epsilon}_{x^{*}}
for all

. To be more specific, we recursively define for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\begin{split}\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}:=\inf\left\{t\geq\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n-1}:X^{\epsilon}_{t}=\beta\right\}\wedge T^{\epsilon}_{0},\quad\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n}:=\inf\left\{t\geq\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}:X^{\epsilon}_{t}=x_{*}\right\}\wedge T^{\epsilon}_{0}.\end{split}

Clearly,
\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}=\inf\{t\geq\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n-1}:X^{\epsilon}_{t}=0\text{ or }\beta\}
and
\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}\uparrow T^{\epsilon}_{0}
as
n\to\infty
for
\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}
-a.e. Hence,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}]=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}-\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n-1}]+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n}-\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}].\end{split}

(7.14)

Set
p_{\epsilon}:=\mathbb{P}_{x_{*}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}}=\beta]
. Following arguments as in the proof of Lemma
7.3
, we have for each
n\geq 1
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[X^{\epsilon}_{\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}}=\beta]=p^{n}_{\epsilon},\quad\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}-\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n-1}]=p^{n-1}_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\hat{\tau}^{\epsilon}_{n}-\tau^{\epsilon}_{n}]=p^{n}_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\beta}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}],\end{split}

This together with (
7.14
) yields

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(p^{n-1}_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}]+p^{n}_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\beta}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}]\right)=\frac{1}{1-p_{\epsilon}}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}]+\frac{p_{\epsilon}}{1-p_{\epsilon}}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\beta}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}].\end{split}

(7.15)

Case:
\Lambda_{0}<0
.
Thanks to Lemmas
7.1
,
7.2
and
7.3
, there are
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that
C_{1}|\ln\epsilon|\lesssim_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]\lesssim_{\epsilon}C_{2}|\ln\epsilon|
. From which and (
7.13
), the desired result follows.

Case:
\Lambda_{0}>0
.
We rewrite (
7.15
) as

\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]=\frac{1}{1-p_{\epsilon}}\left(\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}]+\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\beta}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}]\right)-\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\beta}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}].

(7.16)

By Lemmas
7.1
,
7.2
and
7.3
, there are positive constants
C_{3},C_{4},C_{5}
and
C_{6}
such that

C_{3}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{-}-1)}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\frac{1}{1-p_{\epsilon}}\lesssim_{\epsilon}C_{4}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{+}-1)},\,\,C_{5}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{1}]+\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{\beta}[\tau^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}]\lesssim_{\epsilon}C_{6}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{+}-\kappa_{-}+\delta)},

which together with (
7.16
) yield
C_{3}C_{5}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{-}-1)}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]\lesssim_{\epsilon}C_{4}C_{6}\epsilon^{-2(\kappa_{+}-1+\kappa_{+}-\kappa_{-}+\delta)}
.

We see from the definition of
\kappa_{+}
and
\kappa_{-}
in (
7.2
) that for any
0<\gamma\ll 1
, there exists
\delta>0
(and corresponding
x_{*}=x_{*}(\delta)
) so that

-(\kappa_{-}-1)\leq 1-(1-\gamma)\frac{2b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,1-(1+\gamma)\frac{2b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|}\leq-(\kappa_{+}-1+\kappa_{+}-\kappa_{-}+\delta),

leading to
C_{3}C_{5}\epsilon^{2-(1-\gamma)\frac{4b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|}}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\epsilon}_{x_{*}}[T^{\epsilon}_{0}]\lesssim_{\epsilon}C_{4}C_{6}\epsilon^{2-(1+\gamma)\frac{4b^{\prime}(0)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(0)|}}
. This together with (
7.13
) yields the result.

The proof is complete.
∎

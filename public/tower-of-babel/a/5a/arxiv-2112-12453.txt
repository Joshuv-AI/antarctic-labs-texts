Ergodic Optimization restricted on certain subsets of invariant measures
Wanshan Lin and Xueting Tian

Abstract
. In this article, we pay attention to transitive dynamical systems having the shadowing property and the entropy functions are upper semicontinuous. As for these dynamical systems, when we consider ergodic optimization restricted on the subset of invariant measures whose metric entropy are equal or greater than a given constant, we prove that for generic real continuous functions the ergodic optimization measure is unique, ergodic, full support and have metric entropy equal to the given constant. Similar results also hold for suspension flows over transitive subshift of finite type, C r ​ ( r ≥ 2 ) C^{r}(r\geq 2) -generic geometric Lorenz attractors and C 1 C^{1} -generic singular hyperbolic attractors.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Shadowing property

An infinite sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{+\infty}
of
X
is a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit
if
d(T(x_{n}),x_{n+1})<\delta
for each
n\leq\mathbb{N}
. We say that a dynamical system has the
shadowing property
if for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is
\delta>0
such that for any
\delta
-pseudo-orbit
\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{+\infty}
can be
\varepsilon
-traced
by a point
y\in X
, that is
d(T^{n}y,x_{n})<\varepsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.2.
Metric entropy

Given
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)
, suppose that
\xi=\{A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{k}\}
is a finite partition of
(X,\mathcal{B}(X),\mu)
. Denote
H_{\mu}(\xi):=-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\mu(A_{i})\log\mu(A_{i})
. If
\eta=\{B_{1},B_{2},\cdots,B_{r}\}
is also a finite partition, denote
\xi\vee\eta:=\{A_{i}\cap B_{j}\mid 1\leq i\leq k,1\leq j\leq r\}
and
\bigvee\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}T^{-i}\xi:=\xi\vee T^{-1}\xi\vee\cdots\vee T^{n-1}\xi
for any
n\geq 1
. Then the
metric entropy
of
\mu
is defined as

h_{\mu}(T):=\sup_{\xi}\lim_{n\to=\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu}(\bigvee\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}T^{-i}\xi).

And the affine function
\mu\mapsto h_{\mu}(T)
from
\mathcal{M}(X,T)
to
[0,+\infty)\cup\{+\infty\}
is said to be the
entropy function
.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose that
(X,T)
is a dynamical system, we say that
(X,T)
has entropy-dense property if for any
\varepsilon>0
, any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)
, there exists
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,T)
such that
\varrho(\mu,\nu)<\varepsilon
and
h_{\nu}(T)>h_{\mu}(T)-\varepsilon
.

2.3.
Topological entropy

Given
n\in\mathbb{N^{+}}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Define
d_{n}(x,y):=\max\limits_{0\leq i\leq n-1}d(T^{i}x,T^{i}y)
for any
x,y\in X
. A subset
E\subset X
is said to be
(n,\varepsilon)
-separated if for any
x\neq y\in E
,
d_{n}(x,y)>\varepsilon
. Define

s_{n}(\varepsilon):=\sup\{\#E\mid E\text{ is }(n,\varepsilon)\text{-separated}\}.

Then the
topological entropy
of
(X,T)
is defined as

h_{top}(T):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log s_{n}(\varepsilon).

The variational principle for topological entropy is

h_{top}(T)=\sup\limits_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)}h_{\mu}(T).

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose that
(X,T)
is a dynamical system, we say that
(X,T)
has intermediate entropy property if for any
0\leq c<h_{top}(T)
, there exists
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,T)
such that
h_{\mu}(T)=c
.

2.4.
Topological pressure

Given
\varphi\in C(X)
, denote
S_{n}\varphi(x):=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\varphi(T^{i}x)
for any
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N^{+}}
. Given
n\in\mathbb{N^{+}}
and
\varepsilon>0
, define

P_{n}(T,\varphi,\varepsilon):=\sup\left\{\sum_{x\in E}e^{S_{n}\varphi(x)}\mid E\text{ is }(n,\varepsilon)\text{-separated}\right\}.

Then the
topological pressure
of
(X,T)
is defined as

P(T,\varphi):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log P_{n}(T,\varphi,\varepsilon).

The variational principle for topological pressure is

P(T,\varphi)=\sup\limits_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)}\left\{h_{\mu}(T)+\int\varphi\mathrm{d\mu}\right\}.

Given

4.2.
Asymptotically additive sequences

Given
f\in C(X)
, denote
S_{n}f=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}f\circ T^{i}
. The sequence
(S_{n}f)_{n\geq 1}
is called
additive sequence
. Asymptotically additive sequences was first introduced in
[
15
]
. A sequence
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset C(X)
is said to be
asymptotically additive
if
\inf\limits_{f\in C(X)}\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|=0
. Denote
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, by
[
15
, Proposition A.1]
,
\mathcal{F}_{\ast}(\mu):=\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int f_{n}\mathrm{d\mu}
exists for any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)
and is said to be the
average Lyapunov exponent
of
\mathcal{F}
. We say that the sequence
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the
variational principle
if

P(T,\mathcal{F})=\sup\limits_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)}(\mathcal{F}_{\ast}(\mu)+h_{\mu}(T)).

Where,

P(T,\mathcal{F}):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sup\left\{\sum_{x\in E}e^{f_{n}(x)}\mid E\text{ is }(n,\varepsilon)\text{-separated}\right\},

is defined as the
topological pressure
of
\mathcal{F}
.

An important link between asymptotically additive sequences and additive sequences was showed in
[
11
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
11
, Theorem 1.2]

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a asymptotically additive sequence. Then there exists
f\in C(X)
such that

\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}-S_{n}f\|=0.

Moreover, it was shown in
[
11
, Section 3.1]
that
\mathcal{F}_{\ast}(\mu)=\int f\mathrm{d\mu}
and
P(T,\mathcal{F})=P(T,f)
. As a consequence,
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the variational principle.

Denote
ADS
the space of asymptotically additive sequences. Define a equivalence relation
\thicksim
on
ADS
by
\mathcal{F}_{1}=(f_{1,n})_{n\geq 1}\thicksim\mathcal{F}_{2}=(f_{2,n})_{n\geq 1}
if and only if
\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{1,n}-f_{2,n}\|=0
. For any
\mathcal{F}\in ADS

the equivalence class of
\mathcal{F}
. In particular, for any

, denote

. By Theorem
4.1
,
ADS/\thicksim=\{[f]\mid f\in C(X)\}
. The norm on

is defined by
\|[\mathcal{F}]\|_{\star}=\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\|f_{n}\|
for any

is a normed vector space.

To show that

and the equivalence relation

for any

[

Suppose that

Proof.

Given
\tilde{\mu}\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)
, there exists a unique
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)
such that
\mu_{\rho}=\tilde{\mu}
, by Abramov’s theorem,
h_{\tilde{\mu}}(\phi_{t})=h_{\mu_{\rho}}(\phi_{t})=h_{\mu}(T)/\int\rho\mathrm{d\mu}
. Given
0\leq c\leq h_{\tilde{\mu}}(\phi_{t})
, let
\tilde{c}=c\int\rho\mathrm{d\mu}
, then
0\leq\tilde{c}\leq h_{\mu}(T)
. Since
(X,T)
has refined entropy-dense property, there exists a sequence
\{{\mu}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{+\infty}
of
\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,T)
such that
\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}{\mu}_{n}={\mu}
and
\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}h_{{\mu}_{n}}(\phi_{t})=\tilde{c}
. Hence,
(({\mu}_{n})_{\rho})_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{M}^{e}(X_{\rho},\phi_{t})
,
\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}({\mu}_{n})_{\rho}={\mu_{\rho}}=\tilde{\mu}
,
\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}h_{({\mu}_{n})_{\rho}}(\phi_{t})=c
.
∎

Since
\mathcal{P}
is a homeomorphism. By Abramov’s theorem, when the entropy function of
(X,T)
is upper semicontinuous, we have the entropy function of
(X_{\rho},\phi_{t})
is upper semicontinuous. Similar, given
\psi\in C(X_{\rho})
, for any
\inf\limits_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X_{\rho},\phi_{t})}(h_{\mu}(\phi_{t})+\int\psi\mathrm{d\mu})\leq c<P(\phi_{t},\psi)
, denote
\Lambda_{c}:=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X_{\rho},\phi_{t})\mid h_{\mu}(\phi_{t})+\int\psi\mathrm{d\mu}\geq c\}
and
\Delta_{c}:=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X_{\rho},\phi_{t})\mid h_{\mu}(\phi_{t})+\int\psi\mathrm{d\mu}=c\}
. Then
\Lambda_{c}
is a nonempty and compact convex subset of
\mathcal{M}(X_{\rho},\phi_{t})
. By Lemma
3.1
,
\Lambda_{c}^{\star}
is residual in
\Lambda_{c}
. By Theorem
5.2
, we have

Theorem 5.3
.

Suppose that
(X,T)
is a dynamical system with refined entropy-dense property.
T
is a homeomorphism and entropy function is upper semicontinuous. Let
\rho:X\mapsto(0,+\infty)
be a continuous roof function. The flow
(\phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
X_{\rho}
is the suspension flow associate with
\rho
. Then for
\psi\in C(X_{\rho})
and
\sup\limits_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X_{\rho},\phi_{t})}\int\psi\mathrm{d\mu}\leq c<P(\phi_{t},\psi)
, there is a residual subset
\mathcal{R}
of
C(X_{\rho})
, such that for any
f\in\mathcal{R}
, there is a unique
\mu_{f}^{c}\in\mathcal{M}(X_{\rho},\phi_{t})
such that

(1)

\int f\mathrm{d}{\mu_{f}^{c}}=\sup\{\int f\mathrm{d\mu}\mid h_{\mu}(\phi_{t})+\int\psi\mathrm{d\mu}\geq c\}
;

(2)

\mu_{f}^{c}\in\mathcal{M}^{e}(X_{\rho},\phi_{t})
;

(3)

h_{\mu_{f}^{c}}(\phi_{t})+\int\psi\mathrm{d}\mu_{f}^{c}=c
.

Theorem
B
is directly from Theorem
5.3
.

Now, we will consider geometric Lorenz attractors and singular hyperbolic attractors. The Lorenz attractor wae observed by E. Lorenz
[
22
]
in 1963, whose dynamics sensitively dependent on initial conditions. Later, a geometric model for the Lorenz attractor was introduced by J. Guckenheimer
[
17
]
and V. S. Afraĭmovič, V. V. Bykov and L. P. Sil’nikov
[
2
]
, nowadays known as

[
38
, Definition 3.5]
. In the study of
C^{1}
robustly transitive flows, C. A. Morales, M. J. Pacifico and E. Pujals
[
25
]
introduced the concept of
singular hyperbolic flows

[
38
, Definition 3.2]
, which include the geometric Lorenz attractors as a special class of examples.

Lemma 5.2
.

[
38
, Corollary 4.14]

The following holds:

(1)

There exists a residual subset
\mathcal{A}^{r}\subset\mathscr{X}^{r}(M^{3})
where
r\geq 2
such that if
\Lambda
is a geometric Lorenz attractor of
X\in\mathcal{A}^{r}
, then
\Lambda
has entropy-dense property;

(2)

There exists a residual subset
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathscr{X}^{1}(M)
such that if
\Lambda
is a non-trivial singular hyperbolic attractor of
X\in\mathcal{A}
, then
\Lambda
has entropy-dense property.

Lemma 5.3
.

[
3
, Theorem A]

Suppose that
\Lambda
is a singular hyperbolic attractor of
X\in\mathscr{X}^{r}(M^{3})
. Then
\Lambda
is expansive.

Hence, if
\Lambda
is a geometric Lorenz attractor of
X\in\mathscr{X}^{r}(M^{3})
, then the entropy function of it is upper semicontinuous. This result also can be deduced from the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

[
34
, Theorem A]

Suppose that
\Lambda
is a compact invariant set that is sectional hyperbolic for a
C^{1}
flow
\phi_{t}
, with all the singularities in
\Lambda
hyperbolic. Then
\phi_{t}
is robustly
\delta
-entropy expansive near
\Lambda
.

Remark 5.1
.

Non-trivial singular hyperbolic attractors satisfy the assumption in this lemma. Hence, the entropy functions of non-trivial singular hyperbolic attractors are upper semicontinuous. See
[
34
]
for more details.

By Theorem
5.2
, to prove Theorem
C
and Theorem

, it is enough to show that
C^{r}(r\geq 2)
-generic geometric Lorenz attractors and
C^{1}
-generic singular hyperbolic attractors have refined entropy-dense property.

Let’s recall the definition of
horseshoe
.

Definition 5.1
.

Suppose that
\varphi_{t}:M\to M
is a
C^{1}
flow generated by
X\in\mathscr{X}^{1}(M)
. A compact
\varphi_{t}
-invariant set
\Lambda\subset X
is called a horseshoe of
\varphi_{t}
, if there exists a suspension flow
\phi_{t}:(\Sigma_{k})_{\rho}\to(\Sigma_{k})_{\rho}
with a continuous roof function
\rho
and a homeomorphism
\Pi:(\Sigma_{k})_{\rho}\to\Lambda
, such that

\varphi_{t}\circ\Pi=\Pi\circ\phi_{t}.

By Lemma
5.1
, horseshoes have refined entropy-dense property.

Given a compact and invariant set
\Lambda
of
X\in\mathscr{X}^{1}(M)
, denote
\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Lambda):=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{inv}(\Lambda)\mid\mu(\text{Sing}(\Lambda))=0\}
and
\mathcal{M}_{0}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{M}_{erg}(\Lambda)\cap\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Lambda)
, where
\text{Sing}(\Lambda)
is the set of singularities of
\Lambda
.

Lemma 5.5
.

[
38
, Lemma 4.10 and Remark 4.11]

Suppose that
X\in\mathscr{X}^{1}(M)
and
\Lambda
is a singular hyperbolic homoclinic class of
X
. Assume each pair of periodic orbits of
\Lambda
are homoclinic related and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(\Lambda)
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a horseshoe
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\subset\Lambda
such that
\varrho(\mu,\nu)<\varepsilon
for any
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{inv}(\Lambda_{\varepsilon})
and there exists
\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{erg}(\Lambda_{\varepsilon})
such that
h_{\nu_{0}}(X)>h_{\mu}(X)-\varepsilon
.

Remark 5.2
.

In
[
12
]
, S. Crovisier and D. Yang proved that for
C^{1}
-open and dense set of vector field
X\in\mathscr{X}^{1}(M)
, any singular hyperbolic attractor
\Lambda
is a robustly transitive attractor
[
12
, Theorem A]
. Moreover, if
\Lambda
is non-trivial, then it is a homoclinic class and any two periodic orbits contained in
\Lambda
are homoclinically related
[
12
, Theorem B]
. Therefore, combine with Lemma
5.2
, we have

(1)

There exists a residual subset
\mathcal{A}^{r}\subset\mathscr{X}^{r}(M^{3})
where
r\geq 2
such that if
\Lambda
is a geometric Lorenz attractor of
X\in\mathcal{A}^{r}
, then
\Lambda
has entropy-dense property and satisfies the assumptions in Lemma
5.5
;

(2)

There exists a residual subset
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathscr{X}^{1}(M)
such that if
\Lambda
is a non-trivial singular hyperbolic attractor of
X\in\mathcal{A}
, then
\Lambda
has entropy-dense property and satisfies the assumptions in Lemma
5.5
.

Hence, to prove Theorem
C
and Theorem
D
, it’s enough to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose that
X\in\mathscr{X}^{1}(M)
and
\Lambda
is a singular hyperbolic homoclinic class of
X
with entropy-dense property. Assume each pair of periodic orbits of
\Lambda
are homoclinic related and the entropy function of
\Lambda
is upper semicontinuous. Then
\Lambda
has refined entropy-dense property.

Proof.

When
h_{top}(\Lambda)=0
, since
\mathcal{M}_{inv}(\Lambda)
is entropy dense,
\Lambda
has refined entropy-dense property. Now suppose that
h_{top}(\Lambda)>0
. Suppose that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{inv}(\Lambda)
and
0\leq c\leq h_{\mu}(\Lambda)=h_{\mu}(X)
.

When
h_{\mu}(X)=0
, it is directly from that
\Lambda
has entropy-dense property and the entropy function of
\Lambda
is upper semicontinuous. Now, suppose that
h_{\mu}(X)>0
.

When
0\leq c<h_{\mu}(X)
, choose
\varepsilon>0
such that
h_{\mu}(X)-2\varepsilon>c
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{inv}(\Lambda)
is entropy dense, there exists
\mu_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}_{erg}(\Lambda)
such that
\varrho(\mu_{\varepsilon},\mu)<\varepsilon
and
h_{\mu_{\varepsilon}}(X)>h_{\mu}(X)-\varepsilon>0
. Hence,
\mu_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(\Lambda)
. By Lemma
5.5
, there exists a horseshoe
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\subset\Lambda
such that
\varrho(\mu_{\varepsilon},\nu)<\varepsilon
for any
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{inv}(\Lambda_{\varepsilon})
and there exists
\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{erg}(\Lambda_{\varepsilon})
such that
h_{\nu_{0}}(X)>h_{\mu_{\varepsilon}}(X)-\varepsilon>h_{\mu}(X)-2\varepsilon>c
. Since horseshoes have refined entropy-dense property, there exists
\nu_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}_{erg}(\Lambda_{\varepsilon})
such that
\varrho(\nu_{\varepsilon},\nu_{0})<\varepsilon
and
|h_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(X)-c|<\varepsilon
. Hence,
\nu_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}_{erg}(\Lambda)
,
\varrho(\nu_{\varepsilon},\mu)<3\varepsilon
and
|h_{\nu_{\varepsilon}}(X)-c|<\varepsilon
.

When
c=h_{\mu}(X)
, consider
c_{n}:=\frac{n}{n+1}h_{\mu}(X)
, then
0\leq c_{n}<h_{\mu}(X)
and
\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}c_{n}=c
.
∎

.

7.1.
Proof of Theorem
2.2

We will use the results of Theorem
2.3
to simplify the proof.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

When
\#\Lambda=1
,
\mathcal{U}^{\Lambda}_{\varSigma}=\Lambda
. Now, suppose that
\#\Lambda>1
and
\Lambda=\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\cdots,\mu_{k}\}
. Given
f\in\mathcal{U}^{\Lambda}_{\varSigma}
, suppose that
\mathcal{M}_{max}^{\Lambda}(f)=\{\mu_{l}\}
. Then,
\int f\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}<\int f\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}
for any
i\neq l
. Since
h\mapsto\int h\mathrm{d\mu}
is a continuous function on
\varSigma
for any
\mu\in\Lambda
, for any
i\neq l
, there is an open subset
U_{i}
of
\varSigma
such that
f\in U_{i}
and for any
g\in U_{i}
,
\int g\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}<\int g\mathrm{d}\mu_{l}
. Let
U=\bigcap\limits_{i\neq l}U_{i}
, then
f\in U\subset\mathcal{U}^{\Lambda}_{\varSigma}
. Hence,
\mathcal{U}^{\Lambda}_{\varSigma}
is open in
\varSigma
. By Theorem
2.3
,
\mathcal{U}^{\Lambda}_{\varSigma}
is open and dense in
\varSigma
.
∎

7.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.3

Suppose that
A
and
B
are two non-empty compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
, the
Hausdorff metric
is defined by
d_{H}(A,B):=\max\limits_{a\in A}\min\limits_{b\in B}|a-b|+\max\limits_{b\in B}\min\limits_{a\in A}|a-b|
. Given
f,g\in C(X)
, define
\beta^{\Lambda}(g\mid f):=\sup\limits_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{max}^{\Lambda}(f)}\int g\mathrm{d\mu}
and
\mathcal{M}_{max}^{\Lambda}(g\mid f):=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{max}^{\Lambda}(f)\mid\int g\mathrm{d\mu}=\beta^{\Lambda}(g\mid f)\right\}
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Given
f,g\in C(X)
, we have

\left\{\int g\mathrm{d\mu}\mid\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(f+\varepsilon g)\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\beta^{\Lambda}(g\mid f)\right\}\text{ as }\varepsilon\searrow 0,

in the sense of the Hausdorff metric.

Proof.

Given
f,g\in C(X)
. Since
\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(f+\varepsilon g)
is a nonempty and compact subset of
\mathcal{M}(X,T)
, the set
\left\{\int g\mathrm{d\mu}\mid\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(f+\varepsilon g)\right\}
is a nonempty compact subset of
\mathbb{R}
. To prove the lemma, it is enough to show that if
a_{\varepsilon}\in\left\{\int g\mathrm{d\mu}\mid\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(f+\varepsilon g)\right\}
, then
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}a_{\varepsilon}=\beta^{\Lambda}(g\mid f)
. Writing
a_{\varepsilon}=\int f\mathrm{d}m_{\varepsilon}
for some
m_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(f+\varepsilon g)
, it is in turn enough to prove that any limit point of
m_{\varepsilon}
, as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
, belongs to
\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(g\mid f)
.
Suppose that
\lim\limits_{i\to+\infty}m_{\varepsilon_{i}}=m
. By Lemma
3.2
, we have that
m\in\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(f)
. For any fixed
i\geq 1
, since
m_{\varepsilon_{i}}\in\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(f+\varepsilon g)
, we have

\int f\mathrm{d}m_{\varepsilon_{i}}+\varepsilon_{i}\int g\mathrm{d}m_{\varepsilon_{i}}\geq\int f\mathrm{d\mu}+\varepsilon_{i}\int g\mathrm{d}\mu,

for any
\mu\in\Lambda
. For any fixed
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(f)
, we have

\int f\mathrm{d\mu}\geq\int f\mathrm{d}m_{\varepsilon_{i}},

for any
i\geq 1
. Combining these two estimates, we obtain
\varepsilon_{i}\int g\mathrm{d}m_{\varepsilon_{i}}\geq\varepsilon_{i}\int g\mathrm{d}\mu
, i.e.
\int g\mathrm{d}m_{\varepsilon_{i}}\geq\int g\mathrm{d}\mu
for any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(f)
, any
i\geq 1
. Let
i\to+\infty
, then
\int g\mathrm{d}m\geq\int g\mathrm{d}\mu
for any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(f)
. Hence,
m\in\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(g\mid f)
.
∎

Let’s finish the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Since
\varSigma
is a topological vector space which is densely and continuously embedded in
C(X)
, there exists a countable subset of
\varSigma
which is dense in
C(X)
. Denote the subset by
\{\gamma_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N^{+}}\}
. For any
f\in\varSigma
, any
i\geq 1
, define

M^{\Lambda}_{i}(f):=\left\{\int\gamma_{i}\mathrm{d\mu}\mid\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(f)\right\}.

Then
A\in\mathcal{U}^{\Lambda}_{\varSigma}
if and only if
M^{\Lambda}_{i}(f)
is a singleton for any
i\geq 1
. Define

E^{\Lambda}_{i,j}:=\left\{f\in\varSigma\mid\operatorname{diam}(M^{\Lambda}_{i}(f))\geq\frac{1}{j}\right\},

where
i\geq 1
,
j\geq 1
. Then

\begin{split}\varSigma\setminus\mathcal{U}^{\Lambda}_{\varSigma}&=\left\{f\in\varSigma\mid\operatorname{diam}(M^{\Lambda}_{i}(f))>0\text{ for some }i\in\mathbb{N^{+}}\right\}\\
&=\bigcup_{i=1}^{+\infty}\bigcup_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left\{f\in\varSigma\mid\operatorname{diam}(M^{\Lambda}_{i}(f))\geq\frac{1}{j}\right\}\\
&=\bigcup_{i=1}^{+\infty}\bigcup_{j=1}^{+\infty}E^{\Lambda}_{i,j}.\end{split}

And

\mathcal{U}^{\Lambda}_{\varSigma}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{+\infty}\bigcap_{j=1}^{+\infty}(\varSigma\setminus E^{\Lambda}_{i,j}).

To prove Theorem
2.3
, it is enough to show that
E^{\Lambda}_{i,j}
is closed and has empty interior in
\varSigma
.

To show that
E^{\Lambda}_{i,j}
is closed in
\varSigma
, let
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{+\infty}\subset E^{\Lambda}_{i,j}
with
\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}f_{n}=f\in\varSigma
. Write
\operatorname{diam}(M^{\Lambda}_{i}(f_{n}))=\int\gamma_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}^{+}-\int\gamma_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}^{-}\geq\frac{1}{j}
for measures
\mu_{n}^{-},\mu_{n}^{+}\in\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(f_{n})
. Then there exists
\mu^{-},\mu^{+}\in\Lambda
such that
\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\mu_{r_{n}}^{-}=\mu^{-}
and
\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\mu_{s_{n}}^{+}=\mu^{+}
, where
r_{1}<r_{2}<\cdots,s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots
. By Lemma
3.2
,
\mu^{-},\mu^{+}\in\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(f)
. Since
\int\gamma_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu_{r_{n}}^{-}\to\int\gamma_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu^{-}
and
\int\gamma_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu_{s_{n}}^{+}\to\int\gamma_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu^{+}
, we have
\int\gamma_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu^{+}-\int\gamma_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu^{-}\geq\frac{1}{j}
. Hence,
f\in E^{\Lambda}_{i,j}
and
E^{\Lambda}_{i,j}
is closed.

To show that
E^{\Lambda}_{i,j}
has empty interior in
\varSigma
, let
f\in E^{\Lambda}_{i,j}
be arbitrary. Choose
g\in\Sigma
. By Lemma
7.1
,

\left\{\int g\mathrm{d\mu}\mid\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{\Lambda}_{max}(f+\varepsilon g)\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\beta^{\Lambda}(g\mid f)\right\}\text{ as }\varepsilon\searrow 0.

Hence,
\operatorname{diam}(M^{\Lambda}_{i}(f+\varepsilon g))<\frac{1}{j}
for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small. As a result, we have that
E^{\Lambda}_{i,j}
has empty interior in
\varSigma
.
∎

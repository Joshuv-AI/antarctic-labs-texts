On 1-bend Upward Point-set Embeddings of s ​ t st -digraphs
Emilio Di Giacomo, Henry Förster, Daria Kokhovich, Tamara Mchedlidze, Fabrizio Montecchiani, Antonios Symvonis, Anaïs Villedieu

Abstract
We study the upward point-set embeddability of digraphs on one-sided convex point sets with at most 1 bend per edge. We provide an algorithm to compute a 1-bend upward point-set embedding of outerplanar s ​ t st -digraphs on arbitrary one-sided convex point sets. We complement this result by proving that for every n ≥ 18 n\geq 18 there exists a 2 2 -outerplanar s ​ t st -digraph G G with n n vertices and a one-sided convex point set S S so that G G does not admit a 1-bend upward point-set embedding on S S .

A

Gritzmann et al.

9

and trees

An

For points in convex position Binucci et al.

The results about (U)PSEs with zero and two bends naturally motivates the study of (U)PSEs with one bend. Testing whether a (upward) planar graph admits a 1-bend (U)PSE is NP-complete in both the upward and the non-upward variants. (u,v)
and
(w,z)

A set of points

Lemma 1
(
\star

Let

Proof

of a subdivision

or completely inside

that is drawn completely outside

has necessarily at least one bend, as edges with no bends necessarily lie inside

. Thus, an edge of

We can define a 2UBE

are assigned to the top page. Since

is an upward drawing, the total order of

is a topological sorting. Further, in

, no two edges in the same page cross, as otherwise the same edges would cross in

is a subdivision of

is a 2UTBE of

1

If

We remove all edges that have a top sub-edge covering only one vertex and nest no edges inside. If after such a removal, some new edges with the same properties are created they are recursively removed, until no more edge exists with the same properties. The reason we remove these edges is to guarantee that, in the description that follows, we can assume that if a pair of edges satisfies Condition (a) of the definition of forbidden configuration, they also satisfy Condition (c), and therefore they do not satisfy Condition (b). The removed edges will be reinserted at the end in reverse order of removal using

Let

be the corresponding graph. We now compute a

on

. We first map each vertex

to the point

(

). Notice that by the choice of the additional points, the dummy vertices are mapped to the dummy points. We then draw the (sub-)edges that are in the bottom page as a straight-line segments inside the convex hull

Let

assigned to an end-vertex of

An edge of an embedded planar graph

otherwise. The

of

is a tree. If

is a path,

is an

. A

is an internally-triangulated outerpath whose inner edges all share an end-vertex.

An

-digraph
is a directed acyclic graph with a single source

and a single sink

; an

-outerplanar graph
(resp.

-outerpath

-digraph whose underlying undirected graph is an outerplanar graph (resp. an outerpath). An

-fan
is an

-digraph whose underlying graph is a fan and whose inner edges have

as an end-vertex. An

-outerplanar graph such that the edge

exists is

if

is an outer edge, it is

if

is an inner edge.

We recall a decomposition of

7
]

The

-outerpath

\overline{G}
. An

-outerpath

if and only if one of its extreme faces is incident to

and the other one to

In this section we utilize a tool, called

, that is another way to look at 2UTBEs.
An upward planar graph

-digraph

that has a directed Hamiltonian

More generally, there is an analogy between upward topological book embeddings and a more general form of subhamiltonicity. Let

be an upward planar graph and

be an embedded

-digraph such that: (1)

has a directed Hamiltonian

is crossed by at most one edge of

is a

of

is an

Lemma 7
(
[
30
]
)

An upward planar graph has a 2UTBE with at most one spine-crossing per edge if and only if it has an HP-completion.
The order of the vertices along the spine in the 2UTBE is the same as in the subhamiltonian path.

The subhamiltonian path crosses some edges of

by splitting them into

. We inherit the definition of nesting (sub-)edges from 2UTBE to HP-completion. Thus, the (sub-)edges
(u,v)
,

if in the embedding of

they are on the same side of the path

and

or

. Since the order of the vertices on the spine of the book and along the Hamiltonian path coincide, two (sub-)edges nest in

if and only if they nest in the corresponding 2UTBE. We now prove the key result of this section.

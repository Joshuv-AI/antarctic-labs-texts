1
Introduction

An
n
-fold Kuga variety, which we will refer to as a Kuga variety, is a variety over a Siegel modular variety such that each fibre is a product of
n
copies of the abelian variety or Kummer variety to which it corresponds in the base. In this paper, we study the Kodaira dimension of
n
-fold Kuga varieties,
\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p}
, over the moduli spaces
\mathcal{A}_{p}
of

(1,p)
-polarised abelian surfaces with canonical level structure for prime
p\geq 3
.

There are results about the Kodaira dimensions of some special kinds of Kuga varieties:
[
V
]
,
[
FV
]
have proved the unirationality of Kuga families, i.e. the
1
-fold Kuga varieties, over moduli spaces of principally polarised abelian varieties of dimension
4
and
5
; in
[
PSMS
]
, we computed the Kodaira dimension of any Kuga variety over moduli spaces of principally polarised abelian varieties of dimension
g\geq 2
. It is therefore natural to ask for the Kodaira dimension of Kuga varieties of other kinds, for example, the
\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p}
described above.

A connection between modular forms and differential forms on arbitrary Kuga varieties is established in
[
M
]
. This gives us some information about the Kodaira dimensions, assuming a specific compactification for the Kuga varieties, which is referred to as a Namikawa
compactification in
[
PSMS
]
.
Specifically,
[

, Theorem 1.3]
translates to:

Theorem 1
.

Let
X
be a Namikawa compactification of
\mathcal{X}_{p}^{n}
. Then

\kappa(\overline{\mathcal{A}_{p}},(n+3)\mathcal{L}-\Delta_{\mathcal{A}})\leq\kappa(K_{X})\leq 3

where
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{p}}
is a toroidal compactification of
\mathcal{A}_{p}
,
\mathcal{L}
is the
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle of weight
1
modular forms of

\Gamma_{p}
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}
is the boundary divisor of

\overline{\mathcal{A}_{p}}
.

The Kodaira dimension is a birational invariant by definition, so

\kappa(\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p})=\kappa(X)
. Moreover, if
X
has canonical singularities,
then
\kappa(X)=\kappa(K_{X})
, and hence

\kappa((n+3)\mathcal{L}-\Delta_{\mathcal{A}})
is a lower bound for

\kappa(\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p})
.

We say a Kuga variety

\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p}
is of

if its Kodaira
dimension equals the dimension of the base
\mathcal{A}_{p}
of
\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p}
,
namely

, which is also the maximum value

This paper is divided into two parts: in Section

A Kuga variety

•

p\geq 5

1.1
Construction of Kuga varieties

The moduli space
\mathcal{A}_{p}
of
(1,p)
-polarised abelian surfaces with canonical level structure, defined and studied in
[
HKW1
, Chapter I.1]
, is given as the quotient of the Siegel upper half plane
\mathbb{H}_{2}
by the action of a certain arithmetic subgroup
\Gamma_{p}
of
\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}(4,\mathbb{Z})
.
Recall the Siegel upper half space of degree
g
is defined as

\mathbb{H}_{g}=\left\{\tau\in M_{g\times g}(\mathbb{C}):\tau=\prescript{t}{}{\tau},\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\tau>0\right\}.

Note that for any integer
k\geq 2
, the Grassmannian

\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}(2,\mathbb{C}^{k})

is isomorphic to the orbit space
M_{k\times 2}(\mathbb{C})/\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}(2,\mathbb{C})

of all
k\times 2
matrices modulo right multiplication by the invertible matrices in

\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}(2,\mathbb{C})
. So the Siegel upper half plane
\mathbb{H}_{2}
can be identified with a subset
of
\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}(2,\mathbb{C}^{4})
by sending an element
\tau
to the

\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}(2,\mathbb{C})
-equivalence class of block matrices:

\tau\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}\tau\\
{\mathbf{1}}_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

For any prime
p\geq 3
, we define

\Gamma_{p}=\left\{\gamma\in\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}(4,\mathbb{Z}):\gamma-{\mathbf{1}}\in\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}&p\mathbb{Z}\\
p\mathbb{Z}&p\mathbb{Z}&p\mathbb{Z}&p^{2}\mathbb{Z}\\
\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}&p\mathbb{Z}\\
\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}&p\mathbb{Z}\\
\end{pmatrix}\right\},

which is of finite index in
\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}(4,\mathbb{Z})
.
This group
\Gamma_{p}
acts on
\mathbb{H}_{2}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}(2,\mathbb{C}^{4})
by left multiplication on the GL
(2,\mathbb{C})
-equivalence classes of block
matrices, which is an analogue of linear fractional transformations:

\gamma\cdot\tau=\begin{bmatrix}\gamma\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\tau\\
{\mathbf{1}}_{2}\end{pmatrix}\end{bmatrix},\quad\gamma\in\Gamma_{p},\tau\in\mathbb{H}_{g}.

The quotient of
\mathbb{H}_{2}
by this action of
\Gamma_{p}
gives the moduli space

\mathcal{A}_{p}
.

In
[
HKW1
, Chapter I.2B]
, a
1
-fold Kuga variety
\mathcal{X}^{1}_{p}
over
\mathcal{A}_{p}
is constructed. This method can be extended to construct an
n
-fold Kuga variety
\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p}
over
\mathcal{A}_{p}
for any positive integer
n
by defining an extension
\tilde{\Gamma}^{n}_{p}
of
\Gamma_{p}
and a left action of it on
\mathbb{C}^{2n}\times\mathbb{H}_{2}
which descends to that of
\Gamma_{p}
on
\mathbb{H}_{2}
. First, by identifying

\mathbb{C}^{2n}
with the set of
n\times 2
complex matrices, we can
identify
\mathbb{C}^{2n}\times\mathbb{H}_{2}
with a subset of
\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}(2,\mathbb{C}^{n+4})
by sending an element
(Z,\tau)
to a
\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}(2,\mathbb{C})
-equivalence class of block matrices:

(Z,\tau)\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}Z\\
\tau\\
{\mathbf{1}}_{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

We define the following group

\tilde{\Gamma}_{p}^{n}=\left\{(l,\gamma)=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{1}}_{n}&l\\
0&\gamma\end{pmatrix}\in\text{M}_{n\times 4}(\mathbb{Z})\rtimes\Gamma_{p}:\begin{matrix}\gamma\in\Gamma_{p}\end{matrix}\right\}.

The group
\tilde{\Gamma}_{p}^{n}
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{H}_{2}
by
left multiplication on the
\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}(2,\mathbb{C})
-equivalence classes of block
matrices. Explicitly, for
\tilde{\gamma}=(l,\gamma)\in\tilde{\Gamma}^{n}_{p}
and
\tilde{\tau}=(Z,\tau)\in\mathbb{C}^{2n}\times\mathbb{H}_{2}
, then

\tilde{\gamma}\cdot\tilde{\tau}=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{1}}_{n}&l\\
0&\gamma\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
\tau\\
{\mathbf{1}}_{2}\end{pmatrix}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}Z+l\cdot\tau\\
\gamma\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\tau\\
{\mathbf{1}}_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}(Z+l\cdot\tau)\cdot N\\
\gamma\cdot\tau\\
{\mathbf{1}}_{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}

(
*
)

for some
N\in\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}(2,\mathbb{C})
.

The quotient of
(\mathbb{C}^{2n}\times\mathbb{H}_{2})
by this action of
\tilde{\Gamma}^{n}_{p}
gives the Kuga variety
\mathcal{X}_{p}^{n}
. The projection
\mathbb{C}^{2n}\times\mathbb{H}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}_{2}
induces a map
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{p}
. Indeed
[
PSMS
]

[
N
]
, all fibres are the product of
n
copies of the torus parametrised by the corresponding point in the base
\mathcal{A}_{p}
, up to a change of basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
for each copy.

2.1
Namikawa compactification

The explicit construction of a Namikawa compactification
X
of the Kuga variety
\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p}
is the main purpose of section 1 in
[

]
. Here we briefly introduce its idea and some notations to be used in the latter sections.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Namikawa compactification
of
\mathcal{X}_{p}^{n}
is an irreducible
normal projective variety
X
containing
\mathcal{X}_{p}^{n}
as an open
subset, together with a projective toroidal compactification

\overline{\mathcal{A}_{p}}
of
\mathcal{A}_{p}
for which the following conditions hold.

1.

\pi\colon\mathcal{X}_{p}^{n}\to\mathcal{A}_{p}
extends to a projective morphism

\overline{\pi}\colon X\to\overline{\mathcal{A}_{p}}
;

2.

every irreducible component of

\Delta_{X}:=X\smallsetminus\mathcal{X}_{p}^{n}
dominates an
irreducible component of

\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}:=\overline{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\smallsetminus\mathcal{A}_{p}
.

Therefore
X
sits inside the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{p}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathcal{A}_{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\pi}}

and
\overline{\pi}
does not contract any divisor.

Namikawa compactifications are constructed by toroidal methods in
[
N
]
. We now describe the partial compactification at an integral boundary component
\tilde{F}
of rank

of
\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p}
which leads to a Namikawa compactification. The boundary component
\tilde{F}
can be written as
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times F
, an extension of a rank
g^{\prime}
boundary component

of
\mathcal{A}_{p}
, where
F
corresponds to a rank
g^{\prime\prime}:=g-g^{\prime}
isotropic sublattice of
\mathbb{Z}^{4}
defined up to the transitive action of
\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}(4,\mathbb{Z})
. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(\tilde{F})
be the stabiliser subgroup of
\tilde{F}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4n}\rtimes\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}(4,\mathbb{R})
, which can be embedded in
\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}(n+4,\mathbb{R})
. We also define the following subgroups of
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(\tilde{F})
:

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}(\tilde{F})

\displaystyle:=\text{ Centre of the unipotent radical of }\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(\tilde{F})

\displaystyle\tilde{\Upsilon}^{n}

\displaystyle:=\tilde{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}(\tilde{F})\cap\tilde{\Gamma}^{n}_{p}

\displaystyle\tilde{P^{\prime\prime}}(\tilde{F})

\displaystyle:=\big(\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(\tilde{F})\cap\tilde{\Gamma}^{n}_{p}\big)/\tilde{\Upsilon}^{n}

An explicit expression of the above matrix groups can be found in
[
N
, Section 2]
and
[

, Section 1]
. Also note that both
\tilde{\Upsilon}^{n}
and
\tilde{P^{\prime\prime}}(\tilde{F})
inherit the

\tilde{\Gamma}^{n}_{p}
on
\mathbb{C}^{2n}\times\mathbb{H}_{2}

Consider the Siegel domain realisation of
\mathbb{H}_{2}
, which gives
\mathbb{C}^{2n}\times\mathbb{H}_{2}
as an open subset of

(\mathbb{C}^{g^{\prime}n}\times\mathbb{C}^{g^{\prime\prime}n})\times(\mathbb{H}_{g^{\prime}}\times M_{g^{\prime}\times g^{\prime\prime}}(\mathbb{C})\times M_{g^{\prime\prime}\times g^{\prime\prime}}^{sym}(\mathbb{C})).

Then taking the partial quotient by
\tilde{\Upsilon}^{n}
near the boundary
component
\tilde{F}
corresponds to translations in the

\mathbb{C}^{g^{\prime\prime}n}
and

Using a suitable cone decomposition

, we can extend

\tilde{P^{\prime\prime}}(\tilde{F})

\mathop{\mathrm{Temb}}(\Sigma(\tilde{F}))

\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p}
at the boundary component

\tilde{F}
is then given as the quotient of (an open subset of) the torus bundle

2.2
The general strategy

We will separately examine the singularities in the interior and the boundary of
X
, and check if they are canonical by applying the Reid–Shepherd-Barron–Tai (RST) criterion.

We will need the following set up to state the RST criterion
[
R
]
:
Suppose
G
is a finite group acting on the complex vector space
\mathbb{C}^{m}
linearly. For a non-trivial element

\gamma\in G
of order
k
, the eigenvalues of
its action on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
can be expressed as an
m
-tuple
(\xi^{\alpha_{1}},\cdots,\xi^{\alpha_{m}})
, with
\xi
being a primitive
k
-th root of unity and
\alpha_{j}
being a non-negative integer less than
k
for any
j
. We define, with dependence on the choice of
\xi
, the
type
of
\gamma
to be

\frac{1}{k}(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m})

and its associated
RST sum
to be

\mathop{\mathrm{RST}}(\gamma):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{k}.

Furthermore, we say that
\gamma
is a
quasi-reflection
if all but one
\alpha_{j}
’s are

0
, or equivalently
\gamma
preserves a divisor.

The RST criterion is then given by the following:

Theorem 3

(
[
R
, 4.11]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group which acts on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
as above. Then
\mathbb{C}^{m}/G
has a canonical singularity if
G
contains no quasi-reflection, and if every non-trivial element
\gamma\in G
satisfies the inequality

\mathop{\mathrm{RST}}(\gamma)\geq 1.

Note that, since we need to check the above inequality involving the RST sum for every element in
G
, it does not matter which root of unity
\xi
was chosen to give the type of a generator
\gamma
of
G
.

3
Low weight cusp form trick

In this section, we will prove the following theorem:

Theorem 5
.

The equality
\kappa(\overline{\mathcal{A}_{p}},(n+3)L-\Delta_{\mathcal{A}})=3
is
satisfied for the following values of
n
and
p
:

•

p\geq 3
and
n\geq 4
;

•

p\geq 5
and
n\geq 3
.

To find a lower bound for
\kappa((n+3)\mathcal{L}-\Delta_{\mathcal{A}})
, which is
the rate of growth with respect to
m
of the dimension of the space
of weight
m(n+3)
-cusp forms of
\Gamma_{p}
, we use the “low weight
cusp form trick”, which has been used in this context in
[
GH
]
and
[
GS
]
, and more widely thereafter.

Suppose
n>N
and there exists a non-zero weight
3+N
cusp form
F

of
\Gamma_{p}
, that is,
F\in H^{0}((3+N)\mathcal{L}-\Delta_{A})
. For any non-zero

F^{\prime}\in H^{0}(m(n-N)\mathcal{L})
,

F^{m}F^{\prime}\in H^{0}(m((n+3)\mathcal{L}-\Delta_{A})).

Fixing
F
, the space of cusp forms in the form of
F^{m}F^{\prime}
then grows
at the same rate as
H^{0}(m(n-N)\mathcal{L})
with respect to
m
, which is known
to be
\mathcal{O}(m^{3})
. So
\kappa((n+3)\mathcal{L}-\Delta_{A})\geq 3
.

Therefore,
\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p}
is of relative general type if

h:=\dim H^{0}((3+N)\mathcal{L}-\Delta_{A})>0.

To find a lower bound for
h
,
we apply Gritsenko’s lifting of Jacobi cusp forms mentioned in
[
G
, Theorem 3]
, which states the existence of an injective lifting

J^{cusp}(k,p)\hookrightarrow S_{k}(\Gamma[p])

where
J^{cusp}(k,p)
is the space of Jacobi cusp forms of weight
k

and index
p\geq 1
, and
S_{k}(\Gamma[p])
is the space of weight

k
cusps forms of
\Gamma[p]
, with the paramodular group
\Gamma[p]

defining the moduli space of
(1,p)
-polarised abelian surfaces
without level structure as
\Gamma[p]\backslash\mathbb{H}_{2}
. But since

\Gamma_{p}\leq\Gamma[p]
, the image of the lifting is also contained
in
S_{k}(\Gamma_{p})
.

From
[
EZ
]
, dim
J^{cusp}(k,p)\geq j(k,p)
(equality holds when

k\geq p
), where

j(k,p):=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{t}\left(\dim M_{k+2j}-\left(\left\lfloor\frac{j^{2}}{4p}\right\rfloor+1\right)\right),&\text{if }k\text{ is even}\\
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{t-1}\left(\dim M_{k+2j-1}-\left(\left\lfloor\frac{j^{2}}{4p}\right\rfloor+1\right)\right),&\text{if }k\text{ is odd}\end{cases}

with
M_{r}
being the space of modular forms of weight
r
for
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.

It is a general fact that

\dim M_{r}=\begin{cases}\left\lfloor\frac{r}{12}\right\rfloor,&\text{if }r\equiv 2\mod 12\\
\left\lfloor\frac{r}{12}\right\rfloor+1,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

By a simple computation, it can be found that the first prime
p
such
that
j(k,p)>0
for
k=5
and
6
are
p=5
and
3

respectively. Note:

1.

\dim(S_{k}(\Gamma_{p}))\geq j(k,p)
for any
k,p
;

2.

j(k,p)
increases with
p
;

3.

the isomorphism in
[
M
, Theorem 1.1]

\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}(\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p},K_{\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p}}^{\otimes m})=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}M_{(n+3)m}(\Gamma_{p})

establishes that
\kappa(\mathcal{X}^{n}_{p})
is non-decreasing with
respect to
n
, because the same is true for
dim
(M_{(n+3)m}(\Gamma_{p}))
.

By letting
k=3+N=2+n
, this shows that for the values of
n
and

p
stated in Theorem
5
,
\dim(S_{k}(\Gamma_{p}))\geq j(k,p)\geq 1
. This concludes our proof for Theorem
5
.

, and to draw a boundary

, non-prime

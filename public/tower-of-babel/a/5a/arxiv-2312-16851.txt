On the different Floquet Hamiltonians in a periodic-driven Bose-Josephson junction
Xiaoshui Lin, Zeyu Rao, Ming Gong

Abstract
The bosonic Josephson junction, one of the maximally simple models for periodic-driven many-body systems, has been intensively studied in the past two decades. Here, we revisit this problem with five different methods, all of which have solid theoretical reasoning. We find that to the order of ω − 2 \omega^{-2} ( ω \omega is the modulating frequency), these approaches will yield slightly different Floquet Hamiltonians. In particular, the parameters in the Floquet Hamiltonians may be unchanged, increased, or decreased, depending on the approximations used. Especially, some of the methods generate new interactions, which still preserve the total number of particles; and the others do not. The validity of these five effective models is verified using dynamics of population imbalance and self-trapping phase transition. In all results, we find the method by first performing a unitary rotation to the Hamiltonian will have the highest accuracy. The difference between them will become significate when the modulating frequency is comparable with the driving amplitude. The results presented in this work indicate that the analysis of the Floquet Hamiltonian has some kind of subjectivity, which will become an important issue in future experiments with the increasing of precision. We demonstrate this physics using a Bose-Josephson junction, and it is to be hoped that the validity of these methods and their tiny differences put forward in this work can be verified in realistic experiments in future using quantum simulating platforms, including but not limited to ultracold atoms.

II
Floquet analysis based on quantum model

In the first two methods, we employ Floquet analysis for the quantum Hamiltonian in Eq.
6
and Eq.
7
, which we term as method Q-I and method Q-II.
Then we apply the mean field approximation by replacing all operators with complex numbers.
From the Floquet theorem
Rahav et al. 2003
, the Floquet eigenstates and eigenvalues can be solved by the following equation

(\hat{H}-i\partial_{t})|u_{n}\rangle=\epsilon_{n}|u_{n}\rangle,

(10)

with
|u_{n}(t)\rangle=|u(t+T)\rangle
.
Thus, it is expected that there should exist a family of time-independent Floquet Hamiltonians
\hat{H}_{\text{F}}
, which has the same eigenstates and eigenvalues with
\hat{H}-i\partial_{t}
.
This Hamiltonian may be obtained from the unitary
\hat{U}=e^{i\hat{K}(t)}

\hat{G}=\hat{U}\hat{H}\hat{U}^{\dagger}-i\hat{U}\partial_{t}\hat{U}^{\dagger}.

(11)

Once the transformation
\hat{U}
makes
\hat{G}

\hat{H}_{\text{F}}=\hat{G}
exactly.
In practice, we assume
\hat{K}(t)
and
\hat{H}_{\text{F}}
can be expanded in powers of
\omega^{-1}

;
Goldman and Dalibard 2014
;
Goldman et al. 2014
and the resulting Floquet Hamiltonian up to the order of
\omega^{-2}
can be written as

\hat{H}_{\text{F }}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{\text{F}}^{(1)}+\hat{H}_{\text{F}}^{(2)}+\mathcal{O}(\omega^{-2}),

(12)

with

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{F}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\omega j}[\hat{H}_{j},\hat{H}_{-j}],

(13)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{F}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2(\omega j)^{2}}([[\hat{H}_{j},\hat{H}_{0}],\hat{H}_{-j}]+\text{H.c.})+\sum_{j,l=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3\omega^{2}jl}

\displaystyle([\hat{H}_{j},[\hat{H}_{l},\hat{H}_{-j-l}]]-[\hat{H}_{j},[\hat{H}_{-l},\hat{H}_{l-j}]]+\text{H.c.}),

(14)

\nu(t)=\nu e^{i\frac{2A}{\omega}\sin(\omega t )}
, the equation captures the dynamics of Eq.
7
.
In the following, we use multiple scale analysis to derivate the Floquet Hamiltonian and term the two methods as methods C-I and C-II.
In the last part of this section, we have also presented a more simple approach for the Floquet Hamiltonian, termed method C-III.
A general treatment of this approach can be found in the mechanics textbook
Landau and Lifshitz 1976
.

III.1
Method C-I

Following the approach developed by Rahav
et. al

Rahav et al. 2003
, we separate the dynamic variables into slow and fast modes as

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=a_{0}+\xi_{a}=a_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\omega^{j}}\xi_{a,j},

(34)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=b_{0}+\xi_{b}=b_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\omega^{j}}\xi_{b,j},

(35)

with
\xi_{a}
and
\xi_{b}
(neglecting the
j
index) being time-dependent as a function of
\tau=\omega t
for fast modes,
a_{0}
and
b_{0}
for the slow modes, respectively.
Obviously,
\xi_{a}
and
\xi_{b}
also depend on
a_{0}
and
b_{0}
; see discussion of Karpitz inverted pendulum in Chap 5 of Ref.
Landau and Lifshitz 1976
. Recently, a quantum version Karpitz pendulum was realized with ultracold atoms in Ref.
Jiang et al. 2023
.
We can assume that
\int_{0}^{T}dt\xi_{a}=\int_{0}^{T}dt\xi_{b}=0
, with
T=2\pi/\omega
the period. In this way, all the fast oscillating terms are averaged out, leaving only the slow variation terms for the effective dynamics. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{d\xi_{a}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\omega\frac{\partial\xi_{a}}{\partial\tau}+\frac{\partial\xi_{a}}{\partial a_{0}}\dot{a}_{0}+\frac{\partial\xi_{a}}{\partial b_{0}}\dot{b}_{0}+\frac{\partial\xi_{a}}{\partial a_{0}^{*}}\dot{a}_{0}^{*}+\frac{\partial\xi_{a}}{\partial b_{0}^{*}}\dot{b}_{0}^{*},

(36)

\displaystyle\frac{d\xi_{b}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\omega\frac{\partial\xi_{b}}{\partial\tau}+\frac{\partial\xi_{b}}{\partial a_{0}}\dot{a}_{0}+\frac{\partial\xi_{b}}{\partial b_{0}}\dot{b}_{0}+\frac{\partial\xi_{b}}{\partial a_{0}^{*}}\dot{a}_{0}^{*}+\frac{\partial\xi_{b}}{\partial b_{0}^{*}}\dot{b}_{0}^{*}.

(37)

The complete conjugate of these results can give the equation of motion of
\xi_{a}^{*}
and
\xi_{b}^{*}
. Then we can solve the nonlinear equation in Eq.
33
according to the order of
\omega^{-1}
.
In this method, we choose
\gamma(t)=\Delta+A\cos(\omega t )
and
\nu(t)=\nu
.
In practice, we gather all the terms according to
\omega^{-n}
and then choose the solution of
\xi_{n+1}
to cancel the dependence of
\tau
.
The constant terms in the next order
\omega^{-(n+1)}
give rise to the effective contribution with order
\omega^{-(n+1)}
for the slow part
a_{0}
and
b_{0}
.

(1)
Zero order
. We have the following equations

\displaystyle i(\frac{da_{0}}{dt}+\frac{\partial\xi_{a,1}}{\partial\tau})=

\displaystyle\gamma(\tau)a_{0}+\lambda za_{0}-\nu b_{0},

(38)

\displaystyle i(\frac{db_{0}}{dt}+\frac{\partial\xi_{b,1}}{\partial\tau})=

\displaystyle-\gamma(\tau)b_{0}-\lambda zb_{0}-\nu a_{0},

(39)

with
z=|a_{0}|^{2}-|b_{0}|^{2}
.
To cancel the dependence of
\tau
, we chose

i\frac{\partial\xi_{a,1}}{\partial\tau}=A\cos(\tau)a_{0}
and
i\frac{\partial\xi_{b,1}}{\partial\tau}=-A\cos(\tau)b_{0}
, which yield the solutions

\displaystyle\xi_{a,1}(\tau)=-iA\sin(\tau)a_{0},\quad\xi_{b,1}(\tau)=iA\sin(\tau)b_{0}.

(40)

Substitute these solutions back to Eqs.
38
and
39
, we got the zero-order effective equation for
a_{0}
and
b_{0}
, which consistent with time-averaged mean field Hamiltonian

(2)
First order
.
The equations in this order are

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\xi_{a,2}}{\partial\tau}+i\dot{a}_{0}\frac{\partial\xi_{a,1}}{\partial a_{0}}=(\gamma(\tau)+2\lambda|a_{0}|^{2}-\lambda|b_{0}|^{2})\xi_{a,1}

\displaystyle-(\nu+\lambda b_{0}^{*}a_{0})\xi_{b,1}+\lambda(a_{0})^{2}\xi_{a,1}^{*}-\lambda a_{0}b_{0}\xi_{b,1}^{*},

(41)

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\xi_{b,2}}{\partial\tau}+i\dot{b}_{0}\frac{\partial\xi_{b,1}}{\partial b_{0}}=-(\gamma(\tau)+\lambda|a_{0}|^{2}-2\lambda|b_{0}|^{2})\xi_{b,1}

\displaystyle-(\nu+\lambda a_{0}^{*}b_{0})\xi_{a,1}+\lambda(b_{0})^{2}\xi_{b,1}^{*}-\lambda a_{0}b_{0}\xi_{a,1}^{*}.

(42)

It can be found that the solution in this order should be

\displaystyle\xi_{a,2}(\tau)

\displaystyle=2A\nu\cos(\tau)b_{0}-\frac{A^{2}}{4}\cos(2\tau)a_{0},

(43)

\displaystyle\xi_{b,2}(\tau)

\displaystyle=-2A\nu\cos(\tau)a_{0}+\frac{A^{2}}{4}\cos(2\tau)b_{0}.

(44)

In this order, we can not find any constant terms for
a_{0}
,
b_{0}
, and their conjugate.
Thus, we find
H_{\text{F}}^{(1)}=0
.

(3)
Second order
.
In this order, the equations are much longer than the previous equations.
Thus, we focus on the equation for
a
, while the derivation for
b
is also straightforward.
The equation for
a
reads as

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\xi_{a,3}}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle+i\dot{a}_{0}\frac{\partial\xi_{a,2}}{\partial a_{0}}+i\dot{b}_{0}\frac{\partial\xi_{a,2}}{\partial b_{0}}=

\displaystyle(\gamma(\tau)+2\lambda|a_{0}|^{2}-\lambda|b_{0}|^{2})\xi_{a,2}-(\nu+\lambda b_{0}^{*}a_{0})\xi_{b,2}

\displaystyle+\lambda(a_{0})^{2}\xi_{a,2}^{*}-\lambda a_{0}b_{0}\xi_{b,2}^{*}+\lambda a_{0}^{*}\xi_{a,1}^{2}+2\lambda a_{0}|\xi_{a,1}|^{2}

\displaystyle-\lambda b_{0}^{*}\xi_{b,1}\xi_{a,1}-\lambda b_{0}\xi_{b,1}^{*}\xi_{a,1}-\lambda a_{0}\xi_{b,1}^{*}\xi_{b,1}.

(45)

Gathering all the constant terms, we find the whole effective equation of motion for
a_{0}
and
b_{0}
up to the order of
\omega^{-2}
is

\displaystyle i\frac{da_{0}}{dt}=\Delta a_{0}+\lambda^{\prime}(|a_{0}|^{2}-|b_{0}|^{2})a_{0}-\nu^{\prime}b_{0},

(46)

\displaystyle i\frac{db_{0}}{dt}=-\Delta b_{0}-\lambda^{\prime}(|a_{0}|^{2}-|b_{0}|^{2})b_{0}-\nu^{\prime}a_{0},

(47)

with modified parameters in the Floquet Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta,

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}=(1+\frac{A^{2}}{2\omega^{2}})\lambda,

(48)

\displaystyle V_{\text{new}}=0,

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}=(1-\frac{A^{2}}{\omega^{2}})\nu.

Similar to the results we obtained in Sec.
II.1
, we find the periodic driving suppresses the coupling strength.
However, it also enhances the interaction strength
\lambda
.
Therefore, with this Floquet Hamiltonian, it is expected that the criterion for MSTP become

\lambda_{c}=4\nu\frac{\omega^{2}-A^{2}}{2\omega^{2}+A^{2}},

(49)

when
\Delta=0
,
a(0)=1
, and
b(0)=0
. When
|A/\omega|\ll 1
, we have
\lambda_{c}=2\nu(1-3A^{2}/2\omega^{2})
.

III.2
Method C-II

Here, we choose
\gamma(t)=\Delta
and
\nu(t)=\nu e^{i\frac{2A}{\omega}\sin(\omega t )}
.
Then, we employ a similar method that we have used in Sec.
III.1
.
we can obtain the effective equations for
a_{0}
,
b_{0}
,
a_{0}^{*}
, and
b_{0}^{*}
order by order.

(1)
Zero order
. The effective equations in this order are

\displaystyle i\frac{da_{0}}{dt}+i\frac{\partial\xi_{a,1}}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=\Delta a_{0}+\lambda za_{0}-\nu J_{0}b_{0}-\mathcal{B}(\tau)b_{0},

(50)

\displaystyle i\frac{db_{0}}{dt}+i\frac{\partial\xi_{b,1}}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=-\Delta b_{0}-\lambda zb_{0}-\nu J_{0}a_{0}-\mathcal{B}^{*}(\tau)a_{0},

(51)

with
\mathcal{B}(\tau)=\nu\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}[J_{j}e^{ij\tau}+J_{-j}e^{-ij\tau}]
and
z
same to Eq.
38
.
We chose the solution of
\xi_{a,1}
and
\xi_{b,1}
as

\displaystyle\xi_{a,1}=\mathcal{C}(\tau)b_{0},

\displaystyle\xi_{b,1}=-\mathcal{C}^{*}(\tau)a_{0},

(52)

with
\mathcal{C}(\tau)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\nu}{j}(J_{j}e^{ij\tau}-J_{-j}e^{-ij\tau})
.

(2)
First order
. The equations in this order are

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\xi_{a,2}}{\partial\tau}+i\frac{db_{0}}{dt}\frac{\partial\xi_{a,1}}{\partial b_{0}}=(\Delta+2\lambda|a_{0}|^{2}-\lambda|b_{0}|^{2})\xi_{a,1}-

\displaystyle(\nu J_{0}+\mathcal{B}(\tau)+\lambda b_{0}^{*}a_{0})\xi_{b,1}+\lambda(a_{0})^{2}\xi_{a,1}^{*}-\lambda a_{0}b_{0}\xi_{b,1}^{*},

(53)

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\xi_{b,2}}{\partial\tau}+i\frac{da_{0}}{dt}\frac{\partial\xi_{b,1}}{\partial a_{0}}=-(\Delta+\lambda|a_{0}|^{2}-2\lambda|b_{0}|^{2})\xi_{b,1}-

\displaystyle(\nu J_{0}+\mathcal{B}^{*}(\tau)+\lambda a_{0}^{*}b_{0})\xi_{a,1}+\lambda(b_{0})^{2}\xi_{b,1}^{*}-\lambda a_{0}b_{0}\xi_{a,1}^{*}.

(54)

With these equations, we can find the solutions for
\xi_{a,2}
and
\xi_{b,2}
are

\displaystyle\xi_{a,2}=-\mathcal{D}(\tau)(2\Delta b_{0}+4\lambda|a_{0}|^{2}b_{0}-2\lambda|b_{0}|^{2}b_{0}+\nu J_{0}a_{0})

\displaystyle+\mathcal{D}^{*}(\tau)(\nu J_{0}a_{0}+2\lambda a_{0}^{2}b_{0}^{*})+\mathcal{F}(\tau)a_{0},

(55)

\displaystyle\xi_{b,2}=\mathcal{D}^{*}(\tau)(2\Delta a_{0}+2\lambda|a_{0}|^{2}a_{0}-4\lambda|b_{0}|^{2}a_{0}-\nu J_{0}b_{0})

\displaystyle+\mathcal{D}(\tau)(\nu J_{0}b_{0}+2\lambda b_{0}^{2}a_{0}^{*})+\mathcal{F}^{*}(\tau)b_{0},

(56)

with
\mathcal{D}(\tau)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\nu}{j^{2}}(J_{j}e^{ij\tau}+J_{-j}e^{-ij\tau})
and
\mathcal{F}(\tau)=\sum_{j\neq 0,l\neq 0,j\neq l}=\frac{J_{j}J_{l}}{(j-l)l}e^{i(j-l)\tau}
.
Here,
j
and
l
run from negative infinity to positive infinity.
The solution for
\xi_{a,1}^{*}
and
\xi_{b,1}^{*}
can also be obtained by applying conjugation.

(3)
Second order
. The constant terms in this order will contribute to the effective equations.
The resulting Floquet Hamiltonian at this order is

\displaystyle H_{\text{F}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\nu^{2}B\Delta}{\omega^{2}}(|a_{0}|^{2}-|b_{0}|^{2})-\frac{3\nu^{2}B\lambda}{\omega^{2}}(|a_{0}|^{2}-|b_{0}|^{2})^{2}

(57)

\displaystyle-\frac{2\nu^{3}M}{\omega^{2}}(a_{0}^{*}b_{0}+b_{0}^{*}\tilde{a}_{0})+\frac{8\nu^{2}B\lambda}{\omega^{2}}|a_{0}|^{2}|b_{0}|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{4\nu^{2}C\lambda}{\omega^{2}}(a_{0}^{*}a_{0}^{*}b_{0}b_{0}+b_{0}^{*}b_{0}^{*}a_{0}a_{0}),

with
B
and
C
being given by Eq.
25
, M being given by
M=(C-B)J_{0}+\sum_{j,l>0,j\neq l}\frac{1}{jl}(J_{-j}J_{j+l}J_{l}-J_{-j}J_{j-l}J_{-l})
.
Compared with Eq.
8
, we find

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{4\nu^{2}B}{\omega^{2}})\Delta,

(58)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{6\nu^{2}B}{\omega^{2}})\lambda,

(59)

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(J_{0}+\frac{2\nu^{2}M}{\omega^{2}})\nu,

(60)

\displaystyle V_{\text{new}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\nu^{2}C\lambda}{\omega^{2}}(a^{*}a^{*}bb+b^{*}b^{*}aa)+\frac{8\nu^{2}B\lambda}{\omega^{2}}|a|^{2}|b|^{2}.

In this method, we also find the co-tunneling term as we have discussed in method Q-II.
Similarly, the criterion for MSTP becomes (see Appendix
A
)

\lambda_{c}=2\nu\frac{\omega^{2}J_{0}+2\nu^{2}M}{\omega^{2}-10\nu^{2}B+4\nu^{2}C}.

(62)

Using a similar analysis in method Q-II, we find
\lambda_{c}\sim 2\nu(1-\frac{A^{2}}{\omega^{2}})
, at the limit of
|\omega|\gg|A|
and
|\nu|
.

III.3
Method C-III

In this method, we use a much simpler but useful approach for the derivation of the Floquet Hamiltonian, following the essential idea in the Karpitz pendulum
Landau and Lifshitz 1976
. We expand
a
and
b
into the slow and fast modes up to the order of
\omega^{-2}
as

a=a_{0}+a_{1}+a_{2},\quad b=b_{0}+b_{1}+b_{2},

(63)

with
a_{j}
and
b_{j}
of the
\omega^{-j}
order.
Using this expansion, we can rewrite the nonlinear equations as

\displaystyle i\frac{d}{dt}(a_{0}+a_{1}+a_{2})=\Delta(a_{0}+a_{1}+a_{2})+\lambda(|a_{0}+a_{1}+a_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle-|b_{0}+b_{1}+b_{2}|^{2})(a_{0}+a_{1}+b_{2})-\nu(b_{0}+b_{1}+b_{2})

\displaystyle+A\cos(\omega t)(a_{0}+a_{1}+a_{2}),

(64)

\displaystyle i\frac{d}{dt}(b_{0}+b_{1}+b_{2})=-\Delta(b_{0}+b_{1}+b_{2})-\lambda(|a_{0}+a_{1}+a_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle-|b_{0}+b_{1}+b_{2}|^{2})(b_{0}+b_{1}+b_{2})-\nu(a_{0}+a_{1}+a_{2})

\displaystyle-A\cos(\omega t)(b_{0}+b_{1}+b_{2}).

(65)

Similarly, we can gather all terms according to the power of
\omega
.
We have the equations of
a_{1}
and
b_{1}
, as follows

i\dot{a}_{1}=A\cos(\omega t)a_{0},\quad i\dot{b}_{1}=-A\cos(\omega t)b_{0},

(66)

with solution

a_{1}=-i{A\sin(\omega t)\over\omega}a_{0},\quad b_{1}=i{A\sin(\omega t)\over\omega}b_{0}.

(67)

Strictly speaking, the above solution is not accurate if we consider the time-dependence of
a_{0}
and
b_{0}
, making
a_{1}(\tau,t)
, with
\tau=\omega t
.
In the multiple scale analysis as discussed in methods C-I and C-II, the time-dependence of
a_{0}
and
b_{0}
has been taken into account.
Ignoring this time dependence leads to results with much less accuracy; however, it is still instructive and has pedagogical value
Landau and Lifshitz 1976
.
In this way, there are no constant terms that can be separated in
\omega^{-1}
order.
Then we focus on the solution to the order of
\omega^{-2}
, with

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{A}{\omega^{2}}\cos(\omega t )[\Delta a+\lambda(|a_{0}|^{2}-|b_{0}|^{2})a_{0}+\nu b_{0}]

\displaystyle+\frac{A^{2}}{(2\omega)^{2}}\cos(2\omega t)a_{0},

(68)

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{A}{\omega^{2}}\cos(\omega t )[-\Delta b-\lambda(|a_{0}|^{2}-|b_{0}|^{2})b_{0}+\nu a_{0}]

\displaystyle+\frac{A^{2}}{(2\omega)^{2}}\cos(2\omega t)b_{0}.

(69)

In this way, the effective term for
a_{0}
comes from

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dtA\cos(\omega t )a_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{2}}{2\omega^{2}}[\Delta a_{0}+\lambda(|a_{0}|^{2}-|b_{0}|^{2})a_{0}+\nu b_{0}],

(70)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt\lambda(2a_{0}|a_{1}|^{2}+a_{0}^{*}a_{1}^{2}-a_{0}|b_{1}|^{2}-b_{0}^{*}b_{1}a_{1}-b_{0}b_{1}^{*}a_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{2}}{2\omega^{2}}\lambda(|a_{0}|^{2}-|b_{0}|^{2})a_{0},

(71)

and the same analysis can be employed to
b_{0}
.
Combining all these terms, we have the effective equation of motion for
a_{0}
and
b_{0}
as follows

\displaystyle i\frac{da_{0}}{dt}=

\displaystyle+\Delta^{\prime}a_{0}+\lambda^{\prime}(|a_{0}|^{2}-|b_{0}|^{2})a_{0}-\nu^{\prime}b_{0},

(72)

\displaystyle i\frac{db_{0}}{dt}=

\displaystyle-\Delta^{\prime}b_{0}-\lambda^{\prime}(|a_{0}|^{2}-|b_{0}|^{2})b_{0}-\nu^{\prime}a_{0},

(73)

with

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(1+\frac{A^{2}}{2\omega^{2}})\Delta,

(74)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(1+\frac{A^{2}}{\omega^{2}})\lambda,

(75)

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{A^{2}}{2\omega^{2}})\nu,\quad V_{\text{new}}=0.

(76)

In this result, the criterion for the MSTP is

\lambda_{c}=\nu\frac{2\omega^{2}-A^{2}}{\omega^{2}+A^{2}},

(77)

for
\Delta=0
,
a(0)=1
and

is much smaller than

With the above five methods and their different Floquet Hamiltonian, it is crucial to ask whether all these Floquet Hamiltonian give rise to a good description of the periodic driven BEC or only one yields the correct physics.
To answer this question, we numerically solve the mean field equation of motion that is obtained by Eq.

Then we compare the population dynamics

We present several remarks on the above results. (I) The most striking feature of these different methods is that
\lambda^{\prime}
,
\Delta^{\prime}
, and
\nu^{\prime}
can be unchanged, increased, or decreased, depending strongly on the approximations employed.
However, all of them seem to be reasonable and accurate when
\omega
is large enough.
(II) In all the classical methods, the variable is divided into fast and slow modes. Thus, the normalization of
|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}=1
is not guaranteed in the multiple scale analysis.
In contrast, the Hamiltonian is transformed according to unitary transformation in quantum methods.
This may account for the inaccuracy of the classical methods when the frequency of the driving becomes small. (III) On the other hand, quantum methods also have their problems. From the quantum Hamiltonian in Eq.
15
and perturbative expansion of the Floquet Hamiltonian in Eq.
12
, we can find the Floquet Hamiltonian is highly sensitive to the expression of
\hat{H}_{0}
and
\hat{H}_{j}
.
Thus the unitary transformation would affect
\hat{H}_{0}
and
\hat{H}_{j}
, leading to different Floquet Hamiltonian in the presence of truncation. In this way, some of the approximations will yield much better results than the other methods. Thus, the choice of the
\hat{H}_{0}
and
\hat{H}_{j}
by a proper unitary transformation is a rather tricky issue in practice. In Appendix
B
, we show that Q-II and C-II based on the unitary transformation will yield the most accurate result.

V
Conclusion and discussion

In this work, we have systematically revisited the Floquet Hamiltonian of the BEC in a double well with periodic driving using five different methods, all of which have solid theoretical grounds and seem to be reasonable.
In all methods, the Floquet Hamiltonian and associated mean field dynamics are discussed up to the order of
\omega^{-2}
.
In method Q-I, we find that periodic driving can only weaken the coupling, while it can not affect other parameters.
In method Q-II, all parameters are modified by periodic driving and we find that both the coupling and the interaction strength are weakened.
Moreover, new interaction terms for co-tunneling, which still preserves the total number of particles, emerge from the second-order process.
In methods C-I, C-II, and C-III, we use multiple scale analysis methods to obtain the Floquet Hamiltonian.
It is found that the terms, coming from the nonlinear interaction (i.e.
|\xi_{a,1}|^{2}
,
|\xi_{b,1}|^{2}
,
etc.
), also contribute to the Floquet Hamiltonian.
In method C-III, we have employed the same method used in Karpitz pendulum
Landau and Lifshitz 1976
to derive the Floquet Hamiltonian, yielding results that all parameters are modified.
Finally, we compare all these results on equal footing from the dynamics of the condensate and the associated macroscopic self-trapping process.
We find that while all methods can give a good approximation to the physics in the high frequency limit, significant and observable differences can emerge when the strength of the driving becomes comparable with the modulating frequency.
It is found that the quantum methods give much better approximation than the classical methods.
Especially, the last method (C-III) yields the poorest approximation to all physics. This justifies that the Floquet Hamiltonian based on Eq.
7
is more accurate than that based on Eq.
6
.

These results may indicate that the analysis based on the Floquet Hamiltonian may contain some subjectivity.
If the calculation is performed to infinity orders, one may expect that different starting points may yield the same conclusion.
However, due to the presence of truncation, the different methods can yield different effective Floquet Hamiltonians.
More strikingly, the parameters even get an opposite modification in different methods.
Since all these results give rise to reasonable descriptions at the high frequency limit, it is hard for us to claim that periodic driving can strengthen or weaken some particular parameters.
A similar problem also arise in the other periodic-driven systems.
Therefore, we have to be careful in employing the Floquet Hamiltonian approach to analyze the modulated systems in the future if high precision measurement can be achieved, in which the validity of these different approaches from different approximations can be readily verified using various simulation platforms.
In the current work, we consider the spinless models; and in the presence of spin degrees of freedom, the Floquet engineering can be used to realize new interactions that do not exist in the static Hamiltonian
Jotzu et al. 2014
;
Eckardt 2017
;
Hainaut et al. 2018
;
Oka and Kitamura 2019
;
Zhou et al. 2023b
;
Zhao et al. 2023
, thus the modification of the parameters in the Hamiltonian and the emergence of new terms
\hat{V}_{\text{new}}
from
\hat{H}_{\text{F}}^{(n)}
is reasonable and meaningful.
In the low modulating limit, some of the above approaches may become invalid, yielding new band structures
Lyu et al. 2020
;
Vogl et al. 2020
. This is a common problem in all driven systems based on various methods
Goldman and Dalibard 2014
;
Goldman et al. 2014
;
Vajna et al. 2018
;
Venkatraman et al. 2022
;
Vogl et al. 2019
, and the question that which method is more accurate is then important and needs to be concerned in much more careful in future.

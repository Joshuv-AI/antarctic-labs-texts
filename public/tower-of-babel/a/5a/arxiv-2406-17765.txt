A dimension formula of closed affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties of parahoric level
Arghya Sadhukhan

Abstract
. We establish a dimension formula for certain union X G ​ ( μ , b ) J X^{G}(\mu,b)_{J} of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties associated to arbitrary parahoric level structures of split reductive groups, under certain genericity hypotheses.

1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field with completed maximal unramified extension of
\breve{F}
and residue field
\kappa
. Let
(\mathbf{G},\{\mu\},b)
be a triple where
\mathbf{G}
is a connected reductive group over
F
,
\{\mu\}
is a conjugacy class of cocharacters
\mu:\mathbb{G}_{m,\bar{F}}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}_{\bar{F}}
and
[b]\in\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
. Fix a
F
-rational parahoric level structure
J
of
\mathbf{G}
, with corresponding standard parahoric subgroup
\breve{\mathcal{J}}\subset\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
. Associated to this data, we have the following closed affine Deligne-Lusztig variety, cf.
[
Rap05
]

X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)_{J}(\kappa):=\{g\breve{}\mathcal{J}\in\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})/\breve{}\mathcal{J};g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\breve{}\mathcal{J}\Adm(\mu)\breve{}\mathcal{J}\}\subset\mathcal{F}l^{\mathbf{G}}_{J}(\kappa)=\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})/\breve{}\mathcal{J}.

Here
X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)_{J}
is a subscheme, locally of finite type, of the partial affine flag variety attached to level
J
in the usual
sense in equal characteristic; in the sense of Zhu
[
Zhu17
]
and Bhatt-Scholze
[
BS17
]
in mixed characteristics. Since the underlying topological space of
\mathcal{F}l^{\mathbf{G}}_{J}
is Jacobson, any locally closed subscheme is uniquely determined by its
\kappa
-valued points, hence we freely identify
X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)_{J}
with the above set of its geometric points. These varieties play an important role in the study of the special fiber of both Shimura varieties and moduli spaces of shtukas, see
[
RV14
]
,
[
Vie18
]
.

By He’s work on the Kottwitz-Rapoport conjecture in
[
He16a
]
,
X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)_{J}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
[b]
is neutrally acceptable, i.e.
[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
, which we assume from now on. The next fundamental question concerns the dimension of these geometric objects. The first results in this direction was obtained when
\mathbf{G}
is an unramified group and
\breve{\mathcal{J}}
is a hyperspecial subgroup. In this case, the conjectural dimension formula put forth by Rapoport in
[
Rap05
]
was established in
[
Gör+06
]
and
[
Vie06
]
for split groups, and in
[
Ham15
]
and
[
Zhu17
]
for general quasi-split unramified groups. On the other extreme lies the case of Iwahori level structure. Here we only have a partial understanding. For a quasi-split group
\mathbf{G}
and its Iwahori subgroup
\breve{\mathcal{I}}
, a dimension formula was first obtained in
[
HY21
]
under certain genericity hypotheses, namely that
\mu
is
superregular
and
b
is
sufficiently small
compared to
\mu
; the first of these conditions was subsequently weakened to
\mu
being only regular in
[
Sad23
]
. Note that by varying the level structure, we obtain transition maps
\pi_{J,J^{\prime}}:\mathcal{F}l_{J}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}l_{J^{\prime}}
whenever
J\subset J^{\prime}
, thus inducing
\pi_{J,J^{\prime}}:X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)_{J}\rightarrow X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)_{J^{\prime}}
; by
[
He16a
]
, the latter map is surjective, hence
\dim X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)_{J}\geq\dim X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)_{J^{\prime}}
.

The purpose of this note is to prove a dimension formula in the case of an arbitrary parahoric level for split groups that interpolates these two known formulas.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{G}
is a split group and let
[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
. Then

(1.1)

\dim X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)_{J}=\langle\rho,\underline{\mu}-\nu(b)\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\text{def}(b)+\frac{1}{2}[\{\ell(w_{0})-\ell_{R}(w_{0})\}-\{\ell(w_{J})-\ell_{R}(w_{J})\}],

in the following cases:

(1)

\mu
is
2
-regular,
\mu\geq\nu(b)+\text{wt}(w_{0},1)
and
J=\emptyset
is the Iwahori level structure.

(2)

\mu
is
4
-regular,
\mu\geq\nu(b)+2\rho^{\vee}+\text{wt}(w_{0},1)
and
J
avoids at least one special vertex.

(3)

\mu
is
2\ell(w_{0})+2
-regular,
\mu\geq\nu(b)+2\rho^{\vee}+\text{wt}(w_{0},1)
and
J
is arbitrary.

Here
\ell_{R}(\cdot)
is the reflection length of elements in finite Weyl group, and
\text{wt}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the weight function defined in terms of its associated quantum Bruhat graph, see
section

2
for further details. Note that in part(i) we only improve
[
Sad23
]
by weakening the “gap” condition
\mu-\nu(b)
- which is assumed to be at least
2\rho^{\vee}
in loc. sit., albeit at the cost of introducing some regularity to
\mu
; note that
\text{wt}(w_{0},1)
is substantially smaller than
2\rho^{\vee}
, cf.
[
Sad23
, section 5]
, e.g. in type
A_{2n}
, we have
\text{wt}(w_{0},1)=\varpi_{n}^{\vee}+\varpi_{n+1}^{\vee}
.

1.1.
Strategy

We first discuss the proof of
theorem

1.1
[(i)]
. The assumption
\nu-\nu(b)\geq 2\rho^{\vee}
essentially enters the proof of
[
HY21
]
[Theorem 6.1] in asserting that the KR stratum associated to certain element in
\widetilde{W}
is top-dimensional in
X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)_{\emptyset}
, and the dimension of this KR stratum is known only under such gap hypothesis from
[
He21a
]
. To circumvent this condition, we simply construct a different top dimensional element (one in the antidominant chamber) even under the weaker gap hypothesis, by appealing to the theory of cordial elements in affine Weyl group à la
[
MV20
]
.

Our basic strategy in proving
theorem

1.1
[(ii), (iii)]
is to adapt the methods of
[
HY21
]
to arbitrary parahoric level. To that end, we note that arguments involving the EKOR strata in
X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)_{J}
- similar to those considered in loc. sit. for the KR strata in
X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)_{\emptyset}
- reduces our problem in
section

3
to one of minimizing distances in the quantum Bruhat graph; more specifically, under the conditions in the statement of the theorem, determining the dimension is equivalent to finding

\min\{d(x,xw_{0}):x\in~^{J}W\},

where
{}^{J}W
is the semi-affine quotient of the finite Weyl group
W
by
J
as introduced in
[
Sch24
]
; in case
\breve{}\mathcal{J}
is contained in a fixed hyperspecial subgroup, this is the set of minimal length elements in
W_{J}\backslash W
.

However, explicit construction of an minimizing element is more complicated when
J\neq\emptyset
. We recall that in the case of Iwahori level structure, it was established in
[
HY21
]
that this minimum is realized in the smaller subset of elements satisfying
x\leq xw_{0}
; since distance in the quantum Bruhat graph between two such elements is simply the difference of length, the previous optimization problem simplifies to finding
\max\{\ell(x):x\leq xw_{0}\}
- which is more amenable to an inductive argument. We carry out a similar argument in
section

4
Cartan types
A_{n},B_{n},C_{n}
when
J\subset\mathbb{S}
; however this approach does not seem to work nicely in type
D_{n}
, and in fact the previous observation about the minimum occuring at an element
x
with
x<xw_{0}
is false in general, when
J
contains an affine simple reflection.

Hence, we resort to a different method in
section

5
to handle the optimization problem in an uniform manner: by relating the long elements of the finite Weyl groups
W_{J}
and
W
. We show that for any subset
J
of affine simple roots, one can find a suitable subset
I
of finite simple roots such that
w_{0}
decomposes into product of certain
(~^{J}W)^{-1}
-conjugate of (linearization of) the long element of
w_{J}
and the long element
w_{I}
in a
\ell_{R}
-additive way. This is a proposition of independent interest, and we deduce it by combining explicit decompositions of
w_{0}
listed in
[
Sad23
]
, with classification of certain involutions in terms of long elements of parabolic subgroups carried out in
[
Zib23
]
.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Group-theoretic notations

Recall that
\mathbf{G}
is a connected reductive quasi-split group over a non-archimedean local field
F
. Let
\breve{F}
be the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
F
and
\sigma
be the Frobenius morphism of
\breve{F}/F
. The residue field of
F
is a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the residue field of
\breve{F}
is the algebraically closed field
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. We use the same symbol
\sigma
for the induced Frobenius morphism on
\breve{G}:=\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
. Let
S
be a maximal
\breve{F}
-split torus of
\mathbf{G}
defined over
F
, which contains a maximal
F
-split torus. Let
\mathcal{A}
be the apartment of
\mathbf{G}_{\breve{F}}
corresponding to
S_{\breve{F}}
. We fix a
\sigma
-stable alcove
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathcal{A}
, and let
\breve{}\mathcal{I}\subset\breve{G}
be the Iwahori subgroup corresponding to
\mathfrak{a}
. Then
\breve{}\mathcal{I}
is
\sigma
-stable.

Let
T
be the centralizer of
S
in
\mathbf{G}
. Then
T
is a maximal torus. We denote by
N
the normalizer of
T
in
\mathbf{G}
. The
Iwahori–Weyl group
(associated to
S
) is defined as

\widetilde{W}=N(\breve{F})/T(\breve{F})\cap\breve{}\mathcal{I}.

We denote by
\ell
the length function on
\tilde{W}
determined by the base alcove
\mathfrak{a}
and denote by
\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
the set of simple reflections in
\tilde{W}
. Let
W_{\text{aff}}
be the subgroup of
\widetilde{W}
generated by
\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
. Then
W_{\text{aff}}
is an affine Weyl group. Let
\Omega\subset\widetilde{W}
be the subgroup of length-zero elements in
\widetilde{W}
. Then
\widetilde{W}=W_{\text{aff}}\rtimes\Omega.
Note that this action of
\Omega
preserves
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
. For any
J\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
, let
\widetilde{W_{J}}\subset\widetilde{W}
be the subgroup generated by the simple reflections in
J
and by
{}^{J}\widetilde{W}
the set of minimal length elements for the cosets
\widetilde{W}_{J}\backslash\widetilde{W}
.

Let
W=N(\breve{F})/T(\breve{F})
be the relative Weyl group. We denote by
\mathbb{S}
the subset of
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
consisting of simple reflections generating
W
. We let
\Phi
(resp.
\Delta
,
\Delta_{\text{aff}}
) denote the set of roots (resp. simple roots, simple affine roots). We write
\Gamma
for
\Gal(\bar{F}/F)
, and write
\Gamma_{0}
for the inertia subgroup of
\Gamma
. Then fixing a special vertex of the base alcove
\mathfrak{a}
, we have the splitting

\widetilde{W}=X_{*}(T)\rtimes W=\{t^{\lambda}w;\lambda\in X_{*}(T),w\in W\}.

For an element
x\in\widetilde{W}
we denote by
\overline{x}
its projection to
W
; similarly, for a root
\alpha\in\Delta_{\text{aff}}
we denote its spherical part by
\overline{\alpha}
. For an irreducible Weyl group
W
of rank
n
, we follow the labeling of roots as in
[
Bou02
]
and we usually write
s_{i}
instead of
s_{\alpha_{i}}
, where
\Delta=\{\alpha_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
. The additional element in
\Delta_{\text{aff}}
is then the affine simple root
-\theta+1
, where
\theta
is the longest root in
\Phi
, and we denote the corresponding reflection in
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
by
s_{0}
. We will also sometimes denote by
\alpha_{s}
the affine simple root corresponding to
s\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
. For
J\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
we let
w_{J}
be the longest element of the corresponding parabolic subgroup and write
w_{0}=w_{\mathbb{S}}
. Let
\rho
be the dominant weight with
\langle\alpha^{\vee},\rho\rangle=1
for any
\alpha\in\Delta
. Let
\{\varpi_{i}^{\vee}:1\leq i\leq n\}
be the set of fundamental coweights. If
\varpi^{\vee}_{i}
is minuscule,
we denote the image of
t^{\varpi_{i}^{\vee}}
under the projection
\widetilde{W}\rightarrow\Omega
by
\tau_{i}
; then conjugation by
\tau_{i}
is a length preserving automorphism of
\widetilde{W}
which we denote by
\text{Ad}(\tau_{i})
.

For any dominant coweight
\lambda
we define
\text{depth}(\lambda)=\min\{\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle:\alpha\in\Delta\}
. Given
X\subset\widetilde{W},k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we denote by
X_{>k}
(resp.
X_{\leq k}
) the subset of
X
whose elements have associated dominant translation of depth at least
k+1
(resp. at most
k
); we call such elements to be
k
-regular.

2.2.
The
\sigma
-conjugacy classes of
\breve{G}

We say that two elements
b,b^{\prime}\in\breve{G}
are
\sigma
-conjugate if there is some
g\in\breve{G}
such that
b^{\prime}=gb\sigma(g)^{-1}
. Let
B(\mathbf{G})
be the set of
\sigma
-conjugacy classes on
\breve{G}
. By the work of Kottwitz in
[
Kot85
]
and
[
Kot97
]
, any
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b]
is determined by two invariants:

•

The Kottwitz point
\kappa([b])\in\pi_{1}(\mathbf{G})_{\Gamma}
, the set of
\Gamma
-coinvariants of the Borovoi fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\mathbf{G})
;

•

The Newton point
\nu([b])\in((X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0},\mathbb{Q}})^{+})^{\langle\sigma\rangle}
, the set of
\langle\sigma\rangle
-invariants of the intersection of
X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=X_{*}(T)^{\Gamma_{0}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
with the set
X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{+}
of dominant elements in
X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We denote by
\leqslant
the dominance order on
X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{+}
, i.e., for
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{+}
, we have
\nu\leqslant\nu^{\prime}
if and only if
\nu^{\prime}-\nu
is a non-negative (rational) linear combination of positive coroots over
\breve{F}
. The dominance order on
X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{+}
extends to a partial order on
B(\mathbf{G})
. Namely, for
[b],[b^{\prime}]\in B(\mathbf{G})
, we say that
[b]\leqslant[b^{\prime}]
if and only if
\kappa([b])=\kappa([b^{\prime}])
and
\nu([b])\leqslant\nu([b^{\prime}])
.

2.2.1.
Neutrally acceptable elements

Let
\{\mu\}
be a conjugacy class of cocharacters over
\bar{F}
. Choose
\mu
be a dominant representative of
\{\mu\}
and denote by
\underline{\mu}
its image in
X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0}}
. We define the
\mu
-admissible set

\Adm(\mu)=\{w\in\widetilde{W};\ w\leqslant t^{x(\underline{\mu})}\text{ for some }x\in W\}.

Let
\breve{}\mathcal{J}
be a standard
F
-rational parahoric subgroup of
\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
, i.e., a
\sigma
-invariant parahoric subgroup that contains
\breve{I}
. This corresponds to a spherical type
J
, i.e. a subset
J\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
such that
\widetilde{W}_{J}
is finite. Set
{}^{J}\text{Adm}(\mu)=\Adm(\mu)\cap~^{J}\widetilde{W}
.
We also have the set of
neutrally acceptable elements

B(\mathbf{G},\mu)=\{[b]\in B(\mathbf{G})\mid\kappa([b])=\mu^{\natural},\nu([b])\leq\mu\}.

Here
\mu^{\natural}
denotes the common image of
\mu\in\{\mu\}
in
\pi_{1}(\mathbf{G})_{\sigma}
.

The set

naturally inherits the partial order from
B(\mathbf{G})
.

2.3.
Affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties

The
affine Deligne-Lusztig variety
associated to
w\in\widetilde{W}
and
b\in\breve{G}
is a locally closed subscheme of the partial affine flag variety of
\bf G
, locally of finite type over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and of finite dimension, with the set of geometric points given by

X_{w}(b)=\{g\in\breve{G}/\breve{}\mathcal{I}:g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\breve{}\mathcal{I}w\breve{}\mathcal{I}\}.

If
F
is of equal characteristic, then by affine flag variety we mean the “usual” affine flag variety; in the case of mixed characteristic, this notion should be understood in the sense of perfect schemes, as developed by Zhu
[
Zhu17
]
and by Bhatt and Scholze
[
BS17
]
.

Let
J
be a spherical type. We set

X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)_{J}=\{g\breve{}\mathcal{J}\in\breve{G}/\breve{}\mathcal{J};g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\breve{}\mathcal{J}\Adm(\mu)\breve{}\mathcal{J}\}.

We usually omit the adoration
\mathbf{G}
from the notation and also write
X(\mu,b)
for
X(\mu,b)_{\emptyset}
, which is then an union of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties. Settling the Kottwitz-Rapoport conjecture made in
[
KR03
]
and
[
Rap05
]
about the non-emptiness pattern for
X(\mu,b)_{J}
, He proves the following result in
[
He16
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
He16a
, theorem A]

X(\mu,b)_{J}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
.

2.4.
Quantum Bruhat graphs

We recall the quantum Bruhat graph introduced by Brenti, Fomin, and Postnikov in the context of quantum cohomology ring of complex flag variety, see
[
BFP99
]
, also
[
FGP97
]
. By definition, a
quantum Bruhat graph

\Gamma_{\Phi}
is a directed graph with

•

vertices given by the elements of
W
;

•

upward edges
w\rightharpoonup ws_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
with
\ell(ws_{\alpha})=\ell(w)+1
;

•

downward edges
w\rightharpoondown ws_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
with
\ell(ws_{\alpha})=\ell(w)-\langle 2\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle+1
.

The
weight of a path
in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
is defined to be the sum of weights of the edges in the path. For any
x,y\in W
, we denote by
d(x,y)
the minimal length among all paths in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
from
x
to
y
. Any path between
x
and
y
affording
d(x,y)
as its length is called a
shortest path
between them. Then the following lemma defines a function
\text{wt}:W\times W\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\Phi^{\vee}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
Pos05
, Lemma 1]
,
[
BFP99
, Lemma 6.7]

Let
x,y\in W
.

(1)

There exists a directed path (consisting of possibly both upward and downward edges) in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
from
x
to
y
.

(2)

Any two shortest paths in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
from
x
to
y
have the same weight, which we denote by
\text{wt}(x,y)
.

(3)

Any path in
\Gamma_{\Phi}
from
x
to
y
has weight
\geqslant\text{wt}(x,y)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
HY21
, §4.2]
,
[
HN21
, §2.5]

Let
x,y\in W
. Then

(1)

We have
\langle\text{wt}(x,y),\alpha\rangle\leq 2
, for any simple root
\alpha
.

(2)

\ell(y)-\ell(x)=d(x,y)-\langle 2\rho,\text{wt}(x,y)\rangle
.

Lemma 2.4
.

d(x,xy)=\ell_{R}(y)
if
\ell(xy)=\ell(x)-\ell(y)
.

Proof.

We have
\text{wt}(x,xy)=\text{wt}(x^{-1}\lhd xy,1)
by
[
Sad23
]
[Corollary 3.3], where
\lhd
denotes the left downward Demazure product, see
[
HL15
]
[§2.2]. Thus,
d(x,xy)=\ell(xy)-\ell(x)+\ell(x^{-1}\lhd xy)+\ell_{R}(x^{-1}\lhd xy)
by
[
Sad23
]
[Lemma 8.4] and
lemma

2.3
[(2)]. Now,
x^{-1}\lhd xy=y
if and only if
\ell(y)=\ell(x^{-1})-\ell(xy)
, but in that case the first equation simplifies to
d(x,xy)=\ell_{R}(y)
.
∎

3.
Recollections from previous works

3.1.
Some standard reductions

Note that if
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{G}_{1}\times\mathbf{G}_{2},b=(b_{1},b_{2}),\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),\breve{}\mathcal{J}=\breve{}\mathcal{J}_{1}\times\breve{}\mathcal{J}_{2}
, then by definition
X^{\mathbf{G}}(\mu,b)_{J}\simeq X^{\mathbf{G}_{1}}(\mu_{1},b_{1})_{J_{1}}\times X^{\mathbf{G}_{2}}(\mu_{2},b)_{J_{2}}
; similarly, the terms on the RHS of the dimension formula
eq.

1.1
are additive as well. Hence, we may assume from now on that
\mathbf{G}
is quasi-simple and split over
F
.

Next, we argue that in certain cases we may assume that the parahoric subgroup is contained in a hyperspecial subgroup, i.e.
J\subset\mathbb{S}
. To do so, we need the following observation from
[
He14
]
[Lemma 4.3].

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
w\in\widetilde{W},\tau\in\Omega
. Then
X_{w}(b)
is isomorphic to
X_{{}^{\tau}w}(b)
.

Note that
s_{i}w\geq w\iff~^{\tau}s_{i}~^{\tau}w\geq~^{\tau}w
, and thus
\text{Ad}(\tau)(^{J}\widetilde{W})=^{\text{Ad}(\tau)(J)}\widetilde{W}
. Also,
{}^{\tau}\text{Adm}(\mu)=\text{Adm}(\mu)
, whence

\text{Ad}(\tau)(^{J}\widetilde{W}\cap\text{Adm}(\mu))=^{\text{Ad}(\tau)(J)}\widetilde{W}\cap\text{Adm}(\mu)=^{\text{Ad}(\tau)(J)}\Adm(\mu).

It is proved in
[
GH15
]
[§3.4] (see also
[
HR17
]
[Theorem 6.21]) that

\dim X(\mu,b)_{J}=\max\limits_{w\in^{J}\text{Adm}(\mu)}\dim X_{w}(b).

This comes from a decomposition of
X(\mu,b)_{J}
that is analogous, in terms of Shimura varieties, to the decomposition of a
Newton stratum into its intersections with the EKOR strata, cf.
[
HR17
]
.

In view of
lemma

3.1
, we then have
\dim X(\mu,b)_{J}=\dim X(\mu,b)_{\text{Ad}(\tau)(J)}
. Hence, if there exists a simple reflection
s
corresponding to a special vertex of
\mathfrak{a}
so that
s\notin J
, we may use a suitable element
\tau\in\Omega
so that
\text{Ad}(\tau)(J)\subset\mathbb{S}
and then focus on the dimension problem for this new level structure. For example, this assumption on
J
is satisfied for any parahoric level structure in type affine Weyl group of type
A_{n}
, so here we may assume
J\subset\mathbb{S}
.

3.2.
Virtual dimension formula

Recall that
w\in\widetilde{W}
can be
written in a unique way as
w=ut^{\lambda}v
with
\lambda
dominant,
u,v\in W
such that
t^{\lambda}v\in~^{\mathbb{S}}\widetilde{W}
. We then set
\eta(w)=vu,p_{l}(w)=u
.

3.3.
Minimizing distance on the quantum Bruhat graph

We now state the key technical result on quantum Bruhat graph that will be used in

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
J\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
be a spherical type. Then
\min\limits_{x\in^{J}W}d(x,xw_{0})=\ell_{R}(w_{0})+\ell(w_{J})-\ell_{R}(w_{J})

Here
\ell_{R}(w_{0})
(resp.
\ell_{R}(w_{J})

w_{0}
in
W
(resp.
w_{J}
in the parabolic subgroup
\widetilde{W}_{J}
of
\widetilde{W}

l

w_{0}\in W
(resp.
w_{J}\in\widetilde{W}_{J}

W
(resp. in
\widetilde{W}_{J}
), see
[
HY21
]
[§5.1] for details. We note that
\ell_{R}(\overline{w_{J}})=\ell_{R}(w_{J})
.

Remark 3.5
.

When

, i.e. in the case of the Iwahori level, the conclusion of

is known from
[
HY21
]
[Theorem 5.1]. However, the proof in loc. sit. could be considerably simplified. Indeed,

for any

, and if

by

]
[§5], thereby concluding

.

In this subsection we establish the lower bound. This relies on the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

d(x,xw_{0})=\ell(y)+d(x,\overline{y}xw_{0})
whenever
x\in~^{J}W
and
y\in\widetilde{W}_{J}

Proof.

We will induct on
\ell(y)
. By
lemma

3.2
, we may write
w=xt^{\lambda}z\in~^{J}\widetilde{W}
for some
z\in W
and cocharacter

.

Clearly, the claim is trivial for

and let

, with

. Note that

still has depth

. Arguing as in the proof of

if

is a finite simple reflection, or

∎

In particular,

4.
Explicit constructions for type

In this section, for an irreducible Weyl group

of type
A_{n},B_{n}/C_{n}
and

(a) there exists

with

and
\ell(x)=\frac{1}{2}\{(\ell(w_{0})-\ell_{R}(w_{0}))-(\ell(w_{J})-\ell_{R}(w_{J}))\}

]
[section 5.6] for the case of

{}^{J^{\prime}}W=\coprod\limits_{i\in^{J^{\prime}}W_{J}}i\cdot~^{J}W.

By inductive hypothesis, there exists
x\in~^{J}W
as asserted in (a). Now, suppose that there exists
i\in^{J^{\prime}}W_{J}

(1)

i<iw_{J}
, and

(2)

\ell(i)=\frac{1}{2}\{(\ell(w_{J})-\ell_{R}(w_{J}))-(\ell(w_{J^{\prime}})-\ell_{R}(w_{J^{\prime}})\}

Upon taking minimal length left coset representatives, the inequality

is irreducible. Since we only consider Weyl groups of type

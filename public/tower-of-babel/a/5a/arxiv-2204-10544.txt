Surfaces in the flag threefold containing smooth conics and twistor fibers Thanks: Partially supported by GNSAGA of INdAM and by the INdAM project ‘Teoria delle funzioni ipercomplesse e applicazioni’
Amedeo Altavilla, Edoardo Ballico, Maria Chiara Brambilla

Abstract
. We study smooth integral curves of bidegree ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) , called smooth conics , in the flag threefold 𝔽 \mathbb{F} . The study is motivated by the fact that the family of smooth conics contains the set of fibers of the twistor projection 𝔽 → ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{CP}^{2} . We give a bound on the maximum number of smooth conics contained in a smooth surface S ⊂ 𝔽 S\subset\mathbb{F} . Then, we show qualitative properties of algebraic surfaces containing a prescribed number of smooth conics. Lastly, we study surfaces containing infinitely many twistor fibers. We show that the only smooth cases are surfaces of bidegree ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) . Then, for any integer a > 1 a>1 , we exhibit a method to construct an integral surface of bidegree ( a , a ) (a,a) containing infinitely many twistor fibers.

1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{F}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2\vee}
be the complex flag variety of points and lines contained in a plane.
In this paper we investigate integral (i.e. irreducible and reduced) smooth curves of bidegree
(1,1)
, called
smooth conics
, contained in a surface
S\subset{\mathbb{F}}
.
The interest in this subject emerges from the fact that this family of
curves contains the set of fibers of the standard twistor projection

\pi:{\mathbb{F}}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}

[
4
,
9
,
3
]
.
In fact, from twistor theory, the fibers of
\pi
can be characterized to be the
j
-invariant rational smooth curves with normal bundle
\cal{O}(1)\oplus\cal{O}(1)
,
where
j:{\mathbb{F}}\to{\mathbb{F}}
is an anti-holomorphic involution involved in the twistor construction (see Formula (
2
)).

Continuing from
[
3
]
and inspired from
[
1
,
2
]
, we aim to obtain results on the maximum number of smooth
conics that can be contained in a smooth surface, on the geometry of the
generic surface containing a certain amount of smooth conics and a
description of the surfaces containing infinitely many twistor fibers.

In the preliminary section, we collect
some known results on
{\mathbb{F}}
and its subvarieties. In particular,
we define the bidegree of a curve and of a surface in
{\mathbb{F}}
, we define
smooth conics and twistor fibers and we present a detailed study of
the linear sections, the
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
surfaces, which are
Hirzebruch surfaces of type
1
. For other relevant geometry of the flag we refer
to
[
3
]
.

In Section
3
, we give a bound on the number of disjoint smooth conics contained in a surface
S
of bidegree
(a,b)
with both
a
and
b
greater or equal than
3
. Explicitly, the bound computed in Theorem
3.1
is:

\frac{2(a+b-2)(3a^{2}b-a^{2}+3ab^{2}-4ab+3a-b^{2}+3b)}{(a+b-1)^{2}}.

Since twistor fibers are disjoint smooth conics, the same bound also holds for them. The proof is
based on a classical result of Miyaoka
[
10
]
and it is obtained
by means of formula involving Chern classes of a surface.
The same proof can also be adapted in order to compute
the maximum number of curves of bidegree
(1,0)
or
(0,1)
contained in a smooth surface (see Remark
3.7
).

We expect that if
m
is the maximal number of disjoint smooth conics contained in a smooth surface
S\subset{\mathbb{F}}
, then not all the integers
0\leqslant k\leqslant m
are realized by some smooth surface of bidegree
(a,b)
, at least for high
a
and
b
. So an interesting open question is to find the maximum integer
m^{\prime}\leqslant m
such that for all
0\leqslant k\leqslant m^{\prime}
, there exists a smooth surface which contains
k
disjoint smooth conics.

In Section
4
, as a second main result, we give a range for an integer
x
such that
there exists an irreducible smooth surface in the flag which contains precisely
x
disjoint smooth conics.
More precisely we have the following theorem, in which we denote by
|\cal{I}_{T,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b)|
the linear system of the surfaces of bidegree
(a,b)
contained in
{\mathbb{F}}
that contains a set
T
of disjoint conics.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix integers
b\geqslant a\geqslant 2
and
0\leqslant x\leqslant(a-1)(a-2)/2
, and let
T\subset{\mathbb{F}}
be a general union of
x
smooth conics.
Then:

(i)

a general
S\in|\cal{I}_{T,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b)|
does not contain any smooth conic
C
such that
C\cap T=\emptyset
;

(ii)

a general
S\in|\cal{I}_{T,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b)|
is irreducible and smooth.

The proof of such a result is obtained by means of the classical
Horace method
,
[
8
]
.
Moreover in the same section we prove a unicity result
for surfaces in
{\mathbb{F}}
which contain a given amount of smooth conics,
see Proposition
4.1
.

Finally, in Section
5
, we study surfaces in
{\mathbb{F}}
containing infinitely many twistor fibers.
More precisely, in Proposition
5.2
we prove that if the singular locus of the surface is finite, then a surface containing infinitely many twistor fibers must be a smooth surface of bidegree
(1,1)
. On the other hand,
in Theorem
5.4
we provide a construction of singular surfaces of bidegree
(a,a)
for any
a\geqslant 2
containing infinitely many twistor lines. In particular, for any
n\leqslant a-1
, we give examples of surfaces
S
of bidegree
(a,a)
for which the family of twistor lines
contained in
S
is parametrized by

n
disjoint circles,
up to finitely many identifications and finitely many isolated points.

Collecting the results of the final section, we get that, for any positive integer
a
there exists a surface of bidegree
(a,a)
containing infinitely many twistor lines, belonging to a continuous family.
These results resemble the behavior of the other basic example of algebraic twistor space, which is
\mathbb{P}^{3}
projecting onto
S^{4}
. In that case, considering the results in
[
11
,
2
]
, we obtained that the only smooth case of surface containing infinitely many twistor fibers is that of quadrics, while the other admissible cases are only ruled surfaces of even degree. Here, in a sense, the analogy is complete, as the “total degree” in the Segre embedding of
a surface of bidegree
(a,b)
is
a+b
.

2.
Preliminaries

In the whole paper we will take advantage of notations and basic facts already introduced in
[
3
]
. Nevertheless, we
recall here briefly what is needed in order to be as more self-contained as possible.

Let us denote by
\mathbb{P}^{n}=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{n+1})
the complex projective space. Given a complex projective variety
V
, we write
V_{\mathbb{R}}
for its real locus.
In the paper we will identify
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and its dual
\mathbb{P}^{2\vee}
, both on
\mathbb{R}
and on
\mathbb{C}
. This is possible because, if
P
is a real algebraic
variety whose complexification is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, then
P
is isomorphic to the real projective plane
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by
[
12
, Prop. VI (1.1) p. 109]
.

We will need in the
Segre variety

\Sigma=\sigma(\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2})\subset\mathbb{P}^{8}
, which
is the image of the Segre embedding
\sigma:\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}\to\mathbb{P}^{8}
, defined as follows:

(p,\ell)=([p_{0}:p_{1}:p_{2}],[\ell_{0}:\ell_{1}:\ell_{2}])\mapsto\left[\begin{matrix}\ell_{0}\\
\ell_{1}\\
\ell_{2}\end{matrix}\right]\cdot[p_{0}:p_{1}:p_{2}]=\left[\begin{matrix}p_{0}\ell_{0}&p_{1}\ell_{0}&p_{2}\ell_{0}\\
p_{0}\ell_{1}&p_{1}\ell_{1}&p_{2}\ell_{1}\\
p_{0}\ell_{2}&p_{1}\ell_{2}&p_{2}\ell_{2}\end{matrix}\right].

Recall that
\Sigma
is
a
4
-fold of degree
6
contained in
\mathbb{P}^{8}
.

We denote the standard projections by
\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}:{\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
, where
\Pi_{1}(p,\ell)=p
and
\Pi_{2}(p,\ell)=\ell
. For any
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we set
\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}}}(d_{1},d_{2})=\Pi_{1}^{*}\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(d_{1})\otimes\Pi_{2}^{*}\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(d_{2})
. Recall that

\mathop{\mathrm{Pic}}({\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}})\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and it is freely generated as an abelian group by

\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}}}(1,0)
and
\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}}}(0,1)
.
The canonical line bundle is

\omega_{{\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}}}\simeq\cal{O}(-3,-3).

The flag threefold in
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2\vee}
is defined geometrically as the set of couples
(p,\ell)
such that
p\in\ell
. However, we will mainly use the following algebraic definition as incidence variety in
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Let us denote the elements of the first
\mathbb{P}^{2}
as rows, while those of the second one as columns, i.e.: if
(p,\ell)\in{\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}}
, then

p=[p_{0}:p_{1}:p_{2}],\quad\ell=[\ell_{0}:\ell_{1}:\ell_{2}]^{\top}=\left[\begin{matrix}\ell_{0}\\
\ell_{1}\\
\ell_{2}\end{matrix}\right],

so that
p\ell=p_{0}\ell_{0}+p_{1}\ell_{1}+p_{2}\ell_{2}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
flag threefold
is the algebraic subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}}
defined by

{\mathbb{F}}:=\{(p,\ell)=([p_{0}:p_{1}:p_{2}],[\ell_{0}:\ell_{1}:\ell_{2}]^{\top})\in{\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}}\,|\,p\ell=p_{0}\ell_{0}+p_{1}\ell_{1}+p_{2}\ell_{2}=0\}.

We define the standard projections
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}:{\mathbb{F}}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
as
\pi_{1}:=\Pi_{1|{\mathbb{F}}}
and
\pi_{2}:=\Pi_{2|{\mathbb{F}}}
, and we set

\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(d_{1},d_{2}):=\pi_{1}^{*}\cal{O}(d_{1})\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}\cal{O}(d_{2}).

Since
{\mathbb{F}}
is an effective divisor belonging to
|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}}}(1,1)|
, by the adjunction formula we have

(1)

\omega_{{\mathbb{F}}}\simeq\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(-2,-2).

We now describe some geometry of curves and surfaces in
{\mathbb{F}}
.
Given a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on a projective variety
V
we denote by
|\mathcal{L}|=\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(V,\mathcal{L}))
.

Let us start by defining the bidegree of a surface and of a curve in
{\mathbb{F}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{F}}
be an algebraic surface in
|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(d_{1},d_{2})|
. Then we say that
S
has
bidegree

(d_{1},d_{2})
, and write

\bdeg(S)=(d_{1},d_{2})
.

We recall the
multiplication rules in the Chow ring: by
[
3
, Proposition 3.11]
, we have that

\displaystyle\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)\cdot\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)\cdot\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)=0,

\displaystyle\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)\cdot\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,1)\cdot\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)=1,

\displaystyle\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,1)\cdot\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)\cdot\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,1)=1,

\displaystyle\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,1)\cdot\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,1)\cdot\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,1)=0.

While for surfaces the notion of bidegree is quite natural, in the case of curves we need a little bit more of elaboration.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
C\subset{\mathbb{F}}
be an algebraic curve. We define its
bidegree
as

\bdeg(C)=(d_{1},d_{2})
, where if
\pi_{i}(C)=\{x\}
, then
d_{i}=0
, otherwise

d_{i}=a_{i}b_{i}
with
a_{i}=\deg(\pi_{i}(C))
and
b_{i}=\deg(\pi_{i}|_{C})
.

From the definition we have that
\pi_{1}^{-1}(p)
has bidegree
(0,1)
, while

\bdeg(\pi_{2}^{-1}(\ell))=(1,0)
. Moreover, all curves of bidegree
(0,1)
(or
(1,0)
) can be represented as
\pi_{1}^{-1}(p)
for some
p\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
(respectively, as
\pi_{2}^{-1}(\ell)
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
).
This notion of bidegree for curves is related to that of surfaces by means of general intersections.

Remark 2.4
.

Any curve
C
of bidegree
(0,1)
is obtained as the intersection of
two surfaces of bidegree
(1,0)
(see
[
3
, Section 3.2]
), i.e.
C
is a class of type
\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)\cdot\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)
in the Chow ring. Therefore, if
C
has bidegree
(d_{1},d_{2})
, then it belongs to the class
d_{2}\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)\cdot\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)+d_{1}\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,1)\cdot\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,1)
.
Moreover, thanks to
[
3
, Proposition 3.11]
, we have that if
C
has bidegree
(d_{1},d_{2})
, if and only if

C\cdot\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)=d_{1}
and
C\cdot\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,1)=d_{2}
.

It was shown in
[
3
, Remark 3.2]
that if a curve
C
has bidegree
(d_{1},d_{2})
with
d_{i}=1
(for some
i=1,2
), then
C
is rational. A particularly interesting case is that of curves of bidegree
(1,1)
as this family contains the set of twistor fibers. All these curves can be described as

L_{q,m}:=\{(p,\ell)\in{\mathbb{F}}\,|\,pm=0,q\ell=0\}.

If
qm\neq 0
, then
L_{q,m}
is smooth and irreducible, while if
qm=0
, then

L_{qm}=\pi_{1}^{-1}(q)\cup\pi_{2}^{-1}(m)
.
Any curve
L_{q,m}
has degree
2
in the Segre embedding as it can be
seen as a linear section of a smooth quadric.
We refer the reader to
[
3
, Section 3.1]
for a detailed analysis of these curves.
For the convenience of what follows we will set the following definition.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
L_{q,m}\subset{\mathbb{F}}
be a bidegree
(1,1)
curve. If
qm\neq 0
, then
L_{q,m}
is said to be a
smooth conic
.
The set of smooth conics will be denoted by
\cal{C}
.

Remark 2.6
.

Fix a curve
F
of bidegree
(1,0)
and a curve
G
of bidegree
(0,1)
such that
F\cap G\neq\emptyset
. Note that
F\cap G
is a unique point and that
F\cup G
is a flat limit of a family of smooth conics,
[
3
, Section 2.1]
.

From the definition of
L_{q,m}
, it is clear that the family of bidegree
(1,1)

curves is parametrized by
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
\cal{C}
is parametrized by
(\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2})\setminus{\mathbb{F}}
.

As said before,
\cal{C}
contains the set of twistor fibers
\pi^{-1}(q)
, where the twistor map

\pi:{\mathbb{F}}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{P}^{2}

is defined as
\pi(p,\ell)=\overline{p}\times\ell
(see
[
3
, Section 5]
for more details).
If we denote by
j:{\mathbb{F}}\to{\mathbb{F}}
the antiholomorphic involution map

(2)

j(p,\ell)=(\overline{\ell},\overline{p}),

then a twistor fiber is an integral
(1,1)
curve that is
j
-invariant.
In terms of
L_{q,m}
, we have that
j(L_{q,m})=L_{q,m}
if and only if
m=\overline{q}
.

Definition 2.7
.

The set of twistor fibers will be denoted by
\cal{T}
.

As said before
\cal{T}\subset\cal{C}
, but even more
\cal{T}
is a Zariski dense in
\cal{C}
(see
[
3
, Lemma 5.6]
).

We now turn our attention to surfaces in
{\mathbb{F}}
.
A first study of surfaces of bidegree
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
was carried out in
[
3
, Section 3.2]
. We start with the following definition.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
q,m\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
. We define the following surfaces in
{\mathbb{F}}
:

\displaystyle H_{m}

\displaystyle:=\{(p,\ell)\in{\mathbb{F}}\,|\,pm=0\}\in|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)|,

{}_{q}H

\displaystyle:=\{(p,\ell)\in{\mathbb{F}}\,|\,q\ell=0\}\in|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,1)|.

We will not really need, in the current analysis, the nature of the
(1,0)
or
(0,1)
surfaces as
H_{m}
or
{}_{q}H
. In this view, we will denote throughout the paper
an element of
|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)|
as
X
and an element of
|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,1)|
as
Y
.

Remark 2.9
.

Recall that two general surfaces
X,X^{\prime}\in|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)|
intersect in a fiber of
\pi_{1}
, while a general surface
X
of bidegree
(1,0)
and a general surface
Y
of bidegree
(0,1)
intersect in a smooth conic. Hence a general surface
X
of bidegree
(1,0)
(or
(0,1)
) intersects a general conic in a general point, by
[
3
, Proposition 3.11]
.

In
[
3
, Section 3.2]
we gave several geometric descriptions of
these families of surfaces, noticing that any surface of bidegree
(1,0)
is
a surface of type
H_{m}
and any of bidegree
(0,1)
is of type
{}_{q}H
.
Moreover, as
H_{m}=\pi_{1}^{-1}(\pi_{1}(\pi_{2}^{-1}(m)))
, it is easy to see that
\pi_{2|H_{m}}:H_{m}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
represents the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at
m
(and analogously for
{}_{q}H
at

).
In particular, in
[
3
, Corollary 3.10]
, we proved that these are Hirzebruch surfaces of type
1
, usually denoted by
F_{1}
.
For the purpose of this paper we will need to
deepen their description as Hirzebruch surfaces.

Recall that
\mathop{\mathrm{Pic}}(F_{1})=\mathbb{Z}h\oplus\mathbb{Z}f
, where

f
is the fiber of the ruling of
F_{1}
and
h
is the section with self-intersection
-1
.
and that
h^{2}=-1
,
f^{2}=0
and
hf=1
.

Now, given a surface
X
of bidegree
(1,0)
,
up to identification of
X
to
F_{1}
, we get
that the line bundle
\cal{O}_{X}(1,0)
corresponds to

\cal{O}_{F_{1}}(f)
(and to curves contained in
X
of bidegree (0,1) in
{\mathbb{F}}
) and the line bundle
\cal{O}_{X}(0,1)

corresponds to
\cal{O}_{F_{1}}(h+f)
(and to elements of
\cal{C}
). Hence
we get, for any
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
and for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
:

(3)

\cal{O}_{X}(a,b)\cong\cal{O}_{F_{1}}(bh+(a+b)f),\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\cal{O}_{F_{1}}(\alpha h+\beta f)\cong\cal{O}_{X}(\beta-\alpha,\alpha).

Clearly if
Y
is a surface of bidegree
(0,1)
, identified again with a Hirzebruch surface
F_{1}
, we have analogously that

\cal{O}_{Y}(a,b)\cong\cal{O}_{F_{1}}(ah+(a+b)f),\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\cal{O}_{F_{1}}(\alpha h+\beta f)\cong\cal{O}_{X}(\alpha,\beta-\alpha),

for any
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
and for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
X
be a surface of bidegree
(1,0)
and
Y
be a surface of bidegree
(0,1)
. For any
a\geqslant 0,b\geqslant 0
, we have

h^{0}(\cal{O}_{X}(a,b))=a(b+1)+\binom{b+2}{2},\quad h^{0}(\cal{O}_{Y}(a,b))=b(a+1)+\binom{a+2}{2},

and

h^{1}(\cal{O}_{X}(a,b))=h^{1}(\cal{O}_{Y}(a,b))=0.

Proof.

It is easy to prove, as in
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
, that
h^{1}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(a,b))=0
if

a\geqslant-1
and
b\geqslant-1
.
Hence, by using the exact sequence

0\to\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(a-1,b)\to\cal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}(a,b)\to\cal{O}_{X}(a,b)\to 0,

we conclude.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

Given
X
(resp.
Y
) a surface of bidegree
(1,0)
(resp.
(0,1)
), we know by
[
7
, Corollary 2.18]
that the line bundles

We know that, if

is a fiber of the ruling of

(that is in the class of

), then

hence we have

and by the previous lemma we get

.
Hence a curve

in the class of

has bidegree
(0,1)
.

On the other hand a curve

in the class of

is such that

and by the previous lemma we get
\cal{O}_{L}(a,b)=\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a+b)
,
hence a curve

in the class of

has bidegree
(1,1)
.

3.
A Miyaoka-type bound for smooth surfaces

In this section we aim to obtain a Miyaoka-type bound
[
10
]
in the case of a smooth surfaces of bidegree
(a,b)

contained in the flag manifold
{\mathbb{F}}
.
In order to obtain such a result we need to study the Chow ring of the flag and compute Chern classes.
In particular, applying
[
10
, eq. (6)]
, we will prove the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{F}}
be a smooth surface of bidegree
(a,b)
, with
a\geqslant 3,b\geqslant 3
. Then
S

contains at most

(4)

\frac{2(a+b-2)(3a^{2}b-a^{2}+3ab^{2}-4ab+3a-b^{2}+3b)}{(a+b-1)^{2}}

disjoint smooth conics. Moreover, this bound also holds for twistor fibers.

We recall some known facts about Chern classes, as a general reference see
[
5
]
.
For any exact sequence of vector bundles on any algebraic scheme
Y
:

0\to E^{\prime}\to E\to E^{\prime\prime}\to 0

we have
c_{1}(E)=c_{1}(E^{\prime})+c_{1}(E^{\prime\prime})
and
c_{2}(E)=c_{2}(E^{\prime})+c_{2}(E^{\prime\prime})+c_{1}(E^{\prime})\cdot c_{1}(E^{\prime\prime})
, where

c_{i}(F)

Proposition 3.5

4.
Surfaces with prescribed number of conics

In this section we prove two results. First we show that, if there exists a surface in
{\mathbb{F}}
containing a certain amount of smooth conics, then this
surface is unique. Later, we will prove that, under certain numerical hypotheses, a general surface containing a general set of disjoint smooth
conics, does not contains any other conic disjoint from the previous one and it is irreducible and smooth.

In
[
3
, Proposition 8.1]
we proved that an integral surface
S
of bidegree
(a,b)
that is not
j
-invariant,
is such that
S\cap j(S)
contains at most
a^{2}+ab+b^{2}
smooth conics. We now prove that this number
is critical in order to find at most one integral surface containing that particular number of pairwise disjoint smooth conics.

Proposition 4.1
.

Fix positive integers
a,b
. Let
T\subset{\mathbb{F}}
be any union of

x\geqslant a^{2}+ab+b^{2}

pairwise disjoints smooth conics. Then there exists at most a unique integral surface of bidegree
(a,b)
which contains
T
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction the existence of two integral
S,S^{\prime}\in|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b)|
such that
S\neq S^{\prime}
. Since
S
is integral, the intersection
S\cap S^{\prime}
is scheme-theoretically an effective Cartier divisor of
S

whose degree is
a^{2}+ab+b^{2}
. Thus
T=S\cap S^{\prime}
. Since
S\cap S^{\prime}
is an ample Cartier divisor of an integral variety of positive dimension,
S\cap S^{\prime}
is connected. But
T

has
a^{2}+ab+b^{2}>1
connected components.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The connectedness of
S\cap S^{\prime}
was used also in
[
3
, Corollary]
,
for

in order to prove that an integral surface of bidegree
(a,b)

contains at most

twistor fibers.

We introduce now some notation which we will use in the next proofs.
For all

Set

We will denote by

Remark 4.3

Let

be two curves such that

since

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{T,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b))\leqslant h^{1}(\cal{I}_{C\cup T,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b))

Theorem 4.4

Fix integers

,

Thanks to Remark

\deg(2p\cap T)=2
, we have
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{T,{\mathbb{F}}}\otimes\cal{O}_{2p}(a,b))\geqslant 2
.
Hence it is sufficient to prove that

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{T\cup 2p,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b))=0.

Fix
p\in T
and let
C
be the connected component of
T
containing
p
. Set the following notation
E:=T\setminus C
. By Theorem
4.4
and sequence (
7
), we have
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{T,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b-1))=h^{0}(\cal{I}_{E,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b-1))-a-b
.
Thus
p
is not in the base locus of
|\cal{I}_{E,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b-1)|
.
Fix
S\in|\cal{I}_{E,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b-1)|
such that
p\notin S
.
Let
Y
be surface of
|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,1)|
containing
C
and consider the residual exact sequence with respect to
Y
:

(12)

0\to\cal{I}_{E\cup p,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b-1)\to\cal{I}_{T\cup 2p}(a,b)\to\cal{I}_{{(E\cap Y)}\cup C\cup(2p\cap Y),Y}(a,b)\to 0.

Now we prove that

(13)

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{E\cup p,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b-1))=0.

Recall that
T=E\cup C
and
p\in C
, hence we have the exact sequence

0\to\cal{I}_{T,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b-1)\to\cal{I}_{E\cup p}(a,b-1)\to\cal{I}_{p,C}(a+b-1)\to 0.

Thanks to Theorem
4.4
we have that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{T,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b-1))=0
; on the other hand, since
C
is a smooth rational curve, we have

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{p,C}(a+b-1))=h^{1}(\cal{O}_{C}(a+b-2))=0
and this proves (
13
).

In order to conclude it is sufficient to prove now that

(14)

\cal{I}_{(E\cap Y)\cup C\cup(2p\cap Y),Y}(a,b).

Note that

\cal{I}_{C,Y}(a,b)\cong\cal{O}_{F_{1}}((a-1)h+(a+b-1)f)\cong\cal{O}_{Y}(a-1,b),

hence, by Remark
2.11
, we know that
\cal{I}_{C,Y}(a,b)
is very ample.
Therefore we get

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{C\cup(2p\cap Y),Y}(a,b))=0
. Since
E\cap Y
are
x-1
general points we conclude that (
14
)
holds.

Thus the exact sequence (
12
) gives
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{T\cup 2p,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b))=0
, concluding the proof.
∎

We now pass to apply the previous result to twistor fibers. We denote now by

\cal{T}(k)\subset\cal{C}(k)

the set of all
(C_{1},\dots,C_{k})

such that each
C_{i}
is a twistor fiber.
The action of the anti-holomorphic involution
j
, defined in Formula (
2
), on
\cal{C}(k)
is given by
j((C_{1},\dots,C_{k}))=(j(C_{1}),\dots,j(C_{k}))
, and
\cal{T}(k)
is the fixed locus with respect to this action.

We need to introduce the notion of
general
union of
k
twistor fibers.

Definition 4.5
.

Following
[
1
, Definition 3.1]
, we say that a property
\cal{P}
is true for a
general union
of
k
twistor fibers or that
k

general twistor fibers
satisfy
\cal{P}

if there is a non-empty Zariski open subset
U
of
\cal{C}(k)
such that
\cal{P}
is true for all

(C_{1},\dots,C_{k})\in U\cap\cal{T}(k)
,
such that
C_{m}\neq C_{n}
for any
m\neq n
.

In complete analogy with
[
1
, Lemma 3.2]
we are able to state that
\cal{T}(k)
is a Zariski dense of
\cal{C}(k)
. This observation, Theorem
1.1
and the
fact that two twistor fibers are disjoint, lead us to the following result.

Corollary 4.6
.

Fix integers
b\geqslant a\geqslant 2
,
0\leqslant x\leqslant(a-1)(a-2)/2
. The
following statements hold true.

(1)

If
T\subset{\mathbb{F}}
is a general union of
x
twistor fibers, then
a general
S\in|\cal{I}_{T,{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b)|
is irreducible, smooth and contains exactly
x
twistor fibers.

(2)

There exists a smooth irreducible surface
S
of bidegree
(a,b)
containing exactly
x
twistor fibers.

(3)

Near
S
, the family of all the surfaces of bidegree
(a,b)
is a smooth differentiable manifold of real dimension

2\left(\binom{a+2}{2}\binom{b+2}{2}-\binom{a+1}{2}\binom{b+1}{2}-x(a+b+1)\right)+4x.

Proof.

Recall that the set
\cal{T}(k)
is a connected and smooth real analytic variety of real dimension
4k
which is Zariski dense in
\cal{C}(k)
.
By Theorem
1.1
and semicontinuity, we get our statements.
∎

5.
Surfaces with infinitely many twistor fibers

The last results of the previous section introduce us to to the study of surfaces containing infinitely elements
of
\cal{T}
.
In particular, here we aim to describe a method to construct such kind of surfaces. Moreover, this result will also give
the existence of infinitely many examples.

We start with the following remark.

Remark 5.1
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{F}}
be an integral surface.
If
S
contains infinitely many twistor fibers, then there is a positive dimensional family of
twistor fibers contained in
S
.
In fact, let
G\subset\cal{C}
be any infinite set and let
\overline{G}
be its Zariski closure in
\cal{C}
.
Since
G
is infinite,
\overline{G}
has a positive-dimensional component. If every element of
G
is contained in
S
, then every element of
\overline{G}
is contained in
S
.

In particular any desingularization
S^{\prime}
of
S
is uniruled, i.e. it has Kodaira dimension
\kappa(S^{\prime})=-\infty
.
In particular
\kappa(S)=-\infty
if
S
contains infinitely many twistor fibers.
Notice that the same argument can be adapted to the case of surfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, seen as twistor space of
\mathbb{S}^{4}
, containing infinitely many twistor fibers
[
2
]
.

We now show that surfaces with isolated singularities containing infinitely many twistor fibers must have bidegree
(1,1)
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
S\in|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(a,b)|
be an integral surface such that
\mathrm{Sing}(S)
is finite and
S
contains infinitely many twistor fibers. Then
a=b=1
and
S
is smooth.

Proof.

Since
S
has infinitely many twistor fibers, thanks to
[
3
, Corollary 8.7]
we have
j(S)=S
and hence
a=b

By Remark
5.1
there is a positive dimensional family
\mathcal{A}
of
twistor fibers contained in
S
.

Recall that since
\omega_{{\mathbb{F}}}\cong\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(-2,-2)
, the adjunction formula gives
\omega_{S}\cong\cal{O}_{S}(a-2,a-2)
. Since any two different twistor fibers are disjoint and

\mathrm{Sing}(S)
is finite, there is a twistor fiber
D\in\mathcal{A}
such that
D\cap\mathrm{Sing}(S)=\emptyset
. Since
D
is
contained in
\mathrm{Sm}(S)
, the smooth locus of
S
, the normal bundle sequence

0\to TD\to T(\mathrm{Sm}(S))_{|D}\to N_{D}\to 0,

gives
\omega_{D}\cong\cal{O}_{D}(a-2,a-2)\otimes N_{D}
, where
N_{D}
is the normal bundle of
D
in
\mathrm{Sm}(S)
.

Since we may take as
D

a general element of a positive-dimensional family of curves of
\mathrm{Sm}(S)
, the line bundle
N_{D}

has degree at least
0
. Since
D\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we have
\deg(\omega_{D})=-2
. Thus
a<2
and
S
is a
j
-invariant
(1,1)
surface. Finally, by
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
we conclude that
S
is smooth.
∎

Hence, in order to find other examples of surfaces in
{\mathbb{F}}
containing infinitely many twistor fibers, we must look at surfaces which have singular locus of positive dimension. In the case of surfaces of bidegree
(1,1)
the analysis is
quite simple.
We recall that a reducible
(1,1)
-surfaces is the union of two (Hirzebruch) surfaces of bidegrees
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
, respectively, intersecting on a smooth conic (see
[
3
, Section 3.2 and Proposition 4.5]
). In the next remark,
we show that there are not reducible surfaces of bidegree
(1,1)
containing infinitely many twistor fibers.

Remark 5.3
.

Let
S
be a reducible surface of bidegree
(1,1)
. Write
S=X\cup Y
with
X\in|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)|
and
Y\in|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,1)|
. Any irreducible curve
T\subset S

is contained either in
X
or in
Y
. We have
j(X)\in|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(0,1)|
and
j(Y)\in|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(1,0)|
. Thus if
j(T)=T
, then either
T\subseteq j(X)\cap X
or
T\subseteq j(Y)\cap Y
.
Since
j(X)\cap X
and
j(Y)\cap Y
have dimension
1
,
X
contains at most one twistor fiber. This case happens exactly when
S=j(S)
.

From Proposition
5.2
we know that if
a\geq 2
any
(a,a)
-surface containing infinitely many twistor fibers has singular locus containing a curve. We are now ready to prove the main theorem of this section, describing an explicit construction of such a surface, for any
a\geqslant 2
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
C
be a smooth and connected complex projective curve of genus
g
defined over
\mathbb{R}
and with
C_{\mathbb{R}}\neq\emptyset
. For any
a\geqslant g+2
,
there exists an integral surface
S\in|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(a,a)|
containing
infinitely many twistor fibers and birational to
C\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Proof.

By Remark
5.1
any surface
S\subset{\mathbb{F}}
containing infinitely many twistor fibers is birationally ruled by conics and
hence it is birational over
\mathbb{C}
to
C\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
for some smooth projective curve
C
.

Fix a general non-special
very ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
of degree
a
on
C
defined over
\mathbb{R}
. Since
a\geqslant g+2
, then
h^{0}(C,\mathcal{L})\geqslant{3}
.
Take a general
3
-dimensional real subspace
V_{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq H^{0}(C,\cal{L})_{\mathbb{R}}
and let
V=V_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\subseteq H^{0}(C,\cal{L})=H^{0}(C_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C},\cal{L})
(where the last equality can be found for instance in
[
7
, Chapter III, Proposition 9.3 and 9.4]
).
Thus
V
induces a morphism
f_{V}:C\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
onto a degree
a
plane curve.
The conjugate subspace
\overline{V}
is well-defined and gives a complex holomorphic map
f_{\overline{V}}:C\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Notice that, by construction, we have

(15)

f_{V}(p)=\overline{f_{\overline{V}}(\overline{p})}.

Now, the map,

f=(f_{V},f_{\overline{V}}):C\to\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}

induces a morphism birational onto its image, and the curve
T:=f(C)\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
satisfies
j(T)=T
.
Moreover, thanks to Formula (
15
) any real point of
C
gives a point of
T
corresponding to a twistor fiber.
Since
C
is a smooth curve defined over
\mathbb{R}
with at least a real point, then
C_{\mathbb{R}}
contains infinitely many real points by
[
6
]
and therefore, we conclude that
T
parametrizes infinitely many twistor fibers.

Now we want to show that the surface

S=\bigcup_{(q,m)\in T}L_{q,m}

has bidegree
(a,a)
, where
a=\deg(f_{V}(C))=\deg(L)
.
First of all, thanks to
[
3
, Corollary 8.8]
, it is clear that, if
S\in|\cal{O}_{F}(a,b)|
, then
a=b
.

In order to compute the bidegree of
S
we need to intersect with

\pi^{-1}_{2}(n)
where
n
is a general point of the second
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and represents a general line in the first
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Now

S\cap\pi^{-1}_{2}(n)=\{(p,n):pm=0,pn=0,qn=0,(q,m)\in T\}

Let be
q_{1},\ldots,q_{a}
the
a
points of intersection between the curve
f_{V}(C)
and the line corresponding to
n
.
For any
1\leqslant i\leqslant a
, let
m_{i}
be such that
(q_{i},m_{i})\in T
and set
p_{i}
the intersection of the lines corresponding to
m_{i}
and
n
. Hence it is clear that

S\cap\pi^{-1}_{2}(n)=\{(p_{1},n),\ldots,(p_{a},n)\}
. This proves that the bidegree of
S
is
(a,a)
.
∎

Thanks to Theorem
5.4
and to
[
3
, Theorem 7.2]
, we can state the following corollary.

Corollary 5.5
.

For any
a>0
, there is an integral surface
X\subset{\mathbb{F}}
of bidegree
(a,a)
containing
infinitely many twistor fibers.

We conclude with a topological observation on the set of twistor
fibers contained in a surface containing infinitely many twistor fibers.

Remark 5.6
.

It is well-known that
the real locus
C_{\mathbb{R}}
of a curve
C
of genus
g
consists of
n\leqslant g+1
disjoint circles, see

[
6
, Proposition 3.1]
.
Thus
from the construction in the proof of Theorem
5.4
, we know that, for any
a\geqslant n+1
, there exists
an integral surface
S\in|\cal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(a,a)|
containing
infinitely many twistor fibers and such that the family of twistor fibers is parametrized by
n
disjoint circles,
up to finitely many identifications (all over
real points of
\mathrm{Sing}(C)
) and up to finitely many isolated points (at most
|\mathrm{Sing}(C)|
).

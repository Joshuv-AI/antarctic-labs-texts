Embeddings of semiprime rings into semisimple Artinian rings
V. V. Bavula

Abstract
Goldie’s Theorem implies that a semiprime left Goldie ring is embeddable into a semisimple Artinian ring. On the other hand, there are domains that are not embeddable into division rings. A criterion for a semiprime ring being embeddable into a semisimple Artinian ring is given. Three types of embeddings of semiprime rings into semisimple Artinian rings are introduced and studied: the elementary, natural and collection embeddings. It is proven that for each embedding there is an elementary embedding which is smaller and explicitly constructed. In particular, the minimal embeddings are elementary ones. It is proven that a semiprime ring and all its localizations at regular left Ore sets have the ‘same’ sets of elementary embeddings. A new criterion for the left quotient ring of a ring being a semisimple Artinian ring is given in terms of the centre of the ring which is completely different from Goldie’s Theorem (which is historically the first criterion, [ 7 ] ). It is proven that the minimal embeddings in each Morita equivalence class of embeddings is a non-empty set and they are explicitly described. Descriptions of the minimal embeddings of semiprime left Goldie rings and all their localizations at regular left Ore sets are described. Key Words: monomorphism, semiprime ring, prime ring, semiprimary ring, semisimple Artinian ring, natural embedding, elementary embedding, localization, classical left quotient ring, largest left quotient ring, Ore set, denominator set, order, maximal order. New criterion for a ring to have a semisimple Artinian quotient ring.

A criterion for a ring to have a simple Artinian left quotient ring was given by Goldie (
[
6
]

8
]

[
7
]
, 1960) is a criterion for a ring to have a semisimple Artinian left quotient ring. Goldie’s Theorem states that
a ring has a semisimple left quotient ring iff it is a semiprime left Goldie ring
. A ring is called a

if it satisfies the ascending chain condition on left annihilators and does not contain infinite direct sums of nonzero left ideals. In
[
3
]
, four new criteria are given that are based on a completely different approach and new ideas. The first one is based on the fact that
for an arbitrary ring

the set

of maximal left denominator sets of

is a non-empty set
,
[
4
, Lemma 3.7.(2)]

.

Theorem (The First Criterion,
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
.
A ring

has a semisimple left quotient ring

iff

is a finite set,

and, for each

The Second Criterion,

via the minimal primes of

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
R
be a semiprime ring with
|\min(R)|<\infty
. Then the set
\min(R)
is the only irredundant set that consist of prime ideals of
R
.

Proof.

Evidently, the set
\min(R)
is an irredundant set that consist of prime ideals of
R
.

Suppose that
Q
is an irredundant set that consist of prime ideals of
R
. We have to show that
Q=\min(R)
. Since the set
\min(R)
is an irredundant set of prime ideals, it suffices to show that
Q\supseteq\min(R)
. For each
\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)
,

\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q}\in Q}\mathfrak{q}=\{0\}\subseteq\mathfrak{p},

and so
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
for some
\mathfrak{q}\in Q
. Hence
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}
, by the minimality of
\mathfrak{p}
. Therefore,
Q\supseteq\min(R)
.
∎

Proposition 2.16
.

Suppose that

is a semiprime ring with
|\min(R)|<\infty
. Then

1.

{\cal M}_{R,\mathbb{S}}^{c}\subseteq{\cal N}_{R,\mathbb{S}}\subseteq{\rm Irr}\,{\cal M}_{R,\mathbb{S}}
.

2.

Suppose that
f:R\rightarrow A=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)}A(\mathfrak{p})\in{\cal N}_{R,\mathbb{S}}
. Then

(a)

For all
\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)
,
{\rm l.ann}_{A}(A\mathfrak{p}A)={\rm r.ann}_{A}(A\mathfrak{p}A)=A(\mathfrak{p})
.

(b)

For all
\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)
,
A\mathfrak{p}A=A(1-e_{A(\mathfrak{p})})=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(R)\backslash\{\mathfrak{p}\}}A(\mathfrak{q})
where

is the sum of central orthogonal idempotents of
A
that corresponds to the direct product
A=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)}A(\mathfrak{p})
.

(c)

For all
\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)
,
A/A\mathfrak{p}A\simeq A(\mathfrak{p})
.

(d)

For all
\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)
,
R\cap A\mathfrak{p}A=\mathfrak{p}
and
R/\mathfrak{p}=R/R\cap A\mathfrak{p}A\subseteq A/A\mathfrak{p}A\simeq A(\mathfrak{p})
, i.e. the ring
R/\mathfrak{p}
is a subring of
A(\mathfrak{p})
.

3.

For all
f,f^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{R,\mathbb{S}}
,
{\cal M}_{R,\mathbb{S}}(f,f^{\prime})
consists of ring monomorphisms.

Proof.

1. By Lemma
2.12
.(5),
{\cal M}_{R,\mathbb{S}}^{c}\subseteq{\cal N}_{R,\mathbb{S}}
.

Suppose that
f:R\rightarrow A=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)}A(\mathfrak{p})\in{\cal N}_{R,\mathbb{S}}
.
Then
{\rm r.ann}_{A}(\mathfrak{p})=A(\mathfrak{p})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)
, and so
f\in{\rm Irr}\,{\cal M}_{R,\mathbb{S}}

since for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)
,

\mathfrak{p}^{c}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(R)\backslash\{\mathfrak{p}\}}\mathfrak{q}\neq 0
(by Lemma
2.14
) and
\mathfrak{p}^{c}\subseteq{\rm ker}(f_{\mathfrak{p}})
where

f_{\mathfrak{p}}:R\rightarrow A\rightarrow\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(R)\backslash\{\mathfrak{p}\}}A(\mathfrak{q})

and the second map is the projection epimorphism. Therefore
{\cal N}_{R,\mathbb{S}}\subseteq{\rm Irr}\,{\cal M}_{R,\mathbb{S}}
.

2(a). Since the right annihilator
{\rm r.ann}_{A}(\mathfrak{p})=A(\mathfrak{p})
is an ideal of
A
, we have that
{\rm r.ann}_{A}(A\mathfrak{p}A)=A(\mathfrak{p})
. Since the ring
A
is a semiprime ring,
{\rm l.ann}_{A}(A\mathfrak{p}A)={\rm r.ann}_{A}(A\mathfrak{p}A)=A(\mathfrak{p})
, and the statement (a) follows.

2(b). Since the ring
A=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)}A(\mathfrak{p}
is a finite direct product of simple Artinian rings

, the statement (b) follows from the statement (a).

2(c). The statement (c) follows from the statement (b):
A/A\mathfrak{p}A=A/A(1-e_{A(\mathfrak{p})})\simeq A(\mathfrak{p})
.

2(d). For each
\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)
, the ideal
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}:=R\cap A\mathfrak{p}A
of the ring
R
contains the ideal
\mathfrak{p}
.
Since
f:R\rightarrow A=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)}A(\mathfrak{p})\in{\cal M}_{R,\mathbb{S}}
,

\mathfrak{a}:=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)}\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\subseteq{\rm ker}(f)=\{0\},

and so
\mathfrak{a}=\{0\}
.
For each
\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)
, the inclusion

\mathfrak{a}=\{0\}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, implies that

\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\;\;{\rm for\;some}\;\;\mathfrak{q}\in\min(R).

Since the minimal primes are incomparable, we must have
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. The reverse inclusion also holds. So
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{p}
.

3. Let
f:R\rightarrow A=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)}A(\mathfrak{p})\in{\cal N}_{R,\mathbb{S}}

f^{\prime}:R\rightarrow A^{\prime}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)}A^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})\in{\cal N}_{R,\mathbb{S}}
and
\alpha\in{\cal M}_{R,\mathbb{S}}(f,f^{\prime})

,
A(\mathfrak{p})={\rm r.ann}_{A}(\mathfrak{p})
and
A^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})={\rm r.ann}_{A^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{p})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)
that

where the maps

are monomorphisms for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\min(R)
(since the rings

are simple). Hence, so is the map

.
∎

, where

is a division ring, the natural number

is called the

of
\Lambda
. The matrix size of
\Lambda
is the length of a unique (up to isomorphism) simple left/right
\Lambda
-module. Let
A=\prod_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
be a semisimple Artinian rings where
A_{i}
æsimple Artinian ring. The sum
s(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}s(A_{i})
is called the
matrix size
of

.

Theorem
2.18
is one of the key results of the paper. It captures the essence of the structure of embeddings
{\cal M}_{R,\mathbb{S}}
. It reveals that left
R
-collections play the central role in understanding of the structure of embeddings

. It shows that for each embedding in

there is a smaller embedding which is a collection embedding
\phi_{V}
, and it produces

morphisms in

that proves the smallness. So, the proof is ‘constructive’.

is a semiprime ring.

(ii)
Q(Z(R))\simeq\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}Z(R)_{\mathfrak{q}}

is a finite direct product of fields

Z(R)_{\mathfrak{q}}
: The commutative ring
Z(R)
is semiprime with
|\min(Z(R))|<\infty
. Hence,

{\cal C}_{Z(R)}=Z(R)\backslash\bigcup_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}\mathfrak{q}

and the quotient ring
Q(Z(R))
is a semiprime commutative Artinian ring with

\min(Q(Z(R)))=\{{\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}\mathfrak{q}\,|\,\mathfrak{q}\in\min(R)\}.

Hence, the ring
Q(Z(R))
is a semisimple Artinian ring with
{\rm Spec}(Z(R))=\min(Z(R))
, and so the quotient ring
Q(Z(R))\simeq\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}Z(R)_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a finite direct product of fields.

Let

1=\sum_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}e_{\mathfrak{q}}

be the corresponding sum of central orthogonal idempotents of the ring
Q(Z(R))
.

(iii)
The ring
{\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R
is a semiprime ring:
Since
{\cal C}_{Z(R)}\subseteq{\cal C}_{R}
, the ring
R
is a subring of
{\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{a}
is a nonzero nilpotent ideal of the ring
{\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R
. Then the intersection
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}=R\cap\mathfrak{a}
is a nonzero nilpotent ideal of
R
(since the left
R
-module
R
is an essential submodule of
{\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R
), a contradiction, and the statement (iii) follows.

(iv)
The ring
Q_{l,cl}({\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R)\simeq\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}Q_{l,cl}(R_{\mathfrak{q}})
is a semisimple Artinian ring
:
By the assumption,
{\cal C}_{Z(R)}\subseteq{\cal C}_{R}
. Now,

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle{\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R\simeq{\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}Z(R)\otimes_{Z(R)}R\simeq Q(Z(R))\otimes_{Z(R)}R\simeq\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}Q(Z(R))e_{\mathfrak{q}}R

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}Z(R)_{\mathfrak{q}}e_{\mathfrak{q}}R\simeq\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}Z(R)_{\mathfrak{q}}\otimes_{Z(R)}R\simeq\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}R_{\mathfrak{q}}.

By the statement (iii), the ring
{\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R
is a semiprime ring then so are its direct product components
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
.
Since the rings
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
are semiprime left Goldie rings, the rings
Q_{l,cl}(R_{\mathfrak{q}})
are semisimple Artinian rings, by Goldie’s Theorem. Hence, the finite direct product of these rings,

\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}Q_{l,cl}(R_{\mathfrak{q}})
, is also a semisimple Artinian ring. Finally,

\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}Q_{l,cl}(R_{\mathfrak{q}})\simeq Q_{l,cl}\bigg(\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}R_{\mathfrak{q}}\bigg)\simeq Q_{l,cl}({\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R)\\

and the statement (iv) follows.

(v)
S:=R\cap{\cal C}_{{\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R}\in{\rm Den}_{l}(R,0)

and

S^{-1}R\simeq Q_{l,cl}({\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R)
: The statement (v) is a particular case of
[
4
, Lemma 3.3.(1)]
.

(vi)
Q_{l}(R)\simeq Q_{l,cl}({\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R)\simeq\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}Q_{l,cl}(R_{\mathfrak{q}})

is a semisimple Artinian ring
: Notice that
S_{l}(R)\supseteq S
since
S=R\cap{\cal C}_{{\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R}\in{\rm Den}_{l}(R,0)
(the statement (v)). Then
S^{-1}R\subseteq Q_{l}(R)
. In fact, the equality holds since the ring
S^{-1}R\simeq Q_{l,cl}({\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{-1}R)
is a semisimple Artinian ring, by the statement (iv).

(vii)
Q_{l,cl}(R)\simeq Q_{l}(R)
: The statement (vii) follows from the statement (vi) and Theorem
3.3
.

(1\Rightarrow 2)

(i)
The centre
Z(R)
is a semiprime ring
: Suppose that the centre
Z(R)
is not a semiprime ring. Then it contains a nonzero nilpotent ideal, say
\mathfrak{a}
. Then
{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}\mathfrak{a}
is a nonzero nilpotent ideal of the semisimple Artinian ring
Q_{l,cl}(R)
, a contradiction. Therefore, the centre
Z(R)
is a semiprime ring.

(ii)
|\min(Z(R))|<\infty
: Suppose that
|\min(Z(R))|=\infty
. Then we can choose an infinite set of minimal prime ideals of
Z(R)
, say
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{n},\ldots
. For each natural number
n\geq 1
, the set

S_{n}:=Z(R)\backslash\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{p}_{i}

is a multiplicative subset of
Z(R)
such that the localization
S_{n}^{-1}Z(R)
is a semiprime Artinian commutative ring. Hence, it is a direct product of fields

S_{n}^{-1}Z(R)\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{n}Z(R)_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}.

Let
1=\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{ij}
be the corresponding sum of orthogonal idempotents. Clearly,
S_{n}\in{\rm Den}(Q_{l,cl}(R))
and the left uniform dimension of the ring
S_{n}^{-1}Q_{l,cl}(R)
does not exceed the left uniform dimension of the ring
Q_{l,cl}(R)
which is a natural number. On the other hand, in view of the equality
1=\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{ij}
, the left uniform dimension of the ring
S_{n}^{-1}Q_{l,cl}(R)
cannot be smaller than
n
, a contradiction. Therefore,
|\min(Z(R))|<\infty
.

(iii)
For each
\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))
,
Q_{l,cl}(R_{\mathfrak{q}})\simeq Q_{l}(R_{\mathfrak{q}})\simeq Q_{l,cl}(R)_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a semisimple Artinian ring and
the ring
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a left Goldie ring
:
Notice that

S_{\mathfrak{q}}:=R\backslash\mathfrak{q}\subseteq Z(R)\subseteq R\subseteq Q_{l,cl}(R).

The set
S_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a denominator set of the semisimple Artinian ring
Q_{l,cl}(R)
. Hence, the ring

Q_{l,cl}(R)_{\mathfrak{q}}\simeq Q_{l,cl}(R)/{\rm ass}(S_{\mathfrak{q}})

is a semisimple Artinian ring where
{\rm ass}(S_{\mathfrak{q}}):=\{q\in Q_{l,cl}(R)\,|\,sq=0
for some
s\in S_{\mathfrak{q}}\}
. Since
S_{\mathfrak{q}}\subseteq Z(R)\subseteq Z(Q_{l,cl}(R))
,

Q_{l,cl}(R)_{\mathfrak{q}}\simeq S_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1}{\cal C}_{R}^{-1}R\simeq\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}({\cal C}_{R})^{-1}\Big(S_{\mathfrak{q}}^{-1}R\Big)\simeq\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}({\cal C}_{R})^{-1}R_{\mathfrak{q}}

where
\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}:R\mapsto R_{\mathfrak{q}}
,
r\mapsto\frac{r}{1}
and
\sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}({\cal C}_{R})\in{\rm Den}_{l}(R_{\mathfrak{q}},0)
. Now the statement (iii) follows from Proposition
4.3
.(1).
∎

For a ring
R
such that its centre
Z(R)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}
is a finite direct product of fields
K_{i}
, Corollary
4.4
is a criterion for the ring
Q_{l,cl}(R)
being a semisimple Artinian ring.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
R
be a ring such that its centre
Z(R)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}
is a finite direct product of fields
K_{i}
and
1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
be the corresponding sum of orthogonal idempotents. Then the following statements are equivalent:

1.

The ring
Q_{l,cl}(R)
is a semisimple Artinian ring.

2.

The ring
R
is a semiprime ring and the factor ring
R/Re_{i}
is a left Goldie ring for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.

If one of the equivalent conditions holds then
Q_{l,cl}(R)\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{n}Q_{l,cl}(R/R(1-e_{i}))
,

Q_{l,cl}(R/R(1-e_{i}))\simeq Q_{l,cl}(R)/Q_{l,cl}(R)(1-e_{i})\;\;{\rm for\;all}\;\;i=1,\ldots,n,

and the map
\min(R)\rightarrow\min(Z(R))
,
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\mathfrak{p}^{\rm r}:=\mathfrak{p}\cap Z(R)
is a surjection.

Proof.

By the assumption,
Z(R)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}
is a finite direct product of fields
K_{i}
. Hence
\min(Z(R))=\{\mathfrak{q}_{i}:=Z(R)(1-e_{i})\,|\,i=1,\ldots,n\}=\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\,|\,\lambda_{j}\in K_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
and
\lambda_{i}=0\}
.

In view of Theorem
1.2
, to prove the equivalence of statements 1 and 2, we have to show that
{\cal C}_{Z(R)}\subseteq{\cal C}_{R}
and for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, the ring
R_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}
is a left Goldie ring.

(i)
{\cal C}_{Z(R)}\subseteq{\cal C}_{R}
: Since
Z(R)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}
is a finite direct product of fields
K_{i}
,

{\cal C}_{Z(R)}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}^{\times}={\cal C}_{Z(R)}^{\times},

and so
{\cal C}_{Z(R)}\subseteq R^{\times}\subseteq{\cal C}_{R}
.

(ii)
For each
i=1,\ldots,n
, the ring
Z(R)_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}\simeq Z(R)/Z(R)(1-e_{i})\simeq K_{i}
: Clearly,
Z(R)/Z(R)(1-e_{i})\simeq K_{i}
. Since
e_{i}(1-e_{i})=0
and
\mathfrak{q}_{i}=Z(R)(1-e_{i})
,
e_{i}\not\in\mathfrak{q}_{i}
. It follows from the equality
e_{i}\mathfrak{q}_{i}=0
that

Z(R)_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}\simeq\Big(Z(R)/\mathfrak{q}_{i}\Big)_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}\simeq\Big(K_{i}\Big)_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}=K_{i}.

(iii)
For each
i=1,\ldots,n
,
R_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}\simeq R/R(1-e_{i})
: By the statement (ii),

R_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}\simeq Z(R)_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}\otimes_{Z(R)}R=\Big(Z(R)/Z(R)(1-e_{i})\Big)\otimes_{Z(R)}R\simeq R/R(1-e_{i}).

(iv)
For each
\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))
, the ring
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
is a left Goldie ring
: The statement (iv) follows from the statement (iii) and the equality
\min(Z(R))=\{\mathfrak{q}_{i}=Z(R)(1-e_{i})\,|\,i=1,\ldots,n\}
.

By the statements (i)-(iv) and Theorem
1.2
, statements 1 and 2 are equivalent.

Suppose that statements 1 and 2 are equivalent. Then by Theorem
1.2
,

Q_{l,cl}(R)\simeq\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))}Q_{l,cl}(R_{\mathfrak{q}}),

Q_{l,cl}(R_{\mathfrak{q}})\simeq Q_{l,cl}(R)_{\mathfrak{q}}
for all
\mathfrak{q}\in\min(Z(R))
, and the map
\min(R)\rightarrow\min(Z(R))
,
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\mathfrak{p}^{\rm r}:=\mathfrak{p}\cap Z(R)
is a surjection.
Notice that
R_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}\simeq R/R(1-e_{i})
, and so

Q_{l,cl}(R)\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{n}Q_{l,cl}(R/R(1-e_{i}))
.
Now, for every
i=1,\ldots,n
,

\displaystyle Q_{l,cl}(R/R(1-e_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{l,cl}(R_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}})\simeq Q_{l,cl}(R)_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}\simeq Z(R)_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}\otimes_{Z(R)}Q_{l,cl}(R)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\Big(Z(R)/Z(R)(1-e_{i})\Big)\otimes_{Z(R)}Q_{l,cl}(R)\simeq Q_{l,cl}(R)/Q_{l,cl}(R)(1-e_{i}).

The proof of the corollary is complete.

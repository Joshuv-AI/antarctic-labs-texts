Hyperbolic Distance versus QuasiHyperbolic Distance in Plane Domains
David A. Herron, Jeff Lindquist

Abstract
. We examine Euclidean plane domains with their hyperbolic or quasihyperbolic distance. We prove that the associated metric spaces are quasisymmetrically equivalent if and only if they are bi-Lipschitz equivalent. On the other hand, for Gromov hyperbolic domains, the two corresponding Gromov boundaries are always quasisymmetrically equivalent. Surprisingly, for any finitely connected hyperbolic domain, these two metric spaces are always quasiisometrically equivalent. We construct an example where the spaces are not quasiisometrically equivalent.

2.3 Proposition
.

For any closed set

with

(2)

\mathsf{bp}\leq M

(3)

for any annulus

(4)

The constant

Proof.

It is easy to check that (a) and (c) are equivalent.

]
established the equivalence of (c) and (b), and Pommerenke
[
Pom79
]
,
[
Pom84

demonstrated that these are equivalent to (d) (along with several other variations).

That (e) is equivalent to the other conditions is surely well-known, yet does not seem to be explicitly mentioned in the literature, so we outline an explanation for this.

Suppose

,
R>r>0
, and

\log\frac{R}{r}\leq\log\frac{R\sqrt{4-r^{2}}}{r\sqrt{4-R^{2}}}=\md(D)\leq M\,.

Finally, suppose (e) holds with a constant

. To establish (a), we verify that (
2.2
) holds with the constant

. Let

be an annulus with

; thus

. Assume
R\geq 2r
. Roughly, we exhibit a chordal subannulus
\hat{A}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\subset$\cr\raisebox{0.94722pt}{\hskip 2.84526pt$\scriptstyle\rm a$}\crcr}}}A

to bound

There are three cases depending on whether

|c|\leq r
, or

. Let

\displaystyle a:=\bigl(|c|-r\bigr)\frac{c}{|c|}\,,\;b:=\bigl(|c|+R\bigr)\frac{c}{|c|}\,,\;\hat{r}:=\chi(a,c)\,,\;\hat{R}:=\chi(b,c)\,.

Since
|c|+R\leq 2|c|
and

\displaystyle\frac{1+|b|^{2}}{1+|a|^{2}}=\frac{1+\bigl(|c|+R\bigr)^{2}}{1+\bigl(|c|-r\bigr)^{2}}\leq\frac{1+\bigl(2|c|\bigr)^{2}}{1+\bigl(|c|/2\bigr)^{2}}\leq 16\,.

Thus if

Assume
|c|\leq r

. Let

\displaystyle a:=\bigl(|c|+r\bigr)\frac{c}{|c|}\,,\;b:=\bigl(|c|-R\bigr)\frac{c}{|c|}\,,\;\hat{r}:=\chi(a,0)\,,\;\hat{R}:=\chi(b,0)\,.

\displaystyle\hat{A}:=\Set{\hat{r}<\chi(z,c)<\hat{R}}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\subset$\cr\raisebox{0.94722pt}{\hskip 2.84526pt$\scriptstyle\rm a$}\crcr}}}A\,,\quad\text{whence}\quad\frac{\hat{R}}{\hat{r}}\leq M\,.

We claim that

\displaystyle\sqrt{1+|a|^{2}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}M}{2M-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\sqrt{1+|b|^{2}}\leq\sqrt{2}M\,;

therefore
\displaystyle M\geq\frac{\hat{R}}{\hat{r}}=\frac{|b|}{|a|}\biggl(\frac{1+|a|^{2}}{1+|b|^{2}}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\frac{R/2}{2r}\leq\frac{R-|c|}{r+|c|}=\frac{|b|}{|a|}\leq\biggl(\frac{1+|b|^{2}}{1+|a|^{2}}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}M\leq M(2M-1)

To corroborate the above claim, we utilize chordal subannuli that have

\displaystyle M\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\hat{r}}=\frac{\sqrt{1+|a|^{2}}}{\sqrt{2}|a|}\leq\frac{1+|a|}{2|a|}

and therefore

\displaystyle\sqrt{1+|a|^{2}}\geq\frac{1+|a|}{\sqrt{2}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}M}{2M-1}\,.

If

Thus when

2.C.
Conformal Metrics

A continuous function
X\xrightarrow{\rho}(0,\infty)
on a rectifiably connected metric space
X
induces
a length distance
d_{\rho}
on
X
defined by

d_{\rho}(a,b):=\inf_{\gamma:a\curvearrowright b}\ell_{\rho}(\gamma)\quad\text{where}\quad\ell_{\rho}(\gamma):=\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds

and where the infimum is taken over all rectifiable paths
\gamma:a\curvearrowright b
in
X
. We describe this by calling
\rho\,ds=\rho(x)|dx|
a
conformal metric
on
X
. Below we consider the hyperbolic and quasihyperbolic metrics defined on plane domains.

We call
\gamma
a
\rho
-geodesic
if
d_{\rho}(a,b)=\ell_{\rho}(\gamma)
; these need not be unique. We often write
[a,b]_{\rho}
to indicate a
\rho
-geodesic with endpoints
a,b
, but one must be careful with this notation since these geodesics need not be unique. When
z
is a fixed point on a given geodesic
[a,b]_{\rho}
, we write
[a,z]_{\rho}
to mean the subarc of the given geodesic from
a
to
z
.

We note that the ratio
\rho\,ds/\sigma\,ds
of two conformal metrics is a well-defined positive function. We write
\rho\leq C\,\sigma
to indicate that this metric ratio is bounded above by
C
.

Wen
\rho\,ds
is a conformal metric on
\Omega
, we let
\Omega_{\rho}:=(\Omega,d_{\rho})
. The following is surely folklore, but we briefly outline a proof which employs standard techniques.

2.4 Lemma
.

Let
\rho\,ds
and
\tau\,ds
be conformal metrics on some plane domain
\Omega
. Then the identity map

\Omega_{\rho}\xrightarrow{\id}\Omega_{\tau}\quad\text{is conformal, i.e., it is metrically $1$-QC}.

Also, if
\Omega_{\rho}\xrightarrow{f}\Omega_{\tau}
is
\eta
-QS, then
f
induces a map
\Omega\xrightarrow{f}\Omega
which is
\eta(1)
-QC.

Proof.

The metric spaces
(\Omega,\ed),(\Omega,l),(\Omega,d_{\rho}),(\Omega,d_{\tau})
are all homeomorphic, and even locally bi-Lipschitz. Indeed, given
a\in\Omega
and
0<\varepsilon<\rho(a)/2
, there is an
r_{0}\in(0,\delta(a))
such that

(2.5)

z\in\mathsf{D}[a;r_{0}]\implies\bigl(\rho(a)-\varepsilon\bigr)|z-a|\leq d_{\rho}(z,a)\leq\bigl(\rho(a)+\varepsilon\bigr)|z-a|\,.

Using this we deduce that for all small
\varepsilon>0
and all
r\in(0,r_{0})
(where
r_{0}\in(0,\delta(a))
is chosen as above “for both
d_{\rho}
and
d_{\tau}
”),

\displaystyle L(r):=\sup_{d_{\rho}(z,a)\leq r}d_{\tau}(z,a)\leq\frac{\tau(a)+\varepsilon}{\rho(a)-\varepsilon}\cdot r

and

\displaystyle l(r):=\inf_{d_{\rho}(z,a)\geq r}d_{\tau}(z,a)\geq\frac{\tau(a)-\varepsilon}{\rho(a)+\varepsilon}\cdot r

whence

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{L(r)}{l(r)}\leq\frac{\tau(a)+\varepsilon}{\tau(a)-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{\rho(a)+\varepsilon}{\rho(a)-\varepsilon}\to 1\;\text{as $\varepsilon\to 0^{+}$}\,.

Suppose
\Omega_{\rho}\xrightarrow{f}\Omega_{\tau}
is
\eta
-QS,
a\in\Omega
, and
\varepsilon>0
. Since
f
is a homeomorphism, for all
r\in(0,\delta(a))

\displaystyle L_{f}(r):=\sup_{|z-a|\leq r}|f(z)-f(a)|=\max_{|z-a|=r}|f(z)-f(a)|

and

\displaystyle l_{f}(r):=\inf_{|z-a|\geq r}|f(z)-f(a)|=\min_{|z-a|=r}|f(z)-f(a)|\,.

Below we write
z^{\prime}:=f(z),a^{\prime}:=f(a)
, etc.

As above, pick
r_{0}\in(0,\delta(a))
and
s_{0}\in(0,\delta(a^{\prime}))
so that (
2.5
) holds for
d_{\rho}
and its analog holds for
d_{\tau}
and
w\in\mathsf{D}[a^{\prime};s_{0}]
. Then take
r_{1}\in(0,r_{0}]
so that
f\bigl(\mathsf{D}[a;r_{1}]\bigl)\subset\mathsf{D}(a^{\prime};s_{0})
. Then for all
r\in(0,r_{1})
and all
z,w\in\mathsf{S}^{1}(a;r)
,

\displaystyle\frac{|z^{\prime}-a^{\prime}|}{|w^{\prime}-a^{\prime}|}\leq\frac{\tau(a^{\prime})+\varepsilon}{\tau(a^{\prime})-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{d_{\tau}(z^{\prime},a^{\prime})}{d_{\tau}(w^{\prime},a^{\prime})}\leq\frac{\tau(a^{\prime})+\varepsilon}{\tau(a^{\prime})-\varepsilon}\cdot\eta\biggl(\frac{d_{\tau}(z^{\prime},a^{\prime})}{d_{\tau}(w^{\prime},a^{\prime})}\biggr)\leq\frac{\tau(a^{\prime})+\varepsilon}{\tau(a^{\prime})-\varepsilon}\cdot\eta\biggl(\frac{\rho(a)+\varepsilon}{\rho(a)-\varepsilon}\biggr)\,;

here the inner inequality holds by quasisymmetry and the two outer inequalities follow from repeated applications of (
2.5
). Selecting such
z,w
that attain
L_{f}(r),l_{f}(r)
respectively yields

\displaystyle\frac{L_{f}(r)}{l_{f}(r)}=\frac{|z^{\prime}-a^{\prime}|}{|w^{\prime}-a^{\prime}|}\leq\frac{\tau(a^{\prime})+\varepsilon}{\tau(a^{\prime})-\varepsilon}\cdot\eta\biggl(\frac{\rho(a)+\varepsilon}{\rho(a)-\varepsilon}\biggr)

and so letting
r\to 0^{+}
, then
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we deduce that, with respect to Euclidean distance,
f
is indeed
\eta(1)
-QC.
∎

The careful reader recognizes that in the above, we employ the metric (aka, linear) dilatation for quasiconformal maps, not the geometric dilatation, whereas in Fact
2.12
below

is the geometric dilatation.

2.C.1.
The QuasiHyperbolic Metric

The
quasihyperbolic metric

\delta^{-1}ds
is defined for any proper subdomain
\Omega\subsetneq{\mathbb{C}}
; here
\delta=\delta_{\Omega}
is the Euclidean distance to the boundary of
\Omega
. This metric can be defined in very general metric spaces and has proven useful in many areas of geometric analysis. See
[
BHK01
]
and
[
HRS20
]
.

For domains
\Omega\subsetneq\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}
, we also consider the
chordal quasihyperbolic metric

\chi^{-1}d\hat{s}
and the
spherical quasihyperbolic metric

\sigma^{-1}d\hat{s}
where
d\hat{s}
denotes the chordal (or spherical) arclength “differential”. (Recall the paragraph immediately preceding §
2.A

[
BHK01
, Chapter 7]
.

The
Euclidean, chordal, and spherical quasihyperbolic distances

k=k_{\Omega},k_{\chi}=k_{(\Omega,\chi)}
and
k_{\sigma}=k_{(\Omega,\sigma)}
in
\Omega
are the length distances induced by the Euclidean quasihyperbolic, chordal quasihyperbolic, and spherical quasihyperbolic metrics
\delta^{-1}\,ds,\chi^{-1}\,ds
and
\sigma^{-1}ds
on
\Omega
; here
\Omega\subsetneq{\mathbb{C}}
in the former setting whereas
\Omega\subsetneq\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}
in the latter two. These are geodesic distances.

The Euclidean length space
(\Omega,l)
and spherical length space
(\Omega,l_{\sigma})
also carry quasihyperbolic metrics, but: quasihyperbolic distance in
(\Omega,l)
is Euclidean quasihyperbolic distance and quasihyperbolic distance in

is spherical quasihyperbolic distance.

We remind the reader of the following basic estimates for quasihyperbolic distance, first established by Gehring and Palka
[
GP76
, 2.1]

(2.6a)

\displaystyle k(a,b)\geq\log\left(1+\frac{l(a,b)}{\delta(a)\wedge\delta(b)}\right)\geq\log\left(1+\frac{|a-b|}{\delta(a)\wedge\delta(b)}\right)\geq\left|\log\frac{\delta(a)}{\delta(b)}\right|\,;

where
l(a,b)
is the (intrinsic) length distance between
a
and
b
. The first inequality above is a special case of the more general (and easily proven) inequality

(2.6b)

\displaystyle\ell_{k}(\gamma)\geq\log\Bigl(1+\frac{\ell(\gamma)}{\dist(|\gamma|,{\partial\Omega})}\Bigr)

which holds for any rectifiable path
\gamma
in
\Omega
. See also
[
BHK01
, (2.3),(2.4)]
. There are analogous inequalities for
k_{\chi}
and
k_{\sigma}
where we replace all the Euclidean metric quantities by the appropriate chordal or spherical metric quantities.

It is well known that the holomorphic covering
{\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{\exp}{{\mathbb{C}}}_{\star}

\displaystyle k_{\star}(a,b)=\bigl|\Log(b/a)\bigr|=\bigl|\log|b/a|+i\Arg(b/a)\bigr|

and thus

\displaystyle\bigl|\log\frac{|b|}{|a|}\bigr|\vee\bigl|\Arg\bigl(\frac{b}{a}\bigr)\bigr|\leq k_{\star}(a,b)\leq\bigl|\log\frac{|b|}{|a|}\bigr|+\bigl|\Arg\bigl(\frac{b}{a}\bigr)\bigr|\leq\bigl|\log\frac{|b|}{|a|}\bigr|+\frac{\pi}{2}\,\frac{|a-b|}{|a|\wedge|b|}.

2.C.2.
The Hyperbolic Metric

Every hyperbolic domain in
\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}
carries a unique metric,
\lambda\,ds=\lambda_{\Omega}\,ds
, which enjoys the property that its pullback
p^{*}[\lambda\,ds]
, with respect to any holomorphic universal covering projection
p:{\mathbb{D}}\to\Omega
, is the hyperbolic metric
\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(\zeta)|d\zeta|=2(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{-1}|d\zeta|
on
{\mathbb{D}}
. Another description is that
\lambda\,ds
is the unique maximal (or unique complete) metric on
\Omega
that has constant Gaussian curvature
-1
. In terms of such a covering
p
, the metric-density
\lambda=\lambda_{\Omega}
of the
Poincaré hyperbolic metric

\lambda_{\Omega}\,ds
can be determined from

\lambda(z)=\lambda_{\Omega}(z)=\lambda_{\Omega}(p(\zeta))=2(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{-1}|p^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{-1},

the above being valid for points
z\in\Omega\cap{\mathbb{C}}
whereas one must use local coordinates in any neighborhood of the point at infinity if
\Omega\not\subset{\mathbb{C}}
. (Alternatively, one can use the
chordal hyperbolic metric-density

\hat{\lambda}
and then the hyperbolic metric is
\hat{\lambda}\,\hat{ds}
where
\hat{ds}
denotes the chordal (or spherical) arclength “differential”.)

For example, the hyperbolic metric
\lambda_{*}ds
on the punctured unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}_{*}:={\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{0\}
can be obtained by using the universal covering
z=\exp(w)
from the left-half-plane onto
{\mathbb{D}}_{*}
and we find that

\lambda_{*}(z)|dz|=\frac{|dz|}{|z|\bigl|\log|z|\bigr|}.

The
hyperbolic distance

h=h_{\Omega}
is the length distance
h_{\Omega}:=d_{\lambda}
induced by the hyperbolic metric
\lambda\,ds
on
\Omega
. This is a geodesic distance: for any points
a,b
in
\Omega
, there is an
h
-geodesic
[a,b]_{h}
joining
a,b
in
\Omega
. These geodesics need not be unique, but they enjoy the property that

h(a,b)=\ell_{h}([a,b]_{h}).

Here we are writing

Except for a short list of special cases, the actual calculation of any given hyperbolic metric is notoriously difficult; computing hyperbolic distances and determining hyperbolic geodesics is even harder. Indeed, one can find a number of papers analyzing the behavior of the hyperbolic metric in a twice punctured plane. Typically one is left with estimates obtained by using domain monotonicity and considering ‘nice’ sub-domains and super-domains in which one can calculate, or at least estimate, the metric.

The standard technique for estimating the hyperbolic metric and hyperbolic distance is via domain monotonicity, a consequence of Schwarz’s Lemma. That is, if

h_{\rm in}\geq h\geq h_{\rm out}

2.C.3.
The Beardon-Pommerenke Function
\mathsf{bp}

We desire upper and lower estimates for the hyperbolic metric in terms of the quasihyperbolic metric.
These metrics are 2-bi-Lipschitz equivalent for simply connected hyperbolic plane regions; this is false for any domain with an isolated boundary point (such as the punctured unit disk). The hyperbolic and quasihyperbolic metrics are bi-Lipschitz equivalent precisely when
\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\Omega
is uniformly perfect (see
[
BP78
]
,
[
Pom79
]
,
[
Pom84
]
). Beardon and Pommerenke corroborated this latter assertion as an application of their elegant result
[
BP78
, Theorem 1]
which says:

For any hyperbolic region
\Omega
in
{\mathbb{C}}
and for all
z\in\Omega
,

\frac{1}{\delta(z)\bigl(\mathsf{k}+\mathsf{bp}(z)\bigr)}\leq\lambda(z)\leq\frac{\pi/2}{\delta(z)\,\mathsf{bp}(z)}.

Here the domain function
\Omega\xrightarrow{\mathsf{bp}}{\mathbb{R}}
, introduced by Beardon and Pommerenke, is defined via

\mathsf{bp}(z)=\mathsf{bp}_{\Omega}(z):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\zeta\in\mathsf{B}(z)}\\
\xi\in({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega)\setminus\{\zeta\}\end{subarray}}\biggl|\log\Bigl|\frac{\zeta-z}{\zeta-\xi}\Bigr|\biggr|\,;

note that the infimum is restricted to nearest boundary points
\zeta\in\mathsf{B}(z)={\partial\Omega}\cap{\partial D}(z)
for
z
(that is,
\zeta\in{\partial\Omega}
with
\delta(z)=|z-\zeta|
). Also,

\mathsf{k}:=\bigl(\lambda_{01}(-1)\bigr)^{-1}=\Gamma^{4}(1/4)/\bigl(4\pi^{2}\bigr)=4.3768796\dots.

The definition of
\mathsf{bp}
is motivated by examining the standard lower bound for the hyperbolic metric on a twice punctured plane. The (
BP
) inequalities follow via domain monotonicity: the upper bound for
\lambda(z)
holds because
z
lies on the conformal center of a certain annulus in
\Omega
, and the lower bound holds because
\Omega
lies in a certain twice punctured plane.

A geometric interpretation for
\mathsf{bp}(z)
is seen by defining
\mathsf{bp}(z,\zeta)
—for points
z\in\Omega
and
\zeta\in\mathsf{B}(z)
—as follows:

\mathsf{bp}(z,\zeta):=\inf_{\xi\in({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega)\setminus\{\zeta\}}\biggl|\log\Bigl|\frac{\zeta-z}{\zeta-\xi}\Bigr|\biggr|.\qquad\text{(Thus, $\displaystyle\mathsf{bp}(z)=\inf_{\zeta\in\mathsf{B}(z)}\mathsf{bp}(z,\zeta)$)}.

We find that
2\mathsf{bp}(z,\zeta)
is the conformal modulus of the maximal Euclidean annulus that is contained in
\Omega
and symmetric with respect to the circle
\mathsf{S}^{1}(\zeta;\delta(z))
. It follows that
2\mathsf{bp}(z)
is the minimum of these numbers; so,
2\mathsf{bp}(z)
is the smallest of these maximal moduli.

Thus we see that whenever
\mathsf{bp}(z)>0
, there is an annulus

\displaystyle\mathsf{BP}(z):=\mathsf{A}(\zeta;\delta(z),\mathsf{bp}(z))\in{\mathcal{A}}^{1}_{\Omega}

associated with
z
; here
\zeta\in\mathsf{B}(z)
is any nearest boundary point for
z
that realizes
\mathsf{bp}(z)
, and

\displaystyle\forall\;\xi\in\partial\mathsf{BP}(z)\cap{\partial\Omega}\,,\quad\mathsf{bp}(z)=\mathsf{bp}(z,\zeta)=\biggl|\log\Bigl|\frac{\zeta-z}{\zeta-\xi}\Bigr|\biggr|.

We call
\mathsf{BP}(z)
a
Beardon-Pommerenke annulus
(or briefly, a
BP
annulus
) associated with the point
z
; it needn’t be unique.

Typically, the Beardon-Pommerenke inequalities (
BP
) are employed to give lower estimates for hyperbolic distance, but they can also provide useful upper estimates.

2.15 Example
.

Let
0<a<b
with
\log(b/a)>2
. Put
\Omega:={\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0,-a,-b\}
. Then

h(a,b)\leq 4+\pi\log\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{b}{a}\Bigr)\,.

Proof.

Let
s:=\sqrt{ab}
. Evidently, for
x\in[a,b]
,
\delta(x)=x
and

\displaystyle\mathsf{bp}(x)=\bigl|\log\frac{x}{a}\bigr|\wedge\bigl|\log\frac{b}{x}\bigr|\,.

Thus by (
BP
)

\displaystyle h(ae,s)\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{ae}^{s}\frac{dx}{x\log(x/a)}=\frac{\pi}{2}\log\log\bigl(\frac{b}{a}\bigr)^{1/2}

and similarly,
h(s,b/e)\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\log\log(b/a)^{1/2}
. Hence

\displaystyle h(a,b)\leq 2k(a,ae)+h(ae,b/e)+2k(b/e,b)\leq 4+\pi\log\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{b}{a}\Bigr)

as claimed.
∎

2.D.
Gromov Hyperbolicity and Uniformity

Thanks to the ground-breaking work in
[
BHK01
]
, we know that Gromov hyperbolicity and uniformity are intimately connected.

Roughly speaking, a metric space is
uniform
when points in it can be joined by paths which are not “too long” and which “move away” from the region’s boundary. More precisely,
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is
C
-uniform
(for some constant
C\geq 1
) provided each pair of points can be joined by a
C
-uniform arc. Here a rectifiable arc
\gamma:a\curvearrowright b
is a
C
-uniform arc
if and only if it is both a
C
-quasiconvex arc
and a
double
C
-cone arc
; these conditions mean, respectively, that

(2.18a)

\displaystyle\ell(\gamma)\leq C|a-b|

and

(2.18b)

\displaystyle\forall\;z\in|\gamma|\,,\quad\ell(\gamma[z,a])\wedge\ell(\gamma[z,b])\leq C\delta(z)\,.

Martio and Sarvas introduced the notion of a uniform domain in
[
MS79
]
, and this has proven to be invaluable in geometric function theory and especially for the “analysis in metric spaces” program. A simply connected proper subdomain of the plane is uniform if and only if it is a quasidisk. Each uniform domain has the Sobolev extension property, and the BMO extension property characterizes uniformity. See
[
Geh82
]
and the many references therein, especially
[
Jon81
,
Jon80
]
.

A geodesic metric space
X
is
Gromov hyperbolic
if there exists a constant
\theta\geq 0
such that every geodesic triangle is
\theta
-thin
, meaning that each point on any edge of the triangle is at distance at most
\theta
from the other two edges. See
[
BHK01
, Chapter 3]
,
[
BH99
]
,
[
BBI01
]
, or
[
Väi05
]
and the many references in these.

The
Gromov boundary

\partial_{G}X
of a Gromov hyperbolic space
X
is the set of equivalence classes of geodesic rays, where two rays are equivalent if and only if their Hausdorff distance is finite. One can also use quasi-geodesic rays, or, Gromov sequences. There is no canonical preferred distance on the Gromov boundary. However, for each
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
(usually
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\theta):=1\wedge(1/5\theta)
) there is a so-called
visual distance

d_{\varepsilon}=d_{\varepsilon,o}
on
\partial_{G}X
that satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\exp\bigl(-\varepsilon(\xi|\eta)_{o}\bigr)\leq d_{\varepsilon}(\xi,\eta)\leq\exp\bigl(-\varepsilon(\xi|\eta)_{o}\bigr)

for all
\xi,\eta\in\partial_{G}X
, where
(\xi|\eta)_{o}
is the usual Gromov product and
o\in X
a fixed base point. Standard estimates then give

\displaystyle C^{-1}\exp\Bigl(-\varepsilon\,\dist(o,(\xi,\eta)\bigr)\Bigr)\leq d_{\varepsilon}(\xi,\eta)\leq C\exp\Bigl(-\varepsilon\,\dist(o,(\xi,\eta)\bigr)\Bigr)

where
(\xi,\eta)
is any geodesic line in
X
with endpoints
\xi,\eta\in\partial_{G}X
.

The
conformal gauge on
\partial_{G}X
is the maximal collection of all distance functions on
\partial_{G}X
that are quasisymmetrically equivalent to some (hence all) visual distance(s).

3.C.2.
Quasiisometric Equivalence of Punctured Disks

Here we present some technical details that allow us to streamline our proof of Theorem

and focus on the underlying ideas. Roughly, we show that whenever
\Delta_{*}
is a punctured disk in a hyperbolic plane domain
\Omega
(with the puncture a point of
\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\Omega
), then
(\Delta_{*},h)
and
(\Delta_{*},k)
are quasiisometrically equivalent.

We use the following numerical result whose proof is left for the diligent reader.

(3.11)

\forall\;K>0,L>0,x>0,y\geq L\,:\quad\frac{K+x}{K+y}\geq\frac{L}{K+L}\frac{x}{y}\,.

We require a result similar to Lemma

r_{q}\leq\frac{1}{2}R
, then
|q-c|-r_{q}\geq\frac{1}{2}R
, and if
r_{q}\geq\frac{1}{2}R
, then by (

),
|q-c|-r_{q}\geq r_{q}\geq\frac{1}{2}R
; thus, in both cases
\Delta_{q}\cap\mathsf{D}(c;\frac{1}{2}R)=\emptyset
. Also, if
\infty\in\Pi
, then
|c|+R\leq|c|+|q-c|\leq\frac{3}{4}r_{\infty}
, so here
\Delta_{\infty}\cap\mathsf{D}(c;R)=\emptyset
.

It remains to examine the case
\Pi_{\rm out}=\{\infty\}
and
A\cap\Delta_{\infty}\neq\emptyset
. Here we have no estimates for
r_{\infty}
, but
\mathsf{S}^{1}(A)\cap\Delta=\emptyset
means that
\Delta_{\infty}\cap\mathsf{D}[c;\sqrt{rR}]=\emptyset
. Also, in this setting we must have
A_{\rm out}\cap({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega_{\Pi})=\emptyset
. (If there were a point
\zeta\in A_{\rm out}\cap({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega_{\Pi})
, then by (
3.10c
),
R\leq|\zeta-c|\leq|\zeta|+|c|\leq\frac{1}{2}r_{\infty}
, which would give
A\subset\mathsf{D}[0;\frac{3}{4}r_{\infty}]
contradicting
A\cap\Delta_{\infty}\neq\emptyset
.)

We can use the above to verify that
\mathsf{bp}_{\Pi}\leq M+2
in
\Omega_{\Delta}
which tells us that
\lambda_{\Pi}\,ds
and
\delta_{\Pi}^{-1}\,ds
are
(\mathsf{k}+M+2)
-bi-Lipschitz equivalent in
\Omega_{\Delta}
. However, if
\gamma:a\curvearrowright b
is either a hyperbolic or a quasihyperbolic geodesic in
\Omega_{\Pi}
with endpoints
a,b\in\Omega_{\Delta}
, then
\gamma
may leave
\Omega_{\Delta}
. Nonetheless, the ABC property (see §
2.C.4
) ensures that
\gamma
cannot enter too deep into
\Delta\setminus\Pi=\Omega_{\Pi}\setminus\Omega_{\Delta}
. In particular, if
\gamma
enters some
\Delta_{p}
, then by Fact
2.17
(a),
|\gamma|\cap\mathsf{D}[p;e^{-6\mathsf{k}}r_{p}]=\emptyset
when
p\neq\infty
and
|\gamma|\subset\mathsf{D}(0;e^{6\mathsf{k}}r_{\infty})
when
p=\infty
.

Our final task is to corroborate that
\mathsf{bp}_{\Pi}\lesssim 1
in
\Omega_{\tilde{\Delta}}:=\Omega\setminus{\tilde{\Delta}}
where
\tilde{\Delta}:=\bigcup_{p\in\Pi}\tilde{\Delta}_{p}
and

\tilde{\Delta}_{p}:=\begin{cases}\mathsf{D}[p;e^{-6\mathsf{k}}r_{p}]\quad&\text{if $p\in\Pi^{\star}$}\,,\\
\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\mathsf{D}(0;e^{6\mathsf{k}}r_{\infty})&\text{if $p=\infty\in\Pi$}\,.\end{cases}

To this end, let
a\in\Omega_{\tilde{\Delta}}
and pick
c\in\partial\Omega_{\Pi}
with

A:=\mathsf{BP}_{\Pi}(a)=\mathsf{A}(c,d;m)=\Set{de^{-m}<}{z-c|<de^{m}}=\Set{r<}{z-c|<R}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\Pi}

where
d:=\delta_{\Pi}(a)=|a-c|
and
\mathsf{bp}_{\Pi}(a)=m=\frac{1}{2}\log(R/r)
. Recall (
3.15
) and the notation
\Pi_{\rm in},\Pi_{\rm out}
. We assume
\mathsf{S}^{1}(A)\cap\Delta\neq\emptyset
, so
\Upsilon:=\Set{p\in\Pi}{\mathsf{S}^{1}(A)\cap\Delta_{p}\neq\emptyset}\neq\emptyset
.

We consider several cases. If
p\in\Upsilon\cap\Pi_{\rm in}\setminus\{c\}
, then by (
3.15
)

\displaystyle|p-c|\leq r\quad\text{and}\quad d\leq|p-c|+r_{p}\leq\frac{3}{2}|p-c|\,,

so

\displaystyle\sqrt{R/r}=\frac{d}{r}\leq\frac{3}{2}\quad\text{whence}\quad\frac{R}{r}\leq\frac{9}{4}<3\,.

If
p\in\Upsilon\cap\Pi_{\rm out}^{\star}
, then again by (
3.15
)

\displaystyle|p-c|\geq R\quad\text{and}\quad d+r_{p}\geq|p-c|\,,\quad\text{so}\quad d\geq\frac{1}{2}|p-c|

and therefore

\displaystyle\sqrt{R/r}=\frac{R}{d}\leq 2\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{R}{r}\leq 4\,.

Thus in these two easy cases we have
\mathsf{bp}_{\Pi}(a)=\frac{1}{2}\md(A)\leq\log 2
.

It remains to deal with the case
\Upsilon\cap\{c,\infty\}\neq\emptyset
; here
\mathsf{S}^{1}(A)\cap\Delta_{q}=\emptyset
for all
q\in\Pi^{\star}\setminus\{c\}
. Roughly speaking, we exhibit a concentric subannulus
A^{\prime}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\subset$\cr\raisebox{0.94722pt}{\hskip 2.84526pt$\scriptstyle\rm c$}\crcr}}}A
with
\md A^{\prime}\simeq\md A
and
\mathsf{S}^{1}(A^{\prime})\cap\Delta=\emptyset
. It then follows from earlier work that
\md A\lesssim M
.

Suppose
c\in\Upsilon
. Then
\Upsilon=\Set{c}
,
r_{c}\geq d
, and
R\geq 2r_{c}
(because
c
is an isolated point of
{\partial\Omega}_{\Pi}
nearest to
a
, so
\partial_{\rm out}A\cap{\partial\Omega}_{\Pi}\neq\emptyset
). If
R\leq 2r_{c}e^{m/2}
, then as
a\notin\tilde{\Delta}_{p}
,

\displaystyle 2r_{c}\geq Re^{-m/2}=de^{m/2}=|a-c|e^{m/2}\geq e^{-6\mathsf{k}}r_{c}e^{m/2}

whence

\displaystyle\mathsf{bp}(a)=m\leq 12\mathsf{k}+\log 4\,.

Assume
R\geq 2r_{c}e^{m/2}
. We claim that
r^{\prime}:=(2r_{c})^{2}/R\in[r,d]
. Therefore

\displaystyle A^{\prime}:=\Set{z\in{\mathbb{C}}}{r^{\prime}<|z-c|<R}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\subset$\cr\raisebox{0.94722pt}{\hskip 2.84526pt$\scriptstyle\rm c$}\crcr}}}A\quad\text{is a concentric subannulus of $A$}

with
\mathsf{S}^{1}(A^{\prime})=\mathsf{S}^{1}(c;2r_{c})
. From (
3.10
),
\mathsf{S}^{1}(A^{\prime})\cap\Delta=\emptyset
, so by earlier work,

\displaystyle\mathsf{bp}(a)=\frac{1}{2}\md(A)=\log\frac{R}{d}\leq\log\frac{R}{r^{\prime}}=\md(A^{\prime})\leq 2M+\log 4\,.

To check the claim, note that

\displaystyle r=\frac{d^{2}}{R}\leq\frac{r_{c}^{2}}{R}\leq r^{\prime}=\frac{(2r_{c})^{2}}{R}\leq\frac{(Re^{-m/2})^{2}}{R}=Re^{-m}=d\,.

Suppose
\infty\in\Upsilon
. Here
\Upsilon=\Set{\infty}
,
\frac{3}{4}r_{\infty}\leq d\leq Cr_{\infty}
where
C:=e^{6\mathsf{k}}+\frac{1}{4}
, and,
r\leq\frac{1}{2}r_{\infty}
(because
\partial_{\rm in}A\cap{\partial\Omega}_{\Pi}\neq\emptyset
). If
r\geq\frac{1}{2}r_{\infty}e^{-m/2}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}r_{\infty}\leq re^{m/2}=de^{-m/2}\leq Cr_{\infty}e^{-m/2}

whence

\displaystyle\mathsf{bp}(a)=m\leq 12\mathsf{k}+4\,.

Assume
r\leq\frac{1}{2}r_{\infty}e^{-m/2}
. We claim that
R^{\prime}:=(\frac{1}{2}r_{\infty})^{2}/r\in[d,\frac{4}{9}R]
. Therefore

\displaystyle A^{\prime}:=\Set{z\in{\mathbb{C}}}{r<|z-c|<R^{\prime}}\mathrel{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\subset$\cr\raisebox{0.94722pt}{\hskip 2.84526pt$\scriptstyle\rm c$}\crcr}}}A\quad\text{is a concentric subannulus of $A$}

with
\mathsf{S}^{1}(A^{\prime})=\mathsf{S}^{1}(c;\frac{1}{2}r_{\infty})
. From (
3.10c
),
\mathsf{S}^{1}(A^{\prime})\cap\Delta=\emptyset
, so by earlier work,

\displaystyle\mathsf{bp}(a)=\frac{1}{2}\md(A)=\log\frac{d}{r}\leq\log\frac{R^{\prime}}{r}=\md(A^{\prime})\leq 2M+\log 4\,.

To check the claim, note that

\displaystyle d=re^{m}=\frac{(re^{m/2})^{2}}{r}\leq\frac{(\frac{1}{2}r_{\infty})^{2}}{r}=R^{\prime}\leq\frac{(\frac{2}{3}d)^{2}}{r}=\frac{4rR}{9r}=\frac{4}{9}R\,.

Having established that
\mathsf{bp}_{\Pi}\leq M^{\prime}:=\max\{2M+\log 4,12\mathsf{k}+4\}
in
\Omega_{\tilde{\Delta}}
, we now know that
\lambda_{\Pi}\,ds
and
\delta_{\Pi}^{-1}\,ds
are
K
-bi-Lipschitz equivalent in
\Omega_{\tilde{\Delta}}
with
K:=K(M)=\mathsf{k}+M^{\prime}
. As explained above, any hyperbolic or quasihyperbolic geodesic in
\Omega_{\Pi}
with endpoints in
\Omega_{\Delta}
lies in
\Omega_{\tilde{\Delta}}
. Therefore the identity map
(\Omega_{\Pi},h_{\Pi})\to(\Omega_{\Pi},k_{\Pi})
is
K
-bi-Lipschitz.
∎

3.D.
Proof of Theorem

Here we concoct a plane domain

3.16 Lemma
.

Let
(a_{n})_{0}^{\infty}
be a strictly increasing sequence in
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{\infty\}
with
a_{n+1}/a_{n}\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
. Put
A:=\Set{a_{n}}{n\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}\subset\hat{{\mathbb{R}}}
. Then every QS homeomorphism
f:A\to A
is “eventually the identity”; i.e., there is an
N
such that for all
n\geq N
,
f(a_{n})=a_{n}
.
12
12

12

Here we use the chordal distance from
\hat{{\mathbb{R}}}
.

Proof.

To start, note that as
f
is a homeomorphism, it is a bijection and
f(\infty)=\infty
(as
\infty
is the only non-isolated point of
A
).

We exhibit
p,q
such that for all
n\geq 1
,
f(a_{p+n})=a_{q+n}
. Then
f
maps
\{a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{p}\}
bijectively onto
\{a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{q}\}
. Hence
p=q
and our claim is established.

We need only produce a
p
such that for all
n\geq p
,
f(a_{n})<f(a_{n+1})
with
f(a_{n})
and
f(a_{n+1})

adjacent
(meaning that if
f(a_{n})=a_{m}
, then
f(a_{n+1})=a_{m+1}
). Indeed, given such a
p
we simply let
q
be the unique integer with
a_{q}=f(a_{p})
.

Below we use the quasisymmetry of
f
to verify that there is an
N
such that for all
n\geq N
,
f(a_{n})<f(a_{n+1})
. Let
M
be the unique integer with
a_{M}=f(a_{N})
. Thus for all
n\geq N
,
a_{M}\leq f(a_{n})<f(a_{n+1})
.

We claim that there are a finite number of
m\geq M
such that
a_{m}\notin f\bigl(\Set{a_{n}}{n\geq N}\bigr)
. Indeed, if
m
is such, then
a_{m}\in f\bigl(\{a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{N-1}\}\bigr)
, so, there are at most
N
such
m
. Let
\ell
be the
largest
of all these
m
.

Thus there is a
p>N
such that
a_{M}\leq f(a_{p-1})<a_{\ell}<f(a_{p})
, but for all
n\geq p
,
f(a_{n})
and
f(a_{n+1})
are adjacent. This is the sough after
p
.

To produce
N
, assume
f
is
\eta
-QS. Pick
\tau\in(0,1)
so that
t\in(0,\tau)\implies\eta(t)<\frac{1}{2}
. Since

\displaystyle\frac{\chi(a_{n+1},\infty)}{\chi(a_{n},\infty)}\leq 2\frac{1+a_{n}}{1+a_{n+1}}\to 0\,,

there exists an
N
such that for all

,

\displaystyle t:=\frac{\chi(a_{n+1},\infty)}{\chi(a_{n},\infty)}<\tau\,.

Then for such
n
,

\displaystyle\frac{\chi(fa_{n+1},f\infty)}{\chi(fa_{n},f\infty)}\leq\eta(t)<\frac{1}{2}

whence

\displaystyle\frac{4}{1+f(a_{n+1})}\leq 2\chi(fa_{n+1},\infty)\leq\chi(fa_{n},\infty)\leq\frac{4}{1+f(a_{n})}

and therefore
f(a_{n})<f(a_{n+1})
as asserted.

3.17.

Let
(a_{n})_{1}^{\infty}
be a strictly decreasing sequence in

as

,
A:=\Set{a_{n}}{n\geq 0}
, and
\Omega:={\mathbb{C}}\setminus A
. We verify that the assertions of Theorem

hold for
\Omega
.

13

13

hold for
\Pi=A

Let
h_{\varepsilon}=h_{\varepsilon,o}
and

denote the standard visual distances on

Thanks to

\partial_{G}(\Omega,k)

Given a boundary point

\displaystyle C^{-1}e^{-\varepsilon d(x_{n},1)}\leq d_{\varepsilon}(a_{n},\zeta)\leq Ce^{-\varepsilon d(x_{n},1)}\,,

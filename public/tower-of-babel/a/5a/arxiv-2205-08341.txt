Cohomology and formal deformations of n n -Hom-Lie color algebras
K. Abdaoui , R. Gharbi , S. Mabrouk and A. Makhlouf 1. University of Sfax, Faculty of Sciences Sfax, BP 1171, 3038 Sfax, Tunisia2. University of Gafsa, Faculty of Sciences Gafsa, 2112 Gafsa, Tunisia3. Université de Haute Alsace, IRIMAS - Département de Mathématiques,18, rue des frères Lumière, F-68093 Mulhouse, France

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to provide a cohomology of n n -Hom-Lie color algebras governing one parameter formal deformations. Then, we study formal deformations of a n n -Hom-Lie color algebra and introduce the notion of Nijenhuis operator on an n n -Hom-Lie color algebra, which could give rise to infinitesimally trivial ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -order deformations. Furthermore, in connection with Nijenhuis operators we introduce and discuss the notion of a product structure on n n -Hom-Lie color algebras.

n
-Hom-Lie color algebra, cohomology, deformation, Nijenhuis operator, almost structure, product structure.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon(X,Y_{i})\big[\alpha(y_{1}),\cdots,\alpha(y_{i-1}),[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,y_{i}],\alpha(y_{i+1}),\cdots,\alpha(y_{n})\big],

Theorem 3.1

Let

-Hom-Lie color algebra and

Moreover, suppose that
(\mathfrak{g}^{{}^{\prime}},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]^{{}^{\prime}},\varepsilon,\alpha^{\prime})
is a
n
-Hom-Lie color algebra and
\beta^{{}^{\prime}}:\mathfrak{g}^{{}^{\prime}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{{}^{\prime}}
is a

-Hom-Lie color algebra endomorphism. If

is a

-Hom-Lie color algebra morphism that satisfies

is a morphism of
n
-Hom-Lie color algebras.

Proof.

Obviously
[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]_{\beta}
is a
\varepsilon
-skew-symmetric. Furthermore,
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]_{\beta},\varepsilon,\beta\circ\alpha)
satisfies the
\varepsilon
-

-Hom-Jacobi identity
(
2.2
)
, indeed

\displaystyle[\beta\circ\alpha(x_{1}),\cdots,\beta\circ\alpha(x_{n-1}),[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]_{\beta}]_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta^{2}([\alpha(x_{1}),\cdots,\alpha(x_{n-1}),[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon(X,Y_{i})\beta^{2}(\big[\alpha(y_{1}),\cdots,\alpha(y_{i-1}),[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,y_{i}]\alpha(y_{i+1}),\cdots,\alpha(y_{n})\big])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon(X,Y_{i})\big[\beta\circ\alpha(y_{1}),\cdots,\beta\circ\alpha(y_{i-1}),[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,y_{i}]_{\beta},\beta\circ\alpha(y_{i+1}),\cdots,\beta\circ\alpha(y_{n})\big]_{\beta}.

ad_{\tilde{\alpha}(X)}\cdot[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon(X,Y_{i})[\alpha(y_{1}),\cdots,\alpha(y_{i-1}),ad_{X}\cdot y_{i},\cdots,\alpha(y_{n})].

(3.4)

Proposition 3.2
.

With the above notations,
the map

satisfies for all

and

\displaystyle[\tilde{\alpha}(X),[Y,Z]_{\alpha}]_{\alpha}-\varepsilon(X,Y)[\tilde{\alpha}(Y),[X,Z]_{\alpha}]_{\alpha}=[[X,Y]_{\alpha},\tilde{\alpha}(Z)]_{\alpha}.

(3.6)

Then, we have

\displaystyle[\tilde{\alpha}(X),[Y,Z]_{\alpha}]_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{j<i}^{n-1}\varepsilon(X,Z_{j})\varepsilon(Y,Z_{i})(\alpha^{2}(z_{1}),\ldots,\alpha(ad_{X}z_{j}),\ldots,\alpha(ad_{Y}z_{i}),\ldots,\alpha^{2}(z_{n-1}))

\displaystyle[\tilde{\alpha}(Y),[X,Z]_{\alpha}]_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{j<i}^{n-1}\varepsilon(Y,Z_{j})\varepsilon(X,Z_{i})(\alpha^{2}(z_{1}),\ldots,\alpha(ad_{Y}z_{j}),\ldots,\alpha(ad_{X}z_{i}),\ldots,\alpha^{2}(z_{n-1}))

\displaystyle+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{j>i}^{n-1}\varepsilon(Y,X)\varepsilon(Y,Z_{j})\varepsilon(X,Z_{i})(\alpha^{2}(z_{1}),\ldots,\alpha(ad_{Y}z_{i}),\ldots,\alpha(ad_{X}z_{j}),\ldots,\alpha^{2}(z_{n-1}))

\displaystyle+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon(X+Y,Z_{i})(\alpha^{2}(z_{1}),\ldots,ad_{\tilde{\alpha}(Y)}\cdot ad_{X}z_{i}),\ldots,\alpha^{2}(z_{n-1})).

So, the right hand side of Eq.(

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon(X+Y,Z_{i})\Big(\alpha^{2}(z_{1}),\ldots,(ad_{\tilde{\alpha}(X)}\cdot ad_{y}z_{i})-\varepsilon(X,Y)ad_{\tilde{\alpha}(Y)}\cdot ad_{X}z_{i})),\ldots,\alpha^{2}(z_{n-1})\Big).

(3.7)

The left hand side of Eq.(

\displaystyle[[X,Y]_{\alpha},\tilde{\alpha}(Z)]_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon(X,Y_{j})\varepsilon(X,Z_{i})\varepsilon(Y,Z_{i})(\alpha^{2}(z_{1}),\ldots,[\alpha(y_{1}),\ldots,ad_{X}z_{j},\ldots,\alpha(y_{n-1}),\alpha(z_{i})],\ldots,\alpha^{2}(z_{n-1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon(X+Y,Z_{i})\Big(\alpha^{2}(z_{1}),\ldots,\alpha^{2}(z_{i-1}),ad_{[X,Y]_{\alpha}}\alpha(z_{i}),\ldots,\alpha^{2}(z_{n-1})\Big)

by Eq. (

Definition 3.2

\displaystyle\rho(\tilde{\alpha}(X))\circ\mu=\mu\circ\rho(X),

\displaystyle\rho(\alpha(x_{1}),\cdots,\alpha(x_{n-1}))\circ\rho(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1})-\varepsilon(X,Y)\rho(\alpha(y_{1}),\cdots,\alpha(y_{n-1}))\circ\rho(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon(X,Y_{i})\rho(\alpha(y_{1}),\cdots,\alpha(y_{i-1}),ad_{X}(y_{i}),\cdots,\alpha(y_{n-1}))\circ\mu,

Recall that, if
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\varepsilon,\alpha)

be a Hom-Lie color algebra. The Nijenhuis operator condition is written as

\displaystyle[\mathcal{N}x,\mathcal{N}y]=\mathcal{N}[\mathcal{N}x,y]+\mathcal{N}[x,\mathcal{N}y]-\mathcal{N}^{2}[x,y].

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a Nijenhuis operator on
3
-Hom-Lie color algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot],\varepsilon,\alpha)
. if
\mathcal{N}
is a bijection, then it is a Nijenhuis operator on the Hom-Lie color algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{\cdot,\cdot\},\varepsilon,\alpha)
such that
\mathcal{N}(a)\in Z(\mathfrak{g})
.

6.2
Product structures on
n
-Hom-Lie color algebras

In this subsection, we study the notion of product structure on a
n
-Hom-Lie color algebra and show that it leads to a special decomposition of the original
n
-Hom-Lie color algebra. Moreover, we introduce
the notion of strict product structure on a
n
-Hom-Lie color algebra and provide example.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot],\varepsilon,\alpha)
be a
n
-Hom-Lie color algebra.

An
almost product structure
on
\mathcal{G}
is a linear map of degree zero
\mathcal{P}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
,
(\mathcal{P}\neq\pm Id{g})
, satisfying
\mathcal{P}^{2}=Id{g}
.

An almost product structure is called
product stucture
on
\mathcal{G}
if it is a Nijenhuis operator.

Remark 6.1
.

One can understand a product structure on
\mathcal{G}

as a linear map of degree zero
\mathcal{P}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{2}=Id,\ \ \ \ \mathcal{P}\alpha=\alpha\mathcal{P},

\displaystyle[\mathcal{P}x_{1},\mathcal{P}x_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{P}x_{n}]

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-j-1}\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{n-j}}(\sumop\displaylimits_{i_{1}<i_{2}\ldots<i_{j}}^{n}[\ldots,\mathcal{P}x_{i_{1}},\ldots,\mathcal{P}x_{i_{j}},\ldots])

(6.11)

for
x_{1},...,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
and

\mu_{k}=\begin{cases}1~~~~if~~~~k~is~odd\\
0~~~~if~~~~k~is~even.\end{cases}

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot],\varepsilon,\alpha)
be a
n
-Hom-Lie color algebra. Then
\mathcal{G}
has a product structure if and
only if
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}
admits a decomposition

\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}=\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma_{+}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma_{-}},

where the eigenspaces
\mathfrak{g}_{+}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma_{+}}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma_{-}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
associated to the eigenvalues
1
and
-1
respectively are subalgebras.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a product structure on
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot],\varepsilon,\alpha)
. According to Eq. (
6.11
), for all element
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
we have:

\displaystyle[x_{1},...,x_{n}]

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{P}x_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}x_{n}]

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-j-1}\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{n-j}}(\sumop\displaylimits_{i_{1}<i_{2}\ldots<i_{j}}[\ldots,\mathcal{P}x_{i_{1}},\ldots,\mathcal{P}x_{i_{2}},\ldots,\mathcal{P}x_{i_{j}},\ldots])

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-j-1}\binom{n}{j}\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{n-j}}[x_{1},...,x_{n}].

(6.12)

Then, we have :

\displaystyle[x_{1},...,x_{n}]=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{2j+1<n}\binom{n}{2j+1}\mathcal{P}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]-\sumop\displaylimits_{2j<n}\binom{n}{2j}[x_{1},...,x_{n}],\quad\text{if}\ n\ \text{is\ even},

(6.13)

\displaystyle[x_{1},...,x_{n}]=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{2j<n}\binom{n}{2j}\mathcal{P}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]-\sumop\displaylimits_{2j+1<n}\binom{n}{2j+1}[x_{1},...,x_{n}],\quad\text{if}\ n\ \text{is\ odd}.

(6.14)

By the binomial theorem, we have

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{2j<n}\binom{n}{2j}-\sumop\displaylimits_{2j+1<n}\binom{n}{2j+1}=(-1)^{n+1}.

(6.15)

Apply the above condition to Eqs. (
6.13
) and (
6.14
), we get, for all
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
,
\mathcal{P}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]=[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Let
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
, then we have
\mathcal{P}\circ\alpha(x)=\alpha\circ\mathcal{P}(x)=\alpha(x)
, which implies that
\alpha(x)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{+}
. So
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
is subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Similarly, we show that
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
is subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Conversely, we define a linear map of degree zero
\mathcal{P}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}

such that for all
~x\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}~and~y\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}

\mathcal{P}(x+y)=x-y.

(6.16)

We have
\mathcal{P}^{2}(x+y)=\mathcal{P}(x-y)=x+y
, then
\mathcal{P}^{2}=Id
. Since
~x\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
, then
\alpha(x)\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
. Thus
\mathcal{P}\circ\alpha(x)=\alpha(x)=\alpha\circ\mathcal{P}(x)
. Similarly,
\mathcal{P}\circ\alpha(y)=\alpha\circ\mathcal{P}(y)
.

If
n
is even, since
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
then for
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
, we have:

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-j-1}\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{n-j}}(\sumop\displaylimits_{i_{1}<i_{2}\ldots<i_{j}}[\ldots\mathcal{P}x_{i_{1}},\ldots,\mathcal{P}x_{i_{2}},\ldots,\mathcal{P}x_{i_{j}},\ldots])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{2j+1<n}\binom{n}{2j+1}\mathcal{P}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]-\sumop\displaylimits_{2j<n}\binom{n}{2j}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{2j+1<n}\binom{n}{2j+1}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]-\sumop\displaylimits_{2j<n}\binom{n}{2j}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sumop\displaylimits_{2j+1<n}\binom{n}{2j+1}-\sumop\displaylimits_{2j<n}\binom{n}{2j})[x_{1},...,x_{n}]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{BinN}}{=}

\displaystyle[\mathcal{P}x_{1},\mathcal{P}x_{2},...,\mathcal{P}x_{n}].

Also if
n
is odd, we have :

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-j-1}\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{n-j}}(\sumop\displaylimits_{i_{1}<i_{2}\ldots<i_{j}}[\ldots,\mathcal{P}x_{i_{1}},\ldots,\mathcal{P}x_{i_{2}},\ldots,\mathcal{P}x_{i_{j}},\ldots])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sumop\displaylimits_{2j<n}\binom{n}{2j}-\sumop\displaylimits_{2j+1<n}\binom{n}{2j+1})[x_{1},...,x_{n}]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{BinN}}{=}

\displaystyle[\mathcal{P}x_{1},\mathcal{P}x_{2},...,\mathcal{P}x_{n}].

One may check similarly for all
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
. Then
\mathcal{P}
is a product structure on
\mathcal{G}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{G}=(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot],\varepsilon,\alpha)
be a
n
-Hom-Lie color algebra and
\Theta:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a linear map of degree
\gamma
. Then
\Theta
is said in the
centroid
of
\mathcal{G}
if for all homogeneous elements
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\Theta\circ\alpha=\alpha\circ\Theta
and

\displaystyle\Theta[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]=[\Theta x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}].

(6.17)

The above identity is equivalent to

\displaystyle\Theta[x_{1},x_{2}\ldots,x_{n}]=\varepsilon(\gamma,X_{i})[x_{1},\ldots,\underbrace{\Theta x_{i}}_{i\text{-th place}},\ldots,x_{n}].

(6.18)

Definition 6.4
.

An almost product structure
\mathcal{P}
on
\mathcal{G}
is called a
strict product structure
if it is an element of the centroid.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a strict product structure on
\mathcal{G}
, then
\mathcal{P}
is a product structure on
\mathcal{G}
such that
[\mathfrak{g}_{+},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{+},\underbrace{\mathfrak{g}_{+}}_{i\text{-th place}},\mathfrak{g}_{-},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{-}]=0
, for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

Proof.

The identity (
6.11
) is equivalent to:

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{n-j}}\bigg(\sumop\displaylimits_{i_{1}<i_{2}\ldots<i_{j}}^{n}[\ldots\mathcal{P}x_{i_{1}},\ldots,\mathcal{P}x_{i_{2}},\ldots\mathcal{P}x_{i_{j}},\ldots]\bigg)=0.

Then, if
\mathcal{P}
is a strict product structure on
\mathcal{G}
and
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have:

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{n-j}}\bigg(\sumop\displaylimits_{i_{1}<i_{2}\ldots<i_{j}}^{n}[\ldots\mathcal{P}x_{i_{1}},\ldots,\mathcal{P}x_{i_{2}},\ldots\mathcal{P}x_{i_{j}},\ldots]\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\bigg(\sumop\displaylimits_{i_{1}<i_{2}\ldots<i_{j}}^{n}\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{n-j}}\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{j}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\binom{n}{j}\bigg)\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{n}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Thus
\mathcal{P}
is a product structure.

Fix
i
such that
0<i<n
and let
(k,l)
such that
0<k\leq i<l\leq n
with
x_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
x_{l}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
. According to Eq. (
6.17
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n}]=[\mathcal{P}x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},\ldots,x_{i}]=[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},\ldots,x_{i}].

On the other hand, by Eq. (
6.18
) we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i}]=

\displaystyle[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},\mathcal{P}x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i}].

Then, we obtain

[\mathfrak{g}_{+},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{+},\underbrace{\mathfrak{g}_{+}}_{i\text{-th place}}\mathfrak{g}_{-},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{-}]=0.

∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot],\varepsilon,\alpha)
be a
n
-Hom-Lie color algebra. Then
\mathcal{G}
has a strict product structure, if and
only if,
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}
, admits a decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-},

where
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
are graded subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
[\mathfrak{g}_{+},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{+},\underbrace{\mathfrak{g}_{+}}_{i\text{-th place}},\mathfrak{g}_{-},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{-}]=0
,
\forall~1\leq i\leq n-1
.

Proof.

The first implication is a direct computation from Lemma
6.1
. Conversely, using Theorem
6.2
, the map
\mathcal{P}
defined in Eq.(
6.16
) is an almost structure and, for all
x_{k}=x_{k}^{+}+x_{k}^{-}\in\mathfrak{g}
, where
x_{k}^{+}\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
x_{k}^{-}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}[x_{1}^{+}+x_{1}^{-},x_{2}^{+}+x_{2}^{-},\ldots,x_{n}^{+}+x_{n}^{-}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}[x_{1}^{+},x_{2}^{+},\ldots,x_{n}^{+}]+\mathcal{P}[x_{1}^{-},x_{2}^{-},\ldots,x_{n}^{-}]

\displaystyle=[x_{1}^{+},x_{2}^{+},\ldots,x_{n}^{+}]-[x_{1}^{-},x_{2}^{-},\ldots,x_{n}^{-}]

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{P}x_{1}^{+},x_{2}^{+},\ldots,x_{n}^{+}]+[\mathcal{P}x_{1}^{-},x_{2}^{-},\ldots,x_{n}^{-}]

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{P}x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}].

Then
\mathcal{P}
is a strict product structure on
\mathcal{G}
.
∎

Example 6.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\alpha},\varepsilon,\alpha)
be the
4
-Hom-Lie color algebra defined in Example
3.1
. Define a linear map of degree zero
\mathcal{P}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by

\mathcal{P}(e_{1})=e_{1},\quad\mathcal{P}(e_{2})=e_{2},\quad\mathcal{P}(e_{3})=-e_{3},\quad\mathcal{P}(e_{4})=-e_{4}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{P}(e_{5})=-e_{5}.

It easy to prove that
\mathcal{P}
is a strict product structure, therefore it is a product structure.
Using Theorem
6.2
, we deduce that the graded subalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-}
are generated by
<e_{1},e_{2}>
and
<e_{3},e_{4},e_{5}>
respectively. Thus

\mathfrak{g}_{+}=\mathfrak{g}_{(0,0)}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathfrak{g}_{-}=\mathfrak{g}_{(0,1)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{(1,0)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{(1,1)}.

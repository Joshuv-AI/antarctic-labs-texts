Möbius fluid dynamics on the unitary groups
Daniela Emmanuele 1 , Marcos Salvai 2 and Francisco Vittone 3

Abstract
. We study the nonrigid dynamics induced by the standard birational actions of the split unitary groups G = O o ​ ( n , n ) G=O_{o}\left(n,n\right) , S ​ U ​ ( n , n ) SU\left(n,n\right) and S ​ p ​ ( n , n ) Sp\left(n,n\right) on the compact classical Lie groups M = S ​ O n M=SO_{n} , U n U_{n} and S ​ p n Sp_{n} , respectively. More precisely, we study the geometry of G G endowed with the kinetic energy metric associated with the action of G G on M , M, assuming that M M carries its canonical bi-invariant Riemannian metric and has initially a homogeneous distribution of mass. By the least action principle, force free motions (thought of as curves in G G ) correspond to geodesics of G G . The geodesic equation may be understood as an inviscid Burgers equation with Möbius constraints. We prove that the kinetic energy metric on G G is not complete and in particular not invariant, find symmetries and totally geodesic submanifolds of G G and address the question under which conditions geodesics of rigid motions are geodesics of G G . Besides, we study equivalences with the dynamics of conformal and projective motions of the sphere in low dimensions.



Keywords:
force free motion, kinetic energy metric,
nonrigid dynamics, unitary group, split unitary group, Möbius action,
maximal isotropic subspace, inviscid Burgers equation

Geometric mechanics, introduced by Arnold in
[
1
]
, describes the
force free motions of a system as geodesics of a Riemannian metric on the
configuration space, given by the kinetic energy. Among the first examples
we find invariant metrics on
SO_{3}
and on the diffeomorphism group Diff
{}_{\mu}\left(M\right)
of a compact Riemannian manifold
M
preserving the
volume form
\mu
, in order to describe, respectively, force free motions
of the rigid body and the dynamics of an ideal flow on
M
.

This approach had an even greater impact when many classical differential
equations were recognized within this theory –that is, as geodesic
equations–, among others, Korteweg-de Vries
[
24
]
(see more examples
for instance in
[
32
,
3
]
).

In this paper we study the dynamics of certain finite dimensional Lie groups
endowed with the kinetic energy metric induced by the action of them on
Riemannian manifolds, which do not preserve the volume. We begin by putting
the situation in a broader context.
Ebin and Marsden
[
11
]
extended Arnold’s ideas for the whole
diffeomorphism group Diff
\left(M\right)
, studying in this way
compressible flows. In this case, the metric is no longer invariant. The
force free motions
t\mapsto\gamma\left(t\right)
, or equivalently, the
geodesics in Diff
\left(M\right)
, are rendered in a simple manner:
t\mapsto\gamma\left(t\right)\left(q\right)
turn out to be geodesics in
M
for all
q\in M
. This description is no longer valid if the flow is
constrained, for instance in the case of Diff
{}_{\mu}\left(M\right)
,
which, with the metric inherited from that of Diff
\left(M\right)
, is not
totally geodesic.

Geodesics of the induced metric have also been considered for groups of
diffeomorphisms preserving other structures on
M
, for example for the
group of symplectomorphisms
[
9
,
18
]
and contactomorphisms
[
10
]
. For these groups, and in general for subgroups of Diff
{}_{\mu}\left(M\right)
, the kinetic energy metric is invariant and has been
studied extensively. In the finite dimensional case, invariant metrics
appear in the rigid context, and force free motions, that is, geodesics,
have been much explored and relatively well understood.

Concerning non-invariant metrics, they arise for compressible flows (the
basic example being Diff
\left(M\right)
, which we mentioned above) and
have been less studied. Bauer and Modin have recovered the interest in this
subject in
[
4
]
.

By non-rigid dynamics we understand the study of a dynamical system given by
a kinetic energy metric on a Lie group, induced by the action of it on a
Riemannian manifold, which is not by isometries. Among the reasons why the
nonrigid dynamics in finite dimension has been addressed to a lesser extent,
we can mention that the most significant actions of noncompact Lie groups on
compact Riemannian manifolds are in general scarce, for instance, for the
important family of compact symmetric spaces: the celebrated Nagano’s
Theorem
[
22
]
states that if a Lie group
G
acts on a compact
symmetric space
M
and contains the identity connected component of its
isometry group, then
M
is essentially a symmetric
R
-space (or
equivalently, it is extrinsically symmetric) and
G
is the so called big
group, or group of basic transformations in Takeuchi’s terminology in
[
30
]
. The best known examples are the conformal and projective
transformations of the sphere
S^{n}
, the biholomorphic maps of the complex
quadrics and the diffeomorphisms on Grassmannians which are induced by
linear isomorphisms. More generally, the
R
-spaces, homogeneous models of
the important parabolic geometries
[
8
,
27
]
, admit actions
of finite dimensional Lie groups containing the isometries.

The nonrigid (and non-invariant) dynamics of the simplest among the
symmetric
R
-spaces, the sphere, is studied in
[
25
]
and
[
19
]
,
for its two big groups, associated with conformal and projective
transformations. Also in the nonrigid finite dimensional setting, we can
cite
[
7
]
(force free affine motions in Euclidean space) and the
recent article
[
28
]
, where a non-invariant metric on
SL\left(3,\mathbb{R}\right)
appears in relation to affine motions of 3D ideal fluids
surrounded by vacuum.

A further fundamental problem in this area, apart from existence and
regularity of the geodesic flow, is its completeness, related with the onset
of shocks. The first two issues (existence and regularity) are of interest
only in infinite dimension. The third one (completeness), also in finite
dimension (of course, only when the configuration space is not Riemannian
homogeneous), and arises naturally, as we have pointed out, when the group
acts without preserving the volume.

In finite dimension, in the invariant case, the kinetic energy metric (as
any invariant metric) is complete. Since for infinite dimension the
Hopf-Rinow theorem is no longer valid, not even for invariant metrics,
completeness depends on regularity. Bruveris and Vialard deal with this
topic in depth in
[
6
]
. In
[
4
]

sufficient conditions for completeness are found in the semi-invariant case.

In this article we study the dynamics of Möbius motions of the compact
classical Lie groups, mainly drawing on the symmetries of the systems, in
the interest of advancing the understanding of non-rigid motions in finite
dimension. In this context, non-invariant metrics on Lie groups arise
naturally.

We consider the unitary groups
SO_{n}
,
U_{n}
and
Sp_{n}
, over the
real, complex and quaternionic numbers (that is, the identity components of
the automorphism groups of the positive definite Hermitian forms over these

(1)

\ast:G\times M\rightarrow M\text{, \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\

]

]

in

on this Grassmannian: if

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}Ux\\
x\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}AUx+B\\
CUx+D\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}U^{\prime}y\\

for

Up to coverings and connected components,

is the big group of

, which
is characterized in

as the group of diffeomorphisms of

sending circles in circles (certain distinguished curves in

Now, we consider the Riemannian structure on

induced by the inclusion in
the space
\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
of
\left(n\times n\right)
-matrices
with entries in
\mathbb{F}={\mathbb{R}},\,{\mathbb{C}}
or the quaternions
{\mathbb{H}}

\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle=\operatorname{Re}\left(\operatorname{tr}\left(\overline{X}^{T}Y\right)\right)
, where the superscript

[

). It turns out

We are concerned with the nonrigid dynamics induced by this non-isometric
action. We take
G
as the
configuration space
. This means that, if

M
has initially a homogeneous mass distribution of constant density
1
,
the points of
M
are allowed to move only in such a way that two
configurations differ in an element of
G
. We will see that the action of
G
on
M
is almost effective (only a finite number of elements act as the
identity), except for
G=O_{o}\left(2,2\right)
. For this reason, unless
otherwise stated, from now on we assume that
n>2
when
M=SO_{n}
.
Nevertheless, for the sake of completeness, we describe the Möbius
action of
O_{o}\left(2,2\right)
on
SO_{2}
in Proposition
3
.

A curve
\gamma
in
G
may be thought of as a
motion
of
M
. The
Riemannian metric on
M
allows us to consider the kinetic energy of a
motion and this induces a Riemannian metric on

, introduced originally by
V.I. Arnold in
[

]
, called the

on

associated with the action on

, with the property that

(thought of as curves in
G
) correspond to

of
G
, via the least action principle.

For each
g\in G
and
X\in T_{g}G
, the square norm of
X
for the kinetic
energy metric turns out to be

\left\|X\right\|^{2}=\int_{M}\left|\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\left(ge^{tX}\ast q\right)\right|^{2}d\mu\left(q\right)\text{,}

where
\left|v\right|
denotes the norm of a tangent vector to
M

(see Subsection

).

The kinetic energy metric on
G
is the restriction to
G
of the usual weak
Riemannian metric on
\operatorname{Diff}\left(M\right)
and the equation for
geodesics may be understood as the
inviscid Burgers equation with Möbius constraints
(see § 4.1 in
[
17
]
).

Next we state our first result on the geometry of
G
, its incompleteness.
We denote by
I_{n}
and
0_{n}
the identity and the zero
\left(n\times n\right)
-matrices, respectively.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G
be endowed with the kinetic energy metric.
Then the curve
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow G
defined by

\gamma(t)=\exp\left(t\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0_{n}&I_{n}\\
I_{n}&0_{n}\end{array}\right)\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cosh t~I_{n}&\sinh t~I_{n}\\
\sinh t~I_{n}&\cosh t~I_{n}\end{array}\right)

is the reparametrization of an inextendible geodesic in
G
of finite
length. In particular,
G
is not complete and so the kinetic energy metric
on
G
is neither left nor right invariant.

Incompleteness, in a classical context, is related to the existence of
shocks (collapses in finite time).

We have mentioned above the work of Bruveris and Vialard

[
6
]
about completeness in the invariant case. For the semi-invariant case, in the recent paper
[
4
]
,
Bauer and Modin give

conditions for completeness. Notice
that the metric on

in this article
is semi-invariant in the sense of

]

The following proposition shows the behavior of the mass when
\lim_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}\gamma\left(t\right)
.

Proposition 1
.

If

with odd

\gamma

the mass in

as

More precisely, for

\lim_{t\rightarrow\varepsilon\infty}\gamma\left(t\right)\ast q=\varepsilon I_{n}

for all

, which is an open and dense set in

. If

with odd

, then the same property holds for

.

Next we present some totally geodesic submanifols

of

endowed with

The following inclusions determine totally geodesic

Now we consider the maximal compact subgroups

Proposition 2

a)
The action of

Since the metric on

Let

2.
Preliminaries and basic facts

2.1.
Unitary groups

In this section we recall some basic facts about the classical compact Lie
groups. For more details on the subject we refer to
[
15
]
and
[
31
]
. Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
,
{\mathbb{C}}
or
{\mathbb{H}}
(the skew
field of quaternions) and let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\mathbb{F}^{N}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space, where in the case of
\mathbb{F}={\mathbb{H}}
, we
choose it to be a right
{\mathbb{H}}
-vector space. As usual, we denote by
GL(N,\mathbb{F})
the group of invertible
\mathbb{F}
-linear endomorphisms
of
\mathbb{F}^{N}
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}
set
b_{n,m}^{\mathbb{F}}:\mathbb{F}^{n+m}\times\mathbb{F}^{n+m}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
the sesquilinear form

(4)

b_{n,m}^{\mathbb{F}}((x,y),(\xi,\eta))=\overline{x}^{T}\,\xi-\overline{y}^{T}\,\eta\text{,}

where
x,\,\xi\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
, and
y,\,\eta\in\mathbb{F}^{m}
are
column vectors, the bar denotes conjugation and the superscript
T

indicates transposition, as before.

The form
b_{n,m}^{\mathbb{F}}
is called the
\mathbb{F}
-Hermitian
symmetric inner product of signature
(n,m)
on
\mathbb{F}^{n+m}
and the
space
\mathbb{F}^{n+m}
endowed with this form is called
\mathbb{F}^{n,m}
. Let
U(n,m,\mathbb{F})
be the Lie group

U(n,m,\mathbb{F})=\{A\in GL(n+m,\mathbb{F})\mid b_{n,m}^{\mathbb{F}}(Ax,Ay)=b_{n,m}^{\mathbb{F}}(x,y)\ \ \text{for all }\,x,y\in\mathbb{F}^{n+m}\}\text{.}

We shall denote by
U_{o}(n,m,\mathbb{F})
the connected component of the
identity of
U(n,m,\mathbb{F})
. For
\mathbb{F}={\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{C}}
,
SU(n,m,\mathbb{F})=\left\{A\in U(n,m,\mathbb{F})\mid{\mathrm{det}}A=1\right\}
.

We are interested in the cases where
m=n
or
m=0
. For brevity, we call
U\left(n,\mathbb{F}\right)=U(n,0,\mathbb{F})
. Then, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have:

- the orthogonal, unitary and symplectic groups
U(n,{\mathbb{R}})=O_{n}
,
U(n,\mathbb{C})=U_{n}
and
U(n,\mathbb{H})=Sp_{n}
(also called
HU\left(n\right)
),

-
U(n,n,{\mathbb{R}})=O(n,n)
,
U(n,n,{\mathbb{C}})=U(n,n)
and
U(n,n,{\mathbb{H}})=Sp\left(n,n\right)
(also called
HU\left(n,n\right)
).

2.2.
The action of
U(n,n,\mathbb{F})
on
U(n,\mathbb{F})

An
\mathbb{F}
-subspace
V
of
\mathbb{F}^{n,n}
is said to be
totally isotropic
if
\left.b_{n,n}^{\mathbb{F}}\right|_{V\times V}\equiv 0
. A maximal totally isotropic subspace must
have dimension
n
(see
[
15
]
). Denote by Grass
{}_{0}(n,\mathbb{F})
the Grassmannian of
maximal totally isotropic subspaces of
\mathbb{F}^{n,n}
. Then, it is not
difficult to see that the map

(5)

f:U(n,\mathbb{F})\rightarrow\text{Grass}_{0}(n,\mathbb{F}),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ f(T)=\operatorname{rgraph}(T)=\{(Tx,x))\mid x\in\mathbb{F}^{n}\}

is a diffeomorphism, where
\operatorname{rgraph}(T)
is the graph of
T
reflected
with respect to the diagonal, or equivalently, the graph of
T^{-1}
, or the
graph of
T
as a map from the second factor into the first.

Now, there is a canonical action of
U(n,n,\mathbb{F})
on Grass
{}_{0}(n,\mathbb{F})
. The identification
f
induces an action of
U(n,n,\mathbb{F})

on
U\left(n,\mathbb{F}\right)
, as follows: Given
U\in U(n,\mathbb{F})
,
let
V=\operatorname{rgraph}(U)
. So, if
T\in U(n,n,\mathbb{F})
has the matrix
form

T=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\end{array}\right)\text{,}

then
T(V)=\{(AUx+Bx,CUx+Dx)\mid\,x\in\mathbb{F}^{n}\}
. On the other
hand, there exists
U^{\prime}\in U(n,\mathbb{F})
such that
T(V)=\operatorname{rgraph}(U^{\prime})=\{(U^{\prime}y,y)\mid\,y\in\mathbb{F}^{n}\}
and so
we must have
y=(CU+D)x
and consequently the action reads, as in (
1
),

(6)

T\ast U=U^{\prime}=(AU+B)(CU+D)^{-1}\text{.}

By Witt’s Theorem, the canonical action of
U(n,n,\mathbb{F})
on Grass
{}_{0}(n,\mathbb{F})
is transitive, and then so is the action restricted to
U_{o}(n,n,\mathbb{F})
. Through

we obtain a transitive action of
U_{o}(n,n,\mathbb{F})
on
U(n,\mathbb{F})
.

At this point we should make some remarks:

- As it is well known,
O(n,n)
is not connected (see
[
23
]
). However, since it acts
transitively on
O_{n}
, then
O_{o}(n,n)
acts transitively on
(O_{n})_{o}=SO_{n}
.

- The action of

on

is not effective. It
is not difficult to see that actually, for each
u\in S^{1}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
and
T\in U\left(n,n\right)
, we have
(uT)\ast U=T\ast U
. In order
to obtain an effective action, we can take the group
PU(n,n)=U(n,n)/S^{1}
.
For convenience, we will consider the action of the matrix group
SU(n,n)

on
U_{n}
, which is almost effective since for
T
in this group,
T\ast U=U
for all
U\in U_{n}
if and only if
\ T=uI_{2n}
with
u^{2n}=1
.

We comment that the group
G=U\left(n,n,\mathbb{F}\right)
acts
canonically on the Grassmannian of maximal space-like subspaces of

, from which

, via the

2.3.
The kinetic energy metric

In this section we review the restriction to a finite dimensional subgroup
of the usual
L^{2}
weak Riemannian metric on the space of all
diffeomorphisms of a compact Riemannian manifold (see for instance Theorem
9.1 (i) in
[
11
]
), setting in a broader context some of the
concepts presented in
[
25
]
and
[
19
]
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group acting smoothly and almost effectively on a
compact oriented Riemannian manifold
M
. Suppose that
M
has initially a
homogeneous distribution of mass of constant density
1
and that the points
are allowed to move only in such a way that two configurations differ in an
element of
G
. Then the configuration space may be naturally identified
with
G
(aside from the fact that a finite number of elements act by the
identity) and a curve
\gamma
in
G
may be thought of as a motion of
M
.

The total kinetic energy
E(\gamma)
of the motion
\gamma
at the instant

t
is given by

(7)

E(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\rho_{t}(q)|v_{t}(q)|^{2}d\mu,

where
d\mu
is the volume form on
M
and if
q=\gamma(t)(p)
for some
p\in M
, then

v_{t}(q):=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{t}\gamma(s)(p)\in T_{q}M,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \rho_{t}(q)=1/{\mathrm{det}}(d\gamma(t)_{p})

are the velocity of the point
q
and the density at
q
at the instant
t
,
respectively. This is the smooth analogue of
\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2}m_{i}v_{i}^{2}
, once one recognizes
\rho d\mu
as the mass element.

Changing variables in (
7
), one obtains

(8)

E(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left|\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{t}\gamma(s)(p)\right|^{2}d\mu.

Based on the principle of least action, we say that a smooth curve
\gamma

in
G
, thought of as a motion of
M
, is force free if it is a critical
point of the kinetic energy functional.

In order to introduce a Riemannian metric on
G
whose geodesics correspond
to force free motions of
G
, for
g\in G
and
X\in T_{g}G
, we define the
map
\widetilde{X}:M\rightarrow TM
by

\widetilde{X}(q)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\gamma(t)(q)\in T_{g(q)}M,

where
\gamma
is any smooth curve in
G
with
\gamma(0)=g
and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=X
. Standard arguments show that the map
\widetilde{X}
is
well-defined and smooth, and that
\widetilde{X}
is a vector field on
M

if and only if
X\in T_{e}G
. Following
[
25
, Prop. 1]
, one has that

(9)

\langle X,Y\rangle=\int_{M}\langle\widetilde{X}(q)\,,\,\widetilde{Y}(q)\rangle d\mu\text{,}

for
X,Y\in T_{g}G
and
g\in G
, defines a Riemannian metric on
G
such
that a curve
\gamma
in
G
is a geodesic if and only if it is force free
(on the basis of the principle of least action). This metric is called the
kinetic energy metric of
G
induced by the action on
M
.

3.
Symmetries of the kinetic energy metric

From now on, we will consider
G=O_{o}(n,n),\,SU(n,n)
or
Sp\left(n,n\right)
, their maximal compact subgroups

,

or

Proof of Proposition
2
.

a
) For

F_{k_{1},k_{2}}:G\rightarrow G,\ \ \ \ \ g\mapsto F_{k_{1},k_{2}}(g)=k_{1}gk_{2}{}^{-1}\text{.}

We have to verify that
F_{k_{1},k_{2}}
is an isometry of the kinetic
energy metric on
G
.

Let
X\in T_{g}G
and let
\gamma
be a curve in
G
such that
\gamma(0)=g
,
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=X
. Then
dF_{k_{1},k_{2}}(X)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}k_{1}\gamma(t)k_{2}^{-1}
and so for each

we
have that

\widetilde{dF_{k_{1},k_{2}}(X)}(q)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}(k_{1}\gamma(t))\ast(k_{2}^{-1}\ast q)\text{.}

Observe that since
k_{i}\in K
, then
\displaystyle k_{i}=\operatorname{diag}\left(A_{i},B_{i}\right)
with
A_{i},B_{i}\in M

k_{i}\ast q=A_{i}qB_{i}^{-1}=R_{B_{i}^{-1}}L_{A_{i}}(q)

and
h:M\rightarrow M
by
h=R_{B_{2}}L_{A_{2}^{-1}}
. Then,

(11)

\left\|dF_{k_{1},k_{2}}X\right\|^{2}=\int_{M}\left(f\circ h\right)\,d\mu=\int_{M}f(h^{\ast}d\mu)=\int_{M}fd\mu,

since
d\mu
is bi-invariant by the action of

.

On the other hand,

f(q)=\left|dR_{B_{1}^{-1}}dL_{A_{1}}\left(\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\gamma(t)\ast q\right)\right|^{2}=\left|\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\gamma(t)\ast q\right|^{2}\text{,}

and so

\left\|dF_{k_{1},k_{2}}X\right\|^{2}=\int_{M}\left|\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}\gamma(t)\ast q\right|^{2}d\mu(q)=\left\|X\right\|^{2}\text{.}

Then
F_{k_{1},k_{2}}
is an isometry.

b) We start with the least simple case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, when
K
is
strictly contained in
M\times M
, that is,
K=S\left(U_{n}\times U_{n}\right)
and
M=U_{n}
. The cases
M=SO_{n}
(with

) and
Sp_{n}
, which are semisimple Lie groups, are dealt with in the same way, but in a
more direct manner. We have to show that the kinetic energy metric
restricted to
K=S\left(U_{n}\times U_{n}\right)
is the restriction of a
product metric on
M\times M=U_{n}\times U_{n}
such that
\rho
is an
isometry.

By the first part of the proof, the kinetic energy metric restricted to
K

is bi-invariant, in particular, the inner product on
\mathfrak{k}=T_{\left(I_{n},I_{n}\right)}K
is Ad
\left(K\right)
-invariant. We write

\mathfrak{k}=s\left(u_{n}\oplus u_{n}\right)=su_{n}\oplus su_{n}\oplus\mathbb{R}iI_{2n}^{-}\text{,}

where
I_{2n}^{-}=\operatorname{diag}\left(I_{n},-I_{n}\right)
. Since
su_{n}
is
simple and the last direct summand has dimension 1, the summands are
orthogonal.

Now we check that
d\rho_{\left(I_{n},I_{n}\right)}
restricted to
su_{n}\oplus su_{n}
is a linear isometry, or equivalently, that
\left\|\left(X,0\right)\right\|=\left\|\left(0,X\right)\right\|

for all
\left(X,0\right)\in\mathfrak{k}
(using again that
\mathfrak{k}

carries the restriction of a product metric). We compute

\left|\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}e^{t\left(X,0\right)}\ast q\right|^{2}=\left|\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{0}e^{tX}q\right|^{2}=\left|Xq\right|^{2}=\left|X\right|^{2}\text{,}

for all
q\in M
, since the inner product on
\mathbb{F}^{m\times m}
is
bi-invariant by
M.
Integrating over
M
yields
\left\|\left(X,0\right)\right\|^{2}=\operatorname{vol}\left(M\right)\left|X\right|^{2}
. One obtains similarly that
\left\|\left(0,X\right)\right\|^{2}=\operatorname{vol}\left(M\right)\left|X\right|^{2}
.

Finally, we verify that the metric on
K
is the restriction to
K
of a
product metric on
M\times M
. We observe that
u_{n}\oplus u_{n}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathbb{R}iI_{2n}
. Let
\epsilon_{1}=\operatorname{diag}\left(iI_{n},0_{n}\right)
and
\epsilon_{2}=\operatorname{diag}\left(0_{n},iI_{n}\right)
, which form a basis of
\mathbb{R}iI_{2n}^{-}\oplus\mathbb{R}iI_{2n}
. We have that

u_{n}\oplus u_{n}=\left(su_{n}\oplus\mathbb{R}\epsilon_{1}\right)\oplus\left(su_{n}\oplus\mathbb{R}\epsilon_{2}\right)\equiv\left(su_{n}\oplus su_{n}\right)\oplus\mathbb{R}\epsilon_{1}\oplus\mathbb{R}\epsilon_{2}\text{.}

Suppose that
\left\|iI_{2n}^{-}\right\|=\ell
. We consider an
inner product such that the three summands in the last expression are
orthogonal,
su_{n}\oplus su_{n}
has the metric induced from the kinetic
energy and
\left\|\epsilon_{1}\right\|=\left\|\epsilon_{2}\right\|=\ell/\sqrt{2}
. Since
iI_{2n}^{-}=\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}

satisfies the required

∎

If

.

5.
The incompleteness of the kinetic energy metric

In this section we prove Theorem
1
. Let
\delta=\operatorname{diag}(I_{n},-I_{n})

7.
Equivalences with conformal and projective motions of the sphere in
low dimensions

As we mentioned in the introduction, in this section we explore, on the one
hand, whether the projective and conformal actions on spheres (
2
) are equivalent to the Möbius actions on unitary groups in
low dimensions, and on the other hand, the implications of this in the
dynamics of Möbius motions of the compact classical Lie groups.

The simplest case is the Möbius action
SU\left(1,1\right)\times U_{1}\rightarrow U_{1}
. The equivalences up to coverings with the usual
actions of
SL_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
and
O_{o}\left(1,2\right)

on the circle (Möbius or projective and conformal, respectively) is well
known. In
[
12
]
the first two authors gave a complete description of the
geometry of the kinetic energy metric in this simple case. See also
[
13
]
, where the relationship with the article
[
2
]
is
addressed, providing a dynamical presentation of the metric on the disc
where everything is better than nice. The kinetic energy metric projects to a non-complete negatively curved disc
whose geodesics are hypocycloids.

The next simplest case is the Möbius action
O_{o}\left(2,2\right)\times SO_{2}\rightarrow SO_{2}
, which is the only one of those
actions which is not even almost effective. For the sake of completeness we
comment on its relationship with projective transformations of the circle.

We recall from
[
15
]
the split quaternions
\mathbb{M}
, which is the
skew algebra
M\left(2,\mathbb{R}\right)=\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
with the
usual matrix multiplication and the inner product whose square norm is given
by
\left\langle X,X\right\rangle={\mathrm{det}}X
. In particular,
SL\left(2,\mathbb{R}\right)
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{M}
. The
matrices

\mathbf{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\text{,\ \ \ \ }\mathbf{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right)\text{,\ \ \ \ }\mathbf{j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)\text{,\ \ \ \ }\mathbf{k}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right)

form a basis of
\mathbb{M}
and the following relations hold:

-\mathbf{i}^{2}=\mathbf{j}^{2}=\mathbf{k}^{2}=\mathbf{1}\text{,\ \ \ \ }\mathbf{ij}=-\mathbf{ji}=\mathbf{k}\text{,\ \ \ \ }\mathbf{jk}=-\mathbf{kj}=-\mathbf{i}\text{ \ \ \ and\ \ \ \ }\mathbf{ki}=-\mathbf{ik}=\mathbf{j}\text{.}

The square norm is given by

\left\langle a+b\mathbf{i}+c\mathbf{j}+d\mathbf{k},a+b\mathbf{i}+c\mathbf{j}+d\mathbf{k}\right\rangle=a^{2}+b^{2}-c^{2}-d^{2}

and hence the inner product has signature
\left(2,2\right)
. Observe that

p\overline{p}=\left\langle p,p\right\rangle
for all
p\in\mathbb{M}
and

p^{-1}=\overline{p}/\left\langle p,p\right\rangle
if
\left\langle p,p\right\rangle\neq 0
. Let
L_{p}
and
R_{p}
denote left and right
multiplication by
p
, respectively, which are linear isometries of
\mathbb{M}
if
\left\langle p,p\right\rangle=1
. One has that

SL\left(2,\mathbb{R}\right)\times SL\left(2,\mathbb{R}\right)\longrightarrow O_{o}\left(\mathbb{M}\right)\text{,}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left(p,q\right)\mapsto L_{p}\circ R_{\overline{q}}

is a double covering morphism with kernel
\left\{\left(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}\right),\left(-\mathbf{1},-\mathbf{1}\right)\right\}
. In particular,

O_{o}\left(\mathbb{M}\right)=\left\{L_{p}\circ R_{\overline{q}}\mid p,q\in SL_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\right\}\text{.}

We set
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{1}=\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{R}+\mathbf{i}\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{C}+\mathbf{j}\mathbb{C}
and write the
operators in
O_{o}\left(\mathbb{M}\right)
in blocks with respect to this
decomposition. The ordered bases
\left\{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{i}\right\}

and
\left\{\mathbf{j},\mathbf{ji}=-\mathbf{k}\right\}
of
\mathbb{C}

and
\mathbf{j}\mathbb{C}
determine an isomorphism from
SO_{2}\cong S^{1}

onto
SO\left(\mathbf{j}\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C}\right)
, assigning to
u\in S^{1}
the map
\mathbf{j}\mathbb{C}\ni\mathbf{j}z\mapsto uz
. The
juxtaposition of these bases provides the identification
O_{o}\left(\mathbb{M}\right)\cong O_{o}\left(2,2\right)
.

Now we consider the Möbius action
\ast:O_{o}\left(2,2\right)\times SO_{2}\rightarrow SO_{2}
, using the presentation in (
6
),
identifying
u\in SO_{2}\equiv S^{1}
with the set
\left\{uz+\mathbf{j}z\mid z\in\mathbb{C}\right\}
, according to (
5
). Thus,

(30)

L_{p}R_{\overline{q}}\ast u=v\left(p,q,u\right)\text{,}

where
v\left(p,q,u\right)
is the only point of
S{{}^{1}}
that satisfies that for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
exists
w\left(z\right)\in\mathbb{C}
such that

p\left(uz+\mathbf{j}z\right)\overline{q}=v\left(p,q,u\right)w\left(z\right)+\mathbf{j}w\left(z\right)\text{.}

Proposition 3
.

a)
The Möbius action
O_{o}\left(2,2\right)\times SO_{2}\rightarrow SO_{2}
is not almost effective. More precisely,
\left(L_{p}\circ R_{\overline{q}}\right)\ast u=L_{p}\ast u
for all
p,q\in SL_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
and
u\in S^{1}
.

b)
The following diagram commutes
:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}SL_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\times S^{1}&\overset{\cdot}{\longrightarrow}&S^{1}\\
\downarrow\left(F,\rho\right)&&\downarrow\rho\\
O_{o}\left(2,2\right)\times SO_{2}&\overset{\ast}{\longrightarrow}&SO_{2}\text{,}\end{array}

where
F\left(p\right)=L_{p}
,
\rho\left(u\right)\left(z\right)=uz

and
\cdot
denotes the usual action by conformal transformations of the
plane preserving the circle, that is,

(31)

p\cdot u=\left(\alpha u+\overline{\beta}\right)\left(\beta u+\overline{\alpha}\right)^{-1}

if
p=\alpha+\mathbf{j}\beta
, with
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
,
\left|\alpha\right|^{2}-\left|\beta\right|^{2}=1
.

Proof
.

a) Let
q=\alpha+\mathbf{j}\beta
with
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
.
Given
u\in S^{1}
, we compute

R_{\overline{\alpha}-\mathbf{j}\beta}\left(uz+\mathbf{j}z\right)=\left(uz+\mathbf{j}z\right)\left(\overline{\alpha}-\mathbf{j}\beta\right)=uz\overline{\alpha}-\overline{z}\beta+\mathbf{j}\left(z\overline{\alpha}-\overline{u}\,\overline{z}\beta\right)

(we have used that

for all

for all

, we have by (

) that

, and so,

.

b) Now let

with
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
. We compute

), as desired.

,

(by the Cartan decomposition, it is diffeomorphic to

\Lambda^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)^{\ast}
given by
h\left(\sigma,\tau\right){\mathrm{det}}=\frac{1}{2}\,\sigma\wedge\tau
.

We also have the decomposition

o_{4}=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{R}\cong\operatorname{Im}(\mathbb{H})\times(\mathbb{H})\text{,}

where
\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\xi}\mid\xi\in\operatorname{Im}(\mathbb{H})\right\}
and
\mathcal{R}=\left\{R_{\eta}\mid\eta\in\operatorname{Im}(\mathbb{H})\right\}
. The set
\mathcal{B}=\left\{L_{i},L_{j},L_{k}\right\}\cup\left\{R_{i},R_{j},R_{k}\right\}
is a
\left(3,3\right)
-orthonormal basis of
o_{4}
with respect to
g
, where
the first elements are space-like and the last ones are time-like. Now, the
group
SL_{4}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
acts on
\Lambda^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{4}\right)^{\ast}
by linear isometries with respect to
h
via
A.\sigma=\left(A^{T}\right)^{\ast}\sigma
. This action, through the
isomorphism
\omega,
induces the morphism

\Phi:SL_{4}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow O_{o}\left(o_{4},g\right)\text{,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\Phi\left(A\right)\left(Z\right)=AZA^{T}\text{.}

By means of the basis
\mathcal{B}
, we identify
o_{4}=\mathbb{R}^{3,3}

and so
\Phi
induces a morphism
F:SL_{4}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow O_{o}\left(3,3\right)
. We also define
\rho:S^{3}\rightarrow SO_{3}
by
\rho\left(q\right)=L_{q}R_{\overline{q}}
.

Proposition 5
.

The following diagram is commutative
:

(34)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}SL_{4}(\mathbb{R})\times S^{3}&\overset{\ast_{p}}{\longrightarrow}&S^{3}\\
\downarrow\left(F,\rho\right)&&\downarrow\rho\\
O_{o}\left(3,3\right)\times SO_{3}&\overset{\ast}{\longrightarrow}&SO_{3}\text{,}\end{array}

where
\ast
denotes the Möbius action and
\ast_{p}
the projective
action.

Proof
.

First we verify the commutativity of the diagram for the elements of
SO_{4}\subset SL_{4}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
. We have that
\left.F\right|_{SO_{4}}:SO_{4}\rightarrow SO_{3}\times SO_{3}\subset O_{o}\left(3,3\right)
(canonical immersion) is given by

F\left(L_{p}\circ R_{\overline{q}}\right)=\left(L_{p}\circ R_{\overline{p}},L_{q}\circ R_{\overline{q}}\right)\text{.}

Indeed, calling
L_{p}\circ R_{\overline{q}}=A
,
A\ast_{p}u=pu\overline{q}

holds for all
u\in S^{3}
, since
\left|pu\overline{q}\right|=1
.
Now,
F\left(A\right)\ast\rho\left(u\right)=\rho\left(A\ast_{p}u\right)
if and only if

L_{p}R_{\overline{p}}L_{u}R_{\overline{u}}\left(L_{q}R_{\overline{q}}\right)^{-1}=L_{pu\overline{q}}\left(R_{pu\overline{q}}\right)^{-1}

for all
p,q,u\in S^{3}
, and this can be easily checked.

Since
F
is a morphism, by the
KAK
decomposition of
SL_{4}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
, it remains to check that the diagram commutes for any diagonal
matrix
E
in
SL_{4}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
. Any such
E
has the form
E=\exp\left(x_{1}D+x_{2}D_{2}+x_{3}D_{3}\right)
, where

D=\operatorname{diag}\left(1,1,-1,-1\right),\ \ D_{2}=\operatorname{diag}\left(1,-1,1,-1\right)\text{\ \ \ \ and \ \ \ }D_{3}=\operatorname{diag}\left(1,-1,-1,1\right).

Now, there exist
k_{2},k_{3}\in SO_{4}
such that
D_{l}=k_{l}Dk_{l}^{-1}
for
l=2,3.
Hence,

E=\exp\left(x_{1}D\right)k_{2}\exp\left(x_{2}D\right)k_{2}^{-1}k_{3}\exp\left(x_{1}D\right)k_{3}^{-1}.

Therefore, it suffices to check the commutativity of the diagram for

A_{t}:=\exp\left(tD\right)=\operatorname{diag}\left(e^{t},e^{t},e^{-t},e^{-t}\right).

Let
B_{t}
be the matrix of the transformation
\Phi\left(A_{t}\right)\left(L_{i}\right)=A_{t}L_{i}A_{t}^{T}\in o\left(\mathbb{H}\right)
with
respect to the basis
\left\{1,i,j,k\right\}
. We compute

\displaystyle B_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{t}I_{2}&0_{2}\\
0_{2}&e^{-t}I_{2}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}J&0\\
0&J\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{t}I_{2}&0_{2}\\
0_{2}&e^{-t}I_{2}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{2t}J&0_{2}\\
0_{2}&e^{-2t}J\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cosh\left(2t\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}J&0_{2}\\
0_{2}&J\end{array}\right)+\sinh\left(2t\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}J&0_{2}\\
0_{2}&-J\end{array}\right)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
J=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)
. Similar computations yield

\displaystyle A_{t}L_{ai+bj+ck}A_{t}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cosh\left(2t\right)L_{ai}+\sinh\left(2t\right)R_{ai}+L_{bj+ck}

\displaystyle A_{t}R_{ai+bj+ck}A_{t}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sinh\left(2t\right)L_{ai}+\cosh\left(2t\right)R_{ai}+R_{bj+ck}

for all
a,b,c,t\in\mathbb{R}
. Consequently, calling

C_{t}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\cosh 2t,I_{2}\right)\text{\ \ \ \ \ and\ \ \ \ \ }S_{t}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\sinh 2t,0_{2}\right)\text{,}

both in
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
, the matrix of the operator
\Phi\left(A_{t}\right)
with respect to the basis
\mathcal{B}
, which equals the
matrix of
F\left(A_{t}\right)
, is

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}C_{t}&S_{t}\\
S_{t}&C_{t}\end{array}\right)\in O_{o}\left(3,3\right)\text{.}

Now, for
w\neq 0
in
\mathbb{H}
, we call
I_{w}
the matrix of the
operator
L_{w}\left(R_{w}\right)^{-1}
on
\operatorname{Im}(\mathbb{H})
with
respect to the basis
\left\{i,j,k\right\}
. If
w=a+bi+cj+dk
, we compute

I_{w}=\frac{1}{\left|w\right|^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a^{2}+b^{2}-d^{2}-c^{2}&-2ad+2bc&2bd+2ac\\
2ad+2bc&a^{2}-b^{2}+c^{2}-d^{2}&-2ab+2cd\\
-2ac+2bd&2ab+2cd&a^{2}-b^{2}+d^{2}-c^{2}\end{array}\right)\text{.}

The commutativity of the diagram for
A_{t}
amounts to the validity of

\left(C_{t}I_{u}+S_{t}\right)\left(S_{t}I_{u}+C_{t}\right)^{-1}=L_{\left(A_{t}u\right)/\left|A_{t}u\right|}\left(R_{\left(A_{t}u\right)/\left|A_{t}u\right|}\right)^{-1}=L_{A_{t}u}\left(R_{A_{t}u}\right)^{-1}=I_{A_{t}u}

for all
u\in S^{3}
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, or equivalently,
C_{t}I_{u}+S_{t}=I_{A_{t}u}\left(S_{t}I_{u}+C_{t}\right)
. This can be
checked with a lengthy but straightforward computation.
∎

7.3.
Dynamical implications for Möbius motions in low dimensions

Let
Sp\left(1,1\right)
and
O_{o}\left(3,3\right)
be endowed with the
kinetic energy metrics. By Proposition
2
, the induced
Riemannian metrics on the maximal compact subgroups
Sp_{1}\times Sp_{1}

and
SO_{3}\times SO_{3}
are bi-invariant. Theorem
3
asserts
that each factor is totally geodesic. The results in this section give more
information on the matter. At the same time, they provide an alternative
statement (perhaps more illuminating) for Theorem 3 (b) in
[
19
]
.

Corollary 1
.

Let
Sp(1,1)
and
O_{o}\left(3,3\right)
be endowed with the
kinetic energy metrics. Then:

a)
The subgroup
Sp_{1}\times Sp_{1}
is totally geodesic in
Sp\left(1,1\right)
.

b)
A geodesic in
SO_{3}\times SO_{3}
is a geodesic in
O_{o}\left(3,3\right)
if and only if it remains in one of the factors.

Proof
.

By Propositions
4
and
5
, the Möbius actions
of
Sp\left(1,1\right)
on
Sp_{1}
and of
O_{o}\left(3,3\right)
on
SO_{3}
are equivalent, up to double coverings, to the conformal action of
O_{o}\left(1,4\right)
on
S^{3}
and the projective action of
SL_{4}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
on
S^{3}
, respectively. The morphisms
preserve the corresponding kinetic energy metrics.

By Theorem 2 (b) in
[
25
]
,
SO_{4}
is totally geodesic in
O_{o}\left(1,4\right)
endowed with the kinetic energy metric associated with the
conformal action. Since
Sp_{1}\times Sp_{1}
corresponds to
SO_{4}
under
the corresponding morphism, part (a) of the corollary follows.

Also, Theorem 3 (b) in
[
19
]
asserts that for
Z\in o_{4}
, the
geodesic
\sigma(t)=\exp(tZ)
of
SO_{4}
through the identity is also a
geodesic of
SL_{4}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
if and only if
Z=\lambda X

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
X\in o_{4}
with
X^{2}=-I_{4}
. Now,

Z=L_{\xi}+R_{\eta}
for some
\xi,\eta\in\operatorname{Im}(\mathbb{H})
. We
compute

-\lambda^{2}I_{4}=Z^{2}=\left(L_{\xi}+R_{\eta}\right)^{2}=L_{\xi}^{2}+R_{\eta}^{2}+2L_{\xi}R_{\eta}=-\left(\left|\xi\right|^{2}+\left|\eta\right|^{2}\right)I_{4}+2L_{\xi}R_{\eta}

(
L_{\xi}
and
R_{\eta}
commute). This implies that
L_{\xi}R_{\eta}=\mu I_{4}
for some
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. If
\eta\neq 0
, we have
L_{\xi}=R_{\mu\bar{\eta}/\left|\eta\right|^{2}}
. Now,
p,q\in\mathbb{H}
satisfy
L_{p}=R_{q}
only if
p,q
are in the center of
\mathbb{H}
, that is,
p,q\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\xi\in\operatorname{Im}(\mathbb{H})
, then
\xi=0
, as desired.
∎

Multiple normalized solutions for a Sobolev critical Schrödinger-Poisson-Slater equation
Louis Jeanjean and Thanh Trung Le

Abstract
. We look for solutions to the Schrödinger-Poisson-Slater equation (0.1) −  ​ u + λ ​ u − γ ​ ( | x | − 1 ∗ | u | 2 ) ​ u − a ​ | u | p − 2 ​ u = 0 in R 3 , \displaystyle-\Delta u+\lambda u-\gamma(\lvert x\rvert^{-1}*\lvert u\rvert^{2})u-a\lvert u\rvert^{p-2}u=0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3}, which satisfy ∥ u ∥ L 2 ​ ( R 3 ) 2 = c \lVert u\rVert_{\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=c for some prescribed c > 0 c>0 . When γ > 0 \gamma>0 and a > 0 a>0 , both in the Sobolev subcritical case p ∈ ( 10 3 , 6 ) p\in(\frac{10}{3},6) and in the Sobolev critical case p = 6 p=6 , we show that there exists a c 1 > 0 c_{1}>0 such that, for any c ∈ ( 0 , c 1 ) c\in(0,c_{1}) , ( 0.1 ) admits two solutions u c + u_{c}^{+} and u c − u_{c}^{-} which can be characterized respectively as a local minima and as a mountain pass critical point of the associated Energy functional restricted to the norm constraint. Introduction

We consider the following Schrödinger-Poisson-Slater equation:

(1.1)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}v+\Delta v+\gamma(\lvert x\rvert^{-1}*\lvert v\rvert^{2})v+a\lvert v\rvert^{p-2}v=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},

where
v:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R},

a\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in(\frac{10}{3},6]
. We look for standing wave solutions to (
1.1

v(t,x)=e^{i\lambda t}u(x)

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

satisfies the equation

(1.2)

\displaystyle-\Delta u+\lambda u-\gamma(\lvert x\rvert^{-1}*\lvert u\rvert^{2})u-a\lvert u\rvert^{p-2}u=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Motivated by the fact that the
\mathit{L}^{2}-\mbox{norm}
is a preserved quantity of the evolution we focus on the search of solutions to (
1.2
) with prescribed
\mathit{L}^{2}-\mbox{norm}
. It is standard that for some prescribed
c>0
, a solution of (
1.2
) with

can be obtained as a critical point of the

\displaystyle F(u):=\dfrac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}dx-\dfrac{\gamma}{4}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\dfrac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{2}\lvert u(y)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert}dxdy-\dfrac{a}{p}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\lvert u\rvert^{p}dx

restricted to

\displaystyle S(c):=\{u\in{\mathit{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\lVert u\rVert_{\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=c\}.

Then the parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
in (

Let us define

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u\in S(c)
, there exists

(i)

a constant
K_{H}>0
such that
B(u)\leq K_{H}\sqrt{A(u)}c^{\frac{3}{2}}
.

(ii)

a constant
K_{GN}>0
such that
C(u)\leq K_{GN}[A(u)]^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}c^{\frac{6-p}{4}}
.

Proof.

We first recall the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (see
[
LiebLoss2001analysis
, Chapter 4]
):

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{f(x)g(y)}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{\lambda}}dxdy\right\rvert\leq C(N,\lambda,p,q)\lVert f\rVert_{\mathit{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\lVert g\rVert_{\mathit{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

where
f\in\mathit{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
g\in\mathit{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
p,q>1
,
0<\lambda<N
and

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{1}{q}+\dfrac{\lambda}{N}=2.

Let us also recall the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (see
[
Nirenberg1985
]
) and the Sobolev inequality (see
[
Brezis-2011
, Theorem 9.9]
) in the unified form: if
N\geq 3
and
p\in[2,\frac{2N}{N-2}]
then

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{\mathit{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C(N,p)\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\beta}\lVert f\rVert_{\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{(1-\beta)},\qquad\text{with }\beta=N\left(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{p}\right).

Applying the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality we obtain

(2.2)

\displaystyle B(u)=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\dfrac{\lvert u(x)\rvert^{2}\lvert u(y)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert}dxdy\leq K_{1}\lVert u\rVert_{\mathit{L}^{\frac{12}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{4}

and thus using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we get

\displaystyle B(u)\leq K_{1}\lVert u\rVert_{\mathit{L}^{\frac{12}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{4}\leq K_{1}K_{2}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lVert u\rVert_{\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{3}=K_{H}\sqrt{A(u)}c^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Finally, applying the Sobolev, Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we have

\displaystyle C(u)=\lVert u\rVert_{\mathit{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\leq K_{GN}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\sigma}\lVert u\rVert_{\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{6-p}{2}}=K_{GN}[A(u)]^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}c^{\frac{6-p}{4}}.

∎

Proof.

Our proof is inspired by

Lemma 2.3

3.1.

In this subsection, we follow the approach first introduced in

]

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
c\in(0,\infty)
,

F
restricted to
\Lambda(c)
is coercive on
{\mathit{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, namely when
(u_{n})\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfies
||u_{n}||\to+\infty
then

. In particular

restricted to
\Lambda(c)
is bounded from below.

Proof.

Let
u\in\Lambda(c)
. Taking into account that

\displaystyle\dfrac{a}{p}C(u)=\dfrac{1}{\sigma}A(u)-\dfrac{\gamma}{4\sigma}B(u),

and using
Lemma

2.1
(
i
), we obtain

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}F(u)&=\dfrac{1}{2}A(u)-\dfrac{\gamma}{4}B(u)-\dfrac{a}{p}C(u)=\dfrac{1}{2}A(u)-\dfrac{\gamma}{4}B(u)-\dfrac{1}{\sigma}A(u)+\dfrac{\gamma}{4\sigma}B(u)\\
&=\dfrac{\sigma-2}{2\sigma}A(u)-\dfrac{\gamma(\sigma-1)}{4}B(u)\geq\dfrac{\sigma-2}{2\sigma}A(u)-\dfrac{\gamma(\sigma-1)}{4}K_{H}\sqrt{A(u)}c^{\frac{3}{2}}.\end{split}

This concludes the proof.
∎

For any
u\in S(c)
, we recall that

\displaystyle g_{u}(t)=F(u^{t})=\dfrac{1}{2}t^{2}A(u)-\dfrac{\gamma}{4}tB(u)-\dfrac{a}{p}t^{\sigma}C(u),

\displaystyle g_{u}^{\prime}(t)=tA(u)-\dfrac{\gamma}{4}B(u)-\dfrac{a\sigma}{p}t^{\sigma-1}C(u)=\dfrac{1}{t}Q(u^{t}),

\displaystyle g_{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)=A(u)-\dfrac{a\sigma(\sigma-1)}{p}t^{\sigma-2}C(u).

For any
u\in S(c)
, we set

\displaystyle t_{u}^{\star}:=\left(\dfrac{pA(u)}{a\sigma(\sigma-1)C(u)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma-2}}.

This implies that
t_{u}^{\star}
is the unique solution of equation
g_{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)=0
. So, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle g_{u}^{\prime\prime}(t_{u}^{\star})=0,\quad g_{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)>0\text{ if }0<t<t_{u}^{\star},\quad g_{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)<0\text{ if }t>t_{u}^{\star}.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
c\in(0,c_{1})
and any
u\in S(c)
, we have
g_{u}^{\prime}(t_{u}^{\star})>0
.

Proof.

Let
u\in S(c)
be arbitrary. By the definition of
t_{u}^{\star}
and by
g_{u}^{\prime\prime}(t_{u}^{\star})=0
, we have

\displaystyle g_{u}^{\prime}(t_{u}^{\star})

\displaystyle=t_{u}^{\star}A(u)-\dfrac{\gamma}{4}B(u)-\dfrac{a\sigma}{p}(t_{u}^{\star})^{\sigma-1}C(u)=t_{u}^{\star}A(u)-\dfrac{\gamma}{4}B(u)-\dfrac{1}{\sigma-1}t_{u}^{\star}A(u)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sigma-2}{\sigma-1}t_{u}^{\star}A(u)-\dfrac{\gamma}{4}B(u)=\dfrac{\sigma-2}{\sigma-1}\left(\dfrac{pA(u)}{a\sigma(\sigma-1)C(u)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma-2}}A(u)-\dfrac{\gamma}{4}B(u)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{A(u)}\left[\dfrac{\sigma-2}{\sigma-1}\left(\dfrac{pA(u)}{a\sigma(\sigma-1)C(u)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma-2}}\sqrt{A(u)}-\dfrac{\gamma}{4}\dfrac{B(u)}{\sqrt{A(u)}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{A(u)}\left[\dfrac{\sigma-2}{\sigma-1}\left(\dfrac{p[A(u)]^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}}{a\sigma(\sigma-1)C(u)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma-2}}-\dfrac{\gamma}{4}\dfrac{B(u)}{\sqrt{A(u)}}\right].

Applying
Lemma

2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle g_{u}^{\prime}(t_{u}^{\star})

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{A(u)}\left[\dfrac{\sigma-2}{\sigma-1}\left(\dfrac{p[A(u)]^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}}{a\sigma(\sigma-1)K_{GN}[A(u)]^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}c^{\frac{6-p}{4}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma-2}}-\dfrac{\gamma}{4}\dfrac{K_{H}\sqrt{A(u)}c^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{A(u)}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{A(u)}\left[\dfrac{\sigma-2}{\sigma-1}\left(\dfrac{p}{a\sigma(\sigma-1)K_{GN}c^{\frac{6-p}{4}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma-2}}-\dfrac{\gamma}{4}K_{H}c^{\frac{3}{2}}\right].

By direct computations, we now have

.
∎

Lemma 3.5

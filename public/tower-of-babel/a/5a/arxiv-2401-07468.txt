CarSpeedNet: A Deep Neural Network-based Car Speed Estimation from Smartphone Accelerometer
Barak Or

Abstract
We introduce the CarSpeedNet , a deep learning model designed to estimate car speed using three-axis accelerometer data from smartphones. Using 13 hours of data collected from a smartphone in cars across various roads, CarSpeedNet accurately models the relationship between smartphone acceleration and car speed. Ground truth speed data was collected at 1 [Hz] from GPS receivers. The model provides high-frequency speed estimation by incorporating historical data and achieves a precision of less than 0.72 [m/s] during extended driving tests, relying solely on smartphone accelerometer data without any connection to the car.

II
Learning Approach

II-A

Data Acquisition and Dataset Creation

A dataset was collected using a Samsung Galaxy Smartphone, with accelerometer data recorded at 500 Hz. The data, consisting of 13.2 hours of car operation across various terrains, was split into training (10.3 hours), validation (2.6 hours), and testing (0.5 hours) subsets.

The data aggregation was extensively conducted across various geographical terrains within the state of Israel, encompassing an array of road types, predominantly highways and urban streets. It is imperative to note that the data collection was strictly confined to on-road scenarios, deliberately excluding off-road data acquisition. Each driving session commenced only after attaining a stabilized GPS signal, ascertained through the Global Dilution of Precision (GDOP) metric, thereby minimizing locational inaccuracies
[
27
,
4
]
. The raw data underwent meticulous processing, where the IMU data was subjected to low-pass filtering to mitigate noise interference and subsequently downsampled to a frequency of 20 [Hz]. Concurrently, the car speed data, ascertained from the GPS readings, was maintained at a consistent recording frequency of 1 [Hz], and served as the ground truth label.

II-D

The CarSpeedNet

II-D
1
Architecture

The
CarSpeedNet
architecture processes 80 sequential data points from 3-axis accelerometer inputs over 4 seconds (shown in Figure
1
). It features bidirectional LSTM layers, including one with 100 units, capturing long-range dependencies in both directions. The following four LSTM layers further refine the temporal information. Batch Normalization is applied throughout to improve training stability, with reduced numbers of units (50, 20, 20, 20). After the LSTM layers, the model uses three Conv1D layers for feature extraction with 64, 64, and 32 filters each, a constant kernel size of 3, followed by a fully connected layer with 32 parameters, with the final layer outputting the speed estimation. A Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU)
[
32
]
activation function was applied between the layers.

The architectural framework of
CarSpeedNet
is distinguished by its bifurcated structure, systematically designed to address the dual aspects of feature extraction: temporal and spatial: initially, the architecture focuses on the extraction of temporal features. This phase is crucial for discerning the sequential dependencies and dynamics inherent in time-series data, such as the accelerometer readings. By effectively capturing these temporal relationships, the model lays the groundwork for a nuanced understanding of the car’s motion over time. Subsequently, the model shifts its focus to the extraction of spatial features from the temporally processed data. This stage is instrumental in identifying and isolating spatial patterns and correlations within the data, which are critical for comprehending the multi-dimensional nature of the accelerometer inputs.

IV
Conclusions

We introduced
CarSpeedNet
, a deep learning model designed for car speed estimation using smartphone accelerometer data only. The model achieves an error of less than 0.72 [m/s], outperforming traditional car-based systems and offering a universally accessible solution without requiring additional sensors. Larger input windows enhance accuracy, with the trade-off of increased latency. Also, the rigorous data collection and training methodology validate the model’s effectiveness in diverse driving conditions, including slow-speed and stationary states.
CarSpeedNet
provides a robust and scalable solution for intelligent transportation systems, opening opportunities for further applications in traffic management and safety, leveraging existing smartphone technology.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, let
R
be a Noetherian commutative ring of prime characteristic
p>0
, and
I
an ideal of
R
. The key ingredient in study ring of prime characteristic is using the Frobenius endomorphism
F:R\to R;x\mapsto x^{p}
and its
e
-th iterations
F^{e}
,
e\geq 1
. The
e
-th Frobenius power
of
I
is the extension of
I
via
F^{e}
,
I^{[p^{e}]}=(x^{p^{e}}\mid x\in I)
. The
Frobenius closure
of
I
is
I^{F}=\{x\mid x^{p^{e}}\in I^{[p^{e}]}\text{ for some }e\geq 0\}
. Hence
I^{F}
is the set of all nilpotent elements under Frobenius endomorphism modulo
I
. By the Noetherianess of
R
there is an integer
e
, depending on
I
, such that
(I^{F})^{[p^{e}]}=I^{[p^{e}]}
. The
Frobenius test exponent
of
I
is defined by
\mathrm{Fte}(I)=\min\{e\mid(I^{F})^{[p^{e}]}=I^{[p^{e}]}\}
. Under mild conditions,
R
is
F
-pure if and only if
\mathrm{Fte}(I)=0
for all
I
by Hochster
[
6
]
. In general, we can not expect an upper bound for all
\mathrm{Fte}(I)
by Brenner
[
1
]
. Restricting to the class of parameter ideals, for any local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
of dimension
d
we define the
Frobenius test exponent for parameter ideals

\mathrm{Fte}(R)
as follows

\mathrm{Fte}(R)=\min\{e\mid(\mathfrak{q}^{F})^{[p^{e}]}=\mathfrak{q}^{[p^{e}]}\,\text{for all parameter ideals }\,\mathfrak{q}\},

and
\mathrm{Fte}(R)=\infty
if we have no such integer. Katzman and Sharp asked whether
\mathrm{Fte}(R)<\infty
for any (equidimensional) local ring. Moreover they
[
11
]
proved that it is the case when the ring is Cohen-Macaulay. The main idea in
[
11
]
is connecting
\mathrm{Fte}(R)
with an invariant defined by the Frobenius actions on the local cohomology modules
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
, namely the
Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number
of
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
. Recall that the Frobenius endomorphism induces natural Frobenius actions on the local cohomology modules
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
for all
i\geq 0
. The
Frobenius closure of zero submodule
of
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
, denoted by
0^{F}_{H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
, consists nilpotent elements of
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
under the Frobenius action. The Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number of
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is defined by

\mathrm{HSL}(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))=\min\{e\mid 0^{F}_{H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}=\mathrm{Ker}(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\overset{F^{e}}{\longrightarrow}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))\}.

It is proved that
\mathrm{HSL}(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))
always exists. The Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number of
R
is
\mathrm{HSL}(R)=\max\{\mathrm{HSL}(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))\mid i=0,\ldots,d\}
. In fact Katzman and Sharp proved that
\mathrm{Fte}(R)=\mathrm{HSL}(R)
provided
R
is Cohen-Macaulay. In general, we have
\mathrm{Fte}(R)\geq\mathrm{HSL}(R)
for any local ring by
[
9
]
. Beyond the Cohen-Macaulay case, Huneke, Katzman, Sharp, and Yao
[
8
]
showed that
\mathrm{Fte}(R)<\infty
for generalized Cohen-Macaulay local rings by using several concepts and techniques from commutative algebra, namely unconditioned strong d-sequences, cohomological annihilators, and modules of generalized fractions. In 2019, the second author
[
16
]
not only simplified the proof for generalized Cohen-Macaulay rings but also proved
\mathrm{Fte}(R)<\infty
for
weakly F-nilpotent
rings, i.e.
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=0^{F}_{H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
for all
i<d
. Recently, Maddox
[
13
]
extended this result for
generalized weakly F-nilpotent
rings, i.e.
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)/0^{F}_{H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
has finite length for all
i<d
.

Suppose
\mathrm{Fte}(R)<\infty
, and let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
be a system of parameters of
R
. Then for any
t\leq d
we have
\mathrm{Fte}((x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}))\leq\mathrm{Fte}(R)
by using Krull’s intersection theorem. If we do not know about the finiteness of
\mathrm{Fte}(R)
, in which conditions we have
\mathrm{Fte}((x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}))<\infty
? For a regular sequence
\underline{x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
, Katzman and Sharp
[
11
, Corollary 4.3]
showed that there exists an integer
C_{\underline{x}}
such that
\mathrm{Fte}((x_{1}^{n_{1}},\ldots,x_{t}^{n_{t}}))\leq C_{\underline{x}}
for all
n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\geq 1
. Since
C_{\underline{x}}
depends on the choice of
\underline{x}
, the previous result does not answer for the following question.

Question 1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of prime characteristic and of dimension
d
, and
t\leq\mathrm{depth}(R)
an integer. Does there exist a positive integer
C
such that for any regular sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
we have
\mathrm{Fte}((x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}))\leq C
?

Inspired by known results about the finiteness of
\mathrm{Fte}(R)
, we give an affirmative answer for the above question in a more general form. It covers all aforementioned results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d
and of prime characteristic
p>0
, and
t\leq d
a positive integer such that
H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R)/0^{F}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
has finite length for all
j<t
. Then there exists a positive integer
C
such that for any filter regular sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
we have
\mathrm{Fte}((x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}))\leq C
.

In the next section we recall the basic notions and relevant materials. We prove the main theorem in the last section.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Filter regular sequences

In this subsection, the assumption that
R
is of prime characteristic is unnecessary.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring, and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
a sequence of elements of
R
. Then we say that
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
is a
filter regular sequence
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
,

(2)

x_{i}\notin\mathfrak{p}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}\big(\frac{M}{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})M}\big)\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
,
i=1,\ldots,t
.

The notion of filter regular sequences was introduced by Cuong, Schenzel, and Trung in
[
2
]
. The following is well-known.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
. The following are equivalent

(1)

x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
is a filter regular sequence.

(2)

For each
1\leq i\leq t
the quotient

\frac{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}):_{R}x_{i}}{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})}

is an
R
-module of finite length.

(3)

For each
1\leq i\leq t
the sequence

\frac{x_{1}}{1},\frac{x_{2}}{1},\ldots,\frac{x_{i}}{1}

forms an
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-regular sequence for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}))\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
.

(4)

The sequence
x_{1}^{n_{1}},\ldots,x_{t}^{n_{t}}
is a filter regular sequence for all
n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\geq 1
.

(5)

The sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
is a filter regular sequence of
\widehat{R}
, where
\widehat{R}
is the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion of
R
.

2.2.
The Frobenius action on local cohomology

In this subsection, let
R
be a Noetherian ring containing a field of characteristic
p>0
. Let
F:R\to R,x\mapsto x^{p}
denote the Frobenius endomorphism. If we want to notationally distinguish the source and target of the
e
-th Frobenius endomorphism
F^{e}:R\xrightarrow{x\mapsto x^{p^{e}}}R
, we will use
F_{*}^{e}(R)
to denote the target.
F_{*}^{e}(R)
is an
R
-bimodule, which is the same as
R
as
an abelian group and as a right
R
-module, that acquires its left
R
-module structure via the
e
-th Frobenius
endomorphism
F^{e}
. By definition the
e
-th Frobenius endomorphism
F^{e}:R\to F_{*}^{e}(R)
sending
x
to
F_{*}^{e}(x^{p^{e}})=x\cdot F_{*}^{e}(1)
is an
R
-homomorphism. We say
R
is
F
-finite
if
F_{*}(R)
is a finite
R
-module.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
R
, we define

(1)

The
e
-th Frobenius power
of
I
is
I^{[p^{e}]}=(x^{p^{e}}\mid x\in I)
.

(2)

The
Frobenius closure
of
I
,
I^{F}=\{x\mid x^{p^{e}}\in I^{[p^{e}]}\text{ for some }e\geq 0\}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
R
. By the Noetherianess of
R
there is an integer
e
, depending on
I
such that
(I^{F})^{[p^{e}]}=I^{[p^{e}]}
. The smallest number
e
satisfying the condition is called the
Frobenius test exponent of
I
, and denoted by
\mathrm{Fte}(I)
,

\mathrm{Fte}(I)=\min\{e\mid(I^{F})^{[p^{e}]}=I^{[p^{e}]}\}.

A problem of Katzman and Sharp
[
11
, Introduction]
asks in its strongest form: does there exist an integer
e
, depending only on the ring
R
, such that for every ideal
I
we have
(I^{F})^{[p^{e}]}=I^{[p^{e}]}
. A positive answer to
this question, together with the actual knowledge of a bound for
e
, would give an algorithm to compute the Frobenius closure
I^{F}
. Unfortunately, Brenner
[
1
]
gave two-dimensional normal standard graded domains with no Frobenius test exponent. In contrast, Katzman and Sharp showed the existence of Frobenius test exponent if we restrict to class of parameter ideals in a Cohen-Macaulay ring. Therefore it is natural to ask the following question.

Question 2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an (equidimensional) local ring of prime characteristic
p
. Then does there exist an integer
e
such that for every parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
R
we have
(\mathfrak{q}^{F})^{[p^{e}]}=\mathfrak{q}^{[p^{e}]}
?

We define the
Frobenius test exponent for parameter ideals
of
R
,
\mathrm{Fte}(R)
, the smallest integer
e
satisfying the above condition and
\mathrm{Fte}(R)=\infty
if we have no such
e
. It should be noted that the authors recently used the finiteness of
\mathrm{Fte}(R)
, if have, to find an upper bound of the multiplicity of a local ring
[
10
]
.

For any ideal
I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})
, the Frobenius endomorphism
F:R\to R
and its localizations induce a natural Frobenius action on local cohomology
F:H^{i}_{I}(R)\to H^{i}_{I^{[p]}}(R)\cong H^{i}_{I}(R)
for all
i\geq 0
. In general, let
A
be an Artinian
R
-module with a Frobenius action
F:A\to A
. Then we define the
Frobenius closure
0^{F}_{A}
of the zero submodule
of
A
is the submodule of
A
consisting all elements
z
such that
F^{e}(z)=0
for some
e\geq 0
. Hence
0^{F}_{A}
is the nilpotent part of
A
under the Frobenius action. By
[
5
, Proposition 1.11]
,
[
12
, Proposition 4.4]
and
[
17
]
we have the following.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of prime characteristic
p>0
, and
A
an Artinian
R
-module with a Frobenius action
F:A\to A
. Then there exists a non-negative integer
e
such that
0^{F}_{A}=\operatorname{Ker}(A\overset{F^{e}}{\longrightarrow}A)
.

Definition 2.6
.

(1)

Let
A
be an Artinian
R
-module with a Frobenius action
F
. The
Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number
of
A
is denoted by
\mathrm{HLS}(A)
and is defined to be

\mathrm{HSL}(A)=\min\{e\mid 0^{F}_{A}=\operatorname{Ker}(A\overset{F^{e}}{\longrightarrow}A)\}.

(2)

Notice that
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is always Artinian for all
i\geq 0
. We define the
Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number
of a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
as follows

\mathrm{HSL}(R):=\min\{e\mid 0^{F}_{H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}=\operatorname{Ker}(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\overset{F^{e}}{\longrightarrow}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))\text{ for all }i=0,\ldots,d\}.

As mentioned in the introduction, Question
2
was answered affirmatively in the following cases:

(1)

(Katzman-Sharp)
R
is Cohen-Macaulay ring. Moreover,
\mathrm{Fte}(R)=\mathrm{HSL}(R)
.

(2)

(Huneke-Katzman-Sharp-Yao)
R
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay ring, i.e.
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
has finite length for all
i<d
.

(3)

(Quy)
R
is weakly
F
-nilpotent ring, i.e.
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=0^{F}_{H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
for all
i<d
.

(4)

(Maddox)
R
is generalized weakly
F
-nilpotent ring, i.e.
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)/0^{F}_{H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
has finite length for all
i<d
.

The main idea of the proofs of the affirmative cases is to make a connection between the Frobenius test exponent for parameter ideals with Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik numbers of the local cohomology modules. For example, if
R
is weakly
F
-nilpotent, then the second author
[
16
]
used the notion of relative Frobenius action on local cohomology to prove

\mathrm{Fte}(R)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{d}{i}\mathrm{HSL}(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)).

2.3.
The relative Frobenius action on local cohomology

In this subsection, we recall the notion of relative Frobenius action on local cohomology which was introduced in
[
14
]
by Polstra and Quy in study
F
-nilpotent rings. Let
K\subseteq I
be ideals of
R
. The Frobenius endomorphism
F:R/K\to R/K
can be factored as composition of two natural maps:

R/K\to R/K^{[p]}\twoheadrightarrow R/K,

where the second map is the natural project map. We denote the first map by
F_{R}:R/K\to R/K^{[p]}
,
F_{R}(a+K)=a^{p}+K^{[p]}
for all
a\in R
. The homomorphism
F_{R}
induces
the relative Frobenius actions on local cohomology

F_{R}:H^{i}_{I}(R/K)\to H^{i}_{I}(R/K^{[p]})
via Čech complexes.

Definition 2.7
.

(1)

We define the
relative Frobenius closure of the zero submodule of
H^{i}_{I}(R/K)
with respect to
R
as follows

0^{F_{R}}_{H^{i}_{I}(R/K)}=\{\eta\mid F^{e}_{R}(\eta)=0\in H^{i}_{I}(R/K^{[p^{e}]})\text{ for some }e\gg 0\}.

(2)

If there is an integer
e
such that

0^{F_{R}}_{H^{i}_{I}(R/K)}=\operatorname{Ker}(H^{i}_{I}(R/K)\xrightarrow{F^{e}_{R}}H^{i}_{I}(R/K^{[p^{e}]})),

then we call the smallest of such integers the
Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number of
H^{i}_{I}(R/K)
with respect to
R
, denoted by
\mathrm{HSL}_{R}(H^{i}_{I}(R/K))
. And convention that
\mathrm{HSL}_{R}(H^{i}_{I}(R/K))=\infty
if we have no such integer.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring, and
I
an ideal of
R
. Then

0^{F_{R}}_{H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(R/I)}=I^{F}\cap(I:\mathfrak{m}^{\infty})/I.

Proof.

Pick
a+I\in 0^{F_{R}}_{H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(R/I)}
. We have
a\in I:\mathfrak{m}^{\infty}
and there exists an integer
e
such that
F^{e}(a+I)=a^{p^{e}}+I^{[p^{e}]}=0\in R/I^{[p^{e}]}
. Equivalently,
a+I\in I^{F}\cap(I:\mathfrak{m}^{\infty})/I
.
∎

3.
Main result

Throughout this section, let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of prime characteristic
p>0
and of dimension
d
, and
t\leq d
an integer such that
H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R)/0^{F}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
has finite length for all
j<t
. Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
be a filter regular sequence. Set
I_{i}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})
for all
i\leq t
and
I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of prime characteristic
p>0
and of dimension
d
, and
t\leq d
an integer such that
H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R)/0^{F}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
has finite length for all
j<t
. Let
n_{0}
be an non-negative integer such that
\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R)/0^{F}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}=0
for all
j<t
. Then for every filter regular sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
we have

\mathfrak{m}^{2^{i}n_{0}}H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i})\subseteq 0^{F_{R}}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i})}

for all
i\leq t
and all
j<t-i
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
i
. The case
i=0
is nothing to do.
Suppose
i>0
and the assertion holds true for
i-1
. For each
e\geq 1
we consider commutative diagram

\begin{CD}0\longrightarrow R/(I_{i-1}:x_{i})@>{x_{i}}>{}>R/I_{i-1}@>{}>{}>R/I_{i}@>{}>{}>0\\
@V{(F^{e}_{R})^{\prime}}V{}V@V{F^{e}_{R}}V{}V@V{F^{e}_{R}}V{}V(\star)\\
0\longrightarrow R/(I_{i-1}^{[p^{e}]}:x_{i}^{p^{e}})@>{x_{i}^{p^{e}}}>{}>R/I_{i-1}^{[p^{e}]}@>{}>{}>R/I_{i}^{[p^{e}]}@>{}>{}>0\end{CD}

where the first vertical map is the composition

R/(I_{i-1}:x_{i})\xrightarrow{F^{e}_{R}}R/(I_{i-1}:x_{i})^{[p^{e}]}\twoheadrightarrow R/(I_{i-1}^{[p^{e}]}:x_{i}^{p^{e}}).

Because
x_{1}^{p^{e}},\ldots,x_{t}^{p^{e}}
is a filter regular sequence,
(I_{i-1}^{[p^{e}]}:x_{i}^{p^{e}})/I_{i-1}^{[p^{e}]}
has finite length. Thus

H^{j+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/(I_{i-1}^{[p^{e}]}:x_{i}^{p^{e}}))\cong H^{j+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/(I_{i-1}:x_{i})^{[p^{e}]})\cong H^{j+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i-1}^{[p^{e}]})

for all
j\geq 0
and for all
e\geq 0
. Therefore the diagram
(\star)
induces the following commutative diagram with exact rows

\begin{CD}\cdots\xrightarrow{x_{i}}H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R/I_{i-1})@>{}>{}>H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R/I_{i})@>{\delta}>{}>H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j+1}(R/I_{i-1})\\
@V{F^{e}_{R}}V{}V@V{F^{e}_{R}}V{}V@V{F^{e}_{R}}V{}V\\
\cdots\xrightarrow{x_{i}^{p^{e}}}H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R/I_{i-1}^{[p^{e}]})@>{\alpha}>{}>H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R/I_{i}^{[p^{e}]})@>{\beta}>{}>H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j+1}(R/I_{i-1}^{[p^{e}]}).\end{CD}

Pick any
u\in H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i})
, and
x,y\in\mathfrak{m}^{2^{i-1}n_{0}}
. Then
\delta(u)\in H^{j+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i-1})
. By the inductive hypothesis
x\delta(u)\in 0^{F_{R}}_{H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j+1}(R/I_{i-1})}
, so there is an integer
e
such that

0=F^{e}_{R}(x\delta(u))=\beta(F^{e}_{R}(xu)).

Hence there exists
v\in H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R/I_{i-1}^{[p^{e}]})
such that
\alpha(v)=F^{e}_{R}(xu)
. Moreover,
y^{p^{e}}\in\mathfrak{m}^{2^{i-1}n_{0}}
so
y^{p^{e}}v\in 0^{F_{R}}_{H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R/I_{i-1}^{[p^{e}]})}
by using the induction for the sequence
x_{1}^{p^{e}},\ldots,x_{i-1}^{p^{e}}
. Therefore,

\alpha(y^{p^{e}}v)=y^{p^{e}}.F^{e}_{R}(xu)=F^{e}_{R}(yxu)\in 0^{F_{R}}_{H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R/I_{i}^{[p^{e}]})}.

Leading to
xyu\in 0^{F_{R}}_{H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R/I_{i})}
. Hence
xy\in\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R/I_{i})/0^{F_{R}}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i})})
, and so

\mathfrak{m}^{2^{i}n_{0}}\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R/I_{i})/0^{F_{R}}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i})})

for all
i\leq t
and
j<t-i
. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Using the notation in Lemma
3.1
, and suppose
x_{1}\ldots,x_{t}\in\mathfrak{m}^{2^{t}n_{0}}
. Then we have

\operatorname{Im}(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i-1}:x_{i})\xrightarrow{x_{i}}H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i-1}))\subseteq 0^{F_{R}}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i-1})}

for all
i\leq t
and
j\leq t-i
. Indeed the case
1\leq j\leq t-i
follows from Lemma
3.1
and the fact
H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i-1}:x_{i})\cong H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i-1})
. For
j=0
we use Lemma
3.1
and the surjective map
H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i-1})\twoheadrightarrow H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i-1}:x_{i})
. In particular, every relative nilpotent element of the induced Frobenius action on
\mathrm{Coker}(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i-1}:x_{i})\xrightarrow{x_{i}}H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i-1}))
is an image of some element in
0^{F_{R}}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/I_{i-1})}
.

Using the above Remark and the argument of
[
16
, Proof of the main theorem]
and
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
we obtain the following whose proof is left to the reader.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of prime characteristic
p>0
and of dimension
d
, and
t\leq d
an integer such that
H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R)/0^{F}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
has finite length for all
j<t
. Then there exists a non-negative integer
e_{0}
such that for every filter regular sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
we have

\mathrm{HSL}_{R}(H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})))\leq\sum\limits_{k=0}^{t}\binom{t}{k}\mathrm{HSL}(H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{k}(R))+e_{0}.

Remark 3.4
.

If
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
is a regular sequence then
H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=0
for all
j<t
, and the number
e_{0}
in the previous result can be chosen as zero. Thus we have
\mathrm{HSL}_{R}(H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})))\leq\mathrm{HSL}(H^{t}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))
.

The following is known to experts.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an
F
-finite local ring of dimension
d
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
a prime ideal. Then for all
i\leq d
we have
\mathrm{HSL}(H^{i-\dim R/\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}))\leq\mathrm{HSL}(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))
. In particular,
\mathrm{HSL}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq\mathrm{HSL}(R)
.

Proof.

Since
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is
F
-finite, it is an image of some regular local ring
(S,\mathfrak{n})
by Gabber
[
4
]
. Suppose that
\dim S=n
. For each
i\leq d
, by the definition we have
\mathrm{HLS}(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))
is the smallest non-negative integer
e
such that

\mathrm{Ker}(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\xrightarrow{F^{e}}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(F^{e}_{*}R))=\mathrm{Ker}(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\xrightarrow{F^{e+1}}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(F^{e+1}_{*}R)).

By the local duality theorem we have

\mathrm{Coker}(\mathrm{Ext}^{n-i}_{S}(F^{e}_{*}R,S)\xrightarrow{(F^{e})^{\vee}}\mathrm{Ext}^{n-i}_{S}(R,S))=\mathrm{Coker}(\mathrm{Ext}^{n-i}_{S}(F^{e+1}_{*}R,S)\xrightarrow{(F^{e+1})^{\vee}}\mathrm{Ext}^{n-i}_{S}(R,S)).

Let
P
be the preimage of
\mathfrak{p}
in
S
. Taking localization we have

\mathrm{Coker}(\mathrm{Ext}^{n-i}_{S_{P}}(F^{e}_{*}R_{\mathfrak{p}},S_{P})\to\mathrm{Ext}^{n-i}_{S_{P}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}},S_{P}))=\mathrm{Coker}(\mathrm{Ext}^{n-i}_{S_{P}}(F^{e+1}_{*}R_{\mathfrak{p}},S_{P})\to\mathrm{Ext}^{n-i}_{S_{P}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}},S_{P})).

Applying the local duality theorem for
(R_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})
we have

\mathrm{Ker}(H^{i-\dim R/\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\xrightarrow{F^{e}}H^{i-\dim R/\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(F^{e}_{*}R_{\mathfrak{p}}))=\mathrm{Ker}(H^{i-\dim R/\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\xrightarrow{F^{e+1}}H^{i-\dim R/\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(F^{e+1}_{*}R_{\mathfrak{p}})).

Hence
\mathrm{HSL}(H^{i-\dim R/\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}))\leq e
. The proof is complete.
∎

We are ready to prove the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of dimention
d
and of prime characteristic
p>0
, and
t\leq d
an integer such that
H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R)/0^{F}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
has finite length for all
j<t
. Then there exists an integer
C
such that for any filter regular sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
we have
\mathrm{Fte}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\leq C
.

Proof.

By passing to the completion we can assume that
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is complete. We next use the
\Gamma
-construction of Hochster and Huneke
[
7
]
to obtain a faithfully flat extension
R\to R^{\Gamma}
such that
R^{\Gamma}
is
F
-finite and
\mathfrak{m}R^{\Gamma}
is the maximal ideal of
R^{\Gamma}
. Notice that the induced Frobenius actions on
H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)/0^{F}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
are injecitve for all
j\geq 0
. By
[
3
, Lemmas 2.9 and 4.3]
we can choose a sufficiently small
\Gamma
such that the Frobenius actions on

R^{\Gamma}\otimes_{R}H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)/0^{F}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
are injecitve for all
j\geq 0
. Thus
0^{F}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R^{\Gamma})}\cong R^{\Gamma}\otimes_{R}0^{F}_{H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
. Since
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})^{F}R^{\Gamma}\subseteq((x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})R^{\Gamma})^{F}
, it is enough to prove the requirement for
R^{\Gamma}
. Therefore we can assume henceforth that
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is
F
-finite.

Let
c
be the upper bound in Proposition
3.3
and
h:=\mathrm{HSL}(R)
. Let
I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})
, and pick any
a\in I^{F}\setminus I
. Suppose
\dim R/(I:a)=s
. We will prove
\dim R/(I^{[p^{hs}]}:a^{p^{hs}})\leq 0
by induction on
s
. There is nothing to do if
s=0
. Suppose
s>0
, and let
\mathfrak{p}
be any minimal prime of
R/(I:a)
with
\dim R/\mathfrak{p}=s
. Then
aR_{\mathfrak{p}}\in(IR_{\mathfrak{p}})^{F}
by
[
15
, Lemma 3.3]
. By the minimality we have
a+IR_{\mathfrak{p}}\in H^{0}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/IR_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Since
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
becomes a regular sequence in
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
we have
\mathrm{HSL}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/IR_{\mathfrak{p}}))\leq\mathrm{HSL}(H^{t}_{\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}))\leq h
by Remark
3.4
and Lemma
3.5
. Therefore
F^{h}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(a+IR_{\mathfrak{p}})=0\in R_{\mathfrak{p}}/I^{[p^{h}]}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and so
a^{p^{h}}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\in I^{[p^{h}]}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Hence
R_{\mathfrak{p}}/(I^{[p^{h}]}:a^{p^{h}})R_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Min}(R/(I:a))
with
\dim R/\mathfrak{p}=s
. Thus
\dim R/(I^{[p^{h}]}:a^{p^{h}})\leq s-1
. The claim now follows from the inductive hypothesis for
I^{[p^{h}]}
and
a^{p^{h}}
.

On the other hand
s\leq d-t
, so we always have
\dim R/(I^{[p^{(d-t)h}]}:a^{p^{(d-t)h}})\leq 0
. Moreover,
if
\dim R/(I:a)\leq 0
, then
\bar{a}=a+I\in I^{F}\cap(I:\mathfrak{m}^{\infty})/I=0^{F_{R}}_{H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(R/I)}
by Lemma
2.8
. By Proposition
3.3
we have
F^{c}_{R}(\bar{a})=0
. Thus
a^{p^{c}}\in I^{[p^{c}]}
. Putting all together we have
a^{p^{(d-t)h+c}}\in I^{[p^{(d-t)h+c}]}
for all
a\in I^{F}
, so
\mathrm{Fte}(I)\leq(d-t)h+c
. The proof is complete.
∎

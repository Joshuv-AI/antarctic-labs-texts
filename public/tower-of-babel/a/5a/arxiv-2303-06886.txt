Compressible magnetohydrodynamics as a dissipative system
Jan Březina, Eduard Feireisl

Abstract
We consider the complete system of equations governing the motion of a general compressible, viscous, electrically and heat conductive fluid driven by non–conservative boundary conditions. We show the existence of a bounded absorbing set in the energy space and asymptotic compactness of trajectories. As a corollary, the set of all entire globally bounded solutions is identified as a natural attractor. Examples of boundary conditions giving rise to unbounded solutions are also discussed.

Introduction

Dissipative systems are thermodynamically open systems often in the out of equilibrium regime.
They are examples of the most interesting real world phenomena developing patterns, structures, or behaviours that they did not have when first formed. The dissipative systems exchange energy and matter with their environment,
while dissipating mechanical and other forms of energy in accordance with the Second law of thermodynamics.
In continuum fluid mechanics, dissipative systems are modelled by a family of field equations accompanied
with
inhomogeneous
boundary conditions.

Many spectacular events occurring in geophysics or astrophysics, among which the dynamics of the solar convection
zone, are attributed to the action of the magnetic field, see e.g. Thompson
and Christensen-Dalsgaard
[
35
]
. As direct measurements and data collecting are often hampered by obvious
technical limitations, reliable mathematical models are indispensable for understanding the observed phenomena.
As pointed out by Gough
[
23
]
, a correct choice of boundary conditions plays a crucial role.

The state of a viscous, compressible, electrically and heat conducting fluid is characterized
by its mass density
\varrho=\varrho(t,x)
, the (absolute) temperature
\vartheta=\vartheta(t,x)
, the velocity

{\bf u}={\bf u}(t,x)
and the magnetic field
{\bf B}={\bf B}(t,x)
. The time evolution is governed
by the system of field equations of
compressible magnetohydrodynamics (MHD)
, see e.g. Weiss and Proctor

[
36
]
:

Equation of continuity:

\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})=0.

(1.1)

Momentum equation:

\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf u})+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\varrho{\bf u})+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho,\vartheta)={\rm div}_{x}\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u})+{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}\times{\bf B}+\varrho\nabla_{x}M.

(1.2)

Induction equation:

\partial_{t}{\bf B}+{\bf curl}_{x}({\bf B}\times{\bf u})+{\bf curl}_{x}(\zeta(\vartheta){\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B})=0,\ {\rm div}_{x}{\bf B}=0.

(1.3)

Internal energy balance:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta))+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u})

\displaystyle+{\rm div}_{x}{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\zeta(\vartheta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}|^{2}-p(\varrho,\vartheta){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}.

(1.4)

The system is written in a general
rotating frame

frequently used in astro/geophysics, where
\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\varrho{\bf u}
is the Coriolis force
and the potential
M
,

M=G+\frac{1}{2}|\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x|^{2},

includes the gravitational component
G=G(t,x)
as well as the centrifugal force. Note that
G
may depend on the
time
t
in the rotating frame if the source of gravitation is located outside the fluid domain.

We consider a
Newtonian fluid
,
with the viscous stress tensor

\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u})=\mu(\vartheta)\left(\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\nabla_{x}^{t}{\bf u}-\frac{2}{3}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I}\right)+\eta(\vartheta){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\mathbb{I},

(1.5)

where the viscosity coefficients
\mu>0
and
\eta\geq 0
are continuously differentiable functions of the temperature. Similarly, the heat flux obeys
Fourier’s law
,

{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)=-\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla_{x}\vartheta,

(1.6)

where the heat conductivity coefficient
\kappa>0
is a continuously differentiable function of the temperature.

As pointed out in the introduction, the boundary conditions play a crucial role in the
long time behaviour of the system. We suppose that the fluid occupies a bounded domain
\Omega\subset R^{3}
with a smooth boundary,

\displaystyle\partial\Omega=\Gamma^{\bf u}_{D}\cup\Gamma^{\bf u}_{N}=\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}\cup\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{N}=\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}\cup\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\bf u}_{D},\Gamma^{\bf u}_{N},\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D},\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{N},\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D},\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}\ \mbox{compact},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\bf u}_{D}\cap\Gamma^{\bf u}_{N}=\emptyset,\ \Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}\cap\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{N}=\emptyset,\ \Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}\cap\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}=\emptyset.

(1.7)

Accordingly, each
\Gamma^{*}_{D}
,
\Gamma^{*}_{N}
is either empty or coincides with a finite union of connected components of
\partial\Omega
.
We impose the following boundary conditions:

Boundary velocity:

\displaystyle{\bf u}|_{\Gamma^{\bf u}_{D}}

\displaystyle=0,

(1.8)

\displaystyle{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf u}_{N}}

\displaystyle=0,\ [\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u})\cdot{\bf n}]\times{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf u}_{N}}=0.

(1.9)

Boundary temperature/heat flux:

\displaystyle\vartheta|_{\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}}

\displaystyle=\vartheta_{B},

(1.10)

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{N}}

\displaystyle=0.

(1.11)

Boundary magnetic field:

\displaystyle{\bf B}\times{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}}

\displaystyle={\bf b}_{\tau},

(1.12)

\displaystyle{\bf B}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}}=b_{\nu},\ [({\bf B}\times{\bf u})+\zeta{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}]\times{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}}

\displaystyle=0.

(1.13)

Clearly, the boundary conditions prescribed on
\Gamma^{*}_{D}
are of Dirichlet type, while those on

\Gamma^{*}_{N}
of Neumann type. The fluxes on
\Gamma^{*}_{N}
are set to be zero for the sake of simplicity.
More general fluxes are shortly discussed in Section
5.3
.
In accordance with (
1.8
), (
1.9
), the boundary is impermeable, meaning the fluid velocity
is always tangent to the boundary. In particular, the total mass

m_{0}=\int_{\Omega}\varrho(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(1.14)

is a constant of motion. Accordingly, there is no exchange of mass with the
outer world and the system is therefore driven by the imposed boundary temperature and/or magnetic field.

1.1
Levinson dissipativity

The compressible MHD system admits a natural energy

E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})=\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}}_{\mbox{kinetic energy}}+\underbrace{\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)}_{\mbox{internal energy}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}|^{2}}_{\mbox{magnetic energy}}.

Our goal is to show that the system is
dissipative
in the sense of Levinson in terms of the
total energy

\mathcal{E}=\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

Specifically, there exists a universal constant
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
such that

\limsup_{\tau\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}<\mathcal{E}_{\infty}

(1.15)

for
any
solution
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})
of the compressible MHD system defined on
(T,\infty)
. We point out that
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
depends only on the “data” but it is the same for any global trajectory. In particular, it is independent of the initial energy of the system.

The data are:

•

the total mass
m_{0}
of the fluid, cf. (
1.14
);

•

the gravitational potential
G
;

•

the rotation vector
\boldsymbol{\omega}
;

•

the boundary temperature
\vartheta_{B}
;

•

the boundary tangential magnetic field
{\bf b}_{\tau}
, the boundary normal magnetic field

b_{\nu}
.

We shall use the symbol
\|({\rm data})\|
to denote the norm of the above data in suitable function spaces specified below.

Besides the total mass
m_{0}
, there might be other conserved quantities. As shown e.g. by Bauer, Pauly, and
Schomburg
[
4
]
, see also Kozono and Yanagisawa
[
25
]
, any vector field
{\bf b}
defined on
\Omega
admits a decomposition

{\bf b}=\nabla_{x}P+{\bf h}+{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf A},

(1.16)

where

{\bf h}\in\mathcal{H}(\Omega)=\left\{{\bf h}\in L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})\ \Big|\ {\bf curl}_{x}{\bf h}={\rm div}_{x}{\bf h}=0,{\bf h}\times{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}}=0,\ {\bf h}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}}=0\right\}.

(1.17)

It follows from the induction equation (
1.3
) and our choice of the boundary conditions (
1.12
), (
1.13
) that

\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega}{\bf B}\cdot{\bf h}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=0\ \mbox{for any}\ {\bf h}\in\mathcal{H}(\Omega).

For the sake of simplicity, we shall assume

\int_{\Omega}{\bf B}\cdot{\bf h}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=0\ \mbox{for all}\ {\bf h}\in\mathcal{H}(\Omega).

(1.18)

The general case can be handled in a similar way provided the projection of
{\bf B}
on the space
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)

is included in the “data”. Note that the space
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
is always finite dimensional,
see
[
4
, Section 5.1]
. Moreover, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{H}=\{0\}
whenever
\Omega
is simply connected and
\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}
connected. If
\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}=\partial\Omega
or
\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}=\partial\Omega
,
the dimension of
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
coincides with the first and second Betti number of
\Omega
, respectively, see Kozono and Yanagisawa
[
26
]
.

In view of (
1.15
), the boundary conditions must allow the outflow of the thermal energy.
We suppose
the pressure
p=p(\varrho,\vartheta)
and the internal energy
e=e(\varrho,\vartheta)
are interrelated through
Gibbs’ equation

\vartheta Ds=De+pD\left(\frac{1}{\varrho}\right),

(1.19)

where
s=s(\varrho,\vartheta)
is the entropy. Consequently, the internal energy balance (
1.4
) may be reformulated
in the form of
entropy balance equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta))+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u})

\displaystyle+{\rm div}_{x}\left(\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}+\zeta(\vartheta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}|^{2}\right),

(1.20)

see e.g. Weiss and Proctor
[
36
]
. The quantity on the right–hand side of (
1.20
) represents the
entropy production rate, and, in accordance with the Second law of thermodynamics, it is always non–negative.
Consequently, all forms of energy are eventually transformed to heat that must be allowed to leave through
\partial\Omega
. Thus, necessarily,

\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}\neq\emptyset.

(1.21)

Next, to avoid development of rapidly rotating fluid in a rotationally symmetric domain, we impose

\Gamma^{\bf u}_{D}\neq\emptyset.

(1.22)

Finally, we impose a technical condition to control the boundary magnetic oscillations.
First, we introduce the class of stationary magnetic boundary data.

Definition 1.1
.

[Stationary magnetic field]

We say that the boundary data
b_{\nu}
,
{\bf b}_{\tau}
are
stationary
, if there exists
a continuously differentiable vector field
{\bf B}_{B}
such that

{\rm div}_{x}{\bf B}_{B}=0,\ {\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}_{B}=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\ {\bf B}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}}=b_{\nu},\ {\bf B}_{B}\times{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}}={\bf b}_{\tau}.

Stationarity imposes certain restrictions to be satisfied by
{\bf b}_{\tau}
,

{\rm div}_{\tau}{\bf b}_{\tau}=0\ \mbox{on}\ \Gamma^{\bf B}_{D},\ \mbox{where}\ {\rm div}_{\tau}\ \mbox{denotes the tangential divergence,}

(1.23)

see Auchmuty and Alexander
[
2
]
. To establish the existence of a bounded absorbing set, we need an extra hypothesis imposed on the boundary data if the
magnetic boundary field
is not
stationary, namely

\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}\subset\Gamma^{\bf u}_{D}.

(1.24)

In accordance with Kirchhoff’s rule for currents, we have

{\rm div}_{\tau}{\bf b}_{\tau}+{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}\cdot{\bf n}=0\ \mbox{on}\ \Gamma^{\bf B}_{D},

whenever

{\rm div}_{x}{\bf B}=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\ {\bf B}\times{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}}={\bf b}_{\tau},

see Alexander and Auchmuty
[
2
]
.
Thus prescribing a non–stationary vector field
{\bf b}_{\tau}
yields an inhomogeneous boundary condition satisfied by
the normal component of the electric current
{\bf J}
proportional to
{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}
, cf. Section
5.4
.

1.2
Absence of static solutions - an example from astrophysics

Although all global in time solutions eventually enter a bounded absorbing ball, it is worth noting that the system in question may not admit a static stationary solutions, meaning

{\bf u}=0,\ \varrho=\varrho(x),\ \vartheta=\vartheta(x),\ {\bf B}={\bf B}(x).

Indeed consider a simple model of a solar convective zone discussed by Tao et al.
[
34
]
. The domain
boundary is determined by two concentric balls,

\Omega=\left\{x\in R^{3}\ \Big|\ 0<r_{1}<|x|<r_{2}\right\},

(1.25)

with the gravitational potential
G=\overline{g}\frac{1}{|x|}
. The boundary temperature distribution
is determined by two positive constants
\Theta_{\rm int}
,
\Theta_{\rm ext}
,

\vartheta_{B}=\Theta_{\rm int}\ \mbox{if}\ |x|=r_{1},\ \vartheta_{B}=\Theta_{\rm ext}\ \mbox{if}\ |x|=r_{2},\ 0<\Theta_{\rm ext}<\Theta_{\rm int}.

(1.26)

Finally, the boundary magnetic field is oriented in the normal direction,

\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}=\emptyset,\ {\bf b}_{\tau}=0.

(1.27)

The whole system rotates with the rotation axis
\boldsymbol{\omega}\neq 0
.

Suppose the system (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) endowed with the above boundary conditions admits a static solution,
meaning

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}p(\varrho,\vartheta)

\displaystyle=\varrho\nabla_{x}M+{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}\times{\bf B},

\displaystyle{\bf curl}_{x}(\zeta(\vartheta){\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B})

\displaystyle=0,\ {\rm div}_{x}{\bf B}=0,

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)

\displaystyle=\zeta(\vartheta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}|^{2}.

(1.28)

The first observation is

{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}=0,

which follows easily via multiplying the induction equation on
{\bf B}
and integrating by parts. Consequently,
the system reduces to

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}p(\varrho,\vartheta)=\varrho\nabla_{x}M,\ {\rm div}_{x}{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)=0.

(1.29)

Applying
{\bf curl}_{x}
to the first equation, we get

\nabla_{x}\varrho\times\nabla_{x}M=0.

(1.30)

Similarly, writing

\nabla_{x}p(\varrho,\vartheta)=\partial_{\varrho}p(\varrho,\vartheta)\nabla_{x}\varrho+\partial_{\vartheta}p(\varrho,\vartheta)\nabla_{x}\vartheta=\varrho\nabla_{x}M

we deduce from (
1.30
)
\nabla_{x}\varrho\times\nabla_{x}\vartheta=0
anticipating the condition

\frac{\partial p(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\partial\vartheta}\neq 0.

(1.31)

We conclude

\nabla_{x}M\times\nabla_{x}\vartheta=0.

(1.32)

Since
{\bf q}
is given by Fourier’s law
{\bf q}=-\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla_{x}\vartheta
,
the unique solution
\vartheta
of
{\rm div}_{x}{\bf q}(\varrho,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)=0
with the boundary conditions (
1.26
)
admits a non–zero gradient
\nabla_{x}\vartheta
parallel to
the gravitational force

\nabla_{x}G\times\nabla_{x}\vartheta=0.

Consequently, condition (
1.32
) implies

\nabla_{x}\vartheta\times\nabla_{x}|\boldsymbol{\omega}\times x|^{2}=0,

which is impossible as soon as
\boldsymbol{\omega}\neq 0
.

1.3
Asymptotic compactness

The existence of global–in–time
classical
solutions for the compressible MHD system in the
far from equilibrium regime is not known. Here, we adopt the concept of
weak solution
developed in

[
14
]
. Unfortunately, the weak solutions may not be uniquely determined by the initial/boundary data.
Adapting the approach of Sell
[
31
]
, see also Málek and Nečas
[
29
]
, we consider solutions
of the compressible MHD system in the space of
trajectories
with a natural group operation of
forward time shift:

(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(t,\cdot)\mapsto(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(t+\tau),\ \tau\geq 0.

Anticipating the existence of a bounded absorbing set, we consider a sequence of
global in time solutions
(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n},{\bf B}_{n})
along with a sequence of time shifts

\tau_{n}\to\infty
. We say the system is
asymptotically compact
if

(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n},{\bf B}_{n})(\cdot+\tau_{n})\to(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})\ \mbox{locally on compact time intervals}\ (-L,L),

where
(\varrho,{\bf u},\vartheta,{\bf B})
is a solution of the same problem with suitable data defined for
t\in(-\infty,\infty)
. Here “suitable data” means limits of the corresponding time shifts of the original data. In particular,
the problem is the same if the data are independent of time.

Solutions defined for all
t\in(-\infty,\infty)
are called
entire solutions
. The set of all bounded
entire solutions,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\Big\{(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})\ \Big|

\displaystyle(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})-\mbox{solution of the compressible (MHD) system for}\ t\in(-\infty,\infty)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})\ \,{\rm d}{x}<\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\Big\}

is shift invariant and captures the essential features of the long time behaviour of the system.

2
Main hypothesis, weak solutions

Before introducing the concept of weak solution, let us state the main hypotheses concerning the structural properties
of the constitutive relations.

2.1
Equation of state

The hypotheses imposed on the form of the equations of state are
the same as in
[
15
, Chapter 4]
. They are based on the Second law of thermodynamics enforced
through Gibbs’ relation (
1.19
) and the
hypothesis of thermodynamics stability

\frac{\partial p(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\partial\varrho}>0,\ \frac{\partial e(\varrho,\vartheta)}{\partial\vartheta}>0.

(2.1)

We suppose the pressure obeys the
thermal equation of state

p(\varrho,\vartheta)=p_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)+p_{R}(\vartheta),\ p_{R}(\vartheta)=\frac{a}{3}\vartheta^{4},\ a>0,

(2.2)

where
p_{R}
is the radiation pressure. Augmenting the pressure by a radiation component is not
only technically convenient but also relevant to problems in astrophysics, cf.
Battaner
[
3
]
. The internal energy satisfies the
caloric equation of state

e(\varrho,\vartheta)=e_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)+e_{R}(\varrho,\vartheta),\ e_{R}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\frac{a}{\varrho}\vartheta^{4}.

(2.3)

The gas pressure components
p_{M}
and
e_{M}
satisfy the relation characteristic for monoatomic gases:

p_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\frac{2}{3}\varrho e_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta).

(2.4)

Now, it follows from Gibbs’ relation (
1.19
) applied to
p_{M}
,
e_{M}
that they must take a general form

\displaystyle p_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\vartheta^{\frac{5}{2}}P\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right),\ e_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\vartheta^{\frac{5}{2}}}{\varrho}P\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

(2.5)

for some the function
P\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
. In addition, the hypothesis of thermodynamics stability (
2.1
)
applied to
p_{M}
,
e_{M}
yields

P(0)=0,\ P^{\prime}(Z)>0,\ \frac{\frac{5}{3}P(Z)-P^{\prime}(Z)Z}{Z}>0\ \mbox{for all}\ Z\geq 0.

(2.6)

In accordance with (
2.5
), the entropy takes the form

s(\varrho,\vartheta)=s_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)+s_{R}(\varrho,\vartheta),\ s_{M}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right),\ s_{R}(\varrho,\vartheta)=\frac{4a}{3}\frac{\vartheta^{3}}{\varrho},

(2.7)

where

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(Z)=-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\frac{5}{3}P(Z)-P^{\prime}(Z)Z}{Z^{2}}.

(2.8)

Finally, we impose two technical but physically grounded hypotheses
in the degenerate area

\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{\frac{3}{2}}}=Z>>1.

First, it follows from
(
2.6
) that the function
Z\mapsto P(Z)/Z^{\frac{5}{3}}
is decreasing, and we suppose

\lim_{Z\to\infty}\frac{P(Z)}{Z^{\frac{5}{3}}}=p_{\infty}>0.

(2.9)

Second, by the same token, the function
Z\mapsto\mathcal{S}(Z)
decreasing, and we suppose

\lim_{Z\to\infty}\mathcal{S}(Z)=0.

(2.10)

Hypothesis (
2.9
) reflects the effect of the electron pressure in the degenerate area, while
(
2.10
) is nothing other than the Third law of thermodynamics.

It follows from (
2.5
)–(
2.10
) that

\displaystyle p(\varrho,\vartheta)

\displaystyle\approx\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta),

\displaystyle\varrho^{\frac{5}{3}}+\vartheta^{4}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}p(\varrho,\vartheta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\varrho^{\frac{5}{3}}+\vartheta^{4}+1,

\displaystyle 0\leq\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\vartheta^{3}+\varrho\left(1+[\log\varrho]^{+}+[\log\vartheta]^{+}\right).

(2.11)

Here and hereafter, the symbol
a\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}b
means there is a positive constant
c
such that
a\leq cb
,
the symbol
a\approx b
is used to denote
a\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}b
and
b\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}a
.
The reader may consult
[
15
, Chapter 4]
for the physical background of the above hypotheses as well
as the proof of (
2.1
).

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\mu}\left(1+\vartheta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\mu}\left(1+\vartheta\right),\ |\mu^{\prime}(\vartheta)|\leq c\ \mbox{for all}\ \vartheta\geq 0,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\eta(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\eta}\left(1+\vartheta\right),

2.3
Boundary data

It is convenient to assume the boundary data are given as restrictions of functions defined on
\Omega
and for
any
t\in R
. Specifically,

\vartheta_{B}=\tilde{\vartheta}|_{\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}},\ {\bf b}_{\tau}={\bf B}_{B}\times{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}},\ b_{\nu}={\bf B}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}}

for suitable extensions
\tilde{\vartheta}
,
{\bf B}_{B}
.
Accordingly, certain regularity of the boundary data is necessary.

We suppose the boundary temperature
\vartheta_{B}=\vartheta_{B}(t,x)
belongs to the class

\displaystyle\vartheta_{B}

\displaystyle\in BC(R;C^{2}(\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D})),\ \partial_{t}\vartheta_{B}\in BC(R;C^{1}(\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D})),

\displaystyle\inf_{R\times\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}}\vartheta_{B}

\displaystyle>0.

(2.15)

Similarly, we suppose

\displaystyle{\bf b}_{\tau}\in BC(R;C^{2}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D};R^{3})),\ \partial_{t}{\bf b}_{\tau}\in BC(R;C^{1}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D};R^{3}))

(2.16)

\displaystyle b_{\nu}\in BC(R;C^{2}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N})),\ \partial_{t}b_{\nu}\in BC(R;C^{1}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N})).

(2.17)

Moreover, we impose the necessary compatibility conditions

{\bf b}_{\tau}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}}=0,\ \mbox{and}\ \int_{\partial\Omega}b_{\nu}\ {\rm d}\sigma=0\ \mbox{if}\ \Gamma^{{\bf B}}_{D}=\emptyset.

(2.18)

Finally, we introduce the space

H_{0,\sigma}=\left\{{\bf b}\in L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})\ \Big|\ {\bf curl}_{x}{\bf b}\in L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3}),\ {\rm div}_{x}{\bf b}=0,{\bf b}\times{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}}=0,\ {\bf b}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}}=0\right\}.

(2.19)

The space

is a Hilbert space with the norm
\|{\bf b}\|^{2}_{H_{0}}=\|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf b}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}+\|{\bf b}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}
. The space
\mathcal{H}
introduced in (

) is
a closed subspace of
H_{0,\sigma}
, and the following version of Poincaré inequality

\|{\bf b}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf b}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\ \mbox{holds for all}\ {\bf b}\in\mathcal{H}^{\perp}\cap H_{0,\sigma},

(2.20)

see Csató, Kneuss and Rajendran
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
.

The specific extensions of the boundary data will be constructed in Section
3.1
.

Definition 2.1
.

(Global in time weak solutions)

Momentum equation.

\varrho{\bf u}\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(T,\infty;L^{\frac{5}{4}}(\Omega;R^{3}))

3
Bounded absorbing set

We are ready to state the first main result of the present paper concerning the existence of a bounded absorbing set. It is convenient to suppose that both the potential
G
as well as the
boundary data
\vartheta_{B}
,
{\bf b}_{\tau}
, and

\displaystyle\|(\mbox{data})\|

\displaystyle=m_{0}+m_{0}^{-1}+\|G\|_{W^{1,\infty}(R\times\Omega)}+|\boldsymbol{\omega}|+\|\vartheta_{B}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(R\times\partial\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D})}

(3.1)

bounded for bounded

Theorem 3.1
.

[Bounded absorbing set]

Let

be a bounded domain of class

1.7
). Let the pressure
p
be related to the internal energy

through the equations of state (

), (

), (
2.4

satisfy (
2.9

2.10

Let the transport coefficients
\mu
,
\eta
,
\kappa
, and
\zeta

be continuously differentiable functions of the temperature satisfying (
2.12
)–(
2.14
). Finally, let

and let the boundary data belong to the class (
2.15
)–(
2.18
), where

\Gamma^{\bf u}_{D}\neq\emptyset,\ \Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}\neq\emptyset.

(3.2)

If, in addition, the boundary magnetic field

,

is not stationary for some sequence of times

(3.3)

Then there exists a positive constant
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(\|({\rm data})\|)

(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of Theorem

. A similar result in the context of the

]
. The influence of the magnetic field on the

[

]
. In particular,
we construct a two component extension of the boundary magnetic field, where the first component

As already pointed out, the condition

3.1
Extension of the boundary data

We start by specifying the extension of the boundary data for the temperature and the magnetic field.

3.1.1
Extension of the boundary temperature

Given
\vartheta_{B}
we consider its harmonic extension
\tilde{\vartheta}
,

\Delta_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}(\tau,\cdot)=0,\ \tilde{\vartheta}(\tau,\cdot)|_{\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}}=\vartheta_{B}(\tau,\cdot),\ \nabla_{x}\tilde{\vartheta}(\tau,\cdot)\cdot{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{N}}=0,\ \tau\in R.

(3.4)

We shall use the same symbol
\vartheta_{B}
for the boundary data and their extension

hereafter.

3.1.2
Extension of the boundary magnetic field

If the boundary data

,

are stationary, we simply consider
the solenoidal irrotational extension

claimed in Definition

Extension of the

(3.5)

Indeed we may consider
\widetilde{{\bf B}}_{N}=\nabla_{x}\Phi_{N}
, where

is the unique solution of the mixed
boundary value problem

\Delta_{x}\Phi_{N}=0,\ \Phi_{N}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}}=0,\ \nabla_{x}\Phi_{N}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}}=b_{\nu}.

To find a suitable extension of the Dirichlet data
{\bf b}_{\tau}
, we introduce the so–called
Bogovskii operator

enjoying the following properties, see e.g. Galdi
[
21
]
, Geißert, Heck, and Hieber
[

•

is a bounded linear operator mapping

to

, where

and the symbol

denotes the space of

functions with zero mean:

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}\mathcal{B}[f]=f\ \mbox{in}\ Q\ \mbox{for any}\ f\in L^{q}(Q),\ \int_{Q}f=0,\ \mathcal{B}[f]|_{\partial Q}=0,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}[f]\|_{W^{1,q}(Q;R^{3})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(Q)},\ 1<q<\infty.

(3.6)

•

If, moreover,
f={\rm div}_{x}{\bf g}
,

,

\|\mathcal{B}[{\rm div}_{x}{\bf g}]\|_{L^{r}(Q;R^{3})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\|{\bf g}\|_{L^{r}(Q;R^{3})},\ 1<r<\infty.

(3.7)

Now, consider the boundary vector

{\bf B}_{D}={\bf n}\times{\bf b}_{\tau}\ \mbox{on}\ \Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}\ \Rightarrow\ {\bf B}_{D}\times{\bf n}={\bf b}_{\tau}\ \mbox{on}\ \Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}.

(3.8)

First, let
\widetilde{{\bf B}}_{D}
be an arbitrary, not necessarily solenoidal, Lipschitz extension of
{\bf B}_{D}
inside
\Omega
.
Consider a cut-off function

\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}[0,1),\ 0\leq\chi\leq 1,\ \chi(z)=1\ \mbox{for}\ z\in[0,d),0<d<1,

together with

{\bf B}_{\delta,D}(t,x)=\chi\left(\frac{{\rm dist}[x,\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}]}{\delta}\right)\widetilde{{\bf B}}_{D}(t,x),\ \delta>0.

It follows from (

) that

\displaystyle{\bf B}_{\delta,D}\times{\bf n}

\displaystyle={\bf b}_{\tau}\ \mbox{on}\ \Gamma^{\bf B}_{D},

\displaystyle{\rm supp}[{\bf B}_{\delta,D}]

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{U}_{\delta_{0}}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D})\ \mbox{for all}\ 0<\delta\leq\delta_{0},

(3.9)

where
\mathcal{U}_{\delta_{0}}
denotes the
\delta_{0}-
neighbourhood.

Next, fix
\delta_{0}
so that
\mathcal{U}_{\delta_{0}}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D})\cap\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}=\emptyset
and consider

\widetilde{{\bf B}}_{\delta,D}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\bf B}_{\delta,D}-\mathcal{B}_{\delta_{0}}[{\rm div}_{x}{\bf B}_{\delta,D}]\ \mbox{in}\ \mathcal{U}_{\delta_{0}}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D})\cap\Omega,\\
\\
0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega\setminus\mathcal{U}_{\delta_{0}}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D})\end{array}\right.,

(3.10)

where
\mathcal{B}_{\delta_{0}}
is the Bogovskii operator defined on each component of the open set

enjoys the following properties:

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\bf B}}_{\delta,D}\times{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}}

\displaystyle={\bf b}_{\tau},\ \widetilde{{\bf B}}_{\delta,D}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}}=0,

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}\widetilde{{\bf B}}_{\delta,D}

\displaystyle=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega.

(3.11)

In addition, as a consequence of boundedness of the Bogovskii operator (

3.1.2

\|\widetilde{{\bf B}}_{\delta,D}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega;R^{3})}\leq c(p,\delta)\|{\bf b}_{\tau}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D})},\ 1<p<\infty.

(3.13)

3.2
Ballistic energy inequality

The proof of Theorem
3.1
is based on the ballistic energy inequality (
2.29
) evaluated for

\tilde{\vartheta}=\vartheta_{B}
,
{\bf B}_{B}={\bf B}_{\delta,B}
specified in Section
3.1
. In accordance with (
3.15
),
we get:

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}|^{2}-\vartheta_{B}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)-{\bf B}_{\delta,B}\cdot{\bf B}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\vartheta_{B}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}+\zeta(\vartheta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}|^{2}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad\leq-\int_{\Omega}\left(\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)\partial_{t}\vartheta_{B}+\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta){\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}+\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{B}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}\Big({\bf B}\cdot\partial_{t}{\bf B}_{\delta,B}-({\bf B}\times{\bf u})\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}\widetilde{{\bf B}}_{\delta,D}-\zeta(\vartheta){\bf curl}_{x}\ {\bf B}\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}\widetilde{{\bf B}}_{\delta,D}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\varrho\nabla_{x}M\cdot{\bf u}\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

(3.16)

in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(T,\infty)
. Note that we may suppose
{\bf curl}_{x}\widetilde{{\bf B}}_{\delta,D}=0
if the boundary
magnetic field is stationary.

Remark 3.3
.

Strictly speaking, the pointwise values of the ballistic energy are defined only for a.a.
\tau\in(T,\infty)
. However, thanks to the inequality (
3.16
), we may identify
the ballistic energy with its càglàd representative

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}|^{2}-\vartheta_{B}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)-{\bf B}_{\delta,B}\cdot{\bf B}\right)(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad={\rm ess}\lim_{t\to\tau-}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}|^{2}-\vartheta_{B}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)-{\bf B}_{\delta,B}\cdot{\bf B}\right)(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

(3.17)

defined for any
\tau>T
.

Our goal is to show that all terms on the right–hand side of (
3.16
) can be controlled by the dissipation
rate appearing on the left–hand side. This is relatively straightforward for the integrals independent of the
density.

3.2.1
Coercivity of the dissipation rate

It follows from coercivity of the transport coefficients stated in the hypotheses (
2.12
)–(
2.14
),
and hypothesis (
3.2
)
\Gamma^{\bf u}_{D}\neq\emptyset
that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\vartheta_{B}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}+\zeta(\vartheta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}|^{2}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle>}}{{\sim}}\inf\vartheta_{B}\left[\|{\bf u}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}+\|\nabla_{x}\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}+\|\nabla_{x}\log\vartheta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}+\|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\right].

In addition, as
\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}\neq\emptyset
, we may apply Poincaré inequality obtaining

\|\nabla_{x}\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}+\|\nabla_{x}\log\vartheta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle>}}{{\sim}}\|\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}+\|\log\vartheta\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}-c(\|({\rm data})\|).

Similarly, by virtue of inequality (
2.20
),

\|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle>}}{{\sim}}\|{\bf B}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}^{2}-c(\|({\rm data})\|).

Indeed we may use the extension
{\bf B}_{\delta_{0},B}
of the boundary field constructed in Section

3.1.2
, together with its projection
\widehat{{\bf B}}_{\delta_{0},B}
on the space
\mathcal{H}^{\perp}

to deduce

\displaystyle\|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}

\displaystyle=\|{\bf curl}_{x}({\bf B}-\widehat{{\bf B}}_{\delta_{0},B})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3})}-c_{1}(\|({\rm data})\|)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle>}}{{\sim}}\|{\bf B}-\widehat{{\bf B}}_{\delta_{0},B}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}-c_{1}(\|({\rm data})\|)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle>}}{{\sim}}\|{\bf B}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}-c_{2}(\|({\rm data})\|).

It is important to notice that the above estimate is independent of the value of the parameter
\delta
to be fixed
later in the course of the proof.

Summing up the previous estimates we may infer that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+1)}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+1)}\int_{\Omega}|F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})|(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq c(K,\|({\rm data})\|)\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\left[1+\int_{\Omega}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\|\vartheta^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}\varrho^{\frac{5}{3}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\|{\bf B}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})}\right]\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}(K,\|({\rm data})\|)\left[1+c_{2}(r,K,\|({\rm data})\|)\left(\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+1)}\int_{\Omega}|F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right)^{\frac{6}{11}}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\mathcal{S}(r)\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+1)}\int_{\Omega}|F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right].

(3.43)

Thus choosing
r=r(K,\|({\rm data})\|)
so large that

c_{1}(K,\|({\rm data})\|)\mathcal{S}(r)<1

in (
3.43
) we obtain the desired conclusion (
3.33
).

∎

Having proved Lemma
3.4
we are ready to finish the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Suppose that

\limsup_{t\to T-}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\mathcal{E}_{T}<\infty.

In view of (
3.31
),

\limsup_{t\to T-}\int_{\Omega}F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{E}_{T},\|({\rm data})\|)<\infty.

Fix
K=1
in Lemma
3.4
. First we claim there is
\tau\geq T
, where
\tau
depends only on

\mathcal{F}_{T}
and
\|({\rm data})\|
such that

\int_{\Omega}F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq L(1,\|({\rm data})\|),

(3.44)

where
L(1,\|({\rm data})\|)
is the bound in (
3.33
). More precisely,

\tau-T\leq[\mathcal{F}_{T}+|\Omega|\inf F]+1

since otherwise, in accordance with Lemma
3.4
, we would have

\int_{\Omega}F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}<\int_{\Omega}\inf F\ \,{\rm d}{x}.

Next, we claim the implication

\int_{\Omega}F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq L(1,\|({\rm data})\|)\ \Rightarrow\ \int_{\Omega}F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(\tau+1,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq L(1,\|({\rm data})\|).

Indeed either

\int_{\Omega}F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{\Omega}F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(\tau+1,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq 1

and the desired conclusion follows from Lemma
3.4
, or

\int_{\Omega}F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(\tau+1,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}<\int_{\Omega}F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}-1<L(1,\|({\rm data})\|).

Thus we have a sequence

\tau+n,\ n=0,1,\dots,\ \int_{\Omega}F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(\tau+n,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq L(1,\|({\rm data})\|)

and the desired uniform bound

\sup_{t\geq\tau}\int_{\Omega}F(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(t,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\|({\rm data})\|)

follows from (
3.28
) (with
r=1
) by the standard Gronwall argument.
Clearly, in view of (
3.31
), this yields a similar bound for
\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})\ \,{\rm d}{x}
as
claimed in Theorem
3.1
.

4
Asymptotic compactness

The proof of asymptotic compactness basically mimicks the proof of sequential stability of global in time weak solutions
with the necessary modification to ensure compactness of the density.

We consider a family of data locally
precompact
in the appropriate function spaces. Specifically,

0<\underline{m}_{0}\leq m_{0,n}\leq\overline{m}_{0},\ |\boldsymbol{\omega}_{n}|\leq\omega,

(4.1)

\|G_{n}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(R\times\Omega)}\leq\overline{G},\ (G_{n})_{n\geq 1}\ \mbox{precompact in}\ W^{1,\infty}([-N,N]\times\Omega)\ \mbox{for any}\ N>0.

(4.2)

Similarly,

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\vartheta}\leq\vartheta_{B,n},\

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in R}\left(\|\vartheta_{B,n}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D})}+\|\partial_{t}\vartheta_{B,n}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D})}\right)\leq\overline{\vartheta},

\displaystyle(\vartheta_{B,n})_{n\geq 1}\ \mbox{precompact in}\ C([-N,N];C^{2}(\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}))\ \mbox{for any}\ N>0,

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\vartheta_{B,n})_{n\geq 1}\ \mbox{precompact in}\ C([-N,N];C^{1}(\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}))\ \mbox{for any}\ N>0;

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in R}

\displaystyle\left(\|{\bf b}_{\tau,n}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D};R^{3})}+\|\partial_{t}{\bf b}_{\tau,n}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D};R^{3})}\right)\leq\overline{B}_{\tau},

\displaystyle({\bf b}_{\tau,n})_{n\geq 1}\ \mbox{precompact in}\ C([-N,N];C^{2}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D};R^{3}))\ \mbox{for any}\ N>0,

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}{\bf b}_{\tau,n})_{n\geq 1}\ \mbox{precompact in}\ C([-N,N];C^{1}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D};R^{3}))\ \mbox{for any}\ N>0;

(4.4)

and

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in R}

\displaystyle\left(\|{b}_{\nu,n}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N})}+\|\partial_{t}{\bf b}_{\nu,n}(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N})}\right)\leq\overline{B}_{\nu},

\displaystyle({b}_{\nu,n})_{n\geq 1}\ \mbox{precompact in}\ C([-N,N];C^{2}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}))\ \mbox{for any}\ N>0,

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}{b}_{\nu,n})_{n\geq 1}\ \mbox{precompact in}\ C([-N,N];C^{1}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}))\ \mbox{for any}\ N>0.

(4.5)

Theorem 4.1
.

[Asymptotic compactness]

Let the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
hold. Suppose that
(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n},{\bf B}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a
sequence of weak solutions to the compressible MHD system on
(T_{n},\infty)
,
T_{n}\geq-\infty
,
T_{n}\to-\infty
,
with the data

(m_{0,n},\omega_{n},G_{n},\vartheta_{B,n},{\bf b}_{\tau,n},b_{n,n})_{n\geq 1}

specified in
(
4.1
)–(
4.5
),

\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{n}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=m_{0,n},\ \int_{\Omega}{\bf B}_{n}\cdot{\bf h}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=0\ \mbox{for all}\ {\bf h}\in\mathcal{H}(\Omega;R^{3}).

In addition, suppose

\limsup_{\tau\to T_{n}+}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho_{n},\vartheta_{n},{\bf u}_{n},{\bf B}_{n})(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}<\mathcal{E}_{0}<\infty

uniformly for all
n=1,2,\dots
.

Then there is a subsequence (not relabelled here) such that

\displaystyle\varrho_{n}

\displaystyle\to\varrho\ \mbox{in}\ C_{\rm weak}([-N,N];L^{\frac{5}{3}}(\Omega))\ \mbox{and in}\ C([-N,N];L^{1}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\vartheta_{n}

\displaystyle\to\vartheta\ \mbox{in}\ L^{q}([-N,N];L^{4}(\Omega))\ \mbox{and weakly in}\ L^{2}(-N,N;W^{1,2}(\Omega)),\ 1\leq q<\infty,

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{n}

\displaystyle\to{\bf u}\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{2}(-N,N;W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})),

\displaystyle{\bf B}_{n}

\displaystyle\to{\bf B}\ \mbox{in}\ L^{2}(-N,N;L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3}))\ \mbox{and weakly in}\ L^{2}(-N,N;W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})),

(4.6)

and

\displaystyle m_{0,n}

\displaystyle\to m_{0}>0,\ \boldsymbol{\omega}_{n}\to\boldsymbol{\omega},

\displaystyle G_{n}

\displaystyle\to G\ \mbox{in}\ W^{1,\infty}([-N,N]\times\Omega),

\displaystyle\vartheta_{n,B}

\displaystyle\to\vartheta_{B}\ \mbox{in}\ C([-N,N];C^{2}(\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D})),\ \inf_{R\times\Omega}\vartheta_{B}>0,

\displaystyle{\bf b}_{\tau,n}

\displaystyle\to{\bf b}_{\tau}\ \mbox{in}\ C([-N,N];C^{2}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D};R^{3})),

\displaystyle b_{\nu,n}

\displaystyle\to b_{\nu}\ \mbox{in}\ C([-N,N];C^{2}(\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}))

(4.7)

for any
N>0
, where
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})
is a weak solution of the compressible MHD system with the data

(m_{0},\omega,G,\vartheta_{B},{\bf b}_{\tau},b_{\nu})
defined in
R\times\Omega
(entire solution) satisfying

\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\ \mbox{for all}\ \tau\in R.

(4.8)

As already pointed out, the proof of Theorem
4.1
is based on ideas similar to the proof of sequential stability
of weak solutions on a
compact
time interval
(-N,N)
. Here the necessary energy bounds are provided by
Theorem
3.1
; whence the only missing piece of information is the strong convergence of the densities
at the “initial” time
t=-N
. Indeed compactness
of the sequence
(\varrho_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in the existence proof proposed by Lions
[
28
]
or
[
13
]
is conditioned by compactness of the “initial” density distributions. In the present setting, however,
the “initial” state is
a priori
not given.

Fortunately, this problem can be solved by careful analysis
of propagation of the density oscillations in time elaborated in
[
16
]
and further developed in

[
12
]
in the context of inhomogeneous velocity boundary conditions. Adaptation to the present setting
is straightforward as soon as we observe compactness of the Lorentz force in the momentum equation, specifically

{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}_{n}\times{\bf B}_{n}\to{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}\times{\bf B}\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{\frac{3}{2}}((-N,N)\times\Omega;R^{3}).

However, given the available energy bounds, this can be shown by the standard Aubin–Lions argument. The remaining
compactness properties necessary to establish the convergence in (
4.6
) are well understood
and elaborated in detail in
[
9
]
.

5
Concluding discussion, applications

Suppose that the gravitational potential
G
as well as the boundary data
\vartheta_{B}
,
{\bf b}_{\tau}
,
b_{\nu}
are independent of the time. Under the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
, the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\Big\{(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})\ \Big|

\displaystyle(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})-\mbox{solution of the compressible (MHD) system for}\ t\in(-\infty,\infty)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})\ \,{\rm d}{x}<\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\Big\}

is non–empty, time–shift invariant, meaning

(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})\in\mathcal{A}\ \Rightarrow\ (\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(\cdot+T)\in\mathcal{A},\ T\in R,

and compact with respect to the topologies specified in (
4.6
).

5.1
Convergence to equilibria

A particular situation occurs when
\mathcal{A}
is a singleton containing the unique stationary solution
of the problem. As a direct consequence of Theorem
4.1
we obtain unconditional convergence to equilibrium
for
any
weak solution of the compressible MHD system.

As an example, consider the situation:

•

\vartheta_{B}>0
is a positive constant on
\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}\neq\emptyset
;

•

{\bf b}_{\tau}={\bf B}_{B}\times{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}},\ b_{\nu}={\bf B}_{B}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}},

where

{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}_{B}={\rm div}_{x}{\bf B}_{B}=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega.

Accordingly, the ballistic energy inequality (
3.16
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+\varrho e(\varrho,\vartheta)+\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}|^{2}-\vartheta_{B}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)-\varrho M\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\vartheta_{B}}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}+\zeta(\vartheta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}|^{2}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq 0.

(5.1)

Integrating (
5.1
) in time we deduce that any solution belonging to
\mathcal{A}
necessarily satisfies

{\bf u}\equiv 0,\ \vartheta\equiv\vartheta_{B},\ \partial_{t}\varrho=0,\ {\rm div}_{x}{\bf B}={\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}=0.

Moreover, it turns out that
{\bf B}
is uniquely determined by the boundary conditions and the hypothesis

\int_{\Omega}{\bf B}\cdot{\bf h}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=0\ \mbox{for all}\ {\bf h}\in\mathcal{H}(\Omega).

Finally,
\varrho=\varrho(x)
solves the static problem

\nabla_{x}p(\varrho,\vartheta_{B})=\varrho\nabla_{x}M

that admits a unique solution determined by the mass constraint

\int_{\Omega}\varrho\ \,{\rm d}{x}=m_{0}

as long as
P^{\prime}(0)>0
in (
2.6
), see
[
17
]
.

5.2
Systems with unbounded energy

As mentioned in the introduction, the result claimed in Theorem
3.1
cannot hold if the fluid system is thermally isolated, meaning

\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}=\emptyset.

(5.2)

Indeed it follows from the entropy inequality (
2.28
) that the total entropy

\tau\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

is a non–decreasing function, which precludes the existence of a bounded absorbing set
depending solely on the data specified in (
3.1
).

What is more, we show that (
5.2
) may give rise to trajectories with unbounded energy.
Indeed suppose that there is a global–in–time solution
(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})
and a sequence of times

\tau_{n}\to\infty
such that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(\tau_{n},\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}<\infty.

(5.3)

It follows from the constitutive restrictions (
2.1
) that boundedness of total energy implies boundedness of the total entropy
\int_{\Omega}\varrho s(\varrho,\vartheta)(\tau_{n},\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}
. However, by virtue of the no–flux boundary conditions induced by (
5.2
), the total entropy
is a non–decreasing function of the time. Consequently, we may infer that

\int_{T}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}-\frac{{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta}{\vartheta}+\zeta(\vartheta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}|^{2}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t<\infty.

(5.4)

In particular, it follows from (
5.4
) that

\int_{\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}+1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left(\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}{\bf u}+\zeta(\vartheta)|{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf B}|^{2}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\to 0\ \mbox{as}\ n\to\infty.

(5.5)

Consequently, introducing the time shifts

{\bf u}_{n}={\bf u}(\cdot+\tau_{n}),\ {\bf B}_{n}={\bf B}(\cdot+\tau_{n}),

we deduce

{\bf u}_{n}\to 0\ \mbox{in}\ L^{2}(0,1;W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3}),\ {\bf B}_{n}\to\widehat{{\bf B}}\ \mbox{in}\ L^{2}(0,1;L^{2}(\Omega;R^{3}))\ \mbox{and weakly in}\ L^{2}(0,1;W^{1,2}(\Omega;R^{3})).

As the limits satisfy the induction equation (
1.3
), we conclude

\widehat{{\bf B}}=\widehat{{\bf B}}(x),\ {\rm div}_{x}\widehat{{\bf B}}=0,\ {\bf curl}_{x}\widehat{{\bf B}}=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\ \widehat{{\bf B}}\times{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}}={\bf b}_{\tau},\ \widehat{{\bf B}}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf B}_{N}}=b_{\nu}.

(5.6)

Thus the boundary data
b_{\nu}
,
{\bf b}_{\tau}
must be stationary in the sense of Definition
1.1
.
Otherwise, the conclusion contradicts (
5.3
).

Using the necessary condition for stationarity (
1.23
) we obtain the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

[Systems with unbounded energy]

Suppose

\Gamma^{\bf u}_{D}\neq\emptyset,\ \Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}=\emptyset,

and
b_{\nu}
,
{\bf b}_{\tau}
independent of
t
,

\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}\neq\emptyset,\ \mbox{where}\ {\bf b}_{\tau}\cdot{\bf n}=0,\ {\rm div}_{\tau}{\bf b}_{\tau}\not\equiv 0.

Then

\int_{\Omega}E(\varrho,\vartheta,{\bf u},{\bf B})(\tau,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\to\infty\ \mbox{as}\ \tau\to\infty

for any global–in–time weak solution to the compressible MHD system.

5.3
General boundary fluxes

The results can be extended to general boundary fluxes. Prominent examples are
the Navier–slip boundary condition for the velocity,

{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf u}_{N}}=0,\ \left[\mathbb{S}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}{\bf u})\cdot{\bf n}+d{\bf u}\right]\times{\bf n}|_{\Gamma^{\bf u}_{N}}=0,\ d>0,

(5.7)

see e.g. Bulíček, Málek, and Rajagopal
[
5
]
,
and the radiative heat flux condition

{\bf q}(\vartheta,\nabla_{x}\vartheta)\cdot{\bf n}-d|\vartheta-\vartheta_{0}|^{k}(\vartheta-\vartheta_{0})|_{\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{N}}=0

(5.8)

used in some models in astrophysics, cf. Weiss and Proctor
[
36
]
.

If
d>0
, then the conditions
\Gamma^{\bf u}_{D}\neq\emptyset
,
\Gamma^{\vartheta}_{D}\neq\emptyset
can be dropped in
Theorem
3.1
. Hypothesis
\Gamma^{\bf B}_{D}\subset\Gamma^{\bf u}_{D}
, however, is still necessary.
We leave the details to the interested reader.

5.4
“Do nothing” boundary conditions

Another interesting type of boundary conditions arises under a simple but physically grounded assumption
that the Maxwell equations, here represented by the induction equation (
1.3
), are satisfied both in

\Omega
and in the adjacent component
\Omega_{\rm ext}

with possibly different (discontinuous) transport coefficients. The boundary conditions on
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
separating
\Omega
and
\Omega_{\rm ext}
are then nothing other than the Rankine–Hugeniot compatibility conditions to ensure the satisfaction of the induction equation in the weak sense.

More specifically, we write the Maxwell’s equations yielding (
1.3
) in a more precise way:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bf B}+{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf E}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}{\bf B}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle{\bf J}

\displaystyle=\sigma\left({\bf E}+{\bf u}\times{\bf B}\right),

\displaystyle{\bf J}

\displaystyle={\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H},\ {\bf B}=\overline{\mu}{\bf H},

(5.9)

where
{\bf E}
denotes the electric field,
{\bf J}
the electric current density,
{\bf H}
the magnetic field intensity,
\sigma
is the electric conductivity and
\overline{\mu}
the magnetic permitivity of the material.
Both
\sigma
and
\overline{\mu}
may experience jumps over
\Gamma
.
Accordingly, the Lorentz force in the momentum equation reads

{\bf J}\times{\bf B}={\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}\times{\bf B}.

The associated
Rankine-Hugeniot conditions on
\Gamma
are:

\displaystyle{\bf B}\cdot{\bf n}

\displaystyle={\bf B}_{\rm ext}\cdot{\bf n},

\displaystyle{\bf H}\times{\bf n}

\displaystyle={\bf H}_{\rm ext}\times{\bf n},

\displaystyle{\bf E}\times{\bf n}

\displaystyle={\bf E}_{\rm ext}\times{\bf n}.

(5.10)

If the adjacent component
\Omega_{\rm ext}
happens to be a vacuum, we have
\sigma_{\rm ext}=0
, and
(
5.4
), (
5.4
) give rise to a non–local condition for
{\bf B}
on
\Gamma
given by a Neumann–Dirichlet
type map, see Ladyzhenskaya and Solonnikov
[
27
]
, Sakhaev and Solonnikov
[
30
]
, Solonnikov
[
33
]
.

At the level of the energy estimates yielding the existence of a bounded absorbing set, the above situation
does not create any additional difficulties. Indeed, in view of (
5.4
), the function

\overline{\mu}({\bf B}-{\bf B}_{B})={\bf H}-{\bf H}_{B},\ \overline{\mu}=\overline{\mu}_{\rm int}\mathds{1}_{\Omega}+\overline{\mu}_{\rm ext}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\rm ext}}

is still a legitimate test function for the induction equation (
1.3
) yielding

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega\cup\Omega_{\rm ext}}\overline{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{2}|{\bf B}|^{2}-{\bf B}_{B}\cdot{\bf B}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\cup\Omega_{\rm ext}}({\bf u}\times{\bf B})\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}({\bf H}-{\bf H}_{B})\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega\cup\Omega_{\rm ext}}\frac{1}{\sigma}{\bf curl}_{x}{\bf H}\cdot{\bf curl}_{x}({\bf H}-{\bf H}_{B})\,{\rm d}{x}.

(5.11)

If we set the velocity
{\bf u}_{\rm ext}=0
, all energy bounds derived in the proof of Theorem
3.1
remain valid; whence the existence of a bounded absorbing set follows from Theorem
3.1

The proof of asymptotic compactness claimed in Theorem
4.1
may be more delicate. Still we conjecture that the results of Bauer, Pauly, and Schomburg
[
4
]
may be used to show the asymptotic compactness
for the sequence of magnetic fields to extend Theorem
4.1
to the case of “do nothing” boundary conditions.

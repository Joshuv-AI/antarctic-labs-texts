Filtering the linearization of the category of surjections
Geoffrey Powell

Abstract
A filtration of the morphisms of the 𝕜 \mathbbm{k} -linearization 𝕜 ​ 𝐅𝐒 \mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS} of the category 𝐅𝐒 \mathbf{FS} of finite sets and surjections is constructed using a natural 𝕜 ​ 𝐅𝐈 op \mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}} -module structure induced by restriction, where 𝐅𝐈 \mathbf{FI} is the category of finite sets and injections. In particular, this yields the ‘primitive’ subcategory 𝕜 ​ 𝐅𝐒 0 ⊂ 𝕜 ​ 𝐅𝐒 {\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}\subset\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS} that is of independent interest; for example, the category of 𝕜 ​ 𝐅𝐒 0 {\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}} -modules is closely related to the category of 𝕜 ​ 𝐅𝐀 \mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA} -modules, where 𝐅𝐀 \mathbf{FA} is the category of finite sets and all maps. Working over a field of characteristic zero, the subquotients of this filtration are identified as bimodules over 𝕜 ​ 𝐅𝐁 \mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB} , where 𝐅𝐁 \mathbf{FB} is the category of finite sets and bijections, also exhibiting and exploiting additional structure. In particular, this describes the underlying 𝕜 ​ 𝐅𝐁 \mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB} -bimodule of 𝕜 ​ 𝐅𝐒 0 {\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}} .

Introduction

The main player of this paper is
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}
, the
\mathbbm{k}
-linearization of the category
\mathbf{FS}
of finite sets and surjections, where
\mathbbm{k}
is a field of characteristic zero. Representations of this category (known as
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}
-modules here) and of its opposite are of significant interest. For instance,
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules play an important role in the application of Sam and Snowden’s Gröbner-theoretic methods
[
10
]
; Tosteson
[
12
]
has provided additional interesting information on
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules.

In addition, Pirashvili
[
3
]
proved that the category of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}
-modules is Morita equivalent to that of
\mathbbm{k}\Gamma
-modules, where
\Gamma
is the category of finite pointed sets and pointed maps (this does not require working over a field of characteristic zero). The category of
\mathbbm{k}\Gamma
-modules occurs in numerous situations; for instance, Pirashvili exhibited
\mathbbm{k}\Gamma
-modules as the natural coefficients for his higher Hochschild homology when working with pointed spaces. For
unpointed
spaces,
\mathbbm{k}\Gamma
should be replaced by
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
, where
\mathbf{FA}
is the category of finite sets and all maps. The interplay between these structures was investigated in the joint work with Vespa
[
7
]
, motivated by the joint work
[
8
]
on higher Hochschild homology evaluated on wedges of circles. The latter was inspired by the work of Turchin and Willwacher
[
13
]
.

The purpose of the current paper is to construct a (highly non-trivial) filtration of the morphism spaces of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}
and to investigate its properties.
This is based upon the embedding
\mathbf{FS}\subset\mathbf{FA}
and the following observation: for a finite set
X
, there is a natural
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module structure on
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)
such that the projection
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(-,X)\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)
that sends non-surjective maps to zero is a morphism of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules.

It follows that
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)
has a
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module structure given by restriction along
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}\hookrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}^{\mathrm{op}}
, where
\mathbf{FI}\subset\mathbf{FA}
is the category of finite sets and injections. General properties of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules then yield the exhaustive filtration:

\displaystyle 0=\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{-1}(-,X)\subseteq{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}(-,X)\subseteq\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{1}(-,X)\subseteq\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{2}(-,X)\subseteq\ldots\subseteq\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)

(1)

(see Corollary
3.17
).

As an example, for a finite set
U
,
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}(U,X)
is the subspace of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(U,X)
given by the intersection of the kernels of the
\mathbbm{k}
-linear maps

\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(U,X)\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(V,X)

given by the
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module structure of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)
, for non-bijective injections
V\hookrightarrow U
. Whilst elementary to define, determining
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}(U,X)
as a
\mathbbm{k}
-vector space or, better still, a
\mathbbm{k}(\mathrm{Aut}(U)^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathrm{Aut}(X))
-module (where
\mathrm{Aut}(-)
denotes the automorphism group), is not easy.

There is more structure, which shows the significance of
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}
:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Corollary
3.24
.) The following properties hold:

1.

{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}
forms a wide
\mathbbm{k}
-linear subcategory of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}
;

2.

\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}
has the structure of a
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module and (
1
) is a filtration by
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules.

Part of the interest of the
\mathbbm{k}
-linear category
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}
is that the category of
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}
-modules is closely related to that of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
-modules. The study of this relationship was initiated in
[
7
]
and developed in
[
5
]
: a precise relationship with the category of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
-modules is given by the Morita equivalence result
[
5
, Theorem 7.10]
, which is expressed using a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear category that is closely related to
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}
.

Remark 1.2
.

A conceptual explanation for the relationship between
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}
and
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
is provided in
[
6
, Section 19]
using relative nonhomogeneous Koszul duality à la Positselski
[
4
]
. This is based on the fact that every map between finite sets can be written as the composite of a surjection followed by an injection; this induces an identification of the
\mathbbm{k}
-linear category
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
as
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}\otimes_{\mathbf{FB}}\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}
, equipped with composition defined using a distributive law. Relative non-homogeneous Koszul duality then yields a DG
\mathbbm{k}
-linear category which has underlying complex that identifies with the Koszul complex that is used in
[
7
]
. (This approach is not exploited explicitly here.)

To study the filtration (
1
), we consider the filtration subquotients
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{\ell/(\ell-1)}
, for each
\ell\in{\rm Nature}
(for
\ell=0
, this subquotient is simply
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}
). By Theorem
1.1
, these subquotients are
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules and the aim is to understand this structure.
The first step is to give a precise relationship between the higher filtration quotients and
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}
.

In Theorem
1.3
below,
\mathsf{sgn}_{a}
denotes the sign representation of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{a}
and
S_{(\ell,1^{a})}
the simple representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{\ell+a}
indexed by the partition
(\ell,1^{a})
(considered as a
\mathbbm{k}\mathfrak{S}_{\ell+a}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module). Then
\bigoplus_{a\in{\rm Nature}}\mathsf{sgn}_{a}\boxtimes S_{(\ell,1^{a})}
is considered as a
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module. Moreover,
\odot_{\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}}
denotes the Day convolution product for
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules (see Definition
3.1
) and
\mathsf{triv}_{\ell}
is the trivial
\mathbbm{k}\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{op}}
-representation, considered as a
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module.

We also use that
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}
is equipped with an augmentation
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}
that sends non-bijections to zero. Tensoring
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}
, the restriction of the above augmentation to
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}
, with
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
yields the
\mathbbm{k}
-linear functor
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
4.5
)
.

For
\ell>0
, there is a short exact sequence of
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules

0\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{\ell/(\ell-1)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\theta^{\ell}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}\odot_{\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathsf{triv}_{\ell}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{a\in{\rm Nature}}\mathsf{sgn}_{a}\boxtimes S_{(\ell,1^{a})}\rightarrow 0,

where the cokernel is a
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module via restriction along the homomorphism
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
.

The way in which the cokernel of
\theta^{\ell}
appears is of interest; to explain this, we use the skeleton of the category
\mathbf{FA}
(and hence its wide subcategories) provided by the sets
\mathbf{n}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, for
n\in{\rm Nature}
. Taking
b=\ell+a
and evaluating
\theta^{\ell}
on
(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a})
, the map identifies as a morphism of
\mathbbm{k}(\mathfrak{S}_{a}\times\mathfrak{S}_{b}^{\mathrm{op}})
-modules:

\Theta_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{b}):\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a})\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}^{\mathbf{FI}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})}.

The cokernel of this map identifies as
\mathsf{sgn}_{a}\boxtimes S_{(\ell,1^{a})}
, by Theorem
2.11
and Lemma
2.9
.
Then, by exploiting the
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}
-module structure, it is shown that, as
a
varies, this accounts for all of the cokernel of
\theta^{\ell}
.

Theorem
1.3
gives an explicit relationship between
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}
and
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}\odot_{\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathsf{triv}
, where
\mathsf{triv}
is the constant
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module
\mathbbm{k}
, which encodes all of the
\mathsf{triv}_{\ell}
. Working up to isomorphism in the category of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules,
-\odot_{\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathsf{triv}
is invertible, which leads to:

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
5.7
)
.

In the Grothendieck group of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules, there is an equality

\displaystyle[{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}]+\sum_{b>a\geq 0}(-1)^{b-a}[\mathsf{sgn}_{a}\boxtimes\mathsf{sgn}_{b}]=[\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}]\odot_{\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}}\Big(\sum_{t\in{\rm Nature}}(-1)^{t}[\mathsf{sgn}_{t}]\Big).

This result is equivalent to
[

which was established using different methods (see Remark

As already indicated, the work of
[

]
was motivated in part by the study of the relationship between
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}
-modules and the category of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
-modules. The subtlety arises from the exceptional behaviour of the simple

. (This classification is outlined very briefly in Section

Remark 1.5
.

Throughout this paper,
\mathbbm{k}
is taken to be a field of characteristic zero. Given that many of the results of
[
7
]
hold over an arbitrary commutative ring, it is natural to seek to generalize the current results. Indeed, the construction of the filtration (

However, the proof of Theorem

depends upon
\mathbbm{k}

-modules over a field of characteristic zero. It would be of considerable interest to have a characteristic-free version of Theorem

.

2
Background

This section presents some background and introduces the main characters, namely some familiar wide subcategories of the category of finite sets. The main objective is to state and sketch the proof of Theorem
2.11
(which is proved in detail in
[
5
, Section 6]
); this is the initial input for the proofs of the main theoretical results of the paper in Section
4
.

Throughout
\mathbbm{k}
is a field of characteristic zero. Tensor products of
\mathbbm{k}
-vector spaces are denoted simply by
\otimes
.

2.1
Basics

The symmetric group on
b
letters is denoted by
\mathfrak{S}_{b}
. The hypothesis upon
\mathbbm{k}
allows us to invoke the (semi-simple) representation theory of the symmetric groups in characteristic zero. (Basics of the representation theory of the symmetric groups in characteristic zero are covered, for example, in
[
2
, Chapter 4]
.)
The simple representations of
\mathfrak{S}_{b}
are indexed by the partitions
\lambda\vdash b
, with the corresponding simple module denoted by
S_{\lambda}
. We use the convention that the partition
(b)
indexes the trivial representation,
\mathsf{triv}_{b}=S_{(b)}
.

For a category
\mathcal{C}
, the morphism sets are denoted either
\hom_{\mathcal{C}}(-,-)
or
\mathcal{C}(-,-)
.
The
\mathbbm{k}
-linearization of
\mathcal{C}
is denoted by
\mathbbm{k}\mathcal{C}
; this has the same objects as
\mathcal{C}
and morphisms given by
\hom_{\mathbbm{k}\mathcal{C}}(-,-):=\mathbbm{k}\hom_{\mathcal{C}}(-,-)
, also written
\mathbbm{k}\mathcal{C}(-,-)
. For
f
a morphism of
\mathcal{C}
, the corresponding morphism of
\mathbbm{k}\mathcal{C}
is denoted
[f]
; such elements provide
\mathbbm{k}
-bases for the morphism spaces. Moreover, this defines a faithful functor
\mathcal{C}\hookrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathcal{C}
that is the identity on objects.

If
\mathcal{C}
is essentially small, one can consider the category of functors from
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathtt{Mod}_{\mathbbm{k}}
, the category of
\mathbbm{k}
-vector spaces;
this is sometimes denoted
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C})
. This is equivalent to the category of
\mathbbm{k}
-linear functors from
\mathbbm{k}\mathcal{C}
to
\mathbbm{k}
-vector spaces, which will be referred to here as the category of
\mathbbm{k}\mathcal{C}
-modules (rather than
\mathcal{C}
-modules, as is common in the literature, since we also consider
\mathbbm{k}
-linear categories that are not of the form
\mathbbm{k}\mathcal{C}
).

The category
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C})
is abelian and comes equipped with the tensor product induced by that on
\mathbbm{k}
-vector spaces; namely, for functors
F,G
, their tensor product is defined by
(F\otimes G)(X):=F(X)\otimes G(X)
. Vector space duality induces
D:\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}})
given by
DF(X):=\hom_{\mathbbm{k}}(F(X),\mathbbm{k})
. This restricts to an equivalence between the respective full subcategories of functors taking finite-dimensional values.

The tensor product
\otimes_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}})\times\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathtt{Mod}_{\mathbbm{k}}
is defined as the composite of the external tensor product
\boxtimes:\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}})\times\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C})
(which is simply given by
(F\boxtimes G)(X\times Y)=F(X)\otimes G(Y))
and the coend
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathtt{Mod}_{\mathbbm{k}}
.

A subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{C}
is
wide
if it contains all the objects of
\mathcal{C}
; a wide subcategory is determined by specifying the morphisms that it contains.

2.2
The cast

The category
\mathbf{FA}
of finite sets and
all
maps has skeleton
\{\mathbf{n}\ |\ n\in{\rm Nature}\}
, where
\mathbf{n}
is the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
(understood to be
\emptyset
for
n=0
). An obvious but useful fact is that, for finite sets
X
and
Y
,
\mathbf{FA}(X,Y)
is a finite set.

The wide subcategories
\mathbf{FS}
,
\mathbf{FI}
,
\mathbf{FB}
of
\mathbf{FA}
are our main players, specified by their classes of morphisms:
surjections, injections, bijections respectively. These fit into the commutative diagram of inclusions of wide subcategories:

\textstyle{\mathbf{FB}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbf{FI}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbf{FS}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbf{FA}.}

Any map of
\mathbf{FA}
factors (uniquely up to a morphism of
\mathbf{FB}
) as the composite of a map of
\mathbf{FS}
followed by one of
\mathbf{FI}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Since
\mathbf{FB}
is a groupoid, it is isomorphic to its opposite,
\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
. Thus the category of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}
-modules is isomorphic to the category of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules. This is a global form of the fact that, for
b\in{\rm Nature}
, the category of
\mathbbm{k}\mathfrak{S}_{b}
-modules is isomorphic to the category of
\mathbbm{k}\mathfrak{S}_{b}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules. This will be used to adjust variance, when necessary, without further comment.

If
f:X\rightarrow Y
is a map between finite sets that is not surjective, for any map
g:U\rightarrow X
, precomposing with
g
gives
f\circ g
that is not surjective. Likewise, if
f
is not injective, then postcomposing with any map
h:Y\rightarrow Z
gives
h\circ f
that is not injective. This implies the following:

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a finite set.

1.

There is a unique
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
-module structure on
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(X,-)
extending the canonical
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}
-module structure such that the projection

\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(X,-)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(X,-)

sending non-injective maps to zero is a morphism of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
-modules.

2.

There is a unique
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module structure on
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)
extending the canonical
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module structure such that the projection

\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(-,X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)

sending non-surjective maps to zero is a morphism of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules.

3.

Evaluating on
X
, these both yield the surjective morphism of
\mathbbm{k}
-algebras

\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(X,X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(X)

induced by sending non-bijective maps to zero. This is split by the canonical inclusion of
\mathbbm{k}
-algebras
\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(X,X)
.

For finite sets
X,U,Y
, the surjection
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(-,X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)
yields the following commutative diagram corresponding to the
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module structures:

\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(U,X)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(Y,U)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(Y,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(U,X)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(Y,U)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(Y,X).}

Taking
X=Y
, this refines to

\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(U,X)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(X,U)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(X,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(U,X)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(X,U)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(X),}

using the projection
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(X,U)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(X,U)
to yield the left hand vertical arrow.

The lower horizontal map is compatible with the
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
-actions on
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)
and
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(X,-)
respectively, yielding the following:

Proposition 2.3
.

For a finite set
X
, composition in
\mathbf{FA}
induces the morphism of
\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(X)
-bimodules:

\displaystyle\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)\otimes_{\mathbf{FA}}\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(X,-)\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(X).

(2)

Proof 2.4
.

Associativity of composition implies that, for a finite set
U
, the morphism

\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(U,X)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(X,U)\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(X)

induced by composition and projection to
\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is a morphism of
\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(X)
-bimodules for the obvious bimodule structures.

It remains to check that these induce a morphism
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)\otimes_{\mathbf{FA}}\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(X,-)\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(X)
as claimed. This follows from the associativity of the composition in
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
together with the fact that the factorization observed above across the surjection of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
-modules
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(X,-)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(X,-)
is canonical.

It is worth spelling this out. For composable morphisms
f\in\mathbf{FS}(U,X)
,
g\in\mathbf{FA}(V,U)
and
h\in\mathbf{FI}(X,V)
, we require to prove that the images of
g^{*}[f]\otimes[h]
and
[f]\otimes g_{*}[h]
in
\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(X)
are the same, writing
g^{*}
and
g_{*}
to indicate the
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}^{\mathrm{op}}
and
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
-actions respectively. Now,
g^{*}[f]=[f\circ g]
if
f\circ g\in\mathbf{FA}(V,X)
is surjective and zero otherwise; likewise
g_{*}[h]=[g\circ h]
if
g\circ h\in\mathbf{FA}(X,U)

The standard projective

-module

to the constant functor with value

For each

-module.

The inclusion

induces

which (for

Lemma 2.9
.

For

-module of

Proof 2.10
.

The case

is immediate, using the convention

. Hence, suppose that

First consider

The result then follows from an obvious induction on

The purpose of this section is to introduce the natural filtration of the morphisms of

3.1
Defining the filtration

In this section, we show that a
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module has a natural filtration.

We start by recalling the Day convolution product on
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\odot_{\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}}
be the Day convolution product on
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules that is given by

M\odot_{\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}}N:S\mapsto\bigoplus_{S_{1}\amalg S_{2}=S}M(S_{1})\otimes N(S_{2}),

where the sum is indexed by ordered decompositions of the finite set
S
into two subsets
(S_{1},S_{2})
.

This defines a symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules, with unit
\mathbbm{k}_{\mathbf{0}}
, the module supported on
\mathbf{0}
with value
\mathbbm{k}
.

Remark 3.2
.

It is well known that the category of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}
-modules can be described as a category of modules in the category of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}
-modules equipped with the corresponding Day convolution product. For example, via the Schur correspondence (as explained in
[
9
]
), this description of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}
-modules is given by
[
9
, Proposition 1.3.5]
.

There is an analogous description of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules as a category of comodules, working in the symmetric monoidal category
(\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}),\odot_{\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}},\mathbbm{k}_{\mathbf{0}})
. Write
\mathsf{triv}
for the constant
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module with value
\mathbbm{k}
. This has the structure of a bicommutative Hopf algebra in
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules; in particular,
\mathsf{triv}
has an underlying coaugmented coalgebra structure. One can thus consider the category of
\mathsf{triv}
-comodules. Objects are
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules
M
equipped with a structure morphism
M\rightarrow M\odot_{\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathsf{triv}
that satisfies the appropriate axioms.

The filtration of a
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module that we construct below can be identified with the primitive filtration associated to this comodule structure.

For a
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module
M
and a pair of finite sets
(X,Y)
, one has the structure map
M(Y)\otimes_{\mathrm{Aut}(Y)}\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(X,Y)\rightarrow M(X)
and this is
\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}
-equivariant. By adjunction, this gives the
\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(Y)^{\mathrm{op}}
-equivariant morphism

\displaystyle M(Y)\rightarrow\hom_{\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(X,Y),M(X)).

(6)

We first define the filtration at the level of sections:

Definition 3.3
.

For a
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module
M
, an integer
t\in{\rm Nature}\cup\{-1\}
, and a finite set
Y
, let
M^{t}(Y)\subseteq M(Y)
be the largest sub
\mathbbm{k}
-vector space such that the composite

M^{t}(Y)\subset M(Y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M(i)}}{{\rightarrow}}M(X)

is zero for each
(X,i)
, where
X
is a finite set with
|Y|-|X|=t+1
and
i\in\mathbf{FI}(X,Y)
.

Since
\mathbf{FA}
has a skeleton with objects indexed by their cardinality, one has the following identification:

Lemma 3.4
.

For a
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module
M
,
t\in{\rm Nature}\cup\{-1\}
, and a finite set
Y
:

1.

if
|Y|\leq t
, then
M^{t}(Y)=M(Y)
;

2.

if
|Y|>t
, then

M^{t}(Y)=\ker\Big(M(Y)\rightarrow\hom_{\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(X,Y),M(X))\Big),

where
X
is some object with
|Y|-|X|=t+1
.

In particular,
M^{t}(Y)
is a sub
\mathbbm{k}\mathrm{Aut}(Y)^{\mathrm{op}}
-module of
M(Y)
.

This provides the desired filtration, by the following:

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
M
be a
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module.

1.

For
t\in{\rm Nature}\cup\{-1\}
,
Y\mapsto M^{t}(Y)
yields a sub
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module of
M
.

2.

The subobjects
M^{t}
form a natural, increasing filtration of
M
by sub
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules:

0=M^{-1}\subseteq M^{0}\subseteq M^{1}\subseteq M^{2}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq M

and this filtration is exhaustive.

Proof 3.6
.

It is clear from the definition that the construction of
M^{t}(Y)\subseteq M(Y)
is natural with respect to
M
.

We next establish that, if
t\leq\ell
, then
M^{t}(Y)\subseteq M^{\ell}(Y)
. Let
i:X\hookrightarrow Y
be any injection with
|Y|-|X|=\ell+1
. Since

by hypothesis, there exists a factorization of
i
of the form
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i^{\prime}}}{{\hookrightarrow}}X^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i^{\prime\prime}}}{{\hookrightarrow}}Y
with
|Y|-|X^{\prime}|=t+1
. Thus
M(i)=M(i^{\prime\prime})\circ M(i^{\prime})
, from which one deduces that
M^{t}(Y)
lies in
M^{\ell}(Y)
.

To prove that the construction
M^{t}
yields a submodule of
M
, we require to show that, for any
j:U\hookrightarrow Y
the image of
M^{t}(Y)\subseteq M(Y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\rightarrow}}M(U)
lies in
M^{t}(U)
. If
|U|\leq t
, by Lemma
3.4
,
M^{t}(U)=M(U)
and there is nothing to show. In the case
|U|>t
, consider any
i:V\hookrightarrow U
with
|U|-[V|=t+1
; the composite
V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\hookrightarrow}}U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\hookrightarrow}}Y
has
|Y|-|V|=\ell+1
for some
\ell\geq t
. It follows that the composite

M^{t}(Y)\subseteq M^{\ell}(Y)\subseteq M(Y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M(j)}}{{\rightarrow}}M(U)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M(i)}}{{\rightarrow}}M(V)

is zero, by the definition of
M^{\ell}(Y)
, where the first inclusion is given by the previous argument.
This holds for any such
i
; thus, by the definition of
M^{t}(U)
, the image of
M^{t}(Y)
in
M(U)
lies in
M^{t}(U)
.

The identification of
M^{-1}
is immediate, since any

with
|Y|=|X|
is necessarily a bijection. The fact that the filtration is exhaustive follows from the identification
M^{t}(Y)=M(Y)
if

given in Lemma
3.4
.

There is an augmentation
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}
that sends all non-bijective maps to zero. Restriction along the (opposite of the) augmentation gives an exact functor from
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules to
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules with essential image the full subcategory of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules on which all non-bijective injections act by zero.

We have the following interpretation of

on
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules.

Proposition 3.7
.

The association

defines a functor from
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules to
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules. It is a right adjoint to the above restriction functor.

The adjunction counit identifies as the natural inclusion

of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules.

Proof 3.8
.

By definition, for a
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module

,

is the largest submodule of

with the property that

is zero for every non-bijective injection

induces a functor from
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules to
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}

Remark 3.9

The submodule

corresponds to the primitives of

Lemma 3.11

Evaluated on
Y

We identify

Lemma 3.12

For
M
a
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module, there is a natural isomorphism of

-modules

\hom_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{[n]},M)\cong M\odot_{\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathsf{triv}_{n}.

Proof 3.13
.

If
b<n
, then
\hom_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{[n]}(-,\mathbf{b}),M)
and
(M\odot_{\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathsf{triv}_{n})(\mathbf{b})

By Lemma

, there is an isomorphism of

-modules:

\hom_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{[n]}(-,\mathbf{b}),M)\cong\hom_{\mathbbm{k}\mathfrak{S}_{a}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}),M(\mathbf{a})).

Consider the Young subgroup
\mathfrak{S}_{a}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{b}
corresponding to the canonical inclusion
\mathbf{a}\subset\mathbf{b}
, using that
\mathbf{b}\backslash\mathbf{a}\cong\mathbf{n}
. The
\mathbbm{k}\mathfrak{S}_{a}^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathfrak{S}_{b}

\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
identifies as

\mathbbm{k}(\mathfrak{S}_{b}/\mathfrak{S}_{n})

\mathfrak{S}_{a}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{b}

Since, for finite groups, induction is isomorphic to coinduction, this provides the natural isomorphism:

\hom_{\mathbbm{k}\mathfrak{S}_{a}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}),M(\mathbf{a}))\cong\big(M(\mathbf{a})\boxtimes\mathsf{triv}_{n}\big)\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}(\mathfrak{S}_{a}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n})}\mathbbm{k}\mathfrak{S}_{b}.

The right hand side is naturally isomorphic to
(M\odot_{\mathbf{FB}^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathsf{triv}_{n})(\mathbf{b})

Suppose that

If

is a

Then Proposition

refines to:

\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}

(8)

Proof 3.16
.

The first statement is proved by refining the proof of Proposition

Write

Remark 3.18

Example 3.21
.

The following results are contained in
[
7
, Section 6]
.

1.

For
a=1

is isomorphic to
\mathbbm{k}

2.

For

,

-module) to

(with the regular bimodule structure) for

and, for

if

This can be seen as follows. If

for the corresponding surjection. Then, for

The following result is the key input to Corollary
3.24
:

Proposition 3.22
.

For finite sets
X
,
Y
, composition in
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}
induces a morphism of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules:

{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}(X,Y)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,Y).

For
\ell\in{\rm Nature}
, this restricts to a morphism of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules:

{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}(X,Y)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)^{\ell}\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,Y)^{\ell}.

Proof 3.23
.

Recall that the
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module structure on
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)
(respectively with
Y
in place for
X
) is the restriction of the
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module structure provided by Proposition
2.2
, so that the quotient map
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(-,X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)
is a morphism of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules and hence, by restriction, of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules (resp. for
Y
).

Now, using the inclusions
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}\hookrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
and
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}\hookrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
of wide
\mathbbm{k}
-linear subcategories, for any finite sets
A
,
B
, associativity of composition in
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
yields the following commutative diagram:

\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(X,Y)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(B,X)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(A,B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(B,Y)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(A,B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(X,Y)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(A,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(A,Y),}

In which the maps are given by the appropriate compositions, passing to the larger category
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
where required.

The subtlety comes from the fact that the ‘action’ of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(X,Y)
is not in general compatible with the projections
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(-,X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,X)
and
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(-,Y)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(-,Y)
. Namely, in general, the following diagram does not commute:

\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(X,Y)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(A,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\not\circlearrowleft}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(A,Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(X,Y)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(A,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(A,Y),}

since a non-surjective map
A\rightarrow X
may become surjective after composing with a surjection
X\twoheadrightarrow Y
.

Restricting to
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}(X,Y)\subset\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(X,Y)
resolves this issue. Namely, we claim that the restricted diagram

\textstyle{{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}(X,Y)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(A,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(A,Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}(X,Y)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(A,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(A,Y)}

always commutes.

This is seen as follows. Write
\overline{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}}
for the kernel of the augmentation
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FB}
that is induced by sending the non-bijective maps to zero. One checks that
the map induced by composition in
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}

\overline{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}}(-,X)\otimes_{\mathbf{FB}}\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(A,-)\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(A,X)

surjects to the
\mathbbm{k}
-linear span of the non-surjective maps from
A
to
X
.

Now, for any finite set
U
, the map induced by composition in
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}

{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}(X,Y)\otimes\overline{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}}(U,X)\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(U,Y)

maps to the
\mathbbm{k}
-linear span of the non-surjective maps from

to

, since the composite with
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}(U,Y)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(U,Y)
is zero, by definition of
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}
. The claimed commutativity follows by putting these ingredients together, using associativity of composition in
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FA}
.

Thus, after restricting to
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}
, one can paste the two commutative diagrams together to obtain the commutative diagram

\textstyle{{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}(X,Y)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(B,X)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(A,B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(B,Y)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}(A,B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}(X,Y)\otimes\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(A,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}(A,Y),}

in which the vertical maps are given by the
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-structure maps and the horizontal maps correspond to the action of
{\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FS}^{0}}(X,Y)
. This establishes the first statement.

The second statement then follows from the naturality of the filtration of
\mathbbm{k}\mathbf{FI}^{\mathrm{op}}
-modules, as stated in Proposition
3.5
.

This gives the following fundamental properties of the filtration of morphisms of

\mathbbm{k}

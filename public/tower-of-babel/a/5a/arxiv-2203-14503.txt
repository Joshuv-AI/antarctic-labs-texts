Strong quantum nonlocality and unextendibility without entanglement in N N -partite systems with odd N N
Yiyun He, Fei Shi, Xiande Zhang

Abstract
A set of orthogonal product states is strongly nonlocal if it is locally irreducible in every bipartition, which shows the phenomenon of strong quantum nonlocality without entanglement [ Phys. Rev. Lett. 122 , 040403 (2019) ]. Although such a phenomenon has been shown to any three-, four-, and five-partite systems, the existence of strongly nonlocal orthogonal product sets in multipartite systems remains unknown. In this paper, by using a general decomposition of the N N -dimensional hypercubes, we present strongly nonlocal orthogonal product sets in N N -partite systems for all odd N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 . Based on this decomposition, we give explicit constructions of unextendible product bases in N N -partite systems for odd N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 . Furthermore, we apply our results to quantum secret sharing, uncompletable product bases, and PPT entangled states.



Keywords
: strong quantum nonlocality, unextendible product bases, hypercubes

2
Preliminaries

In the section, we will introduce the concepts of strong quantum nonlocality and UPBs.
To simplify the notation, we do not normalize states and operators.
The positive operator-valued measure (POVM) is a set of positive semi-definite operators
\{E_{m}=M_{m}^{\dagger}M_{m}\}
acting on
{\cal H}
, a Hilbert space, with a property that
\sum_{m}{M_{m}^{\dagger}M_{m}}=\mathbb{I}_{{\cal H}}
, where
\mathbb{I}_{{\cal H}}
is an identity operator on
{\cal H}
. If the set is a POVM, we call each
E_{m}
as a POVM element. We focus on this measurement in this paper, and we regard the POVM measurement as trivial measurement if all the POVM elements
E_{m}
are proportional to the identity operator.

In particular, we consider a specific local measurement called orthogonality-preserving local measurement (OPLM). It is performed to distinguish multipartite orthogonal states, and it is defined with the post-measurement states remaining to be orthogonal.

Definition 1

[
33
]

A set of multipartite orthogonal states is
locally irreducible
if it is not possible to eliminate one or more states from the set by OPLMs. Furthermore, a set of multipartite orthogonal states is
strongly nonlocal
if it is locally irreducible for every bipartition of the subsystems.

For example, the Bell basis
\{\Psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
is locally irreducible, where

\displaystyle|\Psi_{1}\rangle=|0\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|0\rangle_{A_{2}}+|1\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|1\rangle_{A_{2}},

\displaystyle|\Psi_{2}\rangle=|0\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|0\rangle_{A_{2}}-|1\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|1\rangle_{A_{2}},

\displaystyle|\Psi_{3}\rangle=|0\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|1\rangle_{A_{2}}+|1\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|0\rangle_{A_{2}},

\displaystyle|\Psi_{4}\rangle=|0\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|1\rangle_{A_{2}}-|1\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|0\rangle_{A_{2}}.

If
A_{1}
performs an OPLM
\{E=M^{\dagger}M\}
, where
E
can be written as a
2\times 2
matrix
(a_{i,j})_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
under the basis
\{|0\rangle_{A_{1}},|1\rangle_{A_{1}}\}
, then
\{M\otimes\mathbb{I}_{A_{2}}|\Psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{4}
should be mutually orthogonal. Since
\langle\Psi_{1}|E\otimes\mathbb{I}_{A_{2}}|\Psi_{2}\rangle=0
, we obtain
a_{0,0}=a_{1,1}
. Moreover, since
\langle\Psi_{1}|E\otimes\mathbb{I}_{A_{2}}|\Psi_{3}\rangle=\langle\Psi_{1}|E\otimes\mathbb{I}_{A_{2}}|\Psi_{4}\rangle=0
, we have
a_{0,1}=a_{1,0}=0
. It means that arbitrary OPLM performed by
A_{1}
is trvial. By the symmetry of the Bell basis, arbitrary OPLM performed by
A_{2}
is also trvial.
Thus, the Bell basis
\{|\Psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{4}
is locally irreducible.

There is a simple method for showing strong quantum nonlocality.

Lemma 1

[
38
]

Let
{\cal S}:=\{|\psi_{j}\rangle\}
be a set of orthogonal states in a multipartite system
\otimes_{i=1}^{N}{\cal H}_{A_{i}}
. For each
i=1,2,\ldots,N
, define
B_{i}=\{A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{N}\}\setminus\{A_{i}\}
be the joint party of all but the
i
th party. Then the set
{\cal S}
is strongly nonlocal if the following condition holds for any
1\leq i\leq N
: if party
B_{i}
performs any OPLM, then the OPLM is trivial.

Next, we introduce the concept of UPBs.

Definition 2

[
46
]

A set of orthogonal product states
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}
is an unextendible product basis (UPB) if the orthogonal complement of
{\sf Span}\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}
has non-zero dimension and contains no product state.

For example, let

\displaystyle|\psi_{1}\rangle=|0\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|1\rangle_{A_{2}}\otimes|+\rangle_{A_{3}},

(1)

\displaystyle|\psi_{2}\rangle=|1\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|+\rangle_{A_{2}}\otimes|0\rangle_{A_{3}},

\displaystyle|\psi_{3}\rangle=|+\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|0\rangle_{A_{2}}\otimes|1\rangle_{A_{3}},

\displaystyle|\psi_{4}\rangle=|-\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|-\rangle_{A_{2}}\otimes|-\rangle_{A_{3}},

where
|\pm\rangle=|0\rangle\pm|1\rangle
. Then

\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{4}
is a UPB in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Let
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}:=\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
, and denote
\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}:=\mathbb{Z}_{d}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{d}
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{d}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots,d-1\}
and repeat
N
times.
We assume that
\{|j\rangle\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}
is the computational basis of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, then the computational basis of
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}
is

\{|j_{1}\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|j_{2}\rangle_{A_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes|j_{N}\rangle_{A_{N}}\}_{(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}}.

(2)

In the space
\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}
, when there is no ambiguity, we denote the vector
(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{N})
together with the single set
\{(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{N})\}
as

{\{j_{1}\}}_{A_{1}}\times{\{j_{2}\}}_{A_{2}}\times\cdots\times{\{j_{N}\}}_{A_{N}}.

(3)

There are
d^{N}
vectors in
\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}
, and all vectors in
\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}
form an
N
-dimensional hypercube.
Each product state
|j_{1}\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|j_{2}\rangle_{A_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes|j_{N}\rangle_{A_{N}}\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}
corresponds to a vector
{\{j_{1}\}}_{A_{1}}\times{\{j_{2}\}}_{A_{2}}\times\cdots\times{\{j_{N}\}}_{A_{N}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}
in the hypercube.

For a general product state
|\psi\rangle\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}
, there exists a unique subset
E_{A_{i}}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{d}
for
1\leq i\leq N
and nonzero
a_{j_{i}}^{(A_{i})}\in\mathbb{C}
for
j_{i}\in E_{A_{i}}
, such that

|\psi\rangle=\left(\sum_{j_{1}\in E_{A_{1}}}a^{(A_{1})}_{j_{1}}|j_{1}\rangle\right)_{A_{1}}\otimes\left(\sum_{j_{2}\in E_{A_{2}}}a^{(A_{2})}_{j_{2}}|j_{2}\rangle\right)_{A_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\left(\sum_{j_{N}\in E_{A_{N}}}a^{(A_{N})}_{j_{N}}|j_{N}\rangle\right)_{A_{N}}.

(4)

Then the
support set

E=E_{A_{1}}\times E_{A_{2}}\times\cdots\times E_{A_{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}
is a subcube of
\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}
. We denote
|E|:=\prod_{i=1}^{N}|E_{A_{i}}|
as the number of vectors contained in
E
. For instance, in the example above,
|\psi_{1}\rangle=|0\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|1\rangle_{A_{2}}\otimes(|0\rangle_{A_{3}}+|1\rangle_{A_{3}})
, and its support set is
E=\{0\}_{A_{1}}\times\{1\}_{A_{2}}\times\{0,1\}_{A_{3}}
.

We say a set of subcubes forms a
decomposition
of
\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}
, if any two subcubes are disjoint, and the union of all subcubes is
\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}
.

occupies a corner vector in the cube, which explains Lemma
3
. Roughly speaking, we can think of this abstract construction as a delicate partition, where each subcube in the partition grows from a corner, and the whole pattern is symmetric in a certain sense of rotation.

Next we can associate the partition with the tensor product space. For each subcube, we can construct an OPS in
(\mathbb{C}^{3})^{\otimes N}
. More specifically, for the subcube
{\cal P}_{K}={\cal P}_{K}^{(A_{1})}\times{\cal P}_{K}^{(A_{2})}\times\cdots\times{\cal P}_{K}^{(A_{N})}\in{\cal B}
, we can define

|{\cal P}_{K}\rangle_{A_{j}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}|0\rangle_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}_{K}^{(A_{j})}=\{0\}_{A_{j}},\\
|2\rangle_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}_{K}^{(A_{j})}=\{2\}_{A_{j}},\\
|\eta\rangle_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}_{K}^{(A_{j})}=\{\eta\}_{A_{j}},\\
|\xi\rangle_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}_{K}^{(A_{j})}=\{\xi\}_{A_{j}},\\
\end{array}\right.

(13)

where
|\eta\rangle=|0\rangle\pm|1\rangle
,
|\xi\rangle=|1\rangle\pm|2\rangle
. Then

|{\cal P}_{K}\rangle:=|{\cal P}_{K}\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|{\cal P}_{K}\rangle_{A_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes|{\cal P}_{K}\rangle_{A_{N}}

(14)

is an OPS of size
2^{|K|}
in
(\mathbb{C}^{3})^{\otimes N}
. Further, we let

|{\cal B}_{0}\rangle:=|1\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|1\rangle_{A_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes|1\rangle_{A_{N}}.

(15)

By Lemma
3
, we can construct an OPB in
(\mathbb{C}^{3})^{\otimes N}
.

Lemma 4

For odd
N
, the set

\{|{\cal B}_{0}\rangle\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\Lambda\\
{\cal P}\in\{{\cal C},{\cal D}\}\end{subarray}}\\
|{\cal P}_{K}\rangle

(16)

is an OPB in
(\mathbb{C}^{3})^{\otimes N}
.

Then we are ready to introduce the first main result in this paper:

Theorem 1

In
(\mathbb{C}^{3})^{\otimes N}
where
N
is odd, the OPS

{\cal O}:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\Lambda\\
{\cal P}\in\{{\cal C},{\cal D}\}\end{subarray}}|{\cal P}_{K}\rangle
is strongly nonlocal, where

The proof of the theorem is in Appendix
D
. It mainly use two important lemmas in Ref.
[

]
, which allow us to discuss the POVM matrix directly from the construction of OPS. Comparing Theorem

and Lemma

, we can deduce that the OPB in Lemma

would be a strongly nonlocal OPS after removing the state

.

4
UPB in
(\mathbb{C}^{3})^{\otimes N}

In this section, we will introduce another main result in this paper. We construct a UPB in
(\mathbb{C}^{3})^{\otimes N}
for odd
N
. First, we consider the simple case
N=3
. Let

|S\rangle=\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}|i\rangle\right)_{A_{1}}\otimes\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}|j\rangle\right)_{A_{2}}\otimes\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}|k\rangle\right)_{A_{3}}.

(17)

The authors of Ref.
[
51
]
showed that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{6}({\cal B}_{i}\setminus\{|\psi_{i}{(0,0)}\rangle\})\cup\{|S\rangle\}
given by Eqs. (
6
) and (

) is a UPB in
(\mathbb{C}^{3})^{\otimes 3}
. Inspired by this idea, we next show a UPB in
(\mathbb{C}^{3})^{\otimes N}
.

For any subcube
{\cal P}_{K}={\cal P}_{K}^{(A_{1})}\times{\cal P}_{K}^{(A_{2})}\times\cdots\times{\cal P}_{K}^{(A_{N})}
, we define

|{\cal P}_{K}^{+}\rangle_{A_{j}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}|0\rangle_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}^{(A_{j})}=\{0\}_{A_{j}},\\
|2\rangle_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}^{(A_{j})}=\{2\}_{A_{j}},\\

(|1\rangle+|2\rangle)_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}^{(A_{j})}=\{\xi\}_{A_{j}}\\
\end{array}\right.

(18)

and the state

|{\cal P}_{K}^{+}\rangle=|{\cal P}_{K}^{+}\rangle_{A_{1}}|{\cal P}_{K}^{+}\rangle_{A_{2}}\cdots|{\cal P}_{K}^{+}\rangle_{A_{N}}\in\mathrm{Span}\{|v\rangle\mid v\in{\cal P}_{K}\}.

(19)

Also, we define the “stopper" state

|S\rangle\!:=\!\left(\sum_{i_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}|i_{1}\rangle\!\right)_{A_{1}}\!\!\!\!\!\otimes\!\left(\sum_{i_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}|i_{2}\rangle\!\right)_{A_{2}}\!\!\!\!\!\otimes\cdots\otimes\left(\sum_{i_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}}|i_{N}\rangle\!\right)_{A_{N}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!.

(20)

Now we can give a UPB in
(\mathbb{C}^{3})^{\otimes N}
.

Theorem 2

In
(\mathbb{C}^{3})^{\otimes N}
where
N
is odd, the set of orthogonal product states

\mathcal{U}:=\left\{|S\rangle\right\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\Lambda\\
{\cal P}\in\{{\cal C},{\cal D}\}\end{subarray}}\left(|{\cal P}_{K}\rangle\setminus\{|{\cal P}_{K}^{+}\rangle\}\right)

(21)

is a UPB of size
3^{N}-2^{N}
.

The proof is given via contradiction. We would like to explain a sketch proof for the space
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{3}
, where the partition for this specific example is given in Figure
1
and Table
2
. The proof is arranged as follows:

(i)

If
|\psi\rangle
is a product state, its support set
E\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{3}
must be a subcube.

(ii)

If
|\psi\rangle
is orthogonal to
\mathcal{U}
, then
E
must be a union of some subcubes in Table
2
.

(iii)

It’s easy to check from Figure
1
that it is not possible to satisfy both (i) and (ii) unless
E=\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{3}
.

(iv)

Show that
|\psi\rangle
is proportional to
|S\rangle
, which means that all coefficients in the natural linear expansion of
|\psi\rangle
have the same value.

The detailed proof can be found in Appendix
E
. We note that the general proof for (iii) and (iv) would be delicate because of the complexity of the partition, and we can see from step (iv) that the stopper state
|S\rangle
is inevitable.

When
d
is larger than
3
, the construction needs to be generalized. A layer-by-layer generalization of this UPB can be found in Sec.

.

5
The construction for general system
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
for odd
N\geq 3

We have showed strongly nonlocal OPSs and UPBs in
{(\mathbb{C}^{3})}^{\otimes N}
for odd
N
. In this section, we will give a general result in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
. First, we need to give a decomposition of the hypercube
\mathbb{Z}_{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d_{2}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{Z}_{d_{N}}
. Then we show a strongly nonlocal OPS in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
. Finally, we construct a UPB in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
. Without loss of generality, we always assume that
3\leq d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\cdots\leq d_{N}
, and
N\geq 3
is odd. Denote
\prod_{i=1}^{N}\{d_{i}\}_{A_{i}}:=\{d_{1}\}_{A_{1}}\times\{d_{2}\}_{A_{2}}\times\cdots\times\{d_{N}\}_{A_{N}}
.

5.1
The decomposition of a general cube

We define
\delta={\lfloor\frac{d_{1}-1}{2}\rfloor}
many layers
{\cal L}_{1},{\cal L}_{2},\dots,{\cal L}_{\delta}
, where each layer is defined as the difference set

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{k}:=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left\{k-1,k,k+1,\dots,d_{i}-k-1,d_{i}-k\right\}_{A_{i}}\setminus\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left\{k,k+1,\dots,d_{i}-k-1\right\}_{A_{i}}.

(22)

We also denote the central block as
{\cal B}_{0}
, which is defined as

{\cal B}_{0}:=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\delta,\delta+1,\dots,d_{i}-\delta-1\right\}_{A_{i}}.

Then it is easy to see that
\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{N}={\cal B}_{0}\cup{\cal L}_{1}\cup\dots\cup{\cal L}_{\delta}
forms a decomposition of the hypercube
\mathbb{Z}_{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d_{2}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{Z}_{d_{N}}
.

Next, we will give a further decomposition of each layer
{\cal L}_{k}
. The decomposition of
{\cal L}_{k}
is similar to the decomposition of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{N}
, and see Eq. (
11
). For the set
\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\Lambda\\
{\cal P}\in\{{\cal C},{\cal D}\}\end{subarray}}\{{\cal P}_{K}\}
given in Eq. (
11
), we can obtain a decomposition
\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\Lambda\\
{\cal P}\in\{{\cal C},{\cal D}\}\end{subarray}}\{{\cal P}_{k,K}\}
of
{\cal L}_{k}
by the following replacements:

•

replace
\{0\}_{A_{i}}
with
\{k-1\}_{A_{i}}
;

•

replace
\{2\}_{A_{i}}
with
\{d_{i}-k\}_{A_{i}}
;

•

replace
\{\eta\}_{A_{i}}
with
\{\eta_{k}\}_{A_{i}}\!=\!\{k-1,k,\dots,d_{i}-k-1\}_{A_{i}}
;

•

replace
\{\xi\}_{A_{i}}
with
\{\xi_{k}\}_{A_{i}}\!=\!\{k,k+1,\dots,d_{i}-k\}_{A_{i}}
.

Therefore, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 5

The set
\{{\cal B}_{0}\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\Lambda\\
{\cal P}\in\{{\cal C},{\cal D}\}\\
1\leq k\leq\delta\end{subarray}}\{{\cal P}_{k,K}\}
is a decomposition of
\mathbb{Z}_{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d_{2}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{Z}_{d_{N}}
.

5.2
Strongly nonlocal OPS in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
for odd
N\geq 3

For the subcube
{\cal P}_{k,K}={\cal P}_{k,K}^{(A_{1})}\times{\cal P}_{k,K}^{(A_{2})}\times\cdots\times{\cal P}_{k,K}^{(A_{N})}
, we define

|{\cal P}_{k,K}\rangle_{A_{j}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}|k-1\rangle_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}^{(A_{j})}_{k,K}=\{k-1\}_{A_{j}},\\
|d_{j}-k\rangle_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}^{(A_{j})}_{k,K}=\{d_{j}-k\}_{A_{j}},\\
|\eta_{k}\rangle_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}^{(A_{j})}_{k,K}=\{\eta_{k}\}_{A_{j}},\\
|\xi_{k}\rangle_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}^{(A_{j})}_{k,K}=\{\xi_{k}\}_{A_{j}},\\
\end{array}\right.

(23)

where
|\eta_{k}\rangle_{A_{j}}
,
|\xi_{k}\rangle_{A_{j}}
are the Fourier basis spanned by
\left\{|m\rangle_{A_{j}}\right\}_{m=k-1}^{d_{j}-k-1}
and
\left\{|m\rangle_{A_{j}}\right\}_{m=k}^{d_{j}-k}
, respectively. That is

\displaystyle|\eta_{k}\rangle_{A_{j}}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\sum_{m=k-1}^{d_{j}-k-1}\kappa_{j}^{(m-k+1)n}|m\rangle_{A_{j}}\right\}_{n=0}^{d_{j}-2k},

(24)

\displaystyle|\xi_{k}\rangle_{A_{j}}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\sum_{m=k}^{d_{j}-k}\kappa_{j}^{(m-k)n}|m\rangle_{A_{j}}\right\}_{n=0}^{d_{j}-2k},

where
\kappa_{j}=e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{d_{j}-2k+1}}
is a root of unity.
We also denote

|{\cal P}_{k,K}\rangle=|{\cal P}_{k,K}\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|{\cal P}_{k,K}\rangle_{A_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes|{\cal P}_{k,K}\rangle_{A_{N}},

and

|{\cal B}_{0}\rangle:=|\beta_{1}\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|\beta_{2}\rangle_{A_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes|\beta_{N}\rangle_{A_{N}},

(25)

where
|\beta_{j}\rangle_{A_{j}}
is the Fourier basis spanned by
\left\{|m\rangle_{A_{j}}\right\}_{m=\delta}^{d_{j}-\delta-1}
. That is

|\beta_{j}\rangle_{A_{j}}:=\left\{\sum_{m=\delta}^{d_{j}-\delta-1}\tau_{j}^{(m-\delta)n}|m\rangle_{A_{j}}\right\}_{n=0}^{d_{j}-2\delta-1},

(26)

where
\tau_{j}=e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{d_{j}-2\delta}}
is another root of unity.
Now, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 6

When
N
is odd, in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
,
3\leq d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\cdots\leq d_{N}
, the set

\{|{\cal B}_{0}\rangle\}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\Lambda\\
{\cal P}\in\{{\cal C},{\cal D}\}\\
1\leq k\leq\delta\end{subarray}}|{\cal P}_{k,K}\rangle

is an OPB.

We can also give a strongly nonlocal OPS in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
.

Theorem 3

When
N
is odd, in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
, the OPS

{\cal O}:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\Lambda\\
{\cal P}\in\{{\cal C},{\cal D}\}\end{subarray}}|{\cal P}_{1,K}\rangle
is strongly nonlocal, and

|{\cal O}|=d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{N}-(d_{1}-2)(d_{2}-2)\cdots(d_{N}-2).

The proof of Theorem
3
is similar to Theorem
1
.

5.3
UPB in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
for odd
N\geq 3

We denote

|S\rangle\!:=\!\left(\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{d_{1}-1}|i_{1}\rangle\!\right)_{\!A_{1}}\!\!\!\!\!\otimes\left(\sum_{i_{2}=0}^{d_{2}-1}|i_{2}\rangle\!\right)_{\!A_{2}}\!\!\!\!\!\otimes\cdots\otimes\left(\sum_{i_{N}=0}^{d_{N}-1}|i_{N}\rangle\!\right)_{\!A_{N}}\!\!\!\!\!.

(27)

Also, for each element
{\cal P}_{k,K}
(and also
{\cal B}_{0}
) in the decomposition, we denote that

|{\cal P}_{k,K}^{+}\rangle_{A_{j}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}|k-1\rangle_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}^{(A_{j})}=\{k-1\}_{A_{j}},\\
|d_{j}-k\rangle_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}^{(A_{j})}=\{d_{j}-k\}_{A_{j}},\\
\sum_{i\in\eta_{k}}|i\rangle_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}^{(A_{j})}=\{\eta_{k}\}_{A_{j}},\\
\sum_{i\in\xi_{k}}|i\rangle_{A_{j}}&\text{if}\ {\cal P}^{(A_{j})}=\{\xi_{k}\}_{A_{j}}\\
\end{array}\right.

(28)

and the product state

|{\cal P}_{k,K}^{+}\rangle=|{\cal P}_{k,K}^{+}\rangle_{A_{1}}\otimes|{\cal P}_{k,K}^{+}\rangle_{A_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes|{\cal P}_{k,K}^{+}\rangle_{A_{N}}\in|{\cal P}_{k,K}\rangle.

(29)

We also denote

|{\cal B}_{0}^{+}\rangle=\left(\sum_{i_{1}=\delta}^{d_{1}-\delta-1}|i_{1}\rangle\right)_{A_{1}}\otimes\left(\sum_{i_{2}=\delta}^{d_{2}-\delta-1}|i_{2}\rangle\right)_{A_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\left(\sum_{i_{N}=\delta}^{d_{N}-\delta-1}|i_{N}\rangle\right)_{A_{N}}\in|{\cal B}_{0}\rangle.

(30)

Then we are ready to generalize Theorem
2
.

Theorem 4

When
N
is odd, in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
,
3\leq d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\cdots\leq d_{N}
, the set of orthogonal product states

\mathcal{U}:=\left\{|S\rangle\right\}\cup\left(|{\cal B}_{0}\rangle\setminus\{|{\cal B}_{0}^{+}\rangle\}\right)\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\Lambda\\
{\cal P}\in\{{\cal C},{\cal D}\}\\
1\leq k\leq\delta\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\left(|{\cal P}_{K}\rangle\setminus\{|{\cal P}_{K}^{+}\rangle\}\right)

(31)

is a UPB of size
d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{N}-2^{N}\delta
, where
\delta=\lfloor\frac{d_{1}-1}{2}\rfloor
.

We note that in Theorem
4
,
|{\cal B}_{0}\rangle=\{|{\cal B}_{0}^{+}\rangle\}
is a set of a single element when
d_{1}=d_{2}=\dots=d_{N}
is odd, (which corresponds to the center vector in the cube). And hence there is no such term
|{\cal B}_{0}\rangle\!\setminus\!\{|{\cal B}_{0}^{+}\rangle\}
in the result in Section
4
.

The proof is similar to Theorem
2
. A subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
is a completely entangled subspace if it has no non-zero product state, and the maximum dimension of the completely entangled subspace is
\prod_{i=1}^{N}d_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_{i}+N-1

[
55
]
. The orthogonal complement of the subspace spanned by a UPB is a completely entangled subspace. Then our UPB of size
d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{N}-2^{N}\lfloor\frac{d_{1}-1}{2}\rfloor
in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
(
3\leq d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\cdots\leq d_{N}
,
N
is odd and
N\geq 3
) can be used to construct a completely entangled subspace with dimension
2^{N}\lfloor\frac{d_{1}-1}{2}\rfloor
, which is smaller than the maximum dimension.

6
Applications

In this section, we consider some applications of our results in quantum secret sharing, uncompletable product bases, and PPT entangled states.

1. Quantum secret sharing.

It is known that non-orthogonal states cannot be perfectly distinguished
[
56
]
. Therefore, to perfectly distinguish a set of multipartite orthogonal states, it is necessary to use OPLMs. Suppose that information is encoded into a strongly nonlocal orthogonal product set (OPS) in an
N
-partite system and then sent to
N
players.
If the
N
players want to recover the original information, then they must perform OPLMs. Note that it is impossible to eliminate one or more states from the strongly nonlocal OPS by OPLMs for every bipartition of the subsystems. If
k
(
k<N
) players collude with each other, which means they can perform joint OPLMs, then the original information cannot be perfectly recovered by the
N
players. To perfectly recover the original information, the
N
players can perform a global OPLM.

2. Uncompletable product bases in every bipartition.

An OPS in
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}{\cal H}_{i}
is an
uncompletable product basis (UCPB)
if it cannot be extended to an OPB in
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}{\cal H}_{i}

[
7
]
.
It is known that a UPB must be a UCPB, while the converse is not true in general.
In 2003, DiVincenzo
et al.
proposed an open question, whether there exists a UPB which is a UCPB for every bipartition of the subsystems. Recently, Shi
et al.
answered this open question by showing such a UPB in arbitrary three-,and four-partite system
[
52
]
.

Since
\mathcal{U}
is invariant under the cyclic permutation of the
N
parties (Lemma
2
), there is only one case of bipartition when
N=3
,
A_{1}|A_{2}A_{3}
. When
N=5
, there are in total three kinds of different bipartitions,
A_{1}|A_{2}A_{3}A_{4}A_{5}
,
A_{1}A_{2}|A_{3}A_{4}A_{5}
and
A_{1}A_{3}|A_{2}A_{4}A_{5}
. For general
N
, there are
\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{N-1}{2}}\binom{N}{i}}{N}
kinds of different bipartitions.
When the number of parties
N
grows, there would be much more different cases.

Despite of the complexity of the bipartition types, we believe that our construction
{\cal U}
gives a UCPB for every bipartition of the subsystems for any odd
N\geq 3
.
For every bipartition of the subsystems, as
{\cal U}
is a UPB, it suffices to prove that the orthogonal complement
{\cal H}_{{\cal U}}^{\bot}
has no basis of orthogonal product states in every biparition. By definition of
{\cal U}
in Theorem
31
, we have
\mathop{\rm Dim}{\cal H}_{{\cal U}}^{\bot}=2^{N}
and

{\cal H}_{{\cal U}}^{\bot}=\mathrm{Span}\left(\{|{\cal B}_{0}^{+}\rangle\}\cup\{|{\cal P}_{K}^{+}\rangle\mid K\in\Lambda,{\cal P}\in\{{\cal C},{\cal D}\}\}\right)\cap(\mathrm{Span}|S\rangle)^{\bot}.

In other words,
{\cal H}_{{\cal U}}^{\bot}
consists of linear combinations of
|{\cal B}_{0}^{+}\rangle
and
|{\cal P}_{K}^{+}\rangle
(
2^{N}+1
vectors in total) such that the linear combination is perpendicular to
|S\rangle
. Thus the problem became to prove there is no basis of orthogonal product states in such space in every bipartition, with the help of properties of
{\cal U}
.

We present the conjecture and a possible proof procedure as follows.

Conjecture 4

For any odd
N\geq 3
, the construction
{\cal U}
defined in Theorem
2
is an UCPB for every bipartition of the subsystems.

Approach.
For any fixed bipartition of
N
parties, we propose a possible proof idea with the following steps

(i)

Suppose
d=3
and there is a orthogonal product basis
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{2^{N}}
of
{\cal H}_{{\cal U}}^{\bot}
under the bipartition. Then there is at least one
|\psi_{j}\rangle
contains
|{\cal B}_{0}^{+}\rangle
in the linear combination of
|{\cal B}_{0}^{+}\rangle
and
|{\cal P}_{K}^{+}\rangle
’s with non-zero coefficient. Otherwise,
for the complete orthogonal basis
{\cal U}\cup\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{2^{N}}
,
|{\cal B}_{0}^{+}\rangle
is perpendicular to every vector of this basis except
|S\rangle
, which implies
|{\cal B}_{0}^{+}\rangle\propto|S\rangle
. This is a contradiction.

(ii)

Consider
|\psi_{j}\rangle
containing
|{\cal B}_{0}^{+}\rangle
. Similar to the proof of UPB, we might be able to prove the support set of
|\psi_{j}\rangle
must be
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{N}
.

(iii)

Because
|\psi_{j}\rangle
should be a product state under the certain bipartition, given that the support set of
|\psi_{j}\rangle
is
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{N}
, we might be able to prove that all the coefficients (under the linear combination in
|{\cal B}_{0}^{+}\rangle
and
|{\cal P}_{K}^{+}\rangle
’s) of
|\psi_{j}\rangle
are the same. This implies
|\psi_{j}\rangle\propto|S\rangle
, which contradicts to
\langle S|\psi_{j}\rangle=0
.

(iv)

Finally, similar to the proof in Section
5
, we can generalize the result to larger
d
by recursion.

Although we can apply the approach above to check the result technically with some small party numbers such as
N=5
, it is much challenging to deduce the general result because the large number of bipartition types and different shapes in the cube partition construction.

3. PPT entangled states in every biparition.

A mixed state is called a PPT state if it is positive under partial transposition (PPT). PPT entangled states corresponds to bound entangled states, where no pure entanglement
can be distilled
[
53
]
. UPBs can be used to construct PPT entangled states, that is, the normalized projector
\rho_{{\cal H}_{{\cal U}}^{\bot}}
on the orthogonal complement
{\cal H}_{{\cal U}}^{\bot}
of the subspace spanned by a UPB
{\cal U}
is a PPT entangled state
[
46
]
. For our normalized UPB
{\cal U}=\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{m}
in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
with odd
N\geq 3
, where
m=|{\cal U}|=d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{N}-2^{N}\lfloor\frac{d_{1}-1}{2}\rfloor
, the state
\rho_{{\cal H}_{{\cal U}}^{\bot}}
can be written as

\rho_{{\cal H}_{{\cal U}}^{\bot}}=\frac{1}{2^{N}\lfloor\frac{d_{1}-1}{2}\rfloor}\left(\mathbb{I}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\psi_{i}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{i}|\right).

(32)

Next we show that
\rho_{{\cal H}_{{\cal U}}^{\bot}}
is in fact a PPT entangled state in every bipartition. Let
S
be a proper subset of
\{A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{N}\}
, then under the partial transpose of the parties
S

7
Conclusion and Discussion

In this paper, based on the decomposition of
N
-dimensional hypercubes for odd
N\geq 3
, we constructed strongly nonlocal orthogonal product sets and unextendible product bases in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
for odd
N\geq 3
,
d_{i}\geq 3
, and
1\leq i\leq N
. For applications, we showed that our strongly nonlocal orthogonal product sets can be used for quantum secret sharing, and our UPBs might be used to construct uncompletable product bases in every biparition,
and PPT entangled states in every biparition.

During the reviewing process of this paper, strongly nonlocal orthogonal product sets in
\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
with even
N
were found in
[
57
]
. Combining our results, the phenomenon of strong quantum nonlocality without entanglement exists in arbitrary
N
-partite system.
There are some interesting problems left.
The first question is whether one can show that our unextendible product bases are strongly nonlocal for
N\geq 5
? Note that, when
N=3
, strongly nonlocal unextendible product bases has been shown in
[
37
]
. What is the minimum size of the strongly nonlocal orthogonal product sets? How to prove our Conjecture 4?

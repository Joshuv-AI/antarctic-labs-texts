Near-Optimal Distributed Computation of Small Vertex Cuts
Merav Parter, Asaf Petruschka

Abstract
We present near-optimal algorithms for detecting small vertex cuts in the 𝖢𝖮𝖭𝖦𝖤𝖲𝖳 {\mathsf{CONGEST}} model of distributed computing. Despite extensive research in this area, our understanding of the vertex connectivity of a graph is still incomplete, especially in the distributed setting. To this date, all distributed algorithms for detecting cut vertices suffer from an inherent dependency in the maximum degree of the graph, Δ \Delta . Hence, in particular, there is no truly sub-linear time algorithm for this problem, not even for detecting a single cut vertex. We take a new algorithmic approach for vertex connectivity which allows us to bypass the existing Δ \Delta barrier. As a warm-up to our approach, we show a simple O ~ ​ ( D ) \widetilde{O}(D) -round 1 1 1 Throughout the paper, we use the notation O ~ \widetilde{O} to hide poly-logarithmic in n n terms. randomized algorithm for computing all cut vertices in a D D -diameter n n -vertex graph. This improves upon the O ⁡ ( D + Δ / log ⁡ n ) O(D+\Delta/\log n) -round algorithm of [Pritchard and Thurimella, ICALP 2008]. Our key technical contribution is an O ~ ​ ( D ) \widetilde{O}(D) -round randomized algorithm for computing all cut pairs in the graph, improving upon the state-of-the-art O ​ ( Δ ⋅ D ) 4 O(\Delta\cdot D)^{4} -round algorithm by [Parter, DISC ’19]. Note that even for the considerably simpler setting of edge cuts, currently O ~ ​ ( D ) \widetilde{O}(D) -round algorithms are known only for detecting pairs of cut edges. Our approach is based on employing the well-known linear graph sketching technique [Ahn, Guha and McGregor, SODA 2012] along with the heavy-light tree decomposition of [Sleator and Tarjan, STOC 1981]. Combining this with a careful characterization of the survivable subgraphs, allows us to determine the connectivity of G ∖ { x , y } G\setminus\{x,y\} for every pair x , y ∈ V x,y\in V , using O ~ ​ ( D ) \widetilde{O}(D) -rounds. We believe that the tools provided in this paper are useful for omitting the Δ \Delta -dependency even for larger cut values.

paths from
\mathcal{P}_{light}
.
∎

Lemma 4.17
.

There is a randomized
\widetilde{O}(D)
-round algorithm such that w.h.p., for every ordered light pair
\langle x,y\rangle
, both
x
and
y
are informed of their pairing and of the vertices
v_{C^{\langle x,y\rangle}},u_{C^{\langle x,y\rangle}}
. Consequently,
x
and
y
can route messages to each other on
\Pi_{\langle x,y\rangle}
.

Proof.

First, observe that each vertex
x
can identify all vertices
y
such
\langle x,y\rangle
is a light ordered pair, and choose the corresponding component
C^{\langle x,y\rangle}
, by using the information of its connectivity tree
\widehat{T}_{x}
and of Lemma
4.5
. The vertices
v_{C^{\langle x,y\rangle}},u_{C^{\langle x,y\rangle}}
can also be identified by
x
from
\pi_{x}(s,C^{\langle x,y\rangle})
. Hence, our goal is to supply the
y
vertices of each ordered light pair
\langle x,y\rangle
with all the required information, which is the list
I_{\langle x,y\rangle}=[\langle x,y\rangle,v_{C^{\langle x,y\rangle}},u_{C^{\langle x,y\rangle}}]
consisting of
\widetilde{O}(1)
bits (recall that each vertex is identified with its compressed-path, i.e. its
\mathsf{anc}_{T}
-label). We do this by routing the message
I_{\langle x,y\rangle}
from the source
x
to target
y
along
\Pi_{\langle x,y\rangle}
. Note that any vertex in
\Pi_{\langle x,y\rangle}
, upon receiving the message
I_{\langle x,y\rangle}
, can determine the next edge of
\Pi_{\langle x,y\rangle}
along which
I_{\langle x,y\rangle}
should be routed, by using the
\mathsf{anc}_{T}
-labels found in
I_{\langle x,y\rangle}
. Thus, we essentially have an instance of the packet-routing problem, where the source-target pairs, are the ordered light pairs
\langle x,y\rangle
, the packets are the messages
I_{\langle x,y\rangle}
, and the paths are the channels
\Pi_{\langle x,y\rangle}
respectively. The dilation and congestion are both
\widetilde{O}(D)
by
Lemma

4.16
. Hence, we can use the scheduling of
Theorem

1.8
to execute this packet-routing instance within
\widetilde{O}(D)
, w.h.p.
∎

Finally, by combining
Lemmas

4.16
and
4.17
with
Corollary

4.8
, we get that within
\widetilde{O}(D)
-rounds, w.h.p. all ordered light pairs (and thus, also the unordered light pairs) can determine if they form a cut pair in
G
.

4.4.2
Heavy Pairs

We now consider the more challenging task of connectivity checking for heavy pairs. Our strategy is based on classifying these pairs further as
mutual
and
non-mutual
. The special configuration of mutual pairs is dealt with in a similar fashion to light pairs, namely, implementing the promise for the algorithm of
Section

4.3
by choosing bounded-congestion communication channels. Perhaps surprisingly, for the remaining non-mutual pairs it is sufficient for the vertices to collect a small amount of information over the tree
T
, which enables every non-mutual pair
xy
to determine the connectivity of
G\setminus\{x,y\}
by local computation in
x
or

.

We start with some preliminary notions required for our definition of mutual pairs. For a vertex
x\in V
, let
R(x)=\{v\in V\setminus V(T_{x})\mid\text{$v$ has a neighbor in $H_{x}$}\}
, and let

be the lowest common ancestor of the vertices in

(w.r.t.

). Observe that for the last edge
(u_{H_{x}},v_{H_{x}})
of
\pi_{x}(s,H_{x})
we have

and

. As

is an ancestor of any vertex in

, we obtain that

Definition 4.4
.

A heavy pair

and

. Otherwise,

is a

The following claim concerns the collection of preliminary information required for the connectivity algorithm of both mutual and non-mutual pairs.

Claim 4.18

Within
\widetilde{O}(D)
rounds, w.h.p. each

learns

\mathsf{sketch}_{G\setminus\{y\}}(H_{y})

Proof.

We first describe the procedure letting each

learn

.
For a (non-empty)

, denote by
\operatorname{LCA}(U)
the lowest common ancestor of all vertices in
U
. We first importantly note that
\operatorname{LCA}
is an aggregate function. Also, note that given the compressed-paths of the vertices in
U
, the compressed-path of
\operatorname{LCA}(U)
can be deduced. Next, observe that a vertex
a
can use the known compressed-paths of its
G
-neighbors to locally determine which of them belong to

, for each of its ancestors
x\in\pi(s,a)

Hence, by executing bottom-up
\operatorname{LCA}
-aggregation on

\widetilde{O}(D)

\ell_{x,a}:=\operatorname{LCA}(\{v\in V\setminus V(T_{x})\mid\text{$v$ has a neighbor in $V(T_{a})$}\}).

Within another
\widetilde{O}(1)

can learn, for each of its

\ell_{x,x^{\prime}}

,
x
can detect which of its children

belongs to
H_{x}
, and thus it can locally compute the compressed path of

r(x)=\operatorname{LCA}(\{\ell_{x,x^{\prime}}\mid\text{$x^{\prime}$ is a $T$-child of $x$ inside $H_{x}$}\}).

This compressed-path enables
x
to locate
r(x)
in the tree
\widehat{T}_{x}
(stored locally at

by
Lemma

4.2
).

To finish the proof, it remains to show the procedure letting each
x
learn the information
I_{y}=[r(y),\pi_{y}^{*}(s,H_{y}),\mathsf{sketch}_{G\setminus\{y\}}(H_{y})]
for every
y\in V(\widehat{T}_{x})
.
We first observe that each vertex
y
holds
I_{y}
: The vertex
r(y)
is known to

by the previous part of the proof.
\pi_{y}^{*}(s,H_{y})
is known from
\widehat{T}_{y}
(by
Lemma

4.2
).
\mathsf{sketch}_{G\setminus\{y\}}(H_{y})
is obtained from
\mathsf{sketch}_{G}(H_{y})
by
Lemma

1.7
.
We let every vertex
y
exchange
I_{y}
with its neighbors. By propagating this information down on

in a pipeline manner, each vertex
v
learns
I_{y}
for every
y\in\pi(s,v)
, within
\widetilde{O}(D)
rounds. Finally, we run again Step 2 of the procedure for computing connectivity trees
\widetilde{T}_{x}
in
Section

4.1
(with the same random seeds), only now augmenting the path-sketches further by including next to each stored path
\pi(s,u)
the additional
\widetilde{O}(D)
information of
I_{y}
for each

. This lets
x
learn
I_{y}
for all vertices

in the paths included in
\widehat{T}_{x}
(which are obtained by the path-sketches), as required.
∎

Handling Mutual Pairs.

The mutual pairs
xy
are handled almost exactly as the light pairs, by defining

4.3
.

First, notice that for a mutual pair

, both

and

4.18

and

, we assume w.l.o.g. that

\Pi_{x,y}=\pi(x,v_{H_{x}})\circ(v_{H_{x}},u_{H_{x}})\circ\pi(u_{H_{x}},y).

Let
\mathcal{P}_{mutual}=\{\Pi_{x,y}\mid\text{$xy$ is a mutual pair}\}

xr(x)

We assert that
\mathcal{P}_{mutual}
satisfies the promise. The fact that each path has length

is clear. Next, consider any vertex

. Then

can appear in the first segment of a

By applying a procedure identical to
Lemma

4.17
(only replacing

and

rounds, w.h.p. each such

Handling Non-Mutual Pairs.

Finally, we turn to handle the remaining non-mutual pairs. We will show that for each such pair
xy
, one of

can apply a local computation for determining the connectivity of
G\setminus\{x,y\}
. To this end, we start with an observation regarding heavy pairs.

Observation 4.19
.

Let
xy
be a heavy pair. Then
G\setminus\{x,y\}
is connected iff there is some
z_{h}\in\{x_{h},y_{h}\}
that is connected to the source
s
in
G\setminus\{x,y\}
.

Proof.

The ‘only if’ direction is trivial. We show the ‘if’ direction for
z_{h}=y_{h}
(the case
z_{h}=x_{h}
is symmetric). By
Lemma

4.4
, the only vertices that can disconnect from
s
in

are those of components in
\mathcal{FS}(x,y)\cup\mathcal{FS}(y,x)
.

Consider first a component
C\in\mathcal{FS}(x,y)
. By
4.15
, it holds that
(y,y_{h})\in\pi_{x}(s,C)
. Hence, in
G\setminus\{x,y\}
, it is possible to get from
s
to
C
in
G\setminus\{x,y\}
by first walking to
y_{h}
, and then continuing along
\pi_{x}(s,C)
. We can now conclude that all vertices in

are connected to
s
in
G\setminus\{x,y\}
.

Finally, consider a component
C\in\mathcal{FS}(y,x)
. By
4.15
, it holds that
(x,x_{h})\in\pi_{y}(s,C)
. Then, as
x_{h}\in V_{x}
, it is possible to get from
s
to
C
in
G\setminus\{x,y\}
by first walking to
x_{h}
, and then continuing along
\pi_{y}(s,C)
.
∎

Fix some vertex
x

xy
is a heavy-pair such that

is disconnected, then it must be that
y\in\pi_{x}(s,H_{x})
. By
Lemma

4.17

x
knows all of its light-mates, hence it can determine all its heavy-mates
y\in\pi_{x}(s,H_{x})
. We will show that for any heavy-mate

such that

can determine the connectivity of

by local computation (after
\widetilde{O}(D)
rounds of common preprocessing for all vertices). This implies that at least one vertex of every non-mutual pair can determine if it is a cut pair, as desired.

First, we assert that
x
can disregard all heavy-mates

lying (strictly) below

in
\pi_{x}(s,H_{x})
, by the following observation.

Observation 4.20

Let
x\in V

, then the source

is connected to

in

Proof.

First note that
y
cannot be an ancestor of all vertices in

, since this would contradict

being the lowest such vertex. Hence, there exists some

such that

. By definition of

, and

has a neighbor

to
H_{x}
in
G\setminus\{x,y\}

∎

We turn to handle the remaining relevant heavy-mates. Fix some heavy-mate
y

r(x)
in

(recall that we only care now about

the union of all components in

\widehat{C}(x,y)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\in\mathcal{C}_{x}\\

We have the following key claim:

G\setminus\{x,y\}
is connected iff there is an outgoing edge from

in

.

Proof.

The ‘only if’ direction is trivial. For the ‘if’ direction, first note that the vertices of
\widehat{C}(x,y)\cup H_{y}
are all in the same connected component in

, since they are all connected to
y_{h}
in

.
Therefore, it is enough to show that every

is connected to

in

. If

is not a descendant of

or

in

then this is obvious. If
a
is a descendant of
x
, then it belongs to some component

such that
(y,y_{h})\notin\pi_{x}(s,C)
, so by
4.15
(as
xy
is a heavy pair) we have
C\notin\mathcal{FS}(x,y)
, and by
Lemma

4.4

C
is connected to
s
in
G\setminus\{x,y\}
. It remains to consider
a
which is a descendant of
y
, in some component
C\in\mathcal{C}_{y}
,
C\neq H_{y}
. It suffices to show that
(x,x_{h})\notin\pi_{y}(s,C)
, as this would imply that
C
and

are connected in
G\setminus\{x,y\}
by using
4.15
and
Lemma

4.4
(similarly to the previous case). Assume towards a contradiction that
(x,x_{h})\in\pi_{y}(s,C)
. Then for the last edge
(u_{C},v_{C})
of

, we have
u_{C}\in H_{x}
and

. Hence,

, and thus

is an ancestor of

in

. As

(since

), it follows that

in this path, we have that

By this last claim, in order to determine the connectivity of

with

Lemma 4.22
.

Within
\widetilde{O}(D)

of

in

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathsf{sketch}_{G\setminus\{x,y\}}(\widehat{C}(x,y)\cup H_{y})=

\displaystyle\mathsf{sketch}_{G\setminus\{x,y\}}(\widehat{C}(x,y)\setminus H_{x})\oplus\mathsf{sketch}_{G\setminus\{x,y\}}(H_{x})\oplus\mathsf{sketch}_{G\setminus\{x,y\}}(H_{y})~.

above

in
\pi(s,u_{H_{x}})

For the first term, we start by giving an

, each vertex

. Next, observe that if

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{u\in V(T_{v})}[S(u)]_{ij}=\bigoplus_{u\in V(T_{v})}\mathsf{sketch}_{G\setminus\{y_{ij}(v)\}}(u)=\mathsf{sketch}_{G\setminus\{y_{ij}(v)\}}(V(T_{v}))~.

This concludes the

follows.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we provide distributed
{\mathsf{CONGEST}}
algorithms for detecting vertex cuts of size at most two, with round complexity of
\widetilde{O}(D)
.
These bounds are nearly tight, and in addition, nearly match the current bounds obtained for the corresponding edge cut problems.
It would be interesting if our techniques, e.g., for eliminating the dependency in the maximum degree
\Delta
, could be extended to detecting vertex cuts of any constant size within
{\rm poly}(D,\log n)
rounds. The latter bound is currently only achievable for the (simpler) edge cut problem
[
Par19
]
.

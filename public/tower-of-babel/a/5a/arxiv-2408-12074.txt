Abstract
. A digraph is s s -arc-transitive if its automorphism group is transitive on directed paths with s s edges, that is, on s s -arcs. Although infinite families of finite s s -arc transitive digraphs of arbitrary valency were constructed by the third author in 1989, existence of a vertex-primitive 2 2 -arc-transitive digraph was not known until an infinite family was constructed by the second author with Li and Xia in 2017. This led to a conjecture by the second author and Xia in 2018 that, for a finite vertex-primitive s s -arc-transitive digraph, s s is at most 2 2 , together with their proof that it is sufficient to prove the conjecture for digraphs with an almost simple group of automorphisms. This paper confirms the conjecture for finite symplectic groups. Key word: vertex-primitive; s s -arc-transitive; digraph; symplectic groups; maximal subgroups



1.
Introduction

The exploration of finite
s
-arc-transitive digraphs dates back to at least 1989 when the third author
[
25
]
constructed, for each
s
and

s
-arc-transitive digraphs of valency

(s+1)
-arc-transitive. In that paper she asked if there exists a finite vertex-primitive
s
-arc-transitive digraph with

, and whether there

for such digraphs. The situation for digraphs is very different from that for graphs, where for a finite
s
-arc transitive graph of valency at least 3, the parameter

is at most 8, see
[
28
]
, and vertex-primitive
8
-arc-transitive examples exist.

The first question in
[
25
]
was answered positively nearly 30 years later in
[
10
]
by the second author with Li and Xia, and shortly afterwards the second question about determining an upper bound on

was reduced
[
12
]
to the case where the graph automorphism group is almost simple, and in that paper it was conjectured that

. Since then this conjecture has been settled for several infinite families of almost simple groups: by the second author with Li and Xia for groups with socle
\mathrm{PSL}_{n}(q)
in
[
11
]
, by the authors with Pan, Wu and Yin
[
24
,
5
]
for alternating and symmetric groups, and the authors
[
7
]
for the small Ree groups and the Suzuki groups. The aim of this paper is to prove the conjecture for the finite symplectic groups:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a

-vertex-primitive
(G,s)
-arc-transitive digraph such that
\mathrm{Soc}(G)=\mathrm{PSp}_{2n}(q)^{\prime}

.

The condition

is imposed since
\mathrm{PSp}_{2}(q)\cong\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)
, and the conjecture has already been proved for this case in
[
11
]
. As we noted above, the first infinite family of examples of
G
-vertex-primitive
(G,2)
-arc-transitive digraphs was given in
[
10
]
; these examples have

and vertex stabiliser
\mathrm{A}_{6}

is a prime such that

. In seeking the smallest example of such a digraph, Yin, Feng and Xia
[
30

identifed the first member of this family, namely with

and
3,075,815,460>3\cdot 10^{9}
vertices, as being the smallest vertex-primitive
2
-arc-transitive digraph.

Summary of concepts and notation above and our proof strategy:
A transitive action of a group
G
is primitive if and only if the stabiliser of a point is a maximal subgroup of
G
. Also
G
is said to be an
almost simple group
if
G
has a unique
minimal normal subgroup

T
and

T

T\triangleleft G\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(T)
.

Thus we will investigate all the maximal subgroups of all the almost simple groups
G
with socle
\mathrm{PSp}_{2n}(q)^{\prime}
. Aschbacher
[
1

developed a useful tool to classify the maximal subgroups of classical groups: they can be divided into nine classes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{9}
. The maximal subgroups in classes
\mathcal{C}_{1}-\mathcal{C}_{8}
are the so called
maximal subgroups of geometric type
. Each has some geometric interpretation, and the specific subgroup structure can be found in
[
16

. The maximal subgroups of
G
in class
\mathcal{C}_{9}
are almost simple with socle
S
, say, and the preimage of
S
in
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(q)
is absolutely irreducible and not writable over any proper subfield of
\mathbf{F}_{q}
.
For the special case where
2n=4
the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{PSp}_{4}(2^{f}))\nleqslant\mathrm{P\Gamma Sp}_{4}(2^{f})
. Here, in addition to the families
\mathcal{C}_{1}-\mathcal{C}_{9}
of maximal subgroups, if

then by
[
1
, Section 14]
there are three classes of so-called
novelty maximal subgroups

\mathcal{A}_{i}
for

to be considered. Note that a maximal subgroup

of

is a novelty maximal subgroup if

is not maximal in
\mathrm{PSp}_{4}(2^{f})
.

After giving some necessary preliminary results and discussion in Sections
2
and
3

1.1
in Section
4
. We treat in separate subsections the possibilities that a vertex stabiliser

is a maximal subgroup lying in
\mathcal{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{9}

4.5

The methods employed in this paper are a significant development of those used to analyse the analogous problem for linear groups with socle

in

. The new results or ones similar to them, especially those in Section

in this paper, although additional new ideas will be needed. Indeed, in his PhD thesis
[

The situation for orthogonal groups might be trickier, since there are three different types of orthogonal groups, and many more factorisations.

Now we remind the reader about some of the terms used above. A
digraph

\Gamma
is a pair

such that

-arc-transitivity for a

For comparison, note that a graph

is a pair
(V,\sim)
such that

2.2.
Number theoretic results

Throughout the paper, we will frequently investigate the prime divisors of the orders of groups, so we also give some number theoretic results that we will use.

Definition 2.1
.

For integers

and

, a prime number

. For

The next corollary is a product of an elegant theorem of Zsigmondy
[

]

Corollary 2.2
.

For

, the set
\mathrm{ppd}(n,m)\neq\varnothing
except when
(n,m)=(2,6)
, or

is a power of
2
and

. Moreover, if

.

Lemma 2.3
.

(Legendre’s formula)
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]

For any positive integer
n
and prime
p
we have
(n!)_{p}<p^{n/(p-1)}
.

Corollary 2.4
.

For any positive integer
n\geqslant 5
, we have
n_{2}<2^{n-4}
.

Proof.

The result is trivially true for
n\leqslant 8
. Thus it suffices for us to consider the case where
n\geqslant 9
. We note that
((n-1)!)_{2}\geqslant(8!)_{2}=2^{7}
when

. By Lemma

we obtain that
(n!)_{2}<2^{n}
and therefore
(n!)_{2}\leqslant 2^{n-1}
. Thus

n_{2}=\frac{(n!)_{2}}{((n-1)!)_{2}}\leqslant\frac{2^{n-1}}{2^{7}}<2^{n-4}

and the result follows.
∎

2.3.
Graph theoretic results

Here we will give some idea of how to construct a digraph. For a group
G
and subgroup
H
of
G
, let
V:=\{Hx\mid x\in G\}
and

be an element in

such that
g^{-1}\notin HgH
. We define a binary relation

such that
Hx\to Hy
if and only if

for any

. Then

is a digraph and we denote it by
\mathrm{Cos}(G,H,g)
. In particular, the action

of

on

is the right multiplication which preserves the relation

. Thus
R_{H}(G)\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Cos}(G,H,g))
. By
[
12
, Lemma 2.4]
,
\mathrm{Cos}(G,H,g)
is

-arc-transitive.

Here are some results in
[
12
]
and
[

]
that reveal some important properties for an
s
-arc-transitive digraph

where
s\geqslant 2
.
The following results give the conditions for a

-vertex-primitive digraphs to be

-arc-transitive, which are cited directly from
[

]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
11
, Lemma 2.12]

Let
\Gamma
be a digraph and

such that

acts

-arc-transitively on

. Let

-arc-transitively on

Lemma 2.6
.

[

Let
\Gamma

-arc-transitive digraph with arc

Lemma 2.7
.

[

Let

.

Note that the factorisation of

Definition 2.8

For a group

, we say

.

We now set out the following hypothesis that we will use throughout this section.

Hypothesis 2.9
.

Let
\Gamma
be a vertex-primitive
(G,s)
-arc-transitive digraph for some
s\geqslant 2
, and let

be a

-arc and

such that

. Then by Lemma

we have
(G_{uv})^{g}=G_{vw}
and

is a homogeneous factorisation.

Note that a necessary condition for a digraph
\Gamma
to be
G
-vertex-primitive and
(G,2)
-arc-transitive is that
G_{v}
is a maximal subgroup of
G
and
G_{v}
admits a homogeneous factorisation. Therefore, to disprove the

-arc-transitivity of

does not have such a homogeneous factorisation.

We have the following elementary results.

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a
(G,s)
-arc-transitive digraph with arc
u\to v
. Let
g\in G
and
(u,v)^{g}=(v,w)
. Suppose that
G_{v}=T:K
, where

and
|K|=b
such that
\gcd(a,b)=1
. Then

.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that

. Then we deduce by Lemma
2.7
that
G_{v}=G_{uv}G_{vw}
with
G_{uv}^{g}=G_{vw}
. Let us denote by

to

. Then for any subgroup

we have that

. Let

. Moreover, let

is the unique subgroup of order

2.4.
Computational methods

We used the computer system MAGMA
[
3
]
to check small groups to investigate if they admit homogeneous factorisations with certain properties, and if so find all of them. For a group
G
, a necessary condition for
G
to admit a homogeneous factorisation
G=HK
is that for any prime
p\in\Pi(G)
, we have
|H|_{p}=|K|_{p}\geqslant|G|_{p}^{1/2}
. We use the MAGMA command
LowIndexSubgroups
to generate the set ‘
lis
’ of all pairs
(H,K)
of subgroups
H,K
of
G
, up to conjugacy, with this property for all
p\in\Pi(G)
. (Note that
LowIndexSubgroups
generates the representatives of conjugacy classes.) We examine the orders of these subgroups and create a sub-list ‘
lis2
’ consisting of pairs
(H,K)
from ‘
lis
’ such that
|H|=|K|
. To decide, for a pair
(H,K)
in ‘
lis2
’, whether
G=HK
it suffices to check if
|G|\cdot|H\cap K|=|H|\cdot|K|
, and we note that this equality is independent of the choice of
H,K
in their conjugacy classes. For these groups
G
, it was possible to use the MAGMA command
meet
to compute the intersection
H\cap K
and to check whether this equality held by typing the command
# G eq (#a[1])*(#a[2])/#(a[1] meet a[2])

where, for ‘
a
’ in ‘
lis2
’, ‘
a[1]
’ and ‘
a[2]
’ stand for
H
and
K
, respectively. Thus a further sub-list ‘
lis3
’ is generated consisting of all pairs
(H,K)
in ‘
lis2
’ for which
G=HK
. Finally, for pairs
(H,K)
in ‘
lis3
’, we check if
H
and
K
are isomorphic via the MAGMA command
IsIsomorphic(a[1],a[2])
, producing a final sub-list ‘
lis4
’ of pairs
(H,K)
corresponding to homogeneous factorisations of
G
.
Here is an example of how we check the homogeneous factorisations of
G_{v}=\mathrm{Sp}_{2}(3)\wr\mathrm{S}_{4}
in Lemma
4.7
.

> G:=WreathProduct(Sp(2,3),Sym(4));
> lis:=LowIndexSubgroups(G,1152);
> lis2:=[<x,y>:x,y in lis|x ne y and #x eq #y];
> lis3:=[a:a in lis2| #H eq (#a[1])*(#a[2])/#(a[1] meet a[2])];
> #lis4:=[a: a in lis3|IsIsomorphic(a[1],a[2])];

With similar code, we check for homogeneous factorisations of
G_{v}=\mathrm{Sp}_{2}(3)\wr\mathrm{S}_{4}
in Lemma
4.7
, and for
G_{v}=(\mathrm{PSp}_{2}(3)\times\mathrm{PGO}_{4}^{-}(3)).2
and
(\mathrm{PSp}_{2}(3)\times\mathrm{PGO}_{4}^{-}(3)).[4]
in Lemma
4.16
. In each of these cases the resulting ‘
lis4
’ was the empty list, showing that none of these groups have homogeneous factorisations.

3.
Group factorisations

In this chapter, we will develop some results on various group factorisations, which will play an important role in Sections 4 and 5.

3.1.
Basic facts about group factorisations

The following lemma investigates necessary conditions for a group to admit a factorisation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
T
be a proper non-trivial subgroup of
G
such that
T
is not normal in
G
. Suppose that
g\in G
, and that
H,K\leqslant G
are such that
H\leqslant N_{G}(T)
and
K\leqslant N_{G}(T^{g})
. Then
HK\neq G
.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that
G=HK
. Let us consider the transitive action of
G
by conjugation on
\Sigma:=\{T^{x}\mid x\in G\}
. The stabiliser in
G
of an element
T^{x}\in\Sigma
is
N_{G}(T^{x})
, and since
T
is not normal in
G
, the size
|\Sigma|>1
. Since
H\leqslant N_{G}(T)
, the factorisation
G=HK
implies that
G=N_{G}(T)K
, and as
N_{G}(T)
is the stabiliser of the ‘point’
T
of
\Sigma
we conclude that
K
is transitive on
\Sigma
. However the condition
K\leqslant N_{G}(T^{g})
implies that
K
stabilises
T^{g}\in\Sigma
, and we have a contradiction.
∎

Recall that we have defined the homogeneous factorisations in Definition
2.8
. The following results discuss homogeneous factorisations of certain groups.

Lemma 3.2
.

[
11
, Lemma 3.2]

For any homogeneous factorisation
G=AB
we have that
\Pi(A)=\Pi(B)
and for any prime
p
, we have
|A|_{p}^{2}=|B|_{p}^{2}\geqslant|G|_{p}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
11
, Lemma 3.5]

Suppose that
T\wr S_{k}\leqslant G\leqslant R\wr S_{k}
where
T\leqslant R
. Let
M
be the base subgroup of
R\wr S_{k}
and
G=AB
be a homogeneous factorisation for
G
such that
A
is transitive on the set of
k
components of
M
. Then with
\varphi_{i}(A\cap M)
being the projection of
A\cap M
to the
i
-th component of
M
, we have
\varphi_{1}(A\cap M)\cong\cdots\cong\varphi_{k}(A\cap M)
and

For part (ii), it follows from part (i) that there exists

Here

is called a

in the group

of the unordered pair

is maximal in

-subgroup.

Proposition 4.25
.

Suppose that Hypothesis
4.1
holds. and that
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Sp}(V))
, with

. Then
G_{v}
is not a maximal
\mathcal{A}_{1}
-subgroup of

.

Proposition

, which shows that every orbital of the

Lemma 4.26
.

Let
V,G,\Omega
be as above, and let
\{U_{1},W_{1}\},\{U_{2},W_{2}\}\in\Omega
.
Then there exists an element
g\in G
such that
\{U_{1},W_{1}\}^{g}=\{U_{2},W_{2}\}
and
\{U_{2},W_{2}\}^{g}=\{U_{1},W_{1}\}
.

In the proof we use the fact, see
[
4
, p. 360]
, that
\Omega
is the edge set of a bipartite distance transitive graph
\Gamma
with vertex set
\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}
, where
\Delta_{i}
is the set of totally isotropic
i
-subspaces of
V
, for
i=1,2
, The automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
is
G
, the diameter of
\Gamma
is
4
, and by
[
4
, Proposition 7.2.3]
, each pair of vertices at distance
3
in
\Gamma
is joined by a unique path of length
3
.

Proof.

We divide the analysis into three cases:

Case
1
:
W_{1}\cap W_{2}=0.
It is easiest to treat this case algebraically. Since
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are maximal totally isotropic subspaces of
V
with trivial intersection, by Lemma
4.3
(i) there exists
h\in\mathrm{Sp}(V)
such that
W_{1}^{h}=\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle
and
W_{2}^{h}=\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle
. Thus we assume that
W_{1}=\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle
and
W_{2}=\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle
. Moreover, since the stabiliser
\mathrm{Sp}(V)_{W_{1},W_{2}}
induces
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(q)
on
W_{1}
, we may further choose
U_{1}=\langle e_{1}\rangle
. Then, since
\mathrm{Sp}(V)_{W_{1},W_{2},U_{1}}
fixes
U_{1}^{\perp}\cap W_{2}=\langle f_{2}\rangle
, and is transitive on the
q
other
1
-subspaces of
W_{2}
, we may assume that
U_{2}=\langle f_{2}\rangle
or
U_{2}=\langle f_{1}\rangle
. In the former case, the element
g\in\mathrm{GL}(V)
such that
e_{i}^{g}=f_{3-i},f_{i}^{g}=e_{3-i}
, for
i=1,2
, preserves the form
B
(since
q
is even) and hence lies in
\mathrm{Sp}(V)
and interchanges
\{U_{1},W_{1}\}
and
\{U_{2},W_{2}\}
. Similarly in the latter case the element
g\in\mathrm{GL}(V)
such that
e_{i}^{g}=f_{i},f_{i}^{g}=e_{i}
, for
i=1,2
, lies in
\mathrm{Sp}(V)
and interchanges
\{U_{1},W_{1}\}
and
\{U_{2},W_{2}\}
.

Case
2
:
\dim(W_{1}\cap W_{2})=1
.
In this case we make use of properties of the distance transitive graph
\Gamma
discussed above. Let
X:=W_{1}\cap W_{2}
. If
X\not\in\{U_{1},U_{2}\}
, then in
\Gamma
the distances
d(U_{1},W_{2})=3
and
d(U_{2},W_{1})=3
and so there exists
g\in G
such that
(U_{1},W_{2})^{g}=(U_{2},W_{1})
. Further (see
[
4
, Proposition 7.2.3]
),
g
must map the unique
3
-path
(U_{1},W_{1},X,W_{2})
to the
3
-path
(U_{2},W_{2},X,W_{1})
, and hence
g
interchanges
\{U_{1},W_{1}\}
and
\{U_{2},W_{2}\}
. Next suppose that
X=U_{i}\neq U_{3-i}
for some
i\in\{1,2\}
. Then in
\Gamma
,
d(W_{i},U_{3-i})=3

and
P:=(W_{i},U_{i},W_{3-i},U_{3-i})
is the unique corresponding
3
-path. Thus there exists

such that
(W_{i},U_{3-i})^{g}=(U_{3-i},W_{i})
, and
g
must reverse the
3
-path

, and hence must interchange
\{U_{1},W_{1}\}
and
\{U_{2},W_{2}\}
. Finally suppose that
X=U_{1}=U_{2}
, so that

. Here we argue algebraically:
W_{1}/X
and
W_{2}/X
are two
1
-subspaces of the nondegenerate

-space

, and as the group induced by
\mathrm{Sp}(V)_{X}
on

is
\mathrm{Sp}_{2}(q)
which is
2
transitive on
1
-spaces, it follows that
\mathrm{Sp}(V)_{X}
contains an element
g
which interchanges
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
and hence also interchanges
\{U_{1},W_{1}\}
and
\{U_{2},W_{2}\}
.

Case
3
:
W_{1}=W_{2}
In this case, if

. So assume that

induces

, which is

-transitive on the

-subspaces of

which interchanges

and

and hence also interchanges

∎

Here we treat the second family

Proposition 4.27
.

Suppose that Hypothesis
4.1
holds. If

is a maximal
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup, then

is not
(G,3)
-arc-transitive.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that

is a maximal
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup and that

. Then by Lemma
2.5
,
\Gamma
is
(L,2)
-arc-transitive and so

with

. Also
L_{v}=G_{v}\cap L=M:\mathrm{D}_{8}\cong\mathrm{C}_{q\pm 1}^{2}:\mathrm{D}_{8}
, and the complement
\mathrm{D}_{8}=\langle b,c\rangle
such that
(x,y)^{b}=(y^{-1},x)
and
(x,y)^{c}=(y,x)

(x,y)\in M
. Let

, and let
\varphi_{i}
(for
i=1,2
) and
\pi
be the natural projections
\varphi_{i}:(M\to M_{i}
and
\pi:L_{v}\to L_{v}/M

Since
|M|
is odd,
|\pi(L_{uv})|_{2}=|L_{uv}|_{2}=|L_{vw}|_{2}=|\pi(L_{vw})|_{2}
.
Moreover,
\pi(L_{uv})\pi(L_{vw})=\pi(L_{v})=\mathrm{D}_{8}
, and it follows that
|\pi(L_{uv})|,|\pi(L_{vw})|\geqslant 4
. This implies that
b^{2}\in\pi(L_{uv})\cap\pi(L_{vw})
, and we note that
(x,y)^{b^{2}}=(x^{-1},y^{-1})
for all
(x,y)\in M
. Moreover, since
L_{uv}\cap M
and
L_{vw}\cap M
are generated by the odd-ordered elements of

and
L_{vw}
, respectively, it follows since

Since
\pi(L_{uv})\pi(L_{vw})=\pi(L_{v})=\mathrm{D}_{8}
, we deduce that either (i)
\pi(L_{uv})=\pi(L_{vw})=\mathrm{D}_{8}
, or (ii)
\pi(L_{uv})=\langle b^{2},d\rangle
and
\pi(L_{vw})=\langle b^{2},d^{\prime}\rangle
where

. In case (ii) exactly one of
\pi(L_{uv})
and
\pi(L_{vw})
contains

, and hence exactly one of these groups interchanges
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
. We may assume (in either case (i) or case (ii)) that
\pi(L_{uv})
interchanges
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
(since the argument in case (ii) where
\pi(L_{vw})

and

is similar). Thus we have
\varphi_{1}(L_{uv}\cap M)\cong\varphi_{2}(L_{uv}\cap M)
.

Let

and denote by

. Then
|M|_{r}=r^{2a}
and
|\mathrm{Syl}_{r}(M)|=1
(as
M
is abelian). Let
P=P_{1}\times P_{2}\cong C_{r^{a}}^{2}
be the unique Sylow
r
-subgroup of
M
, then since
|L_{v}|_{r}=|M|_{r}

. Let

, then

\mathrm{Syl}_{r}(L_{uv}^{g})=\mathrm{Syl}_{r}(L_{vw})=\{R^{g}\}
.
Note that

. Thus
|R\cap R^{g}|_{r}\leqslant|L_{uv}\cap L_{vw}|_{r}
, which implies that

|P|_{r}=|L_{v}|_{r}=\frac{|L_{uv}|_{r}\cdot|L_{vw}|_{r}}{|L_{uv}\cap L_{vw}|_{r}}\leqslant\frac{|R|_{r}\cdot|R^{g}|_{r}}{|R\cap R^{g}|_{r}}=|RR^{g}|.

Since
RR^{g}\leqslant P
, it follows that
P=RR^{g}
.

Claim:

|R\cap M_{i}|_{r}=1
for
i=1,2
, and
R=\{(x,x^{\alpha})\mid x\in P_{1}\}
for some automorphism
\alpha
of
P_{1}=\mathrm{C}_{r^{a}}
.

Note that

acts transitively on
\{M_{1},M_{2}\}
and

is normal in
L_{uv}
. Suppose for a contradiction that

such that

for

.
On the other hand

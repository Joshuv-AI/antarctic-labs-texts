A confined random walk locally looks like tilted random interlacements
Nicolas Bouchot

Abstract
In this paper we consider the simple random walk on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} , d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , conditioned to stay forever in a large domain D N D_{N} of typical diameter N N . Considering the range up to time t N ≥ N 2 + δ t_{N}\geq N^{2+\delta} for some δ > 0 \delta>0 , we establish a coupling with what Li & Sznitman [ LS14 ] defined as tilted random interlacements . This tilted interlacement can be described as random interlacements but with trajectories given by random walks on conductances c N ​ ( x , y ) = ϕ N ​ ( x ) ​ ϕ N ​ ( y ) c_{N}(x,y)=\phi_{N}(x)\phi_{N}(y) , where ϕ N \phi_{N} is the first eigenvector of the discrete Laplace–Beltrami operator on D N D_{N} . The coupling follows the methodology of the soft local times, introduced by Popov & Teixeira [ PT15 ] and used by Černý & Teixeira [ ČT16 ] to prove the well-known coupling between the simple random walk on the torus and the random interlacements. Keywords: random walk, confined walk, tilted interlacements, coupling 2020 Mathematics subject classification: Primary 60J10, 60K35.



1
Introduction

1.1
Simple random walk on the torus and random interlacements

Consider the discrete torus of size
N\geq 1
, denoted by
\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}/N\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, as well as the simple, nearest-neighbour random walk (SRW)
S=(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}
, started from the uniform measure on
\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}
. For
d\geq 3
, the behaviour of this random walk has been extensively studied in the literature, with a notable focus on the local geometry of its range as
N\to+\infty
. Fixing
u>0
, the range
\mathcal{R}(uN^{d})\vcentcolon=\{S_{0},\dots,S_{\lfloor uN^{d}\rfloor}\}
is “locally close” to what Sznitman introduced in
[
Szn10
]
as random interlacements (RI) at level
u
, denoted by
\mathscr{I}(u)
. Informally speaking,
\mathscr{I}(u)
is a random subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
that is the trace of a Poissonnian collection of independent random walk trajectories; we provide more details in Section
1.3.1
.

In the previous decade, several works provided a quantitative and precise sense of this “closeness” through couplings of the range
\mathcal{R}(uN^{d})
, considered as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and the interlacements
\mathscr{I}(u)
, see
[
Bel13
,
TW11
]
for example. One of the most significant results is due to
[
ČT16
]
, with a coupling on macroscopic boxes inside
\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}
; let us now state their result.
Let
\delta\in(0,1)
be fixed and consider the macroscopic box
B=[0,(1-\delta)N]^{d}\subset\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}
, for some
\delta>0
.
Then, for any
u>0
, there is a non-increasing sequence
(\varepsilon_{N})_{N\geq 1}
(that decreases as
N^{-c}
for some
c
that depends on
\delta
) and a coupling
\mathcal{Q}
of
\mathcal{R}(uN^{d})
and
\mathscr{I}((1\pm\varepsilon_{N})u)
such that for, some
\eta>0
, with
\mathcal{Q}
-probability at least
1-e^{-N^{\eta}}
, we have

\mathscr{I}((1-\varepsilon_{N})u)\cap B\subseteq\mathcal{R}(uN^{d})\cap B\subseteq\mathscr{I}((1+\varepsilon_{N})u)\cap B\,.

(1.1)

This type of results were highly influencial in tackling existing questions for SRW on the torus. We can notably cite the covering time (see
[
Bel13
]
) and the disconnection time for the cylinder (see
[
Szn09a
]
); we will comment further on these questions in Section
1.6
.

The core heuristical argument for these couplings is that after reaching some subset
A\subset\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}
, the random walk
S
will realize a set of excursions, until leaving
A
for a time larger than the mixing time of the walk. The eventual return of
S
in
A
will result in a second set of excursions which is thus mostly independent of the previous set, and so on until time
uN^{d}
. The number of such sets of excursions is then well-approximated by a Poisson random variable. Seeing
A
as a set in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, it can be shown that the trace
\mathscr{I}(u)\cap A
of the RI on
A
, has exactly the law of this collection of sets of excursions.

This core argument is not exactly specific to the SRW on the torus, and one may investigate any situation which involves the random walk visiting a large set for a long time, to see if the range exhibits this “interlacing” property.

In this paper, we consider the random walk conditioned to stay in a large domain
D_{N}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. After heuristical considerations involving this “interlacing” property, we construct a coupling of the random walk range with a random interlacements that very much resembles
\mathscr{I}(u)
. This coupling is constructed using the same ideas as in
[
ČT16
]
, however a major difference lies in the fact that the walk conditioned to stay in
D_{N}
behaves as a random walk in a time-dependent potential. This breaks the “Markovianity” of the walk and implies that we cannot consider standard random interlacements since they do not take the drift into account.

In the next two sections, we explain how we can circumvent both of these issues. First, by considering an “infinite” time horizon for the walk we remove the time dependence of the potential. Second, instead of considering a Poissonnian collection of independent SRW trajectories for interlacements, we use walks on suitable conductances that coincide with the confining potential.

1.2
Simple random walk conditioned to stay in a large domain, confined walk

1.2.1
Motivations and first notations

Let
S
be the simple nearest-neighbour random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d},d\geq 3
and denote by
\mathbf{P}_{x}
its law starting at
x
(with
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{P}_{0}
).
Fix a compact set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
which contains
0
and which we assume to have a smooth boundary and a nonempty, connected interior. Let
N\geq 1
and define
D_{N}\vcentcolon=(ND)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
the discrete blowup of
D
with factor
N
.

In the rest of the paper, we fix
x_{0}\in\mathrm{Int}(D)
and we let
\alpha>0
and
\varepsilon>0
small enough (see below Proposition
2.4
) such that for all
N
large enough, the discrete Euclidean ball of radius
(\alpha+2\varepsilon)N
centered at
x_{0}^{N}=x_{0}N
is contained in
D_{N}
.
We define

B_{N}=B(x_{0}^{N},\alpha N)=\left\{y\in D_{N}\,|\,d(x_{0}^{N},y)\leq\alpha N\right\}\,,

(1.2)

where
d
is the Euclidean distance. For
\delta\in(0,2\varepsilon)
, we will write
B_{N}^{\delta}=B(x_{0}^{N},(\alpha+\delta)N)\subseteq D_{N}
.

Figure 1
:
Representation of
D_{N}
and
B_{N}
with its
\varepsilon N
-neighborhood, and a random walk path conditioned to stay in
D_{N}
.

Our motivation for this paper is to show that the range
\mathcal{R}(t_{N})=\{S_{0},\ldots,S_{t_{N}}\}
of the SRW, under the conditioned probability
\mathbf{P}(\cdot\,|\,\mathcal{R}(t_{N})\subseteq D_{N})
, can be coupled with a proper random interlacements at a well-chosen intensity
u_{N}=u_{N}(t_{N})
so that they coincide on
B_{N}
.
We will prove this under some condition on the sequence
(t_{N})_{N\geq 0}
, but let us stress already that this will include cases where
u_{N}\to 0
, that is cases of RI with very low intensity.

Comment 1.1
.

We restrict ourselves to get a coupling on a ball
B_{N}
mostly for simplicity of the exposition — some estimates for the random walk are simplified in this case.

However, our results remain valid if we replace
B_{N}
with any set that is “
sN
-regular” for some
s>0
, in the sense of
[
PT15
, Def. 8.1]
: there is a
s>0
such that for any
N
large enough, for any point
x\in\partial B_{N}
, there are balls
B^{\mathrm{in}}\subseteq B_{N}
,
B^{\mathrm{out}}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus B_{N}\cup\partial B_{N}
of radius
sN
that are both tangent to
B_{N}
at point
x
. We will comment on this along the proofs.
Note also that
B_{N}
is macroscopic, and may be arbitrarily close to
D_{N}
, for example if
D
is a ball, or if
D
is well approximated by “
s
-regular” sets (meaning there is a subset
D^{s}
of
D
, close to
D
in Hausdorff distance, that is “
s
-regular” in the sense of tangent balls of radius
s
explained previously).

As previously explained, the finite time horizon of the conditioning poses substantial technical issues in its study by breaking the Markov property of the random walk. It should however be noted that if
t_{N}\gg N^{2}
, this induced “defect” in the Markov property is only relevant near time
t_{N}
. We therefore study a Markov chain that we argue properly approximates the conditioned walk up until time
(1-\eta)t_{N}
for some
\eta\in(0,1)
. This process is the infinite time-horizon version of the conditioned walk, which we call the
confined walk
.

1.2.2
The main object of study: the confined random walk

The main difference between the SRW on the torus and the SRW on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
conditioned to stay in
D_{N}
is that the conditioning creates a drift that repels the walk away from the boundary
\partial D_{N}\vcentcolon=\{y\notin D_{N},\,\exists x\in D_{N}\text{ with }x\sim y\}
. In order to manage this space-time drift, we replace the random walk with another Markov chain that is morally the SRW conditioned to stay in
D_{N}

forever
. Fix
N\geq 1
and consider the substochastic matrix
P_{N}
of the SRW killed when exiting
D_{N}
, given by

P_{N}(i,j)=\frac{1}{2d}\quad\text{if $i,j\in D_{N}$ and $i\sim j$}\,,\quad 0\quad\text{else}\,.

Write
\lambda_{N}
and
\phi_{N}
for the first eigenvalue and associated eigenvector of
P_{N}
, with the following normalization

P_{N}\phi_{N}=\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}\,,\quad\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}\vcentcolon=\sum_{z\in D_{N}}\phi_{N}^{2}(z)=N^{d}\,.

(1.3)

We stress that the normalization is not fundamental to our result: the main requirement is that it implies that
\phi_{N}
is of order one in the bulk of
D_{N}
, which is the case with our normalization (see (
2.14
) below). In the paper, we will write
\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}
without replacing it by
N^{d}
to keep this in mind.

Note that
\phi_{N}
is defined on
D_{N}
, but it might be convenient to extend it to
\partial D_{N}
(and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
) by setting
\phi_{N}(x)=0
for
x\notin D_{N}
. This first eigenvector will play a crucial role in the present paper, and we will be interested in its properties as a function
x\mapsto\phi_{N}(x)
. We provide a quick summary of some of these properties below and in Appendix
B
, summarizing results obtained in
[
BB24
]
.

Let us introduce the following notation: for
h:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
a real-valued function on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we define

\bar{h}(z)\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{|e|=1}h(z+e)\,,\quad\Delta_{d}h(z)\vcentcolon=\bar{h}(z)-h(z)\,.

(1.4)

This way, one can rewrite (
1.3
) as the Dirichlet problem
\Delta_{d}\phi_{N}=(1-\lambda_{N})\phi_{N}
, with boundary condition
\phi_{N}\equiv 0
on
\partial D_{N}
.

We then define the transition kernel on
D_{N}\times D_{N}
, which is a Doob’s transform of the initial SRW:

p_{N}(x,y)\vcentcolon=\frac{\lambda_{N}^{-1}}{2d}\frac{\phi_{N}(y)}{\phi_{N}(x)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x\sim y\right\}}\,.

(1.5)

We write
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\mu}
for the law of the associated Markov chain with starting distribution
\mu
; we will also write
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}
when
\mu=\delta_{x}
and
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}
when
\mu=c_{N}\phi_{N}^{2}(\cdot)
with the correct normalizing constant
c_{N}
.
We will refer to this Markov chain as the
confined random walk
(confined RW) and usually denote it by
X
.

It is known, by standard Markov chain theory (see e.g.
[
LL10
, Appendix A.4.1]
), that the transition kernel (
1.5
) is in some sense the limit, as
T\to+\infty
, of the transition kernels of the SRW conditioned to stay in
D_{N}
until time
T
. Indeed, if
\tau_{N}
is the first time the SRW exits
D_{N}
and
x\sim y
, we have

\frac{\mathbf{P}_{x}(X_{1}=y,\tau_{N}>T)}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{N}>T)}=\frac{1}{2d}\frac{\mathbf{P}_{y}(\tau_{N}>T-1)}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{N}>T)}=\frac{\lambda_{N}^{-1}}{2d}\frac{\lambda_{N}\mathbf{P}_{y}(\tau_{N}>T-1)}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{N}>T)}\xrightarrow[T\to+\infty]{}\frac{\lambda_{N}^{-1}}{2d}\frac{\phi_{N}(y)}{\phi_{N}(x)}\,,

the last limit being a consequence of the fact that
\mathbf{P}_{z}(\tau_{N}>T)=\phi_{N}(z)\frac{\|\phi_{N}\|_{1}}{N^{2d}}(\lambda_{N})^{T}+\bar{\mathcal{O}}((\beta_{N})^{T})
as
T\to\infty
, for some
\beta_{N}<\lambda_{N}
(see
[
LL10
, Prop. 6.9.1]
).

Comment 1.2
.

Let us stress here that the transition kernel from (
1.5
) is that of a random walk among conductances
c_{N}(x,y)\vcentcolon=\phi_{N}(x)\phi_{N}(y)
,
i.e.
we can rewrite
p_{N}(x,y)
as

p_{N}(x,y)=\frac{c_{N}(x,y)}{\sum_{z\sim x}c_{N}(x,z)}=\frac{\phi_{N}(y)}{\sum_{z\sim x}\phi_{N}(z)}=\frac{1}{2d}\frac{\phi_{N}(y)}{\bar{\phi}_{N}(x)},\quad\text{ for }x,y\in D_{N},\,x\sim y\,.

(1.6)

Indeed, since
\phi_{N}
is an eigenfunction of
P_{N}
, we have that for any
x\in D_{N}

\bar{\phi}_{N}(x)=\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{z\sim x}\phi_{N}(z)=P_{N}\phi_{N}(x)=\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}(x)\,.

(1.7)

(Recall that we set
\phi_{N}(z)=0
for
z\notin D_{N}
so the sum is only on
z\in D_{N}
.)
Therefore, (
1.6
) coincides with (
1.5
).

In the rest of the paper, we will focus on the confined RW
since it will provide a good description of most of the range of SRW conditioned to stay in
D_{N}
. Indeed, under
\mathbf{P}(\cdot\,|\,\mathcal{R}_{t_{N}}\subseteq D_{N})
, the SRW before time
(1-\eta)t_{N}
should “look like” the confined RW; put otherwise, it should not feel the fact that the time horizon of the conditioning is finite.

Comment 1.3
.

The confined random walk under
\mathbf{P}^{N}
has a stationary occupation measure given by
\phi_{N}^{2}
, with proper normalisation. Therefore, the interlacements that we consider must exhibit a space-inhomogeneous density of trajectories, notably on
B
where it should be given by
\phi_{N}^{2}
. Such interlacements are called
tilted random interlacements
(tilted RI) and were introduced in
[
Tei09
,
LS14
]
.
They correspond to a Poisson point process of trajectories, with trajectories that are not SRW but rather a
tilted
RW, which creates the spatial inhomogeneity (see Section
1.3.2
for details).

1.3
Some preliminaries on random interlacements

1.3.1
Definition of random interlacements

Consider the space of doubly infinite transient paths on the
d
-dimensional lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d},d\geq 3
:

W=\Big\{w:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,:\,\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},|w(n)-w(n+1)|_{1}=1\text{ and }\lim_{|n|\to\infty}|w(n)|=\infty\Big\}\,,

endowed with the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{W}
generated by the maps
w\mapsto w(n),n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We can define the equivalence relation
w\sim w^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow\exists k\in\mathbb{Z},w(\cdot+k)=w^{\prime}
, and we write
W^{*}\vcentcolon=W/\sim
the corresponding quotient space as well as
\pi^{*}
the associated canonical projection. The set
W^{*}
is endowed with the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{W}^{*}
generated by
\pi^{*}
.

Let
K
be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. We define for
w\in W
the hitting times

H_{K}(w)=\inf\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\,:\,w(k)\in K\right\}\,,\quad\bar{H}_{K}=\inf\left\{k\geq 1\,:\,w(k)\in K\right\}

(1.8)

with
\inf\varnothing=+\infty
by convention.
We also define
W_{K}=W\cap\left\{H_{K}<+\infty\right\}
the set of trajectories that hit
K
.

Suppose that
K
is finite. We define the equilibrium measure
e_{K}
on
K
and the harmonic measure
\bar{e}_{K}
as

e_{K}(x)=\mathbf{P}_{x}(\bar{H}_{K}=+\infty)\,,\quad\bar{e}_{K}(x)=\frac{e_{K}(x)}{\mathrm{cap}\left[K\right]}\,.

(1.9)

Here,
\mathrm{cap}\left[K\right]is
the
capacity
of the set
K
, given by

\mathrm{cap}\left[K\right]=e_{K}(K)=\sum_{x\in\partial K}e_{K}(x)\,,

where we have used that the measure
e_{K}
is supported on the (inner) boundary of
K
, which we denote by
\partial K=\{x\in K\,:\,\exists y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus K,x\sim y\}
.

Intuitively,
\bar{e}_{K}
is the law of the first entrance point in
K
of a random walk trajectory that is coming from far away. Indeed, we have
\mathbf{P}_{z}(X_{H_{K}}=x\,|\,H_{K}<+\infty)\to\bar{e}_{K}(x)
as
|z|\to+\infty
, and the capacity can be alternatively written as

\mathrm{cap}\left[K\right]=\lim_{|z|\to+\infty}G(z)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{z}(H_{K}<+\infty)\,,\quad\text{with $G$ the SRW Green function}\,,

and can be interpreted as the « size » of
K
seen from a random walk starting at a faraway point on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Recall that
G(z)\asymp|z|^{2-d}
, where
|\cdot|
is the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see
[
LL10
, Thm. 4.3.1]
).

Now, let
w^{*}\in W^{*}_{K}
be a class of paths hitting
K
, and denote by
\tilde{w}
the unique
w\in w^{*}
such that
H_{K}(w)=0
. For
K
finite, we can define on
\pi^{*}(W_{K})
a finite measure
\nu_{K}
given by

\nu_{K}(w^{*})=\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{w}(0)}(w(\mathbb{Z}_{-})\,|\,\bar{H}_{K}=+\infty)\times e_{K}(w(0))\times\mathbf{P}_{\tilde{w}(0)}(w(\mathbb{Z}_{+}))\,,

(1.10)

which can be interpreted, after normalization, as the law of a SRW trajectory that hits
K
. The measures
\nu_{K}
for
K
finite can be extended to a
\sigma
-finite measure
\nu
on
W^{*}
.

The random interlacements process is a Poisson Point Process
\chi
on the space
W^{*}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with intensity
\nu\otimes du
. The random interlacement of level
u>0
is the subset
\mathscr{I}(u)\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
defined as

\mathscr{I}(u)\vcentcolon=\big\{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,:\,\exists(w,v)\in\chi,v\leq u,z\in w(\mathbb{Z})\big\}=\big\{w(k)\,:\,k\in\mathbb{Z},(w,v)\in\chi,v\leq u\big\}\,.

(1.11)

We denote by
\mathbb{P}
the law of the RI on the space
\mathcal{M}_{p}(W^{*}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
of point measures on
W^{*}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, which we also use to denote the law of the (increasing) family of random subsets
(\mathscr{I}(u))_{u>0}
.

A key property of RI is that its trace in a finite set
K
can be recovered from a collection of SRW trajectories — this is a core component of the coupling. More precisely, let
N_{K}^{u}
be a Poisson-distributed random variable with parameter
u\,\mathrm{cap}\left[K\right]
and let
(X^{(i)})_{i\geq 1}
be i.i.d. SRW trajectories with common law
\mathbf{P}_{\bar{e}_{K}}
, independent from
N_{K}^{u}
. Then we have the equality in law

\mathscr{I}(u)\cap K\;\overset{(d)}{=}\;\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{K}^{u}}\mathcal{R}_{\infty}(X^{(i)})\cap K\,.

(1.12)

In particular, we deduce the following crucial identity, which characterizes the law of
\mathscr{I}(u)
as a random subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(see
[
Szn10
, Remark 2.2-(2)]
): for any fixed
u>0
and any finite set
K\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\mathbb{P}\big(\mathscr{I}(u)\cap K=\varnothing\big)=e^{-u\,\mathrm{cap}\left[K\right]}\,.

1.3.2
Tilting of random interlacements

The tilted random interlacements were first introduced in
[
Tei09
]
as a generalization of random interlacement on transient weighted graphs.
Later,
[
LS14
]
defined the tilting of continuous-time random interlacements as a way to locally modify the trajectories of the RI.
It was used in particular to get large deviation principles for disconnection events, by locally densifying the RI. We refer to subsequent works of Sznitmann
[
Szn17
,
Szn19
,
Szn23
]
for the use of tilted interlacements to study large deviation events for interlacements such as disconnection, covering of sets or excess-type events, with some applications to simple random walk.

We choose to present here a reformulation of the first approach of tilted interlacements by
[
Tei09
]
, with a touch of local tilting of interlacement present in
[
LS14
]
.

Consider a positive function
h:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
which satisfies
h=1
outside a finite set. Informally, the
h
-tilted random interlacements of level
u>0
, denoted by
\mathscr{I}_{h}(u)
, is a Poisson cloud of
h
-tilted random walk trajectories,
i.e.
random walks on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
equipped with conductances
c(x,y)=h(x)h(y)
.

More precisely, we let
\mathbf{P}^{h}_{z}
denote the law of the random walk on conductances
c(x,y)=h(x)h(y)
starting at
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Consider a finite set
K\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then, we can define the
h
-tilted equilibrium measure of
K
by

e^{h}_{K}(z)\vcentcolon=\mathbf{P}^{h}_{z}\Big(\bar{H}_{K}=+\infty\Big)h(z)\bar{h}(z)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{z\in K\right\}}\,.

(1.13)

The measure
e^{h}_{K}
has finite mass and is supported on
\partial K
with total mass
\mathrm{cap}^{h}(K)
, the tilted capacity of
K
. We write
\bar{e}^{h}_{K}\vcentcolon=e^{h}_{K}/\mathrm{cap}^{h}(K)
for the corresponding (tilted harmonic) probability measure. We can then define the analogues
(\nu^{h}_{K})
of the measures
(\nu_{K})
for finite
K\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and their extension
\nu^{h}
on
W^{*}
.

The
h
-tilted RI is then defined as a Poisson cloud of trajectories that is given by a Poisson point process on
W^{*}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with intensity measure
u\otimes\nu^{h}
. It satisfies the following property, analogous to (
1.12
): for any finite set
K
, denote by
N^{h,u}_{K}
a Poisson variable with parameter
u\,\mathrm{cap}^{h}(K)
and let
(X^{(i)})_{i\geq 1}
be i.i.d.
h
-tilted RW trajectories with law
\mathbf{P}^{h}_{\bar{e}_{K}^{h}}
. We then have

\mathscr{I}_{h}(u)\cap K\overset{(d)}{=}\bigg(\bigcup_{i=1}^{N^{h,u}_{K}}\mathcal{R}_{\infty}(X^{i})\bigg)\cap K\,.

(1.14)

Comment 1.4
.

Contrary to
[
Tei09
]
, the continuous-time tilted interlacements at level
u>0
, introduced in
[
LS14
]
, was instead defined as an exponential tilting of the law of the continuous-time RI at level
u
. To do so, define the function
F:W^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
, as

\forall w\in W^{*},F(w^{*})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Delta_{d}h}{h}(\tilde{w}(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\,\,,

where we recall that
\tilde{w}
is defined just above (
1.10
).

The continuous-time tilted RI is the Poisson point process on
W^{*}
with intensity measure
e^{F}\nu
.
This tilted RI posess the interlacement property (
1.14
) (see
[
LS14
, Remark 2.2]
), with trajectories given by the Markov process with generator

Main result

Recall that

as well as the law

Note that on

Let us mention that it is known (see

In particular, there exists

(1.17)

Denote by

the range up to time

-tilted interlacement

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

1.5
Connection with other works

The confined and tilted walks appelations also appear in a paper by Li
[
Li17
]
studying disconnection events for simple random walk. The confined walk essentially designates the same process. The tilted walk is slightly different: in Li’s paper the drift is only active up to a deterministic time.
It can be noted that Li proved that the confined walk locally dominates (in stochastic sense) random interlacements.

Chiarini & Nietzchner
[
CN23
]
also studied excess type events for functionals of the SRW, which also involves tilted interlacements to find a lower bound on the probability of such events. The strategy to achieve these events is to behave like a random walk in a drift, much like the drift that we use for the tilted walk. Their proof used a series of coupling on mesocopic boxes (of size
N^{\lambda},\lambda\in(0,1)
) between the tilted walk, simple random walk and standard random interlacements.

The coupling relies on the regularity of the drift function to control the effect of the drift at these mesocopic scales. In our case, we where able to prove the regularity of
\phi_{N}
(see Appendix
B
) which indicates that at such scales, we may replace the tilted RI by the standard RI. It seems natural to expect that the level
u_{N}
would now depend on the average value of
\phi_{N}
on this mesocopic box. Note that the regularity of
\phi_{N}
is offscaled by the number of excursions of
\mathscr{I}_{\phi_{N}}(u_{N})
inside
B_{N}
, which grows as
u_{N}N^{d-2}\asymp t_{N}N^{-2}\to+\infty
, therefore the proof of
[
CN23
]
does not apply in this context.

2.1
The soft local time coupling method

The soft local time method was introduced in
[
PT15
]
as a way to decorrelate distant regions of the random interlacements. It was later expanded in
[
ČT16
]
to couple the ranges of Markov processes on a finite state space
\Sigma
which have the same invariant measure. In this section, we briefly summarize the method and the main coupling theorem of
[
ČT16
]
, that will be the main tool for the coupling of the walk with the tilted interlacements.

The key idea of is to simulate the range up to time
n\geq 1
of a Markov chain with finite state space
\Sigma
by using a Poisson point process
\eta
on
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
The coupling then simply uses the same Poisson point process to simulate the ranges up to time
n
of two different Markov chains.
The construction of the range is done by choosing the right intensity measure on
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
and then exploring the points of
\eta
in the direction
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, while collecting the corresponding elements of
\Sigma
(the “explored sites”).
This exploration is constructed by means of the transition kernel of the Markov chain at each step, and it correctly builds up the range of the Markov chain up to time
n
. At this time
n
, the construction has explored a part of
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
delimited (on the right/from above) by a “line”
G_{n}:\Sigma\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
, which is called the soft local time at step
n\geq 1
.
The range is thus constituted by the first component of points
(z,v)\in\eta(\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that
v\leq G_{n}(z)
.

To summarize, to each Markov chain on
\Sigma
one can associate soft local times
(G_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, and the range up to time
n
is simply the set
\{z\in\Sigma,(z,v)\in\eta,G_{n}(z)\leq v\}
of “explored sites”. Using the same Poisson process
\eta
to generate ranges of two Markov chains therefore produces a coupling of the two ranges; having inequalities on soft local times then amounts to having inclusion relations for the ranges.
Let us now introduce the necessary elements to be able to state Theorem 3.2 in
[
ČT16
]
, which controls the error in the coupling.

Let
Y
and
Z
be two Markov chains with values in a finite space
\Sigma
, with invariant measure
\pi^{Y}
and
\pi^{Z}
respectively. Let us also consider a measure
\mu
with full support in
\Sigma
(that can be considered as a parameter one can play with). Writing “
\circ
” for either
Y
or
Z
, we define

g^{\circ}(z)\vcentcolon=\frac{\pi^{\circ}(z)}{\mu(z)}\quad,\quad\rho_{z}^{\circ}(w)\vcentcolon=\frac{p^{\circ}(w,z)}{\mu(z)}

(2.1)

where
p^{\circ}
is the transition kernel of the corresponding Markov chain.
Define the mixing times
T^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mix}}
as

T^{\circ}_{\mathrm{mix}}\vcentcolon=\inf\Big\{t\geq 0\,:\,\sup_{x\in\Sigma}\|p_{t}^{\circ}(x,\cdot)-\pi^{\circ}\|_{TV}\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\Big\}\,,

(2.2)

where
\|\cdot\|_{TV}
is the total variation distance, given by
\|\lambda-\nu\|_{TV}=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{z\in\Sigma}|\lambda(z)-\nu(z)|
.
For
\varepsilon>0
, let us define for any
n\geq 1
the following event on the ranges of
Y
and
Z
:

\mathcal{G}_{n,\varepsilon}\vcentcolon=\left\{\left\{Z_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{(1-\varepsilon)n}\subseteq\left\{Y_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq\left\{Z_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{(1+\varepsilon)n}\right\}\,.

(2.3)

Let us also introduce

k(\varepsilon)\vcentcolon=-\inf_{\circ=Y,Z}\inf_{z\in\Sigma}\log_{2}\frac{\pi^{\circ}_{*}\varepsilon^{2}g^{\circ}(z)^{2}}{6\mathrm{Var}_{\pi^{\circ}}(\rho^{\circ}_{z})}\,,

(2.4)

with
\pi^{\circ}_{*}\vcentcolon=\min_{z\in\Sigma}\pi^{\circ}(z)
, and the following condition on
\varepsilon>0
:

0<\varepsilon<\inf_{\circ=Y,Z}\inf_{z\in\Sigma}\frac{\mathrm{Var}_{\pi^{\circ}}(\rho^{\circ}_{z})}{2\|\rho^{\circ}_{z}\|_{\infty}g^{\circ}(z)}\,.

(2.5)

Theorem 2.1

(
[
ČT16
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

Let
Y,Z
be two Markov chains on a finite state space
\Sigma
, with transition matrices
P^{Y},P^{Z}
, starting distribution
\nu_{Y},\nu_{Z}
, and with the same invariant probability measure
\pi
.
Then there are constants
C,c>0
and there exists a (common) probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{Q})

such that, for any
\varepsilon>0
that satisfies (
2.5
), for any
n\geq 2k(\varepsilon)\min\{T^{Y}_{\mathrm{mix}},T^{Z}_{\mathrm{mix}}\}
, we have

1-\mathbb{Q}\left(\mathcal{G}_{n,\varepsilon}\right)\leq C\sum_{\circ=Y,Z}\sum_{z\in\Sigma}\left(e^{-cn\varepsilon^{2}}+e^{-cn\varepsilon\frac{\pi^{\circ}(z)}{\nu_{\circ}(z)}}+\exp\left(-c\frac{\varepsilon^{2}g^{\circ}(z)^{2}}{\mathrm{Var}_{\pi^{\circ}}(\rho^{\circ}_{z})}\frac{n}{k(\varepsilon)T_{\mathrm{mix}}^{\circ}}\right)\right)\,.

(2.6)

In this paper, we will couple Markov chains with close, but different invariant measures. With a similar proof, we can easily get the following coupling theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

Consider
\varepsilon>0
that satisfies (
2.5
) and assume that the invariant measures of
Y
and

satisfy the following:

\text{there is a $\delta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon)$ such that}\quad\sup_{z\in\Sigma}\bigg|\frac{\pi^{Y}(z)}{\pi^{Z}(z)}-1\bigg|<\delta\,.

(2.7)

Then, there is a coupling of

and

1-\mathbb{Q}\left(\mathcal{G}_{n,\varepsilon}\right)\leq C\sum_{\circ=Y,Z}\sum_{z\in\Sigma}\left(e^{-cn\varepsilon^{2}}+e^{-cn\varepsilon\frac{\pi^{\circ}(z)}{\nu_{\circ}(z)}}+\exp\left(-c\frac{\varepsilon^{2}g^{\circ}(z)^{2}}{\mathrm{Var}_{\pi^{\circ}}(\rho^{\circ}_{z})}\frac{n}{k(\varepsilon)T_{\mathrm{mix}}^{\circ}}\right)\right)\,.

Proof.

Recall that, using the soft local times method, the event

is equivalent to having
G_{(1-\varepsilon)n}^{Z}(z)\leq G_{n}^{Y}(z)\leq G_{(1+\varepsilon)n}^{Z}(z)
.
Following the proof of

(1-\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon)ng^{Y}(z)\leq G_{n}^{Y}(z)\leq(1+\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon)ng^{Y}(z)

(2.9)

(1-\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon)(1\pm\varepsilon)ng^{Z}(z)\leq G_{(1\pm\varepsilon)n}^{Z}(z)\leq(1+\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon)(1\pm\varepsilon)ng^{Z}(z)

(2.10)

both hold with high probability (that is one minus the right-hand side of (

Then, (

— it can easily be adapted to more general domains.
For
R>r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we will denote
A_{r}^{R}(x)
the annulus
\{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,:\,r\leq|z-x|\leq R\}=(B(x,R)\setminus B(x,r))\cup\partial B(x,r)
, and
A_{r}^{R}\vcentcolon=A_{r}^{R}(0)

Proposition 2.4
.

There are positive constants
c_{b},c_{a}>0
and
C>0
such that, for all

, for all sequences
a_{N}>b_{N}\to+\infty

\limsup_{N\to+\infty}\tfrac{a_{N}}{b_{N}}<+\infty

\sup_{z\in B_{a_{N}}}\mathbf{P}_{z}\left(S_{[0,T]}\subseteq B_{a_{N}}\right)\leq Ce^{-c_{b}\frac{T}{a_{N}^{2}}}\quad,\quad\sup_{z\in{B_{a_{N}}\setminus B_{b_{N}}}}\mathbf{P}_{z}\left(S_{[0,T]}\subseteq A_{b_{N}}^{a_{N}}\right)\leq Ce^{-c_{a}\frac{T}{(a_{N}-b_{N})^{2}}}\,.

We fix
\varepsilon>0
that appears in the beginning of Section
1.2
to have
c_{a}>\varepsilon^{2}c_{0}
. This choice may seem strange, since we introduced
\varepsilon
to create a buffer zone between
\partial D_{N}
which would imply that larger

is somewhat easier than small

. While it is true in this regard, simple RW estimates are easier for small

Proof.

The first inequality is a consequence of
[
LL10
, Corollary 6.9.5 & 6.9.6]
. For the second inequality, we split
[0,T]
into intervals of length
(a_{N}-b_{N})^{2}
. Then, for all
z\in A_{b_{N}}^{a_{N}}
,

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}_{z}\left(S_{[0,T]}\subseteq A_{b_{N}}^{a_{N}}\right)&\leq\mathbf{P}_{z}\left(\forall i\in\left\{1,\dots,\lfloor\tfrac{T}{(a_{N}-b_{N})^{2}}\rfloor\right\},S_{i(a_{N}-b_{N})^{2}}\in A_{b_{N}}^{a_{N}}\right)\\

(2.16)

Now, according to the local limit theorem

2.3
Some estimates on
\Psi_{N}
-tilted interlacements

We provide in this section a few useful estimates, in particular on the tilted harmonic measure and the tilted capacities appearing in the definition of the tilted RI. To lighten the notation, we write
B
for
B_{N}
.
Recall the definition (
1.13
) of the tilted equilibrium measure and capacity, as well as the fact that
\Psi_{N}\equiv\phi_{N}
on
B^{\varepsilon}
. For the
\Psi_{N}
-tilted RI, we have for any
K\subseteq B
and any
x\in\partial K
, since
\bar{\Psi}_{N}(x)=\lambda_{N}\Psi_{N}(x)
(recall (
1.7
)),

e_{K}^{\Psi_{N}}(x)=\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{K}=+\infty)\Psi_{N}(x)\bar{\Psi}_{N}(x)=\lambda_{N}\Psi_{N}^{2}(x)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{K}=+\infty)\,,

(2.17)

with total mass
\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K)
. The tilted harmonic measure is then
\bar{e}_{K}^{\Psi_{N}}=e_{K}^{\Psi_{N}}/\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(K)
. A useful identity is the so-called « last exit decomposition » that we prove in the following lemma.
We introduce the Green function of the tilted RW:

G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(X_{n}=y)\,.

Let us stress that this Green function is not symmetric in
x
and
y
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
K\subseteq B^{\varepsilon}
. We have for any
x\in B^{\varepsilon}
,

\frac{1}{\phi_{N}^{2}(x)}\sum_{y\in K}e_{K}^{\Psi_{N}}(y)\,G^{\Psi_{N}}(y,x)=\sum_{y\in K}\frac{1}{\phi_{N}^{2}(y)}e_{K}^{\Psi_{N}}(y)\,G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)=\lambda_{N}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{K}<+\infty)\,.

(2.18)

Proof.

Note that the first equality is simply given by the fact that the reversible measure is given by
\Psi_{N}^{2}=\phi_{N}^{2}
on
B
. For the second inequality, consider
x\in K
and define
L_{K}:=\max\{n\geq 0,X_{n}\in K\}
the last time the tilted RW is in
K
. Since the
\Psi_{N}
-tilted RW is transient, we have
L_{K}<+\infty

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}
-a.s.
Therefore,

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(H_{K}<+\infty)&=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{y\in K}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(L_{K}=n,X_{n}=y)\\
&=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{y\in K}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(X_{n}=y)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(\bar{H}_{K}=+\infty)\\
&=\sum_{y\in K}G^{\Psi_{N}}(x,y)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(\bar{H}_{K}=+\infty)\,.\end{split}

By (
2.17
), we have
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(\bar{H}_{K}=+\infty)=e_{K}^{\Psi_{N}}(y)/\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}^{2}(y)
for all
y\in K\subseteq B
, which proves the lemma.
∎

In the following, we also need some estimates on the
tilted
equilibrium measure and capacity of
B
, comparing them with their simple random walk analogues; the results use the fact that
B
is a ball (at distance
\varepsilon N
from
\partial D_{N}
), but could easily be extended to “smooth” domains whose capacity is comparable to that of a ball.

Lemma 2.6
.

There is a constant
c>0
such that for all
x\in\partial B

c\,N^{-(d-1)}\leq\bar{e}^{\Psi_{N}}_{B}(x)\leq c^{-1}N^{-(d-1)}\,,

meaning that the tilted harmonic measure
\bar{e}^{\Psi_{N}}_{B}(x)
is roughly uniform on
\partial B
.
Moreover, we have

cN^{d-2}\leq\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)\leq c^{-1}N^{d-2}\,.

(2.19)

In the course of the proof of Lemma
2.6
, we will use estimates on the probability for the confined/tilted RW to go from
B
to
\partial B^{\varepsilon}
and vice-versa. The following lemma shows that these probabilities are of the same order as for the SRW.

Lemma 2.7
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that for any
N
large enough, any
x\in\partial B
, any
y\in\partial B^{\varepsilon}
,

\frac{1}{CN}\leq\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}>H_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}})\leq\frac{C}{N}\quad,\quad\frac{1}{CN}\leq\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})\leq\frac{C}{N}\,.

(2.20)

Let us first proceed with the proof of Lemma
2.6
.

Proof of Lemma
2.6
.

Note that, by (
2.17
), we have

\bar{e}^{\Psi_{N}}_{B}(x)=\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)\Big/\sum_{z\in\partial B}\phi_{N}^{2}(z)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)\,.

Our main goal is to prove that there exist constants
c,C>0
, that do not depend on
N
or
x\in\partial B
, such that

\frac{c}{N}\leq\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)\leq\frac{C}{N}\,.

(2.21)

Then, thanks to Proposition
2.3
, this will give that
\frac{c\kappa_{2}^{2}}{C|\partial B|}\leq\bar{e}^{\Psi_{N}}_{B}(x)\leq\frac{C}{c\kappa_{2}^{2}|\partial B|}
and conclude the estimates on the harmonic measure.
As far as the tilted capacity is concerned, we immediately get from (
2.21
), recalling also (
2.17
), that

\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)=\sum_{x\in\partial B}e_{B}^{\Psi_{N}}(x)=\sum_{x\in\partial B}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)\asymp\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x\in\partial B}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\,.

Since
\phi_{N}(x)\in[\kappa_{2},\kappa_{2}^{-1}]
(recall (
2.14
)), we get the desired bounds on
\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)
. Note that (
2.19
) can also be deduced from Rayleigh’s monotonicity law (see
[
LPW17
, Theorem 9.12]
) combined with (
2.14
).

It therefore only remains to prove (
2.21
). Our proof consists in showing that
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)
is of the same order as
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}>H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})
, and then conclude using Lemma
2.7
.
We rely on the use of the Markov property at time
H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}}
,
which gives us the following starting point:

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}>H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})\inf_{z\in\partial B^{\varepsilon}}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)\leq\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)\leq\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}>H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})\,.

Then, thanks to Lemma
2.7
, we immediately get that

\frac{1}{CN}\inf_{z\in\partial B^{\varepsilon}}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)\leq\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)\leq\frac{C}{N}\,.

(2.22)

Hence, we now only need to show that there is a constant
c>0
such that, uniformly in
N
,
\inf_{z\in\partial B^{\varepsilon}}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)\geq c
. Fix
z\in\partial B^{\varepsilon}
, and consider a tilted RW trajectory starting at
z
. This trajectory may cross
\partial B^{\varepsilon}
several times before either going to
B
or leaving to infinity. If the ratio of the corresponding probabilities of these “final choice” is not too close to
0
or
1
, the two probabilities are bounded from both sides by constants in
(0,1)
.

We first rewrite
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)
as

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)=\frac{\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)}{\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}<+\infty)+\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)}=\left[1+\frac{\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}<+\infty)}{\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)}\right]^{-1}\,.

(2.23)

Let us denote

p_{1}\vcentcolon=\sup_{z\in\partial B^{\varepsilon}}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(H_{B}<\tau_{1}<+\infty)\,,\qquad q_{1}\vcentcolon=\inf_{z\in\partial B^{\varepsilon}}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}\big(\tau_{1}=+\infty\big)\,.

Then, by decomposing on the time before going to
B
and using the Markov property, we have

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}<+\infty)\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(X_{k}\in\partial B^{\varepsilon},k<H_{B})p_{1}\,.

(2.24)

In the same way, by decomposing on the last time the tilted RW visits
B^{\varepsilon}
and using the Markov property,

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)\geq\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(X_{k}\in\partial B^{\varepsilon},k<H_{B})q_{1}\,.

(2.25)

Combining (
2.24
) and (
2.25
) with (
2.23
), we deduce

\inf_{z\in\partial B^{\varepsilon}}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)\geq\frac{1}{1+\frac{p_{1}}{q_{1}}}\,.

(2.26)

It therefore remains to estimate
p_{1}
and
q_{1}
: we prove below that
p_{1}\leq c_{1}/N
while
q_{1}\geq c_{2}/N
, which gives the desired bound.

(i) Probability
p_{1}
.

According to Lemma
2.7
, an excursion that enters
B^{\varepsilon}
has a probability at most
\tfrac{c_{1}}{N}
of hitting
B
, regardless of its starting point. Hence, we have that
p_{1}\leq c_{1}/N
.

(ii) Probability
q_{1}
.

Consider
w\in\partial B^{\varepsilon}
as well as the slightly larger ball
B_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=B(x_{0}^{N},(\alpha+\varepsilon)N+4)
. Then, we have the lower bound

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(H_{B^{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial B^{\varepsilon}}=+\infty)\geq\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(\bar{H}_{B^{\varepsilon}}>H_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}})\inf_{v\in\partial B_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{v}(\bar{H}_{B_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}=+\infty)\,.

(2.27)

First observe that since
\forall z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\Psi_{N}(z)\in[\kappa_{2}\wedge 1,1\vee\tfrac{1}{\kappa_{2}}]
(see (
2.14
)), we have that
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(\bar{H}_{B^{\varepsilon}}>H_{B_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}})\geq c
for some constant
c>0
(it is enough to take
4
straight steps from
B^{\varepsilon}
to
B_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
).

On the other hand, since
\Psi_{N}\equiv 1
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus B^{\varepsilon}
, we have
\bar{\Psi}_{N}\equiv 1
on
\partial B_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\cup(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})
. Thus,

\inf_{v\in\partial B_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{v}(\bar{H}_{B_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}=+\infty)=\inf_{v\in\partial B_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\mathbf{P}_{v}(\bar{H}_{B_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}=+\infty)\geq\frac{c^{\prime}}{N}\,,

(2.28)

where we used
[
LL10
, Proposition 6.4.2]
for the second inequality.
Therefore, combining (
2.27
) and (
2.28
), there is a constant
c_{2}>0
that does not depend on
N\geq N_{0}
or
w\in\partial B^{\varepsilon}
such that
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(\bar{H}_{B^{\varepsilon}}=+\infty)\geq c_{2}/N
. In conclusion,
q_{1}\geq c_{2}/N
.

∎

Proof of Lemma
2.7
.

A key result in our proof is the following “gambler’s ruin” lemma, which can be extracted from Proposition 1.5.10 in
[
Law13
]
(we refer to Appendix
A
for details on how to get this statement).

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
z\in(B^{\varepsilon}\setminus B)\cup\partial B
and
w\in B^{\varepsilon}\setminus B
be such that there are
\iota,\jmath\in[0,1)
and
\eta>0
such that

|z-x_{0}^{N}|-\alpha N\in[\eta,\tfrac{1}{\eta}]N^{\iota}\quad,\quad|w-x_{0}^{N}|-(\alpha+\varepsilon)N\in-[\eta,\tfrac{1}{\eta}]N^{\jmath}\,.

Then, there exist explicit constants that depend only on
\alpha,\varepsilon,d
such that for
N
large enough,

\begin{split}c_{1}\tfrac{\eta}{2}N^{\iota-1}+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-1})\leq\,&\mathbf{P}_{z}(H_{B^{\varepsilon}}<\bar{H}_{B})\leq c_{2}\tfrac{2}{\eta}N^{\iota-1}+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-1})\,,\\
c_{1}^{\prime}\tfrac{\eta}{2}N^{\jmath-1}+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-1})\leq\,&\mathbf{P}_{w}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})\leq c_{2}^{\prime}\tfrac{2}{\eta}N^{\jmath-1}+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-1})\,.\end{split}

(2.29)

Then, for the lower bounds in Lemma
2.7
, we use (
2.15
) to get

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}>H_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}})\geq\kappa_{1}\mathbf{P}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}>H_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}})\,,\qquad\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})\geq\kappa_{1}\mathbf{P}_{y}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})\,,

then we simply apply (
2.29
) for
\iota=0
and
\jmath=0
.

Let us focus on the first upper bound in Lemma
2.7
.
Using (
2.15
) with
C=B^{\varepsilon}\setminus B
and
A=\left\{\bar{H}_{B}>H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}}\right\}
, we have for
x\in\partial B
,

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}>H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})\leq\frac{c}{N}+c\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{c_{0}k}\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(kN^{2}\leq H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}}<\bar{H}_{B}\right)\,,

(2.30)

where we have used (
2.29
) with
\iota=0
to bound the first term
\mathbf{P}_{x}(H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}}<\bar{H}_{B})
by
c/N
. We fix a
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
then bound each term in the sum by splitting on whether
\partial B^{\delta}
has been reached before time
\tfrac{k}{2}N^{2}
. If it is the case, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}}\wedge\bar{H}_{B}\geq kN^{2}\,,\,H_{\partial B^{\delta}}\leq\frac{k}{2}N^{2}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(H_{\partial B^{\delta}}<\bar{H}_{B}\right)\sup_{z\in\partial B^{\delta}}\mathbf{P}_{z}\left(H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}}\wedge\bar{H}_{B}>\frac{k}{2}N^{2}\right)\leq\frac{C}{\delta N}e^{-\frac{c_{a}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}k}\,,\end{split}

(2.31)

where for the last inequality we used
[
LL10
, Lem. 6.3.4]
to bound the first factor and Proposition
2.4
for the second factor.

In order to get a bound when the tilted RW does not reach
\partial B^{\delta}
before time
\tfrac{k}{2}N^{2}
, we first make the following claim on simple random walks (about “gambler’s ruin duration”), whose proof we postpone to Appendix
A
(again, the result seems standard but we did not find it in the literature).

Claim 2.9
.

For all
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
, there is a
c_{\delta}>0
such that

\sup_{z\in\partial B}\mathbf{P}_{z}\left(\bar{H}_{B}\wedge H_{\partial B^{\delta}}>(\delta N)^{2}\right)\leq\frac{c_{\delta}}{N}\,,\qquad\sup_{w\in\partial B^{\delta}}\mathbf{P}_{w}\left(H_{B}\wedge\bar{H}_{\partial B^{\delta}}>(\delta N)^{2}\right)\leq\frac{c_{\delta}}{N}\,.

Using the first inequality in Claim
2.9
and Proposition
2.4
, we get, for
x\in\partial B
,

\begin{split}&\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}}\wedge\bar{H}_{B}\geq kN^{2}\,,\,H_{\partial B^{\delta}}>\frac{k}{2}N^{2}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(\bar{H}_{B}\wedge H_{\partial B^{\delta}}>\frac{k}{2}N^{2}\right)\sup_{z\in B^{\delta}\setminus B}\mathbf{P}_{z}\left(H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}}\wedge\bar{H}_{B}>\frac{k}{2}N^{2}\right)\leq\frac{c}{N}e^{-c_{a}\frac{k}{2\varepsilon^{2}}}\,,\end{split}

(2.32)

where
c>0
only depends on
\delta
and the dimension. Assembling (
2.31
) and (

) with (

) yields

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}>H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})\leq\frac{c}{N}+\frac{c}{N}\sum_{k\geq 1}\exp\left(c_{0}k-\frac{k}{\varepsilon^{2}}c_{a}\right)\,,

and the last sum is finite thanks to our assumption

.
This proves the upper bound on
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}>H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})
for

.

The upper bound on
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})

2.9
.

3
Preliminary estimates for the coupling

The main tool to prove Theorem
1.1
will be the soft local time coupling Theorem
2.1
, applied to the Markov chain that is constituted by the entrance and exit points of excursions from
\partial B
to an exterior set
\Delta
at a given distance from
B
.

Recall that
B
is a discrete Euclidean ball centered at
x_{0}^{N}\in D_{N}
with radius
\alpha N
, that satisfies that
\partial B
is at distance at least
2\varepsilon N
from
\partial D_{N}
.
In Theorem
1.1
we fix
\delta>0
to have

t_{N}/N^{2+\delta}\to+\infty\quad,\quad\varepsilon_{N}=N^{-\delta/4}\to 0\,.

We also fix
\gamma
that satisfies
1-\frac{\delta}{4(d-2)}<\gamma<1
, as well as
\Delta
by

\Delta=\Delta_{N}=\left\{x\in D_{N}\,:\,d(x,B)>N^{\gamma}\right\}\,.

(3.1)

In all the following, constants will be usually denoted by
C,c,c^{\prime}
and will always only depend on
D,\alpha,\varepsilon,\delta,d
and
\gamma
.

For reasons that will become clear later, we define the boundary of
\Delta
as only the points of the actual boundary that are « truly » inside
D_{N}
, that is
\partial\Delta\vcentcolon=\left\{x\in\Delta\,:\,\exists y\in\Delta^{c}\cap D_{N},x\sim y\right\}
. In another word,
\partial\Delta=\partial B(x_{0}^{N},\alpha N+N^{\gamma})
.

3.1
Notation

We will couple the entrance and exit points of confined RW and tilted RI random walk excursions from
B
to
\partial\Delta
, so we need to introduce some notation for both these objects.
For convenience, we adopt similar notation as in
[
ČT16
]
.

First, consider the confined RW, with transitions given by
p_{N}
. We define the set of hitting times
\mathcal{R}\vcentcolon=\left\{R_{i}\right\}_{i\geq 1}
of
B
and exit time
\mathcal{D}\vcentcolon=\left\{D_{i}\right\}_{i\geq 0}
on
\partial\Delta
as
D_{0}=H_{\Delta}
and for
i\geq 1
,

R_{i}\vcentcolon=H_{B}\circ\theta_{D_{i-1}}+D_{i-1}\,,\quad D_{i}=H_{\Delta}\circ\theta_{R_{i}}+R_{i}\,,

(3.2)

with
\theta_{t}
the time-shift operator at time
t
.
The process
Y_{i}\vcentcolon=(X_{R_{i}},X_{D_{i}})
for
i\geq 1
is a Markov chain on
\Sigma:=\partial B\times\partial\Delta
,
with transition probabilities

p^{Y}((z,w),(x,y))=\mathbf{P}_{x}^{N}(Y_{i+1}=(x,y)\,|\,Y_{i}=(z,w))=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{w}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\mathbf{P}_{x}^{N}(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y)\,,

(3.3)

and starting distribution, under
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}
,

\nu_{Y}(x,y)\vcentcolon=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}(Y_{1}=(x,y))=\frac{1}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}\sum_{z\in D_{N}}\phi_{N}^{2}(z)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{z}(X_{R_{1}}=x)\times\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y)\,.

(3.4)

On the other hand, we want to create a Markov chain
Z
which will contain the information about the tilted interlacement
\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})
restricted to
B
.
To this end, recall (
1.14
):
\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})\cap B
can be decomposed into independent trajectories
\left\{w^{j};j\geq 0\right\}
with law
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{\bar{e}_{B}^{\Psi_{N}}}
.

Since there is an almost surely finite number
N_{B}^{\Psi_{N},u_{N}}
of trajectories
(w^{j})_{j\geq 1}
that touch
B
we can order them by their label
(u_{j})_{j\geq 1}
.
To each trajectory
w^{j}
correspond sets
\mathcal{R}^{j}
and
\mathcal{D}^{j}
which collect the entrance and exit times of
w^{j}
as in (
3.2
):

D^{j}_{0}=H_{\Delta}(w^{j})
and for
i\geq 1
,

R^{j}_{i}\vcentcolon=H_{B}(w^{j})\circ\theta_{D^{j}_{i-1}}+D^{j}_{i-1}\,,\quad D^{j}_{i}=H_{\Delta}(w^{j})\circ\theta_{R^{j}_{i}}+R^{j}_{i}\,.

(3.5)

Write
T^{j}=|\mathcal{R}^{j}|=|\mathcal{D}^{j}|-1
for the number of excursions
(w^{j}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq T^{j}}
of the
j
-th trajectory. We can define for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
the random variable

Z_{k}=(w^{j}_{i}(0),w^{j}_{i}(H_{\Delta}))\,,\quad\text{with}\quad k=i+\sum_{p=1}^{j-1}T^{p}\,.

(3.6)

The resulting process
Z=(Z_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is a Markov chain on
\Sigma
with transition probabilities

p^{Z}((z,w),(x,y))=\left(\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(H_{B}<+\infty,X_{H_{B}}=x)+\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(H_{B}=+\infty)\bar{e}^{\Psi_{N}}_{B}(x)\right)\mathbf{P}_{x}^{N}(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y)\,,

(3.7)

and starting distribution

\nu_{Z}(x,y)=\bar{e}^{\Psi_{N}}_{B}(x)\mathbf{P}_{x}^{N}(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y)\,.

(3.8)

w^{j},w^{j^{\prime}}
of the tilted RI (right).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
then mostly consists of applying Theorem
2.1
to the Markov chains
Y
and
Z
. To do so, we need the following four results:

—

In Section
3.2
, we work out the invariant measures of the Markov chains
Y
and
Z
, and we control their difference in order to apply Theorem
2.2
.

—

We prove bounds on the mixing times (Section
3.3
) and variances (Section
3.4
) that appear in the upper bound (
2.8
) of the theorem.

After that, we estimate in Section
4.1
the required size of the range of
Y
and
Z
, i.e. the number of excursions of the confined RW before time
t_{N}
and of the tilted RI
\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})
. This will give us the time
n
in Theorem
2.1
.

Definition 3.1
.

We define the
(\phi_{N},\Delta)
-equilibrium measure
e_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}
on
\partial B
and its total mass
\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)
by

e_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(x)=\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B})\mathbbm{1}_{\partial B}(x)\quad,\quad\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)=\sum_{x\in\partial B}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B})\,.

(3.9)

We write
\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}
for the probability measure
e_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}/\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)
.

3.2
Invariant measure

As is explained in Section
2.1
, the main requirement to couple the two Markov chains
Y
and
Z
is for them to have the same invariant measure. In this section, we prove this statement and give some related estimates that we will use further in the paper.

Lemma 3.1
.

The Markov chain
Y
admits an invariant probability
\tilde{\pi}_{Y}
on
\partial B\times\partial\Delta
given by

\tilde{\pi}_{Y}(x,y)\vcentcolon=\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(x)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y)\,.

(3.10)

Comment 3.1
.

Using (
2.12
), we can see that for
x\in\partial B
,

e_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(x)=\phi_{N}(x)\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[\phi_{N}(X_{H_{\Delta}})\lambda_{N}^{-H_{\Delta}}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B}\right\}}\right]

and

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y)=\frac{\phi_{N}(y)}{\phi_{N}(x)}\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[\lambda_{N}^{-H_{\Delta}}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{X_{H_{\Delta}}=y\right\}}\right]\,,

so that

\tilde{\pi}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[\phi_{N}(X_{H_{\Delta}})\lambda_{N}^{-H_{\Delta}}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B}\right\}}\right]\phi_{N}(y)\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[\lambda_{N}^{-H_{\Delta}}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{X_{H_{\Delta}}=y\right\}}\right]\,.

Proof.

Consider the stationary random walk
(X_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
under
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}
. With the definition of the return set
\mathcal{R}
, by a last-passage decomposition, we have

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}(n\in\mathcal{R},X_{n}=x)=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}(\exists w\in\partial\Delta,\exists m\geq 1,X_{n-m}=w,X_{[n-m+1,n-1]}\subseteq(B\cup\Delta)^{c},X_{n}=x)\,,

where we have introduced the notation
X_{[a,b]}=\{X_{a},X_{a+1},\ldots,X_{b}\}
for
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Therefore, decomposing on every
m\geq 1
and
w\in\partial\Delta
, since the events are disjoint we obtain

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}(n\in\mathcal{R},X_{n}=x)=\sum_{w\in\partial\Delta}\sum_{m\geq 1}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}(X_{n-m}=w,X_{[n-m+1,n-1]}\subseteq(B\cup\Delta)^{c},X_{n}=x)\,.

(3.11)

Since
\phi_{N}^{2}
is the stationary measure of
(X_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}(X_{n-m}=w,X_{[n-m+1,n-1]}\subseteq(B\cup\Delta)^{c},X_{n}=x)=\frac{\phi_{N}^{2}(w)}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{w}(X_{[1,m-1]}\subseteq(B\cup\Delta)^{c},X_{m}=x)\,.

(3.12)

Moreover, since
\phi_{N}^{2}
is a reversible measure for the confined RW (recall that random walks on conductances are reversible),

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{w}(X_{[1,m-1]}\subseteq(B\cup\Delta)^{c},X_{m}=x)=\frac{\phi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\phi_{N}^{2}(w)}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(X_{[1,m-1]}\subseteq(B\cup\Delta)^{c},X_{m}=w)\,.

(3.13)

Therefore, injecting (
3.13
) in (
3.12
) and then in (
3.11
), we get

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}(n\in\mathcal{R},X_{n}=x)&=\frac{\phi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}\sum_{w\in\partial\Delta}\sum_{m\geq 1}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(X_{[1,m-1]}\subseteq(B\cup\Delta)^{c},X_{m}=w)\\
&=\frac{\phi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(\bar{H}_{B}>H_{\Delta})\,,\end{split}

(3.14)

which does not depend on
n
and is proportional to
e_{B}^{\phi_{N}}(x)
.
Using the ergodic theorem,
\tilde{\pi}
satisfies

\tilde{\pi}_{Y}(\left\{x\right\}\times\partial\Delta)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{i\in\mathcal{R},X_{i}=x\right\}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{i\in\mathcal{R}\right\}}}=\frac{\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}(n\in\mathcal{R},X_{n}=x)}{\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}(n\in\mathcal{R})}\,,

(3.15)

which proves that
\tilde{\pi}_{Y}(\left\{x\right\}\times\partial\Delta)=\tilde{e}_{B}^{\phi_{N}}(x)
. Then, using the Markov property, we see that
\tilde{\pi}(x,y)=\tilde{\pi}_{Y}(\left\{x\right\}\times\partial\Delta)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y)
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The Markov chain
Z
admits the invariant measure

\tilde{\pi}_{Z}(x,y)=\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}\big(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B}<+\infty)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}\big(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y\big)\mathbbm{1}_{x\in\partial B}\,.

(3.16)

Moreover, there is a constant
c>0
such that for all
N
large enough,

\sup_{(x,y)\in\Sigma}\Big|\frac{\tilde{\pi}_{Y}(x,y)}{\tilde{\pi}_{Z}(x,y)}-1\Big|\leq cN^{\gamma-1}\,.

(3.17)

Proof.

Recall that
Z
is constructed thanks to a collection of i.i.d. random walks on conductances, whose trajectories are denoted
(w^{j})_{j\geq 1}
. Therefore, choosing
x\in\partial B
,
\pi_{Z}(\left\{x\right\}\times\partial\Delta)
can be expressed by the average number of visits to
x
between two consecutive visits to a reference site. Here, the reference site is a state
+\infty
added to
B
, which corresponds to count the average number of visits at
x\in\partial B
by a single trajectory
w^{j}
of the tilted RI.
We end up with

\tilde{\pi}_{Z}(\left\{x\right\}\times\partial\Delta)=\frac{1}{\mathbf{E}_{\bar{e}^{\Psi_{N}}_{B}}^{\Psi_{N}}\left[T^{1}\right]}\mathbf{E}_{\bar{e}^{\Psi_{N}}_{B}}^{\Psi_{N}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{T^{1}}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{X_{{R_{i}}}=x\right\}}\right]\,,

(3.18)

where we recall that
T^{1}
is the number of excursions of the first trajectory.

Then, as for the conclusion of the proof of Lemma
3.1
, by the Markov property we get that

\tilde{\pi}_{Z}(x,y)=\tilde{\pi}_{Z}(\left\{x\right\}\times\partial\Delta)\mathbf{P}_{x}^{\Psi_{N}}\left(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y\right)=\tilde{\pi}_{Z}(\left\{x\right\}\times\partial\Delta)\mathbf{P}_{x}^{N}\left(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y\right)\,,

since
\Psi_{N}=\phi_{N}
on
\Delta^{c}
.

Thus, we only need to prove that (
3.18
), or more precisely the second term on its right-hand side, is proportional to
e_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(x)
(normalizing constants are unimportant).
We first rewrite it using the definition of
R_{i}
: with a similar last-passage decomposition as in (
3.11
), the last expectation in (
3.18
) is equal to

\begin{split}\sum_{y\in B}&\bar{e}^{\Psi_{N}}_{B}(y)\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{w\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}_{y}^{\Psi_{N}}\Big(X_{k}=x,\exists j\geq 1,X_{k-j}=w,X_{[k-j+1,k-1]}\subseteq(B\cup\Delta)^{c}\Big)\\
&=\sum_{y\in B}\bar{e}^{\Psi_{N}}_{B}(y)\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{w\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}_{y}^{\Psi_{N}}\left(X_{k-j}=w\right)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}\left(X_{j}=x,X_{[1,j-1]}\subseteq(B\cup\Delta)^{c}\right)\,,\end{split}

(3.19)

where the last line follows by the Markov property.
Reversing both paths, similarly to (
3.13
) we have

\mathbf{P}_{y}^{\Psi_{N}}\left(X_{k-j}=w\right)=\frac{\Psi_{N}(w)^{2}}{\Psi_{N}(y)^{2}}\mathbf{P}_{w}^{\Psi_{N}}\left(X_{k-j}=y\right)

and

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}\left(X_{j}=x,X_{[1,j-1]}\subseteq(B\cup\Delta)^{c}\right)=\frac{\Psi_{N}(x)^{2}}{\Psi_{N}(w)^{2}}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}\left(X_{j}=w,X_{[1,j-1]}\subseteq(B\cup\Delta)^{c}\right)\,.

Hence, using also that
\Psi_{N}=\phi_{N}
on
\Delta^{c}\subset B^{\varepsilon}
(and Fubini’s theorem), (
3.19
) is proportional to

\begin{split}\sum_{j\geq 1}\sum_{w\in\partial\Delta}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}\big(X_{j}=w,X_{[1,j-1]}\subseteq(B\cup\Delta)^{c}\big)\sum_{y\in B}\sum_{k^{\prime}\geq 1}e^{\Psi_{N}}_{B}(y)\frac{1}{\phi_{N}^{2}(y)}\mathbf{P}_{w}^{\Psi_{N}}\left(X_{k^{\prime}}=y\right)&\\
=\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\sum_{w\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}\big(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B},X_{H_{\Delta}}=w\big)\sum_{y\in B}\frac{1}{\phi_{N}^{2}(y)}e^{\Psi_{N}}_{B}(y)G^{\Psi_{N}}(w,y)&\,.\end{split}

Using the last exit decomposition for the tilted walk (
2.18
), we get that

\begin{split}\mathbf{E}_{\bar{e}^{\Psi_{N}}_{B}}^{\Psi_{N}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{T^{1}}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{X_{{R_{i}}}=x\right\}}\right]&=\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\sum_{w\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}\big(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B},X_{H_{\Delta}}=w\big)\mathbf{P}_{w}\big(H_{B}<+\infty\big)\\
&=\lambda_{N}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{x}\left(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B}<+\infty\right)\,,\end{split}

(3.20)

where the last inequality stems from the strong Markov property.

To get the second part of the lemma, we rewrite (
3.20
) as

\tilde{\pi}_{Z}(x,y)=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}\left(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B}\right)-\sum_{w\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}\big(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B},X_{H_{\Delta}}=w\big)\mathbf{P}_{w}\big(H_{B}=+\infty\big)

(3.21)

Observe that a use of Lemma
2.10
yields that
\mathbf{P}_{w}\big(H_{B}=+\infty\big)\asymp N^{\gamma-1}
uniformly in
x\in\partial B,y\in\partial\Delta
, in particular

\frac{\tilde{\pi}_{Z}(x,y)}{\tilde{\pi}_{Y}(x,y)}-1\asymp\frac{1}{\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}\big(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B}\big)}\sum_{w\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}\big(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B},X_{H_{\Delta}}=w\big)N^{\gamma-1}=N^{\gamma-1}\,,

(3.22)

hence proving the second part of the lemma.
∎

In the rest of the paper, we will simply write
\tilde{\pi}=\tilde{\pi}_{Y}
, since according to (
3.17
)
\tilde{\pi}_{Z}
is close to
\tilde{\pi}_{Y}
.

To end this section, we provide estimates on
\tilde{\pi}
that will be useful later. Namely, we will need the probability under
\tilde{\pi}
that
y\in\partial\Delta
is an exit site, as well as estimates on
\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

There are universal constants
c,C>0
such that, uniformly in
y\in\partial\Delta
, we have

cN^{1-d}\leq\tilde{\pi}(\partial B\times\left\{y\right\})\leq CN^{1-d}\,.

(3.23)

Proof.

With the same method as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
(see e.g. (
3.14
)), we get, for
y\in\partial\Delta

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}&(X_{n}=y,n\in\mathcal{D})=\sum_{x\in\partial B}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\phi_{N}^{2}(x)}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(X_{[1,m-1]}\subseteq(B\cup\Delta)^{c},X_{m}=y)\\
&\qquad\qquad=\sum_{x\in\partial B}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\phi_{N}^{2}(y)}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(X_{[1,m-1]}\subseteq(B\cup\Delta)^{c},X_{m}=x)=\frac{\phi_{N}^{2}(y)}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta})\,,\end{split}

where we have reversed time in the second identity, similarly as in (
3.13
).
Now, thanks to the ergodic theorem, we obtain, similarly as in (
3.15
),

\tilde{\pi}(\partial B\times\left\{y\right\})=\frac{\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}(X_{n}=y,n\in\mathcal{D})}{\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}(n\in\mathcal{D})}=\frac{\phi_{N}^{2}(y)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta})}{\sum_{w\in\partial\Delta}\phi_{N}^{2}(w)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{w}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta})}\,.

We now estimate
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta})
uniformly in
y\in\partial\Delta
, with an easy adaptation of the proof of Lemma
2.7
and thanks to the small width of
(B\cup\Delta)^{c}
.
Applying (
2.15
) with the set
C=\Delta^{c}\setminus B
and the event
A=\big\{H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta}\big\}
, we have

\begin{split}\kappa_{1}\mathbf{P}_{y}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta})&\leq\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta})\\
&\qquad\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}\left[e^{c_{0}}\mathbf{P}_{y}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta})+\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}e^{c_{0}(k+1)}\mathbf{P}_{y}(kN^{2}\leq H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta})\right]\,.\end{split}

(3.24)

With Lemma
2.8
, see (
2.29
)), we have that
\mathbf{P}_{y}(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B})\asymp N^{-\gamma}
, uniformly in
y\in\partial\Delta
.
On the other hand, with Proposition
2.4
, we have
\mathbf{P}_{y}(kN^{2}\leq H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta})\leq e^{-c_{a}kN^{2(1-\gamma)}}
. Thus, the last sum in (
3.24
) is a
\bar{o}(N^{-\gamma})
and we have proved that
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta})\asymp N^{-\gamma}
, uniformly in
y\in\partial\Delta
. With Proposition
2.3
, this proves that

\tilde{\pi}(\partial B\times\left\{y\right\})=\frac{\phi_{N}^{2}(y)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta})}{\sum_{w\in\partial\Delta}\phi_{N}^{2}(w)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{w}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta})}\asymp\frac{\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta})}{\sum_{w\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{w}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\Delta})}\asymp\frac{N^{-\gamma}}{N^{d-1-\gamma}}=N^{1-d}\,,

again uniformly in
y\in\partial\Delta
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

There are universal constants
c,C>0
such that for all
x\in\partial B
,

cN^{1-d}\leq\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(x)\leq CN^{1-d}\quad,\quad cN^{d-1-\gamma}\leq\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)\leq CN^{d-1-\gamma}\,.

(3.25)

Proof.

Again, applying (
2.15
) with the set
C=\Delta^{c}\setminus B
and the event
A=\big\{\bar{H}_{\Delta}<H_{B}\big\}
, we have for
x\in\partial B

\begin{split}\kappa_{1}\mathbf{P}_{x}(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B})&\leq\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B})\\
&\qquad\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}\left[e^{c_{0}}\mathbf{P}_{x}(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B})+\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}e^{c_{0}(k+1)}\mathbf{P}_{x}(kN^{2}\leq H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B})\right]\,.\end{split}

(3.26)

Then, for the same reason as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, we have
\mathbf{P}_{x}(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B})\asymp N^{-\gamma}
and the last sum in (
3.26
) is a
\bar{o}(N^{-\gamma})
, thus showing that
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B})\asymp N^{-\gamma}
uniformly in
x\in\partial B
.
Combined with Proposition
2.3
, this proves that

\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(x)=\frac{\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B})}{\sum_{z\in\partial B}\phi_{N}^{2}(z)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{z}(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B})}\asymp\frac{N^{-\gamma}}{|\partial B|N^{-\gamma}}\asymp N^{1-d}\,.

On the other hand,

\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)=\sum_{x\in\partial B}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B})\asymp N^{-\gamma}\sum_{x\in\partial B}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\,,

(3.27)

and with (
2.14
) we have

\sum_{x\in\partial B}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\asymp|\partial B|\asymp N^{d-1}\,.

(3.28)

Combining (
3.27
) and (
3.28
), this concludes the lemma. ∎

3.3
Upper bound on mixing times

In this section, we prove bounds on the mixing times
T^{Y}_{\mathrm{mix}}
and
T^{Z}_{\mathrm{mix}}
that we will use in the upper bound of Theorem
2.1
. To bound the mixing times of a Markov chain
X
on
\Sigma
, we exhibit a coupling
\mathbb{Q}_{x,\tilde{x}}
of two copies
X
and
\tilde{X}
starting at
x
and
\tilde{x}
respectively, such that
X_{i}=\tilde{X}_{i}
implies
X_{j}=\tilde{X}_{j}
for all
j\geq i
. Then, we evaluate their coalescing time under
\mathbb{Q}_{x,\tilde{x}}
, which we use to get an upper bound on the mixing time
T_{\mathrm{mix}}
with

T_{\mathrm{mix}}\leq\inf\bigg\{k\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,\sup_{x,\tilde{x}\in\Sigma}\mathbb{Q}_{x,\tilde{x}}(X_{k}\neq\tilde{X}_{k})\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\bigg\}\,.

(3.29)

We refer to
[
LPW17
, Corollary 5.3]
for the proof of (
3.29
) and more details on coalescing couplings.
In the two following sections, we establish such coupling for the Markov chains
Y
and
Z
, and prove the following result.

Proposition 3.5
.

There is a constant
c>0
such that for all
N
large enough,

T^{Y}_{\mathrm{mix}}\,\vee\,T^{Z}_{\mathrm{mix}}\leq cN^{(1-\gamma)}\,.

(3.30)

3.3.1
Upper bound on the mixing time of the confined random walk

Consider the ball that is concentric with
B
with half radius:

G\vcentcolon=B(x_{0}^{N},\tfrac{\alpha}{2}N)=\Big\{z\in B\,:\,d(z,\partial B)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha N\Big\}\,.

Consider two copies
Y
and
\tilde{Y}
with the law of the Markov chain with transitions
p^{Y}
, and write
X
and
\tilde{X}
for their underlying confined RW. We couple
Y
and
\tilde{Y}
by making
X
and
\tilde{X}
go to
G
during the same
i
-th excursion (between times
R_{i}
and
D_{i}
). Then, trajectories will have a positive probability of being coupled before reaching again
\Delta
,
i.e.
before time
D_{i}
.

Step 1. Probability of reaching
G
.

First, we estimate the probability that a confined RW starting at a point
x\in\partial B
hits
G
before exiting through
\Delta
. This will help control the probability of the event where
X
and
\tilde{X}
touch
G
between
R_{i}
and
D_{i}
for the same
i\geq 1
.

Lemma 3.6
.

We have

\inf_{x\in\partial B}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(H_{G}<H_{\Delta})\geq cN^{\gamma-1}\,.

(3.31)

Proof.

This follows from
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(H_{G}<H_{\Delta})\geq\kappa_{1}\mathbf{P}_{x}(H_{G}<H_{\Delta})
(recall (
2.15
)) and Lemma
2.8
.
∎

With Lemma
3.6
, we see that reaching
G
before
\Delta
is a Bernoulli trial with success probability at least
cN^{\gamma-1}
.

Step 2. Probability of being coupled starting from
\partial G
.
For
y\in\partial\Delta
, we define

\mu_{N}(y)=\inf_{x\in\partial G}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y)\,,

which is a sub-probability measure on
\partial\Delta
. Starting at
z\in\partial G
, the law of
X_{H_{\Delta}}
can be written as
\nu_{z}+\mu_{N}
with
\nu_{z}\vcentcolon=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{z}(X_{H_{\Delta}}\in\cdot)-\mu_{N}
. Defining
W
a random variable with law
\mu_{N}/\mu_{N}(\partial\Delta)
, then our coupling will consist in choosing
X_{D_{i}}=\tilde{X}_{D_{i}}=W
with probability
\mu_{N}(\partial\Delta)
, while sampling
X_{D_{i}}
and
\tilde{X}_{D_{i}}
independently otherwise, according to the relevant exit point distributions.

We prove that
\mu_{N}(\partial\Delta)
is bounded from below by a constant, which means that at each simultaneous visit to
G
, there is a positive probability for the walks to coalesce on their way to
\Delta
.

Lemma 3.7
.

There is a
c_{\mu}\in(0,1)
, such that
\mu_{N}(\partial\Delta)\geq c_{\mu}
for all
N
large enough.

Proof.

With (
2.15
),

\mu_{N}(\partial\Delta)=\sum_{y\in\partial\Delta}\inf_{x\in\partial G}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y)\geq\kappa_{1}\sum_{y\in\partial\Delta}\inf_{x\in\partial G}\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y\right)\,.

Since
x\mapsto\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y\right)
is harmonic on
G
, by the Harnack inequality
[
LL10
, Theorem 6.3.9]
there is
c>0
that depends neither on
N
nor on
y
such that for any
x_{*}\in G
,
\inf_{x\in\partial G}\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y\right)\geq c\mathbf{P}_{x_{*}}\left(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y\right)
, which means that

\mu_{N}(\partial\Delta)=\sum_{y\in\partial\Delta}\inf_{x\in\partial G}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y)\geq c\kappa_{1}\sum_{y\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}_{x_{*}}\left(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y\right)=c\kappa_{1}\,.\qed

Conclusion.
Putting together Step 1 and Step 2, the coalescing time is dominated by a geometric random variable with success parameter at least
cc_{\mu}N^{\gamma-1}
, which gives the bound on
T^{Y}_{\mathrm{mix}}
.

Let us write the coupling in more detail.
For all
i\geq 1
, we define two independent sequences of i.i.d Bernoulli random variables:
(\xi^{1}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
with parameter
cN^{\gamma-1}
and
(\xi^{2}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
with parameter
c_{\mu}
. We couple
(Y_{i}=(X_{R_{i}},X_{D_{i}}))_{i\geq 0}
and
(\tilde{Y}_{i}=(\tilde{X}_{R_{i}},\tilde{X}_{D_{i}}))_{i\geq 0}
in the following way: if
X_{R_{i}}=\tilde{X}_{R_{i}}
, then
X_{D_{i}}=\tilde{X}_{D_{i}}
; if on the other hand
X_{R_{i}}\neq\tilde{X}_{R_{i}}
, then

—

If
\xi^{1}_{i}=0
, we independently sample
X_{D_{i}}\in\partial\Delta
and
\tilde{X}_{D_{i}}\in\partial\Delta
according to the respective probabilities
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{X_{R_{i}}}(X_{H_{\Delta}}\in\cdot)
and
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\tilde{X}_{R_{i}}}(X_{H_{\Delta}}\in\cdot)
.

—

If
\xi^{1}_{i}=1
, we independently sample
U_{i}\in\partial G
and
\tilde{U}_{i}\in\partial G
according to the respective probabilities
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{X_{R_{i}}}(X_{H_{G}}\in\cdot\,|\,H_{G}<H_{\Delta})
and
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{X_{R_{i}}}(X_{H_{G}}\in\cdot\,|\,H_{G}<H_{\Delta})
— this imposes that
X
and
\tilde{X}

both
reach
G
. Then:

—

If
\xi^{2}_{i}=0
, we independently sample
X_{D_{i}}
,
\tilde{X}_{D_{i}}
according to the respective probabilities
\bar{\nu}_{U_{i}}
,
\bar{\nu}_{\tilde{U}_{i}}
, where
\bar{\nu}_{z}=\nu_{z}/\nu_{z}(\partial\Delta)
with
\nu_{z}:=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{z}(X_{H_{\Delta}}\in\cdot)-\mu_{N}
as defined above.

—

If
\xi^{2}_{i}=1
, we sample
X_{D_{i}}=\tilde{X}_{D_{i}}
according to
\bar{\mu}_{N}=\mu_{N}/\mu_{N}(\partial\Delta)
.

After that, if
X_{D_{i}}\neq\tilde{X}_{D_{i}}
, we independently sample
X_{R_{i+1}}
and
\tilde{X}_{R_{i+1}}
according to
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{X_{D_{i}}}(X_{H_{B}}\in\cdot)
and
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\tilde{X}_{D_{i}}}(X_{H_{B}}\in\cdot)
respectively;
if on the other hand
X_{D_{i}}=\tilde{X}_{D_{i}}
, then
X_{R_{i+1}}=\tilde{X}_{R_{i+1}}
, and in practice
Y_{j}=\tilde{Y}_{j}
for all
j\geq i+1
.

To summarise, under this coupling, the set
\left\{i\geq 1\,|\,\xi^{1}_{i}=1\right\}
collects
i
’s for which both
X
and
\tilde{X}
hit
G
before touching
\Delta
. For such
i
’s,
\xi^{2}_{i}
indicates if we force
X
and
\tilde{X}
to attain
\Delta
on the same site. The coalescence time is thus no larger than the first
i\geq 1
for which
\xi^{1}_{i}=\xi^{2}_{i}=1
. In particular, under this coupling, that we denote by
\mathbf{Q}
, the probability of
Y
and
\tilde{Y}
not coalescing before step
k
can be bounded from above by

\mathbf{Q}(Y_{k}\neq\tilde{Y}_{k})\leq\mathbf{Q}\big(\forall i\leq k,\xi^{1}_{i}\xi^{2}_{i}=0\big)\leq\big(1-c_{\mu}cN^{\gamma-1}\big)^{k}\leq e^{-c^{\prime}kN^{\gamma-1}}\,,

in which
c^{\prime}
does not depend on
N
. Therefore, taking
k\geq\tfrac{1}{c^{\prime}}N^{1-\gamma}\log 4
, the right-hand side is less than
\tfrac{1}{4}
, thus proving the upper bound for
T^{Y}_{\mathrm{mix}}
using (
3.29
).

3.3.2
Upper bound on the mixing time of interlacements

To get a coalescing coupling in the case of tilted interlacements, recall that
Z
is given by excursions
w_{i}^{j}
of independent tilted RW trajectories
w^{j}
starting from
\bar{e}_{B}
. In the same way, a copy
\tilde{Z}
is given by excursions
\tilde{w}_{i}^{j}
and trajectories
\tilde{w}^{j}
starting from
\bar{e}_{B}
.
We will couple two copies
Z
and
\tilde{Z}
in a way to have some
k
such that both
Z_{k}
and
\tilde{Z}_{k}
are given by the first excursion of a trajectory, that is by
w_{0}^{j}
and
\tilde{w}_{0}^{\tilde{\jmath}}
for some
j,\tilde{\jmath}
; put otherwise, if
X
and
\tilde{X}
are the underlying tilted RW trajectories, we will force
X,\tilde{X}
to escape to infinity for the same
(k-1)
-th excursion. By (
2.33
), escaping to infinity when starting from
z\in\partial\Delta
is a Bernoulli trial with success probability
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{z}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)\geq cN^{\gamma-1}=p_{N}
. Using the same trial for both
w_{i}^{j}
and
\tilde{w}_{i}^{j}
thus allows for a quick coupling.

Again, let us write the coupling in more details.
We write
Z_{i}=(X_{R_{i}},X_{D_{i}})
and
\tilde{Z}_{i}=(\tilde{X}_{R_{i}},\tilde{X}_{D_{i}})
. Let
(\xi_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be a sequence of i.i.d Bernoulli random variables with parameter
p_{N}
. The coupling of
Z
and
\tilde{Z}
proceeds as follows: for all
i\geq 1
,

—

If
\xi_{i}=0
, independently sample
Z_{R_{i+1}}
according to
\nu_{Z_{D_{i}}}
and
\tilde{Z}_{R_{i+1}}
with
\nu_{\tilde{Z}_{D_{i}}}
, where for
w\in\partial\Delta
, the law
\nu_{w}
is defined by: for any
z\in\partial B

(1-p_{N})\nu_{w}(z)=\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(X_{H_{B}}=z\,,\,\bar{H}_{B}<+\infty)+\left[\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(\bar{H}_{B}=+\infty)-p_{N}\right]\bar{e}^{T}_{B}(z)\,.

—

If
\xi_{i}=1
, sample
Z_{R_{i+1}}=\tilde{Z}_{R_{i+1}}
according to
\bar{e}^{T}_{B}
. Afterwards, set
Z_{j}=\tilde{Z}_{j}
for all
j>i
.

Under this coupling, the first
i\geq 1
that satisfies
\xi_{i}=1
is such that
Z_{R_{i+1}}=\tilde{Z}_{R_{i+1}}
is the entrance point in
B
for trajectories of the two copies of the tilted RI. The probability of not having coalesced before step
k
is then given by

\mathbf{Q}(Z_{k}\neq\tilde{Z}_{k})\leq\mathbf{Q}\big(\forall i\leq k,\xi_{i}=0\big)=\big(1-p_{N}\big)^{k}\leq\big(1-cN^{\gamma-1})^{k}\leq e^{-ckN^{\gamma-1}}\,,

where
c>0
is independent from
N
. Again, taking
k\geq\tfrac{1}{c}N^{1-\gamma}\log 4
proves the upper bound for
T^{Z}_{\mathrm{mix}}
using (
3.29
).

3.4
Controlling the variances for Theorem
2.1

Recall the terms
\mathrm{Var}_{\pi^{\circ}}(\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ})
that appear in the upper bound of Theorem
2.1
, where

\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ}(z,w)\vcentcolon=\frac{p^{\circ}((z,w),(x,y))}{\mu(x,y)}\,,

with
\mu
a measure on
\Sigma
having full support. A natural measure to take is
\mu(x,y)\vcentcolon=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y)
since it appears in both transition probabilities
p^{Y}
and
p^{Z}
, see (
3.3
) and (
3.7
). With this choice, we therefore have

\rho^{Y}_{(x,y)}(z,w)=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{w}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\,,

\rho^{Z}_{(x,y)}(z,w)=\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(X_{H_{B}}=x,H_{B}<+\infty)+\bar{e}_{B}^{T}(x)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(H_{B}=+\infty)\,.

To control the variances
\mathrm{Var}_{\tilde{\pi}}(\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ})
, we need to control these quantities. Since
\bar{e}_{B}^{T}(x)
and
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(H_{B}=+\infty)
were both studied in Section
2.3
, we now seek estimates on the law of the return point to
B
.

Proposition 3.8

(Return point for
Y
)
.

For all
y\in\partial\Delta
and
x\in\partial B
, we have

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\leq cN^{-\gamma(d-1)}\,.

(3.32)

Moreover, for any
x\in\partial B
, there are at least
c^{-1}N^{\gamma(d-1)}
points
y\in\partial\Delta
such that

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\geq cN^{-\gamma(d-1)}\,.

(3.33)

Proof.

We first write, using (
2.12
)

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(X_{H_{B}}=x)=\frac{\phi_{N}(x)}{\phi_{N}(y)}\mathbf{E}_{y}\bigg[\lambda_{N}^{-H_{B}}\mathbbm{1}_{\big\{H_{B}<H_{D_{N}^{c}},\ X_{H_{B}}=x\big\}}\bigg]\,.

(3.34)

Lower bound.
Since
\lambda_{N}<1
, we get a first lower bound using Proposition
2.3
:

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\geq\kappa_{1}\mathbf{P}_{y}\left(H_{B}<H_{D_{N}^{c}},X_{H_{B}}=x\right)=\kappa_{1}\Big(\mathbf{P}_{y}(X_{H_{B}}=x)-\mathbf{P}_{y}(H_{B}>H_{D_{N}^{c}},X_{H_{B}}=x)\Big).

By
[
ČT16
, Lemma 5.3]
, there are at least
c^{-1}N^{\gamma(d-1)}
points
y\in\partial\Delta
such that
\mathbf{P}_{y}\left(X_{H_{B}}=x\right)\geq cN^{-\gamma(d-1)}
. On the other hand, using the (strong) Markov property,

\mathbf{P}_{y}\left(H_{B}>H_{D_{N}^{c}},X_{H_{B}}=x\right)=\mathbf{E}_{y}\bigg[\mathbbm{1}_{\big\{H_{B}>H_{D_{N}^{c}}\big\}}\mathbf{P}_{X_{H_{D_{N}^{c}}}}\left(X_{H_{B}}=x\right)\bigg]\,.

Since
\partial D_{N}^{c}\subseteq(B_{N}^{\varepsilon})^{c}
, using
[
LL10
, Prop. 6.5.4]
and the notation of (
1.9
) we get

\sup_{z\in\partial D_{N}^{c}}\mathbf{P}_{z}\left(X_{H_{B}}=x\right)=\sup_{z\in\partial D_{N}^{c}}\bar{e}_{B}(x)\left[1+c\frac{\alpha N}{d(z,B)}(1+\bar{o}(1))\right]\leq c\bar{e}_{B}(x)\leq cN^{-(d-1)}\,.

In particular, since
\gamma<1
we get

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\geq\kappa_{1}\mathbf{P}_{y}\left(X_{H_{B}}=x\right)(1+\bar{o}(1))

for the
c^{-1}N^{\gamma(d-1)}
points
y\in\partial\Delta
, thus proving (
3.33
).

Upper bound.

We first split
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(X_{H_{B}}=x)
according to the value of
H_{B}
and according to whether or not the walk reaches
\partial B^{\delta}
for some
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
(whose value will be fixed later):

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(X_{H_{B}}=x)=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{B}<N^{2},X_{H_{B}}=x)&+\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{\partial B^{\delta}}>H_{B}\geq N^{2},X_{H_{B}}=x)\\
&\quad+\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{\partial B^{\delta}}<H_{B},H_{B}\geq N^{2},X_{H_{B}}=x)\,.\end{split}

(3.35)

The first term can be bounded from above using (
2.12
) (similarly to (
3.34
)) together with Proposition
2.3
and (
1.17
):

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{B}<N^{2},X_{H_{B}}=x)\leq\frac{c}{\kappa_{1}}e^{c_{0}}\mathbf{P}_{y}\left(H_{B}<H_{D_{N}^{c}},X_{H_{B}}=x\right)\leq cN^{-\gamma(d-1)}\,,

where we used
[
PT15
, Lem. 8.6]
to get a bound on
\mathbf{P}_{y}\left(X_{H_{B}}=x\right)
uniformly for
y\in\partial\Delta
.

For the second term, we claim that uniformly in
x\in\partial B,y\in\partial\Delta

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{\partial B^{\delta}}>H_{B}\geq N^{2},X_{H_{B}}=x)\leq cN^{-\gamma(d-1)}\,.

(3.36)

For the third term, we claim that uniformly in
x\in\partial B,y\in\partial\Delta

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{\partial B^{\delta}}<H_{B},H_{B}\geq N^{2},X_{H_{B}}=x)\leq cN^{\gamma-1}\sup_{z\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{z}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\,.

(3.37)

Plugging these estimates in (
3.35
), we get

\sup_{x\in\partial B,y\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\leq CN^{-\gamma(d-1)}+cN^{\gamma-1}\sup_{x\in\partial B,y\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\,.

Taking
N
large enough so that
cN^{\gamma-1}<1/2
proves (
3.32
).

We now prove the two claims, starting with (
3.36
). Applying (
2.15
) with
C=B^{\delta}\setminus B
, we get that

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{\partial B^{\delta}}>H_{B}\geq N^{2},X_{H_{B}}=x)\leq\frac{c}{\kappa_{1}}\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{c_{0}(k+1)}P_{N}(\delta,k)\,.

(3.38)

where we have set

P_{N}(\delta,k):=\mathbf{P}_{y}\left(H_{B}\in[k,k+1[N^{2},H_{\partial B^{\delta}}>kN^{2}\wedge H_{B},X_{H_{B}}=x\right)
.
We now treat
P_{N}(\delta,k)
for every
k\geq 1
. We use the Markov property at time
\tfrac{1}{2}kN^{2}
, from which two cases appear: either the walk does not return to
\Delta
before hitting
B
, or it returns to
\Delta
in which case since
H_{B}<H_{\partial B^{\delta}}
, the walk will cross
\partial\Delta
again.

By Proposition
2.4
(more precisely its second inequality) applied to
(B\cup\Delta)^{c}
, we have

\mathbf{P}_{y}\left(H_{B}\wedge H_{\partial B^{\delta}}\geq kN^{2},\forall\,t\in[k/2,k)N^{2},X_{t}\in(B\cup\Delta)^{c}\right)\leq Ce^{-c_{a}\frac{1}{2}kN^{2(1-\gamma)}}\,.

(3.39)

We also get

\mathbf{P}_{y}\left(H_{B}\wedge H_{\partial B^{\delta}}\geq kN^{2},\exists t\in[0,k/2)N^{2},X_{t}\in\Delta,X_{H_{B}}=x\right)\\
\leq\mathbf{P}_{y}\left(H_{B}\wedge H_{\partial B^{\delta}}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}kN^{2}\right)\sup_{w\in\Delta\cap B^{\delta}}\mathbf{P}_{w}(H_{B}<H_{\partial B^{\delta}},X_{H_{B}}=x)\,,

Thus, using again Proposition
2.4
and using the Markov property at time
H_{\partial\Delta}
, we get

\mathbf{P}_{y}\left(H_{B}\wedge H_{\partial B^{\delta}}\geq kN^{2},\exists t\in[0,k/2)N^{2},X_{t}\in\Delta,X_{H_{B}}=x\right)\leq ce^{-c_{a}k/2\delta^{2}}\sup_{z\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}_{z}\left(X_{H_{B}}=x\right)\,.

Now, with
[
PT15
, Lem. 8.6]
, we have
\sup_{z\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}_{z}\left(X_{H_{B}}=x\right)\leq cN^{-\gamma(d-1)}
uniformly in
x\in\partial B
. Thus, injecting these estimates in (
3.38
) yields

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{\partial B^{\delta}}>H_{B}\geq N^{2},X_{H_{B}}=x)\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{c_{0}(k+1)}\left[cN^{-\gamma(d-1)}e^{-c_{a}k/2\delta^{2}}+Ce^{-c_{a}\frac{1}{2}kN^{2(1-\gamma)}}\right]\,.

(3.40)

Taking
\delta^{2}<c_{a}/2c_{0}
and
N>c_{a}c_{0}^{1/2(1-\gamma)}
, this proves that the right-hand side of (
3.40
) is bounded by a constant times
N^{-\gamma(d-1)}
, and the claim (
3.36
) follows.

To get the second claim (
3.37
), we forget about the condition
H_{B}\geq N^{2}
and use the Markov property, first at time
H_{\partial B^{\delta}}
, then at time
H_{\partial\Delta}
: we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{\partial B^{\delta}}<H_{B},H_{B}\geq N^{2},X_{H_{B}}=x)&\leq\mathbf{E}^{N}_{y}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{H_{\partial B^{\delta}}<H_{B}\right\}}\mathbf{E}^{N}_{X_{H_{\partial B^{\delta}}}}\left[\mathbf{P}^{N}_{X_{H_{\partial\Delta}}}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\right]\right]\\
&\leq\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{\partial B^{\delta}}<H_{B})\sup_{z\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{z}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\,.\end{split}

With (
2.33
), we have
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{\partial B^{\delta}}<H_{B})\leq cN^{\gamma-1}
uniformly in
y\in\partial\Delta
, which completes the proof of (
3.37
).
∎

Corollary 3.9

(Return point for
Z
)
.

Proposition
3.8
holds for
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}(\,\cdot\,\cap\left\{H_{B}<+\infty\right\})
instead of
\mathbf{P}^{N}(\,\cdot\,)
.

Proof.

For the lower bound, observe that

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\geq\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{B}<H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}},X_{H_{B}}=x)=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{B}<H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}},X_{H_{B}}=x)\,,

since
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}
and
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}
coincide on
B^{\varepsilon}
. With the lower bound (
2.15
) we get

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{B}<H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}},X_{H_{B}}=x)\geq\kappa_{1}\mathbf{P}_{y}(H_{B}<H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}},X_{H_{B}}=x)\\
=\kappa_{1}(\mathbf{P}_{y}(X_{H_{B}}=x)-\mathbf{P}_{y}(H_{B}>H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}},X_{H_{B}}=x))\,.

Similarly as in the proof of the lower bound in Proposition
3.8
we get the desired bound.

For the upper bound, we decompose the probability according to whether
H_{B}<H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}}
or
H_{B}>H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}}
.
First, notice that

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{B}<H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}},X_{H_{B}}=x)=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{B}<H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}},X_{H_{B}}=x)\leq\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\leq cN^{-\gamma(d-1)}\,,

where we have used (
3.32
).
On the other hand, in order to avoid managing the effect of
\Psi_{N}
jumping to
1
at
\partial B^{\varepsilon}
, we choose
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
. Then, using the Markov property at times
H_{\partial B^{\delta}}
and
H_{\partial\Delta}\circ\theta_{H_{\partial B^{\delta}}}
yields

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{B}>H_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}},X_{H_{B}}=x)\leq\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{B}>H_{\partial B^{\delta}})\sup_{w\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\,.

Since
B^{\delta}\subset B^{\varepsilon}
, we have
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{B}>H_{\partial B^{\delta}})=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{y}(H_{B}>H_{\partial B^{\delta}})
(recall the comment under (
1.15
)): thus, Lemma
2.10
(replacing
\varepsilon
with
\delta
) implies that
\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(H_{B}>H_{B^{\delta}})\asymp N^{\gamma-1}
. Assembling the above estimates therefore yields

\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{y}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\leq CN^{-\gamma(d-1)}+cN^{\gamma-1}\sup_{w\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\,.

Then, taking
N
large enough so that
cN^{\gamma-1}\leq 1/2
gives the result.
∎

Recall the expressions for
\rho^{\circ}_{(x,y)}(z,w)
at the beginning of this section

\begin{split}\rho^{Y}_{(x,y)}(z,w)&=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{w}(X_{H_{B}}=x)\,,\\
\rho^{Z}_{(x,y)}(z,w)&=\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(X_{H_{B}}=x,H_{B}<+\infty)+\bar{e}_{B}^{\Psi_{N}}(x)\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{w}(H_{B}=+\infty)\,.\end{split}

Combining Proposition
3.8
, Corollary
3.9
, Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
2.10
we get the following upper bounds for
N
large enough: uniformly for
(x,y),(z,w)\in\Sigma
,

\rho^{Y}_{(x,y)}(z,w)\leq cN^{-\gamma(d-1)}\,,\qquad\rho^{Z}_{(x,y)}(z,w)\leq cN^{-\gamma(d-1)}+cN^{1-d}N^{\gamma-1}\leq c^{\prime}N^{-\gamma(d-1)}\,.

(3.41)

For lower bounds, we can use again Proposition
3.8
and Corollary
3.9
to get that, for every
x\in\partial B
, there are at least
c^{-1}N^{\gamma(d-1)}
points
w\in\partial\Delta
such that

\rho^{\circ}_{(x,y)}(z,w)\geq cN^{-\gamma(d-1)}\,.

(3.42)

As announced at the beginning of this section, we use these bounds to control the variances.

Proposition 3.10
.

There is some constant
c>0
such that uniformly in
x\in\partial B
and
y\in\partial\Delta
, we have

c^{-1}N^{-(\gamma+1)(d-1)}\leq\mathrm{Var}_{\tilde{\pi}_{\circ}}(\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ})\leq cN^{-(\gamma+1)(d-1)}\,.

Proof.

According to (
3.17
), we only need to prove the lemma for
\tilde{\pi}=\tilde{\pi}^{Y}
. Recall that
\Sigma=\partial B\times\partial\Delta
. In the proof, we write for simplicity

\tilde{\pi}\big[\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ}\big]\vcentcolon=\sum_{(z,w)\in\Sigma}\tilde{\pi}(z,w)\rho^{\circ}_{(x,y)}(z,w)\,,\qquad\tilde{\pi}\Big[\big(\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ}\big)^{2}\Big]\vcentcolon=\sum_{(z,w)\in\Sigma}\tilde{\pi}(z,w)\rho^{\circ}_{(x,y)}(z,w)^{2}\,.

For the upper bound, we simply write

\mathrm{Var}_{\tilde{\pi}}(\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ})\leq\tilde{\pi}\Big[\big(\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ}\big)^{2}\Big]\leq cN^{-\gamma(d-1)}\tilde{\pi}(\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ}),

where we have used (
3.41
) for the second inequality.
Then, notice that, by definition of
\rho^{\circ}
and since
\tilde{\pi}
is the invariant measure of
Y
,

\tilde{\pi}\big(\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ}\big)=\frac{1}{\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y)}\sum_{(z,w)}p^{\circ}((z,w),(x,y))\tilde{\pi}(z,w)=\frac{\tilde{\pi}(x,y)}{\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y)}=\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(x)\,,

(3.43)

where the last identity comes from Lemma
3.1
.
Then, we can use Lemma
3.4
to obtain that
\tilde{\pi}(\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ})=\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(x)\leq cN^{-(d-1)}
, which concludes the proof of the upper bound.

To get a lower bound on the second moment, we write

\tilde{\pi}\Big[\big(\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ}\big)^{2}\Big]\geq cN^{-2\gamma(d-1)}\tilde{\pi}\Big(\left\{(z,w)\in\Sigma\,:\,\rho^{\circ}_{(x,y)}(z,w)\geq cN^{-\gamma(d-1)}\right\}\Big)\,.

Using Lemma
3.3
and (
3.42
), we get (note that
\rho^{\circ}_{(x,y)}(z,w)
does not depend on
z
)

\tilde{\pi}\Big(\left\{(z,w)\in\Sigma\,:\,\rho^{\circ}_{(x,y)}(z,w)\geq cN^{-\gamma(d-1)}\right\}\Big)\geq cN^{1-d}c^{\prime}N^{\gamma(d-1)}\,,

so that

\tilde{\pi}\Big[\big(\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ}\big)^{2}\Big]\geq cN^{-(1+\gamma)(d-1)}\,.

On the other hand, using again (
3.43
) and Lemma
3.4
, we get
\tilde{\pi}\big(\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ}\big)^{2}\leq cN^{-2(d-1)}=\bar{o}(N^{-(1+\gamma)(d-1)})
.
All together, we end up with

\mathrm{Var}_{\tilde{\pi}}(\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ})=\tilde{\pi}\Big[\big(\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ}\big)^{2}\Big]-\tilde{\pi}\big[\rho_{(x,y)}^{\circ}\big]^{2}\geq(1-\bar{o}(1))cN^{-(1+\gamma)(d-1)}\,,

which concludes the proof of the lower bound.
∎

For the confined RW

Let us first give an expression for the expectation of

For any

Proof.

Proof of Proposition
4.1

Note that for any

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}\left(\left|\mathcal{N}(t_{N})-t_{N}\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}\right|>\varepsilon_{N}t_{N}\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}\right)\leq\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}(R_{k_{-}}>t_{N})+\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}(R_{k_{+}}<t_{N})\,.

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}[R_{k_{\pm}}]}{(1\pm\varepsilon_{N})t_{N}}=\lim_{N\to+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}k_{\pm}}\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}[R_{k_{\pm}}]=1\,.

As a consequence of Claim

Using (
4.3
) to prove Proposition

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}\Big(\big|R_{k_{\pm}}-\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}[R_{k_{\pm}}]\big|>\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{N}\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}[R_{k_{\pm}}]\Big)\leq C\exp(-c^{\prime}N^{c^{\prime\prime}})\,.

The way of controlling these deviations is through the Azuma inequality, see e.g.

We now control (

There are constants

We now turn to the first term in (
4.11

First of all, notice that using (

) and a union bound, we get similarly to (

\displaystyle\leq m\ell e^{-c^{\prime}N^{a}}+\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}\bigg(\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq j\leq m\\

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(H_{\Delta}>\tfrac{1}{2}sN^{2+a})\leq\kappa_{1}\sum_{k\geq 0}(\lambda_{N})^{-\tfrac{1}{2}sN^{2+a}-(k+1)N^{2}}\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(H_{\Delta}>\tfrac{1}{2}sN^{2+a}+kN^{2}\big)\,.

(4.21)

Using Proposition
B.1
-(
B.6
) and since
N^{2+a}\gg N^{2}\log N
, we have that

\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(H_{\Delta}>\tfrac{1}{2}sN^{2+a}+kN^{2}\big)\leq c\lambda(D_{N}\setminus\Delta)^{\tfrac{1}{2}sN^{2+a}+kN^{2}}\,,

where, if
K\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we set
\lambda(K)
as the first eigenvalue of the transition matrix of the simple random walk killed on
\partial K
. By eigenvalues comparisons (a consequence of the min-max theorem, see
e.g.

[
Str07
, Chap. 11, Th. 1]
) and the expression of eigenvalues on a blowup (see (
B.2
)), we have that

\frac{\lambda(D_{N}\setminus\Delta)}{\lambda_{N}}\leq\frac{\lambda(D_{N}\setminus\Delta)}{\lambda(B^{\varepsilon})}=\frac{1-c_{d}\frac{\lambda_{b}}{(\alpha N+N^{\gamma})^{2}}(1+\bar{o}(1))}{1-c_{d}\frac{\lambda_{b}}{(\alpha N+\varepsilon N)^{2}}(1+\bar{o}(1))}=\exp\Big(-\frac{c_{d}\lambda_{b}}{(\alpha N)^{2}}\Big[\frac{\varepsilon(2\alpha+\varepsilon)}{(\alpha+\varepsilon)^{2}}+\bar{o}(1)\Big]\Big)\,.

In particular, we get an upper bound provided
N
large enough: for any
x\in D_{N}\setminus\Delta
,

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(H_{\Delta}>\tfrac{1}{2}sN^{2+a})\leq c\kappa_{1}\sum_{k\geq 0}\exp\Big[-\frac{c_{d}\lambda_{b}}{2\alpha^{2}}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon(2\alpha+\varepsilon)}{(\alpha+\varepsilon)^{2}}+\bar{o}(1)\Big)(sN^{a}+k)\Big]\leq Ce^{-csN^{a}}\,.

(4.22)

Consider now some
x\in\Delta
. We have

\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(H_{B}>\tfrac{1}{2}sN^{2+a})\leq\frac{1}{\phi_{N}(x)}\sum_{k\geq 0}(\lambda_{N})^{-\tfrac{1}{2}sN^{2+a}-(k+1)N^{2}}\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(H_{B}>\tfrac{1}{2}sN^{2+a}+kN^{2}\big)\,.

(4.23)

Let
\lambda_{j}^{\mathbb{Z}}(K)
stand for the
j
-th eigenvalue of the transition matrix of the walk killed on
\partial K
, and let
\lambda_{j}^{\mathbb{R}}(\Omega)
be the
j
-th Dirichlet eigenvalue of (minus) the Laplacian on the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By (
B.2
) and

, we have that

\forall j\geq 1\,,\quad\lambda_{j}^{\mathbb{Z}}(D_{N}\setminus B_{N})=1-\frac{c_{d}}{N^{2}}\lambda_{j}^{\mathbb{R}}(D\setminus B(x_{0},\alpha))(1+\bar{o}(1))\,.

In particular, the transition kernel of the random walk killed on
\partial B_{N}\cup\partial D_{N}
admits a spectral gap of order
N^{-2}
. With Lemma
B.2
, there is a positive

\lambda_{1}^{\mathbb{R}}(D\setminus B(x_{0},\alpha))>\lambda+\eta_{B}
, where we recall that
\lambda
is the first eigenvalue of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on

. In particular, using again Proposition
B.1
-(
B.6
), we have for any
x\in\Delta
:

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(H_{B}>\tfrac{1}{2}sN^{2+a})&\leq\frac{1}{\phi_{N}(x)}\sum_{k\geq 0}(\lambda_{N})^{-\tfrac{1}{2}sN^{2+a}-(k+1)N^{2}}\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(H_{B}>\tfrac{1}{2}sN^{2+a}+(k+1)N^{2}\big)\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\phi_{N}(x)}\sum_{k\geq 0}\exp\Big(-\eta_{B}(\tfrac{1}{2}sN^{a}+k)\Big)\leq\frac{C}{\phi_{N}(x)}e^{-csN^{a}}\,.\end{split}

(4.24)

If we restrict our choices of
x
to
\partial\Delta
, we have
\phi_{N}(x)\geq\kappa_{1}
(recall Proposition
2.3
). Therefore, injecting (
4.22
) and (
4.24
) in (
4.19
) gives the first part of the claim.

For the second part, also injecting (
4.22
) and (
4.24
) this time in (
4.20

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}(R_{1}>sN^{2+a})&\leq\frac{1}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}\sum_{x\in D_{N}\setminus\Delta}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\Big[Ce^{-csN^{a}}+\frac{C}{\kappa_{1}}e^{-cuN^{a}}\Big]+\frac{1}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}\sum_{x\in\Delta}\phi_{N}(x)Ce^{-csN^{a}}\\
&\leq\Big[1+\tfrac{\|\phi_{N}\|}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}\Big]C^{\prime}e^{-csN^{a}}\,.\end{split}

With (
2.14
) we can easily prove that
\|\phi_{N}\|\asymp N^{d}
, and we recall

by (

) This means that

∎

Proof of Claim
4.3
.

By the Markov property and (

), we have

\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}[R_{m\ell}]=\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}[R_{2\ell}]+(1+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(2^{-N^{\varsigma/2}}))\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}}[R_{(m-2)\ell}]\,.

Since
\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}
is stationary for the entrance site in

of the confined RW, we have

\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}}[R_{(m-2)\ell}]=(m-2)\ell\cdot\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}}[R_{1}]\,.

To get an expression for this expectation, we observe that
(X_{R_{i}},R_{i})_{i\geq 1}
forms a Markov renewal process. The renewal theorem for Markov renewal processes (see

[
Ç69
, Section 6.a]
) then states that for any

, under
\mathbf{P}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}^{N}
,

\frac{\mathcal{N}(t)}{t}\xrightarrow[t\to+\infty]{L^{1}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}}[R_{1}]}\,.

Therefore, using Lemma

, we have

\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}}[R_{(m-2)\ell}]=(m-2)\ell\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}}[R_{1}]=(m-2)\ell\left[\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\mathbf{E}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}^{N}\left[\mathcal{N}(t)\right]\right]^{-1}=(m-2)\ell\frac{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}{\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}\,.

With
m=m_{-}
or
m=m_{+}
, we thus proved that

\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}}[R_{k_{\pm}}]=(1+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(2^{-N^{\varsigma/2}}))k_{\pm}\frac{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}{\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}+\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}[R_{2\ell}]-2\ell\frac{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}{\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}\,.

Note that
k_{\pm}\frac{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}{\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}\asymp t_{N}
by definition and that
\ell:=N^{\varsigma}T^{Y}_{\mathrm{mix}}\leq cN^{\varsigma+1-\gamma}
(see Proposition
3.5
),
\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}=N^{d}
(recall (
1.3
)) and
\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)\asymp N^{d-1-\gamma}
(see Lemma
3.4
), so that

\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}}[R_{k_{\pm}}]=k_{\pm}\frac{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}{\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}+\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}[R_{2\ell}]+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(2^{-N^{\varsigma/2}})t_{N}+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{2+\varsigma})\,.

(4.25)

Note that since
\varepsilon_{N}t_{N}\geq N^{2+\frac{1}{4}\delta}
, taking

\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{2+\varsigma})=\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-c})\varepsilon_{N}t_{N}
. Furthermore, observe that
\bar{\mathcal{O}}(2^{-N^{\varsigma/2}})=\bar{o}(\varepsilon_{N})
.

It then remains to control
\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}[R_{2\ell}]
.
We first observe that because of the differences in the starting point of the confined RW trajectory, we have to consider separately the start (before
R_{1}
) and the following visits to
B
. Therefore,

\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}[R_{2\ell}]\leq\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}[R_{1}]+(2\ell-1)\sup_{x\in\partial B}\mathbf{E}^{N}_{x}[R_{1}]\,.

(4.26)

Recall that
R_{1}
is the first hitting time of
B

after
visiting
\Delta
, therefore

\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}[R_{1}]\leq\frac{1}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}\left[\sum_{x\in\Delta}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\mathbf{E}^{N}_{x}[H_{B}]+\sum_{x\in D_{N}\setminus\Delta}\phi_{N}^{2}(x)\Big(\mathbf{E}^{N}_{x}[H_{\Delta}]+\sup_{y\in\partial\Delta}\mathbf{E}_{y}[H_{B}]\Big)\right]\,.

(4.27)

With the same proof as Claim

\mathbf{E}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}[R_{2\ell}]\leq 2C\ell N^{2+r}\leq cN^{2+r+\varsigma+(1-\gamma)}\,,

4.1.2
For the tilted RI

We now estimate the number of excursions in
B
of the tilted RI at some level
u>0
. Recall notation from Section
3.1
that each trajectory
\omega^{j}
hitting
B
has
T^{j}
excursions
(\omega^{j}_{i})_{i\leq T^{j}}
starting at
B
and ending at
\partial\Delta
. Let
J^{N}_{u}
be a Poisson random variable with parameter
u\,\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)
.
Given
(T^{j})_{j\geq 1}
we define the number of excursions at level
u
of the
\Psi_{N}
-tilted interlacement as

\mathcal{N}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{J^{N}_{u}}T^{j}\,.

(4.28)

Proposition 4.5
.

There are constants
c,C>0
such that for
N
large enough,

\mathbb{P}\left(\big|\mathcal{N}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)-u\,\lambda_{N}\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)\big|\geq\varepsilon_{N}u\lambda_{N}\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)\right)\leq Ce^{-cu\,\varepsilon_{N}^{2}N^{d-2}}\,.

(4.29)

Proof.

Since
J_{u}^{N}
is a Poisson random variable, we can use the Chernov bound (see e.g.
[
BLM13
, Section 2.2]
) to get that

\mathbb{P}\left(\big|J^{N}_{u}-u\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)\big|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{N}\,u\,\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)\right)\leq\exp\Big(-u\,\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)\,[h(\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{N})+h(-\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{N})]\Big)\,,

where
h(x):=(1+x)\log(1+x)-x
. We easily see with a Taylor expansion that for
N
large enough,
h(\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{N})+h(-\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{N})\geq(\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon_{N})^{2}
. Therefore,

\mathbb{P}\left(\big|J^{N}_{u}-u\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)\big|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{N}\,u\,\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)\right)\leq\exp\Big(-c(\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon_{N})^{2}u\,\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)\Big)\leq\exp\Big(-c^{\prime}\varepsilon_{N}^{2}u\,N^{d-2}\Big)\,,

using also that
\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)\asymp N^{d-2}
, recall Lemma
2.6
.

Notice that the random variables
(T^{j})_{j\geq 1}
are i.i.d, with distribution dominated by a geometric variable. Summing (
3.20
) over
x\in\partial B
, we obtain

\mathbf{E}_{\bar{e}^{\Psi_{N}}_{B}}^{\Psi_{N}}\big[T^{1}\big]=\frac{\lambda_{N}}{\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)}\sum_{x\in\partial B}\phi_{N}(x)^{2}\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(H_{\Delta}<\bar{H}_{B})=\lambda_{N}\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}{\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)}\,.

(4.30)

Again, with the Chernov bound (since
T^{j}
has an exponential moment because of the domination), we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\sum_{j=1}^{v}T^{j}-v\lambda_{N}\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}{\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)}\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}v\lambda_{N}\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}{\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)}\right)\leq C\exp\left(-c\eta^{2}v\lambda_{N}\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}{\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)}\right)\,.

(4.31)

In particular, applying the bound of (
4.31
) with
v_{N,u}^{\pm}=(u\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{N})\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)
, and taking
N
large enough so that
\lambda_{N}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\Big|\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{v_{N,u}^{\pm}}T^{j}}{(u\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{N})\lambda_{N}\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}-1\Big|\geq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{N}\right)&\leq C\exp\left(-c\varepsilon_{N}^{2}\lambda_{N}\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)\right)\\
&\leq C\exp\left(-c^{\prime}\varepsilon_{N}^{2}N^{d-1-\gamma}\right)\,,\end{split}

(4.32)

where we used
\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)\asymp N^{d-1-\gamma}
, recall Lemma
2.6
.

Therefore, we can control the deviations of
\mathcal{N}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)
:

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\Big|\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)}{u\lambda_{N}\,\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(B)}-1\Big|\geq\varepsilon_{N}\right)&\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\frac{J^{N}_{u}}{u\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)}-1\Big|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{N}\Big)+2C\exp\left(-c^{\prime}\varepsilon_{N}^{2}N^{d-1-\gamma}\right)\\
&\leq Ce^{-cu\varepsilon_{N}^{2}N^{d-2}}+2C\exp\left(-c^{\prime}\varepsilon_{N}^{2}N^{d-1-\gamma}\right)\,.\end{split}

(4.33)

Since
\gamma<1
, we get (
4.29
) for
N
large enough.
∎

Observe that since we want to have
\mathcal{N}(t_{N})\approx\mathcal{N}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})
, combining Propositions
4.1
&
4.5
imposes to take
u_{N}=t_{N}/\lambda_{N}\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}
.

4.2
Coupling of entrance and exit points

Let us recall that
\mathcal{N}(t_{N})
is the number of excursions from

to

, and

is the same quantity but for the tilted RI
\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})

We want to apply Theorem
2.1
to get a coupling of the ranges of the entrance and exit points
Y,Z
up to a well-chosen time

. By definition, we want to choose

in order to have

(4.34)

where we used the definition of

in Theorem
1.1
for the second identity, and Lemma
3.4
to get the last asymptotics (also using

We will apply Theorem
2.2
with the following parameters:

\begin{split}&|\Sigma|=|\partial B|\times|\partial\Delta|\asymp N^{2(d-1)}\,,\\
&T^{Y}_{\mathrm{mix}}\vee T^{Z}_{\mathrm{mix}}\leq cN^{1-\gamma}\quad\text{(Proposition \ref{prop:temps-mixage})}\,,\\
&\pi_{Y}(x,y)=\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(x)\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(X_{H_{\Delta}}=y)\sim\pi_{Z}(x,y)\quad\text{(Lemma \ref{lem:mes-inv-Y} \& \ref{lem:mes-inv-Z})}\,,\\

&\|\rho_{(x,y)}^{Y}\|_{\infty},\|\rho_{(x,y)}^{Z}\|_{\infty}\asymp N^{-\gamma(d-1)}\quad\text{(Equations \eqref{eq:rho-upper-bound} \& \eqref{eq:rho-lower-bound})}\,.\end{split}

(4.35)

.

3.4
and Proposition
3.8
for the last lower bound.
For

3.8
) and (
3.17
), we have

\frac{\pi_{Z}(x,y)}{\nu_{Z}(x,y)}\geq(1-cN^{\gamma-1})\frac{\tilde{e}_{\Delta}^{\phi_{N}}(x)}{\bar{e}_{B}^{\Psi_{N}}(x)}\geq C\,,

2.6

3.4

in (
2.4

(4.39)

hence

with

(recall (
3.2
)) for the confined RW, in a way that for all
i\geq 1
,
Y_{i}=(X_{R_{i}},X_{D_{i}})=\vcentcolon(Y^{R}_{i},Y^{D}_{i})
. The Markov chain
Z
is defined by ordering the trajectories and excursions of the tilted RI that hit
B
, and for
j\geq 1
,
Z_{j}=(w_{j}(0),w_{j}(H_{\Delta}))
where
w_{j}
is the
j
-th excursion.

We obtained above in (
4.43
) that the ranges of
Y
and
Z
roughly coincide up to a small time correction
\varepsilon_{N}
. Observe that for any
(x,y)\in\Sigma
, the excursions
(X_{R_{i}},X_{R_{i}+1},\dots,X_{D_{i}})
under
\mathbf{P}^{N}_{x}(\cdot\,|\,X_{i}=(x,y))
– meaning a confined walk bridge – have the same law as an excursion of a trajectory of the tilted RI – meaning a tilted RW trajectory – conditioned to enter
B
at
x
and exit through
\partial\Delta
at
y
. Therefore, if we know that
Y_{i}=Z_{j}
for some
i,j\in\left\{0,\dots,n\right\}
, our coupling consists of taking the same path for
(X_{R_{i}},X_{R_{i}+1},\dots,X_{D_{i}})
and for
w_{j}=(w_{j}(0),w_{j}(1),\dots,w(H_{\Delta}))
.

In all the following, we work conditionally on the entrance and exit sites
Y
and
Z
.
We first explain how to construct two sets of excursions
(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
and
(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{i})_{i\geq 0}
of the confined RW. Write
Y_{i}=(Y_{i}^{R},Y_{i}^{D})
, then under
\mathbb{Q}_{N}
the excursions set satisfies the following properties:

—

Conditionally on the values of
Y
and
Z
, the collections
(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{i})
and
(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{i})
are independent sequences of independent variables.

—

For any
i\geq 1
, the random variable
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{i}
is a nearest-neighbour path with law

\mathbb{Q}_{N}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{i}\in\cdot\,|\,Y,Z)=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{Y^{R}_{i}}\left(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{i}\in\cdot\,|\,X_{H_{\Delta}}=Y^{D}_{i}\right)

that is
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{i}
has the law of a confined RW path starting at
Y^{R}_{i}
and ending at
Y^{D}_{i}
when it first hits
\Delta
.

—

For any
i\geq 1
, the random variable
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{i}
is a nearest-neighbour path with law

\mathbb{Q}_{N}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{i}\in\cdot\,|\,Y,Z)=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{Y^{D}_{i}}\left(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{i}\in\cdot\,|\,X_{H_{B}}=Y^{R}_{i+1}\right)\,,

that is
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{i}
has the law of a confined RW path starting at
Y^{D}_{i}
that runs until going back to
B
, with hitting site
Y^{D}_{i}
.

—

The random variable
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{0}
is a nearest-neighbour path with law

\mathbb{Q}_{N}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{0}\in\cdot\,|\,Y,Z)=\mathbf{P}^{N}_{\phi_{N}^{2}}\left(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{0}\in\cdot\,|\,X_{R_{1}}=Y^{R}_{1}\right)\,,

that is
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{0}
is a confined RW path starting from distribution
\phi_{N}^{2}
that ends when it visits
B
for the first time after leaving
\Delta
, with first hitting site
Y^{R}_{1}
.

With this construction, we have for all
i\geq 1
,

\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{i}\overset{(d)}{=}(X_{R_{i}},X_{R_{i}+1},\dots,X_{D_{i}})\quad,\quad\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{i}\overset{(d)}{=}(X_{D_{i}},X_{D_{i}+1},\dots,X_{R_{i}})\,.

The process
X
defined on
(\Omega_{N},\mathscr{F}_{N},\mathbb{Q}_{N})
as the concatenation of
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{0},\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{1},\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{1},\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{2},\dots
therefore has the law of the confined RW started from its stationary distribution
\phi_{N}^{2}
. With this construction, we have (recall the definition of
\mathcal{N}
above Proposition
4.1
)

\mathcal{N}(t)=\sup\left\{p\geq 1\,:\,\ell(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}(\ell(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{i})+\ell(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{i}))\leq t\right\}\,,

(4.44)

where
\ell(\gamma)
is the length of the nearest-neighbour path
\gamma=(\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{\ell(\gamma)})
. Similarly, the range
\mathcal{R}_{\phi_{N}}(t)
of the confined RW up to time
t
is given by the process
X
after the concatenation of the
\mathcal{N}(t)
first excursions with the start of the
(\mathcal{N}(t)+1)
-th excursion which we stop at the appropriate time.

Since it is possible to have
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{0}\cap B\neq\varnothing
, we need to consider this first path as a part of an excursion of the confined RW.
To do so, we fix
\beta>0
and we note that since
X
is stationary, we have the equality in law

\mathcal{R}_{\phi_{N}}(t)\cap B\overset{(d)}{=}\left\{X_{i}\,:\,\beta t\leq i\leq(1+\beta)t\right\}\cap B\,

under
\mathbb{Q}_{N}
. Therefore, we have

\bigcup_{i=\mathcal{N}(\beta t)+1}^{\mathcal{N}((1+\beta)t)-1}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{i}\cap B)\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\phi_{N}}(t)\cap B\subseteq\bigcup_{i=\mathcal{N}(\beta t)}^{\mathcal{N}((1+\beta)t)}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{i}\cap B)\,.

(4.45)

In short, by shifting the times we consider (using
\beta>0
), we can have
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{out}}_{0}\cap B\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{\mathcal{N}(\beta t)}
. Since
\mathcal{N}(\beta t)
is the index of the last excursion before time
\beta t
, this means that the true range
\mathcal{R}_{\phi_{N}}(t)
contains a range that starts later (after it exits through
\Delta
), and is contained in a range that started earlier in time (that hit
B
before).

To construct the range of the tilted RI
\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u_{N})
intersected with
B
, we proceed inductively by plugging excursions
(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{i})
of the tilted/confined RW and regrouping them into trajectories.
To do so, let
\mathcal{I}_{0}=\varnothing
and recursively define

\iota_{i}\vcentcolon=\inf\left\{j\geq 1\,:\,j\not\in\mathcal{I}_{i-1},Y_{j}=Z_{i}\right\}\,,\quad\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{i}=\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{\iota_{i}}\,,\quad\mathcal{I}_{i}=\mathcal{I}_{i-1}\cup\left\{\iota_{i}\right\}\,.

(4.46)

Simply put, we partially order the set
\left\{Z_{i}\right\}_{i\geq 1}
by comparing it to the set
\left\{Y_{i}\right\}_{i\geq 1}
. The excursions
(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{i})_{i\geq 0}
of the tilted RI are then given by the corresponding excursion of the confined RW.

To construct the full tilted RI from this collection of excursions, we also define a sequence of Bernoulli variables
(U_{i})
, that are independent conditionally on
(Z_{i})_{i\geq 0}
, and such that

\mathbb{Q}_{N}(U_{i}=1|(Z_{i})_{i\geq 0})=\mathbf{P}^{\Psi_{N}}_{Z^{D}_{i}}(H_{B}=+\infty)\bar{e}^{T}_{B}(Z^{R}_{i+1})\,/\,p^{Z}(Z_{i},(Z^{R}_{i+1},\partial\Delta))\,,

(4.47)

which is the probability for the
\Psi_{N}
-tilted RW starting at
Z^{D}_{i}
to go to infinity without returning to
B
, and then for another
\Psi_{N}
-tilted walk “coming from infinity” to hit
B
at
Z^{R}_{i+1}
. Setting
V_{0}=0
and
V_{i}=\inf\left\{i>V_{i-1}\,:\,U_{i}=1\right\}
, we can group the excursions
(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
into
(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{i})_{V_{j-1}<i\leq V_{j}}
for
j\geq 1
. The
j
-th group corresponds to excursions of the
j
-th trajectory
w^{j}
, and the full tilted RI can be constructed by plugging bridges and trajectories of the walk on the conductances
\Psi_{N}
, both conditioned to never hit
B
.

Let
(J^{N}_{u})_{u\geq 0}
be a Poisson process on the real line with intensity parameter
\mathrm{cap}^{\Psi_{N}}(B)
, independent of all the previous randomness. Taking the previously fixed
\beta>0
, we then have
\mathcal{N}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)=V_{J^{N}_{u}}
and the equality in law

\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}(u)\cap B\overset{(d)}{=}\bigcup_{j=J_{\beta u}^{N}}^{J_{(1+\beta)u}^{N}}\bigcup_{V_{j-1}<i\leq V_{j}}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{i}\cap B)=\bigcup_{i=\mathcal{N}_{\Psi_{N}}(\beta u)}^{\mathcal{N}_{\Psi_{N}}((1+\beta)u)}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{i}\cap B)\,.

(4.48)

The key property of this construction is the following: for
p,q,m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\left\{Z_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{p}\subseteq\left\{Y_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{m}\subseteq\left\{Z_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{q}\Longrightarrow\big\{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{i}\big\}_{i=1}^{p}\subseteq\big\{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{i}\big\}_{i=1}^{m}\subseteq\big\{\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{i}\big\}_{i=1}^{q}\,,

(4.49)

which is how we use the previous section to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Recall that
u_{N}=t_{N}/\lambda_{N}\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}
and let us write
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{B,i}\vcentcolon=\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{i}\cap B
as well as
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{B,i}\vcentcolon=\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{i}\cap B
. On the probability space
(\Omega_{N},\mathscr{F}_{N},\mathbb{Q}_{N})
, by Proposition
4.1
, with probability at least
1-Ce^{-c^{\prime}N^{c}}
, we have

\bigcup_{i=\beta(u_{N}+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{N}}{4})\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi}(B)}^{(1+\beta)(u_{N}-\tfrac{\varepsilon_{N}}{4})\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi}(B)}\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{B,i}=\vcentcolon\mathcal{R}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\phi_{N}}(t_{N})\cap B\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{2}\vcentcolon=\bigcup_{i=\beta(u_{N}-\tfrac{\varepsilon_{N}}{4})\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi}(B)}^{(1+\beta)(u_{N}+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{N}}{4})\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi}(B)}\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{in}}_{B,i}

With Proposition
4.5
, noting that
u_{N}\asymp t_{N}N^{-d}
and recalling that
\varepsilon_{N}^{2}t_{N}\geq N^{2+\frac{1}{2}\delta}
, with probability at least
1-Ce^{-cN^{\frac{1}{2}\delta+1-\gamma}}
, we have

\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}((1-\tfrac{\varepsilon_{N}}{2})u_{N})\cap B_{N}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{1}\vcentcolon=\bigcup_{i=\beta(u_{N}+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{N}}{3})\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi}(B)}^{(1+\beta)(u_{N}-\tfrac{\varepsilon_{N}}{3})\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi}(B)}\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{B,i}

and

\bigcup_{i=\beta(u_{N}-\tfrac{\varepsilon_{N}}{3})\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi}(B)}^{(1+\beta)(u_{N}+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{N}}{3})\mathrm{cap}_{\Delta}^{\phi}(B)}\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{B,i}=\vcentcolon\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{2}\subseteq\mathscr{I}_{\Psi_{N}}((1+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{N}}{2})u_{N})\cap B_{N}\,.

Finally, using (
4.43
) and (
4.49
) and taking
\beta
small enough, with
\mathbb{Q}_{N}
-probability at least
1-c_{1}e^{-c_{2}N^{\eta}}
, we have the inclusions
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{RI}}_{2}
.
Therefore, combining all of the above concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Appendix A
Some Simple Random Walk (gambler’s ruin) estimates

In this appendix, we prove Lemma
2.8
and Claim
2.9
.
For convenience, we recall the statements.

Lemma
2.8
.

Let
z\in(B^{\varepsilon}\setminus B)\cup\partial B
and
w\in B^{\varepsilon}\setminus B
be such that there are
\iota,\jmath\in[0,1)
and
\eta>0
such that

|z-x_{0}^{N}|-\alpha N\in[\eta,\tfrac{1}{\eta}]N^{\iota}\quad,\quad|w-x_{0}^{N}|-(\alpha+\varepsilon)N\in-[\eta,\tfrac{1}{\eta}]N^{\jmath}\,.

Then, there exist explicit constants that depend only on
\alpha,\varepsilon,d
such that for
N
large enough,

\begin{split}c_{1}\tfrac{\eta}{2}N^{\iota-1}+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-1})\leq\,&\mathbf{P}_{z}(H_{B^{\varepsilon}}<\bar{H}_{B})\leq c_{2}\tfrac{2}{\eta}N^{\iota-1}+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-1})\,,\\
c_{1}^{\prime}\tfrac{\eta}{2}N^{\jmath-1}+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-1})\leq\,&\mathbf{P}_{w}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})\leq c_{2}^{\prime}\tfrac{2}{\eta}N^{\jmath-1}+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-1})\,.\end{split}

(A.1)

Proof.

Proposition 1.5.10 in
[
Law13
]
gives the following estimate: let
x\in B^{\varepsilon}\setminus B
, then

\mathbf{P}_{x}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})=\frac{|x|^{2-d}-[(\alpha+\varepsilon)N]^{2-d}+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{1-d})}{[\alpha N]^{2-d}-[(\alpha+\varepsilon)N]^{2-d}}\,.

(A.2)

Inject
x=z
where
|z-x_{0}^{N}|-\alpha N\in[\eta,\tfrac{1}{\eta}]N^{\iota}
, then simplifying by
N^{2-d}
yields

\frac{(\alpha+\tfrac{1}{\eta}N^{\iota-1})^{2-d}-(\alpha+\varepsilon)^{2-d}+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-1})}{\alpha^{2-d}-(\alpha+\varepsilon)^{2-d}}\leq\mathbf{P}_{x}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})\\
\leq\frac{(\alpha+\eta N^{\iota-1})^{2-d}-(\alpha+\varepsilon)^{2-d}+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-1})}{\alpha^{2-d}-(\alpha+\varepsilon)^{2-d}}\,.

Since
\iota-1<0
, we easily rewrite

\begin{split}(\alpha+\eta N^{\iota-1})^{2-d}=\left[\frac{\alpha^{-1}}{1+\tfrac{\eta}{\alpha}N^{\iota-1}}\right]^{d-2}&=\alpha^{2-d}\left[1-\tfrac{\eta}{\alpha}N^{\iota-1}(1+\bar{o}(1))\right]^{d-2}\\
&=\alpha^{2-d}-(d-2)\alpha^{2-d}\tfrac{\eta}{\alpha}N^{\iota-1}(1+\bar{o}(1))\,.\end{split}

Hence, we get the upper bound

\mathbf{P}_{x}(H_{B}<\bar{H}_{\partial B^{\varepsilon}})\leq 1-\frac{(d-2)\alpha^{2-d}\tfrac{\eta}{\alpha}}{\alpha^{2-d}-(\alpha+\varepsilon)^{2-d}}N^{\iota-1}(1+\bar{o}(1))+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-1})\,,

from which we deduce the first upper bound in (
A.1
). The lower bound is deduced with the same expansion.

Similarly, if
|w-x_{0}^{N}|-(\alpha+\varepsilon)N\in-[\eta,\tfrac{1}{\eta}]N^{\jmath}
, then

|w|^{2-d}-[(\alpha+\varepsilon)N]^{2-d}\leq(\alpha+\varepsilon-\eta N^{\jmath-1})^{2-d}=(\alpha+\varepsilon)^{2-d}\left[1+\frac{d-2}{\alpha+\varepsilon}\eta N^{\jmath-1}(1+\bar{o}(1))\right]

and similarly for the lower bound, hence the lemma.
∎

Claim
2.9
.

For all
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
, there is a
c_{\delta}>0
such that

\sup_{z\in\partial B}\mathbf{P}_{z}\left(\bar{H}_{B}\wedge H_{\partial B^{\delta}}>(\delta N)^{2}\right)\leq\frac{c_{\delta}}{N}\,,\qquad\sup_{w\in\partial B^{\delta}}\mathbf{P}_{w}\left(H_{B}\wedge\bar{H}_{\partial B^{\delta}}>(\delta N)^{2}\right)\leq\frac{c_{\delta}}{N}\,.

Proof.

For this proof, we can consider the ball
B
to be centered at
x_{0}^{N}=0
since we are only interested in the simple random walk.
Write
\tau_{\delta}\vcentcolon=\bar{H}_{B}\wedge H_{\partial B^{\delta}}
and take
z\in\partial B
. Consider
x
such that
x\not\in B
and
x\sim z
. Using the martingale
|X_{t\wedge{\tau_{\delta}}}|^{2}-t\wedge\tau_{\delta}
, we get

|x|^{2}=\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[|X_{t\wedge\tau_{\delta}}|^{2}-t\wedge\tau_{\delta}\right]\xrightarrow{t\uparrow\tau_{\delta}}\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[|X_{\tau_{\delta}}|^{2}-{\tau_{\delta}}\right]\,,

(A.3)

where we used dominated and monotonous convergence. Splitting the last expectation according to whether
X_{\tau_{\delta}}\in B
or not, we have

|x|^{2}=\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(X_{\tau_{\delta}}\in B\right)\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[|X_{\tau_{\delta}}|^{2}-\tau_{\delta}\,\big|\,X_{\tau_{\delta}}\in B\right]+\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(X_{\tau_{\delta}}\not\in B\right)\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[|X_{\tau_{\delta}}|^{2}-\tau_{\delta}\,\big|\,X_{\tau_{\delta}}\not\in B\right]\,.

Rearranging the terms,

\begin{split}\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(X_{\tau_{\delta}}\in B\right)\mathbf{E}_{z}\left[\tau_{\delta}\,\big|\,X_{\tau_{\delta}}\in B\right]=&\,\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(X_{\tau_{\delta}}\in B\right)\mathbf{E}_{z}\left[|X_{\tau_{\delta}}|^{2}-|x|^{2}\,\big|\,X_{\tau_{\delta}}\in B\right]\\
&+\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(X_{\tau_{\delta}}\not\in B\right)\mathbf{E}_{z}\left[|X_{\tau_{\delta}}|^{2}-\tau_{\delta}-|x|^{2}\,\big|\,X_{\tau_{\delta}}\not\in B\right]\,.\end{split}

Since we took
x\not\in B
, on the event
\left\{X_{\tau_{\delta}}\in B\right\}
we have
|X_{\tau_{\delta}}|^{2}\leq|x|^{2}
, and thus we can bound
\mathbf{E}_{z}\left[|X_{\tau_{\delta}}|^{2}-|x|^{2}\,|\,X_{\tau_{\delta}}\in B\right]\leq 0
. Also using that
\tau_{\delta}\geq 0
, we get

\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(X_{\tau_{\delta}}\in B\right)\mathbf{E}_{z}\left[\tau_{\delta}\,\big|\,X_{\tau_{\delta}}\in B\right]\leq\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(X_{\tau_{\delta}}\not\in B\right)\mathbf{E}_{z}\left[|X_{\tau_{\delta}}|^{2}-|x|^{2}\,\big|\,X_{\tau_{\delta}}\not\in B\right]\,.

Note that on
\left\{X_{\tau_{\delta}}\not\in B\right\}
, we have
|X_{\tau_{\delta}}|^{2}-|x|^{2}\leq 2\alpha\delta N^{2}
. Since
\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(X_{\tau_{\delta}}\in B\right)\geq\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(X_{1}=z\right)=1/2d
, we get

\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[\tau_{\delta}\,|\,X_{\tau_{\delta}}\in B\right]\leq 4\alpha d\delta N^{2}\mathbf{P}_{z}\left(X_{\tau_{\delta}}\not\in B\right)\leq c_{\delta}^{\prime}N\,,

(A.4)

where we have used
[
LL10
, Lem. 6.4.3]
for the last inequality.
By the Markov
inequality and decomposing on the possible sites
x
, we get the claim for
z\in\partial B
.
With a similar proof, we also get the result in (
2.9
) for
w\in\partial B^{\delta}
.
∎

Comment A.1
.

To get a version of this claim where the starting point
z
is not in
\partial B
but instead is such that
|z-x_{0}^{N}|-\alpha N\in[\eta,\tfrac{1}{\eta}]N^{\gamma}
for some
\eta>0
, we see that the proof of Claim
2.9
is still true up until (
A.4
), in which the bound
\mathbf{P}_{z}\left(X_{\tau_{\delta}}\not\in B\right)\leq c_{\delta}^{\prime}/N
must be replaced by
\mathbf{P}_{z}\left(X_{\tau_{\delta}}\not\in B\right)\leq c_{\delta}^{\prime}N^{\gamma-1}
. Therefore, we get a bound
\mathbf{E}_{z}\left[\tau_{\delta}\,|\,X_{\tau_{\delta}}\in B\right]\leq c_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}N^{\gamma}
, which by the Markov inequality yields the bound
N^{\gamma-1}
that was announced in the proof of Lemma
2.10
.

Appendix B
On eigenvalues and eigenvectors

The main goal of this section is to summarize results on
\phi_{N}
which are proven in
[
BB24
]
(or easily deduced from this paper). We will also prove some estimates that we use in the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Some preliminaries.

Consider a compact set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a mesh parameter
h>0
. On
D^{(h)}\vcentcolon=D\cap h\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we define the discrete Laplacian so that for any
x\in D^{(h)}\setminus\partial D_{h}
, any
v^{(h)}:D^{(h)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\Delta^{(h)}v^{(h)}(x)\vcentcolon=h^{-2}\sum_{e\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|e|=1}\Big(v^{(h)}(x+he)-v^{(h)}(x)\Big)=\frac{2d}{h^{2}}\Delta_{d}\tilde{v}^{(h)}(\tfrac{1}{h}x)\,,

where
\tilde{v}^{(h)}(x)=v^{(h)}(hx)
. On
D^{(h)}
, we may consider the discrete Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}&\Delta^{(h)}v-\mu^{(h)}v=0\text{ on }D^{(h)}\setminus\partial D^{(h)}\,;\\
&\forall x\in\partial D^{(h)},v(x)=0\,.\end{cases}

Write
(\mu^{(h)}_{k},\phi^{(h)}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
for the (ordered) eigenvalues and
\ell^{1}
-normalized eigenvectors that solve this discrete Dirichlet problem.
We also consider the Dirichlet problem on
D
, with the usual Laplace–Beltrami operator
\Delta
, with
L^{1}
-normalized (ordered) solutions
(\mu_{k},\varphi_{k})_{k\geq 1}
.
The Rayleigh-Ritz method gives that
\mu^{(h)}_{k}=\mu_{k}(1+\bar{o}(1))
as
h\to 0
, for a wide range of domains
D
(see
[
Wei58
]
for more details).

Consider now the transition matrix
P_{N}
of the simple random walk killed on the boundary of
D_{N}=(ND)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Consider now
x=x_{*}N\in D_{N}\setminus\partial D_{N}
and recall the previous notation, then

\begin{split}P_{N}\tilde{v}^{(1/N)}(x)=\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{y\sim x}\tilde{v}^{(1/N)}(x)(y)&=\tilde{v}^{(1/N)}(x)(x)+\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{|e|=1}\Big(\tilde{v}^{(1/N)}(x+e)-\tilde{v}^{(1/N)}(x)\Big)\\
&=\Big(\mathrm{Id}_{N}+\frac{1}{2dN^{2}}\Delta^{(1/N)}\Big)v^{(1/N)}(x_{*})\,.\end{split}

(B.1)

Since every function
f:D_{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
can be expressed as some
\tilde{v}^{(1/N)}
, this proves that
P_{N}
and
\Delta^{(1/N)}
have the same eigenvectors.
Write
(\lambda_{N}^{k},\phi_{N}^{k})
for the (ordered) eigenvalues and eigenvectors of this transition matrix; we also write
\lambda_{N}\vcentcolon=\lambda_{N}^{1}
and
\phi_{N}\vcentcolon=\phi_{N}^{1}
. Using (
B.1
) first, and then
\mu^{(1/N)}_{k}=\mu_{k}(1+\bar{o}(1))
as mentioned above, we get the following asymptotics for
\lambda_{N}^{k}
:

\lambda_{N}^{k}=1-\frac{\mu_{k}^{(1/N)}}{2dN^{2}}=1-\frac{\mu_{k}}{2dN^{2}}+\bar{o}(N^{-2})\,.

(B.2)

Additionally to the convergence of the eigenvalues, there is also a convergence for the first eigenvector
\phi_{N}
. Recall that
\varphi_{1}
is the first eigenfunction of the Laplace operator
\Delta
on the original domain
D
, which is considered to be
L^{1}
-normalized. We define on
D_{N}
the function
\varphi_{N}=\varphi_{1}(\cdot/N)
.
Recall that
\phi_{N}
is the first eigenvector of the matrix
P_{N}
, that we normalize by
\|\phi_{N}\|_{1}=N^{d}
. Then,
[
BB24
, Corollary 1.10]
states that
\phi_{N}
is uniformly close to
\varphi_{N}
. More precisely,
there is a constant
c>0
(that only depends on
D
) such that for all
N
large enough,

\sup_{x\in D_{N}}\big|\phi_{N}(x)-\varphi_{N}(x)\big|\leq cN^{-1/2(d+1)}\,.

(B.3)

In the bulk, this can be refined to have a convergence of the ratios to
1
. For any
\eta>0
, define
D_{N}^{-\eta}\vcentcolon=\{x\in D_{N},d(x,\partial D_{N})>\eta N\}
.
Then we have

\sup_{x\in D_{N}^{-\eta}}\bigg|\frac{\phi_{N}(x)}{\varphi_{N}(x)}-1\bigg|\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}0\,.

(B.4)

Some other useful estimates.

We complement the results by some observations that are used in the proof of Proposition
4.1
. First of all, we state a result that tells that, provided that the time is large enough, the first eigencouple gives the probability for the SRW to stay in
D_{N}
when starting from a given point in
D_{N}
. The following statement can be easily proven with the methods of the proof of
[
DFSX21
, Lemma 3.10]
and the fact that
\|\phi_{N}\|\asymp\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}
(which is a consequence of (
2.14
)).

Proposition B.1
.

Let
\tau
be the exit time of
D_{N}
, then for any sequence
t_{N}\gg N^{2}\log N
and any
x\in D_{N}
, we have

\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau>t_{N})=\phi_{N}(x)(\lambda_{N})^{t_{N}}\frac{\|\phi_{N}\|}{\|\phi_{N}\|_{2}^{2}}(1+\bar{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-2}))\,.

(B.5)

In particular, there is a positive constant
c>0
such that for any
x\in D_{N}
with
N
large enough, we have

c\phi_{N}(x)(\lambda_{N})^{t_{N}}\leq\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau>t_{N})\leq\tfrac{1}{c}\phi_{N}(x)(\lambda_{N})^{t_{N}}\,.

(B.6)

We also give the following lemma which, combined with (
B.2
), ensures that removing a macroscopic ball strictly lowers the first eigenvalue of the domain. By Proposition
B.1
, this implies that staying in
D_{N}
a time
t_{N}\gg N^{2}\log N
while avoiding this macroscopic ball is exponentially improbable.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
x\in D
and
r>0
be such that
B_{r}^{x}=B(x,r)\subset D
. Then, we have
\lambda(D\setminus B_{r}^{x})>\lambda
.

Proof.

By the monotonicity of the eigenvalue
\lambda(D)
with respect to the domain, it suffices to prove this when
r>0
is small. By
[
Hen06
, Theorem 1.4.1]
we have

\lambda(D\setminus B_{r}^{x})=\lambda(D)+s_{d}r^{d-2}\varphi_{1}^{2}(x)+\bar{o}(r^{d-2})\,,

with
s_{d}
the volume of the unit sphere of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
) and
\varphi_{1}
the first Dirichlet eigenfunction on
D
, which is positive on the interior of
D
. The lemma immediately follows.

Selberg’s Central Limit Theorem for automorphic L L -functions in the t t -aspect
Madhuparna Das

Abstract
. We present a proof of Selberg’s Central Limit Theorem for automorphic L L -functions of degree 2 using Radziwiłł and Soundararajan’s method. Additionally, we prove the independence of the automorphic L L -functions associated with the sequence of primitive holomorphic cusp forms.

In 2017, Radziwiłł and Sounadararajan
[

gave a new proof of Selberg’s Central Limit Theorem. Rather than using complicated number theoretic arguments, they have used basic facts of the Riemann zeta function which makes their proof more elegant. Recently, P. Hsu and P. Wong
[

In this paper, we give a simple proof of Selberg’s influential theorem (formally stated below) for classical automorphic

As stated in

from Proposition

As the second step, consider the auxiliary series

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(f,s)=\mathcal{P}(f,s;X)=\sum_{2\leq n\leq X}\frac{\Lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}\log n}

By computing moments we determine the distribution of

Proposition 2
.

As
t

, the distribution of

Now it remains to connect the auxiliary series

. For this, we use mollification technique. Introducing the Dirichlet polynomial

\displaystyle M(f,s)=\sum_{n}\frac{\mu(n)a(n)\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}}.

\displaystyle a(n)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $n$ is composed only primes below $X$ and has at most $100\log\log T$ primes below $Y$,}\\

. It is now evident that

\displaystyle M(f,\sigma_{0}+it)=(1+o(1))\exp(-\mathcal{P}(f,\sigma_{0}+it))

except perhaps on a subset of measure

.

Now it remains to connect the

Proposition 4

For

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\left|1-L(f,\sigma_{0}+it)M(f,\sigma_{0}+it)\right|^{2}dt=o(1)

\displaystyle L(f,\sigma_{0}+it)M(f,\sigma_{0}+it)=1+o(1),

except perhaps on a set of measure

Now we are ready to prove Theorem

1
: Recalling Proposition

\displaystyle L(f,\sigma_{0}+it)=(1+o(1))M(f,\sigma_{0}+it)^{-1}.

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n\text{ non square free}}\frac{a_{k}(n)a_{k}(n)\Psi_{k}(n)\overline{\Psi_{k}(n)}}{n^{2\sigma_{0}}}\right|\leq(|a_{1}|+|a_{2}|)^{2k}\sum_{n\text{ non square free}}\frac{a_{k}(n)^{2}\lambda_{f}(n)^{2}}{n^{2\sigma_{0}}}.

Consider the each term
\prod_{j=1}^{r}a_{1}a_{2}\lambda_{f}(p_{j})
. From the fundamental theorem of arithmetic each
p_{j}
’s are distinct primes. Since,
\lambda_{f}(n)
is a real valued function and gcd of each pair
(p_{j},p_{i})
equals to 1 for
i\neq j
we can write
\prod_{j=1}^{r}|(a_{1}a_{2}\lambda_{g}(p_{j}))|^{\alpha_{j}}=|a_{1}a_{2}|^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{r}|\lambda_{g}(p_{j})|^{\alpha_{j}}=|a_{1}a_{2}|^{k}(p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{j}^{\alpha_{j}})^{\delta}=|a_{1}a_{2}|^{k}n^{\delta}
, where
\delta
is the appropriate constant satisfying
|\lambda_{g}(n)|\leq n^{\delta}
.

Therefore as argued in
[
4
]
the non-square free term contributes a quantity of order
O((\log\log T)^{k-1+\epsilon})
. For
n
square free we can express
\Psi(n)\bar{\Psi}(n)
as

(12)

\displaystyle\sum(a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2})^{\beta}(a_{1}a_{2})^{k-\beta}\lambda_{f}(m)\bar{\lambda}_{f}(m^{\prime})

where the sum is over
\beta+\Omega(m)+\Omega(m^{\prime})=k
such that
0\leq\beta\leq k
and
n=mm^{\prime}
where
n
has the prime composition of
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
. From (
12
) we can express that

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\beta\leq k}(a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2})^{\beta}(a_{1}a_{2})^{k-\beta}\sum_{n}\frac{a_{k}(n)^{2}\lambda_{f}(m)\bar{\lambda}_{f}(m^{\prime})}{n^{2\sigma_{0}}}

where the inner sum runs over
n=mm^{\prime}
with
(m,m^{\prime})=1
and
\beta+\Omega(m)+\Omega(m^{\prime})=k
. Since
\lambda_{f}(n)
is real multiplicative function we have

\displaystyle k!\sum_{0\leq\beta\leq k}\frac{k!}{(k-\beta)!\beta!}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\leq X\\
\text{all $p_{j}$'s are distinct}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p_{1}\cdots p_{\beta})\bar{\lambda}_{f}(p_{\beta+1}\cdots p_{k})}{(p_{1}\cdots p_{k})^{2\sigma_{0}}}

which is

\displaystyle k!\sum_{0\leq\beta\leq k}\frac{k!}{(k-\beta)!\beta!}\left(\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)}{p^{2\sigma_{0}}}\right)^{\beta}\left(\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{f}(p)}{p^{2\sigma_{0}}}\right)^{k-\beta}.

From
[
7
]
we conclude that the sum in (
11
) has its main contribution from
\beta=k
, which is

\displaystyle k!((a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2})\log\log T)^{k}+O_{k}((\log\log T)^{k-1+\epsilon}),

completes the proof.
∎

Let
X_{j}=\log|L(f_{j},\sigma_{0}+it)|
for
j=1,2
. We are now ready to prove Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
4
: Observe that
\log|\mathcal{L}_{a_{1},a_{2}(s)}|=a_{1}X_{1}+a_{2}X_{2}
. Like an similar argument of Proposition
1
we have

(13)

\displaystyle\int_{t-1}^{t+1}\left|\log|\mathcal{L}_{a_{1},a_{2}(\frac{1}{2}+iy)}|-|\mathcal{L}_{a_{1},a_{2}(\sigma_{0}+iy)}|\right|dy\ll\left(\sigma_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\log T.

By a similar argument of the proof of Proposition
1
the above equation is true. However, we can study the function
\log|\mathcal{L}_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\sigma_{0}+it)|
away from the critical line
\Re(s)=\frac{1}{2}
.

Define
\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2}}(s)\coloneqq a_{1}\mathcal{P}(f_{1},s)+a_{2}\mathcal{P}(f_{2},s)
where
\mathcal{P}(f_{j},s)
’s are defined as the auxiliary series
\mathcal{P}(f,s)
. Similarly, the contribution of the higher order terms of the primes is at most
O(1)
. For the terms involving
p^{2}
we can consider
\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2},0}(\sigma_{0}+it)
, as defined. Now by Lemma
10
for
X^{k}\ll T
and odd
k
,

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}(\Re(\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2},0}(\sigma_{0}+it)))^{k}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{k}{\ell}\int_{T}^{2T}\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2},0}(\sigma_{0}+it)^{\ell}\overline{\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2},0}(\sigma_{0}+it)^{k-\ell}}dt\ll T.

Also, by Lemma
10
, for even
k
,

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}(\Re(\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2},0}(\sigma_{0}+it)))^{k}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-k}\binom{k}{k/2}\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)!\left(\frac{a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}}{2}\log\log T\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}+O_{k}((\log\log T)^{\frac{k}{2}-1+\epsilon}).

It proves that the real part of
\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\sigma_{0}+it)
has an approximately normal distribution with mean 0 and variance
\frac{a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}}{2}\log\log T
.

Now it remains to connect
\mathcal{L}_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\sigma_{0}+it)
with
\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\sigma_{0}+it)
. From the similar argument of Proposition
3
and Proposition
4
,
|L(g_{j},\sigma_{0}+it)|=(1+o(1))\exp(\Re(\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\sigma_{0}+it)))
, except for a possible set of measure
o(T)
, for each
j
. Since,
\mathcal{L}_{a_{1},a_{2}}(s)=|L(f_{1},s)|^{a_{1}}|L(f_{2},s)|^{a_{2}}
and
\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2}}(s)=a_{1}\mathcal{P}(f_{1},s)+a_{2}\mathcal{P}(f_{2},s)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\sigma_{0}+it)=(1+o(1))\exp(\Re(\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\sigma_{0}+it))).

It shows
\log|\mathcal{L}_{a_{1},a_{2}}(\sigma_{0}+it)|
has Gaussian distribution with mean 0 and variance
\frac{a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}}{2}\log\log T
, combining with (
13
) we conclude the proof. ∎

.

9.
Gaussian Process for automorphic
L
-functions

In this section, we prove Theorem
3
. The proof follows similarly to the proof of Theorem
2
. Since we are proving a more generalized version of it we need to face more complicated computations.

For
(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
we consider,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N},0}(s)=\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{a_{1}\lambda_{f_{1}}(p)+\cdots+a_{N}\lambda_{f_{N}}(p)}{p^{s}}.

Following the tradition, to prove Theorem
3
we need the next lemma.

Lemma 11
.

Let
(\lambda_{f_{J}})_{j=1}^{N}
be a sequence of distinct primitive holomorphic cusp forms. Assume that
k,\ell
are non-negative integers with
X^{k+\ell}\leq T
. Then for any real numbers
(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
, we have for
k\neq\ell

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N},0}(\sigma_{0}+it)^{k}\overline{\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N},0}(\sigma_{0}+it)^{\ell}}\ll T.

For
k=\ell

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}|\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N},0}(\sigma_{0}+it)|^{2k}dt=k!T\left(\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}^{2}\right)\log\log T\right)^{k}+O_{k}(T(\log\log T)^{k-1+\epsilon}).

Proof.

Similarly like the proof of Lemma
10
we start the proof by setting

\displaystyle\psi(p)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\lambda_{f_{j}}(p)

and

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(n)\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{r}\psi(p_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}

where
n=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}
and
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}=k
. Then we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N},0}(\sigma_{0}+it)^{k}=\sum_{n}\frac{a_{k}(n)\Psi_{k}(n)}{n^{\sigma_{0}+it}}

where
a_{k}(n)
is defined in (
7
). Therefore,

(14)

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N},0}(\sigma_{0}+it)^{k}\overline{\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N},0}(\sigma_{0}+it)^{\ell}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\sum_{n}\frac{a_{k}(n)a_{\ell}(n)\Psi_{k}(n)\overline{\Psi_{\ell}(n)}}{n^{2\sigma_{0}}}+O\left(\sum_{n\neq m}\frac{a_{k}(n)a_{\ell}(m)|\Psi_{k}(n)\overline{\Psi_{\ell}(m)}|}{(nm)^{\sigma_{0}-\frac{1}{2}}}\right).

From the definition of
\Psi_{k}(n)
for
n=\prod_{j=1}^{r}
we have

\displaystyle|\Psi_{k}(n)|=\prod_{j=1}^{r}|\psi(p_{j})|^{\alpha_{j}}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{r}(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{N}|)^{\alpha_{j}}=(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{N}|)^{k}.

Thus the big-
O
term in (
14
) contributes

\displaystyle\sum_{n\neq m}a_{k}(n)a_{\ell}(m)(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{N}|)^{k+\ell}\ll X^{(k+1)+(\ell+1)}\ll T.

As we know that by the definition
a_{k}(n)a_{\ell}(n)
is 0 if
k\neq\ell
, we conclude the first assertion of the lemma.

Now we need to prove the next part of the lemma which is the case for
k=\ell
. For
n=\prod_{j=1}^{r}p_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}
we write

(15)

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(n)\overline{{\Psi}_{k}(n)}=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}^{2}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{i\neq i^{\prime}}a_{i}a_{i^{\prime}}(\lambda_{f_{i}}\bar{\lambda}_{f_{i^{\prime}}}(p_{j})+\lambda_{f_{i^{\prime}}}\bar{\lambda}_{f_{i}}(p_{j}))\right)^{\alpha_{j}}.

We set
\lambda_{f}(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{f_{i}}\bar{\lambda}_{f_{i}}
. By (
15
) and the fact that
\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}=k
we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\text{ non square free}\end{subarray}}\frac{a_{k}(n)a_{k}(n)\Psi_{k}(n)\overline{\Psi_{k}(n)}}{n^{2\sigma_{0}}}\right|\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}|a_{j}|\right)^{2k}\sum_{n\text{ non square free}}\frac{a_{k}(n)^{2}\lambda_{f}(n)^{2}}{n^{2\sigma_{0}}}.

As argued in the proof of Lemma
10
the non square free
n
terms in (
15
) give
O_{k}((\log\log T)^{k-1+\epsilon})
. For square free
n
we write

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(n)\overline{\Psi_{k}(n)}=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\beta\leq k}{A_{N}^{\beta}(a_{1}a_{2})^{\Omega(m_{1})+\Omega(m_{1}^{\prime})}\cdots(a_{N-1}a_{N})^{\Omega(m_{(N-1)N/2})+\Omega(m^{\prime}_{(N-1)N/2})}}

\displaystyle\cdot\sum_{n}\frac{a_{k}(n)^{2}\lambda_{f_{1}}\bar{\lambda}_{f_{2}}(m_{1})\lambda_{f_{2}}\bar{\lambda}_{f_{1}}(m^{\prime}_{1})\cdots\lambda_{f_{N}}\bar{\lambda}_{f_{N-1}(m^{\prime}_{(N-1)N/2})}}{n^{2\sigma_{0}}}

where
A_{N}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}^{2}
and the second sum is over
n=\prod_{j=1}^{(N-1)N/2}m_{j}m^{\prime}_{j}
where
(m_{j},m^{\prime}_{j})=1
for all
j=1,\ldots,N
. Also,
\beta+\sum_{j=1}^{(N-1)N/2}(\Omega(m_{j})+\Omega(m^{\prime}_{j}))=k
. Since each
\lambda_{f_{j}}
is a real multiplicative function and by the change of variables
\Omega(m_{j})\mapsto\gamma_{2j-1}
,
\Omega(m^{\prime}_{j})\mapsto\gamma_{2j}
we can write the second sum of the above equation as

(16)

\displaystyle k!

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{(N-1)N}\leq k}\frac{k!}{\gamma_{1}!\cdots\gamma_{(N-1)N}!(k-\gamma_{1}!-\cdots-\gamma_{(N-1)N})!}

\displaystyle\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\leq X\\
\text{ $p_{j}$'s are distinct}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{f_{1}}\bar{\lambda}_{f_{2}}(p_{1}\cdots p_{\gamma_{1}})\cdots\lambda_{f_{N}}\bar{\lambda}_{N-1}(p_{\gamma_{(N-1)N}}\cdots p_{k})}{(p_{1}\cdots p_{k})^{2\sigma_{0}}}

\displaystyle=k!

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{(N-1)N}\leq k}\frac{k!}{\gamma_{1}!\cdots\gamma_{(N-1)N}!(k-\gamma_{1}!-\cdots-\gamma_{(N-1)N})!}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\lambda_{f_{1}}\bar{\lambda}_{f_{2}}(p)}{p^{2\sigma_{0}}}\right)^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\left(\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\lambda_{f_{N}}\bar{\lambda}_{f_{N-1}}(p)}{p^{2\sigma_{0}}}\right)^{\gamma_{(N-1)N}}.

By (
16
),(
15
) and the similar argument of the proof of Lemma
10
, the first sum of in (
14
) is mainly contributed by
k=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{(N-1)N}
which is

\displaystyle k!\left(\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}^{2}\right)\log\log T\right)^{k}+O_{k}((\log\log T)^{k-1+\epsilon}),

completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

In Lemma
11
, we have computed the moments of the auxiliary series
\mathcal{P}_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{N},0}(s)
, with this calculation in hand in the next theorem we study the joint distribution of the automorphic

Let

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is the same as the proof of Theorem

, instead of Lemma

Now we are ready to prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem
3
: Let
J

10.
Conclusion

We have followed the method of Radziwiłł and Soudararajan
[
3
]
to prove Theorem
1
but the proof for the
L
-functions need some modifications. Like the method established in
[
3
]
, we have used very basic properties of
L
-functions. But the proof of Proposition
4
does not follow the method established in
[
3
]
. Since we need to calculate the mean square of twisted automorphic
L
-function we can not apply the method established in
[
3
]
, directly. So we have taken the help of Hughes and Young’s
[
11
]
work instead. The most important fact we have relied on is the Ramanujan-Peterson conjecture. Also, we have taken the help of P. Kühn et al result
[
14
]
. It is possible to obtain a better error term that we have obtained. But the main focus of this paper concerns the proof of Selberg’s central limit theorem for
L
-functions. Any improvement on the error term for the mean square estimate of
L
-function will not effect our result.

Selberg
[
2
]
mentioned the concept of statistical independence of
L
-functions in his paper. As Hsu-Wong
[
4
]
proved this result for Dirichlet
L
-functions, we have proved this result for the families of automorphic
L
-functions associated with the sequence of primitive holomorphic cusp forms. Such a result can be proved for
q
-aspect as well by fixing the range of
t
.

As mentioned in
[
3
]
Proposition
1
-
3
can be established for the higher degree
L
-functions as well. For Proposition
4
, we rely on the information contributed by shifted convolution problem (to calculate the error term of the mean square of
L
-functions) which is not known for degree 3 or higher. However, some hybrid results for
GL(3)

L
-functions are possible. Moreover, one can extend Selberg’s Central limit theorem for the imaginary part of the Riemann zeta function as well as
L
-functions. As mentioned in
[
3
]
the mollification technique is not helpful but one can use the method developed by J. Najnudel
[
10
]
instead.

Keating and Snaith
[
8
]
have conjectured that central values of
L
-functions in families have the log-normal distribution with an appropriate mean and variance depending on their families. We have proved the main result for
t
-aspect. The hybrid aspect for this type of result is not possible because we are proving Theorem
1
on the critical line. In the hybrid aspect if we average over the Dirichlet characters without fixing the range of
t
then by the assumption of Grand Riemann Hypothesis the statement of these theorem and propositions do not hold, since
\log 0
is undefined. But the proof on weight aspect for such result is possible. For such calculations Lau’s
[
17
]
work might be helpful.

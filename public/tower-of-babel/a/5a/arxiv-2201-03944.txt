Sufficient conditions for perfect mixed tilings Thanks: The research leading to these results was partially supported by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) – 428212407 and 450397222.
Eoin Hurley, Felix Joos, Richard Lang

Abstract
. We develop a method to study sufficient conditions for perfect mixed tilings. Our framework allows the embedding of bounded degree graphs H H with components of sublinear order. As a corollary, we recover and extend the work of Kühn and Osthus regarding sufficient minimum degree conditions for perfect F F -tilings (for an arbitrary fixed graph F F ) by replacing the F F -tiling with the aforementioned graphs H H . Moreover, we obtain analogous results for degree sequences and in the setting of uniformly dense graphs. Finally, we asymptotically resolve a conjecture of Komlós in a strong sense.



1.
Introduction

The search for (optimal) conditions that force a particular graph to contain another graph as a subgraph is arguably among the most central topics in graph theory.
Consider a host graph
G
on
n

n

In this setting, one of the most natural conditions to investigate is surely a minimum degree condition of the host graph.
We define the

\displaystyle\MT(n,H)=\min\{m\colon\delta(G)\geqslant m\text{ implies that $H$ is a subgraph of $G$}\}.

A classic result of Dirac determines the minimum degree threshold for perfect matchings.
More generally, for a graph

Moreover,

Hajnal and Szemerédi
[

extended Dirac’s theorem by proving that

We write

Resolving the Alon–Yuster conjecture

Thus, the chromatic number of

for perfect

To plug this gap, Komlós

Let

The

\displaystyle\crit(F)=k-1+\frac{\alpha(F)}{\frac{1}{k-1}(1-\alpha(F))}={\frac{k-1}{1-\alpha(F)}};

To state the Kühn–Osthus Tiling Theorem, we introduce the class of graphs

\displaystyle F\in\cFcr\text{ if }\ \begin{cases}\chi(F)=2\text{ and }\text{gcd}_{\chi}(F)=\text{gcd}_{\text{c}}(F)=1\text{, or}\\

The contribution of this paper is to extend the above results in several directions:

The latter allows us, for example, to easily obtain similar results for host graphs with particular degree sequences and in the setting of uniformly dense graphs.
Along the way, we resolve a conjecture of Komlós asymptotically in a strong form.

Before we continue, let us make two further remarks.
First, the original Kühn–Osthus Tiling Theorem is proved in terms of an (additive) constant error, which is a more precise statement.
Our framework does not imply a version of this result, but it is conceivable that it can be obtained with additional work.
We return to this problem in the conclusion.
Second, the Kühn–Osthus Tiling Theorem also covers the case of arbitrary

Here, the necessary degree is determined by

Our main theorem can be used to recover such a statement with linear error term.
We omit however the details, since this situation is already covered by the Bandwidth Theorem of Böttcher, Schacht and Taraz
[

[

Our second result concerns mixed tilings which are near-perfect in the sense that the number of vertices they miss in the host graph is only a multiple of the maximum order of a tile.

All the above results are derived from a more general framework that goes beyond local conditions (such as degrees and uniform density) and is formulated in terms of abstract properties of our guest and host graphs.
This also allows us to untangle different conditions that are needed in the proof and address them individually.
In the following,
we discuss these, often necessary, properties for the guest and host graphs separately.

1.1.
Guest graph properties

Let us begin by motivating the properties of guest graphs that allow us to overcome certain divisibility obstructions in the host graph.
We provide two concrete examples to highlight the necessity of this soon-to-be-defined structure.
Consider a large complete bipartite graph
G=K_{a,b}
with
a+b=n
,
6\mid n
and
a\approx b
.
Clearly, if
a,b
are odd, then
G
does not contain a perfect
K_{2,4}
-tiling.
However, if
H
is the union of
\frac{n}{6}-1
disjoint copies of

and one

with in total
n

then

is a subgraph of

.
Unfortunately, then

is no longer a subgraph of

if

are even.
Instructively, if instead of a

is the union of
\frac{n}{6}-1
disjoint copies of

and two copies of

is a subgraph of

regardless of the parity of

.
This is because the union of the two copies of

is what we soon call a proper flexi-chromatic graph.

Similarly, suppose that

is the disjoint union of

and

with

as well as when

Again,

-tiling.
On the other hand,

is the union of

and six isolated vertices,

is a subgraph of

regardless of whether

or not.
This is because the six isolated vertices are what we soon call a topological flexi-chromatic graph.

We now formally define flexi-chromatic graphs thereby capturing the correct properties of the examples above.
For a (not necessarily proper)
k

\phi\colon V(W)\to[k]
of a graph

we denote the tuple of colour class sizes by

\ord(\phi)=\left(|\phi^{-1}(1)|,|\phi^{-1}(2)|,\dots,|\phi^{-1}(k)|\right).

We say that

if no edges in

if all edges are monochromatic.
Roughly speaking, a graph is flexi-chromatic with a central colouring

Definition 1.5

(Flexi-chromatic graphs)
.

A graph

is
(k,s)
-flexi-chromatic
with
central

k
-colouring
\phi\colon V(W)\to[k]

if for all
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
with
\sum_{i\in[k]}\mathbf{w}(i)=0
and
\lVert\mathbf{w}\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant s
, there is a
k
-colouring
\phi^{\prime}
of

with
\ord(\phi)-\ord(\phi^{\prime})=\mathbf{w}
.
We say that
W
is
properly

(k,s)
-flexi-chromatic if
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
are proper and
topologically
if
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
are topological.

One key insight that follows from our proofs is that if

contains enough disjoint flexi-chromatic graphs with the correct parameters, then
\crit(H)
determines the minimum degree threshold for containing
H
, as is the case in Theorem
1.1
.

The most flexible graphs with respect to colourings are surely isolated vertices and we exploit this, for instance, in the proof of Theorem

because we simply add to

has
n

).
Then

contains flexi-chromatic graphs and their strength and number depends on the number of isolated vertices added;
moreover,
\crit(H)\approx\crit(H^{\prime})
.
Therefore,

determines the minimum degree threshold for containing

for almost spanning guest graphs

, because
H^{\prime}
always contains flexi-chromatic subgraphs.

For

, examples of proper

-flexi-chromatic graphs are (odd) cycles of length at least, say,

or the disjoint union of at least

More generally,

In fact, whether a collection of graphs from

it boils down to the question of whether the set of all potential sums of a certain set of integers contains a long interval.
In this paper we adopt a result of Lev
[
33
]
to our setting.
Questions of this type have recently been investigated by Conlon, Fox and Pham in
[

]

For an

1.2.
Host graph properties

We now consider properties of the host graph.
By moving beyond degree conditions we can unravel the consequences of the degree conditions that were implicitly used in earlier results and clearly articulate how these consequences interact with the properties of the guest graphs.

To this end, we introduce the concept of
tiling frameworks
, which describe a set of necessary conditions for a host graph
G
to contain all elements of
\mathcal{H}(n,w,\Delta;\chi;t,\ell,w^{\prime})
.
Our main result states that if
G
satisfies these properties in a robust way, then it contains the entirety of
\mathcal{H}(n,w,\Delta;\chi;t,\ell,w^{\prime})
.
The encoding of the aforementioned picture with this framework leverages ideas from the the third author and Sanhueza-Matamala
[
32
]
.
A related approach has been developed by Keevash and Mycroft
[
21
]
regarding perfect matchings in hypergraphs.

Let us start with a first necessary condition for a graph
G
for containing a perfect tiling;
it clearly must contain an (almost) perfect fractional tiling of the same type.
We refer to this condition (and, at times, obstacle) as a
space
constraint.
Let us introduce this notion in more detail.
We denote by
\Hom(F;G)
the set of
homomorphisms
from
F
to
G
, that is, the set of functions
\phi\colon V(F)\to V(G)
, where
uv\in E(F)
implies
\phi(u)\phi(v)\in E(G)
for all
u,v\in V(F)
.
A
fractional
F
-tiling
of
G
is a function
\omega\colon\Hom(F;G)\rightarrow[0,1]
such that for all
v\in V(G)
, we have
\sum_{\theta\in\Hom(F;G)}\omega(\theta)|\theta^{-1}(v)|\leqslant 1
.
The
weight
of the tiling is
v(F)\sum_{\theta\in\Hom(F;G)}\omega(\theta)
.
A fractional tiling of
G
is
(1-\varrho)
-perfect
(or
perfect
if
\varrho=0
) if its weight is at least
(1-\varrho)v(G)
.
Note that
\{0,1\}
-valued fractional tilings correspond to tilings.
Finally, consider a rational
\chi>1
.
Then there are unique coprime integers
a<b
with
\chi=\lfloor\chi\rfloor+a/b
(if
\chi\in\mathbb{N}
, set
a=0
,
b=1
).
We write
B_{\chi}
for the complete
{\lceil\chi\rceil}
-partite graph
B_{a,({\lceil\chi\rceil}-1)\ast b}
, noting that
\crit(B_{\chi})=\chi
.

One can show that in order to find almost perfect tilings, it suffices that the host graph has a ‘robust’ perfect fractional tiling (
theorem

9.1
) and is large.
However, for a perfect tiling we must ask for more properties of the host graph.

Another trivial necessary condition for a graph
G
to contain a perfect
F
-tiling is that every vertex lies in a copy of
F
.
Suppose that
F
contains the
k
-partite graph
K_{2,\ldots,2}
as a subgraph.
Then it is certainly necessary that for every vertex
v\in V(G)
, there is a
k
-clique
K
disjoint from
v
such that
v
has at least
k-1
neighbours into
K
.
This example shows that for
G
to contain a perfect
F
-tiling, in some cases it is necessary that every vertex is ‘linked’ to a
k
-clique.

We define this formally in terms of hypergraphs.
A
k
-graph

J
is a
k
-uniform
hypergraph, meaning that every edge contains
k
vertices.
We say that a vertex
v\in V(J)
and an edge
f\in E(J)
with
v\notin f
are
linked
in
J
if there is an edge
e\in E(J)
with
v\in e
and
|e\cap f|=k-1
.
Moreover,
J
is
linked
if every vertex is linked to an edge.

In order to model graph structures with hypergraphs,
we define the
k
-clique hypergraph

K_{k}(G)
of a graph
G
to be the
k
-graph with vertex set
V(G)

where
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
is an edge if
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
induces a
k
-clique in
G
.
Properties of
K_{k}(G)
encode the potential divisibility issues when one aims to embed a
k
-chromatic graph
H
into
G
.
These properties are captured by the tight and loose component structure of the host graph, which we introduce now.

For a
k
-graph
J
, we define by
T(J)
the
dual graph
whose vertices are the edges of
J
, where
e,f\in E(J)
are adjacent whenever
|e\cap f|=k-1
.
The
tight components
of
J
are the subgraphs
T\subseteq J
, whose edges form the components of
T(J)
.
For loose connectivity, let
L(J)
be the graph whose vertices are the tight components of
J
and two tight components
T,T^{\prime}
form an edge whenever their vertex sets intersect.
The
loose components
of
J
are unions
L=T_{1}\cup\dots\cup T_{q}
of tight components
T_{1},\dots,T_{q}
which form a component in
L(J)
.
Given this, we say that
J
is
(t,\ell)
-
connected
if there is a (vertex) spanning subgraph
J^{\prime}\subseteq J
such that
J^{\prime}
has at most
t
tight components and at most
\ell
loose components.

We remark that each individual tight component and the combined loose components of
K_{k}(G)
can potentially form a divisibility obstruction to embedding a tiling
H
.
Each proper and topological flexi-chromatic subtiling of
H
helps us to overcome one of these obstacles.
(This we illustrated for
k=2
by some examples in Section
1.1
.)
A more involved construction shows that graphs satisfying the degree conditions of
theorem

1.2
can contain many tight components, where each tight component comes with its own divisibility obstruction (see
appendix

D
).

The following definition packages the structural properties we have just introduced into what we call a tiling framework.

Definition 1.6

(Tiling framework)
.

Let
\chi\in\mathbb{Q}
with
\chi>1
,
\varrho\in[0,1]
and

.
A graph

is a
(\chi,\varrho;t,\ell)
-tiling framework
if

(1)

G
has a
(1-\varrho)
-perfect fractional

-tiling, (space)

(2)

K_{\lceil\chi\rceil}(G)
is
(t,\ell)
-connected and
(divisibility)

(3)

K_{\lceil\chi\rceil}(G)
is linked. (linkage)

We remark here that, in particular, the host graphs in
theorems

1.1
,
1.2
,
1.3
and
1.4
are tiling frameworks for an appropriate choice of the parameters (see Lemmas
2.5
and

).

Main result

To state our main result, we need one final piece of notation.
For
\varepsilon,d\in[0,1]
, a subgraph

of a graph

is an
(\varepsilon,d)
-approximation of

and

.
For

\mu

admits a graph property

if every
(\mu,\mu)
-approximation

also admits

.

Now we are ready to state our main result.
Informally, it states that if the host graph

is a robust tiling framework, then

contains all graphs of

(quantified appropriately).
Note that the number of flexi-chromatic subtilings required of

Theorem 1.7

(Tiling framework theorem)
.

For all
\chi,\Delta>1
,

with
\chi,\mu\in\mathbb{Q}
,
there exist

such that the following holds for all

and sufficiently large

and

.
Suppose

is a
\mu
-robust

-tiling framework on
n
vertices and

for some

.
Then

.

All our previously stated results follow fairly easily from

(see Section

We also remark that under the assumptions of

,
that is, assuming

-robust

-tiling framework on

The well-known Blow-Up Lemma states that in order to show that

, it suffices to show that
H^{\prime}\subseteq R^{\ast}
.

We show that

in three steps (see
Lemma 11.2

(Lemma for
H
)
.
(
lemma

11.2
));
that is, as all vertices in a single cluster of

have the same neighbourhood, we only need to decide for each vertex of

to which cluster it is assigned.
The first step equips us with a certain flexibility used in a later stage.
For each

-clique in

, we embed a few components of

in the corresponding
k
-partite subgraph of

.
This has the following advantage.
For any two hyperedges

with

and

to

.
Roughly speaking, the same modifications can be done between clusters

that are further apart in

as long as

and

are covered by the edges of the same tight component of

.

In the second step, we utilise a framework version of Komlós’ theorem (

10.1
) to obtain an almost embedding of

into

(ignoring the reduced graph) and thus also into

.
This is somewhat counter-intuitive, since we aim to utilise the Blow-Up Lemma which will embed

back into

. But it efficiently deals with the issue that the clusters

might at this point be quite unbalanced.
Next we ignore that we actually found an embedding and only memorise its induced allocation of almost all the vertices in

to the clusters of

.
Note that we did not use any property of

here, but that

is still a robust tiling framework (due to robustness of

) and the space property of the framework.

In the last step, we allocate the remaining few vertices of

.
Here we work again on the level of

As we will see, every tight and loose component of

constitutes an obstacle for embedding the rest of

.
However, as

-tiling framework, its divisibility property ensures that the number of these components matches the number of flexi-chromatic subtilings contained in

Using these subtilings and exploiting the flexibility within tight components created by our first step, we can then finish our embedding of

into

as desired.
The details of the last two steps can be found in
section

.

Some of our ideas have been inspired by the recent work of the third author and Sanhueza-Matamala
[

]
.

7.

This section contains a summary of a few standard tools regarding embeddings of large sparse substructures in dense graphs,

7.1.
Regular and super-regular pairs

The Regularity Lemma guarantees that every large enough graph
G
admits a reduced graph
R
of bounded complexity, which encodes the approximate structure of
G
.
Before we can make this precise, we introduce the notion of regular pairs.

Let
G
be a graph and
A
and
B
be non-empty, disjoint subsets of
V(G)
.
We write
e_{G}(A,B)
for the number of edges in
G
with one vertex in
A
and one in
B
and define the
density
of the pair
(A,B)
to be
d_{G}(A,B)=e_{G}(A,B)/(|A||B|)
.
The pair
(A,B)
is
\varepsilon
-regular
in
G
if
|d_{G}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})-d_{G}(A,B)|\leqslant\varepsilon
for all
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
|A^{\prime}|\geqslant\varepsilon|A|
and
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
with
|B^{\prime}|\geqslant\varepsilon|B|
.
We say that
(A,B)
is
(\varepsilon,d)
-regular
if it is
\varepsilon
-regular and has density at least
d
.
A vertex
b
in
B
(and analogously in
A
) has
typical degree
in an
(\varepsilon,d)
-regular pair
(A,B)
if
b
has at least
(d-\varepsilon)|A|
neighbours (in
G
); and we say
b
has
atypical degree
otherwise.
We say a pair
(A,B)
is
(\varepsilon,d)
-super-regular
if it is
(\varepsilon,d)
-regular
and contains no vertices of atypical degree (in
A
or
B
).

Let
G
be a graph and
\mathcal{V}=\{V_{i}\}_{i\in[r]}
be a partition of
V(G)
of
size

r
.
We refer to the elements of
\mathcal{V}
as
clusters
.
We say that the partition
\mathcal{V}
is
\kappa
-balanced
(or just

8.1.

In this section, we show three propositions about tiling frameworks.
Recall that a vertex

The following two propositions are adapted from the work of Lang and Sanhueza-Matamala

For sake of completeness, their proofs are included in

.
The first result states that in a robust tiling framework, there are in fact many edges linked to each vertex.

Proposition 8.1

(Supersaturated linkage)
.

Let

and
\varrho>0
,
1/n\ll\mu^{\prime}\ll 1/\chi,\mu
with

.

-robust

-tiling framework on

is linked to at least

.

The second result states that the reduced graph of a robust tiling framework is again a tiling framework.

Proposition 8.2

(Reduced frameworks)
.

Let

and

.
Let

be a
4\mu
-robust

-tiling framework on

Let

be a balanced

-partition, where

.

-robust

We conclude this section, by showing

For the proof, we require the following result proved implicitly by Lang and Sanhueza-Matamala
[

, Proposition 3.3]

Its proof can be found in

Proposition 8.3
.

Let

Proof of
proposition

.

Let

.
By

8.1
, every vertex

can be associated with a subgraph

\mu^{\prime}n^{k}\geqslant 2\mu^{\prime}\binom{n}{k}

By

spans the vertices of

Moreover, by definition of tight connectivity,

.

contains a (potentially spanning) guest graph
H
where
H
has bounded maximum degree and where each component of
H
may be huge, but of smaller magnitude than the order of
G
.
In particular, we study this topic beyond the usual setting where one seeks for the optimal minimum degree bound that guarantees the existence of a copy of
H
in
G
.
This has several advantages as for example it covers many other natural conditions a host graph may satisfy apart from (optimal) minimum degree conditions
and it makes our proof more modular.
Nevertheless, our paper is by no means the end of the story and there are a few interesting questions that remain.

We applied our framework to degree conditions and degree sequences.
A related direction, introduced by Ore
[
38
]
, imposes a lower bound on the sum of the degrees of any two non-adjacent vertices.
Such Ore-type conditions have been investigated for perfect matchings
[
22
]
and
F
-tilings
[
29
]
.
Our framework generally applies to such problems after minor workarounds (see
[
32
]
).
Hence, one can extend the results of Kühn, Osthus and Treglown
[
29
]
to mixed tilings using already established structural insights similar as in the proof of
theorem

5.7
.
On the other hand, in its current state our framework is unable to reproduce these results for
F
-tilings with fixed
F
.
This is because the Ore-type ‘threshold’ that guarantees that every vertex of the host graph lies in a copy of
F
can be significantly lower than the ‘threshold’ for the linkage property, in the cases when
F
is not a bottle.
To overcome this limitation, we can define
F
-frameworks
by replacing the linkage condition with the property that every vertex is on a copy of
F
.
It is then not hard to see that the proof of
theorem

1.7
can be modified to show that robust
F
-frameworks lead to perfect
F
-tilings.
Given this, the results of Kühn, Osthus and Treglown
[
29
]
can be fully replicated extending it to
F
with at most
o(n)
vertices.

As mentioned before, the original Kühn–Osthus Tiling Theorem is more precise in comparison to our work as it is stated in terms of an (additive) constant error.
It is plausible that our results can potentially be refined further via a stability analysis, which could lead to sharper error bounds in certain situations and, in particular, replicate the results of Kühn and Osthus for minimum degree conditions.

Turning to more general questions,
the literature on spanning substructures has so far identified three natural barriers to finding perfect tilings, which correspond to obstacles in space, divisibility and local conditions.
Very recently, Freschi and Treglown
[
14
]
asked whether (in an approximate sense) it can be proved that these are the only obstructions.
Our work solves this ‘meta problem’ for mixed tilings, since the space, divisibility and linkage conditions of
definition

1.6
certify that each of the above obstacles can be (individually) overcome.

In a similar vein, there has also been recently quite some research activity on graph tilings in related settings such as directed graphs
[
9
,
45
]
, ordered graphs
[
5
,
14
]
, multipartite graphs
[
36
]
, random (hyper)graphs
[
12
]
and in the discrepancy setting
[
4
]
.
We expect that many ideas of our paper also apply in these cases and think it is an interesting and fruitful task to set up respective frameworks.

While tilings of graphs have been an integral and well-studied question for graphs, not so much is known for uniform hypergraphs
[
21
,
34
,
37
]
; even an analogous Hajnal–Szemerédi result seems currently out of reach.
It would be very interesting, but potentially also very challenging, to approach hypergraph tilings from a framework viewpoint.
The idea would be to identify similar, rather abstract, conditions as in
definition

1.6
and prove that these are sufficient to find a mixed tiling.
Once such a result is obtained, it only remains to verify that these abstract conditions in fact are satisfied given the host hypergraph admits certain minimum degree properties;
however, this might turn out to be the most difficult part.
For recent progress in this direction see the work of Lang
[
30
]
.

Integrability of SLE via conformal welding of random surfaces
Morris Ang MIT Nina Holden ETH Zürich Xin Sun University of Pennsylvania

Abstract
We demonstrate how to obtain integrability results for the Schramm-Loewner evolution (SLE) from Liouville conformal field theory (LCFT) and the mating-of-trees framework for Liouville quantum gravity (LQG). In particular, we prove an exact formula for the law of a conformal derivative of a classical variant of SLE called SLE κ ⁡ ( ρ − ; ρ + ) \SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+}) . Our proof is built on two connections between SLE, LCFT, and mating-of-trees. Firstly, LCFT and mating-of-trees provide equivalent but complementary methods to describe natural random surfaces in LQG. Using a novel tool that we call the uniform embedding of an LQG surface, we extend earlier equivalence results by allowing fewer marked points and more generic singularities. Secondly, the conformal welding of these random surfaces produces SLE curves as their interfaces. In particular, we rely on the conformal welding results proved in our companion paper [ AHS20 ] . Our paper is an essential part of a program proving integrability results for SLE, LCFT, and mating-of-trees based on these two connections. Keywords: Schramm–Loewner evolution; conformal welding; Liouville quantum gravity; mating-of-trees; Liouville conformal field theory.

Introduction

Two dimensional (2D) conformally invariant random processes have been an active area of research in probability theory for the last two decades. In this paper, we consider the interplay between three central topics in this area: Schramm-Loewner evolution (SLE), Liouville conformal field theory (LCFT), and the mating-of-trees framework for Liouville quantum gravity (LQG).
SLE
[
Sch00
]
is a classical family of random planar curves which describe the scaling limits of many 2D statistical physics model at criticality, e.g.
[
Smi01
,
LSW04
,
SS09
,
CDCH+14
]
.
LQG is a family of random planar geometries
[
DS11
,
DDDF20
,
GM21
]
that naturally arise in the study of string theory and 2D quantum gravity
[
Pol81
]
.
It also describes the scaling limit of a large class of random planar maps, see e.g.
[
She16b
,
GM16
,
HS19
]
.
LCFT is the 2D quantum field theory that governs LQG which is recently made rigorous by
[
DKRV16
]
and follow-up works.
Mating-of-trees
[

]
is an encoding of SLE on the LQG background via Brownian motions.
See
[
Law05
,
BP21
,
Var17
,
Gwy20
,
GHS19
]

and references therein for more background on these rapidly developing topics.

One key feature shared by the three topics is the rich integrable (i.e., exactly solvable) structure.
First, since its discovery, many exact formulas
for SLE have been proved or conjectured; see e.g.
[
SSW09
,
Sch01
,

,
Dub06
,

]

Moreover, as an important example of 2D conformal field theory,

,

,

,

, some of which were recently proved in

,

,

,

,

,

In this paper we demonstrate how to obtain integrable results for SLE from LCFT and mating-of-trees by proving

Our paper is part of a program by the first and the third authors connecting the aforementioned three types of integrable structures and proving new results in each direction.

1.1
An integrability result on
\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})

We now present our main result concerning the integrability of SLE.
For
\kappa>0
and
\rho_{-},\rho_{+}>-2
, the (chordal)
\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
is the natural generalization of the chordal
\SLE_{\kappa}
where one keeps track of two extra marked points on the domain boundary called force points. The parameters
\rho_{\pm}
indicate to what extent the force points attract or repulse the curve.
In our paper the force points are always located infinitesimally to the left and right, respectively, of the starting point of the curve.

\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
was introduced in
[
LSW03
]
and studied in e.g.
[
Dub05
,
MS16
]
.
See Appendix
A.1
for more background.

Let
\eta
be an
\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
curve on the upper half plane
\mathbbm{H}
from
0
to
\infty
, which is a random curve on
\mathbbm{H}\cup\mathbbm{R}
.
When
\rho_{-}>-2
and
\rho_{+}>(-2)\vee(\frac{\kappa}{2}-4)
, then the point
1
is almost surely not on the trace of
\eta

[
MS16
]
.
Therefore, we can define the open set
D
to be the connected component of
\mathbbm{H}\backslash\eta
which contains the point
1
. Let
\psi:D\to\mathbbm{H}
be the conformal map which fixes
\psi(1)=1
and maps the first (resp. last) point on
\partial D
traced by
\eta
to 0 (resp.
\infty
). Note that if
\rho_{-}>\frac{\kappa}{4}-2
then the curve does not touch
(0,\infty)
so that
\psi
will fix
0,1,\infty
in this case.

Our first main result gives the exact distribution of
\psi^{\prime}(1)
in terms of its moment generating function. To describe this result, we need the double gamma function
\Gamma_{b}(z)
which arises in LCFT. We recall its precise definition in (
3.1
). Using
\Gamma_{b}(z)
, we introduce

F(x,\kappa,\rho_{-},\rho_{+}):=\frac{\Gamma_{\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}}{2}}(\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}-\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}}{2}+\frac{\rho_{+}}{\sqrt{\kappa}}+\frac{x}{2})\;\;\Gamma_{\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}}{2}}(\frac{4}{\sqrt{\kappa}}+\frac{\rho_{+}}{\sqrt{\kappa}}-\frac{x}{2})}{\Gamma_{\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}}{2}}(\frac{4}{\sqrt{\kappa}}-\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}}{2}+\frac{\rho_{-}+\rho_{+}}{\sqrt{\kappa}}+\frac{x}{2})\Gamma_{\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}}{2}}(\frac{6}{\sqrt{\kappa}}+\frac{\rho_{-}+\rho_{+}}{\sqrt{\kappa}}-\frac{x}{2})}.

(1.1)

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
\kappa>0
,
\rho_{-}>-2
and
\rho_{+}>(-2)\vee(\frac{\kappa}{2}-4)
. Let
\lambda_{0}=\frac{1}{\kappa}(\rho_{+}+2)(\rho_{+}+4-\frac{\kappa}{2})
.
For any
\lambda<\lambda_{0}
, let
\alpha
be a solution to
1-\frac{\alpha}{2}(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}}{2}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}-\frac{\alpha}{2})=\lambda
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}[\psi^{\prime}(1)^{\lambda}]

\displaystyle=\frac{F(\alpha,\kappa,\rho_{-},\rho_{+})}{F(\sqrt{\kappa},\kappa,\rho_{-},\rho_{+})}.

Moreover, for any
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
we have
\mathbbm{E}[\psi^{\prime}(1)^{\lambda}]=\infty
.

In Theorem
1.1
, the value of
F(\alpha,\kappa,\rho_{-},\rho_{+})
does not depend on which value of
\alpha
is chosen as the solution of the quadratic equation. Moreover, the point
1
in
\psi^{\prime}(1)
is merely for concreteness. The result for other points follows from rescaling.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
does not use stochastic calculus coming from the Loewner evolution definition of
\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
, as is done in many exact calculations concerning SLE, see e.g.
[
Law05
]
.
Instead, we rely on
the following ingredients: the description of natural quantum surfaces in LQG via LCFT; conformal welding of finite volume quantum surfaces from
[
AHS20
]
; the integrability results of Remy and Zhu
[
RZ20a
,
RZ20b
]
on boundary LCFT; and mating-of-trees description of some special quantum surfaces.
We will elaborate on these ingredients in Sections
1.2
and
1.3

1.2
Two perspectives on random surfaces in Liouville quantum gravity

A key ingredient in our proof of Theorem
1.1
is a thorough understanding of two perspectives on random surfaces in LQG when the underlying complex structure enjoys an abundance of conformal symmetries. The first perspective is the quantum surface and the second one is the path integral formalism of LCFT.

We start by recalling some basic geometric concepts in LQG. We will keep the review brief and provide more details and references in Section
2.1
.
The free boundary Gaussian free field (GFF) on a planar domain
D\subsetneq C
is the Gaussian process on
D
with covariance kernel given by the Neumann Green function on
D
, which can be viewed as a random generalized function on
D

[
She07
]
.
There are other variants of the GFF
which have the same regularity.
Suppose
h
is a variant of GFF defined on
D
. The
\gamma
-LQG area measure
\mu_{h}
associated with
h
is formally defined by
e^{\gamma h}d^{2}z
, which is made rigorous by regularization and normalization
[
DS11
]
.

Fix
\gamma\in(0,2)
. Suppose
f:D\rightarrow\widetilde{D}
is a conformal map between two domains
D
and
\widetilde{D}
. For a generalized function
h
on
D
, define

f\bullet_{\gamma}h=h\circ f^{-1}+Q\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|(f^{-1})^{\prime}}}\right|\quad\textrm{where }Q=\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{2}{\gamma}.

(1.2)

If
h
is a variant of GFF, then the pushforward of the
\gamma
-LQG area measure
\mu_{h}
under
f
equals
\mu_{\widetilde{h}}
a.s where
\widetilde{h}=f\bullet_{\gamma}h
.
Equation (
1.2
) is called the coordinate change formula for
\gamma
-LQG.

Suppose
h
is the free boundary GFF on
D
.
If
D
has a boundary segment
L\subset\mathbbm{R}
, then we can define
\gamma
-LQG boundary length measure
\nu_{h}=e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}h}dz
on
L
similarly as
\mu_{h}
.
For general domains, the definition of
\nu_{h}
can be extended via conformal maps and the coordinate change formula (
1.2
). It is also possible to define a random metric on
D
associated with
h
(see
[
DDDF20
,
GM21
]
) but the metric will not be considered in our paper.

In light of the coordinate change formula, Sheffield
[
She16a
]
introduced the notion of quantum surface.
Suppose
h
and
\widetilde{h}
are generalized functions on two domains
D
and
\widetilde{D}
, respectively.
For
\gamma\in(0,2)
, we say that
(D,h)\sim_{\gamma}(\widetilde{D},\widetilde{h})
if there exists a conformal map
f:D\to\widetilde{D}
such that
\widetilde{h}=f\bullet_{\gamma}h
.
A
quantum surface
is an equivalence class of pairs
(D,h)
under this equivalence relation, and an

embedding
of the quantum surface is a choice of
(D,h)
from the equivalence class. We can also consider quantum surfaces decorated
by other structures such as points or curves, via a natural generalization of the equivalence relation; see Section
4.1
.

Liouville conformal field theory (LCFT) is the quantum field theory corresponding to the Liouville action which originates from Polyakov’s work on quantum gravity and bosonic string theory
[
Pol81
]
. It associates a random field to each two dimensional Riemannian manifold which all together form a conformal field theory. LCFT was first rigorously constructed on the sphere by David, Kupiainen, Rhodes and Vargas
[
DKRV16
]
by making sense of the path integral for the Liouville action. It was later extended to other surfaces
[
HRV18
,
Rem18
,
DRV16
,
GRV19
]
.

We will focus on the LCFT on the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}
and the upper half plane
\mathbbm{H}
.
The basic inputs are the Liouville fields
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{C}}
and
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}
.
These are infinite measures on the space of generalized functions on
\mathbbm{C}
and
\mathbbm{H}
, obtained from an additive perturbation of GFF.
See Definitions
2.24
and
2.4
. For
z_{1},\cdots,z_{k}\in\mathbbm{C}
, and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}
,
one can add
insertion
s to
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{C}}
by making sense of
\prod_{i=1}^{k}e^{\alpha_{i}\phi(z_{i})}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{C}}
, which we denote by

\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{C}}^{(z_{1},\alpha_{1}),\cdots,(z_{k},\alpha_{k})}
; see Definition
2.25
.
We can similarly define Liouville fields on
\mathbbm{H}
with insertions, where for
z_{k}\in\partial\mathbbm{H}
,
we need to replace
e^{\alpha_{i}\phi(z_{i})}
by
e^{\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2}\phi(z_{i})}
.
The Liouville correlation functions, which are the fundamental observables in LCFT, are defined in terms of certain averages over these random fields.

Quantum surfaces and LCFT provide two perspectives on random surfaces in LQG.
For those arising as the scaling limit of canonical measures on discrete random surfaces (a.k.a. random planar maps), both perspectives provide natural and instrumental descriptions.
Their close relation has been demonstrated by Aru, Huang and the third author
[
AHS17
]
for the
quantum sphere
and by Cerclé
[
Cer19
]
for the
quantum disk
. The quantum sphere with
k
marked points (Definition
B.2
) is a quantum surface with spherical topology with
k
marked points defined by Duplantier, Miller and Sheffield
[
DMS14
]
. They arise as the scaling limit of natural planar maps models on the sphere; see
[
GHS19
]
for a review.
We similarly have the quantum disk with
m
interior marked points and
n
boundary marked points; see Definition
2.2
.
We use
\mathrm{QS}_{k}
and
\mathrm{QD}_{m,n}
to denote their distributions, respectively. We also write
\mathrm{QS}_{0}
as
\mathrm{QS}
and
\mathrm{QD}_{0,0}
as
\mathrm{QD}
.
Without constraints on area or boundary length, these measures are infinite.
The main result of
[
AHS17
]
says that modulo a multiplicative constant,

\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{C}}^{(z_{1},\gamma),(z_{2},\gamma),(z_{3},\gamma)}
equals
\mathrm{QS}_{3}
embedded on
(\mathbbm{C},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
.
By
[
Cer19
]
, the same holds with
\mathbbm{C}
replaced by
\mathbbm{H}
,
\mathrm{QS}_{3}
replaced by
\mathrm{QD}_{0,3}
, and
z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}
assumed to be on

\partial\mathbbm{H}
.

One major difference between the two perspectives is that
for LCFT, the number of marked points is often assumed to be such that
the marked surface has a unique conformal structure. On the other hand, many important quantum surfaces do not have enough marked points to fix the conformal structure, such as
\mathrm{QS}_{k}
and
\mathrm{QD}_{0,k}
for
k\leq 2
.
The starting point of our paper is the observation that even without enough marked points, Liouville fields, possibly with insertions,
describe natural quantum surfaces that are embedded in a uniformly random way.

To concretely demonstrate our point, let
D
be either a simply connected domain conformally equivalent to
\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}
or
\mathbbm{H}
.
Let
\operatorname{conf}(D)
be the group of conformal automorphisms of
D
where the group multiplication is the function composition
f\cdot g=f\circ g
.
Let
\mathbf{m}_{D}
be a Haar measure on
\operatorname{conf}(D)
, which is both left and right invariant.
Suppose
\mathfrak{f}
is a sample from
\mathbf{m}_{D}
and
h
is a function on
D
.
We call the random function
\mathfrak{f}\bullet_{\gamma}h
the
uniform embedding
of
(D,h)
via
\mathbf{m}_{D}
.
By the invariance property of
\mathbf{m}_{D}
, the law of
\mathfrak{f}\bullet_{\gamma}h
only depends on
(D,h)
as a quantum surface.
We write
\mathbf{m}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}\ltimes\mathrm{QS}
as the law of
\mathfrak{f}\circ h

where
(\mathbbm{C},h)
is an embedding of a sample from the quantum sphere measure
\mathrm{QS}
, and
\mathfrak{f}
is independently sampled from
\mathbf{m}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
.
We call
\mathbf{m}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}\ltimes\mathrm{QS}
the
uniform embedding of
\mathrm{QS}
via
\mathbf{m}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
.
We define
\mathbf{m}_{\mathbbm{H}}\ltimes\mathrm{QD}
in the exact same way.
Here although
\mathbf{m}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}},\mathbf{m}_{\mathbbm{H}},\mathrm{QS},\mathrm{QD}
are only
\sigma
-finite measures,
we adopt probability terminologies such as sample, law, and independence.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
\gamma\in(0,2)
, there exist constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

\mathbf{m}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}\ltimes\mathrm{QS}=C_{1}\cdot\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{C}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathbf{m}_{\mathbbm{H}}\ltimes\mathrm{QD}=C_{2}\cdot\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}.

(1.3)

We can also consider the uniform embedding of quantum surfaces with marked points.
For example, for
a,b\in D\cup\partial D
, let
\operatorname{conf}(D,a,b)
be the subgroup of
\operatorname{conf}(D)
fixing
a,b
and
\mathbf{m}_{D,a,b}
be a Haar measure on
\operatorname{conf}(D,a,b)
.
For example,
\mathrm{QD}_{0,2}
can be identified as a measure on
C^{\infty}_{0}(D)^{\prime}/\operatorname{conf}(D,a,b)
for some domain
D
with boundary points
a,b
, where
\operatorname{conf}(D,a,b)
is the subgroup of
\operatorname{conf}(D)
fixing
a,b
. Then
\mathbf{m}_{D,a,b}\ltimes\mathrm{QD}_{0,2}
can be defined in the same way as
\mathbf{m}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}\ltimes\mathrm{QS}
and

\mathbf{m}_{\mathbbm{H}}\ltimes\mathrm{QD}
. We will prove Theorem
1.2
in Section
2
.
The key to our proof is the LCFT description of the uniform embedding of
\mathrm{QS}_{2}
and
\mathrm{QD}_{0,2}
in the cylinder and strip coordinates:

\begin{split}&\mathbf{m}_{\mathcal{C},-\infty,+\infty}\ltimes\mathrm{QS}_{2}=C\mathrm{LF}_{\mathcal{C}}^{(\gamma,+\infty),(\gamma,-\infty)}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\\
&\mathbf{m}_{\mathcal{S},-\infty,+\infty}\ltimes\mathrm{QD}_{0,2}=C\mathrm{LF}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\gamma,-\infty),(\gamma,+\infty)}\end{split}

(1.4)

where
\mathcal{C}
is a horizontal cylinder and
\mathcal{S}
is a horizontal strip.
Although this is essentially equivalent to the results in
[
AHS17
]
and
[
Cer19
]

our proof is much shorter. Thanks to the choice of coordinate,
the identities (
1.4
) are equivalent to an interesting fact about drifted Brownian motion that we prove as Proposition
2.14
.
This fact also gives the analogous result if the singularity at the marked points is more general
(see Section
1.3
and Definition
2.1
).

Our method for proving Theorem
1.2
is also used to give the LCFT description of
\mathrm{QD}_{1,0}
in
[
ARS21
, Section 3]
. It can be extended to quantum surfaces decorated with SLE curves. For example, in
[
AHS
]
we proved that the SLE loop coupled with
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{C}}
is the uniform embedding of the welding of two independent copies of
\mathrm{QD}
.

The LCFT description of quantum surfaces has two advantages. First, it is a common operation to add marked points to quantum surfaces according to
some quantum intrinsic measure. This operation is tractable on the LCFT side via the Girsanov theorem; see Section
2.4
. The second advantage is that LCFT correlation functions are exactly solvable. In Section
1.3
we will explain how these ideas can be applied to prove Theorem
1.1
.

1.3
Integrability of SLE through conformal welding and LCFT

The starting point of our proof of Theorem
1.1
is the conformal welding result we proved in
[
AHS20
]
.
For
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
\kappa=\gamma^{2}\in(0,4)
,
if we run an independent
\SLE_{\kappa}
on top of a certain type of

\gamma
-LQG quantum surface, the two quantum surfaces on the two sides of the SLE curve are independent.
Moreover, the original curve-decorated quantum surface can be recovered by gluing the two smaller quantum surfaces according to the quantum boundary lengths.
The recovering procedure is called
conformal welding
. Such results were
first established by Sheffield
[
She16a
]
and later extended in
[
DMS14
]
.
They play a fundamental role in the mating-of-trees theory.

In
[
AHS20
]
we proved conformal welding results
for a family of finite-area quantum surfaces, generalizing their infinite-volume counterpart proved in
[
She16a
]
and
[
DMS14
]
.
We recall them now.
For
W>0
, let
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)
be the 2-pointed quantum disk of weight
W
introduced in
[
DMS14
]
; see Section
2.1
.
For
W\geq\gamma^{2}/2
,
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)
is an infinite measure on quantum surfaces with two boundary marked points. The log-singularity of the field at each marked point is
-\beta\log|\cdot|
where

\beta=Q+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{W}{\gamma}.

(1.5)

The 2-pointed quantum disk
\mathrm{QD}_{0,2}
is the special case of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)
where
W=2
. For
W\in(0,\gamma^{2}/2)
,

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)
is a Poissonian collection of samples from
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(\gamma^{2}-W)
, viewed as an ordered chain of 2-pointed quantum surfaces.

In
[
AHS20
]
, we showed that the conformal welding of independent samples from
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W_{-})
and
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W_{+})

gives a sample from
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W_{-}+W_{+})
decorated by an independent SLE
{}_{\kappa}(W_{-}-2;W_{+}-2)
running between the two marked point.
To put it more formally, we can write this result as

\begin{split}&\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{-}+W_{+})\otimes\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})\\
&\qquad=c_{W_{-},W_{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{Weld}(\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{-};\cdot,x),\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{+};x,\cdot))\,dx.\end{split}

(1.6)

In (
1.6
),
\rho_{-}=W_{-}-2
,
\rho_{+}=W_{+}-2
, and
c_{W_{-},W_{+}}
is a positive constant which we call the
welding constant
.
The measure
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{-};\cdot,x)
is defined by the disintegration
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{-})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{-};\cdot,x)\,dx
where
x
represents the quantum length of the right boundary arc.
We similarly define
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{+};x,\cdot)
for the left boundary. The operator
\operatorname{Weld}
means conformal welding along the boundary arc with length
x
.
See Section
4.1
for more details on (
1.6
).

At the highest level, our proof of Theorem
1.1
is done in four steps.

1.

Use LCFT to define a variant of
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)
where we add a third boundary marked point with a generic log singularity.

2.

Prove a version of the conformal welding equation (
1.6
) for the three-point variant of
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)
.

3.

Show that the welding constants encode the moments of
\psi^{\prime}(1)
in Theorem
1.1
.

4.

Use the integrability from LCFT and mating-of-trees to compute the welding constants.

We now summarize the key ideas and inputs for implementing the 4-step proof.
To keep the picture simple, we first assume that
W_{-}\geq\gamma^{2}/2
and
W_{+}\geq\gamma^{2}/2
in (
1.6
).
In this case the
\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
curve does not touch the boundary of the weight
(W_{-}+W_{+})
quantum disk.

).

We define
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2,\bullet}(W;\alpha)

to be the measure on quantum surfaces such that after being embedded in
(\mathbbm{H},0,\infty,1)
, the field is distributed as
\frac{\gamma}{2(Q-\beta)^{2}}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta,0),(\beta,\infty),(\alpha,1)}
. For
W=2
and
\alpha=\gamma
, by
[
Cer19
]
,

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(2;\gamma)
agrees with
\mathrm{QD}_{0,3}
.
As alluded in Section
1.2
, we give a concise proof of this result which also extends to surfaces with other singularities. The new method also allows us to show that

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2,\bullet}(W;\gamma)
for a general
W\geq\gamma^{2}/2
is obtained by adding to
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)

an extra point on the right boundary according to the quantum length measure.

We then extend the welding equation (
1.6
).
For all
\alpha\in\mathbbm{R}
we prove that

\begin{split}&\mathcal{M}_{2,\bullet}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{-}+W_{+};\alpha)\otimes\mathsf{m}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+};\alpha)\\
&=c_{W_{-},W_{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{Weld}(\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{-};\cdot,x),\mathcal{M}_{2,\bullet}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{+};\alpha;x,\cdot))\,dx.\end{split}

(1.7)

Here,
\mathsf{m}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+};\alpha)
is a measure on curves obtained from reweighting
\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
by
\psi^{\prime}(0)^{1-\Delta}
with
\Delta=\frac{\alpha}{2}(Q-\frac{\alpha}{2})
and
\psi
as defined in Theorem
1.1
.
For
\alpha=\gamma
, this equation is straightforward from (
1.6
) by adding a quantum typical point on the right boundary. For general
\alpha
, this follows from an application of the Girsanov theorem.
The extra factor of
\psi^{\prime}(0)^{1-\Delta}
arises in a similar fashion as the
Q\log
term
in the
\gamma
-LQG coordinate change formula (
1.2
).

By definition, the total mass of
\mathsf{m}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+};\alpha)
equals
\mathbbm{E}[\psi^{\prime}(0)^{1-\Delta}]
.
Therefore, forgetting the curve in (
1.7
), the integral

\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{Weld}(\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{-};\cdot,x),\mathcal{M}_{2,\bullet}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{+};\alpha;x,\cdot))\,dx

equals
C(\alpha)\mathcal{M}_{2,\bullet}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{-}+W_{+};\alpha)
as measures on quantum surfaces, where
C(\alpha)=c_{W_{-},W_{+}}^{-1}\mathbbm{E}[\psi^{\prime}(0)^{1-\Delta}]
.
To determine
C(\alpha)
, we only need to match the distribution of a single observable on both sides.
The one we choose is the left boundary length of
\mathcal{M}_{2,\bullet}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{-}+W_{+};\alpha)
.

Let
L
and
R
be the left and right, respectively, boundary lengths of a sample from
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)
.
Then both of
\mathcal{M}_{2,\bullet}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W;\alpha)[e^{-sL}]
and
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)[1-e^{-s_{1}L-s_{2}R}]
are LCFT correlation functions computed by Remy and Zhu
[
RZ20a
,
RZ20b
]
.
In particular, let
R(\beta;s_{1},s_{2})=\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)[1-e^{-s_{1}L-s_{2}R}]
with
W=\gamma(Q+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\beta)
.
Then
R
is the so-called
boundary reflection coefficient
.
This allows us to compare the left boundary length of
\mathcal{M}_{2,\bullet}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{-}+W_{+};\alpha)
on both sides of (
1.7
) and express
\mathbbm{E}[\psi^{\prime}(0)^{1-\Delta}]

in terms of certain explicitly known LCFT correlation functions.

Due to the integration on the right side of (
1.7
) the expression for
\mathbbm{E}[\psi^{\prime}(0)^{1-\Delta}]
using LCFT is far from the neat product form in Theorem
1.1
.
However, for
W_{-}=2
or
\gamma^{2}/2
, the mating-of-trees theory provides a simple description of the area and boundary lengths distribution of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W_{-})
in terms of 2D Brownian motion in cones. The case with
W_{-}=2
is known from
[
AG21
]
and
[
DMS14
]
.
The case with
W_{-}=\gamma^{2}/2
is obtained in our paper
[
AHS20
]
.
This allows us to prove Theorem
1.1
for
\kappa\in(0,4)
,
\rho_{-}\in\{0,\kappa/2-2\}
, and
\rho_{+}\geq\kappa/2-2

(recall that
\rho_{-}=W_{-}-2
and
\kappa=\gamma^{2}
).

The same argument can also be run when
W_{+}\in(0,\gamma^{2}/2)
to cover the range
\rho_{+}\in(-2,\kappa/2-2)
.
For
W\in(0,\gamma^{2}/2)
,
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)
is a chain of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(\gamma^{2}-W)
-quantum disks.
In this case we can still define
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2,\bullet}(W;\alpha)
. This new quantum surface is not so natural from the perspective of either
[
DMS14
]
or
[
DKRV16
]
but it becomes natural after we combine the two.
Due to Campbell’s formula for Poisson point process,
both of the boundary length distributions of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)
and
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2,\bullet}(W;\alpha)
can be computed in terms of their counterparts with
W
replaced by
\gamma^{2}-W
. This allows us to carry out the proof as before. Our computation shows that the boundary length distribution of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)
in the thin regime is an analytic continuation of the boundary length distribution in the thick regime, which provides
a probabilistic counterpart for a well-known numerical fact on the reflection coefficient:
R(\beta;s_{1},s_{2})R(2Q-\beta;s_{1},s_{2})=1
.

To prove the general case of Theorem
1.1
, we consider the pair of SLE
curves which are the interfaces when conformally welding
\mathcal{M}_{2}(W_{1})
,
\mathcal{M}_{2}(W_{2})
, and
\mathcal{M}_{2}(W_{3})
.
This allow us to derive a multiplicative relation on
\mathbbm{E}[\psi^{\prime}(1)^{\lambda}]
with different parameters. Specializing to
W_{1}=2
or
W_{1}=\gamma^{2}/2
and
using the proved case of Theorem
1.1
with
\rho_{-}=W_{1}-2
, we obtain two functional equations on

\mathbbm{E}[\psi^{\prime}(1)^{\lambda}]
. In the
\rho_{-}
-variable,
it is a pair of explicit shift equations relating the value of
\mathbbm{E}[\psi^{\prime}(1)^{\lambda}]
at
\rho_{-}
to the value at
\rho_{-}+\gamma^{2}/2
or
\rho_{-}+2
.
Setting
\beta=Q+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{W_{1}}{\gamma}
as in (
1.5
), the two shifts in
\rho_{-}

transfer to
\beta\rightarrow\beta+\frac{\gamma}{2}
and
\beta\rightarrow\beta+\frac{2}{\gamma}
, respectively. Interestingly, the numerical values
\frac{\gamma}{2},\frac{2}{\gamma}
for the shifts turn out to be exactly those appearing in shift relations for DOZZ formula
[
DO94
,
ZZ96
,
Tes95
,
KRV20
]
and other correlation functions in LCFT (see e.g.
[
KRV20
,
RZ20b
]
).

Similarly as in the LCFT context,
if
\kappa=\gamma^{2}
is irrational, then this pair of shift relations has a unique meromorphic solution. On the other hand, we can check that the explicit function in Theorem
1.1
is such a solution. This gives Theorem
1.1
for irrational
\kappa\in(0,4)
. By a standard continuity argument, it extends to all
\kappa\in(0,4]
. Finally the result for
\kappa>4
follows from the SLE duality
[
Zha08
,
Dub09a
,
MS16
]
.

The core of the argument outlined above is to compare boundary lengths of quantum surfaces
on the two sides of the conformal welding equation (
1.7
).
It is equally interesting to compare quantum area and to consider quantum surfaces with marked points in the bulk.
This idea is explored in
[
ARS21
]
by the first and the third author with Remy to prove the
Fateev-Zamolodchikov-Zamolodchikov (FZZ) formula
[
FZZ00
]
for the one-point disk partition function of LCFT.
Moreover in
[
AS21
]
of the first and the third authors, this idea is used to prove two integrable results
on the conformal loop ensemble (CLE). One result relates the three-point correlation function of CLE on the sphere
[
IJS16
]
to the DOZZ formula in LCFT.
The other addresses a conjecture of Kenyon and Wilson (recorded in
[
SSW09
]
) on the electrical thickness of CLE loops.

We will frequently consider non-probability measures and extend the terminology of probability theory to this setting.
In particular, suppose
M
is a measure on a measurable space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
such that
M(\Omega)
is not necessarily
1
, and
X
is an
\mathcal{F}
-measurable function.
Then we say that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
is a sample space and that
X
is a random variable. We call the pushforward measure
M_{X}=X_{*}M
the
law
of
X
.
We say that
X
is
sampled
from
M_{X}
. We also write
\int f(x)\,M_{X}(dx)
as
M_{X}[f]
or

for simplicity.
For a finite positive measure

2.1
Preliminaries on the Gaussian free field and quantum surfaces

We recall the Gaussian free field (GFF) on the upper half-plane
\mathbbm{H}
and the horizontal strip
\mathcal{S}=\mathbbm{R}\times(0,\pi)
. For
\mathcal{X}\in\{\mathbbm{H},\mathcal{S}\}
, we fix a finite measure
m
on
\mathcal{X}
.
Consider the Dirichlet inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{\nabla}:=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\nabla f\cdot\nabla g
.
Let
H(\mathcal{X})
be the Hilbert space closure of
\{f\textrm{ is smooth on }\mathcal{X}\textrm{ and }\int_{\mathcal{X}}f\,dm=0\}

with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\nabla}
.
Let
(\xi_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables and
(f_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis for
H(\mathcal{X})
. Then the summation

h_{\mathcal{X}}:=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}f_{i}

(2.1)

does not converge in
H(\mathcal{X})
but a.s. converges in the space of distributions
[
DMS14
, Section 4.1.4]
.
We call
h_{\mathcal{X}}
a GFF on
\mathcal{X}
with normalization
\int_{\mathcal{X}}h\,dm=0
, and denote its law by
P_{\mathcal{X}}
.

In this paper for each
\mathcal{X}\in\{\mathbbm{H},\mathcal{S}\}
we will only consider one normalization measure
m
.
For
\mathcal{X}=\mathbbm{H}
, it is the uniform measure on the unit semi-circle centered at the origin.
For
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{S}
, it is the uniform measure on
\{0\}\times(0,\pi)
.
This way,
h_{\mathcal{S}}
and
h_{\mathbbm{H}}
are related by the exponential map between
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathbbm{H}
. It will be convenient to have their explicit covariance kernels
G_{\mathcal{X}}(z,w)=\mathbbm{E}[h_{\mathcal{X}}(x)h_{\mathcal{X}}(y)]
:

\displaystyle G_{\mathbbm{H}}(z,w)=-\log|z-w|-\log|z-\overline{w}|+2\log|z|_{+}+2\log|w|_{+}.

(2.2)

\displaystyle G_{\mathcal{S}}(z,w)=-\log|e^{z}-e^{w}|-\log|e^{z}-e^{\overline{w}}|+\max(2\Re z,0)+\max(2\Re w,0).

Here
|z|_{+}
means
\max\{|z|,1\}
. Moreover,
G_{\mathcal{X}}(z,w)=\mathbbm{E}[h_{\mathcal{X}}(x)h_{\mathcal{X}}(y)]
means that
for any compactly supported test function
\rho
on
\mathcal{X}
, the variance of
(h,\rho)
is
\iint_{\mathcal{X}}G_{\mathcal{X}}(z,w)\rho(z)\rho(w)\,d^{2}z\,d^{2}w
.
See
[
RZ20b
, Definition 1.1]
for (
2.2
) in the case of
G_{\mathbbm{H}}
, and the other identity follows from
G_{\mathcal{S}}(z,w)=G_{\mathbbm{H}}(e^{z},e^{w})
.

We now recall the radial-lateral decomposition of
h_{\mathcal{S}}
.
Let
H_{1}(\mathcal{S})\subset H(\mathcal{S})
(resp.
H_{2}(\mathcal{S})\subset H(\mathcal{S})
) be the subspace of functions which are constant (resp. have mean zero) on
\{t\}\times[0,\pi]
for each
t\in\mathbbm{R}
.
This gives the orthogonal decomposition
H(\mathcal{S})=H_{1}(\mathcal{S})\oplus H_{2}(\mathcal{S})
.
If we write
h_{\mathcal{S}}=h^{1}_{\mathcal{S}}+h^{2}_{\mathcal{S}}
with
h^{1}_{\mathcal{S}}\in H_{1}(\mathcal{S})
and
h^{2}_{\mathcal{S}}\in H_{2}(\mathcal{S})
, then
h^{1}_{\mathcal{S}}
and
h^{2}_{\mathcal{S}}
are independent. Moreover,
\{h^{1}_{\mathcal{S}}(t)\}_{t\in\mathbbm{R}}
has the distribution of
\{B_{2t}\}_{t\geq\mathbbm{R}}
where
B_{t}
is a standard two-sided Brownian motion. See
[
DMS14
, Section 4.1.6]
for more details.

We now recall the concept of a quantum surface.
For
n\in\mathbbm{N}
, consider tuples
(D,h,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
such that
D\subset\mathbbm{C}
is a domain,
h
is a distribution on
D
, and

z_{i}\in\cup D\cup\partial D
. Let
(\widetilde{D},\widetilde{h},\widetilde{z}_{1},\cdots,\widetilde{z}_{n})
be another such tuple.
We say

(D,h,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\sim_{\gamma}(\widetilde{D},\widetilde{h},\widetilde{z}_{1},\cdots,\widetilde{z}_{n})

if there is a conformal map
\psi:\widetilde{D}\to D
such that
\widetilde{h}=f\bullet_{\gamma}h=h\circ f^{-1}+Q\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|(f^{-1})^{\prime}}}\right|
and
\psi(\widetilde{z}_{i})=z_{i}
for all
i
.
An equivalence class for
\sim_{\gamma}
is called a quantum surface with
n
marked points.
We write
(D,h,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})/{\sim_{\gamma}}
as the marked quantum surface represented by
(D,h,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
.
When it is clear from context, we simply let
(D,h,z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
denote the marked quantum surface it represents.

Suppose
\phi
is a random function on
\mathbbm{H}
which can be written as
h+g
where
h
is sampled from
P_{\mathbbm{H}}
and

g
a possibly random function that is continuous on
\mathbbm{H}\cup\partial\mathbbm{H}
except at finitely many points.
For
\varepsilon>0
and
z\in\mathbbm{H}\cup\partial\mathbbm{H}
, we write
\phi_{\varepsilon}(z)
for the average of
\phi
on
\partial B_{\varepsilon}(z)\cap\mathbbm{H}
, and define the random measure
\mu_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon^{\gamma^{2}/2}e^{\gamma\phi_{\varepsilon}(z)}\,d^{2}z
on
\mathbbm{H}
, where
d^{2}z
is Lebesgue measure on
\mathbbm{H}
. Almost surely, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the measures
\mu_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
converge weakly to a limiting measure
\mu_{\phi}
called the
quantum area measure

[
DS11
,
SW16
]
.
We also define the
quantum boundary length measure

\nu_{\phi}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\gamma^{2}/4}e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)}dx
.
Suppose
f:\mathbbm{H}\to D
is a conformal map and
\widetilde{\phi}=f\bullet_{\gamma}\phi
. If
D=\mathbbm{H}
,
then
\mu_{\widetilde{\phi}}
is the pushforward of
\mu_{\phi}
under
f
and the same holds for
\nu_{\widetilde{\phi}}
.
We can use this to unambiguously extend the definition of the quantum area and boundary length measures to any

(D,\widetilde{\phi})
that is equivalent to
(\mathbbm{H},\phi)
as a quantum surface.

[

]
.

Let

Remark 2.3
.

For
\mathcal{X}\in\{\mathbbm{H},\mathcal{S}\}
, let

be a smooth metric on
\mathcal{X}
such that
the metric completion of
(\mathcal{X},g)
is a compact Riemannian manifold.
Let

be the Sobolev space whose norm is the sum of the

(\mathcal{X},g)
and the Dirichlet energy.
Let
H^{-1}(\mathcal{X})
be the dual space of

.
Then the function space

and its topology does not depend on the choice of

, and is a Polish (i.e. complete separable metric) space.

is supported on

. This follows from a straightforward adaptation of results in
[

,
Dub09b
]

Random functions on

in our paper, such as the ones in Definition

;

Both of these functions belong to

. So we view their laws as measures on the Polish space

.

2.2
Preliminaries on Liouville conformal field theory

In this section we review some random fields arising in the context of LCFT.
We define the Liouville field on
\mathcal{X}\in\{\mathbbm{H},\mathcal{S}\}
with boundary insertions following
[
HRV18
,
RZ20b
]
.
We will not discuss bulk insertions as they are not needed here.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(h,\mathbf{c})
be sampled from
P_{\mathbbm{H}}\times[e^{-Qc}dc]
and set
\phi=h(z)-2Q\log|z|_{+}+\mathbf{c}
.
We write
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}
as the law of
\phi
and call a sample from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}
a
Liouville field on
\mathbbm{H}
.

Let
(\beta_{i},s_{i})\in\mathbbm{R}\times\partial\mathbbm{H}
for
i=1,\dots,m
, where
m\geq 1
and the
s_{i}
are distinct.
The Liouville field with insertions
(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}

is defined formally by
\prod_{i=1}^{m}e^{\frac{\beta_{i}}{2}\phi(s_{i})}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}(d\phi)
.
To make it rigorous we need to replace
e^{\frac{\beta}{2}\phi(s)}
by the regularization
\varepsilon^{\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}\phi_{\varepsilon}(s)}

and send
\varepsilon\to 0
.
We first give a definition without taking limit and then justify it in the subsequent lemma.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(\beta_{i},s_{i})\in\mathbbm{R}\times\partial\mathbbm{H}
for
i=1,\dots,m
, where
m\geq 1
and the
s_{i}
are pairwise distinct. Let
(h,\mathbf{c})
be sampled from
C_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{i}}P_{\mathbbm{H}}\times[e^{(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\beta_{i}-Q)c}dc]
where

C_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}|s_{i}|_{+}^{-\beta_{i}(Q-\frac{\beta_{i}}{2})}e^{\sum_{j=i+1}^{m}\frac{\beta_{i}\beta_{j}}{4}G_{\mathbbm{H}}(s_{i},s_{j})}.

Let
\phi(z)=h(z)-2Q\log|z|_{+}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\beta_{i}}{2}G_{\mathbbm{H}}(z,s_{i})+\mathbf{c}
. We write
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{i}}
for the law of
\phi
and call a sample from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{i}}
the
Liouville field on
\mathbbm{H}
with insertions
(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
s\notin\{s_{1},\cdots,s_{m}\}
. Then
in the topology of vague convergence of measures, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}\phi_{\varepsilon}(s)}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{i}}(d\phi)=\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{i},(\beta,s)}.

(2.3)

Proof.

Consider bounded continuous functions
f
on
H^{-1}(\mathbbm{H})
and
g
on
\mathbbm{R}
, and suppose
g
is compactly supported. For
h
sampled from
P_{\mathbbm{H}}
let
\widetilde{\phi}:=h+\sum_{i}\frac{\beta_{i}}{2}G(\cdot,s_{i})-2Q\log|\cdot|_{+}
and let
\mathbbm{E}_{\mathbbm{H}}
denote the expectation over
P_{\mathbbm{H}}
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}C_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{i}}\int_{\mathbbm{R}}\mathbbm{E}_{\mathbbm{H}}[\varepsilon^{\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}(\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(s)+c)}f(\widetilde{\phi})g(c)]e^{(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\beta_{i}-Q)c}\,dc

\displaystyle=|s|_{+}^{\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}-Q\beta}e^{\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i}\beta\beta_{i}G_{\mathbbm{H}}(s,s_{i})}C_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{i}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbbm{R}}\mathbbm{E}_{\mathbbm{H}}[e^{\frac{\beta}{2}h_{\varepsilon}(s)}]^{-1}\mathbbm{E}_{\mathbbm{H}}[e^{\frac{\beta}{2}h_{\varepsilon}(s)}f(\widetilde{\phi})g(c)]e^{(\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\beta_{i}-Q)c}\,dc

\displaystyle=C_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta,s),(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{i}}\int_{\mathbbm{R}}\mathbbm{E}_{\mathbbm{H}}[f(\widetilde{\phi}+\frac{\beta}{2}G_{\mathbbm{H}}(\cdot,s))g(c)]e^{(\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\beta_{i}-Q)c}\,dc.

The first equality follows from expanding the definition of
\widetilde{\phi}
and noting that
\Var(h_{\varepsilon}(s))=-2\log\varepsilon+4\log|s|_{+}+o(1)
so

\mathbbm{E}[e^{\frac{\beta}{2}h_{\varepsilon}(s)}]=(1+o(1))\varepsilon^{-\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}}|s|_{+}^{\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}}.

For the second equality, we have the prefactor

|s|_{+}^{\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}-Q\beta}e^{\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i}\beta\beta_{i}G_{\mathbbm{C}}(s,s_{i})}C_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{i}}=C_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta,s),(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{i}}

by definition. Moreover,
Girsanov’s theorem gives
\mathbbm{E}_{\mathbbm{H}}[e^{\frac{\beta}{2}h_{\varepsilon}(s)}]^{-1}\mathbbm{E}_{\mathbbm{H}}[e^{\frac{\beta}{2}h_{\varepsilon}(s)}f(\widetilde{\phi})]=\mathbbm{E}_{\mathbbm{H}}[f(\widetilde{\phi}+\frac{\beta}{2}G_{\mathbbm{H},\varepsilon}(\cdot,s))]
where
G_{\mathbbm{H},\varepsilon}(w,s)
is the average of
G_{\mathbbm{H}}(w,\cdot)
on
\partial B_{\varepsilon}(s)\cap\mathbbm{H}
. Since
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{\mathbbm{H},\varepsilon}(\cdot,s)\to G_{\mathbbm{H}}(\cdot,s)
in
H^{-1}(\mathbbm{H})
, the equality follows from the bounded convergence theorem.
∎

Definitions

LCFT on the half-plane is conformally covariant. To state this, for a measure

Proposition 2.7
.

For
\beta\in\mathbbm{R}
, set
\Delta_{\beta}:=\frac{\beta}{2}(Q-\frac{\beta}{2})
.
Let

Then

Proof.

Theorem 3.5 in
[
HRV18
]
is stated for LCFT on the unit disk, but the result holds also for LCFT on
\mathbbm{H}
by their Proposition 3.7. Rephrasing using

in
[

Lemma 2.9
.

With notation as in Definition
2.8
, we have the convergence in the vague topology on measures on

(see Remark

)

\lim_{r\to+\infty}r^{\beta(Q-\frac{\beta}{2})}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta,r),(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{i}}=\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta,\infty),(\beta_{i},s_{i})_{i}}.

Proof.

In the topology of

, we have

as

. Thus we have
h-2Q\log|\cdot|_{+}+\frac{\beta}{2}G_{\mathbbm{H}}(\cdot,r)+\sum_{i=2}^{m}\frac{\beta_{i}}{2}G_{\mathbbm{H}}(\cdot,s_{i})\to h+(\beta-2Q)\log|\cdot|_{+}+\sum_{i=2}^{m}\frac{\beta_{i}}{2}G_{\mathbbm{H}}(\cdot,s_{i})

When

Definition 2.10

Our next lemma explains how the fields of Definitions

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbbm{R}
and
(\beta_{3},s_{3})\in\mathbbm{R}\times\partial\mathcal{S}
, then

\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta,\infty),(\beta,0),(\beta_{3},e^{s_{3}})}=e^{-\frac{\beta_{3}}{2}(Q-\frac{\beta_{3}}{2})\Re s_{3}}\exp_{*}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\beta,\pm\infty),(\beta_{3},s_{3})}.

Similarly, if
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\in\mathbbm{R}
and
f\in\mathrm{Conf}(\mathbbm{H})
satisfies

\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta_{1},\infty),(\beta_{2},0),(\beta_{3},1)}=2^{\Delta_{\beta_{1}}-\Delta_{\beta_{2}}+\Delta_{\beta_{3}}}\cdot f_{*}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\beta_{1},-1),(\beta_{2},0),(\beta_{3},1)}.

Proof.

If
h
is sampled from
P_{\mathcal{S}}
then
\widetilde{h}:=h\circ\log
has law

, and
G_{\mathcal{S}}(z,w)=G_{\mathbbm{H}}(e^{z},e^{w})
. Thus

\begin{split}&\widetilde{h}(\cdot)+(\beta-2Q)\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\cdot}}\right|_{+}+\frac{\beta}{2}G_{\mathbbm{H}}(\cdot,0)+\frac{\beta_{3}}{2}G_{\mathbbm{H}}(\cdot,e^{s_{3}})\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad=\exp\bullet_{\gamma}(h(\cdot)-(Q-\beta)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Re\cdot}}\right|+\frac{\beta_{3}}{2}G_{\mathcal{S}}(\cdot,s_{3})).\end{split}

Combining this with

For

. By Proposition

and using the

as

.
The

by Lemma

in the topology of uniform convergence of an analytic function and its derivative on compact sets, we have

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\mathbbm{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
denote the expectation over the probability measure
P_{\mathcal{X}}
for
\mathcal{X}\in\{\mathbbm{H},\mathcal{S}\}
.
Let
\mathbbm{E}_{\mathcal{S}}[\nu_{h}(du)]
be the measure on
\mathbbm{R}
given by
A\mapsto\mathbbm{E}_{\mathcal{S}}[\nu_{h}(A)]
.
We similarly define
\mathbbm{E}_{\mathbbm{H}}[\nu_{h}(du)]
. Then

\begin{split}C^{(\beta,\pm\infty),(\gamma,u)}_{\mathcal{S}}du&=e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}(-(Q-\beta)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Re u}}\right|)}\mathbbm{E}_{\mathcal{S}}[\nu_{h}(du)],\quad\\
C^{(\gamma,u)}_{\mathbbm{H}}du&=e^{-2\gamma Q\log|u|_{+}}\mathbbm{E}_{\mathbbm{H}}[\nu_{h}(du)].\end{split}

Proof.

We present the argument for the first identity; the other uses an identical argument. For any smooth compactly supported function
g:\mathbbm{R}\to\mathbbm{R}
, by
[
Ber17
, Theorem 1.1]
we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbbm{R}}e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}(-(Q-\beta)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Re u}}\right|)}g(u)\mathbbm{E}_{\mathcal{S}}[\nu_{h}(du)]\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbbm{R}}e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}(-(Q-\beta)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Re u}}\right|)}g(u)\mathbbm{E}_{\mathcal{S}}[\varepsilon^{\gamma^{2}/4}e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}h_{\varepsilon}(u)}]\,du.\end{split}

(2.4)

Now, since
\Var(h_{\varepsilon}(u))=-2\log\varepsilon+2\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Re u}}\right|+o_{\varepsilon}(1)
, we have
\mathbbm{E}_{\mathcal{S}}[\varepsilon^{\gamma^{2}/4}e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}h_{\varepsilon}(u)}]=(1+o_{\varepsilon}(1))e^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Re u}}\right|}
, where the error terms are uniformly small for
u
in the support of
g
. Therefore the limit in (
2.4
) equals
\int_{\mathbbm{R}}e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}(-(Q-\beta)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Re u}}\right|)}g(u)e^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Re u}}\right|}\,du=\int_{\mathbbm{R}}g(u)C^{(\beta,\pm\infty),(\gamma,u)}_{\mathcal{S}}\,du
, as desired.
∎

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.13

Proposition 2.14

Fix

•

Let

Let
(\widetilde{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}

Lemma 2.15
.

Let

(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}

Consider the excursion measure
\Lambda

follows from comparing two ways of representing

We first give a precise definition of the notation
\ltimes

G
be a locally compact Lie group.
Suppose
\Omega

\Omega/G

Suppose

\int_{G}f(gh)\,\mathbf{m}(dg)=\int_{G}f(g)\,\mathbf{m}(dg)

For each

3
Quantum surface and Liouville correlation function

In this section we consider disks with two or three marked boundary points and we derive the law of boundary lengths of these surfaces. More specifically, we consider surfaces sampled from
\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)
and
\mathcal{M}_{2,\bullet}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W;\alpha)
, both thick and thin variants. The proofs are based on the integrability of boundary LCFT from
[
RZ20b
]
. Interestingly, we will see in Propositions
3.6
and
3.12
below that the same formulas apply for thick and thin variants of the same disk, which provides a probabilistic interpretation of identities satisfied by the reflection coefficient of boundary LCFT.

3.1
Reflection coefficient and thick quantum disk

We recall the double gamma function
\Gamma_{b}(z)
which is prevalent in LCFT. See e.g.
[
Spr09
]
for more detail.
For
b>0
,
\Gamma_{b}(z)
is the meromorphic function in
\mathbbm{C}
such that for
\Re z>0
,

\ln\Gamma_{b}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{e^{-zt}-e^{-(b+\frac{1}{b})t/2}}{(1-e^{-bt})(1-e^{-\frac{1}{b}t})}-\frac{(\frac{1}{2}(b+\frac{1}{b})-z)^{2}}{2}e^{-t}+\frac{z-\frac{1}{2}(b+\frac{1}{b})}{t}}}\right)\,dt

(3.1)

and it satisfies the shift equations

\frac{\Gamma_{b}(z)}{\Gamma_{b}(z+b)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Gamma(bz)b^{-bz+\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\frac{\Gamma_{b}(z)}{\Gamma_{b}(z+\frac{1}{b})}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Gamma(\frac{1}{b}z)(\frac{1}{b})^{-\frac{1}{b}z+\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.2)

These shift equations allow us to meromorphically extend
\Gamma_{b}(z)
from
\{\Re z>0\}
to
\mathbbm{C}
, where it has simple poles at
-nb-m\frac{1}{b}
for nonnegative integers
m,n
. We also define the double sine function

S_{b}(z):=\frac{\Gamma_{b}(z)}{\Gamma_{b}(b+\frac{1}{b}-z)}.

We will only work with
\Gamma_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}
, except in the proof of Lemma
5.12
, where
\Gamma_{\frac{2}{\gamma}}
also appears. Inspecting (
3.1
), we see that
\Gamma_{\frac{2}{\gamma}}=\Gamma_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}
.

We can now recall the boundary Liouville reflection coefficient from
[
RZ20b
]
.
For
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
, let
\sigma_{j}\in\mathbbm{C}
be such that
\mu_{j}=e^{i\pi\gamma(\sigma_{j}-\frac{Q}{2})}
and
\Re\sigma_{j}=\frac{Q}{2}
for
j=1,2
. Let

\displaystyle\begin{split}\overline{R}(\beta,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=&\,\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(Q-\beta)-\frac{1}{2}}(\frac{2}{\gamma})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}(Q-\beta)-\frac{1}{2}}}{(Q-\beta)\Gamma(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4})^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(Q-\beta)}}\\
&\qquad\cdot\frac{\Gamma_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\beta-\frac{\gamma}{2})e^{i\pi(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}-Q)(Q-\beta)}}{\Gamma_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(Q-\beta)S_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\frac{\beta}{2}+\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})S_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\frac{\beta}{2}+\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})}.\end{split}

(3.3)

For
\mu>0
, let

\overline{R}(\beta,\mu,0)=\overline{R}(\beta,0,\mu)=\mu^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(Q-\beta)}\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(Q-\beta)-\frac{1}{2}}(\frac{2}{\gamma})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}(Q-\beta)-\frac{1}{2}}}{(Q-\beta)\Gamma(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4})^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(Q-\beta)}}\frac{\Gamma_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\beta-\frac{\gamma}{2})}{\Gamma_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(Q-\beta)}.

(3.4)

For
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\geq 0
not both zero, the meromorphic function
\beta\mapsto\overline{R}(\beta,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is positive and finite in
(\frac{\gamma}{2},Q+\frac{\gamma}{2})
, has a pole at
\frac{\gamma}{2}
and a zero at
Q+\frac{\gamma}{2}
(along with other poles and zeros). In particular, although the term
\frac{1}{Q-\beta}
suggests that there should be a pole at
\beta=Q
, this cancels with a zero coming from
\frac{1}{\Gamma_{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(Q-\beta)}
to give
\overline{R}(Q,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=1
.
The function
\overline{R}
is called the normalized reflection coefficient. The unnormalized version is defined by

R(\beta,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=-\Gamma(1-\frac{2}{\gamma}(Q-\beta))\overline{R}(\beta,\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

(3.5)

The following solvability result was proved in
[
RZ20b
]
; see Theorem 1.7 and Section 1.3 there.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
RZ20b
]
)
.

Let
\beta\in(\frac{\gamma}{2},Q)
and
W=\gamma(\gamma+\frac{2}{\gamma}-\beta)
, and let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\geq 0
not both be zero.
Recall the field
\widehat{h}
from the definition of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{2}(W)
in Definition
2.1
. We have

\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mu_{1}\nu_{\widehat{h}}(\mathbbm{R})+\mu_{2}\nu_{\widehat{h}}(\mathbbm{R}+\pi i)}}\right)^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(Q-\beta)}}}\right]=\overline{R}(\beta,\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

Remark 3.2
.

Proposition
3.1
is only stated in
[
RZ20b
]
for
\beta\in(\frac{\gamma}{2},Q)
. However, it extends to the case
\beta=Q
, where
W=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}
. In this case, it simply says that a zeroth moment is equal to
1=\overline{R}(Q,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
.
When
\beta\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}
the expectation is infinite, since
\frac{2}{\gamma}(Q-\beta)\geq\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}
and the moment of the Gaussian multiplicative chaos of order at least

is infinite

RV14

.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
W\in[\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2},\gamma Q)
and
\beta=\gamma+\frac{2}{\gamma}-\frac{W}{\gamma}

for the left and right boundary lengths
of a quantum disk from
\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)

\mu_{1}L_{1}+\mu_{2}L_{2}

\mathbf{1}_{\ell>0}\overline{R}(\beta;\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\ell^{-\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}W}\,d\ell.

Proof.

To simplify notation we explain the proof for
\mu_{1}=1
and
\mu_{2}=0
— the general case follows identically.
For
0<\ell<\ell^{\prime}
we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}[\nu_{{\widehat{h}}+c}(\mathbbm{R})\in(\ell,\ell^{\prime})]&=\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}c}\nu_{\widehat{h}}(\mathbbm{R})\in(\ell,\ell^{\prime})}\frac{\gamma}{2}e^{(\beta-Q)c}dc}}\right]\\
&=\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\int_{\ell}^{\ell^{\prime}}\nu_{\widehat{h}}(\mathbbm{R})^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(Q-\beta)}y^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(\beta-Q)}\cdot y^{-1}\,dy}}\right],\end{split}

(3.6)

where we have used the change of variables

(so

∎

For
W>\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}
, the integral
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)[e^{-\mu_{1}L_{1}-\mu_{2}L_{2}}]
is infinite. The below proposition shows that we can obtain a finite integral by subtracting an appropriate polynomial which makes the integrand sufficiently small for small boundary lengths. Furthermore, the integral can be expressed in terms of the reflection coefficient
R
. We will see in Proposition

below that the formula also extends to the case of thin disks, in which case it is not necessary to subtract a polynomial.

Proposition 3.4
.

For
W\in(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2},\gamma^{2})
and
\beta=\gamma+\frac{2}{\gamma}-\frac{W}{\gamma}
, and writing
L_{1},L_{2}
for
the left and right boundary lengths of a quantum disk from
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)
, we have

\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)[e^{-\mu_{1}L_{1}-\mu_{2}L_{2}}-1]=\frac{\gamma}{2(Q-\beta)}R(\beta;\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

(3.7)

More generally, suppose
W\in(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2},\gamma Q)
and there is a positive integer
n
such that
W\in(n\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2},(n+1)\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})
. Let
P_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}
be the
n
-term Taylor polynomial of
e^{x}
. Then

\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)[e^{-\mu_{1}L_{1}-\mu_{2}L_{2}}-P_{n}(-\mu_{1}L_{1}-\mu_{2}L_{2})]=\frac{\gamma}{2(Q-\beta)}R(\beta,\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

Proof.

Write
\alpha=\frac{2W}{\gamma^{2}}\in(1,2)
. Using integration by parts, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-e^{-\ell})\cdot\ell^{-\alpha}\,d\ell=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\ell}\ell^{-(\alpha-1)}\,d\ell=\frac{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}{\alpha-1}.

Thus, by Lemma
3.3
, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)[1-e^{-\mu_{1}L_{1}-\mu_{2}L_{2}}]&=\overline{R}(\beta,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-e^{-\ell})\cdot\ell^{-\alpha}\,d\ell\\
&=\frac{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\overline{R}(\beta,\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).\end{split}

The more general version similarly follows from the following identity for
n
a positive integer and
p\in(n,n+1)

\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{-\ell}-P_{n}(\ell))\ell^{-p}\,d\ell=\Gamma(1-p).

Indeed, repeatedly integrating by parts gives

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{-\ell}-P_{n}(\ell))\ell^{-p}\,d\ell&=\frac{1}{1-p}\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{-\ell}-P_{n-1}(\ell))\ell^{-(p-1)}\,d\ell=\dots\\
&=\frac{1}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}(k-p)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\ell}\ell^{(p-n)}\,d\ell,\end{split}

and since the last integral equals
\Gamma(n+1-p)
, repeatedly using
\Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x)
yields the result.
∎

3.2

The reflection coefficient

R(\beta;\mu_{1},\mu_{2})R(2Q-\beta;\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=1.

In Section

describes quantum lengths for the thick quantum disk.

]

Definition 3.5

with weight

The choice of the constant

above is justified by the following proposition, which states that the quantum disk boundary length distribution extends analytically from thick to thin quantum disks, hence giving a probabilistic meaning to
\overline{R}
and
R
for
\beta\in(Q,Q+\frac{\gamma}{2})
.

Proposition 3.6
.

For
W\in(0,\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})
and
\beta=\gamma+\frac{2}{\gamma}-\frac{W}{\gamma}\in(Q,Q+\frac{\gamma}{2})
, let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be the left and right boundary lengths of a thin quantum disk from
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)
. For constants
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\geq 0
not both zero, the law of
\mu_{1}L_{1}+\mu_{2}L_{2}
is

\mathbf{1}_{\ell>0}\overline{R}(\beta,\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\ell^{-\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}W}\,d\ell,

and

\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)[e^{-\mu_{1}L_{1}-\mu_{2}L_{2}}]=\frac{\gamma}{2(Q-\beta)}R(\beta;\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)
is defined using Poisson point processes. Our proposition will be an immediate consequence of Campbell’s formula for the Laplace functional for Poisson point processes (see e.g.
[
Kin93
, Section 3.2]
): For any measure space
(S,m)
and measurable function
f:S\to(0,\infty)
such that
\int_{S}\min(f(x),1)\,m(dx)<\infty
, we have for a Poisson point process
\Pi
on
(S,m)
that

\mathbbm{E}[\exp(-\sum_{X\in\Pi}f(X))]=\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{S}(e^{-f(x)}-1)\,m(dx)}}\right).

For fixed
T>0
we can set
S_{T}=[0,T]\times\widetilde{S}
where
\widetilde{S}
is the space of two-pointed quantum disks, and
m_{T}=\mathrm{Leb}_{[0,T]}\times\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\gamma^{2}-W)
. Letting
\Pi_{T}
be a Poisson point process on
(S_{T},m_{T})
and
f(\mathcal{D})=\mu_{1}\ell_{1}+\mu_{2}\ell_{2}
where
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
are the quantum lengths of the boundary arcs of
\mathcal{D}
, we have

\mathbbm{E}[\exp(-\sum_{(t,\mathcal{D})\in\Pi_{T}}f(\mathcal{D}))]=\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-T\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\gamma^{2}-W)[1-e^{-f(\mathcal{D})}]}}\right).

Integrating against
\mathbf{1}_{T>0}(1-\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}W)^{-2}\,dT
, we get

\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)[e^{-\mu_{1}L_{1}-\mu_{2}L_{2}}]=\frac{1}{(1-\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}W)^{2}\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\gamma^{2}-W)[1-e^{-f(\mathcal{D})}])}.

Proposition
3.4
gives
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\gamma^{2}-W)[1-e^{-f(\mathcal{D})}])=\frac{\gamma}{2(Q-\beta)}R(2Q-\beta;\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
, and combining with the reflection identity (
3.8
) and
1-\frac{2W}{\gamma^{2}}=\frac{2(\beta-Q)}{\gamma}
yields the second claim.

The first assertion then follows from the fact that
\mu_{1}L_{1}+\mu_{2}L_{2}
has a power law with exponent
-\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}W

[
AHS20
, Lemma 2.17]
, and a similar computation as in Proposition
3.4
to derive the coefficient of the power law.
∎

.

4
SLE observables via conformal welding

In this section, we prove Proposition
4.5
, which is a conformal welding result. Although the measures involved are infinite, a constant of proportionality that arises is finite and encodes the information of the SLE observable in Theorem
1.1
.

4.1
Conformal welding of quantum disks

In this section we recall the main result from our companion paper
[
AHS20
]
, saying that
\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
arise as the interface
between two quantum disks conformally welded together.

We start by extending the definition of a quantum surface to the case where the surface is decorated by a curve. Recall from Section
2.1
that a
\gamma
-LQG surface with
n
marked points is defined to be an equivalence class of tuples
(D,h,z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
where
D\subset\mathbbm{C}
is a domain,
h
is a distribution on
D
, and
z_{j}\in\partial D\cup D
for
j=1,\dots,n
. A curve-decorated quantum surface with
n
marked points is similarly defined to be an equivalence class of tuples
(D,h,z_{1},\dots,z_{n},\eta)
where
\eta:[0,t_{\eta}]\to D
is a curve on
D
and
t_{\eta}
is the duration of
\eta
. More precisely, we say that
(D,h,z_{1},\dots,z_{n},\eta)\sim_{\gamma}(\widetilde{D},\widetilde{h},\widetilde{z}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{z}_{n},\widetilde{\eta})
if there is a conformal map
f:D\to\widetilde{D}
such that
\widetilde{h}=f\bullet_{\gamma}h
,
\widetilde{z}_{j}=f(z_{j})
for
j=1,\dots,n
, and
\widetilde{\eta}(t)=f(\eta(t))
.

For
W>0
, let
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W;\ell,r)
be the measure on weight
W
quantum disks restricting to the event that the left and right boundary arcs have lengths
\ell
and
r
, respectively.
More precisely,

\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)=\iint_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W;\ell,r)\,d\ell\,dr.

(4.1)

In particular,
|\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W;\ell,r)|d\ell\,dr
is the law of the left and right boundary lengths, and the normalized probability measure
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W;\ell,r)^{\#}
is
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)
conditioned on the boundary lengths being
\ell,r
. The identity (
4.1
) a priori only specifies
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W;\ell,r)
for almost every
\ell,r
.
But a canonical version of
\{\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W;\ell,r):\ell,r>0\}
can be chosen such that
it is continuous in
\ell,r
in a proper topology. See
[
AHS20
, Section 2.6]
for details.

For fixed
\ell,r,x
, a pair of quantum disks from
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{1};\ell,x)\times\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{2};x,r)
can a.s. be
conformally welded
along their length
x
boundary arcs according to quantum length, yielding a quantum surface with two boundary marked points joined by an interface. This follows from the local absolute continuity of weight
W
quantum disks with respect to weight
W
quantum wedges, and the conformal welding theorem for quantum wedges
[
DMS14
, Theorem 1.2]
. See e.g.
[
She16a
]
,
[
DMS14
, Section 3.5]
, or
[
GHS19
, Section 4.1]
for more information on conformal welding in the setting of LQG surfaces.

For
W_{1},W_{2}>0
, we now define an infinite measure
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{1}+W_{2};\ell,r)\otimes\SLE_{\kappa}(W_{1}-2;W_{2}-2)
on curve-decorated quantum surfaces.
When
W_{1}+W_{2}\geq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}
, we first sample
\phi
such that the law of the
(\mathcal{S},\phi,-\infty,+\infty)
viewed as a quantum surface is

\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{1}+W_{2};\ell,r)
and then independently sampling an independent
\SLE_{\kappa}(W_{1}-2;W_{2}-2)
curve
\eta
in
(\mathcal{S},-\infty,+\infty)
and parametrize
\eta
by its quantum length. We denote the law of the curve-decorated surface
(\mathcal{S},\phi,\eta,-\infty,+\infty)
by

\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{1}+W_{2};\ell,r)\otimes\SLE_{\kappa}(W_{1}-2;W_{2}-2)
.
When
W_{1}+W_{2}<\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}

\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{1}+W_{2};\ell,r)
, then decorate each bead by an independent
\SLE_{\kappa}(W_{1}-2;W_{2}-2)

We denote the law of this chain of curve-decorated surfaces
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{1}+W_{2};\ell,r)\otimes\SLE_{\kappa}(W_{1}-2;W_{2}-2)
.

The next result shows that the conformal welding of two quantum disks gives the type of curve-decorated surface defined above.

Proposition 4.1

(

&\qquad\qquad=c_{W_{-},W_{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Weld}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{-};\ell,x),\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W_{+};x,r)}}\right)\,dx.\end{split}

4.2
Conformal welding of

and
\mathcal{M}_{2,\bullet}^{\mathrm{disk}}

Lemma 4.2

(4.2)

Proof.

The first assertion is clear since

By Definition

\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})

be the connected component of

on its boundary for

The following lemma treats the case

Lemma 5.12
.

Theorem

holds for
\kappa\in(4,\infty)
,
\rho_{-}>-2
and
\rho_{+}>\frac{\kappa}{2}-4
.

Proof.

By SLE duality (see
[
Zha08
, Theorem 5.1]
and
[
MS16
, Theorem 1.4]
) the right boundary of an
\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
has the law of an
\SLE_{\widetilde{\kappa}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{-};\widetilde{\rho}_{+})
curve, where
\widetilde{\kappa}=\frac{16}{\kappa}<4
,
\widetilde{\rho}_{-}=\frac{\widetilde{\kappa}}{2}-2+\frac{\widetilde{\kappa}}{4}\rho_{-}
and
\widetilde{\rho}_{+}=\widetilde{\kappa}+\frac{\widetilde{\kappa}}{4}\rho_{+}-4
. Hence when
\lambda<\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}=\frac{1}{\widetilde{\kappa}}(\widetilde{\rho}_{+}+2)(\widetilde{\rho}_{+}+4-\frac{\widetilde{\kappa}}{2})
, by Lemma
5.11
we have

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}[\psi^{\prime}(1)^{\lambda}]

\displaystyle=\frac{F(\widetilde{\alpha},\widetilde{\kappa},\widetilde{\rho}_{-},\widetilde{\rho}_{+})}{F(\sqrt{\widetilde{\kappa}},\widetilde{\kappa},\widetilde{\rho}_{-},\widetilde{\rho}_{+})},\quad\text{ where }\widetilde{\alpha}\text{ solves }1-\frac{\widetilde{\alpha}}{2}(\frac{\sqrt{\widetilde{\kappa}}}{2}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\widetilde{\kappa}}}-\frac{\widetilde{\alpha}}{2})=\lambda.

Once can easily verify that
\lambda_{0}=\widetilde{\lambda}_{0}
, and

\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}+\frac{\rho_{-}}{\sqrt{\kappa}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\widetilde{\kappa}}}+\frac{\widetilde{\rho}_{-}}{\sqrt{\widetilde{\kappa}}},\quad\frac{4}{\sqrt{\kappa}}+\frac{\rho_{+}}{\sqrt{\kappa}}=\frac{4}{\sqrt{\widetilde{\kappa}}}+\frac{\widetilde{\rho}_{+}}{\sqrt{\widetilde{\kappa}}},\quad\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}+\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}}{2}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\widetilde{\kappa}}}+\frac{\sqrt{\widetilde{\kappa}}}{2}.

(5.11)

The last identity above means we can take
\alpha=\widetilde{\alpha}
.
Comparing
F(\alpha,\kappa,\rho_{-},\rho_{+})
and
F(\widetilde{\alpha},\widetilde{\kappa},\widetilde{\rho}_{-},\widetilde{\rho}_{+})

5.11
) we can pair up their terms so their arguments agree. Since
\Gamma_{\sqrt{\kappa}/2}=\Gamma_{\sqrt{\widetilde{\kappa}}/2}
as
\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}}{2}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\widetilde{\kappa}}}
, we get termwise equality. Thus
F(\alpha,\kappa,\rho_{-},\rho_{+})=F(\widetilde{\alpha},\widetilde{\kappa},\widetilde{\rho}_{-},\widetilde{\rho}_{+})

F(\sqrt{\kappa},\kappa,\rho_{-},\rho_{+})=F(\sqrt{\widetilde{\kappa}},\widetilde{\kappa},\widetilde{\rho}_{-},\widetilde{\rho}_{+})

\mathbbm{E}[\psi^{\prime}(1)^{\lambda}]=\frac{F(\widetilde{\alpha},\widetilde{\kappa},\widetilde{\rho}_{-},\widetilde{\rho}_{+})}{F(\sqrt{\widetilde{\kappa}},\widetilde{\kappa},\widetilde{\rho}_{-},\widetilde{\rho}_{+})}=\frac{F(\alpha,\kappa,\rho_{-},\rho_{+})}{F(\sqrt{\kappa},\kappa,\rho_{-},\rho_{+})},

hence Theorem
1.1
holds for
\kappa\in(4,\infty)
,
\rho_{-}>-2
,
\rho_{+}>\frac{\kappa}{2}-4
and
\lambda<\lambda_{0}
.

\mathbbm{E}[\psi^{\prime}(1)^{\lambda}]=\infty

\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
.
As before, we have
\psi^{\prime}(1)>1

\mathbbm{E}[\psi^{\prime}(1)^{\lambda}]\geq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathbbm{E}[\psi^{\prime}(1)^{\lambda_{0}-\varepsilon}]=\infty

∎

.

5.11

∎

Appendix A
Backgrounds on Schramm-Loewner evolutions

In this section we provide further background on SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
that is relevant to Theorem
1.1
.

A.1
The Loewner evolution definition of SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})

Let
\mathbbm{H}
be the upper half-plane. For a continuous function
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
that we call the
driving function
consider the solution
g_{t}(z)
of the Loewner differential equation

g_{t}(z)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{2}{g_{s}(z)-W_{s}}\,ds,\qquad g_{0}(z)=z,\,z\in\mathbbm{H}.

For each
z\in\mathbbm{H}
let
\tau_{z}
denote the supremum of times
t>0
such that
g_{t}(z)
is well-defined. For certain choices of
W
one can show that there exists a unique continuous curve
\eta
in
\mathbbm{H}
from 0 to
\infty
such that if
K_{t}\subset\mathbbm{H}
denotes the set of points in
\mathbbm{H}
which are disconnected from
\infty
by
\eta([0,t])
then
K_{t}=\{z\in\mathbbm{H}\,:\,\tau_{z}\leq t\}
. We say that
W
is the Loewner driving function of
\eta
. By setting
W_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t}
for a standard Brownian motion
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
\kappa>0
we get the curve
\eta
which is known as a
Schramm-Loewner evolution with parameter
\kappa
(SLE
κ
). See e.g.
[
Law05
,
Sch00
]
for more details.

SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
is the natural generalization of SLE
κ
when we keep track of two additional marked points on the domain boundary. Let
\rho_{-},\rho_{+}>-2
. Given a standard Brownian motion
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
consider the solutions
W,V^{\pm}
of the following stochastic differential equations

W_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\rho_{-}}{W_{s}-V_{s}^{-}}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\rho_{+}}{W_{s}-V_{s}^{+}}\,ds,\qquad V_{t}^{\pm}=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{2}{V_{s}^{\pm}-W_{s}}\,ds

(A.1)

with initial condition
(W_{0},V_{0}^{-},V_{0}^{+})=(0,0,0)
. The uniqueness in law of the solution was proved in
[
MS16
, Theorem 2.2]
.
Moreover, one can show that there is a unique curve
\eta
from 0 to
\infty
in
\mathbbm{H}
which has Loewner driving function given by
W
.
We call
\eta
an SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
. See
[
LSW03
,
Dub05
,
MS16
]
for further details.

A.2
Finiteness of moments

In this section we prove the following finiteness of moment statement.

Proposition A.1
.

For
\kappa\in(0,4)
and
\rho_{-},\rho_{+}>-2

from

to

\lambda_{0}=\frac{1}{\kappa}(\rho_{+}+2)(\rho_{+}+4-\frac{\kappa}{2})

with

Lemma A.2
.

Proposition

Proof.

We first inductively show that Proposition

denote the bounded set. To prove (

) it is sufficient to argue

\begin{split}&(i)\,\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{n\in\mathbbm{N}}\operatorname{diam}(\widetilde{\eta}_{n}([\sigma_{n}-s(\varepsilon),\sigma_{n}])\cap\widetilde{D}^{\varepsilon}_{n})\rightarrow 0\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\\
&(ii)\,\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{n\in\mathbbm{N}}\operatorname{diam}(\widetilde{\eta}_{n}([\sigma_{n}-s(\varepsilon),\sigma_{n}])\setminus\widetilde{D}^{\varepsilon}_{n})\rightarrow 0.\end{split}

(A.4)

We see that (ii) holds since otherwise there would be a (random) constant
c>0
independent of
\varepsilon
such that for arbitrarily large
n
and all
y>1
sufficiently large,
y
times the harmonic measure of
\widetilde{\eta}_{n}([\sigma_{n}-s(\varepsilon),\sigma_{n}])
seen from
iy
in
\mathbbm{H}\setminus\widetilde{\eta}_{n}([\tau_{n},\sigma_{n}])
would be at least
c
; this contradicts the assumed convergence of
(W^{n},V^{n,+})
.

To prove that (i) holds we can first proceed similarly as in the proof of (ii) and use harmonic measure considerations and convergence of
(W^{n},V^{n,+})
to conclude that

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{n\in\mathbbm{N}}\operatorname{diam}(\widetilde{\eta}_{n}([\tau_{n},\tau_{n}+\bar{s}(\varepsilon)]))\rightarrow 0.

(A.5)

By Lemma
A.4
the map
g^{n}_{\tau_{n}}
converges uniformly to
g_{\tau}
away from the hull of
\eta_{n}|_{[0,\tau_{n}]}
.
Combining this with (
A.5
) we get that for any
\delta>0
we can find a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, such that
\eta_{n}([\tau_{n},\tau_{n}+\bar{s}(\varepsilon)])
is contained in the
\delta
-neighborhood of the hull of
\eta_{n}|_{[0,\tau_{n}]}
. By reversibility of SLE
{}_{\kappa_{n}}(\rho_{+})
the same property holds for
\eta_{n}([\sigma_{n}-s(\varepsilon),\sigma_{n}])
and the hull created by
\eta_{n}|_{[\sigma_{n},\infty)}
. Applying the map
g^{n}_{\tau_{n}}
this gives (i).
∎

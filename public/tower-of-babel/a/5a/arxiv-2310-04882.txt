Triangulations of singular constant curvature spheres via Belyi functions and determinants of Laplacians
Victor Kalvin

Abstract
We study the zeta-regularized spectral determinant of the Friedrichs Laplacians on the singular spheres obtained by cutting and glueing copies of constant curvature (hyperbolic, spherical, or flat) double triangle. The determinant is explicitly expressed in terms of the corresponding Belyi functions and the determinant of the Friedrichs Laplacian on the double triangle. The latter determinant was found in a closed explicit form in ArXiv:2112.02771 [ 18 ] . In examples we consider the cyclic, dihedral, tetrahedral, octahedral, and icosahedral triangulations, and find the determinant for the corresponding spherical, Euclidean, and hyperbolic Platonic surfaces. These surfaces correspond to stationary points of the determinant.

Introduction

We study the zeta-regularized spectral determinant of the Friedrichs scalar Laplacian on the surfaces constructed by cutting and gluing copies of a constant curvature
S^{2}
-like double triangle. We call these surfaces glued or triangulated. The geometric (combinatorial) cutting and gluing scheme is described in terms of the corresponding Belyi function
[
3
]
. Or, equivalently, in terms of
dessins d’enfants

[
12
]
.

The Belyi function maps the (source) Riemann surface to the (target) Riemann sphere. Any constant curvature double triangle is isometric to the target Riemann sphere with explicitly constructed conformal metric with three conical singularities
[
18
]
.
The glued surface is isometric to the source Riemann surface equipped with the pullback of the explicit conformal metric by the Belyi function. This provides us with a geodesic triangulation and an
explicit uniformization of the glued surface. In particular, in the case of flat right isosceles
S^{2}
-like double triangle, we get the square-tiled flat surfaces, see e.g.
[
44
,
58
]
and references therein.

In other words,
for studying the spectral determinant we suggest using the natural decomposition via triangulation based on the celebrated results of Belyi, Grothendieck, Shabat and Voevodskii.
The price for this is that we first need to study the spectral determinant on surfaces with conical singularities
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
.

The main idea is to explicitly express the spectral determinant of the glued surface in terms of the corresponding Belyi function and the spectral determinant of the constant curvature
S^{2}
-like double triangle. The latter determinant was found in a closed explicit form in
[
18
]
.

Let us recall that not all Riemann surfaces can be glued/triangulated in this manner. But for the most interesting surfaces, like the Fermat curve, the Bolza curve, the Hurwitz surfaces, including Klein’s quartic, the Platonic surfaces, etc., it is certainly possible, thanks to the famous Belyi theorem
[
3
,
22
,
45
]
.
Due to the highest possible number of authomorphisms
[
45
,
13
]
, some of the triangulated surfaces (equipped with the natural smooth hyperbolic metric) are expected to be stationary points of the spectral determinant. To the best of our knowledge, no closed explicit expression for the spectral determinant of any of these surfaces is known yet.

In this paper, we study the spectral determinant of the genus zero triangulated surfaces. For the triangulations of singular constant curvature (hyperbolic, spherical, or flat) spheres via Belyi functions, we explicitly express the spectral determinant in terms of the Belyi maps, and the determinant of the constant curvature
S^{2}
-like double triangle. In particular, with each bicolored plane tree
[
4
,
5
]
we can naturally associate an infinite family of constant curvature singular spheres, and then the corresponding infinite family of explicit spectral invariants, that is the family of the corresponding spectral determinants. For some closely related geometric constructions and invariants see e.g.
[
14
,
38
,
39
,
47
,
51
]
.

Let us stress that due to conical singularities on the cuts, none of the presently known BFK-type gluing formulae
[
7
]
can be used. We rely on a completely different approach relating the determinants of Laplacians on the target Riemann sphere and on the ramified covering via anomaly formulae for the determinants
[
18
,
20
]
.

As a byproduct, our approach allows for explicit evaluation of the Liouville action

,

37
,

As is known

, the Liouville action generates the famous accessory parameters as their common antiderivative. We explicitly express the accessory parameters of the triangulated singular constant curvature spheres in terms of the Belyi functions and the orders of conical singularities. We also find the derivative of the Liouville action with respect to the order of a conical singularity in the spirit of
[
59

Recall that for smooth metrics on Riemann surfaces extrema of spectral determinants were studied in a series of papers by Osgood, Phillips, and Sarnak

Let us list the preimages of the marked points

Introduce the ramification divisor

\boldsymbol{f}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\operatorname{ord}_{k}f\cdot x_{k},

where

is the ramification order of the Belyi function

If

is a pole of

. If

. If

as a vertex in the

is its graph-theoretic degree, or, equivalently, the number of edges emanating from the vertex

Lemma 3.2

Recall that

\displaystyle(|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2)\int_{\mathbb{C}}(f^{*}\phi)e^{2f^{*}\phi}\frac{dx\wedge d\bar{x}}{-2i}=(|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2)\deg f\int_{\mathbb{C}}\phi e^{2\phi}\frac{dz\wedge d\bar{z}}{-2i}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k:f(x_{k})\neq\infty}(f^{*}\phi)_{k}\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+\sum_{k:f(x_{k})=\infty}(f^{*}\phi)_{k}\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+\sum_{k}(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}\log\left|(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)c_{k}\right|

\displaystyle+2\sum_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty,f(x_{k})=\infty}(f^{*}\phi)_{k}+\left.2\log\left|(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)c_{k}\right|\right|_{k:x_{k}=\infty}-\left.2(f^{*}\phi)_{k}\right|_{k:x_{k}=\infty,f(\infty)\neq\infty},

Proof.

We begin with the equality

.
Thus, in order to prove the lemma, we only need to evaluate the last integral.

Thanks to the Liouville equation (

\displaystyle(|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2)\int_{\mathbb{C}}(\log|f^{\prime}|)e^{2f^{*}\phi}\frac{dx\wedge d\bar{x}}{-2i}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{\epsilon}}(\log|f^{\prime}|)\bigl(-4\partial_{x}\partial_{\bar{x}}(f^{*}\phi)\bigr)\frac{dx\wedge d\bar{x}}{-2i}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathbb{C}^{\epsilon}}\Bigl((f^{*}\phi)\partial_{\vec{n}}(\log|f^{\prime}|)-(\log|f^{\prime}|)\partial_{\vec{n}}(f^{*}\phi)\Bigr)\,|dx|,

\displaystyle\log|f^{\prime}(x)|=\operatorname{ord}_{k}f\log|x-x_{k}|+\log\left|(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)c_{k}\right|+o(1),\ x\to x_{k}\neq\infty,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\log|f^{\prime}(x)|=-(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+2)\log|x|+\log\left|(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)c_{k}\right|+o(1),\ x\to x_{k}=\infty.

Besides, if

is a pole of

\displaystyle\log|f^{\prime}(x)|=

\displaystyle-(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+2)\log|x-x_{k}|

\displaystyle+\log|(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)c_{k}|+o(1),\quad x\to x_{k}\neq\infty,

\displaystyle\log|f^{\prime}(x)|=

\displaystyle\operatorname{ord}_{k}f\log|x|+\log|(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)c_{k}|+o(1),\quad x\to x_{k}=\infty.

Below, for the derivatives along the unit outward normal vector

we use the equalities

\partial_{\vec{n}}=-\partial_{|x-x_{j}|}\quad\text{on}\quad|x-x_{j}|=\epsilon;\quad\partial_{\vec{n}}=\partial_{|x|}\quad\text{on}\quad|x|=1/\epsilon.

We calculate the integral in right hand side of (

relying on (
3.2

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathbb{C}^{\epsilon}}\Bigl((f^{*}\phi)\partial_{\vec{n}}(\log|f^{\prime}|)-(\log|f^{\prime}|)\partial_{\vec{n}}(f^{*}\phi)\Bigr)\,|dx|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\oint_{|x-x_{j}|=\epsilon}\Bigl((f^{*}\phi)\partial_{\vec{n}}(\log|f^{\prime}|)-(\log|f^{\prime}|)\partial_{\vec{n}}(f^{*}\phi)\Bigr)\,|dx|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty,f(x_{k})\neq\infty}\Bigl(-(f^{*}\phi)_{k}\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}\log\left|(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)c_{k}\right|\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty,f(x_{k})=\infty}\Bigl((f^{*}\phi)_{k}(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+2)+(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}\log\left|(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)c_{k}\right|\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\Bigl(-(f^{*}\phi)_{k}(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+2)+(\log\left|(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)c_{n}\right|)\bigl((f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}+2\bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr|_{k:x_{k}=\infty,f(\infty)\neq\infty}

After regrouping the terms in the right hand side, this implies the assertion of Lemma

Lemma 3.3

For the determinant

the anomaly formula

\displaystyle\log\frac{\det\Delta_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}}{\deg f}=-

\displaystyle\frac{|\beta|+2}{6}\int_{\mathbb{C}}f^{*}\phi e^{2f^{*}\phi}\,\frac{dx\wedge d\bar{x}}{-2i}+\frac{1}{6}\sum_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty}\frac{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}}{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+1}(f^{*}\phi)_{k}

(3.7)

\displaystyle-\left.\frac{1}{6}\frac{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+2}{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+1}(f^{*}\phi)_{k}\right|_{k:x_{x}=\infty}-\sum_{k}\mathcal{C}\bigl(\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}\bigr)+{\bf C}

\mathcal{C}(\beta)

2.10

\bf C
stands for the constant (
2.11

Proof.

The main argument of the proof is similar to the one in
[
18

.
For this reason, we skip the details that can be easily restored from the references
[
18
,
19

Let us first obtain an asymptotics for the determinant of the Friedrichs Dirichlet Laplacian
\Delta^{D}_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}\!\!\restriction_{|x|\leqslant 1/\epsilon}
on the disk
|x|\leqslant 1/\epsilon
endowed with the metric
f^{*}m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
.
Denote by
\Delta^{D}_{0}\!\!\restriction_{|x|\leqslant 1/\epsilon}
the selfadjoint Dirichlet Laplacian on the disk
|x|\leqslant 1/\epsilon
equipped with the flat background metric
|dx|^{2}
. As is known
[
56
]
, for the determinant of the Dirichlet Laplacian on the flat disk one has

\log\det\Delta^{D}_{0}\!\!\restriction_{|x|\leqslant 1/\epsilon}=\frac{1}{3}\log\epsilon+\frac{1}{3}\log 2-\frac{1}{2}\log 2\pi-\frac{5}{12}-2\zeta_{R}^{\prime}(-1).

On the other hand, the Polyakov-Alvarez type formula from
[
20
, Theorem 1.1.2]
reads

\displaystyle\log\frac{\det\Delta^{D}_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}\!\!\restriction_{|x|\leqslant 1/\epsilon}}{\det\Delta^{D}_{0}\!\!\restriction_{|x|\leqslant 1/\epsilon}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{|f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2}{6\deg f}\int_{\mathbb{C}}(f^{*}\phi)e^{2f^{*}\phi}\frac{dx\wedge d\bar{x}}{-2i}

(3.8)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{12\pi}\oint_{|x|=1/\epsilon}(f^{*}\phi)\partial_{\vec{n}}(f^{*}\phi)\,|dx|-\frac{\epsilon}{6\pi}\oint_{|x|=1/\epsilon}(f^{*}\phi)\,|dx|

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_{|x|=1/\epsilon}\partial_{\vec{n}}(f^{*}\phi)\,|dx|+\frac{1}{6}\sum_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty}\frac{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}}{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+1}(f^{*}\phi)_{k}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty}\mathcal{C}\bigl(\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}\bigr),

where
|f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}|=\sum_{k}\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}
is the degree of the divisor
f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}=\sum_{k}\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}\cdot x_{k}

By the Gauss-Bonnet theorem
[
54

equals

. Since the (regularized) Gaussian curvature is the one of
m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
, and the total area is
\deg f
, we conclude that

\frac{|f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2}{\deg f}=|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2.

In (
3.8
) we also replace the integrals along the circle
|x|=1/\epsilon
with their asymptotics.
As a result, the Polyakov-Alvarez type formula (
3.8
) implies

\displaystyle\log{\det\Delta^{D}_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}\!\!\restriction_{|x|\leqslant 1/\epsilon}}=-\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2}{6}\int_{\mathbb{C}}(f^{*}\phi)e^{2f^{*}\phi}\frac{dx\wedge d\bar{x}}{-2i}+\frac{1}{6}\sum_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty}\frac{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}}{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+1}(f^{*}\phi)_{k}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty}\mathcal{C}\bigl(\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}\bigr)+\frac{1}{6}\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}\left((f^{*}\phi)_{k}+3\right)|_{k:x_{k}=\infty}+\frac{1}{3}\log 2-\frac{1}{2}\log 2\pi+\frac{7}{12}

\displaystyle-2\zeta_{R}^{\prime}(-1)+\frac{1}{6}\left(\left(\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+2\right)^{2}-2\left(\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+1\right)\right)|_{k:x_{k}=\infty}\log\epsilon+o(1),\ \epsilon\to 0^{+}.

In a similar way, one can also obtain the asymptotics for the determinant of theFriedrichs Dirichlet Laplacian on the cap
|x|\geqslant 1/\epsilon
of the singular sphere
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},f^{*}m_{\beta})
:

\displaystyle\log\det\Delta^{D}_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}\!\!\restriction_{|x|\geqslant 1/\epsilon}=-\frac{1}{6}((\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+1)^{2}+1)\log\epsilon-\frac{1}{6}\left(\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+1+\frac{1}{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+1}\right)(f^{*}\phi)_{k}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{C}\bigl(\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}\bigr)-2\zeta_{R}^{\prime}(-1)-\frac{5}{12}+\frac{1}{3}\log 2-\frac{1}{2}\log 2\pi-\frac{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}}{2}+o(1),\quad\epsilon\to 0^{+},

where
k
is such that
x_{k}=\infty
. In total, we have

\displaystyle\log{\det\Delta^{D}_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}\!\!\restriction_{|x|\leqslant 1/\epsilon}}+\log\det\Delta^{D}_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}\!\!\restriction_{|x|\geqslant 1/\epsilon}=-\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2}{6}\int_{\mathbb{C}}(f^{*}\phi)e^{2f^{*}\phi}\frac{dx\wedge d\bar{x}}{-2i}

(3.9)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}\sum_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty}\frac{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}}{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+1}(f^{*}\phi)_{k}-\frac{1}{6}\left(1+\frac{1}{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+1}\right)(f^{*}\phi)_{k}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k}\mathcal{C}\bigl(\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}\bigr)+{\bf C}+\log 2+o(1),\quad\epsilon\to 0^{+}.

Now we cut the singular sphere
\left(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},f^{*}m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right)
along the circle
|x|=1/\epsilon
with the help of the BFK formula
[
7
, Theorem B
∗
]
to obtain

\displaystyle\log\det\Delta_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}=

\displaystyle\log\deg f-\log 2

(3.10)

\displaystyle+\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\left(\log\det\Delta^{D}_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}\!\!\restriction_{|x|\leqslant 1/\epsilon}+\log\det\Delta^{D}_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}\!\!\restriction_{|x|\geqslant 1/\epsilon}\right).

Here

In the limit, the asymptotics (

\displaystyle=\left(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1-\frac{1}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\right)\frac{\phi_{f(x_{k})}}{\beta_{f(x_{k})}+1}-\left((f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}+1+\frac{1}{(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}+1}\right)\log(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\log|c_{k}|+(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+2)\log(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)\right).

Here the first two terms in the right-hand side contribute to the first and the second sums in the right-hand side of (
2.9
) correspondingly. The last term goes to
C_{f}
, cf. (
3.1
).

If
x_{k}\neq\infty
is a pole of
f
, then we get the terms listed in (
3.14
) and also
\frac{1}{6}
times

\displaystyle\frac{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}}{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+1}(f^{*}\phi)_{k}-\Bigl((f^{*}\phi)_{k}(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+2)+(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}\log\left|(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)c_{k}\right|\Bigr)

\displaystyle+(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)\frac{\beta_{f(x_{k})}+2}{\beta_{f(x_{k})}+1}\phi_{f(x_{k})}

\displaystyle=\left(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1-\frac{1}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\right)\frac{\phi_{f(x_{k})}}{\beta_{f(x_{k})}+1}-\left((f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}+1+\frac{1}{(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}+1}\right)\log(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+2}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\log|c_{k}|-\operatorname{ord}_{k}f\log(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)\right).

Here again, the first two terms in the right-hand side contribute to the first and the second sums in the right-hand side of (
2.9
). The last term goes to
C_{f}
, cf. (
3.1
).

Similarly, if
x_{k}=\infty
is not a pole of
f
, we get the terms listed in (
3.14
) and
\frac{1}{6}
times

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+2}{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+1}(f^{*}\phi)_{k}-\Bigl(-(f^{*}\phi)_{k}(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+2)+((f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}+2)(\log\left|(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)c_{k}\right|)\Bigr)

\displaystyle-(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)\frac{\beta_{f(x_{k})}}{\beta_{f(x_{k})}+1}\phi_{f(x_{k})}

\displaystyle=\left(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1-\frac{1}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\right)\frac{\beta_{f(x_{k})}}{\beta_{f(x_{k})}+1}\phi_{f(x_{k})}

\displaystyle-\left((f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}+1+\frac{1}{(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}+1}\right)\log(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)

\displaystyle+\left(-\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+2}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\log|c_{k}|+\operatorname{ord}_{k}f\log(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)\right).

Here, as before, the first two terms in the right-hand side are in agreement with (
2.9
), and the last term goes to
C_{f}
, cf. (
3.1
).

Finally, if
x_{k}=\infty
is a pole of
f
, we get the terms listed in (
3.14
) and
\frac{1}{6}
times

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+2}{\left(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)_{k}+1}(f^{*}\phi)_{k}-\Bigl((f^{*}\phi)_{k}\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+\bigl((f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}+2\bigr)\log\left|(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)c_{k}\right|\Bigr)

\displaystyle+(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)\frac{\beta_{f(x_{k})}+2}{\beta_{f(x_{k})}+1}\phi_{f(x_{k})}

\displaystyle=\left(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1-\frac{1}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\right)\frac{\phi_{f(x_{k})}}{\beta_{f(x_{k})}+1}-\left((f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}+1+\frac{1}{(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}+1}\right)\log(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)

\displaystyle+\left(-\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\log|c_{k}|-(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+2)\log(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)\right),

which is in agreement with (
2.9
) and (
3.1
). This completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

3.2
Euclidean equilateral triangulation

By Proposition
3.1
the constant
C_{f}
does not depend on the metric
m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
. In this section, we make a particular choice of
m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
that significantly simplifies the calculation of the constant
C_{f}
. As we show in the proof of Proposition
3.4
below, it makes sense to consider the Euclidean equilateral triangulation naturally associated with Belyi function
f
, cf.
[
45
,
55
]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

The constant
C_{f}
in (
3.1
) satisfies (
2.12
) with
A_{f}
from (
2.13
); see also Remark
3.5
at the end of this subsection.

Proof.

Here we consider the pull-back by
f

of the flat singular metric

m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=c^{2}|z|^{-4/3}|z-1|^{-4/3}|dz|^{2},\quad\boldsymbol{\beta}=\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)\cdot 0+\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)\cdot 1+\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)\cdot\infty,

where
c>0
is a scaling coefficient that
normalizes the area of the metric to one. Note that the surface
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{z},m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}})
is isometric to two congruent Euclidean equilateral triangles glued together along their sides
[
54
,
21
]
. In terms of
[
45
]
, the corresponding bicolored double triangle in Fig.
3
(with
\beta_{0}=\beta_{1}=\beta_{\infty}=-2/3
) is a “butterfly” that puts the wings together to become
S^{2}
-like, see also
[
55
]
.

Relying on anomaly formulae we can obtain expressions for the determinants of Laplacians on the base
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{z},m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}})
and on the ramified covering
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},f^{*}m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}})
. These determinants satisfy the relation (
2.9
) from Theorem
3.1
. As we show below, this leads to the equalities (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) for
C_{f}
and
A_{f}
correspondingly, and thus proves the assertion.

The potential
\phi
of the metric
m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=e^{2\phi}|dz|^{2}
has the asymptotics

\phi(x)=-\frac{2}{3}\log|z|+\log c+o(1),\quad|z|\to 0;\quad\phi(x)=-\frac{2}{3}\log|z-1|+\log c+o(1),\quad|z|\to 1;

\phi(x)=-\frac{4}{3}\log|z|+\log c+o(1),\quad|z|\to\infty.

This asymptotics is particularly simple, because for the coefficients
\phi_{j}
we have
\phi_{j}=\log c
(instead of a cumbersome expression with a lot of Gamma functions for
\phi_{j}
in (
2.3
), see (
5.2
)), and the weights
\beta_{j}
of the marked points are
-2/3
. Moreover, thanks to our special choice of
m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
, for the Laplacian
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
on
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{z},m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}})
the right hand side of the anomaly formula (
3.11
) takes the following particularly simple form:

\log{\det\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}=-\frac{4}{3}\log c-3\mathcal{C}\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)+{\bf C}.

This together with the relation (
2.9
) proved in Proposition
3.1
gives

\displaystyle\log\frac{\det\Delta_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}}{\deg f}=\deg f\cdot\left(-\frac{4}{3}\log c+{\bf C}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(3\deg f-\sum_{k}\frac{1}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\right){\log c}

(3.15)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}\sum_{k}\left(\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}{3}+\frac{3}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\right)\log(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k}\mathcal{C}\left(\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f-2}{3}\right)-(\deg f-1){\bf C}+C_{f}.

One can think of the surface
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},f^{*}m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}})
as of the
\deg f
copies of the flat bicolored double triangle glued along the edges in a way prescribed by
f
. For the pull-back metric we obtain

f^{*}m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=c^{2}|f(x)|^{-4/3}|f(x)-1|^{-4/3}|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}\,|dx|^{2}.

(3.16)

As is well known
[
54
]
, the flat metric
f^{*}m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
can equivalently be written in the standard form

f^{*}m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=c^{2}A_{f}^{2}\prod_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty}|x-x_{k}|^{\frac{2}{3}(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f-2)}\,|dx|^{2}.

(3.17)

Now the representation (
2.13
) for the scaling coefficient
A_{f}
is an immediate consequence of the equalities (
3.16
) and (
3.17
).

Clearly, the metric potential
f^{*}\phi
of
f^{*}m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=e^{2f^{*}\phi}|dx|^{2}
obeys the asymptotics

\displaystyle(f^{*}\phi)(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f-2}{3}\log|x-x_{k}|+\log cA_{f}

(3.18)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell:x_{k}\neq x_{\ell}\neq\infty}\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{\ell}f-2}{3}\log|x_{k}-x_{\ell}|+o(1),\quad x\to x_{k}\neq\infty,

(f^{*}\phi)(x)=-\left(\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f-2}{3}+2\right)\log|x|+\log cA_{f}+o(1),\quad x\to x_{k}=\infty.

(3.19)

Therefore, for the Laplacian
\widetilde{\Delta}_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}
induced by the scaled flat metric

(cA_{f})^{-2}\cdot f^{*}m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\prod_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty}|x-x_{k}|^{\frac{2}{3}(\operatorname{ord}_{j}f-2)}\,|dx|^{2}

of total area
\deg f\cdot(cA_{f})^{-2}
, the anomaly formula from
[
20
, Prop. 3.3]
gives

\displaystyle\log\frac{\det\widetilde{\Delta}_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}}{\deg f\cdot(cA_{f})^{-2}}=\frac{1}{18}\sum_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty}\sum_{\ \ \ell:x_{k}\neq x_{\ell}\neq\infty}\frac{(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f-2)(\operatorname{ord}_{\ell}f-2)}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\log|x_{k}-x_{\ell}|

(3.20)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}\left(\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f-2}{3}\right)+{\bf C}.

The standard rescaling property of the determinant reads

\log\frac{\det\Delta_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}}{\deg f}=\log\frac{\det\widetilde{\Delta}_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}}{\deg f\cdot(cA_{f})^{-2}}-2(\zeta(0)+1)\cdot\log cA_{f},

where

\zeta(0)=-\frac{1}{12}\sum_{k}\left(\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}{3}-\frac{3}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\right)-1

is the value of the spectral zeta function of
\widetilde{\Delta}_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}
at zero; for details we refer to
[
20
, Section 1.2]
.

This together with (
3.13
) allows one to rewrite the equality (
3.20
) in the form

\displaystyle\log\frac{\det\Delta_{f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta}}}{\deg f}=\frac{1}{18}\sum_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty}\sum_{\ \ \ell:x_{k}\neq x_{\ell}\neq\infty}\frac{(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f-2)(\operatorname{ord}_{\ell}f-2)}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\log|x_{k}-x_{\ell}|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}\left(\deg f-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{3}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\right)\log cA_{f}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}\left(\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f-2}{3}\right)+{\bf C}.

Substituting this into the left-hand side of (
3.15
), we finally arrive at (
2.12
). This completes the proof.
∎

4
Uniformization, Liouville action, and stationary points of the determinant

Consider a constant curvature sphere
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},e^{2\varphi}|dx|^{2})
with conical singularities of order
\beta_{k}
located at
x_{k}\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x}
. The parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
are called moduli. By the Gauss-Bonnet theorem
[
54
]
, the (regularized) Gaussian curvature
K
of the singular sphere
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},e^{2\phi}|dx|^{2})
satisfies the equality
K=2\pi(|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2)/S_{\varphi}
, where
|\boldsymbol{\beta}|=\sum_{k}\beta_{k}
is the degree of the divisor
\boldsymbol{\beta}=\sum\beta_{k}\cdot x_{k}
, and

S_{\varphi}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}e^{2\varphi}\frac{dx\wedge d\bar{x}}{-2i}

(4.1)

is the total surface area of
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},e^{2\varphi}|dx|^{2})
.

The potential
\varphi
of the metric
e^{2\varphi}|dx|^{2}
is a solution to the Liouville equation

e^{-2\varphi}(-4\partial_{x}\partial_{\bar{x}}\varphi)=K,\quad x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\},

(4.2)

having the following asymptotics

\displaystyle\varphi(x)=\beta_{k}\log|x-x_{k}|+\varphi_{k}+o(1),\quad x\to x_{k}\neq\infty,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\varphi(x)=-(\beta_{k}+2)\log|x|+\varphi_{k}+o(1),\quad x\to x_{k}=\infty.

The metric potential
\varphi
and the coefficients
\varphi_{k}
in the asymptotics depend on the divisor
\boldsymbol{\beta}
, i.e. on the moduli
x_{k}
and the orders
\beta_{k}
of the conical singularities.

Introduce the classical stress-energy tensor
T_{\varphi}:=2(\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi-(\partial_{x}\varphi)^{2})
of the Liouville field theory.
The stress-energy tensor is a meromorphic function on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying

\displaystyle T_{\varphi}(x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty}\left(\frac{{s}_{k}}{2(x-x_{k})^{2}}+\frac{h_{k}}{x-x_{k}}\right),

(4.4)

\displaystyle T_{\varphi}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{{s}_{k}}{2x^{2}}+\frac{h_{k}}{x^{3}}+O(x^{-4})\text{ as }x\to x_{k}=\infty.

Here
s_{k}=-\beta_{k}(2+\beta_{k})
are the weights of the second order poles, and
h_{k}
are the famous accessory parameters, e.g.
[
15
,
29
,
50
]
and references therein. Note that the meromorphic quadratic differential
T_{\varphi}dx^{2}
is a uniformizing projective connection compatible with the divisor
\boldsymbol{\beta}

[
54
,
53
]
.

Recall that one of the approaches to the uniformization consists of finding appropriate values of the accessory parameters
h_{k}
, and two appropriately normalized linearly independent solutions
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
to the Fuchsian differential equation

\partial_{x}^{2}u+\frac{1}{2}T_{\varphi}u=0.

Then the metric potential
\varphi
can be found in the form

\varphi=\log 2+\log|\partial_{x}w|-\log(1+K|w|^{2}),

where
w=u_{1}/u_{2}
is an analytic in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
function called the developing map. It satisfies the Schwarzian differential equation
\{w,x\}=T_{\varphi}(x)
, where
\{w,x\}=\frac{2w^{\prime}w^{\prime\prime\prime}-3w^{\prime\prime 2}}{2w^{\prime 2}}
is the Schwarzian derivative. However, the accessory parameters can be determined in some special cases only, and, in general, they remain elusive.

It turns out that in the geometric setting of this paper, the accessory parameters can be found explicitly in terms of the Belyi function
f
and the orders
\beta_{0}
,
\beta_{1}
, and
\beta_{\infty}
of the conical singularities of the metric
m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=e^{2\phi}|dz|^{2}
.
Indeed, consider the constant curvature unit area singular sphere
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{z},m_{\boldsymbol{\beta}})
, see Sec.
2.1
.
For the corresponding stress-energy tensor we have

T_{\phi}(z)=\frac{\mathfrak{s}_{0}}{2z^{2}}+\frac{\mathfrak{h}_{0}}{z}+\frac{\mathfrak{s}_{1}}{2(z-1)^{2}}+\frac{\mathfrak{h}_{1}}{z-1},\quad T_{\phi}(z)=\frac{\mathfrak{s}_{\infty}}{2z^{2}}+\frac{\mathfrak{h}_{\infty}}{z^{3}}+O(z^{-4})\quad\text{as}\quad z\to\infty,

where

\mathfrak{s}_{k}=-\beta_{k}(2+\beta_{k}),\quad k\in\{0,1,\infty\}.

(4.5)

The accessory parameters

\mathfrak{h}_{0}=-\mathfrak{h}_{1}=\frac{{\mathfrak{s}}_{0}+{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}-{\mathfrak{s}}_{\infty}}{2},\quad\mathfrak{h}_{\infty}=\frac{{\mathfrak{s}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{s}}_{\infty}-{\mathfrak{s}}_{0}}{2}

(4.6)

were first found by Schwarz. The stress-energy tensors
satisfy the relation

T_{f^{*}\phi}=(T_{\phi}\circ f)(f^{\prime})^{2}+\{f,x\}.

(4.7)

As a consequence, we obtain the following simple result:

Lemma 4.1

(Accessory parameters)
.

Let
f:\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x}\to\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{z}
be a Belyi function unramified outside of the set
\{0,1,\infty\}
and such that
f(\infty)\in\{0,1,\infty\}
. Then the stress-energy tensor
T_{f^{*}\phi}
of the pull back metric
f^{*}m_{\beta}=e^{f^{*}\phi}|dx|^{2}
of
m_{\beta}
by
f

satisfies the relations (
4.4
), where
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
with
n=\deg f+2
are the preimages of the points
\{0,1,\infty\}\subset\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{z}
under
f
, the weights of the second order poles in (
4.4
) are given by the equalities

s_{k}=-(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}((f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}+2)

with orders
(f^{*}\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}
from (
2.8
), and the accessory parameters
h_{k}
can be found as follows:

1. If
x_{k}
is not a pole of
f
, then the accessory parameter
h_{k}
satisfies

h_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{d_{k}}{c_{k}}{\mathfrak{s}}_{f(x_{k})}+c_{k}\mathfrak{h}_{f(x_{k})}&\text{ for }\quad\operatorname{ord}_{k}f=0,\\
-\frac{d_{k}}{c_{k}}\frac{(f^{*}\beta)_{k}((f^{*}\beta)_{k}+2)}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}&\text{ for }\quad\operatorname{ord}_{k}f>0,\end{array}\right.

(4.8)

where
\mathfrak{s}_{k}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{k}
are the same as in (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), and the coefficients
c_{k}
and
d_{k}
are those from the first or the second expansion

\displaystyle f(x)-f(x_{k})

\displaystyle=c_{k}x^{-\operatorname{ord}_{k}f-1}\left(1+\frac{d_{k}}{c_{k}}\frac{1}{x}+O(x^{-2})\right),\quad x\to x_{k}=\infty,

\displaystyle f(x)-f(x_{k})

\displaystyle=c_{k}(x-x_{k})^{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\left(1+\frac{d_{k}}{c_{k}}(x-x_{k})+O\left((x-x_{k})^{2}\right)\right),\quad x\to x_{k}\neq\infty,

depending on whether
x_{k}=\infty
or
x_{k}\neq\infty
,

2. If
x_{k}
is a pole of
f
, then the accessory parameter
h_{k}
satisfies

h_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{d_{k}}{c_{k}}{\mathfrak{s}}_{\infty}+\frac{1}{c_{k}}\mathfrak{h}_{\infty}&\text{ for }\quad\operatorname{ord}_{k}f=0,\\
\frac{d_{k}}{c_{k}}\frac{(f^{*}\beta)_{k}((f^{*}\beta)_{k}+2)}{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}&\text{ for }\quad\operatorname{ord}_{k}f>0,\end{array}\right.

(4.9)

where
\mathfrak{s}_{\infty}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{\infty}
are the same in (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), and the coefficients
c_{k}
and
d_{k}
are those from the first or the second expansion

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=c_{k}x^{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\left(1+\frac{d_{k}}{c_{k}}\frac{1}{x}+O(x^{-2})\right),\quad x\to x_{k}=\infty,

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle={c_{k}}{(x-x_{k})^{-\operatorname{ord}_{k}f-1}}\left(1+\frac{d_{k}}{c_{k}}(x-x_{k})+O((x-x_{k})^{2})\right),\quad x\to x_{k}\neq\infty,

depending on whether
x_{k}=\infty
or
x_{k}\neq\infty
.

Proof.

If
f(x_{k})\neq\infty
and
x_{k}=\infty
, then

f(x)-f(x_{k})=c_{k}x^{-\operatorname{ord}_{k}f-1}\left(1+\frac{d_{k}}{c_{k}}\frac{1}{x}+O(x^{-2})\right),\quad x\to x_{k}=\infty,

and the asymptotics can be differentiated. As a consequence, for the contributions into

(T_{\phi}\circ f)(f^{\prime})^{2}(x)=\left(\frac{-\beta_{f(x_{k})}(2+\beta_{f(x_{k})})}{2(f(x)-f(x_{k}))^{2}}+\frac{\mathfrak{h}_{f(x_{k})}}{f(x)-f(x_{k})}+O(1)\right)(f^{\prime}(x))^{2}

we obtain

\frac{(f^{\prime}(x))^{2}}{(f(x)-f(x_{k}))^{2}}=\frac{(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)^{2}}{x^{2}}+2\frac{d_{k}}{c_{k}}\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}{x^{3}}+O(x^{-4}),

\frac{h_{f(x_{k})}}{f(x)-f(x_{k})}(f^{\prime}(x))^{2}=h_{f(x_{k})}{c_{k}}(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)^{2}x^{-\operatorname{ord}_{k}f-3}+O(x^{-4}).

Besides, for the Schwarzian derivative, we get

\{f,x\}=-\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f)(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+2)\left(\frac{1}{x^{2}}+\frac{2d_{k}}{c_{k}(\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1)}\frac{1}{x^{3}}+O(x^{-4})\right),\quad x\to x_{k}=\infty.

These together with (
4.7
) imply

T_{f^{*}\phi}(x)=-\frac{(f^{*}\beta)_{k}((f^{*}\beta)_{k}+2)}{2x^{2}}+\frac{h_{k}}{x^{3}}+O(x^{-4})\quad\text{as}\quad x\to x_{k}=\infty,

where the accessory parameter
h_{k}
satisfies (
4.8
).

Similarly, if
f(x_{k})=\infty
and
x_{k}=\infty
, then starting with the asymptotics

f(x)=c_{k}x^{\operatorname{ord}_{k}f+1}\left(1+\frac{d_{k}}{c_{k}}\frac{1}{x}+O(x^{-2})\right),\quad x\to x_{k}=\infty,

we arrive at

T_{f^{*}\phi}(x)=-\frac{(f^{*}\beta)_{k}((f^{*}\beta)_{k}+2)}{2x^{2}}+\frac{h_{k}}{x^{3}}+O(x^{-4})\quad\text{as}\quad x\to x_{k}=\infty,

where the accessory parameter
h_{k}
satisfies (
4.9
).

The cases
f(x_{k})=\infty
with
x_{k}\neq\infty
, and
f(x_{k})\neq\infty
with
x_{k}\neq\infty
are similar, we omit the details.
∎

.

, and

). As is demonstrated in

[

Remark 4.3

Lemma 4.4

(After P. Zograf and L. Takhtajan)
.

Assume that
\beta_{k}\in(0,1)
,
k=1,\dots,n
, and
|\boldsymbol{\beta}|\leqslant-2
. Let
\varphi
be a (unique) solution to the Liouville equation (
4.2
) satisfying the area condition (
4.1
) with some fixed
S_{\varphi}>0
, and having the asymptotics (
4.3
). Then the Liouville action (
4.10
) meets the equalities

-\frac{1}{2\pi}{\partial_{x_{k}}\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}[\varphi]}=h_{k},\quad k=1,\dots,n,

(4.11)

where
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
are the moduli, and
h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{n}
are the accessory parameters.

Note that in the geometric setting of this paper, we have
\varphi=f^{*}\phi
. Thus the moduli
x_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,\deg f+2
, are the preimages of the points
\{0,1,\infty\}
under
f
, and the accessory parameters
h_{k}
are those found in Lemma
4.1
.

Proof.

As is shown in
[
18
]
, the expression in the right hand side of (
4.10
) is an equivalent regularization of the Liouville action introduced in
[
8
,
59
,
50
]
. Hence, in the case of
|\boldsymbol{\beta}|<-2
and
K=-1
the assertion of the lemma is just a reformulation of the result proven in
[
8
,
50
]
, see also
[
60
]
for the first proof of Polyakov’s conjecture (
4.11
). Next we show that in the (hyperbolic) case
|\boldsymbol{\beta}|<-2
the assertion remains valid for any
S_{\varphi}>0
and
K=2\pi(|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2)/S_{\varphi}<0
.

Consider a (unique) metric potential
\varphi
such that
|\boldsymbol{\beta}|<-2
and
K=-1
. Clearly,
S_{\varphi}=-2\pi(|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2)
, and for any
C>0
we have the following transformation laws for the surface area, the Liouville action, and the stress-energy tensor:

S_{\varphi+\log C}=C^{2}S_{\varphi},\quad\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}[\varphi+\log C]=\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}[\varphi]+4\pi(|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2)\log C,\quad T_{\varphi+\log C}=T_{\varphi}.

This implies that the rescaling

multiplies the total area by

, but does not affect the equalities (
4.11
).
Thus, in the case

In the case

is flat. As is known

\varphi(\boldsymbol{\beta})=\sum_{k:x_{k}\neq\infty}\beta_{k}\log|x-x_{k}|,\quad|\boldsymbol{\beta}|=-2.

(4.12)

As a result, the equality (
4.11
) follows from a simple direct computation.

Our choice of examples in Section

is partially motivated by the following result:

Theorem 4.6
.

The (hyperbolic or flat) surfaces of five Platonic solids and the regular constant curvature dihedra are critical points of the spectral determinant on the conical metrics of fixed area and fixed Gaussian curvature.

More precisely: Consider the divisors
\boldsymbol{\beta}=\sum_{k}\beta_{k}\cdot x_{k}
of degree
|\boldsymbol{\beta}|\leqslant-2
with distinct marked points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and weights
\beta_{k}\in(-1,0)
.
Then for any fixed
S>0
and any divisor
\boldsymbol{\beta}
there exists a unique metric
e^{2\varphi}|dx|^{2}
on
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
of total area
S
, Gaussian curvature
K=2\pi(|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2)/S
, and representing the divisor
\boldsymbol{\beta}
.
Consider the spectral determinant
\det\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
of the surface
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},e^{2\varphi}|dx|^{2})
as a function on the configuration space

\mathcal{Z}_{n}(S,K)=\left\{\boldsymbol{\beta}=\sum_{k\leqslant n}\beta_{k}\cdot x_{k}:x_{j}\neq x_{k}\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}\text{ for }j\neq k,\beta_{k}\in(-1,0),2\pi(|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2)=SK\right\}

with some fixed values
S>0
,
K\leqslant 0
, and
n\geqslant 3
.

1.

If
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}(S,K)
is a divisor such that the corresponding surface
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},e^{2\varphi}|dx|^{2})
is isometric to the one of a Platonic solid, then

\boldsymbol{\beta}_{0}
is a stationary point of the function

\mathcal{Z}_{n}(S,K)\ni\boldsymbol{\beta}\mapsto\det\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}},

(4.14)

where
n
is the number of vertices of the Platonic solid.

2.

If
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}(S,K)
is a divisor such that the corresponding surface
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},e^{2\varphi}|dx|^{2})
is isometric to the regular dihedron with
n
vertices, then

\boldsymbol{\beta}_{0}
is a stationary point of the function (
4.14
) with the corresponding value of
n
.

Proof.

Consider the potential
\varphi(x;\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4})
of a (unique) unit area constant curvature metric
e^{2\varphi}|dx|^{2}
representing the divisor

\boldsymbol{\beta}=\beta_{1}\cdot 0+\beta_{2}\cdot(-1)+\beta_{3}\cdot e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}}+\beta_{4}\cdot e^{-i\frac{\pi}{3}}.

Recall that the Gauss-Bonnet theorem
[
54
]
implies that the (regularized) Gaussian curvature of the surface
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},e^{2\varphi}|dx|^{2})
equals
2\pi(|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2)
. The four marked points in the divisor
\boldsymbol{\beta}
are in an equi-anharmonic position. In particular, if the orders of the conical singularities satisfy
\beta_{k}=|\boldsymbol{\beta}|/4
, then the surface
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},e^{2\varphi}|dx|^{2})
is isometric to the one of a unit area regular tetrahedron of Gaussian curvature
2\pi(|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2)\leqslant 0
.

Notice that
\varphi(\bar{x};\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4})
is the potential of a (unique) unit area Gaussian curvature
2\pi(|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2)
metric
representing the divisor

\boldsymbol{\beta}=\beta_{1}\cdot 0+\beta_{2}\cdot(-1)+\beta_{4}\cdot e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}}+\beta_{3}\cdot e^{-i\frac{\pi}{3}}.

Similarly, the potential
\varphi(e^{i\frac{2\pi}{3}}x;\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4})

corresponds to the divisor

\boldsymbol{\beta}=\beta_{1}\cdot 0+\beta_{4}\cdot(-1)+\beta_{2}\cdot e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}}+\beta_{3}\cdot e^{-i\frac{\pi}{3}};

\varphi(e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{3}}x;\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4})

corresponds to the divisor

\boldsymbol{\beta}=\beta_{1}\cdot 0+\beta_{3}\cdot(-1)+\beta_{4}\cdot e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}}+\beta_{2}\cdot e^{-i\frac{\pi}{3}};

and
\varphi(\frac{x+1}{2x-1};\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4})+\log 3-2\log|2x-1|

corresponds to the divisor

\boldsymbol{\beta}=\beta_{2}\cdot 0+\beta_{1}\cdot(-1)+\beta_{4}\cdot e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}}+\beta_{3}\cdot e^{-i\frac{\pi}{3}}.

As a consequence of these symmetries, we have

\varphi(x;\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4})=\varphi(\bar{x};\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{4},\beta_{3})=\varphi(e^{i\frac{2\pi}{3}}x;\beta_{1},\beta_{3},\beta_{4},\beta_{2})=\varphi(e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{3}}x;\beta_{1},\beta_{4},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})

=\varphi\left(\frac{x+1}{2x-1};\beta_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{4},\beta_{3}\right)+\log 3-2\log|2x-1|.

For the coefficients
\varphi_{k}=\varphi_{k}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4})
in the asymptotics (
4.3
)
the latter equalities imply

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{\ell})|_{\beta_{k}=|\boldsymbol{\beta}|/4}=(|\boldsymbol{\beta}|/4+1)\log 3,\quad\ell=2,3,4,

(4.15)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{4}(\partial_{\beta_{1}}\varphi_{j}-\partial_{\beta_{\ell}}\varphi_{j})|_{\beta_{k}=|\boldsymbol{\beta}|/4}=\log 3,\quad\ell=2,3,4.

Denote the Friedrichs Laplacian on
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},e^{2\varphi}|dx|^{2})
by
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
. As is proven in
[
18
, Sec. 2]
, the spectral determinant
\det\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
satisfies the anomaly formula

\log\det\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=-\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+1}{6}-\frac{1}{12\pi}\left(\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}[\varphi]-\pi\log\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}[\varphi]\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{4}\mathcal{C}(\beta_{k})+\bf C,

(4.16)

where
\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}[\varphi]
is the Liouville action (
4.10
) and

\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}[\varphi]=\exp\left\{2\sum_{k=1}^{4}\left(\beta_{k}+1-\frac{1}{\beta_{k}+1}\right)\varphi_{k}\right\}.

(4.17)

Since
|\boldsymbol{\beta}|
is fixed, we can set, for example,
\beta_{1}=|\boldsymbol{\beta}|-\sum_{k>1}\beta_{k}
, and consider the determinant
\det\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
as a function of
(\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\beta_{4})
. Then, thanks to the anomaly formula (
4.16
), Lemma
4.5
, and the equality (
4.17
), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\beta_{\ell}}\left(\log\det\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}|_{\beta_{1}=|\boldsymbol{\beta}|-\sum_{k>1}\beta_{k}}\right)|_{\beta_{k}=\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{\ell})|_{\beta_{k}=\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}\left(1+\left(\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}+1\right)^{-2}\right)(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{\ell})|_{\beta_{k}=\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}+1-\frac{1}{\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}+1}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{4}(\partial_{\beta_{1}}\varphi_{j}-\partial_{\beta_{\ell}}\varphi_{j})|_{\beta_{k}=\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}}.

Here the right-hand side is equal to zero because of (
4.15
).

Now we are in a position to study the determinant under a small perturbation of the coordinate of a vertex. Let us consider the potential
\varphi(x)
of a (unique) unit area constant curvature metric
e^{2\varphi}|dx|^{2}
representing the divisor

\boldsymbol{\beta}=\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}\cdot h+\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}\cdot(-1)+\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}\cdot e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}}+\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}\cdot e^{-i\frac{\pi}{3}}.

Here
h
is a small complex number. In the case
h=0
the surface
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},e^{2\varphi}|dx|^{2})
is isometric to the one of a unit area constant curvature regular tetrahedron.

Consider, for example, the rotation
x\mapsto e^{i\frac{2\pi}{3}}x
. Notice that
\chi(x):=\varphi(e^{i\frac{2\pi}{3}}x)
is the potential of a (unique) unit area Gaussian curvature
2\pi(|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2)
metric
representing the divisor

{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}\cdot(e^{-i\frac{2\pi}{3}}h)+\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}\cdot(-1)+\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}\cdot e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}}+\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}\cdot e^{-i\frac{\pi}{3}}.

The surfaces
({\overline{\mathbb{C}}}_{x},e^{2\varphi}|dx|^{2})
and
({\overline{\mathbb{C}}}_{x},e^{2\chi}|dx|^{2})
are isometric, the isometry is given by the rotation.
As a consequence,
\det\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\det\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
. Equating the directional derivative of
\det\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
along
h
with the one along
e^{i\frac{2\pi}{3}}h
, we immediately conclude that

\partial_{\Re h}\det\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\partial_{\Im h}\det\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=0.

It remains to note that the determinants of the Laplacians
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
and
\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^{S_{\varphi}}=\frac{1}{S_{\varphi}}\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
satisfy the standard rescaling property

\log\det\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^{S_{\varphi}}=\log\det\Delta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}+\zeta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(0)\log S_{\varphi},

where the value
\zeta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(0)
of the spectral zeta function at zero
[
20
]
does not depend on the moduli
x_{1},\dots,x_{4}
and satisfies

\zeta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(0)=\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|+2}{6}-\frac{1}{12}\sum_{k}\left(\beta_{k}+1-\frac{1}{\beta_{k}+1}\right)-1,\quad\partial_{\beta_{\ell}}\left(\zeta_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}(0)|_{\beta_{1}=|\boldsymbol{\beta}|-\sum_{k>1}\beta_{k}}\right)|_{\beta_{k}=\frac{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}{4}}=0.

Due to the invariance of the spectral determinant under the Möbius transformations, this completes the proof of the first assertion.

For the octahedron, cube, dodecahedron, icosahedron, and dihedra there are more symmetries to consider, but the idea and the steps of the proof remain exactly the same. We omit the details. The case of constant curvature (flat, spherical, or hyperbolic) metrics with three conical singularities is studied in
[
18
]
.
∎

As is well-known, starting from four punctures on the
2
-sphere, explicit construction of the general uniformization map is an open long-standing problem. In this paper, we rely on the uniformization via Belyi functions. There is another straightforward special case that deserves to be mentioned.

Remark 4.7
.

In the case of a divisor

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\left(-1/2\right)\cdot 0+\left(-1/2\right)\cdot 1+\left(-1/2\right)\cdot\lambda+\left(-1/2\right)\cdot\infty,\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C},

the corresponding constant curvature unit area metric
m_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
with three or four conical singularities of angle
\pi
can be written explicitly, e.g.
[
6
,
29
,
52
]
.

Recall that by using a suitable Möbius transformation we can always normalize the marked points so that any three of them are at
0,1,\infty
. As we permute the marked points
0,1,\lambda,\infty
by Möbius transformations so that three of them are still
0,1,\infty
, the fourth point is one of the following six:

\lambda,\quad\frac{1}{\lambda},\quad 1-\lambda,\quad\frac{1}{1-\lambda},\quad\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1},\quad\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}.

(4.18)

In general, these six points are distinct. The exceptions are the following three cases:

•

\lambda=0
or
\lambda=1
or
\lambda=\infty
. In this case, the set (
4.18
) contains only three distinct numbers:
0,1,\infty
. This case is studied in
[
18
]
, we do not discuss it here.

•

Harmonic position of four points:
\lambda=-1
or
\lambda=1/2
or
\lambda=2
. The set (
4.18
) contains only three distinct numbers:
-1,1/2,2
. The surface
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},m_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
is isometric to a unit area flat regular dihedron with four conical singularities of angle
\pi
.

•

Equi-anharmonic position of four points:
\lambda=\frac{1+i\sqrt{3}}{2}
or
\lambda=\frac{1-i\sqrt{3}}{2}
. The set (
4.18
) contains only the numbers
\frac{1\pm i\sqrt{3}}{2}
. The surface
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},m_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
is isometric to the surface of a unit area regular Euclidean tetrahedron.

In general, for
\lambda\neq 0,1,\infty
, the metric is flat, and we have

m_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=c^{2}_{\lambda}|x|^{-1}|x-1|^{-1}|x-\lambda|^{-1}|dx|^{2},

where
c^{2}_{\lambda}
is a scaling factor that guarantees that the surface
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},m_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
is of unit area, see
[
52
]
. For the spectral determinant of the Friedrichs Laplacian
\Delta_{\lambda}
on the flat surface
(\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{x},m_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
the anomaly formula (
4.16
) gives

\log\det\Delta_{\lambda}=-\log c_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{6}(\log|\lambda|+\log|\lambda-1|)-4\mathcal{C}(-1/2)+{\bf C},

where
{\bf C}
is the same as in (
2.11
). Besides, thanks to
[
20
, Appendix]
, we have

\mathcal{C}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)=-\zeta_{R}^{\prime}(-1)-\frac{1}{6}\log 2+\frac{1}{24}.

(4.19)

As is well-known, e.g.
[
9
, Sec. 2.9]
, the scaling factor
c^{2}_{\lambda}
satisfies

c^{-2}_{\lambda}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{dx\wedge d\bar{x}}{-2i|x|\cdot|x-1|\cdot|x-\lambda|}=8|k|\left(K^{\prime}\overline{K}+\overline{K^{\prime}}K\right),\quad\lambda=\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{4k},

where
K=K(k)
is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind, and
K^{\prime}=K(\sqrt{1-k^{2}})
.

In total, in terms of
\tau=iK^{\prime}/K
, we get

K=\frac{\pi}{2}\vartheta_{3}^{2}(0|\tau),\quad K^{\prime}=-i\tau K,\quad k=\frac{\vartheta_{2}^{2}(0|\tau)}{\vartheta_{3}^{2}(0|\tau)},

\det\Delta_{\lambda}=\frac{2^{2/3}}{\pi}|1-k^{2}|^{1/3}|k|^{1/6}\sqrt{\Im\tau}|K|=\sqrt{\Im\tau}|\eta(\tau/2)|^{2}.

Here
\eta(\tau/2)
is the Dedekind eta function. The last equality is a consequence of the identities

2\eta^{3}(\tau)=\vartheta_{2}(0|\tau)\vartheta_{3}(0|\tau)\vartheta_{4}(0|\tau),\quad\eta^{2}(\tau/2)=\vartheta_{4}(0|\tau)\eta(\tau),\quad\vartheta^{4}_{3}(0|\tau)=\vartheta^{4}_{2}(0|\tau)+\vartheta^{4}_{4}(0|\tau).

By analyzing the expression
\sqrt{\Im\tau}|\eta(\tau/2)|^{2}
, it is not hard to see that there are only two stationary points:
\tau=2i
is a saddle point, and
\tau=2e^{2\pi i/3}
is the unique absolute maximum of the determinant
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}\ni\lambda\mapsto\det\Delta_{\lambda}
, cf.
[
33
, Sec. 4]
. The case
\tau=2i
(resp.
\tau=2e^{i\pi/3}
) corresponds to a harmonic (resp. to an equi-anharmonic) position of four points in the divisor
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
, cf. Theorem
4.6
.

The reader may also find it interesting that in
[
28
, Sec. 3.5.2.]
the authors demonstrate that the determinant
\det\Delta_{\lambda}
is maximal for some (not equi-anharmonic nor harmonic) positions of four points on the unit circle.

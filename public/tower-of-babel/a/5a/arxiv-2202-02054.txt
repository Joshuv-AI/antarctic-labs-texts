Yamabe solitons on conformal Sasaki-like almost contact B-metric manifolds
Mancho Manev

Abstract
. A Yamabe soliton is defined on arbitrary almost contact B-metric manifold, which is obtained by a contact conformal transformation of the Reeb vector field, its dual contact 1-form, the B-metric, and its associated B-metric. The cases when the given manifold is cosymplectic or Sasaki-like are studied. In this way, manifolds from one of the main classes of the studied manifolds are obtained. The same class contains the conformally equivalent manifolds of cosymplectic manifolds by the usual conformal transformation of the B-metric. An explicit 5-dimensional example of a Lie group is given, which is characterized in relation to the obtained results.

Introduction

Richard Hamilton introduced the notion of the Yamabe flow in 1988 (see
[
8
]
) as an apparatus for constructing metrics with constant scalar curvature. The significance of this problem from the point of view of mathematical physics is that the Yamabe flow corresponds to the case of fast diffusion of the porous medium equation
[
4
]
.

A self-similar solution of the Yamabe flow, defined on a Riemannian manifold or a pseudo-Riemannian manifold, is called a Yamabe soliton and is determined by (
[
1
]
)

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{v}g=(\tau-\sigma)g,

where
\mathcal{L}_{v}g
stands for the Lie derivative of the metric
g
along the vector field
v
,
\tau
denotes the scalar curvature of
g
and
\sigma
is a constant.
Many authors have studied the Yamabe soliton on different types of manifolds since the introduction of this concept (see e.g.
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
7
,
14
]
).

In the present paper we begin the study of the mentioned Yamabe solitons on almost contact B-metric manifolds. The geometry of these manifolds is significantly influenced by the presence of two B-metrics, which are interconnected by the almost contact structure.

It is a well-known fact that the Yamabe flow preserves the conformal class of the metric.
So this gives us a reason to study Yamabe solitons and conformal transformations together.
Contact conformal transformations were studied in
[
11
]
, which transform not only the metric but also the Reeb vector field and its associated contact 1-form through the pair of B-metrics.
According to this work, the class of almost contact B-metric manifolds,
which is closed under the action of contact conformal transformations,
is the direct sum of the four main classes among the eleven basic classes of these manifolds
known from the classification of Ganchev-Mihova-Gribachev in
[
6
]
.
Main classes are called those whose manifolds are characterized by the fact that the covariant derivative of the structure tensors with respect to the Levi-Civita connection of some of the B-metrics is expressed only by the pair of B-metrics and the corresponding traces.

We study Yamabe solitons on two of the simplest types of manifolds among those studied, namely cosymplectic and Sasaki-like.
The former have parallel structure tensors with respect to the Levi-Civita connections of the B-metrics.
The latter are those whose complex cone is a Kähler manifold with a pair of Norden metrics, i.e. again with parallel structure tensors with respect to the Levi-Civita connections of the metrics.
Note that the class of Sasaki-like manifolds does not contain cosymplectic manifolds, although they are in each of the eleven basic classes of the classification used.

We find that the manifolds thus constructed in both cases belong to one of the main classes, the only one which contains the conformally equivalent manifolds of the cosymplectic ones by the usual conformal transformations.

The present paper is organized as follows.
Section 1 is devoted to the basic concepts of almost contact B-metric manifolds, contact conformal transformations of the structure tensors on them and the introduction of the notion of a Yamabe soliton on a transformed almost contact B-metric manifold.
In Section 2 and Section 3, we study the constructed manifolds when the initial manifold is cosymplectic and Sasaki-like, respectively.
In Section 4, we provide an explicit example of a Lie group as
a 5-dimensional manifold equipped with the structures studied.

1.
Almost contact B-metric manifolds, contact conformal transformations and Yamabe solitons

Let
(M,\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
be an almost contact manifold with
B-metric or an
almost contact B-metric manifold
, i.e.
M
is
a
(2n+1)

consisting of an endomorphism

\varphi

\xi

\eta

(1)

\displaystyle\varphi\xi=0,\quad\varphi^{2}=-\iota+\eta\otimes\xi,\quad\eta\circ\varphi=0,\quad\eta(\xi)=1,

(2)

\displaystyle g(X,Y)=-g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)+\eta(X)\eta(Y)

for arbitrary
X

of the algebra
\mathfrak{X}(M)
on the smooth vector

\iota
stands for the identity transformation on
\mathfrak{X}(M)

The manifolds

are also known as

(see e.g.
[
9

Further,

,
Y
,

will stand for arbitrary elements of

\mathfrak{X}(M)

The associated B-metric
\tilde{g}
of

on

is defined by

\tilde{g}(X,Y)=g(X,\varphi Y)\allowbreak+\eta(X)\eta(Y)

and it is also of signature
(n+1,n)
as its counterpart
g
.

The fundamental tensor
F
of type (0,3) on
(M,\allowbreak{}\varphi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,\allowbreak{}{}g)
is defined by

F(X,Y,Z)=g\bigl(\left(\nabla_{X}\varphi\right)Y,Z\bigr),

where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of

.
The following properties of

are consequences of (
1

\begin{split}F(X,Y,Z)&=F(X,Z,Y)\\[4.0pt]
&=F(X,\varphi Y,\varphi Z)+\eta(Y)F(X,\xi,Z)+\eta(Z)F(X,Y,\xi)\end{split}

and relations of

with
\nabla\xi
and
\nabla\eta
are:

(3)

\left(\nabla_{X}\eta\right)Y=g\left(\nabla_{X}\xi,Y\right)=F(X,\varphi Y,\xi).

The following 1-forms, known as Lee forms of the manifold, are associated with

:

\theta=g^{ij}F(E_{i},E_{j},\cdot),\qquad\theta^{*}=g^{ij}F(E_{i},\varphi E_{j},\cdot),\qquad\omega=F(\xi,\xi,\cdot),

where

are the components of the inverse matrix of

with respect to a basis
\left\{E_{i};\xi\right\}

(i=1,2,\dots,2n)
of
T_{p}M
.
The following general identities for the Lee forms of
(M,\allowbreak{}\varphi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,\allowbreak{}{}g)
are known from
[
10
]

(4)

\theta^{*}\circ\varphi=-\theta\circ\varphi^{2},\qquad\omega(\xi)=0.

A classification of the almost contact B-metric manifolds
in terms of
F
is given in
[
6
]
. This classification
includes eleven basic classes
\mathcal{F}_{1}
,
\mathcal{F}_{2}
,
\dots
,
\mathcal{F}_{11}
.
Their intersection is the special class
\mathcal{F}_{0}
defined by
condition for the vanishing of
F
. Hence
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is the class of cosymplectic B-metric manifolds,
where the structures
\varphi
,
\xi
,
\eta
,
g
,
\tilde{g}
are
\nabla
-parallel.

Using the pair of B-metrics

and
\tilde{g}
as well as
\eta\otimes\eta
, in
[
10
]
, the author and K. Gribachev introduced the so-called contact conformal transformation of the B-metric

into a new B-metric
\bar{g}
for
(M,\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
. Later, in
[
11
]
, this transformation is generalized as a contact conformal transformation that gives an almost contact B-metric structure
(\varphi,\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta},\bar{g})
as follows

(5)

\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{\xi}=e^{-w}\xi,\qquad\bar{\eta}=e^{w}\eta,\\[4.0pt]
\bar{g}=e^{2u}\cos{2v}\,g+e^{2u}\sin{2v}\,\tilde{g}+\left(e^{2w}-e^{2u}\cos{2v}-e^{2u}\sin{2v}\right)\eta\otimes\eta,\end{array}

where

are differentiable functions on
M
.

The corresponding tensors
\bar{F}
and
F
are related by
[
11
]
, see also
[
13
, (22)]
,

(6)

\displaystyle 2\bar{F}

\displaystyle(X,Y,Z)=2e^{2u}\cos{2v}\,F(X,Y,Z)

\displaystyle+e^{2u}\sin{2v}\left[F(\varphi Y,Z,X)-F(Y,\varphi Z,X)+F(X,\varphi Y,\xi)\eta(Z)\right]

\displaystyle+e^{2u}\sin{2v}\left[F(\varphi Z,Y,X)-F(Z,\varphi Y,X)+F(X,\varphi Z,\xi)\eta(Y)\right]

\displaystyle+(e^{2w}-e^{2u}\cos{2v})\left[F(X,Y,\xi)+F(\varphi Y,\varphi X,\xi)\right]\eta(Z)

\displaystyle+(e^{2w}-e^{2u}\cos{2v})\left[F(X,Z,\xi)+F(\varphi Z,\varphi X,\xi)\right]\eta(Y)

\displaystyle+(e^{2w}-e^{2u}\cos{2v})\left[F(Y,Z,\xi)+F(\varphi Z,\varphi Y,\xi)\right]\eta(X)

\displaystyle+(e^{2w}-e^{2u}\cos{2v})\left[F(Z,Y,\xi)+F(\varphi Y,\varphi Z,\xi)\right]\eta(X)

\displaystyle-2e^{2u}\left[\cos{2v}\,\alpha(Z)+\sin{2v}\,\beta(Z)\right]g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)

\displaystyle-2e^{2u}\left[\cos{2v}\,\alpha(Y)+\sin{2v}\,\beta(Y)\right]g(\varphi X,\varphi Z)

\displaystyle-2e^{2u}\left[\cos{2v}\,\beta(Z)-\sin{2v}\,\alpha(Z)\right]g(X,\varphi Y)

\displaystyle-2e^{2u}\left[\cos{2v}\,\beta(Y)-\sin{2v}\,\alpha(Y)\right]g(X,\varphi Z)

\displaystyle+2e^{2w}\eta(X)\left[\eta(Y)\mathrm{d}w(\varphi Z)+\eta(Z)\mathrm{d}w(\varphi Y)\right],

where we use the following notations for brevity

(7)

\alpha=\mathrm{d}u\circ\varphi+\mathrm{d}v,\qquad\beta=\mathrm{d}u-\mathrm{d}v\circ\varphi.

In the general case, the relations between the Lee forms of the manifolds
(M,\allowbreak{}\varphi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,\allowbreak{}{}g)
and
(M,\varphi,\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta},\bar{g})
are the following (see
[
11
]
)

(8)

\bar{\theta}=\theta+2n\,\alpha,\qquad\bar{\theta}^{*}=\theta^{*}+2n\,\beta,\qquad\bar{\omega}=\omega+\mathrm{d}w\circ\varphi.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that the B-metric
\bar{g}
generates a Yamabe soliton with potential the Reeb vector field
\bar{\xi}
and soliton constant
\bar{\sigma}
on a conformal almost contact B-metric manifold
(M,\varphi,\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta},\bar{g})
, if the following condition is satisfied

(9)

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}}\bar{g}=(\bar{\tau}-\bar{\sigma})\bar{g},

where
\bar{\tau}
is the scalar curvature of
\bar{g}
.

It is well known the following expression of the Lie derivative in terms of the covariant derivative with respect to the Levi-Civita connection
\bar{\nabla}
of
\bar{g}

(10)

\left(\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}}\bar{g}\right)(X,Y)=\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\bar{\xi},Y\right)+\bar{g}\left(X,\bar{\nabla}_{Y}\bar{\xi}\right).

2.
The case when the given manifold is cosymplectic

In this section, we consider
(M,\allowbreak{}\varphi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,\allowbreak{}{}g)
to be a cosymplectic manifold with almost contact B-metric structure, i.e. an
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-manifold, defined by covariant constant structure tensor
\varphi
(and consequently the same condition for
\xi
,
\eta
,
\tilde{g}
is valid) with respect to

\nabla
of
g
.
Therefore
F=0
and (
6
) takes the form

(11)

\displaystyle\bar{F}(X,Y,Z)=

\displaystyle-e^{2u}\left[\cos{2v}\,\alpha(Z)+\sin{2v}\,\beta(Z)\right]g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)

\displaystyle-e^{2u}\left[\cos{2v}\,\alpha(Y)+\sin{2v}\,\beta(Y)\right]g(\varphi X,\varphi Z)

\displaystyle-e^{2u}\left[\cos{2v}\,\beta(Z)-\sin{2v}\,\alpha(Z)\right]g(X,\varphi Y)

\displaystyle-e^{2u}\left[\cos{2v}\,\beta(Y)-\sin{2v}\,\alpha(Y)\right]g(X,\varphi Z)

\displaystyle+e^{2w}\eta(X)\left[\eta(Y)\mathrm{d}w(\varphi Z)+\eta(Z)\mathrm{d}w(\varphi Y)\right].

From the latter equality, bearing in mind the general identity (
3
) for the manifolds under consideration,
we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\bar{\xi},Y\right)=

\displaystyle-e^{2u-w}\left[\cos{2v}\,\beta(\xi)-\sin{2v}\,\alpha(\xi)\right]g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)

\displaystyle+e^{2u-w}\left[\cos{2v}\,\alpha(\xi)+\sin{2v}\,\beta(\xi)\right]g(X,\varphi Y)

\displaystyle+e^{w}\eta(X)\mathrm{d}w(\varphi^{2}Y).

Combining the last expression with (
7
) and (
10
) gives the following

(12)

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}}\bar{g}\right)(X,Y)=

\displaystyle-2e^{2u-w}\left[\cos{2v}\,\mathrm{d}{u}(\xi)-\sin{2v}\,\mathrm{d}{v}(\xi)\right]g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)

\displaystyle+2e^{2u-w}\left[\cos{2v}\,\mathrm{d}{v}(\xi)+\sin{2v}\,\mathrm{d}{u}(\xi)\right]g(X,\varphi Y)

\displaystyle+e^{w}\left[\eta(X)\mathrm{d}w(\varphi^{2}Y)+\eta(Y)\mathrm{d}w(\varphi^{2}X)\right].

Theorem 2.1
.

An almost contact B-metric manifold that is cosymplectic can be transformed by a contact conformal transformation of type (
5
) so that the transformed B-metric is a Yamabe soliton with potential the transformed Reeb vector field
and a soliton constant
\bar{\sigma}

if and only if the functions
(u,v,w)
of the used transformation satisfy the conditions

(13)

\mathrm{d}u(\xi)=0,\qquad\mathrm{d}v(\xi)=0,\qquad\mathrm{d}w=\mathrm{d}w(\xi)\eta.

Moreover, the obtained Yamabe soliton has a constant scalar curvature with value
\bar{\tau}=\bar{\sigma}
and the obtained almost contact B-metric manifold belongs to the subclass of the main class
\mathcal{F}_{1}
determined by the conditions:

(14)

\bar{\theta}=2n\left\{\mathrm{d}u\circ\varphi-\mathrm{d}v\circ\varphi^{2}\right\},\qquad\bar{\theta}^{*}=-2n\left\{\mathrm{d}u\circ\varphi^{2}+\mathrm{d}v\circ\varphi\right\}.

Proof.

Is we assume that
\bar{g}
generates a Yamabe soliton with potential
\bar{\xi}
and a soliton constant
\bar{\sigma}

\begin{split}&\ -e^{2u-w}\left\{\left[\cos{2v}\,\mathrm{d}{u}(\xi)-\sin{2v}\,\mathrm{d}v(\xi)\right]g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)\right.\\[4.0pt]
&\phantom{\hskip 48.0pt}\left.-\left[\sin{2v}\,\mathrm{d}{u}(\xi)+\cos{2v}\,\mathrm{d}v(\xi)\right]g(X,\varphi Y)\right\}\\[4.0pt]
&+\frac{1}{2}e^{w}\left\{\mathrm{d}w(\varphi^{2}X)\eta(Y)+\mathrm{d}w(\varphi^{2}Y)\eta(X)\right\}\\[4.0pt]
&=(\bar{\tau}-\bar{\sigma})\left\{-e^{2u}\left[\cos{2v}\,g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)-\sin{2v}\,g(X,\varphi Y)\right]\right.\\[4.0pt]
&\phantom{=(\bar{\tau}-\bar{\sigma})\left\{\right.}\left.+e^{2w}\eta(X)\eta(Y)\right\}.\end{split}

The substitution
\xi
for
Y
in the latter equality gives the following

(16)

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}w(\varphi^{2}X)=2e^{w}(\bar{\tau}-\bar{\sigma})\eta(X),\end{split}

which is valid if and only if

\bar{\tau}=\bar{\sigma}.

Therefore, (

\mathrm{d}w\circ\varphi=0
.
In this case, because of (
15
), we obtain
\mathrm{d}u(\xi)=\mathrm{d}v(\xi)=0
, which completes (
13
).

Let us remark that
\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}}\bar{g}
vanishes because of (

) and (
9
), i.e.
\bar{\xi}
is a Killing vector field in the considered case.

After that, we apply the following relations to (

), which are equivalent to the last formula in (

\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{g}(\varphi X,\varphi Y)=e^{2u}\cos{2v}\,g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)-e^{2u}\sin{2v}\,g(X,\varphi Y),\\[4.0pt]
\bar{g}(X,\varphi Y)=e^{2u}\cos{2v}\,g(X,\varphi Y)+e^{2u}\sin{2v}\,g(\varphi X,\varphi Y).\end{array}

Then we use (

) and the formula

from (

\displaystyle\bar{F}(X,Y,Z)=

\displaystyle\bar{g}(\varphi X,\varphi Y)\,\alpha(\varphi^{2}Z)+\bar{g}(X,\varphi Y)\,\beta(\varphi^{2}Z)

\displaystyle+\bar{g}(\varphi X,\varphi Z)\,\alpha(\varphi^{2}Y)+\bar{g}(X,\varphi Z)\,\beta(\varphi^{2}Y).

Now, we substitute the Lee forms of
\bar{F}
from (

\displaystyle\bar{F}(X,Y,Z)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}\Bigl\{\bar{g}(\varphi X,\varphi Y)\,\bar{\theta}(\varphi^{2}Z)+\bar{g}(X,\varphi Y)\,\bar{\theta}^{*}(\varphi^{2}Z)

\displaystyle+\bar{g}(\varphi X,\varphi Z)\,\bar{\theta}(\varphi^{2}Y)+\bar{g}(X,\varphi Z)\,\bar{\theta}^{*}(\varphi^{2}Y)\Bigr\}.

The last expression of

means that the obtained manifold belongs to the main class

, according to the classification in

, and the corresponding Lee forms have the following properties:

\bar{\theta}=-\bar{\theta}\circ\varphi^{2},\qquad\bar{\theta}^{*}=-\bar{\theta}^{*}\circ\varphi^{2},\qquad\bar{\omega}=0.

Finally, taking into account (

∎

3.
The case when the given manifold is Sasaki-like

In the present section, we suppose that the given almost contact B-metric manifold
(M,\allowbreak{}\varphi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,\allowbreak{}{}g)
is Sasaki-like, i.e. the

If

\nabla_{X}\xi=-\varphi X.

The Sasaki-like condition in terms of

given in

]

F(X,Y,Z)=g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)\eta(Z)+g(\varphi X,\varphi Z)\eta(Y).

Then, by virtue of (

&+e^{w}\left\{\mathrm{d}w(\varphi^{2}X)\eta(Y)+\mathrm{d}w(\varphi^{2}Y)\eta(X)\right\}.\end{split}

Theorem 3.1
.

An almost contact B-metric manifold that is Sasaki-like can be transformed by a contact conformal transformation of type (
5
) so that the transformed B-metric is a Yamabe soliton with potential the transformed Reeb vector field and a soliton constant
\bar{\sigma}
if and only if the functions
(u,v,w)
of the used transformation satisfy the conditions

(22)

\mathrm{d}u(\xi)=0,\qquad\mathrm{d}v(\xi)=1,\qquad\mathrm{d}w=\mathrm{d}w(\xi)\eta.

Moreover, the obtained Yamabe soliton has a constant scalar curvature with a value
\bar{\tau}=\bar{\sigma}
and the transformed almost contact B-metric manifold belongs to a subclass of the main class
\mathcal{F}_{1}
determined by:

(23)

\bar{\theta}=2n\left\{\mathrm{d}u\circ\varphi-\mathrm{d}v\circ\varphi^{2}\right\},\qquad\bar{\theta}^{*}=2n\left\{\mathrm{d}u-\mathrm{d}v\circ\varphi\right\}.

Proof.

The expression in (
21
) and the assumption that
\bar{g}
generates a Yamabe soliton with potential
\bar{\xi}

\bar{\sigma}

(26)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}w=\mathrm{d}w(\xi)\eta.

Substitute (

\displaystyle\bar{F}(X,Y,Z)=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2u}}{2n}\Bigl\{\left[\cos{2v}\{\alpha(Z)-\eta(Z)\}+\sin{2v}\,\beta(Z)\right]g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)

\displaystyle+\left[\cos{2v}\,\beta(Z)-\sin{2v}\{\alpha(Z)-\eta(Z)\}\right]g(X,\varphi Y)

\displaystyle+\left[\cos{2v}\{\alpha(Y)-\eta(Y)\}+\sin{2v}\,\beta(Y)\right]g(\varphi X,\varphi Z)

\displaystyle+\left[\cos{2v}\,\beta(Y)-\sin{2v}\{\alpha(Y)-\eta(Y)\}\right]g(X,\varphi Z)\Bigr\}.

Using our assumption that
(M,\allowbreak{}\varphi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,\allowbreak{}{}g)

\theta=-2n\,\eta,\qquad\theta^{*}=\omega=0.

) and get the formula

\displaystyle\bar{F}(X,Y,Z)=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2u}}{2n}\Bigl\{\left[\cos{2v}\,\bar{\theta}(\varphi^{2}Z)+\sin{2v}\,\bar{\theta}(\varphi Z)\right]g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)

\displaystyle+\left[\cos{2v}\,\bar{\theta}(\varphi Z)-\sin{2v}\,\bar{\theta}(\varphi^{2}Z)\right]g(X,\varphi Y)

\displaystyle+\left[\cos{2v}\,\bar{\theta}(\varphi^{2}Y)+\sin{2v}\,\bar{\theta}(\varphi Y)\right]g(\varphi X,\varphi Z)

\displaystyle+\left[\cos{2v}\,\bar{\theta}(\varphi Y)-\sin{2v}\,\bar{\theta}(\varphi^{2}Y)\right]g(X,\varphi Z)\Bigr\}.

Then, we apply (
18
) in the last equality
and obtain the following expression

\displaystyle\bar{F}(X,Y,Z)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}\Bigl\{\bar{\theta}(\varphi^{2}Z)\bar{g}(\varphi X,\varphi Y)+\bar{\theta}(\varphi Z)\bar{g}(X,\varphi Y)

\displaystyle+\bar{\theta}(\varphi^{2}Y)\bar{g}(\varphi X,\varphi Z)+\bar{\theta}(\varphi Y)\bar{g}(X,\varphi Z)\Bigr\}.

The obtained form of
\bar{F}
coincides with the definition
(M,\varphi,\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta},\bar{g})
to belong to the basic class
\mathcal{F}_{1}
, according to the classification of Ganchev-Mihova-Gribachev in
[
6
]
.

Finally, the expression of the Lee forms of the transformed manifold given in (
23
) follows from (
28
) and (
7
).
∎

4.
Example

We recall a known example of a Sasaki-like manifold, given in
[
9
]
as Example 2.
A Lie group
G
of
dimension
5
is considered to have
a basis of left-invariant vector fields
\{E_{0},\dots,E_{4}\}

defined by the following commutators for
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
:

\begin{array}[]{ll}[E_{0},E_{1}]=\lambda E_{2}+E_{3}+\mu E_{4},&[E_{0},E_{2}]=-\lambda E_{1}-\mu E_{3}+E_{4},\\[4.0pt]
[E_{0},E_{3}]=-E_{1}-\mu E_{2}+\lambda E_{4},&[E_{0},E_{4}]=\mu E_{1}-E_{2}-\lambda E_{3}.\end{array}

An invariant almost contact B-metric structure is then defined
on
G
by

\begin{array}[]{rl}&g(E_{0},E_{0})=g(E_{1},E_{1})=g(E_{2},E_{2})=1\\[4.0pt]
&g(E_{3},E_{3})=g(E_{4},E_{4})=-1,\\[4.0pt]
&g(E_{i},E_{j})=0,\quad i,j\in\{0,1,2,3,4\},\;i\neq j,\\[4.0pt]
&\xi=E_{0},\quad\varphi E_{1}=E_{3},\quad\varphi E_{2}=E_{4}.\end{array}

It is verified that the constructed manifold
(G,\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)

is an almost contact B-metric manifold that is Sasaki-like.

In
[
12
]
, the components
R_{ijkl}=R(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})
of its curvature tensor are calculated
and the type of the corresponding Ricci tensor
\rho
is found. Namely, it is
\rho=4\,\eta\otimes\eta
and the scalar curvature is
\tau=4
.

Using the non-zero ones of
R_{ijkl}
determined by the following equalities:

\begin{array}[]{l}R_{0110}=R_{0220}=-R_{0330}=-R_{0440}=1,\\[4.0pt]
R_{1234}=R_{1432}=R_{2341}=R_{3412}=1,\qquad R_{1331}=R_{2442}=1\end{array}

and the properties
R_{ijkl}=-R_{jikl}=-R_{ijlk}
, we compute that the associated quantity
\tau^{*}
of
\tau
is zero, i.e.

\tau^{*}=g^{ij}\rho(E_{i},\varphi E_{j})=0.

Now, we define the following functions on
\mathbb{R}^{5}=\left\{(x^{0},x^{1},x^{2},x^{3},x^{4})\right\}
:

(30)

\begin{split}u&=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left\{\left[(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{3})^{2}\right]\left[(x^{2})^{2}+(x^{4})^{2}\right]\right\},\\[4.0pt]
v&=\arctan\frac{x^{1}x^{4}+x^{2}x^{3}}{x^{3}x^{4}-x^{1}x^{2}}+x^{0},\\[4.0pt]
w&=x^{0}.\end{split}

The non-zero ones between their partial derivatives are the following:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x^{1}}=-\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial x^{3}}=\dfrac{x^{1}}{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{3})^{2}},&\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x^{2}}=-\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial x^{4}}=\dfrac{x^{2}}{(x^{2})^{2}+(x^{4})^{2}},\\[9.0pt]
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x^{3}}=\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial x^{1}}=\dfrac{x^{3}}{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{3})^{2}},&\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x^{4}}=\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial x^{2}}=\dfrac{x^{4}}{(x^{2})^{2}+(x^{4})^{2}},\\[9.0pt]
\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial x^{0}}=\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial x^{0}}=1.\end{array}

Then, for an arbitrary vector field
X=X^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}
,
i\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}
, we have

\varphi X=-X^{3}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{1}}-X^{4}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{2}}+X^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{3}}+X^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{4}},\quad X^{0}=\eta(X),\quad\xi=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{0}}.

We verify immediately that the functions defined by (
30
) satisfy the properties

\mathrm{d}u=-\mathrm{d}v\circ\varphi,\qquad\mathrm{d}u(\xi)=0,\qquad\mathrm{d}v(\xi)=1,\qquad\mathrm{d}w=\eta.

Let us consider a contact conformal transformation defined by (
5
), where the functions
(u,v,w)
are determined as in (
30
).

Then, the transformed manifold
(G,\varphi,\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta},\bar{g})
is an
\mathcal{F}_{1}
-manifold with a Yamabe soliton with potential
\bar{\xi}
and a constant scalar curvature
\bar{\tau}=\bar{\sigma}
, according ot Theorem
3.1
.
Moreover, taking into account (
23
), we obtain the corresponding Lee forms as follows

\bar{\theta}=4n\,\mathrm{d}u\circ\varphi,\qquad\bar{\theta}^{*}=4n\,\mathrm{d}u,\qquad\bar{\omega}=0.

According to Proposition 8 in
[
9
]
,
the Ricci tensor of an almost contact B-metric manifold is invariant under a contact homothetic transformation (i.e. when
u
,
v
,
w
are constants), and therefore for the corresponding scalar curvatures we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{\tau}=e^{-2u}\cos{2v}\,\tau-e^{-2u}\sin{2v}\,\tau^{*}+\left\{e^{-2w}-e^{-2u}\cos{2v}\right\}\rho(\xi,\xi),\\[4.0pt]
\bar{\tau}^{*}=e^{-2u}\sin{2v}\,\tau+e^{-2u}\cos{2v}\,\tau^{*}-e^{-2u}\sin{2v}\,\rho(\xi,\xi).\end{array}

Then, bearing in mind the last results, we obtain for our example that

\bar{\tau}=4\,e^{-2w},\qquad\bar{\tau}^{*}=0.

Hence,
(G,\varphi,\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta},\bar{g})
is scalar flat and the Yamabe soliton constant for
\bar{g}
is
\bar{\sigma}=4
, and thus the obtained Yamabe soliton is shrinking.

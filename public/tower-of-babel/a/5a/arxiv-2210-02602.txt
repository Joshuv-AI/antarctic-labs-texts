Convergence rates of the Kaczmarz-Tanabe method for linear systems Journal: Journal of L a T e X Templates
Chuan-gang Kang

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the Kaczmarz-Tanabe method for exact and inexact linear systems. The Kaczmarz-Tanabe method is derived from the Kaczmarz method, but is more stable than that. We analyze the convergence and the convergence rate of the Kaczmarz-Tanabe method based on the singular value decomposition theory, and discover two important factors, i.e., the second maximum singular value of Q Q and the minimum non-zero singular value of A A , that influence the convergence speed and the amplitude of fluctuation of the Kaczmarz-Tanabe method (even for the Kaczmarz method). Numerical tests verify the theoretical results of the Kaczmarz-Tanabe method. If there is perturbation on the right-hand side of (
1
), i.e.,

\displaystyle Ax=b^{\delta},

(19)

where
b^{\delta}=b+\delta b
, then Kaczmarz-Tanabe’s iteration (
7
) (or (
8
)) can be described as

\displaystyle y_{k+1}^{\delta}=(I-A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}MA)y_{k}^{\delta}+A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}Mb^{\delta}=Qy_{k}^{\delta}+A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}Mb^{\delta},\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots.

(20)

Moreover it follows from (
9
) and (
20
) that

\displaystyle y_{k+1}^{\delta}-P_{N(A)}y_{0}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=(I-A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}MA)(y_{k}^{\delta}-P_{N(A)}y_{0}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})+A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M(b^{\delta}-b).

Let
e_{k}^{\delta}=y_{k}^{\delta}-P_{N(A)}y_{0}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}
and
r_{k}^{\delta}=b^{\delta}-Ay_{k}^{\delta}
, consequently there also hold

\displaystyle e_{k+1}^{\delta}=(I-A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}MA)e_{k}^{\delta}+A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M(b^{\delta}-b)=Qe_{k}^{\delta}+A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M(b^{\delta}-b)

(21)

and

\displaystyle r_{k+1}^{\delta}=(I-AA_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M)r_{k}^{\delta}.

(22)

Theorem
3.19
gives the monotonicity of the residues for the Kaczmarz-Tanabe method to solve (
19
).

Theorem 3.19
.

Let
\{r_{k}^{\delta},k\geq 0\}
be the sequence of vectors generated by (
22
) and
\mathcal{L}=I-AA_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M
, then there holds

\displaystyle\|r_{k+1}^{\delta}\|_{2}^{2}=\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|_{2}^{2}-\langle(I-\mathcal{L}^{T}\mathcal{L})r_{k}^{\delta},r_{k}^{\delta}\rangle.

Moreover, if
\|\mathcal{L}\|_{2}\leq 1
there also holds

\displaystyle\|r_{k+1}^{\delta}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|r_{k}^{\delta}\|_{2}^{2}.

Remark 3.20
.

The results can be derived from the procedure of the proof to Theorem
2.16
.

The following theorem gives the results of the convergence rate for the Kaczmarz-Tanabe method to solve the inexact linear system (
19
).

Theorem 3.21
.

For any matrix
A
with nonzero rows and any
m
-dimensional column vector
b^{\delta}
, let
\{y_{k}^{\delta},k\geq 0\}
be the sequence of vectors generated by (
20
), then there hold

\displaystyle\|e_{k+1}^{\delta}\|_{2}\leq\mathcal{K}^{1/2}\|e_{k}^{\delta}\|_{2}+2\|A^{\dagger}\|_{2}\|b^{\delta}-b\|_{2}

(23)

and

\displaystyle\|e_{k+1}^{\delta}\|_{2}\leq\mathcal{K}^{(k+1)/2}\|e_{0}^{\delta}\|_{2}+4(1-\mathcal{K}^{(k+1)/2})\max\limits_{i=1,\ldots,p}\{(1-\sigma_{i}^{2})^{-1},1\}\|A^{\dagger}\|_{2}\|b^{\delta}-b\|_{2},

(24)

where
\mathcal{K}
and
\sigma_{i}
are defined in Theorem
2.17
.

Proof.

From (
21
), we have

\displaystyle\|e_{k+1}^{\delta}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle(I-A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}MA)e_{k}^{\delta}+A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M(b^{\delta}-b),(I-A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}MA)e_{k}^{\delta}+A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M(b^{\delta}-b)\rangle

\displaystyle=\|(I-A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}MA)e_{k}^{\delta}\|_{2}^{2}+2\langle A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M(b^{\delta}-b),(I-A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}MA)e_{k}^{\delta}\rangle+\langle A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M(b^{\delta}-b),A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M(b^{\delta}-b)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\big(\|(I-A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}MA)e_{k}^{\delta}\|_{2}+\|A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M(b^{\delta}-b)\|_{2}\big)^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|e_{k+1}^{\delta}\|_{2}\leq\|(I-A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}MA)e_{k}^{\delta}\|_{2}+\|A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M(b^{\delta}-b)\|_{2}.

(25)

From (
15
) and Lemma
1.2
, then it follows from (
25
) that

\displaystyle\|e_{k+1}^{\delta}\|_{2}\leq\Big(1-\frac{1}{\|\big((I-Q^{T}Q)^{1/2}\big)^{\dagger}\|_{2}^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}\|e_{k}^{\delta}\|_{2}+\|A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M(b^{\delta}-b)\|_{2}.

(26)

Then from Theorems
1.5
and
1.3
, we have
A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M=(I-\tilde{Q})A^{\dagger}
and
\|\tilde{Q}\|_{2}\leq 1
, consequently,

\displaystyle\|A_{\mathcal{S}}^{T}M\|_{2}=\|(I-\tilde{Q})A^{\dagger}\|_{2}\leq\|(I-\tilde{Q})\|_{2}\|A^{\dagger}\|_{2}\leq(1+\|\tilde{Q}\|_{2})\|A^{\dagger}\|_{2}\leq 2\|A^{\dagger}\|_{2}.

(27)

Hence from (
17
), (
26
) and (
27
), there holds

\displaystyle\|e_{k+1}^{\delta}\|_{2}\leq\big(1-\min\limits_{i=1,2,\ldots,p}\{1-\sigma_{i}^{2},1\}\big)^{1/2}\|e_{k}^{\delta}\|_{2}+2\|A^{\dagger}\|_{2}\|b^{\delta}-b\|_{2},

which proves (
23
). Then from the recursion of (
23
) there holds

\displaystyle\|e_{k+1}^{\delta}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{K}^{(k+1)/2}\|e_{0}^{\delta}\|_{2}+2\big(1-\mathcal{K}^{(k+1)/2}\big)(2-\mathcal{K})\cdot\max\limits_{i=1,\ldots,p}\{(1-\sigma_{i}^{2})^{-1},1\}\|A^{\dagger}\|_{2}\|b^{\delta}-b\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{K}^{(k+1)/2}\|e_{0}^{\delta}\|_{2}+4\big(1-\mathcal{K}^{(k+1)/2}\big)\cdot\max\limits_{i=1,\ldots,p}\{(1-\sigma_{i}^{2})^{-1},1\}\|A^{\dagger}\|_{2}\|b^{\delta}-b\|_{2}.

Thus (
24
) is proved.∎

Theorem
3.21
presents the convergence rates of the Kaczmarz-Tanabe method for a perturbed linear system. From (
24
), the error of each iteration consists of two parts, i.e., the iterative error and the perturbed error. The iterative error is decreasing and the perturbed error is increasing about iteration number
k
, they are determined by the factor
\big(1-\min\limits_{i=1,\ldots,p}\{1-\sigma_{i}^{2},1\}\big)^{(k+1)/2}
. However, the amplitude of the fluctuation is nevertheless related with the quantity
\max\limits_{i=1,\ldots,p}\{(1-\sigma_{i}^{2})^{-1},1\}\|A^{\dagger}\|_{2}
. Moreover, the iterative error dominates the iterations to converge at the previous stage, and the perturbed error dominates the iterations to diverge at the latter stage. The more general conclusion is that the faster the iterations converge at the former stage and the faster the iterations diverge at the latter stage.

4
The algorithm of the Kaczmarz-Tanabe method

In this section, we present the algorithm of the Kaczmarz-Tanabe method in Algorithm
4.1
which can avoid a lot of repeated calculation. Algorithm
4.1
is close to optimal without considering parallelism. The calculation cost of the Kaczmarz-Tanabe method mainly comes from the generation of
A_{\mathcal{S}}
and
Q
(that is in the Process I of Algorithm
4.1
), and the cost of Process II is very small. Especially, once
A_{\mathcal{S}}
and
Q
are generated, then they can be used repeatedly for more scenes. However, the Kaczmarz method doesn’t have this advantage.

Algorithm 4.1

The Kaczmarz-Tanabe method solver

Step 1. Given
A,b^{\delta}
, an initial guess
y_{0}^{\delta}
and the maximum iteration number
K_{\max}
;

Step 2. generate the diagonal matrix
M
;

Step 3.
[m,n]=size(A)
;

Process I. Compute
A_{\mathcal{S}}
and
Q

Step 4.
i=1;Q=I_{n}
;

Step 5.
A_{\mathcal{S}}(:,i)=Q*A(i,:)^{T}
;

Step 6.
Q=Q-Q*A(i,:)^{T}*A(i,:)/\|A(i,:)\|_{2}^{2}
;

Step 7.
i=i+1
, if
i\leq m
, go to Step 5; otherwise, go to step 8.

process II. Perform Kaczmarz-Tanabe’s iteration

Step 8.
k=0,Vc=A_{\mathcal{S}}*M*b^{\delta}
;

Step 9.
y_{k+1}^{\delta}=Q*y_{k}^{\delta}+Vc
;

Step 10.
k=k+1
, if
k\leq K_{\max}
, go to Step 9; otherwise, output the numerical solution
y_{k}^{\delta}
.

6
Conclusion

The Kaczmarz-Tanabe method is essentially the Kaczmarz subsequence method. We extract periodically the last iteration of each epoch for Kaczmarz’s iteration and construct a new sequence of vectors, which can actually be generated by a matrix multiply a vector. These operations are beneficial to study the Kaczmarz method for linear system as a whole rather than as a standalone equation. Consequently, we can obtain more general results of the Kaczmarz method.

Numerical results in this paper show that the Kaczmarz-Tanabe method (even the Kaczmarz method) is very suitable for a exact linear system, which can also be verified by Theorem
2.17
. When there exists perturbation in linear system, the amplitude of fluctuation of the iterative error is determined by the second maximum singular value of
Q
and the minimum non-zero singular value of
A

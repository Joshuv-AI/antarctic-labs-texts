Dynamics over Homogeneous Spaces
FİLİZ ÇAĞATAY UÇGUN, Oğul Esen, Serkan Sütlü

Abstract
. We present the Euler-Lagrange and Hamilton’s equations for a system whose configuration space is a unified product Lie group G = M ⋈ γ H G=M\bowtie_{\gamma}H , for some γ : M × M → H \gamma:M\times M\to H . By reduction, then, we obtain the Euler-Lagrange type and Hamilton’s type equations of the same form for the quotient space M ≅ G / H M\cong G/H , although it is not necessarily a Lie group. We observe, through further reduction, that it is possible to formulate the Euler-Poincaré type and Lie-Poisson type equations on the corresponding quotient 𝔪 ≅ 𝔤 / 𝔥 \mathfrak{m}\cong\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h} of Lie algebras, which is not a priori a Lie algebra. Moreover, we realize the n n th order iterated tangent group T ( n ) ​ G T^{(n)}G of a Lie group G G as an extension of the n n th order tangent group T n ​ G T^{n}G of the same type. More precisely, 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} being the Lie algebra of G G , T ( n ) G ≅ 𝔤 × 2 n − 1 − n ⋈ γ T n G T^{(n)}G\cong\mathfrak{g}^{\times\,2^{n}-1-n}\bowtie_{\gamma}T^{n}G for some γ : 𝔤 × 2 n − 1 − n × 𝔤 × 2 n − 1 − n → T n ​ G \gamma:\mathfrak{g}^{\times\,2^{n}-1-n}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\times\,2^{n}-1-n}\to T^{n}G . We thus obtain the n n th order Euler-Lagrange (and then the n n th order Euler-Poincaré) equations over T n ​ G T^{n}G by reduction from those on T ⁡ ( T n − 1 ​ G ) T(T^{n-1}G) . Finally, we illustrate our results in the realm of the Kepler problem, and the non-linear tokamak plasma dynamics.



1.
Introduction

A
Klein geometry
is defined as a pair
(G,H)
of Lie groups, where
H\subseteq G
is a closed subgroup. The group
G
is then called the
principal group
, while the quotient
G/H
, which happens to be a smooth manifold, is referred as the
space
of the Klein geometry.

The classical geometries may all be realized as Klein geometries. For instance, the Euclidean geometry is encoded by the pair
(E(n),O(n))
of the Euclidean group
E(n)\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes O(n)
, and the orthogonal group
O(n)
. The underlying space, then, is the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Similarly, the affine geometry is modeled by the pair
(Aff(n),GL(n))
of the affine group
Aff(n)\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes GL(n)
and the general linear group
GL(n)
, in which case the underlying space is the affine space. The spherical geometry, on the other hand, is represented by
(O(n+1),O(n))
, and its underlying space happens to be the
n
-sphere
S^{n}
. We refer the reader to
[
30
]
for further details, and the Klein models of the projective, hyperbolic, conformal, and elliptic geometries.

A Klein geometry
(G,H)
is called
reductive
, in which case the underlying space
G/H
is named as a
reductive homogeneous space
, if the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
H
has an
H
-invariant complement in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
. Any
symmetric space

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{h},

so that

(1.1)

[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}]\subseteq\mathfrak{h},\qquad[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{m}]\subseteq\mathfrak{m},\qquad[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]\subseteq\mathfrak{h}.

A comparable bracket pattern may be observed in the structure of semisimple Lie algebras,
[
22

. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a (finite-dimensional) complex semisimple Lie algebra. Then,
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
being the Cartan subalgebra, we have

\mathfrak{g}=\left(\bigoplus_{\alpha\neq 0}\,\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\right)\oplus\mathfrak{h},

where each
\alpha
is a (non-wanishing) linear form on
\mathfrak{h}
, called a
root
, and
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is the eigenspace of the operator
\alpha
, called the corresponding
root subspace
. Moreover, setting a basis
H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{\ell}
of
\mathfrak{h}
, it is possible to choose basis vector

\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}

[E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha}]=H_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{h}

\Delta

\alpha

H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{\ell},\quad E_{\alpha},\,\,\alpha\in\Delta

happens to be a basis for
\mathfrak{g}
, called the
Cartan-Weyl basis
. The bracket operation on
\mathfrak{g}
may then be given by

[H_{i},H_{j}]=0,\quad[H_{i},E_{\alpha}]=\alpha(H_{i})E_{\alpha},\quad[E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha}]=H_{\alpha},\quad[E_{\alpha},E_{\beta}]=N_{\alpha\beta}E_{\alpha+\beta}

for any
\alpha,\beta\in\Delta
, so that
\alpha+\beta\neq 0
, and any

, where

\alpha+\beta\notin\Delta

The algebraic foundations of such Lie algebra decompositions has first been studied thoroughly in

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{h}

unified product
of
\mathfrak{h}

On the other hand, for a physical system whose configuration space is a manifold, the Lagrangian realization of the dynamics is determined by a Lagrangian function on the tangent bundle of the configuration space by means of a variational principle, while the Hamiltonian realization is encoded by a Hamiltonian function over the cotangent bundle of the configuration space,
[
1
,
6

.

In case the configuration space is a Lie group
G
, then relying on the (semi-direct product) trivialization
TG\cong G\ltimes\mathfrak{g}
of the tangent group (into
G

\mathfrak{g}
), the equations (of motion) that govern the dynamics are given by

(1.2)

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\xi}=T^{\ast}L_{g}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta g}\right)-\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits^{\ast}_{\xi}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\xi}\right),

which are known as the (trivialized)
Euler-Lagrange equations
of the (trivialized) Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}:G\ltimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(g,\xi)
, see for instance
[
16

From the Hamiltonian point of view, on the other hand, appealing to the trivialization
T^{\ast}G\cong\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\rtimes G

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{d\mu}{dt}=-T^{\ast}R_{g}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta g}\right)+\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits^{\ast}_{\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\mu}}(\mu),\\
&\frac{dg}{dt}=TR_{g}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\mu}\right),\end{split}

which are called the

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}(\mu,g)

[

The theory of reduction in mechanical systems involves investigating how the dimensionality of the system’s phase space can be reduced through the utilization of available symmetries and associated conservation laws. In the case the systems configuration space is a Lie group
G
, both the tangent group
TG
and the cotangent group
T^{\ast}G
serve as excellent examples of systems that exhibit symmetry. In either of these cases the symmetry is encoded by the action of the Lie group
G
.

In the Lagrangian framework, the reduction of (
1.2
) by the
G
-action then yields the
Euler-Poincaré equations

(1.4)

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\xi}=-\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits^{\ast}_{\xi}\left(\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\xi}\right),

of the reduced Lagrangian
\ell:G\backslash TG\cong\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\ell=\ell(\xi)
. For further details of the Lagrangian reduction we refer the reader to
[
8
,
9
]
. The Hamiltonian counterpart of the reduction, on the other hand, produces the
Lie-Poisson equations

(1.5)

\frac{d\mu}{dt}=\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits^{\ast}_{\frac{\delta\mathfrak{H}}{\delta\mu}}(\mu),

of the reduced Hamiltonian
\mathfrak{H}:T^{\ast}G/G\cong\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{H}(\mu)
. For more about the Hamiltonian reduction the reader may consult to
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
.

In the present manuscript we delve into the Euler-Poincaré equations on a unified product Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
, and the corresponding Lie-Poisson equations defined on the dual space
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
. Within both the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formalisms, reduction by
\mathfrak{h}
yields equations that represent the dynamics over the quotient space
\mathfrak{m}
and its dual
\mathfrak{m}^{\ast}
respectively. These equations, as such, are the most natural substitutes of the Euler-Poincaré equations (resp. the Lie-Poisson equations) over the quotient space
\mathfrak{m}\cong\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
(resp.
\mathfrak{m}^{\ast}
), which is not initially a Lie algebra.

The Lie group of a unified product Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
is a
unified product

G
in the level of Lie groups,
[
4
]
. More explicitly, the group
G
is entirely determined by a subgroup
H\subseteq G
, along with a complement
M\subseteq G
(in the sense that
G=M\times H
as manifolds) which is not necessarily a subgroup. Among other things, we shall hereby show that the tangent (resp. cotangent) group of a unified product is also a unified product Lie group. Consequently, the Euler-Lagrange equations on the tangent group
TG\cong TM\times TH
will lead, by the
TH
reduction, to the Euler-Lagrange type equations on
TM=T(G/H)
even though
M=G/H
is not a priori a Lie group. The recipe functions identically in the Hamiltonian counterpart of the dynamics. Specifically, we shall obtain the Hamilton’s equations on the cotangent group
T^{\ast}G\cong T^{\ast}M\times T^{\ast}H
. Then, by the
T^{\ast}H
reduction we shall derive the Hamilton’s type equations over
T^{\ast}M=T^{\ast}(G/H)
, once again, despite
M=G/H
is not inherently a Lie group.

We shall illustrate our results on two examples. We first realize the Lie algebra structure on the energy-momentum space of the Kepler problem considered in
[
26
]
as a unified product. Accordingly we shall obtain the Euler-Poincaré equations over the energy-momentum space, as well as the Lie-Poisson equations over its dual. As a second illustration we will consider the Hamiltonian four-field model, proposed in
[
20
]
, see also
[
19
]
, for two-dimensional compressible reduced magnetohydrodynamics. The model offers a simplified representation of nonlinear tokamak dynamics, accommodating finite ion Larmor radius physics, along with other effects associated with compressibility and electron adiabaticity. The Lie algebra that underlies the four field model was worked out in
[
31
]
either, from the point of view of the Lie algebra extensions. We shall represent this Lie algebra as a unified product, and will thus obtain the equations of motion both in Lagrangian and Hamiltonian frameworks.

The flexible decomposition offered by a unified product allows to study the higher order dynamics as well. More precisely, we observe that the
n
th iterated tangent group

T^{(n)}G:=\underset{n-many}{\underbrace{T(T(T\ldots(T}}G)))

of a Lie group
G
is a unified product of the
n
th order tangent group
T^{n}G
, and
2^{n}-(n+1)
-many copies of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
;

T^{(n)}G\cong\mathfrak{g}^{\times\,2^{n}-1-n}\times T^{n}G.

As such, we shall be able to access the dynamics (both Lagrangian and Hamiltonian) on
T^{n}G
from that on
T^{(n)}G
through
\mathfrak{g}^{\times\,2^{n}-1-n}
-reduction. We shall illustrate all details in the case
n=3
.

Notation and Conventions

We shall consider decompositions of Lie algebras, and Lie groups. As for Lie algebras we shall consider the decomposition
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{m}\,\oplus\,\mathfrak{h}
of a Lie algebra into a vector subspace
\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
and a Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
. Their generic elements are denoted by
v,v_{1},v_{2},...\in\mathfrak{m}
,
\eta,\eta_{1},\eta_{2},...\in\mathfrak{h}
, with
(v_{1},\eta_{1}),(v_{2},\eta_{2})\in\mathfrak{g}
. Similarly, we shall consider the decomposition
G\cong M\times H
of a Lie group into a submanifold
M\subseteq G
and a subgroup
H\subseteq G
. We shall, in this case, refer the generic elements as
m,m_{1},m_{2},...\in M
and
h,h_{1},h_{2},...\in H
, as well as
(m_{1},h_{1}),(m_{2},h_{2})\in G
.

Cocycle Double Cross Constructions

2.1.
Cocycle Double Cross Product Lie Groups

We shall first recall from
[

cocycle double cross product

[
4
, Sec. 3]
, and was named as the

was adopted in
[
13
]

[
13
]

Let
M
be a manifold with a distinguished element
o\in M
, and let
H

M

(2.1)

\varphi:H\times M\to M,\qquad(h,m)\mapsto h\triangleright m,

so that,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sigma:H\times M\to H,\qquad(h,m)\mapsto\sigma(h,m),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Phi:M\times M\to M,\qquad(m_{1},m_{2})\mapsto\Phi(m_{1},m_{2})=:m_{1}\cdot m_{2},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\gamma:M\times M\to H,\qquad(m_{1},m_{2})\mapsto\gamma(m_{1},m_{2}),

(2.5)

\sigma(h,o)=h,\qquad\sigma(1,m)=1,\qquad\gamma(o,o)=1.

[

\displaystyle o\cdot m=m=m\cdot o,

\displaystyle\sigma(h_{1}\ast h_{2},m)=\sigma(h_{1},h_{2}\triangleright m)\ast\sigma(h_{2},m),

(2.11)

\displaystyle m_{1}\cdot(m_{2}\cdot m_{3})=(m_{1}\cdot m_{2})\cdot(\gamma(m_{1},m_{2})\triangleright m_{3}),

\displaystyle\gamma(m_{1},m_{2}\cdot m_{3})\ast\gamma(m_{2},m_{3})=\gamma(m_{1}\cdot m_{2},\gamma(m_{1},m_{2})\triangleright m_{3})\ast\sigma(\gamma(m_{1},m_{2}),m_{3}),

It worths to remark that the cocycle double cross product (or unified product) construction provides a unique frame under which the double cross products of

Cocycle double cross sum Lie algebras

\displaystyle\psi:\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{h},\qquad\psi(\eta\otimes v)=:\psi(\eta,v).

.

In accordance with the Lie groups, both double cross sum Lie algebras of

T^{(n)}G=\{(x,(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I_{n}^{\ast}})\mid x\in G,\,\,X_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}\}.

Labeling the identical copies of the Lie algebra

\displaystyle T^{(1)}G=TG=G\times\mathfrak{g}^{(1)},

\displaystyle T^{(2)}G=G\times\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(21)},

\displaystyle T^{(3)}G=G\times\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(21)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(3)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(31)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(32)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(321)},

\displaystyle T^{(4)}G=G\times\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(21)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(3)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(31)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(32)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(321)}\times

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt\mathfrak{g}^{(4)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(41)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(42)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(421)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(43)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(431)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(432)}\times\mathfrak{g}^{(4321)}.

The group operation of

, on the other hand, is given by

(x,(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I_{n}^{\ast}})(y,(Y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I_{n}^{\ast}})=(xy,(Z_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I_{n}^{\ast}}),

where

\alpha\in I_{n}^{\ast}

of length

(x,(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I_{n}^{\ast}})^{-1}=(x,(W_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I_{n}^{\ast}}),

The following proposition sheds further light on the group structure of the iterated tangent group, which was investigated (from a similar point of view) in
[
14
, Prop. 3.4]
. It was shown therein that

is the double cross product of two subgroups

, and

. It turns out that for

this is no longer the case; the double cross product structure evolves into a cocycle double cross product, which is the subject of the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

The
n
th order iterated tangent group
T^{(n)}G
has the structure of a cocycle double cross product of the
n
th order tangent group
T^{n}G
, by
\mathfrak{g}^{\times\,2^{n}-1-n}
. In short,

(5.4)

for some
\gamma:\mathfrak{g}^{\times\,2^{n}-1-n}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\times\,2^{n}-1-n}\to T^{n}G

Proof.

We embed
T^{n}G
into
T^{(n)}G
via

T^{n}G\to T^{(n)}G,\qquad(x,(\xi_{k})_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n})\mapsto(x,(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I_{n}^{\ast}})

where

X_{\alpha}:=\begin{cases}\xi_{1}&\text{ if }|\alpha|=1,\\
\xi_{2}&\text{ if }|\alpha|=2,\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
\xi_{n}&\text{ if }|\alpha|=n.\end{cases}

It follows at once from the group structure of
T^{(n)}G
that
T^{n}G\subseteq T^{(n)}G
is a subgroup. However its complement, that is the subset of the elements of the form
(e,(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in I_{n}^{\ast}})\in T^{(n)}G

\mathfrak{g}^{\times\,2^{n}-1-n}\times T^{n}G\to T^{(n)}G.

Accordingly, the claim becomes a direct consequence of

Bootstrap percolation on the high-dimensional Hamming graph
Mihyun Kang, Michael Missethan, Dominik Schmid

Abstract
. In the random r r -neighbour bootstrap percolation process on a graph G G , a set of initially infected vertices is chosen at random by retaining each vertex of G G independently with probability p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) p\in(0,1) , and ‘healthy’ vertices get infected in subsequent rounds if they have at least r r infected neighbours. A graph G G percolates if every vertex becomes eventually infected. A central problem in this process is to determine the critical probability p c ​ ( G , r ) p_{c}(G,r) , at which the probability that G G percolates passes through one half. 1.1.
Motivation

Bootstrap percolation was introduced in 1979 by Chalupa, Leath, and Reich
[
19
]
in the context of magnetic systems. Since then it has found many applications ranging from studying the spread of information through social networks
[
31
]
to modelling collective behaviour
[

Consider the
r
-neighbour bootstrap percolation process described as follows. Given a graph
G
, a set

of

vertices and the so-called

r\in\mathbb{N}

r
infected neighbours. Once a vertex has been infected, it remains in this state forever. The process ends if either all vertices have been infected or all healthy vertices have less than
r
infected neighbours. The set of infected vertices can be thought of growing in rounds: In each round
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we move all healthy vertices to the set
A_{j}
that have at least
r
neighbours in
A_{j-1}
, i.e., we let

(1.1)

\displaystyle A_{j}:=A_{j-1}\cup\left\{v\in V(G)\mid\left|N(v)\cap A_{j-1}\right|\geq r\right\}.

Note that the percolation process stops (or stabilises) as soon as
A_{j}=A_{j-1}
for some
j\in\mathbb{N}
. We set

\displaystyle\left[{A}\right]\coloneqq\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}A_{j}.

If
\left[{A}\right]=V(G)
, we say
A

spans

G

; or equivalently

is a percolating set.

Choosing the set of initially infected vertices

1.2.
Main results and key proof techniques

For
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
, consider
random

2
-neighbour bootstrap percolation on the
n
-dimensional Hamming graph
\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k}
, where the set of initially infected vertices is a
p
-random subset
\boldsymbol{A}_{p}
of
V\left(\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k}\right)
, meaning
\mathbb{P}\left[v\in\boldsymbol{A}_{p}\right]=p\in(0,1)
independently for each vertex
v\in V\left(\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k}\right)
. Set
A_{0}:=\boldsymbol{A}_{p}
and for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
let

\displaystyle A_{j}\coloneqq A_{j-1}\cup\left\{v\in V\left(\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k}\right)\mid\left|N(v)\cap A_{j-1}\right|\geq 2\right\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left[{\boldsymbol{A}_{p}}\right]:=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}}A_{j}.

Our main result is that for
any

2\leq k\leq 2^{\sqrt{n}}
the critical probability satisfies

p_{c}\left(\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k},2\right)=\Theta\left(n^{-2}k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1}\right),

which recovers (
1.2
) of Balogh and Bollobás
[
5
]
by taking
k=2
.
More precisely, we determine for which
p
does
\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k}
percolate, i.e.,
\left[{\boldsymbol{A}_{p}}\right]=V\left(\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k}\right)
and for which
p
it does not, i.e.,
V\left(\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k}\right)\setminus\left[{\boldsymbol{A}_{p}}\right]\neq\emptyset.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
2\leq k\leq 2^{\sqrt{n}}
. Consider random
2
-neighbour bootstrap percolation on the
n
-dimensional Hamming graph
G=\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k}
in which the set of initially infected vertices is a
p
-random subset
\boldsymbol{A}_{p}
of
V(G)
. Set
p_{*}:=n^{-2}k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1}
and
p^{*}:=200n^{-2}k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1}
. Then the following hold.

(a)

If
p\leq p_{*}
, then whp
G
does not percolate.

(b)

If
p\geq p^{*}
, then whp
G
percolates.

Note that Theorem
1.1
implies

\displaystyle n^{-2}k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1}\leq p_{c}\left(\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k},2\right)\leq 200n^{-2}k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1}

and determines a threshold for the property that
G
percolates, up to multiplicative constant factors. Regarding the case
k=2
, improving upon (
1.2
) Balogh, Bollobás and Morris
[
7
]
determined a sharp threshold (and more):

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{16\lambda}{n^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}\right)2^{-2\sqrt{n}}\leq p_{c}(Q^{n},2)\leq\frac{16\lambda}{n^{2}}\left(1+\frac{5\log^{2}n}{\sqrt{n}}\right)2^{-2\sqrt{n}},

where
\lambda\approx 1.166
.
Although the constants in
Theorem

1.1
could be improved (especially for
p^{*}
), different techniques are needed to improve our result to a sharp threshold. Furthermore, note that the restriction
k\leq 2^{\sqrt{n}}
on the size of the base graphs
K_{k}
is due to technical reasons in the proof of
Theorem

thm:main:threshold
(a)
and we did not try to optimise this restriction, although the result might still hold for
k
growing arbitrarily fast in
n
.

The advantages of our proof techniques lie in their universality and applicability to more general classes of high-dimensional product graphs.
Two key concepts in the standard analysis of bootstrap percolation on high-dimensional graphs are
internally spanned projections
and a
critical dimension
. Given a Cartesian product graph
G
, a projection of
G
is a subgraph of
G
that is isomorphic to a
lower-dimensional
Cartesian product graph; and a projection is
internally spanned
if its induced subgraph percolates. One of the key properties of the Hamming graph is that two projections within distance two together span another projection containing both of them. This enables us to use
‘hierarchies’
to obtain good upper bounds on the probability that a projection is internally spanned and to show that the probability of
G
percolating is bounded from above by the probability of the appearance of an internally spanned projection of a certain
critical dimension
. To prove
Theorem

thm:main:threshold
(a)
it then suffices to show that for
p\leq p_{*}
there exists no internally spanned projection of this
critical dimension
. We note that in earlier work
[
5
,
7
]
as well as in our work the property that two projections within distance two always span a larger projection was crucial.

To prove
Theorem

thm:main:threshold
(b)
we consider a specific type of percolating set, called a
sequentially spanning set
(see
Definition

2.9
for formal definition). The importance of sequentially spanning sets is two-fold: They are
minimum
percolating sets; and the majority of all minimum percolating sets comes from sequentially spanning sets. We use the second moment method to show the existence of a sufficiently large sequentially spanning set. In comparison to the earlier work of Balogh and Bollobás
[
5
]
and Balogh, Bollobás and Morris
[
7
]
, we incorporate the
ordering
of the vertices in a sequentially spanning set and consider a sequentially spanning
sequence
instead (see
Definition

4.1
for formal definition), which has certain advantages: On the one hand, the correlation between two sequentially spanning sequences can be computed quite explicitly, allowing for much more generous applications of the second moment method; and on the other hand, while sequentially spanning sets are used in literature in order to derive lower bounds on the probability that a projection is internally spanned, the use of sequentially spanning
sequences
allows us to avoid this step.
The concept of sequentially spanning sequences transfers quite naturally to other types of Cartesian product graphs, and so we believe that our techniques will be useful for deriving similar bounds on more general classes of product graphs.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we first introduce necessary notations and useful known results. We then provide some intuition on the deterministic
2
-neighbour bootstrap percolation process on the Hamming graph and characterise percolating or non-percolating sets. In
Section

2.3
, we take a closer look at the
percolation process and set up the scene for what is known as the

[

2.1.
Notation and terminology

We define
\mathbb{N}_{0}\coloneqq\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}
. Given a set
X
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\binom{X}{\ell}
the set of all
\ell
-element subsets of
X
and define
[\ell]:=\{1,2,\ldots,\ell\}
.

Given a graph
G
, the
distance
between two different vertices in
G
, denoted by
\distance\left(x,y\right)
, is defined as the length of a shortest path between them. The distance between two sets of vertices
A,A^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
is defined as the minimum distance between all pairs of vertices from the two sets:

\displaystyle\distance\left(A,A^{\prime}\right)=\distance\left(A^{\prime},A\right)\coloneqq\min_{u\in A}\min_{v\in A^{\prime}}\distance\left(u,v\right).

Now we define the Cartesian product of graphs and introduce important concepts.

Definition 2.1
.

Given graphs
G^{(1)}=\left(V^{(1)},E^{(1)}\right),\ldots,G^{(n)}=\left(V^{(n)},E^{(n)}\right)
, we define the
Cartesian product
of
G^{(1)},\ldots,G^{(n)}
, denoted by
G=G^{(1)}\square\cdots\square G^{(n)}
or
G=\square_{i=1}^{n}G^{(i)}
, as the graph
G=(V,E)
with vertex and edge set

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=V(G)=V^{(1)}\times\cdots\times V^{(n)}:=\left\{v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\mid\forall i\in[n]:v_{i}\in V^{(i)}\right\}

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=E(G):=\left\{\{v,w\}\in\binom{V}{2}\mid\exists j\in[n]:\Big(\left\{v_{j},w_{j}\right\}\in E^{(j)}\Big)\wedge\Big(\forall i\in[n]\setminus\{j\}:v_{i}=w_{i}\Big)\right\}.

Throughout the paper we will call
G=\square_{i=1}^{n}G^{(i)}
the
product graph
instead of the Cartesian product graph, unless specified otherwise, and the graphs
G^{(1)},\ldots,G^{(n)}
the
base graphs
of
G
. A base graph is called
trivial
, if it consists of a single vertex.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a product graph
G=\square_{i=1}^{n}G^{(i)}
we call a subgraph
H\subseteq G
a
projection
of
G
if
H
can be written as
H=\square_{i=1}^{n}H^{(i)}
where for each
i\in[n]
we have either
H^{(i)}=G^{(i)}
or
H^{(i)}=\left\{v_{i}\right\}
for some vertex
v_{i}\in V^{(i)}
. The
dimension
of
H
is defined as the number of non-trivial base graphs
H^{(i)}
in
H
.

In order to keep track of the dimension of a projection, we will often write
H_{\ell}
for a projection of dimension
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. With slight abuse of notation, we will sometimes view a projection not as a subgraph of
G
, but just as a subset of
V(G)
. Similarly, when we say a subset
U\subseteq V(G)
is a projection, we mean that the subgraph of
G
induced by
U
is a projection.

Next we introduce some necessary notations for the bootstrap percolation process.

Definition 2.3
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
and a set
A\subseteq V
, we define the
closure
of
A
, denoted by
\left[{A}\right]
, as the set of eventually infected vertices in the
2
-neighbour bootstrap percolation process with
A
being the set of initially infected vertices. More formally, we define

\displaystyle\left[{A}\right]\coloneqq\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}A_{j},

where
A_{0}:=A
and
A_{j}:=A_{j-1}\cup\left\{v\in V\mid\left|N(v)\cap A_{j-1}\right|\geq 2\right\}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
We say

(1)

A
is
closed
, if
\left[{A}\right]=A
;

(2)

a closed set
U\subseteq V
is called
internally spanned
by
A
, if
U=\left[{A\cap U}\right]
.

The collection of all closed subsets of
V
that are internally spanned by
A
is denoted by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{A}\coloneqq\left\{U\subseteq V\mid U=\left[{A\cap U}\right]\right\}.

Furthermore we say

(3)

two (not-necessarily disjoint) closed sets
U,W\subseteq V
are
disjointly internally spanned
by
A
, if there exist two disjoint subsets
A_{U},A_{W}\subseteq A
that internally span
U
and
W
, respectively, i.e.,
U=\left[{A_{U}\cap U}\right]
and
W=\left[{A_{W}\cap W}\right]
.

In this case, we write

\displaystyle\{U,W\}\subseteq_{d}\mathcal{H}_{A}.

Analogously for arbitrary numbers of sets
U_{1},\ldots,U_{\ell}\subseteq V
we say they are disjointly internally spanned by
A
, if there exist disjoint subsets
A_{U_{1}},\ldots,A_{U_{\ell}}\subseteq A
that internally span
U_{1},\ldots,U_{\ell}
, respectively.

A powerful tool in our analysis is the van den Berg-Kesten Lemma. Essentially in the context of bootstrap percolation it implies the following.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
39
, Theorem 1.6.(iii)]
)
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
and
p\in(0,1)
let
\boldsymbol{A}_{p}
be a
p
-random subset of
V
, i.e.,
\mathbb{P}\left[v\in\boldsymbol{A}_{p}\right]=p
for each
v\in V
independently. For any

, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\{U,W\}\subseteq_{d}\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{A}_{p}}\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[U\in\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{A}_{p}}\right]\cdot\mathbb{P}\left[W\in\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{A}_{p}}\right].

We will use the following version of the second moment method, due to Alon and Spencer
[
3
]
.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
3
]
, Corollary 4.3.4)
.

Let
X=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
be a sum of indicator random variables for the events
(\mathcal{E}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
. For indices

write

, if the events
\mathcal{E}_{i}
and
\mathcal{E}_{j}
are dependent. If

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[X\right]\rightarrow\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{i\sim j}\mathbb{P}\left[\mathcal{E}_{i}\cap\mathcal{E}_{j}\right]=o\left(\mathbb{E}\left[X\right]^{2}\right)\quad\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty,

then

as

.

2.2.
Deterministic bootstrap percolation on the Hamming graph

In this section we collect properties of closed sets in the Hamming graph and how they interact with each other. Note that the results presented in this section are already known and can be found e.g., in
[
38
]
, but for the sake of completeness we include their proofs in Appendix A.
Throughout this section and the next section we consider the
n
-dimensional Hamming graph

G=(V,E)=\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k},

where
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
k\geq 2
.
We start with a simple but important observation about projections.

Lemma 2.6
.

Any projection of
G
is closed.

Next we state the most important result in this section, which tells us how projections within small distance interact in the
2
-neighbour bootstrap percolation process.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
H^{\prime},H^{\prime\prime}
be two projections of
G
satisfying
\distance\left(H^{\prime},H^{\prime\prime}\right)\leq 2
. Then, there exists a projection
H
of
G
satisfying
\dime\left(H\right)\leq\dime\left(H^{\prime}\right)+\dime\left(H^{\prime\prime}\right)+\distance\left(H^{\prime},H^{\prime\prime}\right)
and

\displaystyle\left[{H^{\prime}\cup H^{\prime\prime}}\right]=H.

Lemma

2.7
tells us that two projections within distance two span another larger projection that contains both of them.

Remark 2.8
.

Note that if two projections
H^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime\prime}
lie at distance at least three, they have no common neighbours and thus do not interact under
2
-neighbour bootstrap percolation, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left[{H^{\prime}\cup H^{\prime\prime}}\right]=H^{\prime}\cup H^{\prime\prime}.

By
Lemma

2.7
we know that we can generate larger and larger projections by adding vertices that lie at distance two of the previous projection. The set of vertices used in this construction is very useful in the
2
-neighbour bootstrap percolation process and we define it more precisely in the following.

Definition 2.9
.

A set
U\subseteq V
is called
sequentially spanning
, if there is an ordering
(v_{0},\ldots,v_{|U|-1})
of its vertices such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\distance\left(\left[{\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{i-1}\}}\right],v{{}_{i}}\right)=2\quad\text{ for all }i\in[|U|-1].

In words, Property (
2.1
) guarantees that each vertex in the ordering lies exactly at distance two from the projection spanned by the previous vertices.
We will see in
Lemma

3.2
that a majority of all percolating sets in
G
are sequentially spanning. The next lemma tells us that sequentially spanning sets always span large projections.

Lemma 2.10
.

Assume that
U\subseteq V
is a sequentially spanning set. Then there exists a projection
H
of
G
such that

\displaystyle\dime\left(H\right)=2(|U|-1)\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad\left[{U}\right]=H.

Next we discuss an alternative way of looking at the bootstrap percolation process, using projections.

2.3.
A different view on the bootstrap percolation process

Given a set
A\subseteq V
, consider the collection
\mathcal{H}_{A}
of all subsets that are internally spanned by
A
, as in

2.3
. For each
H\in\mathcal{H}_{A}
(so

) we can think of the set

as a set

of

. As each of these projections consists of a single

. By

2.8
, projections at distance at least three do not interact at all, so it follows from
\left[{S_{0}}\right]=H
that at least two of those projections, w.l.o.g.

and

, are within distance two of each other. By

2.7

spans a projection that is by construction disjointly internally spanned from all other projections
a_{2},\ldots,a_{s}
. Thus, we obtain a set
S_{1}=\left\{\left[{a_{0}\cup a_{1}}\right],\left\{a_{2}\right\},\ldots,\left\{a_{s}\right\}\right\}
consisting of
|S_{1}|=|S_{0}|-1
disjointly internally spanned projections.
Note that the set
S_{1}
is not uniquely determined, as it depends on the choice of projections within distance two. Continuing this process we sequentially obtain sets
S_{i}
of disjointly internally spanned projections until at the final step
S_{s}=\{H\}
. More formally, we obtain the following.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
H\in\mathcal{H}_{A}
and set
s\coloneqq\left|A\cap H\right|-1
. Then, there exist families
S_{0},\ldots,S_{s}
of subsets of
V
such that the following hold.

(1)

S_{0}=\left\{\{v\}\mid v\in A\cap H\right\}
;

(2)

for all
0\leq i\leq s-1,
there exist
H^{\prime},H^{\prime\prime}\in S_{i}\text{ such that }S_{i+1}=S_{i}\cup\left\{\left[{H^{\prime}\cup H^{\prime\prime}}\right]\right\}\setminus\{H^{\prime},H^{\prime\prime}\}
;

(3)

for all
0\leq i\leq s
, the elements of
S_{i}
are disjointly internally spanned projections of
G
;

(4)

S_{s}=\{H\}
.

Now given
H\in\mathcal{H}_{A}
and any integer
1\leq t\leq\dime\left(H\right)
we consider the first time a projection of dimension at least
t
appears in this process. Looking at the two projections that were joined in this step,
Lemma

2.11
immediately implies the following key result.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
H\in\mathcal{H}_{A}
. Then for every
1\leq t\leq\dime\left(H\right)
, there exist indices
(\ell,i,j,d)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{4}
with
\ell\geq t,\ j\leq i<t
and
d\in\{0,1,2\}
and projections
H_{i},H_{j},H_{\ell}
of respective dimensions
i,j,\ell
such that

(1)

\distance\left(H_{i},H_{j}\right)=d
;

(2)

\left[{H_{i}\cup H_{j}}\right]=H_{\ell}
;

(3)

\left\{H_{i},H_{j}\right\}\subseteq_{d}\mathcal{H}_{A}
.

We call the quadruple
(H_{\ell},H_{i},H_{j},d)
a
witnessing quadruple
for
H
at threshold
t
.

3.
The lower threshold: Proof of
Theorem

1.1

(a)

In the rest of the paper we assume that
n\in\mathbb{N}
is sufficiently large and let
G=(V,E)=\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k}
be the
n
-dimensional Hamming graph with
2\leq k\leq 2^{\sqrt{n}}
and let
\boldsymbol{A}_{p}
be a
p
-random subset of
V
, as in
Theorem

1.1
. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{A}_{p}}\coloneqq\left\{U\subseteq V\mid U=\left[{\boldsymbol{A}_{p}\cap U}\right]\right\}

be the collection of all subsets of
V
that are internally spanned by
\boldsymbol{A}_{p}
. Recall that we write
H_{\ell}
for a projection of dimension
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in order to keep track of its dimension.

This section is devoted to the proof of
Theorem

thm:main:threshold
(a)
, along which we will introduce some auxiliary results, but we defer their proofs to
Section

5
. So we assume

\displaystyle p\leq p_{*}\coloneqq n^{-2}k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1}

and will show

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[G\text{ percolates}\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\left[{\boldsymbol{A}_{p}}\right]=V\right]=o\left(1\right).

We first note that in many bootstrap percolation processes the bottleneck for percolation is given by the appearance of a small substructure of the underlying graph, called a
critical droplet
. In our case, this critical droplet is an
internally spanned projection
of a specific dimension
t_{*}\in[n]
, where
t_{*}
is chosen such that the expected number of
internally spanned projections
is minimal: We call
t_{*}
the
critical dimension
.

Assume that
G
percolates, i.e.,
\left[{\boldsymbol{A}_{p}}\right]=V
. As described in Lemma
2.11
, we can view the set of initially infected vertices as a collection of 0-dimensional projections that sequentially pairwise span larger projections until in the final step two projections together span
G
. In particular, for every threshold dimension
t\in[n]
we can look at the first time a projection
H
of dimension at least
t
appears in this process and consider the projections that together span
H
. More precisely, it follows from
Lemma

2.12
that there has to exist a
witnessing quadruple
for
G
at this threshold and thus we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[G\text{ percolates}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\text{there exists a witnessing quadruple{} for $G$ at threshold $t$}\right].

In order for a quadruple
(H_{\ell},H_{i},H_{j},d)
to be a witnessing quadruple, it has to satisfy Properties
(1)
,
(2)
and
(3)
of
Lemma

2.12
and the indices
(\ell,i,j,d)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{4}
should fulfill the dimension constraints
\ell\geq t,\ j\leq i<t
and
d\in\{0,1,2\}
. However, note that all requirements except Property
(3)
of
Lemma

2.12
are deterministic statements that do not depend on the set of initially infected vertices.

We call all quadruples
(H_{\ell},H_{i},H_{j},d)
that satisfy the dimension constraints as well as Properties
(1)
and
(2)
of
Lemma

2.12

candidate quadruples
and denote by
\mathcal{C}(G,t)
the set of all candidate quadruples. Thus, a candidate quadruple is a witnessing quadruple if and only if it satisfies Property
(3)
of
Lemma

2.12
. On the other hand, by the van den Berg-Kesten Lemma
2.4
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\left\{H_{i},H_{j}\right\}\subseteq_{d}\mathcal{H}\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[H_{i}\in\mathcal{H}\right]\cdot\mathbb{P}\left[H_{j}\in\mathcal{H}\right].

Hence, for any candidate quadruple
(H_{\ell},H_{i},H_{j},d)\in\mathcal{C}(G,t)
we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[(H_{\ell},H_{i},H_{j},d)\text{ is a witnessing quadruple{}}\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[H_{i}\in\mathcal{H}\right]\cdot\mathbb{P}\left[H_{j}\in\mathcal{H}\right].

Putting (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) together and taking a union bound over all candidate quadruples we obtain the following bound for all
t\in[n]
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[G\text{ percolates}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\text{there exists a witnessing quadruple{} for $G$ at threshold $t$}\right]

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(H_{\ell},H_{i},H_{j},d)\in\mathcal{C}(G,t)}\mathbb{P}\left[H_{i}\in\mathcal{H}\right]\cdot\mathbb{P}\left[H_{j}\in\mathcal{H}\right].

Due to the symmetry of
G
, we have that for any projection
H
of
G
, the probability
\mathbb{P}\left[H\in\mathcal{H}\right]
solely depends on
\dime\left(H\right)
(and does not depend on its structure). Thus, for each
\ell\in[n]\cup\left\{0\right\}
and each projection
H_{\ell}
of dimension
\ell
, we define

(3.4)

\displaystyle\varphi({\ell})\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\left[H_{\ell}\in\mathcal{H}\right].

Given indices
(\ell,i,j,d)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{4}
let us denote by

(3.5)

\displaystyle U_{G,t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\coloneqq\left|\left\{\left(H_{\ell^{\prime}},H_{i^{\prime}},H_{j^{\prime}},d^{\prime}\right)\in\mathcal{C}(G,t)\mid\left(\ell^{\prime}=\ell\right)\land\left(i^{\prime}=i\right)\land\left(j^{\prime}=j\right)\land\left(d^{\prime}=d\right)\right\}\right|

the number of candidate quadruples in
G
at threshold
t
with fixed indices
(\ell,i,j,d)
. Let us determine for which sets of indices this function is actually non-zero. From
Lemma

2.11
it follows that
\ell\geq t
,
j\leq i<t
and
d\in\{0,1,2\}
. Also, because the projections
H_{i}
and
H_{j}
have to lie at distance
d
it is not possible that
i=\ell-1
and
d=2
, yielding the condition
i+d<\ell
. Furthermore, by
Lemma

2.7
, in order for the projections
H_{i}
and
H_{j}
to span the projection
H_{\ell}
it also has to hold that
i+j+d\geq\ell
. Finally, note that because
H_{\ell}
is a projection of
G
, we have
\ell=\dime\left(H_{\ell}\right)\leq\dime\left(G\right)
. These constraints lead to the set of all
admissible indices
, denoted by

\displaystyle I_{G,t}:=\left\{(\ell,i,j,d)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{4}\mid\left(t\leq\ell\leq\min\left\{i+j+d,\dime\left(G\right)\right\}\right)\land(j\leq i<t)\land(i+d<\ell)\land(d\in\{0,1,2\})\right\}.

Analogously, we define
\mathcal{C}(H,t)
,
U_{H,t}(\cdot)
and
I_{H,t}
for a projection
H
of
G
and a threshold
t\in\left[\dime\left(H\right)\right]
.

In terms of the functions
\varphi({\cdot})
and
U_{H,t}(\cdot)
defined in (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), the percolation probability bound (
3.3
) can be written as follows.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
t\in[n]
we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[G\text{ percolates}\right]\leq\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t}}U_{G,t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\varphi({i})\varphi({j}).

The value of
U_{G,t}(\cdot)
will be calculated quite explicitly in
Lemma

5.1
. Thus, a key step in deriving an upper bound on the percolation probability is to obtain good bounds on the probability of a projection being internally spanned and choosing the optimal threshold value
t
. As we will see in
Section

5
, the
critical dimension
in which the right hand side of (
3.6
) yields the best bounds is

t_{*}:=\left\lfloor 2\sqrt{n}\right\rfloor-2.

In order to bound the right hand side of (
3.6
) from above, we need the following upper bound on the function
\varphi({\cdot})
defined in (
3.4
).

Lemma 3.2
.

For all
m\in[t_{*}]\cup\{0\}
we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\varphi({m})\leq\Phi(m)

where the function
\Phi:\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

(3.8)

\displaystyle\Phi(m)\coloneqq c_{m}p^{\frac{m}{2}+1}m!\ 2^{-\frac{m}{2}}(k-1)^{m}k^{\frac{m^{2}+2m}{4}}

with

c_{m}\coloneqq\mathbb{1}_{\left\{m=0\right\}}+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{1\leq m\leq 14\right\}}\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{9\sqrt{2}}{10}\right)^{m}+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{m\geq 15\right\}}c_{m-1}\left(1+k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right).

Before we proceed with the proof of
Theorem

thm:main:threshold
(a)
let us give some intuition on the function
\Phi
. The value
\Phi(0)=p
is exactly the probability of a
0
-dimensional projection (i.e., a single vertex) being internally spanned by a
p
-random subset
\boldsymbol{A}_{p}
. For
even

m\in[t_{*}]
, the value
\Phi(m)
is equal to the
expected number of sequentially spanning sets
in a projection of dimension
m
, up to a multiplicative factor
c_{m}
and up to permutations of vertices. Thus,
Lemma

3.2
states that a majority of percolating sets is given by sequentially spanning sets. For odd
m\in[t_{*}]
, the value
\Phi(m)
overestimates the actual probability
\varphi({m})
by quite a large margin, but spares us from a tedious case distinction. Lastly, note that for all
m\in[t_{*}]\cup\{0\}
the functions
c_{m}
are bounded from above by a universal constant (see
Lemma

5.7
).

The key to showing
Lemma

3.2
lies in the following observation: Any projection
H
of
G
is isomorphic to a Hamming graph of dimension
\dime\left(H\right)
, and thus the event
\{H\in\mathcal{H}\}=\{H\in\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{A}_{p}}\}
happens with the same probability as the event that the Hamming graph
G^{\prime}:=\square_{i=1}^{\dime\left(H\right)}K_{k}
percolates. Hence, we can apply
Lemma

3.1
to obtain that for all
m\in[n]
and for all
t\in[m]

\displaystyle\varphi({m})\leq\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{H_{m},t}}U_{H_{m},m}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\varphi({i})\varphi({j}).

It turns out that we obtain the best bounds by considering the threshold value
t=m
, i.e., by looking at the
final
step in the percolation process, where two projections of respective dimensions smaller than
m
together span
H_{m}
.
Let us assume by induction that the conclusion of
Lemma

3.2
, i.e.,
\varphi({j})\leq\Phi(j)
, holds for all
j\in[m-1]
for some
m\in[t_{*}]\cup\{0\}
. Because any
(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{H_{m},m}
satisfies
j\leq i<m
, we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle\varphi({m})\leq\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{H_{m},m}}U_{H_{m},m}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\Phi(i)\Phi(j).

Lemma

3.2
then follows from (
3.9
) by a careful comparison of ratios of consecutive terms.

Returning to the proof of
Theorem

thm:main:threshold
(a)
, using
Lemma

3.1
with
t=t_{*}:=\left\lfloor 2\sqrt{n}\right\rfloor-2
and
Lemma

3.2
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[G\text{ percolates}\right]

\displaystyle{\leq}\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}}U_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\varphi({i})\varphi({j})

(3.10)

\displaystyle{\leq}\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}}U_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\Phi(i)\Phi(j).

Due to technical reasons, we will consider the partition of the set
I_{G,t_{*}}
into two sets, depending on the size of
\ell
,

(3.11)

\displaystyle I_{G,t_{*}}^{(1)}\coloneqq\left\{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}\mid\ell\leq 3\sqrt{n}\right\}\ \text{ and }\ I_{G,t_{*}}^{(2)}\coloneqq\left\{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}\mid\ell>3\sqrt{n}\right\},

so that

(3.12)

\displaystyle I_{G,t_{*}}=I_{G,t_{*}}^{(1)}\cup I_{G,t_{*}}^{(2)}.

Furthermore, to ease notation on the right hand side of (
3.10
) we introduce the function

(3.13)

\displaystyle f\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\coloneqq U_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\Phi(i)\Phi(j).

Using (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) we rewrite (
3.10
) as

(3.14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[G\text{ percolates}\right]\leq\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(1)}}f\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)+\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(2)}}f\left(\ell,i,j,d\right).

We deal with the first and second sums in (
3.14
) separately. We claim that the second sum over all indices in
I_{G,t_{*}}^{(2)}
converges to zero (see
Claim

3.3
) and the first sum over all indices in
I_{G,t_{*}}^{(1)}
is bounded from above by a single term
f\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right)
, up to a multiplicative factor (see
Lemma

3.4
).

Claim 3.3
.

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}{}}^{(2)}}f\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

\displaystyle=o(1).

In order to deal with the sum over all indices in
I_{G,t_{*}{}}^{(1)}
, we first note that
f\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)
essentially bounds the expected number of projections of respective dimensions
i
and
j
that lie at distance
d
and together span a projection of dimension
\ell\geq t_{*}
in
G
. We then note that a major role in
2
-neighbour bootstrap percolation on the hypercube is played by sequentially spanning sets (see
Definition

2.9
), and thus we expect that the dominating term of the left hand side of
Claim

3.3
would also stem from sequentially spanning sets and so in the last step of the percolation process a projection of dimension
t_{*}-2
would be joined with a single vertex at distance two: It corresponds to
f\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right)
. The next lemma shows that the sum is indeed dominated by this term, up to a small multiplicative factor.

Lemma 3.4
.

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}{}}^{(1)}}f\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\leq 5k^{\frac{1}{2}}f\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right).

Using simple calculations we can relate the value of
f\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right)
with
\Phi(t_{*})
.

Claim 3.5
.

\displaystyle f\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right)\leq\binom{n}{t_{*}}k^{n-t_{*}}\Phi(t_{*}).

Now, combining
Lemma

3.4
and
Claim

3.5
we obtain

(3.15)

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}{}}^{(1)}}f\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\overset{Lem.\penalty\ \ref{lem:triplesumdomination_part2}}{\leq}5k^{\frac{1}{2}}f\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right)\overset{C.\penalty\ \ref{claim:ffuncSmaller_Upboundcubeintspan}}{\leq}5\binom{n}{t_{*}}k^{n-t_{*}+\frac{1}{2}}\Phi(t_{*}).

Thus, (
3.14
),
Claim

3.3
and (
3.15
) yield

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[G\text{ percolates}\right]

\displaystyle{\leq}\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(1)}}f\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)+\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(2)}}f\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}5\binom{n}{t_{*}}k^{n-t_{*}+\frac{1}{2}}\Phi(t_{*})+o\left(1\right).

Summing up, we obtain an upper bound on the percolation probability in terms of the value of the function
\Phi(\cdot)
at the critical dimension
t_{*}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[G\text{ percolates}\right]\leq 5\binom{n}{t_{*}}k^{n-t_{*}+\frac{1}{2}}\Phi(t_{*})+o\left(1\right).

We observe that the right hand side of the inequality in
Lemma

3.6
is an upper bound on the number of
internally spanned projections of dimension
t_{*}
in
G
, up to a factor of order
\Theta\left(\sqrt{k}\right)
. Indeed, there are exactly
\binom{n}{t_{*}}k^{n-t_{*}}
ways of choosing a projection of dimension
t_{*}
in
G
and for each such projection
H
, the probability that
H
is internally spanned is bounded from above by
\Phi(t_{*})
. Thus,
Lemma

3.6
shows that the bottleneck for percolation in the Hamming graph lies in finding an internally spanned projection of dimension
t_{*}
. On the other hand, we will see in
Lemma

4.4
that as soon as an internally spanned projection of dimension
t_{*}
appears in
G
, the whole graph percolates whp. To complete the proof of
Theorem

thm:main:threshold
(a)
, we show that for
p\leq p_{*}
the expected number of internally spanned projections of dimension
t_{*}
(even with an additional factor
\sqrt{k}
) tends to zero.

Lemma 3.7
.

\displaystyle\binom{n}{t_{*}}k^{n-t_{*}+\frac{1}{2}}\Phi(t_{*})=o(1).

Now we are ready to prove
Theorem

thm:main:threshold
(a)
.

Proof of
Theorem

thm:main:threshold
(a)
.

From Lemmas
3.6
and
3.7
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[G\text{ percolates}\right]=o\left(1\right),

as desired.
∎

4.
The upper threshold: Proof of
Theorem

1.1

(b)

In this section we will prove
Theorem

1.1

(b)
. Along the proof we will derive several auxiliary results, whose proofs are deferred to
Section

6
.
We assume

\displaystyle p\geq p^{*}=200n^{-2}k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1}

and will show

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[G\text{ percolates}\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[[\boldsymbol{A}_{p}]=V\right]=1+o\left(1\right).

In order to show that whp
G
percolates it suffices to find a single percolating set in
G
that is initially infected, contrary to the lower threshold, where we had to consider all possible percolating sets. It turns out the correct type of sets to consider are
sequentially spanning sets
(see
Definition

2.9
). One intuitive reason why these sets are essential to the percolation process is that they form
minimum
percolating sets and are thus more likely to be initially infected than larger percolating sets. To keep track of the order on the vertices of a sequentially spanning set (as given by
Definition

2.9
), we will consider sequences instead of sets.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.

(1)

A sequence
\overline{v}_{\ell}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{\ell})
of vertices in
V
is called
sequentially spanning
, if
(v_{0},\ldots,v_{\ell})
satisfies Property (
2.1
).
The
length
of
\ \overline{v}_{\ell}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{\ell})
is defined as
\ell
.

(2)

A sequentially spanning sequence
\overline{v}_{\ell}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{\ell})
is called
infected
, if
\{\overline{v}_{\ell}\}\coloneqq\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}\subseteq\boldsymbol{A}_{p}
.

(3)

For two sequentially spanning sequences
\overline{v}_{\ell},\overline{w}_{\ell}
we define
\overline{v}_{\ell}\cap\overline{w}_{\ell}\coloneqq\{\overline{v}_{\ell}\}\cap\{\overline{w}_{\ell}\}
.

Note that the length of
\overline{v}_{\ell}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{\ell})
is defined in such a way that a sequentially spanning sequence of length
\ell
spans a projection of dimension
2\ell
(see
Lemma

2.10
).

Our aim is to find a sufficiently large
infected
sequentially spanning sequence in
G
. To that end, let us denote by

S_{\ell}=S_{\ell}(G)\coloneqq\left\{\overline{v}_{\ell}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{\ell})\mid\overline{v}_{\ell}\text{ is sequentially spanning{}}\right\}

the set of all sequentially spanning sequences of length
\ell
in
G
and by
X_{\ell}
the number of all
infected
sequentially spanning sequences of length
\ell
. Then, for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we obtain

\displaystyle X_{\ell}=\sum_{\overline{v}_{\ell}\in S_{\ell}}\mathbb{1}_{\left\{\{\overline{v}_{\ell}\}\subseteq\boldsymbol{A}_{p}\right\}}.

A first step towards our goal is to count the number of sequentially spanning sequences. Because a sequentially spanning sequence of length zero consists of a single vertex, it clearly holds that

|S_{0}|=\left|V\right|=k^{n}.

In the next lemma we explicitly compute the number of sequentially spanning sequences of given length by successively extending such sequences.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
1\leq\ell\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
, let
\overline{v}_{\ell-1}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{\ell-1})\in S_{\ell-1}
be a sequentially spanning sequence of length
\ell-1
. Denote by

\displaystyle C_{\ell}\coloneqq\left|\left\{w\in V\mid(v_{0},\ldots,v_{\ell-1},w)\in S_{\ell}\right\}\right|

the number of vertices in
V
that extend
\overline{v}_{\ell-1}
to a sequentially spanning sequence of length
\ell
.
Then, for
1\leq\ell\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
,

\displaystyle C_{\ell}=\binom{n-2\ell+2}{2}(k-1)^{2}k^{2\ell-2}

and for
0\leq\ell\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle|S_{\ell}|=k^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}C_{j}=\frac{n!}{(n-2\ell)!}(k-1)^{2\ell}2^{-\ell}k^{n+\ell^{2}-\ell}.

Observe that by the definitions of
S_{\ell}
and
C_{\ell}
, we obtain that for
1\leq\ell\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle|S_{\ell}|=|S_{\ell-1}|\cdot C_{\ell}.

Using
Lemma

4.2
we already have a good grip on the expected number of
sequentially spanning sequences
of given length, because
for any sequence
\overline{v}_{\ell}\in S_{\ell}
it holds that
\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\left\{\{\overline{v}_{\ell}\}\subseteq\boldsymbol{A}_{p}\right\}}\right]=p^{\ell+1}
and thus

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[X_{\ell}\right]=p^{\ell+1}|S_{\ell}|.

Next we aim to apply the second moment method to show that whp there exists an
infected sequentially spanning sequence
of length
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
. Note that we cannot hope to find a sequentially spanning sequence of length
\ell
which is strictly larger than
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
, because by
Lemma

2.10
such a sequence would span a projection of dimension
2\ell>n=\dime\left(G\right)
– a contradiction. Thus we set

\displaystyle\ell^{*}\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor;

\displaystyle\Delta\coloneqq\sum_{\left\{\{\overline{v}_{\ell^{*}},\overline{w}_{\ell^{*}}\}\subseteq S_{\ell^{*}}\mid\overline{v}_{\ell^{*}}\cap\overline{w}_{\ell^{*}}\neq\emptyset\right\}}\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\left\{\left(\{\overline{v}_{\ell^{*}}\}\subseteq\boldsymbol{A}_{p}\right)\land\left(\{\overline{w}_{\ell^{*}}\}\subseteq\boldsymbol{A}_{p}\right)\right\}}\right]

and note that
by
Lemma

2.5
it suffices to show

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[X_{\ell^{*}}\right]\xrightarrow{}\infty;

(4.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta}{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{\ell^{*}}\right]^{2}}\xrightarrow{}0.

Like in most applications of the second moment method, the harder part is to prove (
4.5
), which requires us to compute the correlation between different infected sequentially spanning sequences. For
\{\overline{v}_{\ell},\overline{w}_{\ell}\}\subseteq S_{\ell}
it holds that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\left\{\left(\{\overline{v}_{\ell}\}\subseteq\boldsymbol{A}_{p}\right)\land\left(\{\overline{w}_{\ell}\}\subseteq\boldsymbol{A}_{p}\right)\right\}}\right]=p^{2(\ell+1)-\left|\overline{v}_{\ell}\cap\overline{w}_{\ell}\right|}.

Thus, the proof boils down to bounding the number of pairs of sequentially spanning sequences that share a certain amount of vertices. For each
\ell\in[\ell^{*}]\cup\left\{0\right\}
and each
i\in[\ell+1]
we denote by

(4.7)

\displaystyle Y_{\ell}(i)\coloneqq\left\{\{\overline{v}_{\ell},\overline{w}_{\ell}\}\subseteq S_{\ell}\mid\left|\overline{v}_{\ell}\cap\overline{w}_{\ell}\right|=i\right\}

the number of pairs of sequentially spanning sequences of length
\ell
that intersect exactly in
i
vertices.
To give some intuition on what we expect the size of
Y_{\ell}(i)
to be, consider the following way of constructing intersecting sequentially spanning sequences: Start both sequences with the same initial sequence of length
i-1
(with the same
i
vertices) and then use arbitrary vertices to extend them. Clearly, there are
|S_{i-1}|
ways for choosing an initial sequence of length
i-1
. Furthermore, by (
4.2
), we have
C_{j}=\frac{|S_{j}|}{|S_{j-1}|}
and thus there are

\prod_{j=i+1}^{\ell}C_{j}^{2}=\left(\frac{|S_{\ell}|}{|S_{i-1}|}\right)^{2}
ways to extend two sequences of length
i-1
to two sequences of length
\ell
. Note that it is possible that the resulting sequences overlap in even more than
i
vertices, which happens if one of the extensions also appeared previously in the other sequence, however this only reduces the number of possible extensions by an insignificant amount. Thus, we expect that
|Y_{\ell}(i)|\geq\frac{|S_{\ell}|^{2}}{|S_{i-1}|}
and it turns out that this naive bound is almost tight, as shown in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
\ell\in[\ell^{*}]\cup\{0\}
and
i\in[\ell+1]
we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\left|Y_{\ell}(i)\right|\leq\Theta\left(1\right)8^{i}(\ell+1)^{3}\frac{|S_{\ell}|^{2}}{|S_{i-1}|}.

Combining (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\Delta\overset{\eqref{eq:secondmoment:prob},\eqref{eq:def:seqoverlap}}{=}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell^{*}+1}\left|Y_{\ell^{*}}(i)\right|p^{2\ell^{*}+2-i}\leq(\ell^{*}+1)\max_{i\in[\ell^{*}+1]}\left(\left|Y_{\ell^{*}}(i)\right|p^{2\ell^{*}+2-i}\right).

Using (
4.9
) and
Lemma

4.3
(by taking
\ell=\ell^{*}
) we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle{\leq}\Theta(1)(\ell^{*}+1)^{4}\max_{i\in[\ell^{*}+1]}\left(8^{i}\frac{|S_{\ell^{*}}|^{2}}{|S_{i-1}|}p^{2\ell^{*}+2-i}\right)

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq\Theta(n^{4})\max_{i\in[\ell^{*}+1]}\left(8^{i}\frac{|S_{\ell^{*}}|^{2}}{|S_{i-1}|}p^{2\ell^{*}+2-i}\right),

where the last inequality is due to
\ell^{*}=\Theta(n)
.
From (
4.10
) and (
4.3
) (with
\ell=\ell^{*}
and
\ell=i-1
, obtaining
\mathbb{E}\left[X_{\ell^{*}}\right]=p^{\ell^{*}+1}|S_{\ell^{*}}|
and
\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i-1}\right]=p^{i}|S_{i-1}|
, respectively) it follows

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle{\leq}\Theta(n^{4})\max_{i\in[\ell^{*}+1]}\left(8^{i}\frac{|S_{\ell^{*}}|^{2}p^{2\ell^{*}+2}}{|S_{i-1}|p^{i}}\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\Theta(n^{4})\max_{i\in[\ell^{*}+1]}\left(8^{i}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{\ell^{*}}\right]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i-1}\right]}\right)=\frac{\Theta(n^{4})\mathbb{E}\left[X_{\ell^{*}}\right]^{2}}{\min_{i\in[\ell^{*}+1]}\left(8^{-i}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i-1}\right]\right)},

which implies

(4.11)

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta}{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{\ell^{*}}\right]^{2}}\leq\frac{\Theta(n^{4})}{\min_{i\in[\ell^{*}+1]}\left(8^{-i}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i-1}\right]\right)}.

Thus, in order to prove (
4.5
), i.e.,
\frac{\Delta}{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{\ell^{*}}\right]^{2}}\to 0
, it suffices to verify that
\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i}\right]
grows faster than
8^{i+1}n^{4}
for all
i\in[\ell^{*}]\cup\{0\}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For every
i\in[\ell^{*}]\cup\{0\}
,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i}\right]}{8^{i+1}\Theta(n^{4})}=\omega(1).

As we will see in the proof of
Lemma

4.4
, the function
x\mapsto\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{x}\right]}{8^{x+1}\Theta(n^{4})}
attains its minimum over the reals when

\displaystyle x=\sqrt{n}-1.

However, in
Lemma

4.4
the index
i\in[\ell^{*}]\cup\{0\}
is an
integer
, and thus the actual minimiser (or ‘bottleneck’), i.e., the index
i\in[\ell^{*}]\cup\{0\}
at which
\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i}\right]}{8^{i+1}\Theta(n^{4})}
is smallest, happens either for

i=\left\lfloor\sqrt{n}\right\rfloor-1
or for
i=\left\lceil\sqrt{n}\right\rceil-1
. Because in both cases the error terms are insignificant, we only focus on the former. We observe that each sequentially spanning sequence of length
i_{*}:=\left\lfloor\sqrt{n}\right\rfloor-1
spans a projection of dimension
2i_{*}
. Thus, the
critical droplet
for percolation is indeed an internally spanned projection of
critical dimension

t_{*}:=2i_{*}=\left\lfloor 2\sqrt{n}\right\rfloor-2.

(See
Lemma

3.6
for the ‘negative’ statement, meaning that for
p\leq p_{*}
whp there does not exist an internally spanned projection of dimension
t_{*}
).

As discussed before, by the second moment method (see
Lemma

2.5
), in order to show that whp
X_{\ell^{*}}\geq 1
it suffices to verify (
4.4
), i.e.,
\mathbb{E}\left[X_{\ell^{*}}\right]\xrightarrow{}\infty
, and (
4.5
), i.e.,
\frac{\Delta}{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{\ell^{*}}\right]^{2}}\to 0
: We observe that the former follows directly from
Lemma

4.4
(with
i=\ell^{*}
) and the latter from (
4.11
) and
Lemma

4.4
(with
i=\ell^{*}
).

Corollary 4.5
.

Whp
X_{\ell^{*}}\geq 1
.

Note that by
Corollary

4.5
, whp
G
contains an infected sequentially spanning sequence of length
\ell^{*}
and by
Lemma

2.10
, this sequence spans a projection
H
in
G
of dimension
2\ell^{*}
: In other words, whp
G
contains an internally spanned projection
H
of dimension
2\ell^{*}
. Recalling
\ell^{*}:=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
we obtain the following.

Corollary 4.6
.

Whp there exists an internally spanned projection
H
of dimension
2\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
in
G
.

In order to prove
Theorem

thm:main:threshold
(b)
we need one last auxiliary result when
n
is odd.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
n=2m+1
for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then whp the following holds: For every projection
H
of dimension
2m
there exists at least one infected vertex in
G
that is not contained in
H
.

Proof of
Theorem

thm:main:threshold
(b)
.

By
Corollary

4.6
,
whp there exists an internally spanned projection
H
of dimension
2\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
in
G
.

If
n
is even, then
\dime\left(H\right)=2\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor=n
and thus
H=G
, showing that whp
G
percolates.

If
n
is odd, say
n=2m+1
for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
\dime\left(H\right)=2\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor=2m
. But, it follows from
Lemma

4.7
that whp there exists an infected vertex
v
that is not contained in
H
. Clearly,
\{v\}\cup H
can have no trivial coordinates and in addition it has to hold that
\distance\left(v,H\right)=1
. Thus, by
Lemma

2.7
we have
\left[{\{v\}\cup H}\right]=G,
and thus whp
G
percolates.
∎

5.
Proofs of auxiliary results for the lower threshold

In this section we will prove auxiliary results that were necessary to show the lower threshold of
Theorem

thm:main:threshold
(a)
. Recall that for a projection
H
of
G
, threshold dimension
t\in[n]
and indices
(\ell,i,j,d)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{4}
we denote by

\displaystyle U_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\coloneqq\left|\left\{\left(H_{\ell^{\prime}},H_{i^{\prime}},H_{j^{\prime}},d^{\prime}\right)\in\mathcal{C}(H,t)\mid\left(\ell^{\prime}=\ell\right)\land\left(i^{\prime}=i\right)\land\left(j^{\prime}=j\right)\land\left(d^{\prime}=d\right)\right\}\right|

the number of candidate quadruples with fixed indices
(\ell,i,j,d)
. In addition we denote by

\displaystyle I_{H,t}\coloneqq\left\{(\ell,i,j,d)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{4}\mid\left(t\leq\ell\leq\min\left\{i+j+d,\dime\left(H\right)\right\}\right)\land(j\leq i<t)\land(i+d<\ell)\land(d\in\{0,1,2\})\right\}

the set of all
admissible indices
and by

\hat{I}_{H,t}\coloneqq\left\{(\ell,i,d)\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3}\mid\exists j\colon(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{H,t}\right\}

the set of
admissible triples
.

We begin with explicitly computing the function
U_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
m\in[n]
, let
H
be a projection of dimension
m
. Then for
t\in[m]
and
(\ell,i,j,d)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{4}
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle U_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)=\binom{m}{\ell}\binom{\ell}{i}\binom{\ell-i}{d}\binom{i}{i+j+d-\ell}k^{m+\ell-i-j-d}(k-1)^{d}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\mathbb{1}_{\left\{i=j\right\}}}\mathbb{1}_{\left\{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{H,t}\right\}}.

Proof.

Note first that by
Lemma

2.12
, the relations
H_{\ell}\subseteq H_{m}
and
H_{i}\cup H_{j}\subseteq H_{\ell}
have to hold.
We start by choosing a projection
H_{\ell}
in
H_{m}
. There are
\binom{m}{\ell}
ways to choose the coordinates in which
H_{\ell}
will consist of a complete
K_{k}
. In all other coordinates we can choose any of
k
vertices, resulting in
\binom{m}{\ell}k^{m-\ell}
choices for
H_{\ell}
.
Similarly, there are
\binom{\ell}{i}k^{\ell-i}
ways to fix a projection
H_{i}
in
H_{\ell}
. Next, we aim to fix the
d
coordinates in which
H_{i}
and
H_{j}
differ. Clearly,
H_{i}
has to consist of single vertices in these coordinates, implying that there are
\binom{\ell-i}{d}
possibilities for choosing those coordinates and further
(k-1)^{d}
possibilities for specifying the value of
H_{j}
in those coordinates. To recap shortly, we already have specified
H_{\ell}
and
H_{i}
as well as the
d
coordinates of
H_{j}
in which it differs from
H_{i}
.

Continuing with fixing
H_{j}
, let us next assign the
j
coordinates in which
H_{j}
consists of complete graphs. There are
\ell-i-d
remaining coordinates, in which
H_{i}
consists of a single vertex. In each of those
H_{j}
has to be a complete
K_{k}
, as otherwise the two projections would either lie at distance at least three, or not span
H_{\ell}
. Thus, there only remain
j-(\ell-i-d)=i+j+d-\ell
coordinates in which
H_{j}
is complete and these coordinates can be chosen from any coordinate in which
H_{i}
is also complete, resulting in further
\binom{i}{i+j+d-\ell}
choices. Finally, it remains to specify the value of
H_{j}
in the remaining
\ell-j-d
coordinates in which
H_{j}
consists of a single vertex, leading to
k^{\ell-j-d}
further possibilities. After simplifying the expression, we are left with a total of
\binom{m}{\ell}\binom{\ell}{i}\binom{\ell-i}{d}\binom{i}{i+j+d-\ell}k^{m+\ell-i-j-d}(k-1)^{d}
possibilities. Lastly, note that in the case
i=j
a factor of
2
is lost due to symmetry and we have to require
(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{H_{m},t}
in order to make sure that the indices
(\ell,i,j,d)
fulfill the dimensional constraints posed in
Lemma

2.12
.
∎

Our next aim is to prove
Lemma

3.2
. Similarly as in (
3.13
), for every projection
H
, threshold dimension
t\in[n]
and indices
(\ell,i,j,d)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{4}
we define

(5.2)

\displaystyle f_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\coloneqq U_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\Phi(i)\Phi(j),

and set
t_{*}\coloneqq\left\lfloor 2\sqrt{n}\right\rfloor-2
as before. Observe that the functions
c_{\ell}
in
Lemma

3.2
are chosen in such a way that for all
\ell\in[t_{*}]
we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{\ell}}{c_{\ell-1}}\leq\frac{9\sqrt{2}}{10}\leq\frac{9\sqrt{k}}{10},

which can be verified by simple calculations.
To show
Lemma

3.2
we need the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
15\leq m\leq t_{*}
and let
H
be a projection of dimension
m
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{H,m}}f_{H,m}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\leq\left(1+k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)f_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right).

Observe that
Lemma

5.2
is quite similar to
Lemma

3.4
and we will prove
Lemma

5.2
using slightly more general results than necessary in order for them to be also applicable to
Lemma

3.4
. Let us start by comparing consecutive terms of
\Phi(\cdot)
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For
j\in[n]\cup\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(j+1)}{\Phi(j)}\leq\frac{j+1}{n}k^{\frac{j+5-2\sqrt{n}}{2}}.

Proof.

By the definition (
3.8
) of
\Phi(\cdot)
it holds that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(j+1)}{\Phi(j)}=\frac{c_{j+1}p^{\frac{j+1}{2}+1}(j+1)!2^{\frac{-j-1}{2}}(k-1)^{j+1}k^{\frac{(j+1)^{2}+2(j+1)}{4}}}{c_{j}p^{\frac{j}{2}+1}j!2^{\frac{-j}{2}}(k-1)^{j}k^{\frac{j^{2}+2j}{4}}}=\frac{c_{j+1}\sqrt{p}(j+1)k^{\frac{2j+3}{4}}(k-1)}{c_{j}\sqrt{2}}.

Furthermore, using (
5.3
) and
p\leq n^{-2}k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1}
we get

\displaystyle\frac{c_{j+1}\sqrt{p}(j+1)k^{\frac{2j+3}{4}}(k-1)}{c_{j}\sqrt{2}}\leq\sqrt{p}(j+1)k^{\frac{2j+3}{4}}(k-1)\leq\sqrt{p}(j+1)k^{\frac{j+4}{2}}\leq\frac{j+1}{n}k^{\frac{j+5-2\sqrt{n}}{2}},

yielding together with (
5.4
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(j+1)}{\Phi(j)}\leq\frac{j+1}{n}k^{\frac{j+5-2\sqrt{n}}{2}},

as claimed.
∎

In order to prove
Lemma

5.2
, we will now consider the effect it has on
f_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)
to change each of the respective indices
i,j
and
d
. We will first deal with different values of
j
, then, in
Lemma

5.5
, consider different values of
i
and lastly compare different values of
d
in
Lemma

5.6
.

Lemma 5.4
.

For
m\in[n]
, let
H
be a projection of dimension
m
. Let
15\leq t\leq\min\left\{m,t_{*}\right\}
and
(\ell,i,d)\in\hat{I}_{H,t}
such that
15\leq\ell\leq 3\sqrt{n}
. Then,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i}f_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\leq f_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,\ell-i-d,d\right)\left(1+k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{6}}\right).

Proof.

Observe first that
j
is the only index of
f_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)
that is not fixed on the left and right hand sides of the inequality in (
5.4
). To ease notation, let us define

(5.6)

\displaystyle F\left(j\right)\coloneqq f_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\overset{\eqref{def:ffunc2}}{=}U_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\Phi(i)\Phi(j)

and

j^{*}\coloneqq\ell-i-d.

By
Lemma

5.1
, for all
j<j^{*}
or
j\geq t_{*}
it holds that
U_{H_{m},t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)=0
. For
j^{*}\leq j\leq j+1\leq t_{*}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{U_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j+1,d\right)}{U_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)}=\frac{\binom{i}{i+j+1+d-\ell}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\mathbb{1}_{\left\{i=j+1\right\}}}}{\binom{i}{i+j+d-\ell}k\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\mathbb{1}_{\left\{i=j\right\}}}}\leq\frac{\ell-j-d}{k(i+j+d+1-\ell)},

implying together with
Lemma

5.3
that for all
j\geq j^{*}

(5.7)

\displaystyle\frac{F\left(j+1\right)}{F\left(j\right)}

\displaystyle{=}\frac{\Phi(i)\Phi(j+1)U_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j+1,d\right)}{\Phi(i)\Phi(j)U_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)}\leq k^{\frac{j+3-2\sqrt{n}}{2}}\frac{(j+1)(\ell-d-j)}{n(j+1+i+d-\ell)}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leq 6k^{\frac{j+3-2\sqrt{n}}{2}},

where the last inequality uses
j+1\leq t_{*}:=\left\lfloor 2\sqrt{n}\right\rfloor-2\leq 2\sqrt{n}
and
\ell\leq 3\sqrt{n}
.

Next we aim to show that
\frac{F\left(j+1\right)}{F\left(j\right)}\leq 1
. Indeed, if
j\leq\frac{5\sqrt{n}}{3}
we obtain from (
5.8
) that

(5.9)

\displaystyle\frac{F\left(j+1\right)}{F\left(j\right)}\leq 6k^{\frac{j+3-2\sqrt{n}}{2}}\leq 6k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{7}}\leq 1.

If
j>\frac{5\sqrt{n}}{3}
, because
j+1\leq t_{*}-1\leq 2\sqrt{n}-3
and
j+1+i+d-\ell\geq\frac{10\sqrt{n}}{3}-3\sqrt{n}\geq\frac{\sqrt{n}}{3}
, we obtain from (
5.7
)

(5.10)

\displaystyle\frac{F\left(j+1\right)}{F\left(j\right)}\leq k^{\frac{j+3-2\sqrt{n}}{2}}\frac{(j+1)(\ell-d-j)}{n(j+1+i+d-\ell)}\leq\frac{\left(2\sqrt{n}\right)\left(4\sqrt{n}\right)}{n\frac{\sqrt{n}}{3}}\leq\frac{24}{\sqrt{n}}\leq 1.

It thus follows from (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) that

\frac{F\left(j+1\right)}{F\left(j\right)}\leq 1\quad\text{for all}\quad j\geq j^{*}

and hence

(5.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i}F\left(j\right)\leq F\left(j^{*}\right)+iF\left(j^{*}+1\right).

Furthermore, as
j^{*}\leq i
, it follows from
\ell=i+j^{*}+d
that
j^{*}\leq\frac{\ell}{2}\leq\frac{3\sqrt{n}}{2}
, implying together with (
5.8
)

\displaystyle\frac{F\left(j^{*}+1\right)}{F\left(j^{*}\right)}\leq 6k^{\frac{j^{*}+3-2\sqrt{n}}{2}}\leq 6k^{\frac{3-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{n}}{2}}\leq k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{5}},

and thus together with (
5.11
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i}F\left(j\right)\leq F\left(j^{*}\right)\left(1+ik^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{5}}\right)\leq F\left(j^{*}\right)\left(1+k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{6}}\right),

as claimed.
∎

We continue by considering different values of
i
. Here we need to make a case distinction depending on whether
\ell>t_{*}
or not.

Lemma 5.5
.

(a) For
t_{*}<\ell\leq 3\sqrt{n}
and
d\in\{0,1,2\}
it holds that

(5.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{t_{*}-1}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,\ell-i-d,d\right)\leq\left(1+k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{6}}\right)f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,t_{*}-1,\ell-(t_{*}-1)-d,d\right).

(b) For
15\leq m\leq t_{*}
let
H
be a projection of dimension
m
. Then for
d\in\{0,1,2\}
we have

(5.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f_{H,m}\left(m,i,m-i-d,d\right)\leq\left(1+k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right)f_{H,m}\left(m,m-1-\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}},1-d+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}},d\right).

Proof.

We first derive some properties that are relevant for both (a) and (b). Assume
H
is a projection of
G
and let
t\in[n]
(in fact, we will take
t=t_{*}
for part (a) and
t=m
for part (b)). To ease notation, for
(\ell,i,d)\in\hat{I}_{H,t}
we set
j^{*}:=\ell-i-d
and define

(5.14)

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{H,t}\left(i\right)\coloneqq f_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j^{*},d\right)\overset{\eqref{def:ffunc2}}{=}U_{H,t}\left(\ell,i,j^{*},d\right)\Phi(i)\Phi(j^{*}).

We aim to bound the ratio
\frac{\hat{F}_{H,t}\left(i-1\right)}{\hat{F}_{H,t}\left(i\right)}
from above, so let us assume for now that we are on the domain where
\hat{F}_{H,t}\left(\cdot\right)
is non-zero. First observe that by (
5.4
) and (
5.3
) it holds that

(5.15)

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(i-1)\Phi(j^{*}+1)}{\Phi(i)\Phi(j^{*})}\overset{\eqref{eq:ratio:P(j):equality}}{=}k^{-\frac{i-j^{*}-1}{2}}\frac{c_{i-1}c_{j^{*}+1}}{c_{i}c_{j^{*}}}\frac{(j^{*}+1)}{i}\overset{\eqref{eq:cubeintspanconst_bounds}}{\leq}k^{-\frac{i-j^{*}-2}{2}}\frac{9(j^{*}+1)}{10i}.

Furthermore, it follows from
Lemma

5.1
that

(5.16)

\displaystyle\frac{U_{H,m}\left(\ell,i-1,j^{*}+1,d\right)}{U_{H,m}\left(\ell,i,j^{*},d\right)}=\frac{\binom{\ell}{i-1}\binom{\ell-i+1}{d}}{\binom{\ell}{i}\binom{\ell-i}{d}}\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\mathbb{1}_{\left\{i-1=j^{*}+1\right\}}}}{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\mathbb{1}_{\left\{i=j^{*}\right\}}}}\leq\frac{i(\ell-i+1)}{(\ell-i+1)(\ell-i-d+1)}=\frac{i}{j^{*}+1}.

From the definition (
5.14
) of
\hat{F}_{H,t}\left(\cdot\right)
as well as from (
5.15
) and (
5.16
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{F}_{H,t}\left(i-1\right)}{\hat{F}_{H,t}\left(i\right)}

\displaystyle{=}\frac{\Phi(i-1)\Phi(j^{*}+1)U_{H,m}\left(\ell,i-1,j^{*}+1,d\right)}{\Phi(i)\Phi(j^{*})U_{H,m}\left(\ell,i,j^{*},d\right)}

\displaystyle{\leq}k^{-\frac{i-j^{*}-2}{2}}\frac{9(j^{*}+1)}{10i}\frac{i}{j^{*}+1}

(5.17)

\displaystyle=k^{-\frac{i-j^{*}-2}{2}}\frac{9}{10}

(5.18)

\displaystyle<k^{-\frac{i-j^{*}-2}{2}}.

From (
5.17
) we obtain

(5.19)

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{F}_{H,t}\left(i-1\right)}{\hat{F}_{H,t}\left(i\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9}{10},

because
i-1\geq j^{*}+1
on the domain where
\hat{F}_{H,t}\left(i-1\right)
is non-zero. Thus the function
i\mapsto\hat{F}_{H,t}\left(i\right)
is increasing in
i
and it remains to determine the largest value of
i
at which
\hat{F}_{H,t}\left(i\right)
is non-zero. We proceed now with a case distinction, starting with the proof of (
5.12
).

To prove part (a) take
H=G
and
t=t_{*}
. In this case, the largest value
i
may take is at
i=t_{*}-1
at which
j^{*}:=\ell-i-d=\ell-t_{*}-d+1
. Checking all conditions from the definition of the set
I_{G,t_{*}}
of admissible indices it indeed holds that
(\ell,t_{*}-1,j^{*},d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}
and thus it follows from (
5.19
) that

(5.20)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{t_{*}-1}\hat{F}_{G,t_{*}}\left(i\right)\overset{\eqref{eq:ffunc:diagonalbound2}}{\leq}\hat{F}_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*}-1\right)+\hat{F}_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*}-2\right)\sum_{i=0}^{t_{*}-2}\left(\frac{9}{10}\right)^{i}\leq\hat{F}_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*}-1\right)+10\hat{F}_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*}-2\right),

where the last inequality follows from a standard bound on geometric series.
Furthermore, it follows from (
5.18
) (by taking
H=G
,
t=t_{*}
,
i=t_{*}-1
and
j^{*}:=\ell-i-d
) that

(5.21)

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{F}_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*}-2\right)}{\hat{F}_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*}-1\right)}\overset{\eqref{eq:ffunc:diagonalbound}}{\leq}k^{-\frac{(t_{*}-1)-(\ell-t_{*}+1-d)-2}{2}}\leq k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{4}},

where the last inequality is because
(t_{*}-1)-(\ell-t_{*}+1-d)-2\geq 2t_{*}-\ell-4\geq\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}
.
Combining (
5.20
) and (
5.21
) implies

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{t_{*}-1}\hat{F}_{G,t_{*}}\left(i\right)\overset{\eqref{eq:sumdom:i:eq3}}{\leq}\hat{F}_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*}-1\right)\left(1+\frac{10\hat{F}_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*}-2\right)}{\hat{F}_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*}-1\right)}\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\hat{F}_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*}-1\right)\left(1+10k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\hat{F}_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*}-1\right)\left(1+k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{6}}\right),

and thus obtaining (
5.12
).

To prove part (b) let
H
be a projection of dimension
m
and assume
15\leq m\leq t_{*}
. Consider the function
\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(i\right)
as defined in (
5.14
) by taking
\ell=m
. Recalling that
i
now may take values from
0
to
m-1
, it follows from the definition of the set
I_{G,t_{*}}
of admissible indices that
\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(i\right)
is only zero in the case
i=m-1
and
d=2
. Thus,
\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(i\right)
takes its largest value at
i=m-1
if

, and at
i=m-2
if
d=2
. Hence, it follows (analogously to (
5.20
)) from (
5.19
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(i\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(m-1-\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}}\right)+\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(m-2-\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}}\right)\sum_{i=0}^{m-2}\left(\frac{9}{10}\right)^{i}

(5.22)

\displaystyle\leq\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(m-1-\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}}\right)+10\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(m-2-\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}}\right).

Furthermore, if
d\in\left\{0,1\right\}
, we obtain from (
5.18
) (by taking
t=m
,
i=m-1
and
j^{*}:=\ell-i-d=m-i-d=1-d
) that

(5.23)

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(m-2\right)}{\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(m-1\right)}\overset{\eqref{eq:ffunc:diagonalbound}}{\leq}k^{-\frac{m-1-(1-d)-2}{2}}\leq k^{-\frac{m-4}{2}},

and, if
d=2
, it also follows from (
5.18
) (by taking
t=m
,
i=m-2
and

) that

(5.24)

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(m-3\right)}{\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(m-2\right)}\overset{\eqref{eq:ffunc:diagonalbound}}{\leq}k^{-\frac{m-4}{2}}.

Combining (
5.22
), (
5.23
) and (
5.24
) implies

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(i\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(m-1-\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}}\right)\left(1+\frac{10\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(m-2-\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}}\right)}{\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(m-1-\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(m-1-\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}}\right)\left(1+10k^{-\frac{m-4}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\hat{F}_{H,m}\left(m-1-\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}}\right)\left(1+k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right),

where we used the fact that
10\leq 2^{\frac{7}{2}}\leq k^{\frac{7}{2}}
, completing the proof.
∎

Next, we consider all the possible values of
d\in\{0,1,2\}
.

Lemma 5.6
.

(a) For
t_{*}<\ell\leq 3\sqrt{n}
it holds that

(5.25)

\displaystyle\sum_{d=0}^{2}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,t_{*}-1,\ell-(t_{*}-1)-d,d\right)\leq\left(1+k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{6}}\right)f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,t_{*}-1,\ell-t_{*}-1,2\right).

(b) For
15\leq m\leq t_{*}
let
H
be a projection of dimension
m
. Then we have

(5.26)

\displaystyle\sum_{d=0}^{2}f_{H,m}\left(m,m-1-\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}},1-d+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}},d\right)\leq\left(1+\frac{3}{n}\right)f_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right).

Proof.

Let us start with (
5.25
). Note first that the function
d\mapsto U_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,t_{*}-1,\ell-(t_{*}-1)-d,d\right)
is clearly increasing in
d
.
Using
Lemma

5.3
it thus follows that for
d\in\{0,1\}
,

\displaystyle\frac{f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,t_{*}-1,\ell-(t_{*}-1)-d,d\right)}{f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,t_{*}-1,\ell-(t_{*}-1)-d-1,d+1\right)}\leq\frac{\Phi(\ell-t_{*}-d+1)}{\Phi(\ell-t_{*}-d)}\leq\frac{\ell-t_{*}-d+1}{n}k^{\frac{\ell-t_{*}-d+5-2\sqrt{n}}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{6}},

where the last inequality uses
\ell-t_{*}-d+1\leq\sqrt{n}+4
, immediately implying (
5.25
).

Moving on to the case
15\leq m\leq t_{*}
, an analogous argument shows that

(5.27)

\displaystyle\frac{f_{H,m}\left(m,m-1,1,0\right)}{f_{H,m}\left(m,m-1,0,1\right)}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

For the ratio
\frac{f_{H,m}\left(m,m-1,0,1\right)}{f_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right)}
we need to be a bit more precise. A direct computation shows that

(5.28)

\displaystyle\frac{U_{H,m}\left(m,m-1,0,1\right)}{U_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right)}=\frac{2}{(k-1)(m-1)}

and, being a bit more careful in the estimates in
Lemma

5.3
, it can be shown that

(5.29)

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(m-1)}{\Phi(m-2)}\leq\frac{(k-1)(m-1)}{n}k^{\frac{m+2-2\sqrt{n}}{2}}.

Putting these together, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{f_{H,m}\left(m,m-1,0,1\right)}{f_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right)}

\displaystyle{=}\frac{U_{H,m}\left(m,m-1,0,1\right)}{U_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right)}\frac{\Phi(m-1)}{\Phi(m-2)}

\displaystyle{\leq}\frac{2}{(k-1)(m-1)}\frac{\Phi(m-1)}{\Phi(m-2)}

(5.30)

\displaystyle{\leq}\frac{2}{(k-1)(m-1)}\frac{(k-1)(m-1)}{n}k^{\frac{m+2-2\sqrt{n}}{2}}\leq\frac{2}{n},

where the last inequality uses
m\leq t_{*}:=\left\lfloor 2\sqrt{n}\right\rfloor-2\leq 2\sqrt{n}-2
. Combining (
5.27
) and (
5.30
) now yields

\displaystyle\sum_{d=0}^{2}f_{H,m}\left(m,m-1-\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}},1-d+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}},d\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\frac{3}{2}f_{H,m}\left(m,m-1,0,1\right)+f_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\left(1+\frac{3}{n}\right)f_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right),

completing the proof.
∎

Now we have all the tools to show
Lemma

5.2
.

Proof of
Lemma

5.2
.

Let
15\leq m\leq t_{*}
and let
H
be a projection of dimension
m
. Observe first that by definition of
I_{H,m}
, any indices
(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{H,m}
have to satisfy
\ell=m,\ d\in\left\{0,1,2\right\}
and
0\leq j\leq i\leq\ell-1
.
It thus follows from
Lemma

5.4
that

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{H,m}}f_{H,m}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j=0}^{i}f_{H,m}\left(m,i,j,d\right)

(5.31)

\displaystyle{\leq}\left(1+k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{6}}\right)\sum_{d=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f_{H,m}\left(m,i,m-i-d,d\right).

Furthermore, by Lemmas
5.5
(b) and
5.6
(b) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{d=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f_{H,m}\left(m,i,m-i-d,d\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\left(1+k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right)\sum_{d=0}^{2}f_{H,m}\left(m,m-1-\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}},1-d+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{d=2\right\}},d\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\left(1+k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{3}{n}\right)f_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right).

Combining (
5.31
) and (
5.32
) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{H,m}}f_{H,m}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{6}}\right)\left(1+k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{3}{n}\right)f_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)f_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right),

where the last inequality is because
\left(1+k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{6}}\right)\left(1+\frac{3}{n}\right)\leq\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)
holds for large enough

.
∎

Lemma

now enables us to prove

3.2
.

Proof of
Lemma

3.2
.

We will show the statement by induction on
m
. The case
0\leq m\leq 14
can easily be proved by a tedious enumeration and will be omitted. Below we assume
15\leq m\leq t_{*}
. Let
H
be a projection of dimension
m
and assume that
\varphi({m^{\prime}})\leq\Phi(m^{\prime})
for all
m^{\prime}<m
. By
Lemma

3.1
and using the induction hypothesis we obtain analogously as in (
3.9
) that

\displaystyle\varphi({m})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{H,m}}U_{H_{m},m}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\varphi({i})\varphi({j})\leq\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{H,m}}U_{H,m}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\Phi(i)\Phi(j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{H,m}}f_{H,m}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right).

Furthermore,
Lemma

5.2
implies

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{H,m}}f_{H,m}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\leq\left(1+k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)f_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right),

yielding

(5.33)

\displaystyle\varphi({m})\leq\left(1+k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)f_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right).

By the definition of
\Phi(\cdot)
and by
Lemma

5.1
it holds that

\displaystyle f_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi(m-2)\Phi(0)U_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right)

\displaystyle=c_{m-2}p^{\frac{m-2}{2}+1}(m-2)!\ 2^{-\frac{m-2}{2}}(k-1)^{m-2}k^{\frac{(m-2)^{2}+2(m-2)}{4}}p\binom{m}{2}(k-1)^{2}k^{m}

\displaystyle=c_{m-2}p^{\frac{m}{2}+1}m!\ 2^{-\frac{m}{2}}(k-1)^{m}k^{\frac{m^{2}+2m}{4}}

(5.34)

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{m-2}}{c_{m}}\Phi(m).

Using
\left(1+k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)=\frac{c_{m}}{c_{m-1}}\leq\frac{c_{m}}{c_{m-2}}
, it follows together with (

) that

(5.35)

\displaystyle\left(1+k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)f_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right)\leq\frac{c_{m}}{c_{m-2}}\frac{c_{m-2}}{c_{m}}\Phi(m)\leq\Phi(m).

Combining (
5.33
) and (

) we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi({m})\overset{\eqref{eq:proof:upboundcubeintspan:eq2}}{\leq}\left(1+k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)f_{H,m}\left(m,m-2,0,2\right)\overset{\eqref{eq:proof:upboundcubeintspan:eq3}}{\leq}\Phi(m),

completing the induction and thus the proof.
∎

Next, we show that the functions
c_{m}
are bounded from above by a universal constant.

Lemma 5.7
.

For all
m\in[t_{*}]
we have

\displaystyle c_{m}=\Theta\left(1\right).

Proof.

It is easy to see that the function
m\mapsto c_{m}
is increasing in
m
. Thus, it suffices to bound
c_{t_{*}}
from above and we obtain

\displaystyle c_{t_{*}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{9\sqrt{2}}{10}\right)^{14}\prod_{m=15}^{t_{*}}\left(1+k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Theta(1)\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)^{t_{*}}\prod_{m=15}^{t_{*}}\exp\left(k^{-\frac{m-11}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Theta(1)\exp\left(\frac{4t_{*}}{n}\right)\exp\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}k^{-\frac{m}{2}}\right)=\Theta(1),

where the last equality follows because
t_{*}:=\left\lfloor 2\sqrt{n}\right\rfloor-2\leq n
.
∎

We continue by proving
Claim

3.3
.

Proof of
Claim

3.3
.

We aim to bound the function
f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)
for all indices
(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(2)}
.
Using crude bounds we get

\displaystyle U_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n}{\ell}\binom{\ell}{i}\binom{\ell-i}{d}\binom{i}{j+i+d-\ell}k^{n+\ell-i-j-d}(k-1)^{d}

\displaystyle\leq n^{\ell}\ell^{i+d}i^{j+i+d-\ell}k^{n+\ell}

(5.36)

\displaystyle\leq n^{3\ell}k^{n+\ell}.

On the other hand, by the definition (
3.8
) of
\Phi(\cdot)
and using
j!\leq i!\leq n^{\ell}
and
Lemma

5.7
we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi(i)\Phi(j)

\displaystyle{=}c_{i}c_{j}p^{\frac{i+j}{2}+2}(i!)(j!)2^{-\frac{i+j}{2}}(k-1)^{2(i+j)}k^{\frac{1}{4}\left(i^{2}+j^{2}+2i+2j\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\Theta\left(1\right)p^{\frac{i+j}{2}+2}n^{2\ell}k^{\frac{1}{4}\left(i^{2}+j^{2}+10i+10j\right)}.

Furthermore, using
p\leq p_{*}\leq k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1}
it follows that

(5.37)

\displaystyle\Phi(i)\Phi(j)\leq\Theta\left(1\right)n^{2\ell}k^{\frac{1}{4}\left(i^{2}+j^{2}-(i+j)(4\sqrt{n}-12)+16\sqrt{n}-8\right)}.

In order to obtain a universal upper bound of (
5.37
) for all indices
(i,j)
under consideration, we focus on the exponent of (
5.37
) and consider the function

h:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R},(x,y)\mapsto x^{2}+y^{2}-(x+y)(4\sqrt{n}-12)+16\sqrt{n}-8.

Note that the function
h(x,y)
defines a circular paraboloid whose center is shifted to
(2\sqrt{n}-6,2\sqrt{n}-6)
.
Recalling
(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(2)}
, where

\displaystyle I_{G,t_{*}}^{(2)}\coloneqq\left\{(\ell,i,j,d)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{4}\mid\left(3\sqrt{n}<\ell\leq\min\left\{i+j+d,\dime\left(G\right)\right\}\right)\land(j\leq i<t_{*})\land(i+d<\ell)\land(d\in\{0,1,2\})\right\},

we note that the function
h(x,y)
should however be considered only for
(i,j)\in\tilde{I}
, where

\tilde{I}:=\left\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}\mid(j\leq i<t_{*})\wedge(i+j\geq\ell-d\geq 3\sqrt{n}-1)\text{ for }d\in\{0,1,2\}\right\}.

Hence, the (shifted circular paraboloid) function
h:\tilde{I}\to\mathbb{R}
takes its maximum when
i
is maximal and
j
is minimal possible on the domain
\tilde{I}
, in other words, the maximiser of the function
h
restricted on the domain
\tilde{I}
is given by
i=t_{*}-1=\left\lfloor 2\sqrt{n}\right\rfloor-3
and
j=\left\lceil 3\sqrt{n}\right\rceil-1-i=\left\lceil 3\sqrt{n}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor 2\sqrt{n}\right\rfloor+2
. Relaxing the domain
\tilde{I}
of
h
to the reals, it follows that at the function
h:\tilde{I}\to\mathbb{R}
is upper bounded by

h(2\sqrt{n}-3,\sqrt{n}+2)=-7n+48\sqrt{n}-7\leq-6n
, implying together with (
5.37
) that

(5.38)

\displaystyle\Phi(i)\Phi(j)\leq\Theta\left(1\right)n^{2\ell}k^{-\frac{3n}{2}}.

Combining (
5.36
) and (
5.38
) and plugging into the definition (
5.2
) of
f_{G,t_{*}}
, we obtain that for large enough
n
and for all
(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(2)}
,

\displaystyle f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

\displaystyle{=}\Phi(i)\Phi(j)U_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

(5.39)

\displaystyle{\leq}\Theta\left(1\right)n^{5\ell}k^{-\frac{n}{2}+\ell}\leq n^{20\sqrt{n}}k^{-\frac{n}{3}}=o(n^{-3}),

where the penultimate inequality uses that
\ell\leq i+j+d\leq 2t_{*}\leq 4\sqrt{n}
.
Hence, by the definition of
I_{G,t_{*}}^{(2)}
and (
5.39
) it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(2)}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=3\sqrt{n}+1}^{4\sqrt{n}}\sum_{d=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{t_{*}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{i}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3n^{3}\max_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(2)}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\overset{\eqref{eq:prf_of_expec_to_zero_largeterms:4}}{=}o(1).

∎

Moving on, we now aim to show
Lemma

3.4
. As mentioned before, the proof of
Lemma

3.4
goes along similar lines as the proof of
Lemma

5.2
, however in addition to dealing with different values of
i,j
and
d
, we now also have to consider different values of
\ell
.

Lemma 5.8
.

It holds that

(5.40)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=t_{*}+1}^{3\sqrt{n}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,t_{*}-1,\ell-t_{*}-1,2\right)\leq 2k^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right).

Proof.

To ease notation, let us define for
t_{*}+1\leq\ell\leq 3\sqrt{n}

\displaystyle\bar{F}\left(\ell\right)\coloneqq f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,t_{*}-1,\ell-t_{*}-1,2\right)

and consider the ratio

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{F}\left(\ell+1\right)}{\bar{F}\left(\ell\right)}=\frac{\Phi(\ell-t_{*})}{\Phi(\ell-t_{*}-1)}\frac{U_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell+1,t_{*}-1,\ell-t_{*},2\right)}{U_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,t_{*}-1,\ell-t_{*}-1,2\right)}.

Using
Lemma

5.3
and
\ell-t_{*}\leq\sqrt{n}+2
we obtain

(5.41)

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(\ell-t_{*})}{\Phi(\ell-t_{*}-1)}\leq\frac{(\ell-t_{*})}{n}k^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\ell-t_{*}+4-2\sqrt{n}\right)}\leq k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{3}}.

Furthermore, we get

\displaystyle\frac{U_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell+1,t_{*}-1,\ell-t_{*},2\right)}{U_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,t_{*}-1,\ell-t_{*}-1,2\right)}=\frac{\binom{n}{\ell+1}\binom{\ell+1}{t_{*}-1}\binom{\ell-t_{*}+2}{2}}{\binom{n}{\ell}\binom{\ell}{t_{*}-1}\binom{\ell-t_{*}+1}{2}}=\frac{n-\ell}{\ell-t_{*}}\leq n,

implying together with (
5.41
)

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{F}\left(\ell+1\right)}{\bar{F}\left(\ell\right)}\leq nk^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{3}}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

It thus follows that

(5.42)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=t_{*}+1}^{3\sqrt{n}}\bar{F}\left(\ell\right)\leq 2\bar{F}\left(t_{*}+1\right).

On the other hand, using Lemmas
5.1
and
5.3
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{F}\left(t_{*}+1\right)}{f_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi(t_{*}-1)}{\Phi(t_{*}-2)}\frac{U_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*}+1,t_{*}-1,0,2\right)}{U_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t_{*}-1}{n}k^{\frac{1}{2}\left(t_{*}+3-2\sqrt{n}\right)}\frac{\binom{n}{t_{*}+1}\binom{t_{*}+1}{2}}{\binom{n}{t_{*}}\binom{t_{*}}{2}}\leq\frac{(n-t_{*})k^{\frac{1}{2}}}{n}\leq k^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the penultimate inequality uses
t_{*}:=\left\lfloor 2\sqrt{n}\right\rfloor-2\leq 2\sqrt{n}-2
.

Combining with (
5.42
) we thus obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=t_{*}+1}^{3\sqrt{n}}\bar{F}\left(\ell\right)\leq 2\bar{F}\left(t_{*}+1\right)\leq 2k^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right),

completing the proof.
∎

Lemma

5.8
provides us with the final ingredient to prove
Lemma

3.4
.

Proof of
Lemma

3.4
.

Let us partition
I_{G,t_{*}}^{(1)}
into
I_{G,t_{*}}^{(1)}=I_{G,t_{*}}^{(a)}\cup I_{G,t_{*}}^{(b)}
, where

\displaystyle I_{G,t_{*}}^{(a)}\coloneqq\left\{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(1)}\mid\ell=t_{*}\right\}\ \text{ and }\ I_{G,t_{*}}^{(b)}\coloneqq\left\{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(1)}\mid\ell\geq t_{*}+1\right\}.

Starting with the sum over indices in
I_{G,t_{*}}^{(b)}
, we observe that

(5.43)

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(b)}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)=\sum_{\ell=t_{*}+1}^{3\sqrt{n}}\sum_{d=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{t_{*}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{i}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right).

Using
Lemma

5.4
and
Lemma

5.5
(a) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=t_{*}+1}^{3\sqrt{n}}\sum_{d=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{t_{*}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{i}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\left(1+k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{6}}\right)\sum_{\ell=t_{*}+1}^{3\sqrt{n}}\sum_{d=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,\ell-i-d,d\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\left(1+k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{6}}\right)^{2}\sum_{\ell=t_{*}+1}^{3\sqrt{n}}\sum_{d=0}^{2}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,t_{*}-1,\ell-(t_{*}-1)-d,d\right),

and furthermore, by
Lemma

5.6
(a) and
Lemma

5.8

\displaystyle\left(1+k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{6}}\right)^{2}\sum_{\ell=t_{*}+1}^{3\sqrt{n}}\sum_{d=0}^{2}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,t_{*}-1,\ell-(t_{*}-1)-d,d\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\left(1+k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{6}}\right)^{3}\sum_{\ell=t_{*}+1}^{3\sqrt{n}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,t_{*}-1,\ell-t_{*}-1,2\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\left(1+k^{-\frac{\sqrt{n}}{6}}\right)^{3}2k^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3k^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right),

which yields together with (
5.43
)

(5.44)

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(b)}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)\leq 3k^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right).

To bound the sum over indices in
I_{G,t_{*}}^{(a)}
, observe first that by
Lemma

5.1
it holds that for any indices
(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(a)}
and any projection
H
of dimension
t_{*}
,

\displaystyle U_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)=\binom{n}{t_{*}}k^{n-t_{*}}U_{H,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right),

implying

(5.45)

\displaystyle f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)=\binom{n}{t_{*}}k^{n-t_{*}}f_{H,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right).

Furthermore, it follows by
Lemma

5.2
that

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(a)}}f_{H_{t_{*}},t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+k^{-\frac{t_{*}-11}{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{4}{n}\right)f_{H_{t_{*}},t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right)

(5.46)

\displaystyle\leq 2f_{H_{t_{*}},t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right),

yielding together with (
5.45
) that for large enough
n

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(a)}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

\displaystyle{=}\binom{n}{t_{*}}k^{n-t_{*}}\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(a)}}f_{H_{t_{*}},t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}2\binom{n}{t_{*}}k^{n-t_{*}}f_{H_{t_{*}},t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right)

(5.47)

\displaystyle{=}2f_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right).

Finally, using
I_{G,t_{*}}^{(1)}=I_{G,t_{*}}^{(a)}\cup I_{G,t_{*}}^{(b)}
and combining (
5.47
) and (
5.44
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(1)}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(a)}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)+\sum_{(\ell,i,j,d)\in I_{G,t_{*}}^{(b)}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(\ell,i,j,d\right)

\displaystyle\leq 5k^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right),

as desired.
∎

We continue by showing
Claim

3.5
.

Proof of
Claim

3.5
.

As a special case of (
5.45
) it holds that

\displaystyle f_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right)=\binom{n}{t_{*}}k^{n-t_{*}}f_{H_{t_{*}},t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right).

In addition, as a special case of (
5.34
) we have
f_{H,t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right)\leq\frac{c_{t_{*}-2}}{c_{t_{*}}}\Phi(t_{*})
and thus

\displaystyle f_{H_{t_{*}},t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right)\leq\frac{c_{t_{*}-2}}{c_{t_{*}}}\Phi(t_{*})\leq\Phi(t_{*}),

where the last inequality follows as the functions
c_{m}
are clearly increasing in
m
.
This implies

\displaystyle f_{G,t_{*}}\left(t_{*},t_{*}-2,0,2\right)\leq\binom{n}{t_{*}}k^{n-t_{*}}\Phi(t_{*}),

as claimed.
∎

We complete the proof of
Theorem

thm:main:threshold
(a)
by showing that indeed the expected number of internally spanned projections of dimension
t_{*}
turns to
0
as
n
tends to infinity.

Proof of
Lemma

3.7
.

Recalling the definition (
3.8
) of the function
\Phi(\cdot)
and using
Lemma

5.7
we obtain

\displaystyle\binom{n}{t_{*}}n^{k-t_{*}+\frac{1}{2}}\Phi(t_{*})

\displaystyle{=}c_{t_{*}}\binom{n}{t_{*}}(t_{*}!)k^{n-t_{*}+\frac{1}{2}}p^{\frac{t_{*}}{2}+1}2^{-\frac{t_{*}}{2}-1}(k-1)^{t_{*}}k^{\frac{t_{*}^{2}+2t_{*}}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{t_{*}}n^{t_{*}}\left(\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\right)^{\frac{t_{*}}{2}+1}k^{n+\frac{1}{4}\left(t_{*}^{2}+2t_{*}+2\right)-(2\sqrt{n}-1)(\frac{t_{*}}{2}+1)}\quad(\text{because }p\leq p_{*}=n^{-2}k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1})

(5.48)

\displaystyle{\leq}\Theta(1)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{t_{*}}{2}+1}k^{n+\frac{1}{4}\left(t_{*}^{2}+2t_{*}+2\right)-(2\sqrt{n}-1)(\frac{t_{*}}{2}+1)}.

Now observe that the function
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},x\mapsto n+\frac{1}{4}\left(x^{2}+2x+2\right)-(2\sqrt{n}-1)(\frac{x}{2}+1)
defines a convex parabola whose center is shifted to
2\sqrt{n}-2
and thus it takes its minimum at
x=2\sqrt{n}-2
and is decreasing up to this value. In particular, as
t_{*}\coloneqq\left\lfloor 2\sqrt{n}\right\rfloor-2\geq 2\sqrt{n}-3
it holds that
g(t_{*})\leq g(2\sqrt{n}-3)=\frac{3}{4}
.
Thus, we obtain together with (
5.48
) that

\displaystyle\binom{n}{t_{*}}n^{k-t_{*}+\frac{1}{2}}\Phi(t_{*})\leq\Theta\left(1\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\sqrt{n}-\frac{1}{2}}k^{\frac{3}{4}}=o(1),

where the last equality uses that
k\leq 2^{\sqrt{n}}
.
∎

6.
Proofs of auxiliary results for the upper threshold

In this section we prove auxiliary results from
Section

4
. Recall
\ell^{*}\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
. For each
1\leq\ell\leq\ell^{*}
we set

\displaystyle\tilde{C_{\ell}}\coloneqq\binom{n-2\ell+2}{2}(k-1)^{2}k^{2\ell-2}.

Recall that
S_{\ell}
denotes the set of all sequentially spanning sequences of length
\ell
in
G=(V,E)
and we start by computing
|S_{\ell}|
.

Proof of
Lemma

4.2
.

Let
1\leq\ell\leq\ell^{*}
and let
\overline{v}_{\ell-1}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{\ell-1})\in S_{\ell-1}
be a sequentially spanning sequence of length
\ell-1
. Looking at Property (
2.1
) it follows immediately that

\displaystyle C_{\ell}:

\displaystyle=\left|\left\{w\in V\mid\overline{v}_{\ell}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{\ell-1},w)\in S_{\ell}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left\{w\in V\mid\distance\left(\left[{\left\{\overline{v}_{\ell-1}\right\}}\right],w\right)=2\right\}\right|.

By
Lemma

2.10
,
\left[{\left\{\overline{v}_{\ell-1}\right\}}\right]
spans a projection
H
of dimension
2\ell-2
. Thus, it suffices to count the number of vertices that lie at distance two from
H
. To that end, note that any vertex
v
satisfying
\distance\left(v,H_{2\ell-2}\right)=2
has to differ from
H
in exactly two coordinates. As there are
2\ell-2
coordinates, in which
H
consists of a complete graph
K_{k}
, there are
\binom{n-2\ell+2}{2}
possibilities for choosing the coordinates in which
v
differs from
H
and
(k-1)^{2}
possibilities for specifying a value of
v
in those coordinates. In all coordinates, in which
H
consists of a complete graph,
v
can take an arbitrary value
\in[k]
, leading to
k^{2\ell-2}
additional choices. All remaining coordinates of
v
have to coincide with
H
, implying

(6.1)

\displaystyle C_{\ell}=\binom{n-2\ell+2}{2}(k-1)^{2}k^{2\ell-2}=:\tilde{C_{\ell}},

as claimed.

To show (
4.1
) and thus to explicitly compute
|S_{\ell}|
, recall that
|S_{0}|=k^{n}
and note that by the definitions of
S_{\ell}
and
C_{\ell}
we have

(6.2)

\displaystyle|S_{\ell}|=|S_{\ell-1}|\cdot C_{\ell}\quad\quad\text{for all }\quad 1\leq\ell\leq\ell^{*}.

Thus, (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) imply

\displaystyle|S_{\ell}|

\displaystyle=k^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}C_{j}=k^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\binom{n-2j+2}{2}(k-1)^{2}k^{2j-2}=k^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}C_{j}=\frac{n!}{(n-2\ell)!}(k-1)^{2\ell}2^{-\ell}k^{n+\ell^{2}-\ell},

completing the proof.

∎

We continue with the proof of
Lemma

4.3
on the upper bound on the number of pairs of sequentially spanning sequences of length
\ell
that intersect exactly in
i
vertices, which was denoted by

Y_{\ell}(i)\coloneqq\left\{\{\overline{v}_{\ell},\overline{w}_{\ell}\}\subseteq S_{\ell}\mid\left|\overline{v}_{\ell}\cap\overline{w}_{\ell}\right|=i\right\}.

In general, our proof strategy is to start with pairs of very short sequences and then sequentially extend them to length
\ell
. To that end, we first take a look at pairs of sequences that do not intersect until their last index. Note that the resulting sequences can then either intersect in one or two vertices, depending on whether only one of the respective final vertices is contained in the other sequence, or both of them are.
In particular, for
m\in[n]\cup\{0\}
and
j\in[2]
let us denote by

(6.3)

\displaystyle Y_{m}^{(j)}\coloneqq\left\{\left\{\overline{v}_{m}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{m}),\overline{w}_{m}=(w_{0},\ldots,w_{m})\right\}\in Y_{m}(j)\mid\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{m-1}\}\cap\{w_{0},\ldots,w_{m-1}\}=\emptyset\right\},

the set of pairs of sequentially spanning sequences of length
m
with intersection of size
j
such that the cut-off sequences (i.e., the sequences without the last vertex) of length
m-1
have empty intersection.
In the next lemma we bound the sizes of these sets from above.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
m\in[n]\cup\left\{0\right\}
and let
Y_{m}^{(1)}
and
Y_{m}^{(2)}
be defined as in (
6.3
). Then,

(a)

|Y_{m}^{(1)}|\leq\frac{2(m+1)}{k^{n}}|S_{m}|^{2}
;

(b)

|Y_{m}^{(2)}|\leq\mathbb{1}_{\left\{m\geq 1\right\}}\frac{m(m+1)}{C_{m}k^{n}}|S_{m}|^{2}
.

Proof.

We start by showing
(a)
. Due to symmetry we can assume w.lo.g. that all pairs of sequences
\{\overline{v}_{m}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{m}),\overline{w}_{m}=(w_{0},\ldots,w_{m})\}\in Y_{m}^{(1)}
satisfy
w_{m}\in\left\{\overline{v}_{m}\right\}
and
v_{m}\notin\left\{\overline{w}_{m}\right\}
.
For an arbitrary vertex
x\in V
let us denote by

\displaystyle S_{m}(x)\coloneqq\left\{\overline{w}_{m}=(w_{0},\ldots w_{m})\in S_{m}\mid w_{m}=x\right\},

the set of sequentially spanning sequences of length
m
that end with vertex
x
.
Now observe that we can obtain every pair of sequences in
Y_{m}^{(1)}
by first choosing a sequence
\overline{v}_{m}\in S_{m}
, then fixing a vertex
x\in\left\{\overline{v}_{m}\right\}
and finally choosing a sequence
\overline{w}_{m}\in S_{m}(x)
that ends with vertex
x
. In particular, we obtain

(6.4)

\displaystyle|Y_{m}^{(1)}|\leq 2\sum_{\overline{v}_{m}\in S_{m}}\sum_{x\in\left\{\overline{v}_{m}\right\}}|S_{m}(x)|.

To bound
|S_{m}(x)|
from above, note that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{x\in G}S_{m}(x)=S_{m}.

Indeed, because any sequence
\overline{v}_{m}\in S_{m}(x)
is a sequentially spanning sequence of length
m
, it clearly holds that
\bigcup_{x\in G}S_{m}(x)\subseteq S_{m}
.
For the other inclusion, observe that for any sequence
\overline{v}_{m}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{m})\in S_{m}
it holds that
\overline{v}_{m}\in S_{m}(v_{m})
.
Now note that for different values of
x
, the sets
S_{m}(x)
are disjoint and furthermore, due to symmetry, the value
|S_{m}(x)|
does not depend on
x
, implying together that for all
x\in V
,

\displaystyle k^{n}\left|S_{m}(x)\right|=\sum_{x\in V}|S_{m}(x)|=|S_{m}|,

and in particular,

(6.5)

\displaystyle\left|S_{m}(x)\right|=\frac{\left|S_{m}\right|}{k^{n}}.

Plugging it into (
6.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle|Y_{m}^{(1)}|\overset{\eqref{eq:lem:bound:seqoverlaplastindex},\eqref{eq:univeraleq}}{\leq}2\sum_{\overline{v}_{m}\in S_{m}}\sum_{x\in\left\{\overline{v}_{m}\right\}}\frac{|S_{m}|}{k^{n}}=2|S_{m}|\cdot|\left\{\overline{v}_{m}\right\}|\cdot\frac{|S_{m}|}{k^{n}}=\frac{2(m+1)}{k^{n}}|S_{m}|^{2},

completing the proof of
(a)
.

To show
(b)
, note that we can count
Y_{m}^{(2)}
similarly as
Y_{m}^{(1)}
: We fix first a sequence
\overline{v}_{m-1}
and then a vertex
x
of
\overline{v}_{m-1}
with which
\overline{w}_{m}
should end. Next we fix a sequence
\overline{w}_{m}\in S_{m}(x)
and finally choose a vertex
y
from all the vertices in
\overline{w}_{m}
to extend
\overline{v}_{m-1}
. Independently of all these choice we have
\left|\left\{\overline{w}_{m}\right\}\right|=m+1
. Thus, from (
6.5
) and (
6.2
) with
\ell=m
, i.e.,
|S_{m-1}|=|S_{m}|/C_{m}
we obtain

\displaystyle|Y_{m}^{(2)}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\overline{v}_{m-1}\in S_{m-1}}\sum_{x\in\left\{\overline{v}_{m-1}\right\}}\sum_{\overline{w}_{m}\in S_{m}(x)}\left|\left\{\overline{w}_{m}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=|S_{m-1}|\cdot|\left\{\overline{v}_{m-1}\right\}|\cdot|S_{m}(x)|\cdot(m+1)

\displaystyle{=}|S_{m-1}|\cdot m(m+1)\cdot\frac{|S_{m}|}{k^{n}}

\displaystyle{=}\frac{m(m+1)}{C_{m}k^{n}}|S_{m}|^{2},

as required.
∎

Lemma

6.1
essentially tells us how many ways there are to start intersecting pairs of sequences.
We continue by counting the number of ways we can extend such pairs of sequences to sequences of length
\ell
.
For
m\in[n]
and
i\in[m+1]\cup\left\{0\right\}
, let
\{\overline{v}_{m}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{m}),\overline{w}_{m}=(w_{0},\ldots,w_{m})\}\subseteq S_{m}
be two sequentially spanning sequences satisfying
|\overline{v}_{m}\cap\overline{w}_{m}|=i
. For
\ell\geq m+1

let us denote by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}\left(\overline{v}_{m},\overline{w}_{m},\ell,i,s\right)}\coloneqq

(6.6)

\displaystyle\left\{\left\{\overline{v}_{\ell}^{\prime}=\left(v_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,v_{\ell}^{\prime}\right),\overline{w}_{\ell}^{\prime}=\left(w_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,w_{\ell}^{\prime}\right)\right\}\in Y_{\ell}(s+i)\mid\forall 0\leq j\leq m\colon\left(v_{j}=v_{j}^{\prime}\land w_{j}=w_{j}^{\prime}\right)\right\}

\{\overline{v}_{\ell}^{\prime},\overline{w}_{\ell}^{\prime}\}\subseteq S_{\ell}

.
As

\displaystyle E\left(m,i,s\right)\coloneqq\max_{\left\{\overline{v}_{m},\overline{w}_{m}\right\}\in Y_{m}(i)}\left|\mathcal{E}\left(\overline{v}_{m},\overline{w}_{m},\ell,i,s\right)\right|.

In the next lemma we provide bounds on

.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
m\in[n]
,
i\in[m+1]\cup\left\{0\right\}
and
s\in[\ell]\cup\left\{0\right\}
. For
1\leq j\leq\ell^{*}
set

\displaystyle D_{j}\coloneqq\mathbb{1}_{\left\{j=1\right\}}C_{1}+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{j>1\right\}}\max\left\{C_{j},3D_{j-1}\right\}.

Then,

(a)

C_{j}\leq D_{j}
;

(b)

,

where

(6.7)

\displaystyle\Psi\left(m,i,s\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s=0\right\}}+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s>0\right\}}\prod_{j=m+1}^{2m-i+1}\left(\frac{2m-i+2-j}{D_{j}}\right)^{2}\prod_{j=2m-i+2}^{s+i-1}D_{j}^{-1}&\text{ if }\ m-i+1<s/2\\
\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s=0\right\}}+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s>0\right\}}\prod_{j=m+1}^{m+\left\lfloor\frac{s}{2}\right\rfloor}\left(\frac{2m-i+2-j}{D_{j}}\right)^{2}\left(\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s\text{ even }\right\}}+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s\text{ odd }\right\}}\frac{2(m-\left\lfloor\frac{s}{2}\right\rfloor-i+1)+1}{D_{m+\left\lfloor\frac{s}{2}\right\rfloor+1}}\right)&\text{ else.}\end{cases}

Let us first remark that the functions

are only introduced to ensure that the ratio

is at least

for all values of

(which does not hold for the functions

).
To give some further intuition on the bounds stated in

6.2
, observe that the term
\prod_{j=m+1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}
is an upper bound on the number of extensions of a pair
\{\overline{v}_{m},\overline{w}_{m}\}
of sequences of length
m
to a pair
\{\overline{v}_{\ell}^{\prime},\overline{w}_{\ell}^{\prime}\}
of sequences of lenght
\ell
, in which the extended sequences do not have to overlap any further. In a sense, the function
\Psi\left(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right)
quantifies how much having overlap restricts a pair of extensions. In particular, in order to extend a sequence
\overline{v}_{m}
by a vertex that is also appearing in
\overline{w}_{m}
, the number of choices for this vertex are reduced from
C_{m+1}
to the number of vertices appearing in
\overline{w}_{m}
.

In order to prove
Lemma

6.2
, we will first show some general properties of the function
\Psi\left(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right)
.

Lemma 6.3
.

For any
(i,s)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
the following hold.

(a)

The function
\Psi\left(\cdot,i,s\right)
, as defined in (
6.7
), is decreasing in the first coordinate.

(b)

For all
m\geq i-1
and
s\geq 1

\displaystyle\sum_{j=m+1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{2(j+1-i)\Psi\left(j,i+1,s-1\right)}{D_{j}}+\frac{(j-i)^{2}\Psi\left(j,i+2,s-2\right)}{8{D_{j}}^{2}}\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s\geq 2\right\}}\right)\leq 8\Psi\left(m,i,s\right).

The proof of
Lemma

6.3
is simple but tedious and is therefore deferred to the
Appendix

B
. We proceed by showing
Lemma

6.2
.

Proof of
Lemma

6.2
.

Note first that
Lemma

lem:bound:seqoverlap:extensions
(a)
is a trivial observation.

Continuing with
Lemma

lem:bound:seqoverlap:extensions
(b)
we will prove the statement using induction on
s
, starting with the base case
s=0
. By the definition of

C_{j+1}:=\left|\left\{w\in V\mid\overline{v}_{j+1}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{j},w)\in S_{j+1}\right\}\right|,

there are at most
C_{j+1}
ways to extend a sequentially spanning sequence of length
j
. Thus, sequentially extending both sequences (and ignoring any potential overlaps) yields together with
(a)
the upper bound

\displaystyle E\left(m,i,0\right)\leq\prod_{j=m+1}^{\ell}C_{j}^{2}\leq\prod_{j=m+1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}=\left(\prod_{j=m+1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)\Psi\left(m,i,0\right),

proving the induction base.

We proceed with the induction hypothesis. Let
\left\{\overline{v}_{m},\overline{w}_{m}\right\}\in Y_{m}(i)
, let
s\in[\ell]
and assume that
Lemma

lem:bound:seqoverlap:extensions
(b)
holds for all
j\in[s-1]
.
For a fixed index
m+1\leq j^{*}\leq\ell
and
\tau\in\{1,2\}
let us consider the number
E_{j^{*}}^{(\tau)}\left(m,i,s\right)
of extensions
\{\overline{v}_{\ell}=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{\ell}),\overline{w}_{\ell}=(w_{0},\ldots,w_{\ell})\}\subseteq S_{\ell}
satisfying
|\overline{v}_{j^{*}-1}\cap\overline{w}_{j^{*}-1}|=i
and
|\overline{v}_{j^{*}}\cap\overline{w}_{j^{*}}|=i+\tau
, i.e.,
j^{*}
is the first index larger than
m
at which the extended sequences contain an additional vertex that is also contained in the other sequence up to this point.

We will compute
E_{j^{*}}^{(\tau)}\left(m,i,s\right)
in three steps: First, we will compute the number of possible extensions of
\overline{v}_{m}
and
\overline{w}_{m}
to sequences
\overline{v}_{j^{*}-1}
and
\overline{w}_{j^{*}-1}
, then count the number of ways to extend those to sequences
\overline{v}_{j^{*}}
and
\overline{w}_{j^{*}}
and finally extend those to sequences
\overline{v}_{\ell}
and
\overline{w}_{\ell}
.
Analogously to the induction base, there are at most
\prod_{j=m+1}^{j^{*}-1}C_{j}^{2}
ways to extend the sequences
\overline{v}_{m}
and
\overline{w}_{m}
to sequences
\overline{v}_{j^{*}-1}
and
\overline{w}_{j^{*}-1}
. To compute the number of ways how to further extend those sequences, we distinguish two cases.

Case 1:

|\overline{v}_{j^{*}}\cap\overline{w}_{j^{*}}|=i+1
.

By definition of
j^{*}
, one of the following has to hold in this case.

(1)

v_{j^{*}}\in\{\overline{w}_{j^{*}-1}\}
and
w_{j^{*}}\notin\{\overline{v}_{j^{*}}\}
;

(2)

w_{j^{*}}\in\{\overline{v}_{j^{*}-1}\}
and
v_{j^{*}}\notin\{\overline{w}_{j^{*}}\}
;

(3)

v_{j^{*}}=w_{j^{*}}
.

In the first subcase, there are at most
C_{j^{*}}
ways for picking
w_{j^{*}}
. Furthermore,
v_{j^{*}}
has to be one of the
j^{*}-i
vertices contained in
\left\{\overline{w}_{j^{*}-1}\right\}\setminus\left\{\overline{v}_{j^{*}-1}\right\}
. Thus, the number of extensions in this subcase can be bounded from above by
C_{j^{*}}(j^{*}-i)
.
The second subcase is symmetric to the first, so let us continue with the last subcase.
Clearly, if
v_{j^{*}}=w_{j^{*}}
then it follows that there are at most
C_{j^{*}}
ways to extend the sequences.
In total, we obtain that the number of ways to extend
\overline{v}_{j^{*}-1}
and
\overline{w}_{j^{*}-1}
to
\overline{v}_{j^{*}}
and
\overline{w}_{j^{*}}
is bounded from above by
C_{j^{*}}(2(j^{*}-i)+1)\leq 2C_{j^{*}}(j^{*}-i+1)
.

Case 2:

|\{\overline{v}_{j^{*}}\}\cap\{\overline{w}_{j^{*}}\}|=i+2
.

In this case it follows that
v_{j^{*}}\in\{\overline{w}_{j^{*}-1}\}
and
w_{j^{*}}\in\{\overline{v}_{j^{*}-1}\}
. Similar to the previous case,
v_{j^{*}}
has to be one of the
j^{*}-i
vertices contained in
\left\{\overline{w}_{j^{*}-1}\right\}\setminus\left\{\overline{v}_{j^{*}-1}\right\}
and analogously
w_{j^{*}}
has to be one of the
j^{*}-i
vertices contained in
\left\{\overline{v}_{j^{*}-1}\right\}\setminus\left\{\overline{w}_{j^{*}-1}\right\}
. Thus, there are at most
(j^{*}-i)^{2}
possibilities to extend.

It remains to bound the number of ways to extend the sequences
\overline{v}_{j^{*}}
and
\overline{w}_{j^{*}}
to sequences
\overline{v}_{\ell}
and
\overline{w}_{\ell}
. In the first case, this number is clearly given as
E\left(j^{*},i+1,s-1\right)
and in the second case as
E\left(j^{*},i+2,s-2\right)
. Putting everything together and using the induction hypothesis
Lemma

lem:bound:seqoverlap:extensions
(b)
we obtain

\displaystyle E_{j^{*}}^{(1)}\left(m,i,s\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\prod_{j=m+1}^{j^{*}-1}C_{j}^{2}\right)2C_{j^{*}}(j^{*}-i+1)E\left(j^{*},i+1,s-1\right)

(6.8)

\displaystyle{\leq}\left(\prod_{j=m+1}^{j^{*}-1}C_{j}^{2}\right)2C_{j^{*}}(j^{*}-i+1)8^{s-1}\left(\prod_{j=j^{*}+1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)\Psi\left(j^{*},i+1,s-1\right).

Using
Lemma

lem:bound:seqoverlap:extensions
(a)
in (
6.8
) we thus obtain

\displaystyle E_{j^{*}}^{(1)}\left(m,i,s\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}\left(\prod_{j=m+1}^{j^{*}-1}D_{j}^{2}\right)2D_{j^{*}}(j^{*}-i+1)8^{s-1}\left(\prod_{j=j^{*}+1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)\Psi\left(j^{*},i+1,s-1\right)

(6.9)

\displaystyle=8^{s-1}\left(\prod_{j=m+1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)\frac{2(j^{*}-i+1)\Psi\left(j^{*},i+1,s-1\right)}{D_{j^{*}}}.

Analogously to (
6.8
) and (
6.9
) we obtain for
E_{j^{*}}^{(2)}\left(m,i,s\right)
that

(6.10)

\displaystyle E_{j^{*}}^{(2)}\left(m,i,s\right)\leq 8^{s-2}\left(\prod_{j=m+1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)\frac{(j^{*}-i)\Psi\left(j^{*},i+2,s-2\right)}{D_{j^{*}}^{2}}.

Summing over all possible values of
j^{*}
, it follows from (
6.9
) and (
6.10
) that

\displaystyle E\left(m,i,s\right)\leq\sum_{j^{*}=m+1}^{\ell}E_{j^{*}}^{(1)}\left(m,i,s\right)+E_{j^{*}}^{(2)}\left(m,i,s\right)

(6.11)

\displaystyle\leq 8^{s-1}\left(\prod_{j=m+1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)\sum_{j^{*}=m+1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{2(j^{*}+1-i)\Psi\left(j^{*},i+1,s-1\right)}{D_{j^{*}}}+\frac{(j^{*}-i)^{2}\Psi\left(j^{*},i+2,s-2\right)}{8{D_{j^{*}}}^{2}}\right).

Finally, plugging the bounds from
Lemma

lem:bound:auxfunc
(b)
into (
6.11
) we obtain

\displaystyle E\left(m,i,s\right)\leq 8^{s}\left(\prod_{j=m+1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)\Psi\left(m,i,s\right),

completing the proof.
∎

We are now ready to proof
Lemma

4.3
.

Proof of
Lemma

4.3
.

Recall
\ell^{*}:=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
and let
\ell\in[\ell^{*}]\cup\{0\}
and
i\in[\ell+1]
. Splitting the set
Y_{\ell}(i)\coloneqq\left\{\{\overline{v}_{\ell},\overline{w}_{\ell}\}\subseteq S_{\ell}\mid\left|\overline{v}_{\ell}\cap\overline{w}_{\ell}\right|=i\right\}
into parts, depending on the first index
m
at which a pair
\left\{\overline{v}_{\ell},\overline{w}_{\ell}\right\}
of sequentially spanning sequences has non-empty overlap, we obtain

\displaystyle|Y_{\ell}(i)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}\sum_{\{\overline{v}_{m},\overline{w}_{m}\}\in Y_{m}^{(1)}}E\left(m,1,i-1\right)+\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}\sum_{\{\overline{v}_{m},\overline{w}_{m}\}\in Y_{m}^{(2)}}E\left(m,2,i-2\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}\left|Y_{m}^{(1)}\right|E\left(m,1,i-1\right)+\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}\left|Y_{m}^{(2)}\right|E\left(m,2,i-2\right).

Together with the bounds on from
Lemma

6.1
and
Lemma

lem:bound:seqoverlap:extensions
(b)
it follows that

\displaystyle|Y_{\ell}(i)|

\displaystyle{\leq}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}\frac{2(m+1)|S_{m}|^{2}}{k^{n}}E\left(m,1,i-1\right)+\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}\frac{m(m+1)|S_{m}|^{2}}{C_{m}k^{n}}E\left(m,2,i-2\right).

\displaystyle{\leq}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}\frac{2(m+1)|S_{m}|^{2}}{k^{n}}8^{i-1}\left(\prod_{j=m+1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)\Psi\left(m,1,i-1\right)

(6.12)

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}\frac{m(m+1)|S_{m}|^{2}}{C_{m}k^{n}}8^{i-2}\left(\prod_{j=m+1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)\Psi\left(m,2,i-2\right).

Now recall that by
Lemma

4.2
it holds that
\left|S_{m}\right|=k^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}
. In particular, it follows from
Lemma

lem:bound:seqoverlap:extensions
(a)
that

(6.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\left|S_{m}\right|^{2}}{k^{n}}\leq k^{n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}D_{j}^{2}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\left|S_{m}\right|^{2}}{k^{n}C_{m}}\leq\frac{k^{n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}D_{j}^{2}\right)}{D_{m}}.

Plugging (
6.13
) into (
6.12
) and extracting common factors from both sums we get

\displaystyle|Y_{\ell}(i)|

\displaystyle\leq 8^{i-1}k^{n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}2(m+1)\Psi\left(m,1,i-1\right)+\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}\frac{m(m+1)}{8D_{m}}\Psi\left(m,2,i-2\right)\right)

(6.14)

\displaystyle\leq 8^{i-1}k^{n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)\left(\ell+1\right)^{2}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}2\Psi\left(m,1,i-1\right)+\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}\frac{\Psi\left(m,2,i-2\right)}{8D_{m}}\right).

By
Lemma

lem:bound:auxfunc
(a)
, the function
\Psi\left(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right)
is decreasing in the first coordinate and furthermore, the function
m\mapsto 1/D_{m}
is decreasing in
m\in[\ell]
, and thus we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}2\Psi\left(m,1,i-1\right)+\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}\frac{\Psi\left(m,2,i-2\right)}{8D_{m}}\right)\leq\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}2\Psi\left(0,1,i-1\right)+\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}\frac{\Psi\left(1,2,i-2\right)}{8D_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2(\ell+1)\left(\Psi\left(0,1,i-1\right)+\frac{\Psi\left(1,2,i-2\right)}{D_{1}}\right)=2(\ell+1)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}D_{j}^{-1}+D_{1}^{-1}\prod_{j=2}^{i-1}D_{j}^{-1}\right)

(6.15)

\displaystyle=4(\ell+1)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}D_{j}^{-1}\right),

where the penultimate equality is due to the definition (
6.7
) of
\Psi\left(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right)
.
Combining (
6.14
) and (
6.15
) we obtain

\displaystyle|Y_{\ell}(i)|

\displaystyle{\leq}8^{i-1}k^{n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)\left(\ell+1\right)^{2}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}\Psi\left(m,1,i-1\right)+\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}\frac{\Psi\left(m,2,i-2\right)}{8D_{m}}\right)

(6.16)

\displaystyle{\leq}8^{i-1}k^{n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)\left(\ell+1\right)^{2}4(\ell+1)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}D_{j}^{-1}\right)\leq\frac{8^{i}k^{n}(\ell+1)^{3}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)}{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}D_{j}\right)}.

To complete the proof, we need to replace the auxiliary functions
D_{j}
with the functions
C_{j}
. To that end, observe that by
Lemma

4.2
it holds for all
1\leq j\leq\ell^{*}-7
that

\displaystyle\frac{C_{j+1}}{C_{j}}=\frac{\binom{n-2j}{2}k^{2}}{\binom{n-2j+2}{2}}\geq 3,

and thus in particular that

C_{j}=D_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq\ell^{*}-6
.
By the construction of
D_{j}
it now follows that

\displaystyle D_{\ell^{*}-5}\leq 3D_{\ell^{*}-6}=3C_{\ell^{*}-6}\leq 3C_{\ell^{*}-5}.

Repeating this step we obtain for
1\leq j\leq 6
that

\displaystyle D_{\ell^{*}-6+j}\leq 3^{j}C_{\ell^{*}-6+j},

and thus

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell^{*}}D_{j}^{2}\right)=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell^{*}-6}C_{j}^{2}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{6}D_{\ell^{*}-6+j}^{2}\right)\leq\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell^{*}}C_{j}^{2}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{6}3^{2j}\right)=3^{42}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell^{*}}C_{j}^{2}\right).

Clearly, this extends to arbitrary values of
1\leq\ell\leq\ell^{*}
, i.e.,

(6.17)

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)\leq 3^{42}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}C_{j}^{2}\right).

Combining (
6.16
) and (
6.17
) we obtain

\displaystyle|Y_{\ell}(i)|\overset{\eqref{eq:prf:boundseqoverlap:5}}{\leq}\frac{8^{i}k^{n}(\ell+1)^{3}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}D_{j}^{2}\right)}{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}D_{j}\right)}\overset{\eqref{eq:Dj}}{\leq}\Theta(1)\frac{8^{i}k^{n}(\ell+1)^{3}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}C_{j}^{2}\right)}{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}C_{j}\right)}=\Theta(1)\frac{8^{i}(\ell+1)^{3}|S_{\ell}|^{2}}{|S_{i-1}|},

where the last equality uses (
4.1
).
∎

We continue by showing that the expectation of
X_{i}
tends to infinity for all
0\leq i\leq\ell^{*}.

Proof of
Lemma

4.4
.

Let
i\in[\ell^{*}]\cup\{0\}
. We start by bounding
\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i}\right]
from below. By (
4.3
) and
Lemma

4.2
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i}\right]\overset{\eqref{eq:expec(seqspannum)}}{=}|S_{i}|p^{i+1}\overset{\eqref{eq:Sell}}{=}\frac{n!}{(n-2i)!}(k-1)^{2i}2^{-i}k^{n+i^{2}-i}p^{i+1}

(6.18)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n!}{(n-2i)!}(k-1)^{2i}2^{-i}k^{n+i^{2}-i}\left(200n^{-2}k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1}\right)^{i+1},

where the last inequality uses
p\geq p^{*}:=200n^{-2}k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1}
.
To further bound this expression we will use the following estimates:

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{(n-2i)!}

\displaystyle=n^{2i}\prod_{j=1}^{i}\left(1-\frac{j}{n}\right)\geq n^{2i}\exp\left(-\frac{2i^{2}}{n}\right)\geq n^{2i}e^{-i};

\displaystyle(k-1)^{2i}

\displaystyle=k^{2i}\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)^{2i}\geq k^{2i}2^{-2i}.

Plugging these into (
6.18
) and simplifying we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i}\right]\geq n^{2i}e^{-i}k^{2i}2^{-2i}2^{-i}k^{n+i^{2}-i}\left(200n^{-2}k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1}\right)^{i+1}=200n^{-2}\left(\frac{200}{8e}\right)k^{i^{2}-i(2\sqrt{n}-2)+n-2\sqrt{n}+1}.

It thus follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i}\right]}{\Theta\left(n^{4}\right)8^{i+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\Theta(n^{-6})\left(\frac{200}{64e}\right)^{i}k^{i^{2}-i(2\sqrt{n}-2)+n-2\sqrt{n}+1}

(6.19)

\displaystyle\geq\Theta\left(n^{-6}\right)\left(\frac{10}{9}\right)^{i}k^{i^{2}-i(2\sqrt{n}-2)+n-2\sqrt{n}+1}.

To derive a lower bound on (
6.19
) we focus on the the function
\hat{g}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R},x\mapsto x^{2}-x(2\sqrt{n}-2)+n-2\sqrt{n}+1
in the exponent. Observing that the function
\hat{g}
defines a convex parabola centered at
\sqrt{n}-1
, it takes its minimum value of
0
at the minimiser
x=\sqrt{n}-1
.
Thus, we obtain that for all
i\in[\ell^{*}]\cup\{0\}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i}\right]}{\Theta(n^{4})8^{i+1}}\geq\Theta\left(n^{-6}\right)\left(\frac{10}{9}\right)^{\sqrt{n}-1}=\omega\left(1\right),

completing the proof.
∎

Finally we show that in the case when
n
is odd, say
n=2m+1
for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we can always find an infected vertex to extend an infected sequentially spanning sequence of length
m
.

Proof of
Lemma

4.7
.

Let
n=2m+1
for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. For an arbitrary projection
H
of dimension
2m
, the number of vertices in
G
that are not contained in
H
is clearly given as

(k-1)k^{2m}
. Thus, using
p\geq p^{*}:=200n^{-2}k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1}
, the probability that none of these vertices were initially infected, can be bounded from above by

\displaystyle(1-p)^{(k-1)k^{2m}}\leq\exp\left(-pk^{2m}\right)\leq\exp\left(-k^{\frac{n}{2}}\right).

Now observe that there are
nk
projections of dimension
2m
in
G
. Indeed, there are
n
ways for choosing the single coordinate in which a projection of dimension
2m
is not complete and
k
further possibilities for choosing a vertex of the base graph
K_{k}
in this coordinate. Thus it follows by a union bound that the probability that there exists a projection
H
of dimension
2m
such that no vertex in
G\setminus H
was initially infected can be bounded from above by

\displaystyle nk\exp\left(-k^{\frac{n}{2}}\right)=o(1).

∎

7.
Concluding Discussion

In
Theorem

1.1
we determined the asymptotic value of the critical probability up to multiplicative constants:

(7.1)

\displaystyle p_{c}\left(\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k},2\right)=\Theta\left(n^{-2}k^{-2\sqrt{n}+1}\right).

The corresponding result for the case
k=2
was proved in the earlier work of Balogh and Bollobás
[
5
]
which was improved by Balogh, Bollobás and Morris
[
7
]
:

(7.2)

\displaystyle\frac{16\lambda}{n^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}\right)2^{-2\sqrt{n}}\leq p_{c}(\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{2},2)\leq\frac{16\lambda}{n^{2}}\left(1+\frac{5\log^{2}n}{\sqrt{n}}\right)2^{-2\sqrt{n}},

where
\lambda\approx 1.166
.
In order to improve our result, e.g., to obtain a sharp threshold, proof techniques in this paper might be useful. One of the key ingredients in our proof is the bound on the probability of a projection of dimension
2\ell
being
internally spanned
(see
Lemma

3.2
): This bound is off precisely by factor
\lambda^{\ell}
, in comparison to the corresponding bound to obtain (
7.2
). Getting more precise asymptotics on the probability of a projection being internally spanned is the most crucial step to obtain a sharp threshold.

Problem 7.1
.

Determine a sharp threshold for
p_{c}\left(\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k},2\right)
for any
k\geq 3
.

The Hamming graph is a very natural example of a Cartesian product graph and has quite useful properties, e.g., it is regular and vertex-transitive. We believe the proof techniques in this paper are also applicable to similar types of graphs and especially to Cartesian product graphs with different kinds of base graphs. An interesting problem in this context is to determine a set of minimal properties the base graphs have to satisfy in order to obtain a threshold in the flavour of
Theorem

1.1
.

Problem 7.2
.

Determine
p_{c}\left(G,2\right)
for other types of Cartesian product graphs
G
.

Lastly, we note that throughout this paper we only considered the infection parameter
r=2
. For
r\geq 3
, the problem of determining the critical probability
p_{c}\left(\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k},r\right)
is widely open even for the case
k=2
and
r=3
.

Problem 7.3
.

Determine
p_{c}\left(\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{k},3\right)
for arbitrary
k\geq 2
.

For the special case when
r=\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil
and
k=2
, i.e., in the so-called
majority bootstrap percolation
on the
n
-dimensional hypercube
Q^{n}=[2]^{n}=\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{2}
, a sharp threshold was established by Balogh, Bollobás and Morris in
[
9
]
and their results have been extended to Cartesian products of bounded size regular graphs by Collares, Erde, Geisler and Kang
[
20
]
. For arbitrary
r\geq 3
, Morrison and Noel answered the extremal question of determining a smallest percolating set in
Q^{n}
in
[
32
]
; regarding the critical probability, Balogh, Bollobás and Morris
[
7
, Conjecture 6.3]
conjectured the following in 2010, but there is still no progress:

\displaystyle p_{c}\left(\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{2},r\right)=\exp\left(-\Theta\left(n^{1/2^{r-1}}\right)\right).

The sharp threshold (
7.2
) suggests that the base of the exponential function in the above conjecture would be two and thus the conjecture could be reformulated as

\displaystyle-\log_{2}p_{c}\left(\square_{i=1}^{n}K_{2},r\right)=\Theta\left(n^{1/2^{r-1}}\right).

In view of our threshold result (
7.1
) we make an analogous conjecture:

Conjecture 7.4
.

For arbitrary
r\geq 3
and
k\geq 2
,

In this case we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\Psi\left(m+1,i+1,s-1\right)}{\Psi\left(m,i,s\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{j=m+2}^{m+1+\left\lfloor\frac{s-1}{2}\right\rfloor}\left(\frac{2m-i+3-j}{D_{j}}\right)^{2}\left(\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s-1\text{ even}\right\}}+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s-1\text{ odd}\right\}}\frac{2(m-\left\lfloor\frac{s-1}{2}\right\rfloor-i+1)+1}{D_{m+1+\left\lfloor\frac{s-1}{2}\right\rfloor+1}}\right)}{\prod_{j=m+1}^{m+\left\lfloor\frac{s}{2}\right\rfloor}\left(\frac{2m-i+2-j}{D_{j}}\right)^{2}\left(\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s\text{ even}\right\}}+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s\text{ odd}\right\}}\frac{2(m-\left\lfloor\frac{s}{2}\right\rfloor-i+1)+1}{D_{m+\left\lfloor\frac{s}{2}\right\rfloor+1}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{j=m+2}^{m+1+\frac{s-1}{2}}\left(\frac{2m-i+3-j}{D_{j}}\right)^{2}}{\prod_{j=m+1}^{m+\frac{s-1}{2}}\left(\frac{2m-i+2-j}{D_{j}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{2(m-\frac{s-1}{2}-i+1)+1}{D_{m+\frac{s-1}{2}+1}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{D_{m+1}^{2}}{\left(2(m-i+2)-s\right)D_{m+1+\frac{s-1}{2}}}

\displaystyle{\leq}\frac{D_{m+1}}{(m-i+2)}.

This completes the proof of (
B.2
).

We will show (
B.3
) in a similar manner.
Observe first that by (
B.1
) and the fact that
\Psi\left(\cdot,i,s\right)
is decreasing in the first coordinate, it follows that for all
j\geq i+1

\displaystyle\frac{8(j+1-i)^{2}\Psi\left(j+1,i+2,s-2\right){D_{j}}^{2}}{8(j-i)^{2}\Psi\left(j,i+2,s-2\right){D_{j+1}}^{2}}\leq\frac{(j+1-i)^{2}}{9(j-1)^{2}}\leq\frac{4}{9}\leq\frac{1}{2},

implying

\displaystyle\sum_{j=m+1}^{\ell}\frac{(j-i)^{2}\Psi\left(j,i+2,s-2\right)}{8{D_{j}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\mathbb{1}_{\left\{m+1>i\right\}}\frac{(m-i+1)^{2}\Psi\left(m+1,i+2,s-2\right)}{8D_{m+1}^{2}}+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{m+1=i\right\}}\frac{\Psi\left(m+2,i+2,s-2\right)}{8D_{m+2}^{2}}\right).

Now, if
m+1=i
, it immediately follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\Psi\left(m+2,i+2,s-2\right)}{\Psi\left(m,i,s\right)}=\frac{\prod_{j=m+3}^{2m-i+3}\left(\frac{2m-i+4-j}{D_{j}}\right)^{2}\prod_{j=2m-i+4}^{s+i-1}D_{j}^{-1}}{\prod_{j=m+1}^{2m-i+1}\left(\frac{2m-i+2-j}{D_{j}}\right)^{2}\prod_{j=2m-i+2}^{s+i-1}D_{j}^{-1}}=\frac{\prod_{j=m+3}^{s+i-1}D_{j}^{-1}}{\prod_{j=m+1}^{s+i-1}D_{j}^{-1}}=D_{m+1}D_{m+2}\leq 8D_{m+2}^{2}.

If
m+1>i
, we will again show by a case distinction that

\displaystyle\frac{\Psi\left(m+1,i+2,s-2\right)}{\Psi\left(m,i,s\right)}\leq\frac{D_{m+1}^{2}}{(m-i+1)^{2}},

which then implies (
B.3
).
First, observe that

2(m-i+1)<s
is equivalent to
2((m+1)-(i+2)+1)<s-2
.

Case 1:

2(m-i+1)<s
.

It follows from the definition of
\Psi
that

\displaystyle\frac{\Psi\left(m+1,i+2,s-2\right)}{\Psi\left(m,i,s\right)}=\frac{\prod_{j=m+2}^{2m-i+1}\left(\frac{2m-i+2-j}{D_{j}}\right)^{2}\prod_{j=2m-i+2}^{s+i-1}D_{j}^{-1}}{\prod_{j=m+1}^{2m-i+1}\left(\frac{2m-i+2-j}{D_{j}}\right)^{2}\prod_{j=2m-i+2}^{s+i-1}D_{j}^{-1}}=\frac{D_{m+1}^{2}}{(m-i+1)^{2}}.

Case 2:

2(m-i+1)\geq s
.

Similarly to the previous case, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\Psi\left(m+1,i+2,s-2\right)}{\Psi\left(m,i,s\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{j=m+2}^{m+1+\left\lfloor\frac{s-2}{2}\right\rfloor}\left(\frac{2m-i+2-j}{D_{j}}\right)^{2}\left(\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s-2\text{ even}\right\}}+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s-2\text{ odd}\right\}}\frac{2(m-\left\lfloor\frac{s-2}{2}\right\rfloor-i)+1}{D_{m+\left\lfloor\frac{s-2}{2}\right\rfloor+2}}\right)}{\prod_{j=m+1}^{m+\left\lfloor\frac{s}{2}\right\rfloor}\left(\frac{2m-i+2-j}{D_{j}}\right)^{2}\left(\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s\text{ even}\right\}}+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s\text{ odd}\right\}}\frac{2(m-\left\lfloor\frac{s}{2}\right\rfloor-i+1)+1}{D_{m+\left\lfloor\frac{s}{2}\right\rfloor+1}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{D_{m+1}^{2}}{(m-i+1)^{2}}\left(\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s\text{ even}\right\}}+\mathbb{1}_{\left\{s\text{ odd}\right\}}\frac{\left(2(m-\left\lfloor\frac{s-2}{2}\right\rfloor-i)+1\right)D_{m+\left\lfloor\frac{s}{2}\right\rfloor+1}}{\left(2(m-\left\lfloor\frac{s}{2}\right\rfloor-i+1)+1\right)D_{m+\left\lfloor\frac{s-2}{2}\right\rfloor+2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D_{m+1}^{2}}{(m-i+1)^{2}}.

This completes the proof of (
B.3
).

It follows from (
B.2
) and (
B.3
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=m+1}^{\ell}\frac{2(j+1-i)\Psi\left(j,i+1,s-1\right)}{D_{j}}+\frac{(j-i)^{2}\Psi\left(j,i+2,s-2\right)}{8{D_{j}}^{2}}\leq 8\Psi\left(m,i,s\right),

as claimed.
∎

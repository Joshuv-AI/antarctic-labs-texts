Linearized Learning Methods with Multiscale Deep Neural Networks for Stationary Navier-Stokes Equations with Oscillatory Solutions
Lizuo Liu, Bo Wang, Wei Cai

Abstract
Summary In this paper, we present linearized learning methods to accelerate the convergence of training for stationary nonlinear Navier-Stokes equations. To solve the stationary nonlinear Navier-Stokes (NS) equation, we integrate the procedure of linearization of the nonlinear convection term in the NS equation into the training process of multi-scale deep neural network approximation of the NS solution. Four forms of linearizations are considered. After a benchmark problem, we solve the highly oscillating stationary flows utilizing the proposed linearized learning with multi-scale neural network for complex domains. The results show that multiscale deep neural network combining with the linearized schemes can be trained fast and accurately.

Introduction

Deep neural network (DNN) machine learning has been studied as alternative numerical methods for solving partial differential equations arising from fluid dynamics
[
1
]
and wave propagations
[
2
]
, which has the potential of a flexible meshless method to solve governing equations from fluid and solid mechanics applications in complex geometries as an alternative method to traditional finite element method. Moreover, it has shown much power in handling high dimensional parabolic PDEs
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
. Recent work on DNNs have shown that they have a frequency dependence performance in learning solution of PDEs and fitting of functions. Namely, the lower frequency components of the solution are learned first and quickly compared with the higher frequency components
[
6
]
. Several attempts have been made to remove such a frequency bias for the DNN performances. One of the ideas is to convert the higher frequency content of the solution to a lower frequency range so the conventional DNN can learn the solution quickly. One way to achieve this goal is to use a phase shift
[
2
]
while the other way is to introduce a multiscale structure of the DNN
[
7
]
where neural networks with different scales will target different ranges of the frequency in the solutions. The PhaseDNN has been shown to be very effect for high frequency wave propagations while the MscaleDNN has been used to learn highly oscillatory Stokes flow solutions in complex domains as well as high dimensional PDEs
[
5
]
.

Additional difficulties arise when if there are nonlinearity in the PDEs. Based on the results from Jin et al.
[
8
]
, it is known that it might cost
\text{O}\left(10^{4}\right)
epochs to solve a benchmark problem, which is ineffective and impractical especially when highly oscillating problems are to be considered. Also, it is found that the MscaleDNN applied to the nonlinear Navier-Stokes equation did not produce the dramatic improvement over convential DNNs as in the case of the linear Stoke equations
[
1
]
.

The learning of the solution of linear PDEs via least square residual of the PDEs is in some sense equivalent to a fitting problems in the frequency domain from the Parsevel equality of the Fourier transform. So it is understandable to see a multiscale DNN’s performance improvement for a fitting problem also holds for the solution of linear PDEs. Considering the MscaleDNN’s good performance for the linear Stokes problem, we will develop in this paper a linearized learning procedure for the Navier-Stokes equations by integrating a linearization of the Navier-Stokes equation in the loss function of the MscaleDNN and dynamically updating the linearization as the learning is being carried out. Numerical results have demonstrated the convergence of this approach, which has produced highly accurate approximation to oscillatory solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations.

The rest of the paper will be organized as follows. Section
3
will introduce the multiscale DNN structure and then four linearized learning for the Navier-Stokes equation will be proposed in Section
3.2
. Numerical tests of the linearized learning schemes will be conducted for a 2-D oscillatory flows in a domain containing one or multiple random cylinder(s) in Section
4
. Finally, conclusion and future work will be discussed in Section
5
.

Linearized iterative method for stationary Navier–Stokes equation

Consider the following stationary Navier-Stokes equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla)\bm{u}-\nu\Delta\bm{u}+\nabla p=\bm{f}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\bm{u}=\bm{g}&\text{ on }\Gamma_{D},\\
\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
\Omega
is an open bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d},d=2,3

To solve the Stationary Navier-Stokes equation in a least square approach, intuitively, the PDEs (
1

[
1
]
. A velocity-gradient of velocity-pressure formulation will be used for the Navier Stokes equations.

\displaystyle-\nu\nabla\cdot\underline{\mathbf{U}}+\underline{\mathbf{U}}\cdot\mathbf{u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega

(2a)

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{U}}-(\nabla\mathbf{u})^{\top}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega

(2b)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega

(2c)

To obtain the governing equation for the pressure, the Navier Stokes equation is reformulated to Poisson equation with respect to pressure by taking divergence on both sides of equation
\left(\ref{eq_1}\right)
and applying equation
\left(\ref{eq_3}\right)
,

\Delta p+2(-u_{x}v_{y}+u_{y}v_{x})=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{f}.

(3)

Then, the loss function can be defined as

\displaystyle L_{VgVP}\left(\theta_{u},\theta_{p},\theta_{\underline{\mathbf{U}}}\right):=

\displaystyle\|\nu\nabla\cdot{\underline{\mathbf{U}}}-\underline{\mathbf{U}}\cdot\bm{u}-\nabla p+\bm{f}\|_{\Omega}^{2}

(4)

\displaystyle+\alpha\|\Delta p+2(-u_{x}v_{y}+u_{y}v_{x})-\nabla\cdot\bm{f}\|_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla\bm{u}-\underline{\mathbf{U}}\|_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\|_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle+\beta\|\bm{u}-\bm{g}\|_{\partial\Omega}^{2}.

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are the penalty terms.
On the contrary, unlike the results in the paper
[

\left(\ref{lsNS}\right)

In the paper
[

]
, three iterative methods were studied for solving the stationary Navier-Stokes equations. Assume
u_{h}^{n}
be the solution at n-th step of the iteration, three iterative schemes are given as follows.

•

Iterative method I

a\left(u_{h}^{n},v_{h}\right)-d\left(v_{h},p_{h}^{n}\right)+d\left(u_{h}^{n},q_{h}\right)+b\left(u_{h}^{n-1},u_{h}^{n-1},v_{h}\right)=\left(f,v_{h}\right),

•

Iterative method II

\begin{array}[]{l}a\left(u_{h}^{n},v_{h}\right)-d\left(v_{h},p_{h}^{n}\right)+d\left(u_{h}^{n},q_{h}\right)+b\left(u_{h}^{n},u_{h}^{n-1},v_{h}\right)\\

(6)

•

Iterative method III

a\left(u_{h}^{n},v_{h}\right)-d\left(v_{h},p_{h}^{n}\right)+d\left(u_{h}^{n},q_{h}\right)+b\left(u_{h}^{n-1},u_{h}^{n},v_{h}\right)=\left(f,v_{h}\right),

where

\displaystyle\nu\left\|u-u_{h}^{m}\right\|_{0}\leqslant Ch^{2}+Cv\left\|u_{h}^{m}-u_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{0},

\displaystyle\nu\left\|\nabla\left(u-u_{h}^{m}\right)\right\|_{0}+\left\|p-p_{h}^{m}\right\|_{0}\leqslant Ch+

Linearized Learning algorithm with Multiscale Deep Neural Network

3.1
Multiscale deep neural network

In order to improve the capability of the DNN to represent functions with multiple scales, we will apply the MscaleDNN
[
7
]
, which consists of a series of parallel normal sub-neural networks. Each of the sub-networks will receive a scaled version of the input and their outputs will then be combined to make the final output of the MscaleDNN (refer to Fig.
1
). The individual sub-network in the MscaleDNN with a scaled input is designed to approximate a segment of frequency content of the targeted function and the effect of the scaling is to convert a specific high frequency content to a lower frequency range so the learning can be accomplished much quickly. Due to the radial scaling used in the MscaleDNN, it is specially fitting for approximation of high dimensional PDEs.

MscaleDNN.

Fig.

n
networks.
Each scaled input passing through a sub-network of
L

(17)

where
W^{[1]}\cdots W^{[L-1]}

b^{[1]}\cdots b^{[L-1]}
are the weight
matrices and bias unknowns, respectively, to be optimized via the training,

\sigma(x)=\sin(x).

(18)

For the input scales, we could select the scale for the

3.2
Four Linearization Schemes

To speed up the convergence of the training, we propose an iterative solution procedure for the stationary Navier Stokes equation so that each step we train a linearized version of the Navier-Stokes equation
[
9
]
.

Assume the current learned velocities are
\left(u^{tmp},v^{tmp}\right)
and the new velocities to be learnt are denoted as
\left(u,v\right)
, then we can linearize the Navier-Stokes equation in the following four schemes,

•

Scheme 1:
GradFixed: Gradients of velocities in the nonlinear term will be fixed during training and updated after some specific epochs.

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta u+u^{tmp}_{x}u+u^{tmp}_{y}v+p_{x}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}_{1},

(20a)

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta v+v^{tmp}_{x}u+v^{tmp}_{y}v+p_{y}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}_{2},

(20b)

\displaystyle\Delta p+2(-u_{x}v_{y}+u_{y}v_{x})

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{f}.

(20c)

•

Scheme 2:
VFixed: Velocities in the nonlinear term are fixed during training and updated after every specific epochs.

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta u+u_{x}u^{tmp}+u_{y}v^{tmp}+p_{x}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}_{1},

(21a)

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta v+v_{x}u^{tmp}+v_{y}v^{tmp}+p_{y}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}_{2},

(21b)

\displaystyle\Delta p+2(-u_{x}v_{y}+u_{y}v_{x})

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{f}.

(21c)

•

Scheme 3:
VFixed1: This is the strong form of the Iterative method II, which can be seen as A correction of VFixed.

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\nu\Delta u+(u_{x}u^{tmp}+u_{y}v^{tmp}&+u^{tmp}_{x}u+u^{tmp}_{y}v)+p_{x}\\
&=\mathbf{f}_{1}+u^{tmp}_{x}u^{tmp}+u^{tmp}_{y}v^{tmp},\end{split}

(22a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\nu\Delta v+(v_{x}u^{tmp}+v_{y}v^{tmp}&+v^{tmp}_{x}u+v^{tmp}_{y}v)+p_{y}\\
&=\mathbf{f}_{2}+v^{tmp}_{x}u^{tmp}+v^{tmp}_{y}v^{tmp}.\end{split}

(22b)

•

Scheme 4:
Hybrid: The nonlinear term is replaced by the averages of the above two schemes.

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta u+\frac{1}{2}(u_{x}u^{tmp}+u_{y}v^{tmp}+u^{tmp}_{x}u+u^{tmp}_{y}v)+p_{x}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}_{1},

(23a)

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta v+\frac{1}{2}(v_{x}u^{tmp}+v_{y}v^{tmp}+v^{tmp}_{x}u+v^{tmp}_{y}v)+p_{y}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}_{2},

(23b)

\displaystyle\Delta p+2(-u_{x}v_{y}+u_{y}v_{x})

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{f}.

(23c)

It should be noted that the number of epochs of training a given linearized scheme before updating the fixed term will be a hyperparameter to be adjusted carefully.

Based on equations (
20
),(
21
),(
23
), we can design four loss functions as follows.

Loss function for Scheme 1:

\displaystyle\text{L}

\displaystyle{}_{\nabla}=\text{R}_{u}+\text{R}_{v}+\alpha\text{B}_{\bm{u}}+\beta\text{R}_{p},

(24)

\displaystyle\text{R}_{u}=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\nu\Delta{u}+{u}^{tmp}_{x}{u}+u^{tmp}_{y}v+p_{x}-\mathbf{f}_{1}\right)^{2}dxdy,

\displaystyle\text{R}_{v}=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\nu\Delta v+v^{tmp}_{x}u+v^{tmp}_{y}v+p_{y}-\mathbf{f}_{2}\right)^{2}dxdy,

\displaystyle\text{R}_{p}=\int_{\Omega}\left(\Delta p+2(-u_{x}v_{y}+u_{y}v_{x})-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{f}\right)^{2}dxdy,

\displaystyle\text{B}_{\bm{u}}=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{0}\right)^{2}dS.

Loss function for Scheme 2:

\displaystyle\text{L}

\displaystyle{}_{\bm{u}}=\text{R}_{u}+\text{R}_{v}+\alpha\text{B}_{\bm{u}}+\beta\text{R}_{p},

(25)

\displaystyle\text{R}_{u}=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\nu\Delta u+u_{x}u^{tmp}+u_{y}v^{tmp}+p_{x}-\mathbf{f}_{1}\right)^{2}dxdy,

\displaystyle\text{R}_{v}=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\nu\Delta v+v_{x}u^{tmp}+v_{y}v^{tmp}+p_{y}-\mathbf{f}_{2}\right)^{2}dxdy,

\displaystyle\text{R}_{p}=\int_{\Omega}\left(\Delta p+2(-u_{x}v_{y}+u_{y}v_{x})-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{f}\right)^{2}dxdy,

\displaystyle\text{B}_{\bm{u}}=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{0}\right)^{2}dS.

Loss function for Scheme 3:

\displaystyle\text{L}

\displaystyle{}_{H}=\text{R}_{u}+\text{R}_{v}+\alpha\text{B}_{\bm{u}}+\beta\text{R}_{p},

(26)

\displaystyle\text{R}_{u}=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\nu\Delta u+(u_{x}u^{tmp}+u_{y}v^{tmp}+u^{tmp}_{x}u+u^{tmp}_{y}v-u^{tmp}_{x}u^{tmp}-u^{tmp}_{y}v^{tmp})+p_{x}-\mathbf{f}_{1}\right)^{2}dxdy,

\displaystyle\text{R}_{v}=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\nu\Delta v+(v_{x}u^{tmp}+v_{y}v^{tmp}+v^{tmp}_{x}u+v^{tmp}_{y}v-v^{tmp}_{x}u^{tmp}-v^{tmp}_{y}v^{tmp})+p_{y}-\mathbf{f}_{2}\right)^{2}dxdy,

\displaystyle\text{R}_{p}=\int_{\Omega}\left(\Delta p+2(-u_{x}v_{y}+u_{y}v_{x})-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{f}\right)^{2}dxdy,

\displaystyle\text{B}_{\bm{u}}=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{0}\right)^{2}dS.

Loss function for Scheme 4:

\displaystyle\text{L}

\displaystyle{}_{H}=\text{R}_{u}+\text{R}_{v}+\alpha\text{B}_{\bm{u}}+\beta\text{R}_{p},

(27)

\displaystyle\text{R}_{u}=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\nu\Delta u+\frac{1}{2}(u_{x}u^{tmp}+u_{y}v^{tmp}+u^{tmp}_{x}u+u^{tmp}_{y}v)+p_{x}-\mathbf{f}_{1}\right)^{2}dxdy,

\displaystyle\text{R}_{v}=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\nu\Delta v+\frac{1}{2}(v_{x}u^{tmp}+v_{y}v^{tmp}+v^{tmp}_{x}u+v^{tmp}_{y}v)+p_{y}-\mathbf{f}_{2}\right)^{2}dxdy,

\displaystyle\text{R}_{p}=\int_{\Omega}\left(\Delta p+2(-u_{x}v_{y}+u_{y}v_{x})-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{f}\right)^{2}dxdy,

\displaystyle\text{B}_{\bm{u}}=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{0}\right)^{2}dS.

5
Conclusion and future work

In this paper we proposed four linearized learning schemes to solve the stationary highly oscillatory Navier-Stokes flows with multiscale deep neural networks and showed the acceleration of convergence of the schemes are substantial, which demonstrate the capability of the Mscale deep neural networks and the effectiveness of the linearized schemes to solve the nonlinear Navier-Stokes equations. These schemes shed some lights on the practical applications of neural network machine learning algorithms to the nonlinear equations, which are time-consuming using traditional finite element methods. The deep neural network based methods offer an alternative that doesn’t require meshes without the need to solve large-scale linear systems, in constrast to traditional numerical methods.

There are much more works to be done for these linearized learning methods, among them the most important is to understand the convergence property of these schemes. The applications of these schemes to other nonlinear PDEs will also be considered. Another challenging work is to consider the time dependent Navier-Stokes equation, which will be explored in the near future.

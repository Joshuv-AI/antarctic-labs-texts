1.
Menon’s identity

The divisor function
d(m)
counts the number of positive divisors of the positive integer
m
.
Let
(a,b)
denote the greatest common divisor of integers
a
and
b

that are not both 0.
For every integer
s
, the
s
-free divisor function

d_{s}(m)

counts the number of positive divisors
d
of
m
such that
(d,s)=1
.
We have
d_{0}(m)=1
and
d_{s}(m)=d(m)
if
(s,m)=1
.
In particular,
d_{1}(m)=d(m)
.
Note that
d_{s}(m)=d_{-s}(m)
.

For example,
d_{1}(12)=6
,
d_{2}(12)=2
, and
d_{3}(12)=3
.
If
p^{v}
is a prime power, then

d_{s}(p^{v})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $p$ divides $s$}\\
v+1&\text{if $p$ does not divide $s$.}\end{cases}

For every positive integer
m
, the Euler totient function
\varphi(m)
counts the number of integers
a

such that
1\leq a\leq m
and
(a,m)=1
. Thus,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,m)=1\end{subarray}}^{m}1=\varphi(m).

Equivalently,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,m)=1\end{subarray}}^{m}(a,m)=d_{0}(m)\varphi(m).

In 1965 P. Kesava Menon
[
4
]
proved the following beautiful arithmetic identity.

Theorem 1
.

For all positive integers
m
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,m)=1\end{subarray}}^{m}(a-1,m)=d_{1}(m)\varphi(m).

For example, if
m=12
, then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,12)=1\end{subarray}}^{12}(a-1,12)=(0,12)+(4,12)+(6,12)+(10,12)=24

and

d_{1}(12)\varphi(12)=6\cdot 4=24.

If
m=p
is prime, then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,p)=1\end{subarray}}^{p}(a-1,p)=(0,p)+\sum_{b=1}^{p-2}(b,p)=p+(p-2)=2p-2

and

d_{1}(p)\varphi(p)=2(p-1)=2p-2.

Theorem
1
has applications to group theory.
For example, I. M. Richards
[
6
]
used the identity to
prove that if
N
is the number of cyclic subgroups in a finite group
G
of order
m
,
then
N\geq d(m)
and
N=d(m)
if and only if
G
is cyclic.

Menon
[
4
]
gave three proofs of his identity. The excellent survey paper
of László Tóth
[
7
]

contains 10 proofs, many generalizations, and a comprehensive bibliography
of work on Menon’s identity.

A
multiplicative function
is an arithmetic function
f(m)
such that
f(m_{1}m_{2})=f(m_{1})f(m_{2})

for all pairs of relatively prime positive integers
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
.
The divisor functions
d(m)
and
d_{s}(m)
are multiplicative.

With the
s
-free divisor function
d_{s}(m)
, there is the following
extension of Menon’s identity.

Theorem 2
.

For all integers
s
and all positive integers
m
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,m)=1\end{subarray}}^{m}(a-s,m)=d_{s}(m)\varphi(m).

This is identity (4.4) in Tóth
[
7
]
. See also
[
2
,
3
]
.
Theorem
2
reduces to Theorem
1

if
s=1
.

For example, if
s=2
and
m=12
, then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,12)=1\end{subarray}}^{12}(a-2,12)=(-1,12)+(3,12)+(5,12)+(9,12)=8

and

d_{2}(12)\varphi(12)=2\cdot 4=8.

Let
k
be a positive integer and let
a
and
b
be integers that are not both 0.
The
k
th power greatest common divisor
of
a
and
b
, denoted
(a,b)_{k}
,
is the largest
k
th power that divides both
a
and
b
.
The integers
a
and
b
are
k
th power relatively prime
if
(a,b)_{k}=1
.
For example, the integers 4 and 8 are third power relatively prime.
The integers 8 and 27 are also third power relatively prime.

For every positive integer
m
, the

Eckford Cohen totient function

\varphi^{(k)}\left(m\right)
(Cohen
[
1
]
) counts the number of integers
a

such that
1\leq a\leq m^{k}
and
(a,m^{k})_{k}=1
.
Note that
\varphi^{(1)}\left(m\right)=\varphi\left(m\right)
for all
m
.
Properties of the Eckford Cohen totient function are discussed
in Section
2
.
For example, with
m=2^{k}
we see that

\left(a,\left(2^{k}\right)^{2}\right)_{2}=(a,4^{k})_{2}=1

if and only if 4 does not divide
a
and so

\varphi^{(2)}\left(2^{k}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
\left(a,4^{k}\right)_{2}=1\end{subarray}}^{4^{k}}1=4^{k}-4^{k-1}=3\cdot 4^{k-1}.

In 1972, K. Nageswara Rao
[
5
]
applied the Eckford Cohen totient function
to obtain the following generalization of Menon’s identity.

Theorem 3
.

For all integers
s
and all positive integers
k
and
m
,
if
s
and
m^{k}
are
k
th power relatively prime, then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,m^{k})_{k}=1\end{subarray}}^{m^{k}}\left(a-s,m^{k}\right)_{k}=d(m)\varphi^{(k)}(m).

For example, with
s=1
,
k=2
, and
m=4
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,16)_{2}=1\end{subarray}}^{16}\left(a-1,16\right)_{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=0\\
b\neq 3,7,11\end{subarray}}^{14}(b,16)_{2}=36

and

d(4)\varphi^{(2)}(4)=3\cdot 12=36.

Theorem
3
reduces to
Theorem
1
(Menon’s identity) if
k=s=1

and Theorem
2
if
k=1
and
(s,m)=1
.

To remove the
k
th power relatively prime condition from Rao’s result,
we introduce the divisor function
d_{s}^{(k)}(m)
,
which is defined as follows:
If
p^{v}
is a prime power, then

(1)

d_{s}^{(k)}(p^{v})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $p^{k}$ divides $s$}\\
v+1&\text{if $p^{k}$ does not divide $s$.}\end{cases}

Write
p^{v}\|m
if
p^{v}
divides
m
and
p^{v+1}
does not divide
m
.
For every positive integer
m
, we define

d_{s}^{(k)}(m)=\prod_{p^{v}\|m}d_{s}^{(k)}(p^{v}).

The arithmetic function
d_{s}^{(k)}(m)
is multiplicative,
and
d_{s}^{(1)}(m)=d_{s}(m)
.

The following identity generalizes Rao’s Theorem
3
.

Theorem 4
.

For all integers
s
and all positive integers
k
and
m
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,m^{k})_{k}=1\end{subarray}}^{m^{k}}\left(a-s,m^{k}\right)_{k}=d_{s}^{(k)}(m)\varphi^{(k)}(m).

For example, if
s=12
,
k=2
, and
m=4
, then
\left(s,m^{k}\right)_{k}=\left(12,16\right)_{2}=4
.
We have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,16)_{2}=1\end{subarray}}^{16}\left(a-12,16\right)_{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=-11\\
b\neq-8,-4,0\end{subarray}}^{3}(b,16)_{2}=12

and

d_{12}^{(2)}(4)\varphi^{(2)}(4)=1\cdot 12=12.

Theorem
4
reduces to
Theorem
2
if
k=1

and to Theorem
3

if the integers
s
and
m^{k}
are
k
th power relatively prime.
In Section
4
we use multiplicative arithmetic functions to give a simple proof of Theorem
4
.

2.
The Eckford Cohen totient function
\varphi^{(k)}(m)

We have

\varphi^{(k)}(m)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,m^{k})_{k}=1\end{subarray}}^{m^{k}}1.

Note that
(a,m^{k})_{k}>1
if and only if there is an integer
d>1
such that
d
divides
m

and
d^{k}
divides
a
.

Lemma 1
.

The Eckford Cohen totient function

\varphi^{(k)}(m)
is a multiplicative arithmetic function. For every positive integer
m
,

\varphi^{(k)}\left(m\right)=m^{k}\prod_{p|m}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{k}}\right).

If
p
is a prime and
v
is a positive integer, then

\varphi^{(k)}\left(p^{v}\right)=p^{vk}-p^{(v-1)k}.

Proof.

For every divisor
d
of
m
, the number of positive integers up to
m^{k}
that are divisible
by
d^{k}
is
m^{k}/d^{k}
. In particular, if
p_{i_{1}}p_{i_{2}}\cdots p_{i_{\ell}}
is a product
of distinct primes that divide
m
, then the number of positive integers up to
m^{k}

that are divisible by
(p_{i_{1}}p_{i_{2}}\cdots p_{i_{\ell}})^{k}

is
m^{k}/(p_{i_{1}}p_{i_{2}}\cdots p_{i_{\ell}})^{k}
.

Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
be the distinct primes that divide
m
.
Let
1\leq a\leq m^{k}
. We have
(a,m^{k})_{k}=1
if and only if
a
is not divisible
by
p_{i}^{k}
for all
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
.
The inclusion-exclusion principle implies that

\displaystyle\varphi^{(k)}\left(m\right)

\displaystyle=m^{k}-\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{r}\frac{m^{k}}{p_{i_{1}}^{k}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}=1\\
i_{1}<i_{2}\end{subarray}}^{r}\frac{m^{k}}{p_{i_{1}}^{k}p_{i_{2}}^{k}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}=1\\
i_{1}<i_{2}<i_{3}\end{subarray}}^{r}\frac{m^{k}}{p_{i_{1}}^{k}p_{i_{2}}^{k}p_{i_{3}}^{k}}+\cdots

\displaystyle=m^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}^{k}}\right).

This formula implies that the function
\varphi^{(k)}(m)
is multiplicative.
The expression for
\varphi^{(k)}(p^{v})
follows immediately.
This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2
.

If
a\equiv b\pmod{m^{k}}
, then
(a,m^{k})_{k}=(b,m^{k})_{k}
.
If one element of a congruence class modulo
m^{k}
is
k
th power relatively prime to
m^{k}
,
then every element in the congruence class is
k
th power relatively prime to
m^{k}
.

Proof.

If
a=b+qm^{k}
, then
d^{k}
divides both
a
and
m^{k}
if and only if

d^{k}
divides both
b
and
m^{k}
. It follows that
(a,m^{k})_{k}=(b,m^{k})_{k}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

A
k
th power reduced set of residues modulo
m
is a set of

\varphi^{(k)}\left(m\right)
integers, one from each congruence class modulo
m^{k}

whose elements are
k
th power relatively prime to
m^{k}
.
The standard
k
th power reduced set of residues modulo
m
is

\left\{a:1\leq a\leq m^{k}\text{ and }(a,m^{k})_{k}=1\right\}.

Lemma
2
implies that
if
A
is a
k
th power reduced set of residues modulo
m
, then

\varphi^{(k)}(m)=\sum_{a\in A}\left(a,m^{k}\right)_{k}.

Lemma 3
.

Let
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
be relatively prime positive integers.
If
A_{1}
is a
k
th power reduced set of residues modulo
m_{1}
and

A_{2}
is a
k
th power reduced set of residues modulo
m_{2}
, then the set

A=\{a_{1}m_{2}^{k}+a_{2}m_{1}^{k}:a_{1}\in A_{1}\text{ and }a_{2}\in A_{2}\}

is a
k
th power reduced set of residues modulo
m_{1}m_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
a=a_{1}m_{2}^{k}+a_{2}m_{1}^{k}\in A
and let
d^{k}=(a,m_{1}^{k}m_{2}^{k})_{k}
.
Suppose that
d>1
. If the prime
p
divides
d
, then
p^{k}
divides
d^{k}

and
d^{k}
divides
m_{1}^{k}m_{2}^{k}
, and so
p^{k}
divides
m_{1}^{k}m_{2}^{k}
.
If
p
divides
m_{1}
, then
p
does not divide
m_{2}
and so
p^{k}
divides
m_{1}^{k}
.
Because
p^{k}
divides
a=a_{1}m_{2}^{k}+a_{2}m_{1}^{k}
,
it follows that
p^{k}
divides
a_{1}m_{2}^{k}
and so
p^{k}
divides
a_{1}
.
Thus,
1=(a_{1},m_{1}^{k})_{k}\geq p^{k}
,
which is absurd. Therefore,
(a,m_{1}^{k}m_{2}^{k})_{k}=1
and every element of
A

is
k
th power relatively prime to
m_{1}m_{2}
.

Let
a_{1},a^{\prime}_{1}\in A_{1}
and
a_{2},a^{\prime}_{2}\in A_{2}
. If

a_{1}m_{2}^{k}+a_{2}m_{1}^{k}\equiv a^{\prime}_{1}m_{2}^{k}+a^{\prime}_{2}m_{1}^{k}\pmod{m_{1}^{k}m_{2}^{k}}

then

a_{1}m_{2}^{k}\equiv a_{1}m_{2}^{k}+a_{2}m_{1}^{k}\equiv a^{\prime}_{1}m_{2}^{k}+a^{\prime}_{2}m_{1}^{k}\equiv a^{\prime}_{1}m_{2}^{k}\pmod{m_{1}^{k}}.

Because
(m_{1},m_{2})=1
, we have
a_{1}\equiv a^{\prime}_{1}\pmod{m_{1}^{k}}
and so
a_{1}=a^{\prime}_{1}
.
Similarly,
a_{2}=a^{\prime}_{2}
.
Thus, the elements of
A
belong to pairwise distinct congruence classes
modulo
m_{1}^{k}m_{2}^{k}
.

By Lemma
1
,
the Eckford Cohen totient function is multiplicative,
and so

\varphi^{(k)}(m_{1}m_{2})=\varphi^{(k)}(m_{1})\varphi^{(k)}(m_{2})=|A_{1}|\ |A_{2}|=|A|

and the set
A
is a
k
th power reduced set of residues modulo
m_{1}m_{2}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Pillai’s sum functions
P^{(k)}(m)

Let
m
and
k
be positive integers.
The
gcd sum function
,
also called
Pillai’s arithmetic function
, is

P(m)=\sum_{a=1}^{m}(a,m).

The
k
th power gcd sum function
, that is, the
k
th power analogue
of Pillai’s arithmetic function, is

P^{(k)}(m)=\sum_{a=1}^{m^{k}}(a,m^{k})_{k}.

Note that
P^{(1)}(m)=P(m)
.

Lemma 4
.

For every positive integer
m
,

P^{(k)}(m)=\sum_{d|m}d^{k}\varphi^{(k)}\left(\frac{m}{d}\right).

If
p^{v}
is a prime power, then

P^{(k)}\left(p^{v}\right)=(v+1)p^{vk}-vp^{(v-1)k}.

Proof.

Write
a=bd^{k}
if
d^{k}
divides
a
.
We have

\displaystyle P^{(k)}(m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{m^{k}}(a,m^{k})_{k}=\sum_{d|m}d^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
d^{k}|a\\
\left(a/d^{k},(m/d)^{k}\right)_{k}=1\end{subarray}}^{m^{k}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{d|m}d^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
(b,(m/d)^{k})_{k}=1\end{subarray}}^{(m/d)^{k}}1=\sum_{d|m}d^{k}\varphi^{(k)}\left(\frac{m}{d}\right).

The set of
k
th power divisors of the prime power
p^{vk}
is

\left\{p^{ik}:i=0,1,2,\ldots,v\right\}
.
Applying Lemma
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle P^{(k)}\left(p^{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{v}p^{ik}\varphi^{(k)}\left(p^{v-i}\right)=p^{vk}+\sum_{i=0}^{v-1}p^{ik}\left(p^{(v-i)k}-p^{(v-i-1)k}\right)

\displaystyle=p^{vk}+\sum_{i=0}^{v-1}\left(p^{vk}-p^{(v-1)k}\right)=p^{vk}+v\left(p^{vk}-p^{(v-1)k}\right)

\displaystyle=(v+1)p^{vk}-vp^{(v-1)k}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We use the following properties of the

Lemma 5
.

Let

(i)

For all integers

(ii)

For all integers

and

, then

\sum_{a\in A}(a\ell-s,m^{k})_{k}=\sum_{a\in A}(a-s,m^{k})_{k}.

(i) Fix the integer

. Let
m_{1}
and
m_{2}

The positive integer

m_{2}^{k}

The integers

Let

Because

and

m_{1}^{k}

m_{2}^{k}
.

d_{1}^{k}
divides
(a,m_{1}^{k})_{k}

(a,m_{2}^{k})_{k}

Therefore,
(a,(m_{1}m_{2})^{k})_{k}=(a,m_{1}^{k})_{k}(a,m_{2}^{k})_{k}
.
This proves the multiplicativity of the function

.

(ii) Let

be a

. Let

and let

be an integer that is relatively prime to

.
If

divides
a\ell
and
d^{k}
divides
m^{k}
.
It follows from

(d^{k},\ell)=1

divides

.
From

If

.
It follows that the sets

Lemma 7
.

For every integer
s
and every prime power
p^{v}
,

M_{s}^{(k)}(p^{v})=d_{s}^{(k)}(p^{v})\varphi^{(k)}(p^{v}).

Proof.

The set

A=\left\{1,2,\ldots,p^{vk}\right\}\setminus\left\{ip^{k}:i=1,2,\ldots,p^{(v-1)k}\right\}

is a
k
th power reduced set of residues modulo
p^{v}
and

|A|=\varphi^{(k)}(p^{v})=p^{vk}-p^{(v-1)k}.

There are two cases. In the first case, the integer
s
is divisible by
p^{k}
.
If

(a-s,p^{vk})_{k}>1
, then
p^{k}
divides
a-s
and so
p^{k}
divides
a

and
(a,p^{vk})_{k}>1
.
Therefore,
(a,p^{vk})_{k}=1
implies
(a-s,p^{vk})_{k}=1
.

We have
d_{s}^{(k)}(p^{v})=1
by (
1
).
By Lemma
1
, we have

\displaystyle M_{s}^{(k)}(p^{v})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,p^{vk})_{k}=1\end{subarray}}^{p^{vk}}\left(a-s,p^{vk}\right)_{k}=p^{vk}-p^{(v-1)k}

\displaystyle=\varphi^{(k)}(p^{v})=d_{s}^{(k)}(p^{v})\varphi^{(k)}(p^{v}).

In the second case, the integer
s
is not divisible by
p^{k}
, that is,
(s,p^{k})_{k}=1
.
If
\left(bp^{k}-s,p^{vk}\right)_{k}>1
for some
b\in\mathbf{Z}
, then
p^{k}
divides
s
,
which is absurd. Therefore,

\left(bp^{k}-s,p^{vk}\right)_{k}=1

for all integers
b
.
Using the gcd sum function
P^{(k)}(m)
from Lemma
4
, we obtain

\displaystyle M_{s}^{(k)}(p^{v})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,p^{k})_{k}=1\end{subarray}}^{p^{v}}\left(a-s,p^{vk}\right)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{p^{vk}}\left(a-s,p^{vk}\right)_{k}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,p^{k})_{k}>1\end{subarray}}^{p^{v}}\left(a-s,p^{vk}\right)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{p^{vk}}\left(a-s,p^{vk}\right)_{k}-\sum_{b=1}^{p^{(v-1)k}}\left(bp^{k}-s,p^{vk}\right)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{p^{vk}}\left(a,p^{vk}\right)_{k}-\sum_{b=1}^{p^{(v-1)k}}1

\displaystyle=P^{(k)}(p^{v})-p^{(v-1)k}

\displaystyle=\left((v+1)p^{vk}-vp^{(v-1)k}\right)-p^{(v-1)k}

\displaystyle=(v+1)\left(p^{vk}-p^{(v-1)k}\right)

\displaystyle=d_{s}^{(k)}\left(p^{v}\right)\varphi^{(k)}\left(p^{v}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

The arithmetic functions
M_{s}^{(k)}(m)
,

, and
\varphi^{(k)}(m)

\displaystyle M_{s}^{(k)}(m)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p^{v}\|m}M_{s}^{(k)}\left(p^{v}\right)=\prod_{p^{v}\|m}d_{s}^{(k)}(p^{v})\varphi^{(k)}(p^{v})

\displaystyle=d_{s}^{(k)}(m)\varphi^{(k)}(m).

This completes the proof of Theorem

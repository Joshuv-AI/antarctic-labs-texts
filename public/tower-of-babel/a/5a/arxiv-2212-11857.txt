Aut ​ ( F n ) \text{Aut}(F_{n}) actions on representation spaces
Tsachik Gelander

Abstract
. J. Wiegold conjectured that if n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 and G G is a finite simple group, then the action of Aut ​ ( F n ) \text{Aut}(F_{n}) on Epi ​ ( F n , G ) \text{Epi}(F_{n},G) is transitive. In this note we consider analogous questions where G G is a compact Lie group, a non-compact simple analytic group or a simple algebraic group.

1.
Introduction

Let
n
be an integer. We usually suppose that
n\geq 3
. Let
G
be a group and consider the representation space
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
of all homomorphisms from the free group
F_{n}
into
G
. The group
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
acts on
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
by pre-compositions,

\sigma\cdot\psi(x)=\psi(\sigma^{-1}(x))

where
\sigma\in\text{Aut}(F_{n}),\psi\in\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
and
x\in F_{n}
. This paper is concerned with various aspects of this action in different contexts.
By choosing a base for
F_{n}
we can identify (non-canonically)
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
with
G^{n}
.

If
G
carries an additional structure,
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
inherits a related structure which is often preserved by the
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
action.
Let us give some examples:

(1)

If
G
is finite so is
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
and
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
acts by permutations.

(2)

If
G
is a topological group,
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
with the compact open (equivalently, the pointwise convergence) topology is a topological space and
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
acts by homeomorphisms.

(3)

If
G
is a locally compact group, the Haar measure on
G
induces a measure class on
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
which is
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
-invariant.

(4)

Furthermore, if
G
is unimodular, the Haar measure on
G
induces an
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
-invariant measure on
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
.

(5)

If
G
is a linear algebraic group then
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
is an affine algebraic variety and
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
acts by algebraic automorphisms.

Most of the time, we focus on the invariant subset of epimorphisms, where in the context of topological (or algebraic) groups we mean homomorphisms with dense (or Zariski dense) image,

\text{Epi}(F_{n},G):=\{f\in\text{Hom}(F_{n},G):\overline{f(F_{n})}=G\}.

An epimorphism
f:F_{n}\to G
is called
redundant
if there is a non-trivial free product decomposition
F_{n}=A*B
such that the restriction of
f
to
A
is still an epimorphism, i.e.
\overline{f(A)}=G
, where again the bar denotes the closure (or the Zariski closure) and
should be ignored in the discrete case. Denote
by
\mathcal{R}(F_{n},G)\subset\text{Epi}(F_{n},G)
the set of redundant representations.

One main question we are interested in is whether, under certain natural assumptions on
G
, every epimorphism is redundant.

Question 1.1
.

Let
G
be either a finite simple group, a connected compact Lie group or a Zariski connected simple algebraic group and let
n\geq 3
. Does

\mathcal{R}(F_{n},G)=\text{Epi}(F_{n},G)?

Let us denote by
\mathcal{P}_{n}\subset F_{n}
the set of primitive elements. Recall that an element
p\in F_{n}
is primitive iff it belongs to a free basis of
F_{n}
. Note that if
F_{n}=A*B
is a free product decomposition and
b\in B
is primitive, then the coset
Ab
consists of primitive elements.
In the discrete case (in particular when
G
is finite) a map
f:F_{n}\to G
is redundant iff
f(\mathcal{P}_{n})=G
or equivalently,
f
is surjective and
\ker(f)\cap\mathcal{P}_{n}\neq\emptyset
. When
G
is a topological group (or an algebraic group) the image of the primitive elements under a redundant map is dense (resp. Zariski dense).

Remark 1.2
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since
F_{n}
is finitely generated and residually finite, it is Hopfian, i.e.
\text{Epi}(F_{n},F_{n})=\text{Aut}(F_{n})
.
(Indeed, if
f:F_{n}\to F_{n}
is a surjective homomorphism, and
H\leq F_{n}
is a subgroup of index
m
then
f^{-1}(H)
is of index
m
as well. Since for every
m<\infty
, there are only finitely many subgroups of index
m
, all these subgroups must be of the form
f^{-1}(H),~[F_{n}:H]=m
i.e. they all contain
\text{ker}(f)
, and since
F_{n}
is residually finite, it follows that
\text{ker}(f)=\{1\}
.)
Moreover, it follows from the Nielsen theorem that for every
k\leq n
,
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
acts transitively on
\text{Epi}(F_{n},F_{k})
. In particular for
k<n
any epimorphism
F_{n}\to F_{k}
is redundant. See
[
LS77
, Chapter 2]
for a detailed description of the Nielsen theorem (in particular see
[
LS77
, Proposition 2.12]
).
It follows that for
G
discrete, an epimorphism
f:F_{n}\to G
is redundant iff it factors through an epimorphism from
F_{k}
to
G
with
k<n
.
Nielsen’s theorem also produces a convenient generating set for
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
, namely the Nielsen transformations. With respect to an ordered free basis
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
for
F_{n}
the Nielsen transformations take the form:

R_{i,j}^{\pm}:(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j}x_{i}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}),~i\neq j

L_{i,j}^{\pm}:(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{\pm 1}x_{j},\ldots,x_{n}),~i\neq j

I_{j}:(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j}^{-1},\ldots,x_{n}).

1.1.
Finite simple groups

The following famous conjecture is attributed to J. Wiegold.

Conjecture 1.3

(Wiegold)
.

For any finite simple group
G
and
n\geq 3
, the action of
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
on
\text{Epi}(F_{n},G)
is transitive.

It was shown in
[
Gi77
,
E93b
]
that all the redundant representations are in the same
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
-orbit. Thus the Weigold conjecture is equivalent to the conjecture that any epimorphism from
F_{n}
to
G
is redundant, i.e. to an affirmative answer to Question
1.1

in this context.

We will not consider here finite simple groups. For what is known and mostly not known concerning the Wiegold conjecture, as well as interesting applications and related questions we refer the reader to the wonderful exposition by Lubotzky
[
L11
]
.
*
*

*

There is a small overlap between this paper and Lubotzky’s exposition
[
L11
]
, but they are mostly complementary.

Instead, we will consider analogous problems in the context of compact Lie groups, non-compact semisimple Lie groups, semisimple groups over non-archimedean local fields and mainly semisimple algebraic groups.

Remark 1.4
.

The assumption
n\geq 3
is necessary (here and below). Indeed if
G
is a group of matrices (over any field) then the function

\text{Trace}[f(x_{1}),f(x_{2})]+\text{Trace}[f(x_{2}),f(x_{1})]

where
x_{1},x_{2}\in F_{2}
are free generators, and
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the commutator, is
\text{Aut}(F_{2})
invariant and in general non-constant.

2.
Compact Lie groups

In this section
G
denotes a connected compact Lie group. We consider the compact open topology on
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
. Since
G
is compact, this is a compact space.
The normalized Haar measure
\mu
on
G
induces a probability measure
\mu_{n}
on
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
; concretely, by fixing a base for
F_{n}
we may identify
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
with
G^{n}
and take the product measure
\mu_{n}=\mu^{n}
. Since
G
is unimodular,
\mu_{n}
is
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
-invariant. Indeed, it is enough to verify this claim for Nielsen transformations, whose effect on
G^{n}
can be described by multiplying one coordinate from the left or from the right by another or by taking the inverse of one coordinate, and the Haar measure is invariant under these operations.

What makes the compact Lie group case simpler than other scenarios is that up to measure zero we have:

\mathcal{R}(F_{n},G)=\text{Epi}(F_{n},G)=\text{Hom}(F_{n},G),

where the second equality holds for
n\geq 2
and the first one for
n\geq 3
.
Furthermore, if
G
is perfect, i.e.
G=G^{\prime}
, then
\mathcal{R}(F_{n},G)
is also open.
These facts are immediate consequences of the following lemma (see
[
Ge08
]
for more details):

Lemma 2.1
.

[
Ge08
, Lemma 1.4]

Let
G
be a connected compact Lie group.
Let

\Omega=\{(x,y)\in G^{2}:\langle x,y\rangle~\text{is dense in}~G\}.

Then
\Omega
is dense and of full measure. Furthermore, if
G
is perfect, it is also open.

The ergodicity part of the following theorem from
[
Ge08
]
confirmed a conjecture of Goldman, and the minimality, though not stated in
[
Ge08
]
, is implicit in the
argument which served to prove of a conjecture of Margulis and Soifer. Recall that a group action on a topological space is minimal if every orbit is dense.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
G
be a connected compact semisimple Lie group and let
n\geq 3
. Then the
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
action on
\mathcal{R}(F_{n},G)
is both ergodic and minimal.

The theorem is formulated using new terminology and presenting its short proof will allow us to introduce additional useful terms and ideas.

Fix a basis
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
of
F_{n}
and set

\mathcal{O}_{i}=\{f:F_{n}\to G:\langle\{f(x_{j}),j\neq i\}\rangle~\text{is dense in}~G\},~i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Then
\mathcal{R}(F_{n},G)=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\text{Aut}(F_{n})}\sigma\cdot\mathcal{O}_{n}
. Let us also define the set of
strongly redundant
representations as follows:

\mathcal{SR}(F_{n},G)=\bigcap_{\sigma\in\text{Aut}(F_{n})}\sigma\cdot\mathcal{O}_{n}.

Then
\rho\in\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
is strongly redundant iff its restriction to any free factor of rank
n-1
is dense.
Since
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
is countable, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that
\mathcal{SR}(F_{n},G)
is of full measure and of second category.

Proof of ergodicity.

In order to prove that the action of
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
on
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
is ergodic, it is enough to restrict to the invariant subset
\mathcal{SR}(F_{n},G)
. Suppose by way of contradiction that there is an
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
invariant subset
A\subset\mathcal{SR}(F_{n},G)
which is neither null nor co-null. Making use again of the fact that
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
is countable, we may suppose that
A=\bigcap_{\text{Aut}{F_{n}}}\sigma\cdot A
, i.e. that
A
is set-theoretically invariant rather than measurably.
In view of Fubini’s theorem the set
A
is not independent of at least one coordinate, i.e.
there must be
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
g_{j}\in G,j\neq i
such that

B=\{f(x_{i}):f\in A,f(x_{j})=g_{j},\forall j\neq i\}

is neither null nor co-null in
G
. Since
A
is
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
-invariant,
B
is invariant under left multiplication by
g_{j},~j\neq i
. But
\langle\{g_{j},j\neq i\}\rangle
is dense and hence acts ergodically on
G
. A contradiction.
∎

For the proof of minimality it is convenient to introduce some more notations.
For a subset
S\subset G
let
\mathcal{D}(S)
denote the set of elements which together with
S
generate
G
:

\mathcal{D}(S):=\{g\in G:\overline{\langle S,g\rangle}=G\}.

Suppose that
G
is as in Theorem
2.2
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\mathcal{D}(S)
is nonempty then it is open and dense.

This is implicit in
[
Ge08
, §1.1]
.∎

Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(S)=\bigcap_{s\in S}\mathcal{D}(S\setminus\{s\})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
S
is a finite (topological) generating set for
G
, then
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(S)
is open and dense.

Proof.

For
s\in S
the set
\mathcal{D}(S\setminus\{s\})
is nonempty since it contains
s
. Thus
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(S)
is an intersection of finitely many open dense sets.
∎

Abusing notations, for
f\in\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
let us denote

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(f)=\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\{f(x_{1}),\ldots,f(x_{n})\}).

The above lemma says that for
f\in\text{Epi}(F_{n},G)
,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(f)
is open and dense in
G
.
Let
\Omega=\cap_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{i}
.
That is,

\Omega=\{f\in\text{Hom}(F_{n},G):\overline{\langle\{f(x_{j}),j\neq i\}\rangle}=G,~\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\}.

Proof of minimality.

Let
f\in\mathcal{R}(F_{n},G)
be a redundant representation. Up to applying some element from
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
we may suppose that
f\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
. Then
f(x_{1}),\ldots,f(x_{n-1})
generate a dense subgroup and hence by acting by left multiplications on the last coordinate we can take it into
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\{f(x_{1}),\ldots,f(x_{n-1})\})
. This allows us to assume that
\sigma\cdot f\in\Omega
for an appropriate
\sigma\in\text{Aut}(F_{n})
.
Now
\Omega
is open and dense in
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
and by applying transformations
corresponding to left multiplications of one coordinate by words in the other coordinates
we may move every coordinate into any neighborhood in
G
without leaving
\Omega
. This shows that the orbit of
f
is dense in
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
.
∎

Since the ergodicity result holds for any compact connected group
G
†
†

†

Even the assumption that
G
is semisimple is unnecessary (see
[
Ge08
]
).
,
we obtain for free:

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
G
a connected compact Lie group. Then the action of
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
on
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
is weakly mixing.

Recall that a p.m.p action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is weakly mixing if and only if it is doubly ergodic, i.e. if the diagonal action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X\times X
is ergodic.

Proof.

We need to show that the action is doubly ergodic. However

\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)\times\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)

is canonically isomorphic to
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G\times G)
. Since
G^{2}
is a connected compact Lie group, the result follows from Theorem
2.2
.
∎

2.1.
Invariant measure classification

Given a closed subgroup
H\leq G
, the Haar measure of
H
induces an invariant measure on the invariant set
\text{Epi}(F_{n},H)\subset\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
. We shall call such a measure algebraic. More generally we say that an invariant measure
\mu
on
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
is
algebraic
if there is a closed subgroup
H\leq G
such that
\mu
is supported on
\text{Epi}(F_{n},H)
and the restriction of
\mu
to each fiber of the quotient
\text{Epi}(F_{n},H)\to\text{Epi}(F_{n},H/H^{\circ})
coincides with the Haar measure of
(H^{\circ})^{n}
, where
H^{\circ}
is the identity connected component of
H
. This formulation allows us to avoid dealing with finite groups. In analogy to Ratner’s theorem we conjecture:

Conjecture 2.6

(Measure classification conjecture, suggested by Nir Avni)
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group and
n\geq 3
. Then every
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
-invariant ergodic measure on
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
is algebraic.

Restricting to connected groups we have the following formulation:

Conjecture 2.7

(Unique ergodicity conjecture)
.

Let
G
be a connected compact Lie group and
n\geq 3
. Then the only
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
-invariant ergodic measure on
\text{Epi}(F_{n},G)
is the Haar measure on
G^{n}
.

A weaker conjecture, in the spirit of the ‘baby Wiegold conjecture’ discussed in §
8
is that the character variety admits no invariant atomic measures:

Conjecture 2.8
.

Let
G
ba a compact connected semisimple Lie group, and lat
n\geq 3
. Then
\chi_{n}(G)=\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)/G
admits no atomic
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
-invariant measures.

Here
\chi_{n}(G)=\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)/G
is the space quotient of
G
-orbits where
G
acts on
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
by conjugation
(g\cdot f)(x)=gf(x)g^{-1}
.

As we have seen almost every representation is redundant (w.r.t the Haar measure). A much more delicate question is whether
every
dense representation is redundant:

Question 2.9
.

Given
n\geq 3
and a compact connected Lie group
G
, does

\mathcal{R}(F_{n},G)=\text{Epi}(F_{n},G)
? Where the equality here is set theoretically and not up to measure
0
.

3.
Non-compact semisimple groups

The non-compact semisimple case is more involved even when restricting to the Haar measure.
In particular the answer to Question
1.1
is negative in certain cases. Yair Minsky showed in
[
Mi13
]
that for
G=\text{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
or
\text{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
, the set
\text{Epi}(F_{n},G)\setminus\mathcal{R}(F_{n},G)
has a non-empty interior.
On the other hand Yair Glasner showed in
[
Gl09
]
that for
G=\text{SL}(2,k)
with
k
a non-archimedean local field, the set
\text{Epi}(F_{n},G)\setminus\mathcal{R}(F_{n},G)
has (Haar) measure zero.

Y. Minsky considered the action of
\text{Out}(F_{n})
on

\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)/G
.
‡
‡

‡

In fact Minsky considered the action on the space of characters
\chi(F_{n},G)=\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)//G

which is defined in the sense of geometric invariant theory and has the structure of an algebraic variety. However that space differs from the purely topological quotient
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)/G
only at reducible points, and hence the two agree when restricting to representations with dense image (see
[
Mi13
]
and the references therein).

He considered
\text{Out}(F_{n})
rather than
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
since the inner automorphisms acts trivially on that quotient.
He defined the notion of primitive-stable representations and proved that the set
\mathcal{PS}(F_{n},G)
of primitive stable representations is open and the action of
\text{Out}(F_{n})
on its image in
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)/G
is properly discontinuous and in particular non-ergodic. Furthermore, he proved that
\mathcal{PS}(F_{n},G)
contains a dense representation, i.e. the open invariant set
\mathcal{PS}(F_{n},G)\cap\text{Epi}(F_{n},G)
is non-empty. It follows directly from the definition that the image of the primitive elements
\mathcal{P}_{n}
under a primitive stable representation misses a neighborhood of the identity of
G
. In particular one deduces:

Theorem 3.1

(Minsky)
.

Let
G=\text{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
or
\text{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
. Then there is a dense representation
\rho:F_{n}\to G
such that the image of the primitive elements
\rho(\mathcal{P}_{n})
is not dense in
G
.

In contrast to Minsky’s result, Y. Glasner
[
Gl09
]
proved that for
G=\text{SL}(2,k)
with
k
non-Archimedean and
n\geq 3
the action of
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
on
\text{Epi}(F_{n},G)
is ergodic. The dichotomy between the Archimedean and the non-Archimedean cases for
\text{SL}_{2}
can be named the Yairs’ dichotomy. For more details here about these results, see the well written papers by Y. Minsky and Y. Glasner.
[
Mi13
,
Gl09
]
.

Note that the ergodicity question brakes into two independent questions:

•

Is the
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
action on the redundant part
\mathcal{R}(F_{n},G)
ergodic?

•

Does
\text{Epi}(F_{n},G)=\mathcal{R}(F_{n},G)
up to measure zero?

Jointly with Minsky we answered the first question and also proved a minimality results in a quite general context, answering a conjecture of Lubotzky:

Theorem 3.2

(
[
GM13
]
)
.

Let
k
be a local field,
{\mathbb{G}}
a Zariski connected simple

k
group, and
G={\mathbb{G}}(k)
the group of
k
points. If
\text{char}(k)>0
assume further that the adjoint representation of
G
is irreducible.
Then the action of
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
on
\mathcal{R}_{n}(G)
is ergodic with respect to the Haar measure and minimal with respect to the compact open topology.

On the other hand, unlike the compact case, we showed that this action is not always weak mixing in the following sense:

Theorem 3.3

(
[
GM13
]
)
.

Let
G=\text{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
or
\text{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
, and
n\geq 3
. Then the action of

\text{Out}(F_{n})
on
\overline{{\mathcal{R}}}_{n}(G):={\mathcal{R}}(F_{n},G)/G
is not weakly mixing,
in the sense that the diagonal action of
Out(F_{n})

on
\overline{{\mathcal{R}}}_{n}(G)\times\overline{{\mathcal{R}}}_{n}(G)
is not ergodic.

For the
\text{Out}(F_{n})
action on
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)/G
Lubotzky suggested a dynamical decomposition conjecture, namely that the space decomposes up to measure zero to the redundant part, on which the action is ergodic, and the primitive stable part, on which the action is properly discontinuous.

Conjecture 3.4

(Lubotzky)
.

\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)/G=\mathcal{R}(F_{n},G)\cup\mathcal{PS}(F_{n},G)
up to Haar measure zero.

This conjecture is still open even for
\text{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.

4.
Linear algebraic groups

A beautiful conjecture of Zelmanov
[
Ze97
]
states that if
\rho:F_{n}\to\text{GL}(d,{\mathbb{C}})
is a representation such that all primitive
p\in F_{n}
are mapped to elements sharing the same characteristic polynomial, then
\rho(F_{n})
is virtually solvable.
Let us formulate a more general conjecture in the spirit of Question
1.1
.
Let
G
be a simple complex linear algebraic group. We suppose below that
n\geq 3
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that a representation
f:F_{n}\to G
is Zariski redundant if for some proper free factor
A<F_{n}
,
f(A)
is Zariski dense. We let
\mathcal{R}_{n}(G)
denote the set of Zariski redundant representations.

Conjecture 4.2
.

A Zariski dense representation
f:F_{n}\to G
is Zariski redundant. That is
\text{Epi}(F_{n},G)=\mathcal{R}_{n}(G)
.

Let us give two alternative formulations for this conjecture.
Note that
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)\cong G^{n}
inherits a structure of an affine algebraic variety.

Conjecture 4.3
.

Let
\rho\in\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
be a Zariski dense representation. Then the orbit
\text{Aut}(F_{n})\cdot\rho
is Zariski dense in the representation variety
\text{Hom}(F_{n},G)
.

The equivalence between Conjecture
4.3
and Conjecture
4.2
can be deduced from:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
be a simple complex algebraic group.
The set

\{(a,b)\in G^{2}:\langle a,b\rangle~\text{is Zariski dense}\}

contains a Zariski open subset of
G^{2}
.

Proof.

Consider the associative algebra
\mathcal{A}=\text{span}(\text{Ad}(G))
.
By a theorem of Jordan
[
J1878
]
, there is
M=M(G)
such that every finite subgroup of
G
contains a normal abelian subgroup of index at most
M
. Set
m=M!
. Pick

x,y\in G
such that
[x^{m},y^{m}]\neq 1
and
\langle x,y\rangle
is Zariski dense. Let
W_{1},\ldots W_{d}
be words in

A celebrated theorem of Formanek and Procesi

7.
Primitive-unipotent representations

Let
U(d)\subset\text{GL}(d,{\mathbb{C}})
denote the set of
d\times d
unipotent complex matrices. That is
U(d)
is the set of matrices whose characteristic polynomial is
(x-1)^{d}
.
Note that the logarithm restricted to
U(d)
is a polynomial isomorphism between
U(d)
and the set of nilpotent matrices in
M_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
, and its inverse
\exp
is also a polynomial map.
This implies that the cyclic group generated by any non-trivial element
u\in U(d)
is Zariski connected and one dimensional, concretely,
\overline{\langle u\rangle}^{Z}=\exp(\{t\log u:t\in{\mathbb{C}}\})
.

We say that a representation
\rho:F_{n}\to\text{GL}(d,{\mathbb{C}})
is a
unipotent representation
if
\rho(F_{n})\subset U(d)
.
If
\rho
is a unipotent representation then
\rho(F_{n})
is a Zariski connected nilpotent group which can be conjugated into the group of upper triangular unipotents.
This follows from the fact that an algebraic group which is not nilpotent contains a non-trivial semisimple element which is a consequence of the Levi decomposition. In fact if
\rho
is a unipotent representation then the Lie algebra of the Zariski closure of
\rho(F_{n})
is the vector space
\text{span}(\log(\rho(F_{n})))
which consists of nilpotent matrices and form a Nilpotent Lie algebra by Engel’s theorem.

We denote by
\mathcal{U}(n,d)
the variety of
d
-dimensional unipotent representations of
F_{n}
.
We shall say that a representation
\rho:F_{n}\to\text{GL}(d,{\mathbb{C}})
is

if the image of any primitive element is unipotent, that is if
\rho(\mathcal{P}_{n})\subset U(d)
. We shall denote by
\mathcal{PU}(n,d)
the variety of
d
-dimensional primitive-unipotent representations of
F_{n}
. Obviously,
\mathcal{U}(n,d)\subset\mathcal{PU}(n,d)

[

]
whether every primitive-unipotent representation is unipotent, i.e. whether the two varieties coincide.
We conjecture that for

the answer to this questions is positive:

Conjecture 7.1

(Platonov-Potapchik)
.

The two varieties
\mathcal{U}(n,d)
and

We suppose below that

It follows from the last line of the first paragraph of this section that if

Since any non-solvable Lie group admits a semisimple quotient and by Lie’s theorem a connected solvable complex Lie group can be conjugated to the group of upper triangular matrices, Conjecture

Conjecture 7.2

If
\rho:F_{n}\to G

\mathcal{U}(n,d)=\{f^{g}:f\in\text{Hom}(F_{n},N),g\in\text{GL}(d,{\mathbb{C}})\},

Conjecture 7.3

.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
\rho:F_{n}\to\text{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
be a characteristic representation whose type is virtually unipotent. Then
\rho(F_{n})
is virtually solvable.

In view of Theorem
7.7
, Conjecture
8.5
holds for characteristic representations whose type is regular unipotent.
Furthermore, for such representations the image
\rho(F_{n})
is a unipotent group and in particular nilpotent.

9.
Cosets in conjugacy classes

The group
\text{Aut}({F_{n}})
is generated by Nielsen transformations, and the primitive elements of
F_{n}
form a conjugacy class in
\text{Aut}(F_{n})
.
This is one of the motivations to study representations
\rho
of
F_{n}
where
\rho(\mathcal{P}_{n})
is contained in a conjugacy class.
If
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a primitive tuple in
F_{n}
then any element of the form
x_{1}w(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
is primitive, where
w
is a word in
n-1
variables. That is
x_{1}\langle x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle\subset\mathcal{P}_{n}
. Thus, given an algebraic group
G
we are particularly interested in the following:

Problem 9.1
.

Classify pairs
(H,x)
of a subgroup
H\leq G
and an element
x\in G
, such that
xH
is contained in
x^{G}=\{gxg^{-1}:g\in G\}
.

Note that
xH\subset x^{G}\iff Hx\subset x^{G}\iff HxH\subset x^{G}
.

Example 9.2
.

For
G=GL_{2}
the only examples (up to conjugation and replacing
H
with its subgroup) are:

(1)

x
is diagonal,
H
upper unitriangular.

(2)

x
is monomial,
H
is diagonal.

However, already in
\text{GL}_{3}
the picture is much more complicated as we shall demonstrate below.
Yet, we believe that the following always holds:

Conjecture 9.3
.

Let
H\leq G=\text{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
be a group such that for some
x\in G
we have
Hx\subset x^{G}
. Then
H
is virtually solvable.

Remark 9.4
.

Note that the condition
Hx\subset x^{G}
is equivalent to

H\subset[G,x]:=\{gxg^{-1}x^{-1}:g\in G\}.

Let us give two additional formulations for Conjecture
9.3
. The first may suggest
to try to apply dynamics on projective spaces, while the second may suggest
that the problem could be approached by representation theoretic tools.

Conjecture 9.5
.

There is no faithful representation of the free group
\rho:F_{2}\to\text{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
whose image satisfies

\rho(F_{2})\subset[\text{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{C}}),g]

for some
g\in\text{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
.

The equivalence between Conjectures
9.5

and Conjecture
9.3
is due to the Tits alternative, namely, that a linear group is not virtually solvable iff it contains a free subgroup.

A particularly interesting case of Conjecture
9.3
is when restricting to the group
H=\text{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
for which the representation theory is well understood:

Conjecture 9.6
.

There is no nontrivial representation of
\rho:\text{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\to\text{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
whose image satisfies

\rho(\text{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}}))\subset[\text{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{C}}),g]

for some
g\in\text{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Recall that a complex linear algebraic group is not virtually solvable iff it contains
\text{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
as a subgroup.
Note also that the Zariski closure of
[G,g]
coincides with the Hausdorff closure. Thus the following Conjecture implies Conjecture
9.3
:

Conjecture 9.7
.

There is no nontrivial representation of
\text{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
whose image satisfies

\rho(\text{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}}))\subset\overline{[\text{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{C}}),g]}

for some
g\in\text{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Since
[G,g]
is open in its closure, we can differentiate and obtain a formulation in terms of Lie algebras:

Conjecture 9.8
.

There is no faithful linear representation of the Lie algebra
\text{sl}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
whose image is contained in the range of the linear map
\text{Ad}(g)-1

for some
g\in\text{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
.

9.1.
One parameter cosets

As a first step towards Problem
9.1
let us consider cosets of one parameter subgroups in
G=\text{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
. The following quite general result indicates that the examples of such cosets which consist of conjugated elements are very particular.

Proposition 9.9
.

Suppose
H=\{e^{tA}:t\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
for some
A\in\text{sl}_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
. Let the eigenvalues of
A
be
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
. Assume that every partial sum (except the whole sum) of the
\lambda_{i}
’s is non zero. Suppose that
xH\subset x^{G}
. Then
x
is conjugated to
z=\text{diag}(1,\zeta,\zeta^{2},\ldots,\zeta^{n-1})
where
\zeta=1^{\frac{1}{n}}
a primitive
n
’th root of unity.

Proof.

The trace
tr(xe^{tA})
is constant. If we differentiate
k
times and substitute
t=0
, we get
tr(xA^{k})=0
. The matrix
A
is nonsingular, so it satisfies a polynomial with nonzero constant term. Hence
tr(x)=0
. The same argument applied to wedge powers gives that
tr(\bigwedge^{m}x)=0
, and hence the characteristic polynomial of
x
is
t^{n}-c
with
c=det(x)
. Moreover, supposing
x\in\text{SL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, we deduce that
x\sim z
.
∎

Another case of a particular interest is when
x
is unipotent.
We are particularly interested in understanding cosets of subgroups which consist of unipotent elements. Here is one simple observation:

Proposition 9.10
.

Let
H\leq\text{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
be a group such that for some
x\in\text{SL}_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
we have
xH\subset{U}(d)
.
Then every element of
H
has
1
as an eigenvalue.

Indeed, for
A\in H
look at the characteristic polynomial
P_{A}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}t^{i}
. Then

0=tr(xP_{A}(A))=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}\text{tr}(xA^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}d\Rightarrow P_{A}(1)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}=0.

Such a result is useful. For instance one may deduce that

{U}(4)
contains no coset of a non-virtually-solvable subgroup
H
.
To see this, we may argue by contradiction supposing,
without loss of generality, that
H
is isomorphic to
\text{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
. Since
H
is simple the space
{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
decomposes to a direct sum of
H
-irreducible subspaces. By Proposition
9.10
every element of
H
has an eigenvalue
1
. This excludes the case where
{\mathbb{C}}^{4}={\mathbb{C}}^{2}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
with
H=\text{SL}_{2}
acts via the standard representation on each factor. In all other cases it is not hard to show that for every nontrivial unipotent
u
there is
g\in H
such that either
ug
or
u^{-1}g
is proximal, hence satisfies the condition of Lemma
7.5
, and therefore is non-unipotent (see
[
BG03
]
for the definition and some analysis of proximal elements).

However the assumption that
uH\subset\mathcal{U}(d)
does not imply that
H
is unipotent. The following example was shown to me by M. Larsen.

Example 9.11

(A coset of a torus consisting of unipotents)
.

Let

u=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1&3\\
0&3&8\\
-1&0&0\end{matrix}\right),h=\left(\begin{matrix}t&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&t^{-1}\end{matrix}\right).

Then one can compute that

det(uh)=1~\text{and}~tr(uh)=tr((uh)^{2})=3.

It follows that
uh
is unipotent for every
t\neq 0
.

Inspired by Larsen’s example we can construct similar semisimple examples:

Example 9.12
.

There are two conjugate semisimple elements
a,b
such that
ab^{n}
is conjugate to
a
for every
n
: Take

a=\left(\begin{matrix}0&-1&2\\
0&-4&\frac{15}{2}\\
2&0&0\end{matrix}\right)~\text{and}~b=\left(\begin{matrix}t&&\\
&1&\\
&&t^{-1}\end{matrix}\right).

Then
det(ab),tr(ab),tr((ab)^{2})
are independent of
t
, so
ab^{n}
are all conjugate. Moreover,
tr(a)=-4,det(a)=1
, and
a
has an eigenvalue 1. Therefore it is conjugate to
b
for some value of
t
.

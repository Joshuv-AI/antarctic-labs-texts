Improved Trade-offs Between Amortization and Download Bandwidth for Linear HSS
Keller Blackwell, Mary Wootters

Abstract
A Homomorphic Secret Sharing (HSS) scheme is a secret-sharing scheme that shares a secret x x among s s servers, and additionally allows an output client to reconstruct some function f ⁡ ( x ) f(x) using information that can be locally computed by each server. A key parameter in HSS schemes is download rate , which quantifies how much information the output client needs to download from the servers. Often, download rate is improved by amortizing over ℓ \ell instances of the problem, making ℓ \ell also a key parameter of interest. Recent work [ FIKW22 ] established a limit on the download rate of linear HSS schemes for computing low-degree polynomials and constructed schemes that achieve this optimal download rate; their schemes required amortization over ℓ = Ω ⁡ ( s ​ log ⁡ ( s ) ) \ell=\Omega(s\log(s)) instances of the problem. Subsequent work [ BW23 ] completely characterized linear HSS schemes that achieve optimal download rate in terms of a coding-theoretic notion termed optimal labelweight codes . A consequence of this characterization was that ℓ = Ω ⁡ ( s ​ log ⁡ ( s ) ) \ell=\Omega(s\log(s)) is in fact necessary to achieve optimal download rate. In this paper, we characterize all linear HSS schemes, showing that schemes of any download rate are equivalent to a generalization of optimal labelweight codes. This equivalence is constructive and provides a way to obtain an explicit linear HSS scheme from any linear code. Using this characterization, we present explicit linear HSS schemes with slightly sub-optimal rate but with much improved amortization ℓ = O ⁡ ( s ) \ell=O(s) . Our constructions are based on algebraic geometry codes (specifically Hermitian codes and Goppa codes).

The converse requires showing that any labelweight code

implies a linear HSS scheme. In the setting of optimal download rate,

4
Linear HSS from Hermitian Codes

Linear HSS schemes presented by
[
FIKW22
]
,
[
BW23
]
both achieve optimal download rate

but require large amortization parameters to do so.
[
FIKW22
]
showed it was sufficient to take amortization parameter
\ell=(s-dt)\log(s)=O(s\log(s))
, and
[
BW23
]
proved that such an amortization parameter is in fact necessary in many parameter regimes, and is off by at most 1 otherwise.

It is a natural to ask whether linear HSS schemes that achieve a better trade-off between download rate and amortization parameter exist.
Specifically, can we make minor concessions to download rate and save substantially on the amortization needed?

Through the lens of Theorem
12
, this is equivalent to asking whether there exists a labelweight code with minimum labelweight
\geq dt+1
that achieves good rate at low dimension. A natural first attempt at an existential result would be via random coding; specifically, building a linear HSS scheme by starting with a random linear code and following the construction of Theorem
12
. Unfortunately (and perhaps surprisingly!), we show in Appendix
A
that this results in strictly worse parameters than
[
FIKW22
]
,
[
BW23
]
.

In the following sections we take a different approach and base our labelweight codes on well-studied codes from algebraic geometry. In particular, this section constructs a family of linear HSS schemes from Hermitian codes; notably, such schemes achieve asymptotically optimal download rate while requiring an amortization parameter that is only linear in
s
.

Theorem 16

Let

be positive integers and

,

, and

In this section we show that the natural random coding approach does not appear to yield linear HSS schemes that meaningfully outperform the
\ell=O(s\log(s))

[

[

[

A.1
Notation

To justify the notion that “random labelweight codes don’t outperform Reed-Solomon codes in linear HSS amortization”, we proceed by generalizing the well-known Gilbert-Varshamov Bound to the labelweight setting.
One standard proof of this result (see, e.g.,
[
GRS19
]
) analyzes the distance of a random linear code, and we follow the same path here. We first introduce some notation.

Definition 12

(Labelweight Ball)
.

Let
L:[n]\to[s]
be a surjective labeling. We define the labelweight ball
B_{L}(r)
of radius
0\leq r\leq n
to be the set

B_{L}(r):=\left\{c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\;:\;\Delta_{L}(c)\leq r\right\}

and define the volume of the labelweight ball to be
\mathrm{Vol}_{L}(r)=|B_{L}(r)|
.

For the purposes of our analysis, we consider only a fixed labeling function.

Assumption 1
.

Let
n,s,w\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
n=sw
. In this section we will only consider the labeling

L:[n]\to[s],\;x\mapsto\left\lceil\frac{x}{s}\right\rceil.

When paired with a code of length
n
, this balanced labeling simply labels the first
w
coordinates with 1, the second
w
coordinates with 2, and continues analogously until the last
w
coordinates are labeled with
s
. Intuitively, for fixed
n,s\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, such a balanced labeling pattern maximizes labelweight in expectation over random linear codes.

Under this fixed, balanced labeling function of Assumption
1
, we have the following algebraic formulation of labelweight ball volume.

Observation 23
.

Let
n,s,w,r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
n=sw
and
0\leq r\leq n
. Then

\mathrm{Vol}_{L}(r)=|B_{L}(r)|=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{s}{i}\left(q^{w}-1\right)^{i}.

Finally, we will need to define relative labelweight for labelweight codes, which is the natural analogue of relative minimum distance for linear codes.

Definition 13

(Relative Labelweight)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be a linear code and
L:[n]\to[s]
a surjective labeling such that
\Delta_{L}(\mathcal{C})=d
. We define the relative labelweight of
\mathcal{C}
to be
\delta=d/s
.

A.2
Generalization of
q
ary Entropy

In the standard proof of the Gilbert-Varshamov bound, the volume of a Hamming ball is estimated by the
q
-ary entropy function. To generalize the proof to labelweight, we introduce the following generalization of the
q
-ary entropy function, which captures the volume of labelweight balls.

Definition 14

(Generalized
q
-ary Entropy)
.

Let
q\geq 2
,
w\geq 1
. For
x\in(0,1)
, we denote by
H_{q,w}(x)
the generalized
q
-ary entropy function:

H_{q,w}(x)=x\log_{q}(q^{w}-1)-x\log_{q}(x)-(1-x)\log_{q}(1-x),

where
H_{q,w}(0),H_{q,w}(1)
are defined as the limit of
H_{q,w}
as
x\to 0,1
, respectively.

Note that the case where
w=1
is the standard
q
-ary entropy function. We notice that, when properly normalized, the generalized entropy function can be approximated linearly.

Observation 24
.

For all
x\in[0,1-1/q^{w}]
,
x\leq w^{-1}H_{q,w}(x)\leq x+\log_{q}(2^{1/w})
.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle g(x):=w^{-1}H_{q,w}(x)-x

\displaystyle=w^{-1}\left(x\log_{q}(q^{w}-1)-x\log_{q}(x)-(1-x)\log_{q}(1-x)\right)-x

\displaystyle\leq w^{-1}\left(-x\log_{q}(x)-(1-x)\log_{q}(1-x)\right).

Since
-x\log_{q}(x)-(1-x)\log_{q}(1-x)
is a concave function which attains its maximal value when
x=1/2
, it follows that
w^{-1}H_{q,w}(x)-x\leq w^{-1}\log_{q}(2)
as desired. The lower bound follows from observing that
g
is itself a concave function, since

g^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\frac{1}{w\cdot x\cdot(1-x)\cdot\ln(q)}\leq 0\;\forall x\in[0,1-1/q^{w}]

and that its values at the endpoints of the domain
[0,1-1/q^{w}]
are non-negative; i.e.,
g(0),g(1-1/q^{w})\geq 0
.
∎

Equipped with this definition, our goal becomes to express the volume of a given labelweight ball in terms of the generalized entropy function. To do so, we note two helpful relations; we omit the proofs, which are elementary algebraic manipulations.

Observation 25
.

Let
s,p\in\mathbb{R}
such that
s,p\geq 0
. Then

q^{-sH_{q,w}(p)}=(1-p)^{(1-p)s}\left(\frac{p}{q^{w}-1}\right)^{ps}

Observation 26
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
p\in[0,1)
satisfy
0\leq p\leq 1-1/q^{w}
. Then

\frac{p}{(1-p)(q^{w}-1)}\leq 1.

We now give the volume of a labelweight ball in terms of the generalized entropy function.

Lemma 27
.

Let
s,w\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
p\in[0,1)
satisfy
0\leq p\leq 1-1/q^{w}
and
ps\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then

\mathrm{Vol}_{L}(ps)\leq q^{sH_{q,w}(p)}.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=(p+(1-p))^{s}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\binom{s}{i}p^{i}(1-p)^{s-i}\geq\sum_{i=0}^{ps}\binom{s}{i}p^{i}(1-p)^{s-i}.

Multiplying through by
1=(q^{w}-1)^{i}/(q^{w}-1)^{i}
and applying Observation

26
yields

1\geq\sum_{i=0}^{ps}\binom{s}{i}(q^{w}-1)^{i}(1-p)^{s}\left(\frac{p}{(1-p)(q^{w}-1)}\right)^{ps}.

Finally, applying Observation
25
yields

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=0}^{ps}\binom{s}{i}(q^{w}-1)^{i}q^{-sH_{q,w}(p)}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Vol}_{L}(ps)q^{-sH_{q,w}(p)}.

∎

Abstract
. We extend the results of [ GPS19 ] on characterization of conjugacy, isomorphism, and continuous orbit equivalence of ℤ d {\mathbb{Z}}^{d} -odometers to dimensions d > 2 d>2 . We then apply these extensions to the case of odometers defined by matrices with integer coefficients.



1.
Introduction

In this note we first supply an argument that extends the main result of the paper “
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-odometers and cohomology” by T. Giordano, I. F. Putnam, and C. F. Skau
[
GPS19
, Theorem 1.5 (1), (2), (3)]
to dimensions greater than 2. More precisely, the proofs of conjugacy, isomorphism, and continuous orbit equivalence characterizations of
[
GPS19
, Theorem 1.5]
are based on
[
GPS19
, Theorem 4.4]
, the only result in that paper where the dimension restriction
d=1,2
is important. Our Proposition
2.3
below lifts this restriction and therefore leads to the characterizations in arbitrary dimension
d\geq 1
. In turn, our proof of Proposition
2.3
uses Lemma
2.1
that made the relevant computations of the image of the first cohomology group under a natural map simple and possible in higher dimensions.
In the last section we apply these results along with the earlier results by the second author
[
S22a
]
in the setting of odometers defined by matrices with integer coefficients.
Below we use the notations and terminology from
[
GPS19
]
,
[
S22
]
, and
[
S22a
]
and refer to these papers for more details.

If
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
G
is a subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, the group
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
acts on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}/G
by

(1.1)

\phi_{G}^{k}(l+G)=k+l+G,\quad k,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}=\{G_{1},G_{2},\dots\}
be a decreasing sequence of subgroups of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
of finite index,
i.e.
,

{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}=G_{1}\supseteq G_{2}\supseteq\cdots,\quad[{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\colon G_{n}]<\infty,\ n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\supseteq G_{n}\supseteq G_{n+1}
, let
q_{n}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}/G_{n+1}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}/G_{n}
denote the quotient map
q_{n}(k+G_{n+1})=k+G_{n},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}=\{G_{1},G_{2},\dots\}
be as above and let
X_{{\mathcal{G}}}
denote the inverse limit system

{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}/G_{1}\xlongleftarrow{q_{1}}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}/G_{2}\xlongleftarrow{q_{2}}\cdots.

A
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-
odometer
is a pair
(X_{{\mathcal{G}}},\phi_{\mathcal{G}})
, where
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}
is the natural action of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
on

X_{{\mathcal{G}}}
induced by
\phi_{G_{n}}^{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
The natural projection from
X_{{\mathcal{G}}}
to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}/G_{n}
is denoted by
\pi_{n}
.

If
G_{n}\neq G_{n+1}
for infinitely many
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then
X_{\mathcal{G}}
is a Cantor set and
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}
is a minimal action of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
on it, which is free if and only if
\cap_{n=1}^{\infty}G_{n}=\{0\}
. The action
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}
is also isometric in the metric
d_{\mathcal{G}}
given by

d_{\mathcal{G}}(x,y)=\sup\{0,1/n\,\,|\,\,\pi_{n}(x)\neq\pi_{n}(y)\},\quad x,y\in X_{\mathcal{G}}.

Moreover,
X_{\mathcal{G}}
supports a unique
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}
-invariant probability measure
\mu_{\mathcal{G}}
such that

\mu_{\mathcal{G}}\left(\pi_{n}^{-1}(k+G_{n})\right)=\frac{1}{[{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\colon G_{n}]},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

In
[
GPS19
]
the authors proposed a system equivalent to
(X_{\mathcal{G}},\phi_{\mathcal{G}})
in the sense of conjugacy defined below, using Pontryagin duality.
Namely, let
H
be a subgroup of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
so that
H
contains
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Let
Y_{H}=\widehat{H/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
be the Pontryagin dual of the quotient. Here, the groups
H
and
H/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
are endowed with the discrete topology, and hence
Y_{H}
is compact.
Let
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
denote the
d
-torus and let
\rho:H/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
be the map induced by the inclusion
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
i.e.
,
\rho\left((r_{1},\dots,r_{d})+{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\right)=\left(e^{2\pi ir_{1}},\dots,e^{2\pi ir_{d}}\right)
,

(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})\in H
. Identifying the Pontryagin dual of
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
with
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, we have the dual map

\hat{\rho}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\rightarrow Y_{H}
. Then the action
\psi_{H}
of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
on
Y_{H}
is defined via

\psi_{H}^{k}(x)=x+\hat{\rho}(k),\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\ x\in Y_{H}.

The action
(Y_{H},\psi_{H})
is free if and only if
H
is dense in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
X,Y
be topological spaces, let
G
be a group, and let
\phi
,
\psi
be actions of
G
on
X
,
Y
, respectively, by homeomorphisms.
The actions
(X,\phi)
and
(Y,\psi)
of
G
are said to be
conjugate
if there exists a homeomorphism
h\colon X\to Y
such that

h\circ\phi^{g}=\psi^{g}\circ h,

for all
g\in G
. In this case, we refer to
h
as a
conjugacy
between the two actions.

If
H
is an increasing union of finite index extensions
H_{n}
of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then, up to conjugacy,
(Y_{H},\psi_{H})
is the inverse limit of

(1.2)

(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}})\xlongleftarrow{\widehat{i_{1}}}(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})\xlongleftarrow{\widehat{i_{2}}}\cdots,

where
i_{n}\colon H_{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\to H_{n+1}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
is the inclusion,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
The correspondence between
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-odometers and
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions
(Y_{H},\psi_{H})
, up to conjugacy, is established by realizing
(Y_{H},\psi_{H})
as an inverse limit (
1.2
) and by passing to dual lattices; see
[
GPS19
, Theorems 2.5, 2.6]
. Here, if
K
is a lattice in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, its dual lattice
K^{*}
is

K^{*}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,\,|\,\,\langle k,x\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}\ {\rm for\ all}\ k\in K\}.

We now consider odometers associated to integer matrices.
For a non-singular
d\times d
-matrix
A
with integer coefficients,
A\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
, define

G_{A}=\left.\left\{A^{-k}{x}\,\,\right|\,{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\},\quad{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\subseteq G_{A}\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}.

One can readily check that
G_{A}
is a subgroup of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
.
Applying the process described above to the group
H=G_{A}
, we get an associated
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-odometer
Y_{G_{A}}
, defined up to conjugacy.
In
[
S22
]
, the second author classified groups
G_{A}
in the
2
-dimensional case and applied the results to
2
-dimensional odometers
Y_{G_{A}}
using

[
GPS19
, Theorem 1.5]
. In
[
S22a
]
,
it was studied when
G_{A}
,
G_{B}
are isomorphic as abstract groups for non-singular
A,B\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
for an arbitrary
d
. We combine the results from
[
S22a
]
with the results of this paper to analyze when
Y_{G_{A}}
,
Y_{G_{B}}
are equivalent with respect to conjugacy, isomorphism, continuously orbit equivalence, and orbit equivalence.

2.
Conjugacy and Isomorphism

In what follows,
\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
denotes the ring of
d\times d
-matrices with integer coefficients and

\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
denotes the group of non-singular matrices
A\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
with
\det A=\pm 1
.
Proposition
2.3
below extends
[
GPS19
, Theorem 4.4]
to higher dimensions and its proof requires the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
G
be a subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
of finite index. Then for each
i=1,\dots,d
, there exists a free basis
\mathcal{B}=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\}
of
G
such that
f_{i}=a_{i}e_{i}
, where
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
e_{i}
is the
i
-th vector of the standard basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Equivalently, for any non-singular
M\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
and each
i=1,\dots,d
, there exists

P(i)\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
such that the
i
-th column of
MP(i)
is
a_{i}e_{i}
for some
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Since
G
is a subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
of finite index,
G
is a free group of rank
d
. Thus, the columns of
M
form a basis of
G
.
We first prove the following:

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
j
there exists a basis
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}
of
G
such that the
j
-th component
[g_{l}]_{j}

of
g_{l}
is zero for any
l=2,\ldots,d
and
[g_{1}]_{j}=a_{j}
,
a_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Equivalently, the
j
-th row of the matrix
\left(\begin{matrix}g_{1}&g_{2}&\ldots&g_{d}\end{matrix}\right)

(
we write coordinates of each
g_{i}
as a column
)
is
\left(\begin{matrix}a_{j}&0&\ldots&0\end{matrix}\right)
.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Let
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}
be an arbitrary basis of
G
and
let

M=\left(\begin{matrix}g_{1}&g_{2}&\ldots&g_{d}\end{matrix}\right)=(g_{ik}),

M\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
is non-singular. Note that there exists at least one non-zero element in the
j
-th row of
M
, since

\det M\neq 0
. Assume there are two non-zero elements in the
j
-th row of
M
. Without loss of generality (we can interchange columns of
M
),
g_{j1}\neq 0
,
g_{j2}\neq 0
. By multiplying
g_{1}
,
g_{2}
by
-1
if necessary, we can assume
g_{j1}>0
,
g_{j2}>0
. If
g_{j1}=g_{j2}
, then

g^{\prime}_{1},\dots,g^{\prime}_{d}
is a new basis of
G
with
g^{\prime}_{2}=g_{2}-g_{1}
,
g^{\prime}_{l}=g_{l}
,
l\neq 2
, and
[g^{\prime}_{2}]_{j}=0
.
If
g_{j1}\neq g_{j2}
, then without loss of generality, we can assume
g_{j1}>g_{j2}
.
Let
a_{1}=g_{j1}
,
a_{2}=g_{j2}
, and
a_{1}=a_{2}k+a_{3}
, for some
k,a_{3}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
0\leq a_{3}<a_{2}
. Then

g^{\prime}_{1},\dots,g^{\prime}_{d}
is a new basis of
G
with
g^{\prime}_{1}=g_{2}
,
g^{\prime}_{2}=g_{1}-kg_{2}
,
g^{\prime}_{l}=g_{l}
,
l\neq 1,2
, and

[g^{\prime}_{1}]_{j}=a_{2}
,
[g^{\prime}_{2}]_{j}=a_{3}
,
0\leq a_{3}<a_{2}<a_{1}
. Continuing this way, we get a sequence

0\leq\cdots<a_{h}<\cdots<a_{3}<a_{2}<a_{1}
of non-negative integer numbers, which in finitely many steps has to reach zero (this is essentially the Euclidean algorithm). This shows that there exists a basis

g^{\prime}_{1},\dots,g^{\prime}_{d}
of
G
such that
[g^{\prime}_{2}]_{j}=0
. Repeating the process for any other

g^{\prime}_{s}
,
g^{\prime}_{t}
with
s\neq t
,
[g^{\prime}_{s}]_{j}\neq 0
,
[g^{\prime}_{t}]_{j}\neq 0
, we conclude that there exists a basis

g^{\prime\prime}_{1},\dots,g^{\prime\prime}_{d}
of
G
such that
[g^{\prime\prime}_{1}]_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
[g^{\prime}_{l}]_{j}=0
for any
l\neq 1
.
∎

We now use induction on
d
to prove Lemma
2.1
. Let
d=1
. Then
e_{1}=1
,
G=a{\mathbb{Z}}
for some
a>0
,
f_{1}=ae_{1}
, and the claim follows. Let
d>1
. We consider two cases:
i>1
and
i=1
. Let
i>1
. By Lemma
2.2
applied to
j=1
, there exists a basis
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}
of
G
such that

M=\left(\begin{matrix}g_{1}&\ldots&g_{d}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1}&0\\
*&M^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right),\quad\quad a_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}},\,\,M^{\prime}\in\operatorname{M}_{d-1}({\mathbb{Z}}),\,\,\det M^{\prime}\neq 0.

By induction on
d
, there exists
S\in\operatorname{GL}_{d-1}({\mathbb{Z}})
such that
the
(i-1)
-st column of
M^{\prime}S
is
be^{\prime}_{i-1}
, where
b\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
e^{\prime}_{i-1}
is the

(i-1)
-st vector of the standard basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}
.
Let

P=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
0&S\end{matrix}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}}).

Then the
i
-th column of
MP

is
be_{i}
and the claim follows for
i>1
.

Let
i=1
. By Lemma
2.2
applied to
j=d
, there exists a basis
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}
of
G
such that

M=\left(\begin{matrix}g_{d}&\ldots&g_{1}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}M^{\prime\prime}&*\\
0&a_{d}\end{matrix}\right),\quad\quad a_{d}\in{\mathbb{N}},\,\,M^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{M}_{d-1}({\mathbb{Z}}),\,\,\det M^{\prime\prime}\neq 0.

As in the case
i>1
, we apply the induction on
d
to
M^{\prime\prime}
. Thus, there exists

S^{\prime}\in\operatorname{GL}_{d-1}({\mathbb{Z}})
such that
the
1
-st column of
M^{\prime\prime}S^{\prime}
is
b^{\prime}e^{\prime}_{1}
, where
b^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
e^{\prime}_{1}
is the

1
-st vector of the standard basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}
. Let

P^{\prime}=\left(\begin{matrix}S^{\prime}&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}}).

Then the
1
-st column of
MP^{\prime}

is
b^{\prime}e_{1}

and the claim follows in the case
i=1
as well.
∎

For a system
(X,\phi)
, where
X
is a topological space and
\phi
is an action of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
on
X
by homeomorphisms, the first cohomology group
H^{1}(X,\phi)
is defined as follows. A 1-cocycle
\theta
is a continuous function
\theta\colon X\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\theta(x,m+n)=\theta(x,m)+\theta(\phi^{m}(x),n),\quad x\in X,\ m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

A 1-cocycle
\theta
is a coboundary if and only if there exists a continuous function
h\colon X\to{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\theta(x,n)=h\left(\phi^{n}(x)\right)-h(x),\quad x\in X,\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

Let
Z^{1}(X,\phi)
and
B^{1}(X,\phi)
denote the groups of 1-cocycles and coboundaries, respectively, and let
H^{1}(X,\phi)=Z^{1}(X,\phi)/B^{1}(X,\phi)
be the first cohomology group.

We now recall the definition of the map
\tau_{\mu}^{1}
, where
\mu
is an invariant probability measure on
a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-action
(X,\phi)
. If
\theta
is a 1-cocycle,

\tau_{\mu}^{1}(\theta)\in{\rm Hom}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
is given by

\tau_{\mu}^{1}(\theta)(n)=\int_{X}\theta(x,n)\,d\mu(x),\quad n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

Since
\tau_{\mu}^{1}(\theta)=0
if
\theta
is a coboundary,
\tau_{\mu}^{1}
passes to a well-defined group homomorphism

(2.1)

\tau_{\mu}^{1}\colon H^{1}(X,\phi)\to{\rm Hom}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}).

The space
{\rm Hom}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
is identified with
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
via the map that takes
\alpha\in{\rm Hom}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}})
to
(\alpha(e_{1}),\dots,\alpha(e_{d}))\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

We also denote by
B_{\mu}^{1}(X,\phi)
the group of 1-cocycles
\theta(x,n)\in Z^{1}(X,\phi)
such that

\tau_{\mu}^{1}(\theta)=0,

and by
H_{\mu}^{1}(X,\phi)
the group
Z^{1}(X,\phi)/B_{\mu}^{1}(X,\phi)
. The group
H_{\mu}^{1}(X,\phi)
is a quotient of
H^{1}(X,\phi)
. By definition of
B_{\mu}^{1}(X,\phi)
, the map
\tau_{\mu}^{1}
factors through the quotient
H_{\mu}^{1}(X,\phi)
, and, by abuse of notation, we denote the resulting map also by
\tau_{\mu}^{1}
:

(2.2)

\tau_{\mu}^{1}\colon H^{1}_{\mu}(X,\phi)\to{\rm Hom}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
H
be a dense subgroup of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
such that
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\subseteq H
. Let
\mu
be the unique invariant probability measure for
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-action
(Y_{H},\psi_{H})
. Then the map

\tau_{\mu}^{1}\colon H_{\mu}^{1}(Y_{H},\psi_{H})\to H

given by (
2.2
) is an isomorphism.

Proof.

We supplement the proof of
[
GPS19
, Theorem 4.4]
with Lemma
2.1
above. This allows to simplify computations and also extends
[
GPS19
, Theorem 4.4]
to an arbitrary dimension.

It is immediate from the definitions above that
\tau_{\mu}^{1}
is an injective group homomorphism.
We write
H=\cup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}H_{n}
, the union of an increasing sequence of finite index extensions
H_{n}
of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Let
G_{n}=H_{n}^{*}
be the dual lattice. Then
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},G_{2},\dots\}
is a decresing sequence of finite index subgroups of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
such that
(Y_{H},\psi_{H})
and
(X_{\mathcal{G}},\phi_{\mathcal{G}})
are conjugate.

The group
H^{1}(Y_{H},\psi_{H})
is the direct limit of the sequence
H^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}/G_{n},\phi_{G_{n}})
, where
\phi_{G_{n}}
is the natural action of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}/G_{n}
given by (
1.1
),
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Therefore, if
[\theta]\in H_{\mu}^{1}(Y_{H},\psi_{H})
, then
\theta
is a cocycle in
Z^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}/G_{n},\phi_{G_{n}})
for some
n
. We can write

(2.3)

\tau_{\mu}^{1}(\theta)=[{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\colon G_{n}]^{-1}\sum_{k\in F}\left(\theta(k+G_{n},e_{1}),\dots,\theta(k+G_{n},e_{d})\right),

where
F
is a fundamental domain for
G_{n}
. Since
\theta(k+G_{n},e_{i})\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for any
i=1,\dots,d
, we have

\tau_{\mu}^{1}(\theta)\in H_{n}
and hence
\tau_{\mu}^{1}(\theta)\in H
.

Clearly, to show that
\tau_{\mu}^{1}\colon H_{\mu}^{1}(Y_{H},\psi_{H})\to H
given by (
2.2
) is surjective, it is enough to show that
\tau_{\mu}^{1}\colon H^{1}(Y_{H},\psi_{H})\to H
given by (
2.1
) is surjective.
Let
h\in H
. Then
h\in H_{n}
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We show that there exists
[\theta]\in H^{1}(Y_{H},\psi_{H})
such that
\tau_{\mu}^{1}(\theta)=h
.
According to
[
GPS19
, Lemma 4.2]
, for each
\theta\in Z^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}/G_{n},\phi_{G_{n}})
, the map
\alpha(\theta)
given by

\alpha(\theta)(g)=\theta(G_{n},g),\quad g\in G_{n},

induces an isomorphism

\alpha\colon H^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}/G_{n},\phi_{G_{n}})\to{\rm Hom}(G_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
. The group
{\rm Hom}(G_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
is identified with
H_{n}
via the canonical inner product
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and thus there exists
\theta\in Z^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}/G_{n},\phi_{G_{n}})
with
\alpha(\theta)=\langle\cdot\,,h\rangle
.

It remains to show
\tau_{\mu}^{1}(\theta)=h
,
i.e.
,

(2.4)

\sum_{k\in F}\theta(k+G_{n},e_{i})=[{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\colon G_{n}]\langle e_{i},h\rangle\quad{\rm for\ each}\ i=1,\dots,d.

For each fixed
i=1,\dots,d
, we apply Lemma
2.1
to find a basis
\mathcal{B}=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\}
for
G_{n}
such that
f_{i}=ae_{i}
, where
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
We choose a fundamental domain
F
for
G_{n}
in Equation (
2.4
) to consist of elements from
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
inside a parallelepiped in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
determined by the basis
\mathcal{B}
. Namely,

F=\{t_{1}f_{1}+\dots+t_{d}f_{d}\,\,|\,\,0\leq t_{1},\dots,t_{d}<1\}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

The map
\theta
is defined by

\theta(k_{1}+G_{n},k_{2}+g)=\langle g^{\prime},h\rangle,

for
k_{1},k_{2}\in F,g\in G_{n}
, where
g^{\prime}\in G_{n}
is the unique element such that
k_{1}+k_{2}+g=k^{\prime}+g^{\prime}
and
k^{\prime}\in F
.
One can check that so defined,
\theta
is a 1-cocycle.
To compute the left-hand side of (
2.4
), we choose
k_{1}=k\in F
,
k_{2}=e_{i}
, and
g=0
. Note that
k_{2}\in F
, because of our choice of
F
. If
k\in F
is such that
k+e_{i}
is also in
F
, then
g^{\prime}=0
, and thus the term
\theta(k+G_{n},e_{i})
does not contribute anything to the sum in (
2.4
).

Now assume
k\in F
is such that
k+e_{i}
is not in
F
. Let
F_{i}
denote the set of all such
k
.
In this case
g^{\prime}=f_{i}
, because
k^{\prime}=k+e_{i}-f_{i}\in F
. Indeed, let
P_{k,i}
consist of all elements
n\in F
such that for each
j=1,\dots,d,\ j\neq i
, the
j
-th component of
n
coincides with the
j
-th component of
k
. Since
F
is a parallelepiped with side
f_{i}=ae_{i},\ a\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
i
-th coordinates of elements
n\in P_{k,i}
form a sequence of
a
consecutive integers
m,m+1,\dots,m+a-1
.
Now, by the choice of
k
, its
i
-th component is
m+a-1
. For
j=1,\dots,d,\ j\neq i
, the
j
-th component of
k^{\prime}
equals the
j
-th component of
k
. The
i
-th component of
k^{\prime}
equals
m
, and thus
k^{\prime}\in P_{k,i}\subseteq F
.

We conclude that
\theta(k+G_{n},e_{i})=\langle f_{i},h\rangle=a\langle e_{i},h\rangle
for each
k\in F_{i}
.
The number of elements
k
in
F_{i}
equals the number of elements in the projection
P_{i}
of
F
to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}
obtained from
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
by omitting the
i
-th coordinate.
Indeed, by omitting the
i
-th coordinate of
k\in F_{i}\subseteq F
, we get an element in
P_{i}
. Conversely, for any
k_{i}\in P_{i}
, the
i
-th coordinates of all elements in
F
that project to
k_{i}
form a sequence
m,m+1,\dots,m+a-1
, since
F
is a parallelepiped with side
f_{i}=ae_{i},\ a\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The element
k
that projects to
k_{i}
and whose
i
-th coordinate is
m+a-1
is in
F_{i}
.
The projection
P_{i}
is itself a parallelepiped in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}
. Let
b
denote the number of elements in
P_{i}
, which is the same as the number of elements in
F_{i}
. We therefore conclude

\sum_{k\in F}\theta(k+G_{n},e_{i})=\sum_{k\in F_{i}}\theta(k+G_{n},e_{i})=ab\langle e_{i},h\rangle.

Note that
ab
is the number of elements in
F
, which is equal to
[{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\colon G_{n}]
. Thus, (
2.4
) holds.
∎

A characterization of a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-action
(Y_{H},\psi_{H})
up to conjugacy now follows from Proposition
2.3
and the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
If
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions
(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}})
and
(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})
are conjugate, then

\tau_{\mu_{1}}^{1}\left(H_{\mu_{1}}^{1}(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}})\right)=\tau_{\mu_{2}}^{1}\left(H_{\mu_{2}}^{1}(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})\right),

where
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
are the unique probability measures for
(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}}),(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})
, respectively.

Proof.

Let
h
be a conjugacy from
(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}})
to
(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})
. It induces an isomorphism

h^{*}\colon H_{\mu_{2}}^{1}(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})\to H_{\mu_{1}}^{1}(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}})

given by
h^{*}([\theta])=[h^{*}(\theta)]
, where
h^{*}(\theta(y,n))=\theta(h(x),n)
,
y=h(x)
,
x\in Y_{H_{1}}
.
The invariance of
\mu_{1}
and uniqueness of
\mu_{2}
imply
h_{*}(\mu_{1})=\mu_{2}
, where
h_{*}(\mu_{1})
denotes the push-forward measure of
\mu_{1}
under
h
.
Therefore, for
[\theta(y,n)]\in H_{\mu_{2}}^{1}(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})
, one has

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu_{2}}^{1}(\theta)(n)

\displaystyle=\int_{Y_{H_{2}}}\theta(y,n)\,d\mu_{2}=\int_{Y_{H_{2}}}\theta(y,n)\,dh_{*}(\mu_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{Y_{H_{1}}}\theta(h(x),n)\,d\mu_{1}=\tau_{\mu_{1}}^{1}(h^{*}(\theta))(n).

∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
H_{1},H_{2}
be dense subgroups of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
such that
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\subseteq H_{1}
,
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\subseteq H_{2}
.
Two
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions
(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}})
and
(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})
are conjugate if and only if
H_{1}=H_{2}
.

Proof.

If
H_{1}=H_{2}
, then the conjugacy is trivial. Assume
(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}})
and
(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})
are conjugate. By Proposition
2.3
,

\tau_{\mu_{1}}^{1}\left(H_{\mu_{1}}^{1}(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}})\right)=H_{1},\quad\tau_{\mu_{2}}^{1}\left(H^{1}_{\mu_{2}}(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})\right)=H_{2},

where
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are the unique invariant probability measures for
(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}})
and
(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})
, respectively. By Lemma
2.4
, we have
H_{1}=H_{2}
.
∎

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(X,\phi)
be an action of a group
G
and let
(Y,\psi)
be an action of a group
H
. An
isomorphism
between the actions is a pair
(h,\alpha)
, where
h\colon X\to Y
is a homeomorphism and
\alpha\colon G\to H
is a group isomorphism, such that

h\circ\phi^{g}=\psi^{\alpha(g)}\circ h.

If such a pair
(h,\alpha)
exists, then
(X,\phi)
and
(Y,\psi)
are said to be
isomorphic
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
d_{1},d_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
H_{1}

(
resp.,
H_{2})
be a dense subgroup of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d_{1}}

(
resp.,
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d_{2}})
that contains

{\mathbb{Z}}^{d_{1}}

(
resp.,
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d_{2}})
.
A
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d_{1}}
-action
(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}})
is isomorphic to a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d_{2}}
-action
(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})
if and only if
d_{1}=d_{2}
, the common value being denoted by
d
, and there exists
A\in{\rm GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
such that
AH_{1}=H_{2}
.

Proof.

If
d=d_{1}=d_{2}
and
AH_{1}=H_{2}
for some
A\in{\rm GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
, then
(Y_{AH_{1}},\psi_{AH_{1}})
is trivially conjugate to
(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})
. Moreover, the actions
(Y_{AH_{1}},\psi_{AH_{1}})
and
(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}})
are isomorphic via the automorphism of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
defined by
A
(see
[
GPS19
, Proposition 2.8]
).

Conversely, assume
(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}})
and
(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})
are isomorphic. The existence of a group isomorphism
\alpha\colon H_{1}\to H_{2}
implies
d_{1}=d_{2}
. Indeed,
d_{i}
is the rank of
H_{i},\ i=1,2
, and a group isomorphism preserves the rank.
We denote the common value of
d_{1},d_{2}
by
d
. By

[
GPS19
, Proposition 2.8]
, there is
A\in{\rm GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
such that
(Y_{AH_{1}},\psi_{AH_{1}})
is conjugate to
(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})
. We now apply Corollary
2.5
to conclude
AH_{1}=H_{2}
.
∎

3.
Continuous Orbit Equivalence

Definition 3.1
.

An action
(X,\phi)
of a group
G
and an action
(Y,\psi)
of another group
G^{\prime}
are said to be
orbit equivalent
if there exists a homeomorphism
h\colon X\to Y
such that for each
x\in X
one has

(3.1)

h\left(\{\phi^{g}(x)\,\,|\,\,g\in G\}\right)=\{\psi^{g^{\prime}}(h(x))\,\,|\,\,g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}\}.

In
[
GPS19
]
, the authors characterize orbit equivalence of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-action
(Y_{H},\psi_{H})
, where
H
is a dense subgroup of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
containing
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, using superindex
[[H\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}]]
. The
superindex
is defined as

[[H\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}]]=\left\{[H^{\prime}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}]\,\,|\,\,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\subseteq H^{\prime}\subseteq H,\ [H^{\prime}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}]<\infty\right\}.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
GPS19
, Corollary 5.5]

Let
d,d^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
H
be a dense subgroup of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
that contains
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, and let
H^{\prime}
be a dense subgroup of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d^{\prime}}
that contains
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d^{\prime}}
. The
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-action
(Y_{H},\psi_{H})
and the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d^{\prime}}
-action
(Y_{H^{\prime}},\psi_{H^{\prime}})
are orbit equivalent if and only if

[[H\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}]]=[[H^{\prime}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{d^{\prime}}]].

Let
(X,\phi)
be an action of a group
G
and let
(Y,\psi)
be an action of a group
G^{\prime}
. Assume
(X,\phi)
and
(Y,\psi)
are orbit equivalent. By definition, there exists a homeomorphism
h:X\rightarrow Y
satisfying (
3.1
). If the actions

(X,\phi)
,
(Y,\psi)
are free, then there exist unique maps
\alpha\colon X\times G\to G^{\prime}
and
\beta\colon Y\times G^{\prime}\to G
such that

h(\phi^{g}(x))=\psi^{\alpha(x,g)}(h(x)),

for all
x\in X,g\in G
, and also

h^{-1}(\psi^{g^{\prime}}(y)=\phi^{\beta(y,g^{\prime})}(h^{-1}(y)),

for all
y\in Y,g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}
. The maps
\alpha
,
\beta
are called
orbit cocycles
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(X,\phi)
and
(Y,\psi)
be free actions of groups
G
and
G^{\prime}
, respectively, such that
(X,\phi)
and
(Y,\psi)
are orbit equivalent. Then the actions
(X,\phi)
and
(Y,\psi)
are called
continuously orbit equivalent
if there is a homeomorphism
h\colon X\to Y
such that the associated orbit cocycles
\alpha
and
\beta
are continuous in the corresponding product topologies.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
d_{1},d_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}})
be a free
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d_{1}}
-action, and let
(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})
be a free
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d_{2}}
-action. Then
(Y_{H_{1}},\psi_{H_{1}})
and
(Y_{H_{2}},\psi_{H_{2}})
are continuously orbit equivalent if and only if
d=d_{1}=d_{2}
and there exists
A\in{\rm GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Q}})
with
\det A=\pm 1
and
AH_{1}=H_{2}
.

Proof.

Here
{\rm GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Q}})
denotes the group of non-singular
d\times d
-matrices with rational coefficients.
The proof follows the lines of the proof of
[
GPS19
, Theorem 5.7]
, where one uses Proposition
2.7
in place of
[
GPS19
, Corollary 5.1]
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Investigation of continuous orbit equivalence in the general setting was carried out in
[
L18
]
. In particular, conditions on when continuous orbit equivalence implies isomorphic equivalence were given in that paper.

4.
Odometers defined by matrices

In this section we generalize the results in
[

]
on

-odometers defined by matrices with integer coefficients to the

-dimensional case,

.
For convenience, we first put together the results from previous sections in one theorem. To simplify notation,
we denote a

-action
(Y_{H},\psi_{H})
defined above for a subgroup

of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
containing

.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

and let

be dense

such that

. Then

(1)

-actions

,

are conjugate if and only if

.

(2)

{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions

,
Y_{H_{2}}
are isomorphic if and only if there is

such that

.

(3)

{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions
Y_{H_{1}}
,
Y_{H_{2}}
are continuously orbit equivalent if and only if there is

such that

and
TH_{1}=H_{2}
.

(4)

Assume

,
H_{2}
are dense in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
. Then
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions
Y_{H_{1}}
,
Y_{H_{2}}
are orbit equivalent if and only if

[[H_{1}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}]]=[[H_{2}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}]]
.

Proof.

This is the content of Corollary
2.5
, Proposition
2.7
, Theorem
3.2
, and Proposition
3.4
above.
∎

Recall that for a non-singular
d\times d
-matrix
A
with integer coefficients,
A\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
,

G_{A}=\left.\left\{A^{-k}{x}\,\right|\,{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\},\quad{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\subseteq G_{A}\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}.

One can check that
G_{A}
is a subgroup of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
.
For simplicity, we denote
Y_{G_{A}}
by
Y_{A}
. Note that
G_{A}
is naturally isomorphic
to the inductive limit of the system
({\mathbb{Z}}^{d},f_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, where each
f_{j}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
is given by multiplication by
A
,

f_{j}({\bf x})=A{\bf x}
,
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

We start with a characterization of dense groups
G_{A}
in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
.
Let
h_{A}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
be the characteristic polynomial of
A
and
let
h_{A}=h_{1}h_{2}\cdots h_{s}
,
where
h_{1},h_{2},\ldots,h_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
are non-constant and irreducible.

Lemma 4.2
.

[
S22a
, Lemma 8.1]

The group
G_{A}
is dense in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
if and only if
h_{i}(0)\neq\pm 1
for all
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,s\}
.

Next, we describe orbit equivalent odometers.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
S22a
, Lemma 8.2]

Let
A,B\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
be non-singular such that

G_{A}

(
resp.,
G_{B}
)
is dense in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
.
Then
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions
Y_{A}
,
Y_{B}
are orbit equivalent if and only if
\det A,\det B
have the same prime divisors
(
in
{\mathbb{Z}})
.

The next lemma is a special (simple) case when all the equivalences hold at the same time.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
A,B\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
be non-singular such that
G_{A}

(
resp.,
G_{B}
)
is dense in

{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
. Assume that for any prime
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
that divides
\det A
we have

h_{A}\equiv t^{d}\,(\text{mod }p).

Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions
Y_{A}
,
Y_{B}
are conjugate;

(2)

{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions
Y_{A}
,
Y_{B}
are isomorphic;

(3)

{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions
Y_{A}
,
Y_{B}
are continuously orbit equivalent;

(4)

\det A
,
\det B
have the same prime divisors and for any prime

that divides

h_{B}\equiv t^{d}\,(\text{mod }p).

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
4.1
and
[
S22
, Lemma 3.10]
.
∎

Let

\det A=ap_{1}^{s_{1}}p_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots p_{l}^{s_{l}}

be the prime-power factorization of
\det A
, where
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{l}\in{\mathbb{N}}
are distinct primes,

, and
s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{l}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let

\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}(A)=\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{l}\}.

If
\mathcal{P}=\emptyset
, equivalently,
\det A=\pm 1
, then
G_{A}={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and
Y_{A}
is trivial.
Moreover,
for a non-singular

we know that
TG_{A}=G_{B}
for some

T\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Q}})
if and only if
\det B=\pm 1
and hence

G_{B}={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

[
S22
, Lemma 3.2(i)]
.

In what follows we assume
\mathcal{P}\neq\emptyset
.
Denote

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(A)=\left\{p\in\mathcal{P},\,\,h_{A}\not\equiv t^{d}\,(\text{mod }p)\right\},

where
h_{A}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
denotes the characteristic polynomial of
A
. The case

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\emptyset
is settled in Lemma
4.4
, so in what follows we assume
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. Finally, for a prime

let

denote the multiplicity of zero in the reduction of the characteristic polynomial of

modulo

. Thus, in our notation

if and only if

.

Even though the results in
[
S22a
]
apply to isomorphisms between groups

,

for arbitrary non-singular

, to avoid making this paper too technical, we only consider a generic case when the characteristic polynomials of

are irreducible. An interested reader could use
[
S22a
]
together with Theorem
4.1
to treat other cases. Another additional assumption we make is the condition that there exists

such that the greatest common divisor
(t_{p},d)
of

and
d
is

. It seems that this condition provides the right setting for
the generalization of the

2
-dimensional case, if

is irreducible and

for some

, then

is irreducible and

takes an eigenvector of

to an

]
. It turns out these facts remain true under the assumption

and not true in general,
e.g.
, all

and

[

]
.

Let
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
denote a fixed algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Recall that
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
consists of algebraic numbers, roots of non-zero polynomials in one variable with rational coefficients. The eigenvalues of a matrix

.
Let
A,B\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
be non-singular and let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}\in\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}

(resp.,
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{d}\in\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
) denote eigenvalues of

(resp.,
B
). Assume there exists
T\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Q}})
that satisfies

. Suppose further that the characteristic polynomial

of
A
is irreducible and there exists a prime

p\in\mathcal{P}(A)
such that
(t_{p}(A),d)=1
. The following facts follow from
[
S22a
, Proposition 5.7]
. Namely,
h_{B}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
is irreducible. Moreover, (up to rearrangement of eigenvalues)

(4.1)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Q}}(\lambda_{1})={\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{1})\text{ and }\lambda_{1},\,\mu_{1}\text{ have the same}

In particular, the splitting fields of

,

coincide. Furthermore, both

and

are diagonalizable (over

) and there exist

\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\

and

. If

-actions

,

are conjugate (or isomorphic, or continuously orbit equivalent), then by Theorem

, we have

, the identity matrix (or

, or

, respectively)
and, hence,

(or

, or

, respectively). This proves the following lemma.

.

Let

(

)

.

) holds, there exist

) holds, and

.

(ii)

-actions
Y_{A}
,

are isomorphic, then (

) holds, there exist

(iii)

4.1
) holds, there exist
M,N\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}})
such that
(
4.2
) holds, and
\det NM^{-1}=\pm 1
.

As in the
2
-dimensional case, the conditions in Lemma

are also sufficient in the cases of conjugacy and isomorphism.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
A,B\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
be non-singular such that
G_{A}

(
resp.,
G_{B}
)
is dense in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
.
Assume
h_{A}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
is irreducible and
(t_{p},d)=1
for some prime

p\in\mathcal{P}
.

(i)

If (
4.1
) holds and there exists
M\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}})
such that
(
4.2
) holds for
N=M
, then
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions
Y_{A}
,
Y_{B}
are conjugate.

(ii)

If (
4.1
) holds, there exist
M,N\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}})
such that
NM^{-1}\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
and
(
4.2
) holds, then
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions
Y_{A}
,
Y_{B}
are isomorphic.

Proof.

First,
(ii)
follows easily from
(i)
as in the proof of
[
S22
, Lemma 8.10]
. We repeat the argument for the sake of completeness. Assume
(i)
holds, let
X=NM^{-1}\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
, and assume (
4.1
) and
(
4.2
) hold. Then
XM=N
and

XG_{A}=G_{XAX^{-1}}=G_{N\Lambda N^{-1}}=G_{B},\quad\Lambda=\left(\begin{matrix}\lambda_{1}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&\lambda_{d}\end{matrix}\right).

Here,
XAX^{-1}=N\Lambda N^{-1}\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
, since
X\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
. Also,
G_{N\Lambda N^{-1}}=G_{B}
by
(i)
and Theorem
4.1
. Thus,
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions
Y_{A}
,
Y_{B}
are isomorphic by Theorem
4.1
.

We now prove
(i)
. Assume (
4.1
),
(
4.2
) hold and
N=M
. Since
h_{A}
is irreducible, (
4.1
) implies that
h_{B}
is also irreducible.
Indeed,
h_{B}
is irreducible if and only if
[{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{1}):{\mathbb{Q}}]=d
. From (
4.2
) with
N=M
, we see that
A
,
B
share the same eigenvector
{\bf u}\in\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}^{d}
such that
A{\bf u}=\lambda_{1}{\bf u}
,
B{\bf u}=\mu_{1}{\bf u}
. Since
h_{A}
is irreducible, the Galois group
G=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
acts transitively on the eigenvalues of
A
,
i.e.
, there exist
\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{d}\in G
such that
\lambda_{i}=\sigma_{i}(\lambda_{1})
,
\sigma_{1}=\operatorname{id}
,
1\leq i\leq d
. Since
A
,
B
have integer coefficients, by applying
\sigma_{i}
to
A{\bf u}=\lambda_{1}{\bf u}
,
B{\bf u}=\mu_{1}{\bf u}
, we conclude that

\sigma_{i}({\bf u})
is an eigenvector of
A
(resp.,
B
) corresponding to
\lambda_{i}
(resp.,
\sigma_{i}(\mu_{1})
),
1\leq i\leq d
, and
\sigma_{1}(\mu_{1}),\ldots,\sigma_{d}(\mu_{1})
are all (distinct) eigenvalues of
B
.
By abuse of notation, let
\mu_{i}=\sigma_{i}(\mu_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq d
. Moreover, it follows from (
4.1
) that
h_{A}
,
h_{B}
share the same splitting field
K
and each pair
\lambda_{i}
,
\mu_{i}
share the same prime ideal divisors in the ring of integers
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
of

. Therefore,
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}(A)=\mathcal{P}(B)
,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(A)=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(B)
, and
t_{p}=t_{p}(A)=t_{p}(B)
for any prime
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
In
[
S22a
]
, we discuss the characteristic
\{\alpha_{pij}\}_{p,i,j}
of a group
G_{A}
with respect to a free basis
\{{\bf f}_{1},\ldots,{\bf f}_{d}\}
of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, where
p\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(A)
,
\alpha_{pij}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
,
1\leq i\leq t_{p}(A)
,
t_{p}(A)+1\leq j\leq d
. The system

{\mathcal{S}}(A)=\{{\bf f}_{1},\ldots,{\bf f}_{d},\alpha_{pij}\,\,|\,\,p,i,j\}
determines all generators of
G_{A}
over
{\mathbb{Z}}

[
S22a
, Lemma 3.5]
. Furthermore, we show that
{\mathcal{S}}(A)
can be calculated from eigenvectors of
A
corresponding to eigenvalues divisible by a prime ideal of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
that divides
p
,
p\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}

[
S22a
, Remark 4.4]
. Since each pair

\lambda_{i}
,
\mu_{i}
share the same prime ideal divisors and

,
B
share the same eigenvectors corresponding to

\lambda_{i}
,
\mu_{i}
, respectively, we see that

.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

It follows from its proof that in Lemma
4.6
(i)
, instead of (
4.2
) with
N=M
, it is enough to assume that
A
,
B
share the same eigenvector
{\bf u}\in\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}^{d}
such that
A{\bf u}=\lambda_{1}{\bf u}
,
B{\bf u}=\mu_{1}{\bf u}
.

It is well-known (it follows from the Latimer–MacDuffee–Taussky Theorem) that for a fixed monic irreducible polynomial
h\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
of degree
d
there are finitely many
\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
-conjugacy classes
[A]
of
A\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
with characteristic polynomial
h
. Let
n=n(h)
denote the number of conjugacy classes. Also, for
S\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
we have
SG_{A}=G_{SAS^{-1}}
. As was discussed above, we know that if there exists
t_{p}
with
(t_{p},d)=1
,
A,B\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
share the same irreducible characteristic polynomial and
TG_{A}=G_{B}
for some
T\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}({\mathbb{Q}})
, then
TAT^{-1}=B

[
S22a
]
. This implies that there are exactly
n
isomorphism classes

[Y_{A}]_{isom}
of odometers
Y_{A}
, where
A\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
has characteristic polynomial
h
, and there are less or equal than
n

continuously orbit equivalent classes
[Y_{A}]_{cont.orb}
of odometers
Y_{A}
, where
A\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
has characteristic polynomial
h
.

Remark 4.8
.

Note that
M=N
implies
A
,
B
commute. However,
AB=BA
does not imply conjugacy, since the ordering of eigenvalues matters. For example,
G_{A}\neq G_{B}
for

A=\left(\begin{matrix}3&0\\
0&5\end{matrix}\right),\,\,B=\left(\begin{matrix}5&0\\
0&3\end{matrix}\right).

Remark 4.9
.

We know from the
2
-dimensional case that for odometers
Y_{A}
defined by
A\in\operatorname{M}_{d}({\mathbb{Z}})
, continuous orbit equivalence is more subtle than conjugacy and isomorphism. Even in the
2
-dimensional case, general sufficient conditions for
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
-actions
Y_{A}
,
Y_{B}
to be
continuously orbit equivalent under the conditions of Lemma
4.5
become rather technical.
However, in each particular example, it is possible to resolve the question using Theorem
4.1
and the results
in
[
S22
]
and
[
S22a
]
.

Example 4.10
.

In this example,
d=3
and

A,B,C\in\operatorname{M}_{3}({\mathbb{Z}})
have the same irreducible characteristic polynomial
h(t)=t^{3}-39t-91
,

A=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
91&39&0\end{matrix}\right),\quad B=\left(\begin{matrix}7&0&0\\
5&1&0\\
-24&-4&1\end{matrix}\right),\quad C=\left(\begin{matrix}49&0&0\\

Since

4.3
,
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions

All three
A
,
B
, and
C
are conjugate to each other in
\operatorname{GL}_{3}({\mathbb{Q}})
. Moreover,

is a companion matrix of

and
C

i.e.
, any matrix in
\operatorname{M}_{3}({\mathbb{Z}})
with characteristic polynomial

is

\operatorname{GL}_{3}({\mathbb{Z}})
-conjugate to

,
B
, or
C
, and any two matrices out of

,
B
, and

are not
\operatorname{GL}_{3}({\mathbb{Z}})
-conjugate to each other (this can be verified using
[

]
,
[
SAGE
]
). Using the methods of
[
S22a
]
, we can prove that any two out of

, and

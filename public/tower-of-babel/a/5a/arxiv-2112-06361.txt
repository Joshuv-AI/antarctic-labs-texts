Logarithmic resolution via multi-weighted blow-ups
Dan Abramovich, Ming Hao Quek

Abstract




Abstract.
We first introduce and study the notion of multi-weighted blow-ups, which is later used to systematically construct an explicit yet efficient algorithm for functorial logarithmic resolution in characteristic zero, in the sense of Hironaka.

Specifically, for a singular, reduced closed subscheme
X
of a smooth scheme
Y
over a field of characteristic zero, we resolve the singularities of
X
by taking proper transforms
X_{i}\subset Y_{i}
along a sequence of multi-weighted blow-ups
Y_{N}\to Y_{N-1}\to\dotsb\to Y_{0}=Y
which satisfies the following properties:

(i)

The
Y_{i}
are smooth Artin stacks with simple normal crossing exceptional loci.

(ii)

At each step we always blow up the worst singular locus of
X_{i}
, and witness on
X_{i+1}
an immediate improvement in singularities.

(iii)

Finally, the singular locus of
X
is transformed into a simple normal crossing divisor on
X_{N}
.

Keywords.
Resolution, singularity, logarithmic geometry, stack, blow-up, fan, toric variety

1.1. Recent techniques for resolution of singularities in characteristic zero

We revisit the celebrated theorem of Hironaka, see

[

\mathbf{e}_{i}
. Let
M=N^{\vee}
be the dual lattice, with standard dual basis vectors
\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\vee}
. For any fan
\Sigma
on
N
, let
\Sigma(1)
denote the rays in
\Sigma
, and for a ray
\rho
of
\Sigma
, let
\mathbf{u}_{\rho}=(\mathrm{u}_{\rho,i})_{i=1}^{n}
denote the first lattice point on
\rho
.

Let
\sigma_{\mathrm{std}}
be the cone on
N=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
spanned by the standard basis vectors
\mathbf{e}_{i}
, which generates the standard fan
\Sigma_{\mathrm{std}}
in
N
, and whose associated affine toric variety is the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n}:=\Spec(\mathds{k}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}])
. In addition, for a set
S
, we usually write
\mathbb{A}^{S}
for
\Spec(\mathds{k}[x_{s}\colon s\in S])
or
\Spec(\mathds{k}[x_{s}^{\prime}\colon s\in S])
.

In this paper,
\mathfrak{a}
denotes a monomial ideal of
\mathds{k}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]
. Associated to
\mathfrak{a}
are the following notions:

(i)

the submonoid
\Upgamma{a}=\{\mathbf{a}\in M\colon\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathbf{a}}:=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\dotsm x_{n}^{a_{n}}\in\mathfrak{a}\}
of
M
,

(ii)

the Newton polyhedron
P{a}
of
\mathfrak{a}
given by the convex hull of
\Upgamma{a}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}\subset M\otimes{Z}\mathbb{R}=:M{R}
,

(iii)

and the normal fan
\Sigma{a}
of
P{a}
, which is a subdivision of
\Sigma_{\mathrm{std}}
in
N
, and hence induces a toric, proper, birational morphism
X_{\Sigma{a}}\to\mathbb{A}^{n}
.

For every integer
0\leq k\leq n
, there is an inclusion-reversing correspondence between
k
-dimensional cones
\sigma
of
\Sigma{a}
and
(n-k)
-dimensional faces
\tau
of
P{a}
. Therefore, we introduce the following notation:

(a)

H_{\rho}
is the facet of
P{a}
corresponding to a ray
\rho\in\Sigma{a}(1)
. We also let
N_{\rho}(\mathfrak{a})
be the natural number such that the affine span of
H_{\rho}
has equation
\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}{\mathrm{u}_{\rho,i}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{i}}{=N_{\rho}(\mathfrak{a})}
.

(b)

\mathbf{v}_{\sigma}=(\mathrm{v}_{\sigma,i})_{i=1}^{n}
is the vertex of
P{a}
corresponding to a maximal cone
\sigma
of
\Sigma{a}
.

Finally, we identify the subset
\Sigma_{\mathrm{std}}(1)\subset\Sigma{a}(1)
with
[1,n]:=\{1,\dotsc,n\}
and denote its complement by

\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a}):=\Sigma{a}(1)\smallsetminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{std}}(1)=\Sigma{a}(1)\smallsetminus[1,n].

We call the rays in
[1,n]
the
standard rays
and call the rays in
\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})
the
exceptional rays
. We also set
\mathbf{E}^{+}(\mathfrak{a}):=\{\rho\in\Sigma{a}(1)\colon N_{\rho}(\mathfrak{a})>0\}\supset\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})
.

Definition 2.5

(Multi-weighted blow-ups on affine spaces)
.

Let
\mathbf{b}=(\mathrm{b}_{\rho})_{\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}^{\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}
. Then
\mathbf{b}
yields a surjective homomorphism
\beta\colon\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma{a}(1)}\to N=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as follows:

\mathbf{e}_{\rho}\longmapsto\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}_{\rho}=\mathbf{e}_{i}\quad&\textrm{if }\rho=i\in\Sigma_{\mathrm{std}}(1)=[1,n],\\
\mathrm{b}_{\rho}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{\rho}\quad&\textrm{if }\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a}).\end{cases}

Therefore, the data
(\Sigma{a},\beta)
yields the fantastack
\Bl_{\mathfrak{a},\mathbf{b}}\mathbb{A}^{n}:=\mathscr{F}_{\Sigma{a},\beta}
, as described in Definition
2.1
.
Moreover, the homomorphism
\beta
induces, as explained in Remark
2.3
and Section
2.1.1
(
iii
), a toric morphism

\pi_{\mathfrak{a},\mathbf{b}}\colon\Bl_{\mathfrak{a},\mathbf{b}}\mathbb{A}^{n}\xrightarrow{\textrm{good moduli space}}X_{\Sigma{a}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}.

We call this composition the
multi-weighted blow-up
of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
along
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
. When
\mathbf{b}
is the unit vector
(1,1,\dotsc,1)
in
\mathbb{N}_{>0}^{\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}
, we instead write the above expression as
\pi{a}\colon\Bl{a}\mathbb{A}^{n}\to\mathbb{A}^{n}
. This should not be confused with the
schematic
blow-up of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
along
\mathfrak{a}
, which we denote by
\bl{a}\mathbb{A}^{n}
in this paper. (In fact, the normalization of
\bl{a}\mathbb{A}^{n}
is precisely
X_{\Sigma{a}}
; see Remark
3.11
.)

One anticipates that
\Bl{a}\mathbb{A}^{n}
is the “most canonical” multi-weighted blow-up of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
associated to
\mathfrak{a}
. We will make this canonicity precise in Section
3.1
, where, more generally, we also define, with the same goal of canonicity in mind, the multi-weighted blow-up
\Bl_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}\mathbb{A}^{n}
of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
along a monomial Rees algebra
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
.

2.1.2. Alternative notation

To uniformize forthcoming notation, we also set
\mathrm{b}_{i}:=1
for every
i\in[1,n]\subset\Sigma{a}(1)
, and we interpret
\mathbf{b}
as a vector
(\mathrm{b}_{\rho})_{\rho\in\Sigma{a}(1)}
in
\mathbb{N}_{>0}^{\Sigma{a}(1)}
. Then
\beta
in Definition
2.5
is simply
\mathbf{e}_{\rho}\mapsto\mathrm{b}_{\rho}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{\rho}
for
\rho\in\Sigma{a}(1)
.

Remark 2.6
.

The multi-weighted blow-up
\pi_{\mathfrak{a},\mathbf{b}}
is birational, as explained in Remark
2.3
. By
[
Alp13
, Theorem 4.16]
, the good moduli space morphism
\Bl_{\mathfrak{a},\mathbf{b}}\mathbb{A}^{n}\to X_{\Sigma{a}}
is universally closed and surjective. Therefore, so is
\pi_{\mathfrak{a},\mathbf{b}}
.

Remark 2.7
.

It is important to highlight that we have fixed coordinates
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}
on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
in which
\mathfrak{a}
is monomial;
i.e.
we fixed a toroidal
Zariski logarithmic structure, with chart
\mathbb{N}^{n}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{e}_{i}\mapsto x_{i}}\mathds{k}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]
, in which
\mathfrak{a}
is a monomial ideal in the general sense of Section
4.1.6
below.

Then the Newton polyhedron
P{a}
is
well defined
under the lens of logarithmic geometry;
i.e.

P{a}
is
independent
, up to symmetry, of any change in local coordinates at
0\in\mathbb{A}^{n}
which respects the aforementioned toroidal logarithmic structure. Explicitly, any such coordinate change corresponds to a monoid automorphism of
\mathbb{N}^{n}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{n},0}^{\ast}
, which must map each
(\mathbf{e}_{i},1)
to
(\mathbf{e}_{j},\mu)
for some
j\in[1,n]
and
\mu\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{n},0}^{\ast}
.

Remark 2.8
.

Two monomial ideals can possess the same normal fan, and thus yield the same multi-weighted blow-up. For example:

(i)

P{a}=P_{\overline{\mathfrak{a}}}
, where
\overline{\mathfrak{a}}=\{\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathbf{a}}\colon\mathbf{a}\in P{a}\}
is the integral closure of
\mathfrak{a}
.

(ii)

If
f_{1},\dotsc,f_{r}
are monomials generating
\mathfrak{a}
, then for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, the integral closures of
\mathfrak{a}^{\ell}
and
(f_{1}^{\ell},\dotsc,f_{r}^{\ell})
coincide, so by (
i
),
\mathfrak{a}^{\ell}
and
(f_{1}^{\ell},\dotsc,f_{r}^{\ell})
have the same Newton polyhedra.

(iii)

For any
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
\Sigma_{\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathbf{a}}\cdot\mathfrak{a}}=\Sigma{a}
. However, while the multi-weighted blow-up along
\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathbf{a}}\cdot\mathfrak{a}
is the same as that along
\mathfrak{a}
, there is a subtle difference in their “exceptional” divisors (see Remark
2.16
).

(iv)

Lastly, as
\ell
varies, although the Newton polyhedron of
\mathfrak{a}^{\ell}
varies, the normal fan of
\mathfrak{a}^{\ell}
remains the same, and so does the multi-weighted blow-up of
\mathfrak{a}^{\ell}
.

2.1.3. Explicating multi-weighted blow-ups

With the notation in Definition
2.5
, the homomorphism
\beta\colon\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma{a}(1)}\to N=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
induced by
\mathbf{b}
fits into the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}\xrightarrow{\alpha\ =\ \tiny\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{B}\\
-I_{k}\end{bmatrix}}\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma{a}(1)}\xrightarrow{\footnotesize\beta\ =\ \begin{bmatrix}I_{k}&\mathbf{B}\end{bmatrix}}\mathbb{Z}^{n}\longrightarrow 0,

where
k:=\#\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})
and
\mathbf{B}=(\mathrm{B}_{i,\rho})_{1\leq i\leq n,\;\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}
is the matrix whose columns are
\mathrm{b}_{\rho}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{\rho}
for each
\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})
. Unravelling the definitions, let us highlight three details:

(i)

We have the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{\widehat{\Sigma}{a}}=\mathbb{A}^{\Sigma{a}(1)}\smallsetminus V(J_{\Sigma{a}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{\Sigma{a}(1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{n}\hbox to0.0pt{,\hss}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Bl_{\mathfrak{a},\mathbf{b}}\mathbb{A}^{n}=\left[X_{\widehat{\Sigma}{a}}\;/\;\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}\right]}
open
stack-theoretic quotient
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\mathfrak{a},\mathbf{b}}}

where we follow the convention in Section
2.1.1
that

(2.1)

\qquad\qquad\mathbb{A}^{\Sigma{a}(1)}=\Spec\left(\mathds{k}[x_{1}^{\prime},\dotsc,x_{n}^{\prime}]\left[x_{\rho}^{\prime}\colon\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})\right]\right)

so that the above morphism
\mathbb{A}^{\Sigma{a}(1)}\to\mathbb{A}^{n}
of affine spaces corresponds to the homomorphism
\mathds{k}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]\to\mathds{k}[x_{1}^{\prime},\dotsc,x_{n}^{\prime}][x_{\rho}^{\prime}\colon\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})]
defined by

\qquad\qquad x_{i}\longmapsto\left(x_{i}^{\prime}\cdot\prodop\displaylimits_{\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}{\left(x_{\rho}^{\prime}\right)^{\mathrm{B}_{i,\rho}}}\right)=\left(x_{i}^{\prime}\cdot\prodop\displaylimits_{\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}{\left(x_{\rho}^{\prime}\right)^{\mathrm{b}_{\rho}\cdot\mathrm{u}_{\rho,i}}}\right)

for every
1\leq i\leq n
. For
\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})
, the corresponding coordinate
x_{\rho}^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{A}^{\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}
will be written as
u_{\rho}
during examples (
e.g.
Sections
2.2
and
5.1
).

(ii)

The action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}
on
X_{\widehat{\Sigma}{a}}\subset\mathbb{A}^{\Sigma{a}(1)}=\Spec(\mathds{k}[x_{1}^{\prime},\dotsc,x_{n}^{\prime}][x_{\rho}^{\prime}\colon\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})])
can be interpreted from the matrix
\alpha=\small{\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{B}\\
-I_{k}\end{bmatrix}}
as follows:

(a)

For every
1\leq i\leq n
,
x_{i}^{\prime}
has
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}
-grading
(\mathrm{B}_{i,\rho})_{\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}=(\mathrm{b}_{\rho}\cdot\mathrm{u}_{\rho,i})_{\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}
.

(b)

For every
\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})
,
x_{\rho}^{\prime}
has
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}
-grading
-\mathbf{e}_{\rho}=(-\delta_{\rho,\widetilde{\rho}})_{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}
.

(iii)

By Definition
2.1
(
a
),
\Bl_{\mathfrak{a},\mathbf{b}}\mathbb{A}^{n}
admits an open cover by
x_{\sigma}^{\prime}
-charts

D_{+}(\sigma)=D_{+}(x_{\sigma}^{\prime}):=\left[U_{\sigma}\;/\;\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}\right]
, where
\sigma
varies over all maximal cones
\sigma
of
\Sigma{a}
. In this setting, we have

\qquad\qquad U_{\sigma}=\Spec\left(\mathds{k}\left[x_{1}^{\prime},\dotsc,x_{n}^{\prime}\right]\left[x_{\rho}^{\prime}\colon\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})\right]\left[\left(x_{\sigma}^{\prime}\right)^{-1}\right]\right),

where

\qquad\qquad x_{\sigma}^{\prime}=\prodop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\in\Sigma{a}(1)\\
\rho\nsubset\sigma\end{subarray}}{x_{\rho}^{\prime}}=\prodop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\in\Sigma{a}(1)\\
\mathbf{v}_{\sigma}\notin H_{\rho}\end{subarray}}{x_{\rho}^{\prime}}=\prodop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\in\Sigma{a}(1)\\
\mathbf{u}_{\rho}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{\sigma}>N_{\rho}(\mathfrak{a})\end{subarray}}{x_{\rho}^{\prime}}

(see Section
2.1.1
(
a
), (
b
)).

Remark 2.9
.

Slightly more generally, one can also consider
generalized multi-weighted blow-ups

\Bl_{\mathfrak{a},\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}}\mathbb{A}^{n}
of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
along
\mathfrak{a}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}=(\mathrm{b}_{\rho})_{\rho\in\Sigma{a}(1)}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}^{\Sigma{a}(1)}
. Such a
\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}
yields a homomorphism
\widetilde{\beta}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma{a}(1)}\to N=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with finite cokernel, which sends
\mathbf{e}_{\rho}
to
\mathrm{b}_{\rho}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{\rho}
for every
\rho\in\Sigma{a}(1)
. The fantastack
\mathscr{F}_{\Sigma{a},\widetilde{\beta}}
associated to
(\Sigma{a},\widetilde{\beta})
is then denoted by
\Bl_{\mathfrak{a},\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}}\mathbb{A}^{n}
. Likewise, there is a toric morphism
\pi_{\mathfrak{a},\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}}\colon\Bl_{\mathfrak{a},\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}}\mathbb{A}^{n}\to X_{\Sigma}\to\mathbb{A}^{n}
, which is the generalized multi-weighted blow-up morphism.

In the same way as in Section
2.1.3
, one can partially explicate generalized multi-weighted blow-ups: we have

\Bl_{\mathfrak{a},\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}}\mathbb{A}^{n}=\left[\left(\mathbb{A}^{\Sigma{a}(1)}\smallsetminus V\left(J_{\Sigma{a}}\right)\right)\;/\;D\left(\Coker\left(\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee}\right)\right)\right]\xrightarrow{\pi_{\mathfrak{a},\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}}}\mathbb{A}^{n},

where
\pi_{\mathfrak{a},\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}}
is induced by
\mathds{k}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]\to\mathds{k}[x_{1}^{\prime},\dotsc,x_{n}^{\prime}]\left[x_{\rho}^{\prime}\colon\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})\right]
, which is defined by

x_{i}\longmapsto\left(\left(x_{i}^{\prime}\right)^{\mathrm{b}_{i}}\cdot\prodop\displaylimits_{\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}{\left(x_{\rho}^{\prime}\right)^{\mathrm{b}_{\rho}\cdot\mathrm{u}_{\rho,i}}}\right)

and where
D(\Coker(\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee}))
acts via the morphism of diagonalizable groups obtained from
(\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma{a}(1)})^{\vee}\twoheadrightarrow\Coker(\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee})
by applying
D(-):=\Hom_{\mathrm{Grp-Sch}}(-,\mathbb{G}_{m})
. This demonstrates that generalized multi-weighted blow-ups can be seen as an amalgamation of the notion of multi-weighted blow-ups in Definition
2.5
and the notion of
root stacks
(see
[
Cad07
, Definition 2.2.1]
or
[
AGV08
, Appendix B]
or
[
QR22
, Example 2.2.4]
.)

Finally,
\Bl_{\mathfrak{a},\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}}\mathbb{A}^{n}
likewise admits an open cover by
x_{\sigma}^{\prime}
-charts
D_{+}(\sigma)=\left[U_{\sigma}\;/\;D\left(\Coker\left(\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee}\right)\right)\right]
, where
U_{\sigma}
has the same description in Section
2.1.3
.

2.2. Two examples

We illustrate
Section
2.1.3
via two examples. The first is classical and is a special case of multi-weighted blow-ups.

Example 2.10

(Weighted blow-ups)
.

For
w_{1},\dotsc,w_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, set

. The (stack-theoretic) weighted blow-up of

\dotsc,x_{n}])

ATW24
]

Consider

above. The vertices

2.3. Exceptional divisors and transforms

The presentation
\Bl_{\mathfrak{a},\mathbf{b}}\mathbb{A}^{n}=\left[X_{\widehat{\Sigma}{a}}\;/\;\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}\right]
induces an isomorphism

\Pic\left(\Bl_{\mathfrak{a},\mathbf{b}}\mathbb{A}^{n}\right)\xrightarrow{\;\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}\vskip-1.5pt}\;}\Pic^{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}}\left(X_{\widehat{\Sigma}{a}}\right),

where the right-hand side denotes the
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}
-equivariant Picard group of
X_{\widehat{\Sigma}{a}}
. In particular, for each
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}
, there are
tautological line bundles

\mathscr{O}(\mathbf{d}):=\mathscr{O}_{\Bl_{\mathfrak{a},\mathbf{b}}\mathbb{A}^{n}}(\mathbf{d})
on
\Bl_{\mathfrak{a},\mathbf{b}}\mathbb{A}^{n}
which correspond to the trivial line bundle
\mathscr{O}_{X_{\widehat{\Sigma}{a}}}
on
X_{\widehat{\Sigma}{a}}
endowed with the
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}
-linearization given by the “
\mathbf{d}

To reinterpret the weak transform, we begin with the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 2.22
.

For
(k_{\rho})_{\rho\in\Sigma{a}(1)}\in\mathbb{N}^{\Sigma{a}(1)}

(i)

\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}I\cdot\mathscr{O}\subset\prodop\displaylimits_{\rho\in\Sigma{a}(1)}{(x_{\rho}^{\prime})^{k_{\rho}}}

(ii)

\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}\gamma_{I}\cdot\mathscr{O}\geq\sumop\displaylimits_{\rho\in\Sigma{a}(1)}{k_{\rho}\cdot\gamma_{(x_{\rho}^{\prime})}}

(i

\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}I\cdot\mathscr{O}\subset(x_{\rho}^{\prime})^{k_{\rho}}

(ii

\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}\gamma_{I}\cdot\mathscr{O}\geq k_{\rho}\cdot\gamma_{(x_{\rho}^{\prime})}
for every
\rho\in\Sigma{a}(1)

Proof.

The equivalence (

\Leftrightarrow
(
(i′)

\Leftrightarrow
(

(i′)

(

Our next goal is to provide a recharacterization of statement (

Lemma 2.23

For each
\rho\in\Sigma{a}(1)

\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}\gamma^{[\rho]}\cdot\mathscr{O}\geq\gamma_{(x_{\rho}^{\prime})}.

Proof.

Let
\nu\in\ZR(X_{\widehat{\Sigma}{a}})
be arbitrary. If
\rho=i\in[1,n]

\gamma_{(x_{i}^{\prime}),\nu}=\nu(x_{i}^{\prime})=\nu(x_{i})-\sumop\displaylimits_{\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})}{\left(\mathrm{b}_{\rho}\cdot\mathrm{u}_{\rho,i}\right)\cdot\nu\left(x_{\rho}^{\prime}\right)}\leq\nu(x_{i})=\left(\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}\gamma^{[i]}\cdot\mathscr{O}\right)_{\nu}.

If instead
\rho\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})
, we have, for every
1\leq i\leq n
such that

\displaystyle\gamma_{(x_{\rho}^{\prime}),\nu}=\nu\left(x_{\rho}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{b}_{\rho}\cdot\mathrm{u}_{\rho,i}}\cdot\left(\nu(x_{i})-\nu(x_{i}^{\prime})-\sumop\displaylimits_{\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{a})\smallsetminus\{\rho\}}{\left(\mathrm{b}_{\widetilde{\rho}}\cdot\mathrm{u}_{\widetilde{\rho},i}\right)\cdot\nu\left(x_{\widetilde{\rho}}^{\prime}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{b}_{\rho}\cdot\mathrm{u}_{\rho,i}}\cdot\nu(x_{i}).

Taking the minimum over all such

∎

2.4.5. More conventions

Let
\ZR(\widetilde{\pi})\colon\ZR(X_{\widehat{\Sigma}{a}})\to\ZR(\mathbb{A}^{n})

For

(i)

\left(=k\cdot(\gamma^{[\rho]})_{\nu_{\rho}}=k\cdot\gamma_{(x_{\rho}^{\prime}),\nu_{\rho}^{\prime}}\right)

Proof.

The equivalence (

3. Multi-weighted blow-ups: Canonical aspects

3.1. Canonicity of multi-weighted blow-ups, I

In this section we continue to follow the conventions in Section
2.1.1
, and we endow
\mathbb{A}^{n}
with the logarithmic structure
\mathbb{N}^{n}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{e}_{i}\mapsto x_{i}}\mathds{k}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
be an (
\mathbb{N}
-graded)
monomial Rees algebra on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
,
i.e.
a finitely generated,
\mathbb{N}
-graded
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{n}}

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{m\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathfrak{a}_{m}\cdot t^{m}}\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{n}}[t]

We give a definition of

(see Definition

) for a monomial ideal

Definition 3.1

(Multi-weighted blow-ups along monomial Rees algebras)

Lemma 3.2

The definition of

such that

Let

N_{\rho}(\mathfrak{j})>0
, we must have
N_{\overline{\rho}}(\mathfrak{a})>0
. Then
\rho\mapsto\overline{\rho}
sets up a one-to-one correspondence
\mathbf{E}(\mathfrak{j})=\mathbf{E}^{+}(\mathfrak{j})\xrightarrow{\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}\vskip-1.5pt}}\mathbf{E}^{+}(\mathfrak{a})
. Through this correspondence, Lemma
3.15
can be supplemented as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{u}_{\rho,i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell}{\gcd(\ell,N_{\overline{\rho}}(\mathfrak{a}))}\cdot\mathrm{u}_{\overline{\rho},i}\quad\textrm{for }n-r<i\leq n,

\displaystyle N_{\rho}(\mathfrak{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell N_{\overline{\rho}}(\mathfrak{j})}{\gcd(\ell,N_{\overline{\rho}}(\mathfrak{a}))}.

In particular, the number
\frac{N_{\rho}(\mathfrak{j})}{\ell}
in (
3.10
) is equal to
\frac{N_{\overline{\rho}}(\mathfrak{a})}{\gcd(\ell,N_{\overline{\rho}}(\mathfrak{a}))}
.

Corollary 3.18
.

Suppose that
a_{1}
divides
\lcm(a_{2},\dotsc,a_{k})

(
this is
1
if
k=1)
. Let
\mathbb{A}^{n-1;r}=V(x_{1})\subset\mathbb{A}^{n;r}

\mathfrak{j}_{1}:=\mathfrak{j}|_{V(x_{1})}=(x_{2}^{a_{2}},\dotsc,x_{k}^{a_{k}},\mathfrak{a})\subset\mathds{k}[x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-r},\underline{x}_{n-r+1},\underline{x}_{n}]

of

\pi_{\mathfrak{j}_{1}}\colon\Bl_{\mathfrak{j}_{1}}\mathbb{A}^{n-1;r}

\to\mathbb{A}^{n-1;r}

Proof.

We saw at the start of Section

V(x_{1}\cdot t^{w_{1}})\times_{\tBl_{\underline{\mathfrak{j}}_{\bullet}}\mathbb{A}^{n;r}}\Bl{j}\mathbb{A}^{n;r}=V(x_{1}^{\prime})

On the other hand,

∎

To lift the hypothesis in Corollary

4. Logarithmic resolution via multi-weighted blow-ups

4.1. Recollections

We begin by briefly reviewing the relevant aspects from
[
Que20
]
. Unless otherwise stated,
Y
denotes a strict toroidal
\mathds{k}
-scheme, see
[
Que20
, Definition B.6]
, with logarithmic structure
\mathscr{M}_{Y}\to\mathscr{O}_{Y}
. Let
I\subset\mathscr{O}_{Y}
be a (coherent) ideal on
Y
.

In Sections
4.1.1
–
4.1.3
below we recall some important notions associated to a strict toroidal
\mathds{k}
-scheme; see
[
Que20
, Appendix B]
for details and references.

4.1.1. Logarithmic stratification

For every
p\in Y
, set
\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{Y,p}:=\mathscr{M}_{Y,p}/\mathscr{O}_{Y,p}^{\ast}
. Then
\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{Y,p}^{\mathrm{gp}}
is a free abelian group of finite rank. We define the
rank
of
\mathscr{M}_{Y}
at
p
to be
r(p):=\rank(\overline{\sM}_{Y,p}^\gp)
. Then
r(p)
is upper semi-continuous on
Y
;
i.e.
more precisely, there exists a
logarithmic stratification

Y:=\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{r\in\mathbb{N}}{Y(r)}
, where for each
r\in\mathbb{N}
,

(i)

Y(r)
is a locally closed, pure-dimensional, smooth subscheme of
Y
, consisting precisely of points
p\in Y(r)
such that
r(p)=r
;

(ii)

the schematic closure
\overline{Y(r)}
is
\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{r^{\prime}\geq r}{Y(r^{\prime})}
.

Consider the open immersion
j\colon U:=Y(0)\hookrightarrow Y
. Then the logarithmic structure
\mathscr{M}_{Y}\to\mathscr{O}_{Y}
can be recovered as the inclusion
\mathscr{M}_{Y}:=j_{\ast}(\mathscr{O}_{U}^{\ast})\cap\mathscr{O}_{Y}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{Y}
. In particular, the logarithmic structure
\mathscr{M}_{Y}\to\mathscr{O}_{Y}
is always injective.

We define the
toroidal divisor
of
Y
to be the complement
D:=Y\smallsetminus Y(0)
with the reduced closed subscheme structure. Finally, for every
p\in Y
, the
logarithmic stratum
at
p
is defined as
\mathfrak{s}_{p}:=Y(r(p))
.

4.1.2. Logarithmic derivations

The
\mathscr{O}_{Y}
-module
\Theta_{Y}^{\log}
of
logarithmic derivations
on
Y
is the
\mathscr{O}_{Y}
-submodule of
\Theta_{Y}
(
:=\mathscr{O}_{Y}
-module of usual derivations on
Y
) whose sections are derivations that preserve the ideal of
D
.

4.1.3. Logarithmic coordinates

At every point
p\in Y
, there exists a local system of
logarithmic coordinates
at
p
, which comprises of:

(i)

a local system of
ordinary coordinates

(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-r})
at
p
;
i.e.
these descend to a local system of coordinates of
\mathfrak{s}_{p}
at
p
;

(ii)

and a local
chart

\mathfrak{c}\colon M\to\mathscr{M}_{Y}(U)
which is
neat
at
p
;
i.e.
the composition
M\xrightarrow{\mathfrak{c}}\mathscr{M}_{Y}(U)\to\mathscr{M}_{Y,p}\to\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{Y,p}
is an isomorphism.

Moreover, setting
s:=\codim_{\mathfrak{s}_{p}}\overline{\{p\}}
, it is customary to arrange for the first
s
elements in the ordered tuple
(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-r})
to descend to a regular system of parameters of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathfrak{s}_{p},p}
, in which case
(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{s})
is called a local system of
ordinary parameters
at
p
. Formally, (
i
) and (
ii
) induces an isomorphism

\kappa(p)\left\llbracket x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{s},M\right\rrbracket\xrightarrow{\;\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}\vskip-1.5pt}\;}\widehat{\mathscr{O}}_{Y,p},

which sends
x_{i}
to
x_{i}
and
m\in M
to the image of
\mathfrak{c}(m)
under
\mathscr{M}_{Y}\to\mathscr{O}_{Y}
.

Remark 4.1
.

Let us now explicate the above notions in the event that
Y
is in addition a smooth
\mathds{k}
-scheme. (We refer the reader to
[
Que20
, Appendix B]
for the general case.) In this case, the toroidal divisor
D
is a simple normal crossings (snc) divisor.

At every
p\in Y
, let
D
be given locally on a neighbourhood
U
of
p
by
\prodop\displaylimits_{i>n-r}{x_{i}}=0
for a local system of coordinates
(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n})
at
p
. Then
(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-r})
is a local system of ordinary coordinates at
p
, and
(x_{n-r+1},\dotsc,x_{n})
induces a local chart
\mathfrak{c}\colon\mathbb{N}^{r}\to\mathscr{M}_{Y}(U)
that is neat at
p
. As in Section
3.2
, we usually underline the parameters
(\underline{x}_{n-r+1},\dotsc,\underline{x}_{n})
to emphasize that they are
monomial parameters
at
p
. Finally, the stalk
\Theta_{Y,p}^{\log}
is generated as a free
\mathscr{O}_{Y,p}
-module by
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq n-r
and
x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
for
n-r<i\leq n
.

4.1.4. Logarithmic differential operators

For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathscr{D}_{Y,\log}^{\leq m}
be the sheaf of
logarithmic differential operators
on
Y
of order at most
m
;
i.e.
in characteristic zero, this is simply the
\mathscr{O}_{Y}
-submodule of the total sheaf
\mathscr{D}_{Y}=\mathscr{D}_{Y}^{\infty}
of differential operators generated by
\mathscr{O}_{Y}
and the images of
(\Theta_{Y}^{\log})^{\otimes i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
. The total sheaf of logarithmic differential operators on
Y
is

\mathscr{D}_{Y}^{\log}=\mathscr{D}_{Y,\log}^{\infty}:=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{m\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathscr{D}^{\leq m}_{Y,\log}}.

We write
\mathscr{D}_{Y,\log}^{\leq m}(I)
(resp.
\mathscr{D}_{Y,\log}^{\infty}(I)
) to denote the ideal on
Y
generated by the image of
I\subset\mathscr{O}_{Y}
under the logarithmic differential operators in
\mathscr{D}_{Y,\log}^{\leq m}
(resp.
\mathscr{D}_{Y,\log}^{\infty}
).

4.1.5. Logarithmic order

The
logarithmic order
of
I\subset\mathscr{O}_{Y}
at a point
p\in Y
is

\logord_{p}(I):=\min\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\mathscr{D}^{\leq n}_{Y,\log}(I)_{p}=\mathscr{O}_{Y,p}\right\}\in\mathbb{N}\sqcup\{\infty\},

where we take
\min(\emptyset):=\infty
. We set
\max\logord(I):=\max_{p\in Y}{\logord_{p}(I)}
.

4.1.6. Monomiality

We say an ideal
Q\subset\mathscr{O}_{Y}
is
monomial
if it is generated by the image of an ideal of
\mathscr{M}_{Y}
under
\mathscr{M}_{Y}\to\mathscr{O}_{Y}
. Equivalently,
Q
is monomial if and only if
\mathscr{D}_{Y,\log}^{\leq 1}(Q)=Q
.

As in Section
3.1
, a
monomial Rees algebra

Q_{\bullet}
is a finitely generated, quasi-coherent graded
\mathscr{O}_{Y}
-subalgebra
\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{m\in\mathbb{N}}{Q_{m}\cdot t^{m}}\subset\mathscr{O}_{Y}[t]
such that each
Q_{m}\subset\mathscr{O}_{Y}
is a monomial ideal.

The
monomial saturation

\mathscr{M}_{Y}(I)
of an ideal
I\subset\mathscr{O}_{Y}
is defined as the intersection of all monomial ideals containing
I
. Equivalently,
\mathscr{M}_{Y}(I)=\mathscr{D}_{Y,\log}^{\infty}(I)
. Note that
\mathscr{M}_{Y}(I)
is itself a monomial ideal on
Y
. Lastly, for
p\in Y
,
\logord_{p}(I)=\infty
if and only if
p\in V(\mathscr{M}_{Y}(I))
;
i.e.

\logord_{p}(I)<\infty
if and only if
\mathscr{M}_{Y}(I)_{p}=\mathscr{O}_{Y,p}
.

4.1.7. Maximal contact element

If
1\leq a:=\max\logord(I)<\infty
, the (logarithmic)
maximal contact ideal
of
I
is
\MC(I)=\mathscr{D}_{Y,\log}^{\leq a-1}(I)
. For a point
p\in Y
such that
\logord_{p}(I)=a
, a (logarithmic)
maximal contact element
of
I
at
p
is a section in
\MC(I)_{p}
that is part of a system of ordinary parameters at
p
or, equivalently, that has logarithmic order
1
at
p
. Note that since
\CHAR(\mathds{k})=0
, we have
\mathscr{O}_{Y,p}=\mathscr{D}_{Y,\log}^{\leq a}(I)_{p}=\mathscr{D}_{Y,\log}^{\leq 1}(\MC(I))_{p}
, so maximal contact elements always exist locally.

4.1.8. Coefficient ideal

If
1\leq a:=\max\logord(I)<\infty
, the (logarithmic)
coefficient ideal
of an ideal
I\subset\mathscr{O}_{Y}
is the ideal

\mathscr{C}(I,a):=\left(\prodop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{a-1}{\mathscr{D}_{Y,\log}^{\leq j}(I)^{c_{j}}}\colon c_{j}\in\mathbb{N},\;\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{a-1}{(a-j)c_{j}\geq a!}\right)\subset\mathscr{O}_{Y}.

Note that
\MC(I)^{a!}\subset\mathscr{C}(I,a)
, so for any point
p\in Y
with
\logord_{p}(I)=a
and any maximal contact element
x
of
I
at
p
, we have
x^{a!}\in\mathscr{C}(I,a)_{p}
. See
[
Que20
, Section 5]
and the references therein for the motivation and properties of this construction.

4.1.9. A well-ordered set

For
k\geq 1
, we define

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{>0}^{k,!}

\displaystyle:=\text{\small$\left\{(b_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}^{k}\colon b_{1}\leq\frac{b_{2}}{(b_{1}-1)!}\leq\frac{b_{3}}{\prodop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{2}{(b_{j}-1)!}}\leq\dotsb\leq\frac{b_{k}}{\prodop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{k-1}{(b_{j}-1)!}}\right\}$},

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{\infty}^{k,!}

\displaystyle:=\left(\mathbb{N}_{>0}^{k-1,!}\times\{\infty\}\right)\sqcup\mathbb{N}_{>0}^{k,!},

and for
s\geq 1
, we set

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{>0}^{\leq s,!}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(0)\right\}\sqcup\left(\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{k=0}^{s}{\mathbb{N}_{>0}^{k,!}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{\infty}^{\leq s,!}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(0)\right\}\sqcup\left(\bigsqcupop\displaylimits_{k=0}^{s}{\mathbb{N}_{\infty}^{k,!}}\right).

We well-order the set
\mathbb{N}_{\infty}^{\leq s,!}
by the lexicographic order
<
, with a caveat: our lexicographic order considers truncations of sequences to be strictly larger. For example, in
\mathbb{N}_{\infty}^{\leq 3,!}
, we have

(0)<(1,2,8)<(1,3,6)<(1,3)<(1,4,24)<(1,\infty)<(1)<(\infty)<().

We can now recall the key construction in
[
Que20
, Section 6.1]
.

5.2. Reduction of stabilizers and destackification

In this section we sketch how one can refine Theorem
D
further to obtain a smooth
\mathds{k}
-scheme at the end, instead of a smooth Artin stack over
\mathds{k}
. The first ingredient is a special case of Edidin–Rydh’s
[
ER21
, Theorem 2.11]
. For the definitions of saturated blow-ups and strong morphisms, see
[
ER21
, Definition 3.2]
and
[
ER21
, Definition 6.8]
.

Theorem 5.8

(Reduction of stabilizers: Smooth, toroidal case)
.

Let
X
be a smooth Artin stack over
\mathds{k}
that admits a good moduli space
\mathbf{X}
, has affine diagonal, and has no generic stackiness. Let
E\subset X
be a snc divisor. Then there exists a canonical sequence of saturated blow-ups of Artin stacks
\Phi\colon X_{N}\xrightarrow{\phi_{N}}X_{N-1}\xrightarrow{\phi_{N-1}}\dotsb\xrightarrow{\phi_{1}}X_{0}=X
along smooth, closed substacks
C_{i}\subset X_{i}
, together with snc divisors
E_{i}\subset X_{i}
with
E_{0}=E
, such that the following hold:

(i)

Each
X_{i}
is a smooth Artin stack over
\mathds{k}
admitting a good moduli space
X_{i}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{i}}\mathbf{X}_{i}
.

(ii)

Each
\absolutevalue{C_i}
is the locus in
X_{i}
of points of maximum dimensional stabilizer.

(iii)

Each
\phi_{i}
restricts to an isomorphism
X_{i}\smallsetminus\phi_{i}^{-1}(C_{i-1})\xrightarrow{\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}\vskip-1.5pt}}X_{i-1}\smallsetminus\varphi_{i-1}^{-1}(\varphi_{i-1}(C_{i-1}))
.

(iv)

Each
E_{i}
is the inverse image of
C_{i-1}\cup E_{i-1}
under
\phi_{i}
.

(v)

The maximum dimension of the stabilizers of points of
X_{i}
is strictly smaller than that of the stabilizers of points of
X_{i-1}
.

(vi)

The final stack
X_{N}
has finite inertia, with coarse moduli space
X_{N}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{N}}\mathbf{X}_{N}
.

(vii)

Each
\phi_{i}
induces a schematic blow-up of good moduli spaces
\mathbf{X}_{i}\to\mathbf{X}_{i-1}
, which is an isomorphism over
\mathbf{X}_{i-1}\smallsetminus\varphi_{i-1}(C_{i-1})
.

The sequence
\Phi
does not depend on
E
. This procedure, denoted by
\mathscr{F}_{\mathrm{ros}}\colon X\mapsto X_{N}
, is functorial with respect to strong morphisms.

The second ingredient is due to Bergh–Rydh; see
[
BR19
, Theorem B]
. For the definition of stacky blow-ups, see
[
BR19
, Section 3.5; in particular, Remark 3.7]
.

Theorem 5.9

(Destackification)
.

Let
X
be a smooth Artin stack over
\mathds{k}
with finite inertia, and let
E\subset X
be a snc divisor. Then there exists a sequence of stacky blow-ups
\Psi\colon X_{N}\xrightarrow{\psi_{N}}X_{N-1}\xrightarrow{\psi_{N-1}}\dotsb\xrightarrow{\psi_{1}}X_{0}=X
along smooth weighted centers
(Z_{i},r_{i})
, together with snc divisors
E_{i}\subset X_{i}
, such that the following hold:

(i)

Each
X_{i}
is a smooth Artin stack over
\mathds{k}
, admitting a coarse moduli space
X_{i}\xrightarrow{\varphi}\mathbf{X}_{i}
.

(ii)

Each
E_{i}
is the inverse image of
Z_{i-1}\cup E_{i}
under
\psi_{i}
.

(iii)

If
\mathbf{E}_{N}\subset\mathbf{X}_{N}
denotes the coarse moduli space of
E_{N}\subset X_{N}
, then
\mathbf{E}_{N}
is a snc divisor on
\mathbf{X}_{N}
.

(iv)

Each
\psi_{i}
induces a schematic blow-up of coarse moduli spaces
\mathbf{X}_{i}\to\mathbf{X}_{i-1}
, which is an isomorphism over
\mathbf{X}_{i-1}\smallsetminus\varphi_{i-1}(Z_{i-1})
.

(v)

The final stack

X_{N}
admits a rigidification
X_{N}\to(X_{N})_{\mathrm{rig}}
such that the canonical morphism
(X_{N})_{\mathrm{rig}}\to\mathbf{X}_{N}
is an iterated root stack in
\mathbf{E}_{N}
.

This procedure, denoted by
\mathscr{F}_{\mathrm{destack}}\colon(X,E)\mapsto(X_{N},E_{N})
, is functorial with respect to smooth morphisms of pairs that are either stabilizer-preserving or tame gerbes.

Applying the above procedures after Theorem
D
, we recover Hironaka’s celebrated theorem in
[
Hir64
]
.

Theorem 5.10

(“Coarse” logarithmic resolution)
.

Given a reduced, pure-dimensional scheme
X
of finite type over
\mathds{k}
, there exists a birational and projective morphism
\Pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
such that:

(i)

X^{\prime}
is a smooth
\mathds{k}
-scheme;

(ii)

\Pi
is an isomorphism over the smooth locus
X^{\mathrm{sm}}
of
X
;

(iii)

\Pi^{-1}(X\smallsetminus X^{\mathrm{sm}})
is a snc divisor on
X^{\prime}
.

This procedure, denoted by
\mathscr{F}_{\mathrm{clr}}\colon X\mapsto X^{\prime}
, is functorial with respect to smooth morphisms.

\mathscr{Y}
over the respective strict toroidal
\mathds{k}
-schemes
\widetilde{\mathfrak{Y}}
and
\mathfrak{Y}
;

(ii)

S_{P}
(resp.
S_{Q}
) is the set of extremal rays of
C(P)^{\vee}
(resp.
C(Q)^{\vee}
);

(iii)

and
\iota_{P}
(resp.
\iota_{Q}
) is the morphism that maps every
\mathbf{a}\in P
(resp.
\mathbf{a}\in Q
) to
\sumop\displaylimits_{\rho\in S_{P}}{(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{\rho})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\rho}}
(resp.
\sumop\displaylimits_{\theta\in S_{Q}}{(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{\theta})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\theta}}
).

We now claim that there is one and only one toric morphism
\widetilde{\mathscr{Y}}\to\mathscr{Y}
filling the dotted arrow in (
5.6
). Indeed, a monoid homomorphism
\nu=(c_{\theta,\rho})_{\rho\in S_{P},\ \theta\in S_{Q}}\colon\mathbb{N}^{S_{P}}\to\mathbb{N}^{S_{Q}}
fills up the dotted arrow in (
5.6
) if and only if

\varphi^{\vee}(\mathbf{u}_{\theta})=\sumop\displaylimits_{\rho\in S_{P}}{c_{\theta,\rho}\mathbf{u}_{\rho}}\quad\textrm{for every }\theta\in S_{Q},

where
\varphi^{\vee}\colon C(Q)^{\vee}\to C(P)^{\vee}
is the dual morphism to
\varphi\colon C(P)\to C(Q)
. In light of this observation, it suffices to show that if
f
is equidimensional, then for every
\theta\in S_{Q}
, there is one and only one way of expressing
\varphi^{\vee}(\mathbf{u}_{\theta})
as an
\mathbb{N}
-linear combination of the vectors
\mathbf{u}_{\rho}
for
\rho\in S_{P}
. This is a mere consequence of
[
AK94
, Lemma 4.1]
: if
f
is equidimensional, the image of every extremal ray in
C(Q)^{\vee}
under
\varphi^{\vee}
is an extremal ray in
C(P)^{\vee}
.

Finally, using Kato’s criterion for logarithmic smoothness, see
[
Kat94
, Section 8]
, the above argument can be patched up to settle the case where
\widetilde{\mathfrak{Y}}
and
\mathfrak{Y}
are any strict toroidal
\mathds{k}
-schemes. From this and Remark
3.7
, the lemma can be deduced in the general case where
\widetilde{\mathfrak{Y}}
and
\mathfrak{Y}
are toroidal Artin stacks.
∎

Remark 5.14
.

Note that unless
\widetilde{\mathfrak{Y}}\to\mathfrak{Y}
is strict (see Remark
3.7
), the induced morphism
\widetilde{\mathscr{Y}}\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{Y}}\times{Y}\mathscr{Y}
is typically not an isomorphism. This already happens when
\mathfrak{Y}
is a simplicial toric variety and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{Y}}
is not. Instead, the above morphism exhibits
\widetilde{\mathscr{Y}}
as Satriano’s construction applied to the fibre product
\widetilde{\mathfrak{Y}}\times{Y}\mathscr{Y}
in the category of fs logarithmic Artin stacks.

Appendix. A lemma on quotient stacks

Lemma A.1

(
=

[
QR22
, Lemma 1.3.1]
)
.

Let
A
be a finitely generated abelian group, with corresponding
diagonalizable algebraic group
D(A)
. Let
R=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\alpha\in A}{R_{\alpha}}
be an
A
-graded algebra, and let
r\in R
be a
homogeneous element of degree
a\in A
. Then
R/(r-1)
is an

A/\langle a\rangle
-graded algebra, and the
A/\langle a\rangle
-graded homomorphism
R\to R/(r-1)
induces a morphism of algebraic stacks

\left[\left(\Spec(R/(r-1)\right)\;/\;D\left(A/\langle a\rangle\right)\right]\xrightarrow{\;\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}\vskip-1.5pt}\;}[\Spec(R)\;/\;D(A)].

This is an isomorphism if
r
is invertible and
a
has infinite order.

Note that as
A
-graded modules
R\simeq R/(r-1)[r,r^{-1}]
, but the algebra
structures do not coincide. Similarly,
R/(r-1)\simeq\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{[\alpha]\in A/\langle a\rangle}R_{\alpha}

but only as
A/\langle a\rangle
-graded modules.

Proof.

We need to prove that the natural
D(A)
-equivariant map

\Spec\left(R/(r-1)\right)\times^{D(A/\langle a\rangle)}D(A)\longrightarrow\Spec(R)

is an isomorphism. Let us elaborate on the left-hand side. We have two commuting actions on
{\Spec\left(R/(r-1)\right)\times D(A)}=\Spec(R/(r-1)[v^{A}]):=\Spec(R/(r-1)[v^{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in A])
:

(i)

the diagonal
D(A/\langle a\rangle)
-action, given by
(y,t)\cdot s=(ys,s^{-1}t)
, where in the first factor the action corresponds to the induced
A/\langle a\rangle
-grading on
R/(r-1)
, and

(ii)

the
D(A)
-action on the second factor given by
(y,t)\cdot s=(y,ts)
.

The
D(A/\langle a\rangle)
-action is free with quotient
\Spec\left(R/(r-1)\right)\times^{D(A/\langle a\rangle)}D(A)=\Spec(R^{\circ})
, where
R^{\circ}
is the degree
0
part of
R/(r-1)[v^{A}]
with the
A/\langle a\rangle
-grading. The
D(A)
-action endows
R^{\circ}
with the following
A
-grading:

R^{\circ}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\alpha\in A}\left(R/(r-1)\right)_{[\alpha]}v^{\alpha}.

The natural
A
-graded algebra homomorphism
R\to R^{\circ}
is thus an isomorphism.
∎

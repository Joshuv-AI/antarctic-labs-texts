Abstract
. We study a generalized mean curvature flow involving a positive power of the mean curvature and a driving force. In this paper, we first construct all kinds of radially symmetric translating solutions, and then select one of them to satisfy a prescribed boundary angle in a cylinder. We then consider the flow starting at an initial hypersurface: showing the a priori estimates (especially the uniform-in-time bounds for the mean curvature which guarantee the uniform parabolicity of the corresponding fully nonlinear equation), giving the global existence for the solution of the initial boundary value problem, and proving its convergence to the corresponding translating solution. Our study provides a complete exposition on the influence of the dimension, the power of the mean curvature, the driving force and the boundary angles on the existence and stability of radially symmetric translating solutions.

1.
Introduction

Consider the following mean curvature flow

(1.1)

\frac{\partial{\gamma}(t)}{\partial t}=(H^{\alpha}+b){\bf n},

where
\alpha>0
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
,
{\gamma}(t)
is a moving smooth hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
,
H
denotes its mean curvature, and
{\bf n}
denotes its unit normal vector.
In the special case where
{\gamma}(t)
is a graphic hypersurface in a cylinder

D\times\mathbb{R}
for
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, that is,
{\gamma}(t)
is the graph of a function
x_{N+1}=u(x,t),\ x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in D
, then we have

(1.2)

{\bf n}=\frac{(-Du,1)}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}},\quad H=\mathrm{div}\,{\bf n},

with
Du:=(D_{1}u,D_{2}u,\cdots,D_{N}u)
and
D_{i}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\ (i=1,2,\cdots,N)
. Here we assume
{\bf n}
is the upward unit normal vector. In this case, the equation (
1.1
) is converted into

(1.3)

\frac{u_{t}}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}=(\mathrm{div}\,{\bf n})^{\alpha}+b=\left[\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{D_{i}uD_{j}u}{1+|Du|^{2}}\right)\frac{D_{ij}u}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right]^{\alpha}+b,\quad x\in D,\ t>0.

This is a fully nonlinear parabolic equation when
\alpha\not=1
and
H^{\alpha-1}>0
, and a quasilinear one when
\alpha=1
. For convenience, we call the former as a
generalized mean curvature flow
(GMCF, for short), and the latter as a
mean curvature flow
(MCF, for short).
When
D
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, this equation is often equipped with prescribed boundary angles as boundary conditions:

(1.4)

{\bf n}\cdot(\nu,0)=\frac{-Du\cdot\nu}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}=g(x,t,u(x,t)),\quad x\in\partial D,\ t>0,

where
\nu
denotes the inner unit normal vector on the boundary
\partial D
of
D
,
g
is a function satisfying
|g|<1
, and
\theta:=\arccos(g)
denotes the contact angle between
\gamma(t)
and
\partial D\times\mathbb{R}
, which is called a prescribed boundary angle.

If
g
is independent of
t
and
u
, the problem (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) may have a special solution of the form

(1.5)

u(x,t)=\varphi(x)+ct,

which is called a
translation solution
(TS, for short) or a
traveling wave solution
(TW, for short),
\varphi(x),\ c\in\mathbb{R}
are called the
profile
and the
speed
of the TS, respectively. This kind of solutions are of special importance since they are generally asymptotically stable, and so attract the solutions of the initial or initial-boundary value problems. We now recall some known results on the TSs of MCFs (only for the case
\alpha=1
).

1.
The cases without driving forces:
b=0
.

In 1993, Altschuler and Wu
[
5
]
studied the problem with
N=\alpha=1,\ b=0
, that is, the problem

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle u_{t}=\frac{u_{xx}}{1+u_{x}^{2}},&x\in(-1,1),\ t>0,\\
u_{x}(\pm 1,t)=\pm g_{0},&t>0,\end{array}\right.

for some positive constant
g_{0}
. They proved that any global solution of (
1.6
) with some initial data converges to the unique (up to a shift) TS
\varphi_{0}(x)+c_{0}t
, where

(1.7)

\varphi_{0}(x):=-\frac{1}{c_{0}}\ln[\cos(c_{0}x)],\quad c_{0}:=\arctan g_{0},

which is called
the grim reaper
. In higher space dimension, Huisken
[
13
]
proved in 1989 that, in case
N\geq 1,\ g=0
, the solution of (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) converges to a minimal surface. In 1994, Altschuler and Wu
[
6
]
extended their result in
[
5
]
to two dimension case: when
N=2
,
D
is strictly convex,
g=g(x)
with
|D_{T}g|
small, a solution either converges to a minimal surface or to a TS. Recently, Ma, Wang and Wei
[
18
]
derived uniform gradient estimates for the problem with
N\geq 1
,
g=g(x)
and
D
being strictly convex, and proved that the solution converges to a TS.

2.
The cases with driving forces:
b\not=0
.

Mean curvature flows with driving forces arise in the study of scroll waves in excitable media (cf.
[
14
]
),
and in the singular limits of partial differential equations such as the Allen-Cahn equation (cf.
[
1
,
12
]
), where the driving force represents the difference of the stability between two stable phases; it is also a topic in geometry analysis (cf.
[
3
,
4
,
11
]
etc.).
The TSs of such equations has also been studied in the last decades. For example, in 2000-2001, Ninomiya and Taniguchi
[
21
,
22
]
consider the problem (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) with
N=\alpha=1
and
b>0
, they constructed a TS with V-shaped profile, and specified its shape precisely. In 2006, Nara and Taniguchi
[
20
]
further studied the stability of this TS. In 2009, Lou
[
15
]
also considered the case
N=\alpha=1
. For any given
b\in\mathbb{R}
, he constructed all possible TSs, gave the formulas of their profiles.

3.
MCFs in a cylinder with non-constant boundary angles.
In case the boundary angle
\arccos g
is spatially and/or temporally heterogeneous, that is,
g
depends on
t
and/or
u
, the problem no longer has TSs as in (
1.5
). Instead, it may have TSs with changing profiles. For example, in 2006 and 2013, Matano et al.
[
16
,
19
]
considered the equation (
1.3
) with
N=\alpha=1,\ b>0
in a band domain with undulating boundaries. Their problem, in some sense, is equivalent to (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) with
N=\alpha=1,\ g=g(u)
being (almost) periodic. They constructed the so-called
(almost) periodic traveling wave solution
:
u=\varphi(x,t)+ct
for some (almost) periodic profile
\varphi(x,t)
, studied its uniqueness and asymptotic stability. Later, Cai and Lou
[
7
]
etc. obtained analogues for the problems with
g
being almost periodic in
t
. To distinguish the generalized (almost) periodic TWs from that in (
1.5
), we will call
\varphi(x)+ct
as a
classical TS
.

Another problem about the MCFs in a cylinder is the problem with unbounded boundary slopes. In the works we mentioned above, the boundary slopes
Du\cdot\nu
are all bounded. In 2012, Chou and Wang
[
10
]
considered the equation in (
1.6
) with Robin boundary conditions like

(1.8)

u_{x}(1,t)=u(1,t),\quad u_{x}(-1,t)=-u(-1,t),\quad t>0,

that is, the boundary slopes can be unbounded when
u\to\infty
. In this case, a priori gradient estimates depending on
t
are possible and so the global existence of the solutions can be obtained in a standard way. However, the uniform-in-time a priori gradient estimates is impossible, although it is necessary in the study for the convergence of the profile of a global solution. Recently, we revisited this problem in
[
17
]
. We used the zero number argument to give a uniform interior gradient estimates, and then proved the convergence of any solution to the grim reaper in the open interval
(-1,1)
.

TSs of generalized mean curvature flows, such as
(
1.1
) and (
1.3
) with
\alpha\not=1,\ b\not=0
, seems not well studied till now. In a series of papers, we want to use the equation (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) to provide a complete exposition on the influence of the dimension
N
, the power
\alpha
, the driving force
b
and the boundary angle
g
on the profiles, the propagating speeds and the stability of the corresponding TSs. More precisely, in this series, we will consider the following cases respectively:

•

constant boundary angles;

•

Robin boundary conditions as in (
1.8
) (that is, unbounded boundary slopes);

•

almost periodic boundary angles an almost periodic TSs;

•

general inhomogeneous boundary conditions for non-radially symmetric solutions.

This is the first paper of the series. In this paper we first construct
all possible
radially symmetric TSs of (
1.3
) for any
b\in\mathbb{R}
(which will be used frequently in this series for comparison), and then use the TSs to study the existence, uniqueness and the asymptotical behavior of the flows, starting at some initial data, in a cylinder satisfying (
1.4
) with constant boundary angles.

Throughout this paper we assume
N>1
(the plane curves when
N=1
are simpler and can be considered in a similar way as this paper). We mainly consider the radially symmetric solutions:
u=u(|x|,t)=u(r,t)
. In this case, the equation (
1.3
) is rewritten as

(1.9)

u_{t}=(H^{\alpha}+b)\sqrt{1+u^{2}_{r}}=\left(\frac{u_{rr}}{1+u^{2}_{r}}+\frac{N-1}{r}u_{r}\right)^{\alpha}\left(1+u^{2}_{r}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}+b\sqrt{1+u^{2}_{r}},\ \quad r>0,\ t>0,

where

(1.10)

H(r,t):=\frac{u_{rr}}{(1+u^{2}_{r})^{3/2}}+\frac{(N-1)u_{r}}{r\sqrt{1+u^{2}_{r}}}

is the mean curvature. When we consider the problem in the cylinder

\Omega:=B\times\mathbb{R}\quad\mbox{with}\quad B:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid|x|\leq 1\},

the radially symmetry and the boundary condition (
1.4
) are expressed as

(1.11)

u_{r}(0,t)=0,\quad u_{r}(1,t)=k(t,u(1,t)):=\frac{g(t,u)}{\sqrt{1-g^{2}(t,u)}},\quad t>0.

We consider in this paper the homogeneous boundary conditions, that is, the case
k
is a constant. We will study the global existence of solution
u(r,t)
of (
1.9
)-(
1.11
) with initial data
u(r,0)=u_{0}(r)
satisfying

(1.12)

u_{0}(r)\in C^{2}([0,1]),\quad u^{\prime}_{0}(0)=0,\quad u^{\prime}_{0}(1)=k\in\mathbb{R},

as well as its convergence to the corresponding TS.

Since the equation is a fully nonlinear one when
\alpha\not=1
,
in order to study the global existence and the convergence of
u
, we need not only the uniform-in-time
C^{2}
a priori estimates for
u
, but also the uniform-in-time parabolicity for the equation. More precisely, when we consider a flow with
H>0
, we need to show that
H
has
positive lower and upper bounds
, or in the case where
H<0
, we need to show that
H
has
negative lower and upper bounds
. These estimates are crucial in our approach. They turn out to be true under suitable assumptions on the parameters
b,k
and the initial data
u_{0}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u(r,t)
be a classical solution of (
1.9
)-(
1.11
)-(
1.12
) in the time interval
[0,T)
, then there exist positive numbers
M_{0},M_{1},M_{2},V_{*},V^{*},H_{*},H^{*}
, independent of
t
and
T
, and
\tilde{c}(b,k)>0
such that

(1.13)

\displaystyle|u(r,t)-\tilde{c}(b,k)t|\leq M_{0},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T);

(1.14)

\displaystyle 0<u_{r}(r,t)\cdot{\rm sgn}(k)\leq M_{1},\quad r\in(0,1],\ t\in[0,T);

(1.15)

\displaystyle V_{*}\leq u_{t}(r,t)\leq V^{*},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T);

(1.16)

\displaystyle H_{*}\leq H(r,t)\cdot{\rm sgn}(k)\leq H^{*},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T);

(1.17)

\displaystyle|u_{rr}(r,t)|\leq M_{2},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T),

provided the constants
b,k
and the initial data
u_{0}
satisfy one of the following conditions

(A).

b=0<k
and
H(r,0)>0\ \ (r\in[0,1])
;

(B).

b<0<k
,
(-b)^{{1/\alpha}}\sqrt{1+k^{2}}<kN
and
H^{\alpha}(r,0)+b>0\ \ (r\in[0,1])
;

(C).

b>0,\ k>0
and
u^{\prime\prime}_{0}(r)>0\ \ (r\in[0,1])
;

(D).

\alpha=\frac{q}{p}
for some positive odd number
p
and
q
,
b>0>k
,
b^{{1/\alpha}}\sqrt{1+k^{2}}>-kN
,
u^{\prime\prime}_{0}(r)<0
and
H^{\alpha}(r,0)+b>0\ \ (r\in[0,1])
.

In case (D) we actually consider solutions with negative curvature:
H<0
, so in order for
H^{\alpha}
to make sense and for the equation to be a parabolic one:
H^{\alpha-1}>0
, we consider only the case where
\alpha=\frac{q}{p}
for positive odd
p,q
(see more in Subsection
3.2
).
As we will see in Subsections 4.1 and 5.1, in the cases (A) and (B) where
b\leq 0
, the above estimates can be derived from the equation (
1.9
) and the comparison principle. In the case (C) where
H>0
and
b>0
, however, the derivation for the positive lower bound of
H
is not so easy, which includes several steps: to derive a positive lower bound for
u_{rr}(0,t)
by using the zero number argument (Lemma 5.2), to show
u
is steeper than a TS
\Phi_{1}
(Lemma 5.3), to show
u_{rr}(r,t)>0
for all
r\in[0,1]
(Lemma 5.4), and to derive the positive lower bound for
H
(Proposition 5.5). (In the case (D) where
H<0
and
b>0
, the derivation for the negative upper bound of
H
is similar.)

Once we have the a priori estimates in hand, we can show the convergence of
u
to the corresponding TS, which is our main result in this paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume the hypotheses
(A), (B), (C)
or
(D)
in the previous theorem holds. Then

(i)

the problem (
1.9
)-(
1.11
) has a unique (up to a shift) TS
\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+\tilde{c}(b,k)t
;

(ii)

the problem (
1.9
)-(
1.11
)-(
1.12
) has a unique time-global classical solution
u(r,t)
;

(iii)

there exists
M\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(1.18)

\|u(\cdot,t)-\widetilde{\Phi}(\cdot)-\tilde{c}(b,k)t\|_{L^{\infty}([0,1])}\to M\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ t\to\infty.

This theorem extends the result in
[
5
,
6
]
to the cases where
\alpha\not=1
and
b\not=0
, for radially symmetric solutions.
The convergence in (iii) is equivalent to say that the TS is asymptotically stabile.

A TS of the equation (
1.9
) (without the restriction (
1.11
) at this stage) has the form
u=\varphi(r)+ct
, with
(c,\varphi)
satisfying

(1.19)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle c=\left(\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{1+\varphi^{\prime 2}}+\frac{N-1}{r}\varphi^{\prime}\right)^{\alpha}\left(1+\varphi^{\prime 2}_{r}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}+b\sqrt{1+\varphi^{\prime 2}},\ \quad r>0,\\
\varphi(0)=\varphi^{\prime}(0)=0.\end{array}\right.

The additional condition
\varphi(0)=0
is used to normalize the profile of
\varphi
.
We consider only the case
c>0
, since when the hypersurface moves with speed
c<0
, we only need to regard the flow as one moving to the
-x_{N+1}
direction with a positive speed. To show the existence and the uniqueness of the TS of (
1.9
)-(
1.11
), we first prepare the TSs of (
1.9
), and then select some of them to satisfy the boundary condition at
r=1
. The following result presents
all possible
solutions of (
1.19
).

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
b\in\mathbb{R}
and any
c>0
, the problem (
1.19
) has a unique solution
\varphi=\Phi(r;c,b)
in a maximal existence interval
J:=[0,R_{\infty}(c,b))
. It is smooth in
J
,
\Phi(r;c,b)
and
\Phi^{\prime}(r;c,b)
are strictly increasing in
c
and
-b
. Moreover,

(i).

in case
b=0
, we have
R_{\infty}=\infty
,
\Phi^{\prime}(r),\ \Phi^{\prime\prime}(r)>0
for
r>0
and

(1.20)

\Phi(r)\sim\frac{c}{(\alpha+1)(N-1)^{\alpha}}r^{\alpha+1}\mbox{\ \ as\ \ }r\to\infty;

(ii).

in case
b<0
, we have
R_{\infty}(c,b)<\infty
,
\Phi^{\prime}(r),\ \Phi^{\prime\prime}(r)>0
in
(0,R_{\infty})
with

(1.21)

\Phi(R_{\infty}-0)<\infty,\quad\Phi^{\prime}(R_{\infty}-0)=\Phi^{\prime\prime}(R_{\infty}-0)=\infty;

(iii).

in case

, we have
R_{\infty}=\infty
,
\Phi^{\prime}(r),\ \Phi^{\prime\prime}(r)>0
in
(0,\infty)
with

\Phi^{\prime}(r)\to\Psi_{0}:=\frac{\sqrt{c^{2}-b^{2}}}{b}\mbox{\ \ as\ \ }r\to\infty;

(iv).

in case

, we have
R_{\infty}=\infty
, and
\Phi(r)\equiv 0
;

(v).

in case
\alpha=\frac{q}{p}
for some odd number

and

, we have
R_{\infty}(c,b)<\infty
,
\Phi^{\prime}(r),\ \Phi^{\prime\prime}(r)<0
in
(0,R_{\infty})
, and

(1.22)

\Phi(R_{\infty}-0)>-\infty,\quad\Phi^{\prime}(R_{\infty}-0)=\Phi^{\prime\prime}(R_{\infty}-0)=-\infty.

As we have mentioned above, in case
b=0
, the profile of the TS is a grim reaper when
N=\alpha=1
. Here we present the (asymptotic) profiles of the TSs for all
\alpha>0
as in (
1.20
).
In the special case
\alpha=1
, our result coincides with that in
[

]
, where the authors consider the Allen-Cahn equation and proved that the level set of its solution (which roughly obeys the MCF) is asymptotically a paraboloid

\Phi(r)\sim\frac{c}{2(N-1)}|x|^{2},\quad r\to\infty,

(see also
[

]
for the case

).
In cases (ii) and (v) we have
R_{\infty}<\infty
and
|\Phi(R_{\infty}-0)|<\infty
, so the profiles of the TSs are
cup-like
ones in case (ii) and

ones in case (v), as it was shown in
[
15
]
for the case
N=\alpha=1
.
In case (iii), the profiles of the TSs are cone-shaped ones, as the V-shaped TSs in the case
N=\alpha=1
(
[

22
]
). However, each of them is only an approximate but not exact cone since

We then select some TSs from the above candidates so that they lie in the cylinder

, and satisfy the boundary conditions in (

) for some

, that is,

\varphi^{\prime}(1)=k.

.

Assume one of the following conditions holds:

(a).

b=0<k
;

(b).

(c).

2.
Translating Solutions in Case
b\leq 0

In this section we study the TSs in case
b\leq 0<c
. We first construct TSs of (
1.19
), and then select some of them to satisfy (
1.23
) for given
k>0
.

2.1.
TSs on their maximal existence intervals

In this subsection we construct solutions of (
1.19
) in case
b\leq 0
. Our main result to be proved is the following one.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any
b\leq 0
and any
c>0
, the problem (
1.19
) has a unique solution
\varphi=\Phi(r;c,b)
in a maximal existence interval
J:=[0,R_{\infty}(c,b))
. It is smooth in
J
,
\Phi(r;c,b)
and
\Phi^{\prime}(r;c,b)
are strictly increasing in
c
and
-b
. In addition,

(I).

when
b=0
,
R_{\infty}=\infty
,
\Phi^{\prime}(r),\ \Phi^{\prime\prime}(r)>0
for
r>0
, (
1.20
) holds, and

(2.1)

\Phi^{\prime}(r;c,0)\to\infty\mbox{\ \ as\ \ }c\to\infty;

(II).

when
b<0
,

satisfies

(2.2)

N(c-b)^{-1/\alpha}\leq R_{\infty}(c,b)\leq N(-b)^{-1/\alpha},

it is continuously dependent on and strictly decreasing in

with

(2.3)

R_{\infty}(c,b)\to N(-b)^{-1/\alpha}\mbox{\ \ as\ \ }c\to 0,\quad R_{\infty}(c,b)\to 0\mbox{\ \ as\ \ }c\to\infty.

In addition,
\Phi^{\prime}(r),\ \Phi^{\prime\prime}(r)>0
in
(0,R_{\infty})
, and

(2.4)

\Phi(R_{\infty}-0)<\infty,\quad\Phi^{\prime}(R_{\infty}-0)=\Phi^{\prime\prime}(R_{\infty}-0)=\infty,

and
\Phi(R_{\infty}(c,b)-0)
is decreasing in

.

The problem (
1.19
) is equivalent to

(2.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{1+\varphi^{\prime 2}}+\frac{N-1}{r}\varphi^{\prime}=\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{1+\varphi^{\prime 2}}}-b\right)^{{1/\alpha}}\sqrt{1+\varphi^{\prime 2}},\quad\ r>0,\\
\varphi(0)=\varphi^{\prime}(0)=0.\end{array}\right.

Due to the singularity of the term
\frac{N-1}{r}
at
r=0
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we first consider the approximate problem

(2.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}}{1+\varphi^{\prime 2}}+\frac{N-1}{r+\varepsilon}\varphi^{\prime}=\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{1+\varphi^{\prime 2}}}-b\right)^{{1/\alpha}}\sqrt{1+\varphi^{\prime 2}},\quad\ r\geq 0,\\
\varphi(0)=\varphi^{\prime}(0)=0.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
c>0
, the problem (
2.6
) has a unique solution
\varphi=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(r;c,b)
in a maximal existence interval
[0,R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty})
with

(2.7)

\varphi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(r),\ \varphi^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(r)>0\quad\text{in}\quad\left(0,R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}\right),\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{\varepsilon}(r),\ \varphi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(r)\to\infty\quad\text{as}\quad r\to R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}.

Moreover, when
b=0
there holds
R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}=\infty
; while, when
b<0
, there holds

(2.8)

R_{-}:=\left(c-b\right)^{-{1/\alpha}}\leq R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}\leq R_{+}:=(-b)^{-{1/\alpha}}[N+\ln 2].

Proof.

Set
\psi_{\varepsilon}:=\varphi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
, then (
2.6
) is converted to

(2.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}}=F\left(r,\psi_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon,c,b\right):=\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}}}-b\right)^{{1/\alpha}}\sqrt{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}}-\frac{N-1}{r+\varepsilon}\psi_{\varepsilon},\qquad r\geq 0,\\
\psi_{\varepsilon}(0)=0.\end{array}\right.

Denote its maximal existence interval by
[0,R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty})
.
We first prove the following claim.

Claim 1.

\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)>0,\ \psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(r)>0
in
J^{\varepsilon}:=(0,R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty})
.

In fact, due to
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)=\left(c-b\right)^{{1/\alpha}}>0
, we have
\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)>0
for
0<r\ll 1
. If there exists
r_{1}>0
such that
\psi_{\varepsilon}(r_{1})=0
and
\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)>0
in
(0,r_{1})
, then
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(r_{1})\leq 0
. But this contradicts the equation in (
2.9
). Hence
\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)>0
in
J^{\varepsilon}
. Next, by differentiating (
2.9
) in
r
we have

(2.10)

\frac{\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}\left(1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}\right)-2\psi_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2}}{\left(1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}\right)^{2}}=A(r,\psi_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon,c,b),\quad r\geq 0,

where

A(r,\psi_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon,c,b):=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}}}-b\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{c(\alpha-1)\psi_{\varepsilon}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}}-\frac{b\alpha\psi_{\varepsilon}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}}}\right)-(N-1)\frac{\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(r+\varepsilon)-\psi_{\varepsilon}}{\left(r+\varepsilon\right)^{2}}.

Since
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)>0
, we have
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(r)>0
for
0<r\ll 1
by continuity. Assume
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(r)>0
in
[0,r_{2})
and
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(r_{2})=0
. Then
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(r_{2})\leq 0
. This, however, contradicts the equation (
2.10
) at
r_{2}
. This proves Claim 1.

By Claim 1 we have three possibilities:

(a).

R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}<\infty
and
\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)\to\infty
as
r\to R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}-0
;

(b).

R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}=\infty
and
\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)\to\infty
as
r\to\infty
;

(c).

R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}=\infty
and
\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)\to\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}>0
as
r\to\infty
.

On these possibilities we have the following claim

Claim 2.
(a) and (
2.8
) hold when
b<0
; (b) holds when
b=0
; (c) does not holds for any
b
.

By contradiction we assume (c) holds. Then by the equation in (
2.9
) we have

\frac{\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}}=F\left(r,\psi_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon,c,b\right)\to\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{1+\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}^{2}}}-b\right)^{{1/\alpha}}\sqrt{1+\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}^{2}}>0\mbox{\ \ as\ \ }r\to\infty,

since
c>0\geq b
. This contradicts
\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)\to\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}
.

Next we consider the case
b=0
. If
R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}<\infty
, then
\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)\to\infty
and
r\to R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}-0
, and so by the equation (
2.9
) we have

\frac{\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}{\psi_{\varepsilon}(1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2})}=c^{{1/\alpha}}(1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2})^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha}}\frac{1}{\psi_{\varepsilon}}-\frac{N-1}{r+\varepsilon}\to-\frac{N-1}{R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}+\varepsilon}<0\mbox{\ \ as\ \ }r\to R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}-0,

contradicts
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(r)>0
. Therefore
R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}=\infty
.

Finally we consider the case
b<0
. We show that
R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}<\infty
and so (a) holds in this case. Otherwise, if
R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}=\infty
, then for

r\geq R_{1}:=2(N-1)(-b)^{-{1/\alpha}}
, by the equation (
2.9
) we have

\frac{\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}}>(-b)^{{1/\alpha}}\sqrt{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}}-\frac{N-1}{r+\varepsilon}\psi_{\varepsilon}\geq\left[(-b)^{{1/\alpha}}-\frac{N-1}{r}\right]\psi_{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{(-b)^{{1/\alpha}}}{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}.

Integrating it over
[R_{1},r)
we have

\ln\frac{\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)}{\sqrt{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}(r)}}>\ln\frac{\psi_{\varepsilon}(R_{1})}{\sqrt{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}(R_{1})}}+\frac{(-b)^{{1/\alpha}}}{2}(r-R_{1}).

This implies that

\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)\to\infty\mbox{\ \ as\ \ }r\to R_{2},

for some

(2.11)

R_{2}\leq R_{1}-2(-b)^{-{1/\alpha}}\ln\frac{\psi_{\varepsilon}(R_{1})}{\sqrt{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}(R_{1})}}.

This contradicts
R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}=\infty
. Furthermore, we can derive the upper and lower estimates for
R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}
. By (
2.9
) we have

\frac{\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\leq(c-b)^{{1/\alpha}}\sqrt{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}}.

Integrating it over
[0,R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty})
we obtain
R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}\geq R_{-}=(c-b)^{-{1/\alpha}}
. On the other hand, by the positivity of
\psi_{\varepsilon},\ \psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
and the equation (
2.9
) we have

\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}+\frac{N-1}{r}\psi_{\varepsilon}>\frac{\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}}+\frac{N-1}{r+\varepsilon}\psi_{\varepsilon}\geq(-b)^{{1/\alpha}},\quad r>0.

Hence,
\left(r^{N-1}\psi_{\varepsilon}\right)^{\prime}>(-b)^{{1/\alpha}}r^{N-1}
for
r>0
. Integrating it over
[0,r]
we obtain
\psi_{\varepsilon}>\frac{(-b)^{{1/\alpha}}}{N}r
. Thus

\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)\geq 1,\quad r\geq R_{0}:=N(-b)^{-{1/\alpha}}.

If
R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}\leq R_{0}
, then the upper estimate is already obtained. In what follows we assume
R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}>R_{0}
. Then in
[R_{0},R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty})
, in a similar way as deriving (
2.11
) we have

R^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}\leq R_{0}-2(-b)^{-{1/\alpha}}\ln\frac{\psi_{\varepsilon}(R_{0})}{\sqrt{1+\psi_{\varepsilon}^{2}(R_{0})}}\leq(-b)^{-{1/\alpha}}[N+\ln 2]=R_{+}.

This proves Claim 2, and completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We will take limit as
\varepsilon
going to
0
for
\psi_{\varepsilon}(r;c,b)
to obtain the solution of (

) rather than to (

and
\psi_{\varepsilon}
in

, and their a priori estimates.

Lemma 2.3

Both
\psi_{\varepsilon}
and

For any

\varphi_{\varepsilon_{1}}(r)<\varphi_{\varepsilon_{2}}(r)

.
∎

From this lemma we see that, when

,

and it is decreasing in

, and so

When

Next we give some

.

Lemma 2.4

For any given

(2)

By the definition of

in (

(1). For any

Proof of Theorem

(II).
We prove the theorem in case

Step 1. Construction and monotonicity of
\Phi
. Let

\{\varepsilon_{i}\}\subset(0,\varepsilon_{0}]

\|\varphi_{\varepsilon_{i}}(r;c,b)-\Phi(r)\|_{C\left([0,R]\right)}\to 0{\ \ as\ \ }i\to\infty.

(denote it again by

Moreover, by Lemma

\Phi^{\prime}(r)\geq 0,\ \ \Phi^{\prime\prime}(r)\geq 0,\quad r\in(0,R_{\infty}).

Arguing as in the proof of Claim 1 in Lemma

\Phi^{\prime}(r)>0,\ \ \Phi^{\prime\prime}(r)>0,\quad r\in(0,R_{\infty}).

We remark that the interval

For simplicity, in what follows we always denote

Step 2. To show

For any

\int^{r}_{\delta}\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)dr<\varphi_{\varepsilon}(r)=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(r)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(0)=\int^{r}_{\delta}\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)dr+\int^{\delta}_{0}\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)dr\leq\int^{r}_{\delta}\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)dr+\psi_{\varepsilon_{0}}(R)\delta.

Taking

\int^{r}_{\delta}\Psi(r)dr\leq\Phi(r)\leq\int^{r}_{\delta}\Psi(r)dr+\psi_{\varepsilon_{0}}(R)\delta.

Note that

This implies that

Since

\Phi^{\prime}(0)=0
.
Therefore,
\Phi\in C^{1}\left([0,R_{\infty})\right)

Using the equation (

For any

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\psi^{\prime}}{1+\psi^{2}}+\frac{N-1}{r}\psi=\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{1+\psi^{2}}}-b\right)^{{1/\alpha}}\sqrt{1+\psi^{2}},\quad\ r>0,\\

\frac{-\eta^{\prime}}{1+(\Psi_{0}-\eta)^{2}}+\frac{N-1}{r}(\Psi_{0}-\eta)=\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{1+(\Psi_{0}-\eta)^{2}}}-\frac{c}{\sqrt{1+\Psi_{0}^{2}}}\right)^{1/\alpha}\sqrt{1+(\Psi_{0}-\eta)^{2}},

that is,

\eta^{\prime}(r)=\frac{-F(\eta)}{A(\eta)}\eta^{1/\alpha}+\frac{N-1}{A(\eta)r}(\Psi_{0}-\eta),

with

A(\eta):=\frac{1}{1+(\Psi_{0}-\eta)^{2}},

and

F(\eta):=\left[\frac{c(2\Psi_{0}-\eta)}{\sqrt{1+(\Psi_{0}-\eta)^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{1+\Psi_{0}^{2}}\cdot[\sqrt{1+(\Psi_{0}-\eta)^{2}}+\sqrt{1+\Psi_{0}^{2}}]}\right]^{1/\alpha}\sqrt{1+(\Psi_{0}-\eta)^{2}}.

Therefore, when
r\gg 1
, that is,
0<\eta\ll 1
, we have

A_{0}:=\frac{1}{1+\Psi_{0}^{2}}<A(\eta)\leq\frac{3}{2}A_{0},\quad F(\eta)\leq F^{0}:=\frac{(2c\Psi_{0})^{1/\alpha}}{(1+\Psi_{0}^{2})^{(3-\alpha)/(2\alpha)}}.

In case
0<\alpha\leq 1
we have

(3.3)

\eta^{\prime}(r)\geq-B_{1}\eta^{1/\alpha}+\frac{B_{2}}{r}\geq-B_{1}\eta+\frac{B_{2}}{r},\quad r\geq r_{1},

for sufficiently large

, where

B_{1}:=\frac{F^{0}}{A_{0}}>0,\quad B_{2}:=\frac{(N-1)\Psi_{0}}{2A_{0}}>0.

Solving the inequality (
3.3
) we obtain

(3.4)

\eta(r)\geq\eta(r_{1})e^{B_{1}(r_{1}-r)}+B_{2}F_{1}(r),\quad r>r_{1},

where

F_{1}(r):=e^{-B_{1}r}\int_{r_{1}}^{r}\frac{e^{B_{1}t}}{t}dt\sim\frac{1}{B_{1}r}\mbox{\ \ as\ \ }r\to\infty.

Thus, there exists
r_{2}>r_{1}
such that
F_{1}(r)\geq\frac{1}{2B_{1}r}
for
r\geq r_{2}
. Integrating (
3.4
) we have

\Psi_{0}r-\Phi(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\eta(t)dt>\int_{r_{2}}^{r}\left[\eta(r_{1})e^{B_{1}(r_{1}-t)}+\frac{B_{2}}{2B_{1}t}\right]dt\to\infty\ \ \mbox{ as }\ \ r\to\infty.

This proves the proposition.
∎

4.
Asymptotical Stability of the TSs: the Case
b\leq 0

In this section we study the asymptotical stability for the TSs of (
1.9
) with
b\leq 0
in the cylinder
\Omega
and with prescribed boundary angles:

(4.1)

u_{r}(0,t)=0,\quad u_{r}(1,t)=k,\quad t>0.

Since all the TSs in case
b\leq 0
have convex profiles, it is natural to require the boundary slope is positive:
k>0
and to consider the solutions of (
1.9
)-(
4.1
) with positive mean curvature:

(4.2)

H(r,t)=\frac{u_{rr}}{(1+u_{r}^{2})^{3/2}}+\frac{(N-1)u_{r}}{r\sqrt{1+u_{r}^{2}}}>0,\quad r\in[0,1].

Note that, under this condition,
u_{rr}>0
is not necessarily to be true, but
u_{r}>0
is true (see details in the proof of Theorem
4.1
). In the first subsection we will give some a priori estimates, including the
uniform positive lower and upper bounds for
H
. In the second subsection we prove the asymptotic stability of the TS, that is, any solution of the initial boundary value problem converges to the TS.

4.1.
A priori estimates

To derive the global existence, one needs the
C^{2}
estimate for the solutions of fully nonlinear parabolic equations. We will see below that, the
C^{0}
and
C^{1}
estimates are direct consequences of the maximum principle, as long as the equation is a parabolic one. However, the
C^{2}
estimates and the parabolicity itself is not so obvious. Furthermore, considering our final aim is to show the convergence of the solution to the TS, we even need
uniform-in-time
estimates for
u_{r},u_{rr}
, as well as the
uniform-in-time positive lower and upper bounds for
H
. In other words, the estimate like
H>0
, though it is enough in studying the global existence, but it is not enough to give the convergence of the solution to TS. In this part we will give required uniform estimates by using the maximum principle.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume
u(r,t)
is a classical solution, in the time interval
[0,T)
, of the problem (
1.9
)-(
4.1
) with initial data as in (
1.12
). Assume also
b\leq 0<k
,
(-b)^{1/\alpha}\sqrt{1+k^{2}}<kN
and

(4.3)

H^{\alpha}(r,0)+b>0,\quad r\in[0,1].

Then there exist positive constants
M_{0},M_{1},M_{2},V_{*},V^{*},H_{*},H^{*}
, independent of
t
and
T
, such that

(4.4)

\displaystyle|u(r,t)-\tilde{c}t|\leq M_{0},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T);

(4.5)

\displaystyle 0<u_{r}(r,t)\leq M_{1},\quad r\in(0,1],\ t\in[0,T);

(4.6)

\displaystyle V_{*}\leq u_{t}(r,t)\leq V^{*},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T);

(4.7)

\displaystyle H_{*}\leq H(r,t)\leq H^{*},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T);

(4.8)

\displaystyle|u_{rr}(r,t)|\leq M_{2},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T),

where
\tilde{c}
is the speed of the TS in Theorem
1.4
.

Proof.

By (
4.3
), the mean curvature
H(r,t)
remains positive in
[0,T_{0})
for some
T_{0}\leq T
. We first show that all the inequalities hold in this time interval.

Since
H(r,t)>0
for
t\in[0,T_{0})
, the equation (
1.3
) or (
1.9
) is a parabolic one. So, for some large
M^{\prime}_{0}>0
, the TS
\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+\tilde{c}t+M^{\prime}_{0}
(which exists by
(-b)^{1/\alpha}\sqrt{1+k^{2}}<kN
) is an upper solution of initial-boundary value problem (
1.9
)-(
4.1
) with
u(r,0)=u_{0}(r)
. Similarly,
\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+\tilde{c}t-M^{\prime}_{0}
is a lower solution. (If one worries about the unboundedness of the coefficient
\frac{N-1}{r}
in the equation (
1.9
), he/she can use the equation (
1.3
) instead of (
1.9
) to proceed the comparison.) The estimates (4.4) then follows from the comparison principle, for some
M_{0}
depending on
b,k
and
\|u_{0}\|_{C}
.

We next derive the
C^{1}
bound by using the equation (
1.3
).
(The reason we use the equation (
1.3
) instead of the radially symmetric form (
1.9
) is to avoid the possible unboundedness of the coefficients involving
\frac{u_{r}}{r}
. By further estimates like
u_{r}\leq Kr
, which will be shown below, we see that
\frac{u_{r}}{r}
is actually bounded.) Denote

(4.9)

\xi:=\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}=\sqrt{1+u_{r}^{2}},\quad A:=\delta_{ij}-\frac{D_{i}uD_{j}u}{\xi^{2}}.

Then the equation (
1.3
) is

(4.10)

u_{t}=(H^{\alpha}+b)\xi=\left(\frac{A}{\xi}D_{ij}u\right)^{\alpha}\xi+b\xi,\quad|x|<1,\ t\in[0,T).

Denote
w_{1}(x,t):=u_{x_{1}}(x,t)
, then

w_{1t}=\alpha AH^{\alpha-1}D_{ij}w_{1}+B_{1}DuDw_{1}+B_{2}D_{ij}uD_{i}uD_{j}w_{1},\quad|x|<1,\ t\in[0,T_{0}),

where

B_{1}:=\frac{-\alpha H^{\alpha-1}\xi^{2}AD_{ij}u+2\alpha H^{\alpha-1}D_{i}uD_{j}uD_{ij}u+(H^{\alpha}+b)\xi^{3}}{\xi^{4}},\quad B_{2}:=-\frac{2\alpha H^{\alpha-1}}{\xi^{2}}.

The boundary conditions (
4.1
) imply that

w_{1}(x,t)=\left.u_{r}\frac{x_{1}}{r}\right|_{r=1}=kx_{1},\quad|x|=1,\ t\in[0,T_{0}).

Then, using the maximum principle for
w_{1}
we conclude that

|w_{1}(x,t)|\leq M_{1}:=\|u^{\prime}_{0}\|_{C},\quad|x|\leq 1,\ t\in[0,T_{0}).

Hence,

(4.11)

|u_{r}(r,t)|=|w_{1}(r,0,\cdots,0,t)|\leq M_{1},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0}).

By
H>0
we have
H(0,t)=Nu_{rr}(0,t)>0
. Hence
u_{r}(r,t)>0
for small
r>0
. Though we can not derive
u_{rr}>0
, we do have the conclusion
u_{r}>0
for
r\in(0,1],t\in[0,T_{0})
. Otherwise, assume, for some
t_{1}\in[0,T_{0})
, there exists
r_{1}\in(0,1)
such that it is the smallest positive zero of
u_{r}(\cdot,t_{1})
, then

u_{rr}(r_{1},t_{1})\leq 0
, and so

H(r_{1},t_{1})=\left.\frac{u_{rr}}{(1+u^{2}_{r})^{3/2}}+\frac{(N-1)u_{r}}{r\sqrt{1+u^{2}_{r}}}\right|_{(r_{1},t_{1})}\leq 0,

contradicts our assumption. Hence, the estimate (
4.11
) can be improved to (4.5).

Similarly, if we write
w(x,t):=u_{t}(x,t)
, then

w_{t}=\alpha AH^{\alpha-1}D_{ij}w+B_{3}DuDw+B_{2}D_{ij}uD_{i}uD_{j}w,\quad|x|<1,\ t\in[0,T_{0}),

where
B_{2}
is as above and

B_{3}:=\frac{\alpha H^{\alpha-1}\xi^{2}AD_{ij}u+2\alpha H^{\alpha-1}D_{i}uD_{j}uD_{ij}u+(H^{\alpha}+b)\xi^{3}}{\xi^{4}}.

The boundary condition (
1.4
) implies that

Dw\cdot\nu=0,\quad|x|=1,\ t\in[0,T_{0}),

since
u
is radially symmetric. Then, using the maximum principle for
w
we have

(4.12)

\min u_{t}(r,0)\leq w(x,t)=u_{t}(x,t)\leq\max u_{t}(r,0),\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0}).

Denote

\xi^{0}_{0}:=\max\xi(r,0)=\sqrt{1+[\max u^{\prime}_{0}(r)]^{2}},\quad\xi^{0}:=\sqrt{1+M_{1}^{2}}\geq\xi(r,t).

Then we have

(4.13)

0<V_{*}:=\min H^{\alpha}(r,0)+b\leq u_{t}=(H^{\alpha}+b)\xi\leq V^{*}:=[\max H^{\alpha}(r,0)+b]\xi^{0}_{0}

for
r\in[0,1]
and
t\in[0,T_{0})
, and so

(4.14)

H_{*}:=\left(\frac{V_{*}}{\xi^{0}}-b\right)^{1/\alpha}\leq H(r,t)\leq H^{*}:=\left(V^{*}-b\right)^{1/\alpha},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0}).

This proves (4.7) (note that
H_{*}>0
due to
b\leq 0
).

Furthermore, we can use the second inequality in (
4.14
) to conclude that

u_{rr}\leq H^{*}\xi^{3}-\frac{(N-1)u_{r}\xi^{2}}{r}\leq H^{*}(\xi^{0})^{3},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0}).

This implies that

u_{r}(r,t)=\int_{0}^{r}u_{rr}(r,t)\leq H^{*}(\xi^{0})^{3}r,\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0}).

On the one hand, this implies that

(4.15)

0\leq\frac{u_{r}}{r}\leq H^{*}(\xi^{0})^{3},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0}),

and so the radially symmetric equation (
1.9
) in fact has no singularity at
r=0
. On the other hand, by the first inequality in (
4.14
) we have

(4.16)

u_{rr}\geq H_{*}\xi^{3}-\frac{(N-1)u_{r}\xi^{2}}{r}\geq H_{*}-(N-1)H^{*}(\xi^{0})^{5},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0}).

This proves the bound in (4.8) for
u_{rr}
.

By the above results, especially, the positive lower and upper bounds for
H
, we see that the equation is a uniform parabolic one till the time
T_{0}
. So the interval
[0,T_{0})
where
H
remains to be positive can be extended to
[0,T)
. All the estimates we have proved remain valid in
[0,T)
.

This prove the theorem.
∎

4.2.
Stability of the TS

Based on the a priori estimate we can prove the following stability result of TS in case
b\leq 0

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume
b\leq 0<k
,
(-b)^{1/\alpha}\sqrt{1+k^{2}}<kN
and (
4.3
) holds. Then the problem (
1.9
)-(
4.1
) with initial data
u(r,0)=u_{0}(r)
has a unique global classical solution
u(r,t)
, all the estimate results in the previous theorem hold, and the limit (
1.18
) holds.

Proof.

Using the assumption (
4.3
) one can obtain the local existence for the classical solution
u
. Then using the a priori estimates given in the previous theorem one can obtain the global solution by the parabolic theory.

Next, we study the asymptotic stability of TS, or, the convergence of
u
to the TS. Denote

\tilde{u}(r,t):=u(r,t)-\tilde{c}(b,k)t,\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\geq 0.

By
|\tilde{u}(r,t)|\leq M_{0}
, the uniform-in-time estimates for
u_{r},\ u_{rr}
and
H
, we can use the theory for fully nonlinear uniform parabolic equation to conclude that, for any given
\beta\in(0,1)
,

\|\tilde{u}(r,t)\|_{C^{2+\beta,1+\beta/2}([0,1]\times[t_{1},t_{2}])}\leq C,\quad 0<t_{1}<t_{2},

for some
C
independent of
t_{1},t_{2}
. Therefore, the
\omega
-limit set of
\tilde{u}
is non-empty, connected, invariant and compact. To finish the proof we only need to show that the
\omega
-limit set is a singleton
\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+M
for some
M\in\mathbb{R}
.

We first show that any
\omega
-limit of
\tilde{u}
is a shift of
\widetilde{\Phi}(r)
. Assume, for example,
there exist a time sequence
\{t_{n}\}
and a function
U(r,t)\in C^{2+\beta,1+\beta/2}([0,1]\times(0,\infty))
such that

\tilde{u}(r,t_{n}+t)-U(r,t)\to 0\mbox{\ \ as\ \ }n\to\infty,

in the topology of
C^{2+\beta^{\prime},1+\beta^{\prime}/2}_{loc}([0,1]\times\mathbb{R})
for
\beta^{\prime}\in(0,\beta)
. We show that
U(r,t)\equiv\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+M
for some
M\in\mathbb{R}
. Otherwise, assume by contradiction that

(4.17)

\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+M_{1}\preceq U(r,0)\preceq\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+M_{2},

for some
M_{2}>M_{1}
. Here, we write
f_{1}(r)\preceq f_{2}(r)
if
f_{1}(r)\leq f_{2}(r)
and “equality” does hold at some point. Since both
U
and
\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+M_{2}
satisfy the equation of
\tilde{u}
and the boundary condition (
4.1
), by the strong comparison principle we have

U(r,1)<\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+M_{2}-2\varepsilon,\quad r\in[0,1],

for small
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\tilde{u}(r,t_{n}+1)\to U(r,1)
as
n\to\infty
, for large
n_{0}
, there holds

\tilde{u}(r,t_{n}+1)<U(r,1)+\varepsilon<\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+M_{2}-\varepsilon,\quad r\in[0,1],\ n\geq n_{0}.

Using comparison again we have

\tilde{u}(r,t)\leq\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+M_{2}-\varepsilon,\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\geq t_{n_{0}}+1.

By
\tilde{u}(r,t_{n})\to U(r,0)
we have

U(r,0)\leq\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+M_{2}-\varepsilon,

contradicts the second inequality in (
4.17
). This proves that any
\omega
-limit of
\tilde{u}
is
\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+M
for some
M\in\mathbb{R}
.

Finally, we show that
\omega
-limit set is a singleton, that is, the above
M
is unique. Otherwise, there exists
\epsilon>0
(the case
\epsilon<0
is proved similarly) such that both
\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+M
and
\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+M+2\epsilon
are
\omega
-limits of
\tilde{u}
. So, there exists large
T
such that

\|\tilde{u}(r,T)-\widetilde{\Phi}(r)-M\|_{C^{2}([0,1])}\leq\epsilon,

which, by comparison, implies that

\tilde{u}(r,t)\leq\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+M+\epsilon,\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\geq T.

This, however, contradicts the assumption that
\widetilde{\Phi}(r)+M+2\epsilon
is also an
\omega
-limit of
\tilde{u}
.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

in the former case, and the

, since the profile of the TS in this case is a horizontal plane, whose mean curvature is

5.1.
The case

As above, we first derive the a priori estimates. Assume

On the

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume
b>0,\ k>0

H(r,t)>0
in
[0,T_{0})
for some

. Then there exist positive constants

t
and

\displaystyle|u(r,t)-\tilde{c}(b,k)t|\leq M_{0},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0});

\displaystyle 0<u_{r}(r,t)\leq M_{1},\quad r\in(0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0});

\displaystyle V_{*}\leq u_{t}(r,t)\leq V^{*},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0});

\displaystyle 0<H(r,t)\leq H^{*},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0});

\displaystyle|u_{rr}(r,t)|\leq M_{2},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0}).

Proof.

The proof is similar as that for Theorem
4.1
. The main difference is that the lower bound of

is
0
by the assumption, rather than a positive number as in (
4.14
) due to

in that case. Therefore, the lower bound of

in (
4.16
) is now replaced by

u_{rr}>-\frac{(N-1)u_{r}\xi^{2}}{r}\geq-(N-1)H^{*}(\xi^{0})^{5},\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0}).

This proves the lemma.
∎

As in the previous section, the uniform-in-time positive lower bound of
H
, which has not been given in the above lemma, is the key step in our approach. We will study it under the following condition:

(5.1)

u^{\prime\prime}_{0}(r)>0,\quad r\in[0,1],

which is a little stronger than the condition
H(r,0)>0
.

First, we use the zero number argument to present a uniform positive lower bound for
H(0,t)

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume the hypotheses in the previous lemma hold, and (
5.1
) holds. Then there exists
\delta_{0}>0
depending only on
b,N
and
u_{0}
such that
u_{rr}(0,t)>\delta_{0}
for all
t\in[0,T_{0})
.

Proof.

For any
c>b
with
c-b
sufficient small, by Theorem
3.1
, the equation (
1.9
) has a TS
\Phi(r;c,b)+ct
, and

\Phi^{\prime}(r)<u^{\prime}_{0}(r),\quad r\in(0,1].

This is possible since
u^{\prime\prime}_{0}(r)>0
and
\Phi^{\prime}(r)\to 0
uniformly in
r\in[0,1]
, as
c\to b+0
. Then for any
d\in\mathbb{R}
, the relationship between
\Phi(r;c,b)+d
and
u_{0}(r)
is one of the following 4 cases:

(1). no contact point;

(2).
\Phi(0;c,b)+d=u_{0}(0)
and
\Phi(r;c,b)+d<u_{0}(r)
for
r\in(0,1]
;

(3). there is exactly one point
r_{0}\in(0,1)
with

\Phi(r;c,b)+d>u_{0}(r)\mbox{ for }r\in[0,r_{0}),\quad\Phi(r;c,b)+d<u_{0}(r)\mbox{ for }r\in(r_{0},1].

(4).
\Phi(r;c,b)+d>u_{0}(r)
for
r\in[0,1)
,
\Phi(1;c,b)+d=u_{0}(1)
and

\Phi^{\prime}(1;c,b)<u^{\prime}_{0}(1)
.

Since both
u_{1}(x,t):=u(|x|,t)
and
u_{2}(x,t):=\Phi(|x|;c,b)+d+ct
are radially symmetric solutions of the equation (
1.3
), by using the zero number diminishing property as in
[
2
,
9
]
we conclude that, for any time
t\in(0,T_{0})
, the relationship between
u(r,t)
and
\Phi(r;c,b)+ct+d
is also one of the above 4 cases. In particular, there exists a unique
d=d(t)
such that
\Phi(r;c,b)+ct+d(t)
is tangent to
u(r,t)
at
r=0
, and so

u_{rr}(0,t)\geq\Phi^{\prime\prime}(0;c,b)=\frac{(c-b)^{1/\alpha}}{N},\quad t\in[0,T_{0}).

This proves the lemma.
∎

For the speed parameter
c
in the above proof, we can take another smaller
c_{1}\in(b,c)
such that, the TS
\Phi_{1}(r)+c_{1}t
with
\Phi_{1}(r):=\Phi(r;c_{1},b)
satisfying

(5.2)

u_{rr}(0,t)-\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{1}(0)\geq\delta_{1}:=\frac{(c-b)^{1/\alpha}-(c_{1}-b)^{1/\alpha}}{N},\quad t\in[0,T_{0}),

(5.3)

\Phi^{\prime}_{1}(1)<k,

and

(5.4)

u^{\prime}_{0}(r)-\Phi^{\prime}_{1}(r)>0,\quad r\in(0,1].

Lemma 5.3
.

There holds

(5.5)

\eta(r,t):=u_{r}(r,t)-\Phi^{\prime}_{1}(r)>0,\quad r\in(0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0}).

Proof.

We only need to prove the conclusion in
t\in[0,T_{0}-\epsilon]
for any small
\epsilon>0
. In this time interval we have

H(r,t)\geq h_{*}(\epsilon)>0,\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0}],

and so the equation is a uniform parabolic one. By using the non-radially symmetric equation (
1.3
) if necessary we have the parabolic estimate

|u_{rrr}(r,t)|\leq M_{3}(T_{0},\epsilon),\quad r\in[0,1],\ t\in[0,T_{0}-\epsilon].

Therefore, by (
5.2
), there exists
r_{0}\in(0,1)
small such that

u_{rr}(r,t)-\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{1}(r)>0,\quad r\in[0,r_{0}],\ t\in[0,T_{0}-\epsilon],

and so

(5.6)

\eta(r,t)=u_{r}(r,t)-\Phi^{\prime}_{1}(r)>0,\quad r\in[0,r_{0}],\ t\in[0,T_{0}-\epsilon].

Denote

\xi:=\sqrt{1+u^{2}_{r}},\quad H:=\frac{u_{rr}}{\xi^{3}}+\frac{(N-1)u_{r}}{r\xi},\quad\xi_{1}:=\sqrt{1+(\Phi^{\prime}_{1})^{2}},\quad H_{1}:=\frac{\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{1}}{\xi_{1}^{3}}+\frac{(N-1)\Phi^{\prime}_{1}}{\xi_{1}}.

We will use
A_{1},A_{2},\cdots
to denote continuous functions of
r,u_{r},u_{rr},H
and/or
\Phi^{\prime}_{1},\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{1},H_{1}
. Differentiating the equations

u_{t}=(H^{\alpha}+b)\xi,\quad c_{1}=(H_{1}^{\alpha}+b)\xi_{1}

respectively in

(5.7)

u_{rt}=\frac{\alpha H^{\alpha-1}}{\xi^{2}}u_{rrr}+A_{1}u_{rr}-\frac{(N-1)\alpha H^{\alpha-1}}{r^{2}}u_{r}

and

0=\frac{\alpha H_{1}^{\alpha-1}}{\xi_{1}^{2}}\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}_{1}+A_{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{1}+A_{3}\Phi^{\prime}_{1}.

Subtracting the second equation from the first one we have

\eta_{t}=\frac{\alpha H^{\alpha-1}}{\xi^{2}}\eta_{rr}+A_{4}\eta_{r}+A_{5}\eta,\quad r\in(0,1),\ t\in[0,T_{0}-\epsilon].

Set

D_{1}:=[r_{0},1]\times[0,T_{0}-\epsilon].

Since all of the functions
\frac{1}{r},\ u_{r},\ u_{rr},H^{\alpha-1},\Phi^{\prime}_{1},\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{1},H_{1}^{\alpha-1}
are bounded in
D_{1}
, so are the coefficients in the equation of
\eta
. On the other hand,
\eta(r,t)>0
on the parabolic boundary of

by (
5.6
), (
5.3
) and (

\eta(r,t)>0\mbox{\ \ in\ \ }D_{1}.

Combining with (
5.6

∎

Next, we show that

in

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume the hypotheses in Lemma
5.1
hold and (
5.1
) holds. Then
u_{rr}(r,t)>0
for all
r\in[0,1],t\in[0,T_{0})

Proof.

Since
u^{\prime\prime}_{0}(r)>0

u_{rr}(r,t)>0
for small

by continuity. Assume, by contradiction, that, for some
T_{1}\in(0,T_{0})
and some

u_{rr}(r,t)>0\mbox{ for }r\in[0,1],t\in[0,T_{1}),\quad u_{rr}(r_{1},T_{1})=0.

Differentiating the equation (

with

\zeta_{t}=\frac{\alpha H^{\alpha-1}}{\xi^{2}}\zeta_{rr}+A_{6}\zeta_{r}+A_{7}\zeta+\frac{\alpha^{2}(N-1)^{2}u^{2}_{r}H^{\alpha-2}}{r\xi^{4}}>\frac{\alpha H^{\alpha-1}}{\xi^{2}}\zeta_{rr}+A_{6}\zeta_{r}+A_{7}\zeta,

where, as above,

Since

Combining with (

If the zero

Combining the above lemmas we obtain a positive lower bound for

Proposition 5.5
.

Assume the hypotheses in Lemma
5.1
hold and (
5.1
) holds. Then, there exists

, independent of

, such that

For all

This proves the conclusion.
∎

Lattice uniformities inducing unbounded convergence
Kevin Abela, Emmanuel Chetcuti, Hans Weber

Abstract
. A net ( x γ ) γ ∈ Γ (x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma} in a locally solid Riesz space ( X , τ ) (X,\tau) is said to be unbounded τ \tau -convergent to x x if | x γ − x | ∧ u ​ ⟶ 𝜏 0 |x_{\gamma}-x|\wedge u\mathop{\overset{\tau}{\longrightarrow}}0 for all u ∈ X + u\in X_{+} . We recall that there is a locally solid linear topology 𝔲 ​ τ \mathfrak{u}\tau on X X such that unbounded τ \tau -convergence coincides with 𝔲 ​ τ \mathfrak{u}\tau -convergence, and moreover, 𝔲 ​ τ \mathfrak{u}\tau is characterised as the weakest locally solid linear topology which coincides with τ \tau on all order bounded subsets. It is with this motivation that we introduce, for a uniform lattice ( L , u ) (L,u) , the weakest lattice uniformity u ∗ u^{\ast} on L L that coincides with u u on all the order bounded subsets of L L . It is shown that if u u is the uniformity induced by the topology of a locally solid Riesz space ( X , τ ) (X,\tau) , then the u ∗ u^{*} -topology coincides with 𝔲 ​ τ \mathfrak{u}\tau . This allows comparing results of this paper with earlier results on unbounded τ \tau -convergence. It will be seen that despite the fact that in the setup of uniform lattices most of the machinery used in the techniques of [ 22 ] is lacking, the concept of ‘unbounded convergence’ well fittingly generalizes to uniform lattices. We shall also answer Questions 2.13, 3.3, 5.10 of [ 22 ] and Question 18.51 of [ 21 ] .

Proposition 4.6

Let

x=\sup_{s\in S}s\wedge x=\inf_{s\in S}s\vee x

Assume that

is Hausdorff since
u_{I}\subseteq u
. Let

\sup_{s\in S}x\wedge s=x
. Let
y\leq x
with

Then for every

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
(G,+,\tau)
be a Hausdorff locally solid commutative
\ell
-group and
A
a solid subgroup of
G
. Let

be the uniformity induced by
\tau
and

. Then the

-topology coincides with
\mathfrak{u}_{A}\tau
. In particular, the
u^{*}
-topology coincides with
\mathfrak{u}\tau

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{U}
be a

-neighbourhood base for
(G,\tau)
consisting of solid sets.
For
s,t\in L
with
t-s\in A_{+}
and

let

\hat{U}(x_{0},s,t)=\{x\in G:f_{s,t}(x)-f_{s,t}(x_{0})\in U\}.

Then

is a neighbourhood subbase of
x_{0}
w.r.t. the
u_{J}
-topology. We recall that the family
\{\tilde{U}(x_{0},a)\,:\,U\in\mathfrak{U},\,a\in A_{+}\}
, where

\tilde{U}(x_{0},a):=\{x\in G:|x-x_{0}|\wedge a\in U\},

is a neighbourhood base of
x_{0}
w.r.t.
\mathfrak{u}_{A}\tau
.

Let
U\in\mathfrak{U}
and
s,t\in G
with
a:=t-s\in A_{+}
. We show that
\tilde{U}(x_{0},a)\subseteq\hat{U}(x_{0},s,t)
. Let

. By (ii) of Lemma
2.7
, one obtains

|f_{s,t}(x)-f_{s,t}(x_{0})|\leq|x-x_{0}|\wedge a\in U,

i.e.

is coarser than
\mathfrak{u}_{A}\tau
.

Let us show the converse. Let

and
U\in\mathfrak{U}
. Choose
V\in\mathfrak{U}
with
V+V\subseteq U
, and set

,
s:=x_{0}
and

. We show that

\hat{V}(x_{0},s,t)\cap\hat{V}(x_{0},r,s)\subseteq\tilde{U}(x_{0},a)
.
For
x\in\hat{V}(x_{0},s,t)\cap\hat{V}(x_{0},r,s)
one has by Lemma

|x-x_{0}|\wedge a=|f_{r,s}(x)-f_{r,s}(x_{0})|\,+\,|f_{s,t}(x)-f_{s,t}(x_{0})|\in V+V\subseteq U,

hence

.
∎

Example 4.10
.

Let

and
\tau
be the locally solid group topology induced by
\|(x_{n})\|:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|x_{n}|
. Let

,

and

the

-topology.
Then

is strictly coarser than
\mathfrak{u}_{A}\tau
.

Proof.

Let
x=(1,2,3,\dots)
,
e=(1,1,1,\dots)
,

,

We show that

(
\sigma

(

Let

5.
The uniformity
u^{*}
on sublattices

In this section, let
u
be a lattice uniformity on a distributive lattice
L
.

Let
D(L)
denote the set of semimetrics
d
on
L
satisfying

d(x\vee z,y\vee z)\leq d(x,y)\quad\text{and}\quad d(x\wedge z,y\wedge z)\leq d(x,y)\quad\text{for every $x,y,z\in L$.}

By Proposition
3.9
,
u
is generated by a subset of
D(L)
, and therefore by the set
D_{u}
of all
d\in D(L)
which are uniformly continuous w.r.t.
u
.

Remark 5.1
.

Let
J\subseteq J(L)
. For every
(a,b)\in J
and
d\in D(L)
denote by
d_{a,b}
the semimetric on
L
defined by
d_{a,b}(x,y):=d(f_{a,b}(x),\,f_{a,b}(y))
. It is easy to see then that the lattice uniformity
u_{J}
is generated by the family
\{d_{a,b}:d\in D_{u},\,(a,b)\in J\}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
d\in D(L)
.

(i)

Let
P_{n}:L^{n}\to L
be given inductively by
P_{1}(x):=x
and

P_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=\begin{cases}P_{n-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\,\vee\,x_{n},&\mbox{or }\\
P_{n-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\,\wedge\,x_{n}.&\end{cases}

Then
d(P_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),P_{n}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}d(x_{i},y_{i})
.

(ii)

d(f_{a,b}(x),f_{a,b}(y))\leq d(f_{c,d}(x),f_{c,d}(y))+2d(a,c)+2d(b,d)
, for every
a,b,c,d,x,y\in L
.

Proof.

The proof of (i) is by induction. The assertion is trivially true when
n=1
. Suppose that

d(P_{n-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}),P_{n-1}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1}))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}d(x_{i},y_{i})
. Let
\diamond\in\{\wedge,\vee\}
. Then,

\displaystyle d(P_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),P_{n}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}))

\displaystyle=d(P_{n-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\diamond x_{n},P_{n-1}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1})\diamond y_{n})

\displaystyle\leq d(P_{n-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\diamond x_{n},P_{n-1}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1})\diamond x_{n})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+d(P_{n-1}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1})\diamond x_{n},P_{n-1}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1})\diamond y_{n})

\displaystyle\leq d(P_{n-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}),P_{n-1}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1}))+d(x_{n},y_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}d(x_{i},y_{i}).

To prove (ii) one first writes

d(f_{a,b}(x),f_{a,b}(y))\leq d(f_{a,b}(x),f_{c,d}(x))+d(f_{c,d}(x),f_{c,d}(y))+d(f_{c,d}(y),f_{a,b}(y)),

and then apply (i) to obtain

d(f_{a,b}(x),f_{a,b}(y))\leq d(f_{c,d}(x),f_{c,d}(y))+2d(a,c)+2d(b,d).

∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\emptyset\neq J\subseteq J(L)
and let
\tilde{J}:=\bar{J}\cap J(L)
, where
\bar{J}
is the closure of

The inclusion

follows by Proposition
4.2
(ii). For the reverse inclusion, suppose that

. We show that there exists

such that

. In view of Remark

there exist

,
(a,b)\in\tilde{J}
and

, such that
(x,y)\in W

satisfying

. Let
